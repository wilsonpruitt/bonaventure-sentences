# I Sent., d. 1, a. 1, q. 1
## *Utrum uti sit actus voluntatis, an rationis, vel omnis potentiae*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 30 -->

### Articulus I. *Quid sit uti et de utibili.*
### Quaestio I. *Utrum uti sit actus voluntatis, an rationis, vel omnis potentiae.*

Circa primum[^1] de uti, quid sit per essentiam, utrum sit actus voluntatis, an rationis, sic proceditur. Quod sit actus voluntatis ostenditur sic.

1. Ab actu utendi denominatur unusquisque bonus[^2]; sed nullus dicitur bonus nisi ab actu voluntatis: ergo actus utendi pertinet ad voluntatem.

2. Item, Augustinus decimo *de Trinitate*[^3]: «Uti est assumere aliquid in facultatem voluntatis»: ergo uti est actus voluntatis.

3. Item, hoc videtur per divisionem ipsius uti contra alia, quia decimo *de Trinitate*[^4] dividit Augustinus haec tria, ingenium, doctrinam et usum, et dicit, quod ingenium respicit, quid homo possit, doctrina, quid homo sciat, usus, quid[^5] velit: ergo usus est actus voluntatis.

4. Item, «Opposita nata sunt fieri circa idem»[^6]; sed uti et abuti sunt opposita, ergo cum abuti sit solius voluntatis, quia eius solius est peccare: ergo et uti similiter erit actus solius voluntatis.

**Contra:** 1. Usus sive assuefactio est via in habitum acquirendum[^7]; sed omnis potentia nata est acquirere habitum: ergo uti videtur esse omnis potentiae.

2. Item, omnis potentia mediante operatione sua ordinatur in finem[^8]; sed eo dicitur aliquid utibile sive uti, quo in finem est ordinabile; sed ordinari in finem mediante propria operatione est omnis potentiae: ergo et uti similiter.

3. Item, sicut Augustinus dicit et habetur in littera[^9], uti est id quod in usum venerit referre etc. Sed eius est referre, cuius est conferre; sed conferre est solius rationis, ergo et referre: ergo et uti a definitione.

4. Item, ars utitur suis instrumentis[^10]; sed ars est in potentia rationali sive cognitiva, cum sit scientia: ergo cum habitus et actus sint eiusdem potentiae, si ars est in potentia rationali, et usus similiter erit in eadem.

> **Conclusio.** Uti quinque modis potest intelligi: si sumitur communissime et communiter, est actus omnis potentiae; si sumitur proprie, magis proprie et propriissime, est actus voluntatis.

**Respondeo**[^11]**:** Dicendum, quod uti quinque modis accipitur, et secundum hoc diversificatur secundum rem et secundum definitionem.

Accipitur enim usus sive uti communissime, secundum quod dividitur contra otium vel otiositatem, pro naturali operatione debita cuilibet rei, sive

<!-- page 31 -->

ad quam unaquaeque res ordinatur, et secundum hoc dicit Philosophus[^12]: «Cuius usus bonus etc.».

Secundo modo dicitur communiter; et sic usus dividitur contra dissuetudinem. Et hoc modo definitur a Victorino[^13]: «Usus est actus frequenter elicitus a potentia», et hoc modo potest dici, quod est actus omnis potentiae nec addit super actum nisi frequentiam.

Tertio modo accipitur uti proprie; et sic dividitur contra habitus memoriae et intelligentiae, scilicet[^14] contra ingenium et doctrinam. Et sic definitur ab Augustino decimo *de Trinitate*: «Uti est assumere aliquid in facultatem voluntatis», et sic dicit actum voluntatis proprie.

Quarto modo accipitur magis proprie; et sic dividitur contra actum quietativum, scilicet contra frui. Et sic definitur ab Augustino[^15]: «Uti est assumere aliquid in facultatem voluntatis propter aliud», et sic est actus voluntatis ut ad aliud relatae.

Quinto accipitur propriissime; et sic dividitur contra actum inordinatum, scilicet contra abuti. Et sic definitur ab Augustino *de Doctrina christiana*[^16]: «Uti est id quod in usum venerit referre ad id, quo fruendum est», et sic uti dicit actum voluntatis relatum et ordinatum in finem.

Ratio ergo diversarum notificationum est multiplex acceptio eius quod est uti; et ratio multiplicis acceptionis est oppositio eius ad diversa.

Si ergo quaeratur, cuius potentiae est[^17] actus: dicendum, quod, communissime et communiter loquendo, est actus omnis potentiae; et sic non loquitur Augustinus; proprie vero, magis proprie et propriissime actus est ipsius voluntatis.

**Ad argumenta in contrarium:**

1. Et sic patet primo obiectum in contrarium, scilicet quod[^18] sit omnis potentiae.

2. Quod obiicitur secundo, quod omnis potentia refertur in finem per actum suum; dicendum, quod illa potentia dicitur uti suo actu, quae est domina sui actus; et talis potentiae est referre suum actum, non tantum referri. Et quoniam sola voluntas est domina sui actus et sola est se ipsam movens, ideo ipsa sola est, cuius est active uti. Aliis autem potentiis contingit uti materialiter et passive, quia habent referri, et non referre. Et hinc est, quod ab Augustino dicitur[^19]: «Uti est assumere aliquid in facultatem voluntatis»; facultas enim dicitur eius dominium, quo faciliter potest in actum suum tanquam illius princeps.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod rationis est referre; dicendum, quod dupliciter est referre, scilicet per collationem et illationem, sicut praemissae referuntur ad conclusionem; et hoc est rationis, et hoc non est uti. Et est referre per[^20] inclinationem et ordinationem ad aliud; et hoc est proprie voluntatis, quia potest aliquid assumere quiescendo ibi, vel aliud intendendo; et hoc est referre[^21], per quod definitur uti etc.

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur de arte, dicendum, quod ars dicit quod est operationis, et quod est speculationis. Secundum quod dicit[^22] quod est speculationis, non habet uti, et sic est rationis; secundum vero quod dicit quod est operationis, sic extenditur ad voluntatem et eam regulat in utendo.

## English
<!-- page 30 -->

### Article I. *What use is, and concerning the useful.*
### Question I. *Whether use is an act of the will, of reason, or of every faculty.*

Concerning the first[^1] point about use — what it is by essence, whether it is an act of the will or of reason — one proceeds thus. That it is an act of the will is shown thus.

1. By the act of using, each one is called good[^2]; but no one is called good except from an act of the will: therefore the act of using pertains to the will.

2. Likewise, Augustine in the tenth book *On the Trinity*[^3]: "To use is to take up something into the faculty of the will": therefore to use is an act of the will.

3. Likewise, this is apparent by the division of use itself against other things, because in the tenth book *On the Trinity*[^4] Augustine divides these three — talent (*ingenium*), doctrine, and use — and says that talent regards what a man can do, doctrine what a man knows, use what[^5] he wills: therefore use is an act of the will.

4. Likewise, "Opposites are by nature produced about the same thing"[^6]; but to use and to misuse are opposites; therefore, since to misuse belongs to the will alone, because to sin belongs to it alone: therefore to use will likewise be an act of the will alone.

**On the contrary:** 1. Use or habituation is the way to acquiring a habit[^7]; but every faculty is by nature able to acquire a habit: therefore use seems to belong to every faculty.

2. Likewise, every faculty is ordered to an end through its own operation[^8]; but something is called useful or to be used insofar as it is orderable to an end; but to be ordered to an end through its proper operation belongs to every faculty: therefore use likewise.

3. Likewise, as Augustine says and as is held in the *Lectio* [of the Master][^9], to use is to refer that which has come into use, and so on. But it belongs to that which can refer, to which it belongs to compare; but to compare belongs to reason alone, therefore so does to refer: therefore so also does to use, from its definition.

4. Likewise, art uses its own instruments[^10]; but art is in the rational or cognitive faculty, since it is a science: therefore, since habit and act belong to the same faculty, if art is in the rational faculty, use likewise will be in the same.

> **Conclusion.** Use can be understood in five ways: if it is taken most commonly and commonly, it is an act of every faculty; if it is taken properly, more properly, and most properly, it is an act of the will.

**I respond**[^11]**:** It must be said that use is taken in five ways, and accordingly it is diversified both with respect to the thing and with respect to the definition.

For use or *uti* is taken most commonly insofar as it is divided against idleness or leisure, for the natural operation due to any given thing, or that

<!-- page 31 -->

to which each thing is ordered; and according to this the Philosopher says[^12]: "Whose use is good, etc."

In a second mode it is said commonly; and thus use is divided against disuse. And in this mode it is defined by Victorinus[^13]: "Use is an act frequently elicited from a faculty," and in this mode it can be said that it is an act of every faculty, and it adds nothing to the act except frequency.

In a third mode use is taken properly; and thus it is divided against the habits of memory and intelligence, namely[^14] against talent and doctrine. And thus it is defined by Augustine in the tenth book *On the Trinity*: "To use is to take up something into the faculty of the will," and thus it names an act of the will properly.

In a fourth mode it is taken more properly; and thus it is divided against the quietative act, namely against enjoyment (*frui*). And thus it is defined by Augustine[^15]: "To use is to take up something into the faculty of the will on account of another thing," and thus it is an act of the will as referred to another.

In a fifth mode it is taken most properly; and thus it is divided against the disordered act, namely against misuse (*abuti*). And thus it is defined by Augustine *On Christian Doctrine*[^16]: "To use is to refer what has come into use to that which is to be enjoyed," and thus *uti* names an act of the will referred and ordered to an end.

Therefore the reason for the diverse definitions is the manifold acceptation of *uti*; and the reason for the manifold acceptation is its opposition to diverse things.

If, then, it be asked of which faculty it is[^17] the act: it must be said that, speaking most commonly and commonly, it is the act of every faculty; and in this way Augustine does not speak; but properly, more properly, and most properly, it is the act of the will itself.

**To the arguments on the contrary:**

1. And thus the first objection on the contrary side is clear, namely that[^18] it is of every faculty.

2. As to what is objected secondly, that every faculty is referred to its end through its own act: it must be said that that faculty is said to use its own act which is mistress of its act; and it belongs to such a faculty to refer its act, not only to be referred. And since the will alone is mistress of its act and alone is self-moving, therefore it alone is that to which it belongs actively to use. To the other faculties, however, it pertains to be used materially and passively, because they are referable, not refer-ers. And hence it is that it is said by Augustine[^19]: "To use is to take up something into the faculty of the will"; for *facultas* is called its dominion, by which it can easily proceed into its act as the prince of that act.

3. To what is objected, that to refer belongs to reason: it must be said that to refer is twofold, namely by collation and by inference, as premises are referred to a conclusion; and this belongs to reason, and this is not to use. And there is to refer by[^20] inclination and ordering to another; and this belongs properly to the will, because it can take up something either by resting in it or by directing it toward another; and this is to refer[^21] by which use is defined, and so on.

4. To what is objected concerning art, it must be said that art expresses both what belongs to operation and what belongs to speculation. According to what it says[^22] belongs to speculation, it has no use, and so it belongs to reason; but according to what it says belongs to operation, it extends to the will and regulates it in using.
