# I Sent., d. 1, a. 1, q. 1
## *Utrum uti sit actus voluntatis, an rationis, vel omnis potentiae*

---

## Latin

<!-- page 30 -->

**ARTICULUS I.** *Quid sit* uti *et de* utibili.

**QUAESTIO I.** *Utrum* uti *sit actus voluntatis, an rationis, vel omnis potentiae.*

Circa primum[^1] *de uti*, quid sit per essentiam, utrum sit actus voluntatis, an rationis, sic proceditur. Quod sit actus voluntatis ostenditur sic.

1. Ab actu utendi denominatur unusquisque bonus[^2]; sed nullus dicitur bonus nisi ab actu voluntatis: ergo actus utendi pertinet ad voluntatem.

2. Item, Augustinus decimo *de Trinitate*[^3]: «Uti est assumere aliquid in facultatem voluntatis»; ergo *uti* est actus voluntatis.

3. Item, hoc videtur per divisionem ipsius *uti* contra alia, quia decimo *de Trinitate*[^4] dividit Augustinus haec tria, *ingenium*, *doctrinam* et *usum*, et dicit, quod *ingenium* respicit, quid homo possit, *doctrina*, quid homo sciat, *usus*, quid[^5] velit; ergo *usus* est actus voluntatis.

4. Item, «Opposita nata sunt fieri circa idem[^6]»; sed *uti* et *abuti* sunt opposita, ergo cum *abuti* sit solius voluntatis, quia eius solius est peccare: ergo et *uti* similiter erit actus solius voluntatis.

**Contra:**

1. *Usus* sive *assuefactio* est via in habitum acquirendum[^7]; sed omnis potentia nata est acquirere habitum: ergo *uti* videtur esse omnis potentiae.

2. Item, omnis potentia mediante operatione sua ordinatur in finem[^8]; sed eo dicitur aliquid *utibile* sive *uti*, quo in finem est ordinabile; sed ordinari in finem mediante propria operatione est omnis potentiae: ergo et *uti* similiter.

3. Item, sicut Augustinus dicit et habetur in littera[^9], *uti* est id quod in usum venerit referre etc. Sed eius est referre, cuius est conferre; sed conferre est solius rationis, ergo et referre: ergo et *uti* a definitione.

4. Item, *ars* utitur suis instrumentis[^10]; sed *ars* est in potentia rationali sive cognitiva, cum sit scientia: ergo cum habitus et actus sint eiusdem potentiae, si *ars* est in potentia rationali, et *usus* similiter erit in eadem.

> **Conclusio.**
>
> *Uti quinque modis potest intelligi: si sumitur communissime et communiter, est actus omnis potentiae; si sumitur proprie, magis proprie et propriissime, est actus voluntatis.*

**Respondeo[^11]:** Dicendum, quod *uti* quinque modis accipitur, et secundum hoc diversificatur secundum rem et secundum definitionem.

Accipitur enim *usus* sive *uti* *communissime*, secundum quod dividitur contra otium vel otiositatem, pro naturali operatione debita cuilibet rei, sive

<!-- page 31 -->

ad quam unaquaeque res ordinatur, et secundum hoc dicit Philosophus[^12]: «Cuius usus bonus etc.».

Secundo modo dicitur *communiter*; et sic *usus* dividitur contra dissuetudinem. Et hoc modo definitur a Victorino[^13]: «Usus est actus frequenter elicitus a potentia», et hoc modo potest dici, quod est actus omnis potentiae nec addit super actum nisi frequentiam.

Tertio modo accipitur *uti* *proprie*; et sic dividitur contra habitus memoriae et intelligentiae, scilicet[^14] contra *ingenium* et *doctrinam*. Et sic definitur ab Augustino decimo *de Trinitate*: «Uti est assumere aliquid in facultatem voluntatis», et sic dicit actum voluntatis proprie.

Quarto modo accipitur *magis proprie*; et sic dividitur contra actum quietativum, scilicet contra *frui*. Et sic definitur ab Augustino[^15]: «Uti est assumere aliquid in facultatem voluntatis propter aliud», et sic est actus voluntatis ut ad aliud relatae.

Quinto accipitur *propriissime*; et sic dividitur contra actum inordinatum, scilicet contra *abuti*. Et sic definitur ab Augustino *de Doctrina christiana*[^16]: «Uti est id quod in usum venerit referre ad id, quo fruendum est», et sic *uti* dicit actum voluntatis relatum et ordinatum in finem.

Ratio ergo diversarum notificationum est multiplex acceptio eius quod est *uti*; et ratio multiplicis acceptionis est oppositio eius ad diversa.

Si ergo quaeratur, cuius potentiae est[^17] actus: dicendum, quod, communissime et communiter loquendo, est actus omnis potentiae; et sic non loquitur Augustinus; *proprie* vero, *magis proprie* et *propriissime* actus est ipsius voluntatis.

**Ad 1.** Et sic patet primo obiectum in contrarium, scilicet quod[^18] sit omnis potentiae.

**Ad 2.** Quod obiicitur secundo, quod omnis potentia refertur in finem per actum suum: dicendum, quod illa potentia dicitur *uti* suo actu, quae est domina sui actus; et talis potentiae est referre suum actum, non tantum referri. Et quoniam sola voluntas est domina sui actus et sola est se ipsam movens, ideo ipsa sola est, cuius est active *uti*. Aliis autem potentiis contingit *uti* materialiter et passive, quia habent referri, et non referre. Et hinc est, quod ab Augustino dicitur[^19]: «Uti est assumere aliquid in facultatem voluntatis»; *facultas* enim dicitur eius dominium, quo faciliter potest in actum suum tanquam illius princeps.

**Ad 3.** Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod rationis est referre: dicendum, quod dupliciter est referre, scilicet per *collationem* et *illationem*, sicut praemissae referuntur ad conclusionem; et hoc est rationis, et hoc non est *uti*. Et est referre per[^20] *inclinationem* et *ordinationem* ad aliud; et hoc est proprie voluntatis, quia potest aliquid assumere quiescendo ibi, vel aliud intendendo; et hoc est referre[^21], per quod definitur *uti* etc.

**Ad 4.** Ad illud quod obiicitur de arte, dicendum, quod *ars* dicit quod est *operationis*, et quod est *speculationis*. Secundum quod dicit[^22] quod est *speculationis*, non habet *uti*, et sic est rationis; secundum vero quod dicit quod est *operationis*, sic extenditur ad voluntatem et eam regulat in utendo.

## English

<!-- page 30 -->

**Article I.** *What* use *is, and concerning the* usable.

**Question I.** *Whether* use *is an act of the will, of reason, or of every faculty.*

Concerning the first[^1] — *concerning use*, what it is in essence, whether it is an act of the will or of reason — we proceed thus. That it is an act of the will is shown as follows.

1. From the act of using each one is called good[^2]; but no one is called good except from an act of the will: therefore the act of using pertains to the will.

2. Again, Augustine in Book X[^3] *On the Trinity*: "To use is to take something into the power of the will"; therefore *use* is an act of the will.

3. Again, this seems [to follow] from the division of *use* itself against other things, since in Book X *On the Trinity*[^4] Augustine divides these three — *ingenuity*, *teaching*, and *use* — and says that *ingenuity* considers what man is able [to do], *teaching* what man knows, *use* what[^5] he wills; therefore *use* is an act of the will.

4. Again, "Opposites are by nature concerned with the same thing[^6]"; but *use* and *misuse* are opposites; therefore since *misuse* belongs to the will alone, because to sin belongs to it alone: therefore *use* likewise will be the act of the will alone.

**On the contrary:**

1. *Use* or *habituation* is the way to acquiring a habit[^7]; but every faculty is by nature able to acquire a habit: therefore *use* seems to belong to every faculty.

2. Again, every faculty is ordered to its end through its own operation[^8]; but a thing is called *usable* or said to be *used* by reason of that whereby it is orderable to an end; but to be ordered to an end through one's own operation belongs to every faculty: therefore *use* likewise [belongs to every faculty].

3. Again, as Augustine says, and as it stands in the *littera*[^9], *to use* is to refer what has come into use, etc. But to refer is the office of him whose office is to confer; but conferring belongs only to reason — therefore [the office of] referring also: therefore *use* too [belongs to reason], by [its very] definition.

4. Again, *art* uses its instruments[^10]; but *art* is in a rational or cognitive power, since it is a science: therefore, since habit and act belong to the same faculty, if *art* is in a rational power, *use* likewise will be in the same.

> **Conclusion.**
>
> *"Use" can be understood in five ways: if taken most commonly and commonly, it is an act of every faculty; if taken properly, more properly, and most properly, it is an act of the will.*

**I respond[^11]:** It must be said that *use* is taken in five ways, and accordingly its meaning and its definition are differentiated.

For *use* or *to use* is taken *most commonly*, insofar as it is divided against idleness or inactivity — [namely] for the natural operation owed to each thing, or that

<!-- page 31 -->

to which each thing is ordered; and in this sense the Philosopher says[^12]: "That whose use is good, etc."

In the second way it is said *commonly*; and in this way *use* is divided against disuse. And in this way it is defined by Victorinus[^13]: "Use is an act frequently elicited from a power"; and in this way it can be said that it is the act of every faculty and adds nothing to the act except frequency.

In the third way *use* is taken *properly*; and in this way it is divided against the habits of memory and understanding — namely[^14], against *ingenuity* and *teaching*. And in this way it is defined by Augustine in Book X *On the Trinity*: "To use is to take something into the power of the will"; and in this way it expresses an act of the will properly.

In the fourth way it is taken *more properly*; and in this way it is divided against the quietative act — namely, against *enjoyment*. And in this way it is defined by Augustine[^15]: "To use is to take something into the power of the will for the sake of something else"; and in this way it is the act of the will as referred to something else.

In the fifth way it is taken *most properly*; and in this way it is divided against the disordered act — namely, against *misuse*. And in this way it is defined by Augustine *On Christian Doctrine*[^16]: "To use is to refer what has come into use to that which is to be enjoyed"; and in this way *use* expresses an act of the will referred and ordered to its end.

The ground, therefore, of the various definitions is the manifold acceptance of what *use* is; and the ground of the manifold acceptance is its opposition to different things.

If, then, it be asked of which faculty it is the act[^17]: it must be said that, speaking most commonly and commonly, it is the act of every faculty (and Augustine does not speak in this sense); but *properly*, *more properly*, and *most properly*, the act is of the will itself.

**Reply 1.** And so the first objection on the contrary side is clear — namely, that[^18] it is of every faculty.

**Reply 2.** As to the second objection, that every faculty is referred to its end through its own act: it must be said that that faculty is said to *use* its own act which is mistress of its own act; and it belongs to such a faculty to refer its own act, not merely to be referred. And since the will alone is mistress of its own act, and alone is self-moving, therefore it alone is the faculty whose role is actively to *use*. To the other faculties it falls to be *used* materially and passively, since they have to be referred and not [themselves] to refer. And from this it is that Augustine says[^19]: "To use is to take something into the power of the will"; for *power* (*facultas*) is said to be the will's mastery, by which it can readily pass into its own act as its sovereign.

**Reply 3.** As to the objection that to refer belongs to reason: it must be said that referring is twofold — namely, by *comparison* and *inference*, as premises are referred to a conclusion; and this belongs to reason, and this is not *use*. And there is referring by[^20] *inclination* and *ordering* toward something else; and this properly belongs to the will, since it can take something up either by resting in it or by intending something further; and this is the referring[^21] by which *use* is defined, etc.

**Reply 4.** As to the objection concerning art: it must be said that *art* covers what pertains to *operation* and what pertains to *speculation*. Insofar as it covers[^22] what is *speculative*, it does not involve *use*, and so belongs to reason; but insofar as it covers what is *operative*, it extends to the will and regulates it in *using*.
