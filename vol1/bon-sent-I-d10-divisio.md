# I Sent., d. 10 — Divisio textus
## *Commentary on Distinction X — Division of the text*

---

## Latin

<!-- page 194 -->

### Commentarius in Distinctionem X.

*De aeterna processione Spiritus sancti quantum ad personam quae procedit.*

*Nunc post Filii aeternitatem de Spiritu sancto, quantum Deo donante, etc.*

### Divisio textus.

Supra egit Magister de generatione Filii, hic autem agit de processione Spiritus sancti. Et quia gemina est processio Spiritus sancti[^1], aeterna et temporalis, primo agit de processione aeterna, secundo de temporali, infra, distinctione decima quarta: *Praeterea notandum, quod gemina est processio Spiritus sancti* etc. Et quoniam processio Spiritus sancti tripliciter potest comparari, scilicet ad personam ad quam est, et ad principium a quo est, et ad generationem a qua differt, ideo haec pars habet tres; in quarum prima Magister agit de processione Spiritus sancti quantum ad personam, quae procedit; in secunda, quantum ad principium a quo, infra, distinctione undecima: *Hic dicendum est, Spiritum sanctum esse a Patre et Filio*; in tertia vero agit de ipsa per comparationem ad generationem, a qua distinguitur, infra, distinctione decima tertia: *Post haec considerandum est, quantum a talibus, quales nos sumus.*

Item, prima pars, quae continet praesentem distinctionem, habet quatuor. In prima dicit, quod Spiritus sanctus procedit ut amor sive caritas vel dilectio; et hoc probat auctoritate Augustini. In secunda vero, quia hoc[^2] erat dubium, quod Spiritus sanctus esset caritas, movet quaestionem et solvit, ibi: *Et ideo quaerendum, utrum Deus Pater.* In tertia vero solutionem per auctoritatem confirmat, tum quantum ad distinctionem, quae est per communem acceptionem et propriam, tum etiam quantum ad veritatem, ibi: *Pluribus enim exemplis doceri potest* etc. In quarta vero et ultima praedictae solutionis assignat rationem ostendens, quod[^3] Spiritus sanctus proprie dicatur caritas, cum caritas sit nomen commune, et hoc ibi: *Hic notandum est, quod sicut Spiritus sanctus in Trinitate.*

### Tractatio quaestionum.

Ad evidentiam eorum quae dicuntur de Spiritu sancto, in hac parte duo principaliter quaeruntur.

Primo quaeritur de modo procedendi ipsius Spiritus sancti[^4].

Secundo de proprietate ipsius.

Circa primum quaeruntur tria.

Primum est, utrum in divinis sit necesse ponere personam procedentem per modum liberalitatis.

Secundum, utrum sit necesse ponere personam procedentem per modum amoris.

Tertium, si[^5] sit necesse ponere personam procedere per modum mutuae caritatis.

## English

### Commentary on Distinction X.

*On the eternal procession of the Holy Spirit insofar as it concerns the person who proceeds.*

*Now after the eternity of the Son, concerning the Holy Spirit, insofar as God grants, etc.*

### Division of the text.

Above the Master treated of the generation of the Son; here, however, he treats of the procession of the Holy Spirit. And because the procession of the Holy Spirit is twofold[^1], eternal and temporal, first he treats of the eternal procession, secondly of the temporal, below in distinction fourteen: *Furthermore it is to be noted, that the procession of the Holy Spirit is twofold*, etc. And since the procession of the Holy Spirit can be compared in three ways — namely, to the person to whom it is, and to the principle from which it is, and to the generation from which it differs — therefore this part has three sections; in the first of which the Master treats of the procession of the Holy Spirit insofar as it concerns the person who proceeds; in the second, insofar as it concerns the principle from which, below in distinction eleven: *Here it must be said that the Holy Spirit is from the Father and the Son*; in the third he treats of the procession itself by way of comparison to generation, from which it is distinguished, below in distinction thirteen: *After these things it must be considered, how far from such things as we are.*

Likewise, the first part, which contains the present distinction, has four [subsections]. In the first he says that the Holy Spirit proceeds as love, or charity, or affection (*dilectio*); and this he proves by the authority of Augustine. In the second, because this[^2] was a doubt — that the Holy Spirit should be charity — he raises the question and solves it, there: *And therefore it must be asked, whether God the Father.* In the third he confirms the solution by authority, both as regards the distinction which is by common and by proper acceptation, and also as regards the truth, there: *For by many examples it can be taught* etc. In the fourth and last he assigns the reason of the aforesaid solution, showing that[^3] the Holy Spirit is properly called charity, since *charity* is a common name; and this there: *Here it is to be noted, that just as the Holy Spirit in the Trinity.*

### Treatment of the questions.

For an understanding of the things said concerning the Holy Spirit, in this part two things are principally asked.

First, it is asked concerning the mode of procession of the Holy Spirit himself[^4].

Secondly, concerning his property.

Concerning the first, three things are asked.

The first is, whether in the divine [persons] it is necessary to posit a person proceeding by the mode of liberality.

The second, whether it is necessary to posit a person proceeding by the mode of love.

The third, whether[^5] it is necessary to posit a person to proceed by the mode of mutual charity.
