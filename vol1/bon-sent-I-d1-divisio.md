## *Division of the text and treatment of the questions*

---

## Latin

<!-- page 29 -->

## Commentarius in Distinctionem I

*Divisio libri Sententiarum et disputatio circa frui et uti.*

«*Veteris ac novae Legis continentiam etc.*»

### Divisio textus

In parte ista incipit tractatus libri, qui dividitur in quatuor libros partiales. Prima pars habet duas partes. In prima parte venatur Magister materiam huius libri et per consequens aliorum. In secunda exsequitur divisionem suam, quae incipit ibi distinctione secunda[^1]: «*Hoc itaque vera ac pia fide etc.*»

Item prima pars, in qua venatur materiam, habet duas[^2] partes. In prima parte venatur materiam per divisiones, et ideo primo ponit divisionem, per quam distinguuntur tres libri a quarto, scilicet per *res* et *signa*. Secundo[^3] ponit divisionem, per quam distinguitur primus liber a tribus aliis, quae est per *frui* et *uti*, ibi: «*Id ergo in rebus considerandum*»[^4].

Item, prima pars habet tres particulas. In prima proponit divisionem et auctoritate Augustini confirmat eam, quae est, quod tota doctrina theologica est de rebus, vel de signis, id est, subiectum commune theologiae in haec duo dividitur. In secunda membra divisionis explanat, ibi: «*Proprie autem hic res appellantur*», ostendens, quid sit res et quid signum secundum membra praedictae divisionis. In tertia vero particula divisionem ad propositum applicat, ibi: «*Cumque his intenderit theologorum speculatio*», dicens, quod de rebus et signis sit[^5] dicturus, primo tamen de rebus.

Item, pars illa, in qua ponit divisionem per *frui* et *uti*, per quam primus liber distinguitur ab aliis tribus, habet duas partes. In prima ponit divisionem et explanat ipsam[^6]. In secunda quae dicta sunt epilogat, ibi: «*Omnium igitur, quae dicta sunt etc.*» Prima igitur pars habet tres partes:

<!-- page 30 -->

in prima ponit divisionem in res, quibus fruendum, et res quibus utendum: in secunda ad maiorem evidentiam assignat quasdam definitiones, ibi: «*Frui autem est amore inhaerere etc.*» In tertia vero proponit et determinat quasdam dubitationes, ibi: «*Cum autem homines, qui fruuntur et utuntur etc.*»

Et in prima particula primo ponit divisionem secundum auctoritatem Augustini, secundo exemplificat, ibi: «*Illae, quibus fruendum etc.*»[^7]

«*Frui autem est amore inhaerere*». Haec secunda[^8] particula illius partis, in qua ponit divisionem in qua distinguitur primus liber ab aliis tribus, quae est de quibusdam notificationibus, quatuor habet particulas. Primo enim ponit notificationes *frui* et *uti*; secundo applicat eas ad propositum, scilicet ad res, quibus fruendum est[^9] et utendum, ibi: «*Res igitur quibus fruendum*»; tertio comparat ad alias assignationes, ibi: «*Notandum vero, quod idem Augustinus*»; quarto, quia videntur[^10] sibi contradicere, movet et determinat quandam dubitationem, ibi: «*Et attende quod videtur Augustinus dicere etc.*»

«*Cum autem homines, qui fruuntur et utuntur*». Haec tertia pars, in qua movet et tractat dubitationes quasdam, habet tres partes secundum tres dubitationes, quarum[^11] prima est, utrum homo homine debeat frui, *secunda*, utrum Deus homine fruatur, vel utatur, et hanc movet ibi: «*Sed cum Deus diligat etc.*»; tertia quaestio[^12] est, utrum virtutibus sit fruendum, vel utendum, ibi: «*Hic considerandum est, utrum virtutibus etc.*» Et in qualibet istarum trium partium primo movet quaestionem, secundo motam determinat, tertio determinationem per auctoritatem confirmat. Et particulae in suis locis sunt manifestae.

### Tractatio quaestionum

Ad evidentiam definitionum et aliorum, quae Magister ponit de his verbis *frui* et *uti*, sex quaeruntur in parte ista, quorum tria pertinent ad *uti*, tria ad *frui*.

De *uti* tria quaeruntur.

Primo quaeritur, quid sit uti per essentiam.

Secundo, utrum omni creato contingat uti.

Tertio, utrum solo bono creato sit utendum.

De *frui* similiter quaeruntur tria.

Primo, quid sit frui per essentiam.

Secundo, utrum bono increato fruendum sit.

Tertio, utrum fruendum sit ipso solo bono increato.

## English

## Commentary on Distinction I

*Division of the book of the Sentences and the discussion concerning frui and uti.*

"*The contents of the Old and New Law, etc.*"

### Division of the text

In this part begins the treatise of the book, which is divided into four partial books. The first book has two parts. In the first part the Master pursues the matter of this book and consequently of the others. In the second he carries out his division, which begins there at the second distinction[^1]: "*This therefore by true and pious faith etc.*"

Likewise, the first part, in which he pursues the matter, has two[^2] parts. In the first part he pursues the matter through divisions, and therefore he first sets forth the division by which the three books are distinguished from the fourth, namely through *things* and *signs*. Secondly[^3] he sets forth the division by which the first book is distinguished from the three others, which is through *frui* and *uti* ("to enjoy" and "to use"), there: "*Therefore in things this is to be considered*"[^4].

Likewise, the first part has three particulars. In the first he proposes the division and confirms it by the authority of Augustine — namely, that the whole of theological doctrine is about things or about signs, that is, the common subject of theology is divided into these two. In the second he explains the members of the division, there: "*Properly, however, here are called things*", showing what is a thing and what is a sign according to the members of the aforesaid division. In the third particular he applies the division to his purpose, there: "*And when the speculation of theologians has attended to these*", saying that he is[^5] going to speak of things and of signs, but first about things.

Likewise, that part in which he sets forth the division by *frui* and *uti*, through which the first book is distinguished from the three others, has two parts. In the first he sets forth the division and explains it[^6]. In the second he sums up what has been said, there: "*Of all things, therefore, that have been said etc.*" The first part, then, has three parts:

<!-- page 30 -->

in the first he sets forth the division into things which are to be enjoyed and things which are to be used; in the second, for greater clarity, he assigns certain definitions, there: "*To enjoy, however, is to cleave to with love etc.*" In the third he proposes and determines certain doubts, there: "*Since, however, men who enjoy and use etc.*"

And in the first particular he first sets forth the division according to the authority of Augustine; secondly he gives examples, there: "*Those things which are to be enjoyed etc.*"[^7]

"*To enjoy, however, is to cleave to with love.*" This second[^8] particular of that part, in which he sets forth the division by which the first book is distinguished from the three others — which is concerned with certain definitions — has four particulars. For first he sets forth the definitions of *frui* and *uti*; secondly he applies them to his purpose, namely to the things which are to be enjoyed[^9] and to be used, there: "*The things, therefore, which are to be enjoyed*"; thirdly he compares them with other formulations, there: "*It is to be noted, however, that the same Augustine*"; fourthly, because they seem[^10] to contradict one another, he raises and determines a certain doubt, there: "*And note that Augustine seems to say etc.*"

"*Since, however, men who enjoy and use*". This third part, in which he raises and treats certain doubts, has three parts according to three doubts, of which[^11] the first is whether a man ought to enjoy a man; the *second*, whether God enjoys or uses a man, and this he raises there: "*But since God loves etc.*"; the third question[^12] is whether the virtues are to be enjoyed or to be used, there: "*Here it must be considered whether the virtues etc.*" And in each of these three parts he first raises the question, secondly determines the question raised, thirdly confirms the determination by an authority. And the particulars are evident in their places.

### Treatment of the questions

For the elucidation of the definitions and of the other things which the Master sets forth concerning these words *frui* and *uti*, six [questions] are asked in this part, of which three pertain to *uti*, three to *frui*.

Concerning *uti* three are asked.

First it is asked, what is *uti* by its essence.

Second, whether the use [of something] befalls every creature.

Third, whether only a created good is to be used.

Concerning *frui* likewise three are asked.

First, what is *frui* by its essence.

Second, whether the uncreated good is to be enjoyed.

Third, whether enjoyment is to be of that uncreated good alone.

## Apparatus

> The numbered footnotes below correspond to markers in both the Latin body above and the English translation. Each entry begins with the Latin text from Quaracchi, followed by an English rendering.

[^1]: **La.** Vat., post *suam* addendo *et hoc*, transponit verba *dist. 2* ante relativum *quae*, sed contra mss. et ed. 1.

    **En.** The Vatican edition, by adding *et hoc* after *suam*, transposes the words *dist. 2* before the relative *quae*, but against the manuscripts and ed. 1.

[^2]: **La.** Vat. paulo infra, contradicentibus mss. et ed. 1, tertium membrum subdivisionis adiungendo ponit hic *tres* loco *duas*; sed falso, uti ex dicendis patebit. Error editorum Vat. forte inde provenit, quod ipsi putarent, per verba immediate sequentia *In prima parte* significari primam partem subdivisionis, cum tamen revera per illa nihil aliud significetur nisi prima pars principalis i. e. tota prima distinctio.

    **En.** The Vatican edition a little below, against the manuscripts and ed. 1, by adjoining a third member of the subdivision puts here *tres* ("three") in place of *duas* ("two"); but wrongly, as will be evident from what is to be said. The error of the Vatican editors perhaps arose from their thinking that the words immediately following, *In prima parte*, signify the first part of the subdivision, whereas in fact nothing else is signified by them except the first principal part, i.e. the whole of the first distinction.

[^3]: **La.** Auctoritate codd. et ed. 1 substituimus *secundo* pro *in secunda*, postulante hoc insuper contextu, cum *secundo* referatur ad *primo* paulo superius positum.

    **En.** On the authority of the codices and ed. 1 we have substituted *secundo* for *in secunda*, the context moreover requiring this, since *secundo* refers back to the *primo* placed a little above.

[^4]: **La.** Nullo adstipulante mss. nec favente ed. 1, Vat. adiungit hic *In tertia determinat dubitationes quasdam ex praemissis ortas, ibi: Cum autem homines, qui fruuntur*; sed falso, cum haec pars sit tertia particula subdivisionis partis primae, sicuti infra expresse habetur.

    **En.** With no manuscript supporting it nor ed. 1 favoring it, the Vatican edition adds here *In tertia determinat dubitationes quasdam ex praemissis ortas, ibi: Cum autem homines, qui fruuntur*; but wrongly, since this part is the third particular of the subdivision of the first part, as is expressly stated below.

[^5]: **La.** Contra antiquiores codd. et ed. 1 minus bene Vat. *fuerit*. Mox codd. CS cc *Iterum* pro *Item*.

    **En.** Against the more ancient codices and ed. 1, the Vatican edition less well reads *fuerit*. Soon after, codices CS, cc read *Iterum* in place of *Item*.

[^6]: **La.** Fide mss. et ed. 1 supplevimus *et explanat ipsam* et mox ex fere omnibus codd. cum ed. 1 post *Prima* addidimus *igitur*, pro quo codd. P et Q habent *iterum*.

    **En.** On the testimony of the manuscripts and ed. 1 we have supplied *et explanat ipsam*, and soon afterward, from nearly all the codices with ed. 1, after *Prima* we have added *igitur*, in place of which codices P and Q have *iterum*.

[^7]: **La.** Ita mss. cum ed. 1; in Vat. autem subdivisio, quae incipit a verbis *Et in prima particula* usque ad *Frui autem*, deest.

    **En.** Thus the manuscripts with ed. 1; in the Vatican edition, however, the subdivision which begins with the words *Et in prima particula* and runs as far as *Frui autem* is missing.

[^8]: **La.** Vat. hic interserens verbum *est*, ob variatam constructionem in fine propositionis ante *quatuor* praefixam habet particulam *et*; insuper in media propositione post *divisionem* pro *in qua ponit* *per quam*, sed contradicentibus mss. et ed. 1.

    **En.** The Vatican edition here, inserting the word *est* on account of the altered construction, prefixes the particle *et* before *quatuor* at the end of the clause; moreover in the middle of the clause, after *divisionem*, it reads *per quam* instead of *in qua ponit*, but against the manuscripts and ed. 1.

[^9]: **La.** Fide mss. et ed. 1 addimus *est*.

    **En.** On the testimony of the manuscripts and ed. 1 we add *est*.

[^10]: **La.** Ita plurimi codd. ut ACFGIKRTVXWZ etc. et ed. 1 necnon textus Magistri Sentent. contra Vat., quae habet *videretur*, et aliquos codd. ut HLOS, qui legunt *videtur*.

    **En.** Thus most of the codices, such as ACFGIKRTVXWZ etc., and ed. 1, as well as the text of the Master of the Sentences, against the Vatican edition, which has *videretur*, and against some codices such as HLOS, which read *videtur*.

[^11]: **La.** Ex antiquioribus mss. et ed. 1 supplevimus *quarum*, sicuti et paulo infra post *fruatur* substituimus *vel* pro *et*.

    **En.** From the more ancient manuscripts and ed. 1 we have supplied *quarum*, just as a little below, after *fruatur*, we have substituted *vel* for *et*.

[^12]: **La.** Praeter fidem mss. et ed. 1 omittit Vat. *quaestio*.

    **En.** Against the testimony of the manuscripts and ed. 1, the Vatican edition omits *quaestio*.
