# I Sent., d. 1 — Commentarius
## *Division of the text and treatment of the questions*

---

## Latin

<!-- page 29 -->

### DIVISIO TEXTUS.

In parte ista incipit tractatus libri, qui dividitur in quatuor libros partiales. Prima pars habet duas partes. In prima parte venatur Magister materiam huius libri et per consequens aliorum. In secunda exsequitur divisionem suam, quae incipit ibi distinctione secunda[^1]: *Hoc itaque vera ac pia fide* etc.

Item prima pars, in qua venatur materiam, habet duas[^2] partes. In prima parte venatur materiam per divisiones, et ideo primo ponit divisionem, per quam distinguuntur tres libri a quarto, scilicet per *res* et *signa*. Secundo[^3] ponit divisionem, per quam distinguitur primus liber a tribus aliis, quae est per *frui* et *uti*, ibi: *Id ergo in rebus considerandum*[^4].

Item, prima pars habet tres particulas. In prima proponit divisionem et auctoritate Augustini confirmat eam, quae est, quod tota doctrina theologica est de rebus, vel de signis, id est, subiectum commune theologiae in haec duo dividitur. In secunda membra divisionis explanat, ibi: *Proprie autem hic res appellantur*, ostendens, quid sit res et quid signum secundum membra praedictae divisionis. In tertia vero particula divisionem ad propositum applicat, ibi: *Cumque his intenderit theologorum speculatio*, dicens, quod de rebus et signis sit[^5] dicturus, primo tamen de rebus.

Item, pars illa, in qua ponit divisionem per *frui* et *uti*, per quam primus liber distinguitur ab aliis tribus, habet duas partes. In prima ponit divisionem et explanat ipsam[^6]. In secunda quae dicta sunt epilogat, ibi: *Omnium igitur, quae dicta sunt* etc. Prima igitur pars habet tres partes: in prima ponit divisionem in res, quibus fruendum, et res quibus utendum: in secunda ad maiorem evidentiam assignat quasdam definitiones, ibi: *Frui autem est amore inhaerere* etc. In tertia vero proponit et determinat quasdam dubitationes, ibi: *Cum autem homines, qui fruuntur et utuntur* etc.

Et in prima particula primo ponit divisionem secundum auctoritatem Augustini, secundo exemplificat, ibi: *Illae, quibus fruendum* etc.[^7]

*Frui autem est amore inhaerere.* Haec secunda[^8] particula illius partis, in qua ponit divisionem in qua distinguitur primus liber ab aliis tribus, quae est de quibusdam notificationibus, quatuor habet particulas. Primo enim ponit notificationes *frui* et *uti*; secundo applicat eas ad propositum, scilicet ad res, quibus fruendum est[^9] et utendum, ibi: *Res igitur quibus fruendum*; tertio comparat ad alias assignationes, ibi: *Notandum vero, quod idem Augustinus*; quarto, quia videntur[^10] sibi contradicere, movet et determinat quandam dubitationem, ibi: *Et attende quod videtur Augustinus dicere* etc.

*Cum autem homines, qui fruuntur et utuntur.* Haec tertia pars, in qua movet et tractat dubitationes quasdam, habet tres partes secundum tres dubitationes, quarum[^11] prima est, utrum homo homine debeat frui, *secunda*, utrum Deus homine fruatur, vel utatur, et hanc movet ibi: *Sed cum Deus diligat* etc.; tertia quaestio[^12] est, utrum virtutibus sit fruendum, vel utendum, ibi: *Hic considerandum est, utrum virtutibus* etc. Et in qualibet istarum trium partium primo movet quaestionem, secundo motam determinat, tertio determinationem per auctoritatem confirmat. Et particulae in suis locis sunt manifestae.

## SENTENTIARUM LIB. I.

### TRACTATIO QUAESTIONUM.

Ad evidentiam definitionum et aliorum, quae Magister ponit de his verbis *frui* et *uti*, sex quaeruntur in parte ista, quorum tria pertinent ad *uti*, tria ad *frui*.

*De uti tria quaeruntur.*

Primo quaeritur, quid sit *uti* per essentiam.

*Secundo*, utrum omni creato contingat uti.

*Tertio*, utrum solo bono creato sit utendum.

*De frui similiter quaeruntur tria.*

*Primo*, quid sit *frui* per essentiam.

*Secundo*, utrum bono increato fruendum sit.

*Tertio*, utrum fruendum sit ipso solo bono increato.

## English

<!-- page 29 -->

### DIVISION OF THE TEXT.

In this part begins the treatise of the book, which is divided into four partial books. The first part has two parts. In the first part the Master pursues the subject-matter of this book and, in consequence, of the others. In the second he carries out his division, which begins there in the second distinction[^1]: *Therefore by true and pious faith* etc.

Likewise the first part, in which he pursues the subject-matter, has two[^2] parts. In the first part he pursues the subject-matter by divisions, and so first he sets down a division, by which the three books are distinguished from the fourth, namely by *things* and *signs*. Secondly[^3] he sets down a division, by which the first book is distinguished from the three others, which is by *to enjoy* and *to use*, there: *That therefore in things is to be considered*[^4].

Likewise, the first part has three small parts. In the first he proposes the division and confirms it by the authority of Augustine, which is, that the whole of theological doctrine is about things or about signs, that is, the common subject of theology is divided into these two. In the second he explains the members of the division, there: *Properly however here things are called*, showing what is a thing and what is a sign according to the members of the aforesaid division. In the third small part he applies the division to the matter at hand, there: *And when toward these the speculation of theologians shall have been directed*, saying that he is about to speak[^5] of things and of signs, yet first of things.

Likewise, that part, in which he sets down the division by *to enjoy* and *to use*, by which the first book is distinguished from the other three, has two parts. In the first he sets down the division and explains it[^6]. In the second he sums up what has been said, there: *Therefore of all the things which have been said* etc. Therefore the first part has three parts: in the first he sets down the division into things, by which one ought to enjoy, and things by which one ought to use: in the second, for greater clarity, he assigns certain definitions, there: *To enjoy however is to cleave to with love* etc. In the third he proposes and determines certain doubts, there: *When however men, who enjoy and use* etc.

And in the first small part he first sets down the division according to the authority of Augustine, secondly he exemplifies, there: *Those, by which one ought to enjoy* etc.[^7]

*To enjoy however is to cleave to with love.* This second[^8] small part of that part, in which he sets down the division in which the first book is distinguished from the other three, which is concerning certain notifications, has four small parts. For first he sets down the notifications of *to enjoy* and *to use*; secondly he applies them to the matter at hand, namely to the things by which one ought to enjoy[^9] and to use, there: *The things therefore by which one ought to enjoy*; thirdly he compares to other designations, there: *Now it must be noted, that the same Augustine*; fourthly, since they seem[^10] to contradict each other, he raises and determines a certain doubt, there: *And note that Augustine seems to say* etc.

*When however men, who enjoy and use.* This third part, in which he raises and treats certain doubts, has three parts according to three doubts, of which[^11] the first is, whether man ought to enjoy man; *the second*, whether God enjoys or uses man, and this he raises there: *But since God loves* etc.; the third question[^12] is, whether the virtues are to be enjoyed or used, there: *Here it must be considered, whether the virtues* etc. And in each of these three parts he first raises the question, secondly he determines what has been raised, thirdly he confirms the determination by authority. And the small parts are manifest in their places.

## BOOK I OF THE SENTENCES.

### TREATMENT OF THE QUESTIONS.

For the elucidation of the definitions and of the other matters which the Master sets down concerning these words *to enjoy* and *to use*, six [questions] are inquired into in this part, of which three pertain to *to use*, three to *to enjoy*.

*Concerning to use three are inquired.*

First it is asked, what is *to use* by essence.

*Secondly*, whether to use befalls every created thing.

*Thirdly*, whether one ought to use only a created good.

*Concerning to enjoy likewise three are inquired.*

*First*, what is *to enjoy* by essence.

*Secondly*, whether the uncreated good is to be enjoyed.

*Thirdly*, whether it is to be enjoyed only the uncreated good itself.

[^1]: **La.** Vat., post *suam* addendo *et hoc*, transponit verba *dist. 2* ante relativum *quae*, sed contra mss. et ed. 1.

    **En.** The Vatican edition, by adding *and this* after *suam*, transposes the words *dist. 2* before the relative *quae*, but against the manuscripts and edition 1.

[^2]: **La.** Vat. paulo infra, contradicentibus mss. et ed. 1, tertium membrum subdivisionis adiungendo ponit hic *tres* loco *duas*; sed falso, uti ex dicendis patebit. Error editorum Vat. forte inde provenit, quod ipsi putarent, per verba immediate sequentia *In prima parte* significari primam partem subdivisionis, cum tamen revera per illa nihil aliud significetur nisi prima pars principalis i. e. tota prima distinctio.

    **En.** The Vatican edition a little below, with the manuscripts and edition 1 contradicting [it], by adjoining a third member of the subdivision, puts here *three* in place of *two*; but falsely, as will be clear from what is to be said. The error of the Vatican editors perhaps arises from this, that they thought, by the words immediately following *In the first part* the first part of the subdivision was signified, when however in truth by those words nothing else is signified except the first principal part, i.e. the whole first distinction.

[^3]: **La.** Auctoritate codd. et ed. 1 substituimus *secundo* pro *in secunda*, postulante hoc insuper contextu, cum *secundo* referatur ad *primo* paulo superius positum.

    **En.** On the authority of the codices and of edition 1 we have substituted *secundo* for *in secunda*, the context moreover demanding this, since *secundo* refers to *primo* placed a little above.

[^4]: **La.** Nullo adstipulante mss. nec favente ed. 1, Vat. adiungit hic *In tertia determinat dubitationes quasdam ex praemissis ortas, ibi: Cum autem homines, qui fruuntur*; sed falso, cum haec pars sit tertia particula subdivisionis partis primae, sicuti infra expresse habetur.

    **En.** With no manuscript supporting and edition 1 not favoring [it], the Vatican edition adjoins here *In the third he determines certain doubts arising from what has gone before, there: When however men, who enjoy*; but falsely, since this part is the third small part of the subdivision of the first part, as is expressly held below.

    **En.** The Vatican edition here inserting the word *est*, on account of the varied construction at the end of the proposition before *quatuor* has the particle *et* prefixed; moreover in the middle of the proposition after *divisionem* [it has] *per quam* in place of *in qua ponit*, but with the manuscripts and edition 1 contradicting.

    **En.** Contrary to the faith of the manuscripts and of edition 1, the Vatican edition omits *quaestio*.
