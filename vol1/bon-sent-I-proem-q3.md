## *Whether this book, or theology, is for the sake of contemplation or that we may become good — that is, whether it is a speculative or a practical science*

### QUAESTIO III.
#### *Utrum hic liber sive theologia sit contemplationis gratia, an ut boni fiamus, sive utrum sit scientia speculativa, an practica.*

Tertio quaeritur[^p12-1] de causa *finali.* Et cum dictum sit, quod liber iste est ad revelandum abscondita, quaeritur, utrum opus hoc sit contemplationis gratia, vel ut boni fiamus. Et quod sit, ut boni fiamus, videtur sic.

1. Omnis doctrina, quae est de his, sine quorum cognitione non contingit recte vivere, est, ut boni fiamus: sed iste liber est ad cognitionem verae[^p12-2] fidei, sine qua *impossibile est placere Deo* nec recte vivere, ut dicitur ad Hebraeos undecimo[^p12-3]: ergo iste liber est, ut boni fiamus.

2. Item, omnis doctrina, quae convenit cum *virtute* in obiecto, est, ut boni fiamus; haec per se manifesta est; sed haec doctrina convenit cum fide in obiecto, quia est de credibili, de quo etiam est fides sive in quo: ergo etc.

3. Item, doctrina particularis convenit cum totali in *fine;* sed finis totalis Scripturae sacrae non est tantum, ut fiamus boni, sed etiam ut fiamus beati; et beatitudo est optimum: ergo finis istius scientiae est, ut boni fiamus.

**Contra:** 1. Magister dicit in littera[^p12-4], quod finis sive intentio sua «est theologicarum inquisitionum abdita pandere»; sed hoc pertinet ad eum, cuius finis est speculatio: ergo etc.

2. Item, scientia, quae est, ut boni fiamus, pertinet ad mores; sed cum theologia sit de fide[^p12-5] et moribus, liber autem iste de his quae spectant ad fidem, non de his quae spectant ad mores: ergo opus hoc non est, ut boni fiamus.

3. Item, omnis scientia, quae est, ut boni fiamus, est practica; sed omnis talis est de his quae sunt ab opere nostro[^p12-6]; sed haec non est de his quae sunt ab opere nostro, sed a Deo: ergo est contemplationis gratia, non ut boni fiamus.

<!-- page 13 -->

### Conclusio

> *Scientia theologica est habitus affectivus et medius inter speculativum et practicum, et pro fine habet tum contemplationem, tum ut boni fiamus, et quidem principalius, ut boni fiamus.*

**Respondeo:** Ad intelligentiam praedictorum notandum est, quod perfectibile a scientia est intellectus noster.[^p13-1] Hunc autem contingit considerare tripliciter, scilicet *in se,* vel prout extenditur ad *affectum,* vel prout extenditur ad *opus.* Extenditur autem intellectus per modum dictantis et regulantis. Secundum hunc triplicem statum, quia[^p13-2] errare potest, habet triplicem *habitum directivum.* Nam si consideremus intellectum *in se,* sic est proprie speculativus et perficitur ab habitu, qui est contemplationis gratia, qui[^p13-3] dicitur *scientia speculativa.* Si autem consideremus ipsum ut natum extendi ad *opus,* sic perficitur ab habitu, qui est, ut boni fiamus; et hic est *scientia practica* sive moralis. Si autem medio modo consideretur ut natus[^p13-4] extendi ad *affectum,* sic perficitur ab habitu medio inter pure speculativum et practicum, qui complectitur utrumque; et hic habitus dicitur *sapientia,* quae simul dicit cognitionem et affectum: *Sapientia enim doctrinae est secundum nomen eius,* Ecclesiastici sexto.[^p13-5] Unde hic est contemplationis gratia, et ut boni fiamus, principaliter tamen, ut boni fiamus.

Talis est cognitio tradita in hoc libro. Nam cognitio haec iuvat fidem, et fides sic est in intellectu, ut, quantum est de sui ratione, nata sit movere affectum[^p13-6]. Et hoc patet. Nam haec cognitio, quod Christus pro nobis mortuus est, et consimiles, nisi sit homo peccator et durus, movet ad amorem; non sic ista: quod diameter est asymeter costae[^p13-7].

Concedendum ergo, quod est, ut boni fiamus.

1. Quod obiicitur, quod[^p13-8] est ad pandendum abscondita; dicendum, quod ibi non est status, quia illa revelatio ordinat ad affectum.

2. 3. Quod obiicitur, quod non est de moribus nec de his quae sunt ab opere nostro; iam patet responsio ad illud[^p13-9], quia ipse loquitur de illa quae proprie est, ut boni fiamus, ut de practica.

### Scholion

**I.** Haec quaestio communiter sic proponitur: utrum theologia sit speculativa, an practica? Attamen, stricte loquendo, inter hunc et illum loquendi modum est differentia. Nam proprie *practica* est notitia, quae per se est ordinabilis ad *operationem;* et in hoc sensu S. Doctor negat, theologiam esse practicam (ad 2. et 3.). Sed ut *boni fiamus* per directionem ad *finem ultimum* sive per inflammationem affectus, hoc inter speculativas scientias propriissime convenit theologiae.

**II.** In corp. tangitur quaestio inter scholasticos agitata, utrum theologia sit *sapientia,* quod communiter affirmatur, cfr. Alex. Hal., S. p. I. q. 1. m. 1. — Scot., I. Sent. Prolog. q. 3. — S. Thom., I. Sent. Prolog. a. 1. q. 3; S. I. q. 1. a. 6. — B. Albert., I. Sent. d. 1. a. 4. ad 1. — Richard. a Med., I. Sent. Prolog. q. 5. ad 3. — Aegid. R., I. Sent. Prolog. p. 2. q. 1. — Petr. a Tar., I. Sent. Prolog. q. 1. — Henr. Gand., S. a. 6. q. 2. — Durand., I. Sent. Prolog. q. 1. in fine.

**III.** In ipsa solutione quaestionis cum Seraphico Doctore et re et modo loquendi conveniunt Alex. Hal., B. Albert., Petr. a Tar., Aegid. R., Thom. de Argentina. S. Thom. in Commentar. in Sent. loco infra cit. simpliciter dicit: Ergo (scientia sacra) non est practica, sed speculativa; in Summa vero: Comprehendit sub se utrumque, magis vero est speculativa quam practica. Haec ultima solutio parum distat a doctrina Seraphici, quod patet sic.

<!-- page 14 -->

1. Uterque admittit praeter scientiam speculativam et practicam tertium membrum, quod simul utrumque continet. Licet Scotus (Prolog. q. 4.) de hac divisione male sentiat, tamen fere ab omnibus et rationabiliter admittitur et bene defenditur ab Aegid. Rom. loc. cit. Ita habitus theologicae scientiae, cum sit superioris ordinis, quia fundatur in principiis fidei, quae imperatur a voluntate et involvit inclinationem in Deum, videtur esse eminenter speculativus et aliquo modo practicus; habet enim virtutem et intellectum illuminandi et affectum inflammandi. Unde saltem in sensu largo haec scientia potest appellari practica, licet ex parte *obiecti* non immediate dirigat operationem, quia ex parte *finis* de sui ratione inclinat ad amorem Dei. Pro explicatione servire potest III. Sent. d. 23. a. 1. q. 2. in corp.

2. Si S. Thomas dicit, quod theologia sit magis speculativa quam practica, hoc verum est, si consideratur ex parte *obiecti;* et hanc rationem etiam S. Bonaventura satis innuit, cum in solut. ad 2. et 3. neget, quod haec scientia sit practica in sensu stricto. Si vero Seraphicus affirmat, quod sit «principaliter, ut boni fiamus», hoc iterum verissimum est, si spectatur eius *finis,* qui certe magis est, ut boni fiamus, quam ut tantum divina speculemur.

3. S. Bonaventura hic theologiam expresse non nominat scientiam *affectivam;* tamen tertium membrum divisionis, quod speculativum et practicum in se complectitur, non inepte cum B. Alberto aliisque antiquis hoc vocabulo distingui potest, attendendo finem principalem, qui est caritas in affectu. Nam a fine principali aliqua res nomen bene sortiri potest. — De duplici genere cognitionis, scil. speculationis et devotionis, cfr. III. Sent. d. 35. q. 1.

**IV.** De tota quaestione: Alex. Hal., S. p. I. q. 1. m. 1. et 2. — Scot., I. Sent. Prolog. q. 4. — S. Thom., I. Sent. Prolog. q. 1. a. 3; S. I. q. 1. a. 4. — B. Albert., S. p. I. tr. 1. q. 3. m. 3; I. Sent. d. 1. a. 4. — Petr. a Tar., I. Sent. Prolog. a. 4. — Richard. a Med., Prolog. q. 4. — Henr. Gand., S. a. 8. q. 3. et seqq. — Dionys. Carth., I. Sent. prolog. q. 2. — Durand., I. Sent. Prolog. q. 6. — Biel, I. Sent. Prolog. q. 12.

## English

### QUESTION III.
#### *Whether this book, or theology, is for the sake of contemplation, or that we may become good; that is, whether it is a speculative science or a practical one.*

Thirdly it is asked[^p12-1] about the *final* cause. And since it has been said that this book is for revealing things hidden, it is asked whether this work is for the sake of contemplation, or that we may become good. And that it is that we may become good, is shown thus.

1. Every teaching which is about those things without the knowledge of which one cannot live rightly, is that we may become good: but this book is directed to the knowledge of the true[^p12-2] faith, without which *it is impossible to please God,* nor to live rightly, as is said to the Hebrews, chapter eleven[^p12-3]: therefore this book is that we may become good.

2. Likewise, every teaching which agrees with *virtue* in its object, is that we may become good; this is manifest of itself; but this teaching agrees with faith in its object, because it is about the credible, about which faith also is, or in which it is: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, a particular teaching agrees with the total one in its *end;* but the total end of sacred Scripture is not only that we may become good, but also that we may become blessed; and beatitude is the best: therefore the end of this science is that we may become good.

**On the contrary:** 1. The Master says in the text[^p12-4] that his end or intention «is to lay open the hidden things of theological inquiries»; but this belongs to him whose end is speculation: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, a science which is that we may become good pertains to morals; but whereas theology is about faith[^p12-5] and morals, this book is nevertheless about the things that pertain to faith, not about the things that pertain to morals: therefore this work is not that we may become good.

3. Likewise, every science which is that we may become good is practical; but every such science is about the things which are from our own working[^p12-6]; but this one is not about the things which are from our own working, but from God: therefore it is for the sake of contemplation, not that we may become good.

<!-- page 13 -->

### Conclusion

> *Theological science is an affective habit and a middle one between the speculative and the practical, and it has for its end both contemplation and that we may become good — and indeed, more principally, that we may become good.*

**I respond:** For the understanding of what has been said it must be noted that what is perfectible by a science is our intellect.[^p13-1] Now this can be considered in three ways, namely *in itself,* or as it is extended to *the affections,* or as it is extended to *work.* And the intellect is extended after the manner of one dictating and regulating. According to this threefold state, because[^p13-2] it can err, it has a threefold *directive habit.* For if we consider the intellect *in itself,* it is in that way properly speculative and is perfected by a habit which is for the sake of contemplation, which[^p13-3] is called *speculative science.* But if we consider it as apt to be extended to *work,* it is in that way perfected by a habit which is that we may become good; and this is *practical* or moral *science.* But if it is considered in the middle way, as apt[^p13-4] to be extended to *the affections,* it is in that way perfected by a habit midway between the purely speculative and the practical, which embraces both; and this habit is called *wisdom,* which says at once cognition and affection: for *the wisdom of doctrine is according to her name,* Ecclesiasticus, chapter six.[^p13-5] Hence this one is for the sake of contemplation, and that we may become good — principally, however, that we may become good.

Such is the knowledge handed down in this book. For this knowledge helps faith, and faith is in the intellect in such a way that, so far as its own account goes, it is apt to move the affections[^p13-6]. And this is clear. For this knowledge, that Christ died for us, and the like, unless a man be a sinner and hardened, moves him to love; not so this one: that the diameter is asymmeter to the side[^p13-7].

It must therefore be granted that it is that we may become good.

1. As to what is objected, that it[^p13-8] is for laying open things hidden; it must be said that there is no resting-point there, because that revelation orders one to the affections.

2. 3. As to what is objected, that it is not about morals nor about the things which are from our own working; the answer to that[^p13-9] is already clear, because he is speaking of that science which is properly that we may become good, that is, of practical science.

### Scholion

**I.** This question is commonly put thus: whether theology is speculative or practical? Yet, strictly speaking, there is a difference between the one manner of speaking and the other. For properly *practical* is that knowledge which of itself is orderable to *operation;* and in this sense the holy Doctor denies that theology is practical (in the replies to 2 and 3). But that *we may become good* through direction to *the last end,* or through the enkindling of the affections — this belongs most properly to theology among the speculative sciences.

**II.** In the body of the article there is touched a question debated among the scholastics, whether theology is *wisdom,* which is commonly affirmed; see Alexander of Hales, *Summa* p. I q. 1 m. 1. — Scotus, I *Sent.* Prologue q. 3. — St. Thomas, I *Sent.* Prologue a. 1 q. 3; *Summa* I q. 1 a. 6. — Blessed Albert, I *Sent.* d. 1 a. 4 ad 1. — Richard of Middleton, I *Sent.* Prologue q. 5 ad 3. — Giles of Rome, I *Sent.* Prologue p. 2 q. 1. — Peter of Tarantasia, I *Sent.* Prologue q. 1. — Henry of Ghent, *Summa* a. 6 q. 2. — Durandus, I *Sent.* Prologue q. 1, at the end.

**III.** In the solution of the question itself both in substance and in manner of speaking there agree with the Seraphic Doctor: Alexander of Hales, Blessed Albert, Peter of Tarantasia, Giles of Rome, Thomas of Strasbourg. St. Thomas, in the *Commentary on the Sentences* at the place cited below, says simply: Therefore (sacred science) is not practical, but speculative; but in the *Summa:* It comprehends both under itself, yet it is more speculative than practical. This last solution stands little apart from the Seraphic Doctor's teaching, which is clear thus.

<!-- page 14 -->

1. Each of them admits, besides speculative and practical science, a third member which contains both at once. Although Scotus (Prologue q. 4) thinks ill of this division, nevertheless it is admitted by almost all, and reasonably, and is well defended by Giles of Rome at the place cited. Thus the habit of theological science, since it is of a higher order — because it is founded on the principles of faith, which is commanded by the will and involves an inclination toward God — seems to be eminently speculative and in some way practical; for it has the power both of illuminating the intellect and of enkindling the affections. Hence at least in a broad sense this science can be called practical, although on the side of its *object* it does not immediately direct operation, because on the side of its *end* it of its own account inclines to the love of God. For an explanation III *Sent.* d. 23 a. 1 q. 2 in the body can serve.

2. If St. Thomas says that theology is more speculative than practical, this is true if it is considered on the side of the *object;* and St. Bonaventure sufficiently hints at this reason too, when in the replies to 2 and 3 he denies that this science is practical in the strict sense. But if the Seraphic Doctor affirms that it is «principally, that we may become good», this again is most true if his *end* is regarded, which is certainly more that we may become good than that we should only speculate upon divine things.

3. St. Bonaventure does not here expressly name theology an *affective* science; nevertheless the third member of the division, which embraces in itself the speculative and the practical, can not ineptly be marked off by this word, with Blessed Albert and the other ancients, by attending to the principal end, which is charity in the affections. For from its principal end a thing can well take its name. — On the twofold kind of cognition, namely of speculation and of devotion, see III *Sent.* d. 35 q. 1.

**IV.** On the whole question: Alexander of Hales, *Summa* p. I q. 1 m. 1 and 2. — Scotus, I *Sent.* Prologue q. 4. — St. Thomas, I *Sent.* Prologue q. 1 a. 3; *Summa* I q. 1 a. 4. — Blessed Albert, *Summa* p. I tr. 1 q. 3 m. 3; I *Sent.* d. 1 a. 4. — Peter of Tarantasia, I *Sent.* Prologue a. 4. — Richard of Middleton, Prologue q. 4. — Henry of Ghent, *Summa* a. 8 q. 3 and following. — Denis the Carthusian, I *Sent.* Prologue q. 2. — Durandus, I *Sent.* Prologue q. 6. — Biel, I *Sent.* Prologue q. 12.

Et ibidem iuxta translationem arabico-latinam: Illud quod impossibile est, ut sit aliter, ut diametrum esse symetrum costae (τὴν διάμετρον εἶναι τῇ πλευρᾷ σύμμετρον.). Pro quo textu in Vat. habetur *circulus vincit diametrum,* sed obstant codd. et ed. 1.

     **En.** That is, the diameter of a square is incommensurable with its side — a saying which occurs in Aristotle's *Physics* IV, where, according to the ancient version printed at Venice in 1489 by the care of Bernardino da Trino, at text 120 (Paris edition, c. 12), it reads thus: Since neither will that which is not be in time, as whatever things cannot be otherwise, as that the diameter should be commensurable with the side. And in the same place, according to the Arabic-Latin translation: That which is impossible to be otherwise, as that the diameter should be commensurable with the side (τὴν διάμετρον εἶναι τῇ πλευρᾷ σύμμετρον.). In place of this text the Vatican edition has *circulus vincit diametrum,* but the codices and edition 1 stand against it.

[^p13-8]: **La.** Subaudi hic et in principio sequentis responsionis: liber iste, vel, scientia praesentis libri.

     **En.** Understand here, and at the beginning of the following reply: this book, or, the science of the present book.

[^p13-9]: **La.** Vat. contra mss. et sex primas edd., perturbata propositionis constructione, habet *duo ultima* pro *illud.* Immediate post plures codd. ut C F H L O Q etc. minus bene *quod* loco *quia.* — Mox post *illa* supple cum Vat. *scientia.*

     **En.** The Vatican edition, against the manuscripts and the first six editions, with the construction of the proposition disturbed, has *duo ultima* for *illud.* Immediately after, several codices, as C F H L O Q etc., less well *quod* in place of *quia.* — Presently, after *illa,* supply with the Vatican edition *scientia.*
