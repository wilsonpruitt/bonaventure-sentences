# I Sent., d. 7, a. un., q. 1
## *Utrum potentia generandi dicat aliquid absolutum vel relativum*

---

## Latin

<!-- page 135 -->

### Articulus Unicus

*De comparatione potentiae generandi ad personam.*

### Quaestio I

*Utrum potentia generandi dicat aliquid absolutum vel relativum — hoc est, utrum dicat* quid *vel* ad aliquid.

Quod dicat *quid*, ostenditur[^2]:

**1.**[^3] Per auctoritatem Magistri in littera: *Pater non est potens nisi natura; eius enim potentia natura ipsa[^1] est vel essentia*; sed natura et potentia dicunt *quid*, non *ad aliquid*: ergo et potentia generandi.

**2.** Item, ratione ostenditur sic: *generare est simile sibi producere*[^10]; sed omne tale[^4] dicit *quid*: ergo etc.

**3.** Item, *in perpetuis non differt esse et posse*[^5]: ergo multo fortius in aeternis; ergo idem est *esse* et *posse*, et potentia et essentia; sed *esse* et *essentia* in divinis dicunt *quid*, non *ad aliquid*, sive per se dicatur, sive cum adiuncto; unde essentia Patris dicit *quid*: ergo pari ratione potentia generandi.

**4.** Item, in divinis idem est secundum rem *posse scire* et *velle*[^6]; sed non est in divinis scientia propria sive *ad aliquid*, immo scientia dicit *quid* et est trium[^7] — similiter et voluntas[^8]: ergo potentia generandi dicit *quid* sive essentiam.

<!-- page 136 -->

*Sed contra:*

**1.** Magister dicit in littera, et habetur primo capitulo[^9]: *Posse generare non est posse aliquid* — ergo non dicit *quid*; et dicit *quid* vel *ad aliquid*[^13]: ergo etc.

**2.** Item, ratione ostenditur sic: potentiae distinguuntur per actus[^14]: ergo si actus potentiae dicit *quid*, et potentia; et si dicit *ad aliquid*, tunc et potentia similiter. Sed constat quod *generare* in divinis non dicit *quid*, sed *ad aliquid*: ergo nec *posse generare*.

**3.** Item, quamvis in creaturis differant virtus et operatio, tamen in Deo idem sunt, sicut vult Dionysius[^15]: ergo in Deo idem est *posse generare* et *generare*; sed *generare* in divinis dicit *ad aliquid* et non *quid*: ergo similiter *posse*.

**4.** Item, Pater generat et potest generare: aut ergo ideo potest, quia generat; aut ideo generat, quia potest. Constat quod non ideo potest generare, quia generat: ergo ideo generat, quia potest generare[^16]. Si ergo ratio proprii debet esse propria, ergo potentia generandi debet esse propria; et nihil est proprium in divinis, nisi quod dicit *ad aliquid*: ergo etc.

**5.** Item, omnis potentia activa, quae inest creaturae, inest secundum aliquas conditiones sive[^17] proprietates, ut patet. Posse enim illuminare inest luminoso per lucem, posse calefacere calido per caliditatem. Quamvis autem potentia generandi non sit activa, tamen activae similis est: ergo cum insit Patri, inest per aliquam proprietatem; illa autem proprietas aut dicit *quid*, aut *ad aliquid*. Si *quid*: ergo[^18] inest omnibus personis, et ita posse generare, quod manifeste negat Magister: ergo dicit *ad aliquid*: ergo et potentia, quae inest secundum illam: ergo etc.

Mutilatam Vat. lectionem in propositione tertia restituimus[^11]; in propositione quarta de scientia *propria* lectio Vaticana addit *vel voluntas propria*[^12].

### Conclusio

> *Inter tres de hac re opiniones videtur probabilior ea quae asserit, potentiam generandi significare tam relationem quam substantiam, sed in recto relationem.*

**Respondeo:**[^19] Dicendum, quod circa hoc problema tres fuerunt positiones.

Fuerunt quidam, qui distinxerunt potentiam generandi dicentes, quod potentia generandi potest dici potentia nuda, vel potentia disposita. Si dicatur potentia nuda, sic voluerunt dicere, quod dicit *quid* et in omnibus[^20] tribus personis est, quoniam haec potentia sic dicta consequitur naturam; unde cum natura sit in qualibet persona, similiter et potentia huiusmodi. Secundum autem quod dicit potentiam dispositam, quia non habet dispositionem nec convenientiam, ut exeat in actum generationis[^21] nisi in sola persona Patris, voluerunt dicere, quod dicit *ad aliquid*.

Sed verba istius positionis non videntur vera nec sana. Non, inquam, vera, quoniam cuius potentia est nuda, nata est pariter esse disposita; si ergo trium est potentia generandi, ut est potentia nuda, similiter et disposita; et ita quaelibet posset generare. Praeterea non videtur sana: ponere enim potentiam nudam, quae similiter se habet ad esse et non esse respectu generationis, quam omnino necessarium est esse, non videtur sane dictum esse sive proprie.

Fuerunt etiam alii, qui dixerunt, quod cum in divinis sit idem *posse* et *esse*, et *potentia* et *essentia*, quod, quemadmodum *potentia* et *essentia* dicunt *quid*, similiter *esse* et *posse*. Et quemadmodum, cum dico essentiam Patris et essentiam Filii et Spiritus sancti, sive[^22] esse Patrem, esse Filium, esse Spiritum sanctum, non dico aliam essentiam nec aliud esse, sed alterius et alterius; ita quando dico potentiam Patris, sive Patrem posse generare et Filium posse gigni, non dico aliud et aliud *posse*, sed alterius.

<!-- page 137 -->

Et huius positionis fuit Magister, sicut evidenter apparet in littera[^23]. Unde isti dicebant, quod *posse generare* dicit *quid*, sed secundum respectum ad aliquid, quia est essentiale tractum ad personam. Cum enim non sit omnimodae abstractionis, potest ad personam trahi.

Et ista positio Magistri[^24] satis est probabilis et satis bene videtur sustineri posse.

Fuerunt etiam tertii[^25] moderniores, qui dixerunt, quod potentia generandi dicit *ad aliquid* in divinis de se. Et ratio eorum est, quoniam potentia dicit habitudinem originalis principii ad principiatum. Et quoniam habitudo potest esse personae ad personam, et tunc vere est habitudo; et essentiae ad creaturam, et tunc secundum modum dicendi sive intelligendi: hinc est, quod principium de sui ratione non tantum est essentiale[^26] appropriatum per additionem, immo etiam dicit proprium personae. Pari ratione, cum potentia dicat originalem habitudinem, non tantum essentialem, non solum[^27] dicitur vel trahitur ad personam, ut dicat essentiam personaliter, immo dicit proprium, et ita *ad aliquid* de se.

Et positio ista sine praeiudicio videtur probabilior. Nam potentia generandi non videtur dicere nisi fecunditatem ad actum generationis; et illa est proprium personae, unde similiter et potentia[^28]. Nec est simile de *posse* et *esse* nisi uno modo. Hoc enim verbum *est* aliquando per se praedicatur, aliquando est *tertium adiacens*. Quando per se dicitur, tunc dicit actum absolutum, quia dicit actum entis ratione essentiae; et tunc oportet, quod dicatur absolute et quod dicat *quid*. Quando vero est *tertium adiacens*, ut cum dicitur *Pater est Deus*[^29], *Pater est Pater*, tunc hoc verbum *est* dicit habitudinem praedicati ad subiectum. Et quoniam potest dicere habitudinem ad praedicatum essentiale vel personale, ideo potest dicere esse essentiale et modum essendi personalem. *Posse* autem de sui ratione propria dicit habitudinem secundum originem; et hinc est, quod specificatur per adiunctum, ut dicat conditionem principii essentialis, vel personalis. Hanc ergo positionem sustinendo respondeo[^30] argumentis probantibus, quod dicit *quid*.

**1.** Quod enim primum obiicitur, patet: concedo[^31] enim, Magistrum fuisse huius positionis. Unde dixerunt, quod eadem potentia potest Filius gigni, qua Pater potest gignere; quod non esset, nisi potentia diceret *quid*. Unde dixit, quod potentia generandi est in Filio, sed non ad *generare*, sed ad *generari*. Haec autem positio[^32] dicit, quod est in solo Patre, quia non tantum respicit naturam, sed proprietatem personae.

**2.** Ad illud quod[^33] obiicitur, quod *generare* consequitur naturam; dicendum, quod in divinis non consequitur naturam simpliciter, sed naturam ut in persona; quia non in qualibet persona est natura fecunda, sed solum in persona Patris, et hoc est per aliquam proprietatem, quae est solius illius personae; non sic est in inferioribus, sicut iam patebit[^34]: ideo patet quod illud non valet.

**3.** Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod in aeternis est idem *esse* et *posse*; dicendum, quod verum est; sed tamen argumentum nihil[^35] valet. Quamvis enim omnino sit idem in divinis *esse* et *esse Patrem*, non tamen sequitur, quod si *esse Patrem* est proprium, quod *esse* sit proprium. Similiter nec si *esse* sit commune, quod propter hoc *esse Patrem*[^36]. Similiter dicendum in proposito.

**4. 5.** Duabus ultimis rationibus difficile est bene respondere; tamen potest quis dicere, quod rationes illae non concludunt, quod potentia dicat *ad aliquid* de se, sed solum ratione adiuncti, quod est *generare*; et sic patent cetera.

*Quatuor genera nominum in divinis:* Quaedam enim essentialia sunt dicta essentialiter, ut *deitas* et *maiestas*; quaedam essentialia personaliter dicta, ut *potentia generandi*; quaedam personalia dicta personaliter, ut *generans* et *genitus*; quaedam personalia dicta essentialiter, ut *missus*, *incarnatus* et huiusmodi, quae dicunt respectum ad personam, et ideo personalia, et effectum in creatura, et ideo essentialiter sunt dicta.

(*De hoc quadruplici genere nominum, sumpto ex Alex. Hal., loquitur S. Doctor in solutione argumenti primi sub vocabulo "Unde notandum".*)

### Scholion

**I.** In productione divinarum emanationum distinguitur *principium quod* et *principium quo*. Ipsae personae producentes sunt *principium quod*, cum actiones sint suppositorum. *Principium quo* (sive ratio, qua suppositum agit) divinarum productionum communiter asseritur esse potentiam generandi et spirandi. Aliis verbis haec quaestio sic exprimitur: utrum potentia generandi dicat *quid absolutum* (essentiam), an *relativum* sive relationem. Quaestio utraque est potius *de modo loquendi* quam *de gravi aliqua differentia in re*.

**II.** Quoad diversas opiniones: S. Thom. (S. I. q. 41. a. 5) secundae opinioni favens asserit, quod potentia generandi significat «in recto naturam divinam, sed in obliquo relationem», et cum B. Albert. (hic a. 2) affirmat, eam «esse quasi mediam inter essentiale et personale». Hanc opinionem S. Bonav. dicit esse «satis probabilem, et satis bene videtur sustineri posse», immo ipse in fine solutionis obiectorum argumenta quaedam in eius favorem affert.

## English

### Single Article

*On the comparison of the power of begetting to the person.*

### Question I

*Whether the power of begetting signifies something absolute or relative — that is, whether it signifies* something (quid) *or* in relation to something (ad aliquid).

That it signifies *something* (quid), it is shown[^2]:

**1.**[^3] By the authority of the Master in the text: *«The Father is not powerful except by nature; for His power is the very[^1] nature or essence»*; but nature and power signify *quid*, not *ad aliquid*: therefore so does the power of begetting.

**2.** Likewise, by reason: *to beget is to produce a likeness of oneself*[^10]; but every such[^4] [act] signifies *quid*: therefore, etc.

**3.** Likewise, *in everlasting things being and being-able do not differ*[^5]: therefore much more so in eternal things; therefore *to be* and *to be-able* are the same — power and essence; but *being* and *essence* in God signify *quid*, not *ad aliquid*, whether spoken of by themselves or with an addition; whence the essence of the Father signifies *quid*: therefore by the same reasoning the power of begetting.

**4.** Likewise, in God *to be able to know* and *[to be able to] will*[^6] are the same in reality; but in God there is no proper science (*ad aliquid*) — rather, science signifies *quid* and is of the three[^7] [persons]; likewise will[^8]: therefore the power of begetting signifies *quid* or essence.

*On the contrary:*

**1.** The Master says in the text, and is held in chapter one[^9]: *«To be able to beget is not to be able [to do] something»* — therefore it does not signify *quid*; and it signifies either *quid* or *ad aliquid*[^13]: therefore, etc.

**2.** Likewise, by reason: powers are distinguished by their acts[^14]: therefore if the act of a power signifies *quid*, so does the power; and if it signifies *ad aliquid*, then likewise the power. But it is established that *to beget* in God does not signify *quid* but *ad aliquid*: therefore neither does *to be able to beget*.

**3.** Likewise, although in creatures power and operation differ, yet in God they are the same — as Dionysius holds[^15]: therefore in God *to be able to beget* and *to beget* are the same; but *to beget* in God signifies *ad aliquid* and not *quid*: therefore likewise *to be able*.

**4.** Likewise, the Father generates and can generate: either, then, He can because He generates, or He generates because He can. It is clear that He cannot generate *because* He generates: therefore He generates because He can generate[^16]. If, then, the principle of what is proper must itself be proper, then the power of begetting must be proper; and nothing is proper in God except what signifies *ad aliquid*: therefore, etc.

**5.** Likewise, every active power in a creature is in it according to certain conditions or[^17] properties — as is clear. For the power of illuminating is in the luminous through *light*; the power of heating is in the hot through *heat*. Although the power of begetting is not active, yet it is similar to active power: therefore, since it is in the Father, it is in Him through some property; and that property signifies either *quid* or *ad aliquid*. If *quid*: then[^18] it is in all the persons — and so [also] *to be able to beget*, which the Master manifestly denies: therefore it signifies *ad aliquid*: therefore so does the power that is in [the Father] according to it: therefore, etc.

The Vatican edition's mutilated reading in argument 3 we have restored[^11]; in argument 4 the Vatican edition adds *or proper will* to *proper science*[^12].

### Conclusion

> *Among the three opinions on this matter, the more probable seems to be the one that asserts the power of begetting signifies both relation and substance, but relation directly.*

**I respond:**[^19] It must be said that there were three positions on this problem.

There were certain ones who distinguished the power of begetting, saying that the power of begetting can be called *bare power* or *disposed power*. If it is called bare power, they wished to say that it signifies *quid* and is in all[^20] three persons, since this power so spoken of follows nature; whence since nature is in any person, so likewise this kind of power. But insofar as it signifies disposed power, since it does not have the disposition or fittingness to issue forth into the act of begetting[^21] except in the person of the Father alone, they wished to say that it signifies *ad aliquid*.

But the words of this position seem neither true nor sound. Not true, I say, because that whose power is bare is by its nature equally apt to be disposed; if then the power of begetting belongs to the three insofar as it is bare power, so likewise insofar as disposed; and so any [person] could beget. Furthermore, it does not seem sound: for to posit a bare power, which stands equally toward being and not-being with respect to begetting (which it is altogether necessary to be), does not seem soundly or properly said.

There were also others who said that, since in God *to-be-able* and *to-be* are the same, and *power* and *essence*, that just as *power* and *essence* signify *quid*, so likewise *being* and *being-able*. And just as, when I say *the essence of the Father* and *the essence of the Son* and *of the Holy Spirit*, or[^22] *to be Father*, *to be Son*, *to be the Holy Spirit*, I do not signify another essence or another being, but [the being] of one and of another; so when I say *the power of the Father*, or *the Father can beget* and *the Son can be begotten*, I do not signify a different *being-able* and *being-able*, but [the power] of one and of another.

And of this position was the Master, as plainly appears in the text[^23]. Whence these said that *to be able to beget* signifies *quid*, but according to a respect to something, since it is an essential drawn over to the person. For since it is not of every-mode of abstraction, it can be drawn to the person.

And this position of the Master[^24] is sufficiently probable, and seems sufficiently sustainable.

There were also third[^25], more modern [thinkers], who said that the power of begetting in God signifies *ad aliquid* in itself. And their reason is that *power* signifies the relation of an originating principle to that which is originated. And since the relation can be of person to person — and then it is truly a relation — and of the essence to the creature — and then [it is so] according to the mode of speaking or understanding — hence it follows that *principle* of its own account is not only essential[^26] appropriated by an addition, but rather also signifies what is proper to a person. By the same reasoning, since *power* signifies an *original* relation, not only an *essential* one, it is not merely[^27] said or drawn to the person so as to signify the essence personally, but signifies what is proper, and so *ad aliquid* in itself.

And this position, without prejudice, seems more probable. For the power of begetting seems to signify nothing but the fecundity for the act of begetting; and that fecundity is proper to the person — whence likewise the power[^28]. Nor is it like with *being-able* and *being* except in one way. For this verb *is* is sometimes predicated by itself, and sometimes is *the third adjacent*. When it is said by itself, it then signifies an absolute act, since it signifies the act of the being by reason of the essence; and then it must be said absolutely and signify *quid*. But when it is *the third adjacent* — as when one says *the Father is God*[^29], *the Father is the Father* — then this verb *is* signifies the relation of predicate to subject. And since it can signify a relation to an essential or personal predicate, it can therefore signify essential being and a personal mode of being. *To be able*, however, by its own proper account signifies a relation according to origin; and hence it is specified by what is conjoined with it, so as to signify the condition of an essential or a personal principle. Sustaining this position, then, I respond[^30] to the arguments proving that it signifies *quid*.

**1.** As to what is first objected, it is clear: I grant[^31] indeed that the Master was of this position. Hence they said that by the same power the Son can be begotten by which the Father can beget — which would not be unless power signified *quid*. Hence he said that the power of begetting is in the Son, but not for *begetting*, but for *being begotten*. But this present position[^32] says that it is in the Father alone, since it does not regard nature only, but the property of the person.

**2.** To what is[^33] objected, that *to beget* follows nature: it must be said that in God it does not follow nature simply, but nature as in a person; for not in every person is the nature fecund, but only in the person of the Father, and this is by some property which is of that person alone; it is not so in lower things, as will soon be apparent[^34]: therefore it is clear that this argument does not hold.

**3.** To what is objected, that in eternal things *to be* and *to be able* are the same: it must be said that this is true; yet the argument has no[^35] force. For although in God *to be* and *to be the Father* are altogether the same, it does not follow that, if *to be the Father* is something proper, then *to be* is something proper. So likewise, [it does not follow that] if *to be* is common, that on this account *to be the Father*[^36] [is common]. Likewise [must be said] in the case at hand.

**4., 5.** To the last two arguments it is difficult to respond well; yet one can say that those arguments do not conclude that the power signifies *ad aliquid* in itself, but only by reason of the addition — which is *to beget*; and so the rest is clear.

*Four genera of names in God.* For some are essential names spoken essentially, as *deity* and *majesty*; some essential names spoken personally, as *power of begetting*; some personal names spoken personally, as *begetter* and *begotten*; some personal names spoken essentially, as *sent*, *incarnate*, and the like — which express a respect to a person (and so are personal) and an effect in creature (and so are spoken essentially).

(*Of this fourfold genus of names, drawn from Alexander of Hales, the Holy Doctor speaks in the solution to the first argument under the heading "Note that".*)

### Scholion

**I.** In the production of the divine emanations a distinction is made between *principium quod* (principle *which*) and *principium quo* (principle *by which*). The persons producing themselves are the *principium quod*, since actions belong to supposits. The *principium quo* (or the account by which the supposit acts) of the divine productions is commonly asserted to be the power of begetting and of spirating. In other words, this question is expressed thus: whether the power of begetting signifies *quid absolutum* (essence), or *relativum* — i.e. relation. Either question is rather *of the mode of speaking* than *of any grave difference in the matter*.

**II.** As to the diverse opinions: St. Thomas (*Summa* I, q. 41, a. 5), favoring the second opinion, asserts that the power of begetting signifies «in the direct case the divine nature, but in the oblique a relation», and with Blessed Albert (here a. 2) affirms that it «is, as it were, midway between the essential and the personal». This opinion St. Bonaventure says is «sufficiently probable, and seems sufficiently sustainable»; indeed at the end of the solution of the objections he himself brings forward certain arguments in its favor.
