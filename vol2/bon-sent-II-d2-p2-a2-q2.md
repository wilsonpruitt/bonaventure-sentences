## Latin
<!-- page 78 -->

### Quaestio II. *Utrum Angelus simul et semel sit in pluribus locis.*

Secundo quaeritur, utrum unus Angelus simul et semel sit in pluribus locis, an tantum in uno. Et quod in pluribus locis, videtur:

1. Primo per Augustinum, qui dicit, «quod Angelus ibi est, ubi vult», in libro de Civitate Dei[^1]; sed existens in caelo, vult esse in terra: ergo existens in caelo, est in terra: ergo simul est in pluribus locis.

2. Item, Damascenus[^2] dicit, «quod ibi est, ubi operatur»; sed Angelus vel est vel potest esse motor caeli, et motor operatur super totum mobile: ergo unus Angelus in utraque parte circumferentiae

<!-- page 79 -->

caeli potest esse simul et semel. Ergo si magis distat una pars circumferentiae ab alia quam caelum a terra, patet etc.

3. Item, quanto aliquid simplicius est, tanto in pluribus reperitur[^3]. Et ratio huius est, quia tanto magis recedit a coarctatione et magis appropinquat ad divinum esse, quod ubique est. Ergo si Angelus inter creaturas maxime ad Deum appropinquat, videtur, quod simul et semel in pluribus locis esse possit, sicut universale propter suam simplicitatem est ubique et semper.

4. Item, spiritus glorificatus non habet cogitationes volubiles, immo potest per donum gloriae simul multa cogitare[^4]; sed intellectus per naturam in unico instanti non aspicit nisi ad unicum intelligibile, et per gloriam simul videt multa: ergo pari ratione videtur, quod Angelus glorificatus simul possit esse in diversis locis.

5. Item, corpus nunquam potest elevari, nec per gloriam, nec per unionem, ut sit excelsius[^5] spiritu glorioso; sed corpus Christi per gratiam unionis potest esse in locis diversis: ergo pari ratione, ut videtur, et Angelus gloriosus.

**Contra:**

1. Ambrosius in libro de Spiritu sancto[^6]: «Omnis creatura certis suae naturae est circumscripta limitibus»; sed quod potest esse unum et idem totum et secundum actum ita in hoc loco, quod etiam in alio, non est circumscriptum: ergo vel Angelus non est creatura, vel, si sit, in pluribus locis simul esse non potest.

2. Item, Damascenus in libro primo[^7]: «Angelus dicitur esse in loco, quia adest intelligibiliter et circumscribitur, ubi et operatur, non enim potest secundum idem in diversis locis operari»: ergo idem quod prius.

3. Item, si Angelus simul esset in diversis locis, non indigeret mitti nec moveri; sed moventur Angeli, sicut patet per Scripturam veteris et novi Testamenti[^8]: ergo etc.

4. Item, si Angelus simul et semel esse posset in diversis locis, simul et semel posset in uno loco tacere et in alio loco loqui, in uno moveri et in alio quiescere: ergo duo opposita simul et semel essent vera de eodem.

### Conclusio

> Angelus simul et semel est tantum in uno loco.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod cum Angelus habeat esse individuatum, habet esse hic et nunc[^9]; quia vero habet esse limitatum et virtutem limitatam, habet esse in uno *hic* sive in uno loco tantum. Quoniam ergo tam individuatio quam limitatio naturaliter et inseparabiliter inest Angelo, hinc est, quod Angelum simul et semel oportet esse tantum in uno loco; et hoc dicunt auctoritates, et rationes probant. — Et ideo concedendae sunt pro eo, quod verum concludunt.

1. Ad illud quod obiicitur primo, quod ibi est, ubi vult; dicendum, quod illud non est dictum per concomitantiam simultatis, quod in eodem instanti, in quo est velle esse in quocumque loco, sit esse; sed ideo dictum est, quia esse in loco volito sequitur velle sine mora temporis magna, propter velocitatem angelicam, ut Damascenus[^10] dicit. Sequitur etiam esse in loco tali vel tali velle Angeli ad nutum, quia Angelus non vult, nisi quod competit naturae suae. Unde non vult ibi esse in instanti, sed in eo tempore, in quo scit se posse pervenire. Eodem modo intelligendum est verbum praedictum de corpore glorioso.

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod ibi est, ubi operatur; dicendum, quod operari alicubi est dupliciter: vel mediate, vel immediate; immediate, ut puta anima operatur in manu; sed mediante manu anima[^11] nautae operatur in tota navi, quia movendo manum movet temonem, et movendo temonem totam navem movet; nec tamen oportet, quod sit in tota navi, sed ubi operatur primo. — Per hunc modum

<!-- page 80 -->

intelligendum est in proposito, quod, etsi Angelus esset motor caeli vel movens, primo moveret unam partem, sicut illam, super quam esset influentia motoris, et in illa esset, non in tota re mobili vel mota. Sic etiam intelligendum est de Angelis, qui subverterunt Sodomam[^13].

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur de simplicitate, dicendum, quod esse simplex est dupliciter: aut per privationem partium componentium in esse naturae[^12], aut per privationem partium constituentium in esse definito[^14] sive in genere. Quando ergo dicitur, quod quanto aliquid simplicius, tanto in pluribus; hoc dicitur de simplicitate secundo modo, quam scilicet habet genus respectu speciei, et ideo in pluribus reperitur quam species. Cum autem dicimus Angelum simplicem, hoc est secundum aliud genus simplicitatis; ideo nihil ad propositum.

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur de pluralitate cognoscibilium, quae intelligit simul et semel; dicendum, quod non est simile duplici ratione: quia vis cognitiva magis efficitur deiformis per gloriam, quam vis motiva motu exteriori. Praeterea, aliud est, quia Deus et exemplar[^15] divinum in glorificatis est ratio cognoscendi secundum rem et essentiam; ideo intellectus glorificatus multa cognoscit, ita quod non dispergitur, sed circa unum colligitur. Sed Angelum esse in pluribus locis, hoc non potest esse per collectionem virtutis ad unum locum, sicut intellectus ad unum primum cognoscibile; et ideo non est simile.

5. Ad illud quod obiicitur de corpore Christi, dicendum, quod non est locus *a minori*, nec *a simili*, quia corpus Christi in pluribus locis est, quia multa convertuntur in ipsum. — *Et si quaeras*, quare magis congruit aliqua in ipsum converti quam in aliud corpus; ratio huius est, quia est cibus, in quo uniuntur et reficiuntur omnia membra Christi. — *Et si quaeras*, quare cibus est huiusmodi? eadem quaestio est de carbone, quare calefacit? Propter coniunctionem cum igne. Sic caro Christi repleta est delectatione, ut carbo ignitus igne, et hoc tactum est diffusius in primo libro et in quarto[^16].

### Scholion

**I.** Responsio negativa auctoris, quae communiter approbatur, intelligenda est de locis virtuti Angeli adaequatis, et de potentia naturali. De locis vero inadaequatis, qui intra sphaeram activitatis huius Angeli sunt, contrarium tenetur. Si autem respicitur ad potentiam Dei absolutam, Scotus (hic q. 7. et IV. Sent. d. 10. q. 2.) certissimum esse censet, virtute divina Angelum etiam in adaequatis locis pluribus esse posse; de quo alii dubitant.

**II.** Alex. Hal., S. p. II. q. 32. m. 3. — S. Thom., 1. Sent. d. 37. q. 3. a. 2; S. I. q. 32. a. 2. — B. Albert., S. p. I. tr. 18. q. 73. m. 2. a. 2. — Petr. a Tar., 1. Sent. d. 37. q. 4. a. 2. — Richard. a Med., 1. Sent. d. 37. a. 2. q. 3.

## English

### Question II. *Whether an Angel is at once and at the same time in several places.*

Secondly it is asked whether one Angel is at once and at the same time in several places, or only in one. And that [it is] in several places, it seems:

1. First, through Augustine, who says «that an Angel is there where it wills», in the book *On the City of God*[^1]; but, existing in heaven, it wills to be on earth: therefore, existing in heaven, it is on earth: therefore it is at once in several places.

2. Likewise, Damascene[^2] says «that it is there where it operates»; but the Angel either is or can be the mover of the heaven, and the mover operates over the whole movable: therefore one Angel in either part of the circumference

<!-- page 79 -->

of the heaven can be at once and at the same time. Therefore if one part of the circumference is more distant from another than the heaven [is] from the earth, it is plain etc.

3. Likewise, the simpler something is, the more it is found in several [things][^3]. And the reason for this is that the more it recedes from constriction and the more it approaches the divine being, which is everywhere. Therefore if the Angel among creatures most of all approaches God, it seems that it can be at once and at the same time in several places, just as the universal, on account of its simplicity, is everywhere and always.

4. Likewise, the glorified spirit does not have volatile thoughts; indeed it can by the gift of glory think many [things] at once[^4]; but the intellect by nature in one instant looks only to one intelligible, and by glory sees many at once: therefore by parity of reason it seems that the glorified Angel can at once be in diverse places.

5. Likewise, a body can never be elevated, neither by glory nor by union, so as to be higher[^5] than a glorious spirit; but the body of Christ by the grace of union can be in diverse places: therefore by parity of reason, as it seems, also the glorious Angel.

**On the contrary:**

1. Ambrose, in the book *On the Holy Spirit*[^6]: «Every creature is circumscribed by the fixed limits of its nature»; but what can be one and the same whole and according to act so in this place that [it is] also in another, is not circumscribed: therefore either the Angel is not a creature, or, if it is, it cannot be in several places at once.

2. Likewise, Damascene in the first book[^7]: «An Angel is said to be in a place because it is present intelligibly and is circumscribed where it also operates, for it cannot according to the same [thing] operate in diverse places»: therefore the same [conclusion] as before.

3. Likewise, if an Angel were at once in diverse places, it would not need to be sent nor to be moved; but the Angels are moved, as is plain through the Scripture of the Old and New Testament[^8]: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, if an Angel could be at once and at the same time in diverse places, it could at once and at the same time be silent in one place and speak in another, be moved in one and at rest in another: therefore two opposites would at once and at the same time be true of the same [thing].

### Conclusion

> An Angel is at once and at the same time only in one place.

**I respond:** It must be said that, since the Angel has an individuated being, it has being here and now[^9]; but because it has a limited being and a limited power, it has being in one *here* or in only one place. Since therefore both individuation and limitation are naturally and inseparably in the Angel, hence it is that the Angel at once and at the same time must be only in one place; and this the authorities say, and the reasons prove [it]. — And therefore they are to be granted for that which they truly conclude.

1. To what is objected first, that it is there where it wills; it must be said that this is not said by a concomitance of simultaneity, [as] that in the same instant in which there is the willing to be in any place, there is the being [there]; but it is said for this reason, that being in the willed place follows the willing without great delay of time, on account of the angelic velocity, as Damascene[^10] says. It follows also that being in such or such a place [follows] the Angel's will at [its] nod, because the Angel does not will except what befits its nature. Hence it does not will to be there in an instant, but in that time in which it knows it can arrive. In the same mode the aforesaid saying is to be understood of the glorious body.

2. To what is objected, that it is there where it operates; it must be said that to operate somewhere is twofold: either mediately or immediately; immediately, as for instance the soul operates in the hand; but by means of the hand the soul[^11] of the sailor operates in the whole ship, because by moving the hand it moves the rudder, and by moving the rudder it moves the whole ship; nor however is it necessary that it be in the whole ship, but where it operates first. — In this mode

<!-- page 80 -->

it must be understood in the matter at hand, that, even if the Angel were the mover of the heaven or [its] moving [cause], it would first move one part, like that over which the influence of the mover would be, and would be in it, not in the whole movable or moved thing. So also it must be understood of the Angels who overthrew Sodom[^13].

3. To what is objected concerning simplicity, it must be said that to be simple is twofold: either by the privation of parts composing in the being of nature[^12], or by the privation of parts constituting in defined being[^14] or in a genus. When therefore it is said that the simpler something [is], the more [it is] in several; this is said of simplicity in the second mode, namely which a genus has with respect to a species, and therefore it is found in more [things] than a species. But when we call the Angel simple, this is according to another genus of simplicity; therefore [it is] nothing to the purpose.

4. To what is objected concerning the plurality of knowables, which it understands at once and at the same time; it must be said that it is not similar, for a twofold reason: because the cognitive power is made more deiform by glory than the motive power [is] by exterior motion. Moreover, [there is] another [reason], because God and the divine exemplar[^15] in the glorified is the reason of knowing according to thing and essence; therefore the glorified intellect knows many [things], so that it is not dispersed, but is gathered around one. But that the Angel be in several places, this cannot be through a gathering of power to one place, as the intellect [is gathered] to one first knowable; and therefore it is not similar.

5. To what is objected concerning the body of Christ, it must be said that it is not an argument *from the lesser* nor *from the like*, because the body of Christ is in several places [for the reason] that many [things] are converted into it. — *And if you ask* why it is more fitting that some [things] be converted into it than into another body; the reason for this is that it is food, in which all the members of Christ are united and refreshed. — *And if you ask* why the food is of this kind? [it is] the same question as concerning a coal, why it heats? On account of [its] conjunction with fire. So the flesh of Christ is filled with delight, like a coal ignited by fire, and this has been touched on more diffusely in the first book and in the fourth[^16].

### Scholion

**I.** The negative response of the author, which is commonly approved, is to be understood of places adequate to the power of the Angel, and of natural potency. But of inadequate places, which are within the sphere of activity of this Angel, the contrary is held. But if regard is had to the absolute power of God, Scotus (here q. 7, and IV *Sent.* d. 10, q. 2) reckons it most certain that by the divine power the Angel can be even in several adequate places; about which others doubt.

**II.** Alexander of Hales, S. p. II, q. 32, m. 3. — St. Thomas, 1 *Sent.* d. 37, q. 3, a. 2; S. I, q. 32, a. 2. — Bl. Albert, S. p. I, tr. 18, q. 73, m. 2, a. 2. — Peter of Tarentaise, 1 *Sent.* d. 37, q. 4, a. 2. — Richard of Middleton, 1 *Sent.* d. 37, a. 2, q. 3.
