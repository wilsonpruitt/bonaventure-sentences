## *Distinctio II. Magistri Sententiarum (Pars I et II).*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 52 -->

### *Distinctio II.*

### *Pars I.*

De Angelica itaque natura haec primo consideranda sunt: quando creata fuerit, et ubi, et qualis facta sit, dum primum conderetur: deinde qualis effecta aversione quorundam et conversione quorundam. De excellentia quoque et ordinibus et donorum differentia, et de officiis ac nominibus aliisque pluribus aliqua dicenda sunt[^1].

### Cap. I. *De Angelis, quando facti sunt.*

Quaedam auctoritates[^2] innuere videntur, «quod ante omnem creaturam creati sunt Angeli». Primo omnium creata est sapientia, quod intelligitur de angelica natura, quae in Scriptura saepe vita, sapientia et lux dicitur. Nam sapientia illa, quae Deus est, creata non est. Filius enim, sapientia Patris, est genita, non facta nec creata; et tota Trinitas una sapientia est, quae nec facta nec creata est, nec genita vel procedens. De angelica ergo vita illud accipiendum est, de qua dicit Scriptura, quando facta est, scilicet primo omnium. — Sed rursus alia Scriptura dicit[^3]: *In principio creavit Deus caelum et terram*. Et in Propheta: *Initio tu, Domine, terram fundasti, et opera manuum tuarum sunt caeli*. Et videtur contrarietas quaedam oriri ex assertionibus istis. Nam si primo

<!-- page 53 -->

omnium creata est sapientia, omnia post ipsam facta videntur, et ita post ipsam facta videntur caelum et terra, et ipsa facta ante caelum et terram. Iterum, si in principio creavit Deus caelum et terram, nihil factum est ante caelum et terram; nec ipsa sapientia facta est ante caelum et terram. Cum igitur haec contraria videantur, nec in divina Scriptura fas sit sentire aliquid esse contrarietatis, requiramus intelligentiam veritatis. — Videtur itaque hoc esse tenendum, quod simul creata est spiritualis creatura, id est angelica, et corporalis, secundum quod potest accipi illud Salomonis[^4]: *Qui vivit in aeternum creavit omnia simul*, id est spiritualem et corporalem naturam; et ita non prius tempore creati sunt Angeli quam illa corporalis materia quatuor elementorum. Et tamen primo omnium creata est sapientia, quia etsi non tempore, praecedit tamen dignitate. Quod autem simul creata fuerit spiritualis corporalisque creatura, Augustinus super Genesim[^5] aperte ostendit dicens, per caelum et terram spiritualem corporalemque creaturam intelligi. Et haec creata sunt in principio, scilicet temporis, vel in principio, quia primo facta sunt.

### Cap. II. *Quod nihil factum est ante caelum et terram.*

Antea enim nihil factum est, nec etiam tempus factum est ante spiritualem, scilicet angelicam naturam, et ante corporalem, scilicet materiam illam quatuor elementorum confusam. Illa enim cum tempore creata sunt, nec ex tempore nec in tempore; sicut nec tempus in tempore creatum est, quia non fuit tempus[^6], antequam esset caelum et terra. Unde Augustinus in libro de Trinitate[^7] dicit, quod Deus fuit dominus, antequam esset tempus, et non in tempore coepit esse dominus, quia fuit dominus temporis, quando coepit esse tempus, nec utique tempus coepit esse in tempore, quia non erat tempus, antequam inciperet tempus.

### Cap. III. *Quod simul cum tempore et cum mundo coepit spiritualis creatura et corporalis.*

Simul ergo cum tempore facta est corporalis et spiritualis creatura, et simul cum mundo; nec fuit ante angelica creatura quam mundus, quia, ut Augustinus[^8] ait, «nulla creatura est ante saecula, sed a saeculis, cum quibus coepit». — Hieronymus tamen super Epistolam ad Titum aliud videtur sentire dicens: «Sex millia necdum nostri temporis implentur annorum, et quantas prius aeternitates, quanta tempora, quantas saeculorum origines fuisse arbitrandum est, in quibus Angeli, Throni, Dominationes ceterique Ordines servierunt Deo absque temporum vicibus atque mensuris, et Deo iubente, substiterunt»! His verbis quidam adhaerentes dixerunt[^9], cum mundo coepisse tempus saeculare, sed ante mundum exstitisse tempus aeternum sine mutabilitate, et in eo immutabiliter et intemporaliter astruunt Angelos, Deo iubente, substitisse eique servisse. — Nos autem[^10] quod prius dictum est, pro captu intelligentiae nostrae magis approbamus, salva tamen reverentia secretorum, in quibus nihil temere asserendum est; et illud Hieronymum dixisse non ita sentiendo, sed aliorum opinionem referendo, arbitramur.

### *Pars II.*

### Cap. IV. *Ubi Angeli mox creati fuerint.*

Iam est ostensum, quando creata fuerit angelica natura. Nunc autem attendendum est, ubi facta fuerit. Testimoniis quarundam auctoritatum evidenter monstratur, Angelos ante casum fuisse in caelo, et inde corruisse quosdam propter superbiam, alios vero, qui non peccaverunt, illic perstitisse. Unde Dominus in Evangelio[^11] ait: *Videbam satanam sicut fulgur de caelo cadentem*. Nec appellatur hic caelum firmamentum, quod secunda die factum est, sed caelum splendidum, quod dicitur empyreum, id est igneum a splendore, non a calore, quod statim factum Angelis est repletum, quod est supra firmamentum. Et illud empyreum quidam expositorum sacrae Scripturae nomine caeli intelligi volunt, ubi Scriptura[^12] dicit: *In principio creavit Deus caelum et terram*. «Caelum, inquit Strabus, non visibile firmamentum hic appellat, sed empyreum, id est igneum vel intellectuale, quod non ab ardore, sed a splendore dicitur, quod statim factum repletum est sanctis Angelis. Unde Iob: *Ubi eras, cum me laudarent astra matutina?* etc.» De hoc quoque Beda[^13] ita ait: «Hoc superius caelum, quod a volubilitate mundi secretum est, mox ut creatum est, sanctis Angelis repletum est, quos in principio cum caelo et terra conditos testatur Dominus dicens: *Ubi eras, cum me laudarent astra matutina, et iubilarent omnes filii Dei?* Astra matutina et filios Dei eosdem Angelos vocat. Caelum enim, in quo posita sunt luminaria, non in principio, sed secunda die factum est». Ex his liquet,

<!-- page 54 -->

quod in empyreo omnes Angeli fuerunt ante quorundam ruinam, simulque creati sunt Angeli cum caelo empyreo et cum informi materia omnium corporalium.

### Cap. V. *Quod simul creata est visibilium materia et invisibilium natura, et utraque informis.*

Simul ergo visibilium rerum materia et invisibilium natura condita est, et utraque *informis* fuit secundum aliquid, et *formata* secundum aliquid. Sicut enim corporalium materia confusa et permixta, quae secundum Graecos chaos dicta est, in illo exordio conditionis[^14] primariae et formam confusionis habuit, et non habuit formam distinctionis et discretionis, donec postea formaretur atque distinctas reciperet species; ita spiritualis et angelica natura in sua conditione secundum naturae habitum formata fuit, et tamen illam, quam postea per amorem et conversionem ad Creatorem suum acceptura erat, formam non habuit, sed erat informis sine illa. Unde Augustinus[^15], multipliciter exponens praemissa verba Genesis, per caelum dicit intelligi informem naturam spiritualis vitae, sicut in se potest existere non conversa ad Creatorem, in quo formatur; per terram, corporalem materiam sine omni qualitate, quae apparet in materia formata.

### Cap. VI. *Quomodo dixerit lucifer: Ascendam in caelum.*

Hic quaeri solet, si in caelo empyreo fuerunt Angeli, statim ubi facti sunt, quomodo, ut legitur in Isaia[^16], dicit lucifer: *Ascendam in caelum et exaltabo solium meum, et ero similis Altissimo?* — Sed ibi caelum vocat Dei celsitudinem, cui parificari volebat, et est tale: *Ascendam in caelum*, id est ad aequalitatem Dei.

## English

### *Distinction II.*

### *Part I.*

Concerning the angelic nature, then, these things must first be considered: when it was created, and where, and of what kind it was made when it was first founded; then of what kind it became by the turning-away of some and the turning-toward [God] of others. Concerning also [their] excellence and the orders and the difference of gifts, and concerning [their] offices and names and several other matters, some things must be said[^1].

### Chapter I. *On the angels: when they were made.*

Certain authorities[^2] seem to suggest «that the angels were created before all creation». First of all was created wisdom, which is understood of the angelic nature, which in Scripture is often called *life*, *wisdom*, and *light*. For that wisdom which God is, is not created. For the Son, the wisdom of the Father, is begotten, not made nor created; and the whole Trinity is one wisdom, which is neither made nor created, neither begotten nor proceeding. Of the angelic life, therefore, that [text] is to be taken which Scripture speaks of when it [says] it was made, namely *first of all*. — But again another Scripture says[^3]: *In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth*. And in the Prophet: *In the beginning, Lord, thou didst found the earth, and the heavens are the works of thy hands*. And a certain contrariety seems to arise from these assertions. For if first

<!-- page 53 -->

of all wisdom was created, all things seem to have been made after it, and thus heaven and earth seem to have been made after it, and it [seems] made before heaven and earth. Again, if in the beginning God created the heaven and the earth, nothing was made before heaven and earth; nor was wisdom itself made before heaven and earth. Since therefore these things seem contrary, and since in divine Scripture it is not lawful to suppose that there is anything of contrariety, let us seek the understanding of the truth. — It seems, therefore, that this is to be held: that the spiritual creature, that is the angelic, and the corporeal were created together, according as that [saying] of Solomon[^4] can be taken: *He that liveth for ever created all things together*, that is, the spiritual and the corporeal nature; and thus the angels were not created prior in time to that corporeal matter of the four elements. And yet first of all wisdom was created, because, although not in time, it nevertheless precedes in dignity. That the spiritual and corporeal creature were created together, Augustine on Genesis[^5] plainly shows, saying that by *heaven and earth* the spiritual and corporeal creature are understood. And these were created *in the beginning*, namely of time, or *in the beginning* because they were made first.

### Chapter II. *That nothing was made before heaven and earth.*

For before this nothing was made, nor was even time made before the spiritual — namely the angelic — nature, and before the corporeal — namely that confused matter of the four elements. For these were created with time, not from time nor in time; just as neither was time created in time, because there was no time[^6] before heaven and earth were. Whence Augustine in the book *On the Trinity*[^7] says that God was Lord before there was time, and did not begin to be Lord in time, because he was the Lord of time when time began to be; nor indeed did time begin to be in time, because there was no time before time began.

### Chapter III. *That the spiritual and the corporeal creature began together with time and with the world.*

Together therefore with time the corporeal and the spiritual creature was made, and together with the world; nor was the angelic creature before the world, because, as Augustine[^8] says, «no creature is before the ages, but from the ages, with which it began». — Jerome, however, on the Epistle to Titus seems to think otherwise, saying: «Six thousand years of our time are not yet fulfilled, and how many eternities before, how many times, how many origins of ages must we suppose there to have been, in which the Angels, Thrones, Dominations, and the rest of the Orders served God without the alternations and measures of times, and, at God's bidding, subsisted»! Certain men, adhering to these words, said[^9] that secular time began with the world, but that before the world there existed an eternal time without mutability, and they assert that in it the Angels, immutably and outside time, at God's bidding, subsisted and served him. — But we[^10] rather approve, according to the capacity of our understanding, what was said before, saving nevertheless reverence for the secret things, in which nothing is to be rashly asserted; and we judge that Jerome said that not in the sense of [his own] opinion, but reporting the opinion of others.

### *Part II.*

### Chapter IV. *Where the angels were created at first.*

It has already been shown when the angelic nature was created. Now, however, it must be attended to where it was made. By the testimonies of certain authorities it is evidently shown that the angels were in heaven before the fall, and that thence some fell through pride, while others, who did not sin, stood fast there. Whence the Lord in the Gospel[^11] says: *I saw Satan falling like lightning from heaven*. Nor is heaven here called the firmament, which was made on the second day, but the splendid heaven, which is called *empyrean*, that is, fiery from splendor, not from heat, which as soon as it was made was filled with angels, which is above the firmament. And that empyrean some of the expositors of sacred Scripture wish to be understood by the name of *heaven* where Scripture[^12] says: *In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth*. «Heaven», says Strabus, «here does not name the visible firmament, but the empyrean, that is, the fiery or intellectual [heaven], which is so called not from burning but from splendor, which as soon as it was made was filled with the holy angels. Whence Job: *Where wast thou, when the morning stars praised me?* etc.» Of this too Bede[^13] thus says: «This higher heaven, which is set apart from the revolving of the world, as soon as it was created was filled with the holy angels, whom the Lord testifies to have been founded in the beginning with heaven and earth, saying: *Where wast thou, when the morning stars praised me, and all the sons of God rejoiced?* By *the morning stars* and *the sons of God* he calls the same angels. For the heaven in which the luminaries are placed was made not in the beginning, but on the second day». From these things it is clear

<!-- page 54 -->

that in the empyrean all the angels were before the ruin of some, and that the angels were created together with the empyrean heaven and with the unformed matter of all corporeal things.

### Chapter V. *That the matter of visible things and the nature of invisible things were created together, and both unformed.*

Together therefore the matter of visible things and the nature of invisible things was founded, and each was *unformed* in some respect, and *formed* in some respect. For just as the confused and commingled matter of corporeal things — which according to the Greeks is called *chaos* — in that beginning of [its] primary founding[^14] both had the form of confusion and did not have the form of distinction and discretion, until afterward it should be formed and receive distinct species; so the spiritual and angelic nature in its founding was formed according to the habit of [its] nature, and yet it did not have that form which it was afterward, through love and conversion to its Creator, to receive, but was unformed without it. Whence Augustine[^15], expounding the foregoing words of Genesis in many ways, says that by *heaven* is understood the unformed nature of spiritual life, as it can exist in itself not [yet] converted to the Creator, in whom it is formed; by *earth*, the corporeal matter without any quality, such as appears in formed matter.

### Chapter VI. *How Lucifer said: I will ascend into heaven.*

Here it is customarily asked: if the angels were in the empyrean heaven as soon as they were made, how, as is read in Isaiah[^16], does Lucifer say: *I will ascend into heaven and exalt my throne, and I will be like the Most High?* — But there he calls *heaven* the loftiness of God, to which he wished to make himself equal; and it amounts to this: *I will ascend into heaven*, that is, to equality with God.
