## *Utrum spiritualia habeant propriam mensuram*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 55 -->

### Articulus I. *De mensura angelicae naturae in se.*
### Quaestio I. *Utrum spiritualia habeant propriam mensuram.*

Circa primum sic proceditur. Quod habeant propriam mensuram, primo videtur:

1. Per Philosophum in libro de Causis, in penultima propositione: «Inter rem, cuius substantia et operatio est in aeternitate, et rem, cuius substantia et operatio est in tempore, est res media, cuius substantia est in aeternitate et actio in tempore»; sed hoc non potest intelligi de aeternitate increata: ergo de creata; et hanc distinguit a tempore et ponit[^1] mensuram substantiarum spiritualium et incorruptibilium: ergo videtur etc.

2. Item, Ecclesiastici primo[^2]: *Omnis sapientia a Domino Deo est*, usque ibi, *ante aevum*: ergo si *ante aevum* dicit quid increatum, *aevum* dicit quid creatum; non substantiam: ergo mensuram; non rerum materialium, sed spiritualium; et haec differt a tempore, unde Boethius de Consolatione[^3]:

> *Qui tempus ab aevo*
> *Ire iubes*:

ergo etc. Omne enim quod exit ab alio, differt ab eo.

3. Item, Dionysius de Divinis Nominibus in capitulo de aevo[^4]: «Proprietas aevi est antiquum, inveteratum, secundum totum esse mensurans, temporis vero in alteratione aliter et aliter se habens»: ergo si idem non se habet sic et aliter simul et semel, non est eadem mensura tempus et aevum. Sed aevum est mensura durantium perpetuo, et talis est natura angelica: ergo.

4. Item, sicut se habet *unitas* ad distinctionem, ita *nunc* ad durationem; sed in spiritualibus non solum est reperire distinctionem secundum substantiam, verum etiam[^5] eius mensuram: ergo pari ratione et esse Angeli mensuram durationis habet; sed non tempus, cum non varietur nec mutetur: ergo aliam mensuram propriam.

5. Item, omne quod habet esse limitatum actu, habet mensuram[^6], per quam potest cognosci eius limitatio; sed tale est esse omnis creati: ergo etc.; arguatur ut prius.

6. Item, mensura durationis respicit modum durandi; sed spiritualia habent modum suae durationis differentem a corporalibus, maxime a mutabilibus, cum durent perpetualiter et immutabiliter: ergo per istam mensuram habent mensurari.

**Contra:**

1. Augustinus ad Orosium[^7]: «Deus movet spiritualem naturam per tempora, corporalem vero per loca et tempora»: ergo spiritualis naturae mensura est tempus.

2. Item, Beda[^8]: «Quatuor fuerunt primo creata: angelica natura, materia prima, caelum empyreum et tempus». Si ergo tempus stat pro mensura, ergo aut spiritualia in principio non habuerunt mensuram, aut non habuerunt aliam a corporalibus, aut Beda fuit insufficiens in illa enumeratione.

3. Item, *ratione* videtur. Unus est locus, qui est continentia visibilium et invisibilium, ut empyreum: ergo si mensura, quae est tempus, non est minoris[^9] appropriationis quam locus, ergo videtur quod unum sit tempus, in quo sint spiritualia et corporalia: non ergo habent mensuram propriam.

4. Item, sicut[^10] idem est numerus, quo mensurantur decem canes et decem homines, sic idem est numerus, quo numerantur decem Angeli et decem homines: si ergo spiritualia non habent aliam mensuram distinctionis quam corporalia, ergo nec aliam mensuram durationis.

5. Item, homo beatus et Angelus mensurantur eadem mensura, quia omnes beati; sed homo beatus habet idem *esse* cum se non beato, ergo et eandem mensuram: ergo eadem mensura mensuratur homo viator, qua Angelus, ergo et quaelibet natura corporalis: non ergo habet mensuram propriam.

<!-- page 56 -->

6. Item, *per impossibile*: si habet mensuram, aut est *substantia*, aut est *accidens*; non *substantia*: ergo *accidens*. Si *accidens*, in quo genere? Non nisi in genere *quantitatis*; non *discretae*: ergo *continuae*. Sed non est linea neque superficies, nec corpus nec locus: ergo vel est tempus, vel nihil[^11]: aut ergo angelica natura nullam habet mensuram, aut si habet, non habet propriam.

### Conclusio

> Creaturae spirituales secundum earum esse immutabile habent propriam mensuram, diversam a tempore, quae dicitur aeviternitas.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod cum quaeritur, utrum angelica natura habeat mensuram propriam, diversam a tempore, distinguendum est, quia aut loquimur de mensura spiritualium secundum *esse*, quod non mutatur, aut secundum *affectiones*. Si ergo de mensura loquimur, quae respicit *esse*, quae dicitur aevum, hoc modo consuevit ab antiquis doctoribus responderi: aut loqueris de diversitate in genere *mensurae*, aut in genere *entis*. Si in genere *mensurae*, sic oportet ponere differentiam, sicut ostendunt auctoritates et rationes ad primam partem[^12] inductae. Si vero loqueris de diversitate in genere *entis*, sic triplex fuit opinio.

Nam quidam dixerunt, quod sicut locus et superficies idem sunt per essentiam, differentia secundum comparationem, sic idem est *nunc aevi* et *temporis*, sola comparatione differens. *Nunc* enim respicit substantiam rei[^13]; substantia autem primi mobilis quantum ad *esse* omnino est immobilis: comparata autem ad *situm*, est mobilis. Et primo modo mensuratur *nunc aevi*, secundo modo *nunc temporis*, ita quod idem est *nunc*, diversimode comparatum, sicut idem ultimum, comparatum ad corpus ambiens, est superficies, comparatum ad corpus ambitum, est locus[^14]. — Sed illud non potest stare, quia circa ultimum corporis illae duae comparationes non sunt incompossibiles; sed *nunc temporis* et aevi habent proprietates incompossibiles circa idem. Nam, sicut dicit Augustinus in libro Octoginta trium Quaestionum[^15], hoc, scilicet *nunc aevi*, est stabile, illud, scilicet *nunc temporis*, est fluxibile; sed impossibile est, quod unum et idem et secundum idem simul moveatur et stet.

Et ideo est secunda positio, quod ipsum *nunc* sequitur essentiam materiae primae. Sicut igitur materia in omnibus est eadem per *essentiam*, differens per *esse*[^16]; sic *nunc* temporis et aevi idem est per essentiam, non sicut individuum, sed ad modum materiae idem per *essentiam*[^17], differens autem solum quantum ad *esse*. Et hi posuerunt, quod omnis rei quidditas mensura eadem per essentiam mensuratur. — Sed nec illud multum est intelligibile. Cum enim aevum *esse* stabile et quietum respiciat, quod quidem habet materia a forma perfecta, et haec mensura non respiciat nisi *esse actuale* et modum essendi completum; videtur, quod aevum non possit sic accipi ex parte materiae, quidquid sit de tempore. Cum ergo modus durandi *essentialiter* et *formaliter* differat hinc inde, patet etiam, quod mensura propria.

Et ideo est tertia positio, quod spiritualia habent mensuram diversam a tempore, non solum in genere *mensurae*, verum etiam in genere *entis*; et hoc non solum *comparatione*, sed etiam secundum *substantiam* et formam. Quidquid autem sit de diversitate in genere *entis*, diversitatem tamen secundum modum *mensurandi* nemo est qui negare possit. Et hoc sufficit quaestioni praesenti, qua quaeritur, utrum spiritualia habeant mensuram diversam a corporalibus. Dicendum enim, quod sic.

Quod si quaeras, quae sit illa; respondendum secundum Sanctos et philosophos[^18], quod dicitur *aeternitas creata* aut *aevum*; sed quoniam *aeternitas* proprie accipitur pro increato, et *aevum* frequenter accipitur pro tempore, ideo proprio nomine potest *aeviternitas* appellari. Sic respondendum est, si quaeratur de mensura, quae respicit ipsum *esse* spiritualis creaturae, *esse*, inquam, immutabile et perpetuum.

<!-- page 57 -->

— Si autem quaeratur de mensura angelicarum *affectionum*, utrum illa sit a tempore diversa; hoc habebit quaestionem infra[^19]. Nunc autem planum est, quod illa mensura, quae dicitur *aeviternitas*, variationem affectionum non mensurat, ut dicit Augustinus ad Orosium[^20]. *Aevum* enim solius *esse* incommutabilis est mensura.

Concedendum igitur, sicut ostendunt rationes, quod aliquo modo spiritualia habent mensuram propriam respectu temporis et temporalium.

1. Quod ergo obiicitur, quod spiritualis natura movetur per tempora; iam patet responsio: hoc enim dicit quantum ad *affectiones*, non quantum ad *esse*.

2. Quod obiicitur de quatuor primo creatis, dicendum, quod nomen *temporis* extenditur ibi ad omnis durationis mensuram, cuius est ponere principium; unde valde large accipitur et comprehendit aevum, ut melius infra patebit[^21].

3. Quod obiicitur de loco, non valet, quia *locus* corporalis non est mensura spiritualium, quamvis contineat[^22]; *aevum* autem mensurat et conformatur; ideo non valet: est unus locus non mensurans, ergo unum tempus mensurans. — Si quaeras a me, unde hoc; dicendum, quod Angelus vere habet durationem, sed non habet extensionem; et ideo habet mensuram durationis, non extensionis.

4. Quod obiicitur de numero, dicendum, quod non est simile, quia idem est modus *distinctionis* in spiritualibus et corporalibus; sed non est idem modus *durationis*. Ideo eadem est secundum speciem mensura *distinctionis*, non sic eadem mensura *durationis*.

5. Quod obiicitur de homine beato et Angelo, dicendum, quod mensura consimili mensurantur, scilicet aevo, quia consimilem habent modum durandi; sed homo viator non mensuratur consimili mensura, quia, quamvis habeat idem *esse*, habet tamen *modum* essendi et durandi alium; et mensura non tantum *esse*, sed etiam *modum* durandi respicit.

6. Quod quaeritur: in quo genere sit mensura angelicae durationis; aliqui dicunt, quod non est quantitas nisi quantitas virtutis, et ideo non est in genere quantitatis proprie[^23]. — Sed si vere mensura est, quomodo non est quantitas vera?

Ideo dicunt alii, quod est in genere quantitatis sicut principium. — Si tamen quaeratur, cuius sit principium, cum dicat mensuram diversam a tempore, ut ostensum est; difficile erit assignare[^24].

Et ideo sanius potest dici, quod sicut tempus ponitur in substantiis spiritualibus quantum ad affectiones, secundum Augustinum, licet consideratio Philosophi ad illud non conscenderit; sic et[^25] mensura ponitur differens specie ab aliis quantitatibus, licet de illa non loquatur Philosophus, quia mensuras rerum inferiorum determinare intendebat. Nec ponitur ex hoc in ipso insufficientia; et si etiam poneretur, non esset propter hoc a veritatis tramite aliquatenus discedendum[^26].

### Scholion

**I.** De multis *mensurae* speciebus copiose tractat Richard. a Med. (hic a. 2. q. 1.). Mensura, de qua hic agitur, refertur ad *existentiam actualem*. Differt autem valde *existentia* in creatis rebus corruptibilibus, in incorruptibilibus et in Deo. Hinc est etiam triplex existentiae *duratio*, quae importat permanentiam existentiae, et triplex durationis *mensura*, scil. *tempus, aevum, aeternitas.* — Patet autem, quod etiam rerum corruptibilium motibus continuo fluentibus substat aliquod *esse permanens*, licet variabile et transmutabile tum secundum existentiam, tum secundum operationem. Harum propria mensura est tempus, quod secundum Aristotelem definitur: *numerus motus secundum prius et posterius* (cfr. supra pag. 20, nota 1). — In Deo vero essentia, existentia, operatio idem sunt et invariabile; unde divinae existentiae mensura est *aeternitas*, carens initio, fine, mutabilitate, unde cum Boethio apte definitur: *interminabilis vitae tota simul et perfecta possessio* (cfr. I. Sent. d. 9. q. 3.). — *Aevum* denique, quasi medium tenens inter tempus et aeternitatem, mensurat durationem entis creati, quod quidem naturaliter quoad *esse* habet incorruptibilitatem, *initium* tamen, et quoad operationes et affectiones *variationem.* — Doctores, exceptis paucis Nominalibus, recte tenent, tum aevum realiter differre a tempore, tum rationi aevi et temporis respondere aliquid reale, sive, ut dicit S. Bonav. (hic q. 2. in corp.), est «dispositio rei extra, non fictio animae», licet actualis numeratio sit a parte animae. Hinc a Stephano Parisiensi Episc. a 1276 condemnata est thesis, «quod aevum et tempus nihil sunt in re, sed solum in apprehensione». (Collect. iudic. t. I. pag. 197 Cap. XI. Errores de mundo etc. n. 19.).

<!-- page 58 -->

**II.** Pro faciliore intelligentia plurium locorum in hac et seqq. quaest., ex Richardo a Med. (loc. cit.) notamus, quod in existentia rerum corruptibilium est triplex mensura: «una *intrinseca* existens in ipso [scil. in *esse* variabili] sicut in *subiecto*. Haec est *propria continuatio illius*, quae extenso nomine temporis *tempus* posset vocari [apud recentiores *tempus intrinsecum*], quamvis improprie, quia sic possent dici multa tempora, eo quod numerantur secundum numerationem motuum. — Alia *extrinseca et in genere*; et illa est tempus proprie dictum, quod idem est quod continuatio durationis motus, *ab anima numerata*, qua, mediante motu, cuius est passio, mensurat anima durationes motuum aliorum [*tempus extrinsecum*]. Et haec est in motu primi mobilis manifeste nobis noti, sicut in subiecto. Ad alios autem motus non comparatur sicut accidens ad subiectum, sed sicut mensura ad mensuratum. Unde illius solius motus, in quo est sicut in subiecto, est mensura *intrinseca*; aliorum autem motuum est mensura *extrinseca*. — Praedicto etiam *esse* debetur mensura *extrinseca et extra genus*, et illa Deus est sub ratione, qua est in se habens superexcellenter quidquid est perfectionis in durationibus omnium motionum». — Simili modo Richardus in *esse uniformiter se habente et stabili* sive aeviterno triplicem distinguit mensuram: «una *intrinseca*, scil. propria duratio cuiuslibet aeviterni, existens in suo esse sicut in subiecto, quae extenso nomine aevi posset vocari *aevum*, quamvis non proprie; et sic tot sunt aeva, quot aeviterna. Alia est *extrinseca et in genere*; et illa est duratio nobilissimi aeviterni, existens in *esse* illius sicut in subiecto, ad *esse* autem aliorum aeviternorum non comparatur sicut accidens ad subiectum, sed sicut mensura ad mensuratum [in hoc non consentit S. Bonav., hic q. 2.], quamvis non ita plene habeat rationem mensurae, sicut tempus respectu temporalium... *Tertia* est *extrinseca et extra genus*; et illa est ipse Deus, sub ratione qua aeternitas». Ex his facile intelligitur, quid Seraphico sit «diversitas in genere entis et in genere mensurandi» (hic in corp.).

**III.** In solutione huius quaestionis doctores communiter conveniunt; sed in determinandis seqq. et aliis quaestionibus de aevo diversae fuerunt opiniones. Ad rem Dionys. Carth. (hic q. 2.): «Multa, quae de ea materia dicuntur, certitudine carent et opinative promuntur magis quam assertive». — Alii auctores plerumque hanc praecise quaestionem non explicite tractant, excepto Richard. a Med. (hic a. 1. q. 1. et a. 2. q. 1.); plures de eadem re loquuntur in duabus seqq. qq., vel in quaest. de differentia aeternitatis, aevi et temporis, ut Alex. Hal., S. p. I. q. 12. m. 9. a. 1. — S. Thom., hic q. 1. a. 1; S. I. q. 10. a. 4. 5. — B. Albert., S. p. I. tr. 5. q. 23. m. 4. a. 1. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 2. a. 1. — Aegid. R., hic q. 1. dub. lat. 6. 7. — Henr. Gand., Quodl. 9. q. 7. — Dionys. Carth., hic q. 2. — Biel, hic q. 1.

## English

### Article I. *On the measure of the angelic nature in itself.*
### Question I. *Whether spiritual things have their own proper measure.*

Concerning the first point one proceeds thus. That they have their own proper measure seems true, first:

1. Through the Philosopher in the book *de Causis*, in the next-to-last proposition: «Between a thing whose substance and operation is in eternity, and a thing whose substance and operation is in time, there is a middle thing, whose substance is in eternity and whose action is in time»; but this cannot be understood of uncreated eternity: therefore of created [eternity]; and he distinguishes this from time and posits[^1] it as the measure of spiritual and incorruptible substances: therefore it seems so, etc.

2. Likewise, Ecclesiasticus, chapter one[^2]: *All wisdom is from the Lord God*, up to the place, *before the ages*: therefore if *before the ages* signifies something uncreated, *the age* (*aevum*) signifies something created; not a substance: therefore a measure; not of material things, but of spiritual ones; and this differs from time, whence Boethius, *On Consolation*[^3]:

> *You who bid time*
> *go forth from the age*:

therefore, etc. For everything that goes forth from another differs from it.

3. Likewise, Dionysius, *On the Divine Names*, in the chapter on the age[^4]: «It is the property of the age to be ancient, of old standing, measuring according to its whole being, but [the property] of time to hold itself otherwise and otherwise in alteration»: therefore if the same thing does not hold itself thus and otherwise at once and at the same moment, time and the age are not the same measure. But the age is the measure of things enduring perpetually, and such is the angelic nature: therefore [it has its own measure].

4. Likewise, as *unity* stands to distinction, so *now* stands to duration; but in spiritual things one finds not only distinction according to substance, but also[^5] its measure: therefore by parity of reasoning the being of an Angel also has a measure of duration; but not time, since it is not varied nor changed: therefore [it has] another, proper measure.

5. Likewise, everything that has a being limited in act has a measure[^6], by which its limitation can be known; but such is the being of everything created: therefore, etc.; let it be argued as before.

6. Likewise, the measure of duration regards the mode of enduring; but spiritual things have a mode of their duration differing from corporeal things, especially from changeable ones, since they endure perpetually and immutably: therefore they must be measured by that measure.

**On the contrary:**

1. Augustine to Orosius[^7]: «God moves the spiritual nature through times, but the corporeal nature through places and times»: therefore the measure of the spiritual nature is time.

2. Likewise, Bede[^8]: «Four things were created first: the angelic nature, prime matter, the empyrean heaven, and time». If, then, time stands for the measure, then either spiritual things in the beginning had no measure, or they had none other than corporeal things, or Bede was deficient in that enumeration.

3. Likewise, it seems so *by reason*. There is one place that is the container of visible and invisible things, namely the empyrean: therefore if the measure, which is time, is not of less[^9] appropriation than place, then it seems that there is one time in which spiritual and corporeal things exist: therefore they do not have their own proper measure.

4. Likewise, just as[^10] it is the same number by which ten dogs and ten men are measured, so it is the same number by which ten Angels and ten men are numbered: if, then, spiritual things do not have a measure of distinction other than corporeal things, then neither another measure of duration.

5. Likewise, a beatified man and an Angel are measured by the same measure, since all the blessed [are]; but a beatified man has the same *being* as himself not beatified, therefore also the same measure: therefore a wayfaring man is measured by the same measure as an Angel, therefore also any corporeal nature: therefore it does not have its own proper measure.

<!-- page 56 -->

6. Likewise, *by way of the impossible*: if it has a measure, it is either a *substance* or an *accident*; not a *substance*: therefore an *accident*. If an *accident*, in what genus? In none but the genus of *quantity*; not *discrete*: therefore *continuous*. But it is not a line nor a surface, nor a body nor place: therefore it is either time, or nothing[^11]: therefore either the angelic nature has no measure, or, if it has one, it does not have its own proper one.

### Conclusio

> Spiritual creatures, according to their immutable being, have their own proper measure, distinct from time, which is called aeviternity.

**I respond:** It must be said that when it is asked whether the angelic nature has its own proper measure, distinct from time, a distinction must be made, because either we are speaking of the measure of spiritual things according to *being*, which is not changed, or according to *affections*. If, then, we are speaking of the measure that regards *being*, which is called the age, in this mode it has been customary among the ancient doctors to reply: either you are speaking of diversity in the genus of *measure*, or in the genus of *being*. If in the genus of *measure*, then a difference must be posited, as the authorities and reasons adduced for the first part[^12] show. But if you are speaking of diversity in the genus of *being*, then there was a threefold opinion.

For some have said that, just as place and surface are the same by essence, differing according to comparison, so *the now of the age* and *of time* are the same, differing by comparison alone. For *the now* regards the substance of the thing[^13]; but the substance of the first movable, as to *being*, is wholly immobile: yet compared to position, it is mobile. And in the first mode *the now of the age* is measured, in the second mode *the now of time*, so that the *now* is the same, compared in diverse ways, just as the same outermost [surface], compared to the containing body, is a surface, [and] compared to the contained body, is place[^14]. — But that cannot stand, because around the outermost [point] of a body those two comparisons are not incompossible; whereas *the now of time* and of the age have incompossible properties about the same thing. For, as Augustine says in the book of the *Eighty-three Questions*[^15], the one, namely *the now of the age*, is stable, the other, namely *the now of time*, is flowing; but it is impossible that one and the same thing, and in the same respect, should at once be moved and stand still.

And therefore there is a second position, that the *now* itself follows the essence of prime matter. As, therefore, matter is the same in all things by *essence*, differing by *being*[^16]; so the *now* of time and of the age is the same by essence, not as an individual, but after the manner of matter the same by *essence*[^17], differing however only as to *being*. And these have held that the quiddity of every thing is measured by a measure the same by essence. — But neither is that very intelligible. For since the age regards stable and quiet *being*, which indeed matter has from a perfected form, and this measure regards only *actual being* and the completed mode of being; it seems that the age cannot be taken thus from the side of matter, whatever may be the case concerning time. Since, then, the mode of enduring differs *essentially* and *formally* on both sides, it is also clear that [there is] a proper measure.

And therefore there is a third position, that spiritual things have a measure distinct from time, not only in the genus of *measure*, but also in the genus of *being*; and this not only by *comparison*, but also according to *substance* and form. But whatever may be the case concerning diversity in the genus of *being*, the diversity according to the mode of *measuring* there is no one who can deny. And this suffices for the present question, in which it is asked whether spiritual things have a measure distinct from corporeal things. For it must be said that they do.

But if you ask what that [measure] is; it must be answered, according to the Saints and the philosophers[^18], that it is called *created eternity* or *the age*; but since *eternity* is properly taken for the uncreated, and *the age* is frequently taken for time, therefore by its proper name it can be called *aeviternity*. Thus it must be answered, if it is asked about the measure that regards the very *being* of a spiritual creature — *being*, I say, immutable and perpetual.

<!-- page 57 -->

— But if it is asked about the measure of angelic *affections*, whether that is distinct from time; this will have its question below[^19]. Now, however, it is plain that the measure called *aeviternity* does not measure the variation of affections, as Augustine says to Orosius[^20]. For the age is the measure of immutable *being* alone.

It must therefore be conceded, as the reasons show, that in some way spiritual things have their own proper measure with respect to time and temporal things.

1. As to what is objected, then, that the spiritual nature is moved through times; the response is already clear: for this is said as to *affections*, not as to *being*.

2. As to what is objected about the four things created first, it must be said that the name *time* is there extended to the measure of every duration that has a beginning; whence it is taken very broadly and includes the age, as will better appear below[^21].

3. As to what is objected about place, it does not hold, because corporeal *place* is not the measure of spiritual things, although it contains them[^22]; but the age measures and conforms; therefore it does not hold: there is one place that does not measure, therefore one time that measures. — If you ask me whence this; it must be said that an Angel truly has duration, but does not have extension; and therefore it has a measure of duration, not of extension.

4. As to what is objected about number, it must be said that it is not similar, because the mode of *distinction* is the same in spiritual and corporeal things; but the mode of *duration* is not the same. Therefore the measure of *distinction* is the same according to species, [but] the measure of *duration* is not the same in that way.

5. As to what is objected about a beatified man and an Angel, it must be said that they are measured by a like measure, namely the age, because they have a like mode of enduring; but a wayfaring man is not measured by a like measure, because, although he has the same *being*, he yet has another *mode* of being and of enduring; and the measure regards not only *being*, but also the *mode* of enduring.

6. As to what is asked, in what genus the measure of angelic duration is; some say that it is not a quantity except a quantity of power, and therefore it is not properly in the genus of quantity[^23]. — But if it is truly a measure, how is it not a true quantity?

Therefore others say that it is in the genus of quantity as a principle. — Yet if it is asked of what it is the principle, since it states a measure distinct from time, as has been shown, it will be difficult to assign[^24].

And therefore it can more soundly be said that, just as time is posited in spiritual substances as to affections, according to Augustine, although the consideration of the Philosopher did not ascend to that; so too[^25] a measure is posited differing in species from the other quantities, although the Philosopher does not speak of it, because he intended to determine the measures of lower things. Nor on this account is there posited any insufficiency in him; and even if it were posited, one would not on this account have to depart in any way from the path of truth[^26].

### Scholion

**I.** Of the many species of *measure* Richard of Middleton treats copiously (here a. 2, q. 1). The measure here treated of is referred to *actual existence*. Now *existence* differs greatly in created corruptible things, in incorruptible things, and in God. Hence there is also a threefold *duration* of existence, which implies the permanence of existence, and a threefold *measure* of duration, namely *time, the age, eternity.* — Now it is plain that even under the continuously flowing motions of corruptible things there underlies some *permanent being*, though variable and transmutable both according to existence and according to operation. The proper measure of these is time, which according to Aristotle is defined: *the number of motion according to before and after* (cf. above p. 20, note 1). — But in God essence, existence, operation are the same and invariable; whence the measure of the divine existence is *eternity*, lacking beginning, end, mutability, whence with Boethius it is aptly defined: *the whole, simultaneous and perfect possession of interminable life* (cf. I Sent., d. 9, q. 3). — *The age*, finally, as holding a kind of middle between time and eternity, measures the duration of created being, which indeed naturally has incorruptibility as to *being*, yet a *beginning*, and as to operations and affections *variation.* — The doctors, except a few Nominalists, rightly hold both that the age really differs from time, and that to the formal account of the age and of time there corresponds something real, or, as St. Bonaventure says (here q. 2, in the body), it is «a disposition of the thing outside, not a fiction of the soul», although the actual numbering is on the part of the soul. Hence by Stephen, Bishop of Paris, in the year 1276, the thesis was condemned, «that the age and time are nothing in reality, but only in apprehension». (Collect. iudic., t. I, p. 197, Cap. XI, *Errors concerning the world etc.*, n. 19.).

<!-- page 58 -->

**II.** For the easier understanding of several passages in this and the following questions, we note from Richard of Middleton (loc. cit.) that in the existence of corruptible things there is a threefold measure: «one *intrinsic*, existing in the thing itself [namely, in variable *being*] as in a *subject*. This is *the proper continuation of that thing*, which by an extended use of the name of time could be called *time* [among more recent [writers], *intrinsic time*], though improperly, because in this way many times could be spoken of, in that they are numbered according to the numbering of motions. — Another *extrinsic and in a genus*; and this is time properly so called, which is the same as the continuation of the duration of motion, *numbered by the soul*, by which, through the mediation of motion, of which it is a property, the soul measures the durations of other motions [*extrinsic time*]. And this is in the motion of the first movable, manifestly known to us, as in a subject. But to other motions it is not compared as an accident to a subject, but as a measure to the measured. Whence of that motion alone, in which it is as in a subject, it is the *intrinsic* measure; but of other motions it is the *extrinsic* measure. — To the aforesaid *being* also there is owed an *extrinsic and extra-generic* measure, and that is God, under the account by which He is in Himself possessing super-excellently whatever there is of perfection in the durations of all motions». — In a like manner Richard, in *being uniformly disposed and stable*, that is, in the aeviternal, distinguishes a threefold measure: «one *intrinsic*, namely the proper duration of each aeviternal thing, existing in its own being as in a subject, which by an extended use of the name of the age could be called *the age*, though not properly; and thus there are as many ages as there are aeviternal things. Another is *extrinsic and in a genus*; and this is the duration of the noblest aeviternal thing, existing in its *being* as in a subject, but to the *being* of other aeviternal things it is not compared as an accident to a subject, but as a measure to the measured [in this St. Bonaventure does not agree, here q. 2], though it does not so fully have the account of a measure as time [has] with respect to temporal things... *The third* is *extrinsic and extra-generic*; and this is God Himself, under the account by which [He is] eternity». From these things it is easily understood what for the Seraphic [Doctor] is «the diversity in the genus of being and in the genus of measuring» (here in the body).

**III.** In the solution of this question the doctors commonly agree; but in determining the following and other questions about the age the opinions were diverse. To the point, Dionysius the Carthusian (here q. 2): «Many things that are said about that matter lack certitude and are put forward by way of opinion rather than assertively». — Other authors for the most part do not explicitly treat this question precisely, except Richard of Middleton (here a. 1, q. 1, and a. 2, q. 1); several speak of the same matter in the two following questions, or in the question on the difference of eternity, the age, and time, as Alexander of Hales, S. p. I, q. 12, m. 9, a. 1. — St. Thomas, here q. 1, a. 1; S. I, q. 10, a. 4, 5. — Bl. Albert, S. p. I, tr. 5, q. 23, m. 4, a. 1. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 2, a. 1. — Giles of Rome, here q. 1, dub. lat. 6, 7. — Henry of Ghent, Quodl. 9, q. 7. — Dionysius the Carthusian, here q. 2. — Biel, here q. 1.
