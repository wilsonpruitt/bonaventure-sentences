## Latin
<!-- page 69 -->

### Dubia circa litteram Magistri.

### Dub. I.

In parte ista sunt dubitationes circa litteram, et quaeritur primo circa hoc quod dicit: *Et tu in principio, Domine, fundasti terram.* Videtur enim falsum, primo ex hoc, quod dicit, terram esse fundatam, quia non videtur esse *fundata*, sed potius *appensa*, secundum quod dicitur Isaiae quadragesimo[^1]: *Qui appendit tribus digitis molem terrae*; et Proverbiorum octavo: *Quando appendebat fundamenta terrae.* Item videtur falsum dicere, cum ait: *In principio terram fundasti*[^2], quia terra facta fuit tertio die: ergo non fuit fundata in principio.

Iuxta hoc quaeritur, supra quid terra fundata sit; et videtur, quod super aquas, propter illud Psalmi[^3]: *Qui fundavit terram super aquas.* Contrarium autem videtur per Scripturam, quae dicit[^4]: *Qui fundavit terram super stabilitatem suam*, et per rationem, quae dictat, aquam esse minus gravem quam terram, et ita secundum ordinem naturae non terram supra aquam, sed e converso.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod nomine *terrae*[^5] in Scriptura intelligitur *materia* corporalium, intelligitur etiam *elementum terrae*; et utroque modo accipi potest in praedicta auctoritate, et vere. Si enim *terra* accipiatur pro materia informi, sic recte dicitur: *In principio, tu Domine, terram fundasti*, id est, in principio fecisti materiam informem, quasi fundamentum omnium formarum[^6]. Quae ideo dicitur esse *fundata*, quia omnes formae sustentantur a materia, et ipsa non sustentatur ab alio nisi a Deo. Ideo autem dicitur esse fundata *in principio*, quia nihil ante ipsam creatum est. — Si autem nomine *terrae* intelligatur *elementum terrae*, adhuc verus est praedictus sermo. Terra enim *fundata* dicitur propter sui stabilitatem, secundum illud Ecclesiastae primo[^7]: *Generatio praeterit, et generatio advenit; terra autem in aeternum stat.* — Hoc autem elementum, scilicet terrae, et dicitur *fundatum* et dicitur *appensum*, et fundatum super *stabilitatem suam* et fundatum etiam super *aquas*. Fundatum dicitur, quia, sicut fundamentum est infimum inter omnes partes domus, sic terra est infimum inter omnia corpora mundi, et in centro existens tenet locum fundamenti. — Et cum virtute propria sibi divinitus data centrum appetat et in centro requiescat, fundata dicitur super *stabilitatem suam*. Et hoc dictum esse intelligendum est secundum *veritatem*. Secundum autem nostram *imaginationem* et vulgarem *opinionem*, cum aquae cooperiant inferiorem superficiem terrae respectu nostri, et ultra aquam sit aer, et ultra aerem[^8] sit ignis, dicitur terra esse fundata super *aquas*. Nos enim aestimamus, terram esse super aquam, dum consideramus aquam sub pedibus nostris. — Dicitur etiam esse *tribus digitis appensa*, pro eo quod tribus elementis intermediis distat a caeli circumferentia, et mediantibus illis videtur esse quasi appensa. — Et per hoc patet responsio ad omnia obiecta.

Nota tamen, quod aliter accipitur *principium*, secundum quod nomine terrae intelligitur *materia*, aliter secundum quod intelligitur *elementum terrae*. Secundum enim quod nomine terrae intelligitur materia, tunc dicitur esse fundata *in principio*, id est in primordio productionis rerum; secundum quod terra dicitur elementum, tunc dicitur esse fundata *in principio*, id est in primis operibus sex dierum[^9].

### Dub. II.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicitur, *quod tempus non coepit esse in tempore.* Omne enim quod coepit esse, aut coepit esse in tempore, aut in aeternitate; si igitur tempus coepit esse, et non coepit esse in aeternitate: ergo coepit esse in tempore. — Item, dicit, *quod Deus non coepit esse Dominus ex tempore.* Et videtur esse falsum, quia non fuit Dominus, antequam haberet servum; sed non habuit servum nisi ex tempore: ergo coepit esse Dominus ex tempore.

Iuxta hoc quaeritur, utrum coeperit esse Dominus. Et quod non, videtur, quia dominium est potestas coercendi subditos[^10], et hoc fuit semper in Deo. Contrarium autem videtur, quia «relativa simul sunt natura[^11]»; et servus non fuit ab aeterno: ergo nec Dominus.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod differt dicere, secundum Augustinum[^12] aliquid fieri *ex* tempore, et *in* tempore, et *cum* tempore.

<!-- page 70 -->

*Ex* enim importat ordinem, *in* importat continentiam, *cum* importat simultatem; et ideo nihil est factum *in* tempore, nec *ex* tempore, nisi quod exceditur a tempore. Quia igitur tempus et ea, quae in primordio temporis creata sunt, a tempore non exceduntur, ideo dicit, ea esse creata *cum* tempore, non *in* tempore, nec *ex* tempore. — Et ideo cum Deus dicatur dominus respectu omnis rei creatae, Deus non potuit incipere dominus esse *ex* tempore, nec *in* tempore, proprie accipiendo habitudinem eius quod est *ex* et *in*; nos autem in communi sermone large accipimus.

Quod vero quaeritur, utrum Deus inceperit esse dominus; dicendum, quod *dominus* uno modo importat *potestatem* coercendi subditos; et sic dicit praesidentiam secundum habitum et convenit Deo ab aeterno. Alio modo importat *actualem praesidentiam* respectu servi; et sic incepit esse dominus, non ratione mutationis factae in ipso, sed ratione mutationis factae in suo correlativo[^13].

## English

### Doubts on the text of the Master.

### Doubt I.

In this part there are doubts concerning the text, and it is asked first concerning that which he says: *And you in the beginning, Lord, founded the earth.* For it seems false, first from this, that he says the earth was *founded*, because it does not seem to be *founded*, but rather *suspended*, according to what is said in Isaiah, chapter forty[^1]: *Who weighs the mass of the earth with three fingers*; and Proverbs, chapter eight: *When he was suspending the foundations of the earth.* Likewise it seems false to say, when he says: *In the beginning you founded the earth*[^2], because the earth was made on the third day: therefore it was not founded in the beginning.

Next to this it is asked, upon what the earth is founded; and it seems [it is] upon the waters, on account of that [text] of the Psalm[^3]: *Who founded the earth upon the waters.* But the contrary seems [true] through Scripture, which says[^4]: *Who founded the earth upon its own stability*, and through reason, which dictates that water is less heavy than earth, and so according to the order of nature [it is] not earth upon water, but conversely.

**I respond:** It must be said that by the name *earth*[^5] in Scripture is understood the *matter* of corporeal things, and also is understood the *element earth*; and in both ways it can be taken in the aforesaid authority, and truly. For if *earth* is taken for unformed matter, then it is rightly said: *In the beginning, you Lord founded the earth*, that is, in the beginning you made unformed matter, as it were the foundation of all forms[^6]. Which is therefore said to be *founded*, because all forms are sustained by matter, and it itself is not sustained by anything else except by God. And it is said to be founded *in the beginning*, because nothing was created before it. — But if by the name *earth* is understood the *element earth*, the aforesaid statement is still true. For the earth is said [to be] *founded* on account of its stability, according to that [text] of Ecclesiastes, chapter one[^7]: *A generation passes away, and a generation comes; but the earth stands forever.* — And this element, namely of earth, is both said [to be] *founded* and said [to be] *suspended*, and founded upon *its own stability* and founded also upon *the waters*. It is said [to be] founded because, just as the foundation is the lowest among all the parts of a house, so the earth is the lowest among all the bodies of the world, and existing in the center it holds the place of a foundation. — And since by a proper power divinely given to it it seeks the center and rests in the center, it is said [to be] founded upon *its own stability*. And this is to be understood as said according to the *truth*. But according to our *imagination* and the common *opinion*, since the waters cover the lower surface of the earth with respect to us, and beyond the water there is air, and beyond the air[^8] there is fire, the earth is said [to be] founded upon *the waters*. For we estimate the earth to be upon water, while we consider the water beneath our feet. — It is also said [to be] *suspended by three fingers*, for the reason that by three intermediate elements it stands away from the circumference of the heaven, and through their mediation it seems [to be] as it were suspended. — And through this the response to all the objections is clear.

Note, however, that *beginning* is taken in one way according as by the name of earth is understood *matter*, in another way according as is understood the *element earth*. For according as by the name of earth is understood matter, then it is said to be founded *in the beginning*, that is, in the first origin of the production of things; according as the earth is called an element, then it is said to be founded *in the beginning*, that is, in the first works of the six days[^9].

### Doubt II.

Likewise it is asked concerning that which is said, *that time did not begin to be in time.* For everything that began to be either began to be in time or in eternity; if therefore time began to be, and did not begin to be in eternity: therefore it began to be in time. — Likewise, he says *that God did not begin to be Lord from time.* And it seems to be false, because he was not Lord before he had a servant; but he did not have a servant except from time: therefore he began to be Lord from time.

Next to this it is asked whether he began to be Lord. And that [he did] not, it seems, because lordship is the power of coercing subjects[^10], and this was always in God. But the contrary seems [true], because «relatives are by nature simultaneous[^11]»; and the servant was not from eternity: therefore neither [was] the Lord.

**I respond:** It must be said that it makes a difference to say, according to Augustine[^12], that something comes to be *from* time, and *in* time, and *with* time.

<!-- page 70 -->

For *from* implies order, *in* implies containment, *with* implies simultaneity; and therefore nothing is made *in* time, nor *from* time, except what is exceeded by time. Since therefore time and the things which were created in the first origin of time are not exceeded by time, therefore he says that they were created *with* time, not *in* time, nor *from* time. — And therefore since God is called lord with respect to every created thing, God could not begin to be lord *from* time, nor *in* time, taking properly the relation of that which is *from* and *in*; but we in common speech take [it] broadly.

But as to what is asked, whether God began to be lord; it must be said that *lord* in one way implies the *power* of coercing subjects; and so it states a presidency according to a habit, and belongs to God from eternity. In another way it implies *actual presidency* with respect to a servant; and so he began to be lord, not by reason of a change made in himself, but by reason of a change made in his correlative[^13].
