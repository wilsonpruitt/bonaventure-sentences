## *Utrum aequalis gratia detur omnibus parvulis in baptismo*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 115 -->

### Quaestio III. *Utrum aequalis gratia detur omnibus parvulis in baptismo.*

Tertio quaeritur, utrum aequalis gratia detur omnibus parvulis in Sacramento. Et quod sic, videtur:

1. Quia quod causa differenter imprimat effectus, hoc non est, nisi vel quia agens est voluntarium, vel quia, si non est voluntarium, tamen differenti modo se habet, vel, si eodem modo, materia recipiens se habet alio modo[^4]; sed nec Sacramentum nec virtus passionis est voluntarium agens, nec ipsum diversificatur, nec parvulus unus est magis dispositus quam alter: ergo etc.

2. Item, cum qualicumque dispositione infans nascatur et qualiacumque habeat naturalia, unus non nascitur magis peccator quam alius[^5]: ergo pari ratione in regeneratione unus non debet regenerari magis vel melius alio.

3. Item, unus non punitur magis alio[^6]: ergo nec remuneratur.

**Contra:**

1. Bonitas naturae dispositio est ad gratiam[^7]: ergo ubi melior natura, melior dispositio; sed causa uniformiter agens magis imprimit in magis disposito, ut lux in aere quam in aqua: ergo cum unus parvulus melius sit dispositus quam alius, patet etc.

2. Item, quia Deus praevidit Iacob futurum bonum, dedit illi gratiam, alteri vero, scilicet fratri, non dedit, quia permansurum vidit in malitia[^8]: ergo si parvulorum, qui baptizantur, quosdam praevidet damnandos, quosdam salvandos; videtur, quod salvandis dare debeat maiorem gratiam.

> **Conclusio.** Deus de lege communi parvulis in baptismo aequalem dat effectum.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod gratia mensuratur secundum liberalitatem Dei dantis, non secundum qualitatem suscipientis, nisi de congruo, et hoc, secundum quod suscipiens se praeparat, non secundum quod est capax. Quia ergo Deus operatur ibi secundum exigentiam Sacramenti; et ulterius, unus non est dispositus magis quam alter: ideo[^9] aequaliter omnibus parvulis dat, quantum est de lege communi. Quia tamen potestas eius non est alligata Sacramento, non est necesse, quod semper det aequaliter, sed potest facere, cui vult, privilegium speciale.

**Ad argumenta pro parte affirmativa:**

*Ad 1. 2.* Ad illud ergo quod primo obiicitur de agente diversimode, dicendum, quod illud non valet, quia Deus operatur in Sacramento ut agens voluntarium. — Similiter nec secundum valet, quia propagatio libidinosa hoc facit, quae conversa est in naturam, et ideo uniformiter[^10].

*Ad 3.* Ad illud quod obiicitur tertio de poena, dicendum, quod illud non valet, quia iustitia respicit meritum praecise, sed misericordia respicit dantis benignitatem.

**Ad argumenta pro parte negativa:**

*Ad 1.* Ad illud quod obiicitur ad oppositum, dicendum, quod bonitas naturae non disponit ad gratiam <!-- page 116 -->ut necessitas, nec etiam ut congruitas, nisi interveniat praeparatio determinata, ut sic se praeparet, sicut natura nata est capere[^1bis].

*Ad 2.* Ultimum patet, quia virtus Sacramenti non respicit futurum, sed dispositionem praesentem, Deus autem et sic et sic[^2bis].

## English
<!-- page 115 -->

### Question III. *Whether equal grace is given to all infants in baptism.*

Thirdly it is asked whether equal grace is given to all infants in the Sacrament. And that it is so, seems [to follow]:

1. Because that a cause should imprint its effects differently is not the case unless either because the agent is voluntary, or because, if it is not voluntary, it nevertheless is disposed in a different manner, or, if in the same manner, the matter receiving is disposed in another way[^4]; but neither the Sacrament nor the power of the Passion is a voluntary agent, nor is it itself diversified, nor is one infant more disposed than another: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, with whatever disposition an infant is born and whatever natural endowments it may have, one is not born more a sinner than another[^5]: therefore by parity of reasoning in regeneration one ought not to be regenerated more or better than another.

3. Likewise, one is not punished more than another[^6]: therefore neither is one rewarded [more].

**On the contrary:**

1. The goodness of nature is a disposition toward grace[^7]: therefore where the nature is better, the disposition is better; but a cause acting uniformly imprints more on what is more disposed, as light in air rather than in water: therefore since one infant is better disposed than another, it is evident etc.

2. Likewise, because God foresaw that Jacob would be good in the future, he gave him grace, but to the other, namely his brother, he did not give it, because he saw that he would remain in malice[^8]: therefore if of the infants who are baptized he foresees some to be damned and some to be saved, it seems that he ought to give greater grace to those who are to be saved.

> **Conclusion.** God by common law gives an equal effect to infants in baptism.

**I respond:** It must be said that grace is measured according to the liberality of God who gives, not according to the quality of the one who receives, except by congruity, and this insofar as the one receiving prepares himself, not insofar as he is capable. Therefore, because God works there according to the requirement of the Sacrament, and further, one is not disposed more than another: therefore[^9] he gives equally to all infants, as far as pertains to the common law. Yet because his power is not bound to the Sacrament, it is not necessary that he always give equally, but he can grant to whom he wills a special privilege.

**To the arguments for the affirmative side:**

*To 1 and 2.* To that which is first objected, concerning an agent [acting] in diverse ways, it must be said that it does not hold, because God works in the Sacrament as a voluntary agent. — Likewise neither does the second hold, because lustful propagation brings this about, which has been turned into nature, and therefore [acts] uniformly[^10].

*To 3.* To that which is objected thirdly, concerning punishment, it must be said that it does not hold, because justice regards merit precisely, but mercy regards the benignity of the giver.

**To the arguments for the negative side:**

*To 1.* To that which is objected for the opposite [conclusion], it must be said that the goodness of nature does not dispose toward grace <!-- page 116 -->as a necessity, nor even as a congruity, unless a determinate preparation intervene, such that one prepare oneself as nature is born fit to receive[^1bis].

*To 2.* The last is evident, because the power of the Sacrament does not regard the future but the present disposition, while God [regards] both this way and that[^2bis].
