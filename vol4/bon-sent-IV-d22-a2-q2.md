# IV Sent., d. 22, a. 2, q. 2
## *De Sacramento poenitentiae quoad significationem.*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 580 -->

### Articulus II. *De poenitentia in ratione Sacramenti.*
### Quaestio II. *De Sacramento poenitentiae quoad significationem.*

Secundo quaeritur de poenitentia quantum ad significationem; et quaeritur, quid sit hic *signum*, et quid *signatum*. Et videtur, quod poenitentia exterior significat interiorem, sic.

1. Sacramentum illud *significat ex institutione*, quod *repraesentat naturaliter*; sed poenitentia exterior naturaliter repraesentat interiorem: ergo illud significat ex institutione.

2. Item, sicut se habet baptismus corporalis ad spiritualem, sic poenitentia exterior ad interiorem; sed baptismus fluminis significat baptismum flaminis[^1]: ergo et poenitentia exterior interiorem.

3. Item, poenitentia-Sacramentum liberat a naufragio[^2]; sed impossibile est, peccantem actualiter a naufragio liberari nisi per poenitentiam interiorem: ergo poenitentia-Sacramentum in se continet eam, sed non in ratione signi: ergo in ratione signati.

4. Item, «poenitentia perfecta ad baptismi puritatem conatur reducere[^3]»; sed peccator nunquam reducitur ad puritatem illam nisi per poenitudinem cordis: ergo poenitentia exterior interiorem significat.

**Contra:**
1. Hugo[^4]: «Sacramentum est materiale elementum foris sensibiliter propositum, ex similitudine repraesentans»; sed exterior poenitentia non est talis, quia non est elementum: ergo non est Sacramentum nec signum.

2. Item, interior poenitentia est virtus, vel actus virtutis[^5]; sed gratiae virtutum et Sacramentorum differentes sunt: ergo interior poenitentia non est res alicuius Sacramenti.

3. Item, quandocumque recte ordinantur signum et signatum in Sacramento, signum est causa signati, per definitionem Sacramenti superius[^6] habitam; sed poenitentia exterior non est causa interioris, nec illa effectus, immo e converso: ergo etc.

4. Item, cum in perfectione poenitentiae tria sint, scilicet contritio, confessio et satisfactio[^7]; quaeritur: ratione cuius dicitur exterior, et ratione cuius dicitur interior? Si dicitur interior ratione contritionis, et exterior ratione confessionis et satisfactionis; *contra*: ista non significant contritionem, sed potius voluntatem confitendi et satisfaciendi. Si dicitur exterior ratione trium; tunc ergo, cum sint tria diversa, erunt tria Sacramenta. *Si dicas*, quod uniuntur; quaero: quo genere unitatis uniuntur?

### Conclusio

> *Poenitentiae Sacramenti* res *est peccati remissio perfecta;* signum *vero est humiliatio exterior secundum ecclesiasticam formam; res vero et signum est interior poenitudo.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod sicut in iudicando de institutione oportet iudicium variari de poenitentia, <!-- page 581 --> secundum quod est Sacramentum reconcilians *Deo*, et secundum quod reconciliat non solum *Deo*, sed etiam *Ecclesiae*[^8]; sic et in proposito. Secundum namque quod est Sacramentum reconcilians *Deo*, pro *re* habet peccati remissionem; pro *signo* habet exteriorem humiliationem sive in habitu, sive in verbo; pro *re et signo* interiorem poenitudinem. Nec oportet, quod primum[^9] sit causa medii, sed potius e converso, et hoc, quia est ex naturali dictamine; et ideo *procedit* exterius ab interiori, non exterius *facit* id quod interius est. — Sed in quantum est Sacramentum *Ecclesiae*, institutionem habens, sic habet rationem causae et signi, sicut alia Sacramenta[^10]; et ratio *causalitatis* residet penes id quod *formale* est, ratio vero *signi* penes id quod *materiale* est. *Materiale* autem in hoc Sacramento est humiliatio poenitentis sive quantum ad *actum contritionis*, sive quantum ad *verbum accusationis*, sive quantum ad *poenam satisfactionis*; *formale* vero est *absolutio sacerdotis*. Haec duo simul iuncta significant *poenitentiam interiorem*, secundum quod per ipsam est remissio culpae perfecta quantum ad *poenam* et quantum ad *culpam*; et quantum ad remissionem peccati quantum ad *culpam* habet rationem *signi* exterius Sacramentum, quantum ad remissionem *poenae* habet quodam modo rationem *causae*[^11].

Et sic patet, quid in hoc Sacramento sit *res*, scilicet peccati remissio perfecta; et quid *signum*, scilicet exterior poenitentia secundum ecclesiasticam formam, in qua est humiliatio et absolutio; *res et signum* est interior poenitudo[^12]. — Unde concedendae sunt rationes ad primam partem.

1. Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur, quod non est elementum; dicendum, quod Hugo valde large sumit *elementum* ad omne illud quod exterius apparet sensibus. — Vel si approprietur ad *corporalia* Sacramenta, tunc non intelligitur nisi de Sacramentis propriis novae legis.

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod gratiae virtutum differunt a gratia Sacramentorum; dicendum, quod verum est aliquo modo; sed non oportet quantum ad *essentiam*, sed sufficit quantum ad *effectum*, ut supra determinatum est in principio[^13]; et sic est in proposito. Nam poenitentia interior est *res* exterioris, secundum quod ultra rectitudinem virtutis addit ulteriorem effectum, scilicet remissionis perfectae quantum ad *culpam et poenam*, quando exterior est perfecta; et est simile in baptismo, ut visum est supra[^14].

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod interior est causa exterioris; dicendum, quod quia hoc Sacramentum partim est *naturalis dictaminis*, partim *institutionis*; ideo signum exterius quodam modo *causatur*, et quodam modo *causat*. Nam detestatio interior, in quantum detestatio est, bene facit exterius hominem humiliari; sed secundum quod absolvit in integrum a culpa et poena, hoc habet ab exteriori absolutione et poenitentia. Et sic patet, quod convenientissime est ordinatio in *significando* et *causando*, secundum quod ei competit.

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur, ratione cuius illorum trium est *signum*; dico, quod ratione *omnium*; sumitur enim *contritio* pro exteriori gemitu. Nec tamen tria sunt Sacramenta, sed illa tria ut unum perfectum signum significant poenitentiam perfectam. Et hoc patet: quia, si vere poenitet corde, ergo dolet, et hoc significatur per *gemitum contritionis*; ergo detestatur culpam et se redarguit, et hoc significatur per *verbum confessionis*; ergo habet voluntatem emendandi, et hoc significatur per *poenam satisfactionis*. Haec tria continet poenitentia interior, quae est una et vera virtus[^15]; et ideo omnia signa exteriora in unum perfectum signum concurrunt et sunt unum Sacramentum et habent *unitatem Sacramenti*. — Et est modus specialis unitatis, cum multi modi assignentur a diversis[^16]. Et *si tu obiicias*, quod non sunt simul; dicendum, quod saltem quantum ad *propositum* sive voluntatis votum unum manet in alio, ut qui conteritur vere proponit confiteri et satisfacere; aliter non est contritio vera. Sic intelligendum est de aliis[^17].

## English

### Article II. *On penance in its character as a Sacrament.*
### Question II. *On the Sacrament of penance with respect to its signification.*

Secondly it is asked concerning penance with respect to its signification; and it is asked what here is the *sign*, and what the *thing signified*. And it seems that exterior penance signifies the interior, thus.

1. That Sacrament *signifies by institution* which *represents naturally*; but exterior penance naturally represents the interior: therefore it signifies by institution.

2. Likewise, as bodily baptism stands to the spiritual, so exterior penance to the interior; but the baptism of water signifies the baptism of the Spirit[^1]: therefore exterior penance too [signifies] the interior.

3. Likewise, penance-the-Sacrament frees from shipwreck[^2]; but it is impossible for one actually sinning to be freed from shipwreck except through interior penance: therefore penance-the-Sacrament contains it in itself, yet not in the character of a sign: therefore in the character of a thing signified.

4. Likewise, «perfect penance strives to bring [the sinner] back to the purity of baptism[^3]»; but a sinner is never brought back to that purity except through sorrow of heart: therefore exterior penance signifies the interior.

**Contra:**
1. Hugh[^4]: «A Sacrament is a material element set forth outwardly to the senses, representing by likeness»; but exterior penance is not such, because it is not an element: therefore it is neither a Sacrament nor a sign.

2. Likewise, interior penance is a virtue, or an act of a virtue[^5]; but the grace of the virtues and [the grace] of the Sacraments are different: therefore interior penance is not the *thing* (res) of any Sacrament.

3. Likewise, whenever a sign and a thing signified are rightly ordered in a Sacrament, the sign is the *cause* of the thing signified, by the definition of a Sacrament set forth above[^6]; but exterior penance is not the cause of the interior, nor is the latter its effect, but rather the reverse: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, since in the perfection of penance there are three, namely contrition, confession, and satisfaction[^7], it is asked: by reason of which is it called exterior, and by reason of which interior? If it is called interior by reason of contrition, and exterior by reason of confession and satisfaction; *on the contrary*: these do not signify contrition, but rather the will to confess and to make satisfaction. If it is called exterior by reason of the three; then, since they are three diverse things, there will be three Sacraments. *If you say* that they are united; I ask: by what kind of unity are they united?

### Conclusion

> *The* thing *(res) of the Sacrament of penance is the perfect remission of sin; the* sign, *however, is exterior humiliation according to the ecclesiastical form; while the* thing-and-sign *is interior penitence.*

**I respond:** It must be said that, just as in judging concerning institution the judgment about penance must be varied, <!-- page 581 --> according as it is a Sacrament reconciling to *God*, and according as it reconciles not only to *God* but also to the *Church*[^8]; so also in the matter at hand. For according as it is a Sacrament reconciling to *God*, for its *thing* (res) it has the remission of sin; for its *sign* it has exterior humiliation, whether in bearing or in word; for its *thing-and-sign*, interior penitence. Nor is it necessary that the first[^9] be the cause of the middle, but rather the reverse, and this because it is from a natural dictate; and therefore the exterior *proceeds* from the interior; the exterior does not *make* that which is interior. — But insofar as it is a Sacrament of the *Church*, having institution, it thus has the character of cause and of sign, like the other Sacraments[^10]; and the character of *causality* resides in that which is *formal*, whereas the character of *sign* [resides] in that which is *material*. Now the *material* in this Sacrament is the humiliation of the penitent, whether with respect to the *act of contrition*, or with respect to the *word of accusation*, or with respect to the *penalty of satisfaction*; the *formal*, however, is the *absolution of the priest*. These two joined together signify *interior penance*, according as through it there is perfect remission of guilt with respect to *penalty* and with respect to *guilt*; and with respect to the remission of sin as to *guilt* the exterior Sacrament has the character of a *sign*, [while] with respect to the remission of *penalty* it has in a certain way the character of a *cause*[^11].

And thus it is clear what in this Sacrament is the *thing* (res), namely the perfect remission of sin; and what the *sign*, namely exterior penance according to the ecclesiastical form, in which there is humiliation and absolution; the *thing-and-sign* is interior penitence[^12]. — Hence the arguments for the first part must be conceded.

1. To that, then, which is objected, that it is not an element; it must be said that Hugh takes *element* very broadly for everything that appears outwardly to the senses. — Or if it be appropriated to the *bodily* Sacraments, then it is understood only of the Sacraments proper to the new law.

2. To that which is objected, that the grace of the virtues differs from the grace of the Sacraments; it must be said that this is true in a certain way; but it is not required with respect to *essence*, but it suffices with respect to *effect*, as was determined above at the beginning[^13]; and so it is in the matter at hand. For interior penance is the *thing* (res) of the exterior, according as beyond the rectitude of a virtue it adds a further effect, namely of perfect remission with respect to *guilt and penalty*, when the exterior is perfect; and it is similar in baptism, as was seen above[^14].

3. To that which is objected, that the interior is the cause of the exterior; it must be said that, because this Sacrament is partly of *natural dictate* and partly of *institution*, therefore the exterior sign is in a certain way *caused* and in a certain way *causes*. For interior detestation, insofar as it is detestation, does indeed cause a man to be outwardly humbled; but according as it absolves entirely from guilt and penalty, this it has from the exterior absolution and penance. And thus it is clear that most fittingly there is an ordering in *signifying* and in *causing*, according as befits it.

4. To that which is objected, by reason of which of those three it is a *sign*; I say, by reason of *all*; for *contrition* is taken for the exterior groaning. Nor yet are they three Sacraments, but those three as one perfect sign signify perfect penance. And this is clear: because, if one truly repents in heart, then he grieves, and this is signified through the *groaning of contrition*; therefore he detests the guilt and reproaches himself, and this is signified through the *word of confession*; therefore he has the will of amending, and this is signified through the *penalty of satisfaction*. These three interior penance contains, which is one and a true virtue[^15]; and therefore all the exterior signs converge into one perfect sign and are one Sacrament and have the *unity of a Sacrament*. — And it is a special mode of unity, since many modes are assigned by various [authors][^16]. And *if you object* that they are not simultaneous; it must be said that at least with respect to the *purpose*, or the vow of the will, one remains in the other, as he who is contrite truly purposes to confess and to make satisfaction; otherwise there is no true contrition. So it is to be understood of the others[^17].
