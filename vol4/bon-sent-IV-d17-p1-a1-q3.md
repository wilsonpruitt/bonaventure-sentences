## Latin
<!-- page 421 -->

### Quaestio III. *Utrum ad iustificationem necessaria sit contritio.*

Tertio quaeritur, utrum ad iustificationem necessaria sit contritio. Et quod sic, videtur.

1. Super illud Psalmi[^1]: *Sacrificium Deo spiritus contribulatus*; Glossa: «Spiritus contribulatus et contritus est sacrificium, in quo peccata solvuntur».

<!-- page 422 -->

2. Item, super illud Psalmi[^2]: *Iustitia ante eum ambulabit*; Glossa Augustini: «Prima hominis iustitia est, ut puniat se malum et faciat se bonum; haec est via Deo, ut veniat ad eum».

3. Item, *ratione* videtur: quia impossibile est peccatorem iustificari, quin sequatur poena satisfactoria; sed possibile est, ipsum statim mori, et nulla erit poena satisfactoria nisi voluntarie inchoata in vita, sicut dicit Augustinus[^3]: ergo necesse est, quod in ipso actu iustificationis sit motus poenalis. Sed hic non est nisi contritio; ergo etc.

4. Item, sicut culpa voluntate est introducta, sic etiam conservatur per ipsam: ergo si liberum arbitrium, ex quo semel consensit, semper habetur pro consentiente, nisi dissentiat; necesse est dissentire; sed dissensus eius ante adventum gratiae nihil potest[^4]: ergo oportet, quod dissentiat cum gratia.

5. Item, aliquid plus requiritur in iustificatione eius qui post fidem peccavit, ultra motum fidei[^5]; sed hoc non est nisi contritio: ergo etc.

**Sed contra:** 1. Ecclesiastici primo[^6]: *Timor Domini expellit peccatum*: ergo potest fieri secundum hoc iustificatio sive peccati expulsio motu timoris: non ergo necesse est, quod contritionis.

2. Item, super illud Lucae septimo[^7]: *Dimittuntur ei peccata multa* etc.; Glossa: «Ardor caritatis peccatorum rubiginem in ea consumsit»: ergo non oportet, quod sit dolor.

3. Item, *ratione* videtur: quia duae sunt partes[^8] iustitiae, scilicet facere bonum et declinare a malo; sed prima est maior: ergo multo magis se disponit homo ad gratiam faciendo bonum quam vitando malum: ergo magis appetitu boni quam detestatione mali.

4. Item, cum quis iustificatus est, potest moveri motu omnis virtutis: cum ergo motus omnis virtutis sit gratiae cognatus, motus cuiuslibet virtutis informis disponit ad gratiam: non ergo necessaria est contritio.

5. Item, possibile est, aliquem a Deo averti, nihil de ipso cogitando: ergo similiter possibile est, aliquem a peccato recedere, nihil cogitando de peccato: ergo absque contritione.

<!-- page 422 -->

> **Conclusio.** *Ad iustificationem impii est necessaria contritio, ut voluntas concordet gratiae ut advenienti et ut peccatum expellenti.*

**Respondeo:** Ad hoc dicunt aliqui, quod iustificatio tripliciter dicitur. Uno modo dicitur iustificatio ad iustitiam praeparatio; et huic necessaria est attritio vel dolor. Alio modo iustitiae infusio; et huic necessaria est infusio gratiae et expulsio culpae. Tertio modo iustificatio est in iustitia exercitatio; et huic necessaria sunt duo a gratia et duo ab anima, scilicet motus liberi arbitrii et contritio. Et dicunt, verbum Augustini[^8a] de ista iustificatione intelligi: *Qui creavit te* etc.

Sed certe, si ad hoc, quod gratia infundatur, necesse est, quod homo se praeparet consentiendo gratiae et detestando peccatum; necesse est, in ipsa gratiae infusione ista quatuor esse, scilicet gratiae infusionem et liberi arbitrii motum, et contritionem et peccati expulsionem. Deus enim expellit culpam infundendo gratiam; sed non infundit nisi ei qui concordat, et necesse est concordare gratiae advenienti et peccatum expellenti. Gratiae ut[^9] advenienti concordat per motum liberi arbitrii, gratiae ut peccatum expellenti, per motum contritionis. Unde duo sunt ibi a Deo, scilicet gratiae infusio et peccati expulsio; et duo a nobis his duobus modis consona. — Et haec quatuor tanguntur in illo versiculo Psalmi[^10]: *Commovisti terram et conturbasti eam; sana contritiones eius, quia commota est.* Gratia enim adveniens in animam commovet rationem, secundum quod est pars liberi arbitrii, et conturbat voluntatem per contritionem, et postmodum sanat per peccati expulsionem. Et ratio huius redditur, quia praecessit duplex commotio, per quam anima ipsa se gratiae iustificanti conformavit; et ideo subiungit: *quia commota est.* — Concedendae igitur sunt rationes ad hanc partem inductae.

**Ad illud vero quod obiicitur in contrarium**, quod motu timoris et amoris contingit iustificari; dicendum, quod in peccato tria sunt, scilicet actio mala et ipsa interior macula et sequela.

<!-- page 423 -->

Actio mala expellitur timore, macula dolore, sequela caritatis ardore. Unde timor est in iustificatione, ut dicitur praeparatio ad iustitiam, dolor ut infusio iustitiae, caritatis ardor in consummatione[^11].

*Ad illud* quod obiicitur, quod melius est facere bonum etc.; dicendum, quod revera melius est, sed tamen non est ita consonum gratiae delenti culpam, ut detestatio peccati; et quia contritio propter consonantiam est necessaria: ideo etc.

*Ad illud* quod obiicitur, quod quilibet motus virtutis est cognatus gratiae; dicendum, quod verum est; tamen ordo est in actibus virtutum. Nunquam enim amat quis, nisi moveatur motu fidei[^12]; sic dico, quod motus virtutis poenitentiae in iustificatione post motum fidei est motus primus necessario, et ideo simul est cum ipsa gratiae infusione.

*Ad illud* quod obiicitur, quod potest quis averti a Deo, nihil cogitando; dicendum, quod non valet, tum quia recessus a Deo est inordinatus, sed regressus ordinatus — exemplum est de eo qui cadit de scala — tum quia plura exiguntur ad bonum quam ad malum[^13].

## English

### Question III. *Whether for justification contrition is necessary.*

Thirdly it is asked, whether for justification contrition is necessary. And that it is so, it seems.

1. Upon that text of the Psalm[^1]: *A sacrifice to God is a troubled spirit*; the Gloss: «A troubled and contrite spirit is the sacrifice in which sins are loosed».

2. Likewise, upon that text of the Psalm[^2]: *Justice shall walk before him*; the Gloss of Augustine: «The first justice of a man is that he should punish himself as evil and make himself good; this is the way to God, that He may come to him».

3. Likewise, it seems by *reason*: for it is impossible that a sinner be justified without a satisfactory penalty following; but it is possible for him to die at once, and there will be no satisfactory penalty unless voluntarily begun in this life, as Augustine says[^3]: therefore it is necessary that in the very act of justification there be a penal motion. But this is nothing other than contrition; therefore etc.

4. Likewise, just as fault was introduced by the will, so too it is preserved by it: therefore if free choice, from the moment it once consented, is always held as consenting, unless it dissents; it is necessary to dissent; but its dissent before the coming of grace avails nothing[^4]: therefore it behooves that it dissent together with grace.

5. Likewise, something more is required in the justification of one who has sinned after [receiving] faith, beyond the motion of faith[^5]; but this is nothing other than contrition: therefore etc.

**On the contrary:** 1. Ecclesiasticus the first[^6]: *The fear of the Lord driveth out sin*: therefore according to this, justification or the expulsion of sin can come about by a motion of fear: it is not therefore necessary that [it come about by a motion] of contrition.

2. Likewise, upon that text of Luke the seventh[^7]: *Many sins are forgiven her* etc.; the Gloss: «The ardor of charity consumed in her the rust of sins»: therefore it is not necessary that there be sorrow.

3. Likewise, it seems by *reason*: for there are two parts[^8] of justice, namely to do good and to turn away from evil; but the first is the greater: therefore much more does a man dispose himself to grace by doing good than by avoiding evil: therefore more by the appetite for good than by the detestation of evil.

4. Likewise, when one has been justified, he can be moved by a motion of every virtue: since therefore the motion of every virtue is akin to grace, the motion of any unformed virtue disposes to grace: it is not therefore necessary [that there be] contrition.

5. Likewise, it is possible for someone to be turned away from God while thinking nothing of Him: therefore likewise it is possible for someone to withdraw from sin while thinking nothing of the sin: therefore without contrition.

> **Conclusion.** *For the justification of the impious, contrition is necessary, that the will may accord with grace both as coming and as expelling sin.*

**I respond:** To this some say that justification is spoken of in three ways. In one way justification is called the preparation for justice; and for this attrition or sorrow is necessary. In another way [it is] the infusion of justice; and for this the infusion of grace and the expulsion of fault are necessary. In a third way justification is the exercise in justice; and for this two things from grace and two from the soul are necessary, namely the motion of free choice and contrition. And they say that the saying of Augustine[^8a] is to be understood of this [last] justification: *He who created thee* etc.

But certainly, if for this — that grace be infused — it is necessary that a man prepare himself by consenting to grace and detesting sin; it is necessary that in the very infusion of grace these four be present, namely the infusion of grace and the motion of free choice, and contrition and the expulsion of sin. For God expels fault by infusing grace; but He does not infuse it except in the one who accords [with it], and it is necessary to accord with grace as coming and as expelling sin. With grace as[^9] coming one accords by the motion of free choice; with grace as expelling sin, by the motion of contrition. Hence there are two things there from God, namely the infusion of grace and the expulsion of sin; and two from us consonant with these two modes. — And these four are touched upon in that little verse of the Psalm[^10]: *Thou hast moved the earth and hast troubled it; heal thou the breaches thereof, for it has been moved.* For grace, coming into the soul, moves reason, according as it is a part of free choice, and troubles the will through contrition, and afterward heals through the expulsion of sin. And the reason for this is given, because a twofold disturbance preceded, through which the soul itself conformed itself to the justifying grace; and therefore it adds: *for it has been moved.* — Therefore the reasons brought forward for this side are to be granted.

**But to that which is objected on the contrary**, that it happens that one is justified by a motion of fear and of love; it must be said that in sin there are three things, namely the evil action and the interior stain itself and the consequence.

The evil action is expelled by fear, the stain by sorrow, the consequence by the ardor of charity. Hence fear is in justification, as it is called the preparation for justice, sorrow as the infusion of justice, the ardor of charity in the consummation[^11].

*To that* which is objected, that it is better to do good etc.; it must be said that in truth it is better, but nevertheless it is not so consonant with grace as it blots out fault as the detestation of sin [is]; and because contrition is necessary on account of [this] consonance: therefore etc.

*To that* which is objected, that any motion of virtue is akin to grace; it must be said that this is true; yet there is an order in the acts of the virtues. For one never loves unless he be moved by a motion of faith[^12]; thus I say that the motion of the virtue of penitence in justification, after the motion of faith, is necessarily the first motion, and therefore it is simultaneous with the very infusion of grace.

*To that* which is objected, that one can be turned away from God while thinking nothing; it must be said that it does not hold, both because the departure from God is disordered, but the return is ordered — the example is of one who falls from a ladder — and because more is required for good than for evil[^13].
