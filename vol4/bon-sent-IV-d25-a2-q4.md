## Latin
<!-- page 655 -->

### Quaestio IV. *Utrum ad susceptionem ordinis sit necessaria conditio libertatis.*

Quarto et ultimo quaeritur, utrum ad susceptionem ordinis sit necessaria conditio libertatis. Et quod sic, videtur.

1. Distinctione quinquagesima quarta[^3]: «Nullus episcoporum ad clericatum servum alterius promovere praesumat».

2. Item, in eadem decretali, capitulo secundo: «Nulli de servili conditione ad sacros ordines promoveantur, nisi a dominis suis prius legitimam libertatem consequantur».

3. Item, si servus intret religionem, redditur domino suo et non potest ingredi, nisi efficiatur liber: ergo pari ratione, nisi efficiatur liber, non poterit ad sacros ordines promoveri[^4].

4. Item, *nemo potest duobus dominis servire*, sicut ipse Salvator testatus est[^5]: ergo cum officium clericale totum hominem sibi requirat dedicari, nullus ad hoc poterit promoveri, qui sit in alterius potestate.

**Contra:** 1. *Servus sapiens dominabitur stultis filiis*, dicitur Proverbiorum decimo septimo[^6]: ergo videtur, quod ubi est sapientia et vitae bonitas, quod nihil ex parte servitutis obsistat.

2. Item, ordo dicit potestatem spiritualem, servitus dicit subiectionem corporalem; sed subiectio corporalis non habet repugnantiam cum eo quod spirituale est: ergo etc.

3. Item, *Deus non est acceptor personae*[^7]: ergo tantum placet ei obsequium servi, sicut obsequium domini: ergo sicut possunt ad divinum cultum et clericatum assumi domini, ita etiam possunt servi.

4. Item, alia Sacramenta ita libere possunt accipere servi, ut domini, et ad ea ita communiter admittuntur: ergo pari ratione videtur, quod ad ordinem.

Iuxta hoc quaeritur, si servus sit ordinatus, ignorante domino, utrum sit ei restituendus. Item, si restituitur, quare non res aliae consecratae; et iterum, quanto tempore possit repetere.

> **Conclusio.** Servus propter Sacramenti honestatem ordinari non debet.

**Respondeo:** Ad praedictorum intelligentiam notandum est, quod Sacramentum ordinis non solum abundat ab aliis secundum rationem *signi*, propter hoc quod non tantum *Sacramentum*, sed etiam *suscipiens* Sacramentum tenet rationem signi[^8]; immo etiam abundat secundum rationem *remedii*: quia non solum ibi gratia ad curandum *subiectum* ordinatur, sed etiam datur gratia, quae ordinat ad curandum *alios*; unde collocat hominem non tantum in statu *sanitatis*, immo etiam in gradu *honestatis* et dignitatis: et ideo ad hoc Sacramentum requiritur non persona quaelibet, sed persona *honesta*, cui honor et dignitas competat clericalis. Et quoniam *honestas* multa[^9] respicit exteriora; ideo non solum propter defectum *vitae* efficitur quis inhabilis ad hoc Sacramentum suscipiendum, sed etiam propter defectum *multorum aliorum*.

Ad hoc enim, quod persona sit *honesta*, oportet, quod in ea concurrat honestas a parte *originis*, *nominis*, *corporis*, *actionis*, *conditionis* et *conversationis*. Propter defectum a parte *originis* ineptus est filius sacerdotis spurius, vel etiam quilibet illegitimus, Deuteronomii vigesimo tertio[^10]. — A parte *nominis* sive famae inepti sunt infames et publice poenitentes, distinctione quinquagesima[^11], *Ex poenitentibus*. — Propter defectum a parte *corporis*,
<!-- page 656 -->
corpore vitiati et daemoniaci et epileptici, distinctione trigesima tertia, *Communiter definimus*. — Propter defectum a parte *actionis*, ut quando incidit in peccatum enorme, sive factum, ut homicidium, distinctione quinquagesima fere per totum[^1b]; et hoc est propter magnum horrorem, qui est in sanguinis effusione, sive iuste fiat, sive iniuste; vel propter hoc, quod dividit unitatem animae et corporis. — Propter defectum *conditionis*, ut servus, distinctione quinquagesima quarta. — Propter defectum *conversationis*, ut curiales, distinctione quinquagesima prima. — In omnibus autem tradunt distinctiones sive exceptiones; sed illae spectant ad scientiam inferiorem. Haec tantum in generali dixisse sufficiat, quod quia Sacramentum ordinis non tantum personae *meritum*, sed *honestatem* considerat, quia clericus est persona communis, et ordo ponit in gradu dignitatis; debent excludi illi, in quibus Sacramentum et alia per consequens possint dehonestari. Quoniam *servus*, quamdiu in tali statu est, est ignobilis et ad ignobilia opera potest cogi; et ideo non potest se totum mancipare, cum alii sit subiectus: ideo non potest ad sacros ordines promoveri, quantum est de Sacramenti honestate, recipit tamen ordinem.

1. 2. 3. 4. Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur, quod servitus non impedit, ad totum[^2b] patet responsio per iam dicta.

Verumtamen quod quaerit, si servus debet reddi domino suo; respondendum, quod aut est ordinatus, domino *sciente* et *consentiente*, aut *ignorante* et *nolente*. Si *sciente* et *consentiente*, vel etiam non contradicente, generat[^3b] sibi praeiudicium, ut amplius non possit repetere eum. Si *ignorante* domino et *nolente*; aut *ignorante praesentatore et episcopo*, aut *altero*, vel *utroque sciente*. Si eis *ignorantibus*, tunc servus tenetur se redimere, si habet pecuniam; quodsi non habet, nec Ecclesia vult eum redimere, domino suo debet reddi. Si autem *sciente* ordinante et praesentatore, tenentur domino in pecunia, vel in alio servo, vel in duplum: quod si non habent, dominus potest sibi resumere servum. — Aliqui tamen dicunt, quod non, si pervenerit ad sacerdotium; sed de omnibus videtur[^4b], quod dominus debeat servari indemnis, habet tamen ius petendi eum infra annum; et quidam dicunt, quod post *ordinationem*, quidam dicunt, quod post *cognitionem*. Et haec omnia probantur per illa iura, quae dicuntur distinctione quinquagesima quarta[^5b].

Sunt et alia multa, quae determinantur in iure, quae, inquam, competunt ordinandis, quae ex diversis locis Scripturae sunt tracta, et maxime primae ad Timotheum tertio[^6b], et haec omnia determinantur a distinctione vigesima tertia usque in finem primae partis. Et quoniam haec distinctio magis spectat ad scientiam inferiorem, ideo ad praesens ista sufficiant, ne extra metas nostrae scientiae exeamus. Verum est tamen, quod omnes huiusmodi ordinationes a sacra Scriptura habent ortum[^7b], et omnia ad ipsam sicut ad originem possent reduci, si quis haberet peritiam iuris canonici et divini[^8b].

## English

### Question IV. *Whether the condition of freedom is necessary for receiving an order.*

Fourth and last it is asked whether, for receiving an order, the condition of freedom is necessary. And that it is so, seems [to be the case]:

1. In distinction fifty-four[^3]: «Let no bishop presume to promote to the clerical state the slave of another».

2. Likewise, in the same decretal, chapter two: «Let none of servile condition be promoted to sacred orders, unless they first obtain lawful liberty from their masters».

3. Likewise, if a slave enters religion, he is restored to his master and cannot enter [it], unless he be made free: therefore by parity of reasoning, unless he be made free, he will not be able to be promoted to sacred orders[^4].

4. Likewise, *no one can serve two masters*, as the Savior himself testified[^5]: therefore, since the clerical office requires the whole man to be dedicated to it, no one will be able to be promoted to this who is in the power of another.

**On the contrary:** 1. *A wise servant shall rule over foolish sons*, it is said in Proverbs, chapter seventeen[^6]: therefore it seems that where there is wisdom and goodness of life, nothing on the side of servitude stands in the way.

2. Likewise, an order bespeaks spiritual power, servitude bespeaks bodily subjection; but bodily subjection has no repugnance with that which is spiritual: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, *God is no respecter of persons*[^7]: therefore the service of a slave pleases him just as much as the service of a master: therefore, just as masters can be taken up for divine worship and the clerical state, so also can slaves.

4. Likewise, the other Sacraments can be received by slaves as freely as by masters, and to them they are admitted just as commonly: therefore by parity of reasoning it seems [the same holds] as regards an order.

Alongside this it is asked, if a slave has been ordained, the master being unaware, whether he must be restored to him. Likewise, if he is restored, why not other consecrated things; and again, for how long a time [the master] can reclaim [him].

> **Conclusion.** A slave, on account of the honorableness of the Sacrament, ought not to be ordained.

**I respond:** For the understanding of what has been said it must be noted that the Sacrament of order not only abounds beyond the others according to the character of a *sign*, on account of this, that not only the *Sacrament*, but also the one *receiving* the Sacrament holds the character of a sign[^8]; indeed it also abounds according to the character of a *remedy*: because not only is grace there ordained for healing the *subject* [ordained], but grace is also given which ordains toward the healing of *others*; whence it places a man not only in a state of *health*, but even in a grade of *honorableness* and dignity: and therefore for this Sacrament there is required not just any person, but an *honorable* person, to whom clerical honor and dignity are fitting. And since *honorableness* regards many[^9] exterior things; therefore not only on account of a defect of *life* does one become unfit for receiving this Sacrament, but also on account of a defect of *many other things*.

For, that a person be *honorable*, it is required that there concur in him honorableness on the side of *origin*, of *name*, of *body*, of *action*, of *condition*, and of *manner of life*. On account of a defect on the side of *origin*, the bastard son of a priest is unfit, or also any illegitimate person, Deuteronomy twenty-three[^10]. — On the side of *name* or reputation, the infamous and the publicly penitent are unfit, distinction fifty[^11], *Ex poenitentibus*. — On account of a defect on the side of *body*,
<!-- page 656 -->
those impaired in body and the possessed and epileptics, distinction thirty-three, *Communiter definimus*. — On account of a defect on the side of *action*, as when one falls into an enormous sin, whether a deed, such as homicide, distinction fifty almost throughout[^1b]; and this is on account of the great horror which is in the shedding of blood, whether it be done justly or unjustly; or on account of this, that it divides the unity of soul and body. — On account of a defect of *condition*, such as a slave, distinction fifty-four. — On account of a defect of *manner of life*, such as courtiers, distinction fifty-one. — In all [these], however, the distinctions hand down exceptions; but those pertain to the lower science. Let it suffice to have said these things only in general: that, since the Sacrament of order considers not only the *merit* of the person, but *honorableness*, because a cleric is a public person, and order places [him] in a grade of dignity; there ought to be excluded those in whom the Sacrament and other things could consequently be dishonored. Since a *slave*, as long as he is in such a state, is ignoble and can be compelled to ignoble works; and therefore he cannot give himself over entirely, since he is subject to another: therefore he cannot be promoted to sacred orders, so far as pertains to the honorableness of the Sacrament, yet he does receive the order.

1. 2. 3. 4. To that, then, which is objected, that servitude does not impede, as to the whole[^2b] the answer is clear from what has already been said.

Nevertheless, as to what he asks, whether a slave ought to be restored to his master; it must be answered that either he was ordained with his master *knowing* and *consenting*, or *unaware* and *unwilling*. If [with his master] *knowing* and *consenting*, or even not contradicting, he generates[^3b] a prejudice against himself, so that he can no longer reclaim him. If [with his master] *unaware* and *unwilling*; or with the presenter and the bishop *unaware*, or one, or *both being aware*. If they are unaware, then the slave is bound to redeem himself, if he has the money; but if he does not have it, nor does the Church wish to redeem him, he must be restored to his master. But if [it was done] with the ordainer and the presenter *knowing*, they are bound to the master in money, or in another slave, or in double: but if they do not have [it], the master can take back the slave for himself. — Some, however, say that [he may] not, if he has attained to the priesthood; but concerning all [cases] it seems[^4b] that the master ought to be kept indemnified, yet he has the right of seeking him within a year; and some say that [this holds] after ordination, some say that [it holds] after [his] becoming known. And all these things are proved by those laws which are stated in distinction fifty-four[^5b].

There are also many other things which are determined in law, which, I say, pertain to those to be ordained, which are drawn from various places of Scripture, and especially from the first [Epistle] to Timothy, chapter three[^6b], and all these things are determined from distinction twenty-three to the end of the first part. And since this distinction pertains more to the lower science, therefore for the present let these things suffice, lest we go beyond the bounds of our science. It is true, however, that all ordinations of this kind have their origin from sacred Scripture[^7b], and all could be reduced to it as to their origin, if one had expertise in canon and divine law[^8b].
