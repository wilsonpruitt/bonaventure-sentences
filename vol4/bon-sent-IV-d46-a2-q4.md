### Quaestio IV. *Utrum Deus possit aliquem pure remunerare ex iustitia, vel pure ex misericordia.*

> Quaracchi restarts its footnote numbering on every printed page. The notes below are therefore labelled by page: note 13 of page 965, then notes 1–10 of page 966.

Quarto quaeritur, utrum possit Deus aliquem pure *remunerare* ex iustitia, vel pure ex misericordia, ita quod ista duo separentur. Et quod sic, videtur:

1. Quia Hugo de sancto Victore[^p965-13] dicit, quod si Deus totam poenam relaxaret, non propter hoc esset iniustus: ergo si misericordia velit relaxare poenam, et iustitia non repugnat; patet etc.

2. Item, *ratione* videtur: quia Deus est summe iustus et summe misericors; sed « albius est quod est nigro impermixtius[^p966-1] »: ergo cum misericordia temperet iustitiam, et iustitia misericordiam, istud est summae misericordiae, quod nihil habet de iustitia, et e converso: ergo si Deus summus est in utroque, potest facere opus de iustitia, in quo non sit misericordia.

<!-- page 966 -->

3. Item, tantae culpae debetur tanta poena; quaeritur igitur, utrum Deus *possit* tantam infligere. Si *non*, ergo non est omnipotens; si *potest*, et hoc erat[^p966-2] purae iustitiae: ergo potest facere opus purae iustitiae; similiter concluditur, quod opus purae misericordiae.

4. Item, homo potest punire hominem tantum, quantum meruit, potest etiam totum remittere: ergo si Deus est iustior et misericordior homine, multo fortius Deus potest hoc facere.

**Contra:**
1. Secundae ad Timotheum secundo[^p966-3]: *Iustus est et negare se ipsum non potest*: si ergo non potest facere contra se nec se negare, non potest facere, quin puniat; et sic etc.

2. Item, proprium est Dei misereri[^p966-4]; sed proprium non potest relinquere illud cuius est proprium: ergo impossibile est, quod aliquem remuneret, quin misericorditer faciat.

3. Item, culpa est totam culpam prosequi, et culpa est malum relinquere impunitum: ergo sicut Deus non potest mentiri[^p966-5], quia malum est, sine culpa; sic nec omnino iuste punire nec omnino misericorditer parcere.

4. Item, sicut Deus creat in quantum unus, verus et bonus; ita regit et remunerat in quantum misericors et iustus; sed impossibile est, Deum facere effectum, qui non habeat modum, speciem et ordinem, quae respondent unitati, veritati et bonitati[^p966-6]: ergo impossibile est, quod retribuat, quin in effectu illo aliquid retribuat iustitiae, aliquid misericordiae.

### Conclusio

> *In omni Dei retributione misericordia et iustitia sic coniunctae sunt, ut impossibile sit, eas ab invicem separari.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod in omni retributione Domini sic sunt coniunctae misericordia et iustitia, quod impossibile est, separari eas ab invicem. — Et ratio huius est: quia, sicut in nobis, *quaedam* sunt virtutes, quarum perfectio attenditur per accessum ad extremum, ut puta caritas; *quaedam* per accessum ad medium, ut iustitia et misericordia[^p966-7]; unde non dicitur *summe iustus* per excessum severitatis, nec *summe misericors* propter omnimodam remissionem culpae; sed quia tantum miseretur, quantum est conveniens, similiter et tantum punit. — Et ratio huius est, quia iustitia et misericordia maxime dicuntur respectu *actuum et meritorum nostrorum*.

**Ad argumenta:**

*Ad 1.* Ad illud quod dicit Hugo, quod potest totam poenam remittere; dicendum, quod verum est de potentia *simpliciter*, sed non est verum de potentia *ordinata ad merita*. — Vel intelligitur de poena *subsequente*, non de poena *concomitante culpam*, quia, ut dicitur in primo Confessionum[^p966-8], « impossibile est, esse dedecus peccati sine decore iustitiae ».

*Ad 2.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod est summe iustus; dicendum est, quod non dicitur *summe iustus* per recessum a misericordia ad severitatem, sed per recessum ab iniustitia.

*Ad 3.* Ad illud quod quaeritur, utrum possit isti tantam poenam infligere; dico, quod non potest, et hoc, quia misericordia repugnat, et non potest suam misericordiam negare, hoc enim est *non-posse*; unde sicut *non posse* esse nimis rigidum[^p966-9] non sonat in defectum, sed in nobilitatem; sic in proposito intelligendum. — Posset etiam dici, quod ratione offensae, quae est in Deum infinitum, poena non possit sufficiens taxari, nec peccator esset capax; unde defectus est ex parte *naturae suscipientis*, non *naturae infligentis*.

*Ad 4.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod homo potest parcere totum; dicendum, quod est homo, ad quem spectat *misereri*, ut est ille qui recepit iniuriam, et iste non potest omnino punire, quin excedat; et homo, ad quem spectat *punire*, ut est iudex, et iste non potest omnino dimittere, quin excedat. Et quoniam Deus gerit istam duplicem personam, scilicet eius qui offenditur, et eius qui iudicat; cum[^p966-10] non possit excedere, facit utrumque.

## English

### Article II. Question IV. *Whether God can reward anyone purely out of justice, or purely out of mercy.*

Fourth it is asked whether God can *reward* anyone purely out of justice, or purely out of mercy, so that these two are separated. And that this is so, it seems:

1. Because Hugh of St. Victor[^p965-13] says that if God were to remit the whole punishment, he would not on that account be unjust: therefore if mercy should will to remit the punishment, justice too does not oppose it; it is plain, etc.

2. Likewise, it seems *by reason*: because God is supremely just and supremely merciful; but "that is whiter which is less mixed with black[^p966-1]": therefore, since mercy tempers justice, and justice mercy, that belongs to supreme mercy which has nothing of justice, and conversely: therefore if God is supreme in both, he can do a work of justice in which there is no mercy.

3. Likewise, to so great a fault so great a punishment is due; it is asked therefore whether God *can* inflict so great a one. If *not*, then he is not omnipotent; if he *can*, then this also would be[^p966-2] of pure justice: therefore he can do a work of pure justice; and in like manner it is concluded that he can do a work of pure mercy.

4. Likewise, a man can punish a man only as much as he has deserved, and he can also remit the whole: therefore if God is more just and more merciful than man, much more strongly can God do this.

**Contra:**
1. In the second chapter of the Second Letter to Timothy[^p966-3]: *He is faithful and cannot deny himself*: if therefore he cannot act against himself nor deny himself, he cannot act without punishing; and so forth.

2. Likewise, it is proper to God to have mercy[^p966-4]; but what is proper cannot leave behind that of which it is proper: therefore it is impossible that he should reward anyone without acting mercifully.

3. Likewise, it is a fault to prosecute a fault to the full, and it is a fault to leave an evil unpunished: therefore, just as God cannot lie[^p966-5], because it is an evil, without fault; so neither can he punish wholly justly nor spare wholly mercifully.

4. Likewise, just as God creates inasmuch as he is one, true and good; so he governs and rewards inasmuch as he is merciful and just; but it is impossible for God to make an effect which does not have measure, species and order, which correspond to unity, truth and goodness[^p966-6]: therefore it is impossible that he should repay without repaying in that effect something of justice and something of mercy.

### Conclusion

> *In every retribution of God mercy and justice are so joined together that it is impossible for them to be separated from one another.*

**I respond:** It must be said that in every retribution of the Lord mercy and justice are so joined together that it is impossible for them to be separated from one another. — And the ground of this is: because, just as among us *some* virtues are such that their perfection is measured by approach to an extreme, as for instance charity; *some* by approach to a mean, as justice and mercy[^p966-7]; hence he is not called *supremely just* by an excess of severity, nor *supremely merciful* on account of an all-encompassing remission of fault; but because he has mercy just so much as is fitting, and likewise punishes just so much. — And the ground of this is that justice and mercy are chiefly spoken of in respect of *our acts and merits*.

**To the arguments:**

*To 1.* To that which Hugh says, that he can remit the whole punishment; it must be said that this is true of power taken *simply*, but it is not true of power *ordered to merits*. — Or it is to be understood of the punishment that *follows*, not of the punishment *accompanying the fault*, because, as is said in the first book of the Confessions[^p966-8], "it is impossible that the disgrace of sin should be without the beauty of justice."

*To 2.* To that which is objected, that he is supremely just; it must be said that he is not called *supremely just* by a withdrawal from mercy toward severity, but by a withdrawal from injustice.

*To 3.* To that which is asked, whether he can inflict so great a punishment on this man; I say that he cannot, and this because mercy opposes it, and he cannot deny his own mercy, for this is a *not-being-able*; hence, just as *not being able* to be too rigid[^p966-9] does not sound as a defect but as a nobility; so it is to be understood in the case proposed. — It could also be said that, by reason of the offence, which is against the infinite God, a sufficient punishment could not be assessed, nor would the sinner be capable of it; hence the defect is on the side of the *nature receiving*, not of the *nature inflicting*.

*To 4.* To that which is objected, that a man can spare the whole; it must be said that there is the man to whom it belongs to *have mercy*, as is he who has received the injury, and this one cannot punish wholly without exceeding; and there is the man to whom it belongs to *punish*, as is the judge, and this one cannot wholly dismiss without exceeding. And since God bears this twofold person, namely of him who is offended and of him who judges; since[^p966-10] he cannot exceed, he does both.
