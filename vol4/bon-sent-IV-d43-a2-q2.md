## Latin
<!-- page 897 -->

### Quaestio II. *Utrum in libro vitae omnia scribantur.*

Secundo quaeritur, quae sit huius libri scriptura. Et quod omnia ibi scribantur, videtur:

1. Quia ipse Dei Filius est omnium exemplar[^p897-6] et omnia cognoscit, qui dicitur liber vitae.

2. Item, videtur, quod etiam mali scribantur in eo: quia dicitur in Psalmo[^p897-7]: *Deleantur de libro viventium*; sed nihil deletur de aliquo, nisi quod est scriptum in eo: ergo etc.

3. Item, liber ille est immutabilis: ergo si mali semel scripti sunt, semper scripti sunt: non ergo delentur.

**Contra:**

1. Quod non omnia ibi scribantur, videtur: quia tunc secundum hoc videtur, quod lapis esset scriptus in libro vitae; quod non dicitur[^p897-8] nisi de electis.

2. Item, quod mali non sint in eo scripti,
<!-- page 898 -->
videtur: quia malum non habet exemplar in Deo[^p898-1]: ergo si liber ille est exemplar, patet etc.

3. Item, Lucae decimo[^p898-2] dixit Dominus discipulis: *Gaudete, quia nomina vestra scripta sunt in caelis*; sed si hic liber esset omnium, tunc omnibus esset gaudendum; quod cum non sit: patet etc.

Iuxta hoc quaeritur: quomodo potest in illo libro transumi *deletio*, cum dicat variationem et privationem et corruptionem, quae nullo modo sunt in Deo?

> **Conclusio.** Libri vitae scriptura dicitur praedestinatio salvandorum quoad ipsos et gratiam et gloriam, quam habituri sunt.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod sicut *ars* et *exemplar* est totius Trinitatis, tamen appropriatur Filio, sic *liber vitae*. — Sed notandum, quod creaturarum ad Deum est duplex comparatio: una *in exeundo*, et haec est omnium creaturarum, quia omnes a Deo exierunt; alia est *in redeundo*, et haec est creaturarum ad beatitudinem ordinatarum, quia *regressus* est per virtutem, et *quies* per beatitudinem. Quantum ergo ad *primam* comparationem dicitur[^p898-3] Filius «ars plena omnium rationum viventium»; et scriptura istius libri est *aeterna dispositio omnium producendorum* quantum ad suas rationes et ideas. Quantum ad *secundam* comparationem dicitur Filius *liber vitae*; et scriptura istius libri est *praedestinatio omnium salvandorum* quantum ad ipsos et gratiam et gloriam, quam habituri sunt[^p898-4].

Sed attendendum, quod *gratiam*, per quam est regredi ad Deum, quidam habent *temporaliter*, quidam vero *finaliter*. Qui habent *finaliter*, *ipsi* scribuntur et eorum *gratia*, quia ipsi redeunt per gratiam. Qui autem *temporaliter*, *ipsi* quidem in praedestinatione scripti non sunt, quia non sunt praedestinati, sed eorum *gratia* scripta est secundum veritatem et *merita*, quae semper rememorabuntur; et ratione illius scribi dicuntur, non *simpliciter*, sed solum secundum *reputationem*[^p898-5], quia iustitia divina ad tempus iudicat eos gloria *dignos*, et cum cadunt, iudicat *indignos*. — Ideo dicuntur *deleri*, non quia prius sint scripti et postmodum deleti, sed quia prius secundum *merita scripta* iudicantur *digni*, deinde *indigni*. — Ex his patent quaesita.

1, 2. Primum patet: quia liber vitae non continet rationem *exemplaris totam*, sed tantum *praedestinationis*. — Secundum patet: quia mali, qui habent praesentem iustitiam, secundum reputationem scripti sunt et post non reputantur; ideo deleri dicuntur.

3. Ex his patet ultimum: quia quod Deus iudicat aliquem prius *dignum* et post *indignum*, solum dicit[^p898-6] mutationem in homine, non in Deo: et ita *deletio* illa non dicit permutationem in scriptura, sed in his quae per illam scripturam repraesentantur[^p898-7].

## English

### Question II. *Whether all things are written in the book of life.*

Secondly, it is asked what the writing of this book is. And that all things are written there seems to be the case:

1. Because the Son of God himself is the exemplar of all things[^p897-6] and knows all things, he who is called the book of life.

2. Likewise, it seems that even the wicked are written in it: because it is said in the Psalm[^p897-7]: *Let them be blotted out of the book of the living*; but nothing is blotted out of anything unless it is written in it: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, that book is immutable: therefore if the wicked have once been written, they are always written: therefore they are not blotted out.

**On the contrary:**

1. That not all things are written there seems to be the case: because then, on this reckoning, it would seem that a stone were written in the book of life; which is not said[^p897-8] except of the elect.

2. Likewise, that the wicked are not written in it seems to be the case: because evil does not have an exemplar in God[^p898-1]: therefore, if that book is an exemplar, it is evident, etc.

3. Likewise, in the tenth [chapter] of Luke[^p898-2] the Lord said to the disciples: *Rejoice, because your names are written in heaven*; but if this book were [the book] of all, then all would have to rejoice; which, since it is not so, is evident, etc.

In connection with this it is asked: how can a *blotting-out* be transferred to that book, since it implies variation and privation and corruption, which are in no way in God?

> **Conclusion.** The writing of the book of life is called the predestination of those to be saved, as regards themselves and the grace and glory which they are to have.

**I respond:** It must be said that, just as *art* and *exemplar* belongs to the whole Trinity, yet is appropriated to the Son, so also the *book of life*. — But it must be noted that of the creature there is a twofold comparison to God: one *in going forth*, and this belongs to all creatures, because all went forth from God; the other is *in returning*, and this belongs to creatures ordered to beatitude, because the *return* is through virtue, and the *rest* through beatitude. As regards the *first* comparison, therefore, the Son is called[^p898-3] «the art full of all the living reasons»; and the writing of this book is the *eternal disposition of all things to be produced* as regards their reasons and ideas. As regards the *second* comparison, the Son is called the *book of life*; and the writing of this book is the *predestination of all who are to be saved* as regards themselves and the grace and glory which they are to have[^p898-4].

But it must be attended to, that the *grace* by which there is a returning to God some have *temporally*, but some *finally*. Those who have it *finally*, they *themselves* are written, and their *grace*, because they themselves return through grace. But those who [have it] *temporally*, they *themselves* indeed are not written in predestination, because they are not predestined, but their *grace* is written according to truth and *merits*, which will always be remembered; and by reason of that they are said to be written, not *simply*, but only according to *reckoning*[^p898-5], because the divine justice for a time judges them *worthy* of glory, and when they fall, judges them *unworthy*. — Therefore they are said to be *blotted out*, not because they were first written and afterwards deleted, but because first, according to their *merits, the things written* are judged *worthy*, then *unworthy*. — From these things the matters sought are evident.

1, 2. The first is evident: because the book of life does not contain the *whole account of the exemplar*, but only [that] *of predestination*. — The second is evident: because the wicked, who have present justice, are written according to reckoning and afterwards are not reckoned; therefore they are said to be blotted out.

3. From these the last is evident: because the fact that God judges someone first *worthy* and afterwards *unworthy* states only[^p898-6] a change in the man, not in God: and thus that *blotting-out* does not state a change in the writing, but in those things which are represented through that writing[^p898-7].
