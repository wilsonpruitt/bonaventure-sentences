# IV Sent., d. 12, p. 1, a. 3, q. 1
## *Utrum corpus Christi verum in eucharistia frangatur*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 283 -->

### Articulus III. *De fractione specierum.*

Consequenter quantum ad tertium articulum quaeritur de fractione illarum specierum. Et circa hoc tria principaliter quaeruntur. Primo quaeritur, utrum vera fractio sit in corpore Christi. Secundo, utrum sit in speciebus. Tertio quaeritur, quid partes significent.

### Quaestio I. *Utrum corpus Christi verum in eucharistia frangatur.*

Et quod corpus Christi verum frangatur, videtur:

1. Augustinus[^1] dicere: « Norunt, inquit, fideles: quando manducant carnem Christi, unusquisque accipit partem suam »; quod si verum est: ergo dividitur.

2. Item, ostenditur ex confessione Berengarii[^2], quae acceptata fuit a Nicolao Papa; confessus est enim, « corpus Christi verum sacerdotis manibus attrectari et frangi »: ergo si ista confessio ut fidelis acceptata est, patet etc.

3. Item, *ratione*[^3] videtur: quia corpus Christi verum manducatur[^4]; sed quod manducatur atteritur, et quod atteritur frangitur: ergo etc.

4. Item, hoc Sacramentum est memoriale passionis, et ita memoriale, quod hic non tantum est signum, sed etiam veritas; sed in passione vere fuit corpus Christi fractum et divisum: ergo in hoc Sacramento vere frangitur[^5].

5. *Si dicas*, quod est incorruptibile, ideo non potest frangi; hoc nihil est, quia corpus Christi, quod natum est esse[^6] in uno loco, est in pluribus per miraculum: ergo similiter per miraculum potest incorruptibile frangi.

**Sed contra:**
1. Corpus animatum, quod dividitur, corrumpitur: ergo cum corpus Christi sit animatum[^7], si frangitur et dividitur, corrumpitur; et si corrumpitur, non manet verum Sacramentum: ergo etc.

2. Item, si potest frangi et masticari, ergo eadem ratione et digeri: ergo et eadem ratione et in secessum emitti; quod nefas est dicere[^8].

3. Item, de manna, quod fuit figura huius Sacramenti, *nec qui plus colligebat habuit plus, nec qui minus habuit minus*[^9]: si ergo fuit vera figura, corpus Christi tantum est in parva parte hostiae quantum in utraque, vel in magno pane: ergo non dividitur.

> **Conclusio.** Corpus Christi verum in eucharistia non frangitur.

**Respondeo:** Quidam dixerunt, quos tangit Magister in *littera*[^10], quod corpus Christi verum frangitur in Sacramento, et tamen non corrumpitur; et hoc quidem fit per virtutem supra naturam, sicut factum est, quod corpus Christi passibile datum est discipulis, et tamen non est ab eorundem dentibus passum. Et in assertionem suae positionis habere voluerunt confessionem Berengarii. — Sed haec positio non potest stare: quia, si est incorruptibile, non dividitur; si frangitur, dividitur: ergo simul dividitur et non dividitur: tunc ergo Deus faceret, duo contradictorie opposita simul esse vera; quod numquam faciet.

<!-- page 284 -->

Propter hoc dicendum, quod corpus Christi verum non frangitur, immo in quotcumque partes dividitur hostia, est totum et integrum[^11].

1. 2. Quod ergo obiicitur de confessione Berengarii et verbo Augustini, dicendum, quod utrumque dictum est ratione *specierum exteriorum*, circa quas contingit esse attritionem, fractionem et masticationem. Nec modus iste loquendi est tenendus. Recepit[^12] tamen Ecclesia confessionem Berengarii et publicavit, quamvis nimis expressam, ad elidendum suum errorem, qui non concedebat, corpus Christi verum ibi esse nisi sicut in signo. Unde propter hoc recepit, ut intelligeretur ibi *vere esse*, non vere dividi.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod corpus Christi verum manducatur; dicendum, quod in manducatione tria sunt: masticatio, in ventrem traiectio et incorporatio; et haec tria respondent tribus, quae sunt in Sacramento. Nam masticatio est specierum tantum; incorporatio est quantum ad corpus Christi mysticum[^13]; in ventrem traiectio est non solum specierum, sed etiam corporis Christi veri, quod ibi est, quamdiu species panis. Non ergo dicitur corpus Christi verum vere manducari corporaliter, quia corporaliter masticetur; sicut enim non frangitur, sic nec masticatur.

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod est signum passionis; dicendum, quod non est signum passionis, quae tunc sit, quia tunc semper pateretur Christus; sed passionis, quae semel fuit[^14]. — Et quod obiicit, quod est ibi veritas; dicendum, quod non est ibi veritas passionis, sed veritas eius qui est passus.

5. Ad illud quod dicitur, hoc posse fieri per miraculum; dicendum, quod non est simile de *esse in pluribus locis*, quia ibi nulla est contradictio, hic autem sic.

## English

### Article III. *On the breaking of the species.*

Consequently, as to the third article, it is asked concerning the breaking of those species. And concerning this, three matters are principally asked. First it is asked whether there is a true breaking in the body of Christ. Second, whether there is [a true breaking] in the species. Third it is asked what the parts signify.

### Question I. *Whether the true body of Christ is broken in the Eucharist.*

And that the true body of Christ is broken, it seems:

1. Augustine[^1] [seems] to say: "The faithful know, he says: when they eat the flesh of Christ, each one receives his own part"; and if this is true: therefore it is divided.

2. Likewise, it is shown from the confession of Berengarius[^2], which was accepted by Pope Nicholas; for he confessed "that the true body of Christ is handled and broken by the hands of the priest": therefore, if that confession was accepted as faithful, it is clear, etc.

3. Likewise, it seems so *by reason*[^3]: because the true body of Christ is eaten[^4]; but what is eaten is worn down, and what is worn down is broken: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, this Sacrament is a memorial of the passion, and a memorial in such a way that here there is not only a sign, but also the reality; but in the passion the body of Christ was truly broken and divided: therefore in this Sacrament it is truly broken[^5].

5. *Should you say* that it is incorruptible, and therefore cannot be broken; this is nothing, because the body of Christ, which is by nature [meant] to be[^6] in one place, is in many places by a miracle: therefore likewise by a miracle the incorruptible can be broken.

**On the contrary:**
1. An animate body which is divided is corrupted: therefore, since the body of Christ is animate[^7], if it is broken and divided, it is corrupted; and if it is corrupted, the true Sacrament does not remain: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, if it can be broken and chewed, then by the same reasoning [it can] also be digested: therefore also by the same reasoning [it can] be voided in excrement; which it is wicked to say[^8].

3. Likewise, concerning the manna, which was a figure of this Sacrament, *neither did he who gathered more have more, nor did he who [gathered] less have less*[^9]: if then it was a true figure, the body of Christ is just as much in a small part of the host as in the whole, or in a great bread: therefore it is not divided.

> **Conclusion.** The true body of Christ is not broken in the Eucharist.

**I respond:** Some have said — whom the Master touches upon in his *text*[^10] — that the true body of Christ is broken in the Sacrament, and yet is not corrupted; and this indeed comes about by a power above nature, just as it came about that the passible body of Christ was given to the disciples, and yet was not suffered by their teeth. And in support of their position they wished to have the confession of Berengarius. — But this position cannot stand: because, if it is incorruptible, it is not divided; if it is broken, it is divided: therefore it is at once divided and not divided: then therefore God would bring it about that two contradictory opposites be at once true; which he will never do.

<!-- page 284 -->

On account of this it must be said that the true body of Christ is not broken; rather, into however many parts the host is divided, it is whole and entire[^11].

1. 2. As to what is objected concerning the confession of Berengarius and the saying of Augustine, it must be said that each was said by reason of the *exterior species*, about which there occurs wearing-down, breaking, and chewing. Nor is this manner of speaking to be held. The Church nevertheless accepted[^12] and published the confession of Berengarius, although too explicitly expressed, in order to strike down his error, which did not concede that the true body of Christ was there except as in a sign. Whence on account of this it accepted [it], so that it might be understood that [Christ] is *truly there*, not truly divided.

3. To that which is objected, that the true body of Christ is eaten; it must be said that in eating there are three things: chewing, passing into the belly, and incorporation; and these three correspond to the three things which are in the Sacrament. For chewing is of the species only; incorporation is as regards the mystical body of Christ[^13]; the passing into the belly is not only of the species, but also of the true body of Christ, which is there, as long as the species of bread [remain]. Therefore the true body of Christ is not said to be truly eaten bodily, because it is bodily chewed; for just as it is not broken, so neither is it chewed.

4. To that which is objected, that it is a sign of the passion; it must be said that it is not a sign of a passion which is then [happening], because then Christ would always be suffering; but [a sign] of the passion which happened once[^14]. — And as to what it objects, that the reality is there; it must be said that the reality of the passion is not there, but the reality of him who suffered.

5. To that which is said, that this can come about by a miracle; it must be said that it is not similar to *being in many places*, because there [is] no contradiction, but here there is.

### Scholion
**I.** The opinion here placed in the first position and manifestly false the Master mentions (here c. 3.); likewise also the first and second opinion of the following (2nd) question is rightly disproved. — Concerning each question these treat concordantly: Alexander of Hales, *Summa* p. IV. q. 10. m. 9. a. 1. 2. — Scotus, here q. 1. n. 2-5. ff.; *Reportata* here q. 5. n. 18. — St. Thomas, here q. 1. a. 3. subquestion 1. 2; *Summa* III. q. 77. a. 7. — Bl. Albert, IV Sent. d. 13. a. 1. 2. 4. 6. 7. 8. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 2. a. 1. — Richard of Mediavilla, here a. 2. q. 1. — Dionysius the Carthusian, on this and the following question, here q. 3.

**II.** The explanation of the mystical sense of this breaking, in the 3rd question, is excerpted from Alexander of Hales (loc. cit. a. 4.), which St. Thomas approves in his *Commentary* loc. cit. subquestion 3. — Concerning this same 3rd question: Bl. Albert, IV Sent. d. 13. a. 14. 15. — Richard of Mediavilla, here a. 6. q. 2.
