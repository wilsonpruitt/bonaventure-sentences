## *Utrum humores resurgant*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 907 -->

### Articulus I. *Quid resurgat in corpore humano.*

Circa primum quaeruntur duo. Primo quaeritur, quid resurgat quantum ad indigentiam. Secundo, quid resurgat quantum ad superfluitatem.

### Quaestio I. *Utrum humores resurgant.*

Quantum ergo ad primum quaeritur, utrum humores resurgent. Et quod non, videtur:

1. Quia nihil resurget, nisi quod est de veritate humanae naturae; sed humores non sunt de veritate humanae naturae, quia quotidie mutantur et restaurantur et deperduntur: ergo etc.

2. Item, si humor resurget et est de veritate naturae humanae, ergo totaliter oportet resurgere: ergo totus sanguis, qui exit de homine per phlebotomiam[^p907-3], vel alio modo, resurget; sed hoc generaret indecentiam: ergo etc.

3. Item, quod non vivit non debet resurgere[^p907-4], quia resurrectio est ad vitam; sed humores non vivunt: ergo etc.

4. Item, nihil debet resurgere in corpore, quod non habeat aliquem usum; sed humores sunt ad restaurationem deperditi, quod non est in corpore resurgente: ergo etc.

5. Item, nihil resurget, nisi quod consonat qualitati et naturae corporis resurgentis; sed corpus resurgens erit luminosum et impassibile[^p907-5], humores autem sunt fluxibiles et variabiles: ergo etc.

**Contra:**

1. Quod est de constitutione corporis resurget in illo; sed humores sunt huiusmodi: ergo etc. Unde Augustinus[^p907-6]: «Corpus constat ex membris officialibus, officialia ex *similibus*, similia ex *humoribus*, humores ex *cibis*, cibi ex *elementis*»: ergo immediatius se habent humores ad corpus quam elementa: si ergo illa resurgent, et hi similiter.

2. Item, quod est de perfectione corporis resurget in illo; sed humores sunt huiusmodi, quia penes humores distinguuntur complexiones[^p907-7]: ergo etc.

3. Item, hoc videtur: quia medium et dispositio uniendi animam corpori resurget in illo; sed sanguis est huiusmodi, et hoc patet auctoritate, Deuteronomii duodecimo[^p907-8]: *Sanguis eorum pro anima*; Glossa: «Aliquid vitale est in sanguine, et per ipsum maxime anima carni unitur».

4. Item, hoc ipsum videtur *ratione*: quia spiritus est medium *uniendi* animam corpori; sed spiritus, sicut dicunt physici[^p907-9], ex humoribus generantur; hoc ipsum patet *exemplo*: quia nulla laesione facta in corpore, si totus sanguis exiret, statim <!-- page 908 --> anima recederet: si ergo humores sunt medium et ratio uniendi: ergo necesse est, quod resurgant.

5. Item, si non resurgent, cum huiusmodi humores impleant corpus, resurget vacuum et imperfectum, et per hoc deforme; sed hoc est indecens in Beatis: ergo etc.

6. Item, in Sacramento altaris convertitur vinum in verum sanguinem; sed hoc esset impossibile, nisi sanguis resurrexisset in Christo; sed si in ipso resurrexit, et in quolibet alio[^p908-1]: ergo etc.

### Conclusio.

> *Humiditas complexionalis et humores resurgent.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod differt, sicut dicunt physici, *humor* et *humiditas*. Nam *humor* dicitur, prout est in venis; *humiditas* autem, prout extra venas dirigitur et extra venas est, et prout ab eis derivatur ad membra. Distinguit enim Avicenna[^p908-2] inter *humorem* et *humiditatem* et dicit, quod *humor* dicitur, prout est in venis, *humiditas* autem, prout a venis derivatur ad membra; et hoc in triplici statu: aut in *primo egressu* a venis, et sic vocatur *ros*, humiditas scilicet, quae est in foraminibus parvarum venarum; aut prout incipit *ultra* progredi et dealbari, et sic vocatur *cambium*; aut prout est iam *dealbata* et cibans in actu, et sic vocatur *gluten*. — Haec autem distinctio humiditatis ad duo membra reducitur, scilicet ad humiditatem *nutrimentalem*, et haec complectitur *rorem* et *cambium*; et *complexionalem*[^p908-3], et haec est *gluten*, quae est de complexione membri consimilis, in qua est tertia species *hecticae*. — Et *nutrimentalis*, quia respicit statum et indigentiam, non resurget; sed humiditas *complexionalis* absque dubio resurget.

Sed quid de *humoribus* dicemus, cum sint currentes et non sint de substantia membri nec de complexione radicali? Dicendum, quod absque dubio resurgent; cum vinum convertatur in Sacramento altaris in sanguinem, qui effusus est in remissionem peccatorum[^p908-4], et iste est sanguis habens speciem sanguinis. Unde non video, quomodo vere sanguis non resurrexerit in Christo; et si hoc, pari ratione et in quolibet alio. Et *praeterea*, nunquid venae remanebunt vacuae? Et quod hoc sit verum, quod sanguis resurgat, habetur in sermone sancti Ambrosii de Martyribus[^p908-5]: *Dignum et congruum.* Et ideo dicendum, quod humores resurgent. — Et concedendae sunt rationes ad hoc.

*Ad 1. 2.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod non sunt de veritate humanae naturae; dicendum, quod aliquid est de *veritate humanae naturae* tripliciter: aut quantum ad *esse necessarium*, sicut membra principalia; aut quoad *esse integrum* et completum, ut illa quae faciunt ad corporis perfectionem; aut quoad *esse decorum*. Prima[^p908-6] *necessarium* est resurgere secundum *totalitatem*; sed quae secundo et tertio, *congruum* est resurgere secundum id, secundum quod faciunt ad *complementum et decorem*. Et quia humores ad plenitudinem et integritatem faciunt, resurgent in tanta quantitate, quanta corpori competit. — Et per hoc patet secundum, quia non oportet, quod totus sanguis, quem emittimus per minutionem, resurgat, sed quantum congruit. Similiter de carne, «quae fluit et refluit[^p908-7]», iudicandum est.

*Ad 3.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod non vivunt; dicendum, quod tripliciter est aliquid vivere: aut quia *recipit* influentiam vitae, aut quia *disponit* ad vitam, aut quia *ortum* et conservationem habet ex *actu vitae*. Primo modo vivificatur substantia corporalis et caro, secundo modo vivit spiritus et humor, tertio modo capilli et etiam ungues; et quia omnia ista connexionem habent cum vita, omnia resurgent.

*Ad 4.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod non habent usum; dicendum, quod falsum est, immo habent usum, scilicet ipsum corpus *disponere ad vitam*; sed nunc[^p908-8] habent ulteriorem usum, scilicet *restaurare*, et hunc habent, quia de novo generantur; et hunc ibi non habebunt.

*Ad 5.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod non congruunt corpori luminoso et impassibili; dicendum, quod sicut caro et os secundum statum praesentem non congruunt[^p908-9], sed sublimabuntur et subtiliabuntur; sic est intelligendum de humoribus et membris omnibus interioribus.

## English

### Article I. *What rises again in the human body.*

Concerning the first, two things are asked. First it is asked, what rises again as regards need. Second, what rises again as regards superfluity.

### Question I. *Whether the humors rise again.*

As regards the first, then, it is asked whether the humors rise again. And that they do not, it seems:

1. Because nothing will rise again except what is of the truth of human nature; but the humors are not of the truth of human nature, since they are daily changed and restored and lost: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, if a humor rises again and is of the truth of human nature, then it must rise again wholly: therefore the whole of the blood which goes out of a man through phlebotomy[^p907-3], or in another way, will rise again; but this would generate unseemliness: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, what does not live ought not to rise again[^p907-4], since resurrection is unto life; but the humors do not live: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, nothing ought to rise again in the body which has not some use; but the humors are [there] for the restoration of what is lost, which is not [the case] in the risen body: therefore etc.

5. Likewise, nothing will rise again except what is consonant with the quality and nature of the rising body; but the rising body will be luminous and impassible[^p907-5], whereas the humors are fluid and changeable: therefore etc.

**On the contrary:**

1. What is of the constitution of the body will rise again in it; but the humors are of this kind: therefore etc. Whence Augustine[^p907-6]: «The body is composed of *official* members, the official [members] of *similar* [parts], the similar [parts] of *humors*, the humors of *foods*, the foods of *elements*»: therefore the humors stand in a more immediate relation to the body than the elements do: if therefore the latter rise again, so also do these.

2. Likewise, what is of the perfection of the body will rise again in it; but the humors are of this kind, since complexions are distinguished according to the humors[^p907-7]: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, this is seen [thus]: because the medium and disposition for uniting the soul to the body will rise again in it; but the blood is of this kind, and this is clear by authority, in Deuteronomy twelve[^p907-8]: *Their blood [is] for the soul*; the Gloss: «There is something vital in the blood, and through it especially the soul is united to the flesh».

4. Likewise, this same thing is seen *by reason*: because the spirit is the medium *for uniting* the soul to the body; but the spirit, as the physicians say[^p907-9], is generated from the humors; and this same thing is clear *by example*: because, no injury having been done to the body, if all the blood went out, at once <!-- page 908 --> the soul would depart: if therefore the humors are the medium and ground of uniting: therefore it is necessary that they rise again.

5. Likewise, if they will not rise again, since humors of this kind fill the body, [the body] will rise empty and imperfect, and thereby deformed; but this is unseemly in the Blessed: therefore etc.

6. Likewise, in the Sacrament of the altar the wine is converted into true blood; but this would be impossible unless the blood had risen again in Christ; but if it rose again in him, [it rose] also in every other[^p908-1]: therefore etc.

### Conclusion.

> *The complexional moisture and the humors will rise again.*

**I respond:** It must be said that, as the physicians say, *humor* and *moisture* differ. For *humor* is so called insofar as it is in the veins; but *moisture*, insofar as it is directed outside the veins and is outside the veins, and insofar as it is derived from them to the members. For Avicenna[^p908-2] distinguishes between *humor* and *moisture* and says that *humor* is so called insofar as it is in the veins, but *moisture* insofar as it is derived from the veins to the members; and this in a threefold state: either in the *first egress* from the veins, and thus it is called *dew* (*ros*), namely the moisture which is in the openings of the small veins; or insofar as it begins to advance *further* and to whiten, and thus it is called *cambium*; or insofar as it is now *whitened* and nourishing in act, and thus it is called *gluten*. — But this distinction of moisture is reduced to two members, namely to *nutrimental* moisture, and this comprises *dew* and *cambium*; and *complexional* [moisture][^p908-3], and this is *gluten*, which is of the complexion of the like member, in which is the third species of *hectic* [fever]. — And the *nutrimental*, since it regards state and need, will not rise again; but the *complexional* moisture without doubt will rise again.

But what shall we say of the *humors*, since they are flowing and are not of the substance of the member nor of the radical complexion? It must be said that without doubt they will rise again; since the wine is converted in the Sacrament of the altar into the blood which was poured out for the remission of sins[^p908-4], and this is blood having the species of blood. Whence I do not see how truly the blood did not rise again in Christ; and if this, then by parity of reasoning also in every other. And *besides*, will not the veins remain empty? And that this is true, that the blood rises again, is had in the sermon of Saint Ambrose On the Martyrs[^p908-5]: *Worthy and fitting.* And therefore it must be said that the humors will rise again. — And the reasons for this are to be conceded.

*To 1 and 2.* To that which is objected, that they are not of the truth of human nature; it must be said that something is of the *truth of human nature* in three ways: either as regards *necessary being*, like the principal members; or as regards *whole and complete being*, like those [parts] which contribute to the perfection of the body; or as regards *decorous being*. The first[^p908-6] it is *necessary* to rise again according to *totality*; but as for those in the second and third [way], it is *fitting* to rise again according to that in respect of which they contribute to *completion and comeliness*. And since the humors contribute to fullness and integrity, they will rise again in as great a quantity as befits the body. — And through this the second [objection] is answered, since it is not necessary that the whole of the blood which we emit by bloodletting rise again, but [only] as much as is fitting. Likewise concerning the flesh, «which flows and flows back[^p908-7]», judgment is to be made.

*To 3.* To that which is objected, that they do not live; it must be said that to live belongs to something in three ways: either because it *receives* the influence of life, or because it *disposes* toward life, or because it has its *origin* and conservation from the *act of life*. In the first way the corporeal substance and the flesh are given life; in the second way the spirit and the humor live; in the third way the hair and also the nails [live]; and since all these have a connection with life, they will all rise again.

*To 4.* To that which is objected, that they have no use; it must be said that this is false, rather they do have a use, namely to *dispose the body itself toward life*; but now[^p908-8] they have a further use, namely *to restore*, and this they have because they are generated anew; and this [use] they will not have there.

*To 5.* To that which is objected, that they are not consonant with the luminous and impassible body; it must be said that, just as flesh and bone according to the present state are not consonant[^p908-9], but will be sublimed and subtilized; so it is to be understood of the humors and of all the interior members.

### Scholion

**I.** St. Thomas too (here q. 1, a. 2, quaestiuncula 3; Supplement q. 80, a. 3) employs these same distinctions of *moisture* (he is silent about the distinction between *humor* and *moisture*), [drawn] from Avicenna — who in that age was reckoned skilled beyond the rest in the theoretical art of medicine — for solving this question. The same holy Doctor there resolves special little questions (4 and 5), whether there rises again both the whole that pertains to the *truth of nature*, and the whole that was *materially* in the body; concerning which St. Bonaventure treats here in [the replies to] 1 and 2, and below in a. 2, q. 1. — On the *truth of nature* cf. II Sent. d. 30, a. 3, throughout, and Scotus, here q. 1, n. 1-14.

On the first question: Scotus, in the place cited, n. 15 ff., where he also treats of the following questions. — St. Thomas, in the places cited. — B. Albert, on this and the following question, a. 5-10. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 1, a. 1, quaestiunculae 1, 2. — Richard of Middleton, here a. 1, q. 2. — On this and the following questions of this part: Durandus, here q. 2; Dionysius the Carthusian, here q. 1.

**II.** In the solution of the second question all agree. On the same, besides those praised: St. Thomas, in the places cited, quaestiuncula, or a. 2. — Peter of Tarentaise, in the place cited, quaestiuncula 3. — Richard of Middleton, in the place cited, q. 3.
