### Quaestio III. *De extensione illius ignis.*

> **On the footnote numbering.** The Quaracchi apparatus restarts its numbering on every printed page, so the notes below are labelled by page: notes 6 through 11 of page 978 and notes 1 through 7 of page 979. Notes 1 through 5 of page 978 belong to the preceding question, and notes 8 through 10 of page 979 belong to the following question.

Tertio quaeritur de illius ignis extensione.

1. Super illud secundae ad Thessalonicenses primo[^p978-6]: *In flamma ignis dantis vindictam;* Glossa: « Praecedet ignis in mundo, qui tantum ascendet, quantum aqua in diluvio ».

2. Item, Beda super illud secundae Petri tertio[^p978-7]: *Unus dies* etc.; Glossa: « Tantum ascendet ille ignis, quantum aqua altior super omnes montes ».

3. Item, *ratione* videtur: quia purgatio illa est propter peccatum, ergo debet se solum extendere ad habitationem peccantium; sed hic est aër caliginosus[^p978-8], qui constat quod non est super omnes montes plus quam quindecim cubitis: ergo etc.

4. Item, ille ignis non extendet se secundum totam virtutem, sed secundum divinum imperium; sed divinum imperium noluit, quod ultra procederent aquae quam *quindecim cubitis super montes*[^p978-9]: ergo similiter videtur de igne; aut si non, quare non?

**Contra:** 1. Videtur, quod ultra usque ad caelos: quia dicitur secundae Petri ultimo[^p978-10]: *Caeli ardentes solventur:* ergo si solventur illo igne, videtur, quod ascendet usque ad caelum.

2. Item, videtur, quod saltem usque ad sphaeram elementorum ultimam, quia dicit ibi[^p978-11] Glossa, quod « quatuor elementa ille maximus ignis absumet ».

3. Item, *ratione* videtur: quia ille ignis habet virtutem innovandi: ergo si omnia corpora innovabuntur, scilicet caelestia et elementaria; patet etc.

<!-- page 979 -->

### Conclusio

> *Ignis conflagrationis non ascendet ultra spatium continens elementa, non usque ad sidera, plus tamen, quam aquae diluvii ascenderunt.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod ille ignis ascendet tantum, quantum durant corpora consumtibilia et passibilia; et quia « corpora caelestia non sunt receptibilia alienae impressionis[^p979-1] », dico, quod quantum durat spatium continens elementa, ascendet flamma et ulterius non procedet; unde dico, quod non ascendet usque ad sidera, plus tamen ascendet quam aqua diluvii.

Ad obiecta vero dicendum, quod duplex est purgatio: una, quae fit a *foeditate peccati*, alia vero a *vetustate corruptionis*. Dico ergo, quod *foeditas culpae* se extendit per aërem caliginosum, non quod in ipso aëre sit peccatum, sed[^p979-2] quaedam foeditas contrahitur in loco ex peccato, et etiam fumo sacrificiorum idolorum quodam modo est aër infectus; et quantum ad istam purgationem tantum ascendet, quantum aqua diluvii. — Alia purgatio est a *vetustate corruptionis* sive naturae corruptae, et haec est in ceteris elementis, quae debent esse incorruptibilia post resurrectionem[^p979-3]; et quantum ad hanc ascendet usque ad ultimum sphaerae ignis. — Et secundum has duas rationes solvuntur obiectiones factae de purgatione.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur de innovatione, dicendum, quod duplex est innovatio: uno modo per *abiectionem*, alio modo per *additionem* et *meliorationem*. Primo modo est in corporibus elementaribus, in quibus purgabuntur qualitates corporales; secundo modo in corporibus caelestibus, quibus augebitur lumen[^p979-4]. Prima innovatio est per operationem ignis, quae abiicit et purgat; secunda sine.

1. Ad illud vero quod dicit beatus Petrus, quod caeli ardentes solventur; dicendum, quod hoc non est intelligendum de illis caelis, qui sunt *incorruptibiles* et non corrumpentur; sed intelligendum de caelo *aëreo* et *igneo*, et haec vocat Scriptura *caelos*, secundum illud Matthaei decimo tertio[^p979-5]: *Volucres caeli comederunt illud.* — Similiter, si inveniatur, quod *stellae cadent de caelo*[^p979-6]; intelligitur de caelo *aëreo*; et *stellae* vocantur ibi a simili, quia habent similitudinem stellarum et secundum vulgarem opinionem dicuntur stellae cadere, quoniam a superius descendunt; et Scriptura conformat se modo communi loquendi. Tunc autem maxime fiet talis impressio, quia ignea qualitas abundabit; et hoc faciet Dominus ad terrorem peccatorum[^p979-7].

## English

### Question III. *On the extent of that fire.*

Thirdly it is asked about the extent of that fire.

1. Upon that text of the second [letter] to the Thessalonians, chapter one[^p978-6]: *In a flame of fire, giving vengeance;* the Gloss: « A fire shall go before in the world, which shall ascend as high as the water did in the flood ».

2. Likewise, Bede upon that text of the second [letter] of Peter, chapter three[^p978-7]: *One day* etc.; the Gloss: « That fire shall ascend as high as the water stood above all the mountains ».

3. Likewise, it seems *by reason*: because that purgation is on account of sin, therefore it ought to extend itself only to the dwelling-place of those who sin; but this is the dark air[^p978-8], which it is agreed is not above all the mountains more than fifteen cubits: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, that fire will not extend itself according to its whole power, but according to the divine command; but the divine command did not will that the waters should proceed further than *fifteen cubits above the mountains*[^p978-9]: therefore the like seems [to hold] concerning the fire; or if not, why not?

**On the contrary:** 1. It seems that [it will ascend] beyond, even as far as the heavens: because it is said in the last chapter of the second [letter] of Peter[^p978-10]: *The burning heavens shall be dissolved:* therefore if they shall be dissolved by that fire, it seems that it will ascend as far as heaven.

2. Likewise, it seems that [it will ascend] at least as far as the outermost sphere of the elements, because the Gloss says there[^p978-11] that « that greatest fire shall consume the four elements ».

3. Likewise, it seems *by reason*: because that fire has the power of renewing: therefore if all bodies shall be renewed, namely the celestial and the elemental; it is evident etc.

### Conclusion

> *The fire of the conflagration will not ascend beyond the space containing the elements, not as far as the stars, yet more than the waters of the flood ascended.*

**I respond:** It must be said that that fire will ascend only so far as bodies consumable and passible extend; and because « celestial bodies are not receptive of an alien impression[^p979-1] », I say that as far as the space containing the elements extends, the flame will ascend, and it will proceed no further; hence I say that it will not ascend as far as the stars, yet it will ascend more than the water of the flood.

But to the objections it must be said that purgation is twofold: one which is from the *foulness of sin*, the other from the *oldness of corruption*. I say therefore that the *foulness of guilt* extends itself through the dark air — not that there is sin in the air itself, but[^p979-2] that a certain foulness is contracted in the place from sin, and also that by the smoke of the sacrifices of idols the air is in some manner infected; and as regards that purgation it will ascend only as high as the water of the flood. — The other purgation is from the *oldness of corruption*, or of a corrupted nature, and this is in the remaining elements, which ought to be incorruptible after the resurrection[^p979-3]; and as regards this it will ascend as far as the outermost limit of the sphere of fire. — And according to these two grounds the objections made concerning purgation are resolved.

3. To that which is objected concerning renewal, it must be said that renewal is twofold: in one way by *casting off*, in another way by *addition* and *bettering*. In the first way it is in elemental bodies, in which the bodily qualities shall be purged; in the second way in celestial bodies, whose light shall be increased[^p979-4]. The first renewal is by the operation of fire, which casts off and purges; the second without [it].

1. But to that which blessed Peter says, that the burning heavens shall be dissolved; it must be said that this is not to be understood of those heavens which are *incorruptible* and shall not be corrupted; but it is to be understood of the *airy* and *fiery* heaven, and these Scripture calls *heavens*, according to that text of Matthew, chapter thirteen[^p979-5]: *The birds of heaven ate it up.* — Similarly, if it be found that *the stars shall fall from heaven*[^p979-6], it is understood of the *airy* heaven; and they are there called *stars* by likeness, because they have a likeness of stars, and according to common opinion they are said to be stars falling, since they come down from above; and Scripture conforms itself to the common manner of speaking. But such an impression will then occur most of all, because the fiery quality will abound; and the Lord will do this for the terror of sinners[^p979-7].
