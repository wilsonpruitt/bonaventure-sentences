## *Ad quid fuerit institutus baptismus Ioannis*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 55 -->

### Quaestio II. *Ad quid fuerit institutus baptismus Ioannis.*

Secundo quaeritur, ad quid fuerit baptismus Ioannis institutus. Et quod ad significandum, videtur:

1. Per illud quod dicit Hugo[^1]: « Inter baptisma Christi et baptisma Ioannis hoc interest, quia ibi solum Sacramentum dabatur, hic Sacramentum et res Sacramenti, scilicet in baptismo Christi »: ergo si Sacramenti est significare, patet, quod ad significandum.

2. Item, per rationem videtur: quia magis conveniebat baptismus Ioannis cum baptismo Christi, quam baptismus legalis; sed ille, ut dicit Hugo[^2], significabat baptisma Christi: ergo multo fortius congruit, quod baptisma Ioannis.

Item, quod fuerit institutus ad efficiendum, videtur:

<!-- page 56 -->

1. Quia dicitur Marci primo[^3]: *Erat Ioannes baptizans et praedicans baptismum poenitentiae in remissionem peccatorum*: ergo cum remissio peccatorum non sit sine gratia, videtur, quod dabat gratiam.

2. Item, ratione videtur: quia, sicut dicit Hugo[^4] « quemadmodum crescit in Sacramentis expressio significationis, crescit efficacia sanctificationis, et hoc secundum quod magis appropinquant ad adventum Christi »; sed baptismus Ioannis expressius signum erat quam circumcisio, et adventui magis appropinquabat: ergo maiorem efficaciam habebat.

3. Praeterea, si nullam habebat interius efficaciam, cum ablutio carnis parvae vel nullius esset utilitatis, non erat acceptandum nec approbandum: ergo stulte fecerunt qui susceperunt; male etiam fecit Christus, qui approbavit, dum ab eo baptizari voluit, sicut dicitur Matthaei tertio[^5]: ergo si nec illi faciebant stulte, nec Christus male, ergo aliquam efficaciam habuit.

4. Item, quod maiorem efficaciam habuit quam baptismus Christi, videtur: quia Christus baptizatus est baptismate Ioannis, non suo: ergo ex opere[^6] praeposuit illud suo; sed non esset praeponendum, nisi esset maioris virtutis et efficaciae: ergo etc.

**Contra:** Quod nec ad significandum nec ad efficiendum[^7], videtur:

5. Quia, si ad significandum tantum, ergo fuit Sacramentum legis veteris; si ad efficiendum, fuit novae: ergo cum baptismus Ioannis non fuerit Sacramentum legis veteris — quia Ioannes est terminus Legis — nec novae; manifestum est, quod nec significavit nec efficientiam habuit.

6. Item, quod nihil interius fecerit, videtur: quia Marci primo[^8] dicitur: *Ego baptizo vos aqua*; et Ioannis primo: *Propterea veni ego in aqua baptizans*; et Actuum primo: *Quia Ioannes baptizavit aqua*.

7. Item, super illud Ioannis tertio[^9]: *Erat Ioannes baptizans* etc.; Glossa: « Quantum catechumenis nondum baptizatis prodest doctrina fidei, tantum profuit baptisma Ioannis ante baptismum Christi »: ergo secundum hoc solum instruebat et nihil gratiae conferebat: patet igitur etc.

### Conclusio

> **Conclusio.** Baptismus Ioannis fuit institutus ad significandum, non ad efficiendum.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod baptisma Ioannis fuit institutum ad praesignandum baptisma Christi, sicut dicit Magister[^10], quod « erat sacrae rei signum, quia significabat baptismum Christi », et ratione huius erat quodam modo Sacramentum. — Fuit etiam institutum ad assuefaciendum ad baptismum Christi, Matthaei tertio[^11]: *Ego baptizo* etc.; Glossa: « Tantum corpora lavo, quia peccata solvere nequeo, ut sicut nascendo et praedicando praecurro, sic baptizando ad baptisma Christi dirigam »; et quantum ad hoc non est Sacramentum, sed sacramentale, sicut torneamentum, quo milites assuescunt, non est bellum. — Fuit et tertio ad baptismum Christi initiandum, manifestando auctorem et praeparando materiam, et hoc, cum baptizavit Christum: manifestatus est Christus, Ioannis primo: *Ut manifestaretur in Israel, propterea veni ego in aqua baptizans*; et in illo baptizandi actu « contulit Christus aquis vim regenerativam contactu carnis suae », ut dicit Beda. Et quantum ad hoc, quod fuit initium novae legis, non fuit Sacramentum nec omnino veteris nec omnino novae legis.

Concedendum est igitur, quod fuit institutum ad significandum, non ad efficiendum, sicut dicit Hugo[^12]; nec oportet quod Sacramentum sit veteris legis propter hoc, quod initiabat Sacramentum novae.

**Ad argumenta:**

*Ad 1.* Quod obiicitur de illo verbo: *praedicans* etc.; Glossa[^13] exponit sic: « *Poenitentiae baptismum* dicit *in remissionem peccatorum*, et hunc tantum praedicavit, non dedit ». Unde baptismus ille poenitentiae non erat baptismus Ioannis, quem ipse dabat, sed baptismus Christi, quem Ioannes praedicabat.

*Ad 2.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod expressius signum est quam legalia; dicendum quod circumcisio instituta fuit in remedium, non tantum in signum[^14], sed baptismus Ioannis tantum in signum, non in remedium; quodsi esset in remedium, utique esset efficacius remedium, sicut erat expressius signum.

*Ad 3.4.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod si nullius utilitatis etc.; dicendum, quod quamvis interius non purgaret, non tamen erat inutile, quia instruebat et assuefaciebat; et quod amplius est, fuit via manifestandi Christum et praeparandi materiam baptismo Christi. Et inde est, quod Christus in eo baptizari voluit, ut se baptizando materiam suo baptismati praepararet sanctificando aquam. — Et sic patet sequens, quia non praeposuit suo, sed ordinavit ad suum baptismum.

## English

### Question II. *For what end John's baptism was instituted.*

Second it is asked, for what end John's baptism was instituted. And that it was [instituted] for signifying seems [to be the case]:

1. By that which Hugh says[^1]: "Between the baptism of Christ and the baptism of John there is this difference, that there only the Sacrament was given, here the Sacrament and the reality of the Sacrament, namely in the baptism of Christ": therefore if it belongs to a Sacrament to signify, it is clear that [it was instituted] for signifying.

2. Likewise, by reason it seems [so]: because the baptism of John agreed more with the baptism of Christ than the legal baptism did; but that [legal baptism], as Hugh says[^2], signified the baptism of Christ: therefore much more fittingly does the baptism of John [signify it].

Likewise, that it was instituted for effecting seems [to be the case]:

1. Because it is said in Mark 1[^3]: *John was baptizing and preaching the baptism of penance for the remission of sins*: therefore since the remission of sins is not [given] without grace, it seems that he gave grace.

2. Likewise, by reason it seems [so]: because, as Hugh says[^4], "just as the expression of signification increases in the Sacraments, the efficacy of sanctification increases, and this in proportion as they draw nearer to the coming of Christ"; but the baptism of John was a more express sign than circumcision, and drew nearer to [Christ's] coming: therefore it had greater efficacy.

3. Furthermore, if it had no interior efficacy, since the washing of the flesh would be of little or no use, it was not to be accepted nor approved: therefore those who received it acted foolishly; Christ too did wrongly, who approved it when he willed to be baptized by him, as is said in Matthew 3[^5]: therefore if neither did they act foolishly, nor Christ wrongly, then it had some efficacy.

4. Likewise, that it had greater efficacy than the baptism of Christ seems [to be the case]: because Christ was baptized with John's baptism, not with his own: therefore by the deed itself[^6] he set it before his own; but it would not be preferred unless it were of greater virtue and efficacy: therefore, etc.

**On the contrary:** That it was [instituted] neither for signifying nor for effecting[^7] seems [to be the case]:

5. Because, if [it was instituted] only for signifying, then it was a Sacrament of the old law; if for effecting, it was [a Sacrament] of the new: therefore since John's baptism was not a Sacrament of the old law — because John is the terminus of the Law — nor of the new; it is manifest that it neither signified nor had efficacy.

6. Likewise, that it did nothing interiorly seems [to be the case]: because it is said in Mark 1[^8]: *I baptize you with water*; and in John 1: *Therefore have I come baptizing in water*; and in Acts 1: *Because John baptized with water*.

7. Likewise, on that text of John 3[^9]: *John was baptizing* etc.; the Gloss [says]: "As much as the teaching of faith profits catechumens not yet baptized, so much did the baptism of John profit before the baptism of Christ": therefore according to this it only instructed and conferred nothing of grace: it is clear therefore, etc.

### Conclusion

> **Conclusion.** John's baptism was instituted for signifying, not for effecting.

**I respond:** It must be said that John's baptism was instituted to pre-signify the baptism of Christ, as the Master says[^10], that "it was a sign of a sacred thing, because it signified the baptism of Christ," and by reason of this it was in a certain way a Sacrament. — It was also instituted to accustom [men] to the baptism of Christ, [as in] Matthew 3[^11]: *I baptize* etc.; the Gloss: "I only wash bodies, since I cannot loose sins, so that just as by being born and by preaching I run before [him], so by baptizing I may direct [men] to the baptism of Christ"; and as to this it is not a Sacrament, but a sacramental, like the tournament by which soldiers are trained, [which] is not [real] war. — It was also, thirdly, [instituted] for initiating the baptism of Christ, by manifesting the author and preparing the matter, and this, when he baptized Christ: Christ was manifested, [as in] John 1: *That he might be manifested in Israel, therefore have I come baptizing in water*; and in that act of baptizing "Christ conferred upon the waters a regenerative power by the contact of his flesh," as Bede says. And as to this, that it was the beginning of the new law, it was a Sacrament neither wholly of the old nor wholly of the new law.

It must therefore be granted that it was instituted for signifying, not for effecting, as Hugh says[^12]; nor is it necessary that it be a Sacrament of the old law on account of this, that it initiated the Sacrament of the new.

**To the arguments:**

*To 1.* As for what is objected concerning that word *preaching* etc.; the Gloss[^13] expounds it thus: "He calls it *the baptism of penance* *for the remission of sins*, and this he only preached, he did not give." Hence that baptism of penance was not the baptism of John, which he himself gave, but the baptism of Christ, which John preached.

*To 2.* As for what is objected, that it is a more express sign than the legal [signs]; it must be said that circumcision was instituted as a remedy, not only as a sign[^14], but John's baptism only as a sign, not as a remedy; for if it were a remedy, it would surely be a more efficacious remedy, just as it was a more express sign.

*To 3 and 4.* As for what is objected, that [it would be] of no use, etc.; it must be said that although it did not purge interiorly, yet it was not useless, because it instructed and accustomed [men]; and what is more, it was a way of manifesting Christ and of preparing the matter for the baptism of Christ. And hence it is that Christ willed to be baptized in it, so that by baptizing himself he might prepare the matter for his own baptism by sanctifying the water. — And thus the consequent is clear, that he did not set it before his own, but ordained it to his own baptism.
