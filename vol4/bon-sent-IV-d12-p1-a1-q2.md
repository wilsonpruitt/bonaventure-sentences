# IV Sent., d. 12, p. 1, a. 1, q. 2
## *Utrum in Sacramento eucharistiae congruat species esse sine subiecto*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 272 -->

### Quaestio II. *Utrum in Sacramento eucharistiae congruat species esse sine subiecto.*

Secundo quaeritur, utrum congruat, quod in hoc Sacramento sint species sine subiecto. Et quod non sit inconveniens, immo conveniens, patet:

1. Quia, si esset ibi subiectum, nequaquam tanta esset dependentia specierum ad contentum, sicut est; et ita nunquam esset signum ita ductivum[^1].

**Contra:** 1. Minus inconveniens est, corpus non glorificatum simul esse cum glorificato, quam accidens esse praeter subiectum[^2]: si ergo corpus glorificatum esset simul cum pane, multo minus esset inconveniens, quam accidentia esse praeter subiectum, et magis credibile: ergo magis congruebat, accidentia manere in subiecto quam separari.

2. Item, accidentia illa ducunt ad intelligendum, quod ibi sit substantia panis, quia sunt propria accidentia panis et signa substantiae[^3]: ergo in hoc Sacramento, cum non sit substantia, est aliqua falsitas; sed non decet, in Sacramento veritatis esse falsitatem: ergo etc.

3. Item, opere miraculi non debet fieri praeiudicium naturae rei; sed cum res ponitur in loco, qui suae naturae non convenit, quamvis sit nobilior, tamen fit ei praeiudicium, ut si vermis poneretur in caelo[^4], vel pes in scapulis: ergo similiter, quando datur ei proprietas, quae naturae suae non convenit. Sed esse sine subiecto est proprietas nullo modo conveniens naturae accidentis: ergo non congruit, hoc per miraculum sibi dari.

4. Item, infirmior est intellectus ad credendum contrarium eius quod sentit, quam sensus ad recipiendum aliquod horribile[^5] sibi — nam sensus hoc potest sine gratia, sicut patet in eo qui comedit medicinam, sed intellectus non — sed non debet fieri aliquid in solatium minoris infirmitatis, quod fiat in praeiudicium maioris: si ergo velatio accidentium occultat intellectui quod ibi est, nullo modo debuerunt ibi accidentia remanere.

> **Conclusio.** Congruum est, in eucharistia accidentia remanere, et quidem sine subiecto.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod congruit, ibi accidentia remanere; quia sunt, ut dicit Hugo[^6], in solatium sensus et in fidei meritum. Dum enim velatur verum corpus carnis, et fides magis meretur credendo quod non videt, et sensus libentius recipit.

Congruum autem est, quod sola remaneant sine subiecto: propter *efficaciorem significationem*, quia efficacius et perfectius ducunt in significatum, cum non habeant ibi stabiliens per naturam. — Propter *convenientem cibationem*; quia enim iste cibus spiritualis est, ideo oportet, animam sublevari a carnalitate sensus per fidem et carnali delectatione per caritatem[^7]; et ideo non debuit ibi remanere panis materialis. — Propter *fidei promotionem*; dum enim sublevatur intellectus ad intelligendum quod est supra naturam, scilicet accidentia esse sine subiecto, fit habilior ad credendum quod est supra se ipsum, quod totus Christus sit in tam parva hostia[^8]. — Quarta ratio, propter *divinorum operum perfectionem*, quae debet in hoc Sacramento reperiri, cum sit perfectissimum. Et attenditur duplex perfectio in hoc Sacramento: prima est in hoc, quod accidentia accipiunt modum nobilissimum essendi, scilicet esse per se[^9]; et in hoc, quod substantia illa, quae suberat, cum esset materialis et corruptibilis, transsubstantiatur in substantiam sive in corpus spirituale et incorruptibile et in corpus Divinitati coniunctum et gloriosum.

<!-- page 273 -->

1. Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur, quod minus est inconveniens; dicendum, quod hoc non est inconveniens isti Sacramento, immo valde conveniens.

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur de falsitate, dicendum, quod non est consentiendum sensui, sed fidei; et ideo nihil omnino falsitatis est ibi, sed veritatis.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod non debet fieri praeiudicium naturae rei; dicendum, quod duplex est ordo rerum, unus scilicet universalis, alius particularis, qui debetur isti rei, vel illi. Dico igitur, quod per miraculum non debet fieri aliquid in praeiudicium ordinis universalis; quia, si pes esset in capite, esset in praeiudicium toti corpori; similiter nec quod sit in praeiudicium totius universi. Sed licet alicuius rei natura melioretur et statum recipiat maioris quam naturae suae competat nobilitatis, dummodo fiat ordinatio conveniens secundum ordinem universi convenienter fit; et sic in proposito, quia haec ordinantur ad hominis utilitatem. — Vel dicendum, quod competit naturae uniuscuiusque rei obedire Creatori, et illud est conveniens suae naturae, quod Creator iusserit, quod, si prius non sit conveniens, postea fit conveniens.

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur de infirmitate intellectus, dicendum, quod hoc verum esset, nisi aliud daretur medicamentum ipsi; et hoc est gratia fidei, qua fortificatur intellectus ad credendum sine dubio quod non videt.

## English

### Question II. *Whether in the sacrament of the Eucharist it is fitting that the species exist without a subject.*

Secondly it is asked, whether it is fitting that in this Sacrament there be species without a subject. And that it is not unfitting, but rather fitting, is clear:

1. Because, if there were a subject there, in no way would there be so great a dependence of the species on what is contained, as there is; and so it would never be a sign so leading [to the thing signified][^1].

**On the contrary:** 1. It is less unfitting that a non-glorified body be at the same time with a glorified one, than that an accident be without a subject[^2]: if therefore a glorified body were at the same time with the bread, it would be much less unfitting than that the accidents be without a subject, and more credible: therefore it was more fitting that the accidents remain in a subject than be separated.

2. Likewise, those accidents lead to the understanding that there is the substance of bread there, since they are the proper accidents of bread and signs of the substance[^3]: therefore in this Sacrament, since there is no substance, there is some falsity; but it is not seemly that there be falsity in the Sacrament of truth: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, by the work of a miracle prejudice ought not to be done to the nature of a thing; but when a thing is placed in a location which does not befit its nature, although it be nobler, nevertheless prejudice is done to it, as if a worm were placed in heaven[^4], or a foot on the shoulders: therefore likewise, when a property is given to it which does not befit its nature. But to be without a subject is a property in no way befitting the nature of an accident: therefore it is not fitting that this be given to it by a miracle.

4. Likewise, the intellect is weaker for believing the contrary of what it perceives, than the sense is for receiving something horrible[^5] to it — for the sense can do this without grace, as is clear in one who takes medicine, but the intellect cannot — but nothing ought to be done as a comfort to the lesser weakness, which is done to the prejudice of the greater: if therefore the veiling of the accidents conceals from the intellect what is there, in no way ought the accidents to have remained there.

> **Conclusion.** It is fitting that in the Eucharist the accidents remain, and indeed without a subject.

**I respond:** It must be said that it is fitting that the accidents remain there; because they are, as Hugh says[^6], for the comfort of sense and for the merit of faith. For while the true body of flesh is veiled, both faith merits more by believing what it does not see, and the sense more willingly receives.

But it is fitting that they alone remain without a subject: on account of a *more efficacious signification*, because they lead more efficaciously and more perfectly into what is signified, since they do not have there anything stabilizing them by nature. — On account of *fitting nourishment*; for since this food is spiritual, therefore the soul must be lifted up from the carnality of sense through faith, and from carnal delight through charity[^7]; and therefore material bread ought not to have remained there. — On account of the *promotion of faith*; for while the intellect is lifted up to understand what is above nature, namely that the accidents are without a subject, it becomes more apt to believe what is above itself, that the whole Christ is in so small a host[^8]. — The fourth reason, on account of the *perfection of the divine works*, which ought to be found in this Sacrament, since it is most perfect. And a twofold perfection is observed in this Sacrament: the first is in this, that the accidents receive a most noble mode of being, namely to be through themselves[^9]; and in this, that that substance which was underneath, since it was material and corruptible, is transubstantiated into a substance, that is, into a body spiritual and incorruptible and into a body joined to the Divinity and glorious.

<!-- page 273 -->

1. To the objection therefore which is raised, that it is less unfitting; it must be said that this is not unfitting to this Sacrament, but rather very fitting.

2. To the objection which is raised concerning falsity, it must be said that one must not assent to sense, but to faith; and therefore there is nothing at all of falsity there, but of truth.

3. To the objection which is raised, that prejudice ought not to be done to the nature of a thing; it must be said that there is a twofold order of things, one namely universal, the other particular, which is owed to this thing or that. I say therefore that by a miracle nothing ought to be done to the prejudice of the universal order; for if the foot were in the head, it would be to the prejudice of the whole body; likewise neither [ought there be done] anything that would be to the prejudice of the whole universe. But although the nature of some thing be bettered and receive a state of greater nobility than befits its nature, provided that a fitting ordering be made, it comes about fittingly according to the order of the universe; and so in the case at hand, because these things are ordered to the utility of man. — Or it must be said that it befits the nature of each thing to obey the Creator, and that is befitting to its nature which the Creator has commanded, [a thing] which, if it is not befitting before, afterward becomes befitting.

4. To the objection which is raised concerning the weakness of the intellect, it must be said that this would be true, unless another medicine were given to it; and this is the grace of faith, by which the intellect is strengthened to believe without doubt what it does not see.
