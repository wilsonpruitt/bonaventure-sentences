## Latin

### ARTICULUS III. *De sensuum remuneratione.*

Consequenter quantum ad tertium articulum, circa hoc quaeritur de sensuum remuneratione. Et supposito, quod erunt[^p1017-10] in patria — quia erit anima habens omnes suas potentias, cum nullam amittat, erit etiam corpus habens organa omnia — circa hoc duo quaeruntur.

*Primo* quaeritur, utrum in patria omnes sensus habeant suos actus.

*Secundo*, utrum sensus illi, qui sunt in suo actu, cognoscant per receptionem speciei.

<!-- page 1018 -->

### QUAESTIO I. *Utrum in patria omnes sensus habeant suos actus.*

> The numbered footnotes below correspond to markers in both the Latin body and the English translation. Quaracchi restarts its footnote numbering on every printed page, so the labels are page-qualified: note 10 of page 1017, notes 1–12 of page 1018, and notes 1–7 of page 1019.

Quantum ergo ad primum ostenditur, quod *omnes* sensus erunt in suo actu:

1. Quia nobilior est potentia coniuncta suo actui quam non coniuncta; sed omnes potentiae erunt ibi in statu nobilissimo: ergo omnes erunt coniunctae actui[^p1018-1]: ergo etc.

2. Item, sicut anima appetit uniri corpori, ita sensus obiecto; sed ad hoc, quod anima perfecte glorificetur, necesse est uniri corpori, ut ille appetitus quietetur[^p1018-2]: ergo similiter necesse est, sensum omnem uniri obiecto. Sed unio sensus ad obiectum est mediante actu: ergo etc.

3. Item, constat, quod corpus remuneratur, quia servit animae: ergo et sensus debet remunerari; sed omnis potentia nata delectari, si perfecte remuneratur, in perfecta delectatione constituitur; quiescit enim in illo, et haec quies est delectatio[^p1018-3]. Et hoc potest probari *per simile* in damnatis, quia ibi erit continuus dolor in sensu: ergo in Beatis continua delectatio. Ex hoc arguo: nulla potentia delectatur, nisi in eo quod cognoscit; sed sensus non cognoscit se nec organum nec quod supra se est[^p1018-4]: ergo cum solum obiectum cognoscat, delectatur in obiecto. Sed in illo non delectatur nisi in actu: ergo etc.

4. Item, summa delectatio potentiae est in suo actu: ergo si sensus ibi delectantur, in suis actibus ibi sunt[^p1018-5]. *Praeterea*, si delectatur, sentit; sicut sequitur, si dolet, quod sentiat: ergo cum probatum sit[^p1018-6], delectationem esse in omnibus sensibus, oportet, quod omnes sentiant.

5. Item, *visus* est in suo actu, qui est spiritualissimus; unde Iob decimo nono[^p1018-7]: *In carne mea videbo Deum*; et Apocalypsis primo: *Videbit eum omnis oculus*; et Augustinus dicit, quod « visus reficietur in contemplatione humanitatis », in libro *de Spiritu et anima*. — Est etiam ibi sensus *tactus*; quod patet, quia unum corpus glorificatum poterit aliud amplexari, et iste est maxime materialis[^p1018-8]. Quare, cum sit ibi actus sensus *spiritualis* et *materialis*: ergo et omnium sensuum.

**Contra:** Quod *nullus* sensus sit ibi in actu, videtur:

1. Quia omnis virtus finita plus potest in unum, quam quando dispergitur ad multa[^p1018-9]; sed anima habet virtutem finitam: ergo et intensius moveretur, si in solum Deum moveretur cognitione et delectatione: ergo cognitio et delectatio sensuum minuit motum in Deum, ergo et gloriam. Sed nihil debet ibi esse tale: ergo etc.

2. Item, si est sensus in actu, aut ille est *cum delectatione*, aut *sine*. Si *non est cum delectatione*, ergo non est in actu perfecto. Si vero *est cum delectatione*; sed quando aliquid delectatur in uno oppositorum, tristatur in reliquo; quando delectatur in medio, tristatur in extremis: ergo si delectatur in percipiendo, possibile est tristari[^p1018-10]. — *Si dicas*, quod nihil sibi repugnat nec corrumpit propter incorruptibilitatem; saltem *carentia* rei inducit *tristitiam*, cuius *praesentia* afferebat *delectationem*: ergo etc.

3. Item, *quidam* sensus sunt propter indigentiam, ut patet de gustu, ad[^p1018-11] restaurationem deperditi; sed tunc tolletur omnis indigentia: ergo etc.

### Conclusio

> *Sensus visus et tactus et fortasse etiam auditus erunt in suis actibus, alii vero non.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *posse sentire* est ex coniunctione potentiae cum organo bene disposito, *sentire* vero ex coniunctione ipsius cum obiecto[^p1018-12]. Quoniam igitur ibi erunt potentiae iunctae organis bene dispositis, ibi erunt omnes *potentiae sentiendi*. Quoniam vero *obiecta* omnium sensuum non erunt in gloria, sed quorundam; ideo *quidam*

<!-- page 1019 -->

erunt in *actu suo*, *quidam* vero *non*. — *Obiecta* enim *quorundam* sensuum sunt *proprietates absolutae*, quae reperiuntur in corpore glorificando[^p1019-1], utpote color visus, et levitas ipsius tactus; et quia corpus gloriosum resurget cum luminositate et levitate, erunt ibi obiecta duorum sensuum; ideo isti sensus erunt in suis actibus. Obiecta vero *aliorum* sensuum non sunt *proprietates corporis glorificandi* nec *proprietates absolutae*, sed *emanationes* a corporibus, ut odor et sonus et sapor; et quia non erunt in patria, ideo etc.

Alia etiam ratio sumitur ex parte *medii:* quia medium in *visu* potest esse omne quod habet rationem pervii, medium in *tactu* est caro sibi coniuncta[^p1019-2]; et ideo, quia ista duo media erunt in patria, et isti duo sensus in suis actibus erunt, *alii* vero minime, deficientibus sibi mediis, nisi forte quis instet in *auditu*, de quo relinquitur in dubio, sicut de *laude vocali* in patria, quam ibi ponere nec videtur necessarium nec aperte falsum; et ideo hic opinari est licitum[^p1019-3].

**Ad argumenta pro 1. parte:**

*Ad 1.* Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur, quod *omnes* erunt in actibus propter nobilitatem potentiarum maiorem; dicendum, quod est actus *perfectionis*, et actus, qui habet *imperfectionem coniunctam*; et quoniam actus *gustandi* habet imperfectionem coniunctam ratione *status*, et similiter *olfactus* ratione *obiecti*, quod non glorificatur, et habet fieri per medium receptivum peregrinae impressionis[^p1019-4]: ergo etc.

*Ad 2.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod appetit sensus uniri; dicendum, quod alius est modus appetendi[^p1019-5]; ille enim animae appetitus est ratione perfectionis *esse naturalis*, sed iste solum est quantum ad *bene esse*; et ideo ille non potest terminari, nisi corpus uniatur; iste potest, si in tali statu ponatur, quod illo obiecto non egeat.

*Ad 3. 4.* Ad illud quod obiicitur de delectatione, dicendum, quod delectatio est in sensu dupliciter: una per *redundantiam*, alia per *obiecti praesentiam*. Per *redundantiam*, sicut quando anima est interius plena delectatione, ita inebriantur et potentiae sentiendi, quod nullo modo quaerunt nec egent exterius delectari; et haec delectatio erit praemium omnium sensuum et in omnibus[^p1019-6]. Hanc autem delectationem non habent *a se* nec ab *actu suo*, sed ab *actu animae*; et ideo non oportet, quod propter istam sint sensus in suo actu. — Alia est delectatio per *obiecti convenientis praesentiam*; et quia solum duo sunt sensus, qui habent obiecta gloriosa, ideo solum in duobus est; et hoc non est praemium, nisi valde accidentale, quia non magis est gloriosus sensus in illius praesentia quam absentia. — Aliud patet.

**Ad argumenta pro 2. parte:**

*Ad 1.* Ad illud vero quod obiicitur, quod *nullus* sit sensus, quia unus actus impedit alium; dicendum, quod verum est, quando unus non ordinatur ad alium; non sic in proposito, sed actus *rationis* redundat in sensum, et actus *sensus* ordinatur et subservit actui rationis; et ideo unus vigoratur ex alio.

*Ad 2.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod ubi delectatio, ibi tristitia; dicendum, quod quidam sensus delectatur in obiecto, quia est *conveniens, perficiens* et *conservans*, utpote est obiectum respectu sensus corporis; quidam, quia est *conveniens* et *perficiens*, ut intellectus in intelligibili veritate[^p1019-7]; quidam, quia tantum est *conveniens*, ut sensus gloriosus, *non perficiens*, quia interius perficitur, *non conservans*, quia incorruptibilis est; et talis non tristatur in *contrario*, quia non laeditur, nec in *absentia*, quia non efficitur imperfectus. Quod primo modo delectatur tristatur dupliciter; quod secundo, uno modo.

*Ad 3.* Ultima ratio concedenda est, quod illi sensus, qui sunt *per indigentiam*, non manebunt in suis actibus. Et sic patet totum.

### Scholion

**I.** De I. quaestione nihil temere asserendum; tamen cum S. Thoma (supra d. 44. q. 2. a. 1. quaestiunc. 4; Suppl. q. 82. a. 4.), Scoto (hic q. 13. n. 12.) et Richardo (hic a. 4. q. 3.) alii theologi probabilius esse censent, *omnes* sensus ibi aliquam operationem habituros; nec ipsum *gustum*, de quo magis dubitatur, excipiunt, quamvis non per cibi externi sumtionem saporem habeat. — De *laude vocali* et *auditu* non dubitandum esse, iidem affirmant. — De delectatione *redundante* vide ad 3. pro 1. parte.

**II.** Conclusionem quaestionis 2. approbat S. Thom. (loc. cit. quaestiunc. et a. 3.); qui etiam distinctionem a S. Bonav. adhibitam in solut. ad 4. aliis verbis ponit, scilicet inter immutationem *naturalem* et *spiritualem* (locus refertur infra pag. 1021, nota 3.). In *naturali* immutatione recipitur physice eadem qualitas *naturalis* (v. g. calor), quae inest rei immutanti; in *spirituali* vero recipitur tantum *species* sive *intentio*. Haec autem immutatio, non illa, proprie facit sensus esse in actu, quin immutet *naturam* recipientis. Idem docent Scot. et Richard. locis citt.

## English

### ARTICLE III. *On the remuneration of the senses.*

Next, as to the third article, inquiry is made concerning the remuneration of the senses. And it being supposed that they will be[^p1017-10] in the fatherland — since the soul will be one having all its powers, seeing that it loses none, and the body too will be one having all its organs — concerning this two things are asked.

*First* it is asked, whether in the fatherland all the senses have their acts.

*Second*, whether those senses which are in their act know through the reception of a species.

<!-- page 1018 -->

### QUESTION I. *Whether in the fatherland all the senses have their acts.*

As to the first, then, it is shown that *all* the senses will be in their act:

1. Because a power joined to its act is nobler than one not joined; but all the powers will be there in the noblest state: therefore all will be joined to their act[^p1018-1]: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, as the soul desires to be united to the body, so does sense to its object; but for the soul to be perfectly glorified it is necessary that it be united to the body, so that that appetite may be quieted[^p1018-2]: therefore in like manner it is necessary that every sense be united to its object. But the union of a sense with its object is by means of an act: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, it is agreed that the body is remunerated because it serves the soul: therefore sense too ought to be remunerated; but every power born to be delighted, if it is perfectly remunerated, is established in perfect delight; for it rests in it, and this rest is delight[^p1018-3]. And this can be proved *by a parallel* in the damned, since there will be continuous pain in sense there: therefore in the Blessed continuous delight. From this I argue: no power is delighted except in that which it knows; but sense does not know itself, nor its organ, nor what is above itself[^p1018-4]: therefore, since it knows only its object, it is delighted in its object. But it is not delighted in it except in act: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, the highest delight of a power is in its act: therefore if the senses are delighted there, they are there in their acts[^p1018-5]. *Moreover*, if it is delighted, it senses; just as it follows that if it grieves, it senses: therefore since it has been proved[^p1018-6] that delight is in all the senses, it must be that all of them sense.

5. Likewise, *sight* is in its act, which is the most spiritual; whence Job, chapter nineteen[^p1018-7]: *In my flesh I shall see God*; and Apocalypse, chapter one: *Every eye shall see him*; and Augustine says that « sight will be refreshed in the contemplation of the humanity », in the book *On the Spirit and the Soul*. — There is also there the sense of *touch*; which is clear, because one glorified body will be able to embrace another, and this sense is the most material of all[^p1018-8]. Wherefore, since there is there the act of a *spiritual* and of a *material* sense: therefore also of all the senses.

**On the contrary:** That *no* sense is there in act seems to follow:

1. Because every finite power can do more upon one thing than when it is dispersed among many[^p1018-9]; but the soul has a finite power: therefore it would also be moved more intensely if it were moved toward God alone by knowledge and delight: therefore the knowledge and delight of the senses diminishes its motion toward God, and therefore also its glory. But nothing of that kind ought to be there: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, if sense is in act, that act is either *with delight* or *without*. If it is *not with delight*, then it is not in a perfect act. If, however, it *is with delight*; but when something is delighted in one of two opposites, it is saddened in the other; when it is delighted in the mean, it is saddened in the extremes: therefore if it is delighted in perceiving, it is possible for it to be saddened[^p1018-10]. — *If you say* that nothing is repugnant to it nor corrupts it, on account of its incorruptibility; at least the *absence* of a thing induces *sadness* whose *presence* brought *delight*: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, *certain* senses exist on account of need, as is clear of taste, for[^p1018-11] the restoration of what has been lost; but then all need will be taken away: therefore etc.

### Conclusion

> *The senses of sight and touch, and perhaps also hearing, will be in their acts; the others, however, will not.*

**I respond:** It must be said that *being able to sense* comes from the joining of the power with a well-disposed organ, but *to sense* comes from the joining of that power with an object[^p1018-12]. Since, therefore, the powers will there be joined to well-disposed organs, all the *powers of sensing* will be there. But since the *objects* of all the senses will not be in glory, but only of certain ones, therefore *certain* senses

<!-- page 1019 -->

will be in *their act*, and *certain* ones *not*. — For the *objects* of *certain* senses are *absolute properties*, which are found in the body to be glorified[^p1019-1], such as color for sight and lightness for touch itself; and since the glorious body will rise with luminosity and lightness, the objects of two senses will be there; therefore those senses will be in their acts. But the objects of the *other* senses are not *properties of the body to be glorified* nor *absolute properties*, but *emanations* from bodies, such as odor and sound and savor; and since these will not be in the fatherland, therefore etc.

Another reason as well is drawn from the side of the *medium:* because the medium in *sight* can be everything that has the character of the transparent, while the medium in *touch* is the flesh joined to it[^p1019-2]; and therefore, since these two media will be in the fatherland, these two senses will also be in their acts, while the *others* will by no means be so, their media failing them — unless perhaps someone should press the case of *hearing*, about which the matter is left in doubt, as it is about *vocal praise* in the fatherland, which to posit there seems neither necessary nor openly false; and therefore here it is permissible to hold an opinion[^p1019-3].

**To the arguments for the first side:**

*To 1.* To that, then, which is objected, that *all* will be in their acts on account of the greater nobility of the powers; it must be said that there is an act *of perfection*, and an act which has *imperfection joined to it*; and since the act of *tasting* has imperfection joined to it by reason of the *state*, and likewise *smell* by reason of its *object*, which is not glorified and has to come about through a medium receptive of an alien impression[^p1019-4]: therefore etc.

*To 2.* To that which is objected, that sense desires to be united; it must be said that the mode of desiring is different[^p1019-5]; for that appetite of the soul is by reason of the perfection of *natural being*, but this one is only as regards *well-being*; and therefore the former cannot be terminated unless the body be united; the latter can be, if it be placed in such a state that it does not need that object.

*To 3 and 4.* To that which is objected concerning delight, it must be said that delight is in sense in two ways: one by *redundance*, the other by the *presence of the object*. By *redundance*, as when the soul is inwardly full of delight, the powers of sensing too are so inebriated that they in no way seek nor need to be delighted outwardly; and this delight will be the reward of all the senses and in all of them[^p1019-6]. But this delight they do not have *from themselves* nor from *their own act*, but from the *act of the soul*; and therefore it is not necessary that on its account the senses be in their act. — The other delight is by the *presence of a suitable object*; and since there are only two senses which have glorious objects, therefore it is only in two; and this is not a reward, except a very accidental one, because a sense is not more glorious in that object's presence than in its absence. — The rest is clear.

**To the arguments for the second side:**

*To 1.* To that, however, which is objected, that there is *no* sense, because one act impedes another; it must be said that this is true when the one is not ordered to the other; but it is not so in the present case, for the act of *reason* redounds into sense, and the act of *sense* is ordered to and subserves the act of reason; and therefore the one is invigorated by the other.

*To 2.* To that which is objected, that where there is delight, there is sadness; it must be said that a certain sense is delighted in its object because it is *suitable, perfecting* and *conserving*, as the object is with respect to bodily sense; another, because it is *suitable* and *perfecting*, as the intellect in intelligible truth[^p1019-7]; another, because it is only *suitable*, as the glorified sense — *not perfecting*, since it is perfected inwardly, *not conserving*, since it is incorruptible; and such a sense is not saddened by its *contrary*, since it is not harmed, nor in its *absence*, since it is not made imperfect. That which is delighted in the first way is saddened in two ways; that which is delighted in the second, in one way.

*To 3.* The last reason must be conceded, namely that those senses which exist *by reason of need* will not remain in their acts. And thus the whole is clear.

### Scholion

**I.** Concerning the first question nothing is to be rashly asserted; nevertheless, with St. Thomas (above, d. 44, q. 2, a. 1, quaestiuncula 4; *Supplementum* q. 82, a. 4), Scotus (here, q. 13, n. 12) and Richard (here, a. 4, q. 3), other theologians judge it more probable that *all* the senses will have some operation there; nor do they except *taste* itself, about which there is more doubt, although it would not have savor through the taking of external food. — That there is no doubt to be had concerning *vocal praise* and *hearing*, the same authors affirm. — Concerning *redundant* delight, see the reply to 3 for the first side.

**II.** The conclusion of the second question St. Thomas approves (in the place cited, the same quaestiuncula, and a. 3); he also puts in other words the distinction employed by St. Bonaventure in the solution to 4, namely that between *natural* and *spiritual* alteration (the passage is referred to below, p. 1021, note 3). In *natural* alteration there is received physically the same *natural* quality (e.g. heat) which is present in the thing altering; but in *spiritual* alteration only the *species* or *intention* is received. And it is this alteration, not the former, that properly makes the senses to be in act, without altering the *nature* of the recipient. Scotus and Richard teach the same in the places cited.
