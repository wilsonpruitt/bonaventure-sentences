# IV Sent., d. 6, a. 1, q. 6
## *Utrum baptismus possit et debeat iterari.*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 146 -->

### Quaestio VI. *Utrum baptismus possit et debeat iterari.*

Sexto et ultimo quaeritur, utrum baptismus debeat iterari. Et quod sic, videtur:

1. Quoniam possibile est, aliquem baptizari, qui nihil gratiae percipit per baptismum[^1]: ergo si non debet esse sibi via salutis praeclusa, debet et potest iterari isti baptismus.

2. Item, duo dantur in baptismo, scilicet character et gratia[^2]; sed quamvis character sit indelebilis, tamen gratia est delebilis: ergo saltem ratione gratiae potest et debet iterari.

**Contra:** 1. Si baptismus posset iterari: ergo posset quilibet impune peccare, cum ibi fiat remissio culpae et poenae.

2. Item, si *effectus* non potest iterari, nec *causa*: ergo si character non potest iterari, nec signum visibile sive baptismus.

Item, multa non debent fieri de iure, quae, si fiunt de facto, facta sunt: ut sacerdos praecisus non debet conficere, sed si conficit, confectum est[^3]; quaeritur ergo, utrum in hoc casu aliquid fiat. Et quod sic, videtur: quia « accedente verbo ad elementum, fit Sacramentum[^4] ».

### Conclusio

> Baptismus nec debet nec potest iterari.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod baptismus nec *debet* nec potest iterari. Quia non potest, ideo qui iterat nihil facit; quia vero non *debet*, qui scienter iterat gravissime peccat.

Ratio autem, quare non *debeat* iterari, triplex est. Prima est ratione *morbi*, quem curat, scilicet peccati originalis, qui non potest *iterari*[^5]. Secunda est ratione eius quod principaliter *significat*, ut passionem et resurrectionem, quam impossibile est *iterari*. Tertia est ratione *efficaciae* in curando, quia absolvit a poena et a culpa[^6]; et tunc mali non essent puniti, si semper, cum peccarent, deberent baptizari.

Ratio, quare non *potest*, est *characteris impressio*, qui, ex quo semel imprimitur, amplius imprimi non potest; et ille est effectus Sacramenti primus, sine quo nihil facit Sacramentum. — Alia est *divina institutio*, quia Dominus instituit, ut perfectam et unicam haberet efficaciam et semel tantum fieret; si ergo fit secundo, nihil fit in anima, et ita cassatur Sacramentum scienter. — Et propter hoc iterans baptismum nihil facit omnino et magis peccat quam ille qui ficte accedit, quia facit contra ordinationem divinam; facit etiam contumeliam Sacramento; facit et contumeliam Christo, quia, quantum est de se, Filium Dei crucifigit sibi et ostentui habet[^7], cum unicum sit baptisma et unicam efficaciam habeat perfectam a passione unica. Et ideo sancti Patres gravem poenam imposuerunt rebaptizantibus et rebaptizatis scienter propter gravitatem culpae; quia rebaptizatio aut sapit haeresim, aut si non sapit, gravissimum contemptum Dei et Sacramenti inducit, et ideo graviter debet puniri.

**Ad argumenta:**

1. Ad illud quod obiicitur de fictione, dicendum, quod fictione recedente, character totum supplet, quod Sacramentum sine fictione faceret[^8].

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur de gratia, dicendum, quod gratia non est effectus immediatus, sed mediante charactere[^9]; et ideo nihil fit, quando non imprimitur character.

Ad ultimum dicendum, quod qui iterat baptismum nec de facto nec de iure Sacramentum tradit, quia ille non est susceptibilis; sed[^10] quando exterius abluit, si scienter vel negligenter facit, graviter peccat; si vero diligentia adhibita, etsi nihil faciat interius, tamen non peccat[^11].

## English

### Question VI. *Whether baptism can and ought to be repeated.*

Sixth and last it is asked whether baptism ought to be repeated. And that it ought, it seems:

1. Because it is possible that someone be baptized who receives no grace through baptism[^1]: therefore, if the way of salvation ought not to be closed off to him, this baptism ought and can be repeated for him.

2. Likewise, two things are given in baptism, namely character and grace[^2]; but although the character is indelible, nevertheless the grace is deletable: therefore at least by reason of the grace it can and ought to be repeated.

**On the contrary:** 1. If baptism could be repeated, then anyone could sin with impunity, since therein remission of fault and penalty is made.

2. Likewise, if the *effect* cannot be repeated, neither can the *cause*: therefore if the character cannot be repeated, neither can the visible sign, that is, baptism.

Likewise, there are many things which by right ought not to be done, which, if they are done in fact, are done: as a suspended priest ought not to consecrate, but if he does consecrate, it is consecrated[^3]; it is asked, therefore, whether in this case anything is brought about. And that it is, it seems: because « when the word is joined to the element, the Sacrament comes to be[^4] ».

### Conclusion

> Baptism neither ought to nor can be repeated.

**I respond:** It must be said that baptism neither *ought to* nor can be repeated. Because it cannot, therefore he who repeats it brings nothing about; but because it *ought* not to be, he who knowingly repeats it sins most gravely.

Now the reason why it *ought* not to be repeated is threefold. The first is by reason of the *disease* which it cures, namely original sin, which cannot be *repeated*[^5]. The second is by reason of that which it principally *signifies*, namely the Passion and Resurrection, which it is impossible to *repeat*. The third is by reason of its *efficacy* in curing, since it absolves from penalty and from fault[^6]; and then the wicked would not be punished, if always, whenever they sinned, they ought to be baptized.

The reason why it *cannot* be repeated is the *impression of the character*, which, once it is imprinted, can no longer be imprinted; and that is the first effect of the Sacrament, without which the Sacrament does nothing. — Another is *divine institution*, because the Lord instituted that it should have a perfect and unique efficacy and be done only once; if therefore it is done a second time, nothing is brought about in the soul, and thus the Sacrament is knowingly annulled. — And on account of this, one who repeats baptism brings about nothing at all and sins more than he who approaches feignedly, because he acts against the divine ordinance; he also does insult to the Sacrament; he does insult to Christ as well, because, so far as in him lies, he crucifies the Son of God for himself and holds him up to mockery[^7], since baptism is unique and has its unique and perfect efficacy from the unique Passion. And therefore the holy Fathers imposed a grave penalty upon those who rebaptize and those who are knowingly rebaptized, on account of the gravity of the fault; for rebaptism either savors of heresy, or if it does not savor of it, it brings in a most grave contempt of God and of the Sacrament, and therefore ought to be gravely punished.

**To the arguments:**

1. To that which is objected concerning feigning, it must be said that, when the feigning withdraws, the character supplies the whole of what the Sacrament would have done without feigning[^8].

2. To that which is objected concerning grace, it must be said that grace is not an immediate effect, but [comes about] through the mediation of the character[^9]; and therefore nothing is brought about when the character is not imprinted.

To the last it must be said that he who repeats baptism delivers the Sacrament neither in fact nor by right, because that man is not capable of receiving it; but[^10] when he washes outwardly, if he does so knowingly or negligently, he sins gravely; but if it is done with diligence applied, even though he brings nothing about inwardly, nevertheless he does not sin[^11].
