## *Utrum corpus gloriosum sit passibile ab intrinseco*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 1021 -->

### SECTIO II.
#### *De gloria corporis in speciali.*

Consequenter quaeritur de secundo articulo principali, scilicet de gloria corporis in speciali, quae consistit in quatuor dotibus. Et circa hoc, secundum quod quatuor sunt dotes, quatuor principaliter quaeruntur.

*Primo* quaeritur de dote impassibilitatis.
*Secundo*, de dote claritatis.
*Tertio*, de dote subtilitatis.
*Quarto*, de dote agilitatis.

### ARTICULUS I.
#### *De dote impassibilitatis.*

Circa primum quaeruntur duo. *Primo* quaeritur, utrum corpus sit passibile ab intrinseco. *Secundo*, utrum sit passibile passione veniente ab extrinseco.

### QUAESTIO I.
#### *Utrum corpus gloriosum sit passibile ab intrinseco.*

Quantum ergo ad primum proceditur sic, et ostenditur, quod non sit passibile ab intrinseco.

1. Augustinus, vigesimo secundo *de Civitate Dei*[^p1021-4]: «Aberit a corporibus nostris omnis deformitas, omnis infirmitas, omnis corruptio».

2. Item, *ratione* videtur: quia passio corporis contristat animam; *corpus enim, quod corrumpitur, aggravat animam*[^p1021-5]; sed gaudium beatitudinis est impermixtum tristitiae: ergo etc.

3. Item, si interius pateretur, ergo aliquando corrumperetur; sed anima gaudet de unione sui ad corpus, sed gaudium beatitudinis est perpetuum et securum[^p1021-6]; hoc autem non esset, si esset dissolubile: ergo etc.

4. Item, anima gloriosa est perfectissima omnium formarum; sed sic est, quod aliqua forma est ita nobilis, quod omnino terminat appetitum suae materiae, ut forma corporis quinti[^p1021-7]: ergo multo fortius anima gloriosa appetitum sui corporis. Sed forma terminans appetitum materiae facit ipsam incorruptibilem: ergo etc.

<!-- page 1022 -->

**Contra:** 1. Omne compositum ex contrariis est corruptibile et passibile; sed corpus gloriosum est huiusmodi: ergo etc. — Si dicas, quod non manent contraria, quia contraria non sunt secundum substantiam, sed secundum qualitates, et manent secundum substantiam, non secundum qualitates; contra: «A corporibus gloriosis, ut dicit Augustinus[^p1022-1], vitia detrahentur, et natura servabitur»; sed qualitates elementares sunt eis naturales: ergo manebunt. — Si dicas, quod manent secundum essentiam, non secundum actum; contra: unumquodque nobilius est, quando est coniunctum actui, quam quando non: ergo etc. — Si dicas, quod actus ille est imperfectionis; contra: elementa plura impossibile est venire ad constitutionem corporis unius nisi per mixtionem, ergo necesse est, elementa misceri; sed ubi est mixtio, necessario est mutua actio et passio et qualitatum confractio; et ubi haec sunt, elementa sunt in suis actibus[^p1022-2]: ergo etc.

2. Item, corpus gloriosum[^p1022-3] erit nobilioris complexionis quam nunc; sed quanto aliquod corpus elementatum et animatum nobilioris est complexionis, tanto passibilius; quod patet, quia passibilius est corpus animalis quam plantae, et hominis quam bruti animalis: ergo corpus glorificatum magis quam nunc erit passibile.

3. Item, elementa miscentur in corpore illo: aut ergo aequaliter, aut inaequaliter. Si inaequaliter, sed inaequalis commixtio via est ad dissolutionem, ergo illud corpus erit dissolubile et passibile. Si aequaliter, aut adaequatione punctali, aut citra. Si adaequatione punctali: ergo omnia corpora essent eiusdem complexionis et nobilitatis; quod non est verum. Si non punctali: ergo unum aliquo modo excedit reliquum in virtute; sed excessus generat aliquo modo pugnam et litem, et haec dissolutionem[^p1022-4]: ergo etc.

4. Item, aut erit in corpore *calor*, aut *non*. Si *non*, nec operatio animae, cum operatio motus et sensus et vitae sit mediante calore[^p1022-5]. Quodsi *est ibi calor*, sed calor indiget pabulo, quia eius est consumere; sed omne quod indiget pabulo, est corruptibile et passibile: ergo etc.

### Conclusio

> *Corpora glorificata erunt incorruptibilia ab intrinseco; tamen elementa in eis manent secundum substantiam, qualitates et duas tantum operationes.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod omnes concordant in hoc, quod corpora illa sunt incorruptibilia et indefectibilia; sed rationem sumunt ex diversis causis.

Quidam enim dicunt, quod elementa sunt in corpore glorioso secundum *substantiam*, non secundum *qualitates*; et ideo, quia «substantiae nihil est contrarium[^p1022-6]», et ubi nulla contrarietas, non est pugna nec dissolutio; ideo etc. — Sed hoc improbatum est[^p1022-7], quia natura debet remanere.

Alii dicunt, quod sunt ibi secundum *qualitates*; sed qualitates non agunt nec patiuntur, et hoc divina virtute mirabiliter faciente. — Et hoc est iterum improbatum, quia, si miscentur, constat, quod franguntur et mutuo agunt et patiuntur.

Tertii dicunt, quod sunt secundum *substantiam* et *qualitates* et *actus*. Sed tamen *actus* nunc *sunt in ipsa mixtione*, sed post *non*, quia in eis dominatur natura caelestis, quae omnino reconciliat elementa[^p1022-8] et quasi naturam elementarem absorbet et transfert totum corpus in naturam caelestem; unde debetur ei locus caelestis et incorruptibilitas. — Sed illud est dubium, quomodo natura caelestis sit de compositione corporis nostri; non enim videtur componi nisi ex quatuor elementis[^p1022-9].

Et ideo aliter dicendum, quod elementa manent in corpore illo secundum *substantiam* et *qualitates* et *operationes*. Sed attendendum, quod quadruplex est operatio: una quantum ad *constitutionem*, et haec est mixtionis; alia quantum ad *conservationem*, et haec est ministerialis delatio caloris vitalis per corpus; tertia est pugnae *ordinans ad dissolutionem*; quarta exterioris impressionis, per quam alicuius elementi *operatio intenditur et pugna oritur*. Primae duae operationes manebunt; secundae duae non manebunt, quae[^p1022-10] ordinant ad dissolutionem; et ideo corpus erit indissolubile.

Ratio autem, quare non manent, est triplex. Prima est temperata mixtio, confringens contrarietatem; secunda est elementarium appetituum quietatio; tertia est animae influentis conservatio. Et ratione primi non pugnant, ratione secundi non possunt pugnare, ratione tertii necesse habent quiescere[^p1023-1].

<!-- page 1023 -->

**Ad argumenta:**

*Ad 1.* Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur, quod compositum ex contrariis est dissolubile; dicendum, quod illud debet intelligi, quando contraria habent contrarios appetitus et interminatos[^p1023-2].

*Ad 2.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod corpus nobilioris complexionis facilius patitur; dicendum, quod hoc non est verum, simpliciter loquendo, immo contrarium, quia est fortioris virtutis, nec habet locum in homine nisi ratione poenalitatum inflictarum.

*Ad 3.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, utrum in puncto; dicendum, quod non oportet; nec tamen mutuo pugnant, quia neutrum praevalet, licet aliquo modo excedat[^p1023-3]. Praeterea, non appetit se multiplicare nec ab illo dividi; et ideo, etiam si excedat, non corrumpit aliud.

*Ad 4.* Ad illud quod obiicitur de calore, dicendum, quod ex complexione inaequali nascitur calor elementaris consumens, ex complexione vero aequali calor caelestis conservans; et ideo, quia tunc elementa erunt perfecta et contemperata, calor vitalis erit assimilatus naturae caelesti; et ideo non consumet nec indigebit pabulo.

### Scholion

**I.** De *impassibilitatis* dote plures Lombardi commentatores tractant supra d. 44. Cfr. etiam Alex. Hal., S. p. III. q. 21. et 22, ubi, agens de transfiguratione et resurrectione Christi, plura ad rem propositam profert. — S. Thom. (d. 44. q. 2. a. 1. quaestiunc. 1; Suppl. q. 82. a. 1.) easdem primas tres opiniones circa *rationem* huius dotis recenset et improbat; sed pro *causa* impassibilitatis horum corporum hic tantum affert *perfectum dominium animae super corpus*, quo ligatae maneant qualitates elementorum, ut nunquam exeant in actum. Alibi (S. III. q. 45. a. 2.) videtur docere, hanc dotem esse *qualitatem immanentem*. Similia docet Richardus a Med. (hic a. 4. q. 2.). Scotus autem (hic q. 13.) vult, quod ex parte susceptivi non potest inveniri aliqua causa *intrinseca* huius impassibilitatis (n. 4.), nec ex dominio animae super corpus, vel aliqua dote in corpore (n. 6. 7.), sed quod causa est voluntas divina, non coagens causae secundae corruptivae (n. 9.). Idem sentit Durand. (d. 44. q. 4. n. 14.). S. Bonav. quadam via media incedit, etiam ex parte corporis dispositiones ponens; de quo cfr. etiam supra sect. I. a. 1. q. 2. — Hic docetur pluralitas formarum substantialium in corpore. — De seq. q. alii non tractant specialiter.

De toto hoc articulo praeter citatos: B. Albert., d. 44. a. 13. 14. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 4. a. 1. quaestiunc. 1. 2. — Dionys. Carth., hic q. 7.

**II.** Quoad dotem *claritatis* (a. 2.) S. Thom. (d. 44. q. 2. a. 4. quaestiunc. 1-3; Suppl. q. 85. a. 1-3.) ob auctoritatem Gregorii (arg. 1.) putat, probabilius esse, quod corpora gloriosa simul forent clara et pervia. Hoc non placet Scoto (hic q. 15.) nec Richardo a Med. (hic a. 4. q. 6.) nec Durando (d. 44. q. 8.). — Iterum controvertitur, utrum haec claritas fiat *effective* ab anima per quandam in corpus redundantiam, an vero tantum immediate a Deo. Primum docet S. Thomas (locis citt. et IV. c. Gent. c. 86.), et problematice favet Richardus; secundum praefert Scotus cum Durando et non paucis aliis.

Quoad seq. (2.) quaestionem communiter posteriores cum Praepositivo docent, Beatum posse et ostendere et occultare claritatem suam, et hoc, secundum S. Thomam, propter perfectum dominium animae super suum corpus; secundum Scotum, propter cooperantem actionem Dei.

De utraque huius articuli quaestione praeter locos citt. B. Albert., d. 44. a. 27-31.

## English
<!-- page 1021 -->

### SECTION II.
#### *On the glory of the body in particular.*

Next there is inquiry concerning the second principal article, namely concerning the glory of the body in particular, which consists in four dowries. And concerning this, according as there are four dowries, four things are principally asked.

*First*, there is inquiry concerning the dowry of impassibility.
*Second*, concerning the dowry of clarity.
*Third*, concerning the dowry of subtlety.
*Fourth*, concerning the dowry of agility.

### ARTICLE I.
#### *On the dowry of impassibility.*

Concerning the first, two things are asked. *First* it is asked whether the body is passible from within. *Second*, whether it is passible by a passion coming from without.

### QUESTION I.
#### *Whether the glorified body is passible from within.*

As to the first, then, one proceeds thus, and it is shown that it is not passible from within.

1. Augustine, in the twenty-second book *On the City of God*[^p1021-4]: «There will be absent from our bodies every deformity, every infirmity, every corruption».

2. Likewise, it seems so *by reason*: because the passion of the body saddens the soul; for *the body which is corrupted weighs down the soul*[^p1021-5]; but the joy of beatitude is unmixed with sadness: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, if it were to suffer inwardly, then at some time it would be corrupted; but the soul rejoices in its union to the body, and the joy of beatitude is perpetual and secure[^p1021-6]; but this would not be so, if it were dissoluble: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, the glorified soul is the most perfect of all forms; but the case is such that some form is so noble that it wholly terminates the appetite of its matter, as the form of the fifth body[^p1021-7]: therefore much more strongly does the glorified soul terminate the appetite of its body. But a form terminating the appetite of matter makes it incorruptible: therefore etc.

<!-- page 1022 -->

**On the contrary:** 1. Everything composed of contraries is corruptible and passible; but the glorified body is of this sort: therefore etc. — If you say that the contraries do not remain, because contraries are not according to substance but according to qualities, and they remain according to substance, not according to qualities; on the contrary: «From glorified bodies, as Augustine says[^p1022-1], the vices will be taken away, and the nature will be preserved»; but the elemental qualities are natural to them: therefore they will remain. — If you say that they remain according to essence, not according to act; on the contrary: each thing is nobler when it is joined to act than when it is not: therefore etc. — If you say that that act belongs to imperfection; on the contrary: it is impossible for several elements to come to the constitution of one body except through mixture, therefore it is necessary that the elements be mixed; but where there is mixture, there is necessarily mutual action and passion and a breaking of the qualities; and where these are, the elements are in their acts[^p1022-2]: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, the glorified body[^p1022-3] will be of a nobler complexion than now; but the nobler the complexion of any elemented and animated body is, the more passible it is; which is plain, because the body of an animal is more passible than that of a plant, and that of a man than that of a brute animal: therefore the glorified body will be more passible than it is now.

3. Likewise, the elements are mixed in that body: therefore either equally or unequally. If unequally — but unequal commixture is a road to dissolution — therefore that body will be dissoluble and passible. If equally, then either by a punctual equalization or short of it. If by a punctual equalization: then all bodies would be of the same complexion and nobility; which is not true. If not punctually: then one exceeds the other in some way in power; but excess generates in some way conflict and strife, and this generates dissolution[^p1022-4]: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, either there will be *heat* in the body, or *not*. If *not*, then neither will there be operation of the soul, since the operation of motion and of sense and of life is by means of heat[^p1022-5]. But if *there is heat there* — heat needs fuel, since it belongs to it to consume; but everything that needs fuel is corruptible and passible: therefore etc.

### Conclusion

> *Glorified bodies will be incorruptible from within; yet the elements remain in them according to substance, qualities, and two operations only.*

**I respond:** It must be said that all agree in this, that those bodies are incorruptible and indefectible; but they take the ground of it from different causes.

For some say that the elements are in the glorified body according to *substance*, not according to *qualities*; and therefore, because «nothing is contrary to substance[^p1022-6]», and where there is no contrariety there is neither conflict nor dissolution; therefore etc. — But this has been disproved[^p1022-7], because the nature ought to remain.

Others say that they are there according to *qualities*; but the qualities neither act nor are acted upon, and this by the divine power working marvellously. — And this too has been disproved, because, if they are mixed, it is established that they are broken and mutually act and are acted upon.

A third group say that they are there according to *substance* and *qualities* and *acts*. Yet nevertheless the *acts* now *are in the mixture itself*, but afterwards *not*, because in them the celestial nature dominates, which wholly reconciles the elements[^p1022-8] and as it were absorbs the elemental nature and transfers the whole body into a celestial nature; whence a celestial place and incorruptibility are due to it. — But that is doubtful, how the celestial nature should belong to the composition of our body; for it does not seem to be composed except out of the four elements[^p1022-9].

And therefore it must be said otherwise, that the elements remain in that body according to *substance* and *qualities* and *operations*. But it must be noted that operation is fourfold: one as regards *constitution*, and this is the operation of mixture; another as regards *conservation*, and this is the ministerial conveying of vital heat through the body; the third is that of conflict, *ordering toward dissolution*; the fourth is that of external impression, through which *the operation of some element is intensified and conflict arises*. The first two operations will remain; the second two will not remain, which[^p1022-10] order toward dissolution; and therefore the body will be indissoluble.

But the reason why they do not remain is threefold. The first is the tempered mixture, breaking contrariety; the second is the quieting of the elemental appetites; the third is the conservation of the soul flowing in. And by reason of the first they do not fight, by reason of the second they cannot fight, by reason of the third they must be at rest[^p1023-1].

<!-- page 1023 -->

**To the arguments:**

*To 1.* To that, then, which is objected, that what is composed of contraries is dissoluble; it must be said that this ought to be understood when the contraries have contrary and unbounded appetites[^p1023-2].

*To 2.* To that which is objected, that a body of nobler complexion suffers more easily; it must be said that this is not true, speaking simply, but rather the contrary, because it is of stronger power, and it has place in man only by reason of the penalties inflicted.

*To 3.* To that which is objected, whether [the equalization is] at a point; it must be said that this is not required; nor yet do they mutually fight, because neither prevails, although in some way it exceeds[^p1023-3]. Moreover, it does not seek to multiply itself nor to be divided from the other; and therefore, even if it exceeds, it does not corrupt the other.

*To 4.* To that which is objected concerning heat, it must be said that from an unequal complexion is born an elemental consuming heat, but from an equal complexion a celestial conserving heat; and therefore, because the elements will then be perfect and duly tempered, the vital heat will be assimilated to the celestial nature; and therefore it will neither consume nor need fuel.

### Scholion

**I.** Concerning the dowry of *impassibility* many commentators on Lombard treat above at d. 44. Cf. also Alexander of Hales, *Summa*, p. III, q. 21 and 22, where, treating of the transfiguration and resurrection of Christ, he brings forward much bearing on the matter proposed. — St. Thomas (d. 44, q. 2, a. 1, quaestiuncula 1; *Suppl.* q. 82, a. 1) reviews and rejects these same first three opinions concerning the *ground* of this dowry; but for the *cause* of the impassibility of these bodies he here adduces only the *perfect dominion of the soul over the body*, by which the qualities of the elements remain bound, so that they never issue into act. Elsewhere (*Summa* III, q. 45, a. 2) he seems to teach that this dowry is an *immanent quality*. Richard of Middleton teaches the like (here a. 4, q. 2). Scotus, however (here q. 13), holds that on the part of the recipient no *intrinsic* cause of this impassibility can be found (n. 4), nor from the dominion of the soul over the body, nor from any dowry in the body (nn. 6, 7), but that the cause is the divine will, not co-acting with the second corruptive cause (n. 9). Durandus thinks the same (d. 44, q. 4, n. 14). St. Bonaventure proceeds by a certain middle way, positing dispositions also on the part of the body; on which cf. also above, sect. I, a. 1, q. 2. — Here the plurality of substantial forms in the body is taught. — On the following question the others do not treat specially.

On this whole article, besides those cited: Bl. Albert, d. 44, a. 13, 14. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 4, a. 1, quaestiunculae 1, 2. — Denis the Carthusian, here q. 7.

**II.** As to the dowry of *clarity* (a. 2), St. Thomas (d. 44, q. 2, a. 4, quaestiunculae 1–3; *Suppl.* q. 85, a. 1–3), on the authority of Gregory (arg. 1), thinks it more probable that glorified bodies would be at once bright and pervious. This does not please Scotus (here q. 15), nor Richard of Middleton (here a. 4, q. 6), nor Durandus (d. 44, q. 8). — Again it is disputed whether this clarity comes about *effectively* from the soul by a certain redundance into the body, or rather only immediately from God. St. Thomas teaches the former (in the places cited and *Contra Gentiles* IV, c. 86), and Richard favours it problematically; Scotus, with Durandus and not a few others, prefers the latter.

As to the following (second) question, later writers commonly, with Praepositivus, teach that the Blessed can both display and conceal his clarity, and this, according to St. Thomas, on account of the perfect dominion of the soul over its own body; according to Scotus, on account of the co-operating action of God.

On both questions of this article, besides the places cited: Bl. Albert, d. 44, a. 27–31.
