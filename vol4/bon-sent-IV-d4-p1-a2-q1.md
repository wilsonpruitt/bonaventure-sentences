## Latin
<!-- page 100 -->

### ARTICULUS II.
### *De ficte suscipientibus baptismum.*

Consequenter quantum ad secundum articulum quaeritur de ficte accedentibus. Et circa hoc quaeruntur tria. Primo quaeritur, utrum aliquis invitus sive coactus suscipiat Sacramentum baptismi. Secundo, utrum fictus recipiat Sacramentum et non rem. Tertio, utrum, recedente fictione, baptismus habeat in eo plenam efficaciam.

### QUAESTIO I.
### *Utrum aliquis invitus sive coactus recipiat Sacramentum baptismi.*

Et quod aliquis invitus non recipiat baptismum, videtur:

1. Quia dicitur in decretali Innocentii[^1], quod « si aliquis omnino recusans immergatur, non est baptizatus ».

2. Item, in Sacramento baptismi contrahitur matrimonium spirituale, et desponsatur anima Deo; sed matrimonium spirituale aut tantum, aut plus exigit de consensu, quam matrimonium carnale: ergo si coactio impedit matrimonium carnale[^2], similiter videtur, quod impediat, ne quis suscipiat baptismatis Sacramentum.

3. Item, Sacramentum est donum Dei non solum ratione gratiae gratum facientis, sed etiam characteris impressi; sed Dei gratia non tantum non datur repugnantibus, immo etiam subtrahitur: ergo videtur, quod tali nullo modo conferatur Sacramentum.

4. Item, lex evangelica est lex libertatis[^3]: ergo et Sacramenta evangelica sunt libertatis; sed quod libertatis est libere suscipitur a voluntate: ergo videtur, quod nec de iure nec de facto aliquis Sacramentum baptismi recipiat invitus.

**Contra:**
1. Parvulus et furiosus recipiunt Sacramentum, quantumcumque renitantur, sicut patebit infra[^4]: si ergo hoc verum est, ad receptionem eius non concurrit actus voluntatis: ergo ita recipit invitus, ut voluntarius.

2. Item, baptismus est remedium contra originale[^5]; sed originale contrahit necessario puer, cum nascitur: ergo videtur similiter, quod ad baptismum possit inferri alicui necessitas violentiae.

3. Item, non potest magis cogi voluntas quam comminando vel intentando mortem; sed si aliquis compulsus timore mortis sacrificet[^6], erit idololatra: ergo similiter, si Sacramentum baptismi recipiat timore mortis, Sacramentum verum est.

4. Item, si aliquis baptizatur ex amore male inflammante, baptizatus est de facto: ergo si non minus repugnat baptismo amor male inflammans, quam timor male humilians[^7], si hic baptizatus ex malo amore baptizatus est, similiter videtur, quod baptizatus ex timore sit baptizatus.

> **CONCLUSIO.**
> Deficiente in adultis penitus intentione, non recipitur Sacramentum, ut in invito ob coactionem sufficientem et in illudente.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod coactio duplex est, scilicet *sufficiens* et *inducens*[^8]. Si aliquis mergatur coactione *sufficiente* — utpote quia violenter acceptus est et, ipso renitente manibus et pedibus, mersus est — talis nullo modo baptizatus est, quia nullo modo fuit in eo consensus nec voluntas, immo

<!-- page 101 -->

fuit omnino repugnans. — Si autem aliquis immergatur coactione *inducente*, utpote minis vel flagellis, ita quod magis vult se subiicere Sacramento Ecclesiae quam morte perire; dico, quod aut intendit alios *illudere* et mortem evadere, et tunc, quia non est intentio, non est Sacramentum, cum nullus dicatur baptizatus esse, qui consentit, se immergi ad iocum; aut ipse vult, illud quod facit Ecclesia circa se fieri[^9] magis, quam velit perire; et tunc, sive credat sive non, sive velit habere gratiam sive non, dicendum, quod recipit Sacramentum, eo quod, quamvis non sit ibi voluntas meritoria, est tamen ibi consensus et voluntas, nec est *simpliciter* involuntarium. Unde quia talis vult exterius Sacramentum recipere, etsi nolit interius aliquid in se fieri[^10], fit tamen, propter hoc quod qui subiicit se causae subiicit se necessario annexo: et ideo secundum canones et doctores theologiae talis indicandus est baptizatus.

1. Ex hoc patet illud quod primo obiicitur; nam ista est intentio Innocentii.

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur de matrimonio carnali, dicendum, quod non est simile: quia enim coacta connubia consueverunt habere malos exitus, ideo sacri canones non indulserunt ipsa. — Alia tamen potest esse ratio huius, ut dicetur infra[^11]. Nihilominus tamen illud verum est, quod non tanta voluntas requiritur in aliquo ad susceptionem baptismi, sicut ad contractionem matrimonii. Si enim pater exponat parvulum ad baptizandum, non potest parvulus, cum ad adultam aetatem venerit, revocare[^12]; non sic est de matrimonio, quia, quantumcumque pater obliget parvulum, nunquam obligatur, nisi consentiat, cum pervenerit ad annos discretionis.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod Sacramentum est donum Dei; dicendum, quod est donum gratiae gratis datae et gratum facientis; quamvis autem donum gratiae gratum facientis non detur habenti duplex cor et duplicem voluntatem[^13], datur tamen donum gratiae gratis datae, quod non exigit tantam dispositionem in suscipiente.

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod lex evangelica est lex libertatis; dicendum, quod verum est, quod de *iure* non datur nec dari debet ei qui libera voluntate non accedit; et ius divinum hoc dictat, et Constantinus[^14] de hoc legem condidit. De *facto* tamen, si fiat, factum est; hoc tamen habet veritatem in re[^15] Sacramenti, quod facit esse in lege libertatis.

1. Ad illud quod obiicitur in contrarium, quod quantumcumque renitenti Sacramentum dari potest, propter parvulum et furiosum; dicendum, quod non est simile: quia illi non habent usum voluntatis, unde non statur appetitui suo, sed voluntati offerentium[^16]. — Tamen de *furioso* distinguitur: quia aut habuit aliquando usum liberi arbitrii, aut *non habuit*. Si *non habuit*, indicandum est sicut de parvulo. Si *habuit* et in illo baptizari voluit, indicandum est, eum baptismum recipere; sed si nullo modo voluit, non est credendum, ipsum recipere Sacramentum.

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod originale contrahit quis invitus; dicendum, quod non est simile: quia originalis contractio est quasi *naturalis*, et ideo inevitabilis; sed remedium baptismi non respicit *naturam*, sed potius *voluntatem personae*; et ideo non est simile[^17].

3, 4. Ad illud quod obiicitur ultimo de comminatione mortis; dicendum, quod maior potest esse coactio quam haec; nam haec est solum *inducens*, non *sufficiens*. Sed cum accipitur, velit nolit, et immergitur, ibi est *sufficiens* coactio. Quamvis enim voluntas non possit cogi *in volendo*, potest tamen simpliciter homini fieri coactio *in actu exteriori*. Unde voluntas *inducta* voluntas est[^18]; et ideo hic imprimitur character, et ibi non. — Et est exemplum in eo qui ponit thura timore mortis, et de eo cuius manus trahitur violenter; primus consentit et peccat et dicitur *sacrificare*, secundus non. Sic qui Sacramentum *inductus* suscipit, dicitur consentire; sive indicatur amore bono vel malo, sive timore bono vel malo, suscipit Sacramentum, quia Sacramenti susceptio est bonis malisve communis[^19]. Et ideo talis voluntas sufficit ad Sacramentum, ut seniores dicunt. — Et sic patet, quibus character baptismalis imprimitur.

### SCHOLION

**I.** *Fictus* a Scholasticis communiter definitur esse is qui aliud exterius praetendit, aliud intendit interius. Hoc potest quoad baptismum esse dupliciter: vel quoad *susceptionem* (sive etiam collationem ex parte baptizantis) ipsius baptismi, dum voluntas contradicit ipsi *veritati* Sacramenti, unde collatio est prorsus inanis (cfr. infra d. 6. p. II. a. 2. q. 1. ad 3.); vel quoad *dispositionem* requisitam, dum *gratia* Sacramenti percipiatur, dum scilicet voluntas contradicit *effectui* Sacramenti. Secunda haec fictio iterum tripliciter esse potest: vel quia fictus accedit *non contritus*, vel *non credens*, vel quia *contemnit* (Alex. Hal., S. p. IV. q. 8. m. 7. a. 2. § 2; cfr. S. Bonav. infra q. 2.). — De baptismo *parvulorum* vide hic dub. 2, et infra p. II. a. 2. et dub. 4.

**II.** Quaestio 1. communissime solvitur secundam distinctionem ab Innocentio IV. (cfr. arg. 1.) factam, scilicet inter modum, quo quis est invitus et non invitus, coactus et non coactus. Caietanus (in S. III. q. 68. a. 7.) tamen opinatur, etiam in adultis sufficere *indifferentiam* voluntatis sive non ponere obicem contrariae voluntatis; quod communiter non approbatur. Quaestio autem de necessitate intentionis in *baptizante* tractatur infra d. 6. p. II. a. 2. — S. Bonav. hic fere repetit dicta Alexandri Hal. — Ut autem praeter characterem etiam *gratia* conferatur (de quo agitur in 2. quaest.), in *adultis*, qui peccata actualia gravia habent, requiritur *attritio*, ut communissime docetur, minime refragante Scoto (cfr. hic q. 3. n. 2. 3.), quidquid nonnulli de eo in contrarium dixerint. Pauci auctores parum probabilius putant, sufficere attritionem *naturalem*. — Plurimi auctores 1. et 2. quaestionem simul tractant. De utraque: Alex. Hal., loc. cit. § 1. 2. — Scot., in utroque Scripto hic q. 3. — S. Thom., hic q. 3. a. 2. quaestiune. 1. 2; S. III. q. 68. a 7. 12, q. 69. a. 9. — B. Albert., hic a. 2. 3. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 1. a. 2. quaestiune. 1. — Richard. a Med., hic q. 1. q. 2. — Durand., hic q. 4. — Dionys. Carth., hic q. 3.

**III.** Quoad 3. quaestionem communius cum S. Augustino tenetur, quod recedente fictione, baptismus valeat ad remissionem peccatorum ante baptismum commissorum. Scot. tamen putat, esse probabilius, quod ipsum *peccatum fictionis* non vi baptismi, sed poenitentiae remittatur. Idem affert ibidem etiam duas alias opiniones, sed ut minus probabiles. — Alii cum Vasquez generatim negaverunt, peccata vi baptismi, recedente fictione, remitti, sed hoc tribuerunt subsequenti *poenitentiae*, quae peccatum *fictionis* remittat *per se*, peccata vero baptismum praecedentia *per accidens*. — De expulsione peccati per poenitentiam cfr. hic dub. 3. — De hac 3. quaestione: Alex. Hal., loc. cit. § 2. in fine. — Scot., hic q. 3. n. 4. seqq. — S. Thom., hic q. 3. a. 2. quaestiune. 3; S. III. q. 69. a. 10. — B. Albert., hic a. 4. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 1. a. 2. quaestiune. 2. — Richard. a Med., loc. cit. — Durand., loc. cit. — Dionys. Carth., hic loc. cit.

## English

### ARTICLE II.
### *On those who receive baptism feignedly.*

Next, as to the second article, there is a question about those who approach feignedly. And concerning this three things are asked. First it is asked whether anyone unwilling or coerced receives the Sacrament of baptism. Second, whether the feigned person receives the Sacrament and not the reality. Third, whether, when the feigning has departed, baptism has its full efficacy in him.

### QUESTION I.
### *Whether anyone unwilling or coerced receives the Sacrament of baptism.*

And that anyone unwilling does not receive baptism, it seems:

1. Because it is said in a decretal of Innocent[^1] that « if anyone, refusing altogether, is immersed, he is not baptized ».

2. Likewise, in the Sacrament of baptism a spiritual matrimony is contracted, and the soul is betrothed to God; but spiritual matrimony requires either as much, or more, of consent than carnal matrimony: therefore if coercion impedes carnal matrimony[^2], it seems likewise that it impedes anyone from receiving the Sacrament of baptism.

3. Likewise, the Sacrament is a gift of God not only by reason of grace making pleasing, but also of the character imprinted; but the grace of God is not only not given to those resisting, but is even withdrawn: therefore it seems that to such a one the Sacrament is in no way conferred.

4. Likewise, the evangelical law is a law of liberty[^3]: therefore the evangelical Sacraments are also of liberty; but what is of liberty is freely received by the will: therefore it seems that neither by right nor in fact does anyone receive the Sacrament of baptism unwilling.

**On the contrary:**
1. An infant and a madman receive the Sacrament, however much they resist, as will be evident below[^4]: if therefore this is true, the act of the will does not concur in its reception: therefore one receives it unwilling, just as a willing person does.

2. Likewise, baptism is a remedy against original sin[^5]; but a child necessarily contracts original sin when he is born: therefore it seems likewise that a necessity of violence can be applied to someone for baptism.

3. Likewise, the will cannot be coerced more than by threatening or intending death; but if anyone, compelled by fear of death, sacrifices[^6], he will be an idolater: therefore likewise, if he receives the Sacrament of baptism through fear of death, the Sacrament is true.

4. Likewise, if anyone is baptized out of an evilly inflaming love, he is in fact baptized: therefore since love evilly inflaming resists baptism no less than fear evilly humbling[^7], if this one baptized out of evil love is baptized, it seems likewise that the one baptized out of fear is baptized.

> **CONCLUSION.**
> When intention is entirely lacking in adults, the Sacrament is not received, as in one unwilling on account of sufficient coercion and in one mocking.

**I respond:** It must be said that coercion is twofold, namely *sufficient* and *inducing*[^8]. If anyone is immersed by *sufficient* coercion — namely because he was violently seized and, while he himself resists with hands and feet, he is immersed — such a one is in no way baptized, because in no way was there in him consent or will, but rather

<!-- page 101 -->

he was altogether resisting. — But if anyone is immersed by *inducing* coercion, namely by threats or scourges, such that he wills rather to subject himself to the Sacrament of the Church than to perish by death; I say that either he intends to *mock* others and to escape death, and then, because there is no intention, it is not a Sacrament, since no one is said to be baptized who consents to be immersed in jest; or he himself wills that what the Church does about him be done[^9] rather than that he should perish; and then, whether he believes or not, whether he wills to have grace or not, it must be said that he receives the Sacrament, because, although there is no meritorious will there, there is nevertheless there consent and will, nor is it *simply* involuntary. Hence, because such a one wills outwardly to receive the Sacrament, even if he is unwilling that anything be done inwardly in him[^10], it is nevertheless done, because he who subjects himself to the cause subjects himself to what is necessarily annexed: and therefore according to the canons and the doctors of theology such a one must be judged baptized.

1. From this is evident that which is objected first; for this is the intention of Innocent.

2. To that which is objected about carnal matrimony, it must be said that it is not a similar case: for because coerced marriages were wont to have bad outcomes, therefore the sacred canons did not allow them. — There can nevertheless be another reason for this, as will be said below[^11]. Nevertheless this is true, that not so great a will is required in someone for the reception of baptism, as for the contracting of matrimony. For if a father presents an infant for baptizing, the infant cannot, when he has come to adult age, revoke it[^12]; it is not so with matrimony, because, however much a father obliges an infant, he is never obliged unless he consents, when he has come to the years of discretion.

3. To that which is objected, that the Sacrament is a gift of God; it must be said that it is a gift of grace freely given and making pleasing; and although the gift of grace making pleasing is not given to one having a double heart and a double will[^13], the gift of grace freely given is nevertheless given, which does not require so great a disposition in the recipient.

4. To that which is objected, that the evangelical law is a law of liberty; it must be said that it is true that by *right* it is not given nor ought to be given to him who does not approach with a free will; and the divine law dictates this, and Constantine[^14] established a law about this. In *fact*, however, if it is done, it is done; this nevertheless has the truth in the reality[^15] of the Sacrament, which makes [it] to be in the law of liberty.

1. To that which is objected on the contrary, that the Sacrament can be given to one however much resisting, on account of the infant and the madman; it must be said that it is not a similar case: because those do not have the use of will, hence one does not abide by their appetite, but by the will of those offering them[^16]. — Nevertheless concerning the *madman* a distinction is made: for either he once had the use of free choice, or he *did not have* it. If he *did not have* it, judgment must be made as about an infant. If he *had* it and in it willed to be baptized, it must be judged that he receives baptism; but if he in no way willed it, it must not be believed that he receives the Sacrament.

2. To that which is objected, that one contracts original sin unwilling; it must be said that it is not a similar case: because the contraction of original sin is as it were *natural*, and therefore inevitable; but the remedy of baptism does not regard *nature*, but rather *the will of the person*; and therefore it is not a similar case[^17].

3, 4. To that which is objected last about the threatening of death; it must be said that there can be a greater coercion than this; for this is only *inducing*, not *sufficient*. But when one is taken, willing or unwilling, and is immersed, there is *sufficient* coercion there. For although the will cannot be coerced *in willing*, coercion can nevertheless simply be applied to a man *in the exterior act*. Hence the *induced* will is a will[^18]; and therefore here the character is imprinted, and there it is not. — And there is an example in one who places incense out of fear of death, and in one whose hand is drawn by force; the first consents and sins and is said to *sacrifice*, the second does not. Thus one who *induced* receives the Sacrament is said to consent; whether he is induced by good or evil love, or by good or evil fear, he receives the Sacrament, because the reception of the Sacrament is common to good and evil alike[^19]. And therefore such a will suffices for the Sacrament, as the elders say. — And thus it is evident to whom the baptismal character is imprinted.

### SCHOLION

**I.** A *feigned person* is commonly defined by the Scholastics as one who pretends one thing outwardly, intends another inwardly. This can be twofold with respect to baptism: either with respect to the *reception* (or also the conferral, on the part of the one baptizing) of baptism itself, when the will contradicts the very *truth* of the Sacrament, whence the conferral is altogether void (cf. below d. 6. p. II. a. 2. q. 1. ad 3.); or with respect to the *disposition* required for the *grace* of the Sacrament to be received, that is, when the will contradicts the *effect* of the Sacrament. This second feigning can again be threefold: either because the feigned person approaches *not contrite*, or *not believing*, or because he *despises* (Alexander of Hales, *Summa* p. IV. q. 8. m. 7. a. 2. § 2; cf. St. Bonaventure below q. 2.). — On the baptism of *infants* see here dub. 2, and below p. II. a. 2. and dub. 4.

**II.** Question 1 is most commonly solved by the distinction made by Innocent IV (cf. arg. 1.), namely between the manner in which one is unwilling and not unwilling, coerced and not coerced. Cajetan (in *Summa* III. q. 68. a. 7.), however, is of the opinion that even in adults the *indifference* of the will suffices, or the not-placing of an obstacle of a contrary will; which is commonly not approved. But the question concerning the necessity of intention in the *one baptizing* is treated below d. 6. p. II. a. 2. — St. Bonaventure here almost repeats the sayings of Alexander of Hales. — But that, besides the character, *grace* also be conferred (which is treated in the 2nd question), in *adults* who have grave actual sins, *attrition* is required, as is most commonly taught, with Scotus by no means opposing (cf. here q. 3. n. 2. 3.), whatever some have said about him to the contrary. A few authors, less probably, think that *natural* attrition suffices. — Most authors treat questions 1 and 2 together. On both: Alexander of Hales, loc. cit. § 1. 2. — Scotus, in both *Scripta* here q. 3. — St. Thomas, here q. 3. a. 2. subquestions 1. 2; *Summa* III. q. 68. a. 7. 12, q. 69. a. 9. — Blessed Albert, here a. 2. 3. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 1. a. 2. subquestion 1. — Richard of Mediavilla, here q. 1. q. 2. — Durandus, here q. 4. — Dionysius the Carthusian, here q. 3.

**III.** As to the 3rd question, it is more commonly held with St. Augustine that, when the feigning has departed, baptism is valid for the remission of sins committed before baptism. Scotus, however, thinks it more probable that the *sin of feigning* itself is remitted not by the power of baptism, but of penance. He brings forward in the same place two other opinions as well, but as less probable. — Others with Vásquez generally denied that sins are remitted by the power of baptism when the feigning departs, but attributed this to a subsequent *penance*, which remits the sin of *feigning* *per se*, and the sins preceding baptism *per accidens*. — On the expulsion of sin through penance cf. here dub. 3. — On this 3rd question: Alexander of Hales, loc. cit. § 2. at the end. — Scotus, here q. 3. n. 4. ff. — St. Thomas, here q. 3. a. 2. subquestion 3; *Summa* III. q. 69. a. 10. — Blessed Albert, here a. 4. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 1. a. 2. subquestion 2. — Richard of Mediavilla, loc. cit. — Durandus, loc. cit. — Dionysius the Carthusian, here loc. cit.
