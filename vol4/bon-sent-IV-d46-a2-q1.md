## Latin
<!-- page 962 -->

### Articulus II. *Quomodo misericordia et veritas eandem respiciant operationem.*

Consequenter quantum ad secundum articulum quaeritur, quomodo misericordia et veritas eandem respiciant operationem. Et circa hoc quaeruntur quatuor.

Primo quaeritur, utrum in aliquo opere Domini sit misericordia et veritas.

Secundo, utrum in eodem opere Domini sit misericordia et veritas.

Et dato, quod in eodem, secundum quod misericordia et veritas dicuntur largissime, tertio quaeritur, utrum secundum quod proprie accipiuntur, concurrant in eodem.

Quarto, utrum misericordia et veritas possint ab invicem in aliquo opere separari.

### Quaestio I. *Utrum in aliquo opere Domini sit misericordia et veritas.*

> The numbered footnotes below correspond to markers in both the Latin body above and the English translation. Quaracchi restarts its footer numbering on every printed page, so the markers here are page-qualified: notes 3 through 11 of printed page 962, and notes 1 through 4 of printed page 963.

Quantum ad primum proceditur sic, et ostenditur, quod in *aliquo* opere Domini sit

1. *Misericordia*, Lucae sexto[^p962-3]: *Estote misericordes, sicut et Pater vester misericors est*: ergo etc.

2. Item, in Psalmo[^p962-4]: *Reddes unicuique iuxta opera sua*; sed hoc est *iustitiae*: ergo etc.

3. Item, *ista duo* decorant rectorem, unde Proverbiorum vigesimo[^p962-5]: *Misericordia et veritas custodiunt regem, et clementia* etc.; sed Deus est rector omnium rerum: ergo etc.

4. Item, *ratione* videtur: quia istae sunt duae virtutes in nobis: ergo habent exemplaria in Deo[^p962-6]; et si hoc: ergo cum nos operemur iuste et misericorditer, ergo et Deus similiter.

**Contra:**

1. Damascenus[^p962-7]: « *Misericordia* est compassio de alienis malis »; sed Deus nihil operatur ex compassione, quia non compatitur: ergo in nullo opere eius concurrit misericordia.

2. Item, *iustitia* est, cum fit quod *debet* fieri[^p962-8]; sed Deus nihil operatur ex *debito*, quia nulli est obnoxius: ergo nihil iuste, et sic etc.

3. Item, si *misericordia* et *veritas* est conditio Dei operantis; cum ergo conditio operantis reluceat in opere, ut patet — quia enim bonus est, ideo bona sunt facta[^p962-9] — ergo pari ratione, quia iustus est, omnia facta sunt iusta: ergo et lapis iustus, et lapis misericors est. Quod si non conceditur: ergo nec Deus operatur secundum misericordiam, aut veritatem.

4. Item, nihil ponendum est in Deo, quod non debeat poni in summo; sed iustitia non debet poni in aliquo in summo, unde Ecclesiastis septimo[^p962-10]: *Noli esse multum iustus*.

### Conclusio

> *In operibus Dei est misericordia et veritas.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod misericordia et veritas est[^p962-11] in opere creaturae rationalis; et hoc quidem nobilitatis est: ergo multo fortius et nobilius in operibus Dei.

*Ad 1. 2.* Ad illud ergo quod primo obiicitur quod in Deo non cadit compassio nec debitum; dicendum,

<!-- page 963 -->

quod misericordia et veritas sive iustitia — quia veritas pro iustitia accipitur[^p963-1] — in *creatura* duo dicunt, scilicet *effluxum bonitatis* in alterum secundum merita, vel supra, et in eo qui effluit, *compassionem*, vel *debitum*; et *primum* quidem *nobilitatis* est, *secundum* vero *imperfectionis* est. Et quia omne quod *nobilitatis* est, Deo attribuendum est, quod vero *imperfectionis* est non est ei attribuendum; ideo est in eo misericordia et iustitia quantum ad *primum*, sicut probant rationes primae; non quantum ad *secundum*, sicut probant rationes sequentes.

*Ad 3.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod tunc omnia opera Dei deberent dici *iusta*; dicendum, quod est conditio operantis, quae respicit ipsum secundum *naturam*, et est conditio, quae respicit ipsum secundum *actum*; « *bonum* enim respicit naturam, ut dicit Boethius in libro de Hebdomadibus[^p963-2], sed *iustum* dicit actum ». Conditio ergo illa, quae respicit *naturam* operantis, communicatur rei productae, ut Dei natura bona, et natura operati bona. Sed conditio, quae respicit *actum*, communicatur non in natura *rei productae*, sed ipsi *productioni*; unde cum Deus sit iustus, non oportet, quod omnis *creatura* vel *factum* sit iustum, sed quod ipsa *factio* sit iusta. Et sic patet illud.

Et ex hoc patet illud quod consuevit quaeri, quare *misericordia vel veritas* magis dicatur conditio operis divini quam *bonitas vel potentia*. Dico enim, quod illa respiciunt *naturam*, sed ista respiciunt *actum*; et ideo non tantum *conditiones operis*, sed etiam *viae Domini* appellantur in Psalmo[^p963-3]: *Universae* etc.

*Ad 4.* Ad illud quod obiicitur ultimo de termino, dicendum, quod terminus in summitate alicuius potest attendi propter *excessum*, vel propter *accessum ad medium*. Et primum in iustitia reprehenditur, secundum commendatur[^p963-4]; et ratione huius dicitur *iustissimus*, non ratione primi.

### Scholion

**I.** Multas quaestiones de attributis divinis iustitiae et misericordiae solvit S. Thomas (hic q. 1. et 2.); cfr. etiam Sum. I. q. 21. a. 1-4; item Alex. Hal., S. p. 1. q. 39. per totam; Scot., hic q. 1-3. — Nemini dubium esse potest, in Deo esse, quidquid perfectionis est in proprietatibus iustitiae et misericordiae, econtra ab eo amovendum esse quod sonat in imperfectionem (q. 1. ad 1.). Attamen in applicanda hac regula ad *diversas iustitiae species* (ut sunt *legalis*, *distributiva*, *vindicativa* et praesertim *commutativa*), utrum et quomodo sint in Deo, non eadem via speculative procedunt theologi. — Auctor noster distinguit triplicem sensum utriusque termini, scil. iustitiae et misericordiae; porro docet, *large* loquendo, utramque esse in omnibus Dei operibus (q. 1. 2.), sed *proprie* loquendo, esse tum in iustificatione (q. 3.) tum in remuneratione (q. 4). — Quoad 4. quaestionem antiqui doctores, imprimis Alex. Hal., Ss. Thom. et Bonav. docent, Deum etiam in damnatis punire citra condignum (cfr. et supra a. 1. q. 2, d. 15. p. 1. dub. 1; S. Thom., hic q. 2. a. 2; S. I. q. 21. a. 4. ad 1.); Estius autem (hic § 4.) multis argumentis probare nititur, oppositam sententiam, iam a Magistro (c. 3.) relatam, esse saltem probabilem. — Quatuor quaestiones auctoris nostri non ita ab aliis distinguuntur, sed tamen in re omnes idem fere docent.

De toto 2. articulo: Alex. Hal., loc. cit. m. 3. a. 4. — Scot., hic q. 4. — S. Thom., hic praecipue q. 1. a. 1, q. 2. a. 2. — B. Albert., hic a. 5. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 3. a. 1, q. 2. a. 2. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 2. q. 3. — Durand., hic q. 1. — Dionys. Carth., hic q. 3.

## English

### Article II. *How mercy and truth regard the same operation.*

Next, as to the second article it is asked how mercy and truth regard the same operation. And concerning this four things are asked.

First it is asked whether in any work of the Lord there are mercy and truth.

Second, whether in the same work of the Lord there are mercy and truth.

And granted that they are in the same work, insofar as mercy and truth are said most broadly, third it is asked whether, insofar as they are taken properly, they concur in the same work.

Fourth, whether mercy and truth can be separated from one another in any work.

### Question I. *Whether in any work of the Lord there are mercy and truth.*

As to the first the procedure is as follows, and it is shown that in *some* work of the Lord there is

1. *Mercy*, Luke six[^p962-3]: *Be merciful, as your Father also is merciful*: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, in the Psalm[^p962-4]: *Thou wilt render to every man according to his works*; but this belongs to *justice*: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, *these two* adorn a ruler, whence Proverbs twenty[^p962-5]: *Mercy and truth preserve the king, and clemency* etc.; but God is the ruler of all things: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, it is seen *by reason*: because these are two virtues in us: therefore they have their exemplars in God[^p962-6]; and if this is so: then, since we work justly and mercifully, therefore God likewise.

**On the contrary:**

1. Damascene[^p962-7]: « *Mercy* is compassion over another's evils »; but God works nothing out of compassion, because He does not suffer with another: therefore in no work of His does mercy concur.

2. Likewise, there is *justice* when what *ought* to be done is done[^p962-8]; but God works nothing out of *debt*, because He is bound to no one: therefore He does nothing justly, and so on.

3. Likewise, if *mercy* and *truth* are a condition of God working; since, then, the condition of the one working shines out in the work, as is plain — for because He is good, therefore the things made are good[^p962-9] — therefore by parity of reasoning, because He is just, all things made are just: therefore even a stone is just, and a stone is merciful. But if this is not granted: then neither does God work according to mercy or truth.

4. Likewise, nothing is to be posited in God which ought not to be posited in the highest degree; but justice ought not to be posited in anything in the highest degree, whence Ecclesiastes seven[^p962-10]: *Be not overmuch just*.

### Conclusion

> *In the works of God there are mercy and truth.*

**Respondeo:** It must be said that mercy and truth are[^p962-11] in the work of the rational creature; and this indeed belongs to nobility: therefore much more strongly and more nobly are they in the works of God.

*Ad 1, 2.* To that, then, which is objected first, that in God there falls neither compassion nor debt; it must be said

<!-- page 963 -->

that mercy and truth, or justice — since truth is taken here for justice[^p963-1] — say two things in the *creature*, namely an *outflow of goodness* upon another according to merits, or above them, and, in him who pours forth, *compassion* or *debt*; and the *first* indeed belongs to *nobility*, but the *second* belongs to *imperfection*. And because everything that belongs to *nobility* is to be attributed to God, whereas what belongs to *imperfection* is not to be attributed to Him; therefore there are in Him mercy and justice as regards the *first*, as the first arguments prove; not as regards the *second*, as the following arguments prove.

*Ad 3.* To that which is objected, that then all the works of God would have to be called *just*; it must be said that there is a condition of the one working which regards him according to *nature*, and there is a condition which regards him according to *act*; « for *the good* regards nature, as Boethius says in the book *On the Hebdomads*[^p963-2], but *the just* bespeaks act ». That condition, therefore, which regards the *nature* of the one working is communicated to the thing produced, as God's nature is good and the nature of what is worked is good. But the condition which regards *act* is communicated not in the nature of *the thing produced*, but to the *production* itself; whence, since God is just, it is not necessary that every *creature* or *thing made* be just, but that the *making* itself be just. And thus that is plain.

And from this is plain that which is customarily asked, why *mercy or truth* is said to be a condition of the divine work rather than *goodness or power*. For I say that the latter regard *nature*, but the former regard *act*; and therefore they are called not only *conditions of the work*, but also *the ways of the Lord* in the Psalm[^p963-3]: *All the ways* etc.

*Ad 4.* To that which is objected last concerning the limit, it must be said that a limit at the summit of anything can be regarded on account of *excess*, or on account of *approach to the mean*. And the first is reproved in justice, the second commended[^p963-4]; and by reason of this one is called *most just*, not by reason of the first.

### Scholion

**I.** Many questions concerning the divine attributes of justice and mercy St. Thomas resolves (here q. 1 and 2); cf. also *Summa* I q. 21 a. 1–4; likewise Alexander of Hales, S. p. 1 q. 39 throughout; Scotus, here q. 1–3. — It can be doubtful to no one that there is in God whatever of perfection is in the properties of justice and mercy, and conversely that what sounds of imperfection is to be removed from Him (q. 1 ad 1). Nevertheless in applying this rule to the *diverse species of justice* (such as *legal*, *distributive*, *vindicative* and especially *commutative*), whether and how they are in God, theologians do not proceed speculatively by the same road. — Our author distinguishes a threefold sense of each term, namely of justice and of mercy; he further teaches that, speaking *broadly*, each is in all the works of God (q. 1, 2), but, speaking *properly*, that they are both in justification (q. 3) and in remuneration (q. 4). — As to the fourth question the ancient doctors, especially Alexander of Hales, Saints Thomas and Bonaventure, teach that God punishes even the damned short of what is condign (cf. also above a. 1 q. 2; d. 15 p. 1 dub. 1; St. Thomas, here q. 2 a. 2; S. I q. 21 a. 4 ad 1); Estius however (here § 4) strives by many arguments to prove that the opposite opinion, already reported by the Master (c. 3), is at least probable. — The four questions of our author are not distinguished from the others in this way, yet in substance they all teach nearly the same thing.

On the whole of the 2nd article: Alexander of Hales, loc. cit. m. 3 a. 4. — Scotus, here q. 4. — St. Thomas, here especially q. 1 a. 1, q. 2 a. 2. — Blessed Albert, here a. 5. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 3 a. 1, q. 2 a. 2. — Richard of Middleton, here a. 2 q. 3. — Durandus, here q. 1. — Dionysius the Carthusian, here q. 3.
