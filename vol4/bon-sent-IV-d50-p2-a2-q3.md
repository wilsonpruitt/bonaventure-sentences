## Latin
<!-- page 1053 -->

### Quaestio III. *Utrum vermis ille sit invariabilis.*

Tertio et ultimo quaeritur, utrum vermis ille sit invariabilis. Et quod sic, videtur:

1. Quia culpa est invariabilis; et poena respicit culpam: ergo cum hic vermis sit poena, patet etc.

2. Item, ignis continue affligit et aequaliter: ergo similiter videtur, quod et vermis.

3. Item, omne variabile terminabile; sed vermis ille est interminabilis[^p1053-3]: ergo etc.

**Contra:**

1. Vermis est ex recordatione peccati; sed non simul peccata recolent, cum non sint deiformes: ergo successive. Sed maiori poena[^p1053-4] maior debetur remorsus, et ubi hoc, ibi variabilitas: ergo etc.

2. Item, si vermis est, corrodit; sed quando aliquid diutius roditur, magis debilitatur et magis gravatur: ergo poena malorum semper crescit.

3. Item, si semper rodit; cum non sit infinitum bonum, quod adimit, ergo aliquando adimeret totum; sed hoc est impossibile: ergo etc.

### Conclusio

> *Vermis ille non variatur.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod iusto Dei iudicio, sicut dictum est supra[^p1053-5], fiet, ut mali simul omnia peccata memorentur, ut de omnibus continue torqueantur; et ideo vermis est pro omnibus et non intensior nunc quam ante.

**Ad argumenta ad oppositum:**

*Ad 3.* Ad illud quod obiicitur de ademptione[^p1053-6], dicendum, quod sicut *egressus continuus* alicuius ab aliquo est dupliciter: vel per *innovationem* secundum partem et partem, ut rivus a fonte, et motus a motore; aut *eiusdem continuatione*, ut ramus a radice, et radius a sole: sic *ademptio continua* vel per *novi ablationem*, sicut ignis comburit ligna; vel per *eius continuationem*, sicut ignis infernalis corpus, et dolor animum; et sic vermis continue rodit. Et illud melius supra[^p1053-7] patet de poena ignis; et per hunc modum intelligendum est de poena vermis[^p1053-8].

## English

### Question III. *Whether that worm is unchangeable.*

Third and last it is asked whether that worm is unchangeable. And that it is, seems to be the case:

1. Because guilt is unchangeable; and punishment regards guilt: therefore, since this worm is a punishment, it is evident, etc.

2. Likewise, fire afflicts continuously and equally: therefore it likewise seems that the worm does also.

3. Likewise, everything changeable is terminable; but that worm is unterminable[^p1053-3]: therefore, etc.

**On the contrary:**

1. The worm arises from the recollection of sin; but they will not recall their sins all at once, since they are not deiform: therefore successively. But to a greater punishment[^p1053-4] a greater remorse is owed, and where this is, there is changeableness: therefore, etc.

2. Likewise, if it is a worm, it gnaws; but when something is gnawed at for a longer time, it is more weakened and more burdened: therefore the punishment of the wicked always increases.

3. Likewise, if it always gnaws: since the good which it takes away is not infinite, therefore at some time it would take away the whole; but this is impossible: therefore, etc.

### Conclusion

> *That worm does not vary.*

**I respond:** It must be said that by the just judgement of God, as was said above[^p1053-5], it will come about that the wicked remember all their sins at once, so that they may be continuously tormented over all of them; and therefore the worm is for them all, and is not more intense now than before.

**To the arguments on the contrary:**

*To 3.* To that which is objected concerning the taking away[^p1053-6], it must be said that, just as the *continuous going forth* of something from something else is twofold — either by *renewal* part by part, as a stream from a spring, and motion from a mover; or by *the continuation of the same thing*, as a branch from a root, and a ray from the sun — so *continuous taking away* is either by *the removal of something new*, as fire burns up wood; or by *the continuation of the same thing*, as infernal fire [consumes] the body, and grief the mind; and in this way the worm continuously gnaws. And that is better made plain above[^p1053-7] concerning the punishment of fire; and in this manner it is to be understood concerning the punishment of the worm[^p1053-8].
