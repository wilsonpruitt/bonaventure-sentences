# d.27 a1-q1
## *Utrum matrimonium sit coniunctio maris et feminae*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 675 -->

### ARTICULUS I.
### *De matrimonii quidditate.*

### QUAESTIO I.
### *Utrum matrimonium sit coniunctio maris et feminae.*

Quantum ad primum quaeritur, utrum matrimonium sit coniunctio maris et feminae. Et quod sic, videtur:

1. Per rationem, quam ponit Magister in littera[^p675-2]: «Matrimonium, inquit, est maris et feminae coniunctio».

2. Et quod ita sit, ostenditur *ratione*. Solo viro existente, non est matrimonium; similiter sola muliere existente, non est matrimonium: ergo matrimonium est in simultate et in coniunctione unius ad alterum.

3. Item, nihil absolutum est unum[^p675-3] in duobus subiectis; sed matrimonium unum est in viro et femina: ergo non dicit quid absolutum, sed relatum. Sed illud, quodcumque sit, cum non sit disparitas, sed conformitas, non est disiunctio, sed coniunctio.

4. Item, illud, ad quod consequuntur ista tria bona, scilicet fides, proles et Sacramentum, est recte matrimonium; hoc autem est coniunctio maris et feminae, ubi est *fides* in singularitate, *proles* in fecunditate et *significatio* in insolubilitate[^p675-4]: ergo etc.

**Contra:** 1. Multiplicato superiori essentiali, multiplicatur inferius[^p675-5]: ergo si matrimonium est coniunctio, quoties vir et uxor coniunguntur, toties matrimonium contrahitur, et ita inter eundem virum et eandem mulierem erunt plura matrimonia; quod si falsum est: ergo etc.

2. Item, nullum Sacramentum iteratur super eandem materiam: ergo si matrimonium est coniunctio viri cum muliere, ex quo semel coniungitur, non debet amplius coniungi cum eadem. Sed hoc est falsum et inconveniens: ergo etc.

3. Item, omne Sacramentum est signum sensibile; sed nulla relatio est signum sensibile, coniunctio autem maris et feminae est relatio: ergo coniunctio talis non est signum, ergo nec Sacramentum; ergo *a simplici conversa*[^p675-6], nullum Sacramentum est coniunctio, ergo nec matrimonium.

<!-- page 676 -->
4. Item, si matrimonium est coniunctio, aut ergo *corporum*, aut *animorum*. Non *animorum*, quia tunc matrimonium esset in patria; non *corporum*, quia tunc inter Mariam et Ioseph non fuisset coniugium.

5. Item, sive sit coniunctio *corporum* secundum commixtionem, sive *animorum* secundum consensum; contingit, talem coniunctionem removeri per odium: ergo et matrimonium solvi et destrui. Sed hoc falsum est, ut iam patebit[^p676-1]: ergo matrimonium non est coniunctio.

### CONCLUSIO.

> *Matrimonium recte dicitur coniunctio maris et feminae.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod matrimonium secundum *genus remotum* est *signum*; specificatio autem vel descensus ad *genus inferius* accipitur secundum comparationem ad *signatum*; unde cum signatum in hoc Sacramento sit *coniunctio Christi et Ecclesiae*, et coniunctio non potest melius significari quam per coniunctionem exteriorem[^p676-2], sicut baptismi ablutio per ablutionem aquae exteriorem: ideo, sicut baptismus dicitur *ablutio*, sic matrimonium dicitur essentialiter et secundum genus suum *coniunctio*. — Et nota, quod sicut in *ablutione baptismi* est aliquid *permanens*, sicut impressio characteris interior, et aliquid *transiens*, ut lotio exterior, et utrumque dicitur baptismus[^p676-3]; sic in *matrimonio* est aliquid *permanens*, et hoc est vinculum, per quod ligatur vir et mulier, quantumcumque exterius separentur; et est aliquid *transiens*, sicut coniunctio primo facta per verbum exterius, vel per exteriorem actum; et utraque dicitur *matrimonium*, et utraque est coniunctio. Et ideo recte matrimonium coniunctio dicitur et est, sicut rationes primae ostendunt.

1. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod multoties coniunguntur; dicendum, quod quaedam coniunctio *dicitur vinculum*, quaedam *facit vinculum*, quaedam *est usus vinculi*. Dico igitur, quod prima et secunda, quia respiciunt substantiam matrimonii, non iterantur, sed solum tertia; et ad multiplicationem illius non sequitur multiplicatio matrimonii, quoniam non est *genus in essendo*, sed *actus in utendo* sive *usus in agendo*.

2. Et ex hoc patet sequens, quia non est iteratio Sacramenti; et sicut frequenter *manducare corpus Christi* non dicit Sacramenti *iterationem*, sed *usum*; sic in proposito intelligendum; licet non sit simile omnino, quia quod semel manducatum est iterum manducari non potest; sed haec mulier, quae semel cognita est, iterum cognosci potest.

3. Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur, quod est signum sensibile; dicendum, quod sicut *similitudo corporalis* sensu percipitur non ratione sui[^p676-4], sed ratione qualitatum, in quibus est assimilatio, quae sensu deprehenduntur; sic intelligendum est in proposito, quod quamvis coniunctio *secundum se* non sit perceptibilis ut relatio, est tamen *ratione eorum quae coniunguntur*, ut viri et mulieris, et *ratione signi*, in quod, vel per quod coniunguntur.

4. 5. Ad illud quod quaeritur, utrum sit *corporum*, an *animorum*; dicendum, quod animorum in corporibus[^p676-5], quia est coniunctio ratione utentium et habentium sexus distinctos; illa autem coniunctio, quae respicit *totum coniunctum* et est matrimonium essentialiter, non est affectio *animorum*, vel approximatio *corporum*, sed quoddam *vinculum obligatorium*, quod non perimitur, sive affectu, sive corpore separentur. Et per hoc patet responsio ad ultimum.

### Scholion

**I.** Etiam S. Thom. (hic q. 1. a. 1. quaestiunc. 1; Suppl. q. 44. a. 1.) cum aliis docet, matrimonium esse in genere *coniunctionis*, qua coniuges ordinantur ad aliquod unum, et quae simul est «vinculum, quo ligantur *formaliter*, non *effective*»; «coniunctio autem corporum vel animorum ad matrimonium consequitur». Idem (locc. citt. quaestiunc. 3. et a. 3.) explicat etiam definitionem Hugonis a S. Vict., quod matrimonium sit «duarum idonearum personarum legitimus de coniunctione consensus» [definitio per *causam*]; nec non tertiam, quod sit «consortium vitae communis et communicatio divini et humani iuris» [secundum *effectum*, ad quem ordinatur]. De 3. definitione dicit B. Albert. (hic a. 2.), quod sit decretistarum, qui «parum solent curare de definitionibus, vel aliis ad subtilem intellectum pertinentibus». Cfr. hic dub. 1.

De 1. quaestione praeter citatos locos: Scot., hic q. 1. et supra d. 16. q. unica n. 17. — B. Albert., loc. cit. et a. 1. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 1. a. 1. — Richard. a Med., supra d. 26. a. 4.[?] q. 1. — Dionys. Carth., hic q. 1.

**II.** De nomine *matrimonii* etiam alii coaevi magistri referunt easdem vel similes etymologias, plerumque parum solidas.
<!-- page 677 -->
— Cfr. S. Thom., locc. citt. quaestiunc. 2. et a. 2. — B. Albert., hic a. 1. — Petr. a Tar., loc. cit. a. 2. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 1. q. 1.

## English

### Article I.
### *On the quiddity of marriage.*

### Question I.
### *Whether marriage is the union of male and female.*

Concerning the first point it is asked whether marriage is the union (*coniunctio*) of male and female. And that it is so, it seems:

1. By the reason which the Master sets down in the [Lombard's] text[^p675-2]: «Marriage, he says, is the union of male and female».

2. And that it is so is shown *by reason*. With the man alone existing, there is no marriage; likewise with the woman alone existing, there is no marriage: therefore marriage lies in a togetherness and in the joining of one to the other.

3. Likewise, nothing absolute is one[^p675-3] in two subjects; but marriage is one in the man and the woman: therefore it does not signify something absolute, but relative. But that [relation], whatever it be, since it is not a disparity but a conformity, is not a disjunction but a union.

4. Likewise, that upon which those three goods follow — namely fidelity, offspring, and the Sacrament — is properly marriage; but this is the union of male and female, where there is *fidelity* in singleness, *offspring* in fruitfulness, and *signification* in indissolubility[^p675-4]: therefore etc.

**On the contrary:** 1. When the superior essential [thing] is multiplied, the inferior is multiplied[^p675-5]: therefore if marriage is a union, as often as husband and wife are joined, so often is a marriage contracted, and thus between the same man and the same woman there will be several marriages; which if it is false: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, no Sacrament is repeated over the same matter: therefore if marriage is a union of the man with the woman, once he is joined, he ought not to be joined again with the same [woman]. But this is false and unfitting: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, every Sacrament is a sensible sign; but no relation is a sensible sign, and the union of male and female is a relation: therefore such a union is not a sign, therefore neither a Sacrament; therefore, *by simple conversion*[^p675-6], no Sacrament is a union, therefore neither is marriage.

<!-- page 676 -->
4. Likewise, if marriage is a union, then it is either *of bodies* or *of souls*. Not *of souls*, because then marriage would exist in the fatherland [heaven]; not *of bodies*, because then between Mary and Joseph there would have been no marriage.

5. Likewise, whether it be a union *of bodies* according to commingling, or *of souls* according to consent; it happens that such a union is removed through hatred: therefore marriage too [would be] dissolved and destroyed. But this is false, as will presently be shown[^p676-1]: therefore marriage is not a union.

### Conclusion.

> *Marriage is rightly called the union of male and female.*

**I respond:** It must be said that marriage according to its *remote genus* is a *sign*; but the specification or descent to the *inferior genus* is taken according to comparison to the *thing signified*; hence, since the thing signified in this Sacrament is the *union of Christ and the Church*, and a union cannot be better signified than by an exterior union[^p676-2], just as the washing of baptism [is signified] by the exterior washing of water: therefore, just as baptism is called a *washing*, so marriage is called, essentially and according to its genus, a *union*. — And note that, just as in the *washing of baptism* there is something *permanent*, like the interior impression of the character, and something *transient*, like the exterior washing, and each is called baptism[^p676-3]; so in *marriage* there is something *permanent*, and this is the bond by which the man and the woman are bound, however far they be exteriorly separated; and there is something *transient*, like the union first made by the exterior word, or by the exterior act; and each is called *marriage*, and each is a union. And therefore marriage is rightly called, and is, a union, as the first arguments show.

1. To that which is objected, that they are often joined; it must be said that a certain union *is called a bond*, a certain [union] *makes a bond*, a certain [union] *is the use of the bond*. I say therefore that the first and the second, because they regard the substance of marriage, are not repeated, but only the third; and upon the multiplication of that [third] there does not follow a multiplication of marriage, since it is not a *genus in being*, but an *act in using*, that is, a *use in acting*.

2. And from this the following [objection] is made clear, that there is no repetition of the Sacrament; and just as to *eat the body of Christ* frequently does not signify a *repetition* of the Sacrament, but its *use*; so it is to be understood in the case at hand; although it is not wholly similar, because what has once been eaten cannot be eaten again, whereas this woman, who has once been known, can be known again.

3. To that, then, which is objected, that it is a sensible sign; it must be said that, just as a *bodily likeness* is perceived by sense not by reason of itself[^p676-4], but by reason of the qualities in which there is the assimilation, which are apprehended by sense; so it must be understood in the case at hand, that although a union *in itself* is not perceptible as a relation, it is nevertheless [perceptible] *by reason of the things which are joined*, such as of the man and the woman, and *by reason of the sign* into which, or through which, they are joined.

4. 5. To that which is asked, whether it be *of bodies* or *of souls*; it must be said that [it is] of souls in bodies[^p676-5], because it is a union by reason of those who use and possess distinct sexes; but that union which regards the *whole joined thing* and is marriage essentially, is not an affection *of souls*, or an approximation *of bodies*, but a certain *binding bond*, which is not destroyed, whether they be separated in affection or in body. And by this the response to the last [objection] is made clear.

### Scholion

**I.** St. Thomas too (here q. 1, a. 1, quaestiuncula 1; Suppl. q. 44, a. 1) teaches with others that marriage is in the genus of *union* (*coniunctio*), by which spouses are ordered to some one thing, and which is at the same time «a bond by which they are bound *formally*, not *effectively*»; «but a union of bodies or of souls follows upon marriage». The same [Thomas] (in the places cited, quaestiuncula 3 and a. 3) also explains the definition of Hugh of St. Victor, that marriage is «the lawful consent of two fit persons concerning their conjunction» [a definition by *cause*]; and also a third, that it is «a partnership of common life and a sharing of divine and human law» [according to the *effect* to which it is ordered]. Concerning the 3rd definition B. Albert (here a. 2) says that it belongs to the decretists, who «are wont to care little about definitions, or about other things pertaining to subtle understanding». Cf. here dub. 1.

On the 1st question, besides the places cited: Scotus, here q. 1 and above, d. 16, q. unica, n. 17. — B. Albert, loc. cit. and a. 1. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 1, a. 1. — Richard of Mediavilla, above, d. 26, a. 4[?], q. 1. — Dionysius the Carthusian, here q. 1.

**II.** Concerning the name *matrimony*, other contemporary masters too report the same or similar etymologies, for the most part not very solid.
<!-- page 677 -->
— Cf. St. Thomas, in the places cited, quaestiuncula 2 and a. 2. — B. Albert, here a. 1. — Peter of Tarentaise, loc. cit., a. 2. — Richard of Mediavilla, here a. 1, q. 1.
