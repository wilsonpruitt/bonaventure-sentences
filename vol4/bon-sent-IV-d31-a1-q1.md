---

## Latin
<!-- page 717 -->

### Articulus I. *De bonis matrimonii.*
### Quaestio I. *Utrum matrimonium sit in genere utilis, an honesti.*

Et quod sit in genere *honesti*, videtur.

1. *Super illud primae ad Corinthios septimo*[^1]: *Non ut laqueum* etc.; Glossa: « Non esse turpe demonstravit, sed quod honesto honestius est generali nomine *honesti* commendavit ».

2. Item, hoc videtur *ratione*: quia castitas, cum sit virtus, est in genere honesti; sed actus coniugalis est actus castitatis, quoniam est castitas coniugalis, sicut vidualis[^2]: ergo etc.

3. Item, actus, qui alias esset fornicarius, fit non fornicarius per coniugium[^3]; sed nihil tollit dehonestatem peccati nisi honestum: ergo coniugium etc.

4. Item, servare fidem coniugii hoc est opus iustitiae de se; sed talia cadunt in genere honesti, et hoc est bonum matrimonii: ergo etc.

**Contra:** 1. Augustinus *de Bono coniugali*[^4]: « Quaedam bona data sunt a Deo, quae propter se appetenda sunt, ut sapientia, salus et amicitia; quaedam vero propter aliud necessaria, ut doctrina, cibus et coniugium »; sed honestum est propter se appetendum: ergo etc.

2. Item, Hieronymus in libro *de Virginibus*[^5]: « Fornicationis periculo conceduntur nuptiae, et excusabile fit quod non propter se est appetendum, sicut viduis solum fornicationis periculo conceduntur secunda connubia ».

3. Item, ratione videtur: quia « honestum est quod sua vi nos trahit et dignitate nos allicit[^6] »; sed omne tale est ab omnibus appetendum, coniugium autem non est ab omnibus appetendum: ergo etc.

4. Item, omnis verecundia recta est de eo quod habet aliquid inhonestatis, non de honesto; sed omnis homo sanae mentis actum coniugalem erubescit: ergo etc.

### Conclusio.

> Matrimonium secundum aliquid sui est in genere tum *honesti*, tum *utilis*, tum *delectabilis*, quae tamen bona suis contrariis sunt admixta.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod cum triplex bonum sit in coniugio, scilicet *fides*, *proles* et *Sacra*-

<!-- page 718 -->

*mentum*[^7]; *fides* est, quod suam cognoscat et non aliam; hoc est bonum moris de se ordinatum et cadit in genere *honesti*. — *Prolis* vero *bonum* cadit in genere *conferentis*; confert enim prolis multiplicatio ad speciei conservationem et perfectionem numeri, sicut cibus ad conservationem individui. — Bonum vero *Sacramenti* cadit in genere *delectabilis*, quia tanta est ibi unio, ut *relinquat homo patrem et matrem, et sint duo in carne una*[^8]; unde praecipitur viris, *ut uxores diligant sicut corpora sua*. Et sic patet, « quod cum tria sint in electione, sicut vult Philosophus, scilicet *honestum*, *conferens* et *delectabile* »; in triplici genere est bonum coniugii. Et haec tria bona *pura* essent in statu puritatis et innocentiae; sed per peccatum sunt *suis contrariis admixta*: quoniam in actu illo est quaedam inhonestas contracta ratione improbitatis libidinis et pruritus; et ideo in[^9] bono *honesto* quaedam permiscetur inhonestas. Est etiam quoddam *damnum*, quia impeditur homo ab orationis instantia; unde procreando prolem ad cultum Dei impeditur ut tunc perfecte colere Deum[^10]: sic ergo bono *conferenti* permiscetur *damnum*. Et etiam *delectabili*, scilicet Sacramenti indivisioni, quaedam *tribulatio* et *angustia*, quia *qui cum uxore est cogitat quae sunt mundi, quomodo placeat uxori, et divisus est*, quoniam *tribulationem carnis habet huiusmodi*[^11]: et ita bonum delectabile permiscetur tristitiae et angustiae. — Quoniam ergo *bonum* est, tamen *permixtum*; ideo appeti potest, et etiam melius est non appeti. Unde *qui iungit virginem bene facit, et qui non iungit* virginem *melius facit*. — Concedendae igitur sunt rationes ostendentes, matrimonium secundum aliquid sui esse in genere *honesti*.

**Ad obiecta:**

*1.* Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur, quod est propter aliud appetendum; dicendum, quod loquitur ratione boni *conferentis*, quod ibi est.

*2. 3. 4.* Ad illud quod obiicitur de Hieronymo, et ad rationes sequentes solvendum est, quod non est *pure bonum*, sed aliquid habens admixtum; et quia bonum istud est medicinale, ideo bonum non fit malum propter malum, quod ibi est, immo curat et excusat malum, non ut non sit *poenale* — et ideo est ibi verecundia et tribulatio — sed solum, ut non sit *culpabile*, sicut patebit infra[^12].

### Scholion

**I.** In hoc articulo agitur praecipue de *tribus bonis* matrimonii, a S. Augustino assertis et deinde communiter receptis. Notandum est, quod *fides* hic non accipitur ut *fides theologica* nec proprie ut *virtus*, sed ut quaedam pars sive proprietas virtutis iustitiae. Item, *Sacramentum* hic non dicitur ipsum matrimonium, sed *inseparabilitas* eius; cfr. hic q. 2. ad ult.; Magister, hic c. 2; S. Thom., hic q. 1. a. 2. ad 7; Scot., hic q. unica n. 5. Porro de bono *prolis* vide hic dub. 1.

**II.** Quod in solutione quaestionis 1. dicitur, tria ista bona in lapso per peccatum esse suis contrariis admixta, a S. Thoma aliisque supponitur et adhibetur ad probandum, quod in matrimonio debeant esse aliqua « bona *excusantia* », per quae scilicet fiat compensatio detrimenti spiritualis, quod in matrimonio incurritur. Scoto (hic q. unica n. 2. 3.) non placet hic modus loquendi, iam a Magistro aliisque antiquis usurpatus, quia sensu *proprio* non possit accipi, cum matrimonium ex obiecto et fine iam habeat rationem actus honesti. Nec hoc negat S. Thom., immo dicit (hic q. 1. a. 1. ad 2.): « Ista bona, quae matrimonium honestant, sunt *de ratione* matrimonii; et ideo non indiget eis quasi *exterioribus* quibusdam ad honestandum, sed quasi causantibus in ipso honestatem, quae ei secundum se competit ». — Ceterum notum est, Manichaeos, tum antiquos tum medio aevo redivivos, negasse, matrimonium esse in genere *honesti*, cum esset malum in se, sicut materia; qui error pessimus in Almarico iterum damnatus est ab Innocentio III. et Concilio Laterano IV. (c. 2. *Damnamus*). Cfr. etiam infra a. 2. q. 1.

Quaestionem I. tractant S. Thom., Petr. a Tar. et Richard. a Med. sub titulo: « Utrum debeant haberi aliqua bona ad excusandum matrimonium ». Cfr. Scot., de hac et 2 seqq. hic q. unica. — S. Thom., hic q. 1. a. 1; Suppl. 49. a. 1. — B. Albert., hic a. 1. 2. 7. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 1. a. 1. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 1. q. 1. — De hac et 2 seqq. qq. Durand., hic q. 1; Dionys. Carth., hic q. unica.

**III.** De sufficientia horum 3 bonorum omnes consentiunt. Cfr. S. Thom., locis citt. a. 2. — B. Albert., a. 4-6. 8-10. — Petr. a Tar., loc. cit. a. 2. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 1. q. 2.

**IV.** Circa 3. quaestionem patet, quod tria illa bona non eodem modo spectant ad hoc Sacramentum. Bonum *Sacramenti* sive *inseparabilitas* est bonum « *primum* et intrinsecum et *forma*; *fides*, id est fidelitas in iuste reddendo actum debitum, est bonum eius ut *finis proximus*, sed minus principalis; bonum *prolis* est eius bonum extrinsecum, ut *finis ultimus*, sed principalis » (Scot., loc. cit. n. 4.). — Attamen de bonis *fidei* et *prolis* loquendum est cum distinctione ab omnibus recepta, quod licet in matrimonio *contracto* defectus eorum non solvat vinculum, tamen in *contrahendo* eodem conditio apposita contra haec duo bona (sicut contra bonum Sacramenti) matrimonium reddat invalidum (Scot., loc. cit. n. 9.). De 3. quaestione S. Thom., locis cit. a. 3. — B. Albert., hic a. 3. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 2. a. 1. 2. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 1. q. 3. et a. 2. q. 1-3.

## English

### Article I. *On the goods of matrimony.*
### Question I. *Whether matrimony is in the genus of the useful, or of the honorable.*

And that it is in the genus of the *honorable*, it seems.

1. On that passage of the first [letter] to the Corinthians, chapter seven[^1]: *Not that I may cast a snare* etc.; the Gloss: "He showed it not to be base, but commended, under the general name of the *honorable*, that which is more honorable than the honorable."

2. Likewise, this seems [to hold] by *reason*: because chastity, since it is a virtue, is in the genus of the honorable; but the conjugal act is an act of chastity, since there is a conjugal chastity, just as [there is] a widow's[^2]: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, an act which otherwise would be fornicating becomes non-fornicating through marriage[^3]; but nothing takes away the dishonor of sin except the honorable: therefore marriage etc.

4. Likewise, to keep the faith of marriage is of itself a work of justice; but such things fall in the genus of the honorable, and this is a good of matrimony: therefore etc.

**On the contrary:** 1. Augustine, *On the Good of Marriage*[^4]: "Certain goods have been given by God which are to be sought for their own sake, such as wisdom, health, and friendship; but certain others [are] necessary for the sake of something else, such as teaching, food, and marriage"; but the honorable is to be sought for its own sake: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, Jerome in the book *On Virgins*[^5]: "By the danger of fornication marriages are conceded, and that becomes excusable which is not to be sought for its own sake, just as to widows second wedlocks are conceded only by the danger of fornication."

3. Likewise, it seems by reason: because "the honorable is that which draws us by its own force and allures us by its dignity[^6]"; but everything such is to be sought by all; matrimony, however, is not to be sought by all: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, all right shame is concerning that which has something of dishonor, not concerning the honorable; but every person of sound mind blushes at the conjugal act: therefore etc.

### Conclusion.

> Matrimony, according to something of itself, is in the genus now of the *honorable*, now of the *useful*, now of the *delightful*—goods which nevertheless are mixed with their contraries.

**I respond:** It must be said that, since there is a threefold good in marriage, namely *faith* [fidelity], *offspring*, and *Sacra*-

*ment*[^7]; *faith* is that one should know one's own [spouse] and not another; this is a good of conduct, ordered of itself, and it falls in the genus of the *honorable*. — But the *good of offspring* falls in the genus of the *beneficial*; for the multiplication of offspring is conducive to the conservation of the species and the perfection of number, just as food [is conducive] to the conservation of the individual. — But the good of the *Sacrament* falls in the genus of the *delightful*, because so great is the union there, that *a man leaves father and mother, and the two are in one flesh*[^8]; whence it is enjoined upon husbands, *that they love their wives as their own bodies*. And thus it is clear, "that since there are three things in choice, as the Philosopher wills, namely the *honorable*, the *beneficial*, and the *delightful*"; in a threefold genus is the good of marriage. And these three goods would be *pure* in the state of purity and innocence; but through sin they are *mixed with their contraries*: since in that act there is a certain dishonor contracted by reason of the wickedness of lust and prurience; and therefore in the *honorable* good a certain dishonor is intermingled[^9]. There is also a certain *harm*, because a man is impeded from persistence in prayer; whence in procreating offspring for the worship of God he is impeded from then worshipping God perfectly[^10]: thus therefore to the *beneficial* good is intermingled *harm*. And also to the *delightful* [good], namely to the indivision of the Sacrament, [is intermingled] a certain *tribulation* and *distress*, because *he who is with a wife thinks on the things that are of the world, how he may please his wife, and is divided*, since *he has tribulation of the flesh of this kind*[^11]: and so the delightful good is mingled with sorrow and distress. — Since therefore it is a *good*, yet a *mixed* [one]; therefore it can be sought, and yet it is better not to be sought. Whence *he who joins a virgin does well, and he who does not join* a virgin *does better*. — The reasons showing that matrimony, according to something of itself, is in the genus of the *honorable*, are therefore to be conceded.

**To the objections:**

*1.* To that, then, which is objected, that it is to be sought for the sake of something else; it must be said that it speaks by reason of the *beneficial* good, which is there.

*2. 3. 4.* To that which is objected from Jerome, and to the reasons following, it must be resolved that it is not *purely good*, but something having an admixture; and because this good is *medicinal*, therefore the good does not become evil on account of the evil which is there, but rather cures and excuses the evil—not so that it should not be *penal* (and therefore there is there shame and tribulation)—but only so that it should not be *culpable*, as will be clear below[^12].

### Scholion

**I.** In this article the discussion is chiefly about the *three goods* of matrimony, asserted by St. Augustine and thereafter commonly received. It must be noted that *faith* here is not taken as *theological faith*, nor properly as a *virtue*, but as a certain part or property of the virtue of justice. Likewise, *Sacrament* here is not called matrimony itself, but its *inseparability*; cf. here q. 2, at the end; the Master, here c. 2; St. Thomas, here q. 1, a. 2, ad 7; Scotus, here sole q., n. 5. Further, concerning the good of *offspring*, see here dub. 1.

**II.** As to what is said in the solution of question 1, that these three goods, in [the nature] fallen through sin, are mixed with their contraries, this is presupposed by St. Thomas and others and applied to prove that in matrimony there must be certain "*excusing* goods," by which, namely, a compensation may be made for the spiritual detriment which is incurred in matrimony. To Scotus (here sole q., n. 2, 3) this manner of speaking, already employed by the Master and other ancients, is not pleasing, because it cannot be taken in a *proper* sense, since matrimony from its object and end already has the character of an honorable act. Nor does St. Thomas deny this, but rather says (here q. 1, a. 1, ad 2): "These goods, which make matrimony honorable, are *of the very account* of matrimony; and therefore it does not need them as certain *external* things for making it honorable, but as things causing in it the honorableness which belongs to it in itself." — For the rest, it is known that the Manichaeans, both the ancient ones and those revived in the Middle Ages, denied that matrimony is in the genus of the *honorable*, since it was evil in itself, like matter; which most wicked error was again condemned in Amalric by Innocent III and the Fourth Lateran Council (c. 2, *Damnamus*). Cf. also below, a. 2, q. 1.

Question 1 is treated by St. Thomas, Peter of Tarentaise, and Richard of Middleton under the title: "Whether there ought to be some goods for excusing matrimony." Cf. Scotus, on this and the 2 following, here sole q. — St. Thomas, here q. 1, a. 1; *Supplement* 49, a. 1. — Bl. Albert, here a. 1, 2, 7. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 1, a. 1. — Richard of Middleton, here a. 1, q. 1. — On this and the 2 following questions, Durandus, here q. 1; Denis the Carthusian, here sole q.

**III.** Concerning the sufficiency of these 3 goods all agree. Cf. St. Thomas, in the places cited, a. 2. — Bl. Albert, a. 4–6, 8–10. — Peter of Tarentaise, in the place cited, a. 2. — Richard of Middleton, here a. 1, q. 2.

**IV.** Concerning the 3rd question it is clear that those three goods do not regard this Sacrament in the same manner. The good of the *Sacrament*, or *inseparability*, is a good "*first* and intrinsic and *form*; *faith*, that is fidelity in justly rendering the due act, is its good as a *proximate end*, but less principal; the good of *offspring* is its extrinsic good, as a *last end*, but principal" (Scotus, in the place cited, n. 4). — Nevertheless concerning the goods of *faith* and *offspring* one must speak with a distinction received by all, namely that although in a matrimony [already] *contracted* a defect of them does not dissolve the bond, yet in *contracting* [it], a condition attached against these two goods (just as against the good of the Sacrament) renders the matrimony invalid (Scotus, in the place cited, n. 9). Concerning the 3rd question: St. Thomas, in the places cited, a. 3. — Bl. Albert, here a. 3. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 2, a. 1, 2. — Richard of Middleton, here a. 1, q. 3, and a. 2, q. 1–3.
