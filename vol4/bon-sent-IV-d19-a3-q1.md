## *Utrum sacerdos, qui potest absolvere subditum unum, possit et quemlibet alium absolvere*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 807 -->

### Articulus III. *In quos claves sacerdotales habeant effectum.*

Consequenter quantum ad tertium articulum quaeritur, in quos habeant sacerdotales claves usum vel effectum. Et circa hoc duo quaeruntur.

Primo quaeritur, utrum sacerdos, qui potest absolvere subditum unum, possit et quemlibet alium.

Secundo, utrum subditus sacerdos possit absolvere praelatum suum.

### Quaestio I. *Utrum sacerdos, qui potest absolvere subditum unum, possit et quemlibet alium absolvere.*

Circa primum sic proceditur et ostenditur, quod sacerdos, qui in unum potest, eadem ratione potest et in omnem alium.

1. *Levitici* decimo tertio[^1] dicitur: *Homo, in cuius cute apparuerit plaga leprae, adducetur ad Aaron sacerdotem, vel ad unum quemlibet filiorum eius*; ex hoc patet, quod quilibet sacerdos in veteri lege poterat iudicare de omnibus fidelibus de populo Israel, de quacumque esset tribu; sed quod praecessit in figura debet sequi in veritate: ergo videtur, quod hic possit quilibet sacerdos de lepra cuiuslibet iudicare. Et multo fortius hic videtur, quia maior est unitas in Ecclesia et quantum ad ministrantes Sacramenta et quantum ad caritatem fidelium, quam tunc temporis erat; sed tunc quod poterat unus poterant omnes, et in quem poterat unus, et omnes: ergo et modo debet esse similiter.

2. Item, Dominus virtutem istam sive potestatem dedit primis sacerdotibus, et secundum conformitatem eorum descendit in posteros; sed Dominus dixit generaliter, *Ioannis* vigesimo[^2]: *Quorum remiseritis peccata, remittuntur eis*; et hoc ipsum dicitur sacerdoti: ergo videtur, quod detur potestas in omnes: ergo omnes potest absolvere.

3. Item, ratione videtur: quoniam ita videmus in corporalibus, quod ignis, qui comburit in una materia, comburit et in qualibet simili et eiusdem speciei; et lux, quae fugat tenebram de uno loco, similiter et de alio pellit sine instantia: ergo pari ratione videtur in proposito, quod si aliquis sacerdos potest absolvere unum peccatorem poenitentem, quod pari ratione possit et quemlibet consimilem.

4. Item, ita videmus in artificialibus, quod clavis, quae unam seram aperit, aperit et omnes per eum modum factas, et e converso, multae claves similes in quantitate et qualitate unam seram: si ergo debitum peccati[^3] est conforme in omnibus peccatoribus poenitentibus, et potestas aperiendi in omnibus clavibus sacerdotalibus; ergo qui potest in unum, et in quemlibet alium.

5. Item, ita videmus in supercaelestibus, quod Angelus ordinis inferioris potest purgari, illuminari et perfici a quolibet Angelo superioris ordinis[^4]: ergo si haec hierarchia illam imitatur; cum ordo sacerdotalis sit supra laicos, videtur, quod quilibet laicus a quolibet sacerdote possit absolvi.

6. Item, si non potest absolvere omnes, cum possit unum; quaero, unde hoc sit. Aut enim est
<!-- page 808 -->
potentiae laxatione et corruptione, aut ex ligatione, quia superior potestas non permittit. Primo modo non, illud patet. Secundo modo non debet, quia bonum est diffundendum et multiplicandum et colendum: ergo non est restringenda potestas; et ita videtur, quod qui in unum potest, quod possit in omnem.

**Contra:**
1. Causa decima sexta, quaestione prima[^5]: «Nulli sacerdotum liceat parochianum alterius aut absolvere, aut ligare».

2. Item, Causa nona, quaestione secunda[^6]: «Nullus alterius parochianum iudicare, ordinare, aut excommunicare praesumat, quia tale iudicium aut ordinatio vel excommunicatio nec rata erit nec vires ullas habebit».

3. Item, in Concilio generali, *extra de Poenitentiis et remissionibus*[^7]: «Si quis aliquando ex causa voluerit alteri confiteri, licentiam petat et obtineat a proprio sacerdote, quia aliter ipsum non poterit absolvere vel ligare»; sed non quilibet sacerdos est proprius, quia proprium dicitur quod convenit uni soli: ergo non quilibet sacerdos potest quemlibet absolvere.

4. Item, videtur *ratione*: quia nos videmus in ordinatione praedicamentorum in *rationalibus*[^8] et in ordine causarum in *naturalibus*, quod reductio fit usque ad supremum per unum immediatum, ita quod non sunt plura immediata respectu unius. Et hoc patet, quia una est species specialissima individui unius, similiter est in causis: ergo si ordo est in Ecclesia secundum gradus descendendo et ascendendo a Summo Pontifice usque ad parochianum; videtur tunc, quod per unum sacerdotem, per unum archidiaconum, per unum episcopum, per unum hierarcham[^9] reducatur ad Summum Pontificem: ergo non poterunt secundum rectum ordinem plures sacerdotes in unum: non ergo quilibet quemlibet potest absolvere vel ligare.

5. Item, ita videmus in *exercitibus*, quod sunt ordines distincti, ut tribunus super centuriones, et centurio super quinquagenarios, et quinquagenarius super decuriones, et decurio super decem[^10]; et qui sunt sub uno non sunt sub alio decurione, nec potest decurio compescere nisi suos decem, et sic de aliis ascendendo; secundum hoc pari ratione videtur, quod nec sacerdos alterius parochianum, nec episcopus alienum subditum.

6. Item, si duo haberent *posse* super unum, qui possent discordare, possent contraria iubere; sed nemo potest duobus dominis contraria iubentibus obtemperare rationabiliter[^11]: ergo posset in isto subdito perplexitas generari: ergo secundum rectum ordinem non debet duobus sacerdotibus super eundem subditum aequalis potestas dari: ergo etc.

> **Conclusio.** Non quilibet sacerdos potest quemlibet absolvere nisi in articulo necessitatis.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod, sicut dicit Dionysius[^12], «lex divinitatis est media per prima, et ultima per media perducere ad purgationem, illuminationem et perfectionem». Ideo, sicut videmus in omnibus, sive in rationalibus, sive in naturalibus, sive in caelestibus, quod ad hoc, quod sit concordia et decor, oportet, quod sit *ordo*; sic intelligendum est et in *spiritualibus* potestatibus in ipsa Ecclesia; unde *Cantici* sexto[^13] legitur, quod *terribilis est ut castrorum acies ordinata*.

Quoniam igitur *ordo* attenditur secundum praelationem et subiectionem; et *praelatio* attenditur secundum *ascensum* et *descensum*, non secundum aequalitatem; et in *ascendendo* ad superius est *status et reductio ad unum*, econtra in *descendendo* est *multiplicatio*[^14]: ideo secundum rectum ordinem Ecclesiae oportuit, unum esse praelatum primum et supremum, in quo esset *status* omnis praelationis ecclesiasticae. Et quia in illo *status* est tanquam in primo et simplicissimo in illo genere; ideo *virtus* in eo unita est, et solus est inter praelatos, qui habet *plenitudinem potestatis*[^15]. — Item, quoniam per recessum ab uno fit *multiplicatio*, et per hoc virtutis diminutio; ideo, quanto praelati alii inferiores sunt, tanto *plures* sunt et tanto *minorem* habent potestatem, ita quod *status* est in sacerdo-
<!-- page 809 -->
tibus parochialibus, qui immediate gerunt populi curam; et istorum iurisdictio arctata est ad portiunculam determinatam, et cuilibet commissa est sua portio secundum rectam ordinationem, et illa iudicatur esse *messis sua*[^16]. Et quoniam non licet alii sacerdoti mittere manum vel falcem in alienam messem; ideo alius non habet potestatem eius subditum absolvere, nisi superior, vel vicem habeat superioris, ut poenitentiarii domini Papae et poenitentiarii episcopi, legati et privilegiati, qui faciunt hoc auctoritate superioris, sicut nuntius a principe procedens potest in omnes subditos aliis inferioribus. Haec autem intelligenda sunt secundum *legem communem*, quia in articulo *necessitatis* indulget rectitudo iuris cuilibet sacerdoti quemlibet absolvere, si sit in Ecclesiae unitate.

Pro regula igitur habendum est, quod solus sacerdos proprius habet potestatis exsecutionem in suos, exceptis quinque casibus. Primus est, si ovis aliena in parochia sua deliquit. — Secundus, si mutavit ibi domicilium, sicut est in scholaribus, qui student Parisius. — Tertius est, si sit vagabundus. — Quartus est, si sacerdos proprius sit inscius, vel malitiosus, et petita licentia, vult adire peritiorem. — Ultimus est in articulo necessitatis, et hoc in casu non oportet sacerdotem esse licentiatum[^17]. In aliis autem oportet, non a sacerdote poenitentis solum — quia ille non potest nisi *subditum* licentiare — sed oportet esse licentiatum ab ordinario, scilicet ipso episcopo. — Concedendae igitur sunt rationes probantes, quod non quilibet potest in quemlibet.

**Ad argumenta:**

1. Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur de filiis Aaron, dicendum, quod non est simile: quia populus Israel populus unus erat et unum templum habebat, nec erant ibi regna terrarum sollicitudini sacerdotum commissa, sicut nunc sunt; et ideo oportet modo esse plures episcopos[^18] et plures ecclesias, quarum quaelibet habet suam potestatem.

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur de hoc quod dicit Dominus: *Quorum remiseritis* etc.; dicendum, quod clavis sacerdotalis, quamvis, quantum est de se, indifferens sit ad omnes; tamen vis motiva huius clavis, quae est iurisdictio, ut prius[^19] visum est, non se habet ad omnes nisi in uno solo; et ideo unus solus potest omnes absolvere. Quando ergo Dominus dedit clavem et hanc potestatem, ipse praesupposuit eius exsecutionem secundum regulam potestatis collatae Petro et eius successoribus.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod ignis agit in quodlibet combustibile; dicendum, quod non est simile: quia prima est actio *naturalis*, quae est secundum necessitatem, haec autem actio est *spiritualis* et *rationalis*, quae quidem attenditur secundum ordinem.

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur de clave et sera, dicendum, quod istud verum est, quando adest virtus motiva, quae clavem moveat; sed in aliquibus vis ista arctata est, ut in sacerdotibus inferioribus; in aliquibus *ligata*, ut in haereticis et excommunicatis; in aliquibus est *amputata*, ut in degradatis[^20]. Unde sicut hi non possunt absolvere aliquem, quorum manus est ligata simpliciter, ita in aliis non potest clavis claudere, ubi se manus iurisdictionis non valeat extendere.

5. Ad illud quod obiicitur de Angelis superioris ordinis, dicendum, quod non est simile: quia in hierarchia angelica non potest cadere zelus nec controversia, pro eo quod concordia in sublimibus est facta[^21]; non sic est in Ecclesia, immo quilibet vult sibi ius alterius vindicare: ideo oportuit potestates distinguere et limitare.

6. Ad illud quod ultimo obiicitur, quod potestas non est arctanda; dicendum, quod arctatio potestatis potest venire dupliciter: aut propter *defectum* amoris, aut propter *conservationem ordinis*. Prima est invidiae et repugnat bonitati, secunda vero est sapientiae et concordat paci et communi utilitati; et talis arctatio facta est in minoribus sacerdotibus, regulante iure divino, immo ipso Spiritu sancto[^22], per Patres et Apostolos, qui Ecclesiam fundaverunt et ordinaverunt. Et sic patet totum.

### Scholion

## English

### Article III. *On whom the priestly keys take effect.*

Next, with respect to the third article, it is asked on whom the priestly keys have their use or effect. And concerning this two things are asked.

First it is asked whether a priest who can absolve one subject can absolve any other.

Second, whether a subject priest can absolve his own prelate.

### Question I. *Whether a priest who can absolve one subject can absolve any other.*

Concerning the first the matter proceeds thus, and it is shown that a priest who can act upon one can, by the same reasoning, act upon every other.

1. In *Leviticus* thirteen[^1] it is said: *The man in whose skin the plague of leprosy shall appear shall be brought to Aaron the priest, or to any one of his sons*; from this it is clear that any priest in the old law could judge concerning all the faithful of the people of Israel, of whatever tribe they were; but what went before in figure ought to follow in truth: therefore it seems that here any priest can judge concerning the leprosy of anyone. And this seems much more strongly to be so here, because there is greater unity in the Church, both as regards those ministering the Sacraments and as regards the charity of the faithful, than there was at that time; but then what one could do all could do, and toward whomever one could act, so could all: therefore now too it ought to be similar.

2. Likewise, the Lord gave this power or authority to the first priests, and according to their conformity it descended to those who came after; but the Lord said in general, *John* twenty[^2]: *Whose sins you shall forgive, they are forgiven them*; and this very thing is said to the priest: therefore it seems that power is given over all: therefore he can absolve all.

3. Likewise, it seems so by reason: for we see thus in bodily things, that fire which burns in one matter burns also in any like matter of the same species; and light which drives darkness from one place likewise drives it from another without resistance: therefore by parity of reason it seems so in the case at hand, that if some priest can absolve one penitent sinner, by parity of reason he can also absolve any like one.

4. Likewise, we see thus in things of craft, that a key which opens one lock opens also all locks made in the same manner, and conversely, many keys alike in quantity and quality open one lock: if therefore the debt of sin[^3] is conformable in all penitent sinners, and the power of opening is conformable in all priestly keys; therefore he who can act upon one can act upon any other.

5. Likewise, we see thus in supercelestial things, that an Angel of a lower order can be purified, illumined, and perfected by any Angel of a higher order[^4]: therefore if this hierarchy imitates that one, since the priestly order is above the laity, it seems that any layman can be absolved by any priest.

6. Likewise, if he cannot absolve all, since he can absolve one; I ask whence this is. For either it is
<!-- page 808 -->
from a slackening and corruption of the power, or from a binding, because a higher power does not permit it. That it is not in the first way is clear. In the second way it ought not to be, because the good is to be diffused and multiplied and cultivated: therefore the power is not to be restricted; and so it seems that he who can act upon one can act upon every one.

**On the contrary:**
1. Cause sixteen, question one[^5]: «Let it be lawful for no priest to absolve or to bind another's parishioner».

2. Likewise, Cause nine, question two[^6]: «Let no one presume to judge, ordain, or excommunicate another's parishioner, because such judgment or ordination or excommunication will be neither valid nor have any force».

3. Likewise, in the General Council, *extra, On Penance and Remissions*[^7]: «If anyone at some time should for cause wish to confess to another, let him ask and obtain license from his own priest, because otherwise that one cannot absolve or bind him»; but not every priest is the proper one, because that is called proper which belongs to one alone: therefore not every priest can absolve everyone.

4. Likewise, it seems so by *reason*: for we see in the ordering of the predicaments in *rational matters*[^8] and in the order of causes in *natural things*, that reduction is made up to the supreme through one immediate thing, such that there are not several immediate things with respect to one. And this is clear, because there is one most special species of one individual, and likewise it is in causes: therefore if there is order in the Church according to degrees, descending and ascending from the Supreme Pontiff down to the parishioner; it seems then that through one priest, through one archdeacon, through one bishop, through one hierarch[^9] it is reduced to the Supreme Pontiff: therefore there cannot, according to right order, be several priests over one: therefore not anyone can absolve or bind anyone.

5. Likewise, we see thus in *armies*, that there are distinct orders, as the tribune over the centurions, and the centurion over the captains of fifty, and the captain of fifty over the captains of ten, and the captain of ten over the ten[^10]; and those who are under one are not under another captain of ten, nor can the captain of ten restrain any but his own ten, and so on ascending; according to this, by parity of reason it seems that neither can a priest restrain another's parishioner, nor a bishop another's subject.

6. Likewise, if two had *power* over one, who might disagree, they could command contrary things; but no one can reasonably obey two masters commanding contrary things[^11]: therefore perplexity could be generated in that subject: therefore according to right order equal power ought not be given to two priests over the same subject: therefore etc.

> **Conclusion.** Not every priest can absolve everyone except in a case of necessity.

**I respond:** It must be said that, as Dionysius says[^12], «it is the law of the Godhead to lead through the first to the middle, and through the middle to the last, unto purification, illumination, and perfection». Therefore, as we see in all things, whether in rational, or natural, or celestial things, that for there to be concord and beauty it is required that there be *order*; so it must be understood also in the *spiritual* powers within the Church itself; whence in *Canticles* six[^13] it is read that *she is terrible as an army set in array*.

Since therefore *order* is regarded according to prelacy and subjection; and *prelacy* is regarded according to *ascent* and *descent*, not according to equality; and in *ascending* to what is higher there is *a coming-to-rest and a reduction to one*, while on the contrary in *descending* there is *multiplication*[^14]: therefore according to the right order of the Church it was required that there be one prelate, first and supreme, in whom should be *the coming-to-rest* of all ecclesiastical prelacy. And because in him the coming-to-rest is as in the first and most simple thing in that genus; therefore *power* is united in him, and he alone among prelates is the one who has the *fullness of power*[^15]. — Likewise, since by withdrawal from the one there comes *multiplication*, and through this a diminution of power; therefore, the more the other prelates are inferior, the more *numerous* they are and the *less* power they have, so that the coming-to-rest is in the par-
<!-- page 809 -->
ish priests, who immediately bear the care of the people; and the jurisdiction of these is restricted to a determined portion, and to each is committed his portion according to right ordering, and that is judged to be *his harvest*[^16]. And since it is not lawful for another priest to put his hand or sickle into another's harvest; therefore another does not have the power to absolve his subject, unless a superior, or one who holds the place of a superior, as the penitentiaries of the lord Pope and the penitentiaries of the bishop, legates and the privileged, who do this by the authority of a superior, as a messenger proceeding from a prince can act upon all subjects below others. But these things are to be understood according to the *common law*, because in a case of *necessity* the rectitude of the law grants to any priest to absolve anyone, if he be in the unity of the Church.

As a rule, therefore, it is to be held that only the proper priest has the exercise of power over his own, except in five cases. The first is, if a stray sheep has offended in his parish. — The second, if it has changed its domicile there, as is the case with students who study at Paris. — The third is, if it be a vagabond. — The fourth is, if the proper priest be ignorant or malicious, and, license having been sought, [the penitent] wishes to go to a more skilled one. — The last is in a case of necessity, and in this case the priest need not be licensed[^17]. In the others, however, it is required — not from the priest of the penitent alone, because he can only license a *subject* — but it is required to be licensed by the ordinary, namely the bishop himself. — The reasons proving that not anyone can act upon anyone are therefore to be granted.

**To the arguments:**

1. To that which is objected concerning the sons of Aaron, it must be said that it is not similar: because the people of Israel was one people and had one temple, nor were the kingdoms of the lands committed there to the care of the priests, as they now are; and therefore it is now required that there be several bishops[^18] and several churches, each of which has its own power.

2. To that which is objected concerning what the Lord says: *Whose sins you shall forgive* etc.; it must be said that the priestly key, although of itself it is indifferent to all, nevertheless the moving force of this key, which is jurisdiction, as was seen before[^19], does not bear upon all but only upon one alone; and therefore one alone can absolve all. When therefore the Lord gave the key and this power, he himself presupposed its exercise according to the rule of the power conferred on Peter and his successors.

3. To that which is objected, that fire acts upon any combustible thing; it must be said that it is not similar: because the first is a *natural* action, which is according to necessity, but this action is *spiritual* and *rational*, which indeed is regarded according to order.

4. To that which is objected concerning the key and the lock, it must be said that that is true when there is present the moving force which moves the key; but in some it is restricted, as in inferior priests; in some *bound*, as in heretics and the excommunicate; in some it is *cut off*, as in the degraded[^20]. Whence just as those whose hand is bound absolutely cannot absolve anyone, so in others the key cannot close where the hand of jurisdiction is unable to extend itself.

5. To that which is objected concerning the Angels of a higher order, it must be said that it is not similar: because in the angelic hierarchy zeal or controversy cannot fall, since concord is made among the sublime ones[^21]; it is not so in the Church, but rather everyone wishes to claim for himself the right of another: therefore it was required to distinguish and limit the powers.

6. To that which is last objected, that power is not to be restricted; it must be said that the restriction of power can come in two ways: either on account of a *defect* of love, or on account of the *conservation of order*. The first is of envy and is repugnant to goodness, but the second is of wisdom and accords with peace and the common good; and such restriction was made in the lesser priests, the divine law regulating it, indeed the Holy Spirit himself[^22], through the Fathers and Apostles, who founded and ordered the Church. And thus the whole is clear.

### Scholion

**I.** Concerning the first question of this article, see the authors cited in the preceding scholion and Alex. of Hales, S[umma] p. IV, q. 21, m. 3, a. 2, § 1. — But excellent is the teaching of the Seraphic Doctor, by which from the very nature of the Church he proves the hierarchical order and vindicates to the center of the Church, that is, to the Apostolic See, the fullness of power.

In the second question S. Bonaventure rightly teaches, against certain later writers, that although a superior can subject himself to an inferior and give him jurisdiction over himself for the internal forum, nevertheless «those who are subject to one cannot give another authority over themselves». On this second question: Alex. of Hales, loc. cit. § 2. — S. Thomas, here q. 1, a. 3, subquestion 3; Suppl. q. 20, a. 3. — B. Albert, here a. 9. — Petr. of Tarentaise, here q. 2, a. 2. — Dionysius the Carthusian, here q. 1.
