## Latin

<!-- page 106 -->

### Articulus I. *De sanctificatione adultorum praeter baptismum.*
### Quaestio I. *Utrum baptismus flaminis sine baptismo fluminis sufficiat ad salutem.*

Circa primum sic proceditur et quaeritur, utrum baptismus flaminis sine baptismo fluminis sufficiat ad salutem. Et quod non videtur,

1. *Primo* auctoritate Domini Ioannis tertio[^3]: *Nisi quis renatus fuerit* etc.; sed nullus sic renascitur, nisi baptizetur baptismo aquae: ergo nullus intrat in regnum caelorum sine baptismo aquae: ergo baptismus flaminis sive Spiritus non sufficit.

2. Item, hoc ipsum probatur exemplo, quia Actuum decimo[^4] dicitur, quod Cornelius et qui cum eo erant prius receperunt Spiritum sanctum, et postmodum baptizati sunt baptismo aquae: ergo videtur, quod baptizati baptismo flaminis tenentur ad baptismum aquae: ergo sine hoc non est salus.

3. Item, si aliquis posset salvari sine baptismo aquae, maxime videtur de parvulo, cui citius debet ex misericordia Dei subveniri; sed parvulus, qui non est renatus aqua, damnatur, sicut dicit Augustinus in libro de Fide ad Petrum, et habetur in littera[^5]: «Firmissime tene» etc.: ergo nullus alius sine aquae baptismo, quantumcumque aliter baptizatus sit, salvari potest.

4. Item, si aliter quam per baptismum aquae potest quis salvari, ergo baptismus aquae non est Sacramentum necessitatis: ergo non peccant qui nolunt baptizari; sed hoc est manifeste falsum: ergo sine baptismo aquae non est salus: ergo nullus alius sufficit sine hoc.

**Contra:**
1. Ioannis undecimo[^6]: *Omnis qui vivit et credit in me non morietur in aeternum*; sed iusti credunt et fideles sunt, antequam baptizentur: ergo si moriuntur in tali fide, non moriuntur in aeternum.

2. Item, Ambrosius dicit, et habetur in littera[^7]: «Ille quem regeneraturus eram, gratiam, quam poposcit, non amisit»: ergo ille qui mortuus erat non regeneratus, erat salvus sine baptismo aquae: ergo baptismus poenitentiae sine isto dat salutem.

3. Item, gratia gratum faciens est dispositio sufficiens ad salutem; dispositio autem ad gratiam est toto corde[^8] ad Deum redire et ab errore recedere, et hoc totum potest esse per Dei gratiam sine baptismo fluminis: ergo aliquis solo Spiritus sancti baptismate salvari potest.

<!-- page 107 -->

4. Item, «propter quod unumquodque, et illud magis[^1b]»; sed baptismus fluminis sive aquae non sanctificat nisi per Spiritum: ergo Spiritus magis sanctificat et facit dignum vita aeterna. Si ergo aliquis habeat baptismum flaminis, habet sanctificationem; et qui hoc habet habet quod sufficit ad salutem: ergo etc. *Si tu dicas*, quod nemo potest habere baptismum flaminis, nisi prius baptizetur baptismo aquae; ostenditur esse falsum: primo, quia «Deus nullum obligat ad impossibile[^2b]»; et iterum, nulli se negat requirenti ipsum: si ergo aliquis, qui non potest baptizari, ad Deum convertitur, Deus convertitur ad ipsum. Sed hoc non est nisi per collationem Spiritus sancti, et hoc est baptizari baptismo flaminis: ergo ille potest haberi sine alio.

> **Conclusio.** Baptismi fluminis et flaminis sunt necessarii, ille tantum necessitate praecepti; sed praepediente impossibilitate, sufficit baptismus flaminis.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod baptismus flaminis et baptismus fluminis uterque est necessarius ad salutem. Sed primus est necessarius, non quia sit in praecepto, sed quia sine illo nullus liberatur a peccato. Et quoniam omnes peccatores nascimur, et nullus salvari potest, nisi liberetur a peccato: ideo nullus sine baptismo flaminis, neque parvus neque magnus[^3b], potest salvari. — Baptismus autem fluminis sive aquae est necessarius, quia Deus instituit et quia instituit *sub praecepto*; et quoniam quae praecepta sunt sunt necessaria ad salutem, ideo talis baptismus est ad salutem necessarius. — Sed quoniam «Deus neminem obligat ad impossibile ex suo praecepto», ut dicit Hieronymus[^4b]; et iterum, quod quis non potest facere, si velit facere, reputatur ei pro facto, sicut dicit Glossa super illud Psalmi[^5b]: *Etenim in corde iniquitates operamini* etc.: «Quod non potes facere, et vis, Deus factum reputat»: ideo baptismus aquae non sic est necessarius, quin, si adsit voluntas, et desit possibilitas, non salvetur aliquis sine ipso.

Concedendum est ergo, quod baptismus flaminis sine baptismo fluminis sufficit, dum tamen homo habeat voluntatem et praepediatur, ut ante mortem non recipiat, praepediente necessitate, sicut ostendunt rationes ultimae. Concedendum est nihilominus, quod nullus sine hoc potest salvari, si possit et negligat baptizari. Et hoc est quod dicit Augustinus[^6b], quod «potest quis salvari non percepto Sacramento baptismi, sed non potest, Sacramento contempto». Tunc autem contemnit, cum baptizari potest, et locus et tempus se offert, et negligit; et tunc etiam privatur baptismo flaminis, quia a gratia cadit.

**Ad argumenta pro parte affirmativa:**

*Ad 1, 2.* Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur de Domini mandato, et ad illud de Cornelio, iam patet responsio: quia utrumque intelligitur in eo qui facultatem habet et non praepeditur necessitate. Nam Cornelius ille locum et tempus habebat, et Domini mandatum *affirmativum* eum qui poterat, obligabat[^7b].

*Ad 3.* Ad illud quod obiicitur de parvulo, quod tunc maxime parvulus non baptizatus poterit salvari; dicendum, quod *parvulus*, si haberet baptismum flaminis sine baptismo aquae, numquam damnaretur. Quod ergo damnatur, hoc est, quia privatus baptismo aquae caret gratia Spiritus sancti; quia aliter ad gratiam non potest disponi, quantum est de iure communi, nisi Deus faciat de privilegio speciali, sicut in sanctificatis in utero. — Sed *adultus* de sententia communi potest per fidem et dolorem de peccatis ad gratiam disponi, secundum illud Ezechielis[^8b]: *Quacumque hora ingemuerit peccator, vita vivet* etc. Unde non est simile, quia unicuique plus valet fides sua quam aliena; et ita adulto voluntas sua, quam parvulo voluntas aliena. Nec damnatur parvulus propter transgressionem praecepti, sed propter culpam originalis, quae remanet non deleta.

*Ad 4.* Quod ultimo obiicitur iam solutum est, quia dicitur Sacramentum necessitatis, non quia sine ipso non possit homo salvari, sed quia ad hoc tenetur, si potest, et hoc neglecto, non potest salvari.

## English

<!-- page 106 -->

### Article I. *On the sanctification of adults apart from baptism.*
### Question I. *Whether baptism of the Spirit without baptism of water suffices for salvation.*

Concerning the first one proceeds thus, and it is asked whether baptism of the Spirit [*flaminis*] without baptism of water [*fluminis*] suffices for salvation. And that it does not seems [so]:

1. *First*, by the authority of the Lord in John, third [chapter][^3]: *Unless one be born again* etc.; but no one is thus reborn unless he be baptized with the baptism of water: therefore no one enters into the kingdom of heaven without the baptism of water: therefore baptism of the Spirit, that is, of the Spirit [*flaminis sive Spiritus*], does not suffice.

2. Likewise, this very thing is proved by example, because in Acts, tenth [chapter][^4], it is said that Cornelius and those who were with him first received the Holy Spirit, and afterwards were baptized with the baptism of water: therefore it seems that those baptized with the baptism of the Spirit are bound to the baptism of water: therefore without this there is no salvation.

3. Likewise, if anyone could be saved without the baptism of water, this seems [to be true] most of all of a little child, who ought the sooner, out of God's mercy, to be helped; but a little child who is not reborn by water is damned, as Augustine says in the book *On Faith, to Peter*, and it is had in the text [of the Master][^5]: «Most firmly hold» etc.: therefore no other, however otherwise baptized, can be saved without the baptism of water.

4. Likewise, if someone can be saved otherwise than through the baptism of water, then the baptism of water is not the Sacrament of necessity: therefore those who refuse to be baptized do not sin; but this is manifestly false: therefore without the baptism of water there is no salvation: therefore no other suffices without this.

**On the contrary:**
1. John, eleventh [chapter][^6]: *Everyone who lives and believes in me shall not die forever*; but the just believe and are faithful before they are baptized: therefore if they die in such faith, they do not die forever.

2. Likewise, Ambrose says, and it is had in the text[^7]: «He whom I was about to regenerate did not lose the grace which he sought»: therefore he who had died not regenerated was saved without the baptism of water: therefore the baptism of penance, without that [of water], gives salvation.

3. Likewise, sanctifying grace [*gratia gratum faciens*] is a disposition sufficient for salvation; but the disposition for grace is to return to God with the whole heart[^8] and to withdraw from error, and all this can be through God's grace without the baptism of water [*fluminis*]: therefore someone can be saved by the baptism of the Holy Spirit alone.

<!-- page 107 -->

4. Likewise, «that on account of which a thing [is such], that thing [is so] more[^1b]»; but the baptism of water, that is, of water, does not sanctify except through the Spirit: therefore the Spirit sanctifies more and makes [one] worthy of eternal life. If therefore someone has the baptism of the Spirit, he has sanctification; and he who has this has what suffices for salvation: therefore etc. *If you say* that no one can have the baptism of the Spirit unless he be first baptized with the baptism of water; it is shown to be false: first, because «God obliges no one to the impossible[^2b]»; and again, He denies Himself to none who seek Him: if therefore someone, who cannot be baptized, turns to God, God turns to him. But this is only through the conferral of the Holy Spirit, and this is to be baptized with the baptism of the Spirit: therefore that [baptism] can be had without the other.

> **Conclusion.** The baptisms of water and of the Spirit are necessary, the former only by the necessity of precept; but when impossibility impedes, the baptism of the Spirit suffices.

**I respond:** It must be said that the baptism of the Spirit and the baptism of water are each necessary for salvation. But the first is necessary, not because it is under precept, but because without it no one is freed from sin. And since we are all born sinners, and no one can be saved unless he be freed from sin: therefore no one, neither small nor great[^3b], can be saved without the baptism of the Spirit. — But the baptism of water, that is, of water, is necessary because God instituted it and because He instituted it *under precept*; and since the things which are prescribed are necessary for salvation, therefore such a baptism is necessary for salvation. — But since «God obliges no one to the impossible by His precept», as Jerome says[^4b]; and again, that which one cannot do, if he wills to do it, is reckoned to him as done, as the Gloss says on that [verse] of the Psalm[^5b]: *For in your heart you work iniquities* etc.: «What you cannot do, and will [to do], God reckons as done»: therefore the baptism of water is not necessary in such a way that, if the will be present and the possibility be lacking, someone is not saved without it.

It must therefore be granted that the baptism of the Spirit without the baptism of water suffices, provided nonetheless that the man have the will and be impeded, so that before death he does not receive [it], necessity impeding, as the last arguments show. It must nonetheless be granted that no one can be saved without this, if he be able and neglect to be baptized. And this is what Augustine says[^6b], that «one can be saved without having received the Sacrament of baptism, but he cannot [be saved] if the Sacrament be contemned». But he contemns [it] when he can be baptized, and place and time offer themselves, and he neglects [it]; and then also he is deprived of the baptism of the Spirit, because he falls from grace.

**To the arguments for the affirmative part:**

*To 1, 2.* To that which is objected concerning the Lord's mandate, and to that concerning Cornelius, the response is already clear: because each is understood of him who has the opportunity and is not impeded by necessity. For that Cornelius had place and time, and the Lord's *affirmative* mandate obliged him who was able[^7b].

*To 3.* To that which is objected concerning the little child, that then most of all an unbaptized little child could be saved; it must be said that the *little child*, if he had the baptism of the Spirit without the baptism of water, would never be damned. That [a child] therefore is damned, this is because, deprived of the baptism of water, he lacks the grace of the Holy Spirit; for otherwise he cannot be disposed to grace, as far as concerns common law, unless God act by a special privilege, as in those sanctified in the womb. — But an *adult*, by the common opinion, can be disposed to grace through faith and sorrow for sins, according to that [word] of Ezekiel[^8b]: *In whatever hour the sinner shall groan, he shall live in life* etc. Hence the case is not the same, because for each one his own faith avails more than another's; and so for an adult his own will [avails more] than for a little child another's will. Nor is the little child damned on account of the transgression of a precept, but on account of the fault of original [sin], which remains undeleted.

*To 4.* What is objected last is already solved, because it is called the Sacrament of necessity, not because without it a man cannot be saved, but because he is bound to it, if he is able, and this being neglected, he cannot be saved.

### Scholion
**I.** Baptism of the Spirit [*flaminis*], which by the ancients was also called the baptism of penance, is perfect contrition of heart, or perfect charity, which always includes, at least implicitly, the vow [*votum*] of the Sacrament, as the Council of Trent teaches (Sess. XIV, c. 4) in these words: «Reconciliation itself is nonetheless not to be ascribed to that contrition apart from the vow of the Sacrament, which is included in it». — From the ecclesiastical teaching itself it is established that baptism is necessary for salvation (Trent, Sess. VII, can. 4 on baptism), yet in such a way that the baptism of the Spirit may in necessity supply the place of the Sacrament as to its primary effect (the infusion of grace), although it does not imprint the character; whence (ibid., can. 4 on the Sacraments in general) those are reproved who teach that, without the Sacraments of the new law, «or the vow of them, men obtain from God the grace of justification through faith alone, although all are not necessary for each one». — On *fundamentum* 2, cf. here *dub.* 2. The various kinds of baptisms Alexander of Hales weaves into a diffuse disputation, P. IV, q. 8, m. 9, a. 1–5. — On this first question: Alexander of Hales, loc. cit. a. 1, § 1. — Scotus, on this and the following questions, here q. 6. — St. Thomas, here q. 3, a. 3, quaestiunc. 1, 2; [Summa] III, q. 66, a. 11. — Bl. Albert, on this, here a. 6. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 2, a. 2. — Richard of Mediavilla, here q. 8, and Dionysius the Carthusian, here q. 6.

**II.** In solving the second question St. Bonaventure follows Alexander of Hales, and St. Thomas and many others also agree. Cf. Alexander of Hales, loc. cit. a. 5, § 1. — St. Thomas, here loc. cit. quaestiunc. 3; [Summa], loc. cit. a. 12. — Peter of Tarentaise, loc. cit. a. 3.

**III.** The third question other authors for the most part touch only briefly in the foregoing (St. Thomas, [Summa], loc. cit. a. 11, ad 2). Diffusely on the same Alexander of Hales treats, loc. cit. a. 4, § 1, 2, 3; briefly Richard of Mediavilla, loc. cit. q. 1.
