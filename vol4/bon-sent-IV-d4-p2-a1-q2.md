## *Quis baptismus sit dignior, utrum baptismus aquae, an sanguinis*

---

## Latin

<!-- page 108 -->

### Quaestio II. *Quis baptismus sit dignior, utrum baptismus aquae, an sanguinis.*

Secundo quaeritur de praeeminentia istorum, et quaeritur, quis baptismus sit dignior, utrum baptismus aquae, vel sanguinis. Et quod aquae, videtur:

1. Quia Damascenus[^1] distinguit novem genera baptismatum, ascendendo gradatim, et in ultimo ponit baptismum aquae sive fluminis: ergo si gradatim ascendit, sicut patet in aliis differentiis, videtur, quod illud quod ultimo ponit, est perfectius. Sed ultimo ponit baptismum aquae: ergo etc.

2. Item, baptismus aquae habet efficaciam a passione Christi[^2], sed baptismus sanguinis a passione Martyris; sed maior et nobilior est passio Christi et eius virtus quam passio Martyris et eius virtus: ergo maior est efficacia baptismi aquae quam baptismi sanguinis: ergo baptismus aquae nobilior.

3. Item, baptismus aquae tribuit gratiam gratum facientem, per quam acquiritur *aurea*; sed baptismus sanguinis ducit ad *aureolam*, quae est accidentale praemium[^3]: ergo si praemium substantiale praecellit accidentale, ergo baptismus ordinatus ad meritum substantiale et praemium praecellit alium; et sic etc.

4. Item, remedium illud, quod plures curat et a pluribus morbis et periculosioribus sanat, melius est et utilius; sed baptismus aquae plures personas sanat et a pluribus morbis quam baptismus sanguinis, quia ab omni culpa tam mortali quam veniali[^4]: ergo est remedium melius et utilius, ergo nobilius.

**Contra:**
1. Iudicum sexto[^5] super illud: *Tolles taurum* etc.; Glossa: «Baptismus in sanguine puriores facit, quam baptismus in aqua»; sed baptismus ille est perfectior, qui facit puriorem: ergo baptismus sanguinis est perfectior et nobilior baptismo aquae.

2. Item, Damascenus[^6] dicit, quod «baptismus sanguinis est valde bonus et venerandus, quia secundis sordibus non inquinatur»; sed baptismus aquae secundis sordibus inquinatur: ergo baptismus sanguinis est perfectior et nobilior baptismo aquae.

3. Item, processus est ab imperfecto ad perfectum[^7]; sed a baptismo aquae ad baptismum sanguinis sive martyrii recto ordine proceditur et non retroceditur: ergo ille baptismus, in quo est status perfectionis, est dignior.

4. Item, quod proximius est fini et certius et efficacius et ponit in altiori gradu, illud est simpliciter nobilius et melius[^8]; et talis est baptismus sanguinis, quia statim introducit in gloriam sine impedimento, nec tantum acquirit gloriam, immo gloriam eminentem: ergo etc.

> **Conclusio.** Et in liberando a malo et in ordinando ad bonum baptismus sanguinis excedit intensive baptismum fluminis, sed exceditur extensive.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod dignitatis praeeminentia in his baptismatibus conspicitur ex consideratione effectuum eorundem. Utriusque autem effectus, scilicet sanguinis et fluminis, est et in liberando a malo et ordinando ad bonum; et utroque modo se habent sicut excedentia et excessa. — Nam maior effectus sive efficacia in liberando a malo potest esse vel extensive, vel intensive. Si extensive, baptismus aquae excedit, quia plures personas liberat et a pluribus morbis, quia a peccato

<!-- page 109 -->

mortali et veniali. Baptismus autem sanguinis liberat paucos, et solum a veniali liberat, a mortali non, quia praesupponit caritatem; aliter nihil valeret ad salutem[^1b]. — Similiter in ordinando ad bonum maior potest excessus attendi dupliciter: aut extensive, aut intensive. Si extensive, sic praecellit baptismus aquae, quia dat gratiam gratis datam, scilicet characterem, et gratiam gratum facientem, quae disponit ad gloriam et praemium essentiale. Baptismus autem sanguinis gratiam gratum facientem non tribuit, sed consummat; nec tribuit characterem, per quem distinguitur anima et praeparatur ad gratiam[^2b].

Utroque modorum praedictorum baptismus sanguinis excedit intensive, sicut exceditur extensive. Nam efficacius et melius liberat a malo et culpae et poenae, quia omnem resecat et secundum potentiam et secundum actum. Similiter efficacius ordinat ad bonum, quia statim introducit in gloriam et perfectionem, et gloriosiorem hoc baptismo baptizatus consequitur palmam[^3b]. Respondendum est igitur ad quaestionem praedictam, quod se habent sicut excedentia et excessa. — Si tamen quaeras, quod magis praecellat, credo, quod baptismus martyrii, sicut ostendunt rationes inductae.

1. Ad illud ergo quod primo obiicitur, quod Damascenus ultimo loco ponit baptismum aquae; breviter respondendum, quod non ponit ultimo propter hoc, quod sit dignior, sed quia est in intentione tractandi principalior, et quia nomen baptismi magis proprie habet, quia mundat et interius et exterius.

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod baptismus aquae habet efficaciam a passione Christi: ergo etc.; dicendum, quod argumentum illud deficit dupliciter. Primo, quia passio Christi non operatur ibi secundum totam suam virtutem. Secundo, etiam quia baptismus martyrii efficaciam habet a passione Christi; nullum enim martyrium efficax est nisi per illam. Unde Apocalypsis septimo[^4b] scribitur de Martyribus: *Hi sunt, qui venerunt ex magna tribulatione et laverunt stolas suas et dealbaverunt eas in sanguine Agni*, non tantum in suo, sed etiam Agni. — Ratio autem, quare efficaciam habet maiorem, est, quoniam in baptismo sanguinis amplior et plenior est imitatio et professio passionis Christi quam in baptismo aquae. Nam ibi est professio verbo et signo sine actu, hic autem non solum signo et actione, sed et passione. Unde in baptismo aquae mors *significatur*, hic autem *suscipitur*; unde quia expressior est ibi fidei professio, ideo efficacior. Unde Augustinus loquens ad Fortunatum[^5b] de comparatione horum baptismatum dicit: «Baptizatus confitetur fidem suam coram sacerdote, Martyr coram persecutore. Ille post professionem aspergitur aqua, hic aspergitur sanguine. Ille manus impositione Pontificis accipit Spiritum sanctum, hic templum efficitur Spiritus sancti». Et ita patet, quod illud praecellit.

3. 4. Ultima duo non probant, quod[^6b] sit efficacius simpliciter, sed quod ad plura, quia ad gratiam gratum facientem et contra mortem culpae; hic autem, quamvis non det gratiam, tamen ipsam consummat. Quamvis enim baptismus sanguinis non auferat culpam, aufert tamen omnem possibilitatem ad culpam; et hoc maioris est perfectionis, quam delere culpam et dimittere possibilitatem ad culpam, immo inclinationem[^7b].

## English

<!-- page 108 -->

### Question II. *Which baptism is worthier, that of water or that of blood.*

Secondly it is asked concerning the preeminence of these [baptisms], and it is asked which baptism is worthier, that of water or [that] of blood. And that [the baptism] of water [is worthier] seems [so]:

1. Because Damascene[^1] distinguishes nine kinds of baptisms, ascending by degrees, and in the last place sets the baptism of water, that is, of water [*fluminis*]: therefore if he ascends by degrees, as is clear in the other distinctions, it seems that what he sets last is more perfect. But he sets the baptism of water last: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, the baptism of water has its efficacy from the Passion of Christ[^2], but the baptism of blood from the passion of a Martyr; but greater and nobler is the Passion of Christ and its power than the passion of a Martyr and its power: therefore greater is the efficacy of the baptism of water than of the baptism of blood: therefore the baptism of water is nobler.

3. Likewise, the baptism of water bestows sanctifying grace, by which the *aurea* [the essential reward] is acquired; but the baptism of blood leads to the *aureola*, which is an accidental reward[^3]: therefore if the substantial reward surpasses the accidental, then the baptism ordered to substantial merit and reward surpasses the other; and so etc.

4. Likewise, that remedy which cures more [people] and heals from more and more dangerous diseases is better and more useful; but the baptism of water heals more persons and from more diseases than the baptism of blood, because [it heals] from every fault, both mortal and venial[^4]: therefore it is a better and more useful remedy, therefore nobler.

**On the contrary:**
1. Judges, sixth [chapter][^5], on that [verse]: *You shall take the bull* etc.; the Gloss: «Baptism in blood makes [people] purer than baptism in water»; but that baptism is more perfect which makes [one] purer: therefore the baptism of blood is more perfect and nobler than the baptism of water.

2. Likewise, Damascene[^6] says that «the baptism of blood is very good and venerable, because it is not defiled by second defilements»; but the baptism of water is defiled by second defilements: therefore the baptism of blood is more perfect and nobler than the baptism of water.

3. Likewise, the progression is from the imperfect to the perfect[^7]; but from the baptism of water to the baptism of blood, that is, of martyrdom, one proceeds in right order and does not go backward: therefore that baptism, in which there is the state of perfection, is worthier.

4. Likewise, that which is nearer to the end and more certain and more efficacious and sets [one] in a higher degree, that is simply nobler and better[^8]; and such is the baptism of blood, because it at once introduces [one] into glory without impediment, nor does it merely acquire glory, but rather eminent glory: therefore etc.

> **Conclusion.** Both in freeing from evil and in ordering to the good, the baptism of blood exceeds the baptism of water [*fluminis*] intensively, but is exceeded extensively.

**I respond:** It must be said that the preeminence of dignity in these baptisms is discerned from the consideration of their effects. But the effect of each, namely of blood and of water, lies both in freeing from evil and in ordering to the good; and in both ways they stand as exceeding and exceeded. — For the greater effect or efficacy in freeing from evil can be either extensive or intensive. If extensive, the baptism of water exceeds, because it frees more persons and from more diseases, since [it frees] from

<!-- page 109 -->

mortal and venial sin. But the baptism of blood frees few, and frees only from venial [sin], not from mortal, because it presupposes charity; otherwise it would avail nothing for salvation[^1b]. — Likewise in ordering to the good the greater excess can be considered in two ways: either extensively or intensively. If extensively, thus the baptism of water excels, because it gives gratuitously-given grace, namely the character, and sanctifying grace, which disposes to glory and the essential reward. But the baptism of blood does not bestow sanctifying grace, but consummates it; nor does it bestow the character, by which the soul is marked off and prepared for grace[^2b].

In both of the aforesaid ways the baptism of blood exceeds intensively, just as it is exceeded extensively. For it frees from evil — both from fault and from punishment — more efficaciously and better, because it cuts off all [evil] both as to potency and as to act. Likewise it more efficaciously orders to the good, because it at once introduces [one] into glory and perfection, and the one baptized by this baptism attains a more glorious palm[^3b]. It must therefore be answered to the aforesaid question that they stand as exceeding and exceeded. — But if you ask which excels more, I believe [it is] the baptism of martyrdom, as the arguments adduced show.

1. To that, therefore, which is objected first, that Damascene sets the baptism of water in the last place; it must be briefly answered that he does not set it last on account of its being worthier, but because it is more principal in the intention of his treatment, and because it more properly bears the name of baptism, since it cleanses both inwardly and outwardly.

2. To that which is objected, that the baptism of water has its efficacy from the Passion of Christ: therefore etc.; it must be said that that argument fails in two ways. First, because the Passion of Christ does not operate there according to its whole power. Secondly, also because the baptism of martyrdom has its efficacy from the Passion of Christ; for no martyrdom is efficacious except through it. Hence in Revelation, seventh [chapter][^4b], it is written of the Martyrs: *These are they who came out of great tribulation and washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb*, not only in their own [blood], but also in the Lamb's. — But the reason why it has greater efficacy is that in the baptism of blood the imitation and profession of Christ's Passion is ampler and fuller than in the baptism of water. For there [in water] the profession is by word and sign without act, but here [in blood] not only by sign and action, but also by suffering. Hence in the baptism of water death is *signified*, but here it is *undergone*; whence, because the profession of faith is there more express, therefore it is more efficacious. Hence Augustine, speaking to Fortunatus[^5b] concerning the comparison of these baptisms, says: «The baptized confesses his faith before the priest, the Martyr before the persecutor. The former, after his profession, is sprinkled with water, the latter is sprinkled with blood. The former receives the Holy Spirit by the imposition of the Pontiff's hand, the latter is made a temple of the Holy Spirit». And thus it is clear that that [baptism of blood] excels.

3. 4. The last two [arguments] do not prove that[^6b] it is more efficacious simply, but that [it is so] as to more things, because [the baptism of water leads] to sanctifying grace and against the death of fault; but this [baptism of blood], although it does not give grace, nonetheless consummates it. For although the baptism of blood does not take away fault, it nonetheless takes away every possibility for fault; and this is of greater perfection than to delete fault and to remit the possibility for fault, or rather the inclination [to it][^7b].
