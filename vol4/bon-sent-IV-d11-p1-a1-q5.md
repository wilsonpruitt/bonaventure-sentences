## *Utrum haec conversio sit subita, an successiva.*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 248 -->

### Quaestio V. *Utrum haec conversio sit subita, vel successiva.*

Quinto quaeritur, utrum haec conversio sit subita, an successiva. Et quod sit *subita*, videtur:

1. Quia successio est per resistentiam suscipientis, ut patet in diffusione lucis in aëra, quia nihil resistens[^1]; sed ex parte panis nulla est resistentia: ergo nec successio aliqua.

2. Item, « quantum operatio est a virtute potentiori, tanto velocior », ut patet in sexto Physicorum[^2]; sed haec est a virtute infinita: ergo est in instanti.

3. Item, non ob aliud est successio in operatione nisi propter praeparationem et inductionem proprietatum[^3]; sed hic nulla est praeparatio: ergo nulla successio.

4. Item, si successio, aut haec est, quia panis successive abiicitur, aut quia corpus successive introducitur.

<!-- page 249 -->

Non quia panis; quia non est ponere, quare citius convertatur una pars quam alia, nec materia quam forma. Nec quia Christi *corpus*; quia illud non potest habere nisi *esse* perfectum et integrum.

**Contra:**

1. Etsi divina virtus ibi immediate operetur, operatur tamen ad verbi prolationem[^4]; sed verbi prolatio est successiva: ergo et conversio.

2. Item, si dicas, quod non operatur *per sermonem*; tamen *simul* operatur cum sermone, quia nec ante nec post; sed[^5] sermonis prolatio est successiva: ergo cum successivum non sit simul cum instantaneo, patet etc.

3. Item, si conversio est in instanti, quaero, in illo instanti, in quo est mutatio, quid sit; aut *panis*, aut *corpus*[^6]. Non panis, quia panis est terminus *a quo*; non corpus, quia est terminus, *ad quem* est mutatio; nec *aliquid aliud*: ergo nihil; sed hoc est impossibile: ergo mutatio non fit in instanti.

4. Item, panis in illa conversione habet *esse* ultimum, similiter corpus Christi primum; aut in *eodem instanti*, aut in *alio*. Si in *eodem*: ergo simul est panis et corpus. Si in *alio et alio*; sed inter quaelibet duo instantia cadit tempus medium[^7], et in illo medio est conversio: ergo etc.

> **CONCLUSIO.**
>
> Conversio panis in verum Christi corpus fit in instanti, non successive.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod haec conversio est subito et in instanti. Quamvis enim fiat ad verbi prolationem, non tamen per verbum sicut per effectivum principium, sed per Verbum increatum[^8], quod operatur in instanti.

2. Quod ergo obiicit, quod non sunt simul, dicendum, quod successivum non est simul cum instantaneo secundum totum nec secundum partem, sed tamen secundum aliquid sui, ut puta factum vel dictum esse, quod est vel in ratione medii, vel principii, vel finis. Unde certum est, quod quando totum dictum est, totum factum est; sed quando Dominus facit, ipse novit[^9].

3. Ad illud vero quod obiicitur de illo instanti, utrum sit panis; dicendum, quod in mutatione subita simul est *fieri* et *factum esse*, et simul *desinere* et *desiisse*; quia ergo in instanti desinit esse panis, non est panis. *Rursus*, quia incipit esse corpus vel convertitur in corpus, est corpus Christi.

4. Ad illud vero quod quaeritur de ultimo *esse* panis, multiplex est positio.

Quidam enim dicunt, quod quia panis habet *esse* temporale, sed corpus Christi *esse* aeternum, quod ultimum *esse* panis non est nisi in tempore, primum *esse* corporis in instanti. Unde sicut instans copulatur ad tempus, ita quod nihil est medium; sic et in proposito habere se dicunt. — Sed hoc est non probabilis fuga: primum, quia, si panis nunquam haberet esse in instanti, maneret obiectio, et non est hic solutio. Secundo, quia quod corpus Christi sit in altari vel sub speciebus, hoc est temporale. Tertio vero, quia utrumque habet *esse* permanens, et ideo *esse* utriusque est in instanti.

Propterea est alia positio, quod in eodem instanti est ultimo *esse panem* et primo *esse corpus*, eodem, inquam, secundum *substantiam*, differente secundum *rationem*; quia secundum quod est mensura ultimi *esse* panis, est terminus *praeteriti*, secundum quod primi *esse corporis*, est principium *futuri*. — Sed haec positio non potest stare, quia quaecumque sit diversitas in ratione in uno instanti, nunquam contradictoria sunt simul vera in eodem: ergo nec *disparata* de eodem. Et *praeterea*, si *praeteritum* in sui termino esset, et *futurum* esset in sui initio; nunquam continuarentur per unum instans, immo essent simul.

Propterea est tertia positio, quod est in uno instanti secundum *rem*, sed plura secundum *virtutem*. Sicut enim unus punctus fit duo puncta divisione lineae et commensuratione, ut puta si duarum linearum fiat contiguatio super lineam continuam substratam; ille unus punctus, ad quem contiguantur, aequivalet *duobus*: sic in instanti, quamvis non multiplicetur, quia tempus non dividitur, tamen *duobus* aequivalet propter commensurationem duorum ultimorum, durationis scilicet panis et corporis. — Sed illud non potest stare, quia in *contiguis* ultima simul sunt; sed in *durationibus*, quae se habent secundum prius et posterius,

<!-- page 250 -->

impossibile est, quod ultima sint simul; unde in illa *contiguatione* punctus substratus non *fit duo*, sed duo puncta *uniuntur in uno*, nec tamen *cedunt* in unum propter distinctionem corporum, in quibus sunt. Et *iterum*, quantumcumque illud verum sit, quod duo *indivisibilia* sint simul, nunquam tamen verum est, quod duo *contradictorie opposita* simul insint eidem, quia sunt incompossibilia; nec etiam ea quae necessario requirunt ordinem, non tantum *causaliter* sive secundum naturam, sed etiam secundum *esse* in actu et durationem actualem.

Propterea est quarta positio, quod est in *alio et alio* instanti secundum *rem*, eo modo, quo in tempore est ponere instantia multa; quia *unum* est secundum suam *essentiam*, cuius fluxus est continuus; unde non[^10] est ponere plura instantia, ut *consequenter* se habentia *secundum se*; sed tamen, secundum quod *mensurat* duas actiones discontinuas est in eo duo signare; sicut si linea secet aliam, signat duo puncta. — Sed illud non potest stare, quia in nullo *continuo* nec esse nec intelligi nec signari possunt vere duo indivisibilia, ipso manente *continuo*, quae se habeant *consequenter*, alioquin intelligitur de necessitate ut multiplicatum. Si ergo tempus manet non intercisum, patet etc.

Et ideo dicendum est ultimo, quod tempus sive instans mensura ultimi *esse* panis et primi *esse* corporis, potest dupliciter accipi: vel in *duratione propria* uniuscuiusque, sicut dicit Philosophus, quod « unumquodque propria periodo mensuratur », quae illa quidem cum re incipit et desinit; et sic sunt *diversae mensurae discontinuae* et diversa instantia *consequenter se habentia*, sicut sunt duo *esse*, *esse* scilicet panis et corporis. Si autem accipiatur instans secundum *communem mensuram*, quae est *tempus continuum*, non intercisum, ut est mensura motus primi mobilis, vel etiam ut tempus consideratur secundum *essentiam*, non secundum *esse*; tunc accipit *determinatum instans* secundum nostram signationem. Signetur ergo unum instans *in tempore*, in quo ultimo est *panis*; signetur et aliud instans, in quo primo est *corpus*? Ego dico, non signetur, quia[^11] non est signare *duo instantia consequenter* se habentia in *continuo*, manente continuo; unde quodlibet horum *per se* signari potest, sed duo *simul* impossibile est signare. Et sic patet, quod in alio et alio instanti habet esse panis et corpus secundum *rem*, et secundum quod est, *mensura realis et propria*; licet secundum *mensuram communem* duo instantia immediate signare non contingat.

— Hic autem modus solvendi rationabilior et certior videtur omnibus praedictis, quoniam supra tria principia certa fundatur. Primum est, quod in eodem *instanti simplici* impossibile est contradictoria esse, quantumcumque instans habeat diversitatem secundum considerationem. Secundum est, quod in *continuo*, manente continuo, impossibile est instantia esse *consequenter* nec vere signari. Tertium est, quod inter *esse panem* et *corpus* Christi, vel inter duo contradictoria nihil est medium[^12].

## English

### Question V. *Whether this conversion is sudden or successive.*

Fifth, it is asked whether this conversion is sudden or successive. And that it is *sudden*, it seems:

1. Because succession occurs through the resistance of the recipient, as is clear in the diffusion of light into air, since [there is succession only when] something resists[^1]; but on the side of the bread there is no resistance: therefore there is no succession either.

2. Likewise, "the more powerful the agent from which an operation proceeds, the swifter it is," as is clear in the sixth book of the *Physics*[^2]; but this [conversion] is from an infinite power: therefore it is in an instant.

3. Likewise, there is no succession in an operation except on account of the preparation and the introduction of properties[^3]; but here there is no preparation: therefore no succession.

4. Likewise, if there were succession, either it would be because the bread is cast off successively, or because the body is introduced successively.

Not because of the bread; for there is no ground for asserting why one part should be converted sooner than another, nor the matter sooner than the form. Nor because of Christ's *body*; for that cannot have any but a perfect and entire *being*.

**On the contrary:**

1. Although the divine power operates there immediately, it nevertheless operates at the utterance of the word[^4]; but the utterance of the word is successive: therefore the conversion also [is successive].

2. Likewise, if you say that it does not operate *through the discourse*; nevertheless it operates *together* with the discourse, since [it operates] neither before nor after; but[^5] the utterance of the discourse is successive: therefore, since what is successive is not simultaneous with what is instantaneous, it is clear, etc.

3. Likewise, if the conversion is in an instant, I ask: in that instant in which the change occurs, what is there? — either *bread* or *body*[^6]. Not bread, since bread is the term *from which*; not body, since [the body] is the term *to which* the change is; nor *anything else*: therefore nothing; but this is impossible: therefore the change does not occur in an instant.

4. Likewise, the bread in that conversion has its last *being*, and similarly Christ's body its first; [and this is] either in the *same instant* or in *another*. If in the *same*: then bread and body exist simultaneously. If in *one and another*; but between any two instants there falls an intervening time[^7], and in that intervening [time] the conversion occurs: therefore etc.

> **CONCLUSION.**
>
> The conversion of the bread into the true body of Christ occurs in an instant, not successively.

**I respond:** It must be said that this conversion is sudden and in an instant. For although it occurs at the utterance of the word, it does not occur through the word as through an effective principle, but through the uncreated *Word*[^8], which operates in an instant.

2. As for the objection that they are not simultaneous, it must be said that what is successive is not simultaneous with what is instantaneous as a whole nor according to a part, but nevertheless according to something of itself — for instance, its having-been-done or having-been-said, which is either in the character of a middle, or of a beginning, or of an end. Hence it is certain that when the whole has been said, the whole has been done; but when the Lord does it, He Himself knows[^9].

3. As for the objection concerning that instant, whether it be bread; it must be said that in a sudden change the *coming-to-be* and the *having-been-made* are simultaneous, and the *ceasing* and the *having-ceased* are simultaneous; since therefore in an instant the bread ceases to be, it is not bread. *Again*, since it begins to be the body, or is converted into the body, it is the body of Christ.

4. As for what is asked concerning the last *being* of the bread, there are several positions.

For some say that, since the bread has *temporal* being but Christ's body *eternal* being, the last *being* of the bread is only in time, the first *being* of the body in an instant. Hence, just as the instant is joined to time so that there is nothing in between, so they say it holds in the case at hand. — But this is no probable evasion: first, because if the bread never had being in an instant, the objection would remain, and there is no solution here. Secondly, because the fact that Christ's body is upon the altar or under the species, this is temporal. Thirdly, because each has permanent *being*, and therefore the *being* of each is in an instant.

Therefore there is another position: that in the same instant there is the last *being of the bread* and the first *being of the body* — the same, I say, according to *substance*, differing according to *reason*; because insofar as it is the measure of the last *being* of the bread, it is the term of the *past*, [and] insofar as [it is the measure] of the first *being of the body*, it is the beginning of the *future*. — But this position cannot stand, because whatever diversity there may be in reason within one instant, contradictories are never simultaneously true in the same [instant]: therefore neither are *disparate* [things true] of the same. And *furthermore*, if the *past* were in its term, and the *future* in its beginning, they would never be continuous through one instant; rather, they would be simultaneous.

Therefore there is a third position: that it is in one instant according to *reality*, but several according to *virtual power*. For just as one point becomes two points by the division of a line and by commensuration — as, for instance, if a juncture of two lines is made upon an underlying continuous line; that one point at which they are joined is equivalent to *two*: so in the instant, although it is not multiplied (since time is not divided), nevertheless it is equivalent to *two* on account of the commensuration of two terms, namely of the duration of the bread and of the body. — But this cannot stand, because in *contiguous* things the terms are simultaneous; but in *durations*, which are ordered according to before and after,

it is impossible that the terms be simultaneous; whence in that *junction* the underlying point does not *become two*, but two points *are united in one*, yet they do not *merge* into one, on account of the distinction of the bodies in which they are. And *again*, however true it may be that two *indivisibles* are simultaneous, it is nevertheless never true that two *contradictory opposites* are simultaneously present in the same [subject], since they are incompossible; nor likewise those things which necessarily require an order — not only *causally* or according to nature, but also according to *being* in act and actual duration.

Therefore there is a fourth position: that it is in *one and another* instant according to *reality*, in the manner in which in time many instants are to be posited; because [time] is *one* according to its *essence*, whose flow is continuous; hence one must not[^10] posit several instants as ordered *consequently in themselves*; but nevertheless, insofar as it *measures* two discontinuous actions, two [instants] are to be marked in it; just as if a line cuts another, it marks two points. — But this cannot stand, because in no *continuum*, while it remains continuous, can two indivisibles truly be, or be understood, or be marked off, as ordered *consequently*; otherwise [the continuum] is of necessity understood as multiplied. If, then, time remains uninterrupted, it is clear, etc.

And therefore it must be said, lastly, that the time or instant which is the measure of the last *being* of the bread and the first *being* of the body can be taken in two ways: either in the *proper duration* of each, as the Philosopher says, that "each thing is measured by its proper period," which [duration] indeed begins and ceases with the thing; and thus there are *diverse discontinuous measures* and diverse instants *ordered consequently*, just as there are two *beings*, namely the *being* of the bread and of the body. But if the instant is taken according to the *common measure*, which is *continuous time*, uninterrupted, as it is the measure of the motion of the first mobile, or also as time is considered according to its *essence*, not according to its *being*; then it takes a *determinate instant* according to our marking. Let one instant then be marked *in time*, in which the bread last is; and let another instant be marked, in which the body first is? I say: let it not be marked, because[^11] one cannot mark *two instants ordered consequently* in a *continuum*, the continuum remaining; whence each of these can be marked *by itself*, but it is impossible to mark the two *simultaneously*. And thus it is clear that in one and another instant the bread and the body have being according to *reality*, and insofar as it is, [it is] a *real and proper measure*; although according to the *common measure* it does not happen that the two instants are marked immediately.

— Now this manner of solving seems more reasonable and more certain than all the aforesaid, since it is founded upon three certain principles. The first is that in the same *simple instant* it is impossible for contradictories to be, however much the instant may have diversity according to consideration. The second is that in a *continuum*, while it remains continuous, it is impossible for instants to be *consequent*, nor to be truly marked off. The third is that between *being bread* and [being] the *body* of Christ, or between two contradictories, there is nothing in between[^12].
