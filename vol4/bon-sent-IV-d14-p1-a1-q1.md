# IV Sent., d. 14, p. 1, a. 1, q. 1
## *Utrum poenitentia sit virtus*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 318 -->

### Tractatio Quaestionum.

Ad intelligentiam huius partis quaeritur de poenitentia-virtute, et circa hoc duo principaliter quaeruntur.

*Primo* quaeritur de ipsa absolute.

*Secundo*, in comparatione.

Circa primum quaeruntur tria.

*Primo* quaeritur, utrum poenitentia sit virtus.

*Secundo*, dato quod sic, quaeritur, utrum sit virtus generalis, an specialis.

*Tertio* quaeritur, utrum sit virtus theologica, vel cardinalis.

### Articulus I. *De virtute poenitentiae absolute.*
### Quaestio I. *Utrum poenitentia sit virtus.*

Circa primum proceditur sic:

1. Matthaei tertio[^1]: *Poenitentiam agite*; Glossa: «*Prima virtus est per poenitentiam perimere veterem hominem*»; hoc ipsum dicit Magister in littera.

2. Item, sicut dicit Philosophus[^2], «*virtus est quae habentem perficit et opus eius bonum reddit*»; sed talis est poenitentia: ergo est virtus.

3. Item, nullus actus est laudabilis nec meritorius nisi actus virtutis[^3]; sed poenitere est laudabile et meritorium: ergo est actus virtutis. Sed est actus poenitentiae: ergo poenitentia est virtus.

4. Item, nihil expellit vitium et peccatum nisi gratia vel virtus[^4]; sed poenitentia expellit haec: ergo poenitentia est gratia, vel virtus. Sed non est gratia, quia gratia dicit pure quod a Deo est, poenitentia autem actum nostrum: ergo etc.

5. Item, *poenitere* non ideo bonum est, quia praeceptum, sed magis e converso: ergo per se bonum. Et iterum, arduum et difficile est modo debito poenitere, et circa ipsum contingit errare: ergo necessario indigemus virtute ordinante ad hoc[^5]; sed haec non est nisi poenitentia: ergo etc.

**Contra:** 1. Sacramentum et virtus ex opposito dividuntur[^6]; sed poenitentia est Sacramentum: ergo non est virtus. *Si dicas*, quod poenitentia dupliciter accipitur et secundum duas acceptiones est virtus et Sacramentum; quaeritur: quare magis poenitentia quam baptismus, vel aliud Sacramentum?

2. Item, poenitentia est passio; sed «*omnis virtus, ut dicit Philosophus[^7], est habitus*»: ergo poenitentia non est virtus.

3. Item, «*omnis virtus habet obiectum bonum, sicut scientia verum*[^8]»; sed poenitentiae obiectum est peccatum — poenitens enim non poenitet recte nisi de culpa — ergo etc.

4. Item, omnes virtutes sunt connexae et manent aliquo modo in patria[^9] et fuerunt in Christo et in primo homine; sed poenitentia non fuit in Christo nec in primo homine nec etiam erit in patria: ergo etc.

> **Conclusio.** Poenitentia recte dici potest gratia, virtus, Sacramentum, actus et passio, et hoc sensu analogo.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod circa hoc multiplex fuit opinio. — Quidam enim dicere voluerunt, quod poenitentia non est virtus, sed actus virtutis, non unius, sed omnium, et concurrunt omnes ad hunc actum; sicut chordae citharae ad unum sonum secundum regulam tangentis, sic omnes virtutes secundum dictamen prudentiae[^10]. — Sed ista positio non est conveniens. Si enim iste actus est bonus et difficilis, et circa quem contingit errare;

<!-- page 319 -->

sicut ira per zelum est habitus, quo homo irascitur vitiis alienis[^1b]; sic poenitentia est habitus, quo homo irascitur vitiis *propriis*. Si enim non esset habitus, tunc non diceretur habere poenitentiam nisi actu poenitens.

Secunda opinio fuit, quod est *habitus*, non[^2b] virtutis, sed *gratiae*; nec tantum nominat gratiam secundum *substantiam*, sed etiam secundum *actum*, ut sicut *gratia baptismalis* dicitur innocentia, sic *gratia iustificationis* poenitentia. — Sed haec positio non debet stare: quia gratia non dirigit liberum arbitrium ad aliquem motum specialem per se, nisi mediante *virtute*[^3b]; quod facit poenitentia.

Tertia positio est, quod poenitentia est *virtus*, sed non est virtus nisi *extenso nomine* virtutis. Nam virtus *proprie* est in dirigendo ad bonum — virtus enim est qualitas mentis, qua recte vivitur[^4b] — sed poenitentia revocat a malo. — Sed haec positio non videtur adhuc sufficiens, quia poenitentia non facit tantum *dolere* de culpa perpetrata, sed etiam facit *emendare*; unde eius est subiectum punire et Deo reconciliare[^5b]; et hoc patet ex definitione[^6b], et ita est virtus proprie.

*Ultima positio* est, quod poenitentia est *habitus virtutis* et virtutis secundum propriam acceptionem, non tantum secundum communem. — Et haec probabilior est, ut credo, sicut patebit melius in sequentibus[^7b].

Ad intelligentiam autem obiectorum notandum, quod poenitentia est expulsiva peccati actualis, quod est macula animam *deformans* et iterum ad malum inclinans, sive a bono *dehabilitans* et iterum naturam *vulnerans*; quam etiam perpetravit quis *per actum voluntatis cum delectatione et libidine*[^8b]. Quia igitur delere culpam ut *deformantem* est *gratiae* reformantis; ut *dehabilitantem*, *virtutis* habilitantis; ut *vulnerantem*, *Sacramenti* curantis; ut *actu perpetratam*, per *actum* similiter *voluntatis*; ut cum *delectatione* perpetratam, cum *passione* contraria: ideo poenitentia recte dicitur gratia, virtus, Sacramentum, actus et passio. Et iste modus est secundum diversas naturas in ea repertas, tamen per ordinationem ad unum; unde non dicitur poenitentia his modis omnino aequivoce, sed analogice. — Et propter hoc concedendae sunt rationes ad primam partem.

1. 2. Ad illud autem quod obiicitur in contrarium, quia est Sacramentum et passio; patet responsio et ratio illius analogiae; non sic enim est in aliis Sacramentis. Quia enim poenitentia respicit culpam *actualem*, in cuius deletione concurrit motus liberi arbitrii concordans gratiae ad culpae expulsionem[^9b], qui quidem motus rectus et virtuosus est; et propter displicentiam peccati, quod necesse est esse perpetratum, non potest esse sine *passione* doloris: patet, quare poenitentia magis est *virtus* quam alia Sacramenta; patet etiam, quare magis habet *passionem* coniunctam quam alia.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod omnis virtus habet *obiectum bonum*; dicendum, quod falsum est — instantia enim est in temperantia, in pietate et etiam in fortitudine[^10b] — sed sufficit, quod actus sit bonus. Nihilominus tamen poenitentia duplicem habet actum, scilicet *refugere* vel detestari malum et *emendare*; isti sunt actus ordinati, et ratione secundi habet *obiectum bonum*.

4. Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur de connexione virtutum et huiusmodi; dicendum, quod virtutes habent connexionem et durationem secundum id quod *perfectionis* est in ipsis; poenitentia autem habet aliquid perfectionis, et hoc est *velle emendare*, si in aliquo contingat offendere. Et haec quidem voluntas est in omnibus iustis; impossibile enim est, quod sit voluntas recta sine hoc. Habet etiam aliquid *imperfectionis*, sicut est luctus pro peccato perpetrato; et ratione huius non attenditur connexio nec duratio, immo potius fit evacuatio, tanquam eius quod est *ex parte*[^11b].

### Scholion

**I.** Vocabulum *poenitentiae* communiter sumitur quadrupliciter, scilicet ut *passio* sensibilis doloris pro peccatis, ut *habitus* virtutis habilitans ad debito modo poenitendum, ut *actus* eiusdem virtutis, denique ut *Sacramentum*; quibus S. Bonav. (hic in fine corp.) quintum addit, quod est *gratia* reformans. De essentia huius virtutis vide infra dub. 1. et p. II. dubia 2-6; quibus locis contra Lutherum, qui veram poenitentiam in sola emendatione vitae futurae posuit, iam stabilitur doctrina Concilii Trident. (Sess. 14. c. 4.) docentis, «contritionem non solum cessationem a peccato et vitae novae propositum et inchoationem, sed veteris etiam odium continere». S. Bonav. docet etiam (infra p. I. et II. dub. 4.), detestationem mali praeteriti esse actum poenitentiae principalem, alterum vero ex consequenti huic annexum. Quod detestatio et displicentia de peccato praeterito sit actus principalis poenitentiae approbat S. Thom. (S. III. q. 85. a. 2); Scot. autem vult (hic q. 2. n. 5.), principalem actum eius esse velle vindicare peccatum (cfr. Bonav., hic p. II. dub. 6.).

**II.** Quoad 1. quaestionem notamus, B. Albertum tenere 3. opinionem hic relatam, refragante sententia communi. — De eadem quaestione: Alex. Hal., S. p. IV. q. 12. m. 1. a. 1. § 1. — Scot. de hac et 2 seqq. qq. in utroque Scripto hic q. 2. — S. Thom., hic q. 1. a. 1. quaestiunc. 2; S. III. q. 85. a. 1. — B. Albert., hic a. 3. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. unica a. 2. — Richard. a Med. hic, a. 1. q. 1. — Durand., hic q. 1. — Dionys. Carth., de hac et 2 seqq. qq. hic q. 1.

**III.** In seq. (2.) quaestione solvenda communiter docetur, quod licet obiectum *materiale* poenitentiae se extendat ad omnia peccata, etiam venialia (infra q. 16. a. 3. q. 2.), obiectum tamen *formale* esse quid speciale et constituere virtutem *specialem*, ut explicatur hic ad 3. De *obiecto formali* cfr. infra q. 3. et dub. 4; circa quod plures opiniones circumferuntur, quae magis in modo dicendi quam in re notabili inter se differre videntur. — De eadem quaestione: Alex. Hal., loc. cit. § 2. — S. Thom., loc. cit. quaestiunc. 3; S. loc. cit. a. 2. — B. Albert., hic a. 4. 5. — Petr. a Tar., loc. cit. quaestiunc. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 1. q. 2. et a. 2. q. 1. — Durand., de hac et seq. q. hic q. 2.

**IV.** Quoad 3. quaestionem conveniunt doctores in hoc, quod poenitentia non sit virtus theologica, et quod aliquatenus reducenda sit ad cardinalem virtutem *iustitiae*. Hoc in scholis fit dupliciter: vel reducitur ad iustitiam *commutativam* — non quidem ut pars eius subiectiva, sed *potentialis* tantum (quia scilicet ad eam reducitur sicut imperfectum ad perfectum, cum ordinata sit tantum ad aliquos actus secundarios iustitiae commutativae) — vel ad iustitiam *vindicativam*, licet imperfectam. Illud docet S. Thom. (S. loc. cit. a. 3), hoc vero Scot. (hic q. 2. n. 8.). — Porro constat, quod vera poenitentia sit habitus supernaturalis; sed controvertitur, utrum sit habitus *per se infusus*, ut dicit schola S. Thomae, an frequenter tantum *auxilio gratiae acquisitus*, vel etiam *infusus per accidens*, ut affirmant cum Scoto (loc. cit. n. 20.) Durandus aliique Scotistae. Utramque opinionem ut probabilem ponit Richard. a Med. (hic a. 4. q. 2.). Redit hic controversia in scholio ad III. Sent. d. 33. a. 1. q. 3. exposita. — Quoad quaestionem ibi tractatam cfr. Bonav., infra d. 17. p. I. a. 2. q. 3. ad 4, et Richard. a Med., hic a. 6. q. 1. — De ipsa 3. quaestione praeter locos citt.: Alex. Hal., loc. cit. § 3. 4. — S. Thom., hic q. 1. a. 1. quaestiunc. 4. 5; B. Albert., hic a. 6. 7. — Petr. a Tar., loc. cit. a. 3. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 2. q. 2.

## English

### Treatment of the Questions.

For the understanding of this part, inquiry is made concerning penance-as-a-virtue, and concerning this two things are principally asked.

*First*, [it is] asked concerning it absolutely.

*Second*, in comparison.

Concerning the first, three things are asked.

*First*, it is asked whether penance is a virtue.

*Second*, granting that it is, it is asked whether it is a general or a special virtue.

*Third*, it is asked whether it is a theological or a cardinal virtue.

### Article I. *On the virtue of penance absolutely.*
### Question I. *Whether penance is a virtue.*

Concerning the first [point], one proceeds thus:

1. Matthew, chapter three[^1]: *Do penance*; the Gloss: «*The first virtue is, through penance, to slay the old man*»; the Master says this same thing in the text.

2. Likewise, as the Philosopher says[^2], «*virtue is that which perfects the one having it and renders his work good*»; but such is penance: therefore it is a virtue.

3. Likewise, no act is praiseworthy or meritorious except an act of virtue[^3]; but to do penance is praiseworthy and meritorious: therefore it is an act of virtue. But it is the act of penance: therefore penance is a virtue.

4. Likewise, nothing expels vice and sin except grace or virtue[^4]; but penance expels these: therefore penance is grace, or virtue. But it is not grace, because grace expresses purely what is from God, whereas penance [expresses] our act: therefore etc.

5. Likewise, *to do penance* is not good because it is commanded, but rather the converse: therefore it is good in itself. And again, it is arduous and difficult to do penance in the due manner, and in this matter one happens to err: therefore we necessarily need a virtue ordering [us] to this[^5]; but this is nothing but penance: therefore etc.

**On the contrary:** 1. Sacrament and virtue are divided by opposition[^6]; but penance is a Sacrament: therefore it is not a virtue. *If you say* that penance is taken in two ways and according to the two acceptations is a virtue and a Sacrament; it is asked: why penance rather than baptism, or another Sacrament?

2. Likewise, penance is a passion; but «*every virtue, as the Philosopher says[^7], is a habit*»: therefore penance is not a virtue.

3. Likewise, «*every virtue has a good object, as knowledge [has] the true*[^8]»; but the object of penance is sin — for the penitent does not rightly repent except of fault — therefore etc.

4. Likewise, all the virtues are connected and remain in some manner in the fatherland[^9] and were in Christ and in the first man; but penance was not in Christ nor in the first man nor will it even be in the fatherland: therefore etc.

> **Conclusion.** Penance can rightly be called grace, virtue, Sacrament, act, and passion, and this in an analogous sense.

**I respond:** It must be said that concerning this there was a manifold opinion. — For certain men wished to say that penance is not a virtue, but an act of virtue — not of one [virtue], but of all, and all concur toward this act; just as the strings of a harp [concur] toward one sound according to the rule of the one playing, so all the virtues [concur] according to the dictate of prudence[^10]. — But this position is not fitting. For if this act is good and difficult, and one in which one happens to err;

<!-- page 319 -->

just as anger through zeal is a habit by which a man is angry at the vices of others[^1b], so penance is a habit by which a man is angry at his *own* vices. For if it were not a habit, then one would not be said to have penance except while actually repenting.

The second opinion was that it is a *habit*, not[^2b] of virtue, but of *grace*; nor does it name grace only according to *substance*, but also according to *act*, so that just as *baptismal grace* is called innocence, so *the grace of justification* [is called] penance. — But this position cannot stand: because grace does not direct free choice toward any special motion of itself, except by the mediation of *virtue*[^3b]; which penance does.

The third position is that penance is a *virtue*, but is not a virtue except by an *extended name* of virtue. For virtue *properly* consists in directing toward the good — for virtue is a quality of mind by which one lives rightly[^4b] — but penance calls back from evil. — But this position does not yet seem sufficient, because penance does not only make [one] *grieve* over the fault committed, but also makes [one] *amend*; whence its [office] is to punish the subject and reconcile [it] to God[^5b]; and this is clear from the definition[^6b], and so it is properly a virtue.

*The last position* is that penance is a *habit of virtue* and of virtue according to its proper acceptation, not only according to the common [acceptation]. — And this is more probable, as I believe, as will appear better in what follows[^7b].

But for the understanding of the objections it must be noted that penance is expulsive of actual sin, which is a stain *deforming* the soul and again inclining [it] to evil, or *disabling* [it] from the good and again *wounding* nature; which one has also committed *through an act of the will with delight and lust*[^8b]. Since, therefore, to blot out fault as *deforming* belongs to *grace* reforming; as *disabling*, to *virtue* enabling; as *wounding*, to the *Sacrament* healing; as *actually committed*, through an *act* likewise *of the will*; as committed with *delight*, through a contrary *passion*: therefore penance is rightly called grace, virtue, Sacrament, act, and passion. And this manner [of speaking] is according to the diverse natures found in it, yet by an ordering toward one [thing]; whence penance is not called [by] these modes altogether equivocally, but analogically. — And on account of this the arguments for the first part are to be conceded.

1. 2. To that which is objected to the contrary, [namely] that it is a Sacrament and a passion; the response and the ground of that analogy is clear; for it is not so in the other Sacraments. For because penance regards *actual* fault, in whose deletion there concurs a motion of free choice agreeing with grace for the expulsion of fault[^9b], which motion indeed is right and virtuous; and on account of the displeasure at the sin, which it is necessary to have been committed, it cannot be without a *passion* of grief: it is clear why penance is more a *virtue* than the other Sacraments; it is clear also why it has a *passion* more conjoined [to it] than the others.

3. To that which is objected, [namely] that every virtue has a *good object*; it must be said that this is false — for there is a counterinstance in temperance, in piety, and also in fortitude[^10b] — but it suffices that the act be good. Nevertheless penance has a twofold act, namely *to flee* or detest evil and *to amend*; these are ordered acts, and by reason of the second it has a *good object*.

4. To that, then, which is objected concerning the connection of the virtues and the like; it must be said that the virtues have connection and duration according to that which is of *perfection* in them; but penance has something of perfection, and this is *to will to amend*, if one happen to offend in anything. And this will indeed is in all the just; for it is impossible that there be a right will without this. It has also something of *imperfection*, such as is grief for the sin committed; and by reason of this neither connection nor duration is attended to, nay rather there comes about an evacuation, as of that which is *partial*[^11b].

### Scholion

**I.** The word *penance* is commonly taken in four ways, namely as the *passion* of sensible grief for sins, as the *habit* of virtue enabling [one] to do penance in the due manner, as the *act* of the same virtue, and finally as a *Sacrament*; to which S. Bonaventure (here at the end of the body) adds a fifth, which is *reforming grace*. On the essence of this virtue see below dub. 1 and p. II, dubia 2–6; in which places, against Luther — who placed true penance in the mere amendment of future life — the doctrine of the Council of Trent (Sess. 14, c. 4) is now established, teaching that «contrition embraces not only cessation from sin and the purpose and beginning of a new life, but also a hatred of the old». S. Bonaventure also teaches (below p. I and II, dub. 4) that the detestation of past evil is the principal act of penance, the other [being] annexed to this consequentially. That the detestation of and displeasure at past sin is the principal act of penance S. Thomas approves (S. III, q. 85, a. 2); but Scotus holds (here q. 2, n. 5) that its principal act is to will to punish sin (cf. Bonav., here p. II, dub. 6).

**II.** As to the 1st question we note that Bl. Albert holds the 3rd opinion here reported, the common opinion gainsaying [it]. — On the same question: Alex. of Hales, S. p. IV, q. 12, m. 1, a. 1, § 1. — Scotus on this and the 2 following questions here in both Writings, q. 2. — S. Thomas, here q. 1, a. 1, little-question 2; S. III, q. 85, a. 1. — Bl. Albert, here a. 3. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. unica, a. 2. — Richard of Mediavilla here, a. 1, q. 1. — Durandus, here q. 1. — Denis the Carthusian, on this and the 2 following questions, here q. 1.

**III.** In the following (2nd) question, to be solved, it is commonly taught that although the *material* object of penance extends to all sins, even venial ones (below q. 16, a. 3, q. 2), the *formal* object is nevertheless something special and constitutes a *special* virtue, as is explained here in the [reply] to [obj.] 3. On the *formal object* cf. below q. 3 and dub. 4; concerning which several opinions are current, which seem to differ rather in manner of speaking than in any notable matter among themselves. — On the same question: Alex. of Hales, loc. cit. § 2. — S. Thomas, loc. cit. little-question 3; S. loc. cit. a. 2. — Bl. Albert, here a. 4, 5. — Peter of Tarentaise, loc. cit. little-question. — Richard of Mediavilla, here a. 1, q. 2, and a. 2, q. 1. — Durandus, on this and the following question, here q. 2.

**IV.** As to the 3rd question the doctors agree in this, that penance is not a theological virtue, and that it is in some measure to be reduced to the cardinal virtue of *justice*. This is done in the schools in two ways: either it is reduced to *commutative* justice — not indeed as a subjective part of it, but only as a *potential* [part] (because, namely, it is reduced to it as the imperfect to the perfect, since it is ordered only to certain secondary acts of commutative justice) — or to *vindictive* justice, although imperfect. The former S. Thomas teaches (S. loc. cit. a. 3), the latter Scotus (here q. 2, n. 8). — Further, it is established that true penance is a supernatural habit; but it is controverted whether it is a habit *infused through itself*, as the school of S. Thomas says, or frequently only *acquired with the help of grace*, or even *infused per accidens*, as Durandus and the other Scotists affirm with Scotus (loc. cit. n. 20). Richard of Mediavilla posits both opinions as probable (here a. 4, q. 2). The controversy returns here in the scholion expounded on III Sent. d. 33, a. 1, q. 3. — As to the question there treated cf. Bonav., below d. 17, p. I, a. 2, q. 3, ad 4, and Richard of Mediavilla, here a. 6, q. 1. — On the 3rd question itself, besides the passages cited: Alex. of Hales, loc. cit. § 3, 4. — S. Thomas, here q. 1, a. 1, little-questions 4, 5; Bl. Albert, here a. 6, 7. — Peter of Tarentaise, loc. cit. a. 3. — Richard of Mediavilla, here a. 2, q. 2.
