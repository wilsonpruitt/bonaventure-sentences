## Latin

### Dubia circa litteram Magistri.

### Dub. I.

In parte ista sunt dubitationes circa litteram, et primo dubitatur de definitione Sacramenti, quam ponit Magister: *Sacramentum est sacrae rei signum*. Videtur enim male assignare, eo quod videtur aliis convenire[^1], quae non sunt Sacramenta, et tamen sunt sacrae rei signum, ut est hoc nomen *Deus*. *Si dicas*, quod sit signum rei sacrae et ut sacrae; tunc hoc nomen *sanctum* et *sanctificans* est Sacramentum. *Si tu dicas*, quod hoc intelligitur de signo visibili; tunc ergo ratio haec non est *convertibilis*[^2].

**Respondeo:** Ad hoc dicunt aliqui, quod Sacramentum accipitur tripliciter, scilicet *communiter* et *proprie* et *propriissime*; et secundum hoc Magister dat tres definitiones. Et prima est Sacramenti *largissime* accepti[^3]. — Potest tamen dici secundum Hugonem aliter[^4], quod illa non est *definitio*, sed *nominis interpretatio*; et Magister non intendit ibi definitionem Sacramenti ponere, sed nominis interpretationem sive verbi notificationem. Et ex prima interpretatione habetur, quod Sacramentum sit signum rei sacrae; ex secunda, quod sit signum visibile rei sacrae et sanctificantis; ex tertia, quod sit signum[^5] rei sacrae et sanctificantis, ita quod ad illam habendum efficaciter ordinat suscipientem; et sic dicitur proprie Sacramentum, et ita venatur Magister definitionem Sacramenti. Si ergo prima ratio *determinetur* per has conditiones, tunc dicetur ratio Sacramenti; si autem in sua *generalitate intelligatur*, sic solum est *interpretatio nominis*, non *definitio convertibilis*[^6].

### Dub. II.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Sacramentum est invisibilis gratiae visibilis forma.* Videtur enim non bene definiri, quia corpus Christi verum est Sacramentum[^7], character est Sacramentum, et tamen neutrum est visibilis forma. *Si tu dicas*, quod *visibile* sumitur ad visibile *sensu exteriori*,

<!-- page 29 -->

secundum quod dicitur Genesis tertio[^8]: *Vidit igitur mulier*; et ad visibile quantum ad *sensum communem*, secundum quod dicitur Exodi vigesimo: *Cunctus populus videbat voces* etc.; et ad visibile *secundum intellectum*, secundum quod dicitur Genesis primo: *Vidit Deus cuncta quae fecerat, et erant valde bona*; obiicitur contra hoc, quod gratia secundum istum modum videndi[^9] non est invisibilis: ergo male dicitur invisibilis gratiae. Propter hoc quaeritur: quomodo accipitur ibi *visibilis*; et quare magis signi *visibilis* quam *audibilis*, cum vox sit signum magis expressivum? Et iterum, cum Sacramentum sit compositum, quaeritur, quomodo sit forma.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *visibile* accipitur ibi pro sensibili *sensu exteriori*, maxime quantum ad duos sensus *disciplinales*[^10]; unde *visibile* comprehendit *audibile*. Sed magis dicitur visibile quam audibile propter hoc, quod ratio *signandi*, ut infra[^11] videbitur, magis residet penes elementum; et hoc est perceptibile visu, et utrumque concurrit ad Sacramentum. — Quod obiicitur de charactere[^12], dicendum, quod non est Sacramentum, nisi quia annexum est sensibili sive visibili exteriori, et Magister definit principaliter quod est exterius. — Quod obiicitur de forma, dicendum, quod *forma* uno modo dicitur *perfectio rei*; alio modo *paradigma* sive *exemplar*[^13]; tertio modo dicitur forma quasi *foris manens*, id est aliqua similitudo exterius repraesentans; et sic accipitur in proposito.

### Dub. III.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Sacramentum eius rei similitudinem gerit, cuius est signum*. Videtur enim male dicere, quia Sacramentum est res corporalis, et gratia res spiritualis: ergo cum nulla sit convenientia in aliqua qualitate inter illa, non est similitudo[^14].

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *similitudo* duobus modis attenditur: uno modo secundum *unius rationis proprietatem* sive naturae participationem; alio modo secundum *unius formae comparationem*. Prima dicitur similitudo *proportionis*, secunda dicitur similitudo *proportionalitatis*. Sacramentum[^15] gerit similitudinem gratiae secundo modo, non primo, pro eo quod, sicut aqua abluit carnem, ita gratia mundat animam; et sicut oleum lenit exterius, ita gratia interius. Et haec similitudo est in quatuor terminis[^16].

### Dub. IV.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Cinis vitulae aspersus inquinatos sanctificat ad emundationem carnis, non animae.* Videtur enim falsum dicere, quia immunditia carnis non amovebatur, immo plus[^17] caro inficiebatur.

**Respondeo:** Immunditia carnis dicitur, non quia pure erat carnalis, sed quia erat *irregularitas* contracta ex solo actu carnali; sicut erat tangere mortuum, ex quo dicebatur *immundus*, non quia macula erat[^18] in anima vel in carne, sed quia ex hoc aliquam contraxerat irregularitatem, ut non esset idoneus ingredi in templum[^19].

### Dub. V.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Sed purificabatur secundum Legem die tertio et septimo*;

<!-- page 30 -->

unde hoc erat, quod sacerdos erat purificandus[^20] secundum Legem, cum idem non debeat esse causa purificationis et inquinationis?

**Respondeo:** Ratio huius erat *litteralis*, *allegorica* et *moralis*: *litteralis*, quia tangebat cinerem animalis mortui, et summus etiam sacerdos in peccato populi communicabat; ideo in poena[^21] contrahendi immunditiam communicabat. *Allegorica* ratio est, quia in hoc significabatur, quod immunditia Iudaeorum non erat totaliter auferenda usque ad finem, quando *reliquiae Israel salvae fient*, ad Romanos nono[^22]. *Moralis* vero ratio erat, quia illa immunditia significabat, quod homo debeat se reputare immundum; et sacerdos, qui pro aliis purificandis orat, debet se reputare immundum, quia aliena peccata debet reputare quasi sua; vel etiam debet credere, se in illo officio aliquid commisisse, iuxta illud Gregorii[^23]: «Bonarum mentium est ibi culpam agnoscere, ubi culpa non est».

### Dub. VI.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Mundabant etiam interdum a corporali lepra illa legalia*; quia, si hoc verum erat, tunc ergo illa erant maioris virtutis, quam sint ista.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *mundare* dicuntur, quia mundatum ostendebant. — Et *si tu quaeras*, respondeo: dicendum, quod magna; quia leprosi eiiciebantur extra castra et non poterant redire, nisi[^24] purificarentur et iudicio sacerdotis essent mundi. — Sed tunc *obiicies*: cum lepra sit morbus incurabilis, videtur, quod nullus mundaretur. Dici potest, quod aliqui *radicitus* habebant causam morbi, et isti nunquam mundabantur, nec secundum veritatem nec secundum iudicium sacerdotis. Aliqui vero habebant *signa*[^25], et isti mundari poterant, id est mundati ostendi, cum illa signa iam cessabant.

### Dub. VII.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Triplici ex causa instituta sunt Sacramenta.* Videtur enim male dicere: quia unius rei unicus est finis: ergo videtur, quod una sola sit causa principalis.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod una est causa principalis, scilicet animae ornatus sive reparatio; sed quia anima plures habet potentias, possunt fines plurificari penes illas, uno tamen principali fine existente. Est enim in nobis vis *regitiva*[^26], et quantum ad hanc est *eruditio*; est vis *irascibilis*, et quantum ad hanc est *humiliatio*; est *concupiscibilis*, et quantum ad hanc, ne fastidiat, est *exercitatio*. — Vel aliter: *eruditio* quantum ad *cognitivam*, *humiliatio* quantum ad *affectivam*, sed *exercitatio* quantum ad *operativam*[^27].

### Dub. VIII.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *dum homo rebus sensibilibus, quae natura infra ipsum sunt, subiicitur*. Videtur enim in hoc perverti ordo: quia anima est supra huiusmodi creaturas: ergo si eis se subiicit, pervertitur in hoc ordo naturalis.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod subiicere se his rebus potest homo quatuor considerationibus: aut propter *peccati meritum*, aut propter *Dei praeceptum*, aut quia sunt *gratiae signum*, aut quia in eis *statuit summum bonum* et finem ultimum. Primis tribus de causis est meritorium, et nulla inordinatio. Propter *peccati* enim *meritum* est aliquis infra illa, et ita eius subiectio respectu talium est ordinata. Item, qui propter *praeceptum* se subiicit ordinate facit, quia se subiicit magis praecepto quam rei sensibili[^28]. Et qui propter *significationem*, magis signato subiicit se quam signo. Sed in quarta consideratione ordo pervertitur, et est peccatum; et sic non spectat ad propositum.

### Dub. IX.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Homo, qui ante peccatum sine medio Deum videbat.* Videtur enim dicere falsum: quia qui videt Deum sine medio videt Deum per essentiam, videt etiam *facie ad faciem*[^29]; sed omnis talis est beatus: ergo etc.

<!-- page 31 -->

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *videre sine medio* est dupliciter: aut sine medio *adminiculante*, aut sine medio *obumbrante*. Primum medium est speculare, secundum aenigmaticum. Sine primo non erat homo[^30], sed erat sine secundo, in quod incidit per peccatum; et ideo, quia caecus erat, indiguit erudiri et manuduci; non sic in statu primo[^31].

## English

### Doubts on the text of the Master.

### Doubt I.

In this part there are doubts concerning the text, and first there is a doubt about the definition of a Sacrament which the Master sets down: *A Sacrament is the sign of a sacred thing.* For it seems to be ill assigned, because it seems to fit other things[^1] which are not Sacraments and yet are signs of a sacred thing, as is this name *God*. *If you say* that it is a sign of a sacred thing and as sacred; then this name *holy* and *sanctifying* is a Sacrament. *If you say* that this is understood of a visible sign; then therefore this account is not *convertible*[^2].

**I respond:** To this some say that "Sacrament" is taken in three ways, namely *commonly*, *properly*, and *most properly*; and accordingly the Master gives three definitions. And the first is of a Sacrament taken *most broadly*[^3]. — Yet it can be said, with Hugh, otherwise[^4], that this is not a *definition* but an *interpretation of the name*; and the Master does not intend there to set down a definition of a Sacrament, but an interpretation of the name, or a notification of the word. And from the first interpretation it is had that a Sacrament is a sign of a sacred thing; from the second, that it is a visible sign of a sacred and sanctifying thing; from the third, that it is a sign[^5] of a sacred and sanctifying thing, in such a way that it efficaciously orders the recipient to obtaining it; and thus it is called a Sacrament properly, and so the Master hunts down the definition of a Sacrament. If therefore the first account be *determined* by these conditions, then it will be called the account of a Sacrament; but if it be *understood in its generality*, then it is only an *interpretation of the name*, not a *convertible definition*[^6].

### Doubt II.

Likewise it is asked about this which he says: *A Sacrament is the visible form of invisible grace.* For it seems not to be well defined, since the true body of Christ is a Sacrament[^7], the character is a Sacrament, and yet neither is a visible form. *If you say* that *visible* is taken with respect to what is visible *by the exterior sense*,

according to what is said in Genesis chapter three[^8]: *The woman therefore saw*; and with respect to the visible as regards the *common sense*, according to what is said in Exodus chapter twenty: *And all the people saw the voices*, etc.; and with respect to the visible *according to the intellect*, according to what is said in Genesis chapter one: *God saw all the things that he had made, and they were very good*; one objects against this that grace, according to this mode of seeing[^9], is not invisible: therefore it is ill said to be of invisible grace. On account of this it is asked: in what sense is *visible* taken there; and why rather of a *visible* than of an *audible* sign, since the voice is a more expressive sign? And again, since a Sacrament is composite, it is asked how it is a form.

**I respond:** It must be said that *visible* is taken there for the sensible by the exterior sense, especially as regards the two *disciplinal* senses[^10]; whence the *visible* comprehends the *audible*. But it is rather called visible than audible on account of this, that the account of *signifying*, as will be seen below[^11], rather resides in the element; and this is perceptible by sight, and both concur in the Sacrament. — As to the objection about the character[^12], it must be said that it is not a Sacrament except because it is annexed to something sensible or visible exteriorly, and the Master defines principally what is exterior. — As to the objection about the form, it must be said that *form* in one way is called the *perfection of a thing*; in another way, a *paradigm* or *exemplar*[^13]; in a third way, form is called as it were *remaining outwardly*, that is, some likeness representing exteriorly; and so it is taken in the matter at hand.

### Doubt III.

Likewise it is asked about this which he says: *A Sacrament bears the likeness of that thing of which it is the sign.* For it seems to be ill said, since a Sacrament is a corporeal thing, and grace a spiritual thing: therefore, since there is no agreement in any quality between them, there is no likeness[^14].

**I respond:** It must be said that *likeness* is considered in two ways: in one way according to the *property of one nature* or the participation of a nature; in another way according to the *comparison of one form*. The first is called the likeness of *proportion*, the second is called the likeness of *proportionality*. A Sacrament[^15] bears the likeness of grace in the second way, not the first, for the reason that, just as water washes the flesh, so grace cleanses the soul; and just as oil soothes exteriorly, so grace [soothes] interiorly. And this likeness is in four terms[^16].

### Doubt IV.

Likewise it is asked about this which he says: *The ashes of the heifer, when sprinkled, sanctify the defiled unto the cleansing of the flesh, not of the soul.* For it seems to say something false, since the uncleanness of the flesh was not removed, but rather the flesh was the more[^17] infected.

**I respond:** It is called uncleanness of the flesh, not because it was purely carnal, but because it was an *irregularity* contracted from a carnal act alone; just as touching a dead body, from which one was called *unclean*, not because there was a stain[^18] in the soul or in the flesh, but because from this he had contracted some irregularity, so that he was not fit to enter into the temple[^19].

### Doubt V.

Likewise it is asked about this which he says: *But he was purified according to the Law on the third and the seventh day*;

whence was it that the priest had to be purified[^20] according to the Law, since the same thing ought not to be the cause both of purification and of defilement?

**I respond:** The reason for this was *literal*, *allegorical*, and *moral*: *literal*, because he touched the ash of a dead animal, and the high priest too shared in the sin of the people; therefore in the penalty[^21] of contracting it he shared in the uncleanness. The *allegorical* reason is that in this it was signified that the uncleanness of the Jews was not to be wholly taken away until the end, when *the remnant of Israel shall be saved*, to the Romans chapter nine[^22]. But the *moral* reason was that that uncleanness signified that a man ought to reckon himself unclean; and the priest, who prays for the purifying of others, ought to reckon himself unclean, since he ought to reckon the sins of others as if his own; or also he ought to believe that he has committed something in that office, according to that saying of Gregory[^23]: «It belongs to good minds to acknowledge fault there where there is no fault».

### Doubt VI.

Likewise it is asked about this which he says: *Those legal observances also sometimes cleansed from corporeal leprosy*; for, if this were true, then those things were of greater power than these are.

**I respond:** It must be said that they are called *to cleanse* because they showed cleansed. — And *if you ask* what utility there was, I respond: it must be said, a great one; because lepers were cast out beyond the camp and could not return unless[^24] they were purified and were clean by the judgment of the priest. — But then *you will object*: since leprosy is an incurable disease, it seems that no one would be cleansed. It can be said that some had the cause of the disease *radically*, and these were never cleansed, neither according to truth nor according to the judgment of the priest. But others had [only] *signs*[^25], and these could be cleansed, that is, shown to be cleansed, when those signs now ceased.

### Doubt VII.

Likewise it is asked about this which he says: *Sacraments were instituted from a threefold cause.* For it seems to be ill said: since of one thing there is one end: therefore it seems that there is only one principal cause.

**I respond:** It must be said that there is one principal cause, namely the adornment or repair of the soul; but because the soul has several powers, the ends can be multiplied with respect to them, while one principal end nevertheless exists. For there is in us the *governing* power[^26], and as regards this there is *instruction*; there is the *irascible* power, and as regards this there is *humiliation*; there is the *concupiscible*, and as regards this, lest it grow weary, there is *exercise*. — Or otherwise: *instruction* as regards the *cognitive* [power], *humiliation* as regards the *affective*, but *exercise* as regards the *operative*[^27].

### Doubt VIII.

Likewise it is asked about this which he says, that *while a man subjects himself to sensible things which are by nature beneath him*. For it seems that in this the order is perverted: since the soul is above creatures of this kind: therefore if it subjects itself to them, the natural order is perverted in this.

**I respond:** It must be said that a man can subject himself to these things under four considerations: either on account of the *merit of sin*, or on account of the *precept of God*, or because they are a *sign of grace*, or because in them he *establishes the highest good* and ultimate end. From the first three causes it is meritorious, and there is no disorder. For on account of the *merit of sin* one is beneath them, and so his subjection with respect to such things is ordered. Likewise, he who subjects himself on account of the *precept* acts in an ordered way, because he subjects himself rather to the precept than to the sensible thing[^28]. And he who [subjects himself] on account of the *signification* subjects himself rather to the thing signified than to the sign. But in the fourth consideration the order is perverted, and it is sin; and thus it does not pertain to the matter at hand.

### Doubt IX.

Likewise it is asked about this which he says: *Man, who before sin saw God without a medium.* For it seems to say something false: because he who sees God without a medium sees God through his essence, sees also *face to face*[^29]; but everyone such is blessed: therefore, etc.

**I respond:** It must be said that *to see without a medium* is twofold: either without a *supporting* medium, or without an *obscuring* medium. The first medium is the mirror, the second the riddle. Man was not without the first[^30], but he was without the second, into which he fell through sin; and therefore, because he was blind, he needed to be instructed and led by the hand; not so in the first state[^31].
