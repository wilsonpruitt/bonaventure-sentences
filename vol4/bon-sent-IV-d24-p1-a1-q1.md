### Articulus I. *De ordinis signaculo.*
### Quaestio I. *Utrum ordinandi debeant tonsurari sive coronari.*

> The numbered footnotes below correspond to markers in both the Latin body above and the English translation. Quaracchi restarts footnote numbering on each printed page; here notes 1–10 are the footer of p. 608 and notes 11–19 the footer of p. 609 (see Notes).

Quantum ad primum ostenditur, quod non debeant clerici *tonsurari*.

1. Ieremiae quadragesimo nono[^1] comminatur Dominus his: *Dispergam eos in omnem ventum, qui attonsi sunt in comam:* si ergo Dominus hoc detestatur, videtur etc.

2. Item, ad Galatas ultimo[^2]: *In Christo Iesu neque circumcisio aliquid valet neque praeputium, sed nova creatura:* ergo nihil valet in nova lege capillos tondere.

3. Item, caesaries a natura data est in decorem[^3]; sed omnia gratuita salvant ea quae sunt de decore naturae: ergo in nullo Sacramento videtur deponenda caesaries.

Item, videtur, quod non debeant *coronari*:

4. Primum, quia veritas debet respondere figurae; sed, ut Magister[^4] dicit, ista capillorum amotio in voto Nazaraeorum fuit praefigurata; sed illi non faciebant coronam, sed totaliter radebant, sicut dicitur Numerorum sexto: ergo etc.

5. Item, amotio capillorum significat amotionem superfluorum[^5]: si ergo non pars superfluitatis, sed tota abiicienda est; videtur, quod totum caput radendum sit.

6. Item, coronae signaculo consueverunt signari stulti[^6]; sed clericos non decet nisi signum sanctitatis et honestatis: ergo videtur, quod nullo modo debeant coronari.

Quaeritur igitur, quae est ratio, quare tonsurantur et coronantur *clerici*; et quae sit differentia inter coronam et tonsuram; *et rursus, quare conversi religiosi* tonsuram recipiant, sed non coronam. — Quaeritur etiam, quare in huius Sacramenti susceptione potius consignantur fideles quam in susceptione alterius Sacramenti?

### Conclusio.
> **Conclusio.** Clericos tonsurari cum corona conveniens est ad distinctionem et eruditionem.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod, sicut infra[^7] melius videbitur, in Sacramento ordinis datur potestas ad illud nobile officium exsequendum, et haec non omnibus datur nec omnibus dari debet; ideo oportet, personas hoc officium suscipientes primo consignari, et hoc signaculo, quod ostendat *distinctionem*, et quod etiam sit in *eruditionem*. Nullum autem ita conveniens est, ut tonsura cum corona: primo ratione *situs*, quia in superexcellenti parte debet hoc signaculum poni, ut significetur officium nobilitatis, ad quod[^8] parati sunt. Secundo vero, quia tonsura inferior cum rasura superiori circularem figuram faciunt in capillatura; et illa appellari potest corona, quae significat, eos parari ad regale sacerdotium, secundum illud primae Petri secundo[^9]: *Vos estis genus electum, regale sacerdotium;* ideo signaculum regale rectissime eis competit ad *distinguendum*.

Competit nihilominus ad *erudiendum*; et hoc, quia in talis signaculi collatione est superfluitatis *amotio*, est summitatis capitis *denudatio*, est figurae orbicularis *impressio*. — In primo instruuntur clerici, quid sit *vitandum*, quoniam superfluitas terreneitatis, secundum illud Apostoli primae ad Timotheum ultimo[^10]: *Habentes alimenta, et quibus tegamur, his contenti simus.* Unde et in hoc significatur, quod debent esse *succincti lumbos mentis*. — In secundo instruuntur quantum ad id quod est *desiderandum* et quod est tenendum, et hoc est bonum supernum, ut, secundum illud Apostoli

<!-- page 609 -->
ad Philippenses tertio[^11], *nostra conversatio sit in caelis. Mente* enim debemus *excedere Deo* et *sobrii* esse proximo. — In tertio instruuntur, qualiter sit *perveniendum*. Figura enim orbicularis est *simplicissima, capacissima* et *pulcherrima*. In quo significatur, quod *simplices* debent esse in mente, Sapientiae primo[^12]: *In simplicitate cordis quaerite illum* etc.; *dilatati* in affectione, secundae ad Corinthios sexto: *Os nostrum patet ad vos, o Corinthii, cor nostrum dilatatum est;* et *pulcri* in conversatione, quia *confessio et pulcritudo in conspectu eius,* et hoc est per munditiam, secundum illud Psalmi: *Ambulans in via immaculata, hic mihi ministrabat.*

Et sic patet, quare sic debeant consignari per *tonsuram* et *coronam*. Nam *tonsura* dicitur amotio capillorum a parte inferiori; sed *corona* amotio capillorum orbicularis a parte superiori; vel posset dici etiam ipse *circulus*, et tunc corona utrumque comprehenderet[^13].

1. Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur, quod Dominus minabatur his qui huiusmodi tonsuras observabant; verum est, his qui faciebant ad cultum daemonum[^14], sed non his qui faciunt ad cultum divinum.

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod in Christo Iesu non valet circumcisio etc.; intelligendum est, quod per se non valet ad *iustificationem* capillorum amotio, verumtamen ad *eruditionem* valet[^15], valet etiam ad *distinctionem*.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod caesaries decorat naturam; dicendum, quod decor naturae duplex est: quidam *spiritualis* et quidam *corporalis*. *Spiritualis* decor per gratiam non aufertur, sed augetur; sed corporalis vanus est, secundum illud Proverbiorum ultimo[^16]: *Vana est pulcritudo;* et iste non est servandus, immo contemnendus ab his qui interiorem volunt servare, secundum illud Apostoli, secundae ad Corinthios quarto: *Etsi is qui foris est homo noster, corrumpatur* etc., et licet contemptibilis fiat extra, tamen decorus et honorabilis fit intra. Et in hoc significatur, quod huiusmodi cultura exterior est a spiritualibus fugienda.

4. 5. Ad illud quod obiicitur de Nazaraeis, quod totaliter radebantur etc.; dicendum, quod non oportet, veritatem umbrae per omnia assimilari, quoniam diversa diversis temporibus conveniunt. Illi enim primo servabant capillaturam et post radebant; in quo significabatur velamen legis Moysaicae et revelatio evangelicae; sed hic partim raditur, partim reservatur, ut signaculum regale maneat. Plus enim fit corona, ut sit signaculum distinctivum quam figurativum; e contrario erat in voto Nazaraeorum. — Et per hoc patet sequens. Nam sufficienter abdicatio terrenorum figuratur in tonsura — et ideo conversi laici tonsurantur, qui mundum reliquerunt[^17] — et apertio mentis significatur per summitatis rasuram; ideo non oportet, totam amoveri capillaturam. Praeterea, in ipsa significatione honestas debet attendi; et ideo non debent omnino capilli amoveri, pro eo quod indecens videretur habitus et forma clericalis.

6. Ad illud quod obiicitur de stultis, dicendum, quod non solum stulti coronantur, sed etiam reges; et ideo non est principaliter stultitiae signum; verumtamen et hoc non vacat a mysterio. Qui enim volunt vere Deo servire, oportet, quod stulti fiant huic mundo: quoniam enim in sapientia non cognovit mundus per sapientiam Deum, placuit Deo per stultitiam praedicationis salvos facere credentes[^18]. Unde primae ad Corinthios quarto: *Nos stulti propter Christum* etc.

Alia duo, quae quaeruntur, ex praedictis manifesta sunt, scilicet de tonsura conversorum et de coronatione clericorum: illi enim tondentur, quia temporalibus renuntiant; hi consignantur, quia ad spirituale[^19] et nobile officium assumuntur.

## English

### Article I. *On the seal of order.*
### Question I. *Whether those to be ordained ought to be tonsured or crowned.*

As to the first, it is shown that clerics ought not to be *tonsured*.

1. In Jeremiah the forty-ninth[^1] the Lord threatens these: *I will scatter them into every wind, who are shorn in [their] hair:* if therefore the Lord detests this, it seems etc.

2. Likewise, to the Galatians the last[^2]: *In Christ Jesus neither circumcision avails anything nor uncircumcision, but a new creature:* therefore in the new law it avails nothing to shear the hair.

3. Likewise, the hair is given by nature for adornment[^3]; but all things of grace preserve those things which pertain to the adornment of nature: therefore in no Sacrament does it seem that the hair should be laid aside.

Likewise, it seems that they ought not to be *crowned*:

4. First, because the truth ought to correspond to the figure; but, as the Master[^4] says, this removal of the hair was prefigured in the vow of the Nazarites; but they did not make a crown, but shaved wholly, as is said in Numbers the sixth: therefore etc.

5. Likewise, the removal of the hair signifies the removal of superfluous things[^5]: if therefore not [only] the part of superfluity, but the whole is to be cast off; it seems that the whole head ought to be shaved.

6. Likewise, fools are wont to be marked with the seal of the crown[^6]; but nothing befits clerics except a sign of holiness and honorableness: therefore it seems that they ought in no way to be crowned.

It is asked, therefore, what is the reason why *clerics* are tonsured and crowned; and what is the difference between the crown and the tonsure; *and again, why converted religious* receive the tonsure but not the crown. — It is also asked why in the reception of this Sacrament the faithful are marked rather than in the reception of another Sacrament?

### Conclusion.
> **Conclusion.** That clerics be tonsured with a crown is fitting for distinction and instruction.

**I respond:** It must be said that, as will be seen better below[^7], in the Sacrament of order there is given a power for carrying out that noble office, and this is not given to all nor ought to be given to all; therefore it behooves that the persons receiving this office first be marked, and [marked] with this seal, which would show *distinction*, and which also would be for *instruction*. Now nothing is so fitting as the tonsure with the crown: first, by reason of *situation*, because in the most excellent part ought this seal to be placed, so that the office of nobility may be signified, for which[^8] they are prepared. But secondly, because the lower tonsure with the upper shaving make a circular figure in the head of hair; and it can be called a crown, which signifies that they are prepared for the royal priesthood, according to that [word] of the first [epistle] of Peter, the second [chapter][^9]: *You are a chosen race, a royal priesthood;* therefore the royal seal most rightly befits them for *distinguishing*.

It befits [them] nonetheless for *instructing*; and this, because in the conferral of such a seal there is the *removal* of superfluity, there is the *denuding* of the top of the head, there is the *impression* of a circular figure. — In the first, clerics are instructed what is to be *avoided*, namely the superfluity of earthliness, according to that [word] of the Apostle, first to Timothy the last[^10]: *Having food, and wherewith to be covered, with these let us be content.* Whence also in this it is signified that they ought to be *girded about the loins of the mind*. — In the second they are instructed as to that which is to be *desired* and which is to be held, and this is the supernal good, so that, according to that [word] of the Apostle

<!-- page 609 -->
to the Philippians the third[^11], *our conversation may be in heaven. In mind* indeed we ought to *go beyond unto God* and to be *sober* toward the neighbor. — In the third they are instructed how it is to be *attained*. For the circular figure is the *simplest, most capacious,* and *most beautiful*. In which is signified that they ought to be *simple* in mind, Wisdom the first[^12]: *In simplicity of heart seek him* etc.; *enlarged* in affection, second to the Corinthians the sixth: *Our mouth is open to you, O Corinthians, our heart is enlarged;* and *beautiful* in conversation, because *confession and beauty are in his sight,* and this is through cleanness, according to that [word] of the Psalm: *He that walked in the immaculate way, he ministered to me.*

And thus it is clear why they ought to be marked in this way by the *tonsure* and the *crown*. For *tonsure* is called the removal of hair from the lower part; but *crown* the circular removal of hair from the upper part; or it could also be called the *circle* itself, and then the crown would comprehend both[^13].

1. To that, therefore, which is objected, that the Lord threatened those who observed tonsures of this kind; it is true of those who did [it] for the worship of demons[^14], but not of those who do [it] for the divine worship.

2. To that which is objected, that in Christ Jesus circumcision does not avail etc.; it must be understood that of itself the removal of hair does not avail for *justification*, nevertheless it avails for *instruction*[^15], it avails also for *distinction*.

3. To that which is objected, that the hair adorns nature; it must be said that the adornment of nature is twofold: one *spiritual* and one *corporal*. The *spiritual* adornment is not taken away by grace, but increased; but the corporal is vain, according to that [word] of Proverbs the last[^16]: *Beauty is vain;* and this is not to be preserved, rather to be despised by those who wish to preserve the interior [adornment], according to that [word] of the Apostle, second to the Corinthians the fourth: *Though our outward man be corrupted* etc., and although he becomes contemptible without, yet he becomes comely and honorable within. And in this is signified that such exterior cultivation is to be fled by spiritual [persons].

4. 5. To that which is objected concerning the Nazarites, that they were wholly shaved etc.; it must be said that it is not necessary that the truth be likened to the shadow in all things, since diverse things befit diverse times. For they first kept the head of hair and afterward shaved; in which was signified the veil of the Mosaic law and the revelation of the evangelical [law]; but here it is partly shaved, partly reserved, so that the royal seal may remain. For the crown is made more that it may be a distinctive seal rather than a figurative one; on the contrary [it was] in the vow of the Nazarites. — And by this the following is clear. For the abdication of earthly things is sufficiently figured in the tonsure — and therefore converted laymen are tonsured, who have left the world[^17] — and the opening of the mind is signified by the shaving of the top; therefore it is not necessary that the whole head of hair be removed. Moreover, in the signification itself honorableness ought to be attended to; and therefore the hairs ought not to be wholly removed, for the reason that the habit and form of clerics would seem unbecoming.

6. To that which is objected concerning fools, it must be said that not only fools are crowned, but also kings; and therefore it is not principally a sign of folly; nevertheless this too is not devoid of mystery. For those who wish truly to serve God, it behooves that they become fools to this world: for since in wisdom the world by wisdom did not know God, it pleased God by the folly of preaching to save those who believe[^18]. Whence first to the Corinthians the fourth: *We [are] fools for Christ's sake* etc.

The other two [things] which are asked are manifest from the aforesaid, namely concerning the tonsure of the converted and the coronation of clerics: for the former are tonsured, because they renounce temporal things; the latter are marked, because they are assumed to a spiritual[^19] and noble office.

### Scholion

**1.** According to the force of the name, order is "a disposition apportioning to each of things equal and unequal their fitting places" (Augustine, XIX *On the City of God*, c. 13), or in other words, "a disposition of higher and lower things, which are so fitted to one another that the one is referred to the other. Since therefore in this ministry there are many *grades* and various *functions*, but all are distributed and arranged by a certain plan, rightly and fittingly does the name of *order* seem imposed on it" (Roman Catechism, on the Sacrament of order, n. 9). It is also to be noted that *order* is often taken for *ordination*, which properly is a *transient cause*, but the former [is] a *permanent effect*; the latter *action and Sacrament*, the former *status* and power, not Sacrament (cf. below, a. 2, q. 2). — To the four questions of this article the other ancients respond in the same sense, but more briefly.

**I.** In the 1st question the Holy Doctor, according to his custom, sagaciously and copiously explains the mystical senses of these external rites.

On the 1st and 2nd question: Scotus, in each Writing here q. single at the end. — St. Thomas, here q. 3, a. 1, subquestion 1, 2. — B. Albert, here a. 13, 14. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 1, a. 1, 2. — Richard of Middleton, here a. 1, q. 1, 2. — Durandus, here q. 4. — Dionysius the Carthusian, on this and the following q. here q. 2.

**III.** On the 3rd question: St. Thomas, loc. cit. subquestion 3. — B. Albert, a. 15. — Peter of Tarentaise, on this and the following q. loc. cit. a. 3, subquestion 1, 2. — Richard of Middleton, loc. cit. q. 3.

The fourth question the others, except Peter of Tarentaise, in the cited places only touch upon in the solutions of the objections; others omit it entirely.
