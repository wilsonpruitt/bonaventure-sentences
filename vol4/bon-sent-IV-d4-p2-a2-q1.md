## Latin

### Articulus II. *De sanctificatione parvulorum in baptismo.*

Consequenter quaeritur de sanctificatione collata parvulis per Sacramentum baptismi. Et circa hoc tria principaliter quaeruntur.

Primo quaeritur, utrum parvuli in baptismo recipiant sanctificationem.

Secundo, utrum recipiant plene.

Tertio, utrum recipiant aequaliter.

<!-- page 112 -->

### Quaestio I. *Utrum parvuli in baptismo recipiant sanctificationem.*

Circa primum sic proceditur. Quod parvulus non possit iustificari, videtur:

1. Quoniam Bernardus, de Libero Arbitrio[^1], dicit, quod «duo sunt ad salutem necessaria, scilicet dans et liberum arbitrium», et subiungit, quod «tam non potest esse salus nostra sine voluntate consentientis, quam non potest esse sine gratia dantis»; sed in parvulo non est usus liberi arbitrii: ergo non potest esse in eo salus.

2. Item, Augustinus[^2]: «Qui creavit te sine te, non iustificat te sine te», hoc est sine actu tuae voluntatis; sed parvulus hunc non habet: ergo non potest iustificari.

3. Item, voluntas parvuli non mutatur, ergo semper se habet uniformiter respectu gratiae; sed Deus semper paratus est dare: ergo quod modo dat, modo non, hoc est propter mutationem in voluntate suscipientis: ergo si in parvulo non mutatur voluntas, non recipit rem. Si dicas[^3], quod parvulus disponitur non actu suo, sed fide offerentium; esto quod illi essent infideles, tunc ergo parvulus damnaretur.

4. Item, in Sacramento significatio antecedit sanctificationem: ergo non existente significatione, non est sanctificatio; sed parvulo Sacramentum non significat, quia nihil facit in intellectum eius venire[^4]: ergo parvulum non sanctificat.

**Sed contra:**

1. Magis consonat rationi et pietati, quod conditio parvulorum possit ab aliquo iuvari quam laedi; sed parvuli sine suo consensu possunt trahere et trahunt[^5] culpam: ergo multo magis gratiam.

2. Item, ad Romanos quinto[^6]: *Per unum hominem peccatum intravit in mundum, et per unum vita*; sed non est minus potens Christus quam Adam, et gratia se magis diffundit quam culpa: ergo si Adam potuit ipsos vitiare sine consensu, multo magis salvare eos potuit Sacramentum Christi.

3. Item, omnis suscipiens Sacramentum suscipit rem, nisi habeat obstaculum; sed parvulus suscipit Sacramentum et non habet obstaculum, quia voluntas non repugnat, nec culpa potest resistere[^7]: ergo etc.

### Conclusio.

> Parvuli in baptismo recipiunt rem Sacramenti, qua fit remissio culpae originalis.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod parvuli recipiunt rem Sacramenti, quae est remissio culpae originalis; et hoc merito passionis Christi et alienae fidei et virtute Sacramenti. — Et ratio huius est tum ratione divinae *misericordiae*, tum ratione suae *impotentiae*, tum ratione talis *culpae*. Quia enim per actum alterius fuit culpa contracta, et ipsi non poterant se adiuvare; misericors Deus congruum reputavit, eis per alium subveniri. — Quia enim pro illa culpa nullus satisfacere poterat purus homo, ideo subvenit Deus toti humano generi per passionem Christi. — Et rursus, quia passio effectum non habet nisi in his qui volunt fieri membra Christi et consentiunt; et hoc non poterat parvulus velle: ideo subvenit ei voluntas et fides aliena. — Sed quia fides subvenit profitendo, et hoc est in Sacramento, ubi est professio mortis Christi in signo[^8]; ideo subvenit per Sacramentum. — Tria igitur sunt disponentia parvulum ad salutem: et primum est passio Christi, secundum fides aliena, tertium vero et immediatum est virtus Sacramenti, quae characterem imprimit, qui ad gratiam disponit[^9]. — Et per hoc patent obiecta.

**Ad 1. 2. 3.** Nam primum intelligitur de iustificatione a culpa *acta*, non *contracta*[^10]; secundum
<!-- page 113 -->
dicitur: *Per similitudinem in minori*, id est per simile et minus, probare intendit. Sicut enim per Adam, purum hominem, peccatum est et mors, ita per Christum, hominem et Deum, potest esse iustitia et vita etc. — Tertium similiter patet, quia parvulus non disponitur secundum actum suum, sed alienum, quia divina misericordia alienam voluntatem in propriam reputat[^Adtert].

**4.** Ad quartum dicendum, quod Sacramentum, quantum est de se, semper significat et sanctificat; sed tamen in parvulo non habet effectum significandi, sed sanctificandi, quia non est capax doctrinae, est tamen capax gratiae — sicut et culpae — et hoc est ex largitate divinae misericordiae; quamvis enim non significet parvulo, tamen aliis significat gratiam, quam tribuit parvulo.

### Scholion
**I.** *Error Anabaptistarum*, contra quem militat I. quaestio, perpetua Ecclesiae traditione et Concilii Trid. (Sess. VII. can. 13. de Bapt.) decreto reprobatus est. Sicut S. Bonav. insinuat, consentaneum est, ut qui aliena culpa cecidit etiam resurgat aliena fide, quam Ecclesia, pia mater, accommodat.

De hac I. quaestione: Alex. Hal., S. p. IV. q. 8. m. 7. a. 1. § 1. — Scot., in utroque Scripto hic q. 1. — S. Thom., hic q. 3. a. 1; S. III. q. 68. a. 9. — B. Albert., hic a. 1. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 1. a. 1. quaestiunc. 1. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 1. q. 1. — Durand., hic q. 3. — Dionys. Carth., hic q. 3.

**II.** Ante Concilium Viennense nonnulli docuerunt, parvulis per baptismum remitti quidem peccatum, sed non infundi nec gratiam nec virtutes; quae opinio quoad gratiam manifeste falsa est, cum sine gratia et inhaerente iustitia (non sola imputatione iustitiae Christi) nulla possit esse iustificatio, ut declaratum est a Concil. Trid. (Sess. VI. c. 3. 4. 7. et can. 9. seqq.); ubi simul docetur, iustificationem non esse solum peccatorum remissionem, sed et «sanctificationem et renovationem interioris hominis per voluntariam susceptionem gratiae et donorum» (c. 7.). Quod autem haec renovatio et inhaerens iustitia fiat aliqua forma *permanente*, intrinsecus a Deo animae communicata per modum habitus, post Concil. Trid. tanquam omnino certum communissime tenetur. Controvertitur autem, utrum sit stricte de fide, renovationem illam fieri per qualitatem permanentem et creatam per modum habitus et non per motum actualem, quod respectu caritatis Magister docere videtur (cfr. I. Sent. d. 17. p. I. q. I.). — Quoad infusionem *virtutum* in baptismo parvulorum maior fuit antiquitus dubitatio; de quo videsis scholion ad III. Sent. d. 33. q. 5.

De hac 2. quaestione Alex. Hal., loc. cit. m. 8. a. 3. § 1. — Scot., in utroque Scripto hic q. 2. — S. Thom., hic q. 2. a. 2. — S. loc. cit. q. 69. a. 6. — B. Albert., hic a. 13. — Petr. a Tar., loc. cit. quaestionem tangit. — Dionys. Carth., de hac et seq. q. hic q. 4.

**III.** Quoad 3. quaestionem cfr. supra p. II. a. 1. q. 3, ubi agitur de aequalitate effectus in baptismis adultorum, et notati sunt auctores ibi etiam hanc quaestionem tangentes. — Quoad parvulos S. Thom. docet, semper aequalem eis conferri effectum vi baptismi. Tamen restrictio, quam facit S. Bonav. et cum ipso etiam Petr. a Tar. per verba *quantum est de lege communi*, non videtur esse aliena a mente S. Thomae, cum hic distinguat duplicem effectum baptismi: alterum per se, alterum per accidens, scilicet a divina virtute miraculose collatum. Similiter Scot. (hic q. 7. n. 4. 5.) possibile esse dicit, etiam in parvulis inaequalem interdum fieri effectum, tum quia Deus speciali modo applicet virtutem passionis Christi, tum ob meritum de congruo ex parte offerentium. De facto autem nihil determinat; unde, teste Estio (hic § 9.), non meretur vituperationem Soti aliorumque.

Specialiter de parvulis quaestionem solvunt S. Thom., loc. cit. quaestiunc. 3, et Petr. a Tar., hic q. 2. a. 3. quaestiunc. 2.

## English

### Article II. *On the sanctification of infants in baptism.*

Consequently inquiry is made concerning the sanctification conferred on infants through the Sacrament of baptism. And concerning this, three things are principally asked.

First it is asked, whether infants in baptism receive sanctification.

Second, whether they receive it fully.

Third, whether they receive it equally.

### Question I. *Whether infants in baptism receive sanctification.*

Concerning the first one proceeds thus. That an infant cannot be justified, it seems:

1. Because Bernard, *On Free Choice*[^1], says that "two things are necessary for salvation, namely the one who gives and free choice," and he adds that "our salvation can no more be without the will of the one consenting than it can be without the grace of the one giving"; but in an infant there is no use of free choice: therefore there cannot be salvation in him.

2. Likewise, Augustine[^2]: "He who created you without you does not justify you without you," that is, without the act of your will; but the infant does not have this: therefore he cannot be justified.

3. Likewise, the will of the infant is not changed, therefore it always stands uniformly with respect to grace; but God is always ready to give: therefore that He gives now, and now does not, this is on account of a change in the will of the recipient: therefore if in the infant the will is not changed, he does not receive the reality. If you say[^3] that the infant is disposed not by his own act, but by the faith of those offering him; granted that they were unbelievers, then therefore the infant would be damned.

4. Likewise, in a Sacrament signification precedes sanctification: therefore, signification not existing, there is no sanctification; but the Sacrament does not signify to the infant, because it brings nothing to enter into his understanding[^4]: therefore it does not sanctify the infant.

**On the contrary:**

1. It is more consonant with reason and piety that the condition of infants can be helped by someone rather than harmed; but infants without their own consent can contract, and do contract[^5], guilt: therefore much more grace.

2. Likewise, to the Romans, fifth chapter[^6]: *Through one man sin entered into the world, and through one, life*; but Christ is not less powerful than Adam, and grace is diffused more widely than guilt: therefore if Adam could corrupt them without consent, much more could the Sacrament of Christ save them.

3. Likewise, everyone receiving a Sacrament receives the reality, unless he have an obstacle; but the infant receives the Sacrament and has no obstacle, because the will does not resist, nor can guilt resist[^7]: therefore etc.

### Conclusion.

> Infants in baptism receive the reality of the Sacrament, by which the remission of original guilt is effected.

**I respond:** It must be said that infants receive the reality of the Sacrament, which is the remission of original guilt; and this by the merit of the passion of Christ and by the faith of another and by the power of the Sacrament. — And the reason for this is partly by reason of the divine *mercy*, partly by reason of their *impotence*, partly by reason of such *guilt*. For because the guilt was contracted through the act of another, and they were not able to help themselves; the merciful God deemed it fitting that they be aided through another. — For because no mere man could make satisfaction for that guilt, therefore God aided the whole human race through the passion of Christ. — And again, because the passion has its effect only in those who will to become members of Christ and consent; and this the infant could not will: therefore the will and faith of another came to his aid. — But because faith aids by professing, and this is in the Sacrament, where there is a profession of the death of Christ in a sign[^8]; therefore He aids through the Sacrament. — There are therefore three things disposing the infant to salvation: and the first is the passion of Christ, the second the faith of another, but the third and immediate is the power of the Sacrament, which imprints the character that disposes to grace[^9]. — And through this the objections are plain.

**To 1, 2, 3.** For the first is understood of justification from guilt *committed*, not *contracted*[^10]; the second
is said: *By a likeness in the lesser*, that is, by what is similar and less, it intends to prove. For just as through Adam, a mere man, came sin and death, so through Christ, man and God, there can be justice and life, etc. — The third likewise is plain, because the infant is disposed not according to his own act, but another's, since the divine mercy reckons the will of another as his own[^Adtert].

**4.** To the fourth it must be said that the Sacrament, as far as it is of itself, always signifies and sanctifies; but nevertheless in the infant it does not have the effect of signifying, but of sanctifying, because he is not capable of teaching, yet is capable of grace — as also of guilt — and this is from the largesse of the divine mercy; for although it does not signify to the infant, nevertheless to others it signifies the grace which it bestows on the infant.

### Scholion
**I.** The *error of the Anabaptists*, against which the first question militates, has been reprobated by the perpetual tradition of the Church and by the decree of the Council of Trent (Sess. VII. can. 13. on Baptism). As St. Bonaventure intimates, it is fitting that he who fell by another's guilt should also rise again by another's faith, which the Church, a devoted mother, supplies.

On this first question: Alexander of Hales, S. p. IV. q. 8. m. 7. a. 1. § 1. — Scotus, in both Writings here q. 1. — St. Thomas, here q. 3. a. 1; S. III. q. 68. a. 9. — Blessed Albert, here a. 1. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 1. a. 1. quaestiunc. 1. — Richard of Mediavilla, here a. 1. q. 1. — Durandus, here q. 3. — Dionysius the Carthusian, here q. 3.

**II.** Before the Council of Vienne some taught that to infants sin was indeed remitted through baptism, but that neither grace nor virtues were infused; which opinion as regards grace is manifestly false, since without grace and inherent justice (not by the mere imputation of the justice of Christ) there can be no justification, as was declared by the Council of Trent (Sess. VI. c. 3. 4. 7. and can. 9. ff.); where at the same time it is taught that justification is not only the remission of sins, but also "the sanctification and renewal of the interior man through the voluntary reception of grace and gifts" (c. 7.). But that this renewal and inherent justice comes about by some *permanent* form, communicated intrinsically by God to the soul by way of a habit, is most commonly held after the Council of Trent as altogether certain. It is, however, controverted whether it is strictly of faith that that renewal comes about through a permanent and created quality by way of a habit and not through an actual motion, which with respect to charity the Master seems to teach (cf. I. Sent. d. 17. p. I. q. I.). — As regards the infusion of *virtues* in the baptism of infants there was anciently greater doubt; on which you may see the scholion at III. Sent. d. 33. q. 5.

On this second question: Alexander of Hales, loc. cit. m. 8. a. 3. § 1. — Scotus, in both Writings here q. 2. — St. Thomas, here q. 2. a. 2. — S. loc. cit. q. 69. a. 6. — Blessed Albert, here a. 13. — Peter of Tarentaise, loc. cit. touches the question. — Dionysius the Carthusian, on this and the following question here q. 4.

**III.** As regards the third question, cf. above, Pars II, a. 1. q. 3, where there is treated the equality of effect in the baptisms of adults, and the authors there also touching this question have been noted. — As regards infants, St. Thomas teaches that an equal effect is always conferred on them by the power of baptism. Yet the restriction which St. Bonaventure makes, and with him also Peter of Tarentaise, by the words *as far as it is of the common law*, does not seem alien to the mind of St. Thomas, since here he distinguishes a twofold effect of baptism: one per se, the other per accidens, namely conferred miraculously by the divine power. Similarly Scotus (here q. 7. n. 4. 5.) says it is possible that even in infants an unequal effect should sometimes come about, both because God applies the power of the passion of Christ in a special way, and on account of the merit of congruity on the part of those offering. But in fact he determines nothing; whence, according to Estius (here § 9.), he does not deserve the censure of Soto and others.

Specially concerning infants the question is resolved by St. Thomas, loc. cit. quaestiunc. 3, and Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 2. a. 3. quaestiunc. 2.
