# IV Sent., d. 6, a. 1, q. 4

## *Per quid character imprimatur*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 142 -->

### Quaestio IV. *Per quid character imprimatur.*

Quarto quaeritur, per quid character imprimatur, et quaeritur, utrum imprimatur per *virtutes*, sicut per Sacramenta. Et quod sic, videtur:

1. Quia character est signum conformativum sive configurativum; sed per virtutes configuratur anima Deo[^1]: ergo per virtutes imprimitur character.

2. Item quaero, utrum imprimatur per *Sacramenta veteris Testamenti*. — Et videtur, quod sic: quia character est signum distinctivum, distinguens populum a non-populo; sed Dominus tempore Legis habuit populum, qui debuit distingui, sicut et modo: ergo si distinguebantur per Sacramenta[^2], patet, quod in illis imprimebatur signaculum distinctivum; hoc autem est character: ergo etc.

3. Item quaero, utrum per *omnia Sacramenta novi Testamenti*. — Et videtur, quod sic: quia quod potest in maius potest in minus[^3]; sed omnia dant gratiam, quae maior est quam character: ergo etc.

4. Item, character disponit ad gratiam[^4]: ergo cum omnia Sacramenta dent gratiam et disponant ad illam, omnia dant characterem.

**Contra:** 1. Character est signum indelebile[^5] et solum est impressivum characteris, quod non iteratur circa subiectum; sed tantum tria sunt Sacramenta non iterabilia, ut baptismus, confirmatio et ordo: ergo tantum illa tria imprimunt characterem.

Quaeritur ergo ulterius gratia huius — cum omnis passio[^6], quae inest aliquibus, insit per aliquam naturam communem — quae sit illa *natura communis* reperta in his tribus, et non in aliis. Et constat, quod non est *materia*, quia non est communis; nec *forma*, quia nec illa est communis. — Et *si dicas*, quod conveniunt in *invocatione Trinitatis*, et quia est unus *efficiens*[^7]: similiter et in aliis est invocatio Trinitatis et efficiens, quia ille est unus in omnibus Sacramentis. Necesse igitur est, quod illa natura sumatur ex parte *finis*. Sed hoc non videtur, quia in fine *communi* videntur omnia convenire; in finibus autem *proximis* omnia differunt. — Et *si dicas*, quod hoc est, quia ista tria Sacramenta sunt respicientia statum fidei determinatum: ergo cum fides sit eadem secundum diversos status, videtur, quod in omnibus istis Sacramentis unus imprimatur character. Sed constat, quod multi sunt characteres, alioquin non posset quis ordinari, si non esset confirmatus.

<!-- page 143 -->

Quaeritur ergo, quae sit *ratio multitudinis*; et videtur, quod *superfluant*, cum sufficienter per unum possint distingui. Quod autem sint *multi*; videtur, quia character interior similitudinem habet cum signo exteriori; sed signa sunt dissimilia et diversa: ergo et characteres.

### Conclusio.

> *Character imprimitur non in virtutibus, sed in Sacramentis novae legis, ast tantum in tribus.*

**Respondeo:** Ad praedictorum intelligentiam est notandum, quod character est *signum interius distinctivum*. Quia ergo signare non convenit ei nisi ratione signi visibilis; ideo solum in Sacramentis, non in virtutibus, imprimitur. Quia vero interius spiritualiter distinguens; ideo in Sacramentis, quae sunt non carnalis populi, sed spiritualis: ideo imprimitur in Sacramentis novae legis, non veteris. Quia distinctivum non unius, sed multorum; ideo unitivum multorum in uno statu fidei: ideo in his Sacramentis solum imprimitur, quae respiciunt statum fidei determinatum. — Tres autem sunt status fidei distinctos: scilicet *genitae*, *roboratae* et iam *multiplicatae*; et secundum hoc attenditur triplex distinctio in populo, scilicet *bonorum* a malis, *fortium* ab infirmis, *clericorum* a laicis. — Et haec triplex distinctio praecessit in figura in populo Israel: prima, cum omnes sunt distincti ab Aegyptiis ad veniendum in terram promissionis; secunda, cum fortes ab infirmis ad pugnandum in bello; tertia, cum levitae ab alio populo ad ministrandum in templo[^8]. — Similiter triplex fuit distinctio in domo David: prima, domus eius ab infidelibus; secunda, quia erant ibi specialiter *pugnatores*; tertia, quia levitae et sacerdotes et scribae ab aliis etiam distincti erant, et etiam *duces* et *principes* habebant[^9].

Quia ergo tria tantum sunt Sacramenta, quae respiciunt *statum fidei determinatum*: baptismus, primum quia ibi fides gignitur, et homo ab Aegyptiis separatur; confirmatio, secundum, quia ibi fides roboratur, et ut pugil inungitur homo ad proeliandum et ab infirmis discernitur; et ordo, tertium, quia ibi virtus multimoda datur, et homo ut sanctus ad ministerium templi a laicis separatur: ideo ista tria tantum imprimunt characterem.

Natura ergo communis, reperta in his et non in aliis, est, quia sunt signa visibilia interius operantia, respicientia statum fidei determinatum. Et quia distinctio *populi* a non-populo praecedit alias distinctiones; ideo baptismalis character praecedit alios de necessitate. Sed distinctio *fortium* ab infirmis praecedit distinctionem aliam solum de congruo, non de necessitate. — Ex hoc igitur patet *ordo* characterum; patet et *numerus*, quia tres sunt secundum tres status et tres distinctiones et tria Sacramenta; patet etiam *ratio* imprimendi. Praesupposita namque identitate ex parte materialis elementi, ratione cuius competit significatio, sumitur ratio principalis ex parte finis. Quia tamen finis movet *efficientem*, et efficiens secundum exigentiam finis introducit *formam* in materiam[^10]: concurrit triplex ratio: et prima est status fidei determinatio ex parte finis; ex parte efficientis institutio, qui ad talem finem ordinans dedit talem virtutem, ut in his imprimeretur character; ex parte vero formae est sanctae Trinitatis invocatio. Et sic patent omnia.

1. Quod enim primo obiicitur de *virtutibus*, iam patet: quia secundum non est *configuratio*, quam attendatur signum distinctivum, eo quod non est signum sensibile[^11] extra.

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur de Sacramentis *veteris legis*, similiter patet responsio: quia ibi erat tantum configuratio exterius in carne, quia erant Sacramenta populi carnalis.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur: quod potest in maius potest in minus; dicendum, quod istud non est generaliter verum, quia frequenter virtus creaturae ordinatur ad maius, non ad minus; homo enim potest generare hominem, non festucam[^12].

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod omnia Sacramenta disponunt ad gratiam; dicendum, quod actus disponendi ad gratiam non est *proprius* characteris, sed magis qui praedeterminatus est.

Quod postmodum quaeritur, satis patet et melius patet[^13], si brevius resumantur praedicta. —

<!-- page 144 -->

Ordo enim et summa praedictorum est, quia character est signum interius distinctivum pariter et unitivum in aliquo fidei statu; ideo imprimitur a signo visibili, interius operante, respiciente statum fidei determinatum. Status ergo fidei determinatus est prima ratio *imprimendi*. Quia «finis imponit necessitatem his quae sunt ad finem[^14]», ideo, quia ipse instituit ad hunc finem Sacramenta, dedit *virtutem* et *formam* verborum congruam. — Est etiam ratio *multiplicandi*. Quia enim tantum tres status fidei sunt et tres distinctiones[^15], ideo tantum tres characteres. — Est etiam ratio *ordinandi*. Nam fidei generatio praecedit confirmationem, et confirmatio multiplicationem; sed primum de necessitate praecedit secundum et tertium et secundum praecedit tertium de congruo. Talis ordo est in distinctionibus populi: nam primo est separatio *fidelis* populi ab Aegyptiis vel malis; secundo, *fortium* ab infirmis; tertio, *sanctorum* et perfectorum ab aliis. Et sic est ordo in characteribus.

### Scholion

Hal., S. p. IV. q. 8. m. 8. a. 1. § 4. 7. — S. Thom., IV. Sent. d. 4. q. 1. a. 4; S. III. q. 63. a. 1. 6. — B. Albert., hic a. 6. quaestiunc. 2. — Petr. a Tar., IV. Sent. d. 5. q. 2. a. 3. — Richard. a Med. rem tangit IV. Sent. d. 5. q. 2. a. 3. — Durand., IV. Sent. d. 4. q. 1.

**II.** Quoad seq. (5.) quaestionem tum Eugenii IV. Decretum pro Armenis tum Concil. Trident. (Sess. VII. de Sacram. in generali can. 9.) characterem dicunt esse indelebilem; quod secundum Scot. (loc. cit. n. 20.) intelligendum est non respectu potentiae Dei absolutae («quia nulla forma potest esse in anima, quam non possit Deus de potentia absoluta delere»), sed de potentia ordinaria. Quod character sit indelebilis etiam in altera vita (hic ad 4.), communi theologorum suffragio probatur. — Rationem, quare character sit indelebilis, S. Thom. Bonav., vix ab illo recedit, nisi quod doceat, ut diximus, *imaginem* esse eiusdem subiectum (supra q. 3.). Praeter locos citt.: Alex. Hal., loc. cit. § 5. — S. ## English
<!-- page 142 -->

### Question IV. *By what means the character is imprinted.*

Fourthly it is asked, by what means the character is imprinted, and it is asked whether it is imprinted through the *virtues*, as it is through the Sacraments. And that it is so, seems shown:

1. Because the character is a conforming or configuring sign; but through the virtues the soul is configured to God[^1]: therefore the character is imprinted through the virtues.

2. Likewise I ask whether it is imprinted through the *Sacraments of the Old Testament*. — And it seems that it is: because the character is a distinctive sign, distinguishing a people from a non-people; but the Lord in the time of the Law had a people who had to be distinguished, just as also now: therefore if they were distinguished through the Sacraments[^2], it is clear that in those a distinctive seal was imprinted; but this is a character: therefore etc.

3. Likewise I ask whether it is through *all the Sacraments of the New Testament*. — And it seems that it is: because what can do the greater can do the lesser[^3]; but all give grace, which is greater than the character: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, the character disposes to grace[^4]: therefore since all the Sacraments give grace and dispose to it, all give the character.

**On the contrary:** 1. The character is an indelible sign[^5], and that alone is impressive of a character which is not repeated upon the subject; but only three Sacraments are non-repeatable, namely baptism, confirmation, and order: therefore only those three imprint a character.

It is therefore asked further on account of this — since every passion[^6] which is in certain things is in them through some common nature — what is that *common nature* found in these three, and not in the others. And it is established that it is not *matter*, because it is not common; nor *form*, because neither is that common. — And *if you say* that they agree in the *invocation of the Trinity*, and because there is one *efficient cause*[^7]: likewise in the others too there is invocation of the Trinity and an efficient cause, since he is one in all the Sacraments. It is necessary therefore that that nature be taken from the side of the *end*. But this does not seem so, because in the *common* end all seem to agree; but in the *proximate* ends all differ. — And *if you say* that this is because these three Sacraments are those regarding a determinate state of faith: therefore since faith is the same according to the diverse states, it seems that in all these Sacraments one character is imprinted. But it is established that there are many characters, otherwise no one could be ordained if he were not confirmed.

<!-- page 143 -->

It is therefore asked what is the *ground of the multitude*; and it seems that they are *superfluous*, since they could be sufficiently distinguished by one. But that they are *many*; seems shown, because the interior character has a likeness with the exterior sign; but the signs are dissimilar and diverse: therefore so too are the characters.

### Conclusion.

> *The character is imprinted not in the virtues, but in the Sacraments of the new law, and only in three.*

**I respond:** For the understanding of the foregoing it must be noted that the character is an *interior distinctive sign*. Since, then, to signify does not belong to it except by reason of a visible sign; therefore it is imprinted only in the Sacraments, not in the virtues. But since it distinguishes interiorly and spiritually; therefore it is in the Sacraments, which are of a people not carnal but spiritual: therefore it is imprinted in the Sacraments of the new law, not of the old. Since it is distinctive not of one, but of many; therefore unitive of many in one state of faith: therefore it is imprinted only in those Sacraments which regard a determinate state of faith. — Now there are three states of faith, namely *begotten*, *strengthened*, and now *multiplied*; and according to this a threefold distinction is observed in the people, namely of the *good* from the wicked, of the *strong* from the weak, of *clerics* from the laity. — And this threefold distinction was prefigured in the people of Israel: the first, when all are distinguished from the Egyptians for coming into the land of promise; the second, when the strong from the weak for fighting in war; the third, when the Levites from the rest of the people for ministering in the temple[^8]. — Likewise the distinction was threefold in the house of David: the first, his house from the unbelievers; the second, because there were there especially *warriors*; the third, because the Levites and priests and scribes were also distinguished from the rest, and also had *leaders* and *princes*[^9].

Since therefore there are only three Sacraments which regard a *determinate state of faith*: baptism, the first because there faith is begotten, and a man is separated from the Egyptians; confirmation, the second, because there faith is strengthened, and as a champion a man is anointed for battle and is set apart from the weak; and order, the third, because there manifold power is given, and a man as holy is set apart from the laity for the ministry of the temple: therefore these three alone imprint a character.

The common nature, therefore, found in these and not in the others, is that they are visible signs operating interiorly, regarding a determinate state of faith. And because the distinction of the *people* from the non-people precedes the other distinctions; therefore the baptismal character precedes the others of necessity. But the distinction of the *strong* from the weak precedes the other distinction only by fittingness, not of necessity. — From this, then, is clear the *order* of the characters; clear also the *number*, because there are three according to the three states and the three distinctions and the three Sacraments; clear also the *ground* of imprinting. For, presupposing identity on the side of the material element, by reason of which signification belongs to it, the principal ground is taken from the side of the end. Since, however, the end moves the *efficient cause*, and the efficient cause according to the requirement of the end introduces the *form* into the matter[^10]: a threefold ground concurs: and the first is the determination of the state of faith on the side of the end; on the side of the efficient cause, the institution, who, ordaining to such an end, gave such power that in these a character might be imprinted; on the side of the form, however, is the invocation of the holy Trinity. And thus all things are clear.

1. As for what is first objected concerning the *virtues*, it is now clear: because [it is] not according to a *configuration* that the distinctive sign is regarded, on account of the fact that it is not a sensible sign[^11] outwardly.

2. To that which is objected concerning the Sacraments of the *old law*, the response is likewise clear: because there was there only an exterior configuration in the flesh, since they were the Sacraments of a carnal people.

3. To that which is objected: that what can do the greater can do the lesser; it must be said that this is not generally true, because frequently the power of a creature is ordained to the greater, not to the lesser; for a man can beget a man, not a straw[^12].

4. To that which is objected, that all the Sacraments dispose to grace; it must be said that the act of disposing to grace is not *proper* to the character, but rather to that which is predetermined.

As for what is asked afterward, it is sufficiently clear, and is more clearly clear[^13], if the foregoing be more briefly resumed. —

<!-- page 144 -->

For the order and sum of the foregoing is this: that the character is an interior sign at once distinctive and unitive in some state of faith; therefore it is imprinted by a visible sign, operating interiorly, regarding a determinate state of faith. The determinate state of faith, then, is the first ground of *imprinting*. Since «the end imposes necessity upon the things that are unto the end[^14]», therefore, because he himself instituted the Sacraments unto this end, he gave the *power* and the congruous *form* of words. — There is also a ground of *multiplying*. For since there are only three states of faith and three distinctions[^15], therefore only three characters. — There is also a ground of *ordering*. For the begetting of faith precedes confirmation, and confirmation precedes multiplication; but the first of necessity precedes the second and the third, and the second precedes the third by fittingness. Such is the order in the distinctions of the people: for first there is the separation of the *faithful* people from the Egyptians or the wicked; second, of the *strong* from the weak; third, of the *holy* and perfect from the rest. And thus there is order in the characters.

### Scholion

**I.** In solving this question all agree well enough. Yet Scotus (here q. 9, n. 21) holds that it can be conceded that in circumcision some imperfect character was imprinted. On the same, Alexander of Hales, *Summa* p. IV, q. 8, m. 8, a. 1, § 4, 7. — St. Thomas, IV Sent. d. 4, q. 1, a. 4; *Summa* III, q. 63, a. 1, 6. — Bl. Albert, here a. 6, quaestiuncula 2. — Peter of Tarentaise, IV Sent. d. 5, q. 2, a. 3. — Richard of Middleton touches on the matter, IV Sent. d. 5, q. 2, a. 3. — Durandus, IV Sent. d. 4, q. 1.

**II.** As to the following (5th) question, both Eugenius IV's Decree for the Armenians and the Council of Trent (Sess. VII, on the Sacraments in general, can. 9) say the character is indelible; which according to Scotus (loc. cit. n. 20) is to be understood not with respect to God's absolute power («because no form can be in the soul which God could not by absolute power delete»), but of the ordinary power. That the character is indelible even in the other life (here, to obj. 4) is proved by the common consent of the theologians. — The reason why the character is indelible St. Thomas (*Summa* loc. cit. a. 5) places in this, that «the soul is the subject of the character according to the intellective part»; but (ibid. ad 2) he adds: «on account of the immobility of the principal mover». St. Bonaventure scarcely departs from him, except that he teaches, as we have said, that the *image* is its subject (above, q. 3). Besides the cited places: Alexander of Hales, loc. cit. § 5. — St. Thomas, Commentary, loc. cit. quaestiuncula 4. — Bl. Albert, here a. 7, quaestiuncula 2. — Peter of Tarentaise, loc. cit. a. 2, quaestiuncula 2. — Richard of Middleton, loc. cit. q. 3.

**III.** On the 6th question see below, d. 23, a. 2, q. 4, and the Council of Trent, Sess. VII, on baptism, can. 11, which defines that true and rightly conferred baptism is not to be repeated. On the same question: Alexander of Hales, loc. cit. m. 12. — Scotus, here q. 7. — St. Thomas, here q. 2, a. 5; *Summa* III, q. 66, a. 9. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 3, a. 1. — Richard of Middleton, here a. 4, q. 1. — Dionysius the Carthusian, here q. 3.
