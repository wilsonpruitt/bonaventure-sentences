# Dubia circa litteram Magistri (Dist. VII)

## *Dubia circa litteram Magistri*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 175 -->

### Dub. I

In parte ista sunt dubitationes circa litteram, et primo dubitatur de hoc quod dicit Magister, quod pro scandalo semel tantum concessisse aestimatur[^1][^2].

Videtur enim non fuisse recta talis concessio, quia scandalum de veritate vitae vel doctrinae nihil immutat[^7]; si ergo solus episcopus potest confirmare, videtur, quod propter scandalum non debuit illud permittere.

<!-- page 176 -->

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod quamvis sacerdos non possit propria auctoritate, tamen pro causa necessaria summus Pontifex, in quo est plenitudo potestatis, potest alii quam episcopo[^p176-1] committere; et ille non facit auctoritate sua, sed auctoritate sibi commissa, unde ille potius facit, cuius auctoritate fit: unde non est dubium, quin pontificalis auctoritas sit de necessitate huius Sacramenti. — Aliqui tamen dicunt, quod non concessit eis conferre Sacramentum, sed solum aliquod sacramentale, sicut aliquando datur laicis panis benedictus loco corporis dominici[^p176-2].

### Dub. II

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *virtus huius Sacramenti est donatio Spiritus sancti ad robur;* quia, si hoc verum est, *contra* hoc est, quia eucharistia confirmat, unde in Psalmo[^p176-3]: *Panis cor hominis confirmat.*

**Respondeo:** Ad hoc multipliciter respondetur. Primo modo sic, quod istud Sacramentum ordinatur ad robur *per se,* sed eucharistia *per accidens*[^p176-4]; sed hoc non solvit, quia, sive per se sive per accidens, dum tamen habeant effectum eundem, videtur, alterum superfluere. — Secundus modus dicendi est, quod confirmatio confirmat *contra malum,* sed eucharistia *in bono.* Sed nec illud sufficit, quia omne Sacramentum, cum sit remedium, est contra malum, et ita eucharistia. — Alii dicunt, quod duo sunt nobis necessaria, scilicet *fides* et *mores;* et unum confirmat in *fide,* scilicet confirmatio, sed eucharistia quantum ad bonos *mores;* quia, ut dicit Bernardus[^p176-5], eucharistia non solum delet venialia, sed etiam consensum fortificat, ne inclinetur ad mortalia. — Alii dicunt, quod utrumque fortificat fidem; sed duo necessaria sunt ad fidem firmam, scilicet *corde credere et ore confiteri*[^p176-6]; et eucharistia confirmat quantum ad *fidem in corde,* sed confirmatio quantum ad *confessionem* sive prolationem in ore. — Alii dicunt, quod utrumque fortificat caritatem, quia ab eius fortitudine pendet fortitudo omnium. Sed caritas potest deficere dupliciter, scilicet per defectum alimenti, vel per actionem contrarii, sicut patet in igne materiali[^p176-7]; et contra primum est eucharistia, quae cibat; contra secundum confirmatio, ut impugnantibus resistat. — Alii dicunt aliter et probabilius, quod confirmatio est ad corroborationem *fidei,* sed eucharistia *caritatis;* et hoc patet, quia hic est signum confessionis fidelis, ibi signum unionis. — Et si quaeratur: quare magis confirmantur hae duae virtutes quam aliae? dicendum, quod quia in his duabus pendet fortitudo spiritualis aedificii[^p176-8] quantum ad intellectum et affectum; et quia in *fide* est prima elevatio, in *caritate* elevatio summa, et utrobique difficultas; et quia pronitas est ad contrarium: ideo magis confirmantur[^p176-9].

### Dub. III

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit Rabanus[^p176-10], quod hoc Sacramentum maiori veneratione tenendum est, sicut a maioribus datur. Videtur enim secundum hoc, quod amplior gratia in eo datur.

**Respondeo:** Et dicendum, quod communiter dici consuevit, quod unum Sacramentum dicitur maius alio quinque modis. Primo modo propter *significationem;* sic matrimonium dicitur maius, quia significat unitatem naturarum in Christo[^p176-11]. Secundo modo propter *continentiam;* et sic eucharistia dicitur maius. Tertio, propter *efficaciam;* et sic baptismus. Quarto, propter *dignitatem ministrantium* sive dispensantium; et sic dicitur confirmatio maior, quia datur a summis sacerdotibus sive episcopis[^p176-12]. Quinto, propter hoc, quod *ponit in nobiliori gradu;* et sic dicitur ordo maius Sacramentum.

### Dub. IV

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *In digniori parte corporis fit.* Videtur enim falsum dicere, quia vertex est dignior quantum ad *situm,* et cor quantum ad *virtutem:* ergo potius debet dari in *capite,* vel in *corde.* — Item, videtur, quod in *lingua,* quia ibi est confessio.

<!-- page 177 -->

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod pars dignior quantum ad virtutem et operationem est cor, sed tamen occulta est et latet interius[^p177-1]. Inter partes vero, quae se offerunt sensibus nostris, caput est dignius, et in capite facies, scilicet anterior pars: et ideo hoc Sacramentum datur in facie, sed magis in fronte, quia ibi est sedes verecundiae[^p177-2]. Unde quia hoc Sacramentum est contra confusionem et verecundiam confitendi nomen Christi, quae linguam contrahit, ne aperte dicat, debet hoc Sacramentum in fronte dari. Frons quidem pars est faciei eminentior. Unde dixerunt quidam, quod in nova lege fit prima unctio in *capite* ratione virtutis, secunda vero in *fronte,* tertia iterum in *capite.* Et prima fit in baptismo; secunda vero in confirmatione; tertia vero in episcopi consecratione[^p177-3]. — Et prima signata est in prima unctione David, primi Regum decimo sexto[^p177-4]; secunda vero in unctione David super duas tribus, secundi Regum secundo; tertia vero in unctione David super cunctum regnum, secundi Regum quinto. Et satis possunt aptari istis, si quis inspiciat. In prima enim David non habuit potentiam; in secunda habuit potentiam et resistentiam; in tertia habuit potentiam et praesidentiam super gentem suam[^p177-5].

### Dub. V

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Hoc Sacramentum tantum a ieiunis et ieiunis*[^p177-6] *tradi debet.* Videtur enim, quod quia est ad robur, quod cibus non impediat: cibus enim corporalis non repugnat fortitudini spirituali.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod in hoc Sacramento datur gratia, et gratia corroborans; et iterum, contrahitur affinitas quaedam. Ideo ista tria statuta fuerunt[^p177-7] ab antiquis Patribus in Sacramento servari tanquam congrua: ut ieiuno stomacho propter gratiam Spiritus sancti suscipiendam; ut confirmandus ab alio portetur vel teneatur, qui sit confirmatus, in signum debilitatis et imbecillitatis; ut sit consimilis sexus propter periculum affinitatis[^p177-8].

### Dub. VI

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod nec debet iterari, sicut nec baptismus. Videtur enim quod possit iterari, quia morbus pusillanimitatis potest iterari, contra quem est: ergo et Sacramentum. *Si tu dicas,* quod non potest propter characterem; tunc videtur, quod character praeiudicet morbo: si ergo non est curatio morbi propter characterem, sed magis e converso, patet etc.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod illud Sacramentum non potest iterari propter *characterem,* qui imprimitur in eo indelebiliter. Non debet etiam tum ratione *morbi,* quem curat, tum ratione *effectus,* quem donat, tum ratione *rei,* quam allegorice significat. Ratione *morbi,* quia est poena contracta; haec autem, secundum quod huiusmodi, non iteratur, sed si iteratur, hoc est solum, secundum quod acta[^p177-9]. Ratione *effectus,* quem donat, qui est plenitudo gratiae ad robur; et quia integer effectus, ideo status. Ratione *rei,* quam allegorice significat, quia significat adventum Spiritus sancti de caelo in Apostolos in die Pentecostes[^p177-10], qui postquam advenit, nunquam ab eis discessit. Et sic patet illud.

## English
<!-- page 175 -->

### Doubt I

In this part there are doubts concerning the text, and first it is doubted about this which the Master says, that he is reckoned to have granted it only once on account of scandal[^1][^2].

For such a concession seems not to have been right, because scandal changes nothing of the truth of life or of doctrine[^7]; if therefore the bishop alone can confirm, it seems that he ought not to have permitted that on account of scandal.

<!-- page 176 -->

**I respond:** It must be said that although a priest cannot [confirm] by his own authority, yet for a necessary cause the supreme Pontiff, in whom is the fulness of power, can commit it to another than a bishop[^p176-1]; and that man does not act by his own authority, but by an authority committed to him, whence he rather acts by whose authority it is done: hence there is no doubt that pontifical authority belongs to the necessity of this Sacrament. — Yet some say that he did not grant them to confer the Sacrament, but only some sacramental, just as sometimes blessed bread is given to the laity in place of the Lord's body[^p176-2].

### Doubt II

Likewise it is asked about this which he says, that *the power of this Sacrament is the gift of the Holy Spirit unto strength;* because, if this is true, *against* this it is, that the eucharist confirms, whence in the Psalm[^p176-3]: *Bread strengthens the heart of man.*

**I respond:** To this it is answered in many ways. In the first way thus, that this Sacrament is ordained unto strength *of itself,* but the eucharist *accidentally*[^p176-4]; but this does not solve it, because, whether of itself or accidentally, so long as they have the same effect, one seems to be superfluous. — The second way of speaking is that confirmation confirms *against evil,* but the eucharist *in good.* But neither does that suffice, because every Sacrament, since it is a remedy, is against evil, and so [is] the eucharist. — Others say that two things are necessary for us, namely *faith* and *morals;* and one confirms in *faith,* namely confirmation, but the eucharist as to good *morals;* because, as Bernard says[^p176-5], the eucharist not only blots out venial [sins], but also strengthens consent, lest it be inclined toward mortal [sins]. — Others say that both strengthen faith; but two things are necessary for a firm faith, namely *to believe with the heart and to confess with the mouth*[^p176-6]; and the eucharist confirms as to *faith in the heart,* but confirmation as to *confession* or utterance with the mouth. — Others say that both strengthen charity, because on its strength depends the strength of all. But charity can fail in two ways, namely through defect of nourishment, or through the action of a contrary, as is plain in a material fire[^p176-7]; and against the first is the eucharist, which feeds; against the second confirmation, that it may resist attackers. — Others say otherwise and more probably, that confirmation is for the strengthening *of faith,* but the eucharist *of charity;* and this is plain, because here is the sign of a believer's confession, there the sign of union. — And if it be asked: why are these two virtues confirmed rather than others? it must be said that [it is] because on these two depends the strength of the spiritual edifice[^p176-8] as to intellect and affection; and because in *faith* is the first elevation, in *charity* the highest elevation, and in both [there is] difficulty; and because there is a proneness to the contrary: therefore they are confirmed the more[^p176-9].

### Doubt III

Likewise it is asked about this which Rabanus says[^p176-10], that this Sacrament is to be held in greater veneration, as it is given by greater [ministers]. For according to this it seems that a fuller grace is given in it.

**I respond:** And it must be said that it is commonly wont to be said that one Sacrament is called greater than another in five ways. In the first way on account of *signification;* thus matrimony is called greater, because it signifies the union of the natures in Christ[^p176-11]. In the second way on account of *containment;* and thus the eucharist is called greater. Third, on account of *efficacy;* and thus baptism. Fourth, on account of the *dignity of those ministering* or dispensing; and thus confirmation is called greater, because it is given by the highest priests or bishops[^p176-12]. Fifth, on account of this, that it *places in a nobler grade;* and thus order is called the greater Sacrament.

### Doubt IV

Likewise it is asked about this which he says: *It is done on the worthier part of the body.* For it seems false to say so, because the crown of the head is worthier as to *position,* and the heart as to *power:* therefore it ought rather to be given on the *head,* or on the *heart.* — Likewise, it seems [it should be] on the *tongue,* because there is confession.

<!-- page 177 -->

**I respond:** It must be said that the part worthier as to power and operation is the heart, but yet it is hidden and lies within[^p177-1]. But among the parts which offer themselves to our senses, the head is worthier, and in the head the face, namely the front part: and therefore this Sacrament is given on the face, but rather on the forehead, because there is the seat of shame[^p177-2]. Hence, because this Sacrament is against the confusion and shame of confessing the name of Christ, which contracts the tongue, lest one speak openly, this Sacrament ought to be given on the forehead. The forehead indeed is the more eminent part of the face. Hence some have said that in the new law the first anointing is made on the *head* by reason of power, the second on the *forehead,* the third again on the *head.* And the first is made in baptism; the second in confirmation; the third in the consecration of a bishop[^p177-3]. — And the first was signified in the first anointing of David, in the sixteenth [chapter] of the first [book] of Kings[^p177-4]; the second in the anointing of David over the two tribes, in the second [chapter] of the second [book] of Kings; the third in the anointing of David over the whole kingdom, in the fifth [chapter] of the second [book] of Kings. And they can well enough be fitted to these, if one looks. For in the first David did not have power; in the second he had power and resistance; in the third he had power and presidency over his people[^p177-5].

### Doubt V

Likewise it is asked about this which he says: *This Sacrament ought to be delivered only by those fasting and to those fasting*[^p177-6]. For it seems that, because it is unto strength, food should not impede it: for bodily food is not repugnant to spiritual strength.

**I respond:** It must be said that in this Sacrament grace is given, and a strengthening grace; and again, a certain affinity is contracted. Therefore these three things were established[^p177-7] by the ancient Fathers to be observed in the Sacrament as fitting: that [it be received] on a fasting stomach, on account of the grace of the Holy Spirit to be received; that the one to be confirmed be carried or held by another who is [already] confirmed, in sign of weakness and feebleness; that he be of the like sex, on account of the danger of affinity[^p177-8].

### Doubt VI

Likewise it is asked about this which he says, that neither ought it to be repeated, just as neither [is] baptism. For it seems that it can be repeated, because the sickness of faint-heartedness, against which it is, can be repeated: therefore [so can] the Sacrament also. *If you say* that it cannot, on account of the character; then it seems that the character prejudices the sickness: if therefore there is no cure of the sickness on account of the character, but rather the contrary, it is plain etc.

**I respond:** It must be said that that Sacrament cannot be repeated on account of the *character,* which is imprinted in it indelibly. Nor ought it [to be repeated], whether by reason of the *sickness* which it cures, or by reason of the *effect* which it gives, or by reason of the *thing* which it allegorically signifies. By reason of the *sickness,* because it is a contracted penalty; and this, as such, is not repeated, but if it is repeated, this is only insofar as it is acted[^p177-9]. By reason of the *effect* which it gives, which is the fulness of grace unto strength; and because the effect is whole, therefore [it is] a settled state. By reason of the *thing* which it allegorically signifies, because it signifies the coming of the Holy Spirit from heaven into the Apostles on the day of Pentecost[^p177-10], who, after he came, never departed from them. And thus that is plain.
