# d.6 a.1 q.3 — In quo sit character sicut in subiecto

## *In quo sit character sicut in subiecto*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 141 -->

### Quaestio III. *In quo sit character sicut in subiecto.*

Tertio quaeritur, in quo sit character sicut in subiecto. Et quod in *tota anima*, videtur:

1. Quia character distinguit ovem Christi a non ove, et membrum Christi a non membro[^1]; sed anima non dicitur ovis nec membrum secundum potentiam, sed secundum substantiam: ergo character secundum substantiam distinguit.

2. Item, cuius est dispositio, et habitus; sed character disponit ad gratiam et remissionem originalis, quod respicit totam animam[^2]: ergo character est in animae substantia.

Sed quod sit tantum in *potentia cognitiva*, videtur:

3. Quia omne quod est in anima pertinens ad cognitionem, est in potentia cognitiva; sed character pertinet ad cognitionem, cum sit signum[^3]: ergo etc.

4. Item, in una anima est unus character per unum Sacramentum; sed omnis habitus est in potentia ut in subiecto[^4]: ergo cum accidens sit in uno subiecto, character est in una potentia. Sed potentia, quae maxime huic convenit, est cognitiva: ergo etc.

Sed quod sit in *tota imagine*, videtur:

1. Character est quaedam configuratio[^5]; sed configurari dicitur anima Trinitati secundum tres potentias, non unam tantum: ergo etc.

2. Item, character disponit principaliter ad imaginis reformationem; sed reformatio imaginis attenditur quantum ad informationem ipsarum potentiarum, ut convertuntur ad Deum[^6]: ergo etc.

### Conclusio

> Character est in anima secundum totam imaginem, appropriatur tamen potentiae cognitivae.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod circa hoc triplex fuit opinio.

Quidam dixerunt, quod character est in *tota anima* propter rationes praedictas, quia distinguit ovem ab ove, vel ab ea quae non est ovis: et hoc respicit animae substantiam.

Quidam, quod in *cognitiva* propter rationem signandi, et quoniam signum, quod est ratio cognoscendi, respicit potentiam cognitivam.

Quidam, quod in *tota imagine* propter rationem configurandi. Et quia actus characteris, a quo nomen accepit, et principalis[^7] est *configurare*: ideo haec tertia melior est, quod sit in anima secundum *imaginem* secundum *proprietatem* et *veritatem*. Sed quia imago, etsi sit in tribus, tamen *appropriatur cognitivae*, secundum quod dicitur in libro *de Spiritu et anima*[^8], quod « *imago* est in potentia cognoscendi, *similitudo* in potentia diligendi »: ideo per *appropriationem* est in potentia cognitiva.

**Ad argumenta:**

1. *Ad illud* ergo quod obiicitur, quod distinguit animam: ergo est in anima; verum est, sed non oportet, quod sit in *tota* anima, sicut color non tingit corpus, sed tamen primo modo est in superficie[^9].

<!-- page 142 -->

2. *Ad illud* quod obiicitur: cuius est dispositio, et habitus; dicendum, quod verum est de illa dispositione, quae fit habitus; sed de alia non sic, quia habitus unius potentiae disponit ad habitum alterius, ut habitus fidei ad habitum caritatis[^10].

3. *Ad illud* quod obiicitur, quod spectat ad cognitionem; dicendum, quod verum est, si sit *habitus* cognoscendi. Et *praeterea*, non tantum[^11] ad cognitionem spectat, sed etiam ad gratiam reformantem totam imaginem.

4. *Ad illud* quod obiicitur, quod est unum in anima una; dici potest dupliciter: quod unum accidens est in pluribus, sicut una gratia in pluribus potentiis, et una sanitas in pluribus membris[^12], quando accidens non respicit determinate unam potentiam, sed eas secundum quod fundantur in uno. — Vel aliter: imago una est, et tamen in tribus potentiis, quia dicit relationem ad unum; similiter dici potest in charactere. Quia enim imago importat distinctionem, ideo comparatur ad plura sicut ad unum subiectum. Et exemplum est in numero: ternarius enim unus est in tribus hominibus, et unus est, quamvis illi sint plures in se, qui sunt unum subiectum ternarii, et nullus eorum per se tantum est subiectum. Ita et in charactere intelligendum est, quod dicit, sicut praetactum est[^13], quandam configurationem animae ad beatam Trinitatem, mediam inter configurationem animae quantum ad naturam, et configurationem eiusdem quantum ad gratiam gratum facientem[^14].

### Scholion

**III.** Quoad seq. (3.) quaestionem negari non potest, *radicale* subiectum characteris esse animam; sed quaestio remanet, quid sit *immediatum* et *proximum* eiusdem subiectum. Quod non ipsa *essentia* animae, sed eius *potentiae*, quatenus in eis est imago Dei, sint proximum characteris subiectum, antiqui Scholastici concorditer resolvunt. Iterum autem quaeritur, in *qua* principaliter sit potentia. S. Thom., cui favent B. Albert., Petr. a Tar. et Richard., docet, eum esse in potentia cognitiva, « quia ex memoria et intelligentia oritur voluntas; unde tota imago est in intellectiva parte sicut in radice » (Comment. loc. cit. a. 3. quaestiunc. 3.); Scot. vero pro eo assignat *voluntatem*; S. Bonav. cum Alexandro vult, eum esse in tribus animae potentiis, quatenus sunt imago, et per *appropriationem* tantum specialiter tribui potentiae *cognitivae*. De hac 3. quaestione: Alex. Hal., loc. cit. § 3. — Scot., hic q. 11; Report. hic q. 10. — S. Thom., loc. cit. a. 3; S. loc. cit. a. 4. — B. Albert., hic a. 4. quaestiunc. 9. — Petr. a Tar., loc. cit. a. 2. quaestiunc. 1. — Richard. a Med., loc. cit. q. 2.

## English

### Question III. *In what the character is as in a subject.*

Thirdly it is asked, in what the character is as in a subject. And that it is in the *whole soul*, it seems:

1. Because the character distinguishes the sheep of Christ from the non-sheep, and the member of Christ from the non-member[^1]; but the soul is not called a sheep nor a member with respect to a power, but with respect to substance: therefore the character distinguishes with respect to substance.

2. Likewise, that to which a disposition belongs, also a habit; but the character disposes to grace and to the remission of original [sin], which regards the whole soul[^2]: therefore the character is in the substance of the soul.

But that it is only in the *cognitive power*, it seems:

3. Because everything in the soul pertaining to cognition is in the cognitive power; but the character pertains to cognition, since it is a sign[^3]: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, in one soul there is one character through one Sacrament; but every habit is in a power as in a subject[^4]: therefore, since an accident is in one subject, the character is in one power. But the power to which this most belongs is the cognitive: therefore etc.

But that it is in the *whole image*, it seems:

1. The character is a certain configuration[^5]; but the soul is said to be configured to the Trinity according to the three powers, not one alone: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, the character disposes principally to the reformation of the image; but the reformation of the image is observed with respect to the information of the powers themselves, as they are turned toward God[^6]: therefore etc.

### Conclusio

> The character is in the soul according to the whole image, yet it is appropriated to the cognitive power.

**I respond:** It must be said that concerning this there have been three opinions.

Some have said that the character is in the *whole soul* for the aforesaid reasons, because it distinguishes the sheep from the [other] sheep, or from that which is not a sheep: and this regards the substance of the soul.

Some [have said] that it is in the *cognitive* [power] by reason of signifying, and because a sign, which is a ground of knowing, regards the cognitive power.

Some [have said] that it is in the *whole image* by reason of configuring. And because the act of the character, from which it takes its name, and its principal [act][^7], is *to configure*: therefore this third [opinion] is better, that it is in the soul according to the *image*, according to *property* and *truth*. But because the image, although it is in the three, nevertheless *is appropriated to the cognitive* [power], according to what is said in the book *On the Spirit and the Soul*[^8], that « the *image* is in the power of knowing, the *likeness* in the power of loving »: therefore by *appropriation* it is in the cognitive power.

**To the arguments:**

1. *To that* therefore which is objected, that it distinguishes the soul: therefore it is in the soul; it is true, but it is not necessary that it be in the *whole* soul, just as color does not tinge the [whole] body, yet in the primary mode it is in the surface[^9].

2. *To that* which is objected: that to which a disposition belongs, also a habit; it must be said that this is true of that disposition which becomes a habit; but not so of another, because the habit of one power disposes to the habit of another, as the habit of faith [disposes] to the habit of charity[^10].

3. *To that* which is objected, that it regards cognition; it must be said that this is true, if it be a *habit* of knowing. And *moreover*, it regards not only[^11] cognition, but also grace reforming the whole image.

4. *To that* which is objected, that it is one thing in one soul; it can be said in two ways: that one accident is in many [things], as one grace [is] in many powers, and one health in many members[^12], when an accident does not regard determinately one power, but [regards] them according as they are founded in one. — Or otherwise: the image is one, and yet [is] in the three powers, because it bespeaks a relation to one; likewise it can be said of the character. For since the image implies distinction, therefore it is compared to many [things] as to one subject. And there is an example in number: for the number three is one in three men, and is one, although those [men] are several in themselves, who are the one subject of the three, and none of them by itself alone is the subject. So also in the character it must be understood that it bespeaks, as was touched on before[^13], a certain configuration of the soul to the blessed Trinity, intermediate between the configuration of the soul with respect to nature, and the configuration of the same with respect to grace making [one] pleasing[^14].

### Scholion

**III.** As to the following (3rd) question, it cannot be denied that the *radical* subject of the character is the soul; but the question remains, what is its *immediate* and *proximate* subject. That not the *essence* itself of the soul, but its *powers*, insofar as the image of God is in them, are the proximate subject of the character, the ancient Scholastics resolve in concord. Again, however, it is asked, in *which* [power] it is principally. St. Thomas, whom Bl. Albert, Peter of Tarentaise and Richard favor, teaches that it is in the cognitive power, « because from memory and understanding will arises; whence the whole image is in the intellective part as in a root » (Commentary, in the place cited, a. 3, quaestiuncula 3); but Scotus assigns the *will* for it; St. Bonaventure, with Alexander, holds that it is in the three powers of the soul, insofar as they are the image, and that by *appropriation* alone it is specially attributed to the *cognitive* power. On this 3rd question: Alexander of Hales, in the place cited, § 3. — Scotus, here, q. 11; Reportatio, here, q. 10. — St. Thomas, in the place cited, a. 3; St. [Bonaventure], in the place cited, a. 4. — Bl. Albert, here, a. 4, quaestiuncula 9. — Peter of Tarentaise, in the place cited, a. 2, quaestiuncula 1. — Richard of Mediavilla, in the place cited, q. 2.
