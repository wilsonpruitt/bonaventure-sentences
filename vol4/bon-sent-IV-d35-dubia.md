# d.35 dubia
## *Dubia circa litteram Magistri*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 788 -->

### Dub. I.

In parte ista sunt dubitationes circa litteram, et primo super hoc quod dicit: *Nequit adultera uxor dimitti a viro*; quaeritur, utrum vir, qui fornicatur in occulto, possit dimittere uxorem, quae fornicatur in aperto. Et quod sic, videtur: quoniam vir potest agere contra uxorem, et illa non potest excipere contra virum, et si excipit, nihil valet, cum non possit probare. — Iuxta hoc quaeritur, in quibus casibus competat uxori excipere contra virum petentem divortium.

Respondeo: Dicendum, quod talis vir fornicans non debet eam dimittere nec potest sine peccato in conspectu Dei, licet possit in conspectu Ecclesiae, pro eo quod uxori non defuit ius excipiendi, quamvis desit probatio. Argumentum est Causa trigesima secunda, quaestione sexta per totum. Si tamen vir egisset poenitentiam, et illa accusata nollet agere poenitentiam, sed perseveraret in adulterio; creditur tunc, quod vir possit eam accusare et dimittere per sententiam; si vero vir esset in crimine notorio, sicut uxor, non posset. — Unde septem sunt casus, in quibus secundum iura adultera potest excipere contra virum. Primus est, si fornicari convincitur; Causa trigesima secunda, quaestione sexta per totum, et Extra, *de Divortiis*[^p788-4]. — Secundus, si quis prostituit eam; Extra, *de Eo qui cognovit consanguineam uxoris suae*, *Discretionem* etc. — Tertius, cum credebat maritum in bello defunctum et nupsit alii; Causa trigesima quarta, quaestione prima: *Cum per bellicam*[^p788-5]. — Quartus, si cognita fuit latenter ab aliquo, quem credebat esse virum suum; Argumentum Causa trigesima quarta, quaestione prima[^p788-6]. — Quintus, si fuerit vi oppressa; Causa trigesima secunda, quaestione quinta, *Proposito* etc.; hoc autem intelligendum est de vi absoluta, non de metu. — Sextus, quando reconciliavit eam sibi post adulterium et petiit debitum; Causa trigesima secunda, quaestione prima. — Septimus est rarus, si infidelis repudiat uxorem, et illa ducit virum alterum; si veniat ad fidem, debet eam recipere, non obstante, quod fuerit fornicata; Extra, *de Divortiis*[^p788-7], *Gaudemus*.

### Dub. II.

Item super hoc quod dicit: *Cum tanto gravius eos puniri oportuerit* etc.; quaeritur: quis gravius peccat, utrum mulier, quae adulteratur, quam vir, vel e converso? Et quod magis *vir*, videtur littera[^p788-8] dicere. — Item, sexus muliebris est infirmior et ad

<!-- page 789 -->

lapsum pronior per naturam: ergo magis excusatur in culpa. — Sed *contra* hoc est, quia sexus muliebris naturaliter est verecundior et viro debet esse subiectior: ergo videtur, quod gravius peccat, si adulteratur.

Respondeo: Dicendum, quod ista duo crimina se habent sicut excedentia et excessa. In hoc enim, quod sexus virilis est stabilior per naturam et debet regere feminam, gravius peccat adulterando; sed quia vir maiorem habet impulsionem ad luxuriam propter fortitudinem caloris et quia maiorem habet copiam, nec est ita sexus verecundus per naturam: hinc est, quod mulier gravius peccat, et ideo maior ex peccato mulieris oritur infamia tam viro quam mulieri. Unde aliquo modo verum est hic quod dicit Gregorius[^p789-1] de carnalibus vitiis, quod sunt minoris culpae, sed maioris infamiae. — Fere enim in omnibus vitiis istud reperitur, quod ad invicem comparata se habent ut excedentia et excessa. — Verumtamen, si quaeratur, quod magis excedat; videtur, quod fornicatio mulieris, quia magis laedit virum et minus habet impulsivum, licet sit minus fortis ad resistendum; illud enim recompensatur per humanam verecundiam, quae debet eam retrahere multum[^p789-2].

### Dub. III.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit Chrysostomus: *Patronus enim est turpitudinis qui celat crimen uxoris*. Videtur enim male dicere, quia Proverbiorum undecimo[^p789-3]: *Fidelis est qui celat crimen amici*: ergo bonum est crimen celare. — Item, qui scienter infamat alterum aufert ei maximum bonum: ergo graviter peccat.

Respondeo: Dicendum, quod *celare* est dupliciter: aut cum scit, sed tamen est *occultum aliis*, et non detegit; aut cum scit[^p789-4] *apertum* et notum et dissimulando cooperit. Et primum est *fidelitatis*, secundum vero est *praebere patrocinium sceleri*. — Vel aliter, *celare* hoc est dupliciter: aut in *praeiudicium correptionis*, aut in *supportationem peccatoris* et infirmi; et primum est malum, secundum vero est bonum. — Vel aliter, *celare crimen* est dupliciter: aut personae, quae *potest prodesse*; aut personae, quae *potest obesse*[^p789-5]; primum est malum, secundum licitum. Et sic patet illud.

### Dub. IV.

Item super hoc quod dicit Augustinus: *Quod si tibi durum videtur, ut post adulterium tibi reconcilietur coniux* etc.; quaeritur, si sententia divortii lata est per errorem, et post vir promotus est ad sacerdotium; si error Ecclesiae et deceptio pateat, utrum reddendus sit uxori, si ipsa non vult continere. Et quod *sic*, videtur: quia mulier habebat ius suum, et salvo iure mulieris non poterat continentiam vovere contra voluntatem uxoris.

Respondeo: Dicendum, quod inducenda est ad continentiam; quodsi manifesta deceptio in latione prioris sententiae appareat, et ipsa non vult continere; debet vir suus sibi reddi. Verumtamen ille non poterit *petere* debitum, quoniam, quantum in se fuit, continentiam vovit[^p789-6]; et hoc dico, nisi mulier fuerit fornicata.

### Dub. V.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Et insuper moechae iuramentum dedisse* etc. Videtur enim, quod fides superaddita impediat suum iuramentum. Sed videtur, quod *non*: quia licitum est hanc ducere uxorem post mortem viri: ergo licitum est promittere: ergo propter illam promissionem non debet impediri sequens matrimonium, sed confirmari: si ergo simplex adulterium non impedit, videtur, quod nec impediat cum fidei datione.

Respondeo: Dicendum, quod talis fides non est fides, sed potius *infidelitas*, pro eo quod est contra *bonum fidei*, quo uxor tenetur alligata viro; et ideo, cum in fidei datione matrimonia inchoentur, et talis fides matrimonio repugnet; non initiat, immo simpliciter inhabiles reddit, quia nec initiari amplius inter tales personas potest nec consummari. Similiter, si alter in mortem viri machinetur propter contrahendum matrimonium, potius se facit inhabilem, quam se reddat habilem[^p789-7]. Quod ergo obiicitur, quod est licitum; dicendum, quod non est, tum quia fit ex mala causa, tum quia est occasio multorum malorum; et ideo, si iurat, non obligatur ad iuramentum servandum, sed potius ad poenitentiam faciendam pro iuramento male et inordinate emisso. Et sic patet illud.

## English

### Doubt I.

In this part there are doubts concerning the text, and first upon that which he says: *An adulterous wife cannot be dismissed by her husband*; it is asked whether a husband who fornicates in secret may dismiss a wife who fornicates openly. And that he can, it seems: since the husband can bring an action against the wife, while she cannot lodge an exception against the husband, and if she does lodge it, it is of no avail, since she cannot prove it. — Along with this it is asked in which cases it is competent to a wife to lodge an exception against a husband who seeks a divorce.

I respond: It must be said that such a fornicating husband ought not to dismiss her, nor can he do so without sin in the sight of God, although he can in the sight of the Church, for the reason that the wife did not lack the right of lodging an exception, even though proof is wanting. The argument is Causa 32, question 6, throughout. If, however, the husband had done penance, and she, being accused, were unwilling to do penance but persevered in adultery, then it is held that the husband can accuse her and dismiss her by sentence; but if the husband were in a notorious crime, like the wife, he could not. — Hence there are seven cases in which, according to the laws, an adulteress can lodge an exception against her husband. The first is if he is convicted of fornication; Causa 32, question 6, throughout, and the Decretals, *On Divorces*[^p788-4]. — The second, if someone has prostituted her; the Decretals, *On the man who has known a kinswoman of his wife*, *Discretionem* etc. — The third, when she believed her husband dead in war and married another; Causa 34, question 1: *Cum per bellicam*[^p788-5]. — The fourth, if she was secretly known by someone whom she believed to be her own husband; the argument, Causa 34, question 1[^p788-6]. — The fifth, if she was overpowered by force; Causa 32, question 5, *Proposito* etc.; but this is to be understood of absolute force, not of fear. — The sixth, when he reconciled her to himself after the adultery and demanded the [marriage] debt; Causa 32, question 1. — The seventh is rare: if an unbeliever repudiates his wife, and she takes another husband; if he comes to the faith, he ought to take her back, notwithstanding that she has fornicated; the Decretals, *On Divorces*[^p788-7], *Gaudemus*.

### Doubt II.

Likewise, upon that which he says: *Since they must be punished so much the more gravely* etc.; it is asked: who sins more gravely, whether the woman who commits adultery, or the man, or the reverse? And that the man does more, the text seems[^p788-8] to say. — Likewise, the female sex is weaker and more

<!-- page 789 -->

prone to a fall by nature: therefore it is more excused in fault. — But *against* this stands the fact that the female sex is naturally more modest and ought to be more subject to the man: therefore it seems that she sins more gravely if she commits adultery.

I respond: It must be said that these two crimes stand to one another as the exceeding and the exceeded. For in this respect, that the male sex is more stable by nature and ought to rule the woman, he sins more gravely in committing adultery; but because the man has a greater impulse toward lust on account of the strength of his heat, and because he has greater abundance, and the sex is not so modest by nature: hence it is that the woman sins more gravely, and therefore a greater infamy arises from the woman's sin, both for the man and for the woman. Hence in some way what Gregory[^p789-1] says of the carnal vices is true, namely that they are of lesser guilt but greater infamy. — For in nearly all vices this is found, that when compared with one another they stand as the exceeding and the exceeded. — Nevertheless, if it be asked which exceeds the more, it seems that the woman's fornication does, because it harms the husband more and has less that impels it, although she is less strong for resisting; for this is compensated by the human modesty which ought to hold her back greatly[^p789-2].

### Doubt III.

Likewise it is asked concerning that which Chrysostom says: *For he is a patron of baseness who conceals his wife's crime*. For he seems to speak ill, because in Proverbs, chapter eleven[^p789-3]: *Faithful is he who conceals a friend's crime*: therefore it is good to conceal a crime. — Likewise, he who knowingly defames another takes from him the greatest good: therefore he sins gravely.

I respond: It must be said that to *conceal* is twofold: either when one knows, but it is nonetheless *hidden from others*, and he does not disclose it; or when one knows[^p789-4] it *open* and manifest and, by dissembling, covers it up. And the first belongs to *faithfulness*, but the second is to *furnish patronage to wickedness*. — Or otherwise, to *conceal* this is twofold: either to the *prejudice of correction*, or unto the *bearing-with of a sinner* and a weak person; and the first is evil, but the second is good. — Or otherwise, to *conceal a crime* is twofold: either to a person who *can be benefited*, or to a person who *can be harmed*[^p789-5]; the first is evil, the second permissible. And thus that [saying] is clear.

### Doubt IV.

Likewise, upon that which Augustine says: *But if it seems hard to you that after adultery your spouse should be reconciled to you* etc.; it is asked, if a sentence of divorce was passed through error, and afterward the man was promoted to the priesthood; if the error and deception of the Church become manifest, whether he must be restored to his wife, if she is unwilling to remain continent. And that he must, it seems: because the woman had her right, and, the woman's right being preserved, he could not vow continence against his wife's will.

I respond: It must be said that she must be led to continence; but if a manifest deception should appear in the passing of the prior sentence, and she is unwilling to remain continent, her own man must be restored to her. Nevertheless he will not be able to *demand* the debt, since, as far as lay in him, he vowed continence[^p789-6]; and this I say unless the woman has fornicated.

### Doubt V.

Likewise it is asked concerning that which he says: *And moreover that the adulteress has given her oath* etc. For it seems that the superadded faith [i.e. the pledge given] impedes his oath. But it seems that it does *not*: because it is permissible to take this woman as wife after the husband's death: therefore it is permissible to promise: therefore on account of that promise the subsequent marriage ought not to be impeded but confirmed: if therefore simple adultery does not impede, it seems that neither would it impede along with the giving of faith.

I respond: It must be said that such faith is not faith, but rather *infidelity*, for the reason that it is against the *good of faith* by which the wife is held bound to her husband; and therefore, since marriages are begun in the giving of faith, and such a faith is repugnant to marriage, it does not initiate [the marriage], but rather renders them simply incapable, since such persons can neither be initiated further [into marriage] nor consummate it. Likewise, if one party contrives the death of the [other's] husband in order to contract marriage, he rather makes himself incapable than renders himself capable[^p789-7]. As to the objection, then, that it is permissible, it must be said that it is not, both because it is done from an evil cause, and because it is the occasion of many evils; and therefore, if he swears, he is not bound to keep the oath, but rather to do penance for an oath wickedly and inordinately uttered. And thus that [saying] is clear.
