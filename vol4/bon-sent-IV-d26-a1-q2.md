## *A quo Sacramentum matrimonii fuerit institutum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 663 -->

### Quaestio II. *A quo Sacramentum matrimonii fuerit institutum.*

Secundo quaeritur, a quo fuerit Sacramentum matrimonii institutum[^1]. Et quod a Deo, videtur:

1. Per illud Matthaei decimo nono[^2]: *Quod Deus coniunxit, homo non separet*; hoc dicitur de coniunctione, quae est in matrimonio: ergo si ista est a Deo: patet etc.

2. Item, hoc ipsum videtur: quia Genesis primo[^3] dixit eis Deus: *Crescite et multiplicamini*; sed illud crementum erat per usum actus matrimonialis: ergo matrimonium fuit a Deo institutum.

3. Item, hoc ipsum ostenditur *per rationem*: quia, si Sacramenta sunt a voluntate humana; et omne tale est variabile et mutabile, cum Sacramenta sint immutabilia: videtur, quod non solum hoc, sed etiam omnia alia sunt a Deo instituta.

4. Item, Sacramenta, quanto maiora sunt, tanto a maiore instituenda sunt; sed Sacramentum matrimonii est praecipue magnum, sicut dicitur ad Ephesios quinto[^4]: *Sacramentum hoc magnum est*: ergo a maximo Deo, non ab homine institutum fuit.

**Contra:**

1. Institutio matrimonii fuit in illo verbo[^5]: *Hoc nunc os ex ossibus meis et caro de carne mea* etc.; sed istud verbum fuit Adae, non Dei: ergo fuit institutum ab homine, non a Deo.

2. Item, si Sacramentum matrimonii est institutum a Deo; et in his quae sunt a Deo instituta, non potest Ecclesia aliquid mutare nec disponere[^6]: ergo nec circa matrimonium. Sed tota die dispensat quantum ad hoc Sacramentum: ergo etc.

3. Item, non legitur Deum esse locutum de matrimonio, nisi quando dixit[^7]: *Crescite et multiplicamini* etc.; et cum hoc etiam dictum sit bestiis, videtur, quod etiam inter bestias constituit. Sed hoc est inconveniens: ergo etc.

4. Item, matrimonium contrahitur per humanum consensum[^8]: ergo est contractus humanus;
<!-- page 664 -->
sed contractus humani sunt instituti ab hominibus: ergo etc.

> **Conclusio.** Sacramentum Matrimonii fuit institutum a Deo, Adam interius illustrante.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod institutio matrimonii est a Deo. — Sed notandum, quod quaedam sunt Sacramenta, quae sunt pure fidei, sicut baptismus et confirmatio et consimilia; quaedam autem non tantum sunt fidei, sed etiam naturalis dictaminis; et talia sunt poenitentia et matrimonium, sed tamen non omnino sunt de dictamine iuris naturalis[^9]. Quae primo modo sunt Deus instituit *mandando*; quae secundo modo instituit *interius illustrando*; et sic instituit matrimonium. Unde ipse non praecepit Adam, quod Evam duceret in uxorem, sed illustravit eum interius, ut intelligeret, eam sibi dandam in uxorem, et omnibus posteris per liberum consensum uxores illo modo coniungi. Unde Adam sic illustratus dixit: *Hoc nunc os* etc., et sicut dicit Magister in littera[^10], prophetice est locutus; et ideo verbum illud non fuit a semetipso, sed a Deo: et ideo institutio huius Sacramenti fuit a Deo.

**Ad argumenta:**

*Ad 1.* Et sic patet primum.

*Ad 2.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod tunc Ecclesia nihil mutare potest; dicendum, quod Dominus instituit Sacramentum quoad formam et quoad materiam, quantum illi tempori congruebat, excipiendo solum patrem et matrem[^11]; et quia aliae personae secundum diversa tempora debebant congruere institutioni, de legitimitate personarum reliquit Ecclesiae dispensandum; et circa hanc dispensat Ecclesia.

*Ad 3.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod Dominus non est locutus de matrimonio nisi ibi: *Crescite et multiplicamini*; dicendum, quod falsum est, immo locutus est per Adam, quando Adam, Spiritu sancto illustratus, dixit illud verbum in persona Dei; et hoc patet, quia Matthaei decimo nono[^12] repetit illud ut verbum Dei.

*Ad 4.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod est contractus humanus; dicendum, quod non est *pure* humanus; et hoc patet sic. In matrimonio namque duo sunt: et mutua *obligatio* et obligationis mutuae *indissolubilitas*; et quamvis primum sit *hominis*, secundum est *instituentis*; et ratione illius indissolubilitatis praecipue matrimonium tenet rationem Sacramenti et signi sacri: et ideo non ab homine ortum habet, sed a Deo; et hoc melius patebit infra[^13], quando agetur de insolubilitate matrimonii.

## English

### Question II. *By whom the Sacrament of matrimony was instituted.*

Secondly it is asked, by whom the Sacrament of matrimony was instituted[^1]. And that it was by God, it seems:

1. By that passage of Matthew nineteen[^2]: *What God has joined together, let no man separate*; this is said of the joining which is in matrimony: therefore, if this is from God: it is evident, etc.

2. Likewise, this same thing seems [so]: because in Genesis one[^3] God said to them: *Increase and multiply*; but that increase was through the use of the matrimonial act: therefore matrimony was instituted by God.

3. Likewise, this same thing is shown *by reason*: because, if the Sacraments are from the human will, and everything of that sort is variable and mutable, whereas the Sacraments are immutable, it seems that not only this one, but also all the others are instituted by God.

4. Likewise, the greater the Sacraments are, by so much the greater is he by whom they must be instituted; but the Sacrament of matrimony is especially great, as is said to the Ephesians five[^4]: *This is a great sacrament*: therefore it was instituted by the greatest God, not by man.

**On the contrary:**

1. The institution of matrimony was in that word[^5]: *This now is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh*, etc.; but that word was Adam's, not God's: therefore it was instituted by man, not by God.

2. Likewise, if the Sacrament of matrimony is instituted by God, and in those things which are instituted by God the Church can change or arrange nothing[^6]: therefore neither concerning matrimony. But all day long it dispenses as regards this Sacrament: therefore, etc.

3. Likewise, it is not read that God spoke concerning matrimony, except when he said[^7]: *Increase and multiply*, etc.; and since this was also said to the beasts, it seems that he established it even among the beasts. But this is unfitting: therefore, etc.

4. Likewise, matrimony is contracted through human consent[^8]: therefore it is a human contract;
<!-- page 664 -->
but human contracts are instituted by men: therefore, etc.

> **Conclusion.** The Sacrament of Matrimony was instituted by God, Adam being inwardly illumined.

**I respond:** It must be said that the institution of matrimony is from God. — But it must be noted that certain Sacraments are purely of faith, such as baptism and confirmation and the like; but certain ones are not only of faith, but also of the natural dictate; and such are penance and matrimony, yet nevertheless they are not wholly of the dictate of natural law[^9]. Those which are of the first mode God instituted *by commanding*; those which are of the second mode he instituted *by inwardly illumining*; and thus he instituted matrimony. Hence he himself did not command Adam to take Eve as wife, but illumined him inwardly, that he might understand that she was to be given to him as wife, and that for all his descendants wives were to be joined in that manner through free consent. Hence Adam, thus illumined, said: *This now is bone*, etc., and, as the Master says in the text[^10], he spoke prophetically; and therefore that word was not from himself, but from God: and therefore the institution of this Sacrament was from God.

**To the arguments:**

*Ad 1.* And thus the first is evident.

*Ad 2.* To that which is objected, that then the Church can change nothing; it must be said that the Lord instituted the Sacrament as to form and as to matter, so far as befitted that time, excepting only father and mother[^11]; and because other persons according to diverse times had to accord with the institution, he left the legitimacy of persons to be dispensed by the Church; and concerning this the Church dispenses.

*Ad 3.* To that which is objected, that the Lord did not speak concerning matrimony except there: *Increase and multiply*; it must be said that this is false; nay rather, he spoke through Adam, when Adam, illumined by the Holy Spirit, spoke that word in the person of God; and this is evident, because in Matthew nineteen[^12] he repeats it as the word of God.

*Ad 4.* To that which is objected, that it is a human contract; it must be said that it is not *purely* human; and this is evident thus. For in matrimony there are two things: both the mutual *obligation* and the *indissolubility* of the mutual obligation; and although the first is *of man*, the second is *of the one instituting*; and by reason of that indissolubility matrimony especially holds the character of a Sacrament and of a sacred sign: and therefore it has its origin not from man, but from God; and this will appear better below[^13], when the indissolubility of matrimony is treated.
