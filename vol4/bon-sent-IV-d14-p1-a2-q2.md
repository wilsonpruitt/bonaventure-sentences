# IV Sent., d. 14, p. 1, a. 2, q. 2
## *Utrum poenitentia oriatur ex timore*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 326 -->

### Quaestio II. *Utrum poenitentia oriatur ex timore.*

Secundo quaeritur de origine poenitentiae; dicit Magister in littera, quod «poenitentia-virtus timore concipitur».

**Sed contra hoc est:** 1. Quia, si est virtus, est per infusionem[^1]: non ergo per conceptionem.

2. Item, cum conceptio non sit sine generatione, quare magis dicit *concipitur* quam *generatur*?

3. Item, quare magis *timore* quam *alio principio*? Et videtur, quod magis *amore*, quia timor causatur ab amore[^2]: ergo prima ratio generandi et concipiendi est in amore: ergo etc.

4. Item, tam timor quam amor est ex *fidei cognitione*[^3]: ergo deberet dicere, quod *fide* concipitur.

5. Item, si timore; aut *servili*, aut *initiali*, aut *filiali*. Non *servili*, quia cum illo manet voluntas peccandi; non *initiali* neque *filiali*, quia, antequam habeat timorem *filialem*, vel *initialem*, necesse est, quod de peccato poeniteat[^4].

<!-- page 327 -->

6. Item, timor est *donum*; sed poenitentia-virtus est ante dona naturali ordine[^5]: ergo non concipitur timore aliquo.

> **Conclusio.** Origo poenitentiae effectiva Deus est, sed dispositio quodam modo et ordine est a nobis.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod origo poenitentiae virtutis effectiva Deus est, sed dispositiva a nobis, quia «qui creavit nos sine nobis, non iustificat nos sine nobis»[^6]. — Dispositio autem hoc ordine procedit. Primo necesse est *Dei bonitatem et iustitiam* cognoscere — cui displicet omne malum et inordinatio, et ab illa offenditur et nullo modo malum relinquit impunitum — et *se aliquid fecisse*, quod suae displicere debeat bonitati; et haec est cognitio *culpae*, et ex hoc cognoscit se homo ex divina sententia obligatum ad poenam. — Necesse est etiam secundo cognoscere *Dei misericordiam*, qua paratus est omni revertenti et dolenti de culpa commissa sibi remittere et indulgere. — Ex prima cognitione generatur *timor*; ex secunda generatur *spes* remissionis; et ex his *voluntas* revertendi et confoederandi Deo et satisfaciendi per gemitum et alias poenas; et si incipiat facere quod in se est, dispositus est ad iustificationem[^7].

1. 2. Ex hoc igitur patet responsio, quomodo poenitentiae virtus concipitur per dispositionem suscipiendi[^8] eam; et quare magis dicitur *concipitur* quam *generatur*; quia *generare* est efficientis et principii effectivi, sed *concipere* receptivi.

3. 4. Ex hoc patet quare magis dicit[^9] *timore* quam *amore*, quia peccator non habet amorem Dei, sed sui; et iste amor nunquam generat poenitentiam, nisi quia timet; unde quamdiu *amat* et *credit*, non refugit peccatum, sed quando *timet*; et ideo non concipitur *fide*, quia ante timorem non est poenitentiae seminarium, quod est cessare a malo. Ex hoc etiam patet, quare magis *timore* quam *spe*, quia *timor* concipit, sed *spes* promovet.

5. Ex hoc patet, quod concipitur *timore servili*. Nec est inconveniens, quia conceptio ista non est per *productionem in esse*, sed per *dispositionem* et *praeparationem*; et ideo non oportet, quod poenitentia sit cum timore servili, sed sufficit, quod timor praeparet ad poenitentiam.

6. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod poenitentia est ante dona; dicendum, quod est *donum* Spiritus sancti, cum quo est Spiritus sanctus et datur; et hoc est gratiae gratum facientis, et de hoc verum est quod obiicit. Et est donum, quod est *sine Spiritus sancti donatione* proprie dicta, quae est per inhabitationem; et hoc est gratiae gratis datae, et tale donum est timor servilis, et hic antecedit virtutes; et tali timore concipitur poenitentia. Ideo non valet obiectio[^10].

## English

### Question II. *Whether penance arises from fear.*

Secondly it is asked concerning the origin of penance; the Master says in the text that «penance-as-virtue is conceived in fear».

**But on the contrary:** 1. Because, if it is a virtue, it is [had] through infusion[^1]: therefore not through conception.

2. Likewise, since conception is not without generation, why does he say *is conceived* rather than *is generated*?

3. Likewise, why rather by *fear* than by *another principle*? And it seems [it should rather be] by *love*, because fear is caused by love[^2]: therefore the first ground of generating and conceiving is in love: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, both fear and love are from the *knowledge of faith*[^3]: therefore he ought to say that it is conceived by *faith*.

5. Likewise, if [it is conceived] by fear; [it is] either by *servile*, or by *initial*, or by *filial* [fear]. Not by *servile*, because together with it the will to sin remains; not by *initial* nor by *filial*, because, before one has *filial* or *initial* fear, it is necessary that one repent of sin[^4].

<!-- page 327 -->

6. Likewise, fear is a *gift*; but penance-as-virtue is prior to the gifts in the natural order[^5]: therefore it is not conceived by any fear.

> **Conclusion.** The effective origin of penance is God, but the disposition is in a certain manner and order from us.

**I respond:** It must be said that the effective origin of the virtue of penance is God, but the dispositive [origin is] from us, because «He who created us without us does not justify us without us»[^6]. — But the disposition proceeds in this order. First it is necessary to know *the goodness and justice of God* — to whom every evil and disorder is displeasing, and by it He is offended, and in no way does He leave evil unpunished — and [to know] that *one has done something* which ought to be displeasing to His goodness; and this is the knowledge of *fault*, and from this a man knows himself to be bound to punishment by the divine sentence. — It is necessary, secondly, to know *the mercy of God*, by which He is ready to remit and pardon every one who returns and grieves over the fault committed. — From the first knowledge is generated *fear*; from the second is generated *hope* of remission; and from these [is generated] the *will* of returning and of being reconciled to God and of making satisfaction through groaning and other penalties; and if one begins to do what is in oneself, one is disposed for justification[^7].

1. 2. From this, therefore, the response is clear, how the virtue of penance is conceived through the disposition for receiving[^8] it; and why it is said rather to be *conceived* than *generated*; because *to generate* belongs to the efficient [cause] and to the effective principle, but *to conceive* to the receptive [one].

3. 4. From this it is clear why he says [it is conceived] rather by *fear* than by *love*[^9], because the sinner does not have the love of God, but of himself; and that love never generates penance, except because he fears; whence as long as he *loves* and *believes*, he does not flee from sin, but [only] when he *fears*; and therefore it is not conceived by *faith*, because before fear there is no seedbed of penance, which is to cease from evil. From this it is also clear why [it is conceived] rather by *fear* than by *hope*, because *fear* conceives, but *hope* advances.

5. From this it is clear that it is conceived by *servile fear*. Nor is this unfitting, because this conception is not through a *production into being*, but through *disposition* and *preparation*; and therefore it is not necessary that penance be [held] together with servile fear, but it suffices that the fear prepare [one] for penance.

6. To the objection that penance is prior to the gifts; it must be said that it is a *gift* of the Holy Spirit, together with whom the Holy Spirit is and is given; and this belongs to grace which makes pleasing [*gratia gratum faciens*], and concerning this what he objects is true. And it is a gift which is *without the giving of the Holy Spirit* properly so called, which is through indwelling; and this belongs to gratuitously given grace [*gratia gratis data*], and such a gift is servile fear, and this precedes the virtues; and by such fear penance is conceived. Therefore the objection does not hold[^10].
