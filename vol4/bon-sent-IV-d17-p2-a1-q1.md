# IV Sent., d. 17, p. 2, a. 1, q. 1

## *Utrum mandatum de confessione pertineat ad legem naturae*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 435 -->

### Articulus I. *De confessione quoad praeceptum instituentis.*

Iam secundum quaestionis articulum inspiciamus.

Ad intelligentiam huius partis quaeritur de confessione vocali. Et circa hoc duo principaliter quaeruntur. Primo quaeritur de confessione quantum ad praeceptum instituentis. Secundo, quantum ad obligationem exsequentis. Quantum ad primum quaeruntur tria.

Primo quaeritur, utrum mandatum de confessione pertineat ad legem naturae. Secundo, utrum pertineat ad legem scriptam, vel legem gratiae. Tertio quaeritur, a quo fuerit instituta, utrum a Christo, an ab aliquo eius Apostolo.

### Quaestio I. *Utrum mandatum de confessione pertineat ad legem naturae.*

Circa primum, quod in lege naturae fuerit instituta, videtur.

1. Super illud Genesis tertio[^1]: *Adam, ubi es?* pro Glossa: « Verba increpantis sunt et ad confessionem cogentis, non ignorantis »; sed Adam non tenebatur nisi ad legem naturae: ergo confessio est de iure naturali, confessio scilicet vocalis, quia vocaliter ei Dominus loquebatur, et ille vocaliter respondebat.

2. Item, Iob trigesimo primo[^2]: *Si abscondi quasi homo peccatum meum et celavi iniquitatem meam in sinu meo*, quasi dicat: non: ergo peccatum erat abscondere peccatum, et ut peccatum reprobabat Iob; sed ipse non tenebatur nisi ad ius naturale: ergo ius naturale prohibet absconsionem peccati: ergo praecipit vel suadet confessionem.

3. Item, Gregorius ibidem[^3] in Glossa: « Vere humilis et mala sua cognoscit et voce confessionis aperit »; sed humilitas de peccatis commissis est de iure naturali: ergo et vox confessionis peccati.

4. Item, ratione videtur: quia duplex est confessio, scilicet confessio laudis et confessio peccati, et utraque est in gloriam Dei; sicut patet, quia dixit Iosue ad Achor: *Confitere peccatum et da gloriam Deo*, Iosue septimo[^4]; sed natura dictat Deum glorificandum et dictat Deum laudandum non tantum corde, sed etiam ore: ergo pari ratione dictat peccatum utroque modo confitendum.

<!-- page 436 -->
5. Item, poenitentia tres habet partes, scilicet contritionem, confessionem et satisfactionem; sed contritio et satisfactio sunt de lege naturali: ergo pari ratione et confessio.

6. Item, omne opus virtutis spectat ad dictamen naturae; sed confessio peccati est opus virtutis: ergo etc. Probatio *minoris*: « Veritas, ut vult Philosophus[^5], est virtus », et veritas est illa quae habilitat ad dicendum verum; sed maxima difficultas est in enarrando suum peccatum vere: ergo maxime circa hunc actum consistit virtus: ergo etc.

**Contra:** 1. « Ius naturale, ut dicit Isidorus[^6], est idem apud omnes »; sed confessio peccati non est eadem apud omnes: ergo confessio peccati non est de iure naturali.

2. Item, videtur, quod sit *contra* naturam: quia naturale est cuilibet et de natura recta celare *amici commissum*, sicut dicitur Proverbiorum undecimo[^7]; sed magis se debet quisque diligere quam proximum ex dictamine naturae: ergo natura dictat, peccata propria esse celanda.

3. Item, naturale est cuilibet celare verenda corporis: ergo similiter verenda spiritus; sed peccata sunt turpitudines animae: ergo peccatum confiteri potius est contra naturam quam secundum naturam.

4. Item, recta ratio, quae est naturae conformis, erubescit turpia *cogitare*: ergo si plus est *referre* sive *dicere*[^8], per naturam erubescit dicere: ergo cum *cogitare* turpia et *facere* sit contra naturam et rationem rectam, pari ratione et *referre*.

5. Item, per naturam recordatio iniuriarum est occasio inimicitiae confirmandae; sed peccator per peccatum est iniuriatus Deo: ergo recordando peccatum et narrando[^9] renovat inimicitias. Sed quod facit hominem inimicum Deo vel magis inimicum est contra naturam et rationem rectam: ergo et confessio sceleris.

> **Conclusio.** Vocaliter confiteri Deo non dictat natura specialiter et per modum necessitatis nisi in casu; sed homini confiteri dictat tantum in generali.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod dupliciter sunt aliqua de dictamine iuris naturalis: aut *in generali*, aut *in speciali*. In speciali, ut honorare parentes; in generali vero dictat natura ea quae superaddit lex evangelica, sicut sunt aliqua supererogationis ut consilia, vel etiam aliqua Sacramenta[^10].

Intelligendum est igitur, quod est confessio duplex peccati mentalis et vocalis. Mentalis confessio est recognitio peccati et offensae; et haec est de dictamine naturae *in speciali*, secundum quod *cognitio offensae* est de dictamine naturae. Cognitio autem *offensae humanae* est de dictamine naturae per se, secundum quod est rationalis; sed cognitio *offensae divinae* est de dictamine naturae adiutae aliquo modo per fidem. Quomodo enim scit homo, se *offendere Deum, quem non videt*[^11], nisi per aliquem radium fidei hoc cognosceret? Cum autem hoc cognoscit, quod *Deum per praevaricationem inhonorat*; natura dictat, quod ab ipso est misericordia humiliter petenda; et hoc nunquam est, nisi recognoscatur culpa.

*Vocalis* vero confessio est duplex, scilicet quae fit *Deo*, et quae fit *homini*. Vocaliter *confiteri Deo* dictat natura non *simpliciter*, sed *in casu*, ut puta, quando Deus exigit, sicut exegit ab Adam et a Cain[^12]; vocaliter autem *confiteri homini* non dictat natura nisi *in generali*. Dictat enim, quod omnibus modis reconciliari oportet Deo, quibus ipse statuit. Unde natura dictabat, quodsi Deus arbitrum constitueret inter se et peccatorem, quod ipsi esset culpa revelanda; et ita dictabat *in generali*; dictabat etiam, quod illi essent dicenda vera.

Et sic patet, quomodo confessio spectat ad legem naturae. Quaedam enim est de dictamine naturae per modum *necessitatis*, quia natura dictat illam *simpliciter* et *specialiter*. Quaedam etiam est de dictamine naturae per modum *expedientis simpliciter*, sed *necessitatis in casu*, ut confessio vocalis facta Deo. Quaedam vero dictabat *in universali solum*, et haec est *confessio facta homini*.

1. 2. 3. Ex his patent obiecta ad primam partem; nam auctoritates procedunt secundum primam viam, vel secundam, ut patet.

4. Similiter prima ratio inducta ex simili in confessione vocali[^13] laudis, quia, sicut in casu laudare oportet ore, tamen semper debet actu vel habitu laudare *in corde*; sic etiam intelligendum de confessione peccati.

5. Ad illud quod obiicitur de partibus aliis, scilicet contritione et satisfactione; dicendum, quod <!-- page 437 -->istae tres partes respiciunt poenitentiam, secundum quod est *virtus* et secundum quod est *Sacramentum*; sed specialiter *contritio* et *satisfactio* respiciunt *virtutem*, sed *confessio* magis est poenitentiae, ut est *Sacramentum*, sicut melius patebit infra[^14]. Confessio enim peccati, quae fit homini, ortum habet non tantum a *natura*, sed etiam ab *institutione divina*.

6. Et ex hoc patet responsio ad ultimum, quod dicit, quod est opus virtutis. Dico enim, quod re vera opus *virtutis* est, sed non *omnino*, sed virtutis cum adiunctione institutionis Sacramenti. *Veritas* enim non dictat, quod peccata occulta dicantur homini; similiter nec *poenitentia* dictat illud per se, quantumcumque homo doleat; sed cum Deus instituit, tunc *virtus obedientiae* imperavit, *poenitentia* acceleravit, et *veritas* actum direxit. — Et ita patet, ad quam virtutem spectet confiteri, et quomodo sit opus virtutis et cuius. Unde *poenitentia* in generali dictat illam, secundum quod est virtus volens facere emendam; *obedientia* per mandatum specificat; *veritas* elicit et regulat.

1. Ad illud quod obiicitur in oppositum, quod non est idem apud omnes; dicendum, quod illud intelligitur de eo quod natura dictat *in particulari*, non *in universali*.

2. 3. 4. 5. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod manifestare peccatum est contra rationem; dicendum, quod peccati propalatio potest fieri dupliciter: vel ad sui ipsius commendationem, sicut faciunt peccatores, qui laetantur, cum male fecerint, et exsultant in rebus pessimis, quorum *viae perversae* etc., quibus est facta *frons mulieris meretricis*, et de quibus dicitur: *Peccatum suum sicut Sodoma praedicaverunt* etc.; et haec est contra naturam et contra rationem rectam et ad provocandam divinam inimicitiam; et verecundia et confusio huic opposita est *adducens gratiam et gloriam*. — Alia est confessio ad sui accusationem ad inveniendam divinam propitiationem; et haec est ordinata ad faciendam emendam et ad curandam plagam occultam. Unde sicut natura dictat, vulnus etiam in locis secretis aperiendum medico ad curationem, quamvis dictet pudenda operienda; sic est de peccatis. Et sicut, si aliquis nollet vulnus ostendere, iudicaretur superbus et stultus et faceret contra naturam et salutem suam; sic intelligendum est in proposito.

Et ex hoc patet responsio ad omnes rationes. Nam si vulnus *amici* deberet curari per meam confessionem, sicut et *proprium*; dictaret ratio et natura, quod ego deberem ipsum prodere. Quamvis autem naturae dictamini consonum sit erubescere, et nunquam deberet esse[^14] sine confusione; propter illam tamen non est confessio dimittenda; et hoc propter multiplicem utilitatem, quam habet. Et propter hanc instituit eam Deus.

Valet enim ad *peccati cognitionem*, propter instructionem sacerdotis; Deuteronomii decimo septimo: *Si difficile et ambiguum apud te* etc. — Ad *cogniti deletionem*; Psalmo: *Dixi, confitebor* etc.; si propositum valet, quanto magis valet opus? — Ad *cogniti et deleti satisfactionem*, quia erubescentia in confessione est magna pars satisfactionis; Ecclesiastici quarto: *Est confusio adducens gratiam et gloriam*. — Ad *poenae diminutionem* vi clavium; Matthaei decimosexto: *Quodcumque solveris super terram* etc. — Ad *Dei laudem*; Iosue septimo: *Da gloriam Deo et confitere* etc., et: *Indica mihi, quid feceris*. — Ad *intercessorum multiplicationem*; Iacobi ultimo: *Confitemini alterutrum peccata vestra, et orate pro invicem* etc. — Ad *conscientiae assecurationem*; unde David statim, cum dixit: *Peccavi*, audivit: *Transtulit Dominus* etc., secundi Regum duodecimo. Et hoc magnum bonum est, secundae ad Corinthios primo: *Gloria nostra haec est, testimonium conscientiae nostrae*.

### Scholion

**I.** Circa 1. et 2. quaestionem doctores satis consentiunt; S. Bonav. tamen aliis distinctionibus et attentione dignis utitur. De utraque quaestione: Alex. Hal., S. p. IV. q. 18. m. 2. a. 2. et a. 3. § 1. 2. — Scot., de his et de 3. q. in utroque Scripto d. 17. q. unica. — S. Thom., hic q. 3. a. 1. quaestiunc. 2. 3. Suppl. q. 6. a. 1. 2. — B. Albert., de hac et seq. q. <!-- page 438 -->supra d. 16. et 17. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 3. quaestiunc. 2. 3. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 2. q. 3. — De his et de 3. q. Durand., hic q. 8, et Dionys. Carth., hic q. 5.

**II.** Quoad 3. quaestionem nonnulli, cortici verborum inhaerentes, tribuerunt S. Bonav. doctrinam, quod Christus ipse per se et iure divino non instituerit confessionem, et per consequens nec Sacramentum poenitentiae. Falsa haec interpretatio inde orta est, quod adversarii primo non attenderunt verum scopum huius quaestionis, qui in fine argumentorum praeciso ponitur; unde patet, quod S. Doctor totus est in quaestione levioris momenti discutienda, scilicet quare in s. Scriptura Christus ipsam confessionis obligationem, sive partem materialem huius Sacramenti non sic disertis verbis docuerit, sicut formale eiusdem, scilicet potestatem clavium cum potestate absolvendi, quam immediate et instituit et ipse promulgavit (hic et a. 2. q. 2, p. III. a. 1. q. 1, d. 18. p. 1. a. 1. q. 1. 2.). — Secundo iidem non attenderunt usum verborum instituendi et insinuandi apud S. Bonaventuram occurrentem, licet ex ipso contextu satis clare se prodat. Nam hoc saltem loco sumitur instituere secundum usum, quem iuris adagion adhibet, quod « leges tunc instituuntur quando promulgantur » (cfr. supra d. 7. a. 1. q. 1. scholion n. III.). Item insinuare non excludit manifestationem voluntatis omni modo, sed tantum publicam sive promulgationem formalem. Ita S. Doctor se ipsum explicat infra d. 22. a. 2. q. 1. in corp., ubi dicit, quod « primum [potestatem clavium] Dominus instituit per se et promulgavit... Secundum vero [confessio] institutum fuit per Apostolos, vel melius dicam, promulgatum, quia Dominus Apostolos docuit, et ipsi a Domino docti et auctoritate ab ipso accepta promulgarunt. Non enim nobis tradiderunt nisi quod a Domino vel a Domini Spiritu acceperunt ». — Hic modus loquendi invenitur non tantum apud Alexandrum Hal. (loc. cit. m. 3. a. 2; et cfr. a. 1.) dicentem: « Non instituit Dominus lucide, sed insinuavit »; sed etiam apud Petrum a Tar. (loc. cit. quaestiunc. 4.), qui ait: « In hoc, quod eis potestatem suam iudiciariam ligandi et solvendi dedit, confessionem eis tanquam iudicibus fieri insinuavit; ergo instituit confessionem tacite, sed Apostoli promulgarunt expresse ». Similiter argumentatur Richard. a Med. (loc. cit. q. 1.).

Quaestionem hanc accessoriam, quare Christus ipse in s. Scriptura non promulgaverit debitum confessionis, alii auctores praeter Alexandrum Hal. et Richard. a Med. (hic a. 2. q. 2.) vix tangunt. Ceterum non constat, Christum Apostolis nihil de hac re explicite ore proprio tradidisse, cum multa eius dicta, ut saepius monet etiam S. Bonav., in s. Litteris non sint scripta; quod etiam loc. cit. idem insinuat.

## English

### Article I. *On confession with respect to the precept of the one instituting it.*

Now let us examine the second article of the question.

For the understanding of this part there is an inquiry concerning vocal confession. And concerning this two things are principally asked. First, there is an inquiry concerning confession with respect to the precept of the one instituting it. Second, with respect to the obligation of the one carrying it out. With respect to the first, three things are asked.

First, it is asked whether the precept of confession belongs to the natural law. Second, whether it belongs to the written law, or to the law of grace. Third, it is asked by whom it was instituted, whether by Christ, or by some Apostle of his.

### Question I. *Whether the precept of confession belongs to the natural law.*

Concerning the first — that it was instituted in the natural law — it seems so.

1. On that text of Genesis 3[^1]: *Adam, where are you?* the Gloss says: « These are the words of one rebuking and compelling to confession, not of one ignorant »; but Adam was bound only to the law of nature: therefore confession is of the natural law — namely vocal confession, because the Lord spoke to him vocally, and he answered vocally.

2. Likewise, Job 31[^2]: *If I have hidden my sin as a man, and have concealed my iniquity in my bosom* — as if to say: I have not — therefore to hide a sin was a sin, and Job reprobated it as a sin; but he himself was bound only to the natural law: therefore the natural law forbids the hiding of sin: therefore it commands or counsels confession.

3. Likewise, Gregory on the same passage[^3] in the Gloss: « The truly humble person both acknowledges his evils and discloses them by the voice of confession »; but humility about sins committed is of the natural law: therefore so too is the voice of confession of sin.

4. Likewise, it seems so by reason: because confession is twofold, namely confession of praise and confession of sin, and each is unto the glory of God; as is clear, because Joshua said to Achor: *Confess the sin and give glory to God*, Joshua 7[^4]; but nature dictates that God is to be glorified and dictates that God is to be praised not only in the heart but also with the mouth: therefore by parity of reasoning it dictates that sin is to be confessed in both ways.

<!-- page 436 -->
5. Likewise, penance has three parts, namely contrition, confession, and satisfaction; but contrition and satisfaction are of the natural law: therefore by parity of reasoning so too is confession.

6. Likewise, every work of virtue pertains to the dictate of nature; but confession of sin is a work of virtue: therefore etc. Proof of the *minor*: « Truth, as the Philosopher holds[^5], is a virtue », and truth is that which fits one for speaking the truth; but the greatest difficulty lies in narrating one's own sin truthfully: therefore virtue consists especially about this act: therefore etc.

**On the contrary:** 1. « The natural law, as Isidore says[^6], is the same among all »; but confession of sin is not the same among all: therefore confession of sin is not of the natural law.

2. Likewise, it seems that it is *against* nature: because it is natural to anyone, and of right nature, to conceal *a friend's misdeed*, as is said in Proverbs 11[^7]; but each one ought to love himself more than his neighbor, by the dictate of nature: therefore nature dictates that one's own sins are to be concealed.

3. Likewise, it is natural to anyone to conceal the shameful parts of the body: therefore likewise the shameful parts of the spirit; but sins are the disgraces of the soul: therefore to confess sin is rather against nature than according to nature.

4. Likewise, right reason, which is conformed to nature, blushes to *think* base things: therefore, if to *report* or to *say* them is more[^8], it blushes by nature to say them: therefore, since to *think* base things and to *do* them is against nature and right reason, by parity of reasoning so too is to *report* them.

5. Likewise, by nature the recollection of injuries is an occasion for confirming enmity; but the sinner by sin has injured God: therefore by recollecting the sin and narrating it[^9] he renews enmities. But what makes a man an enemy of God, or more an enemy, is against nature and right reason: therefore so too is the confession of a crime.

> **Conclusion.** That one should confess to God vocally, nature does not dictate specially and by way of necessity except in a particular case; but that one should confess to a man, it dictates only in general.

**I respond:** It must be said that things are of the dictate of the natural law in two ways: either *in general*, or *in particular*. In particular, as to honor one's parents; but in general nature dictates those things which the evangelical law superadds, such as certain works of supererogation as counsels, or even certain Sacraments[^10].

It must be understood, therefore, that confession of sin is twofold, mental and vocal. Mental confession is the recognition of sin and offense; and this is of the dictate of nature *in particular*, insofar as the *knowledge of the offense* is of the dictate of nature. Now the knowledge of a *human offense* is of the dictate of nature in itself, insofar as it is rational; but the knowledge of a *divine offense* is of the dictate of nature aided in some way by faith. For how does a man know that he *offends God, whom he does not see*[^11], unless he knew this by some ray of faith? But when he knows this — that *he dishonors God by transgression* — nature dictates that mercy is to be humbly sought from him; and this never happens unless the fault be recognized.

But *vocal* confession is twofold, namely that which is made *to God*, and that which is made *to a man*. That one should *confess to God* vocally, nature dictates not *absolutely*, but *in a particular case*, as for instance when God demands it, as he demanded it of Adam and of Cain[^12]; but that one should *confess to a man* vocally, nature dictates only *in general*. For it dictates that one ought to be reconciled to God in every way by which he himself has appointed. Hence nature dictated that, if God should appoint an arbiter between himself and the sinner, the fault would have to be revealed to him; and thus it dictated *in general*; it also dictated that true things were to be told to him.

And thus it is clear how confession belongs to the law of nature. For some confession is of the dictate of nature by way of *necessity*, because nature dictates it *absolutely* and *specially*. Some also is of the dictate of nature by way of being *absolutely expedient*, but *necessary in a particular case*, as vocal confession made to God. But some it dictated *in general only*, and this is *confession made to a man*.

1. 2. 3. From these things the objections to the first part are clear; for the authorities proceed according to the first way, or the second, as is evident.

4. Likewise the first reasoning, drawn from the similar case of vocal confession[^13] of praise: because, just as in a particular case one must praise with the mouth, yet one ought always to praise in act or in habit *in the heart*; so too it is to be understood of the confession of sin.

5. To that which is objected concerning the other parts, namely contrition and satisfaction; it must be said that <!-- page 437 -->these three parts regard penance, insofar as it is a *virtue* and insofar as it is a *Sacrament*; but *contrition* and *satisfaction* regard the *virtue* specially, whereas *confession* belongs rather to penance as it is a *Sacrament*, as will better appear below[^14]. For confession of sin, which is made to a man, takes its origin not only from *nature*, but also from *divine institution*.

6. And from this the answer to the last [objection] is clear, which says that it is a work of virtue. For I say that it is indeed a work of *virtue*, but not *wholly*, but a work of virtue with the addition of the institution of the Sacrament. For *truth* does not dictate that hidden sins be told to a man; likewise neither does *penance* dictate that of itself, however much a man may grieve; but when God instituted it, then the *virtue of obedience* commanded it, *penance* hastened it, and *truth* directed the act. — And thus it is clear to which virtue confessing belongs, and how and of what [virtue] it is a work. Hence *penance* in general dictates it, insofar as it is a virtue willing to make amends; *obedience* by the precept specifies it; *truth* elicits and regulates it.

1. To that which is objected to the contrary — that it is not the same among all — it must be said that this is understood of what nature dictates *in particular*, not *in universal*.

2. 3. 4. 5. To that which is objected — that to manifest a sin is against reason — it must be said that the publishing of a sin can be done in two ways: either to one's own commendation, as sinners do who rejoice when they have done evil and exult in the worst things, *whose ways are perverse* etc., who have made themselves *the face of a harlot woman*, and of whom it is said: *They have proclaimed their sin like Sodom* etc.; and this is against nature and against right reason and tends to provoke divine enmity; and the bashfulness and shame opposed to it is *bringing grace and glory*. — The other is confession unto one's own accusation, to find divine propitiation; and this is ordered to making amends and to healing the hidden wound. Hence, just as nature dictates that a wound, even in secret places, is to be opened to a physician for healing, although it dictates that the private parts are to be covered; so it is with sins. And just as, if someone were unwilling to show his wound, he would be judged proud and foolish and would act against nature and his own welfare; so it is to be understood in the matter at hand.

And from this the answer to all the reasonings is clear. For if a *friend's* wound had to be cured through my confession, as also one's *own*; reason and nature would dictate that I ought to betray him. But although it is consonant with the dictate of nature to blush, and it ought never to be without shame; nevertheless confession is not on that account to be omitted; and this on account of the manifold usefulness which it has. And on account of this God instituted it.

For it avails unto the *knowledge of sin*, on account of the instruction of the priest; Deuteronomy 17: *If you perceive a matter difficult and ambiguous* etc. — Unto the *blotting out of what is known*; the Psalm: *I said, I will confess* etc.; if the resolve avails, how much more does the deed avail? — Unto the *satisfaction for what is known and blotted out*, because the bashfulness in confession is a great part of satisfaction; Ecclesiasticus 4: *There is a shame that brings grace and glory*. — Unto the *diminution of punishment* by the power of the keys; Matthew 16: *Whatsoever you shall loose upon earth* etc. — Unto the *praise of God*; Joshua 7: *Give glory to God and confess* etc., and: *Tell me what you have done*. — Unto the *multiplication of intercessors*; James, last chapter: *Confess your sins one to another, and pray for one another* etc. — Unto the *assurance of conscience*; whence David, immediately when he said: *I have sinned*, heard: *The Lord has taken away* etc., 2 Kings 12. And this is a great good, 2 Corinthians 1: *Our glory is this, the testimony of our conscience*.

### Scholion

**I.** Concerning the 1st and 2nd questions the doctors agree well enough; St. Bonaventure, however, employs other distinctions also worthy of attention. On both questions: Alexander of Hales, *Summa* p. IV q. 18 m. 2 a. 2 and a. 3 §§ 1, 2. — Scotus, on these and on the 3rd question, in both *Scripta*, d. 17, q. unica. — St. Thomas, here q. 3 a. 1 quaestiunc. 2, 3; *Supplement* q. 6 a. 1, 2. — Blessed Albert, on this and the following question <!-- page 438 -->above, d. 16 and 17. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 3 quaestiunc. 2, 3. — Richard of Mediavilla, here a. 2 q. 3. — On these and on the 3rd question Durandus, here q. 8, and Dionysius the Carthusian, here q. 5.

**II.** As to the 3rd question, some, clinging to the bark of the words, have attributed to St. Bonaventure the doctrine that Christ himself did not by himself and by divine right institute confession, and consequently neither the Sacrament of penance. This false interpretation arose from the fact that the adversaries, first, did not attend to the true scope of this question, which is set down precisely at the end of the arguments; whence it is clear that St. Bonaventure is wholly engaged in discussing a question of lighter moment, namely why in holy Scripture Christ did not teach in such express words that very obligation of confession, or the material part of this Sacrament, as he did its formal part, namely the power of the keys together with the power of absolving, which he immediately both instituted and himself promulgated (here and a. 2 q. 2; p. III a. 1 q. 1; d. 18 p. 1 a. 1 q. 1, 2). — Secondly, the same persons did not attend to the usage of the words *to institute* and *to insinuate* occurring in St. Bonaventure, although it sufficiently clearly betrays itself from the context. For in this place at least *to institute* is taken according to the usage which the legal adage employs, that « laws are then instituted when they are promulgated » (cf. above d. 7 a. 1 q. 1 scholion n. III). Likewise *to insinuate* does not exclude the manifestation of the will in every manner, but only the public or formal promulgation. Thus St. Bonaventure explains himself below, d. 22 a. 2 q. 1 in the body, where he says that « the first [the power of the keys] the Lord instituted by himself and promulgated... but the second [confession] was instituted by the Apostles, or, to put it better, promulgated, because the Lord taught the Apostles, and they, taught by the Lord and by the authority received from him, promulgated it. For they handed down to us nothing except what they received from the Lord or from the Lord's Spirit. » — This manner of speaking is found not only in Alexander of Hales (loc. cit. m. 3 a. 2; and cf. a. 1) who says: « The Lord did not institute it openly, but insinuated it »; but also in Peter of Tarentaise (loc. cit. quaestiunc. 4), who says: « In this, that he gave them his judiciary power of binding and loosing, he insinuated that confession was to be made to them as to judges; therefore he instituted confession tacitly, but the Apostles promulgated it expressly ». Richard of Mediavilla argues similarly (loc. cit. q. 1).

This accessory question — why Christ himself did not in holy Scripture promulgate the obligation of confession — other authors besides Alexander of Hales and Richard of Mediavilla (here a. 2 q. 2) scarcely touch. Moreover it is not established that Christ handed nothing on this matter explicitly to the Apostles by his own mouth, since many of his sayings, as St. Bonaventure also frequently notes, are not written in holy Scripture; which he also insinuates in the place cited.
