### Quaestio II. *Utrum corpus glorificatum sit palpabile a tactu non glorificato.*

> The numbered footnotes below correspond to markers in both the Latin body and the English translation. Quaracchi restarts footnote numbering on each printed page; this chunk carries notes 1–11 of page 1030, labelled with a page prefix.

Secundo quaeritur de subtilitate per comparationem ad actum nostrum; et quaeritur, utrum corpus glorificatum sit palpabile a tactu non glorificato. Et quod sic, videtur.

1. Lucae ultimo[^p1030-1]: *Palpate et videte, quia spiritus carnem et ossa non habet.*

2. Item, Gregorius in homilia[^p1030-2]: « Palpabile fuit per veritatem naturae et subtile per effectum spiritualis potentiae ».

3. Item, *ratione* videtur: quia in corpore glorioso erit veritas carnis et ossis; sed caro est mollis, et os durum; sed durum et molle sunt obiecta tactus[^p1030-3]: ergo etc.

4. Item, corpus gloriosum potest immutare visum[^p1030-4]: ergo eadem ratione potest immutare tactum.

**Contra:** 1. Gregorius[^p1030-5]: « Corrumpi necesse est quod palpatur »; sed gloriosum corpus est incorruptibile: ergo et impalpabile.

2. Item, Ioannes Chrysostomus in quadam homilia[^p1030-6]: « Dignum est quaerere, qualiter corpus incorruptibile tangibile fuit mortali manu; sed ne tumultueris; condescensionis enim fuit ».

3. Item, *ratione* videtur: quia omne quod sentitur tactu, sentitur aut per *resistentiam*, aut *qualitatem activam*, sicut sentitur aer frigidus; sed corpus gloriosum non potest sentiri per *resistentiam*, quia nihil ei resistit; non per *qualitatem activam*, quia nulla talis immutatio ibi remanet: ergo nullo modo.

4. Item, tactus maxime est materialis; corpus autem illud spirituale[^p1030-7]: ergo etc.

### Conclusio

> **Conclusio.** *Corpus gloriosum potest se praebere tangibile et etiam non se praebere sive latere.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod cum dico *tangibile*, duo dico, scilicet quod potest *immutare sensum*, et hoc est perfectionis; et quod *non potest latere tactum nostrum*, et hoc est imperfectionis. Et quia remanebit quod est perfectionis, et tolletur quod est imperfectionis[^p1030-8]; ideo dico, quod corpus gloriosum potest se praebere tangibile et potest se non praebere sive latere; et hoc habet secundum diversas conditiones. Nam *tangi* potest, quia potest impellenti resistere, cum sit corpus verum, non vanum vel phantasticum; *latere* potest, quia potest penetrare organum tactus. *Primum* habet per virtutem naturae, *secundum* per effectum spiritualis potentiae. Et haec duo contraria sunt in nobis, sed non in illo corpore. — Concedendae ergo sunt rationes, quod tangi potest.

**Ad argumenta pro parte opposita:**

*Ad 1, 2.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod palpatur est corruptibile; dicendum, quod hoc verum est de eo quod est tactui subiectum, sed istud non erat[^p1030-9]; et ideo condescensionis erat.

*Ad 3.* Ad illud quod obiicitur de resistentia, dico, quod corpus tangens non resistit tangendo, sed magis e converso; et licet nihil possit corpori glorioso resistere, ipsum tamen potest aliis; sicut patet, quia constat, quod potest sustollere[^p1030-10] lapidem in manu.

*Ad 4.* Ad ultimum vero patet responsio: quia corpus non efficitur ita spirituale, quin salvetur in eo veritas carnis et ossis[^p1030-11].

## English

### Question II. *Whether the glorified body is palpable to a touch that is not glorified.*

Secondly it is asked about subtlety by comparison with our act; and it is asked whether the glorified body is palpable to a touch that is not glorified. And that it is, seems so.

1. Luke, last chapter[^p1030-1]: *Handle and see, for a spirit hath not flesh and bones.*

2. Likewise, Gregory in a homily[^p1030-2]: "It was palpable through the truth of its nature and subtle through the effect of spiritual power."

3. Likewise, it seems so *by reason*: because in the glorious body there will be the truth of flesh and bone; but flesh is soft, and bone hard; but hard and soft are objects of touch[^p1030-3]: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, a glorious body can alter sight[^p1030-4]: therefore by the same account it can alter touch.

**On the contrary:** 1. Gregory[^p1030-5]: "What is handled must necessarily be corrupted"; but a glorious body is incorruptible: therefore it is also impalpable.

2. Likewise, John Chrysostom in a certain homily[^p1030-6]: "It is worth asking how an incorruptible body was tangible to a mortal hand; but do not be troubled; it was a matter of condescension."

3. Likewise, it seems so *by reason*: because everything that is perceived by touch is perceived either through *resistance* or through an *active quality*, as cold air is perceived; but a glorious body cannot be perceived through *resistance*, because nothing resists it; nor through an *active quality*, because no such alteration remains there: therefore in no way.

4. Likewise, touch is most of all material; but that body is spiritual[^p1030-7]: therefore etc.

### Conclusion

> **Conclusion.** *A glorious body can present itself as tangible and can also not present itself, that is, lie hidden.*

**I respond:** It must be said that when I say *tangible*, I say two things, namely that it can *alter sense*, and this belongs to perfection; and that it *cannot lie hidden from our touch*, and this belongs to imperfection. And because what belongs to perfection will remain, and what belongs to imperfection will be taken away[^p1030-8]; therefore I say that the glorious body can present itself as tangible and can not present itself, that is, lie hidden; and it has this according to diverse conditions. For it *can be touched*, because it can resist one who pushes against it, since it is a true body, not empty or phantastic; it *can lie hidden*, because it can penetrate the organ of touch. The *first* it has through the power of nature, the *second* through the effect of spiritual power. And these two are contrary in us, but not in that body. — Therefore the reasons are to be conceded, that it can be touched.

**To the arguments on the opposite side:**

*To 1 and 2.* To that which is objected, that what is handled is corruptible; it must be said that this is true of what is subject to touch, but that body was not[^p1030-9]; and therefore it was a matter of condescension.

*To 3.* To that which is objected concerning resistance, I say that the touching body does not resist by touching, but rather the reverse; and although nothing can resist a glorious body, that body nevertheless can resist others; as is plain, because it is agreed that it can lift up[^p1030-10] a stone in its hand.

*To 4.* To the last the answer is plain: because the body is not made so spiritual that the truth of flesh and bone is not preserved in it[^p1030-11].
