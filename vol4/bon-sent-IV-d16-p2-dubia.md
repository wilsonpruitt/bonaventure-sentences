# IV Sent., d. 16, p. II — Dubia circa litteram Magistri
## *Dubia circa litteram Magistri*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 411 -->

### DUBIA CIRCA LITTERAM MAGISTRI.

### Dub. I

In parte ista sunt dubitationes circa litteram, et primo quaeritur de ista divisione, quam ponit Magister in littera, quod tribus modis agitur poenitentia, utrum sit *poenitentiae-virtutis,* an *poenitentiae-Sacramenti.* Et videtur, quod non virtutis, quia haec simplex est et species specialissima[^1]. — Item, quod nec Sacramenti: quia, multiplicato superiori, multiplicatur inferius[^2]: ergo secundum hoc videtur, quod poenitentia secundum tres differentias sit triplex Sacramentum. *Praeterea,* «omne Sacramentum, ut dicit Ambrosius, simplex est».

<!-- page 412 -->

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod poenitentia, quae est ante baptismum, est *poenitentia-virtus,* non *poenitentia-Sacramentum;* et poenitentia, quae est post baptismum, est *poenitentia-Sacramentum;* et ideo poenitentia in hac divisione accipitur communiter ad *virtutem* et *Sacramentum;* nec est dictum aequivoce, sed analogice per reductionem ad unam naturam[^3]. — Ulterius notandum, quod partes istae non sunt diversificantes poenitentiam *essentialiter,* sed *secundum statum;* et ideo non est divisio in partes *essentiales,* nec oportet ex hoc Sacramentum multiplicari nec esse compositum. Et sic patet illud[^4].

### Dub. II

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *poenitentia fit pro peccatis, quae Legis decalogus continet.* Videtur enim non bene dicere, quia in decalogo non prohibetur superbia nec hypocrisis nec consimilia: ergo pro his non videtur esse poenitendum.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod sicut in ordine praedicamenti quaedam sunt in genere *per se,* quaedam sunt in genere *per reductionem,* sicut principia et etiam privationes[^5]; sic intelligendum est in articulis fidei. Quaedam enim sunt antecedentia, quaedam consequentia, et quaedam sunt directe credibilia, sicut articuli; et in omnibus his iudicatur quis haereticus, si perverse sentiat. Per hunc modum intelligendum est in praeceptis decalogi, quod quaedam sunt ibi prohibita *principaliter* et *explicite,* quaedam ut *annexa* et *implicite:* ut verbi gratia, si dicatur: *Non moechaberis*[^6], principaliter prohibetur et explicite actus adulterii, sed implicite et *in generali* omnis actus coitus ut *antecedens;* et sic prohibetur concupiscentia coitus sive aspectus mulieris ad moechandum; consequenter vero prohibetur omnis effusio seminis inordinata. Sic intelligendum in aliis, quod in primo praecepto praecipitur cultus Dei; et in hoc praecipitur actus fidei antecedenter, et consequenter omnis elatio et superbia prohibetur; et sic in aliis[^7].

### Dub. III

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Cum dicimus: Dimitte nobis debita nostra* etc.; manifestamus, nos habere quod nobis dimittatur. Esto ergo, quod aliquis non habeat conscientiam peccati in aliquo instanti, utpote quando Missam cantat, et est bene confessus et intentus caelestibus; tunc videtur, quod talis dicendo istam orationem peccet mortaliter, quia facit contra conscientiam.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod omnis homo potest rationabiliter habere conscientiam de peccato veniali, tum propter frequentiam, tum propter ignorantiam, quia in multis ignoranter delinquimus, etiam cum credimus bene facere; tum propter adhaerentiam, quia amor rei temporalis et affectiones istae inordinatae frequenter multum nobis adhaerent. Et ideo quilibet potest dicere: *Ab occultis meis munda me, Domine*[^8] etc. — Vel potest dici, quod adhuc potest dicere in persona aliorum; et ideo non peccat, sed pro aliis impetrat pro illo tempore[^9], vel petit pro se ipso pro statu alio.

### Dub. IV

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Delet omnino haec oratio minima et quotidiana* etc. Videtur enim ex hoc dicere Augustinus[^10], quod oratio dominicalis deleat peccata. *Sed contra:* solius gratiae est delere peccata: ergo etc. — Si tu dicas, quantum ad poenam; *contra:* non est par poena venialis et mortalis; sed ista est satisfactio pro venialibus: non ergo pro mortalibus.

<!-- page 413 -->

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod ipse loquitur de deletione quantum ad poenam. Valet enim haec oratio contra poenam venialis et mortalis; sed plus contra poenam debitam alicui veniali, quae tota potest remitti in hac oratione, si dicatur devote. Ad[^11] poenam mortalis valet, sed non tantum, quia non totum remittit; valet tamen aliquantulum, immo multum, non tantum ratione orationis, sed ratione eleemosynae implicitae; et ista est eleemosyna praecipua, scilicet remittere peccatum proximo; et ideo dicit[^12]: «Si veraciter dicitur: *Dimitte nobis, sicut et nos dimittimus*».

### Dub. V

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Si ita veraciter dicatur, sicut et nos dimittimus* etc. Secundum hoc enim videtur, quod omnis qui habet rancorem, in dicendo istam orationem peccet mortaliter; quia omnis qui petit, sibi dimitti, sicut dimittit alii, si non dimittit, ex hoc petit, sibi non dimitti; sed quicumque petit, sibi peccatum non dimitti, peccat mortaliter: ergo videtur, quod semper peccat mortaliter, quando hanc orationem dicit.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod nullus dicens hanc orationem intendit ipsam dicere ad petendum iram, sed potius ad petendum misericordiam. Quando ergo dicit illam conditionem, non intendit vim conditionis aggravare, sive sit homo intelligens, sive simplex; et ideo non est ibi novus contemptus, ac per hoc nec novum peccatum; nihilominus peccatum aggravatur occasionaliter[^13]. Et si tu obiicias, quod tunc deberet abstinere; dico, quod adhuc aggravaretur magis, dum ab aliis petitionibus saluberrimis cor petentis arceretur. Si tu dicas, quod illam deberet dimittere; adhuc gravius peccaret, dum Domini orationem scienter infringeret. Et ideo sanum consilium est cuilibet, quod in fraterno odio nullatenus perseveret; apparet enim, quod sine dispendio maximo esse non potest[^14].

## English

### DOUBTS CONCERNING THE TEXT OF THE MASTER.

### Doubt I

In this section there are doubts concerning the text, and first it is asked about that division which the Master sets down in the text, that penance is conducted in three ways, whether it is *the penance-virtue,* or *the penance-Sacrament.* And it seems that it is not a virtue, because this is simple and a most specific species[^1]. — Likewise, that it is not a Sacrament either: because, when the superior is multiplied, the inferior is multiplied[^2]: therefore according to this it seems that penance, according to the three differences, is a threefold Sacrament. *Moreover,* «every Sacrament, as Ambrose says, is simple».

**I respond:** It must be said that the penance which is before baptism is the *penance-virtue,* not the *penance-Sacrament;* and the penance which is after baptism is the *penance-Sacrament;* and therefore penance in this division is taken commonly for the *virtue* and the *Sacrament;* nor is it said equivocally, but analogically by reduction to one nature[^3]. — Further it must be noted that these parts do not diversify penance *essentially,* but *according to state;* and therefore it is not a division into *essential* parts, nor is it necessary on this account that the Sacrament be multiplied or composite. And so that is clear[^4].

### Doubt II

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says that *penance is made for the sins which the decalogue of the Law contains.* For it seems not to be said well, because in the decalogue pride is not forbidden, nor hypocrisy, nor the like: therefore for these there seems to be no penance to be done.

**I respond:** It must be said that, just as in the order of a predicament some things are in a genus *per se,* some are in a genus *by reduction,* as principles and also privations[^5]; so it must be understood in the articles of faith. For some are antecedent, some consequent, and some are directly to be believed, as the articles; and in all these one is judged a heretic, if he hold perversely. In this manner it must be understood in the precepts of the decalogue, that some things are there forbidden *principally* and *explicitly,* some as *annexed* and *implicitly:* as, for example, if it be said: *Thou shalt not commit adultery*[^6], the act of adultery is principally and explicitly forbidden, but implicitly and *in general* every act of coition as *antecedent;* and so the concupiscence of coition or the gazing upon a woman to commit adultery is forbidden; consequently indeed every inordinate effusion of seed is forbidden. So it must be understood in the others, that in the first precept the worship of God is commanded; and in this the act of faith is commanded antecedently, and consequently all haughtiness and pride is forbidden; and so in the others[^7].

### Doubt III

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *When we say: Forgive us our debts* etc.; we manifest that we have something to be forgiven us. Suppose then that someone does not have consciousness of sin in some instant, as when he sings Mass, and is well confessed and intent upon heavenly things; then it seems that such a one, in saying that prayer, sins mortally, because he acts against conscience.

**I respond:** It must be said that every man can reasonably have consciousness of venial sin, both on account of frequency, and on account of ignorance, because in many things we offend ignorantly, even when we believe ourselves to do well; and on account of adherence, because the love of a temporal thing and those inordinate affections frequently cling much to us. And therefore anyone can say: *From my hidden sins cleanse me, O Lord*[^8] etc. — Or it can be said that one can still say it in the person of others; and therefore he does not sin, but obtains for others for that time[^9], or asks for himself with respect to another state.

### Doubt IV

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *This least and daily prayer wholly blots out* etc. For it seems from this that Augustine says[^10] that the Lord's Prayer blots out sins. *But on the contrary:* it belongs to grace alone to blot out sins: therefore etc. — If you say, as to the penalty; *on the contrary:* the penalty of a venial and of a mortal sin is not equal; but this is satisfaction for venial sins: therefore not for mortal sins.

**I respond:** It must be said that he is speaking of a blotting-out as to penalty. For this prayer avails against the penalty of venial and of mortal sin; but more against the penalty due to some venial sin, which can be wholly remitted in this prayer, if it be said devoutly. As to[^11] the penalty of mortal sin it avails, but not so much, because it does not remit the whole; yet it avails somewhat, indeed much, not only by reason of the prayer, but by reason of the implied almsgiving; and this is the chief almsgiving, namely to forgive a sin to one's neighbor; and therefore he says[^12]: «If it be truly said: *Forgive us, as we also forgive*».

### Doubt V

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *If it be truly said thus, as we also forgive* etc. For according to this it seems that everyone who has rancor, in saying that prayer, sins mortally; because everyone who asks that it be forgiven him, as he forgives another, if he does not forgive, by this asks that it be not forgiven him; but whoever asks that his sin be not forgiven him sins mortally: therefore it seems that he always sins mortally, when he says this prayer.

**I respond:** It must be said that no one saying this prayer intends to say it for the asking of wrath, but rather for the asking of mercy. When therefore he says that condition, he does not intend to aggravate the force of the condition, whether he be an understanding man or a simple one; and therefore there is no new contempt there, and through this no new sin; nevertheless the sin is aggravated occasionally[^13]. And if you object that then he ought to abstain; I say that it would be aggravated still more, while the heart of the one asking would be cut off from the other most salutary petitions. If you say that he ought to omit that [condition]; he would sin still more grievously, while he would knowingly break the Lord's prayer. And therefore it is sound counsel for everyone that he in no way persevere in fraternal hatred; for it appears that it cannot be without the greatest loss[^14].

**Dubia count: 5.**
- **Dub. I** — On the Master's threefold division of penance: is "penance" here the *virtue* or the *Sacrament*? (Objections: it is a most specific species, so not a virtue; and if the higher multiplied the lower, it would be a threefold Sacrament.) Answer: penance *before* baptism is the virtue, *after* baptism the Sacrament; the term is taken commonly (analogically, by reduction to one nature), the three parts differing by *state* not *essence* — so no multiplication or composition of the Sacrament follows.
- **Dub. II** — On *penance is made for the sins the decalogue contains:* but pride, hypocrisy, etc. are not in the decalogue. Answer: as some things are in a genus *per se* and some *by reduction,* so the decalogue forbids some sins *principally/explicitly* and others *as annexed/implicitly* (e.g. *Non moechaberis* implicitly forbids all inordinate concupiscence; the first precept implicitly forbids pride). Codex P's long gloss (note 7) maps each capital vice to a precept.
- **Dub. III** — On *Dimitte nobis debita nostra:* does one with no consciousness of sin (e.g. saying Mass, well-confessed) sin mortally by praying it against conscience? Answer: everyone can reasonably have consciousness of *venial* sin (frequency / ignorance / adherence — *Ab occultis meis munda me*); or he prays it in the person of others (*debita nostra,* not *mea*), so he does not sin.
- **Dub. IV** — On *Delet omnino haec oratio minima et quotidiana:* but only grace blots out sin, and the penalty of venial ≠ mortal. Answer: he speaks of blotting-out *as to penalty;* the prayer wholly remits a venial's penalty (if said devoutly) and avails much (not wholly) against a mortal's, partly by reason of the implied almsgiving — forgiving one's neighbor (*Dimitte nobis, sicut et nos dimittimus*).
- **Dub. V** — On *Si ita veraciter dicatur, sicut et nos dimittimus:* does one in rancor sin mortally each time he prays it? Answer: no one prays it *intending* to ask wrath but mercy; saying the condition adds no new contempt or new sin, though the existing sin is aggravated *occasionally;* abstaining or omitting the clause would aggravate worse — so the sound counsel is never to persevere in fraternal hatred (it cannot be without the greatest loss).
