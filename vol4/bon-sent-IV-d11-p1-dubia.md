## Latin
<!-- page 252 -->

### DUBIA CIRCA LITTERAM MAGISTRI.

### Dub. I

In parte ista sunt dubitationes circa litteram, et primo de hoc quod dicit Magister: *Mysterium fidei credi salubriter potest, investigari salubriter non potest.* Videtur enim secundum hoc, quod omnes peccent graviter, qui disputant de his quae ad fidem spectant.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod facienda est vis tam in hoc quod dicit *mysterium fidei,* quam in verbo *investigandi.* *Mysterium* enim secretum est, et illud non est *investigandum,* ut homo velit ratione comprehendere, quod Dominus voluit occultare; et qui aliter facit peccat et praesumtuosus est, dum se elevat ad id, ad quod non potest pertingere[^1]. Qui autem disputat de fide non ad mysterii investigationem, sed potius ad defensionem, ille non facit contra hoc quod dicit Augustinus et Magister[^2].

### Dub. II

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod haec concedenda est: *Illud quod erat panis vel vinum, post consecrationem est corpus Christi.* Videtur enim falsum dicere, quia *quod* est relativum identitatis[^3]; sed ibi nihil manet commune: ergo talis locutio est falsa. — Praeterea, quaero: pro quo supponit, cum dicitur: *quod erat panis?* Aut pro supposito *panis,* aut *Christi.* Si pro supposito *panis:* contra: isti nunquam convenit praedicatum. Si pro supposito *corporis:* ergo implicatio est falsa, quia corpus Christi nunquam fuit panis.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod proprie loquendo — cum hoc relativum *quod* sit relativum identitatis, et suppositum non sit idem cum praedicato — quod simpliciter loquendo, proprie falsa est; nec Magister[^4] dicit concedendam secundum opinionem suam. Si autem legatur a Sanctis, exponendam esse credo. Unde cum Augustinus[^5] dicit, quod «corpus Christi dicimus illud quod ex fructibus terrae collectum» etc; dicendum, quod illud dictum est quantum ad *signum exterius,* et secundum metaphoram est intelligendum illud, secundum quod *signum* pro *signato* ponitur; et vocatur hic *corpus Christi* Sacramentum corporis Christi[^6].

### Dub. III

Item quaeritur: quid est quod dicit Gregorius, *in ipsa immolationis hora caelos aperiri?* Videtur enim male dicere, quia, si aperiuntur; quaero, ad quid? Si ut descendat, videtur falsum, quia potest descendere sine apertione caelorum. Et iterum, ad quid descendit, si panis convertitur[^7] in corpus suum hic inferius? Si dicas, hoc dictum tropice; quaero, quo tropo?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod panis iste, qui in altari conficitur, est panis caelestis; caelum autem dicitur aperiri, quando vehemens fit influentia de supernis. Quoniam igitur immolationis hora nobis panis caelestis et supersubstantialis tribuitur; hinc est, quod Gregorius caelos dicit aperiri; sicut in eius nativitate dicitur, quod «per totum mundum

<!-- page 253 -->

melliflui facti sunt caeli[^8], quia datus est nobis ille qui dulcior est super mel et favum.»

### Dub. IV

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *In caelum rapitur, ministerio Angelorum consociandum corpori Christi.* Videtur enim falsum dicere, quia illud corpus sumitur a sacerdote, ergo non rapitur ministerio. — Item quomodo consociatur corpori Christi, cum idem sit corpus? — Item, quaeritur: quomodo intelligitur illud quod scribitur in canone[^9]: *Iube haec perferri per manus sancti Angeli tui?* quid est quod perfertur? et quis est Angelus, qui perfert?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod sicut praedicta verba circa confectionem figurative et secundum tropum intelligenda sunt, sic et ista. Unde sicut manus Angelorum non intelliguntur corporaliter, sic nec rapi hoc Sacramentum intelligendum est localiter vel corporaliter; similiter nec consociari situaliter. Sed hoc intelligendum est, quod sicut Angeli intelliguntur Deo offerre orationes nostras et petitiones, similiter et desideria — non propter ignorantiam Dei, sed propter commoditatem nostram, quia suis sanctis affectibus puris nos adiuvant et merita nostra in conspectu Dei replicant, ut ex eorum puritate sancta et affectione ferventi ratione dignitatis nuntii sint acceptabilia — sic intelligendum est offerre sacrificia, quia sacris mysteriis assistentes una nobiscum precantur, ut nostra munera sint accepta, et una nobiscum reverentur sanctissimum corpus Christi, sicut in caelo. Ideo dicuntur in caelum rapere et consociare quantum ad hoc, quod reputant honore dignum in altari, ut in caelo. Per hunc etiam modum intelligenda est oratio canonis: quia, sicut exponit Innocentius[^10], *Angelus* ille *magni consilii* est Christus; per *manus* illius oramus sacrificium perferri, id est ipsius meritis et precibus nostrum sacrificium Deo acceptari. Unde ipse est *pontifex et advocatus noster* et apparet continue *vultui Dei ad interpellandum pro nobis.* Et sic patent quaesita.

## English

### DOUBTS CONCERNING THE TEXT OF THE MASTER.

### Doubt I

In this section there are doubts concerning the text, and first about this, that the Master says: *The mystery of faith can be believed wholesomely, but cannot be investigated wholesomely.* For it seems, according to this, that all sin gravely who dispute about those things which pertain to faith.

**I respond:** It must be said that emphasis is to be placed both on this, that he says *mystery of faith,* and on the word *investigating.* For a *mystery* is something secret, and that is not to be *investigated,* such that a man should wish to comprehend by reason what the Lord willed to conceal; and one who does otherwise sins and is presumptuous, while he raises himself up to that which he cannot reach[^1]. But one who disputes about the faith not for the investigation of the mystery, but rather for its defense, he does not act against this which Augustine and the Master say[^2].

### Doubt II

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says that this proposition is to be conceded: *That which was bread or wine, after the consecration is the body of Christ.* For it seems to be said falsely, because *which* is a relative of identity[^3]; but there nothing common remains: therefore such a locution is false. — Moreover, I ask: for what does it supposit, when it is said: *that which was bread?* Either for the supposit *bread,* or *Christ.* If for the supposit *bread:* on the contrary, the predicate never belongs to it. If for the supposit *of the body:* therefore the implication is false, because the body of Christ was never bread.

**I respond:** It must be said that, properly speaking — since this relative *which* is a relative of identity, and the supposit is not the same as the predicate — that, simply speaking, it is properly false; nor does the Master[^4] say it is to be conceded according to his own opinion. But if it is read by the Saints, I believe it is to be expounded. Hence when Augustine[^5] says that «we call the body of Christ that which is gathered out of the fruits of the earth» etc.; it must be said that that statement is made as to the *exterior sign,* and is to be understood according to metaphor, in that the *sign* is put for the *signified;* and here the *body of Christ* is called the Sacrament of the body of Christ[^6].

### Doubt III

Likewise it is asked: what is it that Gregory says, that *in the very hour of the immolation the heavens are opened?* For it seems to be said badly, because, if they are opened; I ask, for what? If in order that it may descend, it seems false, because it can descend without the opening of the heavens. And again, for what does it descend, if the bread is converted[^7] into his body here below? If you say this is said tropically; I ask, by what trope?

**I respond:** It must be said that this bread, which is confected on the altar, is heavenly bread; and the heaven is said to be opened, when a vehement influence comes from on high. Since therefore in the hour of the immolation heavenly and supersubstantial bread is bestowed on us; hence it is that Gregory says the heavens are opened; just as at his nativity it is said that «throughout the whole world

the heavens were made to flow with honey[^8], because there has been given to us that one who is sweeter than honey and the honeycomb.»

### Doubt IV

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *It is caught up into heaven, by the ministry of the Angels, to be associated with the body of Christ.* For it seems to be said falsely, because that body is taken by the priest, therefore it is not caught up by ministry. — Likewise, how is it associated with the body of Christ, since it is the same body? — Likewise, it is asked: how is that understood which is written in the canon[^9]: *Bid these things to be carried up by the hands of thy holy Angel?* What is it that is carried up? and who is the Angel, who carries up?

**I respond:** It must be said that, just as the aforesaid words concerning the confection are to be understood figuratively and according to trope, so also these. Hence, just as the hands of the Angels are not understood corporeally, so neither is the catching-up of this Sacrament to be understood locally or corporeally; likewise neither the association in place. But this is to be understood, that just as the Angels are understood to offer to God our prayers and petitions, likewise also our desires — not on account of God's ignorance, but on account of our advantage, because by their holy pure affections they help us and lay our merits again before the sight of God, so that from their holy purity and fervent affection, by reason of the dignity of the messenger, they may be acceptable — so it is to be understood that they offer sacrifices, because, assisting at the sacred mysteries together with us, they pray that our gifts may be accepted, and together with us they revere the most holy body of Christ, as in heaven. Therefore they are said to catch up into heaven and to associate, as to this, that they reckon worthy of honor on the altar what is so in heaven. By this manner also is to be understood the prayer of the canon: because, as Innocent expounds[^10], that *Angel of great counsel* is Christ; through *his hands* we pray that the sacrifice be carried up, that is, that by his merits and prayers our sacrifice be accepted by God. Hence he himself is *our pontiff and advocate* and appears continually *to the face of God to intercede for us.* And thus the things asked are made clear.

**Dubia count: 4.**
- **Dub. I** — On the Master's *Mysterium fidei credi salubriter potest, investigari salubriter non potest:* it would seem all who dispute about faith sin gravely. Answer: emphasis falls both on *mystery* (a secret, not to be reason-comprehended where God willed concealment — to attempt it is presumption) and on *investigating;* one who disputes for *defense,* not investigation, acts against neither Augustine nor the Master.
- **Dub. II** — On *Illud quod erat panis vel vinum, post consecrationem est corpus Christi:* *quod* is a relative of identity, yet nothing common remains, and the supposition (for bread? for Christ?) fails either way. Answer: simply/properly speaking the proposition is false, and the Master does not concede it as his own opinion; if read in the Saints it is to be expounded metaphorically (sign for signified), the "body of Christ" here naming the Sacrament of the body.
- **Dub. III** — On Gregory's *in ipsa immolationis hora caelos aperiri:* why opened, if Christ can descend without it, and what trope? Answer: the altar-bread is heavenly bread; "heaven opened" = a vehement influence from on high, since heavenly/supersubstantial bread is bestowed in the immolation-hour (cf. the Nativity "honey-flowing heavens").
- **Dub. IV** — On *In caelum rapitur, ministerio Angelorum consociandum corpori Christi* and the canon's *Iube haec perferri per manus sancti Angeli tui:* the body is taken by the priest (not caught up), is the same body, so what is carried and who is the Angel? Answer: figurative, like the other confection-words — the Angels' "hands"/"catching-up"/"associating" are not local or corporeal; they offer our prayers/desires and revere the body with us; per Innocent the *Angel of great counsel* is Christ, by whose merits the sacrifice is accepted.
