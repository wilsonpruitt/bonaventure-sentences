### DUBIA CIRCA LITTERAM MAGISTRI.

#### Dub. I.

In parte ista sunt dubitationes circa litteram, et primo de hoc quod dicit, quod *sine intermissione perpetuos* etc. Videtur enim male dicere, quia Iob vigesimo quarto[^p967-1] dicitur: *Transierunt ab aquis nivium ad calorem nimium*: ergo saltem videtur, quod in illo transitu sit intermissio. — Item, super illud Isaiae decimo quarto[^p967-2]: *Tu autem proiectus es*; Glossa: « Etsi omnes animae aliquando habebunt requiem, tu nunquam »: et ita videtur, quod animae aliquando ibi requiescant.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod punitio gehennalis erit sine omni intermissione; et hoc patet per illud quod dicitur Apocalypsis decimo quarto[^p967-3]: *Ascendit fumus tormentorum in saecula saeculorum, et non habebunt requiem die ac nocte.* Cum enim non sit interpolatio in culpa, non debet esse in poena.

Quod obiicitur de transitu, dicendum, quod dupliciter potest intelligi: vel sicut est transitus subiecti ab uno contrariorum immediatorum in aliud[^p967-4], ita tamen, quod semper est sub altero contrariorum; sic intelligitur in proposito; vel ita, quod non intelligatur successio, sed nec transitus cum successione, sed varietas cum confusione.

Quod obiicitur de Glossa, dicendum, quod loquitur non *simpliciter*, sed *sub conditione*, ad exaggerandum magnitudinem peccati illius.

#### Dub. II.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Quae si sola esset alienari a regno* etc. Videtur enim male dicere: quia, ut dicit Augustinus[^p967-5], haec est *mitissima* poenarum omnium: ergo contradicit sibi nunc.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Augustinus[^p967-6] loquitur de illis qui damnum habent et non animadvertunt, vel animadvertunt, non tamen habent conscientiae remorsum. — Vel dicendum, quod quia ipse comparat carentiam visionis Dei aeternam ad poenam temporalem, dicens[^p967-7], quod illa excedit omnem temporalem, quia ibi est privatio boni aeterni, sed hic temporalis. Cum autem dicit, ipsam esse *mitissimam*, loquitur de poenis aeternis.

#### Dub. III.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *quantumcumque mali sint, de poena relaxare*. Videtur enim male dicere, quia Ecclesiastici quadragesimo[^p967-8]: *A praesidente* etc.; dicit Glossa: « In inferno nulla erit mitigatio ».

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod hic loquitur de relaxatione, quae fit *in* taxatione poenae, sed ibi de mitigatione *post* poenam taxatam. — Aliqui tamen exponunt, quod non erit mitigatio ad *liberationem*, sicut exponit Magister quasdam alias auctoritates in littera[^p967-9]; sed ista expositio extorta esse videtur. Aliud est enim poenam *mitigari*, aliud *a poena omnino liberari*.

#### Dub. IV.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Quam incomprehensibilia sunt iudicia eius!* quia videtur dicere contra illud quod dicitur in Psalmo[^p967-10]: *In labiis meis pronuntiavi omnia iudicia oris tui.* Si enim potuit *pronuntiare*: ergo et *comprehendere*.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *iudicia Dei* dicuntur dupliciter: uno modo *iudicialia praecepta*, quae erant in Lege, quae frequenter in Psalmo vocantur *iudicia*. Alio modo dicuntur *divina dispositio* et *divini consilii definitio*, et ista sunt inscrutabilia quantum ad cognitionem *si est*, antequam fiant; et quantum ad cognitionem *propter quid*, ex quo facta sunt, nisi Spiritus Dei revelet, *qui scrutatur etiam profunda Dei*[^p968-1]. Et sic patet, quod alio modo accipitur hic et ibi *iudicium*.

<!-- page 968 -->

#### Dub. V.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *quaedam sunt, quibus ostenditur misericors et aequus distributor*. Videtur enim male dicere: quia opera Dei non ostendunt misericordiam et iustitiam, nisi sicut effectus causam: si ergo misericordia et iustitia est eadem causa omnino, ut dicit in littera[^p968-2], ergo eisdem effectibus, quibus ostenditur *iustus*, ostenditur et *misericors*.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *causa* in Deo, quamvis non dicat veram habitudinem et dependentiam ex parte *Dei*, dicit tamen habitudinem ex parte *creaturae*; et sicut ex parte *creaturae* est habitudinis veritas, ita et multiplicitas; et secundum illorum habituum multiplicitatem est divinam essentiam multipliciter intelligere et significare[^p968-3]. Quoniam igitur alium modum habet effectus, qui respicit *meritum*, alium, qui respicit misericordiae *effectum*[^p968-4]; et iustitia et misericordia istas habitudines connotant; quamvis *principale significatum* sit unum, tamen ille *effectus*, qui attribuitur iustitiae, nec potest nec debet attribui misericordiae[^p968-5].

#### Dub. VI.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *aliud significat sapientia, aliud iustitia* etc.; quia supra[^p968-6] cum dictum sit, quod non dicitur diversitas significati, videtur, quod contra se dicat.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod illud intelligitur ratione *connotati*; sed illud quod superius dictum est, intelligitur ratione *principalis significati*. — Vel dicendum, quod *alietas* non notatur in re significata absolute, sed sub ratione intelligendi, quae est alia et alia, quia voces sunt signa intellectuum[^p968-7]. Et sic patet illud.

## English

### DOUBTS CONCERNING THE TEXT OF THE MASTER.

#### Doubt I.

In this part there are doubts concerning the text, and first concerning what he says, that *without intermission perpetual* etc. For it seems that he speaks badly, because in Job the twenty-fourth chapter[^p967-1] it is said: *They shall pass from the waters of snows to excessive heat*: therefore at least it seems that in that passage there is an intermission. — Likewise, upon that text of Isaiah the fourteenth chapter[^p967-2]: *But thou art cast out*; the Gloss: « Although all souls shall at some time have rest, thou never »: and so it seems that souls do at some time rest there.

**Respondeo:** It must be said that the punishment of hell will be without any intermission; and this is plain from what is said in the Apocalypse, the fourteenth chapter[^p967-3]: *The smoke of their torments ascendeth for ever and ever, and they have no rest day nor night.* For since there is no interruption in the fault, there ought not to be one in the punishment.

To what is objected concerning the passage, it must be said that it can be understood in two ways: either as there is a passage of a subject from one of two immediate contraries into the other[^p967-4], yet in such a way that it is always under one of the two contraries — thus it is understood in the case proposed; or in such a way that no succession is understood, nor even a passage with succession, but a variation together with confusion.

To what is objected concerning the Gloss, it must be said that it speaks not *absolutely*, but *conditionally*, in order to exaggerate the magnitude of that sin.

#### Doubt II.

Likewise it is asked concerning what he says: *Which, if it were the only one, would be to be estranged from the kingdom* etc. For it seems that he speaks badly: because, as Augustine says[^p967-5], this is the *mildest* of all punishments: therefore he now contradicts himself.

**Respondeo:** It must be said that Augustine[^p967-6] is speaking of those who suffer the loss and do not advert to it, or who do advert to it yet have no remorse of conscience. — Or it must be said that he compares the eternal lack of the vision of God to a temporal punishment, saying[^p967-7] that the former exceeds every temporal one, because there is the privation of an eternal good, but here of a temporal one. But when he says that it is the *mildest*, he is speaking of the eternal punishments.

#### Doubt III.

Likewise it is asked concerning what he says, that *however evil they may be, to relax something of the punishment*. For it seems that he speaks badly, because in Ecclesiasticus the fortieth chapter[^p967-8]: *From him that sitteth* etc.; the Gloss says: « In hell there will be no mitigation ».

**Respondeo:** It must be said that here he is speaking of a relaxation which takes place *in* the assessing of the punishment, but there of a mitigation *after* the punishment has been assessed. — Some, however, expound it as meaning that there will be no mitigation unto *deliverance*, as the Master expounds certain other authorities in the text[^p967-9]; but that exposition seems forced. For it is one thing for a punishment to be *mitigated*, another *to be delivered from the punishment altogether*.

#### Doubt IV.

Likewise it is asked concerning what he says: *How incomprehensible are his judgments!* because he seems to speak against what is said in the Psalm[^p967-10]: *With my lips I have pronounced all the judgments of thy mouth.* For if he could *pronounce* them: therefore also *comprehend* them.

**Respondeo:** It must be said that *the judgments of God* are spoken of in two ways: in one way, the *judicial precepts* which were in the Law, which are frequently called *judgments* in the Psalm. In another way there are called judgments *the divine disposition* and *the determination of the divine counsel*, and these are inscrutable as regards the knowledge *whether it is*, before they come to be; and as regards the knowledge *on account of what*, once they have been done, unless the Spirit of God reveal it, *who searcheth even the deep things of God*[^p968-1]. And thus it is plain that *judgment* is taken in one way here and in another there.

<!-- page 968 -->

#### Doubt V.

Likewise it is asked concerning what he says, that *there are certain things by which he is shown to be merciful and an equitable distributor*. For it seems that he speaks badly: because the works of God do not show mercy and justice except as an effect shows its cause: if therefore mercy and justice are altogether the same cause, as he says in the text[^p968-2], therefore by the same effects by which he is shown to be *just*, he is shown also to be *merciful*.

**Respondeo:** It must be said that *cause* in God, although it does not state a true relation and dependence on the part of *God*, nevertheless does state a relation on the part of *the creature*; and just as on the part of *the creature* there is truth of relation, so also multiplicity; and according to the multiplicity of those relations it is possible to understand and to signify the divine essence in manifold ways[^p968-3]. Since therefore an effect which regards *merit* has one mode, and one which regards the *effect* of mercy another[^p968-4]; and justice and mercy connote these relations; although the *principal thing signified* is one, nevertheless that *effect* which is attributed to justice neither can nor ought to be attributed to mercy[^p968-5].

#### Doubt VI.

Likewise it is asked concerning what he says, that *wisdom signifies one thing, justice another* etc.; because, since it was said above[^p968-6] that no diversity of the thing signified is asserted, it seems that he contradicts himself.

**Respondeo:** It must be said that the former is understood by reason of *what is connoted*; but what was said above is understood by reason of *the principal thing signified*. — Or it must be said that *otherness* is not noted in the thing signified absolutely, but under the manner of understanding, which is one and another, because words are signs of acts of understanding[^p968-7]. And thus that is plain.
