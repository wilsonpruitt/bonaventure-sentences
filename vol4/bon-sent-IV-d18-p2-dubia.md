# IV Sent., d. 18, p. II — Dubia circa litteram Magistri
## *Dubia circa litteram Magistri*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 495 -->

### DUBIA CIRCA LITTERAM MAGISTRI.

### Dub. I

In parte ista sunt dubitationes circa litteram, et primo de hoc quod dicit Magister: *Sibi ipsis relinquuntur, ut sit eis liberum arbitrium ruere in peccatum.* Videtur enim male dicere, quia liberum arbitrium non recipit *magis* et *minus*: ergo non est modo liberius post excommunicationem ad peccatum quam ante. — Item, iuxta hoc quaeritur, quae sint mala, quae incurrit quis per excommunicationem.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod libertas peccandi non est libertas, sed defectus libertatis[^1]. Liberum ergo arbitrium, prout dicitur liberum *a culpa*, quia hoc non est libertas *essentialis*, sed *accidentalis*, dicitur magis et minus liberum; et tunc *magis* est liberum, quando melius se potest defendere, et *minus* liberum, quando minus se potest defendere a peccati dominio. Quoniam igitur meritis orationum Ecclesiae multi sustentantur et defenduntur a culpis; tunc dicuntur *liberiores* ad peccandum fieri, quando auferuntur suffragia Ecclesiae. Unde patet, quomodo illud verbum intelligitur.

Et nota, quod multa sunt incommoda, quae incurrunt excommunicati, quae notantur his versibus:

> *Gratia subtrahitur, magis ac magis obice rupto.*
> *De vitio ruit in vitium, suffragia perdit*
> *Ecclesiae; satanae vexandus traditur ille,*
> *Quem ligat ex propriis anathematizatio culpis.*

Et intelliguntur quantum ad *expositionem* vel *desertionem*[^2]; sicut rex diceretur militem tradere inimico, quem non defenderet ab eius vexatione.

### Dub. II

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Sed intelligendum est de his quorum merita solvunt vel ligant.* — Quaeritur ergo hic, si aliquis sit iniuste excommunicatus, utrum se debeat habere pro excommunicato. Et quod sic, videtur: quia dicitur in iure[^3]: «Sententia pastoris, sive iusta sive iniusta, tenenda est et timenda». — Sed contra: Magister[^4] dicit, quod illam solam sententiam approbat curia caelestis, quae datur ex meritis. — Item, favere et tenere sententiam iniustam est praestare patrocinium iniustitiae: ergo talis sententia, ut videtur, nullo modo tenenda est, nec ab eo qui excommunicatur, nec ab aliis.

Item, quaeritur, quomodo sit sententia excommunicationis iniuste lata.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod sententia iniusta dicitur ex *animo*, ex *causa*, ex *ordine*[^5]: ex *animo*, ut cum infertur non zelo iustitiae, sed vindictae; ex *causa*, ut cum non est meritum sufficiens, vel certum, propter quod debeat excommunicari ille cui infligitur; ex *ordine*, ut cum praetermittitur ordo iuris.

Si ergo talis excommunicatio feratur in aliquem, distinguendum est: quia aut fertur ab habente canonicam iurisdictionem, aut a non habente. Si ab habente; dicendum, quod tenenda est et timenda, quia non est subditorum discutere sententias maiorum; et si contemnat, graviter peccat[^6]. — Excipiuntur autem duo casus, videlicet si sententia illa lata est post appellationem legitimam, vel quando continet intolerabilem errorem, ut si diceretur: Excommunico te, quia credis in Deum. In his duobus casibus non quaerentes absolutionem admittuntur ad eorum probationem; alias autem non admittuntur, nisi humiliter petant absolutionem. — Absolvit autem dominus Papa in omnibus *ad cautelam*[^7], non propter hoc quod reputet fuisse excommunicatos, vel excommunicandos, sed ut ostendat, Ecclesiae sententiam, quantumcumque sit iniusta, esse formidandam. Unde timetur et tenetur non consideratione iniustitiae, sed ob reverentiam potestatis ecclesiasticae. Hoc sentiendum est secundum iura, si excommunicetur ab habente iurisdictionem canonicam.

Si autem ab alio excommunicetur, qui non habet canonicam iurisdictionem, et hoc constat; tunc non est tenenda, utpote si sint haeretici, vel schismatici, apostatae, intrusi, suspensi a iurisdictione[^8]. Si autem talium crimen sit occultum, toleranda est talium sententia, sicut et ipsi tolerantur ab Ecclesia.

<!-- page 496 -->

### Dub. III

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *peccatum dicitur tenebrae, quia intellectus obtunditur et totus interior homo obtenebratur.* Videtur enim falsum dicere, quia videmus multos peccatores in peccatis existentes, qui valde bene intelligunt et addiscunt, et ita experimentum est contra hoc. Item, Domini documentum, Lucae decimo sexto[^9]: *Filii saeculi huius prudentiores sunt filiis lucis.* — Item, cum multo magis sit peccatum deordinativum *affectus* quam *intellectus*, et corruptivum *amoris* quam *luminis*, et *caritatis* quam *fidei*; videtur potius esse dicendum, quod peccatum sit *frigus* quam *tenebra*.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod gratia magis dicitur *lux* quam *calor*, quoniam lux in se habet et virtutem illuminandi, et illuminando magis et magis virtutem calefaciendi; et ideo plus complectitur lux quam calor. Et gratia in quantum lux non tantum respicit intellectum, verum etiam affectum, immo lux, ut dicitur de gratia, nominat eam ut respicit totam animam; quoniam, sicut lux[^10] est forma totius orbis primi, et per cuius influentiam fiunt generationes circa partes inferiores, et una est ratio actualitatis et diffusionis, secundum quod innuit et dicit aperte Dionysius in libro de Divinis Nominibus[^11]: et quia hoc competit gratiae, quae est radix omnium habituum gratuitorum et principium omnis boni operis: hinc est, quod dicitur *lux*, et peccatum dicitur *tenebra*; quia[^12] tenebra non tantum privat actum lucis, secundum quem est in sensum visus, sed etiam alios actus, secundum quos est operativa: et ita ista tenebra non respicit intellectum tantum, sed intellectum ut extensum ad calorem affectus, vel ad operationem effectus.

Ex his patent obiecta. Quod enim dicitur, quod intellectus viget in peccatoribus; dicendum, quod verum est secundum actum, qui est purae speculationis, sed non prout extenditur ad affectum, vel opus. Unde aliter habuit prophetiam Balaam[^13], aliter David. Ille enim solum videbat, iste videbat et gaudebat et secundum hoc se in opere regulabat et se ipsum dirigebat. Exemplum est in musico et bono citharoedo; aliter enim novit consonantiam citharae musicus, aliter bonus citharoedus[^14]. Et quantum ad hanc obtunditur intellectus manifeste, licet non quantum ad primam ita aperte; credo tamen firmiter, quod quantum ad utramque.

### Dub. IV

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Ipsa ergo dissimilitudo, quae inest animae ex peccato, animae macula est.* Videtur enim male dicere. Omnis enim macula deformitas dicitur, aut quia aliquid est remotum, aut quia aliquid, quod non debet, circa rem ponitur; sicut patet: truncatus naso est deformatus, sed habens *morpheam*[^15] vel aliquid tale est maculatus; sed peccatum non corrumpit bona naturalia, quia nihil corrumpit «in eo quod est essentia vel natura[^16]»; aut si corrumpit, tamen nihil ponit: ergo nullo modo debet dici macula. Si tu dicas, quod ponit malam habilitatem et pronitatem ad malum, et illa est macula; contra: illa pronitas potest auferri per oppositam pronitatem: ergo macula peccati deleret maculam; quod inconveniens est dicere. Item, a pronitate liberatur per assuefactionem; solus autem Deus liberat a macula, ut dicitur in littera.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod peccatum non est aliud quam *privatio ordinis debiti*, et voco ordinem debitum, prout comprehendit modum, speciem et ordinem, prout circa ipsum consistit praecipue ratio boni[^17]. Ista ergo deordinatio remanens in anima est culpa ipsa et peccatum, quia est ex proprio actu, ubi debet esse ordo iustitiae. Haec autem habet considerari secundum rationem *aversionis* et secundum rationem *conversionis* et utriusque.

<!-- page 497 -->

Secundum primam rationem competit ei ratio *privationis*; secundum secundum modum ratio *infectionis*, et haec[^18] iungit sibi rem turpem; secundum tertium modum ratio *obligationis*. — Quantum ad primum modum dicitur peccatum *tenebra* et *deformitas*; quoniam bonum dupliciter potest considerari, scilicet *in se*, et sic dicitur *lux*, et per oppositum peccatum dicitur *tenebra*; et in comparatione ad vim cognitivam, et sic dicitur *pulcritudo* animae, et per oppositum peccatum *deformitas*. — Quantum autem ad *conversionem*[^19] sic dicitur *macula* et *foetor*; et hoc similiter secundum duplicem comparationem illius coniunctionis cum delectabili terreno: *in se*, et sic *macula*; ad *vim cognitivam*, et sic *foetor*; quantum ad utrumque, sic competit esse *vinculum mortis aeternae*[^20][^21].

## English

### DOUBTS CONCERNING THE TEXT OF THE MASTER.

### Doubt I

In this section there are doubts concerning the text, and first about this, that the Master says: *They are left to themselves, so that they have free choice to rush into sin.* For it seems to be said badly, because free choice does not admit of *more* and *less*: therefore it is not now freer toward sin after excommunication than before. — Likewise, in connection with this it is asked what are the evils that one incurs through excommunication.

**I respond:** It must be said that the freedom of sinning is not freedom, but a defect of freedom[^1]. Therefore free choice, insofar as it is called free *from fault*, because this is not *essential* freedom but *accidental*, is called more and less free; and then it is *more* free when it can better defend itself, and *less* free when it can less defend itself from the dominion of sin. Since therefore by the merits of the prayers of the Church many are sustained and defended from faults; then they are said to become *freer* toward sinning, when the suffrages of the Church are withdrawn. Whence it is plain how that word is understood.

And note that there are many harms which the excommunicate incur, which are noted in these verses:

> *Grace is withdrawn, more and more as the barrier is broken.*
> *From vice he rushes into vice, he loses the suffrages*
> *Of the Church; he is handed over to Satan to be vexed,*
> *He whom the anathematization binds for his own faults.*

And they are understood as to *exposure* or *abandonment*[^2]; just as a king would be said to hand a soldier over to the enemy, whom he would not defend from his vexation.

### Doubt II

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *But it is to be understood of those whose merits loose or bind.* — It is asked therefore here, if someone is unjustly excommunicated, whether he ought to regard himself as excommunicated. And that he should, it seems: because it is said in the law[^3]: «The sentence of the pastor, whether just or unjust, is to be held and feared». — But on the contrary: the Master[^4] says that the heavenly court approves only that sentence which is given from merits. — Likewise, to favor and hold an unjust sentence is to lend patronage to injustice: therefore such a sentence, as it seems, is in no way to be held, neither by him who is excommunicated, nor by others.

Likewise, it is asked how a sentence of excommunication is unjustly passed.

**I respond:** It must be said that an unjust sentence is so called from the *intention*, from the *cause*, from the *order*[^5]: from the *intention*, as when it is imposed not from zeal for justice, but for vengeance; from the *cause*, as when there is no sufficient or certain merit for which he ought to be excommunicated upon whom it is inflicted; from the *order*, as when the order of law is omitted.

If therefore such an excommunication is passed against someone, a distinction must be made: because either it is passed by one having canonical jurisdiction, or by one not having [it]. If by one having [it]; it must be said that it is to be held and feared, because it is not for subjects to discuss the sentences of their superiors; and if one despises [it], he sins gravely[^6]. — But two cases are excepted, namely if that sentence was passed after a legitimate appeal, or when it contains an intolerable error, as if it were said: I excommunicate you, because you believe in God. In these two cases those not seeking absolution are admitted to their proof; but otherwise they are not admitted, unless they humbly ask absolution. — Now the lord Pope absolves in all cases *for caution*[^7], not because he reckons that they were excommunicated, or to-be-excommunicated, but to show that the sentence of the Church, however unjust it be, is to be dreaded. Whence it is feared and held not from consideration of [its] injustice, but out of reverence for ecclesiastical power. This is to be understood according to the laws, if one is excommunicated by one having canonical jurisdiction.

But if one is excommunicated by another, who does not have canonical jurisdiction, and this is established; then it is not to be held, namely if they are heretics, or schismatics, apostates, intruders, [or persons] suspended from jurisdiction[^8]. But if the crime of such persons is hidden, the sentence of such persons is to be tolerated, just as they too are tolerated by the Church.

### Doubt III

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says, that *sin is called darkness, because the intellect is blunted and the whole interior man is darkened.* For it seems to be said falsely, because we see many sinners existing in sins, who understand and learn very well, and so experience is against this. Likewise, the Lord's teaching, Luke sixteen[^9]: *The children of this world are more prudent than the children of light.* — Likewise, since sin is much more disordering of the *affection* than of the *intellect*, and corruptive of *love* than of *light*, and of *charity* than of *faith*; it seems rather to be said that sin is *cold* rather than *darkness*.

**I respond:** It must be said that grace is called *light* rather than *heat*, because light has in itself both the power of illuminating, and, by illuminating, more and more the power of heating; and therefore light comprehends more than heat. And grace insofar as light regards not only the intellect, but even the affection, indeed light, as it is said of grace, names it as it regards the whole soul; because, just as light[^10] is the form of the whole first orb, and through whose influence generations come about in the lower parts, and there is one ground of actuality and of diffusion, according as Dionysius intimates and says openly in the book *On the Divine Names*[^11]: and because this belongs to grace, which is the root of all gratuitous habits and the principle of every good work: hence it is that it is called *light*, and sin is called *darkness*; because[^12] darkness not only deprives of the act of light, according to which it is in the sense of sight, but also [of] the other acts according to which [the soul] is operative: and so this darkness regards not the intellect only, but the intellect as extended to the heat of affection, or to the operation of effect.

From these things the objections are plain. For as to what is said, that the intellect flourishes in sinners; it must be said that it is true as to the act which is of pure speculation, but not as it is extended to affection or work. Whence Balaam had prophecy in one way[^13], David in another. For the former only saw, the latter saw and rejoiced and according to this regulated himself in work and directed himself. There is an example in the musician and the good cithara-player; for the musician knows the consonance of the cithara in one way, the good cithara-player in another[^14]. And as to this [latter] the intellect is manifestly blunted, although not as openly as to the first; yet I firmly believe that [it is blunted] as to both.

### Doubt IV

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *Therefore that very unlikeness, which is in the soul from sin, is the soul's stain.* For it seems to be said badly. For every stain is called a deformity, either because something is removed, or because something which ought not [to be] is placed about the thing; as is plain: one cut off as to the nose is deformed, but one having *morphew*[^15] or some such thing is stained; but sin does not corrupt natural goods, because nothing is corrupted «in that which is essence or nature[^16]»; or if it corrupts, yet it places nothing: therefore in no way ought it to be called a stain. If you say that it places an evil aptitude and proneness to evil, and that is the stain; on the contrary: that proneness can be removed by an opposite proneness: therefore the stain of sin would delete the stain; which it is unfitting to say. Likewise, one is freed from proneness by habituation; but God alone frees from the stain, as is said in the text.

**I respond:** It must be said that sin is nothing else than the *privation of due order*, and I call due order [that] insofar as it comprehends mode, species, and order, insofar as about it consists chiefly the ground of the good[^17]. This disorder therefore remaining in the soul is the fault itself and sin, because it is from one's own act, where there ought to be the order of justice. But this has to be considered according to the ground of *aversion* and according to the ground of *conversion* and of both.

<!-- page 497 -->

According to the first ground there belongs to it the ground of *privation*; according to the second mode the ground of *infection*, and this[^18] joins to itself a base thing; according to the third mode the ground of *obligation*. — As to the first mode sin is called *darkness* and *deformity*; because the good can be considered in two ways, namely *in itself*, and thus it is called *light*, and by opposition sin is called *darkness*; and in comparison to the cognitive power, and thus it is called the *beauty* of the soul, and by opposition sin [is called] *deformity*. — But as to *conversion*[^19] thus it is called *stain* and *foulness*; and this likewise according to the twofold comparison of that conjunction with the earthly delightful thing: *in itself*, and thus *stain*; to the *cognitive power*, and thus *foulness*; as to both, thus there belongs [to it] to be the *bond of eternal death*[^20][^21].
