## Latin

### Articulus III. *De poenitentia post baptismum de venialibus.*
### Quaestio I. *Utrum veniale peccatum possit fieri mortale.*

Primo ergo quaeritur, utrum veniale peccatum possit fieri mortale. Et quod sic,

1. Per *complacentiam* specialiter, videtur: Augustinus[^1]: «Nihil adeo est veniale, quod non fiat mortale, dum placet». — Item, quod per *multitudinem*: Augustinus *de Poenitentia*[^2]: «Quamvis singula non letali vulnere ferire sentiantur; tamen omnia simul congregata, velut scabies, eo quod plura sunt, necant».

2. Item, *ratione* videtur: quia complacentia est plenus consensus; sed delectatio, quae est venialis, adveniente consensu, fit mortalis: ergo veniale per complacentiam fit mortale. *Minor* patet, quia delectatio morosa, quae est venialis ante consensum, postea fit mortalis[^3].

3. Item, *placitum* est quietata voluntas; sed quiescens in creatura fruitur illa, et ita peccat mortaliter[^4]: ergo veniale fit per complacentiam mortale.

4. Item, quod per *multitudinem*, videtur: quia amor mortalis non excedit amorem venialis in infinitum; sed finitum aliquoties sumtum reddit esse *finitum*[^5]: ergo etc. *Si tu dicas*, quod excedit in infinitum, quia hoc est supra Deum, et illud infra; *contra*: ab eadem virtute non exit motus in infinitum maior et minor; sed mortalis amor et venialis sunt ab eadem potentia: ergo etc.

5. Item, veniale est alicuius quantitatis, quia est maius et minus; sed omne quantum est proportionale quanto: ergo mortale, cum sit quantum, non excedit veniale improportionaliter, et sic etc.

6. Item, unum veniale disponit aliquo modo, et aliud magis; sed per maiorem dispositionem fit *necessitas*[^6]: ergo necessario, ut videtur, veniale introducit mortale peccatum.

**Contra:** 1. Veniale et mortale specie differunt[^7]; sed complacentia, eo quod complacentia, non mutat speciem, sed conservat: ergo unum per complacentiam non fit alterum: ergo veniale non fit mortale.

2. Item, mortale excedit veniale in infinitum, quia poena excedit poenam in infinitum[^8]: ergo veniale infinities multiplicatum non fit mortale.

> ### Conclusio
> Peccata venialia per essentiam non fiunt mortalia, fiunt tamen interdum ratione vel pleni consensus, vel quietatae voluntatis, vel dispositionis sive pronitatis.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod triplex est genus venialis peccati. Quoddam enim est ex *subreptione*[^9]; et ideo veniale, quia vitari non potest tunc. Quoddam ex *cogitatione* sine voluntate sive consensu, et ideo veniale, quia non plene consentit homo, ut in cogitatione de muliere et delectatione citra consensum in delectationem. Quoddam est veniale cum *ratione et consensu*; sed tamen ideo est veniale, quia non est deordinatio contra praeceptum, ut ebrietas et mendacium iocosum.

Cum igitur quaeritur, utrum veniale fiat mortale; si intelligat[^10], quod haec culpa per essentiam fiat illa, nulla est quaestio, quia hoc est impossibile. — Si intelligat, quod illud quod alias erat veniale, possit esse mortale, ut mendacium iocosum et ebrietas; dico, quod quoddam non fit mortale, quoddam fit mortale per complacentiam. Primum igitur veniale, quod committitur praeter rationem, semper est veniale; sed secundo modo veniale per delectationem citra consensum fit mortale per consensum, et ita per complacentiam, secundum quod placitum dicitur consensus plenus. Tertium veniale quod est per delectationem citra Deum, potest fieri mortale per libidinem magnam, sicut mendacium iocosum; et hoc fit mortale secundum placitum, secundum quod placitum dicitur quietata voluntas. Et quia iteratio actus disponit ad libidinem et complacentiam; hinc est, quod Augustinus

<!-- page 410 -->

dicit in pluribus locis[^11], quod plura venialia obruunt, non *per se*, sed quia *disponunt* ad libidinem, non dispositione *necessitatis*, sed *pronitatis*.

**Ad fundamenta:** Ad illud ergo quod[^12] probatur, quod venialia non fiunt mortalia per *essentiam*; dicendum, quod verum est.

*Ad 1.* Sed quod obiicit in contrarium de Augustino, solutum est, quomodo intelligitur. Illud enim intelligitur sive per convenientiam in genere actus, sive per dispositionem, quia multa venialia disponunt ad libidinem et contemptum; ideo Augustinus[^13] comparat ea bestiis minutis, comparat guttis, comparat arenae, comparat scabiei, non quia ipsa in se faciunt naufragium, sed quia ad hoc perducunt per accidens.

*Ad 2. 3.* Ad illud quod obiicitur de consensu et quietata voluntate, dicendum, quod quando consensus venit, novum peccatum fit, nec fit veniale mortale, sed quod alias esset veniale, fit mortale.

*Ad 4.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod per multitudinem; dicendum, quod est infinita proportio, non quia motus est infinitus intensione, sed reputatione, sicut patet in eo qui diligit ex caritate: plus diligit Deum quam denarium unum et quam in infinitum multiplicatum[^14]; unde amor infra Deum nunquam comparabilis est amori, qui est supra Deum, secundum istum modum.

*Ad 5.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod veniale est quantum; dicendum, quod quemadmodum linea est in se quanta, sed comparatione superficiei non habet quantitatem[^15]; sic veniale, etsi de se quantum sit, comparatione mortalis caret quantitate.

*Ad 6.* Ad illud quod obiicitur de dispositione, dicendum, quod voluntas semper libere movetur, et ideo non disponitur dispositione, quae sit necessitas, sed tantum dispositione, quae est pronitas; non sic natura, quae ad unam partem est determinata[^16]. — Et alia ratio est in habente caritatem, quia sustentatur a virtute infinita; et talis per nullum pondus compellitur cadere, salva sustentatione; voluntas enim semper libera est et, quamdiu est in via, vertibilis est; et ideo nunquam inevitabiliter, dum est in via, ad malum culpae disponitur. Et sic patet illud.

### Scholion
**I.** Ad quaestionem, utrum veniale possit fieri mortale, respondetur communiter, quod nec idem numero actus potest fieri ex veniali mortale, nec plura venialia per se constituunt unum mortale, nisi dispositive vel ex parte consensus; sed aliter dicendum de circumstantiis graviter speciem mutantibus. Alex. Hal., S. p. IV. q. 15. m. 3. a. 1. § 1; Scot., IV. Sent. d. 21. n. 3. seqq.; III. Sent. d. 39. q. unica n. 3. seq. — S. Thom., II. Sent. d. 24. q. 3. a. 7; S. 1. 11. q. 88. a. 4. — B. Albert., II. Sent. d. 24. a. 13; Sum. p. 11. tr. 18. q. 117. m. 4. a. 1. — Petr. a Tar., hic a. 6. quaestiunc. 3. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 2. q. 2. — Dionys. Carth., de hac et seq. q. hic q. 1.

**II.** Quaestio seq. (2.) intelligitur de necessitate absoluta poenitendi de venialibus, quam quidam aliquatenus sustinere voluerunt cum Alexandro Hal. (loc. cit. m. 3. a. 3. § 5, a. 4. § 2.), hoc duplici principio innixi, quod post vitam in purgatorio non remittatur culpa, sed tantum poena, et quod formalis actus poenitentiae requiratur ad deletionem peccati, etiam venialis. Sed de hac quaestione plerumque alii magistri tractant et diffuse disputant infra d. 21. p. I. a. 1. q. 2, a. 2. q. 1, ubi in scholio citantur ceteri auctores. Attamen hoc loco rem tractat B. Albert., a. 54. 55.

## English

### Article III. *On penance after baptism concerning venial sins.*
### Question I. *Whether a venial sin can become mortal.*

First, then, it is asked whether a venial sin can become mortal. And that it can, is shown thus:

1. By *complacency* specially, it seems so: Augustine[^1]: «Nothing is so venial that it does not become mortal, while it pleases». — Likewise, that it does so by *multitude*: Augustine, *On Penance*[^2]: «Although individually they are not felt to strike with a deadly wound; nevertheless all gathered together at once, like the itch, by the fact that they are many, kill».

2. Likewise, by *reason* it seems so: because complacency is full consent; but a pleasure that is venial, when consent comes, becomes mortal: therefore a venial sin becomes mortal through complacency. The *minor* is clear, because morose pleasure, which is venial before consent, afterward becomes mortal[^3].

3. Likewise, *taking-pleasure* is a will at rest; but one resting in a creature enjoys it, and so sins mortally[^4]: therefore a venial sin becomes mortal through complacency.

4. Likewise, that it does so by *multitude*, it seems: because mortal love does not exceed venial love infinitely; but a finite thing taken several times yields a *finite* being[^5]: therefore etc. *If you say* that it does exceed infinitely, because this is above God and that is below; *on the contrary*: from the same power there does not issue a motion infinitely greater and lesser; but mortal love and venial love are from the same potency: therefore etc.

5. Likewise, a venial sin is of some quantity, since it is greater and lesser; but every quantum is proportional to a quantum: therefore a mortal sin, since it is a quantum, does not exceed a venial sin disproportionately, and so etc.

6. Likewise, one venial sin disposes in some way, and another more; but by a greater disposition there arises *necessity*[^6]: therefore necessarily, as it seems, a venial sin introduces mortal sin.

**On the contrary:** 1. Venial and mortal differ in species[^7]; but complacency, in that it is complacency, does not change the species but preserves it: therefore one does not become the other through complacency: therefore a venial sin does not become mortal.

2. Likewise, a mortal sin exceeds a venial one infinitely, because its punishment exceeds the other's punishment infinitely[^8]: therefore a venial sin multiplied infinitely does not become mortal.

> ### Conclusion
> Venial sins do not become mortal by essence; yet they do become so sometimes by reason of either full consent, or a will at rest, or disposition and proneness.

**I respond:** It must be said that there are three kinds of venial sin. For one is from *surprise*[^9]; and it is therefore venial, because it cannot then be avoided. Another is from *thought* without will or consent, and it is therefore venial, because the man does not fully consent, as in a thought about a woman and a pleasure short of consent into the pleasure. Another is venial with *reason and consent*; but it is venial nonetheless, because there is no disorder against a precept, as drunkenness and a jesting lie.

When, therefore, it is asked whether a venial sin becomes mortal; if one means[^10] that this fault by its essence becomes that one, there is no question, because this is impossible. — If one means that what was otherwise venial can be mortal, as a jesting lie and drunkenness; I say that one kind does not become mortal, another kind becomes mortal through complacency. The first venial sin, then, which is committed apart from reason, is always venial; but the venial sin of the second mode, through pleasure short of consent, becomes mortal through consent, and thus through complacency, insofar as taking-pleasure is called full consent. The third venial sin, which is through pleasure short of God, can become mortal through great lust, like a jesting lie; and this becomes mortal according to taking-pleasure, insofar as taking-pleasure is called a will at rest. And because the repetition of the act disposes toward lust and complacency; hence it is that Augustine

says in many places[^11] that many venial sins overwhelm, not *of themselves*, but because they *dispose* toward lust, not by a disposition of *necessity*, but of *proneness*.

**To the foundations:** To that, then, which proves that venial sins do not become mortal by *essence*[^12]; it must be said that it is true.

*To 1.* But as to what is objected on the contrary from Augustine, it has been solved how it is to be understood. For that is understood either by a fittingness in the genus of the act, or by disposition, because many venial sins dispose toward lust and contempt; therefore Augustine[^13] compares them to little beasts, compares them to drops, compares them to sand, compares them to the itch, not because they of themselves cause shipwreck, but because they lead to this accidentally.

*To 2 and 3.* To that which is objected about consent and the will at rest, it must be said that when consent comes, a new sin is made, and the venial sin does not become mortal, but what was otherwise venial becomes mortal.

*To 4.* To that which is objected about multitude; it must be said that the proportion is infinite, not because the motion is infinite in intensity, but in reckoning, as is clear in one who loves out of charity: he loves God more than a single penny and more than it multiplied to infinity[^14]; whence a love below God is never comparable to the love that is above God, according to this manner.

*To 5.* To that which is objected, that a venial sin is a quantum; it must be said that just as a line is in itself something quantified, but in comparison with a surface it has no quantity[^15]; so a venial sin, though of itself it be a quantum, in comparison with a mortal sin lacks quantity.

*To 6.* To that which is objected about disposition, it must be said that the will is always moved freely, and therefore is not disposed by a disposition that is a necessity, but only by a disposition that is a proneness; not so nature, which is determined to one part[^16]. — And there is another reason in one who has charity, because he is sustained by an infinite power; and such a one is by no weight compelled to fall, his sustaining being preserved; for the will is always free and, as long as it is on the way, changeable; and therefore never, while it is on the way, is it inevitably disposed toward the evil of fault. And so that is clear.

### Scholion
**I.** To the question whether a venial sin can become mortal, the common answer is that neither can the numerically same act become mortal from venial, nor do many venial sins of themselves constitute one mortal sin, except dispositively or on the side of consent; but it must be said otherwise of circumstances that gravely change the species. Alex. of Hales, *Summa* p. IV. q. 15. m. 3. a. 1. § 1; Scotus, IV *Sent.* d. 21. n. 3. ff.; III *Sent.* d. 39. q. unica n. 3. f. — St. Thomas, II *Sent.* d. 24. q. 3. a. 7; S. I–II. q. 88. a. 4. — B. Albert, II *Sent.* d. 24. a. 13; *Summa* p. II. tr. 18. q. 117. m. 4. a. 1. — Petr. a Tarantasia, here a. 6. quaestiuncula 3. — Richard of Mediavilla, here a. 2. q. 2. — Dionysius the Carthusian, on this and the following question, here q. 1.

**II.** The following question (2) is understood concerning the absolute necessity of doing penance for venial sins, which some wished to maintain to some degree with Alexander of Hales (loc. cit. m. 3. a. 3. § 5, a. 4. § 2.), relying on this twofold principle: that after this life the fault is not remitted in purgatory but only the punishment, and that a formal act of penance is required for the blotting-out of sin, even of venial sin. But on this question other masters for the most part treat and dispute at length below at d. 21. p. I. a. 1. q. 2, a. 2. q. 1, where in the scholion the other authorities are cited. Yet in this place B. Albert treats the matter, a. 54, 55.
