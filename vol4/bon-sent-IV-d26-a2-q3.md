## Latin
<!-- page 669 -->

### Quaestio III. *Utrum carnalis commixtio sit de integritate matrimonii quoad significationem et efficaciam.*

Tertio quaeritur, utrum carnalis commixtio sit de integritate matrimonii quantum ad *significationem* et *efficaciam*. Et quod sic, videtur:

1. Quia in institutione matrimonii dicitur[^7]: *Erunt duo in carne una*, et Dominus ipse repetit Matthaei decimo nono; sed hoc non est ante commixtionem: ergo commixtio est de integritate.

2. Item, Augustinus dicit, et habetur in littera[^8]: «Non dubium est, illam mulierem ad coniugium non pertinere, cum qua docetur commixtio sexuum non fuisse».

3. Item, *ratione* videtur: quia coniugium est *signum* coniunctionis Christi et Ecclesiae in conformitate naturae[^9]; sed hoc solum est in commixtione: ergo etc.

4. Item, matrimonium est institutum in *remedium* concupiscentiae; sed hoc remedium habetur ab his solum, qui matrimonio[^10] sunt coniuncti: ergo hoc remedium non est per continentiam, sed per commixtionem, quia aliter non esset magis remedium nubere quam non nubere.

**Contra:** Quod non sit de integritate quantum ad *significationem*, videtur:

1. Quia perfectum matrimonium fuit inter Adam et Evam ante cognitionem carnalem, et ante dixit[^11]: *Hoc nunc os*, quam eam cognosceret: ergo commixtio non est de integritate.

2. Item, perfectum matrimonium fuit inter Mariam et Ioseph, quia fuit ibi bonum prolis, fidei et Sacramenti; et sine commixtione[^12]: ergo etc.

<!-- page 670 -->

Item, quod non sit de integritate in ratione remedii, videtur:

3. Quia Augustinus[^1d] dicit, quod «sanctior est copula mentium quam corporalis»: ergo maior datur ibi efficacia contra concupiscentiam in unione mentali quam carnali.

4. Item, in actu illo efficitur homo totus caro: ergo per oppositum, cum gratia, quae est remedium contra concupiscentiam, faciat spiritualem, nunquam datur in illo actu.

5. Item, in actu illo satisfit concupiscentiae; sed dum satisfit ei, ipsa intenditur; sed quod intendit concupiscentiam non est remedium, immo venenum: ergo carnalis commixtio non prodest, immo obest ad remedium matrimonii.

> **Conclusio.** *Copula carnalis non est de integritate matrimonii quoad esse necessitatis, sed solum quoad esse completionis.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *integritas* rei dupliciter accipitur, videlicet quantum ad *esse necessitatis*, vel quantum ad *esse completionis* sive plenitudinis. Si loquamur quantum ad *esse necessitatis*, verum est, quod Sacramentum matrimonii esse habet sine commixtione carnis; si autem quantum ad *esse plenitudinis*, sic est de eius integritate. Et sic procedunt rationes ad partes oppositas.

Et hoc patet sic. In matrimonio enim considerantur haec tria, scilicet *signum*, *officium*, *remedium*. Quantum ad *significationem*, dicendum, quod duplex est eius significatio: significat enim coniunctionem Christi et Ecclesiae per *caritatem* et coniunctionem per *naturae conformitatem*[^2d]. — Similiter duplex est *officium*: unum est *mutuae educationis* et fomenti, et aliud *procreandae prolis*. — Similiter duplex est *remedium*: unum est quantum ad concupiscentiae defluxionem, ut scilicet non in omnem mulierem intendat; aliud quantum ad *inordinationem*, ut scilicet non libidine, sed amore prolis procreandae[^3d] sive matrimoniali affectu mulierem cognoscat.

Primum igitur signatum et primum *officium* et primum *remedium* sunt in Sacramento matrimonii quantum ad coniunctionem *animorum*, secundum autem solum quantum ad coniunctionem *corporum*. Et primum est de substantia matrimonii quantum ad *esse necessarium*; secundum quantum ad *esse plenum*.

Et sic patet responsio ad utramque partem, distinguendo perfectionem duplicem sive duplicem integritatem sufficientiae et plenitudinis. Et secundum hoc distinguunt iuristae[^4d] matrimonium, scilicet matrimonium *ratum* et matrimonium *consummatum*.

Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur, quod gratia non datur in illo actu, ergo non est remedium contra peccatum; dicendum, quod remedium aliquod contra peccatum est dupliciter: aut quia liberat a peccato commisso, et hoc est per gratiam, quae in eo confertur; quoddam, quod liberat a peccato, quod committeretur alias, et tale remedium est matrimonium, quia multi infirmi urerentur concupiscentia, nisi haberent uxorem, quam cognoscendo carnis adustionem evadunt; et in tali quantum ad *usum* non est necesse gratiam dari, quamvis in actu illo possit homo mereri, sicut habebitur infra[^5d].

Ad illud vero quod ultimo obiicitur, quod non est in remedium, immo contra, quia intendit concupiscentiam in satisfaciendo ei; dicendum, quod qui cognoscit uxorem *debite* concupiscentiae non satisfacit, sed potius obviat, ne uratur; et ideo non intenditur, sed remittitur in eo fervor libidinis. Sed qui cognoscit, ut *delectetur*, non cognoscit matrimonialiter; et talis non habet remedium, immo praestat concupiscentiae fomentum[^6d].

## English

### Question III. *Whether carnal intercourse belongs to the integrity of marriage as to signification and efficacy.*

Thirdly it is asked whether carnal intercourse belongs to the integrity of marriage as to *signification* and *efficacy*. And that it does, seems [to be shown]:

1. Because in the institution of marriage it is said[^7]: *They shall be two in one flesh*, and the Lord himself repeats it in Matthew nineteen; but this is not [so] before intercourse: therefore intercourse belongs to the integrity.

2. Likewise, Augustine says, and it is had in the text [of the Master][^8]: «There is no doubt that that woman does not belong to the marriage, with whom no intercourse of the sexes is taught to have taken place».

3. Likewise, it seems [so] by *reason*: because marriage is a *sign* of the conjunction of Christ and the Church in a conformity of nature[^9]; but this [conjunction] is only in intercourse: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, marriage was instituted as a *remedy* for concupiscence; but this remedy is had only by those who are joined by marriage[^10]: therefore this remedy is not through continence, but through intercourse, since otherwise it would not be more of a remedy to marry than not to marry.

**On the contrary:** That it does not belong to the integrity as to *signification*, seems [to be shown]:

1. Because there was a perfect marriage between Adam and Eve before carnal knowledge, and beforehand he said[^11]: *This now is bone*, before he knew her: therefore intercourse does not belong to the integrity.

2. Likewise, there was a perfect marriage between Mary and Joseph, because there were present there the good of offspring, of fidelity, and of the Sacrament; and [it was] without intercourse[^12]: therefore etc.

<!-- page 670 -->

Likewise, that it does not belong to the integrity in the character of a remedy, seems [to be shown]:

3. Because Augustine[^1d] says that «the coupling of minds is holier than that of bodies»: therefore greater efficacy against concupiscence is given there in the mental union than in the carnal.

4. Likewise, in that act a man is made wholly flesh: therefore, on the opposite side, since grace, which is the remedy against concupiscence, makes [him] spiritual, it is never given in that act.

5. Likewise, in that act concupiscence is satisfied; but while it is satisfied, it is itself intensified; but that which intensifies concupiscence is not a remedy, but rather a poison: therefore carnal intercourse does not profit, but rather harms the remedy of marriage.

> **Conclusion.** *Carnal coupling does not belong to the integrity of marriage as to the being of necessity, but only as to the being of completion.*

**I respond:** It must be said that the *integrity* of a thing is taken in two ways, namely as to the *being of necessity*, or as to the *being of completion* or fullness. If we speak as to the *being of necessity*, it is true that the Sacrament of marriage has its being without carnal intercourse; but if as to the *being of fullness*, then it does belong to its integrity. And thus the reasons for the opposite sides proceed.

And this is made clear thus. For in marriage these three are considered, namely *sign*, *office*, *remedy*. As to *signification*, it must be said that its signification is twofold: for it signifies the conjunction of Christ and the Church through *charity*, and the conjunction through *conformity of nature*[^2d]. — Likewise the *office* is twofold: one is of *mutual upbringing* and fostering, and the other of *procreating offspring*. — Likewise the *remedy* is twofold: one is as to the outflow of concupiscence, namely that a man not direct himself upon every woman; the other as to *disorder*, namely that he know the woman not from lust, but from love of offspring to be procreated[^3d] or from marital affection.

The first thing signified, therefore, and the first *office* and the first *remedy* are in the Sacrament of marriage as to the conjunction of *minds*, but the second only as to the conjunction of *bodies*. And the first belongs to the substance of marriage as to *necessary being*; the second as to *full being*.

And thus the response to both sides is made clear, by distinguishing a twofold perfection or a twofold integrity, of sufficiency and of fullness. And according to this the jurists[^4d] distinguish marriage, namely marriage *ratified* and marriage *consummated*.

To that, then, which is objected—that grace is not given in that act, therefore it is not a remedy against sin—it must be said that a remedy against sin is [such] in two ways: either because it frees from a sin already committed, and this is through the grace which is conferred in it; [or there is] a certain [remedy] which frees from a sin that would otherwise be committed, and such a remedy is marriage, since many weak persons would burn with concupiscence unless they had a wife, by knowing whom they escape the burning of the flesh; and in such [a remedy], as to *use*, it is not necessary that grace be given, although in that act a man can merit, as will be had below[^5d].

But to that which is objected last—that it is not for a remedy, but rather against [it], because it intensifies concupiscence in satisfying it—it must be said that he who knows his wife *duly* does not satisfy concupiscence, but rather withstands it, lest he burn; and therefore it is not intensified, but the fervor of lust is remitted in him. But he who knows [her] in order that he may *take pleasure*, does not know [her] matrimonially; and such a one does not have the remedy, but rather furnishes fuel to concupiscence[^6d].
