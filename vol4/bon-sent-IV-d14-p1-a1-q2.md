# IV Sent., d. 14, p. 1, a. 1, q. 2
## *Utrum poenitentia sit virtus generalis, an specialis*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 320 -->

### Quaestio II. *Utrum poenitentia sit virtus generalis, an specialis.*

Secundo quaeritur, utrum poenitentia sit virtus generalis, an specialis. Et ostenditur, quod generalis:

1. Augustinus in libro *de Quinque Responsionibus*[^1]: « Poenitentia est quaedam res optima et perfecta, omnes defectus revocans ad perfectum »; sed alio habitu restauratur defectus concupiscibilis et irascibilis: ergo poenitentia respicit omnes habitus. Sed hoc non posset facere, si esset specialis virtus: ergo etc.

2. Item, *ratione* videtur; quia eiusdem virtutis, cuius est delectari in aliquo, est tristari et detestari oppositum; sed continentiae est delectari de actu castitatis[^2]: ergo eiusdem est detestari actum incontinentiae, et sic de aliis. Sed poenitens detestatur omne peccatum: ergo etc.

3. Item, « habitus cognoscuntur per actus, et actus per obiecta »[^3]; sed obiectum poenitentiae est peccatum in communi, quia ipsa omne peccatum detestatur et omne peccatum expellit: ergo poenitentia est virtus generalis et communis.

**Contra:** 1. Specialis virtutis est diligere bonum, quia bonum, scilicet caritatis[^4]: ergo a simili, specialis virtutis est odire sive detestari malum, quia malum; sed hoc est poenitentiae: ergo etc.

2. Item, specialis doni est refugere malum faciendum sive declinare a malo, ut timoris[^5]: ergo specialis virtutis est refugere malum iam factum; sed hoc est poenitentiae: ergo etc.

3. Item, poenitentia ordinatur ad unum actum tantum liberi arbitrii, non ad omnes, quia solum ad poenitere et dolere: ergo etc.

> **Conclusio.** Poenitentia est virtus specialis.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod hic fuit multiplex positio. — Quidam enim voluerunt dicere, quod est virtus generalis, et est pars iustitiae generalis, cuius est facere bonum et declinare a malo[^6]. Unde sicut facere omne bonum est iustitiae generalis, sic declinare ab omni malo facto est iustitiae generalis; et hoc est poenitentiae: et ideo poenitentia est pars

<!-- page 321 -->

iustitiae generalis. Et quoniam iustitia generalis dicitur non per *praedicationem*, sed per quandam virtutum *aggregationem*, quia comprehendit multas virtutes — sicut una vinea multas vites, ut dicit Chrysostomus[^7], sic poenitentia comprehendit omnes virtutes — quando poenitet de malo luxuriae, tangit chordam continentiae; quando de malo superbiae, chordam humilitatis; ut sit venter conscientiae resonans in fletu quasi cithara, secundum illud Isaiae decimo sexto[^8]: *Venter meus ad Moab quasi cithara sonabit*; quia, si secundum omnes virtutes flenda sunt peccata aliena, multo magis propria.

Sed haec positio non debet stare: quia, etsi poenitentia respiciat malum in *communi*, tamen respicit sub *propria ratione* et habet *actum specialem*, qui indiget virtute non tantum praecipiente, sed eliciente; ut, sicut *ira per zelum*, quae detestatur omne peccatum in proximo, est specialis virtus, quia respicit illud sub ratione digni poenae; et *obedientia* specialis virtus, quia respicit exsequendum sub ratione mandati[^9]: sic etiam poenitentia specialis virtus, quia respicit sub ratione mali proprii a se contra Deum perpetrati, prout homo ex hoc dignus est puniri.

1. Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur in contrarium, quod effectum habet generalem; dicendum, quod illud est verum ratione *connexionis virtutum*, quia omnes defectus reparat; et magis est verum de ipsa quam de alia, quia aliorum actus est in *formando*[^10], actus poenitentiae in *reformando*.

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod eiusdem virtutis est gaudere de aliquo et de opposito tristari; dicendum, quod verum est *imperative*, sed non *elicitive*. Et hoc patet, quia caritas dictat et imperat fugere peccatum: sed tamen timor elicit motum fugae. Alterius enim virtutis est imperare, et alterius elicere, ut obedientia dictat Deo obediendum in hoc praecepto[^11]: *Non moechaberis*, et continentia elicit.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur de obiecto, dicendum, quod etsi non contrahatur ad speciale peccatum, tamen illud considerat sub *speciali et propria ratione*, hoc est, in quantum a se perpetratum et Dei offensivum, cui vult reconciliari et emendam facere; ideo est virtus specialis, quia virtus ponitur generalis vel specialis, non ratione obiecti, quod est *materiale*, sed secundum quod habet rationem *motivi*[^12].

## English

### Question II. *Whether penance is a general or a special virtue.*

Secondly it is asked, whether penance is a general or a special virtue. And it is shown that it is general:

1. Augustine in the book *On the Five Responses*[^1]: "Penance is a certain most excellent and perfect thing, recalling all defects to perfection"; but it is by another habit that the defect of the concupiscible and the irascible [power] is restored: therefore penance regards all habits. But it could not do this, if it were a special virtue: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, it appears *by reason*; for it belongs to the same virtue whose [property] it is to take delight in something, to be saddened by and to detest its opposite; but it belongs to continence to take delight in the act of chastity[^2]: therefore it belongs to the same [virtue] to detest the act of incontinence, and so of the others. But the penitent detests every sin: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, "habits are known through acts, and acts through objects"[^3]; but the object of penance is sin in general, because it itself detests every sin and expels every sin: therefore penance is a general and common virtue.

**On the contrary:** 1. It belongs to a special virtue to love the good, because it is good, namely [to the virtue] of charity[^4]: therefore by similarity, it belongs to a special virtue to hate or detest evil, because it is evil; but this belongs to penance: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, it belongs to a special gift to flee from doing evil, or to turn away from evil, as [it belongs] to fear[^5]: therefore it belongs to a special virtue to flee from evil already done; but this belongs to penance: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, penance is ordered to only one act of free choice, not to all, because [it is ordered] only to repenting and grieving: therefore etc.

> **Conclusion.** Penance is a special virtue.

**I respond:** It must be said that here there was a manifold position. — For some wished to say that it is a general virtue, and is a part of general justice, whose [property] it is to do good and turn away from evil[^6]. Hence just as to do every good belongs to general justice, so to turn away from every evil deed belongs to general justice; and this belongs to penance: and therefore penance is a part

<!-- page 321 -->

of general justice. And since general justice is so called not by *predication*, but by a certain *aggregation* of virtues, because it comprehends many virtues — just as one vineyard [comprehends] many vines, as Chrysostom says[^7], so penance comprehends all virtues — when one repents of the evil of lust, it touches the chord of continence; when of the evil of pride, the chord of humility; so that the belly of conscience may resound in weeping like a lyre, according to that [word] of Isaiah, the sixteenth [chapter][^8]: *My belly shall sound like a lyre for Moab*; because, if according to all the virtues the sins of others are to be wept over, much more one's own.

But this position ought not to stand: because, although penance regards evil in *general*, nevertheless it regards [it] under its *proper account* and has a *special act*, which requires a virtue not only commanding but eliciting; so that, just as *anger through zeal*, which detests every sin in one's neighbor, is a special virtue, because it regards it under the account of [being] deserving of punishment; and *obedience* [is] a special virtue, because it regards the carrying out [of a command] under the account of a mandate[^9]: so too penance [is] a special virtue, because it regards [sin] under the account of an evil proper [to oneself], perpetrated by oneself against God, inasmuch as man is on this account deserving of punishment.

1. To the objection therefore which is raised on the contrary, that it has a general effect; it must be said that this is true by reason of the *connection of the virtues*, because it repairs all defects; and it is more true of it than of another [virtue], because the act of others is in *forming*[^10], but the act of penance in *reforming*.

2. To the objection which is raised, that it belongs to the same virtue to rejoice over something and to be saddened by its opposite; it must be said that this is true *commandingly*, but not *elicitively*. And this is clear, because charity dictates and commands the fleeing of sin: but nevertheless fear elicits the motion of flight. For it belongs to one virtue to command, and to another to elicit, as obedience dictates that God be obeyed in this precept[^11]: *Thou shalt not commit adultery*, and continence elicits [the act].

3. To the objection which is raised concerning the object, it must be said that although it is not contracted to a special sin, nevertheless it considers it under a *special and proper account*, that is, inasmuch as [it is] perpetrated by oneself and offensive to God, with whom one wishes to be reconciled and to make amends; therefore it is a special virtue, because a virtue is reckoned general or special, not by reason of the object, which is *material*, but according as it has the account of a *motive*[^12].
