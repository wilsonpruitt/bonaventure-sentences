## Latin
<!-- page 80 -->

### Articulus II. *De baptismi instituti administratione.*

Quantum ad secundum articulum quaeritur secundo de baptismi instituti administratione, quae fit in parvuli immersione; et circa hoc duo quaeruntur.

Primo quaeritur, quoties oporteat mergi.

Secundo, quantum.

### Quaestio I. *Quoties baptizandus debeat immergi.*

Et quod semel tantum sit immergendum, videtur:

1. De Consecratione, distinctione quarta[^3] ex Concilio Toletano: «Propter vitandi schismatis scandalum, vel haeretici dogmatis usum, simplam teneamus baptismatis immersionem»: ergo videtur, quod haereticum sit ter mergere.

2. Item, ratione videtur, quia haec est vera per essentiam: baptismus est ablutio vel tinctio in aqua[^4]: ergo si fit triplex tinctio, triplex erit baptismus: ergo si nullo modo licet baptismum iterari, nullo modo licet ter mergi.

3. Item, ista duo sufficiunt, verbum scilicet et elementum[^5]; et si mergatur semel, et si proferatur semel, est plenum Sacramentum: ergo si amplius mergitur, superfluit: ergo non debet nisi semel mergi.

Item, quaeritur, quare magis ter quam bis. Si dicas, quod binarius est numerus infamis, quia recedit ab unitate[^6]; eadem ratione laudabilis, quia accedit maxime.

**Sed contra:**

1. Hieronymus super Epistolam ad Ephesios[^7]: «In Patrem et Filium et Spiritum sanctum baptizamur et ter mergimur, ut Trinitatis unum appareat Sacramentum».

2. Item, Pelagius Gaudentio episcopo, de Consecratione, distinctione quarta[^8]: «Evangelicum praeceptum nos admonet, in nomine Trinitatis trina etiam immersione sanctum baptisma cuique tribuere».

3. Item, de Consecratione, distinctione quarta[^9], ex canonibus Apostolorum: «Si quis presbyter aut episcopus non trinam immersionem unius mysterii celebret, sed semel tantum mergat in baptismate, quod dari a quibusdam in morte Domini perhibetur, deponatur»: ergo necessaria est trina immersio.

### Conclusio.

> Unitas et trinitas immersionum non sunt de necessitate baptismi, sed de congruitate; magis tamen congruit trinitas secundum morem Ecclesiae Romanae.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod unitas vel multitudo immersionum non est de substantia baptismi,
<!-- page 81 -->
sed solum de bene esse, quia, quotiescumque immergat, dum tamen debito modo et sine dilatione magna verba[^1bis] proferat, baptismus est et Sacramentum integrum. Etsi non sit ei de necessitate, est tamen de congruitate propter significationem, cui congruit unitas et trinitas — ut patet in fide Trinitatis, quia unitas est in essentia, sed trinitas in personis; similiter in morte Christi, quia una est in essentia, sed tribus diebus fuit in corde terrae[^2bis]; similiter gratiae, quia una est in se, sed triplex in virtutibus unitas et trinitas convenit — sed tamen magis trinitas propter expressionem maiorem; et ideo utrumque morem servat Ecclesia. Sed Romana Ecclesia tanquam praecellens trinam servat immersionem, tum propter significationem Trinitatis, tum etiam triduanae sepulturae, tum etiam triplicis concupiscentiae[^3bis], a qua anima liberatur, tum etiam propter triplicem virtutem, qua imago in anima reformatur.

Si quaeritur, quoties sit immergendum; respondeo: secundum morem Ecclesiae; qui autem propria auctoritate mutat, peccat. Et ideo dicitur[^4bis], quod deponendus est episcopus, non quia non baptizaret, sed quia propria auctoritate nova introduceret. — Rationes igitur probantes, quod ter mergendum sit, intelligendae sunt secundum morem Ecclesiae Romanae.

**Ad illud quod ad oppositum primo obiicitur,** dicendum, quod loquitur secundum morem illius Ecclesiae, in qua tantum solebat semel mergi; sed haeretici inceperunt immutare[^5bis]: ideo statuerunt tenere, ne viderentur haeresi consentire.

**Ad illud quod obiicitur 2. 3. 4.,** quod baptismus est tinctio; dicendum, quod non est per essentiam, nisi addatur forma praescripta; si tamen tinctio fiat cum invocatione Trinitatis, baptisma est. Et si in una protulit omnia verba, non debet amplius mergere, quia baptisma consummatum est. Sed quando fit trina immersio, in prima debet nominari Pater, in secunda Filius, in tertia Spiritus sanctus; et[^6bis] tunc nulla superfluit, quia non est completum Sacramentum quousque terminatum sit verbum; incipitur enim immersio cum verbo et cum illo terminatur. — Sequens patet; similiter, quare magis ter quam bis, et cetera obiecta.

### Scholion
**I.** Circa duas huius articuli quaestiones observandum est, quod consuetudo baptizandi per ablutionem vix ante saec. XIII. in Ecclesia Romana latius diffusa est. Magister Sententiarum solius immersionis meminit, et B. Albert. (hic a. 5. quaestiunc. 3.) de nunc recepta consuetudine ad mentem S. Bonaventurae ait: «Non mihi videtur esse laudabilis, sed immergendi consuetudo laudabilior est». Adhuc Durandus docet, congruentius baptismum fieri per trinam immersionem. Omnes autem censent, unumquemque debere consuetudinem suae Ecclesiae in ritu baptizandi observare. — Ritum ablutionis nunc in Ecclesia Latina communem esse, notissimum est.

**De utraque quaestione:** Alex. Hal., S. p. IV. q. 8. m. 3. a. 1. — Scot., IV. Sent. d. 6. q. 1. n. 2. et passim. — S. Thom., hic q. 1. a. 4; S. III. q. 66. a. 7. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 3. a. 2. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 1. q. 3. — Durand., hic q. 1. — Dionys. Carth., hic q. 4.

## English

### Article II. *On the administration of the instituted baptism.*

As to the second article, inquiry is made secondly concerning the administration of the instituted baptism, which is done in the immersion of the infant; and concerning this two questions are raised.

First it is asked, how often one ought to be immersed.

Second, how much.

### Question I. *How often the one to be baptized ought to be immersed.*

And that one ought to be immersed only once, it seems:

1. From *On Consecration*, distinction four[^3], from the Council of Toledo: "For the sake of avoiding the scandal of schism, or the practice of heretical teaching, let us hold to a single immersion of baptism": therefore it seems that to immerse three times is heretical.

2. Likewise, it seems by reason, because this is true by essence: baptism is an ablution or dipping in water[^4]: therefore if a threefold dipping is made, there will be a threefold baptism: therefore if in no way is it permitted that baptism be repeated, in no way is it permitted to immerse three times.

3. Likewise, these two suffice, namely the word and the element[^5]; and if one be immersed once, and if it be pronounced once, the Sacrament is complete: therefore if there is further immersion, it is superfluous: therefore one ought to be immersed only once.

Likewise, it is asked why three times rather than twice. If you say that the number two is an infamous number, because it recedes from unity[^6]; by the same reasoning it is praiseworthy, because it draws nearest.

**On the contrary:**

1. Jerome on the Letter to the Ephesians[^7]: "Into the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit we are baptized and immersed three times, that the one Sacrament of the Trinity may appear."

2. Likewise, Pelagius to Gaudentius the bishop, *On Consecration*, distinction four[^8]: "The evangelical precept admonishes us to confer holy baptism upon each one in the name of the Trinity and also by a threefold immersion."

3. Likewise, *On Consecration*, distinction four[^9], from the canons of the Apostles: "If any presbyter or bishop should not celebrate the threefold immersion of the one mystery, but should immerse only once in baptism — which is reported to be conferred by some in the death of the Lord — let him be deposed": therefore a threefold immersion is necessary.

### Conclusion.

> The unity and the trinity of the immersions are not of the necessity of baptism, but of fittingness; yet the trinity is more fitting according to the custom of the Roman Church.

**I respond:** It must be said that the unity or multitude of immersions is not of the substance of baptism,
but only of its well-being, because, however often one immerses, provided that one pronounces the great words[^1bis] in the due manner and without delay, it is a baptism and a complete Sacrament. Although it is not of necessity to it, it is nevertheless of fittingness on account of the signification, to which unity and trinity are fitting — as is plain in the faith of the Trinity, because the unity is in the essence, but the trinity in the persons; likewise in the death of Christ, because it is one in essence, but for three days He was in the heart of the earth[^2bis]; likewise of grace, because it is one in itself, but threefold in the virtues — unity and trinity agree, but nevertheless the trinity rather, on account of the greater expression; and therefore the Church observes both customs. But the Roman Church, as preeminent, keeps the threefold immersion, both on account of the signification of the Trinity, and also of the three-day burial, and also of the threefold concupiscence[^3bis], from which the soul is freed, and also on account of the threefold virtue by which the image in the soul is reformed.

If it is asked how often one ought to be immersed; I respond: according to the custom of the Church; but whoever changes it by his own authority, sins. And therefore it is said[^4bis] that a bishop is to be deposed, not because he would not baptize, but because by his own authority he would introduce something new. — The reasons, therefore, proving that one ought to immerse three times are to be understood according to the custom of the Roman Church.

**To that which is objected in the first place to the contrary,** it must be said that it speaks according to the custom of that Church in which it was customary to immerse only once; but heretics began to change[^5bis] it: therefore they decreed to hold to it, lest they should seem to consent to heresy.

**To that which is objected, 2, 3, 4,** that baptism is a dipping; it must be said that it is not so by essence, unless the prescribed form be added; yet if the dipping be made with the invocation of the Trinity, it is baptism. And if in one immersion one pronounced all the words, one ought not to immerse further, because the baptism is consummated. But when a threefold immersion is made, in the first the Father ought to be named, in the second the Son, in the third the Holy Spirit; and[^6bis] then none is superfluous, because the Sacrament is not complete until the word is finished; for the immersion is begun with the word and is finished with it. — What follows is plain; likewise, why three times rather than twice, and the other objections.

### Scholion
**I.** Concerning the two questions of this article it must be observed that the custom of baptizing by aspersion was scarcely diffused more widely in the Roman Church before the thirteenth century. The Master of the Sentences mentions immersion alone, and Blessed Albert (here a. 5, quaestiunc. 3), concerning the now-received custom, says, in agreement with the mind of St. Bonaventure: "It does not seem to me to be praiseworthy, but the custom of immersing is more praiseworthy." Further, Durandus teaches that baptism is more fittingly done by a threefold immersion. But all hold that each one ought to observe the custom of his own Church in the rite of baptizing. — That the rite of aspersion is now common in the Latin Church is most well known.

**On both questions:** Alexander of Hales, S. p. IV. q. 8. m. 3. a. 1. — Scotus, IV. Sent. d. 6. q. 1. n. 2. and passim. — St. Thomas, here q. 1. a. 4; S. III. q. 66. a. 7. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 3. a. 2. — Richard of Mediavilla, here a. 1. q. 3. — Durandus, here q. 1. — Dionysius the Carthusian, here q. 4.
