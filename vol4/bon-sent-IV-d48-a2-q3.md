### Articulus II. *De innovatione corporum superiorum.*
### Quaestio III. *Utrum elementa innovabuntur.*

> **Note on the apparatus numbering.** Quaracchi restarts its footnote numbering on every printed page. The labels below are page-qualified. This chunk anchors notes 4–10 of page 992 (notes 1–3 of page 992 anchor in the preceding question, `d48-a2-q2`) and notes 1–8 of page 993.

Tertio quaeritur, utrum innovabuntur elementa. Et quod sic, videtur.

1. Primae ad Corinthios septimo[^p992-4]: *Praeterit figura huius mundi*; aut igitur in *nihil*, aut in *melius*, aut in *peius*. In *nihil* non, quia Deus nihil permittit omnino perire; non in *peius*, quia, si homo melioratur, et mundus: ergo in *melius*. Sed quod senescit et in *melius* mutatur innovatur, ergo mundus iste; sed elementa sunt partes mundi: ergo etc.

2. Item, Augustinus, *de Ecclesiasticis Dogmatibus*[^p992-5]: « Elementa non dicimus abolenda, sed in melius commutanda ».

3. Item, *ratione* videtur: quia aut innovabuntur *omnia*, aut *nulla*, aut *quaedam*. Si *omnia*, habeo propositum; si *nulla*: ergo erunt in continua transmutatione et impuritate, quam contraxerunt; sed hoc est inconveniens, cum sint propter hominem incorruptibilem et omnino mundum[^p992-6]. Si *quaedam* sic, et *quaedam* non: ergo solvitur harmonia elementorum: ergo et pulcritudo et decor universi.

4. Item, ignis est completissimum elementorum, unde et *species elementorum* dicitur. Quaero igitur: aut ignis *innovabitur*, aut *non*. Si *non* innovatur, sed consumitur: ergo perit nobilitas et decor elementorum. Si *innovatur*: ergo cum alia elementa magis sint accedentia ad vetustatem, videtur, quod omnia debeant innovari[^p992-7].

**Sed contra:**
1. Matthaei vigesimo quarto[^p992-8]: *Caelum et terra transibunt*: si ergo terra est stabilissimum inter elementa, et ipsa non innovatur, sed transit; nec alia innovabuntur. *Si dicas*, quod non transit quantum ad *substantiam*, sed quantum ad *figuram*, sicut dicitur primae ad Corinthios septimo: *Praeterit figura huius mundi*; tunc ergo, cum *figura* respiciat quantitatem et dispositionem partium, non qualitatem; videtur ergo, quod *alterius figurae* erit universum. Cum ergo modo sit circulare, tunc erit non circulare; sed hoc est inconveniens: ergo etc.

2. Item, super secundae Petri ultimum[^p992-9] dicit Glossa: « Elementa quatuor, quibus mundus consistit, ille maximus ignis absumet, nec cuncta in tantum consumet, ut non sint; sed duo ex toto consumet, duo vero in meliorem restituet faciem ».

3. Item, *ratione* videtur: quia, si elementa innovabuntur, aut ergo quantum ad *substantiam*, aut quantum ad *qualitatem*. Non quantum ad *substantiam*, quia substantia erit eadem, « non recipiens magis et minus[^p992-10] »; non quantum ad *qualitates*, quia

<!-- page 993 -->

accidentia sunt inalterabilia: ergo nullo modo. *Si dicas*, quod quantum ad *qualitates* erit innovatio, quia qualitates istae transibunt; *contra*: forma substantialis in materia est causa immediata ipsarum qualitatum elementarium: ergo si, « posita causa immediata, necessario ponitur effectus »; sed remanebit forma substantialis in materia: ergo et qualitates.

4. Item, calidum in summo est dispositio ad formam ignis; sed remota dispositione et privata, per consequens privatur forma: ergo etc.

### Conclusio

> *Elementorum et substantia et qualitates remanebunt; sed qualitates quoad substantiam remanentes innovabuntur quoad rationem corruptionis.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod ubi est *innovatio*, substantia remanet eadem, sed sub aliis proprietatibus; ideo dicendum, quod omnia elementa secundum *substantiam* remanebunt, sed quantum ad *qualitates* innovabuntur. — Sed circa ipsas *qualitates* duplex est opinio.

Quidam enim dicunt, quod tria sunt genera qualitatum in elementis: quaedam, secundum quas *ordinantur in mundo*, ut gravitas et levitas; et istae remanebunt, quia remanebunt in mundo ordo et situs elementorum. Quaedam sunt qualitates, secundum quas est *receptio influentiae luminis* sine omni corruptione, ut perspicuitas et transparentia; et istae similiter remanebunt, sicut influentia luminis. Quaedam autem sunt, secundum quas *ordinantur ad agendum et patiendum*, et ita per consequens ad transmutationem et corruptionem; et secundum has non remanebunt. Sed inter has quaedam sunt *activae*, ut caliditas et frigiditas; quaedam *passivae*, ut humiditas et siccitas. — Et quae sunt *activae* sunt illae quae excitant et faciunt transmutationem; et istae tollentur ab elementis et non redibunt in nihil, sed cadent in regionem damnatorum, sicut vult Basilius super illud[^p993-1]: *Vox Domini intercidentis flammam ignis*. Dicit enim, quod « calor cedat in partem damnatorum, sed lux in regionem salvandorum »; similiter et in aqua *frigiditas* ad affligendum; siccum vero et humidum non tollentur, sed terminabuntur. Et ideo dicunt, quod Beda[^p993-2] dicit, quod « duo totaliter absumentur » propter coniunctionem, quae erat ex duplici qualitate, non quantum ad *integritatem substantiae*, sed quantum ad *totalitatem duplicis qualitatis* ratione activae. — Sed quia Augustinus dicit vigesimo *de Civitate Dei*[^p993-3], quod « omnium elementorum qualitates ardendo penitus interibunt », et siccitas et humiditas sunt qualitates corruptibiles; non videtur maior ratio de caliditate et frigiditate quam de aliis duabus.

Ideo dicunt alii aliter, quod omnes res, quantum est de se, appetunt *esse* perpetuum et divinum[^p993-4]; sed *quaedam* formae sunt, quae appetunt continuare *esse* per multiplicationem sui in altero, ut puta sunt formae *activae* accidentales et substantiales; *quaedam* autem appetunt continuare *esse* suum per continuam adhaerentiam cum materia, ut sunt formae et qualitates *passivae*. Quoniam igitur post iudicium auferetur ab elementis omnis ratio multiplicandi se, et dabitur virtus continuandi se in se, quia erunt incorruptibilia; hinc est, quod elementa, quae habent qualitates *activas*, dicuntur *totaliter absumi*, quia omnino auferetur ab eis ratio agendi et se multiplicandi; alia vero duo dicuntur *in meliorem faciem restituenda*[^p993-5], quia appetitus continuandi in se *esse*, qui modo est in eis secundum *esse imperfectum*, complebitur et perficietur. Manebunt ergo qualitates omnium elementorum quantum ad *substantiam*, sed quantum ad *rationem corruptionis* innovabuntur; similiter et formae substantiales[^p993-6]. — Et haec opinio est probabilior.

**Ad opposita:**
*Ad 1.* Et patent obiecta praeter primum, quod dicit, quod figura non transit. Dico enim, quod *figura* dicitur tripliciter: uno modo *dispositio ex clausione linearum*; et haec praeterit quantum ad terram, quia non omnino est orbicularis. Secundo modo dicitur figura *exterior rei facies* sive pulcritudo; et haec transibit, sicut dicitur in Glossa[^p993-7], « non substantia, sed pulcritudo ». Tertio modo figura dicitur *fictio* vel *fragilitas* a *fingo, fingis*, ut vasa figuli; et sic etiam pertransibit. — Et sic patet, quod non transibit *substantia*, sed *dispositio partium* quoad terram et *pulcritudo* et *fragilitas*[^p993-8].

## English

### Article II. *On the renewal of the higher bodies.*
### Question III. *Whether the elements will be renewed.*

Thirdly it is asked, whether the elements will be renewed. And that they will, it seems.

1. First to the Corinthians, chapter seven[^p992-4]: *The figure of this world passeth away*; either therefore into *nothing*, or into something *better*, or into something *worse*. Not into *nothing*, because God permits nothing to perish utterly; not into something *worse*, because, if man is made better, so also the world: therefore into something *better*. But that which grows old and is changed into something *better* is renewed; therefore this world is; but the elements are parts of the world: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, Augustine, *On Ecclesiastical Dogmas*[^p992-5]: « We do not say that the elements are to be abolished, but to be changed into something better ».

3. Likewise, it seems *by reason*: because either *all* will be renewed, or *none*, or *some*. If *all*, I have what I proposed; if *none*: then they will be in continual transmutation and in the impurity which they have contracted; but this is unfitting, since they exist for the sake of a man incorruptible and wholly clean[^p992-6]. If *some* thus, and *some* not: then the harmony of the elements is dissolved: therefore also the beauty and comeliness of the universe.

4. Likewise, fire is the most complete of the elements, whence it is even called the *species of the elements*. I ask therefore: either fire *will be renewed*, or *not*. If it is *not* renewed, but consumed: then the nobility and comeliness of the elements perishes. If it *is* renewed: then, since the other elements are more inclined toward oldness, it seems that all ought to be renewed[^p992-7].

**On the contrary:**
1. Matthew, chapter twenty-four[^p992-8]: *Heaven and earth shall pass away*: if therefore the earth is the most stable among the elements, and it itself is not renewed but passes away, neither will the others be renewed. *If you say* that it does not pass away as to *substance*, but as to *figure*, as is said in the first to the Corinthians, chapter seven: *The figure of this world passeth away*; then therefore, since *figure* regards quantity and the disposition of parts, not quality; it seems therefore that the universe will be *of another figure*. Since therefore it is now circular, then it will be non-circular; but this is unfitting: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, on the last chapter of the second [epistle] of Peter[^p992-9] the Gloss says: « The four elements of which the world consists, that greatest fire will consume; nor will it consume all of them so far that they should not be; but two it will consume wholly, while two it will restore to a better appearance ».

3. Likewise, it seems *by reason*: because, if the elements will be renewed, then either as to *substance*, or as to *quality*. Not as to *substance*, because the substance will be the same, « not receiving more and less[^p992-10] »; not as to *qualities*, because

accidents are unalterable: therefore in no way. *If you say* that as to *qualities* there will be a renewal, because those qualities will pass away; *on the contrary*: the substantial form in matter is the immediate cause of those elementary qualities: therefore if, « the immediate cause being posited, the effect is necessarily posited »; but the substantial form will remain in matter: therefore the qualities also.

4. Likewise, heat in the highest degree is the disposition toward the form of fire; but when the disposition is removed and taken away, the form is consequently taken away: therefore etc.

### Conclusion

> *Both the substance and the qualities of the elements will remain; but the qualities, remaining as to their substance, will be renewed as to their character of corruptibility.*

**I respond:** It must be said that where there is *renewal*, the substance remains the same, but under other properties; therefore it must be said that all the elements will remain according to *substance*, but as to *qualities* they will be renewed. — But concerning those *qualities* there is a twofold opinion.

For some say that there are three kinds of qualities in the elements: certain ones according to which they are *ordered within the world*, such as heaviness and lightness; and these will remain, because the order and the situation of the elements will remain in the world. There are certain qualities according to which there is a *reception of the influence of light* without any corruption, such as clarity and transparency; and these likewise will remain, just as the influence of light does. And there are certain ones according to which they are *ordered to acting and to being acted upon*, and thus consequently to transmutation and corruption; and according to these they will not remain. But among these some are *active*, such as heat and cold; some *passive*, such as moisture and dryness. — And those which are *active* are those which stir up and effect transmutation; and these will be taken away from the elements, and will not return into nothing, but will fall into the region of the damned, as Basil holds upon that text[^p993-1]: *The voice of the Lord divideth the flame of fire*. For he says that « the heat should go over to the portion of the damned, but the light into the region of those to be saved »; and likewise in water the *cold* [goes] to afflicting; but the dry and the moist will not be taken away, but will be brought to their term. And therefore they say that Bede[^p993-2] says that « two will be wholly consumed » on account of the conjunction which arose from the twofold quality, not as to the *integrity of the substance*, but as to the *totality of the twofold quality* by reason of the active one. — But since Augustine says in the twentieth [book] *On the City of God*[^p993-3] that « the qualities of all the elements will utterly perish in the burning », and dryness and moisture are corruptible qualities; there seems to be no greater reason concerning heat and cold than concerning the other two.

Therefore others say otherwise, that all things, so far as in them lies, desire a perpetual and divine *being*[^p993-4]; but there are *certain* forms which desire to continue their *being* through the multiplication of themselves in another, as for instance the *active* forms, both accidental and substantial; while *certain* others desire to continue their *being* through a continual adherence to matter, as are the *passive* forms and qualities. Since therefore after the judgment every ground of multiplying themselves will be taken away from the elements, and there will be given a power of continuing themselves in themselves, because they will be incorruptible; hence it is that the elements which have *active* qualities are said to be *wholly consumed*, because there will be entirely taken away from them the ground of acting and of multiplying themselves; while the other two are said to be *restored to a better appearance*[^p993-5], because the appetite of continuing their *being* in themselves, which is now in them according to an *imperfect being*, will be completed and perfected. The qualities of all the elements will therefore remain as to *substance*, but as to their *character of corruptibility* they will be renewed; and likewise the substantial forms[^p993-6]. — And this opinion is the more probable.

**To the opposing arguments:**
*Ad 1.* And the objections are clear except the first, which says that the figure does not pass away. For I say that *figure* is spoken of in three ways: in one way as the *disposition arising from the enclosure of lines*; and this passes away as regards the earth, because it is not wholly spherical. In a second way figure is called the *outer face of a thing* or its beauty; and this will pass away, as is said in the Gloss[^p993-7], « not the substance, but the beauty ». In a third way figure is called *fashioning* or *frailty*, from *fingo, fingis*, as the potter's vessels; and thus too it will pass away. — And thus it is clear that the *substance* will not pass away, but the *disposition of parts* as regards the earth, and the *beauty* and the *frailty*[^p993-8].
