## Latin

<!-- page 1012 -->

### Articulus I. *De corporis glorificatione.*

### Quaestio I. *Utrum gloria corporis sit de substantia beatitudinis.*

Quantum ergo ad primum proceditur sic, et ostenditur, quod gloria corporis sit de beatitudinis substantia sive augmentet gloriam animae:

1. Et primo per auctoritatem Magistri in littera[^p1012-1]: «Sine scrupulo, inquit, credendum est, eos habituros maiorem gloriam post iudicium quam ante, quia maius erit gaudium et amplior cognitio»; sed ista respiciunt essentiam beatitudinis: ergo etc.

2. Item, Bernardus de Diligendo Deo[^p1012-2]: «Anima exuta tamen corpori est coniuncta, etsi non vita vel sensu, tamen affectu, ut sine eo nec possit nec valeat consummari».

3. Item, Augustinus duodecimo super Genesim[^p1012-3] de anima exuta dicit: «Inest ei quidam naturalis appetitus administrandi corpus, quo retardatur, ne tota intentione feratur in illud summum caelum»: ergo si quantitas praemii substantialis consistit in adhaerentia et fruitione illius summi caeli, patet etc.

4. Item, perfecta beatitudo est quies omnium desideriorum[^p1012-4]: si ergo anima appetit naturaliter corpori uniri, et iste appetitus, cum sit naturalis, non possit terminari nisi per unionem; impossibile est, animam perfecte beatificari, quousque resumat corpus.

**Contra:** 1. Anima gaudet nunc de Deo, post iudicium vero non tantum gaudebit de Deo, sed etiam de bonis corporis; sed Deus est bonum infinitum, bonum corporis est bonum finitum; sed finitum additum super infinitum nihil auget[^p1012-5]: ergo bonum corporis non auget praemium: ergo etc.

2. Item, tanta gratia est in anima exuta, quantae gratiae capax est, cum est coniuncta: ergo similiter videtur de gloria.

3. Item, cum Deus se offerat modo animae in aperta visione, sicut post iudicium, quare non est ita modo perfecta gloria, sicut post iudicium? — *Si dicas*, quod propter appetitum administrandi corpus; *contra*: Bernardus, de Diligendo Deo[^p1012-6], de beatis animabus dicit, «quod ita inebriantur Deo, ut obliviscantur sui»: ergo si sui obliviscuntur, multo magis corporis.

4. Item, maiores motus absorbent minores: ergo cum motus amoris in Deum improportionaliter sit maior quam in corpus, ergo totaliter absorbet illum.

5. Item, appetitus administrandi corpus impedit, ergo multo fortius administratio: si enim amor alicuius potest retrahere a Deo, cum est absens, multo fortius, cum est praesens.

6. Item, corpus nunquam fiet spiritus, quamvis fiat spirituale[^p1012-7]: ergo liberior est spiritus a corpore absolutus quam alligatus: ergo melius dispositus ad suscipiendam beatitudinem perfectam.

### Conclusio

> *Glorificatio corporis facit ad maius gaudium quoad extensionem et etiam quoad intensionem, quatenus removetur impedimentum, quin tamen augeatur habitus.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *beatitudo* dicitur gaudium per essentiam; gaudium autem potest dici maius quadrupliciter: aut quia de *maiori obiecto*, aut quia ex *maiori habitu*, aut quia de *pluribus*, aut quia ex *eodem habitu intensius*. Primo modo maius gaudium, quia de maiori[^p1012-8], non reperitur nec respectu *eiusdem* nec respectu *diversorum*, quia quilibet gaudet de bono infinito. — Secundo modo maius invenitur respectu *diversorum*, quia maiori habitu gaudet Petrus quam Linus; sed non reperitur respectu *eiusdem* quantum ad *diversa tempora*, quia non habebit maiorem habitum Petrus post iudicium, quam habet nunc. — Tertio modo maius, quia latius et de pluribus, reperitur in *eodem* secundum *diversa tempora*, quia Petrus de pluribus gaudebit post iudicium quam nunc, quia de gloria animae et corporis et de gloria omnium salvatorum.

<!-- page 1013 -->

Sed non est maius hoc modo respectu *diversorum*, quia de tot et eisdem gaudebit unus Sanctus parvus, sicut et maximus Sanctus. — Quarto modo maius, quia intensius, reperitur et respectu *eiusdem* et respectu *diversorum*: respectu *diversorum*, quia unus intensius movetur quam alter; respectu *eiusdem*, quia ex eodem habitu intensius movebitur anima, resumto corpore[^p1013-1], quia nullum habebit retardans.

Concedendum est igitur, quod glorificatio corporis facit ad maius gaudium quantum ad *extensionem*; facit etiam quantum ad *intensionem*, non augendo habitum, sed removendo impedimentum. Unde non facit ad augmentum praemii essentialis *principaliter*, sed *ex consequenti*. — Et concedendae sunt rationes ad hoc.

*Ad 1. 2.* Ad illud quod obiicitur in contrarium, quod non gaudet de maiori; dicendum, quod ratio[^p1013-2] tenet bene; sed quantum ad hoc non dicitur *maior*, sed *de eodem intensius*. — Similiter, quod secundo obiicit de gratia, patet: quoniam ipsa gratia dicit habitum, et idem etiam habitus gloriae est in anima exuta, et postquam est unita; sed per illum non ita intense movetur, et hoc retardante appetitu.

*Ad 3.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod obliviscitur[^p1013-3] sui; dicendum, quod hoc non intelligitur, quin amet se, sed quod omnis amor privatus, qui non referatur in Deum, ab anima auferetur; et certe anima non appetit corpus amore privato, sed ad gloriam Dei. — Vel dicendum, quod Bernardus loquitur de Beatis post resumtionem secundae stolae.

*Ad 4.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod maiores motus absorbent minores; dicendum, quod verum est, quod absorbent, sed non ita, ut non *sint*, sed ut non ita *intense appareant*; sunt tamen et apparent aliquo modo; et ideo, quamvis parum, tamen aliquo modo minuunt motus minores ipsos maiores, licet vincantur[^p1013-4].

*Ad 5.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod si appetitus impedit, magis praesentia; dicendum, quod *appetitus* duo dicit, scilicet *amorem* alicuius rei et *carentiam* illius. Quando igitur impedit, aut ratione *amoris*, aut *carentiae*. Si ratione *amoris*, tunc magis impedit res praesens; si ratione *carentiae*, tunc non impedit, cum carentia[^p1013-5] per praesentiam removetur.

*Ad 6.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod spiritus absolutus est liberior; dicendum, quod est spiritus a corpore absolutus *substantia et affectu*; et iste est liberrimus. Et est spiritus *alligatus affectu* et *solutus substantia*; et iste non est liber, quia aliquid vult quod non habet. Et est spiritus *alligatus substantia et affectu*; et tunc aut corpus est *inobediens*, et talis non est liber, quia non potest[^p1013-6] quod vult; aut per omnia *obediens*, et talis est liber libertate sibi conveniente, quia nihil vult, nisi quod potest, et habet omne quod vult.

### Scholion

**I.** Quaestio I. a Magistro (hic c. 4.) sic ponitur: «Si maior est beatitudo Sanctorum post iudicium». — *Affirmativa* responsio est communis, si intelligitur, quod ipsa crescat *extensive*. Hoc in Concilio Florentino et Graeci et Latini se credere asseruerunt. Quod autem etiam *intensive* crescat, non quidem per *augmentum habitus*, sed per *remotionem impedimenti* retardantis; docuit S. August. (XII. super Gen. ad lit. c. 35.), non tamen (ut patet ex eiusdem II. Retract. c. 24.) ut omnino certum; quem secuti sunt S. Bernard., Haymo aliique antiqui cum ipso S. Bonav. Primitus etiam S. Thom. (hic q. 1. a. 4. quaestiunc. 1; Suppl. q. 93. a. 1.) eandem sententiam professus est, sed postea, mutata sententia, docuit (S. I. II. q. 4. a. 5. ad 5.), quod «corpore resumto, beatitudo crescit non *intensive*, sed *extensive*»; quod etiam Durand. (hic q. 7.) aliique posteriores propugnant. — Primam vero sententiam tenent Scot., de Rerum principio, q. 9. a. 2. sect. 2. n. 49, et, ut videtur, hic q. 3. n. 9. (aliqui tamen volunt, eum retractasse hanc opinionem), et Richard. (hic a. 2. q. 7.). — Propositio in principio resp. posita, quod per *essentiam* beatitudo sit gaudium sive delectatio, approbatur ab Alexandro Hal. (S. III. q. 23. m. 1. ad 1.) et tanquam probabilis defenditur a Richardo a Med. (hic a. 3. q. 6.). Sed Scot. (hic q. 7; I. Sent. d. 1. q. 3.) docet, quod «stricte accipiendo beatitudinem, delectatio non est de *essentia* eius, quia est *passio*, beatitudo autem consistit in *operatione*» (hic q. 7. n. 4.); prout tamen voluntas est *receptiva*, «perficitur delectatione et gaudio». Idem tenent S. Thom. (hic q. 3. a. 4. quaestiunc. 3; S. I. II. q. 4. a. 1. 2.) et Durand. (hic q. 4. n. 20.). Addit tamen Scotus (n. 6.), quod secundum absolutam potentiam Dei visio eius possit esse sine gaudio; de quo aliter loquitur S. Bonav. supra d. 48. a. 1. q. 2. in fine.

De 2. quaestione alii commentatores Lombardi non tractant specialiter, sed eam solvunt in 1. quaestione, vel potius in quaestionibus de singulis dotibus, ubi notavimus, quomodo Scotus a S. Bonav. et Thoma discedat.

De utraque quaestione praeter locos citt.: Petr. a Tar. rem breviter tangit hic q. 2. a. 4. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 4. q. 1. — Dionys. Carth., hic q. 7.

**II.** De sequenti articulo, qui agit de quatuor dotibus corporis *in generali*, alii commentatores Lombardi, exceptis B. Alberto (d. 44. a. 32.) et Richardo (hic a. 4. q. 1.), specialiter non tractant, sed principia sua ponunt disputantes de eisdem in specie, de quo vide infra sect. 2. — De tribus dotibus *animae* plurima profert S. Thom., hic q. 4. a. 1-5; Suppl. q. 95. a. 1-5.

## English

<!-- page 1012 -->

### Article I. *On the glorification of the body.*

### Question I. *Whether the glory of the body belongs to the substance of beatitude.*

As regards the first, then, one proceeds thus, and it is shown that the glory of the body belongs to the substance of beatitude, or that it increases the glory of the soul:

1. And first through the authority of the Master in the text[^p1012-1]: "Without scruple," he says, "it must be believed that they will have a greater glory after the judgment than before, because the joy will be greater and the knowledge fuller"; but these things regard the essence of beatitude: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, Bernard, *On Loving God*[^p1012-2]: "The soul, though stripped, is nevertheless joined to the body, if not by life or sense, still by affection, so that without it the soul can neither be nor be able to be consummated."

3. Likewise, Augustine, in the twelfth book *On Genesis*[^p1012-3], says of the stripped soul: "There is in it a certain natural appetite for administering the body, by which it is retarded, lest it be borne with its whole intention into that highest heaven": therefore, if the quantity of the substantial reward consists in adherence to and enjoyment of that highest heaven, it is evident etc.

4. Likewise, perfect beatitude is the rest of all desires[^p1012-4]: if therefore the soul naturally desires to be united to the body, and this appetite, since it is natural, cannot be terminated except through union; it is impossible that the soul be perfectly beatified until it takes up the body again.

**On the contrary:** 1. The soul now rejoices in God, but after the judgment it will rejoice not only in God, but also in the goods of the body; but God is an infinite good, the good of the body is a finite good; but the finite added upon the infinite adds nothing[^p1012-5]: therefore the good of the body does not increase the reward: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, there is as much grace in the stripped soul as it is capable of when it is joined [to the body]: therefore the same seems to hold of glory.

3. Likewise, since God offers himself to the soul now in open vision, just as after the judgment, why is the glory not now as perfect as after the judgment? — *If you say* that it is on account of the appetite for administering the body; *on the contrary*: Bernard, *On Loving God*[^p1012-6], says of the blessed souls "that they are so inebriated with God that they forget themselves": therefore, if they forget themselves, much more do they forget the body.

4. Likewise, greater motions absorb lesser ones: therefore, since the motion of love toward God is disproportionately greater than that toward the body, therefore it totally absorbs the latter.

5. Likewise, the appetite for administering the body impedes, therefore much more the administering itself: for if the love of something can draw one back from God when it is absent, much more so when it is present.

6. Likewise, the body will never become spirit, although it becomes spiritual[^p1012-7]: therefore a spirit released from the body is freer than one bound to it: therefore it is better disposed for receiving perfect beatitude.

### Conclusion

> *The glorification of the body makes for a greater joy as regards extension and also as regards intension, inasmuch as an impediment is removed, though the habit is not thereby increased.*

**I respond:** It must be said that *beatitude* is called joy by essence; but joy can be called greater in four ways: either because it is of *a greater object*, or because it is from *a greater habit*, or because it is of *more things*, or because it is *more intense from the same habit*. In the first way a greater joy, namely because of a greater object[^p1012-8], is found neither with respect to *the same person* nor with respect to *different persons*, because each one rejoices in the infinite good. — In the second way a greater joy is found with respect to *different persons*, because Peter rejoices with a greater habit than Linus; but it is not found with respect to *the same person* as regards *different times*, because Peter will not have a greater habit after the judgment than he has now. — In the third way greater, namely because more widely and of more things, it is found in *the same person* according to *different times*, because Peter will rejoice in more things after the judgment than now, since he will rejoice in the glory of soul and body and in the glory of all the saved.

<!-- page 1013 -->

But it is not greater in this way with respect to *different persons*, because a small Saint will rejoice in just as many and the same things as the greatest Saint. — In the fourth way greater, namely because more intense, it is found both with respect to *the same person* and with respect to *different persons*: with respect to *different persons*, because one is moved more intensely than another; with respect to *the same person*, because from the same habit the soul will be moved more intensely once the body has been taken up again[^p1013-1], since it will have nothing retarding it.

It must therefore be granted that the glorification of the body makes for a greater joy as regards *extension*; it makes for it also as regards *intension*, not by increasing the habit, but by removing the impediment. Hence it does not make for an increase of the essential reward *principally*, but *consequently*. — And the reasons for this are to be granted.

*To 1 and 2.* To that which is objected on the contrary side, that one does not rejoice in a greater [object]; it must be said that the reasoning[^p1013-2] holds well; but as regards this it is not called *greater*, but *more intense concerning the same*. — Similarly, what he objects in the second place about grace is evident: for grace itself denotes a habit, and the same habit of glory is in the soul stripped and after it has been united; but through it the soul is not moved so intensely, and this because the appetite retards it.

*To 3.* To that which is objected, that it forgets[^p1013-3] itself; it must be said that this is not understood as though it did not love itself, but that every private love which is not referred to God will be taken away from the soul; and certainly the soul does not desire the body with a private love, but for the glory of God. — Or it must be said that Bernard is speaking of the Blessed after the resumption of the second robe.

*To 4.* To that which is objected, that greater motions absorb lesser ones; it must be said that it is true that they absorb them, but not so that they do not *exist*, but so that they do not *appear so intensely*; yet they are and they appear in some way; and therefore, although but little, still in some way the lesser motions diminish the greater ones themselves, even though they are overcome[^p1013-4].

*To 5.* To that which is objected, that if the appetite impedes, the presence [of the body] does so still more; it must be said that *appetite* denotes two things, namely the *love* of some thing and the *lack* of it. When therefore it impedes, it does so either by reason of *love* or by reason of *lack*. If by reason of *love*, then the thing present impedes more; if by reason of *lack*, then it does not impede, since the lack[^p1013-5] is removed by the presence.

*To 6.* To that which is objected, that a released spirit is freer; it must be said that there is a spirit released from the body *in substance and in affection*; and this one is most free. And there is a spirit *bound in affection* and *loosed in substance*; and this one is not free, because it wills something which it does not have. And there is a spirit *bound in substance and in affection*; and then either the body is *disobedient*, and such a spirit is not free, because it cannot do[^p1013-6] what it wills; or the body is *obedient in all things*, and such a spirit is free with the freedom befitting it, because it wills nothing except what it can, and it has everything that it wills.

### Scholion

**I.** Question I is posed by the Master (here c. 4) thus: "Whether the beatitude of the Saints is greater after the judgment." — The *affirmative* answer is the common one, if it is understood that beatitude grows *extensively*. Both the Greeks and the Latins declared at the Council of Florence that they believed this. But that it grows also *intensively*, not indeed by an *increase of the habit*, but by the *removal of the retarding impediment*, St. Augustine taught (XII. *On Genesis, to the Letter*, c. 35), yet not (as is clear from his own II. *Retractions*, c. 24) as something altogether certain; and him St. Bernard, Haymo, and other ancients followed, together with St. Bonaventure himself. At first St. Thomas too (here q. 1. a. 4. quaestiuncula 1; *Suppl.* q. 93. a. 1) professed the same opinion, but afterwards, having changed his view, he taught (*S.* I. II. q. 4. a. 5. ad 5) that "once the body has been taken up again, beatitude grows not *intensively*, but *extensively*"; which Durandus also (here q. 7) and other later writers defend. — But the first opinion is held by Scotus, *On the Principle of Things*, q. 9. a. 2. sect. 2. n. 49, and, as it seems, here q. 3. n. 9 (some, however, hold that he retracted this opinion), and by Richard (here a. 2. q. 7). — The proposition laid down at the beginning of the response, that beatitude is by *essence* joy or delight, is approved by Alexander of Hales (*S.* III. q. 23. m. 1. ad 1) and is defended as probable by Richard of Middleton (here a. 3. q. 6). But Scotus (here q. 7; I. *Sent.* d. 1. q. 3) teaches that "strictly taking beatitude, delight does not belong to its *essence*, because it is a *passion*, whereas beatitude consists in *operation*" (here q. 7. n. 4); yet inasmuch as the will is *receptive*, "it is perfected by delight and joy." The same is held by St. Thomas (here q. 3. a. 4. quaestiuncula 3; *S.* I. II. q. 4. a. 1. 2) and by Durandus (here q. 4. n. 20). Scotus adds, however (n. 6), that according to the absolute power of God the vision of him could be without joy; concerning which St. Bonaventure speaks otherwise above, d. 48. a. 1. q. 2, at the end.

Concerning the second question the other commentators on Lombard do not treat specially, but solve it in the first question, or rather in the questions on the individual dowries, where we have noted how Scotus departs from St. Bonaventure and Thomas.

On both questions, besides the places cited: Peter of Tarantaise touches the matter briefly here, q. 2. a. 4. — Richard of Middleton, here a. 4. q. 1. — Dionysius the Carthusian, here q. 7.

**II.** Concerning the following article, which treats of the four dowries of the body *in general*, the other commentators on Lombard, with the exception of B. Albert (d. 44. a. 32) and Richard (here a. 4. q. 1), do not treat of it specially, but lay down their principles while disputing about the same in particular, on which see below, section 2. — Concerning the three dowries *of the soul* St. Thomas offers much, here q. 4. a. 1-5; *Suppl.* q. 95. a. 1-5.
