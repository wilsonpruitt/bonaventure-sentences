### Dub. I.

In parte ista sunt dubitationes circa litteram, et primo de hoc quod dicit, quod *originalia reputabuntur ei ad poenam*. Videtur enim dicere falsum, quia originale est a naturali origine[^1]; sed aliquis non bis potest nasci carnaliter: ergo non potest originale iterari. — Item, originale non est in uno nisi unum: ergo male dicit, quod redeunt originalia pluraliter. — Item quaeritur, utrum similiter possit dici, quod redeunt *venialia*.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod in originali duo sunt, scilicet carentia debitae iustitiae et ulterius pronitas; non fiat vis, utrum ista duo differant secundum *rem*[^2]. Ratione primi privat a visione Dei, sed ratione secundi frequenter in malum trahit; ratione utriusque dicitur quodam modo *redire*, quia pronitates intenduntur, et homo indignus visione Dei efficitur, et ita quodam modo redit in consimili reatu[^3].

Quod ergo dicitur, quod non potest iterari; verum est in se. — Quod obiicitur, quod dicitur pluraliter; dicendum, quod hoc est ratione plurium pronitatum. — Quod quaeritur de veniali, dicendum, quod potest aliquo modo dici, sed adhuc magis improprie quam peccata alia. Est tamen ibi quodam modo ingratitudo[^4].

### Dub. II.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Tot debuit cogitare dona Dei* etc. Ex hoc enim videtur, quod ad tot gratiarum actiones teneatur quis, quot percepit beneficia; sed quilibet nostrum recipit quotidie innumerabilia beneficia: ergo ad innumerabiles tenemur gratiarum actiones. — Item, si ad tot tenemur gratiarum actiones, quot peccata sunt dimissa: ergo videtur similiter, quod si aliquis dedit alicui centum denarios, tenetur similiter centies gratias agere.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod sicut de peccatis sufficit *conteri in communi*, ita quod omnia detestetur, ita tamen quod nullum placeat[^5]; sic de omnibus sufficit *gratias agere in communi*, ita tamen, quod de nullo in speciali ingratus existat; et secundum quod plura sunt beneficia, maiores debet gratias agere; et per oppositum, si non agit, magis ingratus iudicatur esse. Et pro tanto dicitur, quod debet pro *omnibus* gratias agere, non ita, quod pro quolibet divisim suam oportet gratiarum actionem <!-- page 586 --> exhibere, sed quanto plura fuerint, magis accusant ingratum de ingratitudine et faciunt dignum increpatione[^6].

### Dub. III.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod propter ingratitudinem ita reus et peccator constituitur, ut ante fuerat; utrum scilicet ista ingratitudo dicatur genus peccati, vel circumstantia. Si genus; videtur tunc, quod necessario quilibet peccet duplici genere peccati in quolibet peccato, quia quilibet immemor est beneficii accepti, cum Deum offendit. Si circumstantia solum: non videtur ita aggravare peccatum. — Item quod non tantum sit circumstantia, videtur, quia gratiarum actio est actus virtutis, quae est gratitudo[^7]: ergo ingratitudo est speciale vitium.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod ad hoc, quod aliquis sit *vere gratus*, duo requiruntur, scilicet quod pro loco et tempore sit *memor* beneficiorum ad gratias agendum, et quod sit *promptus* ad obsequendum et ad reddendum bonum pro bono. — Similiter *ingratitudo* dupliciter dicitur: uno modo *oblivio* beneficii cum contemptu, ut puta, quando est locus et tempus agendi gratias et recognoscendi; et sic est peccatum *speciale* contentum sub omissione. Alio modo dicitur ingratus qui recipit bonum et *reddit malum*, et haec est circumstantia peccati; et sic est in omni peccato, et tanto magis aggravans, quanto homo plura bona percepit[^8].

## English

### Doubt I.

In this part there are doubts concerning the text, and first about what he says, namely that *original [sins] shall be reckoned to him for punishment*. For it seems to say something false, since the original [sin] is from natural origin[^1]; but no one can be born carnally twice: therefore the original [sin] cannot be repeated. — Likewise, the original [sin] is not more than one in any one person: therefore he speaks badly when he says that original [sins] return in the plural. — Likewise it is asked whether it can similarly be said that venial [sins] return.

**I respond:** It must be said that in the original [sin] there are two things, namely the lack of the owed justice and, further, the proneness [to evil]; let no force be made of whether these two differ in reality[^2]. By reason of the first it deprives of the vision of God, but by reason of the second it frequently draws toward evil; by reason of both it is said in a certain way to *return*, because the pronenesses are intensified, and the man is made unworthy of the vision of God, and thus in a certain way he returns into a like guilt[^3].

What is said, therefore, that it cannot be repeated, is true in itself. — As to what is objected, that it is said in the plural, it must be said that this is by reason of the many pronenesses. — As to what is asked about the venial [sin], it must be said that it can in some way be said [to return], but still more improperly than the other sins. Yet there is there, in a certain way, ingratitude[^4].

### Doubt II.

Likewise it is asked about what he says: *He ought to have considered so many gifts of God*, etc. For from this it seems that one is bound to as many thanksgivings as the benefits he has received; but each of us daily receives innumerable benefits: therefore we are bound to innumerable thanksgivings. — Likewise, if we are bound to as many thanksgivings as the sins that have been remitted: then it seems similarly that, if someone has given another a hundred denarii, he is likewise bound to give thanks a hundred times.

**I respond:** It must be said that, just as regarding sins it suffices *to be contrite in general*, so that one detests them all, yet in such a way that none is pleasing[^5]; so regarding all [benefits] it suffices *to give thanks in general*, yet in such a way that one be not ungrateful for any in particular; and according as the benefits are more, the greater thanks he ought to give; and, on the contrary, if he does not give [them], he is judged the more ungrateful. And to this extent it is said that he ought to give thanks for *all*, not in such a way that for each one separately he must render his thanksgiving <!-- page 586 --> [distinctly], but the more they have been, the more they accuse the ungrateful man of ingratitude and make him worthy of rebuke[^6].

### Doubt III.

Likewise it is asked about what he says, namely that on account of ingratitude one is made guilty and a sinner just as he had been before; that is, whether this ingratitude be called a genus of sin, or a circumstance. If a genus: it seems then that necessarily everyone sins with a twofold genus of sin in every sin, since everyone is forgetful of the benefit received when he offends God. If a circumstance only: it does not seem so to aggravate the sin. — Likewise, that it is not merely a circumstance seems [to follow] because thanksgiving is an act of virtue, which is gratitude[^7]: therefore ingratitude is a special vice.

**I respond:** It must be said that, for anyone to be *truly grateful*, two things are required, namely that in due place and time he be *mindful* of benefits so as to give thanks, and that he be *prompt* to serve and to render good for good. — Similarly *ingratitude* is spoken of in two ways: in one way [as] the *forgetting* of a benefit with contempt, as for instance when there is a place and time for giving thanks and acknowledging [them]; and thus it is a special sin contained under omission. In another way he is called ungrateful who receives good and *renders evil*, and this is a circumstance of sin; and thus it is in every sin, and aggravating the more, the more goods a man has received[^8].
