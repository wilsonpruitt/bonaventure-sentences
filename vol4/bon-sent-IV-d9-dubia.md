# IV Sent., d. 9 — Dubia circa litteram Magistri
## *Dubia circa litteram Magistri*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 212 -->

### DUBIA CIRCA LITTERAM MAGISTRI.

### Dub. I

In parte ista sunt dubitationes circa litteram, et primo de hoc quod dicit Augustinus: *Crede, et manducasti.* Cum enim summa unio et incorporatio sit in caritate[^1], videtur potius, quod deberet dicere: *dilige, et manducasti, quam crede.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod ipse intelligit de *fide operante per dilectionem*[^2]. Ratio autem, quare magis dicit de *fide,* duplex est: quia fides respicit intellectum et affectum[^3], et ita utroque modo unit. — Item alia ratio est: quia, licet caritas perfectius dicat unionem, tamen proprietas *manducationis* magis in actu *fidei* invenitur, quia credens masticat recogitando, sed diligens non incorporat, sed incorporatur[^4].

### Dub. II

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Credere in eum, hoc est comedere panem et vinum.* Videtur enim falsum dicere, quia illud est spirituale, scilicet credere, hoc est corporale: ergo unum non est alterum.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod hoc non est dictum per omnimodam identitatem, sed per aequi-

<!-- page 213 -->

pollentiam in effectu, quia idem facit; vel per expressionem significationis, quia hoc significat illud; et sic patet obiectio.

### Dub. III

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Qui non manet in me, nec ego in illo, etsi accipit Sacramentum, acquirit magnum tormentum.* Videtur enim male dicere, quia, cum nemo sciat, utrum maneat in eo; ergo nemo scit, utrum peccet, quando accipit Sacramentum: ergo quilibet, quando accipit, committit se discrimini.

**Respondeo:** Verbum illud intelligendum est de eo qui scit, se esse extra Christum, ita quod plura signa et fortiora habeat ad oppositum quam ad propositum. Nam si probabilia signa habeat de gratia, sufficit sibi ad hoc, quod peccatum non incurrat, quia non incurrit contemptum, quamvis non tantum se disponat, quod Deus acceptet omnino[^5].

### Dub. IV

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *In illo Sacramento corpus et sanguinem suum commendavit, quod et fecit nos ipsos.* Videtur enim male dicere: quia, si nos sumus res Sacramenti: ergo cum recipimus rem Sacramenti, manducamus nosmetipsos; quod est omnino inconveniens, ut videtur.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod nos sumus res huius Sacramenti non *simpliciter,* sed in quantum sumus membra[^6], sive in quantum sumus uniti ipsi capiti; unde Sacramentum illud significat unionem corporis Christi per comparationem membrorum ad invicem et ad caput. Quando ergo nos sibi univit, fecit *nos,* id est ipsam unionem, *rem* huius Sacramenti, quia[^7] nobis *rem* Sacramenti tribuit; quae unio quotidie in nobis crescit et melioratur, secundum quod devotius Sacramentum suscipitur.

### Dub. V

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicitur: *Qui manducat et bibit indigne;* quid sit manducare indigne.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod triplex est indignitas, secundum quod distinguit Glossa, primae ad Corinthios undecimo[^8], quae facit hominem accedere indigne: «Indignus est, inquit, qui aliter celebrat mysterium, quam a Domino institutum est, et qui non devota mente accedit ad eucharistiam, vel in voluntate peccandi manet». Et ex hoc elicere possumus triplicem indignitatem: una quarum est in memoria; secunda in voluntate; tertia in opere. Prima respicit peccatum *praeteritum,* de quo non poenitet; et haec vocatur indevotio. Secunda respicit peccatum *futurum,* quod proponit facere, et hanc vocat voluntatem peccandi. Tertia respicit peccatum *praesens;* et haec est, quando aliter celebrat mysterium, ita quod actu peccat, dum conficit. Et haec indignitas secundum triplicem differentiam inducit contemptum, ac per hoc facit accedere indigne, et ita actu peccare[^9].

### Dub. VI

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Factum est malum, dum male accipitur bonum.* Videtur enim male dicere, quia corpus Christi et ipse Christus est bonum *secundum se:* ergo non potest fieri malum aliquo modo.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Sacramentum dicitur fieri malum *non in se,* sed *isti,* et ita ut comparatum ad hoc; et comparatur per sumtionem et manducationem. Et ita vult dicere, quod sumere corpus Christi, quod erat bonum in genere sive in se, per circumstantiam factum est malum, non, inquam, *ipsum quod sumitur,* sed *actus* sumendi[^10].

## English

### DOUBTS CONCERNING THE TEXT OF THE MASTER.

### Doubt I

In this section there are doubts concerning the text, and first about this, that Augustine says: *Believe, and thou hast eaten.* For since the highest union and incorporation is in charity[^1], it seems rather that he ought to say: *love, and thou hast eaten, rather than believe.*

**I respond:** It must be said that he understands it of *faith working through love*[^2]. But the reason why he speaks rather of *faith* is twofold: because faith regards both the intellect and the affection[^3], and so unites in both ways. — Likewise there is another reason: because, although charity expresses union more perfectly, nevertheless the proper character of *eating* is found more in the act of *faith,* since the believer chews by thinking over, whereas the one who loves does not incorporate, but is incorporated[^4].

### Doubt II

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *To believe in him, this is to eat the bread and wine.* For it seems to be said falsely, because that, namely to believe, is spiritual, this is corporeal: therefore the one is not the other.

**I respond:** It must be said that this is not said by an utter identity, but by an equi-

valence in effect, because it does the same thing; or by an expression of signification, because this signifies that; and thus the objection is clear.

### Doubt III

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *He who does not abide in me, nor I in him, even if he receives the Sacrament, acquires a great torment.* For it seems to be said falsely, because, since no one knows whether he abides in him, therefore no one knows whether he sins when he receives the Sacrament: therefore everyone, when he receives, exposes himself to danger.

**I respond:** That word must be understood of one who knows that he is outside Christ, in such a way that he has more and stronger signs to the contrary than in favor. For if he has probable signs of grace, it suffices him for this, that he not incur sin, because he does not incur contempt, even though he does not so dispose himself that God altogether accepts him[^5].

### Doubt IV

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *In that Sacrament he commended his body and blood, which also made us ourselves.* For it seems to be said falsely: because, if we are the thing of the Sacrament: therefore when we receive the thing of the Sacrament, we eat ourselves; which is altogether unfitting, as it seems.

**I respond:** It must be said that we are the thing of this Sacrament not *simply,* but insofar as we are members[^6], or insofar as we are united to the head itself; hence that Sacrament signifies the union of the body of Christ through the comparison of the members to one another and to the head. When therefore he united us to himself, he made *us,* that is, the union itself, the *thing* of this Sacrament, because[^7] he bestows on us the *thing* of the Sacrament; which union grows and is bettered in us daily, according as the Sacrament is received more devoutly.

### Doubt V

Likewise it is asked about this, that is said: *He who eats and drinks unworthily;* what it is to eat unworthily.

**I respond:** It must be said that there is a threefold unworthiness, according as the Gloss distinguishes, on the first to the Corinthians, chapter eleven[^8], which makes a man approach unworthily: «He is unworthy, it says, who celebrates the mystery otherwise than it was instituted by the Lord, and who approaches the eucharist not with a devout mind, or remains in the will to sin». And from this we can draw out a threefold unworthiness: one of which is in the memory; the second in the will; the third in the deed. The first regards a *past* sin, of which he does not repent; and this is called indevotion. The second regards a *future* sin, which he proposes to do, and this he calls the will to sin. The third regards a *present* sin; and this is, when he celebrates the mystery otherwise, in such a way that he actually sins while he confects. And this unworthiness according to its threefold difference brings on contempt, and through this makes one approach unworthily, and so actually sin[^9].

### Doubt VI

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *Evil is done, when a good is taken badly.* For it seems to be said falsely, because the body of Christ and Christ himself is a good *in itself:* therefore it cannot be made evil in any way.

**I respond:** It must be said that the Sacrament is said to be made evil *not in itself,* but *for that one,* and so as compared to this; and it is compared through reception and eating. And so he means to say that to receive the body of Christ, which was a good in its kind or in itself, has through circumstance been made evil — not, I say, *the very thing which is received,* but the *act* of receiving[^10].

**Dubia count: 6.**
- **Dub. I** — On Augustine's *Crede, et manducasti*: why "believe" rather than "love," since union/incorporation is highest in charity. Answer: faith working through love unites in both intellect and affection, and *eating* (mastication = thinking-over) belongs more to faith's act.
- **Dub. II** — On *Credere in eum, hoc est comedere panem et vinum*: how the spiritual (believing) can be the corporeal (eating). Answer: not by utter identity but by equivalence-in-effect / expression-of-signification.
- **Dub. III** — On *Qui non manet in me… acquirit magnum tormentum*: if no one knows whether he abides in Christ, everyone receiving risks danger. Answer: the warning is for one who knows he is outside Christ (more contrary than favorable signs); probable signs of grace suffice to avoid contempt.
- **Dub. IV** — On *quod et fecit nos ipsos*: do we then eat ourselves when we receive the *res Sacramenti*? Answer: we are the *res* not *simpliciter* but as members united to the Head; the Sacrament signifies the union of Christ's body, which grows daily with devout reception.
- **Dub. V** — On *Qui manducat et bibit indigne*: what is eating unworthily. Answer: a threefold unworthiness (memory = past sin / indevotion; will = future sin / will-to-sin; deed = present sin), per the Gloss on 1 Cor 11:27, each inducing contempt.
- **Dub. VI** — On *Factum est malum, dum male accipitur bonum*: how a good (Christ) can be made evil. Answer: the Sacrament is made evil *not in itself* but for that one, by comparison/reception — the *act* of receiving, not the thing received.
