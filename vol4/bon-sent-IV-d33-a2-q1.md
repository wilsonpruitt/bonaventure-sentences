## *Utrum virginitas sit virtus*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 753 -->

### Articulus II. *De continentia virginali.*

Consequenter quantum ad secundum articulum quaeritur de continentia virginali. Et circa hoc tria quaeruntur. Primo quaeritur de ipsa virginitate quantum ad genus suum. Secundo, de comparatione continentiae virginalis ad matrimonium sive coniugium. Tertio, de virginitate per comparationem ad praemium.

### Quaestio I. *Utrum virginitas sit virtus.*

Quantum ergo ad primum quaeritur, utrum virginitas sit virtus. Et quod sic, videtur:

1. Per definitionem virtutis: quia « virtus est bona qualitas mentis[^p753-1] » etc.; et per definitionem Philosophi illud idem.

2. Item, hoc ostenditur per definitionem virginitatis, quam ponit Augustinus[^p753-2] dicens: « Virginitas est in carne corruptibili incorruptionis perpetuae meditatio »: sed meditari incorruptionem perpetuam hoc est maxime arduum et difficile, et tale est virtutis genus: ergo etc.

3. Item, hoc iterum videtur *ratione*: quia abstinere a coitu illicito est virtutis; sed difficilius est ab omni coitu abstinere quam ab illicito: ergo si illud est virtutis, multo fortius istud.

4. Item, in virginitate meremur non tantum *auream*, sed etiam *aureolam*[^p753-3]; sed omne meritum consistit mediante *virtute*, vel *dono*, vel *beatitudine*: ergo etc.

**Contra:** 1. Omnis virtus, quae non est in finem, in medietate consistit[^p753-4]; sed virginitas in extremo, quia omnino abstinet: ergo etc.

2. Item, actus omnis virtutis est in praecepto, quia est necessarius[^p753-5] ad salutem; sed actus virginitatis non est in praecepto: ergo virginitas non est virtus.

3. Item, nulla virtute quis male utitur, ut patet; sed virginitate contingit male uti, ut patet de fatuis virginibus, Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p753-6].

4. Item, omnes virtutes sunt connexae, unde qui habet unam habet omnes[^p753-7]; sed hoc non est de virginitate: ergo etc.

5. Item, nulla virtus expellitur vel amittitur nisi per vitium et peccatum; sed virginitas perditur sine peccato in his quae violenter corrumpuntur: ergo etc. *Si tu dicas* de his, quod non amittunt[^p753-8]; *obiicitur* de his quae transeunt de continentia virginali ad coniugalem, quae virginitatem perdunt sine culpa.

6. Item, omnis virtus perdita recuperatur per poenitentiam, quia et gratia, in qua est omnis virtus; sed virginitas perdita nunquam recuperatur[^p753-9]: ergo etc.

> **Conclusio.** Virginitas, secundum illud quod nominat habitum, est virtus et consistit in medio.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod continentia virginalis sive virginitas tria dicit: primum est *abstinentia ab omni coitu illicito*; secundum est *abstinentia ab omni coitu licito*; tertium est *integritas mentis et carnis*. Quantum ad primum dicit *habitum*, quantum ad secundum dicit *virtutis statum*, quantum ad tertium dicit *decorem*; unde virginitas est habitus continentiae *perfectus* et *decorus*. — Concedendae igitur sunt rationes probantes, virginitatem virtutem esse, secundum id quod nominat habitum.

*Ad 2–6.* Quae vero obiiciuntur in contrarium, omnia procedunt ratione *status*, vel *decoris*, quantum ad quem *statum* ad extremum tendit. — Ratione etiam status non est in praecepto, ratione cuius status potest perdi per descensum ad inferiorem statum, ratione cuius status non est aliis connexa. — Ratione vero *decoris* contingit, quod perdatur, homine invito; contingit etiam superbe et male uti;
<!-- page 754 -->
contingit etiam, ut amissa non possit recuperari. — Quantum vero ad illud quod est ibi *habitus substrati*, omnes conditiones virtutum in ea reperiuntur.

*Ad 1.* Et sic patent omnia praeter primum, quod obiicit de medietate, quia ita similiter posset opponi de continentia viduali. — Et propterea dicendum, quod quando dicitur, quod virtus consistit in medietate, hoc non est intelligendum[^p754-1] respectu *obiecti* — quia sic nullus esset castus, nisi cognosceret medietatem omnium mulierum de mundo, quia illud est medium inter aliquam et nullam — sed istud intelligitur, ut sic medium teneatur, et virtus sit medium circa delectationes et tristitias[^p754-2]. Nam qui omnino nihil sentit de passionibus carnis, hic *insensibilis* iudicatur, sed hoc habet per naturam; qui vero omnino eis consentit, hic *luxuriosus* est; qui vero excitatur, sed tamen non consentit, talis *virtuosus* est; et hoc omnino bene est in virginibus, quia aliquo modo caro excitat, quamvis non ita, sicut in corruptis. Hoc autem intelligitur, loquendo secundum virtutes *politicas*[^p754-3] et naturam *lapsam*; nam per superabundantem gratiam potest esse, quod non sentiat, et tamen virtutem habeat, ut patet in Christo et eius Matre dulcissima.

### Scholion

**I.** Definitio illa S. Augustini (2. fundam.) a S. Thom. (hic q. 3. a. 1; S. II. II. q. 152. a. 1.) speciali quaestione explicatur, ubi distinguit triplicem integritatem, scilicet corporalem tantum, corporalem simul et spiritualem, denique spiritualem tantum, quae ultima idem est cum *habitu* virginitatis secundum locutionem S. Bonaventurae. — Difficultas principalis huius 1. quaestionis consistit in eo, ut explicetur quomodo haec virtus consistat *in medio* secundum determinationem Aristotelis; de quo diffuse tractat S. Thom., locis citt. a. 2. et in S. a. 3. et I. II. q. 64. a. 1. ad 3. De 1. quaestione praeter S. Thomam: Scot., supra d. 31. q. unica n. 8. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 4. a. 1. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 4. q. 1. — De hac et 2 seqq. qq. Durand., hic q. 4; Dionys. Carth., hic q. 3.

**II.** Quoad 2. quaestionem Conc. Trident. (Sess. XXIV. can. 10.) damnavit errorem dicentium, « statum coniugalem anteponendum esse statui virginitatis, vel caelibatus, et non esse melius ac beatius manere in virginitate aut caelibatu quam iungi matrimonio ». De hac 2. quaestione: S. Thom., hic q. 3. a. 3; S. II. II. q. 152. a. 4. 5. — Petr. a Tar., de hac et seq. q. q. 4. a. 2. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 4. q. 2.

**III.** De praemio virginitatis (q. 3.), scilicet de *fructu centesimo* et de *aureola*, alii commentatores Lombardi hic vel tacent vel breviter illud in praecedente quaestione tangunt; plures de eo tractant infra d. 49. Rem tangit Scot. supra d. 1. q. 6. n. 6. Plura de aureolis generatim habent: S. Thom., infra d. 49. q. 5. a. 1-5; Suppl. q. 96. a. 1-12 (de aureola virginitatis etiam hic q. 3. a. 3. ad 3.). — Richard. a Med., infra d. 49. a. 5. q. 1-7. — Dionys. Carth., infra d. 49. q. 8.

## English

### Article II. *On virginal continence.*

Consequently, with respect to the second article, inquiry is made concerning virginal continence. And concerning this, three questions are asked. First, it is asked concerning virginity itself with respect to its genus. Second, concerning the comparison of virginal continence to marriage or wedlock. Third, concerning virginity by comparison to its reward.

### Question I. *Whether virginity is a virtue.*

With respect to the first, then, it is asked whether virginity is a virtue. And that it is, seems [to be shown]:

1. By the definition of virtue: for « virtue is a good quality of the mind[^p753-1] » etc.; and by the definition of the Philosopher, the same thing.

2. Likewise, this is shown by the definition of virginity, which Augustine[^p753-2] gives, saying: « Virginity is, in corruptible flesh, the meditation of perpetual incorruption »: but to meditate on perpetual incorruption is a thing most arduous and difficult, and such is the class of virtue: therefore, etc.

3. Likewise, this again seems [to hold] by *reason*: for to abstain from illicit intercourse is [a matter] of virtue; but it is more difficult to abstain from all intercourse than from the illicit: therefore, if that is [a matter] of virtue, much more strongly is this.

4. Likewise, in virginity we merit not only the *aurea*, but also the *aureola*[^p753-3]; but all merit consists by means of a *virtue*, or a *gift*, or a *beatitude*: therefore, etc.

**On the contrary:** 1. Every virtue which is not toward an end consists in a mean[^p753-4]; but virginity [consists] in an extreme, since it abstains altogether: therefore, etc.

2. Likewise, the act of every virtue is under precept, since it is necessary[^p753-5] for salvation; but the act of virginity is not under precept: therefore virginity is not a virtue.

3. Likewise, no one uses a virtue badly, as is evident; but it happens that virginity is used badly, as is evident from the foolish virgins, Matthew 25[^p753-6].

4. Likewise, all the virtues are connected, whence he who has one has all[^p753-7]; but this is not the case with virginity: therefore, etc.

5. Likewise, no virtue is expelled or lost except through vice and sin; but virginity is lost without sin in those things which are violently corrupted: therefore, etc. *If you should say* concerning these, that they do not lose [it][^p753-8]; *the objection is raised* concerning those who pass over from virginal continence to conjugal [continence], who lose their virginity without fault.

6. Likewise, every lost virtue is recovered through penance, since [so is] grace also, in which is every virtue; but virginity once lost is never recovered[^p753-9]: therefore, etc.

> **Conclusion.** Virginity, according to that which it names a habit, is a virtue and consists in a mean.

**I respond:** It must be said that virginal continence, or virginity, says three things: the first is *abstinence from all illicit intercourse*; the second is *abstinence from all licit intercourse*; the third is *integrity of mind and flesh*. With respect to the first it says a *habit*, with respect to the second it says the *state of virtue*, with respect to the third it says *comeliness*; whence virginity is a habit of continence, *perfect* and *comely*. — Therefore the reasons proving that virginity is a virtue, according to that which it names a habit, are to be granted.

*To 2–6.* But the things which are objected to the contrary all proceed by reason of the *state* or of the *comeliness*, with respect to which state it tends toward an extreme. — Also, by reason of the state it is not under precept; by reason of which state it can be lost through descent to a lower state; by reason of which the state is not connected with the others. — But by reason of the *comeliness* it happens that it is lost with the person unwilling; it happens also that one uses it proudly and badly;
<!-- page 754 -->
it happens also that, once lost, it cannot be recovered. — But with respect to that which is there the *underlying habit*, all the conditions of the virtues are found in it.

*To 1.* And thus all [the points] are clear except the first, which objects concerning the mean, since likewise it could be objected concerning widowed continence. — And therefore it must be said that when it is said that virtue consists in a mean, this is not to be understood[^p754-1] with respect to the *object* — for thus no one would be chaste unless he knew the mean of all the women in the world, since that is the mean between some [woman] and none — but this is understood in such a way that the mean be held, and virtue be a mean concerning pleasures and sorrows[^p754-2]. For he who feels nothing at all of the passions of the flesh is judged *insensible*, but this he has by nature; but he who altogether consents to them is *lustful*; but he who is aroused, yet nevertheless does not consent, such a one is *virtuous*; and this is altogether well [the case] in virgins, since in some way the flesh is aroused, though not as in the corrupt. But this is understood as speaking according to the *political* virtues[^p754-3] and *fallen* nature; for through superabundant grace it can be that one does not feel [it], and nevertheless has virtue, as is evident in Christ and in His most sweet Mother.

### Scholion

**I.** That definition of St. Augustine (2nd fundamentum) is explained by St. Thomas (here q. 3, a. 1; Summa II-II q. 152, a. 1) in a special question, where he distinguishes a threefold integrity, namely bodily only, bodily and spiritual together, and finally spiritual only — which last is the same as the *habit* of virginity according to St. Bonaventure's manner of speaking. — The principal difficulty of this first question consists in this, that it be explained how this virtue consists *in a mean* according to Aristotle's determination; on which St. Thomas treats at length in the places cited, a. 2, and in the [Summa] a. 3, and I-II q. 64, a. 1, ad 3. On the first question, besides St. Thomas: Scotus, above d. 31, q. unica, n. 8. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 4, a. 1. — Richard of Middleton, here a. 4, q. 1. — On this and the two following questions: Durandus, here q. 4; Dionysius the Carthusian, here q. 3.

**II.** As to the second question, the Council of Trent (Session XXIV, can. 10) condemned the error of those saying « that the conjugal state is to be preferred to the state of virginity or of celibacy, and that it is not better and more blessed to remain in virginity or celibacy than to be joined in marriage ». On this second question: St. Thomas, here q. 3, a. 3; Summa II-II q. 152, a. 4, 5. — Peter of Tarentaise, on this and the following question, q. 4, a. 2. — Richard of Middleton, here a. 4, q. 2.

**III.** On the reward of virginity (q. 3), namely on the *hundredfold fruit* and on the *aureola*, the other commentators on Lombard here either are silent or touch on it briefly in the preceding question; many treat of it below, d. 49. Scotus touches on the matter above, d. 1, q. 6, n. 6. On the aureolae in general many have [treatments]: St. Thomas, below d. 49, q. 5, a. 1–5; Supplement q. 96, a. 1–12 (on the aureola of virginity also here q. 3, a. 3, ad 3). — Richard of Middleton, below d. 49, a. 5, q. 1–7. — Dionysius the Carthusian, below d. 49, q. 8.
