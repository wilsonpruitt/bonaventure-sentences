### Quaestio III.
### *Utrum omnia videantur simul ab omnibus.*

Tertio quaeritur, utrum omnia videantur *simul* ab omnibus. Et quod sic, videtur:

1. Quia super illud Iob octavo[^p902-1]: *Induentur confusione*, Glossa: «Viso iudice, mala omnia ante oculos mentis versantur».

2. Item, *ratione* videtur: quia insimul infligetur tota poena, ergo simul scietur et ponderabitur culpa: ergo si magnitudo culpae pendet ab actibus et circumstantiis et earum[^p902-2] iteratione, omnia haec manifestari debent simul.

3. Item, maior confusio est et dolor in memoria[^p902-3] duorum quam unius, et omnium quam aliquorum malorum; econtra de bonis maius gaudium: ergo si boni habebunt perfectum gaudium, mali perfectam confusionem; patet, quod omnibus simul patebunt.

**Contra:** 1. «Unum solum intelligimus», sicut dicit Philosophus[^p902-4]: ergo non plura simul: non ergo omnia poterunt videri simul.

2. Item, in damnatis non erit eorum natura melior, quam nunc est: ergo si eorum intellectus nunc non potest plura intelligere simul, nec tunc poterit; quod si tunc poterit, ergo sublimatus erit.

3. Item, in iudicio erit discussio[^p902-5], quae est prius de uno quam de alio: ergo prius erit unius recordatio quam alterius.

> **Conclusio.** Omnes repente, vel simul omnia peccata videbunt, et probabilius simul.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod omnes *repente*, vel *simul* omnia peccata videbunt, et credendum est magis, quod *simul*. — Ad quod intelligendum notandum est, quod *simul videre* multa ut multa, hoc potest esse tripliciter: aut ratione eius, *in quo* videt, quia videt multa uno; et sic in speculo aeterno est videre multa, quia per idem ibi homo videt diversa. Aut ratione *sublimationis intellectus*, qui efficitur deiformis per gloriam; unde videt plura, sicut et Deus, licet non omnimode; sicut Angelus beatus simul cognoscit Deum *in se* et creaturam *in Deo* et in *proprio genere*[^p902-6]. Aut ratione *divinae virtutis hoc facientis*; quae tamen non efficit gloriam, ut ostendat ei exemplar aeternum, sed levat intellectum, ut possit aliquid supra se, vel ad gloriam, vel ad poenam. — His *tribus* modis videbunt simul animae *bonae in iudicio*, primis *duobus* modis *in patria*[^p902-7]; sed *tertio* tantummodo animae *malae in iudicio*. — Utrum autem *post* sic videant in inferno; hoc non determino, quamvis non improbabiliter dici possit, ut poena vermis sit consummata et uniformis.

1. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod intelligimus unum solum; dicendum, quod illud verum est *per naturam*; sed iste modus intelligendi erit per *virtutem divinam*[^p902-8].

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod cognitiva in damnatis non debet sublimari; dicendum, quod illa sublimatio potius est *depressio*, scilicet in ostensione omnium turpitudinum. Sicut enim recogitatio multorum bonorum amplificat laetitiam, ita recogitatio multorum malorum amplificat tristitiam.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod erit discussio; dicendum, quod etsi singillatim fiat discussio de singulis, quia non possunt omnia simul dici; tamen ad confusionem erit simul memoria de multis. Nec mirum, quia potentior est virtus *cognitiva* quam *expressiva* sive interpretativa; intelligimus enim statim, quod non possumus exprimere tempore diuturno[^p902-9].

## English

### Question III.
### *Whether all things are seen at once by all.*

Thirdly it is asked whether all things are seen *at once* by all. And that they are, it seems:

1. Because on that passage of Job, chapter eight[^p902-1]: *They shall be clothed with confusion*, the Gloss [says]: "When the judge has been seen, all evils are set before the eyes of the mind."

2. Likewise, it seems by reason: because the whole punishment is inflicted all at once, therefore the fault too will be known and weighed at once: therefore, if the magnitude of the fault depends on the acts and the circumstances and their[^p902-2] repetition, all these things must be made manifest at once.

3. Likewise, there is greater confusion and grief in the memory[^p902-3] of two things than of one, and of all than of some evils; and conversely, greater joy concerning goods: therefore, if the good will have perfect joy, the wicked [will have] perfect confusion; it is clear that all things will lie open to all at once.

**On the contrary:** 1. "We understand one thing only," as the Philosopher says[^p902-4]: therefore not several things at once: therefore not all things will be able to be seen at once.

2. Likewise, in the damned their nature will not be better than it now is: therefore, if their intellect now cannot understand several things at once, neither then will it be able to; but if it will then be able to, then it will have been elevated.

3. Likewise, in the judgment there will be an examination[^p902-5], which is of one before another: therefore the recollection of one will come before that of another.

> **Conclusion.** All will see all their sins suddenly, or at once, and more probably at once.

**I respond:** It must be said that all will see all their sins *suddenly*, or *at once*, and it is rather to be believed that [they will see them] *at once*. — For the understanding of which it must be noted that *to see at once* many things as many can be so in three ways: either by reason of that *in which* one sees, because he sees many things by one thing; and thus in the eternal mirror there is a seeing of many things, because through the same [mirror] a man there sees diverse things. Or by reason of the *elevation of the intellect*, which is made deiform through glory; whence it sees more, like God also, though not in every manner; just as a blessed Angel at once knows God *in himself* and the creature *in God* and in its *proper kind*[^p902-6]. Or by reason of *the divine power doing this*; which nevertheless does not confer glory, so as to show it the eternal exemplar, but raises the intellect so that it can [attain] something above itself, whether unto glory or unto punishment. — By these *three* modes the *good* souls will see at once *in the judgment*, and by the first *two* modes *in the fatherland*[^p902-7]; but by the *third* alone the *wicked* souls [will see] *in the judgment*. — But whether *afterwards* they thus see [things] in hell, this I do not determine, though it may not improbably be said [to be so], so that the punishment of the worm may be complete and uniform.

1. To that which is objected, that we understand one thing only; it must be said that this is true *by nature*; but this mode of understanding will be by *divine power*[^p902-8].

2. To that which is objected, that the cognitive [power] in the damned ought not to be elevated; it must be said that that elevation is rather a *lowering*, namely in the display of all [their] foulnesses. For just as the recollection of many goods amplifies gladness, so the recollection of many evils amplifies sadness.

3. To that which is objected, that there will be an examination; it must be said that, although the examination be made singly concerning individual [sins], because all things cannot be said at once, nevertheless unto [their] confusion there will be at once a memory of many things. Nor is it a wonder, because the cognitive power is more powerful than the expressive or interpretative [power]; for we understand at once what we cannot express in a long time[^p902-9].
