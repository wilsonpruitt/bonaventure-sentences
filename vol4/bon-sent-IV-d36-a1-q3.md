## Latin

### Quaestio III. *Utrum conditio prolis sequatur patrem, an matrem.*

> The numbered footnotes below correspond to markers in both the Latin body above and the English translation. Quaracchi restarts footnote numbering on each printed page; page-labelled markers keep the two pages' sequences distinct.

Tertio quaeritur, cuius conditionem sequatur proles, utrum patris, an matris. Et quod *patris,* videtur:

1. Quoniam denominatio fieri debet a principaliori et digniori[^p794-2]; sed pater est nobilius et dignius principium quam mater, et principalius: ergo si magis debet principiatum regulari secundum regulas dignioris principii, videtur, quod partus debeat sequi patrem.

2. Item, in sacra Scriptura[^p794-3] nunquam ponitur genealogia mulierum, sed potius virorum: ergo generatio principaliter spectat ad viros: ergo et germen generationis.

3. Item, repromissio de semine benedicto, scilicet Christo, facta est viris principaliter, quamvis nasci deberet de muliere — non enim facta est repromissio Sarae, vel Bersabee, sed Abraham et David[^p794-4] — ergo videtur, quod proles magis sit ipsius viri quam mulieris: ergo etc.

4. Item, unumquodque principiatum, quod producitur secundum unitionem, plus sequitur illud cui plus assimilatur; sed filii plus assimilantur patri[^p794-5]: ergo magis debent sequi patres.

**Contra:** 1. De Coniugio servorum[^p794-6] dicit decretalis: « Indecens videtur, ut geniti ex libera filii ad serviendum retrahantur »: ergo qui nascuntur ex matre libera sunt liberi: ergo sequuntur *matrem.*

2. Item, si quis seminat in terra aliena, fructus est domini terrae, non seminantis[^p794-7]; sed venter mulieris respectu seminis viri est sicut terra respectu sementis: ergo videtur, quod sicut fructus sequitur terram, ita partus sequatur ventrem.

3. Item, hoc videtur in animalibus, quoniam si animal masculini sexus impraegnat animal feminini sexus, ut equus equam, pullus sequitur equam, non equum[^p794-8]: ergo pari ratione videtur in proposito.

4. Item, hoc videtur *per simile* in artificialibus: quia, si quis aedificat in terra aliena, aedificium cedit solo, non solum aedificio[^p794-9]: ergo si quis generat in alieno ventre, proles sequitur ventrem.

### Conclusio

> **CONCLUSIO.** Conditio prolis congrue sequitur matrem, licet in aliquibus regionibus alia sit consuetudo.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod secundum leges civiles sive ius imperiale[^p794-10] « partus sequitur ventrem ». — Et ratio huius est valde conveniens, quoniam prolis duplex est principium, pater scilicet et mater. Pater est principium *honorabilius,* sed mater est principium *substantialius;* et ideo in *honoribus* et *municipiis* filius sequitur patrem, quoniam illa acquiruntur paterna sollicitudine et in *hereditate* patrimoniali[^p794-11]. Sed quoniam mater est principium *substantialius,* quantum ad id quod *est,* sequitur matrem; et quoniam libertas et servitus respiciunt hominem secundum id quod *est,* non secundum id quod *habet:* hinc est, quod secundum *leges civiles* rationabiliter valde institutum est, ut partus sequatur ventrem; et hinc est, quod coniunctio ad prolem generandam *matrimonium* appellatur[^p794-12]. — Et huic concordat *ius canonicum,* sicut patet in decretali prius posita; concordat etiam *ius divinum,* scriptum in lege Moysaica, ubi dicitur Exodi vigesimo primo[^p794-13], quodsi dederit dominus uxorem alicui, quem emerit, et ille postmodum egredietur <!-- page 795 --> liber, quod filii debent remanere domino, qui dedit ei uxorem, licet ipse egrediatur liber; concordat etiam *multiplex ratio* et *similitudo naturalis.* Et ideo, quia hoc *dictat* ius civile, et ius canonicum *approbat,* et divinum *confirmat,* et naturale *commendat;* ideo recte dicendum, quod partus debet sequi ventrem.

Verumtamen aliquae partes, quae non sunt huiusmodi legibus subiectae, non servant hanc consuetudinem, immo partus sequitur conditionem deterioris[^p795-1]; unde si alter est servus, filii sunt servi, et similiter, si mater ancilla; et si ambo sint diversorum dominorum, filii dividuntur. Hunc morem servat Francia in aliquibus partibus, licet non ubique; sed tamen constitutio legalis multo nobilior est et rationabilior quam haec consuetudo.

1. Ad illud ergo quod primo obiicitur, quod pater est dignius principium; dicendum, quod verum est, non tamen *substantialius;* et ideo in dignitatibus patrem debet sequi, non in substantia sive in libertate corporis sui[^p795-2]. Et est exemplum: quia nobilius principium fructuum agriculturae est cultor quam ager; fructus tamen est agri, sive domini agri.

2. 3. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod sacra Scriptura genealogias attribuit viris; dicendum, quod verum est et conveniens — nam et hunc modum habemus, quod filium denominamus a patre, unde dicitur Petrus Martini, non Berthae, ut in pluribus — similiter repromissio fit viris; tamen ex hoc non sequitur, quod proles debeat sequi virum secundum conditionem *libertatis,* vel *servitutis,* sed secundum conditionem *dignitatis* et *honoris,* quae non substantiam respicit, sed potius *qualitatem.*

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod filius assimilatur patri; dicendum, quod assimilatio non venit a substantia, sed a *virtute formativa,* quae principaliter viget in semine viri, quando masculus generatur; et ideo frequentius filii assimilantur patribus[^p795-3]. Attamen, quantumcumque sit patri similior in *figura* et *proprietatibus,* plus tamen traxit a matre de *substantia;* et ideo ex hoc non sequitur, quod in libertate patrem sequatur[^p795-4].

## English

### Question III. *Whether the condition of the offspring follows the father or the mother.*

Third, it is asked whose condition the offspring follows, whether the father's or the mother's. And that it follows the *father* seems to be the case:

1. Because denomination ought to be made from the more principal and more worthy [principle][^p794-2]; but the father is a nobler and more worthy principle than the mother, and more principal: therefore, if the thing originated ought rather to be regulated according to the rules of the more worthy principle, it seems that the birth ought to follow the father.

2. Likewise, in sacred Scripture[^p794-3] a genealogy of women is never set down, but rather of men: therefore generation principally regards men: therefore also the germ of generation.

3. Likewise, the promise concerning the blessed seed, namely Christ, was made principally to men, although he was to be born of a woman — for the promise was not made to Sarah, or Bathsheba, but to Abraham and David[^p794-4] — therefore it seems that the offspring belongs rather to the man than to the woman: therefore, etc.

4. Likewise, everything originated that is produced according to union follows more that to which it is more assimilated; but sons are more assimilated to the father[^p794-5]: therefore they ought rather to follow the fathers.

**On the contrary:** 1. Concerning the Marriage of slaves[^p794-6] the decretal says: "It seems unfitting that sons begotten of a free woman should be dragged back into servitude": therefore those who are born of a free mother are free: therefore they follow the *mother.*

2. Likewise, if someone sows in another's land, the fruit belongs to the owner of the land, not to the sower[^p794-7]; but the woman's womb, with respect to the man's seed, is as land with respect to the sowing: therefore it seems that, just as the fruit follows the land, so the birth should follow the womb.

3. Likewise, this is seen in animals, since if an animal of the male sex impregnates an animal of the female sex, as a horse [impregnates] a mare, the foal follows the mare, not the [male] horse[^p794-8]: therefore by parity of reasoning it seems so in the matter proposed.

4. Likewise, this is seen *by a similitude* in artificial things: because, if someone builds on another's land, the building yields to the soil, not the soil to the building[^p794-9]: therefore if someone generates in another's womb, the offspring follows the womb.

### Conclusion

> **CONCLUSION.** The condition of the offspring fittingly follows the mother, although in some regions there is a different custom.

**I respond:** It must be said that, according to the civil laws or imperial law[^p794-10], "the birth follows the womb." — And the reason for this is very fitting, since the offspring has a twofold principle, namely the father and the mother. The father is the *more honorable* principle, but the mother is the *more substantial* principle; and therefore in *honors* and *municipal offices* the son follows the father, since these are acquired by the father's care and in the patrimonial *inheritance*[^p794-11]. But since the mother is the *more substantial* principle, as regards that which a thing *is,* [the offspring] follows the mother; and since liberty and servitude regard the human being according to what he *is,* not according to what he *has:* hence it is that, according to the *civil laws,* it has been very reasonably established that the birth should follow the womb; and hence it is that the union for generating offspring is called *matrimony*[^p794-12]. — And with this the *canon law* agrees, as is clear in the decretal set down before; the *divine law* also agrees, written in the Mosaic law, where it is said in Exodus, chapter twenty-one[^p794-13], that if a master gives a wife to someone whom he has bought, and the man afterward goes out <!-- page 795 --> free, the sons ought to remain with the master who gave him the wife, although he himself goes out free; there agrees also *manifold reasoning* and *natural likeness.* And therefore, since the civil law *dictates* this, and the canon law *approves* it, and the divine [law] *confirms* it, and the natural [law] *commends* it; therefore it must rightly be said that the birth ought to follow the womb.

Nevertheless, some regions, which are not subject to laws of this kind, do not keep this custom, but rather the birth follows the condition of the worse [parent][^p795-1]; whence if one [parent] is a slave, the sons are slaves, and likewise if the mother is a bondwoman; and if both belong to different masters, the sons are divided. This custom France keeps in some parts, though not everywhere; but nevertheless the legal ordinance is far nobler and more reasonable than this custom.

1. To that, then, which is first objected, that the father is the more worthy principle; it must be said that this is true, yet not *more substantial;* and therefore in dignities [the offspring] ought to follow the father, not in substance or in the liberty of its own body[^p795-2]. And there is an example: because the more noble principle of the fruits of agriculture is the cultivator than the field; yet the fruit belongs to the field, or to the owner of the field.

2. 3. To that which is objected, that sacred Scripture attributes genealogies to men; it must be said that this is true and fitting — for we also have this manner [of speaking], that we name a son from the father, whence one is called Peter [son] of Martin, not of Bertha, as in most cases — likewise the promise is made to men; yet from this it does not follow that the offspring ought to follow the man according to the condition of *liberty* or *servitude,* but according to the condition of *dignity* and *honor,* which does not regard substance, but rather *quality.*

4. To that which is objected, that the son is assimilated to the father; it must be said that assimilation does not come from substance, but from the *formative power,* which is chiefly vigorous in the man's seed, when a male is generated; and therefore more frequently sons are assimilated to their fathers[^p795-3]. Nevertheless, however much [a son] may be more similar to the father in *figure* and *properties,* he has drawn more from the mother as regards *substance;* and therefore from this it does not follow that in liberty he should follow the father[^p795-4].
