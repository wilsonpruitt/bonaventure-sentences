## *De numero et sufficientia impedimentorum matrimonii*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 768 -->

### Quaestio II. *De numero et sufficientia impedimentorum.*

Secundo quaeritur de numero et sufficientia impedimentorum matrimonii. Et quod sint multa, videtur:

1. Quia impedimenta sunt propter privationem vel defectum boni matrimonii; sed defectus boni malum est et, ut dicit Dionysius[^1], «malum est infinitis modis, quamvis bonum sit uno modo»: ergo infinitis modis videtur quod possit impediri.

2. Item, impedimenta matrimonii accipiuntur secundum accidentia personarum; sed, ut vult Philosophus[^2], accidentia sunt infinita: ergo et impedimenta. *Maior* patet discurrendo in singulis; *minor* patet per Philosophum.

3. Item, impedimenta matrimonii respiciunt matrimonii conditionem, et illud respicit personas singulares; sed «singularia sunt infinita[^3]» et indeterminata: ergo et matrimonii impedimenta.

**Contra:** 1. Nihil impedit matrimonium nisi secundum institutionem Dei vel Ecclesiae; sed institutio Dei et Ecclesiae quantum ad matrimonium est in determinatis conditionibus: ergo etc.

2. Item, impossibile est scire legem matrimonii et vinculum, nisi sciantur illa quae illud vinculum solvunt; sed lex matrimonii est nobis determinata, ergo et impedimenta: ergo sunt in numero definito.

3. Item, hoc ipsum probatur per hoc quod Magister[^4] de huiusmodi determinat et doctrinam dat; sed si non essent in numero determinato, non daret: ergo etc.

> **Conclusio.** Duplici via numerus et sufficientia impedimentorum ostenditur.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod hoc Sacramentum habet impedimenta plura; sed quaedam impediunt ex *congruitate*, et ista dicuntur impedire *contrahendum*, sed non dirimere iam *contractum*, ut est Ecclesiae interdictum et tempus feriatum, quia talia respiciunt solemnitatem matrimonii[^5]. Quaedam impediunt de *necessitate*, quia non stat cum eis Sacramentum; unde dicuntur impedire *contrahendum* et dirimere iam *contractum*; quia, quamvis esset contractum *de facto*, tamen in veritate non fuit Sacramentum nec matrimonium verum. — Impedimenta

<!-- page 769 -->
autem illa his versibus continentur, de quibus omnibus infra[^6] habebitur singillatim.

*Error, conditio, votum, cognatio, crimen,*  
*Cultus disparitas, vis, ordo, ligamen, honestas;*  
*Si sis affinis, si forte coire nequibis:*  
*Haec facienda vetant connubia, iuncta retractant.*

De sufficientia ergo impedimentorum dicendum, quod impedimentum fit sive potest fieri ex quadruplici causa. — Nam ex *efficiente* causa provenire possunt tria. Efficiens causa est consensus; ad consensum autem concurrit *ratio et voluntas*[^7]. Contra rationis actum est *error*; contra actum *voluntatis vis* vel violentia; contra actum *utriusque conditio servitutis*, quae impedit partim propter defectum libertatis, partim propter errorem.

Ex parte causae *materialis* tria: quia matrimonium est coniunctio *voluntarie* introducta, ergo est prius *naturaliter disparatorum*[^8], coniuncti ergo proximitate sunt inepti ad matrimonium. Sed *coniuncti* possunt esse tripliciter: *consanguinitate, affinitate, iustitia publicae honestatis*, quae contrahitur ex sponsalibus per verba de praesenti; et sic sunt alia tria impedimenta.

Ex parte causae *formalis* tria: quia matrimonium est vinculum, in quo est mutua potestas viri et uxoris; impedimentum autem contra hoc est, quando non potest dare sui corporis potestatem. Et hoc potest esse tripliciter: aut quia tradidit alteri, qui adhuc vivit, et sic est *ligatio*; aut quia se dedicavit Deo, et sic est *sacer ordo*; aut quia Deo et praelato, et sic est *votum*, quando quis vovet ingredi religionem, vel continentiam solemnem.

Ex parte *finis* similiter tria: quia matrimonium est ad procreandam prolem ad cultum Dei, vel saltem in remedium honestatis. Contra rationem procreandi, vel remedii est *impotentia coeundi*; contra cultum Dei, qui est in *fide*, est *disparitas cultus*; qui[^9] est in *moribus*, est *criminis enormitas*. — Ita sunt duodecim impedimenta. Similiter aliqua recipiunt divisionem, ut impotentia coeundi et crimen, sicut melius patebit infra[^10].

Posset tamen aliter horum impedimentorum sumi sufficientia. In matrimonio enim considerantur *personae*, quae iunguntur, et ipsum *vinculum*, et illud *per quod* vinciuntur, et illud *ad quod* vinciuntur: quod vincitur, et vinculum, et *ad quod*, et *per quod*. Personae, quae vinciuntur, debent aliquo modo convenire in cultu Dei et fide, sed distare vinculo naturali et consimili, propter hoc quod natura abhorret, personas multum consimiles in actu illo sociari. Multum igitur distant, quando sunt *dispares in cultu*; multum appropinquant, quando sunt iuncti *cognatione*, vel *affinitate*, vel *publicae honestatis iustitia*. Propinquitas *consanguinitatis* est ex fructu matrimonii, sed *affinitatis* ex actu; iustitia publicae honestatis tenet medium inter utrumque. Et sic sunt quatuor impedimenta, scilicet cultus disparitas, cognatio, affinitas et publicae honestatis iustitia. — Consideratur iterum ipsum *vinculum*, quo vinciuntur individualiter; et ob hoc[^11] non possunt coniungi vel vinciri qui sunt aliis obligati. Obligari autem alii est dupliciter: aut *Deo*, aut homini. Et *homini* dupliciter: aut per matrimonium, et sic *ligamen*; aut per servitutem, et sic conditio. Obligari autem *Deo* est dupliciter: aut per votum, aut per illud quod habet *annexum votum*, ut sacer ordo. Et sic sunt alia quatuor: ligamen, conditio, votum et ordo. — Tertio circa matrimonium consideratur illud, *per quod* contrahitur, et sic est consensus; et hic respicit actum *rationis*, et contra hunc est *error*; et actum *voluntatis*, et contra hunc est *violentia*. Et sic sunt duo. — Quarto consideratur[^12] *usus* matrimonii, et hic dupliciter impeditur: aut per impossibilitatem utendi, et sic *impotentia coeundi*; aut per abusum, et sic *enormitas criminis*. — Et sic patet numerus et sufficientia horum impedimentorum, et quod sunt in numero determinato, scilicet duodecim.

**Ad opposita:**
1. Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur, quod malum est modis infinitis; dicendum, quod Dionysius loquitur de malo, quantum est *de se*, quia privatio et non-ens est et caret fine et determinatione. Si autem loquamur de malo, prout ei *substernitur actus*, vel per comparationem ad *bonum*, quod privat; sic ponitur in genere *per reductionem* et species determinatas habet, sicut patet in vitiis[^13]. — Verumtamen illud non valet ad propositum, quia impedimenta matrimonii non sunt defectus vel mala, sed et aliqua bona huic incompossibilia.

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur secundo penes accidentia, dicendum, quod non penes quaecumque accidentia, utpote quod sit albus, vel niger, sed penes

<!-- page 770 -->
accidentia, quae directe respiciunt matrimonium quantum ad aliquod bonum; et ita sunt in numero determinato.

3. Ad illud, quod sunt circa singularia; dicendum, quod sicut operationes virtutum sunt circa singularia, tamen sermo et cognitio est de eis, prout reducuntur ad aliquod universale — primo modo non habet certitudinem, secundo modo habet[^14] — sic in proposito casus emergentes sunt multi et innumerabiles circa speciales personas; tamen omnes sub hac generalitate impedimentorum comprehenduntur. Et sic patet totum[^15].

## English

### Question II. *On the number and sufficiency of the impediments.*

Secondly it is asked concerning the number and sufficiency of the impediments of marriage. And that they are many, it seems:

1. Because impediments exist on account of the privation or defect of the good of marriage; but the defect of the good is an evil, and, as Dionysius says[^1], «evil is in infinite ways, although the good is in one way»: therefore it seems that [marriage] can be impeded in infinite ways.

2. Likewise, the impediments of marriage are taken according to the accidents of persons; but, as the Philosopher holds[^2], accidents are infinite: therefore so also [are] the impediments. The *major* is plain by running through them one by one; the *minor* is plain through the Philosopher.

3. Likewise, the impediments of marriage regard the condition of marriage, and that regards singular persons; but «singulars are infinite[^3]» and indeterminate: therefore so also [are] the impediments of marriage.

**On the contrary:** 1. Nothing impedes marriage except according to the institution of God or of the Church; but the institution of God and of the Church, as regards marriage, [consists] in determinate conditions: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, it is impossible to know the law of marriage and the bond, unless those things be known which dissolve that bond; but the law of marriage is determinate for us, therefore so also [are] the impediments: therefore they are in a definite number.

3. Likewise, this same thing is proved by the fact that the Master[^4] determines concerning such matters and gives a teaching [about them]; but if they were not in a determinate number, he would not give [it]: therefore etc.

> **Conclusion.** By a twofold way the number and sufficiency of the impediments is shown.

**I respond:** It must be said that this Sacrament has many impediments; but some impede from *fittingness*, and these are said to impede the *contracting*, but not to annul what is *already contracted*, as is the interdict of the Church and a festal season, because such things regard the solemnity of marriage[^5]. Some impede from *necessity*, because the Sacrament does not stand together with them; whence they are said to impede the *contracting* and to annul what is *already contracted*; because, although it were contracted *in fact*, yet in truth it was not a Sacrament nor a true marriage. — But those impediments

<!-- page 769 -->
are contained in these verses, concerning all of which it will be treated below[^6] one by one.

*Error, condition, vow, kinship, crime,*  
*Disparity of worship, force, [holy] order, [prior] bond, [public] decency;*  
*If you be a kinsman by affinity, if perchance you be unable to have intercourse:*  
*These things when done forbid marriages, and when joined undo them.*

Concerning the sufficiency, therefore, of the impediments it must be said that an impediment comes about, or can come about, from a fourfold cause. — For from the *efficient* cause three can arise. The efficient cause is consent; and to consent there concur *reason and will*[^7]. Against the act of reason is *error*; against the act of *will* [is] *force* or violence; against the act of *both* [is] the *condition of servitude*, which impedes partly on account of the defect of liberty, partly on account of error.

From the side of the *material* cause, three: because marriage is a union voluntarily introduced, therefore it is prior [as a union] of things *naturally* disparate[^8], and therefore those joined by [too great] proximity are unfit for marriage. But those *joined* can be [so] in three ways: by *consanguinity, affinity, [and] the justice of public decency*, which is contracted from a betrothal by words of the present [tense]; and thus there are another three impediments.

From the side of the *formal* cause, three: because marriage is a bond, in which there is a mutual power of the husband and of the wife; but the impediment against this is when one cannot give the power of one's own body. And this can be in three ways: either because he has handed [it] over to another, who is still living, and thus it is a *[prior] bond*; or because he has dedicated himself to God, and thus it is *holy order*; or because [he is bound] to God and to a prelate, and thus it is a *vow*, when one vows to enter religion, or solemn continence.

From the side of the *end* likewise three: because marriage is for the procreating of offspring for the worship of God, or at least as a remedy for decency. Against the ground of procreating, or of the remedy, is *impotence for intercourse*; against the worship of God, which consists in *faith*, is *disparity of worship*; [and against that worship] which consists in *morals*, is the *enormity of crime*[^9]. — Thus there are twelve impediments. Likewise some admit of a division, as impotence for intercourse and crime, as will better appear below[^10].

Yet the sufficiency of these impediments could be taken otherwise. For in marriage there are considered the *persons* who are joined, and the *bond* itself, and that *by which* they are bound, and that *unto which* they are bound: that which is bound, and the bond, and that *unto which*, and that *by which*. The persons who are bound ought in some way to agree in the worship of God and in faith, but to stand apart by a natural and like bond, on account of this, that nature abhors that persons very like [one another] be associated in that act. Much, therefore, do they stand apart, when they are *disparate in worship*; much do they draw near, when they are joined by *kinship*, or by *affinity*, or by the *justice of public decency*. Nearness of *consanguinity* is from the fruit of marriage, but [that] of *affinity* from the act; the justice of public decency holds the middle between the two. And thus there are four impediments, namely disparity of worship, kinship, affinity, and the justice of public decency. — Again there is considered the *bond* itself, by which they are bound individually; and on account of this[^11] those who are bound to others cannot be joined or bound [again]. But to be bound to others is [so] in two ways: either to *God* or to a man. And to a man in two ways: either through matrimony, and thus [it is] a *[prior] bond*; or through servitude, and thus [it is] condition. But to be bound to *God* is in two ways: either through a vow, or through that which has a vow annexed, as holy order. And thus there are another four: [prior] bond, condition, vow, and order. — Thirdly, concerning marriage there is considered that *by which* it is contracted, and thus it is consent; and this regards the act of *reason*, and against this is *error*; and the act of *will*, and against this is *violence*. And thus there are two. — Fourthly there is considered[^12] the *use* of marriage, and this is impeded in two ways: either through the impossibility of using [it], and thus [it is] *impotence for intercourse*; or through abuse, and thus [it is] the *enormity of crime*. — And thus the number and sufficiency of these impediments is plain, and that they are in a determinate number, namely twelve.

**To the objections:**
1. To that, then, which is objected, that evil is in infinite ways; it must be said that Dionysius is speaking of evil, insofar as it is *in itself*, because it is a privation and non-being, and lacks end and determination. But if we speak of evil, insofar as an *act underlies* it, or by comparison to the *good* which it deprives; thus it is placed in a genus *by reduction* and has determinate species, as is plain in the vices[^13]. — Nevertheless that does not avail for the point at issue, because the impediments of marriage are not defects or evils, but also certain goods incompatible with this [marriage].

2. To that which is objected secondly by way of accidents, it must be said that [they are taken] not by way of just any accidents, such as that [someone] be white or black, but by way of

<!-- page 770 -->
accidents which directly regard marriage as to some good; and thus they are in a determinate number.

3. To that [objection], that they are about singulars; it must be said that just as the operations of the virtues are about singulars, yet speech and knowledge are of them insofar as they are reduced to some universal — in the first way [knowledge] does not have certitude, in the second way it does[^14] — so in the matter at hand the emerging cases are many and innumerable concerning particular persons; yet all are comprehended under this generality of impediments. And thus the whole [matter] is plain[^15].
