## Latin
<!-- page 634 -->

### Quaestio IV. *Utrum ordines sint septem, an plures, vel pauciores.*

Quarto quaeritur, utrum ordines sint septem, an plures, vel pauciores. Et quod tantum septem sint:

1. Hoc expresse magister Hugo[^p634-4] dicit; Magister etiam dicit in littera.

2. Item, ratione videtur: quia signum debet respondere veritati; sed in Sacramento isto septiformis gratia Spiritus sancti datur[^p634-5]: ergo videtur, quod, si gradus in ordine assignantur, quod septem esse debent.

Sed quod debeant esse tantum tres, videtur:

1. Auctoritate Dionysii, de Ecclesiastica Hierarchia[^p634-6], ubi non ponit nisi tres gradus, scilicet ministros, sacerdotes et episcopos.

2. Item, hoc videtur ratione: quia distinctio graduum debet esse penes actus hierarchicos; isti autem actus sunt tres tantummodo, scilicet «purgare, illuminare et perficere[^p634-7]»: ergo videtur, quod sint tantum tres gradus sive ordines.

Sed quod sint plures, videtur:

3. Quoniam Ecclesia militans imitatur triumphantem, secundum quod dicitur Exodi vigesimo quinto[^p634-8]: *Inspice, et fac secundum exemplar, quod tibi monstratum est* etc.; sed in illa est distinctio et gradus in Angelis secundum numerum novenarium: ergo etc.

4. Item, quanto maior est recessus a principio, tanto magis tenditur in multitudinem[^p634-9]; sed hierarchia ecclesiastica plus distat a principio omnium, Deo summo, quam angelica: ergo pluribus gradibus et ordinibus debet distingui quam angelica: ergo plures deberent esse ordines quam novem.

Est igitur quaestio: propter quid in hoc Sacramento magis est graduum distinctio quam in alio; et propter quid in numero septenario?

> **Conclusio.** Ordines sunt septem et ad unum sacerdotium ordinati.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod omnes in hoc concordant, scilicet quod huic Sacramento competit graduum
<!-- page 635 -->
distinctio, sive propter hoc, quod penes ipsum membra Ecclesiae in corpore mystico ordinantur secundum diversas operationes et gratias, secundum quod dicit Apostolus primae ad Corinthios duodecimo[^p635-1]; sive propter hoc, quod secundum illud Sacramentum configuratur et conformatur pulcritudo Ecclesiae militantis pulcritudini Ecclesiae triumphantis; sive propter hoc, quod istud Sacramentum in suo supremo ponit et disponit hominem ad gratiam perfectionis, et gratia perfecta in pluralitate donorum Spiritus sancti consistit; sive propter hoc, quod istud Sacramentum ordinat ad dignissimum ministerium, quod est consecratio dominici corporis, ad quam non statim est convolandum, sed per gradus paulative ascendendum, cum[^p635-2] illud Sacramentum propter sui nobilitatem et reverentiam multa debeat habere ministeria inferiora. — Omnibus his modis accipitur ratio pluralitatis in gradibus ordinum; et secundum aspectum ad has rationes plurificandi sumitur sufficientia graduum ecclesiasticorum diversimode a diversis.

Quidam enim sumunt penes distinctionem membrorum Ecclesiae, quae attenditur secundum *dona gratiarum*, quae determinat Apostolus primae ad Corinthios duodecimo[^p635-3] dicens: *Alii datur per Spiritum sermo sapientiae* etc.; et isti dicunt, *novem* esse ordines secundum genera gratiarum, quae ibi ponuntur, quae sunt novem. Unde adaptant sic: *Alii datur per Spiritum sermo sapientiae*, scilicet episcopo; *alii sermo scientiae*, scilicet sacerdoti, qui debet scire ligare et solvere; *alii fides*, scilicet diacono, qui debet Evangelium nuntiare, in quo fides nostra continetur; *operatio virtutum* subdiacono, qui aggreditur opera perfectionis; *interpretatio sermonum* acolytho, cuius est alios illuminare et tenebras effugare; *gratia sanitatum* exorcistae; *genera linguarum* psalmistae; *prophetia* lectori; et *discretio spirituum* ostiario, qui admittit bonos et repellit malos. — Sed iste modus sumendi non est omnino competens, quoniam ista dona[^p635-4] gratiarum non eisdem, sed diversis conveniunt membris; omnes autem ordines eisdem dantur, licet successive.

Secundi sumunt aliquantulum aptius penes *conformitatem exempli ad exemplatum*[^p635-5]; dicunt enim, quod sicut in angelica hierarchia est distinctio ordinum penes *purgare, illuminare* et *perficere*, sic et in ecclesiastica. Unde *novem* sunt ordines, secundum quod sumsit hos tres actus triplicatos. Primo enim actui *purgationis* respondent tres: quoniam aut purgari habet *exterius*, aut *interius*, aut *partim sic, partim sic*. Si *exterius* per segregationem corporalem[^p635-6] a malis; sic est ostiarius, qui admittit bonos et excludit malos. Si *interius*; sic est postremus inter inferiores, scilicet acolythus, quia talis non purgat tenebras corporales, cum lumen ferat de die, sed spirituales. Si *partim sic, partim sic*; sic exorcista, qui purgat a vexatione diabolica, quae est quodam modo *intra* respectu corporis, quodam modo *extra* respectu animae, quia non illabitur daemon animae, licet obsideat corpus. — Similiter quantum ad actum *illuminationis* tres ordines accipiuntur, secundum quod triplex est lumen, scilicet *plenum, subsequens* et *antecedens*. Lumen *plenum* est in doctrina evangelica, scilicet doctrina Christi, quae spectat ad diaconos; lumen *subsequens* in doctrina apostolica, quae spectat ad subdiaconos; lumen *praecedens* in prophetica[^p635-7], quae spectat ad lectores. — Similiter secundum opus *perfectionis* sive consummationis triplex est ordo: quia quaedam est consummatio *prima*, utpote per gratiam baptismi et poenitentiae, haec debetur sacerdotibus; quaedam *excellentior*, et haec episcopis, quorum est ordinare et consecrare abbates et virgines; quaedam *excellentissima*, utpote consecrare episcopos et archiepiscopos, et haec pertinet ad Summum Pontificem, quia est «pater patrum[^p635-8]», in quo est plenitudo auctoritatis. Et sic status istius ordinationis *consummatur* in unitate et *descendit* per gradus novem, ut sic conformetur Ierusalem caelesti. Et iste novenarius reducitur ad ternarium actum, ut sic ascendat et configuretur beatissimae Trinitati: nam *purgatio* ad bonitatem, *illuminatio* vero ad sapientiam, sed *consummatio* ad potestatem. Et sic regnum Ecclesiae per Spiritum sanctum et Filium *traditur Deo et Patri*[^p635-9], dum ad ipsam supremam et paternam unitatem reducitur, ultra quam non est cogitare ascensum. — Et iste modus est satis probabilis; tamen adhuc aliquantulum a proprietate recedit, dum non accipitur ordinum distinctio per id quod est proprium sibi et intrinsecum.

Tertii adhuc sumunt aliquantulum aptius penes *gratiam*, ad quam ordinat ordo. Quoniam enim ordinat ad gratiam perfectam, quae septiformis est, ideo ordinum ecclesiasticorum septem competit esse
<!-- page 636 -->
gradus, ut ordo ostiarii respondeat dono *timoris*, per quod recedit homo a malo; ordo sacerdotii dono *sapientiae*, quia gustat et administrat ipsum lignum vitae, ipsum panem caelestem, in quo est omnis sapor[^p636-1]; ordines intermedii respondent *intermediis* donis. — Et iste adhuc modus satis est probabilis et magis attingit ad sufficientiam ordinum vere assignandam quam aliquis praedictorum.

Licet autem omnes hi modi ex dictis magistrorum tracti sint; nam primus ex dictis Isidori[^p636-2], secundus ex dictis Dionysii, tertius ex dictis Hugonis et Magistri; sed tamen adhuc videtur aliquid rationabilius posse dici, si quartum modum sumendi attendamus, qui est penes id, ad quod ordo ordinatur; et hoc quidem est ad corpus Christi verum dispensandum ad utilitatem corporis Christi mystici. Unde in sacerdotio est status, in quo est consummatio potestatis, et utraque potestas in ipso concurrit, scilicet *conficiendi* corpus Christi verum et *absolvendi* mysticum. Hic autem ordo nobilissimus debet habere ordines sibi subministrantes secundum exigentiam huius duplicis potentiae. Unde quidam ministrant quantum ad corpus Christi *verum*; et hi sunt ordines ipsi sacerdotio propinquiores. Nam dupliciter est ministrare: aut accipiendo hostias a plebe, et hoc est subdiaconi; aut offerendo sacerdoti, et hoc est diaconi et illud est principale eorum officium, sicut expresse innuit Isidorus[^p636-3]; et hinc est, quod sic denominantur: unde diaconus quasi *minister*, et subdiaconus quasi *subminister*. — Alii inferiores subministrant sive subserviunt praeparando corpus Christi *mysticum* ad hoc, quod possit perduci ad verum digne percipiendum; et ad hoc sunt quatuor ordines, secundum quod quadrupliciter potest ad hoc praeparari: primo modo admittendo ad locum sacrum: et hoc est ostiariorum; secundo praebendo documentum, et hoc est lectorum; tertio praestando auxilium, et hoc exorcistarum; quarto monstrando bonum exemplum, et hoc ceroferariorum sive acolythorum, ad quos pertinet, *ut luceat lux eorum coram hominibus, ut videant*[^p636-4] etc.

Et sic patet, quod miro modo procedit ordo a multitudine in unitatem. Nam principalis gradus est *unus*, cui subservientes debent esse *duo* — quia enim a primo deficiunt, cadunt in dualitatem — et rursus subservientes duobus sunt *quatuor*, quia deficiunt etiam ab illis. — Et sic patet perfectio et consummatio ordinum et distinctio in numero septenario secundum gradus potestatum, quae sunt essentiales ordini; quarum numerus et sufficientia sumitur penes id, ad quod directae sunt. — His visis, obiecta de facili satis patent. Quod enim obiicitur, quod sunt septem, concedendum.

1. Quod vero obiicitur, quod sunt tres, per[^p636-5] Dionysium; dicendum, quod Dionysius sub ministris comprehendit septem gradus, qui omnes administrant sacerdoti.

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur de illo triplici actu, dicendum, quod distinctio ordinum non sumitur penes illum triplicem actum, sed potius secundum actum ministrandi; et si etiam sumeretur, adhuc non valeret, quia illi[^p636-6] plurificantur, ut patet in Angelis.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod imitatur supernam Ierusalem; dicendum, quod verum est, secundum quod suo statui competit. In illa autem superna Ierusalem ordines assignantur supremi, inferiores et medii secundum aspectus *diversos*, sicut patet in distinctione hierarchiarum angelicarum[^p636-7]; ordines autem ecclesiastici sumuntur per comparationem ad *unum* principaliter, quia ad hoc sunt; et hinc est, quod nec illo modo plurificantur nec in tot membra, in quot ordines angelici[^p636-8].

## English

### Question IV. *Whether the orders are seven, or more, or fewer.*

Fourthly it is asked, whether the orders are seven, or more, or fewer. And that there are only seven:

1. This the master Hugh expressly says[^p634-4]; the Master also says [it] in the text.

2. Likewise, it seems by reason: because a sign ought to correspond to the truth; but in this Sacrament the sevenfold grace of the Holy Spirit is given[^p634-5]: therefore it seems that, if grades are assigned in the order, they ought to be seven.

But that they ought to be only three, it seems:

1. By the authority of Dionysius, *On the Ecclesiastical Hierarchy*[^p634-6], where he posits only three grades, namely ministers, priests, and bishops.

2. Likewise, this seems by reason: because the distinction of grades ought to be according to the hierarchical acts; but these acts are only three, namely «to purge, to illuminate, and to perfect[^p634-7]»: therefore it seems that there are only three grades or orders.

But that there are more, it seems:

3. Because the Church militant imitates the [Church] triumphant, according to what is said in Exodus twenty-five[^p634-8]: *Look, and make [it] according to the exemplar, which was shown to you* etc.; but in that [Church] there is distinction and grade among the Angels according to the ninefold number: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, the greater the withdrawal from the principle, the more it is stretched out into multitude[^p634-9]; but the ecclesiastical hierarchy is farther distant from the principle of all, the most high God, than the angelic: therefore it ought to be distinguished by more grades and orders than the angelic: therefore there ought to be more orders than nine.

There is therefore a question: on account of what in this Sacrament is there a distinction of grades more than in another; and on account of what in the sevenfold number?

> **Conclusion.** The orders are seven and ordained to one priesthood.

**I respond:** It must be said that all agree in this, namely that a distinction of grades befits this Sacrament, whether on account of this, that in it the members of the Church are ordered in the mystical body according to diverse operations and graces, according to what the Apostle says in the first to the Corinthians, chapter twelve[^p635-1]; or on account of this, that according to this Sacrament the beauty of the Church militant is configured and conformed to the beauty of the Church triumphant; or on account of this, that this Sacrament in its highest [grade] sets and disposes man toward the grace of perfection, and perfect grace consists in the plurality of the gifts of the Holy Spirit; or on account of this, that this Sacrament ordains toward the most worthy ministry, which is the consecration of the Lord's body, to which one is not to hasten at once, but to ascend little by little through grades, since[^p635-2] this Sacrament, on account of its nobility and reverence, ought to have many lower ministries. — In all these ways the ground of plurality in the grades of the orders is taken; and according to the regard to these grounds of multiplying, the sufficiency of the ecclesiastical grades is derived in diverse ways by diverse [authors].

For some take [it] according to the distinction of the members of the Church, which is attended to according to the *gifts of graces*, which the Apostle determines in the first to the Corinthians, chapter twelve[^p635-3], saying: *To one is given through the Spirit the word of wisdom* etc.; and these say that there are *nine* orders according to the kinds of graces which are there posited, which are nine. Whence they adapt [it] thus: *To one is given through the Spirit the word of wisdom*, namely to the bishop; *to another the word of knowledge*, namely to the priest, who ought to know how to bind and to loose; *to another faith*, namely to the deacon, who ought to announce the Gospel, in which our faith is contained; *the working of powers* to the subdeacon, who undertakes the works of perfection; *the interpretation of discourses* to the acolyte, whose [task] is to illuminate others and to put darkness to flight; *the grace of healings* to the exorcist; *the kinds of tongues* to the psalmist; *prophecy* to the lector; and *the discernment of spirits* to the doorkeeper, who admits the good and repels the evil. — But this way of taking [it] is not altogether fitting, since these gifts of graces[^p635-4] do not belong to the same, but to diverse members; whereas all the orders are given to the same [persons], though successively.

The second [group] take [it] somewhat more fittingly according to the *conformity of the copy to the exemplar*[^p635-5]; for they say that just as in the angelic hierarchy there is a distinction of orders according to *purging, illuminating,* and *perfecting*, so also in the ecclesiastical. Whence there are *nine* orders, according as it took these three acts tripled. For to the first act, of *purgation*, three correspond: since one must be purged either *exteriorly*, or *interiorly*, or *partly thus, partly thus*. If *exteriorly*, by bodily segregation[^p635-6] from the evil; thus is the doorkeeper, who admits the good and excludes the evil. If *interiorly*; thus is the last among the lower [ones], namely the acolyte, since such a one does not purge bodily darkness, since he bears the light by day, but spiritual [darkness]. If *partly thus, partly thus*; thus the exorcist, who purges from the diabolical vexation, which is in a certain way *within* with respect to the body, in a certain way *without* with respect to the soul, since the demon does not glide into the soul, although he besiege the body. — Likewise, as regards the act of *illumination*, three orders are received, according as the light is threefold, namely *full, subsequent,* and *antecedent*. The *full* light is in the evangelical teaching, namely the teaching of Christ, which pertains to deacons; the *subsequent* light in the apostolic teaching, which pertains to subdeacons; the *preceding* light in the prophetic[^p635-7], which pertains to lectors. — Likewise, according to the work of *perfection* or consummation the order is threefold: since there is a *first* consummation, as [that] through the grace of baptism and of penance, this is owed to priests; another *more excellent*, and this to bishops, whose [task] is to ordain and to consecrate abbots and virgins; another *most excellent*, as [that] to consecrate bishops and archbishops, and this pertains to the Supreme Pontiff, since he is «father of fathers[^p635-8]», in whom is the fullness of authority. And thus the state of this ordination is *consummated* in unity and *descends* through nine grades, so that it may thus be conformed to the heavenly Jerusalem. And this ninefold [number] is reduced to the ternary act, so that it may thus ascend and be configured to the most blessed Trinity: for *purgation* [is directed] to goodness, *illumination* indeed to wisdom, but *consummation* to power. And thus the kingdom of the Church is *handed over to God and the Father*[^p635-9] through the Holy Spirit and the Son, while it is reduced to that supreme and paternal unity, beyond which no ascent can be thought. — And this way is probable enough; yet it still withdraws somewhat from propriety, since the distinction of orders is not taken according to that which is proper and intrinsic to it.

The third [group] still take [it] somewhat more fittingly according to the *grace* to which the order ordains. For since it ordains toward perfect grace, which is sevenfold, therefore it befits that there be seven of the ecclesiastical orders: as
grades, so that the order of doorkeeper corresponds to the gift of *fear*, through which man withdraws from evil; the order of priesthood to the gift of *wisdom*, since he tastes and administers the very tree of life, the very heavenly bread, in which is all savor[^p636-1]; the intermediate orders correspond to the *intermediate* gifts. — And this way too is probable enough and attains more to the sufficiency of the orders to be truly assigned than any of the aforesaid.

But although all these ways are drawn from the sayings of the masters — for the first from the sayings of Isidore[^p636-2], the second from the sayings of Dionysius, the third from the sayings of Hugh and the Master — yet still it seems that something more reasonable can be said, if we attend to the fourth way of taking [it], which is according to that to which the order is ordained; and this indeed is toward the dispensing of the true body of Christ for the benefit of the mystical body of Christ. Whence in the priesthood there is the state in which is the consummation of power, and each power concurs in it, namely of *confecting* the true body of Christ and of *absolving* the mystical [body]. This most noble order, however, ought to have orders subministering to it according to the exigency of this twofold power. Whence some minister with respect to the *true* body of Christ; and these are the orders nearer to the priesthood itself. For it is to minister in two ways: either by receiving the hosts from the people, and this is [the office] of the subdeacon; or by offering to the priest, and this is [the office] of the deacon and that is their principal office, as Isidore expressly intimates[^p636-3]; and hence it is that they are so named: whence the deacon [is] as it were a *minister*, and the subdeacon as it were a *subminister*. — Others, lower, subminister or subserve by preparing the *mystical* body of Christ to the end that it may be able to be led to the true [body] to be worthily received; and to this [end] there are four orders, according as one can be prepared for this in fourfold manner: in the first way by admitting to the sacred place: and this is [the office] of doorkeepers; in the second by furnishing instruction, and this is [the office] of lectors; in the third by rendering aid, and this [the office] of exorcists; in the fourth by showing a good example, and this [the office] of taper-bearers or acolytes, to whom it pertains, *that their light may shine before men, that they may see*[^p636-4] etc.

And thus it is clear that the order proceeds in a wonderful way from multitude into unity. For the principal grade is *one*, to which those subserving ought to be *two* — since indeed they fall short of the first, they fall into duality — and again those subserving the two are *four*, since they fall short also of those. — And thus is clear the perfection and consummation of the orders and the distinction in the sevenfold number according to the grades of powers, which are essential to the order; whose number and sufficiency is taken according to that to which they are directed. — These things seen, the objections are sufficiently clear with ease. For what is objected, that they are seven, is to be conceded.

1. But as to what is objected, that they are three, through[^p636-5] Dionysius; it must be said that Dionysius comprehends under the ministers seven grades, which all administer to the priest.

2. To that which is objected concerning that threefold act, it must be said that the distinction of orders is not taken according to that threefold act, but rather according to the act of ministering; and even if it were taken [thus], it still would not hold, since those are multiplied[^p636-6], as is clear in the Angels.

3. To that which is objected, that it imitates the heavenly Jerusalem; it must be said that it is true, according as it befits its state. But in that heavenly Jerusalem the orders are assigned as supreme, lower, and middle according to *diverse* regards, as is clear in the distinction of the angelic hierarchies[^p636-7]; but the ecclesiastical orders are taken by comparison to *one* principally, since they are [directed] to this; and hence it is that they are neither multiplied in that way nor into as many members as [there are] angelic orders[^p636-8].
