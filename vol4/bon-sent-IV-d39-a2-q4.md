# d.39 a2-q4
## *Utrum in infidelibus venientibus ad fidem solvatur matrimonium*

---

## Latin

### Quaestio IV. *Utrum in infidelibus venientibus ad fidem solvatur matrimonium.*

<!-- page 841 -->

Quarto quaeritur, utrum in infidelibus venientibus ad fidem solvatur matrimonium propter personarum illegitimitatem, ut si habet sororem in uxorem. Et quod non, videtur:

1. Per illam decretalem Innocentii, *Gaudemus*[^p841-8], in qua dicit, non esse separandos.

2. Item, *ratione* videtur: « Per Sacramentum baptismi coniugia non solvuntur, sed crimina tolluntur[^p841-9] »: ergo cum istud fuerit coniugium apud paganos, non solvitur, cum baptizatur.

3. Item, « pagani canonicis constitutionibus non arctantur[^p841-10] », sed tunc solum incipiunt arctari, quando ad fidem veniunt; « sed iura et constitutiones formam futuris imponunt negotiis, non ad praeterita revocantur »: si ergo ante contraxit, quam arctetur; constitutio tale matrimonium non solvit.

**Contra:** 1. Si aliquis infidelis contraxit cum duabus, ad fidem veniens compellitur alteram relinquere, quia hoc est contra constitutiones canonicas, sicut probatur per decretalem *de Divortiis*[^p841-11], *Gaudemus* etc.: ergo a simili in proposito.

2. Item, si aliquis infidelis repudiavit uxorem quod quidem licitum est apud eos, ambobus venientibus ad fidem, compellitur recipere eam quia hoc, apud fideles non licet: ergo a simili in contrario tenetur talem uxorem dimittere.

3. Item, apud fideles profanum est uxorem accipere sororem uxoris[^p841-12]; sed noviter conversus debet se fidelibus conformare: ergo debet huiusmodi coniugium reputare profanum: ergo non debet amplius stare.

> **Conclusio.** Quando conversi ad fidem matrimonium contraxerunt cum impedimento dirimente secundum legem naturae et divinam, separandi sunt; secus est, si impedimentum est ex constitutione Ecclesiae.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod illegitimitas personarum ad matrimonium est duplex. Una est se-

<!-- page 842 -->

cundum *naturam* et *institutionem divinam*, ut puta quod filius non contrahat cum matre, neque pater cum filia[^p842-1]; item, quod alligatus uxori non ducat aliam, vel etiam in gradibus divina lege prohibitis Levitici decimo octavo[^p842-2], ut dicunt iurisperiti, ubi prohibetur secundus gradus; et haec illegitimitas astringit fideles et infideles. Et si in tali illegitimitate contrahitur matrimonium apud infideles, solvitur, cum veniunt ad fidem; quia, ut dicit Ambrosius[^p842-3], « quod extra Dei decretum factum est non est putandum matrimonium, sed, cum cognoscitur, emendandum ». Unde si Iudaeus contrahat cum sorore in secundo gradu, ad fidem veniens ab ea separatur. Si autem contraxit cum uxore fratris, quia hoc est in Lege concessum[^p842-4], minime separatur. — Alia est illegitimitas ex *constitutione Ecclesiae*, ut sunt personae infra quartum gradum; et haec non arctat infideles nec impedit matrimonium, quin contrahatur; et quia non ad praeterita revocantur[^p842-5], non solvunt legitime iam contractum.

*Ad 1, 2.* Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur de bigamia et repudio, solvendum, quod utrumque est contra ius naturae et contra ius divinum[^p842-6]; ideo non est simile.

*Ad 3.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod profanum est sororem uxoris[^p842-7] ducere in uxorem, vel etiam habere; dicendum, quod hoc verum est propter *Ecclesiae mandatum*; quo quia iste non astringitur, non debet illud coniugium reputare profanum; unde iste, ex quo venit ad fidem, alligatur, ut amplius non contrahat contra Ecclesiae statutum; si tamen contraxit, non debet solvi, nec tenetur dimittere, quia ad ipsum non se extendit constitutio Ecclesiae[^p842-8].

## English

### Question IV. *Whether among infidels coming to the faith the marriage is dissolved.*

<!-- page 841 -->

Fourthly it is asked whether among infidels coming to the faith the marriage is dissolved on account of the illegitimacy of the persons, as for instance if one has a sister as wife. And that it is not [dissolved], it seems:

1. By that decretal of Innocent, *Gaudemus*[^p841-8], in which he says that they are not to be separated.

2. Likewise, it seems by reason: "Through the Sacrament of baptism marriages are not dissolved, but crimes are taken away[^p841-9]": therefore, since that was a marriage among the pagans, it is not dissolved when he is baptized.

3. Likewise, "pagans are not bound by canonical constitutions[^p841-10]," but they only begin to be bound when they come to the faith; "but laws and constitutions impose their form on future affairs, they are not called back to the past": if therefore he contracted before he is bound, the constitution does not dissolve such a marriage.

**On the contrary:** 1. If some infidel contracted [marriage] with two [women], upon coming to the faith he is compelled to relinquish one, because this is against the canonical constitutions, as is proved through the decretal *On Divorces*[^p841-11], *Gaudemus* etc.: therefore by a similar [argument] in the matter proposed.

2. Likewise, if some infidel repudiated his wife — which indeed is licit among them — when both come to the faith, he is compelled to take her back, because this, among the faithful, is not licit: therefore by a similar [argument] on the contrary he is bound to dismiss such a wife.

3. Likewise, among the faithful it is profane to take the sister of one's wife as wife[^p841-12]; but the newly converted ought to conform himself to the faithful: therefore he ought to reckon a marriage of this kind profane: therefore it ought no longer to stand.

> **Conclusion.** When those converted to the faith have contracted marriage with a diriment impediment according to the law of nature and the divine [law], they are to be separated; it is otherwise if the impediment is from a constitution of the Church.

**I respond:** It must be said that the illegitimacy of persons with respect to marriage is twofold. One is ac-

<!-- page 842 -->

cording to *nature* and *divine institution*, as, for instance, that a son not contract with his mother, nor a father with his daughter[^p842-1]; likewise, that one bound to a wife not take another, or also in the degrees prohibited by divine law in Leviticus chapter eighteen[^p842-2], as the jurists say, where the second degree is prohibited; and this illegitimacy binds both the faithful and infidels. And if in such illegitimacy a marriage is contracted among infidels, it is dissolved when they come to the faith; because, as Ambrose says[^p842-3], "what is done outside God's decree is not to be reckoned a marriage, but, when it is recognized, is to be amended." Hence if a Jew contracts with a sister in the second degree, upon coming to the faith he is separated from her. But if he contracted with his brother's wife, since this was permitted in the Law[^p842-4], he is by no means separated. — The other [kind of] illegitimacy is from a *constitution of the Church*, as are persons within the fourth degree; and this does not bind infidels nor impede the marriage from being contracted; and because [such constitutions] are not called back to the past[^p842-5], they do not dissolve what was legitimately already contracted.

*To 1, 2.* To that, then, which is objected concerning bigamy and repudiation, it must be resolved that each is against the law of nature and against the divine law[^p842-6]; therefore it is not similar.

*To 3.* To that which is objected, that it is profane to take the sister of one's wife[^p842-7] as wife, or even to have her; it must be said that this is true on account of the *Church's mandate*; and since this man is not bound by it, he ought not to reckon that marriage profane; hence this man, from the time he came to the faith, is bound not to contract further against the Church's statute; if nevertheless he did contract, it ought not to be dissolved, nor is he bound to dismiss [her], because the constitution of the Church does not extend to him[^p842-8].
