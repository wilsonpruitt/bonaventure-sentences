# d.25 dubia
## *Dubia circa litteram Magistri*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 656 -->

### Dub. I.

In parte ista sunt dubitationes circa litteram, et primo quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Nec ex his aliquem in clericatu honorem vel exiguum subrogare.* Videtur enim male dicere, quia aut iste habet *ordinem,* aut *nihil* habet. Si *nihil* habet: ergo poterit ab episcopo catholico promoveri cum dispensatione, sicut patet in haereticis. Si habet *ordines:* ergo iam promotus est.

**Respondeo,** quod hoc non dicit, quia non *sit ordinatus,* sed quia *male sit ordinatus.* Male autem est ordinatus dupliciter: aut quia est ordinatus *a quo* non debet, aut quia *sicut* non debet. Primo modo tripliciter: aut quia ordinatur ab *haeretico,* vel *simoniaco,* sicut hic in proposito; tunc enim, si scienter facit et communicat haeresi, vel simoniae, non potest exsequi nec dispensatur de facili, et hoc vult dicere in littera[^1]. — Aut ordinatur a *catholico non suo* sine licentia episcopi proprii et auctoritate; talis debet deponi, Causa septima, quaestione prima[^2], dispensari tamen potest, si talis ordinans aliquam ponit spem in ratihabitione episcopi proprii. — Aut ordinatur ab episcopo, qui *renuntiavit loco et of*<!-- page 657 -->*ficio,* quia talis non debet conferre ordines; si vero renuntiavit *loco tantum,* potest dare minores ordines et de licentia episcopi potest dare omnes; et qui sine licentia episcopi proprii recipit ordines posset deponi de rigore iuris, potest tamen cum eo dispensari[^3].

Secundo modo dicitur *male ordinatus,* quia non recipit *eo modo,* quo debet; et tunc aut *furtive,* aut *per saltum.* Si *furtive,* hoc est dupliciter: aut quia *duos* recipit et eodem die, ita quod alterum furatur, loquendo de *sacris;* et poena omnium talium est depositio, sicut dicitur distinctione septuagesima septima[^4], *Subdiaconus,* Extra, de Eo qui furtive, *Innotuit* etc. Si autem furtive recipit *unum tantum;* tunc aut *fuit prohibitum* sub interminatione anathematis; et tunc solus Papa dispensat, Extra, de Eo qui furtive. Si autem *non fuit prohibitum;* tunc potest permitti in minoribus, et si intrat claustrum, praelatus potest dispensare cum eo. — Si vero est ordinatus *per saltum,* hoc est dupliciter: aut ex *malitia,* aut ex *negligentia.* Si *ex malitia;* licet characterem recipiat, tamen debet deponi. Si *ignoranter;* sic, poena sibi imposita ab episcopo, poterit ad inferiorem ordinem promoveri, suspenso eo interim ab exsecutione superioris, distinctione quinquagesima secunda[^5], Extra, de Clerico ordinato per saltum.

Nota etiam hic, quod licet possit saltari ab uno ordine, omisso alio, praetermisso tamen *sacerdotio,* non potest esse episcopus, Extra, de Excessibus praelatorum, *Ex litteris,* in fine[^6]. Si vero omisit *diaconatum,* episcopus est, sed illum ordinem, quem omisit, dare non potest, Causa prima, quaestione prima[^7]. Ita tamen non est in baptismo, quia est Sacramentum primum.

### Dub. II.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *omnes dicuntur simoniaci, et dantes et accipientes.* Videtur enim male dicere, quia Simon solum voluit emere: ergo soli emptores *simoniaci* debent dici, et venditores *Giezitae*[^8]. — Item, plus fecit Giezi quam Simon, quia Simon solum voluit emere, sed Giezi vendidit: ergo magis ab ipso debent denominari.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Simon ideo voluit emere, ut post venderet; ideo utrumque malum et peccatum sibi imputatur, et ideo utrique ab ipso denominantur.

Quod obiicitur de voluntate, dicendum quod quia iste iam tempore gratiae revelatae et maiorem potestatem et spiritualiorem voluit emere; et iterum pactum voluit facere: ideo gravius satis et enormius peccavit, quam ille qui petiit ex quadam liberalitate, et credebat ille, quod esset modicum; sed poena leprae sempiternae[^9] ostendit, quam magnum esset illud scelus.

### Dub. III.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *utrique eadem sententia percelluntur.* Videtur enim male dicere, quia gravius peccant vendentes, ut videtur, quia minus appretiant quam ementes: ergo gravius debent puniri. — *Contrarium* videtur, quia emens praestat illi occasionem et accipit[^10] ultra sortem: ergo si gravius peccat usuras accipiens quam dans, patet etc.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod quantum ad *genus* peccati est uniformitas, quia uterque gratiae Spiritus sancti facit iniuriam; sed gravius pensatur peccatum secundum maiorem appetitum et intensionem libidinis et secundum gradum[^11] personae vendentis et ementis. Gravius enim peccat episcopus vendens quam subditus emens; quia tamen genus peccati est idem, utrique eadem sententia percelluntur.

### Dub. IV.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Qui vero ordinantur a simoniacis, quos, cum ordinantur, nesciunt* etc. Videtur enim male dicere, quia tales <!-- page 658 -->simoniaci ordinantes sunt irregulares et non habent exsecutionem; et «*nullus dat quod non habet*[^12]»: ergo non dant exsecutionem. — Item, ponatur, quod aliquis simoniacus, qui est mihi notus, sustineatur ab Ecclesia, et ipse est episcopus meus, et ego compellor ad sacros ordines: quaeritur, quid tunc facere debeam. Si enim scienter ordinor a simoniaco; sum irregularis et punior tanquam simoniacus. Si non obedio; pecco mortaliter et scandalizo: ergo videtur, quod in tali casu homo sit *perplexus.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod in *primo* casu exsecutionem habet a iure, quod propter ignorantiam talem reputat excusatum, ordinem vero habet a simoniaco[^13]. In *secundo* debet, quantum potest, subterfugere; quod si non potest, magis patitur, quam agat, et habetur tunc pro excusato. Unde vel debet ordines differre, vel ad alium episcopum ire; sed si non potest aliud, debet sustinere, et cum Ecclesia sustineat, debet et ipse sustinere et excusatur, sicut ille qui ignorat[^14].

### Dub. V.

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Quisquis horum alterum vendit, sine quo alterum non habetur.* Videtur enim male dicere, quia ius patronatus annexum est fundo inseparabiliter: ergo cum ius patronatus sit spirituale et non possit vendi sine simonia[^15]; impossibile est, quod talis fundus sine simonia vendatur.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Magister non loquitur de *temporalibus,* quibus annexa sunt spiritualia; sed contra[^16] de *spiritualibus,* quibus annexa sunt temporalia, sicut est praebenda; et in talibus habet locum verbum Magistri, non in primis. Fundus enim non est annexus iuri patronatus, sed magis e converso. — Vel dicendum, quod intelligit de eo quod ita iunctum est spirituali, quod tenet locum spiritualis. Sed fundus sive villa, cui ius patronatus annexum est, tenet vere rationem rei temporalis[^17].

### Dub. VI.

Item super hoc quod dicit: *Simoniaca haeresis tripartita est,* quaeritur: quibus modis contrahitur simonia? Et consuevit dici, quod quinque modis, qui his versibus continentur:

> *Nummus*[^18], *lingua, timor, caro cum fama populari*
> *Non faciunt gratis spirituale dari.*

Ergo secundum hoc videtur, quod Magister insufficienter tres modos assignet.

Iuxta hoc quaeritur de sufficientia horum modorum. Cum enim fama sit *bonus odor*[^19], sicut bonum exemplum, non videtur, quod sit quid temporale. — Item, in veteri lege habebatur sacerdotium iure propinquitatis[^20]: ergo cum illud non esset peccatum, non videtur etc.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod proprie loquendo, simonia est in *pretio,* quod quidem intendit avaritia[^21], maxime cum intervenit *pactio,* et ista simonia determinatur hic a Magistro. Et potest fieri tripliciter: quia pretium potest dari ita, quod simoniacus non recipit, utpote quia datur collateralibus; vel potest dari a collateralibus ordinati; vel potest ipse ordinatus dare ordinatori, et tunc est simonia in ordinis susceptione, et proprie loquendo, in his tribus modis. — Vel aliter, quia aut *ordinatus dat,* et *ille recipit,* et tunc simoniace ordinatur a simoniaco; aut *iste dat,* sed ille *non recipit,* et tunc simoniace a non simoniaco[^22]; aut quia *nec iste dat, nec ille recipit,* sed ille alias fuit simoniacus, et tunc ordinatur non simoniace a simoniaco. — Penes hoc sumitur distinctio *Magistri* satis sufficiens, sed in *communi* distinctione modorum accipitur *simonia* generalius, a qua pauci sunt hodie immunes in clero respectu multitudinis, utpote quando ille qui ordines tribuit[^23], ex causa indirecta tribuit, vel alius ex causa inordinata acquirit sive ordinem, sive beneficium; et isti sunt *quinque modi,* quorum sufficientia patet sic. Si aliquis inordinate movetur, aut ex *timore male humiliante,* et sic est unus modus; aut ex *amore male inflammante,* et tunc — cum triplex sit amor, scilicet *superbia vitae,* quae amat honores, et *concupiscentia carnis,* et *concupiscentia oculorum*[^24] — penes *concupiscentiam oculorum* accipitur illa, quae est in *nummo,* quae comprehendit omne terrenum et obsequium vile. Penes *amorem carnalem* accipitur aliud membrum, scilicet *caro,* quod comprehendit inordinatum amorem sive respectu propinquorum, sive respectu aliorum, ut si quis amore fornicariae promoveret filium fornicariae. Si autem quantum ad *superbiam,* dupliciter: aut prout appetit *favorem,* ut hypocrita, et sic est *favor* sive fama popularis; aut prout appetit *laudari,* et sic *lingua.* Et sic patet sufficientia. — Vel ut distinguatur *munus* a *manu,* a *lingua* et a *corde*[^25], et duplex motivum timor et amor, et sic erunt quinque modi. Et per hoc patet numerus et sufficientia.

Ad illud quod obiicitur de fama, dicendum, quod fama magnum bonum est *non appetenti,* sicut viro iusto, sed appetenti et laboranti ad hoc non est magnum bonum, sicut hypocritae; unde Proverbiorum decimo[^26]: *Memoria iusti cum laudibus, et nomen impiorum putrescet.*

Quod obiicitur de consanguinitate, non est simile: quia sacerdotium veteris legis erat carnale, sed illud novae est spirituale[^27]. — Et sic patent ea quae sunt circa istam partem.

## English

<!-- page 656 -->

### Doubt I.

In this part there are doubts concerning the text, and first the question is asked about what he says: *Nor from these to substitute anyone in a clerical honor, even a slight one.* For it seems to be badly said, because either this man has *an order,* or he has *nothing.* If he has *nothing:* then he will be able to be promoted by a catholic bishop with a dispensation, as is clear in the case of heretics. If he has *orders:* then he is already promoted.

**I respond** that he does not say this because a man is not *ordained,* but because he is *badly ordained.* Now one is badly ordained in two ways: either because he is ordained *by one from whom* he ought not, or because he is ordained *in a way in which* he ought not. In the first way, in three ways: either because he is ordained by a *heretic,* or by a *simoniac,* as here in the case proposed; for then, if he does so knowingly and shares in heresy or in simony, he cannot exercise the office nor is he easily dispensed, and this is what he means to say in the text[^1]. — Or he is ordained by a *catholic not his own* without the license of his proper bishop and without authority; such a one must be deposed, Cause 7, question 1[^2], yet he can be dispensed, if such an ordainer places some hope in the ratification of the proper bishop. — Or he is ordained by a bishop who *has renounced his place and of*<!-- page 657 -->*fice,* because such a one ought not to confer orders; but if he renounced his *place only,* he can give the minor orders and, by the bishop's license, can give all of them; and one who receives orders without the license of his proper bishop could be deposed by the rigor of law, yet he can be dispensed[^3].

In the second way one is said to be *badly ordained* because he does not receive *in that manner* in which he ought; and then either *by theft,* or *by a leap.* If *by theft,* this is in two ways: either because he receives *two* orders and on the same day, so that he steals one of them, speaking of *sacred* orders; and the punishment for all such is deposition, as is said in distinction 77[^4], *Subdiaconus,* Extra, *de Eo qui furtive, Innotuit* etc. But if he receives by theft *one only;* then either it *was forbidden* under the threat of anathema, and then the Pope alone dispenses, Extra, *de Eo qui furtive.* But if it *was not forbidden;* then he can be allowed to remain in the minor orders, and if he enters the cloister, the prelate can dispense with him. — But if he is ordained *by a leap,* this is in two ways: either from *malice,* or from *negligence.* If *from malice;* although he receives the character, nevertheless he must be deposed. If *in ignorance;* then, with a punishment imposed on him by the bishop, he will be able to be promoted to a lower order, while he is meanwhile suspended from the exercise of the higher, distinction 52[^5], Extra, *de Clerico ordinato per saltum.*

Note also here that, although one may leap over one order, omitting another, nevertheless when the *priesthood* is passed over he cannot be a bishop, Extra, *de Excessibus praelatorum, Ex litteris,* at the end[^6]. But if he omitted the *diaconate,* he is a bishop, but he cannot give that order which he omitted, Cause 1, question 1[^7]. It is not so, however, in baptism, because it is the first Sacrament.

### Doubt II.

Likewise the question is asked about what he says, that *all are called simoniacs, both those who give and those who receive.* For it seems to be badly said, because Simon wished only to buy: therefore the buyers alone ought to be called *simoniacs,* and the sellers *Gehazites*[^8]. — Likewise, Gehazi did more than Simon, because Simon only wished to buy, but Gehazi sold: therefore they ought rather to be named from him.

**I respond:** It must be said that Simon wished to buy for this reason, that he might afterward sell; therefore both evil and sin are imputed to him, and therefore both are named from him.

To the objection concerning the will, it must be said that because this man, already in the time of revealed grace, wished to buy a greater and more spiritual power, and moreover wished to make a bargain: therefore he sinned rather gravely enough and more enormously than the one who sought out of a certain liberality, and who believed that it was a small thing; but the punishment of everlasting leprosy[^9] showed how great that crime was.

### Doubt III.

Likewise the question is asked about what he says, that *both are struck by the same sentence.* For it seems to be badly said, because the sellers sin more gravely, as it appears, because they value [the thing] less than the buyers do: therefore they ought to be punished more gravely. — The *contrary* seems true, because the buyer furnishes him the occasion and receives[^10] beyond the principal: therefore if the one who receives usury sins more gravely than the one who gives, it is clear, etc.

**I respond:** It must be said that as to the *kind* of sin there is uniformity, because each does injury to the grace of the Holy Spirit; but the sin is weighed as more grave according to the greater appetite and intensity of lust and according to the rank[^11] of the person selling and buying. For a bishop selling sins more gravely than a subject buying; yet because the kind of sin is the same, both are struck by the same sentence.

### Doubt IV.

Likewise the question is asked about what he says: *But those who are ordained by simoniacs, whom, when they are ordained, they do not know,* etc. For it seems to be badly said, because such <!-- page 658 -->simoniac ordainers are irregular and have no power of exercise; and «*no one gives what he does not have*[^12]»: therefore they do not give the power of exercise. — Likewise, suppose that some simoniac, who is known to me, is tolerated by the Church, and he is my bishop, and I am compelled toward sacred orders: it is asked what I ought then to do. For if I am knowingly ordained by a simoniac, I am irregular and am punished as a simoniac. If I do not obey, I sin mortally and give scandal: therefore it seems that in such a case a man is *in a dilemma.*

**I respond:** It must be said that in the *first* case he has the power of exercise from the law, which on account of ignorance reckons such a one excused, but he has the order from a simoniac[^13]. In the *second* he ought, as far as he can, to escape it; but if he cannot, he suffers rather than acts, and is then held as excused. Hence he ought either to defer the orders, or to go to another bishop; but if he can do nothing else, he ought to bear it, and since the Church bears with it, he too ought to bear it and is excused, like one who is ignorant[^14].

### Doubt V.

Likewise the question is asked about what he says: *Whoever sells one of these, without which the other is not had.* For it seems to be badly said, because the right of patronage is annexed to the estate inseparably: therefore since the right of patronage is spiritual and cannot be sold without simony[^15], it is impossible that such an estate be sold without simony.

**I respond:** It must be said that the Master is not speaking of *temporal things,* to which spiritual things are annexed; but on the contrary[^16] of *spiritual things,* to which temporal things are annexed, as is a prebend; and in such cases the word of the Master holds, not in the former. For the estate is not annexed to the right of patronage, but rather the reverse. — Or it must be said that he understands it of that which is so joined to the spiritual that it holds the place of the spiritual. But an estate or a farm, to which the right of patronage is annexed, truly holds the character of a temporal thing[^17].

### Doubt VI.

Likewise upon what he says: *The simoniacal heresy is threefold,* it is asked: in what ways is simony contracted? And it is customarily said, in five ways, which are contained in these verses:

> *Money*[^18], *tongue, fear, flesh together with popular fame*
> *Do not allow a spiritual thing to be given freely.*

Therefore according to this it seems that the Master insufficiently assigns three ways.

In connection with this the question is asked about the sufficiency of these ways. For since fame is a *good odor*[^19], like a good example, it does not seem to be something temporal. — Likewise, in the old law the priesthood was held by right of kinship[^20]: therefore since that was not a sin, it does not seem [simony], etc.

**I respond:** It must be said that, properly speaking, simony is in the *price,* which indeed avarice[^21] intends, especially when a *bargain* intervenes, and this simony is here determined by the Master. And it can occur in three ways: because the price can be given in such a way that the simoniac does not receive it, inasmuch as it is given to those beside him; or it can be given by those beside the ordained man; or the ordained man himself can give to the ordainer, and then there is simony in the receiving of orders, and, properly speaking, in these three ways. — Or otherwise, because either *the ordained gives,* and *the other receives,* and then he is simoniacally ordained by a simoniac; or *this one gives,* but the other *does not receive,* and then simoniacally by a non-simoniac[^22]; or because *neither this one gives, nor the other receives,* but that man was otherwise a simoniac, and then he is ordained not simoniacally by a simoniac. — Within this the distinction of the *Master* is taken as sufficiently adequate, but in the *common* distinction of the ways *simony* is taken more generally, from which few today are immune in the clergy in respect of the multitude, as when the one who bestows orders[^23] bestows them from an indirect cause, or another from a disordered cause acquires either an order or a benefice; and these are the *five ways,* whose sufficiency is shown thus. If someone is disordinately moved, either from *fear wrongly humbling,* and thus there is one mode; or from *love wrongly inflaming,* and then — since love is threefold, namely the *pride of life,* which loves honors, and the *concupiscence of the flesh,* and the *concupiscence of the eyes*[^24] — under the *concupiscence of the eyes* is taken that [mode] which is in *money,* which comprehends everything earthly and base service. Under *carnal love* is taken another member, namely *the flesh,* which comprehends disordered love whether in respect of kinsfolk or in respect of others, as if someone, out of love for a harlot, were to promote the son of a harlot. But if [it be] as regards *pride,* in two ways: either as he seeks *favor,* like a hypocrite, and thus it is *favor* or popular fame; or as he seeks *to be praised,* and thus [it is] the *tongue.* And thus the sufficiency is clear. — Or so that the *gift* be distinguished — by the *hand,* by the *tongue,* and by the *heart*[^25] — and the motive twofold, fear and love, and thus there will be five modes. And through this the number and sufficiency are made clear.

To the objection concerning fame, it must be said that fame is a great good *to one not seeking it,* like a just man, but to one seeking it and laboring for it it is not a great good, like a hypocrite; whence Proverbs 10[^26]: *The memory of the just [is] with praises, and the name of the wicked shall rot.*

The objection concerning consanguinity is not similar: because the priesthood of the old law was carnal, but that of the new is spiritual[^27]. — And thus those things which concern this part are made clear.
