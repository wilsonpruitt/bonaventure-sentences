# IV Sent., d. 11, p. 1, a. 1, q. 1
## *Utrum in Sacramento eucharistiae sit vera conversio panis in corpus Christi*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 241 -->

### Articulus Unicus. *De transsubstantiatione.*
### Quaestio I. *Utrum in Sacramento eucharistiae sit vera conversio panis in corpus Christi.*

Circa primum sic proceditur, et quod sit ibi vera conversio panis in corpus Christi, videtur:

1. *Primo* per ipsam formam: quia, cum dicitur: *Hoc est corpus meum*[^1], aut falsum dicit Veritas, aut panem convertit in corpus; sed non dixit falsum: ergo etc.

2. Item, hoc ipsum probatur auctoritate Doctorum, auctoritate Ambrosii scilicet et Eusebii et Augustini et Damasceni[^2]: panis et vinum supernaturaliter in corpus et sanguinem transit.

3. Item, ratione videtur, quia constat et verum est, quod corpus Christi secundum veritatem est in altari; et prius non fuit: ergo secundum aliquam mutationem sui, vel alterius. Non sui, quia est immutabile. Non alterius nisi panis[^3]: ergo etc. Si dicas, quod corpus Christi est mutabile secundum locum, quia haec est mutatio entis completi, et haec est in corpore Christi; *contra*: omne quod mutatur secundum locum, si accedit ad unum, recedit ab altero; ergo secundum hoc Christus recedit a caelo. Praeterea, nihil quod mutatur secundum locum, simul tendit ad differentias diversas positionum; sed simul consecratur corpus Christi Romae et Parisius: ergo si corpus Christi movetur Romam, non ergo Parisius: ergo etc.

<!-- page 242 -->

**Contra:** 1. Gregorius dicit, et habetur in littera[^4]: «*Quis fidelium habere dubium possit, in hora immolationis caelos aperiri, summa et ima sociari*»: sed haec non fierent, si aliquid in corpus Christi converteretur, immo videtur secundum Gregorium, quod descendat: ergo ibi non est conversio.

2. Item, Damascenus[^1b]: «*Quia consuetudo est hominibus comedere panem et bibere vinum, coniugavit eis Divinitatem et fecit ea Sacramentum corporis et sanguinis sui*». Si coniugavit illis Divinitatem, ergo salvantur in sua natura: non ergo convertuntur in aliud.

3. Item, ratione videtur: quia, si aliquid convertitur in corpus Christi; aut quod *est*, aut quod *non est*[^2b]. Si quod non est, ergo corpus de novo fit: ergo plura corpora simul. Si quod est; sed quandocumque aliquid convertitur in id quod est, augetur: ergo corpus Christi consecratione sive conversione fit maius.

4. Item, nullo alio Sacramento elementum convertitur in id quod significat, sed sufficit illud adesse: ergo videtur, quod similiter debeat esse in hoc Sacramento.

5. Item, miracula non sunt ponenda, ubi aeque bene potest fieri sine miraculo: sed corpus Christi cum sit gloriosum, simul cum pane potest esse, et idem faciet, ac si panis converteretur; quod non potest esse sine miraculo: ergo etc.

6. Item, in nullum corpus convertitur cibus, nisi quod indiget refectione; sed corpus Christi non est tale: ergo etc. Si dicas, quod non absorbet ut terra, sed ut radius aquam, scilicet non indigentia, sed potentia[^3b]; *contra*: radius non convertit aquam in se: ergo nec Christus panem in corpus suum.

> **Conclusio.** In Sacramento eucharistiae est conversio panis in corpus Christi, non in partem, sed in totum.

**Respondeo:** Sicut dicit Magister[^4b], quorundam opinio fuit positio, quod non est ibi aliqua conversio, sed ad invocationem verborum sine conversione fit divina virtute, ut corpus Christi sit ibi. — Sed haec positio contraria est Sanctorum *auctoritati* et *rationi*. Rationi quidem, quia aufert Sacramenti *veritatem*, *congruitatem* et *utilitatem*: veritatem, quia ponit ex hoc falsitatem in forma; ponit etiam mutationem circa Christi substantiam, sicut probatum est in opponendo. *Congruitatem* tollit, quia, cum manet ibi panis, non est ideo ductivum in alterum, sicut cum manent sola accidentia, quae dependent et per se stare non possunt. *Utilitatem* etiam, quia, dum possibilitatem et rationem quaerit, minuit meritum fidei.

Propter hoc igitur haec positio est reprobanda et neminem habet ex doctoribus defensorem; immo communiter tenet Ecclesia, quod est ibi conversio panis in corpus Christi, non, inquam, in partem corporis, sed in totum.

1. 2. Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur primo de verbo Gregorii, dicendum, quod sermo ille est tropicus, et dicuntur caeli aperiri, quia divina bonitas large nobis tunc tribuit panem caelestem. Summa etiam et ima dicit sociari propter corporis Christi praesentiam. Et rursus, per manus Angelorum dicitur deferri quantum ad devotionem offerentis, qui mittit sacrificium et orationem in caelum, quam ad Angelos spectat Domino praesentare; sed hoc in litteralibus patebit[^5b]. — Quod autem dicit Damascenus intelligendum est quantum ad continentiam specierum visibilium, vel in ratione conversionis substantiae panis: quia panis convertitur in corpus Divinitati unitum, et species post conversionem continent illud, et sic Divinitas utrisque dicitur coniugari.

3. Ad aliud patet responsio: quia totum in totum convertitur, ideo nec fit novum nec fit maius; illud autem intelligitur, si in parte converteretur[^6b].

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur de aliis Sacramentis, patet similiter responsio, quod non est simile: quia

<!-- page 243 -->

hoc est maius quantum ad rem contentam[^1c]. Praeterea, in aliis res est gratia, quae creatur; sed corpus Christi non creatur tunc in Sacramento.

5. Ad illud quod obiicitur de miraculo, patet similiter: quia miraculum ponitur hic propter veritatem Sacramenti et congruitatem et etiam utilitatem, sicut visum est.

6. Ad ultimum quod obiicitur de cibo et cibabili, dicendum, quod panis non convertitur in corpus Christi, sicut cibus in cibabile; nec corpus Christi convertitur in se, sed Deus convertit[^2c], ut ubi erat panis, qui est cibus corporalis, sit corpus Christi, non per transmutationem in corpore factam, sed in pane in ipsum prius existens transeunte.

## English

### Article Unique. *On transubstantiation.*
### Question I. *Whether in the sacrament of the Eucharist there is a true conversion of bread into the body of Christ.*

Concerning the first [point], one proceeds thus, and that there is a true conversion of bread into the body of Christ seems to be the case:

1. *First*, by the form itself: because, when it is said: *This is my body*[^1], either the Truth speaks falsely, or he converts the bread into [his] body; but he did not speak falsely: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, this same thing is proved by the authority of the Doctors, namely by the authority of Ambrose and Eusebius and Augustine and Damascene[^2]: the bread and wine pass supernaturally into [his] body and blood.

3. Likewise, it seems so by reason, because it is established and true that the body of Christ is according to truth on the altar; and before it was not: therefore by some change either of itself or of another. Not of itself, since it is immutable. Not of another except the bread[^3]: therefore etc. If you say that the body of Christ is changeable with respect to place, since this is a change of a complete being, and this [change] is in the body of Christ; *on the contrary*: everything that is changed with respect to place, if it approaches one [place], departs from another; therefore accordingly Christ departs from heaven. Moreover, nothing that is changed with respect to place tends at the same time to diverse differences of position; but the body of Christ is consecrated at the same time at Rome and at Paris: therefore if the body of Christ is moved to Rome, then not to Paris: therefore etc.

<!-- page 242 -->

**On the contrary:** 1. Gregory says, and it is found in the text[^4]: «*Who of the faithful could have doubt that, in the hour of the immolation, the heavens are opened, and the highest things are joined with the lowest?*»: but these things would not happen if anything were converted into the body of Christ, nay rather it seems, according to Gregory, that [the body] descends: therefore there is no conversion there.

2. Likewise, Damascene[^1b]: «*Because it is the custom for men to eat bread and drink wine, he joined the Divinity to them and made them the Sacrament of his body and blood*». If he joined the Divinity to them, then they are preserved in their own nature: therefore they are not converted into another thing.

3. Likewise, it seems so by reason: because, if anything is converted into the body of Christ, it is either what *is*, or what *is not*[^2b]. If [it is converted] from what is not, then the body comes to be anew: therefore several bodies at the same time. If from what is; but whenever something is converted into what is, it is increased: therefore the body of Christ by consecration or conversion becomes greater.

4. Likewise, in no other Sacrament is the element converted into that which it signifies, but it suffices for it to be present: therefore it seems that it ought to be similarly in this Sacrament.

5. Likewise, miracles are not to be posited where it can equally well be done without a miracle: but the body of Christ, since it is glorious, can be together with the bread, and it will do the same as if the bread were converted; which cannot be without a miracle: therefore etc.

6. Likewise, food is converted into no body except one that needs refreshment; but the body of Christ is not such: therefore etc. If you say that it does not absorb [the bread] as earth [absorbs water], but as a ray [absorbs] water, namely not by need, but by power[^3b]; *on the contrary*: a ray does not convert water into itself: therefore neither [does] Christ [convert] bread into his body.

> **Conclusion.** In the Sacrament of the Eucharist there is a conversion of bread into the body of Christ, not into a part, but into the whole.

**I respond:** As the Master says[^4b], the position of certain men was the opinion that there is no conversion there, but that at the invocation of the words, without conversion, it comes about by divine power that the body of Christ is there. — But this position is contrary to the *authority* of the Saints and to *reason*. To reason indeed, because it takes away the *truth*, the *congruity*, and the *usefulness* of the Sacrament: the truth, because it thereby posits a falsehood in the form; it also posits a change with respect to the substance of Christ, as was proved in the objection. It takes away *congruity*, because, when the bread remains there, it is not on that account leading into another thing, as [it is] when the accidents alone remain, which depend [on something] and cannot stand by themselves. [It takes away] the *usefulness* also, because, while it seeks possibility and reason, it diminishes the merit of faith.

On account of this, therefore, this position is to be rejected and has no defender among the doctors; nay, the Church commonly holds that there is there a conversion of bread into the body of Christ, not, I say, into a part of the body, but into the whole.

1. 2. To that, therefore, which is objected first concerning the word of Gregory, it must be said that that saying is figurative, and the heavens are said to be opened because the divine goodness then bestows on us abundantly the heavenly bread. And the highest and lowest things are said to be joined on account of the presence of the body of Christ. And again, it is said to be carried down by the hands of the Angels with respect to the devotion of the one offering, who sends his sacrifice and prayer into heaven, which it pertains to the Angels to present to the Lord; but this will be made clear in the literal [comments][^5b]. — But what Damascene says must be understood with respect to the containing of the visible species, or in the account of the conversion of the substance of the bread: because the bread is converted into the body united to the Divinity, and the species after the conversion contain it, and so the Divinity is said to be joined to both.

3. To the other [objection] the response is clear: because the whole is converted into the whole, therefore it neither becomes new nor becomes greater; but that [objection] would hold if it were converted in part[^6b].

4. To that which is objected concerning the other Sacraments, the response is likewise clear, that it is not similar: because

<!-- page 243 -->

this is greater with respect to the thing contained[^1c]. Moreover, in the others the thing [signified] is grace, which is created; but the body of Christ is not created then in the Sacrament.

5. To that which is objected concerning a miracle, [the response] is likewise clear: because the miracle is posited here on account of the truth of the Sacrament and its congruity and also its usefulness, as has been seen.

6. To the last [objection] which is raised concerning food and the thing fed, it must be said that the bread is not converted into the body of Christ as food into the thing fed; nor is the body of Christ converted into itself, but God converts[^2c] [it], so that where there was bread, which is bodily food, there is the body of Christ, not by a transmutation made in the body, but by [the bread], previously existing, passing into it in the bread.
