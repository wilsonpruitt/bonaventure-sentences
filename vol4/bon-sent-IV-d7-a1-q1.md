## Latin

### Articulus I. *De confirmatione quoad integritatem.*
### Quaestio I. *Utrum in confirmatione sit necessaria forma verborum.*

> The numbered footnotes below correspond to markers in both the Latin body and the English translation.

Et quod necessarium sit in hoc Sacramento habere formam verborum, ostenditur:

1. Per *simile* in aliis Sacramentis: quia, si in aliis forma verborum est necessaria[^1], videtur, quod et in isto.

2. Item, hoc ipsum ostenditur per aliam *rationem*: quia forma verborum est sanctificans elementum; sed « Sacramentum est ex sanctificatione sanctificans », secundum quod dicit Hugo[^2]: ergo necesse est, quod formam verborum habeat, cum per ipsam sit sanctificans elementum.

**Sed contra:** 1. Christus fuit institutor formae Sacramentorum; sed formam confirmationis non legitur instituisse, immo pueris tantum manus imposuisse, Matthaei decimo nono[^3]: ergo non est necessaria, cum Christus instituerit, quod pertinet ad ne-

<!-- page 164 -->

cessitatem et formam aliorum Sacramentorum, et non huius.

2. *Si tu dicas*, quod reliquit Apostolis formam; *contra*: Actuum octavo[^4] legitur, quod impositione manuum dabant Spiritum sanctum; sed quod est de necessitate Sacramenti est invariabile ut Sacramentum: ergo etc.

> *Quaestio incidens 1.* — Item, de forma quaeritur, quae est haec[^5]: « Signo te signo crucis, confirmo te chrismate salutis, in nomine Patris et Filii et Spiritus sancti, Amen ». Primo videtur, quod superfluat hoc verbum *signo*, quia sufficit actu signare[^6]. *Si tu dicas*, quod hoc est propter intentionem; tunc ergo, sicut in baptismo dicitur *baptizo*, et non dicitur alius actus; ita in confirmatione sufficit dicere *confirmo*.
>
> *Quaestio incidens 2.* — Item, quaeritur: quare potius fit mentio in forma de *cruce* quam de incarnatione, vel resurrectione?
>
> *Quaestio incidens 3.* — Item, quaeritur: quare potius in hoc Sacramento *materia* nominatur, cum dicitur: « Confirmo te chrismate », quam in baptismo, ut dicatur: « Baptizo te in aqua »?

### Conclusio.

> *Forma confirmationis est de necessitate Sacramenti.*

**Respondeo:** Ad hoc dicendum, quod forma praedicta est de necessitate huius Sacramenti, sicut probant rationes ad hoc inductae.

1. Ad illud autem quod obiicitur in contrarium de eius institutione[^7], diversificantur doctores. Quidam enim dicunt, quod formam illam Christus instituit et Apostolis tradidit, et Apostoli postmodum aliis reliquerunt, licet hoc non sit scriptum in canone Scripturae. Ipse tamen forma illa non fuit usus imponendo manus pueris, quia hoc faciebat ut Dominus, non ut minister. — Aliter potest dici et melius, credo, quod illud Sacramentum Christus nec dispensavit nec instituit, quia post ascensionem suam decebat Apostolos confirmari, et Spiritum ad robur dari. Unde Ioannis septimo[^8]: *Spiritus sanctus nondum erat datus, quia Iesus nondum erat glorificatus*; non, inquam, datus in plenitudine, vel ad robur; sed hoc quidem fecit die Pentecostes. Unde confirmati sunt a Spiritu sancto *immediate* sine ministerio et Sacramento; et ideo ipsi etiam alios confirmabant etiam sine verbo. Sed postquam bases Ecclesiae, Apostoli scilicet, qui a Deo, non per homines, erant ordinati praelati et confirmati, defecerunt; instituit Spiritus sanctus huius Sacramenti formam, cui etiam virtutem sanctificandi dedit. Et per hoc patet responsio ad primum.

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur de forma: quare scilicet Christus non instituit, et quare Apostoli non[^9] instituerunt et posteris tradiderunt? ratio praedicta est. — Possunt tamen et aliae rationes congruentiae assignari: quia enim minuebatur in dispensantibus sanctitas, in suscipientibus idoneitas; ideo opportunum fuit instituere verbum sanctificans. Alia est etiam ratio, sed haec patebit infra[^10].

*Ad 1. quaest. incid.* — Ad illud quod ultra quaeritur de forma, dicendum, quod in hoc Sacramento datur gratia ad robur fidei quantum ad *confirmationem* in corde et quantum ad *confessionem* liberam in ore: et quia difficillimum et arduissimum erat Christum crucifixum confiteri, tum propter *opprobrium*, secundum quod dicitur primae ad Corinthios primo[^11]: *Praedicamus Christum crucifixum, Iudaeis quidem scandalum, gentibus autem stultitiam*; tum propter *periculum*, quia adversarii tales nitebantur occidere, Ioannis decimo quinto: *Haec facient vobis propter nomen meum*: hinc est, quod decuit, duplicem actum in forma poni, unum: qui respicit liberam Crucifixi *confessionem*, quasi eam semper ferret in fronte, alterum, *cordis stabilitionem*.

*Ad 2. quaest. incid.* — Ex hoc patet, quare magis fit mentio de *cruce*, quia illud est arduissimum et ignominiosum confiteri.

*Ad 3. quaest. incid.* — De *materia* vero, quare hoc fit, iam videtur[^12]; et ratio est, quia consecrata est, non sic in Sacramentis aliis. — *Aliter*: Aliqui tamen dicunt, quod hoc est propter praecipuam efficaciam huius Sacramenti et gratiam specialem et singularem, quae datur in hoc Sacramento ad pugnam[^13].

### Scholion

**I.** In solvenda ipsa principali quaestione non est difficultas; sed ea quae dicta sunt in solut. ad 1. de *determinatione formae*, sive, ut ait, de *institutione* huius Sacramenti, et in seq. quaestione de *determinatione materiae*, continent obsoletam sententiam Hugonis, Magistri (infra d. 23. c. 3.) et Alex. Halensis, eaeque non tantum explicatione, sed etiam correctione egent, ut iam diximus tom. I. Prolegom. LXIII. § 3. Profecto non amplius sustinendus est modus loquendi, quod duo Sacramenta confirmationis et extremae unctionis non instituta sint ab ipso Christo, quamvis satis appareat, illos hoc intellexisse tantum quoad institutionem *immediatam*. Nam Alex. Hal. (S. p. IV. q. 9. m. 2. a. 2. § 1. ad 1.) inter alia docet, illud Sacramentum esse institutum « non immediate a legislatore, sed mediante Spiritu S. cooperante ». Nec hanc opinionem idem profert nisi per modum *probabilitatis*; insuper ipsa quaestio, si *sensum* attendamus, proprie tantum respicit *distinctam determinationem* materiae et formae huius Sacramenti, prout in Ecclesia Latina adhibetur. De hac igitur re dicit (loc. cit. m. 1.): « Sine praeiudicio dicendum » quoad formam verborum et materiam elementarem, cui etiam Spiritus S. contulit virtutem sanctificandi, hoc Sacramentum institutum fuisse « Spiritus S. instinctu in Concilio Meldensi » (!); Apostolos autem, sicut « confirmati sunt a Spiritu S. immediate, sine ministerio et Sacramento », sic etiam alios confirmasse tantum « manuum impositione, non ministrando Sacramentum confirmationis » (in corp. et ad 3.). — Quoad hanc rem S. Thom. (hic q. 1. a. 1. quaestiunc. 1. ad 1.) refert, circa *institutionem* confirmationis tres esse opiniones, quarum prima ponat, hoc Sacramentum nec a Christo nec ab Apostolis, sed processu temporis in quodam Concilio institutum esse; quam sententiam reprobat tanquam absurdam; secundam, ait, ponere, illud fuisse institutum non quidem a Christo, sed ab Apostolis; quam iterum non approbat, et prosequitur: « Et ideo probabilior videtur aliorum opinio » etc., scilicet opinio nunc communis.

**II.** Ut vera a falsis et certa ab incertis secernantur, haec notamus.

1. Fide constat ex Concilio Trident. (Sess. VII. de Sacram. in genere, can. 1.), *omnia* Sacramenta a Iesu Christo instituta fuisse; nec sine temeritatis nota negari posset, intelligendum esse hoc de institutione *immediata*. Cum autem ipsum Concilium a vocabulo *immediata* consulto abstinuerit, sententia aliquorum theologorum, quod *heretica* sit opinio illa antiquorum Scholasticorum, communius non probatur. — Idem Concil. Trident. (Sess. XIV. c. 1. et cfr. can. 1.) de extrema unctione dicit, quod « instituta est tanquam vere et proprie Sacramentum novi testamenti a Christo Domino nostro, apud Marcum quidem insinuatum, per Iacobum autem Apostolum ac Domini fratrem fidelibus commendatum ac promulgatum ». — Apta haec distinctio inter *institutionem*, *insinuationem* et *promulgationem* a pluribus antiquis neglecta esse videtur.

2. Licet post Concil. Trident. theologi concorditer doceant, Christum *immediate* septem Sacramenta instituisse, remanet tamen dissensus opinionum circa quasdam annexas quaestiones, e. g. quid proprie requiratur ad institutionem alicuius Sacramenti; item, utrum Christus *omnium* Sacramentorum materiam et formam determinate in sua specie, an aliquorum (ut confirmationis et ordinis) tantum in genere constituerit, ita ut Ecclesiae specialiora reliquerit determinanda. Primae opinioni cum Ss. Thoma et Alphonso multi adhaerent; sed etiam secunda ut probabilis sustinetur a magnis theologis recentioribus (cfr. Natalis Alexandri Theolog. dogmat. tom. I. libr. II. De Sacram. in genere, c. 5. prop. 2.); pro qua sententia praeter Hugonem, Magistrum, Alexandrum Hal., Bonaventuram allegant etiam Innocentium IV. (in C. Presbyter (1.) X. de tit. 16. Sacram. non iterandis); et praecipue urgent hanc rationem, quia alias vix possibile esset differentiam explicare, quae inter Latinam et Orientalem Ecclesiam est in administrandis ordinibus nonnullis et confirmatione. Hi igitur theologi distinguunt institutionem *immediatam*, Christo tribuendam, a determinatione *specifica* materiae et formae, quae Ecclesiae deputata fuerit. — Item, de tempore et de modo, quo Christus confirmationem et extremam unctionem instituit, disputant theologi, utrum scilicet benedictio parvulorum cum impositione manuum (Marc. 10, 16.) et unctio infirmorum (Marc. 6, 13.) fuerint proprie Sacramenta (quod non tantum S. Bonaventura cum praedictis antiquis, sed etiam multi recentiores negant « non sine gravi fundamento », ut dicit Benedictus XIV. (VIII. de Synodo dioecesan. c. 1.). — Quoad haec aliasque de materia et forma huius Sacramenti opiniones et controversias consulantur auctores recentiores.

**III.** His praesuppositis, quoad S. Bonaventuram observandum est, eum in Breviloquio (p. VI. c. 4.) disertis verbis docere, Christum *septem* Sacramenta legis gratiae instituisse *in verbis et elementis*... tamen « diversimode », ut ibi diffusius explicatur. De duobus autem Sacramentis, scilicet confirmatione et extrema unctione, ait: « Christus solum initiavit et insinuavit *confirmationem*, manum parvulis imponendo et praedicendo, discipulos baptizandos Spiritu S. »; *unctionem* vero *extremam*, « discipulos ad curandum mittendo, qui infirmos ungebant oleo, sicut dicitur in Marco (6, 13.). Haec bene conveniunt cum doctrina Concilii Tridentini ». Quando S. Bonav. distinguit inter modum instituendi « complete et clare » sive « distincte et integre », qui competit baptismo, eucharistiae et ordini, et modum per *insinuationem* et *initiationem*, hoc intelligendum est, ut explicat illa secunda opinio. — Similia docet Petr. a Tar. (hic q. 1. a. 1.): « Christus instituit confirmationem praefiguratione, inspiratione et auctoritate; Apostoli traditione, et Ecclesia determinatione et approbatione. Est enim institutio duplex: tacita et expressa, et haec duplex: expressione facti, vel praecepti. Christus ergo instituit innuendo, imprimendo et praefigurando; Apostoli faciendo; Ecclesia praecipiendo ». — Non defuerunt qui cum Barth. de Barberiis (Cursus theolog. p. IV. de Sacram. disp. 1. q. 5. n. 23.) etiam verba in hac et seq. quaestione a S. Bonav. prolata ita interpretati sint, ut, ab aliis male intellecta, reapse eundem sensum habeant, qui in Breviloquio manifestatur. Praedictus auctor enim putat, institutionem Sacramentorum S. Bonaventurae idem esse ac eorundem *promulgationem*, sicut leges dicuntur institui, quando promulgantur (cfr. S. Thom., S. I. II. q. 90. a. 4.). Sed huic interpretationi praeter alia obstat, tum quod, hoc sensu supposito, omnia Sacramenta dici possint post missionem Spiritus S. esse instituta, unde inanis esset distinctio eorum quoad institutionis tempus; tum quod S. Bonav. (infra d. 23, a. 1. q. 2.) eandem praeciso aliorum interpretationem refert eamque ut minus probabilem non acceptat. — Concedendum tamen est, citato loco auctorem nostrum mitius loqui magisque accedere ad sententiam in Breviloquio expositam. Dicit enim: « Haec duo Sacramenta esse a Christo insinuata, sed post a Spiritu S. instituta... Nec sunt duo legislatores Christus et Spiritus S... Unde Sacramentorum institutionem non attribuimus nisi Deo vel per Filium, vel per Spiritum S... Sic institutio Sacramentorum respicit Deum ut auctorem, et Apostolos respicit ut ministros in promulgando ». — Praecipue tamen observandum esse nobis videtur, quod in his duabus de confirmatione quaestionibus proprie sermo est de *determinatione specifica* formae et materiae, quae, licet ab auctore nostro et ab Alexandro confundatur cum institutione, tamen, salva fide, ab eadem cum non paucis theologis distingui et separari potest. Reiecto igitur nomine institutionis, verbisque ad praedictam determinationem restrictis, illa antiqua assertio vix differret a supra relata sententia recentiorum auctorum, quod scilicet Christus materiam et formam aliquorum Sacramentorum tantum in genere determinaverit. — Nihilominus opinamur, S. Bonav. ea quae hic in iuventute, auctoritate fretus magni sui magistri aliorumque, minus correcte scripserit, in Breviloquio suo correxisse et aliquatenus retractasse, sicut etiam S. Augustinus in multis assertionibus et S. Thomas in nonnullis fecerunt. — Quoad seq. quaestionem notandum, in ed. Vat. textum in corp. aliquatenus mutatum esse. Lectio codicum a nobis recepta eundem praebet sensum, quem expressit Alex. Hal., loc. cit. m. 2. a. 1. § 1. ad 1.

**IV.** De hac et seq. (2.) quaestione: Alex. Hal., loc. cit. m. 1. et 2. a. 1. 2. — Scot., in utroque Scripto hic q. 1. — S. Thom., hic q. 1. a. 1. 2. 3; S. III. q. 72. a. 1-4. — B. Albert., hic a. 1-3. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 1. a. 1-3. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 2. q. 1. 2. — Durand., hic q. 1. 2. — Dionys. Carth., hic q. 1-3.

## English

### Article I. *On confirmation as to its integrity.*
### Question I. *Whether a form of words is necessary in confirmation.*

And that it is necessary in this Sacrament to have a form of words is shown:

1. By the *likeness* in the other Sacraments: for if in the others a form of words is necessary[^1], it seems that it is so in this one too.

2. Likewise, this same thing is shown by another *reason*: for the form of words is what sanctifies the element; but « a Sacrament is sanctifying by sanctification », according to what Hugh says[^2]: therefore it is necessary that it have a form of words, since by it the element is sanctified.

**On the contrary:** 1. Christ was the institutor of the form of the Sacraments; but he is not read to have instituted the form of confirmation, but rather only to have imposed hands on children, in Matthew nineteen[^3]: therefore it is not necessary, since Christ instituted what pertains to the ne-

<!-- page 164 -->

cessity and form of the other Sacraments, and not of this one.

2. *If you say* that he left the form to the Apostles; *on the contrary*: in Acts eight[^4] it is read that by the imposition of hands they gave the Holy Spirit; but what is of the necessity of a Sacrament is invariable as the Sacrament: therefore etc.

> *Incidental question 1.* — Likewise, concerning the form it is asked, which is this[^5]: « I sign you with the sign of the cross, I confirm you with the chrism of salvation, in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, Amen ». First it seems that this word *sign* is superfluous, since it suffices to sign by the act itself[^6]. *If you say* that this is on account of the intention; then therefore, just as in baptism one says *I baptize*, and no other act is named; so in confirmation it suffices to say *I confirm*.
>
> *Incidental question 2.* — Likewise, it is asked: why is mention made in the form rather of the *cross* than of the incarnation, or the resurrection?
>
> *Incidental question 3.* — Likewise, it is asked: why in this Sacrament is the *matter* named, when it is said: « I confirm you with chrism », rather than in baptism, so that it be said: « I baptize you in water »?

### Conclusion.

> *The form of confirmation is of the necessity of the Sacrament.*

**I respond:** It must be said to this that the aforesaid form is of the necessity of this Sacrament, as the reasons adduced for it prove.

1. As to that which is objected on the contrary concerning its institution[^7], the doctors differ. For some say that Christ instituted that form and handed it on to the Apostles, and the Apostles afterward left it to others, although this is not written in the canon of Scripture. He himself, however, did not use that form in imposing hands on children, because he was doing this as the Lord, not as a minister. — It can be said otherwise and better, I believe, that Christ neither dispensed nor instituted that Sacrament, because after his ascension it was fitting that the Apostles be confirmed, and the Spirit be given for strength. Hence in John seven[^8]: *The Holy Spirit was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified*; not given, I say, in fullness, or for strength; but this indeed he did on the day of Pentecost. Hence they were confirmed by the Holy Spirit *immediately*, without ministry and Sacrament; and therefore they too confirmed others even without a word. But after the foundations of the Church, namely the Apostles, who were ordained prelates and confirmed by God, not through men, had passed away, the Holy Spirit instituted the form of this Sacrament, to which he also gave the power of sanctifying. And by this the reply to the first is clear.

2. As to that which is objected concerning the form: namely, why Christ did not institute it, and why the Apostles did not[^9] institute it and hand it on to posterity? the aforesaid reason holds. — Yet other reasons of fittingness can also be assigned: for since holiness was diminished in those dispensing, and fitness in those receiving; therefore it was opportune to institute a sanctifying word. There is also another reason, but this will appear below[^10].

*To the first incidental question.* — As to that which is further asked concerning the form, it must be said that in this Sacrament grace is given for the strength of faith, both as to *confirmation* in the heart and as to free *confession* in the mouth: and because it was most difficult and most arduous to confess the crucified Christ, both on account of *reproach*, according to what is said in First Corinthians one[^11]: *We preach Christ crucified, to the Jews indeed a scandal, but to the Gentiles foolishness*; and on account of *peril*, since such adversaries strove to kill, in John fifteen: *These things they will do to you for my name's sake*: hence it is that it was fitting that a twofold act be set in the form, one which regards the free *confession* of the Crucified, as though one were to bear it always on his brow, the other, the *stabilizing of the heart*.

*To the second incidental question.* — From this it is clear why mention is made rather of the *cross*, because it is the most arduous and most ignominious to confess.

*To the third incidental question.* — Concerning the *matter*, however, why this is done, it has now been seen[^12]; and the reason is that it is consecrated, which is not so in the other Sacraments. — *Otherwise*: yet some say that this is on account of the chief efficacy of this Sacrament and the special and singular grace which is given in this Sacrament for the battle[^13].

### Scholion

**I.** In solving the principal question itself there is no difficulty; but the things said in the solution to the first concerning the *determination of the form*, or, as he says, concerning the *institution* of this Sacrament, and in the following question concerning the *determination of the matter*, contain the obsolete view of Hugh, of the Master (below, d. 23, c. 3.) and of Alexander of Hales, and these need not only explanation but also correction, as we have already said in tom. I, Prolegom. LXIII. § 3. Indeed, the manner of speaking is no longer to be sustained, that the two Sacraments of confirmation and extreme unction were not instituted by Christ himself, although it sufficiently appears that they understood this only as to *immediate* institution. For Alexander of Hales (S. p. IV. q. 9. m. 2. a. 2. § 1. ad 1.) teaches among other things that that Sacrament was instituted « not immediately by the lawgiver, but with the cooperation of the Holy Spirit ». Nor does he himself propose this opinion except by way of *probability*; moreover the question itself, if we attend to the *sense*, properly regards only the *distinct determination* of the matter and form of this Sacrament, as it is used in the Latin Church. Concerning this matter, therefore, he says (loc. cit. m. 1.): « It must be said without prejudice » as to the form of words and the elemental matter, to which the Holy Spirit also conferred the power of sanctifying, that this Sacrament was instituted « by the instinct of the Holy Spirit in the Council of Meaux » (!); but that the Apostles, just as « they were confirmed by the Holy Spirit immediately, without ministry and Sacrament », so also confirmed others only « by the imposition of hands, not by administering the Sacrament of confirmation » (in the body and to the third). — As to this matter, S. Thomas (here q. 1. a. 1. subquestion 1, to the first) reports that, concerning the *institution* of confirmation, there are three opinions, the first of which holds that this Sacrament was instituted neither by Christ nor by the Apostles, but in the course of time in a certain Council; which view he rejects as absurd; the second, he says, holds that it was instituted not indeed by Christ, but by the Apostles; which again he does not approve, and he continues: « And therefore the opinion of the others seems more probable » etc., namely the opinion now common.

**II.** That the true may be discerned from the false and the certain from the uncertain, we note these things.

1. By faith it is established from the Council of Trent (Sess. VII. on the Sacraments in general, can. 1.), that *all* Sacraments were instituted by Jesus Christ; nor could it be denied without a note of rashness that this is to be understood of *immediate* institution. But since the Council itself deliberately abstained from the word *immediate*, the opinion of some theologians, that the view of those ancient Scholastics is *heretical*, is more commonly not approved. — The same Council of Trent (Sess. XIV. c. 1. and cf. can. 1.) says of extreme unction, that « it was instituted as truly and properly a Sacrament of the new testament by Christ our Lord, insinuated indeed in Mark, but commended and promulgated to the faithful through the Apostle James, the brother of the Lord ». — This apt distinction between *institution*, *insinuation*, and *promulgation* seems to have been neglected by many of the ancients.

2. Although after the Council of Trent the theologians concordantly teach that Christ *immediately* instituted the seven Sacraments, there yet remains a dissent of opinions concerning certain annexed questions, e.g. what is properly required for the institution of some Sacrament; likewise, whether Christ determined the matter and form of *all* the Sacraments definitely in their species, or constituted those of some (such as confirmation and order) only in genus, so that he left the more specific things to be determined by the Church. To the first opinion many adhere with Sts. Thomas and Alphonsus; but the second too is sustained as probable by great more recent theologians (cf. Natalis Alexander, Theolog. dogmat. tom. I. libr. II. De Sacram. in general, c. 5. prop. 2.); for which view, besides Hugh, the Master, Alexander of Hales, they cite Bonaventure as well, and also Innocent IV. (in C. Presbyter (1.) X. de tit. 16. Sacram. non iterandis); and they especially press this reason, that otherwise it would scarcely be possible to explain the difference which exists between the Latin and the Eastern Church in administering certain orders and confirmation. These theologians therefore distinguish the *immediate* institution, to be attributed to Christ, from the *specific* determination of the matter and form, which was delegated to the Church. — Likewise, concerning the time and the manner in which Christ instituted confirmation and extreme unction, the theologians dispute, namely whether the blessing of children with the imposition of hands (Mark 10, 16.) and the anointing of the sick (Mark 6, 13.) were properly Sacraments (which not only S. Bonaventure with the aforesaid ancients, but also many more recent writers deny « not without grave foundation », as Benedict XIV. says (VIII. de Synodo dioecesan. c. 1.). — As to these and other opinions and controversies concerning the matter and form of this Sacrament, let the more recent authors be consulted.

**III.** These things presupposed, as to S. Bonaventure it is to be observed that in the Breviloquium (p. VI. c. 4.) he teaches in plain words that Christ instituted the *seven* Sacraments of the law of grace *in words and elements*... yet « in diverse manners », as is there more diffusely explained. But concerning the two Sacraments, namely confirmation and extreme unction, he says: « Christ only initiated and insinuated *confirmation*, by imposing his hand on children and by foretelling that the disciples would be baptized with the Holy Spirit »; but *extreme unction*, « by sending the disciples to heal, who anointed the sick with oil, as is said in Mark (6, 13.). These agree well with the doctrine of the Council of Trent ». When S. Bonav. distinguishes between the manner of instituting « completely and clearly » or « distinctly and integrally », which belongs to baptism, the eucharist, and order, and the manner by *insinuation* and *initiation*, this is to be understood as that second opinion explains. — Peter of Tarentaise teaches similar things (here q. 1. a. 1.): « Christ instituted confirmation by prefiguration, inspiration, and authority; the Apostles by tradition, and the Church by determination and approbation. For institution is twofold: tacit and express, and this twofold: by the expression of a fact, or of a precept. Christ therefore instituted by intimating, impressing, and prefiguring; the Apostles by doing; the Church by commanding ». — Nor were there lacking those who, with Barth. de Barberiis (Cursus theolog. p. IV. de Sacram. disp. 1. q. 5. n. 23.), so interpreted the words uttered by S. Bonav. in this and the following question, that, being misunderstood by others, they really have the same sense which is manifested in the Breviloquium. For the aforesaid author thinks that the institution of the Sacraments according to S. Bonaventure is the same as their *promulgation*, just as laws are said to be instituted when they are promulgated (cf. S. Thom., S. I. II. q. 90. a. 4.). But to this interpretation, besides other things, it is opposed, both that, this sense being supposed, all the Sacraments could be said to have been instituted after the mission of the Spirit, whence the distinction of them as to the time of institution would be empty; and that S. Bonav. (below, d. 23, a. 1. q. 2.) reports the very same interpretation of others and does not accept it as less probable. — It must nevertheless be conceded that in the cited place our author speaks more mildly and accedes more to the view set forth in the Breviloquium. For he says: « These two Sacraments were insinuated by Christ, but afterward instituted by the Holy Spirit... Nor are there two lawgivers, Christ and the Holy Spirit... Hence we attribute the institution of the Sacraments to none but God, either through the Son, or through the Holy Spirit... Thus the institution of the Sacraments regards God as author, and regards the Apostles as ministers in promulgating ». — But it seems to us especially to be observed that in these two questions concerning confirmation the discourse is properly about the *specific determination* of the form and matter, which, although it is confused with institution by our author and by Alexander, can nevertheless, the faith being safe, be distinguished and separated from it together with not a few theologians. Therefore, the name of institution being set aside, and the words being restricted to the aforesaid determination, that ancient assertion would scarcely differ from the above-related view of the more recent authors, namely that Christ determined the matter and form of some Sacraments only in genus. — Nevertheless we are of the opinion that what S. Bonav. here in his youth, relying on the authority of his great master and of others, wrote less correctly, he corrected and to some extent retracted in his Breviloquium, just as also S. Augustine in many assertions and S. Thomas in some did. — As to the following question it is to be noted that in the Vatican edition the text in the body was somewhat altered. The reading of the codices received by us provides the same sense which Alex. Hal. expressed, loc. cit. m. 2. a. 1. § 1. ad 1.

**IV.** On this and the following (2nd) question: Alex. Hal., loc. cit. m. 1. and 2. a. 1. 2. — Scot., in both Writings here q. 1. — S. Thom., here q. 1. a. 1. 2. 3; S. III. q. 72. a. 1-4. — B. Albert., here a. 1-3. — Petr. a Tar., here q. 1. a. 1-3. — Richard. of Mediavilla, here a. 2. q. 1. 2. — Durand., here q. 1. 2. — Dionys. Carth., here q. 1-3.
