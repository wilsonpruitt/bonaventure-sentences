## *Utrum tria sint matrimonii bona*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 719 -->

### Quaestio II. *Utrum tria sint matrimonii bona.*

Secundo quaeritur de numero et sufficientia bonorum matrimonii; et assignantur ab Augustino tria in libro de Bono coniugali[^1], scilicet « fides, proles et Sacramentum ». Primo videtur, quod hic sit *defectus*.

1. Nam praeter haec est in coniugio *mutuum obsequium*; et hoc quidem aliquod bonum est, sicut dicitur Ecclesiastis quarto[^2]: *Melius est, duos esse quam unum*, et non est aliquid istorum: ergo etc.

2. Item, ista tria competunt matrimonio, secundum quod est in *officium*; sed matrimonium non solum est in *officium*, sed etiam in *remedium*[^3]: ergo videntur plura esse.

3. Item, bonum *pacis* est aliquod bonum, immo maximum bonum, ut dicit Augustinus[^4]; sed hoc est bonum, quod frequenter facit coniugium: ergo insufficienter enumerat. *Si dicas*, quod enumerat ea quae sunt bona matrimonii, non ut frequenter, sed ut semper; *obiicitur*: quia nec fides nec proles semper est in matrimonio.

*Sed contra*, videtur, quod superflue:

4. Quia unius rei unicus est finis: ergo si matrimonii Sacramentum est unum, ergo unum debet habere principalem finem: ergo unum est bonum eius, quia *bonum* et *finis* idem[^5].

5. Item, nihil dicitur esse bonum sui ipsius; sed matrimonium est Sacramentum: ergo Sacramentum non est bonum matrimonii.

6. Item, nihil debet connumerari suis proprietatibus; sed fides et proles sunt conditiones et proprietates matrimonii, ergo Sacramenti: ergo non debent distingui neque connumerari cum bono Sacramenti. — *Si dicas*, quod Sacramentum aliter accipitur, scilicet pro *proprietate* matrimonii; *quaeritur*, quae sit illa proprietas, et quare magis sibi appropriavit nomen Sacramenti[^6].

### Conclusio.

> *Sufficientia trium bonorum matrimonii monstratur triplici via.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod numerus et sufficientia bonorum matrimonii potest accipi a parte causae *instituentis* sive *efficientis*, et sic a triplici principio: a dictamine scilicet *naturae*, ut vir cubet cum muliere ad conservationem ipsius naturae, et sic bonum *prolis*; a dictamine *rationis rectae*, ut vir cum sua et non cum alia cubet[^7] muliere, et sic bonum *fidei*; a dictamine *gratiae*, ut significetur unio divinae naturae et humanae, et sic bonum *Sacramenti*.

Potest accipi a parte principii *constituentis*, et hoc tripliciter: quia ad hoc, quod matrimonium habeat esse *completum*, tria sunt necessaria, scilicet *institutio*, *usus* et *fructus*. Ratione *institutionis* est bonum Sacramenti, ratione debiti *usus* est bonum fidei, ratione *fructus* conferentis usum est bonum prolis. Et sic patet, quod est triplex bonum.

Potest etiam accipi a parte *finis moventis* sive *terminantis*, et hoc vel quia *ad bonum*, vel quia *contra malum*. Quia *ad bonum*; cum triplex sit eligendum bonum, scilicet *honestum*, *conferens* et *delectabile*, ratione boni *honesti* est fides, ratione *conferentis* proles, ratione *delectabilis* est Sacramentum, secundum quod superius[^8] est monstratum. — Vel aliter: quia *contra malum* concupiscentiae, quae habet in se triplex malum, scilicet *fluxum*, *inordinationem* et *fastidium*; contra *fluxum* est in matrimonio unio *singularis*, contra *inordinationem* unio *utilis*, contra *fastidium* unio *inseparabilis*. Ratione *singularitatis* in unione est fides, ratione *utilitatis* proles, ratione *inseparabilitatis* Sacramentum[^9]. — Et haec tria significant triplicem proprietatem unionis Christi cum Ecclesia, quia scilicet unitur uni, et quia ex ea *filios generat*, et quia nunquam separatur.

Ex his patent obiecta, quia connumerantur non quaecumque bona, sed quae *communia* sunt statui naturae corruptae et stantis[^10]; ideo nec pax nec obsequium nec bonum, quod accipitur, in quantum est *remedium*, inter haec bona connumerantur, sed haec tria bona solum.

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod *superfluunt*, quia unius unus finis; dicendum, quod unum est bonum *principale* et *inseparabile*, scilicet sacra- <!-- page 720 --> mentale vinculum; et alia sunt huic annexa, et nihil impedit, quod aliquid habeat unum principalem finem et plures utilitates adiunctas.

5. 6. Patet etiam responsio ad illud quod obiicitur de bono Sacramenti: nam ipsa unio dicitur Sacramentum matrimonii, sed inseparabilitas unionis dicitur *bonum* Sacramenti sive Sacramentum, quod est bonum matrimonii.

Ratio autem, quare conditio inseparabilitatis vindicavit[^11] sibi nomen istud, est, quia matrimonio est consubstantialis et consequens ipsum inseparabiliter, non sic aliae conditiones. Et sic patent obiecta[^12].

## English

### Question II. *Whether the goods of matrimony are three.*

Secondly, inquiry is made concerning the number and sufficiency of the goods of matrimony; and three are assigned by Augustine in the book On the Good of Marriage[^1], namely « faith, offspring, and Sacrament ». First it seems that here there is a *defect*.

1. For besides these there is in wedlock *mutual service*; and this indeed is some good, as it is said in the fourth [chapter] of Ecclesiastes[^2]: *It is better that two be than one*, and this is none of those [three]: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, these three belong to matrimony insofar as it is in [the character of] a *duty*; but matrimony is not only in a *duty*, but also in a *remedy*[^3]: therefore there seem to be more.

3. Likewise, the good of *peace* is some good, indeed the greatest good, as Augustine says[^4]; but this is a good which wedlock frequently brings about: therefore it enumerates insufficiently. *If you say* that it enumerates those things which are goods of matrimony, not as [occurring] frequently but as [occurring] always; *it is objected*: because neither faith nor offspring is always in matrimony.

*But on the contrary*, it seems that [it enumerates] superfluously:

4. Because of one thing there is one single end: therefore if the Sacrament of matrimony is one, therefore it ought to have one principal end: therefore its good is one, since *good* and *end* are the same[^5].

5. Likewise, nothing is said to be a good of its very self; but matrimony is a Sacrament: therefore the Sacrament is not a good of matrimony.

6. Likewise, nothing ought to be numbered together with its own properties; but faith and offspring are conditions and properties of matrimony, therefore of the Sacrament: therefore they ought not to be distinguished nor numbered together with the good of the Sacrament. — *If you say* that Sacrament is taken otherwise, namely for a *property* of matrimony; *it is asked* what that property is, and why it has rather appropriated to itself the name of Sacrament[^6].

### Conclusion.

> *The sufficiency of the three goods of matrimony is shown by a threefold way.*

**I respond:** It must be said that the number and sufficiency of the goods of matrimony can be taken on the part of the *instituting* or *efficient* cause, and thus from a threefold principle: namely from the dictate of *nature*, that a man should lie with a woman for the conservation of nature itself, and thus the good of *offspring*; from the dictate of *right reason*, that a man should lie with his own and not with another[^7] woman, and thus the good of *faith*; from the dictate of *grace*, that the union of the divine and human nature be signified, and thus the good of the *Sacrament*.

It can be taken on the part of the *constituting* principle, and this in three ways: because, in order that matrimony have *complete* being, three things are necessary, namely *institution*, *use*, and *fruit*. By reason of *institution* there is the good of the Sacrament, by reason of the due *use* there is the good of faith, by reason of the *fruit* conferring use there is the good of offspring. And thus it is clear that there is a threefold good.

It can also be taken on the part of the *moving* or *terminating end*, and this either because it is *toward the good*, or because it is *against evil*. Because *toward the good*: since the good to be chosen is threefold, namely the *honorable*, the *beneficial*, and the *pleasant*, by reason of the *honorable* good there is faith, by reason of the *beneficial* [good] offspring, by reason of the *pleasant* [good] there is the Sacrament, according to what was shown above[^8]. — Or otherwise: because it is *against the evil* of concupiscence, which has in itself a threefold evil, namely *flux*, *disorder*, and *loathing*; against *flux* there is in matrimony a *singular* union, against *disorder* a *useful* union, against *loathing* an *inseparable* union. By reason of *singularity* in the union there is faith, by reason of *usefulness* offspring, by reason of *inseparability* the Sacrament[^9]. — And these three signify the threefold property of the union of Christ with the Church, namely because He is united to one, and because *from her He generates sons*, and because He is never separated.

From these things the objections are made clear, because there are numbered together not any goods whatever, but those which are *common* to the state of corrupt and standing nature[^10]; therefore neither peace nor service nor the good which is taken insofar as it is a *remedy* are numbered among these goods, but these three goods alone.

4. To that which is objected, that they are *superfluous*, since of one thing there is one end; it must be said that there is one *principal* and *inseparable* good, namely the sacra- <!-- page 720 --> mental bond; and the others are annexed to it, and nothing prevents that a thing should have one principal end and several adjoined uses.

5. 6. The response is also clear to that which is objected concerning the good of the Sacrament: for the union itself is called the Sacrament of matrimony, but the inseparability of the union is called the *good* of the Sacrament, or the Sacrament which is the good of matrimony.

But the reason why the condition of inseparability has claimed[^11] this name for itself is that it is consubstantial with matrimony and follows it inseparably, not so the other conditions. And thus the objections are made clear[^12].
