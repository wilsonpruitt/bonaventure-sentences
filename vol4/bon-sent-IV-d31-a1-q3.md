## *Utrum haec tria bona sint de necessitate matrimonii*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 720 -->

### Quaestio III. *Utrum haec tria bona sint de necessitate matrimonii.*

Tertio quaeritur de necessitate horum trium bonorum, utrum scilicet sint de necessitate Sacramenti. Et quod bonum *Sacramenti* sit de necessitate,

*Argg. pro parte affirmativa.*

1. Dicit Augustinus, et habetur in littera[^1]: «Sacramentum ita inseparabiliter coniugio haeret, ut sine illo esse non videatur».

2. Item, *ratione* videtur: bonum Sacramenti est ratione inseparabilitatis; sed inseparabilitas[^2] semper inest ut necessitas: aut ergo bonum Sacramenti non est bonum, aut necessario inest.

Item, quod bonum *prolis* de necessitate consequatur matrimonium, videtur:

3. Quia dicit Augustinus, et habetur in littera[^3], quod «qui venena sterilitatis procuraverint non sunt coniuges».

4. Item, *ratione* videtur: quia ad hoc institutum fuit matrimonium, scilicet ad crescendum et multiplicandum[^4]: ergo frustra est, si hoc fine caret; sed non potest esse omnino frustra, cum sit Sacramentum: ergo etc.

Item, quod bonum *fidei* sit de necessitate Sacramenti, videtur:

5. Quia dicit Augustinus[^5], quod si quis consentiat in aliquam, ut postmodum aliam accipiat, non est Sacramentum; sed hoc est propter defectum fidei: ergo etc.

6. Item, *ratione* videtur: quia licet ex causa fornicationis matrimonium dividere[^6]; sed non liceret, nisi fides esset de substantia: ergo etc.

**Contra:** *(Pro parte negativa.)*

1. Quod *Sacramentum* non sit de substantia matrimonii, videtur: quia si aliquis contrahat cum aliqua per verba *de praesenti*, est matrimonium contractum, et tamen, illo transeunte ad religionem, ista potest nubere[^7]: ergo non erat ibi vinculum inseparabile.

2. Item, quod nec *fides*, videtur: quia matrimonium potest esse inter perpetuo continentes, et potest esse inter incontinentes, quia neuter cum alio vel alia potest contrahere: ergo etc.

3. Item, quod *proles* non sit de necessitate, videtur: quia matrimonium est inter steriles, sicut patet; sed hic non est bonum prolis.

Quaeritur ergo: quid est quod dicit Augustinus[^8], quod «non sunt coniuges»? Si enim prius consenserunt, fuit matrimonium ratum; si post coierunt, fuit consummatum; sed hoc nullo casu, nullo peccato rumpitur.

> **Conclusio.** Bonum *Sacramenti* est de necessitate matrimonii; alia duo, scilicet *fides* et *proles*, quodam modo sunt de esse Sacramenti, et quodam modo tantum de bene esse.

**Respondeo:** Sicut tangit Magister in littera[^9] et accipit ab Augustino, cum sint tria bona matrimonii, scilicet *fides*, *proles* et *Sacramentum*, unum, scilicet *Sacramentum*, est de *necessitate* matrimonii. Sacramentum enim venit ex mutua obligatione et divina institutione et significatione illius unionis; et ideo, quia ista sunt de necessitate matrimonii, per consequens et Sacramentum. — Sed alia duo bona quodam modo sunt de *esse*, quodam modo de *bene esse*. Si enim loquamur quantum ad *matrimonii usum*, sic sunt de *bene esse*[^10], quoniam usus matrimonii absque his esse potest, sicut tota die est; et non solum est *absque his*, sed etiam *cum suis*

<!-- page 721 -->

*oppositis*. Unde sterilitatis et aborsus procuratio et fidei fractio non dissolvunt matrimoniale vinculum contractum.

Si autem loquamur quantum ad *matrimonii contractum*, qui est per matrimonialem consensum; sic potest esse contractus *absque his duobus bonis*, ut puta in sterilibus et in continentibus, sed tamen[^11] non est *contra haec*. Tunc autem est consensus *contra haec*, quando sub *tali pacto consentitur*, ut prolis sterilitas procuretur, vel uxor alii prostituatur; tunc enim non est consensus vel pactio *matrimonii*, sed potius *adulterii*, cum directe repugnet institutioni matrimonii. Sed *carentia* sive absentia horum duorum non repugnat: quia *fides* venit ex recto matrimonii usu; et quia possunt homines abuti ipso, ideo potest deesse fides. Similiter *proles* venit ex fructu ventris; et quia steriles possunt esse vel natura, vel arte: ideo matrimonium hoc bono potest carere.

*(Ad 1.)* Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur, quod Sacramenti bonum non est in matrimonio *ante* carnalem copulam; dicendum, quod falsum est, quia est inter coniuges insolubile vinculum nec solvitur nisi per mortem *spiritualem*, sicut *post* consummationem per mortem *corporalem*[^12]. Verum est tamen, quod non est ibi Sacramentum secundum *completam* et plenariam rationem.

*(Ad arg. 5, pro parte affirm.)* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod fides sit[^13] de necessitate, quia qui sic consentit, ut tamen aliam ducere possit post, non contrahit; dicendum, quod hoc non est tantum propter defectum *fidei*, sed etiam propter defectum *Sacramenti*, quia non vult isti coniungi perpetuo, et quia in pactione introducitur conditio contraria bono *fidei*; nec propter infidelitatem solvitur matrimonii Sacramentum, sed solvitur obligatio ad reddendum debitum[^14].

*(Item ad arg. 4.)* Ad illud quod obiicitur de bono prolis, quod propter hoc institutum est; dicendum, quod non tantum propter hoc, sed etiam in *remedium* et in *signum*; et ideo, quamvis non sit ibi hoc bonum, possunt tamen esse alia bona. Si autem homo stetisset, tunc semper fuisset hoc bonum, quia tantum fuisset in *officium*; et hoc bonum non deesset, quia nulla esset sterilitatis poena[^15].

## English

### Question III. *Whether these three goods are of the necessity of marriage.*

Thirdly it is asked concerning the necessity of these three goods, namely whether they are of the necessity of the Sacrament. And that the good of the *Sacrament* is of necessity [is shown]:

*Arguments for the affirmative side.*

1. Augustine says, and it is contained in the text[^1]: "The Sacrament cleaves so inseparably to the marriage bond that it seems not to be without it."

2. Likewise, it appears by reason: the good of the Sacrament consists in the account of inseparability; but inseparability[^2] is always present as a necessity: therefore either the good of the Sacrament is not a good, or it is present necessarily.

Likewise, that the good of *offspring* follows of necessity upon marriage appears thus:

3. Because Augustine says, and it is contained in the text[^3], that "those who have procured the poisons of sterility are not spouses."

4. Likewise, it appears by reason: because marriage was instituted for this, namely for increasing and multiplying[^4]: therefore it is in vain if it lacks this end; but it cannot be altogether in vain, since it is a Sacrament: therefore etc.

Likewise, that the good of *faith* is of the necessity of the Sacrament appears thus:

5. Because Augustine says[^5] that if someone should consent to one woman so that afterwards he may take another, it is not a Sacrament; but this is on account of a defect of faith: therefore etc.

6. Likewise, it appears by reason: because it is permitted to divide a marriage for the cause of fornication[^6]; but it would not be permitted unless faith belonged to the substance: therefore etc.

**On the contrary:** *(For the negative side.)*

1. That the *Sacrament* is not of the substance of marriage appears thus: because if someone contracts with a woman through words *of the present*, the marriage is contracted, and yet, when he passes over to religion, she is able to marry[^7]: therefore there was not there an inseparable bond.

2. Likewise, that neither is *faith* [of the substance] appears thus: because marriage can exist between those perpetually continent, and can exist between the incontinent, since neither can contract with another man or woman: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, that *offspring* is not of necessity appears thus: because marriage exists among the sterile, as is evident; but here there is no good of offspring.

It is asked therefore: what is it that Augustine says[^8], that "they are not spouses"? For if they first consented, the marriage was ratified; if afterwards they came together, it was consummated; but this is broken in no case, by no sin.

> **Conclusion.** The good of the *Sacrament* is of the necessity of marriage; the other two, namely *faith* and *offspring*, are in a certain manner of the being of the Sacrament, and in a certain manner only of its well-being.

**I respond:** As the Master touches upon in the text[^9] and takes from Augustine, since there are three goods of marriage, namely *faith*, *offspring*, and *the Sacrament*, one, namely *the Sacrament*, is of the *necessity* of marriage. For the Sacrament comes from the mutual obligation and the divine institution and the signification of that union; and therefore, since these are of the necessity of marriage, consequently the Sacrament also is. — But the other two goods are in a certain manner of the *being*, and in a certain manner of the *well-being*. For if we speak with respect to the *use of marriage*, thus they are of the *well-being*[^10], since the use of marriage can exist without them, as is the case all the time; and it not only exists *without them*, but even *with their*

*opposites*. Hence the procuring of sterility and abortion and the breaking of faith do not dissolve the contracted marriage bond.

But if we speak with respect to the *contract of marriage*, which is through the matrimonial consent; thus the contract can exist *without these two goods*, as for instance among the sterile and among the continent, yet nevertheless[^11] it is not *against these things*. But then the consent is *against these things* when it is consented to under such a pact that the sterility of offspring be procured, or the wife be prostituted to another; for then it is not a consent or pact of *marriage*, but rather of *adultery*, since it directly conflicts with the institution of marriage. But the *lack* or absence of these two does not conflict: because *faith* comes from the right use of marriage; and since men can abuse it, therefore faith can be absent. Likewise *offspring* comes from the fruit of the womb; and since the sterile can be so either by nature or by art: therefore marriage can lack this good.

*(To 1.)* To that, then, which is objected, that the good of the Sacrament is not in marriage *before* carnal union; it must be said that this is false, because there is between the spouses an indissoluble bond, and it is not dissolved except by *spiritual* death, just as *after* consummation by *bodily* death[^12]. Yet it is true that the Sacrament is not there according to its *complete* and full account.

*(To argument 5, for the affirmative side.)* To that which is objected, that faith is[^13] of necessity, because he who consents in such a way that he may nevertheless marry another afterwards does not contract; it must be said that this is not only on account of a defect of *faith*, but also on account of a defect of the *Sacrament*, because he does not will to be joined to this woman perpetually, and because in the pact a condition contrary to the good of *faith* is introduced; nor is the Sacrament of marriage dissolved on account of infidelity, but the obligation of rendering the debt is dissolved[^14].

*(Likewise to argument 4.)* To that which is objected concerning the good of offspring, that marriage was instituted for its sake; it must be said that it was not only for its sake, but also for a *remedy* and for a *sign*; and therefore, although this good is not there, the other goods can nevertheless be present. But if man had stood [in innocence], then this good would always have been present, because it would have been only for [an] *office*; and this good would not be lacking, because there would be no penalty of sterility[^15].
