## Latin
<!-- page 592 -->

### Quaestio III. *Quae sit materia extremae unctionis.*

Tertio quaeritur de huius Sacramenti materia; et dicit Magister[^1], quod materia huius Sacramenti est «oleum ab episcopo consecratum». Sed primo videtur, quod magis deberet adhiberi *aliud elementum* quam oleum:

1. *Primum*, quia hoc Sacramentum est periclitantium et morti appropinquantium, et hi maxime sunt in articulo necessitatis: ergo talibus maxime de elemento communi provideri debet: si ergo aqua communior est, videtur etc.

2. Item, in confirmatione, in qua datur gratia abundans, non solum est elementum *olei*, sed etiam *balsami*, quod est pretiosum[^2]: ergo videtur, quod in hoc Sacramento, cum introducat hominem in regnum, quod est pretiosissimum praemium; videtur, quod deberet esse balsamum.

Item, videtur, quod non oporteat esse *consecratum*:

3. Quia baptismus et eucharistia, in quibus est magna gratia et efficacia, non indigent materia consecrata alia sanctificatione, quam quae fit in Sacramenti forma: ergo pari ratione nec unctio extrema.

4. Item, ad perfectum esse Sacramenti ista duo sufficiunt, scilicet *significatio* et *sanctificatio*[^3], significatio est per elementum, sanctificatio est per verbum; sed haec duo sunt, si quis inungatur de simplici oleo: ergo videtur, quod sit ibi Sacramentum perfectum.

Item, si oportet Sacramentum unctionis fieri de oleo *consecrato*; quaero: cui competit nomen vel ratio Sacramenti, an *oleo*, an *unctioni*? — Quod *unctioni*, videtur per ipsam denominationem Sacramenti.

5. Sed quod oleo *consecrato*, videtur, quia *oleum* est signum, et *oleum consecratum* «signum est sacrum sacrae rei», quod non tantum habet significationem, sed etiam quoddam genus causalitatis per benedictionem[^4]: ergo videtur, quod habeat in se completam Sacramenti rationem.

6. Item, panis consecratus est Sacramentum, non ipsa manducatio, immo potius est usus[^5]: ergo videtur similiter in proposito, quod oleum consecratum sit Sacramentum, et unctio sit usus: ergo non convenienter nominatur. Quod si non est verum, non videtur hic exigi materia consecrata.

7. Item, videtur, quod nec oporteat esse *consecratam ab episcopo*, quia plus est consecrare sive corpus Domini conficere, ubi est transsubstantiatio, quam oleum, ubi manet eadem usia[^6]; sed simplex sacerdos potest primum: ergo et secundum.

Iuxta hoc est quaestio: quare debet esse oleum; et quare simplex, non mixtum; et quare consecratum ab episcopo; et si illa consecratio faciat, oleum consecratum esse Sacramentum.

<!-- page 593 -->

### Conclusio

> *Extremae unctionis materia est oleum consecratum ab episcopo.*

**Respondeo:** Ad praedictorum intelligentiam est notandum, quod aliqui dicere voluerunt, quod *materia* in hoc Sacramento est oleum, sed *consecratio* episcopalis superveniens facit esse *Sacramentum*; sed *inunctio* consequens est Sacramenti *usus* sive dispensatio.

Sed quoniam istud repugnat nominationi Sacramenti et etiam communi opinioni; ideo melius dicatur, quod sicut aqua in baptismo est elementum, sic in confirmatione chrisma et in unctione oleum consecratum ab episcopo.

Ratio autem sumi potest a parte *efficientis* sive *instituentis*, et etiam a parte *finis*. A parte *instituentis*: quoniam enim Christus per semetipsum instituit, qui per semetipsum potuit sanctificare, baptismum et eucharistiam; ideo non est alia consecratio praeparans necessaria, sed solum ad solemnitatem facit. Sed hoc Sacramentum Spiritus sanctus instituit per Apostolos, qui non poterant sanctificare nisi per Deum; ideo materia est non simplex elementum, sed oleum per verbum et invocationem sancti Spiritus consecratum, et hoc per verbum episcoporum, qui sunt successores Apostolorum; ut, sicut Spiritus sanctus instituit per Apostolos, sic etiam nunc consecret per episcopos[^7]. — A parte *finis* sumitur ratio: quoniam hic datur gratia sanans languores spirituales, et datur gratia ordinans ad sanitatem perfectam, quae quidem est gloriae; ideo debet significari per *oleum*, quod est curativum, ratione curationis, et per *oleum consecratum*, ratione ordinationis ad supereminentem curationem, et per *oleum simplex*, non mixtum, quia ad ingressum in gloriam requiritur et sufficit bona conscientia[^8]. — His visis, facile est solvere ad obiecta.

1. Ad illud enim quod obiicitur de communitate materiae, dicendum, quod istud non est Sacramentum *necessitatis*; et *praeterea*, modicum de elemento sufficit, et hoc potest ubique locorum haberi, et ideo non est periculum aliquod. *Praeterea*, evitatio periculi non debet praeiudicare dignitati et congruitati Sacramenti.

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod deberet misceri cum balsamo, sicut in confirmatione; dicendum, quod non est simile: quia in *confirmatione* datur gratia ad pugnandum in vita, per quam oportet ambulare non solum secundum placentiam ad Deum, verum etiam ad *proximum*; sed in unctione datur gratia ad resistendum diabolo, ne possit detinere, et ad hoc non est necessaria *fama*, sed *bona conscientia*; et quia *balsamum* aromatizans significat bonam famam[^9], et *oleum* nitens bonam conscientiam: ideo etc.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod eucharistia non indiget alia consecratione; dicendum quod non, est simile tum ex parte *instituentis*, tum ex parte *finis*, ut visum est.

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod in oleo non consecrato, si fiat unctio, est significatio et sanctificatio; dicendum, quod falsum est. Primo enim deficit significatio, quae non tantum exigit *repraesentationem*, sed etiam *institutionem*; sic autem institutum est fieri. Secundo vero deficit sanctificatio, quia non habet verbum efficaciam, nisi habeat competens elementum[^10].

5. 6. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod oleum consecratum est Sacramentum; dicendum, quod proprie loquendo, non est *Sacramentum*, sed *ad Sacramentum ordinatum*. Et ratio huius est, quia non habet nec perfectam *significationem* nec *sanctificationem*, quousque applicetur infirmo, et superveniat sacerdotalis oratio; tunc enim significatur gratia intus ungens, et tunc est primo praesens[^11], non ante: et ideo non est simile de corpore Domini, ubi in prima sanctificatione fit conversio, et post non additur ulterior sanctificatio, quae novam introducat virtutem.

7. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod sacerdotes possunt corpus Domini conficere, et ita oleum consecrare; dicendum, quod non valet: quia tanta est potestas in ministris Ecclesiae, quantam distribuit Dominus membris; nec oportuit, potestatem consecrandi oleum ita communicari sacerdotibus, sicut conficiendi corpus Christi, quia unus episcopus faciliter toti provinciae potest unctionis oleum consecrare; sed non potest quotidie in singulis parochiis Missam cantare, sicut decet, cum ille sit panis quotidianus[^12] et communis omnibus: et sic patet illud.

### Scholion

**I.** Oleum olivae per episcopum benedictum esse materiam remotam huius Sacramenti, docetur in Decreto pro Armenis toties citato. Controversia autem est, utrum illa benedictio episcopi non tantum sit de necessitate *praecepti*, sed omnino de *essentia* Sacramenti. Certa tamen videtur esse sententia, quod ex *concessione Summi Pontificis* etiam a presbyteris praevia benedictio valide dari possit.

De hac 3. quaestione: S. Thom., hic q. 1. a. 2; Suppl. q. 29. a. 4. — B. Albert., hic a. 3. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 1. a. 3. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 1. q. 3. — Durand., hic q. 2. — Dionys. Carth., de hac et seq. q. hic q. 2.

**II.** Quoad 4. quaestionem de *forma* praenotandum est, quod ab antiquo diversae formae in hoc Sacramento administrando a diversis Ecclesiis adhibebantur, pleraeque quidem modo tantum *deprecativo* (ut forma nunc in Ecclesia Romana usitata), sed nonnullae modo tantum *indicativo*, aliae partim *indicativo*, partim *deprecativo*. De validitate Sacramenti cum forma tantum *indicativa* (*Inungo te* etc.) administrati controvertitur; *negativae* sententiae favent S. Thom. et Petr. a Tar. (Durandus nihil determinat), *affirmativae* vero B. Albert., Richard. a Med. et recentiores. Omnes autem antiqui opinionem primo loco hic relatam reprobant. B. Albert. testatur de «tota Ecclesia Alemanniae», quod «antiquissimi libri in illis terris habent indicativam orationem pro forma, et postea subiungitur oratio. Et haec sunt verba scripta in libris antiquissimis, quos prae antiquitate vix legere valebam: *Ungo hos oculos oleo sanctificato in nomine* etc.; et postea sequitur: *Per istam sanctissimam unctionem et suam piissimam*» etc. Consentit autem idem communi sententiae, quod forma indicativa non sit de necessitate Sacramenti, sed de *bene esse*, ut dicit. Pro eadem sententia affertur etiam S. Bonav. a Maurinis (nota 912. ad S. Gregorii librum Sacrament., ed. Migne col. 521.); sed cum ibi agatur de *forma mixta*, ex ipsis verbis auctoris (conclusio 2. et ad 3.) concludendum est, eum consentire potius positioni S. Thomae.

De 4. quaestione: S. Thom., hic q. 1. a. 4; Suppl. q. 29. a. 7-9. — B. Albert., hic a. 4. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 1. a. 4. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 1. q. 4. — Durand., hic q. 3. n. 7.

## English

### Question III. *What the matter of extreme unction is.*

Thirdly it is asked concerning the matter of this Sacrament; and the Master says[^1] that the matter of this Sacrament is «oil consecrated by a bishop». But at first it seems that *some other element* rather than oil ought to be employed:

1. *First*, because this Sacrament is [the Sacrament] of those in peril and drawing near to death, and these are most of all in the article of necessity: therefore for such people provision ought most of all to be made from a common element: if therefore water is more common, it seems [it should be used], etc.

2. Likewise, in confirmation, in which abundant grace is given, there is not only the element of *oil*, but also of *balsam*, which is precious[^2]: therefore it seems that in this Sacrament, since it introduces a man into the kingdom, which is a most precious reward, it seems that it ought to be balsam.

Likewise, it seems that it ought not to be *consecrated*:

3. Because baptism and the eucharist, in which there is great grace and efficacy, do not need matter consecrated by another sanctification than that which is done in the form of the Sacrament: therefore by parity of reasoning neither does extreme unction.

4. Likewise, for the perfect being of a Sacrament these two suffice, namely *signification* and *sanctification*[^3]: signification is through the element, sanctification is through the word; but these two are present, if someone is anointed with simple oil: therefore it seems that there is there a perfect Sacrament.

Likewise, if the Sacrament of unction must be made from *consecrated* oil, I ask: to which does the name or account of Sacrament belong, to the *oil* or to the *unction*? — That [it belongs] to the *unction*, seems [to follow] from the very denomination of the Sacrament.

5. But that [it belongs] to the *consecrated* oil, seems [to follow], because *oil* is a sign, and *consecrated oil* is «a sacred sign of a sacred thing», which not only has signification, but also a certain kind of causality through benediction[^4]: therefore it seems that it has in itself the complete account of a Sacrament.

6. Likewise, consecrated bread is the Sacrament, not the eating itself, which rather is the use[^5]: therefore it seems likewise in the case at hand that consecrated oil is the Sacrament, and the unction is the use: therefore it is not fittingly named. But if this is not true, it does not seem that consecrated matter is here required.

7. Likewise, it seems that it need not be *consecrated by a bishop*, because to consecrate — that is, to confect the body of the Lord, where there is transubstantiation — is a greater thing than [to consecrate] oil, where the same substance remains[^6]; but a simple priest can [do] the first: therefore also the second.

Bound up with this is the question: why it must be oil; and why simple, not mixed; and why consecrated by a bishop; and whether that consecration makes the consecrated oil to be the Sacrament.

### Conclusion

> *The matter of extreme unction is oil consecrated by a bishop.*

**I respond:** For the understanding of the foregoing it must be noted that some have wished to say that the *matter* in this Sacrament is oil, but the episcopal *consecration* supervening makes it to be the *Sacrament*; while the *anointing* that follows is the *use* or dispensation of the Sacrament.

But since this is repugnant to the naming of the Sacrament and also to common opinion, it is therefore better to say that, just as water in baptism is the element, so in confirmation chrism, and in unction oil consecrated by a bishop [is the matter].

Now the reason can be taken from the side of the *efficient* cause, that is, of the *institutor*, and also from the side of the *end*. From the side of the *institutor*: for since Christ instituted baptism and the eucharist through Himself — He who through Himself was able to sanctify — therefore there is no other preparatory consecration necessary, but it only makes for solemnity. But this Sacrament the Holy Spirit instituted through the Apostles, who could not sanctify except through God; therefore the matter is not a simple element, but oil consecrated through the word and invocation of the Holy Spirit, and this through the word of bishops, who are the successors of the Apostles; so that, just as the Holy Spirit instituted through the Apostles, so also now He may consecrate through bishops[^7]. — From the side of the *end* the reason is taken: for here is given grace healing spiritual languors, and grace is given ordering [one] to perfect health, which indeed is [the health] of glory; therefore it ought to be signified through *oil*, which is curative, by reason of the curing, and through *consecrated oil*, by reason of the ordering to a supereminent curing, and through *simple oil*, not mixed, because for entrance into glory a good conscience is required and suffices[^8]. — These things being seen, it is easy to resolve [the objections].

1. To that, then, which is objected concerning the commonness of the matter, it must be said that this is not a Sacrament of *necessity*; and *besides*, a small amount of the element suffices, and this can be had everywhere, and therefore there is no danger. *Besides*, the avoidance of danger ought not to prejudice the dignity and fittingness of the Sacrament.

2. To that which is objected, that it ought to be mixed with balsam, as in confirmation: it must be said that it is not similar, because in *confirmation* grace is given for fighting in life, by which one must walk not only according to what is pleasing to God, but also toward one's *neighbor*; whereas in unction grace is given for resisting the devil, lest he be able to detain [the soul], and for this a [good] *reputation* is not necessary, but a *good conscience*; and because aromatic *balsam* signifies a good reputation[^9], and shining *oil* a good conscience: therefore etc.

3. To that which is objected, that the eucharist does not need another consecration: it must be said that it is not similar, both from the side of the *institutor* and from the side of the *end*, as has been seen.

4. To that which is objected, that in unconsecrated oil, if unction be done, there is signification and sanctification: it must be said that this is false. For first signification is lacking, which requires not only *representation*, but also *institution*; but it has been instituted to be done thus. Secondly sanctification is lacking, because the word does not have efficacy unless it have a fitting element[^10].

5. 6. To that which is objected, that consecrated oil is the Sacrament: it must be said that, properly speaking, it is not the *Sacrament*, but *ordered to the Sacrament*. And the reason for this is that it has neither perfect *signification* nor *sanctification*, until it be applied to the sick person, and the priestly prayer supervene; for then is signified the grace inwardly anointing, and then it is first present[^11], not before: and therefore it is not similar to the body of the Lord, where in the first sanctification the conversion takes place, and afterward no further sanctification is added that would introduce a new power.

7. To that which is objected, that priests can confect the body of the Lord, and so consecrate oil: it must be said that it does not hold, because the power in the ministers of the Church is as great as the Lord distributes to the members; nor was it fitting that the power of consecrating oil be communicated to priests in the same way as [the power] of confecting the body of Christ, because one bishop can readily consecrate the oil of unction for a whole province; but he cannot daily sing Mass in the individual parishes, as is fitting, since that [body] is the daily[^12] bread, common to all: and thus that [objection] is clear.

### Scholion

**I.** That olive oil blessed by a bishop is the remote matter of this Sacrament is taught in the Decree for the Armenians so often cited. But there is a controversy whether that blessing of the bishop is not only of the necessity of the *precept*, but altogether of the *essence* of the Sacrament. Nevertheless it seems to be a certain opinion that, by *concession of the Supreme Pontiff*, a previous blessing can be validly given even by presbyters.

On this 3rd question: S. Thomas, here q. 1, a. 2; Suppl. q. 29, a. 4. — B. Albert, here a. 3. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 1, a. 3. — Richard of Mediavilla, here a. 1, q. 3. — Durandus, here q. 2. — Dionysius the Carthusian, on this and the following question, here q. 2.

**II.** As to the 4th question, concerning the *form*, it must be premised that from antiquity diverse forms in administering this Sacrament were employed by diverse Churches, most indeed in the merely *deprecative* mode (as the form now customary in the Roman Church), but some in the merely *indicative* mode, others partly *indicative*, partly *deprecative*. Concerning the validity of the Sacrament administered with a merely *indicative* form (*I anoint thee*, etc.) there is dispute; the *negative* opinion is favored by S. Thomas and Peter of Tarentaise (Durandus determines nothing), but the *affirmative* by B. Albert, Richard of Mediavilla, and the more recent [authors]. All the ancients, however, reprove the opinion related here in the first place. B. Albert testifies concerning «the whole Church of Germany» that «the most ancient books in those lands have the indicative prayer for the form, and afterward a prayer is subjoined. And these are the words written in the most ancient books, which on account of their antiquity I could scarcely read: *I anoint these eyes with sanctified oil in the name* etc.; and afterward there follows: *Through this most holy unction and His most loving [mercy]*» etc. But the same [author] agrees with the common opinion that the indicative form is not of the necessity of the Sacrament, but of its *well-being*, as he says. For the same opinion S. Bonaventure is also adduced by the Maurists (note 912 on S. Gregory's book on the Sacraments, ed. Migne col. 521); but since the matter there treated is of the *mixed form*, from the very words of the author (conclusion 2 and reply to 3) it must be concluded that he agrees rather with the position of S. Thomas.

On the 4th question: S. Thomas, here q. 1, a. 4; Suppl. q. 29, a. 7-9. — B. Albert, here a. 4. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 1, a. 4. — Richard of Mediavilla, here a. 1, q. 4. — Durandus, here q. 3, n. 7.
