# IV Sent., d. 38, a. 1, q. 2

## *De quo sive circa quam materiam sit votum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 816 -->

### Quaestio II. *De quo sive circa quam materiam sit votum.*

Secundo quaeritur, de quo sit votum, sive de materia, circa quam est votum; et videtur, quod sit circa omnia.

1. Et *primo* videtur, quod de *omni bono*: quia omne bonum potest homo promittere et spondere Deo et in tali sponsione obligatur; sed non est alia[^p816-1] quam votum: ergo etc. Unde et in baptismo promittit quod est necessitatis, scilicet abrenuntiare satanae.

2. Item, quod sit de *minori bono*, videtur: quia licitum est illud velle: ergo licite potest se homo ad illud obligare, ergo licite vovere; et istud videtur *per simile* in matrimonio, in quo quis obligat se ad statum imperfectionis[^p816-2].

3. Item, quod sit de *indifferenti*, videtur: quia cibus et potus, quantum est de se, dicunt actus indifferentes, quia naturales sunt; sed circa cibum et potum contingit esse votum, utpote votum abstinentiae: ergo et circa indifferentia.

4. Item, videtur, quod de *malis* possit esse votum: quia occidere innocentem est malum; sed Iephte[^p816-3] propter votum occidit filiam suam virginem et laudatur de voto. Unde Hieronymus dicit, quod «ideo ponitur in catalogo Sanctorum», quia «Deo placuit animus voventis»: ergo etc.

5. Item, conscientia non magis obligat quam votum; sed conscientia erronea ligat, quia *quod non est ex fide est peccatum*[^p816-4], id est contra conscientiam: ergo et votum erroneum; sed tunc errat homo in voto, quando vovet malum pro bono: ergo etc.

**Contra:**
1. Quod non sit de *bono praecepti*, videtur: quia super illud[^p816-5]: *Vovete et reddite*, Glossa: «Vovere est voluntatis, sed reddere necessitatis»: ergo est de his solum, ad quae homo non aliunde, sed voluntarie obligatur; sed talia sunt supererogationis: ergo etc.

2. Item, quod non sit de *minori bono*, videtur per definitionem voti[^p816-6]: «Votum est conceptio melioris propositi cum deliberatione firmata»: ergo non videtur, quod sit de statu imperfectionis et minori bono. Et ideo videtur, quod votum debet esse ad promotionem salutis nostrae, non in dispendium; sed obligare ad statum minus bonum est in dispendium salutis, quia secundum Bernardum[^p816-7]: «Nemo bonus, qui non vult esse melior»: ergo etc.

3. Item, quod non sit de *indifferenti*, ostenditur per definitionem; dicitur[^p816-8] enim, quod «votum de his quae Deo pertinent, fieri debet»; sed talia non sunt indifferentia: ergo etc.

4. Item, quod non de *malis*, videtur: quia dicit Isidorus[^p816-9]: «In malis promissis rescinde fidem, in turpi voto muta decretum»; sed votum verum est immutabile voventi: ergo votum turpe vel de turpi non est votum verum.

5. Item, votum verum obligat ad id, de quo est; sed nullus obligari potest ad malum: ergo etc.

<!-- page 817 -->

*Probatio minoris*[^p817-1]: nullus faciens id, ad quod tenetur, facit malum; sed si homo obligatur ad malum et facit malum, facit id, ad quod tenetur: ergo faciens malum non facit malum.

### Conclusio.

> Votum, generaliter loquendo, est de his quae ad Deum pertinent et faciunt ad salutem; simpliciter vero et proprie est de his quae promovent ad salutem ultra statum communem.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod votum generaliter loquendo est de his quae ad Deum pertinent et faciunt ad salutem; simpliciter vero et proprie est de his quae promovent ad salutem ultra statum communem. Unde quaecumque sunt talia, cadunt sub voto; quae autem his opposita sunt, ut quae ad dispendium salutis vergunt, sub voto cadere non possunt; quoniam votum debet habere comites, quos habet iuramentum, scilicet *veritatem, iudicium* et *iustitiam*[^p817-2].

Primo namque necesse est, ut in eo sit *iustitia*, scilicet ut illud quod vovetur, spectet ad salutem et sit bonum et honestum. — Secundo est *iudicium*, ut vovens deliberate voveat, non praecipitanter. — Tertio, quod sit in eo *veritas*, ut quod discrete et iuste vovit, hoc persolvat. Si ergo illud quod vovetur, ad detrimentum iustitiae faciat; non est ibi lex voti nec obligatio, sed ipsi voto fit iniuria; nec est obligatio ad servandum, sed potius ad poenitendum, sicut de conscientia erronea iudicatur. Secundum hanc regulam communem iudicandum est de his quae quaeruntur in speciali.

1. Quod enim quaeritur, utrum votum sit de *omni bono*, scilicet praecepti et consilii; dicendum, quod votum, proprie accipiendo, est de bono supererogationis, large tamen, si dicatur quaecumque testificatio, potest esse de bono praecepti. — Et per hoc patent obiecta ad utramque partem. Illud enim votum, quamvis non sit obligatio de novo, est tamen testificatio obligationis, qua homo non solum fatetur se obligatum, sed etiam complacet sibi in illa obligatione, et obligatio necessaria fit voluntaria[^p817-3]; et sic multi faciunt de necessitate virtutem.

2. Ad illud vero quod quaeritur, utrum sit de *minori bono*; dicendum, quod minus bonum est dupliciter considerare: uno modo, prout dicit statum quendam licitum; alio modo, prout dicit privationem maioris boni. Primo modo licitum est vovere illud respectu mali, quia respectu illius est quodam modo maius bonum. Secundo modo, prout privatorium maioris boni, licitum non est, nec obligatur quis ad servandum, sed ad poenitendum; ut si quis voveat, quod Deo serviet caste in statu saeculari, et nunquam ingredietur religionem: primum bonum est et licitum, sed in quantum privat statum altiorem, illicitum est, quia gratiam Dei duro corde repellit; et hoc est in praeiudicium salutis et in derogationem abundantis iustitiae. — Et *si tu obiicias*: licitum est sic esse, ergo et *vovere*; dicendum, quod sicut laudari non est malum, tamen appetere laudem est malum; sic in tali statu esse non est malum, sed obligare se, ut non possit ad melius pervenire, obligatione[^p817-4], quae fiat Deo, Deo iniuriam facit. Nec est simile de matrimonio, quia est obligatio facta alii personae, quae contenta est illa et non quaerit perfectiorem; Deus autem vult semper quod melius est.

3. Ad illud quod quaeritur de *indifferenti*, dicendum, quod indifferens est dupliciter: unum, quod informabile est motu et circumstantia virtutis, sicut comedere et abstinere, et hoc potest voveri; aliud, quod nihil facit, quantum est de se, ut levare festucam vel ire ad prata, et de tali proprie non est votum; et si quis tale quid voveat, iudicio caret, quia nihil vovendum est, nisi quod creditur rationabiliter esse acceptum Deo. Unde si tale votum voveat quis, non est tenendum, sed commutandum. Et per hoc patet utrumque obiectum.

4. Ad illud autem quod obiicitur de *malo*, dicendum, quod aliquid *simpliciter* est malum; et hoc nullo modo potest quis vovere, et si vovet, peccat, sed non obligatur. Aliud est, quod uno casu contingente, potest esse bonum, alio casu contingente, potest esse malum; et hoc potest voveri sub conditione eventus boni; quodsi vovetur *in omnem eventum* sine conditione, indiscretum est votum, et non tenetur ad illud[^p817-5]. — Quod ergo obiicitur de voto Iephte, dicendum, quod quia vovit *in omnem eventum*, votum fuit indiscretum; sed tamen, quia devotio et fides movit, sed indiscrete emisit, inde[^p817-6] duo fuerunt in voti *emissione*: unum laudabile, alterum vituperabile; similiter in voti *impletione* duo fuerunt, scilicet *figura* et *factum*, et factum fuit vituperabile, sed figura laudabilis. Quantum ergo ad diversa in voto Iephte, dicendum, quod laudatur ab Apostolo et Hieronymo[^p817-7], scilicet quantum ad fidem et

<!-- page 818 -->

devotionem; sed quantum ad indiscretionem et crudelitatem vituperatur ab Augustino; dicit enim, quod fuit in vovendo stultus et in reddendo fuit impius.

5. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod conscientia erronea obligat; dicendum, quod conscientia dicitur erronea dupliciter: aut quia credit, se teneri ad id, ad quod non tenetur, tamen non tenetur ad eius oppositum; et talis error non est periculosus, et talis ligat. Est et alia erronea, quae credit bonum quod non est bonum, immo malum; et talis ligat non ad peccatum nec ad factum, sed ad se deponendum[^p818-1]. Sic votum erroneum, quod vergit in malum, est dimittendum, quia non obligat ad faciendum[^p818-2].

## English

### Question II. *On what, or the matter concerning which, a vow is.*

In the second place inquiry is made concerning what a vow is about, or concerning the matter about which a vow is; and it seems that it is about everything.

1. And *first* it seems that it is about *every good*: because a man can promise and pledge every good to God, and by such a pledge he is bound; but this is nothing other[^p816-1] than a vow: therefore etc. Hence also in baptism he promises what is of necessity, namely to renounce Satan.

2. Likewise, that it is about a *lesser good* seems [to be the case]: because it is licit to will it: therefore a man can licitly bind himself to it, therefore [he can] licitly vow it; and this seems [to hold] *by a similar case* in marriage, in which one binds himself to a state of imperfection[^p816-2].

3. Likewise, that it is about the *indifferent* seems [to be the case]: because food and drink, so far as they are of themselves, are called indifferent acts, since they are natural; but concerning food and drink a vow can be made, as for instance a vow of abstinence: therefore also concerning indifferent things.

4. Likewise, it seems that a vow can be about *evils*: because to kill an innocent person is evil; but Jephthah[^p816-3] on account of a vow killed his virgin daughter and is praised for the vow. Hence Jerome says that "he is therefore placed in the catalogue of the Saints," because "the mind of the one vowing was pleasing to God": therefore etc.

5. Likewise, conscience does not bind more than a vow; but an erroneous conscience binds, because *what is not of faith is sin*[^p816-4], that is, contrary to conscience: therefore also an erroneous vow [binds]; but then a man errs in the vow when he vows evil for good: therefore etc.

**On the contrary:**
1. That it is not about the *good of precept* seems [to be the case]: because upon that text[^p816-5]: *Vow and render*, the Gloss [says]: "To vow belongs to the will, but to render belongs to necessity": therefore it is only about those things to which a man is bound not from elsewhere, but voluntarily; but such things are of supererogation: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, that it is not about a *lesser good* seems [to be the case] from the definition of a vow[^p816-6]: "A vow is the conception of a better purpose, confirmed by deliberation": therefore it does not seem that it is about a state of imperfection and a lesser good. And therefore it seems that a vow ought to be for the promotion of our salvation, not to its detriment; but to bind oneself to a lesser good is to the detriment of salvation, because according to Bernard[^p816-7]: "No one is good who does not wish to be better": therefore etc.

3. Likewise, that it is not about the *indifferent* is shown from the definition; for it is said[^p816-8] that "a vow ought to be made concerning those things which pertain to God"; but such things are not indifferent: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, that it is not about *evils* seems [to be the case]: because Isidore says[^p816-9]: "In evil promises break faith, in a base vow change the decree"; but a true vow is unchangeable for the one vowing: therefore a base vow, or one about a base thing, is not a true vow.

5. Likewise, a true vow binds to that of which it is; but no one can be bound to evil: therefore etc.

<!-- page 817 -->

*Proof of the minor*[^p817-1]: no one doing that to which he is bound does evil; but if a man is bound to evil and does evil, he does that to which he is bound: therefore one doing evil does not do evil.

### Conclusion.

> A vow, generally speaking, is about those things which pertain to God and conduce to salvation; but simply and properly it is about those things which promote salvation beyond the common state.

**I respond:** It must be said that a vow, generally speaking, is about those things which pertain to God and conduce to salvation; but simply and properly it is about those things which promote salvation beyond the common state. Hence whatever things are such fall under a vow; but those things which are opposed to these, as those which tend to the detriment of salvation, cannot fall under a vow; since a vow ought to have the companions which an oath has, namely *truth, judgment*, and *justice*[^p817-2].

For first it is necessary that there be *justice* in it, namely that what is vowed pertain to salvation and be good and honorable. — Secondly there is *judgment*, that the one vowing vow deliberately, not precipitately. — Thirdly, that there be *truth* in it, so that what he vowed discreetly and justly, this he should pay. If therefore what is vowed should tend to the detriment of justice, there is there no law of the vow nor obligation, but injury is done to the vow itself; nor is there an obligation to keep it, but rather to repent, as is judged concerning an erroneous conscience. According to this common rule judgment must be made concerning those things which are inquired into in particular.

1. For as to what is inquired, whether a vow is about *every good*, namely of precept and of counsel; it must be said that a vow, taking it properly, is about the good of supererogation, yet broadly, if any testification whatever be so called, it can be about the good of precept. — And through this the objections on both sides are made evident. For that vow, although it is not an obligation anew, is nevertheless a testification of the obligation, by which a man not only confesses himself bound, but also takes pleasure in that obligation, and the necessary obligation becomes voluntary[^p817-3]; and thus many make a virtue of necessity.

2. But to that which is inquired, whether it is about a *lesser good*; it must be said that a lesser good can be considered in two ways: in one way, as it names a certain licit state; in another way, as it names a privation of a greater good. In the first way it is licit to vow it with respect to evil, because with respect to that [evil] it is in a certain way a greater good. In the second way, as depriving of a greater good, it is not licit, nor is anyone bound to keep it, but to repent; as if someone should vow that he will serve God chastely in the secular state, and will never enter religion: the first is good and licit, but insofar as it deprives of a higher state, it is illicit, because it repels the grace of God with a hard heart; and this is to the prejudice of salvation and to the derogation of abounding justice. — And *if you object*: it is licit so to be, therefore also to *vow* it; it must be said that, just as to be praised is not evil, yet to seek praise is evil; so to be in such a state is not evil, but to bind oneself, so that one cannot attain to something better, by an obligation[^p817-4] made to God, does injury to God. Nor is it similar with marriage, because it is an obligation made to another person, who is content with that [state] and does not seek a more perfect one; but God always wills what is better.

3. To that which is inquired concerning the *indifferent*, it must be said that the indifferent is twofold: one that is capable of being informed by the movement and circumstance of virtue, such as eating and abstaining, and this can be vowed; the other, which does nothing, so far as it is of itself, as to lift a straw or to go to the meadows, and about such a thing there is properly no vow; and if anyone should vow such a thing, he lacks judgment, because nothing is to be vowed except what is believed to be reasonably acceptable to God. Hence if anyone should vow such a vow, it is not to be kept, but to be commuted. And through this both objections are made evident.

4. But to that which is objected concerning *evil*, it must be said that something is *simply* evil; and this no one can in any way vow, and if he vows it, he sins, but is not bound. There is another thing, which by one contingent case can be good, by another contingent case can be evil; and this can be vowed under the condition of a good outcome; but if it is vowed *for every outcome* without condition, the vow is indiscreet, and one is not bound to it[^p817-5]. — As to what is therefore objected concerning the vow of Jephthah, it must be said that, because he vowed *for every outcome*, the vow was indiscreet; but nevertheless, because devotion and faith moved him, yet he uttered it indiscreetly, thence[^p817-6] there were two things in the *making* of the vow: one praiseworthy, the other blameworthy; likewise in the *fulfilling* of the vow there were two things, namely the *figure* and the *deed*, and the deed was blameworthy, but the figure praiseworthy. As therefore to the diverse things in the vow of Jephthah, it must be said that he is praised by the Apostle and by Jerome[^p817-7], namely as to faith and

<!-- page 818 -->

devotion; but as to the indiscretion and cruelty he is blamed by Augustine; for he says that he was foolish in vowing and impious in rendering.

5. To that which is objected, that an erroneous conscience binds; it must be said that a conscience is called erroneous in two ways: either because it believes itself bound to that to which it is not bound, yet it is not bound to the opposite of it; and such an error is not dangerous, and such [a conscience] binds. There is also another erroneous [conscience], which believes to be good what is not good, but rather evil; and such [a conscience] binds not to sin nor to the deed, but to laying itself aside[^p818-1]. Thus an erroneous vow, which tends toward evil, is to be dismissed, because it does not bind to the doing of it[^p818-2].
