## Latin
<!-- page 171 -->

### Quaestio III. *Utrum copiosius munus gratiae conferatur in confirmatione quam in baptismo.*

Tertio quaeritur, utrum copiosius munus gratiae conferatur in confirmatione quam in baptismo.

Et quod sic, videtur:

1. *Ratione effectus:* est enim haec ad confirmandum, illa ad iustificandum; haec est ad credendum, haec ad confitendum[^1]: si ergo maioris gratiae est confirmatum esse quam iustificari, maioris etiam publice confiteri quam occulte credere; videtur, quod gratia confirmationis sit copiosior.

2. Item, *ratione alia* videtur: quia signum in hoc Sacramento est pretiosius et carius: ergo et gratia signata pretiosior et maior in confirmatione, cum signum in Sacramentis debeat respondere signato[^2].

3. Item, *ratione dispensationis:* quia non ideo maius est Sacramentum, quia a maioribus, sed quia maius, ideo est eius dispensatio commissa maioribus[^3]; sed dispensatio huius Sacramenti est maioribus commissa quam baptismi: ergo etc.

4. Item, hoc ipsum ostenditur *auctoritate* Rabani[^4]: «In baptismo Spiritus sanctus descendit ad habitationem Deo consecrandam, in hac vero eiusdem septiformis gratia cum omni plenitudine sanctitatis et virtutis venit in hominem»: patet ergo, quod confert maius munus.

**Contra:**

1. Gratia baptismalis est contra culpam originalem et omnes eius sequelas, sed confirmationis contra *unam* sequelam, ut puta pusillanimitatem[^5]; sed congruum est, contra morbum maiorem et efficaciorem dari medicinam: ergo etc.

2. Item, ratione effectus primi videtur: quia maius donum est de inimico fieri amicum quam de amico magis amicum; sed in baptismo fit homo amicus, in confirmatione autem magis[^6]: ergo in baptismo confertur maius donum.

3. Item, baptismus facit hominem innocentem et statim aperit caelum[^7]; sed hoc non facit confirmatio nec aliud Sacramentum: ergo baptismus est efficacius Sacramentum.

4. Item, ad hoc est auctoritas Magistri in litera[^8]: «Maius est hoc Sacramentum, non ob maiorem virtutem, vel utilitatem, quam conferat, sed quia a dignioribus datur»: ergo etc.

### Conclusio.

> Gratia confirmationis non praecise, sed cum praesuppositione dici potest maior gratia baptismali.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod gratia confirmationis praesupponit gratiam baptismalem, sicut bene esse supponit esse. Quando igitur fit comparatio harum gratiarum, potest fieri dupliciter: uno modo cum praesuppositione; et sic omnino maior est gratia confirmationis quam baptismalis; sicut «bene et perfecte vivere melius quam vivere»[^9], quia hoc addit ad illud et complet. — Si autem intelligatur praecise, ut scilicet haec gratia, quae additur, sit maior, vel minor; dicendum sine dubio, quod non est tantae efficaciae, ut patet, quia vivificare mortuum maior gratia est et potentia, quam iam vivificatum sanare vel fortificare, praecise loquendo; sed cum praesuppositione maius est esse vivum et sanum quam vivum tantum.

[Notandum:] ente et subsequente, dicendum, quod non est simile, quia ad eundem effectum ordinantur gratia praeveniens et subsequens, non sic gratia Sacramentorum; nihilominus tamen concedo, quod illa gratia, gratum faciens, secundum quod huiusmodi, *cedit in unum* cum gratia baptismali et illam auget: ideo prima ratio non valet, quod quae habent ordinem temporis non sunt idem. Illud enim fallit in his quorum unum se habet per additionem ad alterum[^10][^11].

<!-- page 172 -->

**[Ad argumenta:]** Rationes ergo probantes, quod confirmationis gratia maior est, intelliguntur *cum praesuppositione,* quia auget gratiam baptismalem; sed rationes probantes contrarium, intelliguntur *cum praecisione,* hanc gratiam illi cui additur, comparando; et hoc patet per singulas rationes cuilibet inspicienti[^12].

## English
<!-- page 171 -->

### Question III. *Whether a more copious gift of grace is conferred in confirmation than in baptism.*

Thirdly it is asked whether a more copious gift of grace is conferred in confirmation than in baptism.

And that it is so, it seems:

1. *By reason of effect:* for this [grace] is for confirming, that one for justifying; this is for believing, this for confessing[^1]: if therefore it is of greater grace to be confirmed than to be justified, of greater grace also to confess publicly than to believe secretly; it seems that the grace of confirmation is more copious.

2. Likewise, *by another reason* it seems: because the sign in this Sacrament is more precious and more dear: therefore also the signified grace is more precious and greater in confirmation, since in the Sacraments the sign ought to correspond to the thing signified[^2].

3. Likewise, *by reason of dispensation:* because a Sacrament is not greater on the ground that it is from greater [persons], but because it is greater, on that account its dispensation has been committed to greater [persons][^3]; but the dispensation of this Sacrament has been committed to greater [persons] than that of baptism: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, this same thing is shown *by the authority* of Rabanus[^4]: «In baptism the Holy Spirit descends to consecrate a dwelling-place for God, but in this [Sacrament] the sevenfold [grace] of the same [Spirit], with all fullness of holiness and virtue, comes into the human being»: it is clear therefore that it confers a greater gift.

**On the contrary:**

1. Baptismal grace is against original fault and all its consequences, but [the grace] of confirmation against *one* consequence, as for instance pusillanimity[^5]; but it is fitting that against a greater and more efficacious disease a [stronger] medicine be given: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, it seems by reason of the first effect: because it is a greater gift to be made a friend from an enemy than from a friend a greater friend; but in baptism a person is made a friend, while in confirmation [he is made] more [a friend][^6]: therefore in baptism a greater gift is conferred.

3. Likewise, baptism makes a person innocent and at once opens heaven[^7]; but confirmation does not do this, nor any other Sacrament: therefore baptism is the more efficacious Sacrament.

4. Likewise, to this there is the authority of the Master in the text[^8]: «This Sacrament is greater, not on account of a greater virtue, or usefulness, which it confers, but because it is given by worthier [ministers]»: therefore etc.

### Conclusion.

> The grace of confirmation can be called greater than the baptismal grace not absolutely, but with a presupposition.

**I respond:** It must be said that the grace of confirmation presupposes baptismal grace, as well-being presupposes being. When therefore a comparison of these graces is made, it can be done in two ways: in one way with presupposition; and thus the grace of confirmation is altogether greater than the baptismal; just as «to live well and perfectly is better than to live»[^9], because the former adds to the latter and completes it. — But if it be understood absolutely, namely so that this grace which is added be greater or lesser; it must be said without doubt that it is not of such efficacy, as is clear, because to give life to a dead thing is a greater grace and power than to heal or strengthen one already given life, speaking absolutely; but with presupposition it is greater to be living and sound than merely living.

[Note:] [as to the preceding and following], it must be said that it is not similar, because preventing and subsequent grace are ordered to the same effect, not so the grace of the Sacraments; nevertheless I concede that that grace, as making pleasing, insofar as it is such, *coincides into one* with the baptismal grace and augments it: therefore the first reason is not valid, [namely] that things which have an order of time are not the same. For that fails in those things of which the one is related to the other by addition[^10][^11].

<!-- page 172 -->

**[To the arguments:]** The reasons therefore proving that the grace of confirmation is greater are understood *with presupposition,* because it augments the baptismal grace; but the reasons proving the contrary are understood *with absoluteness,* comparing this grace to that to which it is added; and this is clear through the individual reasons to anyone who inspects them[^12].
