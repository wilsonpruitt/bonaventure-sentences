# IV Sent., d. 12, p. II — Dubia circa litteram Magistri
## *Dubia circa litteram Magistri*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 298 -->

### DUBIA CIRCA LITTERAM MAGISTRI.

### Dub. I

In parte ista sunt dubitationes circa litteram, et primo de hoc quod dicit, quod *in hoc Sacramento fit memoria illius quod factum est semel.* Si enim hoc verum est, ergo tantum semel deberet fieri ab uno sacerdote et semel manducari. — Item, baptismus, quia significat passionem Christi, non iteratur[^1]: ergo nec illud Sacramentum.

**Respondeo:** sicut dicit Magister in littera, hic[^2] significatur passio Christi tanquam in *signo* et tanquam in signo *rememorativo*; et quoniam frequenter rememorari debemus Christi semel passi: ideo illud Sacramentum iteratur pluries. — Sed *si tunc obiicias* de Sacramento baptismi; dicendum, quod non est simile: quia baptismus significat passionem, prout nos per illam morimur peccato originali, quod traximus ab Adam: et hoc non facit passio in quolibet nisi semel; hinc est, quod baptismus non potest iterari, et si quis iterat, facit contumeliam Sacramento.

<!-- page 299 -->

In hoc autem significatur *Christus* passus ut nos reficiens; et quoniam saepe reficit in omni[^3] memoratione, ideo saepe iteratur illud Sacramentum sine contumelia; nec dicitur proprie *iterari* Sacramentum, sed *frequentari*; quoniam unum et idem continetur in hostia hesterna et hodierna; tunc autem *iteraretur*, si super eandem hostiam fieret iteratio formae[^4]; et hoc non posset fieri scienter absque peccato.

### Dub. II

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *quotidie offerimus ad recordationem mortis.* Est enim instantia in die Veneris sancto, quando sacerdos non conficit nec sacrificium offert[^5]; et videtur, quod tunc magis deberet. Si enim hoc est *memoriale passionis*: ergo cum illo die specialiter *memoria* habeatur, videtur, quod tunc magis esset conficiendum. — Item, quaeritur iuxta hoc: quare Sacramentum reservatur sub forma panis, non vini?

Et respondetur ad primum, quod aliud est *conficere* sive consecrare, aliud est *offerre*; et quamvis in illo die non fiat *confectio*, fit tamen *oblatio*, quia sacerdos corpus in praecedenti die consecratum offert in altari. — Si vero quaeras: quare fit oblatio et non *consecratio*? respondetur, quod *oblatio* sive sumtio fit a sacerdote, ne oratio dominicalis, quae dicitur quotidie et illo die ab Ecclesia, in qua petitur: *Panem nostrum quotidianum*[^6] etc., vacua fiat. — Sed *consecratio* non fit, sed reservatio propter hoc, quod in illo die fuit Dominus passus in propria effigie, non in signo. Et quia tota dies est memoriale passionis et totum officium; ideo non fit consecratio a Coena usque ad Sabbatum, in quo est memoria resurrectionis; unde cantatur Missa de resurrectione.

Quod quaeritur de sanguine: quare non reservatur? aliqui dicunt, quod non oportet, quia vinum, quod sacerdos ponit in calicem, per appositionem corporis consecratur. — Sed illud non potest habere veritatem, sicut probari potest de facili, quia nullo modo fit transsubstantiatio sine verbo[^7]. Et ideo dicunt alii, quod triplici de causa non reservatur: una est propter periculum effusionis; alia propter verbum Domini, quod dixit super calicem[^8]: *Non bibam de hoc genimine* etc., et ideo non sumit sanguinem sacerdos a Coena usque ad resurrectionem; tertia est, quia sacerdos magis sumit ad refectionem quam ad significationem, et quia totus Christus est perfecte sub specie panis, quamvis non perfecte significetur; ideo solum in specie panis sumit, et solum sacrificium sive Sacramentum corporis reservatur[^9].

### Dub. III

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *semel immolatus est Christus.* Si enim semel immolatus est Christus, videtur, quod saltem in die non liceat sacerdoti pluries conficere. — Quaeritur ergo quomodo liceat sacerdoti pluries conficere et sumere in eodem die, cum populo non liceat nisi semel; et videtur, quod magis sit licitum populo, cum minus sit horum alterum, scilicet sumere, quam utrumque, scilicet conficere et sumere.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod propter reverentiam Sacramenti sunt duo instituta circa modum sumendi hoc Sacramentum, scilicet quod sumatur ieiuno stomacho, et quod tantum semel, ut ab aliis cibis discernatur. In primo communicat sacerdos et populus; oportet enim, quod sacerdos ieiunus sit, sicut populus; et hoc habetur in quadam Decretali de Celebratione Missarum[^10]: *Ex parte vestra*; et infra: «Semper sacerdos vino perfundere debet, postquam accepit totum eucharistiae Sacramentum, nisi cum eodem die aliam Missam celebrare debuerit, ne, si forte vinum perfusionis acciperet, aliam celebrationem impediret». — Sed in alio differt, quia alicui[^11] bis communicari non licet, sed licitum est sacerdoti, propter hoc quod est persona *communis* et debet celebrare Missam omni populo. Et quia potest esse, quod unus sacerdos propter tenuitatem et vicinitatem deservit duabus ecclesiis, vel quia in eadem ecclesia propter solemnitatem officii oportet plures Missas cantari, sicut in Nativitate Domini; et quandocumque celebrant, tenentur communicare: ideo indultum est eis, plures Missas celebrare in eodem die et pluries hoc Sacramentum sumere[^12].

### Dub. IV

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit Augustinus, quod *quotidie peccamus peccatis, sine quibus haec mortalis infirmitas duci non potest.* Videtur enim male dicere: quia «nullus peccat in eo quod vitare non potest[^13]»: ergo talia non videntur esse peccata.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod ad hoc potest dupliciter responderi: uno modo, quod quamvis non possint vitari *generaliter* et universaliter, possunt tamen vitari *particulariter* hoc et illud; et ponitur exemplum in navi, in quam ingreditur aqua[^14]. — Sed quomodo possunt vitari motus, qui veniunt ex surreptione, etiam in *particulari*, difficile est dicere.

<!-- page 300 -->

— Et ideo alius modus est dicendi, quod est distinguendum in *necessitate* et in *peccato.* Est enim necessitas ex *naturae institutione*, et est necessitas ex *corruptione.* Prima est *data*; secunda est, quam incurrit homo *ex culpa.* Prima necessitas omnino excludit culpam; secunda vero excludit culpam *actualem mortalem*, non tamen *venialem.* Unde, sicut vult Augustinus de Perfectione iustitiae hominis[^15], «quia homo noluit vitare peccatum, cum potuit; inflictum est ei *non posse*, cum velit».

## English

### DOUBTS CONCERNING THE TEXT OF THE MASTER.

### Doubt I

In this section there are doubts concerning the text, and first about this, that he says that *in this Sacrament there is made a memorial of that which was done once.* For if this is true, then it ought to be done only once by one priest and eaten once. — Likewise, baptism, because it signifies the Passion of Christ, is not repeated[^1]: therefore neither [should] that Sacrament [be repeated].

**I respond:** As the Master says in the text, here[^2] the Passion of Christ is signified as in a *sign* and as in a *commemorative* sign; and since we ought frequently to commemorate Christ once suffered: therefore that Sacrament is repeated many times. — But *if you then object* concerning the Sacrament of baptism; it must be said that it is not similar: because baptism signifies the Passion inasmuch as through it we die to the original sin which we drew from Adam: and the Passion does not effect this in anyone except once; hence it is that baptism cannot be repeated, and if anyone repeats it, he does an outrage to the Sacrament.

In this [Sacrament], however, *Christ* is signified as suffering so as to refresh us; and since he often refreshes in every[^3] commemoration, therefore that Sacrament is often repeated without outrage; nor is the Sacrament properly said to be *repeated*, but *frequented*; since one and the same thing is contained in yesterday's host and today's; but it would then be *repeated*, if over the same host a repetition of the form were made[^4]; and this could not be done knowingly without sin.

### Doubt II

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says that *we daily offer in remembrance of the death.* For there is the instance of Good Friday, when the priest neither confects nor offers the sacrifice[^5]; and it seems that he ought rather [to do so] then. For if this is a *memorial of the Passion*: then since on that day the *memory* is held especially, it seems that it ought rather to be confected then. — Likewise, alongside this it is asked: why is the Sacrament reserved under the form of bread, not of wine?

And it is answered to the first, that to *confect* or consecrate is one thing, to *offer* another; and although on that day no *confection* takes place, yet an *oblation* takes place, because the priest offers on the altar the body consecrated on the preceding day. — But if you should ask: why does an oblation take place and not a *consecration*? it is answered that the *oblation* or reception is made by the priest, lest the Lord's prayer, which is said daily and on that day by the Church, in which is asked: *Our daily bread*[^6] etc., be made void. — But a *consecration* does not take place, but a reservation, on account of this, that on that day the Lord suffered in his own form, not in a sign. And because the whole day is a memorial of the Passion and the whole office; therefore no consecration takes place from the Supper until Saturday, on which is the memory of the resurrection; whence the Mass of the resurrection is sung.

As to what is asked concerning the blood: why is it not reserved? some say that it is not necessary, because the wine which the priest puts in the chalice is consecrated by the placing-with of the body. — But that cannot have truth, as can easily be proven, because in no way is there transubstantiation without the word[^7]. And therefore others say that for a threefold cause it is not reserved: one is on account of the danger of spilling; another on account of the word of the Lord, which he said over the chalice[^8]: *I will not drink of this fruit* etc., and therefore the priest does not receive the blood from the Supper until the resurrection; the third is, because the priest receives rather for refreshment than for signification, and because the whole Christ is perfectly under the species of bread, although not perfectly signified; therefore he receives only in the species of bread, and only the sacrifice or Sacrament of the body is reserved[^9].

### Doubt III

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says that *Christ was immolated once.* For if Christ was immolated once, it seems that at least in one day it is not lawful for a priest to confect many times. — It is asked, then, how it is lawful for a priest to confect and receive many times in the same day, when it is not lawful for the people except once; and it seems that it should rather be lawful for the people, since the one of these [acts] is less, namely to receive, than both, namely to confect and to receive.

**I respond:** It must be said that, on account of reverence for the Sacrament, two [things] are instituted concerning the manner of receiving this Sacrament, namely that it be received on a fasting stomach, and that [it be received] only once, so that it may be distinguished from other foods. In the first the priest and the people communicate alike; for it is fitting that the priest be fasting, just as the people; and this is had in a certain Decretal *On the Celebration of Masses*[^10]: *On your part*; and below: «The priest ought always to rinse with wine after he has received the whole Sacrament of the Eucharist, unless he must on the same day celebrate another Mass, lest, if perchance he were to take the wine of the rinsing, he should impede the other celebration». — But in the other [thing] it differs, because it is not lawful for anyone[^11] to communicate twice, but it is lawful for a priest, on account of this, that he is a *public* person and ought to celebrate Mass for all the people. And because it can happen that one priest, on account of slenderness [of means] and nearness, serves two churches, or because in the same church, on account of the solemnity of the office, several Masses must be sung, as on the Nativity of the Lord; and whenever they celebrate, they are bound to communicate: therefore it has been granted to them to celebrate several Masses on the same day and to receive this Sacrament many times[^12].

### Doubt IV

Likewise it is asked about this, that Augustine says that *we daily sin with sins, without which this mortal infirmity cannot be led [through life].* For it seems to be said badly: because «no one sins in that which he cannot avoid[^13]»: therefore such [things] do not seem to be sins.

**I respond:** It must be said that to this it can be answered in two ways: in one way, that although they cannot be avoided *generally* and universally, yet this and that can be avoided *particularly*; and an example is set down in a ship, into which water enters[^14]. — But how the motions which come from stealthy assault can be avoided, even in *particular*, is difficult to say.

— And therefore there is another way of speaking, that one must distinguish in *necessity* and in *sin.* For there is a necessity from the *institution of nature*, and there is a necessity from *corruption.* The first is *given*; the second is that which man incurs *from fault.* The first necessity altogether excludes fault; but the second excludes *actual mortal* fault, not however *venial.* Whence, as Augustine wishes in [the book] *On the Perfection of the Justice of Man*[^15], «because man would not avoid sin when he could; it was inflicted upon him to be *unable* [to avoid it], when he wills [to]».

- **Dub. I** — On *in hoc Sacramento fit memoria illius quod factum est semel:* if a memorial of a once-done thing, why repeat it (cf. baptism, which signifies the once-for-all Passion and is not repeated)? Answer: the Eucharist signifies the Passion as a *commemorative* sign, which we ought to commemorate frequently; it is *frequented*, not properly *repeated* (the same Christ is in yesterday's and today's host); repetition would mean re-saying the form over the same host.
- **Dub. II** — On *quotidie offerimus ad recordationem mortis:* the Good-Friday instance (no confection that day) + why reserve under bread, not wine. Answer: confecting ≠ offering — on Good Friday there is *oblation* of the prior day's consecrated body but no *confection* (lest the Lord's Prayer's "daily bread" be void), no consecration from Supper to Saturday's resurrection memory; the blood is not reserved (danger of spilling; the Lord's *Non bibam de hoc genimine*; the whole Christ is under the bread).
- **Dub. III** — On *semel immolatus est Christus:* if immolated once, may a priest confect several times a day (when the laity may receive only once)? Answer: two institutions for reverence — fasting reception (priest and people alike) and once-only (to distinguish from food); the priest, as a *public* person serving multiple churches / solemnities, is granted by decretal to celebrate several Masses and receive several times daily.
- **Dub. IV** — On Augustine's *quotidie peccamus peccatis, sine quibus haec mortalis infirmitas duci non potest:* are unavoidable things sins (no one sins in what he cannot avoid)? Answer (two ways): (1) what cannot be avoided generally can be avoided particularly (the ship taking on water example), though stealthy motions are hard to avoid even in particular; (2) distinguish *necessity* (from nature's institution — excuses fault; vs from corruption — excludes actual mortal fault, not venial); per Augustine *On the Perfection of the Justice of Man*, the inability is itself the penalty of an earlier unwillingness.
