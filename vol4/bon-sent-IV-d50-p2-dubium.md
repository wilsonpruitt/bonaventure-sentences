## Latin

### Dubium circa litteram Magistri.

In parte ista circa litteram quaeritur[^p1053-9], qualiter intelligitur illud verbum ultimum *de pedibus sedentis:* quis sit *sedens*, et quid *solium*, et quae *via*.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *sedens* iste Deus est sive Christus, qui requiescit in summa pace, *quae exsuperat omnem sensum*[^p1053-10]. — *Solium* autem dici potest *sancta Scriptura* propter sui eminentiam; vel etiam *anima sancta*, quae dicitur sedes sapientiae[^p1053-11]; aut certe *Virgo Maria*, in quam descendit ad habitandum Dominus; vel etiam *natura assumta*, in qua *posuit tabernaculum suum*[^p1054-1].

<!-- page 1054 -->

— *Facies* autem huius est maiestas Divinitatis, quam videbimus, cum erimus gloriosi. — *Pedes* vero sive *via* sunt opera finalia, quae sunt velata, sicut illa quae pertinent ad finem mundi. — *Media* vero inter Dei magnitudinem aeternam et nostram magnificationem[^p1054-2] futuram sunt opus creationis et redemptionis.

Incipit igitur Magister a *facie Maiestatis* in primo, et decurrit per *media* in secundo et tertio, et terminavit in *pedibus* sive in imis in quarto[^p1054-3].

Isti autem *pedes* nunc sunt velati, sed tunc discooperientur, quando facies manifestabitur. Hoc autem erit, quando «Deus ita erit conspicuus, ut videatur a singulis nobis in singulis nobis, videatur ab altero in altero, videatur in semetipso, videatur in creaturis omnibus, videatur etiam per corpora in omni corpore, quocumque fuerint spiritualis corporis oculi, acie perveniente, directi[^p1054-4]». «Tunc videbimus, amabimus, laudabimus»; tunc apparebit Christus, *vita nostra*, et nos apparebimus cum ipso in gloria[^p1054-5]; ad quam nos perducat Pontifex et praecursor noster Iesus Christus, Filius Dei et beatae Mariae, dominae et adiutricis nostrae, sua pia misericordia; cui est *benedictio et claritas et sapientia et gratiarum actio, honor et virtus et fortitudo per infinita saecula saeculorum. Amen*[^p1054-6].

## English

### A doubt concerning the text of the Master.

In this part, concerning the text, it is asked[^p1053-9] how that last word is to be understood, *concerning the feet of the one seated:* who the *one seated* is, and what the *throne* is, and what the *way* is.

**I respond:** It must be said that this *one seated* is God, or Christ, who rests in the highest peace, *which surpasses all understanding*[^p1053-10]. — The *throne*, moreover, can be said to be *holy Scripture* on account of its eminence; or also the *holy soul*, which is called the seat of wisdom[^p1053-11]; or indeed the *Virgin Mary*, into whom the Lord descended to dwell; or also the *assumed nature*, in which *he placed his tabernacle*[^p1054-1].

— The *face* of this, moreover, is the majesty of the Godhead, which we shall see when we are glorified. — The *feet*, however, or the *way*, are the final works, which are veiled, such as those which pertain to the end of the world. — The *middle things*, however, between God's eternal greatness and our future magnification[^p1054-2] are the work of creation and of redemption.

The Master therefore begins from the *face of the Majesty* in the first [book], and runs through the *middle things* in the second and the third, and has ended in the *feet*, or in the lowest things, in the fourth[^p1054-3].

These *feet*, however, are now veiled, but then they will be uncovered, when the face shall be manifested. And this will be when "God will be so conspicuous that he may be seen by each of us in each of us, may be seen by one in another, may be seen in himself, may be seen in all creatures, may be seen also through bodies in every body, wherever the eyes of the spiritual body shall have been directed, with their gaze reaching there[^p1054-4]." "Then we shall see, we shall love, we shall praise"; then Christ, *our life*, will appear, and we shall appear with him in glory[^p1054-5]; to which may he lead us, our Pontiff and forerunner Jesus Christ, the Son of God and of blessed Mary, our Lady and helper, by his loving mercy; to whom is *blessing and brightness and wisdom and thanksgiving, honour and power and might through infinite ages of ages. Amen*[^p1054-6].

**HERE ENDS THE FOURTH AND LAST BOOK OF BROTHER BONAVENTURE ON THE SENTENCES.**

## Apparatus

[^p1053-9]: **La.** In cod. B additur: De hoc quod dicitur in primo capitulo, *quod qui in hac vita non fecerit, habebit poenitentiam in futuro saeculo de malis suis, sed indulgentiam non inveniet.* Contra: Matth. 18, 21: *Domine, quoties peccabit in me frater meus, et dimittam ei?* etc.; ergo cum ipse sit misericordior et praeceperit remissionem ad infinitas culpas, multo fortius ipse remittet, si ab eo petatur venia. — Item, Bernardus, in libro de Gradibus humilitatis, loquens de damnatione diaboli [c. 10. n. 33.]: Sic Deus temparavit sententiam in vindicta, ut si vellet resipiscere, Deus veniam non neget. — Dicendum, quod agere poenitentiam sive dolere de culpa commisa est dupliciter: aut propter laesionem naturae et odium poenae, aut propter offensam Maiestatis divinae et ipsius iustitiae. Primam poenitentiam habere potest aliquis ex solis naturalibus sine gratia; sicut enim homo naturaliter se diligit, ita etiam odit omne illud, propter quod incurrit malum. Et haec poenitentia est in malis, et per istam nunquam aliquis meretur veniam; sed secunda non est in eis, nec Deus eam dat alicui finaliter, nisi ei, cum quo disposuit misericordiam se facturum. Et de ista procedit ratio et loquitur Bernardus, et nullus est, qui istam habeat, qui non consequatur a Domino veniam [cfr. supra p. I. a. I. q. 1. ad 4.]. — Secundo quaeritur. Idem cod. in fine addit plura nullius momenti.
     **En.** In codex B there is added: Concerning that which is said in the first chapter, *that whoever shall not have done [it] in this life will have repentance in the age to come for his evils, but will not find pardon.* On the contrary: Matthew 18, 21: *Lord, how often shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him?* etc.; therefore, since he himself is more merciful and has commanded remission for infinite faults, much more strongly will he himself remit, if pardon be sought from him. — Likewise, Bernard, in the book *On the Degrees of Humility*, speaking of the damnation of the devil [c. 10, n. 33]: Thus God tempered the sentence in his vengeance, so that if he were willing to repent, God would not deny pardon. — It must be said that to do penance, or to grieve over a fault committed, is twofold: either on account of the injury to nature and hatred of the punishment, or on account of the offence against the divine Majesty and its justice. The first repentance someone can have from his natural powers alone, without grace; for just as a man naturally loves himself, so also he hates everything on account of which he incurs evil. And this repentance is in the wicked, and through it no one ever merits pardon; but the second is not in them, nor does God give it to anyone finally, except to him with whom he has determined to show mercy. And it is of this that the argument proceeds and of which Bernard speaks; and there is no one who has this repentance who does not obtain pardon from the Lord [cf. above, p. I, a. I, q. 1, ad 4]. — *Secondly it is asked.* The same codex adds at the end several things of no importance.
