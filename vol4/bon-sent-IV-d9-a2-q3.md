# IV Sent., d. 9, a. 2, q. 3
## *Utrum peccator sumendo corpus Christi semper mortaliter peccet*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 210 -->

### Quaestio III. *Utrum peccator sumendo corpus Christi semper mortaliter peccet.*

Tertio quaeritur, utrum peccator sumendo corpus Christi semper mortaliter peccet. Et quod sic, videtur:

1. Quia inter dignum et indignum nihil est medium; sed nullus peccator accedit digne: ergo omnis peccator accedit indigne; et omnis talis peccat[^1]: ergo peccator semper peccat sumendo.

2. Item, omnis qui accedit, tenetur se ipsum praeparare, quia dicit Apostolus primae ad Corinthios undecimo[^2]: *Probet autem se ipsum homo*; sed si praeparat se, facit quod in se est; et si hoc, tunc Deus facit quod in se est, et tunc non est peccator: ergo si est peccator et accedit, non se praeparavit, ergo omnis talis peccat.

3. Item, omnis accipiens Sacramentum et non rem ficte accedit[^3]: sed omnis peccator manens in peccato mortali, qui accipit Sacramentum, accipit illud sine re: ergo ficte accedit. Sed qui ficte accedit peccat mortaliter: ergo etc.

4. Item, sicut dicitur in Glossa, primae ad Corinthios undecimo[^4]: « Indigne manducat qui accedit infidelis vel indevotus »; sed omnis peccator est indevotus, quia caritas facit devotum, qua caret peccator: ergo omnis peccator indigne accedit.

5. Item, si peccator, qui habet peccati mortalis conscientiam, peccat mortaliter accedendo; aut ergo quia habet peccatum, aut quia conscientiam. Non quia conscientiam, quia hoc non est malum cognoscere suum peccatum: ergo quia habet peccatum; et sic semper mortaliter peccat.

6. Item, qui peccat et ignorat minus est dispositus ad medicinam — unde Ambrosius[^5] dicit, quod « peccat gravissime qui ignorat » — si ergo peccat qui accedit cum peccato ignoranter, multo fortius qui accedit cum peccato scienter.

7. Item, iustus credens, se esse in peccato mortali, peccat, si accedat; sed iustus est magis dispositus quam quilibet peccator: ergo multo magis peccat peccator.

**Contra:**
1. Si omnis peccator damnabiliter manducat, cum nullus sciat, se esse iustum[^6], omnis homo exponit se discrimini sumendo.

2. Item, Apostolus[^7]: *Nihil mihi conscius sum, sed non in hoc iustificatus sum*; sed non tenetur homo amplius se disponere, quam quod non habeat remorsum conscientiae: ergo si per hoc non est iustificatus, et qui accedit non iustificatus peccat; tunc quilibet sumendo peccat.

> **Conclusio.** Qui minus sufficienter, scilicet tantum secundum probabilitatem, se praeparat ad sumendam eucharistiam non mortaliter peccat.

**Respondeo:** Ad praedictorum intelligentiam dicendum, quod in hoc Sacramento tenetur percipiens

<!-- page 211 -->

se praeparare, quia Deus venit ad habitandum in homine. Praeparatio autem non semper est sufficiens; sed haec est sufficiens, quando inimicus Domini expellitur de hospitio per veram examinationem et contritionem sive iudicium poenitentiae. *Rursus*, quia divina Maiestas summa se dignatione humiliat, et divina Bonitas offert gratiam; debet homo cum metu reverentiae et cum dilectione accedere, ad hoc quod digne manducet. *Digne* igitur *manducare* est sufficienter se praeparare, scilicet peccati omnis expulsione, reverentia et dilectione.

Quidam igitur sunt, qui se *sufficienter* praeparant secundum *veritatem*; quidam autem *non sufficienter* secundum *veritatem*, sufficienter tamen secundum *probabilitatem*; quidam autem *neutro modo* se praeparant, immo contemnunt. Qui ergo *sufficienter* se praeparat[^8] manducat digne; qui se *nullo modo* parat manducat indigne; qui autem *minus sufficienter* se parat, scilicet secundum *probabilitatem* potius quam secundum *veritatem*, non manducat *digne*, quia non se praeparavit *sufficienter*; nec *indigne*, quia se praeparat *probabiliter*; talis, quamvis non recipiat gratiam, non tamen incurrit offensam.

1. Et sic patet illud quod obiicit de digne et indigne sumente: quia est ibi *medium*, quamvis non sit medium inter dignum et indignum.

2. 3. Ad illud quod obiicitur de praeparatione, dicendum, quod nullus tenetur ad hoc sufficienter praeparare se, quia nullus potest scire, utrum hoc faciat; sed quod se praeparet secundum sufficiens dictamen conscientiae; et in hoc contentus debet esse probabilitate.

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur de indevoto, dicendum, quod *indevotus* ibi dicitur *contrarie*, qui accedit cum peccandi proposito, sicut ibi patet in Glossa[^9].

5. 6. Ad illud quod obiicitur de eo qui habet conscientiam peccati; dicendum, quod non dicitur quis peccare, quia recognoscit peccatum suum, sed quia cognoscit peccatum et de illo non poenitet et cum illo accedit; hoc autem non potest esse sine contemptu; secus est, quando ignorat. — Similiter patet sequens, quod ignorans minus est dispositus, *ceteris paribus*; sed ignorans *proponens* poenitere et volens sciente[^10], qui vult in culpa persistere, non minus, sed magis est dispositus; unde ignorantia peccati non excusat ratione *ignorantiae*, sed ratione *probabilitatis*, quod sit in gratia.

7. Ad ultimum dicendum, quod iustus ex tali conscientia reddit se magis inhabilem, non propter *culpam*, sed propter *conscientiam erroneam*, quae quidem, quamdiu persistit, obligat, ut non recipiat, propter hoc quod non potest cum hac conscientia recipere, quin contemnat[^11].

## English

### Question III. *Whether a sinner always sins mortally by receiving the body of Christ.*

Thirdly it is asked whether a sinner always sins mortally by receiving the body of Christ. And it seems that he does:

1. Because between the worthy and the unworthy there is no middle ground; but no sinner approaches worthily: therefore every sinner approaches unworthily; and every such one sins[^1]: therefore a sinner always sins in receiving.

2. Likewise, everyone who approaches is bound to prepare himself, because the Apostle says in First Corinthians, chapter eleven[^2]: *But let a man prove himself*; but if he prepares himself, he does what is in him; and if so, then God does what is in Him, and then he is not a sinner: therefore if he is a sinner and approaches, he has not prepared himself, and so every such one sins.

3. Likewise, everyone who receives the Sacrament and not the reality approaches feignedly[^3]: but every sinner remaining in mortal sin, who receives the Sacrament, receives it without the reality: therefore he approaches feignedly. But whoever approaches feignedly sins mortally: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, as is said in the Gloss on First Corinthians, chapter eleven[^4]: « He eats unworthily who approaches unbelieving or undevout »; but every sinner is undevout, because charity makes one devout, which the sinner lacks: therefore every sinner approaches unworthily.

5. Likewise, if the sinner who has awareness of mortal sin sins mortally by approaching, then it is either because he has the sin, or because he has the awareness. Not because of the awareness, because it is not evil to know one's own sin: therefore because he has the sin; and thus he always sins mortally.

6. Likewise, he who sins and is ignorant is less disposed for the medicine — whence Ambrose[^5] says that « he sins most gravely who is ignorant » — if therefore he sins who approaches with sin ignorantly, much more strongly does he who approaches with sin knowingly.

7. Likewise, a just man believing himself to be in mortal sin sins if he approaches; but the just man is more disposed than any sinner whatever: therefore much more does the sinner sin.

**On the contrary:**
1. If every sinner eats damnably, since no one knows that he is just[^6], every man exposes himself to peril by receiving.

2. Likewise, the Apostle[^7]: *I am conscious of nothing against myself, but I am not hereby justified*; but a man is not bound to dispose himself further than that he have no remorse of conscience: therefore if by this he is not justified, then he too who approaches unjustified sins; and so anyone whatever sins by receiving.

> **Conclusion.** He who prepares himself less than sufficiently — namely, only according to probability — for receiving the Eucharist does not sin mortally.

**I respond:** For the understanding of the foregoing it must be said that in this Sacrament the recipient is bound

to prepare himself, because God comes to dwell in man. But the preparation is not always sufficient; rather, this preparation is sufficient when the enemy of the Lord is expelled from the dwelling through true examination and contrition, that is, the judgment of penance. *Again*, because the divine Majesty humbles itself with the highest condescension, and the divine Goodness offers grace, a man ought to approach with fear of reverence and with love, so that he may eat worthily. To eat *worthily*, therefore, is to prepare oneself sufficiently, namely by the expulsion of all sin, by reverence, and by love.

There are therefore some who prepare themselves *sufficiently* according to *truth*; some, however, *not sufficiently* according to *truth*, yet sufficiently according to *probability*; and some who prepare themselves in *neither way*, but rather hold it in contempt. He, therefore, who prepares himself *sufficiently*[^8] eats worthily; he who prepares himself in *no way* eats unworthily; but he who prepares himself *less than sufficiently*, namely according to *probability* rather than according to *truth*, does not eat *worthily*, because he has not prepared himself *sufficiently*; nor *unworthily*, because he prepares himself *probably*; and such a one, although he does not receive grace, nevertheless does not incur offense.

1. And thus is made clear that which is objected concerning him who eats worthily and unworthily: because there is a *middle* there, although there is no middle between the worthy and the unworthy.

2, 3. To that which is objected concerning preparation, it must be said that no one is bound to prepare himself sufficiently for this, because no one can know whether he does so; but rather to prepare himself according to the sufficient dictate of conscience; and in this he ought to be content with probability.

4. To that which is objected concerning the undevout, it must be said that the *undevout* is there spoken of in the *contrary* sense, that is, of one who approaches with the intention of sinning, as is clear there in the Gloss[^9].

5, 6. To that which is objected concerning the one who has awareness of sin, it must be said that one is not said to sin because he recognizes his own sin, but because he knows the sin and does not repent of it and approaches with it; but this cannot be without contempt; it is otherwise when he is ignorant. — Likewise what follows is clear, that the ignorant man is less disposed, *all else being equal*; but the ignorant man *intending* to repent and willing knowingly[^10], who wishes to persist in fault, is not less but more disposed; whence ignorance of sin does not excuse by reason of *ignorance*, but by reason of *probability* that one is in grace.

7. To the last it must be said that the just man, from such awareness, renders himself the more unfit, not on account of *fault*, but on account of *erroneous conscience*, which indeed, as long as it persists, binds him not to receive, for this reason: that he cannot receive with this conscience without contempt[^11].
