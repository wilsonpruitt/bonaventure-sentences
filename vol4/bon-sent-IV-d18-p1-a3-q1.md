# d. 18, p. 1, a. 3, q. 1 — Utrum scientia sit clavis

## *Utrum scientia sit clavis*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 478 -->

### Articulus III. *De clave scientiae specialiter.*

Consequenter quantum ad tertium articulum quaeritur specialiter de clave, quae dicitur scientia. Et circa hoc duo quaeruntur. Primo quaeritur, utrum scientia sit clavis. Secundo quaeritur, utrum sit alia clavis quam potentia.

### Quaestio I. *Utrum scientia sit clavis.*

Circa primum ostenditur, quod scientia sit clavis. Primo sic.

1. Super illud Matthaei decimo sexto[^1]: *Tibi dabo claves*; Glossa[^2]: « Clavis dicitur scientia et potentia, qua debet dignos recipere et indignos repellere »: ergo scientia est clavis.

2. Item, in quodam sermone de Apostolis Petro et Paulo[^3] dicitur: « Petrus accepit clavem potentiae, Paulus clavem scientiae »: ergo scientia est clavis.

3. Item, ratione videtur: quia omne illud quod directe repugnat errori, scientia est, vel de genere scientiae; sed clavis aliqua est huiusmodi: ergo etc.

*Probatio minoris.* Hieronymus super illud[^4]: *Tibi dabo claves* etc.: « Sciat prudens lector, hoc esse de futuro promissum, quia, si statim datae essent, nunquam in eo error pravae confessionis locum habuisset »; ergo aliqua clavium excludit errorem.

4. Item, Augustinus dicit, et habetur in littera, distinctione praecedenti[^5]: « Qui vult peccata confiteri, ut inveniat gratiam, quaerat sacerdotem, qui sciat ligare et solvere, ne, cum negligens circa se exstiterit, negligatur »: ergo necessarium est absolventi habere scientiam: ergo si illud quod tenet rationem ad aperiendum, est clavis; videtur, quod scientia clavis sit.

**Sed contra:**
1. Super illud[^6]: *Tibi dabo claves* etc.; Glossa: « Praesentis iudicii potestatem »: ergo videtur, quod utraque clavis est potestas, non ergo scientia.

2. Item, claves dantur in ordine et consequuntur praecise ordinem[^7]; sed multi habent ordinem,
<!-- page 479 -->
qui non habent scientiam, et e converso: ergo scientia non est clavis.

3. Item, clavis semel habita nunquam amittitur, sicut nec ordo; sed scientia habita potest amitti: ergo scientia non est clavis.

4. Item, si scientia est clavis, aut scientia *innata*, aut *acquisita*, aut *infusa*. Si *innata*; ergo claves sunt a natura. Si scientia *acquisita*; ergo claves sunt per acquisitionem, non per gratiam. Si *infusa*; sed multi sunt, qui nihil noverunt per infusionem propter suam nequitiam[^8]: ergo mali non habent claves. Si tu dicas, quod infunditur scientia, quae est gratis data, sicut Salomoni; contrarium videmus aperte, quia omnes fere ita sunt simplices et idiotae post susceptionem sacerdotii, ut ante.

> **Conclusio.**
> Scientia non est clavis, secundum quod dicit habitum, sed secundum quod dicit auctoritatem sive officium discernendi.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod circa hoc duplex fuit opinio antiquorum.

Quidam enim dixerunt, quod scientia est clavis, prout scientia nominat habitum sciendi discernere. Sed non est clavis *principalis* nec *per se*, sed prout iuncta est *auctoritati* solvendi et ligandi; et haec clavis non est de *esse* ordinis, sed de *bene esse*, ita quod, si aliquis recipit *ordinem* de facto, non habens scientiam, factum est; sed *exsecutionem* iurisdictionis in absolvendo non recipit sine periculo animae, eo quod altera clavium caret. Et hi dixerunt, quod quidam habeant scientiam *clavium*, ut illi qui habent scientiam acquisitam; quidam *claviculam*, ut illi qui carent acquisita, tamen per naturalem industriam noscunt quod ars docet et Scriptura; quidam vero *nullam*, ut qui carent utraque. Et videtur fuisse opinio Magistri[^9] — Sed non videtur hoc bene posse sustineri — licet aliquam habeat probabilitatem — cum dicamus, has claves ordinem consequi et ita dari sicut ordinem, et ita durare, sicut ordo durat.

Et ideo est secunda opinio, quod scientia non est clavis, secundum quod dicit habitum; sed in quantum dicit auctoritatem sive officium discernendi; hoc autem officium sive haec auctoritas est consequens ordinem inseparabiliter; et haec clavis est essentialiter. — Et haec fuit Praepositivi, et hanc tenent communiter doctores, hoc addentes, quod scientia-habitus est sicut dispositio faciens ad *bene esse* vel etiam sicut dispositio *materialis*, sine qua altera clavis exire non potest in actum. Sicut enim vis visiva non est humidum crystallinum, tamen illud exigit ad suam operationem; sic ipsa *auctoritas* discernendi requirit habitum scientiae; et quanto melius habetur scientia, tanto meliori modo habetur clavis. Et ideo maluerunt Sancti hanc clavem vocare hoc nomine quam alio. — Ex his patent obiecta ad utramque partem.

1. 2. Nam primae auctoritates, quae dicunt scientiam clavem, intelliguntur de scientia, prout dicit *auctoritatem discernendi*.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur de Hieronymo, dicendum, quod non intelligit tantum quantum ad esse nec etiam solum quantum ad bene esse, sed etiam ulterius quantum ad *perfectum esse*; sicut fuerunt in Apostolis, qui post adventum Spiritus sancti non fuerunt decepti nec a veritate abducti.

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur de Augustino, quod solus sciens potest utiliter ligare; dicendum, quod hoc non est, quia habitus scientiae sit clavis, sed quia dispositio est ad clavem, sicut dictum est.

Quae autem ad oppositum inducuntur, concedenda sunt, quoniam verum concludunt, scilicet scientiam, ut dicit *habitum*, non esse clavem.

### Scholion
**I.** Antiqui doctores communiter distinguunt *duas* claves, quae secundum S. Thomam (hic q. 1. a. 1. quaestiunc. 2. ad 1.) non distinguuntur in *auctoritatis essentia*, sed ex comparatione ad diversos actus. Sed Scot. (hic d. 18. q. unica n. 10.) tenet, esse « simpliciter *duas*, ita quod simpliciter verum est, illud verbum Christi: *Tibi dabo claves* etc, et possent de potentia Dei absoluta separari ab invicem »; « una tamen sunt unitate *ordinis* ». Idem etiam in dubio ponit doctrinam Angelici, quod istae claves sint idem cum *charactere* sacerdotali, qui, cum sit unus in se, se extendat ad diversos actus; de quo agitur infra d. 19. a. 2.

**II.** Quoad 1. quaestionem opinio Magistri, quae primo loco ponitur, communiter a posterioribus Scholasticis improbatur. — Circa 2. quaestionem dicit S. Doctor: « Sunt duae claves »;
<!-- page 480 -->
sed addit: « Tamen, quia ex his constat una plena auctoritas, ideo in uno ordine dantur et unum respiciunt »; quae verba nec S. Thomae nec Scoto contradicunt.

De utraque quaestione: Alex. Hal., S. p. IV. d. 20. m. 3. 4. — Scot., loc. cit. n. 7. seqq. — S. Thom., loc. cit. quaestiunc. 2. 3; Suppl. q. 17. a. 3. — B. Albert., hic a. 3. i. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 1. a. 3. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 1. q. 3. — Durand., hic q. 1. — Dionys. Carth., hic q. 1.

## English

### Article III. *On the key of knowledge specifically.*

Next, with respect to the third article, inquiry is made specifically concerning the key which is called knowledge. And concerning this two things are asked. First it is asked whether knowledge is a key. Second it is asked whether it is a key other than power.

### Question I. *Whether knowledge is a key.*

Concerning the first point it is shown that knowledge is a key. First thus.

1. On that text of Matthew, chapter sixteen[^1]: *To thee I will give the keys*; the Gloss[^2]: « The key is called knowledge and power, by which one ought to receive the worthy and repel the unworthy »: therefore knowledge is a key.

2. Likewise, in a certain sermon on the Apostles Peter and Paul[^3] it is said: « Peter received the key of power, Paul the key of knowledge »: therefore knowledge is a key.

3. Likewise, by reason it seems so: for everything that directly opposes error is knowledge, or of the genus of knowledge; but some key is of this kind: therefore etc.

*Proof of the minor.* Jerome on that text[^4]: *To thee I will give the keys* etc.: « Let the prudent reader know that this was promised concerning the future, because, if they had been given at once, no error of perverse confession would ever have had place in him »; therefore some one of the keys excludes error.

4. Likewise, Augustine says, and it is held in the text, in the preceding distinction[^5]: « Let him who wishes to confess his sins, that he may find grace, seek a priest who knows how to bind and to loose, lest, when he has been negligent about himself, he be neglected »: therefore it is necessary for the one absolving to have knowledge: therefore if that which holds the account for opening is a key, it seems that knowledge is a key.

**On the contrary:**
1. On that text[^6]: *To thee I will give the keys* etc.; the Gloss: « The power of present judgment »: therefore it seems that each key is a power, therefore not knowledge.

2. Likewise, the keys are given in ordination and follow precisely upon ordination[^7]; but many have ordination
<!-- page 479 -->
who do not have knowledge, and conversely: therefore knowledge is not a key.

3. Likewise, a key once had is never lost, just as neither is ordination; but knowledge once had can be lost: therefore knowledge is not a key.

4. Likewise, if knowledge is a key, either knowledge is *innate*, or *acquired*, or *infused*. If *innate*; then the keys are from nature. If knowledge is *acquired*; then the keys are by acquisition, not by grace. If *infused*; but there are many who have come to know nothing by infusion on account of their wickedness[^8]: therefore the wicked do not have the keys. If you say that knowledge is infused which is freely given, as to Solomon; we see the contrary plainly, because almost all are as simple and ignorant after receiving the priesthood as before.

> **Conclusion.**
> Knowledge is not a key insofar as it denotes a habit, but insofar as it denotes the authority or office of discerning.

**I respond:** It must be said that concerning this there was a twofold opinion of the ancients.

For some said that knowledge is a key, insofar as knowledge names the habit of knowing how to discern. But it is not a key *principally* nor *of itself*, but insofar as it is joined to the *authority* of loosing and binding; and this key is not of the *being* of ordination, but of its *well-being*, so that, if someone in fact receives *ordination* without having knowledge, it has been done; but he does not receive the *exercise* of jurisdiction in absolving without peril to the soul, because he lacks one of the keys. And these said that some have the knowledge of *keys*, as those who have acquired knowledge; some a *little key*, as those who lack acquired knowledge yet by natural industry know what art and Scripture teach; some none at all, as those who lack both. And this seems to have been the opinion of the Master[^9] — But this does not seem able to be well sustained — although it has some probability — since we say that these keys follow ordination and are thus given just as ordination is, and so endure as ordination endures.

And therefore there is a second opinion, that knowledge is not a key insofar as it denotes a habit; but insofar as it denotes the authority or office of discerning; and this office or this authority follows ordination inseparably; and this is a key essentially. — And this was the opinion of Praepositivus, and the doctors commonly hold it, adding this, that knowledge-as-habit is like a disposition making for *well-being*, or even like a *material* disposition, without which the other key cannot pass into act. For just as the power of sight is not the crystalline humor, yet requires it for its operation; so the very *authority* of discerning requires the habit of knowledge; and the better knowledge is had, the better the manner in which the key is had. And therefore the Saints preferred to call this key by this name rather than by another. — From these things the objections to each side are evident.

1. 2. For the first authorities, which call knowledge a key, are understood of knowledge insofar as it denotes the *authority of discerning*.

3. To that which is objected from Jerome, it must be said that he does not understand it only with respect to being, nor even only with respect to well-being, but further also with respect to *perfect being*; as it was in the Apostles, who after the coming of the Holy Spirit were not deceived nor led away from the truth.

4. To that which is objected from Augustine, that only one who knows can usefully bind; it must be said that this is not because the habit of knowledge is a key, but because it is a disposition toward the key, as has been said.

But the things adduced for the opposite are to be conceded, since they conclude what is true, namely that knowledge, insofar as it denotes a *habit*, is not a key.

### Scholion
**I.** The ancient doctors commonly distinguish *two* keys, which according to St. Thomas (here q. 1, a. 1, quaestiuncula 2, ad 1) are not distinguished in the *essence of authority*, but by comparison to diverse acts. But Scotus (here d. 18, q. unica, n. 10) holds that they are « simply *two*, so that that word of Christ is simply true: *To thee I will give the keys* etc., and they could by God's absolute power be separated from one another »; « yet they are one with a unity of *ordination* ». He also in a doubt sets down the teaching of the Angelic Doctor, that these keys are the same as the priestly *character*, which, since it is one in itself, extends to diverse acts; concerning which it is treated below in d. 19, a. 2.

**II.** As to the first question, the opinion of the Master, which is set down in the first place, is commonly disapproved by the later Scholastics. — Concerning the second question the Holy Doctor says: « There are two keys »;
<!-- page 480 -->
but he adds: « Yet, because from these there results one full authority, therefore they are given in one ordination and regard one thing »; which words contradict neither St. Thomas nor Scotus.

On both questions: Alexander of Hales, S. p. IV, d. 20, m. 3, 4. — Scotus, loc. cit., n. 7 ff. — St. Thomas, loc. cit., quaestiuncula 2, 3; Suppl. q. 17, a. 3. — Blessed Albert, here a. 3, i. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 1, a. 3. — Richard of Mediavilla, here a. 1, q. 3. — Durandus, here q. 1. — Dionysius the Carthusian, here q. 1.
