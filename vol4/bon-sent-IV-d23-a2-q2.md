# d.23 a2-q2

## *Cui debeat dari hoc Sacramentum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 598 -->

### Quaestio II. *Cui debeat dari hoc Sacramentum.*

Secundo quaeritur, cui debeat Sacramentum istud dari. Et quod solis infirmis, videtur:

1. Per illud quod dicitur Iacobi ultimo[^1]: *Infirmatur quis in vobis? inducat presbyteros:* ergo illis indicitur, quod presbyteros inducant; et hoc videtur maxime per litteram sequentem: *Oratio fidei salvabit infirmum* etc.

2. Item, quod solis *ratione utentibus*, ostenditur per textum, cum dicitur[^2]: *Inducat presbyteros;* et post: *Si in peccatis fuerit:* si ergo presbyteros petere et in peccatis esse est proprium adultorum et ratione utentium, videtur etc.

3. Item, quod *solis in periculo mortis existentibus*, videtur per ipsam denominationem, qua dicitur *unctio extrema:* ergo solum in extremis[^3] debet dari.

**Contra:** 1. Quod *non solis infirmis* debeat dari, videtur per ipsam Glossam[^4], quae dicit: «Infirmatur quis in vobis? mente et corpore scilicet, vel fide»: ergo non solum infirmantibus corporaliter, sed etiam infirmantibus spiritualiter debet dari.

2. Item, unctio haec debet dari appropinquantibus ad mortem et existentibus in periculo mortis[^5]; sed qui *ingreditur iustum bellum* in periculo mortis est: ergo etc.

3. Item, quod *non solis ratione utentibus*, videtur: quia non soli tales infirmantur, sed etiam alii, qui indigent alleviatione, ut parvuli et stulti: ergo videtur, quod debeat eis hoc Sacramentum dari, quod est unctio infirmorum.

4. Item, hoc probatur ex textu[^6], in quo dicitur: *Infirmatur quis in vobis? Quis* tenetur *infinite:* ergo quicumque infirmatur, debet inungi, ergo etc.

### Conclusio

> **Conclusio.** *Extrema unctio debet dari solis infirmis ratione utentibus et in periculo mortis existentibus.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod hoc Sacramentum est principaliter ad *venialia delenda*, secundum quod possunt convenientem recipere effectum, et per hoc est in *alleviationem animae* aggravatae a corpore corruptionem habente[^7]: et ideo illud Sacramentum debet dari solis infirmis, qui peccare potuerunt venialiter, et his, prout sunt in articulo mortis; sic enim possunt a culpa veniali liberari convenienter et sufficienter. Et hoc innuitur in ipso textu: *status*, inquam, *infirmitatis*, et hoc est quod dicitur *infirmatur; periculum*, in hoc quod dicitur *quis*, quasi discretive et notabiliter[^8] et graviter, ita quod deficit virtus naturae et auxilium medicinae; *discretio* vero *rationis*, in hoc quod dicitur: *inducat presbyteros*, et iterum in hoc quod subinfertur: *si in peccatis fuerit, remittentur ei.* — Concedendae igitur sunt rationes monstrantes, hoc Sacramentum dari debere solis infirmis ratione utentibus et in periculo mortis existentibus.

1. Ad illud quod obiicitur de Glossa, dicendum, quod illud vel intelligitur subdisiunctive[^9], nec excluditur infirmitas corporis, sed praesupponitur.

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur de his qui intrant iustum bellum, dicendum, quod etsi tales sint in periculo mortis secundum fortunam, non tamen secundum naturam; Sacramenta autem ecclesiastica non fortunam sequuntur, sed naturam respiciunt. Si tu obiicias de illo qui damnatus est per sententiam; dicendum, quod talis infamis est in facie Ecclesiae[^10], etiam si secundum veritatem non habeat culpam. Et sic patet ratio illa.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod alii indigent alleviatione; dicendum, quod ad hoc principaliter Sacramentum istud non ordinatur, sicut patet in ipsa forma[^11], sed ad peccatorum remissionem, quae per sensus exteriores perpetrata sunt; et ideo non datur infantibus nec naturaliter stultis.

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur de hoc nomine *quis*, dicendum, quod magis tenetur *discretive* quam *infinite.* Et si tu obiicias, quod talis modus non competit illi nomini; dicendum, quod falsum est, quia talis modus annexus est modo accipiendi interrogative[^12].

## English

### Question II. *To whom this Sacrament ought to be given.*

Secondly it is asked, to whom this Sacrament ought to be given. And that [it ought to be given] to the sick alone seems [to be the case]:

1. From that which is said in the last [chapter] of James[^1]: *Is any among you sick? let him bring in the priests:* therefore it is enjoined upon them that they bring in priests; and this seems [clear] especially from the following text: *The prayer of faith shall save the sick man,* etc.

2. Likewise, that [it ought to be given] to those alone *who have the use of reason* is shown from the text, when it is said[^2]: *Let him bring in the priests;* and afterward: *If he be in sins:* if therefore to seek priests and to be in sins is proper to adults and to those who have the use of reason, it seems [so], etc.

3. Likewise, that [it ought to be given] *only to those existing in danger of death* is seen from the very name by which it is called *extreme unction:* therefore it ought to be given only in the last extremities[^3].

**On the contrary:** 1. That it ought *not to be given to the sick alone* is seen from the Gloss itself[^4], which says: «Is any among you sick? in mind and body, that is, or in faith»: therefore it ought to be given not only to those ailing bodily, but also to those ailing spiritually.

2. Likewise, this unction ought to be given to those drawing near to death and existing in danger of death[^5]; but he who *enters a just war* is in danger of death: therefore, etc.

3. Likewise, that [it ought] *not [to be given] to those alone who have the use of reason* is seen: because not only such persons are sick, but also others who need relief, such as little children and the mad: therefore it seems that this Sacrament ought to be given to them, which is the unction of the sick.

4. Likewise, this is proved from the text[^6], in which it is said: *Is any among you sick?* *Anyone* [*quis*] is understood *indefinitely:* therefore whoever is sick ought to be anointed, therefore etc.

### Conclusion

> **Conclusion.** *Extreme unction ought to be given only to the sick who have the use of reason and who exist in danger of death.*

**I respond:** It must be said that this Sacrament is principally for *the wiping away of venial [sins]*, according as they can receive its fitting effect, and through this it serves for *the relief of the soul* weighed down by a body subject to corruption[^7]: and therefore that Sacrament ought to be given only to the sick, who have been able to sin venially, and to these insofar as they are at the point of death; for thus can they be freed from venial fault fittingly and sufficiently. And this is intimated in the text itself: *the state*, I say, *of infirmity* — and this is what is meant by *is sick*; *the danger* — in this, that it says *any* [*quis*], as if discriminatingly and notably[^8] and gravely, so that the power of nature and the help of medicine fail; but the *discretion of reason* — in this, that it says: *let him bring in the priests*, and again in what is subjoined: *if he be in sins, they shall be forgiven him.* — Therefore the reasons showing that this Sacrament ought to be given only to the sick who have the use of reason and who exist in danger of death are to be granted.

1. To that which is objected concerning the Gloss, it must be said that it is either understood sub-disjunctively[^9], nor is bodily infirmity excluded, but presupposed.

2. To that which is objected concerning those who enter a just war, it must be said that although such persons are in danger of death according to fortune, yet not according to nature; but the ecclesiastical Sacraments do not follow fortune, but regard nature. If you object concerning him who has been condemned by sentence; it must be said that such a one is infamous in the face of the Church[^10], even if according to the truth he has no fault. And thus that argument is clear.

3. To that which is objected, that others need relief; it must be said that this Sacrament is not principally ordained to this, as is clear from its very form[^11], but to the remission of the sins which have been committed through the exterior senses; and therefore it is not given to infants nor to those naturally mad.

4. To that which is objected concerning this word *any* [*quis*], it must be said that it is taken rather *discriminatingly* than *indefinitely.* And if you object that such a mode does not befit that word; it must be said that this is false, because such a mode is annexed to the interrogative manner of taking it[^12].
