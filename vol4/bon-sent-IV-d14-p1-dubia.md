# IV Sent., d. 14, p. I — Dubia circa litteram Magistri
## *Dubia circa litteram Magistri*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 328 -->

### DUBIA CIRCA LITTERAM MAGISTRI.

### Dub. I

In parte ista sunt dubitationes circa litteram, et primo de hoc quod dicit Hieronymus: *Secunda tabula post naufragium est poenitentia.* Cum enim secundum dicatur respectu primi, et prima tabula sit baptismus, et secunda poenitentia; tunc videtur, quod secundum Sacramentum sit poenitentia: ergo male ordinat Magister quarto loco. — Item, quaero a te: pro quanto dicitur poenitentia *tabula?* et quid est *naufragium? Si tu dicas,* quod est casus in mortale; tunc videtur, quod sit prima tabula, quia, si quis cecidit per mortale, necesse est recurrere ad poenitentiam.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod mare est mundus iste, secundum illud Psalmi[^1]: *Hoc mare magnum* etc. *Navis,* per quam homo transit super undas huius maris, est gratia Spiritus sancti; vel navis est Ecclesia, quae iuncta est glutino caritatis, secundum illud Proverbiorum ultimo: *Facta est quasi navis institoris. Naufragium* vero fuit corruptio et fractio in Adam, in qua fractione omnes filii eius iactati sunt super undas huius maris per concupiscentiam et per poenam. — Ab hoc naufragio primo liberatur homo per baptismum, qui restituit innocentiam et gratiam perditam[^2]; et ideo est *prima tabula.* Et quia frequenter relinquunt plures gratiam baptismalem et illam tabulam, per quam liberantur in undis maris; et per illam amplius non possunt se defendere: ideo indigent *secunda,* et haec est poenitentia. Utraque tamen dicitur tabula, quia sustinet, ne quis periclitetur per culpam; non tamen ita ponit in tuto, sicut erat homo in statu innocentiae.

Quod ergo obiicit, quod est[^3] *secunda tabula:* ergo secundum Sacramentum, non valet; quia non omne Sacramentum est tabula, quia non omne liberat a naufragio. Et est simile: hic est secundus archidiaconus: ergo est secunda persona in hac ecclesia.

<!-- page 329 -->

Quod quaeritur: quid vocatur naufragium? dicunt aliqui, quod quilibet casus in mortale; et quia duplex est differentia mortalis, scilicet originale et actuale; ideo duplex tabula, scilicet baptismus et poenitentia[^4]. — Melius tamen potest dici, quod humana natura perpessa sit naufragium in Adam; nobis autem data est tabula baptismi, quae nos sustentat per innocentiam; et quia hoc Sacramentum iterari non potest[^5], ideo datur aliud Sacramentum, et hoc est poenitentia.

### Dub. II

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit Magister: *Poenitentia dicitur Sacramentum et virtus mentis.* Est enim poenitentia interior et exterior: ergo similiter est virtus et Sacramentum baptismus, quia est baptismus interior et exterior[^6]. — Item, poenitentia interior est res huius Sacramenti[^7]; sed nulla virtus est *res Sacramenti,* quia distinguitur gratia virtutum et gratia Sacramentorum: ergo etc.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod ista non est tota ratio, quod poenitentia *interior* sit virtus, sed quia est poenitentia *interior,* quae non tantum dicit gratiam vel actum gratiae, sed concernit actum liberi arbitrii, et ideo est virtus; non sic baptismus; differt autem poenitentia interior, ut est virtus et res Sacramenti. Primo enim modo est contra dehabilitationem culpae, secundo contra vulnerationem naturae[^8].

### Dub. III

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *A poenitentia coepit Ioannis praedicatio.* Videtur enim, quod debuit incipere a baptismo, quia poenitentia est secunda tabula, sed baptismus prima[^9].

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod incepit praedicationem a poenitentia-virtute, non Sacramento; et poenitentia-virtus in adultis praecedit baptismum; et quia praedicabat adultis, ideo recte incepit a poenitentia. Obiectio sua currit de poenitentiae Sacramento[^10].

### Dub. IV

Item quaeritur de ista definitione Ambrosii quae ponitur in littera: *Poenitentia est mala praeterita plangere et plangenda iterum non committere.* Videtur enim non esse conveniens: quia Philosophus[^11] dicit, quod «peccat qui definit id quod est in quiete, per id quod est in actu et motu»; sed poenitentia dicit habitum, *plangere* actum: ergo etc. — Item, obiicitur de alio membro, quod dicit: «*Plangenda iterum non committere*»; aut enim refertur ad *idem* tempus, aut ad *aliud.* Si ad *idem;* tunc superfluit, quia nemo, dum peccata plangit, in eodem tempore peccata committit simul et semel. Si ad *aliud:* ergo poenitentia excludit iterationem culpae, ergo et iterationem poenitentiae.

Iuxta hoc quaeritur: cum multae definitiones poenitentiae assignentur, et «unius rei una sit definitio»; quomodo differenter assignantur?

**Respondeo:** Ad primum dicendum, quod, sicut vult Philosophus[^12], «habitus cognoscuntur per actus»; et quoniam poenitentia est habitus; ideo per actum recte definitur, et per actum, qui est de *esse* et qui est principalis, et iste est *plangere commissa;* et per actum, qui est de *bene esse* sive de *perfecto esse,* et iste est *plangenda non committere;* vel per actum duplicem, principalem et annexum.

Quod ergo obiicitur, quod male definitur per actum; dicendum, quod actus non accipitur ut in motu, sed ut in quiete. Dicitur enim poenitentia esse *plangere,* non quia poenitentia semper sit in actu plangendi, sed quia habilis est ad plangendum; et sic accipitur ut in quiete.

Ad aliud dicendum, quod potest referri ad *idem tempus,* vel ad *aliud.* Si ad *idem tempus;* tunc non stat pro *actu* non committendi, sed pro *affectione,* ut scilicet, quando poenitemus, velimus iterum non committere, vel non velimus, sicut illi qui de uno peccato poenitent et de alio non poenitent; et ita non excludit *casum sequentem,* sed malam voluntatem praesentem. — Si autem referatur ad *futurum;* sic poenitentia definitur non quaelibet, sed *perfecta* et secundum statum perfectionis et utilitatis[^13].

<!-- page 330 -->

Ad illud quod quaeritur de definitionibus, dicendum, quod poenitentia potest tripliciter notificari, scilicet per *actum principalem,* per *actum principalem et annexum* et per *effectum.* — Secundum primum modum tres dantur definitiones differentes solum voce vel maiori expressione. Prima est in Glossa Matthaei tertio[^14]: «*Prima virtus est per poenitentiam*» etc.; unde talis traditur notificatio: «Poenitentia est prima virtus perimens hominem veterem». — Secunda est Ambrosii in sermone quodam[^15]: «Poenitentia est dolor et amaritudo animae pro peccatis, quae quisque commisit». — Tertia Augustini in libro de Poenitentia[^16]: «Poenitentia est quaedam dolentis vindicta semper puniens in se quod dolet commisisse». In omnibus his rationibus patet, quod est definitio per *actum principalem,* qui est dolere de commisso.

Quantum ad *secundum modum* dantur aliae, quae habentur in littera, differentes similiter secundum maiorem expressionem. Prima est Ambrosii in sermone[^17]: «Poenitentia est praeterita mala plangere et plangenda» etc. — Secunda est Gregorii[^18]: «Poenitentia est anteacta mala deflere» etc. — Tertia Magistri[^19]: «Poenitentia est virtus vel gratia, qua commissa mala cum emendationis proposito» etc. In omnibus istis rationibus non tantum ponitur actus principalis, qui est *plangere* et odire culpam perpetratam, sed etiam *annexus,* scilicet nolle aliam perpetrare.

Quantum ad *tertium modum* dantur aliae tres differentes similiter secundum maiorem expressionem. Prima a Damasceno[^20]: «Poenitentia est ab eo quod est praeter naturam, in id quod est secundum naturam conversio». — Secunda Glossa Matthaei quarto[^21]: *Poenitentiam agite:* «Poenitentia est correctio malae mentis». — Tertia Augustini in libro Quinque Responsionum[^22]: «Poenitentia est quaedam res optima et perfecta, omnes defectus revocans ad perfectum». — In omnibus illis rationibus tangitur secundum plus et minus *effectus* poenitentiae, qui est animam reparare. — Et sic patet quaesitum de differentia definitionum, quae dantur de poenitentia.

## English

### DOUBTS CONCERNING THE TEXT OF THE MASTER.

### Doubt I

In this section there are doubts concerning the text, and first about this, that Jerome says: *The second plank after shipwreck is penance.* For since "second" is said with respect to "first," and the first plank is baptism, and the second penance; then it seems that the second Sacrament is penance: therefore the Master orders it badly in the fourth place. — Likewise, I ask of you: in what sense is penance called a *plank?* and what is *shipwreck? If you say* that it is a fall into mortal sin; then it seems that it is the first plank, because, if anyone has fallen through mortal sin, he must have recourse to penance.

**I respond:** It must be said that the sea is this world, according to that of the Psalm[^1]: *This great sea* etc. The *ship,* by which man crosses over the waves of this sea, is the grace of the Holy Spirit; or the ship is the Church, which is joined together by the glue of charity, according to that of the last [chapter] of Proverbs: *She has become like the merchant's ship.* The *shipwreck,* however, was the corruption and breaking in Adam, in which breaking all his sons were cast upon the waves of this sea through concupiscence and through punishment. — From this shipwreck man is first freed through baptism, which restores the innocence and grace that were lost[^2]; and therefore it is the *first plank.* And because many often abandon baptismal grace and that plank, by which they are freed amid the waves of the sea; and by it can no longer defend themselves: therefore they need a *second,* and this is penance. Yet both are called a plank, because each holds up, lest anyone be wrecked through fault; yet it does not set [him] in safety just as man was in the state of innocence.

As for what he objects, that it is[^3] the *second plank:* therefore the second Sacrament — it does not hold; because not every Sacrament is a plank, since not every one frees from shipwreck. And there is a parallel: this man is the second archdeacon: therefore he is the second person in this church.

<!-- page 329 -->

As for what is asked: what is called shipwreck? some say that it is any fall into mortal sin; and because the difference of mortal sin is twofold, namely original and actual; therefore the plank is twofold, namely baptism and penance[^4]. — Yet it can better be said that human nature suffered shipwreck in Adam; but there has been given to us the plank of baptism, which sustains us through innocence; and because this Sacrament cannot be repeated[^5], therefore another Sacrament is given, and this is penance.

### Doubt II

Likewise it is asked about this, that the Master says: *Penance is called a Sacrament and a virtue of the mind.* For penance is interior and exterior: therefore likewise baptism is a virtue and a Sacrament, since there is an interior and exterior baptism[^6]. — Likewise, interior penance is the *res* of this Sacrament[^7]; but no virtue is the *res of a Sacrament,* because the grace of the virtues and the grace of the Sacraments are distinguished: therefore etc.

**I respond:** It must be said that this is not the whole account, that *interior* penance is a virtue, but rather because it is *interior* penance, which not only states grace or the act of grace, but concerns the act of free choice, and therefore is a virtue; not so baptism; but interior penance differs, as it is a virtue and the *res* of a Sacrament. For in the first way it is against the disabling caused by fault, in the second against the wounding of nature[^8].

### Doubt III

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *From penance John's preaching began.* For it seems that it ought to have begun from baptism, because penance is the second plank, but baptism the first[^9].

**I respond:** It must be said that he began his preaching from penance-as-virtue, not the Sacrament; and penance-as-virtue in adults precedes baptism; and because he was preaching to adults, therefore he rightly began from penance. His objection runs of the Sacrament of penance[^10].

### Doubt IV

Likewise it is asked about this definition of Ambrose which is set in the text: *Penance is to lament past evils and not to commit again those to be lamented.* For it seems not to be fitting: because the Philosopher[^11] says that «he sins who defines that which is at rest by that which is in act and motion»; but penance states a habit, *to lament* an act: therefore etc. — Likewise, an objection is raised about the other member, which says: «*not to commit again those to be lamented*»; for either it refers to the *same* time, or to *another.* If to the *same;* then it is superfluous, because no one, while he laments his sins, at the same time commits sins together and at once. If to *another:* therefore penance excludes the repetition of fault, therefore also the repetition of penance.

In connection with this it is asked: since many definitions of penance are assigned, and «of one thing there is one definition»; how are they assigned differently?

**I respond:** To the first it must be said that, as the Philosopher holds[^12], «habits are known through their acts»; and since penance is a habit; therefore it is rightly defined through an act, both through the act which is of its *being* and which is principal, and this is *to lament things committed;* and through the act which is of its *well-being* or of its *perfect being,* and this is *not to commit things to be lamented;* or through a twofold act, principal and annexed.

As for what is then objected, that it is badly defined through an act; it must be said that the act is not taken as in motion, but as at rest. For penance is said to be *to lament,* not because penance is always in the act of lamenting, but because it is apt for lamenting; and so it is taken as at rest.

To the other it must be said that it can be referred to the *same time,* or to *another.* If to the *same time;* then it does not stand for the *act* of not committing, but for the *affection,* namely that, when we do penance, we should will not to commit again, or should not will [it], like those who do penance for one sin and do not do penance for another; and so it does not exclude the *subsequent fall,* but the present evil will. — But if it be referred to the *future;* thus penance is defined not as just any penance, but as *perfect* and according to the state of perfection and usefulness[^13].

<!-- page 330 -->

To that which is asked about the definitions, it must be said that penance can be made known in three ways, namely through the *principal act,* through the *principal and annexed act,* and through the *effect.* — According to the first way three definitions are given, differing only in wording or in fuller expression. The first is in the Gloss on Matthew the third[^14]: «*The first virtue is through penance*» etc.; whence such a notification is handed down: «Penance is the first virtue, destroying the old man». — The second is Ambrose's in a certain sermon[^15]: «Penance is the grief and bitterness of the soul over the sins which each has committed». — The third Augustine's in the book *On Penance*[^16]: «Penance is a kind of vengeance of one grieving, always punishing in himself that which he grieves to have committed». In all these accounts it is clear that it is a definition through the *principal act,* which is to grieve over what has been committed.

As for the *second way* others are given, which are had in the text, differing likewise according to fuller expression. The first is Ambrose's in a sermon[^17]: «Penance is to lament past evils and [not to commit] things to be lamented» etc. — The second is Gregory's[^18]: «Penance is to bewail evils done beforehand» etc. — The third the Master's[^19]: «Penance is the virtue or grace by which evils committed [are bewailed] with the purpose of amendment» etc. In all these accounts there is set not only the principal act, which is *to lament* and to hate the fault perpetrated, but also the *annexed* act, namely to be unwilling to perpetrate another.

As for the *third way* three others are given, differing likewise according to fuller expression. The first from Damascene[^20]: «Penance is a conversion from that which is beside nature into that which is according to nature». — The second the Gloss on Matthew the fourth[^21]: *Do penance:* «Penance is the correction of an evil mind». — The third Augustine's in the book *Of Five Responses*[^22]: «Penance is a certain most excellent and perfect thing, recalling all defects to the perfect». — In all those accounts there is touched, according to more and less, the *effect* of penance, which is to repair the soul. — And thus what was sought concerning the difference of the definitions which are given of penance is clear.

**Dubia count: 4.**
- **Dub. I** — On Jerome's *Secunda tabula post naufragium est poenitentia:* if "second" implies "second Sacrament," does the Master misorder penance in the fourth place? And is penance the *first* plank (since a fall into mortal sin requires recourse to penance)? Answer: the allegory — sea = world, ship = grace/Church, shipwreck = the fracture in Adam; baptism = first plank (restores lost innocence/grace, unrepeatable), penance = second; "second plank" ≠ "second Sacrament," since not every Sacrament frees from shipwreck. Better said: human nature was wrecked in Adam, baptism is the given plank, and since it cannot be repeated penance is given as another.
- **Dub. II** — On the Master's *Poenitentia dicitur Sacramentum et virtus mentis:* baptism too is interior/exterior, so it should be both virtue and Sacrament; and interior penance is the Sacrament's *res,* yet no virtue is a Sacrament's *res.* Answer: interior penance is a virtue not merely because interior, but because it concerns the act of free choice (unlike baptism); it differs as virtue (vs. the disabling of fault) and as *res* of the Sacrament (vs. the wounding of nature).
- **Dub. III** — On *A poenitentia coepit Ioannis praedicatio:* it should have begun from baptism (the first plank). Answer: he began from penance-as-virtue (not the Sacrament), which in adults precedes baptism; preaching to adults, he rightly began there. The objection runs only of the Sacrament of penance.
- **Dub. IV** — On Ambrose's definition *Poenitentia est mala praeterita plangere et plangenda iterum non committere:* (a) it defines a habit (penance) by an act (lamenting), which the Philosopher faults; (b) "not committing again" is either superfluous (same time) or excludes the repetition of penance (another time); and (c) since one thing has one definition, why are many assigned? Answer: habits are known through acts, so penance is rightly defined by act — principal (*plangere commissa,* of *esse*) and annexed (*plangenda non committere,* of *bene esse*); the act is taken as at rest, not in motion; "not committing again" stands for the *affection,* or, referred to the future, for *perfect* penance. The many definitions notify penance threefold — by principal act (Gloss, Ambrose, Augustine), by principal + annexed act (Ambrose, Gregory, the Master), and by effect (Damascene, Gloss, Augustine's *Five Responses*).
