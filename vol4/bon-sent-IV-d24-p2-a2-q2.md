## *Utrum psalmistatus sit ordo*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 631 -->

### Quaestio II. *Utrum psalmistatus sit ordo.*

Secundo quaeritur, utrum psalmistatus sit ordo. Et quod sic, videtur.

1. Distinctione vigesima prima[^1]: *Cleros* et *clericos,* et infra: «Generaliter clerici dicti sunt omnes qui in Ecclesia Christi deserviunt, quorum gradus et nomina haec sunt: ostiarius, psalmista et lector» etc.; et *iterum,* distinctione vigesima quinta, *Perlectis,* et infra, ubi enumerat officium psalmistae inter alios; et hoc expresse dicit Isidorus septimo libro Etymologiarum.

2. Item, ratione videtur hoc ipsum probari: quia prophetia Psalmorum non est minus nobilis quam aliae: ergo, sicut est ordo in Ecclesia deputatus ad recitandum alias prophetias, scilicet ordo lectoris[^2], ita videtur, quod debeat aliquis ordo ordinari ad recitandum Psalmos; sed iste est psalmistae: ergo etc.

3. Item, nobilius et dignius officium est Psalmos in Ecclesia recitare, quam sit ostia custodire: ergo si ordo deputatus est ad illud officium[^3], multo fortius videtur, quod ad dicendum Psalmos.

**Contra:**
1. Distinctione septuagesima septima[^4], *Illud nos,* et infra: «Si quis episcopus esse meretur, sit primum *ostiarius,* deinde *lector,* postea *exorcista,* deinde sacretur *acolythus,* deinde vero *subdiaconus,* deinde *diaconus,* postea *presbyter*»: hic sufficienter gradus et ordines distinguuntur, et non ponitur ordo psalmistae: ergo etc.

2. Item, omnes ordines, sicut melius patebit, ab episcopo dantur, non a simplici sacerdote; sed psalmistatus datur a simplici sacerdote; unde distinctione vigesima tertia[^5]: «Psalmista potest absque scientia episcopi sola iussione presbyteri officium suscipere cantandi».

3. Item, ad illud quod communiter spectat ad totum chorum, non debet aliquis ordo ordinari[^6] specialiter; sed psalmodia spectat ad omnes qui sunt in choro, et cantus antiphonarum et responsoriorum: ergo etc.

### Conclusio.

> Psalmistatus proprie non est ordo, sed officium ordini annexum.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod psalmistatus proprie non est ordo, sed officium ordini annexum. — Et ratio huius est, quia non dicit officium, cui debeat specialis gradus ordinis deputari, sed solum est annexum ordini.

<!-- page 632 -->

Psalmista enim et cantor pro eodem reputantur, sicut patet ex his quae dicuntur distinctione vigesima tertia[^7]: *Psalmista, id est cantor* etc. Psalmistae enim est non solum Psalmos cantare, sed etiam alios cantus harmonicos lectionibus interserere; et hoc, ut audientium corda excitentur ad Dei laudem et ad eruditionem lectoris et non afficiantur taedio in auditu verbi divini. In hoc ergo, quod est ad laudem Dei, cum illud sit omnium fidelium et communiter insimul[^8], non oportet ad hoc ordinem deputari. In hoc enim, quod excitat corda ad eruditionem et ad percipiendum sensum, quem praedicat lector, est officium annexum ordini lectoris. Et sic patet, quod stricte accipiendo ordines et gradus, psalmistatus non debet inter ordines computari; sed si largius extendatur nomen ordinis ad officium etiam annexum ordini et quod specialiter et principaliter convenit ordinatis[^9], sic psalmistatus potest inter ordines computari, et ita accipit Isidorus.

**Ad argumenta pro parte affirmativa:**

1. Et per hoc patent auctoritates inductae ad hoc, quod sit ordo; loquuntur enim secundum hanc viam.

2. Ad illud vero quod obiicitur, quod prophetia Psalmorum est dignior, vel aeque digna; dicendum, quod *digna* est quantum ad intellectum et sensum ita, sicut aliae. Sed quia psalmista non tantum erudit per sensum verborum, quantum etiam excitat mentes per cantum musicorum et per emissionem vocum resonantium; et hoc competit *multitudini,* alias vero prophetias competit *uni* recitare, cui alii sint intenti: ideo oportet, illum qui legit, esse provectiorem, non ratione eius *quod* recitatur, sed ratione *modi* recitandi. Et sic patet illud.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur de custodia ostiorum et psalmodia, dicendum, quod maioris auctoritatis et dignitatis est excludere indignos et admittere dignos ad divinum officium[^10], quam interesse et Psalmos cantare, pro eo quod primum dicit quandam auctoritatem et potestatem, nec competit alicui, nisi detur ab eo qui habet auctoritatem eminentem, scilicet ab episcopo. Sed psalmodia respicit scientiam et vocem; et qui habet haec sine donatione auctoritatis potest hoc facere sola iussione sacerdotis, ut praedictum est. Quamvis ergo istud officium maius videatur, tamen si consideratur auctoritas utrobique, illud est maius quam hoc[^11].

## English

### Question II. *Whether the office of psalmist is an order.*

Secondly it is asked whether the office of psalmist (*psalmistatus*) is an order. And that it is, seems [to be the case]:

1. Distinction twenty-one[^1]: *Cleros* and *clericos,* and below: «Generally all who serve in the Church of Christ are called clerics, whose ranks and names are these: doorkeeper, psalmist, and reader» etc.; and *again,* distinction twenty-five, *Perlectis,* and below, where it enumerates the office of the psalmist among the others; and Isidore says this expressly in the seventh book of the *Etymologies.*

2. Likewise, this same thing seems to be proved by reason: because the prophecy of the Psalms is not less noble than the others: therefore, just as there is an order in the Church appointed for reciting the other prophecies, namely the order of reader[^2], so it seems that some order ought to be ordained for reciting the Psalms; but this is [the order] of the psalmist: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, it is a nobler and more worthy office to recite the Psalms in the Church than to guard the door: therefore, if an order has been appointed for that office[^3], much more does it seem [there should be one] for saying the Psalms.

**On the contrary:**
1. Distinction seventy-seven[^4], *Illud nos,* and below: «If anyone deserves to be a bishop, let him first be a *doorkeeper,* then a *reader,* afterward an *exorcist,* then let him be consecrated an *acolyte,* then indeed a *subdeacon,* then a *deacon,* afterward a *presbyter*»: here the ranks and orders are sufficiently distinguished, and no order of psalmist is posited: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, all orders, as will better appear, are given by the bishop, not by a simple priest; but the office of psalmist is given by a simple priest; whence distinction twenty-three[^5]: «A psalmist can, without the bishop's knowledge, by the mere command of a presbyter, undertake the office of singing».

3. Likewise, for that which pertains commonly to the whole choir, no order ought to be ordained[^6] specially; but psalmody pertains to all who are in the choir, and the singing of antiphons and responsories: therefore etc.

### Conclusion.

> The office of psalmist is properly not an order, but an office annexed to an order.

**I respond:** It must be said that the office of psalmist is properly not an order, but an office annexed to an order. — And the reason for this is that it does not denote an office to which a special grade of order ought to be assigned, but [one] that is only annexed to an order.

<!-- page 632 -->

For psalmist and cantor are reckoned as the same, as is clear from what is said in distinction twenty-three[^7]: *A psalmist, that is, a cantor,* etc. For it belongs to the psalmist not only to sing the Psalms, but also to interweave other harmonious chants with the readings; and this, so that the hearts of the hearers may be roused to the praise of God and to the instruction of the reader, and not be afflicted with weariness in hearing the divine word. In this, therefore, which is unto the praise of God — since that belongs to all the faithful and in common together[^8] — it is not necessary to appoint an order for it. For in this, that it rouses the hearts to instruction and to the perceiving of the sense which the reader proclaims, it is an office annexed to the order of reader. And thus it is clear that, taking orders and grades strictly, the office of psalmist ought not to be counted among the orders; but if the name of order be extended more broadly to an office also annexed to an order and which belongs specially and principally to those ordained[^9], then the office of psalmist can be counted among the orders, and thus Isidore takes it.

**To the arguments for the affirmative side:**

1. And by this the authorities adduced to [show] that it is an order are made clear; for they speak according to this way.

2. To that which is objected, that the prophecy of the Psalms is more worthy, or equally worthy; it must be said that it is *worthy* as regards the understanding and the sense, just as the others are. But because the psalmist not only instructs through the sense of the words, but as much also rouses minds through the chant of musical [tones] and through the emission of resounding voices; and this belongs to the *multitude,* whereas reciting the other prophecies belongs to *one* [person], to whom the others are attentive: therefore it is necessary that the one who reads be more advanced, not by reason of *that which* is recited, but by reason of the *manner* of reciting. And thus that [argument] is clear.

3. To that which is objected concerning the guarding of doors and psalmody, it must be said that it is of greater authority and dignity to exclude the unworthy and to admit the worthy to the divine office[^10] than to be present and to sing the Psalms, for the reason that the first bespeaks a certain authority and power, and belongs to no one unless it be given by him who has eminent authority, namely by the bishop. But psalmody regards knowledge and voice; and he who has these, without the bestowal of authority, can do this by the mere command of a priest, as has been said. Although, therefore, that office may seem greater, nevertheless, if the authority on both sides is considered, the former is greater than the latter[^11].
