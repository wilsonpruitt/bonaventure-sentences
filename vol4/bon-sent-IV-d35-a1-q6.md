## *Utrum possit fieri divortium, si non praecedat aliqua culpa*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 787 -->

### Quaestio VI. *Utrum possit fieri divortium, si non praecedat aliqua culpa.*

Sexto et ultimo quaeritur, utrum possit fieri divortium, si non praecedat aliqua culpa. Et quod non, videtur.

1. Primae ad Corinthios septimo[^p787-3]: *Praecipio non ego, sed Dominus, uxorem a viro non discedere, excepta causa fornicationis:* ergo praeceptum est, quod non separentur; sed contra praeceptum non potest quis facere, quin incurrat culpam: ergo etc.

2. Item, Ambrosius super Lucam[^p787-4]: « Dimittis uxorem sine crimine; putas hoc tibi licere, quod lex divina prohibet »: ergo hoc est prohibitum, sine crimine dimittere.

3. Item, verbo ipsius Domini, quod est firmius inter cetera, Matthaei decimo nono[^p787-5]: *Qui dimiserit uxorem suam nisi ob fornicationem, moechatur;* sed per fornicationem, sive accipiatur large, sive stricte, semper intelligitur culpa: ergo etc.

4. Item, qui legitime coniuncti sunt ad perpetuam et individuam vitae consuetudinem ex matrimonio sunt ligati; qui ergo contra hoc facit, nisi habeat causam, quare debeat rationabiliter facere, matrimonio facit iniuriam; et non est sine peccato facere iniuriam Sacramento[^p787-6]: ergo etc.

5. Item, primae ad Corinthios septimo[^p787-7]: *Alligatus es uxori? Noli quaerere solutionem:* ergo nisi interveniat culpa sive peccatum, videtur, quod non liceat facere divortium.

**Contra:**

1. Sicut habetur *Extra, de Conversione coniugatorum*[^p787-8], vir et mulier possunt, si volunt, ex consensu mutuo religiones intrare diversas, etiam si non praecedat culpa; sed hoc non fit sine divortio: ergo etc.

2. Item, unusquisque potest licite renuntiare iuri suo[^p787-9]: ergo et quoad cohabitationem et quoad debitum solvendum potest absolvere: ergo etc.

3. Item, uxore volente intrare monasterium, vir potest promoveri ad sacros ordines et ad episcopatum; et hoc totum sine culpa, sicut dicitur *de Conversione coniugatorum*[^p787-10], *Sane si coniugati:* ergo etc.

Iuxta hoc quaeritur, utrum necesse sit, utrumque votum solemne emittere ad hoc, quod divortium possit fieri, et quare.

<!-- page 788 -->

> **Conclusio.** Divortium sine culpa praecedente non potest fieri, nisi utroque ad altiorem statum voluntarie transeunte et voto solemni se obligante.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod divortium inter legitimas coniuges potest fieri dupliciter: aut ut in *eodem statu persistant,* aut ut ad *frugem melioris vitae transeant.* Si, ut in *eodem statu persistant;* tunc non debet fieri, nisi manifesta culpa praecedat, tum propter *periculum,* tum propter scandalum. — Si, ut ad *frugem melioris vitae transeant;* tunc aut *uterque est voluntarius,* aut *alter invitus.* Si *uterque est voluntarius;* tunc potest fieri sine culpa, ita tamen quod uterque votum solemne emittat, et uterque habitum religionis assumat, nisi sit talis persona, « de qua nulla possit oriri suspicio », quae, voto solemni emisso, poterit remanere in domo; sin autem, oportet, utrumque converti, sicut expresse in pluribus decretalibus tangitur *de Conversione coniugatorum*[^p788-1]: rursus, et tale divortium fieri debet per personam publicam, maxime per episcopum. — Si autem *alter vult cohabitare;* nullo modo fieri potest nisi per culpam praecedentem.

Et secundum hanc distinctionem respondetur argumentis factis ad utramque partem, sicut patet aspicienti[^p788-2]. — Verumtamen quod ultimo dicit Apostolus, quod *alligatus non quaerat solutionem,* hoc non dicit, ut dehortetur statum altiorem, sed ut a communione removeat instabilitatem, et ut ostendat, in quolibet statu hominem posse salvari. Unde illud intelligitur, quod *non quaerat solutionem,* scilicet *in praeiudicium uxoris,* sicut nec servus libertatem in praeiudicium domini[^p788-3].

## English

### Question VI. *Whether a divorce can take place, if no fault precedes.*

Sixthly and lastly it is asked whether a divorce can take place, if no fault precedes. And that it cannot, seems [to be the case].

1. First to the Corinthians, chapter seven[^p787-3]: *I command, not I but the Lord, that the wife depart not from her husband, except for the cause of fornication:* therefore it is commanded that they not be separated; but against a command one cannot act without incurring fault: therefore, etc.

2. Likewise, Ambrose on Luke[^p787-4]: « You dismiss your wife without crime; you think this permitted to you, which the divine law forbids »: therefore this is forbidden, to dismiss [her] without crime.

3. Likewise, by the word of the Lord himself, which is firmer than the rest, Matthew, chapter nineteen[^p787-5]: *Whoever dismisses his wife unless for fornication, commits adultery;* but by fornication, whether it be taken broadly or strictly, fault is always understood: therefore, etc.

4. Likewise, those who are legitimately joined for a perpetual and undivided common life are bound by matrimony; whoever therefore acts against this, unless he have a cause for which he ought reasonably to do it, inflicts an injury on the matrimony; and it is not without sin to inflict injury on the Sacrament[^p787-6]: therefore, etc.

5. Likewise, first to the Corinthians, chapter seven[^p787-7]: *Are you bound to a wife? Do not seek release:* therefore, unless fault or sin intervene, it seems that it is not permitted to make a divorce.

**On the contrary:**

1. As is held in the *Decretals, On the Conversion of the Married*[^p787-8], a man and a woman can, if they wish, enter different religious [orders] by mutual consent, even if no fault precedes; but this is not done without a divorce: therefore, etc.

2. Likewise, everyone can licitly renounce his own right[^p787-9]: therefore, both as to cohabitation and as to paying the debt he can absolve [himself]: therefore, etc.

3. Likewise, with the wife wishing to enter a monastery, the man can be promoted to sacred orders and to the episcopate; and all this without fault, as is said in *On the Conversion of the Married*[^p787-10], *Sane si coniugati:* therefore, etc.

Alongside this it is asked whether it is necessary for both to make a solemn vow in order that a divorce may take place, and why.

> **Conclusion.** A divorce without a preceding fault cannot take place, unless both, voluntarily passing over to a higher state, bind themselves by a solemn vow.

**I respond:** It must be said that a divorce between legitimate spouses can take place in two ways: either so that *they remain in the same state,* or so that *they pass over to the fruit of a better life.* If [it be] so that *they remain in the same state;* then it ought not to take place, unless manifest fault precede, both on account of *danger* and on account of scandal. — If [it be] so that *they pass over to the fruit of a better life;* then either *both are willing,* or *the other is unwilling.* If *both are willing;* then it can take place without fault, provided, however, that each make a solemn vow, and each take on the habit of religion — unless it be such a person « of whom no suspicion can arise », who, once the solemn vow has been made, will be able to remain in [her] home; but if not, it is necessary that both be converted [to religious life], as is expressly touched upon in several decretals *On the Conversion of the Married*[^p788-1]: again, such a divorce ought to be made through a public person, especially through a bishop. — But if *the other wishes to cohabit;* in no way can it take place except through a preceding fault.

And according to this distinction, the arguments made for both parts are answered, as is clear to one who looks[^p788-2]. — Nevertheless, as to what the Apostle says lastly, that *the one bound should not seek release,* he does not say this to dissuade [anyone] from the higher state, but to remove instability from the community, and to show that in any state a person can be saved. Hence that [saying] is understood [thus]: that *he should not seek release,* namely *to the prejudice of the wife,* just as neither [should] a slave [seek] freedom to the prejudice of [his] master[^p788-3].
