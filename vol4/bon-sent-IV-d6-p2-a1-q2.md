# bon-sent-IV-d6-p2-a1-q2 — Whether one sanctified in the womb ought to be baptized

## *Utrum sanctificatus in utero debeat baptizari.*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 151 -->

### Quaestio II. *Utrum sanctificatus in utero debeat baptizari.*

Secundo quaeritur circa hoc, utrum sanctificatus in utero debeat baptizari, cum natus est. Et quod sic, videtur:

1. Quia talis per sanctificationem non habet characterem[^2]; sed debet signo Christi consignari, quod fit in baptismo: ergo debet baptizari.

2. Item, baptizati baptismo flaminis baptizantur aqua et tenentur baptizari, ut patet de Cornelio, Actuum decimo[^3]; sed sanctificari non est aliud quam gratiam Spiritus sancti recipere: ergo sanctificati tenentur baptizari.

3. Item, nullus quantumcumque magnus absolvitur a divino mandato; sed Dominus mandatum dedit de suscipiendo baptismum[^4]: ergo ita tenetur ad hoc sanctificatus, ut alius.

**Sed contra:**

1. Vigor singularis privilegii[^5] semper transcendit beneficium commune: ergo in sanctificatione datur gratia curans, sicut in baptismo, et maior et efficacior; sed ordo non est a maiori perfectione ad minorem, sed e converso: ergo sanctificatus non tenetur baptizari.

2. Item, si sanctificatus baptizatur, videtur, quod sit *vanitas,* quia quod potest sufficienter fieri per unum frustra fit per duo in natura[^6]: ergo si «natura non facit frustra», multo minus nec gratia; sed gratia sanctificans sufficit ad curationem, alioquin Deus agit imperfecte: ergo etc.

3. Item, videtur, quod sit ibi contemptus: quia qui baptizatus est, si iterum baptizetur, facit contumeliam Sacramento, quia primum remedium reputat insufficiens[^7]: ergo pari ratione qui baptizatur post sanctificationem iniuriatur sanctificationi.

### Conclusio

> *Sanctificatus in utero debet baptizari.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod sanctificatus baptizari debet baptismo aquae, sicut baptizatus baptismo flaminis[^8] — Et est ratio triplex: prima propter bonum obedientiae, quia Deus praecipit, ut omnes qui nascuntur, renascantur aqua; secunda propter humilitatem, quia debet se reputare peccato[^1]rem et indigere Christi redemptione; tertia propter unitatem, ut ab aliis membris Christi non discordet. Non ergo baptizatur propter hoc, quod sanctificatio non sufficiat, sed propter rationes praedictas. Propter quarum exempla danda Christus fuit circumcisus: ut scilicet obediret Legi, subiecit se signo peccatoris, quo non ipse se peccatorem, sed alios se peccatores profiteri doceret; postremo, ut Patribus non esset dissimilis[^9].

Patet igitur responsio, quod sanctificatus non recipit baptismum in *remedium* — quod si faceret,
<!-- page 152 -->
esset inordinatio, vanitas et contemptus — sed in signaculum, quod habet tria bona coniuncta, scilicet obedientiam, humilitatem et unitatem, quantum ad unitatem characteris.

**Ad oppositum:**

1. Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur, quod sanctificatio transcendit; dicendum, quod quamvis hoc verum sit quantum ad gratiam, quae delet culpam et habilitat ad bonum; tamen non transcendit quantum ad characterem, quia illum non imprimit; et ideo ratione illius adhuc baptismus debet recipi.

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod superfluit; patet responsio: quia non recipit[^p1] ad idem, ad quod praecessit sanctificatio.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod rebaptizatus[^p2] iniuriatur baptismo; dicendum, quod hoc verum est, quia iterat idem remedium et ad idem; non sic est in proposito. Et sic patet totum[^p3].

## English
<!-- page 151 -->

### Question II. *Whether one sanctified in the womb ought to be baptized.*

Secondly it is asked concerning this, whether one sanctified in the womb ought to be baptized once he has been born. And that he ought, it seems:

1. Because such a one does not, through sanctification, have the character[^2]; but he ought to be marked with the sign of Christ, which is done in baptism: therefore he ought to be baptized.

2. Likewise, those baptized by the baptism of the Spirit are baptized with water and are bound to be baptized, as is plain in the case of Cornelius, in the tenth chapter of Acts[^3]; but to be sanctified is nothing other than to receive the grace of the Holy Spirit: therefore the sanctified are bound to be baptized.

3. Likewise, no one however great is released from the divine commandment; but the Lord gave the commandment about receiving baptism[^4]: therefore one who has been sanctified is bound to this just as another is.

**On the contrary:**

1. The force of a singular privilege[^5] always transcends a common benefit: therefore in sanctification a healing grace is given, just as in baptism, and greater and more efficacious; but order does not proceed from greater perfection to lesser, but the reverse: therefore one sanctified is not bound to be baptized.

2. Likewise, if one sanctified is baptized, it seems that it is *in vain,* because what can be done sufficiently by one is done in vain by two in nature[^6]: therefore if "nature does nothing in vain," much less does grace; but sanctifying grace suffices for healing, otherwise God acts imperfectly: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, it seems that there is contempt there: because one who has been baptized, if he is baptized again, does an outrage to the Sacrament, since he reckons the first remedy insufficient[^7]: therefore by parity of reasoning one who is baptized after sanctification does an injury to the sanctification.

### Conclusion

> *One sanctified in the womb ought to be baptized.*

**I respond:** It must be said that one sanctified ought to be baptized with the baptism of water, just as one baptized with the baptism of the Spirit[^8] — And there is a threefold reason: the first on account of the good of obedience, because God commands that all who are born be reborn by water; the second on account of humility, because he ought to reckon himself a sinner[^1] and in need of the redemption of Christ; the third on account of unity, that he not be at variance with the other members of Christ. Therefore he is not baptized on this account, that the sanctification does not suffice, but on account of the reasons aforesaid. For the sake of giving an example of these things Christ was circumcised: namely that he might obey the Law, he subjected himself to the sign of the sinner, by which he taught not that he himself, but that others profess themselves to be sinners; lastly, that he might not be unlike to the Fathers[^9].

Therefore the response is plain, that one sanctified does not receive baptism as a *remedy* — for if he did,
<!-- page 152 -->
it would be disorder, vanity, and contempt — but as a seal, which has three goods joined together, namely obedience, humility, and unity, with respect to the unity of the character.

**To the opposed arguments:**

1. To that, then, which is objected, that the sanctification transcends; it must be said that although this is true with respect to the grace, which blots out fault and disposes toward the good; nevertheless it does not transcend with respect to the character, because it does not imprint it; and therefore by reason of that the baptism ought still to be received.

2. To that which is objected, that it is superfluous; the response is plain: because he does not receive[^p1] for the same end for which the sanctification went before.

3. To that which is objected, that one rebaptized[^p2] does an injury to baptism; it must be said that this is true, because he repeats the same remedy and for the same end; it is not so in the present case. And thus the whole is plain[^p3].
