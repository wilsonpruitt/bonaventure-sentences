# IV Sent., d. 25, a. 2, q. 2

## *Utrum ad susceptionem ordinis necessarius sit usus rationis*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 651 -->

### Quaestio II. *Utrum ad susceptionem ordinis necessarius sit usus rationis.*

Secundo quaeritur, utrum ad susceptionem ordinis necessarius sit usus rationis. Et quod sic, videtur:

1. Quia nobilius et perfectius Sacramentum est Sacramentum ordinis quam Sacramentum matrimonii, et in altiori statu ponit; sed Sacramentum matrimonii non potest a furiosis et ratione carentibus contrahi, sicut expresse habetur infra[^1]: ergo etc.

2. Item, hoc videtur *per formam*: quia nullus offert aliquid non intelligenti; sed in ordine dicit ordinans ordinato: «Accipe potestatem» etc.: ergo praesupponit in eo discretionem. Si ergo non habet discretionem, non videtur ordinem posse suscipere.

3. Item, usus istius Sacramenti nulli potest competere nisi habenti rationis usum: ergo si alii datur, frustra datur: ergo si «Deus nihil frustra facit[^2]», videtur, quod ei qui non habet rationis usum, nunquam Sacramentum ordinis conferatur.

4. Item, si quis offerret potestatem recusanti, non esset ibi potestatis susceptio, quoniam, sicut se habet *collatio* ad voluntatem *dantis*, ita *susceptio* ad voluntatem *suscipientis*: ergo si *dans* non dat, nisi velit et intendat, *suscipiens* non suscipit, nisi velit et intendat.

5. Item, tempus et aetas determinantur in susceptione ordinis distinctione septuagesima septima[^3]; sed hoc non est propter aliud nisi propter discretionem et usum rationis, qui non habetur in minoribus annis: ergo etc.

6. Item, sacris ordinibus de necessitate est annexa continentia; sed nullus obligatur ad continentiam, nisi usum habeat rationis, et per consensum propriae voluntatis: ergo saltem ad sacros ordines nullus potest ante annos discretionis promoveri[^4]: ergo necessarius est usus rationis.

**Contra:**
1. Alia Sacramenta, quae imprimunt characterem, ut baptismus et confirmatio, ante usum rationis dari possunt: ergo videtur pari ratione, quod et istud Sacramentum.

2. Item, hoc ipsum videtur, *Extra, de Clerico ordinato per saltum*[^5], ubi dicitur, quod infra annos discretionis ad minores ordines est promotus, et post sine iteratione concessum est ei in huiusmodi ministrare.

3. Item, sacerdoti, quando ordinatur, dantur claves ligandi et solvendi, etiam si sacerdos scientia careat[^6]: ergo multo fortius puero dari poterit ordo, si careat usu rationis.

4. Item, si aliquis timore mortis compulsus ordinari patiatur et malit ordinari quam mori, est ordinatus, sicut et in baptismo manifeste potest ha-

<!-- page 652 -->

beri[^7]: ergo sufficit voluntas coacta: ergo non est necessarium, quod libere consentiat: ergo non videtur necessaria aetas.

5. Item, sapientis est respicere non tantum praesens, sed etiam quod futurum est[^8]: ergo si multi carent discretione, de quibus praesumitur, quod aliquando sint discreti; videtur, quod saltem ordo possit eis committi, et exsecutio maneat in suspenso.

6. Item, si aliquid ex parte suscipientis esset necessarium in ipsa ordinatione, oporteret, quod saltem consensum suum exprimeret, et quod ab eo requireretur, sicut fit in matrimonio; sed hoc modo non fit in ordine: ergo non videtur, quod sit necessarius consensus, ergo nec usus rationis.

### Conclusio.

> Ad septem ordinum susceptionem non requiritur usus rationis necessitate Sacramenti, sed necessitate sive praecepti, sive congruitatis.

**Respondeo:** Ad hoc dicunt aliqui, quod ad ordinum omnium susceptionem requiritur rationis consensus, pro eo quod in quolibet ordine officium committitur respectu actus *eliciti*[^9] a ratione; et officium nulli committitur, nisi ei qui vult recipere: et ideo dicunt, quod usus rationis requiritur in ordinato, alioquin nihil omnino fit. — Sed tamen illud videtur esse contrarium decretali *de Clerico per saltum ordinato*, ubi tangitur, quod ad minores ordines infra annos discretionis promotus est. *Amplius*, tota die videmus, parvulos ad ordines minores promoveri, qui nec sciunt, quid sit ordo, nec quid ibi detur. Et *iterum*, usus confirmationis non est ante adultam aetatem, et tamen Sacramentum dari potest.

Et ideo est alia opinio maiorum, quod ad aliquid[^10] usus rationis requiritur de necessitate *Sacramenti*, ad aliquem ordinem de necessitate *praecepti*, ad aliquos de *honestate* et *congruitate*. Ad *episcopatum* namque, quoniam ibi contrahitur matrimonium spirituale, necessario requiritur consensus, sine quo non fit matrimonium, nec spirituale nec corporale, ut infra[^11] videbitur; ac per hoc necessario requiritur usus rationis et discretionis de necessitate *Sacramenti*, licet multa alia requirantur de necessitate *praecepti*. — Ad *ordines* vero *sacros* requiritur rationis usus de necessitate *praecepti*, pro eo quod ad illos ordines non debent admitti, nisi qui possunt ad continentiam obligari; et ideo qui ordinant scienter eos qui non possunt obligari, quia ratione carent, faciunt contra statutum et praeceptum Ecclesiae; et ideo, si tales ordinantur exterius, characterem recipiunt *de facto*, quia non contradicunt, quamvis non consentiant[^12]. — Ad *minores* vero *ordines* requiritur de *honestate* rationis usus, quia ibi committuntur officia, quae spectant non solum ad habentes usum rationis, immo etiam ad habentes scientiam litteraturae. Sed tamen, quia illi ordines sunt ad quandam assuefactionem; ubi ex aliqua utilitate vel necessitate videtur expediens, possunt ad minores ordines promoveri etiam ante annos discretionis. Sed quod ita passim promoveantur et beneficiis ecclesiasticis ditentur, vix potest esse sine contemptu canonum et scandalo hominum et periculo animarum. — Concedendae igitur sunt rationes ostendentes, quod non est necessarius usus rationis ad Sacramenti *esse* vel *suscipere*, sed solum quantum ad *bene esse* et *digne suscipere*, loquendo de ordinibus proprie.

1. Ad illud quod obiicitur de matrimonio, dicendum, quod non est simile: quia in matrimonio non requiritur usus rationis propter dignitatem Sacramenti, sed propter significationem coniunctionis Dei et animae, quae non est nisi in mutuo consensu. *Praeterea*, non concludit hoc generaliter de Sacramento ordinis, sed solum de dignitate episcopi[^13].

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod dicit: *Accipe*; dicendum, quod illa oblatio significat magis imperium et potestatem *dantis* quam exercitationem *suscipientis*[^14]. Unde illius non tantum est *offerre*, sed *conferre*; et nisi sit obex contrariae voluntatis, ita quod omnino sit invitus, recipit Sacramentum, secundum quod iudicatur de Sacramento confirmationis.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur de usu, dicendum, quod etsi non habeat statim usum, quia tamen speratur aliquando habere, non frustra datur Sacramentum[^15], sicut etiam datur sacerdotium ei qui longo post tempore differt celebrare.

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur de recusante, dicendum, quod hic est *sophisma consequentis*[^16]: recusans non recipit: ergo oportet, quod recipiens

<!-- page 653 -->

consentiat; quia medium est inter *recusantem omnino* et *consentientem*.

5. Ad illud quod obiicitur de tempore et aetate, dicendum, quod determinatio illa temporis non est quantum ad *Sacramenti necessitatem*, sed potius quantum ad *susceptionis congruitatem* et *solemnitatem*; quam in maioribus ordinibus oportet servari, saltem quantum ad annos discretionis, propter votum continentiae et ordinum dignitatem; ulterior vero protelatio temporis quantum ad sacerdotes quantum ad tempora moderna non exigitur, quia, sicut in multis «malitia supplet aetatem[^17]», ita in multis bonitas et sanctitas supplet. *Praeterea*, multi nunc ordinantur, qui non praeficiuntur populo, ut religiosi; ideo non oportet in eis ita servare tempora, ut in aliis, maxime si sit sanctitas vitae et scientia et maturitas morum.

6. Ad illud quod obiicitur de continentia, iam patet responsio; nam continentia est in sacris ordinibus non de *esse*, sed de *bene esse*[^18].

## English

### Question II. *Whether the use of reason is necessary for receiving an order.*

In the second place it is asked whether the use of reason is necessary for receiving an order. And that it is, seems [to follow]:

1. Because the Sacrament of order is a nobler and more perfect Sacrament than the Sacrament of matrimony, and places [one] in a higher state; but the Sacrament of matrimony cannot be contracted by the insane and by those lacking reason, as is expressly had below[^1]: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, this seems [to hold] *by [reason of] the form*: because no one offers something to one who does not understand; but in [conferring] an order the ordainer says to the ordained: "Receive the power," etc.: therefore he presupposes discretion in him. If therefore he does not have discretion, he does not seem able to receive the order.

3. Likewise, the use of this Sacrament can belong to no one except to one having the use of reason: therefore if it is given to another, it is given in vain: therefore if "God does nothing in vain[^2]," it seems that upon one who does not have the use of reason the Sacrament of order should never be conferred.

4. Likewise, if someone were to offer power to one refusing it, there would be no reception of power there, since, just as the *conferral* stands to the will of the *giver*, so the *reception* [stands] to the will of the *receiver*: therefore if the *giver* does not give unless he wills and intends, the *receiver* does not receive unless he wills and intends.

5. Likewise, the time and age are determined for the reception of an order in distinction seventy-seven[^3]; but this is not on account of anything else except on account of discretion and the use of reason, which is not had in the lesser years: therefore etc.

6. Likewise, to the sacred orders continence is of necessity annexed; but no one is bound to continence unless he has the use of reason, and through the consent of his own will: therefore at least to the sacred orders no one can be promoted before the years of discretion[^4]: therefore the use of reason is necessary.

**On the contrary:**
1. The other Sacraments which imprint a character, such as baptism and confirmation, can be given before the use of reason: therefore it seems by parity of reasoning that this Sacrament [can be given] too.

2. Likewise, this very thing seems [to hold from] the *Decretals, On the Cleric ordained by a leap*[^5], where it is said that within the years of discretion he was promoted to the minor orders, and afterwards, without reiteration, it was granted to him to minister in such [orders].

3. Likewise, to a priest, when he is ordained, are given the keys of binding and loosing, even if the priest lacks knowledge[^6]: therefore much more strongly can an order be given to a boy, [even] if he lacks the use of reason.

4. Likewise, if someone, compelled by fear of death, should allow himself to be ordained and prefer to be ordained rather than to die, he is ordained, just as also in baptism can manifestly be had[^7]: therefore a coerced will suffices: therefore it is not necessary that he freely consent: therefore the age does not seem necessary.

5. Likewise, it belongs to the wise man to regard not only the present, but also what is future[^8]: therefore if many lack discretion, of whom it is presumed that they will at some time be discreet; it seems that at least the order could be committed to them, and its execution remain in suspense.

6. Likewise, if something on the part of the receiver were necessary in the ordination itself, it would be required that he at least express his consent, and that it be required of him, as is done in matrimony; but in this manner it is not done in [conferring] an order: therefore it does not seem that consent is necessary, and therefore neither the use of reason.

### Conclusion.

> For the reception of the seven orders the use of reason is not required by necessity of the Sacrament, but by necessity either of precept or of fittingness.

**I respond:** To this some say that for the reception of all the orders the consent of reason is required, for the reason that in every order an office is committed with respect to an act *elicited*[^9] by reason; and an office is committed to no one except to him who wills to receive it: and therefore they say that the use of reason is required in the ordained, otherwise nothing at all is done. — But nevertheless that seems to be contrary to the decretal *On the Cleric ordained by a leap*, where it is touched upon that he was promoted to the minor orders within the years of discretion. *Furthermore*, all day long we see little children promoted to the minor orders, who neither know what an order is, nor what is given therein. And *again*, the use of confirmation is not before adult age, and yet the Sacrament can be given.

And therefore there is another opinion of the greater [masters], that for something[^10] the use of reason is required by necessity of the *Sacrament*, for some order by necessity of *precept*, for some [orders] by [reason of] *honesty* and *fittingness*. For to the *episcopate*, since therein a spiritual matrimony is contracted, consent is necessarily required, without which no matrimony comes to be, neither spiritual nor corporeal, as will be seen below[^11]; and through this the use of reason and of discretion is necessarily required by necessity of the *Sacrament*, although many other things are required by necessity of *precept*. — But to the *sacred orders* the use of reason is required by necessity of *precept*, for the reason that to those orders none ought to be admitted except those who can be bound to continence; and therefore those who knowingly ordain those who cannot be bound, because they lack reason, act against the statute and precept of the Church; and therefore, if such are ordained outwardly, they receive the character *in fact*, because they do not contradict, although they do not consent[^12]. — But to the *minor orders* the use of reason is required by [reason of] *honesty*, because therein offices are committed which pertain not only to those having the use of reason, but even to those having a knowledge of letters. But nevertheless, since those orders are for a certain habituation; where from some utility or necessity it seems expedient, they can be promoted to the minor orders even before the years of discretion. But that they be thus promoted indiscriminately and enriched with ecclesiastical benefices can scarcely be without contempt of the canons and scandal of men and peril of souls. — The arguments, therefore, showing that the use of reason is not necessary for the *being* or the *receiving* of the Sacrament, but only as regards the *well-being* and the *worthy receiving*, are to be granted, speaking of the orders properly.

1. To that which is objected concerning matrimony, it must be said that it is not similar: because in matrimony the use of reason is not required on account of the dignity of the Sacrament, but on account of the signification of the joining of God and the soul, which is not [effected] except in mutual consent. *Furthermore*, this does not conclude generally concerning the Sacrament of order, but only concerning the dignity of the bishop[^13].

2. To that which is objected, that it says: *Receive*; it must be said that that offering signifies rather the command and power of the *giver* than the exercise of the *receiver*[^14]. Hence it belongs to him not only to *offer*, but to *confer*; and unless there be an obstacle of a contrary will, so that he be altogether unwilling, he receives the Sacrament, according as is judged concerning the Sacrament of confirmation.

3. To that which is objected concerning use, it must be said that even if he does not have the use immediately, since nevertheless it is hoped that he will at some time have it, the Sacrament is not given in vain[^15], just as the priesthood is also given to one who for a long time afterward defers to celebrate.

4. To that which is objected concerning the one refusing, it must be said that here there is a *fallacy of the consequent*[^16]: the one refusing does not receive: therefore it is necessary that the one receiving consent; because there is a mean between *one refusing altogether* and *one consenting*.

5. To that which is objected concerning time and age, it must be said that that determination of time is not as regards the *necessity of the Sacrament*, but rather as regards the *fittingness of reception* and its *solemnity*; which in the greater orders must be observed, at least as regards the years of discretion, on account of the vow of continence and the dignity of the orders; but a further prolongation of time, as regards priests, as regards modern times, is not demanded, because, just as in many [cases] "malice supplies for age[^17]," so in many goodness and sanctity supply [for it]. *Furthermore*, many are now ordained who are not set over a people, as religious; therefore it is not necessary in them so to observe the times, as in others, especially if there be sanctity of life and knowledge and maturity of character.

6. To that which is objected concerning continence, the response is already evident; for continence is in the sacred orders not of [their] *being*, but of [their] *well-being*[^18].
