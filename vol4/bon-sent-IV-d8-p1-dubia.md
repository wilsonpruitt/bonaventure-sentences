## Latin

<!-- page 187 -->

## DUBIA CIRCA LITTERAM MAGISTRI

### Dub. I

In parte ista sunt dubitationes circa litteram, et primo de hoc quod dicit Magister: *Post Sacramentum baptismi et confirmationis*. Videtur enim non bene ordinare Sacramenta; prius enim est *cibari*, quod est eucharistiae, quam *confirmari*, quia parvulorum est *cibari*.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod quamvis eucharistia sit cibus communis omnium bonorum, tamen dicit Augustinus[^1] quod est cibus grandium et perfectorum; et ita sequitur illa duo Sacramenta. Praeterea, secundum consuetudinem modernae Ecclesiae prius confirmatur parvulus, quam communicetur[^2].

### Dub. II

Item quaeritur: quare hoc Sacramentum potius dicitur *eucharistia*[^3], cum in omnibus detur *gratia bona*, et in baptismo maxime detur *magna gratia*?

<!-- page 188 -->

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod hoc proprie dicitur de eucharistia, tum quia principaliter est ibi *ratio boni*, tum etiam quia *ratio gratiae*: ratio boni, quia «bonum est diffusivum et communicativum»[^4]. Unde cibus, qui communicat se pluribus et per partes, est bonus; qui communicat se pluribus totum et successive, est melior, ut verbum Dei, quod praedicatur; qui vero communicat se pluribus et totum et simul, iste est optimus; et talis est iste cibus[^5]. Hic etiam est ille cibus, qui est summum bonum, scilicet Christus Dominus[^6], et sic praecipue reperitur ratio boni. — Reperitur etiam ibi principaliter *ratio gratiae* tum per modum contentivi, tum per modum effectivi[^7]. Per modum contentivi, quia hoc est solum Sacramentum, quo ipsa gratia est contenta secundum veritatem, immo plenitudo gratiae; unde assimilatur ipsi caritati, quae unit fini et habet idem pro obiecto et pro fine[^8]. Ratione effectivi, quoniam hic est carbo ignitus, caro scilicet Christi, quae inflammat et beatificat, non tantum gratificat, sed et multipliciter adiuvat. Primum quidem, quia est cibus incorporans capiti, Ioannis sexto: *Qui manducat carnem meam in me manet*. Secundo, quia fortificat, Psalmus: *Panis cor hominis confirmat*. Tertio, quia delectat, Sapientiae decimo sexto: *Panem de caelo dedisti eis omne habentem delectamentum* etc. Quarto, valet ad augmentum fidei, sicut dicitur infra distinctione duodecima. Quinto, ad sublevationem spei, Matthaei ultimo: *Ecce ego vobiscum* etc. Sexto, ad dilatationem caritatis, quia est memoriale amoris: *Hoc quotiescumque feceritis* etc. Septimo, ad remissionem venialium, sicut dicit Innocentius.

### Dub. III

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod recte dicitur *viaticum, quia nos in via reficit* etc.; quia, si dicitur *viaticum*, quia est viatorum; pari ratione et alia Sacramenta.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod viaticum dicitur triplici ex causa. Prima est, quia est cibus itinerantium, quia datur transeuntibus de hac vita ad patriam. Secunda, quia institutus est a Christo ut itinerante sive transeunte. Tertia, quia signum est transitus Christi, sicut phase.

### Dub. IV

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod hoc Sacramentum Dominus instituit, quare magis Dominus ipse per se sive in propria persona hoc Sacramentum et baptismum instituit?

**Respondeo:** Et ad hoc dicendum, quod quia in baptismo est inchoatio, et in hoc consummatio; ideo illud instituit in principio suae praedicationis, istud in fine et in recessu a discipulis; et ideo haec duo confirmavit, Matthaei ultimo.

## English

## DOUBTS CONCERNING THE TEXT OF THE MASTER

### Doubt I

In this part there are doubts concerning the text, and first concerning what the Master says: *After the Sacrament of baptism and confirmation*. For it seems that he does not order the Sacraments well; for to *be fed*, which belongs to the eucharist, comes before being *confirmed*, since to *be fed* belongs to little children.

**I respond:** It must be said that, although the eucharist is the common food of all the good, nevertheless Augustine[^1] says that it is the food of the grown and of the perfect; and so it follows those two Sacraments. Moreover, according to the custom of the modern Church a little child is confirmed before being given communion[^2].

### Doubt II

Likewise it is asked: why is this Sacrament rather called *eucharist*[^3], since in all of them *good grace* is given, and in baptism especially *great grace* is given?

**I respond:** It must be said that this is properly said of the eucharist, both because the *account of the good* is principally there, and also because the *account of grace* is there: the account of the good, because «the good is self-diffusive and self-communicating»[^4]. Hence the food which communicates itself to many and by parts is good; that which communicates itself to many wholly and successively is better, as the word of God which is preached; but that which communicates itself to many both wholly and all at once, this is best; and such is this food[^5]. This too is that food which is the highest good, namely Christ the Lord[^6], and thus the account of the good is preeminently found here. — There is also found here principally the *account of grace*, both by way of containing and by way of effecting[^7]. By way of containing, because this is the only Sacrament in which grace itself is contained according to truth, indeed the fullness of grace; whence it is likened to charity itself, which unites to the end and has the same thing for object and for end[^8]. By reason of effecting, because here is a burning coal, namely the flesh of Christ, which inflames and beatifies, not only makes pleasing in grace, but also helps in many ways. First indeed, because it is food incorporating us into the Head, John six: *Whoever eats my flesh abides in me*. Second, because it strengthens, the Psalm: *Bread strengthens the heart of man*. Third, because it delights, Wisdom sixteen: *You gave them bread from heaven having all delight* etc. Fourth, it avails for the increase of faith, as is said below in distinction twelve. Fifth, for the lifting up of hope, Matthew last: *Behold I am with you* etc. Sixth, for the enlargement of charity, since it is a memorial of love: *As often as you do this* etc. Seventh, for the remission of venial sins, as Innocent says.

### Doubt III

Likewise it is asked concerning what he says, that it is rightly called *viaticum, because it refreshes us on the way* etc.; for, if it is called *viaticum* because it belongs to wayfarers, by equal reasoning the other Sacraments would be too.

**I respond:** It must be said that it is called viaticum for a threefold cause. The first is, because it is the food of travelers, since it is given to those passing from this life to the fatherland. The second, because it was instituted by Christ as traveling or passing over. The third, because it is the sign of Christ's passover, as the *phase*.

### Doubt IV

Likewise it is asked concerning what he says, that the Lord instituted this Sacrament: why did the Lord rather, by himself or in his own person, institute this Sacrament and baptism?

**I respond:** And to this it must be said that, because in baptism there is the beginning, and in this the consummation; therefore he instituted the former at the beginning of his preaching, the latter at the end and in his departure from the disciples; and therefore he confirmed these two, Matthew last.
