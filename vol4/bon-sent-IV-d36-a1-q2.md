# d.36 a1-q2
## *Utrum conditio servitutis cum errore impediat matrimonium*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 793 -->

### Quaestio II. *Utrum conditio servitutis cum errore impediat matrimonium.*

Secundo quaeritur, utrum conditio servitutis cum errore impediat matrimonium. Et quod non, videtur:

1. Quia magis est tolerabilis in coniuge servitutis conditio quam qualitas leprae: ergo si error qualitatis talis non impedit, multo minus nec error conditionis.

2. Item, error fortunae, sicut iura[^p793-1] dicunt, non impedit matrimonium; sed possibile est, quod servitus sit *a fortuna*, sicut aliquis capitur *a casu* in bello et efficitur servus, vel propter paupertatem, quae est *a fortuna* secundum usum loquendi, se vendit in servum: ergo etc.

3. Item, servus, quantum est de se, persona est legitima ad contrahendum; sed conditio servitutis non tollit plenum consensum, quia, si cognoscit substantiam eius et qualitates et accidentia, talis cognitio sufficit ad hoc, quod consentiat: ergo non est ibi illegitimitas personae nec defectus consensus: ergo est matrimonium plenum.

4. Item, peior est conditio servitutis spiritualis quam corporalis[^p793-2]; sed error conditionis spiritualis non impedit matrimonium: ergo etc.

**Contra:** 1. Iura dicunt Causa vigesima nona, quaestione secunda[^p793-3], et decretalis, de Coniugio servorum, quod error conditionis impedit matrimonium contrahendum et dirimit iam contractum.

2. Item, *ratione* videtur: quia illud est impedimentum matrimonii, quod est contra bonum matrimonii; sed servitus est contra *bonum prolis*, quia, si liber cum ancilla contrahat, filii non sunt liberi, sed servi, quia, sicut dicunt iura[^p793-4], «partus sequitur ventrem».

3. Item, est contra *bonum fidei*, quia dominus potest vendere uxorem alienigenis, ipso viro nolente, maxime infidelibus, ubi, si sequitur eam, imminet ei periculum fidei; si vero non sequitur, non potest petere debitum nec habere[^p793-5].

4. Item, nullus puniri debet sine culpa, ipso nolente et ignorante; sed opprobrium et infamia est alicui, quod sit servus, vel etiam servili personae subiectus: ergo ubi intervenit error conditionis, non est matrimonium.

Quaeritur igitur, quare et quando error conditionis impediat matrimonium.

> **Conclusio.** *Conditio servitutis cum errore tunc matrimonium dirimit, quando liberi cum servis contrahunt.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod error conditionis potest esse tripliciter: aut paris, ut cum servus contrahit cum ancilla; aut melioris, ut cum servus cum libera; aut deterioris, ut cum liber cum ancilla. In primis duobus casibus error non impedit matrimonium, in tertio vero impedit matrimonium et dirimit, nisi post cognitionem conditionis consenserit verbo, vel facto.

Ratio autem huius est propter duo, scilicet propter favorem *libertatis* et *causae matrimonialis*, quae maxime favorabilis est. Unde quia qui contrahit cum ancilla quodam modo perdit libertatem, quae est, sicut dicitur in iure civili[^p793-6], «bonum inaestimabile», et quam nullus perdit nisi nolens, quia perditam recuperare non potest etiam volens: ideo in favorem libertatis ius servatur erranti, ut, si velit, possit ancillam deserere. — Et *praeterea*, servitus est contra *bonum prolis* et *fidei*; et quia iura favent matrimonio, ideo praesumunt, errantem in conditione[^p793-7] non consentire, ideo nec matrimonium esse. Et ideo, quod impediat, hoc facit constitutio Ecclesiae ex praedicta veniens ratione.

1. Ad illud quod obiicitur de qualitate, dicendum, quod non est simile: quia nulla qualitas est ita contra bona matrimonii, ut servitus; nulla etiam ita obviat bonis matrimonii, ut servitus, de his quae non impediunt[^p793-8].

2. Ad aliud dicendum, quod libertas non computatur inter bona fortunae, immo omnibus aliis praeponderat.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod nec error nec servitus impedit; dicendum, quod *servitus per se*
<!-- page 794 -->
non impedit, cum est scita, quia quilibet, cum vult, potest effici servus; sed *ignorata* impedit, quia non debet puniri nolens.

4. Ad illud quod ultimo obiicitur de servitute spirituali, dicendum, quod etsi sit peior ipsi servo, non tamen adeo redundat in alium, utpote in prolem, vel etiam in uxorem; et praeterea facilius potest expelli[^p794-1].

## English
<!-- page 793 -->

### Question II. *Whether the condition of servitude with error impedes marriage.*

Secondly it is asked whether the condition of servitude with error impedes marriage. And that it does not, it seems:

1. Because the condition of servitude is more tolerable in a spouse than the quality of leprosy: therefore if such an error of quality does not impede, much less does an error of condition.

2. Likewise, an error of fortune, as the laws[^p793-1] say, does not impede marriage; but it is possible that servitude be *from fortune*, as someone is captured *by chance* in war and made a slave, or on account of poverty, which is *from fortune* according to the usage of speech, sells himself into slavery: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, a slave, as far as concerns himself, is a legitimate person for contracting; but the condition of servitude does not remove full consent, because, if she knows his substance and qualities and accidents, such knowledge suffices for this, that she consent: therefore there is neither illegitimacy of person nor defect of consent there: therefore the marriage is full.

4. Likewise, the condition of spiritual servitude is worse than corporeal[^p793-2]; but an error of spiritual condition does not impede marriage: therefore etc.

**On the contrary:** 1. The laws say, Cause twenty-nine, question two[^p793-3], and the decretal, On the Marriage of Slaves, that an error of condition impedes a marriage from being contracted and dissolves one already contracted.

2. Likewise, it seems *by reason*: because that is an impediment of marriage which is against the good of marriage; but servitude is against the *good of offspring*, because, if a free man contracts with a bondwoman, the children are not free, but slaves, because, as the laws[^p793-4] say, «the offspring follows the womb».

3. Likewise, it is against the *good of fidelity*, because a master can sell the wife to foreigners, the husband himself being unwilling, especially to unbelievers, where, if he follows her, danger to his faith threatens him; but if he does not follow, he cannot ask for the debt nor have it[^p793-5].

4. Likewise, no one ought to be punished without fault, himself unwilling and ignorant; but it is a reproach and disgrace to someone that he be a slave, or even subject to a servile person: therefore where an error of condition intervenes, there is no marriage.

It is asked therefore why and when an error of condition impedes marriage.

> **Conclusion.** *The condition of servitude with error dissolves marriage in that case when free persons contract with slaves.*

**I respond:** It must be said that an error of condition can be of three kinds: either of an equal, as when a male slave contracts with a female slave; or of a better, as when a male slave [contracts] with a free woman; or of a worse, as when a free man [contracts] with a bondwoman. In the first two cases the error does not impede marriage, but in the third it does impede marriage and dissolves it, unless after knowledge of the condition one has consented by word or by deed.

Now the reason for this is on account of two things, namely on account of favor for *liberty* and for *the matrimonial cause*, which is most favorable. Hence, because he who contracts with a bondwoman in some manner loses liberty—which is, as is said in the civil law[^p793-6], «an inestimable good», and which no one loses except unwillingly, since even one willing cannot recover it once lost—therefore in favor of liberty the law is kept for the one erring, so that, if he wills, he may forsake the bondwoman. — And *moreover*, servitude is against the *good of offspring* and *of fidelity*; and because the laws favor marriage, therefore they presume that one erring as to condition[^p793-7] does not consent, and therefore that there is no marriage either. And therefore, that it impedes, this the constitution of the Church brings about, coming from the aforesaid reason.

1. To that which is objected concerning quality, it must be said that it is not alike: because no quality is so against the goods of marriage as servitude; nor does any so oppose the goods of marriage as servitude does, among those things which do not impede[^p793-8].

2. To the other it must be said that liberty is not counted among the goods of fortune, but rather outweighs all the others.

3. To that which is objected, that neither error nor servitude impedes; it must be said that *servitude in itself*
<!-- page 794 -->
does not impede when it is known, because anyone, when he wills, can be made a slave; but *unknown* it does impede, because one ought not to be punished unwillingly.

4. To that which is objected last concerning spiritual servitude, it must be said that although it be worse for the slave himself, nevertheless it does not so redound upon another, namely upon the offspring, or even upon the wife; and moreover it can more easily be cast off[^p794-1].
