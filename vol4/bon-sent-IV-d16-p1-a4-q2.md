# d. 16, p. 1, a. 4, q. 2 — Whether one ought to be contrite more intensely for one sin than for another

## *Utrum oporteat intensius conteri de uno peccato quam de alio*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 395 -->

### Quaestio II. *Utrum oporteat intensius conteri de uno peccato quam de alio.*

Secundo quaeritur, utrum per contritionem plus vel intensius oporteat conteri de uno peccato quam de alio. Et quod non, videtur:

1. Quia contritio delens culpam est in instanti, sicut patet de iustificatione impii[^1]; sed motus idem in instanti non potest esse magis et minus intensus: ergo etc.

2. Item, quidquid agit in multa simul, ita quod non in singula, non agit in quoddam plus, in quoddam minus; sed contritio simul agit in multa peccata, ita quod non considerat singula, quia tunc oporteret, respectu illorum dividi actionem: ergo contritio non magis detestatur unum peccatum quam alterum.

3. Item, contritio ea ratione detestatur peccatum, qua separativum est a Deo; sed quodlibet se<!-- page 396 -->parat a Deo, et ita amittitur Deus per unum peccatum mortale, sicut per aliud, et ita per parvum, sicut per magnum: ergo etc.

4. Item, contritio respicit peccatum quantum ad duo, scilicet quantum ad *offensam* et quantum ad *damnum*; sed quantum ad *offensam* omne peccatum habet rationem infiniti, cum sit contra Deum infinitum; quantum ad *damnum*, omne peccatum aufert omnes virtutes, sicut dicitur Iacobi secundo[^2], et habetur in littera: *Qui peccat in uno factus est omnium reus*: cum ergo damnum et offensa habeant rationem aequalitatis, videtur, quod contritio debeat de omnibus esse aequalis.

5. Item, quantumcumque contritio detestetur modicum peccatum, laudabile[^3] est; et quantumcumque sit parva contritio etiam de maximo peccato, bona est et laudabilis: ergo videtur, quod non sit necessarius gradus intensionis secundum gradum vitiorum et peccatorum.

**Contra:**
1. Maior est dolor de mortali et debet esse quam de veniali[^4]: ergo pari ratione de uno peccato mortali graviori quam de alio minus gravi[^5].

2. Item, secundum Gregorium[^6]: « magis se debet punire per satisfactionem qui gravius peccavit »: ergo pari ratione idem ipse, cum gravius peccat, gravius se debet punire per contritionem: si ergo de graviori peccato in diversis oportet esse contritionem maiorem, pari ratione in eodem.

3. Item, is est ordo in dolore, qui est et debet esse in amore, quoniam ab ipso habet ortum; sed magis bonum secundum ordinatam dilectionem magis est amandum: ergo pari ratione maius malum magis est detestandum.

4. Item, contritio est dolor rationalis[^7]; sed dolor consequitur existimationem rationis, existimatio autem rationis non aequaliter pensat omnia peccata, sed quaedam maiora: ergo pari ratione et dolor.

5. Item, esto per impossibile, quod aliquis perplexus esset, ut caderet in alterum duorum malorum; constat, quod si haberet dilectionem ordinatam, magis refugeret maius malum[^8]: ergo si magis refugit ante lapsum, pari ratione et post lapsum, et sic etc.

> **Conclusio.** Contritio, ut est deletiva culpae, ordinem habet ad magis detestandum maius malum secundum habitum, non secundum actum, sed ut est deletiva poenae, etiam secundum actum.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod dupliciter est loqui de contritione: aut prout est deletiva *culpae*, aut prout est deletiva *poenae*. Si prout est deletiva *culpae*; sic operatur in iustificatione impii[^9] in instanti, et sic *actu* non potest habere ordinem respectu specialium peccatorum, quia simul et in instanti est contra omne peccatum commissum; habet tamen secundum *habitum*, quia dolor ille habitualiter disponit ad detestandum aliquod plus, aliquod minus; et quia habilitat ad detestandum malum, quia malum, ideo ad magis detestandum maius malum. — Si autem loquamur de[^10] deletiva *poenae*; sic dicendum, quod habet maiorem intensionem et minorem etiam secundum *actum*, sicut ostendunt rationes inductae ad secundam partem.

**Ad argumenta:**
*Ad 1 et 2.* Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur in contrarium, patet responsio, scilicet ad primum et secundum. Procedunt enim de contritione secundum primam viam, scilicet ut est in iustificatione impii.

*Ad 3 et 4.* Ad illud quod obiicitur de separatione et damno et offensa, dicendum, quod *separatio* attenditur in elongatione secundum dissimilitudinem, et *offensa* secundum contemptum, et *damnum* secundum amissionem gratuitorum et vulnerationem naturalium sive dehabilitationem[^11]; et in his poenae gradus est; unde illud non valet. Quamvis enim Deus ab omnibus offendatur, tamen aliqui contemnunt eum plus, aliqui minus; et similiter in aliquo peccato plus natura laeditur et a virtute plus elongatur.

*Ad 5.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod de parvo non minus potest dolere[^11a]; dicendum, quod sicut in caritate non est dare, quin amor, quantumcumque sit parvus et quantumcumque magnus, semper sit bonus, tamen in sua magnitudine et parvitate necessario habet ordinem; sic in proposito est intelligendum. Et sic facile est ad cetera respondere[^12].

## English

### Question II. *Whether one ought to be contrite more intensely for one sin than for another.*

Secondly it is asked whether through contrition one ought to be contrite more, or more intensely, for one sin than for another. And that one ought not, it seems:

1. Because the contrition which blots out guilt is in an instant, as is clear concerning the justification of the ungodly[^1]; but the same motion in an instant cannot be more and less intense: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, whatever acts upon many things at once, so that it does not act upon them singly, does not act upon one more and another less; but contrition acts upon many sins at once, so that it does not consider them singly, because then it would be necessary, with respect to them, that the action be divided: therefore contrition does not detest one sin more than another.

3. Likewise, contrition detests sin by that account by which it is something separating from God; but anything whatever<!-- page 396 -->separates from God, and so God is lost through one mortal sin just as through another, and so through a small one just as through a great one: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, contrition regards sin in two respects, namely as to *offense* and as to *harm*; but as to *offense* every sin has the character of the infinite, since it is against the infinite God; as to *harm*, every sin takes away all the virtues, as is said in the second chapter of James[^2], and is held in the text: *Whoever sins in one is made guilty of all*: since therefore harm and offense have the character of equality, it seems that contrition ought to be equal concerning all of them.

5. Likewise, however much contrition detests a slight sin, it is praiseworthy[^3]; and however small the contrition, even concerning the greatest sin, it is good and praiseworthy: therefore it seems that a degree of intensity according to the degree of vices and sins is not necessary.

**On the contrary:**
1. The grief over a mortal sin is greater, and ought to be, than over a venial one[^4]: therefore by parity of reasoning over one graver mortal sin than over another less grave one[^5].

2. Likewise, according to Gregory[^6]: « he who has sinned more gravely ought to punish himself more by satisfaction »: therefore by parity of reasoning the same person, when he sins more gravely, ought to punish himself more gravely through contrition: if therefore over a graver sin in different persons the contrition must be greater, by parity of reasoning so in the same person.

3. Likewise, that order is in grief which is and ought to be in love, since it takes its origin from it; but the greater good, according to ordered love, is more to be loved: therefore by parity of reasoning the greater evil is more to be detested.

4. Likewise, contrition is a rational grief[^7]; but grief follows the estimation of reason, and the estimation of reason does not weigh all sins equally, but some as greater: therefore by parity of reasoning grief too.

5. Likewise, suppose by the impossible that someone were so perplexed that he would fall into one of two evils; it is settled that, if he had ordered love, he would shun the greater evil more[^8]: therefore if he shuns it more before the fall, by parity of reasoning so after the fall, and so etc.

> **Conclusion.** Contrition, insofar as it is destructive of guilt, has an order toward detesting the greater evil more according to habit, not according to act; but insofar as it is destructive of punishment, also according to act.

**I respond:** It must be said that there are two ways of speaking of contrition: either insofar as it is destructive of *guilt*, or insofar as it is destructive of *punishment*. If insofar as it is destructive of *guilt*; thus it operates in the justification of the ungodly[^9] in an instant, and thus *actually* it cannot have an order with respect to particular sins, because at once and in an instant it is against every sin committed; it has it, however, according to *habit*, because that grief habitually disposes one to detest one thing more, another less; and because it makes one apt to detest evil, as it is evil, therefore to detest the greater evil more. — But if we speak of it as[^10] destructive of *punishment*; then it must be said that it has greater and lesser intensity even according to *act*, as the reasons brought forward for the second part show.

**To the arguments:**
*To 1 and 2.* To that, then, which is objected on the contrary, the response is clear, namely to the first and second. For they proceed concerning contrition according to the first way, namely as it is in the justification of the ungodly.

*To 3 and 4.* To that which is objected concerning separation and harm and offense, it must be said that *separation* is regarded in the estrangement according to unlikeness, and *offense* according to contempt, and *harm* according to the loss of gratuitous goods and the wounding of natural ones, or the disabling of them[^11]; and in these there is a degree of punishment; whence that argument does not hold. For although God is offended by all, yet some despise him more, some less; and likewise in one sin nature is more wounded and is more estranged from virtue.

*To 5.* To that which is objected, that over a small sin one can grieve no less[^11a]; it must be said that, just as in charity it cannot be granted that love — however small and however great it be — is not always good, yet in its greatness and smallness it necessarily has an order; so it is to be understood in the matter proposed. And thus it is easy to respond to the rest[^12].
