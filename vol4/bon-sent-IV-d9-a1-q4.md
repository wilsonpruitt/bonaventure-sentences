# IV Sent., d. 9, a. 1, q. 4
## *Utrum Christus manducaverit utroque modo.*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 205 -->

### Quaestio IV. *Utrum Christus manducaverit utroque modo.*

Quarto quaeritur, utrum Christus manducaverit quolibet supra dictorum modorum. Et quod sacramentaliter, videtur:

1. Levitici octavo[^1]: *Cumque de sanguine arietis; Glossa* Hesychii[^2]: « Primo sacerdos, deinde filii sanguine intingebantur, quia Christus in Coena prius sanguinem accepit, deinde discipulis dedit ». Et rursus ibidem dicit Glossa: « Bibit et aliis bibere dedit ».

<!-- page 206 -->

2. Item, *ratione* videtur, quia baptismum accepit in exemplum[^3]: ergo si hoc Sacramentum est magis arduum, ergo de hoc magis exemplum dare debebat.

Item, quod *spiritualiter* manducaverit, videtur:

1. Quia spiritualiter manducatur per cognitionem et dilectionem[^4]: sed Christus summe se cognoscebat et diligebat: ergo etc.

2. Item, Christus adhuc erat viator, ergo adhuc debebat uti viatico; sed corpus Christi spiritualiter est viaticum[^5]: ergo etc.

**Contra**, quod non manducaverit spiritualiter, videtur:

3. Quia ubi est spiritualis manducatio, ibi unio[^6]; ubi autem unio, ibi diversitas nonnulla: ergo cum idem non differat a se, patet, quod Christus non comedit spiritualiter, cum fuerit idem sibi.

4. Item, ubi est spiritualis manducatio, Sacramentum habet spiritualem effectum; sed in Christo non habuit talem effectum manducatio: ergo etc.

Item, quod non sacramentaliter manducaverit, videtur:

3. Quia manducare sacramentaliter est manducare sub velamine; sed Christus sub velamine non manducavit: ergo etc. Minor patet, quia aperte totum cognoscebat.

4. Item, si sacramentaliter tantum; cum iste sit modus debitus malis, ipse autem aut sumsit optimo modo, aut nullo modo sumsit; et iste modus non est optimus; ergo non convenit ei.

### Conclusio

> Christus eucharistiam sacramentaliter, non spiritualiter sumsit.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod quidam dicere voluerunt, quod nullo modo sumsit, quia sibi non competebat. Verumtamen nunc communiter dicunt, quod Christus manducavit sacramentaliter, sicut dicit Chrysostomus[^7], quod primus bibit, ne alii potum sanguinis abhorrerent.

Sed de spirituali quidam dicunt, quod sic; dicunt enim, quod fuit ibi unio per actum amoris, et effectus fuit non in se, sed in membris, ut, sicut Christus non sibi meruit, sed nobis, sic virtute illius manducationis meruit, ut membra corporis sui magis unirentur sibi. — Sed si istud verum esset, tunc haberet effectum in non manducante et etiam[^8] in dormiente. Amplius, ipso baptizato, non sunt alii baptizati. Postremo, spiritualis manducatio dicit intentionem unionis manducantis ad manducatum actu vel habitu; quorum neutrum fuit in Christo.

Propterea dicendum, quod sicut baptismum tantum sacramentaliter suscepit et non rem; sic et hoc Sacramentum; et hoc quidem in exemplum fecit.

<!-- page 207 -->

1. Ad illud ergo quod obiicitur, quod manducavit spiritualiter, quia fuit in eo cognitio et dilectio; dicendum, quod hoc non sufficit, nisi etiam sit ibi incorporatio, quae non potuit esse in ipso, quia non erat diversus ab eo cui incorporat ista manducatio.

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod erat viator; dicendum, quod verum est; sed nihilominus erat comprehensor perfectissimus et nullatenus poterat proficere, nec habitu nec fervoris motu. Unde esto, quod aliquis quantumcumque perfectus — etsi non beatus — recipiat digne Sacramentum; intenditur in eo caritas motu, vel habitu.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod non manducavit sacramentaliter, quia non sub velamine; dicendum, quod ad plenam rationem sacramentalis manducationis non oportet quod veniat signum tanquam *velans*, sed sufficit, ut veniat tanquam *significans*; quod autem velet, hoc est *per accidens*, quia oculus videns non est illuminatus plene; unde velando illuminat et illuminando velat[^9].

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod mali tantum comedunt sacramentaliter; dicendum, quod aliquis sacramentaliter tantum recipit, quia non est idoneus ad spiritualem efficaciam. Sed hoc potest esse duplici de causa: aut propter *gratiae* plenitudinem, et hoc perfectionis est et in solo Christo fuit: aut hoc est propter ipsius ineptitudinem et indignitatem, et sic est in peccatoribus. Et sic patet illud[^10].

## English

### Question IV. *Whether Christ ate in both modes.*

Fourthly it is asked, whether Christ ate by any whatever of the modes mentioned above. And that he did so sacramentally, it seems:

1. Leviticus, chapter eight[^1]: *And when [Moses dipped his finger] in the blood of the ram*; the Gloss of Hesychius[^2]: « First the priest, then the sons were dipped in the blood, because Christ in the Supper first took the blood, then gave it to the disciples ». And again in the same place the Gloss says: « He drank and gave to others to drink ».

2. Likewise, it seems by reason, because he received baptism as an example[^3]: therefore if this Sacrament is more arduous, then concerning this he ought rather to give an example.

Likewise, that he ate *spiritually*, it seems:

1. Because one eats spiritually through knowledge and love[^4]: but Christ knew and loved himself in the highest degree: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, Christ was still a wayfarer, therefore he still ought to use the viaticum; but the body of Christ is spiritually the viaticum[^5]: therefore etc.

**On the contrary**, that he did not eat spiritually, it seems:

3. Because where there is spiritual eating, there is union[^6]; but where there is union, there is some diversity: therefore since the same does not differ from itself, it is plain that Christ did not eat spiritually, since he was the same as himself.

4. Likewise, where there is spiritual eating, the Sacrament has a spiritual effect; but in Christ the eating did not have such an effect: therefore etc.

Likewise, that he did not eat sacramentally, it seems:

3. Because to eat sacramentally is to eat under a veil; but Christ did not eat under a veil: therefore etc. The minor is plain, because he knew the whole thing openly.

4. Likewise, if [he ate] only sacramentally; since this is the mode owed to the wicked, but he either took in the best mode, or took in no mode; and this mode is not the best; therefore it does not befit him.

### Conclusion

> Christ took the Eucharist sacramentally, not spiritually.

**I respond:** It must be said that some have wished to say that he took it in no mode, because it did not befit him. Yet now they commonly say that Christ ate sacramentally, as Chrysostom says[^7], that he drank first, lest the others should shrink from the drink of the blood.

But concerning the spiritual [eating] some say that he did so; for they say that there was there a union through an act of love, and the effect was not in himself, but in the members, so that, just as Christ did not merit for himself, but for us, so by the power of that eating he merited that the members of his body should be more united to himself. — But if this were true, then it would have its effect in one not eating and even[^8] in one sleeping. Furthermore, when he is baptized, others are not baptized. Finally, spiritual eating implies the intention of the union of the one eating with the one eaten, in act or in habit; neither of which was in Christ.

Therefore it must be said that, just as he received baptism only sacramentally and not the reality; so also this Sacrament; and this indeed he did as an example.

1. To that, then, which is objected, that he ate spiritually, because there was in him knowledge and love; it must be said that this does not suffice, unless there also be there incorporation, which could not be in him, because he was not diverse from that into which this eating incorporates.

2. To that which is objected, that he was a wayfarer; it must be said that it is true; but nonetheless he was the most perfect comprehensor and could in no way advance, neither in habit nor in motion of fervor. Hence granted that someone however perfect — even if not blessed — receives the Sacrament worthily; charity is intensified in him in motion, or in habit.

3. To that which is objected, that he did not eat sacramentally, because not under a veil; it must be said that for the full account of sacramental eating it is not required that the sign come as *veiling*, but it suffices that it come as *signifying*; that it veil, however, is *per accidens*, because the seeing eye is not fully illumined; whence by veiling it illumines and by illumining it veils[^9].

4. To that which is objected, that only the wicked eat sacramentally; it must be said that someone receives only sacramentally because he is not fit for spiritual efficacy. But this can be from a twofold cause: either on account of the fullness of *grace*, and this belongs to perfection and was in Christ alone: or this is on account of his own ineptitude and unworthiness, and thus it is in sinners. And so that [text] is plain[^10].
