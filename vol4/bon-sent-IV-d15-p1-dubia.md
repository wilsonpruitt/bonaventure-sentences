## *Dubia circa litteram Magistri*

## Latin

### DUBIA CIRCA LITTERAM MAGISTRI.

### Dub. I

In parte ista sunt dubitationes circa litteram, et primo de hoc quod dicit: *Non consurget duplex tribulatio.* Videtur enim contrarium: Ecclesiastici undecimo: *Error et tenebrae peccatoribus concreata sunt.* — Item, in Psalmo: *Induantur sicut diploide confusione sua.*[^1] — Item, in Psalmo: *Ignis et sulphur et spiritus procellarum, pars calicis eorum.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod tribulatio potest intelligi duplicari[^2] aut quantum ad *subiectum punitum,* aut quantum ad *modum puniendi,* aut secundum *proportionem ad meritum,* aut secundum *diversitatem status.* Dico igitur, quod tribus modis duplicatur. Aut duplicatur quantum ad *subiectum,* quia duplex est hominis pars, scilicet corpus et anima, et duplex vis in libero arbitrio peccante. Ideo punitur homo in anima et corpore, in ratione et voluntate; et iste est unus modus duplicandi. — Alio modo duplicatur quantum ad *modum puniendi,* quia per carentiam et calorem, per ignem et frigus, sicut peccavit per timorem et amorem[^3]. — Tertio modo duplicatur quantum ad *statum,* quia punitur quis in praesenti, et si est impoenitens, punitur in futuro. — Sed quantum ad *proportionem ad meritum* non consurget duplex tribulatio, quia semper Deus punit citra condignum; et hoc modo intelligitur auctoritas Prophetae. — Secundum alias

<!-- page 359 -->

vias multae inveniuntur auctoritates[^4], sic etiam est intelligenda expositio Hieronymi sequens.

### Dub. II

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Quinque modis flagella contingunt.* Videtur enim insufficienter tangere modos, cum multo plures assignari possint. Flagella enim sunt ad vitam aeternam ordinata et ad probationem et ad multa alia, quae hic non tanguntur: ergo videtur, quod sit insufficiens.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod utilitates flagellorum possunt accipi ex Scripturis in *generali,* vel in *speciali.* In *generali* omnes modi flagellandi ad hos reducuntur; et hoc patet, si consideretur sufficientia. Omne enim flagellum, quod contingit, aut est ordinatum ad *utilitatem flagellati,* aut ad *gloriam flagellantis*[^5]. Si ad *utilitatem flagellati;* aut ut *promoveatur* in bono, et sic primus modus, et est exemplum de Iob; aut ut *conservetur,* et sic secundus, et est exemplum in beato Paulo; aut ut *reducatur* ad bonum, et sic tertius, et est exemplum in Maria, sorore Moysi. — Aut ad *gloriam flagellantis,* et hoc dupliciter: aut ut manifestetur potentia in *miserendo,* et sic quartus, et est exemplum in caeco nato, quem Dominus illuminavit; aut ut manifestetur potentia in *iudicando,* et sic quintus, et est exemplum in Antiocho.

Aliter possunt distingui: quia omne flagellum aut est ordinatum *ad bonum,* aut *contra malum.* Si *ad bonum;* aut ad *promovendum,* aut ad *demonstrandum.* Si *contra malum;* aut *evitandum,* aut *corrigendum,* aut *vindicandum.* Et secundum hoc accipiuntur quinque modi. Unde versus:

> Iob probat, inclinat Paulum, purgatque Mariam, / Herodem punit, in caeco se manifestat.

Sunt et alii modi *speciales* et utilitates, qui his versibus continentur:

> Erudit et vertit, probat et laetum facit, arctat, / Confortat, satiat, consummat, deinde coronat.

*Erudit,* Ieremiae sexto[^6]: *Per omne flagellum erudieris, Ierusalem.* — *Vertit,* id est convertit, Psalmus: *Multiplicatae sunt infirmitates eorum, postea acceleraverunt.* — *Probat,* Ecclesiastici vigesimo septimo: *Vasa figuli probat fornax.* — *Laetum facit,* Iacobi primo: *Omne gaudium existimate* etc. — *Arctat,* Isaiae vigesimo octavo: *Vexatio dabit intellectum auditui.* — *Confortat,* Iob: *Haec sit mihi consolatio, ut affligens* etc. — *Satiat,* id est fecundat, Exodi primo: *Quanto opprimebant eos, tanto magis multiplicabantur.* — *Consummat,* Matthaei quinto: *Beati qui persecutionem patiuntur propter iustitiam, quoniam* etc. — *Coronat,* secundae ad Corinthios quarto: *Id quod in praesenti est momentaneum et leve tribulationis* etc. — Omnes autem ad praedictos modos reducuntur. Hic tanguntur flagellorum utilitates, quae paucioribus modis assignatis continentur, et amplius duo modi.

### Dub. III

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit in primo modo: *Ut iustis per patientiam merita augeantur.* Videtur enim male exemplificare, quia dicitur Iob: *Utinam appenderentur peccata mea, quibus iram merui.* — Item, Gregorius in Moralibus[^7]: quidquid mali patimur, pro peccatis nostris accidit... manifestum est».

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *causa* dupliciter dicitur, scilicet *proprie* et *communiter*[^8]. Communiter, et sic dicitur causa *sine qua non.* Accipiendo causam large pro causa *sine qua non,* sic poena dicitur venire ob meritum culpae originalis, quia nisi peccatum praecessisset, nulla esset. — Si autem accipiatur *proprie* pro causa *meritoria,* vel *finali;* sic non meruit Iob illam poenam. Et quod dicitur: *Utinam appenderentur,* hoc facit ad oppositum; quia, sicut ipse dicit contra, quod poena improportionaliter excedit meritum[^9].

<!-- page 360 -->

### Dub. IV

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit in ultimo modo: *Vel ad initium poenae, ut Herodi.* Videtur enim male dicere, quia Augustinus de Poenitentia[^10]: «Quidquid Deus permiserit pati, scias esse hominem corrigentis, non damnantis». — Item, quamdiu homo vivit, potest converti et potest mereri, et est in statu viae: ergo non debet inchoari poena.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod flagellum infligens habet comparationem ad *infligentem,* et ad eum cui infligitur. De *infligente* autem est loqui secundum voluntatem *antecedentem,* aut *consequentem*[^11]. Si quantum ad voluntatem *antecedentem,* omne quod Deus nobis facit et infligit in praesenti, ordinationem aliquam habet ad gloriam, quia secundum voluntatem istam *vult omnes salvos fieri.* Si autem quantum ad voluntatem *consequentem,* quia secundum hanc voluntatem vult, secundum quod praescit, et praescit istum in malitia perseveraturum; poenam dicitur infligere in vindictam, et secundum hoc est *ex* vi culpae. Similiter de *flagellato* est loqui dupliciter: aut quantum ad *statum,* et quia est in statu, in quo potest reverti; tam ipse quam poena aliquam habet ordinationem ad bonum. Aut contingit loqui quantum ad *obstinationem* animi, quae convertit benignitatem in iram et gratiam impugnat; et sic est in statu, in quo ei poena est in damnationem. Et sic patet utraque pars.

### Dub. V

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Non praeveniri sententiam iudicis* etc. Videtur enim male dicere: quia qui semel iudicatus est et punitus a sacerdote amplius non iudicabitur[^12] nec punietur: ergo sententia iudicis praevenitur.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod sacerdos aut absolvit non absolutum[^13] et imponit minorem poenam, quam debet; et sic iudicat sententia hominis et non iudicis superni; et hoc modo, si videatur praeveniri sententia iudicis, non tamen praevenitur, quia oportet, hominem puniri in foro Dei. — Aut sacerdos, servato recto ordine et quantitate, punit, et sententia sacerdotis est sententia Dei hic hominem absolventis; et tunc non praevenitur, quia ipse iudex iudicat per ministrum suum, cui commisit ministerium; quod tamen cassum est, nisi praecedat auctoritas[^14] Dei absolventis.

### Dub. VI

Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Levis culpa levi supplicio compensatur*; et exemplificat de Israelita, qui collegit ligna in Sabbato; et ulterius de illo qui Israelitae maledixerat, et qui etiam blasphemaverat; et videtur in utroque male exemplificare tum ratione *poenae,* tum ratione *culpae.* Ratione *poenae,* quia uterque occisus est et lapidatus: et haec est gravissima poenarum, scilicet poena mortis. — Item, de *culpa* videtur falsum, quia blasphemia est gravissimum peccatum. — *Praeterea,* transgressio mandati decalogi non est leve peccatum, maxime quia punitur poena mortis, quae erat poena debita enormissimis peccatis.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Hieronymus[^15] non vocat supplicium mortis *leve* absolute, sed in comparatione ad poenam aeternam; et *levem* culpam vocat tum propter hoc, quod semel perpetrata est, tum etiam, quia neutrum habebat deformitatem notabilem; et ideo, si uterque voluit poenitere[^16], de facili potuit misericordiam invenire et graves poenas evadere; et uterque illorum occisus fuit ex rigore Legis, quae data erat in *timorem.* — Et quod obiicitur de blasphemia, dicendum, quod secundum Richardum de sancto Victore[^17] non omnis blasphemia est peccatum in Spiritum sanctum, sed illa quae dicitur *spiritus blasphemiae,* quando quis voluntatem habet blasphemandi Deum. Sed illa quae dicitur ex quadam *surreptione* et animi *conturbatione,* non est semper inter peccata enormia reputanda, quamvis et ipsa grave possit esse peccatum[^18].

## English

### DOUBTS CONCERNING THE TEXT OF THE MASTER.

### Doubt I

In this section there are doubts concerning the text, and first about this, that he says: *A double tribulation shall not arise.* For it seems to the contrary: Ecclesiasticus the eleventh: *Error and darkness are created together with sinners.* — Likewise, in the Psalm: *Let them be clothed as with a double cloak with their own confusion.*[^1] — Likewise, in the Psalm: *Fire and brimstone and the spirit of storms shall be the portion of their cup.*

**I respond:** It must be said that tribulation can be understood to be doubled[^2] either as to the *subject punished,* or as to the *mode of punishing,* or according to *proportion to merit,* or according to *diversity of state.* I say therefore that it is doubled in three ways. Either it is doubled as to the *subject,* because the part of man is twofold, namely body and soul, and the power in the free choice that sins is twofold. Therefore man is punished in soul and body, in reason and will; and this is one mode of doubling. — In another way it is doubled as to the *mode of punishing,* because by privation and heat, by fire and frost, as he sinned by fear and love[^3]. — In a third way it is doubled as to *state,* because one is punished in the present, and if he is impenitent, he is punished in the future. — But as to *proportion to merit* a double tribulation shall not arise, because God always punishes short of what is deserved; and in this way the authority of the Prophet is understood. — According to other

ways many authorities are found[^4], and so too is the following exposition of Jerome to be understood.

### Doubt II

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *Scourges befall in five ways.* For it seems that he touches the modes insufficiently, since many more could be assigned. For scourges are ordained to eternal life and to probation and to many other things which are not touched here: therefore it seems that he is insufficient.

**I respond:** It must be said that the uses of scourges can be taken from the Scriptures in *general,* or in *particular.* In *general* all the modes of scourging are reduced to these; and this is clear if their sufficiency be considered. For every scourge that befalls is either ordained to the *use of the one scourged,* or to the *glory of the one scourging*[^5]. If to the *use of the one scourged;* either that he be *advanced* in good, and so the first mode, and there is the example of Job; or that he be *preserved,* and so the second, and there is the example in blessed Paul; or that he be *led back* to good, and so the third, and there is the example in Miriam, the sister of Moses. — Or to the *glory of the one scourging,* and this in two ways: either that power be manifested in *showing mercy,* and so the fourth, and there is the example in the man born blind, whom the Lord illumined; or that power be manifested in *judging,* and so the fifth, and there is the example in Antiochus.

Otherwise they can be distinguished: because every scourge is either ordained *to a good,* or *against an evil.* If *to a good;* either for *advancing,* or for *demonstrating.* If *against an evil;* either for *avoiding,* or for *correcting,* or for *avenging.* And according to this five modes are taken. Hence the verses:

> He proves Job, inclines Paul, and purges Mary, / He punishes Herod, in the blind man shows himself.

There are also other *special* modes and uses, which are contained in these verses:

> He instructs and turns, proves and makes glad, constrains, / Strengthens, satisfies, perfects, then at last crowns.

*He instructs,* Jeremiah the sixth[^6]: *By every scourge thou shalt be instructed, O Jerusalem.* — *He turns,* that is, converts, the Psalm: *Their infirmities were multiplied; afterwards they made haste.* — *He proves,* Ecclesiasticus the twenty-seventh: *The furnace tries the potter's vessels.* — *He makes glad,* James the first: *Count it all joy* etc. — *He constrains,* Isaiah the twenty-eighth: *Vexation shall give understanding to the hearing.* — *He strengthens,* Job: *Let this be my consolation, that afflicting* etc. — *He satisfies,* that is, makes fruitful, Exodus the first: *The more they oppressed them, the more they were multiplied.* — *He perfects,* Matthew the fifth: *Blessed are they that suffer persecution for justice' sake, for* etc. — *He crowns,* second to the Corinthians, the fourth: *That which is at present momentary and light of our tribulation* etc. — But all are reduced to the aforesaid modes. Here are touched the uses of scourges, which are contained in the fewer modes assigned, and two more besides.

### Doubt III

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says in the first mode: *That the merits of the just may be increased through patience.* For it seems that he exemplifies badly, because it is said in Job: *Would that my sins, by which I have earned wrath, were weighed!* — Likewise, Gregory in the *Morals*[^7]: whatever evil we suffer befalls us for our sins... it is manifest».

**I respond:** It must be said that *cause* is said in two ways, namely *properly* and *commonly*[^8]. Commonly, and thus it is called a cause *without which not.* Taking cause broadly for a cause *without which not,* thus punishment is said to come on account of the merit of original guilt, because unless sin had gone before, there would be none. — But if it be taken *properly* for a *meritorious* cause, or *final* cause; thus Job did not earn that punishment. And what is said: *Would that they were weighed,* this makes for the opposite; because, as he himself says to the contrary, that the punishment disproportionately exceeds the merit[^9].

### Doubt IV

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says in the last mode: *Or for the beginning of punishment, as for Herod.* For it seems to be said badly, because Augustine *On Penance*[^10]: «Whatever God permits one to suffer, know it to be of one correcting, not of one damning». — Likewise, as long as man lives, he can be converted and can merit, and is in the state of the wayfarer: therefore punishment ought not to be begun.

**I respond:** It must be said that the scourge inflicting has a relation to the *one inflicting,* and to him on whom it is inflicted. Now concerning the *one inflicting* there is speaking according to *antecedent* will, or *consequent* will[^11]. If as to *antecedent* will, everything that God does and inflicts upon us in the present has some ordination to glory, because according to that will *he wills all to be saved.* But if as to *consequent* will, since according to this will he wills according to what he foreknows, and he foreknows this man to be going to persevere in malice; he is said to inflict punishment in vengeance, and according to this it is *from* the force of guilt. Likewise concerning the *one scourged* there is speaking in two ways: either as to *state,* and because he is in a state in which he can return; both he himself and the punishment have some ordination to good. Or it happens to speak as to the *obstinacy* of soul, which turns kindness into wrath and impugns grace; and thus he is in a state in which punishment is unto damnation for him. And thus both sides are clear.

### Doubt V

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *That the sentence of the judge be not anticipated* etc. For it seems to be said badly: because he who has once been judged and punished by the priest will no more be judged[^12] nor punished: therefore the judge's sentence is anticipated.

**I respond:** It must be said that the priest either absolves one not absolved[^13] and imposes a lesser penalty than he ought; and thus he judges by the sentence of man and not of the supernal judge; and in this way, if the judge's sentence seem to be anticipated, yet it is not anticipated, because it is needful that man be punished in the court of God. — Or the priest, the right order and quantity being kept, punishes, and the sentence of the priest is the sentence of God here absolving the man; and then it is not anticipated, because the judge himself judges through his minister, to whom he committed the ministry; which yet is void, unless the authority[^14] of God absolving go before.

### Doubt VI

Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *A light fault is compensated by a light penalty*; and he exemplifies of the Israelite who gathered wood on the Sabbath; and further of him who had cursed the Israelite, and who had also blasphemed; and it seems that in both he exemplifies badly, both by reason of the *penalty,* and by reason of the *fault.* By reason of the *penalty,* because each was killed and stoned: and this is the gravest of penalties, namely the penalty of death. — Likewise, concerning the *fault* it seems false, because blasphemy is the gravest sin. — *Moreover,* transgression of the precept of the decalogue is not a light sin, especially because it is punished with the penalty of death, which was the penalty due to the most enormous sins.

**I respond:** It must be said that Jerome[^15] does not call the punishment of death *light* absolutely, but in comparison to eternal punishment; and he calls the fault *light* both on account of this, that it was perpetrated once, and also because neither had a notable deformity; and therefore, if each had been willing to repent[^16], he could easily have found mercy and escaped grave penalties; and each of them was killed by the rigor of the Law, which had been given for *fear.* — And as for what is objected concerning blasphemy, it must be said that according to Richard of Saint Victor[^17] not every blasphemy is a sin against the Holy Spirit, but that which is called the *spirit of blasphemy,* when one has the will of blaspheming God. But that which is said to be from a certain *surprise* and *disturbance* of soul is not always to be reckoned among enormous sins, although it too can be a grave sin[^18].

**Dubia count: 6.**
- **Dub. I** — On the Master's *Non consurget duplex tribulatio:* but Scripture seems to speak of a *double* punishment (*diploide,* fire+brimstone). Answer: tribulation is "doubled" in three real ways (subject: soul+body, reason+will; mode: fire+frost; state: present+future), but as to *proportion to merit* it never doubles, since God punishes short of desert — which is the Prophet's sense; so too Jerome's exposition.
- **Dub. II** — On *Quinque modis flagella contingunt:* are five modes sufficient? Answer: in *general* every scourge serves the use of the one scourged (advance/preserve/lead-back — Job/Paul/Miriam) or the glory of the scourger (mercy/judgment — the blind man/Antiochus); a second division (to-good vs against-evil) also yields five. The mnemonic verses (*Iob probat...; Erudit et vertit...*) gather the special modes; all reduce to the five.
- **Dub. III** — On *Ut iustis per patientiam merita augeantur:* but Job says his sins outweigh his sufferings (penalty exceeds merit). Answer: *cause* is taken commonly (= *sine qua non:* original guilt) or properly (meritorious/final); Job did not *properly* merit that penalty, and his complaint that the penalty disproportionately exceeds the merit makes for the opposite side.
- **Dub. IV** — On *Vel ad initium poenae, ut Herodi:* but Augustine says God's scourge corrects, does not damn, and the living man is still a wayfarer. Answer: distinguish the *one inflicting* (antecedent will = ordained to glory, *vult omnes salvos fieri;* consequent will = vindictive, *ex vi culpae,* foreseeing perseverance in malice) and the *one scourged* (in a state of return = ordained to good; in obstinacy = unto damnation).
- **Dub. V** — On *Non praeveniri sententiam iudicis:* but one judged/punished by the priest is not judged again, so the divine sentence IS anticipated. Answer: if the priest absolves the unabsolved / under-penances, that is man's sentence not God's, and the man is still punished in God's court (no real anticipation); if the priest keeps right order, his sentence IS God's, given through his minister — but void without the prior authority of God absolving.
- **Dub. VI** — On *Levis culpa levi supplicio compensatur* (the Sabbath wood-gatherer; the blasphemer): both were stoned (gravest penalty) for grave faults. Answer: Jerome calls death "light" only relative to *eternal* punishment, and the fault "light" because once-committed and without notable deformity (had they repented, mercy was easy; they died from the rigor of the Law given *in timorem*). And per Richard of St. Victor, not every blasphemy is sin against the Holy Spirit — only the deliberate *spiritus blasphemiae;* blasphemy from *surreptio* / disturbance of soul is not always enormous, though still grave.
