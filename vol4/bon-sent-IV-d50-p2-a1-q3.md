### Quaestio III. *Utrum Beati et damnati mutuo se cognoscant.*

> **On the footnote numbering.** Quaracchi restarts its footnote numbering on every printed page. The markers below are therefore page-qualified: notes 1–11 of page 1049, then notes 1–3 of page 1050. Notes 4–10 of page 1050 anchor in Articulus II, quaestio I, and are rendered there.

Tertio quaeritur de cognitione animae separatae per comparationem ad statum Beatorum; et quaeritur, utrum mutuo se cognoscant, scilicet damnati Beatos, et e converso. Et quod sic, videtur:

1. Per illud Lucae decimo sexto[^p1049-1], quod dives positus in tormentis vidit Abraham, et Abraham e converso vidit ipsum.

2. Item, Gregorius[^p1049-2]: « Infideles in imo positi fideles super se in requie attendunt ».

3. Item, *ratione* videtur: quia opposita, sibi invicem comparata et simul visa, magis elucescunt[^p1049-3] et etiam decorant universum: ergo si mali habebunt poenam eatenus intensam, qua potest cogitari, et similiter boni gloriam, et utraque intenditur respectu alterius; ergo utrique debent mutuo se videre.

4. Item, constat, quod boni gaudebunt et laudabunt Dei iustitiam, mali similiter dolebunt de gloria amissa: ergo si non contingit gaudere et dolere nisi de cognito[^p1049-4], utrique cognoscent, scilicet boni severitatem iustitiae, et mali dignitatem gloriae. Sed boni hoc non vident in se, nec mali in se: ergo constat, quod vident invicem.

**Contra:** 1. Inter eos est improportionalis distantia: ergo si ad visionem requiritur praesentia, videtur, quod non possint mutuo se videre[^p1049-5].

2. Item, gloria est qualitas spiritualis, sicut gratia[^p1049-6]; sed qualitas gratiae non videtur exterius a non habente: ergo nec gloriae.

3. Item, consideratio poenae incutit terrorem, et consideratio gloriae generat gaudium; sed in malis nullum erit gaudium, in bonis nullus terror: ergo etc. — *Si dicas,* quod « recipitur secundum modum recipientis, non recepti », et ideo in malis generat confusionem et in bonis gaudium; ergo si haec duo debent esse post iudicium, ergo post se cognoscent; quod est contra Gregorium, ut habetur in littera[^p1049-7].

4. Item, in bonis est compassio de bonitate naturae: ergo si non tollitur bonitas naturae, ergo maxime vigebit in eis compassio: ergo videtur, quodsi videant tormenta malorum, quod maxime doleant. Et hoc videtur Gregorius dicere, et habetur in littera[^p1049-8], quod « volunt transire per affectum misericordiae ».

### Conclusio

> *Electi damnatorum poenas et nunc et post iudicium vident in Deo ad gloriae cumulum; damnati vero electorum gloriam nunc aliquatenus considerant, non vero post iudicium.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod consideratio sive cognitio gloriae Beatorum est *reprobis* poena, et consideratio sive cognitio poenae reproborum est *electis* gaudium; quia illi considerant *gloriam* sub ratione *amissionis*, isti *poenam* sub ratione *divinae ultionis*; unde[^p1049-9]: *Laetabitur iustus, cum viderit vindictam.* Quoniam igitur gloria electorum semper manet et post iudicium erit copiosor; ideo electi et nunc et tunc vident poenam reproborum, secundum quod dicitur ultimo Isaiae; vident, inquam, in Deo, sive in speculo aeterno, cum faciat ad eorum gloriam cumulandam. Reproborum poena similiter post iudicium intendetur, et adeo intendetur, ut Beatorum gloriam non liceat eis considerare nec ab eis auxilium petere, ut dives petiit[^p1049-10]; quia tormentorum suorum magnitudinem non poterunt sustinere, ita quod liceat aliis vacare, immo absorbebuntur totaliter a poenis. — Concedendae ergo sunt rationes, quod mutuo se cognoscunt.

1. Ad illud quod obiicitur in contrarium, quod est inter eos improportionalis distantia; dicendum, quod non agnoscunt *mali* bonos per intuitum praesentiae, sed per considerationem intelligentiae, *boni* vero malos in speculo et in Deo[^p1049-11].

<!-- page 1050 -->

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur de gloria, dicendum, quod non vident ipsam gloriae *essentiam*, sed considerant *requiem*; quia cognoscunt, quod gaudent[^p1050-1], sed ipsi laborant et dolent; et cognoscunt, sicut infirmus cognoscit sanitatem alterius, quam non habet.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod generat dolorem etc.; patet responsio; quia consideratio *bonorum* non est in poena, quia *afflictiva*, sed fertur ulterius, quia *iusta et optime et digne inflicta*; et talis consideratio amanti[^p1050-2] generat gaudium. Similiter nec consideratio *malorum* sistit in bono gloriae, quia *bonum*, sed quia *perditum*; et haec consideratio generat dolorem. — Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod post iudicium videre debent etc.; dicendum, quod tunc maiori poena erunt absorpti, ut iam non liceat eis cogitare gloriae dignitatem; semper tamen in mente habebunt eius amissionem et carentiam, ut tristentur.

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod compati est de bonitate naturae; dicendum, quod hoc non est verum generaliter, nisi intelligatur de natura passibili[^p1050-3].

## English

### Question III. *Whether the blessed and the damned know one another.*

Thirdly there is asked, concerning the knowledge of the separated soul by comparison with the state of the blessed; and it is asked whether they know one another, namely the damned the blessed, and conversely. And that they do, it seems:

1. By that text of Luke, chapter sixteen[^p1049-1], that the rich man set in torments saw Abraham, and Abraham conversely saw him.

2. Likewise, Gregory[^p1049-2]: « The unbelieving, set in the depth, look upon the faithful above them in rest ».

3. Likewise, it seems *by reason*: because opposites, compared with one another and seen together, shine out the more[^p1049-3] and also adorn the universe: therefore if the wicked will have a punishment intense to the degree that can be conceived, and likewise the good their glory, and each is intensified in respect of the other; therefore both ought to see one another mutually.

4. Likewise, it is agreed that the good will rejoice and will praise God's justice, and the wicked likewise will grieve over the glory they have lost: therefore, if it does not happen that one rejoices and grieves except over what is known[^p1049-4], both will know, namely the good the severity of justice, and the wicked the dignity of glory. But the good do not see this in themselves, nor the wicked in themselves: therefore it is agreed that they see one another.

**On the contrary:** 1. Between them there is a disproportionate distance: therefore, if presence is required for vision, it seems that they cannot see one another mutually[^p1049-5].

2. Likewise, glory is a spiritual quality, just as grace is[^p1049-6]; but the quality of grace is not seen from without by one who does not have it: therefore neither is that of glory.

3. Likewise, the consideration of punishment strikes terror, and the consideration of glory begets joy; but in the wicked there will be no joy, in the good no terror: therefore etc. — *If you say* that « a thing is received according to the mode of the receiver, not of the received », and that therefore in the wicked it begets confusion and in the good joy; therefore if these two must be after the judgement, therefore afterwards they will know one another; which is against Gregory, as is had in the text[^p1049-7].

4. Likewise, in the good there is compassion arising from the goodness of nature: therefore if the goodness of nature is not taken away, therefore compassion will be most vigorous in them: therefore it seems that, if they should see the torments of the wicked, they would grieve exceedingly. And this Gregory seems to say, and it is had in the text[^p1049-8], that « they wish to pass over through the affection of mercy ».

### Conclusion

> *The elect see the punishments of the damned both now and after the judgement in God, unto the increase of their glory; but the damned now to some extent consider the glory of the elect, yet not after the judgement.*

**I respond:** It must be said that the consideration or knowledge of the glory of the blessed is a punishment *to the reprobate*, and the consideration or knowledge of the punishment of the reprobate is a joy *to the elect*; because the former consider *glory* under the aspect of *loss*, the latter *punishment* under the aspect of *divine vengeance*; whence[^p1049-9]: *The just man shall rejoice when he shall see the vengeance.* Since therefore the glory of the elect always remains and after the judgement will be more abundant; therefore the elect both now and then see the punishment of the reprobate, according to what is said in the last chapter of Isaiah; they see it, I say, in God, or in the eternal mirror, since it makes for the heaping up of their glory. The punishment of the reprobate likewise will be intensified after the judgement, and will be so intensified that it will not be permitted them to consider the glory of the blessed nor to seek help from them, as the rich man sought[^p1049-10]; because they will not be able to endure the magnitude of their own torments, so as to be free for other things; rather they will be wholly absorbed by their punishments. — The arguments are therefore to be conceded, that they know one another mutually.

1. To that which is objected on the contrary, that there is between them a disproportionate distance; it must be said that *the wicked* do not recognize the good by the intuition of presence, but by the consideration of the understanding, whereas *the good* recognize the wicked in the mirror and in God[^p1049-11].

2. To that which is objected concerning glory, it must be said that they do not see the very *essence* of glory, but consider *the rest*; because they know that the others rejoice[^p1050-1], while they themselves labour and grieve; and they know it as a sick man knows the health of another, which he does not have.

3. To that which is objected, that it begets grief etc.; the answer is plain; because the consideration *of good things* does not rest in the punishment, as *afflictive*, but is carried further, as *just and most excellently and worthily inflicted*; and such a consideration begets joy in one who loves[^p1050-2]. Likewise neither does the consideration *of evil things* rest in the good of glory as *good*, but as *lost*; and this consideration begets grief. — To that which is objected, that after the judgement they must see etc.; it must be said that then they will be absorbed by a greater punishment, so that it will no longer be permitted them to think upon the dignity of glory; yet they will always have in mind its loss and privation, so that they may be saddened.

4. To that which is objected, that to suffer with another belongs to the goodness of nature; it must be said that this is not true universally, unless it be understood of a nature capable of suffering[^p1050-3].
