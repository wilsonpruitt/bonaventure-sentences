## Latin

## Commentarius in Distinctionem XV
### Pars II.

*Respondetur rationibus pro praedicto errore ex auctoritatibus Sanctorum sumtis.*

**Alias quoque auctoritates inducunt etc.**

> TEXTUM MAGISTRI VIDE SUPRA PAG. 346.

### Divisio textus.

Supra respondit Magister rationi sumtae ex sacra Scriptura. In hac parte respondet rationibus sumtis ex auctoritatibus Sanctorum et consuetudine Ecclesiae. Habet autem haec pars quatuor. In prima ponit rationes, quae videntur facere ad huius erroris defensionem. In secunda vero ponit veras responsiones, ibi: *His responderi potest* etc. In tertia improbat falsam, et quamvis probabilem[^1] responsionem, ibi: *Quibusdam tamen videtur satisfactio* etc. In quarta ad veritatis confirmationem et erroris confutationem adducit auctoritates probantes, quod non possit fieri poenitentia de uno peccato, si quis permaneat in alio, ibi: *Satis illis arbitror esse responsum.*

Quaelibet autem partium posset subdividi in duas partes. Nam in prima ponit duas rationes. In secunda ponit duas responsiones ad praedictas rationes. In tertia primo ponit aliorum responsionem, deinde improbat. In quarta primo confirmat veritatem per auctoritates, secundo epilogat. Partes autem cuilibet inspicienti patent.

### Tractatio quaestionum.

Ad intelligentiam huius partis, quia supra[^2] quaesitum est de potentia satisfaciendi, quaeritur hic de modo. Et circa hoc duo principaliter quaeruntur.

Primo quaeritur de modo in generali.

Secundo quaeritur de modo in speciali.

Circa primum quaeruntur quatuor.

Primo quaeritur, quid sit satisfactio.

Secundo, cui sit satisfaciendum.

Tertio, per qualia opera.

Quarto, per quae.

### Articulus I.
*De modo satisfaciendi in generali.*

## English

## Commentary on Distinction XV
### Part II.

*A reply is made to the reasons for the aforesaid error, drawn from the authorities of the Saints.*

**They also adduce other authorities, etc.**

> FOR THE TEXT OF THE MASTER SEE ABOVE, PAGE 346.

### Division of the text.

Above the Master replied to the reason drawn from sacred Scripture. In this part he replies to the reasons drawn from the authorities of the Saints and the custom of the Church. Now this part has four [subdivisions]. In the first he sets down the reasons which seem to make for the defense of this error. In the second he sets down the true replies, there: *To these it can be replied* etc. In the third he disproves the false, and however probable[^1] reply, there: *Yet to some it seems that satisfaction* etc. In the fourth, toward the confirmation of the truth and the refutation of the error, he adduces authorities proving that penance cannot be done for one sin if one remains in another, there: *I think this is enough of a reply for them.*

Now each of the parts could be subdivided into two parts. For in the first he sets down two reasons. In the second he sets down two replies to the aforesaid reasons. In the third he first sets down the reply of others, then disproves it. In the fourth he first confirms the truth through authorities, second he sums up. The parts are evident to anyone who inspects them.

### Treatment of the questions.

For the understanding of this part, since above[^2] it was inquired about the power of making satisfaction, here it is inquired about the manner. And concerning this two things are principally inquired.

First it is asked about the manner in general.

Second it is asked about the manner in particular.

Concerning the first, four things are asked.

First it is asked what satisfaction is.

Second, to whom satisfaction is to be made.

Third, by what sort of works.

Fourth, by what means.

### Article I.
*On the manner of making satisfaction in general.*
