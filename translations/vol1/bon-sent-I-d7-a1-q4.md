Likewise, when I say "a creature is able to be created," I am not saying anything created; because that is truly said of what is altogether nothing, as of the soul of the Antichrist: therefore I am only expressing the power of the agent; but the power of God is single, having no multiplicity whatsoever: therefore his power is univocal with respect to the power to be created and the power to be generated.

---

*The power to be generated and the power to be created are in reality the same with respect to their principal signification and are said univocally; but with respect to what they connote, they are said analogically.*

I respond: It must be said that when I say "the power to be created" and "the power to be generated," I express both a principal signification and a connotation. With respect to the principal signification, I say that the power to be created expresses in reality the same thing as the power to create, differing only in mode of expression: because what is expressed by way of action through "the power to create" is expressed by way of passion through "the power to be created." But with respect to what is connoted, it expresses an effect in the creature.

When therefore the power to be created and the power to be generated are compared, if they are compared by reason of the principal signification, I say that they are not merely univocal but indeed identical, as has been seen, yet understood and expressed in different ways — just as the power to generate and the power to create are. But if they are compared by reason of what is connoted, then I concede that they are analogical, just as the name *res* is with respect to what is enjoyable and what is useful. For although Creator and creature have no univocal common term, they nonetheless have an analogical one.

It must be noted, however, that analogy is twofold. One is by reduction to a unity of nature according to prior and posterior; and this can obtain in comparing creature to creature, and in this case a sign distributes for all things contained under it, unless the distribution is restricted by an addition or by usage or by mode of expression — as in the case of what is called an accommodated distribution, as when it is said "the heavens cover all things." The other is analogy by reduction to a unity of proportional likeness, not of nature; and what is analogized in this way is not among other things but above them. Hence a sign does not properly distribute for it unless it is extended. Thus God is not a being among all things but above all things.

And from this it follows that "omnipotent" does not in a proper distribution distribute for the power of generating — unless a certain extension is made, and from that extension a certain appropriation follows with respect to the supposit. Hence "omnipotent" properly belongs to the three persons, because it does not distribute for the power of generating; but by appropriation, according as an ampliation is made on the part of the sign, it belongs to the Father alone.

From these considerations all the objections are answered; for they proceed along different lines, as is evident to one who examines them.

### Notes

- **Chunk structure**: This chunk completes Q.IV, which is the final question of Distinction VII's sole article. The *sed contra* arguments 2–3 from the preceding chunk are here resolved, and the full *Respondeo* with its two *Conclusiones* is given. There are no individual numbered replies; the final sentence ("from these considerations all the objections are answered") serves as a collective dismissal.

- **"principal signification" vs. "connotation" (*principale significatum* / *connotatum*)**: The same distinction deployed in Q.III reply 4 is here applied to the passive sense. The *principale significatum* of "the power to be created" is simply the divine power itself (identical for all divine operations); the *connotatum* is the creaturely effect or terminus, which introduces the analogical difference.

- **"the power to be created expresses in reality the same as the power to create"**: A striking claim: the active and passive formulations refer to identically the same divine power, differing only grammatically (action vs. passion). This follows from divine simplicity — there is no real passive potency in God, only the one omnipotence expressed from different vantage points.

- **"soul of the Antichrist"**: A standard scholastic example for something that is genuinely nothing yet can be spoken of as a possible object of divine power. When one says "the Antichrist's soul can be created," nothing actual is being referred to; the statement concerns only the divine power as its ground. This supports the claim that "the power to be created" signifies primarily the agent's power, not any property of the recipient.

- **Two kinds of analogy**: Bonaventure here articulates a distinction between:
  (1) *Analogy of prior and posterior* (*per reductionem ad unitatem naturae*): applies within creaturely or categorical comparisons; the analogized term distributes normally for all instances, subject to contextual restriction. Example: "healthy" predicated of organism, urine, diet.
  (2) *Analogy of proportional likeness* (*per reductionem ad unitatem similitudinis proportionalis*): applies when one term is not *among* but *above* the others; proper distribution does not extend to it without explicit extension. Example: God as "being" — he is not a being among beings but above being.
  This twofold distinction anticipates the more developed treatments of analogy in later scholasticism (Cajetan's *analogia proportionis* vs. *analogia attributionis*), though Bonaventure's formulation is his own.

- **"omnipotent" and appropriation**: "Omnipotent" properly distributes over the three persons equally (since it regards the common essence), but does not in a *proper* distribution include the power of generating (which is personal and proper to the Father). Only by *extensio* — an ampliation of the term's range — and subsequent *appropriatio* does "omnipotent Father" come to connote the power of generating specifically. This resolves Q.IV's third negative argument, which claimed that if "omnipotent" does not distribute for the power to generate, then the Son (who lacks that power) cannot be omnipotent — a false inference, since "omnipotent" does not include the personal power of generating in its proper distribution at all.

- **"accommodated distribution" (*distributio accommoda*)**: A technical term from medieval logic for a distribution that is contextually or idiomatically restricted, not reflecting the term's full logical extension. Example: "the heavens cover all things" — "all things" is accommodated to terrestrial objects, not a universal quantification. Rendered with the technical term retained.

- **Conclusion of Distinction VII**: With Q.IV, the Sole Article of Distinction VII is complete. The four questions have treated: (1) whether *potentia generandi* is absolute or relative; (2) whether it is in the Son; (3) whether it is identical with the power to create; (4) whether the power to be generated and the power to be created are univocal. Distinction VIII will take up the next topic in the Lombard's sequence.
