# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. IV, Vers. 38-44
## *Surgens Iesus de synagoga. Post miraculosam curationem sexus virilis a daemone sequitur hic miraculosa curatio sexus muliebris a febre*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 107 -->

79. *Surgens Iesus de synagoga.* Post miraculosam curationem sexus virilis a daemone sequitur hic miraculosa curatio *sexus muliebris a febre;* quae ab Evangelista describitur hoc ordine. Primo notatur ipsius *febricitationis incommodum;* secundo vero, *intercessionis adiutorium;* tertio, *curationis remedium;* quarto, *perfectae liberationis indicium.* Haec enim quatuor concurrerunt ad hoc miraculum ostendendum[^p107-12].

80. (Vers. 38.). Quantum igitur ad *febricitationis incommodum,* quod compellebat socrum Simonis iacere in domo, dicitur: *Surgens autem Iesus de*
<!-- page 108 -->
*synagoga, introivit in domum Simonis,* ut potestas eius, quae communiter manifestata fuerat coram omnibus, in domo secretius manifestaretur coram simplicibus et coram suis imitatoribus. In domo erat *mulier* curanda, non in synagoga, ad insinuandum quod dicitur primae ad Timotheum secundo[^p108-1]: « Docere autem mulieri non permitto ». — Ad hunc ingressum movebat misericordia propter mulieris miseriam relevandam; quam exprimit, cum dicit: *Socrus autem Simonis tenebatur magnis febribus;* in quo designatur infirmitatis gravitas, quae disponit ad gratiam, iuxta illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo primo[^p108-2]: « Infirmitas gravis sobriam facit animam ». Licet autem esset gravis, poterat tamen de ea dici illud Ioannis undecimo: « Infirmitas haec non est ad mortem, sed ut manifestetur gloria Dei »; quam gloriam non solum debuit in altero sexu ostendere, sed in utroque, quia, sicut uterque lapsus fuerat, ita pertinebat ad redemptionem. Unde in Christi *nativitate* fuit Ioseph cum Maria[^p108-3]; in Christi *praedicatione,* discipuli et mulieres, secundum quod dicitur infra octavo; in *passione,* Maria et Ioannes, Ioannis decimo nono; in *resurrectione,* discipuli et Magdalena, Ioannis vigesimo. Sic vir et mulier ad Dei gloriam ostendendam curantur, et a graviori mulier quam vir, quia pluribus poenalitatibus est subiecta; Genesis tertio: « Multiplicabo aerumnas tuas et conceptus tuos; in dolore paries filios et sub viri potestate eris ».

81. Quantum vero ad *intercessionis suffragium* subditur: *Et rogaverunt illum pro illa*[^p108-4], discipuli et illi qui aderant. Quamvis enim Dominus velit dare, nihilominus vult rogari, secundum illud Psalmi: « In die tribulationis tuae invoca me; eruam te, et honorificabis me »; Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo: « Invocabis, et Dominus exaudiet; clamabis, et dicet: Ecce adsum », « quia misericors sum, Dominus Deus tuus ».

82. Et nota, quod Dominus aliquem curavit *sine precibus,* sicut daemoniacum prius[^p108-5]; alium *precibus alienis,* ut hic; alium *precibus propriis,* ut infra quinto leprosum. In quo significatur, quod tripliciter animas peccatrices curat gratia Salvatoris, nunc intercedente *sola benignitate suae clementiae;* ad Romanos secundo[^p108-6]: « An ignoras, quod Dei benignitas ad poenitentiam te adducit »? Nunc interveniente *oratione Ecclesiae;* Iacobi quinto: « Infirmatur quis in vobis? Inducat presbyteros Ecclesiae, et orent super eum, et oratio fidei salvabit infirmum ». Nunc vero interveniente *proprio gemitu;* Exodi sexto[^p108-7]: « Ego audivi gemitum filiorum Israel »; quarti Regum vigesimo: « Audivi orationem tuam et vidi lacrymam tuam et sanavi te ». Et ideo dicebat anima sancta in Psalmo: « Ego autem ad Dominum clamavi, et Dominus salvavit me ».

83. (Vers. 39.). Quantum ad *curationis remedium* adiungitur: *Et stans super illam, imperavit febri, et dimisit illam.* Imperat autem tanquam dominus et rex, sicut Ecclesiastae quinto[^p108-8]: « Insuper rex universae terrae imperat servienti »; et quia « non est ei impossibile omne verbum »; ideo « dixit, et factum est ». Unde ad praeceptum eius recessit febris, secundum illud Psalmi: « Praeceptum posuit, et non praeteribit ». Nec mirum, quia[^p108-9] « omnia, quaecumque voluit, fecit in caelo et in terra, in mari et in omnibus abyssis »; et Ieremiae quinto: « Me ergo non timebitis, ait Dominus, et a facie mea non pavebitis? Qui posui arenam terminum mari, praeceptum sempiternum, quod non praeteribit ». Unde miro modo in hoc arguitur et reprehensibilis ostenditur pervicacia humanae voluntatis, quod omnis creatura sensibilis, et quod plus est, defectus poenalis obedit divinis imperiis, quibus homo miserabilis contradicit.

84. Postremo quantum ad *perfectae liberationis indicium* subditur: *Et continuo surgens ministrabat*[^p108-10]. Hoc enim certum est perfectae curationis indicium, quod nullae remanserunt reliquiae, pro eo quod statim facta est potens ad motum et potens ad obsequium. Unde surrexit et ministrabat tanquam bona matrona et quasi altera Martha, de qua infra decimo[^p108-11]: « Martha autem satagebat circa frequens ministerium ». Et tales merentur divinum beneficium; quarti Regum quarto dicit Eliseus Sunamiti: « Ecce, sedule in omnibus ministrasti nobis, quid vis, ut faciam tibi »? Talem viduam dicebat Apostolus esse eligendam primae ad Timotheum quinto: « Vidua eligatur non minus sexaginta annorum, in operibus
<!-- page 109 -->
bonis testimonium habens, si filios educavit, si hospitio recepit, si sanctorum pedes lavit, si tribulationem patientibus subministravit, si omne opus bonum subsecuta est ».

85. Notandum autem est hic quantum ad sensum *allegoricum,* quod curatio *viri a daemone* significat curationem gentilis populi ab errore; in cuius rei figuram dicitur Isaiae trigesimo quarto[^p109-1]: « Occurrent ibi daemonia, onocentaurus et pilosus clamabit alter ad alterum ». Hoc dicitur de Idumaea sub figura gentilitatis. Hoc daemonium eiicitur per fidem Christi, secundum illud Marci ultimo[^p109-2]: « In nomine meo daemonia eiicient, linguis loquentur novis, serpentes tollent » etc.; illos *serpentes* diffundentes virus erroris, sicut serpens, de quo secundae ad Corinthios undecimo: « Timeo, ne, sicut serpens Evam seduxit astutia sua, ita corrumpantur sensus vestri et excidant a simplicitate, quae est in Christo ». — Per curationem *socrus Simonis a febre* significatur curatio synagogae ab aestu carnalis concupiscentiae, quo inardescit ille populus carnalis; Osee septimo[^p109-3]: « Omnes adulterantes sicut clibanus succensus a coquente ». Et ideo praemittitur curatio viri, quia, sicut dicitur ad Romanos undecimo, « cum plenitudo gentium intraverit, tunc omnis Israel salvus fiet ».

86. Secundum sensum *moralem* curatio *hominis a daemone* significat curationem a *peccatis spiritualibus,* secundum quod dicitur primi Regum decimo sexto[^p109-4]: « Spiritus nequam a Domino vexabat Saul »; et infra undecimo: « Cum immundus spiritus exierit ab homine, ambulat » etc. — Curatio *mulieris a febre* est curatio animae a *peccatis carnalibus;* Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio[^p109-5]: « Anima calida quasi ignis ardens, non exstinguetur, donec aliquid glutiat ». Unde Beda: « *Homo a daemonio* liberatur, cum animus ab immunda cogitatione purgatur; *femina a febribus,* cum caro a fervore concupiscentiae refrenatur ». Et ideo homo in medio synagogae eliditur, quia qui spiritualibus peccatis detinetur indiget humiliari in conspectu hominum; Psalmus[^p109-6]: « Custodiens parvulos Dominus »; ista vero, Domino stante super eam in domo, quia necessaria est cum divina gratia custodia disciplinae; Ecclesiastici vigesimo sexto: « In filia non avertente se firma custodiam, ne, inventa occasione, abutatur se »; et
ideo primae ad Corinthios nono: « Castigo corpus meum et in servitutem redigo » etc.

87. (Vers. 40). *Cum autem sol occidisset* etc. Hic post curationem sexus virilis et muliebris subiungitur *curatio universalis* respectu omnis sexus, aetatis et infirmitatis. In qua manifestatur admirabilis potentia Christi ex quatuor, scilicet ex *varietate languorum,* ex *facilitate curationum,* ex *confessione daemonum,* ex *cohibitione spirituum immundorum.*

Primo igitur introducitur *varietas languorum* in hoc quod dicitur: *Cum autem sol occidisset, qui*[^p109-7] *habebant infirmos variis languoribus ducebant illos ad eum,* ut scilicet curaret; et merito, quia, Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio, « vere languores nostros ipse tulit » etc.; et Actuum decimo: « Pertransiit benefaciendo et sanando omnes oppressos a diabolo »; et sine distinctione, quia super omnia poterat aequaliter; unde Sapientiae decimo sexto[^p109-8]: « Non herba neque malagma sanavit eos, sed tuus, Domine, sermo, qui sanat omnia »; unde et Matthaei quarto dicitur, quod « sanabat omnem languorem et omnem infirmitatem ».

88. Et nota, quod adducebant *post solis occasum,* ut ostenderetur, quod Christus non tantum de die, sed etiam de nocte sollicitus et potens erat circa salutem humani generis. Quod fuit figuratum Genesis trigesimo primo[^p109-9] in Iacob: « Die noctuque aestu urebar et gelu, fugiebatque somnus ab oculis meis »; et Sapientiae ultimo: « In omnibus magnificasti populum tuum, Domine, et exaltasti, in omni tempore et in omni loco assistens eis ». — Vel ideo *post solis occasum,* ut ostenderet, vitandam esse gloriam hominum; unde reprehendit Pharisaeos Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p109-10], quia « faciunt omnia opera sua, ut videantur ab hominibus ». — Aut ideo *post solis occasum,* ut designetur, per *passionem* acquirendum esse generale remedium. Christus enim sol est, secundum illud Malachiae quarto[^p109-11]: « Orietur vobis timentibus nomen meum sol iustitiae »; iste *occubuit* in passione; unde Amos octavo: « Occidet eis sol in meridie »; et tunc plures sanati sunt, quod figuratum est Iudicum decimo sexto in morte Samsonis, qui interpretatur *sol eorum.* Unde Beda[^p109-12]: « Solis occubitus est passio Christi, per quam plures sanantur daemoniaci et aegroti quam ante ».

<!-- page 110 -->
89. Secundo additur *facilitas curationum,* cum additur: *At ille, singulis manus imponens, curabat omnes*[^p110-1]. Faciliter curabat, quia sine interpellatione, sed sola manus impositione sanabat. Nec mirum, quia, secundum quod dicitur Isaiae quinquagesimo nono, « non est abbreviata manus Domini, ut salvare nequeat ». Et non solum poterat hoc per se, sed etiam aliis communicare, secundum illud Marci ultimo: « Super aegros manus imponent, et bene habebunt ». — Et nota, quod *singulis* imposuisse dicitur sine exclusione, sicut dicitur Actuum decimo[^p110-2]: « Non est personarum acceptor Deus »; et ideo in Psalmo: « Aperis tu manum tuam et imples omne animal benedictione ». Et hoc est quod dicitur Sapientiae sexto: « Pusillum et magnum ipse fecit, et aequaliter cura est illi de omnibus ». Et ideo dicitur: *Curabat omnes,* scilicet ad se venientes, quia dicitur Ioannis sexto: « Omnis, qui venerit ad me, non eiiciam foras ». Nec mirum, quia « idem Dominus omnium dives in omnes, qui invocant illum », ad Romanos decimo.

90. (Vers. 41.). Tertio subditur *confessio daemonum,* cum subditur: *Exibant autem daemonia a multis clamantia,* scilicet prae dolore et timore et admiratione, Isaiae sexagesimo quinto[^p110-3]: « Clamabitis prae dolore cordis et prae contritione spiritus ululabitis ». Sed cassus erat eorum clamor; Psalmus: « Clamaverunt, nec erat qui salvos faceret ». — Vel *clamantia,* propter publicam veritatis annuntiationem. Et ideo subditur vera confessio: *Et dicentia: Quia tu es Filius Dei.* Hoc autem dicebant non ex certitudine, sed ex coniecturatione propter potentiae magnitudinem. Et ideo prius[^p110-4] dixit daemon: « Scio, quod sis *Sanctus Dei* »; nunc, quod *Filius Dei,* quia per maiora experimenta in cognitione profecerat. Hoc autem, quod coniiciebant, confitebantur *non corde* dicendo; de quo primae ad Corinthios duodecimo[^p110-5]: « Nemo potest dicere: Dominus Iesus, nisi in Spiritu sancto »; cui *dicere* respondet verbum, quod est « cum amore notitia »; *non corde et opere* confitebantur; de quo primae Ioannis quarto: « Omnis, qui confitetur, Christum Iesum venisse in carnem, ex Deo est »; sed *solum ore* dicebant et *corde odiebant* et *opere negabant;* ad Titum primo: « Confitentur, se nosse Deum, factis autem negant ». Nec mirum, quia, secundum quod dicitur in Psalmo[^p110-6], « in inferno quis confitebitur tibi »? Nullus, scilicet confessione meritoria, sed omnes confessione extorta et necessaria; Sapientiae quinto: « Dicentes intra se, poenitentiam agentes » etc.

91. Postremo additur *cohibitio spirituum immundorum,* cum subiungitur: *Et increpans non sinebat ea loqui,* ut verificetur illud Psalmi[^p110-7]: « Ab increpatione tua fugient, a voce tonitrui tui formidabunt ». *Non sinebat* etc., quia frequenter Dominus obicem opponit malignantibus; unde secundi Machabaeorum sexto: « Non sinere peccatoribus agere ex sententia magni beneficii est indicium ». — Vel ideo *non sinebat,* quia per daemones manifestari nolebat; unde et subditur: *Quia sciebant, ipsum esse Christum,* id est, probabiliter coniiciebant. « Non enim innotuit daemonibus, ut dicit Augustinus[^p110-8], ut splendor et lux, sed per quandam suae virtutis efficaciam ». Nolebat autem Dominus sui nominis cognitionem per daemones divulgari, tum quia erant *indigni,* tum quia *odiosi,* tum etiam quia *fraudulenti;* et ideo talis confessio nec conveniebat Deo nec congruebat daemoni nec proderat homini. Beda[^p110-9]: « Improbus magister veris falsa permiscet, ut specie veritatis fraudis testimonium obtegat ». Ex quo nobis datur intelligi, quod qui in praedicando Christum veris falsa permiscent omnino sunt cohibendi, sicut haeretici; primae ad Corinthios quinto[^p110-10]: « Modicum fermenti totam massam corrumpit ». Si qui autem vera praedicant, non tamen intentione recta, sustinendi sunt; et de talibus dicitur ad Philippenses primo: « Sive per occasionem, sive per veritatem annuntietur Christus, et in hoc gaudeo, sed et gaudebo ». Si autem praedicant vera et bona, et tamen non faciunt, nihilominus sunt audiendi; Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p110-11]: « Super cathedram Moysi sederunt scribae et Pharisaei ». Et tales designati
<!-- page 111 -->
sunt per illum, de quo dicitur infra nono, quod praedicabat Christum, non sequens eum; quem Dominus noluit prohiberi. Ex praedictis liquet, quod Salvatoris potestas per omnem modum est manifestata.

92. (Vers. 42.). *Facta autem die* etc. Post communicationem doctrinae Christi et authenticationem[^p111-1] ipsius ex operatione miraculi subiungitur hic tertio *acceptatio eiusdem a multitudine populi.* Ad hoc autem, quod doctrina aliqua debito modo auditoribus fiat accepta, oportet, quod sit immunis ab *ostentatione,* ab *ambitione,* ab *adulatione* et a *personarum acceptione.* His enim modis quatuor consuevit doctrinae acceptatio depravari.

Primo ergo ab acceptatione doctrinae Christi excluditur *ostentatio,* cum dicitur: *Facta autem die, egressus ibat in desertum locum,* volens se magis humiliter occultare quam impudenter ostentare, secundum illud Psalmi[^p111-2]: « Elongavi fugiens et mansi in solitudine ». Huius figura praecessit in Elia, de quo tertii Regum decimo nono dicitur, quod relicto puero suo, solus perrexit in desertum, postquam fecerat miraculum de igne consumente holocaustum. Huius etiam figura praecessit in David, qui dixit ad Ionatham primi Regum vigesimo[^p111-3]: « Dimitte me, ut abscondar in agro usque ad vesperam diei tertiae », id est usque ad resurrectionem. Ob quam etiam causam dixit discipulis Matthaei decimo septimo: « Nemini dixeritis visionem, donec Filius hominis a mortuis resurgat »; et Ioannis duodecimo: « Haec locutus est Iesus et abiit et abscondit se ».

93. Illud autem *iter in desertum* et *egressus de civitate* significat egressum vitae saecularis et ingressum religionis. *Desertum* enim est locus *paupertatis, asperitatis* et *vilitatis,* contra tria, quae sunt in mundo, secundum illud primae Ioannis secundo[^p111-4]: « Omne, quod est in mundo, aut est concupiscentia oculorum, aut concupiscentia carnis, aut superbia vitae ». Contra haec statuit Dominus *desertum religionis,* in quo notatur triplex bonum his oppositum, scilicet continentiae, paupertatis et obedientiae; et ideo merito dicitur *desertum.* In hoc deserto moratus est Ioannes; unde Matthaei undecimo[^p111-5]: « Quid existis in desertum videre? Hominem mollibus vestitum »? in quo patet asperitas paupertatis; « arundinem vento agitatam »? in quo designatur humilitas, quae non delectatur in vento laudum nec deiicitur in vituperio detractionum et conviciorum. — In hoc *deserto* fuit Christus, cuius figura praecessit primi Regum vigesimo tertio[^p111-6]: « Morabatur David in deserto, in locis munitissimis, in monte solitudinis Ziph », quia altissimam religionem tenuit Christus. Unde Augustinus de Vera Religione: « Omne peccatum provenit, aut quia appetimus quae Christus contempsit, aut quia fugimus quae sustinuit. Satellites enim diaboli divitias appetebant, Christus pauper esse voluit; honoribus inhiabant, rex fieri noluit; delicias cupiebant, crucifigi voluit ».

94. Secundo excluditur *ambitio* in hoc quod sequitur: *Et turbae requirebant eum et venerunt usque*[^p111-7] *ad ipsum.* Non ipse se ingerebat ex *ambitione;* unde dicitur Ioannis sexto[^p111-8]: « Qui cum cognovisset, quod venturi essent, ut facerent eum regem, fugit ». In quo docuit, quod honoris officium non est assumendum, nisi homo requiratur; unde ad Hebraeos quinto: « Nec quisquam sumit sibi honorem ».

95. In hoc etiam commendatur *devotio populi,* quia latere volentem requirebat eum, dum tempus erat; Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto[^p111-9]: « Quaerite Dominum, dum inveniri potest »; et Osee decimo: « Tempus requirendi Dominum, cum venerit qui docebit vos iustitiam »; et anima sancta in Psalmo: « Exquisivit te facies mea ». Et quod *devote* requirerent eum, apparet, quia scilicet *venerunt usque ad ipsum,* secundum illud Isaiae vigesimo primo[^p111-10]: « Si quaeritis, quaerite, convertimini et venite »; unde Matthaei undecimo: « Venite ad me omnes, qui laboratis et onerati estis » etc. Unde quia devote quaesierunt, invenerunt, secundum illud Sapientiae primo: « In simplicitate cordis quaerite illum ». — Et quia perfecta devotio non solum facit quaerere, sed *detinere* inventum; ideo subditur: *Et detinebant illum, ne discederet ab eis,* secundum illud Canticorum
<!-- page 112 -->
tertio[^p112-1]: « Inveni quem diligit anima mea: tenui eum nec dimittam » etc. Nec mirum, quia, Proverbiorum tertio, « lignum vitae est his qui apprehenderint eam; et qui tenuerit eam, beatus ». Unde poterant dicere illud animae sanctae in Psalmo: « Ne derelinquas me, Domine; Deus meus, ne discesseris a me ». In quo ostenditur, quod Christus nec ambiebat honorem, antequam offerrent, nec etiam iam oblatum, quia quasi violenter detinebatur. — Huius figura praecessit Genesis trigesimo secundo[^p112-2]: « Dimitte me, quia aurora est », quod dixit Angelus ad Iacob.

96. (Vers. 43.). Tertio excluditur *adulatio* in hoc quod additur: *Quibus ille ait: Quia*[^p112-3] *aliis civitatibus oportet me evangelizare regnum Dei.* Non eis blanditur, sed ostendit, se aequaliter curare de omnibus; unde poterat dicere illud primae ad Thessalonicenses secundo: « Neque fuimus in sermone adulationis, neque quaerentes ab hominibus gloriam, neque a vobis neque ab aliis ». Non enim quaerebat hominibus placere, sed Deo; et ideo dicit: *Oportet me evangelizare,* quia haec est voluntas Patris; unde subdit: *Quia ideo missus sum;* Isaiae sexagesimo primo[^p112-4]: « Evangelizare pauperibus misit me ». Et ideo *oportebat,* quia Ioannis decimo quarto dicitur: « Sicut mandatum dedit mihi Pater, sic facio ». Missus autem fuerat evangelizare *pauperibus,* secundum quod dicitur in auctoritate praemissa in Isaia. Missus etiam fuit evangelizare *regnum Dei,* sicut dicitur hic. Et ideo colligitur, quod missus fuerat evangelizare pauperibus regnum Dei. Unde Matthaei quinto[^p112-5]: « Beati pauperes spiritu, quoniam ipsorum est regnum caelorum »; et vere *ipsorum,* quia, decimo nono, « difficile qui pecunias habent introibunt in regnum caelorum ». Quia nemo potest habere illud regnum, nisi contemnat terrenum; et difficile pecuniae ab habentibus contemnuntur: ideo difficile acquirunt quod pauperibus est promissum.

97. (Vers. 44.). Quarto vero excluditur *personarum acceptio* in hoc quod sequitur: *Et erat praedicans in synagogis Galilaeae,* scilicet omnibus indifferenter: Matthaei nono[^p112-6]: « Circuibat Iesus omnes civitates et castella, docens in synagogis eorum ». Hoc decet facere bonum praedicatorem, secundum illud Proverbiorum quinto: « Deriventur fontes tui foras, et in plateis aquas tuas divide »; et Isaiae trigesimo secundo: « Beati, qui seminatis super omnes aquas ». « *Semen* autem est verbum Dei », infra octavo. Unde bonus praedicator, imitator Christi, debet esse communis persona, sicut Paulus ad Romanos primo[^p112-7]: « Graecis et barbaris, sapientibus et insipientibus debitor sum, ita quod in me promptum est et vobis, qui Romae estis, evangelizare ». — Quod significatum fuit Zachariae sexto in equis deferentibus quadrigam, id est legem evangelicam, ubi dicitur, quod isti sunt, quos misit Dominus, ut « perambulent terram »; unde in fine Matthaei[^p112-8] dicitur praedicatoribus: « Ite, docete omnes gentes »; et in fine Marci: « Ite in mundum universum, praedicate Evangelium omni creaturae ».

98. Ex praedictis colligitur, quod praedicator bonus, si est acceptus hominibus, ad hoc quod sit acceptus Deo et Angelis eius, debet esse immunis ab *ostentatione in signo,* ab *ambitione in animo,* ab *adulatione in verbo,* a *personarum acceptione in facto* sive exercitio. Et haec quatuor tangit Apostolus secundae ad Corinthios secundo[^p112-9]: « Non, inquit, sumus, sicut plurimi, adulterantes verbum Dei »; quod fit quatuor modis praedictis; « sed ex sinceritate », sine personarum acceptione, « sicut ex Deo », sine ambitione, « coram Deo », sine ostentatione, « in Christo loquimur », sine adulatione; nam Christus est veritas.

## English
<!-- page 107 -->

79. *Jesus rising up out of the synagogue.* After the miraculous cure of the male sex from a demon there follows here the miraculous cure *of the female sex from a fever;* which is described by the Evangelist in this order. First is noted the *discomfort of the fever* itself; secondly, the *help of intercession;* thirdly, the *remedy of the cure;* fourthly, the *sign of perfect deliverance.* For these four concurred to show this miracle[^p107-12].

80. (Vers. 38.). As to the *discomfort of the fever,* then, which compelled Simon's wife's mother to lie in the house, it is said: *And Jesus rising up out of the*
<!-- page 108 -->
*synagogue, went into Simon's house,* so that his power, which had been made manifest in common before all, might be made manifest more privately in the house before the simple and before his own imitators. In the house was the *woman* to be cured, not in the synagogue, to intimate what is said in the first to Timothy, the second chapter[^p108-1]: « But I suffer not a woman to teach ». — To this entering mercy moved him, for the relieving of the woman's misery; which he expresses when he says: *And Simon's wife's mother was taken with a great fever;* in which is designated the gravity of the infirmity, which disposes to grace, according to that saying of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-first chapter[^p108-2]: « A grievous sickness maketh the soul sober ». But although it was grievous, yet that saying of John, the eleventh chapter, could be said of it: « This sickness is not unto death, but that the glory of God may be made manifest »; which glory he ought to show not only in the one sex, but in both, because, as each had fallen, so it belonged to the redemption. Whence at the *nativity* of Christ there was Joseph with Mary[^p108-3]; at the *preaching* of Christ, disciples and women, according to what is said below, the eighth chapter; at the *passion,* Mary and John, John, the nineteenth chapter; at the *resurrection,* disciples and Magdalen, John, the twentieth chapter. So man and woman are cured to show the glory of God, and the woman from a graver ill than the man, because she is subject to more penalties; Genesis, the third chapter: « I will multiply thy sorrows and thy conceptions; in sorrow shalt thou bring forth children, and thou shalt be under thy husband's power ».

81. As to the *suffrage of intercession,* there is added: *And they besought him for her*[^p108-4], the disciples and those who were present. For although the Lord wills to give, he nonetheless wills to be asked, according to that saying of the Psalm: « Call upon me in the day of thy trouble; I will deliver thee, and thou shalt glorify me »; Isaias, the fifty-eighth chapter: « Thou shalt call, and the Lord shall hear; thou shalt cry, and he shall say: Here I am », « because I am merciful, the Lord thy God ».

82. And note that the Lord cured one *without prayers,* as the demoniac before[^p108-5]; another *by the prayers of others,* as here; another *by his own prayers,* as the leper below, the fifth chapter. In which it is signified that the grace of the Saviour cures sinful souls in three ways, now with *the sole kindness of his clemency* intervening; to the Romans, the second chapter[^p108-6]: « Knowest thou not, that the kindness of God leadeth thee to penance »? Now with the *prayer of the Church* intervening; James, the fifth chapter: « Is any man sick among you? Let him bring in the priests of the Church, and let them pray over him, and the prayer of faith shall save the sick man ». But now with *one's own groaning* intervening; Exodus, the sixth chapter[^p108-7]: « I have heard the groaning of the children of Israel »; the fourth of Kings, the twentieth chapter: « I have heard thy prayer and I have seen thy tears and I have healed thee ». And therefore the holy soul said in the Psalm: « But I have cried to the Lord, and the Lord hath saved me ».

83. (Vers. 39.). As to the *remedy of the cure* there is added: *And standing over her, he commanded the fever, and it left her.* Now he commands as lord and king, as in Ecclesiastes, the fifth chapter[^p108-8]: « Moreover the king of all the land commandeth him that serveth »; and because « no word is impossible to him »; therefore « he spoke, and it was made ». Whence at his precept the fever departed, according to that saying of the Psalm: « He hath made a precept, and it shall not pass away ». Nor is it a wonder, because[^p108-9] « whatsoever he pleased, he hath done in heaven and in earth, in the sea and in all the deeps »; and Jeremias, the fifth chapter: « Will not you then fear me, saith the Lord, and will you not be afraid at my presence? I who have set the sand a bound for the sea, an everlasting precept, which it shall not pass over ». Whence in a wonderful manner the obstinacy of the human will is reproved in this and shown to be reprehensible, because every sensible creature, and what is more, a penal defect, obeys the divine commands, which wretched man contradicts.

84. Lastly, as to the *sign of perfect deliverance* there is added: *And immediately rising she ministered*[^p108-10]. For this is a certain sign of perfect cure, that no remnants remained, in that she was straightway made able for motion and able for service. Whence she rose and ministered as a good matron and as another Martha, of whom below, the tenth chapter[^p108-11]: « But Martha was busy about much serving ». And such merit the divine benefit; in the fourth of Kings, the fourth chapter, Eliseus says to the Sunamitess: « Behold, thou hast diligently ministered to us in all things, what wilt thou that I do for thee »? Such a widow, the Apostle said, is to be chosen, in the first to Timothy, the fifth chapter: « Let a widow be chosen of no less than threescore years of age, having testimony for her
<!-- page 109 -->
good works, if she have brought up children, if she have received to harbour, if she have washed the saints' feet, if she have ministered to them that suffer tribulation, if she have followed every good work ».

85. But here it is to be noted, as to the *allegorical* sense, that the cure of *the man from a demon* signifies the cure of the Gentile people from error; in figure of which thing it is said in Isaias, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p109-1]: « There shall demons meet, the onocentaur and the hairy one shall cry out one to another ». This is said of Idumea under the figure of the Gentile world. This devil is cast out through faith in Christ, according to that saying of Mark, the last chapter[^p109-2]: « In my name they shall cast out devils, they shall speak with new tongues, they shall take up serpents » etc.; those *serpents* that pour forth the poison of error, as the serpent of which the second to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter: « I fear lest, as the serpent seduced Eve by his subtilty, so your minds should be corrupted and fall from the simplicity that is in Christ ». — By the cure of *Simon's wife's mother from the fever* is signified the cure of the synagogue from the heat of carnal concupiscence, with which that carnal people burns; Osee, the seventh chapter[^p109-3]: « All committing adultery are as an oven heated by the baker ». And therefore the cure of the man is put first, because, as is said to the Romans, the eleventh chapter, « when the fulness of the Gentiles shall have come in, then all Israel shall be saved ».

86. According to the *moral* sense the cure of *the man from a demon* signifies the cure from *spiritual sins,* according to what is said in the first of Kings, the sixteenth chapter[^p109-4]: « An evil spirit from the Lord troubled Saul »; and below, the eleventh chapter: « When the unclean spirit is gone out of a man, he walketh » etc. — The cure of *the woman from the fever* is the cure of the soul from *carnal sins;* Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third chapter[^p109-5]: « A hot soul is as a burning fire, it will not be quenched, till it swallow something ». Whence Bede: « *A man* is freed *from a devil,* when the mind is purged from unclean thought; *a woman from fevers,* when the flesh is reined in from the heat of concupiscence ». And therefore the man is dashed down in the midst of the synagogue, because he who is held by spiritual sins needs to be humbled in the sight of men; the Psalm[^p109-6]: « The Lord is the keeper of little ones »; but she, with the Lord standing over her in the house, because the guard of discipline is necessary together with divine grace; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-sixth chapter: « On a daughter that turneth not away herself, set a strict watch, lest, finding an opportunity, she abuse herself »; and therefore the first to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter: « I chastise my body and bring it into subjection » etc.

87. (Vers. 40). *And when the sun was down* etc. Here, after the cure of the male and the female sex, there is subjoined a *universal cure* with respect to every sex, age and infirmity. In which the wonderful power of Christ is made manifest from four things, namely from the *variety of diseases,* from the *ease of the cures,* from the *confession of the demons,* from the *restraint of the unclean spirits.*

First, then, the *variety of diseases* is introduced in that it is said: *And when the sun was down, they that*[^p109-7] *had any sick with divers diseases brought them to him,* namely that he might cure them; and deservedly, because, Isaias, the fifty-third chapter, « surely he hath borne our diseases » etc.; and Acts, the tenth chapter: « He went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed by the devil »; and without distinction, because he had power over all things equally; whence Wisdom, the sixteenth chapter[^p109-8]: « It was neither herb nor mollifying plaster that healed them, but thy word, O Lord, which healeth all things »; whence also in Matthew, the fourth chapter, it is said that « he healed every disease and every infirmity ».

88. And note that they brought them *after the setting of the sun,* so that it might be shown that Christ was solicitous and powerful about the salvation of the human race not only by day, but also by night. Which was figured in Genesis, the thirty-first chapter[^p109-9], in Jacob: « Day and night was I parched with heat and with frost, and sleep fled from my eyes »; and Wisdom, the last chapter: « In all things thou didst magnify thy people, O Lord, and didst exalt them, assisting them at every time and in every place ». — Or *after the setting of the sun* for this reason, that he might show that the glory of men is to be shunned; whence he reproves the Pharisees, Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p109-10], because « all their works they do for to be seen of men ». — Or *after the setting of the sun* for this reason, that it may be designated that the general remedy was to be acquired through the *passion.* For Christ is the sun, according to that saying of Malachias, the fourth chapter[^p109-11]: « Unto you that fear my name the sun of justice shall arise »; this sun *went down* in the passion; whence Amos, the eighth chapter: « The sun shall go down for them at midday »; and then more were healed, which is figured in Judges, the sixteenth chapter, in the death of Samson, who is interpreted *their sun.* Whence Bede[^p109-12]: « The setting of the sun is the passion of Christ, through which more demoniacs and sick are healed than before ».

<!-- page 110 -->
89. Secondly there is added the *ease of the cures,* when there is added: *But he, laying his hands on every one of them, cured them all*[^p110-1]. Easily he cured, because he healed without entreaty, but by the sole laying on of the hand. Nor is it a wonder, because, according to what is said in Isaias, the fifty-ninth chapter, « the hand of the Lord is not shortened, that it cannot save ». And not only could he do this by himself, but he could also communicate it to others, according to that saying of Mark, the last chapter: « They shall lay their hands upon the sick, and they shall recover ». — And note that he is said to have laid them on *every one* without exclusion, as is said in Acts, the tenth chapter[^p110-2]: « God is not a respecter of persons »; and therefore in the Psalm: « Thou openest thy hand and fillest with blessing every living creature ». And this is what is said in Wisdom, the sixth chapter: « He made the little and the great, and he hath equally care of all ». And therefore it is said: *He cured them all,* namely those coming to him, because it is said in John, the sixth chapter: « Every one that shall come to me, I will not cast out ». Nor is it a wonder, because « the same is Lord of all, rich unto all that call upon him », to the Romans, the tenth chapter.

90. (Vers. 41.). Thirdly there is added the *confession of the demons,* when there is added: *And devils went out from many, crying out,* namely for sorrow and fear and wonder, Isaias, the sixty-fifth chapter[^p110-3]: « You shall cry for sorrow of heart, and shall howl for grief of spirit ». But their crying was vain; the Psalm: « They cried, and there was none to save them ». — Or *crying out,* on account of the public announcement of the truth. And therefore the true confession is added: *And saying: Thou art the Son of God.* Now this they said not from certitude, but from conjecturing on account of the greatness of his power. And therefore before[^p110-4] the demon said: « I know that thou art the *Holy One of God* »; now, that he is the *Son of God,* because through greater experiences he had advanced in knowledge. But this which they conjectured they confessed *not by saying it with the heart;* of which the first to the Corinthians, the twelfth chapter[^p110-5]: « No man can say: The Lord Jesus, but by the Holy Spirit »; to which *saying* the word answers, which is « knowledge with love »; *not with the heart and the deed* did they confess; of which the first of John, the fourth chapter: « Every one that confesseth that Christ Jesus is come in the flesh, is of God »; but they said it *with the mouth only* and *hated with the heart* and *denied with the deed;* to Titus, the first chapter: « They confess that they know God, but in their deeds they deny him ». Nor is it a wonder, because, according to what is said in the Psalm[^p110-6], « who shall confess to thee in hell »? None, namely with a meritorious confession, but all with a confession extorted and necessary; Wisdom, the fifth chapter: « Saying within themselves, doing penance » etc.

91. Lastly there is added the *restraint of the unclean spirits,* when there is subjoined: *And rebuking them he suffered them not to speak,* that that saying of the Psalm may be verified[^p110-7]: « At thy rebuke they shall flee, at the voice of thy thunder they shall fear ». *He suffered them not* etc., because the Lord often sets an obstacle against the malignant; whence the second of Machabees, the sixth chapter: « Not to suffer sinners to act according to their mind is a sign of a great benefit ». — Or *he suffered them not* for this reason, because he did not will to be made manifest through demons; whence also there is added: *For they knew that he was Christ,* that is, they conjectured it probably. « For he became known to the demons, as Augustine says[^p110-8], not as splendour and light, but through a certain efficacy of his power ». But the Lord did not will the knowledge of his name to be spread abroad through demons, both because they were *unworthy,* and because they were *hateful,* and also because they were *fraudulent;* and therefore such a confession neither befitted God nor suited the demon nor profited man. Bede[^p110-9]: « The wicked master mingles false things with true, that under the appearance of truth he may cover over the testimony of fraud ». From which it is given us to understand that those who in preaching Christ mingle false things with true are altogether to be restrained, as heretics; the first to the Corinthians, the fifth chapter[^p110-10]: « A little leaven corrupteth the whole lump ». But if any preach true things, yet not with a right intention, they are to be borne with; and of such it is said to the Philippians, the first chapter: « Whether by occasion, or by truth, Christ be announced, in this also I rejoice, yea, and will rejoice ». But if they preach true and good things, and yet do them not, they are nonetheless to be heard; Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p110-11]: « Upon the chair of Moses have sat the scribes and the Pharisees ». And such are designated
<!-- page 111 -->
by that man of whom it is said below, the ninth chapter, that he preached Christ, not following him; whom the Lord would not have forbidden. From the aforesaid it is clear that the power of the Saviour was made manifest in every manner.

92. (Vers. 42.). *And when it was day* etc. After the communication of the doctrine of Christ and its authentication[^p111-1] from the working of a miracle, there is subjoined here thirdly the *acceptance of the same by the multitude of the people.* Now for any doctrine to be accepted by its hearers in due manner, it must be free from *ostentation,* from *ambition,* from *flattery* and from *respect of persons.* For in these four ways the acceptance of doctrine is wont to be depraved.

First, then, *ostentation* is excluded from the acceptance of the doctrine of Christ, when it is said: *And when it was day, going out he went into a desert place,* willing rather to hide himself humbly than to display himself shamelessly, according to that saying of the Psalm[^p111-2]: « I have gone far off flying away, and I abode in the wilderness ». The figure of this went before in Elias, of whom it is said in the third of Kings, the nineteenth chapter, that, leaving his servant, he went forward alone into the desert, after he had done the miracle of the fire consuming the holocaust. The figure of this also went before in David, who said to Jonathan, the first of Kings, the twentieth chapter[^p111-3]: « Let me go, that I may hide myself in the field until the evening of the third day », that is, until the resurrection. For which cause also he said to the disciples, Matthew, the seventeenth chapter: « Tell the vision to no man, till the Son of man be risen from the dead »; and John, the twelfth chapter: « These things Jesus spoke, and he went away and hid himself ».

93. Now that *journey into the desert* and *going out from the city* signifies the going out from secular life and the entering into religion. For the *desert* is a place of *poverty, asperity* and *vileness,* against the three things which are in the world, according to that saying of the first of John, the second chapter[^p111-4]: « All that is in the world is either the concupiscence of the eyes, or the concupiscence of the flesh, or the pride of life ». Against these the Lord has set up the *desert of religion,* in which is noted a threefold good opposed to these, namely of continence, of poverty and of obedience; and therefore it is deservedly called a *desert.* In this desert John abode; whence Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p111-5]: « What went you out into the desert to see? A man clothed in soft garments »? in which the asperity of poverty is plain; « a reed shaken with the wind »? in which is designated humility, which neither delights in the wind of praises nor is cast down in the vituperation of detractions and insults. — In this *desert* was Christ, of whom the figure went before in the first of Kings, the twenty-third chapter[^p111-6]: « David abode in the desert, in most strong places, in the mountain of the wilderness of Ziph », because Christ held the highest religion. Whence Augustine, *On True Religion:* « Every sin comes about either because we covet what Christ despised, or because we flee what he endured. For the satellites of the devil coveted riches, Christ willed to be poor; they gaped after honours, he would not be made king; they desired delights, he willed to be crucified ».

94. Secondly *ambition* is excluded in that which follows: *And the multitudes sought him and came even*[^p111-7] *unto him.* He did not thrust himself forward out of *ambition;* whence it is said in John, the sixth chapter[^p111-8]: « Who, when he knew that they would come to make him king, fled ». In which he taught that the office of honour is not to be assumed unless a man be sought for; whence to the Hebrews, the fifth chapter: « Neither doth any man take the honour to himself ».

95. In this also the *devotion of the people* is commended, because they sought him while he willed to lie hid, while there was time; Isaias, the fifty-fifth chapter[^p111-9]: « Seek ye the Lord, while he may be found »; and Osee, the tenth chapter: « The time to seek the Lord, when he shall come that shall teach you justice »; and the holy soul in the Psalm: « My face hath sought thee ». And that they sought him *devoutly* is apparent, because namely *they came even unto him,* according to that saying of Isaias, the twenty-first chapter[^p111-10]: « If you seek, seek; return, come »; whence Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « Come to me all you that labour and are burdened » etc. Whence because they sought devoutly, they found, according to that saying of Wisdom, the first chapter: « Seek him in simplicity of heart ». — And because perfect devotion makes one not only seek, but *detain* what is found; therefore there is added: *And they detained him, that he should not depart from them,* according to that saying of the Canticle,
<!-- page 112 -->
the third chapter[^p112-1]: « I found him whom my soul loveth; I held him, and I will not let him go » etc. Nor is it a wonder, because, Proverbs, the third chapter, « she is a tree of life to them that lay hold on her; and he that shall hold her is blessed ». Whence they could say that saying of the holy soul in the Psalm: « Forsake me not, O Lord; my God, do not thou depart from me ». In which it is shown that Christ neither sought honour before they offered it, nor even when already offered, because he was detained as it were by force. — The figure of this went before in Genesis, the thirty-second chapter[^p112-2]: « Let me go, for it is break of day », which the Angel said to Jacob.

96. (Vers. 43.). Thirdly *flattery* is excluded in that which is added: *To whom he said: That*[^p112-3] *to other cities I must preach the gospel of the kingdom of God.* He does not flatter them, but shows that he cares equally for all; whence he could say that saying of the first to the Thessalonians, the second chapter: « Neither were we in the speech of flattery, nor seeking glory of men, neither of you nor of others ». For he did not seek to please men, but God; and therefore he says: *I must preach the gospel,* because this is the will of the Father; whence he adds: *For therefore am I sent;* Isaias, the sixty-first chapter[^p112-4]: « To preach the gospel to the poor he hath sent me ». And therefore *he must,* because it is said in John, the fourteenth chapter: « As the Father hath given me commandment, so do I ». Now he had been sent to preach the gospel *to the poor,* according to what is said in the authority set down before in Isaias. He was sent also to preach the gospel of *the kingdom of God,* as is said here. And therefore it is gathered that he had been sent to preach the gospel of the kingdom of God to the poor. Whence Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p112-5]: « Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven »; and truly *theirs,* because, in the nineteenth chapter, « with difficulty shall they that have riches enter into the kingdom of heaven ». Because no one can have that kingdom unless he despise the earthly one; and riches are despised with difficulty by those who have them: therefore with difficulty do they acquire what is promised to the poor.

97. (Vers. 44.). But fourthly *respect of persons* is excluded in that which follows: *And he was preaching in the synagogues of Galilee,* namely to all without difference: Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p112-6]: « Jesus went about all the cities and towns, teaching in their synagogues ». This it befits a good preacher to do, according to that saying of Proverbs, the fifth chapter: « Let thy fountains be conveyed abroad, and in the streets divide thy waters »; and Isaias, the thirty-second chapter: « Blessed are ye that sow upon all waters ». « Now the *seed* is the word of God », below, the eighth chapter. Whence the good preacher, an imitator of Christ, ought to be a common person, as Paul to the Romans, the first chapter[^p112-7]: « To the Greeks and to the barbarians, to the wise and to the unwise I am a debtor, so that what is in me is ready to preach the gospel to you also that are at Rome ». — Which was signified in Zacharias, the sixth chapter, in the horses drawing the chariot, that is, the evangelical law, where it is said that these are they whom the Lord sent, that they « might walk through the earth »; whence at the end of Matthew[^p112-8] it is said to the preachers: « Go, teach all nations »; and at the end of Mark: « Go ye into the whole world, preach the gospel to every creature ».

98. From the aforesaid it is gathered that the good preacher, if he is acceptable to men, in order that he may be acceptable to God and to his Angels, ought to be free from *ostentation in sign,* from *ambition in mind,* from *flattery in word,* from *respect of persons in deed* or exercise. And these four the Apostle touches in the second to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p112-9]: « For we are not, he says, as many, adulterating the word of God »; which is done in the four aforesaid ways; « but with sincerity », without respect of persons, « as from God », without ambition, « before God », without ostentation, « in Christ we speak », without flattery; for Christ is the truth.
