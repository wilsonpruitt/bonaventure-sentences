# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. II, Vers. 25-35
## *Et ecce, homo erat in Ierusalem etc.*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 58 -->

58. *Et ecce, homo erat in Ierusalem* etc. Postquam descripta est Christi humiliatio secundum Legis praeceptum, hic secundo describitur eius *clarificatio per veritatis testimonium.* Et quoniam testimonium ad hoc, quod sit firmum, debet procedere ex ore plurium[^p58-1]; ideo introducitur testimonium duorum sexuum, scilicet virilis et muliebris, utrumque tamen authenticum, quia utrumque *propheticum* et utrumque fide dignum propter *meritum sanctitatis.* Et praemittitur testimonium viri tanquam dignioris, et subditur testimonium mulieris tanquam inferioris, et hoc ibi[^p58-2]: *Et erat Anna prophetissa* etc.

Circa *Simeonis* testimonium tria introducuntur: primum *testificantis meritum* solidans ad credendum; secundum est *testificationis praeconium* elevans ad admirandum, et hoc ibi[^p58-3]: *Et benedixit Dominum et ait* etc. Tertium est *praeconii temperamentum* inclinans ad compatiendum, ibi: *Et benedixit illis Simeon.*

Quantum ad primum nota, quod *perfectio meriti* Simeonis in duobus explicatur, scilicet in *habitu virtutis* et *spiritu veritatis,* ibi[^p58-4]: *Et responsum acceperat.* Et haec duo reddunt hominem in sanctitate perfectum. — *Habitus* autem sive usus *virtutis* in tribus consistit perfecte, scilicet in *fama, vita* et *gratia.* Primum est ab *extra,* secundum ab *intra,* tertium a *supra.*

59. (Vers. 25.). Quantum ad *famam* dicitur: *Et ecce, homo erat in Ierusalem, cui nomen Simeon;* quae scilicet civitas erat regalis et sacerdotalis, ubi potissime vigebat cultus Dei secundum observantiam Legis, unde *sancta* dicitur[^p58-5]. Ex quo datur intelligi, quod magnae honestatis erat homo, qui in tali civitate habebat famam. Et hoc designat Evangelista, ipsum demonstrando et nomen eius explicando; unde sibi potest competere illud Ecclesiastici decimo quinto[^p58-6]: « In medio Ecclesiae aperiet os eius, et implebit eum Dominus spiritu sapientiae »; et post subditur: « Et nomine aeterno hereditabit illum ». Unde recte iste designatur per Simeonem, filium Oniae, de quo Ecclesiastici quinquagesimo: « Simeon, Oniae filius, sacerdos magnus »; et post subditur, quibusdam interpositis: « Circa illum corona fratrum quasi plantatio cedri in monte Libano ».

60. Quantum ad *vitam* subditur: *Et homo iste iustus;* ubi ostenditur perfecte ordinatus in vita quantum ad *eligenda;* propter quod dicitur: *Et homo iste iustus.* « *Iustitia* namque est rectitudo voluntatis[^p58-7] », quae facit hominem incedere per rectam viam; Sapientiae decimo: « Iustum deduxit Dominus per vias rectas et ostendit » etc. Et ad hoc maxime iuvat recta intentio; Proverbiorum undecimo: « Iustitia simplicis diriget viam eius »; et iterum: « Simplicitas iustorum diriget eos ». — Et quantum ad *fugienda;* propter quod dicitur: *Et timoratus.* « Timor enim expellit peccatum », Ecclesiastici primo[^p58-8]; Proverbiorum decimo quinto: « In timore Domini declinat omnis a malo »; Iob primo: « Vir erat in terra Hus nomine Iob, rectus et simplex ac timens Deum et recedens a malo ». — Et quantum ad *exspectanda;* propter quod addit: *Exspectans consolationem Israel,* secundum illud Genesis quadragesimo nono[^p58-9]: « Salutare tuum exspectabo, Domine »; et Iudith octavo: « Exspectemus humiles consolationem eius ». Unde sibi maxime dicebat Spiritus sanctus illud Habacuc secundo: « Si moram fecerit, exspecta eum, quia veniens veniet et non tardabit ».

61. Quantum ad *gratiam* subditur: *Et Spiritus sanctus erat in eo,* scilicet per gratiam et caritatem, secundum illud ad Romanos quinto[^p58-10]: « Caritas Dei diffusa est in cordibus nostris per Spiritum sanctum »; et primae Ioannis quarto: « Qui manet in caritate in Deo manet ». Unde de ipso poterat dici illud quod dicitur de Stephano Actuum sexto, quod erat « plenus fide et Spiritu sancto »; de quo scilicet primae ad Corinthios ultimo: « Gratia Domini nostri Iesu Christi vobiscum ».

62. (Vers. 26.). *Et responsum acceperat.* Hic secundo commendatur *a Spiritu veritatis* ut *inducente ad exspectandum* ex divino responso; quantum ad quod dicit: *Et responsum acceperat a Spiritu sancto,* scilicet per revelationem; Isaiae trigesimo[^p58-11]: « Ad vocem clamoris tui statim, ut audierit, respondebit tibi ». Responsum acceperat consolatorium, secundum illud Zachariae primo: « Respondit Dominus Angelo, qui loquebatur in me verba bona, verba consolatoria ». Et propterea subditur: *Non*
<!-- page 59 -->
*visurum se mortem, nisi prius videret Christum Domini.* Unde poterat dicere cum Iob decimo nono[^p59-1]: « In carne mea videbo Deum Salvatorem meum », ita quod determinatio *in carne* respiciat videntem et visum. Et etiam poterat cum Propheta cantare: « Non moriar, sed vivam » etc. Poterat etiam dicere cum Apostolo, primae ad Corinthios secundo: « Nos spiritum huius mundi non accepimus, sed spiritum, qui ex Deo est, ut sciamus, quae a Deo donata sunt nobis ».

63. (Vers. 27.). Secundo etiam commendatur *a Spiritu veritatis* ut *deducente ad occurrendum* in templo; quantum ad quem dicitur: *Et venit in Spiritu in templum;* venit utique tanquam Spiritu sancto ductore; ad Romanos octavo[^p59-2]: « Quicumque Spiritu Dei aguntur, hi sunt filii Dei ». Venit utique ad obsequendum et venerandum Dominum, ut posset dicere illud Psalmi: « Introibo in domum tuam, adorabo ad templum sanctum tuum in timore tuo »; et iterum: « Introibimus in tabernaculum eius, adorabimus in loco, ubi steterunt pedes eius ».

64. Postremo commendatur a *Spiritu veritatis* ut *perducente ad comprehendendum* cum gaudio; et hoc, cum dicitur: *Et cum inducerent puerum Iesum parentes eius,* scilicet in templum, secundum illud Malachiae tertio[^p59-3]: « Statim veniet ad templum sanctum suum dominator, quem vos quaeritis » etc. *Inducerent,* inquam, *ut facerent secundum consuetudinem Legis pro eo;* quia, sicut dicitur ad Philippenses secundo, « in similitudinem hominum factus, et habitu inventus ut homo ».

65. (Vers. 28.). *Et ipse accepit eum in ulnas suas.* Ecce, devotio senis ad comprehendendum parvulum, qua Christo exposuit se totum, ut posset dicere illud sponsae Canticorum primo[^p59-4]: « Fasciculus myrrhae dilectus meus mihi, inter ubera mea commorabitur ». Volebat enim implere illud Canticorum ultimo: « Pone me ut signaculum super cor tuum, ut signaculum super brachium tuum »; immo super duo brachia: *duo,* ut ostendat, quod fortiter comprehendendus est; Canticorum tertio: « Inveni quem diligit anima mea, tenui eum nec dimittam » etc. Ostendit etiam, quod utraque manu et tota virtute nostra debemus Domino famulari, sicut illi qui una manu pugnabant et alia faciebant opus, Nehemiae quarto[^p59-5]; non sicut illi « qui imponunt onera gravia et importabilia, digito autem suo nolunt ea movere », Matthaei vigesimo tertio.

66. *Et benedixit Deum et dixit.* Post sanctitatis meritum subditur hic *veritatis praeconium* elevans ad admirandum; unde in admiratione concluditur, cum dicitur[^p59-6]: *Et erant mirantes,* prae magnitudine praeconii. Huius autem praeconii excellentia colligitur ex tribus, scilicet ex *praeambula benedictione;* ex *devota Christi laude et magnificatione,* ibi[^p59-7]: *Nunc dimittis,* in cantico Simeonis; et ex *parentum admiratione,* ibi: *Et erant pater eius et mater mirantes.*

67. Praemittitur ergo *Dei benedictio,* cum dicitur: *Et benedixit Deum,* id est, benedicendo gratias egit, ita ut diceret[^p59-8]: « Benedic, anima mea, Domino, et omnia, quae intra me sunt, nomini sancto eius »; secundi Machabaeorum decimo: « In hymnis et confessionibus benedicebant Deum, qui magna fecit in Israel »; et Ecclesiastici trigesimo secundo: « Benedic Deum, qui creavit te, inebriantem te ab omnibus bonis suis ».

68. (Vers. 29.). *Nunc dimittis* etc. Hic subditur *devota Christi magnificatio* facta a Simeone impleto Spiritu sancto. Et primo in hoc cantico praemittitur *spiritualis consolatio senis.* Secundo subditur *sublimis commendatio infantis,* ibi[^p59-9]: *Quia viderunt oculi mei salutare tuum.*

Ostendit igitur Simeon senex *se consolatum* in praesentia Christi ex ratione triplici: propter *dignationem maiestatis;* propter quam dicit: *Nunc dimittis servum tuum, Domine,* quia Dominus venerat ad servum; secundi Regum vigesimo quarto[^p59-10]: « Quid causae est, ut veniat dominus meus rex ad servum suum »? et Psalmus: « Quid est homo, quia memor es eius, aut filius hominis, quoniam visitas eum »? — Propter *impletionem veritatis;* propter quam dicit: *Secundum verbum tuum,* verbum scilicet promissum; Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto[^p59-11]: « Verbum, quod egredietur de ore meo, non revertetur vacuum ». Et ideo illud Psalmi dicere poterat: « Bonitatem fecisti cum servo tuo, Domine, secundum verbum tuum ». — Propter *adeptionem tranquillitatis;* propter quam dicit: *In pace.* Iam enim adveniente illo qui dicit: « In me pacem habebitis », Ioannis decimo sexto[^p59-12], iustus dicebat, se in pace mori. Iam pro vera consolatione poterat dicere illud quod dixit Iacob ad Ioseph, Genesis quadragesimo sexto: « Iam laetus moriar, quia vidi faciem tuam et superstitem te relinquo ». Et hoc petebat Tobias, tertio[^p59-13]: « Et nunc,
<!-- page 60 -->
Domine, fac mecum misericordiam et praecipe, in pace recipi spiritum meum ». Hoc iam praestiterat ipsi Simeoni, sicut promiserat; unde poterat dicere illud: « In pace in id ipsum dormiam et requiescam », quia nihil aliud exspectabat.

69. (Vers. 30.). *Quia viderunt oculi mei* etc. Hic secundo additur *Christi magnificatio* secundum triplicem excellentiam *pietatis, sapientiae* et *maiestatis.* Ex *pietate* et clementia venit *salus,* ex *sapientia* fulget *lux,* ex *maiestate, gloria et laus; salus* scilicet omnibus populis, *lux* gentibus et *gloria* Israelitis.

Quantum ad *salutem clementiae* dicitur: *Quia viderunt oculi mei salutare tuum;* unde poterat dici Simeoni illud Iob trigesimo tertio[^p60-1]: « Videbis faciem eius in iubilo »; Baruch tertio: « Post haec in terris visus est et cum hominibus conversatus est ».

70. (Vers. 31.). Haec autem salus non erat particularis, sed *universalis;* et ideo subdit: *Quod parasti ante faciem omnium populorum,* secundum illud Isaiae quinquagesimo secundo[^p60-2]: « Videbunt omnes fines terrae salutare Dei nostri ». Iam verificatum videbat illud Psalmi: « Notum fecit Dominus salutare suum in conspectu gentium »; sequitur: « Viderunt omnes fines terrae salutare Dei nostri »; Isaiae quadragesimo nono: « Parum est, ut sis mihi servus ad faeces Israel convertendas. Dedi te in lucem gentibus, ut sis salus mea usque ad extremum terrae ».

71. (Vers. 32.). Et quantum ad *lucem sapientiae;* propter quod addit: *Lumen ad revelationem gentium;* Isaiae nono[^p60-3]: « Habitantibus in regione umbrae mortis lux orta est eis ». Et hoc lumen gentibus revelabit gloriam Dei; Isaiae quadragesimo: « Revelabitur gloria Domini, et videbit omnis caro salutare Dei »; Danielis secundo: « Ipse revelat profunda et abscondita et novit in tenebris constituta, et lux cum eo est ».

72. Quantum ad *sublimitatem gloriae* subditur: *Et gloriam plebis tuae Israel,* quia de Israel natus est secundum carnem; Isaiae quarto[^p60-4]: « Erit germen Domini in magnificentia et gloria, et fructus terrae sublimis, et exsultatio his qui salvati fuerint ex Israel ». Et nunc impletum est illud quod dicitur Esther octavo: « Iudaeis nova lux oriri visa est, gaudium et honor et tripudium apud omnes populos ».

73. Magnificatur ergo Christus sub ratione *salutis omnium, lucis gentium* et *gloriae Iudaeorum.* — primum *clementiam,* secundum *sapientiam,* tertium vero *potentiam* manifestat — sub ratione *pacis* ex consolatione senis; unde laudatur in hoc cantico ut *pax,* ut *salus,* ut *lux,* ut *gloria. Pax* est, quia *mediator; salus,* quia *redemptor; lux,* quia *doctor; gloria,* quia *praemiator.* Et in his quatuor consistit perfecta Christi commendatio et magnificatio[^p60-5], immo totius evangelicae historiae quaedam brevissima comprehensio quantum ad *incarnationem* in pace, *praedicationem* in luce, *redemptionem* in salute, *resurrectionem* in gloria. — Et quia canticum illud sic continet laudis Christi plenitudinem et senis morientis consolationem, ideo in sero cantatur in Completorio. Unde ista tria cantica sunt ordinata, Mariae, Zachariae, Simeonis: primum in vespere, secundum in mane, tertium in sero, quia sequens incipit, ubi terminatur praecedens[^p60-6]. — Significatur etiam in hoc, quod de incarnatione omnis status debet laudare Deum, scilicet virginum, coniugatorum et viduarum; contemplativorum, praelatorum et activorum; laici et clerici et religiosi, qui et Domino debent esse consecrati.

74. (Vers. 33.). *Et erant pater eius et mater* etc. Hic tertio subditur ex hoc consurgens *parentum admiratio,* cum subditur: *Et erant pater eius et mater mirantes super his quae dicebantur de illo,* scilicet super quatuor praedictis. Nam Christus admirabilis est secundum quod *mediator* in pacis concordia; Esther decimo quinto[^p60-7]: « Valde admirabilis es, Domine, et facies tua plena gratiarum ». — Admirabilis etiam secundum quod *salvator* in clementia; propter quod in Psalmo[^p60-8] dicitur ita: « Mirabilis Deus in Sanctis suis », quia « gratia et misericordia est in Sanctos eius et respectus in electos illius », Sapientiae quarto. — Admirabilis secundum quod *doctor* in sapientia; Psalmus[^p60-9]: « Mirabilis
<!-- page 61 -->
facta est scientia tua ex me ». — Admirabilis secundum quod *triumphator* in potentia, secundum illud Psalmi: « Mirabilis in altis Dominus »; et Exodi decimo quinto: « Quis similis tui in fortibus, Domine? Quis similis tui, magnificus in sanctitate, terribilis atque laudabilis, faciens mirabilia »? — Et ideo non est mirum, si pater putativus et mater vera mirabantur super his quae dicebantur.

75. *Et benedixit illis Simeon* etc. Post testificantis meritum et testificationis praeconium subiungitur hic tertio *praeconii temperamentum* humilians ad compatiendum, unde in compassione terminatur; Psalmus[^p61-1]: « Quia calix in manu Domini vini meri plenus misto »; ideo post Christi dignitatem subiungit infirmitatem. In hoc autem temperamento primo praemittitur *propheticae benedictionis solatium;* deinde additur *dominicae passionis mysterium;* tertio subiungitur *maternae compassionis martyrium.*

76. (Vers. 34.). Quantum ad *solatium benedictionis propheticae* praemittitur: *Et benedixit illis Simeon,* id est, benedictos praedicavit Mariam, quia concepit, et Ioseph, quia educavit; unde poterat eis dicere illud Davidicum[^p61-2]: « Benedicti vos a Domino ». Benedicuntur enim parentes in filio et pro filio, secundum quod, primi Regum secundo, « benedixit Eli Elcanae et uxori eius », scilicet pro Samuele, optimo puero.

Nota, quod praemittitur hic benedictio tum ad *consolationem,* tum ad *confortationem,* tum etiam ad *commendationem,* ne passio praedicenda turbet, ne terreat, ne credatur culpa proximorum parentum esse in causa. — Vel ideo benedixit, ut significaret, quod in illo impletae sunt benedictiones Patrum; Genesis vigesimo secundo[^p61-3]: « Benedicens benedicam tibi » etc. — Vel ideo, ut insinuet, per passionem Christi nos ab omni maledictione liberatos; ad Galatas tertio: « Christus nos redemit de maledicto Legis, factus pro nobis maledictum ».

77. Quantum ad *mysterium dominicae passionis* additur: *Et dixit ad Mariam, matrem eius: Ecce, positus est hic in ruinam et in resurrectionem multorum in Israel;* et hoc divino iudicio; Ioannis nono[^p61-4]: « In iudicium ego veni in hunc mundum, ut qui non vident videant, et qui vident caeci fiant »; et Isaiae octavo: « Erit vobis Dominus in sanctificationem; in lapidem autem offensionis et in petram scandali duabus domibus Israel, in laqueum et in ruinam habitantibus Ierusalem ». Et ideo dicitur *positus,* scilicet divino iudicio; primae Petri secundo[^p61-5]: « Vobis honor credentibus, non credentibus autem lapis, quem reprobaverunt aedificantes, hic factus est lapis offensionis et petra scandali his qui offendunt verbo »; et hoc pro culpa ipsorum. Secundae ad Corinthios secundo simile dicitur de Apostolis: « Christi bonus odor sumus Deo, aliis quidem odor mortis in mortem, aliis autem odor vitae in vitam ». — Et ratio huius est contradictio erroris; propter quod additur: *Et in signum, cui contradicetur;* Threnorum tertio[^p61-6]: « Tetendit arcum suum et posuit me quasi signum ad sagittam »; hoc dicitur propter contradicentes; ad Hebraeos duodecimo: « Recogitate eum qui talem adversus semetipsum a peccatoribus contradictionem sustinuit ». Et ista contradictio fuit occasio ruinae et causa in multis; Isaiae quadragesimo primo[^p61-7]: « Peribunt viri, qui contradicunt tibi »; et ideo dicitur Ecclesiastici quarto: « Non contradicas verbo veritatis ullo modo ».

78. (Vers. 35.). Quantum ad *martyrium compassionis maternae* dicitur: *Et tuam ipsius animam pertransibit gladius,* id est tui ipsius, *gladius* compassionis; unde sibi competit illud Psalmi[^p61-8]: « Ferrum pertransivit animam eius ». Damascenus: « Dolores, quos pariens effugit, tempore passionis sustinuit ». Et Bernardus[^p61-9]: « Dum perforatur lancea ille tuus Iesus, omnium quidem, sed specialiter *tuus;* ipsius animam non gladius pertransivit, quia iam ille exspiraverat, sed tuam, unde te plus quam Martyrem praedicamus ». Et Hieronymus[^p61-10]: « Quia mente passa est, plus quam Martyr fuit ». Unde, quia ipsa veraciter diligebat, ex affectu intimo vehementer compatiebatur. — Alii vero, qui in corde non
<!-- page 62 -->
diligebant, exsultabant; ideo additur: *Ut revelentur ex multis cordibus cogitationes.* Sic est hic consecutum, quia hoc consecutum est ad passionem Christi, quia videlicet Iudaeorum carnalium et hypocritarum fuit cordis iniquitas revelata; Osee septimo[^p62-1]: « Cum sanare vellem Israel, revelata est iniquitas Ephraim et Samariae coram me »; quia, sicut dicitur Matthaei decimo, « nihil est opertum, quod non reveletur, et absconditum, quod non sciatur ». Hoc autem maxime erit in iudicio; primae ad Corinthios quarto: « Nolite ante tempus iudicare, quousque veniat Dominus, qui illuminabit abscondita tenebrarum et manifestabit consilia cordium » etc.

79. Nota *spiritualiter,* quod ex consideratione passionis Christi debet triplex gladius spiritualis animam pertransire. Primus est gladius *discretionis rectae fidei;* ad Ephesios sexto[^p62-2]: « Et gladium spiritus, quod est verbum Dei ». — Secundus est gladius *districtionis severi iudicii;* Apocalypsis primo: « De ore eius gladius utraque parte acutus exibat ». — Tertius est gladius *divisionis humani desiderii;* Matthaei decimo: « Non veni mittere pacem, sed gladium ». — Pertransit autem animas primus gladius per *veram fidem;* ad Hebraeos quarto[^p62-3]: « Vivus est sermo Dei et penetrabilior omni gladio ». — Secundus, per *rigorem poenitentiae;* Psalmus: « Gladii ancipites in manibus eorum ». — Tertius, per *ardorem benevolentiae;* Canticorum tertio: « En lectulum Salomonis », et post: « Omnes tenentes gladios ».

80. Contra hunc triplicem gladium est alius triplex: scilicet gladius *rancoris;* primi Regum trigesimo primo[^p62-4]: « Arripuit Saul gladium suum et irruit super eum ». — Gladius *detractionis;* Psalmus: « Filii hominum, dentes eorum arma et sagittae, et lingua eorum gladius acutus ». — Gladius *haeresis,* qui est divisio ab unitate fidei; Proverbiorum quinto[^p62-5], sub metaphora meretricis: « Novissima illius amara quasi absinthium, et lingua eius acuta quasi gladius biceps »; et Psalmus: « Gladium evaginaverunt », sequitur: « ut trucident rectos corde ».

Primus gladius spiritualis est emendus; Lucae vigesimo secundo[^p62-6]: « Qui non habet vendat tunicam suam et emat gladium ». Alius vero est in vaginam remittendus; Ioannis decimo octavo: « Mitte gladium tuum in vaginam ». Et ratio huius est, quia primus occidit vitam carnalem, secundus vero vitam spiritualem.

## English
<!-- page 58 -->

58. *And behold there was a man in Jerusalem* etc. After the humiliation of Christ according to the precept of the Law has been described, here secondly is described his *glorification by the testimony of truth.* And since testimony, in order that it be firm, ought to proceed from the mouth of several[^p58-1]; therefore the testimony of the two sexes is introduced, namely the male and the female, each however authentic, because each is *prophetic* and each worthy of belief on account of the *merit of holiness.* And the testimony of the man is put first as of the worthier, and the testimony of the woman is subjoined as of the lesser, and this there[^p58-2]: *And there was one Anna, a prophetess* etc.

Concerning the testimony of *Simeon* three things are introduced: first, the *merit of the one testifying,* making firm unto believing; second is the *proclamation of the testification,* raising up to wondering, and this there[^p58-3]: *And he blessed the Lord and said* etc. Third is the *tempering of the proclamation,* inclining to compassion, there: *And Simeon blessed them.*

As to the first, note that the *perfection of the merit* of Simeon is unfolded in two things, namely in the *habit of virtue* and the *spirit of truth,* there[^p58-4]: *And he had received an answer.* And these two render a man perfect in holiness. — Now the *habit* or use *of virtue* consists perfectly in three things, namely in *fame, life* and *grace.* The first is from *without,* the second from *within,* the third from *above.*

59. (Vers. 25.). As to *fame* it is said: *And behold there was a man in Jerusalem, named Simeon;* which city, namely, was royal and priestly, where the worship of God flourished most of all according to the observance of the Law, whence it is called *holy*[^p58-5]. From which it is given to be understood that he was a man of great honour who had fame in such a city. And this the Evangelist designates, pointing him out and unfolding his name; whence there can befit him that of Ecclesiasticus, the fifteenth chapter[^p58-6]: « In the midst of the church he shall open his mouth, and the Lord shall fill him with the spirit of wisdom »; and afterwards it is subjoined: « And shall cause him to inherit an everlasting name ». Whence rightly is this man designated by Simeon, the son of Onias, of whom Ecclesiasticus, the fiftieth chapter: « Simeon, the son of Onias, the high priest »; and afterwards it is subjoined, certain things being interposed: « About him was the ring of his brethren, as the cedar planted in mount Libanus ».

60. As to *life* it is subjoined: *And this man was just;* where he is shown perfectly ordered in life as to the things *to be chosen;* on account of which it is said: *And this man was just.* « For *justice* is rectitude of will[^p58-7] », which makes a man walk by the right way; Wisdom, the tenth chapter: « The Lord conducted the just through the right ways, and shewed » etc. And to this a right intention helps most of all; Proverbs, the eleventh chapter: « The justice of the simple shall direct his way »; and again: « The simplicity of the just shall guide them ». — And as to the things *to be fled;* on account of which it is said: *And God-fearing.* « For fear driveth out sin », Ecclesiasticus, the first chapter[^p58-8]; Proverbs, the fifteenth chapter: « By the fear of the Lord every one declineth from evil »; Job, the first chapter: « There was a man in the land of Hus, whose name was Job, upright and simple, and fearing God, and avoiding evil ». — And as to the things *to be awaited;* on account of which he adds: *Waiting for the consolation of Israel,* according to that of Genesis, the forty-ninth chapter[^p58-9]: « I will look for thy salvation, O Lord »; and Judith, the eighth chapter: « Let us humbly wait for his consolation ». Whence the Holy Spirit was saying to him most of all that of Habacuc, the second chapter: « If he make any delay, wait for him: for coming he shall come, and shall not be slack ».

61. As to *grace* it is subjoined: *And the Holy Spirit was in him,* namely through grace and charity, according to that of the Romans, the fifth chapter[^p58-10]: « The charity of God is poured forth in our hearts by the Holy Spirit »; and the first of John, the fourth chapter: « He that abideth in charity abideth in God ». Whence there could be said of him that which is said of Stephen in the Acts, the sixth chapter, that he was « full of faith and of the Holy Spirit »; of which, namely, the first to the Corinthians, the last chapter: « The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you ».

62. (Vers. 26.). *And he had received an answer.* Here secondly he is commended *by the Spirit of truth,* as *inducing to waiting* from the divine answer; as to which he says: *And he had received an answer from the Holy Spirit,* namely through revelation; Isaias, the thirtieth chapter[^p58-11]: « At the voice of thy cry, as soon as he shall hear, he will answer thee ». He had received a consoling answer, according to that of Zacharias, the first chapter: « The Lord answered the Angel that spoke in me, good words, consoling words ». And therefore it is subjoined: *That he should not*
<!-- page 59 -->
*see death, before he had seen the Christ of the Lord.* Whence he could say with Job, the nineteenth chapter[^p59-1]: « In my flesh I shall see God my Saviour », so that the determination *in the flesh* regards both the one seeing and the one seen. And also he could sing with the Prophet: « I shall not die, but live » etc. He could also say with the Apostle, the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter: « We have received not the spirit of this world, but the spirit that is of God; that we may know the things that are given us from God ».

63. (Vers. 27.). Secondly also he is commended *by the Spirit of truth* as *conducting to meeting* in the temple; as to which it is said: *And he came by the Spirit into the temple;* he came indeed as with the Holy Spirit for guide; to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p59-2]: « Whosoever are led by the Spirit of God, they are the sons of God ». He came indeed to do service to the Lord and to venerate him, so that he could say that of the Psalm: « I will come into thy house; I will worship towards thy holy temple, in thy fear »; and again: « We will go into his tabernacle: we will adore in the place where his feet stood ».

64. Lastly he is commended by the *Spirit of truth* as *bringing to laying hold* with joy; and this, when it is said: *And when his parents brought in the child Jesus,* namely into the temple, according to that of Malachias, the third chapter[^p59-3]: « Presently the Lord, whom you seek, shall come to his holy temple » etc. *Brought in,* I say, *to do for him according to the custom of the Law;* because, as is said to the Philippians, the second chapter, « being made in the likeness of men, and in habit found as a man ».

65. (Vers. 28.). *He also took him into his arms.* Behold the devotion of the old man in laying hold of the little one, by which he exposed his whole self to Christ, so that he could say that of the bride, the Canticles, the first chapter[^p59-4]: « A bundle of myrrh is my beloved to me, he shall abide between my breasts ». For he wished to fulfil that of the Canticles, the last chapter: « Put me as a seal upon thy heart, as a seal upon thy arm »; nay rather upon both arms: *two,* to show that he is to be laid hold of strongly; the Canticles, the third chapter: « I found him whom my soul loveth: I held him, and I will not let him go » etc. It shows also that with both hands and with all our power we ought to serve the Lord, as those who fought with one hand and with the other did the work, Nehemias, the fourth chapter[^p59-5]; not as those « who lay heavy and insupportable burdens, but with a finger of their own they will not move them », Matthew, the twenty-third chapter.

66. *And he blessed God and said.* After the merit of holiness is subjoined here the *proclamation of truth,* raising up to wondering; whence it is concluded in wonder, when it is said[^p59-6]: *And they were wondering,* for the greatness of the proclamation. Now the excellence of this proclamation is gathered from three things, namely from the *preceding blessing;* from the *devout praise and magnification of Christ,* there[^p59-7]: *Nunc dimittis,* in the canticle of Simeon; and from the *wonder of the parents,* there: *And his father and mother were wondering.*

67. First, then, is put the *blessing of God,* when it is said: *And he blessed God,* that is, by blessing he gave thanks, so that he said[^p59-8]: « Bless the Lord, O my soul, and all that is within me, his holy name »; the second of Machabees, the tenth chapter: « With hymns and confessions they blessed God, who had done great things in Israel »; and Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-second chapter: « Bless God, who created thee, and inebriateth thee with all his good things ».

68. (Vers. 29.). *Nunc dimittis* etc. Here is subjoined the *devout magnification of Christ* made by Simeon filled with the Holy Spirit. And first, in this canticle, there is put first the *spiritual consolation of the old man.* Secondly is subjoined the *sublime commendation of the infant,* there[^p59-9]: *Because my eyes have seen thy salvation.*

Simeon the old man shows, then, that *he is consoled* in the presence of Christ by a threefold reason: on account of the *condescension of majesty;* on account of which he says: *Now thou dost dismiss thy servant, O Lord,* because the Lord had come to the servant; the second of Kings, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p59-10]: « What is the cause that my lord the king should come to his servant »? and the Psalm: « What is man that thou art mindful of him? or the son of man that thou visitest him »? — On account of the *fulfilment of truth;* on account of which he says: *According to thy word,* the word, namely, promised; Isaias, the fifty-fifth chapter[^p59-11]: « The word which shall go forth from my mouth shall not return void ». And therefore he could say that of the Psalm: « Thou hast done goodness with thy servant, O Lord, according to thy word ». — On account of the *attainment of tranquillity;* on account of which he says: *In peace.* For now, he having come who says: « In me you shall have peace », John, the sixteenth chapter[^p59-12], the just man was saying that he was dying in peace. Now for true consolation he could say that which Jacob said to Joseph, Genesis, the forty-sixth chapter: « Now shall I die with joy, because I have seen thy face, and leave thee surviving ». And this Tobias asked, the third chapter[^p59-13]: « And now,
<!-- page 60 -->
O Lord, deal with me in mercy, and command my spirit to be received in peace ». This he had already granted to Simeon himself, as he had promised; whence he could say that: « In peace in the selfsame I will sleep, and I will rest », because he was awaiting nothing else.

69. (Vers. 30.). *Because my eyes have seen* etc. Here secondly is added the *magnification of Christ* according to the threefold excellence of *piety, wisdom* and *majesty.* From *piety*[^tr-pietas] and clemency comes *salvation,* from *wisdom* shines *light,* from *majesty, glory and praise; salvation,* namely, for all peoples, *light* for the Gentiles and *glory* for the Israelites.

As to the *salvation of clemency* it is said: *Because my eyes have seen thy salvation;* whence there could be said to Simeon that of Job, the thirty-third chapter[^p60-1]: « Thou shalt see his face with jubilation »; Baruch, the third chapter: « Afterwards he was seen upon earth, and conversed with men ».

70. (Vers. 31.). Now this salvation was not particular, but *universal;* and therefore he subjoins: *Which thou hast prepared before the face of all peoples,* according to that of Isaias, the fifty-second chapter[^p60-2]: « All the ends of the earth shall see the salvation of our God ». Already he was seeing verified that of the Psalm: « The Lord hath made known his salvation in the sight of the Gentiles »; it follows: « All the ends of the earth have seen the salvation of our God »; Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter: « It is a small thing that thou shouldst be my servant to convert the dregs of Israel. I have given thee for a light to the Gentiles, that thou mayest be my salvation even to the farthest part of the earth ».

71. (Vers. 32.). And as to the *light of wisdom;* on account of which he adds: *A lumen*[^tr-lumen] *to the revelation of the Gentiles;* Isaias, the ninth chapter[^p60-3]: « To them that dwelt in the region of the shadow of death, light is risen ». And this lumen will reveal to the Gentiles the glory of God; Isaias, the fortieth chapter: « The glory of the Lord shall be revealed, and all flesh shall see the salvation of God »; Daniel, the second chapter: « He revealeth deep and hidden things, and knoweth what is in darkness: and light is with him ».

72. As to the *sublimity of glory* it is subjoined: *And the glory of thy people Israel,* because he was born of Israel according to the flesh; Isaias, the fourth chapter[^p60-4]: « The bud of the Lord shall be in magnificence and glory, and the fruit of the earth shall be high, and exultation to them that shall have been saved of Israel ». And now is fulfilled that which is said in Esther, the eighth chapter: « To the Jews a new light seemed to rise, joy, and honour, and dancing among all peoples ».

73. Christ, then, is magnified under the account of *the salvation of all, the light of the Gentiles* and *the glory of the Jews.* — the first manifests *clemency,* the second *wisdom,* but the third *power* — under the account of *peace* from the consolation of the old man; whence he is praised in this canticle as *peace,* as *salvation,* as *light,* as *glory. Peace* he is, because *mediator; salvation,* because *redeemer; light,* because *doctor; glory,* because *rewarder.* And in these four consists the perfect commendation and magnification[^p60-5] of Christ, nay rather a certain most brief comprehension of the whole evangelical history as to the *incarnation* in peace, the *preaching* in light, the *redemption* in salvation, the *resurrection* in glory. — And because that canticle thus contains the fulness of the praise of Christ and the consolation of the dying old man, therefore it is sung late in the evening, at Compline. Whence these three canticles have been ordered, Mary's, Zachary's, Simeon's: the first at evening, the second in the morning, the third late in the evening, because the following begins where the preceding ends[^p60-6]. — It is signified also in this that every state ought to praise God for the incarnation, namely of virgins, of the married and of widows; of contemplatives, of prelates and of actives; laymen and clerics and religious, who also ought to be consecrated to the Lord.

74. (Vers. 33.). *And his father and mother* etc. Here thirdly is subjoined the *wonder of the parents* arising from this, when it is subjoined: *And his father and mother were wondering at those things which were spoken concerning him,* namely at the four aforesaid. For Christ is wonderful insofar as he is *mediator* in the concord of peace; Esther, the fifteenth chapter[^p60-7]: « Thou art very wonderful, O Lord, and thy face is full of graces ». — Wonderful also insofar as he is *saviour* in clemency; on account of which in the Psalm[^p60-8] it is said thus: « Marvellous is God in his saints », because « grace and mercy is upon his saints, and respect upon his elect », Wisdom, the fourth chapter. — Wonderful insofar as he is *doctor* in wisdom; the Psalm[^p60-9]: « Marvellous
<!-- page 61 -->
is become thy knowledge to me ». — Wonderful insofar as he is *triumpher* in power, according to that of the Psalm: « Marvellous is the Lord on high »; and Exodus, the fifteenth chapter: « Who is like to thee, among the strong, O Lord? who is like to thee, magnificent in holiness, terrible and praiseworthy, doing marvels »? — And therefore it is no marvel if the putative father and the true mother were wondering at those things which were said.

75. *And Simeon blessed them* etc. After the merit of the one testifying and the proclamation of the testification, here thirdly is subjoined the *tempering of the proclamation,* humbling to compassion, whence it ends in compassion; the Psalm[^p61-1]: « For in the hand of the Lord there is a cup of strong wine full of mixture »; therefore after the dignity of Christ he subjoins his infirmity. Now in this tempering there is first put the *solace of the prophetic blessing;* then is added the *mystery of the Lord's passion;* thirdly is subjoined the *martyrdom of the mother's compassion.*

76. (Vers. 34.). As to the *solace of the prophetic blessing* there is put first: *And Simeon blessed them,* that is, he preached Mary blessed, because she conceived, and Joseph, because he brought him up; whence he could say to them that of David[^p61-2]: « Blessed be you of the Lord ». For parents are blessed in the son and for the son, according as, in the first of Kings, the second chapter, « Heli blessed Elcana and his wife », namely for Samuel, a most excellent boy.

Note that the blessing is put first here, both for *consolation,* and for *strengthening,* and also for *commendation,* lest the passion to be foretold should trouble, lest it should terrify, lest the fault of the nearest parents be believed to be the cause. — Or he blessed for this, to signify that in him were fulfilled the blessings of the Fathers; Genesis, the twenty-second chapter[^p61-3]: « Blessing I will bless thee » etc. — Or for this, to intimate that through the passion of Christ we have been freed from every curse; to the Galatians, the third chapter: « Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the Law, being made a curse for us ».

77. As to the *mystery of the Lord's passion* it is added: *And he said to Mary his mother: Behold, this child is set for the fall, and for the resurrection of many in Israel;* and this by divine judgement; John, the ninth chapter[^p61-4]: « For judgement I am come into this world; that they who see not, may see; and they who see, may become blind »; and Isaias, the eighth chapter: « The Lord shall be a sanctification to you; but for a stone of stumbling, and for a rock of offence to the two houses of Israel, for a snare and a fall to the inhabitants of Jerusalem ». And therefore he is said to be *set,* namely by divine judgement; the first of Peter, the second chapter[^p61-5]: « To you that believe, honour; but to them that believe not, the stone which the builders rejected, the same is made a stone of stumbling, and a rock of scandal, to them who stumble at the word »; and this for their own fault. In the second to the Corinthians, the second chapter, the like is said of the Apostles: « We are the good odour of Christ unto God, to some indeed the odour of death unto death, but to others the odour of life unto life ». — And the reason of this is the contradiction of error; on account of which it is added: *And for a sign which shall be contradicted;* Lamentations, the third chapter[^p61-6]: « He hath bent his bow, and set me as a sign for the arrow »; this is said on account of those who contradict; to the Hebrews, the twelfth chapter: « Think diligently upon him that endured such contradiction from sinners against himself ». And this contradiction was the occasion of fall and the cause in many; Isaias, the forty-first chapter[^p61-7]: « The men shall perish that contradict thee »; and therefore it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the fourth chapter: « Contradict not the word of truth in any way ».

78. (Vers. 35.). As to the *martyrdom of the mother's compassion* it is said: *And thy own soul a sword shall pierce,* that is, of thyself, the *sword* of compassion; whence there befits her that of the Psalm[^p61-8]: « The iron pierced his soul ». Damascene: « The sorrows which she escaped in bearing, she endured in the time of the passion ». And Bernard[^p61-9]: « While that Jesus of thine is pierced by the lance, of all indeed, but especially *thine;* the sword did not pierce his soul, because he had already expired, but thine, whence we preach thee more than a Martyr ». And Jerome[^p61-10]: « Because she suffered in mind, she was more than a Martyr ». Whence, because she herself loved truly, from inmost affection she had compassion vehemently. — But others, who did not
<!-- page 62 -->
love in heart, were exulting; therefore it is added: *That out of many hearts thoughts may be revealed.* So it has followed here, because this followed upon the passion of Christ, because namely the iniquity of heart of the carnal Jews and of the hypocrites was revealed; Osee, the seventh chapter[^p62-1]: « When I would have healed Israel, the iniquity of Ephraim and of Samaria was revealed before me »; because, as is said in Matthew, the tenth chapter, « nothing is covered that shall not be revealed, nor hid that shall not be known ». Now this will be most of all in the judgement; the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « Judge not before the time, until the Lord come, who will bring to light the hidden things of darkness, and will make manifest the counsels of the hearts » etc.

79. Note *spiritually* that from the consideration of the passion of Christ a threefold spiritual sword ought to pierce the soul. The first is the sword of the *discretion of right faith;* to the Ephesians, the sixth chapter[^p62-2]: « And the sword of the spirit, which is the word of God ». — The second is the sword of the *strictness of severe judgement;* the Apocalypse, the first chapter: « From his mouth came out a sword sharp on both sides ». — The third is the sword of the *division of human desire;* Matthew, the tenth chapter: « I came not to send peace, but the sword ». — Now the first sword pierces souls through *true faith;* to the Hebrews, the fourth chapter[^p62-3]: « The word of God is living, and more piercing than any sword ». — The second, through the *rigour of penance;* the Psalm: « Two-edged swords in their hands ». — The third, through the *ardour of benevolence;* the Canticles, the third chapter: « Behold the little bed of Solomon », and afterwards: « All holding swords ».

80. Against this threefold sword there is another threefold: namely, the sword of *rancour;* the first of Kings, the thirty-first chapter[^p62-4]: « Saul took his own sword, and fell upon it ». — The sword of *detraction;* the Psalm: « The sons of men, whose teeth are weapons and arrows, and their tongue a sharp sword ». — The sword of *heresy,* which is division from the unity of the faith; Proverbs, the fifth chapter[^p62-5], under the metaphor of the harlot: « Her end is bitter as wormwood, and her tongue sharp as a two-edged sword »; and the Psalm: « They have drawn out the sword », it follows: « to slay the upright of heart ».

The first spiritual sword is to be bought; Luke, the twenty-second chapter[^p62-6]: « He that hath not, let him sell his coat, and buy a sword ». But the other is to be put back into the scabbard; John, the eighteenth chapter: « Put up thy sword into the scabbard ». And the reason of this is that the first kills the carnal life, but the second the spiritual life.
