# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XII, Vers. 35-48
## *Primo invitatur consideratione secundi adventus*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 324 -->

#### Ad sollicitudinem providentiae invitatur dupliciter.

49. *Sint lumbi vestri praecincti* etc. Postquam revocavit a sollicitudine avaritiae, hic secundo *invitat ad sollicitudinem providentiae,* ne quis crederet, quod omnem vellet sollicitudinem amovere a corde[^p324-1]. Invitat autem ad huiusmodi sollicitudinem ex consideratione duplicis adventus: primo scilicet consideratione *secundi adventus,* qui erit terribilis; secundo vero, consideratione *primi adventus,* qui fuit amabilis, ibi[^p324-2]: *Ignem veni mittere in terram.*

#### Primo invitatur consideratione secundi adventus.

Prima pars habet duas: in quarum prima invitat ad sollicitudinem vigilantiae *omnes communiter;* in secunda vero, *praelatos specialiter,* ibi[^p324-3]: *Ait autem ei Petrus.*

Circa *generalem introductionem omnium* ad sollicitudinem vigilantiae procedit hoc ordine: primo praemittens *vigilantiae modum;* secundo adiungens *motivum,* ibi[^p324-4]: *Beati servi illi* etc.; tertio subiungens *stimulativum,* ibi: *Hoc autem scitote* etc. Primum horum respicit *meritum,* secundum respicit *praemium,* tertium respicit *imminens periculum.*

Circa *modum* autem *vigilantiae* duo introducit Evangelista, ad quae Dominus discipulos invitat, scilicet ad *promptitudinem expeditionis in corpore* et ad *sollicitudinem exspectationis in corde.*

50. (Vers. 35.). Primo igitur quantum ad *promptitudinem expeditionis in corpore* dicit: *Sint lumbi vestri praecincti* etc. Sicut ille expeditus est, qui est praecinctus ad viam progrediendam, sic ille expeditus est, qui restringit in se desideria carnalia. Unde Gregorius[^p324-5]: « A principali sexu *lumborum* nomine luxuria designatur »; Iob quadragesimo: « Fortitudo eius in lumbis eius » etc. « *Lumbos* igitur *praecingimus,* cum carnis luxuriam per continentiam coarctamus. Sed quia non sufficit mala non agere, nisi etiam quisque studeat bonis operibus insudare, protinus subditur: *Et lucernae ardentes in manibus vestris.* Lucernas quippe ardentes in manibus habemus, cum per bona opera proximis nostris lucis exempla monstramus ». *Lucerna* enim dicitur recte divinum mandatum; Proverbiorum sexto[^p324-6]: « Mandatum lucerna est, et lex lux » etc. Haec lucerna est *in manu,* quando mandatum est in operatione; Proverbiorum ultimo: « Non exstinguetur in nocte lucerna eius. Manum autem suam misit ad fortia » etc.; et Matthaei quinto: « Sic luceat lux vestra coram hominibus, ut videant opera vestra bona » etc. — Et nota, quod sicut lucerna abscondit lumen a vento, sed non a visu; sic bona opera comparantur lucernae; quia[^p324-7] « sic debet opus esse in publico, quatenus intentio maneat in occulto »; sic velit homo aliis dare exemplum virtutis, ut tamen non quaerat munus[^p324-8] transitorii favoris.

51. (Vers. 36.). Secundo quantum ad *sollicitudinem exspectationis in corde* subdit: *Et vos similes hominibus exspectantibus dominum suum, quando revertatur a nuptiis,* id est, quando veniet ad iudicium, descendendo de caelis. Unde Gregorius[^p324-9]: « Ad nuptias Dominus abiit, cum post resurrectionem suam Angelorum sibi multitudinem novus homo copulavit; qui tunc revertetur, cum per iudicium nobis manifestatur ». Unde a bonis semper debet exspectari; ad Philippenses tertio[^p324-10]: « Salvatorem exspectamus Dominum Iesum Christum » etc. Et haec exspectatio non est *vana;* Proverbiorum decimo septimo: « Gemma gratissima exspectatio praestolantis »; non est etiam *somnolenta;* Psalmus: « Exspecta Dominum et viriliter age ». — Et ideo subdit: *Ut, cum venerit,* « properando ad iudicium[^p324-11] », *et pulsaverit,* per infirmitatis flagellum; *confestim aperiant ei,* per desiderium intimum; Apocalypsis tertio: « Ecce, sto ad ostium et pulso; si quis aperuerit mihi, intrabo ad illum » etc. Beda[^p324-12]: « Non vult aperire iudici pulsanti qui, timens videre iratum, quem contempsit,
<!-- page 325 -->
de corpore exire metuit. Aperit qui iudicem laetus et securus sustinet et de propinqua morte gaudet »; Canticorum quinto[^p325-1]: « Vox dilecti mei pulsantis »; et post: « Surrexi, ut aperirem » etc.

52. Et notandum est hic, quod hoc Evangelium legitur in festis Confessorum, pro eo quod ipsi commendantur tripliciter, scilicet quantum ad *declinationem mali* in praecinctione lumborum, et quantum ad *operationem boni* in portatione lucernarum, et quantum ad *exspectationem optimi* in similitudine hominum exspectantium dominum suum; secundum illa tria, quae dicuntur in Michaeae sexto[^p325-2]: « Indicabo tibi, o homo, quid sit bonum: facere iudicium », respectu tui, « et diligere misericordiam », respectu proximi, « et sollicite ambulare cum Deo tuo », respectu Dei.

53. Ad hoc autem, quod *malum* in nobis *perfecte deficiat,* sunt *lumbi nostri tripliciter praecingendi,* scilicet lumbi carnalis *contactus,* de quibus in Psalmo[^p325-3]: « Lumbi mei impleti sunt illusionibus, et non est sanitas in carne mea »; et hi praecingendi cingulo *castitatis;* Isaiae trigesimo secundo: « Accingite lumbos vestros, super ubera plangite » etc. — Item, lumbi carnalis *affectus* cingulo *virtutis;* Iob quadragesimo[^p325-4]: « Accinge quasi vir lumbos tuos »; et Ieremiae primo: « Accinge lumbos tuos, surge et loquere ad eos ». — Item, lumbi carnalis *cogitatus* cingulo *veritatis;* primae Petri primo[^p325-5]: « Succincti lumbos mentis vestrae, sobrii » etc.

54. Ad hoc autem, quod *bonum* in nobis *perfecte clarescat, portanda est lucerna rectae intentionis;* supra undecimo[^p325-6]: « Lucerna corporis tui est oculus tuus ». — Item, portanda lucerna *verae praedicationis;* Psalmus[^p325-7]: « Lucerna pedibus meis verbum tuum »; Ecclesiastici quadragesimo octavo: « Surrexit Elias quasi ignis, et verbum ipsius quasi facula ardebat ». — Item, portanda lucerna *honestae conversationis;* Ioannis quinto[^p325-8]: « Ille erat lucerna ardens et lucens »; et in Psalmo: « Illuc producam cornu David; paravi lucernam Christo meo ».

55. Postremo ad hoc, quod *desiderium perfecte ignescat, exspectandus est Christus fidenter;* Habacuc secundo[^p325-9]: « Si moram fecerit, exspecta eum, quia veniens veniet » etc. — Item exspectandus *gaudenter;* Proverbiorum decimo: « Exspectatio iustorum laetitia » etc. — Item exspectandus *vigilanter;* Iob decimo quarto: « Cunctis diebus, quibus nunc milito, exspecto, donec veniat immutatio mea ». Et hoc modo exspectant servi « dominum, quando revertatur a nuptiis[^p325-10] ». — Sic beati Confessores perfecte laudantur, secundum illa ad Titum secundo: « Sobrie et iuste et pie vivamus in hoc saeculo, exspectantes beatam spem et adventum gloriae magni Dei » etc.

56. (Vers. 37.). *Beati servi illi* etc. Hic secundo introducit *motivum ad sollicite vigilandum*[^p325-11]; et hoc est duplex, scilicet *beatificatio vigilantium* sine *defectione* et sine omni *exceptione.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *beatificationem vigilantium sine defectione* dicit: *Beati servi illi, quos cum venerit dominus, invenerit vigilantes;* Proverbiorum octavo[^p325-12]: « Beatus homo, qui audit me et qui vigilat ad fores meas quotidie »; et ideo Ecclesiastici trigesimo nono: « Iustus cor suum tradet ad vigilandum diluculo » etc. Tales Dominus beatificat; Iob octavo: « Si diluculo consurrexeris et Omnipotentem fueris deprecatus, statim evigilabit ad te et pacatum reddet habitaculum iustitiae tuae ». — Ideo subdit: *Amen dico vobis, quod praecinget se,* « praeparando se ad retributionem[^p325-13] »; Psalmus: « Dominus regnavit, decorem indutus est » etc. *Et faciet illos discumbere,* scilicet in aeterna refectione; Ezechielis trigesimo quarto: « Ego pascam oves meas, et ego accubare eas faciam ». — *Et transiens ministrabit illis,* per liberalissimam communicationem. *Transiens,* id est transire faciens; Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto[^p325-14]: « Transite ad me omnes, qui concupiscitis me » etc.; quia *a Christo* et *per Christum* transimus *ad Christum,* a gloria scilicet corporis ad gloriam animae, et ab hac ad gloriam Deitatis. Propter quod dicit Ioannis decimo[^p325-15]: « Ego sum ostium; per me si quis introierit, salvabitur »; et decimo quarto: « Ego sum via, veritas et vita ». *Ministrare* autem dicitur Christus, quia semper dabit materiam gaudii actualis
<!-- page 326 -->
refectionis indeficientis; Apocalypsis septimo[^p326-1]: « Non esurient neque sitient amplius »; « quoniam Agnus, qui in medio throni est, reget illos et deducet ad vitae fontes aquarum ». « Beati igitur, qui ad coenam nuptiarum Agni vocati sunt », Apocalypsis decimo nono; in qua Agnus sine macula erit *sponsus, cibus, dominus* et *minister;* Psalmus[^p326-2]: « Inebriabuntur ab ubertate domus tuae, et torrente voluptatis tuae potabis eos ». Ipse *ministrabit* et *invitabit,* secundum illud Canticorum quinto: « Comedite, amici, et bibite et inebriamini, carissimi ».

57. (Vers. 38.). Deinde quantum ad *beatitudinem sine exceptione* subdit: *Et si venerit in secunda vigilia et si in tertia*[^p326-3] *venerit et ita invenerit; beati sunt servi illi.* Et nota hic, quod per *tres vigilias* intelliguntur tres status praesentis vitae, scilicet *pueritiae, iuventutis* et *senectutis.* Unde Glossa Bedae[^p326-4]: « Vigilias vocat ad similitudinem excubantium in nocte. Prima vigilia custodia pueritiae est, secunda est iuventutis, et tertia senectutis. Si quis in *pueritia* vigilare neglexerit, non tamen desperet; si in *iuventute* neglexerit, saltem tandem in *senio* resipiscat, quia pius Dominus poenitentiam nostram exspectat patienter »; Isaiae trigesimo[^p326-5]: « Propterea exspectat Dominus, ut misereatur vestri; et ideo exaltabitur parcens vobis, quia Deus iudicii Dominus; beati omnes, qui exspectant eum ». — Et nota, quod Marci decimo tertio[^p326-6] insinuantur *quatuor* vigiliae secundum modum distinctionis horarum apud excubantes: « Vigilate, inquit; nescitis enim, quando Dominus veniat: sero, an media nocte, an galli cantu, an mane ». Et per *has vigilias* intelliguntur quatuor status, in quibus homo utitur libertate arbitrii: primus et ultimus et duo intermedii; unus est in profectu, et alter in declinatione. In quo insinuatur, quod Dominus omni hora sine exceptione nostram acceptat vigiliam, sed potissime illam quae incipit a pueritia; Threnorum tertio[^p326-7]: « Bonum est viro, cum portaverit iugum ab adolescentia sua »; et tamen etiam ultimum senium non recusat; unde dicitur Matthaei decimo quarto, quod « quarta vigilia noctis venit ad discipulos ambulans super mare ». Qualibet ergo hora non est inutile, sed perutile *vigilare;* infra vigesimo primo[^p326-8]: « Vigilate, omni tempore orantes, ut digni habeamini fugere ista omnia, quae ventura sunt, et stare ante Filium hominis » etc.

58. (Vers. 39.). *Hoc autem scitote* etc. Hic tertio subiungit *stimulativum ad vigilandum*[^p326-9]; quod introducit per hunc modum, scilicet *proponendo exemplum parabolicum* et *concludendo principale intentum.*

Primum ergo *exponit exemplum parabolicum,* cum dicit: *Hoc autem scitote, quoniam, si sciret paterfamilias, qua hora fur veniret, vigilaret utique,* scilicet ad custodiam domus[^p326-10], ne fur furtim aliquid asportaret. — Ideo dicit: *Et non sineret perfodi domum suam.* Et si semper haberet horam suspectam, nunquam relinqueret domum suam sine custodia; alioquin non sapienter, sed stulte curam familiae procuraret. Exemplum de Isboseth, de quo legitur secundi Regum quarto[^p326-11], quod « Isboseth dormiebat super stratum suum meridie. Et ostiaria purgans triticum obdormivit. Ingressi sunt autem Rechab et Baana, frater eius, latenter domum et percusserunt eum in inguine et fugerunt ». Sic et *spiritualiter* fit ei qui negligit domum suam vigilanter custodire; unde Gregorius[^p326-12]: « Ostiaria dormiente, Isboseth interficitur, quia, dum discretionis sollicitudo cessaverit, ad interficiendum animam malignis spiritibus iter pandit ». Et ideo vir spiritualis e contrario dicit illud Isaiae vigesimo primo[^p326-13]: « Super speculam Domini ego sum, stans iugiter per diem, et super custodiam meam ego sum, stans totis noctibus »; et ideo primae Petri ultimo: « Sobrii estote et vigilate, quia adversarius vester » etc.

59. (Vers. 40.). Deinde concludit principale intentum. *Et vos estote parati, quia, qua hora non putatis, Filius hominis veniet.* Glossa[^p326-14]: « Ultimam horam semper ignorari voluit Dominus, ut semper sit suspecta, et ad eam semper praeparemur ». Unde Matthaei vigesimo quarto[^p326-15]: « De die illa et hora nemo scit » etc.; et primae ad Thessalonicenses quinto: « Dies Domini sicut fur in nocte veniet »; et post: « Vos autem, fratres, non estis in tenebris, ut dies
<!-- page 327 -->
ille tanquam fur vos comprehendat »; et Ecclesiastae nono: « Nescit homo finem suum, sed sicut pisces capiuntur hamo, ita comprehenduntur homines tempore malo ». Sic et illi qui non se praeparant; ideo dicitur Ecclesiastici quinto[^p327-1]: « Ne tardes converti ad Dominum, et ne differas de die in diem; subito enim veniet ira eius et in tempore vindictae disperdet te ». Unde Alcuinus[^p327-2]: « Dissoluta est cogitatio de crastina conversione cogitare et hodiernam negligere ». Et Seneca[^p327-3]: « Quilibet dies vitae nostrae ordinandus est ultimus ». Propter hoc ergo, ut semper simus parati, voluit Dominus, nos ignorare horam mortis et diem iudicii. « Nihil enim est certius morte, et nihil est incertius hora mortis[^p327-4] »; ideo Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo: « Memor esto iudicii mei; sic enim erit et tuum: mihi heri, et tibi hodie »; et de iudicii hora dicitur Matthaei vigesimo quinto: « Media nocte clamor factus est: Ecce, sponsus venit »; et post: « Quae paratae erant intraverunt cum eo ad nuptias, et clausa est ianua ». Gregorius[^p327-5]: « O si sapere palato cordis possit, quid admirationis habet *venit sponsus!* quid dulcedinis *intraverunt cum eo ad nuptias!* quid amaritudinis *clausa est ianua* »!

60. (Vers. 41.). *Ait autem ei*[^p327-6] *Petrus.* Postquam invitavit ad vigilantiam omnes generaliter, hic invitat *praelatos specialiter.* Quod quidem facit hoc ordine. Primo namque *commendat praelatorum vigilantiam;* secundo *arguit insolentiam,* ibi[^p327-7]: *Quodsi dixerit servus ille* etc.; tertio *aggravat negligentiam,* ibi: *Ille autem servus, qui cognovit.*

*Commendans* autem *vigilantiam praelatorum* primo commendat eam quantum ad praerogativam *meriti;* secundo, quantum ad *praecellentiam praemii;* ibi[^p327-8]: *Beatus ille servus.*

Primum ostendit in respondendo ad quaestionem Petri, cum dicit: *Ait autem ei Petrus: Domine, ad nos dicis hanc parabolam, an*[^p327-9] *ad omnes?* Bene quaerit et utiliter, ut sciat, ad quid debeat obligari; unde Ecclesiastici tertio: « Quae praecipit tibi Deus, illa cogita semper »; et maxime illa sunt discenda, quae sunt parabolice dicta; Proverbiorum primo: « Audiens sapiens, sapientior erit. Animadvertet parabolas et interpretationem, verba sapientum et aenigmata eorum ».

61. (Vers. 42.). Huic autem quaestioni Petri superficietenus non videtur Dominus respondere; sed si profundius attendatur, respondet perfecte, insinuans, quod praedicta parabola de vigilantia spectat *communiter ad universos,* sed *specialiter ad praelatos,* cum dicit: *Dixit autem Dominus: Quis, putas, est fidelis dispensator et prudens? Dispensatores* praelatos vocat; primae ad Corinthios quarto[^p327-10]: « Sic nos existimet homo ut ministros Christi et dispensatores mysteriorum Dei » etc. Tales difficile est invenire fideles; unde primae ad Corinthios quarto: « Hic iam quaeritur inter dispensatores, ut fidelis quis inveniatur »; et Proverbiorum vigesimo: « Multi homines misericordes vocantur, virum autem fidelem quis inveniet »? *Quis, putas,* ergo *est dispensator fidelis?* in *intentione;* primae ad Timotheum primo[^p327-11]: « Misericordiam consecutus sum, ut fidelis essem »; et Apocalypsis secundo: « Esto fidelis usque ad mortem ». *Et prudens,* in *electione;* Proverbiorum decimo quarto[^p327-12]: « Acceptus est regi minister intelligens »; unde Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo: « Diligenter agnosce vultum pecoris tui, tuosque greges considera ».

62. *Quem constituit Dominus super*[^p327-13] *familiam suam,* per auctoritatem commissam; ad Hebraeos quinto: « Nec quisquam sumit sibi honorem, sed qui vocatur a Domino tanquam Aaron »; Psalmus: « Ego autem constitutus sum rex ab eo super Sion, montem sanctum eius ». — *Ut det illis in tempore tritici mensuram,* communicando sanam doctrinam; Ecclesiastae octavo[^p327-14]: « Tempus et responsionem cor sapientis intelliget » etc. Attendat etiam audientium capacitatem; unde dicit: *mensuram.* Gregorius: « Pro
<!-- page 328 -->
qualitate audientium formari debet sermo doctorum, ut et sua singulis congruenter tribuat, et tamen a communis aedificationis arce nunquam recedat ». Huius exemplum habemus in Ioseph, qui ad dispensandum frumentum constitutus est super terram Aegypti, Genesis quadragesimo primo[^p328-1], in quo fuit *fidelitas* et *prudentia, auctoritas* et *vigilantia* seu diligentia. — Et quia ista fuerunt in sanctis praelatis, ideo Evangelium istud decantatur in laudem Confessorum et Pontificum, qui in his quatuor praemissis commendabiles exstiterunt. — Et de his quatuor dicitur in Glossa Bedae[^p328-2]: « Quam rarus est Domino *propter Dominum serviens,* oves Christi non ad lucrum, sed amore Christi pascens! *prudens,* in futuro sibi providens; *quem constituit,* id est, quam rarus, qui sit *vocatus a Deo tanquam Aaron* et non magis se ingesserit, et qui non *magis se pascat* quam oves ». Unde propter *defectum fidelitatis* Malachiae primo[^p328-3]: « Quis est ex vobis, qui claudat ostia mea et incendat altare meum gratuito »? et ad Philippenses secundo: « Omnes quaerunt quae sua sunt » etc. Pauci etiam sunt *prudentes;* unde Isaiae vigesimo quarto[^p328-4]: « Sicut populus, sic sacerdos »; et Osee quarto: « Quia tu scientiam repulisti, repellam et ego te, ne sacerdotio fungaris mihi ». Pauci etiam sunt *divinitus constituti;* ideo dicitur Osee octavo[^p328-5]: « Ipsi regnaverunt, et non ex me ». Pauci similiter *utilitati gregis intenti;* unde Ezechielis trigesimo quarto: « Vae pastoribus Israel! qui pascebant semetipsos. Nonne greges a pastoribus pascuntur » etc. Ideo signanter dicens praemittit: *Quis putas?* propter raritatem et difficultatem et pretiositatem, qua tales excellunt in praerogativa meriti.

63. (Vers. 43.). Secundo quantum ad *praecellentiam praemii* subdit: *Beatus ille servus, quem, cum venerit dominus, invenerit ita facientem;* Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p328-6]: « Euge, serve bone et fidelis » etc. *Ita facientem,* id est *fideliter, prudenter* et *vigilanter,* secundum illud primae Petri quarto[^p328-7]: « Unusquisque sicut accepit gratiam, in alterutrum illam administrantes, sicut boni dispensatores multiformis gratiae Dei ». Et Chrysostomus: « Haec dicta sunt de sermone et virtute, de pecuniis et de omni dispensatione, quae unicuique commissa est. Unumquemque enim ad communem utilitatem uti oportet his quae habet, sive sapientia, sive principatu, sive divitiis, non ad nocumentum conservorum ».

64. (Vers. 44.). Huius autem usui gratiae redditur *perfectus fructus gloriae,* quantum ad quam subdit: *Vere dico vobis, quia super*[^p328-8] *omnia, quae possidet, constituet illum,* « super caelestis regni gaudia », « non ut soli, sed ut prae ceteris habeant gaudia aeterna tum pro sua vita, tum pro gregis custodia ». Unde primae Petri quinto[^p328-9]: « Seniores, pascite qui in vobis est gregem Dei ». « Et cum apparuerit princeps pastorum, percipietis immarcescibilem gloriae coronam ». Unde Chrysostomus[^p328-10]: « Bene agente populo, unusquisque pro suo bono remuneratur; sacerdos autem pro bonis omnium »; primae ad Thessalonicenses secundo: « Quae est spes mea, aut gaudium, aut corona gloriae? Nonne vos ante Dominum nostrum Iesum Christum estis in adventu eius »?

65. (Vers. 45.). *Quodsi dixerit servus ille* etc. Postquam commendavit praelatorum vigilantiam et providentiam seu curam, hic *vituperat insolentiam*[^p328-11]; quod quidem facit *arguendo enormitatem peccati* et *comminando severitatem iudicii.*

Primo igitur quantum ad argutionem *enormitatis peccati* dicit: *Quodsi dixerit servus ille in corde suo: Moram facit dominus meus venire*[^p328-12]. Glossa: « Econtra, si dissimulaverit iudicium in proximo venturum, securitatem sibi promittens », secundum illud Ezechielis duodecimo: « Quod est proverbium in terra Israel dicentium: In longum differentur dies, et peribit omnis visio »? et secundae Petri tertio[^p328-13]: « Venient illusores, iuxta proprias concupiscentias ambulantes, dicentes: Ubi est promissio aut adventus eius? Ex quo enim patres dormierunt, omnia sic perseverant ab initio creaturae ». — Et quoniam talis cogitatio parit securitatem, et securitas contemptum, et contemptus iniquitatem; ideo subdit: *Et coeperit percutere pueros*[^p328-14] *et ancillas,* scilicet parvulos scandalizando, cum tamen deberet
<!-- page 329 -->
fovere verbo et exemplo. Propter quod Isaiae tertio[^p329-1]: « Quare atteritis populum meum et facies pauperum commolitis »? Et hoc est grave peccatum; primae ad Corinthios octavo: « Peccantes in fratres et percutientes conscientias eorum infirmas, in Christum peccatis »; et maxime, quando in parvulos peccant; Matthaei decimo octavo: « Qui scandalizaverit unum de pusillis istis, qui in me credunt, expedit ei » etc. — Et quia perfidiae mater est *concupiscentia,* ideo addit: *Et edere et bibere et inebriari,* faciendo voluntatem scilicet carnis, secundum illud Isaiae vigesimo octavo[^p329-2]: « Sacerdos et propheta nescierunt prae ebrietate; absorpti sunt a vino, erraverunt prae ebrietate ». Et hoc maxime est indecens pastoribus et praelatis, in quibus regnare debet spiritus sapientiae et intellectus; Malachiae secundo[^p329-3]: « Labia sacerdotis custodient scientiam, et legem requirent ex ore eius ». Sed econtra, Isaiae quinquagesimo sexto: « Speculatores eius caeci omnes, nescierunt universi, canes muti, non valentes latrare, videntes vana et amantes somnia; et canes impudentissimi nescierunt saturitatem » etc.

66. (Vers. 46.). Secundo quantum ad *comminationem severitatis iudicii* adiungit: *Veniet dominus servi illius in die, qua non sperat, et hora, qua nescit;* Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo[^p329-4]: « Iuxta est dies perditionis, et adesse festinant tempora »; et Sophoniae primo: « Iuxta est dies Domini magnus, iuxta et velox nimis ». Et licet sit *iuxta,* tamen venit mundanis ex improviso; unde Ecclesiastae nono[^p329-5]: « Nescit homo finem suum, sed sicut pisces capiuntur hamo, et sicut aves laqueo comprehenduntur; sic capiuntur homines tempore malo, cum eis extemplo supervenerit ». — Et quia contemptoribus iudicis grave imminet iudicium, ideo subdit: *Et dividet eum,* scilicet a consortio bonorum, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p329-6]: « Statuet agnos a dextris suis, haedos autem a sinistris ». *Partemque eius cum infidelibus ponet,* qui scilicet iam iudicati sunt; Ioannis tertio[^p329-7]: « Qui non credit iam iudicatus est »; et hoc recte, quia primae ad Timotheum quinto dicitur: « Si quis suorum et maxime domesticorum curam non habet, fidem negavit et est infideli deterior ». Haec *pars* est valde mala, secundum illud Psalmi[^p329-8]: « Ignis, sulphur, spiritus procellarum, pars calicis eorum »; et Apocalypsis vigesimo primo: « Timidis et incredulis et exsecratis et homicidis et fornicatoribus et veneficis et idololatris et omnibus mendacibus pars illorum erit in stagno igne ardenti et sulphure ». Ex quo apparet, quod sicut est magna merces bonorum praelatorum, ita et maxima erit poena reproborum; Sapientiae sexto[^p329-9]: « Iudicium durissimum in his qui praesunt, fiet. Exiguo enim conceditur misericordia, potentes autem potenter tormenta patientur ».

67. (Vers. 47.). *Ille autem servus* etc. Postquam invitavit ad vigiliam et vituperavit insolentiam, hic tertio *aggravat negligentiam*[^p329-10]; quod facit dupliciter, scilicet ratione *maioritatis scientiae* et ratione *auctoritatis commissae.*

Primo ergo quantum ad *aggravationem negligentiae* ratione *maioritatis scientiae* dicit: *Ille autem servus, qui cognovit voluntatem domini sui,* per donum scientiae; primae ad Corinthios octavo[^p329-11]: « Scientia inflat, caritas aedificat. Si quis autem existimat, se scire aliquid; nondum cognovit, quemadmodum oporteat eum scire ». — *Et non se*[^p329-12] *praeparavit,* ad suscipiendum donum gratiae; Proverbiorum decimo sexto: « Hominis est praeparare animam » etc.; unde primi Regum septimo: « Praeparate corda vestra Domino ». — *Et non fecit secundum voluntatem eius,* supple: per vitium propriae negligentiae; Iacobi quarto[^p329-13]: « Scienti bonum et non facienti peccatum est illi ». — *Vapulabit multis,* scilicet per severitatem divinae sententiae; unde secundae Petri secundo: « Melius erat viam veritatis non cognoscere » etc.; et ad Romanos primo: « Qui, cum Deum cognovissent, non sicut Deum glorificaverunt » etc. « Propter quod tradidit illos Deus in desideria cordis eorum, in immunditiam, ut contumeliis afficiant corpora sua in semetipsis ». Et ratio huius est, quia, ut dicit Gregorius[^p329-14], « ubi maius est donum scientiae, ibi transgressor maiori subiacet culpae »; unde et ignorantia excusat.

68. (Vers. 48.). Propter quod subdit: *Qui autem non cognovit*[^p329-15], per defectum notitiae, *et fecit*
<!-- page 330 -->
*digna plagis,* per commissionem culpae, *vapulabit paucis,* per mitigationem divinae sententiae.

Et nota, quod est ignorantia *simplex,* et haec excusat, secundum illud primae ad Timotheum primo[^p330-1]: Ideo « misericordiam Dei consecutus sum, quia ignorans feci »; et infra vigesimo tertio: « Pater, ignosce eis, quia nesciunt, quid faciunt ». Est et alia ignorantia, *affectata,* de qua in Psalmo[^p330-2]: « Noluit intelligere, ut bene ageret ». Tales graviter punientur; unde primae ad Corinthios decimo quarto: « Qui ignorat ignorabitur »; et Osee quarto: « Populus non intelligens vapulabit ». Et hoc dicit Beda in Glossa[^p330-3], quia Proverbiorum vigesimo octavo: « Qui avertet aurem suam, ne audiat legem, oratio sua erit exsecrabilis »; et Psalmus: « Furor illis secundum similitudinem serpentis, sicut aspidis surdae et obturantis aures suas » etc.

69. Secundo quantum ad *aggravationem negligentiae* ratione *auctoritatis commissae* dicit: *Omni autem, cui multum datum est,* scilicet in munere, *multum quaeretur ab eo,* in ratione. Unde Gregorius[^p330-4]: « Cum augentur dona, rationes etiam crescunt donorum. Tanto ergo humilior atque ad serviendum Deo promptior quisque debet esse ex munere, quanto se obligatiorem esse conspicit in reddenda ratione »; infra decimo sexto: « Redde rationem villicationis tuae ». — Unde subdit: *Et cui commendaverunt multum,* « scilicet divina iudicia[^p330-5] », vel Pater et Filius et Spiritus sanctus; *plus petent ab eo,* scilicet in futuro iudicio universorum; Matthaei vigesimo quinto: « Post multum vero temporis venit dominus servorum illorum et posuit rationem cum eis ». Unde a praelatis, quibus cura subditorum commissa est, requiret Dominus non tantum animas proprias, verum etiam animas commissas; propter quod dicitur ad Hebraeos ultimo[^p330-6]: « Obedite praepositis vestris et subiacete eis; ipsi enim pervigilant, quasi rationem reddituri pro animabus vestris ». Nam unicuique eorum dicitur illud tertii Regum vigesimo[^p330-7]: « Custodi virum istum; qui si lapsus fuerit, erit anima tua pro anima illius »; et Ieremiae decimo tertio: « Ubi est grex, qui datus est tibi, pecus inclytum tuum? Quid dices, cum visitaverit te? Tu enim docuisti eos adversum te et erudisti in caput tuum ». Et ideo Actuum vigesimo[^p330-8] dicebat Paulus ad maiores natu Ecclesiae: « Attendite vobis et universo gregi, in quo vos Spiritus sanctus posuit episcopos regere Ecclesiam Dei, quam acquisivit sanguine suo »; et Ezechielis trigesimo quarto: « Ecce, ego ipse super pastores requiram gregem meum de manu eorum et cessare eos faciam, ut ultra non pascant gregem, nec pascant amplius pastores semetipsos ».

#### They are invited to the solicitude of providence in two ways.

49. *Let your loins be girt* etc. After he called them back from the solicitude of avarice, here secondly he *invites them to the solicitude of providence,* lest anyone should believe that he wished to remove all solicitude from the heart[^p324-1]. Now he invites them to solicitude of this kind from the consideration of a twofold coming: first, namely, by the consideration of *the second coming,* which will be terrible; but secondly, by the consideration of *the first coming,* which was lovable, there[^p324-2]: *I am come to cast fire on the earth.*

#### First they are invited by the consideration of the second coming.

The first part has two: in the first of them he invites *all in common* to the solicitude of vigilance; but in the second, *the prelates specially,* there[^p324-3]: *And Peter said to him.*

Concerning *the general introduction of all* to the solicitude of vigilance he proceeds in this order: first putting before them *the manner of vigilance;* secondly adjoining *the motive,* there[^p324-4]: *Blessed are those servants* etc.; thirdly subjoining *the spur,* there: *But this know ye* etc. The first of these regards *merit,* the second regards *the reward,* the third regards *the imminent danger.*

But concerning *the manner of vigilance* the Evangelist introduces two things, to which the Lord invites the disciples, namely to *the readiness of being unencumbered in the body* and to *the solicitude of expectation in the heart.*

50. (Vers. 35.). First, then, as to *the readiness of being unencumbered in the body,* he says: *Let your loins be girt* etc. As he is unencumbered who is girt to go forward on the way, so he is unencumbered who restrains the carnal desires in himself. Whence Gregory[^p324-5]: « By the name of *the loins* lust is designated, from the principal sex »; Job, the fortieth chapter: « His strength is in his loins » etc. « *We gird* our *loins,* then, when we straiten the lust of the flesh by continence. But because it is not enough not to do evil things, unless each one also study to toil in good works, there is subjoined forthwith: *And lamps burning in your hands.* For we have lamps burning in our hands when through good works we show examples of light to our neighbours ». For the divine commandment is rightly called *a lamp;* Proverbs, the sixth chapter[^p324-6]: « The commandment is a lamp, and the law a light » etc. This lamp is *in the hand* when the commandment is in operation; Proverbs, the last chapter: « Her lamp shall not be put out in the night. But she hath put out her hand to strong things » etc.; and Matthew, the fifth chapter: « So let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works » etc. — And note that, as a lamp hides its lumen[^tr-lumen] from the wind, but not from sight, so good works are compared to a lamp; because[^p324-7] « the work ought so to be in public, that the intention remain in secret »; so let a man wish to give others an example of virtue, that yet he seek not the reward[^p324-8] of transitory favour.

51. (Vers. 36.). Secondly, as to *the solicitude of expectation in the heart,* he subjoins: *And you like to men who expect their lord, when he shall return from the wedding,* that is, when he shall come to the judgement, descending from heaven. Whence Gregory[^p324-9]: « The Lord went away to the wedding when after his resurrection, the new man, he coupled to himself the multitude of the Angels; who will return then, when he is manifested to us through the judgement ». Whence he ought always to be expected by the good; to the Philippians, the third chapter[^p324-10]: « We expect the Saviour, our Lord Jesus Christ » etc. And this expectation is not *vain;* Proverbs, the seventeenth chapter: « The expectation of him that expecteth is a most acceptable jewel »; nor is it *drowsy;* the Psalm: « Expect the Lord, do manfully ». — And therefore he subjoins: *That when he cometh,* « hastening to the judgement[^p324-11] », *and knocketh,* through the scourge of sickness, *they may open to him immediately,* through inmost desire; the Apocalypse, the third chapter: « Behold, I stand at the gate and knock; if any man shall open to me, I will come in to him » etc. Bede[^p324-12]: « He does not wish to open to the judge knocking who, fearing to see angry him whom he contemned,
<!-- page 325 -->
fears to go out of the body. He opens who awaits the judge glad and secure and rejoices at the nearness of death »; the Canticle, the fifth chapter[^p325-1]: « The voice of my beloved knocking »; and after: « I arose to open » etc.

52. And it is to be noted here that this Gospel is read on the feasts of Confessors, because they are commended in three ways, namely as to *the declining of evil* in the girding of the loins, and as to *the operation of good* in the carrying of lamps, and as to *the expectation of the best* in the likeness of men who expect their lord; according to those three things which are said in Micheas, the sixth chapter[^p325-2]: « I will shew thee, O man, what is good: to do judgement », in respect of thyself, « and to love mercy », in respect of thy neighbour, « and to walk solicitously with thy God », in respect of God.

53. But in order that *evil* may *perfectly fail* in us, *our loins are to be girt in three ways,* namely the loins of carnal *contact,* of which in the Psalm[^p325-3]: « My loins are filled with illusions; and there is no health in my flesh »; and these are to be girt with the girdle of *chastity;* Isaias, the thirty-second chapter: « Gird your loins, mourn for your breasts » etc. — Likewise, the loins of carnal *affection* with the girdle of *virtue;* Job, the fortieth chapter[^p325-4]: « Gird up thy loins like a man »; and Jeremias, the first chapter: « Gird up thy loins, and arise, and speak to them ». — Likewise, the loins of carnal *thought* with the girdle of *truth;* the first of Peter, the first chapter[^p325-5]: « Having the loins of your mind girt up, being sober » etc.

54. But in order that *good* may *perfectly shine forth* in us, *the lamp of right intention is to be carried;* above, the eleventh chapter[^p325-6]: « The lamp of thy body is thy eye ». — Likewise, the lamp *of true preaching* is to be carried; the Psalm[^p325-7]: « Thy word is a lamp to my feet »; Ecclesiasticus, the forty-eighth chapter: « Elias stood up as a fire, and his word burnt like a torch ». — Likewise, the lamp *of honest conversation* is to be carried; John, the fifth chapter[^p325-8]: « He was a burning and a shining lamp »; and in the Psalm: « There will I bring forth a horn to David: I have prepared a lamp for my anointed ».

55. Lastly, in order that *desire* may *perfectly take fire, Christ is to be expected confidently;* Habacuc, the second chapter[^p325-9]: « If he make any delay, expect him, for coming he shall come » etc. — Likewise he is to be expected *joyfully;* Proverbs, the tenth chapter: « The expectation of the just is joy » etc. — Likewise he is to be expected *watchfully;* Job, the fourteenth chapter: « All the days in which I am now in warfare, I expect until my change come ». And in this manner the servants expect « their lord, when he shall return from the wedding[^p325-10] ». — Thus the blessed Confessors are perfectly praised, according to those words to Titus, the second chapter: « Let us live soberly, and justly, and piously in this world, expecting the blessed hope and coming of the glory of the great God » etc.

56. (Vers. 37.). *Blessed are those servants* etc. Here secondly he introduces *the motive for watching solicitously*[^p325-11]; and this is twofold, namely the beatification of those who watch, without *failing* and without any *exception.*

First, then, as to *the beatification of those who watch without failing,* he says: *Blessed are those servants, whom the lord when he cometh shall find watching;* Proverbs, the eighth chapter[^p325-12]: « Blessed is the man that heareth me, and that watcheth daily at my gates »; and therefore Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-ninth chapter: « The just man will give his heart to watch early in the morning » etc. Such the Lord beatifies; Job, the eighth chapter: « If thou wilt arise early and wilt beseech the Almighty, he will presently awake unto thee, and will make the dwelling of thy justice peaceable ». — Therefore he subjoins: *Amen I say to you, that he will gird himself,* « preparing himself for the retribution[^p325-13] »; the Psalm: « The Lord hath reigned, he is clothed with beauty » etc. *And make them sit down to meat,* namely in the eternal refreshment; Ezechiel, the thirty-fourth chapter: « I will feed my sheep, and I will cause them to lie down ». — *And passing will minister unto them,* through a most liberal communication. *Passing,* that is, making them pass; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p325-14]: « Pass over to me, all ye that desire me » etc.; because *from Christ* and *through Christ* we pass *to Christ,* namely from the glory of the body to the glory of the soul, and from this to the glory of the Deity. On account of which he says, John, the tenth chapter[^p325-15]: « I am the door; by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved »; and the fourteenth chapter: « I am the way, the truth and the life ». Now Christ is said *to minister,* because he will always give the matter of actual joy
<!-- page 326 -->
of unfailing refreshment; the Apocalypse, the seventh chapter[^p326-1]: « They shall no more hunger nor thirst »; « for the Lamb, which is in the midst of the throne, shall rule them, and shall lead them to the fountains of the waters of life ». « Blessed then are they that are called to the marriage supper of the Lamb », the Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter; in which the Lamb without spot will be *bridegroom, food, lord* and *minister;* the Psalm[^p326-2]: « They shall be inebriated with the plenty of thy house; and thou shalt make them drink of the torrent of thy pleasure ». He himself *will minister* and *will invite,* according to that of the Canticle, the fifth chapter: « Eat, O friends, and drink, and be inebriated, my dearly beloved ».

57. (Vers. 38.). Next, as to *beatitude without exception,* he subjoins: *And if he shall come in the second watch, and if in the third*[^p326-3] *he shall come and so shall find, blessed are those servants.* And note here that by *the three watches* are understood three states of the present life, namely *of boyhood, of youth* and *of old age.* Whence the Gloss of Bede[^p326-4]: « He calls them watches after the likeness of those keeping guard in the night. The first watch is the guard of boyhood, the second is of youth, and the third of old age. If anyone has neglected to watch in *boyhood,* let him not however despair; if he has neglected in *youth,* let him at least at length come to his senses in *old age,* because the pious[^tr-pietas] Lord expects our penance patiently »; Isaias, the thirtieth chapter[^p326-5]: « Therefore the Lord expecteth, that he may have mercy on you; and therefore shall he be exalted sparing you, because the Lord is the God of judgement; blessed are all they that expect him ». — And note that in Mark, the thirteenth chapter[^p326-6], *four* watches are intimated, according to the manner of distinguishing the hours among those who keep guard: « Watch, he says; for you know not when the Lord cometh: at even, or at midnight, or at the cockcrowing, or in the morning ». And by *these watches* are understood four states in which a man uses the freedom of choice: the first and the last and two intermediate; one is in advancing, and the other in declining. In which it is intimated that the Lord accepts our watch at every hour without exception, but most of all that which begins from boyhood; Lamentations, the third chapter[^p326-7]: « It is good for a man, when he hath borne the yoke from his youth »; and yet he does not refuse even the last old age; whence it is said in Matthew, the fourteenth chapter, that « in the fourth watch of the night he came to the disciples walking upon the sea ». At whatever hour, then, it is not useless, but very useful *to watch;* below, the twenty-first chapter[^p326-8]: « Watch ye, praying at all times, that you may be accounted worthy to escape all these things that are to come, and to stand before the Son of man » etc.

58. (Vers. 39.). *But this know ye* etc. Here thirdly he subjoins *the spur to watching*[^p326-9]; which he introduces in this manner, namely *by setting forth a parabolic example* and *by concluding the principal intent.*

First, then, *he expounds the parabolic example,* when he says: *But this know ye, that if the householder did know at what hour the thief would come, he would surely watch,* namely for the guarding of the house[^p326-10], lest the thief carry off anything by stealth. — Therefore he says: *And would not suffer his house to be broken open.* And if he always held the hour suspect, he would never leave his house without a guard; otherwise he would procure the care of the household not wisely, but foolishly. An example of Isboseth, of whom it is read in the second of Kings, the fourth chapter[^p326-11], that « Isboseth was sleeping upon his bed at noon. And the portress cleansing wheat fell asleep. And Rechab and Baana his brother entered the house secretly, and struck him in the groin, and fled ». So too it happens *spiritually* to him who neglects to guard his house watchfully; whence Gregory[^p326-12]: « While the portress sleeps, Isboseth is slain, because, when the solicitude of discretion has ceased, it lays open the way to the malignant spirits for slaying the soul ». And therefore the spiritual man says on the contrary that of Isaias, the twenty-first chapter[^p326-13]: « I am upon the watchtower of the Lord, standing continually by day, and I am upon my ward, standing whole nights »; and therefore the first of Peter, the last chapter: « Be sober and watch, because your adversary » etc.

59. (Vers. 40.). Next he concludes the principal intent. *And be you ready, for at what hour you think not, the Son of man will come.* The Gloss[^p326-14]: « The Lord willed the last hour always to be unknown, that it may always be suspect, and that we may always be prepared for it ». Whence Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p326-15]: « Of that day and hour no one knoweth » etc.; and the first to the Thessalonians, the fifth chapter: « The day of the Lord shall come as a thief in the night »; and after: « But you, brethren, are not in darkness, that that day
<!-- page 327 -->
should overtake you as a thief »; and Ecclesiastes, the ninth chapter: « Man knoweth not his own end, but as fishes are taken with the hook, so men are taken in the evil time ». So too those who do not prepare themselves; therefore it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the fifth chapter[^p327-1]: « Delay not to be converted to the Lord, and defer it not from day to day; for his wrath shall come on a sudden, and in the time of vengeance he will destroy thee ». Whence Alcuin[^p327-2]: « It is a dissolute thought to think of tomorrow's conversion and to neglect today's ». And Seneca[^p327-3]: « Every day of our life is to be ordered as the last ». On account of this, then, that we may always be ready, the Lord willed us not to know the hour of death and the day of judgement. « For nothing is more certain than death, and nothing is more uncertain than the hour of death[^p327-4] »; therefore Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter: « Remember my judgement, for thine also shall be so: yesterday for me, and today for thee »; and of the hour of judgement it is said in Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter: « At midnight there was a cry made: Behold, the bridegroom cometh »; and after: « They that were ready went in with him to the marriage, and the door was shut ». Gregory[^p327-5]: « O if it could be tasted with the palate of the heart, what of wonder *the bridegroom cometh* has! what of sweetness *they went in with him to the marriage!* what of bitterness *the door was shut* »!

60. (Vers. 41.). *And Peter said to him*[^p327-6]. After he invited all generally to vigilance, here he invites *the prelates specially.* Which indeed he does in this order. For first he *commends the vigilance of prelates;* secondly he *reproves their insolence,* there[^p327-7]: *But if that servant shall say* etc.; thirdly he *aggravates their negligence,* there: *And that servant who knew.*

Now *commending the vigilance of prelates,* he first commends it as to the prerogative *of merit;* secondly, as to *the pre-eminence of the reward;* there[^p327-8]: *Blessed is that servant.*

The first he shows in answering Peter's question, when he says: *And Peter said to him: Lord, dost thou speak this parable to us, or*[^p327-9] *to all?* He asks well and usefully, that he may know to what he ought to be bound; whence Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter: « The things that God hath commanded thee, think on them always »; and those things are most of all to be learned which are spoken in parables; Proverbs, the first chapter: « A wise man hearing shall be wiser. He shall observe parables and the interpretation, the words of the wise and their mysterious sayings ».

61. (Vers. 42.). Now to this question of Peter's the Lord does not seem on the surface to answer; but if it is attended to more deeply, he answers perfectly, intimating that the aforesaid parable of vigilance looks *in common to all,* but *specially to the prelates,* when he says: *And the Lord said: Who, thinkest thou, is the faithful and prudent dispenser? Dispensers* he calls the prelates; the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter[^p327-10]: « Let a man so account of us as of the ministers of Christ and the dispensers of the mysteries of God » etc. Such it is difficult to find faithful; whence the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « Here now it is required among the dispensers, that a man be found faithful »; and Proverbs, the twentieth chapter: « Many men are called merciful, but who shall find a faithful man »? *Who, thinkest thou,* then, *is the faithful dispenser?* in *intention;* the first to Timothy, the first chapter[^p327-11]: « I have obtained mercy, that I might be faithful »; and the Apocalypse, the second chapter: « Be thou faithful unto death ». *And prudent,* in *choice;* Proverbs, the fourteenth chapter[^p327-12]: « An understanding minister is acceptable to the king »; whence Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter: « Be diligent to know the countenance of thy cattle, and consider thy own flocks ».

62. *Whom the Lord hath appointed over*[^p327-13] *his family,* through the authority committed; to the Hebrews, the fifth chapter: « Neither doth any man take the honour to himself, but he that is called by the Lord, as Aaron was »; the Psalm: « But I am appointed king by him over Sion, his holy mountain ». — *To give them their measure of wheat in due season,* by communicating sound doctrine; Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter[^p327-14]: « The heart of a wise man understandeth time and answer » etc. Let him attend also to the capacity of the hearers; whence he says: *measure.* Gregory: « According to
<!-- page 328 -->
the quality of the hearers ought the discourse of teachers to be formed, that it may both give to each what suits him, and yet never withdraw from the citadel of common edification ». Of this we have an example in Joseph, who was appointed over the land of Egypt to dispense the grain, Genesis, the forty-first chapter[^p328-1], in whom there was *fidelity* and *prudence, authority* and *vigilance* or diligence. — And because these things were in the holy prelates, therefore this Gospel is sung in praise of Confessors and Pontiffs, who in these four aforesaid things stood forth commendable. — And of these four it is said in the Gloss of Bede[^p328-2]: « How rare is one serving the Lord *for the Lord's sake,* feeding the sheep of Christ not for gain, but for the love of Christ! *prudent,* providing for himself in the future; *whom he hath appointed,* that is, how rare is he who is *called by God as Aaron was* and has not rather thrust himself in, and who does not *feed himself* more than the sheep ». Whence on account of *the defect of fidelity,* Malachias, the first chapter[^p328-3]: « Who is there among you that will shut my doors and will kindle my altar gratis »? and to the Philippians, the second chapter: « All seek the things that are their own » etc. Few too are *prudent;* whence Isaias, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p328-4]: « As with the people, so with the priest »; and Osee, the fourth chapter: « Because thou hast rejected knowledge, I will reject thee, that thou shalt not do the office of priesthood to me ». Few too are *divinely appointed;* therefore it is said in Osee, the eighth chapter[^p328-5]: « They have reigned, but not by me ». Few likewise are *intent upon the profit of the flock;* whence Ezechiel, the thirty-fourth chapter: « Woe to the shepherds of Israel, that fed themselves! Are not the flocks fed by the shepherds » etc. Therefore, speaking pointedly, he puts first: *Who, thinkest thou?* on account of the rarity and the difficulty and the preciousness by which such men excel in the prerogative of merit.

63. (Vers. 43.). Secondly, as to *the pre-eminence of the reward,* he subjoins: *Blessed is that servant, whom when his lord shall come, he shall find so doing;* Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p328-6]: « Well done, good and faithful servant » etc. *So doing,* that is, *faithfully, prudently* and *watchfully,* according to that of the first of Peter, the fourth chapter[^p328-7]: « As every man hath received grace, ministering the same one to another, as good dispensers of the manifold grace of God ». And Chrysostom: « These things are said of speech and of power, of money and of every dispensation which is committed to each one. For each one ought to use the things he has for the common profit, whether wisdom, or rule, or riches, not for the hurt of his fellow servants ».

64. (Vers. 44.). Now to the use of this grace there is rendered *the perfect fruit of glory,* as to which he subjoins: *Verily I say to you, that over*[^p328-8] *all that he possesseth he will set him,* « over the joys of the heavenly kingdom », « not that they alone, but that before the rest they may have eternal joys, both for their own life and for the guarding of the flock ». Whence the first of Peter, the fifth chapter[^p328-9]: « Ancients, feed the flock of God which is among you ». « And when the prince of pastors shall appear, you shall receive a never fading crown of glory ». Whence Chrysostom[^p328-10]: « When the people do well, each one is rewarded for his own good; but the priest for the goods of all »; the first to the Thessalonians, the second chapter: « What is my hope, or joy, or crown of glory? Are not you, before our Lord Jesus Christ at his coming »?

65. (Vers. 45.). *But if that servant shall say* etc. After he commended the vigilance and providence or care of prelates, here he *upbraids their insolence*[^p328-11]; which indeed he does *by reproving the enormity of the sin* and *by threatening the severity of the judgement.*

First, then, as to the reproving *of the enormity of the sin,* he says: *But if that servant shall say in his heart: My lord delayeth to come*[^p328-12]. The Gloss: « On the contrary, if he has dissembled the judgement soon to come, promising himself security », according to that of Ezechiel, the twelfth chapter: « What is this proverb in the land of Israel of them that say: The days shall be prolonged, and every vision shall fail »? and the second of Peter, the third chapter[^p328-13]: « Scoffers shall come, walking after their own lusts, saying: Where is the promise or his coming? For since the fathers slept, all things so continue from the beginning of the creation ». — And since such a thought begets security, and security contempt, and contempt iniquity; therefore he subjoins: *And shall begin to strike the boys*[^p328-14] *and the maidservants,* namely by scandalizing the little ones, when yet he ought to
<!-- page 329 -->
cherish them by word and example. On account of which Isaias, the third chapter[^p329-1]: « Why do you consume my people, and grind the faces of the poor »? And this is a grave sin; the first to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter: « Sinning against the brethren, and wounding their weak consciences, you sin against Christ »; and most of all when they sin against the little ones; Matthew, the eighteenth chapter: « He that shall scandalize one of these little ones that believe in me, it is expedient for him » etc. — And because *concupiscence* is the mother of perfidy, therefore he adds: *And to eat and to drink and be drunk,* doing, namely, the will of the flesh, according to that of Isaias, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p329-2]: « The priest and the prophet have been ignorant through drunkenness; they are swallowed up with wine, they have gone astray in drunkenness ». And this is most of all unbecoming to shepherds and prelates, in whom the spirit of wisdom and of understanding ought to reign; Malachias, the second chapter[^p329-3]: « The lips of the priest shall keep knowledge, and they shall seek the law at his mouth ». But on the contrary, Isaias, the fifty-sixth chapter: « His watchmen are all blind, they are all ignorant, dumb dogs not able to bark, seeing vain things and loving dreams; and most impudent dogs, they knew no satiety » etc.

66. (Vers. 46.). Secondly, as to *the threatening of the severity of the judgement,* he adjoins: *The lord of that servant will come in the day that he hopeth not, and at the hour that he knoweth not;* Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter[^p329-4]: « The day of destruction is at hand, and the times make haste to come »; and Sophonias, the first chapter: « The great day of the Lord is near, it is near and exceeding swift ». And though it be *near,* yet it comes upon worldly men unawares; whence Ecclesiastes, the ninth chapter[^p329-5]: « Man knoweth not his own end, but as fishes are taken with the hook, and as birds are caught with the snare, so men are taken in the evil time, when it shall suddenly come upon them ». — And because a grave judgement threatens the contemners of the judge, therefore he subjoins: *And shall separate him,* namely from the fellowship of the good, according to that of Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p329-6]: « He shall set the lambs on his right hand, but the goats on his left ». *And shall appoint him his portion with unbelievers,* who namely are already judged; John, the third chapter[^p329-7]: « He that doth not believe is already judged »; and this rightly, because it is said in the first to Timothy, the fifth chapter: « If any man have not care of his own, and especially of those of his house, he hath denied the faith, and is worse than an unbeliever ». This *portion* is very evil, according to that of the Psalm[^p329-8]: « Fire and brimstone and storms of winds shall be the portion of their cup »; and the Apocalypse, the twenty-first chapter: « For the fearful and unbelieving and the abominable and murderers and whoremongers and sorcerers and idolaters and all liars, their portion shall be in the pool burning with fire and brimstone ». From which it appears that, as the reward of good prelates is great, so too the punishment of the reprobate will be very great; Wisdom, the sixth chapter[^p329-9]: « A most severe judgement shall be for them that bear rule. For to him that is little, mercy is granted, but the mighty shall be mightily tormented ».

67. (Vers. 47.). *And that servant* etc. After he invited them to watching and upbraided their insolence, here thirdly he *aggravates their negligence*[^p329-10]; which he does in two ways, namely by reason of *greater knowledge* and by reason of *the authority committed.*

First, then, as to *the aggravation of negligence* by reason of *greater knowledge,* he says: *And that servant who knew the will of his lord,* through the gift of knowledge; the first to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter[^p329-11]: « Knowledge puffeth up, but charity edifieth. And if any man think that he knoweth anything, he hath not yet known as he ought to know ». — *And prepared not*[^p329-12] *himself,* to receive the gift of grace; Proverbs, the sixteenth chapter: « It is the part of man to prepare the soul » etc.; whence the first of Kings, the seventh chapter: « Prepare your hearts unto the Lord ». — *And did not according to his will,* supply: through the vice of his own negligence; James, the fourth chapter[^p329-13]: « To him who knoweth good and doth it not, to him it is sin ». — *Shall be beaten with many stripes,* namely through the severity of the divine sentence; whence the second of Peter, the second chapter: « It had been better not to have known the way of truth » etc.; and to the Romans, the first chapter: « Who, when they knew God, did not glorify him as God » etc. « Wherefore God gave them up to the desires of their heart, unto uncleanness, to dishonour their own bodies among themselves ». And the reason of this is because, as Gregory says[^p329-14], « where the gift of knowledge is greater, there the transgressor is subject to greater guilt »; whence also ignorance excuses.

68. (Vers. 48.). On account of which he subjoins: *But he that knew not*[^p329-15], through the defect of knowledge, *and did*
<!-- page 330 -->
*things worthy of stripes,* through the commission of guilt, *shall be beaten with few stripes,* through the mitigation of the divine sentence.

And note that there is a *simple* ignorance, and this excuses, according to that of the first to Timothy, the first chapter[^p330-1]: Therefore « I obtained the mercy of God, because I did it ignorantly »; and below, the twenty-third chapter: « Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do ». There is also another ignorance, *affected,* of which in the Psalm[^p330-2]: « He would not understand, that he might do well ». Such will be gravely punished; whence the first to the Corinthians, the fourteenth chapter: « He that knoweth not shall not be known »; and Osee, the fourth chapter: « The people that doth not understand shall be beaten ». And Bede says this in the Gloss[^p330-3], because Proverbs, the twenty-eighth chapter: « He that turneth away his ear from hearing the law, his prayer shall be an abomination »; and the Psalm: « Their madness is according to the likeness of a serpent, like the deaf asp that stoppeth her ears » etc.

69. Secondly, as to *the aggravation of negligence* by reason of *the authority committed,* he says: *And unto whomsoever much is given,* namely in office, *of him much shall be required,* in account. Whence Gregory[^p330-4]: « When the gifts are increased, the accounts of the gifts also grow. Therefore each one ought to be so much the humbler and the readier to serve God from his office, as he sees himself the more bound in rendering account »; below, the sixteenth chapter: « Render an account of thy stewardship ». — Whence he subjoins: *And to whom they have committed much,* « namely the divine judgements[^p330-5] », or the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit; *of him they will demand the more,* namely in the future judgement of all; Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter: « But after a long time the lord of those servants came, and reckoned with them ». Whence from the prelates, to whom the care of subjects is committed, the Lord will require not only their own souls, but also the souls committed to them; on account of which it is said to the Hebrews, the last chapter[^p330-6]: « Obey your prelates, and be subject to them; for they watch, as being to render an account for your souls ». For to each of them is said that of the third of Kings, the twentieth chapter[^p330-7]: « Keep this man; who if he shall slip away, thy soul shall be for his soul »; and Jeremias, the thirteenth chapter: « Where is the flock that is given thee, thy beautiful flock? What wilt thou say when he shall visit thee? For thou hast taught them against thee, and instructed them against thy head ». And therefore in the Acts, the twentieth chapter[^p330-8], Paul said to the elders of the Church: « Take heed to yourselves and to the whole flock, wherein the Holy Spirit hath placed you bishops, to rule the Church of God, which he hath purchased with his blood »; and Ezechiel, the thirty-fourth chapter: « Behold, I myself will require my flock of the shepherds at their hand, and I will cause them to cease, that they feed the flock no more, nor shall the shepherds feed themselves any more ».
