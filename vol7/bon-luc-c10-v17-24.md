# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. X, Vers. 17-24
## *Reversi sunt autem etc. Post missionem concomitantium et informationem euntium subditur hic tertio consolatio redeuntium*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 261 -->

28. *Reversi sunt autem* etc. Post missionem concomitantium et informationem euntium subditur hic tertio *consolatio redeuntium*[^p261-4]. Habet autem haec pars duas. In quarum prima *reprimit Dominus in discipulis gaudium vanum;* in secunda *invitat eos ad gaudium verum,* ibi[^p261-5]: *Gaudete autem, quia nomina vestra* etc.

Circa *repressionem gaudii vani* in discipulis quatuor introducit. Primum est vanae exsultationis *expressio;* secundum est vanae exsultationis *repressio;* tertium est vanae exsultationis *occasio;* quartum est vanae exsultationis *prohibitio.*

29. (Vers. 17.). Primo igitur praemittit *vanae exsultationis expressionem,* cum dicit: *Reversi sunt autem septuaginta duo cum gaudio,* quod scilicet non tantum sentiebatur in corde, sed etiam exprimebatur in sermone, ut verificaretur illud Proverbiorum decimo quinto[^p261-6]: « Cor gaudens exhilarat faciem ». — Propter quod addit: *Dicentes: Domine, etiam daemonia subiiciuntur nobis in nomine tuo;* in quo exprimebant, se gaudere, quia fortiores daemonibus facti erant. Et ista quidem laetitia veniebat ex latente superbia, sicut et illius Pharisaei, de quo infra decimo octavo[^p261-7] dicitur, quod orans dicebat: « Gratias tibi, Deus, ago, quia non sum sicut ceteri hominum » etc.

30. Et nota, quod de divinis donis quidam *manifeste efferuntur,* sicut illi qui sibi ea attribuunt; de quibus Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo[^p261-8]: « Manus mea excelsa, et non Dominus, fecit haec omnia ». Contra quos Deuteronomii nono: « Ne dicas in corde tuo, cum deleverit Dominus gentes in conspectu tuo: Propter iustitiam meam induxit me Dominus in terram hanc ». — Quidam autem *manifeste humiliantur,* de quibus dicitur infra decimo septimo[^p261-9]: « Cum omnia bene feceritis, dicite, quia servi inutiles sumus ». Talis erat Paulus, qui de se ipso primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto: « Ego sum minimus Apostolorum, qui non sum dignus vocari Apostolus. Gratia autem Dei sum id quod sum, et gratia eius in me vacua non fuit, sed abundavit » etc. — Quidam vero *partim humiliantur* gratias agendo, *partim efferuntur* exsultando inepte, sicut isti discipuli, qui gaudebant de subiectione daemonum, attribuebant tamen hoc non sibi, sed nomini Domini, cum dicunt: *in nomine tuo,* sicut ille Pharisaeus[^p261-10]. Unde aliquid habebant *commendabile,* hoc scilicet, quod revertebantur ad fontem gratiarum, gratias ab ipso susceptas recognoscendo et eidem gratias referendo; iuxta quod dicitur Ecclesiastae primo[^p261-11]: « Ad locum, unde exeunt flumina, revertuntur, ut iterum fluant »; et Iob trigesimo octavo: « Nunquid mittes fulgura, et ibunt, et revertentia dicent tibi: Adsumus »? Super quo dicit Glossa: « *Vadunt fulgura,* cum praedicatores miraculis coruscant et superna reverentia subditorum corda transfigunt; *revertentia dicunt: Adsumus,* cum non sibi, sed Dei virtutibus tribuunt, quidquid se fortiter egisse cognoscunt ». Tales sunt viri perfecti, in quorum persona dicitur Isaiae vigesimo sexto[^p261-12]: « Omnia opera nostra operatus es in nobis, Domine »; et in Psalmo: « Non nobis, Domine, non nobis, sed nomini tuo da gloriam ». — Aliquid etiam habebant *reprehensibile* in hoc, quod de potestatis altitudine efferebantur; quod
<!-- page 262 -->
patet in hoc quod aiunt: *Daemonia subiiciuntur nobis;* contra quod Ecclesiastici undecimo[^p262-1]: « In die honoris tui ne extollaris, quoniam mirabilia opera Altissimi solius, et gloriosa et absconsa et invisa opera illius ». Et hoc est quod dicit Beda: « Bene confitentur, sed quia de signorum operatione efferuntur, exemplo terrentur et ad humilitatem revocantur ».

31. (Vers. 18.). Secundo adiungit *vanae exsultationis repressionem,* cum addit: *Et ait illis: Videbam satanam sicut fulgur de caelo cadentem;* quasi dicat Dominus: ne extollamini divinis beneficiis, ne cum superbis angelis corruatis; secundae Petri secundo[^p262-2]: « Si Deus peccantibus angelis non pepercit, sed rudentibus inferni detractos in tartarum tradidit cruciandos »; supple: non vobis parcet, si peccaveritis superbiendo. Unde Bernardus[^p262-3]: « Superbis angelis non pepercit Dominus; quanto magis nec tibi, putredo et vermis »! — *Cecidit* autem *sicut fulgur,* hoc est velociter, crudeliter et irrevocabiliter. Unde exemplo eius ceteri caveant, ne quaerant alta, propter quae lucifer cecidit in ima, secundum illud Isaiae decimo quarto[^p262-4]: « Quomodo cecidisti, lucifer, qui mane oriebaris? Qui dicebas in corde tuo: In caelum conscendam, super astra caeli exaltabo solium meum »; et post: « Verumtamen ad infernum detraheris in profundum laci ». Et ideo ad Romanos undecimo: « Noli altum sapere, sed time. Si enim Deus naturalibus ramis non pepercit, ne forte nec tibi parcat ». — Ex hoc exemplo miro modo repressit Dominus in discipulis superbiam, quasi dicat illud Proverbiorum decimo septimo[^p262-5]: « Qui altam facit domum suam », scilicet cum lucifero superbiendo, « quaerit ruinam », cum eodem corruendo. Unde Bernardus[^p262-6], loquens ambitioso: « Sequere ducem tuum, multiplica praebendas, accelera ad episcopatum, ad archiepiscopatum aspira; paulatim ascendis, sed non paulatim descendes. *Videbam satanam quasi fulgur de caelo cadentem* ». Et ideo econtra dicit Seneca[^p262-7]: « Redige te ad parva, a quibus cadere non potes ». Unde Proverbiorum decimo octavo: « Antequam conteratur, exaltatur cor hominis; et antequam glorificetur, humiliatur »; quia sententia est iudicis, infra decimo octavo: « Omnis, qui se exaltat, humiliabitur, et omnis, qui se humiliat, exaltabitur ».

32. (Vers. 19.). Tertio vero subiungit *vanae exsultationis occasionem,* cum addit: *Ecce, dedi vobis potestatem calcandi supra serpentes et scorpiones,* scilicet quantum ad insidias *corporales,* iuxta quod dicitur serpenti Genesis tertio[^p262-8]: « Ipsa conteret caput tuum, et tu insidiaberis calcaneo eius »; *et super omnem virtutem inimici,* quantum ad insidias *spirituales,* de quibus ad Ephesios sexto[^p262-9]: « Non est nobis colluctatio adversus carnem et sanguinem, sed adversus principes et potestates, adversus mundi rectores » etc.; usque ibi « in caelestibus ». — *Et nihil vobis nocebit,* propter supernas protectiones, secundum illud Marci ultimo[^p262-10]: « In nomine meo daemonia eiicient, linguis loquentur novis, serpentes tollent. Et si mortiferum quid biberint, non eis nocebit ». Et haec potestas eis collata poterat esse occasio praesumendi; iuxta quod dicitur de Ezechia, ad cuius preces Deus fecit mirabilia, secundi Paralipomenon trigesimo secundo[^p262-11]: « Oravit Ezechias Dominum, exaudivitque eum et dedit ei signum; sed non iuxta beneficia, quae acceperat, retribuit, quia elevatum est cor eius, et facta est ira contra eum et contra Iudam et Ierusalem ». — Unde divinorum donorum multitudo multis fuit ruinae occasio, sicut dicitur de lucifero Ezechielis vigesimo octavo[^p262-12]: « Tu signaculum similitudinis, plenus sapientia et perfectus decore »; et paulo post: « Elevatum est cor tuum »; et propterea: « Peccasti, et eieci te de monte sancto Dei et perdidi te » etc. Et ideo dicebat quidam sanctus homo, quod magna gratia est non habere gratiam; quia omnes istae gratiae exteriores non sunt nisi tentationes, et quilibet exterius elevatus debet timere illud Iob trigesimo[^p262-13]:
<!-- page 263 -->
« Elevasti me et quasi super ventum ponens, elisisti me valide ».

33. (Vers. 20.). Quarto vero subiungit *vanae exsultationis prohibitionem,* cum dicit: *Verumtamen in hoc nolite gaudere, quia spiritus subiiciuntur vobis;* quia scilicet tale gaudium est sicut *gaudium superborum* de promotione sua et deiectione aliena, quod non competit veris Sanctis, secundum illud Ecclesiastici octavo[^p263-1]: « Noli de mortuo inimico tuo gaudere »; et Iob trigesimo primo: « Si gavisus sum ad ruinam eius qui me oderat ». *Nolite* etiam *gaudere,* quia tale gaudium est *ruinosum,* secundum illud Proverbiorum decimo quarto[^p263-2]: « Extrema gaudii luctus occupat ». — Tale est gaudium de bono transitorio, secundum illud Iob vigesimo: « Gaudium hypocritae ad instar puncti »; et rursus vigesimo primo: « Gaudent ad sonitum organi, ducunt in bonis dies suos, sed in puncto ad inferna descendunt ». Unde non volebat Dominus, quod discipuli gauderent in miraculis, sed potius in tormentis et conviciis, secundum illud Matthaei quinto[^p263-3]: « Beati estis, cum maledixerint vobis homines et persecuti vos fuerint » etc.; et post: « Gaudete et exsultate, quoniam merces vestra copiosa est in caelis ». Sic etiam Apostoli faciebant, secundum illud Actuum quinto: « Ibant Apostoli gaudentes a conspectu concilii, quia digni habiti sunt pro nomine Iesu contumeliam pati ». *Nolite ergo in hoc gaudere, quia spiritus subiiciuntur vobis,* licet sit divinitus concessum, ne sit occasio superbiae et per consequens ruinae. Propter quod dicit Augustinus[^p263-4], quod « Dominus dicebat discipulis suis: *Discite a me,* non mortuos suscitare, non super aquas ambulare, sed *quia mitis sum et humilis corde* »; quia in miraculis non debebant gaudere, sed in humilitate.

34. *Gaudete autem, quia*[^p263-5] *nomina* etc. Postquam Salvator repressit in discipulis gaudium vanum, hic secundo *invitat eos ad gaudium verum.* Et hoc quidem facit proponendo quadruplicem causam sive materiam gaudii. Gaudium namque discipulis fore ostendit de Dei *praescientia infallibili,* de *providentia irreprehensibili,* de *potentia incomprehensibili,* de *praesentia desiderabili.* Et in his quatuor habebant valde magnam materiam exsultandi.

Primo gaudium erat de Dei *praescientia infallibili;* quantum ad quam dicit: *Gaudete autem, quia nomina vestra scripta sunt,* in libro vitae, *in caelis;* qui *liber vitae* dicitur, quia in ipso scripta *vivunt,* secundum illud Ioannis primo[^p263-6]: « Quod factum est in ipso vita erat »; vel quia secundum eius scripturam est homo praedestinatus ad vitam. Unde secundum ipsum fiet finale iudicium, sicut dicitur Apocalypsis vigesimo[^p263-7]: « Libri aperti sunt, et alius liber apertus est, qui est liber vitae »; in quo quidem libro mali non invenientur finaliter scripti, secundum quod in Psalmo dicitur: « Deleantur de libro viventium et cum iustis non scribantur ». Quod quidem dicitur, non quia liber iste mutetur, sed quia multi videntur *scripti* secundum praesentem iustitiam, qui apparebunt *non scripti* secundum divinam praescientiam[^p263-8]. Quoniam ergo hoc tantum est bonorum finaliter et salvandorum et bonum interminabile et infallibile; ideo merito de hoc gaudendum, non de dono miraculorum, quod est bonorum et malorum. Unde mali non sunt *scripti in caelis,* sed potius *in terra,* secundum illud Ieremiae decimo septimo[^p263-9]: « Recedentes a te in terra scribentur ». In cuius rei signum Dominus, confutans perfidiam Pharisaeorum, qui erant alieni a regno caelorum, « digito scribebat in terra », sicut dicitur Ioannis octavo.

35. Et nota, quod divina cognitio dicitur *liber,* sed cognitio *simplicis notitiae* dicitur *liber simpliciter;* et in hoc omnia scribuntur et omnes, iuxta illud Psalmi[^p263-10]: « Imperfectum meum viderunt oculi tui, et in libro tuo omnes scribentur ». Et est cognitio *approbationis,* et haec dicitur *liber vitae,* et in hoc non scribuntur secundum veritatem nisi finaliter boni; et de hoc Exodi trigesimo secundo[^p263-11]: « Aut dimitte eis hanc noxam, aut dele me de libro
<!-- page 264 -->
tuo, quem scripsisti ». Super quo dicit Glossa, quod « securus hoc dicebat, sciens, quod deleri non possit »; loquitur enim de scriptura praescientiae, non praesentis iustitiae.

36. (Vers. 21.). Secundo ostendit, esse gaudendum de *providentia irreprehensibili;* quantum ad quam adiungit: *In ipsa hora exsultavit in*[^p264-1] *Spiritu sancto,* scilicet in spirituali gaudio, quod debet esse in Spiritu sancto, non in carne, secundum illud ad Romanos decimo quarto: « Regnum Dei non est esca et potus, sed iustitia et pax et gaudium in Spiritu sancto »; unde et gloriosa Virgo supra primo: « Exsultavit, inquit, spiritus meus in Deo salutari meo ». — Et in hoc dedit discipulis modum exsultandi et simul cum hoc expressit, unde exsultare debeant, cum addit: *Confiteor*[^p264-2] *tibi, Pater, Domine caeli et terrae,* quod scilicet non tantum es creator per potentiam, sed etiam gubernator per providentiam, secundum illud Ieremiae vigesimo tertio: « Caelum et terram ego impleo »; et Isaiae ultimo: « Caelum mihi sedes est, terra autem scabellum pedum meorum ». Huic scilicet generali provisori confitetur confessione, scilicet *laudis. Confessio* enim non tantum est *culpae,* secundum illud Iacobi quinto[^p264-3]: « Confitemini alterutrum peccata vestra »; sed etiam *fidei verae,* de qua dicitur ad Romanos decimo: « Corde creditur ad iustitiam, ore autem confessio fit ad salutem »; verum etiam *laudis divinae,* secundum illud ad Hebraeos decimo tertio[^p264-4]: « Offeramus hostiam laudis, id est fructum labiorum nostrorum confitentium nomini eius »; et in Psalmo: « Confitemini Domino, quoniam bonus » etc. Et hoc modo accipitur hic pro *laude* ipsius Dei gubernatoris.

37. Laudat autem eum pro sua *providentia;* propter quam addit: *Quod abscondisti haec a sapientibus et prudentibus et revelasti ea parvulis.* Hoc enim fit secundum iudicium divinae providentiae, quod « Deus superbis resistit et humilibus dat gratiam », sicut dicitur Iacobi quarto[^p264-5]; et in Psalmo: « Excelsus Dominus et humilia respicit et alta a longe cognoscit ». Ideo *haec,* id est mysteria nostrae redemptionis, *abscondit a sapientibus et prudentibus mundi,* qui reputant, se esse *sapientes* in divinis et *prudentes* in temporalibus, iuxta quod dicitur primae ad Corinthios primo[^p264-6]: « Perdam sapientiam sapientium et prudentiam prudentium reprobabo »; et postea subdit: « Stultam fecit Deus sapientiam huius mundi »; et ideo Isaiae quinto: « Vae! qui sapientes estis in oculis vestris et coram vobismetipsis prudentes ». Talibus enim absconduntur divina sacramenta, sed econtra *parvulis revelantur;* unde in Psalmo[^p264-7]: « Declaratio sermonum tuorum illuminat et intellectum dat parvulis ». — Et nota, quod « non dicit *stultis et hebetibus,* sed *parvulis,* id est humilibus, ut ostendat, se non damnare acumen intelligentiae, sed tumorem superbiae », sicut dicit Glossa[^p264-8]. Unde Bernardus: « Humilitas est clavis scientiae », secundum illud Proverbiorum undecimo: « Ubi humilitas, ibi sapientia ».

38. Et quoniam hoc iudicium providentiae est irreprehensibile, ideo addit: *Etiam, Pater, quoniam sic complacuit ante te;* quasi dicat: iuste, eo quod tibi placeat; quoniam, ut dicit Glossa[^p264-9], « iniustum esse non potest quod placuit iusto ». Unde sufficit hanc rationem reddidisse, nec voluit aliam assignare, sive quia ista est *sufficientissima et prima,* secundum illud Exodi trigesimo tertio[^p264-10]: « Miserebor cui voluero, et clemens ero in quem mihi placuerit »; et ad Romanos nono: « Cuius vult miseretur » etc. Sive ut *reprimatur curiositas,* ne discutere audeamus superna iudicia; unde Glossa[^p264-11]: « Ex hoc accipimus humilitatis exemplum, ne temere discutiamus de supernis consiliis »; dicitur enim Proverbiorum vigesimo quinto: « Perscrutator Maiestatis opprimetur a gloria ». Sive ut *ostendat,* quod divina providentia est *laudanda in omnibus factis suis* et omnino in omnibus irreprehensibilis, secundum illud Psalmi[^p264-12]: « Iustus Dominus in omnibus viis suis et sanctus in omnibus operibus suis ». — Gaudendum est igitur et exsultandum de providentia Dei irreprehensibili, non praesumtuosis ratiunculis disceptandum; quia, ut dicit Gregorius[^p264-13], « divina iudicia non sunt temere discutienda, sed formidoloso silentio veneranda ».
<!-- page 265 -->

39. (Vers. 22.). Tertio ostendit, esse gaudendum de *potentia incomprehensibili,* cum ait: *Omnia mihi tradita sunt a Patre meo.* In hoc namque, quod dicit: *Omnia,* infinite, *tradita,* ostendit, potestatem suam esse *universalem,* secundum illud Ioannis primo[^p265-1]: « Omnia per ipsum facta sunt », id est per Verbum; et rursus decimo septimo: « Omnia mea tua sunt, et tua mea sunt ». In hoc quod adiungit: *A Patre meo,* notat, potentiam illam esse *naturalem,* secundum illud Ioannis quinto[^p265-2]: « Quaecumque Pater facit, haec omnia Filius similiter facit ». In utrisque simul ostendit *aequalitatem* et *immensitatem;* unde Glossa: « Cum audis *omnia,* omnipotentem agnoscis; cum audis *tradita,* Filium confiteris, cui per naturam unius substantiae omnia iure sunt propria, non dono collata per gratiam ». Et ex hoc apparet, quod potentia Filii est immensa et incomprehensibilis. — Et postea subdit: *Et nemo novit*[^p265-3]*, quis sit Filius, nisi Pater,* id est, nullus intellectus hoc perfecte comprehendit nisi paternus; et ideo in hoc non excluditur Spiritus sanctus, sed solus intellectus creatus, qui non potest ipsum comprehendere, cum sit immensus. Unde Iob undecimo[^p265-4]: « Forsitan vestigia Dei comprehendes et usque ad perfectum Omnipotentem reperies? Excelsior caelo est, et quid facies » etc.

40. Et quia Filius incomprehensibilis, ideo potest Patrem comprehendere. Propter quod addit: *Et quis sit Pater, nisi Filius;* supple: nemo novit; quia, Ioannis primo[^p265-5], « Deum nemo vidit unquam nisi Unigenitus, qui est in sinu Patris, ipse enarravit ». — Quoniam ipse solus comprehendit, ideo solus potest revelare; et ideo subdit: *Et cui voluerit Filius revelare.* Filius enim Sapientia et Verbum Patris est, et Dicens manifestat se per Verbum suum; unde Sapientiae nono[^p265-6]: « Sensum tuum quis sciet, nisi tu dederis sapientiam de altissimis »? Unde Chrysostomus: « Philosophi de Deo quaerere contendentes, nihil aliud se invenisse confessi sunt, nisi quod incognoscibilis est Deus, sicut qui innavigabilem oceanum usurpat navigare, cum non potest transire, necesse est, ut per eandem viam revertatur; sic illi ab ignorantia coeperunt et in ignorantiam finierunt ». Et ratio huius est, quia non fuerunt discipuli Iesu Christi, qui est veritas[^p265-7], nec habuerunt Spiritum, de quo Ioannis decimo sexto: « Cum venerit ille Spiritus veritatis, docebit vos omnem veritatem ».

41. (Vers. 23.). Quarto ostendit, esse gaudendum de *praesentia desiderabili,* quantum ad quam subdit: *Et conversus ad discipulos suos, dixit*[^p265-8]*: Beati oculi, qui vident quae vos videtis;* ideo merito gaudere potestis, quia videtis me tam mente quam corpore, cum Abraham gavisus fuerit, qui vidit tantum fide, secundum illud Ioannis octavo: « Abraham, pater vester, exsultavit, ut videret diem meum; et vidit et gavisus est ». — Et ideo in figura huius dicitur tertii Regum decimo[^p265-9] ad Salomonem: « Beati viri tui et beati servi tui, hi qui stant coram te semper et audiunt sapientiam tuam ». Hoc dicitur de Salomone sub figura Christi, quia, ut ipse de se dicit Matthaei duodecimo, « ecce, plus quam Salomon hic ». Unde hoc erat donum speciale.

42. (Vers. 24.). Et propterea subdit: *Dico autem*[^p265-10] *vobis, quod multi Prophetae et reges voluerunt videre quae vos videtis, et non viderunt.* Sublimes in scientia et potentia desideraverunt Christi *praesentiam,* secundum illud Aggaei secundo[^p265-11]: « Veniet desideratus cunctis gentibus ». Unde propter vehemens desiderium dicitur Isaiae sexagesimo quarto: « Utinam dirumperes caelos et descenderes »! et Numerorum vigesimo quarto: « Orietur stella ex Iacob, et consurget virga de Israel »; et post: « Heu! quis victurus est, quando ista faciet Dominus »? Nec mirum, quia Esther penultimo[^p265-12] dicitur ad Assuerum in persona Christi: « Valde mirabilis es, domine, et facies tua plena gratiarum ». Et ideo desiderabant Christi videre praesentiam. — Desiderabant etiam *audire doctrinam,* et propterea addit: *Et audire quae auditis, et non audierunt,* id est mea verba; quia, sicut dicitur Osee decimo[^p265-13], « tempus requirendi Dominum, cum venerit qui doceat vos iustitiam ». Hoc enim erat permaximum beneficium, audire Deum loquentem, non per creaturam subiectam, sed in propria persona et creatura unita. Quod beneficium commemorans, ait Apostolus ad Hebraeos primo[^p265-14]: « Multifariam multisque modis olim Deus loquens Patribus in Prophetis, novissime » etc. *Beatus,* qui audit humiliter et obtemperanter hanc locutionem,
<!-- page 266 -->
secundum illud infra undecimo[^p266-1]: « Beati, qui audiunt verbum Dei et custodiunt illud ». Unde beati, qui per fidem Christum viderunt et audierunt in via, secundum illud Proverbiorum octavo[^p266-2]: « Beatus homo, qui audit me et qui vigilat ad fores meas quotidie et observat ad postes ostii mei ». Sed *beatissimi,* qui videbunt in patria, iuxta illud Apocalypsis decimo nono: « Beati, qui ad coenam nuptiarum Agni vocati sunt ».

## English
<!-- page 261 -->

28. *And they returned,* etc. After the mission of those accompanying together and the formation of those going, there is added here, thirdly, *the consolation of those returning*[^p261-4]. Now this part has two. In the first of them *the Lord represses in the disciples a vain joy;* in the second *he invites them to the true joy,* there[^p261-5]: *But rejoice, because your names,* etc.

Concerning *the repression of the vain joy* in the disciples he brings in four things. The first is the *expression* of the vain exultation; the second is the *repression* of the vain exultation; the third is the *occasion* of the vain exultation; the fourth is the *prohibition* of the vain exultation.

29. (Vers. 17.). First, then, he sets forth *the expression of the vain exultation,* when he says: *And the seventy-two returned with joy,* which namely was not only felt in the heart but was also expressed in speech, so that that of Proverbs fifteen[^p261-6] might be verified: « A glad heart maketh a cheerful countenance ». — On account of which he adds: *Saying: Lord, the devils also are subject to us in thy name;* in which they expressed that they rejoiced because they had been made stronger than the devils. And this gladness indeed came from a hidden pride, as did also that of the Pharisee of whom it is said below in eighteen[^p261-7] that, praying, he said: « I give thee thanks, O God, that I am not as the rest of men » etc.

30. And note that over the divine gifts some *are manifestly lifted up,* like those who attribute them to themselves; of whom Deuteronomy thirty-two[^p261-8]: « My high hand, and not the Lord, hath done all these things ». Against whom Deuteronomy nine: « Say not in thy heart, when the Lord shall have destroyed the nations in thy sight: For my justice hath the Lord brought me into this land ». — But some *are manifestly humbled,* of whom it is said below in seventeen[^p261-9]: « When you shall have done all things well, say: We are unprofitable servants ». Such was Paul, who says of himself in First Corinthians fifteen: « I am the least of the Apostles, who am not worthy to be called an Apostle. But by the grace of God I am what I am, and his grace in me hath not been void, but it hath abounded » etc. — But some *are partly humbled* by giving thanks, *partly lifted up* by exulting foolishly, like these disciples, who rejoiced over the subjection of the devils, yet attributed this not to themselves but to the name of the Lord, when they say: *in thy name,* like that Pharisee[^p261-10]. Whence they had something *commendable,* namely this, that they returned to the fountain of graces, recognising the graces received from him and rendering thanks to the same; according to what is said in Ecclesiastes one[^p261-11]: « Unto the place from whence the rivers come, they return, to flow again »; and Job thirty-eight: « Canst thou send lightnings, and will they go, and will they return and say to thee: Here we are »? Upon which the Gloss says: « *The lightnings go,* when the preachers flash with miracles and with heavenly reverence pierce through the hearts of their subjects; *returning they say: Here we are,* when they ascribe not to themselves but to the powers of God whatever they know themselves to have done mightily ». Such are the perfect men in whose person it is said in Isaias twenty-six[^p261-12]: « Thou hast wrought all our works in us, O Lord »; and in the Psalm: « Not to us, O Lord, not to us; but to thy name give glory ». — They had also something *reprehensible* in this, that they were lifted up over the height of the power; which
<!-- page 262 -->
is plain in this, that they say: *The devils are subject to us;* against which Ecclesiasticus eleven[^p262-1]: « Be not exalted in the day of thy honour, for the works of the Most High alone are marvellous, and his works glorious and hidden and unseen ». And this is what Bede says: « They confess well, but because they are lifted up over the working of signs, they are terrified by an example and called back to humility ».

31. (Vers. 18.). Secondly, he joins *the repression of the vain exultation,* when he adds: *And he said to them: I saw Satan like lightning falling from heaven;* as if the Lord were saying: be not exalted by the divine benefits, lest you fall together with the proud angels; Second Peter two[^p262-2]: « If God spared not the angels that sinned, but delivered them, drawn down by infernal ropes to the lower hell, unto torments »; supply: he will not spare you, if you sin by growing proud. Whence Bernard[^p262-3]: « The Lord spared not the proud angels; how much less thee, rottenness and worm »! — Now *he fell like lightning,* that is, swiftly, cruelly and irrevocably. Whence by his example let the rest beware, lest they seek the high things on account of which lucifer fell into the depths, according to that of Isaias fourteen[^p262-4]: « How art thou fallen, O lucifer, who didst rise in the morning? Who saidst in thy heart: I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above the stars of heaven »; and afterwards: « But yet thou shalt be brought down to hell, into the depth of the pit ». And therefore to the Romans eleven: « Be not highminded, but fear. For if God hath not spared the natural branches, fear lest perhaps he also spare not thee ». — By this example the Lord in a wonderful way repressed pride in the disciples, as if saying that of Proverbs seventeen[^p262-5]: « He that maketh his house high », namely by growing proud with lucifer, « seeketh a downfall », by falling together with him. Whence Bernard[^p262-6], speaking to an ambitious man: « Follow thy leader, multiply prebends, hasten to the episcopate, aspire to the archiepiscopate; little by little thou ascendest, but not little by little wilt thou descend. *I saw Satan as lightning falling from heaven* ». And therefore Seneca[^p262-7] says on the contrary: « Bring thyself down to small things, from which thou canst not fall ». Whence Proverbs eighteen: « Before he be broken, the heart of a man is exalted; and before he be glorified, it is humbled »; for it is the sentence of the judge, below in eighteen: « Every one that exalteth himself shall be humbled, and every one that humbleth himself shall be exalted ».

32. (Vers. 19.). But thirdly he subjoins *the occasion of the vain exultation,* when he adds: *Behold, I have given you power to tread upon serpents and scorpions,* namely as regards *bodily* snares, according to what is said to the serpent in Genesis three[^p262-8]: « She shall crush thy head, and thou shalt lie in wait for her heel »; *and upon all the power of the enemy,* as regards *spiritual* snares, of which to the Ephesians six[^p262-9]: « Our wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but against princes and powers, against the rulers of the world » etc.; as far as « in the high places ». — *And nothing shall hurt you,* on account of the heavenly protections, according to that of Mark at the end[^p262-10]: « In my name they shall cast out devils, they shall speak with new tongues, they shall take up serpents. And if they shall drink any deadly thing, it shall not hurt them ». And this power conferred on them could be an occasion of presuming; according to what is said of Ezechias, at whose prayers God did marvels, Second Paralipomenon thirty-two[^p262-11]: « Ezechias prayed to the Lord, and he heard him and gave him a sign; but he did not render again according to the benefits which he had received, because his heart was lifted up, and wrath came upon him and upon Juda and Jerusalem ». — Whence the multitude of divine gifts was to many an occasion of ruin, as is said of lucifer in Ezechiel twenty-eight[^p262-12]: « Thou wast the seal of resemblance, full of wisdom and perfect in beauty »; and a little after: « Thy heart was lifted up »; and therefore: « Thou hast sinned, and I cast thee out from the holy mountain of God and destroyed thee » etc. And therefore a certain holy man used to say that it is a great grace not to have grace; for all these exterior graces are nothing but temptations, and anyone exteriorly raised up ought to fear that of Job thirty[^p262-13]:
<!-- page 263 -->
« Thou hast lifted me up, and as it were setting me upon the wind, thou hast mightily dashed me ».

33. (Vers. 20.). But fourthly he subjoins *the prohibition of the vain exultation,* when he says: *But yet rejoice not in this, that spirits are subject unto you;* because namely such a joy is like *the joy of the proud* over their own promotion and another's casting down, which does not befit the true Saints, according to that of Ecclesiasticus eight[^p263-1]: « Rejoice not over thy dead enemy »; and Job thirty-one: « If I have been glad at the downfall of him that hated me ». *Rejoice* also *not,* because such a joy is *ruinous,* according to that of Proverbs fourteen[^p263-2]: « Mourning taketh hold of the end of joy ». — Such is the joy over a transitory good, according to that of Job twenty: « The joy of the hypocrite is but for a moment »; and again twenty-one: « They rejoice at the sound of the organ, they spend their days in wealth, but in a moment they go down to hell ». Whence the Lord did not wish that the disciples should rejoice in miracles, but rather in torments and insults, according to that of Matthew five[^p263-3]: « Blessed are ye when men shall revile you and persecute you » etc.; and afterwards: « Be glad and exult, for your reward is very great in heaven ». So also the Apostles did, according to that of Acts five: « The Apostles went rejoicing from the presence of the council, because they were accounted worthy to suffer reproach for the name of Jesus ». *Rejoice not therefore in this, that spirits are subject unto you,* although it is divinely granted, lest it be an occasion of pride and in consequence of ruin. On account of which Augustine[^p263-4] says that « the Lord said to his disciples: *Learn of me,* not to raise the dead, not to walk upon the waters, but *because I am meek and humble of heart* »; for they ought not to rejoice in miracles, but in humility.

34. *But rejoice, because*[^p263-5] *your names,* etc. After the Saviour had repressed the vain joy in the disciples, here secondly *he invites them to the true joy.* And this indeed he does by setting forth a fourfold cause or matter of joy. For he shows that the disciples will have joy over God's *infallible foreknowledge,* over his *irreprehensible providence,* over his *incomprehensible power,* over his *desirable presence.* And in these four they had a very great matter for exulting.

First, the joy was over God's *infallible foreknowledge;* as to which he says: *But rejoice, because your names are written,* in the book of life, *in heaven;* which is called *the book of life* because the things written in it *live,* according to that of John one[^p263-6]: « What was made in him was life »; or because according to its writing a man is predestined to life. Whence according to it the final judgement will be made, as is said in the Apocalypse twenty[^p263-7]: « The books were opened, and another book was opened, which is the book of life »; in which book indeed the wicked will not be found written at the last, according to what is said in the Psalm: « Let them be blotted out of the book of the living, and with the just let them not be written ». Which indeed is said, not because this book is changed, but because many seem *written* according to present justice who will appear *not written* according to the divine foreknowledge[^p263-8]. Since, then, this belongs only to the good who are finally to be saved, and is a good interminable and infallible; therefore rightly one must rejoice over this, not over the gift of miracles, which belongs to the good and the wicked. Whence the wicked are not *written in heaven,* but rather *in the earth,* according to that of Jeremias seventeen[^p263-9]: « They that depart from thee shall be written in the earth ». In sign of which thing the Lord, confuting the perfidy of the Pharisees, who were strangers to the kingdom of heaven, « was writing with his finger on the ground », as is said in John eight.

35. And note that the divine cognition is called a *book,* but the cognition of *simple knowledge* is called *the book simply;* and in this all things are written and all men, according to that of the Psalm[^p263-10]: « Thy eyes did see my imperfect being, and in thy book all shall be written ». And there is the cognition of *approbation,* and this is called *the book of life,* and in this none are written according to truth except those finally good; and of this Exodus thirty-two[^p263-11]: « Either forgive them this trespass, or strike me out of thy
<!-- page 264 -->
book which thou hast written ». Upon which the Gloss says that « he said this securely, knowing that he could not be blotted out »; for he speaks of the writing of foreknowledge, not of present justice.

36. (Vers. 21.). Secondly, he shows that one must rejoice over the *irreprehensible providence;* as to which he joins: *In that same hour he exulted in*[^p264-1] *the Holy Ghost,* namely in a spiritual joy, which ought to be in the Holy Ghost, not in the flesh, according to that of Romans fourteen: « The kingdom of God is not meat and drink, but justice and peace and joy in the Holy Ghost »; whence also the glorious Virgin, above in one: « My spirit, she says, hath exulted in God my Saviour ». — And in this he gave the disciples the manner of exulting, and together with this expressed whence they ought to exult, when he adds: *I confess*[^p264-2] *to thee, O Father, Lord of heaven and earth,* that is, that thou art not only creator by power but also governor by providence, according to that of Jeremias twenty-three: « I fill heaven and earth »; and Isaias at the end: « Heaven is my throne, and the earth my footstool ». To this general provider, namely, he confesses with a confession, namely *of praise.* For *confession* is not only *of fault,* according to that of James five[^p264-3]: « Confess your sins one to another »; but also *of the true faith,* of which it is said to the Romans ten: « With the heart we believe unto justice, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation »; but also *of the divine praise,* according to that of Hebrews thirteen[^p264-4]: « Let us offer the sacrifice of praise, that is, the fruit of our lips confessing to his name »; and in the Psalm: « Confess to the Lord, for he is good » etc. And in this manner it is taken here for *the praise* of God himself the governor.

37. Now he praises him for his *providence;* on account of which he adds: *That thou hast hidden these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them to little ones.* For this comes about according to the judgement of the divine providence, that « God resisteth the proud and giveth grace to the humble », as is said in James four[^p264-5]; and in the Psalm: « The Lord is high and looketh on the low, and the high he knoweth afar off ». Therefore *these things,* that is, the mysteries of our redemption, *he hid from the wise and prudent of the world,* who reckon themselves to be *wise* in divine things and *prudent* in temporal things, according to what is said in First Corinthians one[^p264-6]: « I will destroy the wisdom of the wise, and the prudence of the prudent I will reject »; and afterwards he subjoins: « God hath made foolish the wisdom of this world »; and therefore Isaias five: « Woe to you that are wise in your own eyes, and prudent in your own sight ». For from such the divine sacraments are hidden, but on the contrary *they are revealed to little ones;* whence in the Psalm[^p264-7]: « The declaration of thy words giveth light, and giveth understanding to little ones ». — And note that « he does not say *to the foolish and dull,* but *to little ones,* that is, to the humble, to show that he does not condemn the sharpness of intelligence, but the swelling of pride », as the Gloss says[^p264-8]. Whence Bernard: « Humility is the key of knowledge », according to that of Proverbs eleven: « Where humility is, there is wisdom ».

38. And since this judgement of providence is irreprehensible, therefore he adds: *Yea, Father, for so it hath well pleased before thee;* as if he were saying: justly, because it pleases thee; since, as the Gloss says[^p264-9], « that cannot be unjust which has pleased the just one ». Whence it suffices to have rendered this reason, and he willed not to assign another, either because this is *the most sufficient and the first,* according to that of Exodus thirty-three[^p264-10]: « I will have mercy on whom I will, and I will be merciful to whom it shall please me »; and to the Romans nine: « He hath mercy on whom he will » etc. Or so that *curiosity may be repressed,* lest we dare to discuss the heavenly judgements; whence the Gloss[^p264-11]: « From this we receive an example of humility, lest we rashly discuss the heavenly counsels »; for it is said in Proverbs twenty-five: « He that is a searcher of majesty shall be overwhelmed by glory ». Or so that *he may show* that the divine providence is *to be praised in all its deeds* and altogether irreprehensible in all things, according to that of the Psalm[^p264-12]: « The Lord is just in all his ways, and holy in all his works ». — One must therefore rejoice and exult over the irreprehensible providence of God, not dispute it with presumptuous little reasonings; for, as Gregory says[^p264-13], « the divine judgements are not to be rashly discussed, but venerated with a fearful silence ».
<!-- page 265 -->

39. (Vers. 22.). Thirdly, he shows that one must rejoice over the *incomprehensible power,* when he says: *All things are delivered to me by my Father.* For in this, that he says: *All things,* infinitely, *delivered,* he shows that his power is *universal,* according to that of John one[^p265-1]: « All things were made by him », that is, by the Word; and again seventeen: « All my things are thine, and thine are mine ». In this that he joins: *By my Father,* he notes that that power is *natural,* according to that of John five[^p265-2]: « What things soever the Father doth, these the Son also doth in like manner ». In both together he shows *equality* and *immensity;* whence the Gloss: « When thou hearest *all things,* thou recognisest him omnipotent; when thou hearest *delivered,* thou confessest the Son, to whom by nature of one substance all things are by right his own, not conferred as a gift through grace ». And from this it appears that the power of the Son is immense and incomprehensible. — And afterwards he adds: *And no one knoweth*[^p265-3] *who the Son is, but the Father,* that is, no understanding comprehends this perfectly but the paternal; and therefore in this the Holy Ghost is not excluded, but only the created understanding, which cannot comprehend him, since he is immense. Whence Job eleven[^p265-4]: « Peradventure thou wilt comprehend the steps of God, and wilt find out the Almighty perfectly? He is higher than heaven, and what wilt thou do » etc.

40. And because the Son is incomprehensible, therefore he can comprehend the Father. On account of which he adds: *And who the Father is, but the Son;* supply: no one knoweth; for, John one[^p265-5], « No man hath seen God at any time but the Only-begotten, who is in the bosom of the Father; he hath declared him ». — Since he alone comprehends, therefore he alone can reveal; and therefore he adds: *And to whom the Son will reveal him.* For the Son is the Wisdom and the Word of the Father, and the Speaker manifests himself through his Word; whence Wisdom nine[^p265-6]: « Who shall know thy thought, unless thou give wisdom from on high »? Whence Chrysostom: « The philosophers, striving to inquire about God, confessed that they had found nothing else but that God is unknowable; just as one who presumes to sail the unnavigable ocean, since he cannot cross it, must return by the same way; so they began from ignorance and ended in ignorance ». And the reason of this is that they were not disciples of Jesus Christ, who is the truth[^p265-7], nor did they have the Spirit of whom John sixteen: « When that Spirit of truth is come, he will teach you all truth ».

41. (Vers. 23.). Fourthly, he shows that one must rejoice over the *desirable presence,* as to which he adds: *And turning to his disciples, he said*[^p265-8]*: Blessed are the eyes that see the things which you see;* therefore you can rightly rejoice, because you see me as much with the mind as with the body, whereas Abraham rejoiced who saw only by faith, according to that of John eight: « Abraham your father exulted that he might see my day; he saw it and was glad ». — And therefore in figure of this it is said in Third Kings ten[^p265-9] to Solomon: « Blessed are thy men, and blessed are thy servants, these who stand before thee always and hear thy wisdom ». This is said of Solomon under the figure of Christ, because, as he himself says of himself in Matthew twelve, « behold, more than Solomon here ». Whence this was a special gift.

42. (Vers. 24.). And therefore he adds: *But I say*[^p265-10] *to you, that many prophets and kings have desired to see the things that you see, and have not seen them.* Men lofty in knowledge and power desired the *presence* of Christ, according to that of Aggeus two[^p265-11]: « The desired of all nations shall come ». Whence on account of the vehement desire it is said in Isaias sixty-four: « O that thou wouldst rend the heavens and wouldst come down »! and in Numbers twenty-four: « A star shall rise out of Jacob, and a sceptre shall spring up from Israel »; and afterwards: « Alas! who shall live when the Lord shall do these things »? Nor is it a wonder, since in Esther, the second-to-last chapter[^p265-12], it is said to Assuerus in the person of Christ: « Thou art very marvellous, my lord, and thy face is full of graces ». And therefore they desired to see the presence of Christ. — They desired also *to hear the teaching,* and therefore he adds: *And to hear the things that you hear, and have not heard them,* that is, my words; because, as is said in Osee ten[^p265-13], « the time to seek the Lord, when he shall come who may teach you justice ». For this was a very great benefit, to hear God speaking, not through a subject creature, but in his own person and in a united creature. Commemorating which benefit, the Apostle says to the Hebrews one[^p265-14]: « At sundry times and in divers manners God, speaking in times past to the fathers by the prophets, last of all » etc. *Blessed* is he who hears this speech humbly and obediently,
<!-- page 266 -->
according to that of below, eleven[^p266-1]: « Blessed are they who hear the word of God and keep it ». Whence blessed are they who by faith saw and heard Christ on the way, according to that of Proverbs eight[^p266-2]: « Blessed is the man that heareth me, and that watcheth daily at my gates, and waiteth at the posts of my doors ». But *most blessed* are they who will see in the fatherland, according to that of the Apocalypse nineteen: « Blessed are they that are called to the marriage supper of the Lamb ».
