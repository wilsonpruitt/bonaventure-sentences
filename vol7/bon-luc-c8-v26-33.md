# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. VIII, Vers. 26-33
## *Et navigaverunt ad regionem etc. Postquam primo ostendit Evangelista, Christum habere dominium supra creaturam corporalem; hic secundo ostendit, habere dominium supra spiritualem in expulsione admirabili daemoniacae legionis*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 201 -->

46. *Et navigaverunt ad regionem*[^p201-11] etc. Postquam primo ostendit Evangelista, Christum habere dominium supra creaturam corporalem; hic secundo ostendit, habere dominium supra *spiritualem* in expulsione admirabili daemoniacae legionis. Unde intendit Evangelista in hac parte explicare mirabilem liberationem daemoniaci factam mediante virtute Christi. Ad huius autem miraculi explicationem quatuor introducuntur. Primum est *violentia daemonis obsidentis;* secundum est *omnipotentia Domini liberantis,* quod determinat ibi[^p201-12]: *Et rogabant illum, ne imperaret illis* etc.; tertium est *reverentia populi occurrentis,* ibi: *Quod ut viderunt factum, qui pascebant* etc.; quartum est *benevolentia curati obtemperantis,* ibi: *Et rogabat illum vir, a quo daemonia exierant* etc.

*Violentia* autem *diaboli obsidentis* ostenditur ex quatuor, scilicet ex *fremitu furoris,* ex *strepitu*
<!-- page 202 -->
*clamoris,* ex *impetu fortitudinis* et ex *comitatu multitudinis.*

47. (Vers. 26.). Primo igitur quantum ad *fremitum furoris* in daemoniaco occurrente Domino applicanti dicitur: *Et navigaverunt ad regionem Gerasenorum.* « *Gerasa* autem, ut dicit Beda[^p202-1], civitas est Arabiae »; in quo ostenditur, quod post miracula facta circa Iudaeos divina dispensatio hoc requirit, ut fiant in gentibus; iuxta quod dicitur Actuum decimo tertio: « Vobis primum oportebat loqui verbum Dei, sed quoniam repellitis illud et indignos vos iudicatis aeternae vitae, ecce, convertimur ad gentes », quasi in contemptum rebellium. Unde addit: *Quae est contra Galilaeam; quae* refertur ad *civitatem* intellectam per illam gentem, quae scilicet *Gerasa* dicitur. Illuc navigavit, ut virtutem potentiae per miracula ostenderet et ad se traheret. Interpretatur namque *Gerasa,* ut dicit Glossa[^p202-2], « advena »; et ideo designat gentes, secundum illud ad Ephesios secundo: « Memores estote, quod aliquando vos eratis gentes, alienati a conversatione Israel, promissionis spem non habentes et sine Deo in hoc mundo. Nunc autem in Christo Iesu vos, qui aliquando eratis longe, facti estis prope ». Unde in hoc patet, quod « idem Dominus omnium, dives in omnes, qui invocant illum », ad Romanos decimo[^p202-3]. « Omnis enim, quicumque invocaverit nomen Domini, salvus erit ».

48. (Vers. 27.). Ideo additur: *Et cum egressus esset ad terram, occurrit ei*[^p202-4] *vir quidam,* ut sicut mirabilia fecerat in mari, ita et in terra. Et per hoc apparet, quod ipse est ille, de quo in Psalmo dicitur: « Omnia, quaecumque voluit Dominus, fecit in caelo et in terra, in mari et in omnibus abyssis ». Et hoc ostendit in viro isto, qui merito dicitur *vir quidam* singularis passionis, qui notabilis erat persona et notabiliter et miserabiliter erat obsessus. Et ideo, licet duo occurrerent, secundum quod dicitur Matthaei octavo[^p202-5], Lucas tamen non commemorat nisi occursum unius, cuius notabilem vexationem determinat ex *temporis diuturnitate.* — Propter quod dicit: *Qui habebat daemonium iam temporibus multis,* ita quod iam sibi eum vindicaverat in possessionem, ita ut posset dici de eo illud Isaiae trigesimo quarto[^p202-6]: « Cubile erit draconum et pascua struthionum, et occurrent daemonia, onocentaurus »; hoc est de plene a diabolo possessis. — Aggravatur etiam ex *habitus deformitate;* unde et additur: *Et vestimento non induebatur*[^p202-7]; quod erat valde deforme, iuxta quod comminatur Dominus Ezechielis decimo sexto: « Denudabunt te vestimentis tuis, ut auferant vasa decoris tui ». Et in hoc apparet fremitus daemonis, qui non tantum vexabat corporis membra, sed etiam laniabat vestimenta, sicut de illo dicitur Actuum decimo nono: « Cum invaluisset daemonium pessimum contra duos filios Scevae Iudaei, nudi et vulnerati effugerunt de domo ». — Aggravatur etiam ex *loci horribilitate;* propterea addit: *Neque in domo manebat, sed in monumentis,* ubi est locus valde horribilis. Et talia loca quaerunt principes tenebrarum; Iob trigesimo[^p202-8]: « In desertis habitant montium et in cavernis terrae »; et tamen ab his eripit Dominus, secundum illud Psalmi: « Qui educit vinctos in fortitudine, similiter eos qui exasperant, qui habitant in sepulcris ».

49. (Vers. 28.). Secundo vero quantum ad *strepitum clamoris* in daemone vociferante, Domino praecipiente, subditur: *Is ut vidit Iesum, procidit ante illum.* Et sic verificatur illud Psalmi[^p202-9]: « Coram illo procident Aethiopes »; et illud Isaiae sexagesimo: « Venient ad te qui detrahebant tibi et adorabunt vestigia pedum tuorum », « ut in nomine Iesu omne genu flectatur caelestium, terrestrium et infernorum », ad Philippenses secundo. Hoc autem non faciebat humilitate devotionis, sed cum murmure et strepitu clamoris. — Et ideo addit: *Et exclamans voce magna dixit: Quid mihi et tibi est, Iesu, Fili Dei altissimi?* Quod non exhibebat ex reverentia, sed magis abhorrebat praesentiam cum clamore, per quem ostendit gemitum et dolorem in corde, secundum illud Isaiae penultimo[^p202-10]: « Clamabitis prae dolore cordis et prae contritione spiritus ululabitis ». Ideo
<!-- page 203 -->
autem clamat et dolet de eius praesentia, quia nulla erat ad Christum convenientia; secundae ad Corinthios sexto[^p203-1]: « Quae conventio Christi ad Belial? Quae societas lucis ad tenebras, iustitiae cum iniquitate »? et Ecclesiastici decimo tertio: « Quid communicabit lupus cum agno; aut quae communicatio homini sancto ad canem »?

50. Et nota, quod in hac confessione duplicem elidit errorem: unum *Iudaeorum,* qui dicebant, Christum habere daemonium, Ioannis octavo[^p203-2], et in Beelzebub eiicere daemonia, Matthaei duodecimo. Unde Beda: « Iudaei dicunt, in principe daemoniorum eiicere eum daemones, quem daemonia negant aliquid secum habere commune ». — Alium errorem elidit *Arianorum,* qui dicunt Christum *creaturam puram,* cum daemon dicat eum *Dei altissimi Filium,* iuxta illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto[^p203-3]: « Ego ex ore Altissimi prodivi, primogenita ante omnem creaturam ». Hoc autem fatetur daemon non venerando eius dominium, sed pertimescendo eius praeceptum, iuxta illud Iacobi secundo: « Daemones credunt et contremiscunt ».

51. Et ideo additur: *Obsecro te, ne me torqueas,* illa scilicet virtute, de qua Sapientiae undecimo[^p203-4]: « Absentes et praesentes similiter torquebantur ». Ideo volebat Christum confiteri, ut saltem confitendo inveniret copiam evadendi; quia, Sapientiae decimo sexto, « negantes te nosse impii, per fortitudinem brachii tui flagellati sunt ».

52. (Vers. 29.). Sed haec supplicatio parum sibi valuit, non erat enim voluntaria, sed coacta; ideo subiungitur: *Praecipiebat enim spiritui immundo, ut exiret ab homine*[^p203-5]; cui praecepto non poterat resistere, secundum illud Sapientiae decimo nono: « Omnis creatura deserviebat praeceptis tuis, ut pueri tui servarentur illaesi ». Ideo autem hoc praeceptum timebat, quia solatium est immundo spiritui obsidere hominem et tormentum exhibere, iuxta illud infra undecimo[^p203-6]: « Cum immundus spiritus exierit ab homine, ambulat per loca inaquosa, quaerens requiem »; et Beda in Glossa: « Tormentum est diaboli ab hominis laesione cessare, et quanto diutius possideat, tanto difficilius dimittit. Caveat ergo laesus a diabolo, ut cito iugum disrumpat ». — Sed in hac petitione non meruit daemon exaudiri, quia non erat ex affectu devotionis, sed ex impatientia et strepitu clamoris; Psalmus[^p203-7]: « Clamaverunt, nec erat qui salvos faceret ».

53. Tertio quantum ad *impetum fortitudinis* in daemone resistente humanae ligationi subiungitur: *Multis enim temporibus arripiebat eum*[^p203-8], scilicet impetu fortitudinis, cui non poterat obsessus resistere; iuxta quod dicitur de Saule primi Regum decimo octavo, quod « spiritus Domini malus arripiebat Saul ». Sed hunc aliter quam Saul, quia ille leniebatur cithara, hic autem non poterat detineri catena. — Unde additur: *Et vinciebatur catenis,* quantum ad manus, *compedibus custoditus*[^p203-9], quantum ad pedes, quod erat retinaculum valde forte, iuxta illud Psalmi: « Ad alligandos reges eorum in compedibus et nobiles eorum in manicis ferreis ». — Et licet tam forte esset, non poterat sustinere impetum fortitudinis diabolicae; propter quod addit: *Et ruptis vinculis, agebatur a daemonio in deserto*[^p203-10]. In quo ostenditur superbia et audacia diaboli, qui nullis retinaculis poterat detineri, iuxta illud Ieremiae secundo: « A saeculo confregisti iugum meum, rupisti vincula ». Sic utique faciunt superbi imitatores diaboli, contra quos Ieremias, quinto[^p203-11], loquens de principibus: « Confregerunt iugum, ruperunt vincula », scilicet divinorum praeceptorum; cum tamen dicatur Ecclesiastici sexto: « Iniice pedem tuum in compedes illius, et ne acedieris vinculis eius ». Sed tolerabilius est peccatori obsesso a diabolo agitari in deserto, quam istis vinculis compediri; Iob trigesimo[^p203-12]: « In desertis habitant torrentium et in cavernis terrae, qui in huiusmodi laetabantur et esse sub sentibus delicias computabant ».

54. (Vers. 30.). Quarto vero quantum ad *comitatum multitudinis* in daemone respondente dominicae interrogationi subiungitur: *Interrogavit autem illum Iesus dicens: Quod tibi nomen est?* Ipsius
<!-- page 204 -->
enim est interrogare, cuius est et iudicare; Sapientiae sexto[^p204-1]: « Interrogabit Altissimus opera vestra et cogitationes vestras scrutabitur »; et undecimo: « Hos tanquam durus rex interrogans, condemnasti »; quia, secundum illud Sapientiae primo, « in cogitationibus impii interrogatio erit ». Interrogabat autem de nomine, non quia ignoraret, secundum quod dicunt ei discipuli Ioannis decimo sexto[^p204-2]: « Nunc scimus, quia scis omnia, et non opus est tibi, ut quis te interroget », et multo minus nec tu alios. — Sed quaerebat, ut ex responsione daemonis appareret comitatus multitudinis; unde et subditur: *At ille dixit: Legio.* « Legio continet sex millia sexcenta et sexaginta sex[^p204-3] »; et ideo non respondet nomen singularis personae, sed multitudinis; quod exponens Evangelista, adiungit: *Quia intraverant daemonia multa in eum,* ita ut posset dicere Domino[^p204-4]: « Domine, quid multiplicati sunt qui tribulant me »? Et iterum: « Multiplicati sunt super capillos capitis mei qui oderunt me gratis ». Iam verificatum erat in isto quod de Antiocho dicitur primi Machabaeorum primo: « Ascendit Antiochus Ierosolymam in multitudine gravi. Et intravit in sanctificationem cum superbia ». — Et sic ex quatuor praemissis apparet violentia diaboli obsidentis.

55. (Vers. 31.). *Et rogabant illum* etc. Post violentiam daemonii obsidentis subiungit hic Evangelista *omnipotentiam Domini liberantis.* Ostenditur autem in hac parte Christus potens per omnem modum, potens scilicet in *imperando,* potens in *permittendo,* potens in *salvando* et potens in *perdendo.*

Primo ergo ostenditur potens in *imperando dura* in hoc quod dicitur: *Et rogabant illum,* scilicet daemones, *ne imperaret illis, ut in abyssum irent;* quia mandatum eius non poterant praeterire, iuxta illud Psalmi[^p204-5]: « Praeceptum posuit et non praeteribit ». Ideo rogabant eum, quia sciebant, quod ipse erat ille Angelus, de quo Apocalypsis vigesimo: « Vidi Angelum descendentem de caelo, habentem clavem abyssi », id est potestatem mittendi in abyssum iusto suo iudicio, sicut fecit Aegyptiis; Exodi decimo quinto: « Abyssi operuerunt eos, descenderunt in profundum quasi lapis »; et Apocalypsis vigesimo: « Iudicatum est de singulis secundum opera ipsorum. Et infernus et mors missi sunt in stagnum ignis ». Hoc iudicium timebant daemones accelerari sibi per imperium Christi, non tanquam praescii futurorum, sed propter dicta Prophetarum. Unde Ambrosius[^p204-6]: « Sciebant daemones, per adventum Christi se aliquando in abyssum mergendos, non ipsi futura praedivinantes, sed Prophetarum de se dicta recolentes ».

56. (Vers. 32.). Secundo dicitur potens in *permittendo damna* in hoc quod subditur: *Erat autem ibi grex porcorum multorum pascentium in monte*[^p204-7], in quos, licet bruta sint animalia, daemones non habebant *posse* nisi ex divina permissione. Nam de Christo dictum est in Psalmo[^p204-8]: « Omnia subiecisti sub pedibus eius »; et post: « insuper et pecora campi ». Unde in istum gregem non poterant quidquam nisi ex permissione Dei, secundum illud Matthaei decimo: « Nonne duo passeres asse veneunt? et unus ex eis non cadet in terram sine Patre vestro », id est sine potentia Patris. — Et ideo subditur: *Et rogabant eum, ut permitteret, eos*[^p204-9] *in illos ingredi;* in quo ostendunt daemones Christi potentiam et suam impotentiam, quia nihil possunt in bestias nisi permissi, multo magis nec in hominem. Unde Iob primo dixit satan: « Domine, extende manum tuam et tange cuncta, quae possidet ». Propter quod et in tribulatione, quam facturus est diabolus Ecclesiae, divina permissio praecedit, secundum illud Apocalypsis nono[^p204-10]: « Soluti sunt quatuor angeli, qui parati erant in horam et diem »; et vigesimo dicitur, quod « solvetur satanas ». — Et quia Dominus iusto suo iudicio permittit eis multa, subditur: *Et permisit, eos*[^p204-11]; in qua permissione ostendit se dominum non solum prohibentem daemones, sed possidentem porcos, secundum illud Psalmi: « Meae sunt omnes ferae silvarum, iumenta in montibus et boves ». Possessores vero solum erant villici Domini Iesu, et ideo in nullo iniuriatus est eis. — Ostendit etiam Dominus, quod magis diligit salutem unius hominis quam millia brutorum animalium; primae ad Corinthios nono[^p204-12]: « Nunquid de bobus cura est
<!-- page 205 -->
Deo »? Sapientiae undecimo: « Parcis autem omnibus, Domine, quoniam tua sunt, qui amas animas ». — Rursus, in hoc ostendit Dominus, carnales homines, qui designantur per *porcos,* iusto Dei iudicio expositos esse daemonibus; ad Romanos octavo[^p205-1]: « Si secundum carnem vixeritis, moriemini ». Unde primae ad Corinthios quinto dicit Apostolus de illo carnali, quod « iudicavit tradere huiusmodi satanae in interitum carnis ». Hoc etiam fecit, ut non solum innotesceret hominibus terrae illius potentia salvandi, sed etiam occidendi, secundum illud Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo[^p205-2]: « Ego occidam et ego vivere faciam, percutiam et ego sanabo; et non est qui de manu mea possit eruere ».

57. (Vers. 33.). Tertio ostenditur potens in *salvando infirmos* in hoc quod adiungitur: *Exierunt ergo daemonia ab homine et intraverunt in porcos;* in quo exitu liberatus est homo. *Exierunt* autem divinae virtutis imperio, secundum illud Zachariae decimo tertio[^p205-3]: « Spiritum immundum eiiciam de terra ». Hoc dicebat ille qui ait de se infra decimo tertio: « Ecce, daemonia eiicio et sanitates perficio hodie et cras et tertia die consummor ». In virtute namque Christi exibant, in cuius nomine eiiciebantur ab eius discipulis, iuxta quod dicitur Actuum decimo sexto, quod « Paulus dixit spiritui pythonico: Praecipio tibi in nomine Iesu Christi exire ab ea; et exiit eadem hora ». Egredientes autem ab homine *in porcos intrant,* quia immundus spiritus immunda quaerit habitacula; Iob trigesimo septimo[^p205-4]: « Ingredietur bestia latibulum suum et in antro suo morabitur »; et de Behemoth dicitur Iob quadragesimo: « Sub umbra dormit in secreto calami, in locis humentibus ». Hoc dicit quantum ad carnales, qui nihil aliud quaerunt nisi umbram et lasciviam sicut porci.

58. Quarto ostenditur potens in *deiiciendo superbos* in hoc quod subditur: *Et impetu abiit grex per praeceps in stagnum et suffocatus est;* in quo ostenditur Domini virtus eximia, quae non permisit, eos ingredi in porcos ad quietem, sed ad submersionem, ut sic impleretur in eis illud Exodi decimo quinto[^p205-5]: « Flavit spiritus tuus, et operuit eos mare; submersi sunt quasi plumbum in aquis vehementibus »; in quo spiritualiter designatur submersio peccatorum, iuxta illud propheticum Michaeae ultimo: « Deponet omnes iniquitates nostras et proiiciet in profundum maris omnia peccata nostra ».

59. Ostenditur igitur, quod ex praedictis secundum litteram colligi possunt septem notabilia: primum, quod potentia solius verbi Christi excedit virtutem infinitorum millium daemonum; secundum, quod maximus est furor daemonum respectu generis humani; tertium, quod maximus est timor[^p205-6] respectu hominis Christi; quartum, quod daemones sunt incorporei, cum tot essent in homine uno; quintum, quod ipsi nihil possunt etiam super bruta animalia nisi permissi; sextum, quod homines in his temporalibus possidendis non sunt nisi sicut villici Christi; septimum, quod Deus salutem unius hominis praeferendam ostendit infinitis animalibus brutis.

## English
<!-- page 201 -->

46. *And they sailed to the country*[^p201-11] etc. After the Evangelist has shown, first, that Christ has dominion over the bodily creature, he here shows, secondly, that he has dominion over the *spiritual,* in the marvellous expulsion of the demoniac legion. Whence the Evangelist intends in this part to set forth the marvellous deliverance of the demoniac, wrought by means of the power of Christ. Now for the setting forth of this miracle four things are brought in. The first is *the violence of the demon besetting;* the second is *the omnipotence of the Lord delivering,* which he determines there[^p201-12]: *And they besought him that he would not command them* etc.; the third is *the reverence of the people running to meet him,* there: *Which when they that fed them saw done* etc.; the fourth is *the benevolence of the cured man obeying,* there: *And the man, out of whom the devils were departed, besought him* etc.

Now *the violence of the devil besetting* is shown from four things, namely from *the raging of fury,* from *the din*
<!-- page 202 -->
*of clamour,* from *the onset of strength* and from *the company of a multitude.*

47. (Vers. 26.). First, then, as to *the raging of fury* in the demoniac running to meet the Lord as he put in to shore, it is said: *And they sailed to the country of the Gerasens.* « Now *Gerasa,* as Bede says[^p202-1], is a city of Arabia »; in which it is shown that after the miracles done among the Jews the divine dispensation requires this, that they be done among the Gentiles; according to what is said in the Acts, the thirteenth chapter: « To you it behoved us first to speak the word of God, but because you reject it, and judge yourselves unworthy of eternal life, behold, we turn to the Gentiles », as it were in contempt of the rebellious. Whence he adds: *Which is over against Galilee; which* refers to *the city* understood through that nation, which namely is called *Gerasa.* Thither he sailed, that he might show the power of his potency through miracles and draw them to himself. For *Gerasa* is interpreted, as the Gloss says[^p202-2], « a stranger »; and therefore it designates the Gentiles, according to that to the Ephesians, the second chapter: « Be mindful that in time past you were Gentiles, being aliens from the conversation of Israel, having no hope of the promise, and without God in this world. But now in Christ Jesus, you, who some time were afar off, are made nigh ». Whence in this it is plain that « the same is Lord over all, rich unto all that call upon him », to the Romans, the tenth chapter[^p202-3]. « For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord, shall be saved ».

48. (Vers. 27.). Therefore it is added: *And when he was come forth to the land, there met him*[^p202-4] *a certain man,* that as he had done marvellous things on the sea, so also on the land. And by this it appears that he is the one of whom it is said in the Psalm: « Whatsoever the Lord pleased he hath done, in heaven, in earth, in the sea, and in all the deeps ». And this he showed in this man, who is deservedly called *a certain man* of singular suffering, who was a notable person and was notably and miserably beset. And therefore, though two met him, according to what is said in Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p202-5], Luke nevertheless records the meeting of only one, whose notable vexation he determines from *the length of the time.* — On account of which he says: *Who had a devil now a very long time,* so that it had now claimed him for itself as a possession, so that there could be said of him that of Isaias, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p202-6]: « It shall be the habitation of dragons, and the pasture of ostriches, and demons shall meet, the onocentaur »; this is said of those fully possessed by the devil. — It is aggravated also by *the deformity of his attire;* whence it is also added: *And he wore no clothes*[^p202-7]; which was very unseemly, according to what the Lord threatens in Ezechiel, the sixteenth chapter: « They shall strip thee of thy garments, and shall take away the vessels of thy beauty ». And in this there appears the raging of the demon, which not only vexed the members of the body but also tore the garments, as is said of that one in the Acts, the nineteenth chapter: « When the most wicked demon had prevailed against two sons of Sceva, a Jew, they fled out of the house naked and wounded ». — It is aggravated also by *the horribleness of the place;* for this reason he adds: *Neither did he abide in a house, but in the sepulchres,* where the place is very horrible. And such places the princes of darkness seek; Job, the thirtieth chapter[^p202-8]: « They dwell in the desert places of the mountains, and in caves of the earth »; and yet from these the Lord rescues, according to that of the Psalm: « Who bringeth out them that were bound in strength; in like manner them that provoke, that dwell in sepulchres ».

49. (Vers. 28.). Secondly, as to *the din of clamour* in the demon crying out, the Lord enjoining, it is subjoined: *He, when he saw Jesus, fell down before him.* And so there is verified that of the Psalm[^p202-9]: « Before him the Ethiopians shall fall down »; and that of Isaias, the sixtieth chapter: « They that slandered thee shall come to thee, and shall worship the steps of thy feet », « that in the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of those that are in heaven, on earth, and under the earth », to the Philippians, the second chapter. But this he did not do with the humility of devotion, but with murmuring and the din of clamour. — And therefore he adds: *And crying out with a loud voice, he said: What have I to do with thee, Jesus, Son of the most high God?* Which he did not show out of reverence, but rather he abhorred his presence with clamour, by which he showed groaning and sorrow in the heart, according to that of Isaias, the last chapter but one[^p202-10]: « You shall cry for sorrow of heart, and shall howl for grief of spirit ». Therefore
<!-- page 203 -->
he cries out and grieves at his presence, because there was no agreement with Christ; the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter[^p203-1]: « What concord hath Christ with Belial? What fellowship hath light with darkness, justice with iniquity »? and Ecclesiasticus, the thirteenth chapter: « What shall the wolf have in common with the lamb; or what communication hath a holy man with a dog »?

50. And note that in this confession he strikes down a twofold error: one *of the Jews,* who said that Christ had a devil, John, the eighth chapter[^p203-2], and cast out devils by Beelzebub, Matthew, the twelfth chapter. Whence Bede: « The Jews say that he casts out demons by the prince of demons, whom the demons deny to have anything in common with themselves ». — The other error he strikes down is *of the Arians,* who say that Christ is *a pure creature,* since the demon calls him *Son of the most high God,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p203-3]: « I came out of the mouth of the most High, the firstborn before all creatures ». Now this the demon confesses not by venerating his dominion, but by dreading his precept, according to that of James, the second chapter: « The devils believe and tremble ».

51. And therefore it is added: *I beseech thee, do not torment me,* namely with that power of which Wisdom, the eleventh chapter[^p203-4]: « Absent and present they were tormented alike ». Therefore he willed to confess Christ, that at least by confessing he might find a means of escaping; because, Wisdom, the sixteenth chapter, « the wicked that denied to know thee, were scourged by the strength of thy arm ».

52. (Vers. 29.). But this supplication availed him little, for it was not voluntary, but forced; therefore it is subjoined: *For he enjoined the unclean spirit to go out of the man*[^p203-5]; which precept he could not resist, according to that of Wisdom, the nineteenth chapter: « Every creature served thy precepts, that thy children might be kept without hurt ». Now he feared this precept for this reason, that it is a solace to the unclean spirit to beset a man and to inflict torment, according to that below, the eleventh chapter[^p203-6]: « When the unclean spirit is gone out of a man, he walketh through places without water, seeking rest »; and Bede in the Gloss: « It is a torment to the devil to cease from hurting a man, and the longer he possesses, the more hardly he lets go. Let him therefore who is hurt by the devil take care to break his yoke quickly ». — But in this petition the demon did not merit to be heard, because it was not out of the affection of devotion, but out of impatience and the din of clamour; the Psalm[^p203-7]: « They cried, but there was none to save them ».

53. Thirdly, as to *the onset of strength* in the demon resisting human binding, it is subjoined: *For many times it seized him*[^p203-8], namely with the onset of strength, which the beset man could not resist; according to what is said of Saul in the first of Kings, the eighteenth chapter, that « the evil spirit of the Lord seized Saul ». But this one otherwise than Saul, because he was soothed by the harp, whereas this one could not be held by a chain. — Whence it is added: *And he was bound with chains,* as to the hands, *kept in fetters*[^p203-9], as to the feet, which was a very strong restraint, according to that of the Psalm: « To bind their kings with fetters, and their nobles with manacles of iron ». — And though it was so strong, it could not withstand the onset of the devil's strength; on account of which he adds: *And breaking the bonds, he was driven by the devil into the desert*[^p203-10]. In which is shown the pride and boldness of the devil, who could be held by no restraints, according to that of Jeremias, the second chapter: « Of old time thou hast broken my yoke, thou hast burst the bonds ». So indeed do the proud imitators of the devil, against whom Jeremias, the fifth chapter[^p203-11], speaking of the princes: « They have broken the yoke, they have burst the bonds », namely of the divine precepts; although it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the sixth chapter: « Put thy foot into her fetters, and be not grieved with her bands ». But it is more tolerable for a sinner beset by the devil to be driven about in the desert, than to be fettered with these bonds; Job, the thirtieth chapter[^p203-12]: « They dwell in the desert places of torrents, and in caves of the earth, who rejoiced in things of this sort, and counted it delightful to be under the briers ».

54. (Vers. 30.). Fourthly, as to *the company of a multitude* in the demon answering the Lord's questioning, it is subjoined: *And Jesus questioned him, saying: What is thy name?* For it is his
<!-- page 204 -->
to question whose it is also to judge; Wisdom, the sixth chapter[^p204-1]: « The most High shall question your works, and search out your thoughts »; and the eleventh chapter: « These, as a severe king questioning, thou didst condemn »; because, according to that of Wisdom, the first chapter, « in the thoughts of the ungodly there shall be questioning ». Now he questioned him about his name, not because he did not know it, according to what the disciples say to him in John, the sixteenth chapter[^p204-2]: « Now we know that thou knowest all things, and thou needest not that any man should question thee », and much less thou others. — But he asked, that from the demon's answer the company of a multitude might appear; whence also it is subjoined: *But he said: Legion.* « A legion contains six thousand six hundred and sixty-six[^p204-3] »; and therefore he does not answer with the name of a single person, but of a multitude; which the Evangelist, explaining, adjoins: *Because many devils were entered into him,* so that he could say to the Lord[^p204-4]: « Lord, why are they multiplied that afflict me »? And again: « They are multiplied above the hairs of my head, who hate me without cause ». Now there was verified in this one what is said of Antiochus in the first of Machabees, the first chapter: « Antiochus went up to Jerusalem with a great multitude. And he entered into the sanctuary with pride ». — And so from the four things set down before there appears the violence of the devil besetting.

55. (Vers. 31.). *And they besought him* etc. After the violence of the demon besetting the Evangelist here subjoins *the omnipotence of the Lord delivering.* Now in this part Christ is shown powerful in every way, powerful, namely, in *commanding,* powerful in *permitting,* powerful in *saving* and powerful in *destroying.*

First, then, he is shown powerful in *commanding hard things* in this, that it is said: *And they besought him,* namely the demons, *that he would not command them to go into the abyss;* because they could not pass by his commandment, according to that of the Psalm[^p204-5]: « He hath made a decree, and it shall not pass away ». Therefore they besought him, because they knew that he was that Angel of whom the Apocalypse, the twentieth chapter: « I saw an Angel coming down from heaven, having the key of the abyss », that is, the power of sending into the abyss by his just judgement, as he did to the Egyptians; Exodus, the fifteenth chapter: « The abysses have covered them, they are sunk to the bottom like a stone »; and the Apocalypse, the twentieth chapter: « Judgement was given of every one according to their works. And hell and death were cast into the pool of fire ». This judgement the demons feared to have hastened upon them by the command of Christ, not as foreknowing things to come, but on account of the sayings of the Prophets. Whence Ambrose[^p204-6]: « The demons knew that by the coming of Christ they were at some time to be plunged into the abyss, not themselves divining things to come, but recalling the sayings of the Prophets concerning them ».

56. (Vers. 32.). Secondly, he is said to be powerful in *permitting losses* in this, that it is subjoined: *And there was there a herd of many swine feeding on the mountain*[^p204-7], into which, though they are brute animals, the demons had no *ability* save from the divine permission. For of Christ it is said in the Psalm[^p204-8]: « Thou hast subjected all things under his feet »; and after: « moreover the beasts also of the fields ». Whence against this herd they could do nothing save by the permission of God, according to that of Matthew, the tenth chapter: « Are not two sparrows sold for a farthing? and not one of them shall fall on the ground without your Father », that is, without the power of the Father. — And therefore it is subjoined: *And they besought him that he would permit them*[^p204-9] *to enter into them;* in which the demons show Christ's power and their own powerlessness, because they can do nothing against beasts unless permitted, much less against a man. Whence in Job, the first chapter, Satan said: « Lord, stretch forth thy hand, and touch all that he hath ». On account of which also, in the tribulation which the devil is to bring upon the Church, the divine permission goes before, according to that of the Apocalypse, the ninth chapter[^p204-10]: « The four angels were loosed, who were prepared for an hour and a day »; and in the twentieth chapter it is said that « Satan shall be loosed ». — And because the Lord by his just judgement permits them many things, it is subjoined: *And he permitted them*[^p204-11]; in which permission he showed himself lord, not only forbidding the demons, but possessing the swine, according to that of the Psalm: « For all the beasts of the woods are mine, the cattle on the hills, and the oxen ». But the possessors were only stewards of the Lord Jesus, and therefore he did them wrong in nothing. — The Lord also shows that he loves the salvation of one man more than thousands of brute animals; the first to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter[^p204-12]: « Doth God take care for
<!-- page 205 -->
oxen »? Wisdom, the eleventh chapter: « But thou sparest all, O Lord, because they are thine, who lovest souls ». — Again, in this the Lord shows that carnal men, who are designated by *the swine,* are exposed to the demons by the just judgement of God; to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p205-1]: « If you live according to the flesh, you shall die ». Whence in the first to the Corinthians, the fifth chapter, the Apostle says of that carnal man that « he has judged to deliver such a one to Satan for the destruction of the flesh ». This too he did, that there might become known to the men of that land not only the power of saving, but also of killing, according to that of Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter[^p205-2]: « I will kill and I will make to live, I will strike and I will heal; and there is none that can deliver out of my hand ».

57. (Vers. 33.). Thirdly, he is shown powerful in *saving the sick* in this, that it is adjoined: *The devils therefore went out of the man, and entered into the swine;* in which going out the man was delivered. Now *they went out* by the command of the divine power, according to that of Zacharias, the thirteenth chapter[^p205-3]: « I will cast out the unclean spirit from the earth ». This he was saying who says of himself below, the thirteenth chapter: « Behold, I cast out devils, and do cures today and tomorrow, and the third day I am consummated ». For they went out in the power of Christ, in whose name they were cast out by his disciples, according to what is said in the Acts, the sixteenth chapter, that « Paul said to the pythonical spirit: I command thee in the name of Jesus Christ to go out from her; and he went out the same hour ». Now going out from the man *they enter into the swine,* because the unclean spirit seeks unclean dwellings; Job, the thirty-seventh chapter[^p205-4]: « The beast shall go into his covert, and shall abide in his den »; and of Behemoth it is said in Job, the fortieth chapter: « He sleepeth under the shadow, in the covert of the reed, in moist places ». This he says as to carnal men, who seek nothing else but shade and wantonness, like swine.

58. Fourthly, he is shown powerful in *casting down the proud* in this, that it is subjoined: *And the herd ran violently down a steep place into the lake, and was stifled;* in which is shown the excellent power of the Lord, which did not permit them to enter into the swine for rest, but for submersion, that so there might be fulfilled in them that of Exodus, the fifteenth chapter[^p205-5]: « Thy wind blew, and the sea covered them; they were sunk as lead in the mighty waters »; in which is spiritually designated the submersion of sins, according to that prophetic saying of Micheas, the last chapter: « He will put away all our iniquities, and will cast all our sins into the bottom of the sea ».

59. It is shown, then, that from what has been said seven notable things can be gathered according to the letter: the first, that the power of the mere word of Christ exceeds the power of infinite thousands of demons; the second, that the fury of the demons toward the human race is very great; the third, that the fear[^p205-6] toward the man Christ is very great; the fourth, that the demons are incorporeal, since so many were in one man; the fifth, that they can do nothing even over brute animals unless permitted; the sixth, that men, in possessing these temporal things, are nothing but stewards of Christ; the seventh, that God shows the salvation of one man to be preferred to infinite brute animals.
