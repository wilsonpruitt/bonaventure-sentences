# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XI, Vers. 1-4
## *Tertia pars agit de forma orandi tripliciter*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 277 -->

### Capitulum XI.

#### Tertia pars agit de forma orandi tripliciter.

1. *Et factum est, cum esset in quodam loco* etc. Postquam tradidit discipulis formam docendi et formam vivendi, in hac parte tribuit *formam orandi*[^p277-7], per quam impetratur gratia cognoscendi et recte vivendi. Habet autem haec pars partes tres; in quarum prima praemittitur orationis *exemplum;* in secunda vero additur *documentum,* ibi[^p277-8]: *Et ait illis: Cum oratis* etc.; in tertia subiungitur orationis *incitamentum,* ibi: *Et ait ad illos: Quis vestrum* etc.

Circa *orationis exemplum* duo introducuntur: primum est *sollicitudo Domini ad orandum,* secundum est *promptitudo*[^p277-9] *discipulorum ad imitandum.*

2. (Vers. 1.). Primo igitur quantum ad *sollicitudinem Domini in orando* dicitur: *Et factum est, dum*[^p277-10] *esset in quodam loco orans; quodam,* id est solitario et secreto, quia talia loca sunt orationi apta, secundum illud Matthaei sexto: « Tu autem, cum oraveris, intra in cubiculum tuum, et clauso ostio, ora Patrem tuum in abscondito ». Orat autem Dominus in *argumentum verae humanitatis,* iuxta quod dicitur infra vigesimo secundo[^p277-11]: « Factus in agonia, prolixius orabat ». In *adiumentum nostrae infirmitatis,* secundum illud ad Hebraeos quinto: « Exauditus est in omnibus pro sua reverentia »; et ad Romanos octavo: « Quid oremus, secundum quod oportet, nescimus; sed ipse Spiritus », scilicet Domini Iesu, « adiuvat infirmitatem nostram ». In *exemplum perfectae virtutis;* unde dicitur Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p277-12]: Postquam oravit, ipsis discipulis ait: « Vigilate et orate, ut non intretis in tentationem ». Ob hanc etiam causam orationi *frequenter* instabat, ut doceret, semper orandum, secundum illud primae ad Thessalonicenses quinto[^p277-13]: « Sine intermissione orate ». Et *prolixe* etiam orabat, sicut dicitur supra sexto: « Factum est »; « et ipse erat pernoctans in oratione ». Et ideo fortasse tangitur hic *locus quidam,* ut ostendatur, quod orantibus
<!-- page 278 -->
requirendus est locus secretus; sed *tempus* tacetur, ut ostendatur, quod omni tempore indifferenter est orandum, secundum quod dicitur infra decimo octavo[^p278-1]: « Oportet orare semper et non deficere »; et Ecclesiastici decimo octavo: « Ne impediaris orare semper ».

3. Secundo quantum ad *promptitudinem discipulorum in imitando* subditur: *Ut cessavit, dixit unus ex discipulis eius ad eum: Domine, doce nos orare.* Recte a Domino petebant discipuli, qualiter et ipsi orare deberent, ne forte aliquid peterent contra eius voluntatem; unde Sapientiae nono[^p278-2]: « Quis hominum poterit scire consilium Dei; aut quis poterit cogitare, quid velit Deus? Cogitationes enim mortalium timidae, et incertae providentiae nostrae ». Ideo *doce nos orare,* cum sis Dominus noster; unde Isaiae quadragesimo octavo[^p278-3]: « Ego Dominus, docens te utilia ». — Et quod debeat audiri, ostendit per simile, cum addit: *Sicut et Ioannes discipulos suos docuit.* Scire namque orare Deum pertinet ad doctrinam pietatis, quoniam in oratione praecipue colitur Deus. Unde dicebat Propheta in Psalmo[^p278-4]: « Introibo in domum tuam, adorabo » etc.; et Chrysostomus: « Solam orationem quasi spirituale tributum anima offert de suis visceribus. Magna quippe dignitas orationis. Mox ut ab ore processit, suscipiunt eam Angeli manibus suis et offerunt ante Deum, sicut ait Angelus Tobiae duodecimo: *Ego obtuli orationem tuam ante Deum* » etc. Ideo ergo sic sollicite docetur et requiritur propter sui *excellentiam;* nec solum propter hoc, sed etiam propter *efficaciam,* secundum illud Marci undecimo[^p278-5]: « Quaecumque orantes petitis, credite, quod accipietis, et evenient vobis »; et Iacobi ultimo: « Orate pro invicem, ut salvemini; multum enim valet deprecatio iusti assidua. Elias homo erat » etc. — Unde per hoc innuitur nobis oratio multum amanda, quia per ipsam impetratur obtentio omnis boni et amotio omnis mali; unde Tobiae duodecimo[^p278-6]: « Bona est oratio cum ieiunio, et eleemosyna magis quam thesauros recondere. Quoniam eleemosyna a morte liberat, et ipsa est, quae purgat peccata et facit invenire misericordiam et vitam aeternam ».

4. (Vers. 2.). *Et ait illis: Cum oratis* etc. Post orationis exemplum subiungit *orationis documentum;* in quo primo traditur *forma invocandi;* secundo vero, *forma postulandi,* ibi[^p278-7]: *Sanctificetur nomen tuum.*

Quantum autem ad *formam invocandi* dicit: *Et ait illis: Cum oratis, dicite: Pater,* id est, primo invocate Patrem; *dicite,* inquam, non voce tantum, sed etiam corde, ne forte dicatur vobis illud Isaiae vigesimo nono[^p278-8]: « Populus hic labiis me honorat, cor autem eorum longe est a me ». *Dicite,* non tantum corde, verum etiam ore, quia vocalis oratio accepta est Deo, secundum illud Psalmi[^p278-9]: « Confitebor Domino nimis in ore meo »; et hoc, tum quia valet ad *memoriam suscitandam,* ad *somnolentiam excitandam,* ad *desiderium accendendum,* ad *obsequium impendendum,* ad *gaudium exprimendum* et ad *exemplum demonstrandum.* — Invocamus autem nomen *patris.* Ipse enim est *pater* ratione *conditionis naturae,* secundum illud ad Ephesios tertio[^p278-10]: « Ex quo omnis paternitas in caelo et in terra nominatur ». Unde Malachiae secundo: « Nunquid non pater unus omnium nostrum »? Est etiam *pater* ratione *collationis gratiae;* ad Romanos octavo[^p278-11]: « Accepistis spiritum adoptionis filiorum, in quo clamamus: Abba, Pater »; et ad Galatas quarto: « Quoniam estis filii Dei, misit Deus spiritum Filii sui clamantem: Abba, Pater ». Est etiam *pater* ratione *consummationis gloriae,* secundum illud Ieremiae tertio[^p278-12]: « Patrem vocabis me et post me ingredi non cessabis ». Quoniam igitur in nomine *patris* intelligitur Deus ut conditor naturae, largitor gratiae, consummator gloriae; hoc ipso datur intelligi, quod ipse est, a quo solo debemus petere.

5. Sed quoniam Matthaeus[^p278-13] orationem describit ut enarratam *Apostolis,* quibus Dominus cetera explicabat, ideo explicite tangit ista tria, dicens: *Pater,* ratione naturae; *noster,* ratione gratiae; *qui es in caelis,* ratione gloriae. Lucas vero exprimit ut *minoribus discipulis* traditam, ideo tradit formam magis implicitam. Uterque tamen concordat in invocatione nominis *patris,* ut in hoc uno nomine excitetur homo ad *reverentiam* et *confidentiam,* sine quibus duabus alis oratio non habet efficaciam. Habetur autem *reverentia* ex nomine *patris;* unde Malachiae primo[^p278-14]: « Si ego pater, ubi est honor meus »? et Ecclesiastici tertio: « Gloria hominis ex honore patris sui ». Habetur etiam et *confidentia,* secundum
<!-- page 279 -->
illud Isaiae quadragesimo nono[^p279-1]: « Nunquid oblivisci potest mulier infantem suum, ut non misereatur filio uteri sui » etc.; et infra eodem: « Si vos, cum sitis mali, nostis bona data dare filiis vestris » etc. Et Bernardus[^p279-2]: « Oratio, quae paterno dulcescit nomine, omnium petitionum mearum impetrandarum mihi fiduciam praestat ».

6. *Sanctificetur nomen tuum.* Post invocationem adiungit *petitionem.* Petit autem tria principaliter in hac oratione. Et in hoc concordant Matthaeus et Lucas, sed differunt in explicatione, quia propter causam praeassignatam[^p279-3] Matthaeus assignat petitiones magis explicitas; unde Matthaeus ponit septem, Lucas quinque, sed in his quinque septem illae sunt implicite.

7. Harum autem *distinctio, ordo* et *sufficientia* patet sic. A Deo enim Patre tria sunt postulanda: primum et principale est *consummatio gloriae,* secundum est *conservatio*[^p279-4] *gratiae,* tertium est *condonatio veniae.* Et ista tria ordinata sunt secundum gradum et ordinem maioris dignitatis. — Ad *consummationem gloriae* autem duo concurrunt, scilicet perfecta *notitia* et perfecta *reverentia,* et secundum haec sunt duae primae petitiones. — Ad *conservationem* autem *gratiae*[^p279-5] sufficit continua subministratio caelestis alimoniae, et haec petitur in tertia petitione. — Ad *condonationem veniae* duo concurrunt, scilicet *remissio culpae* et *remotio poenae,* et haec petuntur duabus ultimis petitionibus[^p279-6].

Sed quoniam *perfectio reverentiae* non tantum attenditur in affectu, sed etiam in effectu; ideo duabus primis petitionibus Matthaeus adiungit tertiam, scilicet « Fiat voluntas tua ». — Rursus, quia *remotio poenae* attenditur non solum quantum ad repulsionem tentationum, sed etiam quantum ad remotionem afflictionum et tribulationum; ideo duabus ultimis petitionibus Matthaeus adiungit septimam: « Sed libera nos a malo[^p279-7] ». — Et sic patet *sufficientia* petitionum secundum utrumque Evangelistam.

8. Patet etiam huius orationis *nobilitas.* Quamquam sit brevissima, continet in se omnem orationem et omnia postulanda: quoniam petens aut petit *amotionem mali,* aut *collationem boni*[^p279-8]. Si *mali,* aut mali *culpae,* aut mali *poenae:* aut mali, quod *patimur,* aut mali, quod *agimus*[^p279-9]; et sic sunt duae petitiones. Sed malum *poenae* potest subdividi: quia quoddam est *occasio culpae,* et sic est *tentatio;* quoddam mere tenet *rationem poenae,* et sic *tribulatio;* et sic ex illa petitione eliciuntur duae ultimae. Si autem petimus *bonum:* aut *aeternum,* aut *temporale.* Si *aeternum,* aut ex parte *intellectus,* aut ex parte *affectus,* et sic duae petitiones. Sed *affectus* habet ordinari respectu *maiestatis* et respectu *bonitatis;* et sic illa petitio prima divisione geminatur, sed subdivisione triplicatur. Si vero petitur bonum *temporale,* aut ratione *mentis,* aut ratione *corporis.* Sed quia *corporale* bonum non debet desiderari nisi propter *spirituale;* ideo tam secundum Lucam quam Matthaeum una fit petitio de utroque. — Et sic patet *sufficientia* divisionum per membra opposita et immediata, et patet *concordia* inter Evangelistas, et patet diversitatis *congruitas*[^p279-10].

9. Sunt igitur quinque petitiones secundum Lucam, *ordinatae* secundum nobilitatem maiorem et minorem. In prima petitur perfecta *notitia* sive sapientia in intellectu; in secunda, perfecta *reverentia* in affectu; in tertia, *sufficientia* in victu; in quarta, *indulgentia* in reatu; in quinta, *victoria* in conflictu. — Et in his quinque implicantur septem petitiones, ac per hoc septem virtutes, septem dona, septem beatitudines et *omnes* petitiones. Unde Glossa dicit super illud Matthaei sexto[^p279-11]: *Libera nos a malo:* « Nihil deest, quod in his septem petionibus non contineatur, sive ad praesentem, sive ad futuram pertineat vitam ».

10. Primo igitur quantum ad *notitiam* sive sapientiam *in intellectu* dicit: *Sanctificetur nomen tuum,* id est sanctum appareat *nomen tuum,* hoc est tua notitia; unde in Psalmo[^p279-12]: « Notus in Iudaea Deus, in Israel magnum nomen eius ». Et haec notitia incipit in gratia, sed consummatur in gloria; de quo Malachiae primo: « Ab ortu solis usque ad
<!-- page 280 -->
occasum magnum est nomen meum in gentibus; in omni loco sacrificatur et offertur nomini meo oblatio munda ». Hoc autem verificabitur in gloria, quando, secundum illud Ieremiae trigesimo primo[^p280-1], « non docebit vir ultra proximum suum, et vir fratrem suum, dicens: Cognoscite Dominum; omnes enim cognoscent me, a minore eorum usque ad maximum, dicit Dominus ».

11. Et nota, quod triplici notitia sanctificatur nomen Dei *in nobis,* cum *in se* semper sit sanctum. Prima est notitia, qua cognoscitur per fidem, secundum illud ad Hebraeos undecimo[^p280-2]: « Accedentem oportet credere, *quia est* ». — Secunda, qua cognoscitur, *quid non est;* de qua Augustinus: « Multum comprehendis, si comprehendis, quid non sit Deus ». — Tertia est, qua cognoscitur, *sicuti est;* de qua primae ad Corinthios decimo tertio[^p280-3]: « Nunc cognosco ex parte, tunc cognoscam, sicut et cognitus sum ». — Prima notitia liberat ab *insipientia,* de qua in Psalmo: « Dixit insipiens in corde suo: Non est Deus ». — Secunda, ab *idololatria,* qua colitur quod non est Deus; de qua primae ad Corinthios octavo: « De his quae immolantur idolis, scimus, quia idolum nihil est in mundo, et quia nullus Deus nisi unus. Nam etsi sint qui dicantur dii, sive in caelo, sive in terra, siquidem sunt dii multi et domini multi, nobis tamen unus est Deus Pater, ex quo omnia » etc. — Tertia liberat ab *omni miseria;* et haec erit in patria, quando complebitur donum sapientiae et pacis, per quam vocamur filii Dei. Et tunc nomen Dei sanctum erit in nobis; unde primae Ioannis tertio[^p280-4]: « Carissimi, nunc filii Dei sumus, et nondum apparuit, quid erimus. Scimus, quoniam, cum apparuerit, similes ei erimus, quia videbimus eum sicuti est ». Perfecta igitur *notitia* facit nos similes, et perfecta *similitudo* filios, et perfecta *filiatio* facit sanctificatione divini nominis dignos. Unde Chrysostomus[^p280-5]: « Sanctificatur in nobis nomen Dei, quando, scientes eum sanctum, timemus et sollicite vigilamus, ne forte violemus sanctitatem nominis eius in nobis ». Hoc autem erit, quando totaliter ei vacabimus, et per nullam distractionem inficientur mentes nostrae; quod erit in gloria, secundum illud Psalmi[^p280-6]: « In conspectu Angelorum psallam tibi, adorabo ad templum sanctum tuum et confitebor nomini tuo, super misericordia tua et veritate tua, quoniam magnificasti super omne nomen sanctum tuum ».

12. Secundo quantum ad perfectam *reverentiam in affectu* subdit: *Adveniat regnum tuum.* Tunc enim Deus perfecte *regnat* in nobis, quando nos sumus omnino ei subiecti, quod erit in fine, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p280-7]: « Deinde finis, cum tradiderit regnum Deo et Patri ». « Oportet autem eum regnare, donec ponat inimicos sub pedibus eius »; et post: « Cum subiecta fuerint ei omnia, tunc et ipse Filius subiectus erit ei qui subiecit sibi omnia, ut sit Deus omnia in omnibus ». Hoc autem regnum, quo Deus regnat in Sanctis, facit etiam ipsos *Sanctos* regnare et esse reges, secundum illud Apocalypsis quinto[^p280-8]: « Redemisti nos Deo in sanguine tuo ex omni tribu et lingua et populo et natione, et fecisti nos Deo nostro regnum et sacerdotes, et regnabimus super terram ». Hoc est regnum petendum et desiderandum; de quo in Psalmo[^p280-9]: « Regnum tuum, regnum omnium saeculorum »; et Apocalypsis undecimo: « Factae sunt voces magnae in caelo dicentes: Factum est regnum huius mundi Domini nostri et Christi, Filii eius, et regnabunt in saecula saeculorum, Amen ». — Per huius regni adventum non accrescit Deo potentia, sed in hominibus perfecta obedientia. Et ideo addit Matthaeus[^p280-10] post hanc petitionem: « Fiat voluntas tua, sicut in caelo et in terra ». Terreni enim non perfecte Deo obediunt, ut caelestes; et hoc insinuabat Dominus Ioannis decimo octavo: « Regnum meum non est de hoc mundo »; et hoc, quia diabolus regnat in illis qui obediunt ei, secundum illud ad Ephesios sexto: « Mundi rectores tenebrarum harum ». Sed in finali iudicio tolletur eius potestas, quando subditus erit omnis mundus Deo, secundum illud Danielis septimo[^p280-11]: « Dedit ei potestatem et honorem et regnum, et omnes populi, tribus et linguae ipsi servient ».

13. (Vers. 3.). Tertio vero quantum ad *sufficientiam in victu* subdit: *Panem nostrum quotidianum da nobis hodie;* ubi petitur principaliter panis alimoniae *spiritualis;* unde Matthaei sexto[^p280-12]:
<!-- page 281 -->
« Panem nostrum supersubstantialem da nobis hodie ». Sed quia non tantum hoc petitur, sed omne, quod necessarium est praesenti vitae; ideo, cum Ecclesia frequentet orationem dominicam secundum ipsum, non dicit *supersubstantialem* secundum ipsum, sed addit *quotidianum* secundum Lucam, ut ostendat, se petere quidquid est necessarium alimoniae huius vitae, quae per *hodie* intelligitur. Unde quemadmodum Hieronymus[^p281-1] dicit, sive dicatur *quotidianum,* sive *supersubstantialem,* utrumque consonat Hebraicae veritati. In Hebraica enim lingua unum vocabulum, id est *sogolla,* comprehendit utrumque intellectum. Sic et apud nos *quotidianum* utrumque panem comprehendit, scilicet spiritualem et corporalem, quia uterque nobis quotidie est necessarius et quotidie a Domino percipiendus, et ideo quotidie postulandus. Et hoc est quod dicit Beda in Glossa[^p281-2]: « Panis *quotidianus* dicitur, quia est hic necessarius, quantum animae carnique est tribuendum, sive spiritualiter, sive corporaliter, sive utroque modo accipiatur ».

14. Unde nota, quod quintuplex panis hic petitur. Primus est *alimonia praesentis vitae,* de quo dicitur Ecclesiastici vigesimo nono[^p281-3]: « Initium vitae hominis, aqua et panis et vestimentum ». — Secundus est *intelligentia sacrae Scripturae,* de quo Threnorum quarto: « Parvuli petierunt panem, et non erat qui frangeret eis ». — Tertius panis est *Sacramentum eucharistiae,* de quo Sapientiae decimo sexto[^p281-4]: « Praeparatum panem de caelo praestitisti eis, habentem in se omne delectamentum et omnem saporis suavitatem »; et Ioannis sexto: « Panis, quem ego dabo, caro mea est pro mundi vita ». — Quartus est *adiutorium gratiae,* de quo in Psalmo[^p281-5]: « Panem Angelorum manducavit homo »; et infra decimo quarto: « Beatus, qui manducabit panem in regno Dei ». — Quintus est *obsequium obedientiae,* de quo dicitur Ioannis quarto[^p281-6]: « Meus cibus est, ut faciam voluntatem Patris mei, qui est in caelis »; et de hoc tertii Regum decimo nono: « Respe
xit Elias, et ecce, ad caput eius panis subcinericius », in cuius fortitudine ambulavit et pervenit « usque ad montem Dei Horeb ». — His panibus nos reficit Dominus; quod designatum fuit Ioannis sexto[^p281-7], ubi dicitur, quod de quinque panibus satiavit quinque millia hominum. Et ideo quilibet istorum semper est petendus, secundum illud ibidem: « Domine, semper da nobis panem hunc ».

15. (Vers. 4.). Quarto quantum ad *indulgentiam in reatu* subdit: *Et dimitte nobis peccata nostra,* et hoc quoad *reatum obligationis;* unde Matthaei sexto[^p281-8] dicuntur *debita.* Peccata enim nos faciunt debitores eorum quae non possumus solvere; unde in Psalmo: « Mutuabitur peccator et non solvet » etc. Haec sunt *debita,* quibus obligata erat mulier Sunamitis, quarti Regum quarto[^p281-9], quae non poterat solvere, quousque Eliseus multiplicavit oleum. Per *oleum* misericordiae Christi peccata nostra solvuntur et dimittuntur. — Sed quia « iudicium sine misericordia fiet ei qui non fecerit misericordiam », secundum quod dicitur Iacobi secundo[^p281-10]; ideo subdit conditionem: *Siquidem et ipsi dimittimus omni debenti nobis.* Haec quidem conditio necessaria est ad impetrandum peccatorum veniam; unde Ecclesiastici vigesimo octavo[^p281-11]: « Relinque proximo nocenti te, et tunc deprecanti tibi peccata solventur. Homo homini servat iram, et a Deo quaerit medelam »? quasi dicat: frustra quaerit. Unde Matthaei sexto: « Si dimiseritis hominibus peccata eorum, dimittet vobis et Pater vester caelestis peccata vestra; si autem non dimiseritis, nec Pater vester dimittet vobis peccata vestra ». Unde et Matthaei decimo octavo[^p281-12] proponit parabolam de servo nequam, in cuius fine subiungit: « Sic Pater vester caelestis faciet vobis, si non remiseritis unusquisque fratri suo de cordibus vestris ». Et hoc recte, quia qui refugit legem clementiae cadit in legem iustitiae, de quo supra sexto: « Dimittite, et dimittemini »; « eadem quippe mensura, qua mensi fueritis, remetietur vobis ». Hanc conditionem apposuit hic Dominus, ut ostendat, illud esse verum,
<!-- page 282 -->
quod dicitur Iudith nono[^p282-1]: « Humilium et mansuetorum semper tibi placuit deprecatio », superborum autem et calumniatorum nunquam. Unde Isaiae primo: « Cum multiplicaveritis orationem, non exaudiam; manus enim vestrae sanguine plenae sunt ». Unde Chrysostomus[^p282-2]: « Si is qui laesus est, frustra orat, nisi indulserit; quomodo, putas, orat is qui laesus non est, si ipse per iniustitiam alios laedit et gravat? Qui autem non sic orat, ut Christus docuit, non est discipulus; nec Pater exaudit orationem, quam Filius non docuit. Cognoscit enim Pater Filii sui sensum et verba, non suscipit quae usurpatio humana excogitavit, sed quae sapientia Christi exposuit ».

16. Quinto vero quantum ad *victoriam in conflictu* subiungit: *Et ne nos inducas in tentationem,* id est, non permittas *induci,* id est superari; quia, sicut dicitur Iacobi primo[^p282-3], « Deus intentator malorum est; sed unusquisque tentatur, a concupiscentia sua abstractus et illectus ». — Et nota, quod non petit *non tentari,* quia tentatio hominem probat, secundum illud Psalmi[^p282-4]: « Proba me, Domine, et tenta me »; et Ecclesiastici trigesimo quarto: « Qui non est tentatus pauca recognoscit ». Sed petit *a tentatione non superari* nec vinci, sed vincere et triumphare. Hoc quippe petendum est propter *divini auxilii fidelitatem,* de qua primae ad Corinthios decimo[^p282-5]: « Fidelis est Deus, qui non permittet, vos tentari supra id quod potestis; sed faciet etiam cum tentatione proventum, ut possitis sustinere ». Petendum est etiam propter *recognoscendam nostram infirmitatem,* secundum illud secundi Paralipomenon vigesimo[^p282-6]: « In nobis quidem non est tanta fortitudo, ut possimus huic multitudini resistere, quae irruit super nos. Sed cum ignoremus, quid agere debeamus, hoc solum habemus residui, ut oculos nostros dirigamus ad te ». Unde Chrysostomus[^p282-7]: « Cognoscant, se esse infirmos, et cognitio infirmitatis glorificationis causam exstinguet », quia, secundum quod dicitur primi Machabaeorum tertio, « non in multitudine exercitus victoria belli, sed de caelo fortitudo est ».

17. Quoniam ergo *tribulatio* est tentatio, secundum illud Iacobi primo[^p282-8]: « Beatus vir, qui suffert tentationem » etc.; ideo qui eripitur ab omni malo *tentationis* eripitur per consequens a malo *tribulationis;* et ideo non oportet ultra addi: *Libera nos a malo,* quasi petitio omnino diversa, sed explicari quasi in hac inclusa. Et hoc est quod dicit Beda in Glossa[^p282-9]: « Sciat unusquisque, in eo se liberari a malo, quod non infertur in tentationem ». — Finit ergo Lucas orationem in *tentatione,* in qua dubium est de statu, vel lapsu; et ideo non addit *Amen,* quod est indicium certitudinis orationis exauditae; Matthaeus vero in *liberatione ab omni malo* poenae, ac per hoc a morte; de qua primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p282-10]: « Novissime autem destruetur inimica mors », et post haec est certitudo salutis; ideo finit dicendo *Amen,* quod est indicium certitudinis pro illo tempore. — Sed quia, quamdiu hic sumus, ad hanc certitudinem non pervenimus; ideo tacite dicitur et submisse a sacerdote, cum cetera in Missa dicantur elevata voce[^p282-11]. — Sunt et aliae rationes congruentiae; sed haec sufficiat ad praesens.

### Chapter XI.

#### The third part treats of the form of praying in three ways.

1. *And it came to pass, as he was in a certain place* etc. After he has delivered to the disciples the form of teaching and the form of living, in this part he gives *the form of praying*[^p277-7], by which is obtained the grace of knowing and of living rightly. Now this part has three parts; in the first of which the *example* of prayer is put first; in the second the *instruction* is added, at[^p277-8]: *And he said to them: When you pray* etc.; in the third the *incitement* of prayer is subjoined, at: *And he said to them: Which of you* etc.

Concerning *the example of prayer* two things are introduced: the first is *the solicitude of the Lord for praying,* the second is *the promptness*[^p277-9] *of the disciples for imitating.*

2. (Vers. 1.). First, then, as to *the solicitude of the Lord in praying* it is said: *And it came to pass, as*[^p277-10] *he was in a certain place praying; a certain,* that is, solitary and secret, because such places are apt for prayer, according to that of Matthew six: « But thou, when thou shalt pray, enter into thy chamber, and having shut the door, pray to thy Father in secret ». Now the Lord prays as *an argument of true humanity,* according to what is said below in the twenty-second[^p277-11]: « Being in an agony, he prayed the longer ». As *a help to our infirmity,* according to that of Hebrews five: « He was heard in all things for his reverence »; and Romans eight: « For we know not what we should pray for as we ought; but the Spirit himself », namely of the Lord Jesus, « helpeth our infirmity ». As *an example of perfect virtue;* whence it is said in Matthew twenty-six[^p277-12]: After he had prayed, he said to the disciples themselves: « Watch ye, and pray that ye enter not into temptation ». For this cause also he applied himself to prayer *frequently,* that he might teach that we must always pray, according to that of the first to the Thessalonians five[^p277-13]: « Pray without ceasing ». And he also prayed *at length,* as is said above in the sixth: « It came to pass »; « and he passed the whole night in prayer ». And therefore perhaps *a certain place* is touched on here, that it may be shown that for those who pray
<!-- page 278 -->
a secret place is to be sought; but *the time* is passed over in silence, that it may be shown that one must pray at every time indifferently, according to what is said below in the eighteenth[^p278-1]: « We ought always to pray, and not to faint »; and Ecclesiasticus eighteen: « Let nothing hinder thee from praying always ».

3. Secondly, as to *the promptness of the disciples in imitating,* it is subjoined: *When he ceased, one of his disciples said to him: Lord, teach us to pray.* Rightly did the disciples ask of the Lord how they too ought to pray, lest perhaps they should ask something against his will; whence Wisdom nine[^p278-2]: « For who among men is he that can know the counsel of God? or who can think what the will of God is? For the thoughts of mortal men are fearful, and our counsels uncertain ». Therefore *teach us to pray,* since thou art our Lord; whence Isaias forty-eight[^p278-3]: « I am the Lord, teaching thee profitable things ». — And that he ought to be heard, he shows by a like case, when he adds: *As John also taught his disciples.* For to know how to pray to God belongs to the doctrine of piety, since in prayer God is chiefly worshipped. Whence the Prophet said in the Psalm[^p278-4]: « I will come into thy house; I will worship » etc.; and Chrysostom: « Prayer alone the soul offers from its own bowels as a spiritual tribute. Great indeed is the dignity of prayer. As soon as it has gone forth from the mouth, the Angels take it up in their hands and offer it before God, as the Angel says in Tobias twelve: *I offered thy prayer before God* » etc. Therefore, then, it is so solicitously taught and sought for on account of its *excellence;* and not only on this account, but also on account of its *efficacy,* according to that of Mark eleven[^p278-5]: « Whatsoever you ask praying, believe that you shall receive, and they shall come unto you »; and James at the last: « Pray one for another, that you may be saved; for the continual prayer of a just man availeth much. Elias was a man » etc. — Whence by this it is intimated to us that prayer is much to be loved, because through it is obtained the getting of every good and the removal of every evil; whence Tobias twelve[^p278-6]: « Prayer is good with fasting, and alms more than to lay up treasures. For alms delivereth from death, and the same is that which purgeth away sins, and maketh to find mercy and life everlasting ».

4. (Vers. 2.). *And he said to them: When you pray* etc. After the example of prayer he subjoins *the instruction of prayer;* in which first is delivered *the form of invoking;* and secondly, *the form of asking,* at[^p278-7]: *Sanctified be thy name.*

As to *the form of invoking,* then, he says: *And he said to them: When you pray, say: Father,* that is, first invoke the Father; *say,* I say, not with the voice only, but also with the heart, lest perhaps that of Isaias twenty-nine be said to you[^p278-8]: « This people honoureth me with their lips, but their heart is far from me ». *Say,* not only with the heart, but also with the mouth, because vocal prayer is acceptable to God, according to that of the Psalm[^p278-9]: « I will give great thanks to the Lord with my mouth »; and this because it avails for *rousing the memory,* for *stirring up drowsiness,* for *kindling desire,* for *rendering homage,* for *expressing joy* and for *showing an example.* — Now we invoke the name of *father.* For he is *father* by reason of *the founding of nature,* according to that of Ephesians three[^p278-10]: « Of whom all paternity in heaven and earth is named ». Whence Malachias two: « Have we not all one father »? He is *father* also by reason of *the conferring of grace;* Romans eight[^p278-11]: « You have received the spirit of adoption of sons, whereby we cry: Abba, Father »; and Galatians four: « Because you are sons of God, God hath sent the Spirit of his Son crying: Abba, Father ». He is *father* also by reason of *the consummation of glory,* according to that of Jeremias three[^p278-12]: « Thou shalt call me father, and shalt not cease to walk after me ». Since, then, in the name of *father* God is understood as the founder of nature, the bestower of grace, the consummator of glory; by this very thing it is given to be understood that he it is from whom alone we ought to ask.

5. But since Matthew[^p278-13] describes the prayer as set forth to *the Apostles,* to whom the Lord explained the other things, therefore he touches these three explicitly, saying: *Father,* by reason of nature; *our,* by reason of grace; *who art in heaven,* by reason of glory. But Luke expresses it as delivered to *the lesser disciples,* therefore he delivers the form more implicitly. Both, however, agree in the invocation of the name of *father,* that in this one name man may be stirred up to *reverence* and *confidence,* without which two wings prayer has no efficacy. Now *reverence* is had from the name of *father;* whence Malachias one[^p278-14]: « If I be a father, where is my honour »? and Ecclesiasticus three: « The glory of a man is from the honour of his father ». And *confidence* also is had, according to
<!-- page 279 -->
that of Isaias forty-nine[^p279-1]: « Can a woman forget her infant, so as not to have pity on the son of her womb » etc.; and below in the same: « If you, then, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children » etc. And Bernard[^p279-2]: « Prayer, which grows sweet with the name of father, gives me confidence of obtaining all my petitions ».

6. *Sanctified be thy name.* After the invocation he adds *the petition.* Now he asks for three things principally in this prayer. And in this Matthew and Luke agree, but they differ in the unfolding, because for the cause assigned before[^p279-3] Matthew assigns the petitions more explicitly; whence Matthew puts seven, Luke five, but in these five those seven are contained implicitly.

7. Now the *distinction, order* and *sufficiency* of these is plain thus. For three things are to be asked of God the Father: the first and principal is *the consummation of glory,* the second is *the preservation*[^p279-4] *of grace,* the third is *the granting of pardon.* And these three are ordered according to the degree and order of greater dignity. — Now to *the consummation of glory* two things run together, namely perfect *knowledge* and perfect *reverence,* and according to these are the first two petitions. — But for *the preservation of grace*[^p279-5] the continual supplying of heavenly nourishment suffices, and this is asked in the third petition. — To *the granting of pardon* two things run together, namely *the remission of guilt* and *the removal of punishment,* and these are asked in the last two petitions[^p279-6].

But since *the perfection of reverence* is regarded not only in the affection, but also in the effect; therefore to the first two petitions Matthew adds a third, namely « Thy will be done ». — Again, because *the removal of punishment* is regarded not only as to the repelling of temptations, but also as to the removal of afflictions and tribulations; therefore to the last two petitions Matthew adds a seventh: « But deliver us from evil[^p279-7] ». — And so the *sufficiency* of the petitions according to both Evangelists is plain.

8. The *nobility* of this prayer is also plain. Although it is very short, it contains in itself every prayer and all things to be asked for: since he who asks asks either *the removal of evil,* or *the conferring of good*[^p279-8]. If of evil, either of the evil of *guilt,* or of the evil of *punishment:* either of the evil which we *suffer,* or of the evil which we *do*[^p279-9]; and so there are two petitions. But the evil of *punishment* can be subdivided: because one is *an occasion of guilt,* and so it is *temptation;* another holds merely *the account of punishment,* and so it is *tribulation;* and so from that petition the last two are drawn out. But if we ask for *good:* either *eternal,* or *temporal.* If *eternal,* either on the part of *the intellect,* or on the part of *the affection,* and so there are two petitions. But *the affection* has to be ordered in respect of *majesty* and in respect of *goodness;* and so that first petition is doubled by division, but tripled by subdivision. But if *temporal* good is asked, it is either by reason of *the mind,* or by reason of *the body.* But because *bodily* good ought not to be desired except for the sake of *the spiritual;* therefore according to Luke as well as Matthew one petition is made of both. — And so is plain the *sufficiency* of the divisions through opposite and immediate members, and the *concord* between the Evangelists is plain, and the *fittingness* of the diversity is plain[^p279-10].

9. There are, then, five petitions according to Luke, *ordered* according to greater and lesser nobility. In the first is asked perfect *knowledge* or wisdom in the intellect; in the second, perfect *reverence* in the affection; in the third, *sufficiency* in sustenance; in the fourth, *indulgence* in guilt; in the fifth, *victory* in conflict. — And in these five are implied seven petitions, and by this seven virtues, seven gifts, seven beatitudes and *all* petitions. Whence the Gloss says upon that of Matthew six[^p279-11]: *Deliver us from evil:* « There is nothing lacking that is not contained in these seven petitions [so printed: *petionibus* for *petitionibus*], whether it pertain to the present or to the future life ».

10. First, then, as to *knowledge* or wisdom *in the intellect* he says: *Sanctified be thy name,* that is, let *thy name* appear holy, that is, the knowledge of thee; whence in the Psalm[^p279-12]: « In Judea God is known: his name is great in Israel ». And this knowledge begins in grace, but is consummated in glory; of which Malachias one: « From the rising of the sun even to the
<!-- page 280 -->
going down, my name is great among the Gentiles; in every place there is sacrifice, and there is offered to my name a clean oblation ». And this will be made true in glory, when, according to that of Jeremias thirty-one[^p280-1], « they shall teach no more every man his neighbour, and every man his brother, saying: Know the Lord; for all shall know me, from the least of them even to the greatest, saith the Lord ».

11. And note that the name of God is sanctified *in us* by a threefold knowledge, though *in itself* it is always holy. The first is the knowledge by which he is known through faith, according to that of Hebrews eleven[^p280-2]: « He that cometh must believe *that he is* ». — The second, by which it is known *what he is not;* of which Augustine: « Thou comprehendest much, if thou comprehendest what God is not ». — The third is that by which he is known *as he is;* of which the first to the Corinthians thirteen[^p280-3]: « Now I know in part; but then I shall know even as I am known ». — The first knowledge frees from *folly,* of which in the Psalm: « The fool hath said in his heart: There is no God ». — The second, from *idolatry,* by which that is worshipped which is not God; of which the first to the Corinthians eight: « Concerning the things that are sacrificed to idols, we know that an idol is nothing in the world, and that there is no God but one. For although there be that are called gods, either in heaven or on earth, for there be gods many and lords many, yet to us there is but one God, the Father, of whom are all things » etc. — The third frees from *all misery;* and this will be in the fatherland, when the gift of wisdom and of peace will be fulfilled, through which we are called sons of God. And then the name of God will be holy in us; whence the first of John three[^p280-4]: « Dearly beloved, we are now the sons of God, and it hath not yet appeared what we shall be. We know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like to him, because we shall see him as he is ». Perfect *knowledge,* therefore, makes us like, and perfect *likeness* makes us sons, and perfect *sonship* makes us worthy of the sanctification of the divine name. Whence Chrysostom[^p280-5]: « The name of God is sanctified in us when, knowing him to be holy, we fear and solicitously keep watch, lest perhaps we violate the holiness of his name in us ». And this will be when we shall be wholly free for him[^tr-vacare], and our minds will be infected by no distraction; which will be in glory, according to that of the Psalm[^p280-6]: « In the sight of the Angels I will sing praise to thee; I will worship towards thy holy temple, and I will give glory to thy name, for thy mercy and for thy truth; for thou hast magnified thy holy name above all ».

12. Secondly, as to perfect *reverence in the affection* he subjoins: *Thy kingdom come.* For then God reigns perfectly in us when we are wholly subject to him, which will be at the end, according to that of the first to the Corinthians fifteen[^p280-7]: « Afterwards the end, when he shall have delivered up the kingdom to God and the Father ». « For he must reign, until he hath put all his enemies under his feet »; and after: « And when all things shall be subdued unto him, then the Son also himself shall be subject unto him that put all things under him, that God may be all in all ». Now this kingdom, by which God reigns in the Saints, makes the *Saints* themselves also reign and be kings, according to that of the Apocalypse five[^p280-8]: « Thou hast redeemed us to God in thy blood, out of every tribe, and tongue, and people, and nation, and hast made us to our God a kingdom and priests, and we shall reign on the earth ». This is the kingdom to be asked for and desired; of which in the Psalm[^p280-9]: « Thy kingdom is a kingdom of all ages »; and the Apocalypse eleven: « There were great voices in heaven, saying: The kingdom of this world is become our Lord's and Christ's, his Son's, and they shall reign for ever and ever, Amen ». — By the coming of this kingdom power does not increase to God, but in men perfect obedience. And therefore Matthew adds[^p280-10] after this petition: « Thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven ». For earthly men do not obey God perfectly, as the heavenly do; and the Lord intimated this in John eighteen: « My kingdom is not of this world »; and this, because the devil reigns in those who obey him, according to that of Ephesians six: « The rulers of the world of this darkness ». But in the final judgement his power will be taken away, when the whole world will be subject to God, according to that of Daniel seven[^p280-11]: « He gave him power, and glory, and a kingdom; and all peoples, tribes and tongues shall serve him ».

13. (Vers. 3.). Thirdly, as to *sufficiency in sustenance* he subjoins: *Give us this day our daily bread;* where the bread of *spiritual* nourishment is asked principally; whence Matthew six[^p280-12]:
<!-- page 281 -->
« Give us this day our supersubstantial bread ». But because not only this is asked, but everything that is necessary to the present life; therefore, though the Church uses the Lord's prayer according to him, it does not say *supersubstantial* according to him, but adds *daily* according to Luke, to show that it asks for whatever is necessary to the nourishment of this life, which is understood by *this day.* Whence, as Jerome[^p281-1] says, whether *daily* is said or *supersubstantial,* both accord with the Hebrew truth. For in the Hebrew tongue one word, namely *sogolla,* comprehends both understandings. So too with us *daily* comprehends both breads, namely the spiritual and the bodily, because each is necessary to us daily and is to be received daily from the Lord, and therefore to be asked for daily. And this is what Bede says in the Gloss[^p281-2]: « The bread is called *daily* because it is necessary here, as much as is to be given to soul and flesh, whether it be taken spiritually, or bodily, or in both ways ».

14. Note, then, that a fivefold bread is asked for here. The first is *the nourishment of the present life,* of which it is said in Ecclesiasticus twenty-nine[^p281-3]: « The chief thing for man's life is water and bread, and clothing ». — The second is *the intelligence of sacred Scripture,* of which Lamentations four: « The little ones have asked for bread, and there was none to break it unto them ». — The third bread is *the Sacrament of the eucharist,* of which Wisdom sixteen[^p281-4]: « Bread prepared from heaven thou gavest them, having in it all that is delicious, and the sweetness of every taste »; and John six: « The bread that I will give is my flesh, for the life of the world ». — The fourth is *the help of grace,* of which in the Psalm[^p281-5]: « Man ate the bread of Angels »; and below in the fourteenth: « Blessed is he that shall eat bread in the kingdom of God ». — The fifth is *the homage of obedience,* of which it is said in John four[^p281-6]: « My meat is to do the will of my Father who is in heaven »; and of this the third of Kings nineteen: « Elias looked, and behold, at his head a hearth cake », in the strength of which he walked and came « unto the mount of God, Horeb ». — With these breads the Lord refreshes us; which was designated in John six[^p281-7], where it is said that with five loaves he satisfied five thousand men. And therefore each of these is always to be asked for, according to that in the same place: « Lord, give us always this bread ».

15. (Vers. 4.). Fourthly, as to *indulgence in guilt* he subjoins: *And forgive us our sins,* and this as to *the guilt of obligation;* whence in Matthew six[^p281-8] they are called *debts.* For sins make us debtors of what we cannot pay; whence in the Psalm: « The sinner shall borrow, and not pay again » etc. These are the *debts* by which the Sunamite woman was bound, in the fourth of Kings four[^p281-9], which she could not pay until Eliseus multiplied the oil. By the *oil* of Christ's mercy our sins are paid and forgiven. — But because « judgement without mercy to him that hath not done mercy », according to what is said in James two[^p281-10]; therefore he subjoins a condition: *For we also forgive every one that is indebted to us.* This condition indeed is necessary for obtaining pardon of sins; whence Ecclesiasticus twenty-eight[^p281-11]: « Forgive thy neighbour if he hath hurt thee, and then shall thy sins be forgiven to thee when thou prayest. Man to man reserveth anger, and doth he seek remedy of God »? as if to say: he seeks in vain. Whence Matthew six: « If you will forgive men their sins, your heavenly Father will forgive you also your sins; but if you will not forgive, neither will your Father forgive you your sins ». Whence also Matthew eighteen[^p281-12] sets forth the parable of the wicked servant, at the end of which he subjoins: « So shall your heavenly Father do to you, if you forgive not every one his brother from your hearts ». And this rightly, because he who flees the law of clemency falls into the law of justice, of which above in the sixth: « Forgive, and you shall be forgiven »; « for with the same measure that you shall mete withal, it shall be measured to you again ». This condition the Lord set here, to show that to be true
<!-- page 282 -->
which is said in Judith nine[^p282-1]: « The prayer of the humble and the meek hath always pleased thee », but never that of the proud and of calumniators. Whence Isaias one: « When you multiply prayer, I will not hear; for your hands are full of blood ». Whence Chrysostom[^p282-2]: « If he who has been hurt prays in vain unless he has pardoned; how, thinkest thou, does he pray who has not been hurt, if he himself hurts and burdens others through injustice? But he who does not pray as Christ taught is not a disciple; nor does the Father hear a prayer which the Son did not teach. For the Father knows the sense and the words of his Son; he does not receive what human usurpation has devised, but what the wisdom of Christ has set forth ».

16. Fifthly, as to *victory in conflict* he subjoins: *And lead us not into temptation,* that is, do not permit us *to be led in,* that is, to be overcome; because, as is said in James one[^p282-3], « God is not a tempter of evils; but every man is tempted by his own concupiscence, being drawn away and allured ». — And note that he does not ask *not to be tempted,* because temptation proves a man, according to that of the Psalm[^p282-4]: « Prove me, O Lord, and tempt me »; and Ecclesiasticus thirty-four: « He that hath not been tempted knoweth little ». But he asks *not to be overcome by temptation* nor vanquished, but to vanquish and triumph. For this is to be asked on account of *the faithfulness of the divine help,* of which the first to the Corinthians ten[^p282-5]: « God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that which you are able; but will make also with temptation issue, that you may be able to bear it ». It is to be asked also on account of *the recognizing of our infirmity,* according to that of the second of Paralipomenon twenty[^p282-6]: « In us indeed there is not strength enough to be able to resist this multitude, which cometh violently upon us. But as we know not what we ought to do, we have only this left, to turn our eyes to thee ». Whence Chrysostom[^p282-7]: « Let them know that they are infirm, and the cognition of infirmity will extinguish the cause of glorification », because, according to what is said in the first of Machabees three, « the success of war is not in the multitude of the army, but strength cometh from heaven ».

17. Since, then, *tribulation* is temptation, according to that of James one[^p282-8]: « Blessed is the man that endureth temptation » etc.; therefore he who is delivered from every evil of *temptation* is delivered in consequence from the evil of *tribulation;* and therefore *Deliver us from evil* need not be added further, as a petition wholly diverse, but unfolded as included in this one. And this is what Bede says in the Gloss[^p282-9]: « Let every one know that he is delivered from evil in this, that he is not brought into temptation ». — Luke, then, ends the prayer in *temptation,* in which there is doubt of standing or of falling; and therefore he does not add *Amen,* which is a sign of the certitude of the prayer heard; but Matthew in *deliverance from every evil* of punishment, and by this from death; of which the first to the Corinthians fifteen[^p282-10]: « And last of all the enemy death shall be destroyed », and after this is the certitude of salvation; therefore he ends by saying *Amen,* which is a sign of certitude for that time. — But because, as long as we are here, we do not arrive at this certitude; therefore it is said silently and in a low voice by the priest, while the other things in the Mass are said with raised voice[^p282-11]. — There are also other reasons of fittingness; but let these suffice for the present.
