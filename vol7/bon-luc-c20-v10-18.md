# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XX, Vers. 10-18
## *Et in tempore vindemiae misit etc. Postquam descripsit benignitatem liberalitatis divinae, describit hic malignitatem perversitatis Iudaicae*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 504 -->

14. *Et in tempore vindemiae*[^p504-10] *misit* etc. Postquam descripsit benignitatem liberalitatis divinae, describit hic *malignitatem perversitatis Iudaicae*[^p504-11]. Describitur autem perversitas malignitatis Iudaeorum dupliciter: primo, scilicet *ante Christi adventum;* secundo, *post Christi adventum,* ibi[^p504-12]: *Dixit autem dominus vineae: Quid faciam?*

*Ante Christi adventum* describitur tripliciter, scilicet respectu *legislatoris,* respectu *regalis potestatis* et respectu *cunei prophetalis*[^p504-13]; et secundum tria, per quae populus ille manuduci poterat ad iustitiam, scilicet per *Legem, psalmodiam* et *prophetiam,* secundum *altitudinem* potestatis, *dulcedinem* bonitatis et *certitudinem* veritatis.

15. (Vers. 10.). Primo quantum ad *perversitatem Iudaicae gentis respectu legislatoris Moysi* dicit: *Et in tempore vindemiae misit ad cultores servum, ut de fructu vineae darent illi;* Glossa[^p504-14]: « Primus servus, qui mittitur, est Moyses legislator, qui per quadraginta annos fructum Legis, quam dederat, a cultoribus inquirebat »; Psalmus: « Misit Moysen servum suum, Aaron, quem elegit ipsum ». De hac missione Exodi tertio: « Veni, mittam te ad Pharaonem, ut educas populum meum, filios Israel de Aegypto ». — Sed quia hunc servum Iudaeorum perversitas non acceptavit, ideo subdit: *Qui caesum dimiserunt
<!-- page 505 -->
eum inanem.* Glossa[^p505-1]: « Caesum dimittunt inanem, quia *irritaverunt Moysen in castris, Aaron sanctum Domini;* et iterum: *Vexatus est Moyses propter eos, quia exacerbaverunt spiritum eius* ». Unde Deuteronomii trigesimo primo[^p505-2]: « Ego scio contentionem tuam et cervicem tuam durissimam. Adhuc vivente me et ingrediente vobiscum, semper contentiose egistis contra Dominum; quanto magis, cum mortuus fuero »? Hoc autem fecerunt *coloni specialiter,* id est proceres synagogae; Numerorum decimo sexto[^p505-3]: « Ecce, Core, filius Isaar, et Dathan et Abiron surrexerunt contra Moysen, alii quoque filii Israel, ducenti quinquaginta viri proceres synagogae, et qui tempore concilii per nomina vocabantur ». Et hos sequebantur minores; unde et sequitur[^p505-4] post, quod « murmuravit omnis multitudo filiorum Israel sequenti die contra Moysen et Aaron. Cumque oriretur seditio et tumultus incresceret, Moyses et Aaron fugerunt in tabernaculum foederis ».
16. (Vers. 11.). Secundo quantum ad *perversitatem respectu regis David,* praecipui cultoris Dei, subdit: *Et addidit alterum servum mittere;* Glossa[^p505-5]: « Alter servus, David propheta, qui post Moysen colonos vineae psalmodiae modulamine et citharae dulcedine ad exercitium boni operis invitavit ». Hunc Dominus misit, quando de pascuis elegit eum ad regendum et pascendum populum suum, secundum quod ipse propheta David dicit in Psalmo[^p505-6]: « Elegit David servum suum et sustulit eum de gregibus ovium. De post foetantes accepit eum, pascere Iacob servum suum et Israel hereditatem suam ». Unde secundi Regum septimo: « Haec dices servo meo David: Ego te tuli de pascuis sequentem greges, ut esses dux super populum meum Israel; et fui tecum in omnibus, ubicumque ambulasti, fecique tibi nomen grande iuxta nomen magnorum, qui sunt in terra »; unde et ei, scilicet David, dedit sedem regalem in sempiternum; eidem dedit prophetiae spiritum, eidem dedit psalmodiae cantum, ut sic inclinaret populum illum durum[^p505-7]. — Et tamen mitigare non potuit duritiam Iudaeorum, propter quod addit: *Illi autem, hunc quoque caedentes et afficientes contumelia, dimiserunt inanem,* quia contempserunt eius regimen et ducatum. Unde Glossa[^p505-8]: « Contumelia affectum dimittunt inanem dicentes: *Quae nobis pars in David, aut quae hereditas in filio Isai* »? secundi Regum vigesimo. Ubi etiam praemittitur, qualiter David cum Absalome persecuti sunt; secundi Regum decimo quinto[^p505-9]: « Venit nuntius ad David dicens: Toto corde universus Israel sequitur Absalom. Et ait David servis suis: Surgite, fugiamus; non erit nobis effugium a facie Absalom ». Et post decimo sexto dicitur de contumeliis ei illatis: « Ita loquebatur Semei, cum malediceret regi: Egredere, egredere, vir sanguinum et vir Belial. Reddidit tibi Dominus universum sanguinem domus Saul; et ecce, premunt te mala, quoniam vir sanguinum es ».

17. (Vers. 12.). Tertio quantum ad *perversitatem illius populi respectu cunei prophetalis* subiungitur: *Et addidit tertium mittere;* Glossa[^p505-10]: « Tertius servus est chorus Prophetarum, qui populum corripiebant et mala, quae vineae imminebant, praedicebant ». De missione huius servi dicitur Ieremiae septimo[^p505-11]: « Misi ad vos omnes servos meos Prophetas per diem, consurgens diluculo et mittens. Et non audierunt me nec inclinaverunt aurem suam, sed induraverunt cervicem suam et peius operati sunt quam patres eorum ». — Unde et addit: *Qui et illum vulnerantes eiecerunt.* Nam Isaiam serraverunt, Ieremiam lapidaverunt et Ezechielem excerebraverunt. Ideo Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p505-12]: « Ierusalem, Ierusalem, quae occidis Prophetas et lapidas eos qui ad te missi sunt »; et Actuum septimo dicebat Stephanus Iudaeis: « Dura cervice et incircumcisis cordibus et auribus, vos semper Spiritui sancto restitistis, sicut patres vestri, ita et vos. Quem Prophetarum non sunt persecuti patres vestri? Et occiderunt eos qui praenuntiabant de adventu Iusti ». Ideo ipsi Prophetae, videntes eius duritiam, et arguebant et deplorabant. Nam dicitur Isaiae quinto[^p505-13] de vinea: « Exspectavi, ut faceret uvas, et fecit labruscas »; et Ieremiae secundo: « Quomodo conversa es mihi in pravum, vinea aliena »? et Ezechielis decimo nono: « Mater tua quasi vinea in sanguine tuo super aquam plantata est ». « Et evulsa est in ira, in terram proiecta est, et ventus urens siccavit fructum eius ». In hoc autem mira apparet Iudaeorum perfidia, quia omnes isti quos persecuti sunt, maxime diligebant eos. Nam Moyses pro populo non cessavit orare; Exodi trigesimo secundo[^p505-14]: « Cur, Domine, irascitur furor tuus contra populum tuum, quem eduxisti de Aegypto »? « Aut dimitte
<!-- page 506 -->
eis hanc noxam » etc. Similiter et David[^p506-1]: « Domine Deus virtutum, convertere; respice de caelo et vide et visita vineam istam ». Similiter chorus Prophetarum, unde Ieremiae decimo octavo: « Recordare, quod steterim in conspectu tuo, ut loquerer pro eis bonum et averterem iram indignationis tuae ab eis ». Et hi omnes figuram Christi gesserunt, de quo Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p506-2]: « Ipse peccata multorum tulit et pro transgressoribus exoravit ».

18. (Vers. 13.). *Dixit autem dominus vineae* etc. Post descriptam perversitatem Iudaeorum ante Christi adventum in personas servorum hic describit *malignitatem perversitatis eorundem post adventum contra ipsum Dei Filium Iesum Christum*[^p506-3]. Circa cuius descriptionem tria introducuntur, scilicet *gratuitum Dei beneficium, Iudaeorum propositum perversum* et *eorundem factum flagitiosum.*

Primo quantum ad *gratuitum Dei beneficium* praemittit: *Dixit autem dominus vineae: Quid faciam? Mittam filium meum dilectum,* hoc fuit maximum beneficium, secundum illud primae Ioannis quarto[^p506-4]: « In hoc apparuit caritas Dei in nobis, quoniam Filium suum unigenitum misit Deus in mundum, ut vivamus per eum »; et Ioannis tertio: « Sic Deus dilexit mundum, ut Filium suum unigenitum daret ». Haec autem missio fuit ex occulto et secretissimo proposito et consilio Dei[^p506-5]. — Et quoniam hoc proposuerat definito consilio, ideo dicit: *Quid faciam?* Propter quod Moyses Exodi quarto[^p506-6] dicebat: « Obsecro, Domine, mitte quem missurus es »; sed Dominus reservavit eum mittere in fine, tum propter consummationem ordinis, secundum illud ad Galatas quarto[^p506-7]: « Cum essemus parvuli, sub elementis mundi eramus servientes. At ubi venit plenitudo temporis, misit Deus Filium suum »; tum etiam, ut appareret, quod per nullum alium poterat subveniri humanae perfidiae et obdurationi. — Propter quod addit: *Forsitan, cum hunc viderint, verebuntur.* *Forsitan* non praenotat dubitationem in Deo, qui certissime cuncta praevidet, sed arbitrii libertatem, quod est ad utrumque vertibile. Unde Glossa[^p506-8]: « Quod ambigit, id est, verbo dubitationi utitur, non est Dei ignorantia, sed ut reservetur homini libera voluntas ». Unde quod Christum non sunt veriti Iudaei, non fuit defectus ex parte Dei, sed ex parte duritiae cordis humani; Isaiae quinto[^p506-9]: « Quid est, quod ultra debui facere vineae meae et non feci »? Qui enim Filium Dei non verentur nunquam amplius Deum nec eius nuntium verebuntur, quia, Ioannis quinto, « Pater omne iudicium dedit Filio, ut omnes honorificent Filium, sicut honorificant Patrem; qui non honorificat Filium non honorificat Patrem, qui misit illum ».

19. (Vers. 14.). Secundo quantum ad *propositum perversum* subditur: *Quem cum vidissent, coloni cogitaverunt intra se dicentes: Hic est heres,* per coniecturationem veritatis; nam certa scientia non cognoverunt, sicut dicitur primae ad Corinthios secundo[^p506-10]: « Quem nemo principum huius saeculi cognovit. Si enim cognovissent, nunquam Dominum gloriae crucifixissent ». Cognoverunt tamen, etsi non per fidem, per aliqua signa manuducentia ad credendum, quod esset Messias in Lege promissus. Ideo non ex ignorantia, sed ex certa malitia contra Christum conspiraverunt. Unde Glossa[^p506-11]: « Aperte probat, Iudaeos non per ignorantiam crucifixisse Filium Dei, sed per invidiam. Intellexerunt enim, eum esse hunc cui dictum est: *Postula a me, et dabo tibi gentes hereditatem tuam* ». Quod ergo dicitur Actuum tertio[^p506-12]: « Nunc scio, fratres, quia per ignorantiam fecistis, sicut et principes vestri », non excludit a Iudaeis qualemcumque notitiam, sed notitiam certam; vel dicitur quoad minores, qui peccaverunt ex ignorantia. Nam maiores hoc fecerunt ex certa malitia, quia timebant temporalem perdere gloriam. — Ideo addit: *Occidamus eum*[^p506-13]*, ut nostra fiat hereditas,* per machinationem perversitatis; quod dixerunt, non ut hereditatem novam acquirerent, cum Christus esset summe pauper, sed ut habitam conservarent. Unde Ioannis undecimo[^p506-14]: « Collegerunt pontifices et Pharisaei consilium, dicentes: Quia hic homo multa signa facit. Si dimittimus eum sic, omnes
<!-- page 507 -->
credent in eum, et venient Romani et tollent locum nostrum et gentem ». Unde istud propositum nequam originem traxit ex *avaritia,* traxit etiam ortum ex *invidia;* unde Sapientiae secundo[^p507-1]: « Circumveniamus virum iustum, quoniam contrarius est operibus nostris et improperat in nos peccata legis et diffamat in nos peccata disciplinae nostrae »; et inferius: « Morte turpissima condemnemus eum. Haec cogitaverunt et erraverunt, excaecavit enim eos malitia eorum »; quoniam « invidia diaboli mors intravit in orbem terrarum ». Huius figura praecessit in Ioseph; Genesis trigesimo septimo[^p507-2]: « Cogitaverunt fratres eius occidere eum et mutuo loquebantur dicentes: Ecce, somniator venit; venite, occidamus eum et mittamus in cisternam veterem dicemusque: Fera pessima devoravit eum » etc. — Ideo Evangelium istud legitur simul cum historia Ioseph imminente passione[^p507-3].

20. (Vers. 15.). Tertio quantum ad *factum flagitiosum* subiungitur: *Et eiectum illum extra vineam occiderunt,* id est extra Ierusalem, quia dicitur ad Hebraeos ultimo[^p507-4]: « Extra portam Christus passus est. Exeamus ergo ad eum extra castra, improperium eius portantes ». *Occiderunt* autem, dum tradiderunt eum Pilato ad occidendum et petierunt, quod crucifigeretur; unde Actuum tertio[^p507-5]: « Iesum, Filium Dei tradidistis et negastis ante faciem Pilati, iudicante illo dimitti. Vos autem petistis, virum homicidam donari vobis, auctorem vero vitae interfecistis ». Et sic impletum est illud Isaiae quinto de vinea plantata[^p507-6]: « Exspectavi, ut faceret iudicium, et ecce, iniquitas; et iustitiam, et ecce, clamor »; *clamor* scilicet ad Christum occidendum, de quo Marci decimo quinto: « Clamabant: Crucifige eum. Pilatus vero dicebat illis: Quid enim mali fecit? At illi magis clamabant: Crucifige eum ». Unde de hac vinea non exivit vinum, sed fel, secundum illud Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo[^p507-7]: « De vinea Sodomorum vinea eorum et de suburbanis Gomorrhae, uva eorum uva fellis et botrus amarissimus. Fel draconum vinum eorum et venenum aspidum insanabile ».

21. (Vers. 15. 16.). *Quid ergo faciet illis* etc. Postquam descripsit parabolice benignitatem liberalitatis divinae et perversitatem malignitatis Iudaicae, hic iam tertio[^p507-8] describit *severitatem ultionis iudiciariae.* Circa quam tria insinuantur, scilicet *severitas ultionis divinae, incredulitas rebellionis Iudaicae, firmitas attestationis Scripturae.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *severitatem ultionis divinae* dicit: *Quid ergo faciet illis dominus vineae?* Hoc quaerit non ut dubitans, sed ut excitet ad intelligentiam sententiae[^p507-9], quantum ad quam subdit: *Veniet et perdet colonos istos et vineam dabit aliis;* in quo datur sententia reprobationis Iudaeorum et electionis gentium; et haec sub figura vineae et vinitorum, de qua Isaiae quinto[^p507-10]: « Nunc ostendam vobis, quid faciam vineae meae: auferam sepem eius, et erit in direptionem; diruam maceriam eius, et erit in conculcationem. Et ponam eam desertam, non putabitur et non fodietur, et ascendent super eam vepres et spinae; et nubibus mandabo, ne pluant super eam imbrem ». Hanc *reprobationem* plangit Propheta in Psalmo[^p507-11] dicens: « Ut quid destruxisti maceriam eius, et vindemiant eam omnes, qui praetergrediuntur viam? Exterminavit eam aper de silva, et singularis ferus depastus est eam ». De *electione* vero *gentium* dicitur ad Romanos undecimo[^p507-12]: « Caecitas ex parte contigit in Israel, ut plenitudo gentium subintraret »; et de hoc ad Romanos nono: « Vocabo non plebem meam plebem meam; et non misericordiam consecutam misericordiam consecutam »; quod sumtum est de Osee secundo[^p507-13]. Et de hoc Matthaei vigesimo primo: « Auferetur a vobis regnum Dei et dabitur genti facienti fructus eius ».

22. Secundo quantum ad *incredulitatem rebellionis Iudaicae* subdit: *Quo audito, dixerunt illi: Absit,* quasi dicant: non ita eveniet, ut dixisti. Sed contrarium huius videtur Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p507-14], ubi dicitur sic: « Cum venerit dominus vineae, quid faciet agricolis illis? Aiunt illi: Malos male perdet et vineam suam locabit aliis agricolis, qui reddent ei fructum temporibus suis ». Hic autem dicitur, quod contradixerunt. — Sed hanc apparentem contrarietatem dissolvit Augustinus in libro de Concordia Evangelistarum[^p507-15], ubi dicit sic: « Intelligimus,
<!-- page 508 -->
quosdam respondisse quod Matthaeus commemorat; quosdam vero, quod Lucas ». — Aliter respondet Chrysostomus[^p508-1], quod « *unum* dixerunt corde, scilicet quod Matthaeus dicit; *aliud* ore, scilicet quod dicit Lucas. Unde conscientia erat consona divinae sententiae et parabolae, sed lingua erat contraria ». Unde Glossa[^p508-2]: « Contradicunt sententiae, quam contra perfidiam suam dictam cognoscunt; quod enim in mente gerebant in parabola contineri intelligebant ». Et ex hoc mirum Dei apparet iudicium, quod contradicendo volebant latere, et tamen se ipsos detegebant, dum veritati se ipsos opponebant, contra illud Ecclesiastici quarto[^p508-3]: « Non contradicas verbo veritatis ullo modo, et de mendacio » etc.

23. (Vers. 17.). Tertio quantum ad *firmitatem attestationis Scripturae* subiungit: *Ille autem respiciens*[^p508-4] *eos, ait: Quid est ergo hoc quod scriptum est: Lapidem, quem reprobaverunt aedificantes, hic factus est in caput anguli?* Hoc scribitur in Psalmo[^p508-5], ubi statim additur: « A Domino factum est istud » etc. In hoc autem ostenditur *mira Christi humilitas,* qui voluit reprobari; propter quod admiratur Isaias quinquagesimo tertio[^p508-6]: Domine, « quis credidit auditui nostro? Et brachium Domini cui revelatum est? Et ascendet sicut virgultum coram eo et sicut radix de terra sitienti ». « Et nos reputavimus eum quasi leprosum et percussum a Deo et humiliatum ». In hoc etiam ostenditur *mira Christi potestas,* quod disiuncta maxime ad unitatem reduxit quasi lapis angularis. — Dicitur autem Christus *lapis* propter permanentiam stabilitatis, de qua Danielis secundo[^p508-7]: « Post haec videbas, donec abscissus est de monte lapis sine manibus et percussit statuam ». « Lapis autem, qui percusserat statuam, factus est mons magnus et implevit universam terram ». — Dicitur autem *angularis* propter virtutem connexionis; ad Ephesios secundo[^p508-8]: « Ipse est pax nostra, qui fecit utraque unum, et medium parietem maceriae solvens »; « ut duos condat in semetipsum in unum novum hominem, faciens pacem ». De hoc Iob trigesimo octavo[^p508-9]: « Ubi eras, quando ponebam fundamenta terrae? Quis posuit mensuras eius, aut quis demisit lapidem angularem eius »? Hic lapis angularis terrae est lapis unitivus Ecclesiae, de quo Isaiae vigesimo octavo[^p508-10]: « Ecce, ego mittam in fundamentis Sion lapidem, lapidem probatum, angularem, pretiosum, in fundamento fundatum ». — Dicitur etiam *caput anguli* propter summitatem perfectionis; ad Ephesios secundo[^p508-11]: « Ipso summo angulari lapide Christo, in quo omnis aedificatio constructa crescit in templum sanctum in Domino » etc. Et ideo primae Petri secundo: « Ad quem accedentes, lapidem vivum, ab hominibus reprobatum ». « Et ipsi tanquam lapides vivi » etc. — Imaginandus est ergo Christus tanquam lapis centralis[^p508-12] in toto corpore Ecclesiae, quod circumferentiae circuli comparatur; ad quem quidem omnes lineae deductae angulum faciunt et uniuntur in ipso tanquam in puncto indivisibilitatis, in quo fit osculum linearum crucis in medio sui concurrentium in unum. Est igitur lapis iste stabiliens electos, et econtra est conterens adversarios et reprobos.

24. (Vers. 18.). Et ideo subdit: *Omnis, qui ceciderit super illum lapidem, conquassabitur,* per corruptionem peccati; *super quem autem ceciderit, comminuet eum*[^p508-13], per ultionem iudicii futuri. Culpa enim naturam infirmam reddit, sed poena aeterna totaliter conterit et in pulverem reducit. Unde Glossa[^p508-14]: « Qui modo contemnit et iniuriis afficit, nondum penitus interit, sed quassatur, ut non ambulet rectus; cadet autem super eum lapis, quem veniens in iudicio opprimet ». Ad hunc ergo lapidem pedem
<!-- page 509 -->
offendunt qui in ipsum non credunt, vel non imitantur; primae Petri secundo[^p509-1]: « Vobis honor credentibus, non credentibus autem lapis, quem reprobaverunt aedificantes, hic factus est in caput anguli, et lapis offensionis et petra scandali his qui offendunt verbo et non credunt, in quo et positi sunt ». Et sic factum est Iudaeis, secundum illud Isaiae octavo[^p509-2]: « Erit vobis Dominus in sanctificationem, in lapidem autem offensionis et petram scandali duabus domibus Israel, in laqueum et in ruinam habitantibus Ierusalem. Et offendent ex eis plurimi et cadent et conterentur et irretientur et capientur ». « Exspectabo Dominum, qui abscondit faciem suam a domo Iacob ». Qui ergo scandalizantur super hunc lapidem cadunt, sed qui condemnantur ab hoc lapide conteruntur, secundum illud Isaiae trigesimo[^p509-3]: « Subito, dum non speratur, veniet contritio eius, et conteritur velut lagena figuli contritione pervalida, et non invenietur de fragmentis eius testa, in qua portetur igniculus de incendio, aut hauriatur parum aquae de fovea ». Propter quod[^p509-4] « non sic impii, non sic, sed tanquam pulvis, quem proiicit ventus a facie terrae ». — In hac igitur auctoritate confirmat Dominus intentionem parabolae, scilicet quod ipse *reprobandus* est a Iudaeis, *electurus gentes, repulsurus Iudaeos* propter suam perfidiam, et tandem *condemnaturus* per sententiam definitivam.

## English
<!-- page 504 -->

14. *And in the time of the vintage*[^p504-10] *he sent* etc. After he has described the kindness of the divine liberality, he describes here *the malignity of the Jewish perversity*[^p504-11]. Now the perversity of the malignity of the Jews is described in two ways: first, namely, *before the coming of Christ;* second, *after the coming of Christ,* there[^p504-12]: *Then the lord of the vineyard said: What shall I do?*

*Before the coming of Christ* it is described in three ways, namely in respect of *the lawgiver,* in respect of *the royal power* and in respect of *the prophetic company*[^p504-13]; and according to three things by which that people could be led by the hand to justice, namely by *the Law, the psalmody* and *the prophecy,* according to *the height* of power, *the sweetness* of goodness and *the certitude* of truth.

Of this sending, Exodus, the third chapter: « Come, I will send thee to Pharao, that thou mayst bring forth my people, the children of Israel, out of Egypt ». — But because the perversity of the Jews did not accept this servant, therefore he subjoins: *Who, beating him, sent
<!-- page 505 -->
him away empty.* The Gloss[^p505-1]: « Beaten, they send him away empty, because *they provoked Moses in the camp, Aaron the holy one of the Lord;* and again: *Moses was afflicted for their sakes, because they exasperated his spirit* ». Whence Deuteronomy, the thirty-first chapter[^p505-2]: « I know thy obstinacy and thy most stiff neck. While I am yet living and going in with you, you have always been rebellious against the Lord; how much more when I shall be dead »? Now this the *husbandmen especially* did, that is, the nobles of the synagogue; Numbers, the sixteenth chapter[^p505-3]: « Behold, Core the son of Isaar, and Dathan and Abiron rose up against Moses, and with them others of the children of Israel, two hundred and fifty men, nobles of the synagogue, and who in the time of assembly were called by name ». And the lesser ones followed these; whence it also follows[^p505-4] after, that « all the multitude of the children of Israel murmured on the following day against Moses and Aaron. And when there arose a sedition and the tumult increased, Moses and Aaron fled to the tabernacle of the covenant ».

16. (Vers. 11.). Second, as to *the perversity in respect of king David,* the chief worshipper of God, he subjoins: *And again he sent another servant;* the Gloss[^p505-5]: « The other servant, David the prophet, who after Moses invited the husbandmen of the vineyard to the exercise of good work by the melody of psalmody and the sweetness of the harp ». Him the Lord sent when he chose him from the pastures to rule and feed his people, according to what the prophet David himself says in the Psalm[^p505-6]: « He chose David his servant, and took him from the flocks of sheep. He brought him from following the ewes great with young, to feed Jacob his servant and Israel his inheritance ». Whence the second of Kings, the seventh chapter: « Thus shalt thou say to my servant David: I took thee out of the pastures from following the flocks, to be ruler over my people Israel; and I have been with thee wheresoever thou hast walked, and I have made thee a great name, like unto the name of the great ones that are on the earth »; whence also to him, namely David, he gave a royal seat for ever; to the same he gave the spirit of prophecy, to the same he gave the song of psalmody, that so he might bend that hard people[^p505-7]. — And yet he could not soften the hardness of the Jews, on account of which he adds: *But they beat him also, and treating him reproachfully, sent him away empty,* because they despised his rule and leading. Whence the Gloss[^p505-8]: « Treated reproachfully, they send him away empty, saying: *What portion have we in David, or what inheritance in the son of Isai* »? the second of Kings, the twentieth chapter. Where it is also set down before how they persecuted David with Absalom; the second of Kings, the fifteenth chapter[^p505-9]: « There came a messenger to David, saying: All Israel with their whole heart followeth Absalom. And David said to his servants: Arise, and let us flee; for we shall not escape else from the face of Absalom ». And after, in the sixteenth chapter, it is said of the reproaches brought upon him: « And thus said Semei, when he cursed the king: Come out, come out, thou man of blood and thou man of Belial. The Lord hath repaid thee for all the blood of the house of Saul; and behold, thy evils press upon thee, because thou art a man of blood ».

17. (Vers. 12.). Third, as to *the perversity of that people in respect of the prophetic company* it is subjoined: *And again he sent the third;* the Gloss[^p505-10]: « The third servant is the choir of the Prophets, who corrected the people and foretold the evils which hung over the vineyard ». Of the sending of this servant it is said in Jeremias, the seventh chapter[^p505-11]: « I have sent to you all my servants the Prophets by day, rising up early and sending. And they hearkened not to me, nor inclined their ear, but hardened their neck and have done worse than their fathers ». — Whence he also adds: *And they wounded him also, and cast him out.* For they sawed Isaias asunder, stoned Jeremias, and dashed out the brains of Ezechiel. Therefore in Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p505-12]: « Jerusalem, Jerusalem, that killest the Prophets and stonest them that are sent unto thee »; and in Acts, the seventh chapter, Stephen said to the Jews: « You stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, you always resist the Holy Spirit; as your fathers did, so do you also. Which of the Prophets have not your fathers persecuted? And they have slain them who foretold of the coming of the Just One ». Therefore the Prophets themselves, seeing its hardness, both reproved and deplored it. For it is said in Isaias, the fifth chapter[^p505-13], of the vineyard: « I looked that it should bring forth grapes, and it hath brought forth wild grapes »; and in Jeremias, the second chapter: « How then art thou turned unto me into that which is good for nothing, O strange vineyard »? and in Ezechiel, the nineteenth chapter: « Thy mother is like a vine in thy blood, planted by the water ». « And she was plucked up in wrath and cast on the ground, and the burning wind dried up her fruit ». Now in this the perfidy of the Jews appears wonderful, because all these whom they persecuted loved them most. For Moses did not cease to pray for the people; Exodus, the thirty-second chapter[^p505-14]: « Why, O Lord, is thy indignation enkindled against thy people, whom thou hast brought out of Egypt »? « Either forgive
<!-- page 506 -->
them this trespass » etc. Likewise also David[^p506-1]: « O Lord God of hosts, turn again; look down from heaven, and see, and visit this vineyard ». Likewise the choir of the Prophets, whence Jeremias, the eighteenth chapter: « Remember that I have stood in thy sight, to speak good for them and to turn away the wrath of thy indignation from them ». And all these bore the figure of Christ, of whom Isaias, the fifty-third chapter[^p506-2]: « He hath borne the sins of many and hath prayed for the transgressors ».

18. (Vers. 13.). *Then the lord of the vineyard said* etc. After describing the perversity of the Jews before the coming of Christ toward the persons of the servants, here he describes *the malignity of the perversity of the same after the coming, against the very Son of God, Jesus Christ*[^p506-3]. About the description of which three things are brought in, namely *the gratuitous benefit of God, the perverse purpose of the Jews* and *their shameful deed.*

First, as to *the gratuitous benefit of God* he sets down first: *Then the lord of the vineyard said: What shall I do? I will send my beloved son;* this was the greatest benefit, according to that of the first of John, the fourth chapter[^p506-4]: « By this hath the charity of God appeared towards us, because God hath sent his only begotten Son into the world, that we may live by him »; and John, the third chapter: « God so loved the world, as to give his only begotten Son ». Now this sending was from the hidden and most secret purpose and counsel of God[^p506-5]. — And since he had purposed this by a determined counsel, therefore he says: *What shall I do?* On account of which Moses in Exodus, the fourth chapter[^p506-6], said: « I beseech thee, Lord, send whom thou wilt send »; but the Lord reserved to send him at the end, both on account of the consummation of the order, according to that to the Galatians, the fourth chapter[^p506-7]: « When we were children, we were serving under the elements of the world. But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent his Son »; and also that it might appear that by no other could help be brought to human perfidy and obduracy. — On account of which he adds: *It may be, when they see him, they will reverence him.* *It may be* does not mark beforehand a doubt in God, who most certainly foresees all things, but the liberty of choice, which is turnable to either side. Whence the Gloss[^p506-8]: « That he hesitates, that is, uses a word of doubt, is not the ignorance of God, but that free will may be reserved to man ». Whence that the Jews did not reverence Christ was not a defect on the part of God, but on the part of the hardness of the human heart; Isaias, the fifth chapter[^p506-9]: « What is there that I ought to do more to my vineyard, that I have not done to it »? For those who do not reverence the Son of God will never more reverence God nor his messenger, because, John, the fifth chapter, « The Father hath given all judgement to the Son, that all men may honour the Son as they honour the Father; he who honoureth not the Son honoureth not the Father, who hath sent him ».

19. (Vers. 14.). Second, as to *the perverse purpose* it is subjoined: *Whom when the husbandmen saw, they thought within themselves, saying: This is the heir,* by a conjecturing of the truth; for they did not know it by certain knowledge, as is said in the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p506-10]: « Which none of the princes of this world knew. For if they had known it, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory ». Yet they knew, though not by faith, through certain signs leading them by the hand to believe, that he was the Messias promised in the Law. Therefore they conspired against Christ not out of ignorance, but out of certain malice. Whence the Gloss[^p506-11]: « He plainly proves that the Jews crucified the Son of God not through ignorance, but through envy. For they understood that he was this one to whom it was said: *Ask of me, and I will give thee the Gentiles for thy inheritance* ». That, then, which is said in Acts, the third chapter[^p506-12]: « Now, brethren, I know that you did it through ignorance, as did also your rulers », does not exclude from the Jews every sort of knowledge, but certain knowledge; or it is said as to the lesser ones, who sinned out of ignorance. For the greater ones did this out of certain malice, because they feared to lose their temporal glory. — Therefore he adds: *Let us kill him*[^p506-13]*, that the inheritance may be ours,* by the machination of perversity; which they said not that they might acquire a new inheritance, since Christ was supremely poor, but that they might keep the one they had. Whence John, the eleventh chapter[^p506-14]: « The chief priests and the Pharisees gathered a council, and said: For this man doth many signs. If we let him alone so, all
<!-- page 507 -->
will believe in him, and the Romans will come and take away our place and nation ». Whence this wicked purpose drew its origin from *avarice,* drew its rise also from *envy;* whence Wisdom, the second chapter[^p507-1]: « Let us lie in wait for the just, because he is contrary to our doings, and upbraideth us with transgressions of the law, and divulgeth against us the sins of our way of life »; and below: « Let us condemn him to a most shameful death. These things they thought and were deceived, for their own malice blinded them »; since « by the envy of the devil death came into the world ». The figure of this went before in Joseph; Genesis, the thirty-seventh chapter[^p507-2]: « His brethren thought to kill him, and said one to another: Behold, the dreamer cometh; come, let us kill him and cast him into some old pit, and we will say: A most evil wild beast hath devoured him » etc. — Therefore this Gospel is read together with the history of Joseph as the passion draws near[^p507-3].

20. (Vers. 15.). Third, as to *the shameful deed* it is subjoined: *And casting him out of the vineyard, they killed him,* that is, outside Jerusalem, because it is said to the Hebrews, the last chapter[^p507-4]: « Christ suffered without the gate. Let us go forth therefore to him without the camp, bearing his reproach ». And they *killed* him when they delivered him up to Pilate to be killed and asked that he should be crucified; whence Acts, the third chapter[^p507-5]: « Jesus, the Son of God, you delivered up and denied before the face of Pilate, when he judged he should be released. But you desired a murderer to be granted unto you, but the author of life you killed ». And so was fulfilled that of Isaias, the fifth chapter, concerning the vineyard planted[^p507-6]: « I looked that it should do judgement, and behold, iniquity; and justice, and behold, a cry »; *a cry,* namely, for killing Christ, of which in Mark, the fifteenth chapter: « They cried out: Crucify him. And Pilate saith to them: Why, what evil hath he done? But they cried out the more: Crucify him ». Whence from this vineyard there came out not wine but gall, according to that of Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter[^p507-7]: « Their vineyard is of the vineyard of Sodom and of the suburbs of Gomorrha; their grape is a grape of gall, and their cluster most bitter. Their wine is the gall of dragons and the venom of asps, which is incurable ».

21. (Vers. 15. 16.). *What therefore will he do to them* etc. After he has described in parable the kindness of the divine liberality and the perversity of the Jewish malignity, here now third[^p507-8] he describes *the severity of the judicial vengeance.* About which three things are intimated, namely *the severity of the divine vengeance, the unbelief of the Jewish rebellion, the firmness of the attestation of Scripture.*

First, then, as to *the severity of the divine vengeance* he says: *What therefore will the lord of the vineyard do to them?* This he asks not as doubting, but to rouse them to the intelligence of the sentence[^p507-9], as to which he subjoins: *He will come and will destroy these husbandmen, and will give the vineyard to others;* in which is given the sentence of the reprobation of the Jews and of the election of the Gentiles; and this under the figure of the vineyard and the vinedressers, of which Isaias, the fifth chapter[^p507-10]: « And now I will shew you what I will do to my vineyard: I will take away the hedge thereof, and it shall be wasted; I will break down the wall thereof, and it shall be trodden down. And I will make it desolate; it shall not be pruned and it shall not be digged, but briers and thorns shall come up; and I will command the clouds to rain no rain upon it ». This *reprobation* the Prophet bewails in the Psalm[^p507-11], saying: « Why hast thou broken down the wall thereof, so that all they who pass by the way do pluck it? The boar out of the wood hath laid it waste, and a singular wild beast hath devoured it ». And of *the election of the Gentiles* it is said to the Romans, the eleventh chapter[^p507-12]: « Blindness in part has happened in Israel, that the fulness of the Gentiles should come in »; and of this to the Romans, the ninth chapter: « I will call that which was not my people, my people; and her that had not obtained mercy, one that hath obtained mercy »; which is taken from Osee, the second chapter[^p507-13]. And of this Matthew, the twenty-first chapter: « The kingdom of God shall be taken from you and shall be given to a nation yielding the fruits thereof ».

22. Second, as to *the unbelief of the Jewish rebellion* he subjoins: *Which they hearing, said to him: God forbid,* as if they should say: it will not come about so, as thou hast said. But the contrary of this seems in Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p507-14], where it is said thus: « When the lord of the vineyard shall come, what will he do to those husbandmen? They say to him: He will bring those evil men to an evil end, and will let out his vineyard to other husbandmen, who shall render him the fruit in due season ». But here it is said that they contradicted. — But this apparent contrariety Augustine resolves in the book *On the Agreement of the Evangelists*[^p507-15], where he says thus: « We understand
<!-- page 508 -->
that some answered what Matthew records; but some, what Luke records ». — Otherwise Chrysostom answers[^p508-1] that « they said *one thing* with the heart, namely what Matthew says; *another* with the mouth, namely what Luke says. Whence the conscience was consonant with the divine sentence and the parable, but the tongue was contrary ». Whence the Gloss[^p508-2]: « They contradict the sentence which they know to have been spoken against their perfidy; for they understood that what they bore in mind was contained in the parable ». And from this the wonderful judgement of God appears, that by contradicting they wished to lie hidden, and yet they laid themselves bare while they set themselves against the truth, against that of Ecclesiasticus, the fourth chapter[^p508-3]: « Contradict not the word of truth in any wise, and of a lie » etc.

23. (Vers. 17.). Third, as to *the firmness of the attestation of Scripture* he subjoins: *But he looking*[^p508-4] *on them, said: What is this then that is written: The stone which the builders rejected, the same is become the head of the corner?* This is written in the Psalm[^p508-5], where it is added at once: « This is the Lord's doing » etc. Now in this is shown *the wonderful humility of Christ,* who willed to be rejected; on account of which Isaias wonders, the fifty-third chapter[^p508-6]: Lord, « who hath believed our report? And to whom is the arm of the Lord revealed? And he shall grow up as a tender plant before him, and as a root out of a thirsty ground ». « And we have thought him as it were a leper, and as one struck by God and afflicted ». In this is shown also *the wonderful power of Christ,* that he brought things most disjoined to unity, as a corner stone. — Now Christ is called *a stone* on account of the permanence of his stability, of which Daniel, the second chapter[^p508-7]: « After this thou sawest, till a stone was cut out of a mountain without hands, and it struck the statue ». « But the stone that struck the statue became a great mountain and filled the whole earth ». — And he is called *a corner stone* on account of the power of joining together; to the Ephesians, the second chapter[^p508-8]: « He is our peace, who hath made both one, and breaking down the middle wall of partition »; « that he might make the two in himself into one new man, making peace ». Of this Job, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p508-9]: « Where wast thou when I laid up the foundations of the earth? Who hath laid the measures thereof, or who laid the corner stone thereof »? This corner stone of the earth is the uniting stone of the Church, of which Isaias, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p508-10]: « Behold, I will lay a stone in the foundations of Sion, a tried stone, a corner stone, a precious stone, founded in the foundation ». — He is called also *the head of the corner* on account of the summit of perfection; to the Ephesians, the second chapter[^p508-11]: « Christ himself being the chief corner stone, in whom all the building, being framed together, groweth up into a holy temple in the Lord » etc. And therefore the first of Peter, the second chapter: « Unto whom coming, as to a living stone, rejected indeed by men ». « Be you also as living stones » etc. — Christ, then, is to be imagined as the central stone[^p508-12] in the whole body of the Church, which is compared to the circumference of a circle; to which indeed all lines drawn make an angle and are united in him as in the point of indivisibility, in which is made the kiss of the lines of the cross running together into one in its midst. This stone, then, is establishing the elect, and on the contrary is crushing the adversaries and the reprobate.

24. (Vers. 18.). And therefore he subjoins: *Whosoever shall fall upon that stone shall be bruised,* through the corruption of sin; *and upon whomsoever it shall fall, it will grind him to powder*[^p508-13], through the vengeance of the judgement to come. For fault renders nature weak, but eternal punishment crushes it totally and reduces it to dust. Whence the Gloss[^p508-14]: « He who now despises and does him injuries does not yet utterly perish, but is bruised, so that he does not walk upright; but the stone will fall upon him, which coming in judgement will overwhelm him ». Against this stone, then,
<!-- page 509 -->
they strike their foot who do not believe in him, or do not imitate him; the first of Peter, the second chapter[^p509-1]: « To you therefore that believe, honour; but to them that believe not, the stone which the builders rejected, the same is made the head of the corner, and a stone of stumbling and a rock of scandal to them who stumble at the word, neither do believe, whereunto also they are set ». And so it was done to the Jews, according to that of Isaias, the eighth chapter[^p509-2]: « The Lord shall be a sanctification unto you, but for a stone of stumbling and for a rock of scandal to the two houses of Israel, for a snare and a ruin to the inhabitants of Jerusalem. And very many of them shall stumble and fall, and shall be broken in pieces, and shall be snared and taken ». « And I will wait for the Lord, who hath hid his face from the house of Jacob ». Those, then, who are scandalized upon this stone fall, but those who are condemned by this stone are crushed, according to that of Isaias, the thirtieth chapter[^p509-3]: « Suddenly, when it is not looked for, the breaking thereof shall come, and it shall be broken small as the potter's vessel is broken all to pieces with mighty breaking, and there shall not a sherd be found of the pieces thereof, wherein a little fire may be carried from the hearth, or a little water be drawn out of the pit ». On account of which[^p509-4] « not so the wicked, not so; but like the dust which the wind driveth from the face of the earth ». — In this authority, then, the Lord confirms the intention of the parable, namely that he himself is *to be rejected* by the Jews, *is to choose the Gentiles, to cast off the Jews* on account of their perfidy, and at length *to condemn* them by a definitive sentence.
