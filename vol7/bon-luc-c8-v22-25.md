# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. VIII, Vers. 22-25
## *Tertio ponitur confirmatio authentica in manifestis miraculis*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 198 -->

#### Tertio ponitur confirmatio authentica in manifestis miraculis.

34. *Factum est*[^p198-7] *in una dierum, et ipse ascendit* etc. Post instructionem parabolicam in occultis mysteriis et informationem apostolicam in apertis eloquiis subdit hic Evangelista more suo *confirmationem authenticam in expressis miraculis.* Introducuntur autem in hac parte tria signa miraculorum, « ut in ore trium testium stet omne verbum[^p198-8] », et ut ostendatur Christus dominus universorum. Primum est in *sedatione tempestatis,* ex quo ostenditur dominus super creaturam corporalem; secundum est in *expulsione daemoniacae legionis,* ex quo ostenditur dominus super spiritualem, ibi[^p198-9]: *Et navigaverunt ad regionem Gerasenorum* etc.; tertium est in *restitutione vitae et sanitatis,* ex quo ostenditur dominus super composita ex utraque, ibi: *Factum est autem, cum rediisset* etc.

Circa primum miraculi genus, quod est in *sedatione tempestatis,* quatuor introducuntur ab Evangelista ad eius explicationem perfectam. Primum est *occasio praecedens;* secundum, *opportunitas imminens;* tertium, *potestas efficiens;* quartum, *utilitas consequens. Occasio* autem praecedens fuit transitus maris; *opportunitas* imminens, impetus tempestatis; *potestas* efficiens, virtus Divinitatis; *utilitas* consequens est profectus nostrae credulitatis. — Primo ergo introducitur ab Evangelista *transitus maris;* secundo, *impetus tempestatis,* ibi[^p198-10]: *Navigantibus autem illis* etc.; tertio vero, *virtus Divinitatis,* ibi: *At ille surgens increpavit* etc.; quarto, *profectus nostrae credulitatis,* ibi: *Dixit autem illis: Ubi est fides?*

Circa primum notandum, quod duo introducit Evangelista: primum est *ascensus naviculae*[^p198-11]; secundum est *transitus stagni.*

35. (Vers. 22.). Primo ergo quantum ad *ascensum naviculae* dicitur: *Factum est autem in una dierum, et ipse ascendit in naviculam et discipuli*
<!-- page 199 -->
*eius,* ut scilicet per eius obsequium veheretur, iuxta illud Sapientiae decimo quarto[^p199-1]: « Exiguo ligno credunt homines animas suas ». Et nota, quod *Christus* ascendit cum discipulis in naviculam, ut ostendat se eis similem in natura et indigentia, secundum illud ad Hebraeos secundo[^p199-2]: « Per omnia debuit fratribus similari »; et ad Philippenses secundo: « In similitudinem hominum factus et habitu inventus ut homo ». *Discipuli* ascenderunt cum Christo, ut ostendatur perfectio comitatus, quo nunquam deserebant Magistrum, sicut Eliseus fecit magistro suo Eliae, quarti Regum secundo[^p199-3]: « Vivit Dominus, et vivit anima tua, quia non derelinquam te ». Unde in persona talium dicebat Iob vigesimo tertio: « Vestigia eius secutus est pes meus, viam eius custodivi nec declinavi ex ea ». Non erant de illis « qui ad tempus credunt et in tempore tentationis recedunt », supra eodem, et ideo ad veritatis sapientiam nunquam pertingunt.

36. Secundo vero quantum ad *transitum stagni* subditur: *Et ait ad illos: Transfretemus trans stagnum. Et ascenderunt,* scilicet ut transfretarent et ultra fretum sive mare irent, quia aqua illa, sicut habitum est supra quinto[^p199-4], et *mare* et *stagnum* appellabatur. Ideo autem voluit Dominus, quod discipuli eius transfretarent cum eo, ut miracula eius eis apparerent; Psalmus[^p199-5]: « Qui descendunt mare in navibus, ipsi viderunt opera Domini et mirabilia eius in profundo ». Vel ideo voluit, ut secum ascenderent, ut ostenderet, quod non possunt pervenire ad portum salutis nisi per mare tribulationis praesentis. Unde Sapientiae decimo quarto[^p199-6]: « Transeuntes mare per ratem liberati sunt »; et in Psalmo: « Transivimus per ignem et aquam »; quia, secundum quod dicitur Actuum decimo quarto, « per multas tribulationes oportet nos intrare in regnum Dei ».

37. (Vers. 23.). *Navigantibus autem illis*[^p199-7] etc. Hic secundo describitur *impetus tempestatis* cum *importunitate periculi* et cum *opportunitate refugii.* — Primo quantum ad *importunitatem periculi* emergentis, Christo *dormiente,* dicitur: *Et navigantibus illis, obdormivit.* Et ipso dormiente, periculum incurrerunt in navigando, iuxta illud Ecclesiastici quadragesimo tertio[^p199-8]: « Qui navigant mare enarrant pericula eius ». — Ideo subditur periculum: *Et descendit procella venti in stagnum, et complebantur et periclitabantur,* id est, procella venti exagitabat stagnum et implebat navem aquis, iuxta illud Marci quarto[^p199-9]: « Et facta est procella magna venti et fluctus mittebat in navim, ita ut impleretur navis ». Et hoc factum est divina dispensatione, sicut de Iona dicitur Ionae primo: « Facta est tempestas magna in mari, et navis periclitabatur conteri ». Unde sicut illa navis propter Ionam fugientem a facie Domini erat in periculo, sic et ista propter praesentiam proditoris, secundum quod Glossa[^p199-10] dicit: « Merito turbatur navis, in qua proditor est cum bonis, et qui suis meritis firmi fuerant turbantur alienis ».

38. (Vers. 24.). Secundo quantum ad *opportunitatem refugii* succurrentis, Christo *evigilante,* subditur: *Accedentes autem suscitaverunt eum,* ut scilicet per eius vigiliam obtinerent remedium qui in dormitione incurrerant periculum; Psalmus[^p199-11]: « Excitatus est tanquam dormiens Dominus ». Suscitaverunt autem eum non ex animi audacia, sed ex indigentia; unde additur: *Dicentes: Praeceptor, perimus;* in quo allegant *Christi potentiam* et *suam indigentiam: Christi potentiam* in hoc, quod aiunt: *Praeceptor;* Iob nono[^p199-12]: « Praecipit soli, et non oritur, et stellas claudit quasi sub signaculo »; Psalmus: « Tu dominaris potestati maris, motum autem fluctuum eius tu mitigas »; Sapientiae decimo quarto: « Dedisti in mari viam et in fluctibus semitam firmissimam ». — *Suam indigentiam* allegant in hoc, quod dicunt: *Perimus,* cum tamen tu nolis, aliquem perire; secundae Petri tertio[^p199-13]: « Patienter agit nolens, aliquos perire », et maxime praedestinatos. Unde Ioannis decimo: « Oves meae vocem meam audiunt, et ego vitam aeternam do eis, et non peribunt in aeternum »; et decimo septimo: « Ego custodivi eos, et nemo ex eis periit nisi filius perditionis ».

39. *At ille surgens increpavit ventum.* Hic tertio describitur *virtus Divinitatis,* ut *praecipiens verbo* et ut *perficiens facto.* — Primo igitur quantum ad *praeceptum in verbo* dicitur: *At ille surgens increpavit ventum et tempestatem aquae;* in qua increpatione intelligitur maiestatis imperium super utrumque. Unde dicitur Matthaei octavo[^p199-14]: « Imperavit ventis et mari, et facta est tranquillitas magna »; Marci quarto dicitur, quod dixit mari: « Tace,
<!-- page 200 -->
obmutesce ». Et in hoc ostendit, se esse illum, qui fecit mare; Iob trigesimo octavo[^p200-1]: « Circumdedi mare terminis meis; et dixi: Hucusque venies et hic confringes tumentes fluctus tuos »; et Nahum primo: « Increpans mare et exsiccans illud ».

40. Secundo vero quantum ad *effectum in opere* subditur: *Et cessavit, et facta est tranquillitas; cessavit,* scilicet ventus; Ecclesiastici quadragesimo tertio[^p200-2]: « In sermone eius siluit ventus »; quia in Psalmo: « Ignis, grando, nix, glacies, spiritus procellarum, quae faciunt verbum eius ». Et sic apparet, quod ipse est ille, ad quem dicit Tobias, tertio[^p200-3], « Post tempestatem tranquillum facis ». Et sic verum est in Christo quod in Psalmo dicitur: « Et stetit spiritus procellae ».

41. (Vers. 25.). *Dixit autem illis* etc. Hic quarto determinatur *profectus nostrae credulitatis* dupliciter, scilicet quantum ad *cognitionem in corde* et quantum ad *confessionem in ore*[^p200-4]. — Primo ergo quantum ad *cognitionem cordis* procedentis ab haesitatione dubietatis ad certitudinem credulitatis dicitur: *Dixit autem illis: Ubi est fides vestra?* Quod quidem dicit increpando dubietatem et suadendo fidei firmitatem, quasi dicat: Quare dubitastis mergi et periclitari? Iuxta quod dixit Petro Matthaei decimo quarto[^p200-5]: « Modicae fidei, quare dubitasti »? Nam dicitur ad Hebraeos undecimo: « Fide filii Israel transierunt mare rubrum tanquam per aridam terram ». Ideo autem discipulos arguit de pusillanimitate, ut excitet ex viso miraculo ad fidei firmitatem, per quam resiliant in propriam parvitatem et admirentur immensitatem divinam. — Unde et subditur: *Qui timentes mirati sunt.* Iam enim cognoscebant suam parvitatem, in quam resiliebant per timorem, et divinam immensitatem, in quam excedebant per admirationem; Ieremiae decimo[^p200-6]: « Quis non timebit te, o rex gentium? Tuum enim est decus inter cunctos sapientes »; Ieremiae quinto: « Me ergo non timebitis? dicit Dominus ». « Qui posui arenam terminum mari, praeceptum sempiternum, quod non praeteribit ». — Et quia timorem maiestatis comitatur admiratio potestatis, recte dicitur: *Mirati sunt;* Ecclesiastici undecimo[^p200-7]: « Mirati sunt in illo multi et honoraverunt Deum », quia « magnus Dominus vehementer, et admirabilis potentia eius », Ecclesiastici quadragesimo tertio.

42. Secundo vero quantum ad *confessionem oris* subditur: *Ad invicem dicentes: Quis, putas, hic est?* quasi dicant: nullus est huic similis; Exodi decimo quinto[^p200-8]: « Quis similis tui magnus in fortibus, Domine? Quis similis tui magnificus in sanctitate, terribilis atque laudabilis, faciens mirabilia »? — Et rationem reddunt: *Quia et ventis et mari imperat,* per potentiam ineffabilem; *et obediunt ei,* per reverentiam admirabilem; Iob vigesimo octavo[^p200-9]: « Qui posuit ventis pondus et aquas appendit in mensura, quando ponebat pluviis legem et viam procellis sonantibus ». Et sic verificatum est illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo nono: « In verbo eius stetit aqua sicut congeries ». Iste ergo est ille, de quo Iob trigesimo octavo[^p200-10]: « Quis conclusit ostiis mare »? Ex quo enim venti et mare obediunt ei, nulla resistere possunt; Ecclesiastici quadragesimo secundo: « Omnia in necessitate obaudiunt ei ». Unde Beda in Glossa[^p200-11]: « Nota, quod omnis creatura sentit Creatorem, quia maiestati Conditoris sunt sensibilia, quae apud nos sunt insensibilia ».

43. *Spiritualiter* autem exprimitur hic liberatio animae poenitentis a tentatione diaboli per gratiam Christi. Unde tria hic spiritualiter sunt attendenda: primum est *animae conversio,* quae designatur in ascensu naviculae; secundum est *tentatio,* quae designatur in impetu procellae; tertium est *animae consolatio,* quae designatur in sedatione tempestatis maximae.

Circa *ascensum naviculae* designantem *conversionem animae* tria attendenda sunt, scilicet Christi *exemplum,* quia prior ascendit; Michaeae secundo[^p200-12]: « Ascendet pandens iter ante eos »; quia, Actuum primo, « coepit Iesus facere et docere ». Et hoc est quod dicit Ambrosius: « Christus, relictis parentibus, navem crucis ascendit », ut doceat nos, relicto amore
<!-- page 201 -->
carnali, cum ipso crucifigi. In figura huius primi Machabaeorum decimo sexto[^p201-1] dicitur, quod Ioannes « vidit populum trepidantem ad transfretandum torrentem et transfretavit ad eos primus, et viderunt eum viri et transierunt post eum ».

Secundo, Christi *consilium,* quia discipulos invitavit, ut secum transirent; Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto[^p201-2]: « Transite ad me, omnes, qui concupiscitis me », scilicet ex hoc mundo, qui non est nisi transitus quidam; Ioannis decimo tertio: « Ut transeat ex hoc mundo ad Patrem »; et ad Hebraeos decimo tertio: « Non habemus hic manentem civitatem, sed futuram inquirimus ». Beatus, qui sic transit cum Christo « per bona temporalia, ut non amittat aeterna[^p201-3] »; quia, Sapientiae decimo quarto, « transeuntes mare per ratem liberati sunt ».

Tertio vero, Christi *consortium,* quia simul cum ipsis in navi transivit; Isaiae quadragesimo tertio[^p201-4]: « Cum transieris per aquas, tecum ero, et flumina non operient te » etc., quia in Psalmo: « Cum ipso sum in tribulatione »; et Matthaei vigesimo octavo: « Ecce, ego vobiscum sum omnibus diebus usque ad consummationem saeculi ».

44. Circa *impetum procellae,* in quo designatur *tentatio animae,* tria sunt attendenda. Primum est *obdormitio Christi;* quod quidem est, quando Christi amor et memoria torpescit in corde; nam in se non obdormivit; Psalmus[^p201-5]: « Ecce, non dormitabit neque dormiet qui custodit Israel »; sed in nobis; propter quod dicitur Matthaei vigesimo sexto: « Vigilate et orate, ut non intretis in tentationem »; et ideo primae Petri ultimo: « Sobrii estote et vigilate »; et primae ad Thessalonicenses quinto: « Igitur non dormiamus sicut ceteri, sed vigilemus et sobrii simus; qui enim dormiunt nocte dormiunt ».

Secundum est *descensus procellae venti,* in quo designatur *tentatio diaboli;* Ezechielis primo[^p201-6]: « Ventus turbinis veniebat ab aquilone ». Hic est ventus veniens « a regione deserti, qui concutit quatuor angulos domus », id est quatuor mentis affectus, de quo dicitur Iob primo; hic est, de quo Ecclesiastici quadragesimo tertio: « Frigidus ventus aquilo flavit, et gelavit crystallus ab aqua ».

Tertium est *periclitatio navis impulsae,* quae designat *perturbationem animae.* Aqua enim ingrediens in naviculam est perturbatio intrans in animam, et tunc imminet periculum. Unde in Psalmo[^p201-7]: « Salvum me fac, Deus, quoniam intraverunt aquae usque ad animam meam »; Threnorum tertio: « Inundaverunt aquae super caput meum; dixi: perii ».

45. Circa *consolationem animae* tria sunt attendenda. Primum est *oratio in Christi excitatione;* quod quidem fit per orationem; infra undecimo[^p201-8]: « Quis vestrum habebit amicum » etc.; Isaiae sexagesimo quarto: « Non est qui invocet nomen tuum, qui surgat et teneat te ».

Secundum est *mentis devotio in tranquillitate;* Tobiae tertio[^p201-9]: « Post tempestatem tranquillum facis et post lacrymationem et fletum exsultationem infundis »; Sapientiae duodecimo: « Tu autem, dominator virtutis, cum tranquillitate iudicas ».

Tertium est *Dei honorificatio* in hoc, quod Deum venerantur et timent et confitentur; Exodi decimo quarto[^p201-10]: « Liberavit Dominus Israel de manu Aegyptiorum ». « Tunc cecinit Moyses canticum Domino » etc. — De his tribus dicitur in Psalmo: « Invoca me in die tribulationis, et eruam te, et honorificabis me ». — Et sic patet, quomodo spiritualiter hic designatur transitus animae poenitentis.

## English

#### Thirdly, the authentic confirmation in manifest miracles is set forth.

34. *It came to pass*[^p198-7] *on a certain day, that he went up* etc. After the parabolic instruction in hidden mysteries and the apostolic formation in open utterances, the Evangelist, after his manner, here subjoins *the authentic confirmation in express miracles.* Now in this part three signs of miracles are brought in, « that in the mouth of three witnesses every word may stand[^p198-8] », and that Christ may be shown to be the lord of all things. The first is in *the calming of the tempest,* from which he is shown to be lord over the bodily creature; the second is in *the expulsion of the demoniac legion,* from which he is shown to be lord over the spiritual, there[^p198-9]: *And they sailed to the country of the Gerasens* etc.; the third is in *the restoration of life and health,* from which he is shown to be lord over things composed of both, there: *And it came to pass, when he was returned* etc.

Concerning the first kind of miracle, which is in *the calming of the tempest,* four things are brought in by the Evangelist for its perfect explanation. The first is *the preceding occasion;* the second, *the impending opportuneness;* the third, *the efficient power;* the fourth, *the consequent usefulness. The occasion* preceding was the crossing of the sea; *the opportuneness* impending, the onset of the tempest; *the power* efficient, the power of the Godhead; *the usefulness* consequent is the progress of our belief. — First, then, there is brought in by the Evangelist *the crossing of the sea;* secondly, *the onset of the tempest,* there[^p198-10]: *Now when they were sailing* etc.; thirdly, *the power of the Godhead,* there: *But he arising, rebuked* etc.; fourthly, *the progress of our belief,* there: *And he said to them: Where is faith?*

Concerning the first it is to be noted that the Evangelist brings in two things: the first is *the going up into the little ship*[^p198-11]; the second is *the crossing of the lake.*

35. (Vers. 22.). First, then, as to *the going up into the little ship,* it is said: *And it came to pass on a certain day, that he went up into a little ship, and his*
<!-- page 199 -->
*disciples,* namely that he might be carried by its service, according to that of Wisdom, the fourteenth chapter[^p199-1]: « Men commit their lives even to a little wood ». And note that *Christ* goes up with the disciples into the little ship, that he may show himself like to them in nature and in need, according to that to the Hebrews, the second chapter[^p199-2]: « In all things he ought to be made like unto his brethren »; and to the Philippians, the second chapter: « Being made in the likeness of men, and in habit found as a man ». *The disciples* went up with Christ, that there may be shown the perfection of their company, by which they never forsook their Master, as Eliseus did to his master Elias, the fourth of Kings, the second chapter[^p199-3]: « As the Lord liveth, and as thy soul liveth, I will not leave thee ». Whence in the person of such Job said, the twenty-third chapter: « My foot hath followed his steps, I have kept his way, and have not declined from it ». They were not of those « who believe for a while, and in time of temptation fall away », above, in the same chapter, and therefore never attain to the wisdom of truth.

36. Secondly, as to *the crossing of the lake,* it is subjoined: *And he said to them: Let us go over to the other side of the lake. And they went up,* namely that they might cross the strait and go beyond the strait or sea, because that water, as was had above, the fifth chapter[^p199-4], was called both *sea* and *lake.* Now the Lord willed that his disciples should cross over with him for this, that his miracles might appear to them; the Psalm[^p199-5]: « They that go down to the sea in ships, these have seen the works of the Lord and his wonders in the deep ». Or he willed that they should go up with him for this, that he might show that they cannot come to the haven of salvation save through the sea of present tribulation. Whence Wisdom, the fourteenth chapter[^p199-6]: « Passing over the sea by ship they are saved »; and in the Psalm: « We have passed through fire and water »; because, according to what is said in the Acts, the fourteenth chapter, « through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God ».

37. (Vers. 23.). *Now when they were sailing*[^p199-7] etc. Here secondly is described *the onset of the tempest,* with *the importunity of the danger* and with *the opportuneness of the refuge.* — First, as to *the importunity of the danger* arising, Christ *sleeping,* it is said: *And when they were sailing, he slept.* And while he slept, they incurred danger in sailing, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the forty-third chapter[^p199-8]: « They that sail on the sea tell the dangers thereof ». — Therefore the danger is subjoined: *And there came down a storm of wind upon the lake, and they were filled, and were in danger,* that is, the storm of wind was tossing the lake and filling the ship with waters, according to that of Mark, the fourth chapter[^p199-9]: « And there arose a great storm of wind, and the waves beat into the ship, so that the ship was filled ». And this was done by divine dispensation, as is said of Jonas, Jonas, the first chapter: « There was a great tempest in the sea, and the ship was in danger to be broken ». Whence, as that ship was in danger on account of Jonas fleeing from the face of the Lord, so this one too on account of the presence of the traitor, according to what the Gloss[^p199-10] says: « Rightly is the ship troubled in which the traitor is with the good, and those who had been firm by their own merits are troubled by another's ».

38. (Vers. 24.). Secondly, as to *the opportuneness of the refuge* coming to their aid, Christ *awaking,* it is subjoined: *And they came and awaked him,* namely that through his waking they might obtain a remedy who in his sleeping had incurred danger; the Psalm[^p199-11]: « And the Lord was awaked as one sleeping ». Now they awaked him not out of boldness of mind, but out of need; whence it is added: *Saying: Master, we perish;*[^tr-praeceptor] in which they allege *Christ's power* and *their own need: Christ's power* in this, that they say: *Master;* Job, the ninth chapter[^p199-12]: « Who commandeth the sun, and it riseth not, and shutteth up the stars as it were under a seal »; the Psalm: « Thou rulest the power of the sea, and appeasest the motion of the waves thereof »; Wisdom, the fourteenth chapter: « Thou hast given a way in the sea, and a most sure path among the waves ». — *Their own need* they allege in this, that they say: *We perish,* although thou willest not that any should perish; the second of Peter, the third chapter[^p199-13]: « He dealeth patiently, not willing that any should perish », and most of all the predestined. Whence John, the tenth chapter: « My sheep hear my voice, and I give them life everlasting, and they shall not perish for ever »; and the seventeenth chapter: « I have kept them, and none of them hath perished but the son of perdition ».

39. *But he arising, rebuked the wind.* Here thirdly is described *the power of the Godhead,* as *enjoining by word* and as *accomplishing by deed.* — First, then, as to *the precept in word* it is said: *But he arising, rebuked the wind and the rage of the water;* in which rebuke is understood the command of majesty over both. Whence it is said in Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p199-14]: « He commanded the winds and the sea, and there came a great calm »; in Mark, the fourth chapter, it is said that he said to the sea: « Peace,
<!-- page 200 -->
be still ». And in this he showed that he is the one who made the sea; Job, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p200-1]: « I compassed the sea with my bounds; and I said: Hitherto thou shalt come, and here thou shalt break thy swelling waves »; and Nahum, the first chapter: « Rebuking the sea and drying it up ».

40. Secondly, as to *the effect in work* it is subjoined: *And it ceased, and there was a calm; it ceased,* namely the wind; Ecclesiasticus, the forty-third chapter[^p200-2]: « At his word the wind was still »; because in the Psalm: « Fire, hail, snow, ice, stormy winds, which fulfil his word ». And so it appears that he is the one to whom Tobias says, the third chapter[^p200-3], « After a storm thou makest a calm ». And so there is true in Christ what is said in the Psalm: « And the stormy wind stood ».

41. (Vers. 25.). *And he said to them* etc. Here fourthly is determined *the progress of our belief* in two ways, namely as to *the knowledge in the heart* and as to *the confession in the mouth*[^p200-4]. — First, then, as to *the knowledge of the heart* proceeding from the hesitation of doubt to the certitude of belief, it is said: *And he said to them: Where is your faith?* Which indeed he says rebuking the doubt and persuading to firmness of faith, as if he should say: Why did you doubt, for fear of being sunk and endangered? According to what he said to Peter, Matthew, the fourteenth chapter[^p200-5]: « O thou of little faith, why didst thou doubt »? For it is said to the Hebrews, the eleventh chapter: « By faith the children of Israel passed through the Red Sea as by dry land ». Now he reproves the disciples for faintheartedness for this, that from the miracle seen he may rouse them to firmness of faith, through which they may spring back into their own littleness and wonder at the divine immensity. — Whence it is also subjoined: *Who being afraid, wondered.* For now they knew their own littleness, into which they sprang back through fear, and the divine immensity, into which they went out through wonder; Jeremias, the tenth chapter[^p200-6]: « Who shall not fear thee, O king of nations? For thine is the glory among all the wise »; Jeremias, the fifth chapter: « Will you not then fear me, saith the Lord ». « Who have set the sand a bound for the sea, an everlasting ordinance, which it shall not pass over ». — And because wonder at the power accompanies the fear of the majesty, it is rightly said: *They wondered;* Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter[^p200-7]: « Many have wondered at him, and have glorified God », because « the Lord is exceedingly great, and his power is admirable », Ecclesiasticus, the forty-third chapter.

42. Secondly, as to *the confession of the mouth* it is subjoined: *Saying one to another: Who is this, think you?* as if they should say: none is like to this one; Exodus, the fifteenth chapter[^p200-8]: « Who is like to thee, great among the strong, O Lord? Who is like to thee, magnificent in holiness, terrible and praiseworthy, doing wonders »? — And they render the reason: *For he commandeth both the winds and the sea,* through ineffable power; *and they obey him,* through admirable reverence; Job, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p200-9]: « Who made a weight for the winds, and weighed the waters by measure, when he gave a law for the rain, and a way for the sounding storms ». And so there was verified that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-ninth chapter: « At his word the waters stood as a heap ». He therefore is the one of whom Job, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p200-10]: « Who shut up the sea with doors »? For since the winds and the sea obey him, nothing can resist; Ecclesiasticus, the forty-second chapter: « All things in necessity obey him ». Whence Bede in the Gloss[^p200-11]: « Note that every creature feels the Creator, because to the majesty of the Founder those things are sensible which with us are insensible ».

43. *Spiritually,* however, there is expressed here the deliverance of the penitent soul from the temptation of the devil through the grace of Christ. Whence three things are here to be attended to spiritually: the first is *the conversion of the soul,* which is designated in the going up into the little ship; the second is *temptation,* which is designated in the onset of the storm; the third is *the consolation of the soul,* which is designated in the calming of the very great tempest.

Concerning *the going up into the little ship* designating *the conversion of the soul* three things are to be attended to, namely Christ's *example,* because he went up first; Micheas, the second chapter[^p200-12]: « He shall go up opening the way before them »; because, Acts, the first chapter, « Jesus began to do and to teach ». And this is what Ambrose says: « Christ, having left his parents, went up into the ship of the cross », that he may teach us, having left carnal
<!-- page 201 -->
love, to be crucified with him. In figure of this it is said in the first of Machabees, the sixteenth chapter[^p201-1], that John « saw the people trembling to pass over the torrent, and he passed over to them first, and the men saw him and passed over after him ».

Secondly, Christ's *counsel,* because he invited the disciples to cross over with him; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p201-2]: « Come over to me, all ye that desire me », namely out of this world, which is nothing but a certain passing over; John, the thirteenth chapter: « That he should pass out of this world to the Father »; and to the Hebrews, the thirteenth chapter: « For we have not here a lasting city, but we seek one that is to come ». Blessed is he who so passes over with Christ « through temporal goods, that he lose not the eternal[^p201-3] »; because, Wisdom, the fourteenth chapter, « passing over the sea by ship they are saved ».

Thirdly, Christ's *fellowship,* because he crossed over together with them in the ship; Isaias, the forty-third chapter[^p201-4]: « When thou shalt pass through the waters, I will be with thee, and the rivers shall not cover thee » etc., because in the Psalm: « I am with him in tribulation »; and Matthew, the twenty-eighth chapter: « Behold, I am with you all days, even to the consummation of the world ».

44. Concerning *the onset of the storm,* in which is designated *the temptation of the soul,* three things are to be attended to. The first is *the falling asleep of Christ;* which indeed is, when the love and memory of Christ grow numb in the heart; for in himself he did not fall asleep; the Psalm[^p201-5]: « Behold, he shall neither slumber nor sleep that keepeth Israel »; but in us; on account of which it is said in Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter: « Watch ye and pray, that ye enter not into temptation »; and therefore the first of Peter, the last chapter: « Be sober and watch »; and the first to the Thessalonians, the fifth chapter: « Therefore let us not sleep, as others do, but let us watch and be sober; for they that sleep, sleep in the night ».

The second is *the coming down of the storm of wind,* in which is designated *the temptation of the devil;* Ezechiel, the first chapter[^p201-6]: « A whirlwind came from the north ». This is the wind coming « from the side of the desert, which shook the four corners of the house », that is, the four affections of the mind, of which it is said in Job, the first chapter; this is the one of which Ecclesiasticus, the forty-third chapter: « The cold north wind blew, and the water was congealed into crystal ».

The third is *the endangering of the ship driven on,* which designates *the perturbation of the soul.* For the water entering into the little ship is perturbation entering into the soul, and then danger is imminent. Whence in the Psalm[^p201-7]: « Save me, O God, for the waters are come in even unto my soul »; Lamentations, the third chapter: « Waters flowed over my head; I said: I am cut off ».

45. Concerning *the consolation of the soul* three things are to be attended to. The first is *prayer in the awakening of Christ;* which indeed comes about through prayer; below, the eleventh chapter[^p201-8]: « Which of you shall have a friend » etc.; Isaias, the sixty-fourth chapter: « There is none that calleth upon thy name, that riseth up and taketh hold of thee ».

The second is *devotion of mind in tranquillity;* Tobias, the third chapter[^p201-9]: « After a storm thou makest a calm, and after tears and weeping thou pourest in joyfulness »; Wisdom, the twelfth chapter: « But thou, being master of power, judgest with tranquillity ».

The third is *the honouring of God,* in this, that they venerate and fear and confess God; Exodus, the fourteenth chapter[^p201-10]: « The Lord delivered Israel out of the hand of the Egyptians ». « Then Moses sung a canticle to the Lord » etc. — Of these three it is said in the Psalm: « Call upon me in the day of tribulation, and I will deliver thee, and thou shalt honour me ». — And so it is plain how the passing over of the penitent soul is spiritually designated here.
