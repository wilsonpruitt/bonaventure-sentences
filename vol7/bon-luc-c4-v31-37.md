# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. IV, Vers. 31-37
## *Authenticatur doctrina Christi ex miraculo potentiae*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 104 -->

#### Authenticatur doctrina Christi ex miraculo potentiae.

64. *Et descendit in Capharnaum* etc. Postquam ostendit doctrinam Christi authenticam ex merito vitae et testimonio Scripturae, hic modo ostendit ex *miraculo potentiae,* ut in ore trium testium stet firmum auctoritatis testimonium[^p104-10]. Habet autem haec pars tres. In prima proponitur *communicatio doctrinae Christi;* in secunda, *confirmatio virtutum miraculi,* ibi[^p104-11]: *Et in synagoga erat* etc.; in tertia, *acceptatio eiusdem a multitudine populi,* ibi: *Facta autem die, ibat* etc.

Circa *communicationem* autem *doctrinae* Christi quatuor exprimuntur, scilicet *locus, tempus, effectus* et *virtus.* Ex his autem quatuor colligitur doctrina Christi fuisse *humilis* et *celebris, admirabilis* et *sublimis.*

65. (Vers. 31.). *Humilis,* inquam, ostenditur ex situ loci, quem notat, cum dicit: *Descendit in Capharnaum, civitatem Galilaeae. Descensus* enim in Christo signum humilitatis, sicut *ascensus* in superbis signum elationis; unde Canticorum sexto[^p104-12]: « Descendi in hortum nucum, ut viderem poma convallium »; ad quod etiam designandum dicitur supra tertio, quod « descendit Spiritus sanctus corporali specie » etc.; et ideo ipse dicebat Ioannis sexto: « Descendi de caelo, non ut faciam voluntatem meam ». Et haec magna est humilitas, et hanc docuit *verbo* Matthaei undecimo[^p104-13]: « Discite a me, quia mitis sum et humilis corde »; Ioannis decimo tertio *exemplo:* « Si ego lavi pedes vestros etc.; exemplum enim dedi vobis ». Huic etiam competit,
<!-- page 105 -->
quia civitas illa erat in confinio gentium et Iudaeorum, « in finibus Zabulon et Nephtalim[^p105-1] », ut sic ostenderet, se non despicere gentiles ratione culpae aut idololatriae; Isaiae nono: « Primo tempore alleviata est terra Zabulon et terra Nephtalim, et in novissimo aggravata est ». Huic accedit, quia istae duae tribus prius ceteris migraverant, sicut dicitur quarti Regum decimo quinto[^p105-2]; et ideo tanquam despectiores primo fuerant visitandae.

66. *Celebris* autem ostenditur communicatio doctrinae ex tempore solemni; propter quod ait: *Docebat,* in synagoga eorum, *Sabbatis*[^p105-3]. Tunc enim populus conveniebat ad auditum Legis, quia cessabant ab omni opere servili, sicut praecipitur Levitici vigesimo tertio. Et ideo Baruch primo: « Legite librum istum, quem misimus ad vos recitari in die solemni et tempore opportuno ». Sic etiam Paulus Sabbatis docebat, secundum quod dicitur Actuum decimo quinto: « Moyses a temporibus antiquis habet in singulis civitatibus qui eum praedicent in synagogis, ubi per omne Sabbatum legitur ». In quo erudimur, secundum consilium Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo[^p105-4], sapientiam discere et docere, « in tempore vacuitatis, quia qui minoratur actu percipiet sapientiam ». Et etiam erudimur, in diebus solemnibus et in conspectu multitudinis maxime intendere divinis laudibus et praedicationibus; unde in Psalmo: « Narrabo nomen tuum fratribus meis, in medio ecclesiae »; et rursus: « In ecclesiis benedicite Domino ».

67. (Vers. 32.). *Admirabilis* autem ostenditur ex stupore populi, cum additur: *Et stupebant in doctrina eius;* et hoc, quia aliquid magnum et insolitum in ea imaginantur. Sic et in doctrina Apostolorum post Spiritus sancti adventum, Actuum secundo[^p105-5], « stupebant autem omnes, qui eos audiebant »; Iob duodecimo: « Adducit consiliarios in stultum finem et iudices in stuporem ». Nec mirum, quia, sicut dixerunt ministri Ioannis septimo, « nunquam sic locutus est homo ».

68. *Sublimis* vero ostenditur ex virtute singulari, et hoc notat, cum addit: *Quia in potestate erat sermo ipsius;* sive quia loquebatur ut Dominus omnipotens, secundum illud Matthaei septimo[^p105-6]: « Admirabantur turbae super doctrina eius; erat enim docens sicut potestatem habens »; sive quia sermoni iuncta erat virtus faciens miracula, secundum illud Ecclesiastae octavo: « Sermo eius potestate plenus est »; et infra vigesimo quarto: « De Iesu Nazareno, qui fuit vir propheta potens in opere et sermone »; sive etiam, quia verba eius spiritualem virtutem sapiebant, secundum illud Psalmi[^p105-7]: « Dabit voci suae vocem virtutis ». Sic Apostolus primae ad Corinthios secundo: « Sermo noster et praedicatio non in persuasibilibus humanae sapientiae verbis, sed in ostensione spiritus et virtutis ». Nec mirum, si sermoni eius erat virtus coniuncta, quia ipse est « Dei virtus et sapientia », primae ad Corinthios primo[^p105-8]. Et ideo ad Hebraeos primo dicitur: « Portans omnia verbo virtutis ».

69. *Et in synagoga erat* etc. Post communicationem doctrinae Christi sequitur hic authenticatio per *virtutem miraculi.* Ostenditur autem haec virtus admirabilis in Christo docente tripliciter: primo respectu *sexus virilis;* secundo, respectu *sexus muliebris,* ibi[^p105-9]: *Surgens autem Iesus;* tertio, respectu *omnis sexus et aetatis,* ibi: *Cum autem sol occidisset.* — Rursus, alia differentia est ex parte *naturae,* quia primum[^p105-10] consistit in liberatione hominis a daemone; secundum, in liberatione mulieris a febre; tertium, in liberatione ab utrisque indifferenter. — Rursus, primum est in *loco eruditionis,* quia in synagoga; secundum vero, in *loco refectionis,* scilicet in domo Simonis; tertium, in *loco*[^p105-11] *requietionis,* quia post solis occasum. — Postremo, primum horum ostenditur factum fuisse circa *mane;* secundum, circa *meridiem;* tertium, in *fine diei.* Ac per hoc ex his omnibus est, quod Christus habet *posse* super omnem personam et super omnem naturam et in omni loco et in omni tempore; ac per hoc sufficienter ostenditur authenticari[^p105-12] doctrina eius per virtutem multiplicis miraculi.

Circa curationem igitur *hominis* a daemone[^p105-13] notanda sunt quatuor concurrentia, quae faciunt ad
<!-- page 106 -->
manifestandum Christi potentiam: ex parte *daemoniaci* status manifestae miseriae; ex parte *daemonis* sensus divinae praesentiae; ex parte *Christi* operatio immensae potentiae; ex parte *populi* exhibitio debitae reverentiae; quae omnia convenientissime describuntur ab Evangelista ad praesentis miraculi magnificentiam.

70. (Vers. 33.). Ex parte igitur *daemoniaci* ostenditur *status manifestae miseriae,* cum dicitur: *Et in synagoga erat homo habens daemonium immundum,* quia in communi apparebat omnibus, quod erat a daemone obsessus; unde in loco communi erat, quia in synagoga, ubi multi conveniebant, secundum illud Ioannis decimo octavo[^p106-1]: « Ego semper docui in synagogis et in templo, ubi omnes Iudaei conveniunt ». In hac erat homo habens spiritum *immundum,* ab effectu, quia immundum facit; Zachariae decimo tertio: « Spiritum immundum auferam de terra ». *Immundus* iste spiritus dicitur, quia semper immunditiam suggerit; unde in Apocalypsis decimo sexto[^p106-2]: « Et vidi de ore draconis et de ore bestiae et de ore pseudoprophetae exire tres spiritus immundos in modum ranarum ».

71. Ex parte vero *daemonis* ostenditur *sensus divinae praesentiae,* cum dicitur: *Et clamavit*[^p106-3] *voce magna,* ut ex vi clamoris ostenderetur in daemone passio doloris; Isaiae sexagesimo quinto: « Ecce, servi mei laudabunt prae exsultatione cordis, et vos clamabitis prae dolore cordis et prae contritione spiritus ululabitis »; unde colligitur ex clamore, quod in daemone fuit dolor et tremor ex sensu praesentiae Christi. Unde Augustinus[^p106-4]: « Utinam sicut Deum daemones iudicem, sic homines agnoscerent Salvatorem! Daemones viderunt et contremuerunt, homines vero viderunt et occiderunt. O quanta infelicitas, homines peiores daemonibus inveniri »!

72. (Vers. 34.). Unde ipse timor compulit veritatem confiteri, scilicet, quod Christus esset *destructor daemonum* et *sanctificator hominum.* Propter primum additur: *Dicens: Sine, quid nobis et tibi, Iesu Nazarene? Venisti perdere nos? Sine,* id est, cessa ab afflictione et nostra persecutione, quia importabilis est; Iob decimo tertio[^p106-5]: « Manum tuam longe fac a me, et formido tua non me terreat ». Vel: *Sine,* id est, permitte, me elongari a te, quia *quid tibi et nobis?* quasi dicat: nulla convenientia, et ideo simul esse non possumus; secundae ad Corinthios sexto: « Quae conventio Christi ad Belial, lucis ad tenebras, iustitiae cum iniquitate »? Et quod non sit communicatio vel conventio, ostendit, cum adiungit: *Venisti perdere nos?* Proverbiorum vigesimo quarto[^p106-6]: « Repente consurget perditio eorum »; unde primae Ioannis tertio: « In hoc apparuit Filius Dei, ut dissolvat opera diaboli ».

73. Et nota, quod dupliciter est *perditio* daemonum, una scilicet propter peccatum superbiae, qua perditi sunt ab initio; Ezechielis vigesimo octavo[^p106-7]: « Perdidi te, o Cherub, procedens de medio lapidum ignitorum »; alia propter peccata superaddita, qua perdentur in extremo iudicio, cum auferetur omnis potestas, et infligetur omnis poena debita; de qua Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo: « Iuxta est dies perditionis ». Et haec perditio non fiet omnino ante diem iudicii; unde Marci primo[^p106-8]: « Venisti huc ante tempus perdere nos »? sed tamen ex tempore Christi inchoavit; Ioannis duodecimo: « Nunc iudicium est mundi, nunc princeps huius mundi eiicietur foras ».

74. Non solum autem confitetur ipsum destructorem daemonum, sed etiam *sanctificatorem hominum,* cum adiungit: *Scio te, quod*[^p106-9] *sis Sanctus Dei,* ille quidem, de quo Danielis nono: « Ungetur Sanctus Sanctorum ». Hoc non sciebat certa scientia, cum dicatur primae ad Corinthios secundo: « Si cognovissent, nunquam Dominum gloriae crucifixissent »; sed cognoscebat coniecturando; Proverbiorum vigesimo tertio: « In similitudinem arioli et coniectoris aestimat quod ignorat ». Existimabat enim hoc per poenam, quam sentiebat.

75. (Vers. 35.). Ex parte autem *Christi* ostenditur *operatio immensae potentiae,* cum adiungitur: *Et increpavit illum Iesus dicens: Obmutesce.* Tanquam enim omnipotens daemoni sola increpatione *silentium imponit;* Zachariae tertio[^p106-10]: « Ostendit mihi Dominus Iesum, sacerdotem magnum »; et sequitur: « Increpet Dominus in te, satan, et Dominus increpet in te ». Licet autem vera diceret, tamen *silentium imponebat,* quia, sicut dicitur Ecclesiastici decimo quinto[^p106-11], « non est speciosa laus in ore peccatoris »; unde et in Psalmo: « Peccatori dixit Deus: Quare tu enarras iustitias meas »? — Vel ideo prohibuit, quia dolose loquitur et immiscet frequenter
<!-- page 107 -->
falsa veris; unde et Marci primo[^p107-1]: « Non sinebat, daemonia loqui », dicit Glossa, « ne, dum viderent vera dicentem, sequerentur errantem ». Et ideo in Psalmo: « Muta fiant labia dolosa, quae loquuntur adversus iustum iniquitatem ». Ostendit autem hic, vanam gloriam esse vitandam, ut a vanis et falsis non quaeramus laudari. Exemplum de Paulo, Actuum decimo sexto[^p107-2].

76. Non solum autem imponit silentium increpando, verum etiam *expellit praecipiendo,* cum addit: *Exi ab illo*[^p107-3]. Iam impletur illud Habacuc tertio: « Egredietur diabolus ante pedes eius ». Et ideo sequitur: *Et cum proiecisset illum daemonium in medium, exiit ab illo;* in quo ostenditur iracundia diaboli, quia invitus exiit et cum murmure; unde et in Marci primo[^p107-4]: « Et discerpens eum, exivit ab illo ». Hoc autem facit diabolus, cum videt, se perdere quod male possidet; Apocalypsis duodecimo: « Descendit ad vos diabolus, habens iram magnam » etc. Sed ira eius non laesit, Domino prohibente; unde subditur: *Nihilque ei*[^p107-5] *nocuit;* Isaiae undecimo: « Non nocebunt et non occident in omni monte sancto meo ». — Ex hoc autem colligitur, quod diabolus habet semper voluntatem nocendi, quia hanc habet a semetipso, sed potestatem nocendi non semper, nisi quantum et quando permittitur a Deo iusto modo; unde ad Romanos decimo tertio[^p107-6]: « Non est potestas nisi a Deo ». Et ideo primae Petri tertio: « Quis est, qui vobis noceat, si boni aemulatores fueritis » ?

77. (Vers. 36.). Postremo ex parte *populi* ostenditur *exhibitio debitae reverentiae,* cum subditur: *Et factus est pavor in omnibus.* Hoc dicitur quantum ad reverentiam secundum *actum cordis.* Nec mirum, quia, Iob vigesimo sexto[^p107-7], « columnae caeli contremiscunt et pavent ad nutum eius »; unde Iob quarto: « Pavor tenuit me et tremor, et omnia ossa mea perterrita sunt ». Et hoc ad aspectum tanti miraculi; Psalmus: « Confitebor tibi, quia terribiliter magnificatus es; mirabilia opera tua »; et Ieremiae decimo: « Quis non timebit te, o rex gentium » ? — Quantum vero ad *actum oris* adiungitur: *Et colloquebantur ad invicem,* scilicet alter ad alterum conferendo: *Quod*[^p107-8] *est hoc verbum?* id est res digna verbo, sicut quarti Regum vigesimo accipitur: « Non fuit verbum in domo mea, quod non ostenderim eis ». — *Quia in potestate et virtute imperat spiritibus immundis*[^p107-9]; *potestate* praecipiente, et *virtute* faciente. Nec mirum, quia, Iob vigesimo quinto, « potestas et terror apud eum, qui facit concordiam in sublimibus ». Sed hoc imperium non est humanum nec etiam angelicum; unde in Canonica Iudae dicitur, quod « Michael Archangelus, cum altercaretur cum diabolo de Moysi corpore, hoc solum dicebat: Imperet tibi Dominus, satan ».

78. (Vers. 37.). Et quia hoc magnum et inusitatum erat, non silendum, sed cum reverentia divulgandum erat; ideo sequitur: *Et divulgabatur fama de illo in omnem locum regionis.* Sic de Salomone dicitur Ecclesiastici quadragesimo septimo[^p107-10]: « Ad insulas longe divulgatum est nomen tuum »; hoc ratione sapientiae est. Hoc praefiguratum fuit in David, primi Paralipomenon decimo quarto: « Divulgatum est nomen David in universis regionibus, et Dominus dedit pavorem super omnes inimicos eius »; similiter secundi Paralipomenon vigesimo sexto: « Divulgatum est nomen eius usque ad introitum Aegypti propter crebras victorias ». — Nota, quod fuit divulgatum nomen David ratione *iustitiae;* nomen Salomonis, ratione *sapientiae;* nomen vero Romanorum[^p107-11], ratione *potentiae;* nomen Christi, ratione omnis *excellentiae* et dignitatis vel supereminentiae.

## English
<!-- page 104 -->

#### The doctrine of Christ is authenticated from the miracle of power.

64. *And he went down into Capharnaum* etc. After he has shown the doctrine of Christ authentic from the merit of life and the testimony of Scripture, here now he shows it from the *miracle of power,* so that in the mouth of three witnesses the testimony of authority may stand firm[^p104-10]. Now this part has three. In the first is set forth the *communication of the doctrine of Christ;* in the second, the *confirmation by the powers of a miracle,* there[^p104-11]: *And in the synagogue there was* etc.; in the third, the *acceptance of the same by the multitude of the people,* there: *And when it was day, he went* etc.

Now concerning the *communication* of the *doctrine* of Christ four things are expressed, namely the *place,* the *time,* the *effect* and the *power.* And from these four it is gathered that the doctrine of Christ was *humble* and *renowned, wonderful* and *sublime.*

65. (Vers. 31.). *Humble,* I say, it is shown from the situation of the place, which he notes when he says: *He went down into Capharnaum, a city of Galilee.* For *going down* in Christ is a sign of humility, as *going up* in the proud is a sign of elation; whence the Canticle, the sixth chapter[^p104-12]: « I went down into the garden of nuts, to see the fruits of the valleys »; and to designate this it is also said above, in the third chapter, that « the Holy Spirit descended in a bodily shape » etc.; and therefore he himself said, John, the sixth chapter: « I came down from heaven, not to do my own will ». And this is great humility, and this he taught by *word,* Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p104-13]: « Learn of me, because I am meek and humble of heart »; John, the thirteenth chapter, by *example:* « If I have washed your feet etc.; for I have given you an example ». To this also it is fitting,
<!-- page 105 -->
because that city was on the border of the Gentiles and of the Jews, « in the borders of Zabulon and of Nephthalim[^p105-1] », so that he might thus show that he did not despise the Gentiles by reason of fault or of idolatry; Isaias, the ninth chapter: « At the first time the land of Zabulon and the land of Nephthali was lightly touched, and at the last it was heavily loaded ». To this is added that these two tribes had migrated before the others, as is said in the fourth of Kings, the fifteenth chapter[^p105-2]; and therefore, as the more despised, they were to be visited first.

66. But the communication of the doctrine is shown *renowned* from the solemn time; on account of which he says: *He taught,* in their synagogue, *on the sabbath days*[^p105-3]. For then the people came together for the hearing of the Law, because they ceased from every servile work, as is enjoined in Leviticus, the twenty-third chapter. And therefore Baruch, the first chapter: « Read this book, which we have sent to you to be recited on a solemn day and at a proper time ». So also Paul taught on the sabbath days, according to what is said in Acts, the fifteenth chapter: « Moses of old time hath in every city them that preach him in the synagogues, where he is read every sabbath ». In which we are schooled, according to the counsel of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p105-4], to learn and to teach wisdom « in the time of leisure, because he that is less in action shall receive wisdom ». And we are also schooled, on solemn days and in the sight of the multitude, to attend most of all to divine praises and preachings; whence in the Psalm: « I will declare thy name to my brethren, in the midst of the church »; and again: « In the churches bless ye the Lord ».

67. (Vers. 32.). But it is shown *wonderful* from the astonishment of the people, when there is added: *And they were astonished at his doctrine;* and this because they imagine something great and unwonted in it. So also in the doctrine of the Apostles after the coming of the Holy Spirit, Acts, the second chapter[^p105-5], « and all that heard them were astonished »; Job, the twelfth chapter: « He bringeth counsellors to a foolish end, and judges into astonishment ». Nor is it a wonder, because, as the ministers said, John, the seventh chapter, « never did man speak thus ».

68. But it is shown *sublime* from singular power, and this he notes when he adds: *For his speech was with power;* either because he spoke as the Lord omnipotent, according to that saying of Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p105-6]: « The multitudes wondered at his doctrine; for he was teaching as one having power »; or because a power working miracles was joined to the speech, according to that saying of Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter: « His word is full of power »; and below, the twenty-fourth chapter: « Concerning Jesus of Nazareth, who was a man, a prophet, mighty in work and word »; or also because his words savoured of a spiritual power, according to that saying of the Psalm[^p105-7]: « He will give to his voice the voice of power ». So the Apostle, the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter: « Our speech and preaching was not in the persuasive words of human wisdom, but in showing of the spirit and of power ». Nor is it a wonder if power was conjoined to his speech, because he is « the power of God and the wisdom », the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p105-8]. And therefore it is said in Hebrews, the first chapter: « Upholding all things by the word of power ».

69. *And in the synagogue there was* etc. After the communication of the doctrine of Christ there follows here the authentication through the *power of a miracle.* Now this wonderful power in Christ teaching is shown in three ways: first in respect of the *male sex;* second, in respect of the *female sex,* there[^p105-9]: *And Jesus rising up;* third, in respect of *every sex and age,* there: *And when the sun was down.* — Again, another difference is on the part of *nature,* because the first[^p105-10] consists in the deliverance of a man from a demon; the second, in the deliverance of a woman from a fever; the third, in the deliverance from both indifferently. — Again, the first is in a *place of erudition,* because in the synagogue; but the second, in a *place of refection,* namely in the house of Simon; the third, in a *place*[^p105-11] *of resting,* because after the setting of the sun. — Lastly, the first of these is shown to have been done about *morning;* the second, about *midday;* the third, at the *end of the day.* And through this it is from all these that Christ has *ability* over every person and over every nature and in every place and in every time; and through this it is sufficiently shown that his doctrine is authenticated[^p105-12] through the power of a manifold miracle.

Concerning, then, the cure of the *man* from the demon[^p105-13] four concurring things are to be noted, which make for
<!-- page 106 -->
manifesting the power of Christ: on the part of the *demoniac,* the state of manifest misery; on the part of the *demon,* the sense of the divine presence; on the part of *Christ,* the working of immense power; on the part of the *people,* the showing of due reverence; all of which are described most fittingly by the Evangelist for the magnificence of the present miracle.

70. (Vers. 33.). On the part, then, of the *demoniac* is shown the *state of manifest misery,* when it is said: *And in the synagogue there was a man who had an unclean devil,* because it appeared to all in common that he was obsessed by a demon; whence he was in a common place, because in the synagogue, where many came together, according to that saying of John, the eighteenth chapter[^p106-1]: « I have always taught in the synagogues and in the temple, whither all the Jews resort ». In this was a man having an *unclean* spirit, from the effect, because it makes unclean; Zacharias, the thirteenth chapter: « The unclean spirit I will take away out of the earth ». This spirit is called *unclean* because it always suggests uncleanness; whence in the Apocalypse, the sixteenth chapter[^p106-2]: « And I saw from the mouth of the dragon, and from the mouth of the beast, and from the mouth of the false prophet three unclean spirits go forth in the manner of frogs ».

71. But on the part of the *demon* is shown the *sense of the divine presence,* when it is said: *And he cried out*[^p106-3] *with a loud voice,* so that from the force of the cry the passion of sorrow might be shown in the demon; Isaias, the sixty-fifth chapter: « Behold, my servants shall praise for exultation of heart, and you shall cry for sorrow of heart, and shall howl for grief of spirit »; whence it is gathered from the cry that in the demon there was sorrow and trembling from the sense of the presence of Christ. Whence Augustine[^p106-4]: « Would that, as the demons acknowledge God as judge, so men might acknowledge the Saviour! The demons saw and trembled, but men saw and slew. O how great an unhappiness, that men should be found worse than demons »!

72. (Vers. 34.). Whence fear itself compelled him to confess the truth, namely that Christ was the *destroyer of demons* and the *sanctifier of men.* On account of the first there is added: *Saying: Let alone, what have we to do with thee, Jesus of Nazareth? Art thou come to destroy us? Let alone,* that is, cease from the affliction and our persecution, because it is unbearable; Job, the thirteenth chapter[^p106-5]: « Withdraw thy hand far from me, and let not thy dread terrify me ». Or: *Let alone,* that is, permit me to be far from thee, because *what hast thou to do with us?* as if to say: there is no agreement, and therefore we cannot be together; the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter: « What concord hath Christ with Belial, light with darkness, justice with iniquity »? And that there is no communication or concord, he shows when he adjoins: *Art thou come to destroy us?* Proverbs, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p106-6]: « Their destruction shall rise suddenly »; whence the first of John, the third chapter: « For this the Son of God appeared, that he might destroy the works of the devil ».

73. And note that the *destruction* of the demons is twofold: the one, namely, on account of the sin of pride, by which they were destroyed from the beginning; Ezechiel, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p106-7]: « I destroyed thee, O Cherub, going forth from the midst of the stones of fire »; the other on account of the sins added over, by which they will be destroyed in the last judgement, when all power shall be taken away and every due punishment shall be inflicted; of which Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter: « The day of destruction is at hand ». And this destruction will not take place wholly before the day of judgement; whence Mark, the first chapter[^p106-8]: « Art thou come hither before the time to destroy us »? but yet it began from the time of Christ; John, the twelfth chapter: « Now is the judgement of the world, now shall the prince of this world be cast out ».

74. And he confesses him not only the destroyer of demons, but also the *sanctifier of men,* when he adjoins: *I know thee, that*[^p106-9] *thou art the Holy One of God,* he indeed of whom Daniel, the ninth chapter: « The Saint of saints shall be anointed ». This he did not know with certain knowledge, since it is said in the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter: « If they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory »; but he knew it by conjecturing; Proverbs, the twenty-third chapter: « In the likeness of a soothsayer and a conjecturer he esteemeth that which he knoweth not ». For he supposed this through the punishment which he felt.

75. (Vers. 35.). But on the part of *Christ* is shown the *working of immense power,* when there is adjoined: *And Jesus rebuked him, saying: Hold thy peace.* For as omnipotent he *imposes silence* on the demon by rebuke alone; Zacharias, the third chapter[^p106-10]: « The Lord showed me Jesus, the high priest »; and there follows: « The Lord rebuke thee, Satan, and the Lord rebuke thee ». But although he was saying true things, yet he *imposed silence,* because, as is said in Ecclesiasticus, the fifteenth chapter[^p106-11], « praise is not seemly in the mouth of a sinner »; whence also in the Psalm: « To the sinner God hath said: Why dost thou declare my justices »? — Or he forbade it for this reason, because he speaks deceitfully and frequently mingles
<!-- page 107 -->
false things with true; whence also Mark, the first chapter[^p107-1]: « He suffered not, the devils to speak », says the Gloss, « lest, while they saw him speaking true things, they should follow him erring ». And therefore in the Psalm: « Let deceitful lips be made dumb, which speak iniquity against the just ». And here he shows that vainglory is to be avoided, so that we may not seek to be praised by the vain and the false. An example concerning Paul, Acts, the sixteenth chapter[^p107-2].

76. And not only does he impose silence by rebuking, but he also *expels by enjoining,* when he adds: *Go out of him*[^p107-3]. Now is fulfilled that saying of Habacuc, the third chapter: « The devil shall go forth before his feet ». And therefore there follows: *And when the devil had thrown him into the midst, he went out of him;* in which is shown the wrath of the devil, because he went out unwilling and with murmuring; whence also in Mark, the first chapter[^p107-4]: « And tearing him, he went out of him ». Now the devil does this when he sees that he is losing what he wrongly possesses; the Apocalypse, the twelfth chapter: « The devil is come down unto you, having great wrath » etc. But his wrath did not hurt, the Lord forbidding it; whence there is subjoined: *And hurt him*[^p107-5] *not at all;* Isaias, the eleventh chapter: « They shall not hurt, and they shall not kill in all my holy mountain ». — And from this it is gathered that the devil always has the will of hurting, because he has this from himself, but not always the power of hurting, except as much and when it is permitted by God in a just manner; whence to the Romans, the thirteenth chapter[^p107-6]: « There is no power but from God ». And therefore the first of Peter, the third chapter: « Who is it that can hurt you, if you be zealous of good » ?

77. (Vers. 36.). Lastly, on the part of the *people* is shown the *showing of due reverence,* when there is subjoined: *And there came fear upon all.* This is said as to reverence according to the *act of the heart.* Nor is it a wonder, because, Job, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p107-7], « the pillars of heaven tremble and dread at his beck »; whence Job, the fourth chapter: « Fear held me and trembling, and all my bones were affrighted ». And this at the sight of so great a miracle; the Psalm: « I will praise thee, because thou art fearfully magnified; marvellous are thy works »; and Jeremias, the tenth chapter: « Who shall not fear thee, O king of nations » ? — But as to the *act of the mouth* there is adjoined: *And they talked among themselves,* namely one conferring with another: *What*[^p107-8] *word is this?* that is, a thing worthy of a word, as is taken in the fourth of Kings, the twentieth chapter: « There was not a word in my house that I have not shown them ». — *For with authority and power he commandeth the unclean spirits*[^p107-9]; *authority* enjoining, and *power* doing it. Nor is it a wonder, because, Job, the twenty-fifth chapter, « power and terror are with him who maketh peace in his high places ». But this command is not human nor even angelic; whence in the Canonical Epistle of Jude it is said that « Michael the Archangel, when he disputed with the devil about the body of Moses, said this only: The Lord command thee, Satan ».

78. (Vers. 37.). And because this was great and unwonted, it was not to be kept silent, but to be spread abroad with reverence; therefore there follows: *And the fame of him was spread abroad into every place of the country.* So of Solomon it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the forty-seventh chapter[^p107-10]: « To the islands far off thy name was spread abroad »; this is by reason of wisdom. This was prefigured in David, the first of Paralipomenon, the fourteenth chapter: « The name of David was spread abroad in all regions, and the Lord gave fear upon all his enemies »; likewise the second of Paralipomenon, the twenty-sixth chapter: « His name was spread abroad even to the entering in of Egypt for his frequent victories ». — Note that the name of David was spread abroad by reason of *justice;* the name of Solomon, by reason of *wisdom;* but the name of the Romans[^p107-11], by reason of *power;* the name of Christ, by reason of every *excellence* and dignity or supereminence.
