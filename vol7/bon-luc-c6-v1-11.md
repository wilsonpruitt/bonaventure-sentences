# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. VI, Vers. 1-11
## *Factum est autem in Sabbato. Supra egit Evangelista de discipulorum vocatione; in parte ista agit de ipsorum defensione*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 134 -->

### Capitulum VI.

1. *Factum est autem in Sabbato.* Supra[^p134-8] egit Evangelista de discipulorum vocatione; in parte ista agit de *ipsorum defensione,* quia boni pastoris est non solum oves congregare, sed etiam a lupis defendere[^p134-9]. Et habet haec pars duas. In prima defendit eos *testimonio Scripturae;* in secunda vero, miraculo potentiae, ibi[^p134-10]: *Factum est autem et in alio Sabbato.* Ipse enim Christus est « Dei virtus et Dei sapientia »; unde discipuli vocabant eum: « Magister et Domine », Ioannis decimo tertio; et ideo primo defendit ut *Magister* per testimonium authenticum, secundo, ut *Dominus* per opus miraculosum.

Circa primum autem defendendi modum tria introducuntur: primum est *criminandi occasio,* secundum est *criminationis oppositio*[^p134-11], tertium est *criminatorum confutatio.*

2. (Vers. 1.). *Criminandi* igitur *occasionem* dedit discipulorum necessitas, propter quam vellebant spicas in Sabbatis. Et hanc notat, cum dicit: *Factum est in Sabbato secundo primo,* id est secundo a primo, id est immediate sequente Sabbatum praecedens, de quo habitum est supra quarto[^p134-12], in quo curavit daemoniacum. Vel, sicut dicit Chrysostomus, in Sabbato *secundo primo,* id est bis
<!-- page 135 -->
primo sive principali, propter duplicem solemnitatem concurrentem, quia Sabbatum ex se solemnitas erat, et etiam ratione *Neomeniae,* quae tunc erat. Vel secundum Bedam[^p135-1] *secundo primo* est una dictio composita ex integro et corrupto, ut *secundo primum* dicatur, hoc est secundo praelatum, id est superius et dignius illo Sabbato legali, quia iam tunc incipiebat Sabbatum novum, in quo feriandum est a vitiis, non ab operibus necessariis.

3. Et hoc patet per ipsorum discipulorum exemplum, de quibus subditur: *Cum transiret per sata; transibat,* scilicet ipse Christus, quia viator erat; unde Actuum decimo[^p135-2]: « Pertransiit benefaciendo et sanando omnes oppressos ». In transitu autem suo designat, omnia haec esse transitoria, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo quarto: « Caelum et terra transibunt ». *Transibat* autem, non manebat, et discipuli eius similiter, ut ipsi etiam hoc faterentur, quia « non habemus hic manentem civitatem », ad Hebraeos ultimo. — Quia vero, per haec transeundo, non debemus immorari, sed indigemus sustentari; ideo addit: *Vellebant discipuli eius spicas et manducabant, confricantes manibus suis*[^p135-3]; *vellebant,* id est, avellebant, a stipula discerpentes, secundum quod dicitur Amos septimo: « Armentarius ego sum, vellicans sycomoros ». *Vellebant,* dicit, non metebant, quia hoc secundum Legem erat concessum, illud inhibitum; Deuteronomii vigesimo tertio: « Si intraveris segetem amici tui, franges spicas et manibus conteres, falce autem non metas ». Et hoc quidem faciebant, famis necessitate compulsi, sicut, Matthaei duodecimo[^p135-4], « discipuli eius esurientes coeperunt vellere spicas et manducare ». Nec mirum, quia, Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo, « anima esuriens amarum pro dulci sumet »; et Iob sexto: « Quae tangere nolebat anima mea, nunc prae angustia cibi mei sunt ».

4. Unde *humana necessitas* ipsos excusabat, quae hoc facere compellebat; et hanc rationem assignat Beda[^p135-5]: « Non habentes, inquit, discipuli spatium manducandi propter importunitatem turbarum, esuriebant ut homines, sed vellentes spicas inediam consolabantur ». — Alia ratio est *necessitas paupertatis assumtae,* qua erant inopes, ut nec sufficienter haberent panem; unde Apostolus secundae ad Corinthios undecimo[^p135-6]: « In fame et siti, in frigore et nuditate ». — Tertia ratio est *austeritatis exemplum,* quo docebant nos, sicut dicit Ambrosius[^p135-7], « non praeparatas escas, sed simplices quaerere cibos »; Isaiae trigesimo: « Dabit vobis Dominus panem arctum et aquam brevem ». — Quarta est, ut *ostendant,* quod non ad litteram, sed *spiritualiter* debemus legale Sabbatum observare, contra haereticos post futuros; ad Hebraeos quarto[^p135-8]: « Relinquitur sabbatismus populo Dei ». — Quinta ratio est *spiritualis intelligentia.* Per *Sabbatum* enim intelligitur otium divinae contemplationis; Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo[^p135-9]: « Vocaveris Sabbatum Domini delicatum ». *Spicae* sunt praegustationes dulcedinis internae, quae asperitatibus sunt permixtae, et ideo *manibus confricantur,* ut granum purum eliciatur, sicut Ruth, quae « colligebat spicas remanentes post terga metentium ». — Sexta ratio est ex parte *dulcedinis verbi Christi,* qua fortiter illecti, obliviscuntur manducare; unde Psalmus[^p135-10]: « Quam dulcia faucibus meis eloquia tua, super mel ori meo »; et Ioannis sexto: « Verba. quae locutus sum vobis, spiritus et vita sunt ».

5. (Vers. 2.). *Criminationis* vero *obiectionem* intulit Pharisaeorum perversitas, qua libenter insultabant discipulis. Quod notat, cum subdit: *Quidam autem*
<!-- page 136 -->
*Pharisaei*[^p136-1] *dicebant illis: Quid facitis quod non licet in Sabbatis?* quia praeceptum erat in Lege, Exodi decimo sexto et trigesimo quinto et Levitici vigesimo tertio, Sabbatum diligentissime observari. Et ideo arguebant eos tanquam legis divinae transgressores, sicut Magistrum eorum; Ioannis nono: « Non est hic homo a Deo, qui Sabbatum non custodit ». Unde eos morte dignos reputabant, quia, secundum illud ad Hebraeos decimo[^p136-2], « irritam quis faciens Legem Moysi, absque ulla miseratione sub duobus vel tribus testibus moritur », et maxime quantum ad mandatum de Sabbato, quod erat praecipue observandum; unde ille qui inventus est colligere ligna in Sabbato, interfectus est, sicut dicitur Numerorum decimo quinto: « Morte moriatur homo iste, obruat eum lapidibus omnis turba extra castra ».

6. Haec autem criminatio partim veniebat ex *ignorantia Legis,* partim ex *superbia mentis,* partim ex *simulatione hypocrisis,* partim ex *superstitione traditionis.* — Ex *ignorantia Legis,* quia non prohibebatur sumtio necessitatis, sed operatio servitutis. Et ideo talibus dicitur Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p136-3]: « Erratis, nescientes Scripturas neque virtutem Dei ». — Ex *superbia mentis,* quia alios despiciebant, sicut infra decimo octavo dicitur de Pharisaeo, qui se ipsum iactabat: « Gratias tibi, Deus, ago, quia non sum sicut ceteri hominum »; ad Romanos decimo: « Dei ignorantes iustitiam, suam quaerentes statuere, iustitiae Dei non sunt subiecti ». — Ex *simulatione hypocrisis,* secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p136-4]: « Vae vobis! scribae et Pharisaei hypocritae, qui decimatis mentham et reliquistis quae graviora sunt Legis ». — Ex *superstitione traditionis,* in qua maiorem vim faciebant quam in Lege; unde dicitur eis Matthaei decimo quinto: « Quare et vos transgredimini mandatum Dei propter traditionem vestram »? — Haec autem criminatio potissime veniebat ex *ignorantia Legis,* ex qua inflammabatur Pharisaeorum perversitas.

7. (Vers. 3.). *Criminatorum* autem *confutationem* subiecit auctoritas Veritatis, et hanc notat, cum subdit: *Et respondens Iesus ad eos, dixit.* Confundit autem Veritas Pharisaeos et defendit discipulos *auctoritate Scripturae* et etiam *propria.* — Per *auctoritatem Scripturae* ostendit, quod mandatum de Sabbato non obligabat in casu necessitatis; et hoc ostendit exemplo David, quod insinuat, cum dicit: *Nec hoc legistis?* et si legistis, non *intellexistis;* Iob quarto[^p136-5]: « Quia nullus intelligit, in aeternum peribunt »; vel si legistis, non *meministis,* secundum illud Ezechielis septimo: « Peribit Lex a sacerdote et consilium a senioribus eius », quasi dicat: vos, qui estis Legis aemulatores, deberetis legisse, non ignorare, ne posset vobis dici illud Ioannis tertio: « Tu es magister in Israel, et haec ignoras »? Et vere debebant legisse, quia valde notabile erat factum, scilicet *quod fecit David, cum esurisset ipse et qui cum eo*[^p136-6] *erant.* — Sed contrarium huius dicti videtur primi Regum vigesimo primo: « Quare tu solus venisti, et nullus tecum »? Sed illud dicit, quia non habebat nobilem societatem, cum esset « gener regis[^p136-7] » et magnus princeps. Unde subditur ibidem, quod « vasa puerorum erant sancta ». Societatem igitur habuit, licet non ita magnam vel notabilem; et illi fuerunt socii eius in facto, ut non possint dicere, factum esse causa privilegii specialis.

8. (Vers. 4.). Unde addit: *Quomodo intravit in domum Dei,* id est in tabernaculum, quia domus Dei, id est templum, nondum erat aedificata; primi Paralipomenon decimo septimo[^p136-8]: « Non aedificabis mihi domum ad habitandum. Non enim mansi in domo, ex quo eduxi Israel de Aegypto, usque ad diem hanc ». — Nec tantum in domum, id est tabernaculum, introivit, sed, quod plus est, *panes*[^p136-9] *propositionis sumsit, sumsit* scilicet a sacerdote, *et manducavit.* Et de his panibus Exodi vigesimo quinto: « Panes super mensam pones propositionis in conspectu meo semper », qui ideo *propositionis* dicuntur, quia per singula Sabbata super mensam proponebantur. — Et quod plus est: *Manducavit ipse et qui cum eo erant*[^p136-10], sicut dicitur primi Regum vigesimo primo: « Dedit sacerdos David sanctificatum panem; neque enim erant ibi nisi tantum panes propositionis », contra Legem; unde subdit: *Quos non licebat manducare nisi tantum sacerdotibus;* unde dicitur Levitici vigesimo secundo, quod « inquilinus etiam sacerdotis et mercenarius non debebant vesci de sanctificatis ». Sed Levitici vigesimo quarto dicitur de panibus propositionis, quod erant « Aaron et filiorum, ut comedant in loco sancto, quia Sanctum
<!-- page 137 -->
sanctorum est foedere sempiterno ». Si ergo David non peccavit, quia necessitas eum excusavit ab illo praecepto, pariter et hi qui sunt necessitate compulsi, non sunt reprehendendi, quia per necessitatem famis sunt excusati.

9. (Vers. 5.). Et ne quid desit auctoritati Scripturae, subiungit *suam auctoritatem,* per quam cum eis volebat et poterat dispensare. Quod notat, cum subdit: *Et dicebat illis, quod*[^p137-1] *dominus est Filius hominis etiam Sabbati.* Vocat autem se *Filium hominis* quantum ad *humilitatem humanitatis assumtae,* et dicit se *Dominum Sabbati* et Legis quantum ad *sublimitatem Divinitatis occultae;* unde in Apocalypsis decimo nono[^p137-2] dicitur, quod « habebat in vestimento et in femore suo scriptum: Rex regum et Dominus dominantium », Et ideo dicitur in Psalmo: « Omnia subiecisti sub pedibus eius »; dicitur hoc de Filio hominis « paululum minorato ab Angelis » propter ignominiam passionis. Et ideo tanquam Dominus cum eis dispensabat ad tempus, qui postmodum ipsius observantiam ablaturus erat.

10. *Factum est in alio Sabbato* etc. Supra[^p137-3] defendit discipulos ut Magister auctoritate Scripturae sive testimonio; hic defendit ut *Dominus* virtute *potentiae in miraculo,* in quo, sanando hominem in Sabbato, ostendit, se dominum esse Sabbati, et quod non oportet in Sabbato ab omni opere abstinere, sed a malo solum. Circa huius miraculi descriptionem tria insinuantur: primum est *opportunitas miraculosi operis;* secundum est *sublimitas mirabilis operatoris,* ibi[^p137-4]: *Ipse autem sciebat cogitationes eorum;* tertium est *perversitas miserabilis plebis,* ibi: *Ipsi autem repleti sunt insipientia.*

Quantum ad *opportunitatem* autem *miraculosi operis* notandum, quod ipsa colligitur ex tribus, scilicet ex *confirmatione propriae doctrinae,* ex *relevatione humanae miseriae* et ex *confutatione*[^p137-5] *Iudaicae perfidiae.* Primum erat sapientiae, secundum clementiae, tertium iustitiae, quae sunt in Christo omnipotente.

11. (Vers. 6.). Erat ergo primo *propria doctrina confirmanda,* quam publicabat Sabbatis; propter quod dicit: *Factum est autem et in alio Sabbato, ut intraret in synagogam et doceret:* quia, sicut Ioannis decimo octavo[^p137-6] dicit, « ego semper docui in synagogis et in templo, ubi omnes Iudaei conveniunt »; quia illud Isaiae quinquagesimo quarto debebat in Christo impleri: « Ponam omnes filios tuos doctos a Domino ». Unde hic erat, de quo Ecclesiastici trigesimo nono[^p137-7]: « Ipse palam faciet disciplinam doctrinae suae et in lege testamenti Domini gloriabitur ». Docebat enim iustitiam, quae est communis virtus; unde Osee decimo: « Tempus requirendi Dominum, cum venerit qui docebit vos iustitiam ».

12. Erat *humana miseria relevanda* propter penuriam infirmitatis, ob quam dicit: *Et erat ibi homo, et manus eius dextera erat arida;* et ita ineptus erat ad omnia opera, quia fortiorem manum et melius membrum amiserat. Unde vere erat homo iste ille, de quo Iob decimo quarto[^p137-8], quod « brevi vivens tempore, repletur multis miseriis ». Unde iam in isto impletum erat illud Zachariae undecimo: « Brachium eius dexterum ariditate siccabitur »; et ideo iam potuit gemere et dicere Domino: « Aruit tanquam testa virtus mea »; et propterea relevanda per divinam potentiam.

13. (Vers. 7.). Erat nihilominus *Iudaica perfidia confutanda* propter observantiam malignitatis, ratione cuius dicit: *Observabant autem scribae et Pharisaei, si in Sabbato curaret.* Unde impletum est in eis illud Psalmi[^p137-9]: « Omnes cogitationes eorum in malum, inhabitabunt et abscondent; ipsi calcaneum meum observabunt ». — Observatio ista erat insidiosa, quod spectat ad malignum animum, iuxta illud Ieremiae nono: « In ore suo pacem cum amico suo loquitur, et occulte ponit ei insidias ». Et ideo subditur: *Ut invenirent, unde accusarent illum*[^p137-10]. Unde isti dicebant illud Babyloniorum, Danielis sexto: « Non inveniemus huic aliquam occasionem nisi forte in lege Dei sui ». In hac igitur die praecipue observabant, sicut dicit Beda[^p137-11], « ut, si non curaret, crudelitatis vel imbecillitatis; si autem curaret, transgressionis arguerent ». Sed certe neutrum inveniunt, quia, dum curat, ostendit se misericordem; et dum non curat, iustum; unde Ioannis nono[^p137-12]: « Nisi esset hic a Deo, non posset facere quidquam ». Et ideo Ieremiae secundo: « Quid invenerunt patres vestri iniquitatis in me, quia elongaverunt a me »? quasi dicat: nihil, quia ipse est, de quo Isaiae
<!-- page 138 -->
quinquagesimo tertio: « Peccatum non fecit, nec inventus est dolus in ore eius ». Propterea dicebat ipse Ioannis octavo: « Quis ex vobis arguet me de peccato? Si veritatem dico vobis, quare non creditis mihi »?

14. (Vers. 8.). *Ipse vero sciebat cogitationes eorum.* Post opportunitatem miraculosi operis subditur hic *sublimitas mirabilis operatoris* quantum ad tria, scilicet quantum ad *sapientiam discernendi* in corde, *prudentiam interrogandi* in sermone et *efficaciam curandi* in opere[^p138-1].

Primo igitur per *sapientiam in corde* deprehendit eorum cogitationes; quod notat, cum dicit: *Ipse vero sciebat cogitationes eorum;* et vere, quia Dominus sapientissimus; unde in Psalmo[^p138-2]: « Dominus scit cogitationes hominum, quoniam vanae ». Et ideo oculis Dei erant cogitationes eorum apertae; propter quod dicit Isaiae primo: « Auferte malum cogitationum vestrarum ab oculis meis »; unde et Ioannis decimo sexto: « Dicebant ei discipuli: Nunc scimus, quia scis omnia, et non est opus, ut quis te interroget ». Nec mirum, quia ipse est ille, de quo Ieremiae decimo septimo[^p138-3]: « Ego Dominus scrutans corda et renes, qui do unicuique iuxta viam suam ».

15. In cuius rei testimonium, ut ostendat se cognitorem cordium, iubet, hunc curandum surgere in medium, cum subdit: *Et ait homini, qui habebat manum aridam;* propter quem illi cogitationes superbas cogitabant, ita ut posset eis dici illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo septimo[^p138-4]: « O praesumtio nequissima! unde creata es cooperire aridam malitia »? *Surge et sta in medium,* id est in loco perspicuo, ut omnes cognoscant, cogitatus suos esse deprehensos, iuxta illud Ecclesiastici primo[^p138-5]: « Ne accesseris ad illum duplici corde »; « ne revelet Deus abscondita tua et in medio synagogae elidat te »; quia, secundum quod dicitur Proverbiorum vigesimo sexto, « qui operit odium fraudulenter manifestabitur malitia eius in concilio ». — *Et surgens stetit,* non timens indignationem Pharisaeorum. Dicebat enim illud Iob decimo septimo[^p138-6] intra se: « Libera me, Domine, et pone me iuxta te, et cuiusvis manus pugnet contra me »; et rursus illud Psalmi: « Si consistant adversum me castra, non timebit cor meum »; unde ad Domini imperium confortatus, securus stetit. Nec mirum, quia ipsi recte potest dici illud Iob quarto[^p138-7]: « Ecce, docuisti multos et manus lassas roborasti »; unde poterat hic dicere illud Danielis decimo: « Cumque loqueretur mecum, convalui et dixi: Loquere, Domine mi, quia confortasti me ».

16. (Vers. 9.). Secundo vero per *prudentiam in sermone* declinabat eorum machinationes; propter quod additur: *Et ait*[^p138-8] *ad illos Iesus: Interrogo vos.* — Sed contrarium huius videtur dici Matthaei duodecimo, ubi dicitur, quod ipsi « interrogabant Dominum: Si licet Sabbato curare »? — Ad quod respondet Augustinus, de Concordia Evangelistarum[^p138-9], quod primo ipsi interrogaverunt, et Dominus eis replicavit. Vel potest esse, quod cogitationem interiorem sequitur interrogationis forma. Matthaeus intelligit, quod in auribus Dei *cogitare* est loqui, et *dubitare* est interrogare. — Et proponit cautam interrogationem: *Si licet Sabbato*[^p138-10] *bene facere, an male; animam salvam facere, an perdere?* Hoc quaerit non tanquam dubium, sed tanquam indubitanter verum, ut altera pars detur. Constat enim, quod licet Sabbatis *bene facere,* vel *non bene facere; salvare* animam, vel *non salvare,* quia inter contradictorie opposita nihil est medium[^p138-11]. Sed *non bene facere,* cum est locus et opportunitas, est negligere bonum, et hoc est male facere. Igitur hoc est certum et verum disiunctum, de quo necesse est dare alteram partem; sed hanc non possunt dare: licet male facere, vel animam perdere: restat igitur, ex hac interrogatione ipsos esse convictos, quod licet Sabbato bene facere et animam curare; et sic non poterant respondere. Unde impletum est illud Iob nono[^p138-12]: « Si repente interroget, quis respondebit ei? Aut quis poterit ei dicere: Cur ita facis »? Et ideo ad Romanos nono: « O homo! quis es tu, ut respondeas Deo »? Unde Iob nono dicitur, quod « non poterit respondere ei unum pro mille ». In Matthaei autem duodecimo[^p138-13] istud disiunctum non sic proponitur, sed altera pars quaeritur et probatur *a minori* per liberationem ovis a fovea; multo igitur fortius liberandus est homo, de quo in Psalmo: « Omnia subiecisti sub pedibus eius » etc.

<!-- page 139 -->
17. (Vers. 10.). Tertio autem per *efficaciam in opere* deiiciebat eorum praesumtiones; propter quod subiungitur: *Et circumspectis omnibus,* tanquam iam confusis, ut iam posset dicere illud Iob decimo septimo[^p139-1]: « Ergo vos convertimini et venite, et non inveniam in vobis ullum sapientem ». — Et quia nullus poterat contradicere, ad veritatis confirmationem subditur: *Dixit homini: Extende manum tuam.* — Et quoniam « sermo eius potestate plenus est », sicut dicitur Ecclesiastae octavo[^p139-2]; ideo subditur: *Et extendit, et restituta est* sanitati *manus eius;* in quo ostendit Dominus, se esse Legis et Sabbati dominum. Nam ipse erat, qui Moysi, Exodi quarto[^p139-3], sanavit manum per solum imperium; ipse etiam erat, qui Ieroboam, idololatrae regis Israel, manum siccavit et iterum sanavit, sicut dicitur tertii Regum decimo tertio; ipse, inquam, est ille, de quo dicitur, quod « vulnerat et medetur, percutit, et manus eius sanabunt »; de quo in Psalmo: « Qui propitiatur omnibus iniquitatibus tuis, qui sanat omnes infirmitates tuas »; et hoc solo sermone, ut manifestetur, quod ipse est ille, de quo Sapientiae decimo sexto[^p139-4]: « Tuus, Domine, sermo sanavit eos, qui sanat omnia ». Sicut enim Pater per Verbum increatum omnia fecit, ita per Verbum incarnatum, ut dicit Augustinus, omnia refecit.

18. (Vers. 11.). *Ipsi autem repleti sunt insipientia.* Hic tertio tangitur *perversitas miserabilis plebis,* quae inde obstinata est in malo, unde debuit relevari ad bonum. Haec autem perversitas ostenditur in tribus, scilicet in *excaecatione cordis, conspiratione oris* et *persecutione operis.*

Quantum ad *excaecationem cordis* dicitur: *Ipsi autem repleti sunt insipientia,* hoc est caecitate malitiae; Sapientiae secundo[^p139-5]: « Haec cogitaverunt et erraverunt, excaecavit enim illos malitia eorum ». Hac quippe fuerunt repleti et stulti facti sunt iusto iudicio Dei, secundum illud ad Romanos primo: « Tradidit illos Deus in reprobum sensum »; « repletos omni iniquitate, malitia, avaritia, nequitia; plenos invidia, homicidio, dolo, malignitate ». Hac quippe invidia erant excaecati, quia[^p139-6] « sapiens est ille cui sapiunt res, prout sunt »; invidus autem aliena felicitate torquetur. Tales erant hi; unde eis competebat illud Baruch tertio: « Quoniam non habuerunt sapientiam, perierunt propter suam insipientiam ». Haec autem plenitudo insipientiae est vera *vacuitas,* quia[^p139-7] « *divitiae salutis,* sapientia et scientia »; igitur *ab oppositis* insipientia est *inopia;* ideo dicitur Proverbiorum decimo nono: « Melior est pauper ambulans in simplicitate sua, quam dives torquens labia sua et insipiens ».

19. Quantum ad *conspirationem* autem *oris* additur: *Et colloquebantur ad invicem,* mutuo sermone se ipsos provocantes ad malum. Sic fecerunt Ioannis undecimo[^p139-8]: « Collegerunt pontifices et Pharisaei consilium et dicebant: Quid facimus » etc. Hoc consilium figuraliter praecessit in fratribus Ioseph; Genesis trigesimo septimo: « Mutuo loquebantur, dicentes: Ecce, somniator venit; venite, occidamus eum ». Hoc consilium erat impium; ideo Genesis quadragesimo nono: « In consilium eorum non veniat anima mea »; Ieremiae decimo octavo: « Tu scis omne consilium eorum adversum me in mortem; ne propitieris iniquitatibus eorum ».

20. Quantum ad *persecutionem operis* subditur: *Quidnam facerent de*[^p139-9] *Iesu,* id est, quomodo eum morti tradere possent; quod et fecerunt, licet non statim, secundum illud Ioannis septimo: « Nemo misit in eum manus, quia nondum venerat hora eius ». Sed tamen post fecerunt, sicut dicitur Actuum tertio: « Auctorem vitae interemistis et petistis virum homicidam donari vobis ». Unde ipse Salvator dicere poterat illud Ieremiae undecimo[^p139-10]: « Tu autem, Domine, demonstrasti mihi, et cognovi consilia eorum. Ego autem quasi agnus mansuetus, qui portatur ad victimam, et non cognovi, quoniam cogitaverunt consilia, dicentes: Mittamus lignum in panem eius » etc.

21. *Spiritualiter* intelligendum, quod *ariditas* est in anima defectus vigoris propter absentiam gratiae, secundum illud Ioannis decimo quinto[^p139-11]: « Si quis in me non manserit, mittetur foras sicut palmes et arescet ». Haec autem ariditas septem de causis introducitur secundum septem capitalia peccata. Unde nota, quod est ariditas *superbiae* sive ex superbia proveniens; Ecclesiastici decimo[^p139-12]: « Radices gentium superbarum arefecit Dominus ». — Et *invidiae;* Iacobi primo: « Exortus est sol cum ardore et arefecit foenum »; et in Psalmo: « Fiant sicut foenum tectorum ». — Et *irae,* de qua Marci nono[^p139-13]
<!-- page 140 -->
de daemoniaco, qui « spumat et stridet dentibus suis et arescit ». — Et *accidiae;* Proverbiorum decimo septimo: « Spiritus tristis exsiccat ossa »; Numerorum undecimo: « Arida est anima nostra » « nauseat super hoc cibo levissimo ». — Et *avaritiae;* Ecclesiastici decimo quarto[^p140-1]: « Insatiabilis est oculus cupidi, in parte iniquitatis non satiabitur, donec consumet arefaciens animam suam ». — Et *gulae;* Nahum primo: « Convivium pariter potantium consumetur quasi stipula ariditate plena ». — Et *luxuriae;* Psalmus[^p140-2]: « Ossa mea sicut cremium aruerunt, percussus sum ut foenum, et aruit cor meum » etc.; « Omnis caro foenum », Isaiae quadragesimo; et Iob trigesimo: « Cutis mea denigrata est super me, et ossa mea aruerunt ».

22. Sic igitur ex *septem causis* accipitur *ariditas spiritualis,* quae etiam est septiformis: scilicet *ariditas cordis* ad bene *cogitandum;* Isaiae vigesimo primo[^p140-3]: « Emarcuit cor meum, tenebrae stupefecerunt me ». — Et *ariditas oris* ad *desiderandum;* Iudith septimo: « Erit finis noster brevis in ore gladii, qui longior efficitur in ariditate sitis ». — Et *verbi* ad *docendum;* Osee nono[^p140-4]: « Da eis, Domine, vulvam sine liberis et ubera arentia ». — Et *ariditas brachii* ad *operandum;* Zachariae undecimo: « Brachium eius ariditate siccabitur ». — Et *ariditas manus* ad *largiendum;* Iob decimo quinto: « Antequam dies eius impleatur, peribit, et manus eius arescet. — Et *cutis* ad *conversandum;* Iob septimo[^p140-5]: « Cutis mea aruit et contracta est ». — Est postremo *ariditas virtutis* ad *sustinendum;* Psalmus: « Aruit tanquam testa virtus mea, et lingua mea adhaesit faucibus meis ».

23. Hic autem aegrotus habens manum aridam significat *ariditatem avaritiae,* quae sanatur, cum divino imperio manus extenditur ad eleemosynam largiendam, secundum illud Proverbiorum ultimo[^p140-6]: « Manum suam aperuit inopi et palmas suas extendit ad pauperem ». Et illa *extensio* impetrat vigorem gratiae, secundum illud Ecclesiastici decimo septimo: « Eleemosyna viri quasi sacculus cum ipso, et gratiam hominis quasi pupillam conservabit ». — Exemplum autem et experimentum apparet manifeste in quodam rege Angliae[^p140-7], qui faciebat eleemosynas largas, cuius manus et brachium manet integrum et incorruptum, dicente sibi quadam die quodam episcopo, cum extendisset manum, ut daret pauperi scutellam argenteam, quam ante se habebat, nunquam manum eius siccandam; quod verbum impletum dicitur esse usque in praesentem diem.

## English
<!-- page 134 -->

### Chapter VI.

1. *And it came to pass on the sabbath.* Above[^p134-8] the Evangelist treated of the calling of the disciples; in this part he treats of *their defence,* because it belongs to a good shepherd not only to gather the sheep, but also to defend them from the wolves[^p134-9]. And this part has two. In the first he defends them *by the testimony of Scripture;* but in the second, by a miracle of power, there[^p134-10]: *And it came to pass also on another sabbath.* For Christ himself is « the power of God and the wisdom of God »; whence the disciples called him « Master and Lord », John, the thirteenth chapter; and therefore first he defends them as *Master* through authentic testimony, secondly, as *Lord* through a miraculous work.

Now concerning the first mode of defending three things are introduced: the first is the *occasion of accusing,* the second is the *opposing of the accusation*[^p134-11], the third is the *confutation of the accusers.*

2. (Vers. 1.). The *occasion of accusing,* then, was given by the necessity of the disciples, on account of which they plucked ears on the sabbaths. And he notes this when he says: *And it came to pass on the second first sabbath,* that is, second from the first, that is, immediately following the preceding sabbath, of which there was treatment above, in the fourth chapter[^p134-12], on which he cured the demoniac. Or, as Chrysostom says, on the *second first* sabbath, that is, twice
<!-- page 135 -->
first or principal, on account of a twofold solemnity concurring, because the sabbath was of itself a solemnity, and also by reason of the *New Moon,* which was then. Or according to Bede[^p135-1] *second first* is one word compounded from a whole and a corrupted, so that it may be called *second-first,* that is, preferred to the second, that is, higher and worthier than that legal sabbath, because already then the new sabbath was beginning, on which we must keep holiday from vices, not from necessary works.

3. And this is plain through the example of the disciples themselves, of whom it is added: *As he went through the corn fields; he went,* namely Christ himself, because he was a wayfarer; whence Acts, the tenth chapter[^p135-2]: « He went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed ». And in his passing he signifies that all these things are passing, according to that of Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter: « Heaven and earth shall pass ». And *he went through,* he did not remain, and his disciples likewise, so that they themselves also might confess this, that « we have not here a lasting city », to the Hebrews, the last chapter. — But since, in passing through these things, we must not linger, but we need to be sustained; therefore he adds: *His disciples plucked the ears, and did eat, rubbing them in their hands*[^p135-3]; *they plucked,* that is, they plucked off, tearing them from the stalk, according to what is said in Amos, the seventh chapter: « I am a herdsman plucking wild figs ». *They plucked,* he says, they did not reap, because the one was granted according to the Law, the other forbidden; Deuteronomy, the twenty-third chapter: « If thou go into thy friend's corn, thou shalt break the ears, and rub them in thy hands: but thou shalt not reap with a sickle ». And this indeed they did, driven by the necessity of hunger, as, in Matthew, the twelfth chapter[^p135-4], « his disciples being hungry, began to pluck the ears, and to eat ». Nor is it a wonder, because, in Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter, « a soul that is hungry will take even bitter for sweet »; and Job, the sixth chapter: « The things which my soul would not touch, now through anguish are my meats ».

4. Whence *human necessity* excused them, which compelled them to do this; and Bede[^p135-5] assigns this reason: « Not having, he says, room for eating, the disciples, because of the importunity of the multitudes, were hungry as men, but plucking the ears they consoled their want of food ». — Another reason is the *necessity of the poverty they had assumed,* by which they were needy, so that they had not bread sufficiently; whence the Apostle in the second to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter[^p135-6]: « In hunger and thirst, in cold and nakedness ». — The third reason is the *example of austerity,* by which they taught us, as Ambrose[^p135-7] says, « to seek not prepared dishes, but simple foods »; Isaias, the thirtieth chapter: « The Lord will give you spare bread, and short water ». — The fourth is, that they might *show* that we must observe the legal sabbath not according to the letter, but *spiritually,* against heretics who were to come after; to the Hebrews, the fourth chapter[^p135-8]: « There remaineth a sabbatism for the people of God ». — The fifth reason is the *spiritual intelligence.* For by the *sabbath* is understood the leisure of divine contemplation; Isaias, the fifty-eighth chapter[^p135-9]: « Thou shalt call the sabbath of the Lord delightful ». The *ears* are foretastes of the interior sweetness, which are mixed with roughnesses, and therefore *they are rubbed in the hands,* that the pure grain may be drawn out, like Ruth, who « gathered the ears remaining behind the backs of the reapers ». — The sixth reason is on the part of the *sweetness of the word of Christ,* by which, strongly allured, they forget to eat; whence the Psalm[^p135-10]: « How sweet are thy words to my palate! more than honey to my mouth »; and John, the sixth chapter: « The words that I have spoken to you, are spirit and life ».

5. (Vers. 2.). But the *objection of the accusation* was brought in by the perversity of the Pharisees, by which they gladly insulted the disciples. Which he notes when he adds: *And some*
<!-- page 136 -->
*Pharisees*[^p136-1] *said to them: Why do you that which is not lawful on the sabbaths?* because it was a precept in the Law, Exodus, the sixteenth and the thirty-fifth chapter, and Leviticus, the twenty-third chapter, that the sabbath be observed most diligently. And therefore they reproved them as transgressors of the divine law, as they did their Master; John, the ninth chapter: « This man is not of God, who keepeth not the sabbath ». Whence they reckoned them worthy of death, because, according to that of the Hebrews, the tenth chapter[^p136-2], « one making void the law of Moses dieth without any mercy under two or three witnesses », and most of all as regards the commandment of the sabbath, which was especially to be observed; whence he who was found gathering sticks on the sabbath was put to death, as is said in Numbers, the fifteenth chapter: « Let that man die the death, let all the multitude overwhelm him with stones without the camp ».

6. Now this accusation came partly from *ignorance of the Law,* partly from *pride of mind,* partly from *the pretence of hypocrisy,* partly from *the superstition of tradition.* — From *ignorance of the Law,* because the taking of necessity was not forbidden, but the working of servitude. And therefore to such it is said in Matthew, the twenty-second chapter[^p136-3]: « You err, not knowing the Scriptures, nor the power of God ». — From *pride of mind,* because they despised others, as is said below, in the eighteenth chapter, of the Pharisee who boasted himself: « O God, I give thee thanks that I am not as the rest of men »; to the Romans, the tenth chapter: « Not knowing the justice of God, and seeking to establish their own, they have not submitted themselves to the justice of God ». — From *the pretence of hypocrisy,* according to that of Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p136-4]: « Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, who tithe mint, and have left the weightier things of the Law ». — From *the superstition of tradition,* in which they set greater force than in the Law; whence it is said to them in Matthew, the fifteenth chapter: « Why do you also transgress the commandment of God for your tradition »? — But this accusation came most of all from *ignorance of the Law,* by which the perversity of the Pharisees was inflamed.

7. (Vers. 3.). But the *confutation of the accusers* was subjoined by the authority of the Truth, and he notes this when he adds: *And Jesus answering them, said.* Now the Truth confounds the Pharisees and defends the disciples *by the authority of Scripture* and also *by his own.* — By the *authority of Scripture* he shows that the commandment of the sabbath did not bind in a case of necessity; and he shows this by the example of David, which he intimates when he says: *Have you not read this?* and if you have read, you have not *understood;* Job, the fourth chapter[^p136-5]: « Because no one understandeth, they shall perish for ever »; or if you have read, you have not *remembered,* according to that of Ezechiel, the seventh chapter: « The law shall perish from the priest, and counsel from the ancients », as if to say: you, who are zealous for the Law, ought to have read it, not to be ignorant of it, lest that of John, the third chapter, could be said to you: « Art thou a master in Israel, and knowest not these things »? And truly they ought to have read it, because the deed was very notable, namely *what David did, when himself was hungry, and they that were with him*[^p136-6]. — But the contrary of this saying seems to be in the first of Kings, the twenty-first chapter: « Why art thou alone, and no man with thee »? But he says that because he had no noble company, since he was « the king's son in law[^p136-7] » and a great prince. Whence it is added in the same place that « the vessels of the young men were holy ». He had company, then, though not so great or notable; and these were his companions in the deed, so that they cannot say that the deed was done by reason of a special privilege.

8. (Vers. 4.). Whence he adds: *How he went into the house of God,* that is, into the tabernacle, because the house of God, that is, the temple, was not yet built; the first of Paralipomenon, the seventeenth chapter[^p136-8]: « Thou shalt not build me a house to dwell in. For I have not remained in a house from the time that I brought out Israel from Egypt, to this day ». — Nor did he only enter into the house, that is, the tabernacle, but, what is more, *he took the loaves*[^p136-9] *of proposition, took* them namely from the priest, *and ate.* And of these loaves Exodus, the twenty-fifth chapter: « Thou shalt set upon the table loaves of proposition in my sight always », which are called *of proposition* because they were set forth upon the table every sabbath. — And what is more: *He ate, and they that were with him*[^p136-10], as is said in the first of Kings, the twenty-first chapter: « The priest gave David hallowed bread; for there was no bread there, but only the loaves of proposition », against the Law; whence he adds: *Which it was not lawful to eat but only for the priests;* whence it is said in Leviticus, the twenty-second chapter, that « the sojourner also of the priest, and the hired servant, ought not to eat of the sanctified things ». But in Leviticus, the twenty-fourth chapter, it is said of the loaves of proposition that they were « Aaron's and his sons', that they may eat them in the holy place, because it is the Holy
<!-- page 137 -->
of holies by an everlasting covenant ». If, then, David did not sin, because necessity excused him from that precept, likewise these too, who were compelled by necessity, are not to be reprehended, because they are excused through the necessity of hunger.

9. (Vers. 5.). And lest anything be wanting to the authority of Scripture, he subjoins *his own authority,* through which he both willed and was able to dispense with them. Which he notes when he adds: *And he said to them, that*[^p137-1] *the Son of man is lord also of the sabbath.* Now he calls himself *Son of man* as regards the *humility of the humanity he assumed,* and he calls himself *Lord of the sabbath* and of the Law as regards the *sublimity of the hidden Divinity;* whence in the Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter[^p137-2], it is said that « he had on his garment and on his thigh written: King of kings and Lord of lords », And therefore it is said in the Psalm: « Thou hast subjected all things under his feet »; this is said of the Son of man « made a little lower than the angels » on account of the ignominy of the passion. And therefore as Lord he dispensed with them for a time, he who was afterwards to take away its observance.

10. *And it came to pass on another sabbath* etc. Above[^p137-3] he defended the disciples as Master by the authority or testimony of Scripture; here he defends them as *Lord* by virtue *of power in a miracle,* in which, by healing a man on the sabbath, he shows that he is lord of the sabbath, and that on the sabbath one need not abstain from every work, but from evil only. Concerning the description of this miracle three things are intimated: the first is the *opportuneness of the miraculous work;* the second is the *sublimity of the marvellous worker,* there[^p137-4]: *But he knew their thoughts;* the third is the *perversity of the wretched people,* there: *And they were filled with folly.*

Now as regards the *opportuneness of the miraculous work* it must be noted that it is gathered from three things, namely from the *confirmation of his own doctrine,* from the *relieving of human misery* and from the *confutation*[^p137-5] *of Jewish perfidy.* The first belonged to wisdom, the second to clemency, the third to justice, which are in the almighty Christ.

11. (Vers. 6.). First, then, *his own doctrine was to be confirmed,* which he made public on the sabbaths; on account of which he says: *And it came to pass also on another sabbath, that he entered into the synagogue and taught:* because, as he says in John, the eighteenth chapter[^p137-6], « I have always taught in the synagogues and in the temple, whither all the Jews resort »; because that of Isaias, the fifty-fourth chapter, was to be fulfilled in Christ: « I will make all thy children taught of the Lord ». Whence this was he of whom Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-ninth chapter[^p137-7]: « He shall make manifest the discipline of his doctrine, and shall glory in the law of the covenant of the Lord ». For he taught justice, which is a common virtue; whence Osee, the tenth chapter: « The time to seek the Lord, when he shall come that shall teach you justice ».

12. *Human misery was to be relieved* on account of the penury of infirmity, by reason of which he says: *And there was a man there, and his right hand was withered;* and so he was unfit for all works, because he had lost the stronger hand and the better member. Whence truly this man was he of whom Job, the fourteenth chapter[^p137-8], that « living for a short time, he is filled with many miseries ». Whence already in this man that of Zacharias, the eleventh chapter, had been fulfilled: « His right arm shall be dried up with aridity »; and therefore he could already groan and say to the Lord: « My power is dried up like a potsherd »; and on that account it was to be relieved through the divine power.

13. (Vers. 7.). *Jewish perfidy* was no less *to be confuted* on account of the watching of malignity, by reason of which he says: *And the scribes and Pharisees watched, if he would cure on the sabbath.* Whence that of the Psalm was fulfilled in them[^p137-9]: « All their thoughts are unto evil; they will dwell and hide themselves; they will watch my heel ». — This watching was insidious, as regards the malignant mind, according to that of Jeremias, the ninth chapter: « With his mouth he speaketh peace with his friend, and secretly he layeth snares for him ». And therefore it is added: *That they might find whereof to accuse him*[^p137-10]. Whence these men said that of the Babylonians, Daniel, the sixth chapter: « We shall not find any occasion against this man, unless perhaps concerning the law of his God ». On this day, then, they watched especially, as Bede[^p137-11] says, « that, if he did not cure, they might reprove him of cruelty or of weakness; but if he cured, of transgression ». But surely they find neither, because, when he cures, he shows himself merciful; and when he does not cure, just; whence John, the ninth chapter[^p137-12]: « Unless this man were of God, he could not do any thing ». And therefore Jeremias, the second chapter: « What iniquity have your fathers found in me, that they are gone far from me »? as if to say: nothing, because he is the one of whom Isaias,
<!-- page 138 -->
the fifty-third chapter: « He did no sin, neither was guile found in his mouth ». Therefore he himself said in John, the eighth chapter: « Which of you shall convince me of sin? If I say the truth to you, why do you not believe me »?

14. (Vers. 8.). *But he knew their thoughts.* After the opportuneness of the miraculous work there is here added the *sublimity of the marvellous worker* as regards three things, namely as regards *wisdom in discerning* in the heart, *prudence in questioning* in speech and *efficacy in curing* in work[^p138-1].

First, then, through *wisdom in the heart* he detects their thoughts; which he notes when he says: *But he knew their thoughts;* and truly, because he is the most wise Lord; whence in the Psalm[^p138-2]: « The Lord knoweth the thoughts of men, that they are vain ». And therefore their thoughts were open to the eyes of God; on account of which he says in Isaias, the first chapter: « Take away the evil of your thoughts from my eyes »; whence also John, the sixteenth chapter: « His disciples said to him: Now we know that thou knowest all things, and thou needest not that any man should ask thee ». Nor is it a wonder, because he is the one of whom Jeremias, the seventeenth chapter[^p138-3]: « I the Lord who search the hearts and the reins, who give to every one according to his way ».

15. In testimony of which thing, to show himself the knower of hearts, he bids this man who was to be cured to rise up into the midst, when he adds: *And he said to the man who had the withered hand;* on whose account they were thinking proud thoughts, so that that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-seventh chapter[^p138-4], could be said to them: « O most wicked presumption, whence wast thou created to cover the dry land with malice »? *Arise, and stand forth in the midst,* that is, in a conspicuous place, that all may know that their thoughts have been detected, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the first chapter[^p138-5]: « Come not to him with a double heart »; « lest God discover thy secrets, and cast thee down in the midst of the congregation »; because, according to what is said in Proverbs, the twenty-sixth chapter, « he that covereth hatred deceitfully, his malice shall be made manifest in the council ». — *And rising he stood forth,* not fearing the indignation of the Pharisees. For he said within himself that of Job, the seventeenth chapter[^p138-6]: « Deliver me, O Lord, and set me beside thee, and let any man's hand fight against me »; and again that of the Psalm: « If armies in camp should stand together against me, my heart shall not fear »; whence, strengthened at the Lord's command, he stood secure. Nor is it a wonder, because that of Job, the fourth chapter[^p138-7], can rightly be said to him: « Behold thou hast taught many, and thou hast strengthened the weary hands »; whence this man could say that of Daniel, the tenth chapter: « And when he spoke with me, I grew strong, and I said: Speak, my lord, because thou hast strengthened me ».

16. (Vers. 9.). But secondly, through *prudence in speech* he turned aside their machinations; on account of which it is added: *And Jesus said*[^p138-8] *to them: I ask you.* — But the contrary of this seems to be said in Matthew, the twelfth chapter, where it is said that they « asked the Lord: Is it lawful to cure on the sabbath »? — To which Augustine responds, in the *Harmony of the Evangelists*[^p138-9], that first they asked, and the Lord replied to them. Or it may be that the form of a question follows the interior thought. Matthew understands that in the ears of God *to think* is to speak, and *to doubt* is to ask. — And he puts a cautious question: *If it be lawful on the sabbath*[^p138-10] *to do good, or evil; to save a soul, or to destroy?* He asks this not as something doubtful, but as something undoubtedly true, so that one part might be granted. For it is established that it is lawful on the sabbaths *to do good,* or *not to do good; to save* a soul, or *not to save* it, because between contradictory opposites there is no middle[^p138-11]. But *not to do good,* when there is place and opportunity, is to neglect good, and this is to do evil. Therefore this is a certain and true disjunction, of which it is necessary to grant one part; but they cannot grant this part: it is lawful to do evil, or to destroy a soul: it remains, therefore, that by this question they are convicted that it is lawful on the sabbath to do good and to cure a soul; and so they could not answer. Whence that of Job, the ninth chapter[^p138-12], was fulfilled: « If he question on a sudden, who shall answer him? Or who can say to him: Why dost thou so »? And therefore to the Romans, the ninth chapter: « O man! who art thou, that thou answerest God »? Whence it is said in Job, the ninth chapter, that « he cannot answer him one for a thousand ». But in Matthew, the twelfth chapter[^p138-13], this disjunction is not so put, but one part is asked and proved *from the lesser* through the freeing of a sheep from a pit; much more, then, is a man to be freed, of whom in the Psalm: « Thou hast subjected all things under his feet » etc.

<!-- page 139 -->
17. (Vers. 10.). But thirdly, through *efficacy in work* he cast down their presumptions; on account of which it is subjoined: *And looking round about on all,* as if already confounded, so that he could now say that of Job, the seventeenth chapter[^p139-1]: « Therefore be you converted, and come, and I shall not find among you any wise man ». — And because no one could contradict, for the confirmation of the truth it is added: *He said to the man: Stretch forth thy hand.* — And since « his word is full of power », as is said in Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter[^p139-2]; therefore it is added: *And he stretched it forth, and his hand was restored* to health; in which the Lord showed that he is lord of the Law and of the sabbath. For he it was who, in Exodus, the fourth chapter[^p139-3], healed the hand of Moses by command alone; he it was also who dried up the hand of Jeroboam, idolatrous king of Israel, and healed it again, as is said in the third of Kings, the thirteenth chapter; he, I say, is the one of whom it is said that « he woundeth, and cureth: he striketh, and his hands shall heal »; of whom in the Psalm: « Who is merciful to all thy iniquities, who healeth all thy infirmities »; and this by word alone, that it may be made manifest that he is the one of whom Wisdom, the sixteenth chapter[^p139-4]: « Thy word, O Lord, healed them, which healeth all things ». For just as the Father made all things through the uncreated Word, so through the incarnate Word, as Augustine says, he remade all things.

18. (Vers. 11.). *And they were filled with folly.* Here thirdly is touched the *perversity of the wretched people,* which was hardened in evil by that by which it ought to have been raised up to good. Now this perversity is shown in three things, namely in the *blinding of the heart,* the *conspiring of the mouth* and the *persecution of the deed.*

As regards the *blinding of the heart* it is said: *And they were filled with folly,* that is, with the blindness of malice; Wisdom, the second chapter[^p139-5]: « These things they thought, and erred, for their malice blinded them ». With this indeed they were filled and were made fools by the just judgement of God, according to that of the Romans, the first chapter: « God delivered them up to a reprobate sense »; « being filled with all iniquity, malice, avarice, wickedness; full of envy, murder, deceit, malignity ». By this envy indeed they were blinded, because[^p139-6] « he is wise to whom things savour as they are »; but the envious man is tormented by another's happiness. Such were these men; whence that of Baruch, the third chapter, fitted them: « Because they had not wisdom, they perished through their folly ». But this fullness of folly is a true *emptiness,* because[^p139-7] « *riches of salvation,* wisdom and knowledge »; therefore *from opposites* folly is *want;* therefore it is said in Proverbs, the nineteenth chapter: « Better is the poor man, that walketh in his simplicity, than a rich man that is perverse in his lips, and unwise ».

19. But as regards the *conspiring of the mouth* it is added: *And they talked one with another,* provoking one another to evil by mutual speech. So they did in John, the eleventh chapter[^p139-8]: « The chief priests and the Pharisees gathered a council, and said: What do we » etc. This counsel went before in figure in the brothers of Joseph; Genesis, the thirty-seventh chapter: « They said one to another: Behold the dreamer cometh; come, let us kill him ». This counsel was impious; therefore Genesis, the forty-ninth chapter: « Let not my soul go into their counsel »; Jeremias, the eighteenth chapter: « Thou knowest all their counsel against me unto death; forgive not their iniquities ».

20. As regards the *persecution of the deed* it is added: *What they might do concerning*[^p139-9] *Jesus,* that is, how they might deliver him to death; which they also did, though not at once, according to that of John, the seventh chapter: « No man laid hands on him, because his hour was not yet come ». But nevertheless afterwards they did it, as is said in Acts, the third chapter: « The author of life you killed, and desired a murderer to be granted unto you ». Whence the Saviour himself could say that of Jeremias, the eleventh chapter[^p139-10]: « But thou, O Lord, hast shewn me, and I have known their counsels. And I was as a meek lamb, that is carried to be a victim, and I knew not that they had devised counsels, saying: Let us put wood on his bread » etc.

21. *Spiritually* it must be understood that *aridity* is in the soul a defect of vigour on account of the absence of grace, according to that of John, the fifteenth chapter[^p139-11]: « If any one abide not in me, he shall be cast forth as a branch, and shall wither ». Now this aridity is brought in from seven causes, according to the seven capital sins. Whence note that there is an aridity *of pride,* or proceeding from pride; Ecclesiasticus, the tenth chapter[^p139-12]: « The Lord hath dried up the roots of proud nations ». — And *of envy;* James, the first chapter: « The sun rose with a burning heat, and dried up the grass »; and in the Psalm: « Let them be as grass upon the tops of houses ». — And *of anger,* of which Mark, the ninth chapter[^p139-13],
<!-- page 140 -->
of the demoniac, who « foameth, and gnasheth with his teeth, and withereth ». — And *of sloth;* Proverbs, the seventeenth chapter: « A sorrowful spirit drieth up the bones »; Numbers, the eleventh chapter: « Our soul is dry » « loatheth this very light food ». — And *of avarice;* Ecclesiasticus, the fourteenth chapter[^p140-1]: « The eye of the covetous man is insatiable; in his portion of iniquity he will not be satisfied, till he consume his own soul, drying it up ». — And *of gluttony;* Nahum, the first chapter: « The banquet of them that drink together shall be consumed as stubble full of aridity ». — And *of lust;* the Psalm[^p140-2]: « My bones are dried up like fuel for the fire, I am smitten as grass, and my heart is withered » etc.; « All flesh is grass », Isaias, the fortieth chapter; and Job, the thirtieth chapter: « My skin is become black upon me, and my bones are dried up ».

22. Thus, then, from *seven causes* is taken *spiritual aridity,* which is also sevenfold: namely the *aridity of the heart* for *thinking* well; Isaias, the twenty-first chapter[^p140-3]: « My heart withered, darkness amazed me ». — And the *aridity of the mouth* for *desiring;* Judith, the seventh chapter: « Our end shall be short by the mouth of the sword, which is made longer in the aridity of thirst ». — And *of the word* for *teaching;* Osee, the ninth chapter[^p140-4]: « Give them, O Lord, a womb without children, and dry breasts ». — And the *aridity of the arm* for *working;* Zacharias, the eleventh chapter: « His arm shall be dried up with aridity ». — And the *aridity of the hand* for *bestowing;* Job, the fifteenth chapter: « Before his days be full, he shall perish, and his hand shall wither. — And *of the skin* for *conversing;* Job, the seventh chapter[^p140-5]: « My skin is withered and drawn together ». — Lastly there is the *aridity of power* for *enduring;* the Psalm: « My power is dried up like a potsherd, and my tongue hath cleaved to my jaws ».

23. Now this sick man having a withered hand signifies the *aridity of avarice,* which is healed when at the divine command the hand is stretched forth to bestow alms, according to that of Proverbs, the last chapter[^p140-6]: « She hath opened her hand to the needy, and stretched out her palms to the poor ». And that *stretching forth* obtains the vigour of grace, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the seventeenth chapter: « The alms of a man is as a purse with him, and shall preserve the grace of a man as the apple of the eye ». — But an example and experiment appears manifestly in a certain king of England[^p140-7], who gave large alms, whose hand and arm remain whole and incorrupt, a certain bishop having said to him one day, when he had stretched forth his hand to give a poor man the silver dish which he had before him, that his hand would never wither; which word is said to have been fulfilled even to the present day.
