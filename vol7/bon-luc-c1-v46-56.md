# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. I, Vers. 46-56
## *Secundo subditur canticum laetitiae*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 30 -->

#### Secundo subditur canticum laetitiae.

86. *Et ait Maria* etc. Post testimonium prophetiae sequitur *cantus laetitiae,* in quo beatissima virgo Maria laudat divinam clementiam propter excellentissimam gratiam sibi collatam. In hoc autem cantico tria notantur. Primum est *laudantis affectio,* quam notat ibi[^p30-8]: *Magnificat anima mea* etc. Secundum est *laudandi ratio,* quam notat, cum subdit: *Quia respexit* etc. Tertium, *divinae laudis amplificatio,* quam notat ibi: *Et misericordia eius a progenie.* Non enim est perfecta laus, nisi adsit debitus *affectus,* debita *causa* et debitus *modus.*

87. (Vers. 46.). Insinuatur ergo primo *laudantis affectio,* ut *grata,* magnificando divinam virtutem, cum dicit: *Ait Maria: Magnificat anima mea Dominum,* id est divinam virtutem. « Magnus enim Dominus noster, et magna virtus eius[^p30-9] »; et ideo magno affectu debet collaudari, quia magna operatur; Danielis quarto: « Ego laudo et magnifico et glorifico Regem caeli, quia omnia opera eius vera »; et ad hoc invitat anima devota in Psalmo: « Magnificate Dominum mecum, et exaltemus nomen eius in id ipsum ». Tunc anima nostra vere magnificat Dominum, quando sub ipso se ipsam captivat et humiliat; Ecclesiastici tertio[^p30-10]: « Quam magna potentia Dei solius, et a solis humilibus honoratur ». Unde virgo Maria, quia prae ceteris se ipsam humiliavit, ceteris altius Dominum magnificavit. Et ideo a magnificatione incepit, quia hoc competebat *humilitati Virginis de-
<!-- page 31 -->
cantantis.* — Competebat etiam *dignitati Dei,* cuius summa dignatio non cognoscitur, nisi dignitas praecognoscatur; unde Apostolus[^p31-1]: « Qui cum in forma Dei esset, non rapinam arbitratus est, esse se aequalem Deo, sed semetipsum exinanivit, formam servi accipiens ». — Competebat etiam *intentioni cantici,* quae est Deum perfecte laudare; Ecclesiastici quadragesimo tertio[^p31-2]: « Glorificantes Dominum, quantumcumque potueritis; supervalebit adhuc, et admirabilis magnificentia eius ». — Competebat etiam *Mariae cantico,* quia de magnis, immo de maximis beneficiis erat; unde Proverbiorum octavo[^p31-3]: « Audite, quoniam de rebus magnis locutura sum »; et infra: « Quia fecit mihi magna » etc. — Competebat et *ordinato progressui,* qui est a timore inchoando; unde Psalmus: « Initium sapientiae timor Domini ». Timor autem reverentiae Dominum magnificat.

88. (Vers. 47.). Insinuatur etiam *laudantis affectio* non solum ut grata, sed ut *iucunda,* exsultando in divina salute, cum subditur: *Et exsultavit spiritus meus in Deo salutari meo,* id est in Deo salvatore, ut possit dicere illud Psalmi[^p31-4]: « Anima mea exsultabit in Domino et delectabitur super salutari suo »; et rursum: « Cor meum et caro mea exsultaverunt in Deum vivum ». Vere iam dicere poterat illud Canticorum primo: « Introduxit me rex in cellaria sua; exsultabimus et laetabimur in te » etc. Iam verificatum est in Maria, quod optabat Psalmista[^p31-5]: « Exsultent et laetentur in te omnes quaerentes te et dicant semper: Magnificetur Dominus, qui diligunt salutare tuum ». Quoniam ergo virgo Maria Dominum *quaesivit* et *salutare dilexit;* ideo anima eius Dominum *magnificavit,* et spiritus eius in salutari Dei *exsultavit.* Ideo sibi competit illud Annae, primi Regum secundo: « Exsultavit cor meum in Domino, et exaltatum est cornu meum in Deo meo; quia laetata sum in salutari tuo ».

89. Et nota, quod *spiritus* accipitur pro *substantia* animae; Ecclesiastae tertio[^p31-6]: « Quis novit, si spiritus » etc.; pro *superiori parte;* Malachiae secundo: « Custodite spiritum vestrum »; et ad Romanos octavo: « Spiritus postulat pro nobis gemitibus inenarrabilibus »; pro *imaginatione;* primae ad Corinthios decimo quarto: « Nam si orem lingua, spiritus meus orat » etc. Hic autem accipitur pro anima secundum *suum supremum,* et *anima* accipitur in comparatione ad corpus, sicut illud primae ad Thessalonicenses quinto[^p31-7]: « Ut integer spiritus vester et anima et corpus sine querela in adventu Domini nostri Iesu Christi servetur ». Quia enim *spiritus* interius exsultaverat, ideo *anima* voce magnificabat; et ideo *exsultationem* de praeterito et *magnificationem* de praesenti ponit, quia exsultatio est natura prior.

90. (Vers. 48.). *Quia respexit humilitatem* etc. Postquam descripta est laudantis affectio sive devotio, subditur hic *laudandi ratio,* quae sumitur ex duplici causa. Prima est *beneficium gratiae,* quod fecit ipsam *amabilem Deo* et *laudabilem hominibus.* Quia *amabilem Deo,* dicit: *Quia respexit humilitatem ancillae suae.* Genesis vigesimo nono[^p31-8] praecessit huius figura in Lia, quae, concepta prole, dixit: « Vidit Dominus humilitatem meam; nunc amabit me vir meus ». Et humilitas fuit dispositio ad respectum gratiae; Isaiae ultimo[^p31-9]: « Ad quem respiciam nisi ad pauperculum et contritum spiritu et trementem sermones meos »? quia, sicut dicitur in Psalmo, « excelsus Dominus et humilia respicit et alta a longe cognoscit ». Unde de viro iusto, Ecclesiastici undecimo: « Oculus Dei respexit illum in bono et erexit ab humilitate sua »; et ideo orabat Propheta in Psalmo: « Respice in me et miserere mei, quia unicus et pauper sum ego ».

91. Fecit etiam *laudabilem hominibus* illud gratiae beneficium, et propterea addit: *Ecce enim, ex hoc beatam me dicent omnes generationes,* tam viri quam mulieres; Proverbiorum ultimo[^p31-10]: « Surrexerunt filii eius et beatissimam praedicaverunt; vir eius et laudavit eam ». Fuit autem virgo Maria *beata* propter *meritum castitatis;* Psalmus: « Beati immaculati in via »; et Apocalypsis decimo sexto: « Beatus, qui custodit vestimenta sua, ne nudus ambulet, et videant turpitudinem eius »; et Sapientiae tertio: « Felix est sterilis et incoinquinata, quae nescivit torum in delicto ». — Fuit etiam *beatior* propter *consilium virginitatis;* primae ad Corinthios
<!-- page 32 -->
septimo[^p32-1]: « Beatior autem erit, si sic permanserit secundum meum consilium ». — Fuit etiam *beatissima* propter *privilegium fecunditatis,* propter quam ceterae mulieres *beatissimam* eam praedicant, Canticorum sexto: « Viderunt eam filiae Sion et beatissimam praedicaverunt ».

92. (Vers. 49.). Secunda ratio laudandi est *miraculum potentiae,* quod fuit *magnum* et *pium.* Quia *magnum,* dicit: *Quia fecit mihi magna qui potens est.* Hoc refertur ad incarnationis mysterium, sicut dicit Beda in Glossa[^p32-2]; et de hoc Iob quinto: « Qui facit magna et inscrutabilia et mirabilia absque numero ». Incarnationis mysterium est magnum et imperscrutabile, et ideo admirabile; et hoc *fecit qui potens est,* quia « in rebus mirabilibus, ut dicit Augustinus[^p32-3], tota ratio facti est potentia facientis »; Ecclesiastae octavo: « Omne, quod voluerit; faciet, quia sermo illius potestate plenus est, nec dicere ei quisquam potest: Quare ita facis »?

93. Fuit etiam *pium,* et propterea dicit: *Sanctum nomen eius.* Unde sancta et pia facit, ut sanctitatem ostendat nominis sui; Danielis tertio[^p32-4]: « Benedictum nomen gloriae tuae, quod est sanctum »; et Psalmus: « Sanctum et terribile nomen eius ». Et ratio huius praemittitur: « Redemptionem misit populo suo ». Et quia nomen eius decet sanctitudo, ideo oramus: « Sanctificetur nomen tuum », Matthaei sexto; et rursus Psalmus: « Domum tuam decet sanctitudo, Domine, in longitudine dierum ». De his duobus, scilicet *magnitudine* et *pietate,* primae ad Timotheum tertio[^p32-5]: « Et manifeste magnum est pietatis sacramentum, quod manifestatum est in carne, iustificatum est in spiritu, apparuit Angelis, praedicatum est gentibus, creditum est in mundo, assumtum est in gloria »; *magnum* quippe ratione Divinitatis, *sanctum* ratione assumtae humanitatis; supra eodem: « Quod nascetur ex te sanctum vocabitur Filius Dei ». Et ideo Virgo in conceptione fuit magnificata et sanctificata; Psalmus: « Sanctificavit tabernaculum suum Altissimus ». Et haec duo respondent duobus praemissis, quia magnificata et sanctificata, ideo magnificavit et exsultavit; ideo optime rationem reddit.

94. *Et misericordia eius* etc. Postquam descripta est laudantis affectio et laudandi ratio, subditur hic tertio divinae *laudis amplificatio.* Et haec consistit in commendatione divinae misericordiae quantum ad opus redemptionis humanae, iam in conceptione Virginis inchoatae. Opus autem nostrae redemptionis est declarativum *misericordiae* et *potentiae,* estque[^p32-6] manifestativum *largitatis* et *veritatis: misericordiae,* in relevatione hominis lapsi; *potentiae,* in prostratione diaboli; *largitatis,* in diffusione Spiritus sancti; et *veritatis,* in adimpletione promissi.

95. (Vers. 50.). Primo igitur laudat Dominum suum in opere redemptionis, secundum quod est manifestativum *misericordiae,* ibi: *Et misericordia eius a progenie in progenies timentibus eum;* ad Ephesios secundo[^p32-7]: « Deus, qui dives est in misericordia, propter nimiam caritatem suam, qua dilexit nos, et cum essemus mortui peccatis, convivificavit nos Christo »; et in Psalmo: « Misericordia autem Domini ab aeterno et usque in aeternum super timentes eum ». Hoc enim fit super *omnes* quantum ad beneficium *conservationis,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici decimo octavo[^p32-8]: « Miseratio hominis circa proximum suum, misericordia autem Dei super omnem carnem ». Misericordia tamen *liberans* et *salvans* ad solos *timentes* se extendit, sicut in Psalmo scriptum est: « Quam magna multitudo dulcedinis tuae, Domine, quam abscondisti timentibus te »! Ideo Ecclesiastici secundo: « Qui timetis Dominum, sperate in eum ».

96. (Vers. 51.). Secundo laudat eum in hoc opere, secundum quod est manifestativum *potentiae* in deiectione *daemonum superborum* et *divitum iniquorum.* Propter primum dicit: *Fecit potentiam in brachio suo, dispersit superbos mente cordis sui.* In hoc est laus Dei specialis; Iob quadragesimo[^p32-9]: « Disperge superbos furore tuo, et respiciens omnem arrogantem humilia ». Et sic impletum est illud Ecclesiastici decimo: « Sedes ducum superborum de-
<!-- page 33 -->
struxit Deus et sedere fecit mites pro eis ». Et dicitur hoc fecisse *in brachio,* id est in Filio; Isaiae quadragesimo[^p33-1]: « Ecce, Dominus Deus in fortitudine veniet, et brachium eius dominabitur ». *Superbos* autem *mente cordis* vocat illos qui superbiunt in intellectu per *praesumtionem* et in affectu per *ambitionem,* sicut lucifer et eius sequaces; Iob penultimo: « Omne sublime videt, et ipse est rex super omnes filios superbiae ». Et sic est sensus: *dispersit* eos qui superbiunt *mente cordis.* — Vel aliter: *Superbos dispersit mente cordis sui,* secundum quod dicitur, quia superbi iusto Dei iudicio habent cor dispersum; Sapientiae decimo septimo[^p33-2]: « Dum putant, se latere in obscuris peccatis, tenebroso oblivionis velamento dispersi sunt ». — Vel: *Dispersit superbos mente cordis sui,* id est definitione consilii sui; Iob quadragesimo: « Si habes brachium sicut Deus et si voce simili tonas? Disperge superbos in furore tuo, et respiciens omnem arrogantem humilia ».

97. (Vers. 52.). Daemones superbientes definitive proiecit, sed non sic homines; unde Threnorum tertio[^p33-3]: « Non enim humiliavit ex corde suo et abiecit filios hominum ». Laudatur ergo potentia in deiectione superborum daemonum et etiam *tyrannorum potentium,* cum subdit: *Deposuit potentes de sede et exaltavit humiles;* Danielis quinto[^p33-4] dicitur de Nabuchodonosor: « Quos volebat interficiebat, et quos volebat percutiebat, et quos volebat exaltabat, et quos volebat humiliabat. Sed quando elevatum est cor eius, et spiritus illius obfirmatus est ad superbiam; depositus est de solio regni sui, et gloria eius ablata est »; et Ecclesiastici decimo: « Radices gentium superbarum arefecit Deus et plantavit humiles ex ipsis gentibus ». Et *ratio* huius infra decimo octavo[^p33-5] redditur: « Quia omnis, qui se exaltat, humiliabitur; et qui se humiliat exaltabitur ». Et in hoc est laus divinae potentiae; primi Regum secundo: « Dominus pauperem facit et ditat, humiliat et sublevat. Suscitat de pulvere egenum et de stercore elevat pauperem, ut sedeat cum principibus et solium gloriae teneat ». Exemplum in Saul et David, Vasthi et Esther.

98. (Vers. 53.). Tertio laudat Dominum in praedicto opere, secundum quod est manifestativum *largitatis,* cum dicit: *Esurientes implevit bonis et divites dimisit inanes;* quia vera largitas est dare pauperibus, non divitibus; Proverbiorum decimo tertio[^p33-6]: « Iustus comedit et replet animam suam, venter autem impiorum insaturabilis »; et rursus in Psalmo: « Divites eguerunt et esurierunt, inquirentes autem Dominum non deficient omni bono »; et Isaiae sexagesimo quinto: « Ecce, servi mei comedent, et vos esurietis; ecce, servi mei bibent, et vos sitietis; ecce, servi mei laetabuntur, et vos confundemini »; quia, sicut dicitur primae ad Corinthios primo[^p33-7], « non multi sapientes secundum carnem, non multi potentes, non multi nobiles, sed quae stulta sunt mundi elegit Deus, ut confundat sapientes; et infirma mundi elegit Deus, ut confundat fortia, et ignobilia mundi et contemptibilia elegit Deus; et ea quae non sunt, ut ea quae sunt, destrueret, ut non glorietur omnis caro in conspectu eius »; et Psalmus: « Non salvatur rex per multam virtutem, et gigas non salvabitur in multitudine virtutis suae ».

99. (Vers. 54.). Quarto laudat Deum in praedicto opere, secundum quod est manifestativum *veritatis promissi,* cum dicit: *Suscepit Israel puerum suum, recordatus misericordiae suae;* ubi primo praemittitur *promissionis impletio,* quam promissionem fecit divina benignitas, et implevit divina veritas; unde Psalmus[^p33-8]: « Misericordia et veritas obviaverunt sibi, iustitia et pax osculatae sunt ». Et hoc factum est, quando carnem nostram suscepit; ad Hebraeos secundo: « Nusquam enim Angelos apprehendit, sed semen Abrahae »; Isaiae quadragesimo secundo: « Ecce, servus meus, suscipiam eum; electus meus, complacuit sibi in illo anima mea »; et Osee undecimo: « Puer Israel, et dilexi eum et ex Aegypto vocavi filium meum ». — Et hoc: *Recordatus misericordiae suae,* cuius non potest oblivisci; Psalmus[^p33-9]: « Aut obliviscetur misereri Deus? Aut continebit in ira sua misericordias suas »? et Isaiae quadragesimo nono: « Nunquid oblivisci potest mulier infantem suum, ut non misereatur filio uteri sui? Et si illa oblita fuerit, ego tamen non obliviscar tui ».

100. (Vers. 55.). Deinde subditur *vera promissio,* cum dicitur: *Sicut locutus est ad Patres nostros, Abraham et semini eius in saecula.* Et debet ista littera construi sic: *Sicut locutus est ad Patres,* et sicut locutus est *Abrahae* specialiter, *et semini eius,* ubi tangit statum naturae et legis scriptae. — Vel aliter, ut construatur cum praecedenti: *Suscepit Israel, recordatus misericordiae,* et hoc, dico, *Abrahae et semini eius, sicut locutus est ad Patres.* Simile habetur in Psalmo[^p33-10]: « Re-
<!-- page 34 -->
cordatus est misericordiae suae et veritatis suae domui Israel ». Inter *Patres* specialiter nominat Abraham, quia sibi primo facta est repromissio; Genesis duodecimo et vigesimo secundo: « Benedicam benedicentibus tibi et maledicam maledicentibus tibi, atque in te benedicentur universae cognationes terrae »; « Benedicam tibi et multiplicabo semen tuum sicut stellas caeli et velut arenam, quae est in littore maris; possidebit semen tuum portas inimicorum suorum, et benedicentur in semine tuo omnes gentes terrae, quia obedisti voci meae »; et ad Galatas tertio[^p34-1]: « Abrahae dictae sunt promissiones et semini eius ». *Semen* autem Abrahae sunt credentes; ad Romanos nono: « Non qui filii carnis, hi filii Dei; sed qui filii sunt promissionis aestimantur in semine »; unde infra tertio: « Potens est Deus de lapidibus istis suscitare filios Abrahae ». Usque *in saecula,* id est in aeternum; Psalmus[^p34-2]: « A saeculo et usque in saeculum tu es Deus ». — Unde Virgo beata canticum incipit a magnitudine summi principii et canticum finit in aeternitate finis; laudat enim eum qui « alpha est et omega, principium et finis », Apocalypsis primo; et quidem satis recte, quia in hoc cantico ostendit, consummationem esse factam omnium beneficiorum promissorum, et ideo consummativum est omnium laudum et canticorum et etiam Scripturarum.

#### Tertio subditur obsequium benevolentiae.

101. (Vers. 56.). *Mansit autem Maria* etc. Post testimonium prophetiae et canticum laetitiae subditur *obsequium benevolentiae,* quod exhibuit virgo Maria Elisabeth, cognatae suae; in quo commendatur a tribus, scilicet a *sedulitate obsequii,* a *longanimitate propositi,* ab *honestate contubernii.* — Propter *sedulitatem obsequii* dicit: *Mansit autem Maria cum illa,* scilicet cum cognata praegnante. Non statim abscessit, sed fecit, secundum quod Dominus discipulis suis praecipit Matthaei decimo[^p34-3]: « In eadem autem domo manete, donec exeatis ». *Manebat* autem Maria, non egrediebatur sicut Dina, de qua Genesis trigesimo quarto: « Egressa est, ut videret mulieres regionis illius »; et sequitur post, quod oppressa fuit. *Manebat* sicut sedula obsecutrix, non circuibat sicut curiosa investigatrix; primae ad Timotheum quinto, de viduis adolescentibus: « Otiosae discunt circuire domos, non solum autem otiosae, sed et verbosae et curiosae, loquentes quae non oportet ». Quocontra Seneca[^p34-4]: « Indicium mentis bene constitutae est, secum posse morari et secum posse consistere ».

102. Propter *longanimitatem propositi* subdit: *Quasi mensibus tribus,* id est usque ad tempus partus, secundum quod dicit Beda in Glossa[^p34-5]: « Tamdiu mansit Maria, donec, Elisabeth partus tempore completo, praecursoris Domini sui, propter quam maxime venerat, nativitatem videret »; et hoc significatum est secundi Regum sexto, ubi dicitur, quod « arca Domini mansit in domo Obededom tribus mensibus ». — *Mystice* vero tribus mensibus designatur profectus trium virtutum, secundum quod in Glossa[^p34-6] dicitur. Nam « anima casta, quae spiritualis verbi desiderium concipit, necesse est, ut alta exercitus caelestis iuga subeat et quasi trium mensium dies ibidem demorata, quousque fidei, spei et caritatis luce radietur, perseverare non desistat ».

103. Propter *honestatem contubernii* subditur: *Et reversa est in domum suam,* ut habitaret cum sponso, virginitatis suae custode; unde dicere poterat illud Genesis trigesimo[^p34-7]: « Benedixit tibi Dominus ad introitum meum. Iustum est igitur, ut aliquando provideam etiam domui meae ». *Reversa est in domum suam,* quia sponsus et familia sua reditum desiderabat, secundum illud Canticorum sexto: « Revertere, revertere Sulamitis, revertere ». Sic ergo virgo Maria dat exemplum omnibus imitandum, secundum quod dicit Ambrosius[^p34-8]: « Discite, inquit, a Maria religiositatem, longanimitatem et honestatem ».

## English
<!-- page 30 -->

#### Secondly the canticle of gladness is subjoined.

86. *And Mary said* etc. After the testimony of prophecy there follows *the song of gladness,* in which the most blessed virgin Mary praises the divine clemency on account of the most excellent grace conferred on her. Now in this canticle three things are noted. The first is *the affection of the one praising,* which he notes there[^p30-8]: *My soul doth magnify* etc. The second is *the reason for praising,* which he notes when he subjoins: *Because he hath regarded* etc. The third, *the amplification of the divine praise,* which he notes there: *And his mercy is from generation.* For praise is not perfect unless there be present the due *affection,* the due *cause* and the due *mode.*

87. (Vers. 46.). First, then, is insinuated *the affection of the one praising,* as *grateful,* by magnifying the divine power, when she says: *And Mary said: My soul doth magnify the Lord,* that is, the divine power. « For great is our Lord, and great is his power[^p30-9] »; and therefore he ought to be praised with great affection, because he works great things; Daniel, the fourth chapter: « I praise, and magnify, and glorify the King of heaven, because all his works are true »; and to this the devout soul invites in the Psalm: « O magnify the Lord with me; and let us extol his name together ». Then does our soul truly magnify the Lord, when under him it takes itself captive and humbles itself; Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter[^p30-10]: « How great is the power of God alone, and by the humble alone he is honoured ». Whence the virgin Mary, because she humbled herself before the rest, magnified the Lord more highly than the rest. And therefore she began from magnification, because this befitted *the humility of the Virgin singing* it.
<!-- page 31 -->
— It befitted also *the dignity of God,* whose supreme condescension is not known unless the dignity is first known; whence the Apostle[^p31-1]: « Who being in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant ». — It befitted also *the intention of the canticle,* which is to praise God perfectly; Ecclesiasticus, the forty-third chapter[^p31-2]: « Glorify the Lord as much as ever you can; he will yet far exceed, and his magnificence is wonderful ». — It befitted also *the canticle of Mary,* because it was of great, nay, of the greatest benefits; whence Proverbs, the eighth chapter[^p31-3]: « Hear, for I will speak of great things »; and below: « Because he hath done great things to me » etc. — It befitted also *the ordered progress,* which is by beginning from fear; whence the Psalm: « The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom ». And the fear of reverence magnifies the Lord.

88. (Vers. 47.). There is insinuated also *the affection of the one praising* not only as grateful, but as *joyful,* by exulting in the divine salvation, when it is subjoined: *And my spirit hath exulted in God my salvation,* that is, in God the Saviour, so that she can say that of the Psalm[^p31-4]: « My soul shall exult in the Lord, and shall be delighted in his salvation »; and again: « My heart and my flesh have exulted in the living God ». Truly she could now say that of the Canticle, the first chapter: « The king hath brought me into his storerooms; we will exult and rejoice in thee » etc. Now has been verified in Mary what the Psalmist wished[^p31-5]: « Let all that seek thee exult and be glad in thee, and let such as love thy salvation say always: The Lord be magnified ». Since, then, the virgin Mary *sought* the Lord and *loved* his *salvation,* therefore her soul *magnified* the Lord, and her spirit *exulted* in the salvation of God. Therefore there befits her that of Anna, the first of Kings, the second chapter: « My heart hath exulted in the Lord, and my horn is exalted in my God, because I have joyed in thy salvation ».

89. And note that *spirit* is taken for *the substance* of the soul; Ecclesiastes, the third chapter[^p31-6]: « Who knoweth if the spirit » etc.; for *the superior part;* Malachias, the second chapter: « Keep then your spirit »; and to the Romans, the eighth chapter: « The spirit asketh for us with unspeakable groanings »; for *the imagination;* the first to the Corinthians, the fourteenth chapter: « For if I pray in a tongue, my spirit prayeth » etc. Here, however, it is taken for the soul according to *its supreme part,* and *soul* is taken in comparison with the body, as that of the first to the Thessalonians, the fifth chapter[^p31-7]: « That your whole spirit, and soul, and body may be preserved blameless in the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ ». For because *the spirit* had exulted inwardly, therefore *the soul* magnified with the voice; and therefore she puts *the exultation* in the past and *the magnification* in the present, because exultation is prior by nature.

90. (Vers. 48.). *Because he hath regarded the humility* etc. After the affection or devotion of the one praising has been described, there is subjoined here *the reason for praising,* which is taken from a twofold cause. The first is *the benefit of grace,* which made her *lovable to God* and *praiseworthy to men.* Because it made her *lovable to God,* she says: *Because he hath regarded the humility of his handmaid.* In Genesis, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p31-8], the figure of this went before in Lia, who, having conceived offspring, said: « The Lord saw my humility; now my husband will love me ». And humility was the disposition for the regard of grace; Isaias, the last chapter[^p31-9]: « To whom shall I have regard, but to him that is poor and little, and of a contrite spirit, and that trembleth at my words »? because, as is said in the Psalm, « the Lord is high, and regardeth the low, and the high he knoweth afar off ». Whence of the just man, Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter: « The eye of God hath regarded him for good, and hath lifted him up from his humility »; and therefore the Prophet prayed in the Psalm: « Regard me, and have mercy on me; for I am alone and poor ».

91. That benefit of grace made her also *praiseworthy to men,* and therefore she adds: *For behold from henceforth all generations shall call me blessed,* men as well as women; Proverbs, the last chapter[^p31-10]: « Her children rose up, and preached her most blessed; her husband, and he praised her ». Now the virgin Mary was *blessed* on account of *the merit of chastity;* the Psalm: « Blessed are the undefiled in the way »; and the Apocalypse, the sixteenth chapter: « Blessed is he that keepeth his garments, lest he walk naked, and they see his shame »; and Wisdom, the third chapter: « Happy is the barren, and the undefiled, that hath not known bed in sin ». — She was also *more blessed* on account of *the counsel of virginity;* the first to the Corinthians, the
<!-- page 32 -->
seventh chapter[^p32-1]: « But more blessed shall she be, if she so remain, according to my counsel ». — She was also *most blessed* on account of *the privilege of fecundity,* on account of which the other women preach her *most blessed,* the Canticle, the sixth chapter: « The daughters of Sion saw her, and preached her most blessed ».

92. (Vers. 49.). The second reason for praising is *the miracle of power,* which was *great* and *pious.* Because it was *great,* she says: *Because he that is mighty hath done great things to me.* This refers to the mystery of the incarnation, as Bede says in the Gloss[^p32-2]; and of this Job, the fifth chapter: « Who doth great things and unsearchable and wonderful things without number ». The mystery of the incarnation is great and unsearchable, and therefore wonderful; and *he hath done* this *who is mighty,* because « in wonderful things, as Augustine says[^p32-3], the whole account of the deed is the power of the doer »; Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter: « All that he will, he shall do; because his word is full of power, neither can any man say to him: Why dost thou so »?

93. It was also *pious,* and therefore she says: *And holy is his name.* Whence he does holy and pious things, so as to show the holiness of his name; Daniel, the third chapter[^p32-4]: « Blessed is the name of thy glory, which is holy »; and the Psalm: « Holy and terrible is his name ». And the reason of this is put before it: « He hath sent redemption to his people ». And because holiness becometh his name, therefore we pray: « Hallowed be thy name », Matthew, the sixth chapter; and again the Psalm: « Holiness becometh thy house, O Lord, in length of days ». Of these two, namely *greatness* and *piety*[^tr-pietas], the first to Timothy, the third chapter[^p32-5]: « And evidently great is the sacrament of piety, which was manifested in the flesh, was justified in the spirit, appeared unto angels, hath been preached unto the Gentiles, is believed in the world, is taken up in glory »; *great,* to be sure, by reason of the Divinity, *holy* by reason of the humanity assumed; above, in the same chapter: « The Holy which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God ». And therefore the Virgin in the conception was magnified and sanctified; the Psalm: « The most High hath sanctified his own tabernacle ». And these two answer to the two put before, because, being magnified and sanctified, therefore she magnified and exulted; therefore she renders the reason excellently.

94. *And his mercy* etc. After the affection of the one praising and the reason for praising have been described, there is subjoined here thirdly *the amplification of the divine praise.* And this consists in the commendation of the divine mercy as to the work of human redemption, already begun in the conception of the Virgin. Now the work of our redemption is declarative of *mercy* and of *power,* and it is[^p32-6] manifestative of *largess* and of *truth: of mercy,* in the raising up of fallen man; *of power,* in the overthrowing of the devil; *of largess,* in the pouring forth of the Holy Spirit; and *of truth,* in the fulfilment of the promise.

95. (Vers. 50.). First, then, she praises her Lord in the work of redemption, inasmuch as it is manifestative of *mercy,* there: *And his mercy is from generation unto generations, to them that fear him;* to the Ephesians, the second chapter[^p32-7]: « God, who is rich in mercy, for his exceeding charity wherewith he loved us, even when we were dead in sins, hath quickened us together with Christ »; and in the Psalm: « But the mercy of the Lord is from eternity and unto eternity upon them that fear him ». For this comes upon *all* as to the benefit of *conservation,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter[^p32-8]: « The compassion of man is toward his neighbour, but the mercy of God is upon all flesh ». Yet mercy *delivering* and *saving* extends to those alone *who fear* him, as is written in the Psalm: « O how great is the multitude of thy sweetness, O Lord, which thou hast hidden for them that fear thee »! Therefore Ecclesiasticus, the second chapter: « Ye that fear the Lord, hope in him ».

96. (Vers. 51.). Secondly she praises him in this work inasmuch as it is manifestative of *power* in the casting down of *the proud demons* and of *the wicked rich.* On account of the first she says: *He hath shewed power in his arm; he hath scattered the proud in the mind of his heart.* In this is the special praise of God; Job, the fortieth chapter[^p32-9]: « Scatter the proud in thy indignation, and behold every arrogant man, and humble him ». And thus was fulfilled that of Ecclesiasticus, the tenth chapter: « God hath overturned the thrones of proud princes, and
<!-- page 33 -->
hath set up the meek in their stead ». And he is said to have done this *in his arm,* that is, in the Son; Isaias, the fortieth chapter[^p33-1]: « Behold the Lord God shall come with strength, and his arm shall rule ». Now he calls *proud in the mind of the heart* those who are proud in the understanding through *presumption* and in the affection through *ambition,* as Lucifer and his followers; Job, the next to last chapter: « He beholdeth every high thing, and he is king over all the children of pride ». And thus the sense is: *he scattered* those who are proud *in the mind of the heart.* — Or otherwise: *He scattered the proud in the mind of his heart,* according as it is said, because the proud by the just judgement of God have a scattered heart; Wisdom, the seventeenth chapter[^p33-2]: « While they thought to be hid in their obscure sins, they were scattered under a dark veil of forgetfulness ». — Or: *He scattered the proud in the mind of his heart,* that is, by the determination of his counsel; Job, the fortieth chapter: « Hast thou an arm like God, and canst thou thunder with a voice like him? Scatter the proud in thy indignation, and behold every arrogant man, and humble him ».

97. (Vers. 52.). The proud demons he cast away definitively, but not so men; whence Lamentations, the third chapter[^p33-3]: « For he hath not humbled from his heart, nor cast off the children of men ». Power, then, is praised in the casting down of the proud demons and also of *mighty tyrants,* when she subjoins: *He hath put down the mighty from their seat, and hath exalted the humble;* in Daniel, the fifth chapter[^p33-4], it is said of Nabuchodonosor: « Whom he would, he slew; and whom he would, he destroyed; and whom he would, he set up; and whom he would, he brought down. But when his heart was lifted up, and his spirit hardened unto pride, he was put down from the throne of his kingdom, and his glory was taken away »; and Ecclesiasticus, the tenth chapter: « God hath dried up the roots of proud nations, and hath planted the humble of these nations ». And *the reason* of this is rendered below, in the eighteenth chapter[^p33-5]: « Because every one that exalteth himself shall be humbled; and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted ». And in this is the praise of the divine power; the first of Kings, the second chapter: « The Lord maketh poor and maketh rich, he humbleth and he exalteth. He raiseth up the needy from the dust, and lifteth up the poor from the dunghill, that he may sit with princes, and hold the throne of glory ». An example in Saul and David, Vasthi and Esther.

98. (Vers. 53.). Thirdly she praises the Lord in the aforesaid work inasmuch as it is manifestative of *largess,* when she says: *He hath filled the hungry with good things, and the rich he hath sent empty away;* because true largess is to give to the poor, not to the rich; Proverbs, the thirteenth chapter[^p33-6]: « The just eateth and filleth his soul, but the belly of the wicked is never to be filled »; and again in the Psalm: « The rich have wanted, and have suffered hunger; but they that seek the Lord shall not be deprived of any good »; and Isaias, the sixty-fifth chapter: « Behold, my servants shall eat, and you shall be hungry; behold, my servants shall drink, and you shall be thirsty; behold, my servants shall rejoice, and you shall be confounded »; because, as is said in the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p33-7], « not many wise according to the flesh, not many mighty, not many noble; but the foolish things of the world hath God chosen, that he may confound the wise; and the weak things of the world hath God chosen, that he may confound the strong, and the base things of the world and the things that are contemptible hath God chosen, and things that are not, that he might bring to nought things that are, that no flesh should glory in his sight »; and the Psalm: « The king is not saved by a great army, nor shall the giant be saved by his own great strength ».

99. (Vers. 54.). Fourthly she praises God in the aforesaid work inasmuch as it is manifestative of *the truth of the promise,* when she says: *He hath received Israel his child, being mindful of his mercy;* where first is put forward *the fulfilment of the promise,* which promise the divine kindness made, and the divine truth fulfilled; whence the Psalm[^p33-8]: « Mercy and truth have met each other; justice and peace have kissed ». And this was done when he received our flesh; to the Hebrews, the second chapter: « For nowhere doth he take hold of the angels, but of the seed of Abraham »; Isaias, the forty-second chapter: « Behold my servant, I will receive him; my elect, my soul delighteth in him »; and Osee, the eleventh chapter: « Israel was a child, and I loved him, and I called my son out of Egypt ». — And this: *Being mindful of his mercy,* which he cannot forget; the Psalm[^p33-9]: « Or will God forget to shew mercy? Or will he in his anger shut up his mercies »? and Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter: « Can a woman forget her infant, so as not to have pity on the son of her womb? And if she should forget, yet will not I forget thee ».

100. (Vers. 55.). Then is subjoined *the true promise,* when it is said: *As he spoke to our fathers, to Abraham and to his seed for ever.* And this text ought to be construed thus: *As he spoke to the fathers,* and as he spoke *to Abraham* specially, *and to his seed,* where he touches the state of nature and of the written law. — Or otherwise, so that it is construed with what goes before: *He hath received Israel, being mindful of his mercy,* and this, I say, *to Abraham and to his seed, as he spoke to the fathers.* The like is had in the Psalm[^p33-10]: « He hath
<!-- page 34 -->
remembered his mercy and his truth toward the house of Israel ». Among *the fathers* he names Abraham specially, because to him first the promise was made; Genesis, the twelfth and the twenty-second chapter: « I will bless them that bless thee, and curse them that curse thee, and in thee shall all the kindreds of the earth be blessed »; « I will bless thee, and I will multiply thy seed as the stars of heaven, and as the sand that is by the sea shore; thy seed shall possess the gates of their enemies, and in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed, because thou hast obeyed my voice »; and to the Galatians, the third chapter[^p34-1]: « To Abraham were the promises made, and to his seed ». Now *the seed* of Abraham are those who believe; to the Romans, the ninth chapter: « Not they that are the children of the flesh are the children of God; but they that are the children of the promise are accounted for the seed »; whence below, in the third chapter: « God is able of these stones to raise up children to Abraham ». *For ever,* that is, unto eternity; the Psalm[^p34-2]: « From eternity and to eternity thou art God ». — Whence the blessed Virgin begins the canticle from the greatness of the supreme principle and ends the canticle in the eternity of the end; for she praises him who « is Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end », the Apocalypse, the first chapter; and indeed rightly enough, because in this canticle she shows that the consummation of all the promised benefits has been made, and therefore it is consummative of all praises and canticles and even of the Scriptures.

#### Thirdly the service of benevolence is subjoined.

101. (Vers. 56.). *And Mary abode* etc. After the testimony of prophecy and the canticle of gladness there is subjoined *the service of benevolence,* which the virgin Mary showed to Elizabeth, her kinswoman; in which she is commended for three things, namely for *the sedulousness of the service,* for *the long-suffering of the purpose,* for *the honesty of the dwelling together.* — On account of *the sedulousness of the service* he says: *And Mary abode with her,* namely with her kinswoman who was with child. She did not go away at once, but did according as the Lord commands his disciples in Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p34-3]: « And in the same house remain, until you go out ». Mary, then, *was abiding,* she was not going out like Dina, of whom Genesis, the thirty-fourth chapter: « She went out to see the women of that country »; and it follows after that she was oppressed. She *was abiding* as a sedulous attendant, she was not going about as a curious investigator; the first to Timothy, the fifth chapter, of the young widows: « Being idle they learn to go about from house to house; and are not only idle, but tattlers also, and busybodies, speaking things which they ought not ». Against which Seneca[^p34-4]: « It is the mark of a well-ordered mind to be able to tarry with itself and to stay with itself ».

102. On account of *the long-suffering of the purpose* he subjoins: *About three months,* that is, up to the time of the birth, according as Bede says in the Gloss[^p34-5]: « So long did Mary abide, until, the time of Elizabeth's delivery being completed, she should see the nativity of the precursor of her Lord, on account of which she had chiefly come »; and this was signified in the second of Kings, the sixth chapter, where it is said that « the ark of the Lord abode in the house of Obededom three months ». — *Mystically,* however, by the three months is designated the progress of the three virtues, according as is said in the Gloss[^p34-6]. For « the chaste soul, which conceives the desire of the spiritual word, must needs climb the lofty heights of the heavenly host and, having tarried there as it were the days of three months, not cease to persevere until it is irradiated with the light of faith, hope and charity ».

103. On account of *the honesty of the dwelling together* it is subjoined: *And she returned to her own house,* to dwell with her spouse, the guardian of her virginity; whence she could have said that of Genesis, the thirtieth chapter[^p34-7]: « The Lord hath blessed thee at my coming in. It is just therefore that I should at length provide also for my own house ». *She returned to her own house,* because her spouse and her household desired her return, according to that of the Canticle, the sixth chapter: « Return, return, O Sulamitess, return ». Thus, then, the virgin Mary gives an example to be imitated by all, according as Ambrose says[^p34-8]: « Learn, he says, from Mary religiousness, long-suffering and honesty ».
