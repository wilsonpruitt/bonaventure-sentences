# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XIX, Vers. 15-19
## *Et factum est, ut rediret etc.*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 480 -->

22. (Vers. 15.). *Et factum est, ut rediret* etc. Postquam descripsit statum meriti, describit hic *formam et statum iudicii*[^p480-6]; quem quidem describit Evangelista, primo scilicet *generaliter respectu omnium;* secundo, *respectu servorum fidelium,* ibi[^p480-7]: *Venit autem primus dicens;* tertio *respectu negligentium,* ibi: *Et tertius venit dicens;* quarto *respectu rebellium,* ibi: *Verumtamen inimicos meos* etc.

Statum autem iudicii *in generali* describit tripliciter, scilicet quantum ad ipsius iudicis *apparitionem manifestam, iussionem potestativam* et *inquisitionem*[^p480-8] *districtam.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *iudicis apparitionem manifestam* dicit: *Et factum est, ut rediret, accepto regno.* Glossa[^p480-9]: « Hoc erit, quando ille qui humilis apparuit, in manifestissima claritate iudicaturus adveniet »; Apocalypsis primo: « Ecce, veniet cum nubibus, et videbit eum omnis oculus, et qui eum pupugerunt ». Tunc enim erit *manifestus* ille adventus eius; unde supra decimo septimo[^p480-10]: « Sicut fulgur de caelo in ea quae sub caelo sunt, fulget; ita erit et adventus Filii hominis »; quia, sicut dicitur primae ad Corinthios quarto, « illuminabit abscondita tenebrarum et manifestabit consilia cordium ». Quod autem dicitur Amos quinto: « Dies Domini tenebrae et non lux, caligo et non splendor », intelligitur quantum ad superbos et impios, quos tenebrosus turbo involvet, et tollentur, ne videant Domini claritatem.

23. Secundo quantum ad *iussionem potestativam* subdit: *Iussit*[^p480-11]*, vocari servos, quibus pecuniam dedit.* Haec autem iussio *summae potestatis* erit, quia etiam mortuos suscitabit; primae ad Thessalonicenses quarto: « Ipse Dominus in iussu et in voce Archangeli et in tuba Dei descendet de caelo, et mortui, qui in Christo sunt, resurgent primi ». Haec autem iussio erit et *in propria virtute,* secundum illud Ioannis quinto[^p480-12]: « Venit hora, in qua omnes, qui in monumentis sunt, audient vocem Filii Dei ». Erit etiam *per angelicum ministerium;* secundae ad Thessalonicenses primo: « In revelatione Domini nostri Iesu Christi de caelo cum Angelis virtutis eius, in flamma ignis dantis vindictam »; unde Matthaei decimo tertio: « Mittet Filius hominis Angelos suos, et colligent de regno eius omnia scandala et eos qui faciunt iniquitatem ». Congregant etiam bonos virtute et velocitate naturae; Psalmus[^p480-13]: « Benedicite Domino,
<!-- page 481 -->
omnes Angeli eius, potentes virtute, facientes verbum eius ad audiendam vocem sermonum eius ».

24. Tertio quantum ad *inquisitionem districtam* subdit: *Ut sciret, quantum quisque negotiatus esset;* Glossa[^p481-1]: « *Sciri* faciet; tunc omnium opera et cogitationes omnibus palam ostendentur ». Unde Danielis septimo: « Iudicium sedit, et libri aperti sunt »; haec autem *apertio* est ponderatio meritorum et demeritorum secundum comparationem factorum ad rationes faciendorum. Unde Apocalypsis vigesimo[^p481-2]: « Vidi mortuos pusillos et magnos, stantes in conspectu throni, et libri aperti sunt, et alius liber apertus est, qui est liber vitae; et iudicati sunt mortui ex his quae scripta erant in libris »; quia, secundum quod dicitur secundae ad Corinthios quinto, « omnes nos oportet manifestari ante tribunal Christi, ut referat unusquisque propria corporis sui, prout gessit, sive bonum sive malum ». Unde cuilibet dicetur illud quod dicitur villico supra decimo sexto[^p481-3]: « Redde rationem villicationis tuae ».

25. *Venit autem primus* etc. Postquam descripsit iudicii formam respectu omnium, hic consequenter describit eam *respectu servorum fidelium*[^p481-4]. Et quoniam duplex fuit ordo praedicantium, secundum duplicem parietem Ecclesiae coniunctum per lapidem angularem[^p481-5], quidam scilicet, qui converterunt populum Iudaeorum, alii vero, qui converterunt populum gentium: ideo primo describit iudicium remunerativum fidelis servi *in eruditione Iudaeorum;* secundo vero, *in conversione gentilium populorum,* ibi[^p481-6]: *Et alter venit dicens* etc.

Circa *iudicium servorum fidelium in conversione Iudaeorum* tria introducuntur, scilicet *multiplicatio talenti commissi, approbatio superni iudicii* et *retributio praemii cumulati.*

26. (Vers. 16.). Primo igitur quantum ad *multiplicationem talenti commissi* dicit: *Venit autem primus dicens: Domine, mna tua decem mnas acquisivit. Primus* autem servus dicitur ille cui primo commissum est praedicationis officium, sicut fuit Petrus, cui commissa est convertenda synagoga Iudaeorum; unde Glossa[^p481-7]: « Primus servus est ordo doctorum in circumcisionem missus », qui ideo dicitur *primus,* quia, sicut dicitur Actuum decimo tertio, « vobis primum oportebat evangelizare regnum Dei ». Hic enim servus dicitur *unam mnam accepisse* propter unitatem gratiae et doctrinae; unde Glossa[^p481-8]: « Mnam unam negotiationis accepit, quia unum Deum, unam fidem, unum baptisma iussus est praedicare »; ad Ephesios quarto: « Solliciti, servare unitatem spiritus in vinculo pacis, sicut vocati estis in una spe vocationis vestrae. Unus Dominus, una fides, unum baptisma ». Dicitur etiam *decem mnas acquisivisse* propter conversionem eorum qui erant sub Lege; unde et Glossa[^p481-9]: « Decem mnas acquisivit, dum populum sub Lege constitutum docendo sibimet sociavit »; Actuum quinto: « Magis augebatur credentium in Domino multitudo virorum ac mulierum, ita ut in plateas eiicerent infirmos, ut, veniente Petro, obumbrarentur et liberarentur ab infirmitate ». Hanc ergo plebem acquisitam Domino offert tanquam meritum. In cuius figuram Genesis vigesimo septimo[^p481-10]: « Surge, comede de venatione mea, ut benedicat mihi anima tua ». — Et nota, quod non dicit: *ego acquisivi,* sed: *mna,* ut dicat illud Isaiae vigesimo sexto: « Omnia opera nostra in nobis operatus es, Domine »; et illud primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto: « Non autem ego, sed gratia Dei mecum ».

27. (Vers. 17.). Secundo quantum ad *acceptationem superni iudicii* subdit: *Et ait illi: Euge, serve bone, quia in modico fuisti fidelis.* Et nota, quod *euge* aliquando est *irrisorium,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p481-11]: « Dixerunt: Euge, euge, viderunt oculi nostri ». Aliquando *adulatorium;* Psalmus: « Avertantur statim erubescentes qui dicunt mihi: Euge, euge »; et iterum: « Ferant confestim confusionem suam qui dicunt mihi: Euge, euge »; quia adulatio est citissime repellenda. Aliquando est *adhortatorium,*
<!-- page 482 -->
sicut hic et Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p482-1]: « Euge, serve bone et fidelis, super multa te constituam ». Hic autem servus dicitur *bonus* in obsequio faciendo et *fidelis* in commisso servando et dispensando; primae ad Corinthios quarto: « Hic iam quaeritur inter dispensatores, ut fidelis quis inveniatur »; et Proverbiorum vigesimo: « Multi homines misericordes vocantur, virum autem fidelem quis inveniet »? Ideo autem *fidelis* dicitur simpliciter, quia fidelitatem circa parva servavit. Nam supra decimo sexto[^p482-2]: « Qui fidelis est in minimo et in maiori fidelis est ». « Modicum autem, sicut dicit Glossa, est quidquid in praesenti percipimus donorum ad comparationem futurorum. *Ex parte enim cognoscimus et ex parte prophetamus* », sicut dicitur primae ad Corinthios decimo tertio. In his autem est fidelis qui ea fideliter communicat, « non quaerens quae sua sunt, sed quae Iesu Christi[^p482-3] ». Bernardus: « Fidelis re vera servus Domini es, si de multa gloria Domini tui, etsi non exeunte ex te, tamen per te transeunte, nihil manibus tuis adhaerere contigerit ». Talis servus fuit Paulus, qui dicebat secundae ad Corinthios secundo[^p482-4]: « Non sum sicut plurimi adulterantes verbum Dei, sed ex sinceritate, sicut ex Deo, coram Deo, in Christo loquimur ».

28. Tertio quantum ad *retributionem praemii cumulati* subiungit: *Eris potestatem habens super decem civitates.* Nota, quod *decem civitates* istae intelliguntur esse animae *beatificatae,* sicut per *decem mnas* acquisitas animae *ad Christum conversae;* quas vocat *mnas* in statu viae propter vertibilitatem, sed in statu patriae *civitates* propter immutabilitatem gloriae. Et de hoc Glossa[^p482-5]: « Decem civitates sunt animae per verba Legis ad gratiam Evangelii pervenientes, quibus tunc glorificandus praeponitur qui eis pecuniam verbi digne Deo commendaverit. Unde primae ad Thessalonicenses secundo: *Quae est spes nostra, aut gaudium, aut corona gloriae? Nonne vos ante Dominum Iesum* » etc. Dicuntur autem eis *praeponi* propter quandam excellentiam gloriosam, non per imperium potestativum, quia, primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p482-6], « cum evacuaverit omnem principatum et potestatem et virtutem » etc.; quod dicitur quantum ad imperium dominationis, non quantum ad gradum excellentiae et dignitatis, quia magna erit dignitas de multorum conversione; ideo dicitur primae ad Timotheum tertio[^p482-7]: « Qui bene ministrant gradum bonum sibi acquirunt »; et iterum quinto: « Qui bene praesunt presbyteri duplici honore digni habeantur », quia et ratione sui et ratione populi acquisiti. Unde et Danielis duodecimo[^p482-8]: « Qui ad iustitiam erudiunt multos erunt quasi stellae » etc. Et propterea Ecclesiastae septimo: « Sapientia confortavit sapientem super decem principes civitatis ». Licet igitur Iudaeorum populi pro maxima sui parte fuerint obdurati, non frustra tamen eis praedicaverunt Apostoli. Unde ad Romanos undecimo[^p482-9]: « Nunquid Deus repulit plebem suam? Absit; nam et ego Israelita sum ex semine Abraham. Non repulit Deus plebem suam, quam praescivit »; et post: « Sic ergo et in hoc tempore reliquiae secundum electionem per gratiam salvae factae sunt », quae quidem sunt in gloriam praedicantium, secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo nono[^p482-10]: « His omnibus velut ornamento vestieris ».

29. (Vers. 18.). *Et alter venit dicens* etc. Postquam expressit iudicii formam circa ordinem praedicantium, qui fideliter laboraverunt in conversione Iudaeorum, hic secundo[^p482-11] exprimit circa ordinem praedicantium, qui fideles fuerunt *in conversione gentilium populorum.* Circa quem ordinem duo introducuntur, scilicet *fidelitas ministerii* et *sublimitas praemii.*

Primo igitur quoad *ministerii fidelitatem* dicitur: *Et alter venit dicens: Domine, mna tua fecit quinque mnas;* Glossa[^p482-12]: « Mna una quinque facit, quia gentes quinque sensibus antea mancipatas ad gratiam fidei evangelicae convertit ». In cuius figuram dictum est illi mulieri Samaritanae, quae figuram gentium gerebat: « Quinque viros habuisti », Ioannis quarto[^p482-13]. Sed quando isti quinque sensus convertuntur ad Dei obsequium, tunc mna gratiae dicitur quintuplicari, secundum illud Isaiae decimo nono[^p482-14]: « In die illa erunt quinque civitates in terra Aegypti loquentes lingua Chananaea et iurantes per Dominum exercituum. In die illa erit altare Domini exercituum in medio terrae Aegypti et titulus Domini iuxta terminum eius ». Dicuntur ergo animae de gentibus conversae *quinque,* non propter hoc, quod pauciores sint illis quae de Iudaeis conversae sunt, sed quia illae agebantur secundum legem decalogi, istae vero secundum leges sensuum et carnalium
<!-- page 483 -->
voluptatum. Propter quod et Iudaei dicuntur vocati de *prope,* gentiles vero de *longe;* ad Ephesios secundo[^p483-1]: « Evangelizavit pacem his qui prope, et pacem his qui longe ».

30. (Vers. 19.). Secundo quantum ad *praemii sublimitatem* subdit: *Et huic ait: Et tu esto super quinque civitates,* id est, gloriam habeas de universitate fidelium conversorum. Unde Glossa[^p483-2]: « Respectu earum animarum, quas imbuisti fide et conversatione, magnus et sublimis esto, et non tantum de tuis, sed et de auditorum profectibus honoreris ». De his gentibus Paulus, nobilissimus praedicator, licet minimum se diceret et sentiret Apostolorum[^p483-3], maximam multitudinem adduxit ad ovile dominicum, cum, secundum illud Isaiae sexagesimo, « minimus erit in mille, et erit parvulus in gentem fortissimam ». Unde et ipse dicebat ad Galatas secundo: « Qui operatus est Petro in apostolatum circumcisionis, operatus est et mihi inter gentes ».

31. Et notandum, quod licet decem servos dixerit vocatos propter universitatem[^p483-4] praedicatorum, non resumit nisi de tribus, duobus scilicet bonis et uno malo, ad insinuandum, quod omnis *praedicatio bonorum* est bipartita secundum duplicem *populum* et duplicem *parietem,* ratione cuius etiam *duplex sacerdotium* fuit et *duplex testamentum;* et *duplex* dicitur esse *ordo praedicantium bonorum* secundum duplicem differentiam ovium pascendarum. In cuius figuram Genesis trigesimo secundo[^p483-5]: « In baculo meo transivi Iordanem istum, et nunc cum duabus turmis regredior »; et Ioannis decimo: « Alias oves habeo, quae non sunt ex hoc ovili »; et ad Ephesios secundo: « Ut duos condat in semetipsum in unum novum hominem, faciens pacem ». In cuius etiam designationem duos principes Apostolorum tanquam duos praedicatores praecipuos et duces praedicantium Dominus praemisit, Petrum scilicet et Paulum, dans Petro praecipuam potestatem faciendi miracula, Paulo vero profundissimam sapientiam ad intelligenda mysteria; quia, sicut dicitur primae ad Corinthios primo[^p483-6], « Iudaei signa petunt, et Graeci sapientiam quaerunt ». — Quicumque igitur boni praedicatores sunt, imitatores sunt horum duorum et adducunt ad praesepe dominicum vel bovem, vel asinum[^p483-7], id est decem mnas, vel quinque, secundum horum differentiam populorum. — Et ex hoc apparet differentia inter parabolam, quam proponit Matthaeus[^p483-8] de talentis, et quam hic ponit Lucas; quia ista differentiam respicit praedicatorum respectu *audientium,* illa vero respectu *propriorum meritorum.* Ideo hic dicitur, quod tantundem omnibus dedit; ibi vero, quod uni plus, alii minus. Unde et illa in Ierusalem proposita fuit, haec autem in via[^p483-9]; in utraque tamen fidelium praedicatorum studiosa sedulitas approbatur, et negligentia reprobatur; in hac specialiter Iudaeorum rebellium perfidia condemnatur, cum in fine parabolae[^p483-10] subditur de interfectione inimicorum, hoc est rebellium Iudaeorum.

## English
<!-- page 480 -->

22. (Vers. 15.). *And it came to pass that he returned* etc. After he described the state of merit, here he describes *the form and state of judgement*[^p480-6]; which indeed the Evangelist describes, first, namely, *generally in respect of all;* second, *in respect of the faithful servants,* there[^p480-7]: *And the first came, saying;* third, *in respect of the negligent,* there: *And the third came, saying;* fourth, *in respect of the rebellious,* there: *But as for those my enemies* etc.

Now he describes the state of judgement *in general* in a threefold way, namely as to *the manifest appearing,* the *powerful command* and the *strict inquiry*[^p480-8] of the judge himself.

First, then, as to *the manifest appearing of the judge* he says: *And it came to pass that he returned, having received the kingdom.* The Gloss[^p480-9]: « This will be when he who appeared humble will come to judge in most manifest brightness »; the Apocalypse, the first chapter: « Behold, he shall come with the clouds, and every eye shall see him, and they that pierced him ». For then that coming of his will be *manifest;* whence above, the seventeenth chapter[^p480-10]: « As the lightning shineth from heaven unto the things that are under heaven, so shall also the coming of the Son of man be »; because, as is said in the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter, he « will bring to light the hidden things of darkness, and will make manifest the counsels of the hearts ». But what is said in Amos, the fifth chapter: « The day of the Lord is darkness and not light, obscurity and not brightness », is understood as to the proud and the impious, whom the dark whirlwind will wrap about, and they shall be taken away, lest they see the brightness of the Lord.

23. Second, as to *the powerful command* he subjoins: *He commanded*[^p480-11]*, his servants to be called, to whom he had given the money.* Now this command will be *of the highest power,* because it will even raise the dead; the first to the Thessalonians, the fourth chapter: « The Lord himself shall come down from heaven with command, and with the voice of an Archangel, and with the trumpet of God; and the dead who are in Christ shall rise first ». And this command will be also *in his own power,* according to that of John, the fifth chapter[^p480-12]: « The hour cometh wherein all that are in the graves shall hear the voice of the Son of God ». It will be also *through the angelic ministry;* the second to the Thessalonians, the first chapter: « At the revelation of our Lord Jesus Christ from heaven with the Angels of his power, in a flame of fire giving vengeance »; whence Matthew, the thirteenth chapter: « The Son of man shall send his Angels, and they shall gather out of his kingdom all scandals, and them that work iniquity ». They gather the good also by the power and swiftness of their nature; the Psalm[^p480-13]: « Bless the Lord,
<!-- page 481 -->
all ye his Angels, you that are mighty in power, and execute his word, hearkening to the voice of his orders ».

24. Third, as to *the strict inquiry* he subjoins: *That he might know how much every man had gained by trading;* the Gloss[^p481-1]: « He will make it *known;* then the works and thoughts of all will be openly shown to all ». Whence Daniel, the seventh chapter: « The judgement sat, and the books were opened »; now this *opening* is the weighing of merits and demerits according to the comparison of the things done with the accounts of the things to be done. Whence the Apocalypse, the twentieth chapter[^p481-2]: « I saw the dead, small and great, standing in the presence of the throne, and the books were opened, and another book was opened, which is the book of life; and the dead were judged by those things which were written in the books »; because, according to what is said in the second to the Corinthians, the fifth chapter, « we must all be manifested before the judgement seat of Christ, that every one may receive the proper things of his body, according as he hath done, whether it be good or evil ». Whence to every one will be said that which is said to the steward above, in the sixteenth chapter[^p481-3]: « Give an account of thy stewardship ».

25. *And the first came* etc. After he described the form of judgement in respect of all, here he consequently describes it *in respect of the faithful servants*[^p481-4]. And since the order of preachers was twofold, according to the twofold wall of the Church joined through the corner stone[^p481-5], some, namely, who converted the people of the Jews, and others who converted the people of the Gentiles: therefore first he describes the remunerative judgement of the faithful servant *in the erudition of the Jews;* second, *in the conversion of the gentile peoples,* there[^p481-6]: *And the second came, saying* etc.

Concerning *the judgement of the faithful servants in the conversion of the Jews* three things are introduced, namely *the multiplication of the talent entrusted, the approval of the supernal judgement* and *the retribution of the heaped-up reward.*

26. (Vers. 16.). First, then, as to *the multiplication of the talent entrusted* he says: *And the first came, saying: Lord, thy pound hath gained ten pounds. The first* servant is said to be he to whom the office of preaching was first entrusted, as was Peter, to whom the synagogue of the Jews was entrusted to be converted; whence the Gloss[^p481-7]: « The first servant is the order of doctors sent to the circumcision », who is therefore called *the first,* because, as is said in Acts, the thirteenth chapter, « to you it behoved us first to preach the gospel of the kingdom of God ». For this servant is said *to have received one pound* on account of the unity of grace and of doctrine; whence the Gloss[^p481-8]: « He received one pound of traffic, because he was commanded to preach one God, one faith, one baptism »; to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter: « Careful to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace, as you are called in one hope of your calling. One Lord, one faith, one baptism ». He is said also *to have gained ten pounds* on account of the conversion of those who were under the Law; whence also the Gloss[^p481-9]: « He gained ten pounds when by teaching he associated to himself the people established under the Law »; Acts, the fifth chapter: « The multitude of men and women who believed in the Lord was more increased, insomuch that they brought forth the sick into the streets, that, when Peter came, they might be overshadowed and delivered from their infirmity ». This people, then, gained for the Lord, he offers as a merit. In figure of which, Genesis, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p481-10]: « Arise, eat of my venison, that thy soul may bless me ». — And note that he does not say: *I have gained,* but: *the pound,* that he may say that of Isaias, the twenty-sixth chapter: « Thou hast wrought all our works in us, O Lord »; and that of the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter: « Yet not I, but the grace of God with me ».

27. (Vers. 17.). Second, as to *the acceptance of the supernal judgement* he subjoins: *And he said to him: Well done, good servant, because thou hast been faithful in a little.* And note that *euge* ("well done") is sometimes *derisive,* according to that of the Psalm[^p481-11]: « They said: Well done, well done, our eyes have seen it ». Sometimes *flattering;* the Psalm: « Let them be presently turned away blushing for shame that say to me: Well done, well done »; and again: « Let them immediately bear their confusion that say to me: Well done, well done »; because flattery is to be repelled most swiftly. Sometimes it is *hortatory,*
<!-- page 482 -->
as here and in Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p482-1]: « Well done, good and faithful servant, I will place thee over many things ». Now this servant is called *good* in doing service and *faithful* in keeping and dispensing what was entrusted; the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « Here now it is required among the dispensers, that a man be found faithful »; and Proverbs, the twentieth chapter: « Many men are called merciful, but who shall find a faithful man »? And he is called *faithful* simply for this reason, that he kept fidelity concerning small things. For above, the sixteenth chapter[^p482-2]: « He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in that which is greater ». « Now the little, as the Gloss says, is whatever of gifts we receive in the present in comparison with those to come. *For we know in part, and we prophesy in part* », as is said in the first to the Corinthians, the thirteenth chapter. And in these he is faithful who communicates them faithfully, « not seeking the things that are his own, but the things that are Jesus Christ's[^p482-3] ». Bernard: « Thou art truly a faithful servant of the Lord if, of the abundant glory of thy Lord, which, though it does not go out from thee, yet passes through thee, nothing happens to cling to thy hands ». Such a servant was Paul, who said in the second to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p482-4]: « I am not as many, adulterating the word of God, but with sincerity, as from God, before God, in Christ we speak ».

28. Third, as to *the retribution of the heaped-up reward* he subjoins: *Thou shalt have power over ten cities.* Note that these *ten cities* are understood to be the *beatified* souls, as by the *ten pounds* gained are understood the souls *converted to Christ;* which he calls *pounds* in the state of the way on account of changeableness, but in the state of the fatherland *cities* on account of the immutability of glory. And of this the Gloss[^p482-5]: « The ten cities are the souls coming through the words of the Law to the grace of the Gospel, over which he is then set, to be glorified, who has worthily commended to God for them the money of the word. Whence the first to the Thessalonians, the second chapter: *What is our hope, or joy, or crown of glory? Are not you, before the Lord Jesus* » etc. Now they are said *to be set over* them on account of a certain glorious excellence, not by powerful rule, because, in the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter[^p482-6], « when he shall have brought to nought all principality and power and virtue » etc.; which is said as to the rule of domination, not as to the degree of excellence and dignity, because great will be the dignity from the conversion of many; therefore it is said in the first to Timothy, the third chapter[^p482-7]: « They that minister well purchase to themselves a good degree »; and again, in the fifth: « Let the priests that rule well be esteemed worthy of double honour », because both by reason of themselves and by reason of the people gained. Whence also Daniel, the twelfth chapter[^p482-8]: « They that instruct many to justice shall be as stars » etc. And on that account Ecclesiastes, the seventh chapter: « Wisdom hath strengthened the wise more than ten princes of the city ». Although, then, the peoples of the Jews were for the greatest part of them hardened, yet not in vain did the Apostles preach to them. Whence to the Romans, the eleventh chapter[^p482-9]: « Hath God cast away his people? God forbid; for I also am an Israelite of the seed of Abraham. God hath not cast away his people, which he foreknew »; and after: « Even so then at this time also the remnant are saved according to the election through grace », who indeed are unto the glory of the preachers, according to that of Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter[^p482-10]: « With all these thou shalt be clothed as with an ornament ».

29. (Vers. 18.). *And the second came, saying* etc. After he expressed the form of judgement concerning the order of preachers who laboured faithfully in the conversion of the Jews, here, second[^p482-11], he expresses it concerning the order of preachers who were faithful *in the conversion of the gentile peoples.* Concerning which order two things are introduced, namely *the fidelity of the ministry* and *the sublimity of the reward.*

First, then, as to *the fidelity of the ministry* it is said: *And the second came, saying: Lord, thy pound hath made five pounds;* the Gloss[^p482-12]: « One pound makes five, because it converted to the grace of the evangelical faith the Gentiles formerly enslaved to the five senses ». In figure of which it was said to that Samaritan woman, who bore the figure of the Gentiles: « Thou hast had five husbands », John, the fourth chapter[^p482-13]. But when these five senses are converted to the service of God, then the pound of grace is said to be fivefolded, according to that of Isaias, the nineteenth chapter[^p482-14]: « In that day there shall be five cities in the land of Egypt, speaking the language of Chanaan, and swearing by the Lord of hosts. In that day there shall be an altar of the Lord of hosts in the midst of the land of Egypt, and a monument of the Lord at the borders thereof ». The souls converted from the Gentiles, then, are said to be *five,* not on this account, that they are fewer than those which were converted from the Jews, but because those were led according to the law of the decalogue, these, however, according to the laws of the senses and of carnal
<!-- page 483 -->
pleasures. On account of which also the Jews are said to be called from *nigh,* but the Gentiles from *afar;* to the Ephesians, the second chapter[^p483-1]: « He preached the gospel of peace to them that were nigh, and peace to them that were afar off ».

30. (Vers. 19.). Second, as to *the sublimity of the reward* he subjoins: *And he said to him: Be thou also over five cities,* that is, mayest thou have glory from the universality of the faithful converted. Whence the Gloss[^p483-2]: « In respect of those souls which thou hast imbued with faith and conversation, be thou great and sublime, and be honoured not only for thy own advances, but also for those of thy hearers ». From these Gentiles Paul, the most noble preacher, though he called and felt himself the least of the Apostles[^p483-3], brought a very great multitude to the Lord's fold, since, according to that of Isaias, the sixtieth chapter, « the least shall become a thousand, and a little one a most strong nation ». Whence he himself also said, to the Galatians, the second chapter: « He who wrought in Peter to the apostleship of the circumcision wrought in me also among the Gentiles ».

31. And it is to be noted that, although he said that ten servants were called on account of the universality[^p483-4] of preachers, he does not take up again any but three, namely two good and one bad, to intimate that all *the preaching of the good* is twofold according to the twofold *people* and the twofold *wall,* by reason of which there was also a *twofold priesthood* and a *twofold testament;* and the *order of good preachers* is said to be *twofold* according to the twofold difference of the sheep to be fed. In figure of which, Genesis, the thirty-second chapter[^p483-5]: « With my staff I passed over this Jordan, and now I return with two companies »; and John, the tenth chapter: « Other sheep I have, that are not of this fold »; and to the Ephesians, the second chapter: « That he might make the two in himself into one new man, making peace ». In designation of which also the Lord sent before the two princes of the Apostles as two chief preachers and leaders of the preachers, namely Peter and Paul, giving to Peter a chief power of working miracles, but to Paul a most profound wisdom for understanding mysteries; because, as is said in the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p483-6], « the Jews require signs, and the Greeks seek after wisdom ». — Whoever, then, are good preachers are imitators of these two and bring to the Lord's manger either the ox or the ass[^p483-7], that is, ten pounds or five, according to the difference of these peoples. — And from this appears the difference between the parable which Matthew[^p483-8] sets forth concerning the talents and that which Luke sets down here; because this one regards the difference of preachers in respect of *the hearers,* but that one in respect of *their own merits.* Therefore here it is said that he gave the same amount to all; but there, that he gave more to one, less to another. Whence also that one was set forth in Jerusalem, but this one on the way[^p483-9]; yet in both the zealous sedulousness of faithful preachers is approved and negligence is reproved; in this one especially the perfidy of the rebellious Jews is condemned, since at the end of the parable[^p483-10] is subjoined the killing of the enemies, that is, of the rebellious Jews.
