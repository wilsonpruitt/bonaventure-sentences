# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XVII, Vers. 5-10
## *Manuductio per duplex exemplum sensibile*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 429 -->

#### Manuductio per duplex exemplum sensibile.

12. *Et dixerunt Apostoli Domino.* Postquam informavit discipulos ad spiritualem pietatem rationabili documento, hic secundo *manuducit exemplo.* Et quoniam duplex est radix pietatis spiritualis, scilicet *fides fervida* et *humilitas timorata,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo quinto[^p429-10]: « Timor Dei initium dilectionis eius; fidei autem initium agglutinandum est illi »; ideo hic primo proponit exemplum, per quod manuducitur ad *fervidam fidem;* secundo vero, per quod manuducitur ad *timoratam humilitatem,* ibi[^p429-11]: *Quis autem vestrum habens servum.*

Circa igitur *exemplum, quo manuducuntur ad fidem* tria insinuantur, quae ad perfectionem fidei concurrunt, scilicet *fidei ortus, profectus* et *virtus. Fidei ortus* consistit in susceptione doni divini, *profectus* in cooperatione liberi arbitrii, *virtus* vero in exsecutione mirandi imperii.

13. (Vers. 5.). Primo ergo quantum ad *fidei ortum* consistentem in *susceptione doni divini* praemittit: *Et dixerunt Apostoli Domino: Adauge nobis fidem.* Videntes enim Apostoli, se non sufficere ad illa sublimia opera pietatis, quae consistunt in scandalis vitandis et iniuriis remittendis, sine magnitudine fidei, petunt, sibi fidem augeri per donum divinum, a quo fides habet originem, quae non est homini a se ipso, sed ex dono et munere divino. Unde primae ad Corinthios duodecimo[^p429-12]: « Alii quidem
<!-- page 430 -->
datur per Spiritum sermo sapientiae, alii sermo scientiae secundum eundem Spiritum, alteri fides in eodem Spiritu ». Petunt autem, fidem a Domino sibi augeri tanquam donum *primarium;* ad Hebraeos undecimo[^p430-1]: « Fides est fundamentum sperandarum rerum, argumentum non apparentium » etc.; et post: « Sine fide impossibile est placere Deo; accedentem ergo oportet credere » etc. Petunt similiter tanquam donum aliorum donorum *impetrativum;* Iacobi primo[^p430-2]: « Postulet in fide nihil haesitans »; Matthaei vigesimo primo: « Omnia, quaecumque petieritis in oratione credentes, accipietis »; et Matthaei decimo quinto: « O mulier, magna est fides tua; fiat tibi, sicut petisti » etc. Petunt etiam tanquam donum aliorum *custoditivum;* ad Ephesios tertio[^p430-3]: « Huius rei gratia flecto genua ad Patrem Domini Iesu Christi », « ut det vobis secundum divitias gloriae suae virtutem, corroborari per Spiritum eius in interiori homine, Christum habitare per fidem in cordibus vestris ». Et ideo Dominus dicebat Petro infra vigesimo secundo[^p430-4]: « Ego pro te rogavi, Petre, ut non deficiat fides tua; et tu aliquando conversus, confirma fratres tuos ». Ob hoc etiam dictum est ipsi Petro Matthaei decimo sexto: « Tu es Petrus, et super hanc petram aedificabo Ecclesiam meam ».

14. (Vers. 6.). Secundo quantum ad *fidei profectum* consistentem in *cooperatione liberi arbitrii* subditur: *Dixit autem Dominus: Si habueritis fidem sicut granum sinapis.* Per *granum sinapis,* quod in se est quantitate parvum, acumine fervidum et incremento magnum, intelligimus liberi arbitrii cooperationem quantum ad *captivationem intellectus, accensionem affectus* et *multitudinem boni effectus.* Unde *interlinearis*[^p430-5] determinat, cuiusmodi fidem debeant quaerere, scilicet humilem et ferventem, cui nunquam sufficiat quod bene gessit. In grano namque sinapis propter parvitatem intelligitur fides, in qua est *purificatio intellectus;* Matthaei decimo tertio[^p430-6]: « Simile est regnum caelorum grano sinapis, quod est minimum omnibus seminibus ». Nulla enim virtus adeo captivat intellectum humanum, sicut fides; secundae ad Corinthios decimo: « In captivitatem redigentes omnem intellectum in obsequium Christi » etc. — Propter *acumen* autem intelligitur *accensio affectus;* unde Glossa[^p430-7]: « Fidem perfectam grano sinapis comparat, quae in facie est humilis et nullarum virium apparens, sed pressuris trita, quantum intus ferveat, ostendet ». Unde de Apollo Actuum decimo octavo[^p430-8] dicitur: « Hic erat edoctus viam Domini et fervens spiritu loquebatur ea quae sunt Iesu ». — Propter *incrementum* intelligitur per illud *multiplex boni effectus,* secundum illud Matthaei decimo tertio[^p430-9]: « Simile est regnum caelorum grano sinapis, quod accipiens homo seminavit; quod cum creverit, maius est omnibus oleribus et fit arbor ». In quo intelligitur bonorum operum multitudo et congeries cumulata; Iacobi secundo[^p430-10]: « Vis scire, o homo inanis, quoniam fides sine operibus mortua est? Abraham, pater noster, nonne ex operibus iustificatus est, offerens Isaac filium suum super altare? Vides, quoniam fides cooperabatur operibus illius, et ex operibus fides consummata est » etc.

15. Tertio quantum ad *fidei virtutem* in *exsecutione mirandi imperii* subiungit: *Dicetis huic arbori moro: Eradicare et transplantare in mare; et obediet vobis.* In quo datur intelligi mira virtus fidei quantum ad ea quae aguntur secundum *cursum naturae* et secundum *impetum culpae* et secundum *meritum iustitiae.* Omnia enim haec subsunt regimini divinae providentiae et quodam modo imperio fidei perfectae, secundum illud Marci nono[^p430-11]: « Si potes credere, omnia possibilia sunt credenti ». Secundum *litteram* enim per imperium fidei supra morum transplantandam intelligitur imperium respectu *corporalium elementorum.* Unde Glossa[^p430-12]: « Species ponitur pro genere, quia fides consummata etiam elementis potest imperare »; unde et Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p430-13]: « Si habueritis fidem et non haesitaveritis et dixeritis huic monti: Tolle et iacta te in mare; ita fiet ». Unde ad Hebraeos undecimo exemplificat Apostolus de Sanctis dicens, quod « per fidem vicerunt regna, obturaverunt ora leonum, exstinxerunt impetum ignis, effugaverunt aciem gladii et fortes facti sunt in bello ».

16. Secundum autem *tropologiam* intelligitur in hoc imperium respectu *diaboli et tentationum*
<!-- page 431 -->
*eius.* Unde Ambrosius[^p431-1]: « Fructus mori in flore albet, formatus rutilat, maturitate nigrescit; sic diabolus, flore angelicae naturae et potestate rutilans, praevaricando tetro inhorruit odore peccati, qui per fidem excluditur ». Unde primae Petri ultimo[^p431-2]: « Adversarius vester diabolus tanquam leo rugiens circuit, quaerens quem devoret; cui resistite fortes in fide »; et ad Ephesios sexto: « In omnibus sumentes scutum fidei; in quo possitis omnia tela nequissimi ignea exstinguere ».

17. Secundum *allegoriam* autem intelligitur imperium respectu *praedicationis crucis* et *evangelicorum Sacramentorum.* Unde Glossa[^p431-3]: « *Per morum,* cuius colore sanguineo fructus et virgulta virent, Evangelium crucis exprimitur, quae per fidem Apostolorum de gente Iudaeorum, in qua velut in stirpe generis tenebatur, verbi praedicatione eradicata est et in mare gentium transplantata ». Huius sanguis accendit nos ad conflictum contra monstra vitiorum; unde primi Machabaeorum sexto[^p431-4]: « Ostenderunt elephantis sanguinem uvae et mori » etc. Haec transplantatio per fidem Apostolorum et docentium et miracula facientium facta est in Ecclesia de gentibus congregata. Unde Ioannis decimo quarto[^p431-5]: « Qui credit in me, opera, quae ego facio, et ipse faciet et maiora horum faciet ». In huius figuram dicitur ad Hebraeos undecimo: « Fide muri Iericho corruerunt circuitu dierum septem. Fide Rahab meretrix non periit cum incredulis ». In quo intelligitur transplantatio mori in mare per reprobationem Iudaeorum et electionem gentilium peccatorum[^p431-6].

18. (Vers. 7.). *Quis autem vestrum, habens servum.* Post exemplum manuducens ad fidem fervidam subditur hic *exemplum manuducens ad humilitatem timoratam*[^p431-7]. In huius autem exempli descriptione hoc ordine procedit. *Proponit* namque *obsequiositatem servitutis, assumit necessitatem serviendi* et *concludit humilitatem spiritualis servitutis.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *obsequiositatem servitutis,* quam quaerendo *proponit,* dicitur: *Quis autem vestrum, habens servum arantem aut boves pascentem,* per laborem vitae *activae,* quae consistit in *operando viriliter;* et hoc insinuatur in actu *arandi,* secundum illud Osee decimo[^p431-8]: « Arabit Iudas, et confringet sibi sulcos Iacob »; et supra nono: « Nemo mittens manum ad aratrum » etc. Consistit etiam in *praesidendo sollicite;* et hoc insinuatur in actu *pascendi,* secundum illud quod dictum est Petro Ioannis ultimo[^p431-9]: « Pasce oves meas ». Unde Glossa: « Servus arans vel pascens doctor est Ecclesiae, de quo dicitur: *Nemo mittens manum ad aratrum* etc.; et Dominus Petro ait: *Pasce oves meas* ». — *Qui regresso de agro dicat illi*[^p431-10]*: Statim transi, recumbe,* per dulcorem *contemplativae,* in quo est *regressus ad cor;* Ezechielis primo[^p431-11]: « Animalia ibant et revertebantur in similitudinem fulguris coruscantis »; est etiam *transitus ad delectationis convivium,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto: « Transite ad me, omnes, qui concupiscitis me, et a generationibus meis implemini; spiritus enim meus super mel est dulcis ». Haec autem refectio non statim datur homini venienti de exercitio actionis; unde Canticorum tertio[^p431-12]: « In lectulo meo quaesivi per noctes quem diligit anima mea; quaesivi eum, et non inveni »; quia Dominus non statim se offert, ut humilius et vigilantius requiratur.

19. (Vers. 8.). Unde subdit: *Et non dicat: Para quod coenem et praecinge te et ministra mihi, donec manducem et bibam,* scilicet per gemitum *compunctionis internae,* in qua quilibet convivium *parat;* Ecclesiastici decimo octavo[^p431-13]: « Ante orationem praepara animam tuam, et noli esse quasi homo, qui tentat Deum ». *Se ipsum praecingit,* restringendo mentales defluxus; primae Petri primo: « Succincti lumbos mentis vestrae » etc. In hac etiam *ministrat,* se ipsum deiiciendo, et Deum *cibat et potat,*
<!-- page 432 -->
dum facit quod maxime acceptat; Apocalypsis tertio[^p432-1]: « Si quis aperuerit mihi ianuam, intrabo ad illum et coenabo cum illo ». Unde Glossa: « *Ministrat* Conditori qui, eius misericordiam considerans et eius iudicia pertimescens, de suis virtutibus se humiliat ». Et quia post hanc compunctionem sequitur *devotio,* ideo subdit: *Et post haec tu manducabis et bibes,* per gustum *suavitatis aeternae.* Glossa[^p432-2]: « Postquam tua praedicatione et compunctione refectus ero, tunc tandem aeternis meae sapientiae dapibus reficieris »; Psalmus[^p432-3]: « Renuit consolari anima mea, memor fui Dei et delectatus sum » etc.; et iterum: « Fuerunt mihi lacrymae meae panes die ac nocte »; et post: « In voce exsultationis et confessionis sonus epulantis »; et iterum: « Convertisti planctum meum in gaudium mihi » etc. Hoc autem totum facit Dominus ad humilitatem conservandam, quae ceterarum virtutum est radix, conservatio et custodia. Ideo et Paulus iam perfectus dicebat secundae ad Corinthios duodecimo[^p432-4]: « Ne magnitudo revelationum extollat me, datus est mihi stimulus carnis meae angelus satanae »; et Ecclesiastici tertio: « Quanto magnus es, humilia te in omnibus, et coram Deo invenies gratiam »; nam Ecclesiastici decimo octavo: « Cum consummaverit homo, tunc incipiet ».

20. (Vers. 9. 10.). Secundo quantum ad *necessitatem serviendi,* quam *assumit,* subdit: *Nunquid gratiam habet servo illi, quia fecit quae*[^p432-5] *ei imperaverat? Non puto,* id est, puto, quod non. Glossa: « Quid mirum, si facit servus quod debet, cuius est laborare et servire assidue »? Ecclesiastici trigesimo tertio: « Servum inclinant operationes assiduae ». — Et nota, quod Dominus dicere videtur, quod dominus non habet agere gratiam servo, non quod eius opera non acceptet, cum dicatur Ecclesiastici trigesimo tertio[^p432-6]: « Si est tibi servus fidelis, sit tibi quasi anima tua, quasi fratrem sic eum tracta, quoniam in sanguine animae comparasti eum »; sed hoc dicitur, quia quod facit magis est debitum quam donum, magis iustitia tentionis quam gratia supererogationis. Et hoc maxime verum est in Deo; unde Iob trigesimo quinto[^p432-7]: « Porro, si iuste egeris, quid donabis ei, aut quid de manu tua recipiet »? Unde Glossa: « Si homo ab homine servo non uniforme, sed multiplex ministerium exigit nec gratum habet; quanto magis nec vos, qui *sine me nihil potestis,* non unius usum operis vel laboris exercere debetis, sed semper novis studiis augere priora »? Si enim verum istud est in homine, servo alterius hominis, quia servitia eius magis sunt debita quam grata; hoc multo verius est in homine, servo Dei; tum quia omnia sunt *ab ipso;* Isaiae vigesimo sexto[^p432-8]: « Omnia opera nostra in nobis operatus es, Domine »; et primi Paralipomenon vigesimo nono: « Tua sunt, Domine, omnia, et quae de manu tua accepimus dedimus tibi ». « Deus enim est, qui operatur in vobis et velle et perficere pro bona voluntate », ad Philippenses secundo. Tum quia omnia sunt *sub ipso;* Isaiae quadragesimo[^p432-9]: « Quis appendit tribus digitis molem terrae et libravit in pondere montes et colles in statera »? « Ecce, gentes quasi stilla situlae et quasi momentum staterae reputatae sunt ». « Omnes gentes quasi non sint, sic sunt coram eo et quasi nihil et inane reputatae sunt ei ». Item, quia omnia sunt *propter ipsum;* Proverbiorum decimo sexto[^p432-10]: « Propter semetipsum omnia operatus est Dominus ». Et ideo Apostolus primae ad Corinthios decimo: « Sive manducatis, sive bibitis, sive aliud quid facitis, omnia in gloriam Dei facite ».

21. Tertio quantum ad *humilitatem spiritualis servitutis,* quam *concludit,* subiungit: *Sic et vos, cum feceritis omnia, quae praecepta sunt vobis,* per *completionem obedientiae;* haec enim est vera obedientia, in qua omnia implentur mandata. Nam Iacobi secundo[^p432-11]: « Quicumque autem totam legem servaverit, offendat autem in uno, factus est omnium reus »; et Deuteronomii vigesimo septimo: « Maledictus, qui non permanet in sermonibus Legis huius
<!-- page 433 -->
et eos opere perficit ». Et ideo Dominus dicit Ieremiae prophetae Ieremiae primo[^p433-1]: « Ad omnia, quae mittam te, ibis et universa, quaecumque mandavero tibi, loqueris ad eos »; et Propheta in Psalmo: « Propterea ad omnia mandata tua dirigebar » etc. — Qui sic est obediens in opere debet esse humilis in reputatione, et ideo subdit: *Dicite: Servi inutiles sumus; quod debuimus facere fecimus,* per *recognitionem infirmitatis propriae; servi,* inquam, quia empti, ultra quam valeatis; primae ad Corinthios sexto[^p433-2]: « Empti estis pretio magno; glorificate et portate » etc.; et primae Petri primo: « Non corruptibilibus auro vel argento redempti estis de vana vestra conversatione, sed pretioso sanguine quasi Agni immaculati » etc. *Inutiles,* non quia opera nostra bona nullam habeant mercedem vel fructum, quia utilia sunt *nobis,* sed quia nihil ex hoc *divinae bonitati* conferimus; Psalmus[^p433-3]: « Dixi Domino: Deus meus es tu, quoniam bonorum meorum non eges ». — Nihil est quod *facimus* comparatione eius, quod recepimus et Deo debemus; unde Glossa[^p433-4]: « *Qui venit ministrare et non ministrari,* nos debitores sibi fecit, ne nostris operibus confidentes, sed eius examen expavescentes, omnibus, quae praecepta sunt, impletis, nos imperfectos esse cognoscamus ». Ideo dicebat Propheta[^p433-5]: « Quid retribuam Domino pro omnibus, quae retribuit mihi »? — Item, nihil est quod *sustinemus* respectu eius quod exspectamus; ad Romanos octavo[^p433-6]: « Non sunt condignae passiones huius temporis ad futuram gloriam, quae revelabitur in nobis ». Unde Glossa super hoc: *Servi inutiles sumus,* « quia bonorum operum nostrorum non indiget Deus; vel quia non sunt condignae passiones huius temporis ad futuram gloriam, quae revelabitur in nobis ». Qui igitur se humiliat sic, ille est qui disponit se ad perfectam gloriam et aeternam. Unde haec humilitas, quae totum Deo tribuit et nihil sibi, perfecte erit in *patria,* secundum illud Apocalypsis quarto[^p433-7]: « Procidebant viginti quatuor seniores ante sedentem in throno et adorabant viventem in saecula saeculorum et mittebant coronas suas ante thronum dicentes: Dignus es; Domine Deus noster, accipere gloriam et honorem et virtutem » etc. Et in *statu viae* hoc est in viris perfectis, secundum illud Danielis nono[^p433-8]: « Nobis confusio faciei, regibus nostris et principibus nostris et patribus nostris, qui peccaverunt; tibi autem, Domino Deo nostro, misericordia et propitiatio » etc. Ideo singulis diebus dicere debemus illud primae ad Timotheum primo: « Regi autem saeculorum immortali, invisibili, soli Deo honor et gloria in saecula saeculorum. Amen ».

## English
<!-- page 429 -->

#### Leading by the hand through a twofold sensible example.

12. *And the Apostles said to the Lord.* After he has formed the disciples to spiritual piety by a rational instruction, here second he *leads* them *by the hand by an example.* And since the root of spiritual piety is twofold, namely *fervent faith* and *God-fearing humility,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p429-10]: « The fear of God is the beginning of his love; and the beginning of faith is to be fast joined unto it »; therefore here first he sets forth an example by which they are led by the hand to *fervent faith;* but second, one by which they are led by the hand to *God-fearing humility,* there[^p429-11]: *But which of you having a servant.*

Concerning, then, *the example by which they are led by the hand to faith* three things are intimated which concur in the perfection of faith, namely *the birth, the progress* and *the power of faith. The birth of faith* consists in the receiving of a divine gift, *the progress* in the cooperation of free choice, but *the power* in the execution of a command to be wondered at.

13. (Vers. 5.). First, then, as to *the birth of faith,* consisting in *the receiving of a divine gift,* he sets forth: *And the Apostles said to the Lord: Increase our faith.* For the Apostles, seeing that they did not suffice for those sublime works of piety which consist in avoiding scandals and remitting injuries without greatness of faith, ask that faith be increased for them by a divine gift, from which faith has its origin, which is not to man from himself, but from the divine gift and bounty. Whence in the first to the Corinthians, the twelfth chapter[^p429-12]: « To one indeed
<!-- page 430 -->
by the Spirit is given the word of wisdom, to another the word of knowledge according to the same Spirit, to another faith in the same Spirit ». And they ask the Lord that faith be increased for them as the *primary* gift; to the Hebrews, the eleventh chapter[^p430-1]: « Faith is the foundation of things to be hoped for, the evidence of things that appear not » etc.; and after: « Without faith it is impossible to please God; for he that cometh must believe » etc. They ask likewise for it as the gift that *obtains* the other gifts; James, the first chapter[^p430-2]: « Let him ask in faith, nothing wavering »; Matthew, the twenty-first: « All things whatsoever you shall ask in prayer believing, you shall receive »; and Matthew, the fifteenth: « O woman, great is thy faith; be it done to thee as thou hast asked » etc. They ask also for it as the gift that *keeps* the others; to the Ephesians, the third chapter[^p430-3]: « For this cause I bow my knees to the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ », « that he would grant you, according to the riches of his glory, power, to be strengthened by his Spirit in the inward man, that Christ may dwell by faith in your hearts ». And therefore the Lord said to Peter below, in the twenty-second chapter[^p430-4]: « I have prayed for thee, Peter, that thy faith fail not; and thou, being once converted, confirm thy brethren ». For this reason also it was said to Peter himself in Matthew, the sixteenth chapter: « Thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church ».

14. (Vers. 6.). Second, as to *the progress of faith,* consisting in *the cooperation of free choice,* there is subjoined: *And the Lord said: If you have faith as a grain of mustard seed.* By *the grain of mustard seed,* which in itself is small in quantity, fervent in sharpness and great in increase, we understand the cooperation of free choice as to *the captivating of the understanding, the kindling of the affection* and *the multitude of good effect.* Whence the *interlinear*[^p430-5] determines what kind of faith they ought to seek, namely humble and fervent, for which what it has done well never suffices. For in the grain of mustard seed, on account of its smallness, faith is understood, in which is *the purification of the understanding;* Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p430-6]: « The kingdom of heaven is like to a grain of mustard seed, which is the least of all seeds ». For no virtue so captivates the human understanding as faith; the second to the Corinthians, the tenth: « Bringing into captivity every understanding unto the obedience of Christ » etc. — But on account of the *sharpness* is understood *the kindling of the affection;* whence the Gloss[^p430-7]: « He compares perfect faith to a grain of mustard seed, which in its face is humble and appears of no strength, but, crushed by pressures, will show how much it burns within ». Whence of Apollo it is said in Acts, the eighteenth chapter[^p430-8]: « This man was instructed in the way of the Lord, and being fervent in spirit spoke the things that are of Jesus ». — On account of the *increase* is understood by it *the manifold good effect,* according to that of Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p430-9]: « The kingdom of heaven is like to a grain of mustard seed, which a man took and sowed; which when it is grown up is greater than all herbs, and becometh a tree ». In which is understood the multitude of good works and a heaped-up mass; James, the second chapter[^p430-10]: « Wilt thou know, O vain man, that faith without works is dead? Was not Abraham our father justified by works, offering up Isaac his son upon the altar? Seest thou that faith did cooperate with his works, and by the works faith was made perfect » etc.

15. Third, as to *the power of faith* in *the execution of a command to be wondered at,* he subjoins: *You shall say to this mulberry tree: Be thou rooted up, and be thou transplanted into the sea; and it shall obey you.* In which is given to be understood the marvellous power of faith as to those things which are done according to *the course of nature* and according to *the onset of fault* and according to *the merit of justice.* For all these are subject to the governance of divine providence and in a certain way to the command of perfect faith, according to that of Mark, the ninth chapter[^p430-11]: « If thou canst believe, all things are possible to him that believeth ». For *according to the letter,* by the command of faith over the mulberry tree to be transplanted is understood command with respect to *the bodily elements.* Whence the Gloss[^p430-12]: « The species is put for the genus, because perfected faith can command even the elements »; whence also Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p430-13]: « If you shall have faith and stagger not and shall say to this mountain: Take up and cast thyself into the sea; it shall be done ». Whence to the Hebrews, the eleventh, the Apostle gives examples of the Saints, saying that « by faith they conquered kingdoms, stopped the mouths of lions, quenched the violence of fire, put to flight the edge of the sword and became valiant in battle ».

16. But *according to tropology* there is understood in this command with respect to *the devil and his*
<!-- page 431 -->
*temptations.* Whence Ambrose[^p431-1]: « The fruit of the mulberry is white in the flower, when formed it glows red, at ripeness it grows black; so the devil, glowing red with the flower of angelic nature and with power, by transgressing grew horrid with the foul odour of sin, who is shut out by faith ». Whence in the first of Peter, the last chapter[^p431-2]: « Your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, goeth about seeking whom he may devour; whom resist ye, strong in faith »; and to the Ephesians, the sixth: « In all things taking the shield of faith, wherewith you may be able to extinguish all the fiery darts of the most wicked one ».

17. But *according to allegory* there is understood command with respect to *the preaching of the cross* and *the Gospel Sacraments.* Whence the Gloss[^p431-3]: « *By the mulberry tree,* whose fruit and shoots flourish with a blood-red colour, the Gospel of the cross is expressed, which through the faith of the Apostles was rooted up by the preaching of the word from the nation of the Jews, in which it was held as in the stock of its kin, and transplanted into the sea of the Gentiles ». Its blood kindles us to the conflict against the monsters of the vices; whence in the first of Machabees, the sixth chapter[^p431-4]: « They shewed the elephants the blood of grapes and of mulberries » etc. This transplanting was done through the faith of the Apostles, both teaching and working miracles, in the Church gathered from the Gentiles. Whence John, the fourteenth chapter[^p431-5]: « He that believeth in me, the works that I do he also shall do, and greater than these shall he do ». In figure of this it is said to the Hebrews, the eleventh: « By faith the walls of Jericho fell down, by the going round of seven days. By faith Rahab the harlot perished not with the unbelievers ». In which is understood the transplanting of the mulberry tree into the sea through the reprobation of the Jews and the election of the Gentile sinners[^p431-6].

18. (Vers. 7.). *But which of you, having a servant.* After the example leading by the hand to fervent faith, there is subjoined here *the example leading by the hand to God-fearing humility*[^p431-7]. And in the description of this example he proceeds in this order. For he *proposes the dutifulness of servitude, assumes the necessity of serving* and *concludes the humility of spiritual servitude.*

First, then, as to *the dutifulness of servitude,* which he *proposes* by asking, it is said: *But which of you, having a servant ploughing or feeding cattle,* by the labour of the *active* life, which consists in *working manfully;* and this is intimated in the act of *ploughing,* according to that of Osee, the tenth chapter[^p431-8]: « Juda shall plough, and Jacob shall break the furrows for himself »; and above, in the ninth: « No man putting his hand to the plough » etc. It consists also in *presiding solicitously;* and this is intimated in the act of *feeding,* according to that which was said to Peter in John, the last chapter[^p431-9]: « Feed my sheep ». Whence the Gloss: « The servant ploughing or feeding is the doctor of the Church, of whom it is said: *No man putting his hand to the plough* etc.; and the Lord says to Peter: *Feed my sheep* ». — *Who will say to him when he is returned from the field*[^p431-10]*: Immediately pass over, sit down,* by the sweetness of the *contemplative* life, in which there is *a return to the heart;* Ezechiel, the first chapter[^p431-11]: « The living creatures went and returned after the likeness of flashing lightning »; there is also *a passing over to the banquet of delight,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter: « Pass over to me, all ye that desire me, and be filled with my fruits; for my spirit is sweet above honey ». But this refreshment is not given immediately to a man coming from the exercise of action; whence the Canticle, the third chapter[^p431-12]: « In my bed by nights I sought him whom my soul loveth; I sought him, and found him not »; because the Lord does not offer himself immediately, so that he may be sought more humbly and more watchfully.

19. (Vers. 8.). Whence he subjoins: *And will not say: Make ready my supper, and gird thyself, and serve me, whilst I eat and drink,* namely by the groaning of *inward compunction,* in which each one *makes ready* the banquet; Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter[^p431-13]: « Before prayer prepare thy soul, and be not as a man that tempteth God ». *He girds himself* by restraining the mental outflowings; the first of Peter, the first chapter: « Having the loins of your mind girt up » etc. In this also he *serves,* by casting himself down, and *feeds and gives drink* to God,
<!-- page 432 -->
while he does what he most accepts; the Apocalypse, the third chapter[^p432-1]: « If any man shall open to me the door, I will come in to him and will sup with him ». Whence the Gloss: « He *serves* the Creator who, considering his mercy and dreading his judgements, humbles himself concerning his virtues ». And since after this compunction *devotion* follows, therefore he subjoins: *And afterwards thou shalt eat and drink,* by the taste of *eternal sweetness.* The Gloss[^p432-2]: « After I shall have been refreshed by thy preaching and compunction, then at length thou shalt be refreshed with the eternal dainties of my wisdom »; the Psalm[^p432-3]: « My soul refused to be comforted; I remembered God, and was delighted » etc.; and again: « My tears have been my bread day and night »; and after: « With the voice of exultation and confession, the noise of one feasting »; and again: « Thou hast turned for me my mourning into joy » etc. And the Lord does all this for the preserving of humility, which is the root, the preservation and the custody of the other virtues. Therefore Paul too, already perfect, said in the second to the Corinthians, the twelfth chapter[^p432-4]: « Lest the greatness of the revelations should exalt me, there was given me a sting of my flesh, an angel of Satan »; and Ecclesiasticus, the third: « The greater thou art, the more humble thyself in all things, and thou shalt find grace before God »; for Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth: « When a man hath done, then shall he begin ».

20. (Vers. 9. 10.). Second, as to *the necessity of serving,* which he *assumes,* he subjoins: *Doth he thank that servant, because he did the things*[^p432-5] *which he had commanded him? I think not,* that is, I think that he does not. The Gloss: « What wonder, if the servant does what he owes, whose part it is to labour and to serve assiduously »? Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-third chapter: « Assiduous labours bow a servant ». — And note that the Lord seems to say that the master has not to give thanks to the servant, not that he does not accept his works, since it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-third chapter[^p432-6]: « If thou have a faithful servant, let him be to thee as thy own soul; treat him so as a brother, because in the blood of thy soul thou hast gotten him »; but this is said because what he does is more a debt than a gift, more a justice of holding than a grace of supererogation. And this is most of all true in God; whence Job, the thirty-fifth chapter[^p432-7]: « Moreover, if thou do justly, what shalt thou give him, or what shall he receive of thy hand »? Whence the Gloss: « If a man requires of a man, his servant, not a uniform but a manifold ministry, and is not grateful; how much more ought not you, who *without me can do nothing,* to exercise the use of not one work or labour, but always to increase the former things with new zeal »? For if this is true in a man, the servant of another man, because his services are more owed than welcome; this is much more true in a man, the servant of God; both because all things are *from him;* Isaias, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p432-8]: « Thou hast wrought all our works in us, O Lord »; and the first of Paralipomenon, the twenty-ninth: « All things are thine, O Lord, and we have given thee what we received of thy hand ». « For it is God who worketh in you both to will and to accomplish, according to his good will », to the Philippians, the second. And because all things are *under him;* Isaias, the fortieth chapter[^p432-9]: « Who hath poised with three fingers the bulk of the earth, and weighed the mountains in scales, and the hills in a balance »? « Behold, the Gentiles are as a drop of a bucket, and are counted as the smallest grain of a balance ». « All nations are as if they were not, so are they before him, and are counted to him as nothing and vanity ». Likewise, because all things are *for his sake;* Proverbs, the sixteenth chapter[^p432-10]: « The Lord hath wrought all things for himself ». And therefore the Apostle in the first to the Corinthians, the tenth: « Whether you eat, or drink, or whatsoever else you do, do all to the glory of God ».

21. Third, as to *the humility of spiritual servitude,* which he *concludes,* he subjoins: *So you also, when you shall have done all things that are commanded you,* by *the fulfilment of obedience;* for this is true obedience, in which all the commandments are fulfilled. For James, the second chapter[^p432-11]: « Whosoever shall keep the whole law, but offend in one, is become guilty of all »; and Deuteronomy, the twenty-seventh: « Cursed is he that abideth not in the words of this Law
<!-- page 433 -->
and fulfilleth them in work ». And therefore the Lord says to Jeremias the prophet, Jeremias, the first chapter[^p433-1]: « To all things to which I shall send thee thou shalt go, and whatsoever I shall command thee thou shalt speak to them »; and the Prophet in the Psalm: « Therefore was I directed to all thy commandments » etc. — He who is thus obedient in work ought to be humble in his own estimation, and therefore he subjoins: *Say: We are unprofitable servants; we have done that which we ought to do,* by *the recognition of one's own infirmity; servants,* I say, because bought, beyond what you are worth; the first to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter[^p433-2]: « You are bought with a great price; glorify and bear » etc.; and the first of Peter, the first: « You were not redeemed with corruptible gold or silver from your vain conversation, but with the precious blood as of a Lamb unspotted » etc. *Unprofitable,* not because our good works have no reward or fruit, since they are profitable *to us,* but because by this we confer nothing on *the divine goodness;* the Psalm[^p433-3]: « I have said to the Lord: Thou art my God, for thou hast no need of my goods ». — Nothing is what we *do* in comparison with what we have received and owe to God; whence the Gloss[^p433-4]: « *He who came to minister and not to be ministered unto* has made us his debtors, that we, not trusting in our works, but dreading his examination, when all things that are commanded have been fulfilled, may know ourselves to be imperfect ». Therefore the Prophet said[^p433-5]: « What shall I render to the Lord for all the things that he hath rendered to me »? — Likewise, nothing is what we *bear* with respect to what we await; to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p433-6]: « The sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come, that shall be revealed in us ». Whence the Gloss on this: *We are unprofitable servants,* « because God needs not our good works; or because the sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come, that shall be revealed in us ». He, then, who humbles himself thus is he who disposes himself to perfect and eternal glory. Whence this humility, which attributes all to God and nothing to itself, will be perfectly in *the fatherland,* according to that of the Apocalypse, the fourth chapter[^p433-7]: « The four and twenty ancients fell down before him that sat on the throne and adored him that liveth for ever and ever, and cast their crowns before the throne, saying: Thou art worthy; O Lord our God, to receive glory, and honour, and power » etc. And in *the state of the way* this is in perfect men, according to that of Daniel, the ninth chapter[^p433-8]: « To us confusion of face, to our kings and to our princes and to our fathers that have sinned; but to thee, the Lord our God, mercy and forgiveness » etc. Therefore every day we ought to say that of the first to Timothy, the first chapter: « Now to the King of ages, immortal, invisible, the only God, be honour and glory for ever and ever. Amen ».

LUCAE*, THE SECOND OF CAPITULUM XVII: THE LEADING BY THE HAND THROUGH A TWOFOLD SENSIBLE EXAMPLE.** ¶ 12 (*Et dixerunt Apostoli Domino*) is the second member of `c17-v1-4` ¶ 1's divisio (*manuducit exemplo sensibili, ibi: Et dixerunt Apostoli Domino*) and divides it by the twofold root of spiritual piety, *fides fervida* and *humilitas timorata* (Ecclus. 25:16): the example leading to fervent faith (¶¶ 12–17: the birth of faith in the receiving of a divine gift, *Adauge nobis fidem*, faith as the primary gift, obtaining and keeping the others (¶ 13); its progress in the cooperation of free choice, the grain of mustard seed — smallness, sharpness, increase: the captivating of the understanding, the kindling of the affection, the multitude of good works (¶ 14); its power in the execution of a command, the mulberry tree — *according to the letter* over the elements (¶ 15), *according to tropology* over the devil and his temptations (¶ 16), *according to allegory* the preaching of the cross transplanted from the Jews into the sea of the Gentiles (¶ 17)); and the example leading to God-fearing humility (¶ 18, *Quis autem vestrum habens servum*, Vers. 7: a syllogism — the major, the dutifulness of servitude, ploughing and feeding, the return from the field and the passing over to the banquet of contemplation (¶¶ 18–19); the minor, the necessity of serving, *Nunquid gratiam habet servo illi* — the servant's work more a debt than a gift, all things from God, under God, for God (¶ 20); the conclusion, the humility of spiritual servitude, *Servi inutiles sumus*, the fulfilment of obedience, the recognition of one's own infirmity, perfect in the fatherland and in perfect men on the way (¶ 21)).
