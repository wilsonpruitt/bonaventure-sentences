# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XV, Vers. 25-32
## *Erat autem filius eius etc. Postquam ostendit misericordiam pii patris in acceptando conversionem poenitentis, hic secundo ostendit misericordiam eius in placando indignationem filii obedientis*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 398 -->
42. *Erat autem filius eius* etc. Postquam ostendit misericordiam pii patris in acceptando conversionem poenitentis, hic secundo ostendit *misericordiam eius in placando indignationem filii obedientis*[^p398-12]. Dividitur autem haec pars in duas. In quarum prima exprimitur *impatientia filii* ad indignationem concipiendam; secundo, *clementia patris* ad indignationem placandam, ibi[^p398-13]: *Pater ergo illius egressus* etc. — Circa *impatientiam filii indignati* tria introducit Evangelista, ex quibus indignatio illa originem traxit, scilicet *difficultatem laboris peracti, solemnitatem gaudii auditi* et *liberalitatem paterni beneficii indicati;* ex quorum consideratione incidit ille filius maior in indignationem.

43. (Vers. 25.). Primo igitur quantum ad *difficultatem laboris peracti* dicit: *Erat autem filius eius senior in agro. Filius* iste *senior* dicitur ille qui moribus maturior est, patri conformior et obedientior; quia, Sapientiae quarto[^p398-14], « cani sunt sensus hominis, et aetas senectutis vita immaculata ». Et quia bona
<!-- page 399 -->
vita nunquam est otiosa[^p399-1], ideo dicit, quod erat *in agro,* scilicet ad laborandum, ut posset dicere illud Zachariae decimo tertio: « Homo agricola ego sum, quoniam Adam exemplum meum ab adolescentia mea ». Unde in agro laborabat secundum Sapientis consilium, Proverbiorum duodecimo[^p399-2]: « Qui operatur terram suam saturabitur panibus »; et Proverbiorum vigesimo quarto: « Praepara foris opus tuum et diligenter exerce agrum tuum »; quia sic dictum est homini Genesis tertio: « In sudore vultus tui vesceris pane tuo ».

44. Secundo quantum ad *solemnitatem gaudii auditi* subdit: *Et cum veniret et appropinquaret domui, audivit symphoniam et chorum.* Per *domum* istam intelligitur congregatio iustorum; Psalmus[^p399-3]: « Deus in loco sancto suo, Deus, qui inhabitare facit unius moris in domo »; et primae ad Timotheum tertio: « Ut scias, quomodo oporteat te in domo Dei conversari, quae est Ecclesia Dei vivi, columna et firmamentum veritatis ». In hac domo auditur *symphonia et chorus* per concordem laetitiam quantum ad conformitatem interiorem et exteriorem morum, animorum et sermonum. « Est enim *symphonia* concordia et collectio quorumcumque sonorum[^p399-4] », et designat laetitiam, quae venit ex *concordia animorum.* Unde et Ambrosius: « Symphonia est diversarum aetatum et virtutum velut variarum chordarum indiscreta concordia, secundum quod dicitur Actuum quarto: *Multitudinis credentium erat cor unum et anima una* ». « *Chorus* vero est collectio vocum[^p399-5] » et significat laetitiam, quae venit ex *concordia vocum et verborum* in praedicando et laudando Deum, secundum illud Prophetae in Psalmo[^p399-6]: « Laudate eum in tympano et choro », et hoc est in sancta Ecclesia; primae ad Corinthios primo: « Obsecro, ut id ipsum dicatis omnes ». Et de his duobus dicitur ad Philippenses secundo: « Si qua consolatio in Christo, si quod solatium caritatis, si qua societas spiritus, si qua viscera miserationis; implete gaudium meum, ut idem sapiatis, eandem caritatem habentes, unanimes, id ipsum sentientes ». — Hanc ergo harmoniam iucundam in hac domo Dei audit qui ad illam *appropinquat. Appropinquatio* autem ista est per considerationem sacrae Scripturae, quae quasi est quoddam ostium ad cognoscendum ea quae sunt intra unitatem sanctae Ecclesiae; Ecclesiastici ultimo[^p399-7]: « Appropinquate ad me, indocti, et congregamini in domo disciplinae ». Et tunc, quando quis attendit Scripturam, in ipsa audit istius Ecclesiae concordiam et harmoniam intra eius membra; quia tota Scriptura nihil aliud docet quam caritatis concordiam et harmoniam, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p399-8]: « In his duobus mandatis » etc.; et ad Romanos decimo tertio: « Qui diligit proximum legem implevit ». Nam primae ad Timotheum primo: « Finis praecepti est caritas de corde puro » etc.

45. (Vers. 26.). Tertio quantum ad *liberalitatem paterni beneficii indicati* subdit: *Et vocavit unum de servis et interrogavit, quae*[^p399-9] *haec essent.* Iste *unus servus* vocatus est ordo praedicantium et docentium, de quo servo supra decimo quarto[^p399-10]: « Misit servum suum hora coenae dicere invitatis »; ubi Glossa: « Servus est ordo praedicantium »; ab hoc servo requirenda sunt ecclesiastica Sacramenta et mysteria Scripturarum, quia, Malachiae secundo[^p399-11], « labia sacerdotis custodiunt scientiam, et legem requirunt ex ore eius, quia angelus Domini exercituum est ».

46. (Vers. 27.). Et quoniam verus praedicator est annuntiator praecipuus divinae pietatis, ideo subdit: *Isque dixit illi: Frater tuus venit,* per *humilitatem poenitentiae;* Isaiae vigesimo primo[^p399-12]: « Si quaeritis, quaerite; convertimini et venite »; et sexagesimo: « Filii tui de longe venient, et filiae tuae de latere surgent ». — *Et occidit pater tuus vitulum saginatum,* per *refectionem eucharistiae,* secundum illud Ioannis sexto[^p399-13]: « Caro mea vere est cibus, et sanguis meus vere est potus. Qui manducat meam carnem et bibit meum sanguinem » etc. — *Quia salvum illum recepit,* per *recuperationem iustitiae,* quam solus Christus potest restituere, secundum illud Actuum quarto[^p399-14]: « Non est aliud nomen sub caelo datum hominibus, in quo oporteat nos salvos fieri ». Haec autem tria praedicat *sacra Scriptura* tota, scilicet poenitentiam, gratiam et salutem. Unde *praecursor Christi* haec praedicabat, scilicet: « Agite poenitentiam[^p399-15] », et promittens gratiam et salutem; similiter et *ipse Christus:* « Poenitentiam agite »; similiter et *universus chorus Apostolorum,*
<!-- page 400 -->
et maxime Petrus et Paulus, qui non tantum verbo, verum etiam exemplo clamabant, secundum illud primae ad Timotheum primo[^p400-1]: « Christus Iesus venit in hunc mundum, peccatores salvos facere, quorum primus ego sum. Sed ideo misericordiam consecutus sum, ut in me primo ostenderet Christus Iesus omnem patientiam, ad informationem eorum qui credituri sunt illi in vitam aeternam ». Unde et propter peccatores dicebat, *vitulum saginatum esse occisum,* cum dicebat ad Romanos quinto[^p400-2]: « Commendat autem caritatem suam Deus in nobis, quoniam, cum adhuc peccatores essemus, Christus pro nobis mortuus est. Multo igitur magis nunc, iustificati in sanguine ipsius, salvi erimus ab ira per ipsum »; et paulo post: « Ubi abundavit delictum, superabundavit et gratia; ut, sicut regnavit peccatum in mortem, ita gratia regnet per iustitiam ».

47. (Vers. 28.). Et quia iustus, de suis meritis praesumens, hanc iustitiam non acceptat, immo potius indignatur contra misericordiam Dei, quasi inique agat reddendo bona pro malis; ideo subdit: *Indignatus est autem et nolebat introire.* Qui enim non acceptat affluentiam misericordiae Dei, sed sufficientiam iustitiae suae, non potest introire ad unitatis ecclesiasticae caritatem; et hoc est, quod dicitur ad Romanos decimo[^p400-3] de talibus: « Ignorantes Dei iustitiam et suam volentes statuere, iustitiae Dei non sunt subiecti ». Unde et hac de causa populus Iudaicus non vult introire in ecclesiasticam unitatem, quia, superbiens de sua iustitia, non acceptat summi Patris misericordiam peccatoribus impensam. Et contra tales dicitur ad Romanos tertio[^p400-4]: « Iustificati gratis per gratiam ipsius, per redemptionem quae est in Christo Iesu, quem proposuit Deus propitiatorem per fidem in sanguine ipsius », « ut sit ipse iustus et iustificans eum qui est ex fide Iesu Christi. Ubi est ergo gloriatio tua »? et sequitur: « Exclusa est ». « Arbitramur enim, iustificari hominem per fidem sine operibus Legis ». Iste igitur, praesumens de iustitia et indignans pro misericordia fratri impensa, non ambulat secundum iustitiam, sed secundum iniustitiam, quia, Proverbiorum vigesimo nono[^p400-5], « qui ad indignandum facilis est erit ad peccata proclivior ».

48. *Pater ergo illius egressus* etc. Post descriptam impatientiam filii ad indignationem concipiendam hic secundo[^p400-6] ostenditur *clementia patris ad indignationem placandam,* ubi tria introducuntur. Primo enim praemittitur ex parte patris *allocutio pia;* secundo, a parte filii *responsio proterva;* tertio subiungitur *persuasio placativa.*

Primo igitur quantum ad patris allocutionem piissimam dicit: *Pater ergo illius egressus coepit rogare eum*[^p400-7]. *Egreditur* autem pater ad exteriora per condescensionem, secundum illud Habacuc tertio[^p400-8]: « Egressus es in salutem populi tui et in salutem cum Christo tuo »; egressus scilicet iste non est aliud nisi manifestare se exterius in carne; Isaiae sexagesimo secundo: « Propter Sion non tacebo et propter Ierusalem non quiescam, donec egrediatur ut splendor iustus eius » etc. — Et quia hic egressus fuit ex magna pietate ad hominem salvandum, ideo dicit: *Coepit rogare eum;* ad Hebraeos duodecimo[^p400-9]: « Tanquam filiis se offert vobis Deus ». Rogat ergo, si contra ipsum dedignamur, secundum illud Isaiae trigesimo: « Audies verbum post tergum monentis: Haec est via bona, ambulate in ea ». Unde ipse Deus Pater contra ipsum filium indignatum non indignatur, sed potius eum rogat et consolatur, ut impleatur illud Isaiae sexagesimo sexto[^p400-10]: « Quomodo si cui mater blandiatur, ita ego consolabor vos »; nec immerito, quia, sicut dicitur secundae ad Corinthios primo, « ipse est pater misericordiarum et Deus totius consolationis, qui consolatur nos in omni tribulatione nostra ». Unde Ieremiae tertio[^p400-11] dicitur de hac allocutione: « Ergo saltem amodo voca me: Pater meus, dux virginitatis meae tu es » etc. Et sic impletur illud Iob decimo nono: « Orabam filios uteri mei ».

49. (Vers. 29.). Secundo quantum ad *filii responsionem protervam* subdit: *At ille respondens dixit patri suo: Ecce, tot annis servio tibi;* ubi proterve respondet, et quia *se ipsum iactat de iustitia,* dicens, quod longo tempore servivit patri, cum tamen ipse Pater obsequio nostro non indigeat, secundum illud Psalmi[^p400-12]: « Dixi Domino: Deus meus es tu, quoniam bonorum meorum non eges »; et Iob trigesimo quinto: « Porro, si iuste egeris, quid
<!-- page 401 -->
donabis ei, aut quid de manu tua accipiet »? Ostentat ergo se in hoc, quod iactat se pro patre magna fecisse. Ostentat etiam se in hoc quod dicit, se nihil contra eum fecisse, cum addit: *Et nunquam mandatum tuum praeterivi.* Et in hoc iustificat se ipsum, cum tamen dicat Apostolus primae ad Corinthios quarto[^p401-1]: « Nihil mihi conscius sum, sed non in hoc iustificatus sum ». Contra illud supercilium dicitur tertii Regum octavo[^p401-2]: « Non est homo, qui non peccet »; et primae Ioannis primo: « Si dixerimus, quia peccatum non habemus, mendaces sumus »; quia dicitur ad Romanos tertio: « Omnes peccaverunt et egent gloria Dei ». — *Ostentat* ergo *suam iustitiam, accusat et patris duritiam,* cum subdit: *Et nunquam dedisti mihi haedum, ut cum amicis meis epularer;* quasi dicat: semper fuisti mihi durus et non dedisti mihi aliquam interiorem consolationem, quae esset indicativa dilectionis tuae. Et tamen hoc facit Deus iusto iudicio et occulto, secundum illud Ecclesiastae nono[^p401-3]: « Sunt iusti atque sapientes, et opera eorum in manu Dei; et tamen nescit homo, utrum amore, vel odio dignus sit, sed omnia in futurum servantur incerta ». Iste autem, non attendens occulta Dei iudicia, patris duritiam accusat, quasi diceret illud Iob trigesimo[^p401-4]: « Mutatus es mihi in crudelem et in duritia manus tuae adversaris mihi ». In hoc igitur accusat patris duritiam.

50. (Vers. 30.) Similiter *reprehendit patris clementiam,* cum adiungit: *Sed postquam filius tuus hic;* quasi dicat: non frater meus — sicut dicebat Dominus ex indignatione Exodi trigesimo secundo[^p401-5]: « Descende de monte, quia peccavit populus tuus », *tuus,* inquit, non meus — quia propter culpam dedignor, ut fratrem ipsum recognoscam. — Unde addit: *Qui devoravit suam substantiam cum meretricibus, venit,* et ita dignus esset ira, quia prodigus, immundus et foedus, et ideo disperdendus; Psalmus[^p401-6]: « Perdes eos qui fornicantur abs te »; et Threnorum primo: « Facta est Ierusalem quasi polluta menstruis inter eos. Iustus est Dominus, quia os eius ad iracundiam provocavi ». — *Occidisti ei*[^p401-7] *vitulum saginatum,* ostendendo summam benevolentiam, quam ostendit, cum alicui devotionem dat circa beneficium dominicae passionis, secundum illud Canticorum primo[^p401-8]: « Fasciculus myrrhae dilectus meus mihi, inter ubera mea commorabitur. Botrus Cypri dilectus meus mihi in vineis Engaddi ». *Occidisti ei,* id est, per devotionem dedisti, ac si solum pro ipso occisus esset, sicut et Paulus dicebat ad Galatas secundo[^p401-9]: « Quod autem nunc vivo in carne, in fide vivo Filii Dei, qui dilexit me et tradidit semetipsum pro me »; unde et praemittebat: « Christo confixus sum cruci ». — Arguit ergo iste sua responsione proterva et patris clementiam et accusat duritiam et ostentat suam iustitiam, nec attendit, quod « Deus superbis resistit, humilibus autem dat gratiam », sicut dicitur Iacobi quarto[^p401-10]. Propter quod etiam Deus peccatoribus est *dulcis,* ut eos excitet ad spem, et *durus* iustis, ut eos inclinet ad humilitatem; propter quod infra decimo septimo: « Cum feceritis quaecumque praecepta sunt vobis, dicite: Servi inutiles sumus » etc.

51. (Vers. 31.). Tertio quantum ad *satisfactionem placativam* subiungit: *At ipse dixit illi: Fili, tu semper mecum es.* Nota hic benignissimam patris responsionem, quia, cum posset arguere filium de praesumtione, de indignatione, de protervitate et de inhumanitate; tamen non arguit, ne magis perturbet, sed pie blanditur, ut leniat; quia, Proverbiorum decimo quinto[^p401-11], « responsio mollis frangit iram ». Non ergo arguit perturbatum, sed dulciter et rationabiliter placat iratum, *laudando* scilicet *filii obedientiam* in hoc, quod dicit: *Fili, tu semper mecum es,* id est, semper mihi obtemperas, ita ut sis de numero illorum quibus dicitur infra vigesimo secundo[^p401-12]: « Vos estis, qui permansistis mecum in tentationibus meis »; ut possis dicere illud Psalmi: « Ut iumentum factus sum apud te, et ego semper tecum ». *Semper mecum es,* in domo manens sicut filius; Ioannis octavo[^p401-13]: « Filius manet in domo in aeternum ». — Placat etiam *ostendendo benevolentiam,* cum addit: *Et omnia mea tua sunt,* per benevolentiam scilicet caritatis, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios tertio[^p401-14]: « Omnia vestra sunt,
<!-- page 402 -->
sive Paulus, sive Apollo, sive praesentia, sive futura. Omnia vestra sunt, vos autem Christi, Christus autem Dei »; quia, sicut dicitur ad Romanos octavo, « qui proprio Filio non pepercit, sed pro nobis omnibus tradidit eum, quomodo non etiam cum illo omnia nobis donavit »? Vult ergo dicere, quod, licet magna non ostendat, tamen magna reservat, secundum illud Isaiae sexagesimo quarto[^p402-1]: « Oculus non vidit, Deus, absque te, quae praeparasti exspectantibus te ». Omnia ergo sunt viri iusti, vel in re, vel in spe. Nam cum sint quaedam *superiora,* quaedam *inferiora,* quaedam *paria; superiora* sunt viri iusti ad *fruendum;* Psalmus[^p402-2]: « Deus cordis mei et pars mea Deus in aeternum ». *Aequalia,* Angeli scilicet, iusti sunt ad *coadiuvandum;* ad Hebraeos primo: « Omnes sunt administratorii spiritus, in ministerium missi propter eos qui hereditatem capient salutis ». *Inferiora* iusti sunt ad *utendum* et *dominandum;* Psalmus: « Quid est homo, quod memor es eius, aut filius hominis, quoniam visitas eum »? « Omnia subiecisti sub pedibus eius » etc. Et in hoc apparet mira patris benevolentia.

52. (Vers. 32.). Placat etiam *approbando impensam misericordiam,* cum subiungit: *Epulari autem oportebat et gaudere,* caritate scilicet urgente, quia caritas est quae cogit de bono gaudere. Unde Apostolus secundae ad Corinthios quinto[^p402-3]: « Sive mente excedimus Deo, sive sobrii sumus vobis. Caritas enim Christi urget nos ». Et sicut caritatis est *uri* de alieno scandalo, secundum illud secundae ad Corinthios undecimo: « Quis scandalizatur, et ego non uror »? sic *laetari et refici* de alieno remedio. — Ideo dicit: *Quia frater tuus hic mortuus fuerat*[^p402-4] *et revixit, perierat et inventus est; mortuus fuerat, deserendo Christum,* qui de se dicit Ioannis decimo quarto[^p402-5]: « Ego sum via, veritas et vita »; et primo: « Vita erat lux hominum ». *Perierat, amando peccatum,* quia, Ecclesiastici tertio, « qui amat periculum peribit in illo ». *Revixit* autem, *redeundo ad Christi gratiam;* Ioannis undecimo: « Ego sum resurrectio et vita » etc. *Et inventus est, redeundo ad poenitentiam,* sicut supra eodem[^p402-6] inventa fuit *ovicula,* inventa etiam fuit perdita *drachma.* Et ideo *epulari oportebat et gaudere,* quia, sicut supra dictum est eodem[^p402-7], « gaudium est Angelis Dei super uno peccatore poenitentiam agente », gaudium etiam pastori de ove, gaudium similiter mulieri de drachma: ergo multo fortius gaudium debet esse fratri de fratre suo et patri de filio reinvento.

## English
<!-- page 398 -->
42. *Now his elder son* etc. After he has shown the mercy of the pious father in accepting the conversion of the penitent, here, in the second place, he shows *his mercy in placating the indignation of the obedient son*[^p398-12][^tr-pietas]. Now this part is divided into two. In the first of which there is expressed *the impatience of the son* in conceiving indignation; second, *the clemency of the father* in placating the indignation, at[^p398-13]: *His father therefore going forth* etc. — Concerning *the impatience of the indignant son* the Evangelist introduces three things, from which that indignation drew its origin, namely *the difficulty of the labour accomplished, the solemnity of the joy heard* and *the liberality of the fatherly benefit made known;* from the consideration of which that elder son fell into indignation.

43. (Vers. 25.). First, then, as to *the difficulty of the labour accomplished* he says: *Now his elder son was in the field. This elder son* is called he who is riper in manners, more conformed to the father and more obedient; because, Wisdom, the fourth chapter[^p398-14], « the understanding of a man is grey hairs, and a spotless life is old age ». And since a good
<!-- page 399 -->
life is never idle[^p399-1], therefore he says that he was *in the field,* namely for labouring, so that he could say that of Zacharias, the thirteenth chapter: « I am a husbandman, for Adam is my example from my youth ». Whence he laboured in the field according to the counsel of the Wise man, Proverbs, the twelfth chapter[^p399-2]: « He that tilleth his land shall be satisfied with bread »; and Proverbs, the twenty-fourth chapter: « Prepare thy work without, and diligently till thy ground »; because thus it was said to man in Genesis, the third chapter: « In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat thy bread ».

44. Second, as to *the solemnity of the joy heard* he subjoins: *And when he came and drew near to the house, he heard symphony and choir.* By this *house* is understood the congregation of the just; the Psalm[^p399-3]: « God in his holy place: God who maketh men of one manner to dwell in a house »; and the first to Timothy, the third chapter: « That thou mayest know how thou oughtest to behave thyself in the house of God, which is the Church of the living God, the pillar and ground of the truth ». In this house *symphony and choir* are heard through concordant gladness as to the interior and exterior conformity of manners, of minds and of speech. « For *symphony* is the concord and collection of any sounds whatever[^p399-4] », and it designates the gladness which comes from *the concord of minds.* Whence Ambrose too: « Symphony is the undivided concord of diverse ages and virtues, as of various strings, according to what is said in the Acts, the fourth chapter: *The multitude of believers had one heart and one soul* ». « But *choir* is the collection of voices[^p399-5] », and it signifies the gladness which comes from *the concord of voices and of words* in preaching and praising God, according to that of the Prophet in the Psalm[^p399-6]: « Praise him with timbrel and choir », and this is in the holy Church; the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter: « I beseech you that you all speak the same thing ». And of these two it is said to the Philippians, the second chapter: « If there be any consolation in Christ, if any comfort of charity, if any society of the spirit, if any bowels of commiseration: fulfil ye my joy, that you be of one mind, having the same charity, being of one accord, agreeing in sentiment ». — This pleasant harmony, then, in this house of God he hears who *draws near* to it. Now this *drawing near* is through the consideration of sacred Scripture, which is as it were a kind of door for knowing the things that are within the unity of holy Church; Ecclesiasticus, the last chapter[^p399-7]: « Draw near to me, ye unlearned, and gather yourselves together into the house of discipline ». And then, when one attends to Scripture, he hears in it the concord and harmony of this Church among its members; because the whole of Scripture teaches nothing else than the concord and harmony of charity, according to that of Matthew, the twenty-second chapter[^p399-8]: « On these two commandments » etc.; and to the Romans, the thirteenth chapter: « He that loveth his neighbour hath fulfilled the law ». For the first to Timothy, the first chapter: « The end of the commandment is charity from a pure heart » etc.

45. (Vers. 26.). Third, as to *the liberality of the fatherly benefit made known* he subjoins: *And he called one of the servants, and asked what*[^p399-9] *these things were.* This *one servant* who is called is the order of those preaching and teaching, of which servant above, the fourteenth chapter[^p399-10]: « He sent his servant at the hour of supper to say to them that were invited »; where the Gloss: « The servant is the order of preachers »; from this servant the ecclesiastical Sacraments and the mysteries of the Scriptures are to be sought, because, Malachias, the second chapter[^p399-11], « the lips of the priest keep knowledge, and they seek the law at his mouth, because he is the angel of the Lord of hosts ».

46. (Vers. 27.). And since the true preacher is the chief announcer of the divine piety, therefore he subjoins: *And he said to him: Thy brother is come,* through *the humility of penance;* Isaias, the twenty-first chapter[^p399-12]: « If you seek, seek: return, come »; and the sixtieth: « Thy sons shall come from afar, and thy daughters shall rise up at thy side ». — *And thy father hath killed the fatted calf,* through *the refection of the eucharist,* according to that of John, the sixth chapter[^p399-13]: « My flesh is meat indeed, and my blood is drink indeed. He that eateth my flesh and drinketh my blood » etc. — *Because he hath received him safe,* through *the recovery of justice,* which Christ alone can restore, according to that of the Acts, the fourth chapter[^p399-14]: « There is no other name under heaven given to men, whereby we must be saved ». Now these three things the whole of *sacred Scripture* preaches, namely penance, grace and salvation. Whence *the forerunner of Christ* preached these things, namely: « Do penance[^p399-15] », and promising grace and salvation; likewise also *Christ himself:* « Do penance »; likewise also *the whole choir of the Apostles,*
<!-- page 400 -->
and most of all Peter and Paul, who cried out not only by word but also by example, according to that of the first to Timothy, the first chapter[^p400-1]: « Christ Jesus came into this world to save sinners, of whom I am the chief. But for this cause have I obtained mercy, that in me first Christ Jesus might shew forth all patience, for the formation of them that shall believe in him unto life everlasting ». Whence also he was saying, on account of sinners, *that the fatted calf had been killed,* when he said to the Romans, the fifth chapter[^p400-2]: « But God commendeth his charity in us, because when as yet we were sinners, Christ died for us. Much more therefore, being now justified by his blood, shall we be saved from wrath through him »; and a little after: « Where sin abounded, grace did more abound; that as sin hath reigned unto death, so also grace might reign by justice ».

47. (Vers. 28.). And since the just man, presuming on his own merits, does not accept this justice, nay rather is indignant against the mercy of God, as though he acted unjustly by rendering good things for evil; therefore he subjoins: *And he was indignant, and would not go in.* For he who does not accept the abundance of the mercy of God, but the sufficiency of his own justice, cannot go in to the charity of ecclesiastical unity; and this is what is said to the Romans, the tenth chapter[^p400-3], of such men: « Not knowing the justice of God, and seeking to establish their own, they have not submitted themselves to the justice of God ». Whence also for this cause the Jewish people will not go in to ecclesiastical unity, because, being proud of its own justice, it does not accept the mercy of the highest Father bestowed on sinners. And against such it is said to the Romans, the third chapter[^p400-4]: « Being justified freely by his grace, through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus, whom God hath proposed to be a propitiator, through faith in his blood », « that he himself may be just, and the justifier of him who is of the faith of Jesus Christ. Where is then thy boasting »? and it follows: « It is excluded ». « For we account a man to be justified by faith without the works of the Law ». This one, then, presuming on justice and indignant over the mercy bestowed on his brother, does not walk according to justice but according to injustice, because, Proverbs, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p400-5], « he that is easily stirred up to indignation shall be more prone to sins ».

48. *His father therefore going forth* etc. After the impatience of the son in conceiving indignation has been described, here, in the second place[^p400-6], there is shown *the clemency of the father in placating the indignation,* where three things are introduced. For first there is put forward on the father's part *a pious address;* second, on the son's part *a froward answer;* third there is subjoined *a placative persuasion.*

First, then, as to the father's most pious address he says: *His father therefore going forth began to entreat him*[^p400-7]. Now the father *goes forth* to outward things through condescension, according to that of Habacuc, the third chapter[^p400-8]: « Thou wentest forth for the salvation of thy people, for salvation with thy Christ »; this going forth, namely, is nothing else than to manifest himself outwardly in the flesh; Isaias, the sixty-second chapter: « For Sion's sake I will not hold my peace, and for the sake of Jerusalem I will not rest, till her just one come forth as brightness » etc. — And since this going forth was out of great piety for saving man, therefore he says: *He began to entreat him;* to the Hebrews, the twelfth chapter[^p400-9]: « God offereth himself to you as to sons ». He entreats, then, if we disdain against him, according to that of Isaias, the thirtieth chapter: « Thou shalt hear the word of one admonishing behind thy back: This is the good way, walk ye in it ». Whence God the Father himself is not indignant against the indignant son himself, but rather entreats and consoles him, that there may be fulfilled that of Isaias, the sixty-sixth chapter[^p400-10]: « As one whom the mother caresseth, so will I console you »; and not without merit, because, as is said in the second to the Corinthians, the first chapter, « he is the Father of mercies and the God of all consolation, who consoleth us in all our tribulation ». Whence in Jeremias, the third chapter[^p400-11], it is said of this address: « Therefore at least from this time call to me: My father, thou art the guide of my virginity » etc. And thus is fulfilled that of Job, the nineteenth chapter: « I entreated the children of my womb ».

49. (Vers. 29.). Second, as to *the froward answer of the son* he subjoins: *And he answering said to his father: Behold, for so many years do I serve thee;* where he answers frowardly, and since *he boasts himself of justice,* saying that he has served the father for a long time, although the Father himself has no need of our service, according to that of the Psalm[^p400-12]: « I have said to the Lord: Thou art my God, for thou hast no need of my goods »; and Job, the thirty-fifth chapter: « Moreover, if thou do justly, what
<!-- page 401 -->
shalt thou give him, or what shall he receive of thy hand »? He makes a show of himself, then, in this, that he boasts of having done great things for the father. He makes a show of himself also in this, that he says he has done nothing against him, when he adds: *And I have never transgressed thy commandment.* And in this he justifies himself, although the Apostle says in the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter[^p401-1]: « I am not conscious to myself of any thing, yet am I not hereby justified ». Against that haughtiness it is said in the third of Kings, the eighth chapter[^p401-2]: « There is no man who sinneth not »; and in the first of John, the first chapter: « If we say that we have no sin, we are liars »; because it is said to the Romans, the third chapter: « All have sinned, and do need the glory of God ». — He *makes a show,* then, *of his own justice, and accuses also the father's hardness,* when he subjoins: *And yet thou hast never given me a kid to make merry with my friends;* as if he said: thou hast always been hard to me and hast not given me any interior consolation which would be indicative of thy dilection. And yet God does this by a just and hidden judgement, according to that of Ecclesiastes, the ninth chapter[^p401-3]: « There are just men and wise men, and their works are in the hand of God; and yet man knoweth not whether he be worthy of love or hatred, but all things are kept uncertain for the time to come ». But this one, not attending to the hidden judgements of God, accuses the father's hardness, as though he said that of Job, the thirtieth chapter[^p401-4]: « Thou art changed to be cruel toward me, and in the hardness of thy hand thou art against me ». In this, then, he accuses the father's hardness.

50. (Vers. 30.) Likewise *he censures the father's clemency,* when he adds: *But as soon as this thy son;* as if he said: not my brother — as the Lord was saying out of indignation, Exodus, the thirty-second chapter[^p401-5]: « Go down from the mountain, for thy people hath sinned », *thy,* he says, not mine — because on account of the fault I disdain to recognize him as a brother. — Whence he adds: *Who hath devoured his substance with harlots, is come,* and so he would be worthy of wrath, because prodigal, unclean and foul, and therefore to be destroyed; the Psalm[^p401-6]: « Thou wilt destroy them that commit fornication away from thee »; and Lamentations, the first chapter: « Jerusalem is become as one polluted with her menses among them. The Lord is just, for I have provoked his mouth to wrath ». — *Thou hast killed for him*[^p401-7] *the fatted calf,* showing the highest benevolence, which he shows when he gives to someone devotion concerning the benefit of the Lord's passion, according to that of the Canticles, the first chapter[^p401-8]: « A bundle of myrrh is my beloved to me, he shall abide between my breasts. A cluster of cypress my beloved is to me, in the vineyards of Engaddi ». *Thou hast killed for him,* that is, thou hast given through devotion, as if he had been slain for him alone, just as Paul too was saying to the Galatians, the second chapter[^p401-9]: « And that I live now in the flesh, I live in the faith of the Son of God, who loved me, and delivered himself for me »; whence he also put first: « With Christ I am nailed to the cross ». — So this one by his froward answer both reproves the father's clemency and accuses his hardness and makes a show of his own justice, and does not attend that « God resisteth the proud, and giveth grace to the humble », as is said in James, the fourth chapter[^p401-10]. On account of which God is also *sweet* to sinners, that he may rouse them to hope, and *hard* to the just, that he may bend them to humility; on account of which below, the seventeenth chapter: « When you shall have done whatsoever things are commanded you, say: We are unprofitable servants » etc.

51. (Vers. 31.). Third, as to *the placative satisfaction* he subjoins: *But he said to him: Son, thou art always with me.* Note here the father's most kind answer, because, though he could reprove the son for presumption, for indignation, for frowardness and for inhumanity, yet he does not reprove him, lest he disturb him more, but piously caresses him, that he may soften him; because, Proverbs, the fifteenth chapter[^p401-11], « a mild answer breaketh wrath ». He does not, then, reprove the disturbed one, but sweetly and reasonably placates the angry one, *by praising,* namely, *the obedience of the son* in this, that he says: *Son, thou art always with me,* that is, thou dost always comply with me, so that thou art of the number of those to whom it is said below, the twenty-second chapter[^p401-12]: « You are they who have continued with me in my temptations »; so that thou canst say that of the Psalm: « I am become as a beast before thee, and I am always with thee ». *Thou art always with me,* abiding in the house as a son; John, the eighth chapter[^p401-13]: « The son abideth in the house for ever ». — He placates also *by showing benevolence,* when he adds: *And all I have is thine,* namely through the benevolence of charity, according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p401-14]: « All things are yours,
<!-- page 402 -->
whether it be Paul, or Apollo, or things present, or things to come. All are yours, and you are Christ's, and Christ is God's »; because, as is said to the Romans, the eighth chapter, « he that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how hath he not also with him given us all things »? He means, then, that, although he does not show great things, yet he reserves great things, according to that of Isaias, the sixty-fourth chapter[^p402-1]: « The eye hath not seen, O God, besides thee, what things thou hast prepared for them that wait for thee ». All things, then, are the just man's, either in fact or in hope. For since some things are *superior,* some *inferior,* some *equal; superior* things are the just man's for *enjoying;* the Psalm[^p402-2]: « The God of my heart, and the God that is my portion for ever ». *Equal* things, namely the Angels, are the just men's for *helping together;* to the Hebrews, the first chapter: « They are all ministering spirits, sent to minister for them who shall receive the inheritance of salvation ». *Inferior* things are the just men's for *using* and *ruling;* the Psalm: « What is man, that thou art mindful of him, or the son of man, that thou visitest him »? « Thou hast subjected all things under his feet » etc. And in this there appears the wonderful benevolence of the father.

52. (Vers. 32.). He placates also *by approving the mercy bestowed,* when he subjoins: *But it was fit that we should make merry and be glad,* charity, namely, urging, because it is charity that compels one to rejoice over good. Whence the Apostle in the second to the Corinthians, the fifth chapter[^p402-3]: « For whether we be transported in mind, it is to God; or whether we be sober, it is for you. For the charity of Christ presseth us ». And just as it belongs to charity *to burn* over another's scandal, according to that of the second to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter: « Who is scandalized, and I do not burn »? so also *to be glad and be refreshed* over another's remedy. — Therefore he says: *For this thy brother was dead*[^p402-4] *and is come to life again, he was lost and is found; he was dead, by deserting Christ,* who says of himself in John, the fourteenth chapter[^p402-5]: « I am the way, and the truth, and the life »; and the first: « The life was the light of men ». *He was lost, by loving sin,* because, Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter, « he that loveth danger shall perish in it ». *He is come to life again,* then, *by returning to the grace of Christ;* John, the eleventh chapter: « I am the resurrection and the life » etc. *And is found, by returning to penance,* as above in the same chapter[^p402-6] *the little sheep* was found, and the lost *groat* was found too. And therefore *it was fit to make merry and be glad,* because, as was said above in the same chapter[^p402-7], « there is joy to the Angels of God upon one sinner doing penance », joy also to the shepherd over the sheep, joy likewise to the woman over the groat: much more, therefore, ought there to be joy to the brother over his brother and to the father over the son found again.

**THE EIGHTY-SEVENTH CHUNK OF THE *COMMENTARIUS IN EVANGELIUM S. LUCAE*, THE FIFTH AND LAST OF CAPITULUM XV: THE PARABLE OF THE PRODIGAL SON, ITS FOURTH MEMBER, SECOND HALF — IT CLOSES THE CAPITULUM.** ¶ 42 opens the second member of ¶ 33's division, the mercy of the pious father in placating the indignation of the obedient son, and divides it two ways: the impatience of the son in conceiving indignation; the clemency of the father in placating it (*Pater ergo illius egressus*, ¶ 48). The impatience from three things: the difficulty of the labour accomplished — the elder son ripe in manners, a good life never idle, in the field (¶ 43); the solemnity of the joy heard — the house as the congregation of the just, symphony as the concord of minds and choir as the concord of voices, the drawing near through the consideration of Scripture, which teaches nothing but the concord of charity (¶ 44); the liberality of the fatherly benefit made known — the one servant as the order of preachers (¶ 45), the preacher as the chief announcer of the divine piety, preaching penance, grace and salvation, the forerunner, Christ and the choir of the Apostles (¶ 46); then the son indignant, presuming on his own justice, the Jewish people refusing ecclesiastical unity (¶ 47).
