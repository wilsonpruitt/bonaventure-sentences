# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. IX, Vers. 46-56
## *Norma praesidendi tripliciter explicatur*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 243 -->

#### Norma praesidendi tripliciter explicatur.

85. *Intravit autem cogitatio* etc. Haec est tertia pars huius partis[^p243-5], in qua agitur de *missione Apostolorum,* ubi post explicationem formae praedicandi et viae proficiendi explicatur *norma praesidendi.* Apostoli enim gerebant personam *praedicatorum, virorum perfectorum* et *praelatorum.* Quoniam autem *praelatis* ad plebem regendam sunt necessaria tria, scilicet *humilitas animi, aequanimitas zeli* et *perspicacitas iudicii;* ideo pars ista tres habet; in quarum prima informat ad *animi humilitatem;* in secunda, ad *zeli aequanimitatem,* ibi[^p243-6]: *Factum est autem, dum complerentur* etc.; in tertia, ad *iudicii perspicacitatem,* ibi: *Factum est autem, ambulantibus illis* etc.

Prima pars habet duas. Primo enim informat *ad humilitatem* respectu *domesticorum;* secundo vero, respectu *extraneorum,* ibi[^p243-7]: *Respondens autem Ioannes dixit* etc.

In persuasione autem *humilitatis ad domesticos* habendae[^p243-8] tria introducuntur ab Evangelista. Primum est *laudandae humilitatis occasio* propter suum contrarium; secundum est *laudativa manuductio* per sensibile exemplum; tertium est *perfectae laudis conclusio* per definitivum iudicium.

86. (Vers. 46.). Primo ergo introducitur *laudandae humilitatis occasio,* et hoc propter suum contrarium repellendum, cum dicit: *Intravit autem cogitatio in eos, quis eorum maior esset.* — Et nota, quod Lucas dicit, in eos *cogitationem intrasse;* Marci nono[^p243-9] dicuntur *disputasse;* Matthaei decimo octavo dicuntur Dominum *interrogasse.* Nec est contradictio, quia hoc totum potuit esse, ut primo *cogitatio* intraret, deinde sequeretur *disputatio,* et postremo ad Magistrum deferretur *quaestio.* Et quod unus Evangelistarum reticet, alius dicit. — Haec autem *cogitatio* via erat ad superbiam et intraverat in eos ex corruptione antiqua, de qua Genesis octavo[^p243-10]: « Sensus et cogitatio humani cordis prona sunt in malum ab adolescentia sua », et maxime ad superbiam; quia, sicut dicitur Tobiae quarto, « superbiam in tuo sensu, aut in verbo nunquam dominari permittas; in ipsa enim initium sumsit omnis perditio ». Ideo viro superbe cogitanti dicitur Iob decimo quinto[^p243-11]: « Quid te elevat cor tuum, et quasi magna cogitans attonitos habes oculos »? Tales erant tunc Apostoli contra consilium Ecclesiastici sexto: « Non te extollas in cogitatione tua velut taurus, ne elidatur virtus tua ». Ista autem superba cogitatio habuit ingressum ex hoc, quod tres discipulos in montem duxerat secum; et tamen soli Petro dixerat: « Tibi dabo claves regni caelorum », ut dicitur Matthaei decimo sexto[^p243-12]; ideo quidam volebant Ioannem Petro parificare, alii volebant Petrum praeferre; et hoc est quod dicitur in Glossa: « Quia viderant Petrum » etc.

87. (Vers. 47.). Secundo vero adiungitur *laudativa manuductio* per sensibile exemplum, cum additur: *At Iesus videns cogitationes cordis eorum*[^p243-13], tanquam verus Deus, secundum illud primi Regum decimo sexto: « Homo videt ea quae patent, Deus
<!-- page 244 -->
autem intuetur cor »; et Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio: « Oculi Domini multo plus lucidiores super solem, circumspicientes omnes vias hominum et profundum abyssi et hominum corda intuentes ». — Et quoniam cogitationem videbat esse viam ad morbum, ideo tanquam sapiens medicus apponit remedium, cum ait: *Apprehendens puerum statuit eum secus se*[^p244-1], tanquam sibi conformem et propinquum; unde Matthaei decimo nono dicebat: « Sinite parvulos venire ad me, et nolite prohibere ad me venire, talium enim est regnum caelorum »; et Osee undecimo: « Quia puer Israel, et dilexi eum ». Et propter hoc dicebat Sapiens Sapientiae nono: « Noli me reprobare a pueris tuis, quia servus tuus sum ego et filius ancillae tuae ». *Secus se* igitur parvulum statuit, ut ostendat, parvulum oportere fieri qui vult esse amicus Dei[^p244-2].

88. (Vers. 48.). Et hoc notat, cum subdit: *Et ait illis: Quicumque susceperit puerum istum in nomine meo, me recipit;* in quo ostendit, tantum affectum se habere ad parvulos et humiles, ut quod fit eis reputet sibi fieri; unde, Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p244-3], dicet in iudicio: « Quod uni ex minimis meis fecistis mihi fecistis ». Tales enim potissime acceptat, secundum illud Psalmi: « Excelsus Dominus et humilia respicit »; et secundum illud Isaiae sexagesimo sexto: « Ad quem respiciam nisi ad pauperculum et contritum spiritu » etc. — Ex hoc autem colligitur magna excellentia esse in parvulo, dum in eo recipitur Christus non solum secundum humilitatem humanam, verum etiam secundum auctoritatem divinam; ad quam insinuandam addit: *Et quicumque me recipit*[^p244-4]*, recipit eum qui me misit.* Ex hoc colligitur, quod qui honorat et recipit parvulum Christi recipit et honorat Deum; magnus igitur est honor et gloria parvulorum. — Ex quo persuadet eis, ne ambiant esse magni, sed studeant esse parvuli. Et ad hoc invitat Apostolus ad Philippenses secundo[^p244-5]: « In humilitate superiores sibi invicem arbitrantes ». « Hoc enim sentite in vobis, quod et in Christo Iesu, qui, cum in forma Dei esset, semetipsum exinanivit, formam servi accipiens ». Sic etiam faciebat Apostolus, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios nono[^p244-6]: « Cum essem liber ex omnibus, omnium me servum feci »; et primae ad Thessalonicenses secundo: « Facti sumus parvuli in medio vestri, tanquam si nutrix foveat filios suos ».

89. Tertio vero subiungitur *perfectae laudis conclusio* per definitivum iudicium, cum ait: *Nam qui minor est inter vos omnes,* per reputationem et humilitatem, *hic maior est,* secundum divinam acceptionem et veritatem. Minimus ergo est maximus, et hoc, quia ad magna praemia ordinatus, iuxta illud infra decimo octavo[^p244-7]: « Qui se humiliat exaltabitur ». Si haec est per se vera tanquam Dei sententia, qui magis se humiliat, ille maior est, et qui maxime, ille est maximus. — Et ratio huius est, quia quanto sumus humiliores, tanto sumus a tumore superbiae inaniores; et quanto inaniores a tumore superbiae, tanto pleniores dilectione; et quanto hac pleniores, tanto maiores. De ipsa enim scribitur primae ad Corinthios decimo tertio[^p244-8]: « Maior autem horum est caritas »; et ideo Ecclesiastici tertio: « Quanto magnus es, humilia te in omnibus, et coram Deo invenies gratiam »; et rursus decimo: « Magnus est iudex et potens in honore, sed non est maior eo qui timet Deum ». — Vel ideo *minor* est maior, quia, quanto quis humilior, tanto Christo similior, et per hoc tanto ei proximior; Christus autem est maximus; sed quanto aliquis maximo propinquior, tanto maior: quanto ergo quis minor, tanto maior; et hoc est quod ipse innuit infra vigesimo secundo[^p244-9]: « Ego autem in medio vestrum sum, sicut qui ministrat ». Et ideo dicebat: « Qui maior est in vobis fiat sicut minor, et qui praecessor est, sicut ministrator »; et Marci nono: « Si quis vult primus esse, erit omnium novissimus et omnium minister », quia dicitur Matthaei vigesimo: « Sic
<!-- page 245 -->
erunt primi novissimi, et novissimi primi ». Et Paulus hoc attendens primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p245-1] dicebat: « Ego sum minimus Apostolorum, qui non sum dignus vocari Apostolus »; et ad Ephesios tertio: « Mihi omnium sanctorum minimo data est gratia haec ». — Ergo inter viros spirituales non debet esse contentio de primo loco, sed de postremo, quia ille honorabilior est, sicut dicitur infra decimo quarto: « Cum vocatus fueris ad nuptias, recumbe in novissimo loco » etc.

90. (Vers. 49.). *Respondens autem Ioannes* etc. Post doctrinam humilitatis ad domesticos docet humilitatem habendam *ad extraneos;* circa cuius insinuationem tria introducuntur. Primo enim introducitur ex parte *viri extranei operatio virtutis;* secundo vero, ex parte *discipuli prohibitio temeritatis*[^p245-2]; tertio vero, ex parte *Magistri persuasio humilitatis.*

Primo ergo introducit *operationem virtutis ex parte viri extranei,* cum dicit: *Respondens autem Ioannes dixit: Praeceptor, vidimus quendam in nomine tuo eiicientem daemonia.* Quia enim Dominus persuaserat humilitatem, volens scire Ioannes, utrum documentum ad extraneos se extendat; ideo respondet et hunc extraneum facientem mirabilia introducit. Extranei autem a Christo possunt miracula facere, secundum illud Matthaei septimo[^p245-3]: « Multi dicent mihi: Domine, in nomine tuo daemonia eiecimus et in nomine tuo virtutes multas fecimus ». Tunc dicet illis: « Discedite a me, qui operamini iniquitatem ». Hoc autem fit, quando quis habet fidem nominis Christi sine caritatis amore, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios decimo tertio[^p245-4]: « Si habuero fidem, ita ut montes transferam, caritatem autem non habuero; nihil sum ». Et hoc est, quia caritas est illa quae facit amicum Christi. In hoc autem apparet virtus nominis Christi, ut magnae sit efficaciae invocatum ab extraneis, ut, secundum illud ad Philippenses secundo[^p245-5], « in nomine Iesu omne genuflectatur caelestium, terrestrium et infernorum ». Unde infra decimo: « Domine, in nomine tuo etiam daemonia subiiciuntur nobis ».

91. Secundo vero introducit *prohibitionem temeritatis ex parte discipuli,* cum ait: *Et prohibuimus eum,* scilicet ego cum aliis; quod non fuit absque nota praesumtionis, quoniam Proverbiorum tertio[^p245-6]: « Noli prohibere, benefacere eum qui potest; si potes, et ipse benefac ». — Quia tamen frequenter quod fit typho superbiae palliatur zelo iustitiae, ideo adiungit: *Quia non sequitur te*[^p245-7] *nobiscum;* et ideo non est dignus, ut sit nobis similis in opere miraculorum, qui non est similis in impletione consiliorum. In hoc autem latebat elatio, quasi diceret illud Isaiae sexagesimo quinto[^p245-8]: « Qui dicunt: Recede a me, ne appropinques mihi, quia immundus es ». Et apparebat aemulatio illa, de qua Numerorum undecimo dicitur de viris, qui prophetaverunt in castris sine Moyse, quod « dixit Iosue, filius Nun: Domine mi Moyses, prohibe eos. At ille: Quid, inquit, aemularis pro me »? Unde Ioannes, qui erat praecipuus amator Domini et honoris eius aemulator, omnem, qui Dominum non amabat, a praedicatione nominis Christi excludere volebat. Unde Beda in Glossa[^p245-9]: « Ioannes, diligens Dominum et a Domino dilectus, excludendum putat a beneficio qui non utatur obsequio ». Verumtamen fallebatur, quia, ad Philippenses primo, « sive per occasionem, sive per veritatem annuntietur Christus, sed in hoc gaudeo et gaudebo ».

92. (Vers. 50.). Tertio quantum ad *persuasionem humilitatis ex parte Magistri* adiungit: *Et ait ad illum Iesus: Nolite prohibere,* sed potius humiliter sustinete licet imperfectum, quia, ad Romanos decimo quinto[^p245-10], « debemus nos firmiores imbecillitates infirmorum sustinere et non nobis placere »; et maxime, quando infirmus non est veritatis adversarius. — Propter quod addit: *Qui*[^p245-11] *non est adversum vos pro vobis est.* Et ideo iste utilis est nobis et tolerabilis, cum nos non impugnet, sed adiuvet; unde Ecclesiastici undecimo: « De ea re, quae te non molestat, ne certaveris ».

93. Sed huic videtur contrarium illud Matthaei duodecimo[^p245-12]: « Qui non est mecum contra me est, et qui non congregat mecum dispergit »; sed iste
<!-- page 246 -->
non erat cum Domino neque sequebatur eum: ergo erat *ei contrarius* et discipulis eius. Quomodo ergo erat *pro eis?* — Ex quo datur intelligi, quod tria sunt genera praedicatorum. Quidam sunt *pastores veri,* et hi quaerunt solum Dei honorem et subditorum salutem, secundum illud Ioannis decimo[^p246-1]: « Bonus pastor animam suam dat pro ovibus suis »; et isti sunt *excipiendi et honorandi.* Et hi sunt imitatores Christi, sicut Timotheus Apostoli, ad Philippenses secundo: « Omnes quae sua sunt quaerunt, non quae sunt Iesu Christi. Experimentum autem eius accipite, quoniam sicut patri filius mecum servivit in Evangelio ». — Quidam vero sunt *mercenarii,* qui pro commodo praedicant, tamen vera et bona, de quibus Ioannis decimo[^p246-2] dicitur: « Mercenarius autem fugit, quia mercenarius est »; et isti *tolerandi.* — Quidam sunt *lupi,* sicut haeretici, qui in doctrina veritatis a Christo discordant; de quibus dicitur Ioannis decimo[^p246-3]: « Lupus rapit et dispergit oves »; et Actuum vigesimo: « Intrabunt in vos lupi rapaces, non parcentes gregi »; et isti sunt *arcendi.* Et de *talibus* intelligitur illud Matthaei duodecimo[^p246-4]; de secundis tantum loquitur in proposito. Unde Marci nono dicitur: « Nemo est, qui faciat virtutem in nomine meo, et possit cito male loqui de me »; unde Actuum decimo nono habetur de illis filiis Scevae, quod « tentaverunt invocare nomen Domini Iesu »; et subditur, quod « spiritus nequam dixit eis: Iesum novi et Paulum scio; vos autem unde estis? Et praevaluit contra ambos, ita ut nudi et vulnerati exirent domum ».

94. *Factum est autem, dum complerentur* etc. Postquam informavit Apostolos, Ecclesiae praelatos, ad humilitatem animi, informat hic secundo *ad aequanimitatem zeli*[^p246-5]. Et habet haec pars duas, in quarum prima introducitur *occasio zeli deordinandi;* in secunda vero adiungitur *correptio zeli inordinati,* per quam informentur ad zeli aequanimitatem; et incipit secunda pars ibi[^p246-6]: *Cum vidissent autem discipuli eius.*

*Occasio* autem *zeli deordinandi* ex tribus ortum habuit, scilicet ex *Domini necessitate,* qua hospitio indigebat; ex *discipulorum auctoritate,* qua hospitium requirebant; ex *Samaritanorum inhumanitate,* qua hospitium denegabant[^p246-7].

95. (Vers. 51.). Primo igitur introducitur *necessitas Domini,* qua hospitio indigebat, quia tempus peregrinationis eius advenerat; quod notat, cum dicit: *Factum est autem, dum complerentur dies assumtionis eius,* id est, dum appropinquaret tempus passionis, per quam debebat assumi in caelum post resurrectionem; unde Psalmus de resurrectione[^p246-8] inscribitur « de assumtione matutina »; Ioannis decimo tertio: « Sciens Iesus, quia venit hora » etc. — Dum adveniret passionis tempus, conveniens erat accedere ad patiendi locum; et ideo adiungit: *Et ipse faciem suam firmavit, ut iret in Ierusalem,* ad patiendum; quia dicitur infra decimo tertio[^p246-9]: « Non capit, Prophetam perire extra Ierusalem »; ideo illuc tanquam ad locum confusionis et improperii volebat accedere. Propter quod signanter praemittit, quod *faciem firmavit,* scilicet per constantiam et divinam patientiam, secundum illud Ezechielis tertio[^p246-10]: « Ecce, dedi faciem tuam valentiorem faciebus eorum et frontem tuam duriorem frontibus eorum, et ut adamantem et ut silicem dedi faciem tuam ». Vere firma erat facies, quae non solum non fugit, sed etiam ad mortis opprobrium non concussa accessit, ita quod oportebat, eum per terram alienigenarum transire, iuxta illud quod dicitur infra decimo septimo[^p246-11]: « Dum iret Iesus in Ierusalem, transibat per mediam Samariam et Galilaeam ». Et ita oportebat, eum mendicare aliena hospitia, iuxta illud Ieremiae decimo quarto: « Quare quasi colonus futurus es in terra et sicut viator declinans ad manendum »?

96. (Vers. 52.). Secundo adiungitur *auctoritas discipulorum,* qua hospitium requirebant, cum ait: *Et misit nuntios ante conspectum suum.* Decet enim magnum Dominum habere nuntios praecursores, iuxta quod dicitur Malachiae tertio[^p246-12]: « Ecce, ego mitto Angelum meum », id est nuntium, « qui praeparabit viam ante faciem tuam ». Sic etiam legimus de Iacob Genesis trigesimo secundo, quod « misit nuntios ante se ». — Et quia boni nuntii
<!-- page 247 -->
fideliter et velociter exsequuntur imperium domini, ideo additur: *Et euntes intraverunt in civitatem Samaritanorum, ut praepararent illi*[^p247-1]. Samaritani isti fuerunt coloni positi in civitatibus Samariae pro filiis Israel in Assyrios translatis, sicut dicitur quarti Regum decimo septimo, quod « rex Assyriorum adduxit de civibus Assyriorum viros et collocavit eos in civitatibus Samariae pro filiis Israel; qui possederunt Samariam et habitaverunt in urbibus eius »; et de istis subditur postmodum ibidem, quod « fuerunt timentes Dominum, nihilominus idolis suis servientes ». In horum civitatem intraverunt, non sua voluntate, cum dicatur Ioannis quarto[^p247-2], quod « Iudaei non coutuntur Samaritanis », sed vel necessitate compulsi, quae legem non habet, sive de mandato Domini speciali. Nam in missione Apostolorum dicitur Matthaei decimo[^p247-3]: « In viam gentium ne abieritis et in civitates Samaritanorum ne intraveritis »; sed illud dicitur ad praedicandum, non autem ad hospitium requirendum. Nam Ioannis quarto de hac civitate dicitur, quod « discipuli abierant in civitatem, ut cibos emerent »; et hoc mandato Domini faciebant, qui ostendebat, nullum hominem contemnendum, sed quemlibet amandum ut proximum, sicut infra decimo dicitur de Samaritano in parabola, quod « proximus fuit illi qui incidit in latrones ».

97. (Vers. 53.). Tertio adiungitur *inhumanitas Samaritanorum,* qua hospitium denegabant, cum dicitur: *Et non receperunt eum,* cum tamen ipse esset Dominus eorum; ut impleretur illud Ioannis primo[^p247-4]: « In propria venit, et sui eum non receperunt ». Similis fuit inhumanitas istorum inhumanitati illorum de Gabaa ad levitam; dicitur Iudicum decimo nono, quod « sedebant in platea civitatis, nullusque voluit eos recipere hospitio ». Isti Samaritani non admittebantur a Iudaeis ad cultum Dei, et ideo euntibus in Ierusalem erant contrarii. — Et propterea subditur: *Quia facies eius erat euntis in Ierusalem,* id est, quia manifeste hoc cognoscebant, quod iret in Ierusalem ad colendum Deum iuxta ritum Iudaicum. Unde mulier Samaritana dixit ei Ioannis quarto[^p247-5]: « Domine, patres nostri in monte hoc adoraverunt, vos autem dicitis, quod Ierosolymis est locus, ubi adorare oportet ». Et inter Iudaeos et Samaritanos de loco orationis erat contrarietas; sicut etiam *spiritualiter* nunc contingit, quod viri spirituales et colentes Deum deridentur et despiciuntur et abiiciuntur ab his qui amant mundum. Unde Proverbiorum decimo quarto[^p247-6]: « Ambulans recto itinere et timens Dominum despicitur ab eo qui infami graditur via »; et Ecclesiastici decimo tertio: « Sicut communicabit lupus cum agno, sic peccator iusto ». Unde Sapientiae secundo: « Dixerunt impii: Circumveniamus iustum, quoniam contrarius est operibus nostris »; et paulo post: « Gravis est nobis etiam ad videndum, quoniam dissimilis est aliis vita illius, et immutatae sunt viae eius ». Qui enim habent dissimilitudinem vitae non de facili habent societatem viae. Et propterea Samaritani, odientes Ierusalem, eunti in Ierusalem nolebant praestare humanitatem.

98. (Vers. 54.). *Cum vidissent autem discipuli* etc. Posita occasione zeli deordinandi, subiungit *correptionem zeli deordinati.* Circa quam tria introducuntur ab Evangelista, scilicet *indignatio reprehensibilis, increpatio rationabilis, informatio rememorabilis*[^p247-7]. Primum fuit in discipulorum animo, secundum fuit in Domini verbo, tertium fuit in dominico facto.

Primo igitur quantum ad *indignationem reprehensibilem* in discipulorum animo dicitur: *Cum vidissent autem discipuli eius, Iacobus et Ioannes; vidissent,* inquam, istius civitatis crudelitatem, ita ut possent dicere illud Psalmi[^p247-8]: « Vidi iniquitatem et contradictionem in civitate »; et iterum: « Zelavi super iniquos, pacem peccatorum videns ». Unde indignatione commoti sunt, quia Dominum, cuius maiestatem in monte viderant cum Moyse et Elia, sic videbant despici in terra. — Propter quod additur: *Dixerunt: Domine, vis dicimus, ut ignis descendat de caelo et consumat eos*[^p247-9]*?* In quo ostenditur indignationis furor, quo in illos commoti erant, ut vellent perdere de terra. Ideo autem isti duo praecipue hoc dixerunt, quia magni erant honoris Domini zelatores; unde poterat de eis dici illud ad Romanos decimo: « Testimonium illis perhibeo, quia aemulationem quidem Dei habent, sed non secundum scientiam »; sicut duo filii Iacob, zelo iniuriae sibi factae commoti, destruxerunt urbem Sichimorum, sicut dicitur Genesis trigesimo quarto. — Vel ideo hoc dicebant, quia ipsi fuerant cum Domino in
<!-- page 248 -->
monte, ubi cognoverant eius maiestatem et viderant Eliae societatem; et ideo similem illi ultionem volebant facere, iuxta quod dicitur quarti Regum primo[^p248-1], quod « dixit Elias: Si homo Dei sum, descendat ignis de caelo et devoret te et quinquaginta tuos ». Et factum est sic.

99. (Vers. 55.). Secundo vero quantum ad *reprehensionem rationabilem* in Domini verbo subditur: *Et conversus increpavit illos dicens: Nescitis, cuius spiritus estis.* Hoc dicit, quia ipsi credebant, se moveri a spiritu rectitudinis; et ipsi movebantur zelo amaritudinis, quod non debebant, sicut dicit Iacobus tertio[^p248-2]: « Quodsi zelum amarum habetis, et contentiones sunt in cordibus vestris; nolite gloriari et mendaces esse adversus veritatem ». Et propterea dicitur primae Ioannis quarto[^p248-3]: « Nolite omni spiritui credere, sed probate spiritus, si ex Deo sint ». Spiritus enim Christi est spiritus mansuetudinis, secundum illud Isaiae sexagesimo primo: « Spiritus Domini super me, eo quod unxerit me, ad annuntiandum mansuetis misit me »; et quadragesimo secundo: « Ecce, servus meus, suscipiam eum. Dedi spiritum meum super eum. Calamum quassatum non confringet et linum fumigans non exstinguet ». Et hunc spiritum debebant isti habere tanquam boni discipuli et imitatores Magistri.

100. (Vers. 56.). Et propterea addit: *Filius enim*[^p248-4] *hominis non venit animas hominum perdere, sed salvare;* quoniam hic adventus non erat iustitiae, sed misericordiae, secundum illud Ioannis tertio[^p248-5]: « Non misit Deus Filium suum in mundum, ut iudicet mundum, sed ut salvetur mundus per ipsum »; unde ab isto fine nomen accepit, ut diceretur *Iesus,* secundum illud Matthaei primo: « Vocabis nomen eius Iesum; ipse enim salvum faciet populum suum a peccatis eorum »; et Matthaei vigesimo: « Filius hominis non venit ministrari, sed ministrare et dare animam suam redemptionem pro multis ». Et ideo qui spiritum Christi habet non debet expetere vindictam, sed exhibere patientiam, secundum illud Ambrosii in Glossa[^p248-6]: « Perfecta, inquit, virtus non habet studium ultionis; nec ulla est iracundia, ubi caritatis est plenitudo ». Ideo dicitur ad Romanos duodecimo: « Non vosmetipsos defendentes, carissimi, sed date locum irae ».

101. Tertio quantum ad *informationem rememorabilem* in Domini facto subditur: *Et abierunt in aliud castellum;* in quo dedit formam discipulis, ut ante fugiant homines, quam cum eis contendant; iuxta quod dedit formam discipulis suis Matthaei decimo[^p248-7]: « Cum autem persequentur vos in una civitate, fugite in aliam ». Exemplum autem huius habemus de Abraham et Lot, Genesis decimo tertio, ubi « dixit Abraham: Ne, quaeso, sit iurgium inter me et te; fratres enim sumus. Si ad sinistram ieris, ego dexteram tenebo ». In quo docuit perfectam mansuetudinem, quae decet Christi servos, secundum illud secundae ad Timotheum secundo[^p248-8]: « Servum autem Domini non oportet litigare, sed mansuetum esse »; unde secundum illud Iacobi primo: « Sit omnis homo tardus ad iram. Ira enim viri iustitiam Dei non operatur ». Si enim noluit Dominus maiestatis irasci tantae inhumanitati, quanto minus nos debemus alicui indignari? Et haec potissime mansuetudo reperiri debet in praepositis Ecclesiae; unde Apostolus secundae ad Timotheum quarto[^p248-9]: « Argue, obsecra, increpa in omni patientia et doctrina »; quoniam in Psalmo dicitur: « Supervenit mansuetudo, et corripiemur ». Et Seneca[^p248-10]: « Generosus est animus hominis, et facilius ducitur, quam trahatur »; unde ipse dicit, quod rex apum non habet aculeum.

#### The norm of presiding is explained in three ways.

85. *But there entered a thought* etc. This is the third part of this part[^p243-5], in which the *mission of the Apostles* is treated, where, after the explanation of the form of preaching and of the way of making progress, *the norm of presiding* is explained. For the Apostles bore the person of *preachers, of perfect men* and *of prelates.* But since three things are necessary to *prelates* for ruling the people, namely *humility of mind, equanimity of zeal* and *perspicacity of judgement;* therefore this part has three; in the first of which he forms them to *humility of mind;* in the second, to *equanimity of zeal,* there[^p243-6]: *And it came to pass, when … were accomplishing* etc.; in the third, to *perspicacity of judgement,* there: *And it came to pass, as they walked* etc.

The first part has two. For first he forms them *to humility* in respect of *those of the household;* but secondly, in respect of *strangers,* there[^p243-7]: *And John, answering, said* etc.

Now in persuading them to *humility to be had toward those of the household*[^p243-8] three things are brought in by the Evangelist. The first is *the occasion for praising humility,* on account of its contrary; the second is *a praising leading-by-the-hand* through a sensible example; the third is *the conclusion of perfect praise* through a definitive judgement.

86. (Vers. 46.). First, then, there is brought in *the occasion for praising humility,* and this for the repelling of its contrary, when he says: *And there entered a thought into them, which of them should be greater.* — And note that Luke says that *a thought entered* into them; in Mark, the ninth chapter[^p243-9], they are said *to have disputed;* in Matthew, the eighteenth, they are said *to have questioned* the Lord. Nor is it a contradiction, for all this could have been, so that first *the thought* entered, then *the disputation* followed, and last *the question* was carried to the Master. And what one of the Evangelists is silent on, another says. — Now this *thought* was a way to pride, and had entered into them from the ancient corruption, of which Genesis, the eighth chapter[^p243-10]: « The imagination and thought of man's heart are prone to evil from his youth », and most of all to pride; for, as is said in Tobias, the fourth chapter, « never suffer pride to reign in thy mind, or in thy word; for in it all perdition took its beginning ». Therefore to the man thinking proudly it is said in Job, the fifteenth chapter[^p243-11]: « Why doth thy heart elevate thee, and as one thinking great things hast thou astonished eyes »? Such were the Apostles then, against the counsel of Ecclesiasticus, the sixth chapter: « Extol not thyself in thy thought like a bull, lest thy strength be quashed ». Now this proud thought had its entrance from this, that he had taken three disciples with him into the mountain; and yet to Peter alone he had said: « To thee will I give the keys of the kingdom of heaven », as is said in Matthew, the sixteenth chapter[^p243-12]; therefore some wished to make John equal to Peter, others wished to prefer Peter; and this is what is said in the Gloss: « Because they had seen Peter » etc.

87. (Vers. 47.). But secondly there is joined *the praising leading-by-the-hand* through a sensible example, when it is added: *But Jesus, seeing the thoughts of their heart*[^p243-13], as true God, according to that of the first of Kings, the sixteenth chapter: « Man seeth those things that appear, but God
<!-- page 244 -->
beholdeth the heart »; and Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third chapter: « The eyes of the Lord are far brighter than the sun, beholding round about all the ways of men, and the bottom of the deep, and looking into the hearts of men ». — And since he saw that the thought was a way to disease, therefore as a wise physician he applies a remedy, when he says: *Taking a child, he set him by him*[^p244-1], as one conformed and near to himself; whence in Matthew, the nineteenth chapter, he said: « Suffer the little children to come to me, and forbid them not to come to me; for of such is the kingdom of heaven »; and Osee, the eleventh chapter: « Because Israel was a child, and I loved him ». And on account of this the Wise man said in Wisdom, the ninth chapter: « Reject me not from among thy children; for I am thy servant, and the son of thy handmaid ». He set the little one *by him,* then, to show that he who wishes to be a friend of God must become a little one[^p244-2].

88. (Vers. 48.). And he notes this when he subjoins: *And he said to them: Whosoever shall receive this child in my name, receiveth me;* in which he shows that he has such affection toward the little and the humble, that what is done to them he reckons done to himself; whence, in Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p244-3], he will say in the judgement: « What you did to one of my least, you did to me ». For such he most of all accepts, according to that of the Psalm: « The Lord is high, and looketh on the low »; and according to that of Isaias, the sixty-sixth chapter: « To whom shall I have respect, but to him that is poor and little, and of a contrite spirit » etc. — Now from this is gathered that there is a great excellence in the little one, since in him Christ is received not only according to the human humility, but also according to the divine authority; to intimate which he adds: *And whosoever receiveth me*[^p244-4]*, receiveth him that sent me.* From this is gathered that he who honours and receives a little one of Christ receives and honours God; great, therefore, is the honour and glory of little ones. — From which he persuades them not to strive after being great, but to be eager to be little. And to this the Apostle invites, to the Philippians, the second chapter[^p244-5]: « In humility esteeming one another better than themselves ». « For let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus, who, being in the form of God, emptied himself, taking the form of a servant ». So also the Apostle did, according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter[^p244-6]: « Whereas I was free as to all, I made myself the servant of all »; and the first to the Thessalonians, the second chapter: « We became little ones in the midst of you, as if a nurse should cherish her children ».

89. But thirdly there is subjoined *the conclusion of perfect praise* through a definitive judgement, when he says: *For he that is the lesser among you all,* by reckoning and humility, *he is the greater,* according to the divine acceptance and truth. The least, then, is the greatest, and this because he is ordained to great rewards, according to that below, in the eighteenth chapter[^p244-7]: « He that humbleth himself shall be exalted ». If this is true of itself, as the sentence of God, he who humbles himself more is the greater, and he who most, he is the greatest. — And the reason of this is that the humbler we are, the emptier we are of the swelling of pride; and the emptier of the swelling of pride, the fuller of dilection; and the fuller of this, the greater. For of it is written in the first to the Corinthians, the thirteenth chapter[^p244-8]: « But the greater of these is charity »; and therefore Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter: « The greater thou art, the more humble thyself in all things, and thou shalt find grace before God »; and again, the tenth: « Great is the judge, and mighty in honour, but he is not greater than he that feareth God ». — Or *the lesser* is the greater for this reason, that the humbler one is, the liker to Christ, and by this the nearer to him; but Christ is the greatest; and the nearer one is to the greatest, the greater: the lesser one is, therefore, the greater; and this is what he himself intimates below, in the twenty-second chapter[^p244-9]: « But I am in the midst of you, as he that ministereth ». And therefore he said: « He that is the greater among you, let him become as the lesser, and he that goeth before, as he that ministereth »; and Mark, the ninth chapter: « If any man desire to be first, he shall be the last of all, and the minister of all », because it is said in Matthew, the twentieth chapter: « So shall
<!-- page 245 -->
the first be last, and the last first ». And Paul, attending to this, said in the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter[^p245-1]: « I am the least of the Apostles, who am not worthy to be called an Apostle »; and to the Ephesians, the third chapter: « To me, the least of all the saints, is given this grace ». — Among spiritual men, therefore, there ought not to be contention over the first place, but over the last, for that one is more honourable, as is said below, in the fourteenth chapter: « When thou art invited to a wedding, sit down in the lowest place » etc.

90. (Vers. 49.). *And John, answering* etc. After the teaching of humility toward those of the household he teaches the humility to be had *toward strangers;* about the intimating of which three things are brought in. For first there is brought in, on the part *of a strange man, the working of power;* but secondly, on the part *of a disciple, the forbidding of temerity*[^p245-2]; but thirdly, on the part *of the Master, the persuasion to humility.*

First, then, he brings in *the working of power on the part of a strange man,* when he says: *And John, answering, said: Master*[^tr-praeceptor]*, we saw a certain man casting out devils in thy name.* For since the Lord had persuaded them to humility, John, wishing to know whether the instruction extends to strangers, therefore answers and brings in this stranger doing marvels. Now strangers to Christ can work miracles, according to that of Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p245-3]: « Many will say to me: Lord, in thy name we have cast out devils, and in thy name done many miracles ». Then he will say to them: « Depart from me, you that work iniquity ». And this comes about when someone has faith in the name of Christ without the love of charity, according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the thirteenth chapter[^p245-4]: « If I should have faith, so that I could remove mountains, and have not charity, I am nothing ». And this is because charity is that which makes a friend of Christ. But in this appears the power of the name of Christ, that it is of great efficacy when invoked by strangers, so that, according to that to the Philippians, the second chapter[^p245-5], « in the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of those in heaven, on earth and under the earth ». Whence below, in the tenth chapter: « Lord, in thy name the devils also are subject to us ».

91. But secondly he brings in *the forbidding of temerity on the part of a disciple,* when he says: *And we forbade him,* that is, I with the others; which was not without the mark of presumption, since in Proverbs, the third chapter[^p245-6]: « Forbid not him to do good who is able; if thou art able, do good thyself also ». — Yet because what is done by the puffing-up of pride is often cloaked by the zeal of justice, therefore he adds: *Because he followeth not thee*[^p245-7] *with us;* and therefore he is not worthy to be like us in the work of miracles, who is not like us in the fulfilling of the counsels. But in this lay hidden elation, as if he said that of Isaias, the sixty-fifth chapter[^p245-8]: « Who say: Depart from me, come not near me, because thou art unclean ». And there appeared that emulation of which it is said in Numbers, the eleventh chapter, about the men who prophesied in the camp without Moses, that « Josue the son of Nun said: My lord Moses, forbid them. But he: Why, said he, hast thou emulation for me »? Whence John, who was a special lover of the Lord and an emulator of his honour, wished to exclude from the preaching of the name of Christ everyone who did not love the Lord. Whence Bede in the Gloss[^p245-9]: « John, loving the Lord and loved by the Lord, thinks that he who does not use the service is to be excluded from the benefit ». Nevertheless he was mistaken, for, to the Philippians, the first chapter, « whether by occasion or by truth Christ be announced, in this also I rejoice, yea, and will rejoice ».

92. (Vers. 50.). Thirdly, as to *the persuasion to humility on the part of the Master,* he adds: *And Jesus said to him: Forbid him not,* but rather humbly bear with him, though imperfect, for, to the Romans, the fifteenth chapter[^p245-10], « we that are stronger ought to bear the infirmities of the weak, and not to please ourselves »; and most of all when the weak man is not an adversary of the truth. — On account of which he adds: *He*[^p245-11] *that is not against you, is for you.* And therefore such a one is useful and tolerable to us, since he does not attack us, but helps; whence Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter: « Strive not in a matter which doth not concern thee ».

93. But to this that of Matthew, the twelfth chapter[^p245-12], seems contrary: « He that is not with me is against me, and he that gathereth not with me scattereth »; but this man
<!-- page 246 -->
was not with the Lord nor following him: therefore he was *contrary to him* and to his disciples. How, then, was he *for them?* — From which it is given to be understood that there are three kinds of preachers. Some are *true shepherds,* and these seek only the honour of God and the salvation of their subjects, according to that of John, the tenth chapter[^p246-1]: « The good shepherd giveth his life for his sheep »; and these are *to be received and honoured.* And these are imitators of Christ, as Timothy was of the Apostle, to the Philippians, the second chapter: « For all seek the things that are their own, not the things that are Jesus Christ's. Now know ye the proof of him, that as a son with the father, so hath he served with me in the gospel ». — But some are *hirelings,* who preach for gain, yet true and good things, of whom it is said in John, the tenth chapter[^p246-2]: « But the hireling flieth, because he is a hireling »; and these are *to be tolerated.* — Some are *wolves,* as heretics, who are at discord with Christ in the doctrine of the truth; of whom it is said in John, the tenth chapter[^p246-3]: « The wolf catcheth and scattereth the sheep »; and Acts, the twentieth chapter: « Ravening wolves will enter in among you, not sparing the flock »; and these are *to be kept off.* And of *such* is that of Matthew, the twelfth chapter[^p246-4], understood; he speaks in the matter at hand only of the second. Whence it is said in Mark, the ninth chapter: « There is no man that doth a miracle in my name, and can soon speak ill of me »; whence in Acts, the nineteenth chapter, it is had of those sons of Sceva that « they attempted to invoke the name of the Lord Jesus »; and it is subjoined that « the wicked spirit said to them: Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but whence are you? And he prevailed against both, so that they went out of the house naked and wounded ».

94. *And it came to pass, when … were accomplishing* etc. After he has formed the Apostles, the prelates of the Church, to humility of mind, he forms them here secondly *to equanimity of zeal*[^p246-5]. And this part has two, in the first of which there is brought in *the occasion of zeal's becoming disordered;* but in the second is joined *the correction of the inordinate zeal,* through which they are formed to equanimity of zeal; and the second part begins there[^p246-6]: *And when his disciples had seen.*

Now *the occasion of zeal's becoming disordered* had its rise from three things, namely from *the Lord's need,* by which he needed lodging; from *the disciples' authority,* by which they sought lodging; from *the Samaritans' inhumanity,* by which they denied lodging[^p246-7].

95. (Vers. 51.). First, then, there is brought in *the Lord's need,* by which he needed lodging, because the time of his pilgrimage had come; which he notes when he says: *And it came to pass, when the days of his assumption were accomplishing,* that is, when the time of the passion was drawing near, through which he was to be assumed into heaven after the resurrection; whence the Psalm on the resurrection[^p246-8] is entitled « of the morning assumption »; John, the thirteenth chapter: « Jesus knowing that his hour was come » etc. — When the time of the passion was coming, it was fitting to go to the place of suffering; and therefore he adds: *He made his face firm, to go to Jerusalem,* to suffer; for it is said below, in the thirteenth chapter[^p246-9]: « It cannot be that a Prophet perish out of Jerusalem »; therefore he willed to go there as to the place of confusion and reproach. On account of which he pointedly puts first that *he made his face firm,* namely through constancy and divine patience, according to that of Ezechiel, the third chapter[^p246-10]: « Behold, I have made thy face stronger than their faces, and thy forehead harder than their foreheads; and like an adamant and like flint have I made thy face ». Firm indeed was the face which not only did not flee, but even went unshaken to the opprobrium of death, so that he had to pass through the land of foreigners, according to that which is said below, in the seventeenth chapter[^p246-11]: « While Jesus went to Jerusalem, he passed through the midst of Samaria and Galilee ». And so he had to beg the lodgings of others, according to that of Jeremias, the fourteenth chapter: « Why wilt thou be as a stranger in the land, and as a wayfaring man turning in to lodge »?

96. (Vers. 52.). Secondly there is joined *the disciples' authority,* by which they sought lodging, when he says: *And he sent messengers before his face.* For it befits a great Lord to have messengers going before, according to what is said in Malachias, the third chapter[^p246-12]: « Behold, I send my Angel », that is, a messenger, « who shall prepare the way before thy face ». So also we read of Jacob in Genesis, the thirty-second chapter, that « he sent messengers before him ». — And since good messengers
<!-- page 247 -->
carry out their lord's command faithfully and swiftly, therefore it is added: *And going, they entered into a city of the Samaritans, to prepare for him*[^p247-1]. These Samaritans were settlers placed in the cities of Samaria in place of the children of Israel carried over into Assyria, as is said in the fourth of Kings, the seventeenth chapter, that « the king of the Assyrians brought men of the citizens of the Assyrians, and placed them in the cities of Samaria instead of the children of Israel; and they possessed Samaria, and dwelt in the cities thereof »; and of these it is subjoined afterwards in the same place that « they were fearing the Lord, nevertheless serving their idols ». Into their city they entered, not of their own will, since it is said in John, the fourth chapter[^p247-2], that « the Jews do not communicate with the Samaritans », but either compelled by necessity, which has no law, or by a special command of the Lord. For in the mission of the Apostles it is said in Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p247-3]: « Go ye not into the way of the Gentiles, and into the cities of the Samaritans enter ye not »; but that is said of preaching, not of seeking lodging. For in John, the fourth chapter, it is said of this city that « the disciples were gone into the city to buy meats »; and this they did by the Lord's command, who showed that no man is to be despised, but anyone at all to be loved as a neighbour, as is said below, in the tenth chapter, of the Samaritan in the parable, that he « was neighbour to him that fell among the robbers ».

97. (Vers. 53.). Thirdly is joined *the Samaritans' inhumanity,* by which they denied lodging, when it is said: *And they received him not,* though he was their Lord; that there might be fulfilled that of John, the first chapter[^p247-4]: « He came unto his own, and his own received him not ». Like was the inhumanity of these to the inhumanity of those of Gabaa toward the levite; it is said in Judges, the nineteenth chapter, that « they sat in the street of the city, and no man would receive them to lodge ». These Samaritans were not admitted by the Jews to the worship of God, and therefore they were contrary to those going to Jerusalem. — And on account of this it is subjoined: *Because his face was of one going to Jerusalem,* that is, because they knew this plainly, that he was going to Jerusalem to worship God according to the Jewish rite. Whence the Samaritan woman said to him in John, the fourth chapter[^p247-5]: « Lord, our fathers adored on this mountain, and you say that at Jerusalem is the place where men must adore ». And between the Jews and the Samaritans there was contrariety over the place of prayer; as now also it happens *spiritually* that spiritual men and those who worship God are derided and despised and cast off by those who love the world. Whence Proverbs, the fourteenth chapter[^p247-6]: « He that walketh in the right way and feareth the Lord is despised by him that goeth by an infamous way »; and Ecclesiasticus, the thirteenth chapter: « As the wolf shall have fellowship with the lamb, so the sinner with the just ». Whence Wisdom, the second chapter: « The wicked said: Let us lie in wait for the just, because he is contrary to our doings »; and a little after: « He is grievous unto us, even to behold, because his life is unlike other men's, and his ways are changed ». For those who have unlikeness of life do not easily have fellowship of way. And on account of this the Samaritans, hating Jerusalem, would not show humanity to one going to Jerusalem.

98. (Vers. 54.). *And when his disciples had seen* etc. Having set down the occasion of zeal's becoming disordered, he subjoins *the correction of the disordered zeal.* About which three things are brought in by the Evangelist, namely *reprehensible indignation, reasonable rebuke, formation to be remembered*[^p247-7]. The first was in the disciples' mind, the second was in the Lord's word, the third was in the Lord's deed.

First, then, as to *the reprehensible indignation* in the disciples' mind, it is said: *And when his disciples James and John had seen; had seen,* I say, the cruelty of this city, so that they could say that of the Psalm[^p247-8]: « I have seen iniquity and contradiction in the city »; and again: « I was zealous over the wicked, seeing the peace of sinners ». Whence they were moved with indignation, because they saw the Lord, whose majesty they had seen on the mountain with Moses and Elias, thus despised on earth. — On account of which it is added: *They said: Lord, wilt thou that we command fire to come down from heaven, and consume them*[^p247-9]*?* In which is shown the fury of indignation with which they were moved against those men, so that they would destroy them from the earth. And these two said this chiefly for this reason, that they were great zealots of the Lord's honour; whence that of the Romans, the tenth chapter, could be said of them: « I bear them witness, that they have a zeal of God, but not according to knowledge »; as the two sons of Jacob, moved by zeal for the injury done them, destroyed the city of the Sichemites, as is said in Genesis, the thirty-fourth chapter. — Or they said this for this reason, that they had been with the Lord on
<!-- page 248 -->
the mountain, where they had known his majesty and had seen the fellowship of Elias; and therefore they wished to take a vengeance like his, according to what is said in the fourth of Kings, the first chapter[^p248-1], that « Elias said: If I be a man of God, let fire come down from heaven and devour thee and thy fifty ». And it was done so.

99. (Vers. 55.). But secondly, as to *the reasonable reprehension* in the Lord's word, it is subjoined: *And turning, he rebuked them, saying: You know not of what spirit you are.* He says this because they believed that they were moved by the spirit of rectitude; and they were moved by the zeal of bitterness, which they ought not, as James says, the third chapter[^p248-2]: « But if you have bitter zeal, and there be contentions in your hearts, glory not, and be not liars against the truth ». And on account of this it is said in the first of John, the fourth chapter[^p248-3]: « Believe not every spirit, but try the spirits if they be of God ». For the spirit of Christ is the spirit of meekness, according to that of Isaias, the sixty-first chapter: « The spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me; he hath sent me to announce to the meek »; and the forty-second: « Behold my servant, I will uphold him. I have given my spirit upon him. The bruised reed he shall not break, and smoking flax he shall not quench ». And this spirit these men ought to have had, as good disciples and imitators of the Master.

100. (Vers. 56.). And on account of this he adds: *For*[^p248-4] *the Son of man came not to destroy the souls of men, but to save;* since this coming was not of justice, but of mercy, according to that of John, the third chapter[^p248-5]: « God sent not his Son into the world to judge the world, but that the world may be saved by him »; whence from this end he received the name, so that he was called *Jesus,* according to that of Matthew, the first chapter: « Thou shalt call his name Jesus; for he shall save his people from their sins »; and Matthew, the twentieth chapter: « The Son of man is not come to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a redemption for many ». And therefore he who has the spirit of Christ ought not to seek vengeance, but to show patience, according to that of Ambrose in the Gloss[^p248-6]: « Perfect virtue, he says, has no eagerness for revenge; nor is there any wrath where there is fullness of charity ». Therefore it is said to the Romans, the twelfth chapter: « Not defending yourselves, my dearly beloved, but give place unto wrath ».

101. Thirdly, as to *the formation to be remembered* in the Lord's deed, it is subjoined: *And they went into another town;* in which he gave the disciples a form, that they should flee men rather than contend with them; according as he gave a form to his disciples in Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p248-7]: « And when they shall persecute you in one city, flee into another ». Now an example of this we have in Abraham and Lot, Genesis, the thirteenth chapter, where « Abraham said: Let there be no quarrel, I beseech thee, between me and thee; for we are brethren. If thou wilt go to the left hand, I will take the right ». In which he taught the perfect meekness that befits the servants of Christ, according to that of the second to Timothy, the second chapter[^p248-8]: « But the servant of the Lord must not wrangle, but be meek »; whence according to that of James, the first chapter: « Let every man be slow to anger. For the anger of man worketh not the justice of God ». For if the Lord of majesty would not be angry at so great an inhumanity, how much less ought we to be indignant at anyone? And this meekness ought most of all to be found in those set over the Church; whence the Apostle in the second to Timothy, the fourth chapter[^p248-9]: « Reprove, entreat, rebuke in all patience and doctrine »; since in the Psalm it is said: « Meekness is come upon us, and we shall be corrected ». And Seneca[^p248-10]: « The mind of man is generous, and is more easily led than dragged »; whence he himself says that the king of the bees has no sting.
