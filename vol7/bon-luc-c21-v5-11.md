# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XXI, Vers. 5-11
## *Informatio ad providentiam respectu futurorum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 523 -->

#### Informatio ad providentiam respectu futurorum.

9. *Et quibusdam dicentibus de templo* etc. Postquam informavit discipulos ad cautelam malorum et ad eligentiam meliorum, hic tertio[^p523-5] informat eos ad *providentiam respectu futurorum.* Dividitur autem pars ista in quatuor partes. In prima praedicit quae ventura sunt *generaliter.* In secunda, quae ventura sunt *circa persecutionem Ecclesiae;* ibi[^p523-6]: *Videte, ne seducamini* etc. In tertia, quae ventura sunt *circa destructionem Ierusalem,* ibi: *Cum autem videritis circumdari ab exercitu.* In quarta, quae ventura sunt *circa iudicium finale,* ibi: *Et erunt signa in sole et luna.*

In explicando autem *praedictionem futurorum in quadam generalitate* tria introducuntur, scilicet *praedicendi occasio,* ex occasione *praedictio* et ex praedictione *subsequens quaestio.*

10. (Vers. 5.). Primo igitur quoad *occasionem praedicendi futura* dicit: *Et quibusdam dicentibus de templo, quod bonis lapidibus et donis ornatum esset, dixit.* Ad huiusmodi laudatores templi quantum ad permanentiam et opulentiam praedicendo futura respondit, ne confiderent in his transitoriis, quia, sicut dicitur ad Hebraeos decimo tertio[^p523-7], « non habemus hic manentem civitatem, sed futuram inquirimus »; et ideo secundae ad Corinthios quarto: « Non contemplantibus nobis quae videntur, sed quae non videntur. Quae enim videntur temporalia sunt; quae autem non videntur aeterna ». Discipuli autem, nondum existentes spirituales, magnificabant templum quantum ad *soliditatem aedificii;* unde Marci decimo tertio[^p523-8]: « Cum egrederetur de templo, ait illi unus ex discipulis suis: Magister, aspice, quales lapides et quales structurae »! Magnificabant ipsum quantum ad *sublimitatem cultus Domini,* quia ornatum erat donis, quasi Deus semper in illo deberet coli. Et in hoc tripliciter fallebantur: et quia *parva magnificabant,* tanquam si essent magna, cum tamen dicatur Isaiae ultimo[^p523-9]: « Caelum mihi sedes est, terra autem scabellum pedum meorum. Quae est ista domus, quam aedificabitis mihi? Et quis est iste locus requietionis meae? Omnia haec manus mea fecit, et facta sunt universa ista, dicit Dominus ». Et ideo sapientissimus Salomon tertii Regum octavo: « Si caelum et caeli caelorum te capere non possunt » etc. — Secundo, quia *corporalia et figuralia magnificabant* ut vera et spiritualia. Quocontra dicitur Actuum decimo septimo[^p523-10]: « Deus, qui fecit mundum et omnia, quae in eo sunt, hic, caeli et terrae cum sit Dominus, non in manufactis templis habitat neque manibus humanis colitur, indigens aliquo », cum sit Deus. — Tertio fallebantur, quia *momentanea et caduca magnificabant,* quasi essent perpetua et aeterna, cum dicatur tamen primae ad Corinthios septimo[^p523-11]: « Praeterit enim figura huius mundi ».

11. (Vers. 6.). Secundo quantum ad *praedictionem introductam* ex occasione accepta subditur: *Haec quae videtis, venient dies, in quibus non relinquetur lapis super lapidem, qui non destruatur.* Hoc quidem generaliter dictum est et verum est pro *tempore finalis iudicii,* quando omnia destruentur, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo quarto[^p523-12]: « Caelum et terra transibunt » etc.; et Apocalypsis vigesimo primo: « Primum caelum et prima terra abiit, et
<!-- page 524 -->
mare iam non est », ut in Psalmo: « Ipsi peribunt, tu autem permanebis » etc. Est etiam verum pro *destructione Ierusalem* specialiter, quae facta est a Tito et Vespasiano quadragesimo secundo anno post passionem Domini; supra decimo nono[^p524-1]: « Venient dies in te, et circumdabunt te inimici tui vallo et ad terram prosternent te et non relinquent in te lapidem super lapidem ». Unde Gregorius[^p524-2] dicit, quod illa Ierusalem cum templo funditus est eversa, et nova postmodum est reaedificata. Ratio autem, quare Dominus distulit per quadraginta duos annos, fuit, ut exspectaret illos ad poenitentiam, sicut dicitur secundae Petri tertio[^p524-3]: « Patienter agit propter vos, nolens aliquem perire ». Ratio autem, quare amplius non distulit, fuit propter Iudaeorum perfidiam obduratam, quae veritatem contemnebat propter velamen et umbram. Unde Glossa[^p524-4]: « Curavit Deus civitatem et templum et omnia figuralia subvertere, ne quis post adventum Christi ad illa recurreret ». Et pro illo tempore intelligitur illud Isaiae primo[^p524-5]: « Quo mihi multitudinem victimarum vestrarum? dicit Dominus. Plenus sum ». « Ne offeratis ultra sacrificium frustra; incensum abominatio est mihi. Neomeniam et Sabbatum et festivitates alias non feram ». Et ideo ad Galatas quinto[^p524-6]: « State et nolite iterum iugo servitutis contineri. Ecce, ego Paulus dico vobis » etc.

12. (Vers. 7.). Tertio quantum ad *interrogationem subortam* ex praedictione audita subiungit: *Interrogaverunt autem illum dicentes: Praeceptor, quando haec erunt, et quod signum, cum fieri incipient?* Interrogaverunt autem non omnes, sed quatuor principales, scilicet Petrus, Iacobus, Ioannes et Andreas, quia erant ei familiares. Unde Glossa[^p524-7]: « Quia laudantibus aedificationes templi praedixerat omnia destruenda, discipuli, sicut Marcus ait, scilicet Petrus, Iacobus, Ioannes et Andreas, separatim quaerunt tempus et signa praedictae destructionis ». Et attendendum, quod dupliciter potest quis inquirere de temporibus futuris: *uno modo,* inquirendo diei finalis horam determinatam, et haec est quaestio *curiosa,* quia, sicut Matthaei vigesimo quarto[^p524-8] dicitur, « de die illa et hora nemo scit, neque Angeli caelorum, nisi solus Pater ». Et ideo discipulis quaerentibus, Actuum primo: « Domine, si in tempore hoc restitues regnum Israel »? responsum est: « Non est vestrum scire tempora vel momenta, quae Pater posuit in sua potestate ». Et ideo primae ad Thessalonicenses quinto: « De temporibus et momentis, fratres, non indigetis, ut scribamus vobis. Ipsi enim diligenter scitis, quia dies Domini, sicut fur in nocte, ita veniet ». *Alio modo* potest quis inquirere in quadam generalitate et sine certi temporis significatione; et haec quaestio *studiosa* est. Nam huius ignorantia est periculosa; propter quod de Iudaeis, Ieremiae octavo[^p524-9]: « Turtur et hirundo et ciconia cognoverunt tempus adventus sui, populus autem meus » etc.; et supra duodecimo: « Hypocritae, faciem caeli et terrae nostis probare, hoc autem tempus quomodo non probatis »? Et ideo Dominus quaerentes non reprehendit, sed eis satisfacit. Unde Gregorius[^p524-10]: « Dominus ac Redemptor noster perituri mundi praecurrentia mala denuntiat, ut eo minus perturbent venientia, quo fuerint praescita. Minus enim iacula feriunt, quae praevidentur; et nos tolerabilius mundi mala suscipimus, si contra haec per praescientiae clypeum munimur ».

13. (Vers. 8.). *Qui dixit: Videte, ne seducamini* etc. Post praedictionem futurorum in quadam generalitate hic specialius descendit ad describendam persecutionem Ecclesiae[^p524-11]. Hanc autem describit dupliciter: primo *communiter respectu omnium;* secundo, *specialiter respectu perfectorum,* ibi[^p524-12]: *Sed ante haec omnia iniicient vobis manus suas.*

In describendo autem persecutiones futuras in Ecclesia post passionem Christi introducuntur tria, ex quibus ostenditur persecutio Ecclesiae esse conflanda, scilicet *multiplicatio haeresis*[^p524-13], *aggravatio bellorum* et *superventio pestilentiarum.*

Primo ergo quantum ad *multitudinem haeresum* dicit: *Qui dixit: Videte, ne seducamini,* scilicet per varios errores; propter quod ad Ephesios quarto[^p524-14]: « Iam non simus sicut parvuli fluctuantes et circumferamur omni vento doctrinae, in nequitia
<!-- page 525 -->
hominum, in astutia ad circumventionem erroris »; sicut secundae ad Thessalonicenses secundo: « Rogamus vos, ut non cito moveamini a vestro sensu; ne quis vos seducat ullo modo ». Et bene opus erit cautela, praecipue propter seducentium improbitatem.

14. Et ideo subdit: *Multi enim venient in nomine meo, dicentes: Quia ego sum,* praedicando scilicet haereses tanquam haeresiarchae. *Multos* quidem dicit propter multitudinem et varietatem haeresum, quae a principio Ecclesiae inceperunt et in fine Ecclesiae per filium perditionis vigebunt. Unde Glossa[^p525-1]: « Imminente Ierosolymorum excidio, multi fuerunt principes, qui se Christos dicerent et libertatis tempus appropinquare. Temporibus quoque Apostolorum haeresiarchae multi prodierunt ». Unde primae Ioannis quarto[^p525-2]: « Multi pseudoprophetae exierunt in mundum ». Inter hos fuit primus Simon magus, qui dicebat et in suis voluminibus scriptum reliquit, ut dicit Hieronymus[^p525-3]: « Ego sum speciosus, ego sum paracletus, ego sum omnipotens, ego sum sermo Dei ». Unde Actuum octavo: « Vir quidam erat, nomine Simon, dicens, se esse aliquem magnum; cui auscultabant omnes a minimo usque ad maximum, dicentes: Hic est virtus Dei, quae vocatur magna ». Refert etiam Iosephus[^p525-4], Simonem Essaeum fuisse in campis Iezrahel, qui praedicabat, se esse Christum. Legitur etiam[^p525-5], quod cum essent filii Herodis Romae et ibi disceptarent de regno, erant quatuor in Iudaea, quorum unusquisque se Christum praedicabat. Manichaeus etiam dixit, se esse Spiritum sanctum[^p525-6]. Mahometus etiam dixit, se esse aliquem magnum. Et finaliter post omnes sequetur impius antichristus, qui erit omnium malorum caput, de quo secundae ad Thessalonicenses secundo[^p525-7]: « Tunc revelabitur ille iniquus, cuius adventus erit secundum operationem satanae, in omni virtute et signis et prodigiis mendacibus et in omni seductione iniquitatis his qui pereunt, eo quod caritatem veritatis non receperunt, ut salvi fierent ». Unde Chrysostomus[^p525-8]: « Homo circa mortem phantasias videt, sic et mundus in exitu suo multos patitur errores ». — Sed quia post istos errores illucescet veritas, ideo subdit: *Et tempus appropinquabit*[^p525-9], scilicet cognoscendae veritatis; de quo tempore primae ad Corinthios quarto: « Nolite ante tempus iudicare, quoadusque veniat Dominus, qui et illuminabit abscondita tenebrarum »; Psalmus: « Cum accepero tempus, ego iustitias iudicabo ». — Et ideo addit: *Nolite ergo ire post illos*[^p525-10], per approbationem falsitatis, secundum illud Proverbiorum primo: « Fili mi, ne ambules cum eis. Pedes enim eorum ad malum currunt » etc. Vadunt autem post *Simonem* omnes simoniaci, post *Mahometum* omnes luxuriosi, post *antichristum* omnes superbi et ambitiosi; quia omnes tales sunt adversarii Christi, et multi sunt. Ideo primae Ioannis secundo[^p525-11]: « Filioli mei, novissima hora est; et sicut audistis, quia antichristus venit; nunc autem antichristi multi facti sunt ». Sed tales non sunt imitandi; propter quod Psalmus: « Noli aemulari in malignantibus »; et post: « Quoniam tanquam foenum velociter arescent ».

15. (Vers. 9.). Secundo quantum ad *aggravationem bellorum* subdit: *Cum autem audieritis proelia et seditiones, nolite terreri.* Sicut enim non est *errantium dolositas* subsequenda, sic non est *bellantium improbitas* formidanda, maxime viris iustis; unde Matthaei decimo[^p525-12]: « Nolite timere eos qui occidunt corpus, animam autem non possunt occidere »; quoniam vir iustus per proelia non perdit quod amat, et ideo huiusmodi proelia non formidat; Proverbiorum vigesimo octavo: « Fugit impius, nemine persequente; iustus autem quasi leo confidens absque terrore erit ». Unde Chrysostomus[^p525-13]:
<!-- page 526 -->
« Sancti viri in auditione sive corporalium, sive spiritualium proeliorum imperturbabiles permanent, quia ea quae sunt in mundo, non possunt nocere eis qui sunt extra mundum; sicut qui est in tabernaculo sonum quidem tempestatis audit, flatum autem non sentit ». Ideo Proverbiorum duodecimo[^p526-1]: « Non contristabit iustum, quidquid ei acciderit ». Non sunt igitur haec formidanda, quia non sunt *nociva.* — Non sunt etiam formidanda, quia sunt *opportuna;* ideo addit: *Oportet, primum haec fieri, sed nondum statim finis.* Chrysostomus[^p526-2]: « Non potest esse signum, quod semper fuit in usu. Nam bella haec corporalia semper fuerunt et erunt ». Quamdiu enim regnat concupiscentia, oportet, quod sint lites et bella; Iacobi quarto[^p526-3]: « Unde bella et lites in vobis? Nonne ex concupiscentiis, quae militant in membris vestris »? Permittit autem Dominus, haec fieri ad subversionem malorum et exercitationem bonorum, ut sic appareat illud Iob septimo[^p526-4]: « Militia est vita hominis super terram ». Et ideo ille bonus miles dicebat secundae ad Corinthios septimo: « Cum venissemus in Macedoniam, nullam requiem habuit caro nostra, sed omnem tribulationem passi sumus: foris pugnae, intus timores ». Nam Ioannis decimo sexto: « In mundo pressuram habebitis; sed confidite, ego vici mundum ».

16. (Vers. 10. 11.). Tertio quantum ad *superventionem pestilentiarum* subiungit: *Tunc dicebat illis: Surget gens contra gentem, et regnum adversus regnum.* Gregorius[^p526-5]: « Ecce, perturbatio hominum »; *et terraemotus magni erunt per loca,* « ecce, respectus irae desuper »; *et pestilentiae,* « ecce, inaequalitas corporum »; *et fames,* « ecce, sterilitas terrae »; *terroresque de caelo,* « ecce, inaequalitas aëris »; *et signa magna erunt,* non solum quia multa, sed etiam quia insolita. — Huiusmodi autem signa magna et multa praecesserunt eversionem Ierosolymorum, secundum quod narrat Iosephus[^p526-6], qui dicit, « stellam gladio similem per annum supra Ierusalem pependisse, et currus et equites armatos quadraginta diebus in aëre debellasse, et vitulam inter manus immolantium peperisse agnam ». Et haec signa magna praecesserunt eversionem secundam Ierusalem, sicut praecesserant destructionem ipsius factam sub Antiocho; secundi Machabaeorum quinto[^p526-7]: « Contigit, per universam Ierosolymorum civitatem videri diebus quadraginta per aëra equites discurrentes, auratas stolas habentes, et congressiones fieri cominus et scutorum motus et telorum iactus et aureorum splendorem armorum omnisque generis loricarum ».

17. Haec multiformis et pestilentialis turbatio, licet secundum partes aliquando fuerit in Ecclesia, sicut Gregorius[^p526-8] narrat, plene tamen erit in finali tribulatione, qua imminente, erit *perturbatio hominum* et *elementorum.* Nam perturbatio *hominum* erit, secundum illud Isaiae decimo nono[^p526-9]: « Concurrere faciam Aegyptios adversus Aegyptios, et pugnabit vir contra fratrem suum et vir contra amicum suum et civitas adversus civitatem et regnum adversus regnum »; et Zachariae octavo: « Dimisi omnes homines, unumquemque contra proximum suum ». Nec tantum erit perturbatio hominum, verum etiam *elementorum.* Unde Gregorius[^p526-10]: « Prius corda hominum et post elementa turbabuntur, ut, cum rerum ordo confunditur, ex qua re tribulatio veniat, monstretur ». Turbabuntur ergo elementa, secundum illud Isaiae vigesimo quarto[^p526-11]: « Cataractae de excelsis apertae sunt, et concutientur fundamenta terrae. Confractione confringetur terra » etc. Et Gregorius[^p526-12]: « Omnia, quae ad usum vitae accepimus, vertimus in culpam: tranquillitatem humanae pacis in securitatem, peregrinationem viae pro habitatione dileximus patriae, salutem corporum redegimus in usum vitiorum, ubertatis abundantiam non ad necessitatem carnis, sed ad perversitatem intorsimus voluptatis; serenitatem aëris coegimus nobis servire ad amorem terrenae delectationis. Iure ergo nos omnia feriunt, ut, quot in mundo habuimus gaudia, tot postmodum sentiamus tormenta ». Et hoc quidem recte, quia, Sapientiae undecimo[^p526-13], « per quae peccat quis, per haec et torquetur »; et iterum Sapientiae quinto: « Pugnabit pro eo orbis terrarum contra insensatos »; et ideo Sapientiae decimo sexto: « Creatura, tibi Factori deserviens, exardescit in tormentum adversus iniustos; et lenior fit
<!-- page 527 -->
ad benefaciendum pro his qui in te confidunt ». Unde et ipse Gregorius[^p527-1] dicit: « Quia omnia consummanda sunt, ante consummationem omnia perturbabuntur; et qui in cunctis deliquimus in cunctis ferimur, ut impleatur quod dictum est: *Pugnabit pro eo orbis terrarum contra insensatos* », Sapientiae quinto.

#### The formation unto foresight in respect of things to come.

9. *And some saying of the temple* etc. After he formed the disciples unto the caution of evils and unto the choosing of better things, here, third[^p523-5], he forms them unto *foresight in respect of things to come.* Now this part is divided into four parts. In the first he foretells the things that are to come *in general.* In the second, the things that are to come *concerning the persecution of the Church;* there[^p523-6]: *Take heed you be not seduced* etc. In the third, the things that are to come *concerning the destruction of Jerusalem,* there: *And when you shall see compassed about with an army.* In the fourth, the things that are to come *concerning the final judgement,* there: *And there shall be signs in the sun and the moon.*

Now in explaining *the prediction of things to come in a certain generality* three things are brought in, namely, *the occasion of foretelling,* from the occasion *the prediction,* and from the prediction *the ensuing question.*

10. (Vers. 5.). First, then, as to *the occasion of foretelling things to come,* he says: *And some saying of the temple, that it was adorned with goodly stones and gifts, he said.* To praisers of the temple of this kind, as to its permanence and opulence, he answered by foretelling things to come, lest they should trust in these transitory things, because, as is said to the Hebrews, the thirteenth chapter[^p523-7], « we have not here a lasting city, but we seek one that is to come »; and therefore the second to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « While we look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen. For the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal ». But the disciples, not yet being spiritual, magnified the temple as to *the solidity of the building;* whence Mark, the thirteenth chapter[^p523-8]: « As he was going out of the temple, one of his disciples said to him: Master, behold what manner of stones and what manner of buildings »! They magnified it as to *the sublimity of the worship of the Lord,* because it was adorned with gifts, as though God ought always to be worshipped in it. And in this they were deceived in three ways: both because *they magnified small things,* as if they were great, whereas yet it is said in Isaias, the last chapter[^p523-9]: « Heaven is my throne, and the earth my footstool. What is this house that you will build to me? And what is this place of my rest? My hand made all these things, and all these things were made, saith the Lord ». And therefore the most wise Solomon, the third of Kings, the eighth chapter: « If heaven, and the heavens of heavens cannot contain thee » etc. — Second, because *they magnified bodily and figural things* as true and spiritual things. Against which it is said in the Acts, the seventeenth chapter[^p523-10]: « God, who made the world, and all things therein, he, since he is Lord of heaven and earth, dwelleth not in temples made with hands, neither is he served with men's hands, as though he needed any thing », since he is God. — Third, they were deceived because *they magnified momentary and perishing things,* as though they were perpetual and eternal, whereas yet it is said in the first to the Corinthians, the seventh chapter[^p523-11]: « For the fashion of this world passeth away ».

11. (Vers. 6.). Second, as to *the prediction brought in* from the occasion received, it is subjoined: *These things which you see, the days will come in which there shall not be left a stone upon a stone that shall not be destroyed.* This indeed was said generally, and is true for *the time of the final judgement,* when all things shall be destroyed, according to that of Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p523-12]: « Heaven and earth shall pass » etc.; and the Apocalypse, the twenty-first chapter: « The first heaven and the first earth was gone, and the
<!-- page 524 -->
sea is now no more », as in the Psalm: « They shall perish, but thou shalt remain » etc. It is true also for *the destruction of Jerusalem* specially, which was done by Titus and Vespasian in the forty-second year after the passion of the Lord; above, the nineteenth chapter[^p524-1]: « The days shall come upon thee, and thy enemies shall cast a trench about thee, and shall beat thee flat to the ground, and they shall not leave in thee a stone upon a stone ». Whence Gregory[^p524-2] says that that Jerusalem with the temple was overthrown from the foundations, and a new one was afterwards rebuilt. Now the reason why the Lord deferred for forty-two years was that he might wait for them unto penance, as is said in the second of Peter, the third chapter[^p524-3]: « He dealeth patiently for your sake, not willing that any should perish ». But the reason why he did not defer longer was on account of the hardened perfidy of the Jews, which despised the truth on account of the veil and the shadow. Whence the Gloss[^p524-4]: « God took care to overturn the city and the temple and all the figural things, lest anyone after the coming of Christ should run back to them ». And for that time is understood that of Isaias, the first chapter[^p524-5]: « To what purpose do you offer me the multitude of your victims? saith the Lord. I am full ». « Offer sacrifice no more in vain; incense is an abomination to me. The new moons and the sabbaths and other festivals I will not abide ». And therefore to the Galatians, the fifth chapter[^p524-6]: « Stand fast, and be not held again under the yoke of bondage. Behold, I Paul tell you » etc.

12. (Vers. 7.). Third, as to *the question that arose* from the prediction heard, he subjoins: *And they asked him, saying: Master, when shall these things be, and what the sign when they shall begin to come to pass?* Now not all asked, but four principal ones, namely Peter, James, John and Andrew, because they were familiar with him. Whence the Gloss[^p524-7]: « Because to those praising the buildings of the temple he had foretold that all things were to be destroyed, the disciples, as Mark says, namely Peter, James, John and Andrew, ask apart the time and the signs of the foretold destruction ». And it is to be attended that one can inquire about future times in two ways: *in one way,* by inquiring the determinate hour of the last day, and this is a *curious* question, because, as is said in Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p524-8], « of that day and hour no one knoweth, neither the Angels of heaven, but the Father alone ». And therefore to the disciples asking, in the Acts, the first chapter: « Lord, wilt thou at this time restore the kingdom to Israel »? it was answered: « It is not for you to know the times or moments, which the Father hath put in his own power ». And therefore the first to the Thessalonians, the fifth chapter: « Of the times and moments, brethren, you need not that we should write to you. For yourselves know perfectly that the day of the Lord shall so come as a thief in the night ». *In another way* one can inquire in a certain generality and without the signifying of a certain time; and this question is *studious.* For the ignorance of it is perilous; on account of which, of the Jews, Jeremias, the eighth chapter[^p524-9]: « The turtle, and the swallow, and the stork have known the time of their coming, but my people » etc.; and above, the twelfth chapter: « Hypocrites, you know how to discern the face of the heaven and of the earth; but how is it that you do not discern this time »? And therefore the Lord does not rebuke those asking, but satisfies them. Whence Gregory[^p524-10]: « Our Lord and Redeemer announces the evils running before the world that is to perish, that they may disturb the less when they come, the more they have been foreknown. For darts which are foreseen strike less; and we bear the evils of the world more tolerably, if against them we are fortified by the shield of foreknowledge ».

13. (Vers. 8.). *Who said: Take heed you be not seduced* etc. After the prediction of things to come in a certain generality, here he descends more specially to describing the persecution of the Church[^p524-11]. Now this he describes in two ways: first *commonly in respect of all;* second, *specially in respect of the perfect,* there[^p524-12]: *But before all these things they will lay their hands upon you.*

Now in describing the persecutions to come in the Church after the passion of Christ, three things are brought in, from which it is shown that the persecution of the Church is to be kindled, namely, *the multiplication of heresy*[^p524-13], *the aggravation of wars* and *the supervening of pestilences.*

First, then, as to *the multitude of heresies* he says: *Who said: Take heed you be not seduced,* namely through various errors; on account of which to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter[^p524-14]: « That we be now no more as children tossed to and fro and carried about with every wind of doctrine, in the wickedness
<!-- page 525 -->
of men, in craftiness unto the circumvention of error »; as the second to the Thessalonians, the second chapter: « We beseech you that you be not easily moved from your sense; let no man deceive you by any means ». And caution will well be needed, especially on account of the improbity of the seducers.

14. And therefore he subjoins: *For many will come in my name, saying: I am he,* namely preaching heresies as heresiarchs. *Many* indeed he says on account of the multitude and variety of heresies, which began from the beginning of the Church and at the end of the Church will flourish through the son of perdition. Whence the Gloss[^p525-1]: « When the destruction of Jerusalem was imminent, there were many princes who called themselves Christs and said that the time of liberty was approaching. In the times of the Apostles also many heresiarchs came forth ». Whence the first of John, the fourth chapter[^p525-2]: « Many false prophets are gone out into the world ». Among these the first was Simon magus, who said, and left written in his volumes, as Jerome says[^p525-3]: « I am the beautiful one, I am the paraclete, I am the almighty, I am the word of God ». Whence the Acts, the eighth chapter: « There was a certain man named Simon, saying that he was some great one; to whom they all gave ear, from the least to the greatest, saying: This man is the power of God, which is called great ». Josephus also relates[^p525-4] that Simon the Essene was in the fields of Jezrahel, who preached that he was the Christ. It is read also[^p525-5] that when the sons of Herod were at Rome and were disputing there about the kingdom, there were four in Judaea, each one of whom preached himself the Christ. Manichaeus also said that he was the Holy Spirit[^p525-6]. Mahomet also said that he was some great one. And finally after all will follow the impious antichrist, who will be the head of all evils, of whom the second to the Thessalonians, the second chapter[^p525-7]: « Then shall that wicked one be revealed, whose coming shall be according to the working of satan, in all power, and signs, and lying wonders, and in all seduction of iniquity to them that perish, because they received not the love of the truth, that they might be saved ». Whence Chrysostom[^p525-8]: « A man about death sees phantasies; so too the world in its end suffers many errors ». — But because after these errors the truth will shine forth, therefore he subjoins: *And the time shall draw near*[^p525-9], namely of the truth to be known; of which time the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « Judge not before the time, until the Lord come, who both will bring to light the hidden things of darkness »; the Psalm: « When I shall take a time, I will judge justices ». — And therefore he adds: *Go ye not therefore after them*[^p525-10], by approving falsity, according to that of Proverbs, the first chapter: « My son, walk not thou with them. For their feet run to evil » etc. Now after *Simon* go all simoniacs, after *Mahomet* all the lustful, after *antichrist* all the proud and ambitious; because all such are adversaries of Christ, and they are many. Therefore the first of John, the second chapter[^p525-11]: « My little children, it is the last hour; and as you have heard that antichrist cometh, even now there are become many antichrists ». But such are not to be imitated; on account of which the Psalm: « Be not emulous of evildoers »; and after: « For they shall shortly wither away as grass ».

15. (Vers. 9.). Second, as to *the aggravation of wars* he subjoins: *And when you shall hear of battles and seditions, be not terrified.* For as *the deceitfulness of those who err* is not to be followed, so *the improbity of those who war* is not to be feared, especially by just men; whence Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p525-12]: « Fear ye not them that kill the body, and are not able to kill the soul »; for the just man does not lose through battles what he loves, and therefore he does not fear battles of this kind; Proverbs, the twenty-eighth chapter: « The wicked man fleeth, when no man pursueth; but the just, bold as a lion, shall be without dread ». Whence Chrysostom[^p525-13]:
<!-- page 526 -->
« Holy men, in the hearing of battles whether bodily or spiritual, remain imperturbable, because the things which are in the world cannot harm those who are outside the world; as he who is in a tent hears indeed the sound of the storm, but does not feel the blast ». Therefore Proverbs, the twelfth chapter[^p526-1]: « Whatsoever shall befall the just man, it shall not make him sad ». These things then are not to be feared, because they are not *harmful.* — Nor are they to be feared, because they are *opportune;* therefore he adds: *These things must first come to pass, but the end is not yet presently.* Chrysostom[^p526-2]: « That cannot be a sign which was always in use. For these bodily wars always were and will be ». For as long as concupiscence reigns, there must be strifes and wars; James, the fourth chapter[^p526-3]: « Whence are wars and strifes among you? Are they not from your concupiscences, which war in your members »? Now the Lord permits these things to be done for the overthrow of the wicked and the exercising of the good, that so there may appear that of Job, the seventh chapter[^p526-4]: « The life of man upon earth is a warfare ». And therefore that good soldier said in the second to the Corinthians, the seventh chapter: « When we were come into Macedonia, our flesh had no rest, but we suffered all tribulation: combats without, fears within ». For John, the sixteenth chapter: « In the world you shall have distress; but have confidence, I have overcome the world ».

16. (Vers. 10. 11.). Third, as to *the supervening of pestilences* he subjoins: *Then he said to them: Nation shall rise against nation, and kingdom against kingdom.* Gregory[^p526-5]: « Behold, the disturbance of men »; *and there shall be great earthquakes in divers places,* « behold, the regard of wrath from above »; *and pestilences,* « behold, the inequality of bodies »; *and famines,* « behold, the barrenness of the earth »; *and terrors from heaven,* « behold, the inequality of the air »; *and there shall be great signs,* not only because many, but also because unwonted. — Now great and many signs of this kind preceded the overthrow of Jerusalem, according as Josephus relates[^p526-6], who says « that a star like a sword hung over Jerusalem for a year, and chariots and armed horsemen fought in the air for forty days, and a heifer between the hands of those sacrificing brought forth a lamb ». And these great signs preceded the second overthrow of Jerusalem, as they had preceded its destruction done under Antiochus; the second of Machabees, the fifth chapter[^p526-7]: « It came to pass that through the whole city of Jerusalem for forty days there were seen horsemen running in the air, having gilded raiment, and encounters made hand to hand, and the shakings of shields, and casting of darts, and glittering of golden armour, and of harnesses of all sorts ».

17. This manifold and pestilential disturbance, although according to parts it has sometimes been in the Church, as Gregory relates[^p526-8], yet will be fully in the final tribulation, at whose imminence there will be *a disturbance of men* and *of elements.* For there will be a disturbance *of men,* according to that of Isaias, the nineteenth chapter[^p526-9]: « I will make the Egyptians to fight against the Egyptians, and a man shall fight against his brother, and a man against his friend, and city against city, and kingdom against kingdom »; and Zacharias, the eighth chapter: « I let all men go, every one against his neighbour ». Nor will there be only a disturbance of men, but also *of elements.* Whence Gregory[^p526-10]: « First the hearts of men and afterwards the elements will be disturbed, that, when the order of things is confounded, it may be shown from what thing the tribulation comes ». The elements therefore will be disturbed, according to that of Isaias, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p526-11]: « The flood-gates from on high are opened, and the foundations of the earth shall be shaken. With breaking shall the earth be broken » etc. And Gregory[^p526-12]: « All things which we received for the use of life we turn into fault: the tranquillity of human peace into security; we have loved the pilgrimage of the way instead of the habitation of the fatherland; we have reduced the health of bodies to the use of vices; the abundance of plenty we have twisted not to the necessity of the flesh, but to the perversity of pleasure; we have compelled the serenity of the air to serve us for the love of earthly delight. Rightly therefore do all things strike us, that as many joys as we have had in the world, so many torments we may afterwards feel ». And this indeed rightly, because, Wisdom, the eleventh chapter[^p526-13], « by what things a man sinneth, by the same also he is tormented »; and again Wisdom, the fifth chapter: « The whole world shall fight with him against the unwise »; and therefore Wisdom, the sixteenth chapter: « The creature serving thee the Maker is enkindled unto torment against the unjust; and is made gentler
<!-- page 527 -->
to do good for them that trust in thee ». Whence also Gregory himself[^p527-1] says: « Because all things are to be consummated, before the consummation all things will be disturbed; and we who have offended in all things are struck in all things, that what was said may be fulfilled: *The whole world shall fight with him against the unwise* », Wisdom, the fifth chapter.
