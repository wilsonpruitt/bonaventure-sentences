# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. I, Vers. 39-45
## *Primo praemittitur testimonium prophetiae*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 27 -->

#### Secundo, de gratiarum actione pro utraque conceptione.

71. *Exsurgens autem Maria* etc. Terminata parte illa, in qua egit de sterilis et Virginis fecundatione, hic sequitur pars secunda, in qua agit de *gratiarum actione.* Et iuxta hunc duplicem modum pars ista habet duas, in quarum prima fit gratiarum actio et laudis decantatio propter *conceptum Salvatoris;* in secunda, propter *conceptum et partum praecursoris,* ibi[^p27-7]: *Elisabeth impletum est tempus* etc.

#### De prima gratiarum actione dicuntur tria.

Ad hoc autem, quod decantatio laudis sit ex integro et perfecto affectu, exigitur *certificatio veritatis* in intellectu et *exhibitio caritatis* in affectu et effectu[^p27-8], unum sicut *praecedens,* alterum sicut *consequens.* Ideo prima pars habet tres, in quarum prima praemittitur *testimonium prophetiae;* in secunda subditur *canticum laetitiae,* ibi[^p27-9]: *Et ait Maria: Magnificat* etc.; in tertia subiungitur *obsequium beneficentiae,* ibi: *Mansit autem Maria cum illa* etc. Primum astruit fidem, secundum erigit spem, tertium ostendit caritatem.

#### Primo praemittitur testimonium prophetiae.

In descriptione autem *testimonii prophetiae* procedit Evangelista hoc ordine. Nam primo introducit *testificandi opportunitatem;* secundo, *testimonii veritatem,* ibi[^p27-10]: *Et unde hoc mihi* etc. — Ad *testificandi* autem *opportunitatem* tria concurrunt, scilicet *Virginis salutantis occursus* et *virgineae salutationis effectus* et *Elisabeth resalutantis affectus;* primum, ibi[^p27-11]: *Exsurgens Maria* etc.; secundum, ibi: *Factum est, ut audivit* etc.; tertium, ibi: *Et repleta est Spiritu sancto* etc.

72. (Vers. 39.). Primo igitur introducitur *Virginis salutantis occursus* ut *festinus* et *familiaris.* — Propter *festinationem* ait: *Exsurgens autem Maria in diebus illis, abiit in montana cum festinatione
<!-- page 28 -->
in civitatem Iuda;* quasi dicat: non tardavit, sed diluculo surrexit, secundum illud Canticorum secundo[^p28-1]: « Surge, propera, amica mea, columba mea, formosa mea, et veni ». Unde sibi competit illud Proverbiorum trigesimo primo, quod dicitur de muliere forti, quae « de nocte surrexit deditque praedam domesticis suis et cibaria ancillis suis »; surrexit enim, ut ministraret Elisabeth seni et debili. Unde « nec eam retardavit *asperitas itineris* », secundum quod dicitur in Glossa[^p28-2], et littera designat, quia *abiit in montana,* scilicet *cum festinatione,* ad veritatem explorandam, sicut illi exploratores, Iosue secundo: « Ad montana conscendite »; vel etiam cum ipso sponso, Canticorum secundo: « Ecce, iste venit saliens in montibus et transiliens colles ». Non etiam retardavit *viae prolixitas,* quod notat ibi: *In civitatem Iuda,* id est Ierusalem[^p28-3], ut iam possit ipsa clamare cum Isaia, secundo[^p28-4]: « Venite, ascendamus ad montem Domini et ad domum Dei Iacob ». Nec in itinere fecit moram, quia *cum festinatione,* sicut Rachel, Genesis vigesimo nono, quae, viso Iacob, « festinavit, ut nuntiaret patri suo ».

73. *Spiritualiter* autem datur ex hoc nobis intelligi, quod necessaria est ei festinatio, qui vult ad perfectionem attingere; ad Hebraeos quarto[^p28-5]: « Festinemus ingredi in illam requiem »; ideo primae ad Corinthios nono: « Sic currite, ut comprehendatis ». Quocontra dicitur de fatuis virginibus Matthaei vigesimo quinto, quod « novissime venerunt »; et Ecclesiastici quinto: « Ne tardes converti ad Dominum, et ne differas de die in diem ».

74. (Vers. 40.). Fuit occursus Virginis festinus, fuit etiam *familiaris;* propter quod dicit: *Et intravit in domum Zachariae et salutavit Elisabeth. Intravit,* inquam, ut familiariter cohabitaret; Sapientiae octavo[^p28-6]: « Intrans in domum meam, conquiescam cum illa ». *Intravit,* non foris stabat, sicut illa « mulier vaga, quietis impatiens nec valens in domo consistere pedibus suis », Proverbiorum septimo. *Intravit* etiam, non ut litigaret, sed ut *salutaret,* secundum quod moris est Sanctis; ad Romanos decimo sexto: « Salutate invicem in osculo sancto ». *Salutavit,* inquam, non solum optando, sed etiam salutem in Ierusalem apportando, ut adimpleatur illud Isaiae sexagesimo secundo[^p28-7]: « Propter Sion non tacebo et propter Ierusalem non quiescam, donec egrediatur ut splendor iustus eius, et Salvator eius ut lampas accendatur »; et post: « Dicite filiae Sion: Ecce, Salvator tuus venit; ecce, merces eius cum eo, et opus illius coram illo ». — Et nota, quod Virgo prius domum intravit et postea salutavit, quia iam incipiebat Virgo mandatis evangelicis consentire; nam infra decimo[^p28-8] dicitur: « Neminem per viam salutaveritis »; et postea subditur: « In quamcumque domum intraveritis, primum dicite: Pax huic domui ». Quod et fecit Virgo beata.

75. (Vers. 41.). Secundo vero introducitur *virgineae salutationis effectus,* qui fuit *excitatio matris* et *laetificatio prolis.* — Propter *matris excitationem* dicitur: *Et factum est, ut audivit salutationem Mariae Elisabeth.* Salutationem namque Virginis Elisabeth audiverat, quia eam amabat; Canticorum octavo[^p28-9]: « Quae habitas in hortis, amici auscultant te, fac me audire vocem tuam »; et Ioannis octavo: « Qui ex Deo est verba Dei audit »; unde primae ad Thessalonicenses secundo: « Cum accepissetis a nobis verbum auditus Dei, accepistis illud non ut verbum hominum, sed, sicut est vere, verbum Dei ». Audivit utique verbum salutis ab ea quae conceperat Verbum incarnatum, in quo solo salus est, secundum illud Iacobi primo: « In mansuetudine suscipite insitum verbum, quod potest salvare animas vestras ».

76. Fuit ergo mater verbo Virginis excitata, fuit etiam *proles laetificata;* unde dicit: *Exsultavit,* in gaudio, *infans in utero eius.* In quo ostenditur, quod Ioannes erat amicus Sponsi praecipuus, secundum quod de ipso scribitur Ioannis tertio[^p28-10]: « Amicus sponsi, qui stat et audit vocem eius, gaudio gaudet propter vocem sponsi ». *Exsultavit,* inquam, prae desiderio, sicut Patres; Ioannis octavo: « Abraham exsultavit, ut videret diem meum; vidit et gavisus est ». *Exsultavit,* ad praesentiam Domini Salvatoris, quasi gestiens salutare et assurgere Domino suo, cuius erat praecursor. Unde dicitur in Glossa Bedae[^p28-11], « quia lingua non poterat, animo exsultante salutat et suae praecursionis officium inchoat. Ecce, apparet quod Angelus dixerat: Spiritu sancto replebitur adhuc ex utero matris suae ». — Ex quo datur *spiritualiter* intelligi, quod propositum sanctum non potest latere, quin se manifestet, et amor interior prorumpat in opus; unde Gregorius[^p28-12]: « Nunquam amor Dei est otiosus ».

77. (Vers. 42.). Tertio subiungitur *Elisabeth salutantis affectus magnus* et *benignus.* — *Propter
<!-- page 29 -->
magnitudinem* dicitur: *Et repleta est Spiritu sancto Elisabeth et exclamavit voce magna.* Magnitudo namque vocis signum est magnae affectionis, quia[^p29-1] « voces sunt notae passionum »; Ecclesiastici decimo quinto: « Adimplebit eum Dominus spiritu sapientiae et intellectus »; et: « Ipse tanquam imbres mittet eloquia sapientiae suae, et in oratione confitebitur Domino ». Ideo autem *voce magna clamavit,* quia illum continebat in utero, qui erat *vox Verbi,* secundum quod de ipso dicitur Isaiae quadragesimo[^p29-2]: « Vox clamantis in deserto: Parate viam Domini ». Cui etiam dicitur ibidem: « Super montem excelsum ascende tu, qui evangelizas Sion; exalta in fortitudine vocem tuam, qui evangelizas Ierusalem, exalta, noli timere. Dic civitatibus Iudae: Ecce, Deus vester, ecce, Dominus Deus in fortitudine veniet ».

78. Nec tam fuit vox eius clamosa quam etiam[^p29-3] *devota;* unde ad exprimendum *benignitatem affectus* subdit dicens: *Benedicta tu in mulieribus, et benedictus fructus ventris tui.* Benedicit Elisabeth Virginem simul et prolem, ut compleat angelicam salutationem. Benedictionem sibi optat et iam benedictam annuntiat ea benedictione, quam Dominus Abrahae promiserat Genesis duodecimo[^p29-4]: « Benedicam tibi et magnificabo nomen tuum, erisque benedictus. Benedicam benedicentibus tibi et maledicam maledicentibus tibi, atque in semine tuo benedicentur universae cognationes terrae ». Hanc benedictionem praedixit Isaac in figura *agri,* Genesis vigesimo septimo: « Ecce, odor filii mei sicut odor agri pleni, cui benedixit Dominus ». Hanc confirmavit Iacob, Genesis penultimo: « Omnipotens benedicet tibi benedictionibus caeli desuper, benedictionibus abyssi iacentis deorsum, benedictionibus uberum et vulvae; benedictiones patris tui confortatae sunt benedictionibus patrum eius, donec veniret desiderium collium aeternorum ». Hanc desiderabat et hanc imprecabatur Moyses, Deuteronomii vigesimo octavo[^p29-5]: « Benedictus fructus ventris tui et fructus terrae tuae ». Et ideo cantemus cum David: « Benedixisti, Domine, terram tuam ».

79. Completum est etiam in Maria illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto[^p29-6]: « In multitudine electorum habebit laudem et inter benedictos benedicetur ». Benedicta quidem fuit Iahel, Iudicum quinto: « Benedicta in mulieribus Iahel ». Benedicta fuit Ruth, Ruth tertio: « Benedicta es a Domino, filia ». Benedicta Abigail, primi Regum vigesimo quinto: « Benedicta tu, quae prohibuisti me hodie, ne me mea manu ulciscerer ». Benedicta Iudith, decimo tertio: « Benedicta es tu, filia, a Domino Deo excelso prae omnibus mulieribus super terram ». Inter has mulieres et super has mulieres benedicta est virgo Maria, quia illae[^p29-7] benedictiones impletae sunt in hac.

80. *Et unde hoc mihi, ut veniat* etc. Praemissa opportunitate testificandi, subditur hic *veritas testimonii,* ad cuius explicationem tria introducit Evangelista. Primum est *praeconium admirativum;* secundum est *signum approbativum;* tertium vero, *testimonium affirmativum*[^p29-8]. Primum tenet rationem propositionis, secundum assumtionis, et tertium conclusionis.

81. (Vers. 43.). Introducit ergo *praeconium admirativum,* cum dicit Elisabeth: *Et unde hoc mihi, ut veniat Mater Domini mei ad me?* Hoc enim est valde laudabile et etiam admirabile, quod mulier sit Mater Dei, et quod Mater Dei visitet ancillam Dei. Unde poterat dicere, sicut dixit Areuna regi David secundi Regum ultimo[^p29-9]: « Quid causae est, ut veniat dominus meus rex ad servum suum »? Maius enim est esse Matrem Dei; unde dignatio fuit Matris Domini omnium suis pedibus visitare alienum hospitium. Unde Virgo gloriatur Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto[^p29-10]: « Ego mater pulcrae dilectionis et timoris et sanctae spei »; quia propter dignitatem maternam
<!-- page 30 -->
est virgo Maria *diligenda, veneranda* et cum omni *fiducia adeunda* tanquam summae misericordiae mater.

82. (Vers. 44.). Secundo subdit *indicium approbativum,* cum dicit: *Ecce enim, ut facta est vox salutationis tuae in auribus meis, exsultavit in gaudio infans in utero meo;* et hoc est signum, quod « diffusa est gratia in labiis tuis »; et propterea certum est, quod « benedixit te Deus in aeternum », in Psalmo[^p30-1]. Et ex quo infans supra naturam exsultavit, certum est, quod Dominus naturae sibi occurrerit. Et ex quo infantulus respondet ex utero, certum est, te concepisse illum qui vocat ab utero, secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo nono: « Dominus ab utero vocavit me, de ventre matris meae recordatus est nominis mei » etc. Et rursus, ex quo puer exsultavit, certum est, iam consummatum esse veraciter quod in Isaia[^p30-2] dicitur: « Exsulta et lauda, habitatio Sion, quia magnus in medio tui Sanctus Israel »; et illud Zachariae nono: « Exsulta satis, filia Sion, iubila, filia Ierusalem; ecce, rex tuus venit tibi iustus et salvator ».

83. (Vers. 45.). Ex quo igitur mirabiliter exsultavit praecursor, iam certum habebat indicium, quod venerit Salvator; et quoniam iam certum habebat indicium, ideo tertio veritati subditur *testimonium,* sive oraculum *affirmativum,* cum dicitur: *Beata, quae credidisti, quoniam perficientur ea quae dicta sunt tibi a Domino.* Fides enim beatam facit; Matthaei decimo sexto[^p30-3]: « Beatus es, Simon Bariona, quia caro et sanguis non revelavit tibi, sed Pater meus, qui in caelis est »; et Ioannis vigesimo: « Beati, qui non viderunt et crediderunt ». — Facit autem fides beatos, quia inchoando in *merito* disponit ad consummationem in *praemio;* ad Philippenses primo: « Confidens hoc ipsum, quia qui coepit in vobis opus bonum perficiet », et hoc merito fidei; unde Matthaei octavo: « Vade, et sicut credidisti, fiat tibi »; et sic factum est Mariae.

84. Nota, quod beatificantur in Scriptura non solum *credentes,* sed etiam *sperantes;* Psalmus[^p30-4]: « Beatus vir, cuius est nomen Domini spes eius ». — Item, *diligentes;* Ecclesiastici quadragesimo octavo: « Beati, qui te viderunt et in amicitia tua decorati sunt ». — Item, *timentes;* Ecclesiastici vigesimo quinto: « Beatus, cui datum est habere timorem Dei »; rursus Psalmus: « Beatus vir, qui timet Dominum ». — Item, *operantes;* Ioannis decimo tertio[^p30-5]: « Si haec scitis, beati eritis, si feceritis ea »; et infra undecimo: « Beati, qui audiunt verbum Dei et custodiunt illud ». — Item, *sustinentes;* primae Petri tertio: « Sed et si quid patimini propter iustitiam, beati »; et Matthaei quinto: « Beati estis, cum maledixerint vobis homines et persecuti vos fuerint et dixerint omne malum adversum vos, mentientes, propter me ». — Item, *contemplantes;* tertii Regum decimo: « Beati viri tui, et beati servi tui hi, qui stant coram te semper et audiunt sapientiam tuam ».

85. Ex omnibus his causis *beata* fuit virgo Maria, ex causa etiam singulari fuit *beatissima,* quia concepit Filium Dei; infra undecimo[^p30-6]: « Beatus venter, qui te portavit, et ubera, quae suxisti ». Et propterea ipsamet dicit infra eodem: « Ecce enim, ex hoc beatam me dicent omnes generationes ». *Beata ergo, quae credidisti,* quia credendo concepisti et concipiendo beatitudinem omnibus gentibus quantum ad sufficientiam protulisti. In huius *figura,* Genesis trigesimo[^p30-7] « dixit Lia: Hoc pro beatitudine mea, beatam quippe me dicent omnes mulieres ».

## English
<!-- page 27 -->

#### Secondly, on the thanksgiving for each conception.

71. *And Mary rising up* etc. That part being ended in which he treated of the fecundation of the barren woman and of the Virgin, here follows the second part, in which he treats of *the giving of thanks.* And according to this twofold mode this part has two parts, in the first of which there is a giving of thanks and a chanting of praise on account of *the conception of the Saviour;* in the second, on account of *the conception and birth of the precursor,* there[^p27-7]: *Elizabeth's full time was come* etc.

#### Three things are said of the first thanksgiving.

Now for the chanting of praise to be from an entire and perfect affection, there is required *the certification of truth* in the intellect and *the showing of charity* in the affection and effect[^p27-8], the one as *preceding,* the other as *consequent.* Therefore the first part has three parts, in the first of which is put forward *the testimony of prophecy;* in the second is subjoined *the canticle of gladness,* there[^p27-9]: *And Mary said: Magnificat* etc.; in the third is added *the service of beneficence,* there: *And Mary abode with her* etc. The first builds up faith, the second raises hope, the third shows charity.

#### Firstly the testimony of prophecy is put forward.

Now in the description of *the testimony of prophecy* the Evangelist proceeds in this order. For first he introduces *the opportunity of testifying;* secondly, *the truth of the testimony,* there[^p27-10]: *And whence is this to me* etc. — Now to *the opportunity of testifying* three things concur, namely *the meeting of the Virgin saluting* and *the effect of the virginal salutation* and *the affection of Elizabeth saluting in return;* the first, there[^p27-11]: *Mary rising up* etc.; the second, there: *And it came to pass, that when Elizabeth heard* etc.; the third, there: *And Elizabeth was filled with the Holy Spirit* etc.

72. (Vers. 39.). First, then, is introduced *the meeting of the Virgin saluting* as *hasty* and *familiar.* — On account of *the haste* he says: *And Mary rising up in those days, went into the hill country with haste
<!-- page 28 -->
into a city of Juda;* as if to say: she did not delay, but rose at dawn, according to that of the Canticle, the second chapter[^p28-1]: « Arise, make haste, my love, my dove, my beautiful one, and come ». Whence there befits her that of Proverbs, the thirty-first chapter, which is said of the valiant woman, who « hath risen in the night, and given a prey to her household, and victuals to her maidens »; for she rose up to minister to Elizabeth, old and weak. Whence « neither did *the roughness of the journey* delay her », according as is said in the Gloss[^p28-2], and the letter indicates, because *she went into the hill country,* namely *with haste,* to explore the truth, like those spies, Josue, the second chapter: « Get ye up to the mountains »; or else with the bridegroom himself, the Canticle, the second chapter: « Behold, he cometh leaping upon the mountains, skipping over the hills ». Nor yet did *the length of the way* delay her, which he notes there: *Into a city of Juda,* that is, Jerusalem[^p28-3], so that now she herself could cry out with Isaias, the second chapter[^p28-4]: « Come, and let us go up to the mountain of the Lord, and to the house of the God of Jacob ». Nor did she make delay on the journey, because *with haste,* like Rachel, Genesis, the twenty-ninth chapter, who, when she had seen Jacob, « made haste to tell her father ».

73. *Spiritually,* however, it is given us to understand from this that haste is necessary to him who wills to attain to perfection; to the Hebrews, the fourth chapter[^p28-5]: « Let us hasten to enter into that rest »; therefore the first to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter: « So run that you may obtain ». Against which it is said of the foolish virgins in Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter, that « at last came »; and Ecclesiasticus, the fifth chapter: « Delay not to be converted to the Lord, and defer it not from day to day ».

74. (Vers. 40.). The meeting of the Virgin was hasty, it was also *familiar;* on account of which he says: *And she entered into the house of Zachary, and saluted Elizabeth. She entered,* I say, to dwell with her familiarly; Wisdom, the eighth chapter[^p28-6]: « When I go into my house, I shall repose myself with her ». *She entered,* she did not stand outside, like that « wandering woman, not bearing to be quiet, not able to abide still at home on her feet », Proverbs, the seventh chapter. *She entered* also, not to quarrel, but to *salute,* according as is the custom of the Saints; to the Romans, the sixteenth chapter: « Salute one another with a holy kiss ». *She saluted,* I say, not only by wishing, but also by bringing salvation into Jerusalem, so that there may be fulfilled that of Isaias, the sixty-second chapter[^p28-7]: « For Sion's sake I will not hold my peace, and for the sake of Jerusalem I will not rest, till her just one come forth as brightness, and her saviour be lighted as a lamp »; and after: « Say to the daughter of Sion: Behold, thy Saviour cometh; behold, his reward is with him, and his work before him ». — And note that the Virgin first entered the house and afterwards saluted, because the Virgin was already beginning to consent to the evangelical commands; for below, in the tenth chapter[^p28-8], it is said: « Salute no man by the way »; and afterwards is subjoined: « Into whatsoever house you enter, first say: Peace be to this house ». Which the blessed Virgin also did.

75. (Vers. 41.). Secondly, then, is introduced *the effect of the virginal salutation,* which was *the stirring up of the mother* and *the gladdening of the offspring.* — On account of *the stirring up of the mother* it is said: *And it came to pass that when Elizabeth heard the salutation of Mary.* For Elizabeth had heard the salutation of the Virgin because she loved her; the Canticle, the eighth chapter[^p28-9]: « Thou that dwellest in the gardens, the friends hearken: make me hear thy voice »; and John, the eighth chapter: « He that is of God heareth the words of God »; whence the first to the Thessalonians, the second chapter: « When you had received of us the word of the hearing of God, you received it not as the word of men, but, as it is indeed, the word of God ». She heard indeed the word of salvation from her who had conceived the incarnate Word, in whom alone is salvation, according to that of James, the first chapter: « With meekness receive the ingrafted word, which is able to save your souls ».

76. The mother, then, was stirred up by the word of the Virgin; *the offspring* was also *gladdened;* whence he says: *The infant leaped,* in joy, *in her womb.* In which it is shown that John was the chief friend of the Bridegroom, according as is written of him in John, the third chapter[^p28-10]: « The friend of the bridegroom, who standeth and heareth him, rejoiceth with joy because of the bridegroom's voice ». *He leaped,* I say, from desire, like the Fathers; John, the eighth chapter: « Abraham rejoiced that he might see my day; he saw it and was glad ». *He leaped* at the presence of the Lord Saviour, as if longing to salute and to rise up to his Lord, whose precursor he was. Whence it is said in the Gloss of Bede[^p28-11], « because he could not with the tongue, he salutes with exulting spirit and begins the office of his precursorship. Behold, there appears what the angel had said: He shall be filled with the Holy Spirit even from his mother's womb ». — From which it is given to understand *spiritually* that a holy purpose cannot lie hidden without manifesting itself, and interior love bursts forth into work; whence Gregory[^p28-12]: « The love of God is never idle ».

77. (Vers. 42.). Thirdly is subjoined *the affection of Elizabeth saluting, great* and *kind.* — *On account of
<!-- page 29 -->
its greatness* it is said: *And Elizabeth was filled with the Holy Spirit, and she cried out with a loud voice.* For greatness of voice is a sign of great affection, because[^p29-1] « voices are the marks of the passions »; Ecclesiasticus, the fifteenth chapter: « The Lord shall fill him with the spirit of wisdom and understanding »; and: « He shall pour forth the words of his wisdom as showers, and in his prayer he shall confess to the Lord ». And she *cried out with a loud voice* for this reason, that she was holding in her womb him who was *the voice of the Word,* according as is said of him in Isaias, the fortieth chapter[^p29-2]: « The voice of one crying in the desert: Prepare ye the way of the Lord ». To whom also it is said in the same place: « Get thee up upon a high mountain, thou that bringest good tidings to Sion[^tr-evangelizare]; lift up thy voice with strength, thou that bringest good tidings to Jerusalem, lift it up, fear not. Say to the cities of Juda: Behold your God, behold the Lord God shall come with strength ».

78. Nor was her voice so much loud as also[^p29-3] *devout;* whence, to express *the kindness of her affection,* he subjoins, saying: *Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb.* Elizabeth blesses the Virgin and the offspring together, so as to complete the angelic salutation. She wishes her blessing and announces her already blessed with that blessing which the Lord had promised to Abraham in Genesis, the twelfth chapter[^p29-4]: « I will bless thee, and magnify thy name, and thou shalt be blessed. I will bless them that bless thee, and curse them that curse thee, and in thy seed shall all the kindreds of the earth be blessed ». This blessing Isaac foretold in the figure of *the field,* Genesis, the twenty-seventh chapter: « Behold, the smell of my son is as the smell of a plentiful field, which the Lord hath blessed ». This Jacob confirmed, Genesis, the next to last chapter: « The Almighty shall bless thee with the blessings of heaven above, with the blessings of the deep that lieth beneath, with the blessings of the breasts and of the womb; the blessings of thy father are strengthened with the blessings of his fathers, until the desire of the everlasting hills should come ». This Moses desired and this he invoked, Deuteronomy, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p29-5]: « Blessed shall be the fruit of thy womb, and the fruit of thy ground ». And therefore let us sing with David: « Lord, thou hast blessed thy land ».

79. There was fulfilled also in Mary that of Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p29-6]: « In the multitude of the elect she shall have praise, and among the blessed she shall be blessed ». Blessed indeed was Jahel, Judges, the fifth chapter: « Blessed among women be Jahel ». Blessed was Ruth, Ruth, the third chapter: « Blessed art thou of the Lord, daughter ». Blessed Abigail, the first of Kings, the twenty-fifth chapter: « Blessed be thou, who hast kept me today from revenging me with my own hand ». Blessed Judith, the thirteenth chapter: « Blessed art thou, O daughter, by the Lord the most high God, above all women upon the earth ». Among these women and above these women is the virgin Mary blessed, because those[^p29-7] blessings were fulfilled in her.

80. *And whence is this to me, that there should come* etc. The opportunity of testifying having been put forward, there is subjoined here *the truth of the testimony,* for the unfolding of which the Evangelist introduces three things. The first is *the admiring proclamation;* the second is *the approving sign;* the third, then, *the affirming testimony*[^p29-8]. The first holds the place of the proposition, the second of the assumption, and the third of the conclusion.

81. (Vers. 43.). He introduces, then, *the admiring proclamation,* when Elizabeth says: *And whence is this to me, that the Mother of my Lord should come to me?* For this is very praiseworthy and also wonderful, that a woman should be the Mother of God, and that the Mother of God should visit the handmaid of God. Whence she could have said, as Areuna said to king David in the second of Kings, the last chapter[^p29-9]: « What is the cause that my lord the king should come to his servant »? For it is a greater thing to be the Mother of God; whence it was a condescension of the Mother of the Lord of all to visit with her own feet another's lodging. Whence the Virgin glories in Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p29-10]: « I am the mother of fair love, and of fear, and of holy hope »; because on account of her maternal dignity
<!-- page 30 -->
the virgin Mary is *to be loved, to be venerated,* and *to be approached* with all *confidence* as the mother of supreme mercy.

82. (Vers. 44.). Secondly she subjoins *the approving indication,* when she says: *For behold, as soon as the voice of thy salutation sounded in my ears, the infant in my womb leaped for joy;* and this is a sign that « grace is poured abroad in thy lips »; and therefore it is certain that « God hath blessed thee for ever », in the Psalm[^p30-1]. And since the infant leaped above nature, it is certain that the Lord of nature had come to meet him. And since the little infant responds from the womb, it is certain that thou hast conceived him who calls from the womb, according to that of Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter: « The Lord hath called me from the womb, from the bowels of my mother he hath been mindful of my name » etc. And again, since the child leaped, it is certain that there is now truly accomplished what is said in Isaias[^p30-2]: « Rejoice and praise, O thou habitation of Sion, for great in the midst of thee is the Holy One of Israel »; and that of Zacharias, the ninth chapter: « Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Sion, shout for joy, O daughter of Jerusalem; behold, thy king cometh to thee, the just and saviour ».

83. (Vers. 45.). Since, then, the precursor leaped wonderfully, she now had a certain indication that the Saviour had come; and since she now had a certain indication, therefore thirdly there is subjoined to the truth *the testimony,* or *affirming* oracle, when it is said: *Blessed art thou that hast believed, because those things shall be accomplished that were spoken to thee by the Lord.* For faith makes blessed; Matthew, the sixteenth chapter[^p30-3]: « Blessed art thou, Simon Bar-Jona, because flesh and blood hath not revealed it to thee, but my Father who is in heaven »; and John, the twentieth chapter: « Blessed are they that have not seen, and have believed ». — Now faith makes men blessed because, by beginning in *merit,* it disposes to consummation in *reward;* to the Philippians, the first chapter: « Being confident of this very thing, that he who hath begun a good work in you will perfect it », and this by the merit of faith; whence Matthew, the eighth chapter: « Go, and as thou hast believed, so be it done to thee »; and so it was done to Mary.

84. Note that in Scripture not only *those believing* are called blessed, but also *those hoping;* the Psalm[^p30-4]: « Blessed is the man whose hope is the name of the Lord ». — Likewise, *those loving;* Ecclesiasticus, the forty-eighth chapter: « Blessed are they that saw thee, and were honoured with thy friendship ». — Likewise, *those fearing;* Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fifth chapter: « Blessed is he to whom it is given to have the fear of God »; again the Psalm: « Blessed is the man that feareth the Lord ». — Likewise, *those working;* John, the thirteenth chapter[^p30-5]: « If you know these things, you shall be blessed if you do them »; and below, in the eleventh chapter: « Blessed are they who hear the word of God, and keep it ». — Likewise, *those enduring;* the first of Peter, the third chapter: « But if also you suffer any thing for justice' sake, blessed »; and Matthew, the fifth chapter: « Blessed are ye when men shall revile you, and persecute you, and speak all that is evil against you, untruly, for my sake ». — Likewise, *those contemplating;* the third of Kings, the tenth chapter: « Blessed are thy men, and blessed are these thy servants, who stand before thee always and hear thy wisdom ».

85. For all these causes the virgin Mary was *blessed;* for a singular cause also she was *most blessed,* because she conceived the Son of God; below, in the eleventh chapter[^p30-6]: « Blessed is the womb that bore thee, and the paps that thou hast sucked ». And therefore she herself says below, in the same chapter: « For behold, from henceforth all generations shall call me blessed ». *Blessed,* then, *art thou that hast believed,* because by believing thou hast conceived, and by conceiving thou hast brought forth beatitude for all nations as to sufficiency. In *figure* of this, in Genesis, the thirtieth chapter[^p30-7], « Lia said: This is for my happiness, for all women will call me blessed ».
