# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. VI, Vers. 39-49
## *Dicebat autem illis et similitudinem. Post praecepta propria et expressa subiungit hic Evangelista documenta Domini similitudinaria et translata*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 160 -->

92. *Dicebat autem illis et similitudinem.* Post praecepta propria et expressa subiungit hic Evangelista documenta Domini *similitudinaria* et translata, in quibus Dominus quadrupliciter nos informat. Primo namque *retrahit a vanitate praesidendi.* Secundo vero *revocat a temeritate iudicandi,* ibi[^p160-7]: *Quid autem vides festucam* etc. Tertio *informat ad prudentiam discernendi,* ibi: *Non enim est arbor bona* etc. Quarto vero *invitat ad diligentiam operandi,* ibi: *Omnis, qui venit ad me* etc.

Primo ergo *retrahit a vanitate praesidendi* ex consideratione triplici, scilicet ex consideratione *periculi declinandi* et *vitii detestandi* et *exempli imitandi.*

93. (Vers. 39.). *Declinandum* namque est *ruinae periculum,* quod imminet praesidenti superbe; propter quod dicit: *Nunquid potest caecus caecum ducere?* quasi dicat: non potest sane propter periculum ruinae, quod adiungit: *Nonne ambo in foveam cadunt?* Sed homo praesumtuosus caecus est, secundum illud Apocalypsis tertio[^p160-8]: « Dicis, quod dives sum et locupletatus et nullius egeo; et nescis, quia tu es miser et miserabilis et pauper et caecus et nudus ». De tali ad Romanos secundo: « Confidis, te ipsum ducem esse caecorum, lumen eorum qui in tenebris sunt, eruditorem insipientium ». « Qui ergo alios doces te ipsum non doces »; quasi dicat: caecus es. — Et ideo, qui ambit et praesumit aliis praeferri, cum sit caecus, se et alios in praecipitium demergit; unde Ieremiae quinquagesimo[^p160-9]: « Grex perditus factus est populus meus, pastores eorum seduxerunt eos ». Et ratio huius redditur Ieremiae decimo: « Stulte egerunt pastores et Dominum non requisierunt; propterea non intellexerunt, et omnis grex eorum dispersus est ». Et ideo subdit: *Ambo in foveam cadunt.* Et hoc est quod dicit Gregorius[^p160-10]: « Dum pastor per abrupta vitiorum graditur, necesse est, ut grex in praecipitium trahatur ». Et tales maledictionem Domini merentur, secundum illud Deuteronomii vigesimo septimo: « Maledictus, qui errare facit caecum »; sicut et lucifer, qui voluit aliis praeesse, et qui ei adhaeserunt omnes corruerunt in
<!-- page 161 -->
foveam, secundum illud Isaiae decimo quarto[^p161-1]: « Quomodo cecidisti, lucifer, qui mane oriebaris? Corruisti in terram, qui vulnerabas gentes »?

94. (Vers. 40.). *Detestandum* etiam est *superbiae vitium,* quod adiacet volenti praesidere; propter quod subdit: *Non est discipulus supra*[^p161-2] *magistrum,* scilicet secundum rectum ordinem. Qui ergo, magistro subiacente, cupit praeesse, magistrum suum contendit praecedere. Unde Bernardus: « Quoties hominibus praeesse desidero, toties Deum meum praeire contendo, et tunc non sapio quae Dei sunt ». Et hoc est quod dicit Dominus discipulis Ioannis decimo tertio[^p161-3]: « Amen dico vobis, non est maior servus domino suo, neque apostolus maior eo qui misit eum ». Qui ergo vult aliis superefferri ignorat doctrinam Christi; Matthaei undecimo: « Discite a me, quia mitis sum et humilis corde »; aut si aliquando novit, in apostasiam cadit, iuxta illud quod dicit Gregorius[^p161-4]: « Quoties pastor praeesse aliis delectatur, toties in apostasiae crimen dilabitur ». Desinit autem esse discipulus, dum vult dominari et magister aliorum vocari, contra illud Matthaei vigesimo tertio: « Nec vocemini magistri, quia magister vester unus est, Christus ».

95. *Imitandum* est *vitae perfectae exemplum,* quod consistit in volendo subiacere; propter quod dicit: *Perfectus autem omnis erit, si sit sicut magister eius,* Christus scilicet, qui « non venit ministrari, sed ministrare et dare animam suam redemptionem pro multis », Matthaei vigesimo[^p161-5]. Et ideo dicitur infra vigesimo secundo: « Qui maior est in vobis fiat sicut minor, et qui praecessor, sicut ministrator ». Et ratio redditur: « Ego autem in medio vestrum sum, sicut qui ministrat ». Et hoc ipse ostendit eis per exemplum, Ioannis decimo tertio[^p161-6]: « Postquam lavit pedes », subditur: « Scitis, quid fecerim vobis? Vos vocatis me *magister* et *domine;* et bene dicitis, sum etenim. Si ergo ego lavi pedes vestros » etc. Et post subditur: « Exemplum enim dedi vobis, ut quemadmodum ego feci vobis, ita et vos faciatis ». Et ad hoc perfectum exemplum invitat Apostolus ad Philippenses secundo: « Nihil, inquit, per contentionem neque per inanem gloriam operantes, sed in humilitate superiores sibi invicem arbitrantes ». « Hoc enim sentite in vobis, quod et in Christo Iesu », de quo subdit, quod, « humiliavit semetipsum » etc.

96. (Vers. 41.). *Quid autem vides festucam* etc. Hic *revocat a temeritate iudicandi,* quod quidem
facit ostendendo eum qui temere iudicat, *iniquum* in cogitatione, *indiscretum* in locutione, *inordinatum* in operatione.

Ostendit igitur primo temere iudicantem *iniquum in cogitatione,* quia non iudicat *aequaliter;* propter quod dicit: *Quid autem vides festucam in oculo fratris tui?* tu scilicet, qui iudicas. *Festucam* vocat leve peccatum, quod quis reprehendit in alio, magnum negligens in se ipso; quantum ad quod addit: *Trabem autem, quae in oculo tuo est, non consideras; trabem,* id est magnum peccatum; ex quo colligitur, quod sic iudicantis iniquum est iudicium, quia, sicut dicitur Proverbiorum vigesimo[^p161-7], « abominatio est apud Deum pondus et pondus ». Non habet *aequale pondus* qui magis ponderat culpam in alio quam in se; et talis dicitur *abominabilis,* quia dupliciter peccat: et se ipsum, qui est superbus et impius, *iustificando,* et suum proximum *condemnando;* unde Proverbiorum decimo septimo[^p161-8]: « Et qui iustificat impium, et qui condemnat iustum, uterque est abominabilis apud Deum ». Et sanum est consilium, aliis indulgere et semetipsum considerare et diiudicare, secundum illud ad Galatas sexto: « Si praeoccupatus fuerit homo in aliquo delicto, vos, qui spirituales estis, instruite huiusmodi in spiritu lenitatis, considerans te ipsum, ne et tu tenteris » etc. « Nam qui putat, se aliquid esse, cum nihil sit, ipse se seducit ». Et ideo talis *trabem habet in oculo,* id est superbiam excaecantem, sicut Balaam, Numerorum vigesimo quarto[^p161-9]: « Dixit homo, cuius obturatus est oculus »; cuius obturatio est valde gravis in iudicando iniquum; Ecclesiastici trigesimo primo: « Nequius oculo quid creatum est »? Et merito, quia, sicut dicitur infra undecimo, « si oculus tuus nequam fuerit, totum corpus tuum tenebrosum erit ».

97. (Vers. 42.). Secundo ostendit, *indiscretum* esse *in locutione,* quia non loquitur *rationabiliter;* propter quod subdit: *Aut quomodo potes dicere fratri tuo,* exterius scilicet alloquendo: *Frater, sine, eiiciam festucam de oculo tuo,* in reprehendendo et corrigendo; *ipse autem*[^p161-10] *in oculo tuo trabem non videns?* peccatum gravissimum in te non attendendo, quasi dicat: non potes hoc rationabiliter dicere. Poterit enim tibi dici: Quid alium iudicas, te ipsum non iudicas? « Qui alium doces te ipsum non doces »? ad Romanos secundo; et illud supra quarto: « Medice, cura te ipsum ». Unde talis admonitio potius merito despicitur, quam auditur,
<!-- page 162 -->
secundum illud Gregorii[^p162-1]: « Cuius vita despicitur, restat, ut eius praedicatio contemnatur », quia:

« Turpē est doctori, dum culpa redarguit ipsum ».

Chrysostomus[^p162-2]: « Quare in alienis vides tam parva peccata, et in te tam magna transcurris »? Ecclesiastici trigesimo quarto: « Ab immundo quis mundabitur, et a mendace quid verum dicetur »? Et ideo in Psalmo: « Peccatori autem dixit Deus: Quare tu enarras iustitias meas »?

98. Tertio vero ostendit *inordinatum in operatione,* quia postponit illud, ad quod tenetur principaliter; propter quod dicit: *Hypocrita, eiice primum trabem de oculo tuo. Hypocritam* vocat talem iudicem temerarium, quia in *iudicando alios* ostendit et praetendit zelum iustitiae, et *se ipsum non iudicando* habet vitium intus latens. Unde Gregorius[^p162-3]: « Hypocrita ceteros reprehendit, ut in aliorum comparatione ipse sanctior videatur ». Vel *hypocritam* vocat, quia, exteriora ponderans, interiora negligit, quae sunt magis ponderanda, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p162-4]: « Vae vobis, scribae et Pharisaei! hypocritae, qui mundatis quod deforis est calicis et paropsidis, intus autem pleni estis rapina et immunditia. Pharisaee caece! munda prius quod intus est ». Et ideo recte nunc dicit: *Eiice primum,* quia circa hoc principaliter insistendum est, ut trabes, id est grave peccatum, de interiori oculo eiiciatur; Iacobi quarto[^p162-5]: « Purificate corda, duplices animo »; quoniam, sicut dicitur Ecclesiastici decimo quarto, « qui sibi nequam est, cui alii bonus erit »? Debet enim caritas esse ordinata, ut primo intendat sibi et post alii, secundum illud primae ad Timotheum quarto: « Attende tibi et doctrinae. Hoc enim faciens, et te ipsum salvum facies et eos qui tecum sunt ». Unde subditur: *Et tunc perspicies,* id est, perfecte aspicies, oculo iam mundato, *ut educas festucam de oculo fratris tui;* Iob vigesimo sexto[^p162-6]: « Obstetricante manu, eductus est coluber tortuosus »; unde Glossa: « Non est doctor prius idoneus alii mederi, qui nondum se sanavit ». Unde in omnibus talibus est *ordo* praecipue attendendus; stultus enim est et hypocrita, qui sollicitius curat aliena facta quam propria. Exemplum de Collationibus patrum[^p162-7], de eo qui posuit arenam in sacco, quem superposuit humero, habens posterius multum et anterius modicum; et interpretatus est, illud *multum* esse peccata propria, et illud *modicum* esse peccata aliena, sicut facit hypocrita.

99. (Vers. 43.). *Non est enim arbor bona* etc. Postquam retraxit a temeritate iudicandi, hic *informat ad prudentiam discernendi,* quod quidem facit hoc ordine. Primo enim proponit *similitudinem metaphoricam;* secundo vero elicit *eruditionem explicitam;* tertio vero ex hoc redarguit *simulationem deceptoriam.*

Proponitur ergo *metaphorica similitudo,* cum dicitur: *Non est enim arbor bona, quae facit fructus malos. Enim* non est hic causale, sed continuativum; *arborem* vocat non voluntatem, quae est a Deo; sic est bona, quia a Deo; sed voluntatem, quae non est bona, cum facit malum; nec etiam est mala, cum facit bonum; quantum ad quod addit: *Neque arbor mala faciens fructum bonum.* Conformis est enim voluntas operi, sicut arbor fructui; Proverbiorum duodecimo[^p162-8]: « De fructu oris sui unusquisque replebitur bonis, et iuxta opera manuum suarum retribuetur ei »; quia, sicut dicitur supra tertio, « omnis arbor, quae non facit fructum bonum, excidetur et in ignem mittetur ». Unde *spiritualiter* arbores fructibus privantur per gelicidium iniquitatis, secundum illud Iudae in Canonica sua[^p162-9] de malis: « Arbores sunt autumnales, infructuosae, bis mortuae ». Tunc autem fructibus replentur, cum appropinquant calori caritatis, secundum illud infra vigesimo primo: « Videte ficulneam et omnes arbores; cum producunt ex se fructum, scitis, quia prope est aestas ». Unde prudens considerator arbores discernit per fructum.
<!-- page 163 -->

100. (Vers. 44.). Et propterea adiungit: *Unaquaeque enim arbor ex*[^p163-1] *fructu suo cognoscitur.* Et hoc est quod dicitur Matthaei septimo: « A fructibus eorum cognoscetis eos », quia, sicut dicitur ad Galatas quinto, « manifesta sunt opera carnis, quae sunt fornicatio, immunditia, impudicitia, luxuria, idolorum servitus etc. Fructus autem spiritus est caritas, gaudium, pax » etc.; unde de viro iusto in Psalmo[^p163-2] dicitur: « Erit tanquam lignum, quod plantatum est secus decursus aquarum, quod fructum suum dabit ». Nec mirum, quia, Sapientiae tertio, « bonorum laborum gloriosus est fructus », sed malorum nullus; ad Romanos sexto: « Quem fructum habuistis, in quibus nunc erubescitis »? Et ideo dicitur de avaro Ecclesiastae quinto: « Qui amat divitias fructum non capiet ex eis ».

101. Et quod arbor discernatur per fructum, probatur per sensibile experimentum, cum subditur: *Neque enim de spinis colligunt ficus, neque de rubo vindemiant uvam.* Duos ponit fructus praecipue laudabiles, scilicet *ficus* et *uvas;* unde Iudicum nono[^p163-3] introducitur ficus et vitis per prosopopoeam loquens; ficus sic: « Nunquid possum deserere dulcedinem meam fructusque suavissimos »? Et vitis sic: « Nunquid possum deserere vinum meum, quod laetificat Deum et homines »? Duo etiam arbusta ponit pessima, scilicet *rubum* et *spinam,* quae gignit terra propter peccata humana, secundum illud Genesis tertio[^p163-4]: « Maledicta terra in opere tuo; spinas et tribulos germinabit tibi ».

102. Sed *spiritualiter* per *ficum* dulcem intelligitur *dulcedo pietatis,* per *spinas* homines *iracundi* et *schismatici,* qui non possunt aliis compati; secundi Regum vigesimo tertio[^p163-5]: « Praevaricatores quasi spinae avellentur universi; et si quis eas tangere voluerit, armabitur ferro et ligno lanceato ». — Per *uvam* intelligitur *perspicua cognitio veritatis,* et per *rubos* homines *haeretici,* a quibus detinentur superbi, secundum illud Genesis vigesimo secundo[^p163-6]: « Levavit Abraham oculos suos viditque inter vepres arietem haerentem cornibus ». — Ista duo genera hominum dissipant vineam Domini exercituum, scilicet schismatici et haeretici; Isaiae trigesimo secundo[^p163-7]: « Super humum populi mei ascendent spinae et vepres »; Isaiae quinto: « Exspectavi, ut faceret uvas, fecit autem labruscas »; et post: « Ascendent super eam vepres et spinae ».

103. (Vers. 45.). Secundo autem elicitur *expressa eruditio,* cum adiungitur: *Bonus homo de bono*
*thesauro cordis sui profert bona*[^p163-8], ad similitudinem scilicet arboris bonae. *Bonum thesaurum* vocat habitum interioris perfectionis, sive in *affectu,* quantum ad quem dicitur Proverbiorum vigesimo primo[^p163-9]: « Thesaurus desiderabilis et oleum in habitaculo iusti »; sive in *intellectu,* quantum ad quem Matthaei decimo tertio: « Omnis scriba doctus in regno caelorum similis est homini patrifamilias, qui profert de thesauro suo nova et vetera ». De utroque bono thesauro proferuntur tam opera quam etiam verba; unde Iacobi tertio: « Quis sapiens et disciplinatus est inter vos? Ostendat ex bona conversatione operationem suam in mansuetudine sapientiae ».

104. Et sicut bonus homo bonae arbori comparatur, ita et malus malae; unde et addit: *Et malus homo de malo thesauro profert mala*[^p163-10]. *Thesaurum malum* vocat malam voluntatem et malam conscientiam; Michaeae sexto: « Adhuc ignis in domo impii et thesauri iniquitatis ». Et certe de malo corde procedunt mala *opera* et immunda *verba;* unde Marci septimo[^p163-11]: « De corde hominum procedunt adulteria, fornicationes, homicidia, furta, avaritiae, nequitiae, dolus, impudicitiae; omnia haec mala ab intus procedunt et coinquinant ». Similiter et mala *verba,* secundum illud Proverbiorum decimo quarto: « Testis fidelis non mentietur; profert autem mendacium testis dolosus ». — Et rationem huius ipse reddit, cum subiungit: *Ex abundantia enim cordis os loquitur, bonum* scilicet verbum, aut *malum.* De *bono* Ecclesiastici sexto[^p163-12]: « Lingua eucharis in bono homine abundabit »; et de *malo* Proverbiorum decimo quinto: « Mens iusti meditabitur sapientiam, os impiorum redundat malis »; et in eodem: « Os fatuorum ebullit stultitiam ».

105. (Vers. 46.). Tertio vero redarguitur *deceptoria simulatio,* cum subiungitur: *Quid autem vocatis me: Domine*[^p163-13]? scilicet confessione oris, *et non facitis quae dico,* omissione boni operis. Ostensio enim reverentiae in ore falsa est, nisi adsit pariter exhibitio obedientiae in opere; unde de talibus Isaiae vigesimo nono[^p163-14]: « Appropinquat populus iste ore suo et labiis suis glorificat me, cor autem eius longe est a me »; et Matthaei decimo quinto: « Populus hic labiis me honorat, cor autem eorum longe est a me »; et ad Titum primo: « Confitentur, se nosse Deum, factis autem negant ». Et ita est in eis repugnantia, quia verbum indicat bonitatem in corde, et factum ostendit malignitatem in opere. Tales non decipiunt Dominum, sed fallunt semetipsos, secundum
<!-- page 164 -->
illud Iacobi primo[^p164-1]: « Estote factores verbi et non auditores tantum, fallentes vosmetipsos »; unde Matthaei septimo: « Non omnis, qui dicit mihi: Domine, Domine, intrabit in regnum caelorum ».

106. (Vers. 47.). *Omnis, qui venit ad me* etc. Postquam informavit ad prudentiam discernendi, hic invitat ad *diligentiam operandi,* et hoc sub duplici metaphora, in quarum prima *commendat obedientes;* in secunda vero *vituperat negligentes,* ibi[^p164-2]: *Qui autem audit et non facit* etc. — Circa *commendationem obedientis* primo praemittit *obedientis actum;* secundo, *modum;* tertio, *fructum.*

Quantum ad *actum* igitur *vere obedientis* dicit: *Omnis, qui venit ad me,* scilicet fideliter credendo, *et audit sermones meos,* devote intelligendo, *et facit eos,* scilicet opere complendo; *ostendam vobis, cui sit similis.* Ex quo colligitur, quod vere obedientis est primo *venire* per fidem, *credendo fideliter;* de quo adventu Ioannis sexto[^p164-3]: « Nemo venit ad me, nisi Pater, qui misit me, traxerit eum ». Sic veniunt parvuli, secundum illud Matthaei decimo nono: « Sinite, parvulos venire ad me ». — Deinde *audire, intelligendo vivaciter;* Ecclesiastici tertio[^p164-4]: « Auris bona cum omni concupiscentia audiet sapientiam »; Iacobi primo: « Sit omnis homo velox ad audiendum » etc. — Est tertio vere obedientis *implere efficaciter;* unde Deuteronomii trigesimo[^p164-5]: « Iuxta te est sermo valde in ore tuo et in corde tuo, ut facias illum ». Alioquin non iustificaberis apud Dominum, quia, ut dicitur ad Romanos secundo, « factores legis iustificabuntur ».

107. (Vers. 48.). Quantum ad *vere obediendi modum* adiungit documentum similitudinarium de aedificante domum, cum subdit: *Similis est homini aedificanti domum,* per *profectum virtutis;* Proverbiorum vigesimo quarto[^p164-6]: « Diligenter exerce agrum tuum, ut postea aedifices domum tuam ». Haec quidem *domus* est conscientia bona; Sapientiae octavo: « Intrans in domum meam, conquiescam cum illa »; Proverbiorum decimo quarto: « Sapiens mulier aedificat domum suam, insipiens exstructam quoque manibus destruit ». — *Qui fodit in altum,* per *descensum humilitatis,* secundum praeceptum Elisei quarti Regum tertio[^p164-7]: « Facite alveum torrentis huius fossas et fossas », scilicet per humilitatem in intellectu pariter et affectu. Sed huic respondet superbus illud mali villici, infra decimo sexto: « Fo-
dere non valeo, mendicare erubesco ». Quocontra Isaiae secundo: « Ingredere in petram, et abscondere in fossa humo a facie timoris Domini » etc. Ille autem altius fodit, qui magis per humilitatem descendit, et huius aedificium altius ascendit, quia « qui se humiliat exaltabitur », infra decimo octavo[^p164-8]. — *Et posuit fundamentum supra petram,* per *stabilimentum caritatis,* secundum illud ad Ephesios tertio[^p164-9]: « In caritate radicati et fundati ». Haec caritas fundat super Christum, qui est petra solida, secundum illud Isaiae vigesimo octavo: « Ecce, ego mittam in fundamentis Sion lapidem, lapidem probatum, angularem, pretiosum, in fundamento fundatum ». Super hunc fundat caritas, dum facit implere eius mandata, secundum illud Ioannis decimo quarto[^p164-10]: « Qui habet mandata mea et servat ea, ille est qui diligit me ». Et hic est in Christo fundatus; Ecclesiastici vigesimo sexto: « Fundamenta aeterna super petram solidam, mandata Dei in corde mulieris sanctae ». Ambrosius[^p164-11]: « Omnium virtutum fundamentum est caelestium obedientia mandatorum ». — Hic est ergo modus vere obediendi divinis praeceptis, ut sit *sedulitas in profectu virtutis,* sit *profunditas in descensu humilitatis,* sit *firmitas in affectu caritatis.*

108. Quantum ad *verae obedientiae fructum* subiungit: *Inundatione autem facta* etc. Observatio namque divinorum mandatorum firmat contra impulsum tentationum, quam designat per *inundationem* quantum ad tentationem *exteriorem* a daemonibus, secundum illud Isaiae octavo[^p164-12]: « Adducet Dominus aquas fluminis fortes et multas, regem Assyriorum »; et infra: « Et ibit per Iudam transiens et inundans usque ad collum »; hic aperte designat diabolum; et Iob vigesimo primo: « Quoties lucerna impiorum exstinguetur, et superveniet eis inundatio »? — Designat etiam tentationem per *illisionem fluminis,* cum addit: *Illisum est flumen domui illi,* quantum scilicet ad tentationem *interiorem,* scilicet timoris, vel doloris, vel laetitiae, vel amoris; Nahum secundo[^p164-13]: « Portae fluviorum apertae sunt »; quod quidem fit, quando ingrediuntur tentationes interius, ut possit clamare illud Psalmi: « Salvum me fac, Deus, quoniam intraverunt aquae usque ad animam meam ». — Sed hae tentationes non praevalent contra divinorum mandatorum observatores; propterea subditur: *Et non potuit eam movere;* Proverbiorum decimo[^p164-14]: « Quasi tempestas transiens non erit impius,
<!-- page 165 -->
iustus autem quasi fundamentum sempiternum »; in Psalmo: « Perfice gressus meos in semitis tuis, ut non moveantur vestigia mea ». — Huius autem stabilitatis ratio redditur, cum adiungitur: *Fundata enim erat supra firmam petram*[^p165-1]. Hinc dicitur de iusto Isaiae vigesimo secundo: « Figam illum paxillum in loco fideli ». Haec quidem *fixio* est per caritatem; ad Romanos octavo: « Quis nos separabit a caritate Christi? Tribulatio? an angustia? an fames? an nuditas? an periculum? an persecutio? an gladius »? — Verae igitur obedientiae fructus est victoria tentationis exterioris et interioris per virtutem stabilis fundamenti.

109. (Vers. 49.). *Qui autem audit et non facit.* Hic post commendationem vere obedientis subiungit *vituperium negligentis,* praemittens *negligentis vitium,* addens *periculum* et subiungens[^p165-2] *praecipitium.*

Praemittit igitur *negligentis vitium,* cum dicit: *Qui autem audit et non facit,* sicut Balaam, Numerorum vigesimo quarto[^p165-3]: « Dixit auditor sermonum Dei, qui novit doctrinam Altissimi ». — Hic reprehensibilis est, quod ostendit per simile, cum subdit: *Similis est homini aedificanti domum suam supra*[^p165-4] *terram sine fundamento. Terram* vocat locum inconstantem et facile mobilem, secundum illud Psalmi: « Non sic impii, non sic, sed tanquam pulvis, quem proiicit ventus a facie terrae »; Isaiae vigesimo quarto: « Confractione confringetur terra, contritione conteretur terra, et auferetur sicut tabernaculum unius noctis »; Iob quarto: « Quanto magis hi qui habitant domos luteas, qui terrenum habent fundamentum, consumuntur velut a tinea »! Et hoc est, quia non sunt « in caritate radicati[^p165-5] », sed in terrena cupiditate; Sapientiae quarto: « Adulterinae plantationes non dabunt radices ».

110. Adiungit *negligentis periculum* in illisione fluminum, cum adiungit: *In quam illisus est fluvius,* id est tentatio impellens, ex qua imminet periculum ruinae; Ezechielis decimo tertio[^p165-6]: « Dic ad eos qui liniunt parietem absque temperamento, quod casurus sit paries. Erit enim imber inundans, et dabo lapides praegrandes desuper irruentes et ventum procellae dissipantem ». De talibus Isaiae vigesimo octavo: « Flagellum inundans cum transierit, eritis ei in conculcationem; quandocumque pertransierit, tollet vos ». Hi enim sunt, de quibus dicitur infra octavo[^p165-7]: « Ad tempus credunt et in tempore tentationis recedunt »; et hi sunt « qui non habent radices ».

111. Subiungit postremo *negligentis praecipitium,* cum ait: *Et continuo cecidit, et facta est ruina domus illius magna;* Zachariae decimo quarto[^p165-8]: « Erit ruina equi et muli, cameli et asini et omnium iumentorum », id est omnium hominum animalium et negligentium et impiorum; Sophoniae primo: « Haec dicit Dominus: Congregans hominem et pecus, congregans volatilia caeli et pisces maris; et erunt ruinae impiorum, et disperdam homines a facie terrae ». Nec mirum, quia, Proverbiorum quarto[^p165-9], « via impiorum tenebrosa; nesciunt, ubi corruant ». — Ex periculo igitur et vituperatione *negligentium* et commendatione *obedientium* sufficienter invitatur ad *diligentiam agendorum,* et in hoc documenta divina habent terminum.

## English

92. *And he spoke also to them a similitude.* After the precepts proper and express, the Evangelist here subjoins the Lord's lessons *by way of likeness* and transferred, in which the Lord forms us in four ways. For first *he draws us back from the vanity of presiding.* Secondly *he calls us back from the rashness of judging,* there[^p160-7]: *And why seest thou the mote* etc. Thirdly *he forms us unto prudence in discerning,* there: *For there is no good tree* etc. Fourthly *he invites us to diligence in working,* there: *Every one that cometh to me* etc.

First, then, *he draws us back from the vanity of presiding* by a threefold consideration, namely by the consideration of *the peril to be shunned* and *the vice to be detested* and *the example to be imitated.*

93. (Vers. 39.). For *to be shunned* is *the peril of ruin,* which hangs over one who presides proudly; on account of which he says: *Can the blind lead the blind?* as if to say: he cannot, surely, on account of the peril of ruin, which he adds: *Do they not both fall into the ditch?* But the presumptuous man is blind, according to that of the Apocalypse, the third chapter[^p160-8]: « Thou sayest: I am rich, and made wealthy, and have need of nothing; and knowest not that thou art wretched, and miserable, and poor, and blind, and naked ». Of such a one, to the Romans, the second chapter: « Thou art confident that thou thyself art a guide of the blind, a light of them that are in darkness, an instructor of the foolish ». « Thou therefore that teachest others, teachest not thyself »; as if to say: thou art blind. — And therefore he who seeks and presumes to be preferred to others, since he is blind, plunges himself and others into the precipice; whence Jeremias, the fiftieth chapter[^p160-9]: « My people hath become a lost flock, their shepherds have caused them to go astray ». And the reason of this is rendered in Jeremias, the tenth chapter: « The pastors have done foolishly, and have not sought the Lord: therefore have they not understood, and all their flock is scattered ». And therefore he subjoins: *Both fall into the ditch.* And this is what Gregory says[^p160-10]: « While the pastor walks through the steep places of vices, it is necessary that the flock be drawn into the precipice ». And such men merit the Lord's curse, according to that of Deuteronomy, the twenty-seventh chapter: « Cursed be he that maketh the blind to wander »; as also Lucifer, who willed to be over others, and all who cleaved to him fell into the
<!-- page 161 -->
ditch, according to that of Isaias, the fourteenth chapter[^p161-1]: « How art thou fallen, O Lucifer, who didst rise in the morning? Thou art fallen to the earth, that didst wound the nations »?

94. (Vers. 40.). *To be detested,* too, is *the vice of pride,* which lies close to one who wills to preside; on account of which he subjoins: *The disciple is not above*[^p161-2] *his master,* namely according to right order. He, then, who, while subject to a master, desires to be over, strives to go before his master. Whence Bernard: « As often as I desire to be over men, so often do I strive to go before my God, and then I savour not the things that are of God ». And this is what the Lord says to the disciples in John, the thirteenth chapter[^p161-3]: « Amen I say to you, the servant is not greater than his lord; neither is an apostle greater than he that sent him ». He, then, who wills to be lifted up above others is ignorant of the doctrine of Christ; Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « Learn of me, because I am meek, and humble of heart »; or if he once knew it, he falls into apostasy, according to that which Gregory says[^p161-4]: « As often as a pastor delights to be over others, so often does he slip into the crime of apostasy ». And he ceases to be a disciple when he wills to lord it and to be called the master of others, against that of Matthew, the twenty-third chapter: « Neither be ye called masters, for one is your master, Christ ».

95. *To be imitated* is *the example of the perfect life,* which consists in willing to be subject; on account of which he says: *But every one shall be perfect, if he be as his master,* namely Christ, who « is not come to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a redemption for many », Matthew, the twentieth chapter[^p161-5]. And therefore it is said below, in the twenty-second chapter: « He that is the greater among you, let him become as the lesser, and he that is the leader, as he that ministereth ». And the reason is rendered: « But I am in the midst of you, as he that ministereth ». And this he himself showed them by example, John, the thirteenth chapter[^p161-6]: « After he had washed their feet », it is subjoined: « Know you what I have done to you? You call me *Master* and *Lord;* and you say well, for so I am. If then I have washed your feet » etc. And after it is subjoined: « For I have given you an example, that as I have done to you, so you do also ». And to this perfect example the Apostle invites in Philippians, the second chapter: « Doing nothing, he says, through contention, neither by vain glory, but in humility each esteeming others better than themselves ». « For let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus », of whom he subjoins that « he humbled himself » etc.

96. (Vers. 41.). *And why seest thou the mote* etc. Here *he calls us back from the rashness of judging,* which he does indeed by showing him who judges rashly to be *iniquitous* in thought, *indiscreet* in speech, *inordinate* in operation.

He shows, then, first that one who judges rashly is *iniquitous in thought,* because he does not judge *equally;* on account of which he says: *And why seest thou the mote in thy brother's eye?* thou, namely, who judgest. *Mote* he calls a light sin, which one reprehends in another, neglecting a great one in himself; as to which he adds: *But the beam that is in thy own eye thou considerest not; beam,* that is, a great sin; from which it is gathered that the judgement of one who judges so is iniquitous, because, as is said in Proverbs, the twentieth chapter[^p161-7], « weight and weight is an abomination before God ». He has not *an equal weight* who weighs a fault more in another than in himself; and such a one is called *abominable,* because he sins doubly: both by *justifying* himself, who is proud and wicked, and by *condemning* his neighbour; whence Proverbs, the seventeenth chapter[^p161-8]: « Both he that justifieth the wicked, and he that condemneth the just, both are abominable before God ». And sound is the counsel, to be indulgent to others and to consider and adjudge oneself, according to that to the Galatians, the sixth chapter: « If a man be overtaken in any fault, you, who are spiritual, instruct such a one in the spirit of meekness, considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted » etc. « For he that thinketh himself to be something, whereas he is nothing, deceiveth himself ». And therefore such a one *has a beam in his eye,* that is, a blinding pride, like Balaam, Numbers, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p161-9]: « The man hath said, whose eye is stopped up »; whose stopping up is very grievous in judging iniquitously; Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-first chapter: « What is created more wicked than an eye »? And deservedly, because, as is said below, in the eleventh chapter, « if thy eye be evil, thy whole body will be darksome ».

97. (Vers. 42.). Secondly he shows that he is *indiscreet in speech,* because he does not speak *reasonably;* on account of which he subjoins: *Or how canst thou say to thy brother,* namely by addressing him outwardly: *Brother, let me cast out the mote from thy eye,* in reprehending and correcting; *thou thyself*[^p161-10] *not seeing the beam in thy eye?* by not attending to a most grievous sin in thyself, as if to say: thou canst not say this reasonably. For it could be said to thee: Why dost thou judge another, and judgest not thyself? « Thou that teachest another, teachest not thyself »? to the Romans, the second chapter; and that above, in the fourth chapter: « Physician, heal thyself ». Whence such an admonition is deservedly despised rather than heard,
<!-- page 162 -->
according to that of Gregory[^p162-1]: « Whose life is despised, it remains that his preaching be contemned », because:

« It is shameful for a doctor, when a fault convicts himself ».

Chrysostom[^p162-2]: « Why in others dost thou see sins so small, and in thyself passest over sins so great »? Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-fourth chapter: « By the unclean who shall be made clean, and by a liar what truth shall be spoken »? And therefore in the Psalm: « But to the sinner God hath said: Why dost thou declare my justices »?

98. And thirdly he shows that he is *inordinate in operation,* because he puts after that to which he is bound principally; on account of which he says: *Hypocrite, cast out first the beam from thy eye. Hypocrite* he calls such a rash judge, because in *judging others* he shows and pretends a zeal for justice, and in *not judging himself* he has a vice lying hidden within. Whence Gregory[^p162-3]: « The hypocrite reprehends the others, that in comparison with others he himself may seem holier ». Or he calls him *hypocrite* because, weighing the exterior things, he neglects the interior, which are more to be weighed, according to that of Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p162-4]: « Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees! hypocrites, who make clean that which is outside of the cup and of the dish, but within you are full of rapine and uncleanness. Thou blind Pharisee! first make clean that which is within ». And therefore he rightly says now: *Cast out first,* because about this one must insist principally, that the beam, that is, the grave sin, be cast out from the interior eye; James, the fourth chapter[^p162-5]: « Purify your hearts, ye double minded »; since, as is said in Ecclesiasticus, the fourteenth chapter, « he that is evil to himself, to whom will he be good »? For charity ought to be ordered, so that it attends first to itself and afterwards to another, according to that of the first to Timothy, the fourth chapter: « Take heed to thyself and to doctrine. For in doing this thou shalt both save thyself and those who are with thee ». Whence it is subjoined: *And then thou shalt see clearly,* that is, thou shalt look perfectly, the eye now made clean, *to bring out the mote from thy brother's eye;* Job, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p162-6]: « By the midwifing hand the winding serpent was brought out »; whence the Gloss: « A doctor is not fit to heal another first, who has not yet healed himself ». Whence in all such things *order* is chiefly to be attended to; for he is foolish and a hypocrite who cares more anxiously for another's deeds than his own. An example from the Collations of the Fathers[^p162-7], of him who put sand in a sack, which he placed upon his shoulder, having much behind and a little in front; and he interpreted that *much* to be his own sins, and that *little* to be another's sins, as the hypocrite does.

99. (Vers. 43.). *For there is no good tree* etc. Having drawn us back from the rashness of judging, he here *forms us unto prudence in discerning,* which indeed he does in this order. For first he proposes *a metaphorical likeness;* secondly he draws out *an explicit erudition;* thirdly from this he convicts *a deceptive simulation.*

The *metaphorical likeness,* then, is proposed when it is said: *For there is no good tree which maketh evil fruits. For* is not causal here, but continuative; *tree* he calls not the will, which is from God; so it is good, because from God; but the will, which is not good when it does evil; nor again is it evil when it does good; as to which he adds: *Nor an evil tree making good fruit.* For the will is conformed to the work, as the tree to the fruit; Proverbs, the twelfth chapter[^p162-8]: « Of the fruit of his own mouth every one shall be filled with good things, and according to the works of his hands it shall be repaid him »; because, as is said above, in the third chapter, « every tree that bringeth not forth good fruit shall be cut down and cast into the fire ». Whence *spiritually* trees are deprived of fruits through the frost of iniquity, according to that of Jude in his Canonical Epistle[^p162-9], concerning the wicked: « Trees of the autumn, unfruitful, twice dead ». But they are filled with fruits when they draw near to the warmth of charity, according to that below, in the twenty-first chapter: « See the fig tree, and all the trees; when they bring forth fruit from themselves, you know that summer is nigh ». Whence the prudent considerer discerns trees by their fruit.
<!-- page 163 -->

100. (Vers. 44.). And therefore he adds: *For every tree is known by*[^p163-1] *its fruit.* And this is what is said in Matthew, the seventh chapter: « By their fruits you shall know them », because, as is said to the Galatians, the fifth chapter, « the works of the flesh are manifest, which are fornication, uncleanness, immodesty, luxury, the service of idols etc. But the fruit of the Spirit is charity, joy, peace » etc.; whence of the just man it is said in the Psalm[^p163-2]: « He shall be like a tree which is planted near the running waters, which shall bring forth its fruit ». Nor is it a wonder, because, Wisdom, the third chapter, « the fruit of good labours is glorious », but of evil ones there is none; to the Romans, the sixth chapter: « What fruit had you in those things of which you are now ashamed »? And therefore it is said of the avaricious man in Ecclesiastes, the fifth chapter: « He that loveth riches shall reap no fruit from them ».

101. And that a tree is discerned by its fruit is proved by sensible experience, when it is subjoined: *For men do not gather figs from thorns, nor from a bramble do they gather the grape.* He sets down two fruits especially praiseworthy, namely *figs* and *grapes;* whence in Judges, the ninth chapter[^p163-3], the fig tree and the vine are brought in speaking by prosopopoeia; the fig tree thus: « Can I leave my sweetness and my delicious fruits »? And the vine thus: « Can I forsake my wine, that cheereth God and men »? He sets down also two most evil shrubs, namely *the bramble* and *the thorn,* which the earth brings forth on account of human sins, according to that of Genesis, the third chapter[^p163-4]: « Cursed is the earth in thy work; thorns and thistles shall it bring forth to thee ».

102. But *spiritually* by the sweet *fig* is understood *the sweetness of piety;* by the *thorns,* men *wrathful* and *schismatic,* who cannot have compassion on others; the second of Kings, the twenty-third chapter[^p163-5]: « Transgressors shall all of them be plucked up as thorns; and if any one will touch them, he shall be armed with iron and with the staff of a lance ». — By the *grape* is understood *the clear cognition of truth,* and by the *brambles,* men *heretical,* by whom the proud are held fast, according to that of Genesis, the twenty-second chapter[^p163-6]: « Abraham lifted up his eyes, and saw among the briers a ram sticking fast by the horns ». — These two kinds of men lay waste the vineyard of the Lord of hosts, namely schismatics and heretics; Isaias, the thirty-second chapter[^p163-7]: « Upon the land of my people shall come up thorns and briers »; Isaias, the fifth chapter: « I looked that it should bring forth grapes, and it brought forth wild grapes »; and after: « Briers and thorns shall come up upon it ».

103. (Vers. 45.). And secondly *an express erudition* is drawn out, when it is added: *A good man out of the good*
*treasure of his heart bringeth forth good things*[^p163-8], namely after the likeness of the good tree. *Good treasure* he calls the habit of interior perfection, whether in the *affection,* as to which it is said in Proverbs, the twenty-first chapter[^p163-9]: « There is a treasure to be desired, and oil in the dwelling of the just »; or in the *intellect,* as to which Matthew, the thirteenth chapter: « Every scribe instructed in the kingdom of heaven is like to a man that is a householder, who bringeth forth out of his treasure new things and old ». From each good treasure are brought forth works as well as words; whence James, the third chapter: « Who is wise and endued with knowledge among you? Let him shew, by a good conversation, his work in the meekness of wisdom ».

104. And as the good man is compared to the good tree, so too the evil man to the evil; whence he also adds: *And an evil man out of the evil treasure bringeth forth evil things*[^p163-10]. *Evil treasure* he calls an evil will and an evil conscience; Micheas, the sixth chapter: « As yet there is a fire in the house of the wicked, and the treasures of iniquity ». And certainly out of an evil heart proceed evil *works* and unclean *words;* whence Mark, the seventh chapter[^p163-11]: « From the heart of men proceed adulteries, fornications, murders, thefts, covetousness, wickedness, deceit, lasciviousness; all these evil things proceed from within and defile ». Likewise also evil *words,* according to that of Proverbs, the fourteenth chapter: « A faithful witness will not lie; but a deceitful witness uttereth a lie ». — And he himself renders the reason of this, when he subjoins: *For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh, good,* namely the good word, or *evil.* Of the *good,* Ecclesiasticus, the sixth chapter[^p163-12]: « A gracious tongue in a good man shall abound »; and of the *evil,* Proverbs, the fifteenth chapter: « The mind of the just shall meditate wisdom, the mouth of the wicked overfloweth with evils »; and in the same: « The mouth of fools bubbleth out folly ».

105. (Vers. 46.). And thirdly *the deceptive simulation* is convicted, when it is subjoined: *And why call you me: Lord*[^p163-13]? namely by the confession of the mouth, *and do not the things which I say,* by the omission of the good work. For the show of reverence in the mouth is false, unless there be present equally the exhibition of obedience in the work; whence of such men Isaias, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p163-14]: « This people draw near with their mouth, and with their lips glorify me, but their heart is far from me »; and Matthew, the fifteenth chapter: « This people honoureth me with their lips, but their heart is far from me »; and to Titus, the first chapter: « They confess that they know God, but in their deeds they deny him ». And so there is in them a repugnance, because the word indicates goodness in the heart, and the deed shows malignity in the work. Such men do not deceive the Lord, but beguile themselves, according to
<!-- page 164 -->
that of James, the first chapter[^p164-1]: « Be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves »; whence Matthew, the seventh chapter: « Not every one that saith to me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven ».

106. (Vers. 47.). *Every one that cometh to me* etc. Having formed us unto prudence in discerning, he here invites us to *diligence in working,* and this under a twofold metaphor, in the first of which *he commends the obedient;* but in the second *he vituperates the negligent,* there[^p164-2]: *But he that heareth and doth not* etc. — About *the commendation of the obedient man* he first sets down beforehand *the act of the obedient man;* secondly, *the mode;* thirdly, *the fruit.*

As to the *act,* then, of *the truly obedient man,* he says: *Every one that cometh to me,* namely by believing faithfully, *and heareth my words,* by devoutly understanding, *and doth them,* namely by fulfilling them in work; *I will shew you to whom he is like.* From which it is gathered that it belongs to the truly obedient man first *to come* through faith, *by believing faithfully;* of which coming John, the sixth chapter[^p164-3]: « No man can come to me, except the Father, who hath sent me, draw him ». So the little ones come, according to that of Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « Suffer the little children to come to me ». — Then *to hear, by understanding vividly;* Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter[^p164-4]: « A good ear will hear wisdom with all desire »; James, the first chapter: « Let every man be swift to hear » etc. — It belongs thirdly to the truly obedient man *to fulfil efficaciously;* whence Deuteronomy, the thirtieth chapter[^p164-5]: « The word is very nigh unto thee, in thy mouth and in thy heart, that thou mayst do it ». Otherwise thou shalt not be justified before the Lord, because, as is said to the Romans, the second chapter, « the doers of the law shall be justified ».

107. (Vers. 48.). As to *the mode of truly obeying* he adds an instruction by way of likeness concerning one who builds a house, when he subjoins: *He is like to a man building a house,* through *the progress of virtue;* Proverbs, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p164-6]: « Diligently till thy ground, that afterwards thou mayest build thy house ». This *house* indeed is a good conscience; Wisdom, the eighth chapter: « When I go into my house, I shall repose myself with her »; Proverbs, the fourteenth chapter: « A wise woman buildeth her house, but the foolish will pull down with her hands that also which is built ». — *Who digged deep,* through *the descent of humility,* according to the precept of Eliseus in the fourth of Kings, the third chapter[^p164-7]: « Make the channel of this torrent ditches and ditches », namely through humility in the intellect and in the affection alike. But to this the proud man answers that of the wicked steward, below, in the sixteenth chapter: « To dig I am not able; to beg I am ashamed ». Against which Isaias, the second chapter: « Enter thou into the rock, and hide thee in the pit, from the face of the fear of the Lord » etc. And he digs deeper who descends more through humility, and his building ascends higher, because « he that humbleth himself shall be exalted », below, in the eighteenth chapter[^p164-8]. — *And laid the foundation upon a rock,* through *the establishing of charity,* according to that to the Ephesians, the third chapter[^p164-9]: « Rooted and founded in charity ». This charity founds upon Christ, who is the solid rock, according to that of Isaias, the twenty-eighth chapter: « Behold, I will lay in the foundations of Sion a stone, a tried stone, a corner stone, a precious stone, founded in the foundation ». Upon him charity founds, while it makes one fulfil his commandments, according to that of John, the fourteenth chapter[^p164-10]: « He that hath my commandments and keepeth them, he it is that loveth me ». And this man is founded in Christ; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-sixth chapter: « Everlasting foundations upon a solid rock, the commandments of God in the heart of a holy woman ». Ambrose[^p164-11]: « The foundation of all the virtues is obedience to the heavenly commandments ». — This, then, is the mode of truly obeying the divine precepts: that there be *sedulousness in the progress of virtue,* that there be *depth in the descent of humility,* that there be *firmness in the affection of charity.*

108. As to *the fruit of true obedience* he subjoins: *And when an inundation was made* etc. For the observance of the divine commandments makes firm against the onset of temptations, which he designates by the *inundation* as to the *exterior* temptation from the demons, according to that of Isaias, the eighth chapter[^p164-12]: « The Lord will bring upon them the waters of the river strong and many, the king of the Assyrians »; and below: « And he shall go through Juda, passing and inundating, even to the neck »; here he openly designates the devil; and Job, the twenty-first chapter: « How often shall the lamp of the wicked be put out, and an inundation come upon them »? — He designates temptation also by *the dashing of the stream,* when he adds: *The stream dashed against that house,* namely as to the *interior* temptation, namely of fear, or of sorrow, or of gladness, or of love; Nahum, the second chapter[^p164-13]: « The gates of the rivers are opened »; which indeed happens when temptations enter within, so that one can cry out that of the Psalm: « Save me, O God, for the waters are come in even unto my soul ». — But these temptations do not prevail against the observers of the divine commandments; therefore it is subjoined: *And it could not move it;* Proverbs, the tenth chapter[^p164-14]: « As a tempest that passeth, the wicked shall not be,
<!-- page 165 -->
but the just as an everlasting foundation »; in the Psalm: « Perfect thou my goings in thy paths, that my footsteps be not moved ». — And the reason of this stability is rendered when it is added: *For it was founded upon a firm rock*[^p165-1]. Hence it is said of the just man in Isaias, the twenty-second chapter: « I will fasten him as a peg in a sure place ». This *fixing* indeed is through charity; to the Romans, the eighth chapter: « Who then shall separate us from the charity of Christ? Tribulation? or distress? or famine? or nakedness? or danger? or persecution? or the sword »? — The fruit, therefore, of true obedience is the victory over temptation exterior and interior through the power of a stable foundation.

109. (Vers. 49.). *But he that heareth and doth not.* Here, after the commendation of the truly obedient man, he subjoins *the vituperation of the negligent man,* setting down beforehand *the vice of the negligent man,* adding *the peril* and subjoining[^p165-2] *the precipice.*

He sets down beforehand, then, *the vice of the negligent man,* when he says: *But he that heareth and doth not,* like Balaam, Numbers, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p165-3]: « The hearer of the words of God hath said, who knoweth the doctrine of the Highest ». — This man is reprehensible, which he shows by a likeness, when he subjoins: *Is like to a man building his house upon*[^p165-4] *the earth without a foundation. Earth* he calls an inconstant and easily movable place, according to that of the Psalm: « Not so the wicked, not so, but like the dust, which the wind driveth from the face of the earth »; Isaias, the twenty-fourth chapter: « With breaking shall the earth be broken, with crushing shall the earth be crushed, and shall be removed as the tent of one night »; Job, the fourth chapter: « How much more they that dwell in houses of clay, who have an earthly foundation, are consumed as by the moth »! And this is because they are not « rooted in charity[^p165-5] », but in earthly cupidity; Wisdom, the fourth chapter: « Bastard slips shall not take roots ».

110. He adds *the peril of the negligent man* in the dashing of the rivers, when he adds: *Against which the stream dashed,* that is, the temptation driving on, from which the peril of ruin hangs over; Ezechiel, the thirteenth chapter[^p165-6]: « Say to them that daub the wall without tempering, that the wall shall fall. For there shall be an inundating shower, and I will send very great stones rushing down from above, and a stormy wind scattering ». Of such men Isaias, the twenty-eighth chapter: « When the inundating scourge shall pass, you shall be trodden down by it; whensoever it shall pass through, it shall take you away ». For these are they of whom it is said below, in the eighth chapter[^p165-7]: « For a while they believe, and in time of temptation they fall away »; and these are they « who have no roots ».

111. He subjoins last *the precipice of the negligent man,* when he says: *And immediately it fell, and the ruin of that house was great;* Zacharias, the fourteenth chapter[^p165-8]: « The ruin shall be of the horse and of the mule, of the camel and of the ass, and of all the beasts », that is, of all men who are animal and negligent and wicked; Sophonias, the first chapter: « Thus saith the Lord: Gathering man and beast, gathering the birds of the air and the fishes of the sea; and there shall be ruins of the wicked, and I will destroy men from the face of the land ». Nor is it a wonder, because, Proverbs, the fourth chapter[^p165-9], « the way of the wicked is darksome; they know not where they fall ». — By the peril, therefore, and the vituperation of *the negligent* and the commendation of *the obedient* one is sufficiently invited to *diligence in the things to be done,* and in this the divine lessons have their end.
