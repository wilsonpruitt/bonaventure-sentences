# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XVII, Vers. 1-4
## *Informatio per documentum rationabile*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 425 -->

### Capitulum XVII.

#### De pietate humana quoad opera spiritualia tripliciter.

1. *Et ad discipulos suos ait.* Postquam, confutata Iudaeorum impietate, invitavit discipulos ad pietatem secundum eleemosynas corporales; sequitur hic secunda pars, in qua invitat ad *pietatem secundum eleemosynam spiritualem*[^p425-11]. Dividitur autem haec pars in tres, in quarum prima discipulos *informat documento rationabili;* in secunda *manuducit exemplo sensibili,* ibi[^p425-12]: *Et dixerunt Apostoli Domino;*
<!-- page 426 -->
in tertia vero *confirmat facto admirabili,* ibi: *Et factum est, dum iret Iesus.*

#### Informatio per documentum rationabile.

Circa *documentum rationabile* primo praemittit *dissuasionem spiritualis impietatis,* quae consistit in scandalo proximorum; deinde adiungit *persuasionem pietatis spiritualis,* quae consistit in remissione offensarum, ibi[^p426-1]: *Attendite vobis: Si peccaverit.*

Circa *dissuasionem* igitur *impietatis scandalizantis* procedit hoc ordine, ostendens scilicet *scandalum passivum* inevitabile, *activum* autem damnabile et *supplicium* eius horribile.

2. (Vers. 1.). Primo igitur quantum ad *inevitabilitatem scandali passivi* dicit: *Et ad discipulos suos ait: Impossibile est, ut non veniant scandala.* Hoc enim non dicitur de *scandalo activo,* quia illud bene potest quis evitare, sed de *passivo,* quod non est in potestate alicuius hominis, quia illud aliquando occasionem sumit non solum de malo, verum etiam de bono, sicut dicitur de Iudaeis Matthaei decimo tertio[^p426-2]: « Qui scandalizabantur in eo »; propter quod Matthaei undecimo: « Beatus, qui non fuerit scandalizatus in me »; quia, primae Petri secundo, « non credentibus lapis, quem reprobaverunt aedificantes, factus est in caput anguli, et lapis offensionis et petra scandali ». Hoc autem permittit Deus iusto iudicio evenire ad vindictam malorum et probationem iustorum; unde Matthaei decimo octavo[^p426-3]: « Necesse est, ut veniant scandala ». Et ratio redditur primae ad Corinthios undecimo: « Oportet haereses esse, ut et qui probati sunt manifesti fiant ». Licet autem per malos et a malis veniant scandala, debent tamen a viris iustis, quantum possunt, evitari, salva veritate triplici. Unde Beda[^p426-4]: « Nota, quod in quantum potes sine peccato, vitare scandalum proximi debes; quod si de veritate est, pro scandalis relinqui veritas non debet ».

3. Est autem triplex veritas, quae non debet pro scandalo relinqui, scilicet *vitae, doctrinae* et *iustitiae.* Unde Hieronymus[^p426-5]: « Omne, quod potest fieri, vel non fieri, salva triplici veritate, dimittendum est ». Istud autem non est necessitatis, sed perfectae iustitiae, secundum quam dicebat Apostolus primae ad Corinthios octavo[^p426-6]: « Si esca scandalizat fratrem meum, non manducabo carnes in aeternum, ne fratrem meum scandalizem ». Unde ad Romanos decimo quarto: « Bonum est non manducare carnes aut bibere vinum, neque in quo frater tuus offenditur, aut scandalizatur, aut infirmatur ». Unde si aliquis propter perfectionis opera scandalizatur, non sunt tamen dimittenda; unde Hieronymus de morte Blaesillae[^p426-7]: « Scandalizat quempiam vestis fuscior, scandalizat Ioannes, qui *camelorum pilis vestitus erat* ». Exemplum autem dimittendi indifferentia propter scandalum habemus in Christo, Matthaei decimo septimo[^p426-8]: « Ut autem non scandalizemus eos, vade ad mare » etc.; ubi solvit tributum. Et tamen documenta veritatis propter scandalum non dimisit; unde Matthaei decimo quinto: « Scis, quia Pharisaei, audito verbo hoc, scandalizati sunt ». At ille dixit: « Sinite eos; caeci sunt et duces caecorum » etc.

4. Secundo quantum ad *damnabilitatem scandali activi* subdit: *Vae autem illi, per quem veniunt!* id est, qui active scandalizat. Illi autem *active* scandalizant, qui per dictum vel factum minus rectum praebent occasionem ruinae. Nam Glossa dicit Numerorum trigesimo primo in principio[^p426-9]: « Scandalum est, ubi recte ambulanti deceptio ad peccandum subiicitur », sicut fecit Balaam filiis Israel. Propter quod dicitur Apocalypsis secundo: « Habes tenentes doctrinam Balaam, qui docebat Balac mittere scandalum coram filiis Israel », scilicet edere de sacrificiis etc. Hoc scandalum est impietatis spiritualis, cum quis malo exemplo animam proximi occidit, et ideo ipse dignus morte efficitur. In cuius figuram dicitur Numerorum trigesimo primo[^p426-10]: « Cur feminas reservastis? Nonne istae sunt, quae deceperunt filios Israel ad suggestionem Balaam et praevaricari vos fecerunt in Domino »? « Igitur cunctos interficite, quidquid generis est masculini, etiam parvulos et mulieres, quae noverunt viros in coitu, iugulate »; et vigesimo quinto[^p426-11]: « Hostes vos sentiant Madianitae, et percutite eos, quia et ipsi hostiliter egerunt contra vos ». Maxime enim hostiliter agit qui spiritum vitae in fonte cordis exstinguit; ideo Matthaei decimo octavo[^p426-12]: « Si manus tua vel pes tuus scandalizat te, abscinde eum et proiice abs te; et si oculus tuus scandalizat te, erue eum et proiice abs
<!-- page 427 -->
te; bonum est enim cum uno oculo vitam intrare, quam duos oculos habentem mitti in gehennam ignis ». Hoc autem non intelligitur de corporalibus membris, sed de amicis, qui tanquam membra sunt nobis carnaliter coniuncti, a quibus, si scandalizant, debemus separari, secundum illud Matthaei decimo[^p427-1]: « Veni separare hominem adversus patrem suum et nurum adversus socrum suam; et inimici hominis domestici eius ». Ideo Ecclesiastici sexto: « Ab inimicis separare et ab amicis attende ». Chrysostomus[^p427-2]: « Nihil ita nocivum est, sicut conversari cum perniciosis »; et ideo ipse dicit: « Si membra abscidimus, cum incurabiliter se habuerint et reliquis obfuerint, multo magis in amicis hoc facere debemus ».

5. (Vers. 2.). Tertio quantum ad *horribilitatem supplicii* subdit: *Utilius est illi, si lapis molaris imponatur*[^p427-3] *circa collum eius, et proiiciatur in mare.* Beda: « Secundum ritum provinciae loquitur, ubi erat ista poena criminum, ut ligato saxo in profundum demergeretur ». Hoc, inquam, dicit *utilius, quam ut scandalizaret unum de pusillis istis;* quia longe minor poena est ista, quam illa quam scandalizando proximum meretur. Unde Beda[^p427-4]: « Vere utilius est innoxium gravissima poena vitam finire, quam laedendo fratrem mortem mereri perpetuam ». Et iterum idem[^p427-5]: « Utilius est enim pro culpa brevem recipere poenam quam aeternis cruciatibus reservari ».

6. Ratio autem, quare tanta poena debeatur scandalizanti, est, quia aufert Christo illud quod maxime amat, scilicet animam, secundum illud Sapientiae undecimo[^p427-6]: « Tua sunt, Domine, omnia, qui amas animas ». Unde et Apostolus primae ad Corinthios octavo: « Peribit infirmus in tua conscientia frater, propter quem Christus mortuus est »; et sequitur: « Sic autem peccantes in fratres in Christum peccatis ». — Alia ratio est, quia, sicut operibus aedificationis magna debetur corona, ita deaedificantibus magna debetur poena; ad Romanos decimo quarto[^p427-7]: « Quae pacis sunt sectemur, et quae aedificationis sunt invicem custodiamus; noli propter escam destruere opus Dei ». — Tertia ratio est, quia, sicut magna crudelitas est affligere aegrotantes, sic magna perversitas est seducere simplices; propter quod Levitici decimo nono[^p427-8]: « Nec coram caeco pones offendiculum »; et ideo signanter dicit: *Unum de pusillis illis.* — Quarta ratio est, quia scandalizans obligatur non solum ad reddendam animam suam, verum etiam et animam scandalizatam; unde Exodi vigesimo primo[^p427-9]: « Si quis aperuerit cisternam et non operuerit eam, cecideritque bos aut asinus in eam; reddet dominus cisternae pretium iumentorum ». — Est autem hoc peccatum grave in omnibus, sed maxime in his quorum vita trahitur in exemplum, sicut sunt praelati, sicut magni clerici, sicut etiam viri religiosi et famosi; unde Malachiae secundo[^p427-10]: « Labia sacerdotis custodiunt scientiam, et legem de ore eius requirunt ». « Vos autem recessistis de via et scandalizastis plurimos, irritum fecistis pactum Levi. Propter quod dedi vos contemptibiles et humiles » etc.

7. (Vers. 3.). *Attendite vobis* etc. Postquam dissuasit impietatem, quae consistit in scandalo proximorum, hic consequenter *suadet pietatem spiritualem,* quae consistit in *remissione iniuriarum*[^p427-11]. Et quoniam pietas debet exhiberi secundum ordinatam caritatem, quae consistit in *custodia respectu sui* et in *clementia respectu proximi;* ideo in hac parte hortatur Apostolos ad *custodiam suarum mentium,* ad *disciplinam respectu aberrantium* et ad *indulgentiam respectu poenitentium.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *custodiam suarum mentium* dicit: *Attendite vobis;* quasi dicat: prius debetis circa vestram correptionem invigilare quam circa peccata proximi; Ecclesiastici trigesimo[^p427-12]: « Miserere animae tuae, placens Deo ». Hoc docebat Apostolus primae ad Timotheum quarto: « Attende tibi et doctrinae. Hoc enim faciens, te ipsum salvum facies et eos qui te audiunt ». Et istud maxime est ipsorum praelatorum, qui primo debent circa se invigilare; Actuum vigesimo[^p427-13]: « Attendite vobis et universo gregi, in quo vos Spiritus sanctus posuit episcopos regere Ecclesiam Dei, quam acquisivit sanguine suo ». Hic autem ordo est necessarius, ne
<!-- page 428 -->
forte dicatur intendenti correptioni alienae et negligenti suam illud ad Romanos secundo[^p428-1]: « Qui alium doces te ipsum non doces »? et illud Matthaei septimo: « Hypocrita! eiice primum trabem de oculo tuo, et tunc videbis, ut educas festucam de oculo fratris tui ». Et ideo dicebat ille bonus speculator Isaias vigesimo primo[^p428-2]: « Super speculam Domini ego sum, stans iugiter per diem, et super custodiam meam ego sum, stans totis noctibus ». Intendebat *proximo,* non sicut Cain; Genesis quarto: « Nunquid custos fratris mei sum ego »? Intendebat etiam *sibi,* ne diceretur illud supra quarto: « Medice, cura te ipsum ».

8. Secundo quantum ad *disciplinam respectu peccantium* subdit: *Si peccaverit in te frater tuus, increpa illum;* et hoc per correptionem amicabilem, secundum illud secundae ad Timotheum quarto[^p428-3]: « Argue, obsecra, increpa in omni patientia et doctrina »; et Ecclesiastici decimo nono: « Corripe amicum, ne forte non intellexerit et dicat: Non feci; aut si fecit, ne iterum addat ». Unde ista increpatio non debet venire ex ira, sed ex clementia, non ex libidine vindictae, sed ex amore iustitiae; unde Gregorius in Moralibus[^p428-4]: « Si proximos sicut et nos diligere debemus, restat, ut eorum peccatis, sicut et nostris, irascamur ». Dicitur autem *peccare in nos,* quando non solum in personam nostram, sed etiam, nobis praesentibus, peccat; unde Augustinus de Verbis Domini[^p428-5]: « Peccat frater in nos, si etiam, nobis scientibus, peccat », quia, quantum in eo est, exemplo nos corrumpit, quos testes iniquitatis efficit.

9. Et nota, quod in hac increpatione servari debet ordo, qui exprimitur Matthaei decimo octavo[^p428-6]: « Si peccaverit in te frater tuus, corripe eum inter te et ipsum solum ». Et post subdit, quod adhibendi sunt testes, deinde dicendum est Ecclesiae. Nam primo peccator debet revocari *amore;* quod fit, quando secrete monetur; ad Galatas sexto[^p428-7]: « Fratres, si praeoccupatus fuerit homo in aliquo delicto » etc. — Secundo vero, *pudore;* quod fit, cum testes adhibentur, secundum illud Levitici decimo nono: « Non oderis fratrem tuum in corde tuo, sed publice argue eum, ne habeas super illo peccatum ». — Tertio autem, *terrore;* quod fit in facie Ecclesiae; primae ad Timotheum quinto: « Peccantem coram omnibus argue, ut et ceteri timorem habeant ». Hic autem ordo congruitatis est, non necessitatis, et maxime, si percipiatur, quod proximus sit incorrigibilis ad verbum fratris, sed tantum in manu forti, secundum illud Proverbiorum nono[^p428-8]: « Noli arguere derisorem, ne oderit te ». Unde Hieronymus: « Frustra niti et nihil aliud laborando quam odium quaerere extremae dementiae est ». Propter hoc et sanctus Ioseph istum ordinem non servavit, de quo dicitur Genesis trigesimo septimo[^p428-9], quod « accusavit apud patrem fratres suos crimine pessimo ». Ex quo datur forma, quod praelato, qui est persona valens prodesse, etiam nulla praecedente correptione, potest et debet culpa proximi accusari extra iudicium, si sit occulta, et in iudicio, si habeatur probatio certa. Unde Beda[^p428-10]: « Ita peccat qui peccantem videns tacet, sicut qui poenitenti non indulget »; quod quidem intelligitur, quando verbo suo potest proximus corrigi, et maius periculum non timetur; unde super illud secundae ad Corinthios sexto[^p428-11]: « Exite de medio eorum », dicit Glossa: « *Exire* est facere quod pertinet ad correptionem malorum, quantum licet pro gradu uniuscuiusque, salva pace ».

10. Tertio quantum ad *indulgentiam respectu poenitentium* subdit: *Et si poenitentiam egerit, dimitte illi.* Beda[^p428-12]: « Non iubemur passim *peccanti,* sed *poenitenti* dimittere. Primum ergo est *peccantem increpare,* ut post *poenitenti possimus dimittere* ». Hanc enim legem statuit Dominus in oratione
<!-- page 429 -->
dominica Matthaei sexto[^p429-1]: « Dimitte nobis debita nostra, sicut et nos dimittimus debitoribus nostris »; et sequitur: « Si enim dimiseritis hominibus peccata eorum, dimittet vobis Pater caelestis debita vestra. Si autem non dimiseritis hominibus, nec Pater vester caelestis dimittet vobis peccata vestra ». Ideo dicitur Ecclesiastici vigesimo octavo[^p429-2]: « Relinque proximo nocenti te, et tunc deprecanti tibi peccata solventur. Homo homini servat iram, et a Deo quaerit medelam? In hominem similem sibi non habet misericordiam, et de peccatis suis deprecatur? Ipse, cum caro sit, reservat iram, et propitiationem petit a Deo »?

11. (Vers. 4.). Et quoniam misericordia debet excedere offensam, ideo subdit: *Et si septies in die peccaverit in te,* per laesionem multiplicem. Beda[^p429-3]: « Solet universitas septenario designari », propter quod dicitur etiam Proverbiorum vigesimo quarto: « Septies in die cadit iustus et resurget ». Possunt autem per istas septem vices septiformes colligi tribulationes, quibus quis proximum laedit, *seducendo* intellectum, *corrumpendo* affectum, *affligendo* corpus, *subtrahendo* propria, *denegando* sua, *verbo laedendo* famam et *exemplo vitiando* vitam; de quibus dicitur Iob quinto[^p429-4]: « In sex tribulationibus liberabit te, et in septima non tanget te malum ». — Et haec omnia dimittenda sunt poenitenti: *Et septies in die conversus fuerit ad te, dicens: Poenitet me,* per mentalem compunctionem; *dimitte illi,* per misericordem condonationem, ne forte dicatur tibi illud quod dictum est servo nequam Matthaei decimo octavo[^p429-5]: « Serve nequam, omne debitum dimisi tibi, quoniam rogasti me; nonne ergo oportuit, et te misereri conservi tui, sicut et ego tui misertus sum »? Sicut ergo Deus omni petenti veniam tribuit, secundum illud Ezechielis trigesimo tertio[^p429-6]: « Impietas impii non nocebit ei, in quacumque die conversus fuerit » etc.; sic et nos debemus poenitenti dimittere. Unde ponitur hic numerus finitus pro infinito; unde Chrysostomus[^p429-7]: « Etsi septuagies septies dimiseris, tamen, sicut gutta aquae ad pelagus, immo multo magis deficit tua philanthropia ad infinitam bonitatem Dei ». Debent igitur omnes offensae, quantaecumque et quotcumque et quandocumque et a quocumque factae fuerint, dimitti, si humiliter per veram poenitentiam venia postulatur; et debet etiam non petenti offensa remitti, sed aliter *petenti,* aliter *persequenti.* Unde Glossa Bedae[^p429-8]: « Aliter *fratri petenti,* aliter *inimico persequenti* veniam dare praecipimur. *Fratri,* concessa remissione, socia caritate communicamus; *persecutori* malum nobis volenti, vel, si potest, nobis facienti, bona volumus et facimus quod possumus »; et hoc exemplo *Christi,* de quo infra vigesimo tertio[^p429-9]: « Pater, ignosce illis, quia nesciunt, quid faciunt »; exemplo *David,* qui dicebat: « Si reddidi retribuentibus mihi mala » etc.; exemplo protomartyris *Stephani,* de quo Actuum septimo: « Positis autem genibus, clamavit voce magna dicens: Domine, accipe spiritum meum et ne statuas illis hoc peccatum. Et cum hoc dixisset, obdormivit in Domino ».

## English
<!-- page 425 -->

### Chapter XVII.

#### On human piety as to spiritual works, in three ways.

1. *And he said to his disciples.* After, the impiety of the Jews having been confuted, he invited the disciples to piety according to corporal alms; there follows here the second part, in which he invites to *piety according to spiritual alms*[^p425-11]. And this part is divided into three, in the first of which he *forms* the disciples *by a rational instruction;* in the second he *leads* them *by the hand by a sensible example,* there[^p425-12]: *And the Apostles said to the Lord;*
<!-- page 426 -->
but in the third he *confirms* them *by a wonderful deed,* there: *And it came to pass, as Jesus was going.*

#### Formation by a rational instruction.

Concerning *the rational instruction* he first sets forth *the dissuasion from spiritual impiety,* which consists in the scandal of neighbours; then he adds *the persuasion to spiritual piety,* which consists in the remission of offences, there[^p426-1]: *Take heed to yourselves: If thy brother sin.*

Concerning *the dissuasion* therefore *from scandalizing impiety* he proceeds in this order, namely showing *passive scandal* to be inevitable, but *active* scandal damnable and its *punishment* horrible.

2. (Vers. 1.). First, then, as to *the inevitability of passive scandal* he says: *And he said to his disciples: It is impossible that scandals should not come.* For this is not said of *active scandal,* because that one can well avoid, but of *passive,* which is not in the power of any man, because it sometimes takes occasion not only from evil, but also from good, as is said of the Jews in Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p426-2]: « They were scandalized in his regard »; on account of which, Matthew, the eleventh: « Blessed is he that shall not be scandalized in me »; because, in the first of Peter, the second chapter, « to them that believe not, the stone which the builders rejected, the same is made the head of the corner, and a stone of stumbling and a rock of scandal ». And God permits this to come about by a just judgement, for the vengeance of the wicked and the proving of the just; whence Matthew, the eighteenth chapter[^p426-3]: « It must needs be that scandals come ». And the reason is given in the first to the Corinthians, the eleventh: « There must be also heresies, that they also who are approved may be made manifest ». But although scandals come through the wicked and from the wicked, yet they ought to be avoided by just men, as far as they can, saving a threefold truth. Whence Bede[^p426-4]: « Note that, in so far as thou canst without sin, thou oughtest to avoid the scandal of thy neighbour; but if it is concerning truth, truth ought not to be abandoned for scandals ».

3. Now there is a threefold truth which ought not to be abandoned for scandal, namely of *life,* of *doctrine* and of *justice.* Whence Jerome[^p426-5]: « Everything that can be done, or not be done, saving the threefold truth, is to be given up ». But this is not of necessity, but of perfect justice, according to which the Apostle said in the first to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter[^p426-6]: « If meat scandalize my brother, I will never eat flesh, lest I should scandalize my brother ». Whence to the Romans, the fourteenth: « It is good not to eat flesh, and not to drink wine, nor any thing whereby thy brother is offended, or scandalized, or made weak ». Whence if anyone is scandalized on account of the works of perfection, yet they are not to be given up; whence Jerome on the death of Blaesilla[^p426-7]: « A darker garment scandalizes someone; John scandalizes, who *was clothed with camel's hair* ». And an example of giving up indifferent things on account of scandal we have in Christ, Matthew, the seventeenth chapter[^p426-8]: « But that we may not scandalize them, go to the sea » etc.; where he paid the tribute. And yet he did not give up the lessons of truth on account of scandal; whence Matthew, the fifteenth: « Dost thou know that the Pharisees, when they heard this word, were scandalized »? But he said: « Let them alone; they are blind, and leaders of the blind » etc.

4. Second, as to *the damnableness of active scandal* he subjoins: *But woe to him through whom they come!* that is, who scandalizes actively. And they scandalize *actively* who by a word or deed less right furnish occasion of ruin. For the Gloss says on Numbers, the thirty-first chapter, at the beginning[^p426-9]: « Scandal is where a deception to sinning is put before one walking rightly », as Balaam did to the children of Israel. On account of which it is said in the Apocalypse, the second chapter: « Thou hast there them that hold the doctrine of Balaam, who taught Balac to cast a scandal before the children of Israel », namely to eat of sacrifices etc. This scandal is of spiritual impiety, when one kills the soul of his neighbour by bad example, and therefore he himself is made worthy of death. In figure of which it is said in Numbers, the thirty-first chapter[^p426-10]: « Why have you saved the women? Are not these they that deceived the children of Israel by the counsel of Balaam, and made you transgress against the Lord »? « Therefore kill all that are of the male sex, even of the children, and put to death the women that have known men by coition »; and in the twenty-fifth[^p426-11]: « Let the Madianites find you enemies, and slay you them: because they also have acted like enemies against you ». For he acts most like an enemy who extinguishes the spirit of life in the fountain of the heart; therefore Matthew, the eighteenth chapter[^p426-12]: « If thy hand or thy foot scandalize thee, cut it off, and cast it from thee; and if thy eye scandalize thee, pluck it out, and cast it from

<!-- page 427 -->
thee; for it is better for thee having one eye to enter into life, than having two eyes to be cast into hell fire ». But this is not understood of bodily members, but of friends, who are joined to us carnally as it were members, from whom, if they scandalize, we ought to be separated, according to that of Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p427-1]: « I came to set a man at variance against his father, and the daughter in law against her mother in law; and a man's enemies shall be they of his own household ». Therefore Ecclesiasticus, the sixth: « Separate thyself from thy enemies, and take heed of thy friends ». Chrysostom[^p427-2]: « Nothing is so harmful as to converse with pernicious men »; and therefore he himself says: « If we cut off members, when they are incurably disposed and harm the rest, much more ought we to do this in friends ».

5. (Vers. 2.). Third, as to *the horribleness of the punishment* he subjoins: *It were better for him, that a millstone were hanged*[^p427-3] *about his neck, and he cast into the sea.* Bede: « He speaks according to the custom of the province, where this was the punishment of crimes, that one should be sunk into the deep with a stone bound to him ». This, I say, he calls *better, than that he should scandalize one of these little ones;* because this punishment is far less than that which he merits by scandalizing his neighbour. Whence Bede[^p427-4]: « Truly it is better for an innocent man to end his life by the gravest punishment than by hurting a brother to merit perpetual death ». And again the same[^p427-5]: « For it is better to receive a brief punishment for a fault than to be reserved for eternal torments ».

6. And the reason why so great a punishment is due to the one who scandalizes is, because he takes away from Christ that which he most loves, namely the soul, according to that of Wisdom, the eleventh chapter[^p427-6]: « All are thine, O Lord, who lovest souls ». Whence also the Apostle in the first to the Corinthians, the eighth: « Through thy conscience shall the weak brother perish, for whom Christ hath died »; and there follows: « Now when you sin thus against the brethren, you sin against Christ ». — Another reason is, because, just as a great crown is due to works of edification, so a great punishment is due to those who undo edification; to the Romans, the fourteenth chapter[^p427-7]: « Let us follow after the things that are of peace, and keep the things that are of edification one towards another; destroy not the work of God for meat ». — The third reason is, because, just as it is a great cruelty to afflict the sick, so it is a great perversity to seduce the simple; on account of which Leviticus, the nineteenth chapter[^p427-8]: « Neither shalt thou put a stumblingblock before the blind »; and therefore he says pointedly: *One of those little ones.* — The fourth reason is, because the one who scandalizes is bound not only to render up his own soul, but also the soul scandalized; whence Exodus, the twenty-first chapter[^p427-9]: « If a man open a pit and cover it not, and an ox or an ass fall into it; the owner of the pit shall pay the price of the beasts ». — And this sin is grave in all, but most of all in those whose life is drawn into an example, as are prelates, as are great clerics, as also men religious and renowned; whence Malachias, the second chapter[^p427-10]: « The lips of the priest shall keep knowledge, and they shall seek the law at his mouth ». « But you have departed out of the way, and have scandalized many, you have made void the covenant of Levi. Therefore have I also made you contemptible, and base » etc.

7. (Vers. 3.). *Take heed to yourselves* etc. After he has dissuaded from impiety, which consists in the scandal of neighbours, here consequently he *persuades to spiritual piety,* which consists in *the remission of injuries*[^p427-11]. And since piety ought to be shown according to ordered charity, which consists in *custody with respect to oneself* and in *clemency with respect to one's neighbour;* therefore in this part he exhorts the Apostles to *the custody of their own minds,* to *discipline with respect to those going astray* and to *indulgence with respect to penitents.*

First, then, as to *the custody of their own minds* he says: *Take heed to yourselves;* as if he were saying: you ought to keep watch over your own correction before over the sins of your neighbour; Ecclesiasticus, the thirtieth chapter[^p427-12]: « Have pity on thy own soul, pleasing God ». This the Apostle taught in the first to Timothy, the fourth chapter: « Take heed to thyself and to doctrine. For in doing this thou shalt both save thyself and them that hear thee ». And this most of all belongs to prelates themselves, who ought first to keep watch over themselves; Acts, the twentieth chapter[^p427-13]: « Take heed to yourselves, and to the whole flock, wherein the Holy Spirit hath placed you bishops, to rule the Church of God, which he hath purchased with his own blood ». And this order is necessary, lest
<!-- page 428 -->
perhaps there be said to one intent upon another's correction and negligent of his own that of the Romans, the second chapter[^p428-1]: « Thou that teachest another, teachest not thyself »? and that of Matthew, the seventh: « Thou hypocrite! cast out first the beam out of thy own eye, and then shalt thou see to cast out the mote out of thy brother's eye ». And therefore that good watchman Isaias said, the twenty-first chapter[^p428-2]: « I am upon the watchtower of the Lord, standing continually by day, and I am upon my ward, standing whole nights ». He was intent upon his *neighbour,* not as Cain; Genesis, the fourth: « Am I my brother's keeper »? He was intent also upon *himself,* lest there be said that of above, the fourth chapter: « Physician, heal thyself ».

8. Second, as to *discipline with respect to sinners* he subjoins: *If thy brother sin against thee, rebuke him;* and this by friendly correction, according to that of the second to Timothy, the fourth chapter[^p428-3]: « Reprove, entreat, rebuke in all patience and doctrine »; and Ecclesiasticus, the nineteenth: « Correct a friend, lest he may not have understood, and say: I did it not; or if he did it, that he may do it no more ». Whence this rebuke ought not to come from anger, but from clemency, not from lust of vengeance, but from love of justice; whence Gregory in the Morals[^p428-4]: « If we ought to love our neighbours as also ourselves, it remains that we be angry at their sins, as also at our own ». Now he is said *to sin against us,* when he sins not only against our person, but also with us present; whence Augustine on the Words of the Lord[^p428-5]: « A brother sins against us, if he sins even with us knowing it », because, as far as in him lies, he corrupts us by example, whom he makes witnesses of iniquity.

9. And note that in this rebuke an order ought to be kept, which is expressed in Matthew, the eighteenth chapter[^p428-6]: « If thy brother shall offend against thee, correct him between thee and him alone ». And afterwards he subjoins that witnesses are to be brought in, then it is to be told to the Church. For first the sinner ought to be called back by *love;* which is done when he is admonished secretly; to the Galatians, the sixth chapter[^p428-7]: « Brethren, if a man be overtaken in any fault » etc. — But second, by *shame;* which is done when witnesses are brought in, according to that of Leviticus, the nineteenth: « Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thy heart, but reprove him publicly, lest thou have sin upon him ». — But third, by *terror;* which is done in the face of the Church; the first to Timothy, the fifth: « Them that sin reprove before all, that the rest also may have fear ». But this order is of congruity, not of necessity, and most of all if it be perceived that the neighbour is incorrigible at the word of a brother, but only by a strong hand, according to that of Proverbs, the ninth chapter[^p428-8]: « Rebuke not a scorner, lest he hate thee ». Whence Jerome: « To strive in vain and by labouring to seek nothing else than hatred is of extreme madness ». On account of this the holy Joseph also did not keep this order, of whom it is said in Genesis, the thirty-seventh chapter[^p428-9], that « he accused his brethren to his father of a most wicked crime ». From which a form is given, that to a prelate, who is a person able to profit, even with no correction preceding, the fault of a neighbour can and ought to be accused, outside judgement, if it be hidden, and in judgement, if there is certain proof. Whence Bede[^p428-10]: « He sins as much who, seeing one sinning, is silent, as he who does not pardon one doing penance »; which indeed is understood when by his word the neighbour can be corrected, and a greater danger is not feared; whence on that of the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter[^p428-11]: « Go out from among them », the Gloss says: « *To go out* is to do what pertains to the correction of the wicked, as far as is lawful according to the degree of each one, saving peace ».

10. Third, as to *indulgence with respect to penitents* he subjoins: *And if he do penance, forgive him.* Bede[^p428-12]: « We are not commanded to forgive *one sinning* indiscriminately, but *one doing penance.* First, then, it is *to rebuke the one sinning,* that afterwards *we may forgive the one doing penance* ». For the Lord established this law in the
<!-- page 429 -->
Lord's prayer, Matthew, the sixth chapter[^p429-1]: « Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors »; and there follows: « For if you will forgive men their offences, your heavenly Father will forgive you your debts. But if you will not forgive men, neither will your heavenly Father forgive you your sins ». Therefore it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p429-2]: « Forgive thy neighbour if he hath hurt thee, and then shall thy sins be forgiven to thee when thou prayest. Man to man reserveth anger, and doth he seek remedy of God? He hath no mercy on a man like himself, and doth he entreat for his own sins? He that is but flesh, nourisheth anger, and doth he ask forgiveness of God »?

11. (Vers. 4.). And since mercy ought to exceed the offence, therefore he subjoins: *And if he sin against thee seven times in a day,* by manifold hurting. Bede[^p429-3]: « Universality is wont to be designated by the number seven », on account of which it is said also in Proverbs, the twenty-fourth chapter: « A just man shall fall seven times in a day, and shall rise again ». Now through these seven times can be gathered the sevenfold tribulations by which one hurts his neighbour: by *seducing* the understanding, by *corrupting* the affection, by *afflicting* the body, by *withdrawing* his own, by *denying* what is one's own, by *hurting* reputation *by word* and by *vitiating* life *by example;* of which it is said in Job, the fifth chapter[^p429-4]: « In six troubles he shall deliver thee, and in the seventh evil shall not touch thee ». — And all these are to be forgiven to one doing penance: *And seven times in a day be converted unto thee, saying: I repent,* by mental compunction; *forgive him,* by merciful condoning, lest perhaps there be said to thee that which was said to the wicked servant in Matthew, the eighteenth chapter[^p429-5]: « Thou wicked servant, I forgave thee all the debt, because thou besoughtest me; shouldst not thou then have had compassion also on thy fellow servant, even as I had compassion on thee »? Just as, then, God grants pardon to everyone asking, according to that of Ezechiel, the thirty-third chapter[^p429-6]: « The wickedness of the wicked shall not hurt him, in what day soever he shall turn » etc.; so we too ought to forgive one doing penance. Whence a finite number is put here for an infinite; whence Chrysostom[^p429-7]: « Even if thou forgive seventy times seven times, yet, as a drop of water to the sea, nay much more, thy philanthropy falls short of the infinite goodness of God ». All offences, then, however great and however many and whenever and by whomever they have been done, ought to be forgiven, if pardon is humbly asked through true penance; and an offence ought also to be remitted to one not asking, but in one way to *one asking,* in another to *one persecuting.* Whence the Gloss of Bede[^p429-8]: « We are commanded to give pardon in one way *to a brother asking,* in another *to an enemy persecuting.* *To the brother,* remission granted, we communicate in companionable charity; *to the persecutor* who wills evil to us, or, if he can, does it to us, we will good things and do what we can »; and this by the example of *Christ,* of whom below, the twenty-third chapter[^p429-9]: « Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do »; by the example of *David,* who said: « If I have rendered to them that repaid me evils » etc.; by the example of the protomartyr *Stephen,* of whom in Acts, the seventh chapter: « And falling on his knees, he cried with a loud voice, saying: Lord, receive my spirit and lay not this sin to their charge. And when he had said this, he fell asleep in the Lord ».
