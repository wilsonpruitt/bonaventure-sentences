# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. VII, Vers. 1-16
## *Primo propter excellentiam potestatis probatam per duo miracula*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 165 -->

### Capitulum VII.

#### Secundo declaratur sublimitas in Doctore tripliciter.

1. *Cum autem implesset omnia verba sua* etc. Postquam prosecutus est Evangelista doctrinae Christi veritatem[^p165-10], hic ad ipsius commendationem manifestat *Doctoris sublimitatem,* quam ostendit esse praecipuam propter tria, scilicet propter *excellentiam potestatis;* propter *evidentiam veritatis,* ibi[^p165-11]: *Et exiit hic sermo* etc.; et propter *affluentiam pietatis,* ibi: *Rogabat autem illum quidam de Pharisaeis.* Excellentia potestatis ostenditur in operando magna miracula; evidentia veritatis, in approbando vera testimonia; affluentia pietatis, in condonando peccata.

#### Primo propter excellentiam potestatis probatam per duo miracula.

Quantum ad primum intelligendum, quod prima pars habet duas secundum duo miracula, quorum primum est *curatio pueri morituri;* secundum est *resuscitatio adolescentis iam mortui,* quae ibi[^p165-12] describitur: *Et factum est deinceps* etc. In sanatione namque *morituri* ostenditur dominus *vitae,* qui potest vitam pereuntem salvare; in suscitatione *mortui,* dominus *mortis,* qui potest mortem superare. Item, in *sanatione corporis morituri* ostenditur dominus *corporum,* qui potest corpus reparare; in
<!-- page 166 -->
*suscitatione mortui,* dominus *animarum,* qui potest animas ad corpora revocare.

Quantum igitur ad *curationem pueri aegrotantis* mirabiliter consummatam tria introducit Evangelista ad explicationem miraculi facientia. Primum est *opportunitas faciendi miraculum.* Secundum est *dignitas impetrandi remedium,* ibi[^p166-1]: *Cum audisset de Iesu, misit* etc. Tertium est *congruitas accelerandi beneficium,* ibi: *Et cum iam non longe esset* etc. — *Opportunitas* autem faciendi miraculum colligitur ex tribus, scilicet ex *temporis commoditate,* ex *loci congruitate* et ex *morbi necessitate.*

2. (Vers. 1.). Primo igitur quantum ad *commoditatem temporis* dicitur: *Cum autem implesset omnia verba sua in aures plebis,* id est post tempus praedicationis et eruditionis perfectae et completae, quae spectat ad illum Doctorem, secundum illud Ecclesiastici quadragesimo tertio[^p166-2]: « Multa dicimus et deficimus in verbis, consummatio sermonum ipse est »; ipse scilicet *abbreviatus* in carne, secundum illud Isaiae decimo: « Consummatio abbreviata inundabit iustitiam »; et vigesimo octavo: « Consummationem et abbreviationem audivi a Domino Deo exercituum »; hoc dicebat in persona populi audientis Christum ad quem supra quarto[^p166-3] Christus dixit: « Hodie impleta est haec Scriptura in auribus vestris », scilicet Iudaeorum.

Secundo quantum ad *congruitatem loci* subditur: *Intravit Capharnaum.* Dicitur supra quarto[^p166-4]: « Quanta audivimus a te facta in Capharnaum »; unde Matthaei undecimo dicitur: « Et tu, Capharnaum, nunquid usque in caelum exaltaberis »? Quod dicit propter multas virtutes, quas ibi fecit. — Ratio huius est, quia haec est civitas Galilaeae, quae erat in confinio gentium et Iudaeorum, ut ostendatur Christus *lapis angularis,* secundum illud ad Ephesios secundo[^p166-5]: « Ut duos condat in semetipso in unum novum hominem, faciens pacem ». Et in hoc impleta est prophetia Isaiae nono: « Primo tempore alleviata est terra Zabulon et terra Nephthalim, et novissimo aggravata est via maris trans Iordanem Galilaeae gentium » etc.

3. (Vers. 2.). Tertio quantum ad *morbi necessitatem* adiungitur: *Centurionis autem cuiusdam servus male habens erat moriturus;* et ita gravem habebat morbum, secundum illud Matthaei octavo[^p166-6]: « Puer meus iacet in domo paralyticus et male torquetur ». *Centurionem* autem vocat eum qui praeerat centum militibus; quod faciebant ex industria in proeliis, ut scirent de facili numerum bellatorum; unde erant ibi *tribuni, centuriones, quinquagenarii* et *decani,* qui denominantur a numero eorum quibus praesunt; *tribunus* autem dicitur qui praeest mille. Et hos ad hortationem populi constituere consulebatur Moysi; Exodi decimo octavo[^p166-7]: « Constitue ex eis tribunos, centuriones, quinquagenarios et decanos, qui iudicent populum ». — Hic centurio, licet plures haberet servos, hunc carum habebat inter alios, et ideo subdit: *Qui erat illi pretiosus,* id est affectuose dilectus, secundum illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo tertio[^p166-8]: « Si est tibi servus fidelis, sit tibi quasi anima tua »; quia, secundum illud Proverbiorum decimo septimo, « servus sensatus dominabitur stultis filiis ». Unde servus hic, licet esset infirmus, non tamen erat suo domino onerosus, sed carus propter domini sui pietatem; Proverbiorum duodecimo: « Novit iustus animas iumentorum suorum » etc.

4. (Vers. 3.). *Et cum audisset de Iesu* etc. Post opportunitatem faciendi miraculum subdit hic Evangelista *dignitatem impetrandi remedium,* quam colligit Evangelista ex tribus, scilicet ex *centurionis reverentia* in supplicando, ex *seniorum confidentia* in postulando, ex *Salvatoris clementia* in condescendendo. Quae tria in unum concurrentia dignum faciunt servum aegrotum ad inveniendum remedium sanitatis.

Quantum igitur ad centurionis *reverentiam supplicantem* praemittit: *Et cum audisset de Iesu,* non solum aure corporis, sed etiam aure cordis, secundum illud ad Romanos decimo[^p166-9]: « Fides ex auditu, auditus autem per verbum Christi ». Et quia fides facit cum reverentia accedere, dum admirabilem Dei praedicat maiestatem; ideo subdit: *Misit ad eum seniores Iudaeorum,* quasi ipse non esset dignus accedere nec auderet; sicut filii Israel tempore dationis Legis, Exodi vigesimo[^p166-10], dixerunt ad Moysen: « Loquere tu nobis, non loquatur nobis Dominus, ne forte moriamur ». Et hanc causam ipse reddit infra eodem: « Propter quod et me ipsum non sum dignum arbitratus, ut venirem ad te ». Et ideo illos
<!-- page 167 -->
misit, non tanquam nuntios inferiores, sed tanquam digniores. Et ideo mittebat *seniores,* quia ipsi sunt pro aetate et sapientia reverendi et audiendi; Iob duodecimo[^p167-1]: « In antiquis est sapientia, et in multo tempore prudentia ». — Unde per eos Dominum interpellabat; quod notatur, cum addit: *Rogans eum, ut veniret et sanaret*[^p167-2] *servum eius,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo: « In tua infirmitate ne despicias te ipsum, sed ora Dominum, et curabit *te* »; similiter et *alium* propter te, quia, Iacobi ultimo, « oratio fidei salvabit infirmum, et alleviabit ipsum Dominus ».

5. Sed videtur huic contrariari Matthaeus[^p167-3], qui dicit, quod « centurio ipse accessit ad Dominum ». — Sed non est contrarietas, quia, sicut Beda[^p167-4] ait, Matthaeus loquitur de accessu *per fidem,* Lucas narrat *ordinem rei gestae.* Augustinus[^p167-5] etiam dicit, quod secundum communem modum dicitur *accedere* qui incipit ire; dicitur etiam *rogare* qui per alium facit. Unde nulla est contrarietas, sed tunc esset, si diceret Matthaeus, quod corporaliter ad eum pervenisset in propria persona, quod non dicit; sed aliquid dicit, quod stat cum dicto Lucae, et unus alterum explicat et confirmat.

6. (Vers. 4.). Quantum autem ad *seniorum confidentiam* intercedentem subdit: *At illi, cum venissent ad Iesum, rogaverunt*[^p167-6] *eum sollicite,* quia hoc faciebant ex amore et confidentia; ad Romanos duodecimo: « Sollicitudine non pigri, spiritu ferventes; Domino servientes, orationi instantes, necessitatibus Sanctorum communicantes ». — Et quia potissima ratio impetrandi est meritum eius, pro quo petitur; ideo addunt *dicentes*[^p167-7]: *Quia dignus est, ut hoc ei praestes,* et sic iam invenisti quod quaeris, secundum illud Sapientiae sexto de Sapientia: « Quoniam dignos se ipsa circuit quaerens et in viis suis ostendit se illis hilariter et in omni providentia occurrit illis ».

7. (Vers. 5.). Et ad huius dignitatis comprobationem adiungunt: *Diligit enim gentem nostram,* cum tamen sit alienigena; et ideo tu, qui es de gente nostra, debes eum diligere, secundum quod dicis de te Proverbiorum octavo[^p167-8]: « Ego diligentes me diligo ». — Nec tantum « diligit ore vel lingua, sed opere et veritate »; unde et addunt: *Et synagogam ipse aedificavit nobis,* quod quidem magnum beneficium fuit et signum magni amoris. Unde Gregorius[^p167-9] de caritate dicit, quod « amor operatur magna, si est; si autem operari renuit, amor non est ». Et quoniam magnam praestat eleemosynam, dignus est, ut magnam ei facias gratiam, ut cognoscat, illud Ecclesiastici duodecimo[^p167-10] esse verum: « Benefac iusto et invenies retributionem magnam, et si non ab illo, certe a Domino »; quasi dicant: ostende te Dominum populi nostri, ut facias huic sicut obstetricibus, Exodi primo, quae fecerunt misericordiam cum Hebraeis, de quibus dicitur: « Quia timuerunt obstetrices Deum, aedificavit illis domos ».

8. (Vers. 6.). Quantum ad *Salvatoris clementiam condescendentem* subditur: *Iesus autem ibat cum illis,* omnino satisfaciens petitioni eorum. Maluit autem Dominus petitioni eorum satisfacere quam petitioni reguli, Ioannis quarto[^p167-11] dicentis: « Domine, descende, priusquam moriatur filius meus », sive propter *maiorem fidem et humilitatem* centurionis, cui Dominus magis condescendebat, secundum illud Ieremiae quinto: « Domine, oculi tui respiciunt fidem ». Sive ad *commendandam humilitatem,* secundum quod dicit Ambrosius[^p167-12]: « Ad filium reguli ire noluit, ne divitias honorasse videretur; ad servum vero centurionis vadit, ne servilem conditionem sprevisse putaretur. Elucet fides in operibus, dum sanat; sed plus operatur humilitas in affectibus, dum vadit ». Vel hoc fecit ad *excludendam personarum acceptionem,* secundum quod dicitur Sapientiae sexto[^p167-13]: « Pusillum et magnum ipse fecit, et aequaliter est illi cura de omnibus »; unde Actuum decimo: « In veritate comperi, quia non est personarum acceptor Deus »; et ideo Iacobi secundo: « Nolite, fratres, in personarum acceptione habere fidem Domini nostri Iesu Christi gloriae »; quod fit, quando dives praefertur pauperi, sicut dicitur ibidem.

9. *Et cum iam non longe esset* etc. Post opportunitatem faciendi miraculum et dignitatem impetrandi remedium subditur hic tertio *congruitas accelerandi beneficium;* quae quidem consistit penes meritum *fidei excellentis,* cuius excellentia tripliciter comprobatur, primo scilicet ex *humilitate confessionis devotae;* secundo, ex *auctoritate testificationis divinae,* ibi[^p167-14]: *Quo audito* etc.; et tertio, ex *celeritate curationis optatae,* ibi: *Et reversi qui missi fuerant.*

Quantum ad *humilitatem devotae confessionis* nota, quod centurio *excellentiam divinae potestatis insinuat* signo, *explicat* verbo et *probat* argumento. *Indicat signo,* cum non audet eum suscipere
<!-- page 168 -->
*explicat verbo,* dum dicit, eum omnia posse; *probat argumento,* quia omnia debent ei obtemperare.

10. Propter quod ait: *Et cum iam non longe esset a domo;* prope quidem erat domui corporali, sed propior domui mentali, quia, secundum illud Psalmi[^p168-1], « prope est Dominus omnibus invocantibus eum »; quocontra de malis Ieremiae duodecimo: « Prope es tu ori eorum et longe a renibus eorum ». Non talis erat iste; cuius *signum* erat praecipua humilitas, quam ostendit primo *confitendo suam indignitatem,* cum dicit: *Misit ad eum centurio amicos, dicens: Domine, noli vexari,* quasi dicat: non sum talis, quod propter me debeas laborare et corporaliter fatigari; quia laboriosum erat ei ire, secundum illud Ioannis quarto[^p168-2]: « Iesus autem fatigatus ex itinere sedebat sic super fontem ». — Quod non dicebat, quia recusaret praesentiam Christi corporalem, sed propter suam indignitatem; unde et addit: *Non enim sum dignus, ut sub tectum meum intres,* quod dicit consideratione suae immunditiae et peccati, sicut et Petrus supra quinto[^p168-3]: « Exi a me, Domine, quia homo peccator sum »; secundi Regum vigesimo quarto dixit Areuna regi: « Quid causae est, ut veniat dominus meus rex ad servum suum »? Et haec indignitas non latebat eum per superbiam, sed nota erat per humilitatem praecipuam.

11. (Vers. 7.). Propter quod adiungit: *Propter quod et me ipsum non sum dignum arbitratus, ut venirem ad te.* Hoc *arbitrium* est humilitatis, secundum illud ad Philippenses secundo[^p168-4]: « In humilitate superiores sibi invicem arbitrantes ». Hoc arbitrium erat in Apostolo per veram humilitatem, qui dicebat primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto: « Ego sum minimus Apostolorum, qui non sum dignus vocari Apostolus ». Ambrosius[^p168-5]: « Militari honore deposito, reverentiam sumit, ad fidem facilis, ad honorificentiam promptus ». Et hoc quidem facit vera humilitas, quia, secundum illud Ecclesiastici tertio, « magna potentia Dei solius, et a solis humilibus honoratur ».

12. Unde iste non solum honorificabat Dominum confitendo suam indignitatem, sed etiam *profitendo divinae virtutis sublimitatem,* cum addit: *Sed dic verbo, et sanabitur puer meus,* quasi dicat: « non est apud te impossibile omne verbum[^p168-6] », tu es enim illud Verbum, per quod « omnia facta sunt », Ioannis primo. Tu es ille « sermo, qui sanas omnia », Sapientiae decimo sexto; qui « potestate plenus es », Ecclesiastae octavo; qui es « vivus et efficax » etc., ad Hebraeos quarto. *Dic* ergo *verbo, et sanabitur puer meus.*

13. (Vers. 8.). Et quod illud sufficiat, ostendit *a minori,* cum adiungit: *Nam et ego homo sum sub potestate constitutus,* et ita aliis me recognosco subiectum, secundum illud ad Romanos decimo tertio[^p168-7]: « Omnis anima potestatibus sublimioribus subdita sit »; sic tamen *subditus,* quod et aliis *praepositus.* Propterea dicit: *Habens sub me milites,* qui scilicet verbo meo obediunt; unde addit: *Et dico huic: Vade, et vadit; et alii: Veni, et venit,* sine contradictione, secundum illud ad Hebraeos ultimo[^p168-8]: « Obedite praepositis vestris et subiacete eis ». Et hoc dicit quantum ad maiora ministeria; et quantum ad minora addit: *Et servo meo,* scilicet dico: *Fac hoc, et facit;* infra duodecimo: « Beatus ille servus, quem, cum venerit dominus eius, invenerit sic facientem », scilicet sicut praecepit. Ex quo arguit, cum ipse sub nulla sit potestate et innumerabiles habeat subditos, quod potest sanare solo verbo; in quo confitetur Iesum Dominum, de quo Ecclesiastici quadragesimo secundo[^p168-9]: « Omnia in omni necessitate obediunt ei ».

14. (Vers. 9.). Quantum autem ad *auctoritatem divinae testificationis* subditur: *Quo audito, Iesus miratus est,* id est, ad modum admirantis se habuit, non quia hoc esset sibi ante incognitum vel magnum, sed ut signo ostenderet, fidem gentilis hominis esse magnam, sicut illius mulieris gentilis; Matthaei decimo quinto[^p168-10]: « O mulier! magna est fides tua ». — Et hoc manifestat verbo, cum addit: *Et conversus sequentibus se*[^p168-11] *dixit,* scilicet discipulis, ad quos benignitate convertebatur, ut doceret, secundum illud Deuteronomii trigesimo tertio: « Qui appropinquant pedibus eius accipient de doctrina illius »; Proverbiorum vigesimo primo: « Qui sectatur sapientem sumet sapientiam ». — Et sequitur, quod dixit: *Amen dico vobis, nec in Israel tantam fidem inveni,* ubi maior deberet inveniri, quia ipsi habent eam quasi per *hereditatem* ab Abraham, de quo dicitur Genesis decimo quinto[^p168-12]: « Credidit Abraham Deo, et reputatum est illi ad iustitiam ». Ipsi etiam habent per *Legem;* Ioannis quinto: « Si Moysi crederetis, crederetis
<!-- page 169 -->
et mihi; de me ille scripsit; si autem illius litteris non creditis, mihi quomodo credetis »?

15. Sed quomodo potest illud intelligi? cum beata Virgo de Israel fuerit, cui dicitur supra primo[^p169-1]: « Beata, quae credidisti »; et etiam Apostoli. — Sed hoc dicitur, non quia maior sit *simpliciter,* sed *respectu* habito ad personam. Unde Chrysostomus[^p169-2]: « In modico sermone sapientiae plus laudatur rusticus quam in magno philosophus; et puer, si dicat aliquid sapienter, laudatur, ubi perfectior non auditur ». Sic et in proposito intelligendum est. — Vel potest dici, quod intelligit quantum ad reverentiam exterius exhibendam; vel quod in hoc intelligitur fides gentium[^p169-3].

16. (Vers. 10.). Quantum ad *celeritatem optatae curationis* subiungitur: *Et reversi qui missi fuerant*[^p169-4], quasi spe de salute impetrata per verbum Christi, quod dixit quod exprimit Matthaeus, licet Lucas taceat: « Sicut credidisti, fiat tibi. Et sanatus est puer »; quod tacet Lucas, quia satis potest colligi ex praedictis, quia, ut dicit Beda[^p169-5], « mos est beati Lucae ab aliis plene exposita breviare, vel etiam de industria praeterire; quae autem brevius dicta ab aliis vel omissa sollicitius dilucidare ». Et ideo tacet verbum Domini, quod alii dicunt, et dicit nuntiorum reversionem, ut exprimat miraculi celeritatem, quam insinuat, cum adiungit: *Invenerunt servum, qui languerat, sanum.* Ante ergo pervenit virtutis operatio quam nuntiorum reversio, ut sic verum esse comprobetur quod in Psalmo[^p169-6] dicitur: « Velociter currit sermo eius ». Nec mirum, quia non refert apud eum *dicere* et *facere,* sicut centurio fideliter credidit et ore confessus est; quia « corde creditur ad iustitiam, ore autem fit confessio ad salutem ». Et ideo credendo et confitendo, quod subito solo verbo posset facere, hoc merito fidei impetravit, ut sic impleta ostendatur dominica promissio, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p169-7]: « Quidquid orantes petitis, credite, quia accipietis, et fiet vobis ». — Et sic sufficienter expressit Evangelista mirabilem curationem servi.

17. *Spiritualiter* hic secundum *allegoriam* intelligitur *curatio populi gentilis,* quod colligitur ex *persona aegrotante* et ex *persona interveniente* et ex *curationis ordine.* — *Persona* namque *aegrotans* erat servilis conditionis; sic et populus gentilis ante adventum Christi, quia serviebat peccato — secundum illud Ioannis octavo[^p169-8]: « Qui facit peccatum servus est peccati », et ratio huius dicitur secundae Petri secundo: « A quo quis superatus est, eius et servus est » — et propter peccatum specialiter idololatriae, propter quod erant servi infirmarum rerum; ad Galatas quarto[^p169-9]: « Cum essemus parvuli, sub elementis mundi eramus servientes »; et post: « Quomodo revertimini ad infirma et egena elementa, quibus denuo servire vultis »? ad Galatas loquitur, qui fuerant gentiles. — Ex *persona interveniente,* qui fuerunt seniores Iudaeorum, per quos intelliguntur Apostoli, secundum illud Isaiae tertio[^p169-10]: « Dominus ad iudicium veniet cum senioribus terrae et principibus populi ». Isti sunt Apostoli, quibus dicitur Matthaei decimo nono: « Sedebitis et vos super sedes duodecim, iudicantes duodecim tribus Israel » etc. Horum interventione gentilis populus est curatus, secundum illud Matthaei ultimo[^p169-11]: « Ite, docete omnes gentes »; et per hoc conversi sunt ad lumen Christi, per quod illuminati et salvati sunt, secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo nono: « Parum est, ut sis mihi servus ad suscitandas tribus Iacob et faeces Israel convertendas; dedi te in lucem gentium ». — Ex *curationis ordine,* quia per fidei magnitudinem, quae praefertur fidei Israeliticae; ad Romanos undecimo[^p169-12]: « Ex parte caecitas contigit in Israel, donec plenitudo gentium intraret, et sic omnis Israel salvus fieret »; Actuum decimo quinto: « Elegit Deus per os meum, dicit Petrus, gentes audire verbum Evangelii et credere »; « et nihil discrevit inter nos et illos, fide purificans corda eorum »; ad Romanos decimo: « Non est distinctio Iudaei et Graeci »; « omnis enim, quicumque invocaverit nomen Domini, salvus erit ».

18. *Spiritualiter* autem secundum *tropologiam* designatur hic *curatio viri peccatoris,* ad quam tria concurrunt, sicut hic. Primum est *recognitio infirmitatis propriae,* quia primo requiritur, ut homo cognoscat delictum; ad quod hortatur Ieremiae
<!-- page 170 -->
secundo[^p170-1]: « Vide vias tuas in convalle, scito, quid feceris »; alioquin non poteris iustificari, sicut ibidem subditur: « Ecce, ego iudicio contendam tecum, eo quod dixeris: Non peccavi ». Hoc attendens, anima poenitens dicit Ieremiae trigesimo primo: « Converte me, Domine, et convertar ». « Postquam enim convertisti me, egi poenitentiam; et postquam ostendisti mihi, percussi femur meum » etc. — Secundum est *interventio auctoritatis apostolicae,* quia dictum est Petro Matthaei decimo sexto[^p170-2]: « Et tibi dabo claves regni caelorum, et quodcumque ligaveris super terram, erit ligatum et in caelis » etc. Et Ioannis vigesimo omnibus Apostolis dicitur: « Quorum remiseritis peccata, remittuntur eis » etc. Ideo dicitur in Levitico: « Orabit pro eo sacerdos, et remittetur ei »; unde ad Hebraeos quinto: « Omnis pontifex ex hominibus assumtus, pro hominibus constituitur in his quae sunt ad Deum, ut offerat dona et sacrificia pro peccatis ». — Tertium est *condescensio pietatis divinae,* appropinquantis ad domum aegroti per gratiae infusionem, secundum illud cantici Zachariae, supra primo[^p170-3]: « Ad dandam scientiam salutis » etc., « per viscera misericordiae Dei nostri ». Domini autem gratia magis appropinquat humilibus, quia, secundum illud Iacobi quarto, « Deus superbis resistit, humilibus autem dat gratiam ». « Excelsus enim Dominus et humilia respicit et alta a longe cognoscit[^p170-4] ». Unde in Psalmo: « Iuxta est Dominus his qui tribulato sunt corde, et humiles spiritu salvabit »; sicut dicitur infra decimo nono de Zachaeo pusillo: « Festinans descende, quia hodie in domo tua oportet me manere »; quia libenter humilibus condescendit. Augustinus[^p170-5]: « Altus est Deus; humilias te, et venit ad te; exaltas te, et fugit a te ».

19. Et nota, quod aliter humiliavit se Zachaeus, aliter centurio: Zachaeus suscipiendo, centurio non audendo suscipere; utroque enim modo fit Domino reverentia. Unde bene facit qui propter reverentiam corpus dominicum suscipit frequenter in Sacramento; et bene etiam, qui per[^p170-6] reverentiam cessat; utrique enim salus datur, sicut centurionis servo et ipsi Zachaeo. Sic intelligendum est in proposito.

20. *Et factum est, deinceps* etc. Post miraculum de curatione morituri, subditur hic *miraculum de suscitatione mortui;* in quo expressius fit demonstratio excellentiae Salvatoris. Ad huius autem miraculi explicitam descriptionem tria introducuntur ab Evangelista. Primum est *ratio movens* respectu miraculi *faciendi;* secundum est *causa efficiens* respectu miraculi *in fieri,* et hoc notatur ibi[^p170-7]: *Quam cum vidisset Dominus* etc. Tertium est *efficacia sequens* respectu miraculi *iam perfecti,* ibi: *Et resedit qui erat mortuus.*

*Ratio* autem *movens* Dominum ad mirabiliter resuscitandum mortuum duplex ostenditur in littera, scilicet *documentum ignorantium* in intellectu et *solatium moerentium* in affectu. Et secundum haec duo genera[^p170-8] introducuntur in prima parte, scilicet *comitantium in via,* ut dirigantur per eius doctrinam, et *occurrentium in porta,* ut consolentur per eius gratiam.

21. (Vers. 11.). Primo igitur quantum ad *comitatum multitudinis erudiendae* dicitur: *Et factum est, deinceps ibat Iesus*[^p170-9] *in civitatem, quae vocatur Naim; ibat,* scilicet ad docendum, secundum illud Matthaei quarto: « Circuibat Iesus totam Galilaeam, docens in synagogis eorum ». — Et quia volentes discere comitantur doctorem, ideo addit: *Et ibant cum eo discipuli eius et turba copiosa.* Sciebant, enim illud verum esse, quod Deuteronomii trigesimo tertio[^p170-10] scribitur: « Qui appropinquant pedibus eius accipient de doctrina illius »; et illud Proverbiorum decimo tertio: « Qui cum sapientibus graditur sapientior fiet ». Et ideo dicitur Ecclesiastici octavo: « Ne despicias narrationem presbyterorum sapientium, et in proverbiis eorum conversare; ab ipsis enim disces sapientiam et doctrinam ». Et ideo turbae comitantur Dominum et discipulos eius, secundum illud Psalmi[^p170-11]: « Accedite ad eum, et illuminamini ».

22. (Vers. 12.). Secundo quantum *ad occursum multitudinis consolandae* subditur: *Cum autem appropinquaret portae civitatis, ecce, defunctus efferebatur, filius unicus matris suae;* de quo erat moeror et luctus, quia *mortuus,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo secundo[^p170-12]: « Super mortuum plora; defecit enim lux eius ». *Maior* etiam luctus, quia erat *unigenitus,* de quo fit planctus tanto amarior, quanto et carior; Ieremiae sexto: « Luctum unigeniti fac tibi, planctum amarum »; et secundi Regum primo David lugens Ionathan mortuum: « Sicut mater unicum amat filium, ita te diligebam ». *Maximus* autem luctus, quia mater omnino erat desolata; propter quod dicit: *Et haec vidua erat,* et ita iam
<!-- page 171 -->
omnino desolata, carens tam solatio filii quam mariti. Quod allegat illa mulier Thecuitis ad misericordiam impetrandam, secundi Regum decimo quarto[^p171-1]: « Heu! mulier vidua ego sum ». Et ideo plangebat Ieremias viduitatem Ierusalem Threnorum primo: « Quomodo sedet sola civitas plena populo » etc. Et propterea multi ei compatiebantur; unde addit: *Et turba civitatis multa cum illa,* scilicet ad condolendum et consolandum, quia hic erat mos Iudaeorum, secundum illud Ioannis undecimo[^p171-2]: « Multi ex Iudaeis venerant ad Mariam et Martham, ut consolarentur eas de fratre suo ». Ad quos autem omnes poterat haec vidua dicere: « O vos omnes, qui transitis per viam, attendite et videte, si est dolor sicut dolor meus »! post parum: « Posuit me desolatam, tota die moerore confectam ». Et ideo egebat consolatione.

23. (Vers. 13.). *Quam cum vidisset Dominus* etc. Hic tangitur *causa efficiens* respectu miraculi *in fieri,* quae quidem fuit duplex, scilicet *affectus pietatis* in corde inclinans ad miserandum; et *virtus maiestatis* in sermone imperans ad surgendum. Primum respicit naturam assumtam; secundum vero, aeternam.

Quantum ad *affectum pietatis in corde* dicitur: *Quam cum vidisset Dominus, misericordia motus*[^p171-3], scilicet quantum ad *cordis affectum,* ut posset dicere illud Iob trigesimo: « Flebam quondam super eo qui afflictus erat »; et trigesimo primo: « Ab infantia mea crevit mecum miseratio ». — Et quia misericordia latens in *affectu* apparet in *affatu, dixit*[^p171-4]: *Noli flere,* benigno sermone eam consolando, secundum illud Iob vigesimo nono: « Cor viduae consolatus sum »; et Ieremiae trigesimo primo: « Quiescat vox tua a ploratu, et oculi tui a lacrymis ».

24. (Vers. 14.). Et quia *affectum cordis* et *affatum oris* comitari decens est *effectum operis,* ideo addit: *Et accessit et tetigit loculum,* ut omnibus ostenderet suam benignitatem, qua non despicit mortuos, cum secundum Legem tangens mortuum immundus esset, sicut dicitur Levitici vigesimo primo[^p171-5]; et ut portantes humiliter faceret sistere *tangendo,* non imperando. — Unde subditur: *Hi autem, qui portabant, steterunt,* scilicet miraculum exspectantes, secundum quod ipsi Iob, trigesimo septimo[^p171-6], dicitur: « Ausculta haec, Iob, sta et considera mirabilia Dei ».

25. Quantum autem ad *imperium maiestatis in sermone* subditur: *Et ait: Adolescens, tibi dico: Surge;* ad Ephesios quinto[^p171-7]: « Surge, qui dormis, et exsurge a mortuis, et illuminabit te Christus »; ut sic verificetur illud Ioannis quinto: « Mortui audient vocem Filii Dei »; quae quidem est vox imperans et potens mortuos suscitare ratione suae virtutis, secundum illud Psalmi: « Dabit voci suae vocem virtutis ». Et recte, quia, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p171-8], « nemo tam facile dormientem excitat de somno, sicut Christus revocat a sepulcro ». Dirigit autem hunc sermonem ad mortuum, non solum ut excitativum, sed ut auditus reparativum, ut ostendat, verum esse illud Ioannis octavo[^p171-9]: « Ego principium, qui et loquor vobis », *principium* scilicet interius operando, *qui loquor* exterius excitando. Unde in Christo, quia caro iuncta est Verbo aeterno, *vox exterior* iuncta est illi *dicere interiori,* quod est principium omnis *conditionis,* sicut patet in operibus sex dierum, Genesis primo[^p171-10]; est etiam principium omnis *reparationis;* Sapientiae decimo sexto: « Sermo tuus, Domine, sanat omnia ».

26. (Vers. 15.). *Et resedit qui mortuus erat.* Hic tertio notatur *efficacia subsequens* respectu miraculi *iam perfecti,* quae quidem duplex est secundum duplicem finem, scilicet *operis* et *intentionis.* Prima efficacia est *suscitatio mortui;* secunda, *promotio fidei.*

Primo ergo designatur *suscitatio mortui* ut *nota plebi assistenti,* cum dicit: *Et resedit qui erat mortuus, et coepit loqui;* in quo apparet manifeste, quod vivebat, quia dupliciter vita dignoscitur, scilicet per sensum et motum. Non est dubium, quin loqueretur verba divinae laudis, quod quidem non est mortuorum, sed viventium, secundum illud quod in Psalmo[^p171-11] dicitur: « Non mortui laudabunt te, Domine, sed nos, qui vivimus, benedicimus Domino ».

27. Et nota, quod suscitatio puellae probatur *manducatione,* Marci quinto[^p171-12]; suscitatio Lazari *ambulatione,* Ioannis undecimo; suscitatio adolescentis *locutione,* sicut hic. Et respicit *manducatio* actum vegetabilis, *ambulatio* actum sensibilis, *locutio* actum rationalis, ut hac triplici probatione ostendatur dominus animae secundum omnem sui potentiam et virtutem.

28. Nec tantum erat nota plebi assistenti, sed *grata matri desideranti;* unde et subdit: *Et dedit
<!-- page 172 -->
illum matri suae;* in quo ostendit, se illum suscitasse ad matris consolationem. Unde ipsa poterat dicere illud Annae primi Regum primo[^p172-1]: « Oravi Dominum pro puero isto, et dedit mihi petitionem meam ». Sic Elias tradidit puerum resuscitatum matri suae, dicens tertii Regum decimo septimo: « En, filius tuus vivit »; sed ille *tradidit,* iste *dedit,* quia ille fuit minister suscitationis, iste vero dator vitae et salutis.

29. (Vers. 16.). Secundo vero designatur *promotio fidei* quantum ad *reverentiam Creatoris,* cum dicitur: *Accepit autem omnes timor, et magnificabant Deum; timor* scilicet reverentiae magnificantis; Iob trigesimo septimo[^p172-2]: « Timebunt eum viri, et non audebunt contemplari omnes, qui sibi videntur esse sapientes »; et Isaiae quinquagesimo nono: « Timebunt qui ab occidente nomen Domini, et qui ab ortu solis, gloriam eius ». Et sic timentes corde glorificabant et magnificabant ore; Ecclesiastici quadragesimo tertio: « Glorificantes eum, quantumcumque potestis, supervalebit adhuc ». « Et quis magnificabit eum, sicut est a principio »?

30. Sed non sufficienter promovetur fides ad reverentiam Creatoris sine *intelligentia Mediatoris;* et ideo quantum ad illam adiungitur: *Dicentes: Quia propheta magnus surrexit in nobis,* id est Christus mediator, secundum illud Deuteronomii decimo octavo[^p172-3]: « Prophetam suscitabit vobis Dominus de fratribus vestris tanquam me; ipsum audietis ». *Et quia Deus visitavit plebem suam,* scilicet per Christum redemptorem, secundum illud Zachariae supra primo[^p172-4]: « Benedictus Dominus Deus Israel, quia visitavit et fecit redemptionem plebis suae »; et hoc secundum illud Genesis ultimo: « Post mortem meam visitabit vos Dominus et educet vos de loco isto »; quod intelligit de visitatione per mediatorem Christum. — Et sic apparet ex praedictis, quomodo manifestatus est quantum ad excellentiam potestatis suae.

31. Secundum *spiritualem intelligentiam* nota, quod triplex status in prosecutione huius miraculi designatur, scilicet status *iustitiae* in comitatu dominico; status *iniustitiae* in adolescente mortuo; et status *poenitentiae* in eodem resuscitato. — Status quidem *iustitiae* designatur in Christo et comitatu suo. Nam triplex est status iustorum, scilicet *contemplationis, praelationis* et *actionis.* Status *praelatorum* designatur in Christo, de quo Ioannis decimo[^p172-5]: « Ego sum pastor bonus; bonus pastor animam suam dat pro ovibus suis »; ubi in se ostendit formam rectae praelationis. — Status *contemplativorum* in discipulis, qui admittuntur in montem in transfiguratione Domini, secundum illud Matthaei decimo septimo[^p172-6]: « Assumsit Iesus Petrum et Iacobum et Ioannem fratrem eius, et duxit illos in montem excelsum seorsum, et transfiguratus est ante eos »; et infra decimo: « Conversus ad discipulos suos, dixit: Beati oculi, qui vident quae vos videtis »; hoc dicitur contemplativis. — Status *activorum* designatur in turba, quae versatur in fluctibus mundi; infra decimo[^p172-7]: « Martha, Martha, sollicita es et turbaris erga plurima »; quia, secundum quod dicitur Ioannis decimo sexto, « in mundo pressuram habebitis; sed confidite, ego vici mundum ». — De hoc triplici statu *iustitiae* dicitur in figura Ezechielis decimo quarto[^p172-8]: « Si fuerit in medio eius Noe, Daniel et Iob; ipsi quidem animas suas liberabunt » etc. Per *Noe* intelliguntur *praelati;* per *Danielem,* cui data est intelligentia visionum, *contemplativi;* per *Iob, activi.*

32. Status vero *culpae* designatur in *adolescente mortuo,* cuius triplex est gradus: primus in *interiori affectione;* secundus, in *exteriori operatione;* tertius vero, in *consuetudine* sive obstinatione. Hi tres status per tres mortuos a Domino suscitatos designantur tum ratione *locorum,* tum ratione *aetatum.* — Nam status *peccantium in corde* designatur per puellam mortuam in *domo,* de qua habetur Marci quinto[^p172-9]. — Status *peccantium in opere,* per adolescentem, qui ad *portam civitatis* inventus est, quia iam processit peccatum et patuit, de quo hic. — Status *peccantium per consuetudinem* et obstinationem, per Lazarum, iam in virili aetate iacentem, iam quatriduanum mortuum et foetidum in sepulcro, Ioannis undecimo[^p172-10]. — His tribus respondent tres mortui suscitati in veteri testamento, scilicet *puerulus* ab Eliseo, filius mulieris Sunamitis, quarti Regum quarto[^p172-11]; *adolescens,* filius mulieris Sareptanae, suscitatus ab Elia; tertii Regum decimo septimo; et *vir,* ab Eliseo mortuo, quarti Regum decimo tertio. In his tribus designantur tres praedicti status. — Possunt etiam in his tribus tres *radices peccatorum* designari, quia unus mortuus ex *languore aegritudinis,* in quo concupiscentia carnis; alius ex *fervore solis*[^p172-12], in quo cupiditas mundi; tertius *invisibiliter,* in quo superbia vitae.

Status autem *poenitentiae* designatur in *adolescente resuscitato,* de quo dicitur, quod *resedit et coepit loqui et redditus est matri. Resedit* quidem per contritionem, quae levat caput a terra; in Psalmo[^p172-13]: « Surgite, postquam sederitis, qui manducatis panem doloris »; et secundum illud Ieremiae decimo quinto: « Solus sedebam, quoniam amaritudine replesti me ». *Coepit loqui,* per confessionem; Iob
<!-- page 173 -->
decimo[^p173-1]: « Loquar in amaritudine animae meae, dicam Deo: Noli me condemnare »; et septimo: « Non parcam ori meo, loquar in tribulatione spiritus mei, confabulabor cum amaritudine animae meae ». *Redditus est matri,* Ecclesiae per satisfactionem, quia *satisfacere* est debitum reddere, non Deo Patri tantum, sed etiam matri Ecclesiae; in cuius figura dicitur quarti Regum quarto[^p173-2]: « Tolle puerum istum et duc eum ad matrem suam ». Et sicut mater libenter suscipit filium suscitatum, sic gaudenter Christus et Ecclesia peccatorem poenitentem; infra decimo quinto: « Epulari oportebat et gaudere, quia frater tuus hic mortuus erat et revixit ».

### Chapter VII.

#### Secondly, the sublimity in the Doctor is declared in three ways.

1. *And when he had finished all his words* etc. After the Evangelist has followed out the truth of the doctrine of Christ[^p165-10], here, for his commendation, he manifests the *sublimity of the Doctor,* which he shows to be pre-eminent on account of three things, namely on account of the *excellence of power;* on account of the *evidence of truth,* there[^p165-11]: *And this rumour went forth* etc.; and on account of the *affluence of piety*[^tr-pietas], there: *And one of the Pharisees desired him.* The excellence of power is shown in working great miracles; the evidence of truth, in approving true testimonies; the affluence of piety, in pardoning sins.

#### First, on account of the excellence of power, proved through two miracles.

As to the first it is to be understood that the first part has two, according to the two miracles, of which the first is the *cure of the boy about to die;* the second is the *raising again of the young man already dead,* which is described there[^p165-12]: *And it came to pass afterwards* etc. For in the healing of the one *about to die* he is shown the lord of *life,* who can save life that is perishing; in the raising of the *dead,* the lord of *death,* who can overcome death. Likewise, in the *healing of the body of one about to die* he is shown the lord of *bodies,* who can repair the body; in the
<!-- page 166 -->
*raising of the dead,* the lord of *souls,* who can call souls back to their bodies.

As to the *curing of the sick boy,* then, marvellously consummated, the Evangelist introduces three things that make for the explication of the miracle. The first is the *opportuneness of working the miracle.* The second is the *worthiness of obtaining the remedy,* there[^p166-1]: *And when he had heard of Jesus, he sent* etc. The third is the *congruity of hastening the benefit,* there: *And when he was now not far* etc. — The *opportuneness* of working the miracle is gathered from three things, namely from the *convenience of the time,* from the *congruity of the place* and from the *necessity of the sickness.*

2. (Vers. 1.). First, then, as to the *convenience of the time* it is said: *And when he had finished all his words in the hearing of the people,* that is, after the time of preaching and of erudition perfect and complete, which belongs to that Doctor, according to that saying of Ecclesiasticus, the forty-third chapter[^p166-2]: « We say many things, and fall short in words; the consummation of discourses is he »; he, namely, *abridged* in the flesh, according to that saying of Isaias, the tenth chapter: « The consummation abridged shall overflow with justice »; and the twenty-eighth chapter: « I have heard of the Lord God of hosts a consummation and an abridging »; this he was saying in the person of the people hearing Christ, to whom above, in the fourth chapter[^p166-3], Christ said: « This day is fulfilled this Scripture in your ears », namely of the Jews.

Secondly, as to the *congruity of the place* it is subjoined: *He entered into Capharnaum.* It is said above, in the fourth chapter[^p166-4]: « As great things as we have heard done in Capharnaum »; whence in Matthew, the eleventh chapter, it is said: « And thou, Capharnaum, shalt thou be exalted up to heaven »? Which he says on account of the many miracles which he did there. — The reason of this is that this is a city of Galilee which was on the border of the Gentiles and of the Jews, so that Christ might be shown to be the *corner stone,* according to that saying to the Ephesians, the second chapter[^p166-5]: « That he might make the two in himself into one new man, making peace ». And in this was fulfilled the prophecy of Isaias, the ninth chapter: « At the first time the land of Zabulon and the land of Nephthali was lightly touched, and at the last the way of the sea beyond the Jordan of the Galilee of the Gentiles was heavily loaded » etc.

3. (Vers. 2.). Thirdly, as to the *necessity of the sickness* it is added: *And the servant of a certain centurion, being sick, was ready to die;* and so he had a grave sickness, according to that saying of Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p166-6]: « My servant lieth at home sick of the palsy, and is grievously tormented ». He calls *centurion* him who was over a hundred soldiers; which they did of set purpose in battles, so that they might easily know the number of the fighting men; whence there were there *tribunes, centurions, captains of fifty* and *decans,* who are named from the number of those over whom they are set; but he is called a *tribune* who is over a thousand. And it was counselled to Moses to appoint these at the exhortation of the people; Exodus, the eighteenth chapter[^p166-7]: « Appoint of them tribunes, centurions, captains of fifty and decans, who may judge the people ». — This centurion, though he had many servants, held this one dear among the others, and therefore he subjoins: *Who was dear to him,* that is, affectionately loved, according to that saying of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-third chapter[^p166-8]: « If thou have a faithful servant, let him be to thee as thy own soul »; because, according to that saying of Proverbs, the seventeenth chapter, « a wise servant shall rule over foolish sons ». Whence this servant, though he was infirm, was nevertheless not burdensome to his master, but dear on account of the piety of his master; Proverbs, the twelfth chapter: « The just knoweth the lives of his beasts » etc.

4. (Vers. 3.). *And when he had heard of Jesus* etc. After the opportuneness of working the miracle the Evangelist here subjoins the *worthiness of obtaining the remedy,* which the Evangelist gathers from three things, namely from the *reverence of the centurion* in supplicating, from the *confidence of the ancients* in requesting, from the *clemency of the Saviour* in condescending. Which three, concurring in one, make the sick servant worthy to find the remedy of health.

As to the centurion's *supplicating reverence,* then, he puts first: *And when he had heard of Jesus,* not only with the ear of the body, but also with the ear of the heart, according to that saying to the Romans, the tenth chapter[^p166-9]: « Faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of Christ ». And because faith makes one approach with reverence, while it preaches the wonderful majesty of God; therefore he subjoins: *He sent unto him the ancients of the Jews,* as if he himself were not worthy to approach nor dared to; as the children of Israel at the time of the giving of the Law, Exodus, the twentieth chapter[^p166-10], said to Moses: « Speak thou to us; let not the Lord speak to us, lest perhaps we die ». And this cause he himself gives below in the same chapter: « For which cause neither did I judge myself worthy to come to thee ». And therefore he sent
<!-- page 167 -->
them, not as inferior messengers, but as worthier ones. And therefore he sent *ancients,* because they are to be revered and heard by reason of age and wisdom; Job, the twelfth chapter[^p167-1]: « In the ancient is wisdom, and in much time prudence ». — Whence through them he was entreating the Lord; which is noted when he adds: *Desiring him to come and heal*[^p167-2] *his servant,* according to that saying of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter: « In thy infirmity despise not thyself, but pray to the Lord, and he shall heal *thee* »; likewise also *another* on account of thee, because, James, the last chapter, « the prayer of faith shall save the sick man, and the Lord shall raise him up ».

5. But Matthew[^p167-3] seems to be contrary to this, who says that « the centurion himself came to the Lord ». — But there is no contrariety, because, as Bede[^p167-4] says, Matthew speaks of the approach *by faith,* Luke narrates the *order of the thing done.* Augustine[^p167-5] also says that according to the common manner he is said to *approach* who begins to go; he is also said to *beseech* who does it through another. Whence there is no contrariety; but there would be, if Matthew said that he had come to him bodily in his own person, which he does not say; but he says something which stands with what Luke said, and the one explains and confirms the other.

6. (Vers. 4.). And as to the interceding *confidence of the ancients* he subjoins: *And when they came to Jesus, they besought*[^p167-6] *him solicitously,* because they did this out of love and confidence; to the Romans, the twelfth chapter: « In solicitude not slothful, in spirit fervent; serving the Lord, instant in prayer, communicating to the necessities of the Saints ». — And because the most powerful reason for obtaining is the merit of him for whom one asks; therefore they add, *saying*[^p167-7]: *He is worthy that thou shouldest do this for him,* and so thou hast already found what thou seekest, according to that saying of Wisdom, the sixth chapter, concerning Wisdom: « For she goeth about seeking such as are worthy of her, and she sheweth herself to them cheerfully in the ways, and meeteth them with all providence ».

7. (Vers. 5.). And for the proof of this worthiness they add: *For he loveth our nation,* though he is nevertheless a foreigner; and therefore thou, who art of our nation, oughtest to love him, according to what thou sayest of thyself in Proverbs, the eighth chapter[^p167-8]: « I love them that love me ». — Nor does he love only « with mouth or with tongue, but in deed and in truth »; whence they also add: *And he hath built us a synagogue,* which indeed was a great benefit and a sign of great love. Whence Gregory[^p167-9] says of charity that « love works great things, if it is; but if it refuses to work, it is not love ». And since he gives great alms, he is worthy that thou do him a great grace, so that he may know that saying of Ecclesiasticus, the twelfth chapter[^p167-10], to be true: « Do good to the just, and thou shalt find great recompense; and if not of him, assuredly of the Lord »; as if they should say: show thyself the Lord of our people, so that thou do for this man as for the midwives, Exodus, the first chapter, who showed mercy to the Hebrews, of whom it is said: « Because the midwives feared God, he built them houses ».

8. (Vers. 6.). As to the *condescending clemency of the Saviour* it is subjoined: *And Jesus went with them,* altogether satisfying their petition. But the Lord preferred to satisfy their petition rather than the petition of the ruler, John, the fourth chapter[^p167-11], who said: « Lord, come down before that my son die », whether on account of the *greater faith and humility* of the centurion, to whom the Lord condescended the more, according to that saying of Jeremias, the fifth chapter: « O Lord, thy eyes regard faith ». Or to *commend humility,* according to what Ambrose[^p167-12] says: « To the son of the ruler he would not go, lest he should seem to have honoured riches; but to the servant of the centurion he goes, lest he should be thought to have spurned the servile condition. Faith shines forth in works, while he heals; but humility works more in the affections, while he goes ». Or he did this to *exclude respect of persons,* according to what is said in Wisdom, the sixth chapter[^p167-13]: « He made the little and the great, and he hath equally care of all »; whence in Acts, the tenth chapter: « In very deed I perceive that God is not a respecter of persons »; and therefore James, the second chapter: « Brethren, have not the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ of glory with respect of persons »; which is done when the rich man is preferred to the poor, as is said in the same place.

9. *And when he was now not far* etc. After the opportuneness of working the miracle and the worthiness of obtaining the remedy, there is subjoined here thirdly the *congruity of hastening the benefit;* which indeed consists in the merit of *excellent faith,* whose excellence is proved in three ways: first, namely, from the *humility of devout confession;* secondly, from the *authority of divine testifying,* there[^p167-14]: *Which being heard* etc.; and thirdly, from the *swiftness of the desired cure,* there: *And they who had been sent being returned.*

As to the *humility of devout confession* note that the centurion *intimates the excellence of the divine power* by a sign, *explains* it by a word and *proves* it by an argument. *He indicates it by a sign,* when he does not dare to receive him;
<!-- page 168 -->
*he explains it by a word,* when he says that he can do all things; *he proves it by an argument,* because all things ought to obey him.

10. On account of which he says: *And when he was now not far from the house;* near indeed he was to the bodily house, but nearer to the house of the mind, because, according to that saying of the Psalm[^p168-1], « the Lord is nigh unto all them that call upon him »; whereas of the wicked Jeremias, the twelfth chapter: « Thou art near in their mouth, and far from their reins ». Not such was this man; whose *sign* was pre-eminent humility, which he showed first by *confessing his unworthiness,* when he says: *The centurion sent his friends to him, saying: Lord, trouble not thyself,* as if he should say: I am not such that thou oughtest to labour and be bodily wearied on my account; because it was laborious for him to go, according to that saying of John, the fourth chapter[^p168-2]: « Jesus therefore being wearied with his journey, sat thus on the well ». — Which he was not saying because he refused the bodily presence of Christ, but on account of his unworthiness; whence he also adds: *For I am not worthy that thou shouldest enter under my roof,* which he says in consideration of his uncleanness and sin, as Peter too above, in the fifth chapter[^p168-3]: « Depart from me, O Lord, for I am a sinful man »; in the second of Kings, the twenty-fourth chapter, Areuna said to the king: « What is the cause that my lord the king should come to his servant »? And this unworthiness was not hidden from him through pride, but was known through pre-eminent humility.

11. (Vers. 7.). On account of which he adds: *For which cause neither did I judge myself worthy to come to thee.* This *judgement* is of humility, according to that saying to the Philippians, the second chapter[^p168-4]: « In humility judging one another superiors to themselves ». This judgement was in the Apostle through true humility, who said in the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter: « I am the least of the Apostles, who am not worthy to be called an Apostle ». Ambrose[^p168-5]: « Having laid aside military honour, he takes up reverence, ready for faith, prompt for honouring ». And this indeed true humility does, because, according to that saying of Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter, « great is the power of God alone, and he is honoured by the humble alone ».

12. Whence this man not only honoured the Lord by confessing his unworthiness[^tr-corrigenda], but also by *professing the sublimity of the divine power,* when he adds: *But say the word, and my servant shall be healed,* as if he should say: « no word is impossible with thee[^p168-6] », for thou art that Word through which « all things were made », John, the first chapter. Thou art that « word which healest all things », Wisdom, the sixteenth chapter; who « art full of power », Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter; who art « living and effectual » etc., to the Hebrews, the fourth chapter. *Say* therefore *the word, and my servant shall be healed.*

13. (Vers. 8.). And that this suffices he shows *from the lesser,* when he adds: *For I also am a man set under power,* and so I acknowledge myself subject to others, according to that saying to the Romans, the thirteenth chapter[^p168-7]: « Let every soul be subject to higher powers »; yet so *subject* that I am also *set over* others. Therefore he says: *Having under me soldiers,* who, namely, obey my word; whence he adds: *And I say to one, Go, and he goeth; and to another, Come, and he cometh,* without contradiction, according to that saying to the Hebrews, the last chapter[^p168-8]: « Obey your prelates and be subject to them ». And this he says as to the greater ministries; and as to the lesser he adds: *And to my servant,* namely I say: *Do this, and he doth it;* below, in the twelfth chapter: « Blessed is that servant, whom when his lord shall come, he shall find so doing », namely as he commanded. From which he argues that, since he himself is under no power and has innumerable subjects, he can heal by his word alone; in which he confesses Jesus as Lord, of whom Ecclesiasticus, the forty-second chapter[^p168-9]: « In every need all things obey him ».

14. (Vers. 9.). And as to the *authority of divine testifying* it is subjoined: *Which being heard, Jesus wondered,* that is, he bore himself after the manner of one wondering, not because this was unknown to him before, or great, but so that he might show by a sign that the faith of a Gentile man was great, as was that of that Gentile woman; Matthew, the fifteenth chapter[^p168-10]: « O woman, great is thy faith ». — And this he makes manifest by a word, when he adds: *And turning to them that followed him*[^p168-11] *he said,* namely to the disciples, to whom he turned with kindness, so that he might teach, according to that saying of Deuteronomy, the thirty-third chapter: « They that approach to his feet shall receive of his doctrine »; Proverbs, the twenty-first chapter: « He that followeth the wise shall take up wisdom ». — And there follows what he said: *Amen I say to you, I have not found so great faith, not even in Israel,* where it ought to be found greater, because they have it as it were by *inheritance* from Abraham, of whom it is said in Genesis, the fifteenth chapter[^p168-12]: « Abraham believed God, and it was reputed to him unto justice ». They have it also through the *Law;* John, the fifth chapter: « If you did believe Moses, you would believe
<!-- page 169 -->
me also; he wrote of me; but if you do not believe his writings, how will you believe me »?

15. But how can that be understood? since the blessed Virgin was of Israel, to whom it is said above, in the first chapter[^p169-1]: « Blessed art thou that hast believed »; and the Apostles too. — But this is said, not because it is greater *simply,* but *with respect* had to the person. Whence Chrysostom[^p169-2]: « In a small word of wisdom a rustic is praised more than a philosopher in a great one; and a boy, if he says something wisely, is praised, where a more perfect man is not heard ». So too in the matter proposed it is to be understood. — Or it can be said that he understands it as to the reverence to be shown outwardly; or that in this is understood the faith of the Gentiles[^p169-3].

16. (Vers. 10.). As to the *swiftness of the desired cure* it is subjoined: *And they who had been sent being returned*[^p169-4], as it were with the hope of health obtained through the word of Christ, which he said, which Matthew expresses, though Luke is silent: « As thou hast believed, so be it done to thee. And the servant was healed »; which Luke passes over in silence, because it can be sufficiently gathered from what was said before, because, as Bede[^p169-5] says, « it is the custom of blessed Luke to abridge what has been fully set forth by others, or even of set purpose to pass it over; but what has been said more briefly by others, or omitted, to clarify more carefully ». And therefore he is silent about the word of the Lord, which the others tell, and tells the return of the messengers, so as to express the swiftness of the miracle, which he intimates when he adds: *They found the servant whole who had been sick.* The working of the power, therefore, arrived before the return of the messengers, so that thus that may be proved true which is said in the Psalm[^p169-6]: « His word runneth swiftly ». Nor is it a wonder, because with him it makes no difference *to say* and *to do,* as the centurion faithfully believed and confessed with his mouth; because « with the heart we believe unto justice, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation ». And therefore by believing and confessing that he could do it suddenly by his word alone, he obtained this by the merit of faith, so that thus the Lord's promise may be shown fulfilled, according to that saying of Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p169-7]: « Whatsoever you ask when you pray, believe that you shall receive, and it shall be done to you ». — And so the Evangelist has sufficiently expressed the marvellous healing of the servant.

17. *Spiritually* there is understood here, according to *allegory,* the *cure of the Gentile people,* which is gathered from the *person who is sick* and from the *person who intervenes* and from the *order of the cure.* — For the *person who is sick* was of servile condition; so too the Gentile people before the coming of Christ, because it served sin — according to that saying of John, the eighth chapter[^p169-8]: « Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin », and the reason of this is told in the second of Peter, the second chapter: « By whom a man is overcome, of the same also he is the servant » — and on account of the sin specially of idolatry, on account of which they were servants of weak things; to the Galatians, the fourth chapter[^p169-9]: « When we were children, we were serving under the elements of the world »; and after: « How turn you back to the weak and needy elements, which you desire to serve again »? He speaks to the Galatians, who had been Gentiles. — From the *person who intervenes,* who were the ancients of the Jews, by whom are understood the Apostles, according to that saying of Isaias, the third chapter[^p169-10]: « The Lord will come to judgement with the ancients of the land and the princes of the people ». These are the Apostles, to whom it is said in Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « You also shall sit on twelve seats, judging the twelve tribes of Israel » etc. By the intervention of these the Gentile people has been cured, according to that saying of Matthew, the last chapter[^p169-11]: « Go, teach all nations »; and through this they were converted to the lumen[^tr-lumen] of Christ, through which they were enlightened and saved, according to that saying of Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter: « It is a small thing that thou shouldst be my servant to raise up the tribes of Jacob and to convert the dregs of Israel; I have given thee for a light of the Gentiles ». — From the *order of the cure,* because it was through the greatness of faith, which is preferred to the faith of Israel; to the Romans, the eleventh chapter[^p169-12]: « Blindness in part has happened in Israel, until the fulness of the Gentiles should come in, and so all Israel should be saved »; Acts, the fifteenth chapter: « God chose that by my mouth, says Peter, the Gentiles should hear the word of the Gospel and believe »; « and put no difference between us and them, purifying their hearts by faith »; to the Romans, the tenth chapter: « There is no distinction of the Jew and the Greek »; « for whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved ».

18. *Spiritually,* however, according to *tropology,* there is designated here the *cure of the sinful man,* to which three things concur, as here. The first is the *recognition of one's own infirmity,* because it is first required that a man know his fault; to which Jeremias exhorts,
<!-- page 170 -->
the second chapter[^p170-1]: « See thy ways in the valley, know what thou hast done »; otherwise thou wilt not be able to be justified, as is subjoined in the same place: « Behold, I will contend with thee in judgement, because thou hast said: I have not sinned ». Attending to this, the penitent soul says in Jeremias, the thirty-first chapter: « Convert me, O Lord, and I shall be converted ». « For after thou didst convert me, I did penance; and after thou didst shew unto me, I struck my thigh » etc. — The second is the *intervention of apostolic authority,* because it was said to Peter in Matthew, the sixteenth chapter[^p170-2]: « And I will give to thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven, and whatsoever thou shalt bind upon earth, it shall be bound also in heaven » etc. And in John, the twentieth chapter, it is said to all the Apostles: « Whose sins you shall forgive, they are forgiven them » etc. Therefore it is said in Leviticus: « The priest shall pray for him, and it shall be forgiven him »; whence to the Hebrews, the fifth chapter: « Every high priest taken from among men is ordained for men in the things that appertain to God, that he may offer up gifts and sacrifices for sins ». — The third is the *condescension of the divine piety,* drawing near to the house of the sick man through the infusion of grace, according to that saying of the canticle of Zachary, above, in the first chapter[^p170-3]: « To give knowledge of salvation » etc., « through the bowels of the mercy of our God ». But the grace of the Lord draws nearer to the humble, because, according to that saying of James, the fourth chapter, « God resisteth the proud, and giveth grace to the humble ». « For the Lord is high, and looketh on humble things, and the high he knoweth afar off[^p170-4] ». Whence in the Psalm: « The Lord is nigh unto them that are of a contrite heart, and he will save the humble of spirit »; as is said below, in the nineteenth chapter, of little Zacheus: « Make haste and come down, for this day I must abide in thy house »; because he willingly condescends to the humble. Augustine[^p170-5]: « God is high; thou humblest thyself, and he comes to thee; thou exaltest thyself, and he flees from thee ».

19. And note that Zacheus humbled himself in one way, the centurion in another: Zacheus by receiving, the centurion by not daring to receive; for in either way reverence is shown to the Lord. Whence he does well who, on account of reverence, receives the Lord's body frequently in the Sacrament; and he too does well who abstains through[^p170-6] reverence; for to both salvation is given, as to the centurion's servant and to Zacheus himself. So it is to be understood in the matter proposed.

20. *And it came to pass afterwards* etc. After the miracle of the cure of one about to die, there is subjoined here the *miracle of the raising of the dead;* in which the demonstration of the excellence of the Saviour is made more expressly. And for the explicit description of this miracle three things are introduced by the Evangelist. The first is the *moving reason* with respect to the miracle *to be done;* the second is the *efficient cause* with respect to the miracle *in the doing,* and this is noted there[^p170-7]: *Whom when the Lord had seen* etc. The third is the *consequent efficacy* with respect to the miracle *already accomplished,* there: *And he that was dead sat up.*

But the *moving reason* that moved the Lord to raise the dead man marvellously is shown in the letter to be twofold, namely the *instruction of the ignorant* in the intellect and the *solace of the mourning* in the affection. And according to these two, two kinds[^p170-8] are introduced in the first part, namely of *those accompanying on the way,* that they may be directed through his doctrine, and of *those meeting at the gate,* that they may be consoled through his grace.

21. (Vers. 11.). First, then, as to the *accompanying of the multitude to be schooled* it is said: *And it came to pass afterwards, that Jesus*[^p170-9] *went into a city that is called Naim; he went,* namely to teach, according to that saying of Matthew, the fourth chapter: « And Jesus went about all Galilee, teaching in their synagogues ». — And because those who wish to learn accompany the doctor, therefore he adds: *And there went with him his disciples, and a great multitude.* For they knew that to be true which is written in Deuteronomy, the thirty-third chapter[^p170-10]: « They that approach to his feet shall receive of his doctrine »; and that of Proverbs, the thirteenth chapter: « He that walketh with the wise shall become wiser ». And therefore it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the eighth chapter: « Despise not the discourse of the ancients that are wise, and acquaint thyself with their proverbs; for of them thou shalt learn wisdom and doctrine ». And therefore the multitudes accompany the Lord and his disciples, according to that saying of the Psalm[^p170-11]: « Come ye to him, and be enlightened ».

22. (Vers. 12.). Secondly, as to the *meeting of the multitude to be consoled* it is subjoined: *And when he came nigh to the gate of the city, behold a dead man was carried out, the only son of his mother;* over whom there was grief and mourning, because he was *dead,* according to that saying of Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-second chapter[^p170-12]: « Weep for the dead, for his light hath failed ». The mourning was *greater* too, because he was *only begotten,* over whom the lamentation is made the more bitter, as he is the more dear; Jeremias, the sixth chapter: « Make thee the mourning of an only begotten son, a bitter lamentation »; and in the second of Kings, the first chapter, David mourning the dead Jonathan: « As the mother loveth her only son, so did I love thee ». But the mourning was *greatest,* because the mother was altogether desolate; on account of which he says: *And she was a widow,* and so now
<!-- page 171 -->
altogether desolate, lacking the solace both of son and of husband. Which that woman of Thecua alleges to obtain mercy, in the second of Kings, the fourteenth chapter[^p171-1]: « Alas, I am a widow woman ». And therefore Jeremias lamented the widowhood of Jerusalem in Lamentations, the first chapter: « How doth the city sit solitary that was full of people » etc. And therefore many had compassion on her; whence he adds: *And much people of the city were with her,* namely to condole and to console, because this was the custom of the Jews, according to that saying of John, the eleventh chapter[^p171-2]: « Many of the Jews were come to Mary and Martha, to comfort them concerning their brother ». But to all of these this widow could say: « O all ye that pass by the way, attend and see if there be any sorrow like to my sorrow »! a little after: « He hath made me desolate, wasted with sorrow all the day long ». And therefore she needed consolation.

23. (Vers. 13.). *Whom when the Lord had seen* etc. Here is touched the *efficient cause* with respect to the miracle *in the doing,* which indeed was twofold, namely the *affection of piety* in the heart, inclining to commiserate; and the *power of majesty* in speech, commanding to arise. The first regards the assumed nature; but the second, the eternal.

As to the *affection of piety in the heart* it is said: *Whom when the Lord had seen, being moved with mercy*[^p171-3], namely as to the *affection of the heart,* so that he could say that saying of Job, the thirtieth chapter: « I wept heretofore for him that was afflicted »; and the thirty-first chapter: « From my infancy mercy grew up with me ». — And because mercy, lying hid in the *affection,* appears in the *speech, he said*[^p171-4]: *Weep not,* consoling her with a kindly word, according to that saying of Job, the twenty-ninth chapter: « I comforted the heart of the widow »; and Jeremias, the thirty-first chapter: « Let thy voice cease from weeping, and thy eyes from tears ».

24. (Vers. 14.). And because it is fitting that the *effect of work* accompany the *affection of the heart* and the *speech of the mouth,* therefore he adds: *And he came near and touched the bier,* so that he might show to all his kindness, by which he does not despise the dead, though according to the Law one touching a dead man was unclean, as is said in Leviticus, the twenty-first chapter[^p171-5]; and so that he might make the bearers humbly stand still *by touching,* not by commanding. — Whence it is subjoined: *And they that carried it stood still,* namely expecting a miracle, according to what is said to Job himself, the thirty-seventh chapter[^p171-6]: « Hearken to these things, Job; stand, and consider the marvellous works of God ».

25. And as to the *command of majesty in speech* it is subjoined: *And he said: Young man, I say to thee, arise;* to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter[^p171-7]: « Rise, thou that sleepest, and arise from the dead, and Christ shall enlighten thee »; so that thus that saying of John, the fifth chapter, may be verified: « The dead shall hear the voice of the Son of God »; which indeed is a voice commanding and able to raise the dead by reason of its power, according to that saying of the Psalm: « He will give to his voice the voice of power ». And rightly, because, as Augustine[^p171-8] says, « no one rouses a sleeper from sleep so easily as Christ calls back from the sepulchre ». But he directs this speech to the dead man, not only as rousing him, but as repairing his hearing, so that he may show that saying of John, the eighth chapter[^p171-9], to be true: « I am the Beginning, who also speak unto you », the *Beginning,* namely, by working inwardly, *who speak* by rousing outwardly. Whence in Christ, because the flesh is joined to the eternal Word, the *exterior voice* is joined to that *interior speaking* which is the beginning of all *creation,* as is clear in the works of the six days, Genesis, the first chapter[^p171-10]; it is also the beginning of all *reparation;* Wisdom, the sixteenth chapter: « Thy word, O Lord, healeth all things ».

26. (Vers. 15.). *And he that was dead sat up.* Here thirdly is noted the *subsequent efficacy* with respect to the miracle *already accomplished,* which indeed is twofold according to a twofold end, namely of the *work* and of the *intention.* The first efficacy is the *raising of the dead;* the second, the *promotion of faith.*

First, then, the *raising of the dead* is designated as *known to the people standing by,* when he says: *And he that was dead sat up, and began to speak;* in which it appears manifestly that he was living, because life is discerned in two ways, namely by sense and motion. There is no doubt that he was speaking words of divine praise, which indeed belongs not to the dead but to the living, according to that which is said in the Psalm[^p171-11]: « The dead shall not praise thee, O Lord, but we that live bless the Lord ».

27. And note that the raising of the girl is proved by *eating,* Mark, the fifth chapter[^p171-12]; the raising of Lazarus by *walking,* John, the eleventh chapter; the raising of the young man by *speaking,* as here. And *eating* regards the act of the vegetative, *walking* the act of the sensitive, *speaking* the act of the rational, so that by this threefold proof he may be shown the lord of the soul according to its every potency and power.

28. Nor was it only known to the people standing by, but also *welcome to the mother desiring it;* whence he also subjoins: *And he gave
<!-- page 172 -->
him to his mother;* in which he shows that he raised him for the consolation of the mother. Whence she could say that saying of Anna, in the first of Kings, the first chapter[^p172-1]: « I prayed the Lord for this child, and he gave me my petition ». So Elias delivered the boy raised again to his mother, saying, in the third of Kings, the seventeenth chapter: « Behold, thy son liveth »; but that one *delivered,* this one *gave,* because that one was the minister of the raising, but this one the giver of life and of salvation.

29. (Vers. 16.). But secondly there is designated the *promotion of faith* as to the *reverence of the Creator,* when it is said: *And there came a fear on them all, and they magnified God;* the *fear,* namely, of magnifying reverence; Job, the thirty-seventh chapter[^p172-2]: « Men shall fear him, and all that seem to themselves to be wise shall not dare to behold him »; and Isaias, the fifty-ninth chapter: « And they from the west shall fear the name of the Lord, and they from the rising of the sun, his glory ». And so, fearing in heart, they glorified and magnified with the mouth; Ecclesiasticus, the forty-third chapter: « Glorifying him as much as ever you can, he will yet far exceed ». « And who shall magnify him as he is from the beginning »?

30. But faith is not sufficiently promoted to the reverence of the Creator without the *intelligence of the Mediator;* and therefore as to that it is added: *Saying: A great prophet is risen up among us,* that is, Christ the mediator, according to that saying of Deuteronomy, the eighteenth chapter[^p172-3]: « A prophet shall the Lord raise up to you of your brethren like unto me; him you shall hear ». *And, God hath visited his people,* namely through Christ the redeemer, according to that saying of Zachary, above, in the first chapter[^p172-4]: « Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, because he hath visited and wrought the redemption of his people »; and this according to that saying of Genesis, the last chapter: « After my death the Lord will visit you and will bring you out of this place »; which he understands of the visitation through Christ the mediator. — And so it appears from what has been said how he was made manifest as to the excellence of his power.

31. According to the *spiritual intelligence* note that a threefold state is designated in the course of this miracle, namely the state of *justice* in the Lord's company; the state of *injustice* in the dead young man; and the state of *penance* in the same man raised again. — The state of *justice* indeed is designated in Christ and his company. For the state of the just is threefold, namely of *contemplation, prelacy* and *action.* The state of *prelates* is designated in Christ, of whom John, the tenth chapter[^p172-5]: « I am the good shepherd; the good shepherd giveth his life for his sheep »; where in himself he shows the form of right prelacy. — The state of *contemplatives* in the disciples, who are admitted to the mount in the transfiguration of the Lord, according to that saying of Matthew, the seventeenth chapter[^p172-6]: « Jesus took unto him Peter and James and John his brother, and brought them up into a high mountain apart, and he was transfigured before them »; and below, in the tenth chapter: « Turning to his disciples, he said: Blessed are the eyes that see the things which you see »; this is said to the contemplatives. — The state of the *active* is designated in the multitude, which is tossed about in the waves of the world; below, in the tenth chapter[^p172-7]: « Martha, Martha, thou art careful and art troubled about many things »; because, according to what is said in John, the sixteenth chapter, « in the world you shall have distress; but have confidence, I have overcome the world ». — Of this threefold state of *justice* it is said in figure in Ezechiel, the fourteenth chapter[^p172-8]: « If Noe, Daniel and Job shall be in the midst thereof, they indeed shall deliver their own souls » etc. By *Noe* are understood *prelates;* by *Daniel,* to whom was given the intelligence of visions, *contemplatives;* by *Job, the active.*

32. But the state of *fault* is designated in the *dead young man,* whose degree is threefold: the first in *interior affection;* the second, in *exterior operation;* but the third, in *custom* or obstinacy. These three states are designated by the three dead raised by the Lord, both by reason of the *places* and by reason of the *ages.* — For the state of *those sinning in the heart* is designated by the girl dead in the *house,* of whom it is had in Mark, the fifth chapter[^p172-9]. — The state of *those sinning in deed,* by the young man who was found at the *gate of the city,* because the sin had now gone forth and lay open, of whom it is here. — The state of *those sinning by custom* and obstinacy, by Lazarus, lying already in manly age, already four days dead and stinking in the sepulchre, John, the eleventh chapter[^p172-10]. — To these three correspond three dead raised in the old testament, namely the *little boy* by Eliseus, the son of the Sunamitess, in the fourth of Kings, the fourth chapter[^p172-11]; the *young man,* the son of the woman of Sarephta, raised by Elias; in the third of Kings, the seventeenth chapter; and the *man,* by Eliseus dead, in the fourth of Kings, the thirteenth chapter. In these three are designated the three aforesaid states. — In these three can also be designated the three *roots of sins,* because one died from the *languor of sickness,* in which is the concupiscence of the flesh; another from the *heat of the sun*[^p172-12], in which is the covetousness of the world; the third *invisibly,* in which is the pride of life.

But the state of *penance* is designated in the *young man raised again,* of whom it is said that he *sat up and began to speak and was restored to his mother. He sat up* indeed through contrition, which lifts the head from the earth; in the Psalm[^p172-13]: « Rise ye after you have sitten, you that eat the bread of sorrow »; and according to that saying of Jeremias, the fifteenth chapter: « I sat alone, because thou hast filled me with bitterness ». *He began to speak,* through confession; Job,
<!-- page 173 -->
the tenth chapter[^p173-1]: « I will speak in the bitterness of my soul; I will say to God: Do not condemn me »; and the seventh chapter: « I will not spare my mouth, I will speak in the affliction of my spirit, I will talk with the bitterness of my soul ». *He was restored to his mother,* to the Church, through satisfaction, because *to satisfy* is to render what is owed, not to God the Father only, but also to mother Church; in figure of which it is said in the fourth of Kings, the fourth chapter[^p173-2]: « Take this boy and carry him to his mother ». And as the mother willingly receives the son raised again, so joyfully do Christ and the Church receive the penitent sinner; below, in the fifteenth chapter: « It was fit that we should make merry and be glad, because this thy brother was dead and is come to life again ».

[^tr-pietas]: **En.** Translator's note: *pietas* is rendered "piety" throughout, and *pius* "pious" (ruling 12: the six mercy nouns stay six words). Here (¶ 1) the *pietas* is Christ's, God's: the third ground of the Doctor's sublimity is the *affluentia pietatis*, shown *in condonando peccata*, "in pardoning sins"; ¶ 18's *condescensio pietatis divinae* and ¶ 23's *affectus pietatis* in Christ's heart, *inclinans ad miserandum*, are the same. [^tr-corrigenda]: **En.** Translator's note: Printed: *dignitatem* ("worthiness"); corrected by the editors, Corrigenda, t. [^tr-lumen]: **En.** Translator's note: this translation keeps *lux* as "light" and *lumen* as "lumen", except where a verse is quoted as a verse and no surrounding prose leans on the word.
