# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XX, Vers. 41-47
## *Secundo confutatur error circa mysterium incarnationis, de quo tria*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 516 -->

#### Secundo confutatur error circa mysterium incarnationis, de quo tria.

47. (Vers. 41.). *Dixit autem ad illos: Quomodo dicunt* etc. Postquam confutavit errantes circa mysterium resurrectionis, hic *confutat errantes circa mysterium incarnationis*[^p516-9], qui scilicet non credunt, Christum esse verum hominem et Deum. Facit autem hoc Christus quaerendo et introducendo quaestionem, quam impossibile est dissolvere, nisi credatur et intelligatur Christus duplicis esse naturae. In hac igitur quaestione Christus, magister fidei, *proponit* ipsius Christi *generationem carnalem* a Iudaeis communiter creditam; secundo *assumit generationem aeternalem* a Propheta praedicatam; et *concludit apparentem utriusque controversiam.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *generationem Christi carnalem communiter creditam* dicit: *Dixit autem ad illos: Quomodo dicunt, Christum filium esse David?* Hoc communiter dicunt Iudaei omnes et etiam omnes viri christiani. Nam ipsi David facta est de Christo repromissio, sicut in Psalmo[^p516-10] dicitur: « De fructu ventris tui ponam super sedem tuam »; et secundi Regum septimo: « Suscitabo semen tuum post te, quod egredietur de utero tuo, et firmabo regnum eius et stabiliam thronum regni eius usque in sempiternum ». Hoc non potest dici nisi de Christo, Angelo attestante, secundum illud supra primo[^p516-11]: « Dabit illi Dominus Deus sedem David, patris eius, et regnabit in domo Iacob in aeternum ». Ideo recte « clamabant turbae: Hosanna filio David; benedictus qui venit in nomine Domini »! Matthaei vigesimo primo. Et hoc Apostolus praedicabat secundum illud secundae ad Timotheum secundo[^p516-12]: « Memor esto, Dominum Iesum resurrexisse a mortuis ex semine David, secundum Evangelium meum »; et Actuum secundo dixit Petrus: « Propheta ergo cum esset David, et sciret, quod iureiurando iurasset illi Deus, de fructu lumbi eius sedere super sedem eius, providens locutus est de resurrectione ».

48. (Vers. 42.). Secundo quantum ad *generationem aeternalem a Propheta praedicatam* subdit: *Et ipse David dicit in libro Psalmorum: Dixit Dominus domino meo: Sede a dextris meis.* Hoc
<!-- page 517 -->
absque dubio David de verbo ad verbum dicit in Psalmo[^p517-1], qui est de duabus naturis Christi excellentissime et profundissime tractans. Constat enim, quod illud intelligitur de Christo. Nam nulli dedit Deus regnum supremum, sacerdotium aeternum et iudicium universorum nisi Christo; et hoc totum in illo Psalmo describitur. Oportet igitur, quod versus iste de Christo intelligatur; in quo exprimit ipse David, Spiritu sancto afflatus, Filii *aeternam generationem* in hoc quod dicit: « Dixit Dominus Domino meo »; *dicere* enim nihil aliud est quam verbum mente concipere; quod quidem *dicere* non potuit esse in Deo nisi ab aeterno, quia, sicut dicitur Iob trigesimo tertio[^p517-2], « semel loquitur Deus et secundo idipsum non repetit ». Et ideo Ioannis primo: « In principio erat Verbum, et Verbum erat apud Deum, et Deus erat Verbum ». Dixit ergo aeternaliter, cum Filium genuit; Psalmus: « Dominus dixit ad me: Filius meus es tu, ego hodie genui te ». — Exprimit etiam Filii *aequalitatem cum Patre,* cum dicit: « Sede a dextris meis », id est aequalis mihi; ad Hebraeos primo[^p517-3]: « Qui, cum sit splendor gloriae et figura substantiae eius portansque omnia verbo virtutis suae, purgationem peccatorum faciens, sedet ad dexteram Maiestatis in excelsis » etc. usque ibi: « Ego hodie genui te ». Ideo ad Philippenses secundo: « Cum in forma Dei esset, non rapinam arbitratus est, esse se aequalem Deo ». *Sessio* ergo illa non dicit positionem localem, sed potius summam quietem, dignitatem et auctoritatem, ac per hoc et aequalitatem, quae soli Filio competit. Ideo corruit lucifer, qui eam appetiit; Isaiae decimo quarto[^p517-4]: « Quomodo cecidisti, lucifer! qui mane oriebaris, qui dicebas in corde tuo: Super astra Dei exaltabo solium meum et sedebo in monte testamenti et similis ero Altissimo ».

49. (Vers. 43.). Exprimit nihilominus ipsius Filii Dei *imperium universale,* cum addit[^p517-5]: *Donec ponam inimicos tuos scabellum pedum tuorum;* Psalmus: « Omnia subiecisti sub pedibus eius » etc., quia per virtutem omnipotentem et regit bonos et conculcat perversos. Unde primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p517-6]: « Oportet, illum regnare, donec ponat inimicos suos sub pedibus suis »; « omnia enim subiecit sub pedibus eius »; Isaiae quadragesimo quinto: « Haec dicit Dominus Christo meo Cyro, cuius apprehendi dexteram, ut subiiciam omnes gentes et dorsa regum vertam » etc. Nam de Christo dicitur Apocalypsis decimo nono: « Habebat in vestimento et in femore suo scriptum: Rex regum » etc.

50. (Vers. 44.). Tertio quantum ad *utriusque apparentem controversiam* subdit: *David ergo dominum illum vocat, et quomodo filius eius est*[^p517-7]? Impossibile enim est dicere, quod secundum naturam humanam Christus esset dominus David, quia Christus nondum erat natus nec conceptus; et praeterea, nunquam filius dicitur *dominus* patris sui; et iterum, David, cum esset rex excelsus, non videtur, quod aliquem purum hominem dominum habuerit. Restat igitur, quod cum Christus secundum eandem naturam David regis non possit dici simul esse verus *filius* et *dominus,* necesse est, simul ipsum esse Deum et hominem. Et hoc quidem est verum, quod nullus potest inficiari, nisi totaliter infringat Scripturam[^p517-8] et fidem a patribus acceptam.

51. Sunt autem de hac duplici natura Christi multiplicia testimonia in Scriptura, sed magis hoc testimonium accepit, tum quia David inter Prophetas de Christo excellentissime prophetavit, tum etiam, quia Psalmus ille, ex quo versus iste accipitur, excellentissime exprimit totius veritatis fidei sacramentum. Unde et Cassiodorus[^p517-9] de illo Psalmo dicit, quod « est speculum caelestis arcani, armarium sanctarum Scripturarum, ubi totum summatim dicitur, quidquid utriusque testamenti narratione decantatur ». — Nam totius utriusque testamenti intentio principaliter versatur circa Christi utramque naturam, de qua ipse David[^p517-10]: « Descendet sicut pluvia in vellus »; et post: « Sit nomen eius benedictum in saecula »; et iterum: « Speciosus forma prae filiis hominum »; et post: « Sedes tua, Deus, in saeculum saeculi »; et Baruch tertio[^p517-11]: « Hic est Deus noster, et non aestimabitur alius adversus eum ». « Post haec in terris visus est et cum hominibus conversatus est »; et Isaiae septimo: « Ecce, virgo concipiet et pariet filium, et vocabitur nomen eius Emmanuel »; et Isaiae nono: « Parvulus natus est nobis, et filius datus est nobis, et vocabitur nomen eius admirabilis, consiliarius, Deus, fortis » etc. Item, Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p517-12]: « Ascendet sicut virgultum »; et post: « Generationem eius quis enarrabit »? Et Michaeae quinto: « Et tu Bethlehem Ephratha, parvulus es in millibus
<!-- page 518 -->
Iuda; ex te enim egredietur qui sit dominator in Israel; et egressus eius ab initio, a diebus aeternitatis ». Ideo ad Romanos primo[^p518-1]: « Qui factus est ei ex semine David secundum carnem, qui praedestinatus est Filius Dei in virtute, secundum spiritum sanctificationis » etc.; et ad Romanos nono: « Quorum est adoptio filiorum et gloria et testamentum et legislatio et obsequium et promissa, quorum patres, ex quibus est Christus secundum carnem, qui est super omnia benedictus Deus ». Et hoc expresse dicitur Ioannis primo[^p518-2]: « In principio erat Verbum »; et post: « Verbum caro factum est et habitavit in nobis, et vidimus gloriam eius » etc. Postremo ipse Christus, Ioannis octavo[^p518-3]: « Principium, qui et loquor vobis »; et Apocalypsis ultimo: « Ego sum alpha et omega, primus et novissimus, principium et finis ». « Ego sum radix et genus David, stella splendida et matutina ». — In hoc igitur « finem constituit suae doctrinae », sicut dicit Ambrosius[^p518-4]; quia in hoc est *fundamentum* christianae religionis, in quo radicatur et clauditur omne credendum, scilicet quod credatur Christus homo et Deus. Nam omnes articuli vel ad Divinitatem vel ad humanitatem reducuntur; et ideo dicebat Apostolus primae ad Corinthios tertio[^p518-5]: « Ut sapiens architectus fundamentum posui; fundamentum autem aliud nemo potest ponere praeter id quod positum est, quod est Christus Iesus ». Et ipse etiam est *complementum,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici quadragesimo tertio[^p518-6]: « Multa dicimus et deficimus in verbis, consummatio autem sermonum ipse est ».

#### Informatio discipulorum ad prudentiam, de qua tria.

52. *Audiente autem omni populo* etc. Postquam confutavit Pharisaeos errantes ex malitia et Sadducaeos errantes ex ignorantia, hic tertio[^p518-7] *informat discipulos ad prudentiam.* Et quoniam prudentia in tribus consistit, scilicet in *cautela mali, eligentia boni* et *providentia futuri*[^p518-8]; ideo pars ista habet tres partes. In quarum prima informat discipulos ad *cautelam malorum;* in secunda invitat ad *eligentiam meliorum,* ibi[^p518-9]: *Respiciens autem vidit* etc.; in tertia vero, ad *providentiam futurorum,* ibi: *Et quibusdam dicentibus de templo* etc.

#### Informatio ad cautelam quatuor malorum.

*Informat* ergo *discipulos ad cautelam malorum,* quae erant in scribis et Pharisaeis, ne eorum exemplo diverterent a tramite veritatis[^p518-10]; et hoc quidem facit revocando discipulos a quadruplici vitio scribarum, scilicet a *cultu vestium, ambitione honorum, appetitu divitiarum* et *simulatione virtutum.* Primum spectat ad lasciviam, secundum ad vanam gloriam, tertium ad avaritiam, quartum ad fraudulentiam; ad quae quatuor tanquam ad quatuor rotas currus Pharaonis[^p518-11] reduci habent omnia mala.

53. (Vers. 45. 46.). Primo ergo quantum ad *cultum vestium,* qui erat scribarum vitium *manifestum,* dicit: *Audiente autem omni populo, dixit discipulis suis: Attendite a scribis, qui volunt ambulare in stolis.* Ideo autem dixit: *omni populo audiente,* quia hoc erat vitium manifestum, et ideo in manifesto redarguendum, secundum illud primae ad Timotheum quinto[^p518-12]: « Peccantes coram omnibus argue, ut et ceteri timorem habeant »; et hoc ipsis praesentibus dixit, ne faceret contra illud Levitici decimo nono: « Non maledicas surdo » etc. Ideo autem magis de scribis loquitur, quia efficaciores ad nocendum erant, quia videbantur viri fide digni in verbo et imitandi exemplo. Unde Glossa[^p518-13]: « Duplici ratione iubemur attendere a vanae gloriae cupidis, ne vel eorum simulatione decipiamur, vel eorum exemplo ad amorem temporalium inflammemur ». Attendendum est ergo, ne quis eos imitetur in cultu vestium, qui intelligitur in nomine *stolarum.* Dicuntur enim *stolae* camisiae albae et longae, quibus utebantur scribae; unde Glossa[^p518-14] super hoc: « *Volunt ambulare in stolis,* id est cultioribus vestimentis induti in publicum procedere; quod reprehenditur in divite, qui *induebatur purpura et bysso* ». Et ideo Augustinus dicit in Regula[^p518-15]: « Non affectetis vestibus placere, sed moribus »; et Ecclesiastici undecimo: « In vestitu ne glorieris unquam »;
<!-- page 519 -->
et primae ad Timotheum ultimo: « Habentes alimenta et quibus tegamur, his contenti simus ».

54. Secundo quantum ad *ambitionem honorum,* quae erat scribarum vitium *detestandum,* subdit: *Et amant salutationes in foro,* quantum ad gloriam *in verbo,* quasi dicat, quod volunt salutari in loco publico prius tempore, altiori voce et inclinato capite; quocontra supra decimo[^p519-1]: « Neminem per viam salutaveritis » etc. — *Et primas cathedras in synagogis,* quantum ad gloriam *in officio;* quocontra Ecclesiastici septimo[^p519-2]: « Noli quaerere ab homine ducatum neque a rege cathedram honoris ». — *Et primos discubitus in conviviis,* quantum ad gloriam *in signo;* quocontra supra decimo quarto[^p519-3]: « Cum invitatus fueris ad nuptias, recumbe in novissimo loco ». Reprehendit autem scribas, non quia *salutari* sit malum, vel *super cathedram poni,* vel *primo loco in convivio collocari,* sed quia talis *appetitus* et *gloriatio* talis merito debet reprehendi. Unde Beda[^p519-4]: « Non salutari in foro, non primas sedes vetat eis quibus debetur ex ordine officii, sed eis qui indebite amant. Animum enim, non gradum redarguit ». Arguitur ergo in scribis non excellentia dignitatis, sed ambitio honoris. Nam in his veritas locum non habet, secundum illud Ioannis quinto[^p519-5]: « Quomodo potestis vos credere, qui gloriam ab invicem accipitis et gloriam, quae a solo Deo est, non quaeritis »? Et de hac gloria Osee quarto: « Gloriam eorum in ignominiam commutabo ». Et talis debet contemni; Isaiae vigesimo tertio[^p519-6]: « Dominus exercituum cogitavit hoc, detrahere superbiam omnis gloriae et ad ignominiam universos inclytos terrae »; et primi Machabaeorum secundo: « Gloria viri peccatoris stercus et vermis est; hodie extollitur et cras non invenietur ».

55. (Vers. 47.). Tertio quantum ad *appetitum divitiarum* subdit: *Qui devorant domos viduarum*[^p519-7], et hoc per cupiditatem; cuius voracitas nunquam satiatur, et maxime, quando carnalitas ei sociatur. Ideo dicit: *Qui devorant domos viduarum,* simul implicans voracitatem cupiditatis ad domum et carnalitatis ad sexum; et de his duobus Proverbiorum trigesimo[^p519-8]: « Sanguisugae duae sunt filiae dicentes: Affer, affer »; et statim subdit: « Tria sunt insaturabilia et quartum, quod nunquam dicit: Sufficit. Infernus, os vulvae et terra, quae nunquam satiatur aqua; ignis nunquam dicit: Sufficit »; quia, sicut dicitur Ecclesiastae quinto, « avarus non implebitur pecunia, et qui amat divitias fructum non capiet ex eis ». Devoratio igitur reprehensibilis est propter avaritiam, sed conversatio cum viduis reprehensibilis est propter suspicionem malam. Unde Chrysostomus[^p519-9]: « Dum Iudaicos sacerdotes confundit, Christianos monet, non permanere cum viduis amplius quam cum ceteris, quia, etsi voluntas mala non est, suspicio tamen mala est ». Et Hieronymus ad Nepotianum[^p519-10]: « Ostiolum tuum aut raro aut nunquam mulierum pedes terant. Omnes puellas et virgines Christi aut aequaliter ignora, aut aequaliter dilige. Vide, ne sub eodem tecto manseris nec in praeterita castitate confidas. Nec David sanctior nec Samsone fortior nec Salomone sapientior potes esse. Memento semper, quod paradisi colonum mulier de possessione sua eiecerit ». Non sunt igitur scribae imitandi, nec quantum ad carnalitatem nec quantum ad cupiditatem, quia de imitatoribus horum secundae Petri secundo[^p519-11]: « Hi sunt velut irrationabilia pecora », « pellicientes animas instabiles, cor exercitatum avaritia habentes, maledictionis filii, derelinquentes viam rectam erraverunt, secuti viam Balaam ex Bosor, qui mercedem iniquitatis amavit ». Contra tales indignatur Dominus, secundum illud Isaiae tertio[^p519-12]: « Vos depasti estis vineam meam, et rapina pauperis in domo vestra ».

56. Quarto quantum ad *simulationem virtutum* subdit: *Simulantes longam orationem;* Glossa[^p519-13]: « Ut religiosiores appareant, prolixius orant, ut ab infirmis quibuslibet et peccatorum suorum conscientia turbatis quasi patroni laudes et pecunias accipiant ». — Sed nunquid prolixa oratio mala est? Certe non est, cum dicatur supra decimo octavo[^p519-14]: « Oportet orare semper »; et primae ad Thessalonicenses quinto: « Sine intermissione orate ». Sed hoc dicitur propter simulationem, quia, cum simulatio et fraus in omnibus reprehensibilis sit, potissime reprehenditur in his actibus, qui maxime debent fieri in *veritate;* et talis est actus orationis; Ioannis quarto[^p519-15]: « Eos qui adorant eum, in spiritu et veritate adorare oportet ». De illo igitur, qui fraudulenter orat et simulatorie, dicitur in Psalmo: « Oratio
<!-- page 520 -->
eius fiat in peccatum », quia, Ieremiae quadragesimo octavo, « maledictus qui facit opus Dei negligenter ». — Et ideo subdit: *Hi accipient damnationem maiorem,* quia, sicut dicitur Iob trigesimo sexto[^p520-1], « simulatores et callidi provocant iram Dei ». Ideo Ecclesiastici primo: « Ne fueris hypocrita in conspectu hominum », « ne revelet Deus absconsa tua et in medio synagogae elidat te ». Et ideo scribis tanquam hypocritis imprecatur septies vae, Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p520-2]: « Vae vobis, scribae et Pharisaei hypocritae »! propter septiformem simulationem, propter quam digni erant, cum suis sequacibus in aeternam praecipitari damnationem, secundum illud Apocalypsis decimo nono: « Apprehensa est bestia et cum ea pseudopropheta, qui fecit signa coram ipso, quibus seduxit eos qui acceperunt characterem bestiae; et vivi missi sunt hi duo in stagnum ignis ardentis et sulphuris. Et omnes aves saturatae sunt carnibus eorum ».

#### Second, the error concerning the mystery of the incarnation is confuted, concerning which three things.

47. (Vers. 41.). *But he said to them: How say they* etc. After he confuted those erring concerning the mystery of the resurrection, here he *confutes those erring concerning the mystery of the incarnation*[^p516-9], who, namely, do not believe that Christ is true man and God. Now Christ does this by asking and bringing in a question which it is impossible to dissolve unless Christ be believed and understood to be of a twofold nature. In this question, then, Christ, the master of faith, *proposes* the *carnal generation* of Christ himself, commonly believed by the Jews; second, he *takes up the eternal generation* preached by the Prophet; and he *concludes the apparent controversy of both.*

First, then, as to *the carnal generation of Christ commonly believed,* he says: *But he said to them: How say they that Christ is the son of David?* This all the Jews commonly say, and also all Christian men. For to David himself the promise was made concerning Christ, as is said in the Psalm[^p516-10]: « Of the fruit of thy womb I will set upon thy throne »; and the second of Kings, the seventh chapter: « I will raise up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy womb, and I will establish his kingdom, and I will settle the throne of his kingdom for ever ». This cannot be said except of Christ, the Angel bearing witness, according to that above, the first chapter[^p516-11]: « The Lord God shall give unto him the throne of David his father, and he shall reign in the house of Jacob for ever ». Therefore rightly « the multitudes cried: Hosanna to the son of David; blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord »! Matthew, the twenty-first chapter. And this the Apostle preached, according to that of the second to Timothy, the second chapter[^p516-12]: « Be mindful that the Lord Jesus is risen again from the dead, of the seed of David, according to my gospel »; and in the Acts, the second chapter, Peter said: « David therefore, whereas he was a prophet, and knew that God had sworn to him with an oath that of the fruit of his loin one should sit upon his throne, foreseeing it, spoke of the resurrection ».

48. (Vers. 42.). Second, as to *the eternal generation preached by the Prophet,* he subjoins: *And David himself saith in the book of Psalms: The Lord said to my lord: Sit thou on my right hand.* This
<!-- page 517 -->
without doubt David says word for word in the Psalm[^p517-1] which treats most excellently and most profoundly of the two natures of Christ. For it is established that it is understood of Christ. For to none did God give the supreme kingdom, the eternal priesthood and the judgement of all things save to Christ; and this whole is described in that Psalm. It must be, then, that this verse be understood of Christ; in which David himself, breathed upon by the Holy Spirit, expresses the *eternal generation* of the Son in this, that he says: « The Lord said to my Lord »; for *to say* is nothing else than to conceive a word in the mind; which *saying*, indeed, could not be in God save from eternity, because, as is said in Job, the thirty-third chapter[^p517-2], « God speaketh once, and repeateth not the selfsame thing the second time ». And therefore John, the first chapter: « In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God ». He said, then, eternally, when he begot the Son; the Psalm: « The Lord hath said to me: Thou art my son, this day have I begotten thee ». — He expresses also the Son's *equality with the Father,* when he says: « Sit thou on my right hand », that is, equal to me; to the Hebrews, the first chapter[^p517-3]: « Who, being the brightness of glory and the figure of his substance, and upholding all things by the word of his power, making purgation of sins, sitteth on the right hand of the Majesty on high » etc., as far as there: « This day have I begotten thee ». Therefore to the Philippians, the second chapter: « Who, being in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God ». That *sitting*, then, does not signify a local position, but rather the highest rest, dignity and authority, and through this also equality, which belongs to the Son alone. Therefore Lucifer fell, who desired it; Isaias, the fourteenth chapter[^p517-4]: « How art thou fallen, O Lucifer, who didst rise in the morning, who saidst in thy heart: Above the stars of God I will exalt my throne, and I will sit in the mountain of the covenant, and I will be like the Most High ».

49. (Vers. 43.). He expresses no less the *universal empire* of the Son of God himself, when he adds[^p517-5]: *Till I make thy enemies the footstool of thy feet;* the Psalm: « Thou hast subjected all things under his feet » etc., because by almighty power he both rules the good and tramples the perverse. Whence the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter[^p517-6]: « He must reign, until he hath put his enemies under his feet »; « for he hath put all things under his feet »; Isaias, the forty-fifth chapter: « Thus saith the Lord to my Christ Cyrus, whose right hand I have taken hold of, that I may subdue all nations and turn the backs of kings » etc. For of Christ it is said in the Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter: « He had on his garment and on his thigh written: King of kings » etc.

50. (Vers. 44.). Third, as to *the apparent controversy of both,* he subjoins: *David then calleth him lord, and how is he his son*[^p517-7]? For it is impossible to say that according to human nature Christ was the lord of David, because Christ was not yet born nor conceived; and moreover, a son is never called the *lord* of his father; and again, David, since he was a lofty king, does not seem to have had any mere man as lord. It remains, then, that since Christ according to the same nature cannot be said to be at once the true *son* and *lord* of King David, it is necessary that he be at once God and man. And this indeed is the truth, which no one can deny unless he wholly break the Scripture[^p517-8] and the faith received from the fathers.

51. Now there are manifold testimonies in Scripture concerning this twofold nature of Christ, but he took this testimony rather, both because David among the Prophets prophesied most excellently of Christ, and also because that Psalm, from which this verse is taken, most excellently expresses the sacrament of the whole truth of the faith. Whence Cassiodorus too[^p517-9] says of that Psalm that « it is a mirror of the heavenly secret, the book-press of the holy Scriptures, where all is said in sum that is sung in the narration of both testaments ». — For the intention of the whole of both testaments is principally concerned with Christ's two natures, of which David himself[^p517-10]: « He shall come down like rain upon the fleece »; and after: « Let his name be blessed for ever »; and again: « Beautiful in form above the sons of men »; and after: « Thy throne, O God, is for ever and ever »; and Baruch, the third chapter[^p517-11]: « This is our God, and there shall no other be accounted of in comparison of him ». « Afterwards he was seen upon earth, and conversed with men »; and Isaias, the seventh chapter: « Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and his name shall be called Emmanuel »; and Isaias, the ninth chapter: « A child is born to us, and a son is given to us, and his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, God, Mighty » etc. Likewise, Isaias, the fifty-third chapter[^p517-12]: « He shall grow up as a tender plant »; and after: « Who shall declare his generation »? And Micheas, the fifth chapter: « And thou, Bethlehem Ephrata, art a little one among the thousands
<!-- page 518 -->
of Juda; for out of thee shall he come forth that is to be the ruler in Israel; and his going forth is from the beginning, from the days of eternity ». Therefore to the Romans, the first chapter[^p518-1]: « Who was made to him of the seed of David according to the flesh, who was predestinated the Son of God in power, according to the spirit of sanctification » etc.; and to the Romans, the ninth chapter: « Whose is the adoption of sons, and the glory, and the testament, and the giving of the law, and the service, and the promises; whose are the fathers, of whom is Christ according to the flesh, who is over all things, God blessed ». And this is said expressly in John, the first chapter[^p518-2]: « In the beginning was the Word »; and after: « The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, and we saw his glory » etc. Lastly Christ himself, John, the eighth chapter[^p518-3]: « The beginning, who also speak unto you »; and the Apocalypse, the last chapter: « I am Alpha and Omega, the first and the last, the beginning and the end ». « I am the root and stock of David, the bright and morning star ». — In this, then, « he set the end of his teaching », as Ambrose says[^p518-4]; because in this is the *foundation* of the Christian religion, in which everything to be believed is rooted and enclosed, namely, that Christ be believed man and God. For all the articles are reduced either to the Divinity or to the humanity; and therefore the Apostle said, the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p518-5]: « As a wise architect I have laid the foundation; for other foundation no man can lay but that which is laid, which is Christ Jesus ». And he himself is also the *completion,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the forty-third chapter[^p518-6]: « We say many things, and we fail in words; but the consummation of words is he ».

#### The formation of the disciples unto prudence, concerning which three things.

52. *And in the hearing of all the people* etc. After he confuted the Pharisees erring out of malice and the Sadducees erring out of ignorance, here third[^p518-7] he *forms the disciples unto prudence.* And since prudence consists in three things, namely in *caution of evil, choosing of good* and *foresight of the future*[^p518-8]; therefore this part has three parts. In the first of them he forms the disciples unto *caution of evils;* in the second he invites them to *the choosing of the better things,* there[^p518-9]: *And looking on, he saw* etc.; in the third, however, to *foresight of things to come,* there: *And some saying of the temple* etc.

#### The formation unto caution of four evils.

*He forms,* then, *the disciples unto caution of the evils* that were in the scribes and Pharisees, lest by their example they should turn aside from the path of truth[^p518-10]; and this indeed he does by calling the disciples back from the fourfold vice of the scribes, namely from *the adornment of garments, the ambition of honours, the appetite for riches* and *the feigning of virtues.* The first pertains to wantonness, the second to vainglory, the third to avarice, the fourth to fraudulence; to which four, as to the four wheels of Pharao's chariot[^p518-11], all evils are to be reduced.

53. (Vers. 45. 46.). First, then, as to *the adornment of garments,* which was a *manifest* vice of the scribes, he says: *And in the hearing of all the people he said to his disciples: Beware of the scribes, who desire to walk in robes.* Now he said *in the hearing of all the people* for this reason, because this was a manifest vice, and therefore to be convicted openly, according to that of the first to Timothy, the fifth chapter[^p518-12]: « Them that sin reprove before all, that the rest also may have fear »; and he said this with themselves present, lest he should act against that of Leviticus, the nineteenth chapter: « Thou shalt not speak evil of the deaf » etc. And he speaks rather of the scribes for this reason, because they were more effective for doing harm, because they seemed men worthy of credit in word and to be imitated in example. Whence the Gloss[^p518-13]: « For a twofold reason we are bidden to beware of those desirous of vainglory: lest we be either deceived by their feigning, or by their example inflamed to the love of temporal things ». Heed must be taken, then, lest any one imitate them in the adornment of garments, which is understood under the name of *robes.* For *robes* are what the white and long shirts were called which the scribes used; whence the Gloss[^p518-14] upon this: « *They desire to walk in robes,* that is, to go forth in public clothed in more elegant garments; which is reprehended in the rich man, who *was clothed in purple and fine linen* ». And therefore Augustine says in the Rule[^p518-15]: « Do not aim to please by garments, but by manners »; and Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter: « Glory not in apparel at any time »;
<!-- page 519 -->
and the first to Timothy, the last chapter: « Having food, and wherewith to be covered, with these we are content ».

54. Second, as to *the ambition of honours,* which was a vice of the scribes *to be detested,* he subjoins: *And love salutations in the marketplace,* as to glory *in word,* as if to say that they wish to be saluted in a public place first in time, with a louder voice and with bowed head; against which, above, the tenth chapter[^p519-1]: « Salute no man by the way » etc. — *And the first chairs in the synagogues,* as to glory *in office;* against which Ecclesiasticus, the seventh chapter[^p519-2]: « Seek not of man a leadership, nor of the king the seat of honour ». — *And the chief rooms at feasts,* as to glory *in sign;* against which, above, the fourteenth chapter[^p519-3]: « When thou art invited to a wedding, sit down in the lowest place ». Now he reprehends the scribes, not because *to be saluted* is evil, or *to be set upon a chair,* or *to be placed in the first place at a feast,* but because such an *appetite* and such a *glorying* deservedly ought to be reprehended. Whence Bede[^p519-4]: « He does not forbid being saluted in the marketplace, nor the first seats, to those to whom they are due by the order of their office, but to those who love them unduly. For he convicts the mind, not the rank ». What is reproved, then, in the scribes is not the excellence of dignity, but the ambition of honour. For in these truth has no place, according to that of John, the fifth chapter[^p519-5]: « How can you believe, who receive glory one from another, and the glory which is from God alone you do not seek »? And of this glory Osee, the fourth chapter: « I will change their glory into ignominy ». And such glory ought to be despised; Isaias, the twenty-third chapter[^p519-6]: « The Lord of hosts hath designed this, to pull down the pride of all glory, and to bring to ignominy all the glorious ones of the earth »; and the first of Machabees, the second chapter: « The glory of a sinful man is dung and worms; today he is lifted up, and tomorrow he shall not be found ».

55. (Vers. 47.). Third, as to *the appetite for riches,* he subjoins: *Who devour the houses of widows*[^p519-7], and this through cupidity, whose voracity is never sated, and most of all when carnality is joined to it. Therefore he says: *Who devour the houses of widows,* at once implying the voracity of cupidity toward the house and of carnality toward the sex; and of these two, Proverbs, the thirtieth chapter[^p519-8]: « The horseleech hath two daughters that say: Bring, bring »; and straightway he subjoins: « Three things are insatiable, and a fourth, which never saith: It is enough: hell, and the mouth of the womb, and the earth which is never satisfied with water; the fire never saith: It is enough »; because, as is said in Ecclesiastes, the fifth chapter, « the avaricious man shall not be filled with money, and he that loveth riches shall reap no fruit from them ». The devouring, then, is reprehensible on account of avarice, but the conversing with widows is reprehensible on account of evil suspicion. Whence Chrysostom[^p519-9]: « While he confounds the Jewish priests, he warns Christians not to remain with widows more than with others, because, even if the will is not evil, yet the suspicion is evil ». And Jerome to Nepotian[^p519-10]: « Let the feet of women seldom or never wear your little door. All girls and virgins of Christ either ignore equally or love equally. See that you do not stay under the same roof, nor trust in past chastity. You cannot be holier than David, nor stronger than Samson, nor wiser than Solomon. Remember always that a woman cast the tiller of paradise out of his possession ». The scribes, then, are not to be imitated, neither as to carnality nor as to cupidity, because of the imitators of these, the second of Peter, the second chapter[^p519-11]: « These are as irrational beasts », « alluring unstable souls, having a heart exercised with avarice, children of malediction; leaving the right way they have gone astray, having followed the way of Balaam of Bosor, who loved the wages of iniquity ». Against such the Lord is indignant, according to that of Isaias, the third chapter[^p519-12]: « You have eaten up my vineyard, and the spoil of the poor is in your house ».

56. Fourth, as to *the feigning of virtues,* he subjoins: *Feigning long prayer;* the Gloss[^p519-13]: « That they may appear more religious, they pray at greater length, so that from any of the weak and from those troubled by the conscience of their sins they may receive praises and moneys as patrons ». — But is long prayer evil? Certainly it is not, since it is said above, the eighteenth chapter[^p519-14]: « We ought always to pray »; and the first to the Thessalonians, the fifth chapter: « Pray without ceasing ». But this is said on account of the feigning, because, since feigning and fraud are reprehensible in all things, they are most of all reprehended in those acts which ought most of all to be done in *truth;* and such is the act of prayer; John, the fourth chapter[^p519-15]: « They that adore him must adore him in spirit and in truth ». Of him, then, who prays fraudulently and feignedly, it is said in the Psalm: « May his prayer
<!-- page 520 -->
be turned to sin », because, Jeremias, the forty-eighth chapter, « cursed is he that doth the work of God negligently ». — And therefore he subjoins: *These shall receive greater damnation,* because, as is said in Job, the thirty-sixth chapter[^p520-1], « dissemblers and crafty men provoke the wrath of God ». Therefore Ecclesiasticus, the first chapter: « Be not a hypocrite in the sight of men », « lest God discover thy secrets, and cast thee down in the midst of the congregation ». And therefore he imprecates on the scribes, as hypocrites, woe seven times, Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p520-2]: « Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites »! on account of the sevenfold feigning, on account of which they were worthy, with their followers, to be cast headlong into eternal damnation, according to that of the Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter: « The beast was taken, and with it the false prophet, who wrought signs before him, by which he seduced them who received the character of the beast; and these two were cast alive into the pool of fire burning with brimstone. And all the birds were filled with their flesh ».
