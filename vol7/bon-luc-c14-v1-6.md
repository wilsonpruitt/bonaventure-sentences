# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XIV, Vers. 1-6
## *Primo confutantur Pharisaei observantes, de quo tria*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 358 -->

### Capitulum XIV.

#### Confutatio calumniantium miracula, quae fit in convivio, de qua tria.

1. *Et factum est, cum intraret*[^p358-6] *in domum* etc. Supra confutavit calumniantes Christi miracula in concilio, hic aperte confutat eos *in convivio.* In his enim duobus locis consueverunt potissime detractiones fieri et observationes, scilicet in conciliis et in conviviis[^p358-7]. Dividitur autem pars ista, quae continet praesens capitulum, in tres partes. In quarum prima *confutat Pharisaeos observantes;* in secunda *informat convivas discumbentes,* ibi[^p358-8]: *Dicebat autem et ad invitatos;* in tertia vero *erudit turbas subsequentes,* ibi: *Ibant autem et turbae multae cum eo.*

#### Primo confutantur Pharisaei observantes, de quo tria.

Circa autem *confutationem observantium* hoc ordine procedit: quia primo ponitur *observatio Pharisaeorum;* secundo adiungitur *detectio observatorum,* ibi[^p358-9]: *Et ecce, homo quidam hydropicus;* tertio vero, *detectorum confutatio,* ibi: *Ipse vero apprehensum sanavit eum.* — *Observatio* autem *Pharisaeorum,* qua[^p358-10] observabant Christum, describitur quantum ad duo, scilicet quantum ad *benignitatem Christi* et *Pharisaeorum malignitatem.*

2. (Vers. 1.). Quantum ergo ad Christi benignitatem dicit: *Et factum est, cum intraret*[^p358-11] *in domum principis Pharisaeorum Sabbato manducare panem,* in quo apparet mira Christi benignitas: *magna* in hoc, quod conversabatur cum hominibus mortalibus, cum esset Deus; Baruch tertio[^p358-12]: « Hic est Deus noster, et non aestimabitur alius ad eum ». « Post haec in terris visus est et cum hominibus conversatus est ». *Maior* quidem, quia conversabatur cum suis persecutoribus; unde impletum est in eo illud Ezechielis secundo[^p358-13]: « Fili hominis, increduli et subversores sunt tecum, et cum scorpionibus habitas ». Sed *maxima* benignitas, quia conversabatur usque ad familiare contubernium, ut impleatur illud Apocalypsis tertio: « Ego sto ad ostium et pulso; si quis aperuerit mihi, intrabo ad illum et coenabo cum illo, et ipse mecum ». In hoc igitur, quod *intravit domum alienam,* commendatur Christi *humilitas;* in hoc, quod in *domum Pharisaei,* caritas; in hoc, quod *manducavit panem alienum,* ipsius Christi *paupertas;* et in his ostenditur *summa benignitas,* qua *altissimus* voluit pro nobis humiliari, *iustissimus* cum impiis conversari, *ditissimus* inter homines pauper fieri; unde secundae ad Corinthios octavo[^p358-14]: « Scitis gratiam Domini nostri Iesu Christi,
<!-- page 359 -->
quia, cum dives esset, propter nos egenus factus est, ut illius inopia nos divites essemus ».

3. Secundo autem quantum ad *observantium malignitatem* subdit: *Et ipsi observabant eum.* Non dicit Evangelista *in quo,* ut insinuet, quod observabant et *verba* et *miracula,* et observabant ad reprehendendum et arguendum; supra undecimo[^p359-1]: « Coeperunt Pharisaei et legisperiti insistere et os eius opprimere, insidiantes ei et quaerentes capere aliquid ex ore eius, ut accusarent eum »; unde et Ioannis decimo quinto: « Si me persecuti sunt, et vos persequentur; si sermonem meum servaverunt, et vestrum servabunt ». Observabant etiam *miracula,* secundum quod dicitur Marci tertio[^p359-2]: « Observabant eum, si Sabbato curaret, ut accusarent eum ». Unde haec observatio procedebat ex *malitia insidiante;* contra quam dicitur Proverbiorum vigesimo quarto: « Ne insidieris et quaeras impietatem in domo iusti neque vastes requiem eius ». Sed quia difficillimum est homini malitioso declinare proximi observationem, ideo dicitur Proverbiorum vigesimo tertio[^p359-3]: « Ne comedas cum homine invido et ne desideres cibos eius, quoniam in similitudinem arioli et coniectoris aestimat quod ignorat. Comede et bibe, dicet tibi; mens eius non est tecum. Cibos, quos comederas, evomes et perdes pulcros sermones tuos ».

4. (Vers. 2.). *Et ecce, homo quidam*[^p359-4] *hydropicus.* Post descriptam observationem Pharisaeorum hic secundo describit *detectionem observatorum;* circa quam introducuntur tria, scilicet *aegritudo ad detectionem disponens, quaestio detegens* et *simulatio tegens.*

Primo ergo quantum ad *aegritudinem disponentem ad observatorum detectionem* dicit: *Et ecce, homo quidam hydropicus erat ante illum.* Ad litteram hic *hydropicus* sua infirmitate manifesta[^p359-5] et gravi viam praebebat ad confutandam et detegendam Iudaeorum perfidiam. Et quoniam tempus et locus se offerebat, ideo dicit: *Et ecce,* quasi Dominus tunc adduxisset eum in medium; unde Ecclesiastici trigesimo nono[^p359-6]: « Opera omnis carnis coram illo, et non est quidquam absconditum ab oculis eius ». « Nec est dicere: Quid est hoc, aut quid est istud? Omnia enim in tempore suo quaerentur »; et post: « Omnia opera Domini bona, et omne opus sua hora subministrabit ». Unde haec aegritudo corporalis valebat ad impugnandam et expurgandam aegritudinem spiritualem, quia directe erat illi contraria[^p359-7]. Nam hic homo hydropicus erat ante Iesum ad misericordiam implorandam, quam Pharisaei impugnabant. Implorabat namque misericordiam Dei, quia erat *homo;* nam Ecclesiastici decimo octavo[^p359-8]: « Misericordia Dei super omnem carnem »; et quia *homo infirmus,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici undecimo: « Est homo marcidus et indigens recuperatione, plus deficiens virtute, et oculus Dei respexit illum in bono »; et quia *ante illum* erat, quia tales recurrunt ad Deum, et talibus condescendit Deus; Psalmus[^p359-9]: « Quoniam prospexit de excelso sancto suo, Dominus de caelo in terram aspexit ».

5. (Vers. 3.). Secundo quantum ad *quaestionem detegentem* subdit: *Et respondens Iesus dixit ad legisperitos et Pharisaeos.* Signanter dicit *respondens,* cum nullus prius aliquid quaereret, quia respondebat ad eorum *cogitationes,* quas videbat et audiebat, secundum illud Matthaei nono[^p359-10]: « Cum vidisset Iesus cogitationes eorum, dixit: Ut quid cogitatis mala in cordibus vestris »? Unde Glossa: « *Respondens Iesus,* insidiosis observatoribus », quia dicitur Sapientiae primo: « Auris zeli audit omnia, et tumultus murmurationum non abscondetur ». Et ideo respondet cogitationibus faciendo illis quaestionem, quam et ipsi cogitabant, cum subdit: *Dicens: Si licet Sabbato curare?* — Hanc quaestionem leguntur Pharisaei Domino fecisse, sicut legitur Matthaei duodecimo[^p359-11]: « Interrogabant, si licet Sabbato curare, ut accusarent eum ». Sed Lucas exprimit illorum cogitatus, Matthaeus exprimit verbum. Hoc quaerebat Dominus ab eis, quia ipsi fatebantur se legisperitos; unde Glossa[^p359-12]: « A legisperitis iudicium Legis quaerit ». Propter quod Malachiae secundo: « Labia sacerdotis custodiunt scientiam, et legem requirunt de ore eius »; et Glossa ibidem: « Si sacerdos de lege interrogatur, doceat; alioquin frustra iactat dignitatem, cuius non exhibet operationem ».

6. (Vers. 4.). Tertio quantum ad *simulationem tegentem* subdit: *At illi tacuerunt.* Ideo tacuerunt,
<!-- page 360 -->
quia, videntes se deprehensos et detectos, timebant sua responsione confundi; unde Glossa Bedae[^p360-1]: « Merito tacent qui contra se dictum, quidquid dixerint, vident. Si enim licet, cur *observant?* Si non licet, cur *pecora curant* »? Unde ipsi tacendo simulabant ignorantiam, ut cooperirent malitiam. Simile habetur Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p360-2], ubi, cum quaesisset Dominus de baptismo Ioannis, unde erat, noluerunt respondere, ne convincerentur de incredulitate. Unde poterat de istis dici illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo septimo: « O praesumtio nequissima! unde creata es cooperire aridam malitiam »? Quando autem istis deficiebant folia verborum, recurrebant ad ignorantiae tenebras et defectum verborum; unde tacebant non ex prudentia, sed ex ignorantia, quae habet adiunctam malitiam; unde Ecclesiastici vigesimo[^p360-3]: « Est tacens, non habens sensum loquelae; et est tacens, sciens tempus apti temporis ».

7. *Ipse vero apprehensum* etc. Post detectionem observatorum subditur hic *confutatio detectorum*[^p360-4], quam describit Evangelista tripliciter, scilicet quantum ad *magnificentiam facti, efficaciam verbi, evidentiam signi.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *magnificentiam facti* dicit: *Ipse vero apprehensum sanavit eum ac dimisit.* In hoc, quod *apprehendit,* apparet eius *humilitas,* quia non dedignatur tangere infirmitates ad docendam humilitatem; propter quod dicit Apostolus ad Hebraeos secundo[^p360-5]: « Nusquam Angelos apprehendit, sed semen Abrahae ». In hoc, quod *sanavit,* apparet *virtuositas;* unde supra sexto: « Virtus de illo exibat et sanabat omnes ». In hoc, quod *dimisit,* apparet *liberalitas,* scilicet abire liberum. Non enim redegit in servitutem propter collatum beneficium, sed dimisit abire liberum; Glossa[^p360-6]: « *Dimisit* eum abire corporaliter sanatum, ut se transferret ad salutem animarum ». Unde supra octavo ad illum daemoniacum, quem sanaverat, dixit: « Vade in domum tuam et narra, quanta tibi fecerit Deus ». Ac per hoc Christus in modo faciendi illud miraculum per *humilitatem* confutat Iudaeorum superbiam; per *virtutem,* eorum desidiam; per *liberalitatem* et *benignitatem,* eorum perfidiam, cum ipsi nec dignarentur tangere nec possent curare nec vellent liberare.

8. (Vers. 5.). Secundo quantum ad *efficaciam verbi* subdit: *Et respondens ad illos dixit: Cuius vestrum asinus aut bos in puteum cadet,* scilicet per casualem eventum, quia haec temporalium possessio multis modis potest periclitari et amitti: aliquando ab *interiori,* per propriam mortem, sicut tota die videmus; aliquando ab *exteriori,* per alienam tyrannidem, sicut Iob primo[^p360-7]: « Boves arabant, et asinae pascebantur, et irruerunt Sabaei et tulerunt omnia »; aliquando a *superiori,* per pestilentiam; Exodi nono: « Ecce, manus mea erit super equos tuos et asinos et camelos et boves, pestis valde gravis »; aliquando ab *inferiori,* per ruinam, sicut hic. Unde non sunt haec multum amanda, quae possunt tot modis amitti[^p360-8].

9. Et tamen istas possessiones diligebant multum Pharisaei; unde Glossa[^p360-9]: « Omnes in hac avaritia similes estis »; amor enim bovis et asini faciebat eos non curare de observantia Sabbati. Ideo subdit: *Et non continuo extrahet illum die Sabbati?* per velocem succursum; Glossa[^p360-10]: « Non animali, sed suae avaritiae consulens ». Et hoc quidem reputabant se facere sine offensione Legis. Si ergo salus hominis praeferenda est saluti asini et bovis, manifestum est, in curatione hominis Sabbatum non solvi; et est argumentum *a maiori.* Magis enim videtur, quod Sabbatum debeat solvi per opus magis *servile,* quia dicitur Levitici vigesimo tertio[^p360-11]: « Omne opus servile non facietis in eo »; sed magis est servile opus eruere asinum aut bovem quam sanare hominem. Si ergo ille non solvit Sabbatum, qui bovem de fovea extrahit, multo fortius nec ille qui hominem sanat. Nam si opus *avaritiae* non solvit, ergo nec opus *misericordiae,* cum opus avaritiae sit servitutis, opus misericordiae liberalitatis.

10. (Vers. 6.). Tertio quantum ad *evidentiam signi* subdit: *Et non poterant ad haec respondere illi;* Glossa[^p360-12]: « Convicti ». Tunc enim est evidens signum, quod homo sit convictus, quando deficit via aliqua ad fugiendum. Nec mirum, si non poterant respondere sapientiae Christi, cum nec sapientissimi possent resistere eius discipulis; unde infra vigesimo primo[^p360-13]: « Ego dabo vobis os et sapientiam, cui non poterunt resistere omnes adversarii vestri ». Et hoc impletum est in Stephano, de quo Actuum sexto:
<!-- page 361 -->
« Surrexerunt autem quidam de synagoga, quae appellabatur Libertinorum et Cyrenensium et Alexandrinorum, et eorum qui erant a Cilicia et Asia, disputantes cum Stephano; et non poterant resistere sapientiae et spiritui, qui loquebatur ».

11. His autem secundum litteram pertractatis, tria nobis consideranda occurrunt secundum *spiritualem intelligentiam;* quae secundum Sanctos elici possunt ex verbis praemissis. — Primum est *morbus hydropisis,* cuius proprietas est, sicut dicit Glossa[^p361-1], quod « quanto plus bibit, tanto plus sitit »; et in hoc designat *omnem concupiscentiam,* quae nunquam satiari potest, et maxime *avaritiam,* secundum illud Proverbiorum trigesimo: « Ignis nunquam dicit: Sufficit ». Unde notandum, quod septem, secundum expositores[^p361-2], sunt accidentia hydropisis. — Primum est *tumor corporis,* et per hoc *superbia* intelligitur; Deuteronomii decimo septimo[^p361-3]: « Cunctus populus audiens timebit, ut nullus deinceps intumescat superbia » etc. — Secundum est *compressio spiritualium,* et in hoc intelligitur *invidia,* quae spiritualia comprimit; Proverbiorum decimo quarto[^p361-4]: « Putredo ossium, invidia ». — Tertium est *foetor in anhelitu,* per quod intelligitur *iracundia,* quae facit prorumpere in verba contumeliosa; Psalmus[^p361-5]: « Sepulcrum patens est guttur eorum, linguis suis dolose agebant ». — Quartum est *pigrities pedum,* per quod intelligitur *accidia;* ad Titum primo[^p361-6]: « Cretenses semper mendaces, malae bestiae, ventres pigri »; et Sapientiae decimo quinto: « Pedes eorum pigri ad ambulandum ». — Quintum est *sitis in appetitu,* per quod *avaritia* intelligitur; Proverbiorum trigesimo[^p361-7]: « Terra non satiatur aqua », id est homo terrenus temporali opulentia; et Ecclesiastae quinto: « Avarus non implebitur pecunia ». — Sextum est *inflatio genitalium,* per quod *luxuria* designatur; unde in Psalmo[^p361-8]: « Quoniam lumbi mei impleti sunt illusionibus, et non est sanitas in carne mea ». — Septimum est *infectio cutis* vel partium exteriorum, in quo intelligitur *gula,* quae tota est circa cutem curandam; ad Philippenses tertio[^p361-9]: « Quorum Deus venter est, et gloria in confusione ipsorum, qui terrena sapiunt ».

12. Secundum autem, quod considerare debemus, est *opus virtutis,* in quo intelligitur *curatio spiritualis aegritudinis.* Circa quod tria dicuntur, scilicet quod *apprehendit,* quod *sanavit,* quod *dimisit. Apprehendit,* scilicet per *infusionem gratiae;* Isaiae quadragesimo secundo[^p361-10]: « Ego Dominus vocavi te in iustitia, apprehendi manum tuam et servavi te »; Psalmus: « Emitte manum tuam de alto, eripe me et libera me de aquis multis » etc. — *Sanavit* autem per *expiationem culpae;* Psalmus[^p361-11]: « Qui propitiatur omnibus iniquitatibus tuis, qui sanat omnes infirmitates tuas »; et Matthaei primo: « Ipse salvum faciet populum suum a peccatis eorum ». — *Dimisit* vero per *relaxationem poenae;* Matthaei decimo octavo[^p361-12]: « Misertus dominus servi illius, dimisit eum et debitum dimisit ei ». Et hoc petimus in oratione dominica: « Dimitte nobis debita nostra » etc.

13. Tertium autem, quod considerandum est, est *instructionis exemplum,* in quo similitudinem ponit de *bove* et *asino* ruentibus in puteum, qui in Sabbato extrahuntur. Per *bovem* et *asinum* intelligitur *uterque populus,* scilicet gentilis et Iudaicus, secundum illud Isaiae primo[^p361-13]: « Cognovit bos possessorem suum, et asinus praesepe domini sui ». Nam sic exponit Gregorius. — Vel per *bovem* et *asinum* intelligitur vir *sapiens* et *stultus;* unde Glossa super illud Deuteronomii vigesimo secundo[^p361-14]: « *Non arabis in bove simul et asino,* id est, fatuum sapienti in praedicatione non sociabis, ut aequali potestate verbum Dei annuntient ». Hi ergo *cadunt in puteum* concupiscentiae per originale peccatum, et deinde in limbum infernalem; et universaliter hoc ante Christi adventum. Propter quod Psalmus[^p361-15]: « Non me demergat tempestas aquae, neque absorbeat me profundum, neque urgeat super me puteus os suum ». Hinc ergo Christus *extrahit die Sabbati,* id est septima die, quae est dies sepulturae Christi, in qua quies incipit animarum, secundum illud Zachariae nono[^p361-16]: « Tu quoque in sanguine testamenti tui emisisti vinctos tuos de lacu, in quo non est aqua ».

## English
<!-- page 358 -->

### Chapter XIV.

#### The confutation of those who calumniate the miracles, which is made at the banquet, concerning which three things.

1. *And it came to pass, when he went*[^p358-6] *into the house* etc. Above he confuted in the council those who calumniate the miracles of Christ; here he openly confutes them *at the banquet.* For in these two places detractions and watchings are most of all wont to be made, namely in councils and at banquets[^p358-7]. Now this part, which contains the present chapter, is divided into three parts. In the first of which he *confutes the watching Pharisees;* in the second he *forms the guests sitting at table,* there[^p358-8]: *And he spoke a parable also to them that were invited;* but in the third he *schools the crowds following,* there: *And there went great multitudes with him.*

#### First the watching Pharisees are confuted, concerning which three things.

Now concerning *the confutation of the watchers* he proceeds in this order: for first is set down *the watching of the Pharisees;* secondly is joined *the detection of the watchers,* there[^p358-9]: *And behold, there was a certain man that had the dropsy;* but thirdly, *the confutation of the detected,* there: *But he, taking hold of him, healed him.* — Now *the watching of the Pharisees,* by which[^p358-10] they watched Christ, is described as to two things, namely as to *the kindness of Christ* and *the malignity of the Pharisees.*

2. (Vers. 1.). As to the kindness of Christ, then, he says: *And it came to pass, when he went*[^p358-11] *into the house of a chief of the Pharisees, on the sabbath day, to eat bread,* in which there appears the wonderful kindness of Christ: *great* in this, that he conversed with mortal men, though he was God; Baruch, the third chapter[^p358-12]: « This is our God, and there shall no other be accounted of in comparison of him ». « Afterwards he was seen upon earth, and conversed with men ». *Greater* indeed, because he conversed with his persecutors; whence there was fulfilled in him that of Ezechiel, the second chapter[^p358-13]: « Son of man, unbelievers and destroyers are with thee, and thou dwellest with scorpions ». But the *greatest* kindness, because he conversed even to familiar companionship, that there might be fulfilled that of the Apocalypse, the third chapter: « I stand at the gate, and knock; if any man shall open to me, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me ». In this, then, that *he entered another's house,* the *humility* of Christ is commended; in this, that he entered *the house of a Pharisee,* his charity; in this, that *he ate another's bread,* the *poverty* of Christ himself; and in these is shown *the highest kindness,* by which *the most high* willed to be humbled for us, *the most just* to converse with the impious, *the most rich* to become poor among men; whence the second to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter[^p358-14]: « You know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ,
<!-- page 359 -->
that being rich he became needy for our sakes, that through his want we might be rich ».

3. But secondly, as to *the malignity of the watchers,* he adds: *And they watched him.* The Evangelist does not say *in what,* to insinuate that they watched both his *words* and his *miracles,* and watched them in order to reprehend and to reprove; above, the eleventh chapter[^p359-1]: « The Pharisees and the lawyers began violently to urge him, and to oppress his mouth, lying in wait for him, and seeking to catch something from his mouth, that they might accuse him »; whence also John, the fifteenth chapter: « If they have persecuted me, they will also persecute you; if they have kept my word, they will keep yours also ». They watched his *miracles* too, according to what is said in Mark, the third chapter[^p359-2]: « They watched him whether he would heal on the sabbath, that they might accuse him ». Whence this watching proceeded from *malice lying in wait;* against which it is said in Proverbs, the twenty-fourth chapter: « Lie not in wait, nor seek after wickedness in the house of the just, nor spoil his rest ». But because it is most difficult for a malicious man to avoid his neighbour's watching, therefore it is said in Proverbs, the twenty-third chapter[^p359-3]: « Eat not with an envious man, and desire not his meats, because like a soothsayer and diviner he thinketh that which he knoweth not. Eat and drink, will he say to thee; and his mind is not with thee. The meats which thou hadst eaten, thou shalt vomit up, and shalt lose thy beautiful words ».

4. (Vers. 2.). *And behold, there was a certain man*[^p359-4] *that had the dropsy.* After the watching of the Pharisees has been described, here secondly he describes *the detection of the watchers;* concerning which three things are brought in, namely *the sickness disposing to the detection, the question detecting* and *the dissembling covering.*

First, then, as to *the sickness disposing to the detection of the watchers,* he says: *And behold, there was a certain man before him that had the dropsy.* According to the letter, this dropsical man by his manifest[^p359-5] and grievous infirmity offered a way to confuting and detecting the perfidy of the Jews. And because the time and the place offered themselves, therefore he says: *And behold,* as though the Lord had then brought him into the midst; whence Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-ninth chapter[^p359-6]: « The works of all flesh are before him, and there is nothing hid from his eyes ». « There is no saying: What is this, or what is that? for all things shall be sought in their time »; and afterwards: « All the works of the Lord are good, and he will furnish every work in due time ». Whence this bodily sickness availed for attacking and purging out the spiritual sickness, because it was directly contrary to it[^p359-7]. For this dropsical man was before Jesus to implore the mercy which the Pharisees attacked. For he implored the mercy of God because he was *a man;* for Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter[^p359-8]: « The mercy of God is upon all flesh »; and because he was *a sick man,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter: « There is an inactive man that wanteth help, very weak in ability, and the eye of God hath looked upon him for good »; and because he was *before him,* for such run back to God, and to such God condescends; the Psalm[^p359-9]: « Because he hath looked forth from his high sanctuary; from heaven the Lord hath looked upon the earth ».

5. (Vers. 3.). Secondly, as to *the question detecting,* he adds: *And Jesus answering, spoke to the lawyers and Pharisees.* He says *answering* pointedly, since no one had asked anything before, because he was answering their *thoughts,* which he saw and heard, according to that of Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p359-10]: « When Jesus had seen their thoughts, he said: Why do you think evil in your hearts »? Whence the Gloss: « *Jesus answering* the insidious watchers », for it is said in Wisdom, the first chapter: « The ear of jealousy heareth all things, and the tumult of murmuring shall not be hid ». And therefore he answers their thoughts by putting to them the question which they themselves were thinking, when he adds: *Saying: Is it lawful to cure on the sabbath day?* — The Pharisees are read to have put this question to the Lord, as is read in Matthew, the twelfth chapter[^p359-11]: « They asked whether it is lawful to cure on the sabbath, that they might accuse him ». But Luke expresses their thoughts, Matthew expresses the word. The Lord asked this of them because they professed themselves lawyers; whence the Gloss[^p359-12]: « He asks the judgement of the Law of the lawyers ». On account of which Malachias, the second chapter: « The lips of the priest keep knowledge, and they seek the law at his mouth »; and the Gloss in the same place: « If the priest is questioned about the law, let him teach; otherwise he vaunts in vain a dignity whose working he does not show ».

6. (Vers. 4.). Thirdly, as to *the dissembling covering,* he adds: *But they held their peace.* They held their peace for this reason,
<!-- page 360 -->
that, seeing themselves caught and detected, they feared to be confounded by their own answer; whence Bede's Gloss[^p360-1]: « Rightly are they silent who see that whatever they may say is said against themselves. For if it is lawful, why do they *watch?* If it is not lawful, why do they *cure their cattle* »? Whence by being silent they dissembled ignorance, that they might cover up their malice. The like is had in Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p360-2], where, when the Lord had asked about the baptism of John, whence it was, they would not answer, lest they should be convinced of unbelief. Whence there could be said of them that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-seventh chapter: « O most wicked presumption! whence camest thou to cover dry malice »? But when the leaves of words failed them, they ran back to the darkness of ignorance and the lack of words; whence they were silent not out of prudence but out of ignorance, which has malice joined to it; whence Ecclesiasticus, the twentieth chapter[^p360-3]: « There is one that holdeth his peace, because he knoweth not what to say; and there is another that holdeth his peace, knowing the proper time ».

7. *But he, taking hold of him* etc. After the detection of the watchers there is added here *the confutation of the detected*[^p360-4], which the Evangelist describes in three ways, namely as to *the magnificence of the deed, the efficacy of the word, the evidence of the sign.*

First, then, as to *the magnificence of the deed,* he says: *But he, taking hold of him, healed him, and sent him away.* In this, that *he took hold of him,* his *humility* appears, because he does not disdain to touch infirmities, so as to teach humility; on account of which the Apostle says to the Hebrews, the second chapter[^p360-5]: « Nowhere doth he take hold of the angels, but of the seed of Abraham ». In this, that *he healed him,* his *powerfulness* appears; whence above, the sixth chapter: « Virtue went out from him, and healed all ». In this, that *he sent him away,* his *liberality* appears, namely to go away free. For he did not reduce him to servitude on account of the benefit conferred, but sent him away to go free; the Gloss[^p360-6]: « *He sent* him *away* to go off bodily healed, that he might pass over to the salvation of souls ». Whence above, the eighth chapter, he said to that possessed man whom he had healed: « Go into thy house, and tell how great things God hath done to thee ». And by this Christ, in the manner of doing that miracle, through *humility* confutes the pride of the Jews; through *power,* their sloth; through *liberality* and *kindness,* their perfidy, since they neither deigned to touch, nor could cure, nor would set free.

8. (Vers. 5.). Secondly, as to *the efficacy of the word,* he adds: *And answering them, he said: Which of you whose ass or ox shall fall into a pit,* namely by a chance event, because this possession of temporal things can be endangered and lost in many ways: sometimes from *within,* through its own death, as we see all day long; sometimes from *without,* through another's tyranny, as in Job, the first chapter[^p360-7]: « The oxen were ploughing, and the asses feeding, and the Sabeans rushed in, and took all away »; sometimes from *above,* through pestilence; Exodus, the ninth chapter: « Behold, my hand shall be upon thy horses, and asses, and camels, and oxen, a very grievous murrain »; sometimes from *below,* through a fall, as here. Whence these things are not much to be loved, which can be lost in so many ways[^p360-8].

9. And yet the Pharisees loved these possessions much; whence the Gloss[^p360-9]: « You are all alike in this avarice »; for the love of the ox and the ass made them not care about the observance of the sabbath. Therefore he adds: *And he will not immediately draw him out on the sabbath day?* by a swift succour; the Gloss[^p360-10]: « Consulting not the animal, but his own avarice ». And this indeed they reckoned they did without offence against the Law. If, then, the safety of a man is to be preferred to the safety of an ass and an ox, it is manifest that in the curing of a man the sabbath is not broken; and it is an argument *from the greater.* For it seems rather that the sabbath ought to be broken by a work more *servile,* since it is said in Leviticus, the twenty-third chapter[^p360-11]: « You shall do no servile work therein »; but to rescue an ass or an ox is a more servile work than to heal a man. If, then, he does not break the sabbath who draws an ox out of a pit, much more neither does he who heals a man. For if a work of *avarice* does not break it, then neither does a work of *mercy,* since a work of avarice is one of servitude, a work of mercy one of liberality.

10. (Vers. 6.). Thirdly, as to *the evidence of the sign,* he adds: *And they could not answer him to these things;* the Gloss[^p360-12]: « Convinced ». For then it is an evident sign that a man is convinced, when every way of escape fails him. Nor is it a wonder if they could not answer the wisdom of Christ, since not even the wisest could resist his disciples; whence below, the twenty-first chapter[^p360-13]: « I will give you a mouth and wisdom, which all your adversaries shall not be able to resist ». And this was fulfilled in Stephen, of whom Acts, the sixth chapter:
<!-- page 361 -->
« Now there arose some of the synagogue which is called that of the Libertines, and of the Cyrenians, and of the Alexandrians, and of them that were of Cilicia and Asia, disputing with Stephen; and they were not able to resist the wisdom and the spirit that spoke ».

11. These things having been treated according to the letter, three things occur to us to be considered according to *the spiritual intelligence;* which, according to the Saints, can be drawn out of the words set down before. — The first is *the disease of dropsy,* whose property is, as the Gloss says[^p361-1], that « the more it drinks, the more it thirsts »; and by this it designates *every concupiscence,* which can never be sated, and most of all *avarice,* according to that of Proverbs, the thirtieth chapter: « The fire never saith: It is enough ». Whence it is to be noted that, according to the expositors[^p361-2], there are seven accidents of dropsy. — The first is *a swelling of the body,* and by this *pride* is understood; Deuteronomy, the seventeenth chapter[^p361-3]: « All the people hearing it shall fear, that no one afterwards swell with pride » etc. — The second is *a compression of the spiritual parts,* and in this is understood *envy,* which compresses spiritual things; Proverbs, the fourteenth chapter[^p361-4]: « The rottenness of the bones, envy ». — The third is *a stench in the breath,* by which is understood *wrath,* which makes one break out into contumelious words; the Psalm[^p361-5]: « Their throat is an open sepulchre; with their tongues they acted deceitfully ». — The fourth is *a sluggishness of the feet,* by which is understood *sloth;* to Titus, the first chapter[^p361-6]: « The Cretians are always liars, evil beasts, slothful bellies »; and Wisdom, the fifteenth chapter: « Their feet slothful to walk ». — The fifth is *thirst in the appetite,* by which *avarice* is understood; Proverbs, the thirtieth chapter[^p361-7]: « The earth is not satisfied with water », that is, the earthly man with temporal opulence; and Ecclesiastes, the fifth chapter: « The avaricious man shall not be satisfied with money ». — The sixth is *a swelling of the genitals,* by which *lust* is designated; whence in the Psalm[^p361-8]: « For my loins are filled with illusions, and there is no health in my flesh ». — The seventh is *an infection of the skin* or of the exterior parts, in which is understood *gluttony,* which is wholly about caring for the skin; to the Philippians, the third chapter[^p361-9]: « Whose God is their belly, and whose glory is in their shame, who mind earthly things ».

12. Now the second thing which we ought to consider is *the work of power,* in which is understood *the cure of spiritual sickness.* Concerning which three things are said, namely that *he took hold of him,* that *he healed him,* that *he sent him away. He took hold of him,* namely through *the infusion of grace;* Isaias, the forty-second chapter[^p361-10]: « I the Lord have called thee in justice, and taken hold of thy hand, and preserved thee »; the Psalm: « Put forth thy hand from on high, take me out, and deliver me from many waters » etc. — But *he healed him* through *the expiation of guilt;* the Psalm[^p361-11]: « Who forgiveth all thy iniquities, who healeth all thy diseases »; and Matthew, the first chapter: « He shall save his people from their sins ». — But *he sent him away* through *the relaxation of punishment;* Matthew, the eighteenth chapter[^p361-12]: « The lord of that servant being moved with pity, sent him away, and forgave him the debt ». And this we ask in the Lord's prayer: « Forgive us our debts » etc.

13. Now the third thing which is to be considered is *the example of instruction,* in which he sets down a similitude of the *ox* and the *ass* falling into a pit, which are drawn out on the sabbath. By the *ox* and the *ass* is understood *each people,* namely the gentile and the Jewish, according to that of Isaias, the first chapter[^p361-13]: « The ox knoweth his owner, and the ass his master's crib ». For so Gregory expounds it. — Or by the *ox* and the *ass* is understood the *wise* man and the *foolish;* whence the Gloss on that of Deuteronomy, the twenty-second chapter[^p361-14]: « *Thou shalt not plough with an ox and an ass together,* that is, thou shalt not join the fool to the wise in preaching, so that they announce the word of God with equal power ». These, then, *fall into the pit* of concupiscence through original sin, and thereafter into the limbo of hell; and universally this was so before the coming of Christ. On account of which the Psalm[^p361-15]: « Let not the tempest of water drown me, nor the deep swallow me up, and let not the pit shut her mouth upon me ». Hence, then, Christ *draws out on the sabbath day,* that is, on the seventh day, which is the day of Christ's burial, on which the rest of souls begins, according to that of Zacharias, the ninth chapter[^p361-16]: « Thou also by the blood of thy testament hast sent forth thy prisoners out of the pit, wherein is no water ».
