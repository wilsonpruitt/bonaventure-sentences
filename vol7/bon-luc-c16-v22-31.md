# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XVI, Vers. 22-31
## *Mortuus est autem et dives etc.*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 419 -->

46. *Mortuus est autem et dives* etc. Post immisericordiae causam et culpam subiungit hic *immisericordiae poenam*[^p419-7], quam describit dupliciter: primo quantum ad *miseriam inflictam;* secundo, quantum ad *misericordiam denegatam,* ibi[^p419-8]: *Et dixit illi Abraham* etc.

*Miseria* autem *inflicta* propter immisericordiae culpam tripliciter describitur, scilicet quantum ad *deiectionem in locum miseriarum,* quantum ad *elongationem a loco iustorum* et quantum ad *multitudinem multiplicium calamitatum.*

47. Primo igitur quantum ad *deiectionem in locum miseriarum* dicit: *Mortuus est autem et dives,* per *desertionem temporalium gaudiorum.* Et nota, quod utitur praeterito tempore ad innuendum, quod ille miser dives mortuus est, non una tantum, sed triplici morte. Prima est *culpae,* qua separatur anima a Deo; hac mortuus fuerat etiam, dum viveret; primae ad Timotheum quinto[^p419-9]: « Vidua, quae in deliciis est, vivens mortua est »; *vidua* est anima a Deo separata. — Secunda est mors *naturae,* qua *separatur anima a corpore;* Ecclesiastici quadragesimo primo: « O mors, quam amara est memoria tua homini iniusto, pacem habenti in substantia sua »! — Tertia est mors *gehennae,* qua separatur anima a vita beata; de hac Apocalypsis vigesimo[^p419-10]: « Et infernus et mors missi sunt in stagnum ignis ». In huius triplicis mortis designationem Apocalypsis octavo dicitur: « Vae, vae, vae habitantibus in terra » etc. — *Et sepultus est in inferno,* per *deiectionem in locum suppliciorum;* et hoc propter *culpam superbiae;* Isaiae decimo quarto[^p419-11]: « Detracta est ad inferos superbia tua, concidit cadaver tuum »; propter *culpam lasciviae;* Iob vigesimo primo: « Ducunt in bonis dies suos et in puncto ad inferna descendunt »; propter *culpam immisericordiae;* Psalmus[^p419-12]: « Sicut oves in inferno positi sunt; mors depascet eos »; et iterum: « Veniat mors super illos, et descendant in infernum viventes, quoniam nequitiae in habitaculis eorum » etc. — Ex hoc apparet, quanta sit differentia inter parochianos Christi et diaboli, quia illi *sublimantur* in caelo, isti *sepeliuntur* in inferno, ubi est fovea valde profunda; Iob decimo septimo[^p419-13]: « In profundissimum inferni descendent omnia mea » etc.; et in Psalmo: « Ipsi introibunt in inferiora terrae, tradentur in manus gladii, partes vulpium erunt ».
<!-- page 420 -->

48. (Vers. 23.). Secundo quantum ad *elongationem a loco iustorum* subdit: *Elevans autem oculos suos, cum esset in tormentis,* in loco scilicet continuae afflictionis; Apocalypsis decimo quarto[^p420-1]: « Ascendit fumus tormentorum eorum in saecula saeculorum »; Isaiae trigesimo: « Praeparata est ab heri Topheth ». « Nutrimenta eius ignis et ligna multa; flatus Domini sicut torrens sulphuris succendens eam ». — *Vidit Abraham a longe et Lazarum in sinu eius.* Vere *a longe* et quantum ad *situm* et quantum ad *statum,* quia dives in loco *infimo,* ille vero in *supremo;* Proverbiorum vigesimo nono[^p420-2]: « Superbum sequitur humilitas, et humilem spiritu suscipiet gloria »; et Proverbiorum decimo quarto: « Iacebunt mali ante bonos, et impii ante portas iustorum ». Item, dives in *afflictione* erat, pauper in *requie;* Isaiae sexagesimo quinto[^p420-3]: « Servi mei comedent, et vos esurietis ». Item, quia dives in *egestate,* et pauper in *abundantia;* supra primo[^p420-4]: « Esurientes implevit bonis et divites dimisit inanes »; Psalmus: « Edent pauperes et saturabuntur »; et alibi: « Divites eguerunt et esurierunt, inquirentes autem Dominum » etc. Dicitur autem, quod *vidit Abraham,* et non ad solatium, sed potius ad cumulum tormentorum. Unde Gregorius[^p420-5]: « Credendum est, quod ante retributionem extremi iudicii iniusti in requie quosdam conspiciant, ut, eos videntes in gaudio, non solum de suo supplicio, verum etiam de eorum bono crucientur »; Psalmus[^p420-6]: « Peccator videbit et irascetur, dentibus suis fremet et tabescet; desiderium peccatorum peribit »; et Sapientiae quinto: « Videntes turbabuntur timore horribili » etc.

49. (Vers. 24). Tertio quantum ad *aggregationem multiplicium calamitatum* subditur: *Et ipse exclamans*[^p420-7] *dixit: Pater Abraham, miserere mei;* ut sic ex clamore appareat magnitudo moestitiae, secundum illud Isaiae sexagesimo quinto: « Ecce, servi mei laudabunt prae exsultatione cordis, et vos clamabitis prae dolore cordis et prae contritione spiritus ululabitis ». Et hoc quidem conveniens fuit diviti; Proverbiorum vigesimo primo[^p420-8]: « Qui obturat aures suas ad clamorem pauperis, et ipse clamabit et non exaudietur ». — *Et mitte Lazarum, ut intingat extremum digiti sui in aquam;* ut sic ex petitione guttae aquae appareat *multitudo inopiae;* Iob vigesimo septimo[^p420-9]: « Dives, cum dormierit, nihil secum auferet; aperiet oculos suos et nihil inveniet. Et operiet eum tanquam aqua inopia »; Psalmus: « Dormierunt somnum suum, et nihil invenerunt omnes viri divitiarum in manibus suis ». Unde Chrysostomus[^p420-10]: « Qui in pretiosis excessit in culpa, vilissima hic petiit in poena »; et iterum, loquens ad divitem: « Quondam Lazarum videre non dignabaris, et nunc extremum eius digitum desideras »? Et Beda[^p420-11]: « Dives ille, qui mensae suae minima dare noluit, in inferno positus usque ad minima petenda pervenit; nam guttam aquae petiit qui Lazaro micam panis negavit »; Proverbiorum vigesimo octavo[^p420-12]: « Qui dat pauperi non indigebit, qui despicit deprecantem sustinebit penuriam ». — *Ut refrigeret linguam meam;* ut sic ex afflictione linguae appareat *rectitudo divinae iustitiae.* Unde Petrus Ravennas[^p420-13]: « Si iam te totum possidet ignis inferni, quare solius linguae desideras aestibus subveniri? An quia lingua magis ardet, quae pauperi insultavit, quae misericordiam denegavit? Haec praecedit ad iudicium, quae praecessit ad peccatum; haec primo degustat cruciatus, quae pretiosos cibos et odorifera pocula degustavit », secundum illud Sapientiae undecimo: « Per quae peccat quis, per haec et torquetur ». Unde et Beda in Glossa[^p420-14]: « Nota, quod, sicut mos est, inter convivia loquacitati studuit ». Propter quod Proverbiorum decimo: « In multiloquio non deerit peccatum ». — *Quia crucior in hac flamma;* ut sic appareat *rigor divinae vindictae;* Psalmus[^p420-15]: « Pones eos ut clibanum ignis in tempore vultus tui »; et Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo: « Ignis succensus est in furore meo et ardebit usque ad inferni novissima »; praeterea Ecclesiastici septimo: « Humilia
<!-- page 421 -->
valde spiritum tuum, quoniam vindicta carnis impii, ignis et vermis ».

50. Et nota, quod flamma haec erat vera, sed lingua erat imaginaria, quia verus ignis affligit *spiritus,* secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p421-1]: « Discedite a me; maledicti, in ignem aeternum » etc. Et *anima* etiam affligitur in imaginibus, quas traxit a corpore, secundum illud Sapientiae decimo septimo[^p421-2]: « Apparebat illis subitaneus ignis timore plenus, et timore percussi eius, quae non videbatur faciei, aestimabant, deteriora esse quae videbantur ». Et post: « Ipsi igitur sibi erant graviores tenebris ». Unde ex hoc colligitur, quod in afflictione reproborum simul concurrit poena vera et imaginaria, simul actio naturalis et supernaturalis, simul afflictio intrinseca et extrinseca, simul etiam propria et aliena, ut sic impleatur illud Sapientiae quinto[^p421-3]: « Pugnabit pro illo orbis terrarum contra insensatos ». Vera namque flamma adurebat, lingua etiam imaginaria et phantastica in anima divitis adustioni illi fomentum praebebat. Magis autem expressio fit de lingua quam de aliquo alio membro, quod crucietur et affligatur in incendio infernali, quia ipsa in culpis est quasi fomes totius iniquitatis; Iacobi tertio[^p421-4]: « Lingua modicum quidem membrum est et magna exaltat; ecce, quantus ignis quam magnam silvam incendit! Et lingua nostra ignis est, universitas iniquitatis. Lingua constituitur in membris nostris, quae maculat totum corpus et inflammat rotam nativitatis nostrae, inflammata a gehenna. Omnis enim natura bestiarum domatur, linguam autem nullus hominum domare potest »; est enim « inquietum malum, plena veneno mortifero ».

51. Hoc autem dicitur, quia ipsa lingua impugnat divinam *maiestatem, veritatem* et *bonitatem, mutuam caritatem* et omnem *morum honestatem.* — Impugnat *maiestatem* per verba *iactantiae;* Psalmus[^p421-5]: « Qui dixerunt: Linguam nostram magnificabimus » etc.; et ideo praemittit ibi: « Disperdat Dominus universa labia dolosa et linguam magniloquam ». Item, per verba *blasphemiae;* Apocalypsis decimo sexto[^p421-6]: « Commanducaverunt linguas suas prae dolore et blasphemaverunt Deum »; et secundi Machabaeorum decimo quinto: « Linguam impii Nicanoris praecisam iussit particulatim avibus dari »; blasphemaverat enim. — *Veritatem,* per verba *mendacia;* Proverbiorum vigesimo sexto[^p421-7]: « Lingua fallax non amat veritatem, et os lubricum operatur ruinas »; Ieremiae nono: « Extenderunt linguam suam quasi arcum mendacii et non veritatis ». « Docuerunt linguam suam loqui mendacium » etc.; Psalmus: « Dilexerunt eum in ore » etc. Per verba *duplicia;* Psalmus[^p421-8]: « Tota die cogitavit lingua tua iniustitiam, sicut novacula acuta fecisti dolum »; et iterum: « Lingua tua concinnabat dolos »; et Ecclesiastici quinto: « Non ventiles te in omnem ventum et non eas in omnem viam; sic enim omnis peccator probatur in duplici lingua ». — *Bonitatem,* per verba *blandientia;* Proverbiorum sexto[^p421-9]: « Ut custodiant te a muliere mala et a blanda linguae extraneae »; et Ecclesiastici vigesimo: « Fatuo non erit amicus, et non erit gratia in bonis illius. Qui enim edunt panem eius falsae linguae sunt »; Proverbiorum vigesimo octavo: « Qui corripit hominem » etc. Item, per verba *detrahentia;* Sapientiae primo[^p421-10]: « Custodite vos a murmuratione, quae nihil prodest, et a detractione parcite linguae »; et Ecclesiastici vigesimo octavo: « Sepi aures tuas spinis et noli audire linguam nequam »; et Proverbiorum vigesimo quinto: « Ventus aquilo dissipat pluvias, et facies tristis linguam detrahentem »; Iob vigesimo: « Cum dulce fuerit in ore eius malum, abscondet illud sub lingua sua ».

52. *Mutuam caritatem* per verba *litigantia;* Psalmus[^p421-11]: « Proteges eos in tabernaculo tuo a contradictione linguarum »; Ecclesiastici octavo: « Non litiges cum homine linguato et ne struas ignem in ligna illius »; et Ecclesiastici quarto: « Noli esse citatus in lingua tua ». Item, per verba *provocantia;* Psalmus[^p421-12]: « Filii hominum, dentes eorum arma et sagittae » etc.; et Ieremiae nono: « Sagitta vulnerans lingua eorum, dolum locuta est »; Psalmus: « Acuerunt linguam suam sicut serpentes ». — Omnem *morum honestatem,* per verba *impudica;* Ecclesiastici ultimo[^p421-13]: « Liberasti me a lingua
<!-- page 422 -->
coinquinata et a verbo mendacii »; Iacobi tertio: « Lingua constituitur in membris nostris, quae maculat totum corpus et inflammat » etc. Item, per verba *infructuosa;* Iacobi primo[^p422-1]: « Si quis putat, se religiosum esse, non refrenans linguam suam » etc.; et Ecclesiastici decimo nono: « Quis enim est, qui non deliquerit lingua sua » etc. Et ideo Iacobi tertio[^p422-2]: « Qui non offendit in verbo, hic perfectus est vir »; unde dicitur Proverbiorum decimo sexto: « Hominis est praeparare animam, et Domini gubernare linguam »; Proverbiorum decimo octavo: « Mors et vita in manu linguae ». Et ideo orabat Ecclesiasticus, vigesimo secundo[^p422-3]: « Quis dabit ori meo custodiam et super labia mea signaculum certum, ut non cadam ab ipsis, et lingua mea perdat me »? — Propter hoc ergo dives hic in inferno positus dicit, ut *refrigeret linguam meam, quia crucior in hac flamma.*

53. *Et dixit illi Abraham* etc. Postquam descripsit poenam quantum ad miseriam inflictam, hic describit eam quantum ad *misericordiam denegatam*[^p422-4]; et haec pars dividitur in duas. In quarum prima exprimitur *denegatio misericordiae* respectu *personae propriae,* in secunda respectu *personae coniunctae,* ibi[^p422-5]: *Et ait: Rogo ergo te, pater* etc.

Circa *denegationem misericordiae* respectu *personae propriae* introducuntur tria, scilicet *dissimilitudo meritorum, oppositio stipendiorum* et *ratitudo*[^p422-6] *divinorum iudiciorum.*

54. (Vers. 25.). Primo igitur quantum ad *dissimilitudinem meritorum* dicit: *Et dixit illi Abraham: Fili, recordare, quia recepisti bona in vita tua. Filium* vocat non ex imitatione, sed ex naturali generatione; Psalmus[^p422-7]: « Filii alieni mentiti sunt mihi, filii alieni inveterati sunt ». Unde dicit Petrus Ravennas: « Tu vocas me *patrem,* et ego te *filium,* ut te doleas graviter perdidisse quod natus es, quod tibi gratia dederat et natura ». Huic filio dicit, quod *recordetur bonorum,* non ad solatium, sed potius ad tormentum; unde Boethius[^p422-8]: « Infelicissimum genus fortunae est, fuisse felicem ». Et hoc recepit in remunerationem bonorum suorum; unde Beda[^p422-9]: « Nota, quod dives iste aliquid boni habuit, ex quo bonum transitorium in hac vita recepit »; unde erat de numero illorum, de quibus dicitur Matthaei sexto: « Amen dico vobis, receperunt mercedem suam ». Hoc ergo meritum fuit transitorium et praeambulum ad supplicium. — Sed e contrario fuit meritum Lazari; unde subdit: *Et Lazarus similiter mala;* hoc enim est *similiter in contrario:* Lazarus enim recepit mala ad tempus ad purgandum mala culpae et ad acquirendum bona felicitatis aeternae. Unde Beda[^p422-10]: « Lazarus aliquid mali habuit, sed ignis inopiae purgavit »; et Gregorius: « Si qua reprehensibilia in pauperibus videmus, non debemus despicere, quia fortasse, quos morum infirmitas vulnerat, hos medicina paupertatis sanat ». Et haec est via perveniendi ad regnum; Actuum decimo quarto[^p422-11]: « Per multas tribulationes oportet nos introire in regnum Dei ». Unde in hoc verificatum est illud Ecclesiastae octavo: « Sunt iusti, quibus mala proveniunt, quasi opera egerint impiorum »; nec immerito, quia Tobiae duodecimo: « Quia acceptus eras Deo, necesse fuit, ut tentatio probaret te ».

55. Secundo quantum ad *oppositionem stipendiorum* subditur: *Nunc autem hic consolatur, tu vero cruciaris. Hic,* inquam, consolatur pro tribulatione; Ioannis decimo sexto[^p422-12]: « Mundus gaudebit, vos vero contristabimini, sed tristitia vestra vertetur in gaudium »; unde Matthaei quinto: « Beati, qui lugent, quoniam ipsi consolabuntur ». Sicut enim dicitur secundae ad Corinthios quarto[^p422-13]: « Id quod est in praesenti momentaneum et leve tribulationis nostrae, supra modum » etc. Et ideo Iacobi primo: « Omne gaudium existimate, fratres, cum in tentationes varias incideritis »; et post: « Beatus vir, qui suffert tentationem » etc. Et ideo Chrysostomus[^p422-14]: « Si aegrotamus, si pauperes sumus; gaudeamus, accipiamus mala in vita nostra, ut post recipiamus bona ». Nam sic dicit Propheta: « Transivimus per ignem et aquam, et eduxisti nos in refrigerium ». Et in huius figuram, Ioannis secundo[^p422-15], ad nuptias architriclini aqua conversa est in vinum. Sic ergo pauper consolatur, et dives e contrario cruciatur; supra sexto: « Vae vobis! divites, qui habetis hic consolationem vestram »; et Proverbiorum decimo quarto: « Risus dolore miscebitur, et extrema gaudii luctus
<!-- page 423 -->
occupat ». Haec enim delectatio temporalis in aeternum deiicit cruciatum; unde Gregorius[^p423-1]: « Momentaneum est quod delectat, sed aeternum est quod cruciat ». Iusto igitur Dei iudicio est correspondentia, ut sic sit transitus a contrario in contrarium, a tribulatione scilicet ad consolationem et a consolatione ad sempiternam afflictionem, ad modum cuiusdam crucis. In cuius figuram dicitur de Iacob Genesis quadragesimo octavo[^p423-2], quod in benedictione filiorum Ioseph manus cancellavit ad modum crucis.

56. (Vers. 26.). Tertio quantum ad *ratitudinem divinorum iudiciorum* subdit: *Et in his omnibus chaos magnum firmatum est inter nos et vos;* in quo insinuatur impossibilitas appropinquandi inter reprobos et electos; Ecclesiastae undecimo[^p423-3]: « Si ceciderit lignum ad austrum, aut ad aquilonem, in quocumque loco ceciderit, ibi erit ». Hanc intransgressibilitatem vocat *chaos,* quia nec possunt boni in tenebras peccatorum descendere, nec mali in lucem bonorum; propter quod dicitur Ioannis duodecimo[^p423-4]: « Ambulate, dum lucem habetis, ut non tenebrae vos comprehendant ». De hac intransgressibilitate in Glossa dicit Ambrosius: « Inter pauperem et divitem chaos est, quia post mortem nequeunt merita mutari », ac per hoc nec haberi aliqua consolatio mutua. — Et ideo subdit: *Ut hi qui volunt hinc transire ad vos, non possint,* praestando pietatis auxilium, *neque inde huc transmeare,* attingendo ad praemium aeternum. Unde Gregorius[^p423-5]: « Iustorum animae, etsi in naturae suae bonitate misericordiam habent, iam tunc, auctoris sui iustitiae coniunctae, tanta rectitudine constringuntur, ut nulla ad reprobos compassione moveantur ». *Mali* quidem non possunt de ergastulo exire tartari infernalis; Psalmus[^p423-6]: « Relinquent alienis divitias suas, et sepulcra eorum domus illorum in aeternum ». De hoc non possunt exire, quia, Ecclesiastae nono[^p423-7], « nec opus nec ratio nec scientia nec sapientia erunt apud inferos, quo tu properas »; nec enim de inferno possunt egredi nec in requiem electorum intrare; Isaiae quinquagesimo secundo: « Non adiiciet ultra, ut pertranseat per te incircumcisus aut immundus »; unde Apocalypsis ultimo: « Foris canes et venefici, impudici et idolis servientes » etc. *Boni* autem non possunt transire ad malos iuvandos, possunt tamen ad contemplandos; unde Isaiae ultimo[^p423-8]: « Egredientur et videbunt cadavera virorum, qui praevaricati sunt in me; vermis eorum non morietur, et ignis non exstinguetur; et erunt usque ad satietatem visionis omni carni ».

57. (Vers. 27.). *Et ait: Rogo ergo te, pater* etc. Post denegationem misericordiae respectu personae propriae subditur hic *denegatio* respectu *personae coniunctae*[^p423-9]. Circa quam introducuntur quatuor, scilicet *petitio misericordiae* respectu personae coniunctae, *denegatio misericordiae postulatae, ratio petitionis propositae* et *approbatio responsionis iam datae.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *petitionem misericordiae respectu personae coniunctae* dicit: *Et ait: Rogo ergo te, pater, ut mittas eum in domum patris mei.* Haec autem petitio erat *carnalis,* quia solum ad fratres se extendit.

58. (Vers. 28.). Unde subdit: *Habeo enim quinque fratres;* Ecclesiastici decimo octavo[^p423-10]: « Miseratio hominis circa proximum suum » etc. Ideo *carnalis* erat, quia pro fratribus carnalibus et secundum carnem viventibus. Et hoc insinuat etiam ipse numerus quinarius, in quo intelligitur, quod quinque sensibus corporis erant dediti, secundum illud quod dicitur Samaritanae Ioannis quarto[^p423-11]: « Quinque viros habuisti, et nunc quem habes » etc.; et supra decimo quarto: « Iuga boum emi quinque ». Carnalis etiam, quia hoc non faciebat propter vitandum supplicium alienum, sed ne ex eorum adventu augeretur suum. — Propter quod subdit: *Ut testetur illis, ne et ipsi veniant in hunc locum tormentorum.* Hoc quidem dicebat, ne ex eorum praesentia sua calamitas cresceret per multiplicata proximorum tormenta; unde Beda[^p423-12]: « Servatur diviti ad poenam
<!-- page 424 -->
et cognitio pauperis, quem despexit, et memoria fratrum, quos reliquit, ut de visa gloria despecti et de poena inutiliter amatorum amplius torqueatur ». Et illud est praecipue verum, quando imitatores scelerum alienorum sunt participes suppliciorum; unde Gregorius[^p424-1]: « Ut peccatores in suppliciis amplius torqueantur, et eorum videbunt gloriam, quos contempserunt, et illorum poenam, quos inutiliter amaverunt ». — Erat autem haec petitio non solum carnalis respectu sui, verum etiam *crudelis* respectu proximi, quia propter amorem suorum parentum carnalium volebat, quod Lazarus egrederetur sinum quietis et iret in locum periculorum, ut verificetur illud in eo Proverbiorum duodecimo[^p424-2]: « Novit iustus animas iumentorum suorum, viscera autem impiorum crudelia ». Unde et Christus, qui Lazarum, fratrem Mariae, ad vitam revocavit, flevisse dicitur Ioannis undecimo: « Et lacrymatus est Iesus ». — Erat etiam *fatua,* quia credebat, plus valere verbum hominis mortui quam immortalis Dei; unde impletur illud Ecclesiastae decimo[^p424-3]: « Sed in via stultus ambulans, cum ipse sit stultus, omnes stultos aestimat » etc. Unde Glossa: « Serius hic dives praedicator incipit esse, cum iam nec discendi tempus habeat nec docendi ». — Erat similiter *inverecunda,* quia parva negaverat et modo maxima postulabat, contra illud quod dicitur Ecclesiastici quarto[^p424-4]: « Non sit porrecta manus tua ad accipiendum et ad dandum collecta ».

59. (Vers. 29.). Secundo quantum ad *denegationem misericordiae postulatae* subdit: *Et ait illi Abraham: Habent Moysen et Prophetas; audiant illos. Moysen,* id est doctrinam Legis, quae audienda est pro magnitudine auctoritatis. Nam, Proverbiorum vigesimo octavo[^p424-5], « qui avertit aurem suam, ne audiat Legem, oratio eius fiet exsecrabilis ». Et ideo dicebat legislator Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo: « Audite, caeli, quae loquor, audiat terra verba oris mei ». « Date magnificentiam Deo nostro; Dei perfecta sunt opera ». Et habent etiam *Prophetas,* id est doctrinam Prophetarum audiendam pro certitudine veritatis, secundum illud secundae Petri primo[^p424-6]: « Habemus firmiorem propheticum sermonem, cui bene facitis attendentes tanquam lucernae lucenti in caliginoso loco, donec dies illucescat, et lucifer oriatur in cordibus vestris ». Qui huius duplicis doctrinae verba tam authentica et tam certa audire recusat, nullam prorsus habet excusationem. Propter quod dicit Dominus per Ieremiam prophetam Ieremiae vigesimo sexto[^p424-7]: « Si non audieritis me, ut ambuletis in Lege mea, quam dedi vobis, ut audiatis sermones servorum meorum Prophetarum, quos ego misi ad vos de nocte consurgens, et non audistis; dabo domum istam sicut Silo et urbem hanc dabo in maledictionem ». Si igitur inexcusabiles sunt qui non audiunt Legem et Prophetas, prorsus inexcusabiles sunt qui non audiunt Apostolos et Evangelistas, immo nec ipsam Sapientiam incarnatam. Unde ipsa conqueritur Proverbiorum primo[^p424-8]: « Quia vocavi, et renuistis, extendi manum meam, et non fuit qui aspiceret »; « ego quoque in interitu vestro ridebo et subsannabo, cum vobis hoc quod timebatis, advenerit ». Qui hanc Sapientiam, quae « foris praedicat in plateis[^p424-9] », non vult audire prorsus inexcusabilis est, quia sibi perhibent testimonium Lex et Prophetae. Unde Matthaei decimo septimo[^p424-10]: « Apparuerunt eis Moyses et Elias, cum eo loquentes »; « et vox de nube facta est: Hic est Filius meus dilectus, ipsum audite ». Et propterea dicitur ad Hebraeos secundo[^p424-11]: « Oportet, nos observare ea quae audivimus, ne forte pereffluamus. Si enim qui per Angelos dictus est sermo factus est firmus; quomodo nos effugiemus, si tantam neglexerimus salutem »? Et ad Hebraeos duodecimo: « Accessistis ad novi testamenti mediatorem Iesum et sanguinis aspersionem multo melius loquentem quam Abel. Videte, ne recusetis loquentem ».

60. (Vers. 30.). Tertio quantum ad *rationem petitionis propositae* addit: *At ille dixit: Non, pater Abraham, sed si quis ex mortuis ierit ad illos*[^p424-12], *poenitentiam agent.* In hoc ostendebat fratrum suorum *carnalitatem,* utpote qui magis moverentur ad corporalem suscitationem quam ad spiritualem revelationem. Unde hi erant de numero illorum, de quibus Sapientiae secundo[^p424-13]: « Dixerunt impii apud semetipsos cogitantes non recte »: « Non est refrigerium in fine hominis, et non est qui agnitus sit reversus ab inferis ». In hoc etiam ostendebat
<!-- page 425 -->
fratrum suorum *perversitatem,* ut testimonium hominis mortui praeferrent testibus Dei vivi, contra illud Isaiae octavo[^p425-1]: « Nunquid non populus a Deo suo requiret visionem pro vivis a mortuis? Ad legem magis, et ad testimonium. Quod si non dixerint iuxta verbum hoc, non erit eis matutina lux ». In hoc etiam ostendebat fratrum *incredulitatem,* ut nollent credere, nisi signa et prodigia viderent, sicut ille regulus Ioannis quarto[^p425-2]: « Nisi signa et prodigia videritis, non creditis ». In hoc similiter ostendebat fratrum *fatuitatem,* quia magis deberent ad poenitentiam provocari ex hoc, quod vident homines mori, quam si viderent, eos resuscitari; Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo[^p425-3]: « Memor esto iudicii mei; sic enim erit et tuum, mihi heri et tibi hodie »; et Ecclesiastici decimo septimo: « Ante mortem confitere; a mortuo quasi nihil perit confessio »; Psalmus: « Non mortui laudabunt te, Domine, neque omnes, qui descendunt in infernum ».

61. (Vers. 31.). Quarto quantum ad *approbationem responsionis iam datae* subdit: *Ait autem illi*[^p425-4]*: Si Moysen et Prophetas non audiunt,* eis scilicet obediendo; sic enim audiendi sunt, secundum illud Apocalypsis primo[^p425-5]: « Beatus, qui legit et audit verba prophetiae huius et servat ea » etc. Qui tantos testes non audit veritatem Dei contemnit, secundum illud Ezechielis tertio: « Domus Israel nolunt audire te, quia nolunt audire me. Omnis quippe domus Israel attrita fronte est et indomabili corde ». — Unde quia tantae veritati resistunt obdurati et inhabiles ad credendum, ideo addit: *Neque si quis ex mortuis resurrexerit, credent ei*[^p425-6]. Et est ratio huius consequentiae, quia qui non credit testimonio magis credibili, nec etiam minus credibili. Huius exemplum habemus in *Lazaro,* alio suscitato, cui Iudaei increduli non solum non crediderunt, immo, sicut dicitur Ioannis duodecimo[^p425-7], « cogitaverunt Iudaei, ut Lazarum interficerent, quia multi propter illum abibant et credebant ex Iudaeis ». Sed Pharisaei obstinati non solum non crediderunt, sed et fidem credentium exstinguere volebant. Huius exemplum etiam habemus in *Christo,* cui suscitato etiam Iudaei credere noluerunt. Unde Chrysostomus[^p425-8]: « Christus a mortuis resurrexit, et quae mala erant in inferno nuntiavit; descendit et de caelo, et quae bona ibi erant praedicavit; nec tamen est ei creditum ». Et hoc est, quia tales recusaverunt testimonium Legis et Prophetarum; Ioannis quinto[^p425-9]: « Si crederetis Moysi, crederetis forsitan et mihi; de me enim ille scripsit. Si autem illius litteris non creditis, quomodo verbis meis credetis »? — Ex praedictis igitur apparet diviti immisericordi omnimode ab Abraham patre piissimo misericordia denegata, ut impleatur illud Isaiae sexagesimo tertio[^p425-10]: « Tu, Domine, pater noster, et Abraham nescivit nos ». Et sic verificatum est illud Iacobi secundo: « Iudicium sine misericordia fiet illi qui non facit misericordiam »; et Sapientiae decimo nono: « Impiis usque in novissimum sine misericordia ira supervenit ». Talibus enim dicitur in iudicio illud Matthaei vigesimo quinto: « Discedite a me, maledicti, in ignem aeternum, qui praeparatus est diabolo et angelis eius ».

46. *And the rich man also died* etc. After the cause and the fault of unmercifulness he subjoins here *the punishment of unmercifulness*[^p419-7], which he describes in two ways: first as to *the misery inflicted;* second, as to *the mercy denied,* there[^p419-8]: *And Abraham said to him* etc.

*The misery* moreover *inflicted* on account of the fault of unmercifulness is described in three ways, namely as to *the casting down into the place of miseries,* as to *the removal far from the place of the just* and as to *the multitude of manifold calamities.*

47. First, then, as to *the casting down into the place of miseries* he says: *And the rich man also died,* through *the forsaking of temporal joys.* And note that he uses the past tense to intimate that that wretched rich man died not by one death only, but by a threefold death. The first is *of fault,* by which the soul is separated from God; by this he had been dead even while he lived; the first to Timothy, the fifth chapter[^p419-9]: « She that liveth in pleasures is dead while she is living »; *the widow* is the soul separated from God. — The second is the death *of nature,* by which *the soul is separated from the body;* Ecclesiasticus, the forty-first chapter: « O death, how bitter is the remembrance of thee to an unjust man that hath peace in his possessions »! — The third is the death *of gehenna,* by which the soul is separated from the blessed life; of this the Apocalypse, the twentieth chapter[^p419-10]: « And hell and death were cast into the pool of fire ». In designation of this threefold death it is said in the Apocalypse, the eighth chapter: « Woe, woe, woe to the inhabitants of the earth » etc. — *And he was buried in hell,* through *the casting down into the place of punishments;* and this on account of *the fault of pride;* Isaias, the fourteenth chapter[^p419-11]: « Thy pride is brought down to hell, thy carcass is fallen down »; on account of *the fault of wantonness;* Job, the twenty-first chapter: « They spend their days in wealth, and in a moment they go down to hell »; on account of *the fault of unmercifulness;* the Psalm[^p419-12]: « They are laid in hell like sheep; death shall feed upon them »; and again: « Let death come upon them, and let them go down alive into hell, for there is wickedness in their dwellings » etc. — From this it appears how great is the difference between the parishioners of Christ and of the devil, because those *are exalted* in heaven, these *are buried* in hell, where there is a very deep pit; Job, the seventeenth chapter[^p419-13]: « All that I have shall go down into the deepest pit of hell » etc.; and in the Psalm: « They shall go into the lower parts of the earth, they shall be delivered into the hands of the sword, they shall be the portions of foxes ».
<!-- page 420 -->

48. (Vers. 23.). Second, as to *the removal far from the place of the just* he subjoins: *And lifting up his eyes when he was in torments,* namely in the place of continual affliction; the Apocalypse, the fourteenth chapter[^p420-1]: « The smoke of their torments ascendeth for ever and ever »; Isaias, the thirtieth chapter: « Topheth is prepared from yesterday ». « The nourishment thereof is fire and much wood; the breath of the Lord as a torrent of brimstone kindling it ». — *He saw Abraham afar off, and Lazarus in his bosom.* Truly *afar off,* both as to *position* and as to *state,* because the rich man was in the *lowest* place, but he in the *highest;* Proverbs, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p420-2]: « Humiliation followeth the proud, and glory shall uphold the humble of spirit »; and Proverbs, the fourteenth chapter: « The evil shall lie down before the good, and the wicked before the gates of the just ». Likewise, the rich man was in *affliction,* the poor man in *rest;* Isaias, the sixty-fifth chapter[^p420-3]: « My servants shall eat, and you shall be hungry ». Likewise, because the rich man was in *want,* and the poor man in *abundance;* above, the first chapter[^p420-4]: « He hath filled the hungry with good things, and the rich he hath sent empty away »; the Psalm: « The poor shall eat and shall be filled »; and elsewhere: « The rich have wanted and have suffered hunger, but they that seek the Lord » etc. It is said, moreover, that *he saw Abraham,* and not for solace, but rather for the heaping up of torments. Whence Gregory[^p420-5]: « It is to be believed that before the retribution of the last judgement the unjust behold certain ones in rest, so that, seeing them in joy, they may be tormented not only by their own punishment, but also by the good of those others »; the Psalm[^p420-6]: « The wicked shall see, and shall be angry; he shall gnash with his teeth and pine away; the desire of the wicked shall perish »; and Wisdom, the fifth chapter: « Seeing it, they shall be troubled with terrible fear » etc.

49. (Vers. 24). Third, as to *the aggregation of manifold calamities* there is subjoined: *And he crying out*[^p420-7] *said: Father Abraham, have mercy on me;* so that thus from the cry the greatness of his sorrow may appear, according to that of Isaias, the sixty-fifth chapter: « Behold, my servants shall praise for exultation of heart, and you shall cry for sorrow of heart, and shall howl for grief of spirit ». And this indeed was fitting for the rich man; Proverbs, the twenty-first chapter[^p420-8]: « He that stoppeth his ear against the cry of the poor shall also cry himself and shall not be heard ». — *And send Lazarus, that he may dip the tip of his finger in water;* so that thus from the asking of a drop of water *the multitude of his want* may appear; Job, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p420-9]: « The rich man, when he shall sleep, shall take away nothing with him; he shall open his eyes and find nothing. And want shall cover him like water »; the Psalm: « They have slept their sleep, and all the men of riches have found nothing in their hands ». Whence Chrysostom[^p420-10]: « He who exceeded in precious things in his fault, here sought the vilest things in his punishment »; and again, speaking to the rich man: « Once thou didst not deign to see Lazarus, and now thou desirest the tip of his finger »? And Bede[^p420-11]: « That rich man, who would not give the least things of his table, being placed in hell came even to begging the least things; for he who denied Lazarus a crumb of bread asked for a drop of water »; Proverbs, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p420-12]: « He that giveth to the poor shall not want; he that despiseth him that entreateth shall suffer penury ». — *To cool my tongue;* so that thus from the affliction of the tongue *the rectitude of divine justice* may appear. Whence Peter of Ravenna[^p420-13]: « If the fire of hell already possesses thee whole, why dost thou desire that the tongue alone be relieved from the heats? Is it because the tongue burns the more which insulted the poor man, which denied mercy? It goes first to the judgement which went first to the sin; it first tastes the torments which tasted precious foods and fragrant cups », according to that of Wisdom, the eleventh chapter: « By what things a man sinneth, by the same also he is tormented ». Whence also Bede in the Gloss[^p420-14]: « Note that, as the custom is, he gave himself to talkativeness among banquets ». On account of which Proverbs, the tenth chapter: « In the multitude of words there shall not want sin ». — *For I am tormented in this flame;* so that thus *the rigour of divine vengeance* may appear; the Psalm[^p420-15]: « Thou shalt make them as an oven of fire in the time of thy anger »; and Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter: « A fire is kindled in my wrath, and shall burn even to the lowest hell »; moreover Ecclesiasticus, the seventh chapter: « Humble
<!-- page 421 -->
thy spirit very much, for the vengeance on the flesh of the ungodly is fire and worms ».

50. And note that this flame was true, but the tongue was imaginary, because true fire afflicts *spirits,* according to that of Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p421-1]: « Depart from me; you cursed, into everlasting fire » etc. And *the soul* also is afflicted in the images which it drew from the body, according to that of Wisdom, the seventeenth chapter[^p421-2]: « There appeared to them a sudden fire, full of fear, and being struck with the fear of that face which was not seen, they thought the things which they saw to be worse ». And after: « They then were to themselves more grievous than the darkness ». Whence from this it is gathered that in the affliction of the reprobate there concur at once a true and an imaginary punishment, at once a natural and a supernatural action, at once an intrinsic and an extrinsic affliction, at once also one's own and another's, that thus that of Wisdom, the fifth chapter[^p421-3], may be fulfilled: « The whole world shall fight for him against the unwise ». For the true flame burned, while the tongue, imaginary and phantastic in the soul of the rich man, furnished fuel to that burning. But the expression is made concerning the tongue rather than concerning any other member, that it is tormented and afflicted in the infernal burning, because it is in faults as it were the tinder of all iniquity; James, the third chapter[^p421-4]: « The tongue is indeed a little member, and boasteth great things; behold how small a fire kindleth a great wood! And the tongue is a fire, a world of iniquity. The tongue is placed among our members, which defileth the whole body and inflameth the wheel of our nativity, being set on fire by hell. For every nature of beasts is tamed, but the tongue no man can tame »; for it is « an unquiet evil, full of deadly poison ».

51. Now this is said because the tongue itself assails the divine *majesty, truth* and *goodness, mutual charity* and all *honesty of morals.* — It assails *majesty* by words *of boasting;* the Psalm[^p421-5]: « Who have said: We will magnify our tongue » etc.; and therefore it sets before it there: « May the Lord destroy all deceitful lips, and the tongue that speaketh proud things ». Likewise, by words *of blasphemy;* the Apocalypse, the sixteenth chapter[^p421-6]: « They gnawed their tongues for pain and blasphemed God »; and the second of Machabees, the fifteenth chapter: « He commanded that the tongue of the wicked Nicanor should be cut out and given by pieces to birds »; for he had blasphemed. — *Truth,* by *lying* words; Proverbs, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p421-7]: « A deceitful tongue loveth not truth, and a slippery mouth worketh ruin »; Jeremias, the ninth chapter: « They have bent their tongue as a bow of lying and not of truth ». « They have taught their tongue to speak lies » etc.; the Psalm: « They loved him with their mouth » etc. By *double* words; the Psalm[^p421-8]: « All the day long thy tongue hath devised injustice; as a sharp razor thou hast wrought deceit »; and again: « Thy tongue framed deceits »; and Ecclesiasticus, the fifth chapter: « Winnow not with every wind, and go not into every way; for so is every sinner proved by a double tongue ». — *Goodness,* by *flattering* words; Proverbs, the sixth chapter[^p421-9]: « To keep thee from the evil woman and from the flattering tongue of the stranger »; and Ecclesiasticus, the twentieth chapter: « A fool shall have no friend, and there shall be no thanks for his good deeds. For they that eat his bread are of a false tongue »; Proverbs, the twenty-eighth chapter: « He that rebuketh a man » etc. Likewise, by *detracting* words; Wisdom, the first chapter[^p421-10]: « Keep yourselves from murmuring, which profiteth nothing, and refrain your tongue from detraction »; and Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-eighth chapter: « Hedge in thy ears with thorns, and hear not a wicked tongue »; and Proverbs, the twenty-fifth chapter: « The north wind driveth away rain, and a sad countenance a detracting tongue »; Job, the twentieth chapter: « When evil shall be sweet in his mouth, he will hide it under his tongue ».

52. *Mutual charity,* by *quarrelling* words; the Psalm[^p421-11]: « Thou shalt protect them in thy tabernacle from the contradiction of tongues »; Ecclesiasticus, the eighth chapter: « Strive not with a man that is full of tongue, and heap not fire upon his wood »; and Ecclesiasticus, the fourth chapter: « Be not hasty in thy tongue ». Likewise, by *provoking* words; the Psalm[^p421-12]: « The sons of men, their teeth are weapons and arrows » etc.; and Jeremias, the ninth chapter: « Their tongue is a piercing arrow, it hath spoken deceit »; the Psalm: « They have sharpened their tongues like a serpent ». — All *honesty of morals,* by *unchaste* words; Ecclesiasticus, the last chapter[^p421-13]: « Thou hast delivered me from a tongue
<!-- page 422 -->
defiled and from the word of lying »; James, the third chapter: « The tongue is placed among our members, which defileth the whole body and inflameth » etc. Likewise, by *unfruitful* words; James, the first chapter[^p422-1]: « If any man think himself to be religious, not bridling his tongue » etc.; and Ecclesiasticus, the nineteenth chapter: « For who is there that hath not offended with his tongue » etc. And therefore James, the third chapter[^p422-2]: « If any man offend not in word, the same is a perfect man »; whence it is said in Proverbs, the sixteenth chapter: « It is the part of man to prepare the soul, and of the Lord to govern the tongue »; Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter: « Death and life are in the power of the tongue ». And therefore Ecclesiasticus prayed, the twenty-second chapter[^p422-3]: « Who will set a guard before my mouth and a sure seal upon my lips, that I fall not by them, and that my tongue destroy me not »? — On account of this, therefore, the rich man placed here in hell says *to cool my tongue, for I am tormented in this flame.*

53. *And Abraham said to him* etc. After he has described the punishment as to the misery inflicted, here he describes it as to *the mercy denied*[^p422-4]; and this part is divided into two. In the first of which is expressed *the denial of mercy* in respect of *his own person,* in the second in respect of *the persons joined to him,* there[^p422-5]: *And he said: Then, father, I beseech thee* etc.

Concerning *the denial of mercy* in respect of *his own person* three things are introduced, namely *the dissimilitude of merits, the opposition of wages* and *the fixedness*[^p422-6] *of the divine judgements.*

54. (Vers. 25.). First, then, as to *the dissimilitude of merits* he says: *And Abraham said to him: Son, remember that thou didst receive good things in thy lifetime. Son* he calls him not by imitation, but by natural generation; the Psalm[^p422-7]: « The sons that are strangers have lied to me, the sons that are strangers have faded away ». Whence Peter of Ravenna says: « Thou callest me *father,* and I thee *son,* that thou mayest grieve heavily to have lost what thou wast born, what grace and nature had given thee ». To this son he says that he should *remember good things,* not for solace, but rather for torment; whence Boethius[^p422-8]: « The most unhappy kind of fortune is to have been happy ». And this he received in remuneration of his good things; whence Bede[^p422-9]: « Note that this rich man had something of good, from which he received a transitory good in this life »; whence he was of the number of those of whom it is said in Matthew, the sixth chapter: « Amen I say to you, they have received their reward ». This merit, therefore, was transitory and a prelude to punishment. — But the merit of Lazarus was the contrary; whence he subjoins: *And likewise Lazarus evil things;* for this is *likewise by contrary:* for Lazarus received evil things for a time, for purging the evils of fault and for acquiring the goods of eternal felicity. Whence Bede[^p422-10]: « Lazarus had something of evil, but the fire of want purged it »; and Gregory: « If we see any things reprehensible in the poor, we ought not to despise them, because perhaps those whom the infirmity of morals wounds, the medicine of poverty heals ». And this is the way of coming to the kingdom; Acts, the fourteenth chapter[^p422-11]: « Through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God ». Whence in this is verified that of Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter: « There are just men to whom evils happen, as though they had done the works of the wicked »; nor undeservedly, because Tobias, the twelfth chapter: « Because thou wast acceptable to God, it was necessary that temptation should prove thee ».

55. Second, as to *the opposition of wages* there is subjoined: *But now here he is comforted, and thou art tormented. Here,* I say, he is comforted for tribulation; John, the sixteenth chapter[^p422-12]: « The world shall rejoice, and you shall be made sorrowful, but your sorrow shall be turned into joy »; whence Matthew, the fifth chapter: « Blessed are they that mourn, for they shall be comforted ». For as it is said in the second to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter[^p422-13]: « That which is at present momentary and light of our tribulation, above measure » etc. And therefore James, the first chapter: « Count it all joy, brethren, when you shall fall into divers temptations »; and after: « Blessed is the man that endureth temptation » etc. And therefore Chrysostom[^p422-14]: « If we are sick, if we are poor, let us rejoice; let us receive evil things in our life, that afterwards we may receive good things ». For so says the Prophet: « We have passed through fire and water, and thou hast brought us out into a refreshment ». And in figure of this, John, the second chapter[^p422-15], at the marriage of the chief steward water was turned into wine. Thus, therefore, the poor man is comforted, and the rich man on the contrary is tormented; above, the sixth chapter: « Woe to you! rich men, who have here your consolation »; and Proverbs, the fourteenth chapter: « Laughter shall be mingled with sorrow, and mourning taketh hold of the end of joy
<!-- page 423 -->
». For this temporal delight casts down into eternal torment; whence Gregory[^p423-1]: « Momentary is that which delights, but eternal is that which torments ». By the just judgement of God, therefore, there is a correspondence, so that thus there is a passage from contrary to contrary, namely from tribulation to consolation and from consolation to everlasting affliction, after the manner of a cross. In figure of which it is said of Jacob in Genesis, the forty-eighth chapter[^p423-2], that in the blessing of the sons of Joseph he crossed his hands after the manner of a cross.

56. (Vers. 26.). Third, as to *the fixedness of the divine judgements* he subjoins: *And besides all this a great chaos is fixed between us and you;* in which is intimated the impossibility of drawing near between the reprobate and the elect; Ecclesiastes, the eleventh chapter[^p423-3]: « If the tree fall to the south or to the north, in what place soever it shall fall, there shall it be ». This impassableness he calls *chaos,* because neither can the good descend into the darkness of sinners, nor the wicked into the light of the good; on account of which it is said in John, the twelfth chapter[^p423-4]: « Walk whilst you have the light, that the darkness overtake you not ». Of this impassableness Ambrose says in the Gloss: « Between the poor man and the rich man there is a chaos, because after death merits cannot be changed », and through this neither can any mutual consolation be had. — And therefore he subjoins: *So that they who would pass from hence to you cannot,* by furnishing the help of piety, *nor from thence come hither,* by attaining to the eternal reward. Whence Gregory[^p423-5]: « The souls of the just, although in the goodness of their nature they have mercy, even then, being joined to the justice of their author, are constrained by so great a rectitude that they are moved by no compassion towards the reprobate ». *The wicked* indeed cannot go out of the prison-house of infernal Tartarus; the Psalm[^p423-6]: « They shall leave their riches to strangers, and their sepulchres shall be their houses for ever ». From this they cannot go out, because, Ecclesiastes, the ninth chapter[^p423-7], « neither work nor reason nor knowledge nor wisdom shall be in hell, whither thou art hastening »; for neither can they go forth from hell nor enter into the rest of the elect; Isaias, the fifty-second chapter: « The uncircumcised or unclean shall no more pass through thee »; whence the Apocalypse, the last chapter: « Without are dogs and sorcerers, the unchaste and servers of idols » etc. *The good,* however, cannot pass over to the wicked to help them, yet they can to behold them; whence Isaias, the last chapter[^p423-8]: « They shall go out and see the carcasses of the men that have transgressed against me; their worm shall not die, and their fire shall not be quenched; and they shall be a loathsome sight to all flesh ».

57. (Vers. 27.). *And he said: Then, father, I beseech thee* etc. After the denial of mercy in respect of his own person, there is subjoined here *the denial* in respect of *the persons joined to him*[^p423-9]. Concerning which four things are introduced, namely *the petition for mercy* in respect of the persons joined to him, *the denial of the mercy asked, the reason for the petition put forward* and *the approval of the response already given.*

First, then, as to *the petition for mercy in respect of the persons joined to him* he says: *And he said: Then, father, I beseech thee that thou wouldst send him to my father's house.* Now this petition was *carnal,* because it extended only to his brothers.

58. (Vers. 28.). Whence he subjoins: *For I have five brethren;* Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter[^p423-10]: « The compassion of man is toward his neighbour » etc. It was *carnal,* therefore, because it was for brothers carnal and living according to the flesh. And the very number five intimates this too, in which it is understood that they were given over to the five senses of the body, according to that which is said to the Samaritan woman in John, the fourth chapter[^p423-11]: « Thou hast had five husbands, and he whom thou now hast » etc.; and above, the fourteenth chapter: « I have bought five yoke of oxen ». Carnal also, because he did this not for the sake of avoiding another's punishment, but lest his own be increased by their coming. — On account of which he subjoins: *That he may testify unto them, lest they also come into this place of torments.* This indeed he said lest by their presence his calamity should grow through the multiplied torments of his kinsmen; whence Bede[^p423-12]: « There are kept for the rich man unto punishment
<!-- page 424 -->
both the knowledge of the poor man whom he despised and the memory of the brothers whom he left, that he may be tormented the more by the glory seen of the one despised and by the punishment of those uselessly loved ». And that is especially true when the imitators of others' crimes are partakers of their punishments; whence Gregory[^p424-1]: « That sinners may be tormented the more in punishments, they will see both the glory of those whom they despised and the punishment of those whom they uselessly loved ». — Now this petition was not only carnal in respect of himself, but also *cruel* in respect of his neighbour, because for the love of his carnal kinsmen he wished that Lazarus should go out of the bosom of rest and go into a place of dangers, so that that of Proverbs, the twelfth chapter[^p424-2], may be verified in him: « The just regardeth the lives of his beasts, but the bowels of the wicked are cruel ». Whence also Christ, who recalled Lazarus, the brother of Mary, to life, is said to have wept, John, the eleventh chapter: « And Jesus wept ». — It was also *foolish,* because he believed that the word of a dead man availed more than that of the immortal God; whence that of Ecclesiastes, the tenth chapter[^p424-3], is fulfilled: « But the fool walking in the way, whereas he himself is a fool, esteemeth all men fools » etc. Whence the Gloss: « Too late does this rich man begin to be a preacher, when he has now neither time for learning nor for teaching ». — It was likewise *unshamefast,* because he had denied small things and now asked for the greatest, against that which is said in Ecclesiasticus, the fourth chapter[^p424-4]: « Let not thy hand be stretched out to receive, and shut to give ».

59. (Vers. 29.). Second, as to *the denial of the mercy asked* he subjoins: *And Abraham said to him: They have Moses and the Prophets; let them hear them. Moses,* that is, the doctrine of the Law, which is to be heard for the greatness of its authority. For, Proverbs, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p424-5], « he that turneth away his ear from hearing the Law, his prayer shall be an abomination ». And therefore the lawgiver said in Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter: « Hear, O ye heavens, the things I speak; let the earth hear the words of my mouth ». « Give ye magnificence to our God; the works of God are perfect ». And they have also *the Prophets,* that is, the doctrine of the Prophets, to be heard for the certitude of its truth, according to that of the second of Peter, the first chapter[^p424-6]: « We have the more firm prophetical word, whereunto you do well to attend as to a lamp that shineth in a dark place, until the day dawn and the day star arise in your hearts ». He who refuses to hear the words of this twofold doctrine, so authentic and so certain, has no excuse whatever. On account of which the Lord says through Jeremias the prophet, Jeremias, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p424-7]: « If you will not hear me, to walk in my Law which I gave you, to hear the words of my servants the Prophets, whom I sent to you rising up in the night, and you have not heard: I will make this house like Silo, and will make this city a curse ». If, therefore, they are inexcusable who do not hear the Law and the Prophets, they are altogether inexcusable who do not hear the Apostles and Evangelists, nay not even Wisdom incarnate herself. Whence she herself complains in Proverbs, the first chapter[^p424-8]: « Because I called, and you refused; I stretched out my hand, and there was none that regarded »; « I also will laugh in your destruction and will mock, when that shall come to you which you feared ». He who will not hear this Wisdom, which « preacheth abroad in the streets[^p424-9] », is altogether inexcusable, because the Law and the Prophets give testimony to her. Whence Matthew, the seventeenth chapter[^p424-10]: « There appeared to them Moses and Elias talking with him »; « and a voice was made from the cloud: This is my beloved Son, hear ye him ». And therefore it is said to the Hebrews, the second chapter[^p424-11]: « We ought to observe the things which we have heard, lest perhaps we should let them slip. For if the word spoken by Angels became steadfast, how shall we escape if we neglect so great salvation »? And to the Hebrews, the twelfth chapter: « You are come to Jesus the mediator of the new testament, and to the sprinkling of blood which speaketh better than that of Abel. See that you refuse him not that speaketh ».

60. (Vers. 30.). Third, as to *the reason for the petition put forward* he adds: *But he said: No, father Abraham, but if one went to them*[^p424-12] *from the dead, they will do penance.* In this he showed the *carnality* of his brothers, inasmuch as they would be more moved by a bodily raising up than by a spiritual revelation. Whence these were of the number of those of whom Wisdom, the second chapter[^p424-13]: « The wicked have said, reasoning with themselves, but not right »: « There is no remedy in the end of a man, and there is no man known to have returned from hell ». In this he showed also
<!-- page 425 -->
the *perversity* of his brothers, that they would prefer the testimony of a dead man to the witnesses of the living God, against that of Isaias, the eighth chapter[^p425-1]: « Shall not the people seek a vision of their God, for the living from the dead? To the law rather, and to the testimony. And if they speak not according to this word, they shall not have the morning light ». In this he showed also the *unbelief* of his brothers, that they would not believe unless they saw signs and wonders, as that ruler, John, the fourth chapter[^p425-2]: « Unless you see signs and wonders, you believe not ». In this likewise he showed the *foolishness* of his brothers, because they ought more to be provoked to penance by this, that they see men die, than if they should see them raised up; Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p425-3]: « Remember my judgement, for thine also shall be so: yesterday for me, and today for thee »; and Ecclesiasticus, the seventeenth chapter: « Confess before death; confession perisheth from the dead as nothing »; the Psalm: « The dead shall not praise thee, O Lord, nor any of them that go down to hell ».

61. (Vers. 31.). Fourth, as to *the approval of the response already given* he subjoins: *And he said to him*[^p425-4]*: If they hear not Moses and the Prophets,* namely by obeying them; for so are they to be heard, according to that of the Apocalypse, the first chapter[^p425-5]: « Blessed is he that readeth and heareth the words of this prophecy and keepeth them » etc. He who does not hear such great witnesses despises the truth of God, according to that of Ezechiel, the third chapter: « The house of Israel will not hear thee, because they will not hear me. For all the house of Israel are of a hard forehead and an untamable heart ». — Whence, because they resist so great a truth, hardened and unfit for believing, therefore he adds: *Neither, if one rise again from the dead, will they believe him*[^p425-6]. And the reason of this consequence is that he who does not believe a more credible testimony does not believe a less credible one either. An example of this we have in *Lazarus,* another one raised up, whom the unbelieving Jews not only did not believe, but rather, as it is said in John, the twelfth chapter[^p425-7], « the Jews thought to kill Lazarus, because many of the Jews by reason of him went away and believed ». But the obstinate Pharisees not only did not believe, but even wished to extinguish the faith of those believing. An example of this we have also in *Christ,* whom, though raised up, the Jews would not believe. Whence Chrysostom[^p425-8]: « Christ rose from the dead, and announced what evil things were in hell; he came down also from heaven, and preached what good things were there; and yet he was not believed ». And this is because such men refused the testimony of the Law and the Prophets; John, the fifth chapter[^p425-9]: « If you did believe Moses, you would perhaps believe me also; for he wrote of me. But if you do not believe his writings, how will you believe my words »? — From the things said, therefore, it appears that mercy was in every way denied to the unmerciful rich man by Abraham, the most pious father, so that that of Isaias, the sixty-third chapter[^p425-10], may be fulfilled: « Thou, O Lord, art our father, and Abraham hath not known us ». And so is verified that of James, the second chapter: « Judgement without mercy to him that hath not done mercy »; and of Wisdom, the nineteenth chapter: « Upon the wicked even to the end wrath came without mercy ». For to such is said in the judgement that of Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter: « Depart from me, you cursed, into everlasting fire, which was prepared for the devil and his angels ».
