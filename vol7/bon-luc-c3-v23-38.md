# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. III, Vers. 23-38
## *Secundo, de genealogia temporali tria*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 85 -->

#### Secundo, de genealogia temporali tria.

57. (Vers. 23.). *Et ipse Iesus erat* etc. Post regenerationem sacramentalem, in qua etiam declaravit generationem aeternalem, subiungit hic *genealogiam temporalem,* circa quam tria introducuntur consideranda. Primum est *tempus procedentis* ad excludendum intellectum phantasticum. Secundum est *modus procedendi* ad excludendum intellectum carnalem. Tertium est *processionis gradus* sive progressus ad fortificandum intellectum fidelem.

Primo igitur insinuat *tempus aetatis,* ut illi convincantur, qui credunt, Christum verum hominem non fuisse; propter quod dicit: *Et ipse Iesus erat incipiens quasi annorum triginta,* incipiens scilicet aliis se manifestare et praedicare. *Quasi* dictum est, quia secundum quosdam erat viginti novem annorum et tredecim dies; secundum alios habebat triginta annos et tredecim dies ultra[^p85-1]. In quo etiam praebuit exemplum, ut nullus debeat docere ante aetatem adultam, quantamcumque habeat sapientiam; unde Ecclesiastici vigesimo[^p85-2]: « Homo sapiens tacebit usque ad tempus ». Et ideo dicitur Ecclesiastici trigesimo secundo: « Adolescens loquere in tua causa vix ». Unde filii Gerson non nisi a triginta annis usque ad quinquaginta numerabantur ad portanda onera tabernaculi, Numerorum quarto. Licet autem ita sit de lege communi, tamen aliquando aliter fit de privilegio speciali, sicut dicitur Ieremiae primo[^p85-3]: « Noli dicere, quia puer ego sum »; et Danielis penultimo, de quo dicitur, quod « suscitavit Deus spiritum pueri iunioris ». Ideo dicitur Sapientiae decimo, quod « sapientia aperuit os mutorum et linguas infantium fecit disertas »; et in Psalmo: « Ex ore infantium et lactentium perfecisti laudem ». Attamen privilegia paucorum non sunt trahenda ad consequentiam[^p85-4], sed nec divinae operationi et gratiae est lex imponenda, quia, sicut dicitur Sapientiae quarto, « cani sunt sensus hominis, et aetas senectutis vita immaculata »; et iterum: « Consummatus in brevi explevit tempora multa ».

58. Deinde describit *modum processionis,* cum dicit: *Ut putabatur filius Ioseph;* in quo ostendit, quod secundum generationem temporalem non habebat patrem verum, sed putativum, quia de Virgine natus. Ioseph namque putabant homines eius patrem, secundum quod dicitur infra quarto[^p85-5]: « Nonne hic est filius Ioseph »?

Sed ex hoc videtur, quod genealogia sequens nihil faciat ad Christum secundum veritatem. — Ad quod respondet Beda[^p85-6] secundum Hieronymum, quod non est consuetudinis Scripturarum, ut mulierum in generationibus ordo texatur; deinde et Ioseph et Mariam ex eadem tribu dicit esse generatos, unde ex Lege tenebatur eam accipere ut propinquus. Cuius etiam signum est, quod simul in Bethlehem censentur, sicut habetur supra secundo[^p85-7]. Et ideo generationis series in uno pertinet ad alium. — Ex hoc apparet, quod ita parum in Scriptura de Maria fit sermo. Si enim de ipsa non fiat sermo in Christi genealogia propter servandum morem Scripturarum, cum tamen videatur quasi necessarium; multo minus fieri debet circa proprios actus. — Non fit autem sermo de mulieribus in ordine genealogiarum tum propter defectum a parte *naturae,* quia « femina est vir occasionatus[^p85-8] », tum propter defectum ex parte *significantiae,* quia non Christum, sed Ecclesiam significat, non superiorem portionem rationis, sed inferiorem, primae ad Corinthios undecimo[^p85-9]; tum propter defectum ex parte *officii,* quia nec docere nec praesidere eis
<!-- page 86 -->
competit[^p86-1]; tum propter defectum ex parte *remedii,* quia ei competit non remediare, sed remediari; tum propter defectum *principii,* quia « non vir de muliere est, sed mulier de viro[^p86-2] »; tum propter *memoriam primi peccati;* primae ad Timotheum secundo: « Adam non est seductus, sed mulier ». Et hac ratione non fit decursus per mulieres — unde nec est mos in Scripturis, ut sit mentio magna de Maria — et quia Evangelia inceperunt a praecursore, Lucae primo[^p86-3] et Marci primo: « Initium Evangelii Iesu Christi » etc., et etiam propter praedictas rationes. Scriptum tamen in veteri testamento fuit, quod esse poterat figura Virginis in aliquibus mulieribus, quae Mariae et Ecclesiae figuram gesserunt[^p86-4].

59. Tertio adiungit *gradus processionum,* cum addit: *Qui fuit Heli* etc. — Sed huic videtur contrariari illud Matthaei primo[^p86-5], ubi dicitur, quod « Iacob genuit Ioseph », qui descendit per Salomonem. Si ergo impossibile est, quod aliquis simul a duobus generetur, aut ista genealogia, aut illa Matthaei est omnino erronea.

Ad cuius rei evidentiam est notandum, quod Mathat, qui descendit a David per Nathan, et Mathan, qui descendit a David per Salomonem, habuerunt successive unam uxorem, ex qua Mathan genuit Iacob, et Mathat genuit Heli. Et ita Heli et Iacob fuerunt duo fratres uterini. Heli autem, accepta uxore, mortuus est sine liberis, et ideo secundum Legem Iacob frater eius illam accepit, ut suscitaret semen fratris sui, et ex illa « genuit Ioseph, virum Mariae[^p86-6] ». Ioseph ergo secundum carnem fuit filius Iacob, qui descendit per Salomonem; sed secundum Legem fuit Heli, qui descendit per Nathan. Et quoniam ad perfecte scribendam genealogiam Salvatoris oportuit, eam scribi et secundum Legem et secundum naturam; ideo Spiritus sanctus hoc fecit per duos Evangelistas, ita quod per Matthaeum carnalem et per Lucam legalem.

60. Hoc autem non est factum sine causa rationabili. Matthaeus namque prosequitur Christi *regnum et humanitatem,* secundum quod successit David, et ideo agit de descensu eius ad nostrae carnis susceptionem. Et quia haec fuit primo promissa ipsi Abrahae[^p86-7] et repromissa ipsi David, ideo incipit genealogiam Salvatoris ab Abraham descendendo per David et Salomonem, qui successit ei in regno. — Hinc est etiam, quod descendendo solum *quadraginta* intermedias generationes ponit; quia per illum numerum universitas temporis designatur, secundum quod dicit Augustinus in libro de Concordia Evangelistarum[^p86-8]; generatur enim, si denarius ducatur per quaternarium. Vel secundum alium modum computandi *quadraginta duas,* secundum quadraginta duas mansiones filiorum Israel, Numerorum trigesimo tertio[^p86-9];
<!-- page 87 -->
ita quod Iechonias bis computetur, ad designandum lapidem angularem Christum. Et generatur hic numerus ex senario ducto per septenarium, in quo designatur praesentis temporis status. Et quia Christi generatio secundum *carnem* est initium Evangelii, ideo ponitur in principio libri[^p87-1]. — Lucas autem prosequitur *sacerdotium* Christi, per quod in adoptionem filiorum reconciliamur a statu peccati, quod quidem fit per sacramentalem regenerationem; ideo prosequitur filiationem Christi adoptivam vel legalem, ascendendo usque ad Deum; et hoc per septuaginta septem gradus, quia per illum numerum, ut dicit Augustinus[^p87-2], peccatorum universitas designatur; generatur enim per undenarium ductum per septenarium. Ideo etiam genealogiam istam non in principio libri, sed post baptismum Christi ponit. Quoniam igitur « finis imponit necessitatem his quae sunt ad finem[^p87-3] »; et Lucas principalis intentionis oculum dirigebat ad Christi sacerdotium, Matthaeus vero ad Christi regnum: hinc est, quod Christi genealogiam aliter atque aliter sunt prosecuti; non modo contrario nec casu fortuito, sed varietate quadam consona et articulari[^p87-4] descripserunt, dirigente eos Spiritu sancto.

61. Ex his apparet causa[^p87-5] septiformis differentiae inter Lucam et Matthaeum, scilicet in *personis,* quia ponunt duplicem modum paternitatis, secundum *naturam* et *Legem;* primum Matthaeus, secundum Lucas; primo modo per Salomonem, secundo per Nathan. — In *progressu,* quia Matthaeus descendendo, Lucas ascendendo, et hoc, quia *natura* est principium propagandi, sed *gratia* reducendi. — In *modo,* quia Matthaeus dicit *genuit,* Lucas *qui fuit;* quia ille generationem naturalem, hic denominationem[^p87-6] legalem. — In *tempore,* quia Matthaeus ab exordio, Lucas ab anno trigesimo, quia ille exordium nascendi, hic reconciliandi. — In *situ,* quia Matthaeus a principio, Lucas a baptismo, quia ille generationem, iste regenerationem principaliter attendit. — In *statu,* quia Matthaeus usque ad Abraham, Lucas usque ad Deum, quia ille naturam, et iste gratiam, quae filios sanctificat. — In *numero,* quia Matthaeus in quadraginta[^p87-7] duabus generationibus et quadraginta personis, una bis computata, ad designandum universitatem peregrinationis, ad quam descendit: Lucas septuaginta septem, per numerum transgressionis, quam Christus per baptismum expiavit.

62. *Spiritualiter* notandum, quod omnia nomina, quae exprimuntur in genealogia Salvatoris, aliquod mysticum important, quod valeat ad salutem. Propter vitandum tamen fastidium et curiositatem attendenda sunt loca dumtaxat[^p87-8] insignia et principalia in his gradibus designata, scilicet primus, septimus, decimus, decimus septimus, septuagesimus, septuagesimus sextus — et ultimus. Et ita septem loca insignia, ita quod in primo est nostrae salutis initium, in ultimo complementum, in intermediis profectus incrementum.

63. Primo loco ponitur *Iesus,* qui interpretatur salvator, quia salvat et liberat a servitute peccati; Matthaei primo[^p87-9]: « Et vocabis nomen eius Iesum; ipse enim salvum faciet populum suum a peccatis eorum ». Et hoc est initium nostrae salutis, a malo recedere; Psalmus: « Quis est homo, qui vult vitam et diligit dies videre bonos »? « Diverte a malo » etc. — Septimo loco *Ianne,* qui interpretatur dextera, quia, peccato remisso, transferimur de
<!-- page 88 -->
sinistra ad dexteram; ad Colossenses primo[^p88-1]: « Eripuit nos de potestate tenebrarum et transtulit nos in regnum filii dilectionis », id est de sinistra ad dexteram; Matthaei vigesimo quinto: « Statuet agnos a dextris suis, haedos vero a sinistris ». — Decimo loco *Amos,* qui interpretatur onerans, quia, translati ad gratiam, invitamur ad onera legis portanda; Matthaei undecimo[^p88-2]: « Venite ad me omnes » etc. « Tollite iugum meum super vos » etc.; de quo iugo et onere dicitur Threnorum tertio: « Bonum est viro, cum portaverit iugum ab adolescentia sua ». — Decimo septimo loco *Ioseph,* qui interpretatur augmentum, quia servando et portando onus legis augmentamur in gratia; ad Ephesios quarto[^p88-3]: « Veritatem facientes in caritate, crescamus in illo » etc.; ad Colossenses primo: « Ambuletis digne Deo, per omnia placentes, in omni opere bono fructificantes ». — Septuagesimo loco *Henoch,* qui interpretatur dedicatio, quia proficiendo in merito et caritate pervenimus ad mentis quietem, in qua habitat Deus sicut in templo dedicato; Ioannis decimo quarto[^p88-4]: « Si quis diligit me, sermonem meum servabit, et Pater meus diliget eum, et ad eum veniemus et mansionem apud eum faciemus ». Et hic sursum fertur in caelum per contemplationem sicut Henoch, de quo Genesis quinto[^p88-5]: « Ambulavit cum Deo et non apparuit, quia tulit eum Deus »; et Sapientiae quarto: « Placens Deo » etc. — Septuagesimo sexto[^p88-6] loco *Adam,* qui interpretatur homo, quia perfectio quietis et contemplationis reformat in nobis imaginem Dei, ad quam factus est homo; Genesis primo[^p88-7]: « Faciamus hominem ad imaginem » etc. Hanc reformat contemplatio; secundae ad Corinthios tertio: « Nos autem revelata facie gloriam Dei speculantes, in eandem imaginem transformamur a claritate in claritatem »; et postmodum: « Licet is qui de foris est, noster homo corrumpatur » etc. — Postremo loco subiungitur *Deus,* ad quem tendimus, in quo est quies et finis omnium laborum; Ioannis decimo septimo[^p88-8]: « Haec est autem vita aeterna, ut cognoscant te Deum verum, et quem misisti Iesum Christum » etc.; et primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto: « Deinde finis, cum tradiderit regnum Deo et Patri, cum evacuaverit omnem principatum et potestatem et virtutem »; et post: « Ut sit Deus omnia in omnibus ». Nunc enim est verum illud quod dicitur primae ad Corinthios undecimo[^p88-9]: « Omnis viri caput est Christus, caput vero mulieris, vir, caput vero Christi, Deus »; nunc verum est illud aliud, quod dicitur primae ad Corinthios tertio: « Omnia vestra sunt, vos autem Christi, Christus autem Dei ». « Quoniam ergo ex ipso et per ipsum et in ipso sunt omnia; ipsi Deo aeterno et vivo omnis honor et gloria in saecula saeculorum. Amen ».

## English

#### Second, on the temporal genealogy, three things.

57. (Vers. 23.). *And Jesus himself was* etc. After the sacramental regeneration, in which he also declared the eternal generation, he here subjoins *the temporal genealogy,* concerning which three things are introduced for consideration. The first is *the time of the one proceeding,* to exclude a fantastical understanding. The second is *the mode of proceeding,* to exclude a carnal understanding. The third is *the degree of the procession,* or its progress, to strengthen the faithful understanding.

First, then, he intimates *the time of his age,* so that those may be convicted who believe that Christ was not a true man; on account of which he says: *And Jesus himself was beginning about the age of thirty years,* beginning, namely, to manifest himself to others and to preach. *About* is said because according to some he was twenty-nine years and thirteen days old; according to others he had thirty years and thirteen days over[^p85-1]. In which he also gave an example, that no one ought to teach before adult age, however much wisdom he may have; whence Ecclesiasticus, the twentieth chapter[^p85-2]: « A wise man will hold his peace till the time ». And therefore it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-second chapter: « Young man, scarcely speak in thy own cause ». Whence the sons of Gerson were numbered only from thirty years up to fifty to carry the burdens of the tabernacle, Numbers, the fourth chapter. But although it is so by the common law, yet sometimes it is done otherwise by special privilege, as is said in Jeremias, the first chapter[^p85-3]: « Say not: I am a child »; and in Daniel, the next to last chapter, of whom it is said that « God raised up the spirit of a young boy ». Therefore it is said in Wisdom, the tenth chapter, that « wisdom opened the mouth of the dumb, and made the tongues of infants eloquent »; and in the Psalm: « Out of the mouth of infants and of sucklings thou hast perfected praise ». Nevertheless the privileges of a few are not to be drawn into a consequence[^p85-4], but neither is a law to be imposed on the divine operation and grace, since, as is said in Wisdom, the fourth chapter, « the understanding of a man is grey hairs, and a spotless life is old age »; and again: « Being made perfect in a short space, he fulfilled a long time ».

58. Next he describes *the mode of the procession,* when he says: *As it was supposed, the son of Joseph;* in which he shows that according to the temporal generation he did not have a true father, but a supposed one, since he was born of the Virgin. For men supposed Joseph to be his father, according to what is said below, in the fourth chapter[^p85-5]: « Is not this the son of Joseph »?

But from this it seems that the genealogy following does nothing for Christ according to the truth. — To which Bede[^p85-6] responds, following Jerome, that it is not the custom of the Scriptures that the order of women be woven into the generations; and then he says that both Joseph and Mary were begotten of the same tribe, whence by the Law he was bound to take her as a kinsman. A sign of which too is that they are enrolled together in Bethlehem, as is had above, in the second chapter[^p85-7]. And therefore the series of the generation in the one pertains to the other. — From this it appears why so little is said of Mary in Scripture. For if nothing is said of her in Christ's genealogy, for the sake of keeping the custom of the Scriptures, though it seems as it were necessary, much less ought it to be done concerning her own acts. — Now nothing is said of women in the order of the genealogies, both on account of a defect on the part of *nature,* since « woman is a misbegotten man[^p85-8] », and on account of a defect on the part of *signification,* since she signifies not Christ but the Church, not the superior portion of reason but the inferior, the first to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter[^p85-9]; and on account of a defect on the part of *office,* since neither to teach nor to preside
<!-- page 86 -->
belongs to them[^p86-1]; and on account of a defect on the part of *remedy,* since it belongs to her not to remedy but to be remedied; and on account of a defect of *origin,* since « the man is not of the woman, but the woman of the man[^p86-2] »; and on account of *the memory of the first sin;* the first to Timothy, the second chapter: « Adam was not seduced, but the woman ». And for this reason the line does not run through women — whence neither is it the custom in the Scriptures that there be great mention of Mary — and because the Gospels began from the forerunner, Luke, the first chapter[^p86-3], and Mark, the first chapter: « The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ » etc., and also on account of the reasons stated before. Yet there was written in the old testament what could be a figure of the Virgin in some women, who bore the figure of Mary and of the Church[^p86-4].

59. Third, he adjoins *the degrees of the processions,* when he adds: *Who was of Heli* etc. — But that of Matthew, the first chapter[^p86-5], seems to contradict this, where it is said that « Jacob begot Joseph », who descended through Solomon. If therefore it is impossible that someone be begotten by two at once, either this genealogy or that of Matthew is altogether erroneous.

To make this matter evident it is to be noted that Mathat, who descended from David through Nathan, and Mathan, who descended from David through Solomon, had successively one wife, of whom Mathan begot Jacob and Mathat begot Heli. And so Heli and Jacob were two brothers by the same mother. Now Heli, having taken a wife, died without children, and therefore according to the Law Jacob his brother took her, to raise up the seed of his brother, and of her « begot Joseph, the husband of Mary[^p86-6] ». Joseph, then, according to the flesh was the son of Jacob, who descended through Solomon; but according to the Law he was Heli's, who descended through Nathan. And since, to write the Saviour's genealogy perfectly, it had to be written both according to the Law and according to nature, therefore the Holy Spirit did this through two Evangelists, so that through Matthew he wrote the carnal and through Luke the legal.

60. Now this was not done without a reasonable cause. For Matthew follows out Christ's *kingdom and humanity,* according as he succeeded David, and therefore treats of his descent to the taking up of our flesh. And because this was first promised to Abraham himself[^p86-7] and promised again to David himself, therefore he begins the Saviour's genealogy from Abraham, descending through David and Solomon, who succeeded him in the kingdom. — Hence it is also that in descending he sets down only *forty* intermediate generations; because by that number the whole of time is designated, according to what Augustine says in the book On the Concord of the Evangelists[^p86-8]; for it is generated if ten is multiplied by four. Or, according to another way of computing, *forty-two,* according to the forty-two stations of the children of Israel, Numbers, the thirty-third chapter[^p86-9];
<!-- page 87 -->
so that Jechonias is counted twice, to designate Christ the corner stone. And this number is generated from six multiplied by seven, in which the state of the present time is designated. And because Christ's generation according to *the flesh* is the beginning of the Gospel, therefore it is set at the beginning of the book[^p87-1]. — But Luke follows out Christ's *priesthood,* through which we are reconciled from the state of sin into the adoption of sons, which indeed is done through the sacramental regeneration; therefore he follows out Christ's adoptive or legal filiation, ascending all the way to God; and this through seventy-seven degrees, since by that number, as Augustine says[^p87-2], the whole of sins is designated; for it is generated by eleven multiplied by seven. Therefore too he sets this genealogy not at the beginning of the book, but after Christ's baptism. Since, then, « the end imposes necessity on those things which are for the end[^p87-3] »; and Luke directed the eye of his principal intention to Christ's priesthood, but Matthew to Christ's kingdom: hence it is that they followed out Christ's genealogy in one way and in another; they described it not in a contrary way nor by fortuitous chance, but by a certain consonant and articulate variety[^p87-4], the Holy Spirit directing them.

61. From these appears the cause[^p87-5] of the sevenfold difference between Luke and Matthew, namely in *the persons,* since they set down a twofold mode of paternity, according to *nature* and *the Law;* Matthew the first, Luke the second; in the first mode through Solomon, in the second through Nathan. — In *the progress,* since Matthew goes descending, Luke ascending, and this because *nature* is the principle of propagating, but *grace* of leading back. — In *the mode,* since Matthew says *begot,* Luke *who was of;* since the former sets down the natural generation, the latter the legal denomination[^p87-6]. — In *the time,* since Matthew begins from the beginning, Luke from the thirtieth year, since the former regards the beginning of being born, the latter of reconciling. — In *the position,* since Matthew sets it at the beginning, Luke at the baptism, since the former principally regards the generation, the latter the regeneration. — In *the stopping-place,* since Matthew goes as far as Abraham, Luke as far as God, since the former regards nature, and the latter grace, which sanctifies sons. — In *the number,* since Matthew goes in forty-two[^p87-7] generations and forty persons, one counted twice, to designate the whole of the pilgrimage to which he descended: Luke in seventy-seven, by the number of transgression, which Christ expiated through baptism.

62. *Spiritually* it is to be noted that all the names which are expressed in the Saviour's genealogy import something mystical which avails for salvation. Yet to avoid tedium and curiosity, only[^p87-8] the notable and principal places designated in these degrees are to be attended to, namely the first, the seventh, the tenth, the seventeenth, the seventieth, the seventy-sixth — and the last. And so there are seven notable places, so that in the first is the beginning of our salvation, in the last its completion, in the intermediate ones the increase of progress.

63. In the first place is set *Jesus,* which is interpreted saviour, since he saves and frees from the servitude of sin; Matthew, the first chapter[^p87-9]: « And thou shalt call his name Jesus; for he shall save his people from their sins ». And this is the beginning of our salvation, to withdraw from evil; the Psalm: « Who is the man that will have life, and loveth to see good days »? « Turn away from evil » etc. — In the seventh place *Janne,* which is interpreted right hand, since, sin having been remitted, we are translated from
<!-- page 88 -->
the left to the right; to the Colossians, the first chapter[^p88-1]: « Who hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the kingdom of the Son of love », that is, from the left to the right; Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter: « He shall set the lambs on his right hand, but the goats on his left ». — In the tenth place *Amos,* which is interpreted burdening, since, translated to grace, we are invited to carry the burdens of the law; Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p88-2]: « Come to me, all you » etc. « Take up my yoke upon you » etc.; of which yoke and burden it is said in Lamentations, the third chapter: « It is good for a man, when he hath borne the yoke from his youth ». — In the seventeenth place *Joseph,* which is interpreted increase, since by keeping and carrying the burden of the law we are increased in grace; to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter[^p88-3]: « Doing the truth in charity, let us grow up in him » etc.; to the Colossians, the first chapter: « That you may walk worthy of God, in all things pleasing, being fruitful in every good work ». — In the seventieth place *Henoch,* which is interpreted dedication, since by advancing in merit and charity we come to the rest of the mind, in which God dwells as in a dedicated temple; John, the fourteenth chapter[^p88-4]: « If any one love me, he will keep my word, and my Father will love him, and we will come to him, and will make our abode with him ». And this one is borne upward into heaven through contemplation, like Henoch, of whom Genesis, the fifth chapter[^p88-5]: « He walked with God, and appeared not, because God took him »; and Wisdom, the fourth chapter: « Pleasing God » etc. — In the seventy-sixth[^p88-6] place *Adam,* which is interpreted man, since the perfection of rest and contemplation reforms in us the image of God, to which man was made; Genesis, the first chapter[^p88-7]: « Let us make man to our image » etc. This contemplation reforms; the second to the Corinthians, the third chapter: « But we, beholding the glory of God with open face, are transformed into the same image from glory to glory »; and afterwards: « Though our man that is outward is corrupted » etc. — In the last place is subjoined *God,* to whom we tend, in whom is the rest and end of all labours; John, the seventeenth chapter[^p88-8]: « Now this is eternal life, that they may know thee, the true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent » etc.; and the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter: « Afterwards the end, when he shall have delivered up the kingdom to God and the Father, when he shall have brought to nought all principality, and power, and virtue »; and after: « That God may be all in all ». For now that is true which is said in the first to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter[^p88-9]: « The head of every man is Christ, but the head of the woman is the man, and the head of Christ is God »; now that other is true which is said in the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter: « All are yours, and you are Christ's, and Christ is God's ». « Since, then, of him, and by him, and in him, are all things; to God himself, eternal and living, be all honour and glory for ever and ever. Amen ».

     **En.** Book II, c. 4, n. 8: For it has been acutely remarked that Matthew, who had undertaken to intimate the *kingly* person in Christ, named, Christ himself excepted, forty men in the series of the generations. Now this number signifies that time in which, in this world and on this earth, we must be *ruled* by Christ according to the laborious discipline with which God *scourgeth,* as it is written [Heb. 12:6], *every son whom he receiveth;* of which the Apostle says [Acts 14:21] that through tribulation we must enter into the kingdom of God etc. Ibid., n. 9, he shows that this number signifies: 1° the *laborious time* of the present life, from the fact that Moses, Elias and Christ fasted forty days each; 2° *the temporal life itself,* and the earthly, since « both the seasons of the years run in fourfold turns, and the world itself is bounded by four parts [East, West etc.]...      **En.** V. 1 ff. Cf. Origen, Homily 27 on Numbers, n. 3, and Jerome, Epistle 78 (otherwise 127), n. 2, where it is noted that these 42 generations correspond to the 42 stations of the Hebrews in the desert. — This way of computing is founded on Matt. 1:17, where three divisions of the generations are distinguished, each of which contains fourteen generations (a tessaradecad), namely from Abraham to David, from David to the transmigration of Babylon (to Jechonias), and from the transmigration of Babylon (from Jechonias) to Christ. Having related these, Augustine, Book II of *On the Agreement of the Evangelists,* c. 4, n. 10, adds: « Yet he [Matthew] did not bring them to a sum, so as to say: They make forty-two in all. For one among those progenitors is counted twice, that is, Jechonias, from whom a certain turning aside into foreign nations was made when the transmigration into Babylon took place. Now where an order is bent from straightness and, so as to go in a different direction, makes as it were an angle, that which is at the angle is counted twice, namely at the end of the former order and at the head of the turning itself. And this very thing already prefigured that Christ would in a certain manner migrate from the circumcision to the uncircumcision [Eph. 2:11], as from Jerusalem to Babylon, and would be, for those on this side and on that who believe in him, as it were the corner stone [Ps. 117:22; Isai. 28:16; Matt. 21:42; Acts 4:11; and I Pet. 2:6 ff.] ». Then he adds: « Thus, then, there are now not forty-two, which three times fourteen make, but, on account of the one counted twice, *forty-one* generations are made, if we count in Christ himself too, who presides in kingly fashion over this our temporal and earthly life, which is to be ruled, as by the number forty ». — To complete the number of *forty-two* generations, Hilary, *Commentary on Matthew,* c. 1, n. 2, has recourse to the *twofold* generation of Christ, namely the eternal and the temporal. — Ambrose, Book III on Luke, n. 46; Jerome, *Commentary on Daniel,* on 1:1, and Book I of the *Commentary on Matthew,* on 1:12 ff., judge that there were two Jechoniases, namely the father, who is named Joakim among the Hebrews and in whom the second tessaradecad ends, and the son, who in Hebrew is called Joachin, from whom the third tessaradecad takes its beginning. Finally it is to be noted that in the first tessaradecad the generation of Abraham is presupposed as known.
