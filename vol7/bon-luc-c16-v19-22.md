# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XVI, Vers. 19-22
## *Exemplum impietatis punitae, de quo tria*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 415 -->

#### Exemplum impietatis punitae, de quo tria.

35. *Homo quidam erat dives* etc. Post exemplum pietatis providae et documentum pietatis perfectae subditur hic *exemplum impietatis punitae;* quod, sicut supra[^p415-7] dictum est, magis tenet rationem *exempli* quam *parabolae,* quia ad litteram iste dives in deliciis fuit et postmodum in tormentis. Unde Glossa Bedae: « Contra derisores avaros quod proposuerat exemplo astruit, scilicet quod dives apud inferos ideo torquebatur, quia Lazarum non fecit sibi amicum, a quo recipi posset in aeterna tabernacula. Et magis videtur narratio quam parabola »; unde et Sancti verba huius parabolae adducunt in testimonium de statu damnatorum; et ut ostendatur, quod ad litteram fuit verum, hic nomen hominis pauperis explicatur. — Ad huius igitur exempli enarrationem explicandam introducit Evangelista tria, scilicet *immisericordiae causam, culpam* et *poenam.* Immisericordiae *causa* fuit *amor* respectu sui; immisericordiae *culpa* fuit *incompassio* respectu proximi, de qua ibi[^p415-8]: *Et erat quidam mendicus;* immisericordiae *poena* fuit *deiectio in calamitatem inferni,* de qua ibi: *Mortuus est autem et dives* etc.

Circa *immisericordiae causam,* quae est amor privati boni, introducit tria circa divitem, de quibus
<!-- page 416 -->
notat eum, quae sunt *concupiscentia oculorum, superbia vitae* et *concupiscentia carnis*[^p416-1].

36. (Vers. 19.). Primo igitur quantum ad *concupiscentiam oculorum* dicit: *Homo quidam erat dives. Homo* iste *dives* dicitur non solum propter divitiarum possessionem, sed propter earum dilectionem, ratione cuius, primae ad Timotheum ultimo[^p416-2], « qui volunt divites fieri incidunt in tentationem et in laqueum diaboli » etc.; quia ratione dilectionis terrenorum incrassatur et praegravatur spiritus, ut non possit pervenire in sublimia regna caelorum. Propter quod Matthaei decimo nono: « Facilius est, camelum transire per foramen acus, quam divitem intrare in regnum caelorum ». Hoc explicans dicit Marci decimo[^p416-3]: « Filioli, quam difficile, confidentes in pecuniis in regnum Dei introire! Facilius est camelum » etc. De huiusmodi divite dicitur Ecclesiastici decimo tertio: « Sicut communicabit lupus agno, sic peccator iusto. Quae communicatio homini sancto ad canem? Aut quae pars bona diviti et pauperi? Venatio leonis, onager in eremo, sic et pascua sunt divitum pauperes. Et sicut abominatio est superbo humilitas, sic et exsecratio est divitis pauper ».

37. Secundo quantum ad *superbiam vitae* subdit: *Et*[^p416-4] *induebatur purpura et bysso.* Beda: « *Purpura* est color regii habitus, e conchis marinis ferro circumcisis emissa; *byssus,* genus lini candidi et mollissimi ». Habebat autem hic vestimenta pulcra et pretiosa ad vanam gloriam; unde Gregorius[^p416-5]: « Nemo vestimenta pretiosa nisi ad gloriam quaerit, ut honorabilior ceteris habeatur ». Et hoc quidem est reprehensibile et peccatum, propter quod Ecclesiastici undecimo: « In vestitu ne glorieris unquam et in die honoris tui ne extollaris ».

38. Peccatur autem in vestibus quadrupliciter: primo propter *pretiositatem;* unde Beda[^p416-6]: « Si cultus pretiosarum vestium culpa non esset, sermo Dei non tam vigilanter exprimeret, quod dives purpura et bysso indutus apud inferos irremediabiliter torqueretur ». Valde enim absurdum est, putridum cadaver pretiosa veste ornari, unde possent multi pauperes sustentari. Excusatur autem hoc ratione *dignitatis personae,* ratione *significantiae* et ratione *cautelae*[^p416-7]. — Secundo, propter *superabundantiam;* Iacobi quinto[^p416-8]: « Vestimenta vestra a tineis comesta sunt. Thesaurizastis vobis iram in novissimis diebus »; et hoc propter iniuriam pauperum; unde Iob vigesimo quarto: « Nudos dimittunt homines, indumenta tollentes, quibus non est operimentum » etc. Hieronymus[^p416-9]: « Aliena rapere convincitur qui temporalia ultra necessitatem retinere comprobatur ». — Tertio, propter *curiositatem;* Matthaei sexto[^p416-10]: « De vestimentis quid solliciti estis »? Et iterum: « Considerate lilia agri: non laborant neque nent. Dico autem vobis, quoniam nec Salomon in omni gloria sua coopertus est sicut unum ex istis ». — Quarto, propter *indecentiam,* quia non decet, clericum vestiri ut mulierem vel ut histrionem; Sophoniae primo[^p416-11]: « Visitabo super omnes viros indutos veste peregrina »; et Deuteronomii vigesimo secundo: « Non induetur vir veste muliebri ». — Omnibus autem his modis consueverunt homines in vestitu excedere et excedendo gloriari et gloriando peccare, et ista gloriatio vana est et similis gloriationi corniculae[^p416-12]. Ovis enim de lana et vermis de serico debet gloriari; nam « homo putredo et filius hominis vermis ».

39. Tertio quantum ad *concupiscentiam carnis* subdit: *Et epulabatur quotidie splendide;* Glossa[^p416-13]: « Ecce, voracitas », quae ut plurimum habet luxuriam adiunctam; Iacobi quinto: « Epulati estis super terram et in luxuriis enutristis corda vestra ». — Et nota, quod est *splendor* in *epulatione spirituali,* quem facit *sapientia;* et hic est laudandus, sicut et illa epulatio, de qua supra decimo quinto[^p416-14]: « Manducemus et epulemur, quia filius » etc. Haec epulatio est *splendida,* quia nihil habet sordis, sed venit ex fonte luminis sempiterni; Psalmus[^p416-15]: « Inebriabuntur ab ubertate »; et post: « Quoniam apud
<!-- page 417 -->
te est fons vitae, et in lumine tuo videbimus lumen »; et rursum: « Quoniam transibo in locum tabernaculi admirabilis; in voce exsultationis et confessionis, sonus epulantis ». — Item est *splendor* in *epulatione corporali,* quem facit *misericordia,* et hic etiam laudandus, de quo secundae ad Corinthios nono[^p417-1]: « Non ex tristitia, aut necessitate; hilarem enim datorem diligit Deus ». Et hic splendor consistit in faciei hilaritate et eleemosynarum largitate. De hoc dicitur Ecclesiastici trigesimo primo: « Splendidum in panibus benedicent labia multorum, et testimonium veritatis illius fidele ». — Est etiam *splendor* in *epulatione carnali,* quem facit *lascivia;* Zachariae septimo[^p417-2]: « Nunquid non vobismetipsis manducastis et vobismetipsis bibistis »? Et iste splendor extenditur non solum ad fercula artificialiter praeparata, sed etiam ad vasa argentea, ad supellectilia candida, ad luminaria multiplicata. De hoc Lucanus[^p417-3]:

> O quaesitorum terra pelagoque ciborum
> Ambitiosa fames et lautae gloria mensae!

De hoc Bernardus[^p417-4]: « Ceterum voluptas gutturis, quae hodie tanti aestimatur, vix duorum spatium obtinet digitorum. Huius tam modicae partis tam exigua delectatio quanta paratur sollicitudine! Quantam denique molestiam parit »! Haec autem abundantia epularum parit inopiam sapientiae et virtutum et frequenter etiam temporalium; unde Proverbiorum vigesimo primo[^p417-5]: « Qui diligit epulas in egestate erit; qui amat vinum et pinguia non ditabitur ».

40. (Vers. 20.). *Et erat quidam mendicus* etc. Post immisericordiae causam introducit hic Evangelista *immisericordiae culpam*[^p417-6], quam ostendit reprehensibilem ex triplici circumstantia, scilicet ex parte pauperis, quae ipsum reddebat misericordia dignum et divitem illum per consequens immisericordem et impium. Ostenditur enim immisericors respectu *pauperis languidi,* respectu *pauperis derelicti,* respectu *pauperis Deo cari et iusti.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *pauperis languidi egestatem* dicit: *Et erat quidam mendicus nomine Lazarus.* Et nota hic, quod cum dives nominatus sit nomine communi, pauper nominatur nomine proprio, ut ostendatur illud quod dicitur in Psalmo[^p417-7]: « Parcet pauperi et inopi » etc.; et post: « Et honorabile nomen eorum coram illo ». Unde Glossa[^p417-8]: « Pauper humilis per approbationem ex nomine scitur ». Unde Dominus Moysi, Exodi trigesimo tertio: « Invenisti gratiam coram me, et te ipsum novi ex nomine », et hoc quidem magnum est. Unde licet de divitibus dicatur in Psalmo[^p417-9]: « Vocaverunt nomina sua in terris suis », pauperibus dicitur supra decimo: « Gaudete, quia nomina vestra scripta sunt in caelis ».

41. Et nota, quod immisericordia divitis aggravatur ex hoc, quod ille pauper erat *unicus;* unde dicit quidam Psalmus[^p417-10]: « Respice in me et miserere mei, quia unicus et pauper sum ego ». — Quia erat *mendicus;* unde addit *mendicus,* quasi pulsans ad ostium per petitionem eleemosynarum; Ecclesiastici quarto: « Rogationem contribulati ne abiicias et ne avertas faciem tuam ab egeno ». — Quia erat *manifestus;* unde addit: *Qui iacebat ad ianuam eius,* ut iam non posset de ignorantia excusari; propter quod primae Ioannis tertio[^p417-11]: « Qui viderit, fratrem suum necessitatem habere, et clauserit viscera sua » etc. O quam dissimilis erat iste Iob! de quo trigesimo primo: « Foris non mansit peregrinus, ostium meum viatori patuit »; quam dissimilis etiam Abrahae, qui sedebat « in ostio tabernaculi », ut statim susciperet peregrinos, Genesis decimo octavo. — Aggravatur etiam, quia erat *languidus,* et ideo addit: *Ulceribus plenus;* Ecclesiastici septimo[^p417-12]: « Non te pigeat visitare infirmum; ex his enim in dilectione firmaris »; unde poterat hic dicere: « Ego sum pauper et dolens » etc.

42. (Vers. 21.). Item aggravatur, quia fuit *famelicus;* unde addit: *Cupiens saturari;* Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo[^p417-13]: « Frange esurienti panem tuum et egenos vagosque induc in domum tuam » etc.; et Ecclesiastici quarto: « Animam esurientem ne despexeris et ne exasperes pauperem in inopia sua ». — Item aggravatur, quia *micis contentus,* unde sequitur: *De micis, quae cadebant de mensa divitis;*
<!-- page 418 -->
Iob vigesimo secundo[^p418-1]: « Aquam lasso non dedisti et esurienti subtraxisti panem »; et Ambrosius: « Divites, tanquam supra naturam siti, de miseriis pauperum incentiva suarum capiunt voluptatum ». — Ex his autem sex manifeste ostenditur istius pauperis egestas, quae dignum ipsum misericordia faciebat et divitem de duritia reprehensibilem ostendebat.

43. Secundo quantum ad *pauperis derelictionem* subdit: *Et nemo illi dabat;* unde poterat dicere Lazarus illud Isaiae sexagesimo tertio[^p418-2]: « Circumspexi, et non erat auxiliator; quaesivi, et non erat qui adiuvaret »; et Ecclesiastae nono: « Nullus deinceps recordatus est hominis illius pauperis »; quia, sicut dicitur Proverbiorum decimo nono, « fratres hominis pauperis oderunt eum, insuper et amici eius recesserunt ab eo ». Unde iste pauper vere erat derelictus, secundum illud Psalmi[^p418-3]: « Tibi derelictus est pauper »; quia nullus eius relevabat egestatem, nullus etiam fovebat infirmitatem. — Unde et subditur: *Sed et canes veniebant et lingebant ulcera eius.* Glossa[^p418-4]: « Hos nec a se removere poterat, nec visitator erat, qui removeret »; ex quo apparet, quod dives inexcusabilis erat, qui pauperem ita derelictum videbat. Ex hoc etiam potest mira huius divitis crudelitas adverti, quia canibus fuit liberalior quam pauperi, et canes fuerunt mitiores ipsi pauperi quam dives; unde Petrus Ravennas[^p418-5]: « Canes pauperis ulcera curant magisterio naturae, dives pauperem negligit, natura accusante »; et hoc contra illud quod dicitur Iob quinto: « Visitans speciem tuam, non peccabis ». Unde idem Petrus Ravennas[^p418-6]: « Miser dives, si micam pauperi non dedisti, saltem quare canes abigere neglexisti? Sed canes tui mitiores te, immo tu immitior eis et mitior canibus tuis quam pauperi fuisti; canes tui aliquando pinguia, sed pauper nec micam de mensa tua aliquando recepit ». Ex magnitudine igitur miseriae pauperis aggravatur immisericordia divitis, et ex abundantia divitis augetur patientia pauperis mendici. Unde Gregorius[^p418-7]: « In una re duo iudicia Dei explentur: et ex *visione pauperis* diviti non miserenti cumulus damnationis infertur, et rursum etiam ex *visione divitis* quotidie tentatus pauper probatur »; Proverbiorum vigesimo secundo: « Pauper et dives obviaverunt sibi, utriusque operator est Deus ».

44. (Vers. 22.). Tertio quantum ad *pauperis sanctitatem* subiungit: *Factum est autem, ut moreretur mendicus et portaretur ab Angelis in sinum Abrahae;* ex quo apparet, quod hic mendicus erat sanctus et bonus, quia moriens ab Angelis suscipitur et in locum pacis deportatur; Sapientiae tertio[^p418-8]: « Visi sunt oculis insipientium mori »; et sequitur: « Illi autem sunt in pace ». Tales angelico portantur ministerio; unde Petrus Ravennas[^p418-9]: « Non dedignantur eum portare Angeli, cui prius hic ministrabant; ad Hebraeos primo: *Omnes sunt administratorii spiritus* » etc.; et post[^p418-10]: « Merito pauperi angelica deputantur officia, cui tam crudeliter denegata sunt humanitatis obsequia ». Deferunt autem Angeli mendicum istum in sinum Abrahae, id est in locum quietis; qui quidem locus ante apertionem ianuae erat in limbo et dicitur *sinus Abrahae,* quia ipse Abraham susceptus est ibi tanquam in sinum merito fidei, quo dictus est *pater multarum gentium*[^p418-11]; et illi qui ibi suscipiuntur, dicuntur *in eius sinu suscipi.* Unde et ei primo facta est repromissio de incarnatione Christi[^p418-12]. Vocatur autem *sinus,* quia, sicut in sinu res absconditur, fovetur et custoditur quasi in loco familiari, sic et sancti Patres in loco illo conservabantur; Isaiae quarto[^p418-13]: « Super omnem gloriam protectio. Et tabernaculum erit in umbraculum diei ab aestu et in absconsionem a turbine et a pluvia »; et in Psalmo: « Quoniam abscondit me in tabernaculo suo, in die malorum protexit me in abscondito tabernaculi sui ». In hoc sinu positus est Lazarus et ab omnibus malis sua morte liberatus, secundum illud Iob quinto[^p418-14]: « In sex tribulationibus liberabit te, et in septima
<!-- page 419 -->
non tanget te malum. In fame eruet te de morte et in bello a manu gladii. A flagello linguae absconderis, et non timebis calamitatem, cum venerit ». « Et scies, quod pacem habeat tabernaculum tuum ».

45. Et nota, quod est sinus *culpabilis, laudabilis* et *desiderabilis.* — *Sinus culpabilis* triplex est, scilicet *carnalitatis;* Proverbiorum quinto[^p419-1]: « Quare seduceris, fili mi, ab aliena et foveris in sinu alterius »? et sexto: « Nunquid potest homo abscondere ignem in sinu suo, ut vestimenta eius non ardeant » etc. « Sic qui ingreditur ad uxorem proximi sui non erit mundus, cum tetigerit eam ». *Cupiditatis;* Proverbiorum decimo septimo[^p419-2]: « Munera de sinu impius accipit, ut pervertat semitas iudicii » etc. *Crudelitatis;* tertii Regum tertio: « Tulit filium meum de latere meo, ancillae tuae dormientis, et posuit in sinu suo; suum autem filium, qui erat mortuus, posuit in sinu meo »; et Iob trigesimo primo: « Si abscondi quasi homo peccatum meum ».

Est etiam *sinus laudabilis,* scilicet *humilitatis* in *abscondendo;* Exodi quarto[^p419-3]: « Retrahe manum tuam in sinum tuum. Retraxit et protulit eam, et erat similis carni reliquae » etc. Sinus etiam *benignitatis* in *refovendo;* Proverbiorum vigesimo primo[^p419-4]: « Munus absconditum exstinguit iras, et donum in sinu indignationem maximam ». Sinus etiam *longanimitatis* in *conservando;* Iob decimo nono[^p419-5]: « Reposita est haec spes mea in sinu meo »; et Ezechielis quadragesimo tertio: « In sinu eius erat cubitus, et definitio eius usque ad labium », cubitus scilicet perfectae mercedis.

Est tertio *sinus desiderabilis,* et de hoc dicit hic: « Factum est, ut moreretur mendicus et portaretur ab Angelis in sinum Abrahae », per quem sinum intelligitur requies beatarum animarum; Matthaei octavo[^p419-6]: « Multi venient ab oriente et occidente et recumbent cum Abraham, Isaac et Iacob in regno caelorum ».

## English
<!-- page 415 -->

#### The example of impiety punished, concerning which three things.

35. *There was a certain rich man* etc. After the example of provident piety and the instruction of perfect piety there is subjoined here *the example of impiety punished;* which, as was said above[^p415-7], holds rather the account of an *example* than of a *parable,* because according to the letter this rich man was in delights and afterwards in torments. Whence the Gloss of Bede: « Against the avaricious scoffers he establishes by an example what he had set forth, namely that the rich man was tormented among those below for this reason, because he did not make Lazarus his friend, by whom he might have been received into everlasting tabernacles. And it seems rather a narrative than a parable »; whence also the Saints adduce the words of this parable in testimony concerning the state of the damned; and that it may be shown that according to the letter it was true, the name of the poor man is set forth here. — For the unfolding, then, of the narration of this example the Evangelist introduces three things, namely *the cause, the fault* and *the punishment of unmercifulness.* The *cause* of unmercifulness was *love* in respect of self; the *fault* of unmercifulness was *lack of compassion* in respect of the neighbour, of which there[^p415-8]: *And there was a certain beggar;* the *punishment* of unmercifulness was *the casting down into the calamity of hell,* of which there: *And the rich man also died* etc.

Concerning *the cause of unmercifulness,* which is the love of private good, he introduces three things about the rich man, by which he
<!-- page 416 -->
marks him, which are *the concupiscence of the eyes, the pride of life* and *the concupiscence of the flesh*[^p416-1].

36. (Vers. 19.). First, then, as to *the concupiscence of the eyes* he says: *There was a certain rich man. This man* is called *rich* not only on account of the possession of riches, but on account of the dilection of them, by reason of which, the first to Timothy, the last chapter[^p416-2], « they that will become rich fall into temptation and into the snare of the devil » etc.; because by reason of the dilection of earthly things the spirit is fattened and weighed down, so that it cannot attain to the sublime kingdoms of heaven. On account of which Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « It is easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle, than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of heaven ». Explaining this he says, Mark, the tenth chapter[^p416-3]: « Children, how hard is it for them that trust in riches to enter into the kingdom of God! It is easier for a camel » etc. Of a rich man of this kind it is said, Ecclesiasticus, the thirteenth chapter: « If the wolf shall at any time have fellowship with the lamb, so the sinner with the just. What fellowship hath a holy man with a dog? Or what part hath the rich with the poor? The wild ass is the lion's prey in the desert: so also the poor are devoured by the rich. And as humility is an abomination to the proud: so also the rich man abhorreth the poor ».

37. Second, as to *the pride of life* he subjoins: *And*[^p416-4] *he was clothed in purple and fine linen.* Bede: « *Purple* is the colour of the royal habit, drawn from sea-shells cut round with iron; *byssus,* a kind of linen most white and most soft ». And this man had beautiful and precious garments for vainglory; whence Gregory[^p416-5]: « No one seeks precious garments except for glory, that he may be held more honourable than others ». And this indeed is reprehensible and a sin, on account of which Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter: « Glory not in apparel at any time, and be not exalted in the day of thy honour ».

38. And one sins in garments in four ways: first, on account of *preciousness;* whence Bede[^p416-6]: « If the wearing of precious garments were not a fault, the word of God would not so watchfully express that the rich man clothed in purple and fine linen was tormented irremediably among those below ». For it is very absurd that a putrid carcass should be adorned with a precious garment by which many poor could be sustained. But this is excused by reason of *the dignity of the person,* by reason of *signification* and by reason of *caution*[^p416-7]. — Second, on account of *superabundance;* James, the fifth chapter[^p416-8]: « Your garments are moth-eaten. You have stored up to yourselves wrath against the last days »; and this on account of the injury of the poor; whence Job, the twenty-fourth chapter: « They send men away naked, taking away their clothes who have no covering » etc. Jerome[^p416-9]: « He is found guilty of seizing what is another's who is proved to retain temporal things beyond necessity ». — Third, on account of *curiosity;* Matthew, the sixth chapter[^p416-10]: « And for raiment why are you solicitous »? And again: « Consider the lilies of the field: they labour not, neither do they spin. But I say to you, that not even Solomon in all his glory was arrayed as one of these ». — Fourth, on account of *unseemliness,* because it is not seemly for a cleric to be clothed as a woman or as an actor; Sophonias, the first chapter[^p416-11]: « I will visit upon all the men that are clothed with strange apparel »; and Deuteronomy, the twenty-second chapter: « Neither shall a man use woman's apparel ». — And in all these modes men have been wont to exceed in apparel, and in exceeding to glory, and in glorying to sin; and this glorying is vain and like the glorying of the little crow[^p416-12]. For the sheep ought to glory in the wool and the worm in the silk; for « man is rottenness and the son of man a worm ».

39. Third, as to *the concupiscence of the flesh* he subjoins: *And he feasted splendidly every day;* the Gloss[^p416-13]: « Behold, voracity », which for the most part has lust joined to it; James, the fifth chapter: « You have feasted upon earth, and in riotousness you have nourished your hearts ». — And note that there is a *splendour* in *spiritual feasting,* which *wisdom* makes; and this is to be praised, as also that feasting of which above, the fifteenth chapter[^p416-14]: « Let us eat and feast, because this my son » etc. This feasting is *splendid,* because it has nothing of filth, but comes from the fountain of eternal lumen[^tr-lumen]; the Psalm[^p416-15]: « They shall be inebriated with the plenty »; and after: « For with
<!-- page 417 -->
thee is the fountain of life, and in thy lumen we shall see lumen »; and again: « For I shall go over into the place of the wonderful tabernacle; with the voice of exultation and praise, the noise of one feasting ». — Likewise there is *splendour* in *bodily feasting,* which *mercy* makes, and this too is to be praised, of which the second to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter[^p417-1]: « Not with sadness, or of necessity; for God loveth a cheerful giver ». And this splendour consists in cheerfulness of face and largess of alms. Of this it is said, Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-first chapter: « The lips of many shall bless him that is splendid in bread, and the testimony of his truth is faithful ». — There is also *splendour* in *carnal feasting,* which *wantonness* makes; Zacharias, the seventh chapter[^p417-2]: « Did you not eat for yourselves, and drink for yourselves »? And this splendour extends not only to dishes artfully prepared, but also to silver vessels, to white furnishings, to multiplied lights. Of this Lucan[^p417-3]:

> O hunger ambitious for foods sought out by land and sea,
> And glory of the sumptuous table!

Of this Bernard[^p417-4]: « But the pleasure of the gullet, which today is so highly esteemed, scarcely occupies the space of two fingers. With how great solicitude is so slight a delight of so small a part provided! How great a trouble, in fine, does it bring forth »! And this abundance of banquets brings forth want of wisdom and of virtues, and frequently also of temporal things; whence Proverbs, the twenty-first chapter[^p417-5]: « He that loveth good cheer shall be in want; he that loveth wine and fat things shall not be rich ».

40. (Vers. 20.). *And there was a certain beggar* etc. After the cause of unmercifulness the Evangelist introduces here *the fault of unmercifulness*[^p417-6], which he shows to be reprehensible from a threefold circumstance, namely on the part of the poor man, which rendered him worthy of mercy and, in consequence, that rich man unmerciful and impious. For he is shown unmerciful in respect of *a poor man languishing,* in respect of *a poor man forsaken,* in respect of *a poor man dear to God and just.*

First, then, as to *the need of the poor man languishing* he says: *And there was a certain beggar, named Lazarus.* And note here that, whereas the rich man is named by a common name, the poor man is named by a proper name, that there may be shown that which is said in the Psalm[^p417-7]: « He shall spare the poor and needy » etc.; and after: « And their name shall be honourable in his sight ». Whence the Gloss[^p417-8]: « The humble poor man is known by name through approbation ». Whence the Lord to Moses, Exodus, the thirty-third chapter: « Thou hast found favour in my sight, and I know thee thyself by name », and this indeed is a great thing. Whence, although of the rich it is said in the Psalm[^p417-9]: « They have called their names in their lands », to the poor it is said above, the tenth chapter: « Rejoice, because your names are written in heaven ».

41. And note that the unmercifulness of the rich man is aggravated by this, that that poor man was *alone;* whence a certain Psalm says[^p417-10]: « Look thou upon me, and have mercy on me; for I am alone and poor ». — Because he was *a beggar;* whence he adds *beggar,* as it were knocking at the door by the asking of alms; Ecclesiasticus, the fourth chapter: « Reject not the petition of the afflicted, and turn not away thy face from the needy ». — Because he was *in plain sight;* whence he adds: *Who lay at his gate,* so that now he could not be excused on the ground of ignorance; on account of which the first of John, the third chapter[^p417-11]: « He that shall see his brother in need, and shall shut up his bowels » etc. O how unlike was this man to Job! of whom, the thirty-first chapter: « The stranger did not stay without, my door was open to the traveller »; how unlike also to Abraham, who sat « at the door of his tent », that he might at once receive strangers, Genesis, the eighteenth chapter. — It is aggravated also because he was *languishing,* and therefore he adds: *Full of sores;* Ecclesiasticus, the seventh chapter[^p417-12]: « Be not slow to visit the sick; for by these things thou shalt be confirmed in love »; whence he could say here: « I am poor and sorrowful » etc.

42. (Vers. 21.). Likewise it is aggravated because he was *famished;* whence he adds: *Desiring to be filled;* Isaiah, the fifty-eighth chapter[^p417-13]: « Deal thy bread to the hungry, and bring the needy and the harbourless into thy house » etc.; and Ecclesiasticus, the fourth chapter: « Despise not the hungry soul, and provoke not the poor in his want ». — Likewise it is aggravated because he was *content with crumbs,* whence there follows: *With the crumbs that fell from the rich man's table;*
<!-- page 418 -->
Job, the twenty-second chapter[^p418-1]: « Thou hast not given water to the weary, and thou hast withdrawn bread from the hungry »; and Ambrose: « The rich, as though thirsty beyond nature, take from the miseries of the poor the incentives of their pleasures ». — From these six, then, is manifestly shown the need of this poor man, which made him worthy of mercy and showed the rich man reprehensible for his hardness.

43. Second, as to *the forsakenness of the poor man* he subjoins: *And no one did give him;* whence Lazarus could say that of Isaiah, the sixty-third chapter[^p418-2]: « I looked about, and there was none to help; I sought, and there was none to give aid »; and Ecclesiastes, the ninth chapter: « No man afterward remembered that poor man »; because, as is said, Proverbs, the nineteenth chapter, « the brethren of the poor man hate him; moreover also his friends have departed far from him ». Whence this poor man was truly forsaken, according to that of the Psalm[^p418-3]: « To thee is the poor man forsaken »; because no one relieved his need, no one even tended his infirmity. — Whence also there is subjoined: *Moreover the dogs came, and licked his sores.* The Gloss[^p418-4]: « These he could not remove from himself, nor was there a visitor to remove them »; from which it appears that the rich man was inexcusable, who saw the poor man so forsaken. From this also the marvellous cruelty of this rich man can be noticed, because he was more liberal to dogs than to the poor man, and the dogs were gentler to the poor man himself than the rich man; whence Peter of Ravenna[^p418-5]: « The dogs cure the poor man's sores by the teaching of nature; the rich man neglects the poor man, nature accusing him »; and this against that which is said, Job, the fifth chapter: « Visiting thy beauty thou shalt not sin ». Whence the same Peter of Ravenna[^p418-6]: « Wretched rich man, if thou didst not give a crumb to the poor man, why at least didst thou neglect to drive away the dogs? But thy dogs were gentler than thou; nay, thou wast ungentler than they, and gentler to thy dogs than to the poor man; thy dogs sometimes received fat things, but the poor man never received even a crumb from thy table ». From the greatness, then, of the misery of the poor man the unmercifulness of the rich man is aggravated, and from the abundance of the rich man the patience of the poor beggar is increased. Whence Gregory[^p418-7]: « In one thing two judgements of God are fulfilled: both from *the sight of the poor man* a heap of damnation is brought upon the rich man who has no pity, and again also from *the sight of the rich man* the poor man, tempted daily, is proved »; Proverbs, the twenty-second chapter: « The poor and the rich have met one another: God is the maker of them both ».

44. (Vers. 22.). Third, as to *the holiness of the poor man* he subjoins: *And it came to pass that the beggar died, and was carried by the Angels into Abraham's bosom;* from which it appears that this beggar was holy and good, because dying he is received by Angels and borne into a place of peace; Wisdom, the third chapter[^p418-8]: « In the sight of the unwise they seemed to die »; and there follows: « But they are in peace ». Such are carried by angelic ministry; whence Peter of Ravenna[^p418-9]: « The Angels do not disdain to carry him to whom they formerly ministered here; to the Hebrews, the first chapter: *All are ministering spirits* » etc.; and after[^p418-10]: « Rightly are angelic offices assigned to the poor man, to whom the services of humanity were so cruelly denied ». And the Angels bear this beggar into Abraham's bosom, that is, into the place of rest; which place indeed, before the opening of the gate, was in limbo and is called *Abraham's bosom,* because Abraham himself was received there as into a bosom by the merit of faith, by which he was called *the father of many nations*[^p418-11]; and those who are received there are said *to be received into his bosom.* Whence also to him first was made the promise concerning the incarnation of Christ[^p418-12]. And it is called a *bosom* because, as in a bosom a thing is hidden, cherished and guarded as in a familiar place, so also the holy Fathers were preserved in that place; Isaiah, the fourth chapter[^p418-13]: « Over all the glory shall be a protection. And there shall be a tabernacle for a shade in the daytime from the heat, and for a hiding-place from the whirlwind and from rain »; and in the Psalm: « For he hath hidden me in his tabernacle; in the day of evils he hath protected me in the secret place of his tabernacle ». In this bosom Lazarus was placed and by his death delivered from all evils, according to that of Job, the fifth chapter[^p418-14]: « In six tribulations he shall deliver thee, and in the seventh
<!-- page 419 -->
evil shall not touch thee. In famine he shall deliver thee from death, and in battle from the hand of the sword. Thou shalt be hidden from the scourge of the tongue, and thou shalt not fear calamity when it cometh ». « And thou shalt know that thy tabernacle is in peace ».

45. And note that there is a *culpable, praiseworthy* and *desirable* bosom. — *The culpable bosom* is threefold, namely *of carnality;* Proverbs, the fifth chapter[^p419-1]: « Why art thou seduced, my son, by a strange woman, and art cherished in the bosom of another »? and the sixth: « Can a man hide fire in his bosom, and his garments not burn » etc. « So he that goeth in to his neighbour's wife shall not be clean when he shall touch her ». *Of cupidity;* Proverbs, the seventeenth chapter[^p419-2]: « The wicked man taketh gifts out of the bosom, that he may pervert the paths of judgement » etc. *Of cruelty;* the third of Kings, the third chapter: « She took my son from my side, while I thy handmaid was asleep, and laid it in her bosom; and her own son, who was dead, she laid in my bosom »; and Job, the thirty-first chapter: « If as a man I have hid my sin ».

There is also *the praiseworthy bosom,* namely *of humility* in *hiding;* Exodus, the fourth chapter[^p419-3]: « Put back thy hand into thy bosom. He put it back, and brought it out, and it was like the other flesh » etc. The bosom also *of kindness* in *cherishing;* Proverbs, the twenty-first chapter[^p419-4]: « A secret present quencheth angers, and a gift in the bosom the greatest wrath ». The bosom also *of long-suffering* in *preserving;* Job, the nineteenth chapter[^p419-5]: « This my hope is laid up in my bosom »; and Ezechiel, the forty-third chapter: « In the bosom thereof was a cubit, and the border thereof even to the lip », namely the cubit of perfect reward.

There is, third, *the desirable bosom,* and of this he says here: « It came to pass that the beggar died, and was carried by the Angels into Abraham's bosom », by which bosom is understood the rest of the blessed souls; Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p419-6]: « Many shall come from the east and the west, and shall sit down with Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven ».
