# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XIV, Vers. 7-11
## *Secundo informantur convivantes quoad tria*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 362 -->

#### Secundo informantur convivantes quoad tria.

14. *Dicebat autem et ad invitatos* etc. Postquam confutavit observantes, hic secundo *informat convivantes*[^p362-1]. Dividitur autem haec pars in tres secundum triplicem informationem. Primo namque informat *invitatos ad convivium nuptiale.* Secundo informat *invitantes ad convivium familiare,* ibi[^p362-2]: *Dicebat autem et ei qui se invitaverat.* Tertio informat *invitandos ad convivium aeternale,* ibi: *Homo quidam fecit coenam magnam* etc. Primum horum respicit convivium *gratiae;* secundum, *naturae,* et tertium, *gloriae;* primum, *sacramentale;* secundum, *materiale;* tertium, *aeternale* et *spirituale.*

Informans igitur *invitatos ad convivium nuptiale,* per quod intelligitur status praesentis Ecclesiae, primo *vituperat vanitatem et arrogantiam;* secundo *commendat humilitatem et reverentiam,* ibi[^p362-3]: *Sed cum vocatus fueris.* — In *vituperando* autem *arrogantiam* tria introducuntur, scilicet *occasio* introducens parabolam, *persuasio* reprehendens superbiam et *ratio* explicans verecundiam.

15. (Vers. 7.). Primo igitur quantum ad *occasionem introducentem parabolam* dicit: *Dicebat autem et ad invitatos parabolam;* Glossa[^p362-4]: « *Parabolam,* aliud mystice significantem ». Quia enim aliquis posset illud Domini documentum referre ad nuptias istas carnales, ideo ipse Evangelista, dirigens intellectum expositoris, vult illud parabolice intelligi. Hic enim in docendo erat modus Domini Salvatoris, secundum illud Psalmi[^p362-5]: « Aperiam in parabolis os meum, loquar propositiones ab initio » etc. Et quia parabola non valet, nisi quando dicitur suo tempore, secundum illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo[^p362-6]: « Ex ore fatui reprobabitur parabola; non enim dicit illam in tempore suo »; et econtra Proverbiorum vigesimo quinto: « Mala aurea in lectis argenteis, qui loquitur verbum in tempore suo »: ideo additur *opportunitas loci* et *temporis* ad inductionem huius parabolae de convivio nuptiali, cum subditur: *Intendens, quomodo primos accubitus eligerent,* more scilicet superborum, qui quidem mos erat in Pharisaeis; Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p362-7]: « Amant primos accubitus in coenis et primas cathedras in synagogis et salutationes in foro »; et hoc in signum honoris, secundum illud Iob vigesimo nono: « Si voluissem ire ad eos, sedebam primus ». *Primum* enim et *summum* coniuncta sunt; et sicut superbia et ambitio est inordinatus appetitus superioritatis, ita et prioritatis. Huiusmodi autem quaerunt superbi, qui ab aliis cupiunt honorari, exemplo Saul, primi Regum decimo quinto[^p362-8]: « Tantum honora me coram senioribus populi mei et coram Israel ».

16. (Vers. 7. 8.). Secundo vero quantum ad *persuasionem dissuadentem superbiam* subdit: *Dicens ad illos: Cum invitatus fueris ad nuptias, non discumbas in primo loco.* Licet istud posset intelligi de *carnalibus nuptiis,* tamen ipse textus sequens et expositores volunt intelligi de *nuptiis spiritualibus;* unde Glossa[^p362-9]: « Cum per gratiam fidei, vocatus a praedicatore, membris Ecclesiae te iunxeris; non te de meritis gloriando, quasi ceteris sis sublimior, extollas ». *Nuptiae* autem istae non sunt quaecumque, sed *nuptiae Agni,* de quibus Apocalypsis decimo nono[^p362-10]: « Venerunt nuptiae Agni, et uxor eius praeparavit se ». Has *nuptias* fecit Deus Pater, secundum quod dicitur Matthaei vigesimo secundo: « Simile est regnum caelorum homini regi, qui fecit nuptias filio suo ». Hae nuptiae celebratae sunt in thalamo uteri virginalis; Psalmus[^p362-11]: « In sole posuit tabernaculum suum, et ipse tanquam sponsus procedens de thalamo suo ». Ibi consummatum est matrimonium inter divinam et humanam naturam et inter Christum et Ecclesiam per consequens, secundum illud ad Ephesios quinto[^p362-12]; loquens Apostolus de matrimonio dicit: « Sacramentum hoc magnum est, ego autem dico, in Christo et in Ecclesia ». — *Convivium* in his nuptiis est in susceptione Sacramentorum Ecclesiae et documentorum sacrae Scripturae; Proverbiorum nono[^p362-13]: « Sapientia aedificavit sibi domum, excidit columnas septem. Immolavit victimas suas, miscuit vinum et proposuit mensam. Et misit ancillas suas, ut vocarent ad arcem » etc. Ad hoc convivium *vocantur* omnes, qui vocantur ad fidem per veritatis praedicationem; Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p362-14]: « Misit servos suos vocare invitatos ad nuptias ». — Sic invitati *discumbunt* et *manducant* per divinorum verborum et mysticorum perscrutationem et ruminationem. In cuius signum cibus ille caelestis vocatus est *manna* Exodi decimo sexto[^p362-15],
<!-- page 363 -->
quod interpretatur: « Quid est hoc »? quia oportet intelligere quae suscipiunt. Unde et Levitici undecimo dicitur, quod animal, quod « non ruminat, immundum est ». — In *primo* autem *loco discumbit* qui aliis praefertur sive in officio dignitatis, sive in privilegiis sanctitatis, sive in magisterio veritatis. Et ad hoc non debet quis ex se ipso conscendere, quia dicitur ad Hebraeos quinto[^p363-1]: « Nec quisquam sumit sibi honorem, sed qui vocatur a Deo, tanquam Aaron ». Et ideo Ecclesiastici sexto: « Non te extollas in cogitatione animae tuae velut taurus, ne forte elidatur virtus tua »; nec etiam *coram aliis;* unde Ecclesiastici undecimo: « In vestitu ne glorieris unquam et in die honoris tui ne extollaris ».

17. Tertio autem quantum ad *rationem explicantem verecundiam* subdit: *Ne forte honoratior te sit invitatus ab illo,* propter gratiam interiorem maiori honore dignus; Glossa[^p363-2]: « Gratiosior invitatori, etsi aliis sit occultus »; unde primi Regum decimo sexto: « Ne respicias vultum eius neque altitudinem staturae eius, quoniam abieci eum; nec iuxta intuitum hominis ego iudico. Homo enim videt ea quae patent, Dominus autem intuetur cor ».

18. (Vers. 9.). *Et tunc*[^p363-3] *veniens is qui te et illum vocavit,* per iustitiae dispositionem; Proverbiorum decimo sexto: « Spirituum ponderator est Deus »; *dicat tibi: Da huic locum,* propter dignitatis praelationem; primi Regum decimo quinto[^p363-4]: « Scidit Dominus regnum tuum a te et tradidit illud proximo tuo meliori te ». — *Et tunc incipies*[^p363-5] *cum rubore novissimum locum tenere,* per manifestam deiectionem, secundum illud Psalmi: « Exaltatus autem humiliatus sum et conturbatus ». Et ideo Ecclesiastici decimo tertio: « Attende, ne seductus in stultitia humilieris »; ille autem *seducitur stultitia,* qui se reputat aliquid magnum; ad Galatas sexto[^p363-6]: « Si quis existimat, se aliquid esse, cum nihil sit, ipse se seducit ». Et talis humiliatur a Deo iusto iudicio, secundum illud supra primo: « Deposuit potentes de sede et exaltavit humiles ». De huiusmodi iudicio dicitur Ecclesiastici undecimo[^p363-7]: « Multi tyranni sederunt in throno, et insuspicabilis portavit diadema. Multi potentes oppressi sunt valide, et gloriosi traditi sunt in manus alienorum ». Et ideo dicitur Ecclesiastici septimo[^p363-8]: « Noli quaerere ab homine ducatum neque a rege cathedram honoris »; quia, Proverbiorum vigesimo, « hereditas, ad quam festinatur in principio, in novissimo benedictione carebit ».

19. (Vers. 10.). *Sed cum vocatus fueris.* Postquam vituperavit arrogantiam, hic secundo *invitat ad reverentiam*[^p363-9]; quod quidem facit *persuadendo humilitatem perfectam, assignando utilitatem adiunctam, confirmando per aequitatem divinam.*

Primo igitur *persuadens humilitatem perfectam,* dicit: *Sed cum vocatus fueris,* ad nuptias, *vade et*[^p363-10] *recumbe in novissimo loco,* te ipsum scilicet omnibus postponendo, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo: « Quicumque voluerit inter vos maior fieri, sit vester minister. Et qui voluerit inter vos primus esse erit vester servus; sicut Filius hominis non venit ministrari, sed ministrare ». Unde ipse resedit in novissimo loco; Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p363-11]: « Vidimus eum novissimum virorum, virum dolorum et scientem infirmitatem; ideo absconditus vultus eius et despectus ». — In huius humilitatis commendationem sedes personarum excellentiorum remotiores sunt ab altari, et in processionibus priores dignitate sunt posteriores, ad ipsius Christi imitationem, qui utique ad hanc humilitatem invitabat, cum omnium Apostolorum pedes lavit; Ioannis decimo tertio[^p363-12]: « Si ego lavi pedes vestros, dominus et magister, et vos debetis alter alterius lavare pedes. Exemplum enim dedi vobis » etc.; quia « non est discipulus super
<!-- page 364 -->
magistrum » etc. Et ideo Apostolus primae ad Corinthios nono[^p364-1]: « Cum essem liber ex omnibus, omnium me servum feci »; et rursus decimo quinto: « Ego sum minimus Apostolorum, qui non sum dignus vocari Apostolus »; et ad Ephesios tertio: « Mihi omnium Sanctorum minimo data est gratia haec ». Et ideo dicebat princeps Apostolorum Petrus primae Petri secundo[^p364-2]: « Subiecti estote omni humanae creaturae propter Deum ». — *Recumbe* igitur *in novissimo loco,* te inferiorem omnibus reputando.

20. Sed dices: Quomodo me vere reputabo omnibus peiorem, cum multi sint peiores? — Ad hoc respondet Bernardus in Cantica, homilia trigesima septima[^p364-3]: « Si enim, in quonam statu unumquemque nostrum habeat Deus, liquido cognosceremus, nec supra sane nec infra sedere deberemus, veritati in omnibus acquiescentes. Nunc autem, quia consilium hoc *posuit tenebras latibulum suum*[^p364-4], et sermo absconditus est a nobis; ita ut *nemo sciat, si dignus sit amore, vel odio;* iustius tutiusque profecto iuxta ipsius Veritatis consilium novissimum nobis locum eligimus, de quo postmodum cum honore superius educamur, quam praesumimus altiorem, unde recedere[^p364-5] mox oporteat cum rubore. Non ergo est periculum, quantumcumque te humilies, quantumcumque te reputes minorem, quam sis, hoc est, quam te Veritas habeat. Est autem grande malum horrendumque periculum, si vel modice plus te vero extollas, et si illi[^p364-6] videlicet in cogitatione tua te praeferas, quem forte parem tibi Veritas iudicat, aut etiam superiorem. Quemadmodum, si per ostium transeas, cuius superliminare, ut ad intelligentiam loquar, nimium bassum sit, non nocet, quantumcumque te inclinaveris; at si vel transversi digiti spatio plus, quam ostii mensura patitur, erexeris, impinges et quassato capite collideris[^p364-7]. Sic in anima non est plane timenda quantalibet humiliatio; horrenda autem nimiumque pavenda vel minima temere praesumta erectio. Quamobrem noli te, homo, comparare maioribus, noli minoribus, noli aliquibus, noli uni. Quid scis enim, o homo, si unus ille, quem forte omnium vilissimum atque miserrimum reputas, cuius vitam scelleratissimam ac singulariter foedissimam horres, et propterea putas spernendum illum non modo prae te, qui forte iam *sobrie et iuste et pie vivere*[^p364-8] te confidis, sed etiam prae ceteris omnibus sceleratis tanquam omnium sceleratissimum: quid scis, inquam, si melior te et illis mutatione dexterae Excelsi[^p364-9] in se quidem futurus sit, in Deo vero iam sit? Et propterea non mediocrem, non vel penultimum, non ipsum saltem inter novissimos eligere locum nos voluit; sed *recumbe,* inquit, *in novissimo loco,* ut videlicet omnium novissimus sedeas, teque nemini non dico praeponas, sed nec comparare praesumas ».

21. Secundo autem *assignans utilitatem adiunctam,* subdit: *Ut, cum venerit qui te invitavit, dicat tibi*[^p364-10]*: Amice, ascende superius.* Qui nos *invitavit* est Christus, qui *venit* ad nos vel per *inspirationem,* secundum illud Ioannis decimo quarto[^p364-11]: « Ad eum veniemus et mansionem apud eum faciemus ». Vel *venit* per *mortem;* supra duodecimo: « Ut, cum venerit et pulsaverit ianuam, confestim aperiant ei ». Vel *venit* per *finale examen;* Apocalypsis ultimo: « Ecce, venio cito, et merces mea mecum est, reddere unicuique secundum opera sua ». — Hic veniens vocat humiles *amicos.* Christus namque amicus verus est, secundum illud Proverbiorum decimo septimo[^p364-12]: « Omni tempore diligit qui amicus est ». Et humiles recognoscit amicos, quia tales obediunt ei; Ioannis decimo quinto: « Vos amici mei estis, si feceritis quae praecipio vobis ». Et tunc vocat non nomine *servi,* sed *amici;* Ioannis decimo quinto: « Iam non dicam vos servos, sed amicos, quia omnia, quaecumque audivi a Patre meo, nota feci vobis ». — Tali dicit: *Ascende superius,* id est ad superiorem honorem et dignitatem; Psalmus[^p364-13]: « Nimis honorificati sunt amici tui, Deus ». — Et ideo subdit: *Tunc erit tibi gloria coram simul discumbentibus,* id est coram omnibus; unde
<!-- page 365 -->
Proverbiorum vigesimo nono[^p365-1]: « Superbum sequitur humilitas, et humilem spiritu suscipiet gloria »; Psalmus: « Cum ipso sum in tribulatione, eripiam eum et glorificabo eum. Longitudine dierum replebo eum et ostendam illi salutare meum ». Et propter hoc Iob vigesimo secundo[^p365-2]: « Qui humiliatus fuerit erit in gloria, et qui inclinaverit oculos suos, ipse salvabitur ».

22. (Vers. 11.). Tertio *confirmans per aequitatem divinam,* adiungit: *Quia omnis, qui se exaltat,* per superbam elationem, secundum illud Iob decimo quinto[^p365-3]: « Quid te elevat cor tuum, et quasi magna cogitans attonitos habes oculos »? — *Humiliabitur,* per iudiciariam districtionem; Isaiae secundo[^p365-4]: « Oculi sublimes hominis humiliabuntur ». « Quia dies Domini super omnem superbum et arrogantem, et humiliabitur ». « Et incurvabitur omnis sublimitas hominum, et humiliabitur altitudo virorum ». Huius exemplum patuit in lucifero; Isaiae decimo quarto[^p365-5]: « Quomodo cecidisti, lucifer, qui mane oriebaris? Qui dicebas in corde tuo: In caelum conscendam, super astra caeli exaltabo solium meum ». Et ideo corruit, quia se exaltavit; unde Ezechielis vigesimo octavo: « Eo quod elevatum est cor tuum sicut cor Dei, idcirco ecce, ego adducam super te alienos, et polluent decorem tuum et interficient te et detrahent te, et morieris in interitu occisorum ». Propter quod dicitur imitatori luciferi, cuilibet superbo, Abdiae primo[^p365-6]: « Superbia cordis tui extulit te habitantem in scissuris petrarum, exaltantem solium tuum »; et post: « Si exaltatus fueris ut aquila, et si inter sidera posueris nidum tuum; inde detraham te ». Ideo consulit Sapiens Ecclesiastici sexto[^p365-7]: « Non te extollas in cogitatione tua velut taurus, ne forte elidatur virtus tua, et relinquaris velut lignum aridum in eremo ».

23. Sic divina iustitia superbos humiliat, et econtra humiles exaltat; ideo subdit: *Et qui se humiliat,* per voluntariam abiectionem; Psalmus[^p365-8]: « Custodiens parvulos Dominus, humiliatus sum, et liberavit me »; et secundi Regum sexto: « Ludam et vilior fiam plus, quam factus sum, et ero humilis in oculis meis »; *exaltabitur,* per divinam glorificationem. Exemplum est in Domino Salvatore; ad Philippenses secundo[^p365-9]: « Humiliavit semetipsum » etc.; et post: « Propter quod et Deus exaltavit illum » etc. Et ideo dicitur primae Petri quinto: « Humiliamini sub potenti manu Dei, ut vos exaltet in tempore visitationis ». Et de his duobus in Psalmo[^p365-10]: « Omnia cornua peccatorum confringam, et exaltabuntur cornua iusti »; et Ezechielis decimo septimo: « Scient omnia ligna regionis, quia ego Dominus humiliavi lignum sublime et exaltavi lignum humile ».

24. Ratio autem huius iustitiae et aequitatis est, quia, quanto quis magis se *exaltat,* tanto minus *honorat Deum* et tanto minus magnificat Deum, ac per hoc et tanto magis est aversus a Deo, et sic per consequens tanto minus est; Ezechielis vigesimo octavo[^p365-11]: « Nihili factus es et non eris in perpetuum ». — Rursus, quanto magis se exaltat, tanto magis *se supra se ipsum erigit,* ac per hoc tanto magis evanescit; et quanto magis evanescit, tanto plus a veritate recedit et tanto minus est: ergo quanto quis magis a se exaltatur, tanto magis deiicitur; Iob trigesimo[^p365-12]: « Elevasti me, et quasi super ventum ponens, elisisti valide ». — Postremo, quanto quis magis se exaltat, tanto *gloriam hominum magis amat;* et quanto illam magis amplectitur, tanto plus est subiectus humanis laudibus, et propter hoc tanto magis hominum servus et tanto magis deiectus; Psalmus[^p365-13]: « Verumtamen propter dolos posuisti eis, deiecisti eos, dum allevarentur ».

25. Sed e contrario, quanto quis magis se *humiliat,* tanto magis *Deum honorat;* Ecclesiastici tertio[^p365-14]: « Magna potentia Dei solius, et ab humilibus honoratur »; et quanto magis Deum honorat, tanto magis ad ipsum appropinquat, ac per hoc tanto elevatur ad sublimiora. — Amplius, quanto magis se humiliat, tanto magis *redit ad interiora;* et quanto magis interius colligitur, tanto virtus eius maior efficitur: quanto ergo quis magis humiliatur, tanto secundum veritatem maior efficitur; unde secundae ad Corinthios duodecimo[^p365-15]: « Virtus in infirmitate perficitur ». — Postremo, quanto magis humiliatur,
<!-- page 366 -->
tanto *terrenam gloriam minus appretiatur;* et quanto minus eam appretiatur, tanto magis eam calcat et tanto magis superponitur omnibus inferioribus, et ita tanto maior efficitur; Isaiae sexagesimo[^p366-1]: « Ponam te in superbiam saeculorum ». — Igitur omnis se exaltans, quanto magis se exaltat, tanto magis *elongat* se a *superioribus;* et quanto se magis elongat a superioribus, tanto magis *recedit ab interioribus,* ac per hoc tanto magis *subiicit se inferioribus.* Ergo quanto quis magis effertur, tanto magis secundum veritatem deiicitur et secundum divinum iudicium est deiiciendus; et e contrario, omnis, qui se humiliat, quanto magis se humiliat[^p366-2], tanto magis *appropinquat ad superiora* et tanto magis *redit ad interiora* et tanto magis *calcat inferiora:* ergo quanto magis se humiliat sub Deo, tanto magis exaltatur divino iudicio.

#### Second the guests at table are formed as to three things.

14. *And he spoke a parable also to them that were invited* etc. After he confuted the watching, here in the second place he *forms the guests at table*[^p362-1]. Now this part is divided into three according to a threefold formation. For first he forms *those invited to the marriage banquet.* Second he forms *those who invite to the familiar banquet,* there[^p362-2]: *And he said to him also that had invited him.* Third he forms *those to be invited to the eternal banquet,* there: *A certain man made a great supper* etc. The first of these regards the banquet *of grace;* the second, *of nature;* and the third, *of glory;* the first, the *sacramental;* the second, the *material;* the third, the *eternal* and *spiritual.*

Forming, then, *those invited to the marriage banquet,* by which is understood the state of the present Church, first he *upbraids vanity and arrogance;* second he *commends humility and reverence,* there[^p362-3]: *But when thou art invited.* — Now in *upbraiding arrogance* three things are brought in, namely the *occasion* introducing the parable, the *persuasion* reproving pride, and the *reason* explaining shamefastness.

15. (Vers. 7.). First, then, as to *the occasion introducing the parable* he says: *And he spoke a parable also to them that were invited;* the Gloss[^p362-4]: « *A parable,* signifying something else mystically ». For since someone could refer that instruction of the Lord to these carnal nuptials, therefore the Evangelist himself, directing the understanding of the expositor, wills it to be understood parabolically. For this was the manner of the Lord Saviour in teaching, according to that of the Psalm[^p362-5]: « I will open my mouth in parables: I will utter propositions from the beginning » etc. And since a parable avails nothing except when it is spoken in its own time, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the twentieth chapter[^p362-6]: « A parable coming out of a fool's mouth shall be rejected: for he doth not speak it in due season »; and on the contrary Proverbs, the twenty-fifth chapter: « Apples of gold on beds of silver, he that speaketh a word in due time »: therefore *the fitness of place* and *of time* is added for the introduction of this parable of the marriage banquet, when there is subjoined: *Marking how they chose the first seats,* namely after the manner of the proud, which manner indeed was in the Pharisees; Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p362-7]: « They love the first places at feasts, and the first chairs in the synagogues, and salutations in the market place »; and this as a sign of honour, according to that of Job, the twenty-ninth chapter: « If I would go to them, I sat first ». For *first* and *highest* are joined together; and just as pride and ambition is an inordinate appetite of superiority, so also of priority. Now such things the proud seek, who desire to be honoured by others, after the example of Saul, the first of Kings, the fifteenth chapter[^p362-8]: « Only honour me before the ancients of my people, and before Israel ».

16. (Vers. 7. 8.). But second, as to *the persuasion dissuading from pride* he subjoins: *Saying to them: When thou art invited to a wedding, sit not down in the first place.* Although this could be understood of *carnal marriage,* yet the text itself that follows and the expositors will it to be understood of *spiritual marriage;* whence the Gloss[^p362-9]: « When through the grace of faith, called by the preacher, thou hast joined thyself to the members of the Church, do not extol thyself, glorying in thy merits, as though thou wert loftier than the rest ». Now this marriage is not any whatever, but *the marriage of the Lamb,* of which Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter[^p362-10]: « The marriage of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath prepared herself ». This *marriage* God the Father made, according to what is said in Matthew, the twenty-second chapter: « The kingdom of heaven is likened to a king, who made a marriage for his son ». This marriage was celebrated in the bridal chamber of the virginal womb; the Psalm[^p362-11]: « He hath set his tabernacle in the sun: and he, as a bridegroom coming out of his bride chamber ». There matrimony was consummated between the divine and the human nature, and consequently between Christ and the Church, according to that to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter[^p362-12]; the Apostle, speaking of matrimony, says: « This is a great sacrament; but I speak in Christ and in the Church ». — The *banquet* at this marriage is in the receiving of the Sacraments of the Church and of the lessons of sacred Scripture; Proverbs, the ninth chapter[^p362-13]: « Wisdom hath built herself a house, she hath hewn her out seven pillars. She hath slain her victims, mingled her wine, and set forth the table. And she hath sent her maids, that they might call to the tower » etc. To this banquet *are invited* all who are called to faith through the preaching of the truth; Matthew, the twenty-second chapter[^p362-14]: « He sent his servants to call them that were invited to the marriage ». — So those invited *sit down* and *eat* by the searching and rumination of the divine words and of the mystical ones. In sign of which that heavenly food is called *manna,* Exodus, the sixteenth chapter[^p362-15],
<!-- page 363 -->
which is interpreted: « What is this »? since one must understand what one receives. Whence also in Leviticus, the eleventh chapter, it is said that the animal that « doth not chew the cud, shall be unclean ». — Now he *sits down in the first place* who is preferred to others, whether in the office of dignity, or in the privileges of holiness, or in the teaching office of truth. And to this no one ought to climb up of himself, since it is said to the Hebrews, the fifth chapter[^p363-1]: « Neither doth any man take the honour to himself, but he that is called by God, as Aaron ». And therefore Ecclesiasticus, the sixth chapter: « Extol not thyself in the thoughts of thy soul like a bull, lest perhaps thy strength be quashed »; nor even *before others;* whence Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter: « Glory not in apparel at any time, and be not exalted in the day of thy honour ».

17. But third, as to *the reason explaining shamefastness* he subjoins: *Lest perhaps one more honourable than thou be invited by him,* worthy of greater honour on account of interior grace; the Gloss[^p363-2]: « More gracious to him who invites, even if he be hidden from others »; whence the first of Kings, the sixteenth chapter: « Look not on his countenance, nor on the height of his stature, because I have rejected him; nor do I judge according to the look of man. For man seeth those things that appear, but the Lord beholdeth the heart ».

18. (Vers. 9.). *And then*[^p363-3] *he that invited thee and him, coming,* by the disposition of justice; Proverbs, the sixteenth chapter: « The weigher of spirits is God »; *say to thee: Give this man place,* on account of the preferment of dignity; the first of Kings, the fifteenth chapter[^p363-4]: « The Lord hath rent thy kingdom from thee, and hath given it to thy neighbour who is better than thee ». — *And then thou shalt begin*[^p363-5] *with blush to take the lowest place,* by a manifest casting down, according to that of the Psalm: « But being exalted I have been humbled and troubled ». And therefore Ecclesiasticus, the thirteenth chapter: « Take heed lest, deceived in folly, thou be humbled »; but he *is deceived by folly* who reckons himself to be something great; to the Galatians, the sixth chapter[^p363-6]: « If any man think himself to be something, whereas he is nothing, he deceiveth himself ». And such a one is humbled by God by a just judgement, according to that above, the first chapter: « He hath put down the mighty from their seat, and hath exalted the humble ». Of such judgement it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter[^p363-7]: « Many tyrants have sat on the throne, and he whom no man would think on hath worn the crown. Many mighty men have been greatly brought down, and the glorious have been delivered into the hands of strangers ». And therefore it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the seventh chapter[^p363-8]: « Seek not of man the leadership, nor of the king the seat of honour »; since, Proverbs, the twentieth chapter, « the inheritance which is hastened after in the beginning, in the end shall be without a blessing ».

19. (Vers. 10.). *But when thou art invited.* After he upbraided arrogance, here in the second place he *invites to reverence*[^p363-9]; which indeed he does *by persuading perfect humility, by assigning the advantage joined to it, by confirming through the divine equity.*

First, then, *persuading perfect humility,* he says: *But when thou art invited,* to the marriage, *go*[^p363-10] *and sit down in the lowest place,* namely by setting thyself after all, according to that of Matthew, the twentieth chapter: « Whosoever will be the greater among you, let him be your minister. And he that will be first among you shall be your servant; even as the Son of man is not come to be ministered unto, but to minister ». Whence he himself sat down again in the lowest place; Isaias, the fifty-third chapter[^p363-11]: « We have seen him the most abject of men, a man of sorrows and acquainted with infirmity; therefore his look was hidden and despised ». — In commendation of this humility the seats of the more excellent persons are more remote from the altar, and in processions those prior in dignity are later, in imitation of Christ himself, who indeed invited to this humility when he washed the feet of all the Apostles; John, the thirteenth chapter[^p363-12]: « If I, Lord and Master, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another's feet. For I have given you an example » etc.; since « the disciple is not above the
<!-- page 364 -->
master » etc. And therefore the Apostle in the first to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter[^p364-1]: « Whereas I was free as to all, I made myself the servant of all »; and again in the fifteenth chapter: « I am the least of the apostles, who am not worthy to be called an apostle »; and to the Ephesians, the third chapter: « To me, the least of all the saints, is given this grace ». And therefore Peter, the prince of the Apostles, said in the first of Peter, the second chapter[^p364-2]: « Be ye subject to every human creature for God's sake ». — *Sit down,* then, *in the lowest place,* by reckoning thyself lower than all.

20. But thou wilt say: How shall I truly reckon myself worse than all, when many are worse? — To this Bernard answers on the Canticles, the thirty-seventh homily[^p364-3]: « For if we knew clearly in what state God holds each one of us, we ought surely to sit neither above nor below, acquiescing to the truth in all things. But now, since this counsel *hath made darkness its covert*[^p364-4], and the word is hidden from us, so that *no one knows whether he be worthy of love, or hatred;* it is assuredly more just and safer that, according to the counsel of the Truth itself, we choose the lowest place for ourselves, from which we may afterwards be led up higher with honour, than that we presume to a higher, from which we must soon withdraw[^p364-5] with blush. There is no danger, therefore, however much thou humble thyself, however much thou reckon thyself less than thou art, that is, than the Truth holds thee. But it is a great evil and a horrible danger if thou extol thyself even a little more than the truth, and if[^p364-6] namely in thy thought thou prefer thyself to him whom perhaps the Truth judges thy equal, or even thy superior. Just as, if thou pass through a door whose lintel, that I may speak to the understanding, is very low, it does no harm however much thou bow thyself; but if thou raise thyself even by the space of a finger's breadth more than the measure of the door allows, thou wilt strike against it and be dashed with a bruised head[^p364-7]. So in the soul no humiliation at all, however great, is to be feared; but even the least rash uprising presumed is to be dreaded and exceedingly feared. Wherefore, O man, do not compare thyself to the greater, nor to the lesser, nor to some, nor to one. For what knowest thou, O man, whether that one whom perhaps thou reckonest the vilest and most wretched of all, whose most wicked and singularly foul life thou abhorrest, and on that account thinkest him to be despised not only in comparison with thyself, who perhaps art already confident that thou livest *soberly and justly and piously*[^p364-8], but even in comparison with all other wicked men, as the most wicked of all: what knowest thou, I say, whether by the change of the right hand of the Most High[^p364-9] he is to be better than thee and than them in himself, and is already so in God? And therefore he willed us to choose not a middling place, not even the next to last, not even a place among the last; but *sit down,* he says, *in the lowest place,* that namely thou mayest sit the last of all, and mayest, I say not merely set thyself before no one, but not even presume to compare thyself with any ».

21. But second, *assigning the advantage joined to it,* he subjoins: *That when he who invited thee cometh, he may say to thee*[^p364-10]*: Friend, go up higher.* He who *invited* us is Christ, who *comes* to us either by *inspiration,* according to that of John, the fourteenth chapter[^p364-11]: « We will come to him, and will make our abode with him ». Or he *comes* by *death;* above, the twelfth chapter: « That when he cometh and knocketh, they may open to him immediately ». Or he *comes* by *the final examination;* Apocalypse, the last chapter: « Behold, I come quickly; and my reward is with me, to render to every man according to his works ». — This one, coming, calls the humble *friends.* For Christ is a true friend, according to that of Proverbs, the seventeenth chapter[^p364-12]: « He that is a friend loveth at all times ». And he acknowledges the humble as friends, because such obey him; John, the fifteenth chapter: « You are my friends, if you do the things that I command you ». And then he calls them not by the name of *servant,* but of *friend;* John, the fifteenth chapter: « I will not now call you servants, but friends, because all things whatsoever I have heard of my Father, I have made known to you ». — To such a one he says: *Go up higher,* that is, to a higher honour and dignity; the Psalm[^p364-13]: « Thy friends, O God, are made exceedingly honourable ». — And therefore he subjoins: *Then shalt thou have glory before them that sit at table with thee,* that is, before all; whence
<!-- page 365 -->
Proverbs, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p365-1]: « Humiliation followeth the proud: and glory shall uphold the humble of spirit »; the Psalm: « I am with him in tribulation, I will deliver him, and I will glorify him. I will fill him with length of days; and I will shew him my salvation ». And on this account Job, the twenty-second chapter[^p365-2]: « He that hath been humbled, shall be in glory: and he that shall bow down his eyes, he shall be saved ».

22. (Vers. 11.). Third, *confirming through the divine equity,* he adds: *Because every one that exalteth himself,* by proud elation, according to that of Job, the fifteenth chapter[^p365-3]: « Why doth thy heart elevate thee, and why, as if thinking great things, dost thou stare with thy eyes »? — *Shall be humbled,* by judicial strictness; Isaias, the second chapter[^p365-4]: « The lofty eyes of man shall be humbled ». « For the day of the Lord is upon every one that is proud and arrogant, and he shall be humbled ». « And all the loftiness of men shall be bowed down, and the haughtiness of men shall be humbled ». The example of this appeared in Lucifer; Isaias, the fourteenth chapter[^p365-5]: « How art thou fallen, O Lucifer, who didst rise in the morning? Who saidst in thy heart: I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above the stars of heaven ». And therefore he fell, because he exalted himself; whence Ezechiel, the twenty-eighth chapter: « Because thy heart is lifted up as the heart of God, therefore, behold, I will bring upon thee strangers, and they shall defile thy beauty, and shall kill thee, and bring thee down, and thou shalt die the death of them that are slain ». On which account it is said to the imitator of Lucifer, to any proud man whatever, Abdias, the first chapter[^p365-6]: « The pride of thy heart hath lifted thee up, who dwellest in the clefts of the rocks, exalting thy throne »; and after: « Though thou be exalted as an eagle, and though thou set thy nest among the stars; thence will I bring thee down ». Therefore the Wise Man counsels, Ecclesiasticus, the sixth chapter[^p365-7]: « Extol not thyself in thy thoughts like a bull, lest perhaps thy strength be quashed, and thou be left as a dry tree in the wilderness ».

23. So the divine justice humbles the proud and, on the contrary, exalts the humble; therefore he subjoins: *And he that humbleth himself,* by voluntary abjection; the Psalm[^p365-8]: « The Lord is the keeper of little ones: I was humbled, and he delivered me »; and the second of Kings, the sixth chapter: « I will play, and I will become meaner than I have been made, and I will be humble in my own eyes »; *shall be exalted,* by divine glorification. The example is in the Lord Saviour; to the Philippians, the second chapter[^p365-9]: « He humbled himself » etc.; and after: « For which cause God also hath exalted him » etc. And therefore it is said in the first of Peter, the fifth chapter: « Be you humbled under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in the time of visitation ». And of these two in the Psalm[^p365-10]: « And I will break all the horns of sinners: but the horns of the just shall be exalted »; and Ezechiel, the seventeenth chapter: « All the trees of the country shall know that I the Lord have humbled the high tree, and exalted the low tree ».

24. Now the reason of this justice and equity is that, the more one *exalts* himself, the less he *honours God* and the less he magnifies God, and by this the more he is turned away from God, and so consequently the less he is; Ezechiel, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p365-11]: « Thou art brought to nothing, and thou shalt not be for ever ». — Again, the more he exalts himself, the more *he raises himself above himself,* and by this the more he vanishes; and the more he vanishes, the further he withdraws from the truth and the less he is: therefore the more one is exalted by himself, the more he is cast down; Job, the thirtieth chapter[^p365-12]: « Thou hast lifted me up, and as it were setting me upon the wind, thou hast mightily dashed me ». — Lastly, the more one exalts himself, the more he *loves the glory of men;* and the more he embraces it, the more he is subject to human praises, and on this account the more he is the servant of men and the more cast down; the Psalm[^p365-13]: « But indeed for deceits thou hast put it to them; thou hast cast them down, when they were lifted up ».

25. But on the contrary, the more one *humbles* himself, the more he *honours God;* Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter[^p365-14]: « For great is the power of God alone, and he is honoured by the humble »; and the more he honours God, the more he draws near to him, and by this the more he is lifted up to loftier things. — Further, the more he humbles himself, the more he *returns to the interior things;* and the more he is gathered inwardly, the greater his power is made: therefore the more one is humbled, the greater he is made according to the truth; whence the second to the Corinthians, the twelfth chapter[^p365-15]: « Power is made perfect in infirmity ». — Lastly, the more he is humbled,
<!-- page 366 -->
the less he *prizes earthly glory;* and the less he prizes it, the more he treads it down and the more he is set over all inferior things, and so the greater he is made; Isaias, the sixtieth chapter[^p366-1]: « I will make thee to be an everlasting glory ». — Therefore every one exalting himself, the more he exalts himself, the more he *removes* himself *far from the superior things;* and the more he removes himself far from the superior things, the more he *withdraws from the interior things,* and by this the more he *subjects himself to the inferior things.* Therefore the more one is borne up, the more he is cast down according to the truth and is to be cast down according to the divine judgement; and on the contrary, every one that humbles himself, the more he humbles himself[^p366-2], the more he *draws near to the superior things* and the more he *returns to the interior things* and the more he *treads down the inferior things:* therefore the more he humbles himself under God, the more he is exalted by the divine judgement.
