# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. VIII, Vers. 11-21
## *Secundo ponitur informatio apostolica in apertis eloquiis per duo*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 193 -->

#### Secundo ponitur informatio apostolica in apertis eloquiis per duo.

17. (Vers. 11.). *Est autem haec parabola.* Postquam praemissa est instructio parabolica in occultis mysteriis, sequitur secunda pars capituli, in qua describitur *informatio apostolica in apertis eloquiis.* Haec autem pars habet duas particulas, in quarum prima *erudiuntur ad intellectum parabolae;* in secunda *invitantur ad communicationem doctrinae,* ibi[^p193-10]: *Nemo autem lucernam accendens* etc. — Quantum ad *explicationem parabolae* attendendum, quod primo explicat eam *generaliter,* deinde *specialiter,* ibi[^p193-11]: *Qui autem secus viam.*

Dicit ergo primo, quasi *generalem* quandam intentionem parabolae praemittens: *Est autem haec parabola,* id est, hic est intellectus parabolae, ut posset dicere illud Ioannis decimo sexto[^p193-12]: « Haec in proverbiis locutus sum vobis. Venit hora, cum iam palam de Patre meo annuntiabo vobis ». — *Semen est verbum Dei.* Est autem divinum semen verbum Dei, quia per illud renascimur, ut fiamus divini; Iacobi primo[^p193-13]: « Voluntarie genuit nos verbo veritatis ». — Iuxta quatuor membra in parabola praemissa quatuor distinguit auditorum genera, in quibus diversimode suscipitur semen divinorum eloquiorum,
<!-- page 194 -->
ita quod unum semen accipit[^p194-1] dispositionem, ut sit in aliis *conculcatum,* in aliis *arefactum,* in aliis *suffocatum* et in aliis *multiplicatum.*

18. (Vers. 12.). Primo igitur quantum ad explicationem *seminis conculcati,* quod est verbum Domini in corde *oblivioso,* dicit: *Quod*[^p194-2] *autem secus viam, hi sunt qui audiunt; quod* non refertur ad semen susceptum, sed ad personas recipientes semen, qui et ipsi dicuntur *semen,* secundum illud Ieremiae trigesimo primo: « Seminabo domum Israel et domum Iuda semine hominum et semine iumentorum », id est perfectorum et imperfectorum. Illi ergo, qui audiunt, semen sunt, sed ideo infructuosi, quia non retinent. — Propter quod addit: *Deinde venit diabolus,* scilicet per suggestionem, *et tollit verbum de corde eorum,* id est de recordatione. Et hoc signatum fuit Iudicum sexto[^p194-3], ubi dicitur: « Cum sevisset Israel, ascendebat Madian et Amalech et vastabant cuncta, prout erant in herbis ». Unde ipse libenter destruit principia bonitatis, quia insidiatur nostrae saluti. — Propter quod adiungit: *Ne credentes salvi fiant.* Sicut enim scriptum est Marci ultimo[^p194-4], « qui crediderit et baptizatus fuerit salvus erit, qui vero non crediderit condemnabitur ». Ipse autem nihil quaerit nisi nostram damnationem tanquam iam damnatus; Apocalypsis duodecimo: « Proiectus est accusator fratrum, qui accusabat eos ante conspectum Dei nostri die ac nocte ».

19. (Vers. 13.). Secundo quantum ad explicationem *seminis arefacti* in corde *desidioso* subditur: *Nam qui supra petram, hi sunt*[^p194-5] *qui, cum audierint, cum gaudio suscipiunt verbum.* Et hoc quidem est laudabile, secundum illud Actuum decimo septimo: « Susceperunt verbum cum omni aviditate ». — Sed tamen in hoc sunt reprehensibiles, quia non fovent nec volunt laborare; et ideo subdit: *Et hi radices non habent,* id est firmitatem caritatis, de qua Apostolus ad Ephesios tertio[^p194-6]: « In caritate radicati et fundati »; sed potius amorem libidinosum sive adulterinum, et ideo non proficiunt; Sapientiae quarto: « Adulterinae plantationes non dabunt radices altas nec stabile firmamentum collocabunt », quia, Osee nono, « radix eorum exsiccata est ». — Et quod isti non habeant radices confirmantes, ostendit, cum adiungit: *Quia*[^p194-7] *ad tempus credunt,* scilicet in tempore prosperitatis, *et in tempore tentationis recedunt,* scilicet a Deo, secundum illud Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo: « Recessit a Deo, salutari suo ». Hoc certe competit huiusmodi desidioso, secundum illud Ecclesiastici sexto[^p194-8]: « Quam aspera est nimium sapientia indoctis hominibus, et non permanebit in illa excors », sed, sicut dicitur Osee septimo, « vae his qui recesserunt a me »! Quocontra Iob vigesimo septimo: « Donec deficiam, non recedam ab innocentia mea ». Et ideo hortatur Ecclesiasticus secundo: « Fili, accedens ad servitutem Dei, sta in iustitia et timore et praepara animam tuam ad tentationem ».

20. (Vers. 14.). Tertio autem quantum ad expositionem *seminis suffocati* in corde *negotioso* subditur: *Quod autem in spinas*[^p194-9] *cecidit, hi sunt qui audierunt,* id est, illi signantur per illud. Et nota, quod *auditus* attribuitur omnibus, ut nullus possit se excusare; ad Romanos decimo: « Nunquid non audierunt? Et quidem in omnem terram exivit sonus eorum, et in fines orbis terrae verba eorum ». — Sed non omnes faciunt fructum spiritualem, quia impediuntur a curis carnalibus; et ideo addit: *Et a sollicitudinibus,* scilicet intra, *et divitiis,* scilicet extra, *et voluptatibus vitae,* scilicet infra; in quo tangitur triplex bonum commutabile, scilicet *interius, exterius* et *inferius; euntes suffocantur,* id est, vitam spiritualem perdunt, quia, secundum quod dicitur primae ad Timotheum sexto[^p194-10], « qui volunt divites fieri incidunt in tentationes et in laqueum diaboli et in desideria multa inutilia et nociva, quae mergunt hominem in interitum et perditionem »; et ideo merito *suffocari* dicuntur. — Et ideo perdendo vitam interiorem, perdunt per consequens efficaciam exteriorem; et ideo subditur: *Et non referunt fructum.* Scribitur enim Ecclesiastae quinto[^p194-11]: « Qui amat divitias fructum non capiet ex eis », quia, sicut dicitur in Psalmo, « dormierunt somnum suum, et nihil invenerunt viri divitiarum in manibus suis »; et ad
<!-- page 195 -->
Romanos sexto: « Quem fructum habuistis in quibus nunc erubescitis »? quasi dicat: nullum. Unde merito divitiae et voluptates comparantur *spinis,* tum quia fructum non afferunt, tum etiam, quia fructum impediunt, tum etiam, quia habentem affligunt, secundum illud Ecclesiastae secundo[^p195-1]: « Peccatori autem dedit Deus afflictionem et curam superfluam, ut addat et congreget. Sed et hoc vanitas et cassa sollicitudo mentis est »; Ecclesiastae quinto: « Dulcis est somnus operanti, sive multum, sive parum comedat; saturitas autem divitis non permittit eum dormire ». Unde quasi super spinas iacet; propter quod dicit Beda[^p195-2]: « Divitiae, etsi delectare videantur, tamen possessoribus suis spinae sunt, quia cum aculeis curarum mentes eorum confodiunt quae avide quaeruntur et sollicite servantur ».

21. (Vers. 15.). Quarto autem ad interpretationem *seminis multiplicati* in corde *fructuoso* subiungitur: *Quod autem in terram bonam,* scilicet cecidit; Numerorum decimo quarto[^p195-3]: « Terra, quam lustravimus, valde bona est »; *hi sunt qui in corde bono,* quoad *cognitivam;* Ecclesiastici trigesimo septimo: « Cor boni consilii statue tecum »; et Ecclesiastici tertio: « Sapiens cor et intelligibile abstinet se a peccato »; *et optimo,* quoad *affectivam;* ad Hebraeos ultimo: « Optimum est gratia stabilire cor ». *Audientes verbum retinent,* quoad *memorativam;* Proverbiorum tertio[^p195-4]: « Fili mi, ne obliviscaris legis meae, et praecepta mea custodiat cor tuum ». *Et fructum afferunt,* quoad *operativam;* Isaiae trigesimo: « Dabitur pluvia semini tuo, ubicumque seminaveris in terra, et panis frugum terrae tuae erit uberrimus ». *In patientia,* quoad potentiam *passivam et supportativam,* in qua est consummatio meriti boni; Iacobi primo[^p195-5]: « Patientia opus perfectum habet, ut sitis perfecti et integri ». Unde ipsa est, quae perducit ad fructum; Iacobi quinto: « Ecce, agricola exspectat pretiosum fructum terrae, patienter ferens, donec accipiat temporaneum et serotinum »; et ad Hebraeos decimo: « Patientia vobis necessaria est, ut voluntatem Dei facientes, reportetis promissionem »; et duodecimo: « Omnis disciplina in praesenti quidem non est gaudii, sed moeroris, postea autem fructum pacatissimum exercitatis per eam reddet iustitiae ».

22. Ad istius tamen explanationis evidentiorem manifestationem notandum, quod *semen est Verbum Dei,* primo, quia tenet rationem *principii activi;* Ioannis octavo[^p195-6]: « Ego principium, qui et loquor vobis »; Matthaei decimo tertio: « Qui seminat bonum semen Filius est hominis », qui scilicet est principium omnis boni. — Secundo, quia tenet rationem *principii propagativi;* Ioannis quinto[^p195-7]: « Pater meus usque modo operatur, et ego operor ». Ideo Marci quarto: « Sic est regnum Dei, quemadmodum si iaciat homo sementem in terram et dormiat » etc.; et Genesis primo: « Germinet terra herbam virentem et facientem semen et lignum pomiferum, cuius semen » etc. — Tertio, quia tenet rationem *principii multiplicativi;* Ioannis duodecimo[^p195-8]: « Nisi granum frumenti cadens in terram, mortuum fuerit, ipsum solum manet; si autem mortuum fuerit, multum fructum affert »; et de hoc primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto: « Insipiens, tu quod seminas non vivificatur, nisi prius moriatur ». Huiusmodi autem semen est Verbum Dei increatum ut *inspiratum,* ut *incarnatum,* ut *praedicatum,* ut *exemplatum*[^p195-9].

23. (Vers. 16.). *Nemo autem accendens lucernam* etc. Postquam eruditi sunt Apostoli ad intellectum parabolae, hic secundo *invitantur ad communicationem doctrinae,* ad quam invitantur Apostoli quadrupliciter: primo, *congruitate generalis exempli;* secundo vero, *necessitate superni iudicii,* ibi[^p195-10]: *Non est enim occultum* etc.; tertio, *utilitate proprii meriti,* ibi: *Videte, quomodo auditis* etc.; quarto vero, *immutabilitate divini beneplaciti,* ibi: *Venerunt autem ad illum* etc. — Circa *congruitatem generalis exempli* duo sunt attendenda: primum est *dissonantia in opposito,* secundum *convenientia in proposito*[^p195-11].

Primo ergo quantum ad *dissonantiam in opposito* dicitur: *Nemo autem lucernam accendens. Lucerna* est verbum Dei, iuxta illud Proverbiorum sexto[^p195-12]: « Mandatum lucerna est, et lex lux, et via vitae increpatio disciplinae »; et in Psalmo: « Lucerna pedibus meis verbum tuum ». Tunc autem *lucerna accenditur,* quando cordi humano intelligentia divini verbi tribuitur. Unde de Ioanne Ioannis quinto: « Ipse erat lucerna ardens et lucens ». — *Operit eam vase.* Lucerna haec non est operienda
<!-- page 196 -->
vase carnalis immunditiae; Osee octavo[^p196-1]: « Israel factus est in nationibus quasi vas immundum ». Sed non est tale vas Paulus, de quo Actuum nono: « Vas electionis est mihi, ut portet nomen meum coram gentibus et regibus et filiis Israel ». Hoc fuit vas apertum, quod illuminavit mundum; Ecclesiastici quadragesimo tertio: « Sol in aspectu annuntians in exitu vas admirabile, opus Excelsi ». — *Aut subtus lectum ponit.* In *lecto* autem accidia designatur; Proverbiorum vigesimo sexto[^p196-2]: « Sicut ostium vertitur in cardine suo, sic piger in lectulo suo ». Hinc est, quod reprehenditur « servus ille, qui abscondit pecuniam domini sui », Matthaei vigesimo quinto, contra illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo nono: « Ne abscondas illam sub lapide in perditionem ». Vult ergo dicere, quod sicut inconveniens est, lucernam accensam abscondere sub vase vel lecto, sic divinam intelligentiam occultare. Et hoc est quod dicitur Ecclesiastici vigesimo[^p196-3]: « Sapientia abscondita et thesaurus invisus, quae utilitas in utrisque »? et post: « Melior est homo, qui abscondit stultitiam, quam qui abscondit sapientiam suam ». Hoc autem intelligitur, quando habet tempus et locum. Nam in Psalmo[^p196-4] dicitur: « In corde meo abscondi eloquia tua, ut non peccem tibi »; et rursum: « Posui ori meo custodiam, cum consisteret peccator adversum me; obmutui et humiliatus sum ».

24. Secundo vero quantum ad *congruitatem in proposito* adiungit: *Sed supra candelabrum ponit*[^p196-5], quantum ad *perfectionem virtutis; ut intrantes videant lumen,* per *apertionem veritatis,* secundum illud Matthaei quinto: « Luceat lux vestra coram hominibus, ut videant opera vestra bona et glorificent Patrem vestrum, qui in caelis est »; ad Philippenses secundo: « Inter quos lucetis sicut luminaria in mundo, verbum vitae continentes ». Illud autem lumen non vident, nisi qui intrant per fidem; quia Isaiae septimo[^p196-6] secundum Septuaginta: « Nisi credideritis, non intelligetis ». Et de hoc fidei ingressu dicitur Ioannis decimo: « Per me si quis introierit, salvabitur »; *introierit,* per fidem, quia « qui crediderit salvus erit ». Isti intrantes per fidem mediante doctrina Sanctorum perveniunt ad intelligentiam sacramentorum, secundum illud Psalmi[^p196-7]: « Illuminans tu mirabiliter a montibus aeternis ». « Qui enim ad iustitiam erudiunt multos, quasi stellae in perpetuas aeternitates », Danielis duodecimo.
Et ideo Dominus dicebat discipulis Matthaei decimo: « Quod dico vobis in tenebris dicite in lumine, et quod in aure auditis praedicate super tecta ».

25. (Vers. 17.). *Non est enim occultum.* Hic secundo invitat eosdem ad communicationem doctrinae *necessitate superni iudicii* dupliciter, tum propter futuram *manifestationem omnis boni,* tum propter futuram *revelationem omnis mali.*

Primo igitur quantum ad futuram *manifestationem omnis boni* dicitur: *Non est enim occultum,* scilicet bonum, *quod non manifestetur,* scilicet per futurum iudicium; unde secundae ad Corinthios quinto[^p196-8]: « Oportet enim, nos omnes manifestari ante tribunal Christi, ut recipiat unusquisque, prout gessit in corpore ». Hoc autem erit in iudicio, de quo primae ad Corinthios quarto dicitur, quod « illuminabit abscondita tenebrarum et manifestabit consilia cordium, et tunc laus erit unicuique »; et hoc, quia lux habet proprietatem manifestativam, secundum illud ad Ephesios quinto: « Quae arguuntur a lumine manifestantur »; unde bona veritas fidei et morum tanquam lux quaerit manifestari, non occultari, secundum illud Ioannis tertio[^p196-9]: « Qui facit veritatem venit ad lucem, ut manifestentur opera eius, quia in Deo sunt facta ». Quod potissime fiet, quando lux illa manifestabitur in iudicio; propter quod in Psalmo: « Qui sedes super Cherubim, manifestare »; et rursus in Psalmo: « Deus manifeste veniet, et non silebit ».

26. Secundo vero quantum ad futuram *revelationem omnis mali* dicitur: *Nec absconditum,* scilicet peccatum vel malum, quod homo libenter abscondit, secundum illud Iob trigesimo primo[^p196-10]: « Si abscondi quasi homo peccatum meum »; *quod non cognoscatur et in palam veniat,* id est in iudicio, secundum illud Proverbiorum vigesimo sexto[^p196-11]: « Qui operit odium fraudulenter, revelabitur malitia eius in concilio ». Unde Ecclesiastici primo: « Ne fueris hypocrita in conspectu hominum »; « ne forte revelet Deus abscondita tua et in medio synagogae elidat te »; quia, sicut dicitur Sapientiae primo[^p196-12], « auris zeli audit omnia, et tumultus murmurationum non abscondetur ». « Ipse enim est, qui revelat profunda et abscondita et novit in tenebris constituta » etc. Quia ergo oportet, omnia manifestari, stultum est ad tempus abscondere; ac per hoc necesse est doctrinam acceptam aliis aperire.
<!-- page 197 -->

27. (Vers. 18.). *Videte ergo, quomodo auditis*[^p197-1] etc. Hic tertio invitantur Apostoli ad idem *utilitate proprii meriti;* et hoc, quia eius *habitus facit opulentum, privatio* vero *reddit egenum.* — Praedicanda est ergo divina doctrina primo, quia *habitus documenti perducit ad opulentiam;* propter quod dicit: *Videte ergo, quomodo auditis;* Beda[^p197-2]: « Ut in vestro pectore suscipiatis et continuo ruminetis et alieno auditui eructare sufficiatis »; quasi dicat: non audiatis verba eruditionis divinae negligenter, ut non curetis, sed diligenter, ut abundetis et aliis tribuatis. Unde et subdit: *Qui enim habet, dabitur ei*[^p197-3]; qui enim habet affectum et voluntatem proficiendi, dabit ei Deus intellectum et facultatem docendi. Unde Sapientiae septimo: « Optavi, et datus est mihi sensus; et invocavi, et venit in me spiritus sapientiae »; et post: « Venerunt mihi omnia bona pariter cum illa ». Unde si quis vult, quod detur sibi abundanter, debet et ipse dare de eo quod habet libenter; supra sexto[^p197-4]: « Date, et dabitur vobis », scilicet donum sapientiae, secundum illud Iacobi primo: « Si quis vestrum indiget sapientia, postulet a Deo, qui dat omnibus affluenter » etc. Unde nulli datur sapientiae donum, nisi habeat desiderium; Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto: « Omnes sitientes venite ad aquas, et qui non habetis argentum properate » etc.

28. Secundo vero, *per contrarium,* quia ipsius *privatio ducit ad inopiam,* adiungit: *Et quicumque non habet,* affectum scilicet divinae doctrinae in corde, *etiam quod putat se habere,* per tumorem superbiae, *auferetur ab eo*[^p197-5], scilicet per rigorem divinae sententiae; infra decimo nono: « Tollite ab eo mnam et date illi qui habet decem mnas »; et Matthaei vigesimo primo: « Auferetur a vobis regnum », scilicet sacrae Scripturae, « et dabitur genti facienti fructus eius ». Ex quo colligitur, quod nullus potest pervenire ad plenitudinem divinae sapientiae, nisi habeat promptitudinem doctrinae communicandae; unde Sapientiae sexto[^p197-6]: « Quid sapientia et quomodo facta sit, referam, et non abscondam a vobis
sacramenta Dei ». « Neque cum invidia tabescente iter habebo; quoniam talis homo non erit particeps sapientiae » Dei.

29. (Vers. 19.). *Venerunt autem ad illum Mater* etc. Hic quarto invitat Apostolos ad doctrinam communicandam *ex imitatione*[^p197-7] *Magistri sui,* scilicet Christi, qui praetulit affectum docendi affectui parentali. Unde duo hic introducuntur: primum est *requisitio propinquorum;* secundum vero, *praeelectio discipulorum.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *parentum requisitionem* dicitur: *Venerunt autem ad illum Mater et fratres eius,* ad quos debet specialis affectus haberi, ad *matrem* quidem, quoniam dicitur Exodi vigesimo[^p197-8]: « Honora patrem tuum et matrem tuam »; et Tobiae quarto: « Honorem habebis matri tuae omnibus diebus vitae eius »; ad *fratres,* scilicet propinquos et cognatos; Levitici decimo nono[^p197-9]: « Diliges fratrem tuum sicut te ipsum ». Horum igitur, quibus debetur praecipua affectio, introducitur requisitio, sed non poterant per se ipsos; unde additur: *Et non poterant adire eum prae turba;* et ideo requirebant per nuntium.

30. (Vers. 20.). Propter quod additur: *Et nuntiatum est illi: Mater tua et fratres tui,* quibus multam debes affectionem ratione propinquitatis, *foris stant, volentes te videre,* per affectum caritatis. Et sic efficaciter requiritur, ut ad propinquos transeat vel exeat tum propter naturam agnoscendam, tum propter caritatem rependendam. Unde super hoc dicit Glossa[^p197-10], Matthaei duodecimo, quod « hoc tergiversando nuntiatum est Christo, ut sciretur, si doctrinam relinqueret ». Sciebant enim, illud esse verum quod dicitur ad Ephesios quinto[^p197-11]: « Nemo unquam carnem suam odio habuit »; et sic volebant experiri, quid amplius acceptaret, utrum affectionem parentum, an eruditionem discipulorum.

31. (Vers. 21.). Secundo vero quantum ad *discipulorum praeelectionem* additur: *Qui respondens dixit ad eos: Mater mea et fratres mei hi sunt, qui verbum Dei audiunt et faciunt.* In quo ostendit,
<!-- page 198 -->
quod magis acceptabat discipulorum eruditionem quam parentum affectionem, et quod magis acceptabat discipulos quam propinquos, et quod magis acceptat[^p198-1] affectum doctorum ad discipulos quam parentum ad filios. — Et nota, quod propter parentum affectum et indigentiam et requisitionem non debet doctor intermittere eruditionem doctrinae; in quo magis invitat facto, quam supra invitaverit verbo.

32. Et nota, quod auditores vocat *matres,* in quantum per documentum alios generant, secundum illud ad Galatas quarto[^p198-2]: « Filioli, quos iterum parturio, donec formetur Christus in vobis ». — *Fratres* vocat, in quantum per verbum generantur et filii Dei fiunt, secundum illud Psalmi: « Narrabo nomen tuum fratribus meis »; et ad Hebraeos secundo: « Non confunditur eos vocare fratres ». Qui enim verbum Dei suscipiunt, illi inseruntur tanquam stipiti radicali; et per consequens rigantur per humorem Spiritus sancti, et ideo efficiuntur filii Dei vivi, secundum illud ad Galatas quarto[^p198-3]: « Quoniam estis filii, misit Deus Spiritum Filii sui in corda vestra, clamantem » etc. Et ideo, quantum praeest spiritus carni, et gratia naturae, et divina humanis, et aeterna caducis, tantum praefertur generatio spiritualis, quae est per verbum vitae[^p198-4], generationi carnali; et ideo huic praeferenda est. Nam propter illam verus praedicator plus afficitur ad filios generatos per praedicationem quam ad parentes carnales. Quod patet, quia Apostoli pro eis confirmandis effuderunt sanguinem proprium, sicut etiam Christus fecerat, qui est magister magistrorum.

33. Et quoniam illi soli semen verbi recte suscipiunt, qui audita exsequuntur; ideo recte addit: *Qui verbum Dei audiunt et faciunt;* quia, ad Romanos secundo[^p198-5], « non auditores Legis iusti sunt apud Deum, sed factores iustificabuntur apud Deum »; et Iacobi primo: « Estote factores verbi et non auditores tantum, fallentes vosmetipsos ». Tales enim praefert Christus parentali cognationi suae, infra undecimo[^p198-6]: « Beatus venter, qui te portavit ». « Quinimmo beati, qui audiunt verbum Dei et custodiunt illud ». Hoc autem dicit, non quia Matrem contemnat, cum ipsam prae omnibus dilexerit, sed ut exemplo ostendat quod praecipit infra decimo quarto: « Qui non reliquerit patrem et matrem et uxorem, filios, fratres et sorores, non potest meus esse discipulus ».

## English
<!-- page 193 -->

#### Secondly, the apostolic formation in open utterances is set forth, through two things.

17. (Vers. 11.). *Now the parable is this.* After the parabolic instruction in hidden mysteries has been set first, there follows the second part of the chapter, in which is described *the apostolic formation in open utterances.* And this part has two small parts, in the first of which *they are schooled unto the understanding of the parable;* in the second *they are invited to the communication of the doctrine,* there[^p193-10]: *Now no man lighting a lamp* etc. — As to *the explanation of the parable* it is to be attended to that he first explains it *generally,* then *specially,* there[^p193-11]: *And they by the wayside.*

He says, then, first, as though setting first a certain *general* intention of the parable: *Now the parable is this,* that is, this is the understanding of the parable, so that he could say that of John, the sixteenth chapter[^p193-12]: « These things I have spoken to you in proverbs. The hour cometh when I will now announce to you plainly of my Father ». — *The seed is the word of God.* Now the divine seed is the word of God, because through it we are born again, that we may become divine; James, the first chapter[^p193-13]: « Of his own will hath he begotten us by the word of truth ». — According to the four members in the parable set before, he distinguishes four kinds of hearers, in whom the seed of the divine utterances is received in diverse ways,
<!-- page 194 -->
so that one seed receives[^p194-1] a disposition such that in some it is *trodden down,* in some *withered,* in some *choked* and in some *multiplied.*

18. (Vers. 12.). First, then, as to the explanation of *the seed trodden down,* which is the word of the Lord in a *forgetful* heart, he says: *And that*[^p194-2] *by the wayside are they that hear; that* is not referred to the seed received, but to the persons receiving the seed, who are themselves also called *seed,* according to that of Jeremias, the thirty-first chapter: « I will sow the house of Israel and the house of Juda with the seed of men and with the seed of beasts », that is, of the perfect and of the imperfect. Those, then, who hear are seed, but they are unfruitful on this account, that they do not retain. — Wherefore he adds: *Then the devil cometh,* namely by suggestion, *and taketh the word out of their heart,* that is, out of their remembrance. And this was signified in Judges, the sixth chapter[^p194-3], where it is said: « When Israel had sown, Madian and Amalec came up and destroyed all things, as they were in the blade ». Whence he gladly destroys the beginnings of goodness, because he lies in wait for our salvation. — Wherefore he adjoins: *Lest believing they should be saved.* For as it is written in Mark, the last chapter[^p194-4], « he that believeth and is baptized shall be saved, but he that believeth not shall be condemned ». But he seeks nothing but our damnation, as being already damned; Apocalypse, the twelfth chapter: « The accuser of our brethren is cast forth, who accused them before our God day and night ».

19. (Vers. 13.). Secondly, as to the explanation of *the seed withered* in a *slothful* heart, there is subjoined: *Now they upon the rock are they*[^p194-5] *who, when they have heard, receive the word with joy.* And this indeed is praiseworthy, according to that of Acts, the seventeenth chapter: « They received the word with all eagerness ». — But yet in this they are reprehensible, that they do not foster it nor will to labour; and therefore he subjoins: *And these have no roots,* that is, the firmness of charity, of which the Apostle speaks to the Ephesians, the third chapter[^p194-6]: « Rooted and founded in charity »; but rather a lustful or adulterous love, and therefore they do not profit; Wisdom, the fourth chapter: « Adulterous plantings shall not strike deep roots nor set any fast foundation », because, Osee, the ninth chapter, « their root is dried up ». — And that these have not confirming roots he shows when he adjoins: *For*[^p194-7] *they believe for a while,* namely in the time of prosperity, *and in time of temptation they fall away,* namely from God, according to that of Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter: « He departed from God his saviour ». This surely befits such a slothful man, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the sixth chapter[^p194-8]: « How very unpleasant is wisdom to the unlearned, and the unwise will not continue with her », but, as is said in Osee, the seventh chapter, « woe to them, for they have departed from me »! On the contrary Job, the twenty-seventh chapter: « Till I die I will not depart from my innocence ». And therefore Ecclesiasticus exhorts, the second chapter: « Son, when thou comest to the service of God, stand in justice and in fear, and prepare thy soul for temptation ».

20. (Vers. 14.). But thirdly, as to the exposition of *the seed choked* in a *busied* heart, there is subjoined: *And that which fell among thorns*[^p194-9], *are they who have heard,* that is, those are signified by it. And note that *hearing* is attributed to all, so that none may be able to excuse himself; to the Romans, the tenth chapter: « Have they not heard? Yes, verily, their sound hath gone forth into all the earth, and their words unto the ends of the whole world ». — But not all make spiritual fruit, because they are hindered by carnal cares; and therefore he adds: *And with the cares,* namely within, *and riches,* namely without, *and pleasures of this life,* namely below; in which is touched a threefold changeable good, namely *interior, exterior* and *inferior; going their way they are choked,* that is, they lose the spiritual life, because, according to what is said in the first to Timothy, the sixth chapter[^p194-10], « they that will become rich fall into temptation and into the snare of the devil and into many unprofitable and hurtful desires, which drown men into destruction and perdition »; and therefore they are deservedly said to be *choked.* — And therefore, by losing the interior life, they lose in consequence the exterior efficacy; and therefore there is subjoined: *And yield no fruit.* For it is written in Ecclesiastes, the fifth chapter[^p194-11]: « He that loveth riches shall reap no fruit from them », because, as is said in the Psalm, « they have slept their sleep, and all the men of riches have found nothing in their hands »; and to the
<!-- page 195 -->
Romans, the sixth chapter: « What fruit therefore had you then in those things of which you are now ashamed »? as if to say: none. Whence riches and pleasures are deservedly compared to *thorns,* both because they do not bear fruit, and also because they hinder fruit, and also because they afflict the one who has them, according to that of Ecclesiastes, the second chapter[^p195-1]: « But to the sinner God hath given vexation and superfluous care, to heap up and to gather together. But this also is vanity and a fruitless solicitude of the mind »; Ecclesiastes, the fifth chapter: « Sleep is sweet to a labouring man, whether he eat little or much; but the fulness of the rich will not suffer him to sleep ». Whence he lies as it were upon thorns; wherefore Bede says[^p195-2]: « Riches, though they seem to delight, are yet thorns to their possessors, because, being greedily sought and anxiously kept, they pierce their minds with the pricks of cares ».

21. (Vers. 15.). But fourthly, for the interpretation of *the seed multiplied* in a *fruitful* heart, there is subjoined: *But that on the good ground,* namely fell; Numbers, the fourteenth chapter[^p195-3]: « The land which we have gone round is very good »; *are they who in a good heart,* as to the *cognitive;* Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-seventh chapter: « Establish within thyself a heart of good counsel »; and Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter: « A wise heart, and which hath understanding, will abstain from sins »; *and a very good one,* as to the *affective;* to the Hebrews, the last chapter: « It is best that the heart be established with grace ». *Hearing the word, keep it,* as to the *memorative;* Proverbs, the third chapter[^p195-4]: « My son, forget not my law, and let thy heart keep my commandments ». *And bring forth fruit,* as to the *operative;* Isaias, the thirtieth chapter: « Rain shall be given to thy seed, wheresoever thou shalt sow in the land, and the bread of the corn of the land shall be most plentiful ». *In patience,* as to the *passive and supportive* power, in which is the consummation of good merit; James, the first chapter[^p195-5]: « Patience hath a perfect work, that you may be perfect and entire ». Whence it is patience that leads through to fruit; James, the fifth chapter: « Behold, the husbandman waiteth for the precious fruit of the earth, patiently bearing till he receive the early and latter rain »; and to the Hebrews, the tenth chapter: « Patience is necessary for you, that, doing the will of God, you may receive the promise »; and the twelfth: « Every chastisement for the present indeed is not of joy, but of sorrow; but afterwards it will yield to them that are exercised by it the most peaceable fruit of justice ».

22. But for a more evident manifestation of this explanation it is to be noted that *the seed is the Word of God,* first, because it holds the account of *an active principle;* John, the eighth chapter[^p195-6]: « The beginning, who also speak unto you »; Matthew, the thirteenth chapter: « He that soweth the good seed is the Son of man », who namely is the principle of every good. — Secondly, because it holds the account of *a propagative principle;* John, the fifth chapter[^p195-7]: « My Father worketh until now, and I work ». Therefore Mark, the fourth chapter: « So is the kingdom of God, as if a man should cast seed into the earth and should sleep » etc.; and Genesis, the first chapter: « Let the earth bring forth the green herb and such as may seed, and the fruit tree, whose seed » etc. — Thirdly, because it holds the account of *a multiplicative principle;* John, the twelfth chapter[^p195-8]: « Unless the grain of wheat falling into the ground die, itself remaineth alone; but if it die, it bringeth forth much fruit »; and of this the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter: « Senseless man, that which thou sowest is not quickened, except it die first ». And such a seed is the uncreated Word of God as *inspired,* as *incarnate,* as *preached,* as *exemplified*[^p195-9].

23. (Vers. 16.). *Now no man lighting a lamp* etc. After the Apostles have been schooled unto the understanding of the parable, here secondly *they are invited to the communication of the doctrine,* to which the Apostles are invited in four ways: first, *by the congruity of a general example;* but secondly, *by the necessity of the supernal judgement,* there[^p195-10]: *For there is not anything secret* etc.; thirdly, *by the usefulness of their own merit,* there: *Take heed how you hear* etc.; but fourthly, *by the immutability of the divine good pleasure,* there: *And there came to him* etc. — Concerning *the congruity of a general example* two things are to be attended to: the first is *the dissonance in the opposite,* the second *the agreement in the proposed*[^p195-11].

First, then, as to *the dissonance in the opposite* it is said: *Now no man lighting a lamp. The lamp* is the word of God, according to that of Proverbs, the sixth chapter[^p195-12]: « The commandment is a lamp, and the law a light, and reproofs of instruction are the way of life »; and in the Psalm: « Thy word is a lamp to my feet ». And then *the lamp is lighted,* when the intelligence of the divine word is bestowed on the human heart. Whence of John it is said in John, the fifth chapter: « He was a burning and a shining lamp ». — *Covereth it with a vessel.* This lamp is not to be covered
<!-- page 196 -->
with the vessel of carnal uncleanness; Osee, the eighth chapter[^p196-1]: « Israel is become among the nations like an unclean vessel ». But Paul is not such a vessel, of whom Acts, the ninth chapter: « He is a vessel of election to me, to carry my name before the Gentiles and kings and the children of Israel ». This was an open vessel, which enlightened the world; Ecclesiasticus, the forty-third chapter: « The sun when it appeareth, shewing forth at its rising, an admirable vessel, the work of the most High ». — *Or putteth it under a bed.* Now by the *bed* acedia is designated; Proverbs, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p196-2]: « As the door turneth upon its hinges, so doth the slothful upon his bed ». Hence it is that « that servant who hid his lord's money » is reproved, Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter, against that of Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-ninth chapter: « Hide it not under a stone to be lost ». He means to say, then, that just as it is unfitting to hide a lighted lamp under a vessel or a bed, so is it to conceal the divine intelligence. And this is what is said in Ecclesiasticus, the twentieth chapter[^p196-3]: « Wisdom that is hid and treasure that is not seen, what profit is there in them both »? and after: « Better is the man that hideth his folly than the man that hideth his wisdom ». But this is understood when it has its time and place. For in the Psalm[^p196-4] it is said: « Thy words have I hidden in my heart, that I may not sin against thee »; and again: « I set a guard to my mouth, when the sinner stood against me; I was dumb and was humbled ».

24. But secondly, as to *the agreement in the proposed,* he adjoins: *But setteth it upon a candlestick*[^p196-5], as to *the perfection of virtue; that they who come in may see the lumen*[^tr-lumen], through *the opening of the truth,* according to that of Matthew, the fifth chapter: « Let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works and glorify your Father who is in heaven »; to the Philippians, the second chapter: « Among whom you shine as lights in the world, holding forth the word of life ». But that lumen they do not see, save those who come in through faith; because in Isaias, the seventh chapter[^p196-6], according to the Septuagint: « If you will not believe, you will not understand ». And of this entrance of faith it is said in John, the tenth chapter: « By me, if any man enter in, he shall be saved »; *enter in,* through faith, because « he that believeth shall be saved ». These, entering in through faith, by means of the doctrine of the Saints come to the intelligence of the sacraments, according to that of the Psalm[^p196-7]: « Thou enlightenest wonderfully from the everlasting hills ». « For they that instruct many to justice shall shine as stars for all eternity », Daniel, the twelfth chapter. And therefore the Lord said to the disciples in Matthew, the tenth chapter: « That which I tell you in the dark, speak ye in the lumen, and that which you hear in the ear, preach ye upon the housetops ».

25. (Vers. 17.). *For there is not anything secret.* Here secondly he invites the same to the communication of doctrine *by the necessity of the supernal judgement* in two ways, both on account of the future *manifestation of every good,* and on account of the future *revelation of every evil.*

First, then, as to the future *manifestation of every good* it is said: *For there is not anything secret,* namely good, *that shall not be made manifest,* namely through the future judgement; whence the second to the Corinthians, the fifth chapter[^p196-8]: « For we must all be manifested before the judgement seat of Christ, that every one may receive according as he hath done in the body ». And this will be in the judgement, of which it is said in the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter, that he « will bring to light the hidden things of darkness and will make manifest the counsels of the hearts, and then shall every man have praise »; and this, because light has a manifestative property, according to that to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter: « All things that are reproved are made manifest by the light »; whence the good truth of faith and of morals, like light, seeks to be made manifest, not to be concealed, according to that of John, the third chapter[^p196-9]: « He that doth truth cometh to the light, that his works may be made manifest, because they are done in God ». Which will come about most of all when that light shall be made manifest in the judgement; wherefore in the Psalm: « Thou that sittest upon the cherubims, be made manifest »; and again in the Psalm: « God shall come manifestly, and shall not keep silence ».

26. But secondly, as to the future *revelation of every evil* it is said: *Nor hidden,* namely sin or evil, which man gladly hides, according to that of Job, the thirty-first chapter[^p196-10]: « If as a man I have hid my sin »; *that shall not be known and come abroad,* that is, in the judgement, according to that of Proverbs, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p196-11]: « He that covereth hatred deceitfully, his malice shall be laid open in the assembly ». Whence Ecclesiasticus, the first chapter: « Be not a hypocrite in the sight of men »; « lest God discover thy secrets and cast thee down in the midst of the congregation »; because, as is said in Wisdom, the first chapter[^p196-12], « the ear of jealousy heareth all things, and the tumult of murmuring shall not be secret ». « For he it is that revealeth deep and hidden things, and knoweth what is in darkness » etc. Since, then, all things must be made manifest, it is foolish to hide them for a time; and by this it is necessary to open the doctrine received to others.
<!-- page 197 -->

27. (Vers. 18.). *Take heed, therefore, how you hear*[^p197-1] etc. Here thirdly the Apostles are invited to the same *by the usefulness of their own merit;* and this, because its *habit makes one opulent,* but its *privation renders one needy.* — The divine doctrine is therefore to be preached, first, because *the habit of instruction leads through to opulence;* wherefore he says: *Take heed, therefore, how you hear;* Bede[^p197-2]: « That you may receive it in your breast and continually ruminate it and suffice to utter it forth to another's hearing »; as if to say: do not hear the words of the divine erudition negligently, so as not to care, but diligently, so as to abound and to bestow on others. Whence he also subjoins: *For he that hath, to him shall be given*[^p197-3]; for he that has the affection and will of profiting, God will give him understanding and the faculty of teaching. Whence Wisdom, the seventh chapter: « I wished, and understanding was given me; and I called upon God, and the spirit of wisdom came upon me »; and after: « Now all good things came to me together with her ». Whence if anyone wills that it be given him abundantly, he ought himself also to give gladly of that which he has; above, the sixth chapter[^p197-4]: « Give, and it shall be given to you », namely the gift of wisdom, according to that of James, the first chapter: « If any of you want wisdom, let him ask of God, who giveth to all men abundantly » etc. Whence to none is the gift of wisdom given unless he have desire; Isaias, the fifty-fifth chapter: « All you that thirst, come to the waters; and you that have no money, make haste » etc.

28. But secondly, *by the contrary,* because its *privation leads to want,* he adjoins: *And whosoever hath not,* namely the affection of the divine doctrine in the heart, *that also which he thinketh he hath,* through the swelling of pride, *shall be taken away from him*[^p197-5], namely through the rigour of the divine sentence; below, the nineteenth chapter: « Take the pound away from him, and give it to him that hath ten pounds »; and Matthew, the twenty-first chapter: « The kingdom shall be taken from you », namely of sacred Scripture, « and shall be given to a nation yielding the fruits thereof ». From which it is gathered that no one can come to the fulness of divine wisdom unless he have a readiness for communicating doctrine; whence Wisdom, the sixth chapter[^p197-6]: « What wisdom is, and what was her origin, I will declare, and I will not hide from you the mysteries of God ». « Neither will I go with consuming envy; for such a man shall not be partaker of wisdom » of God.

29. (Vers. 19.). *And there came to him his mother* etc. Here fourthly he invites the Apostles to communicate doctrine *from the imitation*[^p197-7] *of their Master,* namely Christ, who preferred the affection of teaching to the parental affection. Whence two things are introduced here: the first is *the seeking of the kinsfolk;* but the second, *the choosing beforehand of the disciples.*

First, then, as to *the seeking of the parents* it is said: *And there came to him his mother and his brethren,* toward whom a special affection ought to be had, toward his *mother* indeed, since it is said in Exodus, the twentieth chapter[^p197-8]: « Honour thy father and thy mother »; and Tobias, the fourth chapter: « Thou shalt honour thy mother all the days of her life »; toward his *brethren,* namely his kinsfolk and cousins; Leviticus, the nineteenth chapter[^p197-9]: « Thou shalt love thy brother as thyself ». Of these, then, to whom a principal affection is owed, the seeking is introduced, but they could not by themselves; whence it is added: *And they could not come at him for the crowd;* and therefore they sought him through a messenger.

30. (Vers. 20.). Wherefore it is added: *And it was told him: Thy mother and thy brethren,* to whom thou owest much affection by reason of kinship, *stand without, desiring to see thee,* through the affection of charity. And so it is efficaciously sought that he should pass over or go out to his kinsfolk, both for the acknowledging of nature and for the repaying of charity. Whence upon this the Gloss says[^p197-10], on Matthew, the twelfth chapter, that « this was told to Christ by way of tergiversation, that it might be known whether he would leave off the doctrine ». For they knew that to be true which is said to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter[^p197-11]: « No man ever hated his own flesh »; and so they wished to try which he would accept more, the affection of the parents or the erudition of the disciples.

31. (Vers. 21.). But secondly, as to *the choosing beforehand of the disciples* it is added: *Who answering said to them: My mother and my brethren are they who hear the word of God and do it.* In which he shows
<!-- page 198 -->
that he accepted the erudition of the disciples more than the affection of the parents, and that he accepted the disciples more than the kinsfolk, and that he accepts[^p198-1] the affection of teachers toward their disciples more than that of parents toward their sons. — And note that on account of the affection and the need and the seeking of parents the teacher ought not to leave off the erudition of doctrine; in which he invites more by deed than he had invited above by word.

32. And note that he calls the hearers *mothers,* inasmuch as through instruction they beget others, according to that to the Galatians, the fourth chapter[^p198-2]: « My little children, of whom I am in labour again, until Christ be formed in you ». — He calls them *brethren,* inasmuch as through the word they are begotten and become sons of God, according to that of the Psalm: « I will declare thy name to my brethren »; and to the Hebrews, the second chapter: « He is not ashamed to call them brethren ». For those who receive the word of God are grafted as into a root stock; and in consequence they are watered by the moisture of the Holy Spirit, and therefore they are made sons of the living God, according to that to the Galatians, the fourth chapter[^p198-3]: « Because you are sons, God hath sent the Spirit of his Son into your hearts, crying » etc. And therefore, as much as the spirit is above the flesh, and grace above nature, and divine things above human, and eternal things above perishing ones, so much is the spiritual generation, which is through the word of life[^p198-4], preferred to the carnal generation; and therefore it is to be preferred to this. For on account of it the true preacher is more affected toward the sons begotten through preaching than toward his carnal parents. Which is plain, because the Apostles poured out their own blood for confirming them, as Christ also had done, who is the master of masters.

33. And since those alone rightly receive the seed of the word who carry out what they have heard, therefore he rightly adds: *Who hear the word of God and do it;* because, to the Romans, the second chapter[^p198-5], « not the hearers of the Law are just before God, but the doers shall be justified before God »; and James, the first chapter: « Be ye doers of the word and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves ». For such Christ prefers to his parental kindred, below, the eleventh chapter[^p198-6]: « Blessed is the womb that bore thee ». « Yea rather, blessed are they who hear the word of God and keep it ». But he says this, not because he contemns his Mother, since he loved her before all, but that by example he may show what he enjoins below, the fourteenth chapter: « He that doth not leave father and mother and wife, and children, and brethren and sisters, cannot be my disciple ».
