# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XVIII, Vers. 18-27
## *Primo, commendando paupertatem quadrupliciter*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 460 -->

#### Secundo curiositas in possidendo confutatur dupliciter.

34. *Et interrogavit eum quidam* etc. Post confutationem curiositatis in perscrutando sequitur hic secunda pars huius partis[^p460-10], in qua fit *confutatio curiositatis in possidendo;* et hoc quidem facit dupliciter, primo scilicet *commendando paupertatem,* secundo *approbando liberalitatem,* infra decimo nono[^p460-11]: *Et ingressus perambulabat Iericho.*

#### Primo, commendando paupertatem quadrupliciter.

*Paupertatem* autem *ostendit commendabilem et approbabilem* ex quatuor: primo, ratione *perfectionis in merito suo;* secundo, ratione *imperfectionis in contrario,* ibi[^p460-12]: *His auditis, contristatus est;* tertio, ratione *consummationis in praemio,* ibi: *Ait autem Petrus: Ecce* etc.; quarto, ratione *dignationis Christi in exemplo,* ibi: *Assumsit autem Iesus duodecim discipulos.*

In explicando autem *meritum perfectae paupertatis* quatuor introducuntur, scilicet *interrogatio,* qua quaeritur, quid agendum; *responsio,* qua ostenditur, quo tendendum; *informatio,* qua declaratur iter salutiferum; *exhortatio,* qua suadetur iter perfectum.

35. (Vers. 18.). Primo igitur quantum ad *interrogationem,* qua quaeritur, *quid agendum,* dicit: *Et interrogavit eum quidam princeps.* — Hic videtur, quod Ambrosius et Beda sibi contradicant. Ambrosius enim dicit, quod iste interrogavit Iesum interrogatione *tentativa et curiosa;* et hoc colligitur tum ex hoc, quod Dominus ipsum reprehendit, tum ex hoc, quod ipse Domini consilium non acceptavit. Unde Ambrosius in Glossa[^p460-13]: « Versuta fuit interrogatio, et ideo arguta responsio ». Beda autem dicit, quod
<!-- page 461 -->
interrogavit interrogatione *fideli et studiosa;* quod elicit ex hoc, quod maxime dicitur Dominus Iesus eum dilexisse; unde Beda in Glossa[^p461-1]: « Non putandus est iste, ut quidam aiunt, vel Deum tentasse, vel de sua vita mentitum esse; alioquin, ut Marcus ait, intuitus arcana cordis eius, diligere eum non diceretur Iesus ». — Sed hoc solvitur per quoddam verbum Ioannis Chrysostomi[^p461-2]: « Iste, inquit, dives et tentator et superbus erat, sed Iesus oculis misericordiae intuitus est eum ». Et ita non dilexit in eo quod prius erat per superbiam, sed quod coepit esse per gratiam.

36. Qualicumque autem intentione hoc fecerit, utilem fecit interrogationem; et ideo subdit: *Dicens: Magister bone, quid faciens vitam aeternam possidebo?* Haec est recta et utilis interrogatio, secundum illud Ieremiae sexto[^p461-3]: « State super vias vestras et videte et interrogate de semitis antiquis, quae sit via bona, et ambulate in ea »; et hoc consilio Domini, Matthaei sexto: « Quaerite primum regnum Dei et iustitiam eius », per quam acquiritur regnum; et Tobiae quarto: « Consilium semper a sapiente perquire »; et maxime consilium salutare semper perquirendum est ab uno sapientissimo Christo et summo magistro. Et ideo vocat eum *magistrum,* a quo tanquam a peritissimo petit consilium sanum, secundum illud Ecclesiastici sexto[^p461-4]: « Multi pacifici sint tibi, consiliarius vero unus ex mille ». Et hic Christus est, de quo Canticorum quinto: « Dilectus meus candidus et rubicundus, electus ex millibus »; et Ecclesiastae septimo: « Virum de mille unum reperi ». Ab isto igitur, qui solus est *magister,* secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p461-5]: « Unus est magister vester, Christus », qui solus est *sapientia;* primae ad Corinthios primo: « Christum dicimus Dei virtutem et Dei sapientiam », qui solus est verus *consiliarius,* secundum illud Isaiae nono: « Vocabitur nomen eius admirabilis, consiliarius » etc.; hoc consilium et documentum est requirendum, secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo octavo[^p461-6]: « Ego Dominus docens te utilia, gubernans te in via, qua ambulas ».

37. (Vers. 19.). Secundo quantum ad *responsionem,* qua ostenditur, *quo tendendum,* subditur: *Dixit autem*[^p461-7] *Iesus: Quid me dicis bonum?* quod non dicit arguendo eum tanquam mendacem, sed quia, vocans eum *bonum,* intentionem figebat in humanitate. Unde Augustinus de Trinitate[^p461-8]: « Vocas me, secundum illud quod vides, *magistrum bonum;* huius formae visio non erit in bonum his qui male agunt »; quasi dicat: bonitas, ad quam tendere debes, non est in me secundum quod homo, sed secundum quod Deus. — Et ideo addit: *Nemo bonus nisi solus Deus;* quod non intelligit de bonitate per *participationem,* quae omnibus communicatur a Deo, secundum illud Genesis primo[^p461-9]: « Vidit Deus cuncta, quae fecerat, et erant valde bona »; sed de bonitate per *essentiam,* quae facit beatos et bonos, secundum illud Exodi trigesimo tertio: « Ego ostendam tibi omne bonum »; et hoc solum est desiderandum, secundum illud Proverbiorum undecimo: « Desiderium iustorum omne bonum ». Hoc bonum quaerendum est sicut finis omnis boni; unde Matthaei decimo nono[^p461-10]: « Quid me interrogas de bono? Nemo bonus » etc., id est, bonum finale in solo Deo est, qui est finis omnium, quia « bonum et finis idem ». Et ipse dicit Apocalypsis primo[^p461-11]: « Ego sum alpha et omega, principium et finis »; et ideo Nahum primo: « Bonus Dominus et confortans in die tribulationis et sciens sperantes in se ». Ab hoc bono manat in nos bonitas per Spiritum sanctum; Sapientiae duodecimo[^p461-12]: « O quam bonus et suavis est, Domine, Spiritus tuus in nobis »; et Psalmus: « Quam bonus Israel Deus his qui recto sunt corde ». Haec autem bonitas nostra, ad divinam bonitatem comparata, non est bonitas; unde Iob quarto[^p461-13]: « Nunquid
<!-- page 462 -->
homo Dei comparatione iustificabitur, aut factore suo purior erit »? Ibi Glossa: « Humana iustitia, divinae comparata, iniustitia est ». Et ideo Iob vigesimo quinto[^p462-1]: « Nunquid iustificari potest homo comparatus Deo »? quasi dicat: non; Ecclesiastici decimo octavo: « Deus solus iustificabitur et manet invictus rex in aeternum ». Christus igitur, responsurus quaestioni utili ad salutem totius mundi, tanquam magister morum et moralis totius philosophiae incepit a summo bono per essentiam, a quo et secundum quod et ad quod ordinari debet omne bonum; alioquin desinit esse bonum[^p462-2].

38. (Vers. 20.). Tertio quantum ad *informationem,* qua declaratur *iter salutiferum,* subdit: *Mandata nosti;* quod expressius Matthaei decimo nono[^p462-3]: « Si vis ad vitam ingredi, serva mandata ». Explicat autem ea, cum subinfert: *Non occides,* per iracundiam; primae Ioannis tertio: « Qui odit fratrem suum homicida est »; et ideo Matthaei quinto: « Audistis, quia dictum est antiquis: Non occides ». « Ego autem dico vobis, quia omnis, qui irascitur fratri suo, reus erit iudicio ». *Non moechaberis,* per lasciviam, quia Deuteronomii vigesimo septimo[^p462-4]: « Maledictus omnis, qui dormit cum uxore proximi sui »; et Matthaei quinto: « Audistis, quia dictum est antiquis: Non moechaberis. Ego autem dico vobis, quod omnis, qui viderit mulierem ad concupiscendum eam, iam moechatus est in corde suo ». *Non furtum facies,* per avaritiam; ad Ephesios quarto[^p462-5]: « Qui furabatur iam non furetur, magis autem laboret operando manibus suis » etc. *Non falsum testimonium dices,* per fraudulentiam; Proverbiorum decimo nono[^p462-6]: « Falsus testis non erit impunitus, et qui loquitur mendacia peribit ». *Honora patrem tuum et matrem,* scilicet per beneficentiam; Ecclesiastici tertio: « Qui honorat patrem suum iucundabitur in filiis. Et qui honorat matrem suam vita vivet longiore; et qui obedit patri refrigerabit matrem ». Unde Ambrosius[^p462-7]: « Primus gradus pietatis est iste; nam hos auctores tibi voluit esse Deus. Honora obsequiis et abstineas a contumeliis; quia nec vultu quidem laedenda pietas est parentum »; ideo Ecclesiastici tertio[^p462-8]: « Qui timet Dominum honorat parentes et quasi dominis serviet his qui se genuerunt ».

39. Attende autem hic, quod Dominus enumerat hic praecepta *ordinate* et *sufficienter: ordinate* quidem, quia prius est declinare a malo quam facere bonum[^p462-9]; et ideo prius ponit negativa quam affirmativa. *Sufficienter,* quia mandata primae tabulae non sunt de agendis, sed potius dirigunt in illud summum bonum[^p462-10] desiderandum; duo vero ultima de concupiscentia uxoris et rei alienae reducuntur ad prohibitionem moechiae et furti. Unde in hoc sufficienter docet declinare omnem *deordinationem* respectu proximi et quantum ad irascibilem et quantum ad concupiscibilem, et quantum ad operativam et quantum ad interpretativam; et docet etiam perfectam *beneficentiam* per ultimum mandatum. Et sic in his continentur duo praecepta legis naturalis, de quorum primo Tobiae quarto[^p462-11]: « Quod ab alio oderis tibi fieri vide, ne tu aliquando alteri facias »; de secundo Matthaei septimo: « Omnia, quaecumque vultis, ut faciant vobis homines, et vos eadem facite illis ». In his etiam continetur sufficienter mandatum de dilectione proximi, de qua dicitur ad Romanos decimo tertio: « Qui diligit proximum legem implevit ».

40. (Vers. 21.). Et quoniam haec mandata non iustificant per simplicem notitiam, sed per experimentalem observantiam; ideo non quaerebat Dominus de notitia simplici, cum dicebat: *Mandata nosti,* sed de experimentali, quia, sicut dicitur ad Romanos secundo[^p462-12], « non auditores legis, sed factores legis iusti sunt apud Deum ». Ideo ad hoc respondet iste legisperitus sicut intelligens, cum sequitur: *Qui ait: Haec omnia custodivi a iuventute mea;* et hoc quidem secundum consilium Sapientis Proverbiorum tertio[^p462-13]: « Fili, ne obliviscaris legis meae, et praecepta mea cor tuum custodiat. Longitudinem enim dierum et annos vitae et pacem apponent tibi »; quia dicitur Ecclesiastae octavo: « Qui custodit praeceptum non experietur quidquam mali ».

41. (Vers. 22.). Quarto quantum ad *exhortationem,* qua suadetur *paupertatis iter perfectum,*
<!-- page 463 -->
subditur: *Quo audito, Iesus ait illi*[^p463-1]*: Adhuc unum tibi deest;* Glossa: « Ad perfectionem ». Est autem perfectum, cui nihil deest; Iacobi primo: « Sitis perfecti et integri, in nullo deficientes ». Et hoc fit perfecte implendo voluntatem divinam, non solum faciendo praecepta et vitando prohibita, verum etiam implendo consilia, secundum illud ad Romanos duodecimo[^p463-2]: « Probetis, quae sit voluntas Dei bona et beneplacens et perfecta »; et supra sexto: « Perfectus autem omnis erit, si sit sicut magister eius », id est Christus, qui nihil prorsus habuit. — Propter quod adiungit: *Omnia, quaecumque habes, vende et da pauperibus;* Glossa[^p463-3]: « Non partem », sicut fecit Ananias, de quo dicitur Actuum quinto, quod « fraudavit de pretio agri »; et hoc, quia « regnum caelorum, ut dicit Gregorius[^p463-4], tantum valet, quantum habes ». « Simile est regnum caelorum thesauro abscondito in agro » etc. Et ne hoc grave videatur, adiungit: *Et habebis thesaurum in caelo;* Iob vigesimo secundo[^p463-5]: « Dabit pro terra silicem, et pro silice torrentes aureos »; et hi sunt in gloria aeterna, ubi sunt thesauri incorruptibiles; Matthaei sexto: « Thesaurizate vobis thesauros in caelo, ubi neque aerugo nec tinea demolitur, nec fures effodiunt nec furantur ». — Et « quia, ut dicit Glossa[^p463-6], non sufficit relinquere sua, nisi sequatur Dominum imitando »; ideo subdit: *Et veni, sequere*[^p463-7] *me,* per summam *vitae asperitatem;* secundum illud primae Petri secundo: « Christus passus est pro nobis, vobis relinquens exemplum » etc. Per summam *rerum mendicitatem.* Nam discipulo volenti eum sequi ex cupiditate et dicenti: « Magister, sequar te, quocumque ieris », respondet: « Vulpes foveas habent, et volucres caeli nidos; Filius autem hominis non habet, ubi caput suum reclinet », Matthaei octavo[^p463-8]. Per summam *cordis humilitatem;* Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio: « Gloria magna est sequi Dominum »; et Ioannis octavo: « Qui sequitur me non ambulat in tenebris ». Et de his supra nono[^p463-9]: « Qui vult venire post me abneget semetipsum et tollat crucem suam et sequatur me ». *Sequi* igitur *Christum* est se perfecte illi configurare, secundum illud Iob vigesimo tertio: « Vestigia eius secutus est pes meus, viam eius custodivi nec declinavi ex ea » etc.

42. (Vers. 23.). *His auditis*[^p463-10]*, contristatus est* etc. Postquam ostendit paupertatem commendabilem ratione perfectionis suae in merito, hic ostendit eam *commendabilem ratione imperfectionis in suo contrario*[^p463-11], quod quidem est tenacitas divitiarum. Circa quod quatuor introducuntur ab Evangelista, scilicet humanae tenacitatis *indicium, periculum, obstaculum* et *remedium,* quod est divinae gratiae donum.

Primo igitur quantum ad *humanae tenacitatis indicium* dicitur: *His auditis, contristatus est, quia dives erat valde.* Quia enim abundabat divitiis, horruit penuriam paupertatis, et ideo de paupertatis laude et consilio tristis effectus fuit. Et haec est sequela cupiditatis et indicium tenacitatis, cum quis tristatur pro horrore paupertatis; Ecclesiastici decimo quarto[^p463-12]: « Insatiabilis oculus cupidi in parte iniquitatis; non satiabitur, donec consumat arefaciens animam suam ». « Et non satiabitur pane indigens et in tristitia erit super mensam suam ». Haec autem tristitia mala est, quia est tristitia saeculi, de qua secundae ad Corinthios septimo[^p463-13]: « Saeculi tristitia mortem operatur ». Nam dicitur Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo: « A tristitia festinat mors et cooperit virtutem, et tristitia cordis flectit cervicem ». Qui igitur hanc tristitiam vult fugere, oportet, eum odire cupiditatem divitiarum, quae pungunt ad modum spinarum, sicut insinuatur Matthaei decimo tertio[^p463-14]. Nam de divite dicitur Iob decimo quinto: « Sonitus terroris semper in auribus, et cum pax sit, ille insidias suspicatur ». His autem contrarii sunt viri
<!-- page 464 -->
iusti et perfecti Christiani, ad quos dicitur ad Hebraeos decimo[^p464-1]: « Rapinas bonorum vestrorum cum gaudio suscepistis, cognoscentes, vos meliorem et manentem habere substantiam ».

43. (Vers. 24.). Secundo quantum ad *humanae tenacitatis periculum* subditur: *Videns autem illum Iesus tristem factum,* propter affectum pusillanimitatis; quocontra Ecclesiastici septimo[^p464-2]: « Noli esse pusillanimis in animo tuo. Exorare et facere eleemosynam ne despicias ». — *Dixit: Quam difficile qui pecunias habent in regnum Dei intrabunt!* propter vitium tenacitatis; Glossa[^p464-3]: « *Qui pecunias habent,* id est, cum amore possident et in eis confidunt ». Unde Marci decimo dicitur expressius: « Quam difficile confidentes in divitiis in regnum Dei introibunt »! Et ratio huius est, quia qui temporalia hic amplectuntur tanquam magna contemnunt illa aeterna; unde Glossa[^p464-4]: « Qui divitiis multiplicandis incumbunt, alterius vitae gaudia contemnunt ». — Praeterea, qui haec temporalia diligunt multis tentationum iaculis exponuntur; primae ad Timotheum ultimo[^p464-5]: « Qui volunt divites fieri incidunt in tentationem et in laqueum diaboli et in desideria multa inutilia et nociva, quae mergunt hominem » etc. — Alia ratio est, quia qui his affectu iunguntur haud facile separantur; unde et de talibus in Psalmo[^p464-6]: « Quoniam humiliata est in pulvere anima nostra, conglutinatus est in terra venter noster ». Unde Bernardus[^p464-7]: « Plus mundi concupiscentia quam substantia nocet. Et haec fugiendarum divitiarum causa praecipua est, quia aut vix aut nunquam absque amore valent possideri. Limosa enim et glutinosa est substantia mundi huius, et cor humanum facile omnibus, quae frequentat, adhaeret ». Et ideo Glossa[^p464-8]: « Difficulter divitiae contemnuntur; de omnibus ergo tutius est divitias nec habere nec amare ». In quo dat intelligere quatuor hominum differentias, quas exponit Beda[^p464-9], et habentur in Glossa super decimum Marci: « Multi habent et non amant; multi non habent et amant; alii habent et amant; alii vero nec habere nec amare se gaudent, qui tutiores sunt, quia cum Apostolo dicere possunt: *Mihi mundus crucifixus est, et ego mundo* », ad Galatas sexto.

44. (Vers. 25.). Tertio quantum ad *ipsius tenacitatis obstaculum* subiungit: *Facilius est enim, camelum per foramen acus transire,* licet sit contra naturam, *quam divitem intrare in regnum Dei,* quia hoc est contra divinam iustitiam. Hoc enim minus impossibile est, quod res magna pertranseat per parvam acum, quam quod anima cupida et avara intret in caelum, ubi est sufficientia bonorum; quia, sicut dicit Ecclesiastes quinto[^p464-10], « avarus non implebitur pecunia, et qui amat divitias fructum non capiet ex eis », immo certe interitum sempiternum, quia pondus divitiarum trahit deorsum; Ecclesiastae quinto: « Divitiae conservatae in malum domini sui. Pereunt in afflictione pessima ». Unde quantum distat caelum a terra, tantum distat cor avari, quod circa terrena conversum est, a regno Dei, quod est in caelo. Propter quod Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto[^p464-11]: « Sicut exaltantur caeli a terra, sic exaltatae sunt viae meae a viis vestris, et cogitationes meae a cogitationibus vestris ». Et attendendum, quod recta est comparatio avari *ad camelum,* quia camelus ingentibus membris est animal enorme[^p464-12], monstruosum et gibbosum; sic avarus, per amorem copulatus suis possessionibus, est quasi animal monstruosum. Aditus autem regni caelestis comparatur *foramini acus* propter arctitudinem et spiritualitatem; unde Matthaei septimo[^p464-13]: « Angusta est porta et arcta via, quae ducit ad vitam, et pauci intrant per illam ». Unde maior est impossibilitas ipsius avari ad aditum in regnum caelorum quam cameli ad transeundum foramen acus. Propter quod Bernardus[^p464-14]: « Per angustam contendamus ingredi portam, necesse est. Quid, tu camele, gibbum, quid, tu pecuniose, Iudae proditoris loculos tollis? Non sic, impie, non sic ingredieris; foramen acus huiusmodi sarcinas non admittit. Sed excusant se aliqui dicentes: Abraham, Isaac et Iacob ceterique Sancti nunquid non terrenas divitias habuisse leguntur[^p464-15]? Sufficit nobis; neque enim sumus patribus meliores ». — « Quid respondebimus
<!-- page 465 -->
novis imitatoribus Sanctorum veterum? Imponant vitulos super altare Domini, mactent arietes, hircos immolent, quia etiam hoc Abraham fecit[^p465-1]. Sicut autem sacrificiis illis, sic et divitiis carnalibus adhuc populis dispensatio divina morem gerebat; ubi sane caelestis promissio sonuit, necesse est, spiritualia spiritualibus comparari[^p465-2], et mutari sacrificium, spe mutata ».

45. (Vers. 26.). Quarto quantum ad *ipsius tenacitatis remedium* per divinum adiutorium subdit: *Et dixerunt qui*[^p465-3] *audiebant: Et quis potest salvus fieri?* Quod dicunt quaerentes remedium et horrentes periculum. Cum enim paucissimi sint, qui divitias perfecte contemnant; videtur ex hoc, quod quasi nullus possit adipisci salutem perpetuam. Unde Augustinus[^p465-4]: « Cum plures sint pauperes quam divites, non hoc dixissent Apostoli, nisi in numero *divitum* intellexissent cunctos, qui divitias adipisci volunt »; pauci autem sunt, qui non sunt tales, quia, sicut dicitur ad Philippenses secundo, « omnes quae sua sunt quaerunt, non quae sunt Iesu Christi ». Omnes igitur in periculo damnationis esse videntur.

46. (Vers. 27.). Ideo Dominus ostendit remedium per suae gratiae donum, cum subdit: *Ait*[^p465-5] *illis: Quae impossibilia sunt apud homines,* per virtutem virium suarum, *possibilia sunt apud Deum,* id est, possibilia fiunt per eius adiutorium; quod dicit, quia, etsi difficile sit haec temporalia contemnere per vim naturae, efficitur tamen facile per donum gratiae, secundum illud Canticorum ultimo[^p465-6]: « Si dederit homo omnem substantiam domus suae pro dilectione, quasi nihil despiciet eam ». Unde non est intelligendum, quod Dominus cupidum manentem cupidum introducat per potentiam in caelum, sed quod per gratiae donum convertit ipsum de vitio ad virtutem. Unde Glossa[^p465-7]: « Deus ab amore terrenorum ad caritatem aeternorum convertit, non quod superbi et cupidi intrent ». Et hoc est magnae potentiae, quod tanta fiat mutatio, ut de cupido fiat caritativus, de terreno caelestis, de divite pauper, de superbo humilis, de filio perditionis filius regis, ut possit dici illud Psalmi[^p465-8]: « Haec mutatio dexterae Excelsi »; quia ipse est, qui potest dare sufficientiam cordis per gratiam, quae non potest dari per pecuniam, vel aliquam creaturam, secundum illud secundae ad Corinthios nono[^p465-9]: « Potens est Deus omnem gratiam abundare facere in vobis, ut in omnibus semper omnem sufficientiam habentes » etc., quia, sicut dicitur supra primo, « non erit impossibile apud Deum omne verbum ».

## English

#### Second, curiosity in possessing is confuted in two ways.

34. *And a certain one asked him* etc. After the confutation of curiosity in searching there follows here the second part of this part[^p460-10], in which there is made *the confutation of curiosity in possessing;* and this he does in two ways, namely first *by commending poverty,* second *by approving liberality,* below, the nineteenth chapter[^p460-11]: *And entering in, he walked through Jericho.*

#### First, by commending poverty in four ways.

*Poverty,* moreover, *he shows to be commendable and approvable* from four things: first, by reason *of its perfection in its merit;* second, by reason *of the imperfection in its contrary,* there[^p460-12]: *He having heard these things, became sorrowful;* third, by reason *of its consummation in the reward,* there: *Then Peter said: Behold* etc.; fourth, by reason *of the condescension of Christ in example,* there: *Then Jesus took unto him the twelve disciples.*

And in explaining *the merit of perfect poverty* four things are introduced, namely *the questioning,* by which it is asked what is to be done; *the response,* by which it is shown whither one must tend; *the formation,* by which the saving way is declared; *the exhortation,* by which the perfect way is urged.

35. (Vers. 18.). First, then, as to *the questioning,* by which it is asked *what is to be done,* he says: *And a certain ruler asked him.* — Here Ambrose and Bede seem to contradict one another. For Ambrose says that this man asked Jesus with a *tempting and curious* questioning; and this is gathered both from this, that the Lord reproves him, and from this, that he did not accept the Lord's counsel. Whence Ambrose in the Gloss[^p460-13]: « Crafty was the questioning, and therefore shrewd the response ». But Bede says that
<!-- page 461 -->
he asked with a *faithful and studious* questioning; which he draws out from this, that the Lord Jesus is said most of all to have loved him; whence Bede in the Gloss[^p461-1]: « This man is not to be thought, as some say, either to have tempted God or to have lied about his life; otherwise, as Mark says, Jesus, looking on the secrets of his heart, would not be said to love him ». — But this is solved by a certain word of John Chrysostom[^p461-2]: « This man, he says, was rich and a tempter and proud, but Jesus looked on him with the eyes of mercy ». And so he did not love in him that which he was before through pride, but that which he began to be through grace.

36. But with whatever intention he did this, he made a useful questioning; and therefore he subjoins: *Saying: Good master, what shall I do to possess everlasting life?* This is a right and useful questioning, according to that of Jeremias, the sixth chapter[^p461-3]: « Stand upon your ways, and see, and ask concerning the old paths, which is the good way, and walk in it »; and this by the Lord's counsel, Matthew, the sixth chapter: « Seek ye first the kingdom of God and his justice », through which the kingdom is acquired; and Tobias, the fourth chapter: « Seek counsel always of a wise man »; and most of all saving counsel is always to be sought from the one most wise Christ and highest master. And therefore he calls him *master,* from whom, as from one most skilled, he asks sound counsel, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the sixth chapter[^p461-4]: « Be at peace with many, but let one of a thousand be thy counsellor ». And this is Christ, of whom the Canticle, the fifth chapter: « My beloved is white and ruddy, chosen out of thousands »; and Ecclesiastes, the seventh chapter: « One man among a thousand I have found ». From him, then, who alone is *master,* according to that of Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p461-5]: « One is your master, Christ », who alone is *wisdom;* the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter: « We say Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God », who alone is the true *counsellor,* according to that of Isaias, the ninth chapter: « His name shall be called wonderful, counsellor » etc.; this counsel and instruction is to be sought, according to that of Isaias, the forty-eighth chapter[^p461-6]: « I the Lord teaching thee profitable things, governing thee in the way that thou walkest ».

37. (Vers. 19.). Second, as to *the response,* by which it is shown *whither one must tend,* it is subjoined: *And Jesus*[^p461-7] *said: Why dost thou call me good?* which he does not say in reproving him as a liar, but because, in calling him *good,* he fixed his intention on the humanity. Whence Augustine, *On the Trinity*[^p461-8]: « Thou callest me, according to that which thou seest, *good master;* the vision of this form will not be unto good for those who do evil »; as if he should say: the goodness to which thou oughtest to tend is not in me according as I am man, but according as I am God. — And therefore he adds: *None is good but God alone;* which he does not understand of goodness by *participation,* which is communicated to all things by God, according to that of Genesis, the first chapter[^p461-9]: « God saw all the things that he had made, and they were very good »; but of goodness by *essence,* which makes blessed and good, according to that of Exodus, the thirty-third chapter: « I will shew thee all good »; and this alone is to be desired, according to that of Proverbs, the eleventh chapter: « The desire of the just is all good ». This good is to be sought as the end of every good; whence Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p461-10]: « Why askest thou me concerning good? None is good » etc., that is, the final good is in God alone, who is the end of all things, because « the good and the end are the same ». And he himself says in the Apocalypse, the first chapter[^p461-11]: « I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end »; and therefore Nahum, the first chapter: « The Lord is good, and giveth strength in the day of trouble, and knoweth them that hope in him ». From this good goodness flows into us through the Holy Spirit; Wisdom, the twelfth chapter[^p461-12]: « O how good and sweet, O Lord, is thy Spirit in us »; and the Psalm: « How good is God to Israel, to them that are of a right heart ». But this goodness of ours, compared to the divine goodness, is not goodness; whence Job, the fourth chapter[^p461-13]: « Shall
<!-- page 462 -->
man be justified in comparison of God, or shall he be more pure than his maker »? There the Gloss: « Human justice, compared to the divine, is injustice ». And therefore Job, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p462-1]: « Can man be justified compared with God »? as if he should say: no; Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter: « God alone shall be justified, and he remaineth an invincible king for ever ». Christ, then, being about to respond to a question useful to the salvation of the whole world, as master of morals and of the whole of moral philosophy began from the highest good by essence, from which and according to which and to which every good ought to be ordered; otherwise it ceases to be good[^p462-2].

38. (Vers. 20.). Third, as to *the formation,* by which *the saving way* is declared, he subjoins: *Thou knowest the commandments;* which Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p462-3], has more expressly: « If thou wilt enter into life, keep the commandments ». And he explains them when he infers: *Thou shalt not kill,* through wrath; the first of John, the third chapter: « He that hateth his brother is a murderer »; and therefore Matthew, the fifth chapter: « You have heard that it was said to them of old: Thou shalt not kill ». « But I say to you, that whosoever is angry with his brother shall be in danger of the judgement ». *Thou shalt not commit adultery,* through lasciviousness, because Deuteronomy, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p462-4]: « Cursed be every one that lieth with the wife of his neighbour »; and Matthew, the fifth chapter: « You have heard that it was said to them of old: Thou shalt not commit adultery. But I say to you, that whosoever shall look on a woman to lust after her hath already committed adultery in his heart ». *Thou shalt not steal,* through avarice; to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter[^p462-5]: « He that stole, let him now steal no more; but rather let him labour, working with his hands » etc. *Thou shalt not bear false witness,* through fraudulence; Proverbs, the nineteenth chapter[^p462-6]: « A false witness shall not be unpunished, and he that speaketh lies shall perish ». *Honour thy father and mother,* namely through beneficence; Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter: « He that honoureth his father shall have joy in his children. And he that honoureth his mother shall enjoy a long life; and he that obeyeth the father shall be a comfort to his mother ». Whence Ambrose[^p462-7]: « This is the first step of piety; for God willed these to be thy authors. Honour them with services and abstain from insults; for the piety of parents is not to be wounded even by a look »; therefore Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter[^p462-8]: « He that feareth the Lord honoureth his parents, and will serve them as his masters that begot him ».

39. But attend here that the Lord here enumerates the precepts *orderly* and *sufficiently: orderly* indeed, because it is prior to decline from evil than to do good[^p462-9]; and therefore he puts the negative before the affirmative. *Sufficiently,* because the commandments of the first table are not about things to be done, but rather direct to that highest good[^p462-10] which is to be desired; and the last two, of the concupiscence of a wife and of another's thing, are reduced to the prohibition of adultery and of theft. Whence in this he sufficiently teaches to decline every *disordering* with respect to the neighbour, both as to the irascible and as to the concupiscible, and as to the operative and as to the interpretative; and he teaches also perfect *beneficence* through the last commandment. And so in these are contained the two precepts of the natural law, of the first of which Tobias, the fourth chapter[^p462-11]: « See thou never do to another what thou wouldst hate to have done to thee by another »; of the second Matthew, the seventh chapter: « All things whatsoever you would that men should do to you, do you also the same to them ». In these is also sufficiently contained the commandment of the dilection of the neighbour, of which it is said to the Romans, the thirteenth chapter: « He that loveth his neighbour hath fulfilled the law ».

40. (Vers. 21.). And since these commandments do not justify through simple knowledge, but through experiential observance; therefore the Lord was not asking about simple knowledge when he said: *Thou knowest the commandments,* but about experiential, because, as is said to the Romans, the second chapter[^p462-12], « not the hearers of the law, but the doers of the law are just before God ». Therefore this lawyer answers this as one understanding, when there follows: *Who said: All these things have I kept from my youth;* and this indeed according to the counsel of the Wise Man, Proverbs, the third chapter[^p462-13]: « My son, forget not my law, and let thy heart keep my precepts. For they shall add to thee length of days, and years of life and peace »; because it is said in Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter: « He that keepeth the precept shall experience no evil ».

41. (Vers. 22.). Fourth, as to *the exhortation,* by which *the perfect way of poverty* is urged,
<!-- page 463 -->
it is subjoined: *Which when Jesus had heard, he said to him*[^p463-1]*: Yet one thing is wanting to thee;* the Gloss: « For perfection ». Now that is perfect to which nothing is wanting; James, the first chapter: « That you may be perfect and entire, failing in nothing ». And this is done perfectly by fulfilling the divine will, not only by doing the precepts and avoiding the things prohibited, but also by fulfilling the counsels, according to that to the Romans, the twelfth chapter[^p463-2]: « That you may prove what is the will of God, good and acceptable and perfect »; and above, the sixth chapter: « But every one shall be perfect, if he be as his master », that is, Christ, who had nothing at all. — On account of which he adds: *Sell all whatever thou hast, and give to the poor;* the Gloss[^p463-3]: « Not a part », as Ananias did, of whom it is said in the Acts, the fifth chapter, that « he defrauded of the price of the land »; and this because « the kingdom of heaven, as Gregory says[^p463-4], is worth as much as thou hast ». « The kingdom of heaven is like unto a treasure hidden in a field » etc. And lest this seem grievous, he adds: *And thou shalt have treasure in heaven;* Job, the twenty-second chapter[^p463-5]: « He shall give for earth flint, and for flint torrents of gold »; and these are in eternal glory, where the treasures are incorruptible; Matthew, the sixth chapter: « Lay up to yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither the rust nor moth doth consume, nor thieves break through nor steal ». — And « because, as the Gloss says[^p463-6], it does not suffice to leave one's own, unless one follow the Lord by imitating »; therefore he subjoins: *And come, follow*[^p463-7] *me,* through the highest *asperity of life;* according to that of the first of Peter, the second chapter: « Christ suffered for us, leaving you an example » etc. Through the highest *beggary of things.* For to the disciple wishing to follow him out of cupidity and saying: « Master, I will follow thee whithersoever thou shalt go », he responds: « The foxes have holes, and the birds of the air nests; but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head », Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p463-8]. Through the highest *humility of heart;* Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third chapter: « It is great glory to follow the Lord »; and John, the eighth chapter: « He that followeth me walketh not in darkness ». And of these above, the ninth chapter[^p463-9]: « If any man will come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross and follow me ». *To follow Christ,* then, is to configure oneself perfectly to him, according to that of Job, the twenty-third chapter: « My foot hath followed his steps, I have kept his way and have not declined from it » etc.

42. (Vers. 23.). *He having heard these things*[^p463-10]*, became sorrowful* etc. After he showed poverty commendable by reason of its perfection in merit, here he shows it *commendable by reason of the imperfection in its contrary*[^p463-11], which indeed is the tenacity of riches. Concerning which four things are introduced by the Evangelist, namely the *indication, peril, obstacle* and *remedy* of human tenacity, which is the gift of divine grace.

First, then, as to *the indication of human tenacity* it is said: *He having heard these things, became sorrowful, for he was very rich.* For because he abounded in riches, he shuddered at the penury of poverty, and therefore was made sorrowful at the praise and counsel of poverty. And this is the sequel of cupidity and the indication of tenacity, when one is saddened for the horror of poverty; Ecclesiasticus, the fourteenth chapter[^p463-12]: « The eye of the covetous man is insatiable in his portion of iniquity; he will not be satisfied till he consume his own soul, drying it up ». « And the needy man shall not have his fill of bread, and shall be in sorrow at his own table ». But this sorrow is evil, because it is the sorrow of the world, of which the second to the Corinthians, the seventh chapter[^p463-13]: « The sorrow of the world worketh death ». For it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter: « Of sorrow death hasteneth, and it covereth strength, and the sorrow of the heart boweth down the neck ». He, then, who wishes to flee this sorrow must hate the cupidity of riches, which prick after the manner of thorns, as is intimated in Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p463-14]. For of the rich man it is said in Job, the fifteenth chapter: « The sound of dread is always in his ears, and when there is peace, he suspecteth treason ». But contrary to these are the
<!-- page 464 -->
just and perfect Christians, to whom it is said to the Hebrews, the tenth chapter[^p464-1]: « You took with joy the spoilings of your goods, knowing that you have a better and a lasting substance ».

43. (Vers. 24.). Second, as to *the peril of human tenacity* it is subjoined: *And Jesus seeing him become sorrowful,* on account of the affection of pusillanimity; against which Ecclesiasticus, the seventh chapter[^p464-2]: « Be not pusillanimous in thy mind. Despise not to pray and to give alms ». — *He said: How hardly shall they that have money enter into the kingdom of God!* on account of the vice of tenacity; the Gloss[^p464-3]: « *They that have money,* that is, possess it with love and trust in it ». Whence in Mark, the tenth chapter, it is said more expressly: « How hardly shall they that trust in riches enter into the kingdom of God »! And the reason of this is that they who embrace temporal things here as great despise those eternal things; whence the Gloss[^p464-4]: « They who apply themselves to multiplying riches despise the joys of the other life ». — Moreover, they who love these temporal things are exposed to many darts of temptations; the first to Timothy, the last chapter[^p464-5]: « They that will become rich fall into temptation and into the snare of the devil and into many unprofitable and hurtful desires, which drown a man » etc. — Another reason is that they who are joined to these by affection are not easily separated; whence also of such in the Psalm[^p464-6]: « For our soul is humbled down to the dust; our belly cleaveth to the earth ». Whence Bernard[^p464-7]: « The concupiscence of the world harms more than its substance. And this is the chief cause of fleeing riches, that they can scarcely or never be possessed without love. For the substance of this world is slimy and gluey, and the human heart easily cleaves to all things that it frequents ». And therefore the Gloss[^p464-8]: « Riches are despised with difficulty; of all, therefore, it is safer neither to have riches nor to love them ». In which he gives to understand four differences of men, which Bede sets out[^p464-9], and they are had in the Gloss on the tenth of Mark: « Many have and do not love; many do not have and love; some have and love; but some rejoice neither to have nor to love, who are the safer, because with the Apostle they can say: *The world is crucified to me, and I to the world* », to the Galatians, the sixth chapter.

44. (Vers. 25.). Third, as to *the obstacle of this tenacity* he subjoins: *For it is easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle,* although it be against nature, *than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of God,* because this is against the divine justice. For this is less impossible, that a great thing should pass through a small needle, than that a covetous and avaricious soul should enter into heaven, where is the sufficiency of goods; because, as Ecclesiastes says, the fifth chapter[^p464-10], « the avaricious man shall not be filled with money, and he that loveth riches shall reap no fruit from them », nay rather, certainly, eternal destruction, because the weight of riches drags downward; Ecclesiastes, the fifth chapter: « Riches kept to the hurt of their owner. They are lost with very great affliction ». Whence as far as heaven is distant from earth, so far is the heart of the avaricious man, which is turned about earthly things, distant from the kingdom of God, which is in heaven. On account of which Isaias, the fifty-fifth chapter[^p464-11]: « As the heavens are exalted above the earth, so are my ways exalted above your ways, and my thoughts above your thoughts ». And it must be attended that the comparison of the avaricious man *to the camel* is right, because the camel is an animal enormous with huge members[^p464-12], monstrous and humpbacked; so the avaricious man, coupled by love to his possessions, is as it were a monstrous animal. But the entrance of the heavenly kingdom is compared *to the eye of a needle* on account of its narrowness and spirituality; whence Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p464-13]: « Narrow is the gate and strait is the way that leadeth to life, and few enter by it ». Whence the impossibility of the avaricious man for entrance into the kingdom of heaven is greater than that of the camel for passing through the eye of a needle. On account of which Bernard[^p464-14]: « Through the narrow gate let us strive to enter; it is necessary. Why, thou camel, dost thou carry thy hump, why, thou moneyed man, the purse of Judas the traitor? Not so, wicked one, not so shalt thou go in; the eye of a needle does not admit burdens of this kind. But some excuse themselves, saying: Abraham, Isaac and Jacob and the other Saints, are they not read to have had earthly riches[^p464-15]? It suffices us; for we are not better than the fathers ». — « What shall we answer to
<!-- page 465 -->
these new imitators of the old Saints? Let them lay calves upon the altar of the Lord, let them slay rams, let them immolate goats, because Abraham also did this[^p465-1]. But as the divine dispensation humoured the people, still carnal, with those sacrifices, so also with riches; but when the heavenly promise sounded, it is necessary that spiritual things be compared with spiritual[^p465-2], and that the sacrifice be changed, the hope being changed ».

45. (Vers. 26.). Fourth, as to *the remedy of this tenacity* through the divine help he subjoins: *And they that*[^p465-3] *heard said: And who can be saved?* Which they say seeking a remedy and shuddering at the peril. For since there are very few who perfectly despise riches; it seems from this that almost no one can obtain perpetual salvation. Whence Augustine[^p465-4]: « Since the poor are more than the rich, the Apostles would not have said this unless they had understood in the number *of the rich* all who wish to obtain riches »; but few there are who are not such, because, as is said to the Philippians, the second chapter, « all seek the things that are their own, not the things that are Jesus Christ's ». All, then, seem to be in peril of damnation.

46. (Vers. 27.). Therefore the Lord shows the remedy through the gift of his grace, when he subjoins: *He said*[^p465-5] *to them: The things that are impossible with men,* by the power of their own strength, *are possible with God,* that is, they become possible through his help; which he says because, although it be difficult to despise these temporal things by the force of nature, it is nevertheless made easy by the gift of grace, according to that of the Canticle, the last chapter[^p465-6]: « If a man should give all the substance of his house for love, he shall despise it as nothing ». Whence it is not to be understood that the Lord brings the covetous man, remaining covetous, into heaven by power, but that by the gift of grace he converts him from vice to virtue. Whence the Gloss[^p465-7]: « God converts from the love of earthly things to the charity of eternal things, not that the proud and the covetous should enter ». And this is of great power, that so great a change should be made, that from the covetous man there be made a charitable one, from the earthly a heavenly, from the rich a poor, from the proud a humble, from a son of perdition a son of the king, so that that of the Psalm may be said[^p465-8]: « This is the change of the right hand of the most High »; because he it is who can give sufficiency of heart through grace, which cannot be given through money, or any creature, according to that of the second to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter[^p465-9]: « God is able to make all grace abound in you, that ye always, having all sufficiency in all things » etc., because, as is said above, the first chapter, « no word shall be impossible with God ».
