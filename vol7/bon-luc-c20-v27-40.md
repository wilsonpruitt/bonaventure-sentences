# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XX, Vers. 27-40
## *Primo confutatur error circa resurrectionem per duo*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 512 -->

#### Confutatio Sadducaeorum errantium ex ignorantia quoad duo.

34. *Accesserunt autem quidam.* Postquam confutavit infidelitatem errantium ex malitia, hic secundo[^p512-6] confutat *incredulitatem errantium ex ignorantia;* et habet haec pars duas. In prima confutat errorem circa *resurrectionem;* in secunda vero, circa *incarnationem,* ibi[^p512-7]: *Dixit autem ad illos.*

#### Primo confutatur error circa resurrectionem per duo.

In describendo autem *confutationem erroris Sadducaeorum de finali resurrectione* procedit hoc ordine: primo namque praemittit *quaestionem erroneam;* secundo adiungit *responsionem veridicam,* ibi[^p512-8]: *Et ait illis Iesus: Filii huius* etc.

Circa *quaestionem erroneam* introducuntur Sadducaei ut *negantes verum,* ut *allegantes praeceptum,* ut *proponentes casum,* ut *interrogantes dubium,* per quod praecipitabantur in errorem praedictum.

35. (Vers. 27.). Primo igitur quantum ad *errorem Sadducaeorum negantium verum* dicit: *Accesserunt autem quidam Sadducaeorum, qui negant, esse resurrectionem,* et per consequens destruebant fundamentum divinae culturae. Unde Chrysostomus[^p512-9]: « In omni re aut actu agendi virtus spes est praemii futuri. Qui enim arat arat, ut metat; et qui pugnat pugnat, ut vincat ». Et post: « Tolle ergo spem resurrectionis, et soluta est tota observantia pietatis »; primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p512-10]: « Quid facient qui baptizantur pro mortuis, si omnino mortui non resurgunt? Ut quid et baptizantur pro illis? Ut quid et nos periclitamur omni hora »? Resurrectionem autem negabant Sadducaei tum propter animarum mortalitatem, quam credebant; unde Glossa[^p512-11]: « Sadducaei credebant, animas interire cum corpore »; tum etiam propter corporum irreparabilitatem. — Et pro eis videtur esse quod dicitur in Psalmo[^p512-12]: « Exibit spiritus eius et revertetur in terram suam » etc.; item: « Sepulcra eorum domus illorum in aeternum »; et Iob decimo quarto: « Homo, cum dormierit, non resurget, donec atteratur caelum » etc. Sed hic non dicunt, resurrectionem non esse futuram, sed potius usque ad finem iudicii differendam. — Nam quod *spiritus* sint immortales, hoc probat ipsorum *intellectus, appetitus* et *actus virtuosus*[^p512-13]; quod vero *corpora* reparentur, hoc manifestat simul divina *iustitia,* divina *clementia* et divina *potentia.* Unde contra tales dicitur primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p512-14]: « Sed dicet aliquis: Quomodo resurgent mortui? Aut quali corpore venient? Insipiens tu, quod seminas non vivificatur, nisi prius moriatur. Et quod seminas, non corpus, quod futurum est, seminas, sed nudum granum, utputa tritici, aut alicuius ceterorum. Deus enim dat illi corpus, sicut vult et unicuique seminum proprium corpus »; et post[^p512-15]: « Oportet, corruptibile hoc induere incorruptionem, et mortale hoc induere immortalitatem ».

36. (Vers. 27. 28.). Secundo quantum ad *inquisitionem Sadducaeorum allegantium praeceptum*
<!-- page 513 -->
subditur: *Et interrogaverunt eum dicentes: Magister, Moyses scripsit nobis,* scriptura scilicet authentica et iusta, secundum illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto[^p513-1]: « Legem mandavit Moyses in praeceptis iustitiarum »; unde Deuteronomii trigesimo primo: « Postquam scripsit Moyses verba Legis huius in volumine atque complevit, praecepit Levitis, qui portabant arcam foederis, dicens: Tollite librum istum et ponite in latere arcae » etc. Quidquid igitur Moyses scripsit, est authenticum, ideo scripturam eius tanquam authenticam proponunt. — Propter quod subditur: *Si frater alicuius mortuus fuerit habens uxorem, et hic sine liberis fuerit, ut accipiat eam frater eius uxorem et suscitet semen fratri suo.* Hoc scribitur Deuteronomii vigesimo quinto[^p513-2]: « Quando habitaverint fratres simul, et unus ex illis absque liberis mortuus fuerit, uxor defuncti non nubet alteri, sed accipiet eam frater eius et suscitabit semen fratris sui et primogenitum ex ea filium nomine fratris sui appellabit, ut non deleatur nomen eius ex Israel ».

37. *Litteralis* autem ratio huius erat ad fovendam caritatem inter propinquos et refrenandam[^p513-3] cupiditatem et servandam fidelitatem et conservandam generationem carnalem. Secundum *spiritualem* intelligentiam *frater noster* est Christus; Canticorum octavo[^p513-4]: « Quis det te mihi fratrem meum, sugentem ubera matris meae » etc.; et Psalmus: « Narrabo nomen tuum fratribus meis ». *Uxor eius* est Ecclesia; Apocalypsis decimo nono[^p513-5]: « Venerunt nuptiae Agni, et uxor eius praeparavit se ». Hic *mortuus est* ante fidelium multitudinem, qui sunt filii Dei; Ioannis primo: « Dedit eis potestatem filios Dei fieri » etc., et hoc in *morte;* Ioannis undecimo[^p513-6]: « Iesus moriturus erat pro gente, et non tantum pro gente, sed ut filios Dei, qui erant dispersi, congregaret in unum ». Ut *frater* huius uxorem accipit qui gerit Ecclesiae curam, non ut generet filios carnales, sed spirituales, sicut Paulus; primae ad Corinthios quarto[^p513-7]: « In Christo Iesu per Evangelium ego vos genui ». Et hos non nomine proprio, sed *fratris* debet appellare, quia non in nomine suo, sed Christi debet eos baptizare, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios primo: « Nunquid Paulus pro vobis crucifixus est? Aut in nomine Pauli baptizati estis »? Ei autem, qui hoc negligit implere, imponi debet poena *discalceationis,* secundum illud Deuteronomii vigesimo quinto[^p513-8]: « Si responderit: Nolo eam uxorem accipere; accedet mulier ad eum coram senioribus populi et tollet calceamentum de pede eius et spuet in faciem eius et dicet: Sic fiet homini, qui non aedificat domum fratris sui »; quia ei qui non vult semen Christi suscitare, debet auferri auctoritas praedicandi, quae intelligitur per ablationem calceamenti, secundum illud ad Ephesios sexto[^p513-9]: « Calceati pedes in praeparationem Evangelii pacis ». Propter quod Deuteronomii vigesimo tertio[^p513-10]: « Non intrabit eunuchus, attritis vel amputatis testiculis, Ecclesiam Domini ». Quocontra dicitur de viris sanctis Ecclesiastici quadragesimo quarto: « Cum semine eorum permanent bona, hereditas sancta nepotes eorum ».

38. (Vers. 29. 30. 31. 32.). Tertio quantum ad *allegationem ipsorum introducentium casum* subditur: *Septem ergo fratres erant, et primus accepit uxorem et mortuus est sine filiis. Et sequens accepit illam, et ipse mortuus est sine filio. Et tertius accepit illam. Similiter et omnes septem et non reliquerunt semen et mortui sunt. Novissima*[^p513-11] *omnium mortua est et mulier.* Potuit esse, quod hoc fingerent tanquam possibile; unde Glossa[^p513-12]: « Qui resurrectionem corporum non credunt et animas putant interire cum corpore turpitudinem fabulae confingunt ». Talibus fabulis vacare solent Iudaei; propter quod primae ad Timotheum primo[^p513-13]: « Rogavi te, ut remaneres Ephesi, ut denuntiares quibusdam, ne aliter docerent neque intenderent fabulis et genealogiis interminatis, quae quaestiones magis praestant quam aedificationem ». — Vel potuit esse, quod hoc fuit ad litteram et introducatur hic secundum divinae ordinationis spiritum propter figuram. Nam *spiritualiter* per istos *septem,* qui mortui sunt sine liberis, intelligitur universitas reproborum, qui nullum fructum reliquerunt; unde Glossa[^p513-14]: « Hi septem omnes reprobi sunt, qui tota hac vita, quae septem
<!-- page 514 -->
diebus est, steriles a bono opere sunt ». Hi possunt intelligi per *septem viros Sarae,* de quibus dicitur Tobiae tertio[^p514-1], quod interfecti fuerunt a daemonio, nomine Asmodaeo; quia Sapientiae tertio dicitur: « Ab iniquo toro semen exterminabitur ». — Vel per *septem* viros intelliguntur septem capitalia vitia, quibus anima servit, de quibus Lucae undecimo[^p514-2]: « Assumit septem spiritus nequiores se ». — Vel quinque sensus et duplex consideratio rationis, quae intelliguntur per *mulierem* septemviram; de qua dicitur Ioannis quarto[^p514-3]: « Voca virum tuum ». Qui enim subiicit se sensibus carnis fructum non parit; Iob vigesimo quarto: « Pavit sterilem, quae non parit, et viduae non bene fecit »; quia, sicut dicitur ad Galatas sexto, « qui seminat in carne de carne et metet corruptionem »; et primae Ioannis secundo: « Transit mundus et concupiscentia eius ».

39. (Vers. 33.). Quarto quantum ad *cavillationem Sadducaeorum interrogantium dubium* subditur: *In resurrectione ergo*[^p514-4] *cuius eorum erit uxor? Siquidem septem habuerunt eam uxorem,* quasi dicant: non magis est unius quam alterius: ergo aut habebunt omnes, aut nullus. Si ergo utrumque est inconveniens, contra Legem est ponere resurrectionem. — Ex quo apparet, quod isti errabant circa resurrectionem propter carnalem intellectum spiritualis Scripturae et spiritualis gloriae; unde carnalis intelligentia necat, dum spirituales depravat Scripturas; propter quod secundae ad Corinthios tertio[^p514-5]: « Littera occidit, spiritus autem vivificat ». Ideo Ioannis sexto: « Verba, quae locutus sum vobis, spiritus et vita sunt »; propter quod primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto: « Hoc autem dico, quia caro et sanguis regnum Dei non possidebunt, neque corruptio incorruptionem », id est, qui carnaliter sapiunt. Et tales erant hi Sadducaei, qui, non intelligentes nobilitatem spiritus rationalis nec intellectum Scripturae spiritualis nec potestatem Dei omnipotentis, non putabant resurrectionem futuram. Ideo dicitur Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p514-6]: « Erratis, nescientes Scripturam neque virtutem Dei ».

40. (Vers. 34.). *Et ait illis Iesus* etc. Post inquisitionem erroneam subiungit hic *responsionem veridicam*[^p514-7]. Circa quam introducuntur quatuor, scilicet *veritatis assertio, assertionis ratio, rationis confirmatio* et *confirmationis laudatio.*

Primo quantum ad *veritatis assertionem* dicit: *Et ait illis Iesus: Filii huius saeculi nubunt et traduntur ad nuptias,* propter *habendam successionem,* sicut Tobiae octavo[^p514-8]: « Tu, Domine, scis, quia non luxuriae causa accipio sororem meam coniugem, sed sola posteritatis dilectione, in qua benedicatur nomen tuum in saecula saeculorum ». Et hoc matrimonium, licet posset esse virorum spiritualium, non est tamen nisi in carne viventium; unde *filii saeculi* non sonat in vitium. Alii vero nubunt propter *solam delectationis expletionem,* de quibus Genesis sexto[^p514-9]: « Videntes filii Dei filias hominum, quod essent pulcrae, acceperunt sibi uxores ex omnibus, quas elegerant »; et supra decimo quarto: « Uxorem duxi, et ideo non possum venire ». Et tunc *filii saeculi* sonat in vitium, sicut et supra decimo sexto: « Filii huius saeculi prudentiores sunt filiis lucis » etc. Et hi sunt *filii ancillae,* de quibus ad Galatas quarto: « Non heres erit filius ancillae cum filio liberae ». Sed neutrum horum erit in resurrectionibus, nec successio nec carnalis delectatio.

41. (Vers. 35.). Ideo subdit: *Illi vero, qui digni habebuntur*[^p514-10] *saeculo illo et resurrectione ex mortuis, neque nubent neque ducent uxores; neque nubent,* quantum ad feminas, *neque ducent uxores,* quantum ad viros. *Dignos resurrectione* vocat iustos. Loquitur enim hic specialiter de resurrectione iustorum, qui non tantum resurgent, verum etiam immutabuntur, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p514-11]: « Omnes quidem resurgemus, sed non omnes immutabimur ». « Canet enim tuba, et mortui resurgent incorrupti ». Licet igitur mali resurgant cum bonis, sicut dicitur Ioannis quinto[^p514-12]: « Venit hora, in qua omnes, qui in monumentis sunt, audient vocem Filii Dei »; tamen de bonis specialiter loquitur, qui erunt elevati supra tempus et temporales motus, ac per hoc et supra coniugium et omnem carnalem actum.

42. (Vers. 36.). Secundo quantum ad *assertionis rationem* subdit: *Neque enim ultra mori*
<!-- page 515 -->
*poterunt;* ex quo sequitur, quod uxores non habebunt. Unde Beda[^p515-1]: « Cum connubia propter filios, filii propter successionem, successio sit propter mortem; ubi ergo non est mors, neque connubia ». — Sed tunc potest ex hoc argui, quod cum in paradiso non esset mors ante peccatum, quod connubia non fuissent. — Et responsio est ad hoc, quod licet non esset mors ex *necessitate,* sicut nunc, erat tamen ex *possibilitate,* quia primi parentes habebant corpus *animale,* sed post resurrectionem erit corpus *spirituale,* secundum illud primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p515-2]: « Seminatur corpus animale, surget spirituale; seminatur in infirmitate, surget in virtute; seminatur in ignobilitate, surget in gloria ». — Ideo subdit: *Aequales enim Angelis sunt et filii sunt Dei, cum sint filii resurrectionis,* id est, sicut Angeli sunt immortales, incorruptibiles, supra tempus et variationes motuum temporalium; sic erunt boni, quia post resurrectionem tunc erimus aequales Angelis secundum gloriae dignitatem; propter quod Apocalypsis vigesimo primo[^p515-3]: « Mensura hominis, quae est Angeli ». Nec mirum, quia tunc erimus Deo per gloriam assimilati, secundum illud primae Ioannis tertio[^p515-4]: « Nunc filii Dei sumus, et nondum apparuit, quid erimus. Scimus, quoniam, cum apparuerit, similes ei erimus, quoniam videbimus eum, sicuti est ». Unde Rabanus[^p515-5]: « Sunt sicut Angeli Dei in caelis, qui gloria resurrectionis innovati, absque ullo mortis metu, absque ulla labe corruptionis, absque ullo terreni status actu perpetua Dei visione fruuntur. Ad quam necesse est, angelicae dignitatis aequalitatem quisquis ascendere desiderat, minimis fratribus pie agendo condescendat ». Et hoc recte dicit, quia non potest Angelis esse in gloria similis qui non vult in ministerio assimilari.

43. (Vers. 37.). Tertio quantum ad *rationis confirmationem* subiungitur: *Quia vero resurgant*[^p515-6] *mortui, et Moyses ostendit secus rubum,* id est verbum Domini, quod dixit Moysi in visione illius rubi ardentis et non combusti, secundum quod dicitur Exodi tertio[^p515-7]: « Apparuit ei Dominus in flamma ignis de medio rubi, et videbat, quod rubus arderet et non combureretur »; et sequitur, quod Dominus locutus est verba, quae sequuntur hic: *Sicut dicit Dominum Deum Abraham et Deum Isaac et Deum Iacob;* Exodi tertio[^p515-8]: « Vocavit Dominus Moysen de medio rubi » « et ait: Ego sum Deus Abraham et Deus Isaac et Deus Iacob ».

44. (Vers. 38.). Ex hoc autem vult Dominus inferre resurrectionem futuram, cum addit: *Deus autem non est mortuorum, sed viventium*[^p515-9]. *Omnes enim vivunt ei.* Ex quo sequitur, quod vivunt ei vel actu vel potentia; sed omnes isti erant mortui actualiter: ergo vivebant Deo quantum ad *posse* et dispositionem. Necesse est igitur futuram ponere resurrectionem[^p515-10]. — Sunt autem multa testimonia in veteri testamento, per quae probari potest resurrectio mortuorum; Isaiae vigesimo sexto[^p515-11]: « Vivent mortui tui, interfecti mei resurgent »; et Ezechielis trigesimo septimo: « Ossa arida, audite verbum Domini: Ecce » etc.; item, Danielis duodecimo: « Multi de his qui dormiunt in terrae pulvere, evigilabunt »; item, Iob decimo nono: « Scio, quia Redemptor meus vivit, et in novissimo die de terra surrecturus sum »; item, Osee sexto[^p515-12]: « Post duos dies vivificabit nos, in die tertia suscitabit nos »; item, Ioelis tertio: « Consurgant et ascendant gentes in vallem Iosaphat » etc.; item, secundi Machabaeorum septimo: « Ipse spiritum cum misericordia vobis dabit et vitam ». — Sed Dominus nullum horum septem testium introduxit, quia Sadducaei, quibus respondebat, solum Pentateuchum recipiebant; unde Glossa[^p515-13]: « Poterat apertiora de resurrectione dare testimonia Prophetarum, sed Prophetas Sadducaei non recipiunt ». Vel ideo, quia per hoc probat vitam animarum post mortem, quam Sadducaei negabant, et haec erat radix sui erroris. Vel ideo, quia in istius verbi allegatione simul clauditur mysterium *Trinitatis* in trina *Dei* iteratione, mysterium *triplicis virtutis,* scilicet fidei, spei et caritatis, in tribus
<!-- page 516 -->
Patribus praedictis, mysterium *incarnationis* in rubo ardenti[^p516-1] et etiam mysterium *passionis,* et in tota auctoritate mysterium *resurrectionis,* et ita quasi totum mysterium *fidei christianae;* quia omnis mysteriorum figura et verborum varietas consummata est in Verbo incarnato, secundum illud Isaiae vigesimo octavo[^p516-2]: « Consummationem et abbreviationem audivi » etc.; et ad Hebraeos primo: « Multifarie » etc.

45. (Vers. 39.). Quarto quantum ad *confirmationis collaudationem* adiungit: *Respondentes autem quidam scribarum dixerunt ei: Magister, bene dixisti.* Hoc commendabant scribae, quia erant de secta Pharisaeorum, qui dicunt, resurrectionem esse. Unde Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p516-3] dicitur, quod « Pharisaei videntes, quod silentium imposuisset Sadducaeis, convenerunt in unum », quia ex hoc applaudebant ei. De hoc enim erat contentio inter Sadducaeos et Pharisaeos; unde Actuum vigesimo tertio[^p516-4]: « Sciens Paulus, quia una pars est Sadducaeorum et altera Pharisaeorum, exclamavit in concilio: Viri fratres, ego Pharisaeus sum; de spe et resurrectione mortuorum ego iudicor. Et cum haec dixisset, facta est dissensio inter Pharisaeos et Sadducaeos, et soluta multitudo. Sadducaei enim dicunt, non esse resurrectionem mortuorum neque Angelum neque spiritum, Pharisaei autem utrumque confitentur ».

46. (Vers. 40.). Quoniam ergo utrosque confuderat[^p516-5], ideo utrique tacebant; unde subditur: *Et amplius non audebant eum quidquam interrogare;* ex quo apparet, quod non interrogabant studio addiscendi, sed vincendi et superandi; unde Glossa[^p516-6]: « Confutati in sermonibus, non amplius interrogant, sed aperte comprehensum Romanae potestati tradunt. Ex quo intelligimus, venena invidiae posse superari, sed difficile quiescere ». Ideo non audebant interrogare eum, quia videbant aliquid divinum in se habere, cum quo disputare tutum non est; Iob nono[^p516-7]: « Quantus sum, ut respondeam ei et loquar verbis meis cum eo? Qui, etiam si habuero quippiam iustum, non respondebo, sed meum iudicem deprecabor ». Verificatum est igitur in his illud Psalmi[^p516-8]: « Defecerunt scrutantes scrutinio »; et ideo: « Accedat homo ad cor altum, et exaltabitur Deus ».

## English
<!-- page 512 -->

#### The confutation of the Sadducees erring out of ignorance, as to two things.

34. *And there came certain.* After he confuted the faithlessness of those erring out of malice, here second[^p512-6] he confutes *the unbelief of those erring out of ignorance;* and this part has two. In the first he confutes the error concerning *the resurrection;* in the second, however, concerning *the incarnation,* there[^p512-7]: *But he said to them.*

#### First, the error concerning the resurrection is confuted, through two things.

Now in describing *the confutation of the error of the Sadducees about the final resurrection* he proceeds in this order: for first he sets forth *the erroneous question;* second he adjoins *the truthful answer,* there[^p512-8]: *And Jesus said to them: The sons of this* etc.

Concerning *the erroneous question* the Sadducees are brought in as *denying the truth,* as *alleging a precept,* as *putting forward a case,* as *asking a doubtful point,* by which they were cast headlong into the aforesaid error.

35. (Vers. 27.). First, then, as to *the error of the Sadducees denying the truth,* he says: *And there came certain of the Sadducees, who deny that there is any resurrection,* and consequently they were destroying the foundation of divine worship. Whence Chrysostom[^p512-9]: « In every thing or act of doing, the power is the hope of a future reward. For he that plougheth plougheth that he may reap; and he that fighteth fighteth that he may conquer ». And after: « Take away, then, the hope of the resurrection, and the whole observance of piety is dissolved »; the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter[^p512-10]: « What shall they do that are baptized for the dead, if the dead rise not again at all? Why are they then baptized for them? Why also are we in danger every hour »? Now the Sadducees denied the resurrection both on account of the mortality of souls, which they believed; whence the Gloss[^p512-11]: « The Sadducees believed that souls perish with the body »; and also on account of the irreparableness of bodies. — And there seems to be on their side what is said in the Psalm[^p512-12]: « His spirit shall go forth, and he shall return into his earth » etc.; likewise: « Their sepulchres shall be their houses for ever »; and Job, the fourteenth chapter: « Man, when he is fallen asleep, shall not rise again, till the heavens be broken » etc. But here they do not say that the resurrection is not to come, but rather that it is to be deferred until the end of the judgement. — For that *spirits* are immortal, this their *understanding, appetite* and *virtuous act* prove[^p512-13]; but that *bodies* are restored, this the divine *justice,* the divine *clemency* and the divine *power* together make manifest. Whence against such it is said in the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter[^p512-14]: « But some man will say: How do the dead rise again? Or with what manner of body shall they come? Senseless man, that which thou sowest is not quickened, except it die first. And that which thou sowest, thou sowest not the body that shall be, but bare grain, as of wheat, or of some of the rest. For God giveth it a body as he will, and to every seed its proper body »; and after[^p512-15]: « This corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality ».

36. (Vers. 27. 28.). Second, as to *the inquiry of the Sadducees alleging a precept,*
<!-- page 513 -->
it is subjoined: *And they asked him, saying: Master, Moses wrote unto us,* a writing, namely, authentic and just, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p513-1]: « Moses commanded a law in the precepts of justices »; whence Deuteronomy, the thirty-first chapter: « After Moses had written the words of this law in a volume and finished it, he commanded the Levites, who carried the ark of the covenant, saying: Take this book, and put it in the side of the ark » etc. Whatever, then, Moses wrote is authentic; therefore they put forward his writing as authentic. — On account of which it is subjoined: *If any man's brother die, having a wife, and he be without children, that his brother take her to wife and raise up seed unto his brother.* This is written in Deuteronomy, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p513-2]: « When brethren dwell together, and one of them dieth without children, the wife of the deceased shall not marry to another, but his brother shall take her and raise up seed for his brother, and the firstborn son of her he shall call by the name of his brother, that his name be not abolished out of Israel ».

37. Now the *literal* reason of this was for fostering charity among kinsfolk, and for restraining[^p513-3] cupidity, and for keeping fidelity, and for conserving carnal generation. According to the *spiritual* intelligence, *our brother* is Christ; Canticles, the eighth chapter[^p513-4]: « Who shall give thee to me for my brother, sucking the breasts of my mother » etc.; and the Psalm: « I will declare thy name to my brethren ». *His wife* is the Church; the Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter[^p513-5]: « The marriage of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath prepared herself ». He *died* before the multitude of the faithful, who are the sons of God; John, the first chapter: « He gave them power to be made the sons of God » etc., and this in *death;* John, the eleventh chapter[^p513-6]: « Jesus was to die for the nation, and not only for the nation, but that he might gather together in one the sons of God, that were dispersed ». As *brother,* he who bears the care of the Church takes this one's wife, not that he may beget carnal sons, but spiritual ones, as Paul; the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter[^p513-7]: « In Christ Jesus by the gospel I have begotten you ». And these he ought to call not by his own name, but by *the brother's,* because not in his own name, but in Christ's, he ought to baptize them, according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter: « Was Paul then crucified for you? Or were you baptized in the name of Paul »? But on him who neglects to fulfil this the punishment of *unshoeing* ought to be laid, according to that of Deuteronomy, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p513-8]: « If he answer: I will not take her to wife; the woman shall come to him before the ancients of the people, and shall take off his shoe from his foot, and spit in his face, and say: So shall it be done to the man that buildeth not his brother's house »; because from him who will not raise up the seed of Christ the authority of preaching ought to be taken away, which is understood by the taking away of the shoe, according to that to the Ephesians, the sixth chapter[^p513-9]: « The feet shod unto the preparation of the gospel of peace ». On account of which Deuteronomy, the twenty-third chapter[^p513-10]: « An eunuch, whose testicles are broken or cut away, shall not enter into the Church of the Lord ». Whereas it is said of holy men in Ecclesiasticus, the forty-fourth chapter: « With their seed good things continue, their posterity are a holy inheritance ».

38. (Vers. 29. 30. 31. 32.). Third, as to *the allegation of those bringing in a case,* it is subjoined: *There were therefore seven brethren, and the first took a wife and died without sons. And the next took her, and he also died without a son. And the third took her. In like manner also all the seven, and they left no seed and died. Last*[^p513-11] *of all the woman also died.* It could be that they feigned this as possible; whence the Gloss[^p513-12]: « Those who do not believe the resurrection of bodies, and think that souls perish with the body, invent the shamefulness of a fable ». The Jews are wont to be free[^tr-vacare] for such fables; on account of which the first to Timothy, the first chapter[^p513-13]: « I desired thee to remain at Ephesus, that thou mightest charge some not to teach otherwise, nor to give heed to fables and endless genealogies, which furnish questions rather than edification ». — Or it could be that this was according to the letter, and is brought in here according to the spirit of the divine ordinance on account of a figure. For *spiritually* by these *seven,* who died without children, is understood the whole number of the reprobate, who left no fruit; whence the Gloss[^p513-14]: « These seven are all the reprobate, who through the whole of this life, which is of seven
<!-- page 514 -->
days, are barren of good work ». These can be understood by *the seven husbands of Sara,* of whom it is said in Tobias, the third chapter[^p514-1], that they were slain by a devil named Asmodeus; because it is said in Wisdom, the third chapter: « From the unlawful bed the seed shall be rooted out ». — Or by the *seven* husbands are understood the seven capital vices, which the soul serves, of which Luke, the eleventh chapter[^p514-2]: « He taketh seven spirits more wicked than himself ». — Or the five senses and the twofold consideration of reason, which are understood by the *woman* of seven husbands; of whom it is said in John, the fourth chapter[^p514-3]: « Call thy husband ». For he who subjects himself to the senses of the flesh does not bring forth fruit; Job, the twenty-fourth chapter: « He hath fed the barren that beareth not, and to the widow he hath done no good »; because, as is said to the Galatians, the sixth chapter, « he that soweth in the flesh, of the flesh also shall reap corruption »; and the first of John, the second chapter: « The world passeth away, and the concupiscence thereof ».

39. (Vers. 33.). Fourth, as to *the cavilling of the Sadducees asking a doubtful point,* it is subjoined: *In the resurrection therefore*[^p514-4] *whose wife of them shall she be? For the seven had her to wife,* as if they should say: she is no more one's than another's: therefore either all will have her, or none. If, then, either is unfitting, it is against the Law to posit the resurrection. — From which it appears that these men erred concerning the resurrection on account of a carnal understanding of spiritual Scripture and of spiritual glory; whence the carnal intelligence kills, while it depraves the spiritual Scriptures; on account of which the second to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p514-5]: « The letter killeth, but the spirit quickeneth ». Therefore John, the sixth chapter: « The words that I have spoken to you are spirit and life »; on account of which the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter: « Now this I say, that flesh and blood shall not possess the kingdom of God, nor corruption incorruption », that is, those who savour carnally. And such were these Sadducees, who, not understanding the nobility of the rational spirit, nor the understanding of spiritual Scripture, nor the power of the omnipotent God, did not think the resurrection was to come. Therefore it is said in Matthew, the twenty-second chapter[^p514-6]: « You err, not knowing the Scripture, nor the power of God ».

40. (Vers. 34.). *And Jesus said to them* etc. After the erroneous inquiry he subjoins here *the truthful answer*[^p514-7]. Concerning which four things are brought in, namely *the assertion of the truth, the reason of the assertion, the confirmation of the reason* and *the praising of the confirmation.*

First, as to *the assertion of the truth,* he says: *And Jesus said to them: The sons of this world marry and are given in marriage,* for the sake of *having succession,* as in Tobias, the eighth chapter[^p514-8]: « Thou, O Lord, knowest that not for the sake of lust do I take my sister to wife, but only for the love of posterity, in which thy name may be blessed for ever and ever ». And this matrimony, although it could be of spiritual men, is yet only of those living in the flesh; whence *sons of the world* does not sound as a vice. But others marry for *the sole fulfilment of delight,* of whom Genesis, the sixth chapter[^p514-9]: « The sons of God seeing the daughters of men, that they were fair, took to themselves wives of all which they chose »; and above, the fourteenth chapter: « I have married a wife, and therefore I cannot come ». And then *sons of the world* sounds as a vice, as also above, the sixteenth chapter: « The sons of this world are more prudent than the sons of light » etc. And these are *the sons of the bondwoman,* of whom to the Galatians, the fourth chapter: « The son of the bondwoman shall not be heir with the son of the free woman ». But neither of these will be in the resurrections, neither succession nor carnal delight.

41. (Vers. 35.). Therefore he subjoins: *But they that shall be accounted*[^p514-10] *worthy of that world and of the resurrection from the dead shall neither be married nor take wives; shall neither be married,* as to women, *nor take wives,* as to men. *Worthy of the resurrection* he calls the just. For he speaks here specially of the resurrection of the just, who will not only rise again, but will also be changed, according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter[^p514-11]: « We shall all indeed rise again, but we shall not all be changed ». « For the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall rise again incorruptible ». Although, then, the bad rise again with the good, as is said in John, the fifth chapter[^p514-12]: « The hour cometh, wherein all that are in the graves shall hear the voice of the Son of God »; yet he speaks specially of the good, who will be lifted up above time and temporal motions, and through this also above wedlock and every carnal act.

42. (Vers. 36.). Second, as to *the reason of the assertion,* he subjoins: *For neither can they die*
<!-- page 515 -->
*any more;* from which it follows that they will not have wives. Whence Bede[^p515-1]: « Since marriages are on account of sons, sons on account of succession, succession on account of death; where, therefore, there is no death, there are no marriages ». — But then it can be argued from this that, since in paradise there was no death before sin, there would not have been marriages. — And the answer to this is that, although there was not death out of *necessity,* as now, yet there was out of *possibility,* because the first parents had an *animal* body, but after the resurrection it will be a *spiritual* body, according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter[^p515-2]: « It is sown an animal body, it shall rise a spiritual; it is sown in weakness, it shall rise in power; it is sown in dishonour, it shall rise in glory ». — Therefore he subjoins: *For they are equal to the Angels, and are sons of God, since they are sons of the resurrection,* that is, as the Angels are immortal, incorruptible, above time and the variations of temporal motions; so will the good be, because after the resurrection we shall then be equal to the Angels according to the dignity of glory; on account of which the Apocalypse, the twenty-first chapter[^p515-3]: « The measure of a man, which is of an Angel ». Nor is it to be wondered at, because then we shall be made like to God through glory, according to that of the first of John, the third chapter[^p515-4]: « Now we are the sons of God, and it hath not yet appeared what we shall be. We know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like to him, because we shall see him as he is ». Whence Rabanus[^p515-5]: « They are as the Angels of God in heaven, who, renewed by the glory of the resurrection, without any fear of death, without any stain of corruption, without any act of earthly state, enjoy the perpetual vision of God. To which equality of the angelic dignity whoever desires to ascend must condescend to the least brethren by acting piously ». And this he says rightly, because he cannot be like to the Angels in glory who will not be likened to them in ministry.

43. (Vers. 37.). Third, as to *the confirmation of the reason,* it is subjoined: *But that the dead rise again*[^p515-6], *Moses also shewed at the bush,* that is, the word of the Lord which he spoke to Moses in the vision of that bush burning and not consumed, according to what is said in Exodus, the third chapter[^p515-7]: « The Lord appeared to him in a flame of fire out of the midst of a bush, and he saw that the bush was on fire and was not burnt »; and it follows that the Lord spoke the words which follow here: *As he calleth the Lord the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob;* Exodus, the third chapter[^p515-8]: « The Lord called Moses out of the midst of the bush » « and he said: I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob ».

44. (Vers. 38.). Now from this the Lord wills to infer the resurrection to come, when he adds: *Now God is not of the dead, but of the living*[^p515-9]. *For all live to him.* From which it follows that they live to him either in act or in potency; but all these were dead actually: therefore they lived to God as to *being able* and disposition. It is necessary, then, to posit a resurrection to come[^p515-10]. — Now there are many testimonies in the old testament by which the resurrection of the dead can be proved; Isaias, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p515-11]: « Thy dead men shall live, my slain shall rise again »; and Ezechiel, the thirty-seventh chapter: « Ye dry bones, hear the word of the Lord: Behold » etc.; likewise Daniel, the twelfth chapter: « Many of those that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake »; likewise Job, the nineteenth chapter: « I know that my Redeemer liveth, and in the last day I shall rise out of the earth »; likewise Osee, the sixth chapter[^p515-12]: « After two days he will revive us; on the third day he will raise us up »; likewise Joel, the third chapter: « Let the nations arise and come up into the valley of Josaphat » etc.; likewise the second of Machabees, the seventh chapter: « He will give you again breath with mercy, and life ». — But the Lord brought in none of these seven witnesses, because the Sadducees, to whom he was answering, received only the Pentateuch; whence the Gloss[^p515-13]: « He could have given plainer testimonies of the Prophets concerning the resurrection, but the Sadducees do not receive the Prophets ». Or for this reason, because by this he proves the life of souls after death, which the Sadducees denied, and this was the root of their error. Or for this reason, because in the adducing of this word there is enclosed at once the mystery of *the Trinity* in the threefold repetition of *God,* the mystery of *the threefold virtue,* namely of faith, hope and charity, in the three
<!-- page 516 -->
Fathers aforesaid, the mystery of *the incarnation* in the burning bush[^p516-1], and also the mystery of *the passion,* and in the whole authority the mystery of *the resurrection,* and so as it were the whole mystery of *the Christian faith;* because every figure of the mysteries and variety of words is consummated in the incarnate Word, according to that of Isaias, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p516-2]: « I have heard a consummation and an abridgment » etc.; and to the Hebrews, the first chapter: « At sundry times » etc.

45. (Vers. 39.). Fourth, as to *the praising of the confirmation,* he adjoins: *And some of the scribes answering said to him: Master, thou hast said well.* The scribes commended this because they were of the sect of the Pharisees, who say that there is a resurrection. Whence it is said in Matthew, the twenty-second chapter[^p516-3], that « the Pharisees, seeing that he had put the Sadducees to silence, came together », because on this account they applauded him. For about this there was contention between the Sadducees and the Pharisees; whence the Acts, the twenty-third chapter[^p516-4]: « Paul knowing that the one part were Sadducees and the other Pharisees, cried out in the council: Men brethren, I am a Pharisee; concerning the hope and resurrection of the dead I am called in question. And when he had so said, there arose a dissension between the Pharisees and the Sadducees, and the multitude was divided. For the Sadducees say that there is no resurrection of the dead, neither Angel nor spirit; but the Pharisees confess both ».

46. (Vers. 40.). Since, then, he had confounded both[^p516-5], therefore both held their peace; whence it is subjoined: *And they durst not any more ask him anything;* from which it appears that they did not ask out of eagerness to learn, but to conquer and overcome; whence the Gloss[^p516-6]: « Confuted in their words, they ask no more, but openly deliver him, seized, to the Roman power. From which we understand that the poisons of envy can be overcome, but are hardly at rest ». Therefore they durst not ask him, because they saw that he had something divine in himself, with which it is not safe to dispute; Job, the ninth chapter[^p516-7]: « What am I, that I should answer him, and speak with him in my words? I who, even if I should have any just thing, will not answer, but will make supplication to my judge ». There was verified, then, in them that of the Psalm[^p516-8]: « They have failed in their search »; and therefore: « Let man come to a deep heart, and God shall be exalted ».
