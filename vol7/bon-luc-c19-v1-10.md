# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XIX, Vers. 1-10
## *Secundo confutat curiositatem in possidendo approbando liberalitatem duplicem*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 472 -->

### Capitulum XIX.

#### Secundo confutat curiositatem in possidendo approbando liberalitatem duplicem.

1. *Et ingressus perambulabat Iericho* etc. Supra confutavit cupiditatem per approbationem paupertatis, hic secundo[^p472-8] confutat *tenacitatem per approbationem liberalitatis.* Et quoniam dispersio et communicatio temporalium est via ad multiplicationem spiritualium, ideo in hac parte primo approbat *liberalitatem distribuentem temporalia;* secundo vero, *sedulitatem multiplicandi spiritualia,* ibi[^p472-9]: *Haec illis audientibus adiiciens* etc. Primum facit per *exemplum expressum;* secundum vero, per *parabolicum.*

Introducitur ergo *exemplum,* per quod commendatur liberalitas, per quam Zachaeus de impio factus est iustus; et cum esset camelus, transiit per foramen acus et formam dedit divitibus intrandi in caelum[^p472-10]. In hoc igitur exemplo introducuntur tria *commendabilia* in Zachaeo, per quae ipsius exemplar est imitandum, et est signum etiam vocationis gentium; propter quod et Evangelium istud legitur in dedicatione ecclesiarum. Et haec sunt ipsius Zachaei *studiositas ad Christum videndum;* ipsius Zachaei *hospitalitas* ad Christum *suscipiendum,* ibi[^p472-11]: *Et cum venisset ad locum;* ipsius Zachaei *liberalitas ad distributionem suorum,* ibi: *Stans autem Zachaeus* etc.

*Studiositas* autem Zachaei commendatur ex tribus, scilicet ex parte *attractivi, retardativi* et *expeditivi.* Vera enim studiositas est, in qua animus attrahitur ad Christum, et si occurrant impedimenta, tamen non frangitur desiderium, sed plus inardescit[^p472-12].

2. (Vers. 1.). Primo igitur quantum ad *attractivum,* quod quidem erat praesentia Iesu Christi, dicit: *Et ingressus perambulabat Iericho,* id est, ambulabat per Iericho. *Iericho* enim civitas erat,
<!-- page 473 -->
quam, transito Iordane, subverterunt Iudaei, secundum quod dicitur Iosue sexto[^p473-1]; sed reaedificata fuit sub Achab, rege Israel; unde tertii Regum decimo sexto: « In diebus Achab aedificavit Hiel de Bethel Iericho. In Abiram, primitivo suo, fundavit eam et in Segub, novissimo suo, posuit portas eius, iuxta verbum Domini, quod locutus fuerat Dominus in manu Iosue, filii Nun ». Hanc Dominus sua illustravit praesentia, qua peccatoribus condescendebat, qua etiam peccatores ad se attrahebat. Pietas enim attrahit miseros, sicut adamas ferrum[^p473-2].

3. (Vers. 2.). Ideo subditur *attractio peccatoris;* propter quod sequitur: *Et ecce, vir nomine Zachaeus;* cuius nomen erat notum et vulgatum, non propter famam virtutis, sed infamiam rapacitatis. — Unde subdit: *Et hic erat princeps publicanorum,* qui scilicet lucra saeculi sectabantur per fas et nefas[^p473-3]. Hunc autem *principatum* non habebat propter iustitiam, sed propter pecuniam. — Et ideo addit: *Et ipse dives,* et ita gibbosus sicut camelus et ineptus ad regnum; supra decimo octavo[^p473-4]: « Facilius est, camelum intrare per foramen acus » etc.; et tamen divina virtute fuit introductus, quia amore ipsius Christi fuit attractus.

4. (Vers. 3.). Unde et subditur: *Et quaerebat videre Iesum, quis esset;* quod non ita faceret studiose, nisi amaret, nec amaret vel crederet, nisi eius gratia praeventus esset, quia Ioannis sexto[^p473-5]: « Nemo venit ad me, nisi Pater meus, qui misit me, traxerit eum ». Hunc traxit Iesus, ut esset in exemplum aliis. Unde iste iam implebat consilium Sapientis, secundum illud Proverbiorum octavo[^p473-6]: « Accipite disciplinam meam et non pecuniam, doctrinam magis quam aurum eligite ». Qui sic quaerit Iesum invenit, secundum illud Sapientiae primo: « In simplicitate cordis quaerite illum, quoniam invenitur ab his qui non tentant illum » etc.

5. Secundo quantum ad *retardativum,* quod fuit frequentia populi, subdit: *Et non poterat prae turba, quia statura pusillus erat.* Haec *turba* eum impediebat, quae semper impedire consuevit volentes venire ad Christum. Haec namque turba impediebat *illuminationem caeci;* supra decimo octavo[^p473-7]: « Cum audiret turbam praetereuntem » etc. « At illi increpabant eum, ut taceret »; *curationem paralytici;* Marci secundo: « Venerunt ad eum ferentes paralyticum, qui a quatuor portabatur; et non poterant offerre illi prae turba »; *liberationem surdi et muti;* Marci septimo: « Apprehendens eum seorsum de turba, misit digitos in auriculas eius et exspuens tetigit linguam eius »; *suscitationem mortui;* Matthaei nono[^p473-8]: « Cum eiecta esset turba, intravit et tenuit manum eius ». Impedit etiam *approximationem Zachaei,* sicut dicitur hic, quia *non poterat prae turba*[^p473-9]. Turba enim prona ad malum a Iesu revocat potius quam ad ipsum perducat; propter quod Ecclesiastici decimo octavo[^p473-10]: « Ne oblecteris in turbis nec in modicis; assidua enim est commissio illorum ». Ideo Exodi vigesimo tertio: « Non sequeris turbam ad faciendum malum nec in iudicio plurimorum acquiesces sententiae, ut a vero devies ». Ideo dicitur Matthaei decimo quarto: « Dimissa turba, ascendit Iesus in montem solus orare ».

6. (Vers. 4.). Tertio quantum ad *expeditivum,* quod quidem erat eminentia arboris sycomori, subiungit: *Et praecurrens ascendit in arborem sycomorum, ut videret illum*[^p473-11]*, quia inde erat transiturus.* In quo apparet eius studiositas et in praecurrendo et in ascendendo in altum, ut quod sibi deerat suppleret per adiutorium alienum. Unde Glossa[^p473-12]: « Quia devotione fidei ad videndum Salvatorem quod natura minus habet per ascensum arboris supplet; iuste, quod rogare non audebat, dominicae susceptionis benedictionem accepit, quam desiderabat ».

7. Sed hic attendendum, quod Evangelista sic diligenter nomen *civitatis,* nomen *hominis*[^p473-13] et nomen *arboris* propter *mysterium* magis exprimit quam propter exigentiam narrationis *facti.* — Propter quod intelligendum, quod *iste publicanus* recte designat populum gentilem. *Iericho,* quae luna interpretatur, sicut supra[^p473-14] tactum est, designat *mundum* propter sui defectum et mutabilitatem; quia,
<!-- page 474 -->
primae Ioannis secundo, « mundus transit et concupiscentia eius ». Hanc civitatem destruxit *Iosue* — nec aliquis salvatus est in ea nisi Raab meretrix, quae credidit nuntiis Iosue et ipsos recepit[^p474-1] — quia verus *Iesus* hanc mundanam conversationem in carne apparendo damnavit; nec aliquis potest salvari, nisi qui credit; Ioannis primo: « In mundo erat, et mundus per ipsum factus est, et mundus eum non cognovit »; et post: « Quotquot autem receperunt eum, dedit illis potestatem filios Dei fieri, his qui credunt » etc.; unde ad Hebraeos undecimo: « Fide muri Iericho corruerunt. Fide Raab meretrix non periit ». Unde illa *Raab* Ecclesiam significat gentium ad Christum conversam. — Et hoc ipsum significatur per *Zachaeum publicanum,* qui quaerebat videre Iesum, ut scilicet cognosceret verum Deum; in quo significat populum gentium, sicut et Naaman Syrus, de quo quarti Regum quinto[^p474-2]: « Naaman, princeps militiae regis Syriae, dives, sed leprosus », qui veniens ad Eliseum curatus est. Unde et Ioannis duodecimo dicunt gentiles: « Volumus Iesum videre »; sed non possunt tum propter perturbationem phantasmatum, tum propter defectum naturalis lucis, quia hoc est supra rationem; et ideo opus est *ascendere* per fidem ad cognoscendum eum. — Unde per *sycomorum,* quae ficus fatua interpretatur, intelligitur *eminentia fidei,* quae stultitia esse videtur, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios primo[^p474-3]: « Nos praedicamus Christum crucifixum, Iudaeis quidem scandalum, gentibus autem stultitiam ». In huius fatuitatis credulitatem oportet conscendere eum qui vult ad sapientiam pervenire, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios tertio: « Si quis videtur esse sapiens inter vos, stultus fiat, ut sit sapiens ». Nam stultitia fidei deducit ad eminentiam sapientiae Christi, et hoc quasi per sex gradus, quia fides disponit ad prudentiam, prudentia ad scientiam, scientia ad consilium, consilium ad intellectum et intellectus ad sapientiam. Et hic thronus est Salomonis, ad quem per sex gradus ascenditur, secundum quod insinuatur tertii Regum decimo[^p474-4]. Unde et in Psalmo dicitur: « Beatus vir, cuius est auxilium abs te, ascensiones in corde suo disposuit » etc. « Etenim benedictionem dabit legislator, ibunt de virtute in virtutem » etc. Haec igitur studiositas Zachaei et *exemplar* fuit imitandum et *figura* conversionis gentium ad Christum, per cuius fidem adiuti elevantur ad ipsum contuendum. Unde Glossa[^p474-5]: « Per laudabilem fatuitatem transeuntem prope Dominum cernit, quia, etsi nondum ita solide, ut est, iam tamen raptim et quasi in transitu luci caelestis sapientiae intendit ». Ideo Isaiae septimo[^p474-6], secundum aliam translationem: « Nisi credideritis, non intelligetis ».

8. (Vers. 5.). *Et cum venisset* etc. Postquam expressit Zachaei studiositatem ad Christum videndum, hic secundo[^p474-7] exprimit *hospitalitatem ad Christum suscipiendum.* Circa quam describendam introducuntur tria, scilicet *dignatio* ex parte Christi, *devotio* ex parte Zachaei et *murmuratio* ex parte populi perversi.

Primo igitur quantum ad *dignationem ex parte Christi* dicit: *Et cum venisset ad locum, suspiciens Iesus vidit illum,* per aspectum benignitatis; in quo ostendebat, se approbare affectum publicani per signum; Psalmus[^p474-8]: « Oculi Domini super metuentes eum et in eis qui sperant super misericordia eius ». Hunc *aspiciebat,* « quia[^p474-9] statura pusillus erat »; « excelsus enim Dominus et humilia respicit », scilicet per dignationem. — *Et dixit ad eum: Zachaee, festinans descende,* ad impendendum beneficium hospitalitatis; et hoc facit in signum magnae dignationis, qui hospitium petere non erubuit. Unde Glossa[^p474-10]: « Non invitatus, se invitat, quia, etsi nondum audierat vocem invitantis, audiebat tamen affectum ». — Et nota, quod dicit *festinans,* quia ad opera et beneficia hospitalitatis debemus esse prompti exemplo Zachaei et etiam Abrahae; Genesis decimo octavo[^p474-11]: « Festinavit Abraham in tabernaculum ad Saram et dixit ei: Accelera, tria sata similae commisce et fac subcinericeos panes. Ipse vero cucurrit ad armentum et tulit vitulum tenerrimum et dedit puero, qui festinavit et coxit illum ». — Et
<!-- page 475 -->
hoc petebat Christus, quia indigebat; unde subdit: *Quia hodie in domo tua oportet me manere,* ad exemplum humilitatis. Exemplum namque humilitatis erat, quod in domo peccatoris esse volebat; exemplum etiam humilitatis, quod necessitatem suam et opportunitatem humiliter indicabat, qua alieno hospitio indigebat, secundum illud Matthaei octavo[^p475-1]: « Vulpes foveas habent, et volucres caeli nidos » etc. Et istud est verbum potius humilitatis quam urbanitatis, quia Christus magis venit in mundum ad docendum humilitatem quam ad docendum curialitatem. Ideo dicebat ipse discipulis Matthaei undecimo[^p475-2]: « Discite a me, quia mitis sum et humilis corde ». Hanc dignationem admiratur Ieremias dicens Ieremiae decimo quarto: « Quare sicut colonus futurus es in terra et quasi vir vagus, declinans ad manendum »? et Baruch tertio: « Cum hominibus conversatus est ».

9. (Vers. 6.). Secundo quantum ad *devotionem Zachaei* subdit: *Et festinans descendit et excepit illum gaudens;* in quo ostenditur eius devotio, quia non tantum praebuit hospitalitatem *festinanter,* verum etiam *laetanter.* Talis enim conditio est maxime necessaria; primae Petri quarto[^p475-3]: « Ante omnia mutuam in vobismetipsis caritatem continuam habentes »; et post: « Hospitales invicem sine murmuratione ». Non enim debet esse tristitia, sed iucunditas; secundae ad Corinthios nono[^p475-4]: « Hilarem datorem diligit Deus »; et ad Hebraeos ultimo: « Caritas fraternitatis maneat in vobis, et hospitalitatem nolite oblivisci. Per hanc enim quidam placuerunt, Angelis hospitio receptis ». Propter quod et sanctus Iob dicebat, trigesimo primo[^p475-5]: « Foris non mansit peregrinus, ostium meum viatori patuit ». Et hoc debet fieri, quia quicumque recipit Christi membra, recipit et ipsum Christum; Matthaei vigesimo quinto: « Hospes eram, et collegistis me »; et post quaerentibus, quomodo haec fecerunt, respondet: « Quod uni ex minimis meis fecistis mihi fecistis ». Unde et Zachaeus per hanc hospitalitatem salvatus est; similiter et Lot liberatus est a Sodomis per Angelos hospitio receptos, Genesis decimo nono[^p475-6]. Et similiter mulier Sunamitis per Eliseum, quem hospitio recepit, et habuit prolem, cum esset sterilis, et post filius eius a morte suscitatus est, quarti Regum quarto. Et mulier Sareptana suscipiendo Eliam periculum famis evasit, tertii Regum decimo septimo. Et universaliter qui hospitales fuerunt divinam gratiam adepti sunt, quia, sicut dicitur Deuteronomii decimo[^p475-7], « Dominus amat peregrinum et dat ei victum et vestitum »; unde Proverbiorum decimo nono: « Feneratur Domino qui miseretur pauperis ».

10. (Vers. 7.). Tertio quantum ad *murmurationem populi perversi* subdit: *Et cum viderent omnes,* scilicet oculo iudicii perversi, de quo Ecclesiastici undecimo[^p475-8]: « Sicut prospector videns casum proximi sui; bona enim in mala convertens insidiatur et in electis imponit maculam ». — Et quia ad malum iudicium sequitur murmur, ideo subdit: *Murmurabant dicentes, quod ad hominem peccatorem divertisset,* scilicet susurrio verbi dolosi, quod valde vitandum; ideo dicitur Sapientiae primo[^p475-9]: « Custodite vos a murmuratione, quae nihil prodest, et a detractione parcite linguae ». Hoc autem *murmur* multum displicet Deo per omnem modum; ideo Threnorum tertio[^p475-10]: « Quid murmuravit homo vivens, vir pro peccatis suis »? Hic autem populus semper libenter murmuravit; Exodi decimo sexto: « Murmuravit omnis congregatio filiorum Israel contra Moysen et contra Aaron »; et Numerorum undecimo: « Ortum est murmur populi contra Dominum ». Et ideo primae ad Corinthios decimo: « Neque murmuraveritis, sicut et quidam eorum murmuraverunt et perierunt ab exterminatore ». Et nunc murmurabant contra Dominum — et in hoc figuram gerebant illius gentis murmurantis contra Dominum — quia suscepit gentes. Unde Glossa[^p475-11] dicit: « Manifestum est, Iudaeos semper odisse populum gentium, vel non intellexisse salutem; unde et adversus Petrum dicitur: *Quare introisti ad viros praeputium habentes* »? Actuum undecimo. — Propter quod intelligendum, quod, sicut praetactum est[^p475-12], per ingressum Christi in domum Zachaei, principis publicanorum, intelligitur eius ingressus in Ecclesiam gentium, a qua libenter susceptus est. Ideo Christus dixit Zachaeo, ut *descenderet* ad humilitatem, quia opportunum erat, quod in eius domo maneret, synagoga deserente
<!-- page 476 -->
fidem; Actuum decimo tertio[^p476-1]: « Vobis oportebat primum loqui verbum Dei; sed quoniam illud repellitis et indignos vos iudicatis aeternae vitae, ecce, convertimur ad gentes ».

11. Et nota, quod domus, in qua Christus manet, est septiformis, prima scilicet est domus *habitationis materialis,* in qua manet in persona pauperis; Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo[^p476-2]: « Frange esurienti panem tuum et egenos vagosque induc in domum tuam » etc. — Secunda est domus *basilicae materialis,* in qua manet in Sacramento altaris; Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p476-3]: « Domus mea domus orationis vocabitur »; et Psalmus: « Introibo in domum tuam » etc. — Tertia est domus *Ecclesiae militantis,* in qua manet in membris suis; primae ad Timotheum tertio[^p476-4]: « Haec scribo tibi, fili, ut scias, quomodo te oporteat in domo conversari, quae est Ecclesia Dei vivi, columna » etc. — Quarta est domus *uteri virginalis,* in qua manet per praesentiam carnis; Psalmus[^p476-5]: « Domum tuam decet sanctitudo in longitudinem dierum ». — Quinta est domus *conscientiae mundae,* in qua manet spiritu sanctitatis; Sapientiae octavo[^p476-6]: « Intrans in domum meam conquiescam cum ea; non enim habet amaritudinem » etc.; et iterum Ecclesiastici trigesimo secundo: « Praecurre prior in domum tuam et illic lude et age conceptiones tuas ». — Sexta domus est *Scripturae sacrae,* in qua manet in lumine veritatis; Psalmus[^p476-7]: « Deus in domibus eius cognoscetur, cum suscipiet eam »; et Proverbiorum nono: « Sapientia aedificavit sibi domum, excidit columnas septem » etc. — Septima domus est *Ierusalem supernae,* in qua manet in gaudio felicitatis; Ioannis decimo quarto[^p476-8]: « In domo Patris mei mansiones multae sunt »; et iterum Psalmus: « Laetatus sum in his quae dicta sunt mihi: In domum Domini ibimus ». — Quoniam igitur his septem modis suscipitur in Ecclesia gentium, quae designatur per Zachaeum; ideo hoc Evangelium legitur in Dedicationibus ecclesiarum, quae designant domus spirituales, de quibus primae Petri secundo[^p476-9]: « Ad quem accedentes, lapidem vivum », « et ipsi tanquam lapides vivi superaedificamini domus spirituales, sacerdotium sanctum, offerentes spirituales hostias, acceptabiles » etc.

12. (Vers. 8.). *Stans autem Zachaeus* etc. Postquam descripsit Zachaei studiositatem et hospitalitatem, hic describit *liberalitatem*[^p476-10]; quam quidem describit quantum ad conditionem triplicem, scilicet quantum ad *Zachaei oblationem* sufficientem, *Christi acceptationem* suscipientem et *rationem* principaliter moventem.

Primo igitur quantum ad Zachaei *oblationem sufficientem* praemittit: *Stans autem Zachaeus,* ex cordis *virilitate;* ad Ephesios sexto[^p476-11]: « State ergo, succincti lumbos vestros in veritate »; *dixit ad Dominum: Ecce, dimidium bonorum meorum, Domine, do pauperibus,* ex cordis *liberalitate;* in quo ostenditur perfecta liberalitas, quia dat *abunde;* dat enim *dimidium,* secundum illud Tobiae quarto[^p476-12]: « Fili, si multum tibi fuerit, abundanter tribue; si autem parum, etiam illud libenter » etc., ad imitationem illius « qui dat omnibus affluenter et non improperat », Iacobi primo. — Dat *licite,* unde dicit: *Bonorum meorum;* Proverbiorum tertio[^p476-13]: « Honora Dominum de tua substantia »; quocontra Ecclesiastici trigesimo quarto: « Dona iniquorum non probat Altissimus »; et ideo ibi subditur: « Qui offert sacrificium de substantia pauperis » etc. — Item dat *accelerate;* unde dicit: *Do;* Proverbiorum tertio[^p476-14]: « Ne dicas amico tuo: Vade et revertere, cum statim possis dare »; et Ecclesiastici quarto: « Cor inopis ne afflixeris et ne protrahas datum angustianti ». — Item dat *discrete,* quia *pauperibus;* Ecclesiastici duodecimo[^p476-15]: « Si benefeceris, scito, cui feceris »; et supra decimo quarto: « Voca pauperes, debiles, caecos et claudos ». — Item dat *ordinate,* quia propria, restituendo aliena, et non solum restituendo ablatum, verum etiam damnum. — Unde dicit: *Si aliquem*[^p476-16] *defraudavi, reddo quadruplum.* Hoc dicit secundum Legem, Exodi vigesimo secundo: « Si quis furatus fuerit ovem, reddet quatuor ». Hoc autem necessarium est ad perfectam liberalitatem; unde Augustinus[^p476-17]: « Alterum horum non est satis; nec habet gratiam liberalitatis, quia non spolia, sed dona quaeruntur ».

13. (Vers. 9.). Secundo quantum ad *Christi suscipientem acceptationem* subdit: *Ait autem*[^p476-18] *Iesus ad eum: Quia hodie salus domui huic facta est,*
<!-- page 477 -->
per divinae gratiae collationem, quae data est ad Christi praesentiam, per quam dedicatur et sanctificatur Ecclesia; Glossa[^p477-1]: « *Salus,* quae olim Iudaeorum domum implebat, hodie populo gentium illuxit », secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo nono: « Parum est, ut sis servus meus ad faeces Israel convertendas. Dedi te in lucem gentium, ut sis salus mea usque ad extremum terrae ». Et haec *salus* debetur praedestinatis. — Ideo subdit: *Eo quod et ipse sit filius Abrahae;* Glossa[^p477-2]: « *Filius,* non carne, sed fide »; ad Romanos nono: « Neque qui semen Abrahae sunt, omnes filii, sed in Isaac vocabitur tibi semen ». Nam « non qui filii carnis, hi filii, sed qui filii sunt promissionis aestimantur in semine ». Et de his filiis supra tertio: « Potens est Deus de lapidibus istis suscitare filios Abrahae »; et Ioannis undecimo: « Iesus passus est, ut filios Dei, qui dispersi erant, congregaret in unum ».

14. (Vers. 10.). Tertio quantum ad *motivam rationem* subiungit: *Venit autem*[^p477-3] *Filius hominis quaerere,* per *sapientiam;* Psalmus: « Erravi sicut ovis, quae periit; quaere servum tuum, Domine » etc. — *Et salvum facere quod perierat,* scilicet per *misericordiam;* secundi Paralipomenon ultimo[^p477-4]: « Indignum salvabis me secundum magnam misericordiam tuam »; et haec est misericordia salvans. Propter quod et *Iesus* vocatur; Matthaei primo[^p477-5]: « Vocabis nomen eius Iesum; ipse enim salvum faciet populum suum a peccatis eorum »; et Actuum quarto: « Neque enim in alio aliquo est salus. Non est enim aliud nomen sub caelo datum hominibus, in quo oporteat nos salvos fieri ». Unde ad Romanos decimo: « Omnis, quicumque invocaverit nomen Domini, salvus erit ».

### Chapter XIX.

#### Second, he confutes curiosity in possessing by approving a twofold liberality.

1. *And entering in, he walked through Jericho* etc. Above he confuted cupidity through the approbation of poverty; here, second[^p472-8], he confutes *tenacity through the approbation of liberality.* And since the dispersing and communicating of temporal things is the way to the multiplication of spiritual things, therefore in this part he first approves *the liberality that distributes temporal things;* second, *the sedulousness of multiplying spiritual things,* there[^p472-9]: *As they were hearing these things, he added* etc. The first he does through *an express example;* the second, through *a parabolic one.*

There is introduced, then, *an example* through which liberality is commended, through which Zacheus from impious was made just; and though he was a camel, he passed through the eye of a needle and gave the rich a pattern of entering into heaven[^p472-10]. In this example, therefore, three *commendable things* are introduced in Zacheus, through which his exemplar is to be imitated, and it is also a sign of the calling of the Gentiles; on account of which this Gospel too is read at the dedication of churches. And these are: Zacheus' own *studiousness to see Christ;* Zacheus' own *hospitality* to *receive* Christ, there[^p472-11]: *And when he was come to the place;* Zacheus' own *liberality in the distribution of his goods,* there: *But Zacheus standing* etc.

*Studiousness,* then, is commended in Zacheus from three things, namely on the part of *what draws, what holds back* and *what speeds.* For true studiousness is that in which the mind is drawn to Christ, and if hindrances occur, yet the desire is not broken, but burns the more[^p472-12].

2. (Vers. 1.). First, then, as to *what draws,* which was indeed the presence of Jesus Christ, he says: *And entering in, he walked through Jericho,* that is, he went through Jericho. For *Jericho* was a city
<!-- page 473 -->
which, the Jordan being crossed, the Jews overthrew, according to what is said in Josue, the sixth chapter[^p473-1]; but it was rebuilt under Achab, king of Israel; whence the third of Kings, the sixteenth chapter: « In the days of Achab Hiel of Bethel built Jericho. In Abiram, his firstborn, he laid the foundation thereof, and in Segub, his youngest son, he set up the gates thereof, according to the word of the Lord, which he spoke in the hand of Josue, the son of Nun ». This the Lord made illustrious by his presence, by which he condescended to sinners, by which also he drew sinners to himself. For piety[^tr-pietas] draws the wretched, as the adamant draws iron[^p473-2].

3. (Vers. 2.). Therefore *the drawing of the sinner* is subjoined; on account of which there follows: *And behold, a man named Zacheus;* whose name was known and common, not on account of a fame of virtue, but the infamy of rapacity. — Whence he subjoins: *And he was the chief of the publicans,* who namely pursued the gains of the world through right and wrong[^p473-3]. And this *chiefship* he held not on account of justice, but on account of money. — And therefore he adds: *And he was rich,* and so humped like a camel and unfit for the kingdom; above, the eighteenth chapter[^p473-4]: « It is easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle » etc.; and yet by divine power he was brought in, because he was drawn by the love of Christ himself.

4. (Vers. 3.). Whence it is also subjoined: *And he sought to see Jesus who he was;* which he would not do so studiously unless he loved, nor would he love or believe unless he had been prevented by his grace, because John, the sixth chapter[^p473-5]: « No man cometh to me, unless my Father, who hath sent me, draw him ». Him Jesus drew, that he might be an example to others. Whence this man was already fulfilling the counsel of the Wise Man, according to that of Proverbs, the eighth chapter[^p473-6]: « Receive my instruction, and not money: choose knowledge rather than gold ». He who so seeks Jesus finds him, according to that of Wisdom, the first chapter: « Seek him in simplicity of heart, for he is found by them that tempt him not » etc.

5. Second, as to *what holds back,* which was the throng of the people, he subjoins: *And he could not for the crowd, because he was low of stature.* This *crowd* hindered him, which has always been wont to hinder those wishing
to come to Christ. For this crowd hindered *the illumination of the blind man;* above, the eighteenth chapter[^p473-7]: « When he heard the crowd passing by » etc. « But they rebuked him, that he should hold his peace »; *the cure of the paralytic;* Mark, the second chapter: « They came to him, bringing one sick of the palsy, who was carried by four; and they could not offer him unto him for the crowd »; *the freeing of the deaf and dumb man;* Mark, the seventh chapter: « Taking him aside from the crowd, he put his fingers into his ears, and spitting, he touched his tongue »; *the raising of the dead girl;* Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p473-8]: « When the crowd was put forth, he went in, and took her by the hand ». It hinders also *the approach of Zacheus,* as is said here, because *he could not for the crowd*[^p473-9]. For the crowd, prone to evil, calls back from Jesus rather than leads to him; on account of which Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter[^p473-10]: « Be not delighted in crowds, nor in small ones; for their engagement is continual ». Therefore Exodus, the twenty-third chapter: « Thou shalt not follow the crowd to do evil; neither shalt thou yield in judgement to the opinion of the most part, to stray from the truth ». Therefore it is said in Matthew, the fourteenth chapter: « Having dismissed the crowd, Jesus went up into a mountain alone to pray ».

6. (Vers. 4.). Third, as to *what speeds,* which was indeed the height of the sycamore tree, he subjoins: *And running before, he climbed up into a sycamore tree, that he might see him*[^p473-11]*, for he was to pass that way.* In which his studiousness appears both in running before and in climbing on high, so that what he lacked he might supply through another's help. Whence the Gloss[^p473-12]: « Because by the devotion of faith he supplies, through the climbing of the tree, what nature has less for seeing the Saviour; justly, what he did not dare to ask, he received, the blessing of the Lord's being received by him, which he desired ».

7. But here it is to be attended to, that the Evangelist so diligently expresses the name *of the city,* the name *of the man*[^p473-13] and the name *of the tree* rather on account of the *mystery* than on account of what the narration of the *fact* requires. — On account of which it is to be understood that *this publican* rightly designates the people of the Gentiles. *Jericho,* which is interpreted moon, as was touched on above[^p473-14], designates *the world* on account of its defect and mutability; because,
<!-- page 474 -->
the first of John, the second chapter, « the world passeth away and the concupiscence thereof ». This city *Josue* destroyed — nor was anyone saved in it except Raab the harlot, who believed the messengers of Josue and received them[^p474-1] — because the true *Jesus,* by appearing in the flesh, condemned this worldly conversation; nor can anyone be saved except he who believes; John, the first chapter: « He was in the world, and the world was made by him, and the world knew him not »; and after: « But as many as received him, he gave them power to be made the sons of God, to them that believe » etc.; whence to the Hebrews, the eleventh chapter: « By faith the walls of Jericho fell down. By faith Raab the harlot perished not ». Whence that *Raab* signifies the Church of the Gentiles converted to Christ. — And this very thing is signified through *Zacheus the publican,* who sought to see Jesus, namely that he might know the true God; in which he signifies the people of the Gentiles, as also Naaman the Syrian, of whom the fourth of Kings, the fifth chapter[^p474-2]: « Naaman, general of the army of the king of Syria, rich, but a leper », who, coming to Eliseus, was cured. Whence also in John, the twelfth chapter, the Gentiles say: « We would see Jesus »; but they cannot, both on account of the disturbance of phantasms and on account of the defect of natural light, because this is above reason; and therefore there is need *to climb up* through faith to know him. — Whence through the *sycamore,* which is interpreted foolish fig, is understood *the height of faith,* which seems to be foolishness, according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p474-3]: « We preach Christ crucified, unto the Jews indeed a stumblingblock, and unto the Gentiles foolishness ». Into the belief of this foolishness he must climb who wishes to come to wisdom, according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter: « If any man among you seem to be wise, let him become a fool, that he may be wise ». For the foolishness of faith leads to the height of the wisdom of Christ, and this as it were by six steps, because faith disposes to prudence, prudence to knowledge, knowledge to counsel, counsel to understanding and understanding to wisdom. And this is the throne of Solomon, to which one ascends by six steps, according to what is intimated in the third of Kings, the tenth chapter[^p474-4]. Whence also in the Psalm it is said:
« Blessed is the man whose help is from thee; in his heart he hath disposed to ascend by steps » etc. « For the lawgiver shall give a blessing, they shall go from virtue to virtue » etc. This studiousness of Zacheus, therefore, was both an *exemplar* to be imitated and a *figure* of the conversion of the Gentiles to Christ, by whose faith, helped, they are lifted up to contuit him. Whence the Gloss[^p474-5]: « Through a praiseworthy foolishness he discerns the Lord passing near, because, though not yet so solidly as he is, yet already swiftly and as it were in passing he attends to the light of heavenly wisdom ». Therefore Isaias, the seventh chapter[^p474-6], according to another translation: « Unless you believe, you shall not understand ».

8. (Vers. 5.). *And when he was come* etc. After he expressed Zacheus' studiousness to see Christ, here, second[^p474-7], he expresses *the hospitality to receive Christ.* Concerning the description of which three things are introduced, namely *condescension* on the part of Christ, *devotion* on the part of Zacheus and *murmuring* on the part of the perverse people.

First, then, as to *the condescension on the part of Christ* he says: *And when he was come to the place, looking up, Jesus saw him,* through a look of kindness; in which he showed that he approved the publican's affection by a sign; the Psalm[^p474-8]: « The eyes of the Lord are upon them that fear him, and on them that hope in his mercy ». Him *he looked upon,* « because[^p474-9] he was low of stature »; « for the Lord is high, and looketh on the low », namely through condescension. — *And said to him: Zacheus, make haste and come down,* to bestow the benefit of hospitality; and this he does as a sign of great condescension, who did not blush to ask lodging. Whence the Gloss[^p474-10]: « Not invited, he invites himself, because, though he had not yet heard the voice of the one inviting, yet he heard the affection ». — And note that he says *make haste,* because we ought to be prompt to the works and benefits of hospitality by the example of Zacheus and also of Abraham; Genesis, the eighteenth chapter[^p474-11]: « Abraham made haste into the tent to Sara, and said to her: Make haste, temper together three measures of flour, and make cakes upon the hearth. And he himself ran to the herd, and took a calf very tender, and gave it to a young man, who made haste and boiled it ». — And
<!-- page 475 -->
this Christ asked, because he was in need; whence he subjoins: *For this day I must abide in thy house,* for an example of humility. For it was an example of humility that he wished to be in the house of a sinner; an example also of humility that he humbly showed his necessity and his want, by which he needed another's lodging, according to that of Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p475-1]: « The foxes have holes, and the birds of the air nests » etc. And this is a word of humility rather than of urbanity, because Christ came into the world rather to teach humility than to teach courtesy. Therefore he himself said to the disciples, Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p475-2]: « Learn of me, because I am meek and humble of heart ». This condescension Jeremias wonders at, saying, Jeremias, the fourteenth chapter: « Why wilt thou be as a stranger in the land, and as a wandering man turning in to abide »? and Baruch, the third chapter: « He conversed with men ».

9. (Vers. 6.). Second, as to *the devotion of Zacheus* he subjoins: *And he made haste and came down, and received him with joy;* in which his devotion is shown, because he not only offered hospitality *hastily,* but also *gladly.* For such a condition is most necessary; the first of Peter, the fourth chapter[^p475-3]: « Before all things having a constant mutual charity among yourselves »; and after: « Hospitable one towards another without murmuring ». For there ought not to be sadness, but cheerfulness; the second to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter[^p475-4]: « God loveth a cheerful giver »; and to the Hebrews, the last chapter: « Let the charity of the brotherhood abide in you, and hospitality do not forget. For by this some have pleased, having received Angels in hospitality ». On account of which holy Job also said, the thirty-first chapter[^p475-5]: « The stranger did not stay without, my door was open to the traveller ». And this ought to be done, because whoever receives the members of Christ receives Christ himself also; Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter: « I was a stranger, and you took me in »; and after, to those asking how they had done these things, he answers: « What you did to one of my least, you did to me ». Whence Zacheus too was saved through this hospitality; likewise Lot too was delivered from Sodom through the Angels received in hospitality, Genesis, the nineteenth chapter[^p475-6]. And likewise the Sunamitess, through Eliseus, whom she received in hospitality, both had offspring, though she was barren, and afterwards
her son was raised from death, the fourth of Kings, the fourth chapter. And the woman of Sarephta, by receiving Elias, escaped the peril of famine, the third of Kings, the seventeenth chapter. And universally those who were hospitable obtained divine grace, because, as is said in Deuteronomy, the tenth chapter[^p475-7], « The Lord loveth the stranger, and giveth him food and raiment »; whence Proverbs, the nineteenth chapter: « He that hath mercy on the poor, lendeth to the Lord ».

10. (Vers. 7.). Third, as to *the murmuring of the perverse people* he subjoins: *And when all saw it,* namely with the eye of perverse judgement, of which Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter[^p475-8]: « As a spy that seeth the fall of his neighbour; for turning good into evil he lieth in wait, and on the elect he layeth a blot ». — And since murmur follows upon evil judgement, therefore he subjoins: *They murmured, saying, that he was gone to be a guest with a man that was a sinner,* namely with the whisper of a deceitful word, which is much to be avoided; therefore it is said in Wisdom, the first chapter[^p475-9]: « Keep yourselves from murmuring, which profiteth nothing, and refrain your tongue from detraction ». And this *murmur* displeases God much in every way; therefore Lamentations, the third chapter[^p475-10]: « Why hath a living man murmured, a man for his sins »? But this people always murmured willingly; Exodus, the sixteenth chapter: « All the congregation of the children of Israel murmured against Moses and against Aaron »; and Numbers, the eleventh chapter: « There arose a murmur of the people against the Lord ». And therefore the first to the Corinthians, the tenth chapter: « Neither do you murmur, as some of them murmured, and were destroyed by the destroyer ». And now they murmured against the Lord — and in this they bore the figure of that nation murmuring against the Lord — because he received the Gentiles. Whence the Gloss[^p475-11] says: « It is manifest that the Jews always hated the people of the Gentiles, or did not understand salvation; whence against Peter also it is said: *Why didst thou go in to men uncircumcised* »? Acts, the eleventh chapter. — On account of which it is to be understood that, as was touched on before[^p475-12], by the entrance of Christ into the house of Zacheus, the chief of the publicans, is understood his entrance into the Church of the Gentiles, by which he was willingly received. Therefore Christ told Zacheus that he should *come down* to humility, because it was opportune that he abide in his house, the synagogue forsaking
<!-- page 476 -->
the faith; Acts, the thirteenth chapter[^p476-1]: « To you it behoved us first to speak the word of God; but because you reject it, and judge yourselves unworthy of eternal life, behold, we turn to the Gentiles ».

11. And note that the house in which Christ abides is sevenfold: the first, namely, is the house *of material dwelling,* in which he abides in the person of the poor man; Isaias, the fifty-eighth chapter[^p476-2]: « Deal thy bread to the hungry, and bring the needy and the harbourless into thy house » etc. — The second is the house *of the material basilica,* in which he abides in the Sacrament of the altar; Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p476-3]: « My house shall be called the house of prayer »; and the Psalm: « I will come into thy house » etc. — The third is the house *of the Church militant,* in which he abides in his members; the first to Timothy, the third chapter[^p476-4]: « These things I write to thee, son, that thou mayest know how thou oughtest to behave thyself in the house, which is the Church of the living God, the pillar » etc. — The fourth is the house *of the virginal womb,* in which he abides through the presence of the flesh; the Psalm[^p476-5]: « Holiness becometh thy house unto length of days ». — The fifth is the house *of a clean conscience,* in which he abides by the spirit of holiness; Wisdom, the eighth chapter[^p476-6]: « Entering into my house, I shall repose myself with her; for she hath no bitterness » etc.; and again Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-second chapter: « Run before into thy house, and there take thy pastime, and do what thou hast in mind ». — The sixth house is *of sacred Scripture,* in which he abides in the lumen[^tr-lumen] of truth; the Psalm[^p476-7]: « God shall be known in her houses, when he shall protect her »; and Proverbs, the ninth chapter: « Wisdom hath built herself a house, she hath hewn her out seven pillars » etc. — The seventh house is *of the Jerusalem above,* in which he abides in the joy of felicity; John, the fourteenth chapter[^p476-8]: « In my Father's house there are many mansions »; and again the Psalm: « I rejoiced at the things that were said to me: We shall go into the house of the Lord ». — Since, then, in these seven ways he is received in the Church of the Gentiles, which is designated through Zacheus; therefore this Gospel is read at the Dedications of churches, which designate spiritual houses, of which the first of Peter, the second chapter[^p476-9]: « To whom coming, a living stone », « be you also as living stones built up spiritual houses, a holy priesthood, offering spiritual victims, acceptable » etc.

12. (Vers. 8.). *But Zacheus standing* etc. After he described Zacheus' studiousness and hospitality, here he describes his *liberality*[^p476-10]; which indeed he describes as to a threefold condition, namely as to *Zacheus' offering,* sufficient, *Christ's acceptance,* receiving, and *the reason* principally moving.

First, then, as to Zacheus' *sufficient offering* he sets forth: *But Zacheus standing,* out of the *manliness* of the heart; to the Ephesians, the sixth chapter[^p476-11]: « Stand therefore, having your loins girt about with truth »; *said to the Lord: Behold, the half of my goods, Lord, I give to the poor,* out of the *liberality* of the heart; in which perfect liberality is shown, because he gives *abundantly;* for he gives *the half,* according to that of Tobias, the fourth chapter[^p476-12]: « Son, if thou have much, give abundantly; but if little, even that willingly » etc., in imitation of him « who giveth to all men abundantly, and upbraideth not », James, the first chapter. — He gives *lawfully,* whence he says: *Of my goods;* Proverbs, the third chapter[^p476-13]: « Honour the Lord with thy substance »; against which Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-fourth chapter: « The most High approveth not the gifts of the wicked »; and therefore it is subjoined there: « He that offereth sacrifice of the substance of the poor » etc. — Again he gives *speedily;* whence he says: *I give;* Proverbs, the third chapter[^p476-14]: « Say not to thy friend: Go, and come again, when thou canst give at once »; and Ecclesiasticus, the fourth chapter: « Afflict not the heart of the needy, and defer not to give to him that is in distress ». — Again he gives *discreetly,* because *to the poor;* Ecclesiasticus, the twelfth chapter[^p476-15]: « If thou do good, know to whom thou dost it »; and above, the fourteenth chapter: « Call the poor, the feeble, the blind and the lame ». — Again he gives *in order,* because he gives his own, restoring what is another's, and not only restoring what was taken away, but also the damage. — Whence he says: *If I have wronged any man*[^p476-16]*, I restore fourfold.* This he says according to the Law, Exodus, the twenty-second chapter: « If any man steal a sheep, he shall restore four ». And this is necessary to perfect liberality; whence Augustine[^p476-17]: « One of these is not enough; nor has it the grace of liberality, because not spoils, but gifts are sought ».

13. (Vers. 9.). Second, as to *Christ's receiving acceptance* he subjoins: *And Jesus said*[^p476-18] *to him: This day is salvation come to this house,*
<!-- page 477 -->
through the bestowal of divine grace, which was given at the presence of Christ, through which the Church is dedicated and sanctified; the Gloss[^p477-1]: « *Salvation,* which once filled the house of the Jews, today has shone upon the people of the Gentiles », according to that of Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter: « It is a small thing that thou shouldst be my servant to convert the dregs of Israel. I have given thee to be the light of the Gentiles, that thou mayst be my salvation even to the farthest part of the earth ». And this *salvation* is owed to the predestined. — Therefore he subjoins: *Because he also is a son of Abraham;* the Gloss[^p477-2]: « *A son,* not by flesh, but by faith »; to the Romans, the ninth chapter: « Neither are all they that are the seed of Abraham sons; but in Isaac shall thy seed be called ». For « not they that are the sons of the flesh are sons, but they that are the sons of the promise are accounted for the seed ». And of these sons above, the third chapter: « God is able of these stones to raise up sons to Abraham »; and John, the eleventh chapter: « Jesus suffered, that he might gather together in one the sons of God that were dispersed ».

14. (Vers. 10.). Third, as to *the moving reason* he subjoins: *But the Son of man is come*[^p477-3] *to seek,* through *wisdom;* the Psalm: « I have gone astray like a sheep that is lost; seek thy servant, O Lord » etc. — *And to save that which was lost,* namely through *mercy;* the second of Paralipomenon, the last chapter[^p477-4]: « Me unworthy thou wilt save according to thy great mercy »; and this is the saving mercy. On account of which he is also called *Jesus;* Matthew, the first chapter[^p477-5]: « Thou shalt call his name Jesus; for he shall save his people from their sins »; and Acts, the fourth chapter: « Neither is there salvation in any other. For there is no other name under heaven given to men, whereby we must be saved ». Whence to the Romans, the tenth chapter: « Every one, whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord, shall be saved ».
