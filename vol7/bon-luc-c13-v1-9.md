# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XIII, Vers. 1-9
## *Aderant autem quidam in ipso tempore. Supra redarguit negligentiam respectu gratiae cognoscendae, hic redarguit negligentiam respectu poenitentiae faciendae*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 336 -->

### Capitulum XIII.

1. *Aderant autem quidam in*[^p336-2] *ipso tempore.* Supra redarguit negligentiam respectu gratiae cognoscendae, hic redarguit negligentiam respectu *poenitentiae faciendae.* Et hoc quidem facit tripliciter: primo, *exemplo audito;* secundo, *exemplo assumto,* ibi[^p336-3]: *Sicut illi decem et octo* etc.; tertio, *exemplo adinvento* sive excogitato, ibi: *Dicebat autem hanc similitudinem.*

Circa exemplum *auditum* hoc ordine procedit: primo proponitur *atrocitas supplicii,* secundo negatur *enormitas delicti,* et tertio concluditur *necessitas poenitendi.*

2. (Vers. 1.). Primo ergo introducitur *narratio atrocitatis supplicii,* cum dicitur: *Aderant autem quidam in ipso tempore, nuntiantes*[^p336-4] *illi de Galilaeis,* scilicet dura et terribilia, quae maxime consueverunt recitari; unde et subdit: *Quorum sanguinem Pilatus miscuit cum sacrificiis eorum,* secundum historiam. Narrat Iosephus[^p336-5], quod quidam erat in Galilaea, faciens se Messiam in Lege promissum, et multi de Galilaeis sequebantur ipsum, quibus assignaverat diem, quo ascensurus erat in caelum, videntibus illis, de monte Garizim. Videns autem Pilatus, quod multos seduxerat de tetrarchia Herodis, timuit, ne post eos seduceret eos qui erant de tetrarchia sua. Cum ergo dies assignata esset, et illi quos seduxerat, adorarent eum et sacrificarent ei; repente supervenit Pilatus et interfecit eos inter sacrificia eorum; et inter illos occidit qui faciebat se Christum. Unde de hoc potest intelligi illud Actuum quinto[^p336-6]: « Ante hos dies exstitit Theodas dicens, se esse aliquem, cui consensit numerus virorum circiter quadringentorum, qui occisus est, et omnes, qui credebant ei, dissipati ». « Post hunc exstitit Iudas Galilaeus et avertit populum post se, et ipse periit, et omnes, quotquot consenserunt ei, dispersi sunt ». Et sic acerbitas fuit supplicii, quia *repente, graviter* et *in multitudine* traditi sunt in horrendam mortem.

3. (Vers. 2. 3.). Secundo quantum ad *negationem maioris enormitatis delicti* subditur: *Et respondens dixit illis: Putatis, quod hi Galilaei prae omnibus Galilaeis peccatores fuerint, quia talia passi sunt?* Hoc enim frequenter consuevit credi ab hominibus, qui iudicant secundum exteriora[^p336-7]; et tamen non est ita iudicandum. — Ideo subdit: *Non, dico vobis,* id est, assero, quod non erant prae aliis peccatores. Ideo non est temere iudicandum, sed potius formidandum, quia secreta sunt iudicia Dei; unde Ecclesiastae octavo[^p336-8]: « Vidi impios sepultos, qui, cum adhuc viverent, in loco sancto erant et laudabantur in civitate quasi iustorum operum »; et post: « Sunt iusti, quibus mala proveniunt, quasi opera egerint impiorum; et sunt impii, qui ita securi sunt, quasi iustorum facta habeant »; et iterum nono[^p336-9]: « Omnia in futurum servantur incerta, eo quod universa aeque eveniunt iusto et impio, bono et malo, mundo et immundo, immolanti hostias et sacrificia contemnenti ». Et ideo dicitur primae ad Corinthios quarto: « Nolite ante tempus iudicare, quoadusque veniat Dominus, qui et illuminabit abscondita tenebrarum
<!-- page 337 -->
et manifestabit consilia cordium ». Ut enim dicit Gregorius[^p337-1]: « Iudicia Dei non sunt temere discutienda, sed formidoloso silentio veneranda ».

4. Tertio quantum ad *conclusionem necessitatis poenitendi* subditur: *Sed nisi poenitentiam habueritis, omnes similiter*[^p337-2] *peribitis;* Ecclesiastici secundo: « Qui timent Dominum custodiunt mandata illius et patientiam habebunt usque ad inspectionem illius, dicentes: Si poenitentiam non egerimus, incidemus in manus Domini et non in manus hominum »; sed, sicut dicitur ad Hebraeos decimo[^p337-3], « horrendum est incidere in manus Dei viventis », quia in Psalmo: « Nisi conversi fueritis, gladium suum vibrabit » etc.; et Ecclesiastici quinto: « Ne tardes converti ad Dominum et ne differas de die in diem. Subito enim veniet ira illius, et in tempore vindictae disperdet te ».

5. Et nota, quod duodecim de causis *perit* aliquis, sicut colligitur ex Scripturis.

Primo, propter defectum *poenitentiae,* sicut habetur hic: « Si non habueritis poenitentiam, omnes similiter peribitis »; et Lucae decimo quinto[^p337-4]: « Quanti mercenarii in domo patris mei abundant panibus! ego autem hic fame pereo »; quocontra dicitur secundae Petri tertio: « Patienter agit Deus propter vos, nolens aliquem perire, sed omnes ad poenitentiam reverti ».

Secundo, propter defectum *providentiae;* Iob quarto[^p337-5]: « Quia nullus intelligit, in aeternum peribunt »; quocontra Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio: « Doctrinam oris audite, filii, et qui custodierint illam non perient in labiis suis » etc.

Tertio, propter defectum *patientiae;* Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p337-6]: « Omnes, qui acceperint gladium, gladio peribunt »; Psalmus: « Peccator videbit et irascetur » etc.; quocontra Psalmus: « Non in finem oblivio erit pauperis, patientia pauperum non peribit in finem ».

Quarto, propter defectum *innocentiae;* Psalmus[^p337-7]: « Inimici defecerunt frameae in finem »; et post: « Periit memoria eorum cum sonitu » etc.; quocontra Iob quarto: « Recordare, obsecro, quis unquam innocens periit, aut quando recti deleti sunt »?

Quinto, propter defectum *clementiae;* Psalmus[^p337-8]: « Increpasti gentes, et periit impius »; Ioannis undecimo: « Expedit vobis, ut unus moriatur homo pro populo, et non tota gens pereat ».

Sexto, propter defectum *sapientiae;* Baruch tertio[^p337-9]: « Quoniam non habuerunt sapientiam, perierunt propter suam insipientiam »; quocontra Proverbiorum vigesimo quarto: « Cum inveneris sapientiam, habebis in novissimis spem, et spes tua non peribit » etc.

6. Septimo, propter defectum *credulitatis;* primae ad Corinthios decimo[^p337-10]: « Neque tentemus Christum, sicut quidam eorum tentaverunt et a serpentibus perierunt »; quocontra Ioannis tertio: « Exaltari oportet Filium hominis, ut omnis, qui credit in eum, non pereat, sed habeat vitam aeternam ».

Octavo, propter defectum *caritatis;* secundae ad Thessalonicenses secundo[^p337-11]: « His qui pereunt, eo quod caritatem veritatis non receperunt, ut salvi fierent »; quocontra dicitur Ioannis decimo septimo: « Nemo ex eis periit nisi filius perditionis » etc.

Nono, propter defectum *veritatis;* Proverbiorum decimo nono[^p337-12]: « Falsus testis non erit impunitus, et qui loquitur mendacia peribit »; et vigesimo primo: « Testis mendax peribit ».

Decimo, propter defectum *pacis et unitatis;* primae ad Corinthios decimo[^p337-13]: « Neque murmuraveritis, sicut quidam eorum murmuraverunt et perierunt ab exterminatore »; et Numerorum decimo sexto, de schismaticis, qui contra Dominum murmuraverunt « et descenderunt vivi in infernum, operti humo, et perierunt ».

Undecimo, propter defectum *liberalitatis;* Ecclesiastae quinto[^p337-14]: « Divitiae, congregatae in malum domini sui, perierunt in afflictione maxima », id est, perire fecerunt; et primae ad Timotheum ultimo: « Qui volunt divites fieri incidunt in tentationem et in laqueum diaboli et desideria multa inutilia et nociva, quae mergunt homines in interitum et perditionem ».

Duodecimo, propter defectum *humilitatis;* Ecclesiastici octavo[^p337-15]: « Audax secundum voluntatem suam vadit, et simul cum stultitia illius peries »; quocontra Matthaei decimo octavo: « Non est voluntas ante Patrem vestrum, ut pereat unus ex his pusillis »; Ioannis decimo: « Oves meae vocem meam audiunt, et ego vitam aeternam do eis, et non peribunt in aeternum ».

7. (Vers. 4.). *Sicut illi decem et octo, supra*[^p337-16] *quos* etc. Hic *redarguit negligentiam poenitendi per exemplum assumtum,* quod est conforme ad exemplum auditum; in quo *praemittitur gravitas poenae,*
<!-- page 338 -->
et *negatur profunditas nequitiae,* et *concluditur necessitas poenitentiae.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *gravitatem et horribilitatem poenae* repentinae dicit: *Sicut illi decem et octo, supra quos cecidit turris in Siloa*[^p338-1] *et occidit eos.* Isti decem et octo Ierosolymitae erant, qui ad litteram aedificaverunt turrim iuxta Siloam, et ruina turris oppressi sunt quasi ex improviso; et hoc iusto iudicio Dei, quia Proverbiorum decimo septimo: « Qui altam facit domum suam quaerit ruinam eius » etc.

8. (Vers. 4. 5.). Secundo quantum ad *negationem profunditatis nequitiae* subdit: *Putatis, quia et ipsi debitores fuerint praeter omnes homines habitantes in Ierusalem?* quia soli puniti sunt. — *Non, dico vobis,* quia certe multi alii erant peccatores in ea; Ieremiae quinto[^p338-2]: « Circuite vias Ierusalem et aspicite et considerate et quaerite in plateis eius, an inveniatis virum facientem iudicium et quaerentem fidem, et propitius ero ei ».

9. Tertio quantum ad *conclusionem necessitatis poenitentiae* subiungitur: *Sed si poenitentiam non habueritis*[^p338-3]*, omnes similiter peribitis; similiter,* non quantum ad poenam corporalem, sed quantum ad spiritualem et gehennalem. Sicut enim super istos cecidit *turris,* sic super omnes reprobos cadet ille *lapis angularis,* de quo Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p338-4]: « Qui ceciderit super lapidem istum confringetur; super quem autem ceciderit, conteret eum ». Hic autem lapis, qui crevit in montem[^p338-5], recte dicitur *turris* propter fortitudinem et altitudinem; Proverbiorum decimo octavo: « Turris fortissima, nomen Domini ». Hos reprobos conteret haec turris in iudicio, secundum illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo septimo[^p338-6]: « Conteretur cum delinquente delictum » etc.; et tunc peribunt cum peccatis suis; Ieremiae decimo: « Vana sunt et opera eorum risu digna; in tempore visitationis suae peribunt ». Tales sunt qui opera poenitentiae negligunt et crucem Christi contemnunt; primae ad Corinthios primo[^p338-7]: « Verbum crucis pereuntibus quidem stultitia est, his autem, qui salvi fiunt, virtus est Dei in salutem » etc.

10. Nota autem, quod homo puniri consuevit decem de causis, videlicet ad *purgationem,* ut Maria, soror Moysi; Numerorum duodecimo[^p338-8]: « Apparuit Maria candens lepra quasi nix » etc. — Ad *probationem,* ut Iob; Iob secundo: « Egressus satan a facie Domini, percussit Iob ulcere pessimo a planta pedis usque ad verticem eius ». — Ad *humiliationem,* ut Paulus; secundae ad Corinthios duodecimo[^p338-9]: « Datus est mihi stimulus carnis meae, angelus satanae, qui me colaphizet », scilicet « ne magnitudo revelationum extollat me ». — Ad *glorificationem divinae potentiae,* ut caecus natus; Ioannis nono: « Neque hic peccavit neque parentes eius, sed ut manifestentur opera Dei in illo » etc. — Ad *commemorationem divinae clementiae,* sicut erat pater Ioannis Baptistae; Lucae primo[^p338-10]: « Ecce, eris tacens et non poteris loqui usque in diem » etc. — Ad *manifestationem divinae iustitiae,* sicut Herodes; Actuum duodecimo[^p338-11]: « Percussit eum Angelus Domini ». « Et consumtus a vermibus exspiravit »; et sicut Antiochus, secundi Machabaeorum decimo. — Ad *punitionem parentum;* Exodi vigesimo[^p338-12]: « Ego Deus, zelotes, vindicans peccata » etc.; propter quod pueri Sodomitae submersi sunt, Genesis decimo nono. — Ad *terrorem aliorum;* Proverbiorum decimo nono[^p338-13]: « Flagellato pestilente, sapientior erit stultus ». Sic et isti puniti sunt, de quibus agitur hic, ut ceteri terreantur. — Ad *exempla posterorum;* Tobiae secundo[^p338-14]: « Hanc tentationem permisit Deus evenire ei, ut posteris daretur exemplum patientiae ». — Ad *detestationem criminum,* sicut Dathan et Abiron, Numerorum decimo sexto[^p338-15]: « Dirupta est terra sub pedibus eorum, et aperiens os suum » etc. Et sic Giezi, quarti Regum quinto: « Lepra Naaman adhaerebit tibi et semini tuo usque in sempiternum »; et sic Ananias, Actuum quinto: « Cur tentavit satanas cor tuum » etc. In quo ostenditur, quantum oportet[^p338-16]
<!-- page 339 -->
detestari scissuram, simoniam et apostasiam; ideo statim sic puniti fuerunt.

11. (Vers. 6.). *Dicebat autem*[^p339-1] *hanc similitudinem: Arborem fici habebat* etc. Hic tertio subditur exemplum *excogitatum* sive adinventum de infructuosa ficulnea excidenda; sub cuius metaphora intelliguntur tria circa animam, quae negligit poenitentiam, scilicet *defectus negligentiae* in non fructificando, *rigor sententiae* in comminando et *utilitas*[^p339-2] *poenitentiae* in rimediando.

Primo ergo quantum ad *defectum negligentiae in fructificando* dicit: *Arborem fici habebat quidam plantatam in vinea sua,* scilicet ad fructum suscipiendum ex ea, quem tamen tollit negligentia. — Unde subdit: *Et venit, quaerens fructum in illa, et non invenit.* Per *vineam* intelligitur congregatio ecclesiastica; Isaiae quinto[^p339-3]: « Vinea Domini exercituum domus Israel est ». Per *ficum infructuosam,* sed foliis plenam, intelligitur anima, quae recusat poenitentiam propter negligentiam; Ioelis primo[^p339-4]: « Posuit vineam meam in desertum et ficum meam decorticavit, nudans spoliavit eam et proiecit, albi facti sunt rami eius ». Haec ficus est *foliis* plena, quando verbis abundat ad excusationem suam; Genesis tertio[^p339-5]: « Consuerunt folia ficus et fecerunt sibi perizomata ». Et haec est Domini maledictione digna; Matthaei vigesimo primo: « Videns fici arborem, nihil invenit in ea nisi folia tantum et ait illi: Nunquam ex te nascatur fructus ».

12. (Vers. 7.). Secundo quantum ad *rigorem sententiae in comminando* subdit: *Dixit autem ad cultorem vineae: Ecce, anni tres sunt, ex quo venio, quaerens fructum in ficulnea hac, et non invenio.* Per *tres annos* intelligitur pueritia, adolescentia et iuventus, in quibus negligit quis poenitentiam agere, secundum illud Iob vigesimo quarto[^p339-6]: « Dedit ei Deus locum poenitentiae, et ipse abutitur eo in superbiam ». *Cultor istius vineae* est praedicator et maxime praelatus, de quo primae ad Corinthios tertio: « Ego plantavi, Apollo rigavit ». Unde Augustinus Contra quinque Haereses[^p339-7]: « Ubi estis, o boni agricolae, quid agitis, quare vacatis? et videtis, quot malis plena sit terra: hinc spinae, hinc tribuli, hinc foenum surgit. Spinas incendite, tribulos eradicate, foenum secate et semina bona spargite. Non terreat hiems; etsi abundet iniquitas, tamen ferveat vestra caritas. Serite hieme quod metatis aestate » etc. — Tali agricolae negligenti competit comminari severitatem divini iudicii; propter quod subdit: *Succide ergo illam, ut quid etiam terram occupat?* id est, denuntia eam succidendam, secundum quod dicebat Ioannes Baptista Matthaei tertio[^p339-8]: « Facite fructum dignum poenitentiae ». « Iam enim securis ad radicem posita est. Omnis arbor, quae non facit fructum bonum, excidetur et in ignem mittetur »; et Paulus ad Romanos secundo[^p339-9]: « An ignoras, quia benignitas Dei ad poenitentiam te adducat? Tu autem secundum duritiam tuam et impoenitens cor thesaurizas tibi iram in die irae et revelationis iusti iudicii Dei »; et David in Psalmo: « Propterea Deus destruet te in finem et evellet te et emigrabit te de tabernaculo tuo et radicem tuam de terra viventium ».

13. (Vers. 8.). Tertio quantum ad *necessitatem poenitentiae in remediando* subiungit: *At ille respondens dixit*[^p339-10] *illi: Domine, dimitte illam et hoc anno,* id est in tempore senectutis, *usquedum fodiam circa illam et mittam stercora,* id est, donec reducam ad memoriam ei suam fragilitatem et eius iniquitatem. *Fodit* enim qui se humiliat; Isaiae secundo[^p339-11]: « Ingredere in petram, abscondere in fossa humo a facie timoris Domini et a gloria maiestatis eius »; et quarti Regum tertio: « Facite alveum torrentis huius fossas et fossas ». Unde Glossa[^p339-12]: « *Fossa* quippe in imo est, et increpatio, dum mentem sibi demonstrat, humiliat ». *Stercora mittit* qui peccatum ad memoriam reducit; unde Glossa Bedae[^p339-13]: « *Mittam stercora,* id est, malorum quae fecit abominationem ad animum reducam, et compunctionis gratiam cum fructibus boni operis quasi de pinguedine stercoris exsuscitem ». « Peccata enim[^p339-14] *stercora*
<!-- page 340 -->
dicuntur; unde Ioelis primo: *Computruerunt iumenta in stercore suo* »; et Threnorum quarto: « Qui nutriebantur in croceis amplexati sunt stercora ». Horum recogitatio fecundat animam; unde Isaiae trigesimo octavo[^p340-1]: « Recogitabo tibi omnes annos meos in amaritudine animae meae »; et Ieremiae trigesimo primo: « Statue tibi speculam, pone tibi amaritudines ». Sicut autem fructificat arbor, quando circa eam mittuntur stercora, sic et anima, dum peccatorum suorum recordatur.

14. (Vers. 9.). Ideo subdit: *Et si quidem fecerit fructum,* supple: dimittes eam; et est locutio figurativa, in qua deficit dictio necessaria, sed tamen communis est modus loquendi[^p340-2]. — *Sin autem, in futurum succides illam*[^p340-3], scilicet in senio gladio iudicialis sententiae; Canonica Iudae: « Arbores infructuosae, autumnales, bis mortuae, quibus procella tenebrarum reservatur in aeternum ». — Vel *succides,* per mortem; Deuteronomii vigesimo[^p340-4]: « Si qua ligna non sunt pomifera, sed agrestia, succides ea »; et Iob quarto: « De mane usque ad vesperam succidentur; et quia nullus intelligit, in aeternum peribunt ».

15. Et nota, quod cum sint sex hominis aetates quasi sex anni, scilicet *infantia, pueritia, adolescentia, iuventus, senectus* et *senium*[^p340-5]; in *prima* aetate non quaerit fructum Dominus propter liberi arbitrii defectum; in *secunda* vero requirit aliquo modo, scilicet in *pueritia,* in *adolescentia* magis et in *iuventute* maxime; exspectat tamen negligentem frequenter usque ad *senectutem,* et tunc tandem ad extremum profert severitatem sententiae super negligentem[^p340-6].

16. Potest autem hoc *allegorice* exponi de synagoga, in qua Dominus *quaesivit fructum* in *tribus aetatibus* mundi intermediis, in quibus synagoga viguit, et adhuc ad poenitentiam exspectat; vel in *triplici tempore,* scilicet Patriarcharum, Iudicum et Regum, et tandem *exspectavit* tempore Prophetarum. Et quia non fuit conversa ad verba eorum, ideo *succisa* est in Christi adventu[^p340-7].

Vel isti tres anni referuntur ad tempus *triplicis legis,* scilicet naturae, Scripturae et gratiae; sed haec sufficienter exponuntur in Glossa[^p340-8].

17. Secundum autem *morum informationem spiritualiter* notandum, quod *arbor infructuosa succiditur,* maxime quae per triennium fructum non facit; in quo intelligitur recte homo negligens ad opera virtutis; et quidem recte per *ficum infructuosam* negligentia humana arguitur: primo, quia ficus habet folia multa et magna, et in illo potissime arguitur negligentia illius qui magna habet verba; Ecclesiastici quarto[^p340-9]: « Noli citatus esse in lingua tua et inutilis et remissus in operibus tuis »; quocontra Osee decimo: « Vitis frondosa Israel, fructus adaequatus est ei ». — Secundo, quia folia ficus sunt manui similia; et in illo potissime reprehenditur negligentia eius qui alios ad operandum invitat; ad Romanos secundo[^p340-10]: « Qui alium doces te ipsum non doces » etc.; quocontra dicitur Actuum primo: « Coepit Iesus facere et docere » etc. — Tertio, quia fructus ficus est suavis; ideo merito ipsius carentia negligentiam designat, et praesentia ipsius designat opus virtutis, quod est suave et valde delectabile; « nam[^p340-11] qui continet et hoc ipso gaudet, ille solus est castus ». — Sunt autem opera virtutum suavia virtuti inchoatae, suaviora proficienti, sed suavissima iam perfectae; propter quod Iudicum nono: « Quomodo possum deserere dulcedinem meam fructusque suavissimos et ire, ut inter cetera ligna promovear »?

18. Habere autem dicitur ficus *fructus suavissimos* ad designandum triplex genus fructuum. Quidam enim sunt *suaves,* ut fructus poenitentiae; quidam *suaviores,* ut fructus iustitiae; sed quidam *suavissimi,* ut fructus sapientiae. Haec tria genera fructuum quaerit Dominus in tribus annis, quia in triplici statu. Nam fructus *poenitentiae* quaeritur[^p340-12]
<!-- page 341 -->
ab incipientibus, fructus *iustitiae* a proficientibus, fructus autem *sapientiae* a perfectis.

Primo igitur in *primo anno* requirit *fructum poenitentiae,* de quo supra tertio[^p341-1]: « Facite dignos fructus poenitentiae ». Hic autem fructus in tribus consistit: primo in *resecatione superfluitatis;* Ieremiae secundo: « Induxi vos in terram Carmeli, ut comederetis fructum eius et bona illius ». *Carmelus* interpretatur scientia circumcisionis et designat resecationem superfluitatis. — Secundo in *mortificatione carnis;* Ioannis duodecimo[^p341-2]: « Nisi granum frumenti cadens in terram mortuum fuerit » etc. — Tertio in *purificatione mentis;* Isaiae vigesimo septimo: « Dimittetur iniquitas domui Iacob, et hic omnis fructus eius, ut auferatur peccatum eius ».

19. In *secundo anno,* scilicet in statu proficientium, requirit Dominus *fructum iustitiae;* de quo ad Philippenses primo[^p341-3]: « Sitis sinceres sine offensa, repleti fructu iustitiae per Iesum Christum, in gloriam et laudem Dei ». — Hunc fructum *concordia inchoat* sive seminat; Iacobi tertio[^p341-4]: « Fructus autem iustitiae in pace seminatur facientibus pacem »; Iob vigesimo secundo: « Habeto pacem, et per hanc habebis fructus optimos ». — *Clementia patefacit et provehit;* Psalmus[^p341-5]: « Erit tanquam lignum, quod plantatum est secus decursus aquarum » etc.; unde in Psalmo iterum: « A fructu frumenti, vini et olei sui multiplicati sunt ». — *Patientia conservat;* Iacobi quinto[^p341-6]: « Ecce, agricola exspectat pretiosum fructum terrae, patienter ferens » etc.; et ad Hebraeos duodecimo: « Omnis disciplina in praesenti quidem non est gaudii, sed moeroris; postea autem affert fructum pacatissimum ». — Sed *perseverantia recolligit;* Lucae vigesimo primo[^p341-7]: « Videte ficulneam et omnes arbores: cum producunt ex se fructum, scitis, quoniam prope est aestas »; Proverbiorum ultimo de muliere forti dicitur, quod « de fructu manuum suarum plantavit vineam ».

20. In *tertio anno* requirit *fructum sapientiae,* de quo Proverbiorum tertio[^p341-8]: « Primi et purissimi fructus eius ». Hic autem fructus est *speciosissimus* ad speculandum; Deuteronomii trigesimo tertio: « De benedictione Domini terra eius, de pomis fructuum solis et lunae ». — *Fragrantissimus* ad odorandum; Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto[^p341-9]: « Ego quasi vitis fructificavi suavitatem odoris, et flores mei fructus honoris et honestatis »; Canticorum quarto: « Emissiones tuae paradisus malorum punicorum cum pomorum fructibus ». — *Sapidissimus* ad degustandum; Canticorum secundo[^p341-10]: « Sub umbra illius quem desideraveram, sedi, et fructus eius dulcis gutturi meo »; et Canticorum quinto: « Veniat dilectus meus in hortum suum et comedat fructum pomorum suorum » etc. — *Pretiosissimus* ad possidendum; Proverbiorum octavo[^p341-11]: « Melior est fructus meus auro et lapide pretioso, et genimina mea argento electo ». — *Sempiternus* ad perdurandum; Sapientiae tertio: « Bonorum laborum gloriosus est fructus, et quae non concidat, radix sapientiae »; quia sapientia hic inchoatur et durat in sempiternum.

Cum igitur Dominus requirat in arbore, quam plantavit, in primo statu fructum *poenitentiae,* et ille sit *triformis;* in secundo fructum *iustitiae,* et iste sit *quadriformis;* in tertio fructum *sapientiae,* et iste sit *quinquiformis:* requirit igitur duodecim fructus in arbore plantata. In cuius figuram dicitur Apocalypsis vigesimo secundo[^p341-12]: « Ex utraque parte fluminis lignum vitae, afferens fructus duodecim, per menses singulos reddens fructum suum et folia ligni ad sanitatem gentium ».

### Chapter XIII.

1. *And there were present at that very time some*[^p336-2]. Above, he convicted negligence in respect of grace to be known; here he convicts negligence in respect of *penance to be done.* And this indeed he does in three ways: first, *by an example heard;* secondly, *by an example taken up,* there[^p336-3]: *As those eighteen* etc.; thirdly, *by an example invented* or devised, there: *He spoke this parable.*

About the example *heard* he proceeds in this order: first is set forth *the atrocity of the penalty,* secondly is denied *the enormity of the offence,* and thirdly is concluded *the necessity of doing penance.*

2. (Vers. 1.). First therefore is brought in *the narration of the atrocity of the penalty,* when it is said: *And there were present at that very time some that told*[^p336-4] *him of the Galileans,* that is, hard and terrible things, which men are most wont to recount; and so he subjoins: *Whose blood Pilate had mingled with their sacrifices,* according to the history. Josephus relates[^p336-5] that there was a certain man in Galilee, making himself out to be the Messias promised in the Law, and many of the Galileans were following him, to whom he had appointed a day on which he was to ascend into heaven, in their sight, from mount Garizim. But Pilate, seeing that he had seduced many from the tetrarchy of Herod, feared lest after them he should seduce those who were of his own tetrarchy. When therefore the day had been appointed, and those whom he had seduced were adoring him and sacrificing to him, Pilate came upon them suddenly and slew them among their sacrifices; and among them he slew him who made himself out to be the Christ. Hence that of the Acts, the fifth chapter, can be understood of this[^p336-6]: « Before these days rose up Theodas, affirming himself to be somebody, to whom a number of men, about four hundred, joined themselves: who was slain; and all that believed him were scattered ». « After this man rose up Judas of Galilee, and drew away the people after him: he also perished; and all, even as many as consented to him, were dispersed ». And so the bitterness of the penalty was that they were given over to a horrible death *suddenly, grievously* and *in a multitude.*

3. (Vers. 2. 3.). Secondly, as to *the denial of a greater enormity of offence,* it is subjoined: *And he answering said to them: Think you that these Galileans were sinners above all the men of Galilee, because they suffered such things?* For this is frequently wont to be believed by men, who judge according to the exterior things[^p336-7]; and yet it is not so to be judged. — Therefore he subjoins: *No, I say to you,* that is, I affirm that they were not sinners above the others. Therefore one must not judge rashly, but rather fear, for the judgements of God are secret; whence Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter[^p336-8]: « I saw the wicked buried: who also when they were yet living were in the holy place, and were praised in the city as men of just works »; and after: « There are just men to whom evils happen, as though they had done the works of the wicked: and there are wicked men who are as secure, as though they had the deeds of the just »; and again in the ninth[^p336-9]: « All things are kept uncertain for the time to come, because all things equally happen to the just and to the wicked, to the good and to the evil, to the clean and to the unclean, to him that offereth victims, and to him that despiseth sacrifices ». And therefore it is said in the First to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « Judge not before the time, until the Lord come, who both will bring to light the hidden things of darkness,
<!-- page 337 -->
and will make manifest the counsels of the hearts ». For, as Gregory says[^p337-1]: « The judgements of God are not to be rashly discussed, but to be venerated with a fearful silence ».

4. Thirdly, as to *the conclusion of the necessity of doing penance,* it is subjoined: *But unless you shall do penance, you shall all likewise*[^p337-2] *perish;* Ecclesiasticus, the second chapter: « They that fear the Lord keep his commandments, and will have patience until his visitation, saying: If we do not penance, we shall fall into the hands of the Lord, and not into the hands of men »; but, as is said in Hebrews, the tenth chapter[^p337-3], « it is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God », for in the Psalm: « Except you will be converted, he will brandish his sword » etc.; and Ecclesiasticus, the fifth chapter: « Delay not to be converted to the Lord, and defer it not from day to day. For his wrath shall come on a sudden, and in the time of vengeance he will destroy thee ».

5. And note that for twelve causes one *perishes,* as is gathered from the Scriptures.

First, for lack of *penance,* as is had here: « If you do not do penance, you shall all likewise perish »; and Luke, the fifteenth chapter[^p337-4]: « How many hired servants in my father's house abound with bread, and I here perish with hunger! »; against which it is said in the Second of Peter, the third chapter: « God dealeth patiently for your sake, not willing that any should perish, but that all should return to penance ».

Secondly, for lack of *providence;* Job, the fourth chapter[^p337-5]: « Because no one understandeth, they shall perish for ever »; against which Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third chapter: « Hear, O ye children, the discipline of the mouth: and he that will keep it shall not perish by his lips » etc.

Thirdly, for lack of *patience;* Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p337-6]: « All that take the sword shall perish with the sword »; the Psalm: « The wicked shall see, and shall be angry » etc.; against which the Psalm: « The poor man shall not be forgotten to the end: the patience of the poor shall not perish for ever ».

Fourthly, for lack of *innocence;* the Psalm[^p337-7]: « The swords of the enemy have failed unto the end »; and after: « Their memory hath perished with a noise » etc.; against which Job, the fourth chapter: « Remember, I pray thee, who ever perished being innocent? or when were the just destroyed? »

Fifthly, for lack of *clemency;* the Psalm[^p337-8]: « Thou hast rebuked the Gentiles, and the wicked one hath perished »; John, the eleventh chapter: « It is expedient for you that one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not ».

Sixthly, for lack of *wisdom;* Baruch, the third chapter[^p337-9]: « Because they had not wisdom, they perished through their folly »; against which Proverbs, the twenty-fourth chapter: « When thou hast found wisdom, thou shalt have hope in the latter end, and thy hope shall not perish » etc.

6. Seventhly, for lack of *belief;* the First to the Corinthians, the tenth chapter[^p337-10]: « Neither let us tempt Christ: as some of them tempted, and perished by the serpents »; against which John, the third chapter: « The Son of man must be lifted up, that whosoever believeth in him may not perish, but may have life everlasting ».

Eighthly, for lack of *charity;* the Second to the Thessalonians, the second chapter[^p337-11]: « Them that perish; because they receive not the charity of truth, that they might be saved »; against which it is said in John, the seventeenth chapter: « None of them hath perished, but the son of perdition » etc.

Ninthly, for lack of *truth;* Proverbs, the nineteenth chapter[^p337-12]: « A false witness shall not be unpunished: and he that speaketh lies shall perish »; and in the twenty-first: « A lying witness shall perish ».

Tenthly, for lack of *peace and unity;* the First to the Corinthians, the tenth chapter[^p337-13]: « Neither do you murmur: as some of them murmured, and were destroyed by the destroyer »; and Numbers, the sixteenth chapter, of the schismatics, who murmured against the Lord « and went down alive into hell, the earth closing upon them, and they perished ».

Eleventhly, for lack of *liberality;* Ecclesiastes, the fifth chapter[^p337-14]: « Riches gathered together to the hurt of their owner perished with very great affliction », that is, they made him perish; and the First to Timothy, the last chapter: « They that will become rich, fall into temptation, and into the snare of the devil, and into many unprofitable and hurtful desires, which drown men into destruction and perdition ».

Twelfthly, for lack of *humility;* Ecclesiasticus, the eighth chapter[^p337-15]: « A bold man goeth according to his own will, and thou shalt perish together with his folly »; against which Matthew, the eighteenth chapter: « It is not the will of your Father, that one of these little ones should perish »; John, the tenth chapter: « My sheep hear my voice: and I give them life everlasting; and they shall not perish for ever ».

7. (Vers. 4.). *As those eighteen, upon*[^p337-16] *whom* etc. Here *he convicts the negligence of doing penance by an example taken up,* which is conformable to the example heard; in which *the gravity of the punishment is set first,*
<!-- page 338 -->
and *the depth of wickedness is denied,* and *the necessity of penance is concluded.*

First, then, as to *the gravity and horribleness of the* sudden *punishment* he says: *As those eighteen, upon whom the tower fell in Siloe*[^p338-1] *and slew them.* These eighteen were men of Jerusalem who, to the letter, had built a tower beside Siloe, and they were crushed by the ruin of the tower as it were unawares; and this by the just judgement of God, for Proverbs, the seventeenth chapter: « He that maketh his house high, seeketh a downfall » etc.

8. (Vers. 4. 5.). Secondly, as to *the denial of the depth of wickedness* he subjoins: *Think you that they also were debtors above all the men that dwelt in Jerusalem?* because they alone were punished. — *No, I say to you,* for certainly there were many other sinners in it; Jeremias, the fifth chapter[^p338-2]: « Go about through the streets of Jerusalem, and see, and consider, and seek in the broad places thereof, if you can find a man that executeth judgement, and seeketh faith: and I will be merciful unto it ».

9. Thirdly, as to *the conclusion of the necessity of penance* it is subjoined: *But if you do not do penance*[^p338-3]*, you shall all likewise perish; likewise,* not as to bodily punishment, but as to the spiritual and that of hell. For as *the tower* fell upon these, so upon all the reprobate shall fall that *corner stone,* of which Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p338-4]: « Whosoever shall fall on this stone, shall be broken: but on whomsoever it shall fall, it shall grind him to powder ». And this stone, which grew into a mountain[^p338-5], is rightly called *a tower* for its strength and height; Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter: « The name of the Lord is a strong tower ». This tower shall grind these reprobate in the judgement, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p338-6]: « Sin shall be destroyed with the sinner » etc.; and then they shall perish with their sins; Jeremias, the tenth chapter: « They are vain things, and works worthy to be laughed at: in the time of their visitation they shall perish ». Such are they who neglect the works of penance and despise the cross of Christ; the First to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p338-7]: « The word of the cross, to them indeed that perish, is foolishness; but to them that are saved, it is the power of God unto salvation » etc.

10. But note that man is wont to be punished for ten causes, namely for *purgation,* as Mary, the sister of Moses; Numbers, the twelfth chapter[^p338-8]: « Mary appeared white as snow with a leprosy » etc. — For *probation,* as Job; Job, the second chapter: « Satan went forth from the presence of the Lord, and struck Job with a very grievous ulcer, from the sole of the foot even to the top of his head ». — For *humiliation,* as Paul; the Second to the Corinthians, the twelfth chapter[^p338-9]: « There was given me a sting of my flesh, an angel of Satan, to buffet me », that is, « lest the greatness of the revelations should exalt me ». — For *the glorification of the divine power,* as the man born blind; John, the ninth chapter: « Neither hath this man sinned, nor his parents; but that the works of God should be made manifest in him » etc. — For *the commemoration of the divine clemency,* as was the father of John the Baptist; Luke, the first chapter[^p338-10]: « Behold, thou shalt be dumb, and shalt not be able to speak until the day » etc. — For *the manifestation of the divine justice,* as Herod; Acts, the twelfth chapter[^p338-11]: « An angel of the Lord struck him ». « And being eaten up by worms, he gave up the ghost »; and as Antiochus, Second of Machabees, the tenth chapter. — For *the punishment of the parents;* Exodus, the twentieth chapter[^p338-12]: « I am God, jealous, avenging sins » etc.; for which reason the boys of Sodom were overwhelmed, Genesis, the nineteenth chapter. — For *the terror of others;* Proverbs, the nineteenth chapter[^p338-13]: « The wicked man being scourged, the fool shall be wiser ». So too these were punished of whom there is question here, that the rest might be terrified. — For *examples to posterity;* Tobias, the second chapter[^p338-14]: « This trial God permitted to happen to him, that an example of patience might be given to posterity ». — For *the detestation of crimes,* as Dathan and Abiron, Numbers, the sixteenth chapter[^p338-15]: « The earth broke asunder under their feet: and opening her mouth » etc. And so Giezi, Fourth of Kings, the fifth chapter: « The leprosy of Naaman shall also stick to thee, and to thy seed for ever »; and so Ananias, Acts, the fifth chapter: « Why hath Satan tempted thy heart » etc. In which it is shown how much one ought[^p338-16]
<!-- page 339 -->
to detest schism, simony and apostasy; therefore they were thus punished at once.

11. (Vers. 6.). *He spoke*[^p339-1] *this parable: A certain man had a fig tree* etc. Here thirdly is subjoined the example *devised* or invented, of the unfruitful fig tree to be cut down; under whose metaphor three things are understood about the soul that neglects penance, namely *the defect of negligence* in not bearing fruit, *the rigour of the sentence* in threatening, and *the usefulness*[^p339-2] *of penance* in remedying [so printed: *rimediando* for *remediando*].

First, then, as to *the defect of negligence in bearing fruit* he says: *A certain man had a fig tree planted in his vineyard,* that is, to receive fruit from it, which fruit negligence nevertheless takes away. — Whence he subjoins: *And he came seeking fruit on it, and found none.* By *the vineyard* is understood the ecclesiastical congregation; Isaias, the fifth chapter[^p339-3]: « The vineyard of the Lord of hosts is the house of Israel ». By *the unfruitful fig tree,* but full of leaves, is understood the soul that refuses penance through negligence; Joel, the first chapter[^p339-4]: « He hath laid my vineyard waste, and hath barked my fig tree: he hath stripped it bare, and cast it away: the branches thereof are made white ». This fig tree is full of *leaves* when it abounds in words for its own excuse; Genesis, the third chapter[^p339-5]: « They sewed together fig leaves, and made themselves aprons ». And this is worthy of the Lord's curse; Matthew, the twenty-first chapter: « Seeing a fig tree, he found nothing on it but leaves only, and he saith to it: May no fruit grow on thee ».

12. (Vers. 7.). Secondly, as to *the rigour of the sentence in threatening* he subjoins: *And he said to the dresser of the vineyard: Behold, for these three years I come seeking fruit on this fig tree, and I find none.* By *the three years* are understood childhood, adolescence and youth, in which one neglects to do penance, according to that of Job, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p339-6]: « God hath given him place for penance, and he abuseth it unto pride ». *The dresser of this vineyard* is the preacher and above all the prelate, of whom the First to the Corinthians, the third chapter: « I have planted, Apollo watered ». Whence Augustine, Against Five Heresies[^p339-7]: « Where are you, O good husbandmen, what are you doing, why are you idle? and you see with how many evils the earth is full: here thorns, here thistles, here hay springs up. Burn the thorns, root out the thistles, cut the hay and scatter good seed. Let not the winter terrify you; although iniquity abound, yet let your charity be fervent. Sow in winter what you may reap in summer » etc. — It befits to threaten such a negligent husbandman with the severity of the divine judgement; for which reason he subjoins: *Cut it down therefore: why cumbereth it the ground?* that is, declare that it is to be cut down, according to what John the Baptist said, Matthew, the third chapter[^p339-8]: « Bring forth fruit worthy of penance ». « For now the axe is laid to the root. Every tree therefore that doth not yield good fruit, shall be cut down, and cast into the fire »; and Paul, to the Romans, the second chapter[^p339-9]: « Knowest thou not that the kindness of God leadeth thee to penance? But according to thy hardness and impenitent heart, thou treasurest up to thyself wrath, against the day of wrath, and revelation of the just judgement of God »; and David in the Psalm: « Therefore will God destroy thee for ever: he will pluck thee out, and remove thee from thy dwelling place: and thy root out of the land of the living ».

13. (Vers. 8.). Thirdly, as to *the necessity of penance in remedying* he subjoins: *But he answering said*[^p339-10] *to him: Lord, let it alone this year also,* that is, in the time of old age, *until I dig about it and cast dung,* that is, until I bring back to its memory its frailty and its iniquity. For he *digs* who humbles himself; Isaias, the second chapter[^p339-11]: « Enter thou into the rock, and hide thee in the pit from the face of the fear of the Lord, and from the glory of his majesty »; and the Fourth of Kings, the third chapter: « Make the channel of this torrent full of ditches ». Whence the Gloss[^p339-12]: « For *the pit* is in the depth, and rebuke, while it shows the mind to itself, humbles it ». He *casts dung* who brings back his sin to memory; whence the Gloss of Bede[^p339-13]: « *I will cast dung,* that is, I will bring back to the mind the abomination of the evils it has done, and will rouse up the grace of compunction with the fruits of good work as from the fatness of dung ». « For sins[^p339-14] are called
<!-- page 340 -->
dung; whence Joel, the first chapter: *The beasts have rotted in their dung* »; and Lamentations, the fourth chapter: « They that were brought up in scarlet have embraced the dung ». The thinking over of these makes the soul fruitful; whence Isaias, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p340-1]: « I will recount to thee all my years in the bitterness of my soul »; and Jeremias, the thirty-first chapter: « Set thee up a watchtower, make to thee bitternesses ». And as a tree bears fruit when dung is cast about it, so too the soul, while it remembers its sins.

14. (Vers. 9.). Therefore he subjoins: *And if indeed it bear fruit,* supply: thou shalt let it alone; and it is a figurative locution, in which a necessary word is wanting, but yet it is a common manner of speaking[^p340-2]. — *But if not, hereafter thou shalt cut it down*[^p340-3], that is, in extreme old age, with the sword of the judicial sentence; the Canonical Epistle of Jude: « Trees of the autumn, unfruitful, twice dead, to whom the storm of darkness is reserved for ever ». — Or *thou shalt cut it down,* by death; Deuteronomy, the twentieth chapter[^p340-4]: « If there be any trees that are not fruitful, but wild, thou shalt cut them down »; and Job, the fourth chapter: « From morning till evening they shall be cut down: and because no one understandeth, they shall perish for ever ».

15. And note that, since there are six ages of man, as it were six years, namely *infancy, childhood, adolescence, youth, old age* and *extreme old age*[^p340-5]: in the *first* age the Lord does not seek fruit, because of the defect of free choice; but in the *second* he requires it in some manner, namely in *childhood,* in *adolescence* more, and in *youth* most; yet he frequently waits for the negligent man until *old age,* and then at last, at the extremity, he brings forth the severity of the sentence upon the negligent man[^p340-6].

16. But this can be expounded *allegorically* of the synagogue, in which the Lord *sought fruit* in *the three* intermediate *ages* of the world, in which the synagogue flourished, and still waits for it to do penance; or in *a threefold time,* namely of the Patriarchs, of the Judges and of the Kings, and at last *he waited* in the time of the Prophets. And because it was not converted at their words, therefore it *was cut down* at the coming of Christ[^p340-7].

Or these three years are referred to the time of *the threefold law,* namely of nature, of Scripture and of grace; but these things are sufficiently expounded in the Gloss[^p340-8].

17. But according to *the formation of morals spiritually* it is to be noted that *the unfruitful tree is cut down,* above all that which for three years does not bear fruit; in which is rightly understood the man negligent of the works of virtue; and indeed human negligence is rightly convicted by *the unfruitful fig tree:* first, because the fig tree has many and large leaves, and in this the negligence of him who has large words is most of all convicted; Ecclesiasticus, the fourth chapter[^p340-9]: « Be not hasty in thy tongue: and slack and remiss in thy works »; against which Osee, the tenth chapter: « Israel a vine full of branches, the fruit is agreeable to it ». — Secondly, because the leaves of the fig tree are like a hand; and in this the negligence of him who invites others to work is most of all reprehended; to the Romans, the second chapter[^p340-10]: « Thou that teachest another, teachest not thyself » etc.; against which it is said in the Acts, the first chapter: « Jesus began to do and to teach » etc. — Thirdly, because the fruit of the fig tree is sweet; therefore its lack rightly designates negligence, and its presence designates the work of virtue, which is sweet and very delightful; « for[^p340-11] he who is continent and rejoices in this very thing, he alone is chaste ». — Now the works of the virtues are sweet to virtue begun, sweeter to the one making progress, but most sweet to virtue already perfect; for which reason Judges, the ninth chapter: « Can I leave my sweetness, and my most sweet fruits, and go to be promoted among the other trees? »

18. Now the fig tree is said to have *most sweet fruits* to designate a threefold kind of fruits. For some are *sweet,* as the fruits of penance; some *sweeter,* as the fruits of justice; but some *most sweet,* as the fruits of wisdom. These three kinds of fruits the Lord seeks in three years, because in a threefold state. For the fruit of *penance* is sought[^p340-12]
<!-- page 341 -->
from beginners, the fruit of *justice* from those making progress, but the fruit of *wisdom* from the perfect.

First, then, in *the first year* he requires *the fruit of penance,* of which above, in the third chapter[^p341-1]: « Bring forth fruits worthy of penance ». And this fruit consists in three things: first in *the cutting away of superfluity;* Jeremias, the second chapter: « I brought you into the land of Carmel, to eat the fruit thereof, and the best things thereof ». *Carmel* is interpreted the knowledge of circumcision, and designates the cutting away of superfluity. — Secondly in *the mortification of the flesh;* John, the twelfth chapter[^p341-2]: « Unless the grain of wheat falling into the ground die » etc. — Thirdly in *the purification of the mind;* Isaias, the twenty-seventh chapter: « The iniquity of the house of Jacob shall be forgiven, and this is all its fruit, that the sin thereof should be taken away ».

19. In *the second year,* that is, in the state of those making progress, the Lord requires *the fruit of justice;* of which to the Philippians, the first chapter[^p341-3]: « That you may be sincere and without offence, filled with the fruit of justice through Jesus Christ, unto the glory and praise of God ». — This fruit *concord begins* or sows; James, the third chapter[^p341-4]: « The fruit of justice is sown in peace, to them that make peace »; Job, the twenty-second chapter: « Be at peace: and thereby thou shalt have the best fruits ». — *Clemency discloses and advances it;* the Psalm[^p341-5]: « He shall be like a tree which is planted near the running waters » etc.; whence in the Psalm again: « By the fruit of their corn, their wine and oil, they are multiplied ». — *Patience preserves it;* James, the fifth chapter[^p341-6]: « Behold, the husbandman waiteth for the precious fruit of the earth: patiently bearing » etc.; and to the Hebrews, the twelfth chapter: « Now all chastisement for the present indeed seemeth not to bring with it joy, but sorrow: but afterwards it will yield the most peaceable fruit ». — But *perseverance gathers it in;* Luke, the twenty-first chapter[^p341-7]: « See the fig tree, and all the trees: when they now shoot forth their fruit, you know that summer is nigh »; in the last chapter of Proverbs it is said of the valiant woman that « with the fruit of her hands she hath planted a vineyard ».

20. In *the third year* he requires *the fruit of wisdom,* of which Proverbs, the third chapter[^p341-8]: « Her fruit is the first and the purest ». And this fruit is *most beautiful* for beholding; Deuteronomy, the thirty-third chapter: « Of the blessing of the Lord be his land, of the fruits brought forth by the sun and the moon ». — *Most fragrant* for smelling; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p341-9]: « As the vine I have brought forth a pleasant odour: and my flowers are the fruit of honour and riches »; the Canticle, the fourth chapter: « Thy plants are a paradise of pomegranates with the fruits of the orchard ». — *Most savoury* for tasting; the Canticle, the second chapter[^p341-10]: « I sat down under his shadow, whom I desired: and his fruit was sweet to my palate »; and the Canticle, the fifth chapter: « Let my beloved come into his garden, and eat the fruit of his apple trees » etc. — *Most precious* for possessing; Proverbs, the eighth chapter[^p341-11]: « My fruit is better than gold and the precious stone, and my blossoms than choice silver ». — *Everlasting* for enduring; Wisdom, the third chapter: « The fruit of good labours is glorious, and the root of wisdom never faileth »; for wisdom is begun here and endures for ever.

Since therefore the Lord requires in the tree which he has planted, in the first state the fruit of *penance,* and that is *threefold;* in the second the fruit of *justice,* and this is *fourfold;* in the third the fruit of *wisdom,* and this is *fivefold:* he requires therefore twelve fruits in the planted tree. In figure of which it is said in the Apocalypse, the twenty-second chapter[^p341-12]: « On both sides of the river was the tree of life, bearing twelve fruits, yielding its fruits every month, and the leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations ».
