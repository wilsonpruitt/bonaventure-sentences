# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. IV, Vers. 1-13
## *Authenticatur doctrina Christi ex merito vitae*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 88 -->

### Capitulum IV.

#### Pars II. De praedicationis magisterio agitur dupliciter.

1. *Iesus autem plenus Spiritu sancto* etc. Supra[^p88-10] determinavit Evangelista de incarnationis mysterio sive de Christi nativitate; in hac parte agit de *praedicationis magisterio* sive doctrina. Et quoniam duplex est opus sapientis, scilicet docendo, « non mentiri de quibus novit et mentientem manifestare[^p88-11] », ideo pars ista habet duas. In prima *explicat veritatem,* in secunda *confutat falsitatem,* infra undecimo: *Erat Iesus eiiciens daemonium* etc.

#### *Primo explicatur veritas doctrinae quadrupliciter.*

Prima pars, in qua explicat veritatem doctrinae, habet quatuor: in quarum prima fit *doctrinae Christi authenticatio;* in secunda determinatur *discipulorum vocatio,* infra quinto[^p88-12]: *Factum est, cum*
<!-- page 89 -->
*turbae* etc.; in tertia, *vocatorum institutio,* infra sexto: *Factum est in illis diebus* etc.; in quarta, *institutorum delegatio,* infra nono: *Et convocatis duodecim Apostolis, dedit illis* etc. Ordo et sufficientia partium per se apparet.

#### Primo authenticatur doctrina Christi per tria.

Prima igitur pars, in qua fit *doctrinae Christi authenticatio,* continet praesens capitulum et habet tres partes. Quoniam doctrina tripliciter authenticatur: aut ex *merito vitae,* aut ex *testimonio Scripturae,* aut ex *miraculo potentiae;* ideo in prima parte ostenditur auctorizatio doctrinae Christi ex *vitae merito;* in secunda, ex *Scripturae testimonio,* ibi[^p89-1]: *Regressus est Iesus in virtute Spiritus;* in tertia, ex *potentiae miraculo,* ibi: *Et descendit in Capharnaum* etc., ubi determinantur miracula.

#### Authenticatur doctrina Christi ex merito vitae.

*Meritum* autem consistit in *victoria tentationum,* quia[^p89-2] « militia est vita hominis super terram »; ideo describit Evangelista, qualiter tentationem diabolicam superavit. Ad cuius integram descriptionem tria sunt opportuna, scilicet *tentationis occasio; tentandi modus,* quem tangit ibi[^p89-3]: *Dixit autem illi diabolus;* et tertium, *consummatio tentationis,* quam ibi exprimit: *Et consummata omni tentatione* etc.

Tentandi autem *motivum* sumsit diabolus ex quatuor consideratis in Christo: primum est *gratiae plenitudo,* secundum est *loci solitudo,* tertium est *suspicio Divinitatis* propter mirabilem abstinentiam, quartum *inspectio infirmitatis* propter apparentem indigentiam. Consideratio *plenitudinis gratiae* per invidiam movit diaboli voluntatem ad tentandum; consideratio *solitudinis* praestitit tentandi malignitatem; suspicio *Divinitatis* incitavit tentandi curiositatem; inspectio *necessitatis* praebuit tentandi opportunitatem.

2. (Vers. 1.). Introducit ergo *plenitudinem gratiae* in Christo, dicens: *Iesus autem, plenus Spiritu sancto, regressus est a Iordane,* non quia ibi primo impletus fuerit, sed quia tunc primo plenitudo eius apparere coepit, in alios redundando, secundum illud Ioannis primo[^p89-4]: « Et vidimus gloriam eius »; et post: « De plenitudine eius accepimus omnes ». *Plenus,* inquam, non solum sufficienter, sed etiam superabundanter; unde ad Colossenses primo: « In ipso complacuit omnem plenitudinem inhabitare »; et secundo: « In quo omnis plenitudo Divinitatis inhabitat corporaliter ».

3. Deinde adiungit *loci solitudinem,* quia in deserto; propter quod addit: *Et agebatur a Spiritu in desertum* — ut scilicet experiretur[^p89-5], utrum esset Deus vel Dei Filius, quia forsitan audierat illud: « Hic est Filius meus dilectus » — *a Spiritu sancto agi* est benignitatis; unde ad Romanos octavo[^p89-6]: « Quicumque Spiritu Dei aguntur, filii sunt Dei ». Hinc dicit Gregorius: « Convenienter accipitur, ut a Spiritu sancto ductus credatur, ut illuc eum suus Spiritus duceret, ubi ad tentandum malignus spiritus inveniret ». Unde *in desertum* adduxit, ubi consuevit Deus animae familiariter colloqui, secundum illud Osee secundo[^p89-7]: « Ducam eam in solitudinem et ibi loquar ad cor eius ». Et ideo in Psalmo anima sancta: « Elongavi fugiens et mansi in solitudine »; sic et David, primi Regum vigesimo tertio, « morabatur in deserto mansitque in monte solitudinis ».

4. (Vers. 2.). Et determinatur tempus, scilicet *quadraginta diebus,* ut sic veritas respondeat figurae; sic Moyses, Exodi vigesimo quarto[^p89-8]; sic Elias, tertii Regum decimo nono, ut sic Christi ieiunium tam a Lege quam a Prophetis habeat testimonium. — Et ne credas, ibi Christum ad requiem declinasse, additur: *Et tentabatur a diabolo,* non interius, sed solum exterius. Sicut enim dicit Gregorius[^p89-9]:
<!-- page 90 -->
« Mentem eius peccati delectatio non momordit »; ad Hebraeos quarto: « Non habemus Pontificem, qui non possit compati infirmitatibus nostris, tentatum per omnia pro similitudine, absque peccato ».

5. Et nota *spiritualiter,* quod hic dicitur Christus *agi* a *Spiritu,* in Matthaei quarto[^p90-1] dicitur *duci,* in Marci primo dicitur *expelli.* In quo significatur, quod ad desertum poenitentiae venitur tripliciter, scilicet *timore severitatis,* cuius est *expellere* a malo ad bonum; Ecclesiastici primo[^p90-2]: « Timor Domini expellit peccatum » etc. *Splendore veritatis,* cuius est *ducere;* Proverbiorum quarto: « Ducam te per semitas aequitatis » etc., dicit Sapientia. *Amore bonitatis,* cuius est *agere;* ad Romanos octavo: « Quicumque Spiritu Dei aguntur » etc. Sive per *comminationes, admonitiones* et *promissiones,* quae tria respondent in nobis tribus viribus animae, scilicet irascibili, rationali, concupiscibili.

6. Tertio introducit *mirabilem abstinentiam,* cum adiungit: *Et nihil manducavit in illis diebus,* scilicet quadraginta. Ex quo diabolus est suspicatus eum Deum, quia ieiunabat supra potestatem humanam, quae ultra triduum communiter non procedit; unde Matthaei decimo quinto[^p90-3] dicitur de turbis per triduum ieiunantibus: « Dimittere eos ieiunos nolo, ne deficiant in via ». — Et nota hic, quod Salvator *ieiunavit* ante tentationem et *comedit* post resurrectionem ad nostram instructionem. Unde Augustinus[^p90-4]: « Ieiunavit Christus ante mortem carnis, quasi clamans: *Abstinete vos a carnalibus desideriis,* dum estis in agone. *Comedit* post resurrectionem, quasi dicat: dum habetis corpus mortale, spe immortalitatis iam pascamini, quia, dum viam Domini carpimus, et a vanitate saeculi ieiunare et refici debemus futuri promissione », ut semper dicamus[^p90-5]: « Satiabor, cum apparuerit gloria tua ».

7. Postremo addit *apparentem indigentiam,* cum subiungit: *Et consummatis illis, esuriit.* Noluit enim amplius abstinere, ne diabolus posset percipere. Unde Ambrosius[^p90-6]: « Esurit, ne innotescat sublimis Deus; fames enim Domini pia fraus est, ne caveat tentare diabolus ». — Et nota, quod *esurivit* in tentatione, sicut hic dicitur, et *sitivit* in passione, sicut dicitur Ioannis decimo nono[^p90-7], ut ostenderet, se habere veram humanitatem, et ut invitaret ad paupertatem et poenitentiae poenalitatem; unde Apostolus secundae ad Corinthios undecimo: « In fame et siti, in frigore et nuditate, in ieiuniis multis ». Esurivit autem et sitivit non sibi, sed nobis, quibus promeruit refectionem spiritualem corporis et sanguinis sui, quibus reficimur et potamur; Ioannis sexto[^p90-8]: « Caro mea vere est cibus, et sanguis meus vere est potus ».

8. *Dixit autem illi diabolus* etc. Postquam descripsit occasionem tentationis, hic describit *modum tentandi,* et hoc secundum triplex motivum. Motivum autem ad peccatum est commutabile bonum[^p90-9], quod est in triplici differentia: aut est *inferius,* et hoc est carnis; aut *exterius,* et hoc mundi; aut *interius,* et hoc excellentia animi. Ex primo efficitur homo *voluptuosus,* ex secundo *curiosus,* ex tertio *praesumtuosus.* Primo modo infert tentator suggestionem per bonum inferius, quod est carnis, ut alliciat ad *gulam;* secundo, per bonum exterius, quod est mundi, ut alliciat ad *avaritiam,* et hoc ibi[^p90-10]: *Et duxit eum diabolus in montem;* tertio vero, per bonum interius, quod est excellentia animi, ut alliciat ad pompam sive *superbiam,* ibi: *Et duxit illum in Ierusalem* etc. Et haec tria respondent tribus, in quibus tentavit et vicit hominem primum[^p90-11]; et habent istae tentationes ordinatum processum ab inferiori ad superius: quia ex prima impetitur quantum ad actum *vegetabilis;* ex secunda quantum ad actum *sensibilis;* ex tertia vero, quantum ad actum *rationalis.* — Quantum ergo ad primam tentationem nota *diabolum suggerentem,* et deinde *Dominum respondentem.*
<!-- page 91 -->

9. (Vers. 3.). *Suggerentem diabolum* introducit, cum ait: *Dixit autem ei diabolus: Si Filius Dei es, dic, ut lapides isti panes fiant*[^p91-1]; quod dicit, ut ipse de eo sumat experientiam et ut incitet ad gulam. Quia enim homo es esuriens, dic, ut reficiaris; non quia de refectione Christi aliquid pertineat ad eum; Proverbiorum vigesimo tertio: « Comede et bibe, dicet tibi, et mens eius non est tecum »; sed *si Filius Dei es,* hoc ipsum dicas, ut, si fiat, omnipotens cognoscaris; Ecclesiastae octavo: « Sermo illius potestate plenus est, et omne, quod voluerit, faciet ». — Unde nota dolum diaboli in tentando, quia, cum videret Christi infirmitatem et dubitaret de Divinitate, voluit Divinitatem cognoscendo cavere et humanitatem seducendo deiicere. In quo simul erat fraudulentia et superbia, secundum illud Iob quadragesimo primo[^p91-2]: « Omne sublime videt; et ipse est rex super omnes filios superbiae ».

10. (Vers. 4.). Huic autem tentationi malitiosae et superbae obviat *responsio Dei* humilis et discreta, quia celat Divinitatem et vitat carnalitatem; unde subditur: *Et respondit ad illum Iesus: Scriptum est, quia non in solo pane vivit homo;* Sapientiae decimo sexto[^p91-3]: « Non nativitatis fructus pascunt homines, sed sermo tuus hos qui in te crediderint, conservat ». *Sed in omni verbo, quod procedit ex ore Dei*[^p91-4]. Hoc scriptum est Deuteronomii octavo: « Afflixit te penuria et dedit tibi cibum manna, quod ignorabas, ut ostenderet tibi, quod non in solo pane vivit homo, sed in omni verbo, quod egredietur ex ore Dei »; unde Ioannis sexto: « Verba, quae ego loquor vobis, spiritus et vita sunt ». — Et nota, quod Christus se defendit per Scripturam, in quo revelat[^p91-5] suam sapientiam et occultat potentiam et conservat iustitiam; et sic diaboli suggestio omnino annullata est, quia nec de Deo sumsit experientiam nec hominem pertraxit ad gulam. Unde verificatum est illud Iob vigesimo sexto[^p91-6]: « Prudentia eius percussit superbum », videlicet diabolum. Et ex hoc apparet nobilitas Scripturae, qua Christus in tentatione velut clypeo se defendit, secundum illud Proverbiorum trigesimo: « Omnis sermo Domini ignitus clypeus est omnibus sperantibus in se »; unde et ad Ephesios sexto: « In omnibus sumentes scutum fidei, in quo possitis omnia tela nequissimi ignea exstinguere, et galeam salutis assumite et gladium spiritus, quod est verbum Dei ».

11. (Vers. 5.). *Et duxit illum diabolus.* Hic describitur secunda tentatio, circa quam duo sunt attendenda. Primum est *diabolica suggestio,* et secundum est *dominica responsio,* quae notatur ibi[^p91-7]: *Et respondens Iesus dixit illi* etc. — Circa suggestionem diaboli nota *ductionem, ostensionem, promissionem, suasionem.*

*Ductio* autem fit quasi impotenti, cum tamen dicatur Sapientiae duodecimo[^p91-8]: « Subest tibi, cum volueris, posse ». Unde dicit: *Et duxit illum diabolus in montem excelsum; duxit,* inquam, tanquam infirmum, non tamen involuntarium, sicut Petrum, de quo Ioannis ultimo: « Cum senueris, alius te cinget et ducet, quo tu non vis »; sed tanquam humiliter permittentem ad modum eius, de quo dicit Isaias quinquagesimo tertio: « Tanquam ovis ad occisionem ducetur, et tanquam agnus coram tondente se obmutescet » etc. Si ergo hoc sustinuit a membris diaboli, quid mirum, si patienter sustinuit a capite malorum[^p91-9]? *Et duxit in montem,* quia, cum sit superbus, semper alta petit; Isaiae decimo quarto[^p91-10]: « Super astra ascendam, sedebo in monte testamenti »; et quia in loco eminentiae de facili ruit homo; unde tertii Regum vigesimo secundo: « Vidi cunctum Israel dispersum in montibus ».

12. *Ostensio* fit quasi ignoranti, cum tamen « in ipso sint omnes thesauri sapientiae et scientiae », ad Colossenses secundo[^p91-11]. Unde addit: *Et ostendit ei omnia regna orbis terrae in momento temporis.* — Et nota, quod *ostendisse* dicitur *omnia regna mundi,* non singulariter, sed generaliter, ut dicit quidam[^p91-12], id est omnes partes. Vel, secundum Bedam, « ostendit omnia concupiscibilia mundi, in
<!-- page 92 -->
quibus homines regnant », unde signanter dicitur Matthaei quarto: « Ostendit illi omnia regna mundi et gloriam eorum ». « *Ostendit* autem, non quod visum ei ampliaret, sed, sicut dicit Beda[^p92-1], vanitatem humanae pompae, quam amabat, quasi speciosam ostendens, facere volebat amari ». Et quia haec non durat, sed cito transit, ideo in brevi tempore ostendit; propter quod adiungit: *In momento temporis.* Unde Beda[^p92-2]: « Non tam celeritas aspectus notatur quam fragilitas caducae potestatis. In momento enim cuncta praetereunt », secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo: « Ecce, gentes quasi stilla situlae et quasi momentum staterae reputatae sunt »; quia, Sapientiae undecimo, « quasi momentum staterae, sic est ante te orbis terrarum »; « quoniam in momento, quae erat praeclarior natio, exterminata est », sicut dicitur Sapientiae decimo octavo, et in Psalmo: « Vidi impium superexaltatum etc. Et transivi, et ecce non erat ».

13. (Vers. 6.). *Promissio* vero fit ei quasi indigenti, cum tamen[^p92-3] eius sit « orbis terrarum et plenitudo eius ». Propter quod additur: *Et ait illi: Tibi dabo potestatem hanc universam et gloriam illorum* etc., ut scilicet habeas *dominium potestatis* et *praeconium laudis,* quasi aliquid magni promitteret, cum tamen potestas et gloria mundi quasi nulla sit, secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo[^p92-4]: « Omnis caro foenum, et omnis gloria eius quasi flos agri »; et primi Machabaeorum secundo: « Gloria viri peccatoris stercus et vermis est »; et illud Osee quarto: « Gloriam eorum in ignominiam commutabo ».

14. Et ut promissioni ostendat adhibendam esse fidem, adiungit: *Quia mihi tradita sunt, et cui volo, do ea*[^p92-5]; Iob nono: « Terra tradita est in manibus impii », id est terrena potestas. Hoc autem intelligitur *permissive,* non simpliciter, sed ad tempus, sicut dicitur Danielis undecimo[^p92-6]: « Faciet secundum voluntatem suam rex et magnificabitur adversus omnem deum »; « et multiplicabit gloriam et potestatem dabit in multis et dividet terram gratuito »; et Apocalypsis decimo tertio dicitur, quod « draco dedit potestatem bestiae ». Hoc autem permittit Deus iusto suo iudicio. « Ipse igitur transfert regna atque constituit », sicut dicitur Danielis secundo. — Vel, sicut dicit Beda[^p92-7], hoc dicit *ex arrogantia,* non quod totus mundus fuisset eius, cum solus Filius dicat vere: « Omnia mihi tradita sunt a Patre », Matthaei undecimo; et rursus Matthaei ultimo: « Data est mihi omnis potestas in caelo et in terra » etc.; sed quia ipsi omnis *inordinatio* potestatis adscribitur, ipse, ut superbus, adscribit sibi totum. Unde omnis potestatis *ordinatio* a Deo est, sicut dicitur ad Romanos decimo tertio[^p92-8]; sed *inordinatio* a diabolo, quia primae Ioannis tertio dicitur: « Qui peccat ex diabolo est ».

15. (Vers. 7.). Quarto vero fit *suasio* quasi appetenti, cum tamen ipse « det omnibus affluenter », Iacobi primo[^p92-9]. Unde dicit: *Tu ergo si procidens adoraveris coram me* etc. In qua suasione nititur inclinare ad *veram adorationem* ob *falsam promissionem;* unde illud Isaiae quinquagesimo primo: « Incurvare, ut transeamus ». Non enim potest super hominem ascendere, nisi homo ei procidat voluntarie. Honorem autem adorationis requirebat contra illud Exodi trigesimo quarto[^p92-10]: « Noli adorare deum alienum ». — Et ut pertrahat ad illicitum obsequium, promittit falsum praemium, cum addit: *Erunt tua omnia*[^p92-11]. Falsam spondet potentiam, ut ad veram pertrahat servitutem, quia, Ioannis octavo, « qui facit peccatum servus est peccati »; et qui servus est dominium sui non habet, ac per hoc non ipsi omnia, sed ipse omnibus est subiectus tanquam servus; et secundae Petri secundo: « A quo quis superatus est, huius servus est ».

16. (Vers. 8.). *Et respondens Iesus dixit illi.* Hic post suggestionem diabolicam subiungit *respon-*
<!-- page 93 -->
*sionem dominicam,* quae per testimonium sacrae Scripturae elidit diabolicam suasionem, cum ait: *Scriptum est: Dominum Deum tuum adorabis.* Hoc, inquam, scriptum est Deuteronomii sexto[^p93-1]. Et merito Scripturae huius recordatur Salvator, quia, secundum illud secundae ad Timotheum tertio, « omnis Scriptura, divinitus inspirata, utilis est ad docendum, arguendum et corripiendum », et illa potissime, in qua attenditur divinus cultus. Unde et Dominus dicitur scripsisse Legem digito proprio, Exodi trigesimo secundo[^p93-2], in qua praeceptum adorationis est primum et praecipuum et maxime in mente habendum. Unde Baruch sexto: « Visa turba adorantium ante et retro, dicite in cordibus vestris: Te oportet adorari, Domine », te scilicet et non alium.

17. Et ideo additur: *Et illi soli servies,* scilicet servitute *latriae;* primi Regum septimo[^p93-3]: « Praeparate corda vestra Domino et servite illi soli ». *Solus* non excludit ibi omnem personam respectu cuiuscumque servitii, cum dicatur ad Galatas quinto: « Per caritatem spiritus servite invicem »; sed excludit quantum ad servitutem *latriae,* quae est « cultus soli Deo debitus ». — Vel excludit servitia aliis impensa non *propter* Deum, sed *contra* Deum, quasi dicat: illi soli et non *idolis;* de quo primae ad Corinthios quinto[^p93-4]: « Si quis nominatur inter vos fornicator, aut avarus, aut idolis serviens, cum huiusmodi nec cibum sumere ». Sic servitur diabolo. — Non etiam *peccato,* sed iustitiae; ad Romanos sexto[^p93-5]: « Sicut exhibuistis membra vestra servire immunditiae et iniquitati ad iniquitatem, ita nunc exhibete membra vestra » etc. — Non *ventri* proprio; ad Romanos decimo sexto: « Huiusmodi enim Christo Domino non serviunt, sed ventri suo ». — Non *mundo,* quia, infra decimo sexto[^p93-6], « non potestis Deo servire et mammonae ». — Sed *soli Deo,* ut possitis dicere cum David: « Quia ego servus tuus sum ».

18. (Vers. 9.). *Et duxit illum in Ierusalem* etc. Hic describitur tertia tentatio, circa quam duo sunt attendenda ut prius. Primum est *suggestio diaboli;* secundum est *responsio Christi,* ibi[^p93-7]: *Et respondens Iesus, dixit.* — Circa *persuasionem diaboli* tria attende. Nam primo *eligit congruum locum persuasionis;* secundo *exprimit suasionem praecipitationis;* tertio per auctoritatem Psalmi excutit *timorem laesionis.*

Quantum ergo ad *loci congrui electionem* dicitur: *Duxit illum in Ierusalem,* tanquam ad locum celebrem et communem. *Ductus* autem fuit ex permissione, non ex imbecillitate; unde Chrysostomus[^p93-8] dicit, quod « in Christo non fuit infirmitas, sed patientia; in diabolo vero non virtus, sed superbia ». Et dicit Glossa super Matthaei quarto, quod « haec visio corporeis sensibus est completa »; Chrysostomus[^p93-9] autem dicit, quod « diabolus sic eum assumebat, ut ab omnibus videretur; Christus autem, diabolo nesciente, sic agebat invisibiliter, ut a nemine videretur ». Unde, ut melius videri possit, subditur: *Et statuit eum super pinnaculum*[^p93-10] *templi.* Ille erat locus altus et celebris, ubi doctores et sacerdotes consueverunt loqui ad plebem. Et secundum aliquos expositores erat summitas tecti templi, quod dicitur *tabulatum,* tertii Regum sexto[^p93-11]. Vel secundum alios erat quoddam deambulatorium supra portam templi, ubi ascendebatur per gradus. Secundum alios vero dicitur ibi fuisse tectum cum quadam appodiatione muri, quia in Palaestina domus habent tecta plana,
<!-- page 94 -->
et templum habuit planum tectum, ut tabernaculum, et in talibus consueverunt stare doctores et praedicatores, secundum illud Matthaei decimo[^p94-1]: « Quod in aure auditis, praedicate super tecta ».

19. Quantum ad *suasionem praecipitationis* subditur: *Et dixit illi: Si Filius Dei es, mitte te*[^p94-2] *deorsum.* Quia diabolus deorsum ceciderat, libenter omnes deorsum traheret; infra decimo: « Videbam satanam sicut fulgur de caelo cadentem ». Sed Christus poterat dicere diabolo et angelis eius illud quod dixit Iudaeis Ioannis octavo: « Vos de deorsum estis, ego autem de supernis sum »; unde non erat eius *descendere,* sed *ascendere.* Sed diabolus hoc suggerebat, ut, descendendo corporaliter, faceret eum superbire spiritualiter, ut simul esset *ascensus vanus* et *descensus verus.* In quo erudimur, quod semper coniuncta sunt humiliatio et exaltatio, secundum illud infra decimo octavo[^p94-3]: « Omnis, qui se exaltat, humiliabitur, et qui se humiliat exaltabitur »; et Proverbiorum decimo octavo: « Antequam conteratur, exaltatur cor hominis, et antequam glorificetur, humiliatur ». — Et nota *suggerendi modum,* cum dicit: *Mitte te.* Chrysostomus[^p94-4]: « Non impulit, non tetigit, non appropinquavit, sed tantum dixit: *Mitte te deorsum,* ut sciamus, quia omnis, qui obaudit diabolo, ipse se deponit ». Et Hieronymus: « Satis est debilis, quia nulli nocere potest, nisi ipse se deorsum posuerit ». Et ideo sanum est consilium, Ecclesiastae decimo: « Si spiritus potestatem habentis ascenderit super te, locum tuum ne dimiseris », nec praesumendo nec desperando.

20. (Vers. 10.). Quantum ad *excussionem timoris* adiungit: *Scriptum est enim, quia*[^p94-5] *Angelis suis mandavit de te;* hoc scriptum est in Psalmo. Ideo Scripturam allegat, quia videbat, quod Christus semper per Scripturam se defendebat. Unde dicit Leo Papa: « Ibi occasiones tentandi invenit, ubi viderit homines vehementius occupari ». Unde *scriptum esse* asserit, ut inducat credulitatem, et etiam Scripturam inducit, quae excludat pusillanimitatem propter promissionem adiutorii angelici, tam in *prosperis* quam in *adversis.* — Propter *adiutorium in prosperis* dicit: *Ut custodiant te in omnibus viis tuis*[^p94-6], id est operibus; Exodi vigesimo tertio: « Ecce, ego mitto Angelum meum, qui praecedat te et custodiat te in via ». Sic custodivit Angelus Tobiam, sicut dicitur Tobiae duodecimo; sic etiam Iudith, sicut dicitur decimo tertio; sic etiam custodiunt omnes viros iustos, secundum illud ad Hebraeos primo: « Omnes sunt administratorii spiritus, in ministerium missi » etc.; et totam Ecclesiam, secundum illud Isaiae sexagesimo secundo: « Super muros tuos, Ierusalem, constitui custodes ». Christus vero non indigebat custodia[^p94-7]; et ideo diabolus auctoritatem istam depravabat et sinistro modo adducebat.

21. (Vers. 11.). Propter *adiutorium* vero *in adversis* adiungit: *Et quia in manibus tollent te,* quasi portitores, secundum quod legitur Angelus portasse Habacuc, Danielis ultimo[^p94-8]. *Ne forte offendas ad lapidem pedem tuum,* id est, ut nulla possis laesione offendi, sicut dicitur Iob quinto: « Cum lapidibus regionum erit pactum tuum, et bestiae terrae pacificae erunt tibi ». — Et nota, quod diabolus tanquam versutus non addidit quod sequitur[^p94-9]: « Super aspidem et basiliscum ambulabis et conculcabis leonem et draconem », quia hoc erat contra eum. In quo ostenditur, quod vitium diabolicum est auctoritates truncare et ad partem suam trahere; unde Apocalypsis ultimo: « Si quis diminuerit de verbis prophetiae libri huius, auferet Deus partem eius de libro vitae ».

22. (Vers. 12.). *Et respondens Iesus* etc. Hic post pestiferam suasionem addit *dominicam responsionem* brevem et efficacem, cum dicit: *Respondens*
<!-- page 95 -->
*Iesus ait illi: Dictum est,* scilicet Deuteronomii sexto[^p95-1]: *Non tentabis Dominum Deum tuum;* et Ecclesiastici decimo octavo: « Noli esse quasi homo, qui tentat Deum ». Unde vituperantur filii Israel, Numerorum decimo quarto, quibus dicit Dominus: « Tentaverunt me iam per decem vices ». *Tentare* autem *Deum,* sicut dicit Beda[^p95-2], est, cum homo habet, quid faciat, divinae potentiae quaerere experimentum. Et quia non indigebat Christus hoc experimento potentiae, Deum nolebat tentare nec diabolo consentire, et per auctoritatem respondit auctoritati oppositae, ut clavus retunderetur virtute clavi[^p95-3].

23. (Vers. 13.). *Et consummata omni tentatione.* Postquam descripsit tentationis occasionem et tentandi modum, hic describit tertio *tentationis consummationem,* circa quam tria insinuantur. Primum est *manifestatio perfectionis* ex parte Christi resistentis; quantum ad quod dicit: *Et consummata omni tentatione,* quia, sicut prius[^p95-4] dictum fuit, omnis tentatio reducitur ad tres praedictas; unde primae Ioannis secundo: « Omne, quod est in mundo, concupiscentia carnis est » etc. Hanc *consummavit,* quia patienter tolerando superavit, secundum illud ad Hebraeos secundo[^p95-5]: « Decebat eum qui multos filios in gloriam adduxerat, per passionem consummare », quia dicitur Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo: « Dei perfecta sunt opera ».

24. Secundum est *confutatio praesumtionis* ex parte diaboli recedentis; quantum ad quod dicit: *Diabolus recessit ab illo;* secundum illud Iacobi quarto[^p95-6]: « Resistite diabolo, et fugiet a vobis ». *Recessit,* scilicet confusus; quod designatum est in rege Assyriorum, quarti Regum decimo nono: « Recedens abiit rex Assyriorum Sennacherib »; quod interpretatur *tollens deserta,* quia diabolus maxime persequitur eos qui vivunt in austeritate vitae.

25. Tertium vero est *praefinitio temporis* a parte Dei disponentis; propter quod addit: *Usque ad tempus,* « passionis[^p95-7] », scilicet recessit, quia ille malignus non permittitur malefacere nisi pro tempore, quo Deus permittit, licet semper malam habeat voluntatem; Ecclesiastici decimo nono[^p95-8]: « Si invenerit tempus malefaciendi, malefaciet ». Et ideo consilium est Ecclesiastici quarto: « Fili mi, conserva tempus et declina a malo »; et ad Ephesios quinto: « Videte, quomodo caute ambuletis, redimentes tempus quoniam dies mali sunt ». Unde cum diabolus dupliciter tentet: ut *draco* et *leo*[^p95-9], primo tentavit ut *draco* ad seducendum, postmodum ut *leo* ad frangendum in passione. In primo enim tentat emollire per blanditias, sed post videns, se modicum tempus habere, tentat frangere per poenas; unde Apocalypsis duodecimo[^p95-10]: « Descendit diabolus, habens iram magnam, sciens, quod modicum tempus habeat ». Et ideo ad consummationem temporis non pervenerunt illi, de quibus infra octavo dicitur, quod « ad tempus credunt et in tempore tentationis recedunt ».

### Chapter IV.

#### Part II. The teaching office of preaching is treated in two ways.

1. *And Jesus being full of the Holy Spirit* etc. Above[^p88-10] the Evangelist determined concerning the mystery of the incarnation, or concerning the nativity of Christ; in this part he treats of the *teaching office of preaching,* or doctrine. And since the work of the wise man is twofold, namely in teaching, « not to lie about the things he knows, and to expose the one who lies[^p88-11] », therefore this part has two. In the first he *sets forth the truth,* in the second he *confutes falsity,* below, in the eleventh chapter: *Jesus was casting out a devil* etc.

#### *First the truth of the doctrine is set forth in four ways.*

The first part, in which he sets forth the truth of the doctrine, has four: in the first of which there is made the *authentication of the doctrine of Christ;* in the second is determined the *calling of the disciples,* below, in the fifth chapter[^p88-12]: *And it came to pass, that when the*
<!-- page 89 -->
*multitudes* etc.; in the third, the *instituting of those called,* below, in the sixth chapter: *And it came to pass in those days* etc.; in the fourth, the *delegation of those instituted,* below, in the ninth chapter: *Then calling together the twelve Apostles, he gave them* etc. The order and sufficiency of the parts is clear of itself.

#### First the doctrine of Christ is authenticated through three things.

The first part therefore, in which there is made the *authentication of the doctrine of Christ,* contains the present chapter and has three parts. Since doctrine is authenticated in three ways: either from the *merit of life,* or from the *testimony of Scripture,* or from the *miracle of power;* therefore in the first part is shown the authorization of the doctrine of Christ from the *merit of life;* in the second, from the *testimony of Scripture,* there[^p89-1]: *Jesus returned in the power of the Spirit;* in the third, from the *miracle of power,* there: *And he went down to Capharnaum* etc., where the miracles are determined.

#### The doctrine of Christ is authenticated from the merit of life.

Now *merit* consists in the *victory over temptations,* because[^p89-2] « the life of man upon earth is a warfare »; therefore the Evangelist describes how he overcame the diabolical temptation. For the complete description of which three things are fitting, namely the *occasion of the temptation;* the *mode of tempting,* which he touches there[^p89-3]: *And the devil said to him;* and the third, the *consummation of the temptation,* which he expresses there: *And all the temptation being ended* etc.

Now the devil took the *motive* of tempting from four things considered in Christ: the first is the *fulness of grace,* the second is the *solitude of the place,* the third is the *suspicion of Divinity* on account of his marvellous abstinence, the fourth the *observation of infirmity* on account of his apparent want. The consideration of the *fulness of grace* through envy moved the devil's will to tempt; the consideration of the *solitude* furnished the malignity of tempting; the suspicion of *Divinity* incited the curiosity of tempting; the observation of *necessity* offered the opportunity of tempting.

2. (Vers. 1.). He introduces therefore the *fulness of grace* in Christ, saying: *And Jesus, being full of the Holy Spirit, returned from the Jordan,* not because he was there filled for the first time, but because then for the first time his fulness began to appear, by overflowing into others, according to that saying of John, the first chapter[^p89-4]: « And we saw his glory »; and after: « Of his fulness we all have received ». *Full,* I say, not only sufficiently, but also superabundantly; whence to the Colossians, the first chapter: « In him it hath well pleased that all fulness should dwell »; and the second chapter: « In whom dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead corporeally ».

3. Then he adds the *solitude of the place,* because it was in the desert; on account of which he adds: *And he was moved by the Spirit into the desert* — that is, so that he might find out[^p89-5] whether he was God or the Son of God, because perhaps he had heard that saying: « This is my beloved Son » — *to be moved by the Holy Spirit* is of kindness; whence to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p89-6]: « Whosoever are moved by the Spirit of God, they are the sons of God ». Hence Gregory says: « It is fittingly understood that he is believed to have been led by the Holy Spirit, so that his own Spirit should lead him thither where the malignant spirit should find him to tempt him ». Whence he led him *into the desert,* where God is wont to speak familiarly with the soul, according to that saying of Osee, the second chapter[^p89-7]: « I will lead her into the solitude, and I will speak to her heart ». And therefore in the Psalm the holy soul says: « Lo, I have gone far off flying away, and I abode in the solitude »; so also David, the first of Kings, the twenty-third chapter, « abode in the desert and remained in the mountain of solitude ».

4. (Vers. 2.). And the time is determined, namely *forty days,* so that thus the truth may answer to the figure; so Moses, Exodus, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p89-8]; so Elias, the third of Kings, the nineteenth chapter, so that thus the fast of Christ may have testimony both from the Law and from the Prophets. — And lest you believe that Christ turned aside there to rest, it is added: *And he was tempted by the devil,* not interiorly, but only exteriorly. For as Gregory says[^p89-9]:
<!-- page 90 -->
« The delight of sin did not bite his mind »; to the Hebrews, the fourth chapter: « We have not a High Priest who cannot have compassion on our infirmities, tempted in all things by likeness, without sin ».

5. And note *spiritually* that here Christ is said *to be moved* by *the Spirit,* in Matthew, the fourth chapter[^p90-1], he is said *to be led,* in Mark, the first chapter, he is said *to be driven out.* In which it is signified that one comes to the desert of penance in three ways, namely by the *fear of severity,* whose it is *to drive out* from evil to good; Ecclesiasticus, the first chapter[^p90-2]: « The fear of the Lord driveth out sin » etc. By the *splendour of truth,* whose it is *to lead;* Proverbs, the fourth chapter: « I will lead thee by the paths of equity » etc., says Wisdom. By the *love of goodness,* whose it is *to move;* to the Romans, the eighth chapter: « Whosoever are moved by the Spirit of God » etc. Or through *threatenings, admonitions* and *promises,* which three answer in us to the three powers of the soul, namely the irascible, the rational, the concupiscible.

6. Third he introduces the *marvellous abstinence,* when he adds: *And he ate nothing in those days,* namely the forty. From which the devil suspected him to be God, because he fasted above human power, which commonly does not go beyond three days; whence in Matthew, the fifteenth chapter[^p90-3], it is said of the multitudes fasting for three days: « I will not send them away fasting, lest they faint in the way ». — And note here that the Saviour *fasted* before the temptation and *ate* after the resurrection for our instruction. Whence Augustine[^p90-4]: « Christ fasted before the death of the flesh, as if crying out: *Abstain from carnal desires,* while you are in the contest. He *ate* after the resurrection, as if he should say: while you have a mortal body, be fed now by the hope of immortality, because, while we take the way of the Lord, we ought both to fast from the vanity of the world and to be refreshed by the promise of the future », so that we may always say[^p90-5]: « I shall be satisfied when thy glory shall appear ».

7. Lastly he adds the *apparent want,* when he subjoins: *And when they were ended, he was hungry.* For he did not will to abstain longer, lest the devil should be able to perceive it. Whence Ambrose[^p90-6]: « He hungers, lest the sublime God become known; for the hunger of the Lord is a pious fraud, lest the devil beware of tempting ». — And note that he *hungered* in the temptation, as is said here, and *thirsted* in the passion, as is said in John, the nineteenth chapter[^p90-7], so that he might show that he had a true humanity, and that he might invite to poverty and to the penalty of penance; whence the Apostle in the second to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter: « In hunger and thirst, in cold and nakedness, in many fastings ». But he hungered and thirsted not for himself, but for us, for whom he merited the spiritual refection of his body and blood, by which we are refreshed and given drink; John, the sixth chapter[^p90-8]: « My flesh is meat indeed, and my blood is drink indeed ».

8. *And the devil said to him* etc. After he has described the occasion of the temptation, here he describes the *mode of tempting,* and this according to a threefold motive. Now the motive to sin is the changeable good[^p90-9], which is in a threefold difference: either it is *lower,* and this is of the flesh; or *exterior,* and this of the world; or *interior,* and this the excellence of the mind. From the first a man is made *voluptuous,* from the second *curious,* from the third *presumptuous.* In the first mode the tempter brings in a suggestion through the lower good, which is of the flesh, to entice to *gluttony;* in the second, through the exterior good, which is of the world, to entice to *avarice,* and this there[^p90-10]: *And the devil led him into a mountain;* but in the third, through the interior good, which is the excellence of the mind, to entice to pomp or *pride,* there: *And he brought him to Jerusalem* etc. And these three answer to the three in which he tempted and overcame the first man[^p90-11]; and these temptations have an ordered progress from the lower to the higher: because by the first he is assailed as to the act of the *vegetative;* by the second as to the act of the *sensitive;* but by the third, as to the act of the *rational.* — As to the first temptation, then, note the *devil suggesting,* and then the *Lord responding.*
<!-- page 91 -->

9. (Vers. 3.). He introduces the *devil suggesting,* when he says: *And the devil said to him: If thou be the Son of God, say that these stones be made bread*[^p91-1]; which he says so that he himself may take experience of him and that he may incite him to gluttony. For since you are a man hungering, speak, that you may be refreshed; not because anything of the refreshment of Christ pertains to him; Proverbs, the twenty-third chapter: « Eat and drink, will he say to thee, and his mind is not with thee »; but *if thou be the Son of God,* say this very thing, so that, if it be done, you may be known as omnipotent; Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter: « His word is full of power, and whatsoever he will, he will do ». — Whence note the guile of the devil in tempting, because, since he saw the infirmity of Christ and was in doubt about the Divinity, he willed by knowing the Divinity to beware of it and by seducing the humanity to cast it down. In which there was at once fraudulence and pride, according to that saying of Job, the forty-first chapter[^p91-2]: « He beholdeth every high thing; and he is king over all the children of pride ».

10. (Vers. 4.). But this malicious and proud temptation is met by the *response of God,* humble and discreet, because it conceals the Divinity and avoids carnality; whence it is subjoined: *And Jesus answered him: It is written that man liveth not by bread alone;* Wisdom, the sixteenth chapter[^p91-3]: « Not the growing of fruits feedeth men, but thy word preserveth them that have believed in thee ». *But by every word that proceedeth from the mouth of God*[^p91-4]. This is written in Deuteronomy, the eighth chapter: « He afflicted thee with want, and gave thee manna for thy food, which thou knewest not, to shew thee that not in bread alone doth man live, but in every word that shall proceed from the mouth of God »; whence John, the sixth chapter: « The words that I speak to you are spirit and life ». — And note that Christ defends himself through Scripture, in which he reveals[^p91-5] his wisdom and hides his power and preserves justice; and so the suggestion of the devil was altogether annulled, because he neither took experience of God nor drew the man to gluttony. Whence that saying of Job, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p91-6], was verified: « His prudence hath struck the proud one », namely the devil. And from this appears the nobility of Scripture, with which Christ in the temptation defends himself as with a shield, according to that saying of Proverbs, the thirtieth chapter: « Every word of the Lord is a fiery shield to all that hope in him »; whence also to the Ephesians, the sixth chapter: « In all things taking the shield of faith, wherewith you may be able to extinguish all the fiery darts of the most wicked one, and take unto you the helmet of salvation and the sword of the spirit, which is the word of God ».

11. (Vers. 5.). *And the devil led him.* Here the second temptation is described, concerning which two things are to be attended to. The first is the *diabolical suggestion,* and the second is the *dominical response,* which is noted there[^p91-7]: *And Jesus answering said to him* etc. — Concerning the suggestion of the devil note the *leading, showing, promising, persuading.*

The *leading* is done as if to one powerless, though nevertheless it is said in Wisdom, the twelfth chapter[^p91-8]: « Thy power is at hand when thou wilt ». Whence he says: *And the devil led him into a high mountain; led,* I say, as one infirm, yet not as one unwilling, like Peter, of whom in John, the last chapter: « When thou shalt be old, another shall gird thee and lead thee whither thou wilt not »; but as one humbly permitting, after the manner of him of whom Isaias says in the fifty-third chapter: « He shall be led as a sheep to the slaughter, and shall be dumb as a lamb before his shearer » etc. If therefore he bore this from the members of the devil, what wonder if he patiently bore it from the head of the wicked[^p91-9]? *And he led him into a mountain,* because, since he is proud, he always seeks high things; Isaias, the fourteenth chapter[^p91-10]: « I will ascend above the stars, I will sit in the mountain of the covenant »; and because in a place of eminence a man easily falls; whence in the third of Kings, the twenty-second chapter: « I saw all Israel scattered upon the mountains ».

12. The *showing* is done as if to one ignorant, though nevertheless « in him are all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge », to the Colossians, the second chapter[^p91-11]. Whence he adds: *And he shewed him all the kingdoms of the world in a moment of time.* — And note that he is said *to have shown all the kingdoms of the world,* not singly, but generally, as someone says[^p91-12], that is, all the parts. Or, according to Bede, « he showed all the concupiscible things of the world, in
<!-- page 92 -->
which men reign », whence it is said expressly in Matthew, the fourth chapter: « He shewed him all the kingdoms of the world, and the glory of them ». « Now *he showed,* not that he might enlarge his sight, but, as Bede says[^p92-1], showing the vanity of human pomp, which he loved, as if it were beautiful, he wished to make it loved ». And because this does not endure, but quickly passes, therefore he showed it in a brief time; on account of which he adds: *In a moment of time.* Whence Bede[^p92-2]: « It is not so much the swiftness of the view that is noted as the fragility of perishable power. For in a moment all things pass away », according to that saying of Isaias, the fortieth chapter: « Behold, the Gentiles are as a drop of a bucket, and are counted as the moment of a balance »; because, Wisdom, the eleventh chapter, « as the moment of a balance, so is the world before thee »; « for in a moment the nation that was more noble was destroyed », as is said in Wisdom, the eighteenth chapter, and in the Psalm: « I have seen the wicked highly exalted etc. And I passed by, and lo, he was not ».

13. (Vers. 6.). But the *promising* is made to him as to one needy, though nevertheless[^p92-3] « the world and the fulness thereof » is his. On account of which it is added: *And he said to him: To thee will I give all this power, and the glory of them* etc., namely that you may have *dominion of power* and the *proclamation of praise,* as if he were promising something great, though nevertheless the power and glory of the world is as nothing, according to that saying of Isaias, the fortieth chapter[^p92-4]: « All flesh is grass, and all the glory thereof as the flower of the field »; and in the first of Machabees, the second chapter: « The glory of a sinful man is dung and worms »; and that saying of Osee, the fourth chapter: « I will change their glory into ignominy ».

14. And to show that faith is to be given to the promise, he adds: *For to me they are delivered, and to whom I will, I give them*[^p92-5]; Job, the ninth chapter: « The earth is given into the hands of the wicked », that is, earthly power. But this is understood *permissively,* not simply, but for a time, as is said in Daniel, the eleventh chapter[^p92-6]: « The king shall do according to his will, and shall be magnified against every god »; « and he shall multiply glory, and shall give power over many, and shall divide the land gratis »; and in the Apocalypse, the thirteenth chapter, it is said that « the dragon gave power to the beast ». But God permits this by his just judgement. « He therefore transfers kingdoms and establishes them », as is said in Daniel, the second chapter. — Or, as Bede says[^p92-7], he says this *out of arrogance,* not that the whole world had been his, since the Son alone truly says: « All things are delivered to me by the Father », Matthew, the eleventh chapter; and again Matthew, the last chapter: « All power is given to me in heaven and in earth » etc.; but because every *disordering* of power is ascribed to him, he, as one proud, ascribes the whole to himself. Whence every *ordering* of power is from God, as is said to the Romans, the thirteenth chapter[^p92-8]; but the *disordering* is from the devil, because in the first of John, the third chapter, it is said: « He that sinneth is of the devil ».

15. (Vers. 7.). But fourth is made the *persuading* as to one desiring, though nevertheless he himself « giveth to all abundantly », James, the first chapter[^p92-9]. Whence he says: *If thou therefore, falling down, wilt adore before me* etc. In which persuasion he strives to incline him to a *true adoration* on account of a *false promise;* whence that saying of Isaias, the fifty-first chapter: « Bow down, that we may go over ». For he cannot ascend over a man unless the man fall down before him voluntarily. Now he was requiring the honour of adoration against that saying of Exodus, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p92-10]: « Adore not a strange god ». — And that he may draw him to an unlawful service, he promises a false reward, when he adds: *All shall be thine*[^p92-11]. He pledges a false power, so as to draw him to a true servitude, because, John, the eighth chapter, « he that committeth sin is the servant of sin »; and he who is a servant has not dominion of himself, and through this all things are not subject to him, but he himself is subject to all things as a servant; and in the second of Peter, the second chapter: « By whom a man is overcome, of the same also he is the servant ».

16. (Vers. 8.). *And Jesus answering said to him.* Here after the diabolical suggestion he subjoins the *re-*
<!-- page 93 -->
*sponse of the Lord,* which through the testimony of sacred Scripture dashes down the diabolical persuasion, when he says: *It is written: Thou shalt adore the Lord thy God.* This, I say, is written in Deuteronomy, the sixth chapter[^p93-1]. And deservedly the Saviour remembers this Scripture, because, according to that saying of the second to Timothy, the third chapter, « all Scripture, inspired of God, is profitable to teach, to reprove and to correct », and that one most of all in which divine worship is attended to. Whence also the Lord is said to have written the Law with his own finger, Exodus, the thirty-second chapter[^p93-2], in which the precept of adoration is the first and chief and most of all to be kept in mind. Whence Baruch, the sixth chapter: « When you see a multitude adoring before and behind, say ye in your hearts: Thou oughtest to be adored, O Lord », thou namely and not another.

17. And therefore it is added: *And him only shalt thou serve,* namely with the service of *latria;* the first of Kings, the seventh chapter[^p93-3]: « Prepare your hearts unto the Lord, and serve him only ». *Only* does not there exclude every person with respect to any service whatever, since it is said to the Galatians, the fifth chapter: « By charity of the spirit serve one another »; but it excludes as to the service of *latria,* which is « the worship owed to God alone ». — Or it excludes services rendered to others not *for the sake of* God, but *against* God, as if he should say: to him only and not to *idols;* of which in the first to the Corinthians, the fifth chapter[^p93-4]: « If any one that is named among you be a fornicator, or covetous, or a server of idols, with such a one not so much as to eat ». So is the devil served. — Not also to *sin,* but to justice; to the Romans, the sixth chapter[^p93-5]: « As you have yielded your members to serve uncleanness and iniquity unto iniquity, so now yield your members » etc. — Not to one's own *belly;* to the Romans, the sixteenth chapter: « For they that are such serve not Christ our Lord, but their own belly ». — Not to the *world,* because, below, in the sixteenth chapter[^p93-6], « you cannot serve God and mammon ». — But to *God alone,* so that you may be able to say with David: « For I am thy servant ».

18. (Vers. 9.). *And he brought him to Jerusalem* etc. Here the third temptation is described, concerning which two things are to be attended to, as before. The first is the *suggestion of the devil;* the second is the *response of Christ,* there[^p93-7]: *And Jesus answering, said.* — Concerning the *persuasion of the devil* attend to three things. For first he *chooses a fitting place of persuasion;* second he *expresses the persuading to cast himself headlong;* third through the authority of the Psalm he shakes off the *fear of hurt.*

As to the *choice of a fitting place,* then, it is said: *He brought him to Jerusalem,* as to a place frequented and common. Now he was *brought* by permission, not by weakness; whence Chrysostom[^p93-8] says that « in Christ there was not infirmity, but patience; but in the devil not strength, but pride ». And the Gloss on Matthew, the fourth chapter, says that « this vision was completed by the bodily senses »; but Chrysostom[^p93-9] says that « the devil so took him up that he might be seen by all; but Christ, the devil not knowing it, so acted invisibly that he was seen by no one ». Whence, that he might be better seen, it is subjoined: *And he set him on the pinnacle*[^p93-10] *of the temple.* That was a high and frequented place, where the doctors and priests were wont to speak to the people. And according to some expositors it was the summit of the roof of the temple, which is called the *tabulatum,* the third of Kings, the sixth chapter[^p93-11]. Or according to others it was a certain walkway above the gate of the temple, where one ascended by steps. But according to others there is said to have been a roof there with a certain support of a wall, because in Palestine the houses have flat roofs,
<!-- page 94 -->
and the temple had a flat roof, like the tabernacle, and on such places the doctors and preachers were wont to stand, according to that saying of Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p94-1]: « That which you hear in the ear, preach ye upon the housetops ».

19. As to the *persuading to cast himself headlong* it is subjoined: *And he said to him: If thou be the Son of God, cast thyself*[^p94-2] *down.* Because the devil had fallen down, he would gladly draw all down; below, in the tenth chapter: « I saw Satan like lightning falling from heaven ». But Christ could say to the devil and his angels that which he said to the Jews in John, the eighth chapter: « You are from beneath, I am from above »; whence it was not his *to descend,* but *to ascend.* But the devil was suggesting this, so that, by descending bodily, he might make him grow proud spiritually, so that there might be at once a *vain ascent* and a *true descent.* In which we are schooled that humiliation and exaltation are always joined, according to that saying below, in the eighteenth chapter[^p94-3]: « Every one that exalteth himself shall be humbled, and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted »; and Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter: « Before destruction the heart of a man is exalted, and before he be glorified, it is humbled ». — And note the *mode of suggesting,* when he says: *Cast thyself.* Chrysostom[^p94-4]: « He did not push him, he did not touch him, he did not draw near, but only said: *Cast thyself down,* that we may know that every one who obeys the devil casts himself down ». And Jerome: « He is weak enough, since he can harm no one unless that one has put himself down ». And therefore sound is the counsel of Ecclesiastes, the tenth chapter: « If the spirit of him that hath power ascend upon thee, leave not thy place », neither by presuming nor by despairing.

20. (Vers. 10.). As to the *shaking off of fear* he adds: *For it is written that*[^p94-5] *he hath given his angels charge over thee;* this is written in the Psalm. He adduces Scripture for this reason, because he saw that Christ was always defending himself through Scripture. Whence Pope Leo says: « He finds occasions of tempting there where he sees men more vehemently occupied ». Whence he asserts *that it is written,* so as to induce belief, and he also brings in Scripture that would exclude faintheartedness on account of the promise of angelic help, as much in *prosperity* as in *adversity.* — On account of *help in prosperity* he says: *To keep thee in all thy ways*[^p94-6], that is, works; Exodus, the twenty-third chapter: « Behold, I send my angel, who shall go before thee and keep thee in the way ». So the angel kept Tobias, as is said in Tobias, the twelfth chapter; so also Judith, as is said in the thirteenth chapter; so also they keep all just men, according to that saying to the Hebrews, the first chapter: « All are ministering spirits, sent to minister » etc.; and the whole Church, according to that saying of Isaias, the sixty-second chapter: « Upon thy walls, O Jerusalem, I have appointed keepers ». But Christ did not need keeping[^p94-7]; and therefore the devil was depraving this authority and adducing it in a sinister manner.

21. (Vers. 11.). But on account of *help in adversity* he adds: *And that in their hands they shall bear thee up,* as carriers, according as it is read that the angel carried Habacuc, Daniel, the last chapter[^p94-8]. *Lest perhaps thou strike thy foot against a stone,* that is, that you may be struck by no hurt, as is said in Job, the fifth chapter: « Thy covenant shall be with the stones of the lands, and the beasts of the earth shall be at peace with thee ». — And note that the devil, as one crafty, did not add what follows[^p94-9]: « Thou shalt walk upon the asp and the basilisk, and thou shalt trample under foot the lion and the dragon », because this was against him. In which it is shown that it is a diabolical vice to truncate authorities and to draw them to one's own side; whence the Apocalypse, the last chapter: « If any man shall diminish from the words of the prophecy of this book, God shall take away his part out of the book of life ».

22. (Vers. 12.). *And Jesus answering* etc. Here after the pestiferous persuading he adds the *dominical response,* brief and efficacious, when he says: *Answering*
<!-- page 95 -->
*Jesus said to him: It is said,* namely in Deuteronomy, the sixth chapter[^p95-1]: *Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God;* and Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter: « Be not as a man that tempteth God ». Whence the children of Israel are blamed, Numbers, the fourteenth chapter, to whom the Lord says: « They have tempted me now ten times ». Now *to tempt God,* as Bede says[^p95-2], is, when a man has what he may do, to seek a proof of the divine power. And because Christ did not need this proof of power, he would not tempt God nor consent to the devil, and through an authority he answered the authority opposed, so that the nail might be blunted by the strength of a nail[^p95-3].

23. (Vers. 13.). *And all the temptation being ended.* After he has described the occasion of the temptation and the mode of tempting, here third he describes the *consummation of the temptation,* concerning which three things are intimated. The first is the *manifestation of perfection* on the part of Christ resisting; as to which he says: *And all the temptation being ended,* because, as was said before[^p95-4], every temptation is reduced to the three aforesaid; whence the first of John, the second chapter: « All that is in the world is the concupiscence of the flesh » etc. This he *consummated,* because he overcame it by patiently enduring, according to that saying to the Hebrews, the second chapter[^p95-5]: « It became him, who had brought many children into glory, to consummate by his passion », because it is said in Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter: « The works of God are perfect ».

24. The second is the *confutation of presumption* on the part of the devil departing; as to which he says: *The devil departed from him;* according to that saying of James, the fourth chapter[^p95-6]: « Resist the devil, and he will fly from you ». *He departed,* namely confounded; which was signified in the king of the Assyrians, the fourth of Kings, the nineteenth chapter: « Sennacherib king of the Assyrians departing went away »; which is interpreted *taking away the deserts,* because the devil most of all persecutes[^tr-corrigenda] those who live in austerity of life.

25. But the third is the *predetermining of the time* on the part of God disposing; on account of which he adds: *For a time,* « of the passion[^p95-7] », namely he departed, because that malignant one is not permitted to do evil except for the time for which God permits, although he always has an evil will; Ecclesiasticus, the nineteenth chapter[^p95-8]: « If he find an opportunity of doing evil, he will do evil ». And therefore the counsel of Ecclesiasticus, the fourth chapter, is: « My son, observe the time, and fly from evil »; and to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter: « See how you walk circumspectly, redeeming the time, because the days are evil ». Whence, since the devil tempts in two ways, as a *dragon* and as a *lion*[^p95-9], he tempted first as a *dragon* to seduce, afterwards as a *lion* to break in the passion. For in the first he tries to soften by blandishments, but afterwards, seeing that he has a short time, he tries to break by punishments; whence the Apocalypse, the twelfth chapter[^p95-10]: « The devil is come down, having great wrath, knowing that he hath but a short time ». And therefore those did not attain to the consummation of the time of whom it is said below, in the eighth chapter, that « they believe for a while, and in time of temptation they fall away ».
