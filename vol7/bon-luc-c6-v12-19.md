# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. VI, Vers. 12-19
## *Primo exprimitur veritas doctrinae tripliciter*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 140 -->

#### Tertio determinatur vocatorum instructio per duo.

24. *Factum est autem in illis diebus, exiit* etc. Postquam determinata est doctrinae Christi authenticatio et discipulorum vocatio, hic subditur tertia pars, in qua fit *vocatorum instructio*[^p140-8]. Et quoniam instructio discipulorum duplex est, scilicet *communis* et *specialis: communis* scilicet in doctrina eorum quae requiruntur ad salutem omnium; *specialis,* in doctrina mysterii Scripturarum; ideo pars ista habet duas: in quarum prima erudiuntur Apostoli in *veritate explicata et praeceptis apertis;* in secunda instruuntur in *explicandis parabolis et parabolice dictis,* infra octavo[^p140-9]: *Et factum est deinceps, et ipse iter faciebat* etc.

#### In prima parte Apostoli instruuntur in veritate explicata et praeceptis apertis.

Prima pars habet duas: in prima exprimitur *veritas doctrinae;* in secunda vero ad confirmationem doctrinae declaratur *sublimitas in Doctore,* infra septimo[^p140-10]: *Cum autem implesset omnia verba sua* etc.

#### Primo exprimitur veritas doctrinae tripliciter.

Prima pars, quae continet praesens capitulum, habet tres. Quia enim doctrina non debet communicari indignis et fastidiosis, sed *idoneis* et *studiosis;* ideo primo praemittitur *segregatio discipulorum;* secundo vero, *attractio auditorum,* ibi[^p140-11]: *Et descendens cum illis* de monte; tertio vero, *explicatio documentorum,* ibi: *Et ipse, elevatis oculis* etc.

Circa *segregationem* autem *discipulorum* quatuor introducuntur, quae ipsam perficiunt, scilicet *oratio divina impetrativa doni, electio insinuativa meriti, institutio impositiva officii, nominatio*[^p140-12] *discretiva consortii.*

25. (Vers. 12.). Praemittitur *divina oratio impetrativa doni* ipsis discipulis eligendis, cum dicitur:
<!-- page 141 -->
*Factum est autem in illis diebus, exiit in montem orare; exiit,* scilicet de civitate et consortio turbarum, ut esset eius oratio *quieta.* Hinc dicitur de Iudith, quod « petiit, ut daretur ei copia, nocte et ante lucem egrediendi ad orationem », Iudith duodecimo[^p141-1]. Et quod plus, *exiit in montem,* scilicet ascendendo, ut ostenderet, quod oratio debet esse *elevata,* et orans elevatus a terrenis; quia, sicut dicit Damascenus[^p141-2], « oratio est ascensus intellectus in Deum ».

26. Huius figura praecessit in Moyse, Exodi decimo septimo[^p141-3], ubi dicitur, quod « Moyses ascendit in verticem collis, cumque elevaret Moyses manus, vincebat Israel ». — De his duobus, scilicet de *recessu a turbis* et *ascensu in montem,* dicitur Matthaei decimo quarto: « Dimissa turba, ascendit in montem solus orare ». Horum duorum figura praecessit in Abraham, de quo Genesis vigesimo secundo dicitur, quod « dixit ad pueros suos: Exspectate hic cum asino », scilicet in valle; « ego autem et puer illuc usque pergentes, postquam adoraverimus, revertemur ad vos ». — Per hunc etiam modum debet adorare anima sancta, cuius informatio apparet in Iudith, de qua duodecimo[^p141-4] dicitur, quod « egrediebatur nocte in vallem Bethuliae et ascendebat et adorabat Dominum Deum Israel ».

27. Et quia non sufficit oratio quantumcumque devota, nisi etiam sit *assidua;* ideo subditur: *Et erat pernoctans in oratione Dei;* ita ut posset dicere cum David[^p141-5], quod « media nocte surgebam ad confitendum tibi »; unde et Moyses, « ascendens in montem, fuit ibi quadraginta diebus et quadraginta noctibus », Exodi vigesimo quarto. Per hunc modum debent spirituales viri facere, iuxta quod dicitur Threnorum secundo[^p141-6]: « Consurge, in nocte lauda in principio vigiliarum, effunde sicut aquam cor tuum ante conspectum Domini ». Hinc est, quod dicitur de illa Sara devota Tobiae tertio, quod « perrexit in superius cubiculum domus suae et tribus diebus et tribus noctibus non manducavit neque bibit, sed in oratione persistens, cum lacrymis deprecabatur Dominum ».

28. (Vers. 13.). Secundo vero subditur *electio insinuativa meriti* in discipulis promovendis, cum subditur: *Et cum dies factus esset, vocavit discipulos suos et elegit ex ipsis duodecim.* In *die* vocat, quia, sicut Ioannis undecimo[^p141-7] dicitur, « qui ambulaverit in die non offendit, quia lucem huius mundi videt », scilicet Christum, qui « est lux vera, illuminans omnem hominem venientem in hunc mundum », Ioannis primo; et ideo primae Petri secundo: « De tenebris vos vocavit in admirabile lumen suum ».

29. Nota, quod discipulos vocat, ut eligat, quia discipuli Christi digni erant honorari, secundum illud Psalmi[^p141-8]: « Nimis honorificati sunt amici tui, Deus », et hoc, quia discipuli. Ad discipulatum enim Christi requiritur perfecta *humilitas;* Matthaei undecimo[^p141-9]: « Discite a me, quia mitis sum et humilis corde ». Item, perfecta *paupertas;* infra decimo quarto: « Nisi quis renuntiaverit omnibus, quae possidet, non potest meus esse discipulus ». Item, perfecta *caritas;* Ioannis decimo tertio: « In hoc cognoscent omnes, quod discipuli mei eritis, si dilectionem ad invicem habueritis ». — Et nota, quod *duodecim* elegit ad designandam abundantem perfectionem meriti, quia duodecimus est primus numerus abundans. Unde Matthaei quinto[^p141-10] Christus ait: « Nisi abundaverit iustitia vestra plus quam scribarum et Pharisaeorum, non intrabitis in regnum caelorum ». Hic autem numerus designatur in *duodecim filiis Iacob,* Genesis quadragesimo sexto[^p141-11]; in *duodecim lapidibus,* Exodi vigesimo octavo; in *duodecim principibus* plebis Israel, Numerorum decimo septimo; in *duodecim exploratoribus* missis a Moyse, Numerorum decimo tertio; in *duodecim fontibus* aquarum, Exodi decimo quinto; in *duodecim lapidibus* de Iordane extractis, Iosue quarto; in *duodecim bobus* sustentantibus mare aeneum, tertii Regum septimo; in *duodecim horis* diei, Ioannis undecimo; in *duodecim fundamentis* civitatis, Apocalypsis vigesimo primo. Fuerunt enim Apostoli figuris plurimis designati propter multiplicem dignitatem. Fuerunt enim *patriarchae* filiorum spiritualium procreatione[^p141-12]; *lapides pretiosi,* virtutum varietate; *principes multitudinis,* praelationis sublimitate; *exploratores,* contemplationis subtilitate; *fontes aquarum,* sapientiae profunditate; *lapides Iordanis,* fidei fundatione; *boves,* praedicationis exercitatione; *horae diei,* exemplorum honestate; *fundamenta civitatis,* totius Ecclesiae supportatione[^p141-13].
<!-- page 142 -->

30. Tertio vero adiungitur *institutio impositiva officii* in electis, cum subditur: *Quos et apostolos nominavit,* id est missos; in quo auctoritatem dedit praecipuam praedicandi; ad Romanos decimo[^p142-1]: « Quomodo praedicabunt, nisi mittantur »? Unde conqueritur Dominus de malis prophetis Ieremiae vigesimo tertio: « Non mittebam eos, et ipsi currebant; non loquebar ad eos, et ipsi prophetabant ». At non tales Apostoli, de quibus Matthaei decimo dicitur: « Ecce, ego mitto vos sicut oves in medio luporum ». Hos autem misit ad Ecclesiam Dei fundandam et civitatem construendam super illum lapidem vivum, angularem in fundamentis fundatum. Unde Apocalypsis vigesimo primo[^p142-2]: « Murus civitatis habebat fundamenta duodecim, et in ipsis nomina duodecim Apostolorum Agni ». Et ideo ad Ephesios secundo: « Vos estis cives Sanctorum et domestici Dei, superaedificati super fundamentum Apostolorum et Prophetarum, ipso summo angulari lapide Christo Iesu, in quo omnis aedificatio constructa, crescit in templum sanctum in Domino, in quo et vos coaedificamini in habitaculum Dei in Spiritu sancto ».

31. (Vers. 14.). Quarto subiungitur *denominatio discretiva consortii* in Apostolis iam promotis; quod notatur, cum subditur: *Simonem, qui cognominatur Petrus*[^p142-3], *et Andream, fratrem eius.* Nominat enim Apostolos binos et binos, secundum quod Dominus associavit eos. Unde infra decimo subditur, quod « misit eos binos et binos in omnem civitatem et locum, quo erat ipse venturus ».

32. Et nota, quod hic ponit nomina omnium, sed non omnium ponit et cognomina, sed illorum qui habent nomina aequivoca, ut Simonis Petri et Simonis Zelotis et Iudae Iacobi et Iudae Iscariotis. — Et nota, quod associat fratrem fratri, ut Petrum Andreae et Ioannem Iacobo ad commendandam concordiam, secundum illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo quinto[^p142-4]: « In tribus beneplacitum est spiritui meo: concordia fratrum et amor proximorum ». Nam, sicut dicitur Proverbiorum decimo octavo, « frater, qui adiuvatur a fratre, quasi civitas firma ». — Postremo autem Apostolo, scilicet Iudae, addit non solum cognomen, sed etiam crimen, cum dicit[^p142-5]: *Qui fuit proditor;* in quo designatur, quod Dominus in eligendo eum non ignoravit, qualis futurus erat; unde Ioannis sexto: « Nonne vos elegi, et unus ex vobis diabolus est »?

33. Licet autem hic esset futurus pessimus, tamen Dominus eum elegit. Competebat enim suae *humilitati,* qua non recusabat consortium suum proditori, et suae *benignitati,* qua non solum bonis, sed etiam malis benignum se ostendebat. Unde et fecit eum dispensatorem, sicut dicitur Ioannis duodecimo[^p142-6]: « Sed ea quae mittebantur, portabat ». — Congruebat etiam *statui praesentis Ecclesiae,* in qua mali permiscentur bonis, secundum illud Matthaei decimo tertio[^p142-7]: « Sinite utraque crescere usque ad messem »; quia « simile est », sicut ibidem dicitur, « regnum caelorum sagenae missae in mare et ex omni genere piscium congreganti » etc. — Expediebat hoc etiam *eruditioni nostrae,* ut non *diffidamus,* si aliquos, qui videntur stare, cadere videamus; et propter aliquos malos bonorum consortium non despiciamus, iuxta quod dicitur primae Ioannis secundo[^p142-8]: « Ex nobis prodierunt, sed non erant ex nobis ». Ut etiam non *praesumamus,* si ad dignitates eligamur. Unde Bernardus in epistola ad Coloniensem archiepiscopum sic ait: « Si cunctos, qui eliguntur ad ministerium, constat eligi ad regnum, securus est Coloniensis archiepiscopus. Quodsi Iudam in sacerdotio et Saulem in regno non legitur reprobasse nisi Deus, timeat necesse est et Coloniensis archiepiscopus ». Unde valde terribile est illud Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p142-9]: « Multi sunt vocati, pauci vero electi »; et ideo ad Romanos duodecimo: « Noli altum sapere, sed time ».

34. *Spiritualiter* per has sex combinationes duodecim Apostolorum intelliguntur sex gradus profectus virtutum, quibus pervenitur usque ad *caritatem perfectam,* vel usque ad *pacem,* vel usque ad *sapientiam.* — De primis sex gradibus, quibus ad *caritatem* pervenitur, secundae Petri primo[^p142-10]: « Ministrate in fide vestra virtutem, in virtute autem scientiam, in scientia autem abstinentiam, in abstinentia autem patientiam, in patientia autem pietatem, in pietate autem amorem fraternitatis, in amore fraternitatis caritatem ». Et illi sex gradus recte correspondent istis combinationibus, si nominum interpretationes attendantur. — De gradibus, quibus pervenitur ad *pacem,* Matthaei quinto[^p142-11]: « Beati
<!-- page 143 -->
pauperes » etc. — De gradibus vero, quibus ad *sapientiam* pervenitur, Isaiae undecimo[^p143-1]: « Et requiescet super eum spiritus Domini, spiritus sapientiae et intellectus, spiritus consilii et fortitudinis, spiritus scientiae et pietatis. Et replebit eum spiritus timoris Domini »; ubi incipit a supremo. Horum figura pulcherrima praecessit in throno Salomonis, ubi[^p143-2] dicitur, quod erant « sex gradus », « et duodecim leunculi stantes super sex gradus hinc et inde ». Per *leonem* intelligitur potestatis auctoritas, per *sex gradus* sex combinationes spiritualibus gradibus aptissime respondentes.

35. *Moraliter* etiam per nomina Apostolorum sic combinata insinuatur perfectio praelatorum, specialiter episcoporum, qui ceteros debent praecellere in tribus: in *vita,* in *doctrina,* in *exemplo* sive conversatione bona. Unde Apostolus instruens ad Titum secundo[^p143-3] dicit: « Tu autem loquere quae decent sanam doctrinam »; ecce, *doctrina.* « Nemo te contemnat »; ecce, *vita.* « In omnibus te ipsum praebe exemplum bonorum operum »; ecce, *exemplum* seu conversatio bona. — Quatuor ergo prima nomina designant *perfectionem vitae;* quatuor alia *perfectionem doctrinae;* alia vero quatuor *perfectionem exempli* sive conversationis bonae.

36. Dicit ergo: *Simonem,* supple vocavit, qui interpretatur *obediens. Quem cognominavit Petrum,* qui interpretatur *agnoscens*[^p143-4]; in quo designatur virtus *prudentiae,* qua cognominari debet Petrus, id est *agnoscens, ex Simone;* quia ex humilitate debet procedere cognominatio prudentiae, ut, secundum quod dicitur Proverbiorum vigesimo tertio[^p143-5], « prudentiae suae ponat modum »; « et ne innitatur prudentiae suae », Proverbiorum tertio; et sic non erit « prudens apud semetipsum », ad Romanos duodecimo, sed « in omnibus prudenter aget », secundum quod de David dicitur primi Regum decimo octavo. — *Et Andream,* qui interpretatur *virilis*[^p143-6]; in quo exprimitur virtus *iustitiae.* Alioquin « noli quaerere fieri iudex, nisi virtute valeas irrumpere iniquitates », Ecclesiastici septimo. — *Et Iacobum,* qui interpretatur *supplantator* vel luctator[^p143-7], per quem insinuatur virtus *fortitudinis,* quae fortiter adversa aggreditur et contra *vitia* reluctatur; unde ad Ephesios sexto: « Non est nobis colluctatio adversus carnem et sanguinem, sed adversus principes et potestates, adversus mundi rectores tenebrarum harum, contra spiritualia nequitiae in caelestibus ». Sic episcopus debet esse « leo, fortissimus bestiarum, ut ad nullius paveat occursum », Proverbiorum trigesimo[^p143-8]; etiam contra *Deum,* ut ei dicatur illud quod Iacob dictum est Genesis trigesimo secundo: « Si contra Deum fortis fuisti, quanto magis contra homines praevalebis ». — *Et Ioannem,* qui interpretatur *in quo est gratia*[^p143-9]; per quem intelligitur virtus *temperantiae* sive continentiae, quae gratia Dei est, secundum illud Sapientiae octavo: « Non possum esse continens, nisi Deus det », maxime quia « virgo a Domino est electus et virgo in aevum permansit »; et Ecclesiastici vigesimo sexto: « Gratia super gratiam, mulier sancta et pudorosa ». — Secundum combinationem istarum quatuor virtutum vidit Ezechiel species « quatuor animalium », « et manus hominis sub pennis eorum in quatuor partibus, et facies et pennas per quatuor partes habebant », Ezechielis primo[^p143-10], in praefigurationem quatuor virtutum, quae
<!-- page 144 -->
exiguntur ad regimen praelatorum, quia « his utilius nihil est in vita hominibus », Sapientiae octavo.

37. Sequitur de nominibus apostolicis, quae pertinent ad *doctrinam,* cum dicit: *Philippum,* qui interpretatur *os lampadis*[^p144-1]; in quo insinuatur, quod doctrina episcopalis debet esse *lucida* quantum ad *intellectum audientium,* ut sit « aspectus eorum quasi aspectus lampadarum », Ezechielis primo; ut ponatur « lampas super candelabrum », Zachariae quarto, non « sub modio », infra undecimo; sed sicut de Elia dicitur Ecclesiastici quadragesimo octavo: « Verbum eius quasi facula ardebat ». — *Et Bartholomaeum,* qui interpretatur *filius suspendentis aquas*[^p144-2] », in quo intelligitur doctrina *devota* quantum ad *affectum,* ut ex devotione suspendat et elevet lacrymas usque ad oculum, de quo dicit Dominus ad Iob trigesimo octavo[^p144-3]: « Nunquid elevabis in nebula vocem tuam »? scilicet in devotione seu compunctione in docendo; « et impetus aquarum operiet te »? in lacrymando; quod facit Dominus, qui « ad vocem suam dat multitudinem aquarum in caelo et elevat nebulas ab extremitatibus terrae », Ieremiae decimo; et « fulgura in pluviam facit », sicut dicit Psalmus, id est comminationes.

38. (Vers. 15.). *Matthaeum,* qui interpretatur *donatus*[^p144-4], per quem insinuatur, quod doctrina debet esse *aedificatoria ad operandum.* Nam post habitam devotionem oportet, quod aliquis auditum salutarem impleat per bonam operationem; hoc est ab illo specialiter, a quo « omne datum optimum et omne donum perfectum », Iacobi primo. Ipse verbo praedicatoris confert virtutem sive gratiam praedicationis et aedificationis; unde Isaiae quadragesimo primo: « Primus ad Sion dicet: Ecce, adsum, et Ierusalem evangelistam dabo ». — *Et Thomam,* qui interpretatur *abyssus*[^p144-5], per quod intelligitur *profunditas eruditionis* credendorum, ut sit « consilium eius in abysso magna », ut dicitur Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto. Unde quaeritur a Iob trigesimo octavo: « Nunquid in novissimis abyssi deambulasti »? « O altitudo divitiarum sapientiae et scientiae Dei, quam incomprehensibilia sunt iudicia eius »! quia, « iudicia eius abyssus multa », ad Romanos undecimo et Psalmus. — Et haec quatuor pertinent ad *doctrinam,* ut sic quatuor rotae « in quatuor partes gradiantur et non revertantur ambulantes », Ezechielis decimo[^p144-6].

39. Sequitur de illis nominibus, quae pertinent ad *exemplum: Iacobum,* qui interpretatur *luctator, Alphaei,* qui interpretatur *fugitivus*[^p144-7]; in quo exprimitur exemplum *perfectae paupertatis.* Unde Gregorius: « Qui ad certamen cum diabolo properat vestimenta abiiciat, ne succumbat. Quid autem sunt terrena omnia nisi quaedam corporis indumenta »? Unde volentem in paupertate luctari oportet temporalia fugere, secundum quod dicit Psalmus[^p144-8]: « Ecce, elongavi fugiens et mansi in solitudine ». Sic Elias in figura huius fugit a facie Iezabel, quae interpretatur *fluxus sterquilinii;* per quod designatur temporalium fluxibilitas. — *Et Simonem,* qui interpretatur *obediens, qui vocatur Zelotes*[^p144-9], id est *aemulator;* in quo exemplum exprimitur *perfectae humilitatis* cum aemulatione ordinatae caritatis; unde primae Petri primo: « Animas vestras castificantes in obedientia caritatis, in fraternitatis amore, simplici ex corde invicem diligite attentius »; Ecclesiastici tertio: « Filii sapientiae, ecclesia iustorum, et natio illorum obedientia et dilectio ».
<!-- page 145 -->

40. (Vers. 16.). *Iudam*[^p145-1], qui interpretatur *confitens, Iacobi,* ut sit in exemplum *strenuitatis in agendo;* Matthaei quinto: « Luceat lux vestra coram hominibus, ut videant opera vestra bona et glorificent Patrem vestrum, qui in caelis est ». Hinc Tobiae duodecimo[^p145-2]: « Benedicite Dominum caeli et coram omnibus viventibus confitemini illi ». Hinc est, quod in benedictione Iudae dicitur Deuteronomii trigesimo tertio: « Manus eius pugnabunt pro eo, et adiutor illius contra adversarios eius erit »; unde ipse ascendit ante alios in proelium, Iudicum primo. — *Et Iudam Iscariotem*[^p145-3], qui interpretatur *memoria mortis;* in quo intelligitur exemplum *mortificationis carnis.* Unde Gregorius: « Nihil adeo valet ad domanda carnis vitia quam cogitare, qualis caro nostra post mortem sit futura ». Et hoc debent facere, « ne, cum aliis praedicaverint, ipsi reprobi efficiantur », secundum quod dicit Apostolus primae ad Corinthios nono[^p145-4]: « Castigo corpus meum » etc.; propter quod dicitur infra duodecimo: « Sint lumbi vestri praecincti » etc. Sed quia multi decipiuntur et traduntur occasione mortificatae carnis, quia inde gloriantur, ut hypocritae, qui « ore confitentur, se nosse Deum, et facto negant », secundum quod dicitur ad Titum primo[^p145-5]; caveant, ne per *proditionem* huiusmodi reprobentur, quia hic subditur: *Qui fuit proditor.* Hinc est, quod dicit Apostolus secundae ad Timotheum tertio[^p145-6], quod « in novissimis temporibus instabunt tempora periculosa, et erunt homines se ipsos amantes »; et sequitur: « Proditores, protervi, invidi, voluptatum amatores magis quam Dei, habentes speciem quidem pietatis, virtutem autem eius abnegantes ». — Et haec pertinent ad *exemplum,* et hi sunt quatuor paralytici portitores, supra quinto[^p145-7]. Et in hunc modum nominum apostolicorum designatur mysterium; et sic terminatur haec pars.

41. (Vers. 17.). *Et descendens cum illis* etc. Post discipulorum segregationem subiungit hic Evangelista *auditorum attractionem*[^p145-8], quam describit Evangelista quantum ad quatuor: quantum ad *discipulos comitantes,* quantum ad *populos concurrentes,* quantum ad *causas incitantes* et quantum ad *efficacias subsequentes.*

Quantum ad *discipulos comitantes* dicit: *Et descendens,* de monte, *cum illis,* id est cum duodecim iam electis, qui ipsum semper comitabantur, secundum quod infra vigesimo secundo[^p145-9] dicitur: « Vos estis, qui permansistis mecum in tentationibus meis »; *stetit in loco campestri,* id est plano et communi, ut omnes possent accedere ad audiendam sapientiam, ut possent dicere: « Ecce, audivimus eum in Ephrata, invenimus eum in campis silvae ». Et sic impletum est illud Proverbiorum primo: « Sapientia foris praedicat, in plateis dat vocem suam, in capite turbarum clamitat ». — Ad huius sapientiae clamorem attrahebantur praecipue discipuli; unde subdit: *Et turba discipulorum eius,* scilicet stetit cum eo. Hoc dicit quantum ad alios discipulos, Apostolis inferiores. Nam perfectis discipulis fecerat sermonem in montis cacumine, sicut dicitur Matthaei quinto[^p145-10]: « Videns Iesus turbas, ascendit in montem, et cum sedisset, accesserunt ad eum discipuli eius ». Istis vero tanquam imperfectis condescendens, sermonem fecit in descensu de monte; unde dicuntur *turba,* quia multi erant, multitudini etiam propinqui. Multiplicabantur enim discipuli Christi, sicut dicitur Ioannis quarto[^p145-11]: « Audierunt Pharisaei, quia Iesus plures discipulos facit quam Ioannes ». Istis non erant secreta communicanda, quia ex auditu secretorum scandalizabantur; unde dicitur Ioannis sexto, quod ex verbo, quod dixerat, « multi discipulorum eius abierunt retro et iam cum illo non ambulabant. Et dixit Iesus ad duodecim: Nunquid et vos vultis abire »? cum istis scilicet discipulis.

42. Dicitur Iesus *stare,* sed cum aliis perfectis *sedere;* in quo ostendit, quod « nos firmiores debemus imbecillitates infirmorum sustinere et non nobis placere », ad Romanos decimo quinto[^p145-12]. — *Et*
<!-- page 146 -->
*descendit cum illis,* ad exemplum humilitatis, secundum illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo secundo[^p146-1]: « Rectorem te posuerunt? Noli extolli; esto in illis quasi unus ex illis ». Unde sua benignitate omnes attrahebat et adunabat, sicut gallina pullos; Matthaei vigesimo tertio: « Quoties volui congregare filios tuos, sicut gallina pullos » etc. Nec immerito, quia de ipso dictum est Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo: « Invenit eum in terra deserta, in loco horroris et vastae solitudinis; circumduxit eum et docuit et custodivit quasi pupillam oculi sui. Sicut aquila, provocans ad volandum pullos suos, expandit alas super eos » etc.

43. Quantum ad *populos concurrentes* subditur: *Et multitudo copiosa plebis,* scilicet stetit cum eo, qui scilicet ex diversis locis convenerant; convenerant tam de *locis propinquis,* quantum ad quae dicit: *Ab omni Iudaea et Samaria et Ierusalem,* quae intra regnum Israel erant; *Iudaea* nomen est regni, *Samaria* principalior civitas in regno decem tribuum, et *Ierusalem* principalior in regno duarum[^p146-2]; ab his omnibus confluebant ad Christum, secundum illud Isaiae secundo: « Erit in novissimis diebus praeparatus mons domus Domini in vertice montium, et elevabitur super colles, et fluent ad eum omnes gentes »; quam etiam de *locis remotis,* quantum ad quae dicit: *Et maritima et Tyri et Sidonis.* Et debet iterari: multitudo copiosa plebis, *a maritima,* et multitudo copiosa *Tyri et Sidonis,* id est, de Tyro et Sidone. Et haec erant loca *remota,* sicut dicit Beda in Glossa[^p146-3], quod « *maritima* non a proximo mari Galilaeae, sed a mari magno cognominatur »; Isaiae sexagesimo: « Tunc videbis et afflues, et mirabitur et dilatabitur cor tuum, quando conversa fuerit ad te multitudo maris, fortitudo gentium venerit tibi ». — Hoc autem praefiguratum fuit in Salomone, tertii Regum decimo[^p146-4], ubi dicitur, quod « omnes desiderabant videre vultum Salomonis et audire sapientiam eius ».

44. (Vers. 18.). Quantum ad *causas incitantes* subiungitur: *Qui venerant, ut audirent eum,* quantum ad *illustrationem veritatis* in mente, *et sanarentur ab infirmitatibus suis*[^p146-5], quantum ad *curationem salutis* in carne. Et haec erat recta intentio, secundum illud Osee sexto: « Venite, et revertamur ad Dominum, quia ipse cepit et sanabit nos »; et post: « Et vivemus in conspectu eius, sciemus sequemurque, ut cognoscamus Dominum ». In hoc ostendebant isti, quod erant de ovibus Christi, quia ad ipsum concurrebant ad veritatem et salutem percipiendam; Ioannis decimo[^p146-6]: « Oves meae vocem meam audient, et ego cognosco eas, et sequentur me, et ego vitam aeternam do eis ». Amor igitur veritatis intelligendae et sanitatis obtinendae attrahebat eos, ut venirent ad Christum, sicut ad magistrum et sicut ad medicum. Ad primum invitat Salvator in Psalmo[^p146-7]: « Venite, filii, audite me; timorem Domini docebo vos »; ad secundum in Matthaei undecimo: « Venite ad me, omnes, qui laboratis et onerati estis; et ego reficiam vos ».

45. Quantum ad *efficacias sequentes* subinfertur: *Et qui vexabantur a spiritibus immundis curabantur;* in quo tangitur efficacia virtutis respectu spirituum immundorum; Zachariae decimo tertio[^p146-8]: « Spiritum immundum auferam de terra ». Isti spiritus immundi vexant eos quos possident; Matthaei decimo quinto: « Filia mea male a daemonio vexatur ». Haec vexatio compellebat eos fugere ad Christum, quia, secundum quod dicitur Isaiae vigesimo octavo[^p146-9], tantummodo « vexatio intellectum dabit auditui »; secundum autem quod dicit Gregorius, « mala, quae nos hic premunt, ad Deum ire compellunt ». Certe dum ad Deum compellunt, ad bonam dispositionem deducunt, secundum illud Sapientiae tertio: « In paucis vexati, in multis bene disponentur ».

46. (Vers. 19.). Nec tantum efficacia erat in animam, sed etiam in corpus per ipsius Christi corpus; quod notatur, cum subdit: *Et omnis turba quaerebat eum tangere,* ex fide scilicet et devotione, sicut illa mulier, de qua Matthaei nono[^p146-10]: « Si tetigero fimbriam vestimenti eius, salva ero ». — Et merito devoti erant; unde subditur: *Quia virtus de illo exibat et sanabat omnes,* id est operatio virtutis; infra octavo[^p146-11]: « Ego cognovi, virtutem de me exiisse ». Tunc *virtus exit,* quando, latens in *se,* manifestatur in *opere,* secundum illud Sapientiae duodecimo:
<!-- page 147 -->
« Virtutem ostendis, qui non crederis esse in virtute consummatus ». Tunc etiam *exit,* quando *aliis communicat,* secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo: « Qui dat lasso virtutem et his qui non sunt, fortitudinem et robur multiplicat ». Et *magna* erat haec virtus, quae in omnes poterat, secundum illud Sapientiae decimo quarto[^p147-1]: « Potens es ex omnibus sanare »; et decimo sexto: « Qui conversus erat non per id quod videbat, sanabatur, sed per te, omnium Salvatorem ». Et hoc maxime attrahebat omnes ad Christum; unde dicitur Ioannis sexto: « Sequebatur eum multitudo magna, quia videbant signa, quae faciebat super his qui infirmabantur ».

47. *Spiritualiter* nota hic, quod auditores, qui veniunt ad Christum, attrahuntur de sex locis, de tribus scilicet *Iudaeorum* et tribus *gentilium.* Per loca *Iudaeorum* signantur iusti, per loca *gentilium* peccatores. — Tria autem ponuntur loca *Iudaeorum* secundum statum triplicem *iustorum. Iudaea* enim interpretatur *confitens*[^p147-2] et significat statum *activorum,* qui Christum bonis operibus confitentur; Osee decimo: « Arabit Iudas, et confringet sibi sulcos Iacob »; Tobiae decimo tertio: « Confitemini illi et Regem saeculorum exaltate in operibus vestris ». — *Samaria,* quae interpretatur *custodia*[^p147-3], significat statum *praelatorum.* Nam illis dicitur tertii Regum vigesimo: « Custodi virum istum, qui si lapsus fuerit, erit anima tua pro anima illius ». — *Ierusalem,* quae interpretatur *visio pacis*[^p147-4], significat statum *contemplativorum;* Isaiae sexagesimo: « Surge, illuminare, Ierusalem, quia venit lumen tuum »; Tobiae decimo tertio dicitur de Ierusalem: « Beati omnes, qui diligunt te et gaudent in pace tua ».

48. Tria etiam ponuntur loca *gentilium* secundum triplicem statum *peccatorum. Maritima* enim, quae ventosa est et frequenter turbulenta, significat vanitatem et turbulentiam superborum; Isaiae quinquagesimo septimo[^p147-5]: « Impii quasi mare fervens, quod quiescere non potest ». — *Tyrus,* quae interpretatur *angustia*[^p147-6], significat anxietatem et angustiam *cupidorum;* Ecclesiastici vigesimo septimo: « Inter medium emptionis et venditionis angustiabitur peccator »; Ecclesiastici quinto: « Noli esse anxius in divitiis iniustis ». — *Sidon,* quae interpretatur *venatio*[^p147-7], significat desiderium et concupiscentiam *carnalium;* unde in Psalmo: « Convertentur ad vesperam et famem patientur ut canes et circuibunt civitatem ». — Omnes hi concurrunt ad Christum, sed *iusti* tanquam ad *magistrum,* ut doceantur sapientiam, quia, secundum illud Proverbiorum nono[^p147-8], « doce iustum, et festinabit accipere »; et Deuteronomii penultimo: « Qui appropinquant pedibus eius accipient de doctrina illius ». Sed *peccatores* ut ad *amicum,* ut assequantur misericordiam; infra decimo quinto[^p147-9]: « Erant appropinquantes ad Iesum publicani et peccatores, ut audirent illum »; a quibus primo eiiciuntur daemones per *expulsionem culpae;* secundo sanantur in tactu per *curationem sequelae,* iuxta quod dicitur in Psalmo: « Qui propitiatur omnibus iniquitatibus tuis, qui sanat omnes infirmitates tuas ».

#### Thirdly, the instruction of those called is set out, through two things.

24. *And it came to pass in those days, he went out* etc. After the authentication of the doctrine of Christ and the calling of the disciples have been set out, here the third part is added, in which there takes place the *instruction of those called*[^p140-8]. And since the instruction of the disciples is twofold, namely *common* and *special: common,* namely, in the doctrine of those things which are required for the salvation of all; *special,* in the doctrine of the mystery of the Scriptures; therefore this part has two: in the first of which the Apostles are schooled in *truth explicated and precepts laid open;* in the second they are instructed in *parables to be explicated and things said in parables,* below, in the eighth chapter[^p140-9]: *And it came to pass afterwards, that he travelled* etc.

#### In the first part the Apostles are instructed in truth explicated and precepts laid open.

The first part has two: in the first the *truth of the doctrine* is expressed; but in the second, for the confirmation of the doctrine, the *sublimity in the Doctor* is declared, below, in the seventh chapter[^p140-10]: *And when he had finished all his words* etc.

#### First the truth of the doctrine is expressed in three ways.

The first part, which contains the present chapter, has three. For since doctrine ought not to be communicated to the unworthy and the disdainful, but to the *fit* and the *zealous;* therefore first there is set before the *separation of the disciples;* but secondly, the *attraction of the hearers,* at[^p140-11]: *And coming down with them* from the mountain; but thirdly, the *explication of the lessons,* at: *And he, lifting up his eyes* etc.

Now concerning the *separation of the disciples* four things are introduced, which complete it, namely *the divine prayer that impetrates the gift, the election that indicates merit, the institution that imposes the office, the naming*[^p140-12] *that distinguishes the fellowship.*

25. (Vers. 12.). The *divine prayer that impetrates the gift* for the disciples themselves who are to be chosen is set before, when it is said:
<!-- page 141 -->
*And it came to pass in those days, that he went out into a mountain to pray; he went out,* namely from the city and from the fellowship of the multitudes, so that his prayer might be *quiet.* Hence it is said of Judith that « she asked that she might have liberty to go out at night and before day to prayer », Judith, the twelfth chapter[^p141-1]. And what is more, *he went out into a mountain,* namely by ascending, to show that prayer ought to be *raised up,* and the one praying raised up from earthly things; because, as Damascene says[^p141-2], « prayer is the ascent of the understanding into God ».

26. The figure of this went before in Moses, Exodus, the seventeenth chapter[^p141-3], where it is said that « Moses went up upon the top of the hill, and when Moses lifted up his hands, Israel overcame ». — Of these two, namely of the *withdrawal from the multitudes* and the *ascent into the mountain,* it is said in Matthew, the fourteenth chapter: « Having dismissed the multitude, he went up into a mountain alone to pray ». The figure of these two went before in Abraham, of whom it is said in Genesis, the twenty-second chapter, that « he said to his young men: Stay you here with the ass », namely in the valley; « I and the boy will go with speed as far as yonder, and after we have worshipped, will return to you ». — In this manner too ought the holy soul to worship, whose formation appears in Judith, of whom it is said in the twelfth chapter[^p141-4] that « she went out by night into the valley of Bethulia, and went up, and worshipped the Lord the God of Israel ».

27. And since prayer, however devout, does not suffice unless it be also *assiduous;* therefore it is added: *And he passed the whole night in the prayer of God;* so that he could say with David[^p141-5] that « I rose at midnight to give praise to thee »; whence also Moses, « going up into the mountain, was there forty days and forty nights », Exodus, the twenty-fourth chapter. In this manner ought spiritual men to do, according to what is said in Lamentations, the second chapter[^p141-6]: « Arise, give praise in the night, in the beginning of the watches; pour out thy heart like water before the face of the Lord ». Hence it is that it is said of that devout Sara, Tobias, the third chapter, that « she went into an upper chamber of her house, and for three days and three nights did neither eat nor drink, but continuing in prayer with tears besought the Lord ».

28. (Vers. 13.). But secondly there is added the *election that indicates merit* in the disciples who are to be promoted, when it is added: *And when day was come, he called his disciples, and he chose twelve of them.* He calls in the *day,* because, as is said in John, the eleventh chapter[^p141-7], « he that shall walk in the day stumbleth not, because he seeth the light of this world », namely Christ, who « is the true light, which enlighteneth every man that cometh into this world », John, the first chapter; and therefore in the first of Peter, the second chapter: « He hath called you out of darkness into his wonderful light ».

29. Note that he calls the disciples in order to choose them, because the disciples of Christ were worthy to be honoured, according to that of the Psalm[^p141-8]: « Thy friends, O God, are made exceedingly honourable », and this because they are disciples. For to the discipleship of Christ there is required perfect *humility;* Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p141-9]: « Learn of me, because I am meek and humble of heart ». Likewise, perfect *poverty;* below, the fourteenth chapter: « Unless one renounce all that he possesseth, he cannot be my disciple ». Likewise, perfect *charity;* John, the thirteenth chapter: « By this shall all men know that you are my disciples, if you have love one for another ». — And note that he chose *twelve* to designate the abounding perfection of merit, because twelve is the first abundant number. Whence in Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p141-10], Christ says: « Unless your justice abound more than that of the scribes and Pharisees, you shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven ». Now this number is designated in the *twelve sons of Jacob,* Genesis, the forty-sixth chapter[^p141-11]; in the *twelve stones,* Exodus, the twenty-eighth chapter; in the *twelve princes* of the people of Israel, Numbers, the seventeenth chapter; in the *twelve spies* sent by Moses, Numbers, the thirteenth chapter; in the *twelve fountains* of waters, Exodus, the fifteenth chapter; in the *twelve stones* taken out of the Jordan, Josue, the fourth chapter; in the *twelve oxen* holding up the brazen sea, the third of Kings, the seventh chapter; in the *twelve hours* of the day, John, the eleventh chapter; in the *twelve foundations* of the city, Apocalypse, the twenty-first chapter. For the Apostles were designated by very many figures on account of their manifold dignity. For they were *patriarchs* by the procreation of spiritual sons[^p141-12]; *precious stones,* by the variety of virtues; *princes of the multitude,* by the sublimity of prelacy; *spies,* by the subtlety of contemplation; *fountains of waters,* by the depth of wisdom; *stones of the Jordan,* by the foundation of faith; *oxen,* by the exercise of preaching; *hours of the day,* by the honourableness of examples; *foundations of the city,* by the supporting of the whole Church[^p141-13].
<!-- page 142 -->

30. But thirdly there is joined the *institution that imposes the office* on those chosen, when it is added: *Whom also he named apostles,* that is, sent ones; in which he gave the chief authority of preaching; to the Romans, the tenth chapter[^p142-1]: « How shall they preach unless they be sent »? Whence the Lord complains of the evil prophets, Jeremias, the twenty-third chapter: « I did not send them, yet they ran; I spoke not to them, yet they prophesied ». But not such were the Apostles, of whom it is said in Matthew, the tenth chapter: « Behold I send you as sheep in the midst of wolves ». And these he sent to found the Church of God and to build the city upon that living stone, the corner stone, founded in the foundations. Whence in the Apocalypse, the twenty-first chapter[^p142-2]: « And the wall of the city had twelve foundations, and in them the names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb ». And therefore to the Ephesians, the second chapter: « You are fellow citizens with the saints, and the domestics of God, built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief corner stone, in whom all the building, being framed together, groweth up into an holy temple in the Lord, in whom you also are built together into an habitation of God in the Holy Spirit ».

31. (Vers. 14.). Fourthly there is subjoined the *naming that distinguishes the fellowship* in the Apostles now promoted; which is noted when it is added: *Simon, who is surnamed Peter*[^p142-3], *and Andrew his brother.* For he names the Apostles two and two, according as the Lord associated them. Whence below, in the tenth chapter, it is added that « he sent them two and two into every city and place whither he himself was to come ».

32. And note that here he sets down the names of all, but he does not set down the surnames of all too, but of those who have equivocal names, as of Simon Peter and of Simon Zelotes and of Jude of James and of Judas Iscariot. — And note that he associates brother with brother, as Peter with Andrew and John with James, to commend concord, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p142-4]: « With three things my spirit is pleased: the concord of brethren, and the love of neighbours ». For, as is said in Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter, « a brother that is helped by his brother, is like a strong city ». — But to the last Apostle, namely Judas, he adds not only the surname, but also the crime, when he says[^p142-5]: *Who was the traitor;* in which it is designated that the Lord in choosing him was not ignorant of what sort he was to be; whence in John, the sixth chapter: « Have not I chosen you, and one of you is a devil »?

33. But although this one was to be the worst, yet the Lord chose him. For it befitted his *humility,* by which he did not refuse his fellowship to the traitor, and his *kindness,* by which he showed himself kind not only to the good but also to the evil. Whence also he made him dispenser, as is said in John, the twelfth chapter[^p142-6]: « But he carried the things that were put therein ». — It was fitting also to the *state of the present Church,* in which the evil are mingled with the good, according to that of Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p142-7]: « Suffer both to grow until the harvest »; because « it is like », as is said in the same place, « the kingdom of heaven, to a net cast into the sea, and gathering together of all kind of fishes » etc. — This was expedient also for *our erudition,* that we may not *distrust,* if we see some, who seem to stand, fall; and that on account of some evil men we may not despise the fellowship of the good, according to what is said in the first of John, the second chapter[^p142-8]: « They went out from us, but they were not of us ». That we may also not *presume,* if we are chosen for dignities. Whence Bernard, in the letter to the archbishop of Cologne, speaks thus: « If it is certain that all who are chosen for the ministry are chosen for the kingdom, the archbishop of Cologne is secure. But if Judas in the priesthood and Saul in the kingdom are read to have been reprobated by none but God, the archbishop of Cologne too must needs fear ». Whence very terrible is that of Matthew, the twenty-second chapter[^p142-9]: « Many are called, but few chosen »; and therefore to the Romans, the twelfth chapter: « Be not highminded, but fear ».

34. *Spiritually,* through these six combinations of the twelve Apostles are understood six degrees of the progress of virtues, by which one arrives even to *perfect charity,* or even to *peace,* or even to *wisdom.* — Of the first six degrees, by which one arrives to *charity,* in the second of Peter, the first chapter[^p142-10]: « Minister in your faith, virtue; and in virtue, knowledge; and in knowledge, abstinence; and in abstinence, patience; and in patience, piety; and in piety, love of brotherhood; and in love of brotherhood, charity ». And those six degrees rightly correspond to these combinations, if the interpretations of the names are attended to. — Of the degrees by which one arrives at *peace,* Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p142-11]: « Blessed are the
<!-- page 143 -->
poor » etc. — But of the degrees by which one arrives at *wisdom,* Isaias, the eleventh chapter[^p143-1]: « And the spirit of the Lord shall rest upon him, the spirit of wisdom and of understanding, the spirit of counsel and of fortitude, the spirit of knowledge and of piety. And the spirit of the fear of the Lord shall fill him »; where he begins from the highest. The most beautiful figure of these went before in the throne of Solomon, where[^p143-2] it is said that there were « six degrees », « and twelve little lions standing upon the six degrees on the one side and on the other ». By the *lion* is understood the authority of power, by the *six degrees* the six combinations answering most aptly to the spiritual degrees.

35. *Morally* too, through the names of the Apostles thus combined, there is indicated the perfection of prelates, especially of bishops, who ought to excel the rest in three things: in *life,* in *doctrine,* in *example* or good conversation. Whence the Apostle, instructing, says to Titus, the second chapter[^p143-3]: « But speak thou the things that become sound doctrine »; behold, *doctrine.* « Let no man despise thee »; behold, *life.* « In all things shew thyself an example of good works »; behold, *example* or good conversation. — The first four names, then, designate the *perfection of life;* four others the *perfection of doctrine;* but the other four the *perfection of example* or of good conversation.

36. He says, then: *Simon,* supply "he called", which is interpreted *obedient. Whom he surnamed Peter,* which is interpreted *acknowledging*[^p143-4]; in which is designated the virtue of *prudence,* by which Peter, that is, *acknowledging,* ought to be surnamed *from Simon;* because the surname of prudence ought to proceed from humility, so that, according to what is said in Proverbs, the twenty-third chapter[^p143-5], « let him set bounds to his prudence »; « and let him not lean upon his own prudence », Proverbs, the third chapter; and so he will not be « prudent in his own conceits », to the Romans, the twelfth chapter, but « will behave prudently in all things », according to what is said of David in the first of Kings, the eighteenth chapter. — *And Andrew,* which is interpreted *manly*[^p143-6]; in which is expressed the virtue of *justice.* Otherwise « seek not to be made a judge, unless thou have strength enough to extirpate iniquities », Ecclesiasticus, the seventh chapter. — *And James,* which is interpreted *supplanter* or wrestler[^p143-7], by whom is indicated the virtue of *fortitude,* which bravely attacks adversities and wrestles against *vices;* whence to the Ephesians, the sixth chapter: « Our wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but against principalities and powers, against the rulers of the world of this darkness, against the spirits of wickedness in the high places ». So the bishop ought to be « a lion, the strongest of beasts, who hath no fear of any thing he meeteth », Proverbs, the thirtieth chapter[^p143-8]; even against *God,* so that there may be said to him that which was said to Jacob, Genesis, the thirty-second chapter: « If thou hast been strong against God, how much more shalt thou prevail against men ». — *And John,* which is interpreted *in whom is grace*[^p143-9]; by whom is understood the virtue of *temperance* or continence, which is the grace of God, according to that of Wisdom, the eighth chapter: « I cannot be continent, except God give it », especially because « he was chosen a virgin by the Lord and remained a virgin for ever »; and Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-sixth chapter: « Grace upon grace is a holy and shamefaced woman ». — According to the combination of these four virtues Ezechiel saw the likeness « of four living creatures », « and the hands of a man under their wings on the four sides, and they had faces and wings on the four sides », Ezechiel, the first chapter[^p143-10], in prefiguration of the four virtues, which
<!-- page 144 -->
are required for the government of prelates, because « than these nothing is more profitable to men in life », Wisdom, the eighth chapter.

37. There follows, concerning the apostolic names which pertain to *doctrine,* when he says: *Philip,* which is interpreted *mouth of a lamp*[^p144-1]; in which it is indicated that episcopal doctrine ought to be *lucid* as to the *intellect of the hearers,* so that there may be « their appearance like the appearance of lamps », Ezechiel, the first chapter; so that there may be set « a lamp upon the candlestick », Zacharias, the fourth chapter, not « under a bushel », below, the eleventh chapter; but as is said of Elias, Ecclesiasticus, the forty-eighth chapter: « His word burnt like a torch ». — *And Bartholomew,* which is interpreted *son of him that suspends the waters*[^p144-2] », in which is understood *devout* doctrine as to the *affection,* so that out of devotion it may suspend and raise tears even to the eye, of which the Lord says to Job, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p144-3]: « Canst thou lift up thy voice in the cloud »? namely in devotion or compunction in teaching; « and shall an abundance of waters cover thee »? in weeping; which the Lord does, who « at his voice giveth a multitude of waters in the heaven, and lifteth up the clouds from the ends of the earth », Jeremias, the tenth chapter; and « he maketh lightnings for rain », as the Psalm says, that is, threatenings.

38. (Vers. 15.). *Matthew,* which is interpreted *given*[^p144-4], by whom it is indicated that doctrine ought to be *edifying unto working.* For after devotion has been had, it is needful that one fulfil the salutary hearing by good working; this is especially from him from whom is « every best gift, and every perfect gift », James, the first chapter. He confers on the word of the preacher the power or grace of preaching and of edification; whence in Isaias, the forty-first chapter: « The first shall say to Sion: Behold, I am here, and to Jerusalem I will give an evangelist ». — *And Thomas,* which is interpreted *abyss*[^p144-5], by which is understood the *depth of erudition* in the things to be believed, so that there may be « his counsel in the great abyss », as is said in Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter. Whence it is asked by Job, the thirty-eighth chapter: « Hast thou walked in the lowest parts of the abyss »? « O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and of the knowledge of God! How incomprehensible are his judgments »! because « his judgments are a great abyss », to the Romans, the eleventh chapter, and the Psalm. — And these four pertain to *doctrine,* so that thus the four wheels « may go by their four parts, and turn not when they go », Ezechiel, the tenth chapter[^p144-6].

39. There follows, concerning those names which pertain to *example: James,* which is interpreted *wrestler, of Alpheus,* which is interpreted *fugitive*[^p144-7]; in which is expressed the example of *perfect poverty.* Whence Gregory: « Let him who hastens to the contest with the devil cast off his garments, lest he succumb. But what are all earthly things but certain clothings of the body »? Whence one who wishes to wrestle in poverty must flee temporal things, according to what the Psalm says[^p144-8]: « Lo, I have gone far off flying away; and I abode in the wilderness ». So Elias, in figure of this, fled from the face of Jezabel, which is interpreted *flowing of a dunghill;* by which is designated the fluxibility of temporal things. — *And Simon,* which is interpreted *obedient, who is called Zelotes*[^p144-9], that is, *emulator;* in which is expressed the example of *perfect humility* with the emulation of ordered charity; whence in the first of Peter, the first chapter: « Purifying your souls in the obedience of charity, with a brotherly love, from a sincere heart love one another earnestly »; Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter: « The sons of wisdom, the church of the just, and their generation, obedience and love ».
<!-- page 145 -->

40. (Vers. 16.). *Jude*[^p145-1], which is interpreted *confessing, of James,* so that he may be for an example of *strenuousness in acting;* Matthew, the fifth chapter: « Let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven ». Hence Tobias, the twelfth chapter[^p145-2]: « Bless ye the Lord of heaven, and confess to him in the sight of all that live ». Hence it is that in the blessing of Juda it is said, Deuteronomy, the thirty-third chapter: « His hands shall fight for him, and his help shall be against his enemies »; whence he went up before the others into battle, Judges, the first chapter. — *And Judas Iscariot*[^p145-3], which is interpreted *memory of death;* in which is understood the example of the *mortification of the flesh.* Whence Gregory: « Nothing avails so much to tame the vices of the flesh as to think what our flesh will be after death ». And this they ought to do, « lest, when they have preached to others, they themselves become castaways », according to what the Apostle says, the first to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter[^p145-4]: « I chastise my body » etc.; on account of which it is said below, the twelfth chapter: « Let your loins be girt » etc. But since many are deceived and betrayed on the occasion of the mortified flesh, because they glory in it, like hypocrites, who « confess with the mouth that they know God, and in deed deny him », according to what is said to Titus, the first chapter[^p145-5]; let them beware lest they be reprobated through *betrayal* of this kind, since here it is added: *Who was the traitor.* Hence it is that the Apostle says, the second to Timothy, the third chapter[^p145-6], that « in the last times shall come dangerous times, and men shall be lovers of themselves »; and there follows: « Traitors, stubborn, envious, lovers of pleasures more than of God, having an appearance indeed of godliness, but denying the power thereof ». — And these pertain to *example,* and these are the four bearers of the paralytic, above, the fifth chapter[^p145-7]. And in this manner the mystery of the apostolic names is designated; and so this part is ended.

41. (Vers. 17.). *And coming down with them* etc. After the separation of the disciples the Evangelist here subjoins the *attraction of the hearers*[^p145-8], which the Evangelist describes as to four things: as to the *disciples accompanying,* as to the *peoples running together,* as to the *causes inciting* and as to the *effects following.*

As to the *disciples accompanying* he says: *And coming down,* from the mountain, *with them,* that is, with the twelve already chosen, who always accompanied him, according to what is said below, in the twenty-second chapter[^p145-9]: « You are they who have continued with me in my temptations »; *he stood in a plain place,* that is, level and common, so that all might be able to come to hear wisdom, so that they might be able to say: « Behold we have heard him in Ephrata: we have found him in the fields of the wood ». And thus was fulfilled that of Proverbs, the first chapter: « Wisdom preacheth abroad, she uttereth her voice in the streets, at the head of multitudes she crieth out ». — At the cry of this wisdom the disciples especially were attracted; whence he adds: *And the multitude of his disciples,* namely stood with him. This he says as to the other disciples, inferior to the Apostles. For to the perfect disciples he had made the sermon on the top of the mountain, as is said in Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p145-10]: « Jesus seeing the multitudes, went up into a mountain, and when he was set down, his disciples came unto him ». But condescending to these as to the imperfect, he made the sermon in the descent from the mountain; whence they are called a *multitude,* because they were many, and near also to the multitude. For the disciples of Christ were multiplied, as is said in John, the fourth chapter[^p145-11]: « The Pharisees heard that Jesus maketh more disciples than John ». To these secrets were not to be communicated, because they were scandalized by the hearing of secrets; whence it is said in John, the sixth chapter, that from the word which he had said, « many of his disciples went back, and walked no more with him. Then Jesus said to the twelve: Will you also go away »? namely with these disciples.

42. Jesus is said to *stand,* but with the other perfect ones to *sit;* in which he shows that « we that are stronger ought to bear the infirmities of the weak, and not to please ourselves », to the Romans, the fifteenth chapter[^p145-12]. — *And*
<!-- page 146 -->
*he came down with them,* for an example of humility, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-second chapter[^p146-1]: « Have they made thee ruler? Be not lifted up; be among them as one of them ». Whence by his kindness he attracted and gathered together all, as a hen her chickens; Matthew, the twenty-third chapter: « How often would I have gathered together thy children, as the hen doth gather her chickens » etc. And not undeservedly, because it was said of him, Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter: « He found him in a desert land, in a place of horror, and of vast wilderness; he led him about, and taught him, and he kept him as the apple of his eye. As the eagle enticing her young to fly, he spread his wings over them » etc.

43. As to the *peoples running together* it is added: *And a very great multitude of people,* namely stood with him, who, namely, had come together from divers places; they had come together both from *places near,* as to which he says: *From all Judea and Samaria and Jerusalem,* which were within the kingdom of Israel; *Judea* is the name of the kingdom, *Samaria* the principal city in the kingdom of the ten tribes, and *Jerusalem* the principal in the kingdom of the two[^p146-2]; from all these they flowed together to Christ, according to that of Isaias, the second chapter: « In the last days the mountain of the house of the Lord shall be prepared on the top of mountains, and it shall be exalted above the hills, and all nations shall flow unto it »; and also from *places remote,* as to which he says: *And of the sea coast, and of Tyre and of Sidon.* And it ought to be repeated: a very great multitude of people *from the sea coast,* and a very great multitude *of Tyre and of Sidon,* that is, from Tyre and Sidon. And these were *remote* places, as Bede says in the Gloss[^p146-3], that « the *sea coast* is named not from the nearby sea of Galilee, but from the great sea »; Isaias, the sixtieth chapter: « Then shalt thou see, and abound, and thy heart shall wonder and be enlarged, when the multitude of the sea shall be converted to thee, the strength of the Gentiles shall come to thee ». — And this was prefigured in Solomon, the third of Kings, the tenth chapter[^p146-4], where it is said that « all desired to see the face of Solomon, and to hear his wisdom ».

44. (Vers. 18.). As to the *causes inciting* there is subjoined: *Who were come to hear him,* as to the *enlightening of truth* in the mind, *and to be healed of their infirmities*[^p146-5], as to the *curing unto health* in the flesh. And this was a right intention, according to that of Osee, the sixth chapter: « Come, and let us return to the Lord, for he hath taken us, and he will heal us »; and after: « And we shall live in his sight, we shall know, and we shall follow on, that we may know the Lord ». In this these showed that they were of the sheep of Christ, because they ran together to him to receive truth and health; John, the tenth chapter[^p146-6]: « My sheep shall hear my voice, and I know them, and they shall follow me, and I give them life everlasting ». The love, therefore, of truth to be understood and of health to be obtained attracted them, so that they came to Christ, as to a master and as to a physician. To the first the Saviour invites in the Psalm[^p146-7]: « Come, children, hearken to me; I will teach you the fear of the Lord »; to the second, in Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « Come to me, all you that labour and are burdened, and I will refresh you ».

45. As to the *effects following* it is further added: *And they that were vexed with unclean spirits, were cured;* in which is touched the efficacy of power in respect of unclean spirits; Zacharias, the thirteenth chapter[^p146-8]: « I will take away the unclean spirit out of the earth ». These unclean spirits vex those whom they possess; Matthew, the fifteenth chapter: « My daughter is grievously vexed by a devil ». This vexation compelled them to flee to Christ, because, according to what is said in Isaias, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p146-9], only « vexation shall give understanding to the hearing »; but according to what Gregory says, « the evils which press us here compel us to go to God ». Surely, while they compel to God, they lead to a good disposition, according to that of Wisdom, the third chapter: « Vexed in few things, in many they shall be well disposed ».

46. (Vers. 19.). Nor was the efficacy only upon the soul, but also upon the body, through the body of Christ himself; which is noted when he adds: *And all the multitude sought to touch him,* namely out of faith and devotion, as that woman of whom Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p146-10]: « If I shall touch the hem of his garment, I shall be healed ». — And deservedly were they devout; whence it is added: *For power went out from him, and healed all,* that is, the working of power; below, the eighth chapter[^p146-11]: « I know that power is gone out from me ». Then *power goes out,* when, lying hid in *itself,* it is manifested in *work,* according to that of Wisdom, the twelfth chapter:
<!-- page 147 -->
« Thou shewest thy power, who art not believed to be absolute in power ». Then too it *goes out,* when it *communicates itself to others,* according to that of Isaias, the fortieth chapter: « Who giveth power to the weary, and increaseth force and might to them that are not ». And *great* was this power, which availed upon all, according to that of Wisdom, the fourteenth chapter[^p147-1]: « Thou art able to heal out of all things »; and the sixteenth chapter: « He that turned to it, was not healed by that which he saw, but by thee, the Saviour of all ». And this especially attracted all to Christ; whence it is said in John, the sixth chapter: « And a great multitude followed him, because they saw the miracles which he did on them that were diseased ».

47. *Spiritually,* note here that the hearers who come to Christ are attracted from six places, namely from three of the *Jews* and three of the *Gentiles.* By the places of the *Jews* the just are signified, by the places of the *Gentiles* sinners. — Now three places of the *Jews* are set down according to the threefold state of the *just.* For *Judea* is interpreted *confessing*[^p147-2] and signifies the state of the *active,* who confess Christ by good works; Osee, the tenth chapter: « Juda shall plough, and Jacob shall break his furrows for himself »; Tobias, the thirteenth chapter: « Confess to him, and extol the eternal King in your works ». — *Samaria,* which is interpreted *keeping*[^p147-3], signifies the state of *prelates.* For to them it is said, the third of Kings, the twentieth chapter: « Keep this man; and if he shall slip away, thy life shall be for his life ». — *Jerusalem,* which is interpreted *vision of peace*[^p147-4], signifies the state of the *contemplative;* Isaias, the sixtieth chapter: « Arise, be enlightened, O Jerusalem, for thy light is come »; in Tobias, the thirteenth chapter, it is said of Jerusalem: « Blessed are all they that love thee, and that rejoice in thy peace ».

48. Three places of the *Gentiles* too are set down according to the threefold state of *sinners.* For the *sea coast,* which is windy and frequently turbulent, signifies the vanity and turbulence of the proud; Isaias, the fifty-seventh chapter[^p147-5]: « The wicked are like the raging sea, which cannot rest ». — *Tyre,* which is interpreted *straitness*[^p147-6], signifies the anxiety and straitness of the *covetous;* Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-seventh chapter: « Between buying and selling the sinner shall be straitened »; Ecclesiasticus, the fifth chapter: « Be not anxious for unjust riches ». — *Sidon,* which is interpreted *hunting*[^p147-7], signifies the desire and concupiscence of the *carnal;* whence in the Psalm: « They shall return at evening, and shall suffer hunger like dogs, and shall go round about the city ». — All these run together to Christ, but the *just* as to a *master,* to be taught wisdom, because, according to that of Proverbs, the ninth chapter[^p147-8], « teach a just man, and he shall make haste to receive it »; and Deuteronomy, the last chapter but one: « They that approach to his feet shall receive of his doctrine ». But *sinners* as to a *friend,* to obtain mercy; below, the fifteenth chapter[^p147-9]: « The publicans and sinners were drawing near unto Jesus to hear him »; from whom first the devils are cast out through the *expulsion of fault;* secondly they are healed in the touch through the *cure of the consequence,* according to what is said in the Psalm: « Who forgiveth all thy iniquities, who healeth all thy diseases ».
