## Confutatur fallacia simulantium per duo.

## Latin
<!-- page 300 -->

#### Confutatur fallacia simulantium per duo.

70. *Nemo accendit lucernam* etc. Postquam Salvator confutavit fallaciam blasphematorum et incredulorum sive tentatorum, in hac parte confutat *fallaciam simulatorum*[^p300-2]. Habet autem pars ista duas partes, in quarum prima *commendat simplicitatem intentionis rectae;* in secunda vero *confutat dolositatem simulationis malignae,* ibi[^p300-3]: *Et cum loqueretur, rogavit eum* etc.

Ad *commendandam* igitur *simplicitatem rectae intentionis* primo tria introducuntur, scilicet *similitudo rationalis, adaptatio similitudinis* et *informatio commendabilis.*

71. (Vers. 33.). Primo igitur introducitur *similitudo rationalis,* cum dicitur: *Nemo lucernam accendit et in abscondito ponit neque sub modio*[^p300-4]. *Lucerna* dicitur vehiculum luminis sive vas luminis contentivum, quae sive materialis sit, sive spiritualis, non est abscondenda. Lucerna enim materialis abscondita lumen non tribuit, sed est prorsus inutilis, similiter nec spiritualis; unde Ecclesiastici vigesimo[^p300-5]: « Sapientia abscondita et thesaurus invisus, quae utilitas in utrisque? Melior est vir, qui celat insipientiam suam, quam qui abscondit sapientiam suam ». Et ideo haec lucerna non debet abscondi, sed manifestari. — Unde subdit: *Sed supra candelabrum,* scilicet ponit, *ut qui ingrediuntur lumen videant.* Quanto autem lucernae altius ponuntur, tanto clarius resplendent; et ideo *supra candelabrum* sicut supra locum excelsum debent poni, iuxta visionem Zachariae quarto[^p300-6]: « Vidi, et ecce, candelabrum aureum et septem lucernae eius super illud ».

72. (Vers. 34.). Secundo vero adiungitur *adaptatio similitudinis,* cum dicitur: *Lucerna corporis tui est oculus tuus. Corporis* namque *materialis* oculus corporalis est lucerna, quia totum corpus regit, dirigit et illuminat; *corporis* vero *spiritualis,* id est congeriei bonorum operum, lucerna est oculus spiritualis, id est intentio[^p300-7]. Intentio namque dicitur *oculus* et *lucerna: oculus,* in quantum illuminatur a Deo ad totius hominis directionem; *lucerna* vero, in quantum omnem actionem, cogitationem et affectionem dirigit; quasi gressus hominis « dirigit in viam pacis », secundum illud supra primo[^p300-8]: « Illuminare his qui in tenebris et in umbra mortis sedent » etc. — Lucernae enim est illuminare, et propterea *verbum Dei* dicitur *lucerna,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p300-9]: « Lucerna pedibus meis verbum tuum et lumen semitis meis », quantum ad *credenda;* secundae Petri primo: « Habemus firmiorem propheticum sermonem, cui bene facitis attendentes sicut lucernae lucenti in caliginoso loco »; et quantum ad *agenda* per mandata; Proverbiorum sexto: « Mandatum lucerna est, et lex lux ». *Lucerna* dicitur *praedicator;* Ioannis quinto[^p300-10]: « Ipse erat lucerna ardens et lucens »; et ad Philippenses secundo: « Inter quos lucetis sicut luminaria in mundo ». Dicitur *donum Spiritus sancti;* Exodi vigesimo quinto[^p300-11]: « Facies et lucernas septem et pones eas super candelabrum », id est dona septem Spiritus sancti super Christum. Dicitur etiam *opus;* infra duodecimo[^p300-12]: « Sint lumbi vestri praecincti, et lucernae ardentes in manibus vestris ». Dicitur etiam *intentio,* quae, si est *ficta,* exstinguitur, secundum illud Iob decimo octavo[^p300-13]: « Lux obtenebrescet in tabernaculo eius, et lucerna, quae super eum est, exstinguetur »; si autem *recta* est, totum illuminat
<!-- page 301 -->
bonorum operum corpus. — Unde *lucerna* primo competit *verbo Dei* et *dono Spiritus sancti,* et per hoc competit *intentioni cordis,* et inde descendit in[^p301-1] *eloquium* praedicationis verae et in *opus* conversationis sanctae; unde dicitur de his secundum quoddam genus analogiae. Est ergo *lucerna intentionis rectae* quasi media, suscipiens a superiori et redundans in totam congeriem operum.

73. Propter quod subdit: *Si oculus tuus fuerit simplex,* id est intentio recta, *totum corpus tuum lucidum erit.* Ut enim dicit Ambrosius[^p301-2]: « Intentio operi tuo nomen imponit »; ex quo apparet, quod de *oculo cordis* intelligit, cuius optima dispositio est *simplicitas,* secundum illud primi Paralipomenon ultimo: « Scio, Domine, quod probas corda et simplicitatem diligis ». Dicitur autem oculus *simplex,* qui unicum bonum et summum, quod indivisibile est, intendit; et talis solus est *rectus,* qui tendit ad illud. Unde de Iob dicitur, primo[^p301-3], quod « erat vir ille simplex et rectus »; et talis diligit Deum, secundum illud Canticorum primo: « Recti diligunt te »; et Canticorum quarto: « Vulnerasti cor meum, soror, sponsa, in uno oculorum tuorum et in uno crine colli tui », id est in unitate et simplicitate intentionis rectae, quae sola Deo placet. Et quia tales, quidquid faciunt, *ex caritate faciunt;* ideo totum rectum est et bonum et lucidum, secundum illud ad Ephesios quinto[^p301-4]: « Ut filii lucis ambulate. Fructus autem lucis est in omni bonitate et iustitia et veritate »; et quoniam de corde sic illuminato *non procedunt nisi opera lucis,* sicut « non potest arbor bona fructus malos facere », nec lucida obscuros, nec econtra arbor mala fructus bonos.

74. Et ideo subditur: *Si autem nequam fuerit*[^p301-5]*, etiam totum corpus tuum tenebrosum erit.* Oculus enim nequam depravat voluntatem et facit eam iniquam; illa autem depravata, depravatur et opus. Unde Ecclesiastici decimo quarto[^p301-6]: « Nequam est oculus lividi et avertens faciem », scilicet a Deo, « et despiciens animam suam »; et paulo post: « Oculus malus ad mala »; unde cum depravatur intentio, deviat a via lucis et incedit per tenebras, secundum illud Ecclesiastici undecimo: « Error et tenebrae peccatoribus concreatae sunt ». Cum autem obtenebratur intentio et deformatur, obtenebratur et deformatur opus. Unde in Glossa[^p301-7]: « Si perversa intentio praecessit, pravum opus sequitur, etiam si iustum videatur ». Et ideo dicitur *corpus tenebrosum,* quod quidem dicitur peccati opus, de quali habetur ad Romanos decimo tertio: « Abiiciamus opera tenebrarum ». — In hoc igitur aptatur similitudo lucernae, ut, sicut lucerna super candelabrum est luminosa, sub modio tenebrosa; et ideo nullus eam accendens « ponit sub modio, sed super candelabrum »: sic intentio simplex et sursum directa est lucida, deorsum est curva, nequam et obscura. Non ergo debet quis in suis operibus procedere cum duplicitate et simulatione hypocrisis, sed magis cum luce et simplicitate intentionis. Nam Proverbiorum undecimo[^p301-8]: « Iustitia simplicis diriget viam eius, et in impietate sua corruet impius ».

75. (Vers. 35.). Tertio subditur *informatio commendabilis,* cum dicit: *Vide ergo, ne lumen, quod in te est, tenebrae sint,* id est, ne intentio, per quam debes esse lux, vitiorum tenebris obscuretur. Loquitur autem conversis ad se, de quibus ait Apostolus ad Ephesios quinto[^p301-9]: « Eratis aliquando tenebrae, nunc autem lux in Domino; ut filii lucis ambulate », ut sitis *filii illius lucis,* « quae illuminat omnem hominem venientem in hunc mundum », Ioannis primo; non *filii hominis,* de quibus Ioannis tertio: « Lux venit in mundum, et dilexerunt homines magis tenebras quam lucem; erant enim mala opera eorum ». Quoniam ergo prava intentio totum opus depravat, ideo docet, eam praecipue esse cavendam, cum dicit: *Vide* etc.

76. (Vers. 36.). Sed rursus, quia *intentio* non potest esse lucida[^p301-10], nisi adsit *operatio* bona; ideo subdit: *Si ergo corpus tuum totum lucidum fuerit, non habens aliquam partem tenebrarum,* ut videlicet non sit pravitas in intentione nec cogitatione nec affectione nec locutione nec operatione, quae
<!-- page 302 -->
sunt partes totalitatis nostrae et operationis meritoriae sive demeritoriae. — Si igitur in his non fuerit ex aliqua parte impuritas[^p302-1], erit verum illud quod sequitur: *Erit lucidum totum, et sicut lucerna fulgoris illuminabit te,* id est *conscientiam tuam,* secundum petitionem propheticam: « Quoniam tu illuminas lucernam meam, Domine; Deus meus, illumina tenebras meas », secundum quod habetur in Psalmo[^p302-2]. Illuminabit etiam te *ad aedificandum Christi Ecclesiam,* secundum illud mandatum Domini Matthaei quinto: « Sic luceat lux vestra coram hominibus, ut videant opera vestra bona et glorificent Patrem vestrum, qui in caelis est ». Illuminabit etiam te *ad confutandam pravitatem alienam,* secundum illud ad Philippenses secundo[^p302-3]: « Sitis sinceres in medio nationis pravae et perversae, inter quos lucetis sicut luminaria in mundo, verbum vitae continentes ». Illuminabit etiam te *ad habendam divinam notitiam,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici secundo[^p302-4]: « Qui timetis Dominum diligite illum, et illuminabuntur corda vestra ». Illuminabit etiam te *ad videndam claritatem divinam;* Tobiae decimo tertio: « Luce splendida fulgebis » etc.

77. *Et cum loqueretur, rogavit eum*[^p302-5] etc. Postquam Evangelista commendavit simplicitatem intentionis rectae, in hac parte *confutat dolositatem simulationis malignae.* Habet autem pars ista partes duas. In quarum prima praemittitur *increpatio duplicitatis;* in secunda subditur *comminatio calamitatis,* ibi[^p302-6]: *Sed vae vobis Pharisaeis* etc. — Circa *increpationem duplicitatis* introducuntur tria: primum est *Iudaicae simulationis malignitas,* secundum est *increpationis severitas,* tertium vero *caritativae admonitionis suavitas.*

78. (Vers. 37.). Primo igitur quantum ad *simulationis malignitatem* ex parte Pharisaei dicit: *Et cum loqueretur, rogavit eum quidam Pharisaeus, ut pranderet apud se.* In quo facto exterius praetendebat pietatem ad relevandam Christi indigentiam, de qua ipse dicit in Psalmo[^p302-7]: « Ego autem mendicus sum et pauper »; ad quam relevandam Christus eleemosynas accepit, nec tantum accepit, sed etiam mendicavit, cum adhuc esset duodennis, sicut dicit Bernardus in quodam Sermone[^p302-8]: « Cum factus esset Iesus » etc.; ubi quaerit, quis eum in illo triduo cibaverit, et dicit sic: « Ut te per omnia nostrae conformares paupertati et omnes vitae humanae calamitates susciperes, quasi unus ex turba pauperum stipem per ostia mendicabas. Quis dabit, me buccellarum illarum mendicatarum participem fieri, vel illius divini edulii reliquiis saginari »? Unde Glossa super illud secundae ad Corinthios octavo[^p302-9]: « Pro vobis egenus factus est » etc.: « Non erubescas cum tua mendicitate ad illum accedere, qui pro nobis factus est pauper ». Unde ad suam indigentiam relevandam non solum a bonis, sed etiam a malis accipiebat eleemosynam; unde subditur: *Et ingressus recubuit.* — In quo dat nobis formam, quod peccatores occulti non sunt vitandi; et quod a malis eleemosynae licite accipi possunt, ut fiant boni, dummodo non dent de alienis vel male acquisitis[^p302-10]; et quod peccatores sunt per benevolentiam attrahendi; et quod inimicis et detractoribus nostris debemus esse benevoli, sicut ille qui cum illo recubuit, a quo sciebat in corde se despici et iudicari.

79. (Vers. 38.). Propter quod subditur: *Pharisaeus autem coepit, intra se reputans, dicere,* id est, rationem et causam inquirere, scilicet, *quare non esset baptizatus,* id est lavatus more Iudaeorum, *ante prandium,* ac per hoc ipsum iudicare tanquam immundum et contemptorem paternarum traditionum. Unde similiter leguntur Pharisaei Domino et eius discipulis obiecisse Matthaei decimo quinto[^p302-11]: « Quare discipuli tui transgrediuntur traditionem seniorum? Non enim lavant manus suas, cum manducant ». Hoc autem erat apud Iudaeos praecipuum; unde dicitur Marci septimo[^p302-12]: « Pharisaei autem et omnes Iudaei crebro lavant manus, cum manducant, tenentes traditionem seniorum, et a foro, nisi baptizentur, non comedunt ». Et ratio huius erat, quia maximam vim faciebant in servanda munditia corporum. Unde duplex ostenditur ex hoc simulatio in Pharisaeo: primo, quia eum quem pie reficiebat exterius, impie iudicabat interius; secundo, quoniam zelabat munditiam
<!-- page 303 -->
corporalem nec attendebat spiritualem. Et ideo hypocrita erat tam in conspectu hominum quam Dei, contra illud Ecclesiastici primo[^p303-1]: « Ne accesseris ad illum duplici corde, et ne fueris hypocrita in conspectu hominum ».

80. (Vers. 39.). Secundo quantum ad *increpationis severitatem* ex parte Magistri subdit: *Et ait Dominus ad illum: Nunc vos, Pharisaei, quod deforis est calicis et catini mundatis. Calix* est vas ad bibendum, *catinum* ad manducandum; et haec vasa maxime mundabant Pharisaei et Iudaei, quia se putabant inquinari per immunditiam vasorum. Unde Marci septimo[^p303-2] dicitur, quod « multa sunt illis tradita servare: baptismata calicum et urceorum et aeramentorum ». Quia vero, ut dicit Ambrosius[^p303-3], « calix est vas vitreum, et catinum vas fictile »; et quia utrumque fragile, recte designat fragilitatem humani corporis, circa quam Pharisaei erant potissime intenti, neglecta munditia conscientiae; et hoc erat reprehensibile. Nam dicitur Isaiae primo[^p303-4]: « Lavamini, mundi estote, auferte malum cogitationum vestrarum ab oculis meis »; et Ieremiae quarto: « Lava a malitia cor tuum, Ierusalem, ut salva fias »; quod Pharisaei non observabant. — Propter quod addit: *Quod autem intus est vestrum plenum est rapina et iniquitate,* ac per hoc immundum et abominabile. Unde de eis poterat dici illud Michaeae sexto[^p303-5]: « Divites eius repleti sunt iniquitate, et habitantes in ea loquebantur mendacium »; Ecclesiastici primo: « Accessisti maligne ad Dominum, et cor tuum plenum est dolo et fallacia »; quia non habet intra quod simulat extra. Et ista erat non tantum *malitia,* sed etiam *stultitia,* quia, neglecto meliori, de peiore tantum curabant.

81. (Vers. 40.). Unde subditur: *Stulti, nonne qui fecit quod de foris est,* scilicet corpus, *etiam*[^p303-6] *quod de intus est fecit?* scilicet spiritum. In quo simul damnatur *haeresis Manichaeorum,* qui Deo auctori tribuunt spiritum et diabolo corpus; cum tamen dicatur Genesis secundo, quod « formavit Dominus Deus hominem de limo terrae et inspiravit in faciem eius spiraculum vitae ». Confutatur etiam *hypocrisis Pharisaeorum,* qui Deo satagebant placere solius corporis purificatione, cum tamen Dominus fecerit utrumque, et maxime sit conditor animae, secundum illud Ecclesiastae ultimo[^p303-7]: « Spiritus redeat ad Deum, qui dedit illum »; et Dominus praecipue se vocat *Deum animarum;* Ezechielis decimo octavo: « Meae sunt omnes animae, dicit Dominus, ut anima patris, ita et anima filii ». Et propter hoc maxime vult animarum munditiam; et tali promittit suam praesentiam, secundum illud Matthaei quinto: « Beati mundo corde, quoniam ipsi Deum videbunt »; et ideo Pharisaei, qui de hac non curabant, ut stulti erant. Unde Chrysostomus[^p303-8]: « Deus neque corporum munditiam laudat neque sordes condemnat. Pone tamen, quod sordes odit vasorum, quanto magis conscientiae sordes odit »? Unde de hac praecipue curandum, quia sine munditia conscientiae nihil est mundum; ad Titum primo[^p303-9]: « Omnia munda mundis, coinquinatis autem et infidelibus nihil est mundum, sed inquinatae sunt eorum mens et conscientia ».

82. (Vers. 41.). Tertio quantum ad *admonitionis suavitatem* ex parte medici subiungit: *Verumtamen, quod superest, date eleemosynam; quod superest,* hoc est, remedium, quod restat, est, ut per *eleemosynam purgetis nequitiam.* « Secunda enim tabula, quae restat post naufragium, est poenitentia[^p303-10] ». — Et hoc modo accipitur hic *eleemosyna;* quod patet in hoc quod sequitur: *Et*[^p303-11] *omnia munda sunt vobis.* Impossibile est, omnia mundari per solius *exterioris eleemosynae* erogationem; sed vocatur hic donatio eleemosynae *quodlibet opus misericordiae ordinatae,* quae primo incipit a se, secundum illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo[^p303-12]: « Miserere animae tuae, placens Deo ». Hanc misericordiam sibi impendit, cum se per poenitentiam purgat a vitiis; nam aliter crudelis est sibi, secundum illud Psalmi: « Qui diligit
<!-- page 304 -->
iniquitatem odit animam suam ». — Aliter potest exponi de eleemosyna *proprie dicta,* ut sit sensus: *Quod superest date eleemosynam,* id est, de eo quod superest, ut sic tangatur eleemosynae *forma,* secundum illud secundae ad Corinthios octavo[^p304-1]: « Non ut aliis sit remissio, vobis autem tribulatio ». Et post formam subditur *efficacia,* cum ait: *Et*[^p304-2] *omnia munda sunt vobis;* quia eleemosyna disponit ad gratiam, per quam mundatur anima tota; et propter hoc dicitur primae ad Timotheum quarto: « Pietas valet ad omnia, promissionem habens vitae, quae nunc est et futurae ».

83. Licet autem *eleemosyna* dicatur *valere ad tria* principaliter, tamen *septiformis* ostenditur eius efficacia in Scriptura. Prima est, quia animam *mundat a culpa,* sicut hic habetur; et Ecclesiastici tertio[^p304-3]: « Eleemosyna patris non erit in oblivione »; et paulo post: « Et sicut in sereno glacies, solventur peccata tua ». — Secunda est, quia *liberat a poena aeterna;* Tobiae quarto[^p304-4]: « Eleemosyna ab omni peccato et a morte liberat et non patitur, animam ire in tenebras ». — Tertia, quia *defendit a poena transitoria;* Danielis quarto: « Peccata tua eleemosynis redime » etc. — Quarta, quia *mitigat fomitis concupiscentiam;* Ecclesiastici tertio[^p304-5]: « Ignem ardentem exstinguit aqua, et eleemosyna resistit peccatis ». — Quinta, quia *dat de hoste victoriam;* Ecclesiastici vigesimo nono: « Conclude eleemosynam in corde pauperis, et haec pro te exorabit »; et paulo post: « Super scutum potentis et super lanceam adversus inimicum tuum pugnabit ». — Sexta, quia *conservat gratiam,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici decimo septimo[^p304-6]: « Eleemosyna viri quasi sacculus cum ipso, et gratiam hominis quasi pupillam conservabit ». — Septima, quia *dat fiduciam perveniendi ad gloriam;* Tobiae quarto[^p304-7]: « Fiducia magna erit coram summo Deo eleemosyna facientibus ipsam ». — Hi septem effectus sunt ipsius eleemosynae, quae tam *corporaliter* quam *spiritualiter* exhibetur.

84. Opera *misericordiae corporalis* hoc versu designantur:

Visito, poto, cibo, redimo, tego, colligo, condo.

De quibus, praeterquam de ultimo, Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p304-8], disceptatur in iudicio; de ultimo vero dicitur Tobiae secundo. — Ad hoc autem, quod eleemosyna habeat has efficacias, necessaria est primo *simplicitas in corde;* unde Matthaei sexto[^p304-9]: « Te autem faciente eleemosynam, nesciat sinistra tua » etc. — Secundo vero, *discretio in electione,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici duodecimo: « Si benefeceris, scito, cui benefeceris ». — Tertio vero, *compassio in affectione;* Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo[^p304-10]: « Cum effuderis esurienti animam tuam, tunc orietur in tenebris lux tua »; et Iob trigesimo: « Flebam quondam super eo qui afflictus erat, et compatiebatur anima mea pauperi ». — Quarto vero, *dulcedo in sermone;* Ecclesiastici decimo octavo[^p304-11]: « Nonne ardorem refrigerabit ros? Sic et verbum melius quam datum ». — Quinto vero, *hilaritas in facie;* Ecclesiastici trigesimo quinto: « In omni dato hilarem fac vultum tuum »; Psalmus: « Iucundus homo, qui miseretur » etc. — Sexto vero, *debita quantitas in munere;* Tobiae quarto[^p304-12]: « Quomodo potueris, esto misericors; si multum tibi fuerit » etc. — Septimo vero, *celeritas in opere* et exhibitione; Proverbiorum tertio: « Ne dicas amico tuo: Vade et revertere » etc. — Et tunc fit eleemosyna ex toto homine, ac per hoc est Deo accepta et placens.

## English
<!-- page 300 -->

#### The deceit of the dissemblers is confuted by two things.

70. *No man lighteth a lamp* etc. After the Saviour has confuted the deceit of the blasphemers and of the unbelievers or tempters, in this part he confutes *the deceit of the dissemblers*[^p300-2]. Now this part has two parts, in the first of which *he commends the simplicity of right intention;* but in the second *he confutes the guile of malignant dissembling,* there[^p300-3]: *And as he was speaking, he prayed him* etc.

For *commending,* then, *the simplicity of right intention* three things are first introduced, namely *a reasonable similitude, the adaptation of the similitude* and *a commendable formation.*

71. (Vers. 33.). First, then, *the reasonable similitude* is introduced, when it is said: *No man lighteth a lamp and putteth it in a hidden place, nor under a bushel*[^p300-4]. *A lamp* is called the vehicle of the lumen[^tr-lumen], or the vessel containing the lumen, which, whether it be material or spiritual, is not to be hidden. For a material lamp hidden gives no lumen, but is altogether useless, and likewise neither does the spiritual; whence Ecclesiasticus, the twentieth chapter[^p300-5]: « Wisdom that is hid, and treasure that is not seen: what profit is there in them both? Better is the man that hideth his folly, than he that hideth his wisdom ». And therefore this lamp ought not to be hidden, but made manifest. — Whence he subjoins: *But upon a candlestick,* namely he setteth it, *that they who come in may see the lumen.* Now the higher lamps are set, the more clearly they shine; and therefore they ought to be set *upon a candlestick* as upon a lofty place, according to the vision of Zacharias, the fourth chapter[^p300-6]: « I saw, and behold, a golden candlestick, and the seven lamps thereof upon it ».

72. (Vers. 34.). But secondly *the adaptation of the similitude* is adjoined, when it is said: *The lamp of thy body is thy eye.* For of the *material body* the bodily eye is the lamp, because it rules, directs and enlightens the whole body; but of the *spiritual body,* that is, of the heap of good works, the lamp is the spiritual eye, that is, the intention[^p300-7]. For the intention is called *eye* and *lamp: eye,* inasmuch as it is enlightened by God for the direction of the whole man; but *lamp,* inasmuch as it directs every action, thought and affection; as it were a man's steps it « directs into the way of peace », according to that above, in the first chapter[^p300-8]: « To enlighten them that sit in darkness and in the shadow of death » etc. — For it belongs to a lamp to enlighten, and therefore *the word of God* is called *a lamp,* according to that of the Psalm[^p300-9]: « Thy word is a lamp to my feet, and a light to my paths », as to *the things to be believed;* the second of Peter, the first chapter: « We have the more firm prophetical word, whereunto you do well to attend, as to a lamp shining in a dark place »; and as to *the things to be done* through the commandments; Proverbs, the sixth chapter: « The commandment is a lamp, and the law a light ». *The preacher* is called *a lamp;* John, the fifth chapter[^p300-10]: « He was a burning and a shining lamp »; and to the Philippians, the second chapter: « Among whom you shine as lights in the world ». It is called *the gift of the Holy Spirit;* Exodus, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p300-11]: « Thou shalt make also seven lamps, and shalt set them upon the candlestick », that is, the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit upon Christ. It is called also *work;* below, in the twelfth chapter[^p300-12]: « Let your loins be girt, and lamps burning in your hands ». It is called also *intention,* which, if it is *feigned,* is put out, according to that of Job, the eighteenth chapter[^p300-13]: « The light shall be dark in his tabernacle, and the lamp that is over him shall be put out »; but if it is *right,* it enlightens the whole
<!-- page 301 -->
body of good works. — Whence *the lamp* belongs first to *the word of God* and to *the gift of the Holy Spirit,* and through this it belongs to *the intention of the heart,* and thence it descends into[^p301-1] *the utterance* of true preaching and into *the work* of holy conversation; whence it is said of these according to a certain kind of analogy. *The lamp of right intention,* therefore, is as it were a mean, receiving from what is above and overflowing into the whole heap of works.

73. On account of which he subjoins: *If thy eye be single,* that is, the right intention, *thy whole body will be lightsome.* For as Ambrose says[^p301-2]: « Intention imposes its name on thy work »; from which it appears that he means *the eye of the heart,* whose best disposition is *simplicity,* according to that of the first of Paralipomenon, the last chapter: « I know, O Lord, that thou provest hearts, and lovest simplicity ». Now that eye is called *single* which intends the one and highest good, which is indivisible; and such a one alone is *upright,* who tends towards it. Whence it is said of Job, in the first chapter[^p301-3], that « that man was simple and upright »; and such a one loves God, according to that of the Canticles, the first chapter: « The upright love thee »; and the Canticles, the fourth chapter: « Thou hast wounded my heart, my sister, my spouse, with one of thy eyes, and with one hair of thy neck », that is, in the unity and simplicity of right intention, which alone pleases God. And because such men, whatever they do, *do it out of charity;* therefore the whole is right and good and lightsome, according to that to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter[^p301-4]: « Walk as children of the light. For the fruit of the light is in all goodness, and justice, and truth »; and since from a heart so enlightened *there proceed only works of light,* just as « a good tree cannot bring forth evil fruit », nor a lightsome one dark fruit, nor on the other hand an evil tree good fruit.

74. And therefore there is subjoined: *But if it be wicked*[^p301-5]*, thy whole body also will be darksome.* For the wicked eye depraves the will and makes it unjust; and the will depraved, the work too is depraved. Whence Ecclesiasticus, the fourteenth chapter[^p301-6]: « The eye of the envious is wicked, and he turneth away his face », namely from God, « and despiseth his own soul »; and a little after: « An evil eye is towards evil things »; whence when the intention is depraved, it strays from the way of light and walks through darkness, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter: « Error and darkness are created with sinners ». But when the intention is darkened and deformed, the work too is darkened and deformed. Whence in the Gloss[^p301-7]: « If a perverse intention has gone before, a depraved work follows, even if it seem just ». And therefore it is called *a darksome body,* which indeed is called the work of sin, of which it is had to the Romans, the thirteenth chapter: « Let us cast off the works of darkness ». — In this, then, the similitude of the lamp is adapted, that, just as a lamp upon a candlestick is luminous and under a bushel darksome, and therefore no man lighting it « putteth it under a bushel, but upon a candlestick »: so the intention that is simple and directed upwards is lightsome, while downwards it is crooked, wicked and dark. Let no one, then, proceed in his works with the duplicity and dissembling of hypocrisy, but rather with the light and simplicity of intention. For Proverbs, the eleventh chapter[^p301-8]: « The justice of the simple shall direct his way, and the impious shall fall by his own impiety ».

75. (Vers. 35.). Thirdly *the commendable formation* is subjoined, when he says: *Take heed therefore, that the lumen which is in thee be not darkness,* that is, lest the intention, through which thou oughtest to be light, be obscured by the darkness of vices. Now he speaks to those converted to him, of whom the Apostle says to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter[^p301-9]: « For you were heretofore darkness, but now light in the Lord; walk as children of the light », that you may be *children of that light* « which enlighteneth every man that cometh into this world », John, the first chapter; not *children of men,* of whom John, the third chapter: « The light is come into the world, and men loved darkness rather than the light; for their works were evil ». Since, then, a depraved intention depraves the whole work, therefore he teaches that it is to be guarded against above all, when he says: *Take heed* etc.

76. (Vers. 36.). But again, because *the intention* cannot be lightsome[^p301-10] unless good *operation* be present; therefore he subjoins: *If then thy whole body be lightsome, having no part of darkness,* so namely that there be no depravity in intention, nor in thought, nor in affection, nor in speech, nor in operation, which
<!-- page 302 -->
are the parts of our totality and of meritorious or demeritorious operation. — If, then, there be no impurity in these in any part[^p302-1], that will be true which follows: *The whole shall be lightsome, and as a lamp of brightness shall enlighten thee,* that is, *thy conscience,* according to the prophetic petition: « For thou enlightenest my lamp, O Lord; my God, enlighten my darkness », according to what is had in the Psalm[^p302-2]. It shall enlighten thee also *for building up the Church of Christ,* according to that commandment of the Lord in Matthew, the fifth chapter: « So let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven ». It shall enlighten thee also *for confuting the depravity of others,* according to that to the Philippians, the second chapter[^p302-3]: « Be ye sincere in the midst of a crooked and perverse nation, among whom you shine as lights in the world, holding forth the word of life ». It shall enlighten thee also *for having divine knowledge,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the second chapter[^p302-4]: « Ye that fear the Lord, love him, and your hearts shall be enlightened ». It shall enlighten thee also *for seeing the divine brightness;* Tobias, the thirteenth chapter: « Thou shalt shine with a glorious light » etc.

77. *And as he was speaking, he prayed him*[^p302-5] etc. After the Evangelist has commended the simplicity of right intention, in this part *he confutes the guile of malignant dissembling.* Now this part has two parts. In the first of which is set first *the rebuke of duplicity;* in the second is subjoined *the threatening of calamity,* there[^p302-6]: *But woe to you, Pharisees* etc. — Concerning *the rebuke of duplicity* three things are introduced: the first is *the malignity of the Jewish dissembling,* the second is *the severity of the rebuke,* but the third *the sweetness of the charitable admonition.*

78. (Vers. 37.). First, then, as to *the malignity of the dissembling* on the part of the Pharisee he says: *And as he was speaking, a certain Pharisee prayed him, that he would dine with him.* In which deed he outwardly made a pretence of piety, for relieving the need of Christ, of which he himself says in the Psalm[^p302-7]: « But I am a beggar and poor »; for relieving which Christ received alms, and not only received but also begged, when he was still twelve years old, as Bernard says in a certain Sermon[^p302-8]: « When Jesus was » etc.; where he asks who fed him in those three days, and speaks thus: « That thou mightest conform thyself in all things to our poverty, and take upon thee all the calamities of human life, thou didst beg a dole from door to door as one of the crowd of the poor. Who will grant me to be made a partaker of those begged morsels, or to be fed on the leavings of that divine food »? Whence the Gloss on that of the second to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter[^p302-9]: « For you he became needy » etc.: « Be not ashamed to come with thy beggary to him who for us became poor ». Whence for relieving his need he received alms not only from the good but also from the wicked; whence there is subjoined: *And going in, he sat down to eat.* — In which he gives us a form: that hidden sinners are not to be shunned; and that alms may lawfully be received from the wicked, that they may become good, provided they give not of what is another's or ill gotten[^p302-10]; and that sinners are to be drawn by benevolence; and that we ought to be benevolent to our enemies and detractors, as he was who sat down at table with him by whom he knew he was despised and judged in heart.

79. (Vers. 38.). On account of which there is subjoined: *But the Pharisee began, thinking within himself, to say,* that is, to inquire into the reason and the cause, namely, *why he was not baptized,* that is, washed after the manner of the Jews, *before dinner,* and by this very thing to judge him as unclean and a despiser of the traditions of the fathers. Whence likewise the Pharisees are read to have objected to the Lord and his disciples in Matthew, the fifteenth chapter[^p302-11]: « Why do thy disciples transgress the tradition of the ancients? For they wash not their hands when they eat ». Now this was a chief thing among the Jews; whence it is said in Mark, the seventh chapter[^p302-12]: « For the Pharisees and all the Jews often wash their hands when they eat, holding the tradition of the ancients; and from the market, unless they be washed, they eat not ». And the reason of this was that they laid the greatest stress on keeping the cleanness of bodies. Whence a twofold dissembling is shown in the Pharisee from this: first, because him whom he refreshed piously outwardly he judged impiously inwardly; secondly, because he was zealous for bodily
<!-- page 303 -->
cleanness and did not attend to the spiritual. And therefore he was a hypocrite as much in the sight of men as of God, against that of Ecclesiasticus, the first chapter[^p303-1]: « Come not to him with a double heart, and be not a hypocrite in the sight of men ».

80. (Vers. 39.). Secondly, as to *the severity of the rebuke* on the part of the Master he subjoins: *And the Lord said to him: Now you Pharisees make clean the outside of the cup and of the platter. The cup* is a vessel for drinking, *the platter* for eating; and these vessels the Pharisees and the Jews cleansed most of all, because they thought themselves defiled through the uncleanness of vessels. Whence in Mark, the seventh chapter[^p303-2], it is said that « many things there are that have been delivered to them to observe: the washings of cups and of pots and of brazen vessels ». But because, as Ambrose says[^p303-3], « the cup is a glass vessel, and the platter an earthen vessel »; and because both are fragile, they rightly designate the fragility of the human body, about which the Pharisees were chiefly intent, the cleanness of conscience neglected; and this was reprehensible. For it is said in Isaias, the first chapter[^p303-4]: « Wash yourselves, be clean, take away the evil of your thoughts from my eyes »; and Jeremias, the fourth chapter: « Wash thy heart from wickedness, O Jerusalem, that thou mayst be saved »; which the Pharisees did not observe. — On account of which he adds: *But your inside is full of rapine and iniquity,* and by this unclean and abominable. Whence that of Micheas, the sixth chapter[^p303-5], could be said of them: « The rich men thereof are filled with iniquity, and the inhabitants thereof have spoken lies »; Ecclesiasticus, the first chapter: « Thou camest to the Lord wickedly, and thy heart is full of guile and deceit »; because he has not within what he feigns without. And this was not only *malice,* but also *folly,* because, the better neglected, they cared only for the worse.

81. (Vers. 40.). Whence there is subjoined: *Ye fools, did not he that made that which is without,* namely the body, *make also*[^p303-6] *that which is within?* namely the spirit. In which at once is condemned *the heresy of the Manichaeans,* who ascribe the spirit to God as author and the body to the devil; although it is said in Genesis, the second chapter, that « the Lord God formed man of the slime of the earth, and breathed into his face the breath of life ». There is confuted also *the hypocrisy of the Pharisees,* who strove to please God by the purification of the body alone, although the Lord made both, and is most of all the founder of the soul, according to that of Ecclesiastes, the last chapter[^p303-7]: « The spirit return to God, who gave it »; and the Lord calls himself above all *the God of souls;* Ezechiel, the eighteenth chapter: « All souls are mine, saith the Lord; as the soul of the father, so also the soul of the son ». And on account of this he wills most of all the cleanness of souls; and to such he promises his presence, according to that of Matthew, the fifth chapter: « Blessed are the clean of heart, for they shall see God »; and therefore the Pharisees, who cared not for this, were as fools. Whence Chrysostom[^p303-8]: « God neither praises the cleanness of bodies nor condemns their filth. Yet suppose that he hates the filth of vessels: how much more does he hate the filth of conscience »? Whence this is above all to be cared for, because without cleanness of conscience nothing is clean; to Titus, the first chapter[^p303-9]: « All things are clean to the clean; but to them that are defiled, and to unbelievers, nothing is clean, but both their mind and their conscience are defiled ».

82. (Vers. 41.). Thirdly, as to *the sweetness of the admonition* on the part of the physician he subjoins: *But yet that which remaineth, give alms; that which remaineth,* that is, the remedy that is left, is that by *alms you purge your wickedness.* « For the second plank that is left after shipwreck is penance[^p303-10] ». — And in this way *alms* is taken here; which is plain in that which follows: *And*[^p303-11] *all things are clean unto you.* It is impossible that all things be made clean through the bestowal of *outward alms* alone; but here the giving of alms is called *any work of ordered mercy,* which begins first from oneself, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirtieth chapter[^p303-12]: « Have pity on thy own soul, pleasing God ». This mercy a man spends on himself when he purges himself of vices through penance; for otherwise he is cruel to himself, according to that of the Psalm: « He that loveth
<!-- page 304 -->
iniquity hateth his own soul ». — Otherwise it can be expounded of alms *properly so called,* so that the sense is: *That which remaineth, give alms,* that is, of that which remains over, so that thus *the form* of alms is touched upon, according to that of the second to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter[^p304-1]: « Not that others should be eased, and you burdened ». And after the form *the efficacy* is subjoined, when he says: *And*[^p304-2] *all things are clean unto you;* because alms disposes to grace, through which the whole soul is made clean; and on account of this it is said in the first to Timothy, the fourth chapter: « Piety is profitable to all things, having promise of the life that now is, and of that which is to come ».

83. Now although *alms* is said *to avail for three things* principally, yet its efficacy is shown in Scripture to be *sevenfold.* The first is that it *cleanses the soul from fault,* as is had here; and Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter[^p304-3]: « The alms of the father shall not be forgotten »; and a little after: « And as the ice in fair weather, thy sins shall melt away ». — The second is that it *delivers from eternal punishment;* Tobias, the fourth chapter[^p304-4]: « Alms delivereth from all sin and from death, and suffereth not the soul to go into darkness ». — The third, that it *defends from transitory punishment;* Daniel, the fourth chapter: « Redeem thy sins with alms » etc. — The fourth, that it *mitigates the concupiscence of the fomes;* Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter[^p304-5]: « Water quencheth a flaming fire, and alms resisteth sins ». — The fifth, that it *gives victory over the enemy;* Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-ninth chapter: « Shut up alms in the heart of the poor, and it shall entreat for thee »; and a little after: « Above the shield of the mighty and above the spear it shall fight against thy enemy ». — The sixth, that it *preserves grace,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the seventeenth chapter[^p304-6]: « The alms of a man is as a purse with him, and shall preserve the grace of a man as the apple of the eye ». — The seventh, that it *gives confidence of coming to glory;* Tobias, the fourth chapter[^p304-7]: « Alms shall be a great confidence before the most high God to them that give it ». — These seven are the effects of alms itself, which is bestowed as well *bodily* as *spiritually.*

84. The works of *bodily mercy* are designated by this verse:

I visit, give drink, feed, ransom, clothe, take in, bury.

Of which, except the last, there is disputation in the judgement, in Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p304-8]; but of the last it is spoken in Tobias, the second chapter. — Now for alms to have these efficacies, there is needed first *simplicity in the heart;* whence Matthew, the sixth chapter[^p304-9]: « But when thou dost alms, let not thy left hand know » etc. — But secondly, *discretion in the choice,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the twelfth chapter: « If thou do good, know to whom thou dost it ». — But thirdly, *compassion in the affection;* Isaias, the fifty-eighth chapter[^p304-10]: « When thou shalt pour out thy soul to the hungry, then shall thy light rise up in darkness »; and Job, the thirtieth chapter: « I wept heretofore for him that was afflicted, and my soul had compassion on the poor ». — But fourthly, *sweetness in the speech;* Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter[^p304-11]: « Shall not the dew assuage the heat? So also a word is better than the gift ». — But fifthly, *cheerfulness in the face;* Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-fifth chapter: « In every gift shew a cheerful countenance »; the Psalm: « Acceptable is the man that sheweth mercy » etc. — But sixthly, *due quantity in the gift;* Tobias, the fourth chapter[^p304-12]: « According to thy ability be merciful; if thou have much » etc. — But seventhly, *speed in the deed* and the bestowal; Proverbs, the third chapter: « Say not to thy friend: Go, and come again » etc. — And then alms is made from the whole man, and by this it is accepted and pleasing to God.
