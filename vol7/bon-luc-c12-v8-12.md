# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XII, Vers. 8-12
## *Informatio duplex ad servandam veracitatem*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 313 -->

#### Informatio duplex ad servandam veracitatem.

12. (Vers. 8.). *Dico autem vobis: Omnis* etc. Postquam invitavit ad cavendam dolositatem et repellendam timiditatem, hic tertio invitat ad *servandam veracitatem*[^p313-7]. Veracitas enim confessionis est contra dolositatem simulationis. Ad hoc autem inducit dupliciter: primo insinuando *aequitatem divinae sententiae;* secundo vero insinuando *irremissibilitatem peccati blasphemiae.* Per primum monet ad *veritatem confitendam;* per secundum, ad *falsitatem declinandam.*

Primo igitur monet ad *confitendam veritatem* propter *divinae sententiae aequitatem,* cum dicit: *Dico autem vobis: Omnis quicumque confessus fuerit me*[^p313-8] *coram hominibus. Me* scilicet demonstrat personam in duabus naturis, scilicet qui me confitetur esse verum Deum et hominem, non tantum *corde* dicens, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios duodecimo[^p313-9]: « Nemo potest dicere: Dominus Iesus, nisi in Spiritu sancto », sed etiam *ore* exprimens, secundum quod dicitur ad Romanos decimo: « Corde creditur ad iustitiam, ore autem confessio fit ad salutem »; nec tantum *ore,* sed etiam *opere* contra hypocritas et malos Christianos, de quibus ad Titum primo[^p313-10]: « Confitentur, se nosse Deum, factis autem negant ». Contra quos Matthaei septimo: « Non omnis, qui dicit mihi: Domine, Domine » etc.; et ideo primae Ioannis tertio: « Non diligamus verbo neque lingua, sed opere et veritate ». — Qui sic confitetur plene confitetur, scilicet corde, ore, opere. De tali subiungit: *Et Filius hominis confitebitur illum coram Angelis Dei,* scilicet quod ipse sit de suis, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p313-11]: « Venite, benedicti Patris mei, percipite regnum ». Unde verificabitur illud Proverbiorum undecimo: « Anima, quae benedicit, impinguabitur ».

13. (Vers. 9.). Ex quo apparet aequitas retributionis respectu *confitentium;* et ut nihil desit, ostenditur aequitas respectu *negantium,* cum subdit: *Qui autem negaverit me coram hominibus denegabitur*[^p313-12] *coram Angelis Dei,* quando scilicet
<!-- page 314 -->
Dominus dicet illud Matthaei vigesimo quinto: « Nescio vos » etc. Unde supra nono: « Qui me erubuerit et meos sermones, hunc Filius hominis erubescet » etc. Et hoc exigit aequitas divinae sententiae, de qua primi Regum secundo[^p314-1]: « Qui me honorificaverit, honorificabo eum; qui autem contemnunt me erunt ignobiles ». — Et nota, quod qui veritatem confitetur, quam Christus docuit, Christum confitetur sive in fide, sive in moribus; qui autem illam negat per consequens negat et Christum. Qui dicit[^p314-2], illam esse falsam, dicit Christum per consequens esse mendacem, ac per hoc, Deum non esse, sed seductorem.

14. Et nota, quod *confessio* nominis Christi magnam recompensationem habet, quia confessio, qua Christus confitebitur confitentem se, erit *approbativa, honorificativa* et *salvativa;* nam Christus est rex, iudex et pontifex. Confessio etiam eius est *publica, praeclara* et *perpetua;* nostra vero modica[^p314-3] et momentanea. Et similiter per contrarium intelligitur de *negatione* sua; quod manifeste apparet in disceptatione iudiciali[^p314-4]: « Venite, benedicti », et « Ite, maledicti ». — Haec igitur est nobilissima propositio, quae mentes nostras erigit ad *Veritatem.* Ratio autem huius est haec: quia Veritas est verax; ideo non potest negare se nec confitentem se, nec confiteri mendacem nec contra se; unde secundae ad Timotheum secundo[^p314-5]: « Si negaverimus, et ille negabit nos; si non credimus, ille fidelis permanet, negare se ipsum non potest ».

15. (Vers. 10.). Secundo vero monet ad *declinandam falsitatem* propter *irremissibilitatem blasphemiae,* quae, licet remissibilis sit, cum fit per ignorantiam[^p314-6], irremissibilis tamen est, cum fit ex certa malitia. Propter quod dicit: *Et omnis, qui dicit verbum in Filium hominis, remittetur illi. In Filium hominis verbum dicit* qui falsum imponit, *per ignorantiam deceptus;* et talis ignorantia remissionem potest consequi. Unde *remittetur* non dicit *actum,* quia non omnes poenitent, sed *aptitudinem,* quia ignorantia excusat. Peccatum autem ex ignorantia dicitur esse *in Filium.* Et hoc modo blasphemavit Paulus, ideo fuit misericordiam consecutus; unde primae ad Timotheum primo[^p314-7]: « Fui blasphemus et persecutor et contumeliosus, sed misericor-
diam consecutus sum, quia ignorans haec feci in incredulitate ». — Non sic autem est, cum quis blasphemat *ex certa malitia;* propter quod subditur: *Ei autem, qui in Spiritum sanctum blasphemaverit, non remittetur. In Spiritum sanctum blasphemat* qui ex certa malitia veritatem impugnat et de Deo indigna dicit; et de tali dicitur, quod *non remittetur,* non quia *nunquam* talis consequatur remissionem, sed quia *ineptus est* ad remissionem, quia illud peccatum *nullum habet colorem excusationis.* Unde Richardus[^p314-8]: « Quidam blasphemat ex coactione, et hic peccat in Patrem; alius ex deceptione, et hic in Filium; alius ex sola malignitate, et hic in Spiritum sanctum. Est autem in hoc genere sceleratissimum, in quo malitia crevit usque ad summum, quando quis in vituperiis Dei delectatur. Quid ergo aliud videtur spiritus blasphemiae quam desiderium vituperationis divinae? Ex hoc ergo, quod hoc malum *nihil* habet in se *excusationis,* nihil meretur recipere remissionis ».

16. Ineptus etiam est ad remissionem, quia *impugnat divinam gratiam,* per quam praeparatur quis ad poenitentiam; unde talis aptus est ad impoenitentiam, in qua si perduret, nunquam remittetur culpa. Unde Beda[^p314-9]: « Qui gratiam Spiritus sancti, qua poenitentia inspiratur et ad communionem reditur, non cognoscens, impoenitenti corde perseveraverit, cum iam hoc non sit humanum, sed diabolicum, irremissibile erit ». Et de tali blasphemia, quae adiunctam habet *finalem impoenitentiam,* verum est, quod nunquam remittetur. Unde Matthaei duodecimo[^p314-10] dicitur, quod « non remittetur ei, neque in hoc saeculo neque in futuro ». — Ineptus nihilominus tertio, quia, licet per appropriationem approprietur Spiritui sancto, talis maxime *inhonorat totam Trinitatem.* Qui enim scienter et prudenter veritatem salutarem impugnat et ex certa malitia maximus est *contemptor* divinae virtutis, maximus *calumniator* divinae veritatis, maximus *persecutor* summae bonitatis, secundum illud Iob decimo quinto[^p314-11]: « Cucurrit adversus Deum erecto collo et pingui cervice armatus est ». Unde quia talis *contemptus, calumnia* et *persecutio* non potest esse sine cordis obduratione; et hanc concomitatur impoenitentia, et
<!-- page 315 -->
hanc peccati irremissibilitas: ideo blasphemia in Spiritum sanctum non remittetur[^p315-1].

#### Informatio duplex ad securitatem contra pusillanimitatem.

17. (Vers. 11.). *Cum autem inducent vos* etc. Hic iam quarto hortatur ad *habendam securitatem contra pusillanimitatem*[^p315-2]. Hoc autem facit duplici via, scilicet *auferendo sollicitudinem de periculo* et *conferendo certitudinem de praesidio.*

Primo igitur *aufert sollicitudinem de periculo,* cum dicit: *Cum autem inducent vos in synagogas et ad magistratus et potestates;* in quo innuit, discipulos tribulationem passuros a Iudaeis et gentibus. Unde Matthaei decimo[^p315-3] dicitur hoc expressius: « Tradent enim vos in conciliis et in synagogis » etc.; unde in Psalmo: « Astiterunt reges terrae » etc.

18. Et nota, quod tria genera personarum enumerat, coram quibus formidat homo loqui, scilicet coram *multis,* coram *peritis* et coram *magnis.* Coram *multis,* cum dicit: *In synagogis;* quocontra Iob trigesimo primo[^p315-4]: « Si expavi ad multitudinem nimiam, et despectio propinquorum terruit me ». Coram *peritis,* unde addit: *ad magistratus;* Actuum quinto: « Tunc abiit magistratus cum ministris et adduxerunt Apostolos ». Coram *magnis,* cum subdit: *et potestates;* ad Romanos decimo tertio: « Vis non timere potestatem? Bonum fac ». Primi non sunt timendi, quia « plures nobiscum », quarti Regum sexto[^p315-5]; secundi non, quia prudentior nobiscum, quia Actuum sexto: « Non poterant resistere sapientiae et spiritui » etc.; similiter nec tertii; primae Ioannis quarto: « Vicistis eos, quoniam maior est qui in vobis est, quam qui in mundo ».

19. Et quoniam periculum est loqui in conspectu multitudinis observantis; et periculum inducit timorem, et timor sollicitudinem, et sollicitudo inquietudinem, et inquietudo turbationem, et turbatio est occasio impatientiae et ruinae: ideo revocat a superflua sollicitudine, cum subdit: *Nolite solliciti esse, qualiter, aut quid respondeatis,* scilicet « interrogantibus » malitiose, *aut quid dicatis,* scilicet « volentibus addiscere[^p315-6] »; in quo verbo non tollit sollicitudinem *studiositatis,* sed *curiositatis.* De qua ad Philippenses quarto: « Nihil solliciti sitis, sed in omni oratione cum gratiarum actione petitiones vestrae innotescant apud Deum »; quia, sicut dicitur Iacobi primo, « si quis vestrum indiget sapientia, postulet a Deo » etc. Et illud maxime faciendum est in articulo necessitatis, ubi non est quaerendus ornatus verborum ac profunditas sententiarum, sed mera et simplex fidei veritas cum multa constantia et fervore prolata; iuxta quod dicitur primae ad Corinthios secundo[^p315-7]: « Sermo noster non fuit in persuasibilibus humanae sapientiae verbis » etc. Non autem intendit retrahere a *studio veritatis,* cum ipse dicat Ioannis quinto: « Scrutamini Scripturas, in quibus putatis vitam aeternam habere »; sed intendit persuadere, ut homo in periculo mortis non confidat de ingenio, quia homo deficit in inveniendo, fallitur in iudicando, et ideo deviat frequenter, innitens ingenio suo. Propter quod Proverbiorum tertio[^p315-8]: « Habe fiduciam in Domino ex toto corde tuo, et ne innitaris prudentiae tuae ».

20. (Vers. 12.). Secundo confert *certitudinem de praesidio,* cum subdit: *Spiritus enim sanctus docebit vos in ipsa hora, quae*[^p315-9] *oporteat vos dicere.* Hic autem Spiritus est optimus doctor, quia docet hominem *intelligere* et *ornate exprimere.* Propter primum dicitur Iob trigesimo secundo[^p315-10]: « Inspiratio Omnipotentis dat intelligentiam »; et Ioannis decimo sexto: « Cum venerit ille Spiritus veritatis, docebit vos omnem veritatem », quia, sicut dicitur primae ad Corinthios secundo, « Spiritus omnia scrutatur, etiam profunda Dei ». Propter secundum dicitur Sapientiae decimo[^p315-11]: « Sapientia aperuit os mutorum » etc.; et Matthaei decimo: « Vos non estis, qui loquimini, sed Spiritus Patris vestri ». Ideo dicebat Apostolus secundae ad Corinthios decimo tertio: « An experimentum quaeritis eius qui in me loquitur Christus »? — De his autem duobus infra vigesimo primo[^p315-12]: « Ego dabo vobis os et sapientiam, cui non poterunt resistere et contradicere omnes adversarii vestri »; et primae Ioannis secundo: « Unctio
<!-- page 316 -->
docet vos de omnibus ». Unde Bernardus[^p316-1]: « Bona est lectio, sed melior est unctio, quae *docet de omnibus* ». Nam unctio docet dilectionem Dei et proximi, et in hoc « universa *Lex* pendet et *Prophetae* », secundum quod dicitur Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p316-2]. Tota etiam *sapientia philosophiae,* sicut dicit Augustinus, « hic est physica, ethica et civilis ». Spiritus etiam sanctus, faciendo animam Deo adhaerere, facit sibi familiarem et per hoc facit revelari secreta; Ioannis decimo quinto[^p316-3]: « Vos autem dixi amicos ». Fervor autem spiritus elevat animam supra se, ubi audit et videt arcana, sicut Paulus, secundae ad Corinthios duodecimo[^p316-4]; sicut Ezechiel, tertio: « Assumsit me spiritus » etc.; sicut Ioannes, Apocalypsis primo: « Fui in spiritu » etc.

#### A twofold formation for preserving truthfulness.

12. (Vers. 8.). *And I say to you: Whosoever* etc. After he invited them to guard against guile and to repel timidity, here thirdly he invites them *to preserve truthfulness*[^p313-7]. For the truthfulness of confession is against the guile of dissembling. Now he leads them to this in two ways: first by intimating *the equity of the divine sentence;* but secondly by intimating *the irremissibility of the sin of blasphemy.* By the first he warns them *to confess the truth;* by the second, *to turn aside from falsehood.*

First, then, he warns them *to confess the truth* on account of *the equity of the divine sentence,* when he says: *And I say to you: Whosoever shall confess me*[^p313-8] *before men. Me,* namely, points out the person in two natures, namely he who confesses me to be true God and man, not only saying it *in the heart,* according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the twelfth chapter[^p313-9]: « No man can say, The Lord Jesus, but by the Holy Ghost », but also expressing it *with the mouth,* according to what is said to the Romans, the tenth chapter: « With the heart, we believe unto justice; but, with the mouth, confession is made unto salvation »; nor only *with the mouth,* but also *in deed,* against hypocrites and wicked Christians, of whom to Titus, the first chapter[^p313-10]: « They profess that they know God: but in their works they deny ». Against whom Matthew, the seventh chapter: « Not every one that saith to me, Lord, Lord » etc.; and therefore the first of John, the third chapter: « Let us not love in word, nor in tongue, but in deed, and in truth ». — He who so confesses confesses fully, namely in heart, mouth, deed. Of such a one he subjoins: *And the Son of man shall confess him before the angels of God,* namely that he is of his own, according to that of Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p313-11]: « Come, ye blessed of my Father, possess you the kingdom ». Whence that of Proverbs, the eleventh chapter, will be verified: « The soul which blesseth, shall be made fat ».

13. (Vers. 9.). From which appears the equity of retribution with respect to *those who confess;* and that nothing may be lacking, the equity is shown with respect to *those who deny,* when he subjoins: *But he that shall deny me before men, shall be denied*[^p313-12] *before the angels of God,* namely when
<!-- page 314 -->
the Lord will say that of Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter: « I know you not » etc. Whence above, in the ninth: « He that shall be ashamed of me and of my words, of him the Son of man shall be ashamed » etc. And the equity of the divine sentence requires this, of which the first of Kings, the second chapter[^p314-1]: « Whosoever shall glorify me, him will I glorify: but they that despise me, shall be despised ». — And note that he who confesses the truth which Christ taught confesses Christ, whether in faith or in morals; but he who denies it consequently denies Christ also. He who says[^p314-2] that it is false says consequently that Christ is a liar, and through this, that he is not God, but a seducer.

14. And note that the *confession* of the name of Christ has a great recompense, because the confession by which Christ will confess him who confesses him will be *approving, honouring* and *saving;* for Christ is king, judge and pontiff. His confession also is *public, illustrious* and *perpetual;* but ours is slight[^p314-3] and momentary. And likewise through the contrary it is understood of his *denial;* which manifestly appears in the judicial debate[^p314-4]: « Come, ye blessed », and « Depart, you cursed ». — This, then, is a most noble proposition, which raises our minds to the *Truth.* Now the reason of this is this: because the Truth is truthful; therefore he cannot deny himself nor one who confesses him, nor confess a liar nor one against himself; whence the second to Timothy, the second chapter[^p314-5]: « If we deny him, he also will deny us; if we believe not, he continueth faithful, he cannot deny himself ».

15. (Vers. 10.). But secondly he warns them *to turn aside from falsehood* on account of *the irremissibility of blasphemy,* which, although it be remissible when it is done through ignorance[^p314-6], yet is irremissible when it is done out of certain malice. On account of which he says: *And whosoever speaketh a word against the Son of man, it shall be forgiven him. He speaks a word against the Son of man* who imputes something false, *deceived through ignorance;* and such ignorance can obtain remission. Hence *it shall be forgiven* does not say the *act,* because not all do penance, but the *aptitude,* because ignorance excuses. Now a sin out of ignorance is said to be *against the Son.* And in this way Paul blasphemed, therefore he obtained mercy; whence the first to Timothy, the first chapter[^p314-7]: « I was a blasphemer and a persecutor and contumelious, but I obtained mercy, because I did these things ignorantly in unbelief ». — But it is not so when one blasphemes *out of certain malice;* on account of which there is subjoined: *But to him that shall blaspheme against the Holy Ghost, it shall not be forgiven. He blasphemes against the Holy Ghost* who out of certain malice impugns the truth and says things unworthy of God; and of such a one it is said that *it shall not be forgiven,* not because such a one *never* obtains remission, but because he *is unfit* for remission, because that sin *has no colour of excuse.* Whence Richard[^p314-8]: « One blasphemes out of coercion, and he sins against the Father; another out of deception, and he against the Son; another out of sheer malignity, and he against the Holy Ghost. And in this kind that is most wicked in which malice has grown even to the highest, when one takes delight in vituperations of God. What else, then, does the spirit of blasphemy seem to be than a desire of vituperating God? From this, then, that this evil has *nothing* of *excuse* in it, it merits to receive nothing of remission ».

16. He is unfit for remission also because he *impugns the divine grace,* by which one is prepared for penance; whence such a one is apt for impenitence, in which if he persevere, the fault will never be forgiven. Whence Bede[^p314-9]: « He who, not acknowledging the grace of the Holy Ghost, by which penance is inspired and return is made to the communion, shall have persevered with an impenitent heart, since this now is not human, but diabolical, it will be irremissible ». And of such blasphemy as has *final impenitence* joined to it, it is true that it will never be forgiven. Whence in Matthew, the twelfth chapter[^p314-10], it is said that « it shall not be forgiven him, neither in this world, nor in the world to come ». — He is unfit, nevertheless, thirdly, because, although by appropriation it is appropriated to the Holy Ghost, such a one most of all *dishonours the whole Trinity.* For he who knowingly and wittingly impugns the saving truth, and out of certain malice, is the greatest *contemner* of the divine power, the greatest *calumniator* of the divine truth, the greatest *persecutor* of the highest goodness, according to that of Job, the fifteenth chapter[^p314-11]: « He hath run against God with his neck raised up, and is armed with a fat neck ». Hence because such *contempt, calumny* and *persecution* cannot be without hardening of the heart; and impenitence accompanies this, and
<!-- page 315 -->
the irremissibility of the sin accompanies this: therefore blasphemy against the Holy Ghost will not be forgiven[^p315-1].

#### A twofold formation for security against faintheartedness.

17. (Vers. 11.). *And when they shall bring you* etc. Here now fourthly he exhorts them *to have security against faintheartedness*[^p315-2]. Now this he does by a twofold way, namely *by taking away solicitude about the danger* and *by conferring certitude about the protection.*

First, then, he *takes away solicitude about the danger,* when he says: *And when they shall bring you into the synagogues, and to magistrates and powers;* in which he intimates that the disciples would suffer tribulation from the Jews and the gentiles. Whence in Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p315-3], this is said more expressly: « For they will deliver you up in councils, and in synagogues » etc.; whence in the Psalm: « The kings of the earth stood up » etc.

18. And note that he enumerates three kinds of persons before whom a man dreads to speak, namely before *the many,* before *the learned* and before *the great.* Before *the many,* when he says: *Into the synagogues;* against which Job, the thirty-first chapter[^p315-4]: « If I have been afraid at a very great multitude, and the contempt of kinsmen hath terrified me ». Before *the learned,* whence he adds: *to magistrates;* Acts, the fifth chapter: « Then went the magistrate with the ministers, and they brought the Apostles ». Before *the great,* when he subjoins: *and powers;* to the Romans, the thirteenth chapter: « Wilt thou not be afraid of the power? Do that which is good ». The first are not to be feared, because « more are with us », the fourth of Kings, the sixth chapter[^p315-5]; nor the second, because one more prudent is with us, because in Acts, the sixth chapter: « They were not able to resist the wisdom and the spirit » etc.; likewise neither the third; the first of John, the fourth chapter: « You have overcome them, because greater is he that is in you, than he that is in the world ».

19. And since it is dangerous to speak in the sight of an observing multitude; and danger brings on fear, and fear solicitude, and solicitude disquiet, and disquiet perturbation, and perturbation is the occasion of impatience and of ruin: therefore he calls them back from superfluous solicitude, when he subjoins: *Be not solicitous how or what you shall answer,* namely « to those who question » maliciously, *or what you shall say,* namely « to those who wish to learn[^p315-6] »; in which word he does not take away the solicitude *of studiousness,* but *of curiosity.* Of which to the Philippians, the fourth chapter: « Be nothing solicitous; but in every prayer, with thanksgiving, let your petitions be made known to God »; because, as is said in James, the first chapter, « if any of you want wisdom, let him ask of God » etc. And that is most of all to be done at the point of necessity, where ornament of words and profundity of sentences are not to be sought, but the pure and simple truth of faith, uttered with much constancy and fervour; according to what is said in the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p315-7]: « Our speech was not in the persuasive words of human wisdom » etc. But he does not intend to draw them back from *the study of truth,* since he himself says in John, the fifth chapter: « Search the Scriptures, in which you think to have life everlasting »; but he intends to persuade that a man in danger of death should not trust in his wit, because a man fails in finding, is deceived in judging, and therefore frequently goes astray, leaning upon his own wit. On account of which in Proverbs, the third chapter[^p315-8]: « Have confidence in the Lord with all thy heart, and lean not upon thy own prudence ».

20. (Vers. 12.). Secondly he confers *certitude about the protection,* when he subjoins: *For the Holy Ghost shall teach you in the same hour what things*[^p315-9] *you must say.* Now this Spirit is the best teacher, because he teaches a man *to understand* and *to express ornately.* On account of the first it is said in Job, the thirty-second chapter[^p315-10]: « The inspiration of the Almighty giveth understanding »; and in John, the sixteenth chapter: « When he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will teach you all truth », because, as is said in the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter, « the Spirit searcheth all things, yea, the deep things of God ». On account of the second it is said in Wisdom, the tenth chapter[^p315-11]: « Wisdom opened the mouth of the dumb » etc.; and in Matthew, the tenth chapter: « It is not you that speak, but the Spirit of your Father ». Therefore the Apostle said in the second to the Corinthians, the thirteenth chapter: « Do you seek a proof of him that speaketh in me, Christ »? — Of these two, moreover, below in the twenty-first chapter[^p315-12]: « I will give you a mouth and wisdom, which all your adversaries shall not be able to resist and gainsay »; and the first of John, the second chapter: « The unction
<!-- page 316 -->
teacheth you of all things ». Whence Bernard[^p316-1]: « Reading is good, but unction is better, which *teacheth of all things* ». For unction teaches the dilection of God and of neighbour, and on this « the whole *Law* dependeth and the *Prophets* », according to what is said in Matthew, the twenty-second chapter[^p316-2]. The whole *wisdom of philosophy* also, as Augustine says, « here is the physical, the ethical and the civil ». The Holy Ghost also, by making the soul cleave to God, makes it familiar to himself and through this makes secrets to be revealed; John, the fifteenth chapter[^p316-3]: « But I have called you friends ».
