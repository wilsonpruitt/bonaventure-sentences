# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. IX, Vers. 1-9
## *Forma praedicandi dupliciter explicatur*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 216 -->

### Capitulum IX.

#### Quarto determinatur instructorum discipulorum delegatio, primo quoad Apostolos, deinde quoad septuaginta.

1. *Convocatis autem Iesus*[^p216-8] *duodecim* etc. Completis tribus partibus principalibus huius partis, quae est de doctrina Iesu Christi, in quibus discipuli ipsi excitati fuerunt ad credendum, invitati ad imitandum et instructi ad intelligendum; hic sequitur quarta pars, in qua *intelligentes mittuntur ad praedicandum.* Ordo autem istarum partium satis est in se manifestus. Quoniam autem quidam praedicatorum sunt in gradu superiori, quidam vero in inferiori; ideo pars ista habet duas. In quarum prima agitur de *missione Apostolorum;* in secunda vero, de *missione septuaginta discipulorum,* in decimo capitulo ibi[^p216-9]: *Designavit autem* etc.

#### Primo agitur de missione Apostolorum quoad tria.

Prima pars continet praesens capitulum et habet tres partes, secundum tria quae explicat Evangelista necessaria Apostolis et praelatis ad praedicandum missis. Quorum primum est *forma praedicandi;* secundum est *via proficiendi,* quam explicat ibi[^p216-10]: *Et factum est, cum solus esset orans;* tertium est *norma praesidendi,* quae describitur ibi: *Intravit autem in eos cogitatio, quis eorum* etc.

#### Forma praedicandi dupliciter explicatur.

*Formam* autem *praedicandi* dupliciter explicat; primo sub *divino praecepto;* secundo vero, sub *divino exemplo,* ibi[^p216-11]: *Et reversi Apostoli nuntiaverunt.*
<!-- page 217 -->

Quantum autem ad *informationem praedicatorum per divinum praeceptum* tria introducuntur ab Evangelista, scilicet *auctoritas commissa, honestas iniuncta* et *utilitas adiuncta;* ita quod primo praemittit *auctoritatem* tanquam praecedentem. Secundo adiungit *honestatem* quasi concomitantem, ibi[^p217-1]: *Et ait ad illos: Nihil tuleritis in via.* Tertio subiungit *utilitatem* consequentem, ibi: *Egressi autem circuibant* etc. — Sed quoniam verba, quantumcumque authentici praedicatoris fuerint[^p217-2], sunt inefficacia, nisi habeant divina testimonia; et talia sunt divina miracula: ideo in prima parte primo explicatur *collatio potestatis ad miracula facienda,* deinde *commissio auctoritatis ad mysteria praedicanda.* Prima enim viam facit ad secundam.

2. (Vers. 1.). Quantum igitur ad *collationem potestatis ad miracula facienda* dicit: *Convocatis autem Iesus*[^p217-3] *duodecim Apostolis,* tanquam scilicet principalibus praelatis, electis ex omnibus aliis, secundum quod dicitur supra sexto: « Vocavit Iesus discipulos suos et ex his elegit duodecim, quos et *Apostolos* nominavit ». Istis ergo *convocatis,* id est in unum vocatis ad unitatem commendandam, in qua datur Spiritus sanctus, secundum quod dicitur Actuum secundo[^p217-4]: « Dum complerentur dies Pentecostes, erant omnes discipuli pariter in eodem loco » etc. Unde Numerorum undecimo: « Dominus dixit ad Moysen: Congrega mihi septuaginta viros ad ostium tabernaculi testimonii, et auferam de spiritu tuo et tradam eis ». — Sic nunc; unde et subditur: *Dedit illis virtutem et potestatem,* ita quod *virtus* sit respectu spiritualium, *potestas* respectu corporalium. Propter quod ad explicationem huius adiungit: *Super omnia daemonia,* ut hoc referatur ad *virtutem; et ut languores curarent,* ut hoc referatur ad *potestatem;* et per hoc haberent duplex genus testimonii secundum duplex creaturae genus, scilicet spiritualis et corporalis. Et hoc est quod expresse dicitur Marci tertio[^p217-5]: « Dedit potestatem curandi infirmitates et eiiciendi daemonia »; ita ut vere impleatur illud Psalmi: « Dominus dabit verbum evangelizantibus virtute multa ».

3. (Vers. 2.). Quantum autem ad *commissionem auctoritatis ad mysteria praedicanda* subdit: *Et misit illos praedicare regnum Dei.* In *missione* namque *auctoritas* designatur, sine qua nullus debet praedicare; unde ad Romanos decimo[^p217-6]: « Quomodo praedicabunt, nisi mittantur »? Unde et Isaias non audebat praedicare, nisi mitteretur; Isaiae sexto: « Ecce ego, Domine, mitte me ». Econtra de pseudoprophetis Ieremiae vigesimo tertio: « Non mittebam eos, et ipsi currebant ». At non tales erant Apostoli, qui ideo sic nominati sunt, ut eorum commendaretur auctoritas. Apostolus enim *missus* interpretatur; missi autem fuerunt ad praedicandum, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios primo[^p217-7]: « Non misit me Dominus baptizare, sed evangelizare ». — Fuerunt ad praedicandum missi, non rem parvam, sed magnam, scilicet *regnum Dei,* per quod potest intelligi *doctrina veritatis,* iuxta illud Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p217-8]: « Auferetur a vobis regnum Dei, et dabitur genti facienti fructus eius ». Potest etiam dici *gratia Spiritus sancti,* secundum illud ad Romanos decimo quarto: « Regnum Dei non est esca et potus, sed iustitia et pax et gaudium in Spiritu sancto »; infra decimo septimo: « Regnum Dei intra vos est ». Potest etiam dici *gloria aeterna,* iuxta illud Ioannis tertio[^p217-9]: « Nisi quis renatus fuerit ex aqua et Spiritu sancto, non potest intrare in regnum Dei ». Omnibus his modis Apostoli sunt missi praedicare regnum Dei, scilicet veram doctrinam, divinam gratiam et aeternam gloriam. — Et quia propter auctoritatem praedicationis concesserat potestatem curationis, ideo subdit: *Et sanare infirmos,* scilicet misit ad confirmationem veritatis praedicatae, secundum illud Marci ultimo[^p217-10]: « Illi profecti praedicaverunt ubique, Domino cooperante et sermonem confirmante sequentibus signis ». Unde signum missionis spiritualis praedicationis ad praedicandum est sanatio audientium a morbis vitiorum.

4. Tria autem sunt evidentia signa, quod praedicator a Domino mittatur[^p217-11] ad Evangelium praedicandum. Primum est *auctoritas* mittentis, cuiusmodi est pontificis, et maxime pontificis eius[^p217-12] qui est loco Petri, immo loco Iesu Christi; unde qui ab eo mittitur a Christo mittitur. Secundum est *zelus animarum* in persona, quae mittitur, quando scilicet principaliter quaerit Dei honorem et animarum salutem. Tertio[^p217-13], cum sequitur *fructificatio et conversio* auditorum. — Per primum sunt nuntii Patris,
<!-- page 218 -->
per secundum Filii, per tertium Spiritus sancti. De talibus dicitur: « Posui vos, ut eatis et fructum afferatis, et fructus vester maneat », Ioannis decimo quinto[^p218-1]. Et qui sic mittitur potest dicere illud Isaiae sexagesimo primo: « Spiritus Domini super me, eo quod unxerit me » etc.

5. (Vers. 3.). *Et ait ad illos: Nihil tuleritis in via.* Post explicationem auctoritatis commissae subditur *descriptio honestatis iniunctae.* Haec autem consistit in tribus, scilicet in *paupertate victus, tenuitate vestitus* et *humilitate convictus.*

Primo ergo quantum ad *paupertatem in victu* dicitur: *Et ait ad illos: Nihil tuleritis in via,* occasione scilicet temporalis sustentationis, ut exemplo praedicetis illud primae ad Timotheum sexto[^p218-2]: « Nihil intulimus in hunc mundum, nec dubium, quia nec auferre quid possumus ». Et ut magis innotescat, descendit ad speciale, cum subdit: *Neque virgam,* scilicet ad sustentandum, *neque peram,* ad reponendum, *neque panem,* ad manducandum, *neque pecuniam,* ad emendum. In quo est prohibitio omnis sustentamenti, ut ostendatur, quod praedicator ex magnitudine spei soli Deo debet inniti, secundum illud primae Petri quinto[^p218-3]: « Omnem sollicitudinem vestram proiicientes in eum, quia ipsi cura est de vobis ». Unde etiam beatus Franciscus, quando mittebat fratres ad praedicandum, dicebat eis illud Psalmi[^p218-4]: « Iacta cogitatum tuum in Domino » etc. In hoc autem non prohibet, ut dicit Augustinus, studiosam providentiam, sed curiosam sollicitudinem, secundum illud Matthaei sexto[^p218-5]: « Nolite solliciti esse in crastinum; sufficit enim diei malitia sua ».

6. Et ideo ad huius praecepti rectam intelligentiam, quod unus Evangelista negat, scilicet Lucas, alius Evangelista affirmat, scilicet Marcus, sexto[^p218-6], ubi dicitur: « Et praecepit eis Iesus, ne quid tollerent in via nisi virgam tantum ». Super quo dicit Augustinus de Concordia Evangelistarum: « Utrumque praeceptum est a Domino, ut nihil nisi virgam fer-
rent, et ut nec virgam ferrent. *Virgam non ferre* est sollicitum non esse; *nihil ferre nisi virgam* est uti concessa praedicatoribus accipiendi potestate ». Unde Lucae decimo[^p218-7]: « Manducantes et bibentes quae apud illos sunt ». — *Litteralis* autem observantia istius praecepti est perfectorum praedicatorum, perfecte Christum imitantium; in quorum persona Petrus Matthaei decimo nono[^p218-8]: « Ecce, nos reliquimus omnia et secuti sumus te ». Sed *spiritualis* observantia ad omnes se extendit, ut praedicator veritatis sit contemptor terreneitatis; alioquin, si annuntiant verbum Dei cupiditate lucri, non sunt discipuli Christi, sed Balaam harioli; secundum illud secundae Petri secundo[^p218-9]: « Cor exercitatum avaritia habentes, maledictionis filii, derelinquentes viam rectam », id est paupertatis; de qua hi « erraverunt, secuti viam Balaam, qui mercedem iniquitatis amavit ». — Unde praedicator veritatis super omnia vitare debet vitium cupiditatis; alioquin non erit praedicator, sed adulator. Propter quod primae ad Thessalonicenses secundo[^p218-10]: « Neque aliquando fuimus in sermone adulationis, sicut scitis, neque in occasione avaritiae, Deus testis est ». Sciebat enim, quod ex hoc iram Dei provocaret, sicut dicitur Michaeae tertio: « Principes eius in muneribus iudicabant, et sacerdotes eius in mercede docebant, et prophetae eius in pecunia divinabant » etc. « Ob hanc causam Sion quasi ager arabitur » etc.

7. Secundo quantum ad *tenuitatem in vestitu* subditur: *Neque duas tunicas habeatis,* ut sic concordet doctrina Magistri doctrinae Praecursoris, secundum illud Lucae tertio[^p218-11]: « Qui habet duas tunicas det unam non habenti »; et secundum illud Apostoli primae ad Timotheum sexto: « Habentes alimenta et quibus tegamur, his contenti simus ». In hoc autem, quod non prohibet simplex vestimentum, sed duplex, concedit necessitatem et vetat curiositatem in mutatione et superfluitatem in possessione. Unde Beda[^p218-12]: « *Simplex* vestimentum est quidquid
<!-- page 219 -->
necessarium est alicui secundum qualitatem loci vel temporis; *duplex,* quod est ultra necessitatem »; et hoc manifestum est in ipso Domino, qui praeter tunicam inconsutilem habuit alia vestimenta, quae fuerunt divisa, ut dicitur Ioannis decimo nono. — Secundum *spiritualem* autem intelligentiam prohibetur duplicitas simulationis et hypocrisis, ne aliam vestem habeant in occulto et aliam in aperto, ne sint de illis, de quibus dicitur Matthaei septimo[^p219-1]: « Veniunt ad vos in vestimentis ovium, intrinsecus autem sunt lupi rapaces ». Hoc similiter declinans beatus Franciscus, cum infirmaretur, noluit frustum pellis ponere intrinsecus ante pectus, nisi etiam apponeret exterius[^p219-2].

8. (Vers. 4.). Tertio vero quantum ad *humilitatem in convictu* subiungit: *Et in quamcumque domum intraveritis, ibi manete,* contenti scilicet existentes illo hospitio; *et inde ne exeatis,* propter melius requirendum, quia, sicut dicitur Ecclesiastici vigesimo nono[^p219-3], « vita nequam hospitandi de domo in domum ». Mutatio namque hospitii attestatur impatientiae, quae pro modica causa movetur et non potest quiescere, secundum illud Ieremiae decimo quarto: « Populus iste dilexit movere pedes et non quievit et Domino non placuit ». — Et hoc maxime vitandum est a praedicatore, quia, secundum illud Proverbiorum decimo nono[^p219-4], « doctrina viri per patientiam cognoscitur ». Et ideo Ambrosius: « Alienum est a praedicatore cursitare per domos et immobilis hospitii iura mutare ». Huius autem inconstantiae et impatientiae mater est superbia, quae cum nullo potest in pace cohabitare, secundum illud Proverbiorum decimo tertio[^p219-5]: « Inter superbos semper iurgia sunt ».

9. (Vers. 5.). Et quoniam verae humilitatis est nullius societatem recusare, nec etiam alii onerosum esse; ideo ad perfectionem humilitatis subditur: *Et quicumque non receperint vos,* despiciendo scilicet
vestram doctrinam, sicut illi qui etiam Dominum non receperunt; Ioannis primo[^p219-6]: « In propria venit, et sui eum non receperunt ». — Et quia hoc est magnum delictum, cui non est communicandum; ideo subdit: *Exeuntes de civitate illa,* declinando scilicet eorum nequitiam, iuxta illud Numerorum decimo sexto[^p219-7]: « Recedite a tabernaculis hominum impiorum et nolite tangere quae eorum sunt, ne involvamini cum eis »; Isaiae quinquagesimo secundo: « Recedite de medio eorum, nolite pollutum tangere »; et Ieremiae quadragesimo octavo: « Relinquite civitates et habitate in petra ». — Et hoc, ne inquinentur sordibus alienis; propter quod addit: *Et*[^p219-8] *pulverem pedum vestrorum excutite in testimonium super illos,* ad ostendendum scilicet vestram innocentiam. Hoc fecerunt Barnabas et Paulus, de quibus Actuum decimo tertio dicitur, quod « excusso pulvere pedum suorum, venerunt Iconium ». *Pulverem* autem *de pedibus excutere* est nihil de vana hominum gloria permittere suis affectibus adhaerere. Vana enim gloria recte per *pulverem* designatur, quae elevatur per ventum, secundum illud Psalmi[^p219-9]: « Erit tanquam pulvis, quem proiicit ventus a facie terrae ».

10. (Vers. 6.). *Egressi autem circuibant* etc. Post auctoritatem commissam et honestatem iniunctam subiungit hic tertio Evangelista *utilitatem adiunctam.* Quadruplex autem utilitas ostenditur ad praedicationem Apostolorum subsecuta, scilicet excitatio *hominum ad audiendum,* excitatio *audientium ad conferendum,* excitatio *conferentium ad admirandum,* excitatio *admirantium ad conspiciendum.*

Primo ergo quantum ad *excitationem hominum ad audiendum* dicitur: *Egressi autem circuibant per castella, evangelizantes et curantes ubique,* ita ut iam inciperet impleri illud Psalmi[^p219-10]: « In omnem terram exivit sonus eorum ». Licet enim non omnes doctrinam eorum caperent, tamen propter miracula
<!-- page 220 -->
volebant doctrinam audire; unde Actuum octavo dicitur: « Intendebant turbae his quae a Philippo dicebantur, unanimiter audientes et videntes signa, quae faciebat ». Et ideo isti non solum praedicabant, sed etiam ubique curabant, ut omnes studio eorum et verbo et signo excitarentur ad audiendum; in quo non solum explicatur auditorum utilitas, sed etiam praedicatorum fidelitas. Quia enim *circuibant,* immunes erant ab otiositate; quia *per castella,* immunes ab elatione; quia *evangelizantes,* immunes a nugacitate; quia *curantes ubique,* a personarum acceptione. Et ideo finem habebant in se, qui est sufficienter dicere ad persuasionem et auditores excitare[^p220-1]. Et hic est primus finis et generalis.

11. (Vers. 7.). Secundus finis sive utilitas specialis est *excitatio audientium ad conferendum;* quantum ad quam dicitur: *Audivit autem Herodes tetrarcha omnia, quae fiebant ab eo,* ita quod fama nominis Christi non solum per Apostolos deferebatur ad plebes, sed etiam ad reges, sicut de Paulo dicitur Actuum nono[^p220-2]: « Vas electionis est mihi, ut portet nomen meum coram gentibus et regibus et filiis Israel ». Unde iam poterant auditores dicere illud quod dixerunt Gabaonitae Iosue nono: « Audivimus famam potentiae Dei vestri, cuncta quae fecit ». Et ex hoc auditu excitatus est ad inquirendum et dubitandum propter varietatem opinantium.

12. (Vers. 7. 8.). Unde subditur: *Et haesitabat, eo quod diceretur a quibusdam, quia Ioannes surrexit a mortuis.* Et huic opinioni magis adhaerebat Herodes; unde Matthaei decimo quarto[^p220-3]: « Audivit Herodes famam Iesu et ait pueris suis: Hic est Ioannes Baptista; ipse surrexit a mortuis, et ideo virtutes operantur in illo ». Hoc autem opinabatur propter *eminentiam sanctitatis vitae,* quia, sicut dicitur Marci sexto[^p220-4], « Herodes metuebat Ioannem, sciens eum virum iustum et sanctum ». Huic tamen opinioni non firmiter adhaerebat; ideo dicitur, quod *haesitabat,* id est, vacillabat, secundum illud Iacobi primo: « Qui haesitat similis est fluctui maris, qui a vento movetur et circumfertur ». — Et hoc erat propter alias opiniones; unde subdit: *A quibusdam vero, quia Elias apparuit,* scilicet dicebatur. Et haec opinio videbatur probabilis propter *eminentiam potestatis.* Unde Ecclesiastici quadragesimo octavo[^p220-5]: « Amplificatus est Elias in mirabilibus suis ». Et
haec opinio credibilior videbatur, quia, sicut dicitur Ioannis decimo, « Ioannes quidem signum fecit nullum ». Unde Christus faciens miracula magis videbatur Elias. — Sed tamen huic non omnes assentiebant; propter quod addit: *Ab aliis autem, quia Propheta unus de antiquis surrexit.* Et hoc opinabantur propter *notitiam veritatis;* quia, secundum illud Amos tertio[^p220-6], « non faciet Dominus verbum, nisi revelaverit secretum suum ad servos suos Prophetas ». Et sic patet, quod triplex erat opinio propter triplicem excellentiam, quam audiebant de Christo, scilicet sanctitatis, potestatis et notitiae veritatis. Et ideo invicem conferebant, et quidam hoc, et quidam aliud credebant[^p220-7].

13. (Vers. 9.). Tertius autem finis fuit *excitatio conferentium ad admirandum;* quantum ad quam subditur: *Et ait Herodes: Ioannem ego decollavi,* tanquam hominem mortalem et passibilem. Hunc decollaverat Herodes propter Ioannis veritatem et Herodis malignitatem, secundum quod dicitur Matthaei decimo quarto[^p220-8]: « Dicebat Ioannes Herodi: Non licet tibi habere uxorem fratris tui »; et sequitur post, quod ex hac causa eum decollavit. Et ita ex ipsa morte iudicat in Ioanne, quantumcumque magnus praedicaretur, infirmitatem. — Et propterea ex hac comparatione admirabatur Christi virtutem; unde subdit: *Quis est autem hic, de quo audio talia*[^p220-9]? id est tam magna mirabilia; unde poterat dicere: « Quis Deus magnus sicut Deus noster? Tu es Deus, qui facis mirabilia », qualia scilicet non erant ante audita, secundum illud Ioannis nono: « A saeculo non est auditum, quod quis aperuit oculos caeci nati » etc.

14. Quartus finis fuit *excitatio admirantium ad conspiciendum;* propter quod subditur: *Et quaerebat videre eum.* Libenter enim videmus quod admiramur. Hoc tamen non est tunc assecutus, secundum quod dicitur Lucae decimo[^p220-10]: « Amen dico vobis, quod multi reges et Prophetae voluerunt videre quae vos videtis et non viderunt ». Hoc autem non fuit assecutus, quia non studiose quaesivit; unde Proverbiorum decimo quarto: « Quaerit derisor sapientiam et non invenit ». Unde valde sollicite considerandum, qualiter debet Deus quaeri, ut possit a quaerentibus videri et inveniri.

## English
<!-- page 216 -->

### Chapter IX.

#### Fourthly, the delegation of the instructed disciples is determined, first as to the Apostles, then as to the seventy.

1. *Then Jesus*[^p216-8] *calling together the twelve* etc. The three principal parts of this part being completed, which is concerning the doctrine of Jesus Christ, in which the disciples themselves were aroused to believe, invited to imitate and instructed to understand; here follows the fourth part, in which *those who understand are sent to preach.* Now the order of these parts is sufficiently manifest in itself. But since some of the preachers are in a higher degree, and some in a lower; therefore this part has two. In the first of which the *mission of the Apostles* is treated; but in the second, the *mission of the seventy disciples,* in the tenth chapter, at[^p216-9]: *Now he designated* etc.

#### First, the mission of the Apostles is treated, as to three things.

The first part contains the present chapter and has three parts, according to three things which the Evangelist sets out as necessary to the Apostles and prelates sent to preach. Of which the first is *the form of preaching;* the second is *the way of making progress,* which he sets out at[^p216-10]: *And it came to pass, as he was alone praying;* the third is *the norm of presiding,* which is described at: *And there entered a thought into them, which of them* etc.

#### The form of preaching is explained in two ways.

Now he explains the *form of preaching* in two ways; first under *the divine precept;* but secondly, under *the divine example,* at[^p216-11]: *And the Apostles being returned announced.*
<!-- page 217 -->

Now as to *the formation of preachers through the divine precept,* three things are brought in by the Evangelist, namely *the authority committed, the honesty enjoined* and *the profit adjoined;* so that first he sets forth *authority* as going before. Secondly he adjoins *honesty* as accompanying, at[^p217-1]: *And he said to them: Take nothing for your journey.* Thirdly he subjoins *profit* as following, at: *And going out, they went about* etc. — But since words, of however authentic a preacher they may be[^p217-2], are inefficacious unless they have divine testimonies; and such are divine miracles: therefore in the first part there is first set out *the conferring of power to work miracles,* then *the committing of authority to preach the mysteries.* For the first makes the way for the second.

2. (Vers. 1.). As to *the conferring of power to work miracles,* then, he says: *Then Jesus*[^p217-3] *calling together the twelve Apostles,* as namely the principal prelates, chosen out of all the others, according to what is said above, the sixth chapter: « Jesus called his disciples, and of them he chose twelve, whom also he named *Apostles* ». These then *being called together,* that is, called into one to commend unity, in which the Holy Spirit is given, according to what is said in the Acts, the second chapter[^p217-4]: « When the days of the Pentecost were accomplished, all the disciples were together in one place » etc. Whence in Numbers, the eleventh chapter: « The Lord said to Moses: Gather unto me seventy men at the door of the tabernacle of the covenant, and I will take of thy spirit, and will give to them ». — So now; whence it is also subjoined: *He gave them power and authority,* so that *power* is in respect of spiritual things, *authority* in respect of corporal things. On account of which, to explain this, he adjoins: *Over all devils,* that this may be referred to *power; and to cure diseases,* that this may be referred to *authority;* and by this they would have a twofold genus of testimony according to the twofold genus of creature, namely spiritual and corporal. And this is what is said expressly in Mark, the third chapter[^p217-5]: « He gave power to cure sicknesses, and to cast out devils »; so that there may truly be fulfilled that of the Psalm: « The Lord shall give the word to them that preach good tidings[^tr-evangelizare] with great power ».

3. (Vers. 2.). But as to *the committing of authority to preach the mysteries* he subjoins: *And he sent them to preach the kingdom of God.* For in the *sending* is designated the *authority* without which no one ought to preach; whence to the Romans, the tenth chapter[^p217-6]: « How shall they preach, unless they be sent »? Whence Isaias too did not dare to preach unless he were sent; Isaias, the sixth chapter: « Lo, here am I, O Lord, send me ». Contrariwise, of the false prophets in Jeremias, the twenty-third chapter: « I did not send them, and they ran ». But such were not the Apostles, who were therefore so named, that their authority might be commended. For Apostle is interpreted *sent;* and they were sent to preach, according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p217-7]: « The Lord sent me not to baptize, but to preach the gospel ». — They were sent to preach no small thing, but a great one, namely *the kingdom of God,* by which can be understood *the doctrine of truth,* according to that of Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p217-8]: « The kingdom of God shall be taken from you, and shall be given to a nation yielding the fruits thereof ». It can also be called *the grace of the Holy Spirit,* according to that of Romans, the fourteenth chapter: « The kingdom of God is not meat and drink, but justice and peace and joy in the Holy Spirit »; below, the seventeenth chapter: « The kingdom of God is within you ». It can also be called *eternal glory,* according to that of John, the third chapter[^p217-9]: « Unless a man be born again of water and the Holy Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God ». In all these ways the Apostles were sent to preach the kingdom of God, namely true doctrine, divine grace and eternal glory. — And since on account of the authority of preaching he had granted the power of curing, therefore he subjoins: *And to heal the sick,* namely he sent them for the confirmation of the truth preached, according to that of Mark, the last chapter[^p217-10]: « They going forth preached every where, the Lord working withal, and confirming the word with signs that followed ». Whence the sign of the mission of spiritual preaching to preach is the healing of the hearers from the diseases of vices.

4. Now there are three evident signs that a preacher is sent by the Lord[^p217-11] to preach the Gospel. The first is the *authority* of the one sending, such as is that of a pontiff, and most of all of that pontiff[^p217-12] who is in the place of Peter, nay in the place of Jesus Christ; whence he who is sent by him is sent by Christ. The second is *zeal for souls* in the person who is sent, when namely he seeks principally the honour of God and the salvation of souls. Thirdly[^p217-13], when there follows *the fructifying and conversion* of the hearers. — By the first they are messengers of the Father,
<!-- page 218 -->
by the second of the Son, by the third of the Holy Spirit. Of such it is said: « I have appointed you, that you should go, and should bring forth fruit, and your fruit should remain », John, the fifteenth chapter[^p218-1]. And he who is so sent can say that of Isaias, the sixty-first chapter: « The spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me » etc.

5. (Vers. 3.). *And he said to them: Take nothing for your journey.* After the explanation of the authority committed is subjoined *the description of the honesty enjoined.* Now this consists in three things, namely in *poverty of food, slenderness of clothing* and *humility of living together.*

First then, as to *poverty in food,* it is said: *And he said to them: Take nothing for your journey,* namely on occasion of temporal sustenance, so that by example you may preach that of the first to Timothy, the sixth chapter[^p218-2]: « We brought nothing into this world, and doubtless neither can we carry any thing out ». And that it may be better known, he descends to the special, when he subjoins: *Neither staff,* namely for support, *nor scrip,* for storing, *nor bread,* for eating, *nor money,* for buying. In which is the prohibition of all sustenance, that it may be shown that the preacher, out of the greatness of his hope, ought to lean on God alone, according to that of the first of Peter, the fifth chapter[^p218-3]: « Casting all your care upon him, for he hath care of you ». Whence also blessed Francis, when he sent the brothers to preach, said to them that of the Psalm[^p218-4]: « Cast thy thought upon the Lord » etc. Now in this he does not forbid, as Augustine says, studious providence, but curious solicitude, according to that of Matthew, the sixth chapter[^p218-5]: « Be not solicitous for to morrow; for sufficient for the day is the evil thereof ».

6. And therefore, for the right intelligence of this precept, what one Evangelist denies, namely Luke, another Evangelist affirms, namely Mark, the sixth chapter[^p218-6], where it is said: « And Jesus enjoined them that they should take nothing for the way, but a staff only ». Upon which Augustine says in *On the Agreement of the Evangelists*: « Both are a precept from the Lord, that they should carry nothing but a staff, and that they should not carry even a
staff. *Not to carry a staff* is not to be solicitous; *to carry nothing but a staff* is to use the power, granted to preachers, of receiving ». Whence in Luke, the tenth chapter[^p218-7]: « Eating and drinking such things as they have ». — Now the *literal* observance of this precept belongs to perfect preachers, who imitate Christ perfectly; in whose person Peter says in Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p218-8]: « Behold, we have left all things, and have followed thee ». But the *spiritual* observance extends itself to all, that the preacher of truth be a despiser of earthliness; otherwise, if they announce the word of God out of the cupidity of gain, they are not disciples of Christ, but of Balaam the soothsayer; according to that of the second of Peter, the second chapter[^p218-9]: « Having their heart exercised with covetousness, children of malediction, leaving the right way », that is, of poverty; from which these « have gone astray, having followed the way of Balaam, who loved the wages of iniquity ». — Whence the preacher of truth ought above all to avoid the vice of cupidity; otherwise he will not be a preacher, but a flatterer. On account of which the first to the Thessalonians, the second chapter[^p218-10]: « For neither have we been at any time in the speech of flattery, as you know, nor in an occasion of covetousness, God is witness ». For he knew that by this he would provoke the wrath of God, as is said in Micheas, the third chapter: « Her princes judged for bribes, and her priests taught for hire, and her prophets divined for money » etc. « For this cause Sion shall be ploughed as a field » etc.

7. Secondly, as to *slenderness in clothing,* it is subjoined: *Neither have two coats,* that so the doctrine of the Master may agree with the doctrine of the Precursor, according to that of Luke, the third chapter[^p218-11]: « He that hath two coats, let him give one to him that hath none »; and according to that of the Apostle, the first to Timothy, the sixth chapter: « Having food, and wherewith to be covered, with these let us be content ». Now in this, that he does not forbid single clothing, but double, he grants necessity and forbids curiosity in changing and superfluity in possessing. Whence Bede[^p218-12]: « *Single* clothing is whatever
<!-- page 219 -->
is necessary to anyone according to the quality of the place or time; *double,* what is beyond necessity »; and this is manifest in the Lord himself, who besides the coat without seam had other garments, which were divided, as is said in John, the nineteenth chapter. — But according to the *spiritual* intelligence there is forbidden the doubleness of dissembling and hypocrisy, that they should not have one garment in secret and another in the open, lest they be of those of whom it is said in Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p219-1]: « They come to you in the clothing of sheep, but inwardly they are ravening wolves ». Shunning this likewise, blessed Francis, when he was ill, would not put a piece of fur on the inside before his breast, unless he also put one on the outside[^p219-2].

8. (Vers. 4.). But thirdly, as to *humility in living together,* he subjoins: *And whatsoever house you shall enter into, abide there,* namely being content with that lodging; *and depart not from thence,* for the sake of seeking a better, because, as is said in Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p219-3], « it is a miserable life to go as a guest from house to house ». For the changing of lodging bears witness to impatience, which is moved for a small cause and cannot rest, according to that of Jeremias, the fourteenth chapter: « This people hath loved to move their feet, and hath not rested, and hath not pleased the Lord ». — And this is most of all to be avoided by the preacher, because, according to that of Proverbs, the nineteenth chapter[^p219-4], « the learning of a man is known by patience ». And therefore Ambrose: « It is foreign to the preacher to run about through houses and to change the rights of an unmoved hospitality ». Now the mother of this inconstancy and impatience is pride, which can dwell in peace with no one, according to that of Proverbs, the thirteenth chapter[^p219-5]: « Among the proud there are always contentions ».

9. (Vers. 5.). And since it belongs to true humility to refuse the company of no one, nor yet to be burdensome to another; therefore, for the perfection of humility, it is subjoined: *And whosoever will not receive you,* namely by despising your doctrine, as those also who did not receive the Lord; John, the first chapter[^p219-6]: « He came unto his own, and his own received him not ». — And since this is a great offence, with which there is to be no communion; therefore he subjoins: *Going out of that city,* namely by shunning their wickedness, according to that of Numbers, the sixteenth chapter[^p219-7]: « Depart from the tents of wicked men, and touch not the things that are theirs, lest you be involved with them »; Isaias, the fifty-second chapter: « Depart from the midst of them, touch no unclean thing »; and Jeremias, the forty-eighth chapter: « Leave the cities, and dwell in the rock ». — And this, lest they be defiled with the filth of others; on account of which he adds: *And*[^p219-8] *shake off the dust of your feet for a testimony upon them,* namely to show your innocence. This Barnabas and Paul did, of whom it is said in the Acts, the thirteenth chapter, that « shaking off the dust of their feet, they came to Iconium ». Now *to shake the dust from the feet* is to permit nothing of the vain glory of men to cleave to one's affections. For vain glory is rightly designated by *dust,* which is lifted up by the wind, according to that of the Psalm[^p219-9]: « He shall be like the dust, which the wind driveth from the face of the earth ».

10. (Vers. 6.). *And going out, they went about* etc. After the authority committed and the honesty enjoined the Evangelist here thirdly subjoins *the profit adjoined.* Now a fourfold profit is shown to have followed upon the preaching of the Apostles, namely the arousing *of men to hear,* the arousing *of the hearers to confer,* the arousing *of those conferring to admire,* the arousing *of those admiring to behold.*

First then, as to *the arousing of men to hear,* it is said: *And going out, they went about through the towns, preaching the gospel, and curing every where,* so that that of the Psalm[^p219-10] now began to be fulfilled: « Their sound hath gone forth into all the earth ». For although not all grasped their doctrine, yet on account of the miracles
<!-- page 220 -->
they were willing to hear the doctrine; whence it is said in the Acts, the eighth chapter: « The multitudes were attentive to those things which were said by Philip, with one accord hearing and seeing the signs which he did ». And therefore these not only preached, but also cured every where, that all might be aroused to hear by their zeal, both by word and by sign; in which is set out not only the profit of the hearers, but also the faithfulness of the preachers. For because *they went about,* they were free from idleness; because *through the towns,* free from elation; because *preaching the gospel,* free from triflingness; because *curing every where,* from respect of persons. And therefore they had the end in themselves, which is to speak sufficiently for persuasion and to arouse the hearers[^p220-1]. And this is the first and general end.

11. (Vers. 7.). The second end or special profit is *the arousing of the hearers to confer;* as to which it is said: *Now Herod the tetrarch heard of all things that were done by him,* so that the fame of the name of Christ was carried by the Apostles not only to the peoples, but also to kings, as is said of Paul in the Acts, the ninth chapter[^p220-2]: « He is a vessel of election to me, to carry my name before the Gentiles, and kings, and the children of Israel ». Whence the hearers could now say that which the Gabaonites said in Josue, the ninth chapter: « We have heard the fame of the power of your God, all the things that he did ». And by this hearing he was aroused to inquire and to doubt, on account of the variety of those holding opinions.

12. (Vers. 7. 8.). Whence it is subjoined: *And he was in a doubt, because it was said by some, that John was risen from the dead.* And to this opinion Herod adhered the more; whence in Matthew, the fourteenth chapter[^p220-3]: « Herod heard the fame of Jesus, and said to his servants: This is John the Baptist; he is risen from the dead, and therefore mighty works shew forth themselves in him ». Now this he supposed on account of *the eminence of the holiness of his life,* because, as is said in Mark, the sixth chapter[^p220-4], « Herod feared John, knowing him to be a just and holy man ». Yet he did not adhere firmly to this opinion; therefore it is said that *he was in a doubt,* that is, he wavered, according to that of James, the first chapter: « He that wavereth is like a wave of the sea, which is moved and carried about by the wind ». — And this was on account of other opinions; whence he subjoins: *But by some, that Elias had appeared,* namely it was said. And this opinion seemed probable on account of *the eminence of power.* Whence in Ecclesiasticus, the forty-eighth chapter[^p220-5]: « Elias was magnified in his wondrous works ». And
this opinion seemed the more credible, because, as is said in John, the tenth chapter, « John indeed did no sign ». Whence Christ, working miracles, seemed rather to be Elias. — But yet not all assented to this; on account of which he adds: *But by others, that one of the old Prophets was risen again.* And this they supposed on account of *the knowledge of the truth;* because, according to that of Amos, the third chapter[^p220-6], « the Lord will not do a word, unless he have revealed his secret to his servants the Prophets ». And so it is plain that the opinion was threefold on account of the threefold excellence which they heard of Christ, namely of holiness, of power and of the knowledge of the truth. And therefore they conferred with one another, and some believed this, and some another thing[^p220-7].

13. (Vers. 9.). Now the third end was *the arousing of those conferring to admire;* as to which it is subjoined: *And Herod said: John I have beheaded,* as a mortal and passible man. Herod had beheaded him on account of John's truth and Herod's malignity, according to what is said in Matthew, the fourteenth chapter[^p220-8]: « John said to Herod: It is not lawful for thee to have thy brother's wife »; and it follows after that for this cause he beheaded him. And so from the very death he judges infirmity in John, however great he was preached to be. — And therefore from this comparison he admired the power of Christ; whence he subjoins: *But who is this of whom I hear such things*[^p220-9]? that is, such great marvels; whence he could say: « Who is the great God like our God? Thou art the God that dost marvels », such namely as had not been heard before, according to that of John, the ninth chapter: « From the beginning of the world it hath not been heard, that any man hath opened the eyes of one born blind » etc.

14. The fourth end was *the arousing of those admiring to behold;* on account of which it is subjoined: *And he sought to see him.* For we gladly see what we admire. Yet he did not then attain it, according to what is said in Luke, the tenth chapter[^p220-10]: « Amen I say to you, that many kings and Prophets have desired to see the things that you see, and have not seen them ». But he did not attain it, because he did not seek studiously; whence in Proverbs, the fourteenth chapter: « A scorner seeketh wisdom, and findeth it not ». Whence it is to be considered very solicitously how God ought to be sought, that he may be seen and found by those who seek him.
