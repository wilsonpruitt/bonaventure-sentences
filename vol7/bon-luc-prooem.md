## Latin
<!-- page 3 -->

### Prooemium commentarii in Lucam

1. *Spiritus Domini super me, eo quod unxerit Dominus me. Ad annuntiandum mansuetis misit me, ut mederer contritos*[^p3-1] *corde et praedicarem captivis indulgentiam et clausis apertionem.* Considerantibus nobis aliquod verbum, quod nos introducat in Evangelium beati Lucae, nullum magis occurrit idoneum, quam quod ipse beatus Lucas refert Christum Dominum in suo praedicationis exordio assumsisse, verbum scilicet propositum, quod scribitur Lucae quarto[^p3-2] et sumtum est de Isaiae sexagesimo primo.

2. Hoc autem secundum *generalem* intellectum competere potest cuilibet doctori sacrae Scripturae; secundum *specialem* vero, beato Lucae evangelistae; secundum *singularem,* ipsi Christo, qui est fons veritatis et gratiae.

Secundum autem quod *generaliter* intelligitur, est insinuativum *duplicis personae* ad opus necessariae[^p3-3], scilicet doctoris et auditoris; secundum quod *specialiter, duplicis causae extrinsecae,* scilicet efficientis et finalis; secundum vero quod *singulariter, duplicis causae intrinsecae,* scilicet materialis et formalis. His autem sex praecognitis, ingressus in sequentia erit satis facilis.

3. Primo igitur propositum verbum secundum intellectum *generalem* indicat nobis, quis et qualis debeat esse *doctor* huius Scripturae evangelicae, adiungens nihilominus, qualem debeat habere *auditorem.* Doctor autem Scripturae evangelicae debet esse *inunctus* divina gratia, *institutus* mera obedientia, *inflammatus* benevolentia fraterna. — *Inunctus* debet esse divina gratia; et hoc notatur, cum dicit: *Spiritus Domini super me, eo quod unxerit me.* In cuius rei figuram in veteri testamento inungebantur Prophetae; unde dixit Dominus Eliae tertii Regum decimo nono[^p3-4]: *Eliseum, filium Saphat, unges Prophetam pro te;* et de David dicitur primi Regum decimo sexto, quod postquam inunctus est, *directus est Spiritus Domini in eum ab illa die et in reliquum.* Ideo enim inungebantur, ut Spiritum Domini acciperent, per quem nobis divina secreta reserantur. Si ergo eodem spiritu exponendae sunt Scripturae, quo conditae sunt; et *Spiritu sancto inspirante, locuti sunt sancti Dei homines*[^p3-5]; ad hoc, quod aliquis sit doctor idoneus eorum quae per Christum sunt suggesta et per Spiritum sanctum scripta, necesse est, quod sit inunctus superna gratia.

4. Debet etiam *institutus* esse mera obedientia, et hoc notatur ibi: *Ad annuntiandum mansuetis misit me,* exemplo Moysis, ad quem Dominus Exodi tertio[^p3-6] dixit: *Veni, mittam te ad Pharaonem, ut educas populum meum, filios Israel, de Aegypto;* et Moyses: *Quis ego sum, ut educam filios Israel de Aegypto?* Moyses, qui fuit legislator et eductor filiorum Israel de Aegypto, significat doctorem legis divinae, qui educit populum Domini de tenebris ignorantiae. Ad quod quidem officium nemo debet accedere, nisi mittatur, nec debet procurare, sed recusare magis, quia nullus ad hoc officium se debet idoneum reputare. Si enim cum Domino non est locutus, indignus est; sin ei Dominus loquitur ad cor, sentit se *impeditioris et tardioris linguae* effectum cum Moyse[^p3-7]. Et ideo nescius eloqui divina mysteria, quae aperuit ei Dominus, non debet illa praesumere, nisi institutus mera obedientia.

5. Debet etiam *inflammatus* esse fraterna benevolentia, quod notat, cum dicit: *Ut mederer contritos corde et praedicarem captivis indulgentiam et*
<!-- page 4 -->
*clausis apertionem;* et hoc exemplo Pauli, qui dicit de se ipso primae ad Thessalonicenses secundo[^p4-1]: *Facti sumus parvuli in medio vestri, tanquam si nutrix foveat filios suos; ita desiderantes vos cupide, volebamus tradere vobis non solum Evangelium Dei, sed etiam animas nostras.* Sicut enim proles carnalis non potest generari sine amore carnali, sic nec spiritualis sine benevolentia spirituali. Unde Gregorius[^p4-2]: « Qui caritatem erga alterum non habet praedicationis officium assumere nequaquam debet »; Evangelium autem Dei exponere et docere est verbum divinum praedicare: et ideo doctor inflammatus debet esse fraterna benevolentia.

6. Hic autem doctor, ut doctrina evangelica in ipso habeat efficaciam[^p4-3], debet habere *auditorem humilem, mitem* et *fidelem.* — Auditor namque doctrinae evangelicae debet esse *mitis in affatu* per accommodationem auditus; propter quod dicit: *Ad annuntiandum,* scilicet *mansuetis,* non discolis. Soli enim mansueti verba divina et evangelica recte intelligunt; Ecclesiastici quinto[^p4-4]: *Esto mansuetus ad audiendum verbum Dei, ut intelligas;* et in Psalmo: *Docebit mites vias suas.* Et propterea dicitur Iacobi primo: *In mansuetudine suscipite insitum verbum, quod potest salvare animas vestras.* Doctrina etenim evangelica docet hominem esse discipulum Christi, qui dicit Matthaei undecimo[^p4-5]: *Discite a me, quia mitis sum.* Litigatio enim et altercatio non competit discipulis evangelicis, sed Aristotelicis; unde secundae ad Timotheum secundo: *Servum Domini non oportet litigare, sed mansuetum esse ad omnes, docibilem, patientem* etc. Unde Augustinus secundo de Doctrina christiana[^p4-6]: « Opus est mitescere pietate neque contradicere divinae Scripturae sive intellectae, si aliqua vitia nostra percutit, sive non intellectae, quasi nos aliquid melius sapere meliusque percipere possimus; sed cogitare potius et credere, id esse melius et verius, quod scriptum est ».

7. Debet esse *humilis in affectu* per contritionem spiritus; propter quod dicit: *Ut mederer contritos corde. Moeror* enim *in corde viri humiliabit eum,* Proverbiorum duodecimo[^p4-7]; et talis est idoneus ad discendum; Psalmus: *Bonum mihi, quia humiliasti me, ut discam iustificationes tuas;* Matthaei undecimo: *Abscondisti haec a sapientibus et prudentibus et revelasti ea parvulis.*

8. Debet postremo esse *fidelis in assensu* per captivationem intellectus; quod notat, cum dicit: *Et praedicarem captivis indulgentiam;* secundae ad Corinthios decimo[^p4-8]: *In captivitatem redigentes omnem intellectum in obsequium Christi.* Et hoc quidem fit per veram fidem, sine qua impossibile est, documenta Scripturae evangelicae intelligere; Isaiae septimo, secundum aliam translationem: *Nisi credideritis, non intelligetis.* Si enim in discibilibus oportet discentem credere, quanto magis in divinis? Et haec captivatio facit liberos a peccato; Actuum decimo quinto[^p4-9]: *Fide purificans corda eorum;* et Ioannis primo: *Dedit eis potestatem filios Dei fieri, his qui credunt in nomine eius.* — Sic igitur patet, quod verbum hoc, *generaliter* expositum, indicat *duplicem personam* et describit, scilicet boni *doctoris* et *auditoris.*

9. *Specialiter* autem intellectum, secundum quod competit beato Lucae, indicativum est *duplicis causae extrinsecae,* scilicet *efficientis* et *finalis*[^p4-10]: *efficientis* in hoc quod dicit: *Spiritus Domini super me, eo quod unxerit me Dominus; finalis* vero in hoc quod sequitur: *Ad annuntiandum mansuetis* etc.

10. Causa autem *efficiens* exprimitur satis perfecte: quoniam insinuatur causa efficiens *suprema* in hoc quod dicitur: *Spiritus Domini.* Ipse enim est, de quo Ioannis decimo sexto[^p4-11]: *Cum venerit ille Spiritus veritatis, docebit vos omnem veritatem.* Unde ipse est, qui loquebatur per Evangelistas et locutus est per beatum Lucam, secundum illud Matthaei decimo: *Vos non estis, qui loquimini, sed Spiritus Patris vestri, qui loquitur in vobis;* et Lucae vigesimo primo: *Ego dabo vobis os et sapientiam* etc.

11. Causa *efficiens infima* in hoc quod dicit: *Super me,* id est super beatum Lucam, de quo dicit beatus Hieronymus[^p4-12], quod « obiit plenus Spiritu sancto »; et ideo idoneus fuit ad Evangelium conscribendum, secundum quod de ipso dicit Apostolus secundae ad Corinthios octavo: *Misimus carissimum fratrem nostrum, cuius laus est in Evangelio per omnes Ecclesias.* Unde sibi competit istud Ecclesiastici: *In medio ecclesiae aperuit os eius* etc.

12. Causa *efficiens intermedia,* cum dicitur: *Eo quod unxerit me Dominus.* Unctio enim gratiae
<!-- page 5 -->
disponit animam ad suscipiendum documenta veritatis a Doctore summo; unde primae Ioannis secundo[^p5-1]: *Unctio eius docebit vos de omnibus.* Spiritus enim sanctus per gratiam docuit Evangelistam, et ille instructus docuit Ecclesiam scribendo doctrinam evangelicam. — Et ita in hoc opere triplex fuit causa[^p5-2], scilicet *suprema,* quae quidem Spiritus sancti persona; *infima,* ipse Evangelista; et *intermedia,* scilicet Spiritus sancti gratia; quae omnia intelliguntur in auctoritate praemissa, secundum quod intelligitur proferri a beato Luca.

13. Insinuatur etiam causa *finalis* satis perfecte in hoc quod sequitur[^p5-3], scilicet *prima, media* et *postrema.* Prima est *manifestatio veritatis,* media est *curatio infirmitatis,* tertia est *reseratio aeternitatis;* primum est *gratiae praeparantis;* secundum, *gratiae gratum facientis;* tertium, *gloriae consummantis.*

14. Insinuatur igitur primo causa *finalis prima* huius doctrinae, quae est *manifestatio veritatis,* in hoc quod dicit: *Ad annuntiandum mansuetis misit me,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p5-4]: *Annuntiaverunt opera Dei, et facta eius intellexerunt;* unde et ab hoc nomen accepit, ut *Evangelium* dicatur, id est bona nuntiatio; primae Ioannis primo: *Quod fuit ab initio, quod audivimus, quod vidimus oculis nostris, quod perspeximus, et manus nostrae contrectaverunt de verbo vitae, et vita manifestata est; et vidimus et testamur et annuntiamus vobis vitam aeternam, quae erat apud Patrem et apparuit nobis;* et Isaiae quinquagesimo secundo: *Quam pulcri super montes pedes annuntiantis et praedicantis pacem, annuntiantis bonum, praedicantis salutem!* Hoc primo fuit in sanctis Evangelistis.

15. *Secunda* vero fuit *medicatio nostrae infirmitatis,* quam tangit, cum ait: *Ut mederer contritos corde.* Sermo enim evangelicae praedicationis effectum et fructum affert curationis, secundum illud Sapientiae decimo sexto[^p5-5]: *Etenim neque herba neque malagma sanavit eos, sed tuus, Domine, sermo, qui sanat omnia.* Hunc autem fructum bene affert Evangelium Lucae; de quo Hieronymus: « Si novimus Lucam medicum, *cuius laus est in Evangelio,* animadvertimus pariter, omnia verba illius animae languentis esse medicinam ». Et hoc est secundum intentum a beato Luca, ut scilicet per veritatis cognitionem veniremus ad infirmitatis medicationem.

16. *Tertia* vero et postrema fuit *reseratio aeternitatis,* quae notatur, cum dicitur: *Ut praedicarem captivis indulgentiam et clausis apertionem.* Hoc autem fit in aeternae vitae possessione, ad quod hortatur evangelica doctrina tanquam ad ultimum finem; Ioannis vigesimo[^p5-6]: *Haec scripta sunt, ut credatis, quia Iesus est Christus Filius Dei; et ut credentes vitam habeatis in nomine eius;* et Marci ultimo: *Euntes in mundum universum, praedicate Evangelium omni creaturae; qui crediderit et baptizatus fuerit salvus erit.* Hoc mandatum impleverunt praecipue Evangelistae, qui non solum Evangelium praedicaverunt verbo ad praesentes tunc, sed etiam scripto ad praesentes et futuros in omnes generationes, ut omnes facerent salvos. — Sic igitur praemissum verbum *specialiter* intellectum in persona beati Lucae est insinuativum duplicis *causae extrinsecae,* scilicet efficientis et finalis.

17. *Singulariter* vero intellectum de Domino Iesu, de quo proprie dictum est, insinuativum est duplicis causae *intrinsecae,* scilicet *materialis* et *formalis;* et hoc patet sic. Certum enim est, quod evangelica historia tota circa Christum versatur, vel in quantum *mediator* est, vel in quantum *praedicator,* vel in quantum *reparator,* vel in quantum *triumphator. Mediator* respicit incarnationis mysterium; *praedicator,* eruditionis magisterium; *reparator,* passionis remedium; *triumphator,* resurrectionis tropaeum. Primum refertur ad Christi *naturam;* secundum, ad *doctrinam;* tertium, ad *victimam;* quartum, ad *victoriam.* Haec autem quatuor distincte et ordinate tanguntur in sermone praemisso, secundum quod competit Christo.

18. Insinuat igitur Christus Iesus, se esse *mediatorem,* cum dicit: *Spiritus Domini super me, eo quod unxerit me.* Ipse enim est mediator, de quo Actuum decimo[^p5-7]: *Unxit eum Deus Spiritu sancto et virtute; unxit,* inquam, non sicut alios Sanctos, sed supra alios, secundum illud Psalmi: *Unxit te Deus, Deus tuus oleo laetitiae prae consortibus tuis;* in qua unctione facta est consummatio prophetiae Danielis nono: *Impleatur visio et prophetia, et ungatur Sanctus Sanctorum.*

19. Insinuat se *praedicatorem* in hoc quod subiungit: *Ad annuntiandum mansuetis misit me,* secundum quod promiserat Dominus filiis Israel per Moysen Deuteronomii decimo octavo[^p5-8]: *Prophetam suscitabo eis de medio fratrum suorum, similem tui; et ponam verba mea in ore eius, loqueturque*
<!-- page 6 -->
*ad eos omnia, quae praecepero ei.* Hic fuit Christus, Dominus omnium Prophetarum, qui dicit Ioannis decimo quinto: *Omnia, quaecumque audivi a Patre meo, nota feci vobis.* Et ad hoc missus est; unde dicitur Lucae quarto: *Aliis civitatibus oportet me evangelizare regnum Dei, quia ideo missus sum.*

20. Insinuat etiam sè *reparatorem* in hoc quod subdit: *Ut mederer contritos corde.* Ipse enim est, de quo dicitur Actuum decimo[^p6-1]: *Pertransivit benefaciendo et sanando omnes oppressos a diabolo;* unde de ipso dicitur in Psalmo: *Qui sanat contritos corde* etc. Hoc autem fecit per passionis remedium; Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio: *Vere languores nostros ipse tulit et dolores nostros ipse portavit; ipse autem vulneratus est propter iniquitates nostras, attritus est propter scelera nostra, disciplina pacis nostrae super eum* etc.

21. Insinuat etiam quarto se *triumphatorem* in hoc quod dicit: *Ut praedicarem captivis indulgentiam et clausis apertionem;* et hoc quidem fecit in suae resurrectionis triumpho, secundum quod dicitur ad Colossenses secundo[^p6-2] de Christo: *Exspolians principatus et potestates, traduxit confidenter, palam triumphans illos in semetipso;* et deinde, sicut in Psalmo dicitur, *ascendens in altum, captivam duxit captivitatem, dedit dona hominibus.* — Quoniam igitur sub hac quadruplici conditione est Christus Dominus obiectum fidei et subiectum Evangelii, recte in verbo proposito causa *materialis* sive subiectum designatur.

22. Sed quoniam « scientiae secantur in res[^p6-3] », et inductio *formae* est secundum dispositionem materiae; cum subiectum Evangelii sit hoc unum sub quadruplici conditione et intentione: est necesse, unum librum ad unam tantum *principaliter* aspicere, ad alias ex consequenti, totam vero evangelicam Scripturam de his quatuor principaliter agere. Ideo necesse est, quatuor esse Evangelia, et quodlibet illorum quadripartitum.

23. Ex quo colligitur, quod liber iste debet habere et habet quatuor partes. In prima agit de *incarnationis mysterio* usque ad quartum capitulum; in secunda, de *praedicationis magisterio* usque ad vigesimum secundum; in tertia, de *passionis medicamento,* usque ad vigesimum quartum; in quarta, de *resurrectionis tropaeo,* usque in finem totius libri. — Licet autem omnes has conditiones intendat ad perfectionem historiae, principaliter tamen aspicit[^p6-4] ad *Christi sacerdotium* et *passionis medicamentum;* et hoc medico debebatur. — Colligitur ergo ex verbo proposito, secundum quod convenit Christo, causa *materialis* sive subiectum, et *formalis,* quae est ordinatio partium et capitulorum et agendi modus in prosecutione Scripturarum.

24. Haec autem duo optime praefigurata sunt in illo animali, quod vidit Ezechiel[^p6-5], et quod dicit esse quadripartitum, et rursus quodlibet illorum quatuor facierum, unius tamen principaliter. Quarum prima similis erat *homini,* per quam intelligimus Christi naturam; alia *leoni,* per quam intelligimus victoriam; alia *bovi,* per quam intelligimus victimam; alia *aquilae,* per quam intelligimus doctrinam. Haec autem animalia secundum omnes Sanctos[^p6-6] designant quatuor Evangelia, ita quod figuraliter exprimunt eorum materiam et formam: quia *unum* animal est quadriforme, pro eo quod de uno Christo sub quadruplici conditione sunt quatuor Evangelia, et rursus *quodlibet* illorum quatuor facierum, quia quodlibet est quadripartitum. Et iterum, unum, scilicet primum, *simile homini,* licet habeat quatuor facies, quia primus, scilicet Matthaeus, principaliter prosequitur *incarnationis mysterium.* Secundum, *simile leoni,* quia secundo scribens, scilicet Marcus, prosequitur *resurrectionis tropaeum,* et ex eadem parte, quia plurimum concordant[^p6-7]. Tertium, *simile bovi,* quia tertio scribens, scilicet Lucas, prosequitur *sacerdotium* et *passionis remedium.* Quartum vero, *simile aquilae,* quae habet oculos limpidos, quia quarto scribens, scilicet Ioannes, prosequitur evangelicum
<!-- page 7 -->
Christi *magisterium,* ad quod alii non potuerunt attingere, et ideo describitur *super eos;* Ezechielis primo[^p7-1]: *Facies aquilae desuper.* — Et sic patet, quomodo veritas respondet figurae, patent etiam illa sex praeambula ad doctrinam, scilicet qualis *doctor,* qualis *auditor,* quis *efficiens,* quis *finis,* quae *materia,* et quae *forma,* et ex hoc quaedam totius Libri generalis intelligentia.

### Prooemium of the commentary on Luke

1. *The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because the Lord hath anointed me. He hath sent me to announce to the meek, to heal the contrite*[^p3-1] *of heart, and to preach a release to the captives, and opening to them that are shut up.*[^tr-annuntiare] As we considered some word that might lead us into the Gospel of blessed Luke, none occurred more fitting than that which blessed Luke himself relates that Christ the Lord took up at the opening of his preaching, namely the word set forth, which is written in Luke, the fourth chapter[^p3-2], and is taken from Isaias, the sixty-first chapter.

2. Now this, according to the *general* understanding, can befit any doctor of sacred Scripture; according to the *special* understanding, blessed Luke the Evangelist; according to the *singular,* Christ himself, who is the fount of truth and of grace.

Now according as it is understood *generally,* it is suggestive of *two persons* necessary to the work[^p3-3], namely the doctor and the hearer; according as it is understood *specially,* of *two extrinsic causes,* namely the efficient and the final; and according as it is understood *singularly,* of *two intrinsic causes,* namely the material and the formal. And these six being known beforehand, the entry into what follows will be easy enough.

3. First, then, the word set forth, according to the *general* understanding, indicates to us who and of what sort the *doctor* of this gospel Scripture ought to be, adding none the less what sort of *hearer* he ought to have. Now the doctor of the gospel Scripture ought to be *anointed* with divine grace, *established* by pure obedience, *inflamed* with fraternal benevolence. — He ought to be *anointed* with divine grace; and this is noted when he says: *The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me.* In figure of which thing the Prophets were anointed in the old testament; whence the Lord said to Elias in the third of Kings, the nineteenth chapter[^p3-4]: *Eliseus, the son of Saphat, thou shalt anoint to be prophet in thy room;* and of David it is said in the first of Kings, the sixteenth chapter, that after he was anointed, *the Spirit of the Lord came upon him from that day and forward.* For they were anointed for this reason, that they might receive the Spirit of the Lord, through whom divine secrets are unlocked to us. If then the Scriptures are to be expounded by the same spirit by which they were composed; and *the holy men of God spoke, inspired by the Holy Spirit*[^p3-5]; in order that someone be a fit doctor of the things which were suggested through Christ and written through the Holy Spirit, it is necessary that he be anointed with grace from above.

4. He ought also to be *established* by pure obedience, and this is noted there: *He hath sent me to announce to the meek,* after the example of Moses, to whom the Lord said in Exodus, the third chapter[^p3-6]: *Come, I will send thee to Pharao, that thou mayst bring forth my people, the children of Israel, out of Egypt;* and Moses: *Who am I, that I should bring forth the children of Israel out of Egypt?* Moses, who was the lawgiver and the leader of the children of Israel out of Egypt, signifies the doctor of the divine law, who leads the people of the Lord out of the darkness of ignorance. To which office no one ought to approach unless he is sent, nor ought he to procure it, but rather to refuse it, because no one ought to reckon himself fit for this office. For if he has not spoken with the Lord, he is unworthy; but if the Lord speaks to his heart, he feels himself become *of a more impeded and slower tongue* with Moses[^p3-7]. And therefore, not knowing how to utter the divine mysteries which the Lord has opened to him, he ought not to presume upon them unless he is established by pure obedience.

5. He ought also to be *inflamed* with fraternal benevolence, which he notes when he says: *To heal the contrite of heart, and to preach a release to the captives, and*
<!-- page 4 -->
*opening to them that are shut up;* and this after the example of Paul, who says of himself in the first to the Thessalonians, the second chapter[^p4-1]: *We became little ones in the midst of you, as if a nurse should cherish her children; so desirous of you, we would gladly impart unto you not only the Gospel of God, but also our own souls.* For as carnal offspring cannot be begotten without carnal love, so neither spiritual offspring without spiritual benevolence. Whence Gregory[^p4-2]: « He who has not charity towards another ought by no means to take up the office of preaching »; but to expound and teach the Gospel of God is to preach the divine word: and therefore the doctor ought to be inflamed with fraternal benevolence.

6. Now this doctor, in order that the gospel teaching may have efficacy in him[^p4-3], ought to have a *humble, meek* and *faithful hearer.* — For the hearer of the gospel teaching ought to be *meek in address* through the accommodation of his hearing; on account of which he says: *To announce,* namely *to the meek,* not to the froward. For only the meek rightly understand the divine and gospel words; Ecclesiasticus, the fifth chapter[^p4-4]: *Be meek to hear the word of God, that thou mayst understand;* and in the Psalm: *He will teach the meek his ways.* And for this reason it is said in James, the first chapter: *With meekness receive the ingrafted word, which is able to save your souls.* For the gospel teaching teaches a man to be a disciple of Christ, who says in Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p4-5]: *Learn of me, because I am meek.* For litigation and altercation do not befit the disciples of the Gospel, but those of Aristotle; whence in the second to Timothy, the second chapter: *The servant of the Lord must not wrangle, but be mild towards all men, apt to teach, patient* etc. Whence Augustine in the second book *On Christian Doctrine*[^p4-6]: « There is need to grow meek by piety and not to contradict the divine Scripture, whether understood, if it strikes at some vices of ours, or not understood, as though we could know something better and perceive it better; but rather to think and believe that what is written is better and truer ».

7. He ought to be *humble in affection* through contrition of spirit; on account of which he says: *To heal the contrite of heart. Grief* indeed *in the heart of a man shall bring him low,* Proverbs, the twelfth chapter[^p4-7]; and such a one is fit for learning; the Psalm: *It is good for me that thou hast humbled me, that I may learn thy justifications;* Matthew, the eleventh chapter: *Thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them to little ones.*

8. Lastly he ought to be *faithful in assent* through the captivation of the understanding; which he notes when he says: *And to preach a release to the captives;* the second to the Corinthians, the tenth chapter[^p4-8]: *Bringing into captivity every understanding unto the obedience of Christ.* And this indeed comes about through true faith, without which it is impossible to understand the lessons of the gospel Scripture; Isaias, the seventh chapter, according to another translation: *Unless you believe, you will not understand.* For if in things that can be learned the learner must believe, how much more in divine things? And this captivation makes men free from sin; Acts, the fifteenth chapter[^p4-9]: *Purifying their hearts by faith;* and John, the first chapter: *He gave them power to be made the sons of God, to them that believe in his name.* — Thus, then, it is plain that this word, expounded *generally,* indicates *two persons* and describes them, namely of the good *doctor* and *hearer.*

9. But understood *specially,* according as it befits blessed Luke, it is indicative of *two extrinsic causes,* namely the *efficient* and the *final*[^p4-10]: the *efficient* in this, that he says: *The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because the Lord hath anointed me;* the *final* in this, that follows: *To announce to the meek* etc.

10. Now the *efficient* cause is expressed perfectly enough: for the *supreme* efficient cause is suggested in this, that it is said: *The Spirit of the Lord.* For he it is, of whom John, the sixteenth chapter[^p4-11]: *When that Spirit of truth is come, he will teach you all truth.* Whence it is he who spoke through the Evangelists and spoke through blessed Luke, according to that of Matthew, the tenth chapter: *It is not you that speak, but the Spirit of your Father that speaketh in you;* and Luke, the twenty-first chapter: *I will give you a mouth and wisdom* etc.

11. The *lowest efficient* cause, in this, that he says: *Upon me,* that is, upon blessed Luke, of whom blessed Jerome says[^p4-12] that « he died full of the Holy Spirit »; and therefore he was fit for writing the Gospel, according as the Apostle says of him in the second to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter: *We have sent the most dear brother, whose praise is in the Gospel through all the churches.* Whence that of Ecclesiasticus befits him: *In the midst of the church she shall open his mouth* etc.

12. The *intermediate efficient* cause, when it is said: *Because the Lord hath anointed me.* For the unction of grace
<!-- page 5 -->
disposes the soul to receive the lessons of truth from the supreme Doctor; whence the first of John, the second chapter[^p5-1]: *His unction teacheth you of all things.* For the Holy Spirit taught the Evangelist through grace, and he, instructed, taught the Church by writing the gospel teaching. — And so in this work there was a threefold cause[^p5-2], namely the *supreme,* which is the person of the Holy Spirit; the *lowest,* the Evangelist himself; and the *intermediate,* namely the grace of the Holy Spirit; all of which are understood in the authority set before us, according as it is understood to be uttered by blessed Luke.

13. The *final* cause too is suggested perfectly enough in this which follows[^p5-3], namely the *first, middle* and *last.* The first is the *manifestation of truth,* the middle is the *curing of infirmity,* the third is the *unlocking of eternity;* the first belongs to *preparing grace;* the second, to *grace making pleasing;* the third, to *consummating glory.*

14. First, then, the *first final* cause of this teaching is suggested, which is the *manifestation of truth,* in this, that he says: *He hath sent me to announce to the meek,* according to that of the Psalm[^p5-4]: *They announced the works of God, and understood his doings;* whence also it took its name from this, that it is called *Gospel,* that is, good announcement; the first of John, the first chapter: *That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled, of the word of life; and the life was manifested; and we have seen and do bear witness, and announce unto you the life eternal, which was with the Father, and hath appeared to us;* and Isaias, the fifty-second chapter: *How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him that announceth and preacheth peace, of him that announceth good, that preacheth salvation!* This was first in the holy Evangelists.

15. And the *second* was the *healing of our infirmity,* which he touches when he says: *To heal the contrite of heart.* For the word of gospel preaching brings the effect and fruit of curing, according to that of Wisdom, the sixteenth chapter[^p5-5]: *For it was neither herb nor mollifying plaster that healed them, but thy word, O Lord, which healeth all things.* And the Gospel of Luke brings this fruit well; of which Jerome: « If we know Luke the physician, *whose praise is in the Gospel,* we notice equally that all his words are medicine for the languishing soul ». And this is the second thing intended by blessed Luke, namely that through the knowledge of truth we might come to the healing of infirmity.

16. And the *third* and last was the *unlocking of eternity,* which is noted when it is said: *To preach a release to the captives, and opening to them that are shut up.* Now this comes about in the possession of eternal life, to which the gospel teaching exhorts as to the last end; John, the twentieth chapter[^p5-6]: *These are written, that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing, you may have life in his name;* and Mark, the last chapter: *Going into the whole world, preach the gospel to every creature; he that believeth and is baptized shall be saved.* This command the Evangelists chiefly fulfilled, who not only preached the Gospel by word to those then present, but also by writing to the present and the future in all generations, that they might make all men saved. — Thus, then, the word set before us, understood *specially* in the person of blessed Luke, is suggestive of two *extrinsic causes,* namely the efficient and the final.

17. But understood *singularly* of the Lord Jesus, of whom it was properly said, it is suggestive of two *intrinsic* causes, namely the *material* and the *formal;* and this is plain thus. For it is certain that the whole gospel history turns about Christ, either inasmuch as he is *mediator,* or inasmuch as *preacher,* or inasmuch as *repairer,* or inasmuch as *triumpher.* The *mediator* regards the mystery of the incarnation; the *preacher,* the teaching office of erudition; the *repairer,* the remedy of the passion; the *triumpher,* the trophy of the resurrection. The first is referred to Christ's *nature;* the second, to his *teaching;* the third, to the *victim;* the fourth, to the *victory.* And these four are touched distinctly and in order in the word set before us, according as it befits Christ.

18. Christ Jesus, then, suggests that he is *mediator* when he says: *The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me.* For he is the mediator, of whom Acts, the tenth chapter[^p5-7]: *God anointed him with the Holy Spirit and with power; anointed,* I say, not as the other Saints, but above the others, according to that of the Psalm: *God, thy God, hath anointed thee with the oil of gladness above thy fellows;* in which unction there came about the consummation of the prophecy of Daniel, the ninth chapter: *Vision and prophecy may be fulfilled, and the Saint of saints may be anointed.*

19. He suggests that he is *preacher* in this, that he subjoins: *He hath sent me to announce to the meek,* according as the Lord had promised to the children of Israel through Moses in Deuteronomy, the eighteenth chapter[^p5-8]: *I will raise them up a prophet out of the midst of their brethren, like to thee; and I will put my words in his mouth, and he shall speak*
<!-- page 6 -->
*to them all that I shall command him.* This was Christ, the Lord of all the Prophets, who says in John, the fifteenth chapter: *All things whatsoever I have heard of my Father, I have made known to you.* And for this he was sent; whence it is said in Luke, the fourth chapter: *To other cities also I must preach the gospel of the kingdom of God, for therefore am I sent.*

20. He suggests also that he is *repairer* in this, that he adds: *To heal the contrite of heart.* For he it is of whom it is said in Acts, the tenth chapter[^p6-1]: *He went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed by the devil;* whence of him it is said in the Psalm: *Who healeth the contrite of heart* etc. And this he did through the remedy of the passion; Isaias, the fifty-third chapter: *Surely he hath borne our infirmities and carried our sorrows; but he was wounded for our iniquities, he was bruised for our sins, the chastisement of our peace was upon him* etc.

21. He suggests also, in the fourth place, that he is *triumpher* in this, that he says: *To preach a release to the captives, and opening to them that are shut up;* and this indeed he did in the triumph of his resurrection, according as it is said in Colossians, the second chapter[^p6-2], of Christ: *Despoiling the principalities and powers, he hath exposed them confidently, in open show triumphing over them in himself;* and then, as is said in the Psalm, *ascending on high, he led captivity captive, he gave gifts to men.* — Since, then, under this fourfold condition Christ the Lord is the object of faith and the subject of the Gospel, rightly in the word set before us the *material* cause, or subject, is designated.

22. But since « the sciences are divided as their things are[^p6-3] », and the induction of a *form* is according to the disposition of the matter; since the subject of the Gospel is this one thing under a fourfold condition and intention: it is necessary that one book look *principally* to one alone, and to the others consequently, but that the whole gospel Scripture treat of these four principally. Therefore it is necessary that there be four Gospels, and each of them four-parted.

23. From which it is gathered that this book ought to have, and has, four parts. In the first it treats of the *mystery of the incarnation,* up to the fourth chapter; in the second, of the *teaching office of preaching,* up to the twenty-second; in the third, of the *medicine of the passion,* up to the twenty-fourth; in the fourth, of the *trophy of the resurrection,* up to the end of the whole book. — But although he intends all these conditions for the perfection of the history, he nevertheless looks principally[^p6-4] to *Christ's priesthood* and to the *medicine of the passion;* and this was owed to a physician. — It is gathered, therefore, from the word set before us, according as it befits Christ, the *material* cause, or subject, and the *formal,* which is the ordering of the parts and of the chapters and the mode of proceeding in the pursuit of the Scriptures.

24. Now these two are excellently prefigured in that living creature which Ezechiel saw[^p6-5], and which he says was four-parted, and again each of them of four faces, yet principally of one. Of which the first was like a *man,* by which we understand Christ's nature; another like a *lion,* by which we understand the victory; another like an *ox,* by which we understand the victim; another like an *eagle,* by which we understand the teaching. Now these living creatures, according to all the Saints[^p6-6], designate the four Gospels, so that figuratively they express their matter and form: because the living creature is *one* and four-formed, for this reason, that of the one Christ under a fourfold condition there are four Gospels; and again *each* of them of four faces, because each is four-parted. And again, one, namely the first, is *like a man,* although it has four faces, because the first, namely Matthew, principally pursues the *mystery of the incarnation.* The second, *like a lion,* because the second writer, namely Mark, pursues the *trophy of the resurrection,* and on the same side, because they agree very much[^p6-7]. The third, *like an ox,* because the third writer, namely Luke, pursues the *priesthood* and the *remedy of the passion.* But the fourth, *like an eagle,* which has clear eyes, because the fourth writer, namely John, pursues the gospel
<!-- page 7 -->
*teaching office* of Christ, to which the others could not attain, and therefore it is described as *above them;* Ezechiel, the first chapter[^p7-1]: *The face of an eagle above.* — And thus it is plain how the truth answers to the figure; plain also are those six preambles to the teaching, namely of what sort the *doctor* is, of what sort the *hearer,* what the *efficient,* what the *end,* what the *matter,* and what the *form;* and from this a certain general intelligence of the whole Book.

The Douay renders *ad annuntiandum* "to preach"; here it is rendered "to announce", because the Prooemium sets *annuntiare* beside *praedicare* in the same verse (*praedicarem captivis indulgentiam*) and keeps them apart, and because ¶ 14 derives the name *Evangelium* from it as *bona nuntiatio*, "good announcement". *Praedicare* is "preach" throughout. Quaracchi's *clausis apertionem* is likewise rendered "opening to them that are shut up" (the Douay, following the Hebrew sense, has "deliverance"), since ¶ 16 expounds the word itself.
