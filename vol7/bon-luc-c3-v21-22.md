# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. III, Vers. 21-22
## *Primo, de regeneratione Christi sacramentali duo*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 81 -->

#### Tertio, de baptismo et praedicatione Ioannis in singulari quoad duo.

47. *Factum est autem, cum baptizaretur* etc. Postquam commendavit Evangelista praecursoris praedicationem et officii exsecutionem in generali et speciali, hic commendat *in singulari respectu Christi,* ad quem principaliter tota praedicatio ordinata fuit. Sicut enim dicit ipse Baptista Ioannis primo[^p81-9]: « Ut manifestetur in Israel, propterea veni ego in aqua baptizans ». Describitur ergo hic spiritualis regeneratio Christi, quae principaliter intenta est in toto capitulo. Habet autem haec pars duas. In prima determinatur Christi *regeneratio sacramentalis;* in secunda vero, Christi *genealogia temporalis,* ibi[^p81-10]: *Et ipse Iesus erat incipiens* etc.

#### Primo, de regeneratione Christi sacramentali duo.

Quantum ad primum duo introducuntur: primum est *humiliatio* per susceptionem Sacramenti in assumta humanitate; secundum est *clarificatio* per exhibitionem testimonii a tota Trinitate, ibi[^p81-11]: *Apertum est caelum* etc.

Circa susceptionem Sacramenti in forma servi notandum, quod triplex distinguitur *humiliatio* in
<!-- page 82 -->
Christo: prima, respectu *populi associantis;* secunda, respectu *ministri baptizantis;* tertia, respectu *Dei exaudientis.*

48. (Vers. 21.). *Humiliatio* autem respectu *populi associantis* notatur, cum dicit: *Factum est, cum baptizaretur omnis populus.* Per *populum* plebs humilis intelligitur, inter quos Christus humilis esse non dedignatur, quoniam, secundum Psalmum[^p82-1], « excelsus Dominus et humilia respicit ». Ratione cuius humilitatis dicitur infra vigesimo secundo: « Ego autem in medio vestrum sum, sicut qui ministrat », et illud etiam Ioannis primo dicitur: « Medius autem vestrum stetit, quem vos nescitis ». Et nota, quod *omnis populus* idem est, quod *multi* de populo — quia, infra septimo[^p82-2], « Pharisaei autem spreverunt consilium Dei in semetipsis, non baptizati baptismo Ioannis » — et sic accipitur Matthaei tertio: « Exibat ad eum omnis Iudaea et omnis regio circa Iordanem, ut baptizarentur ab eo ».

49. *Humiliatio* respectu *ministri baptizantis* notatur, cum dicitur: *Et Iesu baptizato,* scilicet a Ioanne, quae fuit mira humilitas, ut Dominus vellet baptizari baptismo servi. Unde dicitur Matthaei tertio[^p82-3], quod Ioannes dixit: « Ego a te debeo baptizari »; et sequitur: « Sine modo; sic enim decet nos implere omnem iustitiam », id est perfectam humilitatem, qua non tantum superiori et pari, sed etiam inferiori subiecit semetipsum. Baptizatus autem est Christus, non ut sanctificaretur baptismo, sed ut *vim regenerativam aquis conferret.* Unde Chrysostomus[^p82-4]: « Peccatorum remissionem non accepit, sed aquas baptizandis omnibus sanctificavit ». Baptizari etiam voluit, ut *aliis exemplum et formam daret,* quia, secundum quod dicitur Actuum primo[^p82-5], « coepit Iesus facere et docere »; ut etiam suo baptismo *innotesceret,* sicut dicitur Ioannis primo: « Ut manifestetur in Israel » etc. Baptizatus est etiam, ut baptismum *praecursoris approbaret* et ostenderet Pharisaeis, quod Ioannis testimonio credendum erat, sicut caelesti; ad quos ait Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p82-6]: « Baptismus Ioannis unde erat? E caelo, an ex hominibus »?

50. *Humiliatio* autem respectu *Dei exaudientis* notatur, cum dicitur: *Et orante,* scilicet ad Dominum, secundum illud Psalmi[^p82-7]: « Subditus esto Domino et ora eum »; ad Hebraeos quinto: « Preces supplicationesque ad Deum offerens, exauditus est pro sua reverentia ». Unde Ioannis undecimo: « Pater, gratias ago tibi, quoniam audisti me; ego autem sciebam, quoniam semper me audis, sed propter populum, qui astat, dixi, ut credant, quia tu me misisti ». In omnibus invenitur Dominus *orans,* sive in baptismi susceptione, sive in solitudine, sive in praedicatione, sive in miraculorum operatione et passione et Sacramenti dispensatione et animae commendatione, ut ostendat quod infra decimo octavo[^p82-8] dicitur: « Oportet semper orare et non deficere ».

51. Oravit enim in *baptismi susceptione,* sicut hic: *Et factum est, Iesu baptizato et orante,* ut daret formam orandi accedentibus ad baptisma. — In *deserti solitudine;* infra quinto[^p82-9]: « Ipse secedebat in desertum et orabat », ut formam daret contemplantibus. — In *praedicatione;* infra sexto: « Factum est autem in diebus illis, exiit in montem, ut oraret », ut formam daret praedicatoribus. — In *miraculorum operatione;* Ioannis undecimo[^p82-10]: « Pater, gratias ago tibi » etc., ut daret formam operantibus. — In *passione;* Matthaei vigesimo sexto: « Progressus cecidit in faciem suam », ut formam daret sustinentibus. — In *corporis administratione;* infra vigesimo secundo[^p82-11]: « Accepto calice, gratias egit Deo », ut formam daret sacerdotibus. — In *spiritus commendatione;* infra vigesimo tertio: « Pater, in manus tuas commendo spiritum meum; et haec dicens exspiravit », ut formam daret morientibus. Et sic omnibus est orandum.

52. *Apertum est caelum* etc. Post humiliationem in susceptione Sacramenti subditur *clarificatio in exhibitione testimonii;* et ut testimonium sit firmum, ideo ostenditur esse trium personarum omni exceptione maiorum[^p82-12], id est personae Filii et Spiritus
<!-- page 83 -->
sancti et Patris. Adest enim *lux, columba* et *vox: lux* ad signandum personam *Filii; columba, Spiritus sancti; vox* vero est in persona *Patris aeterni.*

Ad signandum igitur *Filii personam* dicitur: *Apertum est caelum,* in qua scilicet apertione fulgor magnus apparuit. Ipse enim Filius dicit de se Ioannis octavo[^p83-1]: « Ego sum lux mundi »; et Ioannis primo: « Erat lux vera »; et ad Hebraeos primo: « Qui cum sit splendor gloriae et figura substantiae eius ». Dum ergo caeli aperti dant splendorem, verificatur illud Psalmi: « Caeli enarrant gloriam Dei », id est Filii, qui est splendor. — In hoc etiam designatum est, quod virtute baptismi Christi aperitur ianua caeli ad intrandum baptizatis, secundum illud Psalmi[^p83-2]: « Notas mihi fecisti vias vitae » etc. Apparuit etiam ipse Filius in natura assumta; ad Titum tertio[^p83-3]: « Apparuit benignitas et humanitas Salvatoris Dei nostri, non ex operibus iustitiae, quae fecimus nos » etc. usque: « Heredes simus secundum spem vitae aeternae ». Quia enim « nemo ascendit in caelum, nisi qui descendit de caelo », id est, si non sit membrum Christi, qui descendit; hoc enim non potest esse nisi per Sacramentum regenerationis: ideo in baptismo « aperti sunt caeli ». Et sicut per Adae carnalem generationem clauditur caelum, ita per regenerationem spiritualem aperitur; et sicut propter concupiscentiae ardorem « positus est flammeus gladius ad custodiam ligni vitae », sicut dicitur Genesis tertio[^p83-4]; sic per humorem gratiae mitigantis concupiscentiam in baptismo amovetur romphaea, et aperitur porta; Danielis tertio: « Angelus Domini descendit in fornacem et fecit medium fornacis quasi ventum roris flantem ». Et hic est Angelus, de quo Ioannis quinto: « Angelus Domini descendebat secundum tempus in piscinam, et movebatur aqua, et sanabatur unus ».

53. (Vers. 22.). Rursus ad designandam *Spiritus sancti personam* additur: *Descendit*[^p83-5] *Spiritus sanctus corporali specie sicut columba in ipsum;* Ioannis primo: « Super quem videris Spiritum descendentem et manentem, hic est qui baptizat ». — Descendit autem in specie *columbae* ratione significationis, quia, sicut dicit Chrysostomus[^p83-6], « avis ista prae omnibus cultrix est maximae caritatis ». Unde apparuit Christo in *perfecto animali,* sed discipulis in *linguis,* ut significetur, quod in Christo fuit secundum omnimodam plenitudinem, in aliis per partes; unde dicitur Ioannis tertio[^p83-7]: « Non ad mensuram dat Deus spiritum ». Apparuit etiam super discipulos in *igne,* sed super Christum in *columba,* quia, sicut dicit Gregorius[^p83-8], « Christus nos per mansuetudinem venit colligere »; et ideo super ipsum apparuit in columba, sed « super discipulos in igne; quos ad consumendam rubiginem peccati contra semetipsos veniebat accendere ». — Et optime competit baptismo apparitio Spiritus sancti in *columba* propter innocentiam, quam baptismus restituit. In cuius rei signum dicitur Genesis octavo[^p83-9], quod columba post diluvium attulit ramum virentis olivae. Per illud diluvium — sicut dicit Damascenus et primae Petri tertio dicitur: « In quo paucae, id est octo animae, salvae factae sunt per aquam » etc. — intelligitur baptismus; unde per *columbam* reducentem ad arcam ramum virentis olivae intelligitur innocentia mansuetudinis cum gratia unctionis.

54. Et nota, quod Spiritus sanctus apparuit in triplici specie, scilicet in *columba* in baptismo; in *nube* in transfiguratione, infra nono[^p83-10]; in *igne* super
<!-- page 84 -->
discipulos, Actuum secundo. Et respondent haec tribus statibus, ut dicit expositor. *Incipientibus* enim illabitur ut *columba,* quia reddit eos gementes et meditantes; Isaiae quinquagesimo nono[^p84-1]: « Quasi columbae meditantes, gememus »; et trigesimo octavo: « Sicut pullus hirundinis, sic clamabo, meditabor ut columba ». — *Proficientibus,* ut *nubes,* quia conamine mentis facit eos sursum ascendere de virtute in virtutem, usquequo elevet ad caelum, ut videatur Deus deorum in Sion; Psalmus[^p84-2]: « Ibunt de virtute in virtutem, videbitur » etc. — *Perfectis* in *igne,* quia illos inflammat, ut sursum tendant per desiderium, et etiam ad proximos per beneficium; infra duodecimo:[^p84-3] « Ignem veni mittere in terram ». In cuius rei signum dicitur quarti Regum secundo, quod « Elias ascendit in caelum in curru igneo »; de quo igne Threnorum primo: « De excelso misit ignem in ossibus meis et erudivit me ».

55. Postremo ad signandam *personam Patris* subditur: *Vox*[^p84-4] *de caelo facta est.* Et tunc verificatum est illud Psalmi: « Vox Domini super aquas, Deus maiestatis intonuit »; et illud Deuteronomii quarto: « De caelo te fecit audire vocem suam ». Et quod haec vox esset in persona Patris, ostenditur, cum adiungitur: *Tu es Filius meus,* scilicet unigenitus, ab aeterno genitus; Psalmus[^p84-5]: « Dominus dixit ad me: Filius meus es tu ». Et in hoc perhibuit ei Pater testimonium; Ioannis octavo: « Ego sum, qui testimonium perhibeo de me ipso, et testimonium perhibet de me qui misit me Pater »; unde primae Ioannis quinto: « Si testimonium hominum accipimus, testimonium Dei maius est, quod testificatus est de Filio suo ». *Tu* ergo hic *discretive* accipitur, quia nullus alius; unde *Unigenitus* dicitur Ioannis primo[^p84-6]: « Deum nemo vidit unquam nisi Unigenitus, qui est in sinu Patris », quia, secundum illud Psalmi, « ante luciferum genui te ». Et quia istum summe diligit, ideo additur: *dilectus,* per quem etiam alii odiosi diliguntur; ad Ephesios primo[^p84-7]: « Gratificavit nos in dilecto Filio suo »; et iterum ad Colossenses primo: « Transtulit in regnum Filii dilectionis suae ». Et ad maiorem expressionem additur: *In te complacuit*[^p84-8] *mihi,* omne scilicet, quod placet; et hoc propter perfectissimum nexum amoris, qui est inter Patrem et Filium; Proverbiorum tertio: « Quasi pater in filio complacet sibi »; et ad Colossenses primo: « In ipso complacuit inhabitare omnem plenitudinem, pacificans per sanguinem suum quae in caelis et quae in terris sunt »; et ideo dicitur Ioannis octavo: « Qui misit me mecum est, et non reliquit me solum, quia quae placita sunt ei facio semper ».

56. Sic ergo perhibitum est testimonium Christo in *voce* a Patre, a Spiritu sancto in *columba,* a Filio in *luce;* et sic firmum est testimonium, quia[^p84-9] « in ore trium testium stare debet omne verbum ». Et sic verificatum est illud primae Ioannis quinto: « Tres sunt, qui testimonium dant in caelo: Pater, Verbum et Spiritus sanctus ». — Et nota, quod Matthaei tertio[^p84-10] dicitur de hac voce: « Hic est Filius meus dilectus, in quo mihi complacui »; sed Marci primo: « Tu es Filius meus dilectus, in te complacui »; Lucae, hic: *Tu es Filius meus dilectus, in te complacuit mihi.* In his autem non est contrarietas, quia non sonum verborum, sed sententiam exprimunt, sicut dicit Augustinus in libro de Concordia Evangelistarum[^p84-11]. Ideo autem fit differenter, quia vox ista Patris est significativa, quod Christus sit Verbum Patris simillimum et expressivum, et ideo placitissimum[^p84-12]. Et quoniam Pater dicit se Verbo in se, dicit et nobis, per quod se aliis declarat, dicit etiam omnia Verbo, quaecumque dicit[^p84-13]; et hoc perfectissimum est; et hoc non poterat simplici verbo Evangelista exprimere, licet illa vox caelestis totum hoc insinuaret: ideo Evangelistae expresserunt diversimode, ita quod Matthaeus insinuat complacentiam Patris respectu Verbi, in quantum dicit *se in se;* et ideo dicit: « In quo *mihi* complacui »; Marcus respectu Verbi, in quantum Pater *Verbo* se dicit et complacet *aliis,* et ideo dicit: « In te complacui »; Lucas respectu Verbi, in quantum Pater Verbo *omnia* dicit; et ideo addit: *In te complacuit mihi,* generaliter scilicet, quidquid placet[^p84-14].

## English
<!-- page 81 -->

#### Third, on the baptism and preaching of John in particular, as to two things.

47. *Now it came to pass, when there were baptized* etc. After the Evangelist has commended the preaching of the forerunner and the execution of his office in general and in special, here he commends it *in particular, with respect to Christ,* to whom the whole preaching was principally ordered. For as the Baptist himself says, John, the first chapter[^p81-9]: « That he may be made manifest in Israel, therefore am I come baptizing with water ». Here, then, is described the spiritual regeneration of Christ, which is principally intended in the whole chapter. Now this part has two. In the first is set out Christ's *sacramental regeneration;* in the second, Christ's *temporal genealogy,* at[^p81-10]: *And Jesus himself was beginning* etc.

#### First, on the sacramental regeneration of Christ, two things.

As to the first, two things are introduced: the first is the *humiliation* through the receiving of the Sacrament in the assumed humanity; the second is the *glorification* through the showing of testimony by the whole Trinity, at[^p81-11]: *Heaven was opened* etc.

Concerning the receiving of the Sacrament in the form of a servant, it is to be noted that a threefold *humiliation* is distinguished in
<!-- page 82 -->
Christ: the first, with respect to *the people accompanying;* the second, with respect to *the minister baptizing;* the third, with respect to *God hearing.*

48. (Vers. 21.). Now the *humiliation* with respect to *the people accompanying* is noted when he says: *Now it came to pass, when all the people were baptized.* By *the people* the humble common folk is understood, among whom Christ, humble, does not disdain to be, since, according to the Psalm[^p82-1], « the Lord is high, and looketh on the humble things ». By reason of this humility it is said below, in the twenty-second chapter: « But I am in the midst of you, as he that serveth », and that also is said in John, the first chapter: « But there hath stood in the midst of you one whom you know not ». And note that *all the people* is the same as *many* of the people — since, below, in the seventh chapter[^p82-2], « But the Pharisees despised the counsel of God against themselves, not having been baptized with the baptism of John » — and so it is taken in Matthew, the third chapter: « There went out to him all Judaea and all the country about Jordan, to be baptized by him ».

49. The *humiliation* with respect to *the minister baptizing* is noted when it is said: *And Jesus being baptized,* namely by John, which was a wonderful humility, that the Lord should will to be baptized with the baptism of a servant. Hence it is said in Matthew, the third chapter[^p82-3], that John said: « I ought to be baptized by thee »; and there follows: « Suffer it now; for so it becometh us to fulfil all justice », that is, perfect humility, by which he subjected himself not only to a superior and to an equal, but also to an inferior. Now Christ was baptized not that he might be sanctified by baptism, but that he might *confer regenerative power on the waters.* Hence Chrysostom[^p82-4]: « He did not receive remission of sins, but he sanctified the waters for all who are to be baptized ». He willed also to be baptized that he might *give an example and form to others,* since, according to what is said in Acts, the first chapter[^p82-5], « Jesus began to do and to teach »; that also by his baptism he might *become known,* as is said in John, the first chapter: « That he may be made manifest in Israel » etc. He was baptized also that he might *approve the baptism of the forerunner* and show the Pharisees that John's testimony was to be believed, as heavenly; to whom he says in Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p82-6]: « The baptism of John, whence was it? From heaven, or from men »?

50. Now the *humiliation* with respect to *God hearing* is noted when it is said: *And praying,* namely to the Lord, according to that of the Psalm[^p82-7]: « Be subject to the Lord and pray to him »; to the Hebrews, the fifth chapter: « Offering prayers and supplications to God, he was heard for his reverence ». Hence John, the eleventh chapter: « Father, I give thee thanks that thou hast heard me; and I knew that thou hearest me always, but because of the people who stand about have I said it, that they may believe that thou hast sent me ». In all things the Lord is found *praying,* whether in the receiving of baptism, or in solitude, or in preaching, or in the working of miracles and in the passion and in the dispensing of the Sacrament and in the commending of his soul, that he may show what is said below, in the eighteenth chapter[^p82-8]: « We ought always to pray, and not to faint ».

51. For he prayed in *the receiving of baptism,* as here: *And it came to pass, Jesus being baptized and praying,* that he might give a form of praying to those who come to baptism. — In *the solitude of the desert;* below, in the fifth chapter[^p82-9]: « He retired into the desert and prayed », that he might give a form to those who contemplate. — In *preaching;* below, in the sixth chapter: « And it came to pass in those days, that he went out into a mountain to pray », that he might give a form to preachers. — In *the working of miracles;* John, the eleventh chapter[^p82-10]: « Father, I give thee thanks » etc., that he might give a form to those who work. — In *the passion;* Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter: « Going forward, he fell upon his face », that he might give a form to those who endure. — In *the administration of the body;* below, in the twenty-second chapter[^p82-11]: « Having taken the chalice, he gave thanks to God », that he might give a form to priests. — In *the commending of the spirit;* below, in the twenty-third chapter: « Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit; and saying this, he gave up the ghost », that he might give a form to the dying. And so all must pray.

52. *Heaven was opened* etc. After the humiliation in the receiving of the Sacrament there is added the *glorification in the showing of testimony;* and that the testimony may be firm, it is therefore shown to be of three persons greater than every exception[^p82-12], that is, of the person of the Son and of the Holy
<!-- page 83 -->
Spirit and of the Father. For there are present *light, dove* and *voice: light* to signify the person of *the Son; the dove,* of *the Holy Spirit;* but the *voice* is in the person of *the eternal Father.*

To signify, then, *the person of the Son,* it is said: *Heaven was opened,* in which opening, namely, a great brightness appeared. For the Son himself says of himself, John, the eighth chapter[^p83-1]: « I am the light of the world »; and John, the first chapter: « That was the true light »; and to the Hebrews, the first chapter: « Who being the splendour of his glory and the figure of his substance ». While, then, the opened heavens give splendour, that of the Psalm is verified: « The heavens shew forth the glory of God », that is, of the Son, who is the splendour. — In this also it was designated that by the power of the baptism of Christ the gate of heaven is opened to the baptized for entering, according to that of the Psalm[^p83-2]: « Thou hast made known to me the ways of life » etc. The Son himself also appeared in the assumed nature; to Titus, the third chapter[^p83-3]: « The kindness and humanity of God our Saviour appeared, not by the works of justice, which we have done » etc., as far as: « We may be heirs, according to hope of life everlasting ». For since « no man hath ascended into heaven, but he that descended from heaven », that is, if he be not a member of Christ, who descended; and this cannot be except through the Sacrament of regeneration: therefore in baptism « the heavens were opened ». And as through Adam's carnal generation heaven is closed, so through spiritual regeneration it is opened; and as on account of the ardour of concupiscence « a flaming sword was placed for the keeping of the tree of life », as is said in Genesis, the third chapter[^p83-4]; so through the moisture of grace mitigating concupiscence the sword is removed in baptism, and the gate is opened; Daniel, the third chapter: « The angel of the Lord went down into the furnace and made the midst of the furnace like the blowing of a wind bringing dew ». And this is the Angel of whom John, the fifth chapter, says: « An angel of the Lord descended at certain times into the pond, and the water was moved, and one was healed ».

53. (Vers. 22.). Again, to designate *the person of the Holy Spirit,* there is added: *The Holy Spirit descended*[^p83-5] *in a bodily shape as a dove upon him;* John, the first chapter: « He upon whom thou shalt see the Spirit descending and remaining, he it is that baptizeth ». — Now he descended in the shape of a *dove* by reason of signification, since, as Chrysostom says[^p83-6], « that bird before all is a cultivator of the greatest charity ». Hence he appeared to Christ in *a perfect animal,* but to the disciples in *tongues,* that it may be signified that in Christ he was according to every kind of fulness, in the others by parts; hence it is said in John, the third chapter[^p83-7]: « Not by measure doth God give the Spirit ». He appeared also upon the disciples in *fire,* but upon Christ in *a dove,* since, as Gregory says[^p83-8], « Christ came to gather us by meekness »; and therefore upon him he appeared in a dove, but « upon the disciples in fire; whom he came to kindle against themselves to consume the rust of sin ». — And the appearing of the Holy Spirit in *a dove* befits baptism excellently on account of the innocence which baptism restores. In sign of which thing it is said in Genesis, the eighth chapter[^p83-9], that the dove after the flood brought a branch of green olive. By that flood — as Damascene says, and as is said in the first of Peter, the third chapter: « In which a few, that is, eight souls, were saved by water » etc. — baptism is understood; hence by *the dove* bringing back to the ark a branch of green olive is understood the innocence of meekness with the grace of anointing.

54. And note that the Holy Spirit appeared in a threefold shape, namely in *a dove* at the baptism; in *a cloud* at the transfiguration, below, in the ninth chapter[^p83-10]; in *fire* upon the
<!-- page 84 -->
disciples, Acts, the second chapter. And these answer to three states, as the expositor says. For upon *beginners* he glides in as *a dove,* since he renders them groaning and meditating; Isaias, the fifty-ninth chapter[^p84-1]: « As doves meditating, we shall groan »; and in the thirty-eighth chapter: « I will cry like a young swallow, I will meditate like a dove ». — Upon *the proficient,* as *a cloud,* since by the effort of the mind he makes them ascend upward from virtue to virtue, until he raises them to heaven, that the God of gods may be seen in Sion; the Psalm[^p84-2]: « They shall go from virtue to virtue, shall be seen » etc. — Upon *the perfect* in *fire,* since he inflames them, that they may tend upward through desire, and also to their neighbours through beneficence; below, in the twelfth chapter:[^p84-3] « I am come to cast fire on the earth ». In sign of which thing it is said in the fourth of Kings, the second chapter, that « Elias went up into heaven in a fiery chariot »; of which fire Lamentations, the first chapter: « From above he hath sent fire into my bones, and hath chastised me ».

55. Lastly, to signify *the person of the Father,* there is added: *A voice*[^p84-4] *came from heaven.* And then that of the Psalm was verified: « The voice of the Lord is upon the waters; the God of majesty hath thundered »; and that of Deuteronomy, the fourth chapter: « From heaven he made thee to hear his voice ». And that this voice was in the person of the Father is shown when there is joined: *Thou art my Son,* namely the only-begotten, begotten from eternity; the Psalm[^p84-5]: « The Lord hath said to me: Thou art my Son ». And in this the Father bore him testimony; John, the eighth chapter: « I am he who give testimony of myself, and the Father who sent me giveth testimony of me »; hence the first of John, the fifth chapter: « If we receive the testimony of men, the testimony of God is greater, which he hath testified of his Son ». *Thou,* then, is here taken *discretively,* since no other; hence he is called *the Only-begotten* in John, the first chapter[^p84-6]: « No man hath seen God at any time but the Only-begotten, who is in the bosom of the Father », since, according to that of the Psalm, « before the day star I begot thee ». And because he loves him supremely, therefore is added: *beloved,* through whom others too, who were hateful, are loved; to the Ephesians, the first chapter[^p84-7]: « He hath graced us in his beloved Son »; and again to the Colossians, the first chapter: « He hath translated us into the kingdom of the Son of his dilection ». And for greater expression there is added: *In thee it hath well pleased*[^p84-8] *me,* everything, namely, that pleases; and this on account of the most perfect bond of love which is between the Father and the Son; Proverbs, the third chapter: « As a father in the son he pleaseth himself »; and to the Colossians, the first chapter: « In him it hath well pleased that all fulness should dwell, making peace through his blood the things that are in heaven and the things that are on earth »; and therefore it is said in John, the eighth chapter: « He that sent me is with me, and he hath not left me alone, because I do always the things that are pleasing to him ».

56. Thus, then, testimony was borne to Christ in *the voice* by the Father, by the Holy Spirit in *the dove,* by the Son in *the light;* and so the testimony is firm, since[^p84-9] « in the mouth of three witnesses every word ought to stand ». And so that of the first of John, the fifth chapter, was verified: « There are three who give testimony in heaven: the Father, the Word and the Holy Spirit ». — And note that in Matthew, the third chapter[^p84-10], it is said of this voice: « This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased to myself »; but in Mark, the first chapter: « Thou art my beloved Son, in thee I am well pleased »; in Luke, here: *Thou art my beloved Son, in thee it hath well pleased me.* Now in these there is no contrariety, since they express not the sound of the words but the sense, as Augustine says in the book On the Concord of the Evangelists[^p84-11]. But it is done differently because that voice of the Father is significative that Christ is the Word of the Father, most like and expressive, and therefore most pleasing[^p84-12]. And since the Father speaks himself by the Word in himself, speaks also to us, by which he declares himself to others, and speaks also all things by the Word, whatsoever he speaks[^p84-13]; and this is most perfect; and this the Evangelist could not express in a single word, although that heavenly voice intimated all of this: therefore the Evangelists expressed it diversely, so that Matthew intimates the complacency of the Father with respect to the Word, inasmuch as he speaks *himself in himself;* and therefore he says: « In whom I am well pleased *to myself* »; Mark, with respect to the Word, inasmuch as the Father speaks himself by *the Word* and is well pleased toward *others,* and therefore he says: « In thee I am well pleased »; Luke, with respect to the Word, inasmuch as the Father speaks *all things* by the Word; and therefore he adds: *In thee it hath well pleased me,* generally, namely, whatever pleases[^p84-14].
