# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XV, Vers. 1-7
## *Expressio Iudaicae impietatis describitur tripliciter*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 381 -->

### Capitulum XV.

#### Impietas Iudaeorum confutatur tripliciter.

1. *Erant autem appropinquantes* etc. Postquam confutavit dolositatem Pharisaeorum calumniantium Christi potentiam in miraculis, in hac parte *confutat eorum impietatem* remurmurantium contra clementiam in operibus pietatis[^p381-12]. Dividitur autem haec pars in partes tres. In quarum prima praemittitur *expressio impietatis Iudaicae.* In secunda subditur *manifestatio pietatis divinae,* ibi[^p381-13]: *Et ait ad illos parabolam* etc. In tertia vero adiungitur *commendatio et persuasio pietatis humanae,* infra decimo sexto: *Dicebat autem et ad discipulos suos: Homo quidam erat dives.*

<!-- page 382 -->
#### Expressio Iudaicae impietatis describitur tripliciter.

Circa *expressionem* autem *impietatis Iudaicae* tria introducuntur, scilicet *occasio impietatis exprimendae, expressio iam conceptae* et *simulatio aequitatis iustitiae.*

2. (Vers. 1.). Primo igitur quantum ad *occasionem impietatis exprimendae* dicitur: *Erant autem appropinquantes ei publicani et peccatores;* in quo ostenditur summa pietas Christi ad peccatores publicos, ex quo Pharisaei occasionem accipiebant impie iudicandi, quia mos est impiis impietatem suam augere de visis operibus pietatis. Unde in Psalmo[^p382-1]: « Dispersit, dedit pauperibus » etc., et statim subdit: « Peccator videbit et irascetur ». Ostendebat autem Christus pietatem magnam, quia suscipiebat *publicanos,* qui erant publici peccatores; unde Glossa[^p382-2]: « *Publicani,* qui publica vectigalia exigunt vel conducunt, et qui lucrum saeculi per negotia sectantur », et tales erant multis lucris iniustis intenti. Huiusmodi autem peccatores, licet videantur repellendi ex iustitia, suscipiuntur tamen ex misericordia; de qua confisi, *erant appropinquantes ei* tanquam *clementissimo Domino,* secundum illud Iacobi quarto[^p382-3]: « Appropinquate Deo, et appropinquabit vobis ». *Appropinquate,* inquam, per devotam supplicationem; Psalmus[^p382-4]: « Appropinquet deprecatio mea in conspectu tuo, Domine ». Tanquam etiam *peritissimo magistro;* unde subdit: *ut audirent illum,* secundum illud consilium Sapientis Ecclesiastici ultimo[^p382-5]: « Appropinquate ad me indocti et congregamini in domum disciplinae »; quia, sicut dicitur Deuteronomii trigesimo tertio, « qui appropinquant pedibus eius accipient de doctrina eius ».

3. Huic summo doctori Christo appropinquabant et Pharisaei et publicani, sed *Pharisaei simulate,* secundum illud Ieremiae duodecimo[^p382-6]: « Prope es tu ori eorum et longe a renibus eorum »; quia tales per simulatam iustitiam videntur *proximi,* sed per occultam superbiam sunt *longinqui,* secundum illud Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo: « Me etenim de die in diem quaerunt et scire vias meas volunt quasi gens, quae iustitiam fecerit et iudicium Dei sui non dereliquerit; rogant me iudicia iustitiae et appropinquare Deo volunt. Quare ieiunavimus, et non aspexisti? Humiliavimus animas nostras, et nescisti »? Et subdit: « Ecce, in die ieiunii vestri invenitur voluntas vestra » etc. *Publicani* vero appropinquantes erant *vere,* ut audirent et audito a malis per poenitentiam resipiscerent, secundum dominicam exhortationem Matthaei quarto[^p382-7]: « Poenitentiam agite, appropinquabit enim regnum caelorum »; et ut audiendo obedirent, secundum illud Ecclesiastae quarto: « Custodi pedem tuum, ingrediens in domum Dei, et appropinqua, ut audias. Multo enim melior est obedientia quam stultorum victimae ».

4. (Vers. 2.). Secundo quantum ad *expressionem impietatis conceptae* subdit: *Et murmurabant Pharisaei et scribae. Murmuratio* est oblocutio, quae nec omnino exprimitur nec omnino reticetur; Sapientiae primo[^p382-8]: « Auris zeli audit omnia, et tumultus murmurationum non abscondetur. Custodite ergo vos a murmuratione, quae nihil prodest, et a detractione parcite linguae, quia sermo obscurus in vacuum non ibit ». Et ideo primae ad Corinthios decimo: « Neque murmuraveritis, sicut quidam eorum murmuraverunt et perierunt ab exterminatore ». Hanc murmurationem odit Dominus maxime, quia *nihil prodest,* nec sibi nec proximo; Threnorum tertio[^p382-9]: « Quid murmuravit homo vivens, vir pro peccatis suis »? quasi dicat: frustra; et quia *procedit ex ore maligno,* quod nec audet aperte arguere, nec tamen potest cordis malitiam occultare. Unde hoc sonat ipsum vocabulum *murmurare,* de quo dicit Hugutio[^p382-10], quod dicitur a *mutio, mutis,* quasi *velle loqui* et *non audere.* Sed tamen oritur aliquando *ex infirmitate,* ut Numerorum undecimo[^p382-11]: « Ortum est murmur populi, quasi dolentium pro labore contra Dominum »; aliquando *ex ignorantia et errore,* secundum illud Ioannis septimo: « Murmur multum erat in turba. Quidam dicebant, quod bonus; alii autem: Non, sed seducit turbas. Nemo tamen palam loquebatur de illo »; aliquando etiam *ex malignitate,* sicut Deuteronomii primo[^p382-12]: « Increduli ad sermonem
<!-- page 383 -->
Domini Dei vestri murmurastis in tabernaculis vestris atque dixistis: Odit nos Dominus, et idcirco eduxit nos de terra Aegypti, ut traderet nos in manus Amorrhaei ». Similiter et hi bonum convertebant in malum, et publicanorum poenitentiam et etiam Iesu misericordiam; et in hoc vitium incidebant, quia erant superbi et inhumani; de quibus potest intelligi illud Isaiae sexagesimo quinto[^p383-1]: « Qui dicunt: Recede a me, non appropinques mihi, quia immundus es. Isti fumus erunt in furore meo, ignis ardens tota die ».

5. Tertio quantum ad *simulationem aequitatis iustitiae* subditur: *Dicentes: Quia hic peccatores recipit,* quantum ad affabilitatem colloquii, *et manducat cum illis,* quantum ad familiaritatem convivii. In quo volebant ipsum redarguere tanquam fautorem malorum et contemptorem praeceptorum; unde Glossa[^p383-2]: « Quasi: contrarius est Legi, quae hoc prohibet ». In Lege enim prohibetur contactus rei immundae, Levitici undecimo et decimo tertio; in quo intelligitur vitatio societatis malae. Unde Deuteronomii septimo: « Non inibis cum eis foedus nec sociabis cum eis coniugia ». Sed, sicut dicit Glossa[^p383-3], « dupliciter errant, quia et se iustos putant, cum sint superbi, et illos peccatores, cum iam poeniteant ». Unde ipsi simulando aequitatem cadebant in impietatem *peccando in se ipsos* superbiendo, quod consueverunt facere Pharisaei; infra decimo sexto[^p383-4]: « Vos estis, qui iustificatis vos coram hominibus ». Item, *peccando in proximos* male iudicando, ita ut posset eis dici illud supra sexto: « Hypocrita, eiice primum trabem de oculo tuo » etc. Item, *peccando in Christum* eius misericordiam redarguendo, qui peccatores *recipiebat,* iuxta quod promiserat per Ieremiam, Ieremiae tertio[^p383-5]: « Vulgo dicitur: Si dimiserit vir uxorem suam, et recedens ab eo duxerit virum alterum, nunquid revertetur ad eam ultra? Tu autem fornicata es cum amatoribus multis; tamen revertere ad me, dicit Dominus, et suscipiam te ». Hoc enim est excellentissimae misericordiae; unde Ioannis sexto[^p383-6]: « Omnis, qui venit ad me, non eiiciam eum foras ». Redarguebant etiam misericordiam, quia eis *condescendebat ad manducandum,* cum tamen hoc sit misericordiae summae, secundum illud Apocalypsis tertio[^p383-7]: « Ego sto ad ostium et pulso » etc.; et Matthaei undecimo: « Venite ad me, omnes, qui laboratis et onerati estis »; et Ioannis septimo: « Si quis sitit, veniat ad me et bibat » etc. Nam et Propheta ex repletione spiritus vocat omnes; Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto[^p383-8]: « Omnes sitientes, venite ad aquas ». Et ratio huius est, quia, sicut dicitur ad Romanos decimo, « non est distinctio Iudaei et Graeci; nam idem Dominus omnium, dives in omnes, qui invocant illum » etc.

#### Manifestatio pietatis divinae describitur triplici parabola.

6. *Et ait ad illos parabolam.* Postquam manifestavit impietatem Iudaicam, sic secundo *notificat pietatem divinam* ad impietatem Iudaicam confutandam[^p383-9]. Et quoniam pietas Dei specialiter est ad hominem in humani generis reparatione, in qua indiget homo *sacrificio reconciliante, pretio redimente* et *spiritu adoptante;* ideo in hac parte triplicem introducit parabolam; quarum prima est de *ove,* quae sacrificatur; secunda, de *drachma,* quae est nummus regius; tertia est de *patre et filio,* qui misericorditer recipitur. Amplius, quoniam pietas Dei specialiter ad hominem est ratione trium, quae Deus posuit in homine, scilicet ratione *insitae mansuetudinis,* ratione *impressae imaginis* et ratione *collatae similitudinis;* et per primam assimilatur homo *ovi,* per secundam vero *nummo imperiali,* per tertiam *Filio Patris aeterni:* ideo in hac parte Evangelista, Spiritu[^p383-10] illuminatus, triplicem ponit parabolam, per os carnis Dei prolatam, ad notandam pietatem Dei ad humanam naturam, quarum prima est de *pastore et ovibus suis;* secunda est de *muliere et drachmis,* ibi[^p383-11]: *Aut quae mulier habens drachmas decem;* tertia de *patre et filiis,* ibi: *Ait autem: Homo quidam habuit duos filios;* in quo manifeste ostendit, quod omnis creatura quodam modo praetendit pietatem, quam Deus habet respectu hominis: *creatura brutalis,* ut ovis, et *mere corporalis*[^p383-12], ut drachma, et *rationalis,* ut homo.

#### Parabola de ove perdita.

Circa parabolam igitur de *ove perdita* procedit hoc ordine. Nam primo exprimitur *sollicitudo moestitiae* pro ove perdita; secundo vero, *magnitudo laetitiae* pro ove inventa, ibi[^p383-13]: *Et cum invenerit eam* etc.

<!-- page 384 -->
In explicando autem *sollicitudinem moestitiae* circa ovem perditam quatuor introducuntur ab Evangelista, scilicet *possessio multitudinis integrae, deperditio ovis centesimae* et *dimissio multitudinis habitae* et *requisitio deperditae*[^p384-1].

7. (Vers. 3.). Primo igitur quantum ad *possessionem multitudinis integrae,* quam vult intelligi non ad litteram, sed parabolice, dicit: *Et ait ad illos parabolam istam.* Ideo parabolice loquitur, quia *decet eius sapientiam;* Psalmus[^p384-2]: « Aperiam in parabolis os meum » etc.; et quia hoc *requirebat Iudaeorum perfidia,* quibus debebant velari mysteria, secundum illud Matthaei decimo tertio: « Ideo in parabolis loquor eis, quia videntes non vident, et audientes non audiunt »; vel quia *decebat sacram Scripturam,* in qua non tantum voces, verum etiam res sunt signa ad manuducendum intellectum parvulorum et simplicium ad concipiendum in se multiformitatem intellectuum divinorum, secundum illud Proverbiorum primo[^p384-3]: « Parabolae Salomonis ad sciendam sapientiam et disciplinam, ad intelligenda verba prudentiae et suscipiendam eruditionem doctrinae, ut detur parvulis astutia et adolescenti scientia et intellectus ».

8. (Vers. 4.). Et quoniam parvulis loquitur, ideo exempla manifesta supponit, cum dicit: *Quis ex vobis homo, qui habet centum oves. Homo* iste inter homines est Dei Filius, de quo Baruch tertio[^p384-4]: « Post haec in terris visus est et cum hominibus conversatus est »; et ad Philippenses secundo: « In similitudinem hominum factus et habitu inventus ut homo ». *Oves* istius hominis sunt rationales creaturae ratione insitae mansuetudinis; Matthaei decimo[^p384-5]: « Ecce, ego mitto vos sicut oves in medio luporum » etc.; Ioannis decimo: « Oves meae vocem meam audiunt, et ego vitam aeternam do eis ». Dicuntur autem hae *centum* propter perfectionem, quae insinuatur per centenarium propter reditionem denarii supra se ipsum. Unde Beda[^p384-6]: « Quia numerus centenarius est perfectus, Deus centum oves, id est, numerum perfectum habuit, cum Angelorum et hominum substantiam creavit ». Ob hanc causam etiam in designatione *perfecti meriti* ponitur Matthaei decimo tertio[^p384-7]: « Aliud cecidit in terram bonam et attulit fructum centesimum »; et Genesis vigesimo sexto: « Sevit Isaac in terra illa et invenit in anno illo centuplum ». Similiter etiam quantum ad *perfectionem praemii;* Matthaei decimo nono[^p384-8]: « Omnis, qui reliquerit » etc., sequitur: « Centuplum accipiet et vitam aeternam possidebit ». *Habere* igitur *centum oves* est habere creaturas in perfecto numero et statu perfectionis suae.

9. Secundo quantum ad *perditionem ovis centesimae* subdit: *Et si perdiderit unam ex illis. Unam* ovem de centum perdidit Deus, quando homo peccavit. Unde Beda[^p384-9]: « Una ovis periit, quando homo pascua vitae peccando reliquit ». De hac ove in Psalmo dicitur: « Erravi sicut ovis, quae periit; quaere servum tuum, Domine ». Ideo autem dicitur homo *una ovis,* quia omnes ex uno homine processerunt, in quo et peccaverunt, secundum illud ad Romanos quinto[^p384-10]: « Per unum hominem peccatum intravit in mundum, et per peccatum mors, in quo omnes peccaverunt ». Sed propter varietatem culparum superadditarum dicuntur *multae oves;* Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p384-11]: « Omnes nos quasi oves erravimus, unusquisque in viam suam declinavit »; et primae Petri secundo: « Eratis sicut oves errantes » etc. Et hoc fuit, quousque homo caruit pastore; tertii Regum vigesimo secundo[^p384-12]: « Vidi cunctum Israel dispersum in montibus sicut oves absque pastore ». Impegerat enim haec ovis in lupum, diabolum, de quo Ioannis decimo: « Lupus rapit et dispergit oves » etc.

10. Tertio quantum ad *dimissionem multitudinis habitae* subiungit: *Nonne dimittit nonaginta novem in deserto?* Per *nonaginta novem* intelliguntur Angeli, tum quia novenarius optime competit designationi numeri Angelorum propter ternarium triplicatum in ordinibus Angelorum, ad designandum Deum trinum et unum; tum quia, angelis ruentibus, et homine perdito, remansit eorum numerus imperfectus; unde Glossa[^p384-13]: « Reliquit nonaginta novem, quia rationalis creaturae numerus, pereunte homine, erat imminutus ». Istos *reliquit* Christus, non localiter deserens, cum sit semper ubique, sed quia illos minime apprehendit; ad Hebraeos secundo[^p384-14]: « Nusquam Angelos apprehendit, sed semen Abrahae apprehendit ». Dicitur autem *reliquisse in deserto,* id est in caelo, quod desertum fuit ab angelis et hominibus, et nullus iam ibi poterat introire. Propter quod illa
<!-- page 385 -->
superna patria *deserto* comparatur; unde Glossa[^p385-1]: « *In deserto,* id est in caelo, quod desertum est ab homine peccante »; propter quod etiam illa superna Ierusalem dicitur *deserta,* secundum illud ad Galatas quarto[^p385-2]: « Illa quae sursum est Ierusalem libera est, quae est mater nostra. Scriptum est enim: Laetare, sterilis, quae non parturis, quia multi filii desertae magis, quam eius quae habet virum »; et Isaiae trigesimo quinto: « Laetabitur deserta et invia, et exsultabit solitudo et florebit quasi lilium » etc.

11. Quarto quantum ad *requisitionem singularis ovis deperditae* adiungit: *Et vadit ad illam quae perierat,* ad istam ovem, scilicet hominem, salvandum venit Deus, Dei Filius in hunc mundum; infra decimo nono[^p385-3]: « Venit Filius hominis quaerere et salvum facere quod perierat »; unde primae ad Timotheum primo: « Fidelis sermo et omni acceptione dignus, quia Christus Iesus venit in hunc mundum peccatores salvos facere ». Et hoc fecit Christus tanquam *bonus pastor;* unde Ezechielis trigesimo quarto[^p385-4]: « Ecce, ego ipse requiram oves meas et visitabo eas, sicut pastor visitat gregem suum ». — Sed quia bonus pastor non desistit ab inquisitione ante salutem, ideo subdit: *Donec inveniat illam.* Tunc autem haec ovis a Christo invenitur, quando eius sanguine a perditione liberatur, secundum illud Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo[^p385-5]: « Dominus solus dux eius fuit, et non erat cum eo deus alienus ». « Invenit eum in terra deserta, in loco horroris et vastae solitudinis ». Invenit autem Deus homines perditos cum magna difficultate, secundum illud Osee nono[^p385-6]: « Quasi uvas in deserto inveni Israel et quasi prima poma ficulneae ». Unde pastor iste pro ovibus inveniendis laboravit usque ad mortem, secundum illud Ioannis decimo: « Bonus pastor animam suam ponit pro ovibus suis ». Propter quod etiam pastor iste *agnus* factus est inter oves, ut redimeret oves, secundum illud Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p385-7]: « Sicut ovis ad occisionem ducetur, et sicut agnus coram tondente se obmutescet et non aperiet os suum ». — Unde et hanc inventionem praefigurabat illa inventio, qua Abraham, volens offerre filium, invenit arietem; Genesis vigesimo secundo[^p385-8]: « Levavit Abraham oculos suos viditque arietem inter vepres haerentem cornibus, quem obtulit holocaustum pro filio ». Cetera igitur illa sacrificia oblata, tam ovium quam agnorum et hircorum quam arietum et vitulorum, hoc significant, quod[^p385-9] « Christus per proprium sanguinem introivit semel in sancta, aeterna redemptione inventa ». Et hoc est dicere: « Quaerit diligenter, donec inveniat ».

12. (Vers. 5.). *Et cum invenerit eam.* Post sollicitudinem moestitiae pro ove perdita sequitur hic *magnitudo laetitiae pro ove inventa*[^p385-10], circa quam introducuntur tria, scilicet *exsultatio privata, congratulatio publica* et *conclusio principaliter intenta.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *exsultationem privatam* de ove inventa dicit: *Et cum invenerit eam, imponit in humeros suos gaudens.* « *Humeri* isti », ut dicit Ambrosius[^p385-11], « sunt brachia crucis »; « illic, inquit, peccata mea deposui, in illa nobili patibuli cervice requievi ». In hos ergo humeros ovem perditam imposuit, quia ibi peccata nostra portavit; unde Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p385-12]: « Vere languores nostros ipse tulit et infirmitates nostras ipse portavit »; et primae Petri secundo: « Qui peccata nostra ipse pertulit in corpore suo super lignum, ut peccatis mortui iustitiae vivamus ». Et hoc est quod dicit Beda[^p385-13]: « Ovem in humeris suis imposuit, quia, humanam naturam suscipiens, peccata nostra ipse portavit ». Et peccata nostra portavit in cruce et crucem portavit in humeris, secundum illud Isaiae nono[^p385-14]: « Factus est principatus super humerum eius »; et Isaiae vigesimo secundo: « Dabo clavem domus David super humerum eius ». — Super hunc humerum *gaudens* nos portavit propter maximam caritatem, quam ipse habuit ad liberationem nostram; Isaiae quadragesimo[^p385-15]: « Sicut pastor gregem suum pascet, in brachio suo congregabit agnos »; « foetas ipse portabit ». Et hoc cum maximo gaudio, secundum illud Isaiae ultimo: « Ad ubera portabimini, et super genua blandientur vobis. Quomodo si cui mater blandiatur, ita et ego consolabor vos ». « Videbitis, et gaudebit cor vestrum, et ossa vestra quasi herba germinabunt ».

13. (Vers. 6.). Secundo quantum ad *congratulationem publicam* subdit: *Et veniens domum convocat amicos et vicinos.* Domus ista est Ierusalem
<!-- page 386 -->
superna, de qua Isaiae sexagesimo[^p386-1]: « Domum maiestatis meae glorificabo »; et Ioannis decimo quarto: « In domo Patris mei mansiones multae sunt ». In hanc Christus *venit* in ascensione, de quo adventu Isaiae sexagesimo tertio[^p386-2]: « Quis est iste qui venit de Edom, tinctis vestibus de Bosra »? In hanc *portat ovem,* quia, sicut dicitur ad Ephesios quarto, « ascendens in altum, captivam duxit captivitatem ». Et ibi *convocat* Angelos, qui dicuntur *amici* propter familiaritatem in cognoscendo secreta; hoc enim est signum amicitiae; Ioannis decimo quinto[^p386-3]: « Vos autem dixi amicos » etc.; et *vicini,* propter propinquitatem in communicando solatia; unde Matthaei decimo octavo: « Angeli eorum in caelis semper vident faciem Patris, qui in caelis est ». — Hos convocat Dominus ad concorditer gaudendum propter salutem hominum; unde et subdit: *Dicens illis: Congratulamini mihi, quia inveni ovem meam*[^p386-4]*, quae perierat.* Hoc dicit, quasi dans eis materiam congratulandi de consecutione tanti beneficii et amoris caritativi, quem Deus ostendit homini; unde dicit Sapientia aeterna Proverbiorum octavo[^p386-5]: « Deliciae meae esse cum filiis hominum ». Unde Beda: « Non dicit: Congratulamini ovi inventae, sed *mihi,* quia gaudium eius est vita nostra ». Unde et in expressione huius gaudii permagnifici vocatur humani generis reinventio iucunda *gaudium nuptiale,* de quo exsultant caelestes cives; Apocalypsis decimo nono[^p386-6]: « Audivi vocem tanquam vocem Angelorum multorum dicentium: Alleluia ». « Gaudeamus et exsultemus et demus gloriam Deo, quia venerunt nuptiae Agni, et uxor Agni praeparavit se ». Desponsat enim sibi Agnus[^p386-7] sanctam Ecclesiam de peccatoribus collectam, et habet Agnus oviculam reinventam.

14. (Vers. 7.). Tertio quantum ad *conclusionem principaliter intentam* subiungit: *Dico vobis, quod ita*[^p386-8] *gaudium erit in caelo super uno peccatore poenitentiam agente;* Isaiae quadragesimo nono: « Laudate caeli, et exsulta terra; iubilate montes laudem, quia consolatus est Dominus populum suum et pauperum suorum miserebitur ». — Magnum est gaudium, quia maius est infirmum sanare quam sanum conservare; et ideo subdit: *Quam super nonaginta novem iustis, qui non indigent poenitentia.* Hoc autem dicitur, non quia melior sit peccator poenitens quam nonaginta novem iusti, sed quia in redemptione humani generis maiora operatus est Deus quam in conservatione totius multitudinis caelestis; quia hoc est manifestativum maioris *potentiae,* maioris *prudentiae* et maioris *misericordiae:* maioris *potentiae,* quia maius est iustificare impium quam creare caelum et terram[^p386-9]; maioris *prudentiae,* quia, Iob vigesimo sexto, « prudentia eius percussit superbum »; maioris *clementiae* sive misericordiae; unde supra primo: « Per viscera misericordiae Dei nostri, in quibus visitavit nos Oriens ex alto ». Maior enim fuit *in Christo condescensio,* maior etiam *humani generis sublimatio,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p386-10]: « Quoniam elevata est magnificentia tua super caelos ». Unde hoc intelligitur quantum ad *causam nostrae poenitentiae,* scilicet humani generis redemptionem, in qua maior est materia gaudii quam in omnibus aliis operibus Dei. — Si autem intelligatur de *peccatore quolibet per se,* tunc intelligitur, quia gaudium est *magis novum,* vel quia peccator est *ferventior* frequenter quam iustus. Unde Gregorius in Moralibus[^p386-11]: « Quidam in Dei servitio ex anteacta alicuius debilitate roborantur, eosque ad custodienda mandata et futurorum trahit desiderium et impellit memoria praeteritorum ». Unde hoc non refertur ad omnes peccatores nec ad omnes iustos, sed ad peccatores fervidos post conversionem et iustos tepentes. Unde et Gregorius exemplificat et Beda in Glossa[^p386-12]: « Maius gaudium est de peccatore quam de iusto stante; ut dux in proelio eum militem plus diligit, qui post fugam reversus hostem fortiter premit, quam eum qui nunquam fugit nec unquam fortiter egit ». Vel, hoc dicitur ratione confusionis adversariorum, secundum

<!-- page 387 -->
illud Isaiae nono[^p387-1]: « Laetabuntur coram te, sicut qui laetantur in messe, sicut exsultant victores, capta praeda, quando dividunt spolia » etc.

### Chapter XV.

#### The impiety of the Jews is confuted in three ways.

1. *Now there were drawing near* etc. After he confuted the guile of the Pharisees who calumniate Christ's power in the miracles, in this part he *confutes their impiety,* who murmur against clemency in the works of piety[^p381-12][^tr-pietas]. Now this part is divided into three parts. In the first of which is set first *the expression of Jewish impiety.* In the second is subjoined *the manifestation of divine piety,* there[^p381-13]: *And he spoke to them a parable* etc. But in the third is added *the commendation and persuasion of human piety,* below, the sixteenth chapter: *And he said also to his disciples: There was a certain rich man.*

<!-- page 382 -->
#### The expression of Jewish impiety is described in three ways.

Now concerning *the expression of Jewish impiety* three things are introduced, namely *the occasion of expressing impiety, the expression of that already conceived* and *the simulation of the equity of justice.*

2. (Vers. 1.). First, then, as to *the occasion of expressing impiety* it is said: *Now there were drawing near to him publicans and sinners;* in which is shown the highest piety of Christ toward public sinners, from which the Pharisees took occasion of judging impiously, because it is the custom of the impious to increase their impiety from the works of piety they have seen. Whence in the Psalm[^p382-1]: « He hath distributed, he hath given to the poor » etc., and straightway he subjoins: « The wicked shall see, and shall be angry ». Now Christ showed great piety, because he received *publicans,* who were public sinners; whence the Gloss[^p382-2]: « *Publicans,* who exact or farm the public taxes, and who pursue the gain of the world through business », and such were intent on many unjust gains. Now sinners of this kind, although they seem to be repelled out of justice, are yet received out of mercy; trusting in which, *they were drawing near to him* as to *a most clement Lord,* according to that of James, the fourth chapter[^p382-3]: « Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you ». *Draw near,* I say, through devout supplication; the Psalm[^p382-4]: « Let my supplication draw near in thy sight, O Lord ». As also to *a most skilled master;* whence he subjoins: *that they might hear him,* according to that counsel of the Wise man, Ecclesiasticus, the last chapter[^p382-5]: « Draw near to me, ye unlearned, and gather yourselves together into the house of discipline »; because, as is said in Deuteronomy, the thirty-third chapter, « they that draw near to his feet shall receive of his doctrine ».

3. To this highest doctor Christ both Pharisees and publicans drew near, but *the Pharisees in pretence,* according to that of Jeremias, the twelfth chapter[^p382-6]: « Thou art near in their mouth, and far from their reins »; because such seem *near* through feigned justice, but through hidden pride are *far off,* according to that of Isaias, the fifty-eighth chapter: « For they seek me from day to day, and desire to know my ways, as a nation that hath done justice, and hath not forsaken the judgment of their God: they ask of me the judgments of justice: they are willing to draw near to God. Why have we fasted, and thou hast not regarded: have we humbled our souls, and thou hast not taken notice »? And he subjoins: « Behold in the day of your fast your own will is found » etc. But *the publicans* were drawing near *truly,* that they might hear and, having heard, might come to their senses from evils through penance, according to the Lord's exhortation, Matthew, the fourth chapter[^p382-7]: « Do penance, for the kingdom of heaven will draw near »; and that by hearing they might obey, according to that of Ecclesiastes, the fourth chapter: « Keep thy foot, when thou goest into the house of God, and draw near, that thou mayest hear. For obedience is much better than the victims of fools ».

4. (Vers. 2.). Second, as to *the expression of the impiety conceived* he subjoins: *And the Pharisees and the scribes murmured. Murmuring* is a speaking against, which is neither wholly expressed nor wholly kept silent; Wisdom, the first chapter[^p382-8]: « The ear of jealousy heareth all things, and the tumult of murmuring shall not be hid. Keep yourselves therefore from murmuring, which profiteth nothing, and refrain your tongue from detraction, for an obscure speech shall not go for nought ». And therefore the first to the Corinthians, the tenth chapter: « Neither do you murmur, as some of them murmured, and were destroyed by the destroyer ». This murmuring the Lord hates most, because *it profiteth nothing,* neither to oneself nor to one's neighbour; Lamentations, the third chapter[^p382-9]: « Why hath a living man murmured, man suffering for his sins »? as if to say: in vain; and because *it proceeds from a malignant mouth,* which neither dares to reprove openly, nor yet can hide the malice of the heart. Whence the very word *murmurare* sounds this, of which Hugutio says[^p382-10] that it is said from *mutio, mutis,* as it were *to wish to speak* and *not to dare.* But yet it arises sometimes *from infirmity,* as in Numbers, the eleventh chapter[^p382-11]: « There arose a murmuring of the people against the Lord, as it were repining at their fatigue »; sometimes *from ignorance and error,* according to that of John, the seventh chapter: « There was much murmuring among the multitude. Some said that he is good; but others: No, but he seduceth the multitudes. Yet no man spoke openly of him »; sometimes also *from malignity,* as in Deuteronomy, the first chapter[^p382-12]: « Incredulous to the word

<!-- page 383 -->
of the Lord your God, you murmured in your tents, and said: The Lord hateth us, and therefore he hath brought us out of the land of Egypt, that he might deliver us into the hands of the Amorrhite ». Likewise these too were turning good into evil, both the penance of the publicans and also the mercy of Jesus; and they fell into this vice because they were proud and inhuman; of whom can be understood that of Isaias, the sixty-fifth chapter[^p383-1]: « Who say: Depart from me, draw not near to me, because thou art unclean: these shall be smoke in my anger, a fire burning all the day ».

5. Third, as to *the simulation of the equity of justice* it is subjoined: *Saying: This man receiveth sinners,* as to the affability of conversation, *and eateth with them,* as to the familiarity of the banquet. In which they wished to convict him as a favourer of the wicked and a contemner of the precepts; whence the Gloss[^p383-2]: « As if: he is contrary to the Law, which forbids this ». For in the Law the touching of an unclean thing is forbidden, Leviticus, the eleventh and the thirteenth chapter; in which is understood the avoiding of evil company. Whence in Deuteronomy, the seventh chapter: « Thou shalt make no league with them, neither shalt thou make marriages with them ». But, as the Gloss says[^p383-3], « they err in two ways, because they both think themselves just, though they are proud, and those sinners, though they already do penance ». Whence by simulating equity they fell into impiety, *sinning against themselves* by being proud, which the Pharisees were accustomed to do; below, the sixteenth chapter[^p383-4]: « You are they who justify yourselves before men ». Again, *sinning against their neighbours* by judging ill, so that that of above, the sixth chapter, could be said to them: « Hypocrite, cast first the beam out of thy own eye » etc. Again, *sinning against Christ* by convicting his mercy, who *received* sinners, according to what he had promised through Jeremias, Jeremias, the third chapter[^p383-5]: « It is commonly said: If a man put away his wife, and she go from him, and marry another man, shall he return to her any more? But thou hast prostituted thyself to many lovers: nevertheless return to me, saith the Lord, and I will receive thee ». For this is of most excellent mercy; whence John, the sixth chapter[^p383-6]: « Him that cometh to me, I will not cast out ». They convicted his mercy also because he *condescended to eat* with them, though this is yet of the highest mercy, according to that of the Apocalypse, the third chapter[^p383-7]: « Behold, I stand at the gate, and knock » etc.; and Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « Come to me, all you that labour, and are burdened »; and John, the seventh chapter: « If any man thirst, let him come to me, and drink » etc. For the Prophet too, out of the fullness of the spirit, calls all; Isaias, the fifty-fifth chapter[^p383-8]: « All you that thirst, come to the waters ». And the reason of this is because, as is said to the Romans, the tenth chapter, « there is no distinction of the Jew and the Greek: for the same is Lord over all, rich unto all that call upon him » etc.

#### The manifestation of divine piety is described by a threefold parable.

6. *And he spoke to them a parable.* After he made manifest the Jewish impiety, so secondly he *makes known the divine piety* for confuting the Jewish impiety[^p383-9]. And since the piety of God is especially toward man in the reparation of the human race, in which man has need of *a reconciling sacrifice, a redeeming price* and *an adopting spirit;* therefore in this part he introduces a threefold parable; of which the first is of *the sheep,* which is sacrificed; the second, of *the groat,* which is the king's coin; the third is of *the father and the son,* who is received mercifully. Further, since the piety of God toward man is especially by reason of three things which God has placed in man, namely by reason of *the meekness implanted,* by reason of *the image impressed* and by reason of *the likeness conferred;* and by the first man is likened to *a sheep,* but by the second to *an imperial coin,* by the third to *the Son of the eternal Father:* therefore in this part the Evangelist, illumined by the Spirit[^p383-10], puts a threefold parable, uttered by the mouth of God's flesh, for noting the piety of God toward human nature, of which the first is of *the shepherd and his sheep;* the second is of *the woman and the groats,* there[^p383-11]: *Or what woman having ten groats;* the third of *the father and the sons,* there: *And he said: A certain man had two sons;* in which he shows manifestly that every creature in a certain way holds out the piety which God has in respect of man: *the brute creature,* as the sheep, and *the merely corporeal*[^p383-12], as the groat, and *the rational,* as man.

#### The parable of the lost sheep.

Concerning the parable, then, of *the lost sheep* he proceeds in this order. For first is expressed *the solicitude of sorrow* for the sheep lost; but second, *the greatness of joy* for the sheep found, there[^p383-13]: *And when he hath found it* etc.

<!-- page 384 -->
Now in unfolding *the solicitude of sorrow* concerning the lost sheep four things are introduced by the Evangelist, namely *the possession of the whole multitude, the loss of the hundredth sheep* and *the leaving of the multitude possessed* and *the seeking of the lost one*[^p384-1].

7. (Vers. 3.). First, then, as to *the possession of the whole multitude,* which he wishes to be understood not according to the letter, but parabolically, he says: *And he spoke to them this parable.* Therefore he speaks parabolically, because *it befits his wisdom;* the Psalm[^p384-2]: « I will open my mouth in parables » etc.; and because *the perfidy of the Jews required* this, from whom the mysteries had to be veiled, according to that of Matthew, the thirteenth chapter: « Therefore do I speak to them in parables: because seeing they see not, and hearing they hear not »; or because *it befitted sacred Scripture,* in which not only words, but also things are signs for leading by the hand the understanding of the little ones and the simple to conceive in themselves the multiformity of the divine understandings, according to that of Proverbs, the first chapter[^p384-3]: « The parables of Solomon, to know wisdom, and instruction: to understand the words of prudence, and to receive the erudition of doctrine, to give subtilty to little ones, to the young man knowledge and understanding ».

8. (Vers. 4.). And since he speaks to little ones, therefore he places manifest examples beneath, when he says: *What man of you that hath a hundred sheep. That man* among men is the Son of God, of whom Baruch, the third chapter[^p384-4]: « Afterwards he was seen upon earth, and conversed with men »; and to the Philippians, the second chapter: « Being made in the likeness of men, and in habit found as a man ». *The sheep* of this man are the rational creatures by reason of the meekness implanted; Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p384-5]: « Behold I send you as sheep in the midst of wolves » etc.; John, the tenth chapter: « My sheep hear my voice, and I give them life everlasting ». Now these are called *a hundred* on account of perfection, which is intimated by the hundred on account of the return of ten upon itself. Whence Bede[^p384-6]: « Because the number one hundred is perfect, God had a hundred sheep, that is, a perfect number, when he created the substance of Angels and of men ». For this cause also in the designation of *perfect merit* it is put in Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p384-7]: « Other some fell upon good ground, and brought forth fruit a hundredfold »; and Genesis, the twenty-sixth chapter: « Isaac sowed in that land, and he found that same year a hundredfold ». Likewise also as to *the perfection of the reward;* Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p384-8]: « Every one that hath left » etc., there follows: « shall receive a hundredfold, and shall possess life everlasting ». *To have,* then, *a hundred sheep* is to have creatures in a perfect number and in the state of their perfection.

9. Second, as to *the loss of the hundredth sheep* he subjoins: *And if he shall lose one of them.* God lost *one* sheep of a hundred when man sinned. Whence Bede[^p384-9]: « One sheep perished when man by sinning left the pastures of life ». Of this sheep it is said in the Psalm: « I have gone astray like a sheep that is lost: seek thy servant, O Lord ». Now man is therefore called *one sheep,* because all proceeded from one man, in whom also they sinned, according to that to the Romans, the fifth chapter[^p384-10]: « By one man sin entered into this world, and by sin death; in whom all have sinned ». But on account of the variety of faults added over they are called *many sheep;* Isaias, the fifty-third chapter[^p384-11]: « All we like sheep have gone astray, every one hath turned aside into his own way »; and the first of Peter, the second chapter: « You were as sheep going astray » etc. And this was so until man lacked a shepherd; the third of Kings, the twenty-second chapter[^p384-12]: « I saw all Israel scattered upon the mountains, like sheep that have no shepherd ». For this sheep had run upon the wolf, the devil, of whom John, the tenth chapter: « The wolf catcheth, and scattereth the sheep » etc.

10. Third, as to *the leaving of the multitude possessed* he subjoins: *Doth he not leave the ninety-nine in the desert?* By *the ninety-nine* are understood the Angels, both because the number nine best befits the designation of the number of the Angels on account of the three tripled in the orders of the Angels, for designating God three and one; and because, the angels falling, and man lost, their number remained imperfect; whence the Gloss[^p384-13]: « He left the ninety-nine, because the number of the rational creature, man perishing, was diminished ». These Christ *left,* not deserting them in place, since he is always everywhere, but because he took not hold of them at all; to the Hebrews, the second chapter[^p384-14]: « For nowhere doth he take hold of the Angels: but of the seed of Abraham he taketh hold ». Now he is said *to have left them in the desert,* that is, in heaven, which was deserted by angels and men, and no one now could enter there. On account of which that

<!-- page 385 -->
supernal fatherland is compared to *a desert;* whence the Gloss[^p385-1]: « *In the desert,* that is, in heaven, which is deserted by man sinning »; on account of which also that supernal Jerusalem is called *desolate,* according to that to the Galatians, the fourth chapter[^p385-2]: « That Jerusalem, which is above, is free: which is our mother. For it is written: Rejoice, thou barren, that bearest not: for many are the children of the desolate, more than of her that hath a husband »; and Isaias, the thirty-fifth chapter: « The land that was desolate and impassable shall be glad, and the wilderness shall exult and shall flourish like the lily » etc.

11. Fourth, as to *the seeking of the single lost sheep* he adds: *And goeth to that which was lost;* to this sheep, namely man, God came to save it, the Son of God into this world; below, the nineteenth chapter[^p385-3]: « The Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost »; whence the first to Timothy, the first chapter: « A faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into this world to save sinners ». And this Christ did as *the good shepherd;* whence Ezechiel, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p385-4]: « Behold I myself will seek my sheep, and will visit them, as the shepherd visiteth his flock ». — But because the good shepherd does not desist from the search before salvation, therefore he subjoins: *Until he find it.* Now this sheep is found by Christ when it is freed from perdition by his blood, according to that of Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter[^p385-5]: « The Lord alone was his leader: and there was no strange god with him ». « He found him in a desert land, in a place of horror, and of vast wilderness ». Now God found lost men with great difficulty, according to that of Osee, the ninth chapter[^p385-6]: « I found Israel like grapes in the desert, and like the first fruits of the fig tree ». Whence this shepherd laboured even unto death for finding the sheep, according to that of John, the tenth chapter: « The good shepherd giveth his life for his sheep ». On account of which also this shepherd was made *a lamb* among the sheep, that he might redeem the sheep, according to that of Isaias, the fifty-third chapter[^p385-7]: « He shall be led as a sheep to the slaughter, and shall be dumb as a lamb before his shearer, and he shall not open his mouth ». — Whence also that finding prefigured this finding, by which Abraham, willing to offer his son, found the ram; Genesis, the twenty-second chapter[^p385-8]: « Abraham lifted up his eyes, and saw a ram amongst the briers sticking fast by the horns, which he offered for a holocaust instead of his son ». All those other sacrifices offered, then, of sheep as well as of lambs and of goats as well as of rams and calves, signify this, that[^p385-9] « Christ by his own blood entered once into the holies, having found an eternal redemption ». And this is to say: « He seeketh diligently, until he find ».

12. (Vers. 5.). *And when he hath found it.* After the solicitude of sorrow for the sheep lost there follows here *the greatness of joy for the sheep found*[^p385-10], concerning which three things are introduced, namely *private exultation, public congratulation* and *the conclusion principally intended.*

First, then, as to *private exultation* over the sheep found he says: *And when he hath found it, he layeth it upon his shoulders, rejoicing.* « *Those shoulders* », as Ambrose says[^p385-11], « are the arms of the cross »; « there, he says, I laid down my sins, on that noble neck of the gibbet I rested ». On these shoulders, then, he laid the lost sheep, because there he bore our sins; whence Isaias, the fifty-third chapter[^p385-12]: « Surely he hath borne our languors, and he himself hath carried our infirmities »; and the first of Peter, the second chapter: « Who his own self bore our sins in his body upon the tree: that we, being dead to sins, should live to justice ». And this is what Bede says[^p385-13]: « He laid the sheep upon his shoulders, because, taking up human nature, he himself bore our sins ». And he bore our sins on the cross and bore the cross on his shoulders, according to that of Isaias, the ninth chapter[^p385-14]: « The government is upon his shoulder »; and Isaias, the twenty-second chapter: « I will lay the key of the house of David upon his shoulder ». — Upon this shoulder he carried us *rejoicing,* on account of the very great charity which he had toward our deliverance; Isaias, the fortieth chapter[^p385-15]: « He shall feed his flock like a shepherd: he shall gather together the lambs with his arm »; « he himself shall carry them that are with young ». And this with the greatest joy, according to that of Isaias, the last chapter: « You shall be carried at the breasts, and upon the knees they shall caress you. As one whom the mother caresseth, so will I comfort you ». « You shall see, and your heart shall rejoice, and your bones shall flourish like an herb ».

13. (Vers. 6.). Second, as to *public congratulation* he subjoins: *And coming home he calleth together his friends and neighbours.* That house is the supernal Jerusalem,

<!-- page 386 -->
of which Isaias, the sixtieth chapter[^p386-1]: « I will glorify the house of my majesty »; and John, the fourteenth chapter: « In my Father's house there are many mansions ». Into this Christ *came* in the ascension, of which coming Isaias, the sixty-third chapter[^p386-2]: « Who is this that cometh from Edom, with dyed garments from Bosra »? Into this he *carries the sheep,* because, as is said to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter, « ascending on high, he led captivity captive ». And there he *calls together* the Angels, who are called *friends* on account of familiarity in knowing secrets; for this is a sign of friendship; John, the fifteenth chapter[^p386-3]: « But I have called you friends » etc.; and *neighbours,* on account of nearness in sharing solaces; whence Matthew, the eighteenth chapter: « Their Angels in heaven always see the face of the Father, who is in heaven ». — These the Lord calls together for rejoicing in concord over the salvation of men; whence also he subjoins: *Saying to them: Rejoice with me, because I have found my sheep*[^p386-4]*, that was lost.* This he says as giving them matter for rejoicing with him over the obtaining of so great a benefit and of the charitable love which God showed to man; whence the eternal Wisdom says, Proverbs, the eighth chapter[^p386-5]: « My delights were to be with the children of men ». Whence Bede: « He does not say: Rejoice with the sheep found, but *with me,* because our life is his joy ». Whence also in the expression of this most magnificent joy the joyful finding again of the human race is called *the nuptial joy,* over which the heavenly citizens exult; Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter[^p386-6]: « I heard a voice as it were the voice of many Angels saying: Alleluia ». « Let us be glad and exult, and give glory to God; for the marriage of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath prepared herself ». For the Lamb espouses to himself[^p386-7] the holy Church gathered from sinners, and the Lamb has the little sheep found again.

14. (Vers. 7.). Third, as to *the conclusion principally intended* he subjoins: *I say to you, that so*[^p386-8] *there shall be joy in heaven upon one sinner that doth penance;* Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter: « Give praise, O ye heavens, and exult, O earth, ye mountains, give praise with jubilation: because the Lord hath comforted his people, and will have mercy on his poor ones ». — Great is the joy, because it is a greater thing to heal the sick than to preserve the healthy; and therefore he subjoins: *More than upon ninety-nine just who need not penance.* Now this is said, not because the penitent sinner is better than ninety-nine just, but because in the redemption of the human race God wrought greater things than in the preservation of the whole heavenly multitude; because this is manifestative of greater *power,* of greater *prudence* and of greater *mercy:* of greater *power,* because it is a greater thing to justify the impious than to create heaven and earth[^p386-9]; of greater *prudence,* because, Job, the twenty-sixth chapter, « his prudence hath struck the proud one »; of greater *clemency* or mercy; whence above, the first chapter: « Through the bowels of the mercy of our God, in which the Orient from on high hath visited us ». For greater was *the condescension in Christ,* greater also *the exaltation of the human race,* according to that of the Psalm[^p386-10]: « For thy magnificence is elevated above the heavens ». Whence this is understood as to *the cause of our penance,* namely the redemption of the human race, in which there is greater matter of joy than in all the other works of God. — But if it be understood of *any sinner by himself,* then it is understood because the joy is *newer,* or because the sinner is frequently *more fervent* than the just man. Whence Gregory in the *Morals*[^p386-11]: « Some in the service of God are strengthened from someone's former weakness, and both the desire of things to come draws them and the memory of things past drives them to keeping the commandments ». Whence this is not referred to all sinners nor to all the just, but to sinners fervent after conversion and to the just who are lukewarm. Whence also Gregory gives the example, and Bede in the Gloss[^p386-12]: « There is greater joy over the sinner than over the just man standing; as a captain in battle loves that soldier more who, returning after flight, presses the enemy bravely, than him who never fled nor ever did bravely ». Or, this is said by reason of the confusion of the adversaries, according to

<!-- page 387 -->
that of Isaias, the ninth chapter[^p387-1]: « They shall rejoice before thee, as they that rejoice in the harvest, as conquerors exult after taking a prey, when they divide the spoils » etc.
