# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. VI, Vers. 20-26
## *Et ipse, elevatis oculis etc. Post electionem discipulorum et attractionem auditorum subditur hic explicatio documentorum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 147 -->

49. *Et ipse, elevatis oculis* etc. Post electionem discipulorum et attractionem auditorum subditur hic *explicatio documentorum.* Est autem pars ista tripartita secundum tria, quorum notitia est ad salutem necessaria, scilicet *praemiorum, suppliciorum, praeceptorum.* Prima igitur pars est de *praemiis,* secunda de *suppliciis,* ibi[^p147-10]: *Verumtamen vae vobis divitibus!* tertia de *praeceptis,* ibi: *Sed vobis dico, qui auditis* etc. Prima pars continet promissiones praemiorum; secunda, comminationes poenarum sive suppliciorum; tertia vero, leges praeceptorum. In promissis praemiis docemur, quid *sperandum;* in comminatis suppliciis, quid *timendum;* in praeceptis vero, quid *operandum.*

Circa *promissionem* autem *beatitudinum* nota, quod quatuor promittit, scilicet *regnum,* quod consistit in participatione divinae potestatis; *saturitatem,* quae consistit in gustu divinae bonitatis; *risum,* qui consistit in contemplatione divinae veritatis; et *mercedem multam,* quae consistit in duratione aeternitatis. Et haec quatuor designantur ad Ephesios tertio[^p147-11]: « Ut possitis, inquit, comprehendere cum omnibus Sanctis, quae sit *longitudo, latitudo, sublimitas* et *profundum* »: *longitudo* aeternitatis, *latitudo* bonitatis, *sublimitas* potestatis et *profunditas* sapientiae sive veritatis. Haec autem quatuor promittit quatuor generibus meritorum erigentium nos ad Deum et rectificantium liberum arbitrium nostrum. *Regnum* enim *opulentiae* promittitur contemnentibus bona temporalia, cuiusmodi sunt *pauperes; convivium abundantiae* desiderantibus spiritualia, et hi *esurientes* dicuntur; *risus iucunditatis* detestantibus
<!-- page 148 -->
mala culpae, et hi dicuntur *flentes; multitudo mercedis* sustinentibus mala poenae, et hi *patientes.* In his quatuor clauditur perfectio[^p148-1] voluntatis humanae respectu duplicis boni et respectu duplicis mali.

50. (Vers. 20.). Primo ergo promittit *pauperibus regnum opulentiae* propter *contemptum temporalium,* et tales erant discipuli, et ideo dicit: *Et ipse, elevatis oculis in discipulos suos, dicebat: Beati pauperes* etc. *Elevando oculos* dicit beatos, quia[^p148-2] « oculi Domini super iustos », et maxime super pauperes; Ecclesiastici undecimo: « Est homo marcidus et indigens recuperatione, deficiens virtute et abundans paupertate, et oculus Domini respexit illum in bono ». Ideo autem vocat *beatos* hos pauperes, quia sunt ad beatitudinem praeparati. — Et ideo addit: *Quoniam*[^p148-3] *vestrum est regnum Dei;* quod quidem est valde magnum. Nam in Psalmo: « Regnum tuum, Domine, regnum omnium saeculorum ». Hoc dicit *esse pauperum,* quia illius pauperes sunt heredes, secundum illud Iacobi secundo: « Nonne Deus elegit pauperes in hoc mundo, divites in fide, heredes regni, quod repromisit Deus diligentibus se »? « Melior est ergo pauper, qui ambulat in simplicitate sua, quam dives torquens labia sua et insipiens », Proverbiorum decimo nono.

51. Et nota, quod dicit *est* de praesenti sive propter certitudinem promissionis, sive etiam quia veri pauperes nunc quodam modo incipiunt esse *reges,* secundum illud secundae ad Corinthios sexto[^p148-4]: « Tanquam nihil habentes et omnia possidentes ». « Altissima enim paupertas eorum abundavit in divitias simplicitatis », sicut dicitur secundae ad Corinthios octavo. Unde iam quasi felices sunt, non miseri reputandi pauperes evangelici sive voluntarii. Seneca[^p148-5]: « Nemo nascitur dives: quisquis exit in lucem, iussus est pane et lacte esse contentus ». « Nemo ad haec pauper est; inter quae si quis desiderium suum clauserit, cum Iove poterit de felicitate contendere ».

Et nota, quod a *paupertate* incipit, quia, sicut ibi[^p148-6] dicit Ambrosius, « paupertas est prima parens virtutum, quia qui contempserit saecularia merebitur aeterna », aemulus illius pauperis, « qui, cum dives esset, factus est propter nos egenus », secundae ad Corinthios octavo.

52. (Vers. 21.). Secundo promittit *esurientibus convivium abundantiae* propter *desiderium spiritualium;* et hoc notat, cum subdit: *Beati, qui nunc esuritis,* scilicet cibum iustitiae, secundum quod dicitur in Glossa Matthaei quinto[^p148-7]. *Quoniam saturabimini,* per convivium abundantiae. Ambrosius: « Qui esurit virium quaerit augmentum », secundum illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto: « Qui edunt me adhuc esurient, et qui bibunt me adhuc sitient ». Illi iustitiam esuriunt, qui possunt dicere cum Iesu illud Ioannis quarto[^p148-8]: « Meus cibus est, ut faciam voluntatem Patris mei ». Illi etiam iustitiam esuriunt, qui amore iustitiae libenter esuriem patiuntur; et hi possunt cum Apostolo dicere illud primae ad Corinthios quarto: « Usque in hanc horam esurimus et sitimus ». Tales fuerunt illi sancti Patres, de quibus ad Hebraeos undecimo dicitur, quod « circuierunt in melotis, egentes, angustiati, afflicti » etc. Tales a Domino saturabuntur, quia, Apocalypsis septimo[^p148-9], « non esurient neque sitient amplius, nec cadet super illos sol neque ullus aestus, quoniam Agnus, qui in medio throni est, reget illos ». Et hoc erit in divina gloria, secundum quod in Psalmo dicitur: « Satiabor, cum apparuerit gloria tua »; et iterum: « Inebriabuntur ab ubertate domus tuae, et torrente voluptatis tuae potabis eos ». Et sic impletum est illud primi Regum secundo[^p148-10]: « Famelici saturati sunt ». Nec mirum, quia, Isaiae vigesimo quinto, « faciet Dominus exercituum omnibus populis in monte hoc convivium vindemiae, pinguium medullatorum, vindemiae defaecatae » et rursus, secundum illud Psalmi: « Cibavit eos ex adipe frumenti et de petra melle saturavit eos ».
<!-- page 149 -->

53. Tertio promittit *flentibus risum iucunditatis* propter *poenitentiam de malo culpae,* cum adiungit: *Beati, qui nunc fletis,* scilicet pro *malis commissis,* sicut Ezechias, « conversus ad parietem », « flevit fletu magno », sicut dicitur Isaiae trigesimo octavo[^p149-1]; sicut etiam Petrus, de quo Matthaei vigesimo sexto post negationem dicitur, quod « egressus foras flevit amare »; et Iob trigesimo: « Versa est in luctum cithara mea, et organum meum in vocem flentium ». — Vel pro *peccatis alienis,* sicut David super Saulem, secundi Regum primo[^p149-2]; sic Christus super Ierusalem, infra decimo nono: « Videns civitatem, flevit super eam ». — Vel: *nunc fletis,* scilicet pro *alienis miseriis;* Iob trigesimo: « Flebam quondam super eo qui afflictus erat »; ad Romanos duodecimo: « Flere cum flentibus ». — Vel: *Qui nunc fletis,* pro *evadendis tentationibus* peccatorum et sequelis; Iudicum secundo[^p149-3] dicitur, quod « cum loqueretur ad filios Israel Angelus Domini, elevaverunt vocem suam et fleverunt. Et vocatum est nomen loci illius locus flentium sive lacrymarum ». Et hic locus est status praesentis vitae, qui non durat nisi ad *nunc.* Et ideo dicit: *Qui nunc fletis;* primae Petri primo[^p149-4]: « Modicum nunc si oportet contristari in variis tentationibus, ut probatio vestrae fidei multo pretiosior auro, quod per ignem probatur, inveniatur in laudem » etc. — Vel: *Qui nunc fletis,* scilicet pro *desiderio aeternorum,* et hic est proprius intellectus; Ioannis decimo sexto[^p149-5]: « Plorabitis et flebitis vos, mundus autem gaudebit, vos autem contristabimini; sed tristitia vestra vertetur in gaudium »; et Psalmus: « Fuerunt mihi lacrymae meae panes die ac nocte, dum dicitur mihi quotidie: Ubi est Deus tuus »? Istis dicit: estis beati, *quia ridebitis,* risu spirituali, qui consistit in iucunditate mentis propter contemplationem veritatis; Iob octavo[^p149-6]: « Implebitur risu os tuum, et labia tua iubilo »; quia dicitur Proverbiorum ultimo: « Fortitudo et decor indumentum eius, et ridebit in die novissimo ». Hic risus erit de bonis propriis et alienis, secundum quod dicitur de Sara Genesis vigesimo primo: « Risum fecit mihi Dominus, et quicumque audierit, corridebit mihi ».

54. (Vers. 22.). Quarto vero promittit *patientibus magnitudinem mercedis* propter *tolerantiam in malo poenae,* cum subdit: *Beati eritis, cum vos oderint homines,* scilicet quantum ad *actum cordis.* Optime dicit: *homines,* id est animales et bestiales, quia[^p149-7] « homo, cum in honore esset, non intellexit; comparatus est iumentis insipientibus et similis factus est illis »; primae ad Corinthios tertio: « Cum sit inter vos zelus et contentio, nonne carnales estis et secundum hominem ambulatis »? secundum illum scilicet hominem, de quo dicitur Ecclesiastici vigesimo octavo: « Homo homini servat iram ». Isti oderunt servos Christi; Proverbiorum decimo nono[^p149-8]: « Fratres hominis pauperis oderunt eum »; et Matthaei decimo: « Eritis odio omnibus hominibus propter nomen meum ». — Quantum ad *actum operis* addit: *Et cum separaverint vos,* scilicet eiiciendo extra synagogas, secundum illud Ioannis nono[^p149-9]: « Iam conspiraverant Iudaei, ut, si quis eum confiteretur Christum, extra synagogam fieret », et ita in opprobrium separaretur ab aliis, etiam ab ipsis domesticis, quemadmodum praedicabat Dominus venturum esse suis discipulis; Matthaei decimo: « Veni, separare hominem adversus patrem suum »; et post subditur: « Et inimici hominis domestici eius ». — Quantum ad *actum oris* subiungit: *Et exprobraverint,* vos conviciando, *et eiecerint nomen vestrum tanquam malum,* detrahendo; primae Petri quarto[^p149-10]: « Si exprobramini in nomine Christi, beati eritis »; Iacobi secundo: « Ipsi blasphemant nomen bonum, quod invocatum est super vos », quia *bonum* dicunt malum, secundum illud Matthaei quinto: « Dixerunt omne malum adversum vos mentientes, propter me ». — Et quia Martyrem non facit qualiscumque poena, nisi adsit debita causa, ideo subdit: *Propter Filium hominis;* primae Petri tertio[^p149-11]: « Si quid patimini propter iustitiam, beati eritis », id est propter Christum, « qui factus est a Deo sapientia et iustitia et sanctificatio ». Et hac iustitia triplici volebat pati Paulus; Actuum vigesimo primo: « Ego non solum alligari, sed et occidi paratus sum in Ierusalem propter Christum Iesum ».

55. (Vers. 23.). Sic patientes laetificat per praemium, cum subdit: *Gaudete*[^p149-12] *et exsultate; ecce,*
<!-- page 150 -->
*merces vestra multa est in caelo; gaudete,* scilicet ex retributione; ad Romanos duodecimo: « Spe gaudentes, in tribulatione patientes ». Quod impletum est Actuum quinto: « Ibant Apostoli gaudentes a conspectu concilii, quoniam digni habiti sunt pro nomine Iesu contumeliam pati ». Hoc autem inducit mercedis consideratio; Ieremiae trigesimo primo[^p150-1]: « Quiescat vox tua a planctu, et oculi tui a lacrymis, quia merces est operi tuo ». *Merces,* inquam, *multa,* quia « pax multa diligentibus legem tuam » etc., ut in Psalmo dicitur; et *perpetua,* quia *in caelo;* Sapientiae quinto: « Iusti autem in perpetuum vivent, et apud Dominum est merces eorum ». Et hoc est quod dicit Beda[^p150-2]: « Inter odia cordium, inter probra linguarum, inter manus persequentium laetiori corde versamini intuitu mercedis supernae ». — Et quia hoc opus est arduissimum, scilicet gaudere in tribulationibus; ideo non solum spondet praemium, sed adiungit exemplum, cum subdit:

56. *Secundum hoc*[^p150-3] *enim faciebant Prophetis patres eorum;* secundum quod dicitur Matthaei vigesimo tertio: « Testimonio estis vobismetipsis, quia filii estis eorum qui occiderunt Prophetas »; et Actuum septimo: « Dura cervice et incircumcisis cordibus et auribus, vos semper Spiritui sancto restitistis. Quem Prophetarum non sunt persecuti patres vestri? Et occiderunt eos qui prophetabant de adventu iusti ». Hos proponit in exemplum propter suam constantiam; Iacobi ultimo[^p150-4]: « Exemplum accipite, fratres, patientiae, laboris et longanimitatis Prophetas »; qui a veritate propter flagella non movebantur. Unde de Eliseo Ecclesiastici quadragesimo octavo: « In diebus suis non pertimuit principem, et potentia nemo vicit illum, nec superavit illum homo ». Haec omnia mala sustinebant Prophetae propter mercedem, quam aliis promittunt; Ecclesiastici trigesimo sexto[^p150-5]: « Da mercedem, Domine, sustinentibus te, ut Prophetae tui fideles inveniantur ». Unde ille eximius Propheta Ioannes post omnes tribulationes Ecclesiae subiungit Apocalypsis vigesimo secundo in fine: « Ecce, venio cito, et merces mea mecum est, dare unicuique secundum opera sua ».

57. Unde nota, quod divina Scriptura insinuat nobis differentiam *praemiorum* secundum diversitates *meritorum.* Unde invenitur merces *salva,* merces *magna, digna, plena, opportuna*[^p150-6], *festina, fidelis, copiosa, perpetua.* — Merces *salva* est Angelis nobis ministrantibus; *magna* datur praelatis sive praesidentibus; *digna,* obedientibus; *plena,* contemplantibus; *temporanea* datur operantibus; *festina,* proficientibus; *fidelis,* perficientibus; *multa* sive copiosa, patientibus, sed *perpetua,* perseverantibus.

Unde de *salva mercede Angelorum,* quia ministerium, quod habent erga nos, a suo praemio non fraudatur, dicitur Tobiae quinto[^p150-7]: « Quaere aliquem virum fidelem, qui tecum eat, salva mercede », dicit Tobias. — De *mercede magna praesidentium* dicitur Genesis decimo quinto ad Abraham, qui typum gerit praelatorum: « Ego protector tuus, et merces tua magna nimis ». — De *mercede digna obedientium* dicitur Esther decimo sexto: « Sciant omnes, qui Persis obediunt fideliter, dignam pro fide recipere mercedem ». — De *mercede plena contemplantium* dicitur ad Ruth, secundo, quae typum gerit contemplativorum, qui cuncta relinquunt propter Deum, et etiam interpretatur *ovis,* vel *videns*[^p150-8], ut *ovis* dicatur propter mititatem affectus anima contemplativa, sed *videns* propter illuminationem intellectus; unde dicit ei Booz: « Plenam mercedem recipies a Domino Deo Israel, ad quem venisti et sub cuius confugisti alas ». — De *mercede temporanea operantium* dicitur Ecclesiastici ultimo[^p150-9]: « Operamini opus vestrum, quando tempus est, et dabit vobis mercedem vestram in tempore suo ». — De *mercede festina proficientium* dicitur Ecclesiastici undecimo: « Benedictio Dei in mercedem iusti festinat ». — De *mercede fideli perfectorum,* ut praedicantium vel talium, dicitur Proverbiorum undecimo[^p150-10]: « Seminanti iustitiam merces fidelis ». — De *mercede copiosa patientium* sive militantium dicitur hic et Matthaei quinto: « Ecce enim, merces vestra copiosa est in caelis ». — De *mercede perpetua* vero, quae congruit *perseverantibus,* Ecclesiastici decimo octavo[^p150-11]: « Non verearis usque ad mortem iustificari, quoniam merces Dei manet in aeternum ». — Sic ergo secundum diversitates meritorum appropriantur differentiae praemiorum; et sic « reddet Deus iustis mercedem laborum suorum », Sapientiae decimo.
<!-- page 151 -->

58. (Vers. 24.). *Verumtamen vae vobis divitibus!* Post promissiones beatitudinum subiungit *comminationes suppliciorum.* Et quia contra bonum malum[^p151-1], quatuor ponit comminationes quatuor beatitudinibus respondentes, scilicet *desolationem* in amissione boni temporalis, *esuriem* in amissione boni spiritualis, *fletum* in afflictione mali corporalis, *confusionem* in passione opprobrii sempiternalis. Et haec sunt quatuor *vae,* quae comminatur quatuor generibus hominum oppositis viris evangelicis praedeterminatis, scilicet *cupidis* et *gulosis, lascivis* et *vanagloriosis*[^p151-2].

*Cupidis* ergo comminatur *vae sempiternae desolationis,* cum dicit: *Verumtamen vae vobis divitibus! Adversative* dicit *divitibus,* id est divitias amantibus, quia tales inhiant ad divitias congregandas; Habacuc secundo[^p151-3]: « Vae ei qui multiplicat non sua »! id est bona terrena, quae non sunt nostra, quia nec nobiscum veniunt nec recedunt; terreni tamen homines reputant, esse sua; Apocalypsis octavo: « Vae, vae, vae habitantibus in terra »! Tales sunt cupidi, contra quos dicit Dominus Matthaei sexto: « Nolite vobis thesaurizare thesauros in terra, ubi aerugo et tinea demolitur ». — Quoniam ad divitiarum istarum abundantiam necesse est sequi *inopiam,* et ad consolationem sequi *desolationem;* ideo subdens rationem dicit: *Qui*[^p151-4] *habetis consolationem vestram; habetis,* scilicet de praesenti, sed non de futuro; unde Iacobi quinto: « Agite nunc, divites, plorate, ululantes in miseriis vestris, quae advenient vobis ». Ideo consolatio ista vana et falsa est, quia desolationem inducit; Zachariae decimo[^p151-5]: « Somniatores frustra locuti sunt et vane consolabantur ». Vere *frustra,* quia « dormierunt somnum suum viri divitiarum et nihil invenerunt in manibus suis ». Et propterea dicendum est cum Propheta David[^p151-6]: « Renuit consolari anima mea; memor fui Dei et delectatus sum »; Iob sexto: « Haec sit mihi consolatio, ut affligens dolore, non parcat »; ut per desolationem viae perveniatur ad consolationem patriae, de qua Isaiae ultimo: « Quomodo si cui mater blandiatur, ita ego consolabor vos ». Hanc debemus exspectare, non praeoccupare; Iudith octavo: « Exspectemus humiles consolationem eius ».

59. (Vers. 25.). *Gulosis* comminatur *vae perpetuae famis,* cum subdit: *Vae vobis, qui saturati estis!* vacando scilicet crapulae et ebrietati; Proverbiorum vigesimo tertio[^p151-7]: « Cui vae? Cuius patri vae? Cui rixae et sine causa vulnera? Nonne his qui demorantur in vino et student calicibus epotandis »? « Vae, qui potentes estis ad bibendum vinum et viri fortes ad miscendam ebrietatem »! Et ideo subditur: *Quia esurietis.* Saturitas haec inducit perpetuam famem, secundum illud Iob vigesimo[^p151-8]: « Cum satiatus fuerit, arctabitur, aestuabit, et omnis dolor irruet super eum »; et Isaiae sexagesimo quinto: « Ecce, servi mei comedent, et vos esurietis; ecce, servi mei bibent, et vos sitietis ». Unde de malis in Psalmo: « Manducaverunt et saturati sunt » etc.

60. *Lascivis* comminatur *vae sempiterni fletus,* cum dicit: *Vae vobis, qui ridetis nunc! quia lugebitis et flebitis,* id est in hoc momento temporis; Iob vigesimo[^p151-9]: « Gaudium hypocritae ad instar puncti »; et Proverbiorum decimo quarto: « Risus dolore miscebitur, et extrema gaudii luctus occupabit ». Melius est certe, quod fletus ad risum antecedat quam e converso; et propterea Ecclesiastae tertio[^p151-10]: « Tempus flendi », scilicet prius in tempore, « et tempus ridendi », post in aeternitate. Sed fatuus non vult nunc continere a risu; Ecclesiastici vigesimo primo: « Fatuus in risu exaltat vocem suam; vir autem prudens vix tacite ridebit »; immo Ecclesiastae secundo: « Risum reputavi errorem, et gaudio dixi: Quid frustra deciperis »? Stulti ergo sunt qui nunc rident, et sapientes qui lugent, secundum illud Ecclesiastae septimo[^p151-11]: « Cor sapientium, ubi tristitia est; et cor stultorum, ubi laetitia ». Propter quod dicitur stultis et mundanis Isaiae sexagesimo quinto: « Ecce, servi mei laudabunt prae exsultatione cordis, et vos clamabitis prae dolore cordis et prae contritione spiritus ululabitis », scilicet in inferno, quia dicitur Matthaei decimo tertio: « Ibi erit fletus et stridor dentium ».

61. (Vers. 26.). *Vanagloriosis* vero comminatur *vae sempiterni contemptus,* cum subdit: *Vae vobis, cum benedixerint vobis omnes*[^p151-12] *homines!* vanis scilicet laudibus extollendo; Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo: « Qui benedicit proximo suo voce
<!-- page 152 -->
grandi, de nocte consurgens, maledicenti similis erit »; quia, sicut dicitur Isaiae nono, « erunt qui beatificant populum istum seducentes, et qui beatificantur praecipitantur ». Praecipitantur enim ex vanis laudibus in opprobrium sempiternum, dum ex illis extolluntur, secundum illud Iob trigesimo[^p152-1]: « Elevasti me, quasi super ventum ponens », scilicet vanae laudis, « elisisti valide ». — Et quia difficile est huiusmodi laudes vitare, subiungit exemplum: *Secundum hoc*[^p152-2] *enim faciebant prophetis patres eorum;* prophetis, inquam, non veris, sed falsis; unde Glossa: « Benedicebant pseudoprophetis, qui ad captandum favorem vulgi de corde suo prophetabant »; Ieremiae quinto: « Stupor et mirabilia facta sunt in terra: prophetae prophetabant mendacium, et sacerdotes plaudebant manibus suis, et populus meus dilexit talia ». In hoc ergo, quod ostendit, malos homines laudari a prophetis, ostendit, omnino contemnendum esse favorem popularem; Psalmus[^p152-3]: « Deus dissipavit ossa eorum qui hominibus placent; confusi sunt, quoniam Deus sprevit eos ». Hoc autem dictum est non de his qui bona exempla et laudabilia populis ostendunt ad Deum glorificandum, cum dicatur Matthaei quinto[^p152-4]: « Luceat lux vestra coram hominibus, ut videant opera vestra bona et glorificent Patrem vestrum, qui in caelis est »; sed qui propriam gloriam in placendo hominibus et in eorum laudibus quaerunt; ad Galatas primo: « Si adhuc hominibus placerem, Christi servus non essem ».

62. Nota hic de diversitate inter Matthaeum[^p152-5] et Lucam: quia Lucas ponit *promissiones* et *comminationes vae,* Matthaeus autem solum *promissiones.* Propter quod videtur, vel quod alter dixerit diminute, vel alter superflue. — Sed certe potest responderi, secundum quod Glossa[^p152-6] innuit, et Augustinus dicit, quod alius fuerit sermo hic et ille. Cuius diversitas ex *locis* colligitur et *personis* et *praeceptis.* Nam ille *discipulis,* iste *turbis;* ille *sedendo,* iste *stando;* ille *in monte,* iste *in loco campestri;* ille *plura documenta continet* quam iste, quia *septem* beatitudines[^p152-7], iste *quatuor;* ex quibus omnibus colligitur, quod fuerit alius et alius. — Nec immerito, quia aliis et aliis personis aliter et aliter est loquendum. Discipulis enim tanquam perfectioribus perfectiori modo debuerunt mandata evangelica explicari; turbae vero non possunt ad alta conscendere: ideo non adeo perfecte explicatur verbum Dei turbis, sicut Apostolis. — Et quia perfecti moventur amore, imperfecti vero non solum amore, sed timore[^p152-8]; ideo in sermone Apostolorum ponuntur *beatitudines* et non *vae,* in sermone turbarum et *beatitudines* et *vae.* — Rursus, quia perfecti conformes efficiuntur Christo per septiformem Spiritum, qui requievit super eum, ut dicitur Isaiae undecimo[^p152-9]; ideo septem ponuntur beatitudines, septem donis Spiritus sancti concordantes. Hic notantur quatuor beatitudines respondentes quatuor virtutibus cardinalibus, ut habetur in Glossa[^p152-10]: *Paupertas,* temperantiae; *esuries* boni spiritualis, iustitiae; *fletus,* prudentiae; *passio malorum,* fortitudini sive patientiae, sicut dicit Glossa Bedae.

63. In his tamen quatuor aliae continentur *implicite;* unde non ita exprimit Lucas spiritualem intellectum in huiusmodi promissionibus, ut Matthaeus. Matthaeus enim dicit[^p152-11]: « Beati pauperes spiritu »; hic solum: « Beati pauperes », ille: « Beati qui esuriunt et sitiunt iustitiam » etc. Et iterum in Matthaeo proponuntur pluraliter et in tertia persona, quia loquitur ad *intelligentes;* in Luca particulariter et in secunda persona, quia loquitur ad *sensuales*[^p152-12]. — Ex his colligi possunt rationes *aliarum* diversitatum, quia pro diversitate audientium diversimodus est sermo doctorum, secundum illud Apostoli ad Colossenses quarto[^p152-13]: « Sermo vester semper sit in gratia sale conditus, ut sciatis, quomodo vos oporteat unicuique respondere ». Et hoc faciebat ille doctor gentium primae ad Corinthios secundo: « Ego cum venissem ad vos, veni non in sublimitate sermonis » etc., et post sequitur: « Sapientiam autem loquimur inter perfectos ». Sic faciebat Salvator, qui turbis loquebatur in parabolis, seorsum autem discipulis suis disserebat omnia, sicut dicitur Matthaei decimo tertio[^p152-14], quia eis datum erat « nosse mysteria regni caelorum ».

49. *And he, lifting up his eyes* etc. After the election of the disciples and the attraction of the hearers, here the *explication of the lessons* is added. Now this part is threefold according to three things, the knowledge of which is necessary for salvation, namely of *rewards, of penalties, of precepts.* The first part, then, is of *rewards,* the second of *penalties,* at[^p147-10]: *But woe to you that are rich!* the third of *precepts,* at: *But I say to you that hear* etc. The first part contains the promises of rewards; the second, the threatenings of punishments or penalties; but the third, the laws of the precepts. In the rewards promised we are taught what is *to be hoped for;* in the penalties threatened, what is *to be feared;* but in the precepts, what is *to be done.*

Now concerning the *promise* of the *beatitudes* note that he promises four things, namely the *kingdom,* which consists in participation in the divine power; *satiety,* which consists in the taste of the divine goodness; *laughter,* which consists in the contemplation of the divine truth; and *much reward,* which consists in the duration of eternity. And these four are designated in Ephesians, the third chapter[^p147-11]: « That you may be able, he says, to comprehend with all the saints what is the *length,* the *breadth,* the *height* and the *depth* »: the *length* of eternity, the *breadth* of goodness, the *height* of power, and the *depth* of wisdom or truth. Now these four he promises to four kinds of merits which raise us up to God and set right our free choice. For the *kingdom of opulence* is promised to those who despise temporal goods, of which sort are the *poor;* the *feast of abundance* to those who desire spiritual things, and these are called the *hungry;* the *laughter of joyfulness* to those who detest
<!-- page 148 -->
the evils of fault, and these are called *those that weep;* the *multitude of reward* to those who bear the evils of punishment, and these are *those who suffer.* In these four is enclosed the perfection[^p148-1] of the human will with respect to a twofold good and with respect to a twofold evil.

50. (Vers. 20.). First, then, he promises to the *poor the kingdom of opulence* on account of the *contempt of temporal things,* and such were the disciples, and therefore he says: *And he, lifting up his eyes on his disciples, said: Blessed are ye poor* etc. *Lifting up his eyes* he calls them blessed, because[^p148-2] « the eyes of the Lord are upon the just », and most of all upon the poor; Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter: « There is a man that is inactive and wanteth recovery, very weak in ability and abounding in poverty, and the eye of the Lord hath looked upon him for good ». Now he calls these poor *blessed* for this reason, that they are prepared for beatitude. — And therefore he adds: *For*[^p148-3] *yours is the kingdom of God;* which indeed is very great. For in the Psalm: « Thy kingdom, O Lord, is a kingdom of all ages ». He says that it *is of the poor,* because the poor are its heirs, according to that of James, the second chapter: « Hath not God chosen the poor in this world, rich in faith, and heirs of the kingdom which God hath promised to them that love him »? « Better therefore is the poor man, that walketh in his simplicity, than a rich man that is perverse in his lips, and unwise », Proverbs, the nineteenth chapter.

51. And note that he says *is* in the present, either on account of the certainty of the promise, or also because the truly poor now in a certain manner begin to be *kings,* according to that of the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter[^p148-4]: « As having nothing, and possessing all things ». « For their very deep poverty hath abounded unto the riches of their simplicity », as is said in the second to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter. Whence the gospel or voluntary poor are already as it were happy, not to be reckoned wretched. Seneca[^p148-5]: « No one is born rich: whoever comes out into the light is bidden to be content with bread and milk ». « No one is poor as regards these things; and if anyone has confined his desire within them, he will be able to contend with Jove about happiness ».

And note that he begins from *poverty,* because, as Ambrose says there[^p148-6], « poverty is the first parent of the virtues, because he who has despised worldly things will merit eternal ones », an emulator of that poor man « who, being rich, became needy for us », the second to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter.

52. (Vers. 21.). Secondly he promises to the *hungry the feast of abundance* on account of the *desire of spiritual things;* and this he notes when he adds: *Blessed are ye that hunger now,* namely for the food of justice, according to what is said in the Gloss on Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p148-7]. *For you shall be filled,* by the feast of abundance. Ambrose: « He who hungers seeks an increase of strength », according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter: « They that eat me shall yet hunger, and they that drink me shall yet thirst ». They hunger for justice who can say with Jesus that of John, the fourth chapter[^p148-8]: « My meat is to do the will of my Father ». They also hunger for justice who for love of justice willingly suffer hunger; and these can say with the Apostle that of the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « Even unto this hour we both hunger and thirst ». Such were those holy Fathers of whom it is said in Hebrews, the eleventh chapter, that « they went about in sheepskins, being needy, distressed, afflicted » etc. Such shall be filled by the Lord, because, Apocalypse, the seventh chapter[^p148-9], « they shall no more hunger nor thirst, neither shall the sun fall on them, nor any heat, for the Lamb, which is in the midst of the throne, shall rule them ». And this will be in the divine glory, according to what is said in the Psalm: « I shall be satisfied when thy glory shall appear »; and again: « They shall be inebriated with the plenty of thy house, and thou shalt make them drink of the torrent of thy pleasure ». And so is fulfilled that of the first of Kings, the second chapter[^p148-10]: « The hungry are filled ». Nor is it a wonder, because, Isaias, the twenty-fifth chapter, « the Lord of hosts shall make unto all peoples in this mountain a feast of vintage, of fat things full of marrow, of vintage purified from the lees », and again, according to that of the Psalm: « He fed them with the fat of wheat, and filled them with honey out of the rock ».
<!-- page 149 -->

53. Thirdly he promises to *those that weep the laughter of joyfulness* on account of *penance for the evil of fault,* when he adjoins: *Blessed are ye that weep now,* namely for *evils committed,* as Ezechias, « having turned to the wall », « wept with great weeping », as is said in Isaias, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p149-1]; as also Peter, of whom it is said in Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter, after the denial, that « going forth, he wept bitterly »; and Job, the thirtieth chapter: « My harp is turned to mourning, and my organ into the voice of those that weep ». — Or for *the sins of others,* as David over Saul, the second of Kings, the first chapter[^p149-2]; so Christ over Jerusalem, below, the nineteenth chapter: « Seeing the city, he wept over it ». — Or: *weep now,* namely for *the miseries of others;* Job, the thirtieth chapter: « I wept heretofore for him that was afflicted »; to the Romans, the twelfth chapter: « Weep with them that weep ». — Or: *Ye that weep now,* for *escaping the temptations* of sins and their consequences; in Judges, the second chapter[^p149-3], it is said that « when the Angel of the Lord spoke to the children of Israel, they lifted up their voice and wept. And the name of that place was called the place of weepers or of tears ». And this place is the state of the present life, which lasts only up to *now.* And therefore he says: *Ye that weep now;* the first of Peter, the first chapter[^p149-4]: « If now for a little you must be made sorrowful in divers temptations, that the trial of your faith, much more precious than gold which is tried by the fire, may be found unto praise » etc. — Or: *Ye that weep now,* namely for *desire of eternal things,* and this is the proper understanding; John, the sixteenth chapter[^p149-5]: « You shall lament and weep, but the world shall rejoice, and you shall be made sorrowful; but your sorrow shall be turned into joy »; and the Psalm: « My tears have been my bread day and night, whilst it is said to me daily: Where is thy God »? To these he says: you are blessed, *for you shall laugh,* with spiritual laughter, which consists in the joyfulness of the mind on account of the contemplation of truth; Job, the eighth chapter[^p149-6]: « Thy mouth shall be filled with laughter, and thy lips with rejoicing »; for it is said in Proverbs, the last chapter: « Strength and beauty are her clothing, and she shall laugh in the latter day ». This laughter will be over goods of one's own and of others, according to what is said of Sara in Genesis, the twenty-first chapter: « The Lord hath made laughter for me; whosoever shall hear of it will laugh with me ».

54. (Vers. 22.). But fourthly he promises to *those who suffer the greatness of reward* on account of *endurance in the evil of punishment,* when he adds: *Blessed shall you be when men shall hate you,* namely as regards the *act of the heart.* Excellently does he say *men,* that is, animal and bestial, because[^p149-7] « man, when he was in honour, did not understand; he is compared to senseless beasts, and is become like to them »; the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter: « For whereas there is among you envying and contention, are you not carnal, and walk according to man »? according, namely, to that man of whom it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-eighth chapter: « Man to man reserveth anger ». These hate the servants of Christ; Proverbs, the nineteenth chapter[^p149-8]: « The brethren of the poor man hate him »; and Matthew, the tenth chapter: « You shall be hated by all men for my name's sake ». — As regards the *act of deed* he adds: *And when they shall separate you,* namely by casting you out of the synagogues, according to that of John, the ninth chapter[^p149-9]: « The Jews had already agreed among themselves that, if any man should confess him to be Christ, he should be put out of the synagogue », and so in reproach he would be separated from others, even from those of his own household, as the Lord preached would come upon his disciples; Matthew, the tenth chapter: « I came to separate a man against his father »; and afterwards it is added: « And a man's enemies shall be they of his own household ». — As regards the *act of the mouth* he subjoins: *And shall reproach you,* by insulting you, *and cast out your name as evil,* by detracting; the first of Peter, the fourth chapter[^p149-10]: « If you be reproached for the name of Christ, you shall be blessed »; James, the second chapter: « They blaspheme the good name that is invoked upon you », because they call *good* evil, according to that of Matthew, the fifth chapter: « They have spoken all that is evil against you, untruly, for my sake ». — And since not any punishment whatever makes a Martyr unless the due cause be present, therefore he adds: *For the Son of man's sake;* the first of Peter, the third chapter[^p149-11]: « If you suffer anything for justice' sake, you shall be blessed », that is, for the sake of Christ, « who of God is made wisdom and justice and sanctification ». And by this threefold justice Paul wished to suffer; Acts, the twenty-first chapter: « I am ready not only to be bound, but to die also in Jerusalem for Christ Jesus ».

55. (Vers. 23.). Thus he gladdens those who suffer by the reward, when he adds: *Be glad*[^p149-12] *and exult; for behold,*
<!-- page 150 -->
*your reward is much in heaven; be glad,* namely by reason of the retribution; to the Romans, the twelfth chapter: « Rejoicing in hope, patient in tribulation ». Which was fulfilled in Acts, the fifth chapter: « The Apostles went rejoicing from the presence of the council, because they were accounted worthy to suffer reproach for the name of Jesus ». Now this the consideration of the reward brings about; Jeremias, the thirty-first chapter[^p150-1]: « Let thy voice cease from weeping, and thy eyes from tears, for there is a reward for thy work ». A reward, I say, that is *much,* because « much peace have they that love thy law » etc., as is said in the Psalm; and *perpetual,* because *in heaven;* Wisdom, the fifth chapter: « But the just shall live for evermore, and their reward is with the Lord ». And this is what Bede says[^p150-2]: « Amid the hatreds of hearts, amid the reproaches of tongues, amid the hands of persecutors, go about with a gladder heart in view of the heavenly reward ». — And since this work is most arduous, namely to be glad in tribulations, therefore he not only pledges the reward, but adjoins an example, when he adds:

56. *For according to this*[^p150-3] *did their fathers to the Prophets;* according to what is said in Matthew, the twenty-third chapter: « You are witnesses against yourselves, that you are the sons of them that killed the Prophets »; and Acts, the seventh chapter: « With stiff neck and uncircumcised hearts and ears, you always resisted the Holy Spirit. Which of the Prophets have not your fathers persecuted? And they slew them who prophesied of the coming of the Just One ». These he puts forward as an example on account of their constancy; James, the last chapter[^p150-4]: « Take, brethren, for an example of patience, of labour and of longsuffering, the Prophets »; who were not moved from the truth on account of scourges. Whence it is said of Eliseus in Ecclesiasticus, the forty-eighth chapter: « In his days he feared not the prince, and no one overcame him in power, nor did man overpower him ». All these evils the Prophets bore for the sake of the reward which they promise to others; Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-sixth chapter[^p150-5]: « Give a reward, O Lord, to them that patiently wait for thee, that thy Prophets may be found faithful ». Whence that eminent Prophet John, after all the tribulations of the Church, adds in the Apocalypse, the twenty-second chapter, at the end: « Behold, I come quickly, and my reward is with me, to render to every man according to his works ».

57. Whence note that divine Scripture indicates to us a difference of *rewards* according to the diversities of *merits.* Whence there is found a *safe* reward, a *great, worthy, full, opportune*[^p150-6], *speedy, faithful, plentiful, perpetual* reward. — The *safe* reward is for the Angels who minister to us; the *great* is given to prelates or those who preside; the *worthy,* to those who obey; the *full,* to those who contemplate; the *timely* is given to those who work; the *speedy,* to those who make progress; the *faithful,* to those who perfect; the *much* or plentiful, to those who suffer; but the *perpetual,* to those who persevere.

Whence of the *safe reward of the Angels,* because the ministry which they have towards us is not defrauded of its reward, it is said in Tobias, the fifth chapter[^p150-7]: « Seek some faithful man to go with thee, his reward kept safe », says Tobias. — Of the *great reward of those who preside* it is said in Genesis, the fifteenth chapter, to Abraham, who bears the type of prelates: « I am thy protector, and thy reward exceeding great ». — Of the *worthy reward of those who obey* it is said in Esther, the sixteenth chapter: « That all who faithfully obey the Persians may know that they receive a worthy reward for their fidelity ». — Of the *full reward of those who contemplate* it is said to Ruth, in the second chapter, who bears the type of the contemplative, who leave all things for God's sake, and who is also interpreted *sheep,* or *seeing*[^p150-8], so that the contemplative soul may be called a *sheep* on account of the meekness of its affection, but *seeing* on account of the illumination of its intellect; whence Booz says to her: « Thou shalt receive a full reward of the Lord the God of Israel, to whom thou art come, and under whose wings thou art fled ». — Of the *timely reward of those who work* it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the last chapter[^p150-9]: « Work your work while there is time, and he will give you your reward in his time ». — Of the *speedy reward of those who make progress* it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter: « The blessing of God maketh haste unto the reward of the just ». — Of the *faithful reward of the perfect,* as of preachers or such, it is said in Proverbs, the eleventh chapter[^p150-10]: « To him that soweth justice, a faithful reward ». — Of the *plentiful reward of those who suffer* or who do battle it is said here and in Matthew, the fifth chapter: « For behold, your reward is plentiful in heaven ». — But of the *perpetual reward,* which befits *those who persevere,* Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter[^p150-11]: « Be not afraid to be justified even to death, for the reward of God continueth for ever ». — Thus therefore according to the diversities of merits the differences of rewards are appropriated; and so « God will render to the just the reward of their labours », Wisdom, the tenth chapter.
<!-- page 151 -->

58. (Vers. 24.). *But woe to you that are rich!* After the promises of the beatitudes he subjoins the *threatenings of penalties.* And since evil is set against good[^p151-1], he puts four threatenings answering to the four beatitudes, namely *desolation* in the loss of temporal good, *hunger* in the loss of spiritual good, *weeping* in the affliction of bodily evil, *confusion* in the suffering of everlasting reproach. And these are the four *woes* which he threatens against four kinds of men opposed to the evangelical men set out before, namely the *covetous* and the *gluttonous,* the *wanton* and the *vainglorious*[^p151-2].

Against the *covetous,* then, he threatens the *woe of everlasting desolation,* when he says: *But woe to you that are rich! Adversatively* he says *that are rich,* that is, loving riches, because such men gape after riches to be heaped up; Habacuc, the second chapter[^p151-3]: « Woe to him that heapeth together that which is not his own »! that is, earthly goods, which are not ours, because they neither come with us nor depart with us; yet earthly men reckon that they are their own; Apocalypse, the eighth chapter: « Woe, woe, woe to the inhabitants of the earth »! Such are the covetous, against whom the Lord says in Matthew, the sixth chapter: « Lay not up to yourselves treasures on earth, where the rust and moth consume ». — Since upon the abundance of these riches *want* must follow, and upon consolation *desolation* must follow; therefore, adding the reason, he says: *Who*[^p151-4] *have your consolation; you have,* namely in the present, but not in the future; whence James, the fifth chapter: « Go to now, ye rich men, weep and howl in your miseries, which shall come upon you ». Therefore this consolation is vain and false, because it brings on desolation; Zacharias, the tenth chapter[^p151-5]: « The dreamers have spoken in vain, and comforted vainly ». Truly *in vain,* because « they have slept their sleep, the men of riches, and have found nothing in their hands ». And therefore one must say with the Prophet David[^p151-6]: « My soul refused to be comforted; I remembered God and was delighted »; Job, the sixth chapter: « Let this be my consolation, that afflicting me with sorrow, he spare not »; so that through the desolation of the way one may come to the consolation of the fatherland, of which Isaias, the last chapter: « As if a mother caress one, so will I console you ». This we ought to await, not to anticipate; Judith, the eighth chapter: « Let us humbly wait for his consolation ».

59. (Vers. 25.). Against the *gluttonous* he threatens the *woe of perpetual famine,* when he adds: *Woe to you that are filled!* being free, namely, for[^tr-vacare] surfeiting and drunkenness; Proverbs, the twenty-third chapter[^p151-7]: « Who hath woe? whose father hath woe? who hath brawls, and wounds without cause? Is it not they that tarry long at wine and study to drink off their cups »? « Woe to you that are mighty to drink wine, and stout men at drunkenness »! And therefore it is added: *For you shall hunger.* This satiety brings on perpetual famine, according to that of Job, the twentieth chapter[^p151-8]: « When he shall be filled, he shall be straitened, he shall burn, and every sorrow shall fall upon him »; and Isaias, the sixty-fifth chapter: « Behold, my servants shall eat, and you shall be hungry; behold, my servants shall drink, and you shall be thirsty ». Whence of the wicked in the Psalm: « They did eat and were filled » etc.

60. Against the *wanton* he threatens the *woe of everlasting weeping,* when he says: *Woe to you that laugh now! for you shall mourn and weep,* that is, in this moment of time; Job, the twentieth chapter[^p151-9]: « The joy of the hypocrite is but as a point »; and Proverbs, the fourteenth chapter: « Laughter shall be mingled with sorrow, and mourning taketh hold of the end of joy ». It is certainly better that weeping should go before laughter than the reverse; and therefore in Ecclesiastes, the third chapter[^p151-10]: « A time to weep », namely first in time, « and a time to laugh », afterwards in eternity. But the fool will not now refrain from laughter; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-first chapter: « A fool lifteth up his voice in laughter, but a wise man will scarce laugh low to himself »; nay, Ecclesiastes, the second chapter: « Laughter I counted error, and to mirth I said: Why art thou vainly deceived »? Fools therefore are they who laugh now, and wise they who mourn, according to that of Ecclesiastes, the seventh chapter[^p151-11]: « The heart of the wise is where there is mourning, and the heart of fools where there is mirth ». On account of which it is said to the foolish and worldly in Isaias, the sixty-fifth chapter: « Behold, my servants shall praise for exultation of heart, and you shall cry for sorrow of heart, and shall howl for grief of spirit », namely in hell, because it is said in Matthew, the thirteenth chapter: « There shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth ».

61. (Vers. 26.). But against the *vainglorious* he threatens the *woe of everlasting contempt,* when he adds: *Woe to you, when all*[^p151-12] *men shall bless you!* by extolling you, namely, with vain praises; Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter: « He that blesseth his neighbour with a loud voice,
<!-- page 152 -->
rising in the night, shall be like to him that curseth »; because, as is said in Isaias, the ninth chapter, « they that call this people blessed shall be seducers, and they that are called blessed are cast down headlong ». For they are cast down headlong from vain praises into everlasting reproach, while they are lifted up by them, according to that of Job, the thirtieth chapter[^p152-1]: « Thou hast lifted me up, as it were setting me upon the wind », namely of vain praise, « thou hast mightily dashed me ». — And since it is difficult to avoid praises of this kind, he subjoins an example: *For according to this*[^p152-2] *did their fathers to the prophets;* to prophets, I say, not true, but false; whence the Gloss: « They blessed the false prophets, who prophesied out of their own heart to catch the favour of the crowd »; Jeremias, the fifth chapter: « Astonishment and marvellous things have been done in the land: the prophets prophesied falsehood, and the priests clapped their hands, and my people loved such things ». In this, therefore, that he shows that evil men were praised by prophets, he shows that popular favour is to be wholly despised; the Psalm[^p152-3]: « God hath scattered the bones of them that please men; they have been confounded, because God hath despised them ». Now this is said not of those who show good and praiseworthy examples to the peoples for the glorifying of God, since it is said in Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p152-4]: « Let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works and glorify your Father who is in heaven »; but of those who seek their own glory in pleasing men and in their praises; to the Galatians, the first chapter: « If I yet pleased men, I should not be the servant of Christ ».

62. Note here concerning the diversity between Matthew[^p152-5] and Luke: that Luke puts *promises* and *threatenings of woe,* but Matthew only *promises.* On account of which it seems either that the one spoke defectively or the other superfluously. — But certainly it can be answered, according to what the Gloss[^p152-6] hints and Augustine says, that this sermon and that one were different. Their diversity is gathered from the *places* and the *persons* and the *precepts.* For that one was to the *disciples,* this one to the *multitudes;* that one *sitting,* this one *standing;* that one *on the mountain,* this one *in a plain place;* that one *contains more lessons* than this one, because *seven* beatitudes[^p152-7], this one *four;* from all of which it is gathered that the one was other than the other. — And not without reason, because to different persons one must speak in different ways. For to the disciples, as the more perfect, the gospel commandments had to be explicated in a more perfect manner; but the multitudes cannot climb up to lofty things: therefore the word of God is not explicated so perfectly to the multitudes as to the Apostles. — And since the perfect are moved by love, but the imperfect not only by love but by fear[^p152-8]; therefore in the sermon to the Apostles *beatitudes* are put and not *woes,* in the sermon to the multitudes both *beatitudes* and *woes.* — Again, since the perfect are made conformed to Christ through the sevenfold Spirit, which rested upon him, as is said in Isaias, the eleventh chapter[^p152-9]; therefore seven beatitudes are put, agreeing with the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit. Here four beatitudes are noted, answering to the four cardinal virtues, as is had in the Gloss[^p152-10]: *Poverty,* to temperance; *hunger* for spiritual good, to justice; *weeping,* to prudence; *the suffering of evils,* to fortitude or patience, as the Gloss of Bede says.

63. In these four, however, the others are contained *implicitly;* whence Luke does not express the spiritual understanding in such promises as Matthew does. For Matthew says[^p152-11]: « Blessed are the poor in spirit »; this one only: « Blessed are the poor »; that one: « Blessed are they that hunger and thirst after justice » etc. And again, in Matthew they are put forward in the plural and in the third person, because he speaks to the *intelligent;* in Luke in particular and in the second person, because he speaks to the *sensual*[^p152-12]. — From these the reasons of the *other* diversities can be gathered, because according to the diversity of the hearers the sermon of the doctors is of divers modes, according to that of the Apostle to the Colossians, the fourth chapter[^p152-13]: « Let your speech be always in grace seasoned with salt, that you may know how you ought to answer every man ». And this that doctor of the Gentiles did, the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter: « I, when I came to you, came not in loftiness of speech » etc., and afterwards there follows: « But we speak wisdom among the perfect ». So did the Saviour, who spoke to the multitudes in parables, but apart to his disciples he explained all things, as is said in Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p152-14], because to them it was given « to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven ».
