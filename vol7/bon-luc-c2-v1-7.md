# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. II, Vers. 1-7
## *Qualiter Christi nativitas sit consummata*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 43 -->

### Capitulum II.

#### *Secundo, de nativitate ex utero dupliciter.*

1. *Factum est autem in diebus illis* etc. Terminata parte illa, in qua agitur de conceptu Salvatoris in utero, sequitur pars secunda, in qua agitur de *ortu Salvatoris ex utero,* et hoc facit in isto totali capitulo[^p43-8]. Habet autem haec pars duas, in quarum prima determinatur *nativitatis Christi veritas;* in secunda determinatur *nascentis humilitas.* Quia enim Christus in nativitate sua *factus est ex*
<!-- page 44 -->
*muliere,* et *factus est sub Lege*[^p44-1] per humilitatem; ideo utrumque pertinet ad Evangelistam determinare; et incipit secunda pars ibi: *Et postquam consummati sunt dies octo* etc.

#### Primo, de nativitatis Christi veritate ostenduntur tria.

Prima pars habet tres partes, in quarum prima ostenditur, qualiter Christi nativitas fuit *consummata;* in secunda, qualiter *ab Angelis denuntiata,* ibi[^p44-2]: *Et pastores erant in regione;* in tertia, qualiter *per pastores manifestata et divulgata,* ibi: *Et factum est, ut discesserunt* etc.

#### Qualiter Christi nativitas sit consummata.

Prima pars habet tres, quoniam ad nativitatem Salvatoris exprimendam oportet assignare *tempus* et *locum* et *pariendi modum.* Describit ergo in prima parte Evangelista *tempus debitum;* in secunda, *locum idoneum,* ibi[^p44-3]: *Ascendit autem et Ioseph a Galilaea;* in tertia vero, *Virginis partum,* ibi: *Factum est autem, cum essent ibi.*

2. *Tempus* autem *debitum* nativitati Christi fuit tempus universalis imperii describendi, « quia, ut dicit Gregorius[^p44-4], ille veniebat in carne, qui electos suos ascriberet in aeternitate ». In hac descriptione universalis imperii triplex notatur excellentia, scilicet *potentiae in praecipiendo, sapientiae in discernendo* et *iustitiae in prosequendo,* quoniam haec tria futura erant in Christo, secundum illud Ieremiae vigesimo tertio[^p44-5]: « Ecce, dies veniunt, dicit Dominus, et suscitabo David germen iustum, et regnabit rex », ecce, *potentia;* « et sapiens erit », ecce, *sapientia;* « et faciet iudicium et iustitiam », ecce, *iustitia,* ut sit in eo omnimoda perfectio.

3. (Vers. 1.). Quantum ad excellentiam *potentiae in praecipiendo* dicit: *Exiit edictum a Caesare Augusto, ut describeretur universus orbis.* Caesar iste Augustus proprio nomine fuit Octavianus, sed a Iulio Caesare, qui antea imperavit, *Caesar* est cognominatus, a quo omnes imperatores Romani postea *caesares* dicti sunt. Iste etiam dictus est Augustus, eo quod rem publicam auxit; et ab isto omnes alii, semper dicuntur *Augusti*[^p44-6]. Et iste protulit universale edictum, quia universale habebat dominium, non in omnes et singulos, sed in principales universi partes; unde imperium suum fuit magnum, secundum illud Danielis secundo[^p44-7]: « Et regnum quartum erit ferreum. Quomodo ferrum comminuit et domat omnia, sic comminuet et conteret omnia haec ». Hoc fuit Romanorum imperium, quod designatum fuit per imperium Nabuchodonosor, de quo Ieremiae vigesimo septimo: « Ego dedi omnes terras has in manu Nabuchodonosor, et servient ei omnes gentes. Gens et regnum, quod non servierit Nabuchodonosor, visitabo super gentem illam, dicit Dominus ». In his diebus, huius scilicet regni, erat tempus adventus Christi, quia dicitur Danielis secundo[^p44-8]: « In diebus regnorum illorum suscitabit Dominus regnum, quod in aeternum non dissipabitur, et regnum eius populo alteri non tradetur ». Et istius solius est plena orbis descriptio, secundum illud Psalmi: « Domini est terra et plenitudo eius, orbis terrarum et universi, qui habitant in eo ». Et ideo dicitur Apocalypsis decimo nono, quod « habebat in vestimento et in femore suo scriptum: Rex regum et Dominus dominantium »; quia, sicut dicitur Ecclesiastae quinto, « excelso alius excelsior est, et super hos quoque eminentiores sunt alii ».

4. (Vers. 2.). Quantum ad excellentiam *sapientiae in discernendo* subdit: *Haec descriptio prima facta est a praeside Syriae Cyrino;* quia haec fuit, ut dicit Ambrosius[^p44-9], *prima* descriptio generalis, quae inchoata est a Cyrino; vel: *prima facta,* id est primo inchoata, secundum Bedam, ab illo qui praesidebat in Syria, quae est umbilicus terrae habita-
<!-- page 45 -->
bilis; et sic fiebat dicta descriptio *distincte* et *ordinate,* et sunt duo indicia sapientiae in principe, secundum quod dicitur de Salomone tertii Regum decimo: « Videns regina Saba omnem sapientiam Salomonis et habitacula servorum et ordines ministrantium, non habebat ultra spiritum ». Haec autem descriptio facta est non propter vanitatem, sicut illa David, secundi Regum ultimo[^p45-1], propter quam percussus est populus, sed propter imperii utilitatem, ut sciretur quantitas tributi, et hoc est discretionis, non elationis; Ecclesiastici quadragesimo secundo: « Ubi multae sunt manus, claude; et quodcumque trades, numera et appende; datum vero et acceptum omne describe ». Sed non est cuiuscumque singulorum regum tributa describere, sed Christi nascentis; unde Psalmus: « Et in libro tuo omnes scribentur; dies formabuntur, et nemo in eis ».

5. (Vers. 3.). Quantum ad excellentiam *iustitiae in prosequendo* subditur: *Et ibant omnes, ut profiterentur, singuli in suam civitatem.* Faciebant ergo regis imperium, et nullus occupabat alienum; et ita *subiectionem* habebant ad regem in professione, et *pacem* ad invicem in quieta et *iusta* cohabitatione,
secundum illud ad Romanos decimo tertio[^p45-2]: « Reddite omnibus debita: cui tributum, tributum; cui honorem, honorem; nemini quidquam debeatis, nisi ut invicem diligatis ». *Ut profiterentur* quidem, se subiectos esse Romano imperio, et hoc reddendo censum. Et illud erat iustum, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p45-3]: « Reddite quae sunt caesaris caesari, et quae sunt Dei Deo ». Et quia Iudaea iam facta erat tributaria, significatur, quod tempus advenerat nativitatis Christi, secundum illud Genesis penultimo: « Non auferetur sceptrum de Iuda et dux de femore eius, donec veniat qui mittendus est, et ipse erit exspectatio gentium ». *Ibant,* etiam nullo praestante impedimento, quo ostenditur, quod istud imperium erat pacatissimum; et ideo tempus erat, ut veniret Christus, pacis annuntiator, de quo ad Ephesios secundo[^p45-4]: « Ipse est pax nostra, qui fecit utraque unum ». Et ita iam completum erat illud Michaeae quarto: « Non sumet gens contra gentem gladium, et non discent ultra belligerare; et omnes populi ambulabunt, unusquisque in nomine Dei sui ». Et hoc est quod dicit Beda in Glossa[^p45-5]: « Pacatissimo tempore voluit nasci Christus, quia pacem magnopere quaesivit et amavit et amatores pacis semper invisit ».

6. (Vers. 4.). *Ascendit autem et Ioseph* etc. Postquam descripsit tempus debitum, describit hic *locum idoneum,* et hoc, quia locus ille conveniens erat *prophetali vaticinio,* conveniens erat *divino promisso,* conveniens etiam *imperiali edicto.*

Ostenditur ergo *conveniens prophetali vaticinio* in hoc quod dicit: *Ascendit autem et Ioseph a Galilaea de civitate Nazareth;* quia secundum unam prophetiam Nazareth, « ubi fuerat Christus conceptus », competebat *conceptioni;* Isaiae undecimo[^p45-6], secundum aliam litteram: « Nazaraeus de radice eius ascendet »; secundum aliam vero Bethlehem, *nativitati.* Ideo subdit: *In Iudaeam, in civitatem David, quae vocatur Bethlehem;* Michaeae quinto[^p45-7]: « Et tu, Bethlehem Ephrata, parvulus es in millibus Iuda; ex te enim egredietur, qui sit dominator in Israel; et egressus eius ab initio a diebus aeternitatis ». Unde dicitur Matthaei secundo, quod Herodi quaerenti, ubi Christus nasceretur, responderunt scribae: « In Bethlehem Iudae, sic enim scriptum est per Prophetam: Et tu Bethlehem, terra Iuda, nequaquam minima es
in principibus Iuda. Ex te enim exiet dux, qui regat populum meum ». Haec fuit civitas David, quia inde sumsit originem; primi Regum decimo sexto: « Dixit Dominus ad Samuelem: Veni, mittam te ad Isai Bethlehemitem; providi enim in filiis eius mihi in regem ». — Et nota, quod alibi natus est Christus, alibi conceptus, alibi educatus, ut Herodem et alios impios lateret incarnationis mysterium; unde Ioannis septimo[^p45-8]: « Nunquid a Galilaea venit Christus? Nonne de Bethlehem castello »?

7. Ostenditur etiam locus ille *conveniens divino promisso,* cum subditur: *Eo quod esset de domo et familia David,* ipse scilicet et sponsa sua virgo Maria. Unde dicit Bernardus[^p45-9], quod « in altera completa est promissio facta David, altero vero tantum teste et conscio ». Haec promissio facta est David, secundi Regum septimo, per Nathan: « Praedicit tibi Dominus, quod domum faciat tibi Dominus; cumque completi fuerint dies tui, suscitabo semen tuum post te et stabiliam thronum regni eius usque in sempiternum »; et rursus in Psalmo: « Iuravit Dominus David veritatem et non frustrabitur eam: de fructu ventris tui ponam super sedem tuam ».
<!-- page 46 -->

8. (Vers. 5.). Ostenditur postremo ut *conveniens imperiali edicto,* cum subditur: *Ut profiteretur cum Maria desponsata sibi uxore praegnante.* Hoc enim processerat ex imperiali edicto. Et licet ante iam concepisset Virgo Regem caeli et terrae, tamen imperiali edicto etiam praegnans voluit obedire, ut posset cum Filio dicere illud Matthaei tertio[^p46-1]: « Sic decet nos implere omnem iustitiam »; et per hoc exemplum daret nobis obtemperandi omni potestati superiori, secundum illud primae Petri secundo: « Subiecti estote omni humanae creaturae propter Dominum, sive regi tanquam praecellenti, sive ducibus tanquam ab eo missis ad vindictam malefactorum, laudem vero bonorum ». — In hoc autem apparet, quomodo humanum imperium famulatur divino. Praecipiente enim caesare, ut omnes in civitatem suam irent, et obedientibus[^p46-2] eius imperio Maria et Ioseph, factum est, quod neuter intendebat, scilicet quod Christus nasceretur in Bethlehem, secundum quod erat aeternaliter dispositum et temporaliter promissum et vaticinatum et prophetatum. Et ideo non est contemnendum aliquo modo mandatum praepositorum.

9. (Vers. 6.). *Factum est autem, cum essent ibi* etc. Descripsit supra Christi nativitatem quantum ad tempus debitum et locum idoneum, hic tertio describit quantum ad *partum,* quem describit quantum ad tria, scilicet quantum ad *opportunitatem pariendi, novitatem partus* et *paupertatem parientis;* ex quibus ostenditur ortus Christi admirabilis et inimitabilis[^p46-3] et commendabilis.

Primo igitur insinuat *opportunitatem pariendi,* cum dicit: *Factum est, cum essent ibi, impleti sunt dies* Mariae, *ut pareret,* iuxta quod dictum est supra de eius cognata, proximo capitulo[^p46-4]: « Elisabeth impletum est tempus pariendi ». Vere *impletum,* quia in plenitudine temporis fuit Christus conceptus et natus; ad Galatas quarto: « At ubi venit plenitudo temporis, misit Deus Filium suum factum ex muliere »; Psalmus: « Dies pleni invenientur in eis ».

10. (Vers. 7.). Insinuat etiam *novitatem partus* in hoc quod dicit: *Et peperit filium suum primogenitum.* Hoc autem dicit per privationem prioris; quia enim Virgo ipsum conceperat, ideo hic primogenitus eius erat; Isaiae septimo[^p46-5]: « Ecce, Virgo concipiet et pariet filium »; et ideo partus novus fuit, sicut et conceptus, quia, sicut conceptus sine pudore, sic partus sine dolore; Isaiae ultimo: « Antequam parturiret, peperit; antequam veniret partus eius, peperit masculum. Quis audivit unquam tale? Aut quis vidit huic simile »? et postea: « Nunquid ego, qui alios parere facio, ipse non pariam, dicit Dominus? Si ego, qui generationem ceteris tribuo, sterilis ero, ait Dominus Deus »? Talis partus decebat Christum, de quo ad Colossenses primo[^p46-6]: « In ipso habemus redemptionem et remissionem peccatorum, qui est imago Dei invisibilis, primogenitus omnis creaturae »; unde, sicut est primogenitus Patris, ita etiam primogenitus Matris; sicut etiam unigenitus Patris, ita unigenitus Matris; Ioannis primo: « Vidimus gloriam eius, gloriam quasi Unigeniti a Patre » etc. Unde non dicit hic *primogenitus* ordinem respectu posterioris, sicut dicebat Helvidius haereticus, sed privationem respectu prioris, quia nullum habuit ante illum, ut ostendatur, quod ipse
Domino totaliter erat dedicandus. Primogenita enim omnia erant Deo offerenda; Exodi decimo tertio[^p46-7]: « Sanctifica mihi omne primogenitum, quod aperit vulvam in filiis Israel, tam de hominibus quam de iumentis; mea enim sunt omnia ».

11. Insinuat autem tertio *paupertatem parientis,* quia egens *operimenti, lecti* et *hospitii.* — Propter defectum *operimenti* dicit: *Et pannis eum involvit,* non una veste, sed multis, ut pannosus pauper posset dici et exemplo monstraret illud Apostoli primae ad Timotheum ultimo[^p46-8]: « Habentes alimenta et quibus tegamur, his contenti simus ». Et hoc correspondet illi prophetiae, Zachariae tertio, ubi dicitur, quod « Iesus, sacerdos magnus, erat indutus sordidis vestibus ». Bernardus[^p46-9]: « Agnoscas Iesum sacerdotem magnum, sordidis indutum vestibus, dum altercaretur cum diabolo. At ubi exaltatum est caput nostrum super inimicos nostros, mutavit vestem, de-
<!-- page 47 -->
corem induit, *amictus lumine sicut vestimento.* Utilior siquidem est in conflictu lorica ferrea quam stola linea, licet illa sit oneri, haec honori ». — Et in hoc reprehenditur *cultus vestium* pretiosarum; propter quod dicitur Matthaei undecimo[^p47-1]: « Qui mollibus induuntur in domibus regum sunt »; et Ecclesiastici undecimo: « In vestitu ne glorieris unquam ».

12. Propter *defectum lectuli* subdit: *Et reclinavit eum in praesepio,* non in cubiculo, ita ut verificaretur illud Matthaei octavo[^p47-2]: « Vulpes foveas habent, et volucres caeli nidos; Filius autem hominis non habet, ubi caput suum reclinet ». In *praesepio* ponitur Christus, ut ostendatur illud Ioannis sexto: « Ego sum panis vivus, qui de caelo descendi »; et ut verificaretur illud Isaiae primo: « Cognovit bos possessorem suum, et asinus praesepe domini sui »; et illud Habacuc tertio secundum Septuaginta: « In medio duorum animalium innotesceris, cum appropinquaverint anni, cognosceris » etc.

13. In hoc autem secundum *mysticum* intellectum, quod natus est *in Bethlehem,* intelligitur, quod est panis vivus; *Bethlehem* domus panis interpretatur[^p47-3]. In hoc, quod *in praesepio* positus, notatur, quod est cibus simplicium et humilium ratione carnis assumtae; Isaiae quadragesimo: « Omnis caro foenum, et omnis gloria eius quasi flos foeni ». In hoc, quod *in medio duorum animalium,* significatur, quod in hoc pastu et pastore uniri debebant pecora duplicis ovilis; Ioannis decimo[^p47-4]: « Alias oves habeo, quae non sunt ex hoc ovili; et illas oportet me adducere, et vocem meam audient, et fiet unum ovile et unus pastor ».

*Moraliter* et *ad litteram* in hoc damnavit Christus *lectos delicatos,* contra quos, Amos sexto[^p47-5]:
« Vae vobis, qui dormitis in lectis eburneis et lascivitis in stratis »!

14. Propter *defectum habitaculi* subdit: *Quia non erat eis locus in diversorio. Diversorium* namque secundum Isidorum[^p47-6] dicitur, eo quod diversi ibi conveniant, et hic est locus patens; secundum Bedam vero, eo quod diversa habeat orificia; nam est platea vacua inter duos vicos, habens introitum et exitum ad utrumque, et propter intemperiem aëris superius cooperta, ut ibi cives convenire ad colloquendum possint. Hic peperit virgo Maria, quia non habebant domum, in qua possent hospitari, sive quia pauperes erant, sive quia tarde venerant. Hoc autem erat arctum, vel etiam aliis repletum, ita ut non haberet locum nisi locum arctissimum inter bruta animalia; unde poterat Christus dicere illud Psalmi[^p47-7]: « Ut iumentum factus sum apud te, et ego semper tecum »; et verificatum est illud Ieremiae decimo quarto: « Quare sicut viator futurus es in terra et quasi colonus declinans ad manendum? Quare futurus es velut vir vagus, ut fortis, qui non potest salvare? Tu autem in nobis es, Domine, et nomen tuum invocatum est super nos » etc. — Et in hoc damnatur *aedificiorum amplitudo,* secundum illud Isaiae quinto[^p47-8]: « Vae, qui coniungitis domum ad domum, et agrum agro copulatis »! Augustinus: « Non erat illi nisi angustia in terris, ut tu ei locum cordis tui sibi proprium dilatares. Filius hominis non habet, ubi caput suum reclinet, et tu ampla palatia et ingentes porticus metiris ».

15. Sic ergo Christum paupercula mater pauperem peperit, ut nos ad paupertatem invitaret et sua inopia ditaret, secundum illud secundae ad Corinthios octavo[^p47-9]: « Scitis gratiam Domini nostri Iesu Christi, qui, cum esset dives, propter vos egenus fa-
<!-- page 48 -->
ctus est ». Et secundum hanc omnimodam indigentiam damnavit avaram opulentiam; unde Bernardus[^p48-1]: « Elegit Filius Dei pauperem matrem, quae vix pannos haberet ad involvendum, et praesepe ad reclinandum, locum ad reponendum. Non est tale iudicium mundi. Aut iste fallitur, aut mundus errat; sed divinam falli impossibile est sapientiam: elegit igitur qui non fallitur quod carni molestius est; id ergo melius, id utilius, id magis eligendum ». Simus ergo cum Christo, sicut dicitur secundae ad Corinthios sexto, « sicut egentes, multos autem locupletantes, tanquam nihil habentes, et omnia possidentes ».

16. Ex hoc insinuatur nobis, quod iste erat vere *Salvator mundi,* qui in sui ortus primordio exemplum virtutis praebuit et *viam salutis* ostendit. Habendo enim lectulum vilem, humilem et pauperem, iam incipiebat dicere, quod mundus iste contemnendus est, secundum tria in eo contenta[^p48-2]. Iam incipiebat *statum perfectionis* exemplo monstrare, qui consistit in humilitate, austeritate et paupertate. Ostendit etiam in hoc Dominus summae *dignationis affectum,* quia non tantum parvulus, sed etiam pauperculus et despectus pro nobis est factus, ita ut vere posset dicere illud Psalmi[^p48-3]: « Pauper sum ego et in laboribus a iuventute mea ».

## English
<!-- page 43 -->

### Chapter II.

#### *Secondly, on the nativity from the womb, in two ways.*

1. *And it came to pass in those days* etc. That part being ended in which the conception of the Saviour in the womb is treated, there follows the second part, in which the *birth of the Saviour from the womb* is treated, and he does this in this whole chapter[^p43-8]. But this part has two, in the first of which the *truth of the nativity of Christ* is determined; in the second the *humility of the one born* is determined. For since Christ in his nativity *was made of a*
<!-- page 44 -->
*woman,* and *was made under the Law*[^p44-1] through humility; therefore it pertains to the Evangelist to determine both; and the second part begins there: *And after eight days were accomplished* etc.

#### First, on the truth of the nativity of Christ, three things are shown.

The first part has three parts, in the first of which is shown how the nativity of Christ was *consummated;* in the second, how it was *declared by the Angels,* there[^p44-2]: *And there were in the same country shepherds;* in the third, how it was *manifested and divulged by the shepherds,* there: *And it came to pass, after they departed* etc.

#### How the nativity of Christ was consummated.

The first part has three, since to express the nativity of the Saviour it is fitting to assign the *time* and the *place* and the *manner of bringing forth.* The Evangelist therefore describes in the first part the *due time;* in the second, the *suitable place,* there[^p44-3]: *And Joseph also went up from Galilee;* but in the third, the *bringing forth of the Virgin,* there: *And it came to pass, when they were there.*

2. But the *due time* for the nativity of Christ was the time of the universal empire's being enrolled, « because, as Gregory says[^p44-4], he was coming in the flesh who would enrol his elect in eternity ». In this enrolling of the universal empire a threefold excellence is marked, namely *of power in commanding, of wisdom in discerning* and *of justice in executing,* since these three were to be in Christ, according to that of Jeremias, the twenty-third chapter[^p44-5]: « Behold, the days come, saith the Lord, and I will raise up to David a just branch, and a king shall reign », behold, *power;* « and shall be wise », behold, *wisdom;* « and shall execute judgment and justice », behold, *justice,* so that there may be in him perfection of every kind.

3. (Vers. 1.). As to the excellence *of power in commanding* he says: *There went out a decree from Caesar Augustus, that the whole world should be enrolled.* This Caesar Augustus was by his proper name Octavian, but from Julius Caesar, who ruled before, he was surnamed *Caesar,* from whom all the Roman emperors afterwards were called *caesars.* He was also called Augustus, because he increased the commonwealth; and from him all the others are always called *Augusti*[^p44-6]. And he put forth a universal decree, because he had universal dominion, not over each and every one, but over the principal parts of the universe; whence his empire was great, according to that of Daniel, the second chapter[^p44-7]: « And the fourth kingdom shall be iron. As iron breaketh into pieces and subdueth all things, so shall it break and destroy all these ». This was the empire of the Romans, which was designated through the empire of Nabuchodonosor, of whom Jeremias, the twenty-seventh chapter: « I have given all these lands into the hand of Nabuchodonosor, and all nations shall serve him. The nation and the kingdom that will not serve Nabuchodonosor, I will visit upon that nation, saith the Lord ». In these days, namely of this kingdom, was the time of the coming of Christ, because it is said in Daniel, the second chapter[^p44-8]: « In the days of those kingdoms the Lord will set up a kingdom that shall never be destroyed, and his kingdom shall not be delivered up to another people ». And to him alone belongs the full enrolling of the world, according to that of the Psalm: « The earth is the Lord's and the fulness thereof, the world and all they that dwell therein ». And therefore it is said in the Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter, that « he had on his garment and on his thigh written: King of kings and Lord of lords »; because, as is said in Ecclesiastes, the fifth chapter, « there is another higher than the high, and there are others still higher than these ».

4. (Vers. 2.). As to the excellence *of wisdom in discerning* he adds: *This enrolling was first made by Cyrinus, the governor of Syria;* because this was, as Ambrose says[^p44-9], the *first* general enrolling, which was begun by Cyrinus; or: *first made,* that is, first begun, according to Bede, by him who presided in Syria, which is the navel of the habit-
<!-- page 45 -->
able earth; and so the said enrolling was made *distinctly* and *in order,* and these are two signs of wisdom in a prince, according to what is said of Solomon in the third of Kings, the tenth chapter: « The queen of Saba seeing all the wisdom of Solomon, and the dwellings of his servants, and the orders of his ministers, had no longer any spirit in her ». But this enrolling was made not on account of vanity, as that of David, the second of Kings, the last chapter[^p45-1], on account of which the people was struck, but on account of the utility of the empire, that the amount of the tribute might be known, and this belongs to discretion, not to elation; Ecclesiasticus, the forty-second chapter: « Where there are many hands, shut up; and whatsoever thou deliverest, number and weigh it; and put in writing all that is given and received ». But it is not for any one of the several kings to enrol tributes, but for Christ being born; whence the Psalm: « And in thy book all shall be written; days shall be formed, and no one in them ».

5. (Vers. 3.). As to the excellence *of justice in pursuing* it is added: *And all went to profess, every one into his own city.* They were therefore doing the king's command, and no one was occupying what was another's; and so they had *subjection* to the king in the profession, and *peace* with one another in a quiet and *just* dwelling together, according to that to the Romans, the thirteenth chapter[^p45-2]: « Render to all men their dues: tribute, to whom tribute; honour, to whom honour; owe no man any thing, but to love one another ». *To profess,* that is, that they were subject to the Roman empire, and this by rendering the census. And that was just, according to that of Matthew, the twenty-second chapter[^p45-3]: « Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's ». And because Judea had now been made tributary, it is signified that the time of the nativity of Christ had come, according to that of Genesis, the next to the last chapter: « The sceptre shall not be taken away from Juda, nor a ruler from his thigh, till he come that is to be sent, and he shall be the expectation of nations ». *They went,* moreover, with nothing standing in the way, by which it is shown that that empire was most peaceful; and therefore it was the time that Christ should come, the announcer of peace, of whom to the Ephesians, the second chapter[^p45-4]: « He is our peace, who hath made both one ». And so that of Micheas, the fourth chapter, had now been fulfilled: « Nation shall not take sword against nation, and they shall no more learn to war; and all people will walk every one in the name of his God ». And this is what Bede says in the Gloss[^p45-5]: « In a most peaceful time Christ willed to be born, because he greatly sought and loved peace, and always visited the lovers of peace ».

6. (Vers. 4.). *And Joseph also went up* etc. After he has described the due time, he describes here the *suitable place,* and this because that place was fitting to the *prophetic foretelling,* was fitting to the *divine promise,* was fitting also to the *imperial decree.*

It is shown therefore *fitting to the prophetic foretelling* in that he says: *And Joseph also went up from Galilee, out of the city of Nazareth;* because according to one prophecy Nazareth, « where Christ had been conceived », befitted the *conception;* Isaias, the eleventh chapter[^p45-6], according to the other letter: « A Nazarene shall rise up out of his root »; but according to another, Bethlehem befitted the *nativity.* Therefore he adds: *Into Judea, to the city of David, which is called Bethlehem;* Micheas, the fifth chapter[^p45-7]: « And thou, Bethlehem Ephrata, art a little one among the thousands of Juda; for out of thee shall he come forth that is to be the ruler in Israel; and his going forth is from the beginning, from the days of eternity ». Whence it is said in Matthew, the second chapter, that to Herod asking where Christ should be born, the scribes answered: « In Bethlehem of Juda, for so it is written by the Prophet: And thou Bethlehem, the land of Juda, art not the least among the princes of Juda. For out of thee shall come forth the captain that shall rule my people ». This was the city of David, because he took his origin from there; the first of Kings, the sixteenth chapter: « The Lord said to Samuel: Come, I will send thee to Isai the Bethlehemite; for I have provided me a king among his sons ». — And note that Christ was born in one place, conceived in another, brought up in another, so that the mystery of the incarnation might be hidden from Herod and other impious men; whence John, the seventh chapter[^p45-8]: « Doth the Christ come out of Galilee? Is it not from Bethlehem the town »?

7. The place is shown also *fitting to the divine promise,* when it is added: *Because he was of the house and family of David,* he namely and his spouse the virgin Mary. Whence Bernard says[^p45-9] that « in the one the promise made to David was fulfilled, the other being only witness and privy to it ». This promise was made to David, in the second of Kings, the seventh chapter, through Nathan: « The Lord foretelleth to thee that the Lord will make thee a house; and when thy days shall be fulfilled, I will raise up thy seed after thee, and I will establish the throne of his kingdom for ever »; and again in the Psalm: « The Lord hath sworn truth to David, and he will not make it void: of the fruit of thy womb I will set upon thy throne ».

<!-- page 46 -->

8. (Vers. 5.). It is shown lastly as *fitting to the imperial decree,* when it is added: *To profess with Mary his espoused wife, who was with child.* For this had proceeded from the imperial decree. And although the Virgin had already conceived the King of heaven and earth, yet even while with child she willed to obey the imperial decree, so that she could say with the Son that of Matthew, the third chapter[^p46-1]: « So it becometh us to fulfil all justice »; and by this she might give us an example of obeying every higher power, according to that of the first of Peter, the second chapter: « Be ye subject to every human creature for God's sake, whether it be to the king as excelling, or to governors as sent by him for the punishment of evildoers, and for the praise of the good ». — But in this it appears how human empire serves the divine. For when Caesar commanded that all should go into their own city, and Mary and Joseph obeyed[^p46-2] his command, it came about, what neither intended, namely that Christ should be born in Bethlehem, according to what had been eternally disposed and temporally promised and foretold and prophesied. And therefore the command of those set over us is in no way to be contemned.

9. (Vers. 6.). *And it came to pass, when they were there* etc. Above he described the nativity of Christ as to the due time and the suitable place; here thirdly he describes it as to the *bringing forth,* which he describes as to three things, namely as to the *opportuneness of bringing forth,* the *newness of the bringing forth* and the *poverty of her who brings forth;* from which the birth of Christ is shown to be wonderful and inimitable[^p46-3] and commendable.

First, then, he insinuates the *opportuneness of bringing forth,* when he says: *It came to pass, when they were there, the days* of Mary *were fulfilled, that she should bring forth,* according to what was said above of her kinswoman, in the preceding chapter[^p46-4]: « Elizabeth's time of bringing forth was fulfilled ». Truly *fulfilled,* because Christ was conceived and born in the fulness of time; to the Galatians, the fourth chapter: « But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent his Son, made of a woman »; the Psalm: « Full days shall be found in them ».

10. (Vers. 7.). He insinuates also the *newness of the bringing forth* in that he says: *And she brought forth her firstborn son.* But he says this by privation of a prior; for because the Virgin had conceived him, therefore this one was her firstborn; Isaias, the seventh chapter[^p46-5]: « Behold, a Virgin shall conceive and bear a son »; and therefore the bringing forth was new, as was the conception too, because, as the conception was without shame, so the bringing forth was without pain; Isaias, the last chapter: « Before she was in labour, she brought forth; before her time came to be delivered, she brought forth a man child. Who hath ever heard such a thing? Or who hath seen the like to this »? and afterwards: « Shall not I, that make others to bring forth, myself bring forth, saith the Lord? Shall I, that give generation to others, be barren, saith the Lord God »? Such a bringing forth befitted Christ, of whom to the Colossians, the first chapter[^p46-6]: « In whom we have redemption and the remission of sins, who is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of every creature »; whence, as he is the firstborn of the Father, so also the firstborn of the Mother; as also the only-begotten of the Father, so the only-begotten of the Mother; John, the first chapter: « We saw his glory, the glory as it were of the Only-begotten of the Father » etc. Whence he does not say *firstborn* here meaning order with respect to a later one, as Helvidius the heretic said, but privation with respect to a prior one, because he had none before him, so that it may be shown that he was to be dedicated wholly to the Lord. For all firstborn were to be offered to God; Exodus, the thirteenth chapter[^p46-7]: « Sanctify unto me every firstborn that openeth the womb among the children of Israel, as well of men as of beasts; for they are all mine ».

11. But thirdly he insinuates the *poverty of her who brings forth,* because she was in need *of covering, of bed* and *of lodging.* — On account of the lack *of covering* he says: *And wrapped him up in swaddling clothes,* not in one garment, but in many, so that he could be called a ragged poor man and might show by example that of the Apostle, the first to Timothy, the last chapter[^p46-8]: « Having food, and wherewith to be covered, with these we are content ». And this corresponds to that prophecy, Zacharias, the third chapter, where it is said that « Jesus, the high priest, was clothed with filthy garments ». Bernard[^p46-9]: « Thou mayest recognize Jesus the high priest, clothed with filthy garments, while he was contending with the devil. But when our head was exalted above our enemies, he changed his garment, he put
<!-- page 47 -->
on beauty, *clothed with light as with a garment.* For more useful in the conflict is an iron breastplate than a linen stole, though that be a burden, this an honour ». — And in this the *adornment of costly garments* is reproved; on account of which it is said in Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p47-1]: « They that are clothed in soft garments are in the houses of kings »; and Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter: « Glory not in apparel at any time ».

12. On account of the *lack of a bed* he adds: *And laid him in a manger,* not in a chamber, so that that of Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p47-2], might be verified: « The foxes have holes, and the birds of the air nests; but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head ». Christ is put *in a manger,* so that that of John, the sixth chapter, may be shown: « I am the living bread which came down from heaven »; and so that that of Isaias, the first chapter, might be verified: « The ox knoweth his owner, and the ass his master's crib »; and that of Habacuc, the third chapter, according to the Septuagint: « In the midst of two animals thou shalt be made known, when the years draw nigh thou shalt be known » etc.

13. But in this, according to the *mystical* understanding, that he was born *in Bethlehem,* it is understood that he is the living bread; *Bethlehem* is interpreted the house of bread[^p47-3]. In this, that he was placed *in a manger,* it is noted that he is the food of the simple and humble by reason of the flesh assumed; Isaias, the fortieth chapter: « All flesh is grass, and all the glory thereof as the flower of the grass ». In this, that he was *in the midst of two animals,* it is signified that in this pasture and shepherd the flocks of a double fold were to be united; John, the tenth chapter[^p47-4]: « Other sheep I have, that are not of this fold; them also I must bring, and they shall hear my voice, and there shall be one fold and one shepherd ».

*Morally* and *according to the letter* Christ in this condemned *delicate beds,* against which Amos, the sixth chapter[^p47-5]: « Woe to you that sleep upon beds of ivory, and are wanton on your couches »!

14. On account of the *lack of a dwelling* he adds: *Because there was no room for them in the inn.* For the *inn* (*diversorium*), according to Isidore[^p47-6], is so called because diverse people come together there, and this is an open place; but according to Bede, because it has diverse openings; for it is an empty square between two streets, having an entrance and an exit to each, and covered above on account of the inclemency of the air, so that the citizens can come together there to converse. Here the virgin Mary brought forth, because they did not have a house in which they could lodge, either because they were poor, or because they had come late. But this place was narrow, or even filled with others, so that he had no place except a most narrow place among the brute animals; whence Christ could say that of the Psalm[^p47-7]: « I am become as a beast before thee, and I am always with thee »; and that of Jeremias, the fourteenth chapter, was verified: « Why wilt thou be as a wayfaring man in the land, and as a stranger turning in to lodge? Why wilt thou be as a wandering man, as a mighty man that cannot save? But thou, O Lord, art among us, and thy name is called upon by us » etc. — And in this the *largeness of buildings* is condemned, according to that of Isaias, the fifth chapter[^p47-8]: « Woe to you that join house to house, and lay field to field »! Augustine: « There was for him nothing but narrowness on earth, so that thou mightest enlarge for him the place of thy heart as his own. The Son of man hath not where to lay his head, and thou measurest out ample palaces and huge porticoes ».

15. Thus therefore the poor little mother brought forth Christ poor, so that he might invite us to poverty and enrich us by his want, according to that of the second to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter[^p47-9]: « You know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that, being rich, he became needy
<!-- page 48 -->
for your sakes ». And according to this want of every kind he condemned avaricious opulence; whence Bernard[^p48-1]: « The Son of God chose a poor mother, who scarcely had cloths to wrap him in, and a manger to lay him in, a place to put him in. Such is not the judgement of the world. Either he is deceived, or the world errs; but it is impossible for the divine wisdom to be deceived: therefore he who is not deceived chose what is more troublesome to the flesh; that therefore is better, that more useful, that more to be chosen ». Let us therefore be with Christ, as it is said in the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter, « as needy, yet enriching many, as having nothing, and possessing all things ».

16. From this it is insinuated to us that he was truly the *Saviour of the world,* who in the very beginning of his birth gave an example of virtue and showed the *way of salvation.* For by having a bed that was vile, lowly and poor, he was already beginning to say that this world is to be contemned, according to the three things contained in it[^p48-2]. He was already beginning to show by example the *state of perfection,* which consists in humility, austerity and poverty. The Lord showed also in this the *affection* of the highest *condescension,* because he was made for us not only a little one, but also a poor little one and despised, so that he could truly say that of the Psalm[^p48-3]: « I am poor, and in labours from my youth ».
