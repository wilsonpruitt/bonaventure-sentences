# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. X, Vers. 38-42
## *Ad informationem discipulorum subiungitur humanum exemplum dupliciter propositum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 272 -->

#### Ad informationem discipulorum subiungitur humanum exemplum dupliciter propositum.

65. *Factum est autem, dum irent* etc. Postquam tradidit formam vivendi per divinum praeceptum, hic secundo tradit per *humanum exemplum.* Unde Glossa Bedae[^p272-4]: « Habito sermone de dilectione Dei et proximi, supponit exemplum utriusque ». Introducitur enim hic ad litteram *exemplum perfectionis,* exemplum vitae activae et contemplativae, et *comparationis utriusque.* Unde haec pars habet duas partes: in quarum prima praemittitur *comparatio rationalis;* in secunda vero subditur *definitio iudicialis,* ibi[^p272-5]: *Et respondens dixit illi Dominus.*

Circa *rationalem* autem *comparationem* quatuor introducuntur: primum est *consortium divinae praesentiae,* secundum est *otium contemplativae,* tertium est *exercitium operativae,* quartum est *litigium inter utrumque.*

66. (Vers. 38.). Primo igitur quantum ad *consortium divinae praesentiae* dicitur: *Factum est autem, dum irent, et ipse intravit in quoddam castellum,* sive propter *regnum Dei evangelizandum,* secundum quod dicitur supra octavo[^p272-6]: « Ipse iter faciebat per civitates et castella, praedicans et evangelizans regnum Dei »; sive propter *hospitium quaerendum,* secundum quod dicitur supra nono, quod cum Samaritani nollent eum et discipulos hospitio recipere, quod « abierunt in aliud castellum ». De hoc castello dicitur expressius Ioannis undecimo[^p272-7]: « Erat quidam languens Lazarus a Bethania, de castello Mariae et Marthae, sororum eius ». In hoc, inquam, castello invenit hospitium. — Propter quod subditur: *Et mulier quaedam, Martha nomine, excepit*[^p272-8] *illum in domum suam,* tanquam scilicet pauperem et egenum. Unde talibus dicet illud in iudicio Matthaei vigesimo quinto: « Hospes eram, et collegistis me », similibus scilicet Marthae, qualis erat Iob, de quo trigesimo primo: « Foris non mansit peregrinus, et ostium meum viatori patuit ». Et in illo hospitio praesens erat *corporaliter,* sicut activis et contemplativis praesens est *spiritualiter,* secundum illud Apocalypsis tertio[^p272-9]: « Ego sto ad ostium et pulso; si quis aperuerit mihi, introibo ad illum et coenabo cum illo »; quia Proverbiorum octavo dicitur: « Deliciae meae esse cum filiis hominum »; et e converso, unde Sapientiae octavo: « Intrans in domum meam, conquiescam cum illa; non enim habet amaritudinem conversatio illius, nec taedium convictus illius ».

67. (Vers. 39.). Secundo vero quantum ad *otium contemplativae* subdit: *Et huic erat soror, nomine Maria,* quae scilicet erat in otio contemplationis perfecta; unde subditur: *Quae etiam, sedens secus pedes Domini, audiebat verbum illius.* Hoc quidem erat istius mulieris otium: *Domino intendere, vacare, sedere* et *tacere.* Unde dicitur Ioannis undecimo[^p272-10], quod « Maria domi *sedebat* », et hoc *secus pedes eius;* quia, Deuteronomii trigesimo tertio, « qui
<!-- page 273 -->
appropinquant pedibus eius accipient de doctrina illius ». Per *sessionem secus pedes eius* intelligitur humilitas, quae debet esse in viris contemplativis ad hoc, quod abundent fructibus devotionis, secundum illud Psalmi[^p273-1]: « Qui emittis fontes in convallibus » etc. Qui autem sic sedet sicut humilis irrigatur fletibus compunctionis, secundum illud Ieremiae decimo quinto[^p273-2]: « Solus sedebam, quoniam amaritudine replesti me »; et illud est officium animae contemplativae, vacare scilicet lacrymis compunctionis et devotionis. Unde haec Maria, exemplum contemplationis, semper quasi describitur plorans, supra scilicet septimo[^p273-3], ubi dicitur, quod « stans retro secus pedes Domini, lacrymis coepit rigare pedes eius » etc.; et Ioannis undecimo, ubi dicitur, quod « Maria, cum venisset, ubi erat Iesus, videns eum, cecidit ad pedes eius. Iesus ergo, cum vidisset eam plorantem, infremuit spiritu »; et Ioannis vigesimo: « Maria stabat ad monumentum foris, plorans ». Et primae sunt lacrymae *compunctionis;* secundae, *compassionis;* tertiae, *devotionis,* quas debent habere contemplativi, sedendo secus pedes Domini[^p273-4].

68. (Vers. 40.). Tertio quantum ad *exercitium operativae* subditur: *Martha autem satagebat circa frequens ministerium;* et hoc tanquam bona activa, declinans otium, secundum consilium Sapientis, Ecclesiastae nono[^p273-5]: « Quodcumque facere potest manus tua, instanter operare, quia nec opus nec ratio nec sapientia nec scientia erit apud inferos, quo tu properas ». Hoc semper faciebat Martha; unde dicitur Ioannis duodecimo[^p273-6], quod « Iesus venit in Bethaniam; fecerunt autem ibi coenam ei, et Martha ministrabat ». — Et nota, quod dicit, quod *satagebat,* id est, satis agebat, *circa frequens ministerium,* ut ostendat, simul in eius opere fuisse perfectionem et modum debitum, secundum beati Petri consilium, secundae Petri primo[^p273-7]: « Fratres, magis satagite, ut per bona opera certam vestram vocationem et electionem faciatis ». Opus enim ministerii est illud quod maxime Domino placet, et in quo maxime quis imitatur Christum, sicut dicitur infra vigesimo secundo[^p273-8]: « Ego autem in medio vestrum sum, sicut qui ministrat »; et iterum Matthaei vigesimo: « Filius hominis non venit ministrari, sed ministrare ». Unde tale ministerium est placens et honorabile apud Deum et remuneratione dignum, secundum illud Ioannis duodecimo: « Si quis mihi ministraverit, honorificabit eum Pater meus » etc.

69. Quarto quantum ad *litigium inter utrumque*[^p273-9] subiungit: *Quae stetit et ait: Domine, non est tibi curae, quod soror mea reliquit me solam ministrare?* ubi Martha laboriosa conqueritur de Maria otiosa, quasi non possit sola pondus laboris portare, secundum quod conquestus est Moyses Domino Numerorum undecimo[^p273-10]: « Non possum solus sustinere omnem populum hunc, quia gravis est mihi ». Sic etiam Martha propter oneris gravitatem quaerebat auxilium Mariae, sciens, pertinere ad legem Christi illud ad Galatas sexto: « Alter alterius onera portate ». Ideo secure requirebat Christi iudicium, ut sororis impetraret auxilium. — Propter quod addit: *Dic ergo illi, ut me adiuvet,* ut faciat secundum consilium Apostoli ad Galatas quinto[^p273-11]: « Per caritatem Spiritus servite invicem »; et ad Ephesios quarto: « Supportantes invicem in caritate ». — In hac contentione tacet Maria; et rationem huius reddit Gregorius[^p273-12]: « Maria non respondet, sed causam suam tanquam otiosa iudici committit. Si enim pararet respondendi sermonem, remitteret audiendi intentionem ». Contemplativorum namque non est contendere, sed potius tacere et audire et meditari, secundum illud Threnorum tertio[^p273-13]: « Sedebit solitarius et tacebit »; unde Iob quarto: « Porro ad me dictum est verbum absconditum, et quasi furtive suscepi venas susurri eius ». Nihil autem perdit Maria tacendo, quia Dominus causam eius assumit defendendo. Unde Bernardus[^p273-14]: « Ubique Dominus pro Maria respondet, sive cum improbatur a Pharisaeo, supra septimo, sive cum arguitur a sorore, ut hic, sive cum a discipulis, sicut dicitur Matthaei vigesimo sexto ».

70. Conqueritur autem Martha aliquando, aliis *suum officium praeponendo,* et tunc est reprehensibile.
<!-- page 274 -->
Unde Glossa[^p274-1]: « Ex illorum persona loquitur Martha, qui, adhuc divinae contemplationis ignari, solum, quod didicere, fraternae dilectionis opus Deo placere dicunt, ideoque cunctos, qui Christo devoti esse volunt, huic operi mancipandos autumant ». — Aliquando conqueritur, *otium Mariae praeoptando.* Unde Bernardus[^p274-2]: « Putas, in domo, in qua Christus suscipitur, vox murmuris audiatur »? Et addit: « Felix domus et congregatio beata, in qua de Maria Martha conqueritur ». Et ratio huius est, quia *contemplativa* per se est eligenda sine querimonia, Martha vero, id est *activa,* necessitate sustinenda. Unde Iacob elegit Rachel, sed secundum quod necesse fuit, prius suscepit Liam, sicut dicitur Genesis vigesimo nono[^p274-3]. — Licet ergo, conqueri Martham, *ut sit sicut* Maria, quia humilitatis est; si autem conqueritur de hoc, quod *non adiuvatur,* hoc est infirmitatis; si vero conqueritur de hoc, quod aliquando Maria iuvare velit, et *ipsa nolit,* hoc est impietatis, quia talis conquestio legem impedit caritatis[^p274-4].

71. (Vers. 41.). *Et respondens dixit illi Dominus.* Post comparationem rationalem subiungit Evangelista *definitionem iudicialem;* circa quam introducuntur quatuor, scilicet *humiliatio vitae activae, commendatio contemplativae, promulgatio sententiae* et *assignatio causae.*

Primo ergo quantum ad *humiliationem vitae activae* dicit: *Martha, Martha, sollicita es et turbaris erga plurima.* Ideo nomen *Marthae* ingeminat, ut eam excitet ad considerandum defectum proprium, et hoc cum divini verbi attentione; sicut de Moyse dicitur Exodi tertio[^p274-5], quod « cernens Dominus, quod pergeret ad videndum, vocavit eum de medio rubi et ait: Moyses, Moyses »; et Dominus, volens excitare peccatores ad attentionem, iterat vocationem Ieremiae vigesimo secundo: « Terra, terra, audi sermonem Domini ». Et sic nunc excitat Martham, ostendens, in ea esse triplicem defectum, scilicet *sollicitudinis* in cogitatu, *turbationis* in affectu et *divisionis* in actu. Et ista omnia impediunt nos, ne totaliter tendamus in Deum. — Unde nimia *sollicitudo* est vitanda, secundum illud ad Philippenses ultimo[^p274-6]: « Nihil solliciti sitis, sed in omni oratione petitiones vestrae innotescant apud Deum »; et primae Petri ultimo: « Omnem sollicitudinem vestram proiicientes in eum, quoniam ipsi cura est de vobis ». — Vitanda est etiam *turbatio;* unde Ioannis decimo quarto[^p274-7]: « Non turbetur cor vestrum neque formidet. Creditis in Deum, et in me credite ». Unde et de Christo Isaiae quadragesimo secundo: « Non erit tristis neque turbulentus ». Oculus enim turbatus non est ad videndum idoneus. — Vitanda est etiam *divisio;* unde Ecclesiastici undecimo[^p274-8]: « Fili, ne in multis sint actus tui ». — Et haec incommoda habet vita activa, non contemplativa; unde primae ad Corinthios septimo: « Qui cum uxore est sollicitus est quae sunt mundi, quomodo placeat uxori, et divisus est. Et mulier innupta et virgo cogitat quae Domini sunt, ut sit sancta corpore et spiritu; quae autem nupta est cogitat quae sunt mundi ». — Sic ergo humiliata est actionis[^p274-9] importunitas per ostensionem sui incommodi ac defectus.

72. (Vers. 42.). Secundo vero quantum ad *contemplativae commendationem* subdit: *Porro unum est necessarium;* hoc scilicet est regnum Dei, quo habito, nihil deest; unde Matthaei sexto[^p274-10]: « Quaerite primum regnum Dei, et haec omnia adiicientur vobis »; et in Psalmo: « Unam petii a Domino, hanc requiram »; ista tamen est vita beata, quae est in Dei adhaerentia, cui vacat contemplativa; in cuius persona dicitur in Psalmo[^p274-11]: « Mihi autem adhaerere Deo bonum est ». Et hoc est illud unum, quod est necessarium; quia « qui adhaeret Domino unus spiritus est », sicut dicitur primae ad Corinthios sexto. Hoc unum qui habet habet omne bonum; unde in figura huius dicitur Tobiae decimo[^p274-12]: « Omnia in te uno simul habentes, te non debuimus dimittere ire a nobis »; et Sapientiae septimo: « Venerunt mihi omnia bona pariter cum illa » etc. Et ideo dixit Dominus Moysi in persona viri contemplativi Exodi trigesimo tertio: « Ego ostendam tibi omne bonum ».
<!-- page 275 -->

73. Tertio vero quantum ad *promulgationem sententiae* subditur: *Maria optimam partem elegit;* quia scilicet praeelegit unum. « Praeponitur autem *unum* multis », sicut dicit Augustinus[^p275-1], quia « non *unum* a multis, sed *multa* ab uno. Multa sunt, quae facta sunt; unus, qui fecit; bona valde quae fecit, quanto melior ille qui fecit »; immo simpliciter optimus. Et haec pars animae contemplativae; unde Threnorum tertio[^p275-2]: « Pars mea Dominus, dixit anima mea, propterea exspectabo eum. Bonus Dominus sperantibus in eum, animae quaerenti illum »; et in Psalmo: « Quam bonus Israel Deus his qui recto sunt corde »! *Valde,* inquam, *bonus* et optimus; ideo dicit anima contemplativa in Psalmo[^p275-3]: « Clamavi ad te, Domine, dixi: Tu es spes mea, portio mea in terra viventium », scilicet cum Maria; quam scilicet iam contemplativi elegerunt, ipsam contemplando et desiderando. Unde in persona contemplativi Deuteronomii tertio[^p275-4] dicitur: « Transibo et videbo terram hanc optimam et montem istum egregium et Libanum ». Et propter istius amorem nihil voluit possidere in terra nisi solam inopiam, secundum illud Psalmi: « Quoniam melior est dies una in atriis tuis super millia. Elegi abiectus esse in domo Dei mei »; quia, sicut dicitur Matthaei decimo tertio, « simile est regnum caelorum thesauro abscondito in agro, quem qui invenit homo abscondit » etc.

74. Quarto quantum ad *assignationem causae* subdit: *Quae non auferetur ab ea.* Glossa[^p275-5]: « Non reprehenditur pars Marthae, quia et ipsa bona, sed laudatur pars Mariae, quae quare sit optima, subinfertur: *Quae non auferetur ab ea* ». « Ex opposito intellige, quod a Martha auferetur pars, quam elegit, quia transit labor multitudinis, et remanet caritas unitatis[^p275-6] ». Et haec est causa, quare pars Mariae simpliciter melior et eligibilior, quia contemplativa hic incipit et in futuro finitur. Quod designatur in figura Ioannis, secundum quod dicitur Ioannis ultimo[^p275-7]: « Sic eum volo manere », quasi inchoata contemplatio maneat, « donec veniam », perficienda, cum venero. Et quia manentior, ideo melior, sicut Apostolus de caritate dicit primae ad Corinthios decimo tertio[^p275-8]: « Caritas nunquam excidit »; et ex hoc concludit, quod maior est caritas. Sic et de vita contemplativa; unde Beda in Glossa: « Qui hic ardere inchoat, cum ipsum quem amat, viderit, in amore amplius ignescit »; Isaiae trigesimo primo[^p275-9]: « Cuius ignis est in Sion et caminus eius in Ierusalem ». Unde quantum est de se, est praeeligenda, secundum illud secundae ad Corinthios quarto: « Non contemplantibus nobis quae videntur, sed quae non videntur. Quae enim videntur temporalia sunt, quae autem non videntur aeterna sunt ».

75. Propter hoc simpliciter vita *contemplativa* magis est desideranda tanquam ea quae melior et de se praeeligenda, tum quia securior[^p275-10], tum quia suavior, tum quia stabilior; non tamen despicienda *activa,* sed pro loco et tempore ad tempus praeeligenda, tum quia anterior, tum quia poenalior, tum quia fecundior; valet enim sibi et aliis. — Et hoc bene designatum est in duabus uxoribus Iacob, Rachel scilicet et Lia, quarum una designat vitam activam, altera contemplativam[^p275-11]. Unde sponsus compellit sponsam aliquando ad actionem egredi, secundum quod dicitur Canticorum secundo: « Sonet vox tua in auribus meis » etc. Unde si de *melioritate* fiat quaestio *simpliciter* contemplativa est melior, secundum quod dicit Gregorius Moralium libro sexto[^p275-12]: « Magna sunt activae merita, sed contemplativae potiora ». *Maria* enim *optimam partem elegit, quae non auferetur ab ea.* — Si autem fiat quaestio de *eligibilitate,* aliquando *praeeligenda est activa,* scilicet viro imperfecto, qui prius debet se exercere in actionis campo, vel cum quis obligatur ad activae opera ex praecepto sive ex officio; et ideo aliquando accidit dubium in praeelectione, secundum illud ad Philippenses primo[^p275-13]: « Mihi vivere Christus est et mori lucrum. Quodsi vivere in carne, hic mihi fructus operis est, et quid eligam, ignoro. Coarctor autem e duobus, desiderium habens dissolvi et esse cum Christo, multo magis melius est; permanere in carne necessarium est propter vos ». — Ideo viros spirituales
<!-- page 276 -->
aliquando oportet egredi, aliquando ingredi, aliquando ascendere, aliquando descendere, sicut vidit Iacob, Genesis vigesimo octavo[^p276-1].

76. Istud autem Evangelium consuevit legi in Assumtione Virginis, sive[^p276-2] quia finis eius Mariae optime congruit, cum dicitur: *Optimam partem elegit sibi Maria, quae non auferetur ab ea.* Quamvis enim hoc ad litteram dictum fuerit de Maria Magdalene, verius tamen est de beata Virgine Maria. Unde Bernardus[^p276-3]: « *Optimam partem elegit sibi Maria. Optimam* plane; bona fecunditas coniugalis, melior autem castitas virginalis, prorsus autem optima est fecunditas virginea, seu fecunda virginitas; Mariae privilegium est, non dabitur alteri, quia *non auferetur ab ea* ». — Sive etiam, quia in hoc Evangelio describitur in duabus sororibus perfectio activae et contemplativae, quarum utraque fuit in Virgine perfectissime. Quod enim datum fuit istis duabus sororibus per partes, Mariae totum datum fuit et integre. Unde Hieronymus[^p276-4]: « Ceteris datum est per partes, Mariae autem se totam simul infudit plenitudo gratiae ». — Sive, quia hic agitur de duplici Christi susceptione: *corporali* et *spirituali: corporali* a Martha in hospitio domus exterioris; *spirituali* a Maria in hospitio domus interioris. Et haec duplex susceptio[^p276-5] perfectissime fuit in Maria, quae ipsum suscepit in *thalamo corporis,* aluit et nutrivit et educavit et sedulo ministravit; suscepit etiam in *thalamo cordis,* ipsum videndo, credendo, amando, imitando. Et ex *utroque* horum beata fuit; unde infra undecimo[^p276-6]: « Beatus venter, qui te portavit »; « Beati, qui audiunt verbum Dei et custodiunt illud ». Unde Augustinus[^p276-7]: « Beatior fuit Maria percipiendo fidem Christi quam concipiendo carnem Christi. Materna namque propinquitas nihil Mariae profuisset, nisi felicius ipsum mente quam carne gestasset ».

77. Sive etiam, quia hic tria determinantur, scilicet divinum *hospitium,* divinum *ministerium,* divinum *contubernium;* quae tria fuerunt perfectissime in Virgine Maria: *hospitium* in castello, *ministerium* in Martha et *contubernium* in Maria.

Recte Virgo Maria in suscipiendo Christum fuit *castellum turribus virtutum munitum* et elevatum; cuius prima turris fuit *fortitudo districtionis,* de qua Canticorum quarto[^p276-8]: « Sicut turris David collum tuum, quae aedificata est cum propugnaculis, mille clypei pendent ex ea », quia Virgo Maria nullo vitio potuit superari. — Secunda turris *rectitudo discretionis,* de qua Canticorum septimo: « Nasus tuus sicut turris Libani, quae respicit contra Damascum »; ubi intelligitur discretio boni a malo. — Tertia turris fuit *multitudo devotionis,* de qua Canticorum ultimo: « Ego murus, et ubera mea sicut turris », propter dulcedinem devotionis, in qua excellebat. Unde istae tres turres aedificatae fuerunt a Spiritu sancto per gratiam super tres potentias animae: prima super *irascibilem,* secunda super *rationalem* et tertia super *concupiscibilem.* — Et ex his Virgo fuit castrum idoneum ad suscipiendum Filium Patris dilectum, qui erat virtus et sapientia Patris[^p276-9], quia Virgo *fortissima, prudentissima* et *devotissima.*

78. Recte etiam in *ministrando* fuit *Martha,* quae Domino ministravit *fideliter* et *humiliter* et *viriliter.* Sic et Maria, cum esset Mater, fecit se ancillam et ministram, secundum illud supra primo[^p276-10]: « Ecce, ancilla Domini, fiat mihi secundum verbum tuum ». Unde designata fuit per illam bonam mulierem Abigail, quae, cum a David peteretur in coniugium, obtulit se ad ministerium; primi Regum vigesimo quinto[^p276-11]: « Ecce, famula tua sit ancilla, ut lavet pedes servorum tuorum ». Talis fuit Virgo Maria prae nimia humilitate; unde de se ipsa dicebat: « Respexit humilitatem ancillae suae ». Et hoc est quod dicit Augustinus[^p276-12]: « Mariam operis exhibitione et fidei rigidissima veritate ministram Christi fuisse, omnis, qui sane sapit, intelligit. Absque dubio enim ministratrix ei exstitit, quae hunc in utero gessit partuque profusum aluit et fovit, atque, ut Evangelium ait, *in praesepio reclinavit* et a facie Herodis fugiens in Aegyptum accessit et omnem infantiam eius pio matris affectu est prosecuta ».

79. Recte etiam in *cohabitando* sive in *contemplando* fuit *Maria.* Nam ipsa, sicut altera Maria, iuxta Christum stabat, secundum illud Ioannis decimo nono[^p276-13]: « Stabant iuxta crucem Iesu Mater eius et soror Matris eius, Maria Cleophae, et Maria Magdalene ». Ipsa namque Virgo fuit, quae *maxime ei*
<!-- page 277 -->
*appropinquavit,* ideo maxime verba eius suscepit et aliis reservavit. Nam supra secundo[^p277-1] dicitur: « Maria conservabat omnia verba haec ». Unde recte designata fuit per *arcam foederis Domini,* de qua ad Hebraeos nono: « In qua erat urna aurea habens manna », per multam *devotionem caritatis;* « et virga Aaron », per multam *rectitudinem virtutis;* « et tabulae testimonii », per multam *cognitionem contemplationis veritatis.* — Et ipsa etiam fuit maxime contemplativa. Unde Bernardus[^p277-2]: « Beata Maria profundissimam divinae sapientiae, ultra quam credi valeat, penetravit abyssum, ut quantum sine personali unione creaturae conditio patitur, illi luci inaccessibili videatur immersa ». Et Beda[^p277-3]: « Quid de Deo non sapiebat, in qua sapientia Dei latebat et cuius uteri corpus sibi aptabat »?

80. Et sic patet, quomodo istud Evangelium non per humanam inventionem, sed per divinam inspirationem[^p277-4] fuit Assumtioni Virginis appropriatum, quia Spiritus sanctus clausit in eo Virginis commendationem quoad multitudinem praerogativarum; ad quarum conservationem subiungit in fine: *Maria optimam partem elegit, quae non auferetur ab ea.* Elegit enim optimam partem Maria et in gratia et in gloria; in qua clauditur Virginis laus perfecta et propria; sicut enim dicit Hieronymus[^p277-5]: « Sicut comparatione Dei nemo bonus, sic comparatione Matris Domini nulla invenitur perfecta, quantumcumque virtutibus eximia comprobetur ». Ideo inter mulieres ipsa sola est *optima* per omnimodam superabundantiam, ratione cuius[^p277-6] « nec primam similem visa est nec habere sequentem ».


## English
<!-- page 272 -->

#### For the formation of the disciples the human example is subjoined, proposed in two ways.

65. *Now it came to pass as they went,* etc. After he has delivered the form of living through the divine precept, here secondly he delivers it through *the human example.* Whence the Gloss of Bede[^p272-4]: « The discourse on the dilection of God and of neighbour being had, he puts beneath it an example of both ». For here is brought in, according to the letter, *the example of perfection,* the example of the active and contemplative life, and *of the comparison of both.* Whence this part has two parts: in the first of which *the rational comparison* is put forward; and in the second *the judicial definition* is subjoined, there[^p272-5]: *And the Lord answering, said to her.*

Now concerning *the rational comparison* four things are brought in: the first is *the fellowship of the divine presence,* the second is *the leisure of the contemplative,* the third is *the exercise of the operative,* the fourth is *the dispute between the two.*

66. (Vers. 38.). First, then, as to *the fellowship of the divine presence* it is said: *Now it came to pass as they went, that he entered into a certain town,* either for the sake of *the kingdom of God to be evangelized,* according to what is said above in the eighth[^p272-6]: « He travelled through the cities and towns, preaching and evangelizing the kingdom of God »; or for the sake of *lodging to be sought,* according to what is said above in the ninth, that when the Samaritans would not receive him and the disciples into lodging, « they went into another town ». Of this town it is said more expressly in John eleven[^p272-7]: « There was a certain man sick, Lazarus of Bethania, of the town of Mary and of Martha, his sisters ». In this town, I say, he found lodging. — On account of which it is subjoined: *And a certain woman named Martha, received*[^p272-8] *him into her house,* as one, namely, poor and needy. Whence to such he will say in the judgement, in Matthew twenty-five: « I was a stranger, and you took me in », to those like Martha, namely, such as was Job, of whom in the thirty-first: « The stranger did not stay without, my door was open to the traveller ». And in that lodging he was present *bodily,* as he is present to the active and contemplative *spiritually,* according to that of the Apocalypse three[^p272-9]: « Behold, I stand at the gate, and knock. If any man shall open to me, I will come in to him, and will sup with him »; because in Proverbs eight it is said: « My delights were to be with the children of men »; and conversely, whence Wisdom eight: « Entering into my house, I shall repose myself with her: for her conversation hath no bitterness, nor her company any tediousness ».

67. (Vers. 39.). Secondly, as to *the leisure of the contemplative,* he adds: *And she had a sister called Mary,* who, namely, was perfect in the leisure of contemplation; whence it is subjoined: *Who sitting also at the Lord's feet, heard his word.* This indeed was the leisure of this woman: *to attend to the Lord, to be free, to sit* and *to be silent*[^tr-vacare]. Whence it is said in John eleven[^p272-10] that « Mary *sat* at home », and this *at his feet;* because, in Deuteronomy thirty-three, « they that
<!-- page 273 -->
approach to his feet, shall receive of his doctrine ». By *the sitting at his feet* is understood humility, which ought to be in contemplative men to this end, that they may abound in the fruits of devotion, according to that of the Psalm[^p273-1]: « Thou sendest forth springs in the vales », etc. Now he who so sits as one humble is watered with the weepings of compunction, according to that of Jeremias fifteen[^p273-2]: « I sat alone, because thou hast filled me with bitterness »; and that is the office of the contemplative soul, namely to be free for the tears of compunction and of devotion. Whence this Mary, the example of contemplation, is always described as it were weeping: above, namely, in the seventh[^p273-3], where it is said that « standing behind at the Lord's feet, she began to wash his feet with tears », etc.; and in John eleven, where it is said that « Mary, when she was come where Jesus was, seeing him, fell down at his feet. Jesus therefore, when he saw her weeping, groaned in the spirit »; and in John twenty: « Mary stood at the sepulchre without, weeping ». And the first tears are those *of compunction;* the second, *of compassion;* the third, *of devotion,* which the contemplatives ought to have, sitting at the Lord's feet[^p273-4].

68. (Vers. 40.). Thirdly, as to *the exercise of the operative,* it is subjoined: *But Martha was busy about much serving;* and this as the good active life, declining leisure, according to the counsel of the Wise man, Ecclesiastes nine[^p273-5]: « Whatsoever thy hand is able to do, do it earnestly: for neither work, nor reason, nor wisdom, nor knowledge shall be in hell, whither thou art hastening ». This Martha always did; whence it is said in John twelve[^p273-6] that « Jesus came to Bethania; and they made him a supper there: and Martha served ». — And note that he says that she *satagebat,* "was busy", that is, *satis agebat,* "was doing enough", *about much serving,* to show that in her work there were at once perfection and the due measure, according to the counsel of blessed Peter, Second Peter one[^p273-7]: « Brethren, be the more busy, that by good works you may make sure your calling and election ». For the work of service is that which most pleases the Lord, and in which one most imitates Christ, as is said below in the twenty-second[^p273-8]: « But I am in the midst of you, as he that serveth »; and again in Matthew twenty: « The Son of man is not come to be served, but to serve ». Whence such service is pleasing and honourable with God and worthy of reward, according to that of John twelve: « If any man serve me, him will my Father honour », etc.

69. Fourthly, as to *the dispute between the two*[^p273-9], he subjoins: *Who stood and said: Lord, hast thou no care that my sister hath left me alone to serve?* where Martha the laborious complains of Mary at leisure, as if she could not carry the weight of the labour alone, according as Moses complained to the Lord, in Numbers eleven[^p273-10]: « I am not able alone to bear all this people, because it is heavy to me ». So also Martha, on account of the heaviness of the burden, sought the help of Mary, knowing that that of Galatians six belongs to the law of Christ: « Bear ye one another's burdens ». Therefore she securely asked the judgement of Christ, that she might obtain her sister's help. — On account of which she adds: *Speak to her therefore, that she help me,* that she may do according to the counsel of the Apostle to the Galatians five[^p273-11]: « By charity of the spirit serve one another »; and to the Ephesians four: « Supporting one another in charity ». — In this contention Mary is silent; and Gregory gives the reason of this[^p273-12]: « Mary does not answer, but, as one at leisure, commits her cause to the judge. For if she were to prepare a speech of answering, she would let go the intention of hearing ». For it is not for contemplatives to contend, but rather to be silent and to hear and to meditate, according to that of Lamentations three[^p273-13]: « He shall sit solitary, and shall be silent »; whence Job four: « Now to me was spoken a hidden word, and as it were by stealth I received the veins of its whisper ». But Mary loses nothing by being silent, because the Lord takes up her cause by defending it. Whence Bernard[^p273-14]: « Everywhere the Lord answers for Mary, whether when she is disapproved by the Pharisee, above in the seventh, or when she is reproved by her sister, as here, or when by the disciples, as is said in Matthew twenty-six ».

70. Now Martha complains sometimes *by preferring her own office to others,* and then it is reprehensible.
<!-- page 274 -->
Whence the Gloss[^p274-1]: « In the person of those Martha speaks, who, still ignorant of divine contemplation, say that only what they have learned, the work of fraternal dilection, pleases God, and therefore judge that all who wish to be devoted to Christ are to be given over to this work ». — Sometimes she complains *by preferring the leisure of Mary.* Whence Bernard[^p274-2]: « Thinkest thou that in the house in which Christ is received the voice of murmuring is heard »? And he adds: « Happy the house and blessed the congregation, in which Martha complains of Mary ». And the reason of this is that *the contemplative* is to be chosen for its own sake without complaint, but Martha, that is, *the active,* is to be borne out of necessity. Whence Jacob chose Rachel, but, according as it was necessary, first took Lia, as is said in Genesis twenty-nine[^p274-3]. — It is lawful, therefore, for Martha to complain *that she may be as* Mary, because this is of humility; but if she complains of this, that *she is not helped,* this is of infirmity; but if she complains of this, that Mary would sometimes wish to help, and *she herself would not,* this is of impiety, because such a complaint hinders the law of charity[^p274-4].

71. (Vers. 41.). *And the Lord answering, said to her.* After the rational comparison the Evangelist subjoins *the judicial definition;* concerning which four things are brought in, namely *the humbling of the active life, the commendation of the contemplative, the promulgation of the sentence* and *the assigning of the cause.*

First, then, as to *the humbling of the active life* he says: *Martha, Martha, thou art solicitous and art troubled about many things.* Therefore he doubles the name of *Martha,* that he may stir her up to consider her own defect, and this with attention to the divine word; as of Moses it is said in Exodus three[^p274-5] that « when the Lord saw that he went forward to see, he called to him out of the midst of the bush, and said: Moses, Moses »; and the Lord, wishing to stir up sinners to attention, repeats the call in Jeremias twenty-two: « Earth, earth, hear the word of the Lord ». And so now he stirs up Martha, showing that there is in her a threefold defect, namely *of solicitude* in thought, *of trouble* in affection and *of division* in act. And all these hinder us, that we may not tend wholly into God. — Whence too great *solicitude* is to be avoided, according to that of the Philippians, the last chapter[^p274-6]: « Be nothing solicitous; but in all prayer let your petitions be made known to God »; and First Peter, the last chapter: « Casting all your solicitude upon him, for he hath care of you ». — *Trouble* also is to be avoided; whence John fourteen[^p274-7]: « Let not your heart be troubled, nor let it be afraid. You believe in God, believe also in me ». Whence also of Christ, Isaias forty-two: « He shall not be sad, nor troublesome ». For a troubled eye is not fit for seeing. — *Division* also is to be avoided; whence Ecclesiasticus eleven[^p274-8]: « Son, let not thy acts be in many things ». — And these disadvantages the active life has, not the contemplative; whence First Corinthians seven: « He that is with a wife, is solicitous for the things of the world, how he may please his wife: and he is divided. And the unmarried woman and the virgin thinketh on the things of the Lord, that she may be holy both in body and in spirit. But she that is married thinketh on the things of the world ». — So, then, the importunity of action[^p274-9] was humbled by the showing of its disadvantage and defect.

72. (Vers. 42.). Secondly, as to *the commendation of the contemplative,* he adds: *But one thing is necessary;* this, namely, is the kingdom of God, which being had, nothing is lacking; whence Matthew six[^p274-10]: « Seek ye first the kingdom of God, and all these things shall be added unto you »; and in the Psalm: « One thing I have asked of the Lord, this will I seek after »; yet this is the blessed life, which is in adherence to God, for which the contemplative is free; in whose person it is said in the Psalm[^p274-11]: « But it is good for me to adhere to God ». And this is that one thing which is necessary; because « he who adhereth to the Lord, is one spirit », as is said in First Corinthians six. He who has this one thing has every good; whence in figure of this it is said in Tobias ten[^p274-12]: « Having all things together in thee alone, we ought not to have let thee go from us »; and Wisdom seven: « Now all good things came to me together with her », etc. And therefore the Lord said to Moses in the person of the contemplative man, in Exodus thirty-three: « I will show thee all good ».
<!-- page 275 -->

73. Thirdly, as to *the promulgation of the sentence,* it is subjoined: *Mary hath chosen the best part;* because, namely, she chose the one thing before others. « Now *the one* is set before the many », as Augustine says[^p275-1], because « not *the one* is from the many, but *the many* from the one. Many are the things that were made; one, he who made them; very good the things he made, how much better he who made them »; nay rather, simply the best. And this is the part of the contemplative soul; whence Lamentations three[^p275-2]: « The Lord is my portion, said my soul: therefore will I wait for him. The Lord is good to them that hope in him, to the soul that seeketh him »; and in the Psalm: « How good is God to Israel, to them that are of a right heart »! *Very good,* I say, and the best; therefore the contemplative soul says in the Psalm[^p275-3]: « I cried to thee, O Lord: I said: Thou art my hope, my portion in the land of the living », namely with Mary; which, namely, the contemplatives have already chosen, by contemplating and desiring it. Whence in the person of the contemplative it is said in Deuteronomy three[^p275-4]: « I will pass over, and will see this best land, and this excellent mountain, and Libanus ». And for the love of this he willed to possess nothing on earth except poverty alone, according to that of the Psalm: « For better is one day in thy courts above thousands. I have chosen to be an abject in the house of my God »; because, as is said in Matthew thirteen, « the kingdom of heaven is like unto a treasure hidden in a field, which a man that findeth hideth », etc.

74. Fourthly, as to *the assigning of the cause,* he adds: *Which shall not be taken away from her.* The Gloss[^p275-5]: « The part of Martha is not reproved, because it too is good, but the part of Mary is praised, and why it is the best is inferred: *Which shall not be taken away from her* ». « From the opposite understand that from Martha shall be taken away the part which she chose, because the labour of multiplicity passes, and the charity of unity remains[^p275-6] ». And this is the cause why the part of Mary is simply better and more choiceworthy, because the contemplative life begins here and is finished in the future. Which is designated in the figure of John, according to what is said in John, the last chapter[^p275-7]: « So I will have him to remain », as if contemplation, begun, should remain « till I come », to be perfected when I shall have come. And because it is more abiding, therefore it is better, as the Apostle says of charity in First Corinthians thirteen[^p275-8]: « Charity never falleth away »; and from this he concludes that charity is the greater. So also of the contemplative life; whence Bede in the Gloss: « He who begins to burn here, when he shall have seen him whom he loves, will kindle the more in love »; Isaias thirty-one[^p275-9]: « Whose fire is in Sion, and his furnace in Jerusalem ». Whence, as far as it is of itself, it is to be chosen before others, according to that of Second Corinthians four: « While we contemplate not the things which are seen, but the things which are not seen. For the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal ».

75. On account of this the *contemplative* life is simply more to be desired, as that which is better and of itself to be chosen before others, both because it is more secure[^p275-10], and because it is sweeter, and because it is more stable; yet *the active* is not to be despised, but in place and time is for a time to be chosen before the other, both because it is earlier, and because it is more penal, and because it is more fruitful; for it avails for itself and for others. — And this is well designated in the two wives of Jacob, Rachel namely and Lia, of whom the one designates the active life, the other the contemplative[^p275-11]. Whence the bridegroom compels the bride sometimes to go out to action, according to what is said in the Canticle two: « Let thy voice sound in my ears », etc. Whence if the question be made of *betterness,* *simply* the contemplative is better, according to what Gregory says in the sixth book of the *Morals*[^p275-12]: « Great are the merits of the active, but those of the contemplative more excellent ». For *Mary hath chosen the best part, which shall not be taken away from her.* — But if the question be made of *choiceworthiness,* sometimes *the active is to be chosen before the other,* namely by the imperfect man, who ought first to exercise himself in the field of action, or when one is bound to the works of the active by precept or by office; and therefore sometimes doubt arises in the choosing before, according to that of the Philippians one[^p275-13]: « For to me, to live is Christ; and to die is gain. And if to live in the flesh, this is to me the fruit of work, and what I shall choose I know not. But I am straitened between two: having a desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ, much more better; to abide in the flesh is necessary for you ». — Therefore spiritual men
<!-- page 276 -->
must sometimes go out, sometimes go in, sometimes ascend, sometimes descend, as Jacob saw, in Genesis twenty-eight[^p276-1].

76. Now this Gospel is customarily read on the Assumption of the Virgin, either[^p276-2] because its end most fittingly suits Mary, when it is said: *Mary hath chosen for herself the best part, which shall not be taken away from her.* For although this was said according to the letter of Mary Magdalen, yet it is more truly of the blessed Virgin Mary. Whence Bernard[^p276-3]: « *Mary hath chosen for herself the best part. The best* indeed; good is conjugal fruitfulness, but better virginal chastity, and wholly the best is virgin fruitfulness, or fruitful virginity; it is Mary's privilege, it shall not be given to another, because *it shall not be taken away from her* ». — Or also, because in this Gospel the perfection of the active and of the contemplative life is described in two sisters, both of which were in the Virgin most perfectly. For what was given to these two sisters by parts was given to Mary whole and entire. Whence Jerome[^p276-4]: « To the others it is given by parts, but into Mary the fulness of grace poured itself whole at once ». — Or, because here there is treatment of a twofold receiving of Christ: *bodily* and *spiritual: bodily* by Martha in the lodging of the outer house; *spiritual* by Mary in the lodging of the inner house. And this twofold receiving[^p276-5] was most perfectly in Mary, who received him in *the chamber of the body,* nourished and fed and brought him up and diligently served him; she received him also in *the chamber of the heart,* by seeing, believing, loving, imitating him. And from *both* of these she was blessed; whence below in the eleventh[^p276-6]: « Blessed is the womb that bore thee »; « Blessed are they who hear the word of God, and keep it ». Whence Augustine[^p276-7]: « Mary was more blessed in receiving the faith of Christ than in conceiving the flesh of Christ. For the maternal nearness would have profited Mary nothing, had she not more happily borne him in her mind than in her flesh ».

77. Or also, because here three things are determined, namely the divine *lodging,* the divine *service,* the divine *companionship;* which three were most perfectly in the Virgin Mary: *lodging* in the town, *service* in Martha and *companionship* in Mary.

Rightly the Virgin Mary, in receiving Christ, was *a town fortified with the towers of the virtues* and raised up; whose first tower was *the fortitude of strictness,* of which Canticle four[^p276-8]: « Thy neck is as the tower of David, which is built with bulwarks: a thousand bucklers hang upon it », because the Virgin Mary could be overcome by no vice. — The second tower, *the rectitude of discretion,* of which Canticle seven: « Thy nose is as the tower of Libanus, that looketh toward Damascus »; where is understood the discretion of good from evil. — The third tower was *the multitude of devotion,* of which Canticle, the last chapter: « I am a wall: and my breasts are as a tower », on account of the sweetness of devotion, in which she excelled. Whence these three towers were built by the Holy Spirit through grace upon the three powers of the soul: the first upon *the irascible,* the second upon *the rational* and the third upon *the concupiscible.* — And from these the Virgin was a castle fit for receiving the beloved Son of the Father, who was the power and wisdom of the Father[^p276-9], because the Virgin was *most strong, most prudent* and *most devout.*

78. Rightly also was she *Martha* in *serving,* who served the Lord *faithfully* and *humbly* and *manfully.* So too Mary, though she was the Mother, made herself a handmaid and a serving-woman, according to that of the first chapter above[^p276-10]: « Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it done to me according to thy word ». Whence she was designated by that good woman Abigail, who, when she was sought by David in marriage, offered herself for service; First Kings twenty-five[^p276-11]: « Behold, let thy servant be a handmaid, to wash the feet of thy servants ». Such was the Virgin Mary from her exceeding humility; whence of herself she said: « He hath regarded the humility of his handmaid ». And this is what Augustine says[^p276-12]: « That Mary was the serving-woman of Christ by the showing of work and by the most rigid truth of faith, everyone who rightly understands perceives. For without doubt she stood as one serving him, who bore him in her womb, and, when he was brought forth in birth, nourished and cherished him, and, as the Gospel says, *laid him in a manger,* and, fleeing from the face of Herod, went into Egypt, and followed all his infancy with the loving affection of a mother ».

79. Rightly also was she *Mary* in *dwelling with him* or in *contemplating.* For she, like the other Mary, stood beside Christ, according to that of John nineteen[^p276-13]: « There stood by the cross of Jesus his mother, and his mother's sister, Mary of Cleophas, and Mary Magdalen ». For it was the Virgin herself who *drew most near to*
<!-- page 277 -->
*him,* therefore she most received his words and kept them for others. For above in the second[^p277-1] it is said: « Mary kept all these words ». Whence she was rightly designated by *the ark of the covenant of the Lord,* of which in Hebrews nine: « In which was a golden pot having manna », through the great *devotion of charity;* « and the rod of Aaron », through the great *rectitude of virtue;* « and the tables of the testimony », through the great *cognition of the contemplation of truth.* — And she herself also was most contemplative. Whence Bernard[^p277-2]: « The blessed Mary penetrated the most profound abyss of the divine wisdom, beyond what can be believed, so that, as far as the condition of a creature allows without personal union, she seems immersed in that light inaccessible ». And Bede[^p277-3]: « What of God did she not know, in whom the Wisdom of God lay hidden, and the body of whose womb he fitted to himself »?

80. And so it is plain how this Gospel was appropriated to the Assumption of the Virgin, not by human invention, but by divine inspiration[^p277-4], because the Holy Spirit enclosed in it the commendation of the Virgin as to the multitude of her prerogatives; for the keeping of which he subjoins at the end: *Mary hath chosen the best part, which shall not be taken away from her.* For Mary chose the best part both in grace and in glory; in which is enclosed the Virgin's praise, perfect and proper to her; for as Jerome says[^p277-5]: « As in comparison with God no one is good, so in comparison with the Mother of the Lord no woman is found perfect, however eminent in virtues she be proved ». Therefore among women she alone is *the best* through superabundance of every kind, by reason of which[^p277-6] « she seemed to have neither the like of her before nor one following after ».
