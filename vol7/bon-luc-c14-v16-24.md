# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XIV, Vers. 16-24
## *At ipse dixit ei: Homo quidam etc. Postquam informavit invitatos ad convivium nuptiale et invitantes ad convivium familiare; hic informat invitandos ad convivium aeternale per introductionem parabolae*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 370 -->
36. (Vers. 16.). *At ipse dixit ei: Homo quidam* etc. Postquam informavit invitatos ad convivium nuptiale et invitantes ad convivium familiare; hic *informat invitandos ad convivium aeternale* per introductionem parabolae[^p370-1]. In hac autem parabola tria introducuntur: quorum primum est *invitatio plurimorum;* secundum est *excusatio invitatorum,* de qua ibi[^p370-2]: *Et coeperunt simul omnes excusare;* tertium est *repulsio contemptorum,* ibi: *Et reversus servus* etc.

Circa autem *invitationem plurimorum* tria introducuntur ab Evangelista, scilicet *praeparatio refectionis aeternae, vocatio multitudinis ad fidem, excitatio eiusdem ad devotionem.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *praeparationem refectionis aeternae* dicit: *Homo quidam fecit coenam magnam,* id est, Christus praeparavit refectionem aeternam[^p370-3]. Nam Christus dicitur *homo quidam* singulariter, conceptus sine semine virili, natus sine dolore Matris, immunis ab omni peccato et mortuus pro hominum peccato; de quo homine in Psalmo[^p370-4]: « Homo natus est in ea, et ipse fundavit eam Altissimus ». Hic *fecit coenam,* quia praeparavit refectionem summam et ultimam; unde Glossa[^p370-5]: « Hoc convivium non *prandium,* sed *coena* dicitur, quia post *prandium,* de quo Matthaeus agit, *coena* restat, et post *coenam* nullum convivium restat ». De hac coena Apocalypsis decimo nono[^p370-6]: « Beati, qui ad coenam nuptiarum Agni vocati sunt ». Haec coena *magna* dicitur, quia *inaestimabilis;* unde Genesis decimo quinto: « Ego merces tua magna nimis »; quia *interminabilis;* Baruch tertio: « O Israel, quam magna est domus Domini et ingens locus possessionis eius! Magnus et non habens finem, excelsus et immensus ». Ideo autem aestimari non potest, quia tam cibus, quam ille qui ministrat, Deus est et bonum summum; infra vigesimo secundo[^p370-7]: « Ego dispono vobis, sicut disposuit mihi Pater, regnum, ut edatis et bibatis super mensam meam in regno meo »; de quo in Psalmo: « Inebriabuntur ab ubertate domus tuae, et torrente voluptatis tuae potabis eos ».

37. Secundo quantum ad *vocationem multitudinis ad fidem* subdit: *Et vocavit multos,* ad fidem, quia de omni statu et gente et aetate, secundum illud Ioelis secundo[^p370-8]: « Sanctificate ieiunium, vocate coetum, congregate populum, sanctificate ecclesiam, coadunate senes et congregate parvulos et sugentes ubera ». Qui sic vocantur per consequens ad aeternam refectionem vocantur; unde primae Petri quinto: « Deus autem gratiae, qui vocavit nos in aeternam gloriam in Christo Iesu, ipse perficiet » etc. Ideo autem *vocat multos,* ut ostendat summam liberalitatem et benignitatem; sed ideo *paucos eligit,* ut ostendat iudicii severitatem; propter quod Matthaei vigesimo[^p370-9]: « Multi sunt vocati, pauci vero electi ». Unde haec vocatio multitudinis comparatur captioni sagenae, quae capit ex omni genere piscium; Matthaei decimo tertio: « Simile est regnum caelorum sagenae missae in mare et ex omni genere piscium congreganti » etc.

38. (Vers. 17.). Tertio quantum ad *excitationem eiusdem multitudinis ad devotionem* subdit: *Et misit servum suum hora coenae dicere invitatis, ut venirent.* « *Servus* iste, ut dicit Glossa[^p370-10], est ordo praedicantium », qui, cum sint multi, intelliguntur tamen nomine *unius servi* pro unitate officii; de qua missione Proverbiorum nono: « Misit ancillas suas, ut vocarent ad arcem et ad moenia civitatis ». De hoc servo Isaiae quadragesimo nono: « Et nunc haec dicit Dominus, formans me ex utero servum sibi: Parum est, ut sis mihi servus ad suscitandas tribus Iacob et faeces Israel convertendas. Dedi te in lucem gentium, ut sis salus mea usque ad extremum terrae ». — *Hora coenae* est finis saeculi, hoc est tempus sextae aetatis, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios decimo[^p370-11]: « Nos sumus, in quos fines saeculorum devenerunt »; et primae Ioannis secundo: Audistis, quia « novissima hora est ». — Hi servi dicunt invitatis, ut *veniant ad coenam; veniant,* inquam, per *internum desiderium et devotionem,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p370-12]: « Sitivit anima mea ad Deum, fontem vivum, quando veniam et apparebo ante faciem Dei »? Hi servi Dei debent *praedicare,* secundum illud Isaiae secundo[^p370-13]: « Venite, ascendamus

<!-- page 371 -->
ad montem Dei et ad domum Dei Iacob, et docebit nos » etc., quia, secundum illud Apocalypsis ultimo, « et qui audit dicat: Veni ».

39. Et quia nihil tantum excitat desiderium, ut veniat ad Deum, quantum consideratio illorum praemiorum; ideo subdit: *Quia iam parata sunt omnia. Parata* namque sunt *habitacula;* Ioannis decimo quarto[^p371-1]: « In domo Patris mei mansiones multae sunt. Si quo minus, dixissem vobis, quia vado parare vobis locum. Et si abiero et praeparavero vobis locum, iterum venio et accipio vos ad me ipsum ». — Item, praeparata sunt *gaudia;* primae ad Corinthios secundo[^p371-2]: « Oculus non vidit, et auris non audivit, nec in cor hominis ascendit, quae praeparavit Deus his qui diligunt illum »; quod acceptum est de Isaiae sexagesimo quarto: Oculus non vidit, Deus, absque te, quae praeparasti exspectantibus te ». — Item, praeparata est *mensa;* Psalmus[^p371-3]: « Parasti in conspectu meo mensam adversus eos qui tribulant me » etc. — Item, praeparata est *sponsa* cum his qui praedestinati sunt ad vitam; Apocalypsis decimo nono[^p371-4]: « Venerunt nuptiae Agni, et uxor eius praeparavit se », quia, sicut dicitur Matthaei vigesimo quinto, « quae paratae erant intraverunt cum eo ad nuptias, et clausa est ianua ».

40. (Vers. 18.). *Et coeperunt simul omnes.* Post invitationem plurimorum sequitur hic *excusatio vocatorum*[^p371-5], quia multi veniunt ad fidem, qui tamen nolunt pervenire usque ad caritatem propter vitiorum pronitatem, quam in excusationis velamen assumunt. — *Et coeperunt simul omnes excusare;* Glossa[^p371-6]: « Excusat omnis, qui plus terrena quam caelestia diligit, etiam si ad caelestia se tendere dicat ». Quoniam ergo triplex est radix omnis peccati, quae nos retrahit a caritate Christi[^p371-7]; ideo hic tria genera hominum citationem huius reprobantium introducit, secundum triformem excusationem. Quarum prima
venit ex *ambitione superbiae;* secunda, ex *sollicitudine avaritiae;* tertia vero, ex *libidine lasciviae.*

41. Primo igitur quantum ad primam excusationem, quae venit ex *ambitione superbiae,* dicit: *Primus dixit illi*[^p371-8]*: Villam emi.* Iste *primus* est superbus, qui semper vult obtinere primatum; Ecclesiastici decimo: « Initium superbiae hominis apostatare a Deo, quoniam ab eo qui fecit illum, recessit cor eius, quoniam initium omnis peccati superbia ». Huius est *villam emere,* quia nihil aliud quaerit quam dominari et praeesse. Unde Augustinus[^p371-9]: « In empta villa dominatio notatur et superbia. Habere enim villam, homines sibi subdere superbiae vitium primum notat ». Hanc *emptionem* facit superbus dando animam in servitutem diaboli, quia, Iob quadragesimo primo[^p371-10], « omne sublime videt, et ipse est rex super omnes filios superbiae ». Unde dum vult esse dominus hominum, efficitur servus vitiorum, et hoc est valde malum forum.

Unde talis fatetur se servum, cum subdit: *Et necesse habeo exire et videre illam.* Hanc *necessitatem* inducit superbia, quae facit hominem evanescere et quodam modo extra se ire; Nahum primo[^p371-11]: « Ex te exibit, cogitans contra Dominum malitiam, mente pertractans praevaricationem ». — Et quia vitium palliat se sub *pallio virtutis,* quia superbus vult videri humilis; ideo subdit: *Rogo te, habe me excusatum;* Glossa[^p371-12]: « Roga pro me, quia homo peccator sum »; se ipsum humiliat in voce, cum superbiat in corde. Unde Gregorius: « Dum dicit: *Rogo te, habe me excusatum,* humilitas sonat in voce; dum autem *venire contemnit,* superbia est in actione ». Tales designantur per illos, de quibus dicitur Matthaei vigesimo septimo[^p371-13]: « Genu flexo ante eum, illudebant ei »; et de huiusmodi dicitur Ecclesiastici decimo nono: « Est qui nequiter se humiliat, et interiora eius plena sunt dolo ». Et de talibus dicit

<!-- page 372 -->
Propheta in Psalmo[^p372-1]: « Non declines cor meum in verba malitiae ad excusandas excusationes in peccatis ». Et ideo e contrario dicitur de viro iusto Proverbiorum decimo octavo: « Iustus prior accusator est sui ».

42. (Vers. 19.). Secundo quantum ad *secundam excusationem,* quae venit ex *sollicitudine avaritiae,* subdit: *Et alter dixit: Iuga boum emi quinque.* Sollicitudo namque avaritiae facit, quod quis non quaerit nisi terrena tractare; ideo labori boum comparatur, quorum est terram sulcare et exercere. Et dicuntur *quinque iuga boum* propter quinque sensus conversos ad haec terrena. Unde Glossa[^p372-2]: « Haec *iuga boum* dicuntur, quia per hos terrena reguntur; boves enim terram versant ». Dici etiam possunt *iuga,* quia collum ligant et cervicem domant et totum deprimunt in terram et animam vendunt pro terra; Ecclesiastici decimo[^p372-3]: « Nihil est iniquius quam amare pecuniam; hic enim animam suam venalem habet, quoniam in vita sua proiecit intima sua ». Quoniam enim, sicut dicitur Matthaei sexto, « ubi est thesaurus tuus, ibi est et cor tuum »; et thesaurus avari est extra se, scilicet in terra: ideo intima hominis avari proiecta sunt in terra. Unde necesse est, quod iugo servitutis opprimatur, quia vendidit semetipsum; unde Ecclesiastici vigesimo septimo[^p372-4]: « Inter medium emptionis et venditionis angustiabitur peccatis ». — Reddit enim avaritia sollicitum et curiosum, ideo subdit: *Et eo probare illa,* quia continuam curam habet de superfluis. Unde Glossa[^p372-5]: « Recte per quinque sensus curiositas designatur, quae, dum exterius investigat vitam proximi, sua intima nescit; et curiosi animus quanto est peritus alieni, tanto ignarus fit sui ». — Et nota, quod caritas non habet nisi *iugum unum,* quia omnem sollicitudinem reducit ad unum, secundum illud supra decimo[^p372-6]: « Unum est necessarium »; et Michaeae sexto: « Sollicitum ambulare cum Deo tuo » etc.; et Matthaei undecimo: « Iugum meum suave est » etc.; et Threnorum tertio: « Bonum est viro, cum portaverit iugum Domini ab adolescentia
sua ». Sed curiositas habet *quinque,* quantum ad universitatem sensibilium, circa quae versatur; unde Ecclesiastae septimo[^p372-7]: « Deus fecit hominem rectum, et ipse miscuit se infinitis quaestionibus ». — Et quoniam vitium cupiditatis et avaritiae palliat se sub *specie providentiae,* ideo subdit: *Rogo te, habe me excusatum.* Sed talibus potest dici illud Ieremiae secundo[^p372-8]: « Quid niteris bonas ostendere vias tuas ad quaerendam dilectionem, quae insuper malitias tuas docuisti vias tuas plurimas, et in alis tuis inventus est sanguis animarum pauperum et innocentium »?

43. (Vers. 20.). Tertio quantum ad *tertiam excusationem,* quae venit ex *libidine lasciviae,* subdit: *Et alius dixit: Uxorem duxi, et ideo non possum venire.* Haec *uxor* est concupiscentia carnis, de qua Ecclesiastae septimo[^p372-9]: « Inveni amariorem morte mulierem, quae laqueus venatorum est et sagena cor eius ». Haec dicitur *uxor* propter vehementem libidinis adhaesionem; primae ad Corinthios sexto: « An nescitis, quia qui adhaeret mulieri unum corpus efficitur? Erunt enim, inquit, duo in carne una ». — Et ideo subdit: *Et ideo non possum venire.* Nec addit: *Habe me excusatum,* quia solum peccatum carnis est, quod minime palliat se sub *specie virtutis* et minime excusatur; et tamen secundum veritatem magis est excusabile propter maiorem pronitatem concupiscentiae, secundum illud ad Romanos septimo[^p372-10]: « Invenio aliam legem in membris meis, repugnantem legi mentis meae et captivantem me in servitutem peccati ». Et pro tanto dicit: *Non possum venire,* quia, sicut dicit Bernardus[^p372-11], « sicut ignis et aqua simul esse non possunt, sic spirituales deliciae et carnales in eodem se non compatiuntur ». Mentitur tamen iste, quia talis, etsi sit concupiscentiae servus, potest facere, quo facto habeat gratiam, per quam habeat *posse* domandi concupiscentiam. Unde ad Romanos septimo[^p372-12]: « Infelix ego homo! quis me liberabit de corpore mortis huius »? Et respondet continuo: « Gratia Dei per Iesum Christum Dominum nostrum », quia, ad Philippenses secundo, « Deus est, qui operatur in vobis velle et perficere

<!-- page 373 -->
pro bona voluntate ». — Et nota hic, quod licet uxor licite habeatur, tamen propter abusum et propter vitium coniunctum per *rem bonam* intelligitur *malum.* Propter quod et Apostolus dicit primae ad Corinthios septimo[^p373-1]: « Reliquum est, ut qui habent uxores sint tanquam non habentes »; sicut et Glossa dicit, quod « multi non propter fecunditatem, sed propter desideria carnis uxores ducunt. Ideo per rem istam carnalis voluptas designatur »; unde non est inconveniens, quod *uxor* ratione *vinculi sacramentalis* designat Ecclesiam, sponsam Christi[^p373-2], et ratione *libidinis* designare possit concupiscentiam carnalis voluptatis.

44. (Vers. 21.). *Et reversus servus* etc. Post invitationem plurimorum et excusationem invitatorum sequitur hic tertio *repulsio contemptorum*[^p373-3]. Circa quam tria introducuntur ab Evangelista, scilicet *vocatio indigentium, compulsio negligentium* et *repulsio contemnentium.*

Primo quantum ad *vocationem indigentium,* quae ortum habuit ex contemptu aliorum, dicit: *Et reversus servus, nuntiavit haec domino suo,* videlicet vocatorum duritiam et rebellionem. Hanc servus nuntiat, quando ordo praedicatorum[^p373-4] non quaerit proprium commodum, sed honorem divinum, ut, sicut missus est a Deo per commissionem auctoritatis, sic revertatur per intentionem puritatis. Et de talibus dicitur Ezechielis primo[^p373-5]: « Animalia ibant et revertebantur in similitudinem fulguris coruscantis »; unde Iob trigesimo octavo: « Nunquid mittes fulgura, et ibunt et revertentia dicent tibi: Adsumus »? ibi Glossa: « *Vadunt fulgura,* cum praedicatores miraculis coruscant; *revertentia dicunt: Adsumus,* cum non sibi, sed Deo tribuunt, quidquid se fortiter egisse cognoscunt ». Vel, *revertuntur* ad Deum per gratiarum actionem, secundum illud Ecclesiastae primo[^p373-6]: « Ad locum, unde exeunt, flumina revertuntur, ut iterum fluant ». Et quia non possunt gratias agere de audientium rebellione, sed potius gemere ex detestatione culpae; ideo dicuntur *Domino nuntiare;* sicut et de Apostolis dicitur Actuum quarto[^p373-7],
quod, cum molestiam paterentur a Iudaeis, « levaverunt vocem unanimiter ad Deum et dixerunt: Tu, Domine, qui fecisti omnia, qui dixisti per os patris nostri David: Quare fremuerunt gentes » etc., « nunc, Domine, respice in minas eorum et da servis tuis cum omni fiducia loqui verbum tuum »; et sequitur, quod, « cum orassent, motus est locus »; in quo intelligitur commotio irae Dei contra rebellionem auditorum.

45. Propter quod et subdit: *Tunc iratus paterfamilias dixit servo suo: Exi cito in plateas et vicos civitatis,* id est ad publicam praedicationem, secundum illud Proverbiorum primo[^p373-8]: « Sapientia foris praedicat et in plateis dat vocem suam, in capite turbarum clamitat, in foribus portarum urbis profert verba sua ». *Exitus iste* est ad exercitium praedicationis, secundum illud Matthaei decimo tertio[^p373-9]: « Exiit qui seminat seminare semen suum ». — Et quia, repulsis superbis, acceptantur humiles; ideo subdit: *Et pauperes ac debiles et caecos et claudos introduc huc;* quod secundum Glossas habet[^p373-10] exponi tripliciter: uno modo, ut ista sonent in *defectus naturales,* ut *pauperes,* qui carent rebus; *debiles,* qui carent viribus; *caecos,* qui habent defectum in visu; *claudos,* qui habent defectum in progressu, quia ad litteram etiam tales admittuntur ad regnum caelorum; primae ad Corinthios primo[^p373-11]: « Respicite, fratres, vocationem vestram, quia non multi sapientes secundum carnem, non multi potentes, non multi nobiles, sed quae stulta sunt mundi elegit Deus, ut confundat sapientes; et infirma mundi elegit Deus, ut confundat fortia; et ignobilia mundi et contemptibilia elegit Deus, et ea quae non sunt, ut ea quae sunt, destrueret, ut non glorietur in conspectu eius omnis caro ».

46. Alio modo legitur, ut sonent in *defectus vitiosos,* ut sint *pauperes* propter defectum *gratiae; debiles,* propter defectum *virtutis; caeci,* propter defectum *prudentiae;* et *claudi,* propter defectum *bonae voluntatis.* Et tamen hos eligit et vocat Deus, quia, Matthaei nono[^p373-12], « non veni vocare iustos, sed

<!-- page 374 -->
peccatores ad poenitentiam »; unde et Matthaei vigesimo primo dictum est Pharisaeis: « Publicani et meretrices praecedent vos in regnum Dei ».

47. Tertio modo legitur, ut sonent in *defectus virtuosos,* ut dicantur *pauperes* et *debiles* quantum ad propriam reputationem; et illos quidem intelligimus *pauperes,* qui non confidunt de nimia *opulentia;* de quibus Proverbiorum decimo tertio[^p374-1]: « Est quasi pauper, cum in multis divitiis sit »; illos vero *debiles,* qui non confidunt de sua *virtute;* primae ad Corinthios primo: « Quod infirmum est Dei fortius est hominibus »; *caecos,* qui non confidunt de sua *scientia,* id est, qui reputant se caecos; unde Ioannis nono[^p374-2]: « Si caeci essetis, peccatum non haberetis; nunc autem, quia dicitis, quia videmus; peccatum vestrum manet »; *claudos,* qui non confidunt de sua *rectitudine;* unde Iacob, postquam vidit Dominum, dicitur claudicasse, Genesis trigesimo secundo. Tales Dominus introducit, scilicet *pauperes* per contemptum terrenae opulentiae; Psalmus[^p374-3]: « Parcet pauperi et inopi » etc.; *debiles* per contemptum propriae confidentiae; Isaiae quadragesimo: « Qui sperant in Domino mutabunt fortitudinem »; *caecos* per contemptum propriae industriae; Ioannis nono: « In iudicium ego veni in hunc mundum, ut qui non vident, videant etc.; *claudos* per contemptum propriae iustitiae; Isaiae trigesimo quinto: « Tunc saliet sicut cervus claudus, et aperta erit lingua mutorum ». — Et pro his omnibus dicitur supra septimo[^p374-4]: « Caeci vident, claudi ambulant, surdi audiunt, mortui resurgunt, pauperes evangelizantur »; quia ipsi ad vitam introducuntur et vocantur. — Et hic ultimus intellectus videtur magis consonus praecedentibus, quia « Deus superbis resistit ».

48. (Vers. 22.). Secundo quantum ad *compulsionem negligentium,* quae sequitur vocationem humilium, subdit: *Et ait servus: Domine, factum est, ut imperasti, et adhuc locus est;* quia non tot sunt *humiles,* quot praedestinati ad vitam, non tot sunt ad vitam aeternam *voluntarii,* quot sunt a Deo praedestinati.

49. (Vers. 23.). Ideo subdit: *Et ait dominus servo: Exi in vias et sepes.* Per *vias et sepes,* quae sunt extra urbem, intelliguntur peccatores, qui sunt extra ecclesiasticam unitatem, et maxime gentiles.
Unde Gregorius[^p374-5]: « Cum de *vicis et plateis* vocat, Iudaeos significat, qui tenere Legem sub urbana conversatione noverant; cum autem de *viis et sepibus* vocat, agrestem populum gentium significat ». Et per hanc eandem rationem *ceteri peccatores* possunt intelligi et maxime pigri, qui ad bonum trahuntur inviti. — Unde subdit: *Et compelle intrare,* comminatione scilicet aeternorum suppliciorum et ostensione praesentium; quia, sicut dicit Gregorius[^p374-6], « mala, quae nos hic premunt, ad Deum ire compellunt »; propter quod in Psalmo: « Cum occideret eos, quaerebant eum ». Unde hos compellit servus Dei, quando eos terret comminatione severitatis iudicii, secundum illud secundae ad Timotheum quarto: « Praedica verbum, insta opportune, importune, argue, obsecra, increpa » etc. — Et *ratio* huius est complementum numeri electorum; unde subdit: *Ut impleatur domus mea,* Glossa[^p374-7]: « Numero praedestinatorum fidelium, qui numerus inexpletus non remanebit », de quo numero Apocalypsis sexto: « Adhuc sustinete modicum tempus, donec impleatur numerus fratrum vestrorum ». Haec ergo *domus impletur,* quando salvatur universitas electorum, de quibus Apocalypsis septimo[^p374-8]: « Post haec vidi turbam magnam, quam dinumerare nemo poterat »; et Apocalypsis decimo quarto: « Vidi supra montem Sion Agnum stantem et cum eo » etc.; et in Cantico Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo[^p374-9]: « Statuit terminos populorum iuxta numerum filiorum Israel »; ubi dicit Gregorius, quod « tot salvabuntur homines, quot Angeli steterunt ». Quidquid tamen sit de hoc, certum est, quod *perfectus* erit numerus electorum; in cuius figuram dicitur Deuteronomii trigesimo tertio[^p374-10]: « Apparuit de monte Pharan, et cum eo Sanctorum millia ».

50. (Vers. 24.). Tertio quantum ad *repulsionem contemnentium* subiungit: *Dico autem vobis, quod nemo virorum illorum, qui vocati sunt,* et se excusaverunt[^p374-11], *gustabit coenam meam;* quia nullus ad coenam illam pertingit nisi humilis; Matthaei decimo octavo: « Nisi conversi fueritis et efficiamini sicut parvuli, non intrabitis in regnum caelorum »; unde et in Psalmo dicitur: « Quam magna multitudo dulcedinis tuae, Domine, quam abscondisti timentibus te »! *Timentibus* enim Dominus reservat,

<!-- page 375 -->
sed superbos repellit. « Si enim peccantibus angelis non pepercit Deus, sed rudentibus inferni » etc., sicut dicitur secundae Petri secundo[^p375-1]; et post: « Novit Dominus pios de tentatione eripere, iniquos vero reservat cruciandos »; et propter hoc ad Romanos undecimo[^p375-2]: « Noli altum sapere, sed time. Si enim Deus naturalibus ramis non pepercit, ne forte nec tibi parcat ». Et ideo dicit ad Hebraeos secundo: « Quapropter oportet nos diligentius observare, ne forte pereffluamus. Si enim qui per Angelos dictus est sermo factus est firmus, quomodo nos effugiemus, si tantam neglexerimus salutem »?

36. (Vers. 16.). *But he said to him: A certain man* etc. After he formed those invited to the marriage banquet and those who invite to the familiar banquet, here he *forms those to be invited to the eternal banquet* through the introduction of a parable[^p370-1]. Now in this parable three things are brought in: of which the first is *the invitation of very many;* the second is *the excuse of those invited,* of which there[^p370-2]: *And they began all at once to make excuse;* the third is *the repulse of the despisers,* there: *And the servant returning* etc.

Now concerning *the invitation of very many* three things are brought in by the Evangelist, namely *the preparation of the eternal refection, the calling of the multitude to faith, the rousing of the same to devotion.*

First, then, as to *the preparation of the eternal refection* he says: *A certain man made a great supper,* that is, Christ prepared the eternal refection[^p370-3]. For Christ is called *a certain man* singularly, conceived without virile seed, born without his Mother's pain, immune from all sin and dead for the sin of men; of which man in the Psalm[^p370-4]: « A man is born in her, and the Highest himself hath founded her ». He *made a supper,* because he prepared the highest and last refection; whence the Gloss[^p370-5]: « This banquet is called not a *dinner,* but a *supper,* because after the *dinner,* of which Matthew treats, the *supper* remains, and after the *supper* no banquet remains ». Of this supper the Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter[^p370-6]: « Blessed are they that are called to the marriage supper of the Lamb ». This supper is called *great,* because it is *inestimable;* whence Genesis, the fifteenth chapter: « I am thy reward exceeding great »; because it is *interminable;* Baruch, the third chapter: « O Israel, how great is the house of the Lord, and how vast is the place of his possession! It is great, and hath no end: it is high and immense ». Now it cannot be estimated for this reason, that both the food and he who ministers it are God and the highest good; below, the twenty-second chapter[^p370-7]: « I dispose to you, as my Father hath disposed to me, a kingdom; that you may eat and drink at my table, in my kingdom »; of which in the Psalm: « They shall be inebriated with the plenty of thy house; and thou shalt make them drink of the torrent of thy pleasure ».

37. Second, as to *the calling of the multitude to faith* he subjoins: *And he called many,* to faith, because from every state and nation and age, according to that of Joel, the second chapter[^p370-8]: « Sanctify a fast, call a solemn assembly, gather together the people, sanctify the church, assemble the ancients, gather together the little ones, and them that suck at the breasts ». Those who are so called are consequently called to the eternal refection; whence the first of Peter, the fifth chapter: « But the God of grace, who hath called us unto eternal glory in Christ Jesus, he will perfect » etc. Now he *calls many* for this reason, that he may show the highest liberality and kindness; but he *chooses few* for this reason, that he may show the severity of the judgement; on account of which Matthew, the twentieth chapter[^p370-9]: « Many are called, but few chosen ». Hence this calling of the multitude is compared to the catch of a net, which takes of every kind of fishes; Matthew, the thirteenth chapter: « The kingdom of heaven is like to a net cast into the sea, and gathering together of every kind of fishes » etc.

38. (Vers. 17.). Third, as to *the rousing of the same multitude to devotion* he subjoins: *And he sent his servant at the hour of supper to say to them that were invited, that they should come.* « This *servant,* as the Gloss says[^p370-10], is the order of preachers », who, though they are many, are yet understood under the name of *one servant* for the unity of the office; of which sending Proverbs, the ninth chapter: « She hath sent her maids to call to the tower, and to the walls of the city ». Of this servant Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter: « And now saith the Lord, that formed me from the womb to be his servant: It is a small thing that thou shouldst be my servant to raise up the tribes of Jacob, and to convert the dregs of Israel. I have given thee for the light of the Gentiles, that thou mayst be my salvation even to the farthest part of the earth ». — *The hour of supper* is the end of the world, that is, the time of the sixth age, according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the tenth chapter[^p370-11]: « We are they upon whom the ends of the world are come »; and the first of John, the second chapter: You have heard that « it is the last hour ». — These servants say to those invited that *they should come to the supper; that they should come,* I say, through *interior desire and devotion,* according to that of the Psalm[^p370-12]: « My soul hath thirsted after God, the living fountain; when shall I come and appear before the face of God »? These servants of God ought to *preach,* according to that of Isaias, the second chapter[^p370-13]: « Come and let us go up

<!-- page 371 -->
to the mountain of God, and to the house of the God of Jacob, and he will teach us » etc., because, according to that of the Apocalypse, the last chapter, « and he that heareth, let him say: Come ».

39. And because nothing so rouses the desire to come to God as the consideration of those rewards; therefore he subjoins: *For now all things are ready.* For *ready* are *the dwellings;* John, the fourteenth chapter[^p371-1]: « In my Father's house there are many mansions. If not, I would have told you: because I go to prepare a place for you. And if I shall go, and prepare a place for you, I come again, and take you to myself ». — Likewise, *the joys* are prepared; the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p371-2]: « Eye hath not seen, nor ear heard, neither hath it entered into the heart of man, what things God hath prepared for them that love him »; which is taken from Isaias, the sixty-fourth chapter: The eye hath not seen, O God, besides thee, what things thou hast prepared for them that wait for thee ». — Likewise, *the table* is prepared; the Psalm[^p371-3]: « Thou hast prepared a table before me against them that afflict me » etc. — Likewise, *the bride* is prepared, with those who are predestined to life; the Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter[^p371-4]: « The marriage of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath prepared herself », because, as is said in Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter, « they that were ready went in with him to the marriage, and the door was shut ».

40. (Vers. 18.). *And they began all at once.* After the invitation of very many there follows here *the excuse of the called*[^p371-5], because many come to faith who yet will not attain as far as charity on account of the proneness of the vices, which they take up as the veil of an excuse. — *And they began all at once to make excuse;* the Gloss[^p371-6]: « Every one makes excuse who loves earthly things more than heavenly, even if he say that he tends to heavenly things ». Since therefore the root of all sin, which draws us back from the charity of Christ, is threefold[^p371-7]; therefore he here brings in three kinds of men rejecting the summons of this one, according to a threefold excuse. Of which the first
comes from *the ambition of pride;* the second, from *the solicitude of avarice;* but the third, from *the lust of wantonness.*

41. First, then, as to the first excuse, which comes from *the ambition of pride,* he says: *The first said to him*[^p371-8]*: I have bought a farm.* This *first* is the proud man, who always wills to hold the primacy; Ecclesiasticus, the tenth chapter: « The beginning of the pride of man is to fall off from God, because his heart hath departed from him that made him, because pride is the beginning of all sin ». His it is *to buy a farm,* because he seeks nothing else than to lord it and to be over others. Whence Augustine[^p371-9]: « In the farm bought, lordship is marked, and pride. For to have a farm, to subject men to oneself, marks the first vice, that of pride ». This *buying* the proud man makes by giving his soul into the servitude of the devil, because, Job, the forty-first chapter[^p371-10], « he beholdeth every high thing, and he is king over all the children of pride ». Hence while he wills to be lord of men, he is made the servant of vices, and this is a very bad bargain.

Hence such a one confesses himself a servant, when he subjoins: *And I must needs go out and see it.* This *necessity* pride induces, which makes a man vanish and in a certain manner go outside himself; Nahum, the first chapter[^p371-11]: « Out of thee shall go forth one imagining malice against the Lord, contriving prevarication in his mind ». — And because the vice cloaks itself under *the cloak of virtue,* since the proud man wills to seem humble; therefore he subjoins: *I pray thee, hold me excused;* the Gloss[^p371-12]: « Pray for me, because I am a sinful man »; he humbles himself in voice, while he is proud in heart. Whence Gregory: « While he says: *I pray thee, hold me excused,* humility sounds in the voice; but while he *disdains to come,* pride is in the action ». Such are designated by those of whom it is said in Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p371-13]: « Bowing the knee before him, they mocked him »; and of such it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the nineteenth chapter: « There is one that humbleth himself wickedly, and his interior is full of deceit ». And of such says

<!-- page 372 -->
the Prophet in the Psalm[^p372-1]: « Incline not my heart to evil words, to make excuses in sins ». And therefore on the contrary it is said of the just man in Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter: « The just is first accuser of himself ».

42. (Vers. 19.). Second, as to *the second excuse,* which comes from *the solicitude of avarice,* he subjoins: *And another said: I have bought five yoke of oxen.* For the solicitude of avarice brings it about that one seeks to handle nothing but earthly things; therefore it is compared to the labour of oxen, whose part it is to furrow and work the earth. And they are called *five yoke of oxen* on account of the five senses turned to these earthly things. Whence the Gloss[^p372-2]: « These are called *yoke of oxen,* because through these earthly things are ruled; for oxen turn over the earth ». They can also be called *yokes,* because they bind the neck and tame the nape and press the whole down to the earth and sell the soul for earth; Ecclesiasticus, the tenth chapter[^p372-3]: « There is not a more wicked thing than to love money; for such a one setteth even his own soul to sale, because while he liveth he hath cast away his inmost parts ». For since, as is said in Matthew, the sixth chapter, « where thy treasure is, there is thy heart also »; and the treasure of the avaricious man is outside himself, namely in the earth: therefore the inmost parts of the avaricious man are cast away into the earth. Hence it is necessary that he be weighed down by the yoke of servitude, because he has sold himself; whence Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p372-4]: « In the midst of buying and selling he shall be straitened with sins ». — For avarice renders one solicitous and curious; therefore he subjoins: *And I go to try them,* because he has continual care about superfluous things. Whence the Gloss[^p372-5]: « Rightly is curiosity designated by the five senses, which, while it searches out the life of the neighbour outwardly, knows not its own inmost things; and the mind of the curious man, the more it is skilled in what is another's, the more ignorant it becomes of itself ». — And note that charity has but *one yoke,* because it brings back all solicitude to one, according to that above, the tenth chapter[^p372-6]: « One thing is necessary »; and Micheas, the sixth chapter: « To walk solicitously with thy God » etc.; and Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « My yoke is sweet » etc.; and Lamentations, the third chapter: « It is good for a man, when he hath borne the yoke of the Lord from his youth
». But curiosity has *five,* as to the universality of the sensibles about which it is busied; whence Ecclesiastes, the seventh chapter[^p372-7]: « God made man right, and he hath entangled himself with an infinity of questions ». — And since the vice of cupidity and avarice cloaks itself under *the appearance of providence,* therefore he subjoins: *I pray thee, hold me excused.* But to such can be said that of Jeremias, the second chapter[^p372-8]: « Why dost thou endeavour to shew thy ways good to seek love, thou who hast moreover taught thy malices to be thy very many ways, and in thy skirts is found the blood of the souls of the poor and innocent »?

43. (Vers. 20.). Third, as to *the third excuse,* which comes from *the lust of wantonness,* he subjoins: *And another said: I have married a wife, and therefore I cannot come.* This *wife* is the concupiscence of the flesh, of which Ecclesiastes, the seventh chapter[^p372-9]: « I have found a woman more bitter than death, who is the snare of hunters, and her heart is a net ». She is called *a wife* on account of the vehement adhesion of lust; the first to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter: « Or know you not, that he who is joined to a woman is made one body? For they shall be, saith he, two in one flesh ». — And therefore he subjoins: *And therefore I cannot come.* Nor does he add: *Hold me excused,* because the sin of the flesh alone is the one that least cloaks itself under *the appearance of virtue* and is least excused; and yet according to the truth it is more excusable on account of the greater proneness of concupiscence, according to that to the Romans, the seventh chapter[^p372-10]: « I find another law in my members, fighting against the law of my mind, and captivating me into the servitude of sin ». And to that extent he says: *I cannot come,* because, as Bernard says[^p372-11], « as fire and water cannot be together, so spiritual and carnal delights do not suffer each other in the same man ». Yet this man lies, because such a one, although he be the servant of concupiscence, can do that by which, once done, he may have grace, through which he may have the *power* of taming concupiscence. Whence to the Romans, the seventh chapter[^p372-12]: « Unhappy man that I am! who shall deliver me from the body of this death »? And he answers at once: « The grace of God by Jesus Christ our Lord », because, to the Philippians, the second chapter, « it is God who worketh in you both to will and to accomplish

<!-- page 373 -->
according to his good will ». — And note here that, although a wife be had lawfully, yet on account of the abuse and on account of the vice joined to it *evil* is understood by *a good thing.* On account of which the Apostle too says in the first to the Corinthians, the seventh chapter[^p373-1]: « It remaineth, that they who have wives be as if they had none »; as the Gloss also says that « many marry wives not for the sake of fruitfulness, but on account of the desires of the flesh. Therefore by this thing carnal pleasure is designated »; whence it is not unfitting that *a wife,* by reason of *the sacramental bond,* designates the Church, the bride of Christ[^p373-2], and by reason of *lust* can designate the concupiscence of carnal pleasure.

44. (Vers. 21.). *And the servant returning* etc. After the invitation of very many and the excuse of those invited there follows here, third, *the repulse of the despisers*[^p373-3]. Concerning which three things are brought in by the Evangelist, namely *the calling of the needy, the compelling of the negligent* and *the repulse of those who despise.*

First, as to *the calling of the needy,* which had its rise from the contempt of the others, he says: *And the servant returning, told these things to his lord,* namely the hardness and rebellion of the called. This the servant tells when the order of preachers[^p373-4] seeks not its own advantage, but the divine honour, that, as it was sent by God through the commission of authority, so it may return through the intention of purity. And of such it is said in Ezechiel, the first chapter[^p373-5]: « The living creatures went and returned after the likeness of flashing lightning »; whence Job, the thirty-eighth chapter: « Canst thou send lightnings, and will they go, and will they return and say to thee: Here we are »? there the Gloss: « *The lightnings go,* when preachers flash with miracles; *returning they say: Here we are,* when they attribute not to themselves, but to God, whatever they know they have done bravely ». Or, *they return* to God through thanksgiving, according to that of Ecclesiastes, the first chapter[^p373-6]: « Unto the place from whence the rivers come they return, to flow again ». And since they cannot give thanks for the rebellion of the hearers, but rather groan out of detestation of the fault; therefore they are said *to tell the Lord;* as is said of the Apostles too in the Acts, the fourth chapter[^p373-7],
that, when they suffered trouble from the Jews, « they lifted up their voice with one accord to God, and said: Thou, Lord, who madest all things, who hast said by the mouth of our father David: Why did the Gentiles rage » etc., « now, Lord, behold their threatenings, and grant unto thy servants, that with all confidence they may speak thy word »; and it follows that, « when they had prayed, the place was moved »; in which is understood the commotion of the wrath of God against the rebellion of the hearers.

45. On account of which he also subjoins: *Then the master of the house, being angry, said to his servant: Go out quickly into the streets and lanes of the city,* that is, to public preaching, according to that of Proverbs, the first chapter[^p373-8]: « Wisdom preacheth abroad, she uttereth her voice in the streets, at the head of multitudes she crieth out, in the entrance of the gates of the city she uttereth her words ». *This going out* is to the exercise of preaching, according to that of Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p373-9]: « He that soweth went out to sow his seed ». — And because, the proud being repulsed, the humble are accepted; therefore he subjoins: *And bring in hither the poor, and the feeble, and the blind, and the lame;* which according to the Glosses has[^p373-10] to be expounded in three ways: in one way, that these sound of *natural defects,* so that *the poor* are those who lack things; *the feeble,* those who lack strength; *the blind,* those who have a defect in sight; *the lame,* those who have a defect in walking, because according to the letter such too are admitted to the kingdom of heaven; the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p373-11]: « Look upon your vocation, brethren, that there are not many wise according to the flesh, not many mighty, not many noble; but the foolish things of the world hath God chosen, that he may confound the wise; and the weak things of the world hath God chosen, that he may confound the strong; and the base things of the world, and the things that are contemptible, hath God chosen, and things that are not, that he might bring to nought things that are, that no flesh should glory in his sight ».

46. In another way it is read so that they sound of *vicious defects,* so that they are *poor* on account of the defect of *grace; feeble,* on account of the defect of *virtue; blind,* on account of the defect of *prudence;* and *lame,* on account of the defect of *good will.* And yet these God chooses and calls, because, Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p373-12], « I am not come to call the just, but

<!-- page 374 -->
sinners to penance »; whence also in Matthew, the twenty-first chapter, it was said to the Pharisees: « The publicans and the harlots shall go into the kingdom of God before you ».

47. In the third way it is read so that they sound of *virtuous defects,* so that they are called *poor* and *feeble* as to their own reckoning; and those indeed we understand as *poor* who do not trust in excessive *opulence;* of whom Proverbs, the thirteenth chapter[^p374-1]: « One is as it were poor, when he hath great riches »; but those as *feeble* who do not trust in their own *virtue;* the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter: « The weakness of God is stronger than men »; *the blind,* those who do not trust in their own *knowledge,* that is, who reckon themselves blind; whence John, the ninth chapter[^p374-2]: « If you were blind, you should not have sin; but now you say: We see; your sin remaineth »; *the lame,* those who do not trust in their own *rectitude;* whence Jacob, after he saw the Lord, is said to have halted, Genesis, the thirty-second chapter. Such the Lord brings in, namely *the poor* through contempt of earthly opulence; the Psalm[^p374-3]: « He shall spare the poor and needy » etc.; *the feeble* through contempt of their own confidence; Isaias, the fortieth chapter: « They that hope in the Lord shall renew their strength »; *the blind* through contempt of their own industry; John, the ninth chapter: « For judgement I am come into this world, that they who see not may see etc.; *the lame* through contempt of their own justice; Isaias, the thirty-fifth chapter: « Then shall the lame man leap as a hart, and the tongue of the dumb shall be opened ». — And for all these it is said above, the seventh chapter[^p374-4]: « The blind see, the lame walk, the deaf hear, the dead rise again, to the poor the gospel is preached »; because they themselves are brought in and called to life. — And this last understanding seems more consonant with the preceding, because « God resisteth the proud ».

48. (Vers. 22.). Second, as to *the compelling of the negligent,* which follows the calling of the humble, he subjoins: *And the servant said: Lord, it is done as thou hast commanded, and yet there is room;* because there are not as many *humble* as there are predestined to life, there are not as many *willing* for eternal life as there are predestined by God.

49. (Vers. 23.). Therefore he subjoins: *And the lord said to the servant: Go out into the highways and hedges.* By *the highways and hedges,* which are outside the city, are understood sinners who are outside the unity of the Church, and above all the Gentiles.
Whence Gregory[^p374-5]: « When he calls from *the lanes and streets,* he signifies the Jews, who knew how to keep the Law in the converse of the city; but when he calls from *the highways and hedges,* he signifies the rustic people of the Gentiles ». And by this same reasoning *the other sinners* can be understood, and above all the slothful, who are drawn to the good unwilling. — Hence he subjoins: *And compel them to come in,* namely by the threatening of eternal torments and the showing of present ones; because, as Gregory says[^p374-6], « the evils which press us here compel us to go to God »; on account of which in the Psalm: « When he slew them, then they sought him ». Hence the servant of God compels these when he terrifies them with the threatening of the severity of the judgement, according to that of the second to Timothy, the fourth chapter: « Preach the word: be instant in season, out of season: reprove, entreat, rebuke » etc. — And the *reason* of this is the completion of the number of the elect; hence he subjoins: *That my house may be filled,* the Gloss[^p374-7]: « With the number of the predestined faithful, which number will not remain unfilled », of which number the Apocalypse, the sixth chapter: « Wait yet a little time, until the number of your brethren be filled up ». This *house,* then, *is filled* when the whole body of the elect is saved, of whom the Apocalypse, the seventh chapter[^p374-8]: « After this I saw a great multitude, which no man could number »; and the Apocalypse, the fourteenth chapter: « I saw upon mount Sion the Lamb standing, and with him » etc.; and in the Canticle of Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter[^p374-9]: « He appointed the bounds of peoples according to the number of the children of Israel »; where Gregory says that « as many men will be saved as Angels stood ». Yet whatever be the case concerning this, it is certain that the number of the elect will be *perfect;* in figure of which it is said in Deuteronomy, the thirty-third chapter[^p374-10]: « He hath appeared from mount Pharan, and with him thousands of Saints ».

50. (Vers. 24.). Third, as to *the repulse of those who despise* he subjoins: *But I say unto you, that none of those men that were called,* and excused themselves[^p374-11], *shall taste of my supper;* because no one attains to that supper but the humble; Matthew, the eighteenth chapter: « Unless you be converted, and become as little children, you shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven »; whence it is said in the Psalm too: « O how great is the multitude of thy sweetness, O Lord, which thou hast hidden for them that fear thee »! For the Lord reserves it *for those who fear,*

<!-- page 375 -->
but repels the proud. « For if God spared not the angels that sinned, but with the ropes of hell » etc., as is said in the second of Peter, the second chapter[^p375-1]; and after: « The Lord knoweth how to deliver the pious from temptation, but reserveth the wicked to be tormented »; and on account of this to the Romans, the eleventh chapter[^p375-2]: « Be not highminded, but fear. For if God hath not spared the natural branches, lest perhaps he also spare not thee ». And therefore he says to the Hebrews, the second chapter: « Wherefore we ought more diligently to observe, lest perhaps we should let them slip. For if the word spoken by Angels became steadfast, how shall we escape if we neglect so great salvation »?
