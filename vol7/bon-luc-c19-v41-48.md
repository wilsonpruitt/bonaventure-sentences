# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XIX, Vers. 41-48
## *Pietatem pontificalem promissam se habere, ostendit per quatuor*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 495 -->

#### Pietatem pontificalem promissam se habere, ostendit per quatuor.

63. (Vers. 41.). *Et ut appropinquavit* etc. Postquam descripsit, qualiter Dominus ostendit, se esse Christum promissum quantum ad potestatem regalem; hic secundo[^p495-6] ostendit id ipsum quoad *pietatem pontificalem.* Sicut enim potestas et honor decent regem, sic pietatis dolor decet pontificem, secundum illud ad Hebraeos quarto: « Non habemus pontificem, qui non possit compati infirmitatibus nostris, tentatum per omnia pro similitudine absque peccato ». Ostenditur autem huiusmodi pietas Christi magna, quia in die summi honoris sui in terris non cessabat a lacrymis per affectum compassionis. Describitur igitur pietas Christi, ut *plorans*[^p495-7] et deplorans civitatis sacerdotalis *gaudium stultum, excidium proximum* et *peccatum praeambulum.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *Christi pontificis fletum piissimum* dicit: *Et ut appropinquavit, videns civitatem, flevit super illam. Appropinquavit* non tantum corporis situ, verum etiam cordis affectu, secundum illud Iacobi quarto[^p495-8]: « Appropinquate Deo, et appropinquabit vobis »; et Deuteronomii quarto: « Quae est enim alia natio tam grandis, quae habeat deos appropinquantes sibi »? *Vidit* etiam *civitatem* non tantum oculis corporis, verum etiam aspectu pietatis; Psalmus[^p495-9]: « Quoniam prospexit de excelso sancto suo; Dominus de caelo in terram aspexit, ut audiret gemitus » etc.; et Exodi tertio: « Vidi afflictionem populi mei in Aegypto » etc. *Flevit super illam* per affectionem piissimam et compassionem affectuosam.

64. Et nota, quod Christus legitur ter flevisse pro nobis: super *Lazarum suscitandum;* Ioannis undecimo[^p495-10]: « Et lacrymatus est Iesus »; super *civitatem Ierusalem,* sicut hic; et tandem *in cruce;* ad Hebraeos quinto: « Cum clamore valido et lacrymis offerens, exauditus est pro sua reverentia »; ex quo perfectissime fuit pietas Christi manifestata ad nos. Creditur etiam flevisse *in sua infantia,* quando intravit in praesentis status miseriam, secundum quod Ecclesia de ipso cantat[^p495-11]:

Vagit infans, inter arcta conditus praesepia.

In hoc autem quadruplici fletu quatuor in nobis ostendit debere esse differentias lacrymarum et fletuum: ex *compunctione;* Psalmus[^p495-12]: « Lavabo per singulas noctes lectum meum, lacrymis meis stratum meum rigabo »; et Matthaei vigesimo sexto dicitur de Petro, quod « egressus foras, flevit amare ». — Ex
<!-- page 496 -->
*compassione;* Iob trigesimo[^p496-1]: « Flebam quondam super eo qui afflictus erat, et compatiebatur anima mea pauperi »; et Ieremiae nono: « Quis dabit capiti meo aquam et oculis meis fontem lacrymarum »? — Pro *incolatu praesentis miseriae;* Ioannis decimo sexto[^p496-2]: « Plorabitis » etc.; et Psalmus: « Heu mihi! quia incolatus meus prolongatus est ». — Pro *appetitu felicitatis aeternae;* Matthaei quinto[^p496-3]: « Beati, qui lugent, quoniam ipsi consolabuntur ». Has lacrymas debemus petere, quamdiu sumus super asellum mortalitatis nostrae. Sicut enim Christus super asinum flevit, sic et anima, quae insidet corpori misero tanquam asello, flere debet. In cuius figuram Iudicum primo[^p496-4] dicitur, quod « Axa, filia Caleb, sedens in asino suspiravit » etc.; et post subditur, quod dixit: « Terram arentem dedisti mihi, da et irriguam aquis; deditque ei Caleb irriguum superius et irriguum inferius ». — His modis lacrymandi irrigari debet paradisus conscientiae. In cuius designationem dicitur Genesis secundo[^p496-5], quod « fluvius egrediebatur de loco voluptatis ad irrigandum paradisum, qui dividitur in quatuor capita »; in quo intelligitur, quod ex pietate cordis nostri debet quadruplex lacrymarum fluvius derivari.

65. (Vers. 42.). Secundo quantum ad *regiae civitatis gaudium stultum* subdit: *Dicens: Quia, si cognovisses et tu,* per providentiam, flevisses, supple: per poenitentiam; unde Glossa[^p496-6]: « *Si cognovisses* ruinam, quae imminet, flevisses ». Et hic est defectus necessariae dictionis, sed Dominus decise loquitur ad modum dolentis et supplet ex ipso actu dolendi et flendi, ut affectus suppleat conceptum, et factum suppleat verbum: flevisses utique. — Et si quaeras: *quando? Et quidem,* hoc est certe, *in hac die tua, quae ad pacem tibi,* in qua scilicet exsultas; unde Glossa[^p496-7]: « Quae modo exsultas ». — Et si quaeras *causam,* quare exsultat et non plorat, est improvidentia. Unde subdit: *Nunc autem abscondita sunt ab oculis tuis;* Glossa[^p496-8]: « Tempore laetitiae non praevidentur futurae miseriae »; ideo ergo habebat gaudium stultum propter *considerationem diei praesentis,* de quo die dicit: *In hac die tua;* propter *obtentum praesentis pacis,* de qua addit: *Quae ad pacem tibi;* et propter *absconsionem calamitatis,* de qua subiungit: *Nunc autem abscondita sunt ab oculis tuis.* Unde Glossa[^p496-9]: « Dum carnem das voluptatibus, mala futura non prospiciens, habes bona praesentia, quae ad pacem tibi possunt esse temporalem. Et cur hoc subdit, scilicet quod non praevidet mala, quae ventura sunt? Si enim praevideret, in praesentibus prosperis laeta non fuisset ». — Et hinc est, quod *viri iusti praesentem diem* despiciunt, secundum illud Ieremiae decimo septimo[^p496-10]: « Diem hominis non desideravi, tu scis »; et vigesimo: « Maledicta dies, in qua natus sum »; et de Iob dicitur Iob tertio: « Maledixit Iob diei suae dicens: Pereat dies, in qua natus sum, et nox, in qua dictum est » etc. *Praesentem pacem* contemnunt; Psalmus[^p496-11]: « Zelavi super iniquos, pacem peccatorum videns »; et Ezechielis decimo tertio: « Prophetae deceperunt populum meum, dicentes: Pax, pax, et non erat pax ». *Futura conspiciunt et attendunt,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo[^p496-12]: « Memento novissorum et noli oblivisci ». — Sed e contrario *viri mali* non cogitant iudicium, et ideo laetantur, secundum illud Proverbiorum secundo: « Laetantur, cum male fecerint, et exsultant in rebus pessimis ». Et hoc est, quia diem istum considerant, non extremum; Iob vigesimo primo[^p496-13]: « Tenent tympanum et citharam et gaudent ad sonitum organi. Ducunt in bonis dies suos et in puncto ad inferna descendunt ». Ideo ironice dicitur Ecclesiastae undecimo[^p496-14]: « Laetare, iuvenis, in adolescentia tua, et in bonis sit cor tuum in diebus iuventutis tuae ». « Et scito, quod pro his omnibus adducet te Deus in iudicium ». Ex hoc igitur apparet illud quod dicitur Ecclesiastae septimo: « Melius est ire ad domum luctus quam ad domum convivii » etc.

66. (Vers. 43.). Tertio quantum ad *regiae civitatis excidium proximum* subdit: *Quia venient dies in te, et circumdabunt te inimici tui vallo,* per obsidionem, secundum illud Ezechielis quarto[^p496-15]: « Sume tibi laterem, et describes in eo civitatem Ierusalem. Et ordinabis adversus eam obsidionem et aedificabis munitiones et comportabis aggerem et dabis contra eam castra per gyrum et pones munitiones » etc.
<!-- page 497 -->
Hoc autem impletum est tempore Romanorum; unde Glossa[^p497-1]: « *Inimici,* scilicet Romani principes », qui ad litteram dicuntur tres aggeres fecisse contra Ierusalem ad eius captionem. — *Et circumdabunt te et coangustabunt te undique,* per obsidionis continuationem, secundum illud Ezechielis quarto[^p497-2]: « Pones sartaginem in murum ferreum inter te et civitatem et obfirmabis faciem tuam ad eam et eris in obsidionem et circumdabis eam; signum est domui Israel ». Et hoc factum est, quia, ut dicit Iosephus[^p497-3], sic fuit obsessa strictissime; ut mater comederet filium, et unus alterum devoraret. Unde impletum est illud Threnorum secundo: « Matribus suis dixerunt: Ubi est triticum et vinum? cum deficerent quasi vulnerati in plateis civitatis, cum exhalarent animas suas in sinu matrum suarum »; et infra ibidem: « Ergone comedent mulieres fructum suum, parvulos ad mensuram palmae »?

67. (Vers. 44.). *Et ad terram prosternent te et filios tuos, qui in te sunt,* per civitatis destructionem; Threnorum secundo[^p497-4]: « Cogitavit Dominus dissipare murum filiae Sion, tetendit funiculum suum et non avertit manum suam a perditione. Luxitque antemurale, et murus pariter dissipatus est ». Nec tantum ipsa dissipata, sed etiam tota gens dispersa, secundum illud Threnorum secundo: « Defixae sunt in terra portae eius; perdidit et contrivit vectes eius, reges eius et principes eius in gentibus; non est lex, et Prophetae eius non invenerunt visionem a Domino ». — *Et non relinquent in te lapidem super lapidem,* per omnimodam eversionem. Unde Glossa[^p497-5]: « Cum Ierusalem modo sit constructa extra portam, ubi Dominus crucifixus erat; apparet, illam Ierusalem ex toto fuisse destructam ». Et sic impletum est illud Threnorum secundo[^p497-6]: « Fecit Dominus quae cogitavit; complevit sermonem suum, quem praeceperat a diebus antiquis; destruxit et non pepercit et laetificavit super te inimicum tuum et exaltavit cornu hostium tuorum »; et statim subditur: « Deduc quasi torrentem lacrymas per diem et noctem » etc.

68. Hoc autem *spiritualiter* contingit contra quemlibet peccatorem *morientem temporaliter:* qui primo *circumdatur* in infirmitate, *coangustatur* in infirmitatis aggravatione, *ad terram prosternitur* in morte et prorsus *evertitur* in incineratione, secundum illud Iob septimo[^p497-7]: « Ecce, nunc in pulvere dormiam, et si mane me quaesieris, non subsistam ». — Potest etiam intelligi de peccatore ad *iudicium accedente,* qui *circumdatur* a multitudine iniquitatum accusantium; Psalmus[^p497-8]: « Quoniam circumdederunt me mala, quorum non est numerus; comprehenderunt me iniquitates meae, et non potui, ut viderem ». *Coangustatur* in districtione divinorum iudiciorum; Ecclesiastae octavo[^p497-9]: « Non est in hominis potestate prohibere spiritum, nec habet potestatem in die mortis nec sinitur quiescere ingruente bello, nec salvabit impietas impium ». Ad *terram prosternitur* in latione sententiarum; Iob vigesimo septimo[^p497-10]: « Si multiplicati fuerint filii eius, cadent in gladio », scilicet divinae sententiae. Et tandem *omnino evertitur* in acerbitate poenarum; Psalmus[^p497-11]: « Dissipata sunt ossa nostra secus infernum ». Tunc enim non relinquitur excusatio impiis; Isaiae quinto: « Auferam maceriam eius, et erit in conculcationem ». — Et de his omnibus dicitur Amos tertio[^p497-12]: « Tribulabitur et circuietur terra, et detrahetur ex te fortitudo tua, et diripientur aedes tuae, dicit Dominus ». Propter quod Anselmus: « Superius erit iudex iratus, subtus horrendum chaos, patens infernus, a dextris peccata accusantia, a sinistris infinita daemonia ad infernum trahentia, intus conscientia urens, foris mundus ardens; miser peccator sic deprehensus quo effugiet? Latere erit impossibile, apparere erit intolerabile ».

69. Quarto quantum ad *peccatum praeambulum* subiungit: *Eo quod non cognoveris tempus visitationis tuae;* et hoc propter incredulitatem; Ieremiae octavo[^p497-13]: « Milvus in caelo cognovit tempus suum, turtur et hirundo et ciconia custodierunt tempus adventus sui; populus autem meus non cognovit iudicium Domini ». Sicut igitur fides fundamentum
<!-- page 498 -->
est totius aedificii spiritualis, per quam Christus habitat in nobis, qui est fundamentum; propter quod dicitur ad Hebraeos undecimo[^p498-1], quod « fides est substantia rerum sperandarum »: sic infidelitas origo est destructionis omnium spiritualium aedificiorum. Ideo dicitur Iob quarto: « Quia nullus intelligit, in aeternum peribunt »; et ad Romanos primo: « Quia non probaverunt Deum habere in notitia, tradidit eos Deus in reprobum sensum »; unde et Iudaei, quia noluerunt in Christum credere, excaecati sunt et reprobati; et hoc fuit maximum peccatum, secundum illud Ioannis decimo sexto[^p498-2]: « Arguet mundum de peccato quidem », scilicet « quia non crediderunt in me ». Ideo Marci ultimo[^p498-3]: « Qui crediderit et baptizatus fuerit salvus erit; qui vero non crediderit condemnabitur »; immo, quod plus est, iam incipit sua damnatio; Ioannis tertio: « Qui non credit, iam iudicatus est, quia non credit in nomine unigeniti Filii eius ».

#### Aequitatem iudicialem promissam se habere, ostendit per tria.

70. (Vers. 45.). *Et ingressus in templum* etc. Postquam descripsit Evangelista, qualiter Christus se ostendit ex facto habere potestatem regalem et pietatem pontificalem, hic tertio[^p498-4] explicatur, quomodo se ostendit habere *aequitatem iudicialem.* Ad cuius declarationem tria introducuntur, scilicet *aequitas iudicis, veritas iuris* et *enormitas* sive impietas *sceleris.* In his autem tribus ostendit, se quasi syllogistice recte iudicare. *Proposita* enim veritate Scripturae sacrae, et *assumta* perversitate nequitiae Iudaicae, recte *concluditur* severitas divinae vindictae, secundum illud Ioannis decimo sexto[^p498-5]: « Arguet mundum de peccato et de iustitia et de iudicio ». Proponit enim *iustitiam,* assumit *peccatum* et concludit *iudicium.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *aequitatem iudicis* dicit: *Et ingressus in templum, coepit eiicere vendentes in illo et ementes;* in quo ostenditur aequitas iudicis, qui, licet esset mansuetus in tolerando iniuriam propriam, secundum illud Ieremiae undecimo[^p498-6]: « Et ego quasi agnus mansuetus, qui portatur ad victimam »; tamen in ulciscendo iniuriam divinam fuit severus iudex et iustus. Et quia maxima iniuria Dei est, cum dehonestatur, ubi debet honorari; ideo dicitur, quod *ingressus est in templum,* ut ibi faceret iudicium; quod fecit et in suae praedicationis *exordio,* secundum quod narratur Ioannis secundo[^p498-7] post miraculum de mutatione aquae in vinum, et in *termino* post miraculum de Lazaro suscitato, ut sic verificetur illud Psalmi[^p498-8]: « Zelus domus tuae comedit me » etc.; et Ioannis secundo dicitur, hoc tunc verificatum esse. — Et attendendum, quod Dominus statim post annuntiationem excidii Ierusalem intravit templum ad purgandum, ut ostendat, quod maxima causa destructionis eius fuit peccatum sacerdotum. Unde Glossa[^p498-9]: « Post enumerata mala ingressus templum, eiecit vendentes et ementes, per hoc innuens, quod ruina populi maxime ex culpa sacerdotum fuit; cum vendentes et ementes percutit, ostendit, unde radix perditionis processit ». Unde Michaeae tertio[^p498-10]: « Audite haec, principes domus Iacob et iudices domus Israel, qui abominamini iudicium et omnia recta pervertitis, qui aedificatis Sion in sanguinibus et Ierusalem in iniquitate. Principes eius in muneribus iudicabant, et sacerdotes eius in mercede docebant, et prophetae eius in pecunia divinabant; propter hoc Sion quasi ager arabitur, et Ierusalem quasi acervus lapidum erit, et mons templi Domini in excelsa silvarum »; et Threnorum quarto: « Propter peccata prophetarum eius et iniquitates sacerdotum eius, qui effuderunt in medio eius sanguinem iustorum ». In hoc autem, quod Dominus eiecit ementes et vendentes, ostendit, se maxime damnare in clericis avaritiam et potissime simoniam. Unde Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p498-11] dicitur, quod « cathedras vendentium columbas evertit ». Omnibus talibus dicit Petrus Actuum octavo: « Pecunia tua tecum sit in perditionem, quia existimasti, donum Dei pecunia possideri ».

71. (Vers. 46.). Secundo quantum ad *veritatem*[^p498-12] *iuris* subdit: *Dicens illis: Scriptum est: Quia*
<!-- page 499 -->
*domus mea domus orationis est*[^p499-1]. Hoc autem scriptum est Isaiae quinquagesimo sexto: « Holocausta eorum et victimae eorum placebunt mihi super altari meo, quia domus mea domus orationis vocabitur cunctis populis, ait Dominus Deus, qui congregat dispersos Israel ». Unde et David in Psalmo[^p499-2] dicit: « Domum tuam decet sanctitudo »; et iterum: « Introibo in domum tuam, adorabo ad templum sanctum tuum »; et in consecratione templi dicitur: « Sint oculi tui aperti super domum hanc die ac nocte, super domum, de qua dixisti: Erit nomen meum ibi », tertii Regum octavo[^p499-3]; et secundi Paralipomenon septimo: « Oculi mei erunt aperti, et aures meae erectae ad orationem eius qui adoraverit in loco isto. Elegi enim et sanctificavi locum istum, ut sit ibi nomen meum in sempiternum ». Et ideo dicebant sacerdotes primi Machabaeorum septimo[^p499-4]: « Tu, Domine, elegisti domum istam ad invocandum nomen tuum in ea, ut esset domus orationis et obsecrationis populo tuo ». — Ad hoc autem praecipue ordinata est domus ecclesiastica, quae facta est, ut illic quidem praecipue colatur per actum orationis, in qua Deus oratur et adoratur, et impletur principium praeceptorum legalium, quo praecipitur, ut adoretur Deus, Levitici vigesimo[^p499-5] et Deuteronomii quinto, et Matthaei quarto replicatur: « Dominum Deum tuum adorabis et illi soli servies ».

72. Tertio quantum ad *enormitatem sceleris* subiungit: *Vos autem fecistis illam speluncam latronum.* Hoc autem dicitur ipsis sacerdotibus; unde Glossa[^p499-6]: « Qui ad hoc residetis in templo, ut vel non dantes munera persequamini corporaliter, vel dantes spiritualiter necetis », secundum illud Michaeae tertio: « Si quis non dederit in ore eorum quippiam » etc. Tunc autem domus Dei fit *spelunca latronum,* quando contra voluntatem Christi contrectatur patrimonium Crucifixi. Hoc enim est furari, et latrocinari, quando publice fit; propter quod Ieremiae septimo[^p499-7]: « Nunquid spelunca latronum facta est domus ista, in qua invocatum est nomen meum in oculis vestris »? « Ite ad locum meum in Silo et videte, quae fecerim ei propter malitiam populi mei Israel ». Haec autem malitia principaliter sacerdotibus imputatur; ideo eis specialiter dicit: *Vos,* quorum erat eam purgare et in sanctitate custodire. Unde Chrysostomus[^p499-8]: « Unusquisque Christianus pro suo peccato reddet rationem, sacerdotes autem non tantum pro suis, sed pro omnium subditorum peccatis. Quemadmodum enim, videns arborem pallentibus foliis marcidam, intelligis, quod causam aliquam habeat in radice; sic, cum videris populum indisciplinatum, cognosce, quia sacerdotium eius non est sanum ». Ideo Malachiae secundo[^p499-9]: « Labia sacerdotis custodient scientiam, et legem requirent de ore eius, quia angelus Domini exercituum est. Vos autem recessistis de via et scandalizastis plurimos, irritum fecistis pactum Levi ». « Propter quod ego dedi vos contemptibiles omnibus populis ».

#### Auctoritatem magistralem promissam se habere, ostendit per tria.

73. (Vers. 47.). *Et erat docens quotidie* etc. Postquam descripsit, qualiter Christus facto ostendit se regem, pontificem et iudicem, hic quarto[^p499-10] describit, *quomodo Christus ostendit se doctorem.* Ad cuius explicationem tria introducuntur, *in Christo* scilicet *assiduitas docendi, in Pharisaeis* autem *assiduitas malignandi, in populis* vero *assiduitas addiscendi.* Ex quibus apparet, quod Christus verus erat doctor, quia doctrina eius erat *publica cunctis*[^p499-11], *exosa perversis* et *gratiosa bonis;* et hoc propter suam *celebritatem, veracitatem* et *gratiositatem.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *doctrinae Christi celebritatem, quae erat publica cunctis,* dicit: *Et erat docens quotidie in templo,* in quo insinuatur perfecta publicatio doctrinae, quia erat *sine intermissione;* nam quotidie docebat. Sic Paulus bonus doctor faciebat, de quo Actuum vigesimo[^p499-12]: « Non subterfugi, quominus annuntiarem vobis omne consilium Dei ». « Propter quod vigilate, memoria retinentes, quoniam per triennium nocte et die non cessavi, cum lacrymis monens unumquemque vestrum ». Hoc etiam
<!-- page 500 -->
fieri hortatur secundae ad Timotheum quarto[^p500-1]: « Testificor coram Deo et Christo Iesu: praedica verbum, insta opportune, importune, argue, obsecra, increpa in omni patientia et doctrina ». — Perfecta publicatio erat, quia *sine alicuius exclusione,* quia non in secreto, sed in loco publico, scilicet in templo; Ioannis decimo octavo[^p500-2]: « Ego palam locutus sum mundo, ego semper docui in synagoga et in templo, quo omnes Iudaei conveniunt, et in occulto locutus sum nihil ». — Perfecta etiam publicatio, quia *sine alicuius utilis celatione;* ideo generaliter dicit *docens,* quia omnem veritatem docebat, secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo octavo[^p500-3]: « Ego Dominus docens te utilia »; et Ioannis decimo quinto: « Omnia, quae audivi a Patre meo, nota feci vobis ». Et ipse erat iuste docens, quia proprie ipse solus doctor, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p500-4]: « Unus est magister vester Christus »; quod exponens Augustinus dicit: « Cathedram habet in caelo qui intus docet corda ». — Perfecta ergo erat communicatio doctrinae ex parte *loci* et ex parte *documenti,* iuxta quod Actuum vigesimo[^p500-5] Paulus de se dicit: « Nihil subtraxi vobis utilium, quominus annuntiarem vobis et docerem vos publice et per domos, testificans Iudaeis et gentibus poenitentiam et fidem in Dominum Iesum Christum ».

74. Secundo quantum ad *doctrinae Christi veracitatem, qua exosa erat incredulis,* subdit: *Principes autem sacerdotum,* propter ambitionem *dignitatis sacerdotalis,* de quibus Isaiae primo[^p500-6]: « Principes tui infideles, socii furum, omnes diligunt munera » etc. — *Et scribae,* propter ambitionem *cathedrae magistralis,* secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p500-7]: « Super cathedram Moysi sederunt scribae et Pharisaei »; et infra vigesimo: « Cavete a scribis, qui volunt ambulare in stolis et amant salutationes in foro et primas cathedras in synagogis ». — *Et principes plebis,* propter ambitionem *potestatis iudicialis;* et de his primi Esdrae nono[^p500-8]: « Manus principum et magistratuum fuit in transgressione hac prima ». Et quia primo eis imputatur delictum populorum, ideo Numerorum vigesimo quinto: « Tolle cunctos principes et suspende eos contra solem in patibulis ». — De his dicitur, quod *quaerebant illum perdere,* per odium malignitatis; ideo Ioannis septimo[^p500-9] dicebat eis: « Quid me quaeritis interficere, hominem, qui vera locutus sum vobis »? Ideo autem isti magis quaerebant quam alii, quia minus volunt sustinere, vitia sua tangi, secundum illud Amos quinto[^p500-10]: « Odio habuerunt corripientem in porta »; vel quia potius timebant perdere honorem suum. Et haec fuit principalis ratio, quae movit illos ad occidendum Christum. Et hac ratione totus hic mundus, quia amat privatum bonum, quasi coniuravit contra Christum, qui dicit ipsum contemnendum; unde Psalmus[^p500-11]: « Astiterunt reges terrae, et principes convenerunt in unum ». Unde Bernardus[^p500-12]: « O bone Iesu! totus mundus videtur contra te coniurasse, et hi primi in persecutione tua sunt, qui videntur regere populum et diligere principatum ».

75. (Vers. 48.). Tertio quantum ad doctrinae *Christi gratiositatem, qua placita erat devotis,* subiungitur: *Et non inveniebant, quid facerent illi,* quia acceptus erat plebi. Unde infra vigesimo[^p500-13]: « Quaerebant principes sacerdotum et scribae mittere in illum manus, sed timuerunt populum »; et hoc, quia diligebant eum sicut magistrum fidelem et bonum. — Et ideo subdit: *Omnis enim populus suspensus erat, audiens illum;* unde Ioannis septimo[^p500-14] dixerunt illi qui missi fuerant ad eum capiendum: « Nunquam sic homo locutus est, sicut hic homo loquitur »; unde suspensi erant *admiratione;* propter quod dicitur Matthaei septimo: « Admirabantur turbae super doctrina eius ». Suspensi etiam erant ex *devotione;* supra sexto: « Multitudo copiosa plebis venerat, ut audirent illum et sanarentur a languoribus suis ». Suspensi etiam erant ex *aviditate et desiderio,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici tertio[^p500-15]: « Auris bona cum omni concupiscentia audiet sapientiam » et maxime sapientiam sapientissimi, qui est ipsa Sapientia, quae dicit Proverbiorum octavo[^p500-16]: « Beatus homo, qui audit me et qui vigilat ad fores meas
<!-- page 501 -->
quotidie et observat ad postes ostii mei. Qui me invenerit inveniet vitam et hauriet salutem a Domino »; et Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto: « Qui audit me non confundetur, et qui operantur in me non peccabunt. Qui elucidant me vitam aeternam habebunt ».

#### That he has the pontifical piety promised, he shows through four things.

63. (Vers. 41.). *And when he drew near* etc. After he has described how the Lord showed that he is the Christ promised as to the royal power, here second[^p495-6] he shows the same as to *the pontifical piety*[^tr-pietas]. For as power and honour become a king, so the sorrow of piety becomes a pontiff, according to that to the Hebrews, the fourth chapter: « We have not a pontiff, who cannot have compassion on our infirmities, but one tempted in all things by likeness, without sin ». Now such piety of Christ is shown to be great, because on the day of his highest honour on earth he did not cease from tears through the affection of compassion. The piety of Christ, then, is described as *weeping*[^p495-7] and bewailing *the foolish joy,* *the near destruction* and *the preceding sin* of the priestly city.

First, then, as to *the most pious weeping of Christ the pontiff* he says: *And when he drew near, seeing the city, he wept over it. He drew near* not only by the position of the body, but also by the affection of the heart, according to that of James, the fourth chapter[^p495-8]: « Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you »; and of Deuteronomy, the fourth chapter: « For what other nation is there so great, that hath gods so nigh them »? He *saw* also *the city* not only with the eyes of the body, but also with the regard of piety; the Psalm[^p495-9]: « Because he hath looked forth from his high sanctuary; from heaven the Lord hath looked upon the earth, that he might hear the groans » etc.; and Exodus, the third chapter: « I have seen the affliction of my people in Egypt » etc. *He wept over it* through a most pious affection and an affectionate compassion.

64. And note that Christ is read to have wept three times for us: over *Lazarus, who was to be raised;* John, the eleventh chapter[^p495-10]: « And Jesus wept »; over *the city of Jerusalem,* as here; and at last *on the cross;* to the Hebrews, the fifth chapter: « With a strong cry and tears offering, he was heard for his reverence »; by which the piety of Christ was most perfectly made manifest toward us. He is believed also to have wept *in his infancy,* when he entered into the misery of the present state, according to what the Church sings of him[^p495-11]:

The infant cries, laid in the narrow manger.

Now in this fourfold weeping he shows that there ought to be in us four differences of tears and weepings: from *compunction;* the Psalm[^p495-12]: « Every night I will wash my bed; I will water my couch with my tears »; and in Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter, it is said of Peter that « going forth, he wept bitterly ». — From
<!-- page 496 -->
*compassion;* Job, the thirtieth chapter[^p496-1]: « I wept heretofore for him that was afflicted, and my soul had compassion on the poor »; and Jeremias, the ninth chapter: « Who will give water to my head, and a fountain of tears to my eyes »? — For *the sojourning of the present misery;* John, the sixteenth chapter[^p496-2]: « You shall weep » etc.; and the Psalm: « Woe is me! that my sojourning is prolonged ». — For *the desire of eternal felicity;* Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p496-3]: « Blessed are they that mourn, for they shall be comforted ». These tears we ought to seek, as long as we are upon the young ass of our mortality. For as Christ wept upon the ass, so also the soul, which sits upon the wretched body as upon a young ass, ought to weep. In figure of which it is said in Judges, the first chapter[^p496-4], that « Axa, the daughter of Caleb, sitting on an ass, sighed » etc.; and after it is subjoined that she said: « Thou hast given me a dry land, give me also a land watered with waters; and Caleb gave her the upper watered place and the nether watered place ». — In these modes of weeping the paradise of conscience ought to be watered. In designation of which it is said in Genesis, the second chapter[^p496-5], that « a river went out of the place of pleasure to water paradise, which is divided into four heads »; in which it is understood that out of the piety of our heart a fourfold river of tears ought to be drawn off.

65. (Vers. 42.). Second, as to *the foolish joy of the royal city* he subjoins: *Saying: If thou also hadst known,* through foresight, thou wouldst have wept, supply: through penance; whence the Gloss[^p496-6]: « *If thou hadst known* the ruin that is impending, thou wouldst have wept ». And here there is a lack of a necessary word, but the Lord speaks abruptly, in the manner of one sorrowing, and supplies it from the very act of sorrowing and weeping, so that the affection may supply the conception, and the deed supply the word: thou wouldst have wept, surely. — And if thou ask: *when? And that,* that is, certainly, *in this thy day, the things that are to thy peace,* namely in which thou exultest; whence the Gloss[^p496-7]: « Thou who now exultest ». — And if thou ask *the cause,* why it exults and does not weep, it is improvidence. Whence he subjoins: *But now they are hidden from thy eyes;* the Gloss[^p496-8]: « In the time of gladness future miseries are not foreseen »; therefore, then, it had a foolish joy on account of *the consideration of the present day,* of which day he says: *In this thy day;* on account of *the holding of present peace,* of which he adds: *The things that are to thy peace;* and on account of *the hiding of the calamity,* of which he subjoins: *But now they are hidden from thy eyes.* Whence the Gloss[^p496-9]: « While thou givest the flesh to pleasures, not looking forward to future evils, thou hast present goods, which can be to thy peace, a temporal one. And why does he subjoin this, namely that it does not foresee the evils that are to come? For if it foresaw them, it would not have been glad in present prosperities ». — And hence it is that *just men* despise *the present day,* according to that of Jeremias, the seventeenth chapter[^p496-10]: « I have not desired the day of man, thou knowest »; and the twentieth: « Cursed be the day wherein I was born »; and of Job it is said in Job, the third chapter: « Job cursed his day, saying: Let the day perish wherein I was born, and the night in which it was said » etc. They contemn *the present peace;* the Psalm[^p496-11]: « I had a zeal on occasion of the wicked, seeing the peace of sinners »; and Ezechiel, the thirteenth chapter: « The prophets have deceived my people, saying: Peace, peace, and there was no peace ». They *look upon and attend to the things to come,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p496-12]: « Remember the last things [so printed: *novissorum* for *novissimorum*], and forget not ». — But on the contrary *evil men* do not think of the judgement, and therefore they are glad, according to that of Proverbs, the second chapter: « They are glad when they have done evil, and exult in most wicked things ». And this is because they consider this day, not the last; Job, the twenty-first chapter[^p496-13]: « They take the timbrel and the harp, and rejoice at the sound of the organ. They spend their days in wealth, and in a moment they go down to hell ». Therefore it is said ironically in Ecclesiastes, the eleventh chapter[^p496-14]: « Rejoice, O young man, in thy youth, and let thy heart be in that which is good in the days of thy youth ». « And know that for all these God will bring thee into judgement ». From this, then, there appears that which is said in Ecclesiastes, the seventh chapter: « It is better to go to the house of mourning than to the house of feasting » etc.

66. (Vers. 43.). Third, as to *the near destruction of the royal city* he subjoins: *For the days shall come upon thee, and thy enemies shall cast a trench about thee,* through siege, according to that of Ezechiel, the fourth chapter[^p496-15]: « Take thee a tile, and thou shalt draw upon it the city of Jerusalem. And thou shalt lay siege against it, and build forts, and cast up a mount, and set a camp against it round about, and place forts » etc.
<!-- page 497 -->
Now this was fulfilled in the time of the Romans; whence the Gloss[^p497-1]: « *Enemies,* namely the Roman princes », who according to the letter are said to have made three mounts against Jerusalem for its capture. — *And they shall compass thee, and straiten thee on every side,* through the continuation of the siege, according to that of Ezechiel, the fourth chapter[^p497-2]: « Thou shalt set a frying pan for a wall of iron between thee and the city, and thou shalt set thy face against it, and it shall be besieged, and thou shalt besiege it; it is a sign to the house of Israel ». And this was done, because, as Josephus says[^p497-3], it was so very straitly besieged; that a mother ate her son, and one devoured another. Whence that of Lamentations, the second chapter, was fulfilled: « They said to their mothers: Where is the corn and wine? when they fainted away as the wounded in the streets of the city, when they breathed out their souls in the bosoms of their mothers »; and below in the same place: « Shall women then eat their own fruit, their children of a span long »?

67. (Vers. 44.). *And beat thee flat to the ground, and thy children who are in thee,* through the destruction of the city; Lamentations, the second chapter[^p497-4]: « The Lord hath purposed to destroy the wall of the daughter of Sion; he hath stretched out his line, and hath not withdrawn his hand from destroying. And the bulwark hath mourned, and the wall hath been destroyed together ». Not only was it itself destroyed, but also the whole nation dispersed, according to that of Lamentations, the second chapter: « Her gates are sunk into the ground; he hath destroyed and broken her bars, her king and her princes are among the Gentiles; the law is not, and her prophets have found no vision from the Lord ». — *And they shall not leave in thee a stone upon a stone,* through an overthrow in every manner. Whence the Gloss[^p497-5]: « Since Jerusalem is now built outside the gate where the Lord had been crucified, it appears that that Jerusalem was wholly destroyed ». And thus was fulfilled that of Lamentations, the second chapter[^p497-6]: « The Lord hath done that which he purposed; he hath fulfilled his word, which he commanded in the days of old; he hath destroyed and hath not spared, and he hath caused the enemy to rejoice over thee, and hath set up the horn of thy adversaries »; and at once it is subjoined: « Let tears run down like a torrent day and night » etc.

68. Now this happens *spiritually* against any sinner *dying temporally:* who first *is compassed* in infirmity, *is straitened* in the aggravation of infirmity, *is beaten flat to the ground* in death, and is utterly *overthrown* in being turned to ashes, according to that of Job, the seventh chapter[^p497-7]: « Behold, now I shall sleep in the dust, and if thou seek me in the morning, I shall not be ». — It can also be understood of the sinner *coming to the judgement,* who *is compassed* by the multitude of accusing iniquities; the Psalm[^p497-8]: « For evils without number have surrounded me; my iniquities have overtaken me, and I was not able to see ». He *is straitened* in the strictness of the divine judgements; Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter[^p497-9]: « It is not in man's power to stop the spirit, neither hath he power in the day of death, neither is he suffered to rest when war is at hand, neither shall wickedness save the wicked ». He *is beaten flat to the ground* in the pronouncing of the sentences; Job, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p497-10]: « If his children be multiplied, they shall fall by the sword », namely of the divine sentence. And at last he *is utterly overthrown* in the bitterness of the punishments; the Psalm[^p497-11]: « Our bones are scattered by the side of hell ». For then no excuse is left to the impious; Isaias, the fifth chapter: « I will take away the wall thereof, and it shall be trodden down ». — And of all these it is said in Amos, the third chapter[^p497-12]: « The land shall be distressed, and compassed round about, and thy strength shall be brought down from thee, and thy houses shall be plundered, saith the Lord ». On account of which Anselm: « Above will be the angry judge, beneath the horrible chaos, hell lying open, on the right the sins accusing, on the left infinite demons dragging to hell, within the conscience burning, without the world aflame; the wretched sinner, thus caught, whither shall he flee? To lie hid will be impossible, to appear will be intolerable ».

69. Fourth, as to *the preceding sin* he subjoins: *Because thou hast not known the time of thy visitation;* and this on account of unbelief; Jeremias, the eighth chapter[^p497-13]: « The kite in the air hath known her time; the turtle, and the swallow, and the stork have observed the time of their coming; but my people have not known the judgement of the Lord ». As, then, faith is the foundation
<!-- page 498 -->
of the whole spiritual building, through which Christ, who is the foundation, dwells in us; on account of which it is said to the Hebrews, the eleventh chapter[^p498-1], that « faith is the substance of things to be hoped for »: so faithlessness is the origin of the destruction of all spiritual buildings. Therefore it is said in Job, the fourth chapter: « Because no one understandeth, they shall perish for ever »; and to the Romans, the first chapter: « Because they liked not to have God in their knowledge, God delivered them up to a reprobate sense »; whence also the Jews, because they would not believe in Christ, were blinded and reprobated; and this was the greatest sin, according to that of John, the sixteenth chapter[^p498-2]: « He will convince the world of sin indeed », namely « because they believed not in me ». Therefore Mark, the last chapter[^p498-3]: « He that believeth and is baptized shall be saved; but he that believeth not shall be condemned »; nay, what is more, his damnation already begins; John, the third chapter: « He that doth not believe is already judged, because he believeth not in the name of the only begotten Son of him ».

#### That he has the judicial equity promised, he shows through three things.

70. (Vers. 45.). *And entering into the temple* etc. After the Evangelist has described how Christ showed by deed that he has the royal power and the pontifical piety, here third[^p498-4] it is set forth how he shows that he has *the judicial equity.* For the declaring of which three things are brought in, namely *the equity of the judge, the truth of the law* and *the enormity* or impiety *of the crime.* Now in these three he shows that he judges rightly, as it were syllogistically. For the truth of sacred Scripture *being proposed,* and the perversity of the Jewish wickedness *being assumed,* the severity of the divine vengeance *is rightly concluded,* according to that of John, the sixteenth chapter[^p498-5]: « He will convince the world of sin, and of justice, and of judgement ». For he proposes *justice,* assumes *sin* and concludes *judgement.*

First, then, as to *the equity of the judge* he says: *And entering into the temple, he began to cast out them that sold therein and them that bought;* in which is shown the equity of the judge, who, although he was meek in bearing his own injury, according to that of Jeremias, the eleventh chapter[^p498-6]: « And I was as a meek lamb, that is carried to be a victim »; yet in avenging the divine injury he was a severe and just judge. And because the greatest injury to God is when he is dishonoured where he ought to be honoured; therefore it is said that *he entered into the temple,* to do judgement there; which he did also at the *beginning* of his preaching, according to what is narrated in John, the second chapter[^p498-7], after the miracle of the changing of water into wine, and at the *end,* after the miracle of Lazarus raised, so that thus that of the Psalm[^p498-8] might be verified: « The zeal of thy house hath eaten me up » etc.; and in John, the second chapter, it is said that this was then verified. — And it is to be attended to that the Lord, immediately after the announcing of the destruction of Jerusalem, entered the temple to purge it, to show that the greatest cause of its destruction was the sin of the priests. Whence the Gloss[^p498-9]: « After the evils were enumerated, entering the temple, he cast out them that sold and bought, by this intimating that the ruin of the people was chiefly from the fault of the priests; when he strikes them that sell and buy, he shows whence the root of perdition proceeded ». Whence Micheas, the third chapter[^p498-10]: « Hear this, ye princes of the house of Jacob, and ye judges of the house of Israel, you that abhor judgement, and pervert all that is right, you that build up Sion with blood, and Jerusalem with iniquity. Her princes have judged for bribes, and her priests have taught for hire, and her prophets divined for money; therefore because of this Sion shall be ploughed as a field, and Jerusalem shall be as a heap of stones, and the mountain of the temple as the high places of the forests »; and Lamentations, the fourth chapter: « For the sins of her prophets, and the iniquities of her priests, that have shed the blood of the just in the midst of her ». Now in this, that the Lord cast out them that bought and sold, he shows that he most of all condemns in clerics avarice, and above all simony. Whence in Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p498-11], it is said that « he overthrew the chairs of them that sold doves ». To all such Peter says in Acts, the eighth chapter: « Keep thy money to thyself, to perish with thee, because thou hast thought that the gift of God may be purchased with money ».

71. (Vers. 46.). Second, as to *the truth*[^p498-12] *of the law* he subjoins: *Saying to them: It is written:*
<!-- page 499 -->
*My house is the house of prayer*[^p499-1]. Now this is written in Isaias, the fifty-sixth chapter: « Their holocausts and their victims shall please me upon my altar, for my house shall be called the house of prayer for all people, saith the Lord God, who gathereth the scattered of Israel ». Whence also David in the Psalm[^p499-2] says: « Holiness becometh thy house »; and again: « I will come into thy house, I will worship towards thy holy temple »; and at the consecration of the temple it is said: « That thy eyes may be open upon this house night and day, upon the house of which thou hast said: My name shall be there », in the third of Kings, the eighth chapter[^p499-3]; and in the second of Paralipomenon, the seventh chapter: « My eyes also shall be open, and my ears attentive to the prayer of him that shall pray in this place. For I have chosen and have sanctified this place, that my name may be there for ever ». And therefore the priests said, in the first of Machabees, the seventh chapter[^p499-4]: « Thou, O Lord, didst choose this house for thy name to be called upon therein, that it might be a house of prayer and supplication for thy people ». — Now for this chiefly the ecclesiastical house is ordained, which was made so that there indeed he might chiefly be worshipped through the act of prayer, in which God is prayed to and adored, and the beginning of the legal precepts is fulfilled, by which it is commanded that God be adored, in Leviticus, the twentieth chapter[^p499-5], and Deuteronomy, the fifth chapter, and it is repeated in Matthew, the fourth chapter: « The Lord thy God shalt thou adore, and him only shalt thou serve ».

72. Third, as to *the enormity of the crime* he subjoins: *But you have made it a den of thieves.* Now this is said to the priests themselves; whence the Gloss[^p499-6]: « You who sit in the temple for this, that either you persecute bodily those who do not give gifts, or kill spiritually those who give », according to that of Micheas, the third chapter: « If a man give not something into their mouth » etc. Now the house of God becomes *a den of thieves* then, when, against the will of Christ, the patrimony of the Crucified is handled. For this is to steal, and to rob, when it is done publicly; on account of which Jeremias, the seventh chapter[^p499-7]: « Is this house then, in which my name hath been called upon, in your eyes become a den of thieves »? « Go ye to my place in Silo, and see what I did to it for the wickedness of my people Israel ». Now this wickedness is imputed chiefly to the priests; therefore he says specially to them: *You,* whose part it was to purge it and to guard it in holiness. Whence Chrysostom[^p499-8]: « Every Christian will render account for his own sin, but the priests not only for their own, but for the sins of all their subjects. For as, seeing a tree withered with pale leaves, thou understandest that it has some cause in the root; so, when thou hast seen a people undisciplined, know that its priesthood is not sound ». Therefore Malachias, the second chapter[^p499-9]: « The lips of the priest shall keep knowledge, and they shall seek the law at his mouth, because he is the angel of the Lord of hosts. But you have departed out of the way, and have caused many to stumble, you have made void the covenant of Levi ». « Therefore have I also made you contemptible before all people ».

#### That he has the magisterial authority promised, he shows through three things.

73. (Vers. 47.). *And he was teaching daily* etc. After he has described how Christ showed by deed that he is king, pontiff and judge, here fourth[^p499-10] he describes *how Christ shows himself a doctor.* For the setting forth of which three things are brought in, namely *in Christ the assiduity of teaching,* but *in the Pharisees the assiduity of malignant dealing,* and *in the peoples the assiduity of learning.* From which it appears that Christ was a true doctor, because his doctrine was *public to all*[^p499-11], *hateful to the perverse* and *gracious to the good;* and this on account of its *celebrity, veracity* and *graciousness.*

First, then, as to *the celebrity of the doctrine of Christ, which was public to all,* he says: *And he was teaching daily in the temple,* in which is intimated the perfect publication of the doctrine, because it was *without intermission;* for he taught daily. So Paul, a good doctor, did, of whom Acts, the twentieth chapter[^p499-12]: « I have not spared to announce unto you all the counsel of God ». « Therefore watch, keeping in memory that for three years I ceased not, with tears to admonish every one of you night and day ». This also
<!-- page 500 -->
he exhorts to be done in the second to Timothy, the fourth chapter[^p500-1]: « I charge thee before God and Jesus Christ: preach the word, be instant in season, out of season, reprove, entreat, rebuke in all patience and doctrine ». — The publication was perfect, because it was *without the exclusion of anyone,* since it was not in secret, but in a public place, namely in the temple; John, the eighteenth chapter[^p500-2]: « I have spoken openly to the world, I have always taught in the synagogue and in the temple, whither all the Jews resort, and in secret I have spoken nothing ». — The publication was perfect also because it was *without the concealing of anything useful;* therefore he says generally *teaching,* because he taught every truth, according to that of Isaias, the forty-eighth chapter[^p500-3]: « I am the Lord that teach thee profitable things »; and John, the fifteenth chapter: « All things whatsoever I have heard of my Father, I have made known to you ». And he himself was teaching justly, because properly he alone is the doctor, according to that of Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p500-4]: « One is your master, Christ »; expounding which Augustine says: « He has his chair in heaven who teaches hearts within ». — Perfect, then, was the communication of the doctrine on the part of *the place* and on the part of *the instruction,* according to what Paul says of himself in Acts, the twentieth chapter[^p500-5]: « I have kept back nothing that was profitable to you, but that I might announce it to you, and teach you publicly and from house to house, testifying both to Jews and Gentiles penance and faith in the Lord Jesus Christ ».

74. Second, as to *the veracity of the doctrine of Christ, by which it was hateful to the unbelieving,* he subjoins: *But the chief priests,* on account of the ambition of *the priestly dignity,* of whom Isaias, the first chapter[^p500-6]: « Thy princes are faithless, companions of thieves, they all love bribes » etc. — *And the scribes,* on account of the ambition of *the magisterial chair,* according to that of Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p500-7]: « The scribes and the Pharisees have sitten on the chair of Moses »; and below, the twentieth chapter: « Beware of the scribes, who desire to walk in long robes, and love salutations in the market place, and the first chairs in the synagogues ». — *And the rulers of the people,* on account of the ambition of *the judicial power;* and of these, the first of Esdras, the ninth chapter[^p500-8]: « The hand of the princes and magistrates hath been first in this transgression ». And because the offence of the peoples is imputed first to them, therefore Numbers, the twenty-fifth chapter: « Take all the princes, and hang them up on gibbets against the sun ». — Of these it is said that *they sought to destroy him,* through the hatred of malignity; therefore he said to them in John, the seventh chapter[^p500-9]: « Why seek you to kill me, a man who have spoken the truth to you »? Now these sought it more than the others for this reason, that they are less willing to bear that their vices be touched, according to that of Amos, the fifth chapter[^p500-10]: « They have hated him that rebuketh in the gate »; or because they rather feared to lose their honour. And this was the principal reason that moved them to kill Christ. And for this reason this whole world, because it loves the private good, has as it were conspired against Christ, who says that it is to be contemned; whence the Psalm[^p500-11]: « The kings of the earth stood up, and the princes met together ». Whence Bernard[^p500-12]: « O good Jesus! the whole world seems to have conspired against thee, and they are first in thy persecution who seem to rule the people and to love the principality ».

75. (Vers. 48.). Third, as to *the graciousness* of the doctrine *of Christ, by which it was pleasing to the devout,* it is subjoined: *And they found not what to do to him,* because he was acceptable to the common people. Whence below, the twentieth chapter[^p500-13]: « The chief priests and the scribes sought to lay hands on him, but they feared the people »; and this because they loved him as a faithful and good master. — And therefore he subjoins: *For all the people hung upon him, hearing him;* whence in John, the seventh chapter[^p500-14], they that had been sent to take him said: « Never did man speak like this man »; whence they hung upon him *in admiration;* on account of which it is said in Matthew, the seventh chapter: « The multitudes were in admiration at his doctrine ». They hung upon him also out of *devotion;* above, the sixth chapter: « A very great multitude of people had come to hear him and to be healed of their diseases ». They hung upon him also out of *avidity and desire,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter[^p500-15]: « A good ear will hear wisdom with all desire » and most of all the wisdom of the most wise, who is Wisdom itself, which says in Proverbs, the eighth chapter[^p500-16]: « Blessed is the man that heareth me, and that watcheth daily at my gates,
<!-- page 501 -->
and waiteth at the posts of my doors. He that shall find me shall find life, and shall have salvation from the Lord »; and Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter: « He that hearkeneth to me shall not be confounded, and they that work by me shall not sin. They that explain me shall have life everlasting ».

The Latin word covers the tenderness of a father toward his own as well as the devotion of a creature toward God; Bonaventure is using the first sense of Christ.
