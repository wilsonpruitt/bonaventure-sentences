# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XVI, Vers. 9-18
## *Documentum pietatis perfectae quoad duo*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 407 -->

#### Documentum pietatis perfectae quoad duo.

13. *Et ego vobis dico* etc. Post exemplum pietatis providae subditur *documentum pietatis perfectae*[^p407-10], quod quidem elicitur ex sensu parabolae supradictae. Primo igitur elicitur documentum ad *informationem discipulorum obedientium;* secundo, ad *confutationem Pharisaeorum rebellium,* ibi[^p407-11]: *Audiebant autem omnia haec Pharisaei* etc.

Circa *informationem* autem *discipulorum ad pietatem* procedit hoc ordine. Invitat enim eos ad misericordiam primo, ratione *mercedis habendae;* secundo, ratione *fidelitatis servandae,* ibi[^p407-12]: *Qui fidelis est in minimo;* tertio, ratione *duplicitatis cavendae,* ibi: *Nemo potest duobus dominis servire.* — Ratione autem *mercedis habendae* invitat hoc ordine, scilicet praemittendo *exhortationem erudientem* et addendo *rationem moventem.*

14. (Vers. 9.). Primo ergo quantum ad *exhortationem erudientem* praemittit: *Et ego dico vobis: Facite vobis amicos de mammona iniquitatis.*
<!-- page 408 -->
« *Mammona,* ut dicit Glossa[^p408-1], lingua Syrorum divitiae dicuntur; dicuntur autem divitiae *iniquitatis,* quia frequenter habent iniquitatem annexam, secundum illud Ecclesiastici undecimo[^p408-2]: « Si dives fueris, non eris immunis a delicto »; unde Augustinus: « Omnis dives aut iniquus, aut heres iniqui est »; vel sicut dicit Beda in homilia[^p408-3]: « *Mammon,* mammonis lingua Punica dicitur lucrum ». Nec debet intelligi, quod de eo quod quis per rapinam et iniquitatem acquisivit et iniusto titulo possidet, debeat facere eleemosynas, quia contra hoc dicitur Ecclesiastici trigesimo quarto[^p408-4]: « Dona iniquorum non probat Altissimus »; et rursus eodem capitulo: « Qui offert sacrificium de substantia pauperis quasi qui immolat filium in conspectu patris »; sed intelligitur, quod de divitiis, quae iusto titulo possidentur; et tamen iniquitatem pariunt, nisi distribuantur, secundum illud Ecclesiastici decimo[^p408-5]: « Nihil est iniquius quam amare pecuniam. Hic enim et animam suam venalem habet ». De his, inquam, divitiis debent fieri amici per largitionem eleemosynarum, quae manifestant pietatis affectum. Nam dicitur primae Ioannis tertio[^p408-6]: « Qui habuerit substantiam huius mundi et viderit, fratrem suum necessitatem habere, et clauserit viscera sua ab eo, quomodo caritas Dei manet in illo »? et ideo Ecclesiastici vigesimo nono: « Perde pecuniam propter amicum et ne abscondas illam sub lapide in perditionem », quia, Proverbiorum undecimo, « qui abscondit frumenta maledicetur in populis » etc.

15. Secundo quantum ad *rationem moventem* subdit: *Ut cum defeceritis, recipiant vos in aeterna tabernacula.* Ex quo intelligitur, quare dandum est munus, et quid debet hominem movere ad faciendam eleemosynam: quia non *praesens commodum,* sed *praemium aeternum,* non propter *tabernaculum transiens,* sed *aeternum,* secundum illud Matthaei sexto[^p408-7]: « Cum facis eleemosynam, noli tuba ante te canere, sicut hypocritae faciunt. Amen dico vobis » etc.; et post: « Te autem faciente eleemosynam, nesciat sinistra tua, quid faciat dextera tua ». Haec autem tabernacula aeterna sunt mansiones gloriae, de quibus Isaiae trigesimo secundo[^p408-8]: « Sedebit populus meus in pulcritudine pacis, in tabernaculis fiduciae, in requie opulenta ». *In aeterna tabernacula* alios *recipiunt* qui adeo perfecti sunt, ut merita eorum etiam aliis valeant, et propter quae merita sibi et aliis aeterna tabernacula acquirant. Unde super illud[^p408-9]: « Facite vobis amicos », dicit Glossa: « Non quoslibet pauperes, sed eos qui possunt in aeterna tabernacula recipere ».

16. Tales enim non sunt pauperes involuntarii, sed pauperes spiritu; de quibus Matthaei quinto[^p408-10]: « Beati pauperes spiritu, quoniam ipsorum est regnum caelorum ». Ipsorum ergo tanquam illius regni *regum et possessorum* est introducere in regnum caelorum. Ipsorum etiam tanquam *iudicum,* secundum illud Iob trigesimo sexto[^p408-11]: « Non salvabit impium, sed iudicium pauperibus tribuit »; pauperibus, inquam, perfectis et voluntariis, de quibus Matthaei decimo nono: « Vos, qui reliquistis omnia et secuti estis me, sedebitis super sedes duodecim, iudicantes duodecim tribus Israel ». Ipsorum etiam tanquam *familiarium et amicorum,* propter quod Iacobi secundo[^p408-12]: « Nonne Deus elegit pauperes in hoc mundo, divites in fide, heredes regni, quod repromisit Deus » etc. Tales introducunt amicos et benefactores suos per illam legem iudicialem, per quam Dominus in futuro iudicio sententiabit, secundum quod dicit Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p408-13]: « Quod uni ex minimis fecistis mihi fecistis », et per illam aliam legem, quam Dominus tulit, de qua Matthaei decimo: « Qui recipit iustum in nomine iusti mercedem iusti accipiet ».

17. (Vers. 10.). *Qui fidelis est in minimo* etc. Postquam invitavit ad pietatem ex consideratione mercedis habendae, hic secundo invitat ex consideratione *fidelitatis servandae*[^p408-14]. Est enim fidelitas servanda in dispensatione temporalium tum propter *meritum et demeritum,* tum propter *periculum et damnum.*

Primo ergo quantum ad considerationem *meriti fidelitatis* in huiusmodi rebus terrenis dicit: *Qui fidelis est in minimo et in maiori fidelis est;* quasi
<!-- page 409 -->
dicat: licet ista temporalia contemnenda sint tanquam minima, tamen fidelis horum dispensatio non debet contemni, quia et eadem est fidelitas in re magna et parva, et fidelis dispensatio rerum temporalium disponit ad fidelem dispensationem et custoditionem spiritualium et aeternorum. Unde Glossa[^p409-1]: « *Qui fidelis est in minimo,* id est in pecunia cum paupere participanda, *et in maiori est fidelis,* adhaerens Creatori ». Et ideo dicitur huiusmodi servo Apocalypsis secundo[^p409-2]: « Esto fidelis usque ad mortem, et dabo tibi coronam vitae »; similiter Matthaei vigesimo quinto: « Euge, serve bone et fidelis, quia super pauca fuisti fidelis, supra multa te constituam; intra in gaudium Domini tui ». Unde fidelitas circa parva fidelitatem ponit etiam circa magna; et e contrario infidelitas circa minima infidelitatem ponit circa maiora.

18. Et ideo subdit: *Et qui in modico iniquus est et in maiori iniquus est. Modicum* recte dicitur omne, quod temporaliter possidetur, tum quia modicae *quantitatis;* Sapientiae undecimo[^p409-3]: « Quoniam sicut momentum staterae, sic est ante te universus orbis terrarum »; tum quia modicae *durationis;* Sapientiae quinto: « Transierunt haec omnia velut umbra »; et Psalmus: « Dormierunt somnum suum, et nihil invenerunt omnes viri divitiarum » etc.; tum quia modicae *iucunditatis;* unde Gregorius[^p409-4]: « Pauca sunt bona praesentis vitae, licet multa videantur », quia non sine alicuius molestiae admixtione sunt; multa autem bona aeterna, quae sunt sine omni corruptione. Qui ergo iniquus est in istis non potest habere caritatem Dei, quia pro parvis maxima contemnit. Vendit enim fidem et iustitiam pro eo quod nihil valet; unde Augustinus[^p409-5]: « Lucrum in arca, damnum in conscientia; tulit vestem, et perdidit fidem; acquirit pecuniam, et amisit iustitiam ». Male igitur dispensando pecuniam perdit felicitatem, perdit caritatem, perdit iustitiam, quae sunt maxima Dei dona. Unde Glossa[^p409-6]: « Qui temporalia, quae possidet, non bene dispensat, aeternorum sibi gloriam, de qua tumet, evacuat. *Si enim fratrem, quem videt, non diligit, Deum, quem non videt, quomodo potest diligere? Et si habens substantiam viderit fratrem necessitatem habere* etc., *quomodo caritas Patris manet in eo* »? sicut habetur primae Ioannis tertio.

19. (Vers. 11.). Secundo quantum ad considerationem *periculi et damni* subdit: *Si ergo in iniquo mammona fideles non fuistis, quod verum est quis credet vobis?* quasi dicat: magnum est periculum male dispensare haec temporalia bona, quia propter infidelem dispensationem perdit quis divitias veras. Unde Glossa[^p409-7]: « Si divitias carnales, quae labuntur, non bene dispensatis, veras et aeternas divitias quis dabit vobis »? Dicuntur autem divitiae *iniquum mammon,* quia, sicut dicit Glossa[^p409-8], « divitiis praeest iniquus mammon, ut de eis tentet »; sicut tentavit Ananiam, de quo Actuum quinto: « Cur tentavit satanas cor tuum » etc. Tentatio illa fuit de furto; similiter de Achor dicitur Iosue septimo. *Verum* autem dicitur illud bonum, quod semper manet. Nam haec temporalia omnia, quia transeunt, vana sunt, secundum illud Ecclesiastae primo[^p409-9]: « Vanitas vanitatum, et omnia vanitas ». Illud autem *verum* est Dei gratia, vel Dei gloria, vel potius ipse Deus, qui est merces bonorum; et ideo Psalmus: « Veritas Domini manet in aeternum »; et iterum: « Usque in generationem et generationem veritas tua ». Et in hac veritate consistit beatitudo, secundum illud Augustini in libro Confessionum[^p409-10]: « Beatitudo est gaudium de veritate »; quia enim « verum est id quod est », ipse solus est pura veritas, qui dicit: « Ego sum qui sum », Exodi tertio. Et istud *verum* non conceditur nec creditur nisi a veracibus et fidelibus; unde Ioannis tertio[^p409-11]: « Omnis, qui male agit, odit lucem et non venit ad lucem, ut non arguantur opera eius. Qui autem facit veritatem venit ad lucem, ut manifestentur opera eius, quia in Deo facta sunt »; et Ioannis octavo: « Si manseritis in sermone meo, cognoscetis veritatem, et veritas liberabit vos ». Magnum ergo periculum infidelitatis est in his parvis, quia pro *vano* perditur verum, pro *parvo* perditur[^p409-12] magnum.

<!-- page 410 -->

20. (Vers. 12.). Nihilominus, quia pro *alieno* perditur proprium; ideo subdit: *Et si in alieno fideles non fuistis,* per debitam boni terreni dispensationem; unde Glossa[^p410-1]: « Alienum a nobis est, quidquid saeculi est, quia praeter naturam ». Haec quidem dicuntur *bona aliena,* quia ea nobiscum nec afferre possumus nec auferre; primae ad Timotheum ultimo[^p410-2]: « Nihil intulimus in hunc mundum, haud dubium, quod nec auferre quid possumus »; et Iob primo: « Nudus egressus sum de utero matris meae et nudus revertar illuc ». — *Quod*[^p410-3] *vestrum est quis dabit vobis?* per assecutionem caelestis praemii. Illud namque *nostrum* est, ad quod sumus creati et praeordinati, et quod habitum non potest perdi; unde Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p410-4]: « Venite, benedicti Patris mei, possidete paratum vobis regnum » etc.; et supra sexto: « Beati pauperes, quia vestrum est regnum Dei ». Hoc autem regnum non est aliud nisi Deus et Dominus Iesus Christus; unde Glossa[^p410-5]: « *Quod vestrum est,* id est, quae proprie homini deputata sunt, Christus noster est, quia Christus vita nostra est »; ad Colossenses tertio: « Cum apparuerit Christus, vita vestra, tunc et vos apparebitis » etc.

21. (Vers. 13.). *Nemo*[^p410-6] *potest duobus dominis servire* etc. Post invitationem ad pietatem ex consideratione mercedis habendae et fidelitatis servandae hic tertio invitat ex consideratione *duplicitatis vitandae*[^p410-7], quia non potest simul haberi pietas ad Deum et cupiditas ad mundum; quod quidem facit hoc ordine, praemittens scilicet *duplicitatis reprobationem,* adiungens *reprobationis assertionem.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *reprobationem duplicitatis* dicit: *Nemo potest duobus dominis servire.* Haec propositio per se nota est; propter quod dicitur Ecclesiastici secundo[^p410-8]: « Vae duplici corde et labiis scelestis etc. et peccatori ingredienti terram duabus viis »! et Iacobi primo: « Vir duplex animo inconstans est in omnibus viis suis ». Et ideo dicitur Ecclesiastici primo: « Ne accesseris ad illum duplici corde », hoc est, non velis duobus dominis simul servire, quia hoc non potest fieri recte. — Domini autem duo ab invicem divisi, quibus non potest simul serviri, sunt in differentia quadruplici. Sunt enim isti domini *vitium* et *virtus;* propter quod tertii Regum decimo octavo[^p410-9]: « Usquequo claudicatis in duas partes? Si Dominus est Deus, sequimini eum; si autem Baal » etc.; et Isaiae vigesimo octavo: « Coangustatum est stratum, ita ut alter decidat, et pallium breve utrumque operire non potest ». — Domini etiam isti sunt *caro* et *spiritus;* ad Galatas quinto[^p410-10]: « Caro concupiscit adversus spiritum, spiritus autem adversus carnem. Haec enim sibi invicem adversantur, ut non, quaecumque vultis, illa faciatis ». Ideo ad Romanos octavo: « Si secundum carnem vixeritis, moriemini; si autem spiritu facta carnis mortificaveritis, vivetis ». — Item, domini isti sunt *Christus* et *diabolus;* secundae ad Corinthios sexto[^p410-11]: » Quae conventio Christi ad Belial »? et primae ad Corinthios decimo: « Non potestis calicem Domini bibere et calicem daemoniorum; non potestis mensae Domini participes esse et mensae daemoniorum » etc. — Item, domini sunt *Deus* et iste *mundus;* Iacobi quarto[^p410-12]: « Adulteri, nescitis, quia amicitia huius mundi inimica est Dei? Si quis voluerit amicus huius mundi fieri, inimicus Dei constituitur »; et ideo primae Ioannis secundo: « Nolite diligere mundum, quia, si quis diligit mundum, non est caritas Patris in eo ». — Et de his duobus dominis intelligit dissuadendo cupiditatem mundanam et invitando ad pietatem divinam. Unde Glossa[^p410-13]: « Corripiens avaritiam dicit, amatores pecuniae Deum amare non posse; qui ergo Deum amat pecuniam contemnat ». Ex quo apparet, quod qui vult esse servus Christi non potest esse servus istius mundi; unde Beda[^p410-14]: « Debetis haec temporalia fideliter distribuere ut domini temporalium, non ut servi ».

22. Secundo quantum ad *assertionem reprobationis* subiungit: *Aut enim unum odiet et alterum diliget;* quod dicitur quantum ad *caritatem,* quae odire facit malum et diligere bonum, secundum illud ad Romanos duodecimo[^p410-15]: « Odientes malum,
<!-- page 411 -->
adhaerentes bono, caritate fraternitatis invicem diligentes »; et Psalmus: « Iniquos odio habui et legem tuam dilexi ». — *Aut uni adhaerebit et alterum contemnet;* quod dicitur quantum ad *cupiditatem,* quae adhaeret mundo et diabolo in contemptum Dei, secundum illud Ieremiae secundo[^p411-1]: « Duo mala fecit populus meus: me dereliquerunt fontem aquae vivae et foderunt sibi cisternas, quae continere non valent aquas ».

23. Ratio autem, quare quis non potest simul servire dominis praedictis, est quadruplex, prima scilicet *oppositio ministeriorum.* Nam servitium Dei est in exercitio *virtutum,* secundum illud supra primo[^p411-2]: « Serviamus illi in sanctitate et iustitia coram ipso »; et primae Petri primo: « Secundum eum qui vocavit vos sanctum, ut et ipsi sancti sitis » etc. Sed servitium diaboli est in exercitio *vitiorum;* unde supra decimo quinto de filio prodigo dicitur, quod « misit illum in villam suam, ut pasceret porcos ». — Secunda ratio est *dissimilitudo ministrorum,* quia Dominus vult habere servientes *oculatos;* propter quod dicitur Apocalypsis quarto[^p411-3], quod « animalia plena erant oculis ante et retro »; et Proverbiorum decimo quarto: « Acceptus est regi minister intelligens ». Sed diabolus vult habere servientes *monoculos;* unde primi Regum undecimo: « In hoc feriam vobiscum pactum, ut eruam omnium vestrum oculos dextros ». Dominus etiam vult habere ministros *compositos et ordinatos;* unde tertii Regum decimo[^p411-4]: « Videns regina Saba habitacula servorum et ordinem ministrantium » etc. Sed diabolus vult habere *incompositos et deformatos;* unde secundi Regum decimo: « Hanon, rex filiorum Ammon, praescidit vestes servorum David usque ad nates et rasit dimidiam barbam eorum ». Dominus etiam vult habere servientes *humiles,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p411-5]: « Non habitabit in medio domus meae qui facit superbiam, qui loquitur iniqua » etc. Sed diabolus vult habere *arrogantes;* Iob penultimo: « Omne sublime videt, et ipse est rex super omnes filios superbiae ». — Tertia ratio est *elongatio locorum,* quia Dominus vult esse in loco *sublimi et mundo;* Psalmus[^p411-6]: « Ad te levavi oculos meos, qui habitas in caelis »; et Canticorum secundo: « Ecce, venit saliens in montibus, transiliens colles ». Sed diabolus, in locis *immundis;* Iob quadragesimo: « Sub umbra dormit in secreto calami, in locis humentibus ». — Quarta ratio est *indiscretio temporum,* quia diabolus vult, quod *sibi* serviatur omni tempore; Ieremiae decimo sexto[^p411-7]: « Servietis diis alienis, qui non dabunt vobis requiem die ac nocte » etc. Similiter Dominus vult, quod *sibi* omni tempore; Psalmus: « In lege Domini meditabitur die ac nocte » etc. Quoniam igitur Dominus vult totum tempus habere et totum cor, secundum illud Deuteronomii sexto[^p411-8]: « Diliges Dominum Deum tuum ex toto corde tuo et ex tota anima tua et ex omni fortitudine tua »; et diabolus et mundus vult similiter totum: ideo impossibile est utrisque servire simul.

24. Et ideo infert: *Non potestis Deo servire et mammonae.* Dicitur autem *servire mammonae* in cuius affectu divitiae dominantur, ita ut possit dici *avarus,* quia tunc est servus idolorum. Nam, sicut dicitur ad Ephesios quinto[^p411-9], « avaritia est idolorum servitus ». Unde Glossa: « Non ait: *habere divitias,* sed *servire.* Servit qui divitias custodit ut servus; sed qui servitutis excussit iugum distribuit ut dominus ». Et ideo dicebat Apostolus primae ad Corinthios septimo[^p411-10]: « Reliquum est, ut qui emunt tanquam non possidentes sint, et qui utuntur hoc mundo tanquam non utantur » etc. Sed aliqui simul volunt habere Deum et mundum, qui sunt similes illis gentibus, de quibus quarti Regum decimo septimo[^p411-11]: « Erant gentes timentes Dominum, et nihilominus idolis servientes ». Contra hos dicitur Levitici decimo nono: « Veste, quae ex duobus contexta est, non vestieris », et contra hos dicit hic Dominus, et contra tales dicit Hieronymus[^p411-12]: « Impossibile est, ut praesentibus et futuris quis fruatur bonis, ut hic ventrem, ibi mentem impleat; ut de deliciis ad delicias transeat; ut hic cum saeculo gaudeat et ibi cum Deo regnet; ut in utroque saeculo primus in caelo et in terra appareat gloriosus ».

25. *Audiebant autem omnia haec* etc. Post documentum informativum humilium subditur documentum *repressivum rebellium*[^p411-13]. Et quia Pharisaei rebelles erant doctrinae Christi hominis propter elationem, quam habebant de meritis, et haec elatio veniebat ex litterali observantia Legis; ideo pars ista habet duas, in quarum prima describitur *repressio*
<!-- page 412 -->
*arrogantiae Pharisaeorum,* in secunda vero, *evacuatio observantiae legalium praeceptorum,* ibi[^p412-1]: *Lex et Prophetae usque ad Ioannem,* ut sic ex ostensione evacuationis Legis auferatur inanis gloriatio a Pharisaeis.

Primo igitur *repressio arrogantiae* Pharisaeorum describitur per hunc modum: praemittitur enim Pharisaeorum *rebellio,* subditur *rebellionis reprehensio* et subiungitur *reprehensionis ratio.*

26. (Vers. 14.). Primo igitur quantum ad *Pharisaeorum rebellionem,* qua Christum deridebant, dicitur: *Audiebant autem omnia haec Pharisaei, qui avari erant, et deridebant eum*[^p412-2]. *Deridebant* autem eum tanquam *superbi* de *verbis simplicitatis,* secundum illud Iob duodecimo: « Deridetur iusti simplicitas; lampas contempta apud cogitationes divitum ». Et sicut *stolidi* de *consiliis utilitatis;* Proverbiorum decimo quinto: « Stultus irridet disciplinam patris sui » etc. Vel sicut *stupidi* de *monitis pietatis,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p412-3]: « Furor illis secundum similitudinem serpentis, sicut aspidis surdae et obturantis aures suas »; unde Glossa: « Illi praeceptorem misericordiae et humilitatis et parsimoniae derident, quasi minus utilia et nunquam facienda noxius imperaret ». Vel sicut *cupidi* derident de *laudibus largitatis;* unde Glossa[^p412-4]: « *Deridebant,* intelligentes scilicet, parabolam adversus se dictam, et carnalia praesentia futuris spiritualibus quasi incertis praeponentes ». Et hoc est quod dicitur Proverbiorum decimo octavo: « Non recipit stultus verba prudentiae, nisi dixeris ea quae versantur in corde suo ». Vel sicut *legisperiti* de *commendationibus paupertatis;* unde Glossa[^p412-5]: « Disputantem contra philargyriam deridebant quasi contrarium Legi et Prophetis, cum in Lege multi divites Deo placuerint, et cum Lex suis observatoribus bona terrae promittat ». Unde Proverbiorum nono: « Et qui arguit impium sibi maculam generat ». Et hoc totum veniebat ex rebellione quadam elata et stolida.

27. (Vers. 15.). Secundo quantum ad *rebellium reprehensionem,* qua Christus eorum supercilium deprimebat, subditur: *Et ait illis: Vos estis, qui iustificatis vos coram hominibus,* per ostentationem iustitiae vestrae, qua vos ipsos iustos *reputatis;* ad Romanos decimo[^p412-6]: « Ignorantes iustitiam Dei et suam volentes statuere, iustitiae Dei non sunt subiecti ». Qua etiam iustos vos *dicitis,* contra illud Iob nono: « Si iustificare me voluero, os meum condemnabit me; si innocentem, pravum me comprobabit ». Qua etiam iustos vos *exterius praetenditis,* contra illud Matthaei sexto: « Attendite, ne iustitiam vestram faciatis coram hominibus, ut videamini ab eis ». — Et quoniam talis iustificatio parit culpam, ideo addit: *Deus autem novit corda vestra,* per detestationem superbiae; unde non *novit* ad probandum, sed ad iudicandum; Psalmus[^p412-7]: « Excelsus Dominus, et humilia respicit et alta a longe cognoscit ». Unde dicitur Proverbiorum decimo sexto: « Omnes viae hominum patent oculis eius, spirituum ponderator est Dominus »; Ieremiae decimo septimo: « Pravum est cor hominum et inscrutabile, et quis cognoscet illud »? Parum igitur valet vobis humano testimonio commendari, cum sitis reprobandi iudicio Dei, qui scrutator est cordis. Ideo primae ad Corinthios quarto[^p412-8]: « Nolite ante tempus iudicare, quoadusque veniat Dominus, qui et illuminabit abscondita tenebrarum ». Unde quia iustitia ista non erat veritatis, sed vanitatis; non ad rem, sed ad hominem; non intra, sed extra: potius erat culpa detestanda quam virtus commendanda, potius digna poena quam retributione aeterna. Unde Matthaei quinto[^p412-9]: « Nisi abundaverit iustitia vestra plus quam scribarum et Pharisaeorum, non intrabitis in regnum caelorum ».

28. Tertio quantum ad *reprehensionis rationem,* qua divinum iudicium exprimebat, subdit: *Quia quod hominibus altum est abominatio est ante Deum.* Nota, quod non reprehendit *altitudinem perfectionis,* de qua secundae ad Corinthios octavo[^p412-10]: « Altissima paupertas eorum abundavit in divitias simplicitatis eorum » etc. Nec *altitudinem contemplationis,* de qua ad Romanos undecimo: « O altitudo divitiarum sapientiae et scientiae Dei! quam incomprehensibilia sunt iudicia eius » etc.; et Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto: « Ego in altissimis habitavi, et thronus meus in columna nubis ». Nec *altitudinem desiderii et exspectationis,* de quo in Psalmo[^p412-11]: « Tu es, Domine, spes mea; altissimum posuisti refugium tuum ». Hae enim *altitudines sunt*
<!-- page 413 -->
*ante Deum.* — Sed est *alia altitudo,* quae elongat ab Altissimo, et haec est *altitudo praesumtionis,* de qua Ieremiae quadragesimo octavo[^p413-1]: « Audivimus superbiam Moab, superbus est valde; sublimitatem eius et arrogantiam et superbiam et altitudinem cordis eius ego scio, ait Dominus ». Haec etiam est *altitudo ambitionis;* Ieremiae quadragesimo nono: « Arrogantia tua decepit te, qui niteris apprehendere altitudinem collis ». Haec etiam est *altitudo ostentationis;* Ezechielis trigesimo primo[^p413-2]: « Pro eo quod sublimatus est in altitudine et dedit summitatem suam virentem atque condensam, et elevatum est cor eius in altitudine sua; nunc tradidi eum in manus fortissimi gentium » etc. Ista altitudo in conspectu hominum est *abominatio apud Deum,* secundum illud Proverbiorum decimo sexto[^p413-3]: « Abominatio est Domino omnis arrogans, etiam si manus ad manum fuerit, non est innocens ». Dicitur autem Dominus *abominari* huiusmodi altitudinem propter severam ipsius deiectionem, secundum illud Isaiae secundo[^p413-4]: « Oculi sublimes hominis humiliati sunt, et incurvabitur altitudo virorum, exaltabitur autem Dominus solus in die illa ».

29. Ideo haec triplex altitudo tanquam Domino abominabilis dissuadetur, primo scilicet *altitudo praesumtionis;* Ecclesiastici tertio[^p413-5]: « Altiora te ne quaesieris »; et ad Romanos undecimo: « Noli altum sapere » etc. Secundo, *altitudo ambitionis;* ad Romanos duodecimo: « Non alta sapientes, sed humilibus consentientes » etc. Dissuadetur tertio *altitudo ostentationis;* Psalmus: « Nolite extollere in altum cornu vestrum, nolite loqui adversus Deum iniquitatem ». — Hanc autem altitudinem abominatur Dominus, quia *fastuosa;* primi Regum decimo sexto[^p413-6]: « Ne respicias altitudinem staturae eius, quoniam abieci eum ». Quia *simulata;* Danielis tertio: « Nabuchodonosor rex fecit statuam auream altitudine cubitorum sexaginta » etc. Quia *ruinosa;* propter quod Proverbiorum decimo septimo[^p413-7]: « Qui altam facit domum suam quaerit ruinam ». Et ideo dicitur secundae ad Corinthios decimo: « Arma militiae nostrae non sunt carnalia, sed potentia Deo ad destructionem munitionum, consilia destruentes et omnem altitudinem extollentem se adversus scientiam Dei, et in captivitatem redigentes omnem intellectum in obsequium Christi ».

30. (Vers. 16.). *Lex et Prophetae usque ad Ioannem* etc. Post repressionem arrogantiae Pharisaeorum rebellium subditur hic secundo descriptio *evacuationis praeceptorum legalium*[^p413-8], quorum causa Pharisaei, erigentes supercilium, Christi documenta spernebant et suam avaritiam palliabant. Et quoniam lex Moysaica per Christum est evacuata, ita quod manet quantum ad *spiritualem intelligentiam caeremonialium* et *litteralem observantiam moralium;* ideo in hac parte insinuat non solum ipsius *Legis evacuationem* secundum *observantiam litteralem,* verum etiam *impletionem* secundum *intelligentiam spiritualem* et *durationem* secundum *ius morale et copulam coniugalem.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *evacuationem Legis* secundum *observantiam litteralem* dicit: *Lex et Prophetae usque ad Ioannem;* quasi dicat Pharisaeis: non debetis supercilium erigere pro Legis observantia, quia[^p413-9] iam cessat quantum ad litteram et figuram. Et nota, quod, licet Christus dicere posset: *usque ad me,* quia, ad Romanos decimo[^p413-10], « finis Legis Christus ad iustitiam » etc.; maluit tamen nominare Praecursorem tanquam suam vocem, quia per ipsius Ioannis ministerium ostensus est digito Agnus; Ioannis primo: « Ecce, Agnus Dei; ecce, qui tollit » etc.; et ipse iam incepit ostendere veritatem apertam ac per hoc evacuare figuram. Quia enim Lex et Prophetae omnes promittebant Christum futurum; et Ioannes ostendit praesentem: ideo « *Lex et Prophetae usque ad Ioannem,* quia non potuit ultra prophetari *futurum* quod claret *venisse* testimonio Ioannis[^p413-11] ».

31. Et quia finis Legis est per inchoationem novi testamenti, ideo sequitur: *Ex eo*[^p413-12] *regnum Dei evangelizatur,* id est, ab eo Evangelium gratiae inchoatur. Unde Marci primo[^p413-13]: « Initium Evangelii Iesu Christi, sicut scriptum est in Isaia: Ecce, ego mitto Angelum meum » etc.; ideo etiam beatus Lucas ab ipsa beati Ioannis conceptione in suo Evangelio exordium sumsit; supra primo: « Fuit in diebus Herodis ». Unde ut ipsius praedicatio *nova* ostenderetur,
<!-- page 414 -->
dicitur Marci primo[^p414-1], quod « erat baptizans et praedicans baptismum poenitentiae in remissionem peccatorum ». — Et quia praedicatio Ioannis fuit de poenitentia, in qua homo facit sibi violentiam; ideo addit: *Et omnis in illud vim facit,* quod expressius dicitur Matthaei undecimo[^p414-2]: « A diebus Ioannis Baptistae regnum caelorum vim patitur, et violenti rapiunt illud ». Haec autem est violentia, qua quis *excedit virtutem naturalem;* unde Beda[^p414-3]: « Magna vis est, cum de terra genitus caelum quaerit possidere per virtutem, qui non potuit tenere per naturam ». Haec etiam est violentia, quae *vincit incurvationem naturae;* unde Glossa[^p414-4]: « Faciamus vim naturae, ut non ad terrena demergat, sed ad superna se erigat ». Haec etiam est violentia, qua quis *vincit torporem pigritiae;* unde Beda[^p414-5]: « Omnis, qui vim facit, vehementi studio properat, non torpenti lentescit affectu. Est ergo fidei religiosa violentia, segnitia criminosa ». Magna enim violentia est, dum per vim et rigorem poenitentiae mitigat quis severitatem divinae sententiae, dum per orationem humilem inclinat districtionem sublimem. In cuius figuram dictum est Iacob, strenuo luctatori, Genesis trigesimo secundo[^p414-6]: « Si contra Deum fortis fuisti, multo magis contra homines praevalebis ».

32. Et nota, quod beatus Bernardus[^p414-7] distinguit quatuor genera hominum possidentium regnum caelorum: « Alii namque violenter *rapiunt,* alii *mercantur,* alii *furantur,* alii *compelluntur* ad illud ». « Illi namque *rapiunt,* qui derelinquunt omnia et sequuntur Christum[^p414-8] », ad quos referri potest illud Genesis penultimo: « Catulus leonis Iuda, ad praedam ascendisti, fili mi »; et iterum ibidem: « Beniamin, lupus rapax, mane rapiet praedam et vespere dividet spolia ». — « *Mercantur* autem qui dant temporalia, ut accipiant aeterna »; de quibus supra duodecimo[^p414-9]: « Facite vobis sacculos, qui non veterascunt »; et supra eodem: « Facite vobis amicos de mammona iniquitatis ». Talibus competit illud Augustini[^p414-10]: « Venale habeo, quid? Regnum caelorum. Quomodo emitur? Paupertate regnum, vilitate gloria, dolore gaudium, labore requies, morte vita ». — « *Furantur* autem qui bona occulte faciunt, et laudem humanam vitantes, solo divino testimonio sunt contenti »; quibus competit illud Matthaei sexto[^p414-11]: « Tu autem, cum ieiunas, unge caput tuum et faciem tuam lava ». — « *Compelluntur* autem intrare pauperes, quos hic ignis paupertatis, Deo dispensante, probat, ne in futuro ignis iudicii damnabiliter premat »; de his supra decimo quarto[^p414-12]: « Exi in vias et sepes et compelle intrare, ut impleatur domus mea ». Licet igitur tempore Legis esset tempus *mercandi,* nunc post Ioannem est tempus *rapiendi.* Nam, sicut tunc promittebatur opulentia, sic nunc suadetur paupertas.

33. (Vers. 17.). Secundo quantum ad *impletionem Legis* secundum *intelligentiam spiritualem* subditur: *Facilius est autem, caelum et terram praeterire,* per conflagrationem mundi; Psalmus[^p414-13]: « Et tu in principio terram fundasti. Ipsi peribunt » etc. — *Quam de Lege unum apicem cadere,* per *frustrationem alicuius promissi.* Rabanus[^p414-14]: « *Caelum et terra transibunt,* per commutationem innovationis deposita forma, manente priori substantia; sed verba Domini sine effectu completionis nullo modo transibunt ». Ideo dicitur Matthaei vigesimo quarto[^p414-15]: « Caelum et terra transibunt, verba autem mea non transibunt »; quia, sicut dicitur in Psalmo, « in aeternum, Domine, permanet verbum tuum ». Unde licet lex vetus videatur transitoria, manens tamen est magis quam mundana creatura. Manet etiam per *divini verbi spiritualem intelligentiam;* unde Matthaei quinto[^p414-16]: « Non veni solvere Legem, sed adimplere. Amen quippe dico vobis, iota unum aut unus apex non praeteribit a Lege, donec omnia fiant ». — Et nota, quod dicit *unum apicem; apex* enim proprie dicitur summitas litterae, quae ponitur ad ipsius litterae decorem et est quasi minimum quiddam. In quo datur intelligi, quod nihil omnino, nec
<!-- page 415 -->
parvum nec magnum, est in Scriptura, nec aliquid, quod non habeat completionem suam[^p415-1]. Omnia enim referuntur ad salutem humanam, quae habebit durationem perpetuam, transeunte etiam mundana creatura secundum exteriorem faciem; Isaiae quinquagesimo primo[^p415-2]: « Caeli sicut fumus liquescent, et terra sicut vestimentum atteretur etc. Salus autem mea in sempiternum erit, et iustitia mea non deficiet ». Unde liber Scripturae est proximior libro vitae quam liber creaturae, et ideo inexpletus remanere non potest.

34. (Vers. 18.). Tertio quantum ad *durationem Legis* secundum *ius naturae* et *copulam coniugalem* subdit: *Omnis, qui dimittit uxorem suam et ducit alteram, moechatur;* primo, quia non potest primam dimittere nisi ex sola causa fornicationis, secundum illud Matthaei decimo nono[^p415-3]: « Quicumque dimiserit uxorem nisi ob fornicationem et aliam duxerit, moechatur ». Secundo, quia quacumque ex causa illam dimittat, non ei licet, illa vivente, aliam ducere, et hoc secundum legem naturae divinitus institutam in hominis formatione; Genesis secundo[^p415-4]: « Relinquet homo patrem suum et matrem » etc. Et haec est quae dictat, quod « matrimonium est maris et feminae coniunctio, individuam vitae retinens consuetudinem »; quae etiam dictat, quod illud est vinculum insolubile. — Et ideo subdit: *Et qui dimissam a viro duxerit*[^p415-5] *moechatur.* Et ratio huius est, quia uxor est illius cuius primo fuit, quantumcumque videatur separari; unde Apostolus ad Romanos septimo: « Quae sub viro est mulier, vivente viro, alligata est legi. Igitur vivente viro, vocabitur adultera, si fuerit cum altero viro ». Et ideo Ieremiae tertio: « Si dimiserit vir uxorem, et recedens ab eo duxerit virum alterum; nunquid revertetur ad eam ultra? Nunquid non polluta et contaminata erit mulier illa »? — Notandum est autem, quod, licet nulla mentio fieret de uxoribus, Dominus tamen, volens ostendere *statum* Legis quantum ad *moralia* et *transitum* quantum ad *caeremonialia* et *iudicialia,* exemplificat de matrimonio, pro eo quod in ipso *libellus* repudii dabatur. Et matrimonii lex in Evangelio confirmatur tanquam ius *morale,* et libellus repudii reprobatur; unde Matthaei decimo nono[^p415-6]: « Moyses mandavit dare libellum repudii et dimittere. Ait illis: Quia Moyses ad duritiam cordis vestri permisit vobis dimittere uxores vestras ». — Ex his igitur colligitur, qualiter Lex *manet,* qualiter *evacuatur,* qualiter *impletur,* et nihilominus Pharisaeorum supercilium de littera Legis gloriantium confutatur.

## English
<!-- page 407 -->

#### The instruction of perfect piety, as to two things.

13. *And I say to you* etc. After the example of provident piety is subjoined *the instruction of perfect piety*[^p407-10], which indeed is drawn out from the sense of the parable said above. First, then, the instruction is drawn out for *the formation of the obedient disciples;* second, for *the confutation of the rebellious Pharisees,* at[^p407-11]: *Now the Pharisees heard all these things* etc.

Concerning the *formation,* then, *of the disciples to piety,* he proceeds in this order. For he invites them to mercy, first, by reason of *the reward to be had;* second, by reason of *the fidelity to be kept,* at[^p407-12]: *He that is faithful in that which is least;* third, by reason of *the duplicity to be avoided,* at: *No one can serve two masters.* — And by reason of *the reward to be had* he invites in this order, namely by setting first *a schooling exhortation* and by adding *a moving reason.*

14. (Vers. 9.). First, then, as to *the schooling exhortation,* he sets first: *And I say to you: Make unto you friends of the mammon of iniquity.*
<!-- page 408 -->
« *Mammon,* as the Gloss says[^p408-1], in the tongue of the Syrians is what riches are called; and riches are called riches *of iniquity,* because they frequently have iniquity annexed, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter[^p408-2]: « If thou be rich, thou shalt not be free from sin »; whence Augustine: « Every rich man is either iniquitous, or the heir of an iniquitous man »; or as Bede says in a homily[^p408-3]: « *Mammon,* of *mammon,* in the Punic tongue is called gain ». Nor ought it to be understood that from that which one has acquired by rapine and iniquity and possesses by an unjust title one ought to make alms, because against this it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p408-4]: « The most High approveth not the gifts of the wicked »; and again in the same chapter: « He that offereth sacrifice of the substance of the poor is as one that sacrificeth the son in the presence of his father »; but it is understood of riches which are possessed by a just title, and yet beget iniquity unless they are distributed, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the tenth chapter[^p408-5]: « There is nothing more iniquitous than to love money: for such a one setteth even his own soul to sale ». Of these riches, I say, friends ought to be made through the bestowal of alms, which manifest the affection of piety. For it is said in the first of John, the third chapter[^p408-6]: « He that hath the substance of this world, and shall see his brother in need, and shall shut up his bowels from him, how doth the charity of God abide in him »? and therefore in Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-ninth chapter: « Lose thy money for thy friend, and hide it not under a stone to be lost », because, in Proverbs, the eleventh chapter, « he that hideth up corn shall be cursed among the people » etc.

15. Second, as to *the moving reason,* he subjoins: *That when you shall fail, they may receive you into everlasting tabernacles.* From which it is understood why the gift is to be given, and what ought to move a man to the making of alms: not *a present advantage,* but *an eternal reward;* not for the sake of *a passing tabernacle,* but *an eternal one,* according to that of Matthew, the sixth chapter[^p408-7]: « When thou dost alms, sound not a trumpet before thee, as the hypocrites do. Amen I say to you » etc.; and after: « But when thou dost alms, let not thy left hand know what thy right hand doth ». Now these eternal tabernacles are the mansions of glory, of which Isaiah, the thirty-second chapter[^p408-8]: « My people shall sit in the beauty of peace, in the tabernacles of confidence, in wealthy rest ». *Into everlasting tabernacles they receive* others who are so perfect that their merits avail for others also, and on account of which merits they acquire everlasting tabernacles for themselves and for others. Whence upon that[^p408-9]: « Make unto you friends », the Gloss says: « Not any poor whatever, but those who can receive into everlasting tabernacles ».

16. For such are not the involuntary poor, but the poor in spirit; of whom Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p408-10]: « Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven ». It belongs to them, therefore, as to *the kings and possessors* of that kingdom, to bring into the kingdom of heaven. To them also as to *judges,* according to that of Job, the thirty-sixth chapter[^p408-11]: « He will not save the wicked, but he giveth judgement to the poor »; to the poor, I say, the perfect and voluntary, of whom Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « You, who have left all things and have followed me, shall sit on twelve seats, judging the twelve tribes of Israel ». To them also as to *intimates and friends,* on account of which James, the second chapter[^p408-12]: « Hath not God chosen the poor in this world, rich in faith, heirs of the kingdom which God hath promised » etc. Such bring in their friends and benefactors through that judicial law by which the Lord will pass sentence in the future judgement, according to what Matthew says in the twenty-fifth chapter[^p408-13]: « What you did to one of the least, you did to me », and through that other law which the Lord laid down, of which Matthew, the tenth chapter: « He that receiveth a just man in the name of a just man shall receive the reward of a just man ».

17. (Vers. 10.). *He that is faithful in that which is least* etc. After he invited to piety from the consideration of the reward to be had, here, second, he invites from the consideration of *the fidelity to be kept*[^p408-14]. For fidelity is to be kept in the dispensing of temporal things, both on account of *merit and demerit,* and on account of *peril and loss.*

First, then, as to the consideration of *the merit of fidelity* in such earthly things, he says: *He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in that which is greater;* as if
<!-- page 409 -->
he were saying: although these temporal things are to be despised as least things, yet the faithful dispensing of them ought not to be despised, both because the fidelity is the same in a great thing and a small, and because the faithful dispensing of temporal things disposes to the faithful dispensing and keeping of spiritual and eternal things. Whence the Gloss[^p409-1]: « *He that is faithful in that which is least,* that is, in money to be shared with the poor man, *is faithful also in that which is greater,* adhering to the Creator ». And therefore it is said to such a servant in the Apocalypse, the second chapter[^p409-2]: « Be thou faithful until death, and I will give thee the crown of life »; likewise Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter: « Well done, good and faithful servant, because thou hast been faithful over a few things, I will place thee over many things; enter thou into the joy of thy lord ». Whence fidelity about small things sets down fidelity also about great ones; and on the contrary, faithlessness about the least things sets down faithlessness about greater ones.

18. And therefore he subjoins: *And he that is iniquitous in that which is little is iniquitous also in that which is greater.* Everything that is possessed temporally is rightly called *little,* both because it is of little *quantity;* Wisdom, the eleventh chapter[^p409-3]: « For the whole world before thee is as the least grain of the balance »; and because it is of little *duration;* Wisdom, the fifth chapter: « All these things are passed away like a shadow »; and the Psalm: « They have slept their sleep, and all the men of riches have found nothing » etc.; and because it is of little *pleasantness;* whence Gregory[^p409-4]: « The goods of the present life are few, although they seem many », because they are not without the admixture of some trouble; but the eternal goods are many, which are without any corruption. He, then, who is iniquitous in these cannot have the charity of God, because for small things he despises the greatest. For he sells faith and justice for that which is worth nothing; whence Augustine[^p409-5]: « Gain in the coffer, loss in the conscience; he took a garment, and lost faith; he acquires money, and has lost justice ». By dispensing money badly, then, he loses felicity, loses charity, loses justice, which are the greatest gifts of God. Whence the Gloss[^p409-6]: « He who does not dispense well the temporal things which he possesses makes void for himself the glory of eternal things, with which he swells. *For if he love not his brother whom he seeth, how can he love God whom he seeth not? And if having substance he see his brother in need* etc., *how doth the charity of the Father abide in him* »? as is had in the first of John, the third chapter.

19. (Vers. 11.). Second, as to the consideration of *peril and loss,* he subjoins: *If then you have not been faithful in the iniquitous mammon, who will entrust to you that which is true?* as if he were saying: great is the peril of dispensing badly these temporal goods, because on account of unfaithful dispensing one loses the true riches. Whence the Gloss[^p409-7]: « If you do not dispense well carnal riches, which slip away, who will give you the true and eternal riches »? And riches are called *the iniquitous mammon,* because, as the Gloss says[^p409-8], « the iniquitous mammon presides over riches, to tempt by them »; as he tempted Ananias, of whom Acts, the fifth chapter: « Why hath Satan tempted thy heart » etc. That temptation was concerning theft; likewise it is said of Achor in Josue, the seventh chapter. And that good is called *true* which always remains. For all these temporal things, because they pass, are vain, according to that of Ecclesiastes, the first chapter[^p409-9]: « Vanity of vanities, and all is vanity ». But that *true* thing is the grace of God, or the glory of God, or rather God himself, who is the reward of the good; and therefore the Psalm: « The truth of the Lord remaineth for ever »; and again: « Unto generation and generation thy truth ». And in this truth beatitude consists, according to that of Augustine in the book of the *Confessions*[^p409-10]: « Beatitude is joy in the truth »; for because « the true is that which is », he alone is pure truth who says: « I am who am », Exodus, the third chapter. And this *true* thing is neither granted nor entrusted except to the truthful and the faithful; whence John, the third chapter[^p409-11]: « Every one that doth evil hateth the light, and cometh not to the light, that his works may not be reproved. But he that doth truth cometh to the light, that his works may be made manifest, because they are done in God »; and John, the eighth chapter: « If you continue in my word, you shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free ». Great, then, is the peril of faithlessness in these small things, because for the *vain* the true is lost, for the *small* is lost[^p409-12] the great.

<!-- page 410 -->

20. (Vers. 12.). Nevertheless, because for *what is another's* one's own is lost, therefore he subjoins: *And if you have not been faithful in that which is another's,* through the due dispensing of earthly good; whence the Gloss[^p410-1]: « Whatever is of the world is another's to us, because it is beside nature ». These indeed are called *another's goods,* because we can neither bring them with us nor carry them away; the first to Timothy, the last chapter[^p410-2]: « We brought nothing into this world, and doubtless we can carry nothing out »; and Job, the first chapter: « Naked came I out of my mother's womb, and naked shall I return thither ». — *Who will give you*[^p410-3] *that which is your own?* through the attainment of the heavenly reward. For that is *ours* to which we were created and foreordained, and which, once had, cannot be lost; whence Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p410-4]: « Come, ye blessed of my Father, possess the kingdom prepared for you » etc.; and above, the sixth chapter: « Blessed are ye poor, for yours is the kingdom of God ». And this kingdom is nothing other than God and the Lord Jesus Christ; whence the Gloss[^p410-5]: « *That which is your own,* that is, the things which are properly assigned to man: Christ is ours, because Christ is our life »; to the Colossians, the third chapter: « When Christ shall appear, who is your life, then you also shall appear » etc.

21. (Vers. 13.). *No one*[^p410-6] *can serve two masters* etc. After the invitation to piety from the consideration of the reward to be had and of the fidelity to be kept, here, third, he invites from the consideration of *the duplicity to be shunned*[^p410-7], because piety toward God and cupidity toward the world cannot be had together; which indeed he does in this order, namely setting first *the reprobation of duplicity,* adjoining *the assertion of the reprobation.*

First, then, as to *the reprobation of duplicity* he says: *No one can serve two masters.* This proposition is known through itself; on account of which it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the second chapter[^p410-8]: « Woe to a double heart, and to wicked lips etc., and to the sinner that goeth on the earth two ways »! and James, the first chapter: « A double-minded man is inconstant in all his ways ». And therefore it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the first chapter: « Come not to him with a double heart », that is, do not will to serve two masters at once, because this cannot be done rightly. — Now the two masters, divided from each other, who cannot be served at once, are in a fourfold difference. For these masters are *vice* and *virtue;* on account of which the third of Kings, the eighteenth chapter[^p410-9]: « How long do you halt between two sides? If the Lord be God, follow him; but if Baal » etc.; and Isaiah, the twenty-eighth chapter: « The bed is straitened, so that one must fall out, and a short covering cannot cover both ». — These masters are also *the flesh* and *the spirit;* to the Galatians, the fifth chapter[^p410-10]: « The flesh lusteth against the spirit, but the spirit against the flesh. For these are contrary one to another, so that you do not the things that you would ». Therefore to the Romans, the eighth chapter: « If you live according to the flesh, you shall die; but if by the spirit you mortify the deeds of the flesh, you shall live ». — Again, these masters are *Christ* and *the devil;* the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter[^p410-11]: » What concord hath Christ with Belial »? and the first to the Corinthians, the tenth chapter: « You cannot drink the chalice of the Lord and the chalice of devils; you cannot be partakers of the table of the Lord and of the table of devils » etc. — Again, the masters are *God* and this *world;* James, the fourth chapter[^p410-12]: « Adulterers, know you not that the friendship of this world is the enemy of God? Whosoever will be a friend of this world becometh an enemy of God »; and therefore the first of John, the second chapter: « Love not the world, because if any man love the world, the charity of the Father is not in him ». — And it is of these two masters that he means it, dissuading worldly cupidity and inviting to divine piety. Whence the Gloss[^p410-13]: « Correcting avarice, he says that the lovers of money cannot love God; let him, then, who loves God despise money ». From which it appears that he who wills to be a servant of Christ cannot be a servant of this world; whence Bede[^p410-14]: « You ought to distribute these temporal things faithfully as masters of temporal things, not as servants ».

22. Second, as to *the assertion of the reprobation,* he subjoins: *For either he will hate the one and love the other;* which is said as to *charity,* which makes one hate evil and love good, according to that of Romans, the twelfth chapter[^p410-15]: « Hating evil,
<!-- page 411 -->
cleaving to good, loving one another with the charity of brotherhood »; and the Psalm: « I have hated the unjust, and have loved thy law ». — *Or he will cleave to the one and despise the other;* which is said as to *cupidity,* which cleaves to the world and to the devil in contempt of God, according to that of Jeremiah, the second chapter[^p411-1]: « My people have done two evils: they have forsaken me, the fountain of living water, and have digged to themselves cisterns that can hold no water ».

23. Now the reason why one cannot serve the aforesaid masters at once is fourfold: the first, namely, *the opposition of the ministries.* For the service of God is in the exercise of *virtues,* according to that above, the first chapter[^p411-2]: « Let us serve him in holiness and justice before him »; and the first of Peter, the first chapter: « According to him who hath called you, the holy one, that you also may be holy » etc. But the service of the devil is in the exercise of *vices;* whence above, in the fifteenth chapter, it is said of the prodigal son that « he sent him into his farm to feed swine ». — The second reason is *the unlikeness of the ministers,* because the Lord wills to have *eyed* servants; on account of which it is said in the Apocalypse, the fourth chapter[^p411-3], that « the living creatures were full of eyes before and behind »; and Proverbs, the fourteenth chapter: « A wise servant is acceptable to the king ». But the devil wills to have *one-eyed* servants; whence the first of Kings, the eleventh chapter: « On this condition will I make a covenant with you, that I may pluck out the right eyes of you all ». The Lord also wills to have ministers *composed and ordered;* whence the third of Kings, the tenth chapter[^p411-4]: « The queen of Saba seeing the apartments of the servants and the order of the ministers » etc. But the devil wills to have them *uncomposed and deformed;* whence the second of Kings, the tenth chapter: « Hanon, king of the children of Ammon, cut away the garments of David's servants even to the buttocks, and shaved off half their beard ». The Lord also wills to have *humble* servants, according to that of the Psalm[^p411-5]: « He that worketh pride shall not dwell in the midst of my house; he that speaketh unjust things » etc. But the devil wills to have *the arrogant;* Job, the last chapter but one: « He beholdeth every high thing, and he is king over all the children of pride ». — The third reason is *the remoteness of the places,* because the Lord wills to be in a place *lofty and clean;* the Psalm[^p411-6]: « To thee have I lifted up my eyes, who dwellest in heaven »; and the Canticle, the second chapter: « Behold, he cometh leaping upon the mountains, skipping over the hills ». But the devil, in *unclean* places; Job, the fortieth chapter: « He sleepeth under the shadow, in the covert of the reed, in moist places ». — The fourth reason is *the indiscreteness of the times,* because the devil wills that *he* be served at every time; Jeremiah, the sixteenth chapter[^p411-7]: « You shall serve strange gods, who will not give you rest day and night » etc. Likewise the Lord wills that *he* be served at every time; the Psalm: « On his law he shall meditate day and night » etc. Since, then, the Lord wills to have the whole time and the whole heart, according to that of Deuteronomy, the sixth chapter[^p411-8]: « Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with thy whole heart, and with thy whole soul, and with thy whole strength »; and the devil and the world likewise will the whole: therefore it is impossible to serve both at once.

24. And therefore he infers: *You cannot serve God and mammon.* Now he is said *to serve mammon* in whose affection riches have dominion, so that he can be called *avaricious,* because then he is a servant of idols. For, as is said to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter[^p411-9], « avarice is a serving of idols ». Whence the Gloss: « He does not say *to have riches,* but *to serve.* He serves who guards riches as a servant; but he who has shaken off the yoke of servitude distributes as a master ». And therefore the Apostle said in the first to the Corinthians, the seventh chapter[^p411-10]: « It remaineth that they that buy be as though they possessed not, and they that use this world as if they used it not » etc. But some will to have God and the world at once, who are like those nations of whom the fourth of Kings, the seventeenth chapter[^p411-11]: « The nations were fearing the Lord, and nevertheless serving idols ». Against these it is said in Leviticus, the nineteenth chapter: « Thou shalt not wear a garment that is woven of two sorts », and against these the Lord speaks here, and against such Jerome speaks[^p411-12]: « It is impossible that one should enjoy present and future goods, that he should fill the belly here and the mind there; that he should pass from delights to delights; that he should rejoice with the world here and reign with God there; that in both worlds he should appear first, glorious in heaven and on earth ».

25. *Now the Pharisees heard all these things* etc. After the instruction forming the humble is subjoined the instruction *repressing the rebellious*[^p411-13]. And because the Pharisees were rebellious against the doctrine of Christ the man on account of the elation which they had from their merits, and this elation came from the literal observance of the Law; therefore this part has two, in the first of which is described *the repression*
<!-- page 412 -->
*of the arrogance of the Pharisees;* in the second, *the voiding of the observance of the legal precepts,* at[^p412-1]: *The law and the prophets were until John,* so that thus, from the showing of the voiding of the Law, vain glorying may be taken away from the Pharisees.

First, then, *the repression of the arrogance* of the Pharisees is described in this manner: for there is set first the *rebellion* of the Pharisees, there is subjoined *the reprehension of the rebellion,* and there is joined on *the reason of the reprehension.*

26. (Vers. 14.). First, then, as to *the rebellion of the Pharisees,* by which they derided Christ, it is said: *Now the Pharisees, who were covetous, heard all these things, and they derided him*[^p412-2]. And they *derided* him as *proud men* deride *words of simplicity,* according to that of Job, the twelfth chapter: « The simplicity of the just man is laughed to scorn; the lamp despised in the thoughts of the rich ». And as *dullards* deride *counsels of usefulness;* Proverbs, the fifteenth chapter: « A fool laugheth at the instruction of his father » etc. Or as *the stupid* deride *admonitions of piety,* according to that of the Psalm[^p412-3]: « Their madness is according to the likeness of a serpent, like the deaf asp that stoppeth her ears »; whence the Gloss: « They deride the teacher of mercy and of humility and of parsimony, as though he were harmfully commanding things less useful and never to be done ». Or as *the cupidinous* deride *praises of largess;* whence the Gloss[^p412-4]: « *They derided,* understanding, namely, the parable spoken against themselves, and setting carnal present things before future spiritual things as though uncertain ». And this is what is said in Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter: « A fool receiveth not the words of prudence, unless thou say those things which are in his heart ». Or as *lawyers* deride *commendations of poverty;* whence the Gloss[^p412-5]: « They derided him disputing against love of money, as contrary to the Law and the Prophets, since in the Law many rich men pleased God, and since the Law promises the goods of the earth to its observers ». Whence Proverbs, the ninth chapter: « And he that rebuketh the wicked getteth himself a blot ». And this whole came from a certain elated and dull rebellion.

27. (Vers. 15.). Second, as to *the reprehension of the rebellious,* by which Christ put down their haughtiness, there is subjoined: *And he said to them: You are they who justify yourselves before men,* through the ostentation of your justice, by which you *reckon* yourselves just; to the Romans, the tenth chapter[^p412-6]: « Not knowing the justice of God, and seeking to establish their own, they have not submitted themselves to the justice of God ». By which also you *call* yourselves just, against that of Job, the ninth chapter: « If I would justify myself, my own mouth shall condemn me; if innocent, he shall prove me wicked ». By which also you *outwardly pretend* yourselves just, against that of Matthew, the sixth chapter: « Take heed that you do not your justice before men, to be seen by them ». — And since such justification begets fault, therefore he adds: *But God knoweth your hearts,* through the detestation of pride; whence he does not *know* in order to approve, but in order to judge; the Psalm[^p412-7]: « The Lord is high, and looketh on the low, and the high he knoweth afar off ». Whence it is said in Proverbs, the sixteenth chapter: « All the ways of men are open to his eyes; the Lord is the weigher of spirits »; Jeremiah, the seventeenth chapter: « The heart of men is perverse and unsearchable, and who can know it »? It avails you little, then, to be commended by human testimony, since you are to be reprobated by the judgement of God, who is the searcher of the heart. Therefore the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter[^p412-8]: « Judge not before the time, until the Lord come, who both will bring to light the hidden things of darkness ». Whence, because that justice was not of truth but of vanity, not for the thing but for the man, not within but without, it was rather a fault to be detested than a virtue to be commended, rather worthy of punishment than of eternal retribution. Whence Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p412-9]: « Unless your justice abound more than that of the scribes and Pharisees, you shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven ».

28. Third, as to *the reason of the reprehension,* by which he expressed the divine judgement, he subjoins: *For that which is high to men is an abomination before God.* Note that he does not reprehend *the height of perfection,* of which the second to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter[^p412-10]: « Their most high poverty abounded unto the riches of their simplicity » etc. Nor *the height of contemplation,* of which to the Romans, the eleventh chapter: « O the height of the riches of the wisdom and of the knowledge of God! How incomprehensible are his judgements » etc.; and Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter: « I dwelt in the highest places, and my throne is in a pillar of a cloud ». Nor *the height of desire and expectation,* of which in the Psalm[^p412-11]: « Thou, O Lord, art my hope; thou hast made the most High thy refuge ». For these *heights are*
<!-- page 413 -->
*before God.* — But there is *another height,* which removes far from the Most High, and this is *the height of presumption,* of which Jeremiah, the forty-eighth chapter[^p413-1]: « We have heard the pride of Moab, he is exceeding proud; his loftiness and his arrogancy and his pride and the height of his heart I know, saith the Lord ». This is also *the height of ambition;* Jeremiah, the forty-ninth chapter: « Thy arrogancy hath deceived thee, that endeavourest to lay hold on the height of the hill ». This is also *the height of ostentation;* Ezechiel, the thirty-first chapter[^p413-2]: « Because he was exalted in height, and shot up his top green and thick, and his heart was lifted up in his height; I have now delivered him into the hands of the mightiest of the nations » etc. This height in the sight of men is *an abomination with God,* according to that of Proverbs, the sixteenth chapter[^p413-3]: « Every arrogant man is an abomination to the Lord; though hand should be joined to hand, he is not innocent ». And the Lord is said *to abominate* such height on account of its severe casting down, according to that of Isaiah, the second chapter[^p413-4]: « The lofty eyes of man are humbled, and the height of men shall be bowed down, and the Lord alone shall be exalted in that day ».

29. Therefore this threefold height, as abominable to the Lord, is dissuaded: first, namely, *the height of presumption;* Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter[^p413-5]: « Seek not the things that are too high for thee »; and to the Romans, the eleventh chapter: « Be not high-minded » etc. Second, *the height of ambition;* to the Romans, the twelfth chapter: « Not minding high things, but consenting to the humble » etc. Third is dissuaded *the height of ostentation;* the Psalm: « Lift not up your horn on high; speak not iniquity against God ». — And the Lord abominates this height because it is *vainglorious;* the first of Kings, the sixteenth chapter[^p413-6]: « Look not on the height of his stature, because I have rejected him ». Because it is *feigned;* Daniel, the third chapter: « King Nabuchodonosor made a statue of gold of sixty cubits high » etc. Because it is *ruinous;* on account of which Proverbs, the seventeenth chapter[^p413-7]: « He that maketh his house high seeketh ruin ». And therefore it is said in the second to the Corinthians, the tenth chapter: « The weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty to God unto the pulling down of fortifications, destroying counsels and every height that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every understanding unto the obedience of Christ ».

30. (Vers. 16.). *The law and the prophets were until John* etc. After the repression of the arrogance of the rebellious Pharisees is subjoined here, second, the description of *the voiding of the legal precepts*[^p413-8], on account of which the Pharisees, raising up their haughtiness, spurned the lessons of Christ and cloaked their avarice. And since the Mosaic law has been voided through Christ, in such a way that it remains as to *the spiritual intelligence of the ceremonial precepts* and *the literal observance of the moral ones;* therefore in this part he insinuates not only *the voiding* of the *Law* itself according to *the literal observance,* but also its *fulfilment* according to *the spiritual intelligence* and its *duration* according to *the moral right and the conjugal bond.*

First, then, as to *the voiding of the Law* according to *the literal observance,* he says: *The law and the prophets were until John;* as if he were saying to the Pharisees: you ought not to raise up your haughtiness for the observance of the Law, because[^p413-9] it now ceases as to the letter and the figure. And note that, although Christ could have said: *until me,* because, to the Romans, the tenth chapter[^p413-10], « the end of the law is Christ, unto justice » etc.; yet he preferred to name the Precursor as his own voice, because through the ministry of John himself the Lamb was shown with the finger; John, the first chapter: « Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who taketh away » etc.; and he himself had already begun to show the open truth and by this to void the figure. For because the Law and the Prophets all promised Christ to come, and John showed him present: therefore « *The law and the prophets were until John,* because what was clear by John's testimony *to have come* could no longer be prophesied *to come*[^p413-11] ».

31. And because the end of the Law is through the beginning of the new testament, therefore there follows: *From that time*[^p413-12] *the kingdom of God is preached as gospel,* that is, from him the Gospel of grace is begun. Whence Mark, the first chapter[^p413-13]: « The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, as it is written in Isaias: Behold, I send my angel » etc.; therefore also blessed Luke took the beginning of his Gospel from the very conception of blessed John; above, the first chapter: « There was in the days of Herod ». Whence, that his preaching might be shown to be *new,*
<!-- page 414 -->
it is said in Mark, the first chapter[^p414-1], that « he was baptizing and preaching the baptism of penance unto remission of sins ». — And because the preaching of John was of penance, in which a man does violence to himself, therefore he adds: *And every one doth violence unto it,* which is said more expressly in Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p414-2]: « From the days of John the Baptist the kingdom of heaven suffereth violence, and the violent seize it ». Now this is the violence by which one *exceeds natural power;* whence Bede[^p414-3]: « It is a great force when one born of earth seeks to possess heaven through power, who could not hold it through nature ». This is also the violence which *conquers the bending-down of nature;* whence the Gloss[^p414-4]: « Let us do violence to nature, that it may not sink down to earthly things, but raise itself to things above ». This is also the violence by which one *conquers the torpor of sluggishness;* whence Bede[^p414-5]: « Every one who does violence hastens with vehement zeal, does not grow slack with a torpid affection. Religious violence, then, is of faith; sloth is criminal ». For it is a great violence when, through the force and rigour of penance, one mitigates the severity of the divine sentence, when through humble prayer one bends the lofty strictness. In figure of which it was said to Jacob, the strenuous wrestler, in Genesis, the thirty-second chapter[^p414-6]: « If thou hast been strong against God, much more shalt thou prevail against men ».

32. And note that blessed Bernard[^p414-7] distinguishes four kinds of men possessing the kingdom of heaven: « For some violently *seize* it, some *buy* it, some *steal* it, some *are compelled* to it ». « For those *seize* it who forsake all things and follow Christ[^p414-8] », to whom can be referred that of Genesis, the last chapter but one: « Juda is a lion's whelp: to the prey, my son, thou art gone up »; and again in the same place: « Benjamin, a ravenous wolf, in the morning shall seize the prey, and in the evening shall divide the spoil ». — « And they *buy* it who give temporal things that they may receive eternal ones »; of whom above, the twelfth chapter[^p414-9]: « Make to yourselves bags which grow not old »; and above, in this same chapter: « Make unto you friends of the mammon of iniquity ». To such that of Augustine applies[^p414-10]: « I have something for sale. What? The kingdom of heaven. How is it bought? The kingdom by poverty, glory by lowliness, joy by sorrow, rest by labour, life by death ». — « And they *steal* it who do good things secretly, and, shunning human praise, are content with the divine testimony alone »; to whom that of Matthew, the sixth chapter, applies[^p414-11]: « But thou, when thou fastest, anoint thy head and wash thy face ». — « And the poor *are compelled* to enter, whom the fire of poverty here, God dispensing it, proves, lest in the future the fire of judgement should press them to damnation »; of these above, the fourteenth chapter[^p414-12]: « Go out into the ways and hedges, and compel them to come in, that my house may be filled ». Though, then, in the time of the Law it was the time for *buying,* now after John it is the time for *seizing.* For, as then opulence was promised, so now poverty is urged.

33. (Vers. 17.). Second, as to *the fulfilment of the Law* according to *the spiritual intelligence,* there is subjoined: *And it is easier for heaven and earth to pass,* through the conflagration of the world; the Psalm[^p414-13]: « And thou in the beginning foundedst the earth. They shall perish » etc. — *Than one tittle of the law to fall,* through *the frustration of some promise.* Rabanus[^p414-14]: « *Heaven and earth shall pass,* their form laid aside through the change of renewal, the former substance remaining; but the words of the Lord shall in no way pass without the effect of completion ». Therefore it is said in Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p414-15]: « Heaven and earth shall pass, but my words shall not pass »; because, as is said in the Psalm, « for ever, O Lord, thy word abideth ». Whence, although the old law seems transitory, it is nevertheless more abiding than the worldly creation. It abides also through *the spiritual intelligence of the divine word;* whence Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p414-16]: « I am not come to destroy the Law, but to fulfil. For amen I say to you, one jot or one tittle shall not pass of the Law, till all be fulfilled ». — And note that he says *one tittle;* for *apex* is properly said of the top of a letter, which is set for the adornment of the letter itself and is as it were a very least something. In which it is given to be understood that nothing at all, not
<!-- page 415 -->
small nor great, is in Scripture, nor anything, which does not have its completion[^p415-1]. For all things are referred to human salvation, which will have perpetual duration, even while the worldly creation passes according to its outward face; Isaiah, the fifty-first chapter[^p415-2]: « The heavens shall vanish like smoke, and the earth shall be worn away like a garment etc. But my salvation shall be for ever, and my justice shall not fail ». Whence the book of Scripture is nearer to the book of life than the book of the creature, and therefore it cannot remain unfulfilled.

34. (Vers. 18.). Third, as to *the duration of the Law* according to *the right of nature* and *the conjugal bond,* he subjoins: *Every one that putteth away his wife and marrieth another committeth adultery;* first, because he cannot put away the first except for the sole cause of fornication, according to that of Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p415-3]: « Whosoever shall put away his wife, except for fornication, and shall marry another, committeth adultery ». Second, because, for whatever cause he put her away, it is not lawful for him, while she lives, to marry another, and this according to the law of nature divinely instituted in the forming of man; Genesis, the second chapter[^p415-4]: « A man shall leave his father and mother » etc. And this is the law which dictates that « matrimony is the joining of man and woman, retaining an undivided habit of life »; which also dictates that it is an indissoluble bond. — And therefore he subjoins: *And he that marrieth her that is put away from her husband*[^p415-5] *committeth adultery.* And the reason of this is because the wife is his whose she was first, however much she may seem to be separated; whence the Apostle to the Romans, the seventh chapter: « The woman that is under a husband, whilst her husband liveth, is bound to the law. Therefore, whilst her husband liveth, she shall be called an adulteress if she be with another man ». And therefore Jeremiah, the third chapter: « If a man put away his wife, and she go from him and marry another man, shall he return to her any more? Shall not that woman be polluted and defiled »? — And it is to be noted that, although no mention was being made of wives, yet the Lord, willing to show the *standing* of the Law as to *the moral precepts* and its *passing* as to *the ceremonial* and *the judicial,* gives the example of matrimony, because in it a *bill* of divorce was given. And the law of matrimony is confirmed in the Gospel as a *moral* right, and the bill of divorce is reprobated; whence Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p415-6]: « Moses commanded to give a bill of divorce and to put away. He saith to them: Because Moses by reason of the hardness of your heart permitted you to put away your wives ». — From these things, then, it is gathered how the Law *abides,* how it *is voided,* how it *is fulfilled,* and nevertheless the haughtiness of the Pharisees glorying in the letter of the Law is confuted.
