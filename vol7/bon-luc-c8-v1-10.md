# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. VIII, Vers. 1-10
## *Primo ponitur instructio parabolica in occultis mysteriis per tria*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 188 -->

### Capitulum VIII.

#### In secunda parte Apostoli instruuntur specialiter in doctrina mystica Scripturarum.

1. *Et factum est deinceps* etc. Supra[^p188-6] posita est instructio communis discipulorum in doctrina eorum quae requiruntur ad salutem omnium. In parte vero ista subditur instructio *specialis in doctrina mystica Scripturarum.* Et quoniam haec doctrina turbis proponitur in parabolis et aperitur discipulis et confirmatur miraculis, ideo pars ista habet tres: in quarum prima ponitur *instructio parabolica* in occultis mysteriis; secundo vero, *informatio apostolica* in apertis eloquiis, ibi[^p188-7]: *Est autem haec parabola;* tertio vero, eius *confirmatio authentica* in manifestis miraculis, ibi: *Factum est autem in una dierum* etc.

#### Primo ponitur instructio parabolica in occultis mysteriis per tria.

Circa *doctrinam parabolicam,* quae communiter omni generi hominum proponitur audienda et Apostolis intelligenda, introducuntur tria. Primum est *coadunatio multitudinis* ad audiendum; secundum est *propositio similitudinis* ad manuducendum, et hoc ibi[^p188-8]: *Exiit qui seminat* etc.; tertium est *excitatio sollicitudinis* ad intelligendum, quod describitur ibi: *Haec dicens, clamabat* etc.

Quantum ad primum notandum, quod ad doctrinam Christi erat *concursus multitudinis* ex causa quadruplici, secundum quod ex littera potest coniici: quia quidam comitabantur Christum ratione *suscepti officii,* ut Apostoli; quidam, ratione *accepti
<!-- page 189 -->
beneficii,* ut infirmi curati; quidam, ratione *impendendi ministerii,* ut mulieres honestae; quidam, ratione *audiendi verbi divini,* ut multitudo turbae. Et haec quatuor genera enumerat Evangelista concurrisse ad Christi doctrinam.

2. (Vers. 1.). Primo igitur quantum ad *Apostolos* concomitantes Christum ratione *suscepti officii* praemittitur: *Et factum est deinceps*[^p189-1]*, et ipse iter faciebat per civitates et castella,* id est per loca, ubi habitant minores et maiores, secundum quod dicitur Sapientiae sexto: « Pusillum et magnum ipse fecit, et aequaliter est illi cura de omnibus », maxime quantum ad ea quae sunt salutis; propter quod dicit: *Praedicans et evangelizans regnum*[^p189-2] *Dei,* secundum illud Isaiae quinquagesimo secundo: « Quam pulcri super montes pedes annuntiantis et praedicantis pacem et annuntiantis salutem, praedicantis bona ». — Et quia verbum frustra proponitur, nisi adsit auditor, ideo additur: *Et duodecim cum illo,* qui scilicet sequebantur eum ex suscepto officio, ad quod elegit eos; supra sexto[^p189-3]: « Elegit duodecim, quos et Apostolos nominavit ». Unde ipsi Apostoli cum evangelizante circuibant, ut cum ipso in Evangelio collaborarent, secundum illud, ad quod hortatur Apostolus secundae ad Timotheum primo: « Sed collabora Evangelio secundum virtutem Dei, qui nos liberavit et vocavit vocatione sua sancta, non secundum opera nostra, sed secundum propositum suum »; quia, Ioannis decimo quinto, « non vos me elegistis, sed ego elegi vos et posui vos, ut eatis et fructum afferatis, et fructus vester maneat ».

3. (Vers. 2.). Secundo, quantum ad *curatos* comitantes ratione *accepti beneficii* subditur: *Et mulieres aliquae,* supple: cum eo iter faciebant, et hoc ratione accepti beneficii; unde adiungit: *Quae erant curatae a spiritibus malignis et infirmitatibus.* Ideo comitabantur eum tanquam suum salvatorem et medicum, secundum consilium Sapientis, Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo[^p189-4]: « Da locum medico, et non discedat a te, quia opera eius sunt necessaria »; unde supra sexto: « Et qui vexabantur a spiritibus immundis curabantur. Et omnis turba quaerebat eum tangere, quia virtus de illo exibat et sanabat omnes ». — Et nota, quod licet curaverit utrumque sexum, magis tamen specificat de muliebri, sive quia sexus infirmior, secundum illud primae Petri tertio[^p189-5]: « Infirmiori vasculo muliebri impartientes honorem »; sive quia clementior quam vir respectu infirmitatis alienae, secundum illud, Proverbiorum ultimo, de bona muliere: « Manum suam aperuit inopi et palmas suas extendit ad pauperes »; sive quia inter personas curatas erant nobiliores. — Et ideo exprimit aliquas nominaliter, cum addit: *Maria, quae vocatur Magdalena*[^p189-6], a Magdalo oppido secundo milliario a Tiberiade; et haec erat notabilis ratione *enormitatis peccati;* unde et addit: *De qua septem daemonia eiecerat*[^p189-7], id est universa vitia; Gregorius: « Septem daemonia Maria habuit, quae universis vitiis plena fuit ». Haec daemonia exierunt, quando peccata dimissa fuerunt, secundum quod dicitur infra undecimo[^p189-8]: « Cum immundus spiritus exiit ab homine, errat per loca inaquosa »; remissa autem fuerunt ei, supra septimo. Haec enim est illa peccatrix, de qua supra sermonem fecit, sicut dicit Beda in Glossa[^p189-9].

4. (Vers. 3.). Erat etiam aliqua nobilis ratione *nobilitatis viri;* unde addit: *Et Ioanna, uxor Chusae, procuratoris Herodis,* qui erat persona nobilis. Erat etiam aliqua nobilis ex *se ipsa,* de qua sine cognomine adiungitur: *Et Susanna.* Has autem tres nominat, ut *commendet castitatem* secundum triplicem differentiam in comitatu Christi, scilicet *vidualem*[^p189-10]*, coniugalem* et *virginalem.* Unde prima dicitur prius fuisse peccatrix, secunda coniugata, de tertia tacetur utrumque. Vel, ut in hoc *ostendatur curatio a triplici aegritudine,* scilicet animae et carnis et utriusque; ideo tres personas curatas exprimit, scilicet Mariam, Ioannam et Susannam.

5. Tertio, quantum ad comitantes ratione *impendendi obsequii* sive ministerii additur: *Et aliae multae, quae ministrabant ei de facultatibus suis,* sicut illa Sunamitis Eliseo, quarti Regum quarto[^p189-11], cui dicitur: « Ecce, sedule in omnibus ministrasti nobis ». Sic et socrus Simonis, de qua Matthaei octavo dicitur, quod postquam curata est, « surrexit et ministrabat eis ». — Hoc enim antiquitus consueverat esse officium mulierum, sicut dicit Beda in
<!-- page 190 -->
Glossa[^p190-1]: « Antiquitus mos erat Iudaeorum, nec ducebatur in culpam, ut mulieres de sua substantia pascerent et vestirent doctores. Sed quia hoc in gentibus poterat facere scandalum, Paulus se memorat abstinuisse primae ad Corinthios nono: *Nunquid non habemus potestatem mulierem sororem circumducendi, sicut ceteri Apostoli* »? Et ideo illud nunc non debet ad consequentiam trahi[^p190-2] propter scandalum vitandum et etiam propter periculum. — In hoc tamen erudimur, quod praedicatori licet accipere stipendium pro labore; primae ad Corinthios nono: « Si seminavimus vobis spiritualia, non est mirum, si carnalia vestra metamus »; et Matthaei decimo: « Dignus est operarius cibo suo »; et infra decimo: « Dignus est enim operarius mercede sua ». Et rursus in hoc erudimur, quod praedicare et laborare actus est virilis et perfectorum; sed ministrare, imperfectorum, utpote mulierum; unde Actuum sexto[^p190-3]: « Non est aequum, nos relinquere verbum Dei et ministrare mensis ».

6. (Vers. 4.). Quarto, quantum ad concurrentes ratione *verbi divini audiendi* adiungitur: *Cum autem plurima turba conveniret,* scilicet ad audiendum doctrinam, secundum illud Ioannis decimo octavo[^p190-4]: « Ego semper docui in synagogis, ubi omnes Iudaei conveniunt ». Et sic impletur illud Isaiae secundo: « Fluent ad eum omnes gentes, et ibunt populi multi ». Unde congregabantur in multitudine et veniebant cum promptitudine; unde et subdit: *Et de civitatibus properarent ad eum,* scilicet audiendum. — *Dixit per similitudinem,* scilicet parabolicam ad apertas res, quibus assueti erant; et ideo ad audiendum cum desiderio properabant. Unde Chrysostomus[^p190-5]: « Commiscens parabolas, cum ampliori mitigatione attrahebat auditores et de area et de vinea et de agro ». Unde ad hanc doctrinam parabolicam omnes invitantur, secundum illud Psalmi[^p190-6]: « Audite haec, omnes gentes, auribus percipite, qui habitatis orbem »; et post subditur: « Inclinabo in parabolam aurem meam, aperiam in psalterio propositionem meam »; et rursus alibi: « Aperiam in parabolis os meum, loquar propositiones ab initio ». Et sic facta est excitatio multitudinis.

7. (Vers. 5.). *Exiit qui seminat* etc. Post congregationem multitudinis additur hic secundo *propositio similitudinis.* Et quia doctrina debet esse secundum exigentiam auditorum, ideo similitudo proposita quatuor habet differentias membrorum, correspondentes quatuor differentiis auditorum. Quidam enim audiunt, sed non retinent, ut *obliviosi;* quidam audiunt et retinent, sed non faciunt, ut *desidiosi;* quidam audiunt, retinent, faciunt, sed non complent, ut *negotiosi;* quidam autem audiunt, retinent, faciunt[^p190-7] et complent, et hi sunt *studiosi et virtuosi.*

Haec quatuor genera hominum similia sunt quatuor differentiis terrarum, quarum prima semen *recipit,* sed *non retinet,* et quantum ad illam dicitur: *Exiit qui seminat seminare semen suum. Exiit,* scilicet de domo ad agrum, hoc est de quiete ad laborem; Psalmus[^p190-8]: « Exibit homo ad opus suum et ad operationem suam usque ad vesperam ». Et hic labor vel est in *opere iustitiae,* vel *misericordiae,* qui recte designatur per *seminationem,* secundum illud Ecclesiastae undecimo[^p190-9]: « Mane semina semen tuum, et vespere ne cesset manus tua »; et Isaiae trigesimo secundo: « Beati, qui seminatis super omnes aquas, immittentes pedem bovis et asini ». — Vel etiam est in *sermone doctrinae;* unde de Paulo praedicante dicitur Actuum decimo septimo[^p190-10]: « Quid vult seminator verborum hic dicere »? Et isto semine multiplicata est Ecclesia, secundum illud primae Petri primo: « Renati non ex semine corruptibili, sed incorruptibili, per verbum Dei vivi et permanentis in aeternum ». Et de hac seminatione intelligit hic; sed haec, licet de se semper sit bona et fructuosa, variatur tamen secundum differentiam terrae suscipientis.

8. Quaedam enim *suscipit,* sed *non retinet,* et ideo quantum ad illam subdit: *Et dum seminat, aliud,* id est alia pars seminis, *cecidit secus viam,* per quam scilicet est discursus, et ideo non est in
<!-- page 191 -->
ea status retentionis, sed transitus oblivionis propter strepitum multitudinis; quod significatur infra decimo octavo[^p191-1], ubi dicitur, quod « caecus sedebat secus viam, mendicans »; et cum clamaret ad Dominum, turbae « increpabant eum, ut taceret ». — Unde hic non est locus fructificationis, quia non est in eo retentio seminis. Ideo adiungit: *Et conculcatum est,* scilicet a pedibus hominum, *et volucres caeli comederunt illud;* in quo intelliguntur morsus daemonum, secundum illud Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo[^p191-2]: « Consumentur fame, et devorabunt eos aves morsu amarissimo »; Matthaei septimo: « Nolite sanctum dare canibus, neque spargatis margaritas vestras ante porcos, ne forte conculcent eas pedibus suis ». — Ex quo colligimus, quod duo expellunt de corde hominis semen divinum, scilicet malae suggestiones hominum et tentationes daemonum. Et ideo dicit Apostolus ad Hebraeos ultimo[^p191-3]: « Doctrinis variis et peregrinis nolite abduci. Optimum est enim gratia stabilire cor, non escis, quae non profuerunt ambulantibus in eis ». Et ideo necesse est, cor nostrum elongari ab huiusmodi discursibus, « ut non simus sicut parvuli fluctuantes et circumferamur omni vento doctrinae »; Ieremiae secundo: « Quid tibi vis in via Aegypti, ut bibas aquam turbidam? Et quid tibi vis in via Assyriorum, ut bibas aquam fluminis »?

9. (Vers. 6.). Secunda vero differentia terrae est, quae *recipit* et *quodam modo retinet,* sed *non fovet;* et quantum ad hanc subdit: *Et aliud cecidit supra petram,* id est super cor durum, de quo Zachariae septimo[^p191-4]: « Cor suum posuerunt ut adamantem »; et Iob quadragesimo primo: « Cor eius indurabitur quasi lapis ». — Haec, quamvis semen possit retinere, non tamen potest fovere, deficiente humore; et propterea addit: *Et natum aruit, quia non habebat*[^p191-5] *humorem,* scilicet vegetantem et foventem; et in tali terra statim nascitur et statim moritur, quia parum habet de alimento, sicut foenum tectorum; de quo in Psalmo[^p191-6]: « Fiant sicut foenum tectorum, quod priusquam evellatur, exaruit »; et Isaiae decimo octavo: « Ante messem totus effloruit, et immatura perfectio germinabit ». Et hoc est propter defectum humoris gratiae, secundum quod Isaiae decimo quinto[^p191-7] dicitur: « Aruit herba, defecit germen, viror omnis interiit »; et hoc contingit propter multitudinem petrarum, quae non sunt aptae ad semen divini verbi percipiendum; propter quod dicitur Amos sexto: « Nunquid currere queunt in petris equi »? id est praedicatores, et hoc propter defectum aquarum; Iob duodecimo: « Si continuerit aquas, omnia siccabuntur ». Quocontra Ieremiae decimo septimo: « Erit lignum, quod transplantatum est secus aquas, et non timebit, cum venerit aestus. Et erit folium eius viride et in tempore siccitatis non erit sollicitum ».

10. (Vers. 7.). Tertia differentia terrae est, quae, etsi aliquo modo *retineat* et *foveat,* non tamen *complet* nec *fructum profert;* quantum ad quam subdit: *Et aliud cecidit inter spinas,* id est inter mundanas sollicitudines, in quibus multi libenter conversantur; Iob trigesimo[^p191-8]: « Esse sub sentibus delicias computabant ». Istis spinis repleta est terra, secundum illud Isaiae septimo: « Vepres et spinae in universa terra ». Et hoc merito maledictionis primae, secundum illud Genesis tertio: « Maledicta terra in opere tuo. Spinas et tribulos germinabit tibi ». — Et in hac terra semen, etsi oriatur, non tamen consummatur; unde subdit: *Et simul exortae spinae,* scilicet cum semine, *suffocaverunt illud,* quia plus in cordibus proficiunt et crescunt cogitationes carnales quam spirituales et eis praevalent; Isaiae trigesimo secundo[^p191-9]: « Super humum populi mei ascendent vepres et spinae ». Et hoc est, quia non curant de spiritualibus; Proverbiorum vigesimo quarto: « Per agrum hominis pigri transivi et per vineam viri stulti; et ecce, operuerant superficiem eius spinae ». Unde spinae totum occupant, ut fructus cum eis nasci non possit. Et ideo Ieremiae quarto[^p191-10]: « Novate vobis novale et nolite serere super spinas »; quia, sicut dicitur ad Hebraeos sexto, « terra proferens spinas et tribulos reproba est et maledicto proxima, cuius consummatio in combustionem ».

11. (Vers. 8.). Quarta differentia terrae est, quae *recipit* et *retinet, fovet* et *fructum profert,* quantum ad quam addit: *Et aliud cecidit in terram bonam,* id est mentem docilem et benignam; de qua Exodi tertio[^p191-11]: « Educam filios Israel in terram bonam et spatiosam ». Haec est terra bona, quae habet quatuor conditiones praedictas, secundum illud ad Hebraeos sexto: « Terra supervenientem bibens imbrem et proferens herbam opportunam his, a quibus colitur, accipiet benedictionem a Deo », secundum illud Psalmi: « Benedixisti, Domine, terram tuam ». — Haec benedictio est in completione fructus; et ideo additur: *Et ortum, fecit fructum
<!-- page 192 -->
centuplum,* id est fructum perfectum. Et sic impletur illud Psalmi[^p192-1]: « Dominus dabit benignitatem, et terra nostra dabit fructum suum », secundum quod dicitur Genesis vigesimo sexto: « Sevit Isaac et invenit in ipso anno centuplum ». Matthaei decimo tertio[^p192-2] hoc expressius dicitur secundum tres gradus: « Et fecit fructum centesimum, aliud sexagesimum, aliud trigesimum »; et hoc secundum tres gradus continentiae, vel tres gradus iustitiae; beatus vero Lucas tangit differentiam supremam, in hac includens alias.

12. Et nota, quod tres ponit terrae infructuosae differentias et unam tantum bonam, quia, secundum quod dicitur Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p192-3], « multi sunt vocati, pauci vero electi ». Et propterea conqueritur Dominus Michaeae septimo: « Vae mihi! quia factus sum, sicut qui colligit in autumno racemos vindemiae ». Et ratio huius redditur Matthaei septimo: « Quia lata est porta et spatiosa via, quae ducit ad perditionem, et multi sunt, qui intrant per eam; angusta est porta et arcta via, quae ducit ad vitam, et pauci sunt, qui inveniunt eam ».

13. *Haec dicens clamabat.* Hic post congregationem multitudinis et propositionem similitudinis subiungitur tertio *excitatio apostolicae sollicitudinis.* Circa huiusmodi[^p192-4] autem excitationem quatuor introducuntur ab Evangelista, scilicet *admonitio magistralis, interrogatio disciplinalis, praeelectio familiaris* et *reprobatio iudicialis.* Primum sollicitat, secundum rectificat, tertium confortat et quartum humiliat.

Primo igitur quantum ad *magistralem admonitionem sollicitantem* dicitur: *Haec dicens clamabat,* ut suo clamore excitaret auditorum attentionem, sicut Ioannis septimo[^p192-5]: « In die magno festivitatis stabat Iesus et clamabat ». Hoc est enim officium praedicatoris; Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo: « Clama, ne cesses; quasi tuba exalta vocem tuam », ut ex ipso *modo dicendi* excitet audientes. — Nec tantum ex modo dicendi, sed etiam ex *sensu verbi;* propter quod adiungit: *Qui habet aures audiendi audiat,* id est, « spiritualiter haec verba intelligat[^p192-6] »; et Beda: « Ubicumque haec admonitio interponitur, mysticum esse quod dicitur et attentius esse quaerendum ostenditur ». Et hoc significat ipsa multiplicatio actus audiendi, sicut Isaiae vigesimo octavo[^p192-7]: « Auribus percipite et audite vocem meam et attendite et audite eloquium ». Ad hanc exhortationem potest dicere vere intelligens illud Isaiae quinquagesimo: « Erigit mane, mane erigit mihi aurem, ut audiam eum quasi magistrum »; et illud Iob quadragesimo secundo: « Auditu auris audivi te, nunc autem oculus meus videt te » etc. Tangit audiendi usum, quia tres sunt gradus: primus in audiendo exterius, secundus in credendo, tertius vero in intelligendo.

14. (Vers. 9.). Secundo quantum ad *disciplinalem quaestionem rectificantem* subditur: *Interrogabant autem eum discipuli eius,* ut scilicet dirigerentur ab eo, secundum illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo tertio[^p192-8]: « Qui interrogationem manifestat parabit verbum, et sic deprecatus exaudietur; et qui servabit disciplinam et tunc respondebit ». Hoc autem dicitur, non quia statim interrogarent, sed quia ad verbum Christi conceperunt voluntatem interrogandi; unde in Marci quarto dicitur: « Cum esset singularis, interrogaverunt eum ». — Et quia interrogatio est de occultis et mysticis, ideo additur: *Quae esset haec parabola,* id est, quid significaret, ut per eius interpretationem possent intellectum capere, secundum illud Proverbiorum primo[^p192-9]: « Audiens sapiens sapientior erit, et intelligens gubernacula possidebit; animadvertet parabolam et interpretationem »; et Ecclesiastici trigesimo nono: « Ideo occulta proverbiorum exquiret sapiens et in absconditis parabolarum conversabitur ».

15. (Vers. 10.). Tertio quantum ad *familiarem praeelectionem confortantem* subditur: *Quibus ipse dixit: Vobis datum est nosse mysterium regni Dei,* id est intelligentiam Scripturae, quae regnum Dei dicitur, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p192-10]: « Auferetur a vobis regnum Dei et dabitur genti facienti fructus eius ». Dicitur autem Scriptura *regnum Dei,* quia ipsum praedicat et ostendit et
<!-- page 193 -->
promittit et obedientem perducit, secundum illud Ioannis quinto: « Scrutamini Scripturas, quia vos putatis in ipsis vitam aeternam habere ». De his igitur dicit Apostolis specialiter et discretive: *Vobis datum est,* quia ipsi ad hoc electi fuerunt; Ioannis decimo quinto[^p193-1]: « Vos autem dixi amicos, quia omnia, quaecumque audivi a Patre meo, nota feci vobis ». Et signanter dicit *datum,* non *venditum,* ut gratiam agnoscamus; Matthaei decimo: « Gratis accepistis, gratis date »; *datum* etiam, non *innatum;* infra vigesimo primo: « Ego enim dabo vobis os et sapientiam »; *datum,* non *acquisitum;* Iacobi primo[^p193-2]: « Omne datum optimum et omne donum perfectum desursum est descendens a Patre luminum ». In hoc ergo, quod familiariter dicit eos praeelectos, confortat, ne deficiant ab inquirendo; infra duodecimo[^p193-3]: « Nolite timere, pusillus grex, quia complacuit Patri vestro dare vobis regnum ».

16. Quarto vero quantum ad *iudicialem reprobationem humiliantem* subiungit: *Ceteris autem in parabolis,* scilicet supple: datum est nosse, quod potius est ignorare; Isaiae vigesimo octavo[^p193-4]: « In loquela labii », scilicet exterioris, « et in lingua altera loquar ad populum istum ». Et hoc facit iusto suo iudicio, quo non vult communicare sancta immundis, secundum illud Matthaei septimo: « Nolite sanctum dare canibus ». — Et propterea subdit: *Ut videntes non videant; ut* non est ibi causale, sed consecutivum, sicut Ioannis nono[^p193-5]: « In iudicium ego veni in hunc mundum, ut qui non vident videant, et qui vident caeci fiant ». Et hoc iudicium praedicitur Isaiae vigesimo nono: « Claudet Dominus oculos vestros; prophetas et principes vestros, qui vident visiones, operiet. Et erit vobis visio Domini sicut verba libri signati ». Non est contradictio in hoc, quod dicit: *Ut videntes non videant,* quia primum refertur ad visum exteriorem, secundum ad interiorem, secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo tertio[^p193-6]: « Educ foras populum caecum et oculos habentem et surdum, et aures ei sunt ». Et hoc explicat sequens clausula: *Et audientes non intelligant;* Isaiae sexto[^p193-7]: « Excaeca cor populi huius et aures eius aggrava »; et ibidem subditur: « Auribus audite et nolite intelligere ». Unde dicitur secundae ad Corinthios tertio: « Usque in hodiernum diem, dum legeretur Moyses, velamen est positum super cor eorum ». Et hoc factum est divino iudicio, secundum illud ad Romanos undecimo[^p193-8]: « Nolo vos ignorare, fratres, quia ex parte caecitas contigit in Israel, donec plenitudo gentium intraret ». Et hoc debet omnes humiliare, secundum quod ibidem dicitur: « Noli altum sapere, sed time. Si enim Deus naturalibus ramis non pepercit, ne forte nec tibi parcat ». — Et haec humilitas est opportuna et praeambula ad intelligentiam mysteriorum, quia dicitur Matthaei undecimo[^p193-9]: « Abscondisti haec a sapientibus et prudentibus et revelasti ea parvulis ».

### Chapter VIII.

#### In the second part the Apostles are instructed specially in the mystical doctrine of the Scriptures.

1. *And it came to pass afterwards* etc. Above[^p188-6] there was set forth the common instruction of the disciples in the doctrine of those things which are required for the salvation of all. But in this part there is subjoined the instruction *special in the mystical doctrine of the Scriptures.* And since this doctrine is set before the multitudes in parables, and opened to the disciples, and confirmed by miracles, therefore this part has three: in the first of which there is set forth the *parabolic instruction* in hidden mysteries; but secondly, the *apostolic formation* in open utterances, there[^p188-7]: *Now the parable is this;* but thirdly, its *authentic confirmation* in manifest miracles, there: *And it came to pass on a certain day* etc.

#### First, the parabolic instruction in hidden mysteries is set forth, through three things.

Concerning the *parabolic doctrine,* which is set forth in common to every kind of men to be heard, and to the Apostles to be understood, three things are introduced. The first is the *gathering together of the multitude* for hearing; the second is the *setting forth of a similitude* for leading by the hand, and this there[^p188-8]: *The sower went out* etc.; the third is the *rousing of solicitude* for understanding, which is described there: *Saying these things, he cried out* etc.

As to the first, it is to be noted that there was a *concourse of the multitude* to the doctrine of Christ from a fourfold cause, according as can be gathered from the letter: for some accompanied Christ by reason of an *office undertaken,* as the Apostles; some, by reason of a *benefit received,*
<!-- page 189 -->
as the sick who were cured; some, by reason of *ministry to be rendered,* as honest women; some, by reason of *hearing the divine word,* as the multitude of the crowd. And these four kinds the Evangelist enumerates as having run together to the doctrine of Christ.

2. (Vers. 1.). First, then, as to the *Apostles* accompanying Christ by reason of the *office undertaken,* there is set first: *And it came to pass afterwards*[^p189-1]*, that he travelled through the cities and towns,* that is, through the places where lesser and greater dwell, according to what is said in Wisdom, the sixth chapter: « He made the little and the great, and he hath equally care of all », most of all as to the things which are of salvation; on account of which he says: *Preaching and evangelizing the kingdom*[^p189-2] *of God,* according to that of Isaias, the fifty-second chapter: « How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him that announceth and preacheth peace, and announceth salvation, that preacheth good things ». — And because the word is set forth in vain unless a hearer be present, therefore there is added: *And the twelve with him,* who namely followed him out of the office undertaken, to which he chose them; above, the sixth chapter[^p189-3]: « He chose twelve, whom also he named Apostles ». Whence the Apostles themselves went about with him as he preached the gospel, so that they might labour together with him in the Gospel, according to that to which the Apostle exhorts, the second to Timothy, the first chapter: « But labour with the Gospel, according to the power of God, who hath delivered us and called us by his holy calling, not according to our works, but according to his own purpose »; because, John, the fifteenth chapter, « you have not chosen me, but I have chosen you and have appointed you, that you should go and should bring forth fruit, and your fruit should remain ».

3. (Vers. 2.). Secondly, as to the *cured* accompanying him by reason of a *benefit received,* there is subjoined: *And certain women,* supply: made the journey with him, and this by reason of the benefit received; whence he adds: *Who had been cured of evil spirits and infirmities.* Therefore they accompanied him as their saviour and physician, according to the counsel of the Wise Man, Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p189-4]: « Give place to the physician, and let him not depart from thee, for his works are necessary »; whence above, the sixth chapter: « And they that were troubled with unclean spirits were cured. And all the multitude sought to touch him, for power went out from him and healed all ». — And note that although he cured both sexes, yet he specifies more of the womanly, either because it is the weaker sex, according to that of the first of Peter, the third chapter[^p189-5]: « Giving honour to the female as to the weaker vessel »; or because it is more clement than man in regard to another's infirmity, according to that, Proverbs, the last chapter, of the good woman: « She hath opened her hand to the needy, and stretched out her hands to the poor »; or because among the persons cured they were the more noble. — And therefore he expresses some by name, when he adds: *Mary who is called Magdalen*[^p189-6], from Magdalum, a town at the second milestone from Tiberias; and she was notable by reason of the *enormity of sin;* whence he also adds: *Out of whom he had cast seven devils*[^p189-7], that is, all vices; Gregory: « Mary had seven devils, who was full of all vices ». These devils went out when her sins were forgiven, according to what is said below, the eleventh chapter[^p189-8]: « When the unclean spirit is gone out of a man, he walketh through places without water »; and they were remitted her, above, the seventh chapter. For this is that sinner of whom he made discourse above, as Bede says in the Gloss[^p189-9].

4. (Vers. 3.). There was also one noble by reason of the *nobility of her husband;* whence he adds: *And Joanna the wife of Chusa, Herod's steward,* who was a noble person. There was also one noble of *herself,* of whom it is added without a surname: *And Susanna.* And he names these three in order to *commend chastity* according to its threefold difference in the company of Christ, namely the *widowly*[^p189-10]*, the conjugal* and the *virginal.* Whence the first is said to have been formerly a sinner, the second married; of the third both are passed over in silence. Or, so that in this there may be *shown a cure from a threefold sickness,* namely of the soul and of the flesh and of both; therefore he expresses three persons cured, namely Mary, Joanna and Susanna.

5. Thirdly, as to those accompanying him by reason of *service to be rendered* or ministry, there is added: *And many others who ministered unto him of their substance,* as that Sunamitess to Eliseus, the fourth of Kings, the fourth chapter[^p189-11], to whom it is said: « Behold, thou hast diligently served us in all things ». So too Simon's wife's mother, of whom it is said in Matthew, the eighth chapter, that after she was cured « she arose and ministered to them ». — For this was of old wont to be the office of women, as Bede says in the
<!-- page 190 -->
Gloss[^p190-1]: « It was the custom of the Jews of old, and it was not counted a fault, that women should feed and clothe their teachers out of their substance. But because this could cause scandal among the gentiles, Paul records that he abstained from it, the first to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter: *Have we not power to carry about a woman, a sister, as well as the rest of the Apostles* »? And therefore that ought not now to be drawn into a precedent[^p190-2], on account of avoiding scandal and also on account of danger. — In this, however, we are schooled that it is lawful for a preacher to receive a stipend for his labour; the first to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter: « If we have sown unto you spiritual things, it is no wonder if we reap your carnal things »; and Matthew, the tenth chapter: « The workman is worthy of his meat »; and below, the tenth chapter: « For the labourer is worthy of his hire ». And again in this we are schooled that to preach and to labour is the act of men and of the perfect; but to minister, of the imperfect, as of women; whence Acts, the sixth chapter[^p190-3]: « It is not reason that we should leave the word of God and serve tables ».

6. (Vers. 4.). Fourthly, as to those running together by reason of *hearing the divine word,* there is adjoined: *And when a very great multitude was gathered together,* namely for hearing the doctrine, according to that of John, the eighteenth chapter[^p190-4]: « I have always taught in the synagogue, whither all the Jews resort ». And so there is fulfilled that of Isaias, the second chapter: « All nations shall flow unto it, and many people shall go ». Whence they were gathered in a multitude and came with promptness; whence he also subjoins: *And hastened out of the cities unto him,* namely to hear. — *He spoke by a similitude,* namely a parabolic one, to open things to which they were accustomed; and therefore they hastened with desire to hear. Whence Chrysostom[^p190-5]: « Mingling parables, he drew his hearers with greater gentleness, both of the threshing-floor and of the vineyard and of the field ». Whence all are invited to this parabolic doctrine, according to that of the Psalm[^p190-6]: « Hear these things, all ye nations, give ear, all ye inhabitants of the world »; and afterwards there is subjoined: « I will incline my ear to a parable, I will open my proposition on the psaltery »; and again elsewhere: « I will open my mouth in parables, I will utter propositions from the beginning ». And so there came about the rousing of the multitude.

7. (Vers. 5.). *The sower went out* etc. After the gathering of the multitude there is added here, secondly, the *setting forth of a similitude.* And because doctrine ought to be according to the requirement of the hearers, therefore the similitude set forth has four differences of members, answering to four differences of hearers. For some hear, but do not retain, as the *forgetful;* some hear and retain, but do not do, as the *slothful;* some hear, retain, do, but do not complete, as the *busied;* but some hear, retain, do[^p190-7] and complete, and these are the *studious and virtuous.*

These four kinds of men are like four differences of ground, the first of which *receives* the seed but *does not retain* it, and as to that one it is said: *The sower went out to sow his seed. Went out,* namely from the house to the field, that is, from rest to labour; the Psalm[^p190-8]: « Man shall go forth to his work and to his labour until the evening ». And this labour is either in the *work of justice* or *of mercy,* which is rightly designated by *sowing,* according to that of Ecclesiastes, the eleventh chapter[^p190-9]: « In the morning sow thy seed, and in the evening let not thy hand cease »; and Isaias, the thirty-second chapter: « Blessed are ye that sow upon all waters, sending thither the foot of the ox and the ass ». — Or else it is in the *word of doctrine;* whence of Paul preaching it is said in Acts, the seventeenth chapter[^p190-10]: « What is it that this sower of words would say here »? And by this seed the Church was multiplied, according to that of the first of Peter, the first chapter: « Being born again not of corruptible seed, but incorruptible, by the word of God who liveth and remaineth for ever ». And it is of this sowing that he understands here; but this, although of itself it is always good and fruitful, is yet varied according to the difference of the ground receiving it.

8. For some *receives* but *does not retain,* and therefore as to that one he subjoins: *And as he sowed, some,* that is, another part of the seed, *fell by the way side,* through which namely there is running to and fro, and therefore there is not in
<!-- page 191 -->
it the state of retaining, but the passage of forgetfulness, on account of the din of the multitude; which is signified below, the eighteenth chapter[^p191-1], where it is said that « a blind man sat by the way side, begging »; and when he cried out to the Lord, the multitude « rebuked him, that he should hold his peace ». — Whence here is no place of fructifying, because in it there is no retaining of the seed. Therefore he adjoins: *And it was trodden down,* namely by the feet of men, *and the fowls of the air devoured it;* in which are understood the bites of the devils, according to that of Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter[^p191-2]: « They shall be consumed with famine, and birds shall devour them with a most bitter bite »; Matthew, the seventh chapter: « Give not that which is holy to dogs, neither cast ye your pearls before swine, lest perhaps they trample them under their feet ». — From which we gather that two things drive out the divine seed from the heart of man, namely the evil suggestions of men and the temptations of the devils. And therefore the Apostle says, to the Hebrews, the last chapter[^p191-3]: « Be not led away with various and strange doctrines. For it is best that the heart be established with grace, not with meats, which have not profited those that walk in them ». And therefore it is necessary that our heart be removed far from such runnings to and fro, « that we may not be children tossed to and fro and carried about with every wind of doctrine »; Jeremias, the second chapter: « What hast thou to do in the way of Egypt, to drink the troubled water? And what hast thou to do with the way of the Assyrians, to drink the water of the river »?

9. (Vers. 6.). But the second difference of ground is that which *receives* and *in a certain manner retains,* but *does not foster;* and as to this one he subjoins: *And other some fell upon a rock,* that is, upon a hard heart, of which Zacharias, the seventh chapter[^p191-4]: « They made their heart as the adamant »; and Job, the forty-first chapter: « His heart shall be as hard as a stone ». — This, although it can retain the seed, yet cannot foster it, moisture failing; and therefore he adds: *And as soon as it was sprung up, it withered away, because it had*[^p191-5] *no moisture,* namely quickening and fostering; and in such ground it is at once born and at once dies, because it has little of nourishment, like the grass upon the tops of houses; of which in the Psalm[^p191-6]: « Let them be as grass upon the tops of houses, which withereth before it be plucked up »; and Isaias, the eighteenth chapter: « Before the harvest it was all flourishing, and an unripe perfection shall bud ». And this is on account of the want of the moisture of grace, according to what is said in Isaias, the fifteenth chapter[^p191-7]: « The grass is withered away, the spring is faded, all the greenness is perished »; and this happens on account of the multitude of rocks, which are not apt for receiving the seed of the divine word; on account of which it is said in Amos, the sixth chapter: « Can horses run upon the rocks »? that is, preachers, and this on account of the want of waters; Job, the twelfth chapter: « If he withhold the waters, all things shall be dried up ». Whereas, on the contrary, Jeremias, the seventeenth chapter: « He shall be as a tree that is planted by the waters, and he shall not fear when the heat cometh. And the leaf thereof shall be green, and in the time of drought it shall not be solicitous ».

10. (Vers. 7.). The third difference of ground is that which, although in some manner it *retains* and *fosters,* yet *does not complete* nor *bring forth fruit;* as to which he subjoins: *And other some fell among thorns,* that is, among worldly solicitudes, in which many willingly have their dwelling; Job, the thirtieth chapter[^p191-8]: « They counted it delightful to be under the briers ». With these thorns the earth is filled, according to that of Isaias, the seventh chapter: « Briers and thorns shall be in all the land ». And this by the desert of the first curse, according to that of Genesis, the third chapter: « Cursed is the earth in thy work. Thorns and thistles shall it bring forth to thee ». — And in this ground the seed, although it spring up, yet is not brought to completion; whence he subjoins: *And the thorns growing up with it,* namely with the seed, *choked it,* because in hearts carnal thoughts make more progress and grow more than spiritual ones, and prevail over them; Isaias, the thirty-second chapter[^p191-9]: « Upon the land of my people shall thorns and briers come up ». And this is because they do not care for spiritual things; Proverbs, the twenty-fourth chapter: « I passed by the field of the slothful man, and by the vineyard of the foolish man; and behold, thorns had covered the face thereof ». Whence the thorns occupy the whole, so that fruit cannot spring up with them. And therefore Jeremias, the fourth chapter[^p191-10]: « Break up anew your fallow ground, and sow not upon thorns »; because, as is said to the Hebrews, the sixth chapter, « the earth bringing forth thorns and briers is reprobate and very near unto a curse, whose end is to be burnt ».

11. (Vers. 8.). The fourth difference of ground is that which *receives* and *retains, fosters* and *brings forth fruit,* as to which he adds: *And other some fell upon good ground,* that is, a mind docile and kind; of which Exodus, the third chapter[^p191-11]: « I will bring the children of Israel into a good and spacious land ». This is the good ground, which has the four conditions aforesaid, according to that to the Hebrews, the sixth chapter: « The earth that drinketh in the rain which cometh upon it, and bringeth forth herbs meet for them by whom it is tilled, shall receive blessing from God », according to that of the Psalm: « Lord, thou hast blessed thy land ». — This blessing is in the completion of the fruit; and therefore there is added: *And being sprung up, it yielded fruit
<!-- page 192 -->
a hundredfold,* that is, perfect fruit. And so there is fulfilled that of the Psalm[^p192-1]: « The Lord will give kindness, and our earth shall yield her fruit », according to what is said in Genesis, the twenty-sixth chapter: « Isaac sowed, and he found that same year a hundredfold ». In Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p192-2], this is said more expressly according to three degrees: « And it yielded fruit, the one a hundredfold, another sixtyfold, another thirtyfold »; and this according to three degrees of continence, or three degrees of justice; but blessed Luke touches the highest difference, including the others in this one.

12. And note that he sets down three differences of unfruitful ground and only one good, because, according to what is said in Matthew, the twenty-second chapter[^p192-3], « many are called, but few chosen ». And therefore the Lord complains in Micheas, the seventh chapter: « Woe is me, for I am become as one that gleaneth in the autumn the grapes of the vintage ». And the reason of this is given in Matthew, the seventh chapter: « For wide is the gate and broad is the way that leadeth to destruction, and many there are who go in thereat; narrow is the gate and strait is the way that leadeth to life, and few there are that find it ».

13. *Saying these things, he cried out.* Here, after the gathering of the multitude and the setting forth of the similitude, there is subjoined, thirdly, the *rousing of apostolic solicitude.* Concerning this[^p192-4] rousing, four things are introduced by the Evangelist, namely the *masterly admonition,* the *disciplinary questioning,* the *familiar choosing beforehand* and the *judicial reprobation.* The first solicits, the second sets right, the third comforts and the fourth humbles.

First, then, as to the *masterly admonition that solicits* it is said: *Saying these things, he cried out,* so as to rouse the attention of the hearers by his crying out, as in John, the seventh chapter[^p192-5]: « On the great day of the festivity Jesus stood and cried ». For this is the office of the preacher; Isaias, the fifty-eighth chapter: « Cry, cease not; lift up thy voice like a trumpet », so as to rouse the hearers by the very *manner of speaking.* — Nor only by the manner of speaking, but also by the *sense of the word;* on account of which he adjoins: *He that hath ears to hear, let him hear,* that is, « let him understand these words spiritually[^p192-6] »; and Bede: « Wherever this admonition is put in, it is shown that what is said is mystical and must be sought out more attentively ». And this the very multiplying of the act of hearing signifies, as in Isaias, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p192-7]: « Give ear and hear my voice, and hearken and hear my speech ». To this exhortation one who truly understands can say that of Isaias, the fiftieth chapter: « He wakeneth in the morning, in the morning he wakeneth my ear, that I may hear him as a master »; and that of Job, the forty-second chapter: « With the hearing of the ear I have heard thee, but now my eye seeth thee » etc. He touches the use of hearing, because there are three degrees: the first in hearing outwardly, the second in believing, but the third in understanding.

14. (Vers. 9.). Secondly, as to the *disciplinary question that sets right* there is subjoined: *And his disciples asked him,* that namely they might be directed by him, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-third chapter[^p192-8]: « He that discovereth the question shall prepare what to say, and so having prayed he shall be heard; and he that will keep discipline, and then he shall answer ». But this is said not because they asked at once, but because at the word of Christ they conceived the will of asking; whence in Mark, the fourth chapter, it is said: « When he was alone, they asked him ». — And because the questioning is about hidden and mystical things, therefore there is added: *What this parable might be,* that is, what it might signify, so that through its interpretation they might be able to lay hold of the understanding, according to that of Proverbs, the first chapter[^p192-9]: « A wise man shall hear and shall be wiser, and he that understandeth shall possess governments; he shall understand a parable and the interpretation »; and Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-ninth chapter: « Therefore the wise man shall seek out the hidden things of proverbs, and shall be conversant in the secrets of parables ».

15. (Vers. 10.). Thirdly, as to the *familiar choosing beforehand that comforts* there is subjoined: *To whom he said: To you it is given to know the mystery of the kingdom of God,* that is, the intelligence of Scripture, which is called the kingdom of God, according to that of Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p192-10]: « The kingdom of God shall be taken from you, and shall be given to a nation yielding the fruits thereof ». But Scripture is called the *kingdom of God* because it preaches and shows it and
<!-- page 193 -->
promises it, and leads the obedient to it, according to that of John, the fifth chapter: « Search the Scriptures, for you think in them to have life everlasting ». Of these, then, he says to the Apostles specially and discretively: *To you it is given,* because they themselves were chosen for this; John, the fifteenth chapter[^p193-1]: « But I have called you friends, because all things whatsoever I have heard of my Father, I have made known to you ». And he says significantly *given,* not *sold,* that we may acknowledge grace; Matthew, the tenth chapter: « Freely have you received, freely give »; *given* also, not *inborn;* below, the twenty-first chapter: « For I will give you a mouth and wisdom »; *given,* not *acquired;* James, the first chapter[^p193-2]: « Every best thing given and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights ». In this, therefore, that he familiarly calls them chosen beforehand, he comforts them, lest they fail in inquiring; below, the twelfth chapter[^p193-3]: « Fear not, little flock, for it hath pleased your Father to give you a kingdom ».

16. But fourthly, as to the *judicial reprobation that humbles* he subjoins: *But to the rest in parables,* namely, supply: it is given to know, which is rather to be ignorant; Isaias, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p193-4]: « With the speech of lip », namely the outward lip, « and with another tongue I will speak to this people ». And this he does by his just judgement, by which he does not will to communicate holy things to the unclean, according to that of Matthew, the seventh chapter: « Give not that which is holy to dogs ». — And therefore he subjoins: *That seeing they may not see; that* is not causal there, but consecutive, as in John, the ninth chapter[^p193-5]: « For judgement I am come into this world, that they who see not may see, and they who see may become blind ». And this judgement is foretold in Isaias, the twenty-ninth chapter: « The Lord will shut up your eyes; he will cover your prophets and princes, that see visions. And the vision of the Lord shall be unto you as the words of a book that is sealed ». There is no contradiction in his saying: *That seeing they may not see,* because the first is referred to the outward sight, the second to the inward, according to that of Isaias, the forty-third chapter[^p193-6]: « Bring forth the people that are blind and have eyes, and that are deaf and have ears ». And the following clause explains this: *And hearing may not understand;* Isaias, the sixth chapter[^p193-7]: « Blind the heart of this people, and make their ears heavy »; and there it is subjoined: « Hear with your ears, and understand not ». Whence it is said in the second to the Corinthians, the third chapter: « Even until this day, when Moses is read, the veil is upon their heart ». And this was done by divine judgement, according to that to the Romans, the eleventh chapter[^p193-8]: « I would not have you ignorant, brethren, that blindness in part has happened in Israel, until the fulness of the Gentiles should come in ». And this ought to humble all, according to what is said in the same place: « Be not highminded, but fear. For if God hath not spared the natural branches, lest perhaps he also spare not thee ». — And this humility is fitting and a forerunner to the intelligence of the mysteries, because it is said in Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p193-9]: « Thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them to little ones ».
