# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XIX, Vers. 36-40
## *Exeunte autem illo etc. Postquam ostendit, se potestatem habere regalem per suam excellentiam in imperando et discipulorum obedientiam in obsequendo, hic ostendit tertio per populorum honorificentiam in collaudando*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 491 -->

53. *Exeunte autem illo*[^p491-13] etc. Postquam ostendit, se potestatem habere regalem per suam excellentiam in imperando et discipulorum obedientiam in obsequendo, hic ostendit tertio[^p491-14] *per populorum honorificentiam in collaudando.* Ad hoc autem exprimendum primo explicatur *devotio laudantium;* secundo vero, *celsitudo laudum,* ibi[^p491-15]: *Benedictus, qui venit in nomine Domini;* tertio vero, *confutatio invidorum,* ibi: *Et quidam Pharisaeorum* etc.

Circa *devotionem* igitur *laudantium* exprimendam duo introducuntur, scilicet expressio *devotionis in opere* et expressio *in sermone et voce.*

<!-- page 492 -->
54. (Vers. 36.). Primo igitur quantum ad *expressionem devotionis in opere* dicit: *Exeunte autem illo, substernebant vestimenta sua in via,* quod signum erat magnae devotionis. Non autem exprimit, qui erant illi qui sternebant, quia communiter hoc faciebant omnes et maxime turbae. Unde Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p492-1]: « Plurima turba straverunt vestimenta sua in via, alii autem caedebant ramos de arboribus et sternebant in via ». — Et nota, quod hic a *mysterio* non vacat, quod discipuli vestimenta *posuerunt super asellum*[^p492-2], sed turba *sternebat vestimenta sua sub pedibus* asini ad conculcandum. In quo intelligimus, quod populus gentium convertendus documenta Apostolorum debebat venerari, sed eruditiones Iudaeorum et caeremonias conculcare. Unde Chrysostomus[^p492-3]: « Super iumentum Apostoli posuerunt vestimenta sua; sub pedibus vero ceteri, quia mandatis Apostolorum evangelicis substernuntur Christiani, Legis autem consuetudines conculcant », Unde ad Philippenses tertio[^p492-4]: « Quae mihi fuerunt lucra arbitratus sum ut stercora, ut Christum lucrifacerem ». — Secundum *moralitatem* autem per *strationem vestimentorum in via* intelligimus conculcationem nostrorum corporum per abstinentiam et patientiam ad dandum aliis bona exempla, per quae dirigantur in via. Unde Glossa[^p492-5]: « Turba, Martyrum exempla sequens, carnem per abstinentiam edomat, ut Domino iter ad mentem paret et sequentibus praebeat bona exempla ». In cuius etiam rei figuram dicitur quarti Regum secundo[^p492-6], quod Elias reliquit pallium suum Eliseo. Quia bonum exemplum quasi quodam odore trahit ad imitationem, ideo Canticorum quarto[^p492-7]: « Odor vestimentorum tuorum super omnia aromata ». Et haec in via strata videmus; unde ad Hebraeos duodecimo: « Habentes tantam nubem testium » etc.

55. (Vers. 37.). Secundo quantum ad *expressionem devotionis in sermone* subditur: *Et cum appropinquaret iam ad descensum montis Oliveti,* propter congruentiam loci, quia prope Ierusalem. Unde et de illo loco ascendit; Actuum primo[^p492-8]: « Reversi sunt in Ierosolymam a monte Oliveti, qui est iuxta Ierusalem, Sabbati habens iter ». Per *descensum* autem illum intelligitur descensus dignationis misericordiae Dei, propter quem merito a nobis debet laudari, secundum illud Psalmi[^p492-9]: « Laudate Dominum omnes gentes, laudate eum omnes populi; quoniam confirmata est super nos misericordia eius » etc. — Et ideo subdit: *Coeperunt omnes turbae descendentium*[^p492-10] *gaudentes laudare Deum voce magna,* per congruentiam *modi;* Ecclesiastici trigesimo nono: « Date nomini eius magnificentiam et confitemini illi in voce labiorum vestrorum »; et Psalmus: « Bene psallite ei in vociferatione ». — Ratio autem huius laudis magnificae fuit consideratio mirabilium Christi; et ideo addit: *Super omnibus quas viderant virtutibus,* per congruentiam *ex parte motivi.* Nam visio mirabilium inducit et movet ad laudandum; Psalmus[^p492-11]: « Cantate Domino canticum novum, quia mirabilia fecit ». Inter omnia autem mirabilia, quae fecit, maximum fuit de suscitatione mortui quatriduani; ratione cuius praecipue turba obviam Christo venit; Ioannis duodecimo[^p492-12]: « Testimonium perhibebat turba, quae erat cum eo, quando Lazarum vocavit de monumento et suscitavit eum a mortuis. Propterea et obviam venit ei turba, quia audierat, eum fecisse hoc signum ». Turbae autem movebantur ad signa, ut crederent, ipsum esse Christum regem promissum in Lege. Unde Ioannis sexto[^p492-13]: « Illi homines, cum vidissent, quod fecerat Iesus signum, dicebant, quia hic est vere Propheta, qui venturus est in mundum ».

56. (Vers. 38.). *Dicentes: Benedictus* etc. Post devotionem laudantium describitur hic secundo[^p492-14] *celsitudo divinarum laudum.* Describitur ergo laus Christi excellens ex ore turbarum excellenter laudantium regem Christum et quantum ad *venientis excellentem personam* et quantum ad ipsius *adventus excellentem efficaciam.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *venientis excellentem personam* dicitur: *Dicentes: Benedictus est rex, qui venit in nomine Domini*[^p492-15]. Benedicitur autem
<!-- page 493 -->
Christus, non quia ex benedictione humana aliquid sibi acquiratur, sed tanquam summe bonus laudatur. Unde Hieronymus[^p493-1]: « Benedictio, quae fit *in Deo,* confessio sola est et laudatio bonorum, quae praestita sunt; benedictio vero, quae fit *a Deo,* in nobis impletur, dum eius beneficiis implemur ». Benedicunt ergo et laudant eum tanquam *regem ex semine David* secundum carnem natum; supra primo[^p493-2]: « Dabit illi Dominus Deus sedem David patris eius, et regnabit » etc.; et ad Romanos decimo quinto allegans Apostolus auctoritatem Isaiae dicit: « Et rursus Isaias ait: Erit radix Iesse, et qui exsurget regere gentes, in eum gentes sperabunt »; quod sumtum est de Isaiae undecimo secundum aliam translationem; et Ieremiae vigesimo tertio: « Ecce, dies venient, et suscitabo David germen iustum, et regnabit rex » etc. — Benedicunt etiam eum tanquam *divinitus missum,* cum dicunt: *Qui venit in nomine Domini. In nomine Domini venit* qui ex parte Dei venit; Ioannis quinto[^p493-3]: « Ego veni in nomine Patris mei ».

57. Venit autem Christus in nomine Domini tripliciter, scilicet *in carnem;* Ioannis primo[^p493-4]: « In propria venit, et sui eum non receperunt »; et hic adventus est *memorandus* propter nimiam dignationem; Aggaei secundo: « Adhuc modicum, et ego movebo caelum et terram »; « et veniet desideratus » etc. In huius figuram dicitur secundi Regum ultimo: « Quid causae est, ut veniat dominus meus rex ad servum suum » etc. — Venit *in mentem;* de quo Iob nono[^p493-5]: « Si venerit ad me, non videbo eum »; et hic adventus est *acceptandus* propter nimiam dilectionem; Ioannis decimo quarto: « Si quis diligit me, sermonem meum servabit, et Pater meus diliget eum, et ad eum veniemus et mansionem apud eum faciemus »; et illud Sapientiae septimo: « Venerunt mihi omnia bona pariter cum illa ». — Venit *ad iudicium finale,* de quo Apocalypsis primo[^p493-6]: « Ecce, venit cum nubibus » etc.; et hic adventus est *praestolandus* per nimiam discretionem; Malachiae tertio: « Ecce, venit, et quis poterit cogitare diem adventus eius » etc.; quia supra duodecimo: « Beatus ille servus, quem, cum venerit Dominus, invenerit vigilantem ». — In quolibet autem trium adventuum ille qui venit, scilicet Christus, est benedicendus; Psalmus[^p493-7]: « O Domine, salvum me fac, o Domine, bene prosperare. Benedictus, qui venit in nomine Domini ». Et pro hoc triplici adventu Ecclesia solemnizat, unum recolens, alium petens et tertium ut propinquum praevidens.

58. Secundo quantum ad *adventus excellentem efficaciam* subdit: *Pax in caelo et gloria in excelsis,* in quo datur intelligi duplex efficacia adventus Christi. Prima est in *reconciliatione peccantium per gratiam;* et hanc notat, cum dicit *pax in caelo;* ad Colossenses primo[^p493-8]: « Pacificans per sanguinem crucis suae quae in caelis sunt et quae in terris. Et vos, cum essetis aliquando alienati et inimici in operibus malis, nunc reconciliati estis in corpore carnis eius ». Et pro hac facienda venit; ad Ephesios secundo[^p493-9]: « Ipse est pax nostra, qui fecit utraque unum ». « Et veniens evangelizavit pacem vobis, qui fuistis longe, et pacem his qui prope » etc. — Secunda est *beatificatio iustorum per gloriam;* Psalmus[^p493-10]: « Gloriabuntur in te omnes, qui diligunt nomen tuum ». Et de hoc duplici effectu rursus in Psalmo: « Quoniam misericordiam et veritatem diligit Deus, gratiam et gloriam dabit Dominus »; et hic duplex effectus est per Christum venientem, secundum illud ad Romanos quinto[^p493-11]: « Iustificati ex fide, pacem habeamus ad Deum per Dominum nostrum Iesum Christum, per quem accessum habemus per fidem in gratiam istam, in qua et stamus et gloriamur in spe gloriae filiorum Dei ».

59. Et de hoc duplici effectu nobili debet Christus veniens collaudari et in sua nativitate et imminente passione; unde, sicut Angeli cantaverunt: « Gloria in excelsis Deo, et in terra pax hominibus » etc., supra secundo[^p493-12]; sic versa vice cantant nunc homines, imminente passione. Et ideo Glossa: « Sicut, nascente Domino hominum et Angelorum, agmina caelestia decantabant: *Gloria in excelsis Deo* etc., sic eidem mox triumphaturo homines concinunt: *Pax in caelo et gloria in excelsis* ». Alii autem Evangelistae dicunt, quod cantabant: « Hosanna filio David, hosanna in excelsis »! Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p493-13] et Marci undecimo; in quo continetur et intellectus gloriae et multorum aliorum, et ideo non fuit translatum. Unde Chrysostomus[^p493-14]: « Quidam
<!-- page 494 -->
interpretantur Hosanna *gloriam,* quidam *redemptionem,* alii *salvifica* sive *salvum me fac* ». Augustinus[^p494-1] autem dicit, quod « Hosanna est vox laetantis vel obsecrantis, ut dicunt nonnulli Hebraei, magis affectum indicans quam aliquam rem significans, sicut *racha* interiectio indignantis esse perhibetur. Unde secundum vocem nec Latinus neque Graecus hoc interpretari potuit, sed secundum sensum ». Et hoc vult beatus Lucas dicere, cum dicit: « Pax in caelo et gloria in excelsis ». — Unde quia ista laus fuit laus sublimis humilitatis Christi, ideo ex ipsa cum laude Seraphim, quae est laus Trinitatis, constituitur laus immediate antecedens canonem Missae in consecratione Sacramenti altaris, qua dicitur: Sanctus, Sanctus, Sanctus[^p494-2] etc.

60. (Vers. 39.). *Et quidam Pharisaeorum* etc. Post devotionem laudantium et celsitudinem laudum subditur *confutatio invidorum*[^p494-3], circa quam describendam duo introducuntur, scilicet *indignatio invidentium* et *approbatio laudatorum.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *indignationem invidentium* dicit: *Et quidam Pharisaeorum de turbis dixerunt ad illum: Magister, increpa discipulos tuos.* Hoc autem dixerunt ex indignatione; unde Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p494-4]: « Videntes principes sacerdotum et scribae mirabilia, quae fecit, et pueros clamantes et dicentes: Hosanna filio David, indignati sunt ». Haec autem indignatio ortum habebat ex invidia, quae praecipitabat eos in stultitiam et dementiam. Unde Glossa[^p494-5]: « Mira invidentium dementia: quem vera docentem noverunt, cum *magistrum* nominari audiunt, discipulos eius, quasi melius edocti, redarguendos aestimant, quos ille instruxerat, quem *Deum* apparet esse per miracula ». Unde ex laude et gloria Christi maxime commoti sunt ad iracundiam scribae et Pharisaei, cum potius deberent laetari; secundum illud Ioannis duodecimo[^p494-6]: « Pharisaei dixerunt ad semetipsos: Videtis, quia nihil proficimus. Ecce, mundus totus post eum abiit ». Tristati sunt igitur, unde debuissent laetari, et excaecati, unde debuissent illuminari; et hoc iusto iudicio Dei, qui dicit Ioannis nono: « In iudicium ego veni in hunc mundum, ut qui non vident videant, et qui vident caeci fiant ». Unde et propter illuminationem fidelium et confutationem rebellium Dominus acceptavit honorem istum, sicut Glossa[^p494-7] dicit: « Frequenter ingressus est civitatem Ierusalem, sed non cum his laudibus, non *rex* vocatus, quod semper fugerat, nisi modo, cum ascendit passurus; quod ideo factum est, ut amplius illorum adversum se excitaret invidiam, quia iam tempus passionis instabat »; non quia Dominus malum eorum vellet intendi, sed quia, iusto iudicio suo faciendo quod debuit, illi occasionem acceperunt, ut ardentius inflammarentur ad odium, ex quo Deus magnum eliceret bonum. Unde Chrysostomus[^p494-8]: « Excitavit eos, non ut facerent quod ante noluerunt, sed ut possent facere quod prius volebant. Facultas data est, non mutata voluntas ». Unde impletum est in his illud Sapientiae secundo: « Gravis est nobis ad videndum, quoniam dissimilis est aliis vita illius, et immutatae sunt viae illius ».

61. (Vers. 40.). Secundo quantum ad *approbationem laudatorum* subditur: *Quibus ipse ait: Dico vobis, quia, si hi tacuerint, lapides clamabunt,* quasi dicat, quod haec voluntas Dei est, quod ipsi laudent, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p494-9]: « At ille dixit: Nunquam legistis, quia ex ore infantium et lactentium perfecisti laudem »? Hoc enim scriptum est in Psalmo; quod voluit Dominus facere ad confusionem Pharisaeorum, unde statim subditur: « Propter inimicos tuos, ut destruas inimicum et ultorem ». Hic autem plus dicit, quod sit voluntas Dei, ut a Pharisaeis non possit[^p494-10] impediri.

62. Voluit autem Dominus sic fieri, imminente passione, et propter *exemplum* et propter *mysterium:* propter *exemplum,* ut ignominia passionis subsequentis gravior esset ex magnitudine gloriae praecedentis, secundum illud Iob decimo sexto[^p494-11]: « Ego, ille quondam opulentus, repente contritus sum ». In hoc etiam ostenditur, quale sit gaudium huius mundi, quia citissime transit; Proverbiorum decimo quarto: « Risus dolore miscebitur, et extrema gaudii luctus occupat ». Et per hoc ostenditur,
<!-- page 495 -->
mundanum gaudium et praeconium laudis et gloriam mundi esse contemnendam; et quod per famam et infamiam oportet transire eum qui vult ire in Ierusalem supernam, secundum illud secundae ad Corinthios sexto[^p495-1]: « Per arma iustitiae a dextris et a sinistris, per gloriam et ignobilitatem, per infamiam et bonam famam, ut seductores et veraces, quasi tristes, semper autem gaudentes, tanquam nihil habentes et omnia possidentes ». — Voluit etiam hoc propter *mysterium,* quia laus parvulorum significat laudem ipsius in Ecclesia gentium. Ideo dicit, quod *lapides clamabunt,* id est gentes, quae dicuntur *lapides,* quia corda habebant dura et lapidea. Ideo in huius figuram supra tertio[^p495-2]: « Dico vobis, quod potens est Deus de lapidibus istis suscitare filios Abrahae », duritiam scilicet cordium auferendo, secundum illud Ezechielis trigesimo sexto: « Auferam a vobis cor lapideum et dabo vobis cor carneum ». Emollitum est autem cor eorum durum per virtutem Crucis, quae petram convertit in aquam. In huius figuram dicitur Matthaei vigesimo septimo[^p495-3], quod in morte Christi « petrae scissae sunt, et monumenta aperta sunt ». Unde Glossa[^p495-4]: « Si caecitas contigerit in Israel, ut a laude Dei conticeat; populus gentium, mollito corde lapideo, Creatorem suum credet et annuntiabit »; secundum illud ad Romanos undecimo: « Caecitas ex parte contigit in Israel, donec plenitudo gentium intraret, et sic omnis Israel salvus fieret ». Unde in huius designationem expressam dicitur Ioannis duodecimo[^p495-5] statim post murmur Pharisaeorum: « Erant autem quidam gentiles, qui ascenderant ad diem festum. Et venerunt ad Philippum dicentes: Domine, volumus Iesum videre »; et sequitur ibi, quod « Philippus et Andreas dixerunt ad Iesum »; « Iesus autem dixit: Pater, venit hora; clarifica Filium tuum ». — Ex quo manifeste apparet, quod haec facta sunt ad manifestandum nomen Christi et ad praesignandum vocationem populi Christiani, excaecatis Iudaeis, de quorum proxima subversione in consequentibus agit, deplorans excidium Ierusalem, civitatis regalis.

## English
<!-- page 491 -->

53. *But as he went out*[^p491-13] etc. After he has shown that he has the royal power through his excellence in commanding and the obedience of the disciples in complying, here he shows it third[^p491-14] *through the honouring of the peoples in praising together.* Now for expressing this there is set forth first *the devotion of those praising;* second, *the loftiness of the praises,* there[^p491-15]: *Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord;* third, *the confutation of the envious,* there: *And some of the Pharisees* etc.

Concerning, then, the *devotion of those praising* that is to be expressed, two things are brought in, namely the expression *of devotion in work* and the expression *in speech and voice.*

<!-- page 492 -->
54. (Vers. 36.). First, then, as to *the expression of devotion in work* he says: *But as he went out, they spread their garments underneath in the way,* which was a sign of great devotion. But he does not express who they were that spread them, because all were commonly doing this, and chiefly the multitudes. Whence Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p492-1]: « A very great multitude spread their garments in the way, and others cut boughs from the trees and strewed them in the way ». — And note that it is not free from *mystery* here that the disciples *put* their garments *upon the young ass*[^p492-2], but the multitude *spread their garments under the feet* of the ass, to be trodden upon. In which we understand that the people of the Gentiles that was to be converted ought to venerate the lessons of the Apostles, but to tread upon the eruditions and ceremonies of the Jews. Whence Chrysostom[^p492-3]: « Upon the beast the Apostles put their garments; but the rest under its feet, because Christians are laid under the evangelical commandments of the Apostles, but tread upon the customs of the Law », Whence to the Philippians, the third chapter[^p492-4]: « The things that were gain to me I have counted as dung, that I might gain Christ ». — But according to *morality,* by *the strewing of the garments in the way* we understand the treading down of our bodies through abstinence and patience, so as to give good examples to others, by which they may be directed in the way. Whence the Gloss[^p492-5]: « The multitude, following the examples of the Martyrs, tames the flesh through abstinence, that it may prepare a road for the Lord to the mind, and may afford good examples to those who follow ». In figure also of which thing it is said in the fourth of Kings, the second chapter[^p492-6], that Elias left his mantle to Eliseus. Because a good example draws to imitation as by a certain smell, therefore Canticles, the fourth chapter[^p492-7]: « The smell of thy garments is above all aromatical spices ». And these we see in the strewn way; whence to the Hebrews, the twelfth chapter: « Having so great a cloud of witnesses » etc.

55. (Vers. 37.). Second, as to *the expression of devotion in speech* it is subjoined: *And when he was now coming near the descent of mount Olivet,* because of the fittingness of the place, since it is near Jerusalem. Whence also from that place he ascended; Acts, the first chapter[^p492-8]: « They returned to Jerusalem from mount Olivet, which is nigh Jerusalem, within a sabbath day's journey ». But by that *descent* is understood the descent of the condescension of the mercy of God, on account of which he ought worthily to be praised by us, according to that of the Psalm[^p492-9]: « Praise the Lord, all ye nations; praise him, all ye people; for his mercy is confirmed upon us » etc. — And therefore he subjoins: *All the multitudes of them that were descending*[^p492-10] *began, rejoicing, to praise God with a loud voice,* by fittingness of *manner;* Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-ninth chapter: « Magnify his name, and give glory to him with the voice of your lips »; and the Psalm: « Sing well unto him with a loud noise ». — But the reason of this magnificent praise was the consideration of the marvellous things of Christ; and therefore he adds: *For all the mighty works they had seen,* by fittingness *on the part of the motive.* For the sight of marvellous things induces and moves to praising; the Psalm[^p492-11]: « Sing ye to the Lord a new canticle, because he hath done marvellous things ». But among all the marvellous things that he did, the greatest was the raising of the man four days dead; by reason of which chiefly the multitude came to meet Christ; John, the twelfth chapter[^p492-12]: « The multitude gave testimony, which was with him, when he called Lazarus out of the grave, and raised him from the dead. For which reason also the multitude came to meet him, because they heard that he had done this sign ». Now the multitudes were moved by the signs to believe that he was the Christ, the king promised in the Law. Whence John, the sixth chapter[^p492-13]: « Those men, when they had seen what a sign Jesus had done, said: This is of a truth the Prophet, that is to come into the world ».

56. (Vers. 38.). *Saying: Blessed* etc. After the devotion of those praising there is described here, second[^p492-14], *the loftiness of the divine praises.* There is described, then, the excellent praise of Christ from the mouth of the multitudes excellently praising Christ the king, both as to *the excellent person of him that cometh* and as to *the excellent efficacy* of his *coming.*

First, then, as to *the excellent person of him that cometh* it is said: *Saying: Blessed is the king who cometh in the name of the Lord*[^p492-15]. But Christ is
<!-- page 493 -->
blessed, not because anything is acquired for him from human blessing, but he is praised as supremely good. Whence Jerome[^p493-1]: « Blessing which is done *in God* is only confession and the praising of the goods that have been bestowed; but blessing which is done *by God* is fulfilled in us, while we are filled with his benefits ». They bless him, then, and praise him as *the king of the seed of David,* born according to the flesh; above, the first chapter[^p493-2]: « The Lord God shall give unto him the throne of David his father, and he shall reign » etc.; and to the Romans, the fifteenth chapter, the Apostle, alleging the authority of Isaias, says: « And again Isaias saith: There shall be a root of Jesse, and he that shall rise up to rule the Gentiles, in him the Gentiles shall hope »; which is taken from Isaias, the eleventh chapter, according to another translation; and Jeremias, the twenty-third chapter: « Behold, the days shall come, and I will raise up to David a just branch, and a king shall reign » etc. — They bless him also as *sent from God,* when they say: *Who cometh in the name of the Lord. He cometh in the name of the Lord* who cometh on the part of God; John, the fifth chapter[^p493-3]: « I am come in the name of my Father ».

57. Now Christ comes in the name of the Lord in three ways, namely *into the flesh;* John, the first chapter[^p493-4]: « He came unto his own, and his own received him not »; and this coming is *to be remembered* on account of the exceeding condescension; Aggeus, the second chapter: « Yet one little while, and I will move the heaven and the earth »; « and the desired shall come » etc. In figure of this it is said in the second of Kings, the last chapter: « Wherefore is my lord the king come to his servant » etc. — He comes *into the mind;* of which Job, the ninth chapter[^p493-5]: « If he come to me, I shall not see him »; and this coming is *to be accepted* on account of the exceeding dilection; John, the fourteenth chapter: « If any one love me, he will keep my word, and my Father will love him, and we will come to him, and will make our abode with him »; and that of Wisdom, the seventh chapter: « Now all good things came to me together with her ». — He comes *to the final judgement,* of which Apocalypse, the first chapter[^p493-6]: « Behold, he cometh with the clouds » etc.; and this coming is *to be awaited* through exceeding discretion; Malachias, the third chapter: « Behold, he cometh, and who shall be able to think of the day of his coming » etc.; because above, the twelfth chapter: « Blessed is that servant, whom, when the Lord shall come, he shall find watching ». — But in each of the three comings he that cometh, namely Christ, is to be blessed; the Psalm[^p493-7]: « O Lord, save me; O Lord, give good success. Blessed be he that cometh in the name of the Lord ». And for this threefold coming the Church keeps solemnity, recalling the one, asking for the other, and foreseeing the third as near.

58. Second, as to *the excellent efficacy of the coming* he subjoins: *Peace in heaven, and glory in the highest,* in which is given to be understood a twofold efficacy of the coming of Christ. The first is in *the reconciliation of sinners through grace;* and this he notes when he says *peace in heaven;* to the Colossians, the first chapter[^p493-8]: « Making peace through the blood of his cross, both the things that are in heaven and the things that are on earth. And you, whereas you were some time alienated and enemies in evil works, now are reconciled in the body of his flesh ». And for the making of this he came; to the Ephesians, the second chapter[^p493-9]: « He is our peace, who hath made both one ». « And coming, he preached the gospel of peace to you that were afar off, and peace to them that were nigh » etc. — The second is *the beatification of the just through glory;* the Psalm[^p493-10]: « All they that love thy name shall glory in thee ». And of this twofold effect again in the Psalm: « For God loveth mercy and truth; the Lord will give grace and glory »; and this twofold effect is through Christ coming, according to that to the Romans, the fifth chapter[^p493-11]: « Being justified by faith, let us have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ, by whom we have access through faith into this grace, wherein we stand, and glory in the hope of the glory of the sons of God ».

59. And for this twofold noble effect Christ, coming, ought to be praised together both in his nativity and when the passion was imminent; whence, as the Angels sang: « Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men » etc., above, the second chapter[^p493-12]; so in turn men now sing, when the passion is imminent. And therefore the Gloss: « As, when the Lord of men and of Angels was born, the heavenly hosts sang: *Glory to God in the highest* etc., so to the same, soon to triumph, men sing together: *Peace in heaven, and glory in the highest* ». But the other Evangelists say that they sang: « Hosanna to the son of David, hosanna in the highest »! Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p493-13], and Mark, the eleventh chapter; in which is contained the understanding both of glory and of many other things, and therefore it was not translated. Whence Chrysostom[^p493-14]: « Some
<!-- page 494 -->
interpret Hosanna *glory,* some *redemption,* others *save* or *save me* ». But Augustine[^p494-1] says that « Hosanna is the voice of one rejoicing or beseeching, as some Hebrews say, indicating an affection rather than signifying any thing, as *racha* is said to be the interjection of one indignant. Hence neither the Latin nor the Greek could interpret this according to the word, but according to the sense ». And this blessed Luke means to say when he says: « Peace in heaven, and glory in the highest ». — Hence, because that praise was the praise of the sublime humility of Christ, therefore from it, with the praise of the Seraphim, which is the praise of the Trinity, is constituted the praise immediately preceding the canon of the Mass in the consecration of the Sacrament of the altar, in which is said: Holy, Holy, Holy[^p494-2] etc.

60. (Vers. 39.). *And some of the Pharisees* etc. After the devotion of those praising and the loftiness of the praises there is subjoined *the confutation of the envious*[^p494-3], for describing which two things are brought in, namely *the indignation of those envying* and *the approval of those praising.*

First, then, as to *the indignation of those envying* he says: *And some of the Pharisees, from amongst the multitudes, said to him: Master, rebuke thy disciples.* Now they said this out of indignation; whence Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p494-4]: « The chief priests and the scribes, seeing the marvellous things that he did, and the children crying and saying: Hosanna to the son of David, were moved with indignation ». Now this indignation had its rise from envy, which cast them headlong into folly and madness. Whence the Gloss[^p494-5]: « Marvellous madness of the envious: him whom they knew to be teaching true things, when they hear him named *Master,* they reckon his disciples, as if themselves better taught, to be convicted, whom he had instructed, he who through the miracles is plainly *God* ». Hence by the praise and glory of Christ the scribes and Pharisees were most of all moved to wrath, when rather they ought to have rejoiced; according to that of John, the twelfth chapter[^p494-6]: « The Pharisees said among themselves: Do you see that we prevail nothing? Behold, the whole world is gone after him ». They were made sad, then, whence they ought to have rejoiced, and blinded, whence they ought to have been enlightened; and this by the just judgement of God, who says in John, the ninth chapter: « For judgement I am come into this world, that they who see not may see, and they who see may become blind ». Hence also for the enlightening of the faithful and the confutation of the rebellious the Lord accepted this honour, as the Gloss[^p494-7] says: « He often entered the city of Jerusalem, but not with these praises, not called *king,* which he had always fled, save now, when he went up to suffer; which was done for this reason, that he might the more stir up their envy against himself, because the time of the passion was already at hand »; not because the Lord willed that their evil should be intensified, but because, while he by his just judgement did what he ought, they took occasion to be more ardently inflamed to hatred, out of which God might draw a great good. Whence Chrysostom[^p494-8]: « He stirred them up, not that they should do what before they would not, but that they might be able to do what before they willed. A faculty was given, not a will changed ». Whence that of Wisdom, the second chapter, was fulfilled in them: « He is grievous unto us, even to behold; for his life is not like other men's, and his ways are very different ».

61. (Vers. 40.). Second, as to *the approval of those praising* it is subjoined: *To whom he said: I say to you, that if these shall hold their peace, the stones will cry out,* as if he should say that this is the will of God, that they should praise, according to that of Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p494-9]: « But he said: Have you never read: Out of the mouth of infants and of sucklings thou hast perfected praise »? For this is written in the Psalm; which the Lord willed to do for the confusion of the Pharisees, whence it is at once subjoined: « Because of thy enemies, that thou mayst destroy the enemy and the avenger ». But here he says more: that it is the will of God that it cannot[^p494-10] be hindered by the Pharisees.

62. But the Lord willed that it be done thus, when the passion was imminent, both on account of *example* and on account of *mystery:* on account of *example,* that the ignominy of the passion following might be heavier from the greatness of the glory going before, according to that of Job, the sixteenth chapter[^p494-11]: « I, that was formerly so wealthy, am all on a sudden broken to pieces ». In this it is shown also what manner of thing is the joy of this world, because it passes away most swiftly; Proverbs, the fourteenth chapter: « Laughter shall be mingled with sorrow, and mourning taketh hold of the end of joy ». And through this it is shown
<!-- page 495 -->
that worldly joy and the proclamation of praise and the glory of the world are to be contemned; and that he who wills to go into the heavenly Jerusalem must pass through good report and evil report, according to that of the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter[^p495-1]: « By the armour of justice on the right hand and on the left, by glory and dishonour, by evil report and good report, as deceivers and yet true, as sorrowful, yet always rejoicing, as having nothing and possessing all things ». — He willed this also on account of *mystery,* because the praise of the little ones signifies his praise in the Church of the Gentiles. Therefore he says that *the stones will cry out,* that is, the Gentiles, who are called *stones* because they had hearts hard and stony. Therefore in figure of this, above, the third chapter[^p495-2]: « I say unto you, that God is able of these stones to raise up children to Abraham », namely by taking away the hardness of hearts, according to that of Ezechiel, the thirty-sixth chapter: « I will take away from you the stony heart, and will give you a heart of flesh ». Now their hard heart was softened through the power of the Cross, which turned the rock into water. In figure of this it is said in Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p495-3], that at the death of Christ « the rocks were rent, and the graves were opened ». Whence the Gloss[^p495-4]: « If blindness has befallen Israel, so that it is silent from the praise of God, the people of the Gentiles, the stony heart softened, will believe and announce its Creator »; according to that to the Romans, the eleventh chapter: « Blindness in part has happened in Israel, until the fulness of the Gentiles should come in, and so all Israel should be saved ». Whence, in express designation of this, it is said in John, the twelfth chapter[^p495-5], immediately after the murmur of the Pharisees: « Now there were certain Gentiles, who had come up to the festival day. And they came to Philip, saying: Sir, we would see Jesus »; and it follows there that « Philip and Andrew told Jesus »; « But Jesus said: Father, the hour is come; glorify thy Son ». — From which it manifestly appears that these things were done to make manifest the name of Christ and to foreshow the calling of the Christian people, the Jews being blinded, of whose near overthrow he treats in what follows, bewailing the destruction of Jerusalem, the royal city.
