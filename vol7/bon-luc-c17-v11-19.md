# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XVII, Vers. 11-19
## *Confirmatio per factum mirabile*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 433 -->

#### Confirmatio per factum mirabile.

22. *Et factum est, dum iret Iesus*[^p433-9] etc. Postquam discipulos ad spiritualia opera pietatis informavit documento rationabili et manuduxit exemplo sensibili, hic iam tertio *confirmat facto mirabili*[^p433-10]; in quo ipsi discipuli *ex viso opere mirabili* confirmantur ad *credendum,* et *ex intellecto* accenduntur ad *imitandum;* et ideo simul *litteraliter* et *spiritualiter* hoc miraculum tractandum est.

In huius autem miraculi descriptione primo praemittitur *curatio aegritudinis incurabilis.* Secundo additur *approbatio gratitudinis commendabilis,* ibi[^p433-11]: *Unus autem ex illis, ut vidit.*

Circa *curationem* autem *aegritudinis incurabilis* tria introducuntur ad expressionem et commendationem miraculi et ad mysterium significationis, scilicet *congruitas loci, calamitas morbi* et *facilitas modi curandi.*

23. (Vers. 11.). Primo igitur quantum ad *congruitatem loci* dicit: *Et factum est, dum iret Iesus in Ierusalem;* per quam significatur *status quietis;* nam interpretatur *visio pacis;* Psalmus[^p433-12]: « Lauda, Ierusalem, Dominum, lauda » etc.; et post: « Qui posuit fines tuos pacem ». — *Transibat per mediam Samariam et Galilaeam;* per quas designatur *decursus praesentis itineris. Samaria* namque interpretatur *custodia,* quae debet esse in actione quantum ad *actum,* secundum illud Ecclesiastae quarto[^p433-13]: « Custodi pedem tuum, ingrediens domum Dei »; item, quantum ad *affectum;* Deuteronomii quarto: « Custodi temetipsum et animam tuam sollicite »; item, quantum ad *verbum;* Psalmus[^p433-14]: « Dixi:
<!-- page 434 -->
Custodiam vias meas, ut non delinquam in lingua mea ». *Galilaea* autem interpretatur *transmigratio*[^p434-1], quae debet esse in contemplatione, secundum illud Ecclesiastae secundo: « Transivi ad contemplandam sapientiam » etc.; et in Psalmo: « Quoniam transibo in locum tabernaculi admirabilis usque ad domum Dei ». — Et nota, quod dicitur *transire per mediam Samariam,* et post exprimitur *Galilaea;* quia prius oportet transire per *activam,* postea per *contemplativam;* unde Iacob non habuisset Rachelem, nisi prius habuisset Liam[^p434-2]. Et dicitur *per mediam transire;* nam in *actione* medium teneri debet, declinando superfluum et diminutum[^p434-3]; in *praelatione* similiter, declinando acceptionem personarum; Exodi trigesimo secundo: « Ite et redite de porta ad portam per medium castrorum » etc.; in *contemplatione* similiter medium tenetur; in cuius figuram dicitur de Seraphim Isaiae sexto[^p434-4], quod « duabus alis velabant faciem et duabus velabant pedes et duabus intermediis volabant ».

24. (Vers. 12.), Secundo quantum ad *calamitatem morbi* subdit: *Et cum ingrederetur quoddam castellum, occurrerunt ei decem viri leprosi. Ad litteram* lepra est morbus miserabilis calamitatis, quia aufert remedium artis, privat solatio societatis, affligit gravamine infirmitatis[^p434-5]. — Et tales desolati recursum habere debent ad Patrem misericordiae; ideo addit: *Qui steterunt a longe,* suam recognoscentes immunditiam.

25. (Vers. 13.). *Et levaverunt vocem,* suam exprimentes miseriam; *dicentes: Iesu praeceptor, miserere nostri,* divinam implorantes misericordiam, iuxta quod Propheta in Psalmo[^p434-6]: « Afflictus sum et humiliatus sum nimis, rugiebam a gemitu cordis mei ».

26. *Spiritualiter* autem per *decem leprosos* intelligitur *universitas peccatorum* peccantium contra decalogum; unde Glossa[^p434-7]: « Qui contra decalogum peccant, nec amando Deum, de quo male sentiunt, nec proximum, a quo dividuntur, sub denario numero ad Deum clamant ». Oritur autem *universitas peccatorum* ex quatuor causis, scilicet ex *timore male humiliante;* et sic designatur per lepram *Mariae* Numerorum duodecimo[^p434-8], ubi dicitur, quod postquam murmuravit contra Moysen, « apparuit candens lepra quasi nix ». — Secundo, ex *amore male inflammante ad concupiscentiam carnis;* et hoc significatur per lepram *Naaman Syri,* de quo dicitur, quod « erat vir fortis et dives, sed leprosus », quarti Regum quinto[^p434-9]. — Tertio, ex *amore male inflammante ad concupiscentiam oculorum;* quae intelligitur per lepram *Giezi,* quarti Regum quinto[^p434-10]. Nam dictum est ei propter cupiditatem et simoniam: « Lepra Naaman adhaerebit tibi et semini tuo » etc. — Quarto, ex *amore male inflammante ad superbiam vitae;* et hoc intelligitur per lepram *Oziae,* de quo dicitur secundi Paralipomenon vigesimo sexto[^p434-11], quod cum vellet ponere incensum in domo Domini et dignitatem usurpare sacerdotalem, percussus est lepra. — In figura harum quatuor causarum leprae dicitur quarti Regum septimo[^p434-12], quod « *quatuor* viri leprosi erant in porta Samariae », ad designandam *quadriformitatem causae;* et hic sunt *decem* ad designandam *universitatem culpae.*

27. *Spiritualiter* autem per *lepram* intelligitur vitium contra christianam religionem, quod quatuor modis habet fieri; unde Glossa[^p434-13]: « Quisquis haeretica pravitate, vel gentili superstitione, vel Iudaica perfidia, vel fraterno schismate, per Dei gratiam mundatus, caruerit; necesse est, ut ad Ecclesiam redeat ». — His autem quatuor vitiis praedictis praecipue impugnatur lex veritatis, quae consistit in observatione decalogi; et ideo merito per *quatuor* et per *decem* habent figurari. Huiusmodi autem peccatores, licet per vias tenebrosas incedant, secundum illud Proverbiorum quarto[^p434-14]: « Via impiorum tenebrosa, nesciunt, ubi corruant », Domino tamen *intrante castellum,* id est veniente in carnem, illuminantur ad videndam viam, secundum illud Isaiae nono: « Habitantibus in regione umbrae mortis lux orta est eis ».. Et *occurrunt ei,* per *poenitentiam,* quae faciem aversam facit converti; Ieremiae trigesimo primo[^p434-15]: « Converte me, Domine, et convertar ». « Postquam convertisti me, egi poenitentiam ». Haec autem poenitentia facit *stare a longe,* per *humilitatem propriae abiectionis;* infra decimo octavo[^p434-16]: « Publicanus a longe stans » etc.; facit *levare vocem,* per *amaritudinem compunctionis;* Habacuc tertio: « Dedit
<!-- page 435 -->
abyssus vocem suam », id est peccator; facit etiam *petere sanitatem,* per *fiduciam orationis,* secundum illud Psalmi: « Ego dixi: Domine, miserere mei, sana animam meam ».

28. (Vers. 14.). Tertio quantum ad *facilitatem modi curandi* subiungit: *Quos ut vidit, dixit: Ite, ostendite vos sacerdotibus.* Ad sacerdotes enim spectat leprae iudicium, secundum illud Levitici decimo quarto[^p435-1]: « Leprosus, quando mundandus est, adducetur ad sacerdotem » etc., per totum capitulum. — *Et factum est, dum irent, mundati sunt,* scilicet per divinum imperium, cui hoc est *dicere* quod *facere;* Ecclesiastae octavo[^p435-2]: « Sermo illius potestate plenus est »; et « omne, quod voluerit, faciet ».

29. *Spiritualiter* autem intelligitur hic modus curandi a *lepra spirituali,* videlicet per ministerium sacerdotis; et hoc insinuat in hoc quod dicit: *Ostendite vos sacerdotibus;* Levitici decimo quarto[^p435-3]: « Hic est ritus leprosi, qui mundandus est: adducetur ad sacerdotem ». Ideo Ecclesiastici septimo: « In tota anima tua time Deum et sacerdotes eius sanctifica ». « Honora Deum ex tota anima tua et honorifica sacerdotes et propurga te cum brachiis ». Honorificentia autem ista est per sui ipsius accusationem, secundum illud Iosue septimo[^p435-4]: « Fili, confitere et da gloriam Deo ». Ideo Ecclesiastici decimo septimo: « Cognosce iustitias et iudicia Dei et sta in sorte propositionis et orationis altissimi Dei; in partes vade saeculi sancti cum vivis et dantibus confessionem Deo ». Ideo dicit: *Ostendite* etc.; Ecclesiastici decimo octavo: « In tempore infirmitatis ostende conversationem ». — Qui sic vult confiteri iam incipit in voluntate rectificari; et ideo dicit: *Dum irent, mundati sunt,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p435-5]: « Dixi: Confitebor adversum me iniustitiam meam Domino » etc. Ex hoc manifeste apparet *efficacia confessionis* curantis omnem morbum atque languorem. Apparet etiam *dignitas sacerdotalis,* quam qui contemnit a Domino sanari non potest. Unde Iob trigesimo quarto[^p435-6]: « Nunquid qui non amat iudicium sanari potest »? Apparet etiam mira *Dei pietas,* quae omnes occurrentes curat, secundum illud Ioannis sexto[^p435-7]: « Omnis, qui venit ad me, non eiiciam foras ». Apparet etiam ex hoc, quanta debet esse *pietas in ministris Dei* ad infirma membra Christi; primae ad Thessalonicenses ultimo[^p435-8]: « Rogamus vos, fratres, consolamini pusillanimes, suscipite infirmos, patientes estote ad omnes. Videte, ne quis malum » etc. Propter hoc divina dispositio caput Apostolorum cadere permisit, ut in suo casu disceret, qualiter aliis misereri debuisset[^p435-9].

30. (Vers. 15.). *Unus autem ex illis* etc. Post curationem incurabilis morbi vel aegritudinis describitur hic secundo[^p435-10] *commendatio approbabilis gratitudinis,* circa quam introducuntur tria, scilicet *gratiarum actio, ingratitudinis argutio* et *gratitudinis approbatio.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *gratiarum actionem* praemittitur: *Unus autem ex illis, ut vidit, quia mundatus est,* per recognitionem scilicet beneficii; ad Romanos undecimo[^p435-11]: « Vide ergo in te bonitatem Dei, si permanseris in bonitate ». — *Regressus est, cum magna voce magnificans Deum,* per glorificationem benefactoris, secundum illud Tobiae duodecimo[^p435-12]: « Benedicite Deum caeli et coram omnibus viventibus confitemini illi, quia fecit vobiscum misericordiam suam »; et Psalmus: « Cantabo Domino, qui bona tribuit mihi » etc.

31. (Vers. 16.). *Et cecidit in faciem ante pedes eius, gratias agens ei*[^p435-13], scilicet per exhibitionem reverentiae et honoris; Ezechielis secundo: « Vidi et cecidi in faciem meam »; et Apocalypsis primo: « Cum vidissem eum, cecidi ad pedes eius tanquam mortuus ». — *Et hic erat Samaritanus,* ad expressionem figurae et significationis. Iste enim, quia Samaritanus et alienigena, significat gentiles ad cultum Christi redeuntes, sicut et illa mulier Samaritana, Ioannis quarto[^p435-14]. Designat autem, non solum quia *gentilis,* verum etiam quia *unus;* in quo intelligitur unitas ecclesiastica. Unde Glossa: « Unus regressus significat unius Ecclesiae devotam Christo humilitatem ». In cuius designationem Canticorum sexto[^p435-15]: « Una est columba mea »; et primae ad Corinthios nono: « Omnes quidem currunt, sed unus accipit bravium ». Propter hoc unus introducitur hic regressus, sicut in illa multitudine unus sanatur; Ioannis
<!-- page 436 -->
quinto[^p436-1]: « Movebatur aqua, et sanabatur unus »; et Christus unum tantum ibi curavit, ut, sicut dicit Augustinus, ostenderet, quod nullus sanari potest nisi intra ecclesiasticam unitatem; de qua unitate ad Ephesios quarto[^p436-2]: « Solliciti, servare unitatem spiritus in vinculo pacis ». « Unus Dominus, una fides, unum baptisma » etc. Hic autem unus dicitur *cadere in faciem,* quia Ecclesia non tantum habet *unitatem* per amorem, verum etiam *humilitatem* per gratitudinem et timorem[^p436-3]. Hoc significat ipse *casus in faciem;* casus enim *in faciem* significat *reverentiam,* secundum illud Apocalypsis septimo[^p436-4]: « Et omnes Angeli stabant in circuitu throni » etc.; sed casus *post tergum* significat *ruinam;* Ioannis decimo octavo: « Abierunt retrorsum et ceciderunt in terram »; et primi Regum quarto: « Cecidit Heli de sella retrorsum ». Unde Glossa[^p436-5]: « Qui in faciem cadit videt, quo cadat; qui retro cadit non videt. Boni ergo in *faciem* cadunt, quia humiliant se in his *visibilibus,* ubi vident, quid eos sequatur, ut ad invisibilia erigantur. Mali *retro* cadunt, quia cadunt in *invisibilibus,* ubi non vident, quid eos sequatur ». Cadit autem quis *in faciem* in *supernorum mirabilium revelatione,* sicut dicitur Danielis decimo[^p436-6]: « Audivi vocem sermonum eius, et audiens iacebam consternatus super faciem meam, et vultus meus haerebat terrae ». In *divinorum charismatum postulatione;* Iudith sexto[^p436-7]: « Omnis populus cecidit in faciem, adorantes Deum, et communi lamentatione et fletu unanimes preces suas Domino effuderunt » etc. In *beneficiorum susceptione;* Genesis decimo septimo: « Cecidit Abraham in faciem suam et risit » etc.

32. (Vers. 17.). Secundo quantum ad *ingratitudinis argutionem* subdit: *Respondens autem Iesus dixit: Nonne decem mundati sunt, et novem ubi sunt?* Non quaerit de novem sicut ignorans cuiuslibet eorum personam, sed reprobans ingratitudinis irreverentiam, sicut quaerebat Dominus Genesis tertio[^p436-8]: « Adam, ubi es »? Unde Glossa: « Ingratos quasi ignotos, ubi sint, requirit »; ita ut in eis verificetur illud primae ad Corinthios decimo quarto: « Ignorans ignorabitur ».

33. (Vers. 18.). Et ideo, ut ostendat, quod hoc reprobando dixit[^p436-9], subdit: *Non est inventus qui rediret et daret gloriam Deo, nisi hic alienigena.* In quo manifeste ostenditur Iudaeorum ingratitudo, quia, cum essent novem, nullus ex eis rediit, sed solus iste Samaritanus, qui unicus erat et solus. In quo intelligitur electio gentium et reprobatio Iudaeorum, secundum illud ad Ephesios secundo[^p436-10]: « Vos, qui eratis aliquando gentes in carne, qui eratis in illo tempore sine Christo, alienati a conversatione Israel et hospites testamentorum, promissionis spem non habentes et sine Deo in hoc mundo. Nunc autem in Christo Iesu vos, qui eratis aliquando longe, facti estis prope » etc. *Alienigena* dicitur qui alibi habitat et alibi natus est; sic est de omnibus, qui carnaliter nati ad spiritum transeunt, secundum illud ad Romanos nono[^p436-11]: « Non qui filii carnis, hi filii Dei, sed qui filii sunt promissionis aestimantur in semine »; quia, sicut dicitur Ioannis tertio, « quod natum est ex carne caro est » etc. Et ideo in Psalmo[^p436-12] dicitur Ecclesiae: « Obliviscere populum tuum et domum patris tui » etc.; et *in figura* dicitur Abrahae Genesis duodecimo: « Egredere de terra tua et de cognatione tua et de domo patris tui et veni in terram » etc. Et ideo primae Petri secundo: « Obsecro vos tanquam advenas et peregrinos, abstinere vos a carnalibus desideriis » etc.

34. (Vers. 19.). Tertio quantum ad *gratitudinis approbationem* subiungit: *Et ait illi*[^p436-13]*: Surge, vade, quia fides tua te salvum fecit. Surge,* per poenitentiam; ad Ephesios quinto: « Surge, qui dormis, et exsurge a mortuis »; et Actuum duodecimo: « Excitavit eum, dicens: « Surge velociter ». *Vade,* per iustitiam; Matthaei octavo[^p436-14]: « Dico huic: Vade, et vadit », scilicet per obedientiam, quae est nobilissima pars iustitiae, quae potissime fuit in Christo, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo sexto: « Filius quidem hominis vadit, sicut scriptum est de illo », hoc est, ire a Deo ad Deum et secundum Deum et propter Deum; Ioannis decimo tertio[^p436-15]: « Sciens, quia a Deo exivit et ad Deum vadit »; et decimo sexto: « Vado ad eum qui misit me » etc. *Fides tua te
<!-- page 437 -->
*salvum fecit,* per confidentiam; Beda[^p437-1]: « Quae ad agendas gratias inclinavit »; est enim *fides* nostrae salutis *initium,* quae facit, cognosci nomen Domini Iesu Christi, de quo Actuum quarto: « Non est aliud nomen sub caelo datum hominibus, in quo oporteat nos salvos fieri ». *Spes* autem est huius salutis *incrementum,* secundum illud ad Romanos octavo[^p437-2]: « Spe salvi facti sumus »; sed caritas, *complementum,* secundum illud Psalmi: « Tuus sum ego, salvum me fac, quoniam iustificationes tuas exquisivi » etc. — Et nota, quod Christus attribuit *salutem* fidei; quam[^p437-3] ipse virtuose fecerat, ut sic ex hoc miraculo fiat confirmatio praecedentium quoad *pietatem animi* in Christo miserente, *humilitatem obsequii* in liberato procidente, *firmitatem fidei* in leproso curationem promerente; ut sic ex praedicto miraculo praedicta omnia firmiter confirmentur. Unde Glossa[^p437-4]: « In superiori parabola decernitur, fidem per humilitatem debere augeri; in ista rebus ipsis manifeste ostenditur, non fidei tantum agnitam rationem, sed fidei exsecutam operationem esse; quae salvum faciat credentem ».

#### Confirmation through a marvellous deed.

22. *And it came to pass, as Jesus was going*[^p433-9] etc. After he has formed the disciples to the spiritual works of piety by a rational instruction and led them by the hand by a sensible example, here now third he *confirms* them *by a marvellous deed*[^p433-10]; in which the disciples themselves are confirmed *from the marvellous work seen* to *believing,* and are kindled *from what is understood* to *imitating;* and therefore this miracle is to be treated at once *literally* and *spiritually.*

And in the description of this miracle there is first set forth *the cure of an incurable sickness.* Second there is added *the approval of commendable gratitude,* there[^p433-11]: *And one of them, when he saw.*

Now concerning *the cure of the incurable sickness* three things are brought in for the expression and commendation of the miracle and for the mystery of its signification, namely *the congruity of the place, the calamity of the disease* and *the ease of the manner of curing.*

23. (Vers. 11.). First, then, as to *the congruity of the place* he says: *And it came to pass, as Jesus was going to Jerusalem;* by which is signified *the state of rest;* for it is interpreted *vision of peace;* the Psalm[^p433-12]: « Praise the Lord, O Jerusalem, praise » etc.; and after: « Who hath placed peace in thy borders ». — *He passed through the midst of Samaria and Galilee;* by which is designated *the course of the present journey.* For *Samaria* is interpreted *custody,* which ought to be in action as to *the act,* according to that of Ecclesiastes, the fourth chapter[^p433-13]: « Keep thy foot, when thou goest into the house of God »; likewise, as to *the affection;* Deuteronomy, the fourth: « Keep thyself and thy soul carefully »; likewise, as to *the word;* the Psalm[^p433-14]: « I said:
<!-- page 434 -->
I will keep my ways, that I sin not with my tongue ». But *Galilee* is interpreted *transmigration*[^p434-1], which ought to be in contemplation, according to that of Ecclesiastes, the second chapter: « I passed over to contemplate wisdom » etc.; and in the Psalm: « For I shall pass over into the place of the wonderful tabernacle, even to the house of God ». — And note that it is said *to pass through the midst of Samaria,* and *Galilee* is expressed after; because one must first pass through the *active* life, afterwards through the *contemplative;* whence Jacob would not have had Rachel, unless he had first had Lia[^p434-2]. And it is said *to pass through the midst;* for in *action* the mean ought to be held, turning aside from the superfluous and the diminished[^p434-3]; in *prelacy* likewise, turning aside from respect of persons; Exodus, the thirty-second chapter: « Go and return from gate to gate through the midst of the camp » etc.; in *contemplation* likewise the mean is held; in figure of which it is said of the Seraphim in Isaias, the sixth chapter[^p434-4], that « with two wings they covered the face, and with two they covered the feet, and with two in the middle they flew ».

24. (Vers. 12.), Second, as to *the calamity of the disease* he subjoins: *And as he entered into a certain town, there met him ten men that were lepers. According to the letter* leprosy is a disease of miserable calamity, because it takes away the remedy of art, deprives of the solace of society, afflicts with the burden of infirmity[^p434-5]. — And such desolate men ought to have recourse to the Father of mercy; therefore he adds: *Who stood afar off,* recognizing their own uncleanness.

25. (Vers. 13.). *And they lifted up their voice,* expressing their misery; *saying: Jesus, Master*[^tr-praeceptor]*, have mercy on us,* imploring the divine mercy, according to what the Prophet says in the Psalm[^p434-6]: « I am afflicted and humbled exceedingly: I roared with the groaning of my heart ».

26. *Spiritually,* however, by *the ten lepers* is understood *the whole of sinners* sinning against the decalogue; whence the Gloss[^p434-7]: « Those who sin against the decalogue, neither by loving God, of whom they think ill, nor their neighbour, from whom they are divided, cry to God under the number ten ». Now *the whole of sinners* arises from four causes, namely from *fear that wrongly humbles;* and so it is designated by the leprosy of *Mary* in Numbers, the twelfth chapter[^p434-8], where it is said that after she murmured against Moses, « she appeared white as snow with a leprosy ». — Second, from *love that wrongly inflames toward the concupiscence of the flesh;* and this is signified by the leprosy of *Naaman the Syrian,* of whom it is said that « he was a valiant man and rich, but a leper », in the fourth of Kings, the fifth chapter[^p434-9]. — Third, from *love that wrongly inflames toward the concupiscence of the eyes;* which is understood by the leprosy of *Giezi,* in the fourth of Kings, the fifth chapter[^p434-10]. For it was said to him on account of his cupidity and simony: « The leprosy of Naaman shall stick to thee and to thy seed » etc. — Fourth, from *love that wrongly inflames toward the pride of life;* and this is understood by the leprosy of *Ozias,* of whom it is said in the second of Paralipomenon, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p434-11], that when he would burn incense in the house of the Lord and usurp the priestly dignity, he was struck with leprosy. — In figure of these four causes of leprosy it is said in the fourth of Kings, the seventh chapter[^p434-12], that « *four* men that were lepers were at the gate of Samaria », to designate *the fourfold form of the cause;* and here there are *ten* to designate *the whole of the fault.*

27. *Spiritually,* however, by *leprosy* is understood the vice against the Christian religion, which comes to be in four ways; whence the Gloss[^p434-13]: « Whoever, cleansed by the grace of God, has been free of heretical depravity, or Gentile superstition, or Jewish perfidy, or fraternal schism; it is necessary that he return to the Church ». — Now by these four vices aforesaid the law of truth, which consists in the observance of the decalogue, is chiefly assailed; and therefore they are fittingly figured by *four* and by *ten.* But sinners of this kind, although they walk by darksome ways, according to that of Proverbs, the fourth chapter[^p434-14]: « The way of the wicked is darksome, they know not where they fall », yet when the Lord *enters the town,* that is, comes into the flesh, they are enlightened to see the way, according to that of Isaias, the ninth: « To them that dwelt in the region of the shadow of death, light is risen ».. And *they meet him,* through *penance,* which makes the averted face be turned; Jeremias, the thirty-first chapter[^p434-15]: « Convert me, O Lord, and I shall be converted ». « After thou didst convert me, I did penance ». And this penance makes them *stand afar off,* through *the humility of one's own abjection;* below, in the eighteenth chapter[^p434-16]: « The publican, standing afar off » etc.; it makes them *lift up their voice,* through *the bitterness of compunction;* Habacuc, the third chapter: « The deep put forth
<!-- page 435 -->
its voice », that is, the sinner; it makes them also *ask for health,* through *confidence of prayer,* according to that of the Psalm: « I said: O Lord, be thou merciful to me: heal my soul ».

28. (Vers. 14.). Third, as to *the ease of the manner of curing* he subjoins: *Whom when he saw, he said: Go, shew yourselves to the priests.* For to the priests belongs the judgement of leprosy, according to that of Leviticus, the fourteenth chapter[^p435-1]: « The leper, when he is to be cleansed, shall be brought to the priest » etc., through the whole chapter. — *And it came to pass, as they went, they were made clean,* namely by the divine command, for which *to say* is *to do;* Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter[^p435-2]: « His word is full of power »; and « all that he will, he shall do ».

29. *Spiritually,* however, there is understood here the manner of curing from *spiritual leprosy,* namely through the ministry of the priest; and this he intimates in that which he says: *Shew yourselves to the priests;* Leviticus, the fourteenth chapter[^p435-3]: « This is the rite of a leper, when he is to be cleansed: he shall be brought to the priest ». Therefore Ecclesiasticus, the seventh: « With all thy soul fear God, and reverence his priests ». « Honour God with all thy soul, and give honour to the priests, and purify thyself with thy arms ». And this honouring is through the accusation of oneself, according to that of Josue, the seventh chapter[^p435-4]: « Son, confess, and give glory to God ». Therefore Ecclesiasticus, the seventeenth: « Know the justices and judgements of God, and stand in the lot set before thee and of prayer to the most high God; go to the side of the holy age, with the living and with them that give confession to God ». Therefore he says: *Shew* etc.; Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth: « In the time of sickness shew thy conversation ». — He who wills thus to confess already begins to be set right in his will; and therefore he says: *As they went, they were made clean,* according to that of the Psalm[^p435-5]: « I said: I will confess against myself my injustice to the Lord » etc. From this there appears manifestly *the efficacy of confession,* curing every disease and infirmity. There appears also *the priestly dignity,* which he who despises cannot be healed by the Lord. Whence Job, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p435-6]: « Can he that loveth not judgement be healed »? There appears also the marvellous *piety of God*[^tr-pietas], which cures all who meet him, according to that of John, the sixth chapter[^p435-7]: « Every one that cometh to me, I will not cast out ». There appears
also from this how great ought to be *the piety in the ministers of God* toward the weak members of Christ; the first to the Thessalonians, the last chapter[^p435-8]: « We beseech you, brethren, comfort the feeble-minded, support the weak, be patient towards all. See that none … evil » etc. For this reason the divine disposition permitted the head of the Apostles to fall, that in his own fall he might learn how he ought to have mercy on others[^p435-9].

30. (Vers. 15.). *And one of them* etc. After the cure of the incurable disease or sickness there is described here second[^p435-10] *the commendation of approvable gratitude,* concerning which three things are brought in, namely *the giving of thanks, the reproof of ingratitude* and *the approval of gratitude.*

First, then, as to *the giving of thanks* there is set forth: *And one of them, when he saw that he was made clean,* namely through the recognition of the benefit; to the Romans, the eleventh chapter[^p435-11]: « See then towards thee the goodness of God, if thou abide in goodness ». — *He went back, with a loud voice glorifying God,* through the glorification of the benefactor, according to that of Tobias, the twelfth chapter[^p435-12]: « Bless ye the God of heaven, and before all that live give glory to him, because he hath shewn his mercy to you »; and the Psalm: « I will sing to the Lord, who hath given me good things » etc.

31. (Vers. 16.). *And he fell on his face before his feet, giving thanks to him*[^p435-13], namely through the showing of reverence and honour; Ezechiel, the second chapter: « I saw, and I fell upon my face »; and the Apocalypse, the first: « When I had seen him, I fell at his feet as dead ». — *And this was a Samaritan,* for the expression of the figure and signification. For this man, because a Samaritan and a stranger, signifies the Gentiles returning to the worship of Christ, as also that Samaritan woman, John, the fourth chapter[^p435-14]. And he designates it, not only because he is a *Gentile,* but also because he is *one;* in which is understood ecclesiastical unity. Whence the Gloss: « The one returning signifies the humility, devoted to Christ, of the one Church ». In designation of which the Canticle, the sixth chapter[^p435-15]: « One is my dove »; and the first to the Corinthians, the ninth: « All run indeed, but one receiveth the prize ». For this reason one is brought in here as returning, as in that multitude one is healed; John, the
<!-- page 436 -->
fifth chapter[^p436-1]: « The water was moved, and one was healed »; and Christ cured only one there, that, as Augustine says, he might show that no one can be healed except within ecclesiastical unity; of which unity to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter[^p436-2]: « Careful to keep the unity of the spirit in the bond of peace ». « One Lord, one faith, one baptism » etc. But this one is said *to fall on his face,* because the Church has not only *unity* through love, but also *humility* through gratitude and fear[^p436-3]. This the very *falling on the face* signifies; for falling *on the face* signifies *reverence,* according to that of the Apocalypse, the seventh chapter[^p436-4]: « And all the Angels stood round about the throne » etc.; but falling *behind the back* signifies *ruin;* John, the eighteenth chapter: « They went backward, and fell to the ground »; and the first of Kings, the fourth: « Heli fell from his stool backwards ». Whence the Gloss[^p436-5]: « He who falls on his face sees where he falls; he who falls backward does not see. The good, then, fall on their *face,* because they humble themselves in these *visible things,* where they see what follows them, that they may be raised to the invisible. The wicked fall *backward,* because they fall in *invisible things,* where they do not see what follows them ». Now one falls *on his face* in *the revelation of supernal marvels,* as is said in Daniel, the tenth chapter[^p436-6]: « I heard the voice of his words, and hearing I lay in a consternation upon my face, and my face was close to the ground ». In *the asking of divine charisms;* Judith, the sixth chapter[^p436-7]: « All the people fell on their face, adoring God, and with common lamentation and weeping with one accord poured out their prayers to the Lord » etc. In *the receiving of benefits;* Genesis, the seventeenth: « Abraham fell upon his face, and laughed » etc.

32. (Vers. 17.). Second, as to *the reproof of ingratitude* he subjoins: *And Jesus answering, said: Were not ten made clean? and where are the nine?* He does not ask about the nine as one ignorant of the person of each of them, but reproving the irreverence of ingratitude, as the Lord asked in Genesis, the third chapter[^p436-8]: « Adam, where art thou »? Whence the Gloss: « He seeks where the ungrateful are, as if unknown »; so that in them that of the first to the Corinthians, the fourteenth, may be verified: « He that knoweth not shall not be known ».

33. (Vers. 18.). And therefore, to show that he said this reproving[^p436-9], he subjoins: *There is no one found to return and give glory to God, but this stranger.* In which the ingratitude of the Jews is manifestly shown, because, though they were nine, none of them returned, but only this Samaritan, who was single and alone. In which is understood the election of the Gentiles and the reprobation of the Jews, according to that to the Ephesians, the second chapter[^p436-10]: « You, who were heretofore Gentiles in the flesh, who were at that time without Christ, being aliens from the conversation of Israel, and strangers to the testaments, having no hope of the promise, and without God in this world. But now in Christ Jesus, you, who some time were afar off, are made nigh » etc. *A stranger* is said of one who dwells elsewhere and was born elsewhere; so it is with all who, born carnally, pass over to the spirit, according to that to the Romans, the ninth chapter[^p436-11]: « Not they that are the children of the flesh are the children of God, but they that are the children of the promise are accounted for the seed »; because, as is said in John, the third chapter, « that which is born of the flesh is flesh » etc. And therefore in the Psalm[^p436-12] it is said to the Church: « Forget thy people and the house of thy father » etc.; and *in figure* it is said to Abraham in Genesis, the twelfth chapter: « Go forth out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and out of the house of thy father, and come into the land » etc. And therefore the first of Peter, the second: « I beseech you as strangers and pilgrims, to refrain yourselves from carnal desires » etc.

34. (Vers. 19.). Third, as to *the approval of gratitude* he subjoins: *And he said to him*[^p436-13]*: Arise, go; for thy faith hath made thee safe. Arise,* through penance; to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter: « Arise, thou that sleepest, and rise from the dead »; and Acts, the twelfth: « He raised him up, saying: « Arise quickly ». *Go,* through justice; Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p436-14]: « I say to this: Go, and he goeth », namely through obedience, which is the noblest part of justice, which was most of all in Christ, according to that of Matthew, the twenty-sixth: « The Son of man indeed goeth, as it is written of him », that is, to go from God to God and according to God and for God's sake; John, the thirteenth chapter[^p436-15]: « Knowing that he came forth from God, and goeth to God »; and the sixteenth: « I go to him that sent me » etc. *Thy faith hath
<!-- page 437 -->
made thee safe,* through confidence; Bede[^p437-1]: « Which inclined him to give thanks »; for *faith* is *the beginning* of our salvation, which makes the name of the Lord Jesus Christ to be known, of whom Acts, the fourth chapter: « There is no other name under heaven given to men, whereby we must be saved ». But *hope* is *the increase* of this salvation, according to that to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p437-2]: « By hope we are saved »; but charity, *the completion,* according to that of the Psalm: « I am thine, save thou me: for I have sought thy justifications » etc. — And note that Christ attributes *salvation* to faith; which[^p437-3] he himself had worked powerfully, so that thus from this miracle there might come a confirmation of the things preceding as to *piety of mind* in Christ having mercy, *humility of service* in the one freed falling down, *firmness of faith* in the leper meriting the cure; so that thus by the aforesaid miracle all the aforesaid things may be firmly confirmed. Whence the Gloss[^p437-4]: « In the parable above it is decided that faith ought to be increased through humility; in this one it is manifestly shown by the things themselves that not the acknowledged account of faith only, but the executed operation of faith is what makes the believer safe ».
