# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XXI, Vers. 12-19
## *Sed ante haec omnia iniicient vobis etc.*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 527 -->

18. *Sed ante haec omnia iniicient vobis* etc. Postquam persecutionem Ecclesiae descripsit communiter respectu omnium, hic *specialiter describit in persona perfectorum*[^p527-2]. Describitur autem hic duplex persecutio contra Apostolos et apostolicos viros: primo *publica,* deinde *privata,* ibi[^p527-3]: *Trademini autem a parentibus* etc.

Circa *publicam persecutionem virorum sanctorum,* scilicet Apostolorum et suorum imitantium, explicandam introducuntur tria, scilicet persecutionis *pugna,* persecutionis *causa* et tribulatorum *victoria aperta.*

19. (Vers. 12.). Primo igitur quantum ad *persecutionis pugnam* dicit: *Sed ante haec omnia iniicient vobis manus suas. Ante,* scilicet tribulationem finalem, in qua tribulabuntur omnes, praecedit persecutio impiorum exagitata contra viros perfectos. In qua quidem persecutione ex parte impiorum erit *violentia;* ideo dicit: *Iniicient vobis manus;* Actuum quinto[^p527-4]: « Exsurgens princeps sacerdotum et omnes, qui cum illo erant, quae est haeresis Sadducaeorum, repleti sunt zelo et iniecerunt manus in Apostolos et posuerunt eos in custodia publica ». — Erit etiam *iniuria* cum violentia; et ideo addit: *Et persequentur, tradentes in synagogas et custodias;* Actuum sexto[^p527-5]: « Commoverunt plebem et seniores et scribas, et concurrentes rapuerunt Stephanum et adduxerunt in concilium ». — Erit etiam cum hoc *simulata iustitia;* propter quod subdit: *Trahentes ad*[^p527-6] *reges et praesides propter nomen meum,* quasi iniquum sit praedicare Crucifixum; Actuum quinto: « Cum adduxissent Apostolos, statuerunt in concilio. Et interrogavit eos princeps sacerdotum dicens: Praecipiendo praecepimus vobis, ne doceretis in nomine isto; et ecce, replestis Ierusalem doctrina vestra et vultis inducere super nos sanguinem hominis istius »; et Actuum decimo septimo: « Zelantes Iudaei et assumentes de vulgo viros quosdam malos, et turba facta, concitaverunt civitatem et trahebant Iasonem et quosdam fratres ad principes civitatis, clamantes: Quoniam hi sunt, qui orbem concitant et huc venerunt. Et hi omnes contra decreta Caesaris faciunt, regem alium dicentes esse Iesum ». Haec autem persecutio desaeviit contra Apostolos et suos etiam sequaces ab adversariis nominis Christi; secundae ad Timotheum tertio[^p527-7]: « Tu autem assecutus es meam doctrinam, institutionem, propositum, fidem, persecutiones, passiones, qualia facta sunt mihi Antiochiae, Iconii, Lystris ». « Et ex omnibus eripuit me Dominus. Et omnes, qui pie volunt vivere in Christo Iesu, persecutionem patientur ». Unde Augustinus in sermone de Innocentibus[^p527-8]: « In Scripturis sanctis, bonos et iustos viros semper persecutionem malorum sustinuisse, cognoscimus », quia, ut dicit Gregorius, « Abel esse renuit quem Cain malitia non exercet ».

20. (Vers. 13.). Secundo quantum ad *persecutionis causam* subdit: *Continget autem vobis in testimonium, dilectionis divinae;* ad Hebraeos duodecimo[^p527-9]: « Quem diligit Dominus castigat; flagellat autem omnem filium, quem recipit. In disciplina perseverate. Tanquam filiis vobis se offert Deus. Quis enim filius, quem non corripit pater? Quodsi extra disciplinam estis, cuius participes facti sunt omnes; ergo adulteri et non filii estis »; Iudith octavo: « Memores esse debetis, quomodo pater noster Abraham, tentatus et per multas tribulationes probatus, Dei amicus effectus est ». — *Continget* etiam *in testimonium virtutis propriae.* Propter quod Genesis vigesimo septimo[^p527-10]: « Accede huc, ut tangam te, fili mi, et probem, utrum tu sis filius meus, an non ». Unde et Iob secundo dixit satan ad Dominum: « Mitte manum tuam et tange os eius et carnem, et tunc videbis, quod in faciem benedicat tibi. Dixitque Dominus ad satan: Ecce, in manu tua est. Egressus igitur satan a facie Domini, percussit Iob ulcere pessimo »; et ad Romanos quinto: « Tribulatio patientiam operatur, patientia autem probationem, probatio vero spem ». — *Continget* etiam *in testimonium damnationis alienae.* Unde Glossa[^p527-11]: « Mors iustorum bonis est in adiutorium, malis in testimonium, ut inde perversi sine excusatione pereant, unde electi exemplum capiunt, ut vivant ». Unde Sapientiae quarto[^p527-12]: « Condemnat autem iustus mortuus vivos impios, et iuventus celerius consummata longam vitam iniusti »; et ideo primae Petri quarto:
<!-- page 528 -->
« Tempus est, ut incipiat iudicium a domo Dei. Si autem primum a nobis, quis finis illorum qui non credunt Dei Evangelio »? — *Continget* etiam *in testimonium veritatis praedicatae;* nam *martyr* testis dicitur, et martyrium testimonium est veritatis; Apocalypsis sexto[^p528-1]: « Vidi sub altari animas interfectorum propter verbum Dei et propter testimonium, quod habebant ». Et haec Martyrum sanctorum testimonia credibilem faciunt fidem christianam; Psalmus[^p528-2]: « Testimonia tua credibilia facta sunt nimis », propter mortem praedicantium. Unde et Actuum decimo: « Praecepit nobis praedicare populo et testificari, quia ipse est, qui constitutus est a Deo iudex vivorum et mortuorum ».

21. (Vers. 14.). Tertio quoad *tribulatorum victoriam* subiungit: *Ponite ergo in cordibus vestris non praemeditari, quemadmodum respondeatis,* per investigationem propriam, quia, secundae ad Corinthios tertio[^p528-3], « non quod simus sufficientes cogitare aliquid a nobis quasi ex nobis, sed sufficientia nostra ex Deo est »; Psalmus: « Dominus scit cogitationes hominum, quoniam vanae sunt ».

22. (Vers. 15.). *Ego enim dabo vobis os et sapientiam,* per inspirationem internam; Matthaei decimo[^p528-4]: « Non enim vos estis, qui loquimini, sed Spiritus Patris vestri, qui loquitur in vobis ». Hic autem Spiritus Domini dat *sapientiam* et dat *eloquentiam: sapientiam in corde,* secundum illud Ioannis decimo sexto[^p528-5]: « Cum venerit ille Spiritus veritatis, docebit vos omnem veritatem »; ideo Iob trigesimo secundo: « Spiritus est in hominibus, et inspiratio Omnipotentis dat intelligentiam ». Et ratio huius redditur primae ad Corinthios secundo[^p528-6]: quia « Spiritus omnia scrutatur, etiam profunda Dei. Quis enim hominum scit, quae sunt in homine, nisi spiritus hominis, qui est in ipso? Ita et quae Dei sunt, nemo cognovit nisi Spiritus Dei ». Et ideo Sapientiae nono: « Sensum tuum quis sciet, nisi tu dederis sapientiam et miseris Spiritum sanctum tuum de altissimis »? — Dat similiter *eloquentiam in sermone* per sapientiam; unde Sapientiae decimo[^p528-7]: « Sapientia aperuit os mutorum et linguas infantium fecit disertas »; ideo Exodi quarto: « Quis fecit os hominis? Aut quis fabricatus est mutum et surdum, videntem et caecum? Perge igitur, et ego ero in ore tuo et docebo te, quid loquaris ». Et ideo Isaiae sexagesimo tertio: « Ego, qui loquor iustitiam et propugnator sum ad salvandum ». — Et quia[^p528-8] « sapientiam non vincit malitia », ideo subdit: *Cui non poterunt resistere et contradicere omnes adversarii vestri,* per cavillationem fraudulentam; Exodi decimo quarto[^p528-9]: « Dominus pugnabit pro vobis, et vos tacebitis ». Huic forti bellatori nullus resistere potest; Psalmus: « Tu terribilis es, et quis resistet tibi »? et Iob nono: « Quis restitit ei et pacem habuit »? Nullus ergo resistit, qui non vincatur a discipulis propter veritatis annuntiationem; quia dicitur tertii Esdrae quarto[^p528-10]: « Et veritas manet et invalescit in aeternum et vivit in saecula saeculorum »; et Apocalypsis duodecimo de antichristo dicitur: « Ipsi vicerunt eum propter sanguinem Agni et propter verbum testimonii sui, et quia non dilexerunt animas suas usque ad mortem ».

23. Sed contrarium dici videtur Apocalypsis undecimo[^p528-11]: « Dabo duobus testibus meis, et prophetabunt diebus mille ducentis sexaginta, amicti saccis »; et post: « Cum finierint testimonium suum, bestia, quae ascendit de abysso, faciet adversum eos bellum et vincet eos ». — Sed hoc non est contrarium, quia, licet veritas ad tempus prosternatur, oportet tamen, quod resurgat. Unde ibidem[^p528-12]: « Post dies tres et dimidium spiritus vitae a Deo intravit in eos. Et steterunt super pedes suos, et timor magnus cecidit super eos qui viderunt eos ». « Et in illa hora factus est terraemotus magnus, et decima pars civitatis cecidit ».

24. (Vers. 16. 17.). *Trademini autem a parentibus* etc. Postquam descripsit perfectorum persecutionem publicam, hic describit *privatam*[^p528-13]; circa quam introducuntur tria, scilicet *domestica pugna, superna tutela* et *interna armatura.*

Primo ergo quantum ad *domesticam pugnam* dicit: *Trademini autem a parentibus et fratribus et cognatis et amicis;* et hoc quidem durissimum erit. *Durum* quidem est *tradi ab amico et cognato,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p528-14]: « Si inimicus meus maledixisset mihi, sustinuissem utique. Tu vero homo unanimis, dux meus et notus meus, qui simul mecum dulces capiebas cibos » etc. *Durius* est *tradi a fratre proprio;* Michaeae septimo[^p528-15]: « Omnes in sanguine insidiantur, vir fratrem suum ad mortem venatur. Malum manuum suarum dicunt bonum ». Sed *gravissimum* est *pati a genitore suo;* Zachariae decimo tertio[^p528-16]: « Configent eum pater eius et
<!-- page 529 -->
mater eius, genitores eius, cum prophetaverit ». Et haec quidem tribulatio valde est dura; unde Glossa[^p529-1]: « Maius tormentum est, cum aliquis ab illis patitur, de quibus praesumebat, quia cum damno corporis mala cruciant amissae caritatis ». Et ideo Ieremiae nono[^p529-2]: « Unusquisque a proximo se custodiat et in omni fratre suo non habeat fiduciam, quia omnis frater supplantans supplantabit, et omnis amicus fraudulenter incedet. Et vir fratrem suum deridebit, et veritatem non loquentur ». — Et quia hoc odium erit pessimum, ideo subdit: *Et morte afficient ex vobis, et eritis odio omnibus hominibus*[^p529-3] *propter nomen meum;* Ieremiae decimo quinto: « Vae mihi! mater mea; quare genuisti me virum rixae, virum discordiae in universa terra? Omnes maledicunt mihi ».

25. Ratio autem odii huius est duplex: prima est *discordia voluntatum.* Ideo Ioannis decimo quinto[^p529-4]: « Si de mundo fuissetis, mundus quod suum erat diligeret; quia vero non estis de mundo, propterea odit vos mundus ». Nam, sicut dicitur Iacobi quarto, « amicitia huius mundi inimica est Deo » etc. — Alia ratio est *perfidia animorum,* secundum illud Ioannis decimo sexto[^p529-5]: « Venit hora, in qua omnis, qui interficit vos, putabit, se obsequium praestare Deo. Et haec facient vobis, quia non noverunt Patrem neque me ». In hoc mira notatur perfidia animorum, quod ex nulla alia causa persequentur viros iustos, nisi quia Christi nomen credunt, amant, praedicant et sequuntur; et ideo dicit: *propter nomen meum.* Et haec[^p529-6] fuit causa, propter quam fratres Ioseph oderunt eum; unde dicit Chrysostomus: « O nefanda rabies iniquorum! Non licet Ioseph, non licet, inquam, impunitum esse qui bonus est, non licet, meliorem tutum esse cum malis! Et quasi invidi aliquid perdant, aut dispendium alicuius rei incurrant; sic bonorum vita detrimentum est pessimorum. Zelant modestum superbi, mansuetum invidi, probum improbi, mitem tumidi inimica aemulatione lacessunt. Bonum mali videre non possunt. Nolunt, meliorem se vivere, ne degeneres ipsi possent improperia sustinere. Cur innocentem zelas, inimica fraternitas? Cur invides bono? Cur pati simplicem non potes? Cur modestum persequeris? Cur probum non diligis? Cur rea innocentis sanguinis inveniris? Saltem distractus aliquatenus vivat, ne in te accusator innocens sanguis, ut in Cain[^p529-7], aliquando exsistat. Serva tibi, serva, inimica fraternitas, vivum quem sentias aliquando placatum; serva incolumem quem necessitatis tempore invenias provisorem[^p529-8] ».

26. (Vers. 18.). Secundo quantum ad *supernam tutelam* subdit: *Et capillus de capite vestro non peribit;* quod tripliciter exponitur: primum quidem, quia divina providentia curam de omnibus agit et nihil perire sinit; supra duodecimo[^p529-9]: « Nonne quinque passeres veneunt dipondio, et unus ex illis non est in oblivione coram Deo? Sed et capilli capitis vestri omnes numerati sunt. Nolite ergo timere, multis passeribus pluris estis vos ». — Secundo, quia divina potentia nullam sinet perire particulam; unde Augustinus in Enchiridio[^p529-10]: « Non perit Deo terrena materies, de qua mortalium creaturarum caro; sed in quemlibet pulverem cineremve solvatur, in quoslibet halitus aurasque diffugiat, in quamcumque aliorum corporum substantiam, vel in ipsa elementa vertatur, in quorumcumque animalium, etiam hominum cibum cedat carnemque mutetur, illi animae in puncto temporis redit, quae ipsam primitus, ut homo fieret, cresceret, viveret, animavit ». — Tertio, quia in omnibus his tribulationibus non incurrunt laesionem vel modicam; in cuius figura Danielis tertio[^p529-11]: « Sidrach, Misach et Abdenago de medio ignis egressi sunt. Et satrapae contemplabantur viros illos, quoniam nihil potestatis habuisset ignis in corporibus eorum, et capillus capitis eorum non esset adustus, et odor ignis non transisset per eos ». Et ideo dicitur primae Petri tertio: « Et quis est, qui vobis noceat, si boni aemulatores fueritis »? Et in hoc mira apparet Dei super iustos protectio; unde Chrysostomus[^p529-12]: « Mira Christi virtus et fortitudo Apostolorum. A gentibus habebantur odio ut Iudaei, a Caesare eiiciebantur ut seditiosi, a Iudaeis lapidabantur ut Legis inimici. Contra Apostolos omnes proeliabantur: princeps, dux, idiota et plebs. Infinita millia
<!-- page 530 -->
Iudaeorum tunc ceperunt Romani et duodecim viros inermes non superaverunt ».

27. (Vers. 19.). Tertio quantum ad *internam armaturam* subiungit: *In patientia vestra possidebitis animas vestras,* id est, defendetis tanquam per armaturam perfectam; Proverbiorum decimo quinto[^p530-1]: « Qui acquiescit increpationibus possessor est cordis »; hoc autem fit per patientiam. Nam illius rei dicitur quis *possessor,* cuius habet plenum dominium; et patientia facit dominari sibi, secundum illud Proverbiorum decimo sexto[^p530-2]: « Melior est patiens viro forti, et qui dominatur animo suo expugnatore urbium ». Et ideo Gregorius: « Patientia radix est et custos virtutum, et in hac animas, quae corpus possident, possidemus ». — Et hoc manifeste apparet, si quis attendat, quid sit patientia. Nam patientia rectificat *rationalem,* temperat *irascibilem,* modificat *concupiscibilem* et *exteriorem* consummat *operationem. Rationalem,* inquam, illuminat et rectificat; unde Proverbiorum decimo quarto[^p530-3]: « Qui patiens est multa gubernatur sapientia »; et Proverbiorum decimo nono: « Doctrina viri per patientiam dignoscitur ». Temperat *irascibilem* et *concupiscibilem;* propter quod Gregorius[^p530-4]: « Patientia vera est, cum quis amat quem portat. Nam tolerare et odisse non est virtus, sed velamen furoris ». Consummat etiam *exteriorem operationem,* secundum illud Iacobi primo[^p530-5]: « Patientia perfectum opus habet, ut sitis perfecti et integri » etc.; et ad Hebraeos decimo: « Patientia vobis necessaria est, ut, voluntatem Dei facientes, reportetis promissionem ». Haec autem patientia facit *reportari praemium,* dum conservat et multiplicat merita passionum. Unde Hieronymus[^p530-6]: « Exercetur patientia, cum a proximo sustinemus persecutiones, damna, contumelias, ab adversario tentamenta, a Domino flagella. Sine ferro Martyres esse possumus, si patientiam in animo servamus ».

## English
<!-- page 527 -->

18. *But before all these things they will lay upon you* etc. After he has described the persecution of the Church commonly in respect of all, here *he describes it specially in the person of the perfect*[^p527-2]. Now here a twofold persecution is described against the Apostles and apostolic men: first *public,* then *private,* there[^p527-3]: *And you shall be betrayed by your parents* etc.

In explaining *the public persecution of holy men,* namely of the Apostles and of their imitators, three things are brought in, namely, the *fight* of the persecution, the *cause* of the persecution, and the *open victory* of the afflicted.

19. (Vers. 12.). First, then, as to *the fight of the persecution* he says: *But before all these things they will lay their hands upon you. Before,* namely the final tribulation, in which all will be afflicted, there goes before the persecution of the impious, stirred up against perfect men. In which persecution indeed there will be on the part of the impious *violence;* therefore he says: *They will lay their hands upon you;* the Acts, the fifth chapter[^p527-4]: « Then the high priest rising up, and all they that were with him, which is the heresy of the Sadducees, were filled with envy, and laid hands on the Apostles, and put them in the common prison ». — There will be also *injury* with the violence; and therefore he adds: *And persecute you, delivering you up to the synagogues and into prisons;* the Acts, the sixth chapter[^p527-5]: « And they stirred up the people, and the ancients, and the scribes; and running together, they took Stephen, and brought him to the council ». — There will be also with this *a feigned justice;* on account of which he subjoins: *Dragging to*[^p527-6] *kings and governors for my name's sake,* as though it were wicked to preach the Crucified; the Acts, the fifth chapter: « And when they had brought the Apostles, they set them before the council. And the high priest asked them, saying: Commanding we commanded you, that you should not teach in this name; and behold, you have filled Jerusalem with your doctrine, and you have a mind to bring the blood of this man upon us »; and the Acts, the seventeenth chapter: « The Jews, moved with envy, and taking unto them some wicked men of the vulgar sort, and making a tumult, set the city in an uproar, and drew Jason and certain brethren to the rulers of the city, crying: These are they that set the world in an uproar, and are come hither. And these all do contrary to the decrees of Caesar, saying that there is another king, Jesus ». Now this persecution raged against the Apostles and also their followers from the adversaries of the name of Christ; the second to Timothy, the third chapter[^p527-7]: « But thou hast fully known my doctrine, manner of life, purpose, faith, persecutions, afflictions, such as came upon me at Antioch, at Iconium, and at Lystra ». « And out of them all the Lord delivered me. And all that will live piously in Christ Jesus shall suffer persecution ». Whence Augustine in the sermon on the Innocents[^p527-8]: « In the holy Scriptures we know that good and just men have always sustained the persecution of the wicked », because, as Gregory says, « he refuses to be Abel whom the malice of Cain does not exercise ».

20. (Vers. 13.). Second, as to *the cause of the persecution* he subjoins: *And it shall happen unto you for a testimony, of the divine love;* to the Hebrews, the twelfth chapter[^p527-9]: « For whom the Lord loveth, he chastiseth; and he scourgeth every son whom he receiveth. Persevere under discipline. God dealeth with you as with his sons; for what son is there, whom the father doth not correct? But if you be without discipline, whereof all are made partakers, then are you bastards, and not sons »; Judith, the eighth chapter: « You must remember how our father Abraham was tempted, and being proved by many tribulations, was made the friend of God ». — *It shall happen* also *for a testimony of one's own virtue.* On account of which Genesis, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p527-10]: « Come hither, that I may feel thee, my son, and may prove whether thou be my son, or no ». Whence also in Job, the second chapter, satan said to the Lord: « Put forth thy hand, and touch his bone and his flesh, and then thou shalt see that he will bless thee to thy face. And the Lord said to satan: Behold, he is in thy hand. So satan went forth from the presence of the Lord, and struck Job with a very grievous ulcer »; and to the Romans, the fifth chapter: « Tribulation worketh patience, and patience trial, and trial hope ». — *It shall happen* also *for a testimony of another's damnation.* Whence the Gloss[^p527-11]: « The death of the just is unto the good for a help, unto the wicked for a testimony, that the perverse may perish without excuse from that whence the elect take an example that they may live ». Whence Wisdom, the fourth chapter[^p527-12]: « But the just that is dead condemneth the wicked that are living, and youth soon ended the long life of the unjust »; and therefore the first of Peter, the fourth chapter:
<!-- page 528 -->
« The time is that judgement should begin at the house of God. And if first at us, what shall be the end of them that believe not the Gospel of God »? — *It shall happen* also *for a testimony of the truth preached;* for a *martyr* is called a witness, and martyrdom is a testimony of the truth; the Apocalypse, the sixth chapter[^p528-1]: « I saw under the altar the souls of them that were slain for the word of God, and for the testimony which they held ». And these testimonies of the holy Martyrs make the christian faith credible; the Psalm[^p528-2]: « Thy testimonies are become exceedingly credible », on account of the death of those preaching. Whence also the Acts, the tenth chapter: « He commanded us to preach to the people, and to testify that it is he who was appointed by God to be judge of the living and of the dead ».

21. (Vers. 14.). Third, as to *the victory of the afflicted* he subjoins: *Lay it up therefore in your hearts not to meditate before how you shall answer,* by one's own investigation, because, as the second to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p528-3], « not that we are sufficient to think any thing of ourselves, as of ourselves, but our sufficiency is from God »; the Psalm: « The Lord knoweth the thoughts of men, that they are vain ».

22. (Vers. 15.). *For I will give you a mouth and wisdom,* by inward inspiration; Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p528-4]: « For it is not you that speak, but the Spirit of your Father that speaketh in you ». Now this Spirit of the Lord gives *wisdom* and gives *eloquence: wisdom in the heart,* according to that of John, the sixteenth chapter[^p528-5]: « When that Spirit of truth is come, he will teach you all truth »; therefore Job, the thirty-second chapter: « There is a spirit in men, and the inspiration of the Almighty giveth understanding ». And the reason of this is given in the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p528-6]: because « the Spirit searcheth all things, yea, the deep things of God. For what man knoweth the things of a man, but the spirit of a man that is in him? So the things also that are of God no man knoweth, but the Spirit of God ». And therefore Wisdom, the ninth chapter: « And who shall know thy thought, except thou give wisdom, and send thy Holy Spirit from above »? — He gives likewise *eloquence in speech* through wisdom; whence Wisdom, the tenth chapter[^p528-7]: « Wisdom opened the mouth of the dumb, and made the tongues of infants eloquent »; therefore Exodus, the fourth chapter: « Who made the mouth of man? Or who made the dumb and the deaf, the seeing and the blind? Go therefore, and I will be in thy mouth, and I will teach thee what thou shalt speak ». And therefore Isaias, the sixty-third chapter: « I, that speak justice, and am a defender to save ». — And because[^p528-8] « no evil can overcome wisdom », therefore he subjoins: *Which all your adversaries shall not be able to resist and gainsay,* by fraudulent cavilling; Exodus, the fourteenth chapter[^p528-9]: « The Lord will fight for you, and you shall hold your peace ». To this strong warrior no one can make resistance; the Psalm: « Thou art terrible, and who shall resist thee »? and Job, the ninth chapter: « Who hath resisted him, and hath had peace »? No one therefore resists who is not overcome by the disciples on account of the announcing of the truth; because it is said in the third of Esdras, the fourth chapter[^p528-10]: « And truth remaineth and groweth strong for ever, and liveth for ever and ever »; and in the Apocalypse, the twelfth chapter, it is said of antichrist: « They overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony, and they loved not their lives unto death ».

23. But the contrary seems to be said in the Apocalypse, the eleventh chapter[^p528-11]: « I will give unto my two witnesses, and they shall prophesy a thousand two hundred sixty days, clothed in sackcloth »; and after: « When they shall have finished their testimony, the beast that ascendeth out of the abyss shall make war against them, and shall overcome them ». — But this is not contrary, because, although the truth be laid low for a time, yet it must rise again. Whence in the same place[^p528-12]: « After three days and a half, the spirit of life from God entered into them. And they stood upon their feet, and great fear fell upon them that saw them ». « And at that hour there was made a great earthquake, and the tenth part of the city fell ».

24. (Vers. 16. 17.). *And you shall be betrayed by your parents* etc. After he has described the public persecution of the perfect, here he describes *the private*[^p528-13]; concerning which three things are brought in, namely *the domestic fight, the protection from on high* and *the inward armour.*

First, then, as to *the domestic fight* he says: *And you shall be betrayed by your parents and brethren and kinsmen and friends;* and this indeed will be the hardest. It is *hard* indeed *to be betrayed by a friend and kinsman,* according to that of the Psalm[^p528-14]: « If my enemy had reviled me, I would verily have borne with it. But thou a man of one mind, my guide, and my familiar, who didst take sweetmeats together with me » etc. It is *harder to be betrayed by one's own brother;* Micheas, the seventh chapter[^p528-15]: « They all lie in wait for blood, every man hunteth his brother to death. The evil of their hands they call good ». But it is *the gravest to suffer from one's own parent;* Zacharias, the thirteenth chapter[^p528-16]: « His father and
<!-- page 529 -->
his mother, his parents, shall thrust him through, when he shall prophesy ». And this tribulation indeed is very hard; whence the Gloss[^p529-1]: « It is a greater torment when one suffers from those of whom he presumed, because with the loss of the body the evils of lost charity torture ». And therefore Jeremias, the ninth chapter[^p529-2]: « Let every man take heed of his neighbour, and let him not trust in any brother of his: for every brother will utterly supplant, and every friend will walk deceitfully. And a man shall mock his brother, and they will not speak the truth ». — And because this hatred will be the worst, therefore he subjoins: *And some of you they will put to death, and you shall be hated by all men*[^p529-3] *for my name's sake;* Jeremias, the fifteenth chapter: « Woe is me, my mother: why hast thou borne me a man of strife, a man of discord in all the earth? All curse me ».

25. Now the reason of this hatred is twofold: the first is *the discord of wills.* Therefore John, the fifteenth chapter[^p529-4]: « If you had been of the world, the world would love its own; but because you are not of the world, therefore the world hateth you ». For, as is said in James, the fourth chapter, « the friendship of this world is the enemy of God » etc. — The other reason is *the perfidy of minds,* according to that of John, the sixteenth chapter[^p529-5]: « The hour cometh, that whosoever killeth you will think that he doth a service to God. And these things will they do to you, because they have not known the Father nor me ». In this is noted a marvellous perfidy of minds, that for no other cause will they persecute just men, except because they believe, love, preach and follow the name of Christ; and therefore he says: *for my name's sake.* And this[^p529-6] was the cause on account of which the brethren of Joseph hated him; whence Chrysostom says: « O wicked rage of the unjust! It is not permitted to Joseph, it is not permitted, I say, for him who is good to be unpunished; it is not permitted for the better man to be safe among the wicked! And as though the envious lost something, or incurred the loss of some thing; so the life of the good is a detriment to the worst. The proud are jealous of the modest, the envious of the meek, the wicked of the upright; the swollen harass the gentle with hostile emulation. The evil cannot bear to see the good. They do not wish the better man to live, lest they, being degenerate, should have to sustain reproaches. Why art thou jealous of the innocent, hostile brotherhood? Why dost thou envy the good? Why canst thou not bear the
simple man to suffer? Why dost thou persecute the modest? Why dost thou not love the upright? Why art thou found guilty of innocent blood? At least let him, though torn away, live in some measure, lest the innocent blood, as in Cain[^p529-7], should one day stand forth in thee as accuser. Keep for thyself, keep, hostile brotherhood, one living whom thou mayest one day feel appeased; keep safe one whom in the time of necessity thou mayest find a provider[^p529-8] ».

26. (Vers. 18.). Second, as to *the protection from on high* he subjoins: *And a hair of your head shall not perish;* which is expounded in three ways: first indeed, because the divine providence takes care of all things and suffers nothing to perish; above, the twelfth chapter[^p529-9]: « Are not five sparrows sold for two farthings, and not one of them is forgotten before God? Yea, the very hairs of your head are all numbered. Fear not therefore: you are of more value than many sparrows ». — Second, because the divine power will suffer no particle to perish; whence Augustine in the Enchiridion[^p529-10]: « The earthly matter of which the flesh of mortal creatures is made does not perish to God; but into whatever dust or ashes it be dissolved, into whatever breaths and airs it flee away, into whatever substance of other bodies, or into the very elements it be turned, into the food of whatever animals, even of men, it pass and be changed into flesh, it returns in a moment of time to that soul which at the first animated it, that it might become a man, grow, live ». — Third, because in all these tribulations they do not incur even a slight hurt; in figure of which Daniel, the third chapter[^p529-11]: « Sidrach, Misach, and Abdenago went out from the midst of the fire. And the satraps considered these men, that the fire had had no power on their bodies, and that the hair of their head had not been singed, and the smell of the fire had not passed on them ». And therefore it is said in the first of Peter, the third chapter: « And who is he that can hurt you, if you be zealous of good »? And in this the protection of God over the just appears marvellous; whence Chrysostom[^p529-12]: « Marvellous the power of Christ and the fortitude of the Apostles. By the Gentiles they were held in hatred as Jews, by Caesar they were cast out as seditious, by the Jews they were stoned as enemies of the Law. All fought against the Apostles: prince, leader, the unlearned and the common people. Countless thousands
<!-- page 530 -->
of the Jews the Romans then took, and twelve unarmed men they did not overcome ».

27. (Vers. 19.). Third, as to *the inward armour* he subjoins: *In your patience you shall possess your souls,* that is, you shall defend them as by perfect armour; Proverbs, the fifteenth chapter[^p530-1]: « He that yieldeth to rebukes is the possessor of the heart »; and this is done through patience. For one is called the *possessor* of that thing of which he has full dominion; and patience makes one to have dominion over oneself, according to that of Proverbs, the sixteenth chapter[^p530-2]: « The patient man is better than the valiant, and he that ruleth his spirit than he that taketh cities ». And therefore Gregory: « Patience is the root and guardian of the virtues, and in it we possess our souls, which possess the body ». — And this appears manifestly, if one attends to what patience is. For patience rectifies *the rational,* tempers *the irascible,* moderates *the concupiscible,* and consummates *the exterior operation. The rational,* I say, it enlightens and rectifies; whence Proverbs, the fourteenth chapter[^p530-3]: « He that is patient is governed with much wisdom »; and Proverbs, the nineteenth chapter: « The learning of a man is known by patience ». It tempers *the irascible* and *the concupiscible;* on account of which Gregory[^p530-4]: « Patience is true when one loves him whom he bears. For to tolerate and to hate is not virtue, but a veil of fury ». It consummates also *the exterior operation,* according to that of James, the first chapter[^p530-5]: « Patience hath a perfect work, that you may be perfect and entire » etc.; and to the Hebrews, the tenth chapter: « Patience is necessary for you, that, doing the will of God, you may receive the promise ». Now this patience makes *the reward to be received,* while it preserves and multiplies the merits of sufferings. Whence Jerome[^p530-6]: « Patience is exercised when we sustain from our neighbour persecutions, losses, insults; from the adversary temptations; from the Lord scourges. Without the sword we can be Martyrs, if we keep patience in the soul ».
