# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XXI, Vers. 25-27
## *Et erunt signa in sole etc.*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 533 -->

41. (Vers. 25.). *Et erunt signa in sole* etc. Postquam praedixit, quae ventura erant generaliter, circa persecutionem, et circa destructionem Ierusalem, hic quarto[^p533-5] praedicit, quae *ventura sunt circa iudicium finale.* In hac autem eruditione primo discipulos *erudit ad providentiam.* Secundo *erigit ad confidentiam,* ibi[^p533-6]: *His autem fieri incipientibus* etc. Tertio vero *excitat ad vigilantiam,* ibi: *Attendite autem vobis* etc.

Circa *iudicium finale* universalem describit *commotionem* ex parte *naturae caelestis, elementaris, rationalis, intellectualis* et ipsius *iudicis apparentis.*

Primo ergo quantum ad *naturam caelestem* dicit: *Et erunt signa in sole et luna et stellis. Solem* et *lunam* specialiter nominat tanquam duo praecipua luminaria et secundum apparentiam et secundum efficaciam; Genesis primo[^p533-7]: « Fecit Deus duo magna luminaria: luminare maius, ut praeesset diei, et luminare minus, ut praeesset nocti, et stellas ». In his ergo luminaribus apparebunt signa obscurationis, secundum illud Ioelis secundo: « Sol convertetur in tenebras et luna in sanguinem, antequam veniat dies Domini magnus et horribilis ». *Horribilis* autem erit propter illorum luminum obscurationem, secundum illud Psalmi[^p533-8]: « Fecit lunam in tempore, sol cognovit occasum suum. Posuisti tenebras » etc.; et de hac Sapientiae decimo septimo: « Nec siderum limpidissimae flammae illuminare poterant illam noctem horrendam ». Unde haec luminaria, quae in principio temporis posita sunt in firmamento in signa per sui lucem, secundum illud Genesis primo[^p533-9]: « Fiant luminaria in firmamento caeli et sint in signa et tempora » etc.; in fine temporum in signa sunt finis per lucis occultationem. Unde Apocalypsis sexto: « Sol factus est niger tanquam saccus cilicinus, et luna tota facta est sicut sanguis, et stellae ceciderunt de caelo super terram »; et Matthaei vigesimo quarto: « Statim autem post tribulationem dierum illorum sol obscurabitur, et luna non dabit lumen suum, et stellae cadent de caelo ». — Hic autem beatus Lucas non exprimit, quod stellae *cadent,* sed quod in eis erunt *signa;* quia revera corpora stellarum caelestium non mutabunt situm secundum substantiam, sed secundum apparentiam. Virtute stellarum, imminente iudicio, impressiones plurimae generabuntur, quas Philosophus[^p533-10] vocat *assub,* quae ita multiplicabuntur, ut cadere videantur stellae caelorum.

42. Et attendendum est, quod sicut luminaria sunt signa naturalium secundum operationes naturales, sic sunt in signa eorum quae supra naturam eveniunt, per aliqua in eis super naturam facta. Nam in designationem *mirabilis virtutis Christi* sol stetit, secundum illud Iosue decimo[^p533-11]: « Sol contra Gabaon ne movearis, et luna contra vallem Aialon ». « Stetitque sol in medio caeli et non festinavit occumbere spatio unius diei ». In designationem autem *mirabilis dignationis* sol decem gradibus retrocessit; Isaiae trigesimo octavo[^p533-12]: « Reversus est
<!-- page 534 -->
sol per decem lineas, per gradus, per quos descenderat », quia homo post novem ordines Angelorum decimum tenet locum. In designationem vero *admirabilis passionis* tam in *capite* quam in *membris* sol obscuratus est in passione *capitis;* infra vigesimo tertio[^p534-1]: « Tenebrae factae sunt in universam terram, et obscuratus est sol »; similiter in passione *corporis;* Apocalypsis octavo: « Percussa est tertia pars solis et tertia pars lunae et tertia pars stellarum »; quod refertur ad corpus, quia, Apocalypsis duodecimo, « draco traxit tertiam partem stellarum ».

43. Secundo quantum ad *naturam elementarem* subdit: *Et in terris pressura gentium prae confusione*[^p534-2] *sonitus maris et fluctuum.* Beda: « Appropinquante extremo iudicio, sonitus maris et fluctuum confunditur, quia, appropinquante termino suo, elementa quasi paventia nutant et tremunt »; Sapientiae quinto[^p534-3]: « Accipiet armaturam zelus illius, et armabit creaturam ad ultionem inimicorum ». « Ibunt directe emissiones fulgurum, et tanquam a bene curvato arcu nubium exterminabuntur et ad certum locum insilient. Et a petrosa ira plenae mittentur grandines, et excandescet in illos aqua maris ». Unde tunc verificabitur illud Exodi decimo quinto[^p534-4]: « In spiritu furoris tui congregatae sunt aquae ». Ipse enim Dominus, qui conturbat mare; Isaiae quinquagesimo primo: « Ego sum Dominus Deus tuus, qui conturbo mare, et intumescunt fluctus eius ». Et hoc quidem iusto iudicio suo; quia, sicut dicitur Isaiae quinquagesimo septimo, « impii quasi mare fervens, quod quiescere non potest, et redundant fluctus eius in conculcationem ». Et tunc erit confusio omnium elementorum, iuxta quod in Aegypto factum est, secundum illud Exodi nono[^p534-5]: « Ignis et grando mixta pariter ferebantur ». Et in hac confusione erit sonitus terribilis; tunc enim verificabitur illud Psalmi: « Vox Domini super aquas, Deus maiestatis intonuit, Dominus super aquas multas ». Hic enim sonitus designat sonitum terribilis vocis ipsius iudicis; unde Apocalypsis primo[^p534-6]: « Oculi eius tanquam flamma ignis, et pedes eius similes aurichalco sicut in camino ardenti, et vox eius sicut vox aquarum multarum »; Ezechielis primo: « Audiebam sonum alarum quasi sonum aquarum multarum et quasi sonum sublimis Dei; cum ambularent, quasi sonus erat multitudinis, ut sonus castrorum ».

44. (Vers. 26.). Tertio quantum ad *naturam rationalem* subdit: *Arescentibus hominibus prae timore et exspectatione, quae supervenient universo orbi.* Tunc enim implebitur illud Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p534-7]: « Media nocte clamor factus est: Ecce, sponsus venit » etc.; unde Glossa: « Hoc de ipso adventu intelligitur, cum omnes virgines prudentes et fatuae, insolito clamore suscitatae, lampades ornant, id est, secum opera numerant, pro quibus maximo cum timore iam instantem aeterni discriminis exspectant adventum ». Dicuntur autem *homines arescere,* qui vixerunt secundum carnem; nam Isaiae quadragesimo[^p534-8]: « Omnis caro foenum ». « Exsiccatum est foenum » etc.; unde et Iacobi primo: « Exortus est sol cum ardore et arefecit foenum », *sol* iustitiae veniens ad iudicium. Unde Beda[^p534-9]: « In exspectatione districti examinis qui in hoc mundo floruerunt exarescent », secundum illud Exodi decimo quinto: « Irruat super eos formido et pavor in magnitudine brachii tui ». Tunc in illis, in quibus securitas fuit et gaudium, non erit nisi timor et luctus; quia, primae ad Thessalonicenses quinto[^p534-10], « cum dixerint: Pax et securitas, tunc repentinus eis superveniet interitus ». Unde Chrysostomus[^p534-11]: « Nec erit resistendi virtus nec poenitentiae locus nec satisfactionis facultas; ex angustia omnium rerum nihil remanet nisi luctus ». Unde Matthaei vigesimo quarto: « Apparebit signum Filii hominis in caelo, et tunc plangent omnes tribus terrae ».

45. Quarto quantum ad *naturam intellectualem* subdit: *Nam virtutes caelorum movebuntur; virtutes* autem *caelorum* sunt Angeli beati, qui ex conturbatione omnium dicuntur *commoveri,* secundum illud Iob vigesimo sexto[^p534-12]: « Columnae caeli contremiscunt et pavent ad nutum eius ». Dicuntur autem *pavere* et *contremiscere* non timore poenae, sed timore reverentiae et horrore divinae vindictae, secundum illud Iob quadragesimo primo[^p534-13]: « Cum
<!-- page 535 -->
sublatus fuerit, timebunt Angeli et territi purgabuntur ». Unde sicut quis super montis supercilio existens quodam modo terretur ex combustione villae alicuius vel hiatus terrae, sic quodam modo caelestes illae virtutes pavebunt in illa terribili ultione. Unde Hieronymus[^p535-1]: « Quid faciet virgula deserti, ubi timore concutitur cedrus Libani paradisi »? Unde Isaiae sexto: « Commota sunt superliminaria cardinum a voce clamantis, et domus repleta est fumo ». Unde Chrysostomus[^p535-2]: « Si rex, processurus ad bellum contra aliquem, expeditionem mandat in populo, dignitates omnes moventur, exercitus concitatur, tota civitas fervet; quanto magis, rege caelesti exsurgente iudicare vivos et mortuos, angelicae virtutes commoventur, terribiles ministri, terribilem Dominum praecedentes, ante ipsum pro candelabris fulgura viva praecedent, pro tubis horrenda tonitrua? Vere magna vox, cui omnia obediunt elementa, quae petras scindit, inferos aperit, vincula mortis disrumpit ».

46. (Vers. 27.). Quinto quantum *ad iudicem apparentem* dicit: *Et tunc videbunt Filium hominis venientem in nube cum potestate magna et maiestate;* Apocalypsis primo[^p535-3]: « Ecce, venit cum nubibus, et videbit eum omnis oculus, et qui eum pupugerunt ». Et de hoc adventu Danielis septimo: « Aspiciebam ego in visione noctis, et ecce, cum nubibus caeli quasi Filius hominis veniebat et usque ad antiquum dierum pervenit, et in conspectu eius obtulerunt eum. Et dedit ei potestatem et honorem et regnum. Potestas eius potestas aeterna, quae non auferetur, et regnum eius, quod non corrumpetur ». Et hic adventus erit terribilissimus adeo, quod impleatur illud Isaiae secundo[^p535-4]: « Incurvabitur sublimitas hominum, et humiliabitur altitudo virorum, et elevabitur Dominus solus in die illa, et idola penitus conterentur ». « Et ingredietur scissuras petrarum et in cavernas saxorum a facie formidinis Domini et a gloria maiestatis eius, cum surrexerit percutere terram ». Et ideo in Psalmo[^p535-5]: « Deus manifeste veniet, Deus noster, et non silebit. Ignis in conspectu eius exardescet, et in circuitu eius tempestas valida ».

47. Licet autem multa et mira contingant in iudicio et quasi inenarrabilia, Hieronymus[^p535-6] tamen quaedam narrat, quae dicit se reperisse in annalibus Iudaeorum, quae a diversis diversimode ordinantur. Dicit enim, quod primo die eriget se mare super altitudinem montium, ut verificetur illud Psalmi[^p535-7]: « Mirabiles elationes maris ». — Secundo die in tantum descendet, ut vix videri possit, secundum illud Nahum primo[^p535-8]: « Dominus in tempestate et turbine viae eius, et nebulae pulvis pedum eius, increpans mare et exsiccans illud et omnia flumina ad desertum deducens ». Et de his duobus in Psalmo: « Dixit, et stetit spiritus procellae, et exaltati sunt fluctus eius » etc. — Tertio die marinae belluae ascendentes super mare dabunt rugitus usque ad caelum, quasi futurum narrantes iudicium; Iob duodecimo[^p535-9]: « Loquere terrae, et annuntiabit tibi, et narrabunt pisces maris » etc. — Quarto die ardebit aqua, secundum illud Isaiae sexagesimo quarto: « Utinam disrumperes caelos et descenderes, a facie tua montes defluerent, sicut exustio ignis tabescerent, aquae arderent igni, ut notum fieret nomen tuum inimicis tuis ». — Quinto die herbae et arbores dabunt rorem sanguineum; Apocalypsis decimo quarto[^p535-10]: « Et calcatus est lacus extra civitatem, et exivit sanguis de lacu usque ad frenos equorum ». — Sexto die ruent aedificia; Apocalypsis decimo sexto: « Et fracta est civitas magna in tres partes, et civitates gentium ceciderunt »; et Matthaei vigesimo quarto: « Non relinquetur lapis super lapidem, qui non destruatur » etc. — Septimo petrae collidentur ad invicem, sicut factum fuit in dominica passione; Matthaei vigesimo septimo[^p535-11]: « Petrae scissae sunt » etc. — Octavo die fiet generalis terraemotus; Apocalypsis decimo sexto: « Et factus est terraemotus magnus, qualis non fuit, ex quo homines esse coeperunt ». — Nono adaequabitur terra; et tunc implebitur illud Isaiae quadragesimo[^p535-12]: « Omnis vallis exaltabitur, et omnis mons et collis humiliabitur »; et Apocalypsis sexto: « Omnis mons et insulae de locis suis motae sunt ». — Decimo exibunt homines de cavernis suis et ibunt velut amentes et non poterunt sibi mutuo loqui, et hoc prae stupore illorum mirabilium, quae videbunt et exspectabunt; et hoc notat hic[^p535-13], cum dicit: « Arescentibus hominibus prae timore ». — Undecimo aperientur sepulcra, et stabunt ossa super
<!-- page 536 -->
ea; et tunc implebitur illud Ezechielis trigesimo septimo[^p536-1]: « Ecce, ego aperiam tumulos vestros et educam vos de sepulcris vestris, popule meus » etc. — Duodecimo stellae cadent de caelo; Matthaei vigesimo quarto: « Stellae cadent de caelo, et virtutes caelorum movebuntur ». — Tertiodecimo morientur homines, ut cum mortuis resurgant, quia, sicut dicit Apostolus primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p536-2] secundum aliam translationem, « omnes quidem moriemur, sed non omnes immutabimur. In momento, in ictu oculi » etc. — Quartodecimo ardebit caelum et terra, id est superficies terrae et aëris; secundae Petri tertio[^p536-3]: « Adveniet dies Domini ut fur, in quo caeli magno impetu transient, elementa vero calore solventur, terra autem et quae in ipsa sunt opera, exurentur ». — Quintodecimo die fiet caelum novum et terra nova, et resurgent homines, et fiet iudicium; de hoc Ioannes in Apocalypsis vigesimo primo[^p536-4]: « Vidi caelum novum et terram novam; primum enim caelum et prima terra abiit, et mare iam non est ».

## English
<!-- page 533 -->

41. (Vers. 25.). *And there shall be signs in the sun* etc. After he has foretold the things that were to come generally, concerning the persecution, and concerning the destruction of Jerusalem, here, fourth[^p533-5], he foretells the things *that are to come concerning the final judgement.* Now in this erudition he first schools the disciples *unto foresight.* Second, *he raises them up unto confidence,* there[^p533-6]: *But when these things begin to come to pass* etc. But third, *he stirs them up unto vigilance,* there: *And take heed to yourselves* etc.

Concerning *the final judgement* he describes a universal *commotion* on the part of *the celestial, elemental, rational, intellectual nature* and of *the judge appearing* himself.

First, then, as to *the celestial nature* he says: *And there shall be signs in the sun, and in the moon, and in the stars. The sun* and *the moon* he names specially as the two chief lights, both according to appearance and according to efficacy; Genesis, the first chapter[^p533-7]: « God made two great lights: a greater light to rule the day, and a lesser light to rule the night, and the stars ». In these lights, then, there shall appear signs of obscuring, according to that of Joel, the second chapter: « The sun shall be turned into darkness, and the moon into blood, before the great and dreadful day of the Lord doth come ». Now it will be *dreadful* on account of the obscuring of those lumens[^tr-lumen], according to that of the Psalm[^p533-8]: « He hath made the moon for seasons: the sun knoweth his going down. Thou hast appointed darkness » etc.; and of this, Wisdom, the seventeenth chapter: « Neither could[^tr-corrigenda] the bright flames of the stars enlighten that horrible night ». Whence these lights, which in the beginning of time were placed in the firmament for signs through their own light, according to that of Genesis, the first chapter[^p533-9]: « Let there be lights made in the firmament of heaven, and let them be for signs and for seasons » etc.; in the end of times are for signs of the end through the hiding of light. Whence the Apocalypse, the sixth chapter: « The sun became black as sackcloth of hair, and the whole moon became as blood, and the stars fell from heaven upon the earth »; and Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter: « And immediately after the tribulation of those days, the sun shall be darkened, and the moon shall not give her lumen, and the stars shall fall from heaven ». — But here blessed Luke does not express that the stars *shall fall,* but that there shall be *signs* in them; because in truth the bodies of the heavenly stars will not change position according to substance, but according to appearance. By the power of the stars, the judgement being imminent, very many impressions will be generated, which the Philosopher[^p533-10] calls *assub,* which will be so multiplied that the stars of the heavens seem to fall.

42. And it is to be attended to, that as the lights are signs of natural things according to natural operations, so they are for signs of those things which come about above nature, through certain things done in them above nature. For in designation of *the marvellous power of Christ* the sun stood still, according to that of Josue, the tenth chapter[^p533-11]: « Move not, O sun, toward Gabaon, nor thou, O moon, toward the valley of Ajalon ». « So the sun stood still in the midst of heaven, and hasted not to go down the space of one day ». But in designation of *the marvellous condescension* the sun went back ten degrees; Isaias, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p533-12]: « The sun returned
<!-- page 534 -->
by ten lines, by the degrees by which it was gone down », because man after the nine orders of Angels holds the tenth place. But in designation of *the wonderful passion,* as well in *the head* as in *the members,* the sun was darkened in the passion of *the head;* below, the twenty-third chapter[^p534-1]: « There was darkness over all the earth, and the sun was darkened »; likewise in the passion of *the body;* the Apocalypse, the eighth chapter: « The third part of the sun was smitten, and the third part of the moon, and the third part of the stars »; which is referred to the body, because, the Apocalypse, the twelfth chapter, « the dragon drew the third part of the stars ».

43. Second, as to *the elemental nature* he subjoins: *And upon the earth distress of nations for the confusion*[^p534-2] *of the sound of the sea and of the waves.* Bede: « As the last judgement draws near, the sound of the sea and of the waves is confounded, because, as their term draws near, the elements, as though dreading, nod and tremble »; Wisdom, the fifth chapter[^p534-3]: « His zeal will take armour, and he will arm the creature for the revenge of his enemies ». « Shafts of lightning shall go directly, and as from a well bent bow of the clouds they shall be shot out, and shall leap to a certain place. And thick hail shall be cast from the stone casting wrath, and the water of the sea shall rage against them ». Whence then shall be verified that of Exodus, the fifteenth chapter[^p534-4]: « With the blast of thy anger the waters were gathered together ». For it is the Lord himself who troubles the sea; Isaias, the fifty-first chapter: « I am the Lord thy God, who trouble the sea, and the waves thereof swell ». And this indeed by his just judgement; because, as is said in Isaias, the fifty-seventh chapter, « the wicked are like the raging sea, which cannot rest, and the waves thereof overflow unto treading under foot ». And then there shall be a confusion of all the elements, according to what was done in Egypt, according to that of Exodus, the ninth chapter[^p534-5]: « Fire and hail mixed drove on together ». And in this confusion there shall be a terrible sound; for then shall be verified that of the Psalm: « The voice of the Lord is upon the waters; the God of majesty hath thundered, the Lord is upon many waters ». For this sound designates the sound of the terrible voice of the judge himself; whence the Apocalypse, the first chapter[^p534-6]: « His eyes as a flame of fire, and his feet like unto fine brass, as in a burning furnace, and his voice as the voice of many waters »; Ezechiel, the first chapter: « I heard the noise of wings, as the noise of many waters, and as the noise of the most high God; when they walked, it was as the noise of a multitude, as the noise of a camp ».

44. (Vers. 26.). Third, as to *the rational nature* he subjoins: *Men withering away for fear and expectation of the things which shall come upon the whole world.* For then shall be fulfilled that of Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p534-7]: « At midnight there was a cry made: Behold, the bridegroom cometh » etc.; whence the Gloss: « This is understood of the coming itself, when all the virgins, wise and foolish, roused by the unwonted cry, trim their lamps, that is, number their works with them, for which with the greatest fear they await the coming, now at hand, of the eternal decision ». Now *men* are said *to wither away* who have lived according to the flesh; for Isaias, the fortieth chapter[^p534-8]: « All flesh is grass ». « The grass is dried up » etc.; whence also James, the first chapter: « The sun rose with a burning heat, and withered the grass », *the sun* of justice coming to judgement. Whence Bede[^p534-9]: « In the expectation of the strict examination those who have flourished in this world shall wither away », according to that of Exodus, the fifteenth chapter: « Let fear and dread fall upon them, in the greatness of thy arm ». Then in those in whom there was security and joy, there shall be nothing but fear and mourning; because, the first to the Thessalonians, the fifth chapter[^p534-10], « when they shall say: Peace and security, then shall sudden destruction come upon them ». Whence Chrysostom[^p534-11]: « There shall be neither power of resisting, nor place of penance, nor faculty of satisfaction; out of the anguish of all things nothing remains but mourning ». Whence Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter: « The sign of the Son of man shall appear in heaven, and then shall all tribes of the earth mourn ».

45. Fourth, as to *the intellectual nature* he subjoins: *For the powers of heaven shall be moved;* now *the powers of heaven* are the blessed Angels, who from the troubling of all things are said *to be stirred,* according to that of Job, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p534-12]: « The pillars of heaven tremble, and dread at his beck ». Now they are said *to dread* and *to tremble* not with fear of punishment, but with the fear of reverence and the horror of the divine vengeance, according to that of Job, the forty-first chapter[^p534-13]: « When
<!-- page 535 -->
he shall be raised up, the Angels shall fear, and being affrighted shall purify themselves ». Whence, as one standing upon the brow of a mountain is in a certain manner terrified by the burning of some village or a gaping of the earth, so in a certain manner those heavenly powers shall dread at that terrible vengeance. Whence Jerome[^p535-1]: « What shall the rod of the desert do, when the cedar of Libanus of paradise is shaken with fear »? Whence Isaias, the sixth chapter: « The lintels of the doors were moved at the voice of him that cried, and the house was filled with smoke ». Whence Chrysostom[^p535-2]: « If a king, about to go forth to war against someone, orders an expedition among the people, all the dignities are moved, the army is roused, the whole city is in a ferment; how much more, when the heavenly king rises up to judge the living and the dead, are the angelic powers stirred, terrible ministers going before the terrible Lord, before him living lightnings shall go in place of candlesticks, horrible thunders in place of trumpets? Truly a great voice, which all the elements obey, which cleaves the rocks, opens hell, bursts asunder the bonds of death ».

46. (Vers. 27.). Fifth, as to *the judge appearing* he says: *And then they shall see the Son of man coming in a cloud, with great power and majesty;* the Apocalypse, the first chapter[^p535-3]: « Behold, he cometh with the clouds, and every eye shall see him, and they that pierced him ». And of this coming, Daniel, the seventh chapter: « I beheld in the vision of the night, and lo, with the clouds of heaven as it were the Son of man came, and he came even to the Ancient of days, and they presented him before him. And he gave him power and honour and a kingdom. His power is an everlasting power that shall not be taken away, and his kingdom that shall not be destroyed ». And this coming will be most terrible, so much that that of Isaias, the second chapter[^p535-4], is fulfilled: « The loftiness of men shall be bowed down, and the haughtiness of men shall be humbled, and the Lord alone shall be exalted in that day, and idols shall be utterly destroyed ». « And he shall go into the clefts of rocks, and into the holes of stones, from the face of the fear of the Lord, and from the glory of his majesty, when he shall rise up to strike the earth ». And therefore in the Psalm[^p535-5]: « God shall come manifestly, our God, and shall not keep silence. A fire shall burn before him, and a mighty tempest round about him ».

47. Now although many and wonderful things happen in the judgement, and as it were unspeakable, Jerome[^p535-6] nevertheless relates certain things which he says he found in the annals of the Jews, which are ordered diversely by diverse men. For he says that on the first day the sea shall raise itself above the height of the mountains, that that of the Psalm[^p535-7] may be verified: « Marvellous are the surges of the sea ». — On the second day it shall go down so far that it can scarcely be seen, according to that of Nahum, the first chapter[^p535-8]: « The Lord, in a tempest and a whirlwind are his ways, and clouds are the dust of his feet, rebuking the sea and drying it up, and leading down all the rivers to a desert ». And of these two in the Psalm: « He said, and the spirit of the storm stood, and the waves thereof were lifted up » etc. — On the third day the beasts of the sea, ascending above the sea, shall give roarings even to heaven, as though telling of the judgement to come; Job, the twelfth chapter[^p535-9]: « Speak to the earth, and it shall answer thee, and the fishes of the sea shall tell » etc. — On the fourth day the water shall burn, according to that of Isaias, the sixty-fourth chapter: « O that thou wouldst rend the heavens, and wouldst come down: the mountains would melt away at thy presence, they would melt as at the burning of fire, the waters would burn with fire, that thy name might be made known to thy enemies ». — On the fifth day the plants and trees shall give a bloody dew; the Apocalypse, the fourteenth chapter[^p535-10]: « And the press was trodden without the city, and blood came out of the press, up to the horses' bridles ». — On the sixth day buildings shall fall; the Apocalypse, the sixteenth chapter: « And the great city was broken into three parts, and the cities of the Gentiles fell »; and Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter: « There shall not be left a stone upon a stone that shall not be destroyed » etc. — On the seventh the rocks shall be dashed against one another, as was done in the Lord's passion; Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p535-11]: « The rocks were rent » etc. — On the eighth day there shall be a general earthquake; the Apocalypse, the sixteenth chapter: « And there was a great earthquake, such as was not since men began to be ». — On the ninth the earth shall be levelled; and then shall be fulfilled that of Isaias, the fortieth chapter[^p535-12]: « Every valley shall be exalted, and every mountain and hill shall be made low »; and the Apocalypse, the sixth chapter: « Every mountain and the islands were moved out of their places ». — On the tenth men shall come out of their caves and shall go about as though out of their minds, and shall not be able to speak to one another, and this for stupor at those marvellous things which they shall see and await; and this he notes here[^p535-13], when he says: « Men withering away for fear ». — On the eleventh the sepulchres shall be opened, and the bones shall stand upon

them; and then shall be fulfilled that of Ezechiel, the thirty-seventh chapter[^p536-1]: « Behold, I will open your graves, and will bring you out of your sepulchres, O my people » etc. — On the twelfth the stars shall fall from heaven; Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter: « The stars shall fall from heaven, and the powers of heaven shall be moved ». — On the thirteenth men shall die, that they may rise again with the dead, because, as the Apostle says, the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter[^p536-2], according to another translation, « we shall all indeed die, but we shall not all be changed. In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye » etc. — On the fourteenth heaven and earth shall burn, that is, the surface of the earth and of the air; the second of Peter, the third chapter[^p536-3]: « The day of the Lord shall come as a thief, in which the heavens shall pass away with great violence, and the elements shall be melted with heat, and the earth and the works which are in it shall be burnt up ». — On the fifteenth day there shall be made a new heaven and a new earth, and men shall rise again, and the judgement shall be made; of this John in the Apocalypse, the twenty-first chapter[^p536-4]: « I saw a new heaven and a new earth; for the first heaven and the first earth was gone, and the sea is now no more ».
