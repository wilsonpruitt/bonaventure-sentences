# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XVIII, Vers. 9-14
## *Dixit autem ad quosdam, qui in se confidebant etc.*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 452 -->

13. *Dixit autem*[^p452-7] *ad quosdam, qui in se confidebant* etc. Postquam induxit ad orationis instantiam, in hac parte intendit iuducere ad *orationis reverentiam*[^p452-8]. Quoniam autem *reverentia* haberi non potest in oratione, nisi habeatur et *humilitas* in conversatione; ideo in hac parte primo introducit *exemplum parabolicum* ad commendandam *humilitatem orationis;* secundo vero *exemplum expressum* ad commendandam *humilitatem conversationis,* ibi[^p452-9]: *Afferebant autem ad illum infantes.*

In parabola autem explicanda procedit per hunc modum. Primo enim tangit *conditiones personarum;* secundo, *conditiones precum,* ibi[^p452-10]: *Pharisaeus autem stans apud se orabat;* tertio, *conditiones exauditionum,* ibi: *Amen dico vobis, descendit hic* etc.

Circa conditiones personarum hoc ordine procedit. Tangit enim conditiones personarum[^p452-11], *quibus loquitur,* et *de quibus loquitur.*

14. (Vers. 9.). Primo igitur quantum ad *conditiones personarum, quibus loquitur,* dicit: *Dixit autem ad quosdam, qui in se confidebant tanquam iusti,* per magnificationem virtutis propriae; et hoc est contra Deum superbire, secundum illud ad Romanos decimo[^p452-12]: « Ignorantes Dei iustitiam et suam volentes statuere, iustitiae Dei non sunt subiecti ». Tunc autem confidit quis in se tanquam iustus, quando se iustum reputat ex suis operibus, sicut faciebant Iudaei; ad Romanos secundo: « Probas utiliora, instructus per Legem; confidis, te ipsum esse ducem caecorum, lumen eorum qui in tenebris sunt, eruditorem insipientium, magistrum infantium, habentem formam scientiae et veritatis in Lege ». Haec autem confidentia potius est verae iustitiae inimica; Proverbiorum undecimo[^p452-13]: « Qui confidit in divitiis suis corruet »; propter quod Ieremiae quadragesimo octavo: « Pro eo, quod habuisti fiduciam in munitionibus tuis et in thesauris tuis, tu quoque capieris ». Et hoc totum est propter defectum humilitatis,
<!-- page 453 -->
quae est totius iustitiae fundamentum. Unde Gregorius[^p453-1]: « Eam quippe, quam non invenit humilem, veritas fugit mentem »; et iterum idem: « Qui sine humilitate virtutes congregat quasi pulverem in ventum portat ». — Et quia praesumtio sui ducit in contemptum proximi, ideo subdit: *Et aspernabantur ceteros,* quantum ad contemptum scilicet meriti alieni, sicut illi, de quibus Isaiae sexagesimo quinto[^p453-2]: « Qui dicunt: Recede a me, ne appropinques mihi. Isti fumus erunt in furore meo, ignis ardens tota die ». Et ideo ad Romanos decimo quarto: « Is qui manducat non manducantem non spernat, et qui non manducat manducantem non iudicet »; et Ecclesiastici undecimo: « Ne spernas hominem in visu suo. Brevis in volatilibus apis, et initium dulcoris habet fructus eius ». — Ad hos igitur *dixit parabolam istam* in detestationem superbiae et laudem humilitatis; unde Glossa[^p453-3]: « In hac parabola ostendit, non fidei tantum verba, sed opera examinanda; inter quae maxime humilitas regnat, quam quia non habent superbi, de sua iustitia praesumentes et infirmos despicientes, quasi fide vacui, cum orant, despiciuntur ».

15. (Vers. 10.). Secundo quantum ad *conditiones personarum, de quibus* loquitur, subdit: *Duo homines ascenderunt in templum, ut orarent,* per intentionem conformem et etiam laudabilem. Ad hoc enim debemus in templum conscendere, ut oremus, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p453-4]: « Domus mea domus orationis vocabitur ». Unde Augustinus: « Nemo in oratorio aliquid agat nisi illud, ad quod factum est, unde et nomen accepit ». Unde et Dominus eos qui in templo non orabant, sed mercabantur, eiecit non sine asperitate verborum; Ioannis secundo[^p453-5]: « Invenit Iesus in templo vendentes oves et boves et columbas, et nummularios sedentes. Et cum fecisset flagellum de funiculis, omnes eiecit de templo. Et dixit eis: Auferte ista hinc et nolite facere domum Patris mei domum negotiationis »; et hoc, quia domus illa erat domus orationis[^p453-6]. Et propterea, ut competeret ei, propter quod erat, ideo in alto erat, et per gradus ascendere oportebat, ad insinuandum, quod qui vult orare, oportet sursum erigere se. Unde et in Praefatione dicitur: « Sursum corda »; et Bernardus[^p453-7] dicit, quod « licet Deus ubique sit, tamen in oratione sursum est cogitandus, ut sursum cor nostrum elevetur ». In cuius figuram Exodi vigesimo quarto « dixit Dominus ad Moysen: Ascende ad me in montem ». — Licet autem ad unum consimiliter tenderent locum, valde tamen habebant differentiam morum; et ideo subdit: *Unus Pharisaeus, et alter publicanus,* per conversationem difformem[^p453-8]. Unde Pharisaeus *apparenter* erat iustus, quia *Pharisaei* dicebantur quasi ab aliis habitu, gestu et observantiis distincti tanquam ipsius Legis observatores praecipui, secundum illud Actuum vigesimo sexto[^p453-9]: « Vitam meam a iuventute noverunt omnes Iudaei, praescientes me ab initio, si velint testimonium perhibere, quoniam secundum certissimam sectam nostrae religionis vixi Pharisaeus ». Econtra publicanus erat *patenter* malus, quia *publicani* dicebantur exactores vectigalium publicorum, sicut saepe expositum est; unde supra decimo quinto[^p453-10]: « Erant appropinquantes ad Iesum publicani et peccatores, ut audirent illum. Et murmurabant scribae et Pharisaei » etc. Magna igitur differentia est inter Pharisaeum et publicanum quantum ad *apparentiam,* magna etiam quantum ad *existentiam,* quia Pharisaeus per arrogantiam suam iustitiam labefactabat, et publicanus per poenitentiam suam iniustitiam expurgabat. Ideo Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p453-11] dicitur Pharisaeis: « Publicani et meretrices praecedent vos in regnum Dei ». Hoc autem dicitur Pharisaeis non ideo, quia apparebant boni, sed quia erant mente superbi; propter quod dicitur supra decimo sexto[^p453-12]: « Vos estis, qui iustificatis vos coram hominibus, Deus autem novit corda vestra, quia quod altum est apud homines abominatio est apud Deum ».

16. *Pharisaeus autem stans*[^p453-13] etc. Postquam descripsit conditiones personarum orantium, hic secundo describit *conditiones orationum*[^p453-14]. Et describitur hic primo conditio precis *ex parte Pharisaei superbientis;* secundo, *ex parte publicani se ipsum humiliantis,* ibi[^p453-15]: *Et publicanus a longe stans.*

Tria autem insinuantur in *modo orandi ipsius Pharisaei,* in quibus manifestatur arrogantia suae
<!-- page 454 -->
mentis tam respectu *Dei* quam *proximi* quam etiam *sui.* Introducuntur[^p454-1] enim hic ipsius Pharisaei *gratiarum actio fastuosa, reprehensio aliorum praesumtuosa* et *commendatio sui vane gloriosa.*

17. (Vers. 11.). Primo igitur quantum ad *gratiarum actionem fastuosam* dicit: *Pharisaeus autem stans apud se orabat: Deus, gratias ago tibi;* in quo arguitur Pharisaeus, non quia gratias agit Deo, sed quia hoc facit modo superbo. Unde Gregorius[^p454-2]: « Si reprehenditur ille qui superbe gratias agit, quid ergo ille qui impugnat gratiam »? Apparet autem fastus ipsius ex hoc, quod *non incurvato corpore orabat,* sed erecta fronte, cum dicatur Isaiae sexagesimo[^p454-3]: « Venient ad te curvi filii eorum qui te humiliaverunt, et adorabunt vestigia pedum tuorum ». Apparet etiam fastus, quia *apud se orabat* tanquam sibi placens, contra illud quod dicitur ad Romanos decimo quinto[^p454-4]: « Debemus nos firmiores imbecillitates infirmorum sustinere et non nobismetipsis placere ». « Etenim Christus non sibi placuit ». Et ideo Proverbiorum tertio: « Noli esse sapiens apud temetipsum. Time Dominum et recede a malo » etc. Apparet nihilominus fastus, quia *incepit a magnificatione bonorum,* quae acceperat, cum potius a sui accusatione debuisset incepisse; Proverbiorum decimo octavo[^p454-5]: « Iustus in principio est accusator sui »; incepit ab altis, cum debuisset incipere ab infimis, ut perveniret ad summa. Unde Hieronymus[^p454-6]: « Humilitas est prima virtus Christianorum », et ideo, ipsa praetermissa, in vacuum laboratur. Unde Beda[^p454-7]: « Ecce, Pharisaeus ad referendas gratias oculum habet, ad humilitatis custodiam non habet. Et quid prodest, si tota civitas custodiatur, et unum foramen, per quod intrent hostes, relinquatur »?

18. Secundo quantum ad *reprehensionem proximi praesumtuosam* subdit: *Quia non sum sicut ceteri hominum,* quasi dicat: ceteri sunt peccatores, ego autem iustus; unde *interlinearis*[^p454-8]: « Quid est *ceteri homines,* nisi omnes praeter ipsum? quasi: solus iustus sum, ceteri peccatores ». Et in hoc dupliciter dicebat falsum: et quia se dicebat non esse peccatorem, cum dicatur primae Ioannis primo[^p454-9]: « Si dixerimus, quia peccatum non habemus, mendaces sumus, et veritas in nobis non est ». Falsum etiam dicebat, quia se solum dicebat iustum, cum Dominus Eliae dicenti: « Relictus sum ego solus », dicat tertii Regum decimo nono[^p454-10]: « Reliqui mihi septem millia virorum, qui non curvaverunt genua sua ante Baal ». Hic autem, cum sit in infinitum inferior[^p454-11], non solum se dicit solum iustum, verum etiam ceteros asserit peccatores, notans eos de multitudine vitiorum. — Et ideo addit: *Raptores, iniusti, adulteri:* ut *raptores* dicantur per concupiscentiam oculorum, *iniusti* per superbiam vitae et *adulteri* per concupiscentiam carnis; in quibus notat, communiter alios a se esse infectos hoc triplici vitio, ex quo manat universalis transgressio et perpetratio peccatorum, secundum illud primae Ioannis secundo[^p454-12]: « Omne, quod est in mundo, aut est concupiscentia carnis, aut concupiscentia oculorum, aut superbia vitae ». — Et ne videretur loqui sicut incertus, adducit etiam publicanum in exemplum, cum subdit: *Velut etiam hic publicanus;* quod non dicit publicano compatiendo, sed potius despiciendo et irridendo; quocontra Ecclesiastici septimo[^p454-13]: « Ne irrideas hominem in amaritudine animae suae. Est enim qui humiliat et qui exaltat circumspector Deus ». — Et nota, quod Pharisaeus ex consideratione publicani consurrexit in *temeritatem iudicii,* quia, cum videret, illum esse peccatorem, non solum ipsum, verum etiam ceteros condemnabat. Consurrexit etiam in *elationem animi;* unde Bernardus[^p454-14]: « Incentivum elationis est consideratio deterioris, sicut incentivum humilitatis est consideratio melioris ».
<!-- page 455 -->

19. (Vers. 12.). Tertio quantum ad *commendationem sui vane gloriosam* subdit: *Ieiuno bis in Sabbato, decimas omnium do, quae possideo,* quasi dicat: perfectam iustitiam servo, et erga me ipsum et erga Deum, quia carnem macero ieiunio bis in Sabbato, id est in septimana[^p455-1]; et pietatem ad Deum servo reddendo decimas et eleemosynas. Et haec duo sunt, quae maxime placent Deo; unde et Angelus dixit Tobiae duodecimo[^p455-2]: « Bona est oratio cum ieiunio et eleemosyna magis quam thesauros auri condere ». Unde et hic multum se ipsum commendat, sed in hoc suam iustitiam per vanam gloriam foedat; Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo[^p455-3]: « Laudet te alienus, et non os tuum; extraneus, et non labia tua »; quia, sicut dicitur secundae ad Corinthios decimo, « non qui se ipsum commendat, ille probatus est », immo certe reprobatus. Unde Ecclesiastici septimo[^p455-4]: « Non te iustifices apud Deum, quoniam agnitor cordis ipse est »; et Ieremiae secundo: « Ecce, ego iudicio contendam tecum, eo quod dixeris: Non peccavi »; et Iob nono: « Si iustificare me voluero, os meum condemnabit me; si innocentem ostendero, pravum me comprobabit ». Et hoc totum propter vitium elationis, quae maxime displicet Deo; ad Ephesios secundo[^p455-5]: « Gratia salvati estis per fidem. Dei enim donum est, non ex operibus, ne quis glorietur ».

20. Sed cum iste Pharisaeus bona, quae habet, a Domino recognoscat; non videtur inaniter gloriari; quia ille inaniter gloriatur, qui « gloriatur, quasi non acceperit[^p455-6] »; hic autem gratias Deo agit. — Ad quod respondet Bernardus[^p455-7], quod non recte recognovit bona, quae accepit; ait enim sic: « Gratias agit, non quia bonus, sed quia solus, non tam de bonis, quae habet, quam de malis, quae in aliis videt ». Talis autem modus est elationis; unde Gregorius[^p455-8]: « Quatuor sunt species, quibus omnis tumor arrogantium demonstratur: cum vel aliquis bonum, quod habet, *a se* habere se existimat; aut, si datum a Deo credit, *pro meritis* datum putat; aut cum iactat, se habere *quod non habet;* aut cum, despectis ceteris, appetit *singulariter videri habere* quod habet ». Hac peste Pharisaeus laborabat, qui sua merita extollendo se ceteris praeferebat.

21. (Vers. 13.). *Et publicanus a longe stans* etc. Postquam expressit conditiones in prece superbi Pharisaei, hic secundo[^p455-9] exprimit *conditiones precis humilis publicani,* in qua describitur perfectae humilitatis insinuatio quantum ad *gestum,* quantum ad *motum* et quantum ad *verbum.*

Primo quantum ad *insinuationem humilitatis per gestum* dicit: *Et publicanus a longe stans, nolebat nec oculos ad caelum levare.* Beda[^p455-10]: « Sicut humilis, non audet appropinquare, ut Deus ad eum appropinquet; non aspicit, ut aspiciatur ». Nihil enim placet Deo tantum in poenitente, sicut dicit Bernardus[^p455-11], quantum humilis verecundia; et taliter vult orari. Unde iste publicanus erat de illis, de quibus Isaiae quadragesimo nono: « Vultu in terra demisso, adorabunt te et pulverem pedum tuorum lingent ». Non audebat *oculos levare ad caelum,* quia recognoscebat, se offendisse Deum caeli, ita ut diceret sicut filius prodigus poenitens, supra decimo quinto[^p455-12]: « Pater, peccavi in caelum et coram te »; et illud secundi Paralipomenon ultimo: « Peccavi super numerum arenae maris, et multiplicata sunt peccata mea, et non sum dignus videre altitudinem caeli prae multitudine iniquitatis meae ». Ideo igitur non audebat levare faciem propter peccati verecundiam, ita ut intra se diceret illud primi Esdrae nono: « Domine Deus meus, confundor et erubesco levare faciem meam ad te, quoniam iniquitates nostrae multiplicatae sunt super caput nostrum, et delicta nostra creverunt usque ad caelum ».

22. Secundo quantum ad *insinuationem humilitatis* quantum ad *corporis motum* subdit: *Sed percutiebat pectus suum;* in quo reddebat se ipsum culpabilem et ostendebat dolentem. In hoc enim, quod pectus percutitur, ostenditur cor introrsus supplicio
<!-- page 456 -->
dignum; in cuius rei signum, secundi Regum ultimo[^p456-1], « percussit autem cor David eum, postquam numeratus est populus; et dixit David ad Dominum: Peccavi valde in hoc facto, sed precor, Domine, ut transferas iniquitatem servi tui ». Sic iste percutiebat pectus, quasi indignatus contra se ipsum. Unde Glossa[^p456-2]: « Percutit pectus et poenas de se exigit, ut Deus parcat ». Unde et consuetudinis nostrae est in confessione et in dicendo culpam tundere pectora nostra in redargutionem et poenitentiam nostrorum cordium et contestationem conscientiarum. Unde infra vigesimo tertio[^p456-3] dicitur, quod « turba eorum qui simul aderant ad spectaculum istud et videbant quae fiebant, percutiebant pectora sua ». Propter quod et *tunsio pectorum,* sicut dicit Augustinus[^p456-4], valet ad remissionem venialium; unde est unum de octo, quibus venialia remittuntur. Venialia enim dicuntur remitti confessione, pectoris tunsione, aquae benedictae aspersione, cordis contritione, crucis signatione, oratione, sacra communione, iniuriarum remissione; unde versus[^p456-5]:

> Confiteor, tundo, respergor, conteror, oro,
> Signor, edo, dono; per haec venialia pono.

23. Tertio quantum ad *insinuationem humilitatis per verbum* subiungit: *Dicens: Deus, propitius esto mihi peccatori.* Nota hic brevissimam orationem et efficacissimam, quia in hac orans se *ipsum humiliat* et *Deum exaltat;* et talis oratio est Deo acceptabilis, secundum illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo quinto[^p456-6]: « Oratio humiliantis se nubes penetrabit ». Deum etiam magnificat *humiliter petendo veniam;* Ecclesiastici ultimo: « Laudabo nomen Domini assidue, et exaudita est oratio mea » etc. Magnificat etiam eum *vocando ipsum Deum,* quia hoc est nomen *summae maiestatis;* Psalmus[^p456-7]: « Scitote, quoniam Dominus ipse est Deus; ipse fecit nos, et non ipsi nos »; et iterum: « Quoniam Deus magnus Dominus et rex magnus » etc. — Est nomen *summae veritatis;* Numerorum vigesimo tertio[^p456-8]: « Non est Deus ut homo, ut mentiatur »; et ad Romanos tertio: « Est enim Deus verax » etc. — Est similiter nomen *summae caritatis;* unde primae Ioannis quarto[^p456-9]: « Deus caritas est, et qui manet in caritate in Deo manet, et Deus in eo ». — Est nihilominus nomen *summae pietatis;* secundae ad Corinthios primo: « Benedictus Deus et pater Domini nostri Iesu Christi, pater misericordiarum et Deus totius consolationis » etc. — Dicit igitur publicanus orando tam humiliter quam devote: *Deus, propitius esto mihi peccatori;* Deus *summae maiestatis: propitius esto mihi peccatori,* ad manifestandam tuam *potentiam,* secundum illud Sapientiae undecimo[^p456-10]: « Misereris omnium, Domine, qui omnia potes, dissimulans peccata hominum propter poenitentiam » etc.; *summae veritatis: propitius esto mihi peccatori,* ad manifestandam tuam *iustitiam;* Psalmus[^p456-11]: « In veritate tua, exaudi me in tua iustitia », in *veritate,* inquam, quam promisisti; Ezechielis trigesimo tertio: « Impietas impii non nocebit ei, in quacumque die conversus fuerit ab iniquitate »; *summae caritatis: propitius esto mihi peccatori,* ad manifestandam tuam *benevolentiam;* Sapientiae undecimo[^p456-12]: « Parcis autem omnibus, quoniam tua sunt, Domine, qui amas animas »; et Ieremiae trigesimo primo: « In caritate perpetua dilexi te, ideo attraxi te miserans tui » etc.; *summae pietatis: propitius esto mihi peccatori,* ad manifestandam tuam *misericordiam,* pro quo orabat Propheta in Psalmo[^p456-13]: « Miserere mei, Deus, secundum magnam misericordiam tuam »; et Ecclesiastici trigesimo sexto: « Miserere nostri, Deus omnium, et respice nos et ostende nobis » etc.

24. (Vers. 14.). *Amen dico vobis*[^p456-14]. Postquam descripsit conditiones personarum et precum, hic iam tertio describit *conditiones exauditionum*[^p456-15]. Describitur autem hic exauditionis conditio quantum ad *iudicium personale* et quantum ad *edictum universale.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *iudicium personale* dicit: *Amen dico vobis: Descendit hic iustificatus in domum suam ab illo,* id est « plus quam ille[^p456-16] »
<!-- page 457 -->
quia Pharisaeus descendit iustificatus secundum suam opinionem, sed iste secundum veritatem. Unde Dominus plus pensat unam humilem supplicationem peccatoris poenitentis quam innumerabiles actiones alicuius iusti de se praesumentis. Propter quod dicit supra decimo quinto[^p457-1]: « Gaudium est Angelis Dei super uno peccatore poenitentiam agente quam super nonaginta novem iustis, qui non indigent poenitentia », id est, qui se reputant non egere; in quo miro modo reprimitur superbia humana, ut non de suis meritis praesumat. Propter hoc etiam Deus reprobavit superbum populum Iudaicum et elegit plebem humilem gentilium populorum, quorum figuram gerunt hic Pharisaeus et iste publicanus. Unde Glossa[^p457-2]: « Typice Pharisaeus Iudaeorum populum designat, qui ex iustificationibus Legis extollit merita sua et superbiendo recedit; humiliatus publicanus gentilem, qui longe a Deo positus peccata confitetur et lamentando appropinquat Deo et exaltatur ». Ideo dicitur ad Romanos undecimo[^p457-3]: « Noli altum sapere, sed time. Si enim Deus naturalibus ramis non pepercit, ne forte nec tibi parcat ». Si ergo Deus repellit iustos propter superbiam, quanto magis impios et superbos; primae Petri quarto: « Si iustus vix salvabitur, impius et peccator ubi parebunt »?

25. Secundo quantum ad *edictum universale* subdit: *Quia omnis, qui se exaltat, humiliabitur; et qui se humiliat exaltabitur.* Hic datur generalis sententia de humilibus et superbis; unde Glossa[^p457-4]: « Posita Pharisaei et publicani controversia, ponitur iudicis sententia, ut caveatur superbia ». In qua sententia universaliter praefertur humilis superbo, quia superbus debet deiici, secundum quod dicitur in Abdia[^p457-5]: « Si exaltatus fueris ut aquila et si inter sidera posueris nidum tuum » etc.; et Iob vigesimo: « Si ascenderit usque ad caelum superbia eius, et caput eius nubes tetigerit; quasi sterquilinium in fine perdetur ». Propter quod Proverbiorum decimo sexto[^p457-6]: « Contritionem praecedit superbia », et e contrario humilis iustus exaltatur; Iob vigesimo secundo: « Qui humiliatus fuerit erit in gloria, et qui inclinaverit oculos suos, ille salvabitur ». Et ideo primae Petri ultimo: « Humiliamini sub potenti manu Dei, ut vos exaltet in tempore visitationis ». De his duobus simul Proverbiorum decimo octavo[^p457-7]: « Antequam conteratur, exaltatur cor hominis, et antequam glorificetur, humiliatur »; quia, sicut dicitur Proverbiorum vigesimo nono, « superbum sequitur humilitas, et humilem spiritu suscipiet gloria ». Et hoc est quod dicitur Ecclesiastici vigesimo[^p457-8]: « Est propter gloriam minoratio, et est qui ab humilitate elevabit caput suum ». — Unde exemplum habemus hic in Pharisaeo et publicano, exemplum in populo Iudaico et gentili, exemplum in Saule reprobo et David electo, exemplum summum in lucifero et Iesu Christo. Unde secundum hanc sententiam decurrit totius regimen universi.

26. Et nota, quod haec eadem sententia supra decimo quarto[^p457-9] fuit posita circa invitatos ad nuptias et nunc iteratur hic; sed non sine causa, quia duplex est humilitas: una est *caritatis,* qua voluntarie quis se humiliat; alia est humilitas *veritatis,* qua quis parva de se sentit[^p457-10]. Supra ergo hoc dixit, ut incitaret ad humilitatem *caritatis;* hic iterat id ipsum, ut incitet ad humilitatem *veritatis,* sicut apparet, quia publicanus propter conscientiam mali meriti de se compellebatur infima sentire. Haec autem humilitas duplex est nobis necessaria, scilicet *veritatis* in intellectu et *caritatis* in affectu, ut excludatur a nobis superbia praesumtionis et ambitionis; et ideo ad haec insinuanda haec sententia non irrationabiliter iteratur.

13. *And he spoke*[^p452-7] *to some, who trusted in themselves* etc. After he led to the instance of prayer, in this part he intends to lead [so printed: *iuducere* for *inducere*] to *the reverence of prayer*[^p452-8]. But since *reverence* cannot be had in prayer unless *humility* also be had in conduct; therefore in this part he first introduces *a parabolic example* to commend *the humility of prayer;* second, *an express example* to commend *the humility of conduct,* there[^p452-9]: *And they brought unto him infants.*

In explaining the parable, then, he proceeds in this manner. For first he touches *the conditions of the persons;* second, *the conditions of the entreaties,* there[^p452-10]: *But the Pharisee standing prayed with himself;* third, *the conditions of the hearings,* there: *Amen I say to you, this man went down* etc.

Concerning the conditions of the persons he proceeds in this order. For he touches the conditions of the persons[^p452-11] *to whom he speaks,* and *of whom he speaks.*

14. (Vers. 9.). First, then, as to *the conditions of the persons to whom he speaks,* he says: *And he spoke to some, who trusted in themselves as just,* through the magnifying of their own virtue; and this is to be proud against God, according to that to the Romans, the tenth chapter[^p452-12]: « Not knowing the justice of God, and seeking to establish their own, they have not submitted themselves to the justice of God ». But one then trusts in himself as just, when he reckons himself just from his own works, as the Jews did; to the Romans, the second chapter: « Thou approvest the more profitable things, being instructed by the Law; thou art confident that thou thyself art a guide of the blind, a light of them that are in darkness, an instructor of the foolish, a teacher of infants, having the form of knowledge and of truth in the Law ». But this confidence is rather an enemy of true justice; Proverbs, the eleventh chapter[^p452-13]: « He that trusteth in his riches shall fall »; on account of which Jeremias, the forty-eighth chapter: « For because thou hast had trust in thy bulwarks and in thy treasures, thou also shalt be taken ». And all this is on account of a lack of humility,
<!-- page 453 -->
which is the foundation of all justice. Whence Gregory[^p453-1]: « For truth flees the mind which it does not find humble »; and again the same: « He who gathers virtues without humility carries as it were dust into the wind ». — And because presumption of self leads into contempt of the neighbour, therefore he subjoins: *And despised others,* namely as to the contempt of another's merit, like those of whom Isaias, the sixty-fifth chapter[^p453-2]: « Who say: Depart from me, come not near me. These shall be smoke in my anger, a fire burning all the day ». And therefore to the Romans, the fourteenth chapter: « Let not him that eateth despise him that eateth not, and he that eateth not, let him not judge him that eateth »; and Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter: « Despise not a man for his look. The bee is small among flying things, but her fruit hath the chiefest sweetness ». — To these, then, *he spoke this parable* in detestation of pride and in praise of humility; whence the Gloss[^p453-3]: « In this parable he shows that not the words of faith only, but the works are to be examined; among which humility reigns most of all, which because the proud have not, presuming of their own justice and despising the weak, as though empty of faith, when they pray, they are despised ».

15. (Vers. 10.). Second, as to *the conditions of the persons of whom* he speaks, he subjoins: *Two men went up into the temple to pray,* by an intention in conformity and also praiseworthy. For to this end we ought to go up into the temple, that we may pray, according to that of Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p453-4]: « My house shall be called the house of prayer ». Whence Augustine: « Let no one do anything in the oratory but that for which it was made, whence also it received its name ». Whence also the Lord cast out, not without harshness of words, those who did not pray in the temple, but trafficked; John, the second chapter[^p453-5]: « Jesus found in the temple them that sold sheep and oxen and doves, and the changers of money sitting. And when he had made a scourge of little cords, he drove them all out of the temple. And he said to them: Take these things hence, and make not the house of my Father a house of traffic »; and this, because that house was a house of prayer[^p453-6]. And therefore, that it might befit that for which it was, it was on high, and one had to go up by steps, to intimate that he who wills to pray must lift himself upward. Whence also in the Preface it is said: « Lift up your hearts »; and Bernard[^p453-7] says that « although God is everywhere, yet in prayer he is to be thought of as above, that our heart may be lifted up above ». In figure of which, in Exodus, the twenty-fourth chapter, « the Lord said to Moses: Come up to me into the mount ». — But although they were tending alike to one place, yet they had a great difference of manners; and therefore he subjoins: *The one a Pharisee, and the other a publican,* through unlike conduct[^p453-8]. Whence the Pharisee was *apparently* just, because they were called *Pharisees* as it were set apart from others by habit, gesture and observances, as the chief observers of the Law itself, according to that of the Acts, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p453-9]: « All the Jews know my life from my youth, knowing me from the beginning, if they will give testimony, that according to the most sure sect of our religion I lived a Pharisee ». On the other hand the publican was *openly* wicked, because those were called *publicans* who were exactors of the public taxes, as has often been expounded; whence above, the fifteenth chapter[^p453-10]: « Now the publicans and sinners drew near unto Jesus to hear him. And the scribes and Pharisees murmured » etc. Great, then, is the difference between the Pharisee and the publican as to *appearance,* great also as to *existence,* because the Pharisee through his arrogance was undermining his justice, and the publican through his penance was purging away his injustice. Therefore in Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p453-11], it is said to the Pharisees: « The publicans and the harlots shall go into the kingdom of God before you ». But this is said to the Pharisees not because they appeared good, but because they were proud in mind; on account of which it is said above, the sixteenth chapter[^p453-12]: « You are they who justify yourselves before men, but God knoweth your hearts, for that which is high to men is an abomination before God ».

16. *But the Pharisee standing*[^p453-13] etc. After he has described the conditions of the persons praying, here second he describes *the conditions of the prayers*[^p453-14]. And here is described first the condition of entreaty *on the part of the Pharisee being proud;* second, *on the part of the publican humbling himself,* there[^p453-15]: *And the publican standing afar off.*

But three things are intimated in *the manner of praying of the Pharisee himself,* in which is manifested the arrogance of his
<!-- page 454 -->
mind as much in respect of *God* as of *his neighbour* as also of *himself.* For there are introduced[^p454-1] here the Pharisee's *haughty giving of thanks, presumptuous reprehension of others* and *vainglorious commendation of himself.*

17. (Vers. 11.). First, then, as to *the haughty giving of thanks* he says: *But the Pharisee standing prayed with himself: O God, I give thee thanks;* in which the Pharisee is reproved, not because he gives thanks to God, but because he does this in a proud manner. Whence Gregory[^p454-2]: « If he is reproved who proudly gives thanks, what then of him who attacks grace »? And his haughtiness appears from this, that *he prayed with his body not bowed,* but with brow erect, since it is said in Isaias, the sixtieth chapter[^p454-3]: « The children of them that humbled thee shall come bowed down to thee, and shall worship the steps of thy feet ». His haughtiness appears also because *he prayed with himself* as one pleasing to himself, against that which is said to the Romans, the fifteenth chapter[^p454-4]: « We that are stronger ought to bear the infirmities of the weak, and not to please ourselves ». « For Christ did not please himself ». And therefore Proverbs, the third chapter: « Be not wise with thyself. Fear the Lord, and depart from evil » etc. His haughtiness appears none the less because *he began from the magnifying of the goods* which he had received, when he ought rather to have begun from the accusing of himself; Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter[^p454-5]: « The just is in the beginning the accuser of himself »; he began from the high things, when he ought to have begun from the lowest, so as to arrive at the highest. Whence Jerome[^p454-6]: « Humility is the first virtue of Christians », and therefore, it being passed over, one labours in vain. Whence Bede[^p454-7]: « Behold, the Pharisee has an eye for rendering thanks, he has none for the keeping of humility. And what does it profit if the whole city be guarded, and one hole be left through which enemies may enter »?

18. Second, as to *the presumptuous reprehension of his neighbour* he subjoins: *That I am not as the rest of men,* as if he were to say: the rest are sinners, but I am just; whence the *interlinear* Gloss[^p454-8]: « What is *the rest of men,* but all except himself? as if: I alone am just, the rest are sinners ». And in this he said what was false in two ways: both because he said that he was not a sinner, since it is said in the first of John, the first chapter[^p454-9]: « If we say that we have no sin, we are liars, and the truth is not in us ». He said what was false also because he said that he alone was just, since the Lord says to Elias, who said: « I alone am left », in the third of Kings, the nineteenth chapter[^p454-10]: « I have left me seven thousand men, who have not bowed their knees before Baal ». But this man, although he is infinitely inferior[^p454-11], not only says that he alone is just, but also asserts that the rest are sinners, marking them with a multitude of vices. — And therefore he adds: *Extortioners, unjust, adulterers:* so that they are called *extortioners* through the concupiscence of the eyes, *unjust* through the pride of life and *adulterers* through the concupiscence of the flesh; in which he marks that others than himself are commonly infected with this threefold vice, from which flows the universal transgression and committing of sins, according to that of the first of John, the second chapter[^p454-12]: « All that is in the world is either the concupiscence of the flesh, or the concupiscence of the eyes, or the pride of life ». — And lest he should seem to speak as one uncertain, he brings forward also the publican as an example, when he subjoins: *As also is this publican;* which he does not say in compassion for the publican, but rather in despising and mocking him; against which Ecclesiasticus, the seventh chapter[^p454-13]: « Mock not a man in the bitterness of his soul. For there is one that humbleth and exalteth, God who seeth all ». — And note that the Pharisee from the consideration of the publican rose up into *rashness of judgement,* because, when he saw that he was a sinner, he condemned not him only, but also the rest. He rose up also into *the lifting up of mind;* whence Bernard[^p454-14]: « The incentive of lifting up is the consideration of the worse, as the incentive of humility is the consideration of the better ».
<!-- page 455 -->

19. (Vers. 12.). Third, as to *the vainglorious commendation of himself* he subjoins: *I fast twice in the Sabbath, I give tithes of all that I possess,* as if he were to say: I keep perfect justice, both towards myself and towards God, because I mortify the flesh by fasting twice in the Sabbath, that is, in the week[^p455-1]; and I keep piety towards God by paying tithes and alms. And these two are the things which most of all please God; whence the Angel also said, Tobias, the twelfth chapter[^p455-2]: « Prayer is good with fasting and alms more than to lay up treasures of gold ». Whence here too he commends himself much, but in this he defiles his justice through vainglory; Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p455-3]: « Let another praise thee, and not thy own mouth; a stranger, and not thy own lips »; because, as is said in the second to the Corinthians, the tenth chapter, « not he who commendeth himself is approved », nay rather, certainly reprobated. Whence Ecclesiasticus, the seventh chapter[^p455-4]: « Justify not thyself before God, for he is the knower of the heart »; and Jeremias, the second chapter: « Behold, I will contend with thee in judgement, because thou hast said: I have not sinned »; and Job, the ninth chapter: « If I would justify myself, my own mouth shall condemn me; if I would shew myself innocent, he shall prove me wicked ». And all this on account of the vice of lifting up, which most of all displeases God; to the Ephesians, the second chapter[^p455-5]: « By grace you are saved through faith. For it is the gift of God, not of works, that no man may glory ».

20. But since this Pharisee acknowledges the goods which he has as from the Lord, he does not seem to glory vainly; because he glories vainly who « glories as if he had not received[^p455-6] »; but this man gives thanks to God. — To which Bernard[^p455-7] answers that he did not rightly acknowledge the goods which he received; for he says thus: « He gives thanks, not because he is good, but because he is alone, not so much for the good things which he has, as for the evils which he sees in others ». And such is the manner of lifting up; whence Gregory[^p455-8]: « There are four kinds by which all the swelling of the arrogant is shown: when one either thinks that he has *from himself* the good which he has; or, if he believes it given by God, thinks it given *for his merits;* or when he boasts that he has *what he has not;* or when, despising the rest, he desires *to seem singularly to have* what he has ». With this plague the Pharisee laboured, who by extolling his merits preferred himself to the rest.

21. (Vers. 13.). *And the publican standing afar off* etc. After he has expressed the conditions in the entreaty of the proud Pharisee, here second[^p455-9] he expresses *the conditions of the entreaty of the humble publican,* in which is described the intimation of perfect humility as to *gesture,* as to *motion* and as to *word.*

First, as to *the intimation of humility through gesture* he says: *And the publican standing afar off would not so much as lift up his eyes towards heaven.* Bede[^p455-10]: « As one humble, he does not dare to draw near, that God may draw near to him; he does not look, that he may be looked upon ». For nothing so pleases God in the penitent, as Bernard says[^p455-11], as humble shamefastness; and in such wise he wills to be prayed to. Whence this publican was of those of whom Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter: « With their face cast down to the earth they shall worship thee, and they shall lick up the dust of thy feet ». He did not dare *to lift up his eyes towards heaven,* because he acknowledged that he had offended the God of heaven, so that he might say, like the penitent prodigal son, above, the fifteenth chapter[^p455-12]: « Father, I have sinned against heaven and before thee »; and that of the second of Paralipomenon, the last chapter: « I have sinned above the number of the sand of the sea, and my sins are multiplied, and I am not worthy to see the height of heaven for the multitude of my iniquity ». Therefore, then, he did not dare to lift up his face on account of the shamefastness of sin, so that he might say within himself that of the first of Esdras, the ninth chapter: « O Lord my God, I am confounded and ashamed to lift up my face to thee, for our iniquities are multiplied over our head, and our sins are grown up even unto heaven ».

22. Second, as to *the intimation of humility* as to *the motion of the body* he subjoins: *But struck his breast;* in which he rendered himself culpable and showed himself grieving. For in this, that the breast is struck, the heart is shown to be inwardly worthy of
<!-- page 456 -->
penalty; in sign of which thing, in the second of Kings, the last chapter[^p456-1], « David's heart struck him, after the people were numbered; and David said to the Lord: I have sinned very much in this deed, but I pray thee, O Lord, to take away the iniquity of thy servant ». So this man struck his breast, as one indignant against himself. Whence the Gloss[^p456-2]: « He strikes his breast and exacts punishments of himself, that God may spare ». Whence it is also our custom in confession and in saying our fault to beat our breasts in conviction and penance of our hearts and in attestation of our consciences. Whence below, the twenty-third chapter[^p456-3], it is said that « the multitude of them that were present together at that sight, and saw the things that were done, struck their breasts ». On account of which *the beating of the breast* also, as Augustine says[^p456-4], avails for the remission of venial sins; whence it is one of the eight by which venial sins are remitted. For venial sins are said to be remitted by confession, the beating of the breast, the sprinkling of holy water, contrition of heart, the signing of the cross, prayer, holy communion, the remission of injuries; whence the verses[^p456-5]:

> I confess, I beat, I am sprinkled, I am contrite, I pray,
> I am signed, I eat, I forgive; through these I lay down venial sins.

23. Third, as to *the intimation of humility through word* he subjoins: *Saying: O God, be merciful to me a sinner.* Note here a most brief prayer and a most efficacious, because in it the one praying *humbles himself* and *exalts God;* and such a prayer is acceptable to God, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-fifth chapter[^p456-6]: « The prayer of him that humbleth himself shall pierce the clouds ». He magnifies God also *by humbly asking pardon;* Ecclesiasticus, the last chapter: « I will praise the name of the Lord continually, and my prayer was heard » etc. He magnifies him also *by calling him God,* because this is the name *of supreme majesty;* the Psalm[^p456-7]: « Know ye that the Lord he is God; he made us, and not we ourselves »; and again: « For the Lord is a great God and a great King » etc. — It is the name *of supreme truth;* Numbers, the twenty-third chapter[^p456-8]: « God is not as a man, that he should lie »; and to the Romans, the third chapter: « For God is true » etc. — It is likewise the name *of supreme charity;* whence the first of John, the fourth chapter[^p456-9]: « God is charity, and he that abideth in charity abideth in God, and God in him ». — It is none the less the name *of supreme piety*[^tr-pietas]; the second to the Corinthians, the first chapter: « Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies and the God of all comfort » etc. — The publican, then, praying as humbly as devoutly, says: *O God, be merciful to me a sinner;* God *of supreme majesty: be merciful to me a sinner,* to manifest thy *power,* according to that of Wisdom, the eleventh chapter[^p456-10]: « Thou hast mercy upon all, O Lord, who canst do all things, and overlookest the sins of men for the sake of repentance » etc.; *of supreme truth: be merciful to me a sinner,* to manifest thy *justice;* the Psalm[^p456-11]: « In thy truth hear me in thy justice », in thy *truth,* I say, which thou hast promised; Ezechiel, the thirty-third chapter: « The wickedness of the wicked shall not hurt him, in what day soever he shall turn from iniquity »; *of supreme charity: be merciful to me a sinner,* to manifest thy *benevolence;* Wisdom, the eleventh chapter[^p456-12]: « But thou sparest all, because they are thine, O Lord, who lovest souls »; and Jeremias, the thirty-first chapter: « In everlasting charity I have loved thee, therefore have I drawn thee, taking pity on thee » etc.; *of supreme piety: be merciful to me a sinner,* to manifest thy *mercy,* for which the Prophet prayed in the Psalm[^p456-13]: « Have mercy on me, O God, according to thy great mercy »; and Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-sixth chapter: « Have mercy upon us, O God of all, and behold us, and shew us » etc.

24. (Vers. 14.). *Amen I say to you*[^p456-14]. After he has described the conditions of the persons and of the entreaties, here now third he describes *the conditions of the hearings*[^p456-15]. And here is described the condition of the hearing as to *the personal judgement* and as to *the universal edict.*

First, then, as to *the personal judgement* he says: *Amen I say to you: This man went down into his house justified rather than the other,* that is, « more than he[^p456-16] »
<!-- page 457 -->
because the Pharisee went down justified according to his own opinion, but this man according to the truth. Whence the Lord weighs one humble supplication of a penitent sinner more than innumerable actions of some just man presuming of himself. On account of which he says above, the fifteenth chapter[^p457-1]: « There is joy to the Angels of God upon one sinner doing penance more than upon ninety-nine just, who need not penance », that is, who reckon that they have no need; in which in a wonderful manner human pride is repressed, that it may not presume of its own merits. On account of this also God reprobated the proud Jewish people and chose the humble commonalty of the Gentile peoples, whose figure the Pharisee here and this publican bear. Whence the Gloss[^p457-2]: « Typically the Pharisee designates the people of the Jews, who from the justifications of the Law extols its merits and by being proud goes away; the humbled publican, the Gentile, who, set far from God, confesses his sins and by lamenting draws near to God and is exalted ». Therefore it is said to the Romans, the eleventh chapter[^p457-3]: « Be not highminded, but fear. For if God hath not spared the natural branches, lest perhaps he also spare not thee ». If therefore God repels the just on account of pride, how much more the impious and the proud; the first of Peter, the fourth chapter: « If the just man shall scarcely be saved, where shall the ungodly and the sinner appear »?

25. Second, as to *the universal edict* he subjoins: *Because every one that exalteth himself shall be humbled; and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted.* Here is given a general sentence concerning the humble and the proud; whence the Gloss[^p457-4]: « The controversy of the Pharisee and of the publican having been set forth, the sentence of the judge is set forth, that pride may be guarded against ». In which sentence the humble man is universally preferred to the proud, because the proud man must be cast down, according to what is said in Abdias[^p457-5]: « Though thou be exalted as an eagle, and though thou set thy nest among the stars » etc.; and Job, the twentieth chapter: « If his pride mount up even to heaven, and his head touch the clouds; in the end he shall be destroyed like a dunghill ». On account of which Proverbs, the sixteenth chapter[^p457-6]: « Pride goeth before destruction », and on the contrary the humble just man is exalted; Job, the twenty-second chapter: « He that hath been humbled shall be in glory, and he that shall bow down his eyes, he shall be saved ». And therefore the first of Peter, the last chapter: « Be you humbled under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in the time of visitation ». Of these two together Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter[^p457-7]: « Before destruction the heart of a man is exalted, and before he be glorified, it is humbled »; because, as is said in Proverbs, the twenty-ninth chapter, « humiliation followeth the proud, and glory shall uphold the humble of spirit ». And this is what is said in Ecclesiasticus, the twentieth chapter[^p457-8]: « There is an abasement because of glory, and there is one that shall lift up his head from humility ». — Whence we have an example here in the Pharisee and the publican, an example in the Jewish and the Gentile people, an example in Saul the reprobate and David the elect, the supreme example in Lucifer and Jesus Christ. Whence according to this sentence runs the governance of the whole universe.

26. And note that this same sentence was set down above, in the fourteenth chapter[^p457-9], concerning those invited to the marriage, and now is repeated here; but not without cause, because humility is twofold: one is *of charity,* by which one humbles himself voluntarily; the other is the humility *of truth,* by which one thinks little of himself[^p457-10]. Above, then, he said this to incite to the humility *of charity;* here he repeats the very same to incite to the humility *of truth,* as appears, because the publican, on account of the consciousness of ill desert, was compelled to think the lowest things of himself. And this twofold humility is necessary to us, namely that *of truth* in the intellect and that *of charity* in the affection, so that the pride of presumption and of ambition may be shut out from us; and therefore, to intimate these things, this sentence is not unreasonably repeated.

[^tr-pietas]: **En.** Translator's note: *pietas* is rendered "piety" throughout, and *pius* "pious" (ruling 12: the six mercy nouns stay six words). Here (¶ 23) the piety is God's: *Deus* is *nomen summae pietatis,* "the name of supreme piety", proved from II Cor. 1:3, *pater misericordiarum,* and the publican's *propitius esto* is then read as a plea to God of supreme piety *ad manifestandam tuam misericordiam,* "to manifest thy mercy". The Latin word covers the tenderness of a father toward his own as well as the devotion of a creature toward God; Bonaventure is using the first sense. ¶ 19's *pietatem ad Deum servo,* the Pharisee's boast, is human piety.

[^tr-lumen]: **En.** Translator's note: this translation keeps *lux* as "light" and *lumen* as "lumen", except where a verse is quoted as a verse and no surrounding prose leans on the word. Here the one *lumen* of Bonaventure's own text is not his but the apostle's: Rom. 2:19 in ¶ 14, *lumen eorum qui in tenebris sunt,* quoted as a verse with nothing in the paragraph leaning on the word, keeps the Douay's "a light of them that are in darkness". The *lumen* rendered so stands in the editors' quotation of Bernard (p. 457 n. 10), *si temetipsum intus ad lumen veritatis … inspicias,* "if you look into yourself inwardly by the lumen of truth", where Bernard's own prose carries the word (rulings 50, 79).
