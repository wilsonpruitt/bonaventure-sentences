# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XVIII, Vers. 1-8
## *De persuasione studiositatis exhibentur duo*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 448 -->

### Capitulum XVIII.

#### De persuasione studiositatis exhibentur duo.

1. *Dicebat autem et parabolam ad illos* etc. Post confutationem Iudaicae curiositatis in perscrutando futura subditur hic *persuasio studiositatis* in postulando divina suffragia[^p448-3]. Et quoniam oratio studiosa est oratio Deo placita, in qua simul est fervor affectionis et humilitas reputationis; ideo pars ista habet duas: in quarum prima exemplo parabolico inducit ad *orationis instantiam;* in secunda vero, ad *orationis reverentiam,* ibi[^p448-4]: *Dixit autem ad quosdam* etc.

Ad *orationis* igitur *instantiam* movet introducendo parabolam de *iudice et vidua;* circa cuius descriptionem primo introducitur *ratio* parabolae introducendae; secundo additur *introductio ipsius parabolae,* ibi[^p448-5]: *Iudex quidam erat;* tertio subdit *aptationem introductae,* ibi: *Ait autem Dominus.*

2. (Vers. 1.). Primo igitur quantum ad *rationem introducendi parabolam* dicit: *Dicebat autem et parabolam ad illos.* Notandum est autem, quod haec parabola non est intelligenda per metaphoram *similitudinis,* quia iudex civitatis nullo modo Deo assimilabatur, qui rogandus est[^p448-6]; sed potius per *dissimilitudinem* et per contrarium intelligenda est, ut quasi *a minori* Dominus manuducat ad cognoscendam efficaciam orationis; unde hic non comparatur persona personae, sed negotium negotio. Et hoc est quod Beda dicit in Glossa[^p448-7]: « Nota, quod Dominus parabolas ponit vel secundum similitudinem, ut de homine, qui habuit duos filios, qui personam Dei gerit; vel secundum dissimilitudinem, ut de iniquo iudice, qui personam Dei nullatenus tenet ». — Introducit ergo hanc parabolam ad persuadendam orationis instantiam; propter quod addit: *Quoniam oportet orare semper et non deficere;* quod debet intelligi, ut *semper* non distribuat pro omni parte temporis, sed pro horis statutis, iuxta quod secundum institutionem propheticam dicitur in Psalmo[^p448-8]: « Septies in die laudem dixi ». Et hunc modum observat Ecclesia; unde Glossa: « *Semper,* canonicis horis quotidie secundum morem Ecclesiae ». — Vel *semper orare* hic intelligitur, ut oratio semper vel sit in *ore* per postulationem, vel sit in *corde* per desiderium, vel sit in *opere* per praeparationem. Qui enim bona agit ad hoc se disponit, ut eius oratio debeat audiri; et secundum hoc dicitur primae ad Thessalonicenses quinto[^p448-9]: « Sine intermissione orate »; ibi Glossa: « Semper orat qui semper bene agit; ipsum enim desiderium bonum oratio est; et si continuum est desiderium, continua est oratio ». Et hoc est quod hic dicit Beda in Glossa[^p448-10]: « Semper orat qui semper agit bona, nec desinit orare, nisi cum desinit iustus esse »; ideo Ecclesiastici decimo octavo: « Non impediaris orare semper, et non veteris usque ad mortem iustificari »; et vigesimo nono: « Conclude eleemosynam in sinu pauperis, et ipsa orabit pro te » etc. Hoc autem intelligitur de *opportunitate necessitatis.* Si autem intelligatur de *opportunitate congruitatis,* tunc oportet *semper orare,* id est frequentissime et instantissime ad orationem recurrere. Unde Hieronymus[^p448-11]: « Frequens oratio
<!-- page 449 -->
vitiorum impugnationem exstinguit »; instans vero oratio impetrat veniam de peccatis, secundum illud Ecclesiastici tertio: « Qui diligit Deum exorabit pro peccatis et in oratione dierum exaudietur » etc.

3. Et ideo utile est orare semper propter decem, videlicet quia impetrat *relaxationem divinae sententiae,* sicut apparet in Ezechia Isaiae trigesimo octavo[^p449-1]: « Oravit Ezechias Dominum et dixit: Obsecro, Domine, memento, quaeso, quomodo ambulaverim coram te »; et post subditur, quod Dominus revocavit sententiam de morte; et Sapientiae decimo octavo: « Properans homo sine querela deprecari pro populis, proferens servitutis suae scutum orationem, et per incensum deprecationem allegans, restitit irae ». — *Remissionem culpae perpetratae;* Matthaei decimo septimo[^p449-2]: « Hoc genus daemonii non eiicitur nisi per orationem et ieiunium »; super quo loco Hieronymus: « Ieiunio sanantur pestes corporis, oratione pestes mentis »; Psalmus: « Dixi: Confitebor adversum me iniustitiam meam Domino, et tu remisisti impietatem peccati mei »; et post: « Pro hac orabit ad te omnis sanctus » etc. — *Repulsionem spiritualis pugnae;* Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p449-3]: « Vigilate et orate, ne intretis in tentationem »; et primi Machabaeorum quarto: « Tuba cecinerunt hi qui fuerunt cum Iuda, et contritae sunt gentes et fugerunt » etc. — *Reparationem virtutis naturae;* Iacobi quinto[^p449-4]: Oratio fidei salvabit infirmum et alleviabit eum » etc.; et Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo: « Fili, in infirmitate tua ne despicias te ipsum, sed ora Dominum, et ipse curabit te ». — *Conservationem pacis in tempore;* primae ad Timotheum secundo[^p449-5]: « Obsecro, primo omnium fieri obsecrationes, orationes, postulationes, gratiarum actiones, ut quietam et tranquillam vitam agamus in omni pietate et castitate » etc. — *Custoditionem tranquillitatis conscientiae;* ad Philippenses quarto[^p449-6]: « Nihil solliciti sitis, sed in omni oratione et obsecratione petitiones vestrae innotescant apud Deum. Et pax Dei, quae exsuperat omnem sensum, custodiat » etc. — *Inflammationem divinae gratiae;* in cuius figuram dicitur de Elia tertii Regum decimo octavo[^p449-7]: « Invocabo nomen Domini mei, et Deus, qui exaudierit per ignem, ipse sit Deus ». Unde Actuum primo dicitur, quod « perseverantes erant unanimiter in oratione »; et post: « Cum complerentur dies Pentecostes, apparuerunt illis dispertitae linguae » etc. — *Illustrationem supernae sapientiae;* cuius exemplum habetur in Salomone tertii Regum tertio[^p449-8], ubi dicitur, quod oravit et petiit sapientiam, quam et obtinuit; et Iacobi primo: « Si quis vestrum indiget sapientia, postulet a Deo, qui dat omnibus affluenter ». — *Adaperitionem caelestis ianuae,* quae virtute orationis clauditur malis et aperitur bonis; Iacobi quinto[^p449-9]: « Elias erat homo similis nobis, passibilis, in oratione oravit, ut non plueret super terram, et non pluit annos tres et menses sex. Et rursus oravit, et caelum dedit pluviam ». — *Acquisitionem mercedis aeternae;* Matthaei sexto[^p449-10]: « Tu autem, cum oraveris, intra in cubiculum tuum, et clauso ostio, ora Patrem tuum in abscondito, et Pater tuus, qui videt in abscondito, reddet tibi »; Psalmus: « Quoniam tu, Deus meus, exaudisti orationem meam; dedisti hereditatem timentibus nomen tuum »; et Ecclesiastici ultimo: « Laudabo nomen Domini assidue et collaudabo illud in confessione, et exaudita est oratio mea ». — In figuram autem harum decem efficaciarum sol ad orationem Ezechiae legitur decem gradibus retrocessisse, quarti Regum vigesimo[^p449-11]. Ex quibus colligitur, quod oratio praevalet naturae inferiori, praevalet virtuti caeli, imperat naturae spirituali, attrahit Spiritum sanctum, inclinat Dei Filium et mitigat Patrem aeternum, ac per hoc quodam modo dominium habet respectu universorum.

4. (Vers. 2.). *Iudex*[^p449-12] *quidam erat* etc. Post rationem parabolae introducendae subditur hic *introductio ipsius parabolae;* circa cuius expressionem tria introducuntur, scilicet *duritia iudicis, instantia pauperis* et *efficacia precis.*

Primo quantum ad *duritiam iudicis* dicit: *Iudex quidam erat in quadam civitate, qui Deum non timebat,* propter duritiam cordis, quae facit Deum non timere, secundum illud Isaiae sexagesimo tertio[^p449-13]: « Indurasti cor nostrum, ne timeremus te ». Tales obdurati Deum non timent, sed potius contemnunt; in quorum persona Iob vigesimo primo[^p449-14]: Quis est Omnipotens, ut serviamus ei »? et vigesimo secundo: « Quasi nihil posset Omnipotens, aestimabant eum ». Tales sunt qui dicunt illud Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo: « Manus nostra excelsa, et
<!-- page 450 -->
non Dominus, fecit haec omnia ». — Et quia ad duritiam concomitatur superbia, ideo addit: *Et hominem*[^p450-1] *non reverebatur,* propter arrogantiam mentis. Unde hic de numero illorum fuit, de quibus Sapientiae secundo[^p450-2]: « Opprimamus pauperem iustum et non parcamus viduae nec veterani revereamur canos multi temporis ». Tali dicit Isaias trigesimo tertio: « Vae! qui praedaris; nonne et ipse praedaberis? Et qui spernis, nonne et ipse sperneris? Cum consummaveris depraedationem, depraedaberis, et cum fatigatus desieris contemnere, contemneris ».

5. (Vers. 3.). Secundo quantum ad *instantiam pauperis* subdit: *Vidua autem quaedam erat in civitate illa; vidua* scilicet destituta solatio viri; et talibus maxime est *subveniendum,* secundum illud Isaiae primo[^p450-3]: « Quaerite iudicium, subvenite oppresso, iudicate pupillo et defendite viduam ». Talibus magis *debet fieri iustitia,* quia minus se possunt defendere et frequentius opprimuntur; et ideo Ecclesiastici quarto[^p450-4]: « Libera eum qui iniuriam patitur, de manu superbi ». « In iudicando esto pupillis misericors ut pater et pro viro matri illorum; et eris velut filius Altissimi obediens, et miserebitur tui magis quam mater ». — De hac igitur vidua indigente iudicis patrocinio propter oppressionem malorum subdit: *Et veniebat ad eum dicens: Vindica me de adversario meo,* per districtionem iudicii iusti; et hoc quidem iustum erat tali personae; Ecclesiastici vigesimo primo[^p450-5]: « Deprecatio pauperis ex ore usque ad aures perveniet, et iudicium festinato adveniet illi », scilicet a Deo; et trigesimo quinto: « Deprecationem laesi exaudiet; nec despiciet preces pupilli nec viduam, si effundat loquelam gemitus. Nonne lacrymae viduae ad maxillam descendunt, et exclamatio eius super deducentem eas? A maxilla enim ascendunt usque ad caelum ». Et tamen non perveniunt usque ad cor hominis induratum.

6. (Vers. 4.). Unde subdit: *Et nolebat per multum tempus*[^p450-6], propter perfidiam animi obdurati; unde hic erat de numero illorum, de quibus Isaiae primo[^p450-7]: « Principes tui infideles, socii furum. Omnes diligunt munera, sequuntur retributiones. Pupillo non iudicant, et causa viduae non ingreditur ad illos »; et Ieremiae quinto: « Causam viduae non iudicaverunt, causam pupilli non direxerunt » etc.

7. Tertio quantum ad *efficaciam precis* subdit: *Post haec autem dixit intra se: Etsi Deum non timeo nec hominem vereor*[^p450-8], per audaciam cordis, propter quam similis erat illi superbo, de quo Iob decimo quinto: « Cucurrit adversus Deum erecto collo et pingui cervice armatus est ». Unde et de numero illorum erat, de quibus in Psalmo: « Contritio et infelicitas in viis eorum » etc.

8. (Vers. 5.). *Tamen, quia molesta est mihi haec vidua,* per instantiam precis, quae omnia vincit, secundum illud Iudicum decimo quarto[^p450-9] de uxore Samsonis, quae « septem diebus convivii flebat apud eum, tandemque die septimo, cum ei esset molesta, exposuit ». Propter quod supra undecimo: « Et ille deintus respondeat: Noli mihi molestus esse, quia pueri mei mecum sunt in cubili ». « Et si ille perseveraverit pulsans, dico vobis, etsi non dabit illi surgens, eo quod amicus eius sit, propter improbitatem tamen eius surget et dabit illi quotquot habet necessarios ». — Sic et iudex iste improbitate vincitur; unde addit: *Vindicabo illam,* secundum exigentiam sui iuris; hoc enim est officium iudicis, ut relevet oppressos et puniat opprimentes; Numerorum trigesimo primo[^p450-10]: « Locutus est Dominus ad Moysen: Ulciscere prius filios Israel de Madianitis, et sic colligeris ad patres tuos ». Hoc non debet iudex facere ex odio personae, sed ex amore iustitiae, sicut Mathathias, de quo primi Machabaeorum secundo: « Accensus est furor eius secundum iudicium Legis, et insiliens trucidavit hominem illum ». — Hic autem iudex non exercebat vindictam propter zelum legis, sed propter vitandum taedium precis; et ideo subdit: *Ne in novissimo veniens sugillet me,* per frequentiam clamoris. Beda[^p450-11]: « *In novissimo,* id est, ne iterum et iterum veniens strangulet me taediosis clamoribus ». Unde Glossa dicit, quod « *sugillo* a *sugo,* tractum a lamiis, id est strangulo ». Lamiae enim
<!-- page 451 -->
parvulos occidunt et sanguinem sugunt, ac per hoc suffocant et strangulando occidunt. Dicitur igitur *sugillari* qui verbis et rationibus[^p451-1] tanto taedio afficitur, quod prae taedio sanguis sugitur et diminuitur. Unde sensus est: sugillet me, id est, sanguinem meum sugat, sicut lamiae, de quibus Threnorum quarto[^p451-2]: « Lamiae nudaverunt mammas, lactaverunt catulos suos ».

9. (Vers. 6.). *Ait autem Dominus* etc. Post rationem parabolae introducendae et introductionem ipsius parabolae sequitur hic *adaptatio introductae*[^p451-3]; circa quam tria introducuntur. *Excitatur* enim *humana audientia, erigitur fiducia* et *arguitur diffidentia.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *excitationem audientiae* dicitur: *Ait autem Dominus: Audite, quid iudex iniquitatis dicit,* id est, considerate huius iudicis responsionem; in qua considerandum est quidem, quantae sit efficaciae orationis instantia, quia instantia peperit molestiam, et molestia extorsit iustitiam. Unde licet haberet cor lapideum, motus tamen est propter frequentiam precum, ut verificetur in illo illud poeticum[^p451-4]:

> « Gutta cavat lapidem, non vi, sed saepe cadendo ».

Licet esset in eo iniquitas, compulsus est tamen ad reddendam iustitiam per viduae importunam instantiam; ex quo intelligimus, quodsi instantia precis emollivit iudicem durissimum et inclinavit iniustissimum ad faciendum iudicium; quod multo fortius inclinabit Deum pium et iustum. Unde Chrysostomus[^p451-5]: « Non iudex talis, fide dilectionis carens, taedio compulsus, nec actio eius hic laudari potest; sed, si iste quocumque animo ultus est viduam, Deus iudex maxime ulciscetur suorum iniuriam ». Ex quo miro modo instantis orationis ostenditur efficacia, quia ille qui nec Deum timebat nec hominis curabat verecundiam nec amabat iustitiam, victus est a vidua per precum instantiam importunam.

10. (Vers. 7.). Secundo quantum ad *erectionem humanae fiduciae* subdit: *Deus autem non faciet vindictam electorum suorum,* ex districtione sui iudicii; ad Romanos duodecimo[^p451-6]: « Mihi vindictam, et ego retribuam »; et Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo: « Mea est ultio, et ego retribuam illis in tempore, ut labatur pes eorum ». — *Clamantium ad se die ac nocte,* scilicet ex afflictione animi; Apocalypsis sexto[^p451-7]: « Vidi subtus altare Dei animas interfectorum propter Verbum Dei, et clamabant voce magna dicentes: Usquequo, Domine, sanctus et verus, non iudicas et non vindicas sanguinem nostrum de his qui habitant in terra » etc.; immo, ipsis ore tacentibus, tribulationes eorum clamant; Genesis quarto[^p451-8]: « Vox sanguinis fratris tui clamat ad me de terra ». — *Et patientiam habebit in illis?* per remissionem zeli; quasi dicat: non; quia Ecclesiastici trigesimo quinto[^p451-9]: « Dominus iudicabit iustos et faciet iudicium, et Fortissimus non habebit in illis patientiam, ut contribulet dorsum illorum ». Hoc autem dicitur, non quia Dominus non exspectat peccantes, sed quia, iudicio imminente, iam amplius nolit exspectare; ad Romanos secundo: « An divitias bonitatis eius et patientiae et longanimitatis contemnis »? « Secundum duritiam autem tuam et cor impoenitens thesaurizas tibi iram in die irae et revelationis iusti iudicii Dei, qui reddet unicuique iuxta opera sua, his qui secundum patientiam boni operis, gloriam et honorem et incorruptionem; his autem, qui sunt ex contentione, qui non acquiescunt veritati, credunt autem iniquitati, ira et indignatio, tribulatio et angustia in omnem animam operantis malum ».

11. (Vers. 8.). *Dico vobis, quia cito faciet*[^p451-10] *vindictam illorum;* et hoc per ultionem peccati; Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo: « Sanguinem servorum suorum ulciscetur », « et vindictam retribuet in hostes eorum, et propitius erit terrae populi sui »; et Ecclesiastici trigesimo quinto: « Gentibus reddet vindictam, donec tollat plenitudinem superborum; et sceptra iniquorum contribulet, donec reddat hominibus secundum actus suos et secundum opera Adae et secundum praesumtionem, donec iudicet iudicium plebis suae ». — Sed nunquid Sanctorum
<!-- page 452 -->
est clamare ad Deum pro vindicta, cum dicatur Ecclesiastici vigesimo octavo[^p452-1]: « Qui vindicari vult a Domino inveniet vindictam, et peccata eius servans servabit »? — Respondeo: Est *vindicta*[^p452-2] *afflictionis facientis ad Deum redire,* vel *facientis a stultitia cessare.* Possunt appetere primam absolute, secundam, si Deo placet. *Vindictam* autem *afflictionis personae* debent horrere et contra *vindictam damnationis* aeternae debent interpellare.

12. Tertio quantum ad *argutionem diffidentiae* subdit: *Verumtamen Filius hominis veniens, putas, inveniet fidem in terra?* Hoc quidem dicit, quia, licet *fides credulitatis* in multis videatur esse, *fides fidelitatis* et *confidentiae* reperitur in paucis; Proverbiorum vigesimo[^p452-3]: « Multi homines misericordes vocantur, virum autem fidelem quis inveniet »? et Ieremiae septimo: « Periit fides et ablata est de ore eorum ». Et hoc maxime erit, approximante adventu Filii Dei; Matthaei vigesimo quarto[^p452-4]: « Quoniam abundabit iniquitas, refrigescet caritas multorum »; et Isaiae vigesimo quarto: « Haec erunt in medio terrae, in medio populorum, quomodo si paucae olivae, quae remanserunt, excutiantur ex olea, et racemi, cum fuerit finita vindemia ». — Et nota, quod Dominus dicit *putas,* non quia dubitet, sed quia dubitationem reprobat et condemnat. Unde Glossa[^p452-5]: « *Putas?* In verbo dubitationis infidelitas increpatur, non opinatur Divinitas; scit enim omnia per quem facta sunt omnia; sed dubitando increpat infidelium corda ». Unde illa putativa Domini inquisitio designat dubitationem et nutationem, quae erit in tribulatione illa finaliter imminente, ob quam non erit qui consurgat et teneat eum, ne consurgat ad delendam terram, secundum illud Isaiae sexagesimo quarto[^p452-6]: « Non est qui invocet nomen tuum, qui consurgat et teneat te; abscondisti faciem tuam a nobis et allisisti nos in manu iniquitatis nostrae ».

## English
<!-- page 448 -->

### Chapter XVIII.

#### On the persuasion of studiousness two things are set forth.

1. *And he spoke also a parable to them* etc. After the confutation of the Jewish curiosity in searching out things to come there is subjoined here *the persuasion of studiousness* in asking the divine suffrages[^p448-3]. And since studious prayer is prayer pleasing to God, in which there is at once fervour of affection and humility of self-esteem; therefore this part has two parts: in the first of which, by a parabolic example, he leads to *the instance of prayer;* in the second, to *the reverence of prayer,* there[^p448-4]: *And he spoke to some* etc.

To *the instance* then *of prayer* he moves by introducing the parable of *the judge and the widow;* concerning whose description first is introduced *the reason* of introducing the parable; second is added *the introduction of the parable itself,* there[^p448-5]: *There was a certain judge;* third he subjoins *the application of what has been introduced,* there: *And the Lord said.*

2. (Vers. 1.). First, then, as to *the reason of introducing the parable* he says: *And he spoke also a parable to them.* But it is to be noted that this parable is not to be understood by the metaphor of *likeness,* because the judge of the city was in no way likened to God, who is to be entreated[^p448-6]; but rather it is to be understood by *unlikeness* and by the contrary, so that the Lord, as it were *from the lesser,* leads by the hand to knowing the efficacy of prayer; whence here person is not compared to person, but business to business. And this is what Bede says in the Gloss[^p448-7]: « Note that the Lord sets down parables either according to likeness, as of the man who had two sons, who bears the person of God; or according to unlikeness, as of the unjust judge, who in no wise holds the person of God ». — He introduces, then, this parable to persuade the instance of prayer; on account of which he adds: *That we ought always to pray and not to faint;* which ought to be understood so that *always* does not distribute for every part of time, but for the appointed hours, according to what is said in the Psalm, following the prophetic institution[^p448-8]: « Seven times a day I have given praise ». And this manner the Church observes; whence the Gloss: « *Always,* at the canonical hours daily according to the custom of the Church ». — Or *always to pray* is understood here so that prayer is always either in the *mouth* through asking, or in the *heart* through desire, or in the *work* through preparation. For he who does good things disposes himself to this, that his prayer ought to be heard; and according to this it is said in the first to the Thessalonians, the fifth chapter[^p448-9]: « Pray without ceasing »; there the Gloss: « He prays always who always does well; for good desire itself is prayer; and if the desire is continual, the prayer is continual ». And this is what Bede says here in the Gloss[^p448-10]: « He prays always who always does good things, and he does not cease to pray, unless when he ceases to be just »; therefore Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter: « Let nothing hinder thee from praying always, and be not forbidden to be justified even to death »; and the twenty-ninth: « Shut up alms in the bosom of the poor, and it shall pray for thee » etc. But this is understood of *the opportunity of necessity.* But if it is understood of *the opportunity of congruity,* then we ought *always to pray,* that is, to have recourse to prayer most frequently and most instantly. Whence Jerome[^p448-11]: « Frequent prayer
<!-- page 449 -->
extinguishes the assault of the vices »; but instant prayer obtains pardon for sins, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter: « He that loveth God shall obtain pardon for his sins, and shall be heard in the prayer of days » etc.

3. And therefore it is profitable to pray always on account of ten things, namely because it obtains *the relaxation of the divine sentence,* as appears in Ezechias, in Isaias, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p449-1]: « Ezechias prayed to the Lord and said: I beseech thee, O Lord, remember, I pray thee, how I have walked before thee »; and afterwards it is subjoined that the Lord revoked the sentence of death; and Wisdom, the eighteenth chapter: « A blameless man, making haste to pray for the peoples, bringing forth the shield of his service, prayer, and by incense alleging supplication, withstood the wrath ». — *The remission of guilt committed;* Matthew, the seventeenth chapter[^p449-2]: « This kind is not cast out but by prayer and fasting »; on which place Jerome: « By fasting the plagues of the body are healed, by prayer the plagues of the mind »; the Psalm: « I said: I will confess against myself my injustice to the Lord, and thou hast forgiven the wickedness of my sin »; and afterwards: « For this shall every one that is holy pray to thee » etc. — *The repulse of the spiritual fight;* Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p449-3]: « Watch ye and pray, that ye enter not into temptation »; and the first of Machabees, the fourth chapter: « They that were with Judas sounded the trumpet, and the Gentiles were crushed and fled » etc. — *The repair of the power of nature;* James, the fifth chapter[^p449-4]: The prayer of faith shall save the sick man and shall lighten him » etc.; and Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter: « My son, in thy sickness despise not thyself, but pray to the Lord, and he shall heal thee ». — *The preservation of peace in time;* the first to Timothy, the second chapter[^p449-5]: « I beseech, first of all, that supplications, prayers, intercessions, thanksgivings be made, that we may lead a quiet and a peaceable life in all piety and chastity » etc. — *The guarding of the tranquillity of conscience;* to the Philippians, the fourth chapter[^p449-6]: « Be nothing solicitous, but in every prayer and supplication let your petitions be made known to God. And the peace of God, which surpasseth all understanding, keep » etc. — *The inflaming of divine grace;* in figure of which it is said of Elias in the third of Kings, the eighteenth chapter[^p449-7]: « I will call on the name of my Lord, and the God that shall answer by fire, let him be God ». Whence in the first of the Acts it is said that « they were persevering with one mind in prayer »; and afterwards: « When the days of the Pentecost were accomplished, there appeared to them parted tongues » etc. — *The illumination of supernal wisdom;* of which an example is had in Solomon, the third of Kings, the third chapter[^p449-8], where it is said that he prayed and asked for wisdom, which he also obtained; and James, the first chapter: « If any of you want wisdom, let him ask of God, who giveth to all abundantly ». — *The opening of the heavenly gate,* which by the power of prayer is shut to the wicked and opened to the good; James, the fifth chapter[^p449-9]: « Elias was a man passible like unto us, in prayer he prayed that it might not rain upon the earth, and it rained not for three years and six months. And he prayed again, and the heaven gave rain ». — *The acquisition of the eternal reward;* Matthew, the sixth chapter[^p449-10]: « But thou, when thou shalt pray, enter into thy chamber, and having shut the door, pray to thy Father in secret, and thy Father, who seeth in secret, will repay thee »; the Psalm: « For thou, my God, hast heard my prayer; thou hast given an inheritance to them that fear thy name »; and Ecclesiasticus, the last chapter: « I will praise the name of the Lord continually, and will praise it in confession, and my prayer was heard ». — And in figure of these ten efficacies the sun is read to have gone back ten degrees at the prayer of Ezechias, the fourth of Kings, the twentieth chapter[^p449-11]. From which it is gathered that prayer prevails over the inferior nature, prevails over the power of heaven, commands the spiritual nature, draws the Holy Spirit, bows the Son of God and appeases the eternal Father, and through this in a certain manner has dominion with respect to all things.

4. (Vers. 2.). *There was*[^p449-12] *a certain judge* etc. After the reason of introducing the parable there is subjoined here *the introduction of the parable itself;* concerning whose expression three things are introduced, namely *the hardness of the judge, the instance of the poor woman* and *the efficacy of entreaty.*

First, as to *the hardness of the judge* he says: *There was a certain judge in a certain city, who feared not God,* on account of the hardness of heart which makes one not fear God, according to that of Isaias, the sixty-third chapter[^p449-13]: « Thou hast hardened our heart, that we should not fear thee ». Such hardened men do not fear God, but rather despise him; in whose person Job, the twenty-first chapter[^p449-14]: Who is the Almighty, that we should serve him »? and the twenty-second: « As if the Almighty could do nothing, they esteemed him ». Such are those who say that of Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter: « Our mighty hand, and
<!-- page 450 -->
not the Lord, hath done all these things ». — And because pride accompanies hardness, therefore he adds: *And regarded not man*[^p450-1], on account of the arrogance of mind. Whence this man was of the number of those of whom Wisdom, the second chapter[^p450-2]: « Let us oppress the poor just man, and not spare the widow, nor honour the ancient grey hairs of the aged ». To such a one Isaias says, the thirty-third chapter: « Woe to thee that spoilest; shalt not thou thyself also be spoiled? And thou that despisest, shalt not thyself also be despised? When thou shalt have made an end of spoiling, thou shalt be spoiled, and when being wearied thou shalt cease to despise, thou shalt be despised ».

5. (Vers. 3.). Second, as to *the instance of the poor woman* he subjoins: *And there was a certain widow in that city; a widow,* namely, destitute of the solace of a husband; and such are most of all *to be relieved,* according to that of Isaias, the first chapter[^p450-3]: « Seek judgement, relieve the oppressed, judge for the fatherless, defend the widow ». To such *justice ought* the more *to be done,* because they are less able to defend themselves and are more frequently oppressed; and therefore Ecclesiasticus, the fourth chapter[^p450-4]: « Deliver him that suffereth wrong out of the hand of the proud ». « In judging be merciful to the fatherless as a father, and instead of a husband to their mother; and thou shalt be as the obedient son of the most High, and he will have mercy on thee more than a mother ». — Of this widow, then, needing the patronage of the judge on account of the oppression of the wicked, he subjoins: *And she came to him, saying: Avenge me of my adversary,* through the strictness of a just judgement; and this indeed was just for such a person; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-first chapter[^p450-5]: « The prayer of the poor out of the mouth shall reach to the ears, and judgement shall come for him speedily », namely from God; and the thirty-fifth: « He will hear the prayer of him that is wronged; he will not despise the prayers of the fatherless, nor the widow, when she poureth out the speech of her groaning. Do not the widow's tears run down the cheek, and her cry against him that causeth them to fall? For from the cheek they go up even to heaven ». And yet they do not reach to the hardened heart of man.

6. (Vers. 4.). Whence he subjoins: *And he would not for a long time*[^p450-6], on account of the perfidy of an obdurate mind; whence this man was of the number of those of whom Isaias, the first chapter[^p450-7]: « Thy princes are faithless, companions of thieves. They all love bribes, they run after rewards. They judge not for the fatherless, and the widow's cause cometh not in to them »; and Jeremias, the fifth chapter: « They have not judged the cause of the widow, they have not managed the cause of the fatherless » etc.

7. Third, as to *the efficacy of entreaty* he subjoins: *But afterwards he said within himself: Although I fear not God, nor regard man*[^p450-8], through boldness of heart, on account of which he was like that proud man of whom Job, the fifteenth chapter: « He hath run against God with his neck raised up, and is armed with a fat neck ». Whence he was also of the number of those of whom it is said in the Psalm: « Destruction and unhappiness in their ways » etc.

8. (Vers. 5.). *Yet because this widow is troublesome to me,* through the instance of entreaty, which overcomes all things, according to that of Judges, the fourteenth chapter[^p450-9], of Samson's wife, who « wept before him the seven days of the feast, and at length on the seventh day, as she was troublesome to him, he told her ». On account of which, above, the eleventh chapter: « And he from within should answer: Trouble me not, for my children are with me in bed ». « Yet if he shall continue knocking, I say to you, although he will not rise and give him, because he is his friend, yet because of his importunity he will rise and give him as many as he needeth ». — So also this judge is overcome by importunity; whence he adds: *I will avenge her,* according to the exigence of his own right; for this is the office of a judge, to relieve the oppressed and to punish the oppressors; Numbers, the thirty-first chapter[^p450-10]: « The Lord spoke to Moses: Revenge first the children of Israel on the Madianites, and so thou shalt be gathered to thy fathers ». This a judge ought not to do out of hatred of the person, but out of love of justice, as Mathathias, of whom the first of Machabees, the second chapter: « His wrath was kindled according to the judgement of the Law, and leaping upon him he slew that man ». — But this judge did not exercise vengeance on account of zeal for the law, but on account of avoiding the weariness of entreaty; and therefore he subjoins: *Lest at the last coming she throttle me,* through the frequency of her clamour. Bede[^p450-11]: « *At the last,* that is, lest coming again and again she strangle me with wearisome clamours ». Whence the Gloss says that « *sugillo* is from *sugo,* drawn from the lamias, that is, I strangle ». For the lamias
<!-- page 451 -->
kill little ones and suck their blood, and through this suffocate them and kill them by strangling. He is said, then, *to be throttled* who by words and reasons[^p451-1] is affected with so great weariness that for weariness his blood is sucked and diminished. Whence the sense is: lest she throttle me, that is, suck my blood, like the lamias, of whom Lamentations, the fourth chapter[^p451-2]: « The lamias have bared the breasts, they have suckled their young ».

9. (Vers. 6.). *And the Lord said* etc. After the reason of introducing the parable and the introduction of the parable itself there follows here *the application of what has been introduced*[^p451-3]; concerning which three things are introduced. For *human hearing is roused, trust is raised up* and *distrust is reproved.*

First, then, as to *the rousing of hearing* it is said: *And the Lord said: Hear what the unjust judge saith,* that is, consider the answer of this judge; in which it is to be considered indeed of how great efficacy the instance of prayer is, because instance brought forth trouble, and trouble wrung out justice. Whence although he had a heart of stone, yet he was moved on account of the frequency of entreaties, so that there might be verified in him that saying of the poet[^p451-4]:

> « The drop hollows the stone, not by force, but by often falling ».

Although there was iniquity in him, yet he was compelled to render justice through the importunate instance of the widow; from which we understand that, if the instance of entreaty softened a most hard judge and bowed a most unjust one to do judgement, much more strongly will it bow God, pious and just[^tr-pietas]. Whence Chrysostom[^p451-5]: « Neither such a judge, lacking the faith of dilection, compelled by weariness, nor his action can be praised here; but, if he with whatever mind avenged the widow, God the judge will most of all avenge the injury of his own ». From which in a wonderful manner is shown the efficacy of instant prayer, because he who neither feared God nor cared for the shamefastness of man nor loved justice was overcome by the widow through the importunate instance of entreaties.

10. (Vers. 7.). Second, as to *the raising up of human trust* he subjoins: *And will not God revenge his elect,* out of the strictness of his judgement; to the Romans, the twelfth chapter[^p451-6]: « Revenge to me, and I will repay »; and Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter: « Revenge is mine, and I will repay them in due time, that their foot may slide ». — *Who cry to him day and night,* namely out of affliction of mind; the Apocalypse, the sixth chapter[^p451-7]: « I saw under the altar of God the souls of them that were slain for the Word of God, and they cried with a loud voice, saying: How long, O Lord, holy and true, dost thou not judge and revenge our blood on them that dwell on the earth » etc.; nay more, while they are silent with the mouth, their tribulations cry out; Genesis, the fourth chapter[^p451-8]: « The voice of thy brother's blood crieth to me from the earth ». — *And will he have patience in their regard?* through remission of zeal; as if he were to say: no; because Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-fifth chapter[^p451-9]: « The Lord will judge the just and will do judgement, and the Most Mighty will not have patience with them, that he may crush their back ». But this is said, not because the Lord does not wait for sinners, but because, the judgement being imminent, he will no longer wait; to the Romans, the second chapter: « Or despisest thou the riches of his goodness and patience and longsuffering »? « But according to thy hardness and impenitent heart thou treasurest up to thyself wrath against the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgement of God, who will render to every man according to his works, to them who according to patience in good work, glory and honour and incorruption; but to them that are of contention, and who acquiesce not to the truth, but give credit to iniquity, wrath and indignation, tribulation and anguish upon every soul that worketh evil ».

11. (Vers. 8.). *I say to you, that he will quickly*[^p451-10] *revenge them;* and this through the revenging of sin; Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter: « He will revenge the blood of his servants », « and will render vengeance to their enemies, and he will be merciful to the land of his people »; and Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-fifth chapter: « He will render vengeance to the Gentiles, till he hath taken away the fulness of the proud and broken the sceptres of the unjust, till he have rendered to men according to their deeds, and according to the works of Adam, and according to his presumption, till he have judged the judgement of his people ». — But is it of the Saints
<!-- page 452 -->
to cry to God for vengeance, since it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p452-1]: « He that will be revenged shall find vengeance from the Lord, and he will surely keep his sins »? — I answer: There is *a vengeance*[^p452-2] *of affliction that makes one return to God,* or *that makes one cease from folly.* They can desire the first absolutely, the second, if it please God. But *the vengeance of affliction of the person* they ought to abhor, and against *the vengeance of* eternal *damnation* they ought to intercede.

12. Third, as to *the reproof of distrust* he subjoins: *But yet the Son of man coming, thinkest thou, shall he find faith on earth?* This indeed he says because, although *the faith of belief* may seem to be in many, *the faith of fidelity* and *of confidence* is found in few; Proverbs, the twentieth chapter[^p452-3]: « Many men are called merciful, but who shall find a faithful man »? and Jeremias, the seventh chapter: « Faith is lost and is taken away from their mouth ». And this will be most of all as the coming of the Son of God draws near; Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p452-4]: « Because iniquity shall abound, the charity of many shall grow cold »; and Isaias, the twenty-fourth chapter: « These things shall be in the midst of the earth, in the midst of the peoples, as if a few olives, that remain, should be shaken out of the olive tree, and grapes, when the vintage is ended ». — And note that the Lord says *thinkest thou,* not because he doubts, but because he reproves and condemns doubting. Whence the Gloss[^p452-5]: « *Thinkest thou?* In a word of doubting faithlessness is rebuked; the Godhead does not opine; for he knows all things, through whom all things were made; but by doubting he rebukes the hearts of the faithless ». Whence that supposing inquiry of the Lord designates the doubting and wavering which will be in that tribulation finally imminent, on account of which there will be none to rise up and take hold of him, lest he rise up to destroy the earth, according to that of Isaias, the sixty-fourth chapter[^p452-6]: « There is none that calleth upon thy name, that riseth up and taketh hold of thee; thou hast hid thy face from us, and hast crushed us in the hand of our iniquity ».
