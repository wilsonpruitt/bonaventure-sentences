# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XXI, Vers. 1-4
## *Informatio ad eligentiam meliorum per exemplum viduae*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 520 -->

### Capitulum XXI.

#### Informatio ad eligentiam meliorum per exemplum viduae.

1. (Vers. 1.). *Respiciens autem vidit* etc. Postquam informavit discipulos ad cautelam malorum, hic secundo[^p520-3] *informat eos ad eligentiam meliorum,* et hoc exemplo pauperculae viduae, offerentis totum victum suum, quae praefertur aliis, qui partem dederunt. In hac igitur parte procedit hoc ordine. Introducitur enim hic *munus multitudinis opulentae, oblatio viduae pauperculae. laus oblationis a vidua factae* et *assignatio rationis et causae.*

Primo ergo quantum ad *munus multitudinis opulentae* dicit: *Respiciens autem vidit eos qui mittebant munera sua in gazophylacium, divites.* Nota, quod *gazophylacium* dicitur a gazis, id est divitiis, et phylasse, quod est servare; inde *gazophylacium,* locus, ubi erat repositorium pecuniae[^p520-4]. Accipitur autem *gazophylacium* aliquando pro *loco,* in quo reponebatur, sicut Nehemiae decimo[^p520-5]: « Levitae offerent decimam partem decimae in domo Dei ad gazophylacium in domo thesauri ». Aliquando pro *domo levitarum et sacerdotum;* Ezechielis quadragesimo secundo[^p520-6]: « Eduxit me in atrium exterius et induxit in gazophylacium, quod erat contra aedificium separatum »; et post: « Haec sunt gazophylacia sancta, in quibus vescuntur sacerdotes ». Aliquando accipitur pro *porticu,* in quo fiebant oblationes, sicut Ioannis octavo[^p520-7]: « Haec verba locutus est Iesus in gazophylacio, docens in templo ». Aliquando accipitur pro *ipsa arca,* quae servabat oblationes, desuper habens foramen, sicut hic; unde Glossa[^p520-8]: « Gazophylacium est arca, habens foramen desuper, in qua mittebant sacerdotes pecunias, quae congregabantur ad necessarios usus templi ». Et haec arca alio nomine dicebatur *musach,* sicut dicitur quarti Regum decimo sexto[^p520-9]: « Musach quoque Sabbati convertit in templum Domini propter regem Assyriorum ». Et hoc alio nomine dicebatur *corbona,* sicut Matthaei vigesimo septimo: « Non licet eos mittere in corbonam, quia pretium sanguinis est ».

2. In hoc autem mittebantur oblationes duplici ex causa: vel ad luminaria concinnanda, vel ad sarta tecta templi reparanda, secundum illud quarti Regum duodecimo[^p520-10]: « Omnem pecuniam sanctorum, quae illata fuerit in templum Domini, accipiant illam sacerdotes; et instaurent sarta tecta domus »; et post: « Tulit Ioiada gazophylacium unum aperuitque foramen desuper et posuit illud iuxta altare, ad dexteram ingredientium, mittebantque in eo sacerdotes omnem pecuniam, quae deferebatur ad templum Domini ». Huiusmodi autem oblationes triplici ex causa fiebant, scilicet ad *reverentiam cultus divini,* propter quod secundi Machabaeorum tertio[^p520-11]: « Cum civitas habitaretur in omni pace, fiebat, ut ipsi reges et principes locum summo honore dignum ducerent et templum maximis muneribus illustrarent ». Et propter *indulgentiam peccati;* Numerorum trigesimo
<!-- page 521 -->
primo[^p521-1]: « Ob hanc causam offerimus in donariis domus Domini, ut depreceris pro nobis ad Dominum ». — Item pro *gratiarum actione;* unde primi Paralipomenon ultimo: « Laetatus est populus Israel, cum vota sponte promitteret » etc.

3. (Vers. 2.). Secundo quantum ad *oblationem viduae pauperculae* subdit: *Vidit autem et quandam viduam pauperculam mittentem duo aera minuta. Vidit,* inquam, oculo benignitatis, quo ad pauperes respicit, secundum illud Isaiae sexagesimo sexto[^p521-2]: « Ad quem respiciam nisi ad pauperculum et contritum spiritu et trementem sermones meos »? Talium audit Dominus preces; Psalmus: « Respexit in orationem humilium et non sprevit preces eorum »; et Ecclesiastici trigesimo quinto: « Nonne lacrymae viduae ad maxillam descendunt et a maxilla ascendunt usque ad caelum »? Talium suscipit quantumcumque parvas oblationes, quia, sicut dicit Glossa[^p521-3], « non perpendit Deus, quantum in sacrificio offertur, sed ex quanto detur ». Unde et Matthaei decimo: « Qui dederit uni ex minimis his calicem aquae frigidae, non perdet mercedem suam ». Ideo dicit Gregorius[^p521-4]: « Exteriora nostra quamlibet parva Deo sufficiunt; cor namque et non substantiam pensat »; et post: « Regnum caelorum tantum valet, quantum habes. Valuit namque Zachaeo dimidium bonorum, valuit Andreae et Petro dimissis retibus et navi, valuit viduae duobus minutis, valuit et alteri calicem aquae frigidae. Regnum itaque caelorum tantum valet, quantum habes ». — Et nota, quod in hoc quod dicit, quod misit duo aera minuta, simul *paupertatem* insinuat et *liberalitatem: paupertatem,* quia non habebat nisi duos obolos; *liberalitatem,* quia, cum posset alterum offerre, obtulit utrumque, ut impleret illud Tobiae quarto[^p521-5]: « Si exiguum tibi fuerit, etiam illud libenter impartiri stude ».

4. (Vers. 3.). Tertio quantum ad *laudem oblationis a vidua factae* adiungitur: *Et dixit*[^p521-6]*: Vere dico vobis, quia vidua haec pauper plus quam omnes misit,* id est, copiosius dedit. Copiosius namque donat qui ex maiori corde donat. Unde Gregorius[^p521-7]: « Non est vacua manus a munere, cum arca cordis repleta fuerit bona voluntate ». De numero enim illorum erat haec vidua, de quibus secundae ad Corinthios octavo: « Secundum virtutem testimonium perhibeo illis, quia supra virtutem voluntarii fuerunt ». Unde Chrysostomus[^p521-8]: « Nullus dicat: Non possum eleemosynas facere. Non es vidua illa pauperior, non es Petro et Ioanne rusticior. Talenta uniuscuiusque sunt sive praeeminentia in doctrina, sive in pecunia, sive in quolibet negotio »; et post: « Nihil est ita Deo amicum, sicut ad communem utilitatem vivere ». Propter quod etiam primae Petri quarto[^p521-9]: « Unusquisque, sicut accepit gratiam, in alterutrum illam administrantes ». Unde, sicut qui dimittit pauca in re et nihil vult habere in voluntate *omnia* dicitur dimittere, sicut Petrus, Matthaei decimo nono[^p521-10]: « Ecce, nos reliquimus omnia »; sicut qui nulli subiicitur *omnia* habet et abundat, quemadmodum Paulus, secundae ad Corinthios sexto: « Tanquam nihil habentes, et omnia possidentes »; et ad Philippenses ultimo: « Habeo omnia et abundo »: sic qui magno corde dat dicitur *abundanter dare,* secundum illud secundae ad Corinthios octavo[^p521-11]: « In multo experimento tribulationis abundantia gaudii ipsorum fuit, et altissima paupertas eorum abundavit in divitias simplicitatis ».

5. (Vers. 4.). Quarto quantum ad *assignationem rationis et causae* subiungit: *Nam omnes hi ex abundanti sibi miserunt in munera Dei;* et impleverunt *praeceptum* illud supra undecimo[^p521-12]: « Verumtamen quod superest date eleemosynam »; quo praecipitur dare *ex abundanti,* sicut Ioannes dicebat supra tertio: « Qui habet duas tunicas det unam non habenti ». — *Haec autem ex eo quod deest illi, omnem victum suum, quem habuit, misit;* et sic implevit *consilium,* quia nihil sibi reservavit, sed totum dedit, ut mulier evangelica esse inciperet, implens illud consilium Domini, supra decimo octavo[^p521-13]: « Si vis perfectus esse, vade et vende omnia, quae habes ». Iam non erat sollicita de crastino, sicut dicitur Matthaei sexto[^p521-14]: « Nolite solliciti esse in crastinum »; « sufficit diei malitia sua ».

6. Sed videtur haec vidua ex hac ratione non esse laudanda, sed potius vituperanda, quia *medium*[^p521-15] non servavit, quia *periculo* se exposuit,
<!-- page 522 -->
quia *ordinem caritatis* praetermisit, contra illud Apostoli: « Non ut aliis sit remissio, vobis autem tribulatio, sed ex aequalitate. In praesenti tempore vestra abundantia illorum inopiae sit supplementum », secundae ad Corinthios octavo. — Sed ideo divino ore laudatur, quia spem ponens in Christo et divinum implens consilium, quia[^p522-1] Dei cultum praeferens suae privatae utilitati. Licet secundum hominem non videatur fuisse discreta eius eleemosyna, prudentissima tamen fuit, dum totum dando facta est imitatrix Christi, de quo secundae ad Corinthios octavo[^p522-2]: « Scitis gratiam Domini nostri Iesu Christi, quoniam propter vos egenus factus est, cum esset dives, ut illius inopia vos divites essetis ». Ubi ergo Christus promittit stipendium, *non est periculum;* ubi dat exemplum, *non deseritur medium,* quia ipse est omnis perfectionis centrum; ubi approbat affectum, *caritatis ordo non perturbatur,* immo completur; quia, ut dicitur primae ad Corinthios decimo tertio[^p522-3], « caritas non quaerit quae sua sunt ». — Ex his colligitur, quod non est vitium totum relinquere, sed perfectum, quia radix merendi principaliter respicit cordis affectum, et quia magis acceptat Deus fervorem cordis quam magnitudinem muneris. In cuius signum dicitur Genesis quarto[^p522-4]: « Respexit Dominus ad Abel et ad munera eius, ad Cain autem et ad munera illius non respexit ». Et ideo contra eum qui est malae voluntatis, de eleemosyna confidentem dicitur Ecclesiastici septimo[^p522-5]: « Non alliges tibi duplicia peccata », et: « Ne dicas: In multitudine munerum meorum respiciet Deus, et offerente me Deo altissimo, suscipiet munera mea »; quia, Ecclesiastici trigesimo quarto, « dona iniquorum non probat Altissimus », sed, quod dicitur in Psalmo, « sacrificium Deo spiritus contribulatus, cor contritum et humiliatum », usque ibi: « altare tuum vitulos ».

7. Haec secundum litteram dicta sunt; secundum *moralem* autem *intellectum* per hanc *viduam pauperculam,* quam benignissime respicit Dominus Iesus, intelligitur *anima,* quae recedit ab amore temporalium; quae ideo *vidua* dicitur, quoniam, quando mundo adhaesit, habuit virum, nunc quasi *vidua* et *paupercula* est: *vidua* quidem, quia sequestrata est a mundo quantum ad *affectum carnalitatis;* Psalmus[^p522-6]: « Viduam eius benedicens benedicam, pauperes eius saturabo panibus ». Et haec vidua omnem suam delectationem ponit in Christo, secundum illud primae ad Timotheum quinto: « Quae vere vidua est et desolata speret in Deum et instet orationibus et obsecrationibus nocte ac die ». — Et haec *paupercula* est, quia sequestrata a mundo quantum ad *affectum terrenitatis;* de qua Isaiae quinquagesimo quarto[^p522-7]: « Paupercula tempestate convulsa, absque ulla consolatione ». Huiusmodi igitur est offerre Deo *duo aera minuta,* id est *mortificationem carnis,* in quantum est *vidua,* secundum illud Apostoli ad Romanos duodecimo[^p522-8]: « Obsecro vos per misericordiam Dei, ut exhibeatis corpora vestra hostiam viventem, sanctam, Deo placentem, rationabile obsequium vestrum »; et offerre *paupertatem spiritus,* in quantum est *paupercula;* primi Paralipomenon vigesimo secundo: « Ecce, in paupertate mea praeparavi impensas domus Domini ». De hoc duplici minuto offerendo secundae ad Corinthios septimo[^p522-9]: « Mundemus nos ab omni inquinamento carnis ac spiritus, perficientes sanctificationem in timore Dei ».

8. Secundum autem *allegoricum intellectum* per *divites* intelliguntur Iudaei; unde Glossa[^p522-10]: « Divites, qui in gazophylacium munera mittunt, Iudaei sunt, qui de iustitia Legis sunt elati », secundum illud Apostoli ad Romanos decimo: « Dei ignorantes iustitiam et suam volentes statuere, iustitiae Dei non sunt subiecti ». — Per *viduam pauperculam* intelligitur Ecclesia de gentibus congregata; unde Glossa[^p522-11]: « *Vidua,* Ecclesia, pro qua vir eius pertulit mortem et in caeli penetralibus, quasi ab oculis eius occultus, quasi in parte alterius regionis vivit ». Ideo ad Ephesios quinto[^p522-12]: « Viri, diligite uxores vestras, sicut et Christus dilexit Ecclesiam et se ipsum tradidit pro ea, ut illam sanctificaret, mundans eam lavacro aquae in verbo vitae »; et post: « Sacramentum hoc magnum est; ego autem dico in Christo et in Ecclesia ». Iudaeus ergo offert *ex abundantia* propter praesumtionem; unde Glossa[^p522-13]: « Iudaeus ex abundanti
<!-- page 523 -->
mittit, qui de sua iustitia praesumens orat ita apud se: Deus, *gratias ago tibi, quia non sum sicut ceteri hominum* », secundum illud supra decimo octavo, ubi agitur de Pharisaeo, qui designat populum Iudaeorum. Sed haec vidua offert *totum victum suum,* quia, ut dicit Glossa[^p523-1], « Ecclesia omnem victum in munera Dei mittit; quae omne, quod vivit, non sui meriti, sed divini muneris intelligit, dicens: *Deus, propitius esto mihi peccatori* ». *Offert* autem *duo minuta,* id est *fidem* et *caritatem,* vel *geminam fidem,* scilicet de *Divinitate* et *humanitate,* seu *geminam caritatem,* scilicet *Dei* et *proximi,* secundum illud Canticorum quarto[^p523-2]: « Omnes gemellis foetibus, et sterilis non est in eis ». Et haec *minuta* sunt propter humilitatem, sed Deo maxime accepta sunt; unde Glossa[^p523-3]: « Consideratione suae fragilitatis *minuta,* sed merito piae intentionis magis sunt accepta quam cuncta superbientium Iudaeorum opera », quia, Iacobi quarto[^p523-4], « Deus superbis resistit, humilibus autem dat gratiam ». — In hac igitur vidua commendatur sancta Ecclesia, commendatur nihilominus castitas, humilitas et paupertas, quae tria sunt consilia evangelica et perducunt animam ad perfectionem praecipuam, quae praecipue est a Christi discipulis eligenda.

## English
<!-- page 520 -->

### Chapter XXI.

#### The formation unto the choosing of better things through the example of the widow.

1. (Vers. 1.). *And looking on, he saw* etc. After he formed the disciples unto the caution of evils, here, second[^p520-3], *he forms them unto the choosing of better things,* and this by the example of the poor little widow offering all her living, who is preferred to the others, who gave a part. In this part, then, he proceeds in this order. For here are brought in *the gift of the opulent multitude, the offering of the poor little widow. the praise of the offering made by the widow* and *the assigning of the reason and cause.*

First, then, as to *the gift of the opulent multitude,* he says: *And looking on, he saw the rich men cast their gifts into the treasury.* Note that *gazophylacium* is said from *gazis,* that is, riches, and *phylasse,* which is to keep; hence *gazophylacium,* the place where the repository of money was[^p520-4]. Now *gazophylacium* is taken sometimes for the *place* in which it was laid up, as in Nehemias, the tenth chapter[^p520-5]: « The Levites shall offer the tithe of the tithe in the house of God, at the treasury in the house of the treasure ». Sometimes for the *house of the levites and priests;* Ezechiel, the forty-second chapter[^p520-6]: « He brought me out into the outward court, and he brought me into the treasury, which was over against the separate building »; and after: « These are the holy treasuries, in which the priests eat ». Sometimes it is taken for the *porch* in which the offerings were made, as in John, the eighth chapter[^p520-7]: « These words Jesus spoke in the treasury, teaching in the temple ». Sometimes it is taken for *the chest itself,* which kept the offerings, having a hole above, as here; whence the Gloss[^p520-8]: « The *gazophylacium* is a chest having a hole above, into which the priests put the moneys which were gathered for the necessary uses of the temple ». And this chest was called by another name *musach,* as is said in the fourth of Kings, the sixteenth chapter[^p520-9]: « The musach also for the sabbath he turned into the temple of the Lord, because of the king of the Assyrians ». And this was called by another name *corbona,* as in Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter: « It is not lawful to put them into the corbona, because it is the price of blood ».

2. Now into this the offerings were put for a twofold cause: either for the trimming of the lights, or for the repairing of the breaches of the temple, according to that of the fourth of Kings, the twelfth chapter[^p520-10]: « All the money of the holy things, which shall be brought into the temple of the Lord, let the priests take it; and let them repair the breaches of the house »; and after: « Joiada took a chest, and bored a hole in the top, and set it beside the altar, at the right hand of them that came in, and the priests put in it all the money that was brought to the temple of the Lord ». Now offerings of this kind were made for a threefold cause, namely, for *the reverence of divine worship,* on account of which the second of Machabees, the third chapter[^p520-11]: « When the city was inhabited with all peace, it came to pass that even the kings themselves and the princes esteemed the place worthy of the highest honour, and glorified the temple with very great gifts ». And for *the indulgence of sin;* Numbers, the thirty-
<!-- page 521 -->
first chapter[^p521-1]: « For this cause we offer as gifts to the house of the Lord, that thou mayst pray to the Lord for us ». — Likewise for *the giving of thanks;* whence the first of Paralipomenon, the last chapter: « The people of Israel rejoiced, when they promised their offerings willingly » etc.

3. (Vers. 2.). Second, as to *the offering of the poor little widow,* he subjoins: *And he saw also a certain poor little widow casting in two brass mites. He saw,* I say, with the eye of kindness, with which he looks upon the poor, according to that of Isaias, the sixty-sixth chapter[^p521-2]: « To whom shall I have respect, but to him that is poor and little, and of a contrite spirit, and that trembleth at my words »? The prayers of such the Lord hears; the Psalm: « He hath had regard to the prayer of the humble, and he hath not despised their petition »; and Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-fifth chapter: « Do not the widow's tears run down the cheek, and from the cheek go up even to heaven »? Of such he receives offerings however small, because, as the Gloss says[^p521-3], « God weighs not how much is offered in the sacrifice, but out of how much it is given ». Whence also Matthew, the tenth chapter: « Whosoever shall give to one of these least a cup of cold water, shall not lose his reward ». Therefore Gregory says[^p521-4]: « Our outward things, however small, suffice to God; for he weighs the heart and not the substance »; and after: « The kingdom of heaven is worth as much as thou hast. For it was worth to Zacchaeus the half of his goods, it was worth to Andrew and Peter the nets and the ship left behind, it was worth to the widow two mites, it was worth to another a cup of cold water. The kingdom of heaven, therefore, is worth as much as thou hast ». — And note that in this, that he says that she cast in two brass mites, he intimates at once *poverty* and *liberality: poverty,* because she had only two obols; *liberality,* because, though she could have offered the one, she offered both, that she might fulfil that of Tobias, the fourth chapter[^p521-5]: « If thou have little, take care even so to bestow that willingly ».

4. (Vers. 3.). Third, as to *the praise of the offering made by the widow,* it is added: *And he said*[^p521-6]*: Verily I say to you, that this poor widow hath cast in more than they all,* that is, she gave more copiously. For he gives more copiously who gives from a greater heart. Whence Gregory[^p521-7]: « The hand is not empty of a gift, when the chest of the heart has been filled with good will ». For this widow was of the number of those of whom the second to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter: « According to their power I bear them witness, that beyond their power they were willing ». Whence Chrysostom[^p521-8]: « Let no one say: I cannot do alms. Thou art not poorer than that widow, thou art not more rustic than Peter and John. The talents of each one are either pre-eminence in doctrine, or in money, or in any business whatever »; and after: « Nothing is so friendly to God as to live for the common utility ». On account of which also the first of Peter, the fourth chapter[^p521-9]: « As every man hath received grace, ministering the same one to another ». Whence, as he who leaves few things in fact and wills to have nothing in will is said to leave *all things,* as Peter, Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p521-10]: « Behold, we have left all things »; as he who is subject to no one has *all things* and abounds, as Paul, the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter: « As having nothing, and possessing all things »; and to the Philippians, the last chapter: « I have all, and abound »: so he who gives with a great heart is said *to give abundantly,* according to that of the second to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter[^p521-11]: « In much experience of tribulation they have had abundance of joy, and their very deep poverty hath abounded unto the riches of their simplicity ».

5. (Vers. 4.). Fourth, as to *the assigning of the reason and cause,* he subjoins: *For all these have of their abundance cast into the offerings of God;* and they fulfilled that *precept* above, the eleventh chapter[^p521-12]: « But yet that which remaineth, give alms »; by which it is commanded to give *of abundance,* as John said, above, the third chapter: « He that hath two coats, let him give to him that hath none ». — *But she of her want hath cast in all the living that she had;* and so she fulfilled the *counsel,* because she reserved nothing to herself, but gave the whole, that she might begin to be an evangelical woman, fulfilling that counsel of the Lord, above, the eighteenth chapter[^p521-13]: « If thou wilt be perfect, go and sell all whatever thou hast ». Now she was not solicitous for the morrow, as is said in Matthew, the sixth chapter[^p521-14]: « Be not solicitous for tomorrow »; « sufficient for the day is the evil thereof ».

6. But it seems that this widow, by this reason, is not to be praised but rather to be blamed, because she did not keep *the mean*[^p521-15], because she exposed herself to *peril,*
<!-- page 522 -->
because she passed over *the order of charity,* against that of the Apostle: « Not that others should be eased, and you burdened, but by an equality. In this present time let your abundance supply their want », the second to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter. — But therefore is she praised by the divine mouth, because, placing her hope in Christ and fulfilling the divine counsel, because[^p522-1] preferring the worship of God to her own private utility. Although according to man her alms does not seem to have been discreet, yet it was most prudent, since by giving the whole she was made an imitator of Christ, of whom the second to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter[^p522-2]: « You know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that for your sakes he became needy, whereas he was rich, that through his poverty you might be rich ». Where, then, Christ promises the wage, *there is no peril;* where he gives the example, *the mean is not deserted,* because he himself is the centre of all perfection; where he approves the affection, *the order of charity is not disturbed,* nay rather it is completed; because, as is said in the first to the Corinthians, the thirteenth chapter[^p522-3], « charity seeketh not her own ». — From these things it is gathered that it is not a vice to leave the whole, but a perfection, because the root of meriting principally regards the affection of the heart, and because God accepts the fervour of the heart more than the greatness of the gift. In sign of which it is said in Genesis, the fourth chapter[^p522-4]: « The Lord had respect to Abel and to his offerings, but to Cain and his offerings he had no respect ». And therefore against him who is of evil will, trusting in alms, it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the seventh chapter[^p522-5]: « Bind not two sins together », and: « Say not: God will have respect to the multitude of my gifts, and when I offer to the most high God, he will accept my offerings »; because, Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-fourth chapter, « the most High approveth not the gifts of the wicked », but, what is said in the Psalm, « a sacrifice to God is an afflicted spirit: a contrite and humbled heart », as far as there: « upon thy altar calves ».

7. These things have been said according to the letter; but according to the *moral understanding* by this *poor little widow,* on whom the Lord Jesus looks most kindly, is understood the *soul* which withdraws from the love of temporal things; which is therefore called a *widow* because, when it clung to the world, it had a husband, now it is as it were a *widow* and *poor little one:* a *widow* indeed, because it is sequestered from the world as to *the affection of carnality;* the Psalm[^p522-6]: « Blessing I will bless her widow: I will satisfy her poor with bread ». And this widow places all her delight in Christ, according to that of the first to Timothy, the fifth chapter: « She that is a widow indeed, and desolate, let her trust in God, and continue in supplications and prayers night and day ». — And she is a *poor little one,* because sequestered from the world as to *the affection of earthliness;* of whom Isaias, the fifty-fourth chapter[^p522-7]: « O poor little one, tossed with tempest, without all comfort ». Of this kind, then, is to offer to God *two brass mites,* that is, *the mortification of the flesh,* inasmuch as she is a *widow,* according to that of the Apostle to the Romans, the twelfth chapter[^p522-8]: « I beseech you by the mercy of God, that you present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, pleasing unto God, your reasonable service »; and to offer *poverty of spirit,* inasmuch as she is a *poor little one;* the first of Paralipomenon, the twenty-second chapter: « Behold, in my poverty I have prepared the charges of the house of the Lord ». Of this twofold mite to be offered the second to the Corinthians, the seventh chapter[^p522-9]: « Let us cleanse ourselves from all defilement of the flesh and of the spirit, perfecting sanctification in the fear of God ».

8. But according to the *allegorical understanding,* by the *rich* are understood the Jews; whence the Gloss[^p522-10]: « The rich, who cast gifts into the treasury, are the Jews, who are lifted up by the justice of the Law », according to that of the Apostle to the Romans, the tenth chapter: « Not knowing the justice of God, and seeking to establish their own, they have not submitted themselves to the justice of God ». — By the *poor little widow* is understood the Church gathered from the Gentiles; whence the Gloss[^p522-11]: « *The widow,* the Church, for whom her husband bore death, and, in the inner places of heaven, as though hidden from her eyes, lives as it were in the part of another region ». Therefore to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter[^p522-12]: « Husbands, love your wives, as Christ also loved the Church, and delivered himself up for it, that he might sanctify it, cleansing it by the laver of water in the word of life »; and after: « This is a great sacrament; but I speak in Christ and in the Church ». The Jew, then, offers *of abundance* on account of presumption; whence the Gloss[^p522-13]: « The Jew casts in of abundance,
<!-- page 523 -->
who, presuming on his own justice, prays thus with himself: God, *I give thee thanks that I am not as the rest of men* », according to that above, the eighteenth chapter, where it treats of the Pharisee, who designates the people of the Jews. But this widow offers *all her living,* because, as the Gloss says[^p523-1], « the Church casts all her living into the gifts of God; who understands all that she lives to be not of her own merit but of the divine gift, saying: *O God, be merciful to me a sinner* ». Now she *offers two mites,* that is, *faith* and *charity,* or *a twin faith,* namely, of the *Divinity* and the *humanity,* or *a twin charity,* namely, of *God* and of *neighbour,* according to that of the Canticle, the fourth chapter[^p523-2]: « All with twins, and there is none barren among them ». And these are *mites* on account of humility, but they are most acceptable to God; whence the Gloss[^p523-3]: « In consideration of her frailty *mites,* but by the merit of a pious intention more acceptable than all the works of the proud Jews », because, James, the fourth chapter[^p523-4], « God resisteth the proud, and giveth grace to the humble ». — In this widow, then, holy Church is commended; commended no less are chastity, humility and poverty, which three are the evangelical counsels and lead the soul to the chief perfection, which chiefly is to be chosen by the disciples of Christ.
