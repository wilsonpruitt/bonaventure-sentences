# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. V, Vers. 12-16
## *Et factum est, cum esset in una civitatum etc. Post discipulorum vocationem subiungit hic eorundem confirmationem per miracula*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 119 -->

24. *Et factum est, cum esset in una civitatum* etc. Post discipulorum vocationem subiungit hic eorundem *confirmationem per miracula.* Et habet haec pars duas. In prima enim introducitur miraculum de *curatione leprosi;* in secunda vero, de *liberatione paralytici,* ibi[^p119-2]: *Et factum est in una dierum.*

Circa miraculum autem de *curatione leprosi* tria introducuntur. Primum est *operatio miraculi.* Secundum est *eruditio leprosi,* ibi[^p119-3]: *Et ipse praecepit ei* etc. Tertium est *aedificatio populi,* ibi: *Perambulabat autem magis sermo de illo* etc.

Circa *operationem miraculi* tria sunt attendenda, scilicet *miserabilis infectio morbi, commendabilis devotio aegri* et *admirabilis operatio medici.*

25. (Vers. 12.). Primo igitur tangitur *miserabilis infectio morbi,* cum dicitur: *Et factum est, cum esset in una civitatum*[^p119-4]; quia morbus erat *publicus* et *perpetuus: publicus,* quia in civitate, ubi multi conveniunt, et ita multis erat infirmitas nota. Et attendendum, quod dicitur fuisse *in civitate,* quia erat *prope civitatem.* Nam Matthaei octavo[^p119-5] dicitur de hoc leproso, quod curavit eum in descensu de monte; unde potest hic accipi, sicut infra decimo tertio: « Non capit, prophetam perire extra Ierusalem »; quia, sicut dicitur ad Hebraeos decimo tertio, Christus « extra portam passus est ». — Erat etiam morbus *perpetuus;* unde et subdit: *Ecce*[^p119-6], *vir plenus lepra,* quae est infirmitas incurabilis, maxime cum est radicata. Unde poterat ei convenire illud Isaiae primo: « A planta pedis usque ad verticem non est in eo sanitas ». Similis etiam erat Iob, de quo secundo: « Percussit satan Iob ulcere pessimo, a planta pedis usque ad verticem, qui testa saniem radebat ».

26. Secundo vero insinuatur *commendabilis devotio leprosi,* cum dicitur: *Et videns Iesum,* quia videndo *adoravit* et *collaudavit.* — *Adoravit signo,* cum ait: *Et procidens in faciem suam*[^p119-7], quod est adorantium, ut in Psalmo: « Venite, adoremus et procidamus » etc.; et hoc requirebat diabolus, Matthaei quarto: « Haec omnia tibi dabo, si procidens adoraveris me ». Unde hoc signo professus est, ipsum esse Dei Filium, sicut ille caecus, de quo Ioannis nono: « Si credis in Filium Dei? Quis est, Domine » etc.; et post sequitur: « Credo, Domine. Et procidens adoravit eum ». — Et nota, quod dicitur *procidisse in faciem,* quod est bonum signum; sicut dicitur de Moyse et Aaron Numerorum vigesimo[^p119-8], quod prociderunt in *facies suas* coram Domino; non sicut Heli, primi Regum quarto: « Cecidit Heli de sella *retrorsum* » etc.

27. *Collaudavit* etiam sive professus est *verbo,* quod notat, cum subditur: *Rogavit eum dicens: Domine, si vis, potes me mundare.* In hoc, quod *rogat,* insinuat *largitatem;* Iob vigesimo secundo[^p119-9]: « Rogabis eum, et exaudiet te ». In hoc, quod dicit: *Domine,* insinuat *dignitatem;* Esther decimo tertio: « Domine, in ditione tua cuncta sunt posita ». In hoc, quod addit: *potes me mundare,* insinuat *potentiae facultatem;* Sapientiae duodecimo: « Subest tibi, cum volueris, posse ». Et hoc est, quod dicebat Iob Domino, decimo quarto[^p119-10]: « Quis potest facere mundum de immundo conceptum semine? Nonne tu, qui solus es »? Et ideo poterat dicere: « Asperges me hyssopo, et mundabor » etc.

28. (Vers. 13.). Tertio subditur *operatio medici,* cum dicitur: *Extendens*[^p119-11] *manum, tetigit eum dicens* etc., quae quidem fuit *admirabilis* tum ratione *clementiae,* tum ratione *potentiae,* tum ratione *efficaciae.* — Propter *clementiam* dicit: *Tetigit eum;* licet enim posset curare solo verbo, maluit tangere, ut ostenderet suam benignitatem; unde Proverbiorum ultimo[^p119-12]: « Manum suam aperuit inopi ». Unde iam non poterat hic murmurare de Christo, sicut Naaman de Eliseo; quarti Regum quinto: « Putabam, quod egrederetur ad me et tangeret locum
<!-- page 120 -->
leprae manu sua et sanaret me ». Sed certe non est repugnantia Prophetae et Domini, quia Propheta subiectus erat Legi, et ideo noluit tangere, ne secundum Legem fieret immundus; Salvator vero supra Legem erat, et ideo secundum Legem immunditiam non contrahebat et Legem evacuare poterat, et ideo tangebat. — Ad insinuandum vero *potentiam* additur: *Dicens: Volo: mundare.* In quo asserit se velle, et praecipit, ut mundetur, quia tantae est potentiae, ut *velle* sit ei *posse facere;* Isaiae quadragesimo sexto[^p120-1]: « Consilium meum stabit, et omnis voluntas mea fiet ». Volebat autem eius munditiam non tam corporalem quam spiritualem; unde primae ad Thessalonicenses quarto: « Haec est voluntas Dei: sanctificatio vestra ». — Ad insinuandam vero *efficaciam* subdit: *Et confestim lepra discessit ab illo,* ut nihil esset medium inter dictum et factum; « dixit enim, et facta sunt[^p120-2] ». *Verbo* igitur et *tactu* curavit, ut ostenderet se Verbum carnem factum; *verbo* etiam et *tactu* curavit, ut curaret interius et exterius; unde Ioannis septimo: « Totum hominem sanum feci » etc. Et in huius rei testimonium concurrunt ad Sacramentum verbum pariter et elementum[^p120-3].

29. (Vers. 14.). *Et ipse praecepit illi, ut nemini diceret.* Post curationem morbi subiungit hic Evangelista *eruditionem curati.* Docet autem Christus leprosum curatum et in eo quemlibet alium tria: primum est, quod non est *quaerendum praeconium laudis;* secundum, quod non est *contemnendum officium sacerdotis;* tertium est, quod non est *praetereundum mandatum Legis.* Primum horum dirigit *intentionem;* secundum, *affectionem;* et tertium regulat *operationem.*

Erudit igitur primo, quod *praeconium laudis non est quaerendum,* cum dicit: *Et ipse praecepit illi, ut nemini diceret.* Hoc non dicit, quia vellet,
quod ille non diceret, cum ille ubique praedicaverit hoc, sicut dicitur Marci primo[^p120-4]; sed instruit suo exemplo laudem vitare. Unde Beda: « Dat exemplum, ut sui in magnis, quae faciunt, latere velint; sed ut prosint aliis, prodantur inviti ». Hoc etiam dicit Ambrosius in Glossa.

30. Unde nota, quod est praeceptum *exsecutionis,* sicut Exodi vigesimo[^p120-5]: « Honora patrem tuum »; et *probationis,* ut praeceptum Abrahae de immolando filio, Genesis vigesimo secundo; et praeceptum *instructionis,* sicut hic. Unde non peccavit hic, cum nomen eius divulgavit. — Chrysostomus[^p120-6] tamen dicit, quod praeceptum fuit, sed ad *tempus* propter cautelam, ne, si forte sacerdotes audirent ipsum per Christum curatum, non reciperent eum tanquam mundum, quando se eis praesentaret, propter odium, quod habebant ad Christum. — Primus tamen modus rectior videtur, quia hoc praecipiebat aliis infirmis, qui tamen illud non observabant, secundum quod dicitur Marci septimo[^p120-7] de surdo et muto curato: « Quanto magis plus praecipiebat, tanto magis plus praedicabant ». Et recte, quia, secundum quod dicitur secundae ad Corinthios duodecimo, « ego a vobis debui commendari »; se autem nemo debet laudare, nisi necessitate aliena compulsus; unde Apostolus: « Factus sum insipiens, vos me coegistis ». Ideo dicebat *se insipientem factum,* quia contra consilium sapientis fecisse videbatur; Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo: « Laudet te alienus, et non os tuum, extraneus, et non labia tua ».

31. Erudit etiam secundo, quod *officium sacerdotis non est contemnendum,* cum addit: *Sed vade, ostende te sacerdotibus*[^p120-8]; quia eorum officium erat secundum Legem leprosos eiicere et mundatos recipere, secundum quod dicitur Levitici decimo tertio et decimo quarto quasi per totum. Ideo autem hoc officium leprae discernendae *sacerdotibus* est
<!-- page 121 -->
commissum, licet magis videatur esse *medicorum,* quia eis vulnera peccatorum mortalium, quae per lepram designantur, tempore veritatis revelatae ostendenda sunt, secundum illud Ecclesiastici decimo octavo[^p121-1]: « In tempore infirmitatis ostende conversationem tuam », quia, secundum quod dicitur Proverbiorum vigesimo octavo, « qui abscondit scelera sua non dirigetur »; unde quamvis Dominus mundet per interiorem contritionem, nihilominus ligat ad sacramentalem confessionem. In hoc autem mandato instruit, sacerdotale officium esse omnibus venerandum, et id ad curationem peccatorum esse necessarium. Et haec est ratio praecipua huius mandati. Sunt tamen et aliae, ut scilicet[^p121-2] ad bonum possint promoveri, viso miraculo de curatione certa leprosi, et ut testes sint miraculi, et ne videretur eis adversari, et ut innotescat eis sacerdotalis dignitas Christi et gratia novi testamenti et sacerdotii, quae non tantum potest *curatum ostendere,* sed etiam *curare.*

32. Erudit nihilominus tertio, quod *praeceptum Legis non est praetermittendum,* cum subdit: *Et offer pro emundatione tua, sicut praecepit Moyses,* sicut Levitici decimo quarto[^p121-3] leprosi mundati iubentur offerre duos passeres vivos et tortam panis et sextarium olei. Haec autem caeremonialia erant, et tamen voluit ea Salvator offerri, ut ostenderet, Legem esse bonam et divinitus latam, contra Manichaeorum perfidiam; ad Romanos septimo[^p121-4]: « Lex quidem sancta, et mandatum sanctum, iustum et bonum »; et hoc, quia adhuc manebant, cum non esset veritas revelata; unde *interlinearis* dicit, quod « non debent auferri signa, nisi prius venerint signata ». — Illa autem signa[^p121-5] Legis sanitatem non dabant, sed ostendebant et testimonio confirmabant; et ideo addit: *In testimonium illis;* quod potest legi dupliciter: offer illis in testimonium, scilicet tuae curationis; vel: offer, ut hoc sit illis in testimonium, quod tu curatus es, et ut tibi ultra non possint no-
cere, cum a te receperint munera testimonialia. Et nota, quod hoc, quod dicit *in testimonium illis,* non refertur ad praeceptum Moysi, sed Domini. Unde Chrysostomus[^p121-6]: « Non sic intelligas, quod Moyses praecepit in testimonium illis, sed tu vade et offer in testimonium illis », ut scilicet assumeret ab eis testimonium apud plebem. Nam, sicut dicitur primae ad Timotheum tertio, « oportet, Sanctos testimonium habere bonum ab eis qui foris sunt ». Unde et opus Christi mirabile debebat testimonio insidiantium approbari ad perfectam solidationem fidei et eiusdem roborationem.

33. (Vers. 15.). *Perambulabat autem multo magis* etc. Postquam descripsit Evangelista curationem morbi et eruditionem curati, hic tertio subiungit *magnificationem*[^p121-7] *nominis Christi,* ut ex his omnibus discipuli confirmentur in fide et amore Christi. Describitur igitur magnificatio nominis Christi ut *famosa,* ut *fructuosa,* ut *virtuosa.*

Primo igitur describitur hic magnificatio nominis Christi ut *famosa,* cum dicitur: *Perambulabat autem magis sermo de illo,* scilicet Christo, *sermo* laudis suae quantum ad scientiam et virtutes. Nec mirum, si fama Magistri diffundebatur, cum de discipulis eius dicatur in Psalmo[^p121-8]: « In omnem terram exivit sonus eorum ». Huius figura praecessit in Iuda Machabaeo, de quo primi Machabaeorum tertio, quod « similis factus est leoni in operibus suis »; et post subditur, quod « nominatus est usque ad novissimum terrae ».

34. Deinde ostenditur nominis Christi magnificatio *fructuosa,* cum additur: *Et conveniebant turbae multae, ut audirent;* scilicet propter *instructionem mentis,* quia scriptum est Ecclesiastici sexto[^p121-9]: « Si dilexeris audire, sapiens eris »; et Iacobi primo: « Scitis, fratres mei: Sit autem omnis homo velox ad audiendum ». — Conveniebant etiam propter *curationem ex parte corporis;* et ideo addit: *Et*
<!-- page 122 -->
*curarentur ab infirmitatibus suis.* Propter quod praedicabat Isaias quinquagesimo tertio[^p122-1]: « Vere languores nostros ipse tulit, et infirmitates ipse portavit ». Et ideo recte faciebant secundum consilium Sapientis, Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo: « Fili, in infirmitate non despicias te ipsum, sed ora Dominum, et curabit te ». — Haec ergo erat perfecta utilitas ex parte *animae* et *corporis,* quae veniebat a fama nominis Christi. Ipse enim Christus est « Dei virtus et Dei sapientia[^p122-2] »; et ideo qui ad ipsum conveniunt illuminantur per *sapientiam* et curantur per *virtutem.* Et ideo dicitur in Psalmo: « Accedite ad eum et illuminamini, et facies vestrae non confundentur »; et ideo Isaiae quadragesimo nono: « Dedi te in lucem gentium, ut sis salus mea usque ad extremum terrae ».

35. (Vers. 16.). Postremo ostenditur *virtuosa,* cum subditur: *Ipse autem secedebat in desertum et orabat;* quia humanam gloriam et famam non quaerebat, sed fugiebat, secundum illud Matthaei decimo quarto[^p122-3]: « Et dimissa turba, ascendit in montem solus orare ». In quo dabat nobis formam recedendi a mundo et appropinquandi ad Deum; propter quod dicebat Apostolus ad Galatas primo: « Si adhuc hominibus placerem, Christi servus non essem ». Et ideo ad hoc, quod oratio sit Deo placita, non debet esse publica in foro, sed occulta in deserto; unde Matthaei sexto[^p122-4]: « Tu autem, cum oraveris, intra cubiculum tuum ». Et hoc est quod dicit Bernardus in Cantica: « O sancta anima! sola esto, ut soli omnium serves te ipsam, quem tibi ex omnibus elegisti. Fuge publicum, fuge et ipsos domesticos, secede ab amicis et intimis et ab illo qui tibi ministrat. An nescis, te verecundum habere sponsum, et qui nequaquam velit tibi suam indulgere praesentiam, praesentibus ceteris? Secede ergo, sed mente, non corpore; sed intentione, sed devotione, sed spiritu; quamquam et corpore interdum non otiose te separas, praesertim in tempore orationis ».

36. Ad praedicti autem miraculi evidentiam duo sunt hic intelligenda[^p122-5]. Nam primo oportet *aperire latentem spiritualem intelligentiam,* deinde *dissolvere apparentem controversiam.*

Circa hoc autem miraculum tria consideranda occurrunt. Primum est, quid intelligitur per *mor-
*bum leprae;* secundo, quid per *tactum manus dominicae;* tertio, quid per *praeceptum Legis implendae.* — *Morbus leprae* designat recte peccatum mortale, quia, sicut lepra inficit et deformat carnem, sic peccatum maculat et deformat animam; unde Threnorum ultimo[^p122-6]: « Denigrata est super carbones facies eorum, adhaesit cutis eorum ossibus ». Lepra etiam eiici facit extra castra, sic peccatum mortale a castris gloriae; Ezechielis vigesimo octavo: « Peccasti, et eieci te de monte Dei ». — Hic autem morbus quadruplicem habet differentiam, secundum quod dicitur Levitici decimo tertio[^p122-7]. Est enim lepra in *carne,* lepra in *pilis,* lepra in *vestibus,* lepra in *habitaculis.* Lepra in *carne* significat peccatum carnale; lepra in *pilis* barbae vel capitis, spirituale peccatum, quia per *pilos* cogitationes intelliguntur; lepra in *veste,* peccatum in conversatione; lepra in *domo,* peccatum in congregatione. Ille ergo est plenus lepra, qui omnibus his criminibus est infectus. De his differentiis leprae Levitici decimo tertio dicitur. In huius *figuram* etiam dicitur quarti Regum septimo[^p122-8], quod quatuor viri leprosi erant in porta Samariae ad has quatuor differentias designandas. Ideo autem voluit Dominus mortale peccatum per lepram designari, ut, sicut lepra fugitur et in se et in consortio, sic et peccatum.

37. Per *tactum dominicae manus* intelligimus visitationem divinae gratiae, quam petebat Propheta[^p122-9]: « Emitte manum tuam de alto et eripe me »; quia, sicut dicitur ad Romanos septimo, « infelix ego homo, quis me liberabit de corpore mortis huius? Gratia Dei per Iesum Christum Dominum nostrum ». Per Christum enim tangit nos manus divina, quomodo dicitur Canticorum quinto[^p122-10]: « Dilectus meus misit manum suam per foramen », id est gratiam per foramen lateris Christi, unde profluunt Sacramenta; « et venter intremiscit », quia haec gratia primo parit contritionem; unde in Psalmo: « Qui respicit terram et facit eam tremere; qui tangit montes, et fumigant », quia contritio fletum parit. — Sed ad hoc, ut ad istum statum perveniat peccator, oportet, quod cum leproso *videat* Deum per fidem; primae ad Corinthios decimo tertio[^p122-11]: « Videmus nunc per speculum in aenigmate »; oportet, quod *procidat* per timorem; Psalmus: « Coram illo procident
<!-- page 123 -->
Aethiopes », id est peccatores; oportet, quod *confidat* per spem; Proverbiorum vigesimo nono: « Qui sperat in Domino salvabitur »; et ad Romanos octavo: « Spe salvi facti sumus ». Et haec tria pariunt contritionem; Isaiae vigesimo sexto: « A facie tua, Domine, concepimus et quasi parturivimus et peperimus spiritum salutis ».

38. Per *praeceptum Legis observandae* intelligimus susceptionem Sacramenti poenitentiae. Unde attende, quod quatuor praecipit, scilicet *ire, tacere, ostendere* et *offerre: ire,* cum dicit: *Vade; tacere: Nemini dixeris; ostendere: Ostende te; offerre: Offer munus.* — *Vade,* scilicet deserendo peccata, sicut adulterae, Ioannis octavo[^p123-1]: « Vade et amplius noli peccare ». — *Tacere* praecipit bona, ne sit sicut ille Pharisaeus, infra decimo octavo, qui dicebat: « Non sum sicut ceteri hominum ». — *Ostendere* praecipit mala, sicut Iob trigesimo primo[^p123-2]: « Si abscondi quasi homo peccatum meum »? id est *non;* Psalmus: « Delictum meum cognitum tibi feci ». — *Offerre* praecipit opera satisfactoria; Psalmus[^p123-3]: « Introibo in domum tuam in holocaustis; reddam tibi vota mea, quae distinxerunt labia mea »; ad Romanos duodecimo: « Exhibeatis corpora vestra hostiam viventem, sanctam, Deo placentem, rationabile obsequium vestrum ». — Haec autem quatuor concurrunt necessario ad Sacramentum poenitentiae perfecte recipiendum, scilicet peccati desertio, peccatoris humiliatio, peccati ostensio, satisfactionis oblatio.

39. Demum *dissolvenda est apparens controversia* inter Matthaeum et Lucam: quia hoc miraculum dicit Matthaeus[^p123-4] esse factum post sermonem Domini in monte, hic Lucas narrat longe ante sermonem. — Ad quod est duplex via dissolvendi controversiam: sive quia alius sermo fuit, quem narrat Lucas; alius, quem Matthaeus — cuius signum, quia multa dicuntur in eo sermone, quae non dicuntur in alio[^p123-5] — sive quia idem sermo fuit differens secundum maiorem explicationem et minorem. Tunc attendendum est, quod Evangelistae non servant omnino seriem rei gestae, sed eo modo narrant, quo magis facit ad suae intentionis prosecutionem. Unde Augustinus in secundo libro[^p123-6] de Concordia Evangelistarum: « Quid interest, quis quo ordine ponat, sive quod ex ordine inserit, sive quod omissum recolit, sive quod postea factum ante praeoccupat, dum tamen non adversetur eadem vel alia narranti vel sibi, vel alteri? Satis enim probabile est, quod unusquisque Evangelistarum eo ordine didicit narrare debuisse, quo voluisset Deus ea ipsa, quae narrabat, eius recordationi suggerere, in eis dumtaxat rebus, quarum ordo, sive iste, sive ille sit, nihil minuit auctoritati veritatique evangelicae ». — Et per hoc patet dissolutio omnis contrarietatis, quae videtur provenire ex ordine differente.

## English
<!-- page 119 -->

24. *And it came to pass, when he was in a certain city* etc. After the calling of the disciples he subjoins here their *confirmation by miracles.* And this part has two. For in the first is introduced the miracle of the *cure of the leper;* but in the second, that of the *deliverance of the paralytic,* there[^p119-2]: *And it came to pass on a certain day.*

Now concerning the miracle of the *cure of the leper* three things are introduced. The first is the *working of the miracle.* The second is the *erudition of the leper,* there[^p119-3]: *And he enjoined him* etc. The third is the *edification of the people,* there: *But the word concerning him went abroad the more* etc.

Concerning the *working of the miracle* three things are to be attended to, namely the *pitiable infection of the disease,* the *commendable devotion of the sick man* and the *wonderful working of the physician.*

25. (Vers. 12.). First, then, is touched the *pitiable infection of the disease,* when it is said: *And it came to pass, when he was in a certain city*[^p119-4]; because the disease was *public* and *perpetual: public,* because in a city, where many come together, and so the infirmity was known to many. And it is to be attended to that he is said to have been *in the city* because he was *near the city.* For in Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p119-5], it is said of this leper that he cured him in the descent from the mountain; whence it can be taken here as below, in the thirteenth chapter: « It cannot be that a prophet perish out of Jerusalem »; because, as is said to the Hebrews, the thirteenth chapter, Christ « suffered without the gate ». — The disease was also *perpetual;* whence he also subjoins: *Behold*[^p119-6], *a man full of leprosy,* which is an incurable infirmity, most of all when it is rooted. Whence that saying of Isaias, the first chapter, could fit him: « From the sole of the foot unto the top of the head, there is no soundness therein ». Like him too was Job, of whom in the second chapter: « Satan struck Job with a very grievous ulcer, from the sole of the foot even to the top of his head, who scraped the corrupt matter with a potsherd ».

26. But secondly is suggested the *commendable devotion of the leper,* when it is said: *And seeing Jesus,* because in seeing he *adored* and *praised.* — *He adored by a sign,* when he says: *And falling on his face*[^p119-7], which is the part of those who adore, as in the Psalm: « Come, let us adore and fall down » etc.; and this the devil required, Matthew, the fourth chapter: « All these will I give thee, if falling down thou wilt adore me ». Whence by this sign he professed him to be the Son of God, like that blind man of whom in John, the ninth chapter: « Dost thou believe in the Son of God? Who is he, Lord » etc.; and afterwards there follows: « I believe, Lord. And falling down he adored him ». — And note that he is said to have *fallen on his face,* which is a good sign; as is said of Moses and Aaron in Numbers, the twentieth chapter[^p119-8], that they fell down upon *their faces* before the Lord; not as Heli, the first of Kings, the fourth chapter: « Heli fell from his stool *backwards* » etc.

27. He also *praised,* or professed, *by word,* which he notes when it is subjoined: *He besought him, saying: Lord, if thou wilt, thou canst make me clean.* In this, that he *beseeches,* he suggests *largess;* Job, the twenty-second chapter[^p119-9]: « Thou shalt beseech him, and he will hear thee ». In this, that he says: *Lord,* he suggests *dignity;* Esther, the thirteenth chapter: « Lord, all things are set in thy power ». In this, that he adds: *thou canst make me clean,* he suggests the *faculty of power;* Wisdom, the twelfth chapter: « Thy power is at hand when thou wilt ». And this is what Job said to the Lord, in the fourteenth chapter[^p119-10]: « Who can make him clean that is conceived of unclean seed? Is it not thou who only art »? And therefore he could say: « Thou shalt sprinkle me with hyssop, and I shall be cleansed » etc.

28. (Vers. 13.). Thirdly is subjoined the *working of the physician,* when it is said: *And stretching forth*[^p119-11] *his hand, he touched him, saying* etc., which indeed was *wonderful* by reason of *clemency,* by reason of *power,* and by reason of *efficacy.* — On account of *clemency* he says: *He touched him;* for although he could cure by a word alone, he preferred to touch, so as to show his kindness; whence in Proverbs, the last chapter[^p119-12]: « She hath opened her hand to the needy ». Whence now he could not murmur here about Christ as Naaman did about Eliseus; the fourth of Kings, the fifth chapter: « I thought he would have come out to me, and touched the place of the
<!-- page 120 -->
leprosy with his hand, and healed me ». But surely there is no conflict between the Prophet and the Lord, because the Prophet was subject to the Law, and therefore would not touch, lest according to the Law he should become unclean; but the Saviour was above the Law, and therefore according to the Law he did not contract uncleanness and could make the Law void, and therefore he touched. — But to suggest *power* it is added: *Saying: I will: be thou cleansed.* In which he asserts that he wills, and enjoins that he be cleansed, because he is of such great power that for him *to will* is *to be able to do;* Isaias, the forty-sixth chapter[^p120-1]: « My counsel shall stand, and all my will shall be done ». Now he willed his cleanness not so much bodily as spiritual; whence the first to the Thessalonians, the fourth chapter: « This is the will of God: your sanctification ». — But to suggest *efficacy* he subjoins: *And immediately the leprosy departed from him,* so that there was nothing in between the saying and the deed; « for he spoke, and they were made[^p120-2] ». By *word,* then, and by *touch* he cured, so as to show himself the Word made flesh; by *word* also and by *touch* he cured, so as to cure inwardly and outwardly; whence John, the seventh chapter: « I have healed the whole man » etc. And in testimony of this thing word and element alike concur in a Sacrament[^p120-3].

29. (Vers. 14.). *And he enjoined him that he should tell no man.* After the cure of the disease the Evangelist subjoins here the *erudition of the man cured.* Now Christ teaches the cured leper, and in him anyone else, three things: the first is that *the proclamation of praise is not to be sought;* the second, that *the office of the priest is not to be despised;* the third is that *the commandment of the Law is not to be passed over.* The first of these directs the *intention;* the second, the *affection;* and the third rules the *operation.*

He schools him, then, first, that *the proclamation of praise is not to be sought,* when he says: *And he enjoined him that he should tell no man.* He does not say this because he willed
that he should not tell it, since he preached this everywhere, as is said in Mark, the first chapter[^p120-4]; but he instructs by his own example to shun praise. Whence Bede: « He gives an example, that his own should wish to lie hidden in the great things that they do; but that, so as to profit others, they should be betrayed against their will ». Ambrose also says this in the Gloss.

30. Whence note that there is a precept *of execution,* as in Exodus, the twentieth chapter[^p120-5]: « Honour thy father »; and *of proof,* as the precept to Abraham about sacrificing his son, Genesis, the twenty-second chapter; and a precept *of instruction,* as here. Whence he did not sin here when he spread abroad his name. — Chrysostom[^p120-6], however, says that it was a precept, but for a *time,* on account of caution, lest, if perhaps the priests heard that he had been cured by Christ, they should not receive him as clean when he presented himself to them, on account of the hatred which they had toward Christ. — The first way, however, seems more correct, because he enjoined this on other sick persons, who nevertheless did not observe it, according to what is said in Mark, the seventh chapter[^p120-7], of the deaf and dumb man cured: « The more he enjoined them, so much the more a great deal did they preach it ». And rightly, because, according to what is said in the second to the Corinthians, the twelfth chapter, « I ought to have been commended by you »; but no one ought to praise himself unless compelled by another's necessity; whence the Apostle: « I am become foolish; you have compelled me ». Therefore he said that he *had become foolish,* because he seemed to have acted against the counsel of the wise man; Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter: « Let another praise thee, and not thy own mouth; a stranger, and not thy own lips ».

31. He schools him also, secondly, that *the office of the priest is not to be despised,* when he adds: *But go, show thyself to the priests*[^p120-8]; because it was their office according to the Law to cast out lepers and to receive back those made clean, according to what is said in Leviticus, the thirteenth and fourteenth chapters, almost throughout. But this office of discerning leprosy is
<!-- page 121 -->
committed to the *priests,* although it seems rather to belong to *physicians,* because to them the wounds of mortal sins, which are designated by leprosy, are to be shown in the time of revealed truth, according to that saying of Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter[^p121-1]: « In the time of sickness show thy conversation », because, according to what is said in Proverbs, the twenty-eighth chapter, « he that hideth his sins shall not prosper »; whence, although the Lord cleanses through inward contrition, nonetheless he binds to sacramental confession. Now in this commandment he instructs that the priestly office is to be venerated by all, and that it is necessary for the cure of sins. And this is the chief reason of this commandment. There are, however, others too, namely that[^p121-2] they might be able to be advanced to the good, having seen the miracle of the certain cure of the leper; and that they might be witnesses of the miracle; and that he should not seem to oppose them; and that the priestly dignity of Christ and the grace of the new testament and priesthood might become known to them, which can not only *show the man cured,* but also *cure.*

32. He schools him, nonetheless, thirdly, that *the precept of the Law is not to be omitted,* when he subjoins: *And offer for thy cleansing, as Moses commanded,* as in Leviticus, the fourteenth chapter[^p121-3], lepers made clean are bidden to offer two living sparrows and a cake of bread and a sextary of oil. Now these were ceremonial things, and yet the Saviour willed them to be offered, so as to show that the Law is good and divinely given, against the perfidy of the Manichaeans; to the Romans, the seventh chapter[^p121-4]: « The law indeed is holy, and the commandment holy, and just, and good »; and this, because they still remained, since the truth had not been revealed; whence the *interlinear* Gloss says that « the signs ought not to be taken away unless the things signified have first come ». — But those signs[^p121-5] of the Law did not give health, but showed it and confirmed it by testimony; and therefore he adds: *For a testimony to them;* which can be read in two ways: offer to them for a testimony, namely of thy cure; or: offer, that this may be to them for a testimony that thou art cured, and that they may no longer be able to
harm thee, since they have received from thee gifts of testimony. And note that this, that he says *for a testimony to them,* is not referred to the precept of Moses but of the Lord. Whence Chrysostom[^p121-6]: « Do not so understand it that Moses enjoined it for a testimony to them; but go thou and offer for a testimony to them », namely, so that he might take from them a testimony before the people. For, as is said in the first to Timothy, the third chapter, « it behoveth the Saints to have a good testimony of them who are without ». Whence also the marvellous work of Christ had to be approved by the testimony of those lying in wait for him, for the perfect making solid of faith and its strengthening.

33. (Vers. 15.). *But the word concerning him went abroad much the more* etc. After the Evangelist has described the cure of the disease and the erudition of the man cured, here thirdly he subjoins the *magnifying*[^p121-7] *of the name of Christ,* so that from all these things the disciples might be confirmed in the faith and love of Christ. The magnifying of the name of Christ, then, is described as *famed,* as *fruitful,* as *virtuous.*

First, then, the magnifying of the name of Christ is described here as *famed,* when it is said: *But the word concerning him went abroad the more,* namely concerning Christ, the *word* of his praise as to knowledge and powers. Nor is it a wonder if the fame of the Master was spread abroad, since of his disciples it is said in the Psalm[^p121-8]: « Their sound hath gone forth into all the earth ». The figure of this went before in Judas Machabeus, of whom in the first of Machabees, the third chapter, that « he became like a lion in his works »; and afterwards it is subjoined that « he was renowned even to the utmost part of the earth ».

34. Next the magnifying of the name of Christ is shown as *fruitful,* when it is added: *And great multitudes came together to hear;* namely for the sake of the *instruction of the mind,* because it is written in Ecclesiasticus, the sixth chapter[^p121-9]: « If thou love to hear, thou shalt be wise »; and James, the first chapter: « You know, my brethren: but let every man be swift to hear ». — They came together also for the sake of *cure on the part of the body;* and therefore he adds: *And
<!-- page 122 -->
*to be healed of their infirmities.* On account of which Isaias preached, in the fifty-third chapter[^p122-1]: « Surely he hath borne our diseases, and he hath carried our infirmities ». And therefore they did rightly according to the counsel of the Wise Man, Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter: « My son, in thy sickness neglect not thyself, but pray to the Lord, and he shall heal thee ». — This, then, was the perfect benefit on the part of *soul* and *body,* which came from the fame of the name of Christ. For Christ himself is « the power of God and the wisdom of God[^p122-2] »; and therefore those who come together to him are enlightened through *wisdom* and cured through *power.* And therefore it is said in the Psalm: « Come ye to him and be enlightened, and your faces shall not be confounded »; and therefore Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter: « I have given thee to be the light of the Gentiles, that thou mayst be my salvation even to the farthest part of the earth ».

35. (Vers. 16.). Lastly it is shown as *virtuous,* when it is subjoined: *But he retired into the desert and prayed;* because he did not seek human glory and fame but fled them, according to that saying of Matthew, the fourteenth chapter[^p122-3]: « And having dismissed the multitude, he went up into a mountain alone to pray ». In which he gave us a form of withdrawing from the world and drawing near to God; on account of which the Apostle said to the Galatians, the first chapter: « If I yet pleased men, I should not be the servant of Christ ». And therefore, in order that prayer may be pleasing to God, it ought not to be public in the market-place, but hidden in the desert; whence Matthew, the sixth chapter[^p122-4]: « But thou, when thou shalt pray, enter into thy chamber ». And this is what Bernard says on the Canticle: « O holy soul! be alone, that thou mayst keep thyself for him alone of all, whom thou hast chosen for thyself out of all. Flee the public, flee even those of thy household, retire from friends and intimates and from him who ministers to thee. Knowest thou not that thou hast a shamefast bridegroom, and one who will by no means grant thee his presence when others are present? Retire, then, but in mind, not in body; but in intention, but in devotion, but in spirit; although thou dost not idly separate thyself sometimes in body too, especially in the time of prayer ».

36. Now for the evidence of the aforesaid miracle two things are to be understood here[^p122-5]. For first it is fitting to *open the latent spiritual intelligence,* then to *dissolve the apparent controversy.*

Now concerning this miracle three things occur to be considered. The first is what is understood by the *dis-
*ease of leprosy;* secondly, what by the *touch of the Lord's hand;* thirdly, what by the *precept of fulfilling the Law.* — The *disease of leprosy* rightly designates mortal sin, because, as leprosy infects and deforms the flesh, so sin stains and deforms the soul; whence Lamentations, the last chapter[^p122-6]: « Their face is made blacker than coals, their skin hath stuck to their bones ». Leprosy also causes one to be cast out outside the camp, as mortal sin does from the camp of glory; Ezechiel, the twenty-eighth chapter: « Thou hast sinned, and I cast thee out from the mountain of God ». — Now this disease has a fourfold difference, according to what is said in Leviticus, the thirteenth chapter[^p122-7]. For there is leprosy in the *flesh,* leprosy in the *hairs,* leprosy in *garments,* leprosy in *dwellings.* Leprosy in the *flesh* signifies carnal sin; leprosy in the *hairs* of the beard or head, spiritual sin, because by the *hairs* thoughts are understood; leprosy in the *garment,* sin in conversation; leprosy in the *house,* sin in the congregation. He, then, is full of leprosy who is infected with all these crimes. Of these differences of leprosy it is spoken in Leviticus, the thirteenth chapter. In *figure* of this it is also said in the fourth of Kings, the seventh chapter[^p122-8], that four men, lepers, were at the gate of Samaria, to designate these four differences. Now the Lord willed mortal sin to be designated by leprosy for this reason, that, as leprosy is fled both in itself and in fellowship, so too is sin.

37. By the *touch of the Lord's hand* we understand the visitation of divine grace, which the Prophet asked for[^p122-9]: « Put forth thy hand from on high, take me out »; because, as is said to the Romans, the seventh chapter, « unhappy man that I am, who shall deliver me from the body of this death? The grace of God, by Jesus Christ our Lord ». For through Christ the divine hand touches us, as is said in the Canticle, the fifth chapter[^p122-10]: « My beloved put his hand through the opening », that is, grace through the opening of the side of Christ, whence the Sacraments flow forth; « and the belly trembleth », because this grace first brings forth contrition; whence in the Psalm: « He looketh upon the earth and maketh it tremble; he toucheth the mountains, and they smoke », because contrition brings forth weeping. — But in order that the sinner may come to this state, it is fitting that with the leper he *see* God through faith; the first to the Corinthians, the thirteenth chapter[^p122-11]: « We see now through a glass in a dark manner »; it is fitting that he *fall down* through fear; the Psalm: « Before him shall fall down the
<!-- page 123 -->
Ethiopians », that is, sinners; it is fitting that he *trust* through hope; Proverbs, the twenty-ninth chapter: « He that hopeth in the Lord shall be saved »; and to the Romans, the eighth chapter: « For we are saved by hope ». And these three bring forth contrition; Isaias, the twenty-sixth chapter: « From thy face, O Lord, we have conceived, and been as it were in labour, and have brought forth the spirit of salvation ».

38. By the *precept of observing the Law* we understand the receiving of the Sacrament of penance. Whence attend that he enjoins four things, namely *to go, to keep silent, to show* and *to offer: to go,* when he says: *Go; to keep silent: Tell no man; to show: Show thyself; to offer: Offer the gift.* — *Go,* namely by forsaking sins, as to the adulteress, John, the eighth chapter[^p123-1]: « Go, and sin no more ». — He enjoins *keeping silent* about good things, lest one be like that Pharisee, below, the eighteenth chapter, who said: « I am not as the rest of men ». — He enjoins *showing* evil things, as Job, the thirty-first chapter[^p123-2]: « If as a man I have hid my sin »? that is, *not;* the Psalm: « I have acknowledged my sin to thee ». — He enjoins *offering* works of satisfaction; the Psalm[^p123-3]: « I will go into thy house with burnt offerings; I will pay thee my vows, which my lips have uttered »; to the Romans, the twelfth chapter: « That you present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, pleasing unto God, your reasonable service ». — Now these four concur of necessity for receiving the Sacrament of penance perfectly, namely the forsaking of sin, the humbling of the sinner, the showing of sin, the offering of satisfaction.

39. Lastly the *apparent controversy* between Matthew and Luke *is to be dissolved:* because Matthew[^p123-4] says that this miracle was done after the Lord's sermon on the mountain, Luke here narrates it long before the sermon. — To which there is a twofold way of dissolving the controversy: either because the sermon that Luke narrates was one, and that which Matthew narrates another — a sign of which is that many things are said in that sermon which are not said in the other[^p123-5] — or because it was the same sermon, differing according to a greater and a lesser unfolding. Then it is to be attended to that the Evangelists do not keep altogether the sequence of the thing done, but narrate in the way that best serves the pursuit of their intention. Whence Augustine in the second book[^p123-6] *On the Agreement of the Evangelists:* « What does it matter in what order anyone places a thing, whether he inserts something out of order, or recalls something omitted, or anticipates something done afterwards, provided that he does not contradict either himself or another narrating the same or other things? For it is quite probable that each of the Evangelists learned that he ought to narrate in that order in which God had willed to suggest to his recollection the very things he was narrating, in those things at least whose order, whether it be this or that, takes nothing away from the authority and truth of the Gospel ». — And by this is evident the dissolution of every contrariety that seems to arise from a differing order.
