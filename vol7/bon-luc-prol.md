# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Prologus S. Hieronymi
## *Prologus Sancti Hieronymi · Expositio huius Prologi*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 7 -->

### Prologus Sancti Hieronymi[^p7-2].

Lucas, natione Syrus, Antiochensis, arte medicus, discipulus Apostolorum, postea vero[^p7-3] Paulum secutus usque ad passionem eius, serviens Domino sine crimine (nam neque uxorem unquam habuit neque filios), septuaginta et quatuor annorum obiit in Bithynia plenus Spiritu sancto. Qui, cum iam scripta essent Evangelia per Matthaeum quidem in Iudaea, per Marcum autem in Italia, sancto instigante Spiritu, in Achaiae partibus hoc scripsit Evangelium, significans etiam ipse in principio, ante suum[^p7-4] alia esse descripta. Cui extra ea quae ordo evangelicae dispositionis exposcit, ea maxime necessitas laboris fuit, ut primum Graecis fidelibus, omni prophetatione venturi in carnem Dei Christi manifestata humanitate[^p7-5], ne Iudaicis fabulis attenti in solo Legis desiderio tenerentur, ne vel haereticis fabulis et stultis sollicitationibus seducti, exciderent a veritate, elaboraret; dehinc ut in principio Evangelii, Ioannis nativitate praesumta, cui Evangelium scriberet, et in quo electus scriberet, indicaret, contestans, in se completa esse quae essent ab aliis inchoata. Cui ideo post baptismum Filii Dei, a perfectione generationis in Christo impletae[^p7-6], repetendae a principio nativitatis humanae potestas permissa est, ut requirentibus demonstraret, in quo apprehendens erat, per Nathan filium introitu recurrentis in Deum generationis admisso, indisparabilis Dei; ut praedicans hominibus Christum suum, perfecti opus hominis redire in se per Filium faceret, qui per David patrem venientibus iter praebebat in Christo. Cui Lucae non immerito etiam scribendorum apostolicorum Actuum potestas in ministerio[^p7-7] datur; ut Deo in Deum pleno, et filio perditionis exstincto, oratione ab Apostolis facta, sorte Domini electionis numerus compleretur; sicque Paulus consummationem apostolicis Actibus daret, quem diu contra stimulum recalcitrantem Dominus elegisset. Quod legentibus ac requirentibus Deum etsi per singula expediri a nobis utile fuerat, sciens tamen, quod operantem agricolam oporteat de fructibus suis edere[^p7-8], vitavimus publicam curiositatem, ne non tam volentibus Deum videremur, quam fastidientibus prodidisse[^p7-9].

### Expositio huius Prologi.

1. Huic autem libro duplex praemittitur prologus, unus est translatoris, scilicet beati Hieronymi, alius vero auctoris, scilicet beati Lucae. Primus sic incipit: *Lucas Syrus* etc.; secundus sic: *Quoniam quidem multi conati sunt*[^p7-10] etc. — Circa prologum expositoris tria nobis consideranda occurrunt, quae in ipso prologo exprimuntur, scilicet causa *efficiens, finalis* et *formalis* operis subsequentis. Primum facit ibi: *Lucas, Syrus* etc.; secundum, ibi: *Cui extra ea quae ordo* etc.; tertium, ibi: *Cui ideo post baptismum* etc.
<!-- page 8 -->

2. Quantum ad causam *effectivam* nota, quod describit ipsum Evangelistam quantum ad tria, scilicet quantum ad *convictum,* quantum ad *meritum* et quantum ad *officium.* — Quantum ad *convictum,* cum dicitur: *Lucas, Syrus* etc. usque ibi: *Paulum secutus usque ad confessionem eius.* Ex quo ostenditur beatus Lucas idoneus ad scribendum Evangelium, quia cum his qui erant peritissimi, fuit conversatus; unde secundae ad Timotheum ultimo[^p8-1]: *Lucas est mecum solus,* dicit Apostolus, quia ei adhaesit tanquam fidelis discipulus.

3. Secundo describit eum quantum ad *meritum,* quod consistit in vita et gratia; et hoc notat, cum dicit: *Serviens Domino sine crimine,* in quo notatur *vita;* et post: *Obiit plenus Spiritu sancto,* in quo notatur *gratia,* et ideo idoneus ad Evangelium conscribendum. Cuius figura praecessit in Ioseph, Genesis quadragesimo primo[^p8-2]: *Num invenire poterimus talem virum, qui Spiritu Dei plenus sit?* Sicut enim Ioseph frumenta dispensavit, sic Lucas evangelicam doctrinam.

4. Tertio describit eum quantum ad *officium,* quia divinitus instigatus est, ut scriberet apud Graecos Evangelium; et hoc notatur, cum dicit: *Qui, cum iam scripta essent Evangelia per Matthaeum in Iudaea, per Marcum in Italia;* ubi ostendit, quod idoneum fuit, quod Lucas haberet officium tertio loco scribendi Evangelium, scilicet inter Graecos, ut sic *in omnem terram exiret sonus eorum*[^p8-3], et in mysterium Trinitatis tribus generibus linguarum praedicaretur et scriberetur Evangelium. Et huic competebat scriptura tituli crucis, Ioannis decimo nono[^p8-4], ubi dicitur, quod titulus ille: Iesus Nazarenus rex Iudaeorum, scriptus erat Hebraice, Graece et Latine.

5. Dubium autem est, utrum Marcus scripserit in lingua Latina, an Graeca, cum dicant Augustinus[^p8-5] et Isidorus, quod omnia Evangelia praeter Evangelium Matthaei scripta fuerint in Graeco. Sed certum est, quod in Italia scripsit[^p8-6]; et quia utraque lingua ibi tunc temporis erat consueta, probabile videtur, quod scripsit in utraque lingua, maxime cum tradat antiquitas, quod Evangelium, quod sua manu scripsit, sit Aquileiae[^p8-7].

6. *Cui extra ea* etc. Hic describitur causa *finalis* sive motiva triplex, scilicet *manifestatio veritatis, confutatio falsitatis* et *honorificatio dominicae maiestatis.* — Primo igitur quantum ad *manifestationem veritatis* dicit: *Cui extra ea quae ordo evangelicae dispositionis,* id est historiae, *exposcit, ea maxime necessitas,* id est necessitas movens, *laboris fuit, ut primum Graecis fidelibus,* quia eis primo et principaliter scriptum fuit, *omni prophetatione venturi in carnem Dei Christi, manifesta*[^p8-8] *esset humanitas; omni prophetatione,* id est, ab omnibus Prophetis praedictum est, Christum Deum in carnem venturum. *Manifesta esset humanitas,* quia versatus fuit circa Christi humanitatem, secundum quod per eam redempti sumus. Unde secundae ad Timotheum secundo[^p8-9]: *Memor esto, Dominum nostrum Iesum Christum resurrexisse a mortuis ex semine David secundum Evangelium meum.* Hoc autem Evangelium specialiter fuit Evangelium Lucae, sicut Evangelium Marci praecipue fuit Petri.
<!-- page 9 -->

7. Secundo, quantum ad *confutationem haereticae pravitatis*[^p9-1] dicit: *Ne Iudaicis fabulis attenti,* quibus seducti erant, posse legalia sufficere sine Evangelio, *in solo Legis desiderio tenerentur,* quod illa possint sufficere etc. *Vel ne haereticis* etc., usque ibi: *elaboraret*[^p9-2]. Et debet ita construi littera: *ut elaboraret, ne Iudaicis fabulis* etc. Et circa hoc erat opus valde insistere circa Ecclesiam primitivam; unde primae ad Timotheum primo[^p9-3]: « Rogavi te, ut denuntiares quibusdam, ne aliter docerent et ne intenderent fabulis et genealogiis interminatis, quae quaestiones praestant magis quam aedificationem Dei, quae est in fide ».

8. Tertio, quantum ad *honorificationem dominicae maiestatis* subdit: *Dehinc, ut in principio Evangelii, nativitate Ioannis praesumta, cui Evangelium scriberet,* « id est ad cuius honorem », ut dicit Glossa[^p9-4]; *indicaret* etc. usque ibi: *ab aliis inchoata.* Ad honorem namque Christi scripsit; unde Tobiae duodecimo: « Sacramentum regis abscondere bonum est, opera autem Dei revelare et confiteri honorificum est ». — Vel potest dici, quod tertio motivum est aedificatio fraternae caritatis; et hoc notat, cum dicit: *cui scriberet,* quia Theophilo, qui « interpretatur: *amans Deum,* vel *amatus a Deo*[^p9-5] »; in quo ostenditur, quod ad caritatem fraternam aedificandam scripsit, secundum illud primae ad Timotheum primo: « Finis praecepti est caritas de corde puro et conscientia bona et fide non ficta ».

9. *Cui ideo post baptismum* etc. Hic describitur *causa formalis* sive modus agendi, primo quantum ad librum *istum;* secundo, quoad librum annexum, scilicet *Actus Apostolorum;* tertio, quoad *prologum praeambulum.* — Primo igitur quoad librum *istum* dicit, quare Lucas genealogiam Christi describit post baptismum, a statu perfectae aetatis, et ordine retrogrado ascendendo per Nathan. Et rationem huius reddit, quia intendebat agere de generatione Christi, secundum quod per illam reducimur in Deum; quod quidem fit per regenerationem baptismalem et adoptivam, Christo Filio Dei naturali reducente nos ad Patrem suum, Patrem etiam nostrum per adoptionem. Et hoc est, quod vult insinuare *per Nathan;* quia, cum Salomon esset filius David naturalis, generatio Ioseph et Mariae non ascendit ad David per Nathan secundum carnem, sed per Salomonem secundum *carnem,* per Nathan vero secundum *adoptionem,* ut in tertio capitulo[^p9-6] ostendetur. Et hoc est quod vult dicere, et dicit valde obscure; et debet littera construi sic: *Cui ideo permissa est potestas repetendae humanae nativitatis a principio,* id est usque ad principium, scilicet usque ad Deum, et hoc *post baptismum a perfectione generationis,* id est post aetatem triginta annorum, sicut in tertio capitulo[^p9-7] dicetur: « Ipse Iesus erat incipiens quasi annorum triginta, ut putabatur filius Ioseph, qui fuit Heli » etc.; ut *introitu generationis indisparabilis Dei,* id est Christi aequalis Patri;
<!-- page 10 -->
generationis, scilicet *recurrentis in Deum; introitu,* dico, *admisso,* id est deducto *per Nathan, filium* David, *demonstraret requirentibus* Deum, *in quo apprehendens erat,* scilicet Deum[^p10-1]. Hic ostendit, per Christum oportere ad Patrem redire; et hoc est quod subdit: *Ut praedicans hominibus Christum suum,* noster enim est Christus; infra secundo[^p10-2]: « Natus est nobis hodie Salvator mundi » etc.; *opus perfecti hominis,* id est hominis, in quo perfecta sunt omnia, *redire in se,* hoc est in statum suum, de quo cecidit; *per Filium faceret,* per Filium scilicet Dei et hominis, Iesum Christum, *qui praebebat iter in Christo,* id est ostendebat praeberi[^p10-3] *venientibus,* scilicet ad Deum, *per David patrem,* Nathan scilicet vel Christi; hoc est dicere, quod volebat in illa genealogia post baptismum ostendere, quomodo per Christum oportet nos regeneratos redire ad Deum; ad Ephesios primo[^p10-4]: « Complacuit in Christo restaurare quae in caelis et quae in terris sunt ».

10. Secundo[^p10-5], quoad librum annexum, scilicet *Actus Apostolorum,* subdit: *Cui Lucae non immerito scribendorum etiam Actuum Apostolorum potestas in ministerio datur* etc. usque ibi: *Dominus elegisset.* Et vult dicere, quod magis competebat Lucae describere Actus quam alii Evangelistae, quia agebat de Christo, in quantum per gratiam reducebat ad Patrem; et hoc est quod potissime determinatur in Actibus Apostolorum; quod notat, cum dicit: *Ut Deo in Deum pleno;* hoc est plene per ascensionem ostenso, per quam datus est plene Spiritus sanctus, fieret *consummatio* in Paulo, qui fuit *vas electionis*[^p10-6], secundum gratiam praecipuam. Unde ad Ephesios quarto: « Qui descendit ipse est, qui ascendit, ut adimpleret omnia »; ad Ephesios primo: « Ipsum dedit caput super omnem Ecclesiam, quae est corpus eius, et plenitudo illius qui omnia in omnibus adimplevit ».

11. Tertio, quantum ad praesentem *prologum: Quod et legentibus et requirentibus etsi per singula,* id est singillatim et distincte omnia praedicta, *tractari*
<!-- page 11 -->
*utile fuerat; sciens tamen, quod operantem agricolam oportet primum de fructibus edere*[^p11-1], *vitavimus publicam curiositatem,* id est superfluam loquacitatem sive praeter necessitatem scientiae ostensionem; *ne non tam volentibus Deum demonstrare videremur quam fastidientibus prodidisse.* Hoc est dicere, quod quia doctor et expositor sicut agricola debet de fructibus suis edere, non debet quaerere curiosa, sed fructuosa, non aliorum laudem, sed testimonium conscientiae, non ostensionem vanitatis, sed aedificationem caritatis[^p11-2]. Et haec est ratio, quare opus est frequenter a pluribus supersedere et dicere necessaria, vitando non opportuna. — Vel aliter potest exponi: quia noluit omnia exponere, ut daret aliis causam laborandi et de suis fructibus manducandi; et hoc figuratum fuit in Lege, ubi dicitur Levitici decimo nono[^p11-3] messoribus, quod non colligerent remanentes spicas, quia primi doctores debent posteris relinquere occasionem exercitii. Et utroque modo satis sane potest exponi.

## English

### The Prologue of St Jerome[^p7-2].

Luke, a Syrian by nation, of Antioch, a physician by art, a disciple of the Apostles, but afterwards[^p7-3] having followed Paul until his passion, serving the Lord without crime (for he never had either wife or sons), died in Bithynia at seventy-four years of age, full of the Holy Spirit. He, when Gospels had already been written, by Matthew indeed in Judaea, but by Mark in Italy, at the instigation of the Holy Spirit wrote this Gospel in the parts of Achaia, signifying also himself in the beginning that others had been written before his own[^p7-4]. For whom, beyond those things which the order of the gospel arrangement demands, this was above all the necessity of the labour: that he should labour first for the Greek faithful, the humanity of the Christ of God who was to come in the flesh having been made manifest by every prophecy[^p7-5], lest, attentive to Jewish fables, they should be held in the desire of the Law alone, or lest, seduced by heretical fables and foolish solicitations, they should fall away from the truth; then that, in the beginning of the Gospel, the nativity of John being set first, he should indicate to whom he was writing the Gospel, and in what he was chosen to write, testifying that the things which had been begun by others were completed in himself. To whom therefore, after the baptism of the Son of God, from the perfection of the generation fulfilled in Christ[^p7-6], the power was permitted of retracing it from the beginning of human nativity, that he might show to those seeking in what he was apprehending, through Nathan the son, the entrance of the generation running back into God being admitted, of the indivisible God; that, preaching to men his Christ, he might make the work of the perfect man return into itself through the Son, who through David the father was furnishing a way in Christ to those coming. To which Luke, not undeservedly, is given also the power of writing the apostolic Acts in ministry[^p7-7]; that, God being full unto God, and the son of perdition being extinguished, prayer having been made by the Apostles, by the lot of the Lord the number of the election should be completed; and thus Paul should give consummation to the apostolic Acts, whom, long kicking against the goad, the Lord had chosen. Which, though it had been useful for us to set forth in detail to those reading and seeking God, yet, knowing that the labouring husbandman ought to eat of his own fruits[^p7-8], we have avoided public curiosity, lest we should seem to have betrayed God not so much to those willing as to the disdainful[^p7-9].

### The exposition of this Prologue.

1. To this book, then, a twofold prologue is prefixed, one is the translator's, namely blessed Jerome's, the other the author's, namely blessed Luke's. The first begins thus: *Luke a Syrian* etc.; the second thus: *Forasmuch as many have taken in hand*[^p7-10] etc. — Concerning the prologue of the expositor three things present themselves for our consideration, which are expressed in the prologue itself, namely the *efficient, final* and *formal* cause of the work that follows. He does the first there: *Luke, a Syrian* etc.; the second, there: *For whom, beyond those things which the order* etc.; the third, there: *To whom therefore after the baptism* etc.
<!-- page 8 -->

2. As to the *effective* cause, note that he describes the Evangelist himself as to three things, namely as to his *companionship,* as to his *merit* and as to his *office.* — As to his *companionship,* when it is said: *Luke, a Syrian* etc. down to there: *having followed Paul until his confession.* From which blessed Luke is shown fit for writing the Gospel, because he lived together with those who were most skilled; whence the second to Timothy, the last chapter[^p8-1]: *Only Luke is with me,* says the Apostle, because he clung to him as a faithful disciple.

3. Secondly he describes him as to his *merit,* which consists in life and grace; and he notes this when he says: *Serving the Lord without crime,* in which *life* is noted; and afterwards: *He died full of the Holy Spirit,* in which *grace* is noted, and therefore he was fit for writing the Gospel. Of which a figure went before in Joseph, Genesis, the forty-first chapter[^p8-2]: *Can we find such another man, that is full of the Spirit of God?* For just as Joseph dispensed the corn, so Luke the gospel teaching.

4. Thirdly he describes him as to his *office,* because he was divinely instigated to write the Gospel among the Greeks; and this is noted when he says: *He, when Gospels had already been written by Matthew in Judaea, by Mark in Italy;* where he shows that it was fitting that Luke should have the office of writing the Gospel in the third place, namely among the Greeks, that thus *their sound might go forth into all the earth*[^p8-3], and the Gospel might be preached and written in three kinds of languages, unto the mystery of the Trinity. And to this the writing of the title of the cross was suited, John, the nineteenth chapter[^p8-4], where it is said that that title: Jesus of Nazareth, the King of the Jews, was written in Hebrew, Greek and Latin.

5. But it is a doubt whether Mark wrote in the Latin tongue or in Greek, since Augustine[^p8-5] and Isidore say that all the Gospels except the Gospel of Matthew were written in Greek. But it is certain that he wrote in Italy[^p8-6]; and because both tongues were customary there at that time, it seems probable that he wrote in both tongues, especially since antiquity hands down that the Gospel which he wrote with his own hand is at Aquileia[^p8-7].

6. *For whom, beyond those things* etc. Here is described the *final* or motive cause, threefold, namely the *manifestation of the truth,* the *confutation of falsity* and the *honouring of the Lord's majesty.* — First, then, as to the *manifestation of the truth* he says: *For whom, beyond those things which the order of the gospel arrangement,* that is, of the history, *demands, this was above all the necessity,* that is, the moving necessity, *of the labour: that first for the Greek faithful,* because it was written first and principally for them, *by every prophecy of the Christ of God who was to come in the flesh, the humanity might be made manifest*[^p8-8]; *by every prophecy,* that is, it was foretold by all the Prophets that Christ God would come in the flesh. *The humanity might be made manifest,* because he was occupied about the humanity of Christ, according as we have been redeemed through it. Whence the second to Timothy, the second chapter[^p8-9]: *Be mindful that the Lord Jesus Christ is risen again from the dead, of the seed of David, according to my Gospel.* Now this Gospel was specially the Gospel of Luke, just as the Gospel of Mark was chiefly Peter's.
<!-- page 9 -->

7. Secondly, as to the *confutation of heretical depravity*[^p9-1] he says: *Lest, attentive to Jewish fables,* by which they had been seduced into holding that the legal observances could suffice without the Gospel, *they should be held in the desire of the Law alone,* namely that those could suffice etc. *Or lest heretical* etc., down to there: *he should labour*[^p9-2]. And the letter ought to be construed thus: *that he should labour, lest Jewish fables* etc. And about this it was needful to insist greatly in the primitive Church; whence the first to Timothy, the first chapter[^p9-3]: « I desired thee that thou wouldst charge some not to teach otherwise, and not to give heed to fables and endless genealogies, which furnish questions rather than the edification of God, which is in faith ».

8. Thirdly, as to the *honouring of the Lord's majesty* he adds: *Then that, in the beginning of the Gospel, the nativity of John being set first, to whom he was writing the Gospel,* « that is, to whose honour », as the Gloss says[^p9-4]; *he should indicate* etc. down to there: *begun by others.* For he wrote to the honour of Christ; whence Tobias, the twelfth chapter: « It is good to hide the secret of a king, but honourable to reveal and confess the works of God ». — Or it can be said that the third motive is the edification of fraternal charity; and he notes this when he says: *to whom he was writing,* because to Theophilus, who « is interpreted: *loving God,* or *loved by God*[^p9-5] »; in which it is shown that he wrote for building up fraternal charity, according to that of the first to Timothy, the first chapter: « The end of the commandment is charity from a pure heart and a good conscience and an unfeigned faith ».

9. *To whom therefore after the baptism* etc. Here is described the *formal cause* or mode of proceeding, first as to *this* book; secondly, as to the annexed book, namely the *Acts of the Apostles;* thirdly, as to the *preliminary prologue.* — First, then, as to *this* book he says why Luke describes the genealogy of Christ after the baptism, from the state of perfect age, and in retrograde order, ascending through Nathan. And he gives the reason of this, because he intended to treat of the generation of Christ according as through it we are led back into God; which indeed comes about through the baptismal and adoptive regeneration, Christ, the natural Son of God, leading us back to his Father, who is our Father also by adoption. And this is what he wishes to insinuate *through Nathan;* because, since Solomon was the natural son of David[^tr-corrigenda], the generation of Joseph and Mary does not ascend to David through Nathan according to the flesh, but through Solomon according to the *flesh,* and through Nathan according to *adoption,* as will be shown in the third chapter[^p9-6]. And this is what he wishes to say, and he says it very obscurely; and the letter ought to be construed thus: *To whom therefore was permitted the power of retracing the human nativity from the beginning,* that is, up to the beginning, namely up to God, and this *after the baptism, from the perfection of the generation,* that is, after the age of thirty years, as will be said in the third chapter[^p9-7]: « And Jesus himself was beginning about the age of thirty years, being, as it was supposed, the son of Joseph, who was of Heli » etc.; *that by the entrance of the generation of the indivisible God,* that is, of Christ equal to the Father;
<!-- page 10 -->
of the generation, namely *running back into God; the entrance,* I say, *being admitted,* that is, led down *through Nathan, the son* of David, *he might show to those seeking* God *in what he was apprehending,* namely God[^p10-1]. Here he shows that it is necessary to return to the Father through Christ; and this is what he adds: *That, preaching to men his Christ,* for Christ is ours; below, the second chapter[^p10-2]: « This day is born to us the Saviour of the world » etc.; *the work of the perfect man,* that is, of the man in whom all things are perfected, *to return into itself,* that is, into its state, from which it fell; *he might make through the Son,* through the Son, namely, of God and of man, Jesus Christ, *who was furnishing a way in Christ,* that is, was showing it to be furnished[^p10-3] *to those coming,* namely to God, *through David the father,* namely of Nathan, or of Christ; this is to say that he wished to show in that genealogy after the baptism how through Christ we, being regenerated, must return to God; to the Ephesians, the first chapter[^p10-4]: « It hath well pleased him to re-establish in Christ the things that are in heaven and the things that are on earth ».

10. Secondly[^p10-5], as to the annexed book, namely the *Acts of the Apostles,* he adds: *To which Luke not undeservedly is given also the power of writing the Acts of the Apostles in ministry* etc. down to there: *the Lord had chosen.* And he means that it was more fitting for Luke to describe the Acts than for the other Evangelists, because he treated of Christ inasmuch as he was leading back to the Father through grace; and this is what is most especially set forth in the Acts of the Apostles; which he notes when he says: *That, God being full unto God;* that is, shown fully by the ascension, through which the Holy Spirit was given fully, there might come about the *consummation* in Paul, who was *a vessel of election*[^p10-6], according to a special grace. Whence to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter: « He that descended is the same also that ascended, that he might fill all things »; to the Ephesians, the first chapter: « He hath made him head over all the Church, which is his body, and the fulness of him who filleth all in all ».

11. Thirdly, as to the present *prologue: And which, to those reading and seeking, though in detail,* that is, all the aforesaid things one by one and distinctly, *to treat*
<!-- page 11 -->
*had been useful; yet knowing that the labouring husbandman ought first to eat of the fruits*[^p11-1], *we have avoided public curiosity,* that is, superfluous loquacity or display of knowledge beyond necessity; *lest we should seem not so much to show God to those willing as to have betrayed him to the disdainful.* This is to say that because the doctor and expositor, like the husbandman, ought to eat of his own fruits, he ought not to seek curious things but fruitful ones, not the praise of others but the testimony of conscience, not the display of vanity but the edification of charity[^p11-2]. And this is the reason why it is frequently needful to forbear from many things and to say what is necessary, avoiding what is not opportune. — Or it can be expounded otherwise: because he did not wish to expound all things, so as to give others occasion of labouring and of eating of their own fruits; and this was figured in the Law, where it is said, Leviticus, the nineteenth chapter[^p11-3], to the reapers, that they should not gather the ears remaining, because the first doctors ought to leave to those after them an occasion of exercise. And in either way it can be expounded quite soundly.

[^p9-6]: **La.** Vers. 23. seqq. — Sedulius Scotus: Sed in principio sui Evangelii non Christi genealogiam proposuit, quomodo Matthaeus in suo fecit Evangelio, sed potius *Ioannis* tanquam praecursoris Domini *nativitate,* quam in aliis descriptam esse non viderat, *praesumta,* sic tandem, apto temporis ordine conservato, Christi nativitatem aut genealogiam pleniter enarravit. Unde sequitur: *Cui ideo post baptismum Filii Dei,* et reliqua usque: *permissa est.* Non caret ratione, quod post Christi baptismum Lucas eiusdem Salvatoris genealogiam edisserit. Haec namque *facultas repetendae nativitatis humanae* Evangelistae a Domino concessa est, et quamvis a fine septuagesimae et septimae *generationis in Christo impletae* ipsa series genealogiae per filios in patres ascendendo recurrit, non proprio tamen principio caret. Etenim *a principio,* id est Christo, qui est totius creationis principium, retrograda genealogia incipit, ut, quomodo ad Patrem principium recurrit, sic a Christo principio exordium ipsa generationum series sumat.

     **En.** V. 23 ff. — Sedulius Scotus: But in the beginning of his Gospel he did not set forth the genealogy of Christ, as Matthew did in his Gospel, but rather, *the nativity* of *John* as forerunner of the Lord, which he had not seen described in the others, *being set first,* so at last, keeping a fit order of time, he narrated fully the nativity or genealogy of Christ. Whence there follows: *To whom therefore after the baptism of the Son of God,* and the rest down to: *was permitted.* Not without reason does Luke set out the genealogy of the same Saviour after the baptism of Christ. For this *faculty of retracing the human nativity* was granted to the Evangelist by the Lord, and although from the end of the seventy-seventh *generation fulfilled in Christ* the series of the genealogy itself runs back, ascending through the sons to the fathers, yet it does not lack its own beginning. For the retrograde genealogy begins *from the beginning,* that is, from Christ, who is the beginning of the whole creation, so that, as it runs back to the Father as beginning, so the series of the generations itself may take its starting-point from Christ the beginning.

[^p9-7]: **La.** Vers. 23. seqq. — In A secunda manus post *annorum* addidit: *impletae in Christo, id est consummatae.* — Sedulius Scotus: Sequitur: *ut requirentibus demonstraret,* et reliqua usque: *indisparabilis Dei.* Studiosis et prudentibus Evangelista intimare curavit, in quo arcano mysterio non per Salomonem, quomodo Matthaeus, sed per Nathan, filium David, introitum *recurrentis in Deum generationis apprehenderat.* Sed quomodo ipsa genealogia in Deum recurrit, protinus declaratur, cum subditur: *admisso,* hoc est cum admissione seu receptione in ipsam genealogiam *indispertibilis Dei,* id est summi Patris, qui est a Filio inseparabilis; tanquam apertius diceret: Apte Deus Pater in Christi genealogia admittitur, cum Adam filius in eadem genealogia pronuntiatur, quoniam ipse Pater a Filio inseparabilis est. Alii namque et loco et tempore a filiis suis sunt dispertibiles. Quia vero summus Pater semper in Filio et Filius in Patre inseparabiliter manet, in eadem Filii etiam secundum carnem genealogia Pater admitti debuit. — Inferius vocibus *ut introitu* G interserit *in;* subinde pro *admisso, id est deducto* D *admisso, id est facto vel recepto* (G *id est inde ducto*). In A secunda manus hanc lectionem posuit: *recurrentis, id est retrograde praecedentis; in Deum, id est usque ad Deum; ut introitu, id est initio, admisso* etc. — Lyrani Glossa *interlinearis* dicit: *admisso,* id est deducto. Card. Hugo: *admisso:* id est deducto, et hoc *per Nathan, filium David.* Infra dicit *David patrem;* vide, quia Matthaeus et Lucas conveniunt in patribus ab Abraham usque ad David. Deinde dissentiunt, quia Matthaeus texuit genealogiam a David per Salomonem, Lucas a David per Nathan filium eius adoptivum.

     **En.** V. 23 ff. — In A a second hand added after *annorum:* *impletae in Christo, id est consummatae* ("fulfilled in Christ, that is, consummated"). — Sedulius Scotus: There follows: *that he might show to those seeking,* and the rest down to: *of the indivisible God.* The Evangelist took care to intimate to the studious and prudent in what hidden mystery he had apprehended the entrance *of the generation running back into God,* not through Solomon, as Matthew did, but through Nathan, the son of David. But how the genealogy itself runs back into God is declared straightway, when it is added: *admitted,* that is, with the admission or reception into the genealogy itself *of the indivisible God,* that is, of the supreme Father, who is inseparable from the Son; as though he were to say more openly: God the Father is fitly admitted into the genealogy of Christ, when Adam is pronounced a son in the same genealogy, since the Father himself is inseparable from the Son. For others are divisible from their sons both in place and in time. But because the supreme Father abides inseparably always in the Son and the Son in the Father, the Father had to be admitted into the same genealogy of the Son, even according to the flesh. — Below, G inserts *in* among the words *ut introitu;* next, in place of *admisso, id est deducto,* D has *admisso, id est facto vel recepto* (G *id est inde ducto*). In A a second hand put this reading: *recurrentis, id est retrograde praecedentis; in Deum, id est usque ad Deum; ut introitu, id est initio, admisso* etc. — Lyra's *interlinear* Gloss says: *admitted,* that is, led down. Cardinal Hugh: *admitted:* that is, led down, and this *through Nathan, the son of David.* Below he says *David the father;* see, Matthew and Luke agree in the fathers from Abraham down to David. Then they differ, because Matthew wove the genealogy from David through Solomon, Luke from David through Nathan his adoptive son.

Non-scripture in guillemets: the Gloss (¶ 8 *id est ad cuius honorem*) and the Theophilus etymology (¶ 8, with italic *amans Deum* / *amatus a Deo* inside the guillemets).

**COLLECTION CHANGE, half 2 — non-expositio sites: the *Prologus S. Hieronymi* (p. 7), BANKED.** Wholly roman, no guillemets, no italic. Its scriptural matter is allusive and woven into the sentence, never set off: Tit. 1:14 (*Iudaicis fabulis attenti*, p. 7 n. 5), John 17:12 (*filio perditionis*, n. 7), Acts 1:24–26 (*sorte … electionis numerus*, n. 7), Acts 9:5 / 26:14 (*contra stimulum recalcitrantem*, n. 7), II Tim. 2:6 (*operantem agricolam oporteat de fructibus suis edere*, n. 8). Role: allusion within a non-Bonaventurean text; no competing italic anywhere in the Prologus. **Five sites, all roman unmarked.** Fathers in the apparatus (Sedulius, Augustine, Isidore, Jerome, Baronius, Molini) were read for the apparatus only, not surveyed for face. Banked, not resolved; no discriminator proposed.
