# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. VII, Vers. 17-23
## *Secundo, declaratur sublimitas Doctoris propter evidentiam veritatis per testimonium Praecursoris*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 173 -->

#### Secundo, declaratur sublimitas Doctoris propter evidentiam veritatis per testimonium Praecursoris.

33. *Et exiit hic sermo* etc. Postquam ostensa est sufficienter excellentia potestatis in Christo per magnitudinem duplicis miraculi, hic secundo ostenditur *evidentia veritatis* in eodem per certitudinem veracis testimonii, quod quidem fuit *testimonium Praecursoris.* Ad huius autem testimonii certificationem dupliciter procedi potest et debet: aut per *probationem veritatis ipsius in se,* aut per *approbationem virtutis in testificante.* Primum respicit Praecursoris *doctrinam,* secundum vero respicit Praecursoris *vitam,* quorum utrumque facit ad comprobationem testimonii. Primo ergo in hac parte ostenditur, *Praecursoris testimonium esse verum;* secundo vero, *testificantis meritum esse magnum,* ibi[^p173-3]: *Et cum discessissent nuntii Ioannis* etc.

Circa primum autem, scilicet veritatem testimonii comprobandam, duo introducuntur. Primum est *quaestio Praecursoris;* secundum est *responsio Salvatoris,* ibi[^p173-4]: *In ipsa autem hora curavit multos* etc. Quaerit autem Ioannes ab eo cui perhibuerat testimonium, non propter dubium amovendum, sed propter testimonium approbandum per infallibile argumentum. — Proponitur autem quaestio ipsius hoc ordine. Primo enim introducitur quaestionis *occasio;* secundo vero, quaestionis *demandatio;* tertio vero, demandatae quaestionis *denuntiatio.*

34. (Vers. 17.). Introducitur ergo primo *quaerendi occasio* ex nominis Christi praeconio; quantum ad quod dicitur: *Et exiit hic sermo,* scilicet de suscitatione mortui, *in universam Iudaeam de eo et in omnem circa regionem*[^p173-5], ita ut possent omnes dicere illud Iob vigesimo octavo: « Auribus nostris audivimus famam eius »; et Iosue nono: « Audivimus famam potentiae eius », scilicet Dei tui; unde et Matthaei quarto dicitur, quod « abiit opinio eius in totam Syriam ». Cuius figura praecessit in Ozia rege; secundi Paralipomenon vigesimo sexto[^p173-6]: « Egressum est nomen eius procul, eo quod auxiliaretur ei Dominus et corroborasset eum ».

35. (Vers. 18.). Hoc autem famae praeconium, licet esset multis causa credendi, erat tamen aliquibus occasio invidendi, sicut discipulis Ioannis, quantum ad quos additur: *Et nuntiaverunt Ioanni discipuli eius de omnibus his.* Beda[^p173-7]: « Non simplici corde, sed invidia stimulante, sicut alibi, scilicet Ioannis tertio, queruntur dicentes: *Rabbi, qui erat tecum trans Iordanem, cui testimonium perhibuisti, ecce, hic baptizat, et omnes veniunt ad eum.* De talibus ad Philippenses primo[^p173-8]: « Quidam ex contentione, non sincere, Christum annuntiant ». Ex hoc ergo sumta est occasio quaerendi, utrum ipse esset, quem Ioannes praenuntiaverat, tum propter discipulorum invidiam exstirpandam, tum propter contentionem consopiendam, tum propter ipsorum dubitationem amovendam, tum etiam propter ipsam veritatem iam clarius divulgandam.

36. (Vers. 19.). Subditur ergo secundo *quaestionis demandatio* ex Ioannis officio, cum dicitur: *Et vocavit duos ex discipulis suis Ioannes et misit ad Iesum*[^p173-9]. Hoc enim spectat ad eius officium, missio discipulorum ad Christum. Praecursoris enim officium est, ad Christum mittere verbo pariter et officio, tam scilicet praedicando quam baptizando, secundum quod dicitur Actuum decimo nono[^p173-10]: « Ioannes baptizavit baptismo poenitentiae dicens, in eum qui venturus erat ut crederent ». Unde Glossa: « Misit eos ad Iesum, ut hac occasione videant signa, et correpti credant in eum ». Et Ambrosius[^p173-11]: « Misit discipulos ad Christum, ut supplementum scientiae assequantur, quia *plenitudo Legis est Christus,* et quia plerumque nutant dicta sine factis, eo fides plenior gestorum testificationibus quam verborum sponsionibus exhiberetur ». Unde mittebat eos ad Veritatem, ut ipsi certificarentur per veritatem, et
<!-- page 174 -->
certificati redderent testimonium veritati; et ideo duos misit, quia, secundum illud Ioannis octavo[^p174-1], vos dicitis, quod « duorum hominum testimonium verum est ». — Quia tamen adhuc dubitabant, ideo formam quaerendi eis dat, cum additur: *Dicens: Tu es qui venturus es,* id est, quem ego praedixi venturum; Ioannis primo[^p174-2]: « Post me venturus est qui ante me factus est », et in ceteris aliis Evangelistis. *An alium exspectamus?* quasi dicat: si tu es ille, iam alius non est exspectandus, ne forte, si alium exspectamus, suscipiamus non Christum, sed antichristum; de quo Ioannis quinto[^p174-3] dicit Dominus ad Iudaeos incredulos: « Ego veni in nomine Patris mei, et non recepistis me; si alius venerit in nomine suo, illum accipietis ».

37. (Vers. 20.). Subiungitur autem tertio *demandatae quaestionis relatio* discipulorum ministerio, cum subditur: *Cum autem venissent ad eum viri.* Isti duo viri designati sunt per duos exploratores missos ultra Iordanem, Iosue secundo[^p174-4]. Isti dicuntur *viri,* quia viriliter et fideliter impleverunt quod eis fuerat imperatum. Unde subditur: *Dixerunt: Ioannes Baptista misit nos ad te,* ut scilicet nos in persona eius a te quaereremus. — Quod notat, cum subdit: *Dicens: Tu es qui venturus es, an alium exspectamus?* Unde nota, quod quaestio ista proponitur a discipulis in persona Ioannis, ut ostendatur, quod discipuli intra se ferunt animum dubium, sed per Ioannem diriguntur ad Christum. — Unde Ioannes non per se quaestionem proposuit, quia non in se, sed in discipulis dubitavit. Et hoc est quod dicit Chrysostomus[^p174-5]: « Qui a Spiritu didicerat, qui vocem Patris audierat, qui ceteris praedicaverat, qui testimonium perhibuerat, quomodo post multa miracula, per quae multis innotuerat, dubitabat? Nunquid timidior propter incarcerationem factus fuerat? Absit, cum de eo Dominus dicat, non esse arundinem vento agitatam ». Unde non dubitavit, sed verba dubitantis proposuit ad certitudinem discipulorum, sicut dicitur Ioannis undecimo[^p174-6]: « Ubi posuistis eum »? et Ioannis sexto: « Unde ememus panes »? — Gregorius[^p174-7] tamen vult, quod dubitaverit et quaesierit non de primo adventu, de quo certus erat, sed de descensu ad inferos, quo Christum adhuc praecedere debebat. Ambrosius[^p174-8] vero vult, quod dubitaverit, non ex infidelitate, aut tarditate, ut discipuli, « sed quadam pietate », sicut dicitur in Glossa.

38. (Vers. 21.). *In ipsa autem hora* etc. Post propositam quaestionem subdit *quaestionis solutionem* ad discipulorum dubium removendum et Ioannis testimonium confirmandum. In quo Dominus perfectissime respondet, scilicet *quaestioni* et *quaerenti* et *omni contradicenti.* — Primum quidem respondet *quaestioni* faciendo miracula, per quae probatur, ipsum esse Christum sive Messiam; et ob hoc dicitur: *In ipsa autem hora,* scilicet adventus discipulorum, quae quidem erat hora propalandae veritatis, secundum illud ad Romanos decimo tertio[^p174-9]: « Hora est, iam nos de somno surgere » etc.; in hac ergo ad veritatem propalandam *curavit multos.* In huius figura dicitur de Simone, filio Oniae, Ecclesiastici quinquagesimo: « Curavit gentem suam et liberavit eam a perditione ». Et quia virtus miraculorum non solum attenditur in multitudine curatorum et ex parte personarum, sed etiam ex parte morborum; ideo subditur, quod a multiplici aegritudine curavit, scilicet *a languoribus,* quoad *morbi prolixitatem;* Ecclesiastici decimo[^p174-10]: « Languor prolixus gravat medicum. Brevem languorem praecidit medicus », ut sic impleretur illud Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio: « Vere languores nostros ipse tulit et infirmitates nostras ipse portavit ». — *A plagis*[^p174-11], quoad *morbi acerbitatem,* secundum illud Ieremiae trigesimo: « Plaga inimici percussi te, castigatione crudeli ». Ab his curavit Dominus, sicut in Psalmo petitur: « Amove a me plagas tuas » etc. — *Et spiritibus malis,* quantum ad *impetum alienae violentiae,* qui fit per spiritum malum; primi Regum decimo sexto[^p174-12]: « Spiritus nequam a Domino exagitabat Saul. » — *Et caecis multis donavit visum,* in quo notatur *defectus intrinsecae potentiae,* ut sic impleatur illud Isaiae vigesimo nono[^p174-13]: « De tenebris et caligine oculi caecorum videbunt ». Et per haec manifestat, se esse illum qui erat venturus; Ioannis quinto: « Vos misistis ad Ioannem, et testimonium perhibuit veritati ».
<!-- page 175 -->
« Sed ego testimonium maius habeo ». « Et opera, quae ego facio, ipsa testimonium perhibent de me ».

39. (Vers. 22.). Secundo vero respondet *quaerenti* denuntiando prodigia a discipulis visa et audita; propter quod dicit: *Et respondens dixit illis: Euntes renuntiate Ioanni quae audistis et vidistis,* quia, sicut dicitur Tobiae duodecimo[^p175-1], « opera Dei revelare et confiteri honorificum est ». Mandat eis denuntiare quae viderunt et audierunt, quia isti duo sensus plurimas nobis differentias ostendunt, ut in hoc ostendatur perfectior fides Ioannis, qui credidit solum audiendo, quam istorum qui viderunt et crediderunt, secundum illud Ioannis vigesimo de Thoma, cui dicitur: « Quia vidisti me, Thoma, credidisti. Beati, qui non viderunt et crediderunt ».

40. Praecipit autem eis narrare prodigia, quae quidem sunt praecipua miracula propter impossibilitatem respectu virtutis creatae, sicut est *restitutio visus, rectificatio gressus, mundatio corporis totius, reparatio auditus, restitutio vitae* et *sublimatio inopiae.* — Quantum ad *restitutionem visus* dicit: *Caeci*[^p175-2] *vident;* Isaiae trigesimo quinto: « Tunc aperientur oculi caecorum », scilicet in adventu Christi; Ioannis nono: « A saeculo non est auditum, quod quis aperuit oculos caeci nati ». — Quantum ad *rectificationem gressus* dicitur: *Claudi ambulant;* Michaeae quarto[^p175-3]: « In die illa congregabo claudicantem et eam quam eieceram, colligam »; Isaiae trigesimo quinto: « Tunc saliet sicut cervus claudus, et aperta erit lingua mutorum ». — Quantum ad *mundationem corporis totius* dicitur: *Leprosi mundantur,* sicut infra decimo septimo[^p175-4] leguntur decem leprosi esse mundati, et sicut dicitur Iob decimo quarto: « Quis potest facere mundum de immundo conceptum semine? Nonne tu, qui solus es »? Tu ergo, qui hoc facis, es ille qui venturus es. — Quantum ad *apertionem auditus* subditur: *Surdi audiunt;* Isaiae trigesimo quinto[^p175-5]: « Aures surdorum patebunt », scilicet in adventu Christi, secundum illud Marci septimo: « Bene omnia fecit: et surdos fecit audire et mutos loqui ». — Quantum ad *reparationem vitae* dicitur: *Mortui resurgunt,* secundum illud Ezechielis trigesimo septimo[^p175-6]: « Sciatis, quia ego Dominus, cum aperuero sepulcra vestra et eduxero vos de tumulis vestris et dedero spiritum meum in vobis, et vixeritis »; et hoc est in adventu Christi; Ioannis quinto: « Venit hora, et nunc est, quando mortui audient vocem Filii Dei ». — Postremo quantum ad *sublimationem inopiae* subditur: *Pauperes evangelizantur,* id est, per Evangelium praedicantur et exaltantur; quod patet, quia sermo Domini incepit a commendatione pauperum; Matthaei quinto[^p175-7]: « Beati pauperes », et supra sexto. Et hoc erat certum signum adventus Christi, secundum illud Isaiae vigesimo nono: « Pauperes homines in sancto Israel exsultabunt »; quia, sicut dicitur Isaiae ultimo, « ad quem respiciam nisi ad pauperculum et contritum spiritu » etc. Et Iacobi secundo[^p175-8]: « Nonne Deus elegit pauperes in hoc mundo » etc.; quia de ipso Christo dictum est: « Parcet pauperi et inopi et animas pauperum salvas faciet »; et post: « Et honorabile nomen eorum coram illo ». Et illud erat magnum prodigium, quod honorabile fieret et amabile et praedicabile nomen pauperum; quod non fuit nisi per Iesum, qui se fecit pauperem ad nos pauperes ditandos et honorandos; secundae ad Corinthios octavo[^p175-9]: « Scitis gratiam Domini nostri Iesu Christi, quoniam propter vos egenus factus est, cum esset dives ».

41. Et nota, quod magis dicit: *Pauperes evangelizantur,* quam virgines vel obedientes, quia paupertas fundamentum est evangelicae perfectionis. Quia fundamentum civitatis Babylonis est avaritia, secundum illud primae ad Timotheum ultimo[^p175-10]: « Radix omnium malorum cupiditas »; et « superbia omnis peccati initium », secundum quod dicitur Ecclesiastici decimo; sic paupertas spiritus, quae includit oppositum utriusque, scilicet paupertatem et humilitatem, est *fundamentum* perfectionis evangelicae; est etiam *consummatio* eiusdem, secundum illud secundae ad Corinthios octavo[^p175-11]: « Altissima paupertas eorum abundavit in divitias simplicitatis eorum ».

42. Et nota, quod paupertas est evangelizabilis et praedicabilis propter decem excellentissimas dignitates. — Primo, propter *intelligentiam infirmitatis propriae;* Threnorum tertio[^p175-12]: « Ego vir videns paupertatem meam » etc.; quocontra de divite Apocalypsis tertio: « Dicis, quia dives sum et locupletatus et nullius egeo, et nescis, quia tu es miser » etc. — Secundo, propter *excellentiam virtutis gratuitae;* secundae ad Corinthios octavo[^p175-13]: « Altissima paupertas » etc.; et Genesis quadragesimo primo: « Crescere
<!-- page 176 -->
me fecit Dominus in terra paupertatis meae ». — Tertio, propter *affluentiam iucunditatis internae;* Isaiae vigesimo nono[^p176-1]: « Pauperes homines in sancto Israel exsultabunt »; et in Psalmo: « Videant pauperes et laetentur; quaerite Deum, et vivet anima vestra ». — Quarto, propter *copiam abundantis sufficientiae;* Tobiae quinto[^p176-2]: « Sufficiebat nobis paupertas nostra » etc.; et iterum Proverbiorum duodecimo: « Melior est pauper et sufficiens sibi quam gloriosus et indigens pane »; quocontra Ecclesiastici decimo quarto: « Insatiabilis oculus cupidi ». — Quinto, propter *custodiam protectionis supernae;* Proverbiorum vigesimo secundo[^p176-3]: « Ne facias violentiam pauperi, quia pauper est, neque conteras egenum in porta, quia Dominus iudicabit causam eius et configet eos qui confixerunt eum »; Iob quinto: « Salvum faciet egenum a gladio oris eorum et de manu violenti pauperem »; et Psalmus: « Factus est Dominus refugium pauperi, adiutor in opportunitatibus, in tribulatione ».

43. Sexto, propter *complacentiam acceptationis divinae;* Isaiae ultimo[^p176-4]: « Ad quem respiciam nisi ad pauperculum » etc.; et in Psalmo: « Oculi eius in pauperem respiciunt »; et Ecclesiastici undecimo: « Est homo marcidus »; « et abundans paupertate, et oculus Dei respexit illum in bono et erexit eum ab humilitate ipsius ». — Septimo, propter *condescendentiam pietatis paternae;* Psalmus[^p176-5]: « Iste pauper clamavit, et Dominus exaudivit eum »; iterum: « Propter miseriam inopum et gemitum pauperum nunc exsurgam »; et rursus: « Parcet pauperi et inopi ». — Octavo, propter *eminentiam auctoritatis iudiciariae;* Iob trigesimo sexto[^p176-6]: « Non salvat impium et iudicium pauperibus tribuit »; Matthaei decimo nono: « Vos, qui reliquistis omnia, sedebitis super sedes » etc. — Nono, propter *evidentiam perfectionis probatae;* Isaiae quadragesimo octavo[^p176-7]: « Elegi te in camino paupertatis »; et Apocalypsis secundo: « Scio tribulationem tuam et paupertatem tuam, sed blasphemaris ab his qui se dicunt Iudaeos, et non sunt, sed sunt synagoga satanae » etc. — Decimo, propter *excellentiam regalis praesidentiae;* Iacobi secundo[^p176-8]: « Nonne Deus elegit pauperes in hoc mundo heredes regni, quod repromisit Deus diligentibus se »? Matthaei quinto: « Beati pauperes spiritu, quoniam ipsorum est regnum caelorum ». — Si igitur despectissimum est genus pauperum et excellentissimum genus divitum, excellentissimum miraculum est, quod pauperes ut reges evangelizantur.

44. (Vers. 23.). Postremo autem respondet omni *contradicenti,* comprimendo iudicia temeraria, quae praecipitant homines in diversa scandala. Propter quod adiungit: *Et beatus, qui*[^p176-9] *non fuerit scandalizatus in me,* id est, qui non iudicaverit me hominem purum et non Deum, in consideratione infirmitatis assumtae; quod quidem erat valde difficile, immo impossibile non credenti; unde primae ad Corinthios primo[^p176-10]: « Nos praedicamus Christum crucifixum, Iudaeis quidem scandalum, gentibus autem stultitiam ». Sed quidam scandalizati sunt propter *infirmitatem,* sicut discipuli; Matthaei vigesimo sexto: « Omnes in me scandalum patiemini in nocte ista »; quidam autem ex *malignitate,* sicut scribae et Pharisaei, secundum illud Matthaei decimo quinto: « Scitis, quia Pharisaei, audito hoc verbo, scandalizati sunt »? Unde generale documentum est hoc omnibus volentibus nosse Christum, ut propter infirma, quae passus est, non incurrant scandalum, secundum illud Isaiae octavo[^p176-11]: « Dominum exercituum ipsum sanctificate, et erit vobis in sanctificationem; in lapidem autem offensionis et in petram scandali duabus domibus Israel et in ruinam habitantibus Ierusalem ». Et ideo recte talem vocat *beatum,* quia vitat periculum erroris et pervenit ad lucem veritatis, sicut Petrus, cui dicitur Matthaei decimo sexto[^p176-12]: « Beatus es, Simon Bariona, quia caro et sanguis non revelavit tibi » etc.

45. *Spiritualiter* hic nota, quod ex curatione morborum praesentialiter exhibita intelligitur perfecta curatio ab *originali;* sed ex curatione morborum denuntiata Ioanni, perfecta curatio ab *actuali.*

Quantum ad curationem ab *originali* nota, quod ad hoc, quod perfecte curetur, oportet, quod fiat curatio et sanatio a quatuor, quae fuerunt propter originale peccatum inflicta, scilicet a *concupiscentia, impotentia, malitia* et *ignorantia*[^p176-13]. Et haec quatuor designantur per curationem quatuor generum aegritudinum. Nam *concupiscentia* intelligitur per *languorem* propter adhaerentiam universalem, secundum illud Isaiae primo[^p176-14]: « Omne caput languidum, et omne cor moerens ». *Impotentia,* per *plagam;* Ieremiae decimo: « Vae mihi super contritione mea! pessima plaga mea ». Per *spiritum malignum* intelligitur *nequitia;* primi Regum decimo sexto: « Exagitabat Saul spiritus nequam ». Per *caecitatem,*
<!-- page 177 -->
*ignorantia;* Isaiae quinquagesimo nono[^p177-1]: « Palpavimus quasi caeci in meridie »; et quinquagesimo sexto: « Speculatores eius caeci omnes ». — In curatione ergo huius quadruplicis aegritudinis intelligitur perfecta curatio originalis.

46. Quantum ad curationem perfectam ab *actuali* nota, quod in consummatione impietatis in actuali quinque sunt gradus. Primus est *deviatio in eligendo,* et haec per *caecitatem* designatur; Threnorum quarto[^p177-2]: « Erraverunt caeci in plateis »; quia « error et tenebrae peccatoribus concreata sunt », secundum illud Ecclesiastici undecimo. — Secundus est *deordinatio in prosequendo,* quae intelligitur per *claudos;* Psalmus[^p177-3]: « Filii alieni inveterati sunt et claudicaverunt a semitis suis »; unde tertii Regum decimo octavo: « Usquequo claudicatis in duas partes » etc. — Tertius, *contagio in conversando,* quae per *lepram* intelligitur; Deuteronomii vigesimo quarto[^p177-4]: « Observa diligenter, ne incurras plagam leprae »; Ezechielis trigesimo sexto: « Mundamini ab omnibus inquinamentis vestris ». — Quartus est *obduratio in perdurando,* quae per *surditatem* intelligitur, secundum illud Psalmi[^p177-5]: « Sicut aspidis surdae et obturantis aures suas ». — Quintus est *desperatio in contemnendo,* quia « impius, cum in profundum venerit peccatorum, contemnit », Proverbiorum decimo octavo[^p177-6]. Et haec designatur per *mortem;* Isaiae vigesimo octavo: « Percussimus foedus cum morte »; et hoc « est peccatum ad mortem », de quo dicitur primae Ioannis quinto. — Ab omnibus curat virtus Christi per gratiam suam, quae designata fuit in piscina, de qua dicitur Ioannis quinto[^p177-7], quod « Angelus Domini descendebat, et movebatur aqua, et qui prior descendebat sanus fiebat, a quacumque detinebatur infirmitate ». Et haec est « virtus, quae de illo exibat et sanabat omnes », secundum quod dicitur supra sexto.

## English
<!-- page 173 -->

#### Secondly, the sublimity of the Doctor is declared on account of the evidence of truth through the testimony of the Precursor.

33. *And this rumour went forth* etc. After the excellence of power in Christ has been sufficiently shown through the greatness of a twofold miracle, here secondly is shown the *evidence of truth* in the same through the certitude of a truthful testimony, which indeed was the *testimony of the Precursor.* Now for the certifying of this testimony one can and ought to proceed in two ways: either through the *proving of the truth itself in itself,* or through the *approving of the virtue in the one testifying.* The first regards the Precursor's *doctrine,* but the second regards the Precursor's *life,* each of which makes for the proving of the testimony. First, then, in this part it is shown that *the Precursor's testimony is true;* but secondly, that *the merit of the one testifying is great,* there[^p173-3]: *And when the messengers of John were departed* etc.

Now about the first, namely the truth of the testimony that is to be proved, two things are brought in. The first is the *question of the Precursor;* the second is the *answer of the Saviour,* there[^p173-4]: *And in that same hour he cured many* etc. Now John asks of him to whom he had given testimony, not for the removing of a doubt, but for the approving of the testimony through an infallible proof. — And his question is set forth in this order. For first is brought in the *occasion* of the question; but secondly, the *commissioning* of the question; but thirdly, the *reporting* of the question commissioned.

34. (Vers. 17.). First, then, is brought in the *occasion of asking* from the proclamation of the name of Christ; as to which it is said: *And this rumour went forth,* namely of the raising of the dead man, *throughout all Judea concerning him, and throughout all the country round about*[^p173-5], so that all could say that of Job, the twenty-eighth chapter: « With our ears we have heard the fame thereof »; and of Josue, the ninth chapter: « We have heard the fame of his power », namely of thy God; whence also in Matthew, the fourth chapter, it is said that « his fame went throughout all Syria ». The figure of which went before in king Ozias; the second of Paralipomenon, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p173-6]: « His name went forth far, because the Lord helped him and had strengthened him ».

35. (Vers. 18.). But this proclamation of fame, though it was to many a cause of believing, was yet to some an occasion of envying, as to the disciples of John, as to whom it is added: *And his disciples shewed John of all these things.* Bede[^p173-7]: « Not with a simple heart, but with envy goading, as elsewhere, namely in John, the third chapter, they complain, saying: *Rabbi, he that was with thee beyond the Jordan, to whom thou gavest testimony, behold he baptizeth, and all men come to him.* Of such it is said to the Philippians, the first chapter[^p173-8]: « Some out of contention, not sincerely, announce Christ ». From this, then, was taken the occasion of asking whether he were the one whom John had foretold: as well for rooting out the envy of the disciples, as for lulling the contention to sleep, as for removing their doubting, as also for spreading abroad the truth itself now more clearly.

36. (Vers. 19.). Secondly, then, is subjoined the *commissioning of the question* from John's office, when it is said: *And John called two of his disciples and sent to Jesus*[^p173-9]. For this belongs to his office, the sending of disciples to Christ. For the office of the Precursor is to send to Christ by word and by office alike, namely as well by preaching as by baptizing, according to that which is said in Acts, the nineteenth chapter[^p173-10]: « John baptized with the baptism of penance, saying that they should believe in him who was to come ». Whence the Gloss: « He sent them to Jesus, that by this occasion they might see signs, and, corrected, believe in him ». And Ambrose[^p173-11]: « He sent his disciples to Christ, that they might obtain a supplement of knowledge, because *Christ is the fulness of the Law,* and because words without deeds commonly waver, so that faith might be shown more full by the testimonies of things done than by the promises of words ». Whence he sent them to the Truth, that they themselves might be certified through the truth, and
<!-- page 174 -->
being certified might render testimony to the truth; and therefore he sent two, because, according to that of John, the eighth chapter[^p174-1], you say that « the testimony of two men is true ». — Yet because they were still doubting, he therefore gives them the form of asking, when it is added: *Saying: Art thou he that art to come,* that is, whom I foretold to be coming; John, the first chapter[^p174-2]: « He that shall come after me, who is preferred before me », and in the other Evangelists too. *Or look we for another?* as if he were saying: if thou art he, another is now not to be looked for, lest perhaps, if we look for another, we receive not Christ but antichrist; of whom in John, the fifth chapter[^p174-3], the Lord says to the unbelieving Jews: « I am come in the name of my Father, and you received me not; if another shall come in his own name, him you will receive ».

37. (Vers. 20.). But thirdly is subjoined the *reporting of the question commissioned* by the ministry of the disciples, when it is subjoined: *And when the men were come unto him.* These two men were designated by the two spies sent beyond the Jordan, Josue, the second chapter[^p174-4]. These are called *men,* because they manfully and faithfully fulfilled what had been commanded them. Whence it is subjoined: *They said: John the Baptist hath sent us to thee,* namely that we in his person should ask of thee. — Which he notes when he subjoins: *Saying: Art thou he that art to come, or look we for another?* Whence note that this question is put by the disciples in the person of John, that it may be shown that the disciples bear a doubting mind within themselves, but are directed to Christ through John. — Whence John did not put the question for himself, because he doubted not in himself but in his disciples. And this is what Chrysostom says[^p174-5]: « He who had learned from the Spirit, who had heard the voice of the Father, who had preached to the others, who had given testimony, how after many miracles, by which he had become known to many, was he doubting? Had he become more timid on account of imprisonment? God forbid, since the Lord says of him that he is not a reed shaken with the wind ». Whence he did not doubt, but put forward the words of one doubting for the certitude of the disciples, as it is said in John, the eleventh chapter[^p174-6]: « Where have you laid him »? and in John, the sixth chapter: « Whence shall we buy bread »? — Gregory[^p174-7], however, would have it that he doubted and asked not about the first coming, of which he was certain, but about the descent into hell, in which he was yet to go before Christ. But Ambrose[^p174-8] would have it that he doubted, not from unbelief, or slowness, as the disciples, « but from a certain piety », as it is said in the Gloss.

38. (Vers. 21.). *And in that same hour* etc. After the question put he subjoins the *solution of the question,* for removing the doubt of the disciples and confirming the testimony of John. In which the Lord answers most perfectly, namely to the *question* and to the *one asking* and to *every gainsayer.* — First indeed he answers the *question* by working miracles, through which it is proved that he himself is the Christ or Messias; and on this account it is said: *And in that same hour,* namely of the coming of the disciples, which indeed was the hour for making the truth public, according to that of Romans, the thirteenth chapter[^p174-9]: « It is now the hour for us to rise from sleep » etc.; in this hour, then, for making the truth public, *he cured many.* In figure of this it is said of Simon, the son of Onias, in Ecclesiasticus, the fiftieth chapter: « He cured his nation and delivered it from destruction ». And because the power of the miracles is regarded not only in the multitude of those cured and on the part of the persons, but also on the part of the diseases; therefore it is subjoined that he cured from manifold sickness, namely *from diseases,* as to the *length of the malady;* Ecclesiasticus, the tenth chapter[^p174-10]: « A long disease is troublesome to the physician. The physician cutteth off a short disease », so that thus might be fulfilled that of Isaias, the fifty-third chapter: « Surely he hath borne our diseases and carried our infirmities ». — *From strokes*[^p174-11], as to the *bitterness of the malady,* according to that of Jeremias, the thirtieth chapter: « With the stroke of an enemy I have struck thee, with a cruel chastisement ». From these the Lord cured, as it is asked in the Psalm: « Remove thy strokes from me » etc. — *And evil spirits,* as to the *assault of an outside violence,* which is done through an evil spirit; the first of Kings, the sixteenth chapter[^p174-12]: « An evil spirit from the Lord troubled Saul. » — *And to many that were blind he gave sight,* in which is noted the *defect of an inward power,* so that thus may be fulfilled that of Isaias, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p174-13]: « Out of darkness and obscurity the eyes of the blind shall see ». And by these things he manifests that he himself is the one who was to come; John, the fifth chapter: « You sent to John, and he gave testimony to the truth ».
<!-- page 175 -->
« But I have a greater testimony ». « And the works which I do, they give testimony of me ».

39. (Vers. 22.). But secondly he answers the *one asking* by reporting the wonders seen and heard by the disciples; on account of which he says: *And answering, he said to them: Go and relate to John what you have heard and seen,* because, as it is said in Tobias, the twelfth chapter[^p175-1], « it is honourable to reveal and confess the works of God ». He bids them report what they saw and heard, because these two senses show us very many differences, that in this it may be shown that the faith of John, who believed by hearing only, was more perfect than that of these, who saw and believed, according to that of John, the twentieth chapter, concerning Thomas, to whom it is said: « Because thou hast seen me, Thomas, thou hast believed. Blessed are they that have not seen and have believed ».

40. Now he enjoins them to narrate the wonders, which indeed are the chief miracles on account of their impossibility in respect of created power, as is the *restoring of sight, the straightening of the gait, the cleansing of the whole body, the repairing of hearing, the restoring of life* and the *uplifting of want.* — As to the *restoring of sight* he says: *The blind*[^p175-2] *see;* Isaias, the thirty-fifth chapter: « Then shall the eyes of the blind be opened », namely in the coming of Christ; John, the ninth chapter: « From the beginning of the world it hath not been heard, that any man hath opened the eyes of one born blind ». — As to the *straightening of the gait* it is said: *The lame walk;* Micheas, the fourth chapter[^p175-3]: « In that day I will gather up her that halteth, and her that I had cast out I will gather up »; Isaias, the thirty-fifth chapter: « Then shall the lame man leap as a hart, and the tongue of the dumb shall be opened ». — As to the *cleansing of the whole body* it is said: *The lepers are made clean,* as below, in the seventeenth chapter[^p175-4], ten lepers are read to have been made clean, and as it is said in Job, the fourteenth chapter: « Who can make clean him that is conceived of unclean seed? Is it not thou who only art »? Thou, then, who doest this, art he who is to come. — As to the *opening of hearing* it is subjoined: *The deaf hear;* Isaias, the thirty-fifth chapter[^p175-5]: « The ears of the deaf shall be unstopped », namely in the coming of Christ, according to that of Mark, the seventh chapter: « He hath done all things well: he hath made both the deaf to hear and the dumb to speak ». — As to the *repairing of life* it is said: *The dead rise again,* according to that of Ezechiel, the thirty-seventh chapter[^p175-6]: « Know ye that I am the Lord, when I shall have opened your sepulchres and shall have brought you out of your graves and shall have put my spirit in you, and you shall live »; and this is in the coming of Christ; John, the fifth chapter: « The hour cometh, and now is, when the dead shall hear the voice of the Son of God ». — Lastly, as to the *uplifting of want* it is subjoined: *To the poor the gospel is preached*[^tr-evangelizare], that is, they are preached through the Gospel and exalted; which is plain, because the Lord's sermon began from the commendation of the poor; Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p175-7]: « Blessed are the poor », and above, in the sixth chapter. And this was a certain sign of the coming of Christ, according to that of Isaias, the twenty-ninth chapter: « The poor men shall exult in the Holy One of Israel »; because, as it is said in Isaias, the last chapter, « to whom shall I have respect, but to him that is poor and little, and of a contrite spirit » etc. And James, the second chapter[^p175-8]: « Hath not God chosen the poor in this world » etc.; because of Christ himself it was said: « He shall spare the poor and needy, and he shall save the souls of the poor »; and after: « And their name shall be honourable in his sight ». And that was a great wonder, that the name of the poor should become honourable and lovable and worthy to be preached; which was not but through Jesus, who made himself poor for the enriching and honouring of us poor; the second to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter[^p175-9]: « You know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that for your sakes he became needy, when he was rich ».

41. And note that he says rather *To the poor the gospel is preached* than to virgins or to the obedient, because poverty is the foundation of evangelical perfection. Because the foundation of the city of Babylon is avarice, according to that of the first to Timothy, the last chapter[^p175-10]: « Covetousness is the root of all evils »; and « pride is the beginning of all sin », according to that which is said in Ecclesiasticus, the tenth chapter; so poverty of spirit, which includes the opposite of each, namely poverty and humility, is the *foundation* of evangelical perfection; it is also its *consummation,* according to that of the second to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter[^p175-11]: « Their very deep poverty abounded unto the riches of their simplicity ».

42. And note that poverty is worthy to be preached as gospel and worthy to be preached on account of ten most excellent dignities. — First, on account of the *intelligence of one's own infirmity;* Lamentations, the third chapter[^p175-12]: « I am the man that see my poverty » etc.; whereas of the rich man, Apocalypse, the third chapter: « Thou sayest: I am rich and made wealthy and have need of nothing, and knowest not that thou art wretched » etc. — Secondly, on account of the *excellence of gratuitous virtue;* the second to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter[^p175-13]: « Their very deep poverty » etc.; and Genesis, the forty-first chapter: « God hath made me to grow
<!-- page 176 -->
in the land of my poverty ». — Thirdly, on account of the *affluence of inward joyfulness;* Isaias, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p176-1]: « The poor men shall exult in the Holy One of Israel »; and in the Psalm: « Let the poor see and be glad; seek ye God, and your soul shall live ». — Fourthly, on account of the *plenty of abundant sufficiency;* Tobias, the fifth chapter[^p176-2]: « Our poverty was sufficient for us » etc.; and again Proverbs, the twelfth chapter: « Better is the poor man and sufficient for himself than he that is glorious and wanteth bread »; whereas Ecclesiasticus, the fourteenth chapter: « The eye of the covetous man is insatiable ». — Fifthly, on account of the *keeping of heavenly protection;* Proverbs, the twenty-second chapter[^p176-3]: « Do no violence to the poor, because he is poor, and do not oppress the needy in the gate, because the Lord will judge his cause and will pierce them that have pierced him »; Job, the fifth chapter: « He shall save the needy from the sword of their mouth, and the poor from the hand of the violent »; and the Psalm: « The Lord is become a refuge for the poor, a helper in due time, in tribulation ».

43. Sixthly, on account of the *good pleasure of divine acceptance;* Isaias, the last chapter[^p176-4]: « To whom shall I have respect, but to him that is poor and little » etc.; and in the Psalm: « His eyes look on the poor man »; and Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter: « There is an inactive man »; « and full of poverty, and the eye of God hath looked upon him for good, and hath lifted him up from his low estate ». — Seventhly, on account of the *condescension of fatherly piety*[^tr-pietas]; the Psalm[^p176-5]: « This poor man cried, and the Lord heard him »; again: « By reason of the misery of the needy and the groans of the poor, now will I arise »; and again: « He shall spare the poor and needy ». — Eighthly, on account of the *eminence of judiciary authority;* Job, the thirty-sixth chapter[^p176-6]: « He saveth not the wicked, and he giveth judgement to the poor »; Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « You who have left all things shall sit on seats » etc. — Ninthly, on account of the *evidence of proved perfection;* Isaias, the forty-eighth chapter[^p176-7]: « I have chosen thee in the furnace of poverty »; and Apocalypse, the second chapter: « I know thy tribulation and thy poverty, but thou art blasphemed by them that say they are Jews and are not, but are the synagogue of Satan » etc. — Tenthly, on account of the *excellence of royal presidency;* James, the second chapter[^p176-8]: « Hath not God chosen the poor in this world heirs of the kingdom which God hath promised to them that love him »? Matthew, the fifth chapter: « Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven ». — If, then, the race of the poor is the most despised and the race of the rich the most excellent, it is a most excellent miracle that the poor, as kings, are preached in the gospel.

44. (Vers. 23.). But lastly he answers *every gainsayer,* restraining rash judgements, which cast men headlong into divers scandals. On account of which he adds: *And blessed is he that*[^p176-9] *shall not be scandalized in me,* that is, who shall not have judged me a mere man and not God, in consideration of the infirmity assumed; which indeed was very difficult, nay impossible, for one not believing; whence the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p176-10]: « We preach Christ crucified, unto the Jews indeed a scandal, and unto the Gentiles foolishness ». But some were scandalized on account of *infirmity,* as the disciples; Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter: « All you shall suffer scandal in me this night »; but some from *malignity,* as the scribes and Pharisees, according to that of Matthew, the fifteenth chapter: « Know ye that the Pharisees, when they heard this word, were scandalized »? Whence this is a general instruction for all who will to know Christ, that on account of the infirm things which he suffered they incur not scandal, according to that of Isaias, the eighth chapter[^p176-11]: « Sanctify the Lord of hosts himself, and he shall be to you for a sanctification; but for a stone of offence and for a rock of scandal to the two houses of Israel, and for a ruin to the inhabitants of Jerusalem ». And therefore rightly he calls such a one *blessed,* because he shuns the peril of error and arrives at the light of truth, as Peter, to whom it is said in Matthew, the sixteenth chapter[^p176-12]: « Blessed art thou, Simon Bar-Jona, because flesh and blood hath not revealed it to thee » etc.

45. *Spiritually* note here that by the curing of maladies shown in presence is understood the perfect curing from *original* sin; but by the curing of maladies reported to John, the perfect curing from *actual* sin.

As to the curing from *original* sin note that, in order that one be perfectly cured, it is needful that there be a curing and healing from four things which were inflicted on account of original sin, namely from *concupiscence, impotence, malice* and *ignorance*[^p176-13]. And these four are designated by the curing of four kinds of sicknesses. For *concupiscence* is understood by *disease,* on account of its universal adherence, according to that of Isaias, the first chapter[^p176-14]: « The whole head is sick, and the whole heart is sad ». *Impotence,* by the *stroke;* Jeremias, the tenth chapter: « Woe is me for my destruction! my stroke is very grievous ». By the *evil spirit* is understood *wickedness;* the first of Kings, the sixteenth chapter: « An evil spirit troubled Saul ». By *blindness,*
<!-- page 177 -->
*ignorance;* Isaias, the fifty-ninth chapter[^p177-1]: « We have groped as the blind at noonday »; and the fifty-sixth chapter: « His watchmen are all blind ». — In the curing, then, of this fourfold sickness is understood the perfect curing of original sin.

46. As to the perfect curing from *actual* sin note that in the consummation of impiety in actual sin there are five degrees. The first is *deviation in choosing,* and this is designated by *blindness;* Lamentations, the fourth chapter[^p177-2]: « They have wandered as blind men in the streets »; because « error and darkness are created with sinners », according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter. — The second is *disordering in pursuing,* which is understood by the *lame;* the Psalm[^p177-3]: « The children that are strangers have grown old, and they have halted from their paths »; whence the third of Kings, the eighteenth chapter: « How long do you halt between two sides » etc. — The third, *contagion in conversing,* which is understood by *leprosy;* Deuteronomy, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p177-4]: « Observe diligently that thou incur not the stroke of the leprosy »; Ezechiel, the thirty-sixth chapter: « Be ye cleansed from all your filthiness ». — The fourth is *obduracy in persisting,* which is understood by *deafness,* according to that of the Psalm[^p177-5]: « As of the deaf asp that stoppeth her ears ». — The fifth is *despair in contemning,* because « the wicked man, when he is come into the depth of sins, contemneth », Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter[^p177-6]. And this is designated by *death;* Isaias, the twenty-eighth chapter: « We have entered into a league with death »; and this « is a sin unto death », of which it is said in the first of John, the fifth chapter. — From all of them the power of Christ cures through his grace, which was designated in the pond, of which it is said in John, the fifth chapter[^p177-7], that « an angel of the Lord descended, and the water was moved, and he that went down first was made whole, of whatsoever infirmity he lay under ». And this is the « power which went out from him and healed all », according to that which is said above, in the sixth chapter.

[^tr-evangelizare]: **En.** Translator's note: *Pauperes evangelizantur* (Luke 7:22, the lemma in ¶¶ 40–41, and ¶ 43's *pauperes ut reges evangelizantur*) is rendered with the Douay's own wording at this verse, "to the poor the gospel is preached", which is also this translation's rendering of *evangelizare*, "preach the gospel", never bare "preach" (kept for *praedicare*). The Latin passive has the poor for its subject, and Bonaventure reads it so: *id est, per Evangelium praedicantur et exaltantur*, "that is, they are preached through the Gospel and exalted", i.e. they are made the matter of the Gospel's preaching; ¶ 43 then says *pauperes ut reges evangelizantur*, "the poor, as kings, are preached in the gospel", and ¶ 42 calls poverty *evangelizabilis et praedicabilis*, "worthy to be preached as gospel and worthy to be preached" (p. 175 n. 12 records D's *enuntiabilis* and the Vatican edition's *evangelizanda*). The English verse cannot carry that construal; the gloss is rendered as the Latin has it.

[^tr-pietas]: **En.** Translator's note: *pietas* is rendered "piety" throughout, and *pius* "pious" (ruling 12: the six mercy nouns stay six words). Here (¶ 43) the *pietas* is God's: the seventh dignity of poverty is the *condescendentia pietatis paternae*, "the condescension of fatherly piety", proved from Ps. 33:7 *et Dominus exaudivit eum*; p. 176 n. 5 records the Vatican edition's *pietatis supernae, id est, paternae*. The Latin word covers the tenderness of a father toward his own as well as the devotion of a creature toward God; Bonaventure is using the first sense. (¶ 37's *quadam pietate*, Ambrose's word for John's doubting, is human piety and takes no note.)
