# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XVIII, Vers. 28-43
## *Ait autem Petrus.*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 465 -->

47. (Vers. 28.). *Ait autem Petrus.* Postquam commendavit paupertatem per perfectionem in merito et per imperfectionem in contrario, hic tertio[^p465-10] *commendabilem eam ostendit propter consummationem in praemio.* Circa cuius descriptionem proceditur hoc ordine. Introducitur enim hic perfectae paupertatis altitudo ut *authenticata* per apostolicam observantiam, ut *approbata* per divinam sententiam, ut *locupletata* per gratiam, ut *praemiata* per gloriam.

Primo igitur quantum ad paupertatis *authenticationem per apostolicam observantiam* dicit: *Ait autem Petrus: Ecce, nos dimisimus*[^p465-11] *omnia et secuti sumus te.* Hoc dicit Petrus tanquam princeps Apostolorum, super quem ecclesiastica perfectio est superaedificata, secundum illud Matthaei decimo sexto: « Tu es Petrus, et super hanc petram aedificabo Ecclesiam meam ». Dicit autem hoc non ad ostentationem, sed ad sciendam remunerationis sublimitatem; unde Matthaei decimo nono[^p465-12]: « Ecce, nos reliquimus omnia et secuti sumus te; quid ergo erit nobis » etc. Hoc etiam dicit Petrus, ut ostendat paupertatis consilium per Apostolos adimpletum; unde Hieronymus[^p465-13]: « Grandis fiducia: Petrus piscator erat, dives non fuerat, cibos manu et arte quaerebat; et tamen confidenter loquitur dicens: *Dimisimus omnia* ». Et rationem huius reddit Gregorius dicens sic[^p465-14]: « Tanta quippe a sequentibus dimissa sunt, quanta a non sequentibus concupisci potuerunt ». Quoniam ergo omnia poterant concupisci; cum concupiscentiam dimiserunt, omnia dimiserunt. Hoc etiam dicit Petrus, non iactanter ad ostentationem, sed sapienter, ut alios invitet ad imitationem. Unde Bernardus[^p465-15] super hoc verbum: *ecce, nos reliquimus omnia:* « Bene, Petre, et non ad insipientiam tibi, quia *mundus transit et concupiscentia eius,* et relinquere magis expedit quam relinqui ».

<!-- page 466 -->

48. Et nota, quod optime hic duo coniungit: *reliquimus* et *te secuti sumus;* quia Christum currentem sequi non possumus, nisi post eum curramus; et currere non possumus, nisi exoneremur; unde ad Hebraeos duodecimo[^p466-1]: « Deponentes omne pondus et circumstans nos peccatum, curramus per patientiam ad propositum nobis certamen, aspicientes » etc. Et de hoc Bernardus[^p466-2]: « Bene, inquit, Petre, et non ad insipientiam tibi, quia currentem sequi non poteras oneratus; sed nec inutilis commutatio est pro eo quod super omnia est, omnia reliquisse ». Christum etiam, quem *sequimur,* non possumus *consequi,* nisi exoneremur; et frustra sequimur, nisi consequamur, secundum illud ad Philippenses tertio[^p466-3]: « Sequar autem, si quo modo comprehendam, in quo et comprehensus sum ». Et de hoc Bernardus: « Felices liquet esse qui exonerati sunt et sequuntur Dominum expediti; arctissimum foramen nos exspectat, si quem sequimur consequi volumus ». Et sic sequebatur Paulus, qui dicebat ad Philippenses tertio[^p466-4]: « Posteriora oblitus, ad anteriora extendens me ipsum, ad destinatum persequor bravium supernae vocationis ». Sic et alii Apostoli, de quibus Matthaei quarto[^p466-5]: « Relictis retibus et patre, secuti sunt Dominum », dicitur de Iacobo et Ioanne; et Marci primo dicitur de Petro et Andrea: « Relictis retibus et navi, secuti sunt eum »; similiter de beato Matthaeo dicitur Lucae quinto, quod « relictis omnibus, secutus est Dominum ».

49. (Vers. 29.). Secundo quantum ad *paupertatis approbationem per divinam sententiam* subditur: *Qui dixit eis: Amen dico vobis: Nemo est, qui reliquerit*[^p466-6] *domum,* quoad hereditatis *fundamentum;* Ieremiae duodecimo: « Reliqui domum meam, dimisi hereditatem meam, dedi dilectam animam meam » etc. — *Aut parentes,* quoad hereditatis *principium;* Psalmus[^p466-7]: « Audi, filia, et vide et inclina aurem tuam et obliviscere populum tuum et domum patris tui ». — *Aut fratres,* quoad hereditatis *consortium,* fratres scilicet carnales, de quibus Iob sexto[^p466-8]: « Fratres mei praeterierunt me sicut torrens, qui raptim transit in convallibus ». — *Aut uxorem,* quoad hereditatis *condominium,* quia uxor et vir sunt unum corpus; ideo dicitur ad Ephesios quinto[^p466-9]: « Viri debent diligere uxores suas sicut corpora sua »; possunt tamen dimitti propter amorem Christi, ut non ducantur, vel si ductae sunt, ex earum consensu, vel propter fornicationis peccatum. Nam Matthaei decimo nono: « Qui dimiserit uxorem suam, nisi ob fornicationem, et aliam duxerit, moechatur ». De hoc Apostolus primae ad Corinthios septimo: « Dico autem non nuptis et viduis, bonum est illis, si sic permanserint sicut et ego ». « His qui matrimonio iuncti sunt, praecipio, non ego, sed Dominus, uxorem a viro non discedere » etc. Quod ergo dicitur de dimissione uxoris intelligitur secundum modum praedictum. — *Aut filios,* quoad hereditatis *successionem;* Matthaei decimo[^p466-10]: « Qui amat filium aut filiam super me non est me dignus ». — *Aut agros*[^p466-11], quoad hereditatis *dilatationem;* de quo Isaiae quinto: « Vae! qui coniungitis domum ad domum et agrum agro copulatis usque ad terminum loci ». — *Propter regnum Dei,* hoc est secundum rectam intentionem, qua quaerendum est regnum Dei; Matthaei sexto[^p466-12]: « Primum quaerite regnum Dei et iustitiam eius, et haec omnia adiicientur vobis ». Hanc autem derelictionem Dominus acceptat, in qua derelinquitur simul *cupiditas* et *carnalitas.* Ideo dicitur Abrahae Genesis duodecimo: « Egredere de terra tua et de cognatione tua et de domo patris tui, et veni in terram, quam monstravero tibi ». Non enim sufficit, quod relinquatur *possessio corporalis,* nisi etiam relinquatur et *affectio carnalis;* propter quod dicitur Deuteronomii trigesimo tertio[^p466-13]: « Qui dixerunt patri suo et matri suae: Nescio vos, et fratribus suis: Ignoro illos, et nescierunt filios suos; hi custodierunt eloquium tuum et pactum tuum servaverunt, o Iacob, et legem tuam et iudicia tua, o Israel ».

50. (Vers. 30.). Tertio quantum ad *paupertatis locupletationem per gratiam* adiungitur: *Et non recipiat multo plura in hoc tempore;* quod non est intelligendum de temporalibus, ut multo plures uxores habeat, vel filios, vel fratres carnales, sed intelligitur de spiritualibus charismatibus gratiarum,
<!-- page 467 -->
quibus paupertas incomparabiliter locupletatur sine aliena iactura[^p467-1]; et hoc per donum *caritatis,* quae facit omnia communia, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios tertio[^p467-2]: « Omnia vestra sunt, sive praesentia, sive futura »; per donum etiam *abundantissimae simplicitatis;* unde secundae ad Corinthios octavo: « Altissima paupertas eorum abundavit in divitias simplicitatis »; et secundae ad Corinthios sexto: « Sicut egentes, multos autem locupletantes » etc. In huius figuram dicitur Genesis vigesimo sexto[^p467-3]: « Sevit Isaac in terra illa et invenit in ipso anno centuplum, benedixitque ei Dominus, et locupletatus est ». Unde dicitur Matthaei decimo nono[^p467-4]: « Centuplum accipiet » etc.; super quem locum dicit Hieronymus: « Qui carnalia pro Domino dimiserit spiritualia recipiet, quae comparatione et merito sui ita erunt, quasi parvo numero centenarius comparetur ». Unde ne intelligatur de temporali locupletatione, dicitur in Marci decimo[^p467-5]: « Nemo est, qui dimittat » etc., « qui non accipiat centies tantum in hoc tempore cum persecutionibus ». Super quo loco dicit Bernardus[^p467-6]: « Signanter additur *cum persecutionibus;* forte enim quis, cum audisset, hic promitti centuplum, coniecturabatur, hoc et de temporalibus exhiberi; sed praeripit hunc intellectum nomen additum *persecutionis.* Quid enim est terrenae consolationis, quod non facile persecutio tollat? Quid terrenum inter persecutiones sancti Martyres acceperunt[^p467-7]? Interim tamen, undecumque centuplum sint accepturi, dummodo sit centuplum, dum centuplum valeat, centupliciter placeat, consoletur, delectet et ametur; quid insaniae est, quod cunctantur homines relinquere simpla pro centuplis »?

51. Quarto quantum ad *paupertatis remunerationem per gloriam* subiungitur: *Et in futuro*[^p467-8] *vitam aeternam.* Recto autem nomine *gloria* vita aeterna dicitur, quia, sicut separatio a fonte vitae *mors* est et damnatio aeterna, sic coniunctio cum illo est *vita aeterna.* Propter quod in Psalmo[^p467-9] signanter dicitur: « Torrente voluptatis tuae potabis eos; quoniam apud te est fons vitae ». Et recte additur *aeternitas* vitae, quia aeternitas nihil aliud est quam « incommutabilis vitae tota simul et perfecta possessio ». Et tale est quod excedit temporalia in infinitum, ita ut vere dicatur ad Romanos octavo[^p467-10]: « Non sunt condignae passiones huius temporis ad futuram gloriam, quae revelabitur in nobis »; quia, secundae ad Corinthios quarto, « id quod est in praesenti momentaneum et leve tribulationis nostrae, supra modum in sublimitate aeternae gloriae pondus operatur in nobis ». Hoc pondus immensum pro levi pondere rerum mercari possumus, quia praemium superexcellit meritum per omnem modum; supra sexto[^p467-11]: « Mensuram bonam et confertam et coagitatam et supereffluentem dabunt in sinum vestrum ». Et ideo dicitur Ecclesiastici decimo octavo: « Non impediaris orare semper, nec verearis usque ad mortem iustificari, quoniam merces Dei manet in aeternum ».

52. *Assumsit autem Iesus duodecim*[^p467-12] etc. Postquam paupertatem ostendit commendabilem et appetibilem per perfectionem meriti sui et per imperfectionem contrarii et consummationem praemii cumulati, hic quarto[^p467-13] ostendit eam *approbabilem et eligibilem ratione exempli perfectissimi,* videlicet Christi crucifixi. Et quoniam exemplum illud posset videri aliquibus contemptibile ratione infirmitatis, ideo in hac parte praemittitur hic *exemplum dignationis Christi;* secundo ad illius confirmationem adiungitur *miraculum virtutis* in illuminatione pauperis caeci, ibi[^p467-14]: *Factum est autem, cum appropinquaret Iericho.*

Circa *exemplum dignationis,* quod consistit in sustinentia crucis, perfecte describendum quatuor introducuntur, scilicet *benignitas Magistri, profunditas mysterii, acerbitas supplicii* et *caecitas cordis humani* respectu talis exempli.

53. (Vers. 31.). Primo igitur quantum ad *benignitatem Magistri* praemittit: *Assumsit autem Iesus duodecim,* hoc est familiariter ad se sumsit, et hoc fuit mirae dignationis et benignitatis; Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo[^p467-15]: « Expandit alas suas et assumsit eos atque portavit in humeris suis ». *Assumsit* autem, ut eos docendo consolaretur et consolando doceret. — Unde subdit: *Et ait illis: Ecce, ascendimus Ierosolymam,* tanquam ad locum scilicet passionis. Glossa[^p467-16]: « Praevidens, ex passione sua discipulos perturbandos, praedicit locum et passionis poenam et gloriam resurrectionis, ut, cum morientem cernerent,
<!-- page 468 -->
resurrecturum non dubitarent ». Praedicit autem hanc futuram in Ierusalem. Nam, sicut supra decimo tertio[^p468-1] dictum est, « non capit, Prophetam perire extra Ierusalem ». Ratio autem huius est, quia Ierosolyma erat locus, in quo erat sedes *regni, sacerdotii, magisterii* et *iudicii;* et ideo ibi debebat pati ille qui est *rex* et *sacerdos, magister* et *iudex,* ut sic ex Ierusalem exiret et inciperet Christi *regnum,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p468-2]: « Virgam virtutis tuae emittet Dominus ex Sion » etc.; exiret *salus* seu remedium; Isaiae sexagesimo secundo[^p468-3]: « Propter Sion non tacebo et propter Ierusalem non quiescam, donec egrediatur ut splendor iustus eius »; exiret etiam *lex* et *iudicium;* Isaiae secundo: « Venite, et ascendamus ad montem Domini »; et post: « Quia de Sion exibit lex, et verbum Domini de Ierusalem ». Ideo in *Ierosolymam ascendit* Christus, ut, in cruce patiens, *regnum* virtutis, *sacerdotium* sanctitatis et *legem* iudicii et veritatis gentibus daret. Propter quod et ipsa Ierusalem typum gerit *Ecclesiae, animae sanctae* et *Ierusalem supernae.* Unde Isaiae quarto[^p468-4]: « Omnis, qui relictus fuerit in Sion et residuus in Ierusalem, sanctus Domino vocabitur, omnis, qui scriptus fuerit in vita in Ierusalem ».

54. Secundo quantum ad *profunditatem mysterii* subditur: *Et consummabuntur omnia, quae scripta sunt per Prophetas de Filio hominis;* in quo apparet mira profunditas mysterii passionis dominicae, pro eo quod in ipsa est consummatio totius revelationis propheticae. Omnes enim Prophetae principalem aspectum habuerunt ad praenuntiandum Christum secundum triplicem *dignitatem,* scilicet *regni, magisterii* et *sacerdotii;* et secundum triplicem *perfectionem, virtutis* scilicet, *veritatis* et *bonitatis,* secundum quod dicitur de Christo Isaiae trigesimo tertio[^p468-5]: « Dominus rex noster, Dominus legifer noster, Dominus iudex noster; ipse veniet et salvabit nos ». Secundum quod *rex* regit nos per praecepta et iudicia; secundum quod *magister* erudit per mysteria et exempla; secundum quod *sacerdos* sanctificat per sacrificia et Sacramenta. — Quoniam ergo in cruce sunt haec sex consummata, ideo dicuntur *consummata omnia, quae dicta sunt per Prophetas.* Consummata namque sunt *praecepta* propter nimiam caritatem, quia « finis praecepti est caritas », primae ad Timotheum primo[^p468-6], de qua in Psalmo: « Omnis consummationis vidi finem, latum mandatum tuum nimis ». Consummata sunt *iudicia* propter nimiam severitatem, quam habuit ad se; Isaiae decimo[^p468-7]: « Consummatio abbreviata inundabit iustitiam »; et iterum vigesimo octavo: « Consummationem et abbreviationem audivi a Domino » etc. Consummata *mysteria;* Apocalypsis decimo[^p468-8]: « In diebus vocis septimi Angeli consummabitur mysterium Dei, sicut evangelizavit per servos suos Prophetas ». Consummata *exempla;* ad Hebraeos secundo[^p468-9]: « Decebat eum, propter quem omnia et per quem omnia, qui multos filios adduxerat in gloriam, auctorem salutis eorum per passionem consummare » etc. Consummata sunt *sacrificia;* unde ad Hebraeos decimo[^p468-10]: « Una oblatione consummavit in sempiternum sanctificatos »; ideo secundi Machabaeorum primo: « Orationem faciebant sacerdotes, dum consummaretur sacrificium » etc. Consummata sunt *Sacramenta;* ad Hebraeos octavo[^p468-11]: « Consummabo super domum Israel et super domum Iuda testamentum novum »; et hoc testamentum consummatum est in sanguine Christi; ad Hebraeos nono: « Iste sanguis testamenti, quod mandavit » etc. Et ex hoc impletum est testamentum vetus, et omnia sunt consummata. Et ideo in huius figuram supra decimo tertio[^p468-12]: « Ecce, sanitates perficio hodie et cras et tertia die consummor »; et ideo Ioannis decimo nono: « Sciens Iesus, quia omnia consummata sunt, dixit: Sitio ». « Cum ergo accepisset Iesus acetum, dixit: Consummatum est. Et inclinato capite, emisit spiritum ». Et sic verificatum est illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo quarto[^p468-13]: « Sine mendacio consummabitur verbum legis, et sapientia in ore fidelis complanabitur ».

55. (Vers. 32.). Tertio quantum ad *acerbitatem supplicii* subdit: *Tradetur enim gentibus,* ut *extraneus,* secundum quod praedicitur a David in Psalmo[^p468-14]: « Traditus sum et non egrediebar ». Huius figura praecessit in Ioseph, de quo dicitur Genesis trigesimo septimo, quod « fratres sui tradiderunt eum Ismaelitis », et in Samsone, de quo Iudicum decimo sexto dicitur,
<!-- page 469 -->
quod uxor eius tradidit eum Philisthaeis. — *Et illudetur,* ut *stultus;* cuius figura praecessit in Iob, de quo Iob trigesimo[^p469-1]: « Nunc derident me iuniores tempore, quorum patres non dignabar ponere cum canibus » etc.; et Marci decimo quinto: « Induunt eum purpura et imponunt ei plectentes spineam coronam, et coeperunt eum salutare dicentes: Ave rex Iudaeorum; et percutiebant caput eius arundine ». — *Et flagellabitur,* ut *infimus;* unde in Psalmo[^p469-2]: « Et fui flagellatus tota die »; et Isaiae quinquagesimo: « Corpus meum dedi percutientibus et genas meas vellentibus »; unde Ioannis decimo nono: « Apprehendit Pilatus Iesum et flagellavit ». — *Et conspuetur,* ut *immundus,* Isaiae quinquagesimo[^p469-3]: « Faciem meam non averti ab increpantibus et conspuentibus in me »; et Iob trigesimo: « Longe fugerunt a me et in faciem meam conspuere non verentur »; Matthaei vigesimo sexto: « Exspuerunt in faciem eius et colaphis eum ceciderunt ».

56. (Vers. 33.). *Et postquam flagellaverunt*[^p469-4], *occident eum,* quasi esset *iniquus;* unde dicitur Danielis nono: « Post sexaginta duas hebdomadas occidetur Christus; et non erit eius populus, qui eum negaturus est »; et Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio: « Sicut ovis ad occisionem ducetur et quasi agnus coram tondente se sic obmutescet »; et Matthaei vigesimo septimo[^p469-5]: « Postquam illuserunt ei, induerunt eum vestimentis suis et duxerunt, ut crucifigeretur ». « Et cum eo crucifixerunt duos latrones ». « Et impleta est Scriptura, quae dixit: Et cum iniquis reputatus est ». — *Et tertia die resurget,* ut *verus Deus,* quod dicitur, ne ex passione terreantur. Huius resurrectionis figura praecessit in Iona; unde dicitur Matthaei duodecimo[^p469-6]: « Sicut fuit Ionas in ventre ceti tribus diebus et tribus noctibus, ita erit Filius hominis in corde terrae ». Unde et Osee sexto: « Vivificabit nos post duos dies et in tertia die suscitabit nos »; et de hoc Ioannis secundo: « Solvite templum hoc, et in tribus diebus excitabo illud ». « Hoc autem dicebat de templo corporis sui ». — In his autem sex, quae facta sunt in nostra redemptione: in traditione, illusione, flagellatione, conspuitione, occisione et resurrectione, recte clauditur radicaliter universitas operum reparationis; ut, sicut in sex diebus totus mundus fuit perfectus, sic etiam in his sex opus reparationis consistat; et tandem sequitur Spiritus sancti datio, sicut in die septima; in cuius mysterium dicitur Genesis secundo[^p469-7]: « Complevit Deus die septimo omne opus suum »; « et benedixit diei septimo et sanctificavit illum ». In quo intelligitur datio gratiae septiformis; Ioannis septimo[^p469-8]: « Spiritus nondum erat datus, quia Iesus nondum erat glorificatus » etc.

57. (Vers. 34.). Quarto quantum ad *caecitatem cordis humani* subiungit: *Et ipsi nihil horum intellexerunt,* quia hanc veritatem non advertebant; Psalmus[^p469-9]: « Nescierunt neque intellexerunt, in tenebris ambulant ». Et in hoc gerunt figuram *ignorantium praelatorum,* qui ignorant documenta Scripturarum, secundum illud Isaiae quinquagesimo sexto: « Ipsi pastores nescierunt intelligentiam ». — *Et erat verbum istud absconditum ab eis,* quia hanc veritatem non perquirebant, tanquam animales et sensibiles; Iob vigesimo octavo[^p469-10]: « Unde venit sapientia, et quis est locus intelligentiae? Abscondita est ab oculis omnium viventium ». Et in hoc gerebant Apostoli figuram *Iudaeorum,* quibus divina secreta velantur; secundae ad Corinthios tertio[^p469-11]: « Cum legitur Moyses, velamen est positum super cor eorum »; quia nondum velum erat scissum per passionem, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo septimo: « Velum templi scissum est ». — *Et non intelligebant quae dicebantur,* et hoc, quia hanc veritatem non diligebant. Cum enim Christum carnaliter diligerent, displicebat eis, quod Christus debebat pro nobis mori; sicut et Petrus dicebat Matthaei decimo sexto[^p469-12]: « Absit a te, Domine, non erit tibi hoc ». Unde Glossa Bedae: « Discipuli sermonem dominicae passionis toties replicatum ideo intelligere non valent, quia, quem maxime videre desiderant, eius mortem audire non possunt; et quem non solum hominem innocentem, sed etiam verum Deum esse sciunt, mori posse non putant. Et quoties dicit aliquid de sua passione, hoc allegorice dictum putant ». — Ex quo apparet caecitas cordis humani, quae mysteria suae redemptionis non intelligit, nisi Deus tanquam caeco lumen ostendat suae lucis et reddat contuitum veritatis. Ideo recte sequitur miraculum de illuminatione
<!-- page 470 -->
caeci, in quo intelligitur illuminatio generis humani, quia genus humanum per se ignorat redemptionis opus et quantum ad *factum* et quantum ad *rationem* et quantum ad *mysterium;* primae ad Corinthios primo[^p470-1]: « Nos praedicamus Christum crucifixum, Iudaeis quidem scandalum, gentibus autem stultitiam ». Necesse est ergo, quod Christus sensum aperiat ad intelligentiam Scripturarum.

58. (Vers. 35.). *Factum est autem, cum appropinquaret* etc. Postquam proposuit exemplum imitabile in passione Crucifixi, hic exponit *factum admirabile in illuminatione pauperis caeci,* in quo simul manifestatur Christi potestas et benignitas, et etiam acceptatur mendicantis paupertas[^p470-2]. Ad huius igitur miraculi instructionem plenarie explicandam quatuor introducuntur ab Evangelista, scilicet *opportunitas mendicantis, importunitas supplicantis, liberalitas medentis, integritas sanitatis.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *opportunitatem* seu necessitatem *mendicantis* dicit: *Factum est autem, cum appropinquaret Iericho, caecus quidam sedebat secus viam mendicans.* Vere hic *mendicus* erat, qui simul indigebat pane et luce, quae omnibus communiter se offerunt. Et designat hic caecus genus humanum, quod est *caecum* per carentiam lucis sapientiae et *mendicans* per defectum gratiae et iustitiae; unde sibi potest dici illud Apocalypsis tertio[^p470-3]: « Nescis, quia tu es miser et miserabilis et pauper et caecus et nudus »; et de Pharisaeis dicitur Matthaei decimo quinto: « Caeci sunt et duces caecorum ». Excaecatum est autem genus humanum per cupiditatem temporalium; Psalmus[^p470-4]: « Supercecidit ignis, et non viderunt solem ». Unde huius notabilis figura praecessit in Tobia, qui, sicut dicitur Tobiae secundo, excaecatus est de stercoribus hirundinum. Et huic consonat, quia caecus iste erat iuxta Iericho; *Iericho* enim interpretatur luna et designat mutabilitatem rerum, ex quarum amore homo excaecatur et stultus efficitur; Ecclesiastici vigesimo septimo[^p470-5]: « Homo sanctus in sapientia permanet sicut sol, stultus vero ut luna mutatur ». Et hic caecus non est *in via,* sed *secus viam,* secundum illud Sapientiae quinto[^p470-6]: « Erravimus a via veritatis, lumen iustitiae non luxit nobis, et sol intelligentiae non est ortus nobis ». Sed tunc caecus sedet *secus viam,* quando ad lumen veritatis *appropinquat* ex consideratione suae defectibilitatis et variabilitatis; unde Glossa[^p470-7]: « *Iericho* luna interpretatur, per quam defectus mortalitatis intelligitur. Dum ergo Verbum Dei infirmitatem nostrae carnis suscipit, homo ad cognoscenda divina redit ».

59. (Vers. 36.). Secundo quantum ad *importunitatem postulantis* subditur: *Et cum audiret turbam praetereuntem;* in quo explicatur importunitas caeci orantis et dantis nobis exemplum orandi; *quaerit, petit* et *pulsat,* secundum illud Matthaei septimo[^p470-8]: « Quaerite, et invenietis; petite, et accipietis; pulsate, et aperietur vobis ». — *Quaerit* igitur *sollicite* ex auditu turbae; propter quod dicit: *Interrogabat, quid hoc esset,* scilicet a turba praetereunte. Turba ista transiens est decursus creaturarum, quae interrogatae aliquo modo manuducunt nos ad Salvatorem; propter quod Iob duodecimo[^p470-9]: « Interroga iumenta, et docebunt te; et volatilia caeli, et indicabunt tibi. Loquere terrae, et respondebit tibi, et narrabunt pisces maris. Quis ignorat, quod haec omnia manus Domini fecerit? In cuius manu anima omnis viventis et spiritus universae carnis ». Ideo haec omnia ducunt ad suum factorem et reparatorem.

60. (Vers. 37.). Propter quod additur: *Dixerunt autem ei, quod Iesus Nazarenus transiret.* Omnes hae creaturae, secundum quod loquuntur nobis per librum *creaturae,* respondent nobis de *Verbo increato;* Danielis tertio[^p470-10]: « Benedicite, omnia opera Domini, Domino »; et Sapientiae decimo tertio: « A magnitudine enim speciei et creaturae cognoscibiliter poterit Creator horum videri »: quia, ad Romanos primo, « invisibilia Dei a creatura mundi per ea quae facta sunt, intellecta conspiciuntur ». Secundum autem quod loquuntur nobis per verbum *Scripturae,* respondent nobis de *Verbo incarnato;* unde Ioannis quinto[^p470-11]: « Scrutamini Scripturas, in quibus vos putatis vitam habere. Illae sunt, quae testimonium
<!-- page 471 -->
perhibent de me »; quia, ad Romanos decimo, « finis legis, Christus ad iustitiam ». Unde in Cantico Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo: « Interroga patrem tuum, et annuntiabit tibi » etc.

61. (Vers. 38.). *Petit* etiam *devote,* et ideo sequitur: *Et clamavit dicens: Iesu, fili David, miserere mei.* In hoc, quod *clamat,* ostendit, se habere *excedentiam desiderii;* propter quod dicitur ad Romanos octavo[^p471-1]: « Spiritus est, qui postulat pro nobis gemitibus inenarrabilibus ». In hoc, quod dicit: *Iesus,* ostendit, se habere *confidentiam spei;* Psalmus[^p471-2]: « Speravit in Domino, eripiet eum, salvum faciet eum ». Spe enim vocat *Iesum;* id est salvatorem, quia, ad Romanos octavo, « spe salvi facti sumus ». In hoc, quod dicit: *Fili David,* ostendit, se habere *intelligentiam fidei,* qua credimus, Christum ex semine David natum, secundum illud ad Romanos primo[^p471-3]: « Qui factus est ei ex semine David secundum carnem, qui praedestinatus est Filius Dei in virtute ». Unde et Matthaei vigesimo primo dicitur, quod « clamabant: Hosanna filio David! benedictus, qui venit in nomine Domini ».

62. (Vers. 39.). Et quoniam orationi nostrae praebent obstaculum rerum imaginationes, quae deberent nos in Deum ducere; ideo subdit: *Et qui praeibant increpabant eum, ut taceret.* Unde Glossa[^p471-4]: « Qui Iesum venientem praeeunt significant tumultus carnalium desideriorum, quae dissipant cogitationem hominis et perturbant vocem orationis, ne Iesus ad illuminandum venire possit cor hominis ». Unde huiusmodi imaginationes, retrahentes nos ab oratione per strepitum suum, nihil aliud sunt quam quoddam obstaculum inter nos et Christum, secundum illud Threnorum tertio[^p471-5]: « Opposuisti nubem tibi, ne transeat oratio »; et ideo opus est importuna *pulsatione.* — Propter quod caecus *pulsat continue,* cum subiungitur: *Ipse vero multo magis clamabat: Fili David, miserere mei;* Psalmus[^p471-6]: « Domine Deus salutis meae, in die clamavi et nocte coram te ». Et haec est virtus orationis, quae ex vera fide procedit. Unde Chrysostomus[^p471-7]: « Talis est natura fidei, quod quanto magis vetatur, tanto magis accenditur; propter quod servi Dei in persecutionibus non vincuntur ». Unde Glossa[^p471-8] dicit, quod « credens, sentiens se gravari phantasmate priorum vitiorum, et vocem orationis suae impediri, ne pro se exorare possit, ardentius clamat ».

63. (Vers. 40.). Tertio quantum ad *liberalitatem medentis* subiungitur: *Stans autem Iesus, iussit, illum adduci ad se.* Dicitur autem *Iesus stare,* quia ipse in se immutabilis est: Psalmus[^p471-9]: « Tu autem idem ipse es, et anni tui non deficient »; unde Glossa: « Iesus *transiens* caecum audit, *stans* illuminat, quia per humanitatem suam nostrae caecitatis vocibus compatitur, sed Divinitatis suae potentia lumen nobis gratiae suae infundit ». Et licet per orationem videamur eum trahere ad nos, ipse magis trahit nos ad se, sicut navis ad petram trahitur, sicut dicit Dionysius[^p471-10]. Ideo sponsa in Cantici primo dicit: « Trahe me post te ». Unde ad ipsum trahimur, quando mente ad ipsum excitamur et supra nos excedimus. In cuius figuram Marci decimo[^p471-11] dicitur de hoc caeco: « Proiecto vestimento suo, exsiliens venit ad eum ». Et tunc et arcana verba audimus, secundum illud Prophetae in Psalmo: « Audiam, quid loquatur in me Dominus Deus ».

64. (Vers. 40. 41.). Et ideo additur: *Et cum appropinquasset, interrogavit illum, dicens: Quid tibi vis faciam?* In quo apparet liberalitas dantis, quia offert se beneplacito petentis. Unde Glossa[^p471-12]: « Non ex ignorantia Dominus quaerit, sed ideo, ut rogetur, ut mentem ad orationem excitet », secundum illud Ioannis decimo sexto: « Si quid petieritis Patrem in nomine meo, dabit vobis ». — Et quoniam qui in nomine Iesu petit petit salutaria, ideo subdit: *At ille dixit: Domine, ut videam.* Glossa[^p471-13]: « Non petit caecus aurum, vel aliquid temporale, sed tantum lumen. Imitemur ergo eum quem corpore et
<!-- page 472 -->
mente audivimus sanatum ». Unde Iacobi primo[^p472-1]: « Si quis vestrum indiget sapientia, postulet illam a Deo, et dabitur ei ». Exemplum huius habetur in Salomone tertii Regum tertio, ubi dicitur, quod « apparuit Dominus Salomoni et dixit: Postula quod vis, ut dem tibi ». At ille dixit: « Dabis servo tuo cor docile » etc. « Et placuit sermo coram Domino, quod rem huiusmodi postulasset. Et dixit Dominus: Quia tu postulasti sapientiam, ecce, dedi tibi cor sapiens et intelligens in tantum, ut nullus ante te similis tui fuerit nec post te surrecturus sit » etc.

65. (Vers. 42.). Quarto quantum ad *integritatem sanitatis* subiungit: *Et Iesus dixit illi*[^p472-2]*: Respice; fides tua te salvum fecit,* per *infusionem divinae gratiae.* Per respectum namque fidei cor nostrum erigitur ad iustitiam; ad Romanos quinto[^p472-3]: « Iustificati per fidem, pacem habeamus ad Deum ». Et haec est prima virtus, de qua ad Hebraeos undecimo: « Accedentem oportet credere », nam « ipsa est substantia rerum sperandarum » etc.

66. (Vers. 43.). *Et confestim vidit,* per *circumspectionem prudentiae;* ad Ephesios quinto[^p472-4]: « Videte, fratres, quomodo caute ambuletis »; Proverbiorum quarto: « Oculi tui recta videant, et palpebrae tuae praecedant gressus tuos ». — *Et sequebatur illum, magnificans Deum,* per *subiectionem obedientiae.* Glossa[^p472-5]: « Videt et sequitur qui bonum, quod intelligit, operatur et Iesum praetereuntem imitatur »; Ioannis octavo: « Ego sum lux mundi; qui sequitur me non ambulat in tenebris, sed habebit lumen vitae ». — *Et omnis plebs, ut vidit, dedit laudem Deo,* per manifestationem *divinae gloriae.* Hoc enim est opus, in quo summe manifestatur potentia, sapientia et misericordia, quando scilicet de peccatore fit iustus, de stulto sapiens, de errante fidelis et de impio virtuosus. Et ideo de hoc maxime laudandus Deus; unde ad Galatas primo[^p472-6]: « Ignotus eram facie », dicit Paulus, « Ecclesiis Iudaeae, quae erant in Christo; tantum autem auditum habebant: quoniam qui persequebatur nos aliquando nunc evangelizat fidem, quam aliquando expugnabat; et in me glorificabant Deum ». Et hoc debemus facere, secundum illud Ecclesiastici quadragesimo tertio[^p472-7]: « Glorificantes Dominum, quantumcumque potueritis, supervalebit adhuc, et admirabilis magnificentia eius, benedicentes Dominum, exaltate illum, quantumcumque potestis; maior enim est omni laude ».

## English

47. (Vers. 28.). *Then Peter said.* After he commended poverty through its perfection in merit and through the imperfection in its contrary, here, third[^p465-10], *he shows it to be commendable on account of its consummation in the reward.* Concerning the description of which one proceeds in this order. For here the height of perfect poverty is introduced as *authenticated* through apostolic observance, as *approved* through the divine sentence, as *enriched* through grace, as *rewarded* through glory.

First, then, as to the *authentication* of poverty *through apostolic observance* he says: *Then Peter said: Behold, we have left*[^p465-11] *all things and have followed thee.* Peter says this as prince of the Apostles, upon whom ecclesiastical perfection is built up, according to that of Matthew, the sixteenth chapter: « Thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church ». But he says this not for ostentation, but for knowing the sublimity of the remuneration; whence Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p465-12]: « Behold, we have left all things and have followed thee; what therefore shall we have » etc. Peter says this also to show the counsel of poverty fulfilled by the Apostles; whence Jerome[^p465-13]: « Great confidence: Peter was a fisherman, he had not been rich, he sought his food by hand and art; and yet he speaks confidently, saying: *We have left all things* ». And Gregory gives the reason of this, saying thus[^p465-14]: « For so much was left by those following as could be coveted by those not following ». Since, then, all things could be coveted; when they left concupiscence, they left all things. Peter says this also, not boastfully for ostentation, but wisely, so as to invite others to imitation. Whence Bernard[^p465-15] on this word: *behold, we have left all things:* « Well, Peter, and not unto foolishness for thee, because *the world passeth away and the concupiscence thereof,* and to leave is more expedient than to be left ».

<!-- page 466 -->

48. And note that here he joins two things excellently: *we have left* and *have followed thee;* because we cannot follow Christ running unless we run after him; and we cannot run unless we are unburdened; whence to the Hebrews, the twelfth chapter[^p466-1]: « Laying aside every weight and sin which surrounds us, let us run by patience to the fight proposed to us, looking » etc. And of this Bernard[^p466-2]: « Well, he says, Peter, and not unto foolishness for thee, because thou couldst not follow one running while burdened; nor is it an unprofitable exchange, for that which is above all things, to have left all things ». Christ also, whom we *follow,* we cannot *overtake* unless we are unburdened; and in vain do we follow unless we overtake, according to that to the Philippians, the third chapter[^p466-3]: « But I will follow, if by any means I may lay hold of, wherein I also am laid hold of ». And of this Bernard: « It is plain that they are happy who are unburdened and follow the Lord unencumbered; a most narrow opening awaits us, if we wish to overtake him whom we follow ». And so Paul followed, who said to the Philippians, the third chapter[^p466-4]: « Forgetting the things that are behind, and stretching forth myself to those that are before, I pursue towards the mark, the prize of the supernal vocation ». So too the other Apostles, of whom Matthew, the fourth chapter[^p466-5]: « Leaving their nets and father, they followed the Lord », is said of James and John; and in Mark, the first chapter, it is said of Peter and Andrew: « Leaving their nets and the ship, they followed him »; likewise of blessed Matthew it is said in Luke, the fifth chapter, that « leaving all things, he followed the Lord ».

49. (Vers. 29.). Second, as to the *approbation of poverty through the divine sentence* it is subjoined: *Who said to them: Amen I say to you, there is no man that hath left*[^p466-6] *home,* as to the *foundation* of the inheritance; Jeremias, the twelfth chapter: « I have left my house, I have forsaken my inheritance, I have given my dear soul » etc. — *Or parents,* as to the *origin* of the inheritance; the Psalm[^p466-7]: « Hearken, O daughter, and see, and incline thy ear; and forget thy people and thy father's house ». — *Or brethren,* as to the *fellowship* of the inheritance, brethren namely carnal, of whom Job, the sixth chapter[^p466-8]: « My brethren have passed by me, as the torrent that passeth swiftly in the valleys ». — *Or wife,* as to the *co-ownership* of the inheritance, because wife and husband are one body; therefore it is said to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter[^p466-9]: « Men ought to love their wives as their own bodies »; they can, however, be left for the love of Christ, so that they are not taken, or, if they have been taken, with their consent, or on account of the sin of fornication. For Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « Whosoever shall put away his wife, except it be for fornication, and shall marry another, committeth adultery ». Of this the Apostle, the first to the Corinthians, the seventh chapter: « But I say to the unmarried and to the widows: It is good for them if they so continue, even as I ». « To them that are joined in matrimony I command, not I but the Lord, that the wife depart not from her husband » etc. What, then, is said of the leaving of a wife is understood according to the manner aforesaid. — *Or children,* as to the *succession* of the inheritance; Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p466-10]: « He that loveth son or daughter more than me is not worthy of me ». — *Or lands*[^p466-11], as to the *enlargement* of the inheritance; of which Isaias, the fifth chapter: « Woe to you that join house to house and lay field to field, even to the end of the place ». — *For the kingdom of God's sake,* that is, according to right intention, by which the kingdom of God is to be sought; Matthew, the sixth chapter[^p466-12]: « Seek ye first the kingdom of God and his justice, and all these things shall be added unto you ». And this forsaking the Lord accepts, in which *cupidity* and *carnality* are forsaken together. Therefore it is said to Abraham in Genesis, the twelfth chapter: « Go forth out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and out of thy father's house, and come into the land which I shall shew thee ». For it does not suffice that *bodily possession* be left, unless *carnal affection* also be left; on account of which it is said in Deuteronomy, the thirty-third chapter[^p466-13]: « Who have said to their father and to their mother: I do not know you; and to their brethren: I know you not; and they have not known their own children; these have kept thy word and observed thy covenant, O Jacob, and thy law and thy judgements, O Israel ».

50. (Vers. 30.). Third, as to the *enrichment of poverty through grace* it is added: *Who shall not receive much more in this present time;* which is not to be understood of temporal things, that he should have many more wives, or children, or carnal brethren, but is understood of the spiritual charisms of graces,
<!-- page 467 -->
by which poverty is incomparably enriched without loss to another[^p467-1]; and this through the gift of *charity,* which makes all things common, according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p467-2]: « All are yours, whether things present or things to come »; through the gift also *of the most abundant simplicity;* whence the second to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter: « Their most deep poverty hath abounded unto the riches of their simplicity »; and the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter: « As needy, yet enriching many » etc. In the figure of this it is said in Genesis, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p467-3]: « Isaac sowed in that land, and he found that same year a hundredfold, and the Lord blessed him, and he was enriched ». Whence it is said in Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p467-4]: « He shall receive an hundredfold » etc.; upon which place Jerome says: « He who has left carnal things for the Lord will receive spiritual things, which in comparison and in their merit will be such as if a hundred were compared with a small number ». Whence, lest it be understood of temporal enrichment, it is said in Mark, the tenth chapter[^p467-5]: « There is no man who leaveth » etc., « who shall not receive an hundred times as much in this time with persecutions ». Upon which place Bernard says[^p467-6]: « Significantly is added *with persecutions;* for perhaps someone, when he had heard that a hundredfold is promised here, was conjecturing that this too was shown of temporal things; but the name of *persecution* added snatches away this understanding. For what is there of earthly consolation which persecution does not easily take away? What earthly thing did the holy Martyrs receive among persecutions[^p467-7]? Meanwhile, however, from wherever they are to receive the hundredfold, provided it be a hundredfold, while the hundredfold avails, pleases a hundredfold, consoles, delights and is loved; what madness is it, that men hesitate to leave single things for hundredfold »?

51. Fourth, as to the *remuneration of poverty through glory* it is subjoined: *And in the world to come*[^p467-8] *life everlasting.* And by a right name is everlasting life called *glory,* because, just as separation from the fountain of life is *death* and eternal damnation, so union with it is *everlasting life.* On account of which it is significantly said in the Psalm[^p467-9]: « Thou shalt make them drink of the torrent of thy pleasure; for with thee is the fountain of life ». And *eternity* of life is rightly added, because eternity is nothing else than « the whole, simultaneous and perfect possession of unchangeable life ». And such is what exceeds temporal things infinitely, so that it is truly said to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p467-10]: « The sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come, that shall be revealed in us »; because, the second to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter, « that which is at present momentary and light of our tribulation worketh in us above measure in sublimity an eternal weight of glory ». This immeasurable weight we can purchase for a light weight of things, because the reward surpasses the merit in every way; above, the sixth chapter[^p467-11]: « Good measure and pressed down and shaken together and running over shall they give into your bosom ». And therefore it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter: « Let nothing hinder thee from praying always, and be not afraid to be justified even to death, for the reward of God continueth for ever ».

52. *Then Jesus took unto him the twelve*[^p467-12] etc. After he showed poverty commendable and desirable through the perfection of its merit and through the imperfection of its contrary and the consummation of the heaped-up reward, here, fourth[^p467-13], he shows it *approvable and choiceworthy by reason of the most perfect example,* namely of Christ crucified. And since that example could seem contemptible to some by reason of infirmity, therefore in this part there is set forth first here *the example of Christ's condescension;* second, for its confirmation, *the miracle of power* is added in the illumination of the poor blind man, there[^p467-14]: *Now it came to pass, when he drew nigh to Jericho.*

Concerning *the example of condescension,* which consists in the enduring of the cross, four things are introduced for its perfect description, namely *the kindness of the Master, the depth of the mystery, the bitterness of the penalty* and *the blindness of the human heart* with respect to such an example.

53. (Vers. 31.). First, then, as to *the kindness of the Master* he sets forth: *Then Jesus took unto him the twelve,* that is, he took them familiarly to himself, and this was of marvellous condescension and kindness; Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter[^p467-15]: « He spread his wings, and took them, and carried them on his shoulders ». And he *took them unto him* so as to console them by teaching and to teach them by consoling. — Whence he subjoins: *And said to them: Behold, we go up to Jerusalem,* namely as to the place of the passion. The Gloss[^p467-16]: « Foreseeing that the disciples would be troubled by his passion, he foretells the place and the punishment of the passion and the glory of the resurrection, so that, when they should see him dying,
<!-- page 468 -->
they should not doubt that he would rise again ». And he foretells that this will be in Jerusalem. For, as was said above, the thirteenth chapter[^p468-1], « it cannot be that a prophet perish out of Jerusalem ». And the reason of this is that Jerusalem was the place in which was the seat *of the kingdom, of the priesthood, of the teaching office* and *of judgement;* and therefore he ought to suffer there who is *king* and *priest, master* and *judge,* so that thus the *kingdom* of Christ might go out from Jerusalem and begin, according to that of the Psalm[^p468-2]: « The Lord will send forth the sceptre of thy power out of Sion » etc.; *salvation* or remedy might go out; Isaias, the sixty-second chapter[^p468-3]: « For Sion's sake I will not hold my peace, and for the sake of Jerusalem I will not rest, till her just one come forth as brightness »; *law* and *judgement* might also go out; Isaias, the second chapter: « Come, and let us go up to the mountain of the Lord »; and after: « For the law shall come forth from Sion, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem ». Therefore Christ *goes up to Jerusalem,* so that, suffering on the cross, he might give to the Gentiles the *kingdom* of power, the *priesthood* of holiness and the *law* of judgement and of truth. On account of which Jerusalem itself also bears the type *of the Church, of the holy soul* and *of the Jerusalem above.* Whence Isaias, the fourth chapter[^p468-4]: « Every one that shall be left in Sion and that shall remain in Jerusalem shall be called holy, every one that is written in life in Jerusalem ».

54. Second, as to *the depth of the mystery* it is subjoined: *And all things shall be consummated which were written by the prophets concerning the Son of man;* in which appears the marvellous depth of the mystery of the Lord's passion, for this reason, that in it is the consummation of the whole prophetic revelation. For all the Prophets had their principal regard to announcing Christ beforehand according to a threefold *dignity,* namely *of the kingdom, of the teaching office* and *of the priesthood;* and according to a threefold *perfection,* namely *of power, of truth* and *of goodness,* according to what is said of Christ in Isaias, the thirty-third chapter[^p468-5]: « The Lord is our king, the Lord is our lawgiver, the Lord is our judge; he will come and will save us ». According as he is *king* he rules us through precepts and judgements; according as he is *master* he schools us through mysteries and examples; according as he is *priest* he sanctifies through sacrifices and Sacraments. — Since, then, on the cross these six things are consummated, therefore *all things which were spoken by the prophets* are said to be *consummated.* For the *precepts* are consummated on account of exceeding charity, because « the end of the precept is charity », the first to Timothy, the first chapter[^p468-6], of which in the Psalm: « I have seen an end of all consummation; thy commandment is exceeding broad ». The *judgements* are consummated on account of the exceeding severity which he had towards himself; Isaias, the tenth chapter[^p468-7]: « The consummation abridged shall overflow with justice »; and again in the twenty-eighth chapter: « I have heard of the Lord a consummation and an abridgement » etc. The *mysteries* are consummated; the Apocalypse, the tenth chapter[^p468-8]: « In the days of the voice of the seventh Angel the mystery of God shall be consummated, as he hath preached the gospel by his servants the Prophets ». The *examples* are consummated; to the Hebrews, the second chapter[^p468-9]: « It became him, for whom are all things and by whom are all things, who had brought many children into glory, to consummate the author of their salvation by his passion » etc. The *sacrifices* are consummated; whence to the Hebrews, the tenth chapter[^p468-10]: « By one oblation he hath consummated for ever them that are sanctified »; therefore in the second of the Machabees, the first chapter: « The priests made prayer while the sacrifice was being consummated » etc. The *Sacraments* are consummated; to the Hebrews, the eighth chapter[^p468-11]: « I will consummate upon the house of Israel and upon the house of Juda a new testament »; and this testament is consummated in the blood of Christ; to the Hebrews, the ninth chapter: « This is the blood of the testament which he hath enjoined » etc. And from this the old testament is fulfilled, and all things are consummated. And therefore in the figure of this, above, the thirteenth chapter[^p468-12]: « Behold, I perform cures today and tomorrow, and the third day I am consummated »; and therefore John, the nineteenth chapter: « Jesus knowing that all things were consummated, said: I thirst ». « When Jesus therefore had taken the vinegar, he said: It is consummated. And bowing his head, he gave up the ghost ». And so that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p468-13], was verified: « The word of the law shall be consummated without a lie, and wisdom shall be made plain in the mouth of the faithful ».

55. (Vers. 32.). Third, as to *the bitterness of the penalty* he subjoins: *For he shall be delivered to the Gentiles,* as a *stranger,* according to what is foretold by David in the Psalm[^p468-14]: « I was delivered up, and came not forth ». The figure of this went before in Joseph, of whom it is said in Genesis, the thirty-seventh chapter, that « his brethren delivered him to the Ismaelites », and in Samson, of whom it is said in Judges, the sixteenth chapter,
<!-- page 469 -->
that his wife delivered him to the Philistines. — *And he shall be mocked,* as a *fool;* the figure of which went before in Job, of whom Job, the thirtieth chapter[^p469-1]: « Now the younger in time deride me, whose fathers I would not have set with the dogs » etc.; and Mark, the fifteenth chapter: « They clothe him with purple, and platting a crown of thorns they put it upon him, and they began to salute him: Hail, king of the Jews; and they struck his head with a reed ». — *And scourged,* as the *lowest;* whence in the Psalm[^p469-2]: « And I have been scourged all the day »; and Isaias, the fiftieth chapter: « I have given my body to the strikers, and my cheeks to them that plucked them »; whence John, the nineteenth chapter: « Pilate took Jesus and scourged him ». — *And spit upon,* as *unclean,* Isaias, the fiftieth chapter[^p469-3]: « I have not turned away my face from them that rebuked me and spit upon me »; and Job, the thirtieth chapter: « They have fled far from me, and are not afraid to spit in my face »; Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter: « They spit in his face and buffeted him ».

56. (Vers. 33.). *And after they have scourged*[^p469-4] *him, they will put him to death,* as though he were *wicked;* whence it is said in Daniel, the ninth chapter: « After sixty-two weeks Christ shall be slain; and the people that shall deny him shall not be his »; and Isaias, the fifty-third chapter: « He shall be led as a sheep to the slaughter, and shall be dumb as a lamb before his shearer »; and Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p469-5]: « After they had mocked him, they put on him his own garments and led him away to be crucified ». « And with him they crucified two thieves ». « And the Scripture was fulfilled which saith: And with the wicked he was reputed ». — *And the third day he shall rise again,* as *true God,* which is said lest they be terrified by the passion. The figure of this resurrection went before in Jonas; whence it is said in Matthew, the twelfth chapter[^p469-6]: « As Jonas was in the whale's belly three days and three nights, so shall the Son of man be in the heart of the earth ». Whence also Osee, the sixth chapter: « He will revive us after two days, and on the third day he will raise us up »; and of this John, the second chapter: « Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up ». « But he spoke of the temple of his body ». — And in these six things which were done in our redemption: in the delivering up, the mocking, the scourging, the spitting, the slaying and the resurrection, the whole of the works of reparation is radically enclosed; so that, just as in six days the whole world was made perfect, so too the work of reparation should consist in these six; and at last there follows the giving of the Holy Spirit, as on the seventh day; in the mystery of which it is said in Genesis, the second chapter[^p469-7]: « God completed on the seventh day all his work »; « and he blessed the seventh day and sanctified it ». In which the giving of sevenfold grace is understood; John, the seventh chapter[^p469-8]: « The Spirit was not as yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified » etc.

57. (Vers. 34.). Fourth, as to *the blindness of the human heart* he subjoins: *And they understood none of these things,* because they did not attend to this truth; the Psalm[^p469-9]: « They have not known nor understood; they walk on in darkness ». And in this they bear the figure *of ignorant prelates,* who are ignorant of the lessons of the Scriptures, according to that of Isaias, the fifty-sixth chapter: « The shepherds themselves knew no understanding ». — *And this word was hid from them,* because they did not search out this truth, as animal and sensible men; Job, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p469-10]: « Whence then cometh wisdom, and where is the place of understanding? It is hid from the eyes of all living ». And in this the Apostles bore the figure *of the Jews,* from whom the divine secrets are veiled; the second to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p469-11]: « When Moses is read, the veil is set upon their heart »; because the veil was not yet rent through the passion, according to that of Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter: « The veil of the temple was rent ». — *And they understood not the things that were said,* and this because they did not love this truth. For since they loved Christ carnally, it displeased them that Christ was to die for us; as Peter also said, Matthew, the sixteenth chapter[^p469-12]: « Lord, be it far from thee, this shall not be unto thee ». Whence the Gloss of Bede: « The disciples are not able to understand the word of the Lord's passion so often repeated for this reason, that they cannot hear of the death of him whom they most desire to see; and him whom they know to be not only an innocent man but also true God, they do not think able to die. And as often as he says something of his passion, they think it said allegorically ». — From which appears the blindness of the human heart, which does not understand the mysteries of its redemption, unless God, as to a blind man, show the lumen[^tr-lumen] of his light and restore the contuition of truth. Therefore the miracle of the illumination
<!-- page 470 -->
of the blind man rightly follows, in which is understood the illumination of the human race, because the human race of itself is ignorant of the work of redemption, both as to the *fact* and as to the *reason* and as to the *mystery;* the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p470-1]: « We preach Christ crucified, unto the Jews indeed a stumblingblock, and unto the Gentiles foolishness ». It is necessary, then, that Christ open the sense to the intelligence of the Scriptures.

58. (Vers. 35.). *Now it came to pass, when he drew nigh* etc. After he set forth the imitable example in the passion of the Crucified, here he expounds *the wonderful fact in the illumination of the poor blind man,* in which are manifested at once the power and the kindness of Christ, and the poverty of the one begging is also accepted[^p470-2]. For the full unfolding, then, of the instruction of this miracle four things are introduced by the Evangelist, namely *the opportuneness of the one begging, the importunity of the one supplicating, the liberality of the one healing, the integrity of the health.*

First, then, as to the *opportuneness* or necessity *of the one begging* he says: *Now it came to pass, when he drew nigh to Jericho, that a certain blind man sat by the way side, begging.* Truly this man was a *beggar,* who lacked at once bread and light, which offer themselves to all in common. And this blind man designates the human race, which is *blind* through the lack of the light of wisdom and *begging* through the defect of grace and justice; whence there can be said to it that of the Apocalypse, the third chapter[^p470-3]: « Thou knowest not that thou art wretched and miserable and poor and blind and naked »; and of the Pharisees it is said in Matthew, the fifteenth chapter: « They are blind, and leaders of the blind ». And the human race is blinded through the cupidity of temporal things; the Psalm[^p470-4]: « Fire hath fallen upon them, and they have not seen the sun ». Whence a notable figure of this went before in Tobias, who, as is said in Tobias, the second chapter, was blinded by the dung of swallows. And this accords with it, that this blind man was near Jericho; for *Jericho* is interpreted moon and designates the mutability of things, from the love of which man is blinded and made a fool; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p470-5]: « A holy man continueth in wisdom as the sun, but a fool is changed as the moon ». And this blind man is not *in the way,* but *by the way side,* according to that of Wisdom, the fifth chapter[^p470-6]: « We have erred from the way of truth, and the lumen of justice hath not shined unto us, and the sun of understanding hath not risen upon us ». But the blind man then sits *by the way side* when he *draws nigh* to the lumen of truth from the consideration of his own defectibility and variability; whence the Gloss[^p470-7]: « *Jericho* is interpreted moon, by which the defect of mortality is understood. While, then, the Word of God takes up the infirmity of our flesh, man returns to the knowing of divine things ».

59. (Vers. 36.). Second, as to *the importunity of the one asking* it is subjoined: *And when he heard the multitude passing by;* in which is unfolded the importunity of the blind man praying and giving us an example of praying; he *seeks, asks* and *knocks,* according to that of Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p470-8]: « Seek, and you shall find; ask, and you shall receive; knock, and it shall be opened to you ». — He *seeks,* then, *solicitously* from the hearing of the multitude; on account of which he says: *He asked what this meant,* namely of the multitude passing by. This multitude passing over is the running course of creatures, which, being questioned, in some manner lead us by the hand to the Saviour; on account of which Job, the twelfth chapter[^p470-9]: « Ask the beasts, and they shall teach thee; and the birds of the air, and they shall tell thee. Speak to the earth, and it shall answer thee, and the fishes of the sea shall tell. Who is ignorant that the hand of the Lord hath made all these things? In whose hand is the soul of every living thing, and the spirit of all flesh ». Therefore all these things lead to their maker and repairer.

60. (Vers. 37.). On account of which it is added: *And they told him that Jesus of Nazareth was passing by.* All these creatures, according as they speak to us through the book *of the creature,* answer us concerning the *uncreated Word;* Daniel, the third chapter[^p470-10]: « All ye works of the Lord, bless the Lord »; and Wisdom, the thirteenth chapter: « For by the greatness of the beauty and of the creature the Creator of them may knowably be seen »: because, to the Romans, the first chapter, « the invisible things of God, from the creation of the world, are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made ». But according as they speak to us through the word *of Scripture,* they answer us concerning the *incarnate Word;* whence John, the fifth chapter[^p470-11]: « Search the Scriptures, in which you think to have life. The same are they that give testimony
<!-- page 471 -->
of me »; because, to the Romans, the tenth chapter, « the end of the law is Christ, unto justice ». Whence in the Canticle of Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter: « Ask thy father, and he will announce to thee » etc.

61. (Vers. 38.). He *asks* also *devoutly,* and therefore there follows: *And he cried out, saying: Jesus, son of David, have mercy on me.* In this, that he *cries out,* he shows that he has *the exceedingness of desire;* on account of which it is said to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p471-1]: « It is the Spirit who asketh for us with unspeakable groanings ». In this, that he says: *Jesus,* he shows that he has *the confidence of hope;* the Psalm[^p471-2]: « He hoped in the Lord, he will deliver him, he will save him ». For in hope he calls on *Jesus,* that is, the saviour, because, to the Romans, the eighth chapter, « we are saved by hope ». In this, that he says: *Son of David,* he shows that he has *the intelligence of faith,* by which we believe Christ born of the seed of David, according to that to the Romans, the first chapter[^p471-3]: « Who was made to him of the seed of David according to the flesh, who was predestinated the Son of God in power ». Whence also in Matthew, the twenty-first chapter, it is said that « they cried, saying: Hosanna to the son of David! blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord ».

62. (Vers. 39.). And since the imaginations of things, which ought to lead us into God, offer an obstacle to our prayer; therefore he subjoins: *And they that went before rebuked him, that he should hold his peace.* Whence the Gloss[^p471-4]: « They who go before Jesus as he comes signify the tumult of carnal desires, which scatter the thought of man and disturb the voice of prayer, lest Jesus be able to come to illuminate the heart of man ». Whence imaginations of this kind, drawing us back from prayer by their din, are nothing else than a certain obstacle between us and Christ, according to that of Lamentations, the third chapter[^p471-5]: « Thou hast set a cloud before thee, that prayer may not pass through »; and therefore there is need of importunate *knocking.* — On account of which the blind man *knocks continually,* when it is subjoined: *But he cried out much more: Son of David, have mercy on me;* the Psalm[^p471-6]: « O Lord, the God of my salvation, I have cried in the day and in the night before thee ». And this is the power of prayer which proceeds from true faith. Whence Chrysostom[^p471-7]: « Such is the nature of faith, that the more it is forbidden, the more it is kindled; on account of which the servants of God are not overcome in persecutions ». Whence the Gloss[^p471-8] says that « the believer, feeling himself weighed down by the phantasm of former vices, and the voice of his prayer hindered, so that he cannot plead for himself, cries out the more ardently ».

63. (Vers. 40.). Third, as to *the liberality of the one healing* it is subjoined: *And Jesus standing, commanded him to be brought unto him.* And *Jesus* is said *to stand,* because he in himself is unchangeable: the Psalm[^p471-9]: « But thou art the selfsame, and thy years shall not fail »; whence the Gloss: « Jesus *passing by* hears the blind man, *standing* illuminates him, because through his humanity he has compassion on the voices of our blindness, but by the power of his Divinity he pours into us the lumen of his grace ». And although by prayer we seem to draw him to us, he rather draws us to himself, as a ship is drawn to the rock, as Dionysius says[^p471-10]. Therefore the bride says in the Canticle, the first chapter: « Draw me after thee ». Whence we are drawn to him when we are roused in mind to him and are transported above ourselves. In the figure of which it is said of this blind man in Mark, the tenth chapter[^p471-11]: « Casting off his garment, leaping up, he came to him ». And then also we hear secret words, according to that of the Prophet in the Psalm: « I will hear what the Lord God will speak in me ».

64. (Vers. 40. 41.). And therefore it is added: *And when he was come near, he asked him, saying: What wilt thou that I do to thee?* In which appears the liberality of the giver, because he offers himself to the good pleasure of the one asking. Whence the Gloss[^p471-12]: « The Lord asks not out of ignorance, but for this, that he may be asked, that he may rouse the mind to prayer », according to that of John, the sixteenth chapter: « If you ask the Father any thing in my name, he will give it you ». — And since he who asks in the name of Jesus asks for saving things, therefore he subjoins: *But he said: Lord, that I may see.* The Gloss[^p471-13]: « The blind man asks not for gold, or anything temporal, but only lumen. Let us imitate, then, him whom in body and
<!-- page 472 -->
mind we have heard healed ». Whence James, the first chapter[^p472-1]: « If any of you want wisdom, let him ask it of God, and it shall be given him ». An example of this is had in Solomon, in the third of Kings, the third chapter, where it is said that « the Lord appeared to Solomon and said: Ask what thou wilt, that I may give it thee ». And he said: « Thou wilt give to thy servant a docile heart » etc. « And the word was pleasing before the Lord, that he had asked a thing of this kind. And the Lord said: Because thou hast asked wisdom, behold, I have given thee a wise and understanding heart, insomuch that there hath been no one like thee before thee, nor shall arise after thee » etc.

65. (Vers. 42.). Fourth, as to *the integrity of the health* he subjoins: *And Jesus said to him*[^p472-2]*: Receive thy sight; thy faith hath made thee whole,* through *the infusion of divine grace.* For through the regard of faith our heart is raised up to justice; to the Romans, the fifth chapter[^p472-3]: « Being justified by faith, let us have peace with God ». And this is the first virtue, of which to the Hebrews, the eleventh chapter: « He that cometh must believe », for « it is the substance of things to be hoped for » etc.

66. (Vers. 43.). *And immediately he saw,* through *the circumspection of prudence;* to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter[^p472-4]: « See, brethren, how you walk circumspectly »; Proverbs, the fourth chapter: « Let thy eyes look straight on, and let thy eyelids go before thy steps ». — *And he followed him, glorifying God,* through *the subjection of obedience.* The Gloss[^p472-5]: « He sees and follows who works the good that he understands and imitates Jesus passing by »; John, the eighth chapter: « I am the light of the world; he that followeth me walketh not in darkness, but shall have the light of life ». — *And all the people, when they saw it, gave praise to God,* through the manifestation *of the divine glory.* For this is the work in which power, wisdom and mercy are supremely manifested, namely when from a sinner one is made just, from a fool wise, from one erring faithful and from an impious man virtuous. And therefore God is most of all to be praised for this; whence to the Galatians, the first chapter[^p472-6]: « I was unknown by face », says Paul, « to the churches of Judea, which were in Christ; but they had heard only: He who persecuted us in times past now preacheth the gospel of the faith which once he impugned; and they glorified God in me ». And this we ought to do, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the forty-third chapter[^p472-7]: « Glorifying the Lord as much as ever you can, he will yet far exceed, and his magnificence is wonderful; blessing the Lord, exalt him as much as you can; for he is greater than all praise ».

[^tr-lumen]: **En.** Translator's note: this translation keeps *lux* as "light" and *lumen* as "lumen", except where a verse is quoted as a verse and no surrounding prose leans on the word. This pericope, which closes on the illumination of the blind man of Jericho, sets the two words side by side in Bonaventure's own prose: ¶ 57 *nisi Deus tanquam caeco lumen ostendat suae lucis*, "unless God, as to a blind man, show the lumen of his light"; ¶ 58 the blind man lacks *pane et luce*, "bread and light", and is blind *per carentiam lucis sapientiae*, "through the lack of the light of wisdom", and sits by the way side *quando ad lumen veritatis appropinquat*, "when he draws nigh to the lumen of truth" — so the verse that sentence expounds, Wis. 5:6 *lumen iustitiae non luxit nobis*, follows it: "the lumen of justice hath not shined unto us". The Gloss's *lumen nobis gratiae suae infundit* (¶ 63) and *sed tantum lumen* (¶ 64), and Gregory's *caecus lumen recipit* in the editors' p. 470 n. 1, take "lumen" likewise. John 8:12 in ¶ 66, *Ego sum lux mundi … sed habebit lumen vitae*, is quoted as a verse for the following of Christ, and nothing in ¶ 66 leans on *lumen*: the Douay's "the light of life" stands.
