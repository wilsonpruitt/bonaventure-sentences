# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. II, Vers. 8-14
## *Qualiter Christi nativitas sit ab Angelis denuntiata per tria*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 48 -->

#### Qualiter Christi nativitas sit ab Angelis denuntiata per tria.

17. *Et pastores erant* etc. Descripsit supra Evangelista, qualiter Christi nativitas fuerit consummata; hic secundo describit, qualiter fuerit *denuntiata.* Ad denuntiationem autem nativitatis Christi perfecte faciendam tria concurrunt. Primum est *dispositio praeparans ad audiendum;* secundum, *annuntiatio invitans ad exsultandum,* quod describit ibi[^p48-4]: *Et dixit illis Angelus;* tertium est *confirmatio certificans ad credendum,* et hoc describit ibi: *Et hoc vobis signum.* — Quantum ad primum notandum, quod *dispositio praeparans ad audiendum* fuit pastorum attentio, quae ortum habuit ex duplici causa, scilicet ex *propriae sollicitudinis vigilantia* et ex *angelici luminis circumfulgentia.*

18. (Vers. 8.). Quantum ad *vigilantiam propriae sollicitudinis* dicitur: *Et pastores erant in regione eadem vigilantes.* Hoc enim spectat ad pastorale officium; unde Iacob, pastor ovium Laban, dicebat Genesis trigesimo primo[^p48-5]: « Die et nocte aestu urebar et gelu, fugiebatque somnus ab oculis meis ». Et ad maioris attentionis insinuationem subdit: *Et custodientes vigilias noctis super gregem suum.* De nocte enim potissimum vigilandum est propter hostilem incursum repellendum, luporum scilicet et latronum.

19. Per hoc autem insinuatur *spiritualiter vigilantia,* quae debet esse in *praelatis;* unde secundae ad Timotheum quarto[^p48-6]: « Tu vero vigila, in omnibus labora, sobrius esto ». Et ideo ad Hebraeos ultimo: « Obedite praepositis vestris per omnia. Ipsi enim vigilant quasi rationem pro animabus vestris reddituri »; quia, ad Romanos duodecimo, « qui praeest in sollicitudine ». — Vigilare etiam debet *quilibet contra hostem spiritualem,* secundum illud Threnorum secundo[^p48-7]: « Consurge, lauda in nocte in principio vigiliarum; effunde sicut aquam cor tuum ante conspectum Domini Dei tui ». — Vigilare etiam debet in *contemplatione;* Psalmus[^p48-8]: « Et nox illuminatio mea in deliciis meis »; et Isaiae vigesimo sexto: « Anima mea desideravit te in nocte, sed et spiritu meo in praecordiis meis de mane vigilabo ad te »; et hoc, quia talis hora competit divinae inspirationi. Talis etiam hora conveniebat divinae nativitati, secundum illud Sapientiae decimo octavo: « Cum quietum silentium tenerent omnia, et nox in suo cursu medium iter haberet, omnipotens sermo tuus, Domine, exsiliens de caelo, a regalibus sedibus venit ». — Et signanter dicit: *Super gregem suum,* secundum illud Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo[^p48-9]: « Diligenter agnosce vultum pecoris tui tuosque greges considera ». Quocontra Zachariae undecimo: « O pastor et idolum derelinquens gregem »! et Ieremiae decimo tertio: « Ubi est grex, qui datus est tibi, pecus inclytum tuum? Quid dices, cum visitaverit te? Tu enim docuisti eos adversum te, et erudisti in caput tuum ».

20. (Vers. 9.). Quantum ad *circumfulgentiam angelici luminis* subditur: *Et ecce, Angelus Domini stetit iuxta illos, et claritas Dei* etc. In hoc, quod *iuxta stetit,* ostendit familiaritatem et humilitatem ministerii, quo Angelus condescendit homini, et astat illi modo ut *adiutor,* secundum illud Danielis duodecimo[^p48-10]: « Consurgit Michael, princeps magnus, qui stat pro filiis populi tui »; modo ut *eruditor* et illuminator, secundum illud supra primo: « Apparuit ei Angelus Gabriel, stans a dextris altaris incensi ». Simili modo et hic; unde Ambrosius: « Angelus Mariam, Angelus Ioseph, Angelus pastores edocet ».

21. In cuius rei signum subditur: *Et claritas Dei circumfulsit illos,* id est fulsit circa illos. Simile
<!-- page 49 -->
dicitur de Angelo, qui apparuit Petro, Actuum duodecimo[^p49-1]: « Ecce, Angelus Domini astitit, et lumen refulsit in habitaculo » carceris. Ideo autem hic Angelus lumen attulit, quia filius lucis fuit, et quia eum nuntiare venerat, qui « est lux vera, quae illuminat omnem hominem venientem in hunc mundum », sicut dicitur Ioannis primo. Et ideo nocte lumen attulit, ut significetur, impleri illud Isaiae nono: « Habitantibus in regione umbrae mortis lux orta est eis ». — Ex hac autem claritate pastores sollicitos reddidit; propter quod subditur: *Et timuerunt timore magno,* sicut et Zacharias in aspectu Angeli; supra primo[^p49-2]: « Et Zacharias turbatus est, et timor irruit super eum ».

22. Et nota, quod Angelus magis apparuit pastoribus quam alii generi hominum, tum quia *pauperes,* propter quos veniebat Christus; Psalmus[^p49-3]: « Propter miseriam inopum et gemitum pauperum nunc exsurgam »; et Bernardus: « Quam multa hodie auro et gemmis fulgent altaria! Putas, ad haec divertent Angeli et pannosos homines declinabunt? Si ita est, cur magis pastoribus ovium apparuerunt quam regibus terrae, quam sacerdotibus templi »? — Tum etiam, quia *simplices;* Proverbiorum tertio[^p49-4]: « Abominatio Domini est omnis illusor, et cum simplicibus sermocinatio eius »; et Matthaei undecimo: « Abscondisti haec a sapientibus et prudentibus, et revelasti ea parvulis ». — Tum etiam, quia *vigiles;* Proverbiorum octavo[^p49-5]: « Qui mane vigilaverint ad me invenient me »; Sapientiae sexto: « Qui de luce vigilaverint propter illam cito erunt securi ». — Sive propter *mysterium,* ut insinuetur, quod doctrina veritatis debet a praelatis pervenire ad subditos; unde Glossa[^p49-6]: « Illi prae ceteris videre sublimia merentur, qui fidelibus sollicite praesunt ».

23. (Vers. 10.). *Et dixit illis Angelus* etc. Hic secundo describitur *annuntiatio invitans ad exsultandum;* in qua praemittitur *exhortatio*[^p49-7] ad *fiduciam* et ad *laetitiam,* et subditur *annuntiatio,* exprimens laetitiae causam. — *Exhortans* igitur Angelus ad *fiduciam* dixit illis: *Nolite timere.* Non enim venerat Angelus nuntiare rem terribilem, sed magis desiderabilem, sicut et ille qui resurrectionem annuntiavit mulieribus; Marci ultimo[^p49-8]: « Nolite expavescere »; in quo tribuit sperandi fiduciam. In hoc agnoscitur differentia inter Angelum lucis et tenebrarum, quia bonus Angelus assecurat, sed malus finaliter terret. — Et quod plus est, *invitat ad laetitiam;* propter quod addit: *Ecce enim, evangelizo vobis gaudium magnum.* Hoc optabat Raphael Tobiae, quinto[^p49-9], cum diceret: « Gaudium semper sit tibi ». Hoc gavisi sunt Magi; Matthaei secundo: « Et videntes stellam gavisi sunt gaudio magno valde ». — Et dicitur *magnum* ad differentiam *gaudii hypocritarum;* Iob vigesimo: « Gaudium hypocritae ad instar puncti », et vigesimo primo: « Gaudent ad sonitum organi, ducunt in bonis dies suos et in puncto ad inferna descendunt ». — *Magnum* etiam, quia *amplum,* quod notat, cum subdit: *Quod erit omni populo,* et hoc ad differentiam *gaudii mundanorum;* Ioannis decimo sexto[^p49-10]: « Mundus gaudebit, vos autem contristabimini ». Quocontra infra decimo tertio: « Et omnis populus gaudebat in universis, quae gloriose fiebant ab eo ».

24. (Vers. 11.). Et quia nullus gaudet perfecte nisi ex causa et ratione, ideo subditur *denuntiatio exprimens laetitiae causam,* cum dicitur: *Quia natus est vobis hodie Salvator; vobis,* scilicet ad *utilitatem vestram,* scilicet hominum; Isaiae nono[^p49-11]: « Parvulus natus est nobis, et filius datus est nobis ». Et vere ad *utilitatem,* quia *Salvator;* Isaiae quadragesimo quinto: « Vere tu es Deus absconditus, Deus Israel, Salvator »; et Isaiae decimo nono: « Mittet eis Salvatorem et propugnatorem, qui liberet eos ». Et vere *Salvator,* quia, secundum illud Actuum quarto[^p49-12], « non est aliud nomen sub caelo datum hominibus, in quo oporteat nos salvos fieri ». In hoc autem insinuatur causa gaudendi pro nativitate; Malachiae quarto: « Orietur vobis, timentibus nomen meum, sol iustitiae ». — Nec tantum gaudendum ratione utilitatis, verum etiam *dignitatis,* et ideo subinfertur: *Qui est Christus Dominus, in civitate David,* et sic reparabit regnum Israeliticum, secundum illud Isaiae nono[^p49-13]: « Super solium David et
<!-- page 50 -->
super regnum eius sedebit, ut confirmet illud et corroboret in iudicio et iustitia, amodo et usque in sempiternum ». Et ideo recte additur *Dominus,* quia, primae ad Timotheum sexto, « ipse est Rex regum et Dominus dominantium »; et Apocalypsis decimo nono: « Habebat in vestimento et in femore suo scriptum: Rex regum et Dominus dominantium ». Et ideo: *in civitate David,* id est in Bethlehem, ubi ortus fuit[^p50-1].

25. (Vers. 12.). *Et hoc vobis signum* etc. Post dispositionem praeparantem ad audiendum et denuntiationem invitantem ad exsultandum subditur hic tertio *confirmatio certificans ad credendum.* Et quoniam in ore duorum testium stare debet omne verbum[^p50-2], ideo dupliciter confirmantur pastores: per *visum* et *auditum;* isti enim duo sensus potissime nobis deserviunt ad cognoscendum. Primo ergo certificantur per *signum probabile,* deinde per *testimonium credibile.*

26. Quantum ergo ad *signi probabilis persuasionem* dicitur: *Et hoc vobis signum.* Nam, secundum quod dicitur primae ad Corinthios primo[^p50-3], « Iudaei signa quaerunt ». Et hoc maxime petebat propheta David: « Fac mecum, inquit, signum in bonum ». Hoc signum *petendum* erat; Isaiae septimo: « Pete tibi signum a Domino Deo tuo ». Hoc etiam *promittebat* Isaiae undecimo: « Erit radix Iesse, qui stat in signum populorum, ipsum gentes deprecabuntur ». — *Et hoc vobis signum; vobis,* inquam, *pauperibus* quod venerit pauperes liberare, secundum illud Psalmi[^p50-4]: « Liberabit pauperem a potente, et pauperem, cui non erat adiutor ». *Quia invenietis infantem, pannis involutum,* et ita pauperem et mendicum; Zachariae nono[^p50-5]: « Ecce, rex tuus veniet tibi iustus et Salvator. Ipse pauper et ascendens super asinam ». Bernardus: « In signum positi sunt panni tui, Domine Iesu, sed in signum, cui hodie a multis contradicitur. *Multi* enim *sunt vocati,* sed *pauci electi,* et ideo nec signati ». — *Vobis* etiam *humilibus* signum, quod venerit liberare humiles, secundum illud Psalmi[^p50-6]: « Populum humilem salvum facies ». In hoc, quod subditur: *Et positum in praesepio;* ecce, mira humilitas, ut Dominum caelorum inclinaret usque ad praesepium animalium brutorum; Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p50-7]: « Vidimus eum, et non erat aspectus, et desideravimus eum, despectum et novissimum virorum ». Unde in Psalmo: « Ego autem sum vermis et non homo, opprobrium hominum et abiectio plebis ».

27. Et nota, quod pastores erant pauperes, simplices et contemptibiles; ideo, ne timerent accedere, datum est eis in Christo infante signum *paupertatis* et *humilitatis;* et in hoc significatur, *qualiter* et *a quibus* invenitur Christus. Unde primae ad Corinthios primo[^p50-8]: « Videte, fratres, vocationem vestram, quia non multi sapientes secundum carnem, non multi potentes, non multi nobiles, sed quae stulta sunt mundi elegit Dominus » etc.; et ideo infra eodem: « Positus est hic in ruinam et in resurrectionem multorum in Israel et in signum, cui contradicetur »; et hoc, quia per ista signa non fuit cognitus a carnalibus et superbis. Unde in Psalmo[^p50-9]: « Signa nostra non vidimus, iam non est propheta »; et iterum: « Posuerunt signa sua signa ».

28. (Vers. 13. 14.). Quantum vero ad *testimonii credibilis persuasionem* subditur: *Et subito facta est cum Angelo multitudo; facta* scilicet est cum eo ad confirmandum pariter dictum angelicum et ad venerandum parvulum natum, secundum illud ad Hebraeos primo[^p50-10]: « Cum introducit primogenitum in orbem terrae, dicit: Et adorent eum omnes Angeli eius ». Et signanter dicitur *multitudo,* secundum illud ad Hebraeos duodecimo: « Accessistis ad Sion montem et civitatem Dei viventis, Ierusalem caelestem, et multorum millium Angelorum frequentiam ». — Et *multitudo militiae caelestis;* secundum illud Iob vigesimo quinto[^p50-11]: « Nunquid est numerus militum eius »? Et ideo quarti Regum sexto: « Nolite timere, dixit Eliseus; plures enim nobiscum sunt quam cum illis ». Et hoc apparet in ortu eius, secundum illud Apocalypsis decimo nono: « Ecce, equus albus, et qui sedebat super eum, fidelis et verax »; « Et exercitus, qui sunt in caelo, sequebantur eum ». — Et haec tota multitudo ad confirmationem verbi angelici prorumpit in decantationem cantici; propter quod dicitur: *Laudantium Deum et dicentium: Gloria in excelsis*[^p50-12] *Deo, et in terra pax hominibus bonae*
<!-- page 51 -->
*voluntatis;* Iob trigesimo octavo: « Ubi eras, cum me laudarent simul astra matutina, et iubilarent omnes filii Dei »?

29. *Gloria in excelsis Deo* etc. Nota, quod glorificandus est Deus in incarnatione, quia, ut dicit Damascenus[^p51-1], in hoc opere « demonstratur sublimis *bonitas, sapientia, potentia* et *iustitia* Dei ». — *Gloria* ergo *in excelsis Deo* pro *bonitate,* quia non despexit proprii plasmatis infirmitatem; ad Galatas primo[^p51-2]: « Dedit semetipsum pro peccatis nostris, ut eriperet nos de praesenti saeculo nequam; ipsi gloria in saecula saeculorum! Amen ». — Pro *sapientia,* quia invenit difficillimi solutionem valde decentissimam; ad Romanos undecimo[^p51-3]: « O altitudo divitiarum sapientiae et scientiae Dei »! et sequitur: « Ipsi gloria in saecula! Amen ». — Pro *potentia;* quid enim potentius, quam Deum fieri hominem? ad Romanos quarto[^p51-4]: « Abraham confortatus est fide, dans gloriam Deo, plenissime sciens, quia, quaecumque promisit Deus, potens est et facere »; et ad Ephesios tertio: « Ei autem, qui potens est omnia facere superabundanter, quam petimus aut intelligimus, ipsi gloria in Ecclesia et in Christo Iesu in omnes generationes saeculi saeculorum! Amen ». — Pro *iustitia,* quia simile simili re salvavit; Apocalypsis quinto[^p51-5]: « Sedenti super throno et Agno benedictio et honor et gloria et potestas in saecula saeculorum »! — Ideo cantatur[^p51-6]:

Gloria tibi, Domine, / Qui natus es de Virgine!

30. Et nota, quod gloriam *Deo* attribuunt, quia Dominus illam sibi reservavit; Isaiae quadragesimo secundo[^p51-7]: « Ego Dominus, hoc est nomen meum; gloriam meam alteri non dabo »; pacem vero optant *nobis in terris,* quia, Ioannis decimo quarto, « pacem relinquo vobis, pacem meam do vobis ». — Et recte dicit: *Bonae voluntatis,* quia, secundum illud Isaiae quinquagesimo septimo, « non est pax impiis, dicit Dominus ». Et praemittit gloriam Dei paci terrae, quia nullus pacem habere potest, qui gloriam Dei nititur usurpare. Bernardus[^p51-8]: « Displicet hominibus angelica partitio, qua Deo gloria et pax hominibus nuntiatur. O stulti filii Adam, qui, contemnentes pacem et gloriam appetentes, et pacem perdunt et gloriam »! Certe qui gloriam appetit Deo resistit; sed dicitur Iob nono[^p51-9]: « Quis restitit ei et pacem habuit »? « Acquiesce igitur ei, et habeto pacem », sicut dicitur in Iob vigesimo secundo, pacem scilicet *pectoris,* de qua dicitur ad Philippenses quarto: « Pax Dei, quae exsuperat omnem sensum, custodiat corda vestra et intelligentias vestras in Christo Iesu » etc.; et pacem *aeternitatis,* de qua Isaiae ultimo[^p51-10]: « Declinabo super eam flumen pacis »; Psalmus: « Qui ponit fines tuos pacem ».

31. Nota etiam, quod pax optatur hominibus et denuntiatur, quia per Christum pacificatur homo cum *Deo;* Isaiae vigesimo septimo[^p51-11]: « Faciet mihi pacem, pacem faciet mihi ». Pacificatur cum *Angelis;* ad Colossenses primo: « Pacificans per sanguinem crucis eius sive quae in terris, sive quae in caelis sunt ». Pacificatur cum *homine;* ad Ephesios secundo: « Ipse est pax nostra, qui fecit utraque unum ». Pacificatur *in se;* Iob quinto[^p51-12]: « Scies, quod pacem habeat tabernaculum tuum ». — In horum figura dicitur Iosue vigesimo primo: « Data est ab eo pax omnibus per circuitum ». Hanc Apostolus semper optat, et Christus praecipit infra decimo: « In quamcumque domum intraveritis, primum dicite: Pax huic domui ».

## English
<!-- page 48 -->

#### How the nativity of Christ was declared by the Angels, through three things.

17. *And there were shepherds* etc. The Evangelist described above how the nativity of Christ was consummated; here, secondly, he describes how it was *declared.* Now for the declaration of the nativity of Christ to be made perfectly three things concur. The first is *a disposition preparing for hearing;* the second, *an announcement inviting to exult,* which he describes there[^p48-4]: *And the angel said to them;* the third is *a confirmation certifying for believing,* and this he describes there: *And this shall be a sign unto you.* — As to the first it is to be noted that *the disposition preparing for hearing* was the attention of the shepherds, which had its origin from a twofold cause, namely from the *watchfulness of their own solicitude* and from the *shining round about of the angelic lumen.*[^tr-lumen]

18. (Vers. 8.). As to *the watchfulness of their own solicitude* it is said: *And there were in the same country shepherds watching.* For this pertains to the pastoral office; whence Jacob, the shepherd of Laban's sheep, said, Genesis, the thirty-first chapter[^p48-5]: « Day and night was I parched with heat and with frost, and sleep departed from my eyes ». And for the insinuation of greater attention he adds: *And keeping the night watches over their flock.* For it is by night above all that one must watch, for repelling the hostile onset, namely of wolves and of robbers.

19. Now by this is insinuated *spiritually the watchfulness* which ought to be in *prelates;* whence the second to Timothy, the fourth chapter[^p48-6]: « But be thou vigilant, labour in all things, be sober ». And therefore to the Hebrews, the last chapter: « Obey your prelates in all things. For they watch as being to render an account of your souls »; because, to the Romans, the twelfth chapter, « he that ruleth, with carefulness ». — Every one also ought to watch *against the spiritual enemy,* according to that of Lamentations, the second chapter[^p48-7]: « Arise, give praise in the night, in the beginning of the watches; pour out thy heart like water before the face of the Lord thy God ». — One ought also to watch in *contemplation;* the Psalm[^p48-8]: « And night shall be my light in my pleasures »; and Isaias, the twenty-sixth chapter: « My soul hath desired thee in the night, yea, and with my spirit within me in the morning early I will watch to thee »; and this, because such an hour befits divine inspiration. Such an hour also befitted the divine nativity, according to that of Wisdom, the eighteenth chapter: « While all things were in quiet silence, and the night was in the midst of her course, thy almighty word, O Lord, leapt down from heaven, from thy royal throne, came ». — And he says expressly: *Over their flock,* according to that of Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p48-9]: « Be diligent to know the countenance of thy cattle, and consider thy own flocks ». Against which, Zacharias, the eleventh chapter: « O shepherd, and idol, that forsaketh the flock »! and Jeremias, the thirteenth chapter: « Where is the flock that is given thee, thy beautiful cattle? What wilt thou say when he shall visit thee? For thou hast taught them against thee, and instructed them against thy own head ».

20. (Vers. 9.). As to *the shining round about of the angelic lumen* it is added: *And behold an angel of the Lord stood by them, and the brightness of God* etc. In this, that *he stood by,* he shows the familiarity and humility of the ministry by which the Angel condescends to man, and stands by him, now as a *helper,* according to that of Daniel, the twelfth chapter[^p48-10]: « Michael shall rise up, the great prince, who standeth for the children of thy people »; now as a *schoolmaster* and enlightener, according to that above, the first chapter: « There appeared to him the Angel Gabriel, standing on the right side of the altar of incense ». In like manner here also; whence Ambrose: « An Angel teaches Mary, an Angel Joseph, an Angel the shepherds ».

21. In sign of which it is added: *And the brightness of God shone round about them,* that is, shone around them. The like
<!-- page 49 -->
is said of the Angel who appeared to Peter, Acts, the twelfth chapter[^p49-1]: « Behold, an angel of the Lord stood by him, and a lumen shined in the room » of the prison. Now the Angel brought a lumen here for this reason, because he was a son of light, and because he had come to announce him who « is the true light, which enlighteneth every man that cometh into this world », as is said in John, the first chapter. And therefore he brought a lumen by night, that it might be signified that that of Isaias, the ninth chapter, was fulfilled: « To them that dwelt in the region of the shadow of death, light is risen ». — Now by this brightness he made the shepherds anxious; on account of which it is added: *And they feared with a great fear,* as Zachary also did at the sight of the Angel; above, the first chapter[^p49-2]: « And Zachary was troubled, and fear fell upon him ».

22. And note that the Angel appeared to shepherds rather than to another kind of men, both because they were *poor,* for whose sake Christ was coming; the Psalm[^p49-3]: « By reason of the misery of the needy, and the groans of the poor, now will I arise »; and Bernard: « How many altars gleam today with gold and gems! Thinkest thou that the Angels will turn aside to these, and will turn away from ragged men? If it is so, why did they appear rather to shepherds of sheep than to the kings of the earth, than to the priests of the temple »? — And also because they were *simple;* Proverbs, the third chapter[^p49-4]: « Every mocker is an abomination to the Lord, and his communication is with the simple »; and Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « Thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them to little ones ». — And also because they were *watchful;* Proverbs, the eighth chapter[^p49-5]: « They that in the morning early watch for me shall find me »; Wisdom, the sixth chapter: « They that watch for her at break of day shall quickly be secure ». — Or on account of the *mystery,* that it might be insinuated that the teaching of truth ought to come from prelates to subjects; whence the Gloss[^p49-6]: « Those merit before others to see sublime things who preside solicitously over the faithful ».

23. (Vers. 10.). *And the angel said to them* etc. Here, secondly, is described *the announcement inviting to exult;* in which there is set first an *exhortation*[^p49-7] to *confidence* and to *gladness,* and there is added an *announcement,* expressing the cause of gladness. — *Exhorting,* then, to *confidence,* the Angel said to them: *Fear not.* For the Angel had not come to announce a terrible thing, but rather a desirable one, as also he who announced the resurrection to the women; Mark, the last chapter[^p49-8]: « Be not affrighted »; in which he gives the confidence of hoping. In this is recognized the difference between the Angel of light and of darkness, because the good Angel makes secure, but the evil one in the end terrifies. — And what is more, *he invites to gladness;* on account of which he adds: *For, behold, I bring you good tidings of great joy*[^tr-evangelizare]. This Raphael wished for Tobias, the fifth chapter[^p49-9], when he said: « Joy be to thee always ». In this the Magi rejoiced; Matthew, the second chapter: « And seeing the star they rejoiced with exceeding great joy ». — And it is called *great* in difference from *the joy of hypocrites;* Job, the twentieth chapter: « The joy of the hypocrite is but for a moment », and the twenty-first chapter: « They rejoice at the sound of the organ, they spend their days in wealth, and in a moment they go down to hell ». — *Great* also, because *ample,* which he notes when he adds: *That shall be to all the people,* and this in difference from *the joy of worldlings;* John, the sixteenth chapter[^p49-10]: « The world shall rejoice, but you shall be made sorrowful ». Against which, below, the thirteenth chapter: « And all the people rejoiced in all things that were gloriously done by him ».

24. (Vers. 11.). And because no one rejoices perfectly except from a cause and reason, therefore there is added *the declaration expressing the cause of gladness,* when it is said: *For this day is born to you a Saviour; to you,* namely for *your profit,* namely of men; Isaias, the ninth chapter[^p49-11]: « A child is born to us, and a son is given to us ». And truly for *profit,* because a *Saviour;* Isaias, the forty-fifth chapter: « Verily thou art a hidden God, the God of Israel, the Saviour »; and Isaias, the nineteenth chapter: « He shall send them a Saviour and a defender to deliver them ». And truly a *Saviour,* because, according to that of Acts, the fourth chapter[^p49-12], « there is no other name under heaven given to men, whereby we must be saved ». Now in this is insinuated the cause of rejoicing for the nativity; Malachias, the fourth chapter: « There shall arise to you that fear my name the Sun of justice ». — Nor is one to rejoice only by reason of profit, but also of *dignity,* and therefore it is subjoined: *Who is Christ the Lord, in the city of David,* and so he will repair the kingdom of Israel, according to that of Isaias, the ninth chapter[^p49-13]: « Upon the throne of David and
<!-- page 50 -->
upon his kingdom he shall sit, to establish it and strengthen it in judgement and justice, from henceforth and for ever ». And therefore *Lord* is rightly added, because, the first to Timothy, the sixth chapter, « he is the King of kings and Lord of lords »; and the Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter: « He had on his garment and on his thigh written: King of kings and Lord of lords ». And therefore: *in the city of David,* that is, in Bethlehem, where he had his birth[^p50-1].

25. (Vers. 12.). *And this shall be a sign unto you* etc. After the disposition preparing for hearing and the declaration inviting to exult, there is added here, thirdly, *the confirmation certifying for believing.* And since in the mouth of two witnesses every word ought to stand[^p50-2], therefore the shepherds are confirmed in two ways: by *sight* and *hearing;* for these two senses serve us most of all for knowing. First, therefore, they are certified by *a probable sign,* then by *a credible testimony.*

26. As to *the persuasion of the probable sign,* then, it is said: *And this shall be a sign unto you.* For, according to what is said in the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p50-3], « the Jews require signs ». And this the prophet David asked for most of all: « Make with me, he says, a sign for good ». This sign was *to be asked for;* Isaias, the seventh chapter: « Ask thee a sign of the Lord thy God ». This also Isaias *promised,* the eleventh chapter: « There shall be the root of Jesse, who standeth for a sign of the peoples; him the Gentiles shall beseech ». — *And this shall be a sign unto you; unto you,* I say, *the poor,* that he has come to set the poor free, according to that of the Psalm[^p50-4]: « He shall deliver the poor from the mighty, and the poor that had no helper ». *For you shall find the infant wrapped in swaddling clothes,* and so poor and a beggar; Zacharias, the ninth chapter[^p50-5]: « Behold, thy king will come to thee, the just and Saviour. He is poor, and riding upon an ass ». Bernard: « Thy swaddling clothes, Lord Jesus, have been set for a sign, but for a sign which today is contradicted by many. For *many are called,* but *few chosen,* and therefore not signed either ». — A sign *unto you* also *the humble,* that he has come to set the humble free, according to that of the Psalm[^p50-6]: « Thou wilt save the humble people ». In this, that it is added: *And laid in a manger;* behold, a wonderful humility, that it should bow down the Lord of the heavens even to the manger of brute animals; Isaias, the fifty-third chapter[^p50-7]: « We have seen him, and there was no sightliness, and we desired him, despised and the most abject of men ». Whence in the Psalm: « But I am a worm, and no man, the reproach of men, and the outcast of the people ».

27. And note that the shepherds were poor, simple and contemptible; therefore, lest they should fear to approach, there was given them in the infant Christ a sign of *poverty* and of *humility;* and in this is signified *how* and *by whom* Christ is found. Whence the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p50-8]: « See your vocation, brethren, that there are not many wise according to the flesh, not many mighty, not many noble, but the foolish things of the world hath the Lord chosen » etc.; and therefore below, in the same: « This one is set for the fall and for the resurrection of many in Israel, and for a sign which shall be contradicted »; and this, because by these signs he was not known by the carnal and the proud. Whence in the Psalm[^p50-9]: « Our signs we have not seen, there is now no prophet »; and again: « They have set up their signs for signs ».

28. (Vers. 13. 14.). As to *the persuasion of the credible testimony,* now, it is added: *And suddenly there was with the angel a multitude;* there was with him, namely, to confirm the angelic saying likewise and to venerate the little one born, according to that of the Hebrews, the first chapter[^p50-10]: « When he bringeth in the firstborn into the world, he saith: And let all his Angels adore him ». And *multitude* is said expressly, according to that of the Hebrews, the twelfth chapter: « You are come to Sion the mountain, and to the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and to the company of many thousands of Angels ». — And *a multitude of the heavenly army;* according to that of Job, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p50-11]: « Is there any numbering of his soldiers »? And therefore the fourth of Kings, the sixth chapter: « Fear not, said Eliseus; for there are more with us than with them ». And this appears in his birth, according to that of the Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter: « Behold, a white horse, and he that sat upon him, faithful and true »; « And the armies that are in heaven followed him ». — And this whole multitude, for the confirmation of the angelic word, breaks forth into the singing of a canticle; on account of which it is said: *Praising God and saying: Glory to God in the highest*[^p50-12]*, and on earth peace to men of good*
<!-- page 51 -->
*will;* Job, the thirty-eighth chapter: « Where wast thou when the morning stars praised me together, and all the sons of God made a joyful melody »?

29. *Glory to God in the highest* etc. Note that God is to be glorified in the incarnation, because, as Damascene says[^p51-1], in this work « there is shown the sublime *goodness, wisdom, power* and *justice* of God ». — *Glory,* then, *to God in the highest* for his *goodness,* because he did not despise the infirmity of his own handiwork; to the Galatians, the first chapter[^p51-2]: « Who gave himself for our sins, that he might deliver us from this present wicked world; to him be glory for ever and ever! Amen ». — For his *wisdom,* because he found a most fitting solution of a most difficult thing; to the Romans, the eleventh chapter[^p51-3]: « O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and of the knowledge of God »! and it follows: « To him be glory for ever! Amen ». — For his *power;* for what is more powerful than that God should become man? to the Romans, the fourth chapter[^p51-4]: « Abraham was strengthened in faith, giving glory to God, most fully knowing that whatsoever God has promised, he is able also to perform »; and to the Ephesians, the third chapter: « Now to him who is able to do all things more abundantly than we ask or understand, to him be glory in the Church and in Christ Jesus unto all generations, world without end! Amen ». — For his *justice,* because he saved the like by a like thing; the Apocalypse, the fifth chapter[^p51-5]: « To him that sitteth on the throne, and to the Lamb, benediction, and honour, and glory, and power, for ever and ever »! — Therefore it is sung[^p51-6]:

Glory to thee, O Lord, / Who wast born of the Virgin!

30. And note that they attribute glory *to God,* because the Lord has reserved it to himself; Isaias, the forty-second chapter[^p51-7]: « I the Lord, this is my name; I will not give my glory to another »; but peace they wish *for us on earth,* because, John, the fourteenth chapter, « peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you ». — And he rightly says: *Of good will,* because, according to that of Isaias, the fifty-seventh chapter, « there is no peace to the wicked, saith the Lord ». And he puts the glory of God before the peace of the earth, because no one can have peace who strives to usurp the glory of God. Bernard[^p51-8]: « The angelic division displeases men, by which glory is announced to God and peace to men. O foolish sons of Adam, who, despising peace and seeking glory, lose both peace and glory »! Surely he who seeks glory resists God; but it is said in Job, the ninth chapter[^p51-9]: « Who hath resisted him, and hath had peace »? « Submit thyself then to him, and have peace », as is said in Job, the twenty-second chapter, the peace, namely, *of the breast,* of which it is said to the Philippians, the fourth chapter: « The peace of God, which surpasseth all understanding, keep your hearts and minds in Christ Jesus » etc.; and the peace *of eternity,* of which Isaias, the last chapter[^p51-10]: « I will bring upon her a river of peace »; the Psalm: « Who hath placed peace in thy borders ».

31. Note also that peace is wished for men and declared, because through Christ man is set at peace with *God;* Isaias, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p51-11]: « He shall make peace with me, peace shall he make with me ». He is set at peace with the *Angels;* to the Colossians, the first chapter: « Making peace through the blood of his cross, both as to the things that are on earth and the things that are in heaven ». He is set at peace with *man;* to the Ephesians, the second chapter: « He is our peace, who hath made both one ». He is set at peace *in himself;* Job, the fifth chapter[^p51-12]: « Thou shalt know that thy tabernacle hath peace ». — In the figure of these it is said in Josue, the twenty-first chapter: « Peace was given by him to all round about ». This the Apostle always wishes, and Christ commands it below, the tenth chapter: « Into whatsoever house you enter, first say: Peace be to this house ».

[^tr-lumen]: **En.** Translator's note: this translation keeps *lux* as "light" and *lumen* as "lumen", except where a verse is quoted as a verse and no surrounding prose leans on the word. This pericope is the first in the Commentary on Luke where *lumen* is Bonaventure's own word: the shepherds' attention arises *ex angelici luminis circumfulgentia* (¶ 17), "from the shining round about of the angelic lumen", taken up again at ¶ 20; and ¶ 21 sets *lumen* against *lux* in one sentence, *Ideo autem hic Angelus lumen attulit, quia filius lucis fuit, et quia eum nuntiare venerat, qui est lux vera*, "the Angel brought a lumen here … because he was a son of light, and because he had come to announce him who is the true light". Acts 12:7, *lumen refulsit in habitaculo*, is adduced for that very word, so under the test the verse keeps "lumen" with the prose ("a lumen shined in the room"). *lux* stays "light" throughout (John 1:9, Isai. 9:2, *filius lucis*).
