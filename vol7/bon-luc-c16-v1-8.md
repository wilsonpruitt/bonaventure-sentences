# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XVI, Vers. 1-8
## *Exemplum parabolicum pietatis providae*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 402 -->

### Capitulum XVI.

#### Commendatio et persuasio pietatis humanae describitur per documenta et exempla parabolica quoad duo.

1. *Dicebat autem et ad discipulos suos* etc. Post expressionem impietatis Iudaicae et notificationem pietatis divinae subintroducit hic Evangelista *commendationem et persuasionem pietatis humanae*[^p402-8]. Et dividitur pars ista in duas. In quarum prima persuadetur ipsis discipulis per Christi documenta et per exempla parabolica pietas humana quantum ad *opera corporalia;* in secunda, quantum ad *spiritualia,* infra decimo septimo[^p402-9]: *Et ad discipulos suos ait* etc.

#### Primo, de pietate humana quoad opera corporalia tripliciter.

Pars prima continet praesens capitulum, quod in tres partes distinguitur. In quarum prima proponitur *exemplum parabolicum pietatis providae.* In secunda elicitur *documentum pietatis perfectae,* ibi[^p402-10]: *Et ego dico vobis: Facite* etc. In tertia subiungit *exemplum impietatis punitae,* ibi: *Homo quidam erat dives* etc.

#### Exemplum parabolicum pietatis providae.

Circa *exemplum* igitur *pietatis providae,* quod proponitur circa villicum, qui fuit de bonis domini
<!-- page 403 -->
sui largus, attendendum, quod illud partim est *exemplum expressum,* partim *dictum parabolicum;* nisi enim esset *exemplum expressum,* non subderetur in fine parabolae[^p403-1], quod « laudavit dominus villicum iniquitatis, quia prudenter egisset » etc.; sed rursus, nisi esset *parabolice dictum,* factum tantae fraudis adeo detestandum in exemplum nullatenus a Domino proponi deberet. Propter quod oportet intelligere, quod exemplum illud oportet partim ad litteram prosequi, partim etiam parabolice. Cum enim habeat rationem *exempli* et *parabolae* tam ipsum quam illud de divite epulone; istud tamen plus tenet rationem *parabolae,* et illud magis rationem *exempli* et ideo magis[^p403-2] intelligitur secundum litteram; istud autem partim secundum metaphoram, partim secundum litteram est intelligendum. — Ad huius autem parabolici exempli evidentiam attendendum, quod aliquid proponitur in eo *recogitandum,* aliquid *refutandum*[^p403-3], aliquid ut *formidandum,* aliquid ut *tolerandum,* aliquid ut *providendum,* aliquid ut *ad imitandum,* et aliquid ut *commendandum.* — *Recogitanda* namque est *origo potestatis humanae,* de qua ibi[^p403-4]: *Homo quidam erat dives.* — *Refugienda* est *abusio potestatis commissae,* quam insinuat ibi: *Et hic diffamatus est apud illum.* — *Formidanda* est *districtio aequitatis supernae,* de qua ibi[^p403-5]: *Et vocavit illum et ait illi.* — *Toleranda* est *imperfectio infirmitatis humanae,* quam ibi[^p403-6] insinuat: *Ait autem villicus intra se.* — *Providenda* est *inventio fidelis amicitiae,* ibi[^p403-7]: *Scio, quid faciam, ut, cum amotus fuero.* — *Imitanda* est *exhibitio liberalis clementiae,* de qua ibi: *Convocatis itaque singulis debitoribus.* — *Laudanda* sive commendanda est *discretio sagacis providentiae,* de qua ibi: *Laudavit dominus villicum iniquitatis.*

2. (Vers. 1.). Primo igitur in hac parabola insinuatur *origo potestatis mundanae* ut discipulis *recogitanda,* cum dicitur: *Dicebat autem et ad discipulos suos,* quibus dixit supra octavo[^p403-8]: « Vobis datum est nosse mysterium regni Dei »; et Ioannis decimo quinto: « Vos autem dixi amicos, quia omnia, quae audivi a Patre meo, nota feci vobis ». Ideo autem *ad discipulos* dicebat, quia, Iudaeis confutatis, eis debebat *pietas* persuaderi, ad quam vehementer incitat consideratio *originis potestatis terrenae,* quantum ad quam dicit: *Homo quidam erat dives, qui habebat villicum. Homo* iste *quidam* singularis et singulariter dives recte intelligitur Deus, secundum illud ad Romanos decimo[^p403-9]: « Idem Dominus omnium, dives in omnes, qui invocant illum ». Iste solus *dives* est, quia habet omnia et abundat; Psalmus: « Meus est enim orbis terrae et plenitudo eius »; et rursus: « Meae sunt omnes ferae silvarum »; et rursus: « Domini est terra et plenitudo eius, orbis terrarum » etc. — Huius hominis divitis *villicus* est quilibet homo, qui habet aliquod *posse* terrenum sive dignitatis, sive divitiarum ad dispensandum. Unde Glossa[^p403-10]: « Villicus est, cui Deus aliquas pecunias ad erogandum pauperibus commisit »; et ut alia Glossa dicit: « *Villicus* est proprie villae custos, sed ponitur hic pro oeconomo, id est dispensatore ». Humana igitur potestas, quoniam non est nisi ad tempus et ab alio collata, non est nisi quaedam villicatio et oeconomia. Unde primi Paralipomenon vigesimo nono[^p403-11]: « Tua sunt omnia, et quae de manu tua accepimus dedimus tibi. Peregrini enim sumus coram te et advenae sicut omnes patres nostri. Dies nostri quasi umbra sunt super terram, et nulla est mora ». Unde et Apostolus nolebat, se aestimari dominum, sed oeconomum et villicum, cum dicebat primae ad Corinthios quarto[^p403-12]: « Sic nos existimet homo, ut ministros Christi et dispensatores mysteriorum Dei » etc. Talem villicum rerum praesentium se existimabat Iob, cum dicebat Iob primo: « Dominus dedit, Dominus abstulit, sicut Domino placuit, ita factum est ».

3. Secundo quantum ad *abusionem potestatis commissae* ab omnibus *fugiendam* subdit: *Et hic diffamatus est apud illum, quasi dissipasset bona illius*[^p403-13]. *Bona* namque Dei *dissipat* qui temporalia ista
<!-- page 404 -->
vel male *retinet,* vel male *accipit,* vel male *dispensat.* Unde Glossa[^p404-1]: « Dissipatio est, quando male congregantur, vel non bene expenduntur ». Et ratio huius est, quia bona temporalia sunt, ut per haec acquirantur bona aeterna. Cum ergo haec temporalia sic habentur, quod propter illa aeterna perduntur; tunc absque dubio *dissipantur.* Qui ergo haec temporalia bona sic expendunt, quod non quaerunt in eis meritum salutis, sed solatium carnis, temporalia non *dispensant* ad modum boni villici, sed *dissipant* ad modum filii prodigi, de quo supra decimo quinto[^p404-2] dicitur, quod « dissipavit substantiam suam vivendo luxuriose ». Contra quos dicit Dominus per Osee Prophetam Osee secundo: « Dedi ei frumentum et vinum et oleum et argentum multiplicavi ei et aurum, quae fecerunt Baal. Idcirco convertar et sumam frumentum meum et vinum meum in tempore suo et liberabo lanam meam et linum meum, quae operiebant ignominiam eius. Et revelabo stultitiam eius in oculis amatorum eius ». Unde quia plures sunt dissipatores et pauci dispensatores, dicitur primae ad Corinthios quarto[^p404-3]: « Hic iam quaeritur inter dispensatores, ut fidelis quis inveniatur ». — Tunc autem haec temporalia bene dispensantur, quando in opera misericordiae distribuuntur, secundum illud supra duodecimo: « Quis, putas, est dispensator fidelis et prudens, quem constituit Dominus super familiam suam » etc. Ideo istius *dissipationis origo* et mater est immisericordia; propter quod Proverbiorum vigesimo nono[^p404-4]: « Novit iustus causam pauperum, impius ignorat scientiam, homines pestilentes dissipant civitatem ». — Tunc autem hic villicus apud Dominum *diffamatur,* quando clamor pauperum ascendit ad Dominum; unde dicitur Iacobi quinto[^p404-5]: « Agite nunc, divites, plorate ululantes in miseriis, quae advenient vobis »; et post: « Ecce, merces operariorum, qui messuerunt regiones vestras, quae fraudata est a vobis, clamat, et clamor eorum in aures Domini Sabaoth introivit ». Et quoniam crudelis est qui famam negligit, et omnis homo maxime horret infamiam; ideo talis bonorum abusio est cavenda, ne ex dissipatione bonorum dispensator cadat in opprobrium et derisionem publicam, secundum illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo[^p404-6]: « Fatuo non erit amicus, et non erit gratia in bonis illius ». « Quoties et quanti deridebunt eum? Neque enim quod habendum erat directo sensu distribuit, similiter et quod non erat habendum ».

4. (Vers. 2.). Tertio quantum ad *districtionem aequitatis divinae* cuilibet *formidandam* subditur: *Et vocavit illum et ait illi: Quid hoc audio de te?* Glossa[^p404-7]: « *Vocat,* quando incutit timorem aeternae damnationis »; de qua vocatione dicitur Ezechielis tertio: « Et audivi post me vocem commotionis magnae: Benedicta gloria Domini de loco suo ». Hac voce vocatur qui ad considerandum futurum iudicium excitatur, ubi arguetur non solum secundum famam, sed secundum conscientiam. — Propter quod addit: *Redde rationem villicationis tuae.* Haec namque vox in aure cuiuslibet debet resonare, quia absque dubio divina aequitas rationem exiget de *bonis commissis,* sicut innuitur Matthaei decimo octavo[^p404-8]: « Simile est regnum caelorum homini regi, qui voluit rationem ponere cum servis suis ». De *malis dimissis;* Matthaei decimo octavo: « Et cum coepisset rationem ponere, oblatus est ei unus, qui debebat ei decem millia talentorum ». « Misertus autem dominus servi illius, dimisit eum »; et post subditur, quod de hoc rationem exegit. De *bonis* etiam *omissis;* Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p404-9]: « Post multum temporis venit dominus servorum illorum et posuit rationem cum servis suis »; et subdit de servo pigro, quod proiectus est « in tenebras exteriores ». De *malis* etiam *perpetratis;* primae Petri quarto[^p404-10]: « Sufficit enim praeteritum tempus ad voluntatem gentium consummandam his qui ambulaverunt in luxuriis, desideriis, vinolentiis, comessationibus »; « qui reddent rationem ei qui paratus est iudicare vivos et mortuos ». Nec tantum de *magnis* malis, verum etiam de *parvis,* secundum illud Matthaei duodecimo[^p404-11]: « De omni verbo otioso, quod locuti fuerint homines, reddent rationem in die iudicii ». Ab hoc ergo villico exigit rationem villicationis quantum ad omnia quatuor praedicta, quia, quemadmodum dicit Bernardus[^p404-12], « sicut nec capillus de capite peribit, sic nec momentum de tempore transit, de quo
<!-- page 405 -->
non exigitur ratio ». — Et post istius rationis exactionem non restabit possibilitas ad merendum; ideo subdit: *Iam enim non poteris villicare,* quia dicitur Ecclesiastae nono[^p405-1]: « Nec opus nec ratio nec scientia nec sapientia erunt apud inferos, quo tu properas ». Unde et Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo dicitur cuilibet villico: « Non habebis iugiter potestatem »; et Ecclesiastici decimo: « Omnis potentatus brevis vita ». Propter quod Propheta in Psalmo[^p405-2]: « Nolite confidere in principibus neque in filiis hominum, in quibus non est salus. Exibit spiritus eius et revertetur in terram suam; in illa die peribunt omnes cogitationes eorum » etc.

5. (Vers. 3.). Quarto quantum ad *imperfectionem infirmitatis humanae* in villico *tolerandam* subditur: *Ait autem villicus intra se: Quid faciam, quia dominus meus aufert a me villicationem?* Quia enim timor iudicii sollicitat mentem ad procurationem remedii, ideo videns villicus, se esse iudicandum, requirit remedium, secundum illud Ecclesiastici decimo octavo[^p405-3]: « Ante iudicium para iustitiam et ante languorem adhibe medicinam et ante iudicium interroga te ipsum ». — Et quia perfectio poenitentiae consistit in afflictione et labore corporali et humilitate et pudore mentali; et haec duo recusat animus infirmi hominis: ideo addit: *Fodere non valeo,* propter laboris corporalis difficultatem, quia, sicut dicitur Proverbiorum vigesimo nono[^p405-4], « qui delicate a pueritia nutrit servum postea sentiet eum contumacem ». *Mendicare erubesco,* propter actus contemptibilis vilitatem; Ecclesiastici vigesimo nono[^p405-5]: « Minimum pro magno placeat tibi, et improperium peregrinationis non audies »; et post subdit: « Gravia haec homini habenti sensum: correptio domus et improperium foeneratoris ». Huic ergo villico similis est qui refugit ordinem Cisterciensium, quia laborant, vel ordinem Minorum, quia mendicant. Et hoc tamen tolerabile est, quia ex infirmitate venit refugere *laborem* et *pudorem,* illum sicut importabilem, istum sicut contemptibilem[^p405-6].

6. Sed licet ista sint difficilia homini infirmo, facilia tamen sunt per Christum perfecto Christiano. Nam Christus pro nobis factus pauper et in laboribus multis, secundum illud Psalmi[^p405-7]: « Pauper sum ego et in laboribus a iuventute mea », facit laborem appetibilem et mendicitatem honorabilem, quia « magna gloria est sequi Dominum », sicut dicitur Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio. Ipse autem Dominus de se ipso dicit[^p405-8]: « Ego autem mendicus sum et pauper, et Dominus sollicitus est mei »; quod quidem non dicitur quantum ad spiritualia, in quibus abundat, sed quantum ad temporalia, in quibus pro nobis egenus et mendicus effectus erat, secundum illud secundae ad Corinthios octavo: « Scitis gratiam Domini nostri Iesu Christi, quoniam pro nobis egenus effectus est, cum esset dives » etc. Et propterea dicit beatus Franciscus in regula sua[^p405-9], quod fratres suos « non oportet verecundari in mendicando, quia Dominus se pro nobis fecit pauperem in hoc mundo ». Verumtamen, quia villico tanquam imperfecto non erat data gratia, per quam perfecte efficeretur conformis Christo; ideo tanquam infirmus dicit: *Mendicare erubesco,* non ait: *mendicare contemno,* quia haec non esset infirmitas toleranda, sed impietas detestanda.

7. (Vers. 4.). Quinto quantum ad *inventionem fidelis amicitiae providendam* subdit: *Scio, quid faciam,* ut acquiram fidelem amicitiam. Unde Glossa[^p405-10]: « Villicus est cui Deus aliquas pecunias ad erogandum pauperibus commisit; qui, si cogitat, talem villicationem cum vita ista finiendam, magis de acquirendis amicis quam de congregandis divitiis pertractat ». Ille enim recte potest dicere: *Scio, quid faciam,* qui intendit acquirere proximi amicitiam, quia econtra dicitur primae Ioannis secundo[^p405-11]: « Qui odit fratrem suum in tenebris est et in tenebris ambulat et nescit, quo vadit, quia tenebrae obcaecaverunt oculos eius ». Sed econtra qui diligit, ipse scit, quid facit; facit enim secundum Christi doctrinam, qua quaerit remedium contra periculum. — Propter quod subdit: *Ut, cum amotus fuero a villicatione,* per divinam sententiam, secundum illud Apocalypsis secundo[^p405-12]: « Veniam tibi et movebo candelabrum tuum de loco suo »; *recipiant me in domos suas,* per amicabilem misericordiam. Nam haec est lex amicitiae in necessitate non deesse amico; unde Proverbiorum decimo septimo[^p405-13]: « Omni tempore diligit qui amicus est, et frater in angustiis comprobatur »,
<!-- page 406 -->
quia, Ecclesiastici sexto, « amicus fidelis, protectio fortis; qui invenit illum invenit thesaurum. Amico fideli nulla est comparatio, et non est digna ponderatio auri et argenti contra bonitatem fidei illius ».

8. (Vers. 5.). Sexto quantum ad *exhibitionem liberalis misericordiae imitandam* subditur: *Convocatis itaque singulis debitoribus domini sui;* ubi *ad litteram* ostenditur liberalitas munificentiae et misericordiae in villico, tum quia largitur *multis,* tum quia *multum,* tum quia *moderate: multis,* quia singulis debitoribus domini sui, secundum illud quod Dominus suadet supra sexto[^p406-1]: « Omni petenti te tribue »; et Isaiae trigesimo secundo: « Beati, qui seminatis super omnes aquas, immittentes pedem bovis et asini ». Et quia misericordia impendi debet indigentibus, ideo non cuilibet dat, sed *debitoribus;* secundum illud Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo[^p406-2]: « Dimitte eos qui confracti sunt liberos, et omne onus dirumpe. Frange esurienti panem tuum et egenos vagosque induc in domum tuam. Cum videris nudum, operi eum et carnem tuam ne despexeris ». — *Multum*[^p406-3], quia magnam summam pretii dimittit; propter quod subdit: *Dicebat primo: Quantum debes domino meo? Dicebat,* inquam, per familiarem allocutionem; Ecclesiastici quarto[^p406-4]: « Congregationi pauperum affabilem te facito. Declina pauperi sine tristitia aurem tuam et redde debitum et responde illi pacifice » etc.; et Ecclesiastici decimo octavo: « Nonne ardorem refrigerabit ros? Sic et verbum melius quam datum ».

9. (Vers. 6.). *At ille dixit ei*[^p406-5]*: Centum cados olei,* per *fidelem recognitionem debiti.* Debitum enim suum debet quisque fideliter recognoscere, ne forte dicatur sibi illud Apocalypsis tertio: « Dicis, quia dives sum et locupletatus et nullius egeo; et nescis, quia tu es miser et miserabilis et pauper et caecus et nudus ». Est autem *cadus* mensura continens tres urnas; unde Glossa[^p406-6]: « *Cadus* Graece, *amphora* Latine, continens urnas tres ». In hoc autem insinuatur debiti magnitudo opprimens debitorem, quia, Proverbiorum vigesimo secundo, « qui accipit mutuum servus est foenerantis ». — *Dixitque illi: Accipe cautionem tuam et sede cito et*[^p406-7] *scribe quinquaginta,* per *liberalem condonationem.* Multum namque exonerat qui medietatem oneris auferendo supportat; et in hoc lex caritatis impletur, secundum illud ad Galatas ultimo: « Alter alterius onera portate, et sic adimplebitis legem Christi ».

10. (Vers. 7.). *Moderate* etiam largitur, quia secundum exigentiam debiti; propter quod addit: *Deinde alii dixit: Tu vero quantum debes?* Ideo mensuram quaerit, ut mensurate distribuat, quia, Ecclesiastici quadragesimo secundo[^p406-8], « ubi manus multae sunt, claude; et quodcumque tradis, numera et appende, datum autem et acceptum omne describe ». — *Qui ait: Centum coros tritici.* Ubi magnam mensuram exprimit, ut largam condonationem obtineat. « *Corus* autem, ut dicit Glossa[^p406-9], modiis triginta impletur », unde maior mensura est quam *cadus.* Et quia in maioribus donis minor debet esse numerus, ut debita mensura servetur; ideo subditur: *Dixitque illi*[^p406-10]*: Accipe litteras tuas et scribe octoginta;* Glossa: « Quintam partem dimittit ». Licet autem minus videatur dimittere secundum proportionem arithmeticam, quia huic non nisi quintam partem dimittit, superiori autem dimidiam; plus tamen dimittit secundum geometricam, quia plus sunt in quantitate viginti cori tritici quam quinquaginta cadi olei. Ostenditur ergo in hoc, quod villicus iste liberaliter condonavit, quia non solum *multis* et *multa,* verum etiam *moderate,* secundum illud Tobiae quarto[^p406-11]: « Quomodo potueris, esto misericors. Si multum tibi fuerit, abundanter tribue; si autem exiguum, etiam illud libenter impartiri stude ». In hac autem condonatione *quinquaginta* partium et *viginti* recte intelligitur plena remissio liberalissimae pietatis. Nam *quinquagenarius,* qui consurgit ex septenario super se ipsum replicato, addita scilicet unitate, designat remissionem septiformis misericordiae. In cuius designationem in iubilaeo fiebat plena remissio, secundum illud Levitici vigesimo quinto[^p406-12]: « Sanctificabis annum quinquagesimum et vocabis remissionem cunctis habitatoribus terrae. Ipse enim est iubilaeus », « quia iubilaeus est annus quinquagesimus ». Iste autem numerus, additis viginti, consurgit in *septuagesimum,* ubi intelligitur remissio plena. In cuius rei figuram populus Domini in captivitatem ductus post septuaginta annos est liberatus, sicut dicitur Ieremiae vigesimo
<!-- page 407 -->
nono[^p407-1]: « Cum coeperint impleri in Babylone septuaginta anni, visitabo vos et suscitabo super vos verbum meum bonum et reducam vos ad locum istum » etc.

11. (Vers. 8.). Septimo quantum ad *discretionem providentiae commendandam* subditur: *Et laudavit dominus villicum iniquitatis, quia prudenter egisset*[^p407-2]*;* Glossa: « Non de fraude, sed de prudentia, quamvis peccantem, laudat ». Unde, licet villicus aliqua habeat in se vituperabilia, providendi tamen modus potest in exemplum bonorum trahi. Sicut enim serpens in diffusione veneni est detestabilis, sed in prudentia custodiendi caput est imitabilis a viris perfectis; propter quod dicitur Matthaei decimo[^p407-3]: « Estote prudentes sicut serpentes et simplices sicut columbae »; sic villicus iste detestandus est, quia fraudem commisit, ratione cuius *villicus iniquitatis* dicitur, et laudandus est, quia prudenter sibi contra periculum remedium adinvenit. — Et quia posset quis mirari, quod Dominus talem villicum in exemplum prudentiae traxit; ideo huic respondens Evangelista subdit: *Quia filii huius saeculi prudentiores filiis lucis in generatione sua sunt.* Non dicit, quod sint *simpliciter* prudentiores; sed prudentiores in his quae ad *carnalem* spectant *generationem,* sicut et bubo et murilegus clarius vident de nocte quam homo, non ad legendum litteras, sed mures capiendum[^p407-4].

12. Est tamen *prudentia spiritus,* quae est in filiis lucis excellentior quam prudentia carnis, quae est in viris carnalibus, secundum illud ad Romanos octavo[^p407-5]: « Prudentia carnis mors est, prudentia vero spiritus, vita et pax ». Habet tamen aliquid prudentia ista mundana, quod excitat et instruit ad prudentiam divinam, sicut sollicitudo hominis quaerentis pecuniam proponitur in exemplum homini quaerenti sapientiam; Proverbiorum secundo[^p407-6]: « Si sapientiam invocaveris et inclinaveris cor tuum prudentiae, si quaesieris illam quasi pecuniam et sicut thesauros effoderis illam; tunc intelliges timorem Domini et scientiam Dei invenies ». Unde quia nullum vitium delet extrema naturae vestigia[^p407-7], ideo habet omnis vitiosus aliquid, quod iustum excitet ad bonum. sicut ex consideratione hominis *lascivi* potest se homo excitare ad *amorem,* ex consideratione *avari* ad *sollicitudinem,* ex consideratione hominis *superbi* ad *magnanimitatem*[^p407-8]. — Unde ex ista parabola et exemplo non solum instruimur particulariter, verum etiam universaliter, ut ex omnibus sciamus elicere documentum, quod nos excitet et informet ad bonum. Sicut enim Sancti sua bonitate excellenti testimonium debent habere et habent ab his qui foris sunt; ita vitia omnia effigiem aliquam habent virtutis et pietatis[^p407-9]. Virtus ab omnibus generali quadam voce approbatur, sed impius ab omnibus condemnatur.

## English
<!-- page 402 -->

### Chapter XVI.

#### The commendation and persuasion of human piety is described by lessons and parabolic examples as to two things.

1. *And he said also to his disciples* etc. After the expression of Jewish impiety and the notification of divine piety[^tr-pietas], the Evangelist here brings in *the commendation and persuasion of human piety*[^p402-8]. And this part is divided into two. In the first of which human piety is persuaded to the disciples themselves by the lessons of Christ and by parabolic examples as to *corporal works;* in the second, as to *spiritual* ones, below, the seventeenth chapter[^p402-9]: *And he said to his disciples* etc.

#### First, of human piety as to corporal works, in three ways.

The first part contains the present chapter, which is distinguished into three parts. In the first of which there is set forth *the parabolic example of provident piety.* In the second there is drawn out *the instruction of perfect piety,* at[^p402-10]: *And I say to you: Make* etc. In the third he subjoins *the example of punished impiety,* at: *There was a certain rich man* etc.

#### The parabolic example of provident piety.

Concerning, then, *the example of provident piety,* which is set forth concerning the steward who was generous with his lord's
<!-- page 403 -->
goods, it is to be attended that it is partly *an expressed example,* partly *a parabolic saying;* for were it not *an expressed example,* there would not be subjoined at the end of the parable[^p403-1] that « the lord praised the steward of iniquity, because he had done prudently » etc.; but again, were it not *said parabolically,* a deed of such great fraud, so detestable, ought in no way to be set forth by the Lord as an example. On account of which it must be understood that that example must be followed partly according to the letter, partly also parabolically. For while both it and that of the rich glutton have the character of *an example* and of *a parable,* this one nevertheless holds more the character of *a parable,* and that one more the character of *an example,* and therefore it is more[^p403-2] understood according to the letter; but this one is to be understood partly according to metaphor, partly according to the letter. — Now for the evidence of this parabolic example it is to be attended that something is set forth in it *to be reflected upon,* something *to be refused*[^p403-3], something as *to be feared,* something as *to be tolerated,* something as *to be provided for,* something as *for imitating,* and something as *to be commended.* — For *to be reflected upon* is *the origin of human power,* of which at[^p403-4]: *There was a certain rich man.* — *To be fled* is *the abuse of committed power,* which he insinuates at: *And the same was defamed before him.* — *To be feared* is *the strictness of the supernal equity,* of which at[^p403-5]: *And he called him and said to him.* — *To be tolerated* is *the imperfection of human infirmity,* which he insinuates at[^p403-6]: *And the steward said within himself.* — *To be provided for* is *the finding of faithful friendship,* at[^p403-7]: *I know what I will do, that when I shall be removed.* — *To be imitated* is *the showing of liberal clemency,* of which at: *Therefore calling together every one of the debtors.* — *To be praised* or commended is *the discretion of shrewd providence,* of which at: *The lord praised the steward of iniquity.*

2. (Vers. 1.). First, then, in this parable there is insinuated *the origin of worldly power* as *to be reflected upon* by the disciples, when it is said: *And he said also to his disciples,* to whom he said above, the eighth chapter[^p403-8]: « To you it is given to know the mystery of the kingdom of God »; and John, the fifteenth chapter: « But I have called you friends, because all things which I have heard of my Father I have made known to you ». Now he was speaking *to the disciples* for this reason, that, the Jews having been confuted, *piety* had to be persuaded to them, to which the consideration of *the origin of earthly power* vehemently incites, as to which he says: *There was a certain rich man who had a steward.* This *certain man,* singular and singularly rich, is rightly understood to be God, according to that of Romans, the tenth chapter[^p403-9]: « The same Lord of all, rich unto all who call upon him ». He alone is *rich,* because he has all things and abounds; the Psalm: « For the world is mine, and the fulness thereof »; and again: « For all the beasts of the woods are mine »; and again: « The earth is the Lord's and the fulness thereof, the world » etc. — The *steward* of this rich man is any man whatever who has some earthly *power* to dispense, whether of dignity or of riches. Whence the Gloss[^p403-10]: « A steward is he to whom God has committed certain monies for paying out to the poor »; and as another Gloss says: « A *villicus* is properly the keeper of a villa, but it is put here for an *oeconomus,* that is, a dispenser ». Human power, then, since it is only for a time and conferred by another, is nothing but a certain stewardship and management. Whence First Paralipomenon, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p403-11]: « All things are thine, and what we have received of thy hand we have given thee. For we are sojourners before thee, and strangers, as all our fathers. Our days are as a shadow upon the earth, and there is no stay ». Whence also the Apostle did not wish to be esteemed a lord, but a manager and a steward, when he said in First Corinthians, the fourth chapter[^p403-12]: « Let a man so account of us as of the ministers of Christ and the dispensers of the mysteries of God » etc. Such a steward of present things Job esteemed himself, when he said, Job, the first chapter: « The Lord gave, the Lord hath taken away; as it hath pleased the Lord, so is it done ».

3. Second, as to *the abuse of committed power,* which is *to be fled* by all, he subjoins: *And the same was defamed before him, as if he had wasted his goods*[^p403-13]. For he *wastes* the *goods* of God who these temporal things
<!-- page 404 -->
either badly *retains,* or badly *receives,* or badly *dispenses.* Whence the Gloss[^p404-1]: « Wasting is when they are badly gathered, or not well spent ». And the reason of this is that temporal goods exist so that through them eternal goods may be acquired. When, therefore, these temporal things are so had that on account of them the eternal are lost, then without doubt they *are wasted.* Those, therefore, who so spend these temporal goods that they seek in them not the merit of salvation but the solace of the flesh do not *dispense* temporal things after the manner of a good steward, but *waste* them after the manner of the prodigal son, of whom it is said above, the fifteenth chapter[^p404-2], that he « wasted his substance, living riotously ». Against whom the Lord speaks through the Prophet Osee, Osee, the second chapter: « I gave her corn, and wine, and oil, and multiplied her silver, and gold, which they made into Baal. Therefore will I return, and take my corn and my wine in its season, and I will set at liberty my wool and my flax, which covered her disgrace. And I will lay open her folly in the eyes of her lovers ». Whence, because the wasters are many and the dispensers few, it is said in First Corinthians, the fourth chapter[^p404-3]: « Here now it is sought among the dispensers, that a man be found faithful ». — But these temporal things are well dispensed then, when they are distributed in works of mercy, according to that above, the twelfth chapter: « Who, thinkest thou, is the faithful and prudent dispenser, whom the Lord setteth over his family » etc. Therefore the *origin* and mother *of this wasting* is unmercifulness; on account of which, Proverbs, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p404-4]: « The just taketh notice of the cause of the poor; the wicked is void of knowledge; pestilent men waste a city ». — And this steward is then *defamed* before the Lord, when the cry of the poor goes up to the Lord; whence it is said in James, the fifth chapter[^p404-5]: « Go to now, ye rich men, weep and howl in your miseries, which shall come upon you »; and after: « Behold, the hire of the labourers, who have reaped down your fields, which by fraud has been kept back by you, crieth, and the cry of them hath entered into the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth ». And since he is cruel who neglects his good name, and every man most of all shudders at infamy, therefore such an abuse of goods is to be guarded against, lest from the wasting of goods the dispenser fall into reproach and public derision, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the twentieth chapter[^p404-6]: « A fool shall have no friend, and there shall be no thanks for his good things ». « How often and how many will laugh him to scorn? For he did not distribute with right sense that which was to be had, and in like manner that which was not to be had ».

4. (Vers. 2.). Third, as to *the strictness of the divine equity,* which is *to be feared* by every one, it is subjoined: *And he called him and said to him: How is it that I hear this of thee?* The Gloss[^p404-7]: « He *calls,* when he strikes in the fear of eternal damnation »; of which calling it is said in Ezechiel, the third chapter: « And I heard behind me the voice of a great commotion: Blessed be the glory of the Lord from his place ». By this voice he is called who is roused to consider the future judgement, where he will be reproved not only according to his good name, but according to his conscience. — On account of which he adds: *Give an account of thy stewardship.* For this voice ought to resound in the ear of every one, because without doubt the divine equity will exact an account of *goods committed,* as is intimated in Matthew, the eighteenth chapter[^p404-8]: « The kingdom of heaven is like to a man, a king, who would take an account with his servants ». Of *evils forgiven;* Matthew, the eighteenth chapter: « And when he had begun to take the account, one was brought to him that owed him ten thousand talents ». « And the lord of that servant, being moved with pity, let him go »; and afterward it is subjoined that he exacted an account of this. Of *goods* also *omitted;* Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p404-9]: « After a long time the lord of those servants came and took an account with his servants »; and he subjoins of the slothful servant that he was cast « into the exterior darkness ». Of *evils* also *perpetrated;* First Peter, the fourth chapter[^p404-10]: « For the time past is sufficient to have consummated the will of the Gentiles, for them who have walked in riotousness, lusts, excess of wine, revellings »; « who shall render an account to him who is ready to judge the living and the dead ». And not only of *great* evils, but also of *small* ones, according to that of Matthew, the twelfth chapter[^p404-11]: « Of every idle word that men shall have spoken, they shall render an account in the day of judgement ». From this steward, then, he exacts an account of the stewardship as to all four things aforesaid, because, as Bernard says[^p404-12], « just as not a hair of the head shall perish, so not a moment of time passes of which
<!-- page 405 -->
an account is not exacted ». — And after the exaction of this account no possibility of meriting will remain; therefore he subjoins: *For now thou canst be steward no longer,* because it is said in Ecclesiastes, the ninth chapter[^p405-1]: « Neither work nor reason nor knowledge nor wisdom shall be in hell, whither thou art hastening ». Whence also in Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter, it is said to every steward: « Thou shalt not have power continually »; and in Ecclesiasticus, the tenth chapter: « All power is of short life ». On account of which the Prophet in the Psalm[^p405-2]: « Put not your trust in princes, nor in the children of men, in whom there is no salvation. His spirit shall go forth, and he shall return into his earth; in that day all their thoughts shall perish » etc.

5. (Vers. 3.). Fourth, as to *the imperfection of human infirmity,* which is *to be tolerated* in the steward, it is subjoined: *And the steward said within himself: What shall I do, because my lord taketh away from me the stewardship?* For since the fear of judgement stirs the mind to procure a remedy, therefore the steward, seeing that he is to be judged, seeks a remedy, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter[^p405-3]: « Before judgement prepare thee justice, and before sickness apply medicine, and before judgement examine thyself ». — And since the perfection of penance consists in affliction and bodily labour and in humility and shame of mind, and the mind of an infirm man refuses these two, therefore he adds: *To dig I am not able,* on account of the difficulty of bodily labour, because, as it is said in Proverbs, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p405-4], « he that nourisheth his servant delicately from his childhood, afterwards shall find him stubborn ». *To beg I am ashamed,* on account of the vileness of a contemptible act; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p405-5]: « Be contented with little instead of much, and thou shalt not hear the reproach of being a stranger »; and afterward he subjoins: « These things are grievous to a man that hath understanding: the upbraiding of houseroom, and the reproaching of the lender ». Like this steward, then, is he who flees the order of the Cistercians, because they labour, or the order of the Minors, because they beg. And this nevertheless is tolerable, because it comes of infirmity to flee *labour* and *shame,* the former as unbearable, the latter as contemptible[^p405-6].

6. But although these things are difficult for an infirm man, yet they are easy through Christ for a perfect Christian. For Christ was made poor for us and in many labours, according to that of the Psalm[^p405-7]: « I am poor, and in labours from my youth »; he makes labour desirable and beggary honourable, because « it is great glory to follow the Lord », as it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third chapter. And the Lord himself says of himself[^p405-8]: « But I am a beggar and poor, and the Lord is careful for me »; which indeed is not said as to spiritual things, in which he abounds, but as to temporal things, in which he had been made needy and a beggar for us, according to that of Second Corinthians, the eighth chapter: « You know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that for us he became needy, when he was rich » etc. And therefore blessed Francis says in his rule[^p405-9] that his brothers « ought not to be shamefast in begging, because the Lord made himself poor for us in this world ». Nevertheless, because to the steward, as being imperfect, grace had not been given through which he might be made perfectly conformed to Christ, therefore as one infirm he says: *To beg I am ashamed;* he does not say: *to beg I despise,* because this would not be an infirmity to be tolerated, but an impiety to be detested.

7. (Vers. 4.). Fifth, as to *the finding of faithful friendship,* which is *to be provided for,* he subjoins: *I know what I will do,* that I may acquire faithful friendship. Whence the Gloss[^p405-10]: « The steward is he to whom God has committed certain monies for paying out to the poor; who, if he considers that such a stewardship is to be ended with this life, treats rather of acquiring friends than of gathering riches ». For he can rightly say: *I know what I will do,* who intends to acquire the friendship of his neighbour, because on the contrary it is said in First John, the second chapter[^p405-11]: « He that hateth his brother is in darkness, and walketh in darkness, and knoweth not whither he goeth, because the darkness hath blinded his eyes ». But on the contrary he who loves, he knows what he does; for he does according to the doctrine of Christ, by which he seeks a remedy against danger. — On account of which he subjoins: *That when I shall be removed from the stewardship,* by the divine sentence, according to that of the Apocalypse, the second chapter[^p405-12]: « I will come to thee and will move thy candlestick out of its place »; *they may receive me into their houses,* by friendly mercy. For this is the law of friendship, not to fail a friend in necessity; whence Proverbs, the seventeenth chapter[^p405-13]: « He that is a friend loveth at all times, and a brother is proved in distress »,
<!-- page 406 -->
because, Ecclesiasticus, the sixth chapter, « a faithful friend is a strong defence; he that hath found him hath found a treasure. Nothing can be compared to a faithful friend, and no weight of gold and silver is worthy to be set against the goodness of his fidelity ».

8. (Vers. 5.). Sixth, as to *the showing of liberal mercy,* which is *to be imitated,* it is subjoined: *Therefore calling together every one of his lord's debtors;* where *according to the letter* there is shown the liberality of munificence and of mercy in the steward, both because he bestows on *many,* and because *much,* and because *in measure: on many,* because on every one of his lord's debtors, according to that which the Lord counsels above, the sixth chapter[^p406-1]: « Give to every one that asketh thee »; and Isaias, the thirty-second chapter: « Blessed are ye that sow upon all waters, sending thither the foot of the ox and the ass ». And because mercy ought to be spent on the needy, therefore he does not give to whomsoever, but to *debtors;* according to that of Isaias, the fifty-eighth chapter[^p406-2]: « Let them that are broken go free, and break asunder every burden. Deal thy bread to the hungry, and bring the needy and the wanderers into thy house. When thou shalt see one naked, cover him, and despise not thy flesh ». — *Much*[^p406-3], because he remits a great sum of the price; on account of which he subjoins: *He said to the first: How much dost thou owe my lord? He said,* I say, by a familiar address; Ecclesiasticus, the fourth chapter[^p406-4]: « Make thyself affable to the congregation of the poor. Bow down thy ear to the poor without sadness, and pay thy debt, and answer him peaceably » etc.; and Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter: « Shall not the dew assuage the heat? So also a word is better than a gift ».

9. (Vers. 6.). *But he said to him*[^p406-5]*: An hundred barrels of oil,* by *the faithful acknowledgement of the debt.* For each one ought faithfully to acknowledge his debt, lest perhaps that of the Apocalypse, the third chapter, be said to him: « Thou sayest: I am rich and made wealthy and have need of nothing; and thou knowest not that thou art wretched and miserable and poor and blind and naked ». Now the barrel, *cadus,* is a measure containing three urns; whence the Gloss[^p406-6]: « *Cadus* in Greek, *amphora* in Latin, containing three urns ». And in this is insinuated the greatness of the debt oppressing the debtor, because, Proverbs, the twenty-second chapter, « he that taketh a loan is servant to him that lendeth ». — *And he said to him: Take thy bill and sit down quickly and*[^p406-7] *write fifty,* by *liberal condonation.* For he unburdens much who supports half of the burden by taking it away; and in this the law of charity is fulfilled, according to that of Galatians, the last chapter: « Bear ye one another's burdens, and so you shall fulfil the law of Christ ».

10. (Vers. 7.). He bestows also *in measure,* because according to the demand of the debt; on account of which he adds: *Then he said to another: And how much dost thou owe?* Therefore he asks the measure, that he may distribute with measure, because, Ecclesiasticus, the forty-second chapter[^p406-8], « where there are many hands, shut up; and whatsoever thou deliverest, number and weigh, and put in writing all that is given and received ». — *Who said: An hundred quarters of wheat.* Where he expresses a great measure, that he may obtain a large condonation. « Now the *corus,* as the Gloss says[^p406-9], is filled with thirty modii », whence it is a greater measure than the *cadus.* And because in greater gifts the number ought to be smaller, that the due measure may be kept, therefore it is subjoined: *He said to him*[^p406-10]*: Take thy bill, and write eighty;* the Gloss: « He remits the fifth part ». Now although he seems to remit less according to arithmetical proportion, because to this one he remits only the fifth part, but to the former the half, yet according to geometrical proportion he remits more, because twenty quarters of wheat are more in quantity than fifty barrels of oil. It is shown, then, in this that this steward condoned liberally, because not only to *many* and *much,* but also *in measure,* according to that of Tobias, the fourth chapter[^p406-11]: « As thou shalt be able, be merciful. If thou have much, give abundantly; but if little, take care to bestow even that willingly ». And in this condonation of *fifty* parts and of *twenty* there is rightly understood the full remission of the most liberal piety. For the *fiftieth number,* which arises from the sevenfold folded upon itself, unity namely being added, designates the remission of sevenfold mercy. In the designation of which there was made in the jubilee a full remission, according to that of Leviticus, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p406-12]: « Thou shalt sanctify the fiftieth year, and shalt call a remission to all the inhabitants of the land. For it is the jubilee », « because the jubilee is the fiftieth year ». And this number, twenty being added, rises to the *seventieth,* where full remission is understood. In figure of which thing the people of the Lord, led into captivity, was freed after seventy years, as it is said in Jeremias, the twenty-
<!-- page 407 -->
ninth chapter[^p407-1]: « When the seventy years shall begin to be accomplished in Babylon, I will visit you, and I will raise up upon you my good word, and bring you back to this place » etc.

11. (Vers. 8.). Seventh, as to *the discretion of providence,* which is *to be commended,* it is subjoined: *And the lord praised the steward of iniquity, because he had done prudently*[^p407-2]*;* the Gloss: « Not for the fraud, but for the prudence he praises him, though sinning ». Whence, although the steward has some things blameworthy in him, yet his manner of providing can be drawn into an example for the good. For just as the serpent is detestable in the spreading of its venom, but in the prudence of guarding its head is to be imitated by perfect men, on account of which it is said in Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p407-3]: « Be ye prudent as serpents and simple as doves »; so this steward is to be detested, because he committed fraud, by reason of which he is called *the steward of iniquity,* and is to be praised, because he prudently devised for himself a remedy against danger. — And because someone might wonder that the Lord drew such a steward into an example of prudence, therefore, answering this, the Evangelist subjoins: *For the children of this world are more prudent than the children of light in their generation.* He does not say that they are *simply* more prudent, but more prudent in those things which look to *carnal generation,* just as the owl and the cat also see more clearly by night than man, not for reading letters, but for catching mice[^p407-4].

12. There is nevertheless *a prudence of the spirit,* which is in the children of light, more excellent than the prudence of the flesh, which is in carnal men, according to that of Romans, the eighth chapter[^p407-5]: « The prudence of the flesh is death, but the prudence of the spirit, life and peace ». Yet this worldly prudence has something which rouses and instructs to divine prudence, just as the solicitude of a man seeking money is set forth as an example to a man seeking wisdom; Proverbs, the second chapter[^p407-6]: « If thou shalt call upon wisdom, and incline thy heart to prudence, if thou shalt seek her as money, and shalt dig for her as for treasures, then shalt thou understand the fear of the Lord, and shalt find the knowledge of God ». Whence, because no vice blots out the last vestiges of nature[^p407-7], therefore every vicious man has something which may rouse the just man to good, just as from the consideration of a *lascivious* man a man can rouse himself to *love,* from the consideration of an *avaricious* man to *solicitude,* from the consideration of a *proud* man to *magnanimity*[^p407-8]. — Whence from this parable and example we are instructed not only particularly but also universally, that we may know how to draw out from all things an instruction that may rouse us and form us to good. For just as the Saints, by their excellent goodness, ought to have and do have a testimony from those who are without, so all vices have some likeness of virtue and of piety[^p407-9]. Virtue is approved by all with a certain general voice, but the impious man is condemned by all.
