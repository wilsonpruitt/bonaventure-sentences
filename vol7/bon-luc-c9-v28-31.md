# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. IX, Vers. 28-31
## *Factum est autem post haec verba. Post exhortationem ad asperitatem viae subiungit Evangelista ostensionem suavitatis gloriae*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 230 -->
43. *Factum est autem post haec verba.* Post exhortationem ad asperitatem viae subiungit Evangelista *ostensionem suavitatis gloriae*[^p230-2]. Procedit autem hoc ordine. Primo enim describit *revelationem gloriae promissae;* secundo vero, *considerationem gloriae revelatae,* ibi[^p230-3]: *Petrus autem, et qui cum ipso erant;* tertio vero, *confirmationem revelationis ostensae,* ibi: *Factum est autem in sequenti die,* et hoc quidem facit per admirabilem curationem daemoniaci.

*Revelationem autem futurae gloriae* dupliciter describit Evangelista: primo, quoad antecedentia; secundo, quoad concomitantia, ibi[^p230-4]: *Et facta est, dum oraret* etc. — Sciendum autem, quod quantum ad transfigurationem Christi fuerunt antecedentia sive disponentia tria, scilicet *exigentia termini, eligentia consortii* et *eminentia loci.* Haec autem tria sunt, quae Deus observat in communicando revelationes divinas.

44. (Vers. 28.). Primo igitur quantum ad *exigentiam termini* dicit: *Factum est autem post haec verba fere dies octo.* Quoniam enim Dominus promiserat, se gloriam discipulis ostensurum, et « Dominus non tardat promissionem suam », sicut dicitur secundae Petri tertio[^p230-5]; ideo inter promissionem et impletionem non debuit nisi modicum spatium temporis intervenire, scilicet unius septimanae. Cuius figura praecessit in desponsatione Rachel, de qua dixit Laban ad Iacob[^p230-6]: « Imple hebdomadam dierum huius copulae, et hanc quoque tibi dabo ». — Et hoc notat, cum dicit, *factum fere dies octo,* quia octavo die hoc fecit, revoluto ordine septimanae, qui non sunt octo dies completi propter defectum partis primae et ultimae diei; et Matthaei decimo septimo[^p230-7] et Marci nono non computantur primus et ultimus dies, sed intermedii integri. Nam ibi dicitur: « Factum est post dies sex », in quo non est aliqua contrarietas, sed una sententia aliam explanat. Voluit autem Spiritus sanctus utroque modo exprimere propter insinuationem mysterii. Quia autem sex diebus mundum Deus fabricavit et post diem sextum requievit[^p230-8], ideo sex diebus erat laborandum et post contemplationi vacandum. Ideo ad hoc insinuandum dicit Matthaeus: « Post dies sex ». Sed quoniam perfecta et summa quies et contemplatio non est ante octavam, scilicet resurrectionem; ideo hic dicit: dies octo. Et hoc est quod dicitur in Glossa[^p230-9] a Beda et Ambrosio: Post haec verba etc.

45. Secundo quantum ad *eligentiam consortii* dicitur: *Et assumsit Petrum, Ioannem et Iacobum.* Hos tres assumsit tanquam magis intimos et familiares, sicut assumsit, supra octavo[^p230-10], in suscitatione puellae. Ideo *tres* assumit propter commendandum mysterium Trinitatis et propter firmitatem testimonii; unde in Glossa dicitur: « Ut tribus idoneis testibus totus mundus crederet ». Ideo autem *hos tres* specialiter eligit propter *eminentiam,* quam considerabat in eis: quia *Petrus* futurus erat primus in Ecclesia gubernanda, sicut dicit Matthaeus decimo sexto[^p230-11]: « Tu es Petrus » etc. « Tibi dabo claves regni caelorum ». Et *Iacobus* primus interficiendus erat inter Apostolos pro Ecclesia multiplicanda; Actuum duodecimo dicitur de Herode, quod « occidit Iacobum, fratrem Ioannis, gladio ». *Ioannes,* praecipuus in Matre custodienda; Ioannis decimo nono: « Videns Iesus Matrem et discipulum stantem » etc. — Vel ideo hos tres assumit propter *instructionem figurae.* Per hos triplex status in Ecclesia salvandorum designatur, scilicet *praelati* in Petro, *activi* in Iacobo, *contemplativi* in Ioanne, sicut in Noe, Daniele et Iob[^p230-12]; vel in Petro *coniugati,* in Iacobo *continentes,* in Ioanne *virgines,* secundum triplicem continentiam, cui debetur fructus trigesimus, sexagesimus et centesimus[^p230-13]. Nam de uxore Petri legimus, de virginitate Ioannis, de Iacobo neutrum dicitur expresse.
<!-- page 231 -->

46. Tertio vero quantum ad *eminentiam loci* subiungit: *Et ascendit in montem, ut oraret. Mons* enim est locus eminens et a terrenis ad caelestia porrectus, et ideo contemplationi et orationi idoneus. Talis erat hic mons; unde dicitur Matthaei decimo septimo[^p231-1], quod « duxit eos in montem excelsum seorsum », ut scilicet sequestrati essent a turba et a terrenis. Et tales sunt divinis revelationibus apti; unde Exodi tertio[^p231-2] dicitur de Moyse, quod « cum minasset gregem ad interiora deserti, venit usque ad montem Dei Horeb », ubi scilicet apparuit Dominus in igne rubi. Similiter in Legis susceptione; Exodi vigesimo quarto: « Dixit Dominus ad Moysen: Ascende ad me in montem et esto ibi, daboque tibi duas tabulas lapideas ». Similiter in terrae promissae circumscriptione, secundum illud Deuteronomii ultimo: « Ascendit Moyses de campestribus Moab in montem Nebo, in verticem Phasga, contra Iericho, ostenditque ei Dominus omnem terram Galaad usque Dan ». — Ex his igitur colligitur, quod mons est idoneus ad divinas apparitiones, instructiones et contemplationes. Unde Beda in Glossa[^p231-3]: « In monte oraturus ascendit, ut innuat, quia qui resurrectionem exspectant mentem in excelsis elevare et continuis precibus debent insistere », et hoc in monte propter sui eminentiam, secundum illud Deuteronomii trigesimo tertio: « Dominus de Sinai venit et de Seir ortus est nobis, apparuit de monte Pharan et cum eo Sanctorum millia. In dextera eius ignea lex ». « Et qui appropinquant pedibus eius accipient de doctrina illius »; sicque apparent tria disponentia ad divinam transfigurationem etiam secundum intelligentiam *litteralem.*

47. *Spiritualiter* autem in his tribus magna est valde informatio volentium pertingere ad videndam Domini transfigurationem per contemplationis excessum. In *numero* enim *dierum* intelligi dantur *gradus* contemplationum succedentium sibi, sicut dies succedit diei. Et secundum Matthaeum[^p231-4] numerantur *sex dies* ad insinuandum *sex gradus* contemplationis, quos exprimit Richardus in libro de Arca sive de Contemplatione[^p231-5], sic inquiens primo libro, sexto capitulo: « Sex sunt contemplationis genera a se et inter se omnino divisa. Primum est *in imaginatione* et secundum *solam imaginationem;* secundum est *in imaginatione* secundum *rationem;* tertium est *in ratione* secundum *imaginationem;* quartum est in *ratione* secundum *rationem;* quintum est *supra rationem,* sed *non praeter* rationem; sextum est *supra rationem* et videtur esse *praeter* rationem. Duo itaque sunt in imaginatione, duo in ratione, duo in intelligentia ». — Prima duo sunt circa *corporalia,* secunda duo circa *spiritualia,* tertia duo circa *sempiternalia* et incomprehensibilia[^p231-6]. — Haec autem sex genera per *sex dies* intelliguntur et per *sex gradus,* quibus ascendebatur ad Salomonis thronum, secundum illud tertii Regum decimo[^p231-7], quod « Salomon fecit sibi thronum de ebore grandem et vestivit eum auro fulvo nimis, qui habebat sex gradus ». Et huic numerus perfectionis congruit; est enim senarius primus numerus perfectorum.

48. Secundum autem Lucam computantur *octo dies.* Lucas enim agit de hac apparitione, secundum quod exprimit *gloriam* post resurrectionem; quod patet, quia ibi[^p231-8] tangit *somnum* et *evigilationem;* ideo insinuat *octo gradus,* quorum septem sunt in via, octavus reservatur in patria. De his septem dicit Augustinus in libro de Quantitate animae in fine[^p231-9]: « Ascendentibus, inquit, sursumversus primus gradus docendi causa dicatur *animatio;* secundus, *sensus;* tertius, *ars;* quartus, *virtus;* quintus, *tranquillitas;* sextus, *ingressio;* septimus, *contemplatio* »; post non restat nisi *beata visio;* et sic sunt fere octo dies. Et ut dicit[^p231-10], « primus est *de* corpore, secundus *per* corpus, tertius *circa* corpus, quartus *ad se ipsam,* quintus *in se ipsa,* sextus *ad Deum,* septimus *apud Deum* ». Et hi gradus non tantum dicunt gradus contemplationis, sed quibus venitur ad contemplationem.

Quidam autem, licet simplici sermone, sed non scientia, frater Aegidius, quem frequentissime rapi probatum est, sic distinguit gradus dicens[^p231-11]: Septem sunt gradus contemplationis: primus est *ignis;* secundus est *unctio;* tertius est *ecstasis;* quartus, *contemplatio;* quintus, *gustus;* sextus, *requies;* septimus, *gloria;* post quos non restat nisi *felicitas sempiterna.* — His igitur septem diebus pervenitur
<!-- page 232 -->
usque ad diem octavum gloriae; quos voluit in assignatione temporis Spiritus sanctus designare.

49. Per *tres* autem *personas assumtas* dantur intelligi *tres virtutes* ad contemplandum necessariae, scilicet *fides, spes, caritas; fides,* per Petrum, qui interpretatur agnoscens; *spes,* per Iacobum luctatorem; per Ioannem, in quo gratia, *caritas*[^p232-1]. Sine his autem pennis nullus potest conscendere ad montana. — Vel per tres personas intelligimus *tres dispositiones* necessarias animae contemplativae, scilicet *industriam, disciplinam* et *gratiam*[^p232-2]: prima in Petro, secunda in Iacobo, tertia in Ioanne. Oportet enim, *intelligentiam* illuminari, *concupiscentiam* domari et *affectum* inflammari, ut possimus sursum cum Domino ferri. Aliquo horum deficiente, contemplationis negotium perit. — Vel per has tres personas intelliguntur *tres modi ascendendi* in apicem contemplationis. Uno enim modo ascenditur per viam *splendoris,* et hunc modum ascendendi docet beatus Augustinus[^p232-3], et hic designatur per Petrum. Alio modo ascenditur per viam *doloris et gemitus,* et hic in Iacobo, et iste modus est communis orantibus. Tertio modo per viam *amoris,* et hunc docet Dionysius[^p232-4], et hic designatur per Ioannem; et hic est praecipuus inter omnes, in quo maxime gratia movet; in primo autem multum valet industria vel scientia; secundus autem modus quasi permixtus est inter gratiam et naturalem industriam et acquisitam.

50. Per *locum* autem, in quem ascendit, recte intelligi potest eminentia vitae *contemplativae.* Est enim mons locus *magisterii;* unde dicitur Matthaei quinto[^p232-5], quod « ascendit Iesus in montem, et cum sedisset » etc. Et ex hoc datur nobis intelligi, quod vita contemplativa abundat *cognitione,* et ideo in persona contemplativorum dicit Isaias, secundo: « Venite, ascendamus ad montem Domini et ad domum Dei Iacob, et docebit nos » etc.; unde et de Moyse dicitur Exodi vigesimo quarto, quod « ascendit in montem et fuit ibi quadraginta diebus et quadraginta noctibus », et tunc accepit Legem. — Est etiam mons locus *sacrificii,* secundum illud Genesis vigesimo secundo[^p232-6]: « Tolle unigenitum filium tuum, quem diligis Isaac, et offer illum mihi super unum montium, quem monstravero tibi »; et Exodi tertio: « Cum eduxeris populum de Aegypto, immolabis Deo super montem istum ». Ex quo colligitur, quod vita contemplativa abundat *devotione;* unde anima contemplativa Canticorum quarto: « Vadam ad montem myrrhae et ad collem thuris » etc. — Est etiam mons locus *incendii* propter radios solis; unde Ecclesiastici quadragesimo tertio[^p232-7]: « Tripliciter sol exurens montes et radios igneos exsufflans »; et hoc propter multam refulgentiam, iuxta illud primi Machabaeorum sexto: « Refulsit sol in clypeos aureos et aereos, et resplenduerunt montes ab eis sicut lampades ignis ». Ex quo datur intelligi, quod contemplativa abundat *caritate;* in cuius rei signum Exodi decimo nono dicitur: « Totus mons Sinai fumabat, eo quod descendisset Dominus Deus super eum in igne ». — Est etiam mons locus *refugii;* unde Genesis decimo nono[^p232-8]: « In monte salvum te fac »; et Matthaei vigesimo quarto dicitur, imminente persecutione: « Tunc qui in Iudaea sunt fugiant ad montes ». Ex quo intelligitur, quod contemplativa abundat *defensione;* unde in persona viri contemplativi dicitur in Psalmo: « Levavi oculos meos in montes, unde veniet auxilium mihi ». — Est etiam mons locus *silentii;* unde dicitur tertii Regum decimo nono[^p232-9], quod « Elias pervenit usque ad montem Dei Horeb » et « mansit in spelunca »; et de Christo, Matthaei decimo quarto, quod « ascendit in montem solus orare ». Ex quo intelligitur, quod contemplativa abundat *tranquillitate;* unde de contemplativis dicitur in Psalmo: « Suscipiant montes pacem populo et colles iustitiam ». — Est etiam mons locus *pascuae,* iuxta illud Iob trigesimo nono[^p232-10]: « Circumspicit montes pascuae suae et virentia »; Ezechielis trigesimo quarto: « In pascuis uberrimis pascam eos, in montibus excelsis Israel erunt pascua eorum ». Ex quo intelligitur, quod contemplativa abundat *suavitate;* unde Ioelis ultimo de contemplatione: « In die illa stillabunt montes dulcedinem, et colles fluent lac et mel ». — Est postremo mons locus *eminentiae,* secundum illud Isaiae secundo[^p232-11]: « Erit praeparatus mons domus Domini in vertice montium »; et de Elia dicitur tertii Regum decimo octavo, quod « ascendit Elias in verticem montis »; et sequitur post, quod vidit nubeculam ascendentem de mari. Ex quo intelligimus, quod vita contemplativa superabundat *sursum-agilitate;* in cuius rei signum Dominus de monte Oliveti ascendit in caelum[^p232-12]; et de anima sancta dicitur in figura Annae Tobiae undecimo, quod « sedebat in supercilio montis »,
<!-- page 233 -->
quia in persona talium dicitur: « Nostra conversatio in caelis est », ad Philippenses tertio. — Ex his colligi potest excellentia vitae contemplativae, et quantum sit desideranda. Est enim plena *discretione,* plena *devotione,* plena *caritate,* plena *securitate,* plena *tranquillitate,* plena *suavitate,* plena *agilitate.*

51. (Vers. 29.). *Et facta est, dum oraret* etc. Post antecedentia ad transfigurationem subiungit hic Evangelista *concomitantia*[^p233-1]. Haec autem fuerunt tria, scilicet *fulgor aspectus, candor vestitus, honor comitatus;* ut sic appareat Christus gloriosus et intra se et circa se et iuxta se, ne quid desit gloriae suae.

Primo igitur quantum ad *fulgorem aspectus* dicitur: *Et facta est, dum oraret, species vultus eius altera;* alterata quidem est per supereminentem claritatis refulgentiam; unde Matthaei decimo septimo[^p233-2] dicitur, quod « transfiguratus est ante eos, et resplenduit facies eius sicut sol ». Nec mirum, quia in hoc ostendit gloriam resurrectionis, de qua Matthaei decimo tertio: « Tunc fulgebunt iusti sicut sol in regno Patris eorum ». Et ideo in claritatis huius ostensione ostendit discipulis regnum Dei, quia *claritas* est praecipua dos resurgentium, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p233-3]: « Alia gloria terrestrium, alia caelestium, alia claritas solis, alia claritas lunae, alia stellarum; stella enim differt a stella in claritate, sic et resurrectio mortuorum ». — Hanc autem *claritatis* dotem ad tempus Christus assumsit, sicut dicitur de *subtilitate,* quam assumsit in nativitate, cum clauso utero matris exivit; *agilitatem,* cum super aquas ambulavit; *impassibilitatem,* cum corpus in Sacramento discipulis tradidit[^p233-4]. Sic et *claritatem* hic ad tempus assumsit, propriam tamen effigiem non amisit, sed fuit in eo propria effigies et qualitas consummata sicut *qualitas passibilis,* claritas autem sicut *passio*[^p233-5]. Quemadmodum enim pallidus rubescit ad tempus prae verecundia, et fit in eo alteratio ad tempus; sic superna et mira potentia corpus terrestre effectum est ad tempus fulgidum et caeleste. Sive autem fuerit in eo alia dos claritatis, sive non; alia tamen fuit claritas et illi claritati supernae conformis, quae discipulorum oculos divina virtute non corrupit, sed potius confortavit, ita ut possent dicere illud Ecclesiastae undecimo[^p233-6]: « Dulce lumen et delectabile oculis videre solem ».

52. Secundo vero quantum ad *candorem vestitus* subditur: *Et vestitus eius albus et refulgens,* scilicet factus est. Tanta enim erat albedo, ut prae candore nimio refulgeret, secundum quod dicitur Marci nono[^p233-7]: « Vestimenta eius facta sunt splendida nimis et candida velut nix, et qualia fullo super terram non potest candida facere ». Nec mirum; hoc enim designavit candorem, qui apparebit in Sanctis, secundum illud Apocalypsis tertio[^p233-8]: « Qui vicerit sic vestietur vestimentis albis »; et septimo: « Hi qui amicti sunt stolis albis, qui sunt? et unde venerunt » etc.

53. (Vers. 30. 31.). Tertio quantum ad *honorem comitatus* subiungit: *Et ecce, duo viri loquebantur cum eo*[^p233-9]. Quia non decebat, Dominum gloriae videri solum, sed habere honorabilem comitatum; ideo quantum ad illum subiungit: *Erant autem Moyses et Elias, visi in maiestate.* Hi autem duo erant personae honorandae: *Moyses,* inquam, propter *legislationem,* secundum illud Numerorum duodecimo[^p233-10]: « At non talis servus meus Moyses, qui in omni domo mea fidelissimus est; ore enim ad os loquor ei, et palam et non per aenigmata et figuras Dominum videt »; *Elias* vero propter *excellentiam prophetiae* cum *miraculorum operatione.* Unde Ecclesiasticus quadragesimo octavo[^p233-11] loquens ad Eliam: « Quis potest tibi similiter gloriari? Qui sustulisti mortuum ab inferis de sorte mortis in verbo Domini Dei ». Unde et propter hanc excellentiam ostendendam Dominus speciali modo apparuit utrisque in figura in monte Horeb: Moysi quidem primo, sicut dicitur Exodi tertio[^p233-12], Eliae autem postea, sicut dicitur tertii Regum decimo nono, in eodem scilicet monte Horeb. — Propter igitur eorum honestatem et praecipuam dignitatem apparuit in comitatu eorum; aut certe, quia unus eorum erat mortuus, alter vivus, unus de caelo, alter de inferno[^p233-13], ut habeat
<!-- page 234 -->
testimonium ex omni genere, et ostendatur, quod ipse est qui vivos et mortuos faciet gloriosos. Unde in Glossa[^p234-1] dicitur: « Ideo vivus et mortuus apparent, ut significent Christum moriturum et iterum victurum, et ut nos vivi simus Deo, mortui mundo ». — Vel ideo isti duo apparent, ut ostendatur, quod Lex et Prophetae concordabant Domino Iesu Christo; unde in Glossa Bedae[^p234-2]: « Moyses legislator et Elias Prophetarum eximius apparent cum Verbo, ut ostendant, ipsum esse qui locutus est in Lege et Prophetis, et quem Lex et Prophetae promiserunt », ut sic verificetur illud Matthaei vigesimo primo: « Qui praeibant et qui sequebantur clamabant: Hosanna filio David ».

54. (Vers. 31.). Et quoniam Lex et Prophetae non tantum attestantur gloriae Christi, sed etiam *passioni,* quae est via in gloriam; ideo subditur: *Et dicebant excessum eius, quem completurus erat in Ierusalem. Excessus* recte nominat passionem, quia in ea fuit excessus *humilitatis,* secundum illud ad Philippenses secundo[^p234-3]: « Semetipsum exinanivit, formam servi accipiens »; et post: « Humiliavit semetipsum » etc. Fuit etiam excessus *paupertatis,* secundum illud Threnorum tertio[^p234-4]: « Recordare paupertatis et transgressionis meae, absinthii et fellis ». Fuit excessus *doloris,* secundum illud Threnorum primo: « O vos omnes! qui transitis per viam » etc. Fuit etiam excessus *amoris,* secundum illud ad Ephesios secundo[^p234-5]: « Deus, qui dives est in misericordia, propter nimiam caritatem suam, cum essemus mortui peccatis » etc.; et ad Romanos octavo: « Qui proprio Filio non pepercit, sed pro nobis omnibus tradidit illum, quomodo non omnia nobis cum illo donavit »? — Istum excessum complevit in Ierusalem, ubi crucifixus est, in quo fuit consummatio nostrae redemptionis, secundum illud ad Hebraeos secundo[^p234-6]: « Decebat eum, propter quem omnia et per quem omnia, qui multos filios in gloriam adduxerat, auctorem salutis eorum per passionem consummare ». Unde et ipse praedixit discipulis suis Lucae decimo octavo: « Ecce, ascendimus Ierosolymam, et consummabuntur omnia » etc.

55. Secundum autem *spiritualem* intelligentiam in oratione et contemplatione *facies hominis illustratur,* quia, dum facies mentis nostrae ad Deum convertitur, illuminatur, melioratur et perficitur. Quod figuratum fuit in Exodi trigesimo quarto[^p234-7], ubi dicitur, quod « cum descendisset Moyses de monte, apparuit facies eius cornuta ex consortio sermonis Domini »; unde et secundae ad Corinthios tertio: « Nos autem revelata facie gloriam Domini speculantes, transformamur in eandem imaginem a claritate in claritatem tanquam a Domini spiritu ». — Non solum hoc, sed et *vestitus candescit,* quia conversatio efficitur irreprehensibilis, secundum illud ad Philippenses secundo[^p234-8]: « Sitis sinceres, sine querela in medio nationis pravae et perversae, inter quos lucetis sicut luminaria in mundo ». Nec mirum, quia tales conformantur Domino Iesu Christo, secundum illud ad Romanos decimo tertio[^p234-9]: « Induamur arma lucis, sicut in die honeste ambulemus »; et paulo post: « Non in commessationibus et ebrietatibus, sed induimini Dominum Iesum Christum ». — Non solum hoc, sed etiam *Moyses et Elias colloquuntur,* quia in oratione datur homini intelligentia Scripturarum et mysteriorum propositorum, secundum illud Danielis secundo[^p234-10]: « Ingressus est Daniel domum suam sociisque suis indicavit, ut peterent misericordiam a Deo caeli super sacramento isto. Tunc Danieli nocte mysterium revelatum est »; et Sapientiae septimo: « Optavi, et datus est mihi sensus; invocavi, et venit in me spiritus sapientiae ». Ideo autem Moyses et Elias loquuntur cum Domino transfigurato ad certitudinem visionis, quia, ut dicit Richardus[^p234-11], « nulli revelationi omnino adhaerendum est, nisi per novum vel vetus valeat confirmari testamentum ».

## English
<!-- page 230 -->
43. *And it came to pass after these words.* After the exhortation to the asperity of the way the Evangelist subjoins *the showing of the sweetness of glory*[^p230-2]. Now he proceeds in this order. For first he describes *the revelation of the glory promised;* but secondly, *the consideration of the glory revealed,* at[^p230-3]: *But Peter and they that were with him;* but thirdly, *the confirmation of the revelation shown,* at: *And it came to pass the day following,* and this indeed he does through the wonderful cure of the demoniac.

Now *the revelation of the future glory* the Evangelist describes in two ways: first, as to the antecedents; secondly, as to the concomitants, at[^p230-4]: *And it came to pass, whilst he prayed* etc. — Now it is to be known that as to the transfiguration of Christ there were three antecedents or disposing things, namely *the exigency of the term, the choosing of the fellowship* and *the eminence of the place.* And these are the three things that God observes in communicating divine revelations.

44. (Vers. 28.). First, then, as to *the exigency of the term* he says: *And it came to pass after these words about eight days.* For since the Lord had promised that he would show his glory to the disciples, and « the Lord delayeth not his promise », as is said in the second of Peter, the third chapter[^p230-5]; therefore between the promise and the fulfilment there ought to intervene only a short space of time, namely that of one week. A figure of this went before in the espousal of Rachel, of whom Laban said to Jacob[^p230-6]: « Make up the week of days of this match: and I will give thee her also ». — And he notes this when he says, *it came to pass about eight days,* because he did this on the eighth day, the order of the week having come round, which are not eight complete days on account of the want of part of the first and of the last day; and in Matthew, the seventeenth chapter[^p230-7], and in Mark, the ninth, the first and the last days are not counted, but the whole ones in between. For there it is said: « It came to pass after six days », in which there is no contrariety, but one statement explains the other. And the Holy Spirit willed to express it in both ways for the intimating of a mystery. For since God made the world in six days and after the sixth day rested[^p230-8], therefore for six days one was to labour and afterwards to be free for contemplation[^tr-vacare]. Therefore, to intimate this, Matthew says: « After six days ». But since perfect and highest rest and contemplation is not before the eighth, namely the resurrection; therefore he says here: eight days. And this is what is said in the Gloss[^p230-9] from Bede and Ambrose: After these words etc.

45. Secondly, as to *the choosing of the fellowship* it is said: *And he took Peter, John and James.* These three he took as more intimate and familiar, as he took them, above in the eighth chapter[^p230-10], at the raising of the girl. Therefore he takes *three* for the commending of the mystery of the Trinity and for the firmness of the testimony; whence it is said in the Gloss: « That by three fit witnesses the whole world might believe ». And he specially chooses *these three* on account of the *eminence* that he considered in them: because *Peter* was to be the first in governing the Church, as Matthew says in the sixteenth chapter[^p230-11]: « Thou art Peter » etc. « And I will give to thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven ». And *James* was to be the first among the Apostles to be slain for the multiplying of the Church; in Acts, the twelfth chapter, it is said of Herod that « he killed James, the brother of John, with the sword ». *John,* the chief one in keeping the Mother; in John, the nineteenth chapter: « Jesus seeing his Mother and the disciple standing » etc. — Or he takes these three on account of *the instruction of a figure.* By these is designated the threefold state in the Church of those to be saved, namely *of the prelate* in Peter, *of the active* in James, *of the contemplative* in John, as in Noe, Daniel and Job[^p230-12]; or in Peter *the married,* in James *the continent,* in John *virgins,* according to the threefold continence, to which is due the thirtyfold, sixtyfold and hundredfold fruit[^p230-13]. For of Peter's wife we read, of John's virginity, of James neither is said expressly.
<!-- page 231 -->

46. But thirdly, as to *the eminence of the place* he subjoins: *And he went up into a mountain to pray. A mountain* is indeed a place eminent and stretched out from earthly things to heavenly, and therefore fit for contemplation and prayer. Such was this mountain; whence it is said in Matthew, the seventeenth chapter[^p231-1], that « he led them into a high mountain apart », that is, that they might be set apart from the crowd and from earthly things. And such are apt for divine revelations; whence it is said in Exodus, the third chapter[^p231-2], of Moses that « when he had driven the flock to the inner parts of the desert, he came to the mountain of God, Horeb », where, that is, the Lord appeared in the fire of the bush. Likewise in the receiving of the Law; Exodus, the twenty-fourth chapter: « The Lord said to Moses: Come up to me into the mount, and be there, and I will give thee two tables of stone ». Likewise in the marking out of the land of promise, according to that of Deuteronomy, the last chapter: « Moses went up from the plains of Moab upon mount Nebo, to the top of Phasga, over against Jericho, and the Lord shewed him all the land of Galaad as far as Dan ». — From these, then, it is gathered that a mountain is fit for divine apparitions, instructions and contemplations. Whence Bede in the Gloss[^p231-3]: « Being about to pray he goes up into a mountain, that he may intimate that they who await the resurrection ought to raise the mind to high things and to persist in continual prayers », and this on a mountain on account of its eminence, according to that of Deuteronomy, the thirty-third chapter: « The Lord came from Sinai, and from Seir he rose up to us; he hath appeared from mount Pharan, and with him thousands of saints. In his right hand a fiery law ». « And they that approach to his feet shall receive of his doctrine »; and so there appear three things disposing to the divine transfiguration even according to the *literal* intelligence.

47. But *spiritually* in these three there is a very great formation for those who wish to attain to seeing the Lord's transfiguration through the transport of contemplation[^tr-excessus]. For in the *number* of the *days* there are given to be understood the *steps* of contemplations succeeding one another, as day succeeds day. And according to Matthew[^p231-4] *six days* are numbered to intimate *six steps* of contemplation, which Richard expresses in the book on the Ark, or on Contemplation[^p231-5], saying thus in the first book, the sixth chapter: « There are six kinds of contemplation, wholly divided from one another and among themselves. The first is *in the imagination* and according to *the imagination alone;* the second is *in the imagination* according to *reason;* the third is *in reason* according to *the imagination;* the fourth is in *reason* according to *reason;* the fifth is *above reason,* but *not beside* reason; the sixth is *above reason* and seems to be *beside* reason. So two are in the imagination, two in reason, two in the intelligence ». — The first two are about *corporeal things,* the second two about *spiritual things,* the third two about *sempiternal things* and incomprehensible ones[^p231-6]. — And these six kinds are understood by the *six days* and by the *six steps* by which one went up to the throne of Solomon, according to that of the third of Kings, the tenth chapter[^p231-7], that « Solomon made himself a great throne of ivory, and overlaid it with exceeding yellow gold, which had six steps ». And the number of perfection agrees with this; for the senary is the first of the perfect numbers.

48. But according to Luke *eight days* are counted. For Luke treats of this apparition according as it expresses the *glory* after the resurrection; which is plain, because there[^p231-8] he touches upon *sleep* and *awakening;* therefore he intimates *eight steps,* of which seven are on the way, the eighth is reserved in the fatherland. Of these seven Augustine says in the book on the Quantity of the Soul, at the end[^p231-9]: « To those ascending upward, he says, let the first step, for the sake of teaching, be called *animation;* the second, *sense;* the third, *art;* the fourth, *virtue;* the fifth, *tranquillity;* the sixth, *ingress;* the seventh, *contemplation* »; after this there remains only *the blessed vision;* and so there are about eight days. And as he says[^p231-10], « the first is *of* the body, the second *through* the body, the third *about* the body, the fourth *toward itself,* the fifth *in itself,* the sixth *toward God,* the seventh *with God* ». And these steps do not only name steps of contemplation, but those by which one comes to contemplation.

But a certain one, in simple speech indeed, but not in knowledge, Brother Giles, who it has been proven was very frequently rapt, distinguishes the steps thus, saying[^p231-11]: There are seven steps of contemplation: the first is *fire;* the second is *unction;* the third is *ecstasy;* the fourth, *contemplation;* the fifth, *taste;* the sixth, *rest;* the seventh, *glory;* after which there remains only *everlasting felicity.* — By these seven days, then, one arrives
<!-- page 232 -->
at the eighth day of glory; which the Holy Spirit willed to designate in the assigning of the time.

49. But by the *three persons taken* there are given to be understood *three virtues* necessary for contemplating, namely *faith, hope, charity; faith,* by Peter, who is interpreted acknowledging; *hope,* by James the wrestler; by John, in whom is grace, *charity*[^p232-1]. And without these wings no one can mount up to the heights. — Or by the three persons we understand *three dispositions* necessary to the contemplative soul, namely *industry, discipline* and *grace*[^p232-2]: the first in Peter, the second in James, the third in John. For it is needful that *the intelligence* be illumined, *concupiscence* be tamed and *the affection* be inflamed, that we may be borne upward with the Lord. If any of these is wanting, the business of contemplation perishes. — Or by these three persons are understood *three modes of ascending* to the summit of contemplation. For in one mode one ascends by the way *of splendour,* and this mode of ascending blessed Augustine teaches[^p232-3], and this is designated by Peter. In another mode one ascends by the way *of sorrow and groaning,* and this in James, and this mode is common to those who pray. In the third mode by the way *of love,* and this Dionysius teaches[^p232-4], and this is designated by John; and this is the chief among them all, the one in which grace most moves; but in the first industry or knowledge avails much; but the second mode is as it were mixed between grace and natural and acquired industry.

50. But by the *place* into which he went up there can rightly be understood the eminence of the *contemplative* life. For a mountain is the place *of the teaching office;* whence it is said in Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p232-5], that « Jesus went up into a mountain, and when he was set down » etc. And from this it is given us to understand that the contemplative life abounds in *cognition,* and therefore in the person of the contemplatives Isaias says, in the second chapter: « Come, let us go up to the mountain of the Lord, and to the house of the God of Jacob, and he will teach us » etc.; whence also it is said of Moses in Exodus, the twenty-fourth chapter, that « he went up into the mountain, and was there forty days and forty nights », and then received the Law. — A mountain is also the place *of sacrifice,* according to that of Genesis, the twenty-second chapter[^p232-6]: « Take thy only begotten son, whom thou lovest, Isaac, and offer him to me upon one of the mountains which I will shew thee »; and Exodus, the third chapter: « When thou shalt have brought the people out of Egypt, thou shalt offer sacrifice to God upon this mountain ». From which it is gathered that the contemplative life abounds in *devotion;* whence the contemplative soul in the Canticle, the fourth chapter: « I will go to the mountain of myrrh, and to the hill of frankincense » etc. — A mountain is also the place *of burning* on account of the rays of the sun; whence Ecclesiasticus, the forty-third chapter[^p232-7]: « The sun three times as much burneth the mountains, breathing out fiery rays »; and this on account of much refulgence, according to that of the first of Machabees, the sixth chapter: « The sun shone upon the shields of gold and of brass, and the mountains glittered therewith like lamps of fire ». From which it is given to understand that the contemplative life abounds in *charity;* in sign of which thing it is said in Exodus, the nineteenth chapter: « All mount Sinai was on a smoke, because the Lord God was come down upon it in fire ». — A mountain is also the place *of refuge;* whence Genesis, the nineteenth chapter[^p232-8]: « In the mountain save thyself »; and in Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter, it is said, when persecution is imminent: « Then they that are in Judea, let them flee to the mountains ». From which it is understood that the contemplative life abounds in *defence;* whence in the person of the contemplative man it is said in the Psalm: « I have lifted up my eyes to the mountains, from whence help shall come to me ». — A mountain is also the place *of silence;* whence it is said in the third of Kings, the nineteenth chapter[^p232-9], that « Elias came unto the mount of God, Horeb » and « abode in a cave »; and of Christ, in Matthew, the fourteenth chapter, that « he went up into a mountain alone to pray ». From which it is understood that the contemplative life abounds in *tranquillity;* whence it is said of the contemplatives in the Psalm: « Let the mountains receive peace for the people, and the hills justice ». — A mountain is also the place *of pasture,* according to that of Job, the thirty-ninth chapter[^p232-10]: « He looketh round about the mountains of his pasture, and the green things »; Ezechiel, the thirty-fourth chapter: « I will feed them in the most fruitful pastures; in the high mountains of Israel shall be their pastures ». From which it is understood that the contemplative life abounds in *sweetness;* whence Joel, the last chapter, of contemplation: « In that day the mountains shall drop down sweetness, and the hills shall flow with milk and honey ». — Lastly, a mountain is the place *of eminence,* according to that of Isaias, the second chapter[^p232-11]: « The mountain of the house of the Lord shall be prepared on the top of mountains »; and of Elias it is said in the third of Kings, the eighteenth chapter, that « Elias went up to the top of the mountain »; and it follows after that he saw a little cloud arising out of the sea. From which we understand that the contemplative life superabounds in *upward-agility;* in sign of which thing the Lord ascended into heaven from mount Olivet[^p232-12]; and of the holy soul it is said in the figure of Anna, in Tobias, the eleventh chapter, that « she sat on the top of a hill »,
<!-- page 233 -->
because in the person of such it is said: « Our conversation is in heaven », in Philippians, the third chapter. — From these can be gathered the excellence of the contemplative life, and how much it is to be desired. For it is full of *discretion,* full of *devotion,* full of *charity,* full of *security,* full of *tranquillity,* full of *sweetness,* full of *agility.*

51. (Vers. 29.). *And it came to pass, whilst he prayed* etc. After the antecedents to the transfiguration the Evangelist here subjoins the *concomitants*[^p233-1]. And these were three, namely *the splendour of the countenance, the whiteness of the raiment, the honour of the company;* so that Christ might appear glorious both within himself and about himself and beside himself, lest anything be wanting to his glory.

First, then, as to *the splendour of the countenance* it is said: *And it came to pass, whilst he prayed, the shape of his countenance became other;* altered it was indeed through the supereminent refulgence of brightness; whence it is said in Matthew, the seventeenth chapter[^p233-2], that « he was transfigured before them, and his face did shine as the sun ». Nor is it a wonder, because in this he showed the glory of the resurrection, of which in Matthew, the thirteenth chapter: « Then shall the just shine as the sun in the kingdom of their Father ». And therefore in the showing of this brightness he showed the disciples the kingdom of God, because *brightness* is the chief dowry of those who rise again, according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter[^p233-3]: « One is the glory of the terrestrial, another of the celestial; one is the brightness of the sun, another the brightness of the moon, another of the stars; for star differeth from star in brightness, so also is the resurrection of the dead ». — Now this dowry of *brightness* Christ took on for a time, as is said of *subtlety,* which he took on in the nativity, when he went out from his mother's closed womb; *agility,* when he walked upon the waters; *impassibility,* when he gave his body to the disciples in the Sacrament[^p233-4]. So he also took on *brightness* here for a time, yet did not lose his own likeness, but there was in him his own likeness and quality consummated as *a passible quality,* but the brightness as *a passion*[^p233-5]. For as a pale man reddens for a time for shamefastness, and an alteration comes about in him for a time; so by a supernal and wonderful power the earthly body was made for a time shining and heavenly. And whether there was in him another dowry of brightness or not, yet there was another brightness, and one conformed to that supernal brightness, which did not corrupt the disciples' eyes by divine power, but rather strengthened them, so that they could say that of Ecclesiastes, the eleventh chapter[^p233-6]: « The light is sweet, and delightful it is for the eyes to see the sun ».

52. But secondly, as to *the whiteness of the raiment* it is subjoined: *And his raiment white and glittering,* that is, was made. For so great was the whiteness that for exceeding whiteness it glittered, according to what is said in Mark, the ninth chapter[^p233-7]: « His garments became exceeding shining and white as snow, and such as no fuller upon earth can make white ». Nor is it a wonder; for this designated the whiteness that will appear in the Saints, according to that of the Apocalypse, the third chapter[^p233-8]: « He that shall overcome shall thus be clothed in white garments »; and in the seventh: « These that are clothed in white robes, who are they? and whence came they » etc.

53. (Vers. 30. 31.). Thirdly, as to *the honour of the company* he subjoins: *And behold, two men were talking with him*[^p233-9]. Because it was not fitting that the Lord of glory should be seen alone, but that he should have an honourable company; therefore as to that he subjoins: *And they were Moses and Elias, appearing in majesty.* Now these two were persons to be honoured: *Moses,* I say, on account of *the legislation,* according to that of Numbers, the twelfth chapter[^p233-10]: « But it is not so with my servant Moses, who is most faithful in all my house; for I speak to him mouth to mouth, and plainly, and not by riddles and figures doth he see the Lord »; but *Elias* on account of *the excellence of prophecy* with *the working of miracles.* Whence Ecclesiasticus, the forty-eighth chapter[^p233-11], speaking to Elias: « Who can glory like to thee? Who raisedst up a dead man from below, from the lot of death, by the word of the Lord God ». Whence also, to show this excellence, the Lord appeared in a special mode to both of them in figure on mount Horeb: to Moses indeed first, as is said in Exodus, the third chapter[^p233-12], but to Elias afterwards, as is said in the third of Kings, the nineteenth chapter, on the same mount Horeb, that is. — On account, then, of their honesty and chief dignity he appeared in their company; or certainly because one of them was dead, the other living, one from heaven, the other from hell[^p233-13], that he might have
<!-- page 234 -->
testimony from every kind, and it might be shown that he is the one who will make the living and the dead glorious. Whence it is said in the Gloss[^p234-1]: « Therefore a living man and a dead one appear, that they may signify Christ about to die and to live again, and that we may be living to God, dead to the world ». — Or these two appear so that it may be shown that the Law and the Prophets were in concord with the Lord Jesus Christ; whence in the Gloss of Bede[^p234-2]: « Moses the lawgiver and Elias the most eminent of the Prophets appear with the Word, that they may show that he is the one who spoke in the Law and the Prophets, and whom the Law and the Prophets promised », so that thus that of Matthew, the twenty-first chapter, might be verified: « They that went before and they that followed cried: Hosanna to the son of David ».

54. (Vers. 31.). And since the Law and the Prophets bear witness not only to the glory of Christ, but also to the *passion,* which is the way into glory; therefore it is subjoined: *And they spoke of his excess, which he was to accomplish in Jerusalem. Excess* rightly names the passion, because in it there was the excess *of humility,* according to that of Philippians, the second chapter[^p234-3]: « He emptied himself, taking the form of a servant »; and after: « He humbled himself » etc. There was also the excess *of poverty,* according to that of Lamentations, the third chapter[^p234-4]: « Remember my poverty and transgression, the wormwood and the gall ». There was the excess *of sorrow,* according to that of Lamentations, the first chapter: « O all ye that pass by the way » etc. There was also the excess *of love,* according to that of Ephesians, the second chapter[^p234-5]: « God, who is rich in mercy, for his exceeding charity, when we were dead in sins » etc.; and Romans, the eighth chapter: « He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how hath he not also with him given us all things »? — This excess he accomplished in Jerusalem, where he was crucified, in which was the consummation of our redemption, according to that of Hebrews, the second chapter[^p234-6]: « It became him, for whom are all things and by whom are all things, who had brought many children into glory, to perfect the author of their salvation by his passion ». Whence also he himself foretold to his disciples, in Luke, the eighteenth chapter: « Behold, we go up to Jerusalem, and all things shall be accomplished » etc.

55. But according to the *spiritual* intelligence, in prayer and contemplation *the face of man is lit up,* because, while the face of our mind is turned to God, it is illumined, bettered and perfected. Which was figured in Exodus, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p234-7], where it is said that « when Moses had come down from the mount, his face appeared horned from the fellowship of the Lord's speech »; whence also the second to the Corinthians, the third chapter: « But we, beholding the glory of the Lord with open face, are transformed into the same image from glory to glory, as by the Spirit of the Lord ». — Not only this, but *the raiment* also *grows white,* because the conversation becomes blameless, according to that of Philippians, the second chapter[^p234-8]: « That you may be sincere, without reproof in the midst of a crooked and perverse generation, among whom you shine as lights in the world ». Nor is it a wonder, because such are conformed to the Lord Jesus Christ, according to that of Romans, the thirteenth chapter[^p234-9]: « Let us put on the armour of light; let us walk honestly, as in the day »; and a little after: « Not in rioting and drunkenness, but put ye on the Lord Jesus Christ ». — Not only this, but also *Moses and Elias converse,* because in prayer there is given to man the intelligence of the Scriptures and of the mysteries set forth, according to that of Daniel, the second chapter[^p234-10]: « Daniel went into his house and told it to his companions, that they should ask mercy of the God of heaven concerning this secret. Then the mystery was revealed to Daniel by night »; and Wisdom, the seventh chapter: « I wished, and understanding was given me; I called, and the spirit of wisdom came into me ». And Moses and Elias speak with the transfigured Lord for the certitude of the vision, because, as Richard says[^p234-11], « to no revelation at all is one to adhere, unless it can be confirmed by the new or the old testament ».

[^tr-vacare]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin *vacare* holds together several senses that English has to split: being at leisure, being free, being free *for* something and being free *from* something. Here (¶ 44) the six days of the world's making are followed by God's rest, *ideo sex diebus erat laborandum et post contemplationi vacandum,* "therefore for six days one was to labour and afterwards to be free for contemplation", and the next sentence names the end of the series *perfecta et summa quies,* "perfect and highest rest". [^tr-excessus]: **En.** Translator's note: *excessus* is one Latin word with two senses in this chunk, and the English gives it two words.
