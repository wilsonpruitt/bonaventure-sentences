# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. VIII, Vers. 34-39
## *Quod ut viderunt factum etc. Hic iam tertio ostendit Evangelista reverentiam populi occurrentis*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 205 -->
60. (Vers. 34.). *Quod ut viderunt factum* etc. Hic iam tertio[^p205-7] ostendit Evangelista *reverentiam populi occurrentis.* Circa cuius explicationem introducuntur quatuor, scilicet *denuntiatio veritatis conspectae, indagatio veritatis auditae, inventio veritatis inquisitae, veneratio veritatis inventae, quaesitae et denuntiatae.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *denuntiationem veritatis conspectae* dicitur: *Quod ut viderunt factum, qui pascebant fugerunt,* scilicet ex timore sicut mercenarii; Ioannis decimo[^p205-8]: « Mercenarius autem, et qui non est pastor, videt lupum venientem et fugit »; et Zachariae undecimo: « O pastor et idolum derelinquens gregem »! Vel fugerunt non timore humani periculi, sed timore divini iudicii; de quo Iob decimo nono[^p205-9]: « Fugite a facie gladii, quia ultor iniquitatis est gladius, et scitote, esse iudicium ». Fugerunt autem, non ut laterent, quia in Psalmo scriptum est: « Quo ibo a spiritu tuo? et quo a facie tua fugiam »? sed ut absentibus celeriter visa narrarent. — Unde infertur: *Et nuntiaverunt in civitatem et in villas,* sicut et ille nuntius, Iob primo[^p205-10]: « Evasi ego solus, ut nuntiarem tibi ». Et in hoc quasi habebant praedicatorum officium, de quibus in Psalmo: « Annuntiaverunt opera Dei et facta eius intellexerunt »; ad Philippenses primo: « Sive per occasionem annuntietur Christus, et in hoc gaudeo, sed et gaudebo ». Unde *bona* de Christo visa non sunt reticenda, sed annuntianda, secundum illud quarti Regum septimo[^p205-11]: « Dixerunt illi quatuor viri leprosi: Haec dies boni nuntii est; si tacuerimus et noluerimus nuntiare usque
<!-- page 206 -->
mane, sceleris arguemur. Venite, eamus et nuntiemus in aula regis ». Sed *mala* non sunt nuntianda; secundi Regum primo: « Nolite annuntiare in Geth neque annuntietis in compitis Ascalonis ».

61. (Vers. 35.). Secundo vero quantum ad *indagationem veritatis auditae* subditur: *Exierunt autem videre quod factum est, et venerunt ad Iesum. Exierunt,* inquam, ad inquisitionem; Canticorum tertio[^p206-1]: « Egredimini, filiae Sion, et videte regem Salomonem »; et in Psalmo: « Venite et videte opera Domini, quae posuit prodigia super terram ». — Exierunt autem *ad Iesum,* qui est Veritas[^p206-2], ut discerent veritatem. Nam, sicut dicit sollicitus inquisitor Iob vigesimo tertio: « Quis mihi tribuat, ut cognoscam et inveniam illum et veniam usque ad solium eius »? Sic Samaritani, audito verbo mulieris, Ioannis quarto: « Abiit mulier in civitatem et dicit hominibus illis: Venite et videte hominem, qui dixit mihi omnia, quaecumque feci ». « Exierunt ergo de civitate et veniebant ad eum ».

Et notandum est valde, quod dicitur, quod dum *exeunt videre quod factum est, venerunt ad Iesum,* quia factura, dum videtur, ducit ad Factorem; ad Romanos primo[^p206-3]: « Invisibilia Dei a creatura mundi, per ea quae facta sunt, intellecta conspiciuntur »; et Sapientiae decimo tertio: « A magnitudine speciei et creaturae cognoscibiliter poterit Creator horum videri ». Et ideo Ecclesiastici quadragesimo secundo: « Quam admirabilia opera illius, et tanquam scintilla, quae est considerare »! In hoc autem apparet, quod non est standum in factura, sed perveniendum usque ad Iesum; Ecclesiastici quadragesimo tertio[^p206-4]: « Vide arcum et benedic qui fecit illum »; et in Psalmo: « Ex ore infantium et lactentium perfecisti laudem » etc.

62. Tertio quantum ad *inventionem veritatis quaesitae* subditur: *Et invenerunt hominem sedentem, a quo daemonia exierant;* et ita in *debito statu* contra inquietationem, quia nunquam quiescebat, quando diabolus eum possidebat; Isaiae quinquagesimo septimo[^p206-5]: « Impii quasi mare fervens, quod quiescere non potest ». Econtra de eo, in quo inhabitat Spiritus Domini, dicitur Threnorum tertio: « Sedebit solitarius et tacebit »; quia, secundum illud Psalmi, « in pace factus est locus eius ». — Invenerunt etiam eum in *debito habitu* contra nudationem; propter quod addit: *Vestitum.* Talis
enim habitus decet hominem tam corporaliter quam spiritualiter; Apocalypsis decimo sexto[^p206-6]: « Beatus, qui custodit vestimenta sua, ne nudus ambulet ». — Invenerunt etiam eum in *debito sensu* contra insanitionem; propter quod subdit: *Ac sana mente;* unde poterat dicere illud Nabuchodonosor Danielis quarto[^p206-7]: « In ipso tempore sensus meus reversus est ad me ». Quem sensum aufert diabolus eis quos possidet, infatuando eorum mentes; secundae ad Corinthios quarto: « In quibus Deus huius saeculi excaecavit mentes infidelium ». — Invenerunt eum etiam in *debito situ* contra deordinationem; propter quod adiungitur: *Ad pedes eius,* tanquam intentum praedicationi eius, secundum illud Deuteronomii penultimo[^p206-8]: « Qui appropinquant pedibus eius accipient de doctrina illius »; et infra decimo: « Maria sedens secus pedes Domini, audiebat verbum illius ». — Et per haec indicia manifeste cognoverunt ipsum liberatum *ex proprio visu.* — Modum autem curationis et causam cognoverunt *per auditum,* quia audierant, hoc factum esse per Christum. Propter quod subditur: *Et timuerunt,* scilicet Christum, attendentes eius miram potentiam et fortitudinem, secundum illud Ieremiae decimo[^p206-9]: « Quis non timebit te, o rex gentium? Tuum est enim decus; inter cunctos sapientes gentium et in universis regnis eorum nullus est similis tui ».

63. (Vers. 36.). Ad hoc inducit certum testimonium, ideo subdit: *Nuntiaverunt autem illis hi*[^p206-10] *qui viderant,* scilicet pastores. Nam tales debent testificari; Ioannis decimo nono: « Et qui vidit testimonium perhibuit ». — *Quomodo sanus factus esset a legione,* per Christi imperium. Tali testimonio est credendum; primae Ioannis primo[^p206-11]: « Quod vidimus et audivimus et perspeximus testamur et annuntiamus vobis »; Psalmus: « Narrantes laudes Domini et mirabilia eius, quae fecit »; quia, Actuum quarto, « non possumus quae audivimus et vidimus non loqui ».

64. (Vers. 37.). Quarto vero quantum ad *venerationem veritatis inventae* subditur: *Rogaverunt eum*[^p206-12] *omnis multitudo regionis Gerasenorum.* Postquam cognoverunt Maiestatis imperium formidandum, rogaverunt eum, *ut discederet ab eis.* Quod quidem non dicebant contemptu superbiae, sicut illi, de quibus Iob vigesimo primo[^p206-13]: « Qui dicunt Deo: Recede a nobis; scientiam viarum tuarum nolumus »; sed
<!-- page 207 -->
ex timore reverentiae. Unde et subditur: *Quia timore magno tenebantur,* sicut Petrus, supra quinto[^p207-1]: « Exi a me, Domine, quia homo peccator sum »; et sicut centurio, supra septimo: « Domine, non sum dignus, ut intres sub tectum meum »; quia, secundum illud Ecclesiastici decimo tertio, « pondus super se tollet qui honestiori se communicat ». Unde Ambrosius[^p207-2]: « Infirma mens non capit Dei verbum nec potest pondus sapientiae sustinere ». Et quoniam ad divinam cohabitationem erant minus idonei, ideo fuerunt exauditi. — Propter quod additur: *Ipse autem, ascendens navim*[^p207-3]*, reversus est.* Nolebat Dominus esse aliquibus onerosus; unde Matthaei decimo: « Et quicumque non receperint vos, exeuntes foras, excutite pulverem de pedibus vestris »; et Matthaei septimo: « Nolite sanctum dare canibus neque spargere margaritas ante porcos ». Sed prudentius his fecerunt Samaritani, de quibus Ioannis quarto[^p207-4]: « Cum venissent ad eum Samaritani, rogaverunt eum, ut ibi maneret. Et mansit apud eos duos dies ». Sed stupor circumdederat istos, sicut et Petrum, supra quinto. Ideo poterant dicere illud Iob trigesimo primo: « Semper quasi tumentes fluctus super me timui Deum et pondus eius ferre non potui ».

In hoc etiam *spiritualiter* datur intelligi, quod Dominus repulsus abscedit; primi Paralipomenon vigesimo octavo[^p207-5]: « Si quaesieris eum, invenies; si autem dereliqueris eum, proiiciet te in aeternum ». — Insinuatur etiam, quod Dominus non relinquit nos, nisi prius eum repellamus, quin potius se ingerit; unde Apocalypsis tertio: « Ego sto ad ostium et pulso » etc.

65. (Vers. 38.). *Et rogabat illum* etc. Hic quarto describitur *benevolentia sanati obtemperantis,* quam commendat Evangelista ex quatuor, scilicet ex *oblatione obsequii,* ex *condescensione Medici,* ex *commissione officii* et ex *divulgatione beneficii,* ita quod unum ad alterum disponit.

Primo igitur quantum ad *oblationem obsequii* dicitur: *Et rogabat illum vir, a quo daemonia exierant,* scilicet sanatus medicum, *ut cum illo*[^p207-6] *esset,* ut scilicet ei serviret tanquam Domino et Sal-
vatori, sicut dicebat Ethai Gethaeus ad David secundi Regum decimo quinto: « Vivit Dominus, et vivit dominus meus rex, quia in quocumque loco fueris, sive in morte, sive in vita, ibi erit servus tuus ». Sic Eliseus ad Eliam, quarti Regum secundo: « Vivit Dominus, et vivit anima tua, quia non dimittam te ». Ideo autem volebat esse cum ipso, ut esset *securior,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici quinquagesimo primo[^p207-7]: « Invocabo Dominum patrem Domini mei, ut non derelinquat me in die tribulationis meae ». Ut etiam esset *perfectior;* Psalmus: « Iustificationes tuas custodiam, non me derelinquas usquequaque ». Ut etiam esset *felicior;* ad Philippenses primo[^p207-8]: « Cupio dissolvi et esse cum Christo »; et Ioannis duodecimo: « Volo, Pater, ut, ubi ego sum, ibi sit et minister meus ». — Ideo volebat cohabitare in via, ut cohabitaret in patria.

66. Secundo quantum ad *condescensionem Medici* subditur: *Dimisit autem eum Iesus,* in omnimoda scilicet libertate, nolens sibi aliud onus imponere, ostendens, quod propter collatum beneficium non est accipiendum obsequium; unde Matthaei decimo octavo[^p207-9]: « Misertus dominus servi illius dimisit eum et debitum dimisit ei ».

67. (Vers. 39.). Dimisit autem eum in quiete; unde subdit: *Dicens: Redi in domum tuam,* ut ibi requiescas, unde te daemonia expulerant; Ecclesiastici trigesimo secundo[^p207-10]: « Praecurre prior in domum tuam, et illic avocare et illic lude et age conceptiones tuas ». Seneca: « Indicium mentis bene constitutae est, in se posse consistere et secum posse morari ».

Et nota, quod Dominus quosdam *dimittit* propter *infirmitatem,* sicut istum cui condescendebat. Quosdam *admittit* propter *fidelitatem,* sicut ipsum, de quo infra nono[^p207-11]: « Dixit ad alterum: Sequere me. Ille autem dixit: Domine, sed dimitte me, ut vadam prius sepelire » etc. Quosdam *attrahit* propter *idoneitatem,* infra nono: « Et ait alter: Sequar te, Domine, sed permitte mihi primum renuntiare » etc. Quosdam *repellit* propter *cupiditatem,* sicut illum, de quo Matthaei octavo[^p207-12]: « Magister, sequar te »,
<!-- page 208 -->
Dominus respondet: « Vulpes foveas habent » etc. — Hunc autem non admisit Dominus ad perfectionis discipulatum, vel quia movebat eum timor humani periculi, vel amor beneficii; vel ut ostenderet, sicut praetactum est[^p208-1], quod propter spirituale beneficium non est accipiendum obsequium; unde Matthaei decimo: « Gratis accepistis, gratis date ». Et exemplum huius apparet in Eliseo, quarti Regum quinto. Potest tamen praedicator accipere stipendium, sicut dicitur primae ad Corinthios nono; sed non debet *praedicare* propter illud, sed propter salutem populi.

68. Et nota hic, quod Dominus, secundum quod historia evangelica narrat, aliquando praecipit curatis, quod nemini dicerent, sicut patet in curatione leprosi Matthaei octavo[^p208-2]: « Vide, nemini dixeris »; et in suscitatione puellae, infra eodem: « Quibus praecepit, ne cui dicerent quod factum erat ». Hic autem praecipit sanato, quod divulget ad hoc, ut Dominus ostendat, veritatem salutarem non esse tacendam, et gloriam propriam non esse quaerendam. Ideo aliquando praecipit, quod dicatur, et aliquando praecipit, quod taceatur, ut in primo det documentum, quod veritas praedicetur, in secundo vero, quod gloria propria non quaeratur[^p208-3]. — In hoc etiam apparet, quod praedicationis opus non tantum est virorum apostolicorum, verum etiam quorumcumque bonorum, qui a Christo edocti sunt. Unde etiam Christus, discipulis prohibentibus, quendam in nomine Christi eiicere daemonia, qui Christum non sequebatur, dixit: « Nolite prohibere », sicut dicitur infra nono[^p208-4]. Bonus enim vir debet dicere quod dixit Moyses Numerorum undecimo: « Quis det, ut omnes prophetent, et det eis Dominus Spiritum sanctum »? secundum illud ad Philippenses primo: « Sive per veritatem, sive per occasionem annuntietur Christus, et in hoc gaudeo, sed et gaudebo ».

69. Tertio quantum ad *commissionem officii* dicitur: *Et narra, quanta tibi fecerit*[^p208-5] *Deus;* in quo mittit eum ad praedicandum et divulgandum divinum beneficium; Psalmus: « Narrabo nomen tuum fratribus meis » etc.; et Tobiae duodecimo: « Sacramentum regis abscondere bonum est; opera autem Dei revelare et confiteri honorificum est ». Et hoc mandat Deus fieri; Psalmus: « Quanta mandavit patribus nostris, nota facere ea filiis suis ». Et illud est officium praedicatoribus iniunctum.

70. Quarto quantum ad *divulgationem beneficii* subditur: *Et abiit per universam civitatem dicens*[^p208-6], quod iniunctum est; Ionae tertio: « Surge et vade in Ninivem, civitatem magnam, et praedica in ea praedicationem ». Sed Ionas praedicabat periculum, iste vero divulgabat et praedicabat beneficium. — Propter quod addit: *Quanta illi fecisset Iesus,* ut vere diceret illud Psalmi[^p208-7]: « Venite, audite, omnes, et narrabo, quanta fecit animae meae ». Ad hoc invitabat Angelus Tobiae duodecimo: « Benedicite Deum et narrate omnia mirabilia eius ». Hoc autem dicit, non quia in nostra sit potestate omnia eius opera et beneficia enarrare, cum dicatur Ecclesiastici decimo octavo[^p208-8]: « Quis sufficit enarrare opera illius »? sed quia hoc debet esse in nostro desiderio et voluntate, iuxta illud Psalmi: « Confitebor tibi, Domine, in toto corde meo; narrabo omnia mirabilia tua »; non quia lingua sufficiat exprimere, sed quia gratitudo cordis de omnibus cupit laudes exsolvere et praedicare.

71. Nota hic, quia secundum litteram quatuor possunt ex dictis elici notabilia. Primum est, quod Dominus per viles et humiles personas voluit aliis notificari, sicut per porcarios et pecorarios. — Secundo, quod non omnes ad statum perfectionis[^p208-9] sunt admittendi, sed tantum illi qui sunt idonei. — Tertio, quod a praedicatione nominis Christi arcendi non sunt quos Christus curat a vexatione diaboli. — Quarto, quod ad gratiarum actionem pro beneficiis acceptis debemus esse promptissimi, exemplo huius daemoniaci, qui beneficium Dei omnibus nuntiavit.

## English
<!-- page 205 -->
60. (Vers. 34.). *Which when they saw done* etc. Here now, thirdly[^p205-7], the Evangelist shows *the reverence of the people running to meet him.* For the setting forth of which four things are brought in, namely *the report of the truth beheld, the searching out of the truth heard, the finding of the truth inquired after, the veneration of the truth found, sought and reported.*

First, then, as to *the report of the truth beheld,* it is said: *Which when they that fed them saw done, they fled,* namely out of fear, as hirelings; John, the tenth chapter[^p205-8]: « But the hireling, and he that is not the shepherd, seeth the wolf coming, and flieth »; and Zacharias, the eleventh chapter: « O shepherd, and idol, that forsaketh the flock »! Or they fled not out of fear of human peril, but out of fear of the divine judgement; of which Job, the nineteenth chapter[^p205-9]: « Flee from the face of the sword, for the sword is the revenger of iniquity, and know ye that there is a judgement ». They fled, however, not that they might lie hid, because it is written in the Psalm: « Whither shall I go from thy spirit? or whither shall I flee from thy face »? but that they might quickly relate to those absent the things they had seen. — Whence it is inferred: *And told it in the city and in the villages,* as did that messenger also, Job, the first chapter[^p205-10]: « I alone have escaped to tell thee ». And in this they had, as it were, the office of preachers, of whom it is said in the Psalm: « They announced the works of God, and understood his doings »; to the Philippians, the first chapter: « Whether by occasion Christ be announced, and in this I rejoice, yea, and will rejoice ». Whence the *good things* seen concerning Christ are not to be kept silent, but to be announced, according to that of the fourth of Kings, the seventh chapter[^p205-11]: « Those four leprous men said: This is a day of good tidings; if we hold our peace and will not tell it till
<!-- page 206 -->
the morning, we shall be charged with a crime. Come, let us go and tell it in the king's court ». But *evil things* are not to be told; the second of Kings, the first chapter: « Announce it not in Geth, neither announce it in the streets of Ascalon ».

61. (Vers. 35.). Secondly, as to *the searching out of the truth heard,* there is subjoined: *And they went out to see what was done, and they came to Jesus. They went out,* I say, to inquiry; the Canticles, the third chapter[^p206-1]: « Go forth, ye daughters of Sion, and see king Solomon »; and in the Psalm: « Come and see the works of the Lord, what wonders he hath done upon earth ». — They went out, moreover, *to Jesus,* who is the Truth[^p206-2], that they might learn the truth. For, as the careful inquirer says, Job, the twenty-third chapter: « Who will grant me that I might know and find him, and come even to his throne »? So the Samaritans, having heard the word of the woman, John, the fourth chapter: « The woman went her way into the city, and saith to those men: Come and see a man who hath told me all things whatsoever I have done ». « They went therefore out of the city, and came unto him ».

And it is very much to be noted that it is said that, while *they go out to see what was done, they came to Jesus,* because the thing made, while it is seen, leads to the Maker; to the Romans, the first chapter[^p206-3]: « The invisible things of God, from the creation of the world, are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made »; and Wisdom, the thirteenth chapter: « By the greatness of the beauty and of the creature the Creator of them may be seen, so as to be known thereby ». And therefore Ecclesiasticus, the forty-second chapter: « How wonderful are his works, and what we can see is but as a spark »! In this, moreover, it appears that one is not to stop at the thing made, but to come through even unto Jesus; Ecclesiasticus, the forty-third chapter[^p206-4]: « Look upon the rainbow, and bless him that made it »; and in the Psalm: « Out of the mouth of infants and of sucklings thou hast perfected praise » etc.

62. Thirdly, as to *the finding of the truth sought,* there is subjoined: *And they found the man sitting, out of whom the devils were departed;* and so in a *due state,* against disquiet, because he never rested when the devil possessed him; Isaias, the fifty-seventh chapter[^p206-5]: « The wicked are like the raging sea, which cannot rest ». On the contrary, of him in whom the Spirit of the Lord dwells it is said, Lamentations, the third chapter: « He shall sit solitary and hold his peace »; because, according to that of the Psalm, « his place is in peace ». — They found him also in *due attire,* against nakedness; on account of which he adds: *Clothed.* For such
attire befits a man, as much bodily as spiritually; the Apocalypse, the sixteenth chapter[^p206-6]: « Blessed is he that keepeth his garments, lest he walk naked ». — They found him also in *due sense,* against insanity; on account of which he subjoins: *And in his right mind;* whence he could say that of Nabuchodonosor, Daniel, the fourth chapter[^p206-7]: « At the same time my sense returned to me ». Which sense the devil takes away from those whom he possesses, by infatuating their minds; the second to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « In whom the god of this world hath blinded the minds of unbelievers ». — They found him also in *due position,* against disorder; on account of which it is added: *At his feet,* as one intent on his preaching, according to that of Deuteronomy, the next to the last chapter[^p206-8]: « They that approach to his feet shall receive of his doctrine »; and below, the tenth chapter: « Mary, sitting at the Lord's feet, heard his word ». — And by these signs they knew manifestly that he had been delivered, *by their own sight.* — But the manner of the cure and its cause they knew *by hearing,* because they had heard that this had been done through Christ. On account of which there is subjoined: *And they feared,* namely Christ, attending to his wondrous power and strength, according to that of Jeremias, the tenth chapter[^p206-9]: « Who shall not fear thee, O king of nations? For thine is the glory; among all the wise men of the nations, and in all their kingdoms, there is none like unto thee ».

63. (Vers. 36.). To this he brings in sure testimony; therefore he subjoins: *And they that had seen*[^p206-10] *told them,* namely the shepherds. For such ought to bear witness; John, the nineteenth chapter: « And he that saw it hath given testimony ». — *How he had been healed from the legion,* through Christ's command. Such testimony is to be believed; the first of John, the first chapter[^p206-11]: « That which we have seen and have heard and have looked upon, we bear witness and announce unto you »; the Psalm: « Shewing the praises of the Lord, and his marvellous works which he hath done »; because, Acts, the fourth chapter, « we cannot but speak the things which we have heard and seen ».

64. (Vers. 37.). Fourthly, as to *the veneration of the truth found,* there is subjoined: *And all the multitude of the country of the Gerasens besought him*[^p206-12]. After they knew the command of the Majesty to be dreaded, they besought him *to depart from them.* Which indeed they did not say out of the contempt of pride, as those of whom Job, the twenty-first chapter[^p206-13]: « Who say to God: Depart from us; we desire not the knowledge of thy ways »; but
<!-- page 207 -->
out of the fear of reverence. Whence also there is subjoined: *For they were taken with great fear,* as Peter, above, the fifth chapter[^p207-1]: « Depart from me, O Lord, for I am a sinful man »; and as the centurion, above, the seventh chapter: « Lord, I am not worthy that thou shouldst enter under my roof »; because, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirteenth chapter, « he shall take a burden upon him that hath fellowship with one more honourable ». Whence Ambrose[^p207-2]: « The weak mind does not take in the word of God, nor can it bear the weight of wisdom ». And since they were less fit for the divine dwelling together, therefore they were heard. — On account of which it is added: *And he, going up into the ship*[^p207-3]*, returned back again.* The Lord did not wish to be burdensome to any; whence Matthew, the tenth chapter: « And whosoever shall not receive you, going forth, shake off the dust from your feet »; and Matthew, the seventh chapter: « Give not that which is holy to dogs, neither cast pearls before swine ». But the Samaritans did more prudently than these, of whom John, the fourth chapter[^p207-4]: « When the Samaritans were come to him, they besought him that he would tarry there. And he abode with them two days ». But stupor had compassed these about, as it had Peter also, above, the fifth chapter. Therefore they could say that of Job, the thirty-first chapter: « I have always feared God as waves swelling over me, and his weight I was not able to bear ».

In this also it is given to be understood *spiritually* that the Lord, when repulsed, goes away; the first of Paralipomenon, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p207-5]: « If thou seek him, thou shalt find him; but if thou forsake him, he will cast thee off for ever ». — It is intimated also that the Lord does not leave us unless we first repel him; rather, he thrusts himself in; whence the Apocalypse, the third chapter: « I stand at the gate and knock » etc.

65. (Vers. 38.). *And he besought him* etc. Here, fourthly, there is described *the benevolence of the healed man obeying,* which the Evangelist commends from four things, namely from *the offering of service,* from *the condescension of the Physician,* from *the committing of an office* and from *the spreading abroad of the benefit,* in such wise that the one disposes to the other.

First, then, as to *the offering of service,* it is said: *And the man, out of whom the devils were departed, besought him,* namely the healed man the physician, *that he might be with him*[^p207-6], that is, that he might serve him as Lord and Saviour, as Ethai the Gethite said to David, the second of Kings, the fifteenth chapter: « As the Lord liveth, and as my lord the king liveth, in what place soever thou shalt be, either in death or in life, there will thy servant be ». So Eliseus to Elias, the fourth of Kings, the second chapter: « As the Lord liveth, and as thy soul liveth, I will not leave thee ». And he wished to be with him for this reason, that he might be *more secure,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the fifty-first chapter[^p207-7]: « I will call upon the Lord, the father of my Lord, that he leave me not in the day of my tribulation ». That he might be also *more perfect;* the Psalm: « I will keep thy justifications; O do not thou utterly forsake me ». That he might be also *more happy;* to the Philippians, the first chapter[^p207-8]: « I desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ »; and John, the twelfth chapter: « Father, I will that where I am, there also may my minister be ». — Therefore he wished to dwell with him on the way, that he might dwell with him in the fatherland.

66. Secondly, as to *the condescension of the Physician,* there is subjoined: *But Jesus let him go,* namely in entire liberty, not wishing to lay any other burden on him, showing that on account of a benefit conferred service is not to be accepted; whence Matthew, the eighteenth chapter[^p207-9]: « The lord of that servant, being moved with pity, let him go and forgave him the debt ».

67. (Vers. 39.). He let him go, moreover, in rest; whence he subjoins: *Saying: Return to thy house,* that thou mayest rest there, whence the demons had driven thee out; Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-second chapter[^p207-10]: « Run before into thy house, and there withdraw thyself, and there take thy pastime, and do what thou hast a mind ». Seneca: « It is the mark of a mind well settled to be able to stand firm in itself and to be able to dwell with itself ».

And note that the Lord *lets* some *go* on account of *infirmity,* as this man, to whom he condescended. Some he *admits* on account of *fidelity,* as him of whom below, the ninth chapter[^p207-11]: « He said to another: Follow me. But he said: Lord, but suffer me first to go and bury » etc. Some he *draws* on account of *fitness,* below, the ninth chapter: « And another said: I will follow thee, Lord, but let me first take my leave » etc. Some he *repels* on account of *cupidity,* as him of whom Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p207-12]: « Master, I will follow thee »,
<!-- page 208 -->
the Lord answers: « The foxes have holes » etc. — Him, however, the Lord did not admit to the discipleship of perfection, either because the fear of human peril moved him, or the love of the benefit; or that he might show, as has been touched on before[^p208-1], that on account of a spiritual benefit service is not to be accepted; whence Matthew, the tenth chapter: « Freely have you received, freely give ». And an example of this appears in Eliseus, the fourth of Kings, the fifth chapter. The preacher can, however, accept a stipend, as is said in the first to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter; but he ought not to *preach* on account of it, but on account of the salvation of the people.

68. And note here that the Lord, according as the gospel history narrates, sometimes enjoins those cured that they tell no one, as is plain in the cure of the leper, Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p208-2]: « See thou tell no man »; and in the raising of the girl, below, in the same chapter: « Whom he enjoined to tell no man what was done ». But here he enjoins the healed man that he spread it abroad, to this end, that the Lord may show that saving truth is not to be kept silent, and that one's own glory is not to be sought. Therefore he sometimes enjoins that a thing be told, and sometimes enjoins that it be kept silent, that in the first he may give the instruction that the truth be preached, and in the second, that one's own glory be not sought[^p208-3]. — In this it appears also that the work of preaching belongs not only to apostolic men, but also to any good men whatever who have been taught by Christ. Whence also Christ, when the disciples were forbidding a certain man to cast out demons in the name of Christ, who did not follow Christ, said: « Forbid him not », as is said below, the ninth chapter[^p208-4]. For a good man ought to say what Moses said, Numbers, the eleventh chapter: « Who will grant that all may prophesy, and that the Lord give them the Holy Spirit »? according to that to the Philippians, the first chapter: « Whether by truth or by occasion Christ be announced, and in this I rejoice, yea, and will rejoice ».

69. Thirdly, as to *the committing of an office,* it is said: *And tell how great things God hath done*[^p208-5] *to thee;* in which he sends him to preach and to spread abroad the divine benefit; the Psalm: « I will declare thy name to my brethren » etc.; and Tobias, the twelfth chapter: « It is good to hide the secret of a king, but honourable to reveal and confess the works of God ». And this God commands to be done; the Psalm: « How great things he commanded our fathers, that they should make the same known to their sons ». And that is the office enjoined upon preachers.

70. Fourthly, as to *the spreading abroad of the benefit,* there is subjoined: *And he went through the whole city, saying*[^p208-6], what had been enjoined; Jonas, the third chapter: « Arise and go to Ninive, the great city, and preach in it the preaching ». But Jonas preached peril, while this man spread abroad and preached a benefit. — On account of which he adds: *How great things Jesus had done to him,* that he might truly say that of the Psalm[^p208-7]: « Come, hear, all ye, and I will tell what great things he hath done for my soul ». To this the Angel invited, Tobias, the twelfth chapter: « Bless ye God, and tell all his marvellous works ». But he says this not because it is in our power to tell out all his works and benefits, since it is said, Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter[^p208-8]: « Who is sufficient to declare his works »? but because this ought to be in our desire and will, according to that of the Psalm: « I will give praise to thee, O Lord, with my whole heart; I will relate all thy marvellous works »; not because the tongue suffices to express it, but because the gratitude of the heart longs to pay praises and to preach for all things.

71. Note here that according to the letter four notable things can be drawn from what has been said. The first is, that the Lord willed to be made known to others through vile and lowly persons, as through swineherds and herdsmen. — Secondly, that not all are to be admitted to the state of perfection[^p208-9], but only those who are fit. — Thirdly, that those whom Christ cures from the vexation of the devil are not to be kept back from preaching the name of Christ. — Fourthly, that we ought to be most ready for the giving of thanks for benefits received, after the example of this demoniac, who told the benefit of God to all.
