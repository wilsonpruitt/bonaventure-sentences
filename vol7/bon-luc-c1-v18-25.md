# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. I, Vers. 18-25
## *Tertio describitur promissionis confirmatio quadrupliciter*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 17 -->

#### Tertio describitur promissionis confirmatio quadrupliciter.

29. *Et dixit Zacharias* etc. Descripsit superius Evangelista conceptionem praecursoris quantum ad parentum conditionem et angelicam promissionem; hic describit quantum ad *promissionis confirmationem,* quam describit hoc ordine. Primo enim introducitur *dubietas sacerdotis;* secundo, *auctoritas nuntii,* ibi[^p17-8]: *Et respondens Angelus;* tertio, *signum auctoritatis,* ibi: *Et ecce, eris tacens;* quarto, *evidentia signi,* ibi: *Et erat plebs exspectans.*

30. (Vers. 18.). Introducit ergo *dubitationem sacerdotis,* cum dicit: *Et dixit Zacharias ad Angelum: Unde hoc sciam?* id est, quo signo certificabor? primae ad Corinthios primo[^p17-9]: « Iudaei signa quaerunt ». — Unde nota, quod quidam signa quae-
<!-- page 18 -->
runt propter *publicationem divini promissi,* sicut Abraham; Genesis decimo quinto: « Domine Deus, unde scire possum, quod possessurus sim terram »? — Quidam recusant propter *obstinationem delicti proprii,* sicut Achaz; Isaiae septimo: « Pete tibi signum a Domino Deo tuo ». — Quidam vero requirunt propter *haesitationem animi,* sicut Zacharias; unde subiungit rationem dubitandi: *Ego enim sum senex, et uxor mea processit in diebus suis;* et ita impotestas est ex parte utriusque parentis, et ita iam non est tempus generandi, sed potius, secundum illud Ecclesiastae tertio[^p18-1], « tempus est longe fieri ab amplexibus »; et ideo dubitabat et haesitabat. At non sic Abraham, de quo ad Romanos quarto: « Non est infirmatus in fide neque consideravit corpus suum emortuum, cum fere centum annorum esset, et emortuam vulvam Sarae. In repromissione etiam Dei non haesitavit diffidentia, sed confortatus est fide, dans gloriam Deo, plenissime sciens, quia, quaecumque promisit, Deus potens est et facere ». Unde Abraham credidit, quia consideravit divinam potentiam; sed Zacharias haesitavit, quia consideravit humanam impotentiam[^p18-2]. In quo instruimur, quod in miraculis aspiciendum est ad causas superiores.

31. (Vers. 19.). *Et respondens Angelus* etc. Hic notatur *nuntii auctoritas* quantum ad virtutem *contemplativam* et quantum ad *administrativam.* Quantum ad *contemplativam* dicit: *Ego sum Gabriel, qui asto ante Deum;* Danielis septimo[^p18-3]: « Decies millies centena millia assistebant ei »; et inter hos erat Gabriel. Et ideo tertii Regum decimo dixit regina Saba in figura Salomoni: « Beati viri tui, et beati servi tui hi, qui stant coram te et audiunt sapientiam tuam ». Hoc vidit Michaeas, tertii Regum ultimo: « Vidi, inquit, Dominum sedentem super solium suum et omnem exercitum caeli assistentem ei a dextris et a sinistris ». Notatur ergo dignitas in assistendo, et notatur etiam in hoc auctoritas in denuntiando. — Quantum ad *ministerium missionis* dicit: *Et missus sum loqui ad te et haec tibi evangelizare.* Unde de numero illorum erat; de quibus ad Hebraeos primo[^p18-4]: « Nonne omnes sunt administratorii spiritus in ministerium missi » etc.; *missus,* inquam, et *loqui* et *evangelizare.* In *loquela* enim est intelligenda expressio veritatis; Danielis nono: « Ecce, vir Gabriel, quem videram in visione a principio, cito volans tetigit me in tempore sacrificii vespertini et docuit me et locutus est mihi ». In *evangelizatione* vero intelligenda est denuntiatio bonitatis. *Evangelium* enim est bona nuntiatio; Zachariae primo[^p18-5]: « Et respondit Dominus Angelo, qui loquebatur in me verba bona, verba consolatoria ». Unde debebat ei dicere illud tertii Regum primo: « Ingredere, quia vir fortis es et bona annuntians ». *Gabriel* enim fortitudo Dei interpretatur, et ideo missus fuit confortare pavidum sacerdotem.

32. (Vers. 20.). *Et ecce, eris tacens* etc. Hic tangitur *signum auctoritatis,* et dat signum poenalitatis propter vitium incredulitatis. Praemittit ergo *taciturnitatis signum* usque ad tempus statutum, *usque in diem, quo haec fiant;* ut sic posset dicere illud Psalmi[^p18-6]: « Ego autem tanquam surdus non audiebam, et sicut mutus non aperiens » etc. Nec mirum, si mutus factus est propter incredulitatem propriam, cum Ezechielem mutum fecerit incredulitas aliena; Ezechielis tertio: « Eris mutus nec quasi vir obiurgans, quia domus exasperans est ». — Et ideo subdit *rationem* taciturnitatis, vitium scilicet incredulitatis: *Pro eo quod non credidisti verbis meis, quae implebuntur in tempore suo.* Econtra dicitur infra eodem[^p18-7] de Maria: « Beata, quae credidisti, quoniam perficientur ea quae dicta sunt tibi a Domino ». Ante poenam non credidit, sed post, quia, sicut dicitur Isaiae vigesimo octavo, « tantummodo vexatio intellectum dabit auditui ». Non ergo contemnenda sunt verba nuntiorum Dei, quia Deus incredulos punit; Exodi vigesimo tertio: « Ecce, ego mitto Angelum »; et sequitur: « Observa eum et audi vocem eius, nec contemnendum putes, quia non dimittet, cum peccaveris »; ad Hebraeos secundo: « Si enim qui per Angelos dictus est sermo factus est firmus, et omnis praevaricatio et inobe-
<!-- page 19 -->
dientia accepit iustam mercedis retributionem; quomodo nos effugiemus, si tantam neglexerimus salutem? Quae cum initium accepisset enarrari per Dominum, ab eis qui audierunt, in nos confirmata est, contestante Deo signis et portentis et variis virtutibus et Spiritus sancti distributionibus secundum suam voluntatem ». Et ideo non est locus incredulitatis.

33. (Vers. 21.). *Et erat plebs* etc. Hic tangitur quarto *evidentia signi* propter *praesentiam* multitudinis[^p19-1] et *absentiam* sermonis. *Praesentiam multitudinis* exspectantis et admirantis tangit dicens: *Et erat plebs exspectans Zachariam, et mirabantur, quod tardaret in templo.* Ad populum enim pertinet *exspectare;* unde Moyses, cum ascenderet in montem, dixit senioribus, Exodi vigesimo quarto[^p19-2]: « Exspectate hic, donec revertamur ad vos », quia, secundum quod dicitur Threnorum tertio, « bonum est praestolari cum silentio salutare Dei ». Plebis etiam est *admirari* divina secreta, non perscrutari; Ecclesiastici undecimo dicitur de iusto, et potest exponi de Zacharia: « Oculus Dei respexit illum in bono et erexit illum ab humilitate ipsius, et exaltavit caput, et mirati sunt in illo multi ».

34. (Vers. 22.). Quantum ad *absentiam sermonis* subdit: *Egressus autem non poterat loqui ad illos.* Hoc enim solet accidere his qui elevantur in apicem contemplationis; Exodi quarto[^p19-3]: « Ex quo locutus es ad servum tuum, impeditioris et tardioris linguae sum »; et Danielis decimo: « Domine mi, in visione tua dissolutae sunt compages meae, et nihil in me remansit virium; et quomodo poterit servus Domini mei eloqui cum Domino meo? Nihil enim in me remansit virium, sed et halitus meus intercluditur ». Et ideo ex hoc tanquam ex signo venerunt in notitiam veritatis. — Propter quod sequitur: *Et cognoverunt, quod visionem vidisset in templo,* et ita, quod esset verus Propheta, sicut Samuel, de quo primi Regum tertio[^p19-4]: « Cognovit universus Israel a Dan usque Bersabee, quod fidelis Samuel Propheta esset Domini ». — Ad perfectam autem signi evidentiam notatur loquendi impossibilitas et diuturnitas, in hoc quod addit: *Et ipse erat innuens illis et permansit mutus.* Ambrosius[^p19-5]: « Sine voce corporales actus moliens nec exprimens voluntatem », permansit mutus, in signum, quod in adventu Christi silentium debebat imponi sacerdotio legali; Matthaei undecimo: « Lex et Prophetae usque ad Ioannem prophetaverunt ». Ideo dicit Chrysostomus de Ioanne, quod est « Apostolorum vox, silentium Prophetarum ».

#### Quarto describitur promissionis impletio quadrupliciter.

35. *Et factum est, ut impleti sunt* etc. Descripsit supra praecursoris conceptionem quantum ad parentum conditionem, angelicam denuntiationem et denuntiationis confirmationem; hic postremo describit quantum ad *denuntiationis consummationem* sive impletionem. Circa quod quatuor nobis breviter explicantur, scilicet *mutua cohabitatio, fecunda conceptio, verecunda occultatio* et *laetabunda exsultatio.* Primum respicit legem coniugii; secundum, virtutem miraculi; tertium, recordationem praecedentis opprobrii; quartum, recognitionem praesentis beneficii.

36. (Vers. 23.). Insinuatur ergo primo *mutua cohabitatio,* cum dicit: *Et factum est, ut impleti sunt dies officii eius, abiit in domum suam,* scilicet ad cohabitandum et reddendum debitum uxori; unde Chrysostomus[^p19-6] dicit, quod eadem nocte filium genuit; hoc autem fecit coniugii lege; primae ad Corinthios septimo: « Nolite fraudare invicem nisi ex consensu ad tempus, ut vacetis orationi; et iterum revertimini in id ipsum ». Et ideo, quamdiu Zacharias dies officii implebat, ad uxorem non accedebat; Exodi decimo nono: « Sacerdotes, qui acce-
<!-- page 20 -->
dunt ad me, sanctificentur, ne percutiam eos »; et ex hac causa dicunt Hebraei Ozam percussum, secundi Regum sexto, quia nocte uxorem cognoverat, quando tetigit arcam. Non enim licebat ab amplexu Liae ad Rachelem immediate transire, sicut Genesis vigesimo nono[^p20-1] dicitur ad Iacob: « Imple hebdomadam dierum huius copulae ».

37. (Vers. 24.). Secundo insinuatur *fecunda conceptio,* cum dicit: *Post hos autem dies concepit Elisabeth;* sicut dicitur primi Regum primo[^p20-2]: « Post circulum dierum concepit Anna ». *Post dies,* officii; in quo ostenditur, quod carnalia postponenda sunt spiritualibus; et ideo infra decimo: « Maria optimam partem elegit ».

38. Tertio describitur *verecunda occultatio,* cum dicitur: *Et occultabat se mensibus quinque.* Hoc enim erat signum humilitatis et pudicitiae; non sicut Agar ancilla, de qua dicitur Genesis decimo sexto[^p20-3], quod « concepisse se videns, despexit dominam suam ». Ratio huius bene redditur in Glossa Ambrosii. — Sed *spiritualiter* erudimur, quod sancti propositi conceptio est in principio occultanda, sicut dicitur Matthaei decimo tertio[^p20-4]: « Simile est regnum caelorum thesauro abscondito in agro »; ubi Gregorius: « Inventus thesaurus absconditur, ut servetur, quia studium caelestis desiderii a malignis spiritibus custodire non sufficit, qui hoc ab humana laude non abscondit ».

39. (Vers. 25.). Quarto tangitur *laetabunda exsultatio* in recognitionem beneficii, ibi: *Dicens, quia sic fecit mihi Dominus.* Simile Genesis vigesimo primo[^p20-5]: « Risum fecit mihi Dominus; quicumque audierit, corridebit mihi ». — *In diebus, quibus respexit auferre opprobrium meum inter homines,* opprobrium scilicet sterilitatis; Genesis trigesimo[^p20-6]: « Concepit Rachel et peperit, dicens: Abstulit Deus opprobrium meum ». Hoc erat opprobrium inter homines, propter illud Deuteronomii septimo: « Non erit sterilis apud te utriusque sexus ». Unde dicitur primi Regum primo, quod « aemula Annae affligebat eam in tantum, ut exprobraret, quod conclusisset Dominus vulvam eius ». Et propter hoc gaudebat, secundum illud Psalmi: « Qui habitare facit sterilem in domo, matrem filiorum laetantem ».

## English
<!-- page 17 -->

#### Thirdly the confirmation of the promise is described in four ways.

29. *And Zachary said* etc. Above, the Evangelist described the conception of the precursor as to the condition of the parents and the angelic promise; here he describes it as to *the confirmation of the promise,* which he describes in this order. For first is introduced *the doubt of the priest;* secondly, *the authority of the messenger,* there[^p17-8]: *And the angel answering;* thirdly, *the sign of the authority,* there: *And behold, thou shalt be silent;* fourthly, *the evidence of the sign,* there: *And the people were waiting.*

30. (Vers. 18.). He introduces, then, *the doubting of the priest,* when he says: *And Zachary said to the angel: Whereby shall I know this?* that is, by what sign shall I be certified? the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p17-9]: « The Jews seek signs ». — Whence note that some seek signs
<!-- page 18 -->
on account of *the publishing of the divine promise,* as Abraham; Genesis, the fifteenth chapter: « Lord God, whereby may I know that I shall possess the land »? — Some refuse them on account of *the obstinacy of their own fault,* as Achaz; Isaias, the seventh chapter: « Ask thee a sign of the Lord thy God ». — But some require them on account of *hesitation of mind,* as Zachary; whence he adds the reason for doubting: *For I am an old man, and my wife is advanced in her days;* and so there is impotence on the part of both parents, and so it is no longer the time for begetting, but rather, according to that of Ecclesiastes, the third chapter[^p18-1], « it is a time to be far from embraces »; and therefore he doubted and hesitated. But not so Abraham, of whom to the Romans, the fourth chapter: « He was not weak in faith, neither did he consider his own body now dead, whereas he was almost a hundred years old, nor the dead womb of Sara. In the promise also of God he staggered not by distrust, but was strengthened in faith, giving glory to God, most fully knowing that whatsoever he has promised, God is able also to perform ». Whence Abraham believed, because he considered the divine power; but Zachary hesitated, because he considered human impotence[^p18-2]. In which we are instructed that in miracles one must look to the higher causes.

31. (Vers. 19.). *And the angel answering* etc. Here is noted *the authority of the messenger* as to the *contemplative* power and as to the *administrative.* As to the *contemplative* he says: *I am Gabriel, who stand before God;* Daniel, the seventh chapter[^p18-3]: « Ten thousand times a hundred thousand stood before him »; and among these was Gabriel. And therefore in the third of Kings, the tenth chapter, the queen of Saba said to Solomon in figure: « Blessed are thy men, and blessed are these thy servants, who stand before thee and hear thy wisdom ». This Micheas saw, the third of Kings, the last chapter: « I saw, he says, the Lord sitting on his throne, and all the army of heaven standing by him on the right hand and on the left ». There is noted, then, dignity in standing by, and there is noted also in this authority in declaring. — As to *the ministry of the mission* he says: *And am sent to speak to thee and to bring thee these good tidings*[^tr-evangelizare]. Whence he was of the number of those; of whom to the Hebrews, the first chapter[^p18-4]: « Are they not all ministering spirits, sent to minister » etc.; *sent,* I say, both *to speak* and *to bring good tidings.* For in *speech* is to be understood the expression of truth; Daniel, the ninth chapter: « Behold, the man Gabriel, whom I had seen in the vision at the beginning, flying swiftly touched me at the time of the evening sacrifice, and he instructed me and spoke to me ». But in *the bringing of good tidings* is to be understood the declaration of goodness. For the *Gospel* is good tidings; Zacharias, the first chapter[^p18-5]: « And the Lord answered the angel that spoke in me good words, comfortable words ». Whence he ought to have said to him that of the third of Kings, the first chapter: « Come in, because thou art a valiant man, and bringest good tidings ». For *Gabriel* is interpreted the strength of God, and therefore he was sent to strengthen the fearful priest.

32. (Vers. 20.). *And behold, thou shalt be silent* etc. Here is touched *the sign of the authority,* and he gives a sign of penalty on account of the vice of unbelief. He sets first, then, *the sign of silence* up to the appointed time, *until the day wherein these things shall come to pass;* so that he might thus say that of the Psalm[^p18-6]: « But I, as a deaf man, heard not, and as a dumb man not opening » etc. Nor is it a wonder if he was made dumb on account of his own unbelief, since another's unbelief made Ezechiel dumb; Ezechiel, the third chapter: « Thou shalt be dumb, and not as a man that reproveth, because it is a provoking house ». — And therefore he adds *the reason* for the silence, namely the vice of unbelief: *Because thou hast not believed my words, which shall be fulfilled in their time.* On the contrary it is said below, in the same chapter[^p18-7], of Mary: « Blessed art thou that hast believed, because those things shall be accomplished that were spoken to thee by the Lord ». Before the punishment he did not believe, but after, because, as is said in Isaias, the twenty-eighth chapter, « vexation alone shall make you understand what you hear ». Therefore the words of the messengers of God are not to be contemned, because God punishes the unbelieving; Exodus, the twenty-third chapter: « Behold, I send an angel »; and it follows: « Take notice of him, and hear his voice, and do not think him one to be contemned, for he will not forgive when thou hast sinned »; to the Hebrews, the second chapter: « For if the word spoken by angels became steadfast, and every transgression and disobe-
<!-- page 19 -->
dience received a just recompense of reward, how shall we escape if we neglect so great salvation? which having begun to be declared by the Lord, was confirmed unto us by them that heard him, God bearing witness by signs and wonders and divers miracles and distributions of the Holy Spirit according to his own will ». And therefore there is no place for unbelief.

33. (Vers. 21.). *And the people were* etc. Here is touched fourthly *the evidence of the sign* on account of *the presence* of the multitude[^p19-1] and *the absence* of speech. He touches *the presence of the multitude* waiting and wondering, saying: *And the people were waiting for Zachary, and they wondered that he tarried in the temple.* For it belongs to the people *to wait;* whence Moses, when he was going up into the mountain, said to the ancients, Exodus, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p19-2]: « Wait ye here till we return to you », because, according to what is said in Lamentations, the third chapter, « it is good to wait with silence for the salvation of God ». It belongs to the people also *to wonder at* divine secrets, not to search them out; in Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter, it is said of the just man, and it can be expounded of Zachary: « The eye of God hath looked upon him for good, and hath lifted him up from his low estate, and hath exalted his head, and many have wondered at him ».

34. (Vers. 22.). As to *the absence of speech* he adds: *But going out he could not speak to them.* For this is wont to befall those who are raised up to the summit of contemplation; Exodus, the fourth chapter[^p19-3]: « Since thou hast spoken to thy servant, I have more impediment and slowness of tongue »; and Daniel, the tenth chapter: « O my lord, at the sight of thee my joints are loosed, and no strength hath remained in me; and how can the servant of my lord speak with my lord? For no strength remaineth in me, moreover my breath is stopped ». And therefore from this as from a sign they came to the knowledge of the truth. — On account of which there follows: *And they understood that he had seen a vision in the temple,* and so that he was a true Prophet, as Samuel, of whom in the first of Kings, the third chapter[^p19-4]: « All Israel from Dan to Bersabee knew that Samuel was a faithful Prophet of the Lord ». — But for the perfect evidence of the sign there is noted the impossibility and the long duration of speaking, in this, that he adds: *And he made signs to them, and remained dumb.* Ambrose[^p19-5]: « Striving at bodily acts without voice, and not expressing his will », he remained dumb, in sign that at the coming of Christ silence was to be imposed on the priesthood of the Law; Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « The Law and the Prophets prophesied until John ». Therefore Chrysostom says of John that he is « the voice of the Apostles, the silence of the Prophets ».

#### Fourthly the fulfilment of the promise is described in four ways.

35. *And it came to pass, when they were accomplished* etc. Above, he described the conception of the precursor as to the condition of the parents, the angelic declaration and the confirmation of the declaration; here lastly he describes it as to *the consummation of the declaration* or its fulfilment. Concerning which four things are briefly set out to us, namely *the mutual cohabitation, the fruitful conception, the shamefast concealment* and *the joyful exultation.* The first regards the law of marriage; the second, the power of the miracle; the third, the remembrance of the preceding reproach; the fourth, the recognition of the present benefit.

36. (Vers. 23.). There is insinuated, then, first *the mutual cohabitation,* when he says: *And it came to pass, when the days of his office were accomplished, he departed to his own house,* namely to cohabit and to render the debt to his wife; whence Chrysostom[^p19-6] says that on the same night he begot the son; and this he did by the law of marriage; the first to the Corinthians, the seventh chapter: « Defraud not one another, except by consent for a time, that you may give yourselves to prayer; and return together again ». And therefore, as long as Zachary was fulfilling the days of his office, he did not approach his wife; Exodus, the nineteenth chapter: « The priests who come
<!-- page 20 -->
to me, let them be sanctified, lest I strike them »; and for this reason the Hebrews say that Oza was struck, the second of Kings, the sixth chapter, because he had known his wife in the night when he touched the ark. For it was not lawful to pass immediately from the embrace of Lia to Rachel, as is said to Jacob in Genesis, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p20-1]: « Make up the week of days of this match ».

37. (Vers. 24.). Secondly is insinuated *the fruitful conception,* when he says: *And after these days Elizabeth conceived;* as is said in the first of Kings, the first chapter[^p20-2]: « After the circuit of days Anna conceived ». *After the days,* of his office; in which it is shown that carnal things are to be put after spiritual things; and therefore below, in the tenth chapter: « Mary hath chosen the best part ».

38. Thirdly is described *the shamefast concealment,* when it is said: *And hid herself five months.* For this was a sign of humility and of purity; not as Agar the handmaid, of whom it is said in Genesis, the sixteenth chapter[^p20-3], that « seeing that she had conceived, she despised her mistress ». The reason of this is well given in the Gloss of Ambrose. — But *spiritually* we are schooled that the conception of a holy purpose is to be concealed in the beginning, as is said in Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p20-4]: « The kingdom of heaven is like unto a treasure hidden in a field »; where Gregory: « The treasure found is hidden, that it may be kept, because he does not suffice to guard the pursuit of the heavenly desire from the malignant spirits who does not hide it from human praise ».

39. (Vers. 25.). Fourthly is touched *the joyful exultation* in recognition of the benefit, there: *Saying: Thus hath the Lord dealt with me.* Similar is Genesis, the twenty-first chapter[^p20-5]: « The Lord hath made a laughter for me; whosoever shall hear of it will laugh with me ». — *In the days wherein he hath had regard to take away my reproach among men,* the reproach, namely, of barrenness; Genesis, the thirtieth chapter[^p20-6]: « Rachel conceived and bore, saying: God hath taken away my reproach ». This was a reproach among men, on account of that of Deuteronomy, the seventh chapter: « There shall be none barren with thee of either sex ». Whence it is said in the first of Kings, the first chapter, that « Anna's rival afflicted her so much as to upbraid her that the Lord had shut up her womb ». And on account of this she rejoiced, according to that of the Psalm: « Who maketh a barren woman to dwell in a house, the joyful mother of children ».
