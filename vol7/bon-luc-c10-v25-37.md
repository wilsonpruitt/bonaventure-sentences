# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. X, Vers. 25-37
## *Ad informationem praemittitur divinum praeceptum dupliciter propositum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 266 -->

#### Secunda pars agit de data discipulis forma vivendi dupliciter.

43. *Et ecce, quidam legisperitus* etc. Postquam descripsit Evangelista formam praedicandi, hic secundo insinuat *formam vivendi*[^p266-3]. Dupliciter autem potest quis informari ad recte vivendum, scilicet *verbo* et *exemplo;* ideo Evangelista primo ad informationem praemittit *divinum praeceptum;* secundo vero subiungit *humanum exemplum,* ibi[^p266-4]: *Factum est, dum irent, intravit* etc.

#### Ad informationem praemittitur divinum praeceptum dupliciter propositum.

Quoniam autem *informatio praecepti* non valet nisi intelligenti, ideo primo praemittitur *praeceptum regulans* ad vivendum; secundo vero adiungitur *documentum adiuvans* ad intelligendum, ibi[^p266-5]: *Suspiciens autem Iesus.* — Circa expressionem autem *praecepti regulativi* quatuor introducuntur, scilicet *inquisitio veritatis* salutiferae, *inventio veritatis* quaesitae, *approbatio veritatis* inventae, *investigatio veritatis* approbatae.

44. (Vers. 25.). Primo ergo quantum ad *inquisitionem veritatis salutiferae* dicit: *Et ecce, quidam legisperitus surrexit, tentans illum.* Iste peritus *erat* et peritum se *reputabat;* et quia peritum se *reputabat,* ideo non requirebat, ut disceret, sed ut probaret, sicut moris est superbis, secundum illud Ecclesiastici decimo tertio[^p266-6] de divite: « Ex multa loquela tentabit te ». Sed rursus, quia peritus *erat,* ideo idonee et recte quaerebat; propter quod addit: *Et dicens: Magister, quid faciendo vitam aeternam possidebo? Magistrum* eum vocat, quia hoc ipso spectat ad eum, ut ad interrogata respondeat, iuxta quod dicitur Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p266-7]: « Magister, scimus, quia verax es et viam Dei in veritate doces ». Unde quia magisterium salutis profitebatur Christus, ideo quaestionem facit de veritate salutari. Similem quaestionem dicitur proposuisse adolescens Matthaei decimo nono[^p266-8]: « Magister bone, quid faciam, ut habeam vitam aeternam »? Isti quaerebant studiose, non curiose, quia non de operibus factis, sed faciendis propter meritum salutis, secundum illud Ecclesiastici tertio[^p266-9]: « In pluribus operibus eius ne fueris curiosus; sed quae praecepit tibi Deus, illa cogita semper ». Unde isti quaerunt de via eundi ad vitam, iuxta consilium Ieremiae sexto: « State super vias et videte et interrogate de semitis antiquis, quae sit via bona, et ambulate in ea, et invenietis refrigerium animabus vestris ». Haec enim sunt quae Dominus docet Isaiae quadragesimo octavo[^p266-10]: « Ego Dominus docens te utilia ».

45. (Vers. 26.). Secundo vero quantum ad *inventionem veritatis quaesitae* subdit: *At ille dixit ad eum: In Lege quid scriptum est? quomodo legis?* Ex quo apparet, quod Magister veritatis erat amicus Legis, iuxta illud Matthaei quinto[^p266-11]: « Putatis, quia veni solvere Legem aut Prophetas? Non veni solvere, sed adimplere ». In quo verbo Salvator *captat benevolentiam* legisperiti in hoc, quod ostendit, se conformari ei in amore Legis; *suscitat* etiam *attentionem,* dum mittit ad Legem sicut ad scripturam authenticam; *praeparat* etiam *docilitatem,* dum tribuit viam ad veritatem inveniendam, et hoc quidem fit legendo legem divinam. Unde Ioannis quinto[^p266-12] dixit legisperitis: « Scrutamini Scripturas, in quibus putatis vitam aeternam habere ». Haec enim est via veniendi ad veritatem et per veritatem ad vitam. Unde Baruch quarto: « Hic liber mandatorum Dei et lex, quae est in aeternum; omnes, qui tenent eam, pervenient ad vitam; qui autem dereliquerint eam, in mortem. Convertere, Iacob, et apprehende eam » etc.

46. (Vers. 27.). Et ista via eum ad veritatem manuduxit; propter quod additur: *Ille autem*[^p266-13]
<!-- page 267 -->
*respondens dixit: Diliges Dominum Deum tuum ex toto corde tuo et ex tota anima tua et ex omnibus viribus tuis et ex omni mente tua.* Hoc legitur Deuteronomii sexto: « Diliges Dominum Deum tuum ex toto corde tuo » etc. In hoc mandato legislator omnia praecepta reduxit ad unum, in quo verbo clauditur tota forma vivendi, iuxta illud quod dicitur Matthaei vigesimo secundo: « In his duobus mandatis universa Lex pendet et Prophetae ».

47. Sed videtur contrarietas, quia hic dicitur, quod *legisperitus* respondit; in Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p267-1] dicitur, quod *Christus;* hic ponuntur *quatuor* conditiones, ibi *tres.* — Sed patet, quod alia fuit ista quaestio, alia illa, sicut innuit Augustinus de Concordia Evangelistarum[^p267-2], ubi dicit, quod Christus primo respondit, secundum quod dicit Matthaeus, et deinde legisperitus iterando approbavit, sicut dicit Lucas, et quod utrumque fuerit, narratur Marci duodecimo. Et sic patet, quod Marcus hos duos ducit ad concordiam.

48. Sed tamen adhuc videtur remanere discordia ex parte *conditionum diligendi,* quia Deuteronomii sexto[^p267-3] dicitur: « Diliges Dominum Deum tuum ex toto corde tuo et ex tota anima tua et ex tota fortitudine tua »; hic autem additur: *Ex omni mente tua,* et in Matthaeo omittitur: *Ex omnibus viribus tuis.*

Ad quod intelligendum, quod ad perfectum modum diligendi Deum requiritur, quod referamus in ipsum omnes *cogitationes,* omnes *affectiones* et omnes *operationes.* Et haec tria tanguntur in Lege[^p267-4]. Sed quia *cogitationes* sunt intellectivae cordis et memorativae mentis; ideo hic et in Matthaeo additur: *Ex tota mente,* in quo est explicatio Legis. Et quia qui refert quod interius est, scilicet actum memoriae, intelligentiae et voluntatis, in Deum, per consequens refert opera[^p267-5]; ideo in his tribus clauditur relatio *omnium virium.* Lucas autem ad maiorem explicationem addit hoc ipsum, scilicet: *Ex omnibus viribus;* similiter Marcus[^p267-6]. — Vel hoc quod est *ex omnibus viribus* est determinatio trium aliarum conditionum, ut secundum Augustinum[^p267-7] sit sensus: *Diliges Dominum Deum tuum ex toto corde tuo,* scilicet intellectu sine errore; *ex tota anima,* id est voluntate sine contradictione; *ex omni mente,* id est memoria sine oblivione; *et ex omnibus viribus tuis,* id est tota virtute sine fictione. — Vel secundum Bernardum[^p267-8]: « *Ex toto corde tuo,* id est sapienter; *ex tota anima,* id est dulciter; *ex tota mente,* id est fortiter »; et in hoc clauditur *ex omnibus viribus.* — Vel sic: *Ex toto corde,* id est vigilanter, secundum illud Canticorum quinto[^p267-9]: « Ego dormio, et cor meum vigilat »; *ex tota anima,* id est ardenter, secundum illud Canticorum quinto: « Anima mea liquefacta est, ut dilectus locutus est »; *ex omnibus viribus tuis,* id est constanter, secundum illud Canticorum ultimo: « Fortis est ut mors dilectio, dura sicut infernus aemulatio; lampades eius, lampades ignis atque flammarum; aquae multae non potuerunt exstinguere caritatem, nec flumina obruent illam »; *ex omni mente tua,* id est incessanter, secundum illud Deuteronomii octavo[^p267-10]: « Observa et cave, ne quando obliviscaris Domini Dei tui »; et Tobiae quarto: « In omnibus diebus vitae tuae in
<!-- page 268 -->
mente habeto Deum ». Et sic patet, quod tertia et quarta conditio sunt valde propinquae, et ideo una clauditur aliquando in alia.

In omnibus autem praedictis conditionibus intelligendum, quod secundum quod dicunt *totalitatem sufficientiae,* sic cadunt sub praecepto et spectant ad statum viae; secundum quod dicunt *totalitatem consummationis,* sic spectant ad statum patriae et magis demonstrant, quo tendendum, quam obligent ad observandum[^p268-1]. Per hoc ergo mandatum totus homo secundum se totum ordinatur in Deum, et per consequens ad se ipsum.

49. Et quia ordinari habet etiam ad proximum, ideo subdit: *Et proximum tuum,* supple: dilige, *sicut te ipsum.* Hoc secundum praeceptum habetur ex Levitici decimo nono[^p268-2], ubi dicitur: « Diliges amicum tuum sicut te ipsum », secundum translationem Septuaginta dicitur: Diliges proximum sicut te ipsum, id est *ex quo* te ipsum, scilicet ex caritatis affectu et effectu; vel *in quo* te ipsum, id est in Deo; vel *propter quod* te ipsum, id est propter Deum; vel *ad quod* te ipsum, id est ad gratiam in praesenti et gloriam in futuro; vel *quomodo* te ipsum, id est supra res et corpus proprium et infra Deum. Nam qui sic diligit proximum, ille est verus observator Legis; unde ad Romanos decimo tertio[^p268-3]: « Qui diligit proximum Legem implevit ». — Et vere debet diligere proximum quasi se ipsum propter *conformitatem naturae;* unde Ecclesiastici decimo tertio[^p268-4]: « Omne animal diligit simile sibi, sic et omnis homo proximum sibi »; propter etiam *unitatem gratiae,* secundum illud ad Ephesios quarto: « Loquimini veritatem, unusquisque cum proximo suo, quoniam sumus invicem membra »; propter *unitatem mercedis aeternae;* ad Ephesios quarto: « Unum corpus et unus spiritus, sicut vocati estis in una spe vocationis vestrae ». — Ideo ad commendandam hanc dilectionem voluit Deus, quod nasceremur ex uno patre Adam; quod redimeremur eodem sanguine, scilicet sanguine Domini nostri Iesu Christi; quod remuneraremur eadem mercede; Psalmus[^p268-5]: « Ierusalem, quae aedificatur ut civitas, cuius participatio eius in idipsum ».

50. (Vers. 28.). Tertio vero quantum ad *approbationem veritatis inventae* subditur: *Dixitque illi: Recte respondisti;* quia verbum rectitudinis protulisti, quod est dilectionis, secundum illud Canticorum primo[^p268-6]: « Recti diligunt te »; vel secundum regulam sapientiae dixisti, secundum illud Proverbiorum octavo: « Iusti sunt omnes sermones tui, non est in eis pravum quid neque perversum, recti sunt intelligentibus et aequi invenientibus scientiam ». — Et quia secundum regulam rectitudinis est vivendum, ideo subdit: *Hoc fac, et vives,* quia, secundum illud Iacobi primo[^p268-7], « si quis est auditor verbi et non factor » etc.; et ad Romanos secundo: « Non auditores legis iusti sunt apud Deum, sed factores legis iustificabuntur ». Et vere *vives,* quia ad aeternam vitam pervenies; quia Matthaei decimo nono[^p268-8]: « Si vis ad vitam ingredi, serva mandata »; unde Ioannis decimo tertio: « Si haec scitis, beati eritis, si feceritis ea ».

51. (Vers. 29.). Quarto quantum ad *investigationem veritatis approbatae* subditur: *Ille autem, volens iustificare se ipsum,* id est iustum ostendere, « quia, ut dicit *Interlinearis*[^p268-9], putabat non ante Deum, sed ante hominem se stare », secundum illud infra decimo sexto: « Vos estis, qui iustificatis vos coram hominibus, Deus autem novit corda vestra » etc. — Vel, *volens se ipsum iustificare,* id est ad iustitiam praeparare; quod fit per fidem et intelligentiam veritatis, secundum illud ad Romanos tertio[^p268-10]: « Arbitramur autem, hominem iustificari per fidem sine operibus Legis »; ideo, ut veritatem traditam intelligat, requirit. — Propter quod subditur: *Dixit ad Iesum: Et quis est meus proximus?* ut intelligam mandatum, secundum illud Osee decimo[^p268-11]: « Tempus requirendi Dominum, cum venerit qui docebit vos iustitiam ». Ideo quaerit de *proximo* diligenter, quia in Scriptura dicitur multipliciter, scilicet *cognatione,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p268-12]: « Amici mei et proximi mei adversum me appropinquarunt et steterunt »; et rursus: « Quasi proximum et quasi fratrem nostrum, sic complacebam » etc. *Religione;* Ecclesiastici decimo quinto[^p268-13]: « Exaltabit illum apud proximos suos ». *Compassione* sive beneficii exhibitione, secundum quod infra eodem: « Quis tibi videtur proximus fuisse ei qui incidit in latrones? At ille dixit: Qui fecit misericordiam cum illo ». *Naturali assimilatione,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici
<!-- page 269 -->
decimo tertio: « Omne animal diligit simile sibi, sic et omnis homo proximum sibi ». Et Augustinus[^p269-1]: « Nomine *proximi* intelligitur omnis homo ». — Et ideo, quia dubium erat, et multiplex erat proximi intelligentia, propterea indagandum erat et requirendum non solum propter illum legisperitum, sed propter utilitatem fidelium, a quibus implendum erat praemissum mandatum, quod est consummatio mandatorum[^p269-2].

52. *Suspiciens*[^p269-3] *autem Iesus, dixit.* Post praeceptum regulans ad vivendum subditur *documentum excitans ad intelligendum,* et hoc per dictum parabolicum; et hoc notat, cum dicit: *Suspiciens autem Iesus;* in quo ostendit, quod qui vult intelligere veritatem debet sursum aspicere, unde micat verum lumen; Isaiae sexagesimo[^p269-4]: « Surge, illuminare, Ierusalem » etc.; quia, Ecclesiastici primo, « fons sapientiae verbum Dei in excelsis ». In hoc autem dicto parabolico quatuor introducuntur. Primum est *persona indigens ex miseria;* secundum est *persona despiciens ex duritia,* ibi[^p269-5]: *Accidit autem, ut sacerdos* etc.; tertium est *persona subveniens ex clementia,* ibi: *Samaritanus autem* etc.; quartum est *doctrina subsequens ex parabola,* ibi: *Quis horum trium videtur* etc.

Circa *personam indigentem ex miseria* duo introducuntur ab Evangelista: primum est *subtractio boni,* secundum est *inflictio mali.*

53. (Vers. 30.). Primo igitur quantum ad *boni subtractionem* dicit: *Homo quidam descendebat ab Ierusalem in Iericho et incidit in latrones, qui etiam despoliaverunt eum.* Ideo fortassis hoc fecerunt, quia *solus erat;* quod notat in hoc quod dicit: *Homo quidam,* ut verum appareat illud Ecclesiastae quarto[^p269-6]: « Vae soli! quia, cum ceciderit, non habet *sublevantem* », immo potius talis habet *depraedantes.* Unde iste homo poterat dicere illud Iob decimo nono: « Spoliavit me gloria mea et abstulit coronam de capite meo ». « Simul venerunt latrones et fecerunt sibi viam per me ». In hos latrones incidit fortassis propter *solitudinem viae,* ubi consueverunt latrones latere, secundum illud Ieremiae tertio[^p269-7]: « In viis sedebas, exspectans eos quasi latro in solitudine ».

54. Secundo quantum ad *mali inflictionem* subdit: *Et plagis impositis, abierunt, semivivo relicto;* quasi dicat, quod usque ad mortem eum *vulneraverunt,* secundum quod de latronibus dicitur Proverbiorum primo[^p269-8]: « Pedes eorum ad malum currunt et festinant, ut effundant sanguinem »; et in Psalmo: « Quorum os maledictione et amaritudine plenum est, veloces pedes eorum ad effundendum sanguinem ». Non solum *verberaverunt,* ne forte proderet eos, sicut dicitur de Ismael Ieremiae quadragesimo primo[^p269-9], quod « viros venientes ad Godoliam interfecit Ismael, filius Nathaniae, ipse et viri, qui erant cum eo circa medium laci »; et hoc, ne proderent eum, sicut et hic.

55. (Vers. 31.). *Accidit autem, ut sacerdos* etc. Post personam indigentem ex duplici miseria subditur hic *persona despiciens ex duritia.* Introducitur autem hic duplex persona, *superior* scilicet et *inferior* in dignitate ecclesiastica. — Primo igitur quantum ad *personam superiorem* dicit: *Accidit autem, ut sacerdos quidam descenderet eadem via,* cuius erat legem Dei docere et facere, secundum illud Malachiae secundo[^p269-10]: « Labia sacerdotis custodient scientiam, et legem requirent de ore eius ». Ipsi autem debebant esse sancti, secundum illud Levitici decimo: « Sanctificabor in his qui appropinquant mihi, et in conspectu omnis populi glorificabor ». Unde sacerdotum erat potissime legem Dei servare et maxime quoad misericordiam; sed tamen hic non servavit propter duritiam. — Unde subdit: *Et viso illo, praeterivit,* non attendens illud Deuteronomii vigesimo secundo[^p269-11]: « Si videris, *asinum fratris* tui aut bovem cecidisse in via, non despicias, sed sublevabis cum eo ». Iste *despexit* istum *fratrem* propter avaritiam. Iam verificatum erat illud Ieremiae sexto[^p269-12]: « A minori usque ad maiorem omnes avaritiae student, et a propheta usque ad sacerdotem cuncti faciunt dolum »; et similiter illud Isaiae primo: « Principes tui infideles, socii furum » etc.

56. (Vers. 32.). Secundo quantum ad *personam inferiorem* subdit: *Similiter et levita, cum esset secus locum et videret eum, pertransiit;* et hic non attendebat illud Ecclesiastici septimo[^p269-13]: « Non desis
<!-- page 270 -->
plorantibus in consolatione et cum lugentibus ambula; non te pigeat visitare infirmum ». Hunc piguit, quia, cum esset secus locum et videret, noluit visitare. Iste non erat similis illi Tobiae, quarto[^p270-1], docenti filium suum: « Noli avertere faciem tuam ab ullo paupere ». Hic autem a paupere nudo, vulnerato et de populo suo avertit oculos suos. Et hoc erat magis reprehensibile in sacerdotibus et levitis quam in aliis personis; propter quod dicitur Osee sexto[^p270-2]: « Misericordiam volui et non sacrificium, et scientiam Dei plus quam holocausta »; unde iste legisperitus dicitur dixisse Marci duodecimo: « Diligere proximum tanquam se ipsum maius est omnibus holocautomatibus et sacrificiis ».

57. (Vers. 33.). *Samaritanus autem* etc. Hic tertio loco subditur *persona subveniens ex clementia.* Circa quam duplex effectus misericordiae ostenditur, scilicet *supportatio infirmitatis* et *relevatio mendicitatis.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *misericordiam supportantem infirmitatem* dicit: *Samaritanus autem quidam iter faciens venit secus eum*[^p270-3] *et,* oculo suae benignitatis ac opere maximae pietatis, *videns eum, misericordia motus est,* ita ut posset dicere illud Iob trigesimo: « Flebam quondam super eo qui afflictus erat, et compatiebatur anima mea pauperi »; et rursus trigesimo primo: « Ab infantia mea crevit mecum miseratio ».

58. (Vers. 34.). Et quia vera misericordia apparet in opere, ideo subdit: *Et appropinquans,* per effectum, cui erat proximus per affectum, revocans ad unitatem[^p270-4], *alligavit vulnera eius, infundens oleum et vinum,* ut *oleum* leniret dolorem, et *vinum* purgaret, et *alligatio* conservaret. Et hoc opus erat medici boni, secundum illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo[^p270-5]: « In his curans mitigabit dolorem, et unguentarius faciet pigmenta suavitatis et unctiones conficiet sanitatis »; Proverbiorum vigesimo primo: « Thesaurus desiderabilis et oleum in habitaculo iusti ». — Et quia non solum indigebat medicamento, verum etiam vehiculo et hospitio et cibo; ideo subdit: *Et imponens illum in iumentum suum,* quantum ad vehiculum, *duxit in stabulum,* quantum ad hospitium, *et curam eius egit,* quantum ad cibum. Et sic implevit illud Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo[^p270-6]: « Frange esurienti panem tuum et egenos vagosque induc in domum tuam » etc.

59. (Vers. 35.). Secundo quantum ad *misericordiam relevantem mendicitatem* subdit: *Et altera die protulit duos denarios et dedit stabulario.* Parvam pecuniam protulit, quia fortassis parvam habebat, secundum illud Tobiae quarto[^p270-7]: « Quomodo potueris, esto misericors. Si multum tibi fuerit, abundanter tribue; si autem exiguum, etiam exiguum libenter impertiri stude ». — Et quia, quamvis modica esset pecunia, magna erat subveniendi voluntas; ideo subdit: *Et ait: Curam illius habe, et quodcumque supererogaveris, ego, cum rediero, reddam tibi.* Ex quo apparet, quod misericordia eius erat integra, non sicut illorum, de quibus Osee sexto[^p270-8] dicitur: « Misericordia vestra sicut nubes matutina et sicut ros mane pertransiens ». De isto autem poterat vere dici illud Psalmi: « Tota die miseretur et commodat, et semen illius in benedictione erit », et vere, quia, secundum quod dicitur Proverbiorum decimo nono, « foeneratur Domino qui miseretur pauperis, et vicissitudinem suam reddet ei ».

60. (Vers. 36. 37.). *Quis horum trium videtur* etc. Hic quarto loco subiungitur *doctrina subsequens ex parabola,* et hoc quantum ad duo: primo quantum ad *eruditionem intellectus,* secundo vero quantum ad *exhortationem affectus.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *eruditionem intellectus* dicitur: *Quis horum trium videtur tibi proximus fuisse illi qui incidit in latrones?* Hoc ab eo quaerit, ut ex ore eius eliciat veram sententiam; quod et fecit. — Unde sequitur: *At ille dixit: Qui fecit misericordiam in illum.* Sed illi duo erant eiusdem gentis, iste alienigena; ergo *proximi* nomen non solum extenditur ad propinquos, verum etiam ad extraneos. Unde Beda[^p270-9]: « Iuxta litteram patet, quod alienigena Ierosolymitae, cui misericordiam impendit, proximior fuit quam sacerdos et levita gentis eiusdem ». Patet etiam, quod *proximitas* magis attenditur penes naturalem dilectionem et compassionem quam penes carnalem cognationem. Unde Ambrosius[^p270-10]: « Cognatio non facit proximum, sed misericordia, quae misericordia secundum naturam est. Nihil enim tam secundum naturam est quam naturae iuvare consortem ». Patet etiam ex hoc, quod « nomine *proximi* intelligitur omnis, qui misericordia indiget, vel qui misericordiam impendere potest », et per hoc « omnis homo[^p270-11] ». Et ita illuminatus est intellectus legisperiti mirabili manuductione. Si
<!-- page 271 -->
enim hoc Dominus dixisset absoluta responsione, ille minime credidisset. Ideo Dominus sagacissime ex ore ipsius veritatem extorsit et eum ad veritatem informavit magis per modum interrogandi quam per modum sententiandi. In quo dat formam, qualiter superbis debeat satisfieri.

61. Secundo vero quantum ad *exhortationem affectus* subditur: *Et ait illi Iesus: Vade et tu fac similiter,* ut omni homini misericordiam impendas, si vis esse vere misericors, quia, Ecclesiastici decimo octavo[^p271-1], « misericordia Dei super omnem carnem », id est misericordia, quae ex Deo procedit. Dei namque misericordia ad omnes se extendit; unde supra sexto: « Eritis filii Altissimi, quia ipse benignus est super ingratos et malos. Estote ergo misericordes, sicut et Pater vester caelestis misericors est ». — Vera etiam misericordia non est tantum in affectu, verum etiam in *effectu;* ideo dicit: *Et tu fac similiter.* Unde ad Galatas sexto[^p271-2]: « Bonum facientes, non deficiamus; tempore enim suo metemus, non deficientes. Ergo dum tempus habemus, operemur bonum ad omnes ». Et in hoc adimpletur lex Dei, quando non solum verbo et animo, verum etiam facto proximus quicumque indigens supportatur, secundum illud ad Galatas sexto[^p271-3]: « Alter alterius onera portate, et sic adimplebitis legem Christi », quae consistit in dilectione proximi; et « qui diligit proximum legem implevit », ad Romanos decimo tertio. — Sic igitur patet secundum sensum *litteralem* doctrina elicita ex parabola.

62. Alia etiam potest elici secundum *sensum spiritualem,* ut *persona indigens* ex miseria intelligatur *genus humanum,* quod in Adam peccante *descendit ab Ierusalem in Iericho,* id est a paradiso in mundum, et *incidit in latrones,* id est in potestatem daemonum, qui *spoliaverunt* eum gratuitis et *vulneraverunt* in naturalibus. Et *reliquerunt semivivum,* quia, subtracta *similitudine,* remansit sola *imago;* ut vere possit dici illud Psalmi[^p271-4]: « Homo, cum in honore esset, non intellexit; comparatus est iumentis insipientibus » etc.; et rursus: « Verumtamen in imagine pertransit homo ». Illa tamen *imago* est *exspoliata* propter aversionem et *vulnerata* propter conversionem, secundum illud Ieremiae secundo[^p271-5]: « Duo mala fecit populus meus: me dereliquerunt fontem aquae vivae et foderunt sibi cisternas dissipatas ». Ideo *spoliatur* et *vulneratur,* secundum illud Threnorum ultimo: « Cecidit corona capitis nostri; vae nobis! quia peccavimus. Propterea moestum factum est cor nostrum, ideo contenebrati sunt oculi nostri ».

63. *Persona* autem *pertransiens cum duritia* fuit *legalis iustitia,* quae nec misericordiam nec medicinam praestabat. Quod bene signatum est Exodi decimo septimo[^p271-6], ubi dicitur, quod « manus Moysi erant graves »; unde in Glossa Bedae: « *Sacerdos* legem Dei annuntiat; descendit quidem Lex in mundum per Moysen et nullam sanitatem contulit homini. Descendit *levita,* qui typum ostendit Prophetarum, sed et hic nullum sanat, quia Lex peccata arguit, sed pertransit, quia indulgentiam non largitur ». Et illud bene designatum fuit quarti Regum quarto[^p271-7] in baculo Elisei, quem misit ad suscitandum puerum, « et tamen non fuit vox neque sensus »; unde potius erat baculus castigans quam sanans. Unde dicitur ad Hebraeos decimo: « Irritam quis faciens legem Moysi, sine ulla miseratione duobus vel tribus testibus moritur ».

64. *Persona* autem *subveniens ex misericordia* recte intelligitur Christus Dominus, quia *Samaritanus* interpretatur custos[^p271-8]; ipse autem est, de quo in Psalmo: « Nisi Dominus custodierit civitatem » etc.; et Isaiae vigesimo primo: « Custos, quid de nocte »? quia « ecce, non dormitabit neque dormiet qui custodit Israel ». — Hic *venit secus vulneratum,* quia « in similitudinem hominum factus et habitu inventus ut homo », ad Philippenses secundo[^p271-9]. Venit, inquam, « in similitudinem carnis peccati », sicut dicitur ad Romanos octavo. — Et veniens tria facit homini vulnerato: adhibuit *medicamentum,* et hoc per *gratiam Sacramentorum,* in quibus fiunt illinitiones et inunctiones sanantes vulnera peccatorum,
<!-- page 272 -->
quod non fiebat ante Salvatoris adventum, secundum illud Isaiae primo[^p272-1]: « Vulnus et livor et plaga tumens non est circumligata nec curata medicamine neque fota oleo ». Sed hoc factum est post Christi adventum; unde primae Petri secundo: « Qui peccata nostra ipse pertulit in corpore suo super lignum, in livore eius sanati sumus ». — Adhibuit etiam *vehiculum,* et hoc per *gratiam virtutum et donorum,* per quam vehimur in *stabulum* Ecclesiae, secundum illud Psalmi[^p272-2]: « Beatus vir, cuius est auxilium abs te; ascensiones in corde suo disposuit, in valle lacrymarum »; et post: « Etenim benedictionem dabit legislator, ibunt de virtute in virtutem ». Et hoc quidem fit in *stabulo* Ecclesiae; unde subditur: « Elegi abiectus esse in domo Dei » etc.; et post: « Quia misericordiam et veritatem diligit Deus, gratiam et gloriam dabit Dominus ». — Adhibuit *nutrimentum,* quod fit per *doctrinam duorum testamentorum,* quae intelligitur in prolatione *duorum denariorum,* quos *dedit stabulario,* id est praelato, cuius est doctrinam Christi ministrare et explanando *supererogare;* unde Ecclesiastici quadragesimo quinto[^p272-3]: « Dedit Moysi Dominus cor ad praecepta et legem vitae et disciplinae, docere Iacob testamentum suum et iudicia sua Israel ». His Dominus *reddet,* cum redierit in die iudicii, *omnia supererogata;* quoniam, secundum quod dicitur Danielis duodecimo, « qui ad iustitiam erudiunt multos, quasi stellae in perpetuas aeternitates »; Matthaei vigesimo quinto: « Euge, serve bone et fidelis, quia super pauca fuisti fidelis, super multa te constituam » etc.

#### The second part treats of the form of living given to the disciples, in two ways.

43. *And behold a certain lawyer,* etc. After the Evangelist has described the form of preaching, here secondly he intimates *the form of living*[^p266-3]. Now one can be formed for living rightly in two ways, namely by *word* and by *example;* therefore the Evangelist first puts forward for the formation *the divine precept;* and secondly he subjoins *the human example,* there[^p266-4]: *Now it came to pass as they went, he entered,* etc.

#### For the formation the divine precept is put forward, proposed in two ways.

Now since *the formation of the precept* avails nothing except to one who understands, therefore first *the precept regulating* for living is put forward; and secondly *the instruction helping* for understanding is added, there[^p266-5]: *And Jesus, looking up.* — Now concerning the expression of *the regulative precept* four things are brought in, namely *the inquiry into* the saving *truth,* the *finding of the truth* inquired into, the *approbation of the truth* found, the *investigation of the truth* approved.

44. (Vers. 25.). First, then, as to *the inquiry into the saving truth* he says: *And behold a certain lawyer stood up, tempting him.* This man *was* learned and *reputed* himself learned; and because he *reputed* himself learned, therefore he did not ask in order to learn, but in order to try, as is the custom of the proud, according to that of Ecclesiasticus thirteen[^p266-6] of the rich man: « By much talk he will tempt thee ». But again, because he *was* learned, therefore he asked fittingly and rightly; on account of which he adds: *And saying: Master, what must I do to possess eternal life?* He calls him *Master,* because by this very fact it belongs to him to answer the things asked, according to what is said in Matthew twenty-two[^p266-7]: « Master, we know that thou art a true speaker, and teachest the way of God in truth ». Whence, because Christ professed the teaching office of salvation, therefore he puts a question about the saving truth. The young man in Matthew nineteen[^p266-8] is said to have proposed a similar question: « Good master, what good shall I do that I may have life everlasting »? These men inquired studiously, not curiously, because not about works done, but about works to be done for the merit of salvation, according to that of Ecclesiasticus three[^p266-9]: « In many of his works be not curious; but the things that God hath commanded thee, think on them always ». Whence these men ask about the way of going to life, according to the counsel of Jeremias six: « Stand ye on the ways, and see, and ask for the old paths which is the good way, and walk ye in it: and you shall find refreshment for your souls ». For these are the things which the Lord teaches in Isaias forty-eight[^p266-10]: « I am the Lord that teach thee profitable things ».

45. (Vers. 26.). Secondly, as to *the finding of the truth inquired into,* he adds: *But he said to him: What is written in the law? how readest thou?* From which it appears that the Master of truth was a friend of the Law, according to that of Matthew five[^p266-11]: « Do you think that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets? I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil ». In which word the Saviour *captures the good will* of the lawyer, in that he shows that he is conformed to him in the love of the Law; he *rouses* also his *attention,* while he sends him to the Law as to an authentic scripture; he *prepares* also his *docility,* while he grants a way to the finding of the truth, and this indeed is done by reading the divine law. Whence in John five[^p266-12] he said to the lawyers: « Search the scriptures, for you think in them to have life everlasting ». For this is the way of coming to the truth and through the truth to life. Whence Baruch four: « This is the book of the commandments of God, and the law, that is for ever: all they that keep it, shall come to life: but they that have forsaken it, to death. Return, O Jacob, and take hold of it », etc.

46. (Vers. 27.). And this way led him by the hand to the truth; on account of which it is added: *But he*[^p266-13]
<!-- page 267 -->
*answering, said: Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with thy whole heart, and with thy whole soul, and with all thy strength, and with all thy mind.* This is read in Deuteronomy six: « Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with thy whole heart », etc. In this commandment the lawgiver reduced all the precepts to one, in which word the whole form of living is enclosed, according to that which is said in Matthew twenty-two: « On these two commandments dependeth the whole law and the prophets ».

47. But there seems to be a contrariety, because here it is said that *the lawyer* answered; in Matthew twenty-two[^p267-1] it is said that *Christ* did; here *four* conditions are set down, there *three.* — But it is plain that that question was one, and this another, as Augustine intimates in *On the Harmony of the Evangelists*[^p267-2], where he says that Christ answered first, according to what Matthew says, and afterwards the lawyer, repeating it, approved it, as Luke says; and that both took place is narrated in Mark twelve. And so it is plain that Mark brings these two into concord.

48. But nevertheless a discord seems still to remain on the part of *the conditions of loving,* because in Deuteronomy six[^p267-3] it is said: « Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with thy whole heart, and with thy whole soul, and with thy whole strength »; but here is added: *With all thy mind,* and in Matthew is omitted: *With all thy strength.*

For the understanding of which, that for the perfect mode of loving God it is required that we refer to him all our *thoughts,* all our *affections* and all our *operations.* And these three are touched on in the Law[^p267-4]. But because *thoughts* belong to the intellective power of the heart and the memorative power of the mind; therefore here and in Matthew is added: *With all thy mind,* in which there is an explication of the Law. And because he who refers what is within, namely the act of memory, of intelligence and of will, to God, consequently refers his works[^p267-5]; therefore in these three is enclosed the referring of *all one's strength.* But Luke, for greater explication, adds this very thing, namely: *With all thy strength;* and likewise Mark[^p267-6]. — Or that which is *with all thy strength* is the determination of the three other conditions, so that according to Augustine[^p267-7] the sense is: *Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with thy whole heart,* that is, with the understanding without error; *with thy whole soul,* that is, with the will without contradiction; *with all thy mind,* that is, with the memory without forgetfulness; *and with all thy strength,* that is, with all one's power without feigning. — Or according to Bernard[^p267-8]: « *With thy whole heart,* that is, wisely; *with thy whole soul,* that is, sweetly; *with all thy mind,* that is, strongly »; and in this is enclosed *with all thy strength.* — Or thus: *With thy whole heart,* that is, watchfully, according to that of the Canticle five[^p267-9]: « I sleep, and my heart watcheth »; *with thy whole soul,* that is, ardently, according to that of the Canticle five: « My soul melted when he spoke »; *with all thy strength,* that is, constantly, according to that of the last of the Canticle: « Dilection is strong as death, jealousy as hard as hell, the lamps thereof are lamps of fire and flames; many waters cannot quench charity, neither can the floods drown it »; *with all thy mind,* that is, unceasingly, according to that of Deuteronomy eight[^p267-10]: « Take heed and beware lest at any time thou forget the Lord thy God »; and Tobias four: « All the days of thy life have God in
<!-- page 268 -->
thy mind ». And so it is plain that the third and fourth condition are very near, and therefore one is sometimes enclosed in the other.

Now in all the aforesaid conditions it is to be understood that, according as they express *the totality of sufficiency,* so they fall under precept and look to the state of the way; according as they express *the totality of consummation,* so they look to the state of the fatherland, and show rather whither one must tend than oblige to observance[^p268-1]. Through this commandment, then, the whole man according to his whole self is ordered to God, and consequently to himself.

49. And because he has to be ordered also to his neighbour, therefore he adds: *And thy neighbour,* supply: love, *as thyself.* This second precept is had from Leviticus nineteen[^p268-2], where it is said: « Thou shalt love thy friend as thyself »; according to the translation of the Seventy it is said: Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself, that is, *from that from which* thou lovest thyself, namely from the affection and effect of charity; or *in that in which* thou lovest thyself, that is, in God; or *for that for which* thou lovest thyself, that is, for God; or *unto that unto which* thou lovest thyself, that is, unto grace in the present and glory in the future; or *in the way in which* thou lovest thyself, that is, above things and one's own body and below God. For he who so loves his neighbour, he is the true observer of the Law; whence to the Romans thirteen[^p268-3]: « He that loveth his neighbour hath fulfilled the law ». — And truly he ought to love his neighbour as himself on account of *the conformity of nature;* whence Ecclesiasticus thirteen[^p268-4]: « Every beast loveth its like: so also every man his neighbour »; on account also of *the unity of grace,* according to that to the Ephesians four: « Speak ye the truth every man with his neighbour; for we are members one of another »; on account of *the unity of the eternal reward;* to the Ephesians four: « One body and one Spirit; as you are called in one hope of your calling ». — Therefore, for commending this dilection, God willed that we should be born of one father, Adam; that we should be redeemed by the same blood, namely by the blood of our Lord Jesus Christ; that we should be rewarded with the same reward; the Psalm[^p268-5]: « Jerusalem, which is built as a city, which is compact together ».

50. (Vers. 28.). Thirdly, as to *the approbation of the truth found,* it is added: *And he said to him: Thou hast answered right;* because thou hast uttered the word of rectitude, which is the word of dilection, according to that of the Canticle one[^p268-6]: « The righteous love thee »; or thou hast spoken according to the rule of wisdom, according to that of Proverbs eight: « All my words are just, there is nothing wicked nor perverse in them. They are right to them that understand, and just to them that find knowledge ». — And because one must live according to the rule of rectitude, therefore he adds: *This do, and thou shalt live,* because, according to that of James one[^p268-7], « if a man be a hearer of the word, and not a doer », etc.; and to the Romans two: « Not the hearers of the law are just before God, but the doers of the law shall be justified ». And truly *thou shalt live,* because thou shalt come to eternal life; for Matthew nineteen[^p268-8]: « If thou wilt enter into life, keep the commandments »; whence John thirteen: « If you know these things, you shall be blessed if you do them ».

51. (Vers. 29.). Fourthly, as to *the investigation of the truth approved,* it is added: *But he, willing to justify himself,* that is, to show himself just, « because, as the *Interlinear* says[^p268-9], he thought that he stood not before God, but before man », according to that of below, sixteen: « You are they who justify yourselves before men, but God knoweth your hearts », etc. — Or, *willing to justify himself,* that is, to prepare himself for justice; which is done through faith and the intelligence of the truth, according to that to the Romans three[^p268-10]: « For we account a man to be justified by faith, without the works of the law »; therefore, in order that he may understand the truth handed down, he inquires. — On account of which it is added: *Said to Jesus: And who is my neighbour?* that I may understand the commandment, according to that of Osee ten[^p268-11]: « The time to seek the Lord, when he shall come that shall teach you justice ». Therefore he inquires diligently about *the neighbour,* because in Scripture it is said in many ways, namely by *kinship,* according to that of the Psalm[^p268-12]: « My friends and my neighbours have drawn near, and stood against me »; and again: « As a neighbour and as an own brother, so did I please », etc. By *religion;* Ecclesiasticus fifteen[^p268-13]: « She shall exalt him among his neighbours ». By *compassion,* or the showing of a benefit, according to what is below in the same: « Which of these three, in thy opinion, was neighbour to him that fell among the robbers? But he said: He that shewed mercy to him ». By *natural likening,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus
<!-- page 269 -->
thirteen: « Every beast loveth its like: so also every man his neighbour ». And Augustine[^p269-1]: « By the name of *neighbour* every man is understood ». — And therefore, because it was doubtful, and the intelligence of *neighbour* was manifold, on that account it was to be searched out and inquired into, not only for the sake of that lawyer, but for the profit of the faithful, by whom the aforesaid commandment was to be fulfilled, which is the consummation of the commandments[^p269-2].

52. *And Jesus*[^p269-3] *looking up, said.* After the precept regulating for living is added *the instruction stirring to understanding,* and this through a parabolic saying; and he notes this when he says: *And Jesus looking up;* in which he shows that he who wills to understand the truth must look upward, whence the true lumen[^tr-lumen] shines forth; Isaias sixty[^p269-4]: « Arise, be enlightened, O Jerusalem », etc.; because, Ecclesiasticus one, « the word of God on high is the fountain of wisdom ». Now in this parabolic saying four things are brought in. The first is *the person in need from misery;* the second is *the person despising from hardness,* there[^p269-5]: *And it chanced, that a certain priest,* etc.; the third is *the person succouring from clemency,* there: *But a certain Samaritan,* etc.; the fourth is *the teaching following from the parable,* there: *Which of these three, in thy opinion,* etc.

Concerning *the person in need from misery* two things are brought in by the Evangelist: the first is *the withdrawing of good,* the second is *the inflicting of evil.*

53. (Vers. 30.). First, then, as to *the withdrawing of good* he says: *A certain man went down from Jerusalem to Jericho, and fell among robbers, who also stripped him.* Perhaps they did this because *he was alone;* which he notes in this that he says: *A certain man,* so that that of Ecclesiastes four[^p269-6] may appear true: « Woe to him that is alone, for when he falleth, he hath none to *lift him up* », nay rather such a one has *plunderers.* Whence this man could have said that of Job nineteen: « He hath stripped me of my glory, and hath taken the crown from my head ». « The robbers have come together, and have made themselves a way by me ». He fell among these robbers perhaps on account of *the solitude of the way,* where robbers were accustomed to lie hid, according to that of Jeremias three[^p269-7]: « Thou didst sit in the ways, waiting for them as a robber in the solitude ».

54. Secondly, as to *the inflicting of evil* he adds: *And having wounded him went away, leaving him half dead;* as if he should say that they *wounded* him even to death, according to what is said of robbers in Proverbs one[^p269-8]: « Their feet run to evil, and make haste to shed blood »; and in the Psalm: « Their mouth is full of cursing and bitterness; their feet are swift to shed blood ». They did not only *beat* him, lest perchance he should betray them, as is said of Ismahel in Jeremias forty-one[^p269-9], that « Ismahel the son of Nathanias killed the men that came to Godolias, he and the men that were with him, in the midst of the pit »; and this, lest they should betray him, as also here.

55. (Vers. 31.). *And it chanced, that a certain priest,* etc. After the person in need from a twofold misery is added here *the person despising from hardness.* Now a twofold person is brought in here, *a higher* namely and *a lower* in ecclesiastical dignity. — First, then, as to *the higher person* he says: *And it chanced, that a certain priest went down the same way,* whose part it was to teach and to do the law of God, according to that of Malachias two[^p269-10]: « The lips of the priest shall keep knowledge, and they shall seek the law at his mouth ». And they were bound to be holy, according to that of Leviticus ten: « I will be sanctified in them that approach to me, and I will be glorified in the sight of all the people ». Whence it belonged chiefly to priests to keep the law of God, and most of all as to mercy; but nevertheless this man did not keep it, on account of hardness. — Whence he adds: *And seeing him, passed by,* not attending to that of Deuteronomy twenty-two[^p269-11]: « If thou see thy *brother's ass* or his ox to be fallen down in the way, thou shalt not despise it, but shalt lift it up with him ». This man *despised* this *brother* on account of avarice. Already that of Jeremias six[^p269-12] had been verified: « From the least even to the greatest, all are given to avarice: and from the prophet even to the priest, all are guilty of deceit »; and likewise that of Isaias one: « Thy princes are faithless, companions of thieves », etc.

56. (Vers. 32.). Secondly, as to *the lower person* he adds: *In like manner also a Levite, when he was near the place and saw him, passed by;* and this man did not attend to that of Ecclesiasticus seven[^p269-13]: « Be not wanting
<!-- page 270 -->
in comforting them that weep, and walk with them that mourn; let it not irk thee to visit the sick ». It irked this man, because, when he was near the place and saw, he would not visit. This man was not like that Tobias, in four[^p270-1], teaching his son: « Turn not away thy face from any poor person ». But this man turned away his eyes from a poor man, naked, wounded and of his own people. And this was more reprehensible in priests and Levites than in other persons; on account of which it is said in Osee six[^p270-2]: « I desired mercy, and not sacrifice: and the knowledge of God more than holocausts »; whence this lawyer is said to have said in Mark twelve: « To love one's neighbour as one's self, is a greater thing than all holocausts and sacrifices ».

57. (Vers. 33.). *But a certain Samaritan,* etc. Here in the third place is added *the person succouring from clemency.* Concerning whom a twofold effect of mercy is shown, namely *the bearing up of infirmity* and *the relieving of beggary.*

First, then, as to *the mercy bearing up infirmity* he says: *But a certain Samaritan being on his journey, came near him*[^p270-3] *and,* with the eye of his kindness and the work of the greatest piety, *seeing him, was moved with mercy,* so that he could say that of Job thirty: « I wept heretofore for him that was afflicted, and my soul had compassion on the poor »; and again in thirty-one: « From my infancy mercy grew up with me ».

58. (Vers. 34.). And because true mercy appears in the work, therefore he adds: *And drawing near,* by effect, to him to whom he was neighbour by affection, calling him back to unity[^p270-4], *bound up his wounds, pouring in oil and wine,* so that the *oil* might soothe the pain, and the *wine* cleanse, and the *binding* preserve. And this was the work of a good physician, according to that of Ecclesiasticus thirty-eight[^p270-5]: « By these, curing, he shall allay the pain, and the apothecary shall make sweet confections, and shall make up ointments of health »; Proverbs twenty-one: « There is a treasure to be desired, and oil in the dwelling of the just ». — And because he needed not only medicine, but also conveyance and lodging and food; therefore he adds: *And setting him upon his own beast,* as to the conveyance, *brought him to an inn,* as to the lodging, *and took care of him,* as to the food. And so he fulfilled that of Isaias fifty-eight[^p270-6]: « Deal thy bread to the hungry, and bring the needy and the harbourless into thy house », etc.

59. (Vers. 35.). Secondly, as to *the mercy relieving beggary* he adds: *And the next day he took out two pence, and gave to the host.* He took out little money, because perhaps he had little, according to that of Tobias four[^p270-7]: « According to thy ability be merciful. If thou have much give abundantly: if thou have little, take care even so to bestow willingly a little ». — And because, although the money was moderate, great was the will to succour; therefore he adds: *And said: Take care of him; and whatsoever thou shalt spend over and above, I, at my return, will repay thee.* From which it appears that his mercy was entire, not like that of those of whom it is said in Osee six[^p270-8]: « Your mercy is as a morning cloud, and as the dew that goeth away in the morning ». But of this man that of the Psalm could truly be said: « He sheweth mercy, and lendeth all the day long; and his seed shall be in blessing », and truly, because, according to what is said in Proverbs nineteen, « he that hath mercy on the poor, lendeth to the Lord: and he will repay him ».

60. (Vers. 36. 37.). *Which of these three, in thy opinion,* etc. Here in the fourth place is subjoined *the teaching following from the parable,* and this as to two things: first as to *the erudition of the understanding,* and secondly as to *the exhortation of the affection.*

First, then, as to *the erudition of the understanding* it is said: *Which of these three, in thy opinion, was neighbour to him that fell among the robbers?* This he asks of him, that from his own mouth he may draw out the true judgement; which he also did. — Whence there follows: *But he said: He that shewed mercy to him.* But those two were of the same nation, this man a foreigner; therefore the name of *neighbour* is extended not only to kinsmen, but also to strangers. Whence Bede[^p270-9]: « According to the letter it is plain that the foreigner was more a neighbour to the man of Jerusalem, to whom he showed mercy, than the priest and the Levite of the same nation ». It is plain also that *neighbourhood* is regarded more according to natural dilection and compassion than according to carnal kinship. Whence Ambrose[^p270-10]: « Kinship does not make a neighbour, but mercy, which mercy is according to nature. For nothing is so much according to nature as to help a partaker of nature ». It is plain also from this that « by the name of *neighbour* is understood everyone who needs mercy, or who can bestow mercy », and through this « every man[^p270-11] ». And so the understanding of the lawyer was enlightened by a marvellous leading by the hand. For
<!-- page 271 -->
if the Lord had said this by an absolute answer, he would by no means have believed it. Therefore the Lord most shrewdly wrung the truth out of his own mouth and formed him to the truth more by way of questioning than by way of pronouncing sentence. In which he gives the form of how the proud ought to be satisfied.

61. Secondly, as to *the exhortation of the affection,* it is added: *And Jesus said to him: Go, and do thou in like manner,* that thou mayest bestow mercy on every man, if thou wilt be truly merciful, because, Ecclesiasticus eighteen[^p271-1], « the mercy of God is upon all flesh », that is, the mercy which proceeds from God. For the mercy of God extends itself to all; whence above, six: « You shall be the sons of the Highest; for he is kind to the unthankful, and to the evil. Be ye therefore merciful, as your heavenly Father also is merciful ». — True mercy also is not only in affection, but also in *effect;* therefore he says: *And do thou in like manner.* Whence to the Galatians six[^p271-2]: « In doing good, let us not fail; for in due time we shall reap, not failing. Therefore, whilst we have time, let us work good to all men ». And in this the law of God is fulfilled, when not only by word and mind, but also by deed, any neighbour in need is borne up, according to that to the Galatians six[^p271-3]: « Bear ye one another's burdens; and so you shall fulfil the law of Christ », which consists in the dilection of the neighbour; and « he that loveth his neighbour hath fulfilled the law », to the Romans thirteen. — Thus, then, the teaching drawn out of the parable is plain according to the *literal* sense.

62. Another also can be drawn out according to *the spiritual sense,* so that by *the person in need* from misery is understood *the human race,* which in Adam sinning *went down from Jerusalem to Jericho,* that is, from paradise into the world, and *fell among robbers,* that is, into the power of the demons, who *stripped* it of the gratuitous gifts and *wounded* it in the natural ones. And *they left it half dead,* because, the *likeness* being withdrawn, the *image* alone remained; so that that of the Psalm[^p271-4] can truly be said: « And man when he was in honour did not understand; he is compared to senseless beasts », etc.; and again: « Surely man passeth as an image ». That *image,* however, is *despoiled* on account of the aversion and *wounded* on account of the conversion, according to that of Jeremias two[^p271-5]: « My people have done two evils: they have forsaken me, the fountain of living water, and have digged to themselves cisterns, broken cisterns ». Therefore it *is stripped* and *is wounded,* according to that of the last of Lamentations: « The crown is fallen from our head; woe to us, because we have sinned. Therefore is our heart sorrowful, therefore are our eyes become dim ».

63. Now *the person passing by with hardness* was *legal justice,* which supplied neither mercy nor medicine. Which is well signified in Exodus seventeen[^p271-6], where it is said that « Moses' hands were heavy »; whence in the Gloss of Bede: « *The priest* announces the law of God; the Law indeed came down into the world through Moses and brought no health to man. *The Levite* came down, who shows the type of the Prophets, but he too heals none, because the Law reproves sins, but passes by, because it does not bestow pardon ». And that was well designated in Fourth Kings four[^p271-7] in the staff of Eliseus, which he sent to raise up the boy, « and yet there was no voice nor sense »; whence it was rather a staff chastising than healing. Whence it is said to the Hebrews ten: « A man making void the law of Moses, dieth without any mercy under two or three witnesses ».

64. Now *the person succouring from mercy* is rightly understood as Christ the Lord, because *Samaritan* is interpreted keeper[^p271-8]; and he it is of whom it is said in the Psalm: « Unless the Lord keep the city », etc.; and Isaias twenty-one: « Keeper, what of the night »? because « behold, he shall neither slumber nor sleep, that keepeth Israel ». — He *came near the wounded man,* because he was « made in the likeness of men, and in habit found as a man », to the Philippians two[^p271-9]. He came, I say, « in the likeness of sinful flesh », as is said to the Romans eight. — And coming he does three things for the wounded man: he applied *medicine,* and this through *the grace of the Sacraments,* in which are made the smearings and anointings healing the wounds of sins,
<!-- page 272 -->
which was not done before the Saviour's coming, according to that of Isaias one[^p272-1]: « Wounds and bruises and swelling sores: they are not bound up, nor cured with medicine, nor fomented with oil ». But this was done after the coming of Christ; whence First Peter two: « Who his own self bore our sins in his body upon the tree, by whose bruise we are healed ». — He applied also *a conveyance,* and this through *the grace of the virtues and of the gifts,* by which we are conveyed into the *inn* of the Church, according to that of the Psalm[^p272-2]: « Blessed is the man whose help is from thee: in his heart he hath disposed to ascend by steps, in the vale of tears »; and afterwards: « For the lawgiver shall give a blessing, they shall go from virtue to virtue ». And this indeed is done in the *inn* of the Church; whence it is added: « I have chosen to be an abject in the house of God », etc.; and afterwards: « For God loveth mercy and truth: the Lord will give grace and glory ». — He applied *nourishment,* which is done through *the teaching of the two testaments,* which is understood in the taking out of *the two pence,* which *he gave to the host,* that is, to the prelate, whose part it is to minister the teaching of Christ and by explaining to *spend over and above;* whence Ecclesiasticus forty-five[^p272-3]: « The Lord gave Moses a heart for the precepts, and the law of life and discipline, to teach Jacob his covenant and Israel his judgements ». To these the Lord *will repay,* when he shall return on the day of judgement, *all things spent over and above;* since, according to what is said in Daniel twelve, « they that instruct many to justice, as stars for all eternity »; Matthew twenty-five: « Well done, good and faithful servant, because thou hast been faithful over a few things, I will place thee over many things », etc.
