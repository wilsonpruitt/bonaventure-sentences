# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XIX, Vers. 20-28
## *Et tertius venit dicens etc.*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 483 -->

32. (Vers. 20.). *Et tertius*[^p483-11] *venit dicens* etc. Postquam descripsit iudicii formam respectu omnium et respectu fidelium, hic describit eam *respectu negligentium,* qui omnes in persona istius servi tertii designantur[^p483-12]. Circa hoc autem introducuntur quatuor ab Evangelista, scilicet servi pigri *absconsio, nequam excusatio* et *recta redargutio* et *condemnatio iusta.*

Primo igitur quantum ad ipsius *servi absconsionem pigram* subdit: *Et tertius venit dicens: Domine, ecce, mna tua, quam habui repositam in sudario;* Glossa[^p483-13]: « Tertius servus sunt illi qui post collectos Iudaeos et gentes negligenter utuntur commisso praedicationis officio ». De quibus dicitur Ieremiae quadragesimo octavo: « Maledictus qui facit opus Dei negligenter et qui prohibet gladium suum a sanguine ». Tunc *negligenter* fit, quando donum sapientiae absconditur et occultatur; ideo dicit, *repositam esse in sudario;* de quo Beda[^p483-14]: « In sudario pecuniam reponit qui, ad praedicandum idoneus, officium praedicandi vel suscipere renuit, vel susceptum non bene gerit; sed percepta dona sub otio torporis abscondit dicens: Sufficit mihi, ut de me rationem reddam. Cur aliis praedicem, vel de aliis cogar reddere rationem »? Talis est merito maledicendus, quia, Proverbiorum undecimo[^p483-15], « qui
<!-- page 484 -->
abscondit frumenta maledicetur in populis ». Pravus enim pastor est, qui cibum habens, permittit, oves fame mori. Propter quod Ecclesiastici vigesimo nono: « Perde pecuniam propter fratrem et amicum, et ne abscondas illam sub lapide in perditionem ». Et si hoc de pecunia dicitur, multo fortius de scientia; unde Ecclesiastici quadragesimo primo[^p484-1]: « Sapientia absconsa et thesaurus invisus, quae utilitas » in utrisque? Unde Ezechielis tertio: « Si me dicente ad impium » etc.; ibi Glossa: « Magnum discrimen est Dei celare sermones ».

33. Sed hoc intelligitur non de occultatione, quae venit *ex prudentia* — nam Ecclesiastae tertio[^p484-2] dicitur: « Tempus tacendi et tempus loquendi »; et in Psalmo: « In corde meo abscondi eloquia tua, ut non peccem tibi »; propter quod dicitur Proverbiorum decimo: « Sapientes abscondunt scientiam »; et Ecclesiastici vigesimo: « Est tacens, qui invenitur sapiens, et est odibilis, qui procax est ad loquendum » — sed intelligitur de absconsione *ex timore,* vel *adulatione,* vel *avaritia,* vel *negligentia*[^p484-3]; ideo signanter dicit: *reposita est in sudario.* In *sudario* enim involvuntur mortui; et tunc mna reposita est in sudario, quando affectio mortua ad proximum abscondit loquelam et doctrinam propter *malam vitam,* sicut illi, de quibus ad Romanos primo[^p484-4]: « Qui veritatem Dei in iniustitiam detinent »; vel propter *cordis desidiam,* quia non habet caritatem fraternam; propter quod Ecclesiastici trigesimo: « Bona abscondita in ore clauso quasi appositiones epularum circumpositae sepulcro ». Et propterea in huius figuram manna absconditum computruit, Exodi decimo sexto[^p484-5]. Et mna in sudario non multiplicatur, et frumentum in horreo non excrescit, sed a tineis manducatur, solum autem fructificat, quando seminatur; sic et verbum Dei, quod pretio et semini comparatur; et ideo Proverbiorum decimo quinto[^p484-6]: « Labia sapientium disseminabunt scientiam, cor stultorum dissimile erit ».

34. (Vers. 21.). Secundo quantum ad *excusationem nequam* subdit: *Timui enim te, quia homo austerus es;* in quo se excusat ut *piger* per timorem; Proverbiorum vigesimo secundo[^p484-7]: « Dicit piger: Leo est in via, leaena in itineribus, et in medio platearum occidendus sum ». Unde et talibus competit illud Psalmi: « Trepidaverunt timore, ubi non erat timor ». — Excusat *nequiter,* quia culpam suae negligentiae imponit austeritati Domini iudicantis, cum e contrario ipse dicat Matthaei undecimo[^p484-8]: « Discite a me, quia mitis sum et humilis corde »; et ad Romanos secundo: « An ignoras, quoniam benignitas Dei ad poenitentiam te adducit »? — Excusat etiam *insipienter,* quia, cum deberet loqui pro se, loquitur contra se, cum subdit: *Tollis quod non posuisti, et metis quod non seminasti;* Glossa[^p484-9]: « Metit Dominus, ubi non seminavit, id est, eos reos impietatis tenet, quibus verbum Legis, vel Evangelii non ministravit ». In quo allegat severitatem divini iudicii, quia Dominus punit etiam ignorantes; et ideo timere debet, quod multo fortius puniet negligentes; unde Ecclesiastici secundo[^p484-10]: « Qui timent Dominum inquirent quae beneplacita sunt ei ». Punit autem Dominus illos etiam, quibus Legem non dedit, a quibus requirit iustitiam, quia, etsi non habent legem scriptam, habent tamen legem naturae interius impressam; propter quod dicitur ad Romanos secundo[^p484-11]: « Qui sine Lege peccaverunt sine Lege peribunt ». Unde et ibidem: « Cum enim gentes, quae Legem non habent, naturaliter ea quae Legis sunt, faciunt, eiusmodi Legem non habentes, ipsi sibi sunt lex, qui ostendunt opus Legis scriptum in cordibus suis, testimonium reddente illis conscientia ipsorum », « cum iudicabit Deus occulta hominum per Iesum Christum ».

35. (Vers. 22.). Tertio quantum ad *servi redargutionem rectam* subditur: *Dicit ei: De ore tuo te iudico, serve nequam. Servum nequam* vocat, quia *piger* erat in negligendo, quia *superbus* fuit se iustificando et *nequam* dominum accusando. Glossa[^p484-12]: « *Nequam,* quia piger ad exercendum officium, superbus, ad accusandum divinum iudicium ». Et ideo *ex suo ore,* dum se credit iustificare, potius accusat, secundum illud Iob nono[^p484-13]: « Si iustificare me voluero, os meum condemnabit me; si innocentem
<!-- page 485 -->
ostendero, pravum me comprobabit »; quia, ut dicit Augustinus, « bene loqui et male vivere nihil aliud est, quam se sua lingua damnare ». — Hic igitur negligens, dum iudicium et severitatem Dei ostendit se scire, loquitur contra se; unde et subdit: *Sciebas, quod*[^p485-1] *ego homo austerus sum, tollens quod non posui, et metens quod non seminavi,* per severitatem vindictae, secundum illud Psalmi: « Cum accepero tempus, ego iustitias iudicabo ».

36. (Vers. 23.). *Et quare non dedisti pecuniam meam ad mensam?* per sedulitatem vigilantiae, per quam pecuniam evangelicae praedicationis debuit multiplicare evangelizando, ne propter negligentiam divinam incurreret iram. Unde Glossa[^p485-2]: « Si me durum et crudelem noveras, quare ista cogitatio non incussit tibi timorem, ut scires, me mea diligentius quaesiturum »? — Et nota, quod vocat mnam *pecuniam suam,* quia scientia et gratia et doctrina Dei potius est quam nostra; et ideo Ezechielis secundo[^p485-3]: « Loqueris verba mea ad eos »; Glossa: « Non tua »; unde et Matthaei decimo: « Non enim vos estis, qui loquimini » etc. — Sed haec est pecunia nobis concessa ad fructificandum, et ideo addit: *Et*[^p485-4] *ego veniens cum usuris utique exegissem illam,* scilicet per aequitatem iustitiae, per quam exigit usuras multiplicis fructificationis. Unde Glossa[^p485-5]: « Qui verbi pecuniam a doctore emit credendo, necesse est, ut eam cum usuris solvat operando ». Qui ergo neglexit alios docere, quos potuit, illorum negligentiae sibi imputabuntur. Unde Chrysostomus[^p485-6]: « Qui seminandi causa semen accepit et tempore seminationis non seminat damnum facit domino, etsi semen custodiat, tantum, quantum poterat lucrum facere, si opportuno tempore seminasset ». Unde et Dominus, veniens ad iudicium, non requirit solum donum, quod dedit, verum etiam fructum, quem ex dono exspectavit. Ideo dicit Matthaei tertio[^p485-7]: « Omnis arbor, quae non facit fructum bonum, excidetur et in ignem mittetur ». Unde et de ficulnea supra decimo tertio: « Ecce anni tres sunt, ex quo venio quaerens fructum in ea et non invenio. Succide ergo eam, ut quid etiam terram occupat »? Quaerit igitur et de arboribus fructum et de pecunia spirituali usuras; unde Gregorius[^p485-8] dicit, quod « cum augentur dona, rationes etiam crescunt donorum ».

37. (Vers. 24.). Quarto quantum ad *ipsius servi condemnationem iustam* subiungitur: *Et astantibus dixit: Auferte ab illo mnam et date illi qui habet decem mnas. Astantes* vocat illos qui assistunt ei in iudicio, sive Angelos sive Sanctos alios, quorum iudicio et approbatione praemium correspondens dono inutiliter habito auferetur, et dabitur gloria et gaudium dispensatori fideli, secundum illud Apocalypsis tertio[^p485-9]: « Tene quod habes, ne alius accipiat coronam tuam ». Hoc etiam iudicium Dominus exercet in praesenti, auferendo dona etiam concessa propter negligentium desidiam et abusionem. Unde Glossa[^p485-10]: « Collatam gratiam amittit qui eam communicando aliis praedicare neglexerit; augetur vero illi qui in ea laboravit, sicut regium chrisma, quod superbiendo Saul amisit, David obediendo promeruit. *Spiritus enim Domini, qui a Saule recessit, directus est a die illa et deinceps in David* », primi Regum decimo sexto. Unde et dicitur Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p485-11]: « Auferetur a vobis regnum Dei et dabitur genti facienti fructus eius ». — Magis autem datur ei qui habet *decem mnas,* propter mysterium, quia ille designat ordinem eorum qui Iudaico populo praedicaverunt, qui etiam finaliter ad Christum convertendus est. Unde Glossa[^p485-12]: « Cum omnis Israel salvus erit, tunc omnis plenitudo spiritualis gratiae, quam modo torpenter exercemus, doctoribus illius temporis abundanter conferetur ». Unde ad Romanos nono[^p485-13]: « Isaias clamat pro Israel: Si fuerit numerus filiorum Israel velut arena maris, reliquiae salvae fient ».

38. (Vers. 25.). Et quia hoc videtur irrationabile et incredibile, quod det abundanti; ideo subditur: *Et dixerunt ei*[^p485-14]*: Domine, habet decem mnas,* quasi dicant: non videtur esse recta sententia.

39. (Vers. 26.). Ideo pro omnibus generaliter sententiat, subdens: *Dico autem vobis, quia omni habenti dabitur, et abundabit;* Glossa[^p485-15]: « Utenti eo quod habet ». — *Ab eo autem, qui non habet,*
<!-- page 486 -->
scilicet rectum usum, *et quod habet,* scilicet divinum donum, *auferetur ab eo,* per divinum iudicium. Quod expressius dicitur in Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p486-1]: « Quod habere videtur auferetur ab eo ». Unde tunc verificabitur illud Proverbiorum undecimo: « Alii dividunt propria et ditiores fiunt, alii rapiunt aliena et semper in egestate sunt ». Et ideo generaliter omni *habenti,* habenti scilicet *desiderium audiendi,* dabitur sensus intelligendi; Ecclesiastici sexto[^p486-2]: « Si dilexeris audire, sapiens eris ». Habenti *voluntatem operandi* dabitur facultas perficiendi; ad Philippenses secundo: « Deus est, qui operatur in vobis velle et perficere ». Habenti *opera* dabitur intelligentia; Psalmus[^p486-3]: « A mandatis tuis intellexi ». « Super senes intellexi » etc. Habenti *iustitiam* dabitur sapientia; Ecclesiastici primo: « Fili, concupiscens sapientiam, conserva iustitiam ». Habenti *gratiam* dabitur gloria; Psalmus[^p486-4]: « Gratiam et gloriam dabit Dominus ». Habenti *arrham* dabitur hereditas aeterna; ad Ephesios primo: « In quo et credentes signati estis Spiritu promissionis sancto, qui est pignus hereditatis nostrae » etc. Habenti *cumulum meritorum* dabitur cumulus praemiorum, quia, secundae ad Corinthios nono[^p486-5], « qui parce seminat parce et metet, et qui seminat in benedictionibus de benedictionibus et metet »; supra sexto: « Date, et dabitur vobis. Mensuram bonam » etc.

40. *Verumtamen inimicos meos* etc. Post formam iudicii respectu omnium et fidelium et negligentium hic subiungitur *poena respectu rebellium*[^p486-6]. Unde ex hac parabola possunt quinque differentiae hominum elici, quarum *duae bonae* et *tres malae.* Nam *boni* sunt fideliter docentes, *boni* fideliter eorum doctrinae adhaerentes. *Mali* vero sunt negligentes et ignorantes et malignantes. Omnes enim boni aut sunt subditi, aut praelati. Mali vero aut ex infirmitate, aut ex ignorantia, aut malitia universaliter peccant. Unde Glossa[^p486-7] dicit: « His quinque personis omne genus hominum in futuro iudicio discutiendum significat ». In hac igitur parabola circa iudicium rebellium duo insinuantur, scilicet rebellium *poena* et rebellium *persona.*

41. (Vers. 27.). Primo igitur quantum ad *rebellium poenam* dicit: *Verumtamen inimicos meos,* per suam arrogantiam, secundum illud Psalmi[^p486-8]: « Superbia eorum qui te oderunt, ascendit semper ». Ille enim maxime inimicatur Deo, qui contra eum erigit cervicem suam per superbiam, secundum illud Iob decimo quinto[^p486-9]: « Cucurrit adversus eum erecto collo et pingui cervice armatus est ». Talis est qui quaerit gloriam in hoc mundo; Iacobi quarto: « Nescitis, quia amicitia huius mundi inimica est Dei? Quicumque ergo » etc.; et primae Ioannis secundo: « Qui diligit mundum non est caritas Dei in eo » etc. — *Illos qui noluerunt, me regnare super se,* per inobedientiam; Ieremiae secundo[^p486-10]: « A saeculo confregisti iugum meum, rupisti vincula mea et dixisti: Non serviam ». Et haec oritur ex arrogantia; Iob vigesimo secundo: « Qui dicebant Deo: Recede a nobis, et quasi nihil posset Omnipotens, aestimabant eum ». — *Adducite huc et interficite ante me,* per severam vindictam, qua interficientur per gladium divinae sententiae et deiicientur in mortem damnationis aeternae; Psalmus[^p486-11]: « Gladii ancipites in manibus eorum ad faciendam vindictam in nationibus ». Unde Apocalypsis primo: « Ex ore eius gladius ex utraque parte acutus exibat ». Et hoc comminatur Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo[^p486-12]: « Si acuero ut fulgur gladium meum, et arripuerit iudicium manus mea, reddam ultionem hostibus meis ». Et de hoc in figura Ezechielis nono: « Transite per medium civitatis et interficite ».

42. (Vers. 28.). Secundo quantum ad *rebellium personam insinuandam* subdit: *Et his dictis, praecedebat ascendens Ierosolymam,* quasi ex ipso facto ostendat[^p486-13], se dicere praedicta propter Ierosolymitas, qui erant eum negaturi non esse suum regem. Propter quam etiam causam statim consequenter agitur, quomodo ut rex venit in Ierusalem, sedens super asinam. Sed post illam magnam gloriam sustinere debebat contumeliam. Et quia ad illam non ibat compulsus, sed voluntarius, ideo signanter dicitur, quod *praecedebat,* ut animaret ceteros ad tolerantiam passionum, secundum illud primae Petri secundo[^p486-14]: « Christus passus est pro nobis, vobis relinquens exemplum, ut sequamini vestigia eius »; et ad Hebraeos duodecimo: « Curramus ad propositum nobis certamen, aspicientes in auctorem fidei et consummatorem Iesum » etc. Ideo etiam *praecedebat,* ut formam daret praelatis praecedendi oves, contra ferocitatem
<!-- page 487 -->
lupinam; Ioannis decimo[^p487-1]: « Bonus pastor, cum proprias oves emiserit, ante eas vadit, et oves illum sequuntur, quia noverunt vocem eius ». In huius figuram dicitur de Iuda primi Machabaeorum quinto, quod, « cum videret populum trepidantem ad transfretandum torrentem, transfretavit primus ». Huius nobilis imitator fuit Paulus, qui, licet sciret, quod multa a Iudaeis deberet pati in Ierusalem, secure ascendit ad subditorum salutem; Actuum vigesimo[^p487-2]: « Spiritus sanctus per omnes civitates protestatur mihi, quod vincula et tribulationes Ierosolymis me manent. Sed non facio animam meam pretiosiorem quam me, dummodo consummem cursum meum et ministerium verbi, quod accepi ». Sic et Christus faciebat; unde licet ad tempus Iudaeam declinasset, quia nondum venerat hora eius; nunc ibat tradere animam suam propter nos in manus inimicorum, secundum illud Ieremiae duodecimo[^p487-3]: « Dedi dilectam animam meam in manus inimicorum eius ». Ideo igitur ascendebat Ierusalem, « quia », sicut supra decimo tertio dictum est, « non capit, Prophetam perire extra Ierusalem ». — Quare autem hoc? Ratio supra[^p487-4] reddita est, quia hoc exigit excellentia pontificalis, magistralis et regiae dignitatis. Unde Glossa: « Finita parabola, vadit Ierosolymam, ut ostendat, de eiusdem maxime civitatis eventu parabolam fuisse praemissam ».

## English
<!-- page 483 -->

32. (Vers. 20.). *And the third*[^p483-11] *came, saying* etc. After he described the form of judgement in respect of all and in respect of the faithful, here he describes it *in respect of the negligent,* who are all designated in the person of this third servant[^p483-12]. Now concerning this four things are introduced by the Evangelist, namely the *hiding* of the sluggish servant, the *wicked excuse,* and the *right conviction,* and the *just condemnation.*

First, then, as to *the sluggish hiding* of the servant himself he subjoins: *And the third came, saying: Lord, behold thy pound, which I have kept laid up in a napkin;* the Gloss[^p483-13]: « The third servant is those who, after the Jews and the Gentiles have been gathered, use negligently the office of preaching entrusted to them ». Of whom it is said in Jeremias, the forty-eighth chapter: « Cursed be he that doth the work of God negligently, and he that withholdeth his sword from blood ». It is done *negligently* when the gift of wisdom is hidden and concealed; therefore he says that it was *laid up in a napkin;* of which Bede[^p483-14]: « He lays up the money in a napkin who, being fit for preaching, either refuses to undertake the office of preaching or does not conduct well the one undertaken, but hides the gifts received in the leisure of torpor, saying: It suffices me that I render an account of myself. Why should I preach to others, or be compelled to render an account for others »? Such a one is deservedly to be cursed, because, Proverbs, the eleventh chapter[^p483-15], « he that
<!-- page 484 -->
hideth up corn shall be cursed among the people ». For he is a wicked shepherd who, having food, allows the sheep to die of hunger. On account of which, Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-ninth chapter: « Lose thy money for thy brother and thy friend, and hide it not under a stone to be lost ». And if this is said of money, much more strongly of knowledge; whence Ecclesiasticus, the forty-first chapter[^p484-1]: « Wisdom that is hid, and treasure that is not seen, what profit is there » in them both? Whence Ezechiel, the third chapter: « If, when I say to the wicked » etc.; there the Gloss: « It is a great peril to conceal the words of God ».

33. But this is understood not of the concealment which comes *from prudence* — for in Ecclesiastes, the third chapter[^p484-2], it is said: « A time to keep silence, and a time to speak »; and in the Psalm: « Thy words have I hidden in my heart, that I may not sin against thee »; on account of which it is said in Proverbs, the tenth chapter: « Wise men hide knowledge »; and Ecclesiasticus, the twentieth chapter: « There is one that holdeth his peace that is found wise, and there is another that is hateful, that is bold in speech » — but it is understood of the hiding *from fear,* or *flattery,* or *avarice,* or *negligence*[^p484-3]; therefore he says pointedly: *it is laid up in a napkin.* For in a *napkin* the dead are wrapped; and the pound is laid up in a napkin when an affection dead toward the neighbour hides speech and doctrine on account of *an evil life,* as those of whom it is said to the Romans, the first chapter[^p484-4]: « Who detain the truth of God in injustice »; or on account of *sloth of heart,* because it does not have fraternal charity; on account of which Ecclesiasticus, the thirtieth chapter: « Good things that are hidden in a mouth that is shut are as messes of meat set about a grave ». And therefore in figure of this the hidden manna putrefied, Exodus, the sixteenth chapter[^p484-5]. And the pound in a napkin is not multiplied, and corn in the barn does not increase, but is eaten by moths, and bears fruit only when it is sown; so also the word of God, which is compared to money and to seed; and therefore Proverbs, the fifteenth chapter[^p484-6]: « The lips of the wise shall sow knowledge abroad; the heart of fools shall be unlike ».

34. (Vers. 21.). Second, as to *the wicked excuse* he subjoins: *For I feared thee, because thou art an austere man;* in which he excuses himself as a *sluggish* man through fear; Proverbs, the twenty-second chapter[^p484-7]: « The sluggish man saith: There is a lion in the way, a lioness in the roads, and I shall be slain in the midst of the streets ». Whence also to such there applies that of the Psalm: « They have trembled for fear, where there was no fear ». — He excuses himself *wickedly,* because he lays the blame of his own negligence on the austerity of the Lord who judges, while on the contrary he himself says in Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p484-8]: « Learn of me, because I am meek and humble of heart »; and to the Romans, the second chapter: « Knowest thou not that the kindness of God leadeth thee to penance »? — He excuses himself also *foolishly,* because, when he ought to speak for himself, he speaks against himself, when he subjoins: *Thou takest up what thou didst not lay down, and thou reapest that which thou didst not sow;* the Gloss[^p484-9]: « The Lord reaps where he did not sow, that is, he holds guilty of impiety those to whom he did not minister the word of the Law or of the Gospel ». In which he alleges the severity of the divine judgement, because the Lord punishes even the ignorant; and therefore he ought to fear, since much more strongly will he punish the negligent; whence Ecclesiasticus, the second chapter[^p484-10]: « They that fear the Lord will seek after the things that are well pleasing to him ». And the Lord punishes even those to whom he did not give the Law, from whom he requires justice, because, although they do not have the written law, yet they have the law of nature impressed within; on account of which it is said to the Romans, the second chapter[^p484-11]: « They who have sinned without the Law shall perish without the Law ». Whence also in the same place: « For when the Gentiles, who have not the Law, do by nature those things that are of the Law, these, having not the Law, are a law to themselves, who show the work of the Law written in their hearts, their conscience bearing witness to them », « when God shall judge the secrets of men by Jesus Christ ».

35. (Vers. 22.). Third, as to *the right conviction of the servant* it is subjoined: *He saith to him: Out of thy own mouth I judge thee, thou wicked servant. A wicked servant* he calls him, because he was *sluggish* in neglecting, because he was *proud* in justifying himself and *wicked* in accusing his lord. The Gloss[^p484-12]: « *Wicked,* because sluggish in exercising the office, proud in accusing the divine judgement ». And therefore *out of his own mouth,* while he believes he justifies himself, he rather accuses himself, according to that of Job, the ninth chapter[^p484-13]: « If I would justify myself, my own mouth shall condemn me; if I would show myself
<!-- page 485 -->
innocent, he shall prove me wicked »; because, as Augustine says, « to speak well and to live ill is nothing else than to condemn oneself by one's own tongue ». — This negligent man, then, while he shows that he knows the judgement and severity of God, speaks against himself; whence he also subjoins: *Thou knewest that*[^p485-1] *I am an austere man, taking up what I laid not down, and reaping that which I did not sow,* by the severity of vengeance, according to that of the Psalm: « When I shall take a time, I will judge justices ».

36. (Vers. 23.). *And why didst thou not give my money into the bank?* by the sedulousness of vigilance, by which he ought to have multiplied the money of evangelical preaching by preaching the gospel, lest on account of negligence he should incur the divine wrath. Whence the Gloss[^p485-2]: « If thou knewest me hard and cruel, why did this thought not strike fear into thee, that thou mightest know that I would seek my own the more diligently »? — And note that he calls the pound *his money,* because knowledge and grace and doctrine are God's rather than ours; and therefore Ezechiel, the second chapter[^p485-3]: « Thou shalt speak my words to them »; the Gloss: « Not thine »; whence also Matthew, the tenth chapter: « For it is not you that speak » etc. — But this is money granted to us for bearing fruit, and therefore he adds: *And*[^p485-4] *I at my coming might have exacted it with usury,* namely by the equity of justice, by which he exacts the usury of manifold fruit-bearing. Whence the Gloss[^p485-5]: « He who buys the money of the word from the teacher by believing must pay it back with usury by working ». He, then, who neglected to teach others whom he could, will have their negligences imputed to himself. Whence Chrysostom[^p485-6]: « He who received seed for the sake of sowing, and in the time of sowing does not sow, does damage to his lord, even if he keeps the seed, as much as he could have made gain if he had sown at the opportune time ». Whence also the Lord, coming to the judgement, requires not only the gift which he gave, but also the fruit which he expected from the gift. Therefore it says in Matthew, the third chapter[^p485-7]: « Every tree that doth not yield good fruit shall be cut down and cast into the fire ». Whence also of the fig tree above, the thirteenth chapter: « Behold, these three years I come seeking fruit on it, and I find none. Cut it down therefore; why doth it also cumber the ground »? He seeks, then, both fruit from the trees and usury from the spiritual money; whence Gregory[^p485-8] says that « when the gifts are increased, the accounts of the gifts also grow ».

37. (Vers. 24.). Fourth, as to *the just condemnation of the servant himself* it is subjoined: *And he said to them that stood by: Take the pound away from him, and give it to him that hath ten pounds. Them that stood by* he calls those who assist him in the judgement, whether Angels or other Saints, by whose judgement and approval the reward corresponding to the gift uselessly held will be taken away, and glory and joy will be given to the faithful dispenser, according to that of the Apocalypse, the third chapter[^p485-9]: « Hold fast that which thou hast, that no other take thy crown ». This judgement too the Lord exercises in the present, taking away gifts even granted, on account of the sloth and abuse of the negligent. Whence the Gloss[^p485-10]: « He loses the grace conferred who has neglected to preach it by communicating it to others; but it is increased for him who has laboured in it, as the royal chrism, which Saul lost by being proud, David merited by obeying. *For the Spirit of the Lord, which withdrew from Saul, was directed upon David from that day and forward* », the first of Kings, the sixteenth chapter. Whence also it is said in Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p485-11]: « The kingdom of God shall be taken from you, and shall be given to a nation yielding the fruits thereof ». — But it is given rather to him who has *ten pounds,* on account of a mystery, because he designates the order of those who preached to the Jewish people, which is also at the last to be converted to Christ. Whence the Gloss[^p485-12]: « When all Israel shall be saved, then all the fulness of spiritual grace, which we now exercise torpidly, will be abundantly conferred on the doctors of that time ». Whence to the Romans, the ninth chapter[^p485-13]: « Isaias crieth out concerning Israel: If the number of the children of Israel be as the sand of the sea, a remnant shall be saved ».

38. (Vers. 25.). And because this seems unreasonable and incredible, that he should give to one abounding, therefore it is subjoined: *And they said to him*[^p485-14]*: Lord, he hath ten pounds,* as if they said: the sentence does not seem to be right.

39. (Vers. 26.). Therefore he pronounces sentence for all generally, subjoining: *But I say to you, that to every one that hath shall be given, and he shall abound;* the Gloss[^p485-15]: « To one using that which he has ». — *And from him that hath not,*
<!-- page 486 -->
namely the right use, *even that which he hath,* namely the divine gift, *shall be taken from him,* by the divine judgement. Which is said more expressly in Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p486-1]: « That which he seemeth to have shall be taken away from him ». Whence then will be verified that of Proverbs, the eleventh chapter: « Some distribute their own goods and grow richer; others take away what is not their own and are always in want ». And therefore generally to every one *that hath,* to one, namely, that has *the desire of hearing,* shall be given the sense of understanding; Ecclesiasticus, the sixth chapter[^p486-2]: « If thou love to hear, thou shalt be wise ». To one that has *the will of working* shall be given the faculty of accomplishing; to the Philippians, the second chapter: « It is God who worketh in you to will and to accomplish ». To one that has *works* shall be given intelligence; the Psalm[^p486-3]: « By thy commandments I have had understanding ». « I have had understanding above the ancients » etc. To one that has *justice* shall be given wisdom; Ecclesiasticus, the first chapter: « Son, if thou desire wisdom, keep justice ». To one that has *grace* shall be given glory; the Psalm[^p486-4]: « The Lord will give grace and glory ». To one that has *the earnest* shall be given the eternal inheritance; to the Ephesians, the first chapter: « In whom also believing you were signed with the Holy Spirit of promise, who is the pledge of our inheritance » etc. To one that has *a heap of merits* shall be given a heap of rewards, because, in the second to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter[^p486-5], « he who soweth sparingly shall also reap sparingly, and he who soweth in blessings shall also reap of blessings »; above, the sixth chapter: « Give, and it shall be given to you. Good measure » etc.

40. *But as for those my enemies* etc. After the form of judgement in respect of all and of the faithful and of the negligent, here is subjoined *the punishment in respect of the rebellious*[^p486-6]. Whence from this parable five differences of men can be drawn out, of which *two are good* and *three bad.* For *good* are those who teach faithfully, *good* those who faithfully adhere to their doctrine. But *bad* are the negligent and the ignorant and the malignant. For all the good are either subjects or prelates. But the bad sin either from infirmity, or from ignorance, or from malice universally. Whence the Gloss[^p486-7] says: « By these five persons it signifies every kind of men to be examined in the future judgement ». In this parable, then, concerning the judgement of the rebellious two things are intimated, namely the *punishment* of the rebellious and the *person* of the rebellious.

41. (Vers. 27.). First, then, as to *the punishment of the rebellious* he says: *But as for those my enemies,* through their arrogance, according to that of the Psalm[^p486-8]: « The pride of them that hate thee ascendeth continually ». For he is most at enmity with God who raises his neck against him through pride, according to that of Job, the fifteenth chapter[^p486-9]: « He hath run against him with his neck raised up, and is armed with a fat neck ». Such is he who seeks glory in this world; James, the fourth chapter: « Know you not that the friendship of this world is the enemy of God? Whosoever therefore » etc.; and the first of John, the second chapter: « He that loveth the world, the charity of God is not in him » etc. — *Who would not have me reign over them,* through disobedience; Jeremias, the second chapter[^p486-10]: « Of old time thou hast broken my yoke, thou hast burst my bands, and thou saidst: I will not serve ». And this arises from arrogance; Job, the twenty-second chapter: « Who said to God: Depart from us, and looked upon the Almighty as if he could do nothing ». — *Bring them hither, and kill them before me,* by severe vengeance, by which they will be killed by the sword of the divine sentence and cast down into the death of eternal damnation; the Psalm[^p486-11]: « Two-edged swords in their hands, to execute vengeance upon the nations ». Whence the Apocalypse, the first chapter: « From his mouth came out a sharp two-edged sword ». And this is threatened in Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter[^p486-12]: « If I shall whet my sword as the lightning, and my hand take hold on judgement, I will render vengeance to my enemies ». And of this in figure Ezechiel, the ninth chapter: « Pass through the midst of the city, and kill ».

42. (Vers. 28.). Second, as to *the person of the rebellious that is to be intimated* he subjoins: *And having said these things, he went before, going up to Jerusalem,* as if he showed by the very deed[^p486-13] that he said the foregoing on account of the inhabitants of Jerusalem, who were going to deny that he was their king. For which cause also, immediately following, it is treated how he came as king into Jerusalem, sitting upon an ass. But after that great glory he was to sustain contumely. And because he was not going to it compelled, but willing, therefore it is said pointedly that *he went before,* to animate the rest to the endurance of sufferings, according to that of the first of Peter, the second chapter[^p486-14]: « Christ suffered for us, leaving you an example, that you should follow his steps »; and to the Hebrews, the twelfth chapter: « Let us run to the fight proposed to us, looking on the author and finisher of faith, Jesus » etc. Therefore also *he went before,* to give prelates the form of going before the sheep, against the ferocity
<!-- page 487 -->
of wolves; John, the tenth chapter[^p487-1]: « The good shepherd, when he hath let out his own sheep, goeth before them, and the sheep follow him, because they know his voice ». In figure of this it is said of Judas in the first of Machabees, the fifth chapter, that, « when he saw the people fearful of passing over the torrent, he passed over first ». Of him a noble imitator was Paul, who, although he knew that he was to suffer many things from the Jews in Jerusalem, went up securely for the salvation of his subjects; Acts, the twentieth chapter[^p487-2]: « The Holy Spirit in every city witnesseth to me that bands and afflictions wait for me at Jerusalem. But I do not count my life more precious than myself, so that I may consummate my course and the ministry of the word which I received ». So also Christ did; whence, although for a time he had turned aside from Judea, because his hour had not yet come, now he was going to deliver up his soul for us into the hands of enemies, according to that of Jeremias, the twelfth chapter[^p487-3]: « I have given my dear soul into the hand of her enemies ». Therefore, then, he went up to Jerusalem, « because », as was said above, in the thirteenth chapter, « it cannot be that a Prophet perish out of Jerusalem ». — But why this? The reason was given above[^p487-4], because the excellence of the pontifical, magisterial and royal dignity requires this. Whence the Gloss: « The parable finished, he goes to Jerusalem, to show that the parable had been set forth above all concerning the outcome of that same city ».
