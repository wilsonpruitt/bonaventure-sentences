# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. V, Vers. 1-11
## *Primo, quomodo vocentur discipuli de statu iustitiae*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 112 -->

### Capitulum V.

#### Secundo determinatur discipulorum vocatio per duo.

1. *Factum est, cum turbae* etc. Supra[^p112-10] egit Evangelista de doctrinae Christi authenticatione, in hac parte agit de *discipulorum vocatione.* Et habet pars ista duas, in quarum prima describitur vocatio discipulorum de *statu iustitiae;* in secunda, de *statu culpae*[^p112-11]: *Post haec exiit et vidit* etc.

#### Primo, quomodo vocentur discipuli de statu iustitiae.

Prima pars habet duas. In prima proponitur *vocatio discipulorum;* in secunda vero, *vocatorum*
<!-- page 113 -->
*confirmatio* per operationem miraculorum[^p113-1]: *Et factum est, cum esset in una civitatum* etc.

Ad *vocationem* autem *discipulorum* tria introducuntur: primum est *eruditio populi;* secundum, *operatio miraculi,* ibi[^p113-2]: *Ut cessavit autem loqui* etc.; tertium est *vocatio Petri,* ibi: *Quod cum videret Simon Petrus.* Primum horum spectat ad *profunditatem sapientiae,* per quam alios docebat; secundum, ad *sublimitatem potentiae,* per quam miracula faciebat; tertium, ad *immensitatem clementiae,* qua piscatores pauperculos ad suum consortium attrahebat. Et habent se ista per ordinem, quia circumstantiae instructionis populi fuerunt introductiva occasio operationis miraculi, et operatio miraculi fuit ratio motiva conversionis Petri et filiorum Zebedaei.

Describitur ergo *eruditio populi* sub quatuor circumstantiis, quae induxerunt ad captionem piscium. Prima est *importunitas plebis;* secunda, *opportunitas navis;* tertia, *obedientia Simonis Petri;* quarta, *affluentia sermonis dominici.*

2. (Vers. 1.). Primo igitur tangitur *importunitas plebis* irruentis in eum propter doctrinam, cum dicitur: *Factum est autem, cum turbae irruerent in eum, ut audirent verbum Dei.* Ideo *irruebant,* quia quilibet appropinquare volebat; nec immerito, quia, Deuteronomii trigesimo tertio[^p113-3], « qui appropinquant pedibus eius accipient de doctrina illius ». Nec tantum volebant *ignorantes* appropinquare propter doctrinam sive scientiam, sed etiam *infirmi* propter sanitatem recuperandam; infra sexto: « Omnis turba quaerebat eum tangere, quia virtus de illo exibat et sanabat omnes ». Nec solum propter hoc, sed *peccatores* propter veniam; unde infra decimo quinto: « Erant appropinquantes ad Iesum publicani et peccatores ». Et vehementi desiderio movebantur, ideo irruebant, considerantes magis Domini bonitatem quam maiestatem. Unde dicit quidam expositor[^p113-4]: « Magni meriti est apud Deum desiderium verbi Dei, quod ipsam quoque irreverentiam turbae irruentis excusat ». Unde Dominus ad hoc praecipue invitabat; Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto: « Omnes sitientes venite ad aquas », *aquas* scilicet sapientiae salutaris. In cuius rei signum erat iuxta aquas; unde et subditur: *Et ipse stabat secus stagnum Genesareth,* ut ad aquas sapientiae invitaret, sicut in Ioannis septimo[^p113-5]: « Stabat Iesus et clamabat: Si quis sitit, veniat ad me et bibat ».

3. Et nota, quod hoc stagnum aquae diversis nominibus appellari consuevit, scilicet *mare Cenereth;* unde Numerorum trigesimo quarto[^p113-6] in divisione sortium dicitur, quod « ab orientali plaga pervenient contra orientem ad *mare Cenereth* »; Glossa ibi: « Stagnum Tiberiadis, secundum consuetudinem Hebraeorum, qui quamlibet congregationem aquarum appellant *mare* », iuxta illud Genesis primo: « Congregationesque aquarum appellavit maria » etc. Et quia civitatem istam post appellavit Herodes Tiberiadem, quia ipsam in honorem Tiberii caesaris reaedificavit; ideo vocatur *mare Tiberiadis;* Ioannis sexto[^p113-7]: « Post haec abiit Iesus trans mare Galilaeae, quod est Tiberiadis ». — Et quia originem trahit a Iordane, ideo dicitur *lacus Iordanis.* — Et quia in Galilaeae regione est, ideo *mare Galilaeae.* — Et quia inundationes fiunt, et aura ibi generatur; ideo dicitur *stagnum Genesareth,* id est generans auras[^p113-8]. — Idem etiam dicitur *lacus salinarum.* — Iuxta illud stagnum stabat Iesus, ut qui miracula fecerat in terra faceret etiam in aquis; unde, secundum quod ex diversis colligitur locis[^p113-9], Dominus in illo stagno multa miracula operatus fuit: in captione piscium, in sedatione tempestatum, calcatione undarum.

4. (Vers. 2.). Secundo subditur *opportunitas navis* sublevantis in aquam, cum subditur: *Et vidit duas naves,* opportunas scilicet ad ferendum, tum quia *propinquae;* propter quod dicit: *Stantes secus stagnum;* tum etiam, quia *otiosae* sive vacuae; unde et addit: *Piscatores autem descenderant et lavabant retia;* et ita opportunae erant ad ministrandum. Has autem naves pauperum piscatorum sibi propinquas vidit, quia, secundum quod in Psalmo[^p113-10] dicitur, « excelsus Dominus et humilia respicit et alta a longe cognoscit ». Et has sibi ut opportunas vidit ad obsequium, secundum illud Ecclesiastici tertio: « Magna potentia Dei solius, et a solis humilibus honoratur ». Unde et ipse se humiliat, ut qui omnia portat a navi portetur; Sapientiae decimo quarto: « Tu autem, Pater, providus cuncta gubernas, quoniam dedisti in mari viam et inter fluctus semitam firmissimam, ostendens, quoniam potens es
<!-- page 114 -->
omnibus subvenire, etiamsi sine rate aliquis adeat mare ».

5. (Vers. 3.). Tertio additur *obedientia Simonis* separantis a turba, cum subditur: *Ascendens autem in unam navim, quae erat Simonis,* ipsum tanquam pauperem et humilem praeeligendo, secundum illud Iacobi secundo[^p114-1]: « Nonne Deus elegit pauperes in hoc mundo, divites in fide, heredes regni, quod repromisit Deus diligentibus se »? Ascendit autem, non tyrannice opprimendo, sed humiliter supplicando. Unde addit: *Rogavit eum a terra reducere pusillum,* ut scilicet parum sequestraret a turba et sublevaret a terra. Qui enim vult alios docere debet *interiori affectu* terrena contemnere et *exterioribus moribus* turbas praecellere; unde Exodi vigesimo tertio[^p114-2] dicitur: « Non sequeris turbam ad faciendum malum nec in iudicio plurimorum acquiesces sententiae » etc. — Nota hic *humilitatem* Domini, qua rogabat Petrum, servum suum; quam reliquit imitandam omnibus praelatis, ut et ipsi rogent subditos; unde Apostolus primae ad Thessalonicenses quarto[^p114-3]: « Rogamus et obsecramus vos in Domino Iesu » etc.; tum quia, ut dicit Ambrosius[^p114-4], « coacta servitia Deo non placent »; tum quia, ut dicit Seneca[^p114-5], « generosus est animus hominis et facilius ducitur, quam trahatur ».

6. Postremo subditur *affluentia sermonis Christi* docentis de navicula, cum additur: *Et sedens docebat de navicula turbas. Sedere* enim spectat ad auctoritatem personae *docentis;* Matthaei quinto[^p114-6]: « Cum sedisset, accesserunt ad eum discipuli eius »; ad excellentiam etiam *iudicantis;* Psalmus: « Sedes *super thronum,* qui iudicas iustitiam ». Haec namque sessio auctoritatem designat iudicialem, quia *in Thronis* residet ad iudicandum Deus. Dicitur autem rursus in Psalmo[^p114-7] *sedere super Cherubim,* cum dicitur: « Qui sedes super Cherubim, appare coram Ephraim » etc.; quia *Cherubim* plenitudo scientiae interpretatur, et qui sedet in loco doctoris plenus debet esse sapientia, ut de plenitudine eius accipiant universi discipuli. — Et de hac plenitudine accipiebat turba; unde et dicitur: *Docebat turbas;* Ioelis secundo[^p114-8]: « Filiae Sion, exsultate et laetamini in Domino Deo vestro, quia dedit vobis doctorem iustitiae et descendere ad vos faciet imbrem matutinum et serotinum, sicut in principio ». Et ideo *super mare* stabat, quia de profundo incomprehensibili quasi rorem parvum sapientiae ad nos transmittit; Iob vigesimo sexto: « Si vix stillam sermonum eius audiverimus, quis poterit tonitruum magnitudinis illius intueri »? — *Docebat autem turbas,* id est simplices et humiles, quia, Proverbiorum tertio[^p114-9], « et cum simplicibus sermocinatio eius »; Matthaei undecimo: « Abscondisti haec a sapientibus et prudentibus, et revelasti ea parvulis ». Et ratio huius est, quia turbae non requirebant subtilia, sed utilia; et talium est Dominus doctor; Isaiae quadragesimo octavo: « Ego Dominus, docens te utilia, gubernans te in via, per quam ambulas ».

7. *Moraliter* autem notandum est, quod in *Christo docente* datur forma *praedicanti:* introducitur ut *stans,* ut *videns,* ut *ascendens* et ut *sedens.* — Veri enim et boni praedicatoris est *stare* per *rectitudinem intentionis;* Ezechielis secundo[^p114-10] dictum est ei in persona praedicatoris: « Sta super pedes tuos, et loquar tecum ». — Est *videre* per *diligentiam discretionis;* sic enim dicitur Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo[^p114-11]: « Diligenter agnosce vultum pecoris tui tuosque greges considera ». — Est *ascendere* per *exercitium perfectae actionis;* Isaiae quadragesimo: « Super montem excelsum ascende tu, qui evangelizas Sion ». — Est *sedere* per *otium contemplationis;* Threnorum tertio[^p114-12]: « Sedebit solitarius et tacebit, quia levavit super se ». — De his
<!-- page 115 -->
quatuor simul dicitur Ieremiae sexto: « State super vias vestras », ecce, rectitudo intentionis; « interrogate de semitis antiquis, quae sit via bona », ecce, sollicitudo discretionis; « et ambulate in ea », per exercitium actionis; « et invenietis refrigerium animabus vestris », per otium contemplationis.

8. In *turba* autem *irruente* datur forma *auditori.* Propter quod nota, quod Evangelista introducit turbas in comparatione ad Iesum primo *requirentes,* secundo *venientes,* tertio *detinentes;* et de his tribus supra proximo[^p115-1]; quarto *irruentes,* sicut hic; quinto *comitantes,* infra decimo quarto: « Ibat cum Iesu turba copiosa »; sexto *subsequentes,* infra vigesimo tertio: « Sequebatur Iesum turba multa mulierum » etc. — Boni enim auditores debent *requirere* sapientiam studio meditationis; Sapientiae octavo[^p115-2]: « Hanc amavi et exquisivi a iuventute mea ». — Debent *venire* ad ipsum desiderio affectionis, secundum illud Psalmi: « Sitivit anima mea ad Deum fortem, vivum » etc. — Debent *detinere* instantia orationis, secundum illud Canticorum tertio[^p115-3]: « Tenui eum nec dimittam ». Sed heu! verificatum est illud Isaiae sexagesimo quarto: « Non est qui invocet nomen tuum, qui consurgat et teneat te ». — Debent *irruere* impetu devotionis; Ezechielis primo dicitur de sanctis animalibus: « Ubi erat impetus spiritus, illuc gradiebantur ». — Debent *comitari* conformitate bonae operationis, sicut Henoch; Genesis quinto[^p115-4]: « Cum Deo ambulavit et non apparuit, quia tulit eum Deus ». — Debent etiam *sequi* imitatione passionis; primae Petri secundo: « Pro nobis passus est, vobis relinquens exemplum, ut sequamini vestigia eius ». Talibus promittitur gloria; Matthaei decimo nono: « Amen dico vobis, quod vos, qui reliquistis omnia et secuti estis me, centuplum accipietis et vitam aeternam possidebitis ».

9. (Vers. 4.). *Ut cessavit autem loqui* etc. Post eruditionem populi subditur *operatio miraculi.* Circa cuius descriptionem quatuor introducuntur: ex parte Christi *altitudo potentiae;* ex parte Petri *certitudo fiduciae;* ex parte miraculi *multitudo copiae;* ex parte consortii *promptitudo assistentiae*[^p115-5].

In *Christo* igitur insinuatur *altitudo potentiae* ad miraculum faciendum, cum dicitur: *Ut cessavit autem loqui,* scilicet per veritatis magisterium; quia Domini est confirmare sermonem sequentibus signis, Marci ultimo[^p115-6]; *dixit ad Simonem,* per potestatis imperium, cui per omnia obediendum, secundum consilium Virginis, Ioannis secundo, in primo miraculo: « Quodcumque dixerit vobis, facite ». — Et sequitur imperium: *Duc in altum,* id est in profundum mare, ubi scilicet est multitudo piscium, sicut Divinitatis oculis aspiciebat; Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio[^p115-7]: « Oculi Domini multo plus lucidiores sunt sole, circumspicientes omnes vias hominum et profundum abyssi ». Et ideo subdit: *Et laxate retia vestra in capturam.* Sic, Ioannis ultimo[^p115-8], post resurrectionem: « Mittite in dexteram navigii rete, et invenietis ». Hoc imperando dicit, ut ostendat, quod « omnia subiecit Deus pedibus eius, volucres caeli et pisces maris » etc.

10. (Vers. 5.). In *Petro* autem insinuatur *certitudo confidentiae* ad miraculum impetrandum, cum subditur: *Et respondens Simon dixit illi: Praeceptor,* non solum *doctorem,* sed etiam *praeceptorem* vocat, credens, non solum habere scientiam ad docendum, sed etiam potentiam ad faciendum. Nec irrationabiliter Petrus piscator tali nomine Christum appellat, quia Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto[^p115-9]: « Ecce, testem populi dedi eum, ducem ac praeceptorem gentibus »; et trigesimo: « Erunt oculi tui videntes praeceptorem tuum, et aures tuae audient verbum post tergum monentis ». — Et quoniam confidentia vera non innititur propriis viribus, sed divinis; ideo subdit: *Per totam noctem laborantes, nihil cepimus,* et ita non mittam, de viribus et industria propria confisus; unde dicere poterat illud Isaiae quadragesimo nono[^p115-10]: « Sine causa et vane fortitudinem meam consumsi »; et Iob septimo: « Ego habui menses vacuos et noctes laboriosas enumeravi mihi », quasi dicat: non de meis, sed de tuis viribus confido. — Et ideo infert: *In verbo autem tuo laxabo rete.* Nec immerito, quia ipse dicit Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto[^p115-11]: « Verbum meum, quod egredietur de ore meo, non revertetur ad me vacuum, sed faciet quaecumque volui, et prosperabitur in omnibus, ad quae misi illud ». Unde de Petro potest dici illud ad Romanos quarto: « In repromissione Dei non haesitavit diffidentia, sed confortatus est fide, plenissime sciens » etc.

11. (Vers. 6.). In *miraculo* vero insinuatur *multitudo copiae* ad ipsum miraculum magnificandum, cum subditur: *Et cum hoc fecissent, concluserunt multitudinem piscium copiosam.* Et sic verificatum est illud Ioannis decimo quarto[^p115-12]: « Quodcumque
<!-- page 116 -->
petieritis Patrem in nomine meo, hoc faciam ». Ecce, iam credulitas Petri impetrabat miracula; unde ipse suae fidei fundamento *Petrus* a firmitate petrae meruit appellari; unde paulo post addit Evangelista: « Quod cum videret Simon Petrus » etc. Et ideo infra vigesimo secundo dicitur ei: « Ego pro te rogavi, Petre, ut non deficiat fides tua ». Et quoniam copia multitudinis non solum fuit sufficiens, sed etiam superexcrescens, ideo subditur: *Rumpebatur autem rete*[^p116-1], scilicet prae nimia multitudine.

12. Et attende, quod Dominus eodem miraculo, quo potuit pisces in retia congregare, potuit retia conservare, sicut et fecit, Ioannis ultimo[^p116-2], ubi dicitur: « Et cum tanti essent, non est scissum rete » ipsius Petri. Sed hoc factum est propter *figuram,* quia miracula Domini, ut dicit Gregorius, semper nobis aliquid insinuant diligentius attendendum. Illa enim piscium captura significat congregationem electorum post resurrectionem in gloria; ista vero, ante resurrectionem vocatorum ad Ecclesiam. Et quia in illa nullus ingreditur, qui egrediatur; de hac multi egrediuntur: ideo hic rumpitur, ibi vero minime. Hinc est, quod illa dicitur fuisse in *dextera navigii,* ista vero non[^p116-3]; discernit illa in numero determinato, ista vero non; hic quasi naves merguntur propter multitudinem tentationum, ibi in terra requiescunt; hic Christus in navi, ibi in littore requiescit.

13. (Vers. 7.). In *consortio* autem insinuatur *promptitudo assistentiae* ad miraculum manifestandum, cum subditur: *Et annuerunt sociis, qui erant in alia navi,* id est, per nutum ostenderunt sive signaverunt, quia, Tobiae duodecimo[^p116-4], « opera Dei revelare et confiteri honorificum est ». Unde dicit auctor quidam: « Nullius boni sine socio iucunda possessio », secundum illud Ecclesiastae quarto: « Melius est, duos esse simul quam unum; habent enim emolumentum societatis suae ». — In cuius rei signum subditur: *Ut venirent et adiuvarent eos,* quia, secundum illud Proverbiorum decimo octavo[^p116-5], « frater, qui adiuvatur a fratre, quasi civitas firma ». Et certe, sicut dicitur ibidem, « vir amicabilis ad societatem magis erit amicus quam frater ». — Et hoc manifeste probatur per ipsorum promptitudinem in adiuvando; et ideo additur: *Et venerunt et impleverunt ambas naviculas.* Et sic verificatum est illud Proverbiorum decimo[^p116-6]: « Benedictio Domini divites facit »; unde poterant dicere illud Tobiae duodecimo: « Bonis omnibus per eum repleti sumus ». Haec autem *impletio,* ut tactum est[^p116-7], significat impletionem Ecclesiae; Matthaei decimo tertio: « Simile est regnum caelorum sagenae missae in mare et ex omni genere piscium congreganti » etc. — Et ideo additur: *Ita ut pene mergerentur;* quia mali adeo multiplicantur, quod pene omnes submergimur, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo quarto[^p116-8]: « Quoniam abundavit iniquitas, refrigescet caritas multorum » etc. Sed tamen navis Petri non submergitur, Paulus tamen dicit secundae ad Corinthios undecimo: « Ter naufragium pertuli ».

14. *Moraliter* hic notandum est, qui sint isti *piscatores,* quae *naves* eorum, quae *retia,* et quae *capiendi via.* — *Piscatores* sunt praedicatores; unde infra eodem[^p116-9]: « Ex hoc iam homines eris capiens »; et Matthaei quarto: « Faciam, vos fieri piscatores hominum »; et hoc Ieremiae decimo sexto: « Ecce, ego mittam vobis piscatores multos, et piscabuntur eos ». Et diabolus econtra habet piscatores suos malos et haereticos seductores; Habacuc primo[^p116-10]: « Facies homines quasi pisces maris. Totum in hamo sublevavit, traxit illud in sagena sua et congregavit in rete suum ». Et certe nunc adeo mali piscatores praevalent, ut boni vix habeant locum; et impletum est illud Isaiae decimo nono: « Moerebunt piscatores, et lugebunt omnes mittentes in flumen hamum et expandentes rete super faciem aquarum ».

15. *Naves* autem, per quas transitur mare, sunt duae, scilicet *obedientia* et *patientia,* quarum altera exercet in labore, altera sustinet in dolore. Et de prima potest intelligi illud Sapientiae decimo quarto[^p116-11]: « Exiguo ligno committunt homines animas suas, et transeuntes mare per ratem liberati sunt » etc. Haec est *obedientia,* quae, cum sit exigua, perducit ad terram beatam. De *secunda* potest intelligi illud Actuum vigesimo septimo[^p116-12]: « Nisi in
<!-- page 117 -->
navi manseritis, non poteritis salvi fieri »; dicitur de his qui erant in tempestate, quia, ad Hebraeos decimo, « patientia vobis necessaria est, ut voluntatem Dei facientes, reportetis promissionem »; ubi tanguntur hae duae naves, de quibus simul in Psalmo dicitur[^p117-1]: « Qui descendunt mare in navibus, facientes operationem », quod est *obedientiae,* « in aquis multis », scilicet tribulationis, quod est *patientiae;* « quia per multas tribulationes oportet nos intrare in regnum Dei ».

16. *Retia* autem sunt *discretio* et *locutio,* quae vicissim debent connecti ad instructionem populi per artificium Spiritus sancti; Threnorum primo[^p117-2]: « De excelso misit ignem in ossibus meis et erudivit me; expandit rete pedibus meis »; ad Colossenses quarto: « In sapientia ambulate ad eos qui foris sunt. Sermo vester semper in gratia sale sit conditus, ut sciatis » etc. Haec autem *retia* contexuntur, quando ex verbis Scripturae in unam seriem congregatis sermones componuntur, in quibus magis est utilitas quam subtilitas attendenda. Unde curiosis comminatur Dominus Isaiae decimo nono[^p117-3]: « Confundentur qui operabantur linum, pectentes et texentes subtilia ». Per haec retia facilius capiuntur simplices laici quam clerici eruditi; unde Proverbiorum primo[^p117-4]: « Frustra iacitur rete ante oculos pennatorum »; sed tales in rete capiuntur sapientiae mundanae; Proverbiorum septimo: « Irretivit eum multis sermonibus et blanditiis labiorum protraxit illum ».

17. *Modus capiendi* est *ducere naves in altum,* id est ascendere ad vitae perfectionem, *et laxare retia in capturam,* per praedicationem; quae duo debent esse coniuncta secundum exemplum Salvatoris; Actuum primo[^p117-5]: « Coepit Iesus facere et docere », quia dicit Gregorius: « Cuius vita despicitur, restat, ut eius praedicatio contemnatur ». Unde ad illos spectat potissime retia in praedicatione laxare, qui ad perfectionis apicem poterunt ascendere; et ideo ad Romanos decimo quinto[^p117-6] dicebat Apostolus: « Non audeo aliquid loqui eorum quae per me non efficit Christus ».

18. (Vers. 8.). *Quod cum videret Simon Petrus.* Post eruditionem populi et operationem miraculi subditur *vocatio Petri et filiorum Zebedaei.* Ad cuius perfectionem quatuor ostenduntur concurrisse, scilicet *humiliatio Petri* habilitans ad gratiam; *admiratio* elevans ad intelligentiam; *alleviatio Christi* stabiliens ad confidentiam; *imitatio magistri* perducens ad perfectam[^p117-7] iustitiam.

Primo ergo praemittitur *humiliatio Petri habilitans ad gratiam,* cum dicitur: *Quod cum videret Simon Petrus, procidit ad genua Iesu.* Ex viso miraculo humiliatus est, *magnificando divinam maiestatem;* et recte, quia, ad Philippenses secundo[^p117-8], « in nomine Iesu omne genu flectatur » etc.; et Isaiae quadragesimo quinto: « Mihi curvabitur omne genu, et iurabit omnis lingua ». Et hoc fecit Petrus quasi invitans alios ad faciendum similiter, ut diceret illud Psalmi: « Venite, adoremus et procidamus » etc. — Humiliatus etiam fuit *resiliendo in propriam parvitatem;* unde et subdit: *Dicens: Exi a me, Domine, quia homo peccator sum,* quasi dicat: non sum talis, ut ego sim dignus esse simul tecum; hoc ex fide et reverentia dicebat, sicut ille centurio, Matthaei octavo[^p117-9]: « Domine, non sum dignus, ut intres sub tectum meum ». Ideo autem hoc dicebat Petrus, quia videbat potentiam et non percipiebat clementiam, qua dixit Matthaei nono: « Non veni vocare iustos, sed peccatores ». Unde Augustinus[^p117-10]: « Ut piscator loquitur Petrus; Deum ac Dominum salutis secum habebat et dicebat: Exi a me, ac si infirmus medico curare volenti diceret: recede a me, quia aegroto ». Et Gregorius[^p117-11] dicit: « Petre, immo, si te
<!-- page 118 -->
peccatorem consideras, oportet, ut a te Dominum non expellas ». Sed certe sic humiliando non expellebat, sed attrahebat; Isaiae ultimo: « Ad quem respiciam nisi ad pauperculum et contritum spiritu et trementem sermones meos »?

19. (Vers. 9.). Secundo vero subditur *admiratio elevans ad intelligentiam,* quae erat ratio humiliationis in Petro. Et hoc notat, cum dicit: *Stupor enim circumdederat eum.* In *circumdatione* autem notatur vehementia stuporis; Isaiae vigesimo nono[^p118-1]: « Ecce, ego addam, ut admirationem faciam populo huic miraculo grandi et stupendo ». — Et ne credas, ipsum solum admirari, sed et alios etiam esse testes miraculi, subditur: *Et omnes, qui cum illo erant, in captura piscium, quam ceperant,* id est, propter capturam magnam et insolitam omnes mirabantur, secundum illud Psalmi[^p118-2]: « Qui descenderunt mare in navibus », « ipsi viderunt opera Domini et mirabilia eius in profundo ».

20. (Vers. 10.). Et quia alii discipuli cum Petro fuerunt vocati, ideo subditur: *Similiter autem et*[^p118-3] *Iacobum et Ioannem, filios Zebedaei,* stupor scilicet circumdederat, qui erant similiter piscatores; unde et subditur: *Qui erant socii Simonis.* Unde sicut socii fuerunt in exercitio piscationis, sic et in dignitate vocationis, Apocalypsis primo: « Ego Ioannes, frater vester et particeps in tribulatione et regno »; secundae ad Corinthios primo: « Scientes, quod sicut estis socii passionum, sic eritis et consolationum ». — Et nota, quod sicut hi tres fuerunt socii in piscatione, sic etiam fuerunt in vocatione; et sicut simul vocati, sic simul electi et assumti ad miraculorum operationem; infra octavo[^p118-4]: « Non permisit introire secum quemquam nisi Petrum et Iacobum et Ioannem »; simul assumti ad transfigurationem; infra nono: « Assumsit secum Petrum et Iacobum et Ioannem » etc.; simul etiam assumti ad orationem; Marci decimo quarto: « Et assumsit secum Petrum et Iacobum » etc.

21. Tertio subditur *alleviatio Christi stabiliens ad confidentiam,* cum dicitur: *Et ait ad Simonem Iesus: Noli timere.* Non excludit timorem reverentiae et humilitatis, quia ille est « principium sapientiae[^p118-5] », et de illo dicitur Ecclesiastici primo: « Qui sine timore est non poterit iustificari », sed excludit timorem pusillanimitatis, qui opponitur confidentiae; de quo Genesis decimo quinto: « Noli timere, Abraham; quia ego protector tuus sum »; et Iosue primo: « Noli timere, quia ego tecum sum ». — Et ut non expavescat minora, promittit maiora, cum addit: *Ex hoc iam homines eris capiens;* quod multo maius est quam capere pisces. Glossa[^p118-6]: « Nondum in apostolatum eligitur, sed praedicitur, quod aliquando sit eligendus »; Matthaei quarto: « Venite post me, et faciam vos fieri piscatores hominum ». Et specialiter alloquitur Petrum, quia ipsi specialiter haec cura committitur; unde Matthaei decimo sexto: « Tibi dabo claves regni caelorum » etc. — Et nota, quod dicit: *capiens homines,* non aurum, secundum illud secundae ad Corinthios duodecimo[^p118-7]: « Non quaero vestra, sed vos »; et ad Philippenses ultimo: « Non quaero datum, sed fructum ».

22. (Vers. 11.). Quarto subditur perfecta *imitatio Magistri perducens ad perfectam iustitiam,* cum subditur: *Et subductis ad terram navibus,* ut ibi eas relinquerent; unde et sequitur: *Relictis omnibus;* infra decimo quarto[^p118-8]: « Omnis ex vobis, qui non renuntiaverit omnibus, quae possidet, non potest meus esse discipulus ». Signanter dicit *omnibus,* quia, ut dicit Gregorius, « tanta a sequentibus dimissa sunt, quanta a non sequentibus concupisci poterant »; et quia omnia poterant concupisci, ideo *omnia* dimissa. Unde Gregorius: « Multum reliquit qui sibi nihil retinuit; multum reliquit qui etiam cum re, quam habuit, voluntatem habendi dereliquit ». — « Et quia non sufficit relinquere sua, quia hoc aliqui fecerunt philosophi[^p118-9] », subditur: *Secuti sunt eum.* Unde Petrus, Matthaei decimo nono: « Ecce, nos reliquimus omnia et secuti sumus te », scilicet per consilia; Iob vigesimo tertio: « Vestigia eius secutus est pes meus »; quia « magna gloria est sequi Dominum », Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio; sic Eliseus fuit secutus Eliam, tertii Regum decimo nono.

23. Et nota, quod contrarietas videtur inter Lucam, Matthaeum et Ioannem quantum ad *locum,* quantum ad *tempus,* quantum ad *modum:* quia Ioannes[^p118-10], *iuxta Iordanem* et *ante adventum* in Galilaeam et *singillatim;* isti vero, Matthaeus et Lucas: *post adventum, in Galilaea* et *simul* vocatos dicunt. — Et propterea intelligendum, quod discipuli isti vocati fuerunt semel in *simplicem notitiam,* et de hac Ioannis primo[^p118-11]; secundo in *familiaritatem,*
<!-- page 119 -->
et de hac dicitur hic, quia adhuc reversi sunt, ut dicit Glossa[^p119-1]; tertio in *discipulatum* sive apostolatum, et de hac dicitur Matthaei quarto et Marci primo: et ideo nulla est contrarietas sive diversitas in locis et temporibus et aliis circumstantiis.

### Chapter V.

#### Secondly the calling of the disciples is determined through two things.

1. *And it came to pass, that when the multitudes* etc. Above[^p112-10] the Evangelist treated of the authentication of the doctrine of Christ; in this part he treats of the *calling of the disciples.* And this part has two, in the first of which is described the calling of the disciples from the *state of justice;* in the second, from the *state of guilt*[^p112-11]: *And after these things he went forth and saw* etc.

#### First, how the disciples are called from the state of justice.

The first part has two. In the first is set forth the *calling of the disciples;* but in the second, the
<!-- page 113 -->
*confirmation of those called* through the working of miracles[^p113-1]: *And it came to pass, when he was in a certain city* etc.

Now for the *calling of the disciples* three things are introduced: the first is the *erudition of the people;* the second, the *working of a miracle,* there[^p113-2]: *Now when he had ceased to speak* etc.; the third is the *calling of Peter,* there: *Which when Simon Peter saw.* The first of these pertains to the *profundity of wisdom,* through which he taught others; the second, to the *sublimity of power,* through which he worked miracles; the third, to the *immensity of clemency,* by which he drew poor little fishermen into his fellowship. And these stand in order, because the circumstances of the instruction of the people were the introductory occasion of the working of the miracle, and the working of the miracle was the moving reason of the conversion of Peter and of the sons of Zebedee.

The *erudition of the people,* then, is described under four circumstances, which led to the catching of the fishes. The first is the *importunity of the crowd;* the second, the *opportuneness of the ship;* the third, the *obedience of Simon Peter;* the fourth, the *abundance of the Lord's discourse.*

2. (Vers. 1.). First, then, is touched the *importunity of the crowd* pressing upon him for the sake of the doctrine, when it is said: *And it came to pass, that when the multitudes pressed upon him to hear the word of God.* They *pressed upon* him for this reason, because everyone wished to come near; and not without merit, because, Deuteronomy, the thirty-third chapter[^p113-3], « they that approach to his feet shall receive of his doctrine ». Nor did only the *ignorant* wish to come near for the sake of doctrine or knowledge, but also the *sick* for the sake of recovering health; below, the sixth chapter: « All the multitude sought to touch him, for virtue went out from him and healed all ». Nor only for this, but *sinners* for the sake of pardon; whence below, the fifteenth chapter: « The publicans and sinners drew near unto Jesus ». And they were moved with vehement desire, and therefore they pressed upon him, considering the goodness of the Lord more than his majesty. Whence a certain expositor says[^p113-4]: « The desire for the word of God is of great merit with God, which excuses even the irreverence of the crowd pressing upon him ». Whence the Lord especially invited to this; Isaias, the fifty-fifth chapter: « All you that thirst, come to the waters », to the *waters,* namely, of saving wisdom. In sign of which thing he was beside the waters; whence it is also added: *And he stood by the lake of Genesareth,* so as to invite to the waters of wisdom, as in John, the seventh chapter[^p113-5]: « Jesus stood and cried, saying: If any man thirst, let him come to me and drink ».

3. And note that this lake of water is wont to be called by various names, namely the *sea of Cenereth;* whence in Numbers, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p113-6], in the division of the lots, it is said that « from the east side they shall come over against the east to the *sea of Cenereth* »; the Gloss there: « The lake of Tiberias, according to the custom of the Hebrews, who call any gathering together of waters a *sea* », according to that saying of Genesis, the first chapter: « And the gathering together of the waters he called seas » etc. And because Herod afterwards called this city Tiberias, since he rebuilt it in honour of Tiberius Caesar, therefore it is called the *sea of Tiberias;* John, the sixth chapter[^p113-7]: « After these things Jesus went over the sea of Galilee, which is that of Tiberias ». — And because it takes its origin from the Jordan, therefore it is called the *lake of the Jordan.* — And because it is in the region of Galilee, therefore the *sea of Galilee.* — And because floods arise and a breeze is generated there, therefore it is called the *lake of Genesareth,* that is, generating breezes[^p113-8]. — The same is also called the *lake of salt-pits.* — Beside that lake Jesus stood, so that he who had worked miracles on land might work them also in the waters; whence, according to what is gathered from various places[^p113-9], the Lord worked many miracles in that lake: in the catching of fishes, in the calming of tempests, in the treading of the waves.

4. (Vers. 2.). Secondly is added the *opportuneness of the ship* lifting him up upon the water, when it is added: *And he saw two ships,* opportune, namely, for carrying, both because *near at hand;* on account of which he says: *Standing by the lake;* and also because *idle* or empty; whence he also adds: *But the fishermen were gone out of them and were washing their nets;* and so they were opportune for ministering. Now he saw these ships of poor fishermen near him, because, according to what is said in the Psalm[^p113-10], « the Lord is high, and looketh on the low, and the high he knoweth afar off ». And he saw these as opportune to him for service, according to that saying of Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter: « Great is the power of God alone, and he is honoured by the humble ». Whence he also humbles himself, so that he who carries all things is carried by a ship; Wisdom, the fourteenth chapter: « But thou, O Father, providently governest all things: for thou hast made a way even in the sea and a most sure path among the waves, showing that thou art able to
<!-- page 114 -->
succour all, even if one should go to sea without a ship ».

5. (Vers. 3.). Thirdly is added the *obedience of Simon* separating him from the crowd, when it is added: *And going up into one of the ships that was Simon's,* choosing him beforehand as poor and humble, according to that saying of James, the second chapter[^p114-1]: « Hath not God chosen the poor in this world, rich in faith, and heirs of the kingdom which God hath promised to them that love him »? Now he went up, not oppressing him tyrannically, but entreating him humbly. Whence he adds: *He desired him to draw back a little from the land,* namely so that he might set him a little apart from the crowd and lift him up from the land. For he who wishes to teach others ought to despise earthly things *by interior affection* and to excel the crowds *by exterior manners;* whence it is said in Exodus, the twenty-third chapter[^p114-2]: « Thou shalt not follow the multitude to do evil: neither shalt thou yield in judgment to the opinion of the most part » etc. — Note here the *humility* of the Lord, by which he entreated Peter, his servant; which he has left to be imitated by all prelates, so that they too may entreat their subjects; whence the Apostle, the first to the Thessalonians, the fourth chapter[^p114-3]: « We pray and beseech you in the Lord Jesus » etc.; both because, as Ambrose says[^p114-4], « forced services do not please God »; and because, as Seneca says[^p114-5], « the mind of man is generous, and is more easily led than dragged ».

6. Lastly is added the *abundance of the discourse of Christ* teaching from the little ship, when it is added: *And sitting he taught the multitudes out of the ship. To sit* pertains to the authority of the person *teaching;* Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p114-6]: « When he was set down, his disciples came unto him »; also to the excellence of one *judging;* the Psalm: « Thou hast sat *on the throne,* who judgest justice ». For this sitting designates judicial authority, because God sits *in the Thrones* to judge. But again he is said in the Psalm[^p114-7] *to sit upon the Cherubim,* when it is said: « Thou that sittest upon the Cherubim, appear before Ephraim » etc.; because *Cherubim* is interpreted the fulness of knowledge, and he who sits in the place of the doctor ought to be full of wisdom, so that of his fulness all the disciples may receive. — And of this fulness the multitude received; whence it is also said: *He taught the multitudes;* Joel, the second chapter[^p114-8]: « Daughters of Sion, exult and be joyful in the Lord your God, because he hath given you a teacher of justice, and he will make the early and the latter rain to come down to you, as in the beginning ». And therefore he stood *upon the sea,* because from an incomprehensible depth he sends across to us as it were a little dew of wisdom; Job, the twenty-sixth chapter: « Seeing we have heard scarce a little drop of his word, who shall be able to behold the thunder of his greatness »? — *Now he taught the multitudes,* that is, the simple and humble, because, Proverbs, the third chapter[^p114-9], « his communication is with the simple »; Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « Thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them to little ones ». And the reason of this is, because the multitudes did not require subtle things but useful ones; and of such the Lord is teacher; Isaias, the forty-eighth chapter: « I am the Lord, that teach thee useful things, that govern thee in the way that thou walkest ».

7. *Morally,* however, it is to be noted that in *Christ teaching* a form is given *to the one preaching:* he is introduced as *standing,* as *seeing,* as *going up* and as *sitting.* — For it belongs to the true and good preacher to *stand* through *rectitude of intention;* in Ezechiel, the second chapter[^p114-10], it was said to him in the person of the preacher: « Stand upon thy feet, and I will speak to thee ». — It belongs to him to *see* through *diligence of discretion;* for so it is said in Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p114-11]: « Be diligent to know the countenance of thy cattle, and consider thy own flocks ». — It belongs to him to *go up* through *the exercise of perfect action;* Isaias, the fortieth chapter: « Get thee up upon a high mountain, thou that bringest good tidings to Sion[^tr-evangelizare] ». — It belongs to him to *sit* through *the leisure of contemplation;* Lamentations, the third chapter[^p114-12]: « He shall sit solitary and hold his peace, because he hath taken it up upon himself ». — Of these
<!-- page 115 -->
four together it is said in Jeremias, the sixth chapter: « Stand ye on the ways », behold, rectitude of intention; « and ask for the old paths, which is the good way », behold, solicitude of discretion; « and walk ye in it », through the exercise of action; « and you shall find refreshment for your souls », through the leisure of contemplation.

8. But in the *multitude pressing upon him* a form is given *to the hearer.* On account of which note that the Evangelist introduces the multitudes in relation to Jesus, first *seeking,* secondly *coming,* thirdly *detaining;* and of these three, above, in the chapter next before[^p115-1]; fourthly *pressing upon him,* as here; fifthly *accompanying,* below, the fourteenth chapter: « There went great multitudes with Jesus »; sixthly *following after,* below, the twenty-third chapter: « There followed Jesus a great multitude of women » etc. — For good hearers ought to *seek* wisdom by the study of meditation; Wisdom, the eighth chapter[^p115-2]: « Her have I loved and have sought her out from my youth ». — They ought to *come* to him by the desire of affection, according to that saying of the Psalm: « My soul hath thirsted after the strong living God » etc. — They ought to *detain* him by the instancy of prayer, according to that saying of the Canticle, the third chapter[^p115-3]: « I held him, and I will not let him go ». But alas! that saying of Isaias, the sixty-fourth chapter, has been made true: « There is none that calleth upon thy name, that riseth up and taketh hold of thee ». — They ought to *press upon* him by the impetus of devotion; in Ezechiel, the first chapter, it is said of the holy living creatures: « Whither the impulse of the spirit was to go, thither they went ». — They ought to *accompany* him by the conformity of good work, as Henoch did; Genesis, the fifth chapter[^p115-4]: « He walked with God, and was seen no more, because God took him ». — They ought also to *follow* him by imitation of his passion; the first of Peter, the second chapter: « He suffered for us, leaving you an example, that you should follow his steps ». To such glory is promised; Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « Amen, I say to you, that you who have left all things and have followed me shall receive a hundredfold and shall possess life everlasting ».

9. (Vers. 4.). *Now when he had ceased to speak* etc. After the erudition of the people is added the *working of a miracle.* About the description of which four things are introduced: on the part of Christ, *the height of power;* on the part of Peter, *the certitude of trust;* on the part of the miracle, *the multitude of the plenty;* on the part of the fellowship, *the readiness of assistance*[^p115-5].

In *Christ,* then, the *height of power* for doing the miracle is intimated, when it is said: *Now when he had ceased to speak,* namely through the teaching office of truth; because it belongs to the Lord to confirm the word with signs that follow, Mark, the last chapter[^p115-6]; *he said to Simon,* through the command of power, which is to be obeyed in all things, according to the counsel of the Virgin, John, the second chapter, at the first miracle: « Whatsoever he shall say to you, do ye ». — And the command follows: *Launch out into the deep,* that is, into the deep sea, where namely there is a multitude of fishes, as he beheld with the eyes of the Godhead; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third chapter[^p115-7]: « The eyes of the Lord are far brighter than the sun, beholding round about all the ways of men and the bottom of the deep ». And therefore he adds: *And let down your nets for a draught.* So, John, the last chapter[^p115-8], after the resurrection: « Cast the net on the right side of the ship, and you shall find ». He says this commanding, to show that « God hath subjected all things under his feet, the birds of the air and the fishes of the sea » etc.

10. (Vers. 5.). But in *Peter* is intimated the *certitude of confidence* for obtaining the miracle, when it is added: *And Simon answering said to him: Master,*[^tr-praeceptor] he calls him not only *doctor* but also *master,* believing that he has not only knowledge for teaching but also power for doing. Nor without reason does Peter the fisherman call Christ by such a name, because Isaias, the fifty-fifth chapter[^p115-9]: « Behold, I have given him for a witness to the people, for a leader and a master to the Gentiles »; and the thirtieth: « Thy eyes shall see thy master, and thy ears shall hear the word of one admonishing behind thy back ». — And since true confidence does not lean on its own strength but on the divine, therefore he adds: *We have laboured all the night and have taken nothing,* and so I will not cast, having trusted in my own strength and industry; whence he could say that saying of Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter[^p115-10]: « I have spent my strength without cause and in vain »; and Job, the seventh chapter: « I have had empty months, and have numbered to myself wearisome nights », as if he should say: I trust not in my own strength but in thine. — And therefore he infers: *But at thy word I will let down the net.* And not without merit, because he himself says in Isaias, the fifty-fifth chapter[^p115-11]: « My word, which shall go forth from my mouth, shall not return to me void, but it shall do whatsoever I please, and shall prosper in the things for which I sent it ». Whence of Peter can be said that saying to the Romans, the fourth chapter: « In the promise of God he staggered not by distrust, but was strengthened in faith, most fully knowing » etc.

11. (Vers. 6.). But in the *miracle* is intimated the *multitude of the plenty* for magnifying the miracle itself, when it is added: *And when they had done this, they enclosed a very great multitude of fishes.* And so was made true that saying of John, the fourteenth chapter[^p115-12]: « Whatsoever
<!-- page 116 -->
you shall ask the Father in my name, that will I do ». Behold, already the belief of Peter was obtaining miracles; whence he himself, from the foundation of his faith, merited to be called *Peter* from the firmness of the rock; whence a little after the Evangelist adds: « Which when Simon Peter saw » etc. And therefore below, in the twenty-second chapter, it is said to him: « I have prayed for thee, Peter, that thy faith fail not ». And since the plenty of the multitude was not only sufficient but even overflowing, therefore it is added: *But the net was breaking*[^p116-1], namely because of the too great multitude.

12. And attend that the Lord, by the same miracle by which he could gather the fishes into the nets, could preserve the nets, as he also did, John, the last chapter[^p116-2], where it is said: « And although there were so many, the net was not broken », Peter's own. But this was done on account of a *figure,* because the miracles of the Lord, as Gregory says, always intimate to us something to be attended to more diligently. For that catch of fishes signifies the gathering of the elect after the resurrection in glory; but this one, before the resurrection, the gathering of those called to the Church. And because in that one no one enters who goes out, but from this one many go out: therefore here it breaks, but there not at all. Hence it is that that one is said to have been on the *right side of the ship,* but this one not[^p116-3]; that one distinguishes in a determinate number, but this one not; here the ships are as it were sunk on account of the multitude of temptations, there they rest on the land; here Christ is in the ship, there he rests on the shore.

13. (Vers. 7.). But in the *fellowship* is intimated the *readiness of assistance* for making the miracle manifest, when it is added: *And they beckoned to their partners that were in the other ship,* that is, they showed or signified by a nod, because, Tobias, the twelfth chapter[^p116-4], « it is honourable to reveal and confess the works of God ». Whence a certain author says: « The possession of no good thing is pleasant without a companion », according to that saying of Ecclesiastes, the fourth chapter: « It is better that two should be together than one: for they have the advantage of their society ». — In sign of which thing it is added: *That they should come and help them,* because, according to that saying of Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter[^p116-5], « a brother that is helped by his brother is like a strong city ». And surely, as is said in the same place, « a man amiable in society shall be more friendly than a brother ». — And this is manifestly proved by their readiness in helping; and therefore it is added: *And they came and filled both the ships.* And so was made true that saying of Proverbs, the tenth chapter[^p116-6]: « The blessing of the Lord maketh men rich »; whence they could say that saying of Tobias, the twelfth chapter: « We have been filled with all good things through him ». But this *filling,* as has been touched on[^p116-7], signifies the filling of the Church; Matthew, the thirteenth chapter: « The kingdom of heaven is like to a net cast into the sea and gathering together of all kind of fishes » etc. — And therefore it is added: *So that they were almost sinking;* because the wicked are so multiplied that almost all of us are submerged, according to that saying of Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p116-8]: « Because iniquity hath abounded, the charity of many shall grow cold » etc. But yet the ship of Peter is not submerged, though Paul says in the second to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter: « Thrice I suffered shipwreck ».

14. *Morally* it is to be noted here who these *fishermen* are, what their *ships,* what the *nets,* and what the *way of catching.* — The *fishermen* are the preachers; whence below, in the same chapter[^p116-9]: « From henceforth thou shalt catch men »; and Matthew, the fourth chapter: « I will make you to be fishers of men »; and this is Jeremias, the sixteenth chapter: « Behold, I will send many fishers, and they shall fish them ». And the devil on the contrary has his fishermen, the wicked and heretical seducers; Habacuc, the first chapter[^p116-10]: « Thou wilt make men as the fishes of the sea. He lifted up all of them with his hook, he drew them in his drag and gathered them into his net ». And surely the wicked fishermen now so prevail that the good scarcely have a place; and that saying of Isaias, the nineteenth chapter, has been fulfilled: « The fishers also shall mourn, and all that cast a hook into the river shall lament, and they that spread nets upon the face of the waters ».

15. But the *ships,* through which the sea is crossed, are two, namely *obedience* and *patience,* of which the one exercises in labour, the other sustains in sorrow. And of the first can be understood that saying of Wisdom, the fourteenth chapter[^p116-11]: « Men trust their souls even to a little wood, and passing over the sea by ship are saved » etc. This is *obedience,* which, though it be little, leads to the blessed land. Of the *second* can be understood that saying of Acts, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p116-12]: « Except
<!-- page 117 -->
these stay in the ship, you cannot be saved »; it is said of those who were in the tempest, because, to the Hebrews, the tenth chapter, « patience is necessary for you, that, doing the will of God, you may receive the promise »; where these two ships are touched, of which together it is said in the Psalm[^p117-1]: « They that go down to the sea in ships, doing business », which belongs to *obedience,* « in the great waters », namely of tribulation, which belongs to *patience;* « for through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God ».

16. But the *nets* are *discretion* and *speech,* which ought to be knit to each other for the instruction of the people through the craftsmanship of the Holy Spirit; Lamentations, the first chapter[^p117-2]: « From above he hath sent fire into my bones and hath chastised me; he hath spread a net for my feet »; to the Colossians, the fourth chapter: « Walk with wisdom towards them that are without. Let your speech be always in grace seasoned with salt, that you may know » etc. Now these *nets* are woven together when, from words of Scripture gathered into one series, sermons are composed, in which usefulness rather than subtlety is to be attended to. Whence the Lord threatens the curious in Isaias, the nineteenth chapter[^p117-3]: « They shall be confounded that wrought in flax, combing and weaving fine things ». By these nets simple layfolk are caught more easily than learned clerics; whence Proverbs, the first chapter[^p117-4]: « A net is spread in vain before the eyes of them that have wings »; but such are caught in the net of worldly wisdom; Proverbs, the seventh chapter: « She entangled him with many words, and drew him away with the flattery of her lips ».

17. The *way of catching* is *to launch the ships out into the deep,* that is, to ascend to perfection of life, *and to let down the nets for a draught,* through preaching; which two ought to be joined according to the example of the Saviour; Acts, the first chapter[^p117-5]: « Jesus began to do and to teach », because Gregory says: « He whose life is despised, it remains that his preaching be contemned ». Whence it belongs most of all to those to let down the nets in preaching who are able to ascend to the summit of perfection; and therefore the Apostle said to the Romans, the fifteenth chapter[^p117-6]: « I dare not speak of any of those things which Christ worketh not by me ».

18. (Vers. 8.). *Which when Simon Peter saw.* After the erudition of the people and the working of the miracle is added the *calling of Peter and of the sons of Zebedee.* For the perfection of which four things are shown to have concurred, namely the *humbling of Peter,* fitting him for grace; *wonder,* raising him to intelligence; the *relieving by Christ,* establishing him in confidence; the *imitation of the master,* leading him to perfect[^p117-7] justice.

First, then, is set down the *humbling of Peter fitting him for grace,* when it is said: *Which when Simon Peter saw, he fell down at Jesus' knees.* At the miracle seen he was humbled, *magnifying the divine majesty;* and rightly, because, to the Philippians, the second chapter[^p117-8], « in the name of Jesus every knee should bow » etc.; and Isaias, the forty-fifth chapter: « Every knee shall be bowed to me, and every tongue shall swear ». And Peter did this as if inviting others to do likewise, so as to say that saying of the Psalm: « Come, let us adore and fall down » etc. — He was humbled also *by shrinking back into his own littleness;* whence he also adds: *Saying: Depart from me, for I am a sinful man, O Lord,* as if he should say: I am not such that I am worthy to be together with thee; this he said out of faith and reverence, as that centurion did, Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p117-9]: « Lord, I am not worthy that thou shouldst enter under my roof ». But Peter said this for this reason, because he saw the power and did not perceive the clemency by which he said, Matthew, the ninth chapter: « I am not come to call the just, but sinners ». Whence Augustine[^p117-10]: « Peter speaks as a fisherman; he had God and the Lord of salvation with him, and he said: Depart from me, as if a sick man should say to a physician wishing to cure him: go back from me, because I am sick ». And Gregory[^p117-11] says: « Peter, nay rather, if thou
<!-- page 118 -->
consider thyself a sinner, it behoves that thou drive not the Lord out from thee ». But surely by so humbling himself he did not drive him out but drew him; Isaias, the last chapter: « To whom shall I have respect, but to him that is poor and little and of a contrite spirit and that trembleth at my words »?

19. (Vers. 9.). But secondly is added *wonder raising him to intelligence,* which was the reason of the humbling in Peter. And he notes this when he says: *For astonishment had encompassed him.* Now in the *encompassing* is noted the vehemence of the astonishment; Isaias, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p118-1]: « Behold, I will add to cause wonder to this people, by a great and astonishing miracle ». — And lest you should believe that he alone wondered, but that others also were witnesses of the miracle, it is added: *And all that were with him, at the draught of the fishes which they had taken,* that is, on account of the great and unwonted catch all wondered, according to that saying of the Psalm[^p118-2]: « They that go down to the sea in ships », « these have seen the works of the Lord and his marvels in the deep ».

20. (Vers. 10.). And because other disciples were called with Peter, therefore it is added: *And so also*[^p118-3] *James and John, the sons of Zebedee,* astonishment, namely, had encompassed them, who were likewise fishermen; whence it is also added: *Who were Simon's partners.* Whence as they were partners in the exercise of fishing, so too in the dignity of the calling, Apocalypse, the first chapter: « I John, your brother and your partner in tribulation and in the kingdom »; the second to the Corinthians, the first chapter: « Knowing that as you are partakers of the sufferings, so shall you be also of the consolation ». — And note that as these three were partners in fishing, so also were they in the calling; and as they were called together, so together were they chosen and taken up for the working of miracles; below, the eighth chapter[^p118-4]: « He suffered not any man to go in with him, but Peter and James and John »; together taken up for the transfiguration; below, the ninth chapter: « He took Peter and James and John » etc.; together also taken up for prayer; Mark, the fourteenth chapter: « And he taketh Peter and James with him » etc.

21. Thirdly is added the *relieving by Christ establishing him in confidence,* when it is said: *And Jesus saith to Simon: Fear not.* He does not exclude the fear of reverence and of humility, because that is « the beginning of wisdom[^p118-5] », and of it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the first chapter: « He that is without fear cannot be justified », but he excludes the fear of faintheartedness, which is opposed to confidence; of which Genesis, the fifteenth chapter: « Fear not, Abram; for I am thy protector »; and Josue, the first chapter: « Fear not, because I am with thee ». — And lest he should dread at lesser things, he promises greater, when he adds: *From henceforth thou shalt catch men;* which is much greater than to catch fishes. The Gloss[^p118-6]: « He is not yet chosen to the apostleship, but it is foretold that he is at some time to be chosen »; Matthew, the fourth chapter: « Come ye after me, and I will make you to be fishers of men ». And he addresses Peter specially, because this care is specially committed to him; whence Matthew, the sixteenth chapter: « To thee will I give the keys of the kingdom of heaven » etc. — And note that he says: *catching men,* not gold, according to that saying of the second to the Corinthians, the twelfth chapter[^p118-7]: « I seek not the things that are yours, but you »; and to the Philippians, the last chapter: « I seek not the gift, but I seek the fruit ».

22. (Vers. 11.). Fourthly is added the perfect *imitation of the Master leading to perfect justice,* when it is added: *And having brought their ships to land,* so as to leave them there; whence it also follows: *Leaving all things;* below, the fourteenth chapter[^p118-8]: « Every one of you that doth not renounce all that he possesseth cannot be my disciple ». He says *all things* expressly, because, as Gregory says, « as much was given up by those who followed as could be coveted by those who did not follow »; and because all things could be coveted, therefore *all things* were given up. Whence Gregory: « He left much who kept nothing for himself; he left much who, together with the thing that he had, forsook also the will to have ». — « And because it is not enough to leave one's own things, since some philosophers have done this[^p118-9] », it is added: *They followed him.* Whence Peter, Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « Behold, we have left all things and have followed thee », namely through the counsels; Job, the twenty-third chapter: « My foot hath followed his steps »; because « it is great glory to follow the Lord », Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third chapter; so Eliseus followed Elias, the third of Kings, the nineteenth chapter.

23. And note that a contrariety seems to exist between Luke, Matthew and John as to *place,* as to *time,* as to *manner:* because John[^p118-10] says they were called *beside the Jordan* and *before the coming* into Galilee and *one by one;* but these, Matthew and Luke, say they were called *after the coming, in Galilee* and *together.* — And therefore it is to be understood that these disciples were called once into *simple acquaintance,* and of this John, the first chapter[^p118-11]; secondly into *familiarity,*
<!-- page 119 -->
and of this it is said here, because they still returned, as the Gloss says[^p119-1]; thirdly into *discipleship* or apostleship, and of this it is said in Matthew, the fourth chapter, and Mark, the first chapter: and therefore there is no contrariety or diversity in places and times and other circumstances.
