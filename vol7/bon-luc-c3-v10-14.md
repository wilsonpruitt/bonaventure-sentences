# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. III, Vers. 10-14
## *Praedicatio Ioannis commendatur primo quoad exigentiam populi tripliciter*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 75 -->

#### Secundo, de baptismo et praedicatione Ioannis in speciali quoad tria.

22. *Et interrogabant eum turbae* etc. Postquam commendavit Evangelista praedicationem praecursoris in generali, hic commendat in *speciali* secundum eruditionem respectu specialium personarum populi[^p75-3]. Commendatur autem doctrina ipsius in parte ista: primo, in comparatione ad *exigentiam populi* in administrando doctrinam sanam; secundo, in comparatione ad *excellentiam Christi* in vitando gloriam falsam, ibi[^p75-4]: *Existimante autem populo* etc.; tertio, in comparatione ad *perfidiam tyranni* in sustinendo poenam inflictam, ibi: *Multa quidem et alia.* In primo commendatur Ioannis *prudentia;* in secundo, Ioannis *innocentia;* in tertio, *patientia.*

#### Praedicatio Ioannis commendatur primo quoad exigentiam populi tripliciter.

Quantum ad primum notandum, quod secundum triplicem differentiam personarum audientium diversas instructiones administrat: primo, ad *turbas,* quae gerunt personam *subditorum;* secundo, ad *publicanos,* qui gerunt personam *ministrorum;* tertio, ad *milites,* qui gerunt personam *rectorum* sive praepositorum. Et in qualibet istarum partium primo praemittitur *quaestio,* deinde additur *responsio.*

23. (Vers. 10.). Primo igitur *turbis interrogantibus* dat documentum de *eleemosynis faciendis;* propter quod dicitur: *Et interrogabant eum turbae dicentes: Quid ergo faciemus?* Ex quo necesse est dignos fructus poenitentiae facere, igitur doce nos. Solliciti erant simplices addiscere magis quam mundi sapientes; unde Ioannis septimo[^p75-5]: « Nunquid aliquis ex principibus credidit in eum, sed turba haec, quae ignorat Legem, maledicti sunt ». Licet autem ab hominibus reputentur maledicti et reprobi, contrario modo est apud iudicium Dei; Matthaei undecimo[^p75-6]: « Abscondisti haec a sapientibus et prudentibus et revelasti ea parvulis »; et Proverbiorum tertio: « Et cum simplicibus sermocinatio eius ». Tales interrogabant Ioannem tanquam prophetam magnum; Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo: « Interroga patrem tuum, et annuntiabit tibi » etc.

24. (Vers. 11.). Et quia humilem proponunt interrogationem, ideo audiunt utilem *responsionem,* cum subditur: *Qui*[^p75-7] *habet duas tunicas det unam non habenti.* Optimum consilium, quia, sicut dicit Salvator infra undecimo, « date eleemosynam, et ecce, omnia munda sunt vobis ». Sic faciebat Iob, trigesimo primo: « Si despexi praetereuntem, eo quod non haberet indumentum, et absque operimento pauperem; si non benedixerunt mihi latera eius, et de velleribus ovium mearum calefactus est » etc. — Et nota, quam discrete docet, ut: *Qui habet duas tunicas,* id est alteram necessariam, alteram superfluam, quod sibi est superfluum donet alteri, cui est necessarium. Unde Beda[^p75-8]: « De duabus tunicis dividendis datur praeceptum, quia, si una dividatur, nemo vestitur; nam nudus remanet et qui accipit et qui dedit ».

25. Et quod dixit de vestimento respectu defectus extrinseci dixit de *alimento* respectu intrinseci; unde et subdit: *Et qui habet escam similiter faciat,* scilicet si habet ultra necessitatem. Sic Iob, trigesimo primo[^p75-9]: « Si comedi buccellam meam solus »; et Tobias, quarto: « Panem tuum cum esurientibus et egenis comede ». — Et de duobus praedictis simul habetur Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo: « Frange esurienti panem tuum » etc.; et post: « Cum videris nudum, operi eum ». — Et nota hic, quod per *tunicam* et *escam* intelliguntur omnia, de quibus debet et potest fieri eleemosyna. Unde Hieronymus
<!-- page 76 -->
ad Hedibiam[^p76-1]: « Quidquid corpori nostro sufficere poterit et humanae imbecillitati succurrere, *una tunica* appellanda est. Nos enim nudos natura profudit; et quidquid in praesentibus alimentis necessarium est, hoc *unius diei victus* appellatur ».

26. Nota etiam, quod in modo docendi exprimit, *qualiter* danda sit eleemosyna et *quales conditiones* debeat habere. — Prima est, ut sit de *propria* substantia; propter quod dicit: *Qui habet;* Proverbiorum tertio[^p76-2]: « Honora Dominum de tua substantia » etc. — Secunda est, quod sit de re aliquantulum *necessaria,* non omnino superflua; et ideo dicit: *Duas tunicas.* Unde Beda[^p76-3]: « Per hoc, quod tunica plus est necessaria usui nostro quam pallium, ad dignum poenitentiae fructum pertinet, ut non solum exteriora quaeque et minus necessaria, sed etiam ipsa naturae necessaria cum proximis dividamus, ut escam, qua vivimus, et tunicam, qua vestimur ». Unde laudatur illa vidua paupercula Marci duodecimo, quae « de penuria sua omne, quod habuit, misit in gazophylacium », sed alii « ex eo quod eis abundabat ». — Tertio nota, quod debet esse *gratuita,* in hoc quod dicitur: *Det unam;* Tobiae quarto[^p76-4]: « Si multum tibi fuerit, abundanter tribue »; et Matthaei decimo: « Gratis accepistis, gratis date ». — Quarto nota, quod sit *indigenti tribuenda,* cum additur: *Det non habenti;* infra decimo quarto[^p76-5]: « Cum facis convivium, voca pauperes » etc.

27. Et nota, quod verbum praemissum a quibusdam expositoribus dicitur esse *praeceptum*[^p76-6], a quibusdam esse *consilium;* et hoc, quia superfluum, quod notatur in altera tunicarum, duplex est, scilicet respectu *naturae,* sed non personae; et hoc dare est perfectionis et *consilii;* superfluum autem *naturae et personae,* cum locus et tempus adest, et videt hominem indigentem, nisi reservet magis egenti, hoc dare est *praeceptum,* secundum illud primae Ioannis tertio[^p76-7]: « Qui viderit, fratrem suum necessitatem habere » etc.

28. (Vers. 12.). Secundo vero *publicanis requirentibus doctrinam* dat documentum de *legibus servandis,* cum additur: *Venerunt autem et publicani ad ipsum, ut baptizarentur.* « *Publicani* autem dicebantur illi qui publica vectigalia exigebant, sive qui conductores erant vectigalium fisci, vel rerum publicarum[^p76-8] », vel qui vectigalia regia accipiebant ad summam. Et isti iam veniebant ad Ioannem, quasi desiderantes salutem magis quam scribae; Matthaei vigesimo primo: « Publicani et meretrices praecedent vos in regnum caelorum ». Et exprimitur eorum diligentia, cum subditur *quaestio: Et dixerunt ad illum: Magister, quid faciemus?* Bene quidem in hoc faciebant, secundum consilium Isaiae primo[^p76-9]: « Quiescite agere perverse, discite bene facere »; et secundum illud Ieremiae sexto: « State super vias et interrogate de semitis antiquis, quae sit via bona, et ambulate in ea ».

29. (Vers. 13.). Et quia discrete quaerunt, ideo fructuose *erudiuntur,* cum subditur: *At ille dixit ad illos*[^p76-10]*: Nihil amplius, quam quod constitutum est vobis, faciatis; constitutum,* inquam, secundum legem divinam; Deuteronomii quarto: « Non addetis ad verbum, quod ego vobis loquor, neque minuetis ex eo; custodite mandata Dei vestri ». Sic respondit Dominus adolescenti diviti; Matthaei decimo nono[^p76-11]: « Si vis ad vitam ingredi, serva mandata », quia, secundum illud Psalmi, « in custodiendis illis retributio multa »; Ecclesiastae octavo: « Qui custodit praeceptum non experietur quidquam mali ». — Et nota prudentiam ipsius, qua unicuique praecipit secundum *posse* suum. Videns enim illos infirmos, non apponit gravia, sed quantum potest, mitigat sarcinam, secundum illud ad Romanos decimo quarto[^p76-12]: « Infirmum autem in fide assumite ». Unde Ioannes, qui sibi erat rigidissimus, publicanis infirmis factus est benignus, ut posset dicere illud Apostoli, primae ad Corinthios nono: « Factus sum infirmus, ut infirmos lucrifacerem. Omnibus omnia factus sum » etc.

30. Et nota, quod hic Ioannes videbatur publicanorum officium sustinere, cum tamen infra quinto[^p76-13] de Matthaeo publicano insinuetur illud officium *infame.* — Et propterea intelligendum est, quod ad hoc, quod tributa debite exigantur, requiritur debita *auctoritas,* debita *causa* et debita *mensura;* et hoc
<!-- page 77 -->
notat, cum dicit: *Nihil amplius,* ecce, *mensura; quam quod constitutum est,* ecce, *auctoritas; vobis,* id est ad utilitatem communitatis, ecce, *causa*[^p77-1]. Et quia haec raro servantur, ideo tales consueverunt esse peccatores et infames, non quia nullo modo bene possit fieri officium illud, sed quia difficile et rarum est, ut non excedant *constitutum*[^p77-2].

31. (Vers. 14.). Tertio vero *militibus percunctantibus* dat documentum de *rapinis vitandis,* cum subditur: *Interrogabant autem eum et milites, dicentes: Quid faciemus et nos?* Et recte, secundum consilium Tobiae quarto[^p77-3]: « Consilium semper a sapiente perquire ». Et in hoc magna apparet virtus in Ioanne praedicante, ut ad se trahat homines, qui maxime videntur mundani. Unde Glossa: « Magna vis in sermone Ioannis, qui et publicanos et milites ad consilium suae salutis cogit ». Et nota, quod quaerunt: *Quid faciemus?* secundum illud adolescentis, infra decimo octavo[^p77-4]: « Magister, quid faciens vitam aeternam possidebo »? Non sic Pharisaei, sed curiose requirunt; Ioannis primo: « Miserunt Iudaei ab Ierosolymis sacerdotes et Levitas ad Ioannem, ut interrogarent eum: Tu quis es »? et post: « Qui missi fuerant, erant ex Pharisaeis et interrogaverunt eum: Quid ergo baptizas »?

32. Et quoniam salutare consilium quaerebant, et verbum in tempore salutis non est retinendum[^p77-5], ideo responsum audierunt salutiferum, cum subditur: *Et ait illis.* In quo documento illicita prohibet, cum ait: *Neminem concutiatis,* id est opprimatis per vim potentiae; *neque calumniam faciatis,* sub specie iustitiae; Levitici decimo nono[^p77-6]: « Non facies calumniam proximo tuo nec vi opprimes eum ». Econtra malis malum comminatur Dominus Isaiae tertio: « Quare atteritis populum meum, et facies pauperum commolitis »? « Rapina pauperis in domo vestra ». In hoc consueverunt milites peccare; et ideo illam docet tanquam illicitam cavere.

33. Sed ne hoc videatur eis grave, ideo licita concedit, cum subdit: *Et contenti estote stipendiis vestris; stipendiis,* id est eventibus pro defensione reipublicae statutis a superioribus vestris, quia talis Deo acceptus est; Ezechielis decimo octavo[^p77-7]: « Si vir fuerit iustus et hominem non contristaverit, pignus debitori reddiderit, per vim nihil rapuerit etc., vita vivet ». Talis enim contentus est stipendiis suis. Sed non sunt tales exactores, de quibus Isaiae tertio[^p77-8]: « Populum meum exactores sui spoliaverunt ». Et ideo Augustinus de Verbis Domini: « Quisquis militiae suae cingulo utitur et stipendia sibi publice decreta consequitur; si amplius quaerit, tanquam calumniator est et concussor. Ideo enim stipendia militibus constituta sunt, ne, dum quaestus quaeritur, praedo grassetur ».

34. Et nota, quod videtur beatus Ioannes hic militare officium approbare, cum tamen videatur repugnare mandatis, quibus praecipitur, ut nullo modo se vindicet quis nec propellat iniuriam; immo dicitur Matthaei quinto[^p77-9]: « Qui te percusserit in dexteram maxillam tuam, praebe ei et alteram ». — Et ideo nota, quod usus militiae potest esse *licitus* et *illicitus;* ad *licitum* autem oportet quod concurrat *conveniens persona* et *causa:* persona scilicet *indicentis* bellum, in qua requiritur, quod habeat potestatem; item persona *agentis* bellum, quae debet esse laicus et saecularis, non clericus vel religiosus; item persona *patientis,* quae talis sit insolentiae, ut per bellum sit compescenda. — *Causa* autem *conveniens* est, cum est pro tutela patriae, vel pacis, vel fidei. Quando ergo haec concurrunt, tunc *licitum* est militare; sed quando deficit aliquid horum, utpote quia defectus est ex parte *personae,* vel *causae:* utpote cum clericus, vel vir religiosus, qui debet esse perfectus, vult bellum gerere, vel quando propter gloriam hominum, vel vindictam; tunc est *illicitum*[^p77-10]. — Quod autem obiicitur de mandato
<!-- page 78 -->
evangelico, dicendum, quod intelligitur de patientia habenda in animo, non de corporali exhibitione, sicut Augustinus ostendit in Sermone Domini in monte[^p78-1]. — Vel dic, quod illud dictum est perfectis, quorum non est « vinci a malo, sed vincere in bono malum[^p78-2] »; dictum etiam subditis, non praelatis vel principibus et eorum officialibus, quia, cum ipsi sint ministri legis, non ipsi puniunt vel occidunt, sed lex.

## English
<!-- page 75 -->

#### Second, on the baptism and preaching of John in special, as to three things.

22. *And the multitudes asked him* etc. After the Evangelist has commended the preaching of the precursor in general, here he commends it in *special* according to erudition with respect to special persons of the people[^p75-3]. Now his doctrine is commended in this part: first, in comparison with the *requirement of the people,* in administering sound doctrine; second, in comparison with the *excellence of Christ,* in avoiding false glory, there[^p75-4]: *And as the people were of opinion* etc.; third, in comparison with the *perfidy of the tyrant,* in sustaining the punishment inflicted, there: *And many other things.* In the first the *prudence* of John is commended; in the second, the *innocence* of John; in the third, his *patience.*

#### The preaching of John is commended first as to the requirement of the people, in three ways.

As to the first it is to be noted that, according to the threefold difference of the persons hearing, he administers diverse instructions: first, to the *multitudes,* who bear the person of *subjects;* second, to the *publicans,* who bear the person of *ministers;* third, to the *soldiers,* who bear the person of *rulers* or superiors. And in each of these parts the *question* is put first, then the *answer* is added.

23. (Vers. 10.). First, then, to the *multitudes asking* he gives an instruction on *alms to be given;* on account of which it is said: *And the multitudes asked him, saying: What then shall we do?* Since it is necessary to bring forth worthy fruits of penance, therefore teach us. The simple were more anxious to learn than the wise of the world; hence John, the seventh chapter[^p75-5]: « Hath any one of the rulers believed in him, but this multitude, that knoweth not the Law, are accursed ». But although they are reputed by men accursed and reprobate, it is the contrary way in the judgement of God; Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p75-6]: « Thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them to little ones »; and Proverbs, the third chapter: « And his communication is with the simple ». Such men asked John as a great prophet; Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter: « Ask thy father, and he will declare to thee » etc.

24. (Vers. 11.). And because they put a humble question, therefore they hear a useful *answer,* when it is added: *He that hath*[^p75-7] *two coats, let him give one to him that hath none.* The best counsel, because, as the Saviour says below, in the eleventh chapter, « give alms; and behold, all things are clean unto you ». So did Job, the thirty-first chapter: « If I have despised him that passed by, because he had no clothing, and the poor man without covering; if his sides have not blessed me, and he was warmed with the fleeces of my sheep » etc. — And note how discreetly he teaches, thus: *He that hath two coats,* that is, the one necessary, the other superfluous, let him give what is superfluous to himself to another, to whom it is necessary. Hence Bede[^p75-8]: « A precept is given about dividing two coats, because, if one be divided, no one is clothed; for both he who receives and he who gave remain naked ».

25. And what he said of clothing with respect to an outward defect, he said of *food* with respect to an inward one; hence he also adds: *And he that hath meat, let him do in like manner,* namely if he has it beyond necessity. So Job, the thirty-first chapter[^p75-9]: « If I have eaten my morsel alone »; and Tobias, the fourth chapter: « Eat thy bread with the hungry and the needy ». — And of the two aforesaid together it is had in Isaias, the fifty-eighth chapter: « Deal thy bread to the hungry » etc.; and after: « When thou shalt see one naked, cover him ». — And note here that by the *coat* and the *meat* are understood all things of which alms ought to be and can be made. Hence Jerome
<!-- page 76 -->
to Hedibia[^p76-1]: « Whatever can suffice for our body and succour human weakness is to be called *one coat.* For nature poured us forth naked; and whatever is necessary in present foods, this is called *the sustenance of one day* ».

26. Note also that in the manner of teaching he expresses *how* alms are to be given and *what conditions* they ought to have. — The first is that they be from one's *own* substance; on account of which he says: *He that hath;* Proverbs, the third chapter[^p76-2]: « Honour the Lord with thy substance » etc. — The second is that they be from a thing somewhat *necessary,* not wholly superfluous; and therefore he says: *Two coats.* Hence Bede[^p76-3]: « By this, that the coat is more necessary to our use than the cloak, it pertains to the worthy fruit of penance that we divide with our neighbours not only all outward and less necessary things, but also the very things necessary to nature, such as the meat by which we live and the coat with which we are clothed ». Hence that poor little widow is praised in Mark, the twelfth chapter, who « of her want cast in all that she had into the treasury », but the others « of that which they had in abundance ». — Thirdly note that they ought to be *gratuitous,* in this that it is said: *Let him give one;* Tobias, the fourth chapter[^p76-4]: « If thou have much, give abundantly »; and Matthew, the tenth chapter: « Freely have you received, freely give ». — Fourthly note that they are *to be bestowed on one in need,* when it is added: *Let him give to him that hath none;* below, in the fourteenth chapter[^p76-5]: « When thou makest a feast, call the poor » etc.

27. And note that the word set down above is said by some expositors to be a *precept*[^p76-6], by some to be a *counsel;* and this, because the superfluous which is noted in the second of the coats is twofold, namely with respect to *nature,* but not to the person; and to give this belongs to perfection and to *counsel;* but what is superfluous *to nature and to the person,* when place and time are present, and one sees a man in need, unless he keep it for one more needy, to give this is a *precept,* according to that saying of the first of John, the third chapter[^p76-7]: « He that shall see his brother in need » etc.

28. (Vers. 12.). But second, to the *publicans seeking doctrine* he gives an instruction on *laws to be kept,* when it is added: *And the publicans also came to him, to be baptized.* « Now they were called *publicans* who exacted the public taxes, or who were farmers of the taxes of the treasury, or of public things[^p76-8] », or who received the royal taxes in the lump. And these were now coming to John, as desiring salvation more than the scribes; Matthew, the twenty-first chapter: « The publicans and the harlots shall go into the kingdom of heaven before you ». And their diligence is expressed when the *question* is added: *And they said to him: Master, what shall we do?* Well indeed did they do in this, according to the counsel of Isaias, the first chapter[^p76-9]: « Cease to do perversely, learn to do well »; and according to that saying of Jeremias, the sixth chapter: « Stand ye on the ways and ask of the old paths, which is the good way, and walk ye in it ».

29. (Vers. 13.). And because they ask discreetly, therefore they *are schooled* fruitfully, when it is added: *But he said to them*[^p76-10]*: Do nothing more than that which is appointed you; appointed,* I say, according to the divine law; Deuteronomy, the fourth chapter: « You shall not add to the word that I speak to you, neither shall you take away from it; keep the commandments of your God ». So the Lord answered the rich young man; Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p76-11]: « If thou wilt enter into life, keep the commandments », because, according to that saying of the Psalm, « in keeping them there is a great reward »; Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter: « He that keepeth the commandment shall find no evil ». — And note his prudence, by which he commands each one according to his *power.* For seeing them weak, he does not lay on heavy things, but, as much as he can, lightens the burden, according to that saying to the Romans, the fourteenth chapter[^p76-12]: « Now him that is weak in faith, take unto you ». Hence John, who was most rigid to himself, became kind to the weak publicans, so that he could say that saying of the Apostle, the first to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter: « I became weak, that I might gain the weak. I became all things to all men » etc.

30. And note that here John seemed to uphold the office of the publicans, while yet below, in the fifth chapter[^p76-13], that office is intimated to be *infamous* in the case of Matthew the publican. — And therefore it is to be understood that, for taxes to be exacted duly, there is required due *authority,* due *cause* and due *measure;* and this
<!-- page 77 -->
he notes when he says: *Nothing more,* behold, the *measure; than that which is appointed,* behold, the *authority; you,* that is, for the utility of the community, behold, the *cause*[^p77-1]. And because these things are rarely kept, therefore such men have been wont to be sinners and infamous, not because that office can in no way be done well, but because it is difficult and rare that they not exceed what is *appointed*[^p77-2].

31. (Vers. 14.). But third, to the *soldiers inquiring* he gives an instruction on *plunder to be avoided,* when it is added: *And the soldiers also asked him, saying: And what shall we do?* And rightly, according to the counsel of Tobias, the fourth chapter[^p77-3]: « Seek counsel always of a wise man ». And in this a great power appears in John preaching, that he draws to himself men who seem most worldly. Hence the Gloss: « Great is the force in the sermon of John, who drives both publicans and soldiers to the counsel of their salvation ». And note that they ask: *What shall we do?* according to that saying of the young man, below, in the eighteenth chapter[^p77-4]: « Master, what shall I do to possess everlasting life »? Not so the Pharisees, but they inquire curiously; John, the first chapter: « The Jews sent from Jerusalem priests and Levites to John, to ask him: Who art thou »? and after: « They that were sent were of the Pharisees, and they asked him: Why then dost thou baptize »?

32. And since they were seeking salutary counsel, and the word is not to be withheld in the time of salvation[^p77-5], therefore they heard a salvific answer, when it is added: *And he said to them.* In which instruction he forbids things unlawful, when he says: *Do violence to no man,* that is, oppress no one by force of power; *neither calumniate any man,* under the appearance of justice; Leviticus, the nineteenth chapter[^p77-6]: « Thou shalt not calumniate thy neighbour, nor oppress him by violence ». On the other hand the Lord threatens evil to the wicked in Isaias, the third chapter: « Why do you consume my people, and grind the faces of the poor »? « The spoil of the poor is in your house ». In this soldiers have been wont to sin; and therefore he teaches them to beware of it as unlawful.

33. But lest this seem heavy to them, therefore he grants things lawful, when he adds: *And be content with your pay; pay,* that is, the proceeds appointed by your superiors for the defence of the commonwealth, because such a one is acceptable to God; Ezechiel, the eighteenth chapter[^p77-7]: « If a man be just, and hath not grieved any man, hath restored the pledge to the debtor, hath taken nothing away by violence etc., he shall surely live ». For such a one is content with his pay. But such are not the exactors of whom Isaias, the third chapter[^p77-8]: « My people, their exactors have spoiled them ». And therefore Augustine, *On the Words of the Lord*: « Whoever uses the belt of his military service and obtains the pay publicly decreed for him; if he seeks more, he is as a calumniator and an extortioner. For pay has been appointed for soldiers for this reason, lest, while gain is sought, the plunderer run riot ».

34. And note that blessed John seems here to approve the military office, while yet it seems to be repugnant to the commandments by which it is commanded that one in no way avenge himself nor repel injury; nay, it is said in Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p77-9]: « If one strike thee on thy right cheek, turn to him also the other ». — And therefore note that the practice of military service can be *lawful* and *unlawful;* but for the *lawful* it is needful that a *fitting person* and *cause* concur: namely the person of the one *declaring* war, in which it is required that he have the power; likewise the person of the one *waging* war, who ought to be a layman and secular, not a cleric or a religious; likewise the person of the one *suffering* it, who should be of such insolence that he must be restrained by war. — But the *cause* is *fitting* when it is for the protection of the fatherland, or of peace, or of the faith. When therefore these concur, then it is *lawful* to serve as a soldier; but when any of these is lacking, as, for instance, because there is a defect on the part of the *person,* or of the *cause:* as when a cleric, or a religious man, who ought to be perfect, wishes to wage war, or when it is for the glory of men, or for vengeance; then it is *unlawful*[^p77-10]. — But as to what is objected about the
<!-- page 78 -->
evangelical commandment, it must be said that it is understood of patience to be had in the mind, not of bodily display, as Augustine shows in *The Sermon of the Lord on the Mount*[^p78-1]. — Or say that this was said to the perfect, whose part it is « not to be overcome by evil, but to overcome evil by good[^p78-2] »; said also to subjects, not to prelates or princes and their officials, because, since they are ministers of the law, it is not they who punish or kill, but the law.
