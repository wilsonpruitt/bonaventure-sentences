# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XIII, Vers. 18-21
## *Dicebat ergo: Cui simile etc. Postquam confutavit calumniantes miracula in Sabbato facta, hic secundo ostendit, quod sine cessatione facienda sunt opera bona*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 346 -->
36. *Dicebat ergo: Cui simile* etc. Postquam confutavit calumniantes miracula in Sabbato facta, hic secundo ostendit, quod *sine cessatione facienda sunt opera bona.* Et primo ad hoc incitat per *manuductionem naturalis exempli;* secundo vero, per *manifestationem divini decreti,* ibi[^p346-1]: *Et ibat per civitates et castella.*

Manuducit igitur per *exemplum* dupliciter: per quorum primum informamur ad *proficiendum continue in notitia veritatis;* per secundum vero, ad *proficiendum in fervore caritatis,* ibi[^p346-2]: *Et iterum dixit: Cui simile.*

Primo igitur *continuum profectum in notitia veritatis* ostendit sub metaphora *grani sinapis,* et hoc tripliciter, videlicet quantum ad *proprietates,* quas granum sinapis habet secundum *se ipsum*[^p346-3] et in quantum *seminatum* et in quantum *augmentatum.*

37. (Vers. 18.). Primo igitur quantum ad *proprietatem grani sinapis secundum se ipsum,* per quam assimilatur notitiae veritatis, dicit: *Dicebat ergo: Cui simile est regnum Dei, et cui simile aestimabo*[^p346-4] *illud? Regnum caelorum* dicitur hic veritatis notitia vel doctrina; unde Beda in Glossa: « Regnum caelorum praedicatio est Evangelii, de qua alibi: *Auferetur a vobis regnum Dei et dabitur genti facienti fructus eius,* secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo primo ». Haec notitia recte dicitur *regnum,* quia revera regnum caelorum non est aliud quam perfecta notitia divinae veritatis; Ioannis decimo septimo[^p346-5]: « Haec est vita aeterna, ut cognoscant te solum Deum verum et quem misisti Iesum Christum ».

38. (Vers. 19.). Et haec in patria est *sublimis* et *iucunda,* sed in via debet esse *humilis* et *fervida,* in cuius designationem subdit: *Simile est grano sinapis.* Ideo *grano sinapis* assimilatur, quia ipsum est *modicum mole* et *magnum in virtute* sive fervore; in quo insinuatur, quod nostra notitia debet esse *humilis,* secundum illud ad Romanos undecimo[^p346-6]: « Noli altum sapere, sed time »; et duodecimo: « Non plus sapere quam oportet sapere, sed sapere ad sobrietatem, et unicuique sicut divisit Deus mensuram fidei », quia, ut dicitur Proverbiorum undecimo, « ubi humilitas, ibi et sapientia » etc. Insinuatur etiam, quod debet esse *fervida* et ardens; unde Glossa Bedae[^p346-7]: « Notitia Scripturarum *grano sinapis* comparatur pro fervore fidei, vel quia venena dicitur expellere, id est omnia dogmata pravitatis ». Ideo dicitur infra decimo septimo: « Si habueritis fidem sicut granum sinapis, dicetis huic arbori moro: Eradicare et transplantare in mare, et obediet vobis ».

39. Secundo quantum ad *proprietatem ipsius in quantum seminatum* subdit: *Quod acceptum homo misit in hortum suum.* Per *hortum* istum intelligitur Ecclesia militans; Canticorum quarto[^p346-8]: « Hortus conclusus, fons signatus, soror mea sponsa, hortus conclusus ». Hic est *hortus voluptatis,* « ubi crescunt semina virtutum[^p346-9] »; in quo primo *seminandum* est semen fidei; sed hoc fit per *hominem,* quia praedicatio fidei per humanum ministerium seminatur; in cuius rei figuram Genesis secundo: « Tulit Deus hominem et posuit eum in paradiso voluptatis, ut operaretur et custodiret illum ». Talis fuit Paulus; primae ad Corinthios tertio[^p346-10]: « Ego plantavi, Apollo rigavit, sed Deus incrementum dedit ». Hominis enim, id est rationalis et prudentis, est praedicare documenta fidei; summus autem seminator huius fidei fuit Christus, qui *homo* dicitur antonomastice, secundum illud Psalmi[^p346-11]: « Nunquid Sion dicet: Homo et homo natus est in ea » etc. Unde et ipse vocat se ubique *Filium hominis,* quia homo fuit
<!-- page 347 -->
in eo secundum plenissimam rationem et quantum ad perfectionem et quantum ad infirmitatem; ad Philippenses secundo[^p347-1]: « In similitudinem hominum factus et habitu inventus ut homo ». Et hic *homo seminavit* hoc semen, cum doctrinam Evangelii praedicavit; Matthaei decimo tertio[^p347-2]: « Simile est regnum caelorum homini, qui seminavit bonum semen in agro suo ». Huic homini adversatur qui praedicat falsitates. Unde subditur ibi: « Cum autem dormirent homines », id est praelati, « venit inimicus homo et superseminavit zizania in medio tritici » etc.

40. Tertio quantum ad *proprietatem ipsius in quantum augmentatum* subdit: *Et crevit et factum est in arborem magnam;* quod dicitur quantum ad profectum fidei in robore virium. Unde Glossa[^p347-3]: « Crescit, non sicut herbae, quae cito arescunt et corruunt, sed sicut arbor, quae longa annositate et inopinata fertilitate gaudet »; ad Colossenses primo[^p347-4]: « In verbo veritatis Evangelii, quod pervenit ad vos, sicut et in universo mundo est et fructificat et crescit, sicut et in vobis »; et paulo post: « Ambuletis digne Deo per omnia placentes, in omni opere bono fructificantes et crescentes in scientia Dei ». Haec autem notitia tanto altius crescit per veritatem, quanto radix profundius descendit per humilitatem; unde Isaiae trigesimo septimo[^p347-5]: « Mittet illud quod salvatum fuerit de domo Iuda et quod reliquum fuerit, radices deorsum et faciet fructum sursum ».

41. Et quia notitia fidei crescens in se redundat in aliorum salutem, ideo subdit: *Et volucres caeli requieverunt*[^p347-6] *in ramis eius.* Per *volucres caeli* intelliguntur viri spirituales; Isaiae sexagesimo: « Qui sunt hi qui ut nubes volant et quasi columbae ad fenestras suas »? de quibus Proverbiorum primo: « Frustra iacitur rete ante oculos pennatorum ». Per *ramos* arboris intelliguntur documenta veritatis procedentia ex ore sapientis; unde Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto[^p347-7] dicit Sapientia: « Ego quasi terebinthus extendi ramos meos, et rami mei honoris et gratiae ». *Volucres* ergo *caeli requiescunt* in ramis sinapis crescentis, quia quietantur sub doctrina fidelis doctoris qui ad notitiam veritatis perveniunt. Unde Glossa[^p347-8]: « *In ramis eius,* id est in diversis sententiis viri spirituales requiescunt, qui pennis virtutum in alta conscendunt ». In cuius figuram Danielis quarto[^p347-9]: « Vidi, et ecce, arbor in medio terrae ». « Folia eius pulcherrima, et fructus eius nimius, et esca universorum in ea. Subter eam habitabant bestiae terrae, et in ramis eius conversabantur volucres caeli ». — Sic ergo parvulum[^p347-10] semen crescit in magnitudinem arboris per continuum incrementum. Qui ergo parvulus est laborare continue debet in profectum meriti, ut sic arbor fiat, cuius cacumen pertingat ad caelum per spem et desiderium aeternorum.

42. (Vers. 20.). *Et iterum dixit: Cui simile* etc. Postquam praemisit exemplum incitativum ad proficiendum continue in notitia veritatis, hic adiungit *exemplum incitativum ad proficiendum in fervore caritatis,* et hoc sub metaphora *fermenti*[^p347-11], ex cuius assimilatione incitat ad continue proficiendum in caritate quantum ad eius similitudinariam proprietatem triplicem, scilicet quam habet fermentum ex parte *naturae propriae,* ex parte *industriae alienae* et ex parte *utriusque.*

Primo igitur quantum ad proprietatem, quam habet ex parte *naturae propriae,* per quam intendit nos ad caritatis profectum excitare, dicit: *Et iterum dixit: Cui simile aestimabo*[^p347-12] *regnum Dei?* Et hic per *regnum Dei* recte intelligitur amor Dei, quia in solis illis regnat Deus, qui ipsum diligunt; ad Colossenses primo[^p347-13]: « Eripuit nos de potestate tenebrarum et transtulit in regnum filii caritatis suae »; quia, ut dicitur ad Romanos decimo quarto, « regnum Dei non est esca et potus, sed iustitia et pax et gaudium in Spiritu sancto ».

43. (Vers. 21.). Et quoniam caritas et gaudium cor dilatat et calefacit ab interiori, et hoc est similitudo exterior[^p347-14] cum fermento; ideo subdit: *Simile est fermento.* — Et nota, quod per *fermentum* aliquando intelligitur corruptio pacis et unitatis, sicut supra duodecimo[^p347-15]: « Attendite a fermento Pharisaeorum, quod est hypocrisis »; et hoc satis recte, quia fermentum est pasta vetus et ex vetustate corrupta et in acredinem versa, quae reliquam massam corrumpit et in acredinem vertit. Ideo signanter dicitur primae ad Corinthios quinto[^p347-16]: « Expurgate vetus fermentum ». — Aliquando per *fermentum* intelligitur fervor et dilectio, sicut hic[^p347-17], quia fermentum pastam calefacit et quendam fervorem inducit quasi ab
<!-- page 348 -->
occulto et interiori; unde non incongrue *fermentum* est caritas. Nec est contrarium rationi, quod *fermentum* accipiatur sic et sic ratione diversae proprietatis. Unde Augustinus, tertio libro de Doctrina christiana[^p348-1]: « Quoniam multis modis res similes rebus apparent, non putemus, esse praescriptum, ut quod in aliquo loco res aliqua per similitudinem significaverit, hoc eam semper significare credamus. Nam et in *vituperatione* posuit *fermentum* Dominus, ut diceret: *Cavete a fermento Pharisaeorum;* et in *laude,* cum diceret: *Simile est regnum caelorum mulieri, quae abscondit fermentum in farinae sata tria* ». Huiusmodi autem multa, ut dicit[^p348-2], sunt similia in Scriptura, sicut « *leo Christum* significat, Apocalypsis quinto: *Vicit leo de tribu Iuda; diabolum,* primae Petri quinto: *Adversarius vester diabolus tanquam leo rugiens* etc.; sic et alia plurima ». Cuius diversitatis causa est multiplicitas proprietatum, ex quibus diversitas similitudinum et repraesentationum oritur in figuris.

44. Secundo quantum ad proprietatem ex parte *industriae alienae* subdit: *Quod acceptum mulier abscondit in farinae sata tria.* Secundum litteram, ut dicit Beda[^p348-3], « *satum* est genus mensurae iuxta morem Palaestinae modium et dimidium capiens ».

Et nota, quod per hanc *mulierem* intelligitur sapientia divina[^p348-4], vel Ecclesia; Proverbiorum ultimo: « Mulierem fortem quis inveniet » etc. *Farina* autem sunt fideles, qui teruntur inter duas molas, superiorem scilicet et inferiorem, timorem videlicet et spem; Deuteronomii vigesimo quarto[^p348-5]: « Non accipies loco pignoris inferiorem et superiorem molam ». Ex qua farina fit *unus panis,* quem Christus in corpus suum mysticum traiicit; primae ad Corinthios decimo: « Multi unus panis sumus in Christo ». — Sed *tria sata* sunt *tres differentiae fidelium,* in quibus Dei sapientia dilectionem abscondit in via, scilicet boni *praelati,* boni *contemplativi* et boni *activi.* Unde Glossa[^p348-6]: « Tria sata, tria genera hominum: Noe, Daniel et Iob »; Ezechielis decimo quarto: « Si fuerint in medio eius Noe, Daniel et Iob, iustitia sua liberabuntur ». Haec igitur prima expositio magis secundum litteram consonat; aliter et multipliciter exponit Beda in Glossa[^p348-7], ut ex hoc appareat « multiformis sapientia Dei », « quae in mysterio abscondita est ».

Secundo per *sata tria* intelliguntur *tres modi diligendi,* scilicet ex toto corde, ex tota anima et ex tota mente. Unde Glossa Bedae[^p348-8]: « Abscondit Ecclesia fermentum dilectionis in farinae sata tria, quia praecipit, ut diligamus Deum ex toto corde, ex tota anima et ex tota virtute »; Deuteronomii sexto: « Diliges Dominum Deum tuum » etc.; et Matthaei vigesimo secundo: « Diliges Dominum Deum tuum » etc. « In hoc mandato » etc.

Tertio modo sic, ut per *tria sata* intelligantur *spiritus* et *anima* et *corpus,* ex quibus homo integratur. Glossa[^p348-9]: « Ut spiritus et anima et corpus, redacta in unum, non discrepent inter se », secundum illud primae ad Thessalonicenses quinto: « Integer spiritus vester et anima et corpus servetur sine querela in die adventus Domini » etc.

Quarto modo sic, ut per *tria sata* intelligantur *tres animae vires,* quae reformantur caritate. Glossa[^p348-10]: « Tres animae vires in unum redigantur, ut in *ratione* possideamus prudentiam, in *ira* odium vitiorum, in *cupiditate* desiderium virtutum », secundum illud Michaeae sexto: « Facere iudicium et diligere misericordiam et sollicitum ambulare cum Deo tuo ».

45. Quinto modo sic, ut caritas iuncta sit et quodam modo immixta *fidei Trinitatis,* ut non tantum credat quis Deum verum, sed etiam credendo tendat in eum. Unde Glossa[^p348-11]: « Ecclesia fidem hominis in farinae sata tria miscet, id est credulitati
<!-- page 349 -->
Patris et Filii et Spiritus sancti ». Unde de caritate dicitur primae ad Corinthios decimo tertio, quod « omnia credit ».

Sexto modo sic: per *tria sata, triplex fructus,* ad quem caritas ordinat; unde Glossa[^p349-1]: « Possunt in his *satis* illius dominici seminis fructus intelligi, trigesimus scilicet, sexagesimus et centesimus, id est coniugatorum, continentium et virginum; de quibus Matthaei decimo tertio: « Fructum affert, et facit aliud quidem centesimum, aliud sexagesimum, aliud vero trigesimum ».

Septimo, per *tria sata* farinae possunt intelligi *tres partes generis humani* a tribus filiis Noe disseminatae per universum orbem; de quibus Genesis decimo[^p349-2]: « Hae sunt generationes Noe: Sem, Cham et Iapheth ».

Octavo modo, secundum Hilarium[^p349-3], per *farinam* intelligitur Scriptura; per *tria sata* tres partes Scripturae, scilicet Moysaica, prophetica et evangelica. Et in his abscondit Dei sapientia caritatem, quia, Matthaei vigesimo secundo, « in his duobus mandatis tota Lex pendet et Prophetae ».

Nono modo, secundum Ambrosium[^p349-4] sic: « *Mulier,* Ecclesia; *farina* eius nos sumus; quae Dominum Iesum abscondit ut *fermentum* in interioribus nostris, donec ea calor caelestis sapientiae obducat ».

Decimo modo, secundum Bernardum[^p349-5] sic: « *Mulier,* virgo Maria; *tria sata,* triplex natura seu substantia in una persona Christi, scilicet caro, anima et Divinitas. Primum satum est *antiquum,* secundum est *novum,* sed tertium *aeternum.* Haec autem miscuit per *fermentum* suae fidei et dilectionis ». Unde Hugo[^p349-6]: « Quia amor in mente eius singulariter ardebat, ideo in carne eius miracula faciebat ». In cuius figura dicitur Genesis decimo octavo: « Festinavit Abraham in tabernaculum ad Saram dixitque ei: Accelera, tria sata similae commisce ». Et sic fecit beata Virgo, quia ad verbum Angeli statim consensit: « Ecce, inquiens[^p349-7], ancilla Domini » etc.

46. Ex his igitur apparet, quomodo de una re parvula Spiritus sanctus varios intellectus exterius eduxit per Sanctos suos, ut ex hoc appareat « multiformis sapientia Dei[^p349-8] », quae ad modum mulieris fermentum in satis abscondit. Nam, sicut dicit Dionysius[^p349-9], tota mystica theologia, « quae scilicet in mysterio abscondita est », ipsa tota consistit in dilectione excessiva secundum triplicem vim hierarchicam: purgativam, illuminativam et perfectivam.

47. Tertio quantum ad effectum ex parte *utriusque* subdit: *Donec fermentaretur totum;* Glossa[^p349-10]: « Tamdiu caritas in mente abscondita crescere debet, donec mentem totam in sui perfectionem commutet, ut nihil praeter Deum diligat ». Sicut enim fermentum magis ac magis diffunditur, sic et caritas Dei. Unde Gregorius[^p349-11]: « Nunquam est amor Dei otiosus; operatur enim magna, si est; si autem operari renuit, amor non est »; et Bernardus: « Caritas aut proficit, aut deficit »; unde[^p349-12] « caritas nunquam excidit », sed totum ad modum fermenti et ignis in sui naturam convertit. Unde Exodi decimo nono: « Totus mons Sinai fumabat, eo quod descendisset Dominus super eum in igne ». — Hic enim ignis et calor caritatis inchoatur in via, sed totum cor occupat in patria; Isaiae trigesimo primo[^p349-13]: « Dixit Dominus, cuius ignis est in Sion et caminus in Ierusalem ». Unde sicut a corde, quod est in centro, manat vitalis calor in totum corpus, sic a caritate perfecta omnes congregationes operum virtutum calorem et vigorem suscipiunt, per quem tendant sursum. Et sicut dicitur[^p349-14]: « Si oculus tuus fuerit simplex, totum corpus tuum lucidum erit »; sic, si cor tuum fuerit *calens,* totum corpus tuum calefactum erit; si cor *divinum,*
<!-- page 350 -->
totus homo divinus per amorem deificum, secundum illud Psalmi: « Defecit caro mea et cor meum; Deus cordis mei, et pars mea Deus in aeternum ».

## English
<!-- page 346 -->
36. *He said therefore: To what is it like* etc. After he had confuted those who calumniated the miracles done on the Sabbath, here secondly he shows that *good works are to be done without cessation.* And first he spurs on to this through *the leading by the hand of a natural example;* but secondly, through *the manifestation of a divine decree,* there[^p346-1]: *And he went through the cities and towns.*

He leads by the hand[^tr-corrigenda], then, through an *example* in two ways: by the first of which we are formed to *make progress continually in the knowledge of the truth;* but by the second, to *make progress in the fervour of charity,* there[^p346-2]: *And again he said: To what is it like.*

First, then, he shows *continual progress in the knowledge of the truth* under the metaphor of *a grain of mustard seed,* and this in three ways, namely as to the *properties* which a grain of mustard seed has according to *itself*[^p346-3] and inasmuch as *sown* and inasmuch as *grown.*

37. (Vers. 18.). First, then, as to *the property of the grain of mustard seed according to itself,* through which it is likened to the knowledge of the truth, he says: *He said therefore: To what is the kingdom of God like, and whereunto shall I esteem*[^p346-4] *it like? The kingdom of heaven* is here said to be the knowledge or doctrine of the truth; whence Bede in the Gloss: « The kingdom of heaven is the preaching of the Gospel, of which it is said elsewhere: *The kingdom of God shall be taken from you, and shall be given to a nation yielding the fruits thereof,* according to that of Matthew, the twenty-first chapter ». This knowledge is rightly called a *kingdom,* because in truth the kingdom of heaven is nothing other than the perfect knowledge of the divine truth; John, the seventeenth chapter[^p346-5]: « This is eternal life: that they may know thee, the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent ».

38. (Vers. 19.). And this in the fatherland is *sublime* and *delightful,* but on the way it ought to be *humble* and *fervent,* in designation of which he subjoins: *It is like to a grain of mustard seed.* It is likened to *a grain of mustard seed* for this reason, that it is *small in bulk* and *great in power* or fervour; in which it is intimated that our knowledge ought to be *humble,* according to that to the Romans, the eleventh chapter[^p346-6]: « Be not highminded, but fear »; and the twelfth: « Not to be more wise than it behoveth to be wise, but to be wise unto sobriety, and according as God hath divided to every one the measure of faith »; because, as is said in Proverbs, the eleventh chapter, « where humility is, there also is wisdom » etc. It is intimated also that it ought to be *fervent* and burning; whence the Gloss of Bede[^p346-7]: « The knowledge of the Scriptures is compared to *a grain of mustard seed* for the fervour of faith, or because it is said to drive out poisons, that is, all the dogmas of depravity ». Therefore it is said below, in the seventeenth chapter: « If you had faith like to a grain of mustard seed, you might say to this mulberry tree: Be thou rooted up, and be thou transplanted into the sea: and it would obey you ».

39. Secondly, as to *its property inasmuch as sown,* he subjoins: *Which a man took and cast into his garden.* By this *garden* is understood the Church militant; the Canticle, the fourth chapter[^p346-8]: « A garden enclosed, a fountain sealed up, my sister, my spouse, a garden enclosed ». This is *the garden of pleasure,* « where the seeds of the virtues grow[^p346-9] »; in which the seed of faith must first be *sown;* but this is done by *a man,* because the preaching of the faith is sown through human ministry; in figure of which thing Genesis, the second chapter: « God took man, and put him into the paradise of pleasure, to dress it, and to keep it ». Such was Paul; the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p346-10]: « I have planted, Apollo watered, but God gave the increase ». For it belongs to a man, that is, to one rational and prudent, to preach the lessons of the faith; but the highest sower of this faith was Christ, who is called *man* by antonomasia, according to that of the Psalm[^p346-11]: « Shall not Sion say: This man and that man is born in her » etc. Whence he also calls himself everywhere *the Son of man,* because he was man
<!-- page 347 -->
in him according to the fullest account, both as to perfection and as to infirmity; to the Philippians, the second chapter[^p347-1]: « Being made in the likeness of men, and in habit found as a man ». And this *man sowed* this seed when he preached the doctrine of the Gospel; Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p347-2]: « The kingdom of heaven is likened to a man that sowed good seed in his field ». To this man he is opposed who preaches falsehoods. Whence it is subjoined there: « But while men were asleep », that is, prelates, « his enemy came and oversowed cockle among the wheat » etc.

40. Thirdly, as to *its property inasmuch as grown,* he subjoins: *And it grew and became a great tree;* which is said as to the progress of the faith in the strength of its powers. Whence the Gloss[^p347-3]: « It grows, not as the herbs, which quickly wither and fall, but as a tree, which rejoices in long years and unlooked-for fertility »; to the Colossians, the first chapter[^p347-4]: « In the word of the truth of the Gospel, which is come unto you, as also it is in the whole world, and bringeth forth fruit and groweth, even as it doth in you »; and a little after: « That you may walk worthy of God, in all things pleasing; being fruitful in every good work, and increasing in the knowledge of God ». But this knowledge grows the higher through truth, the more deeply the root descends through humility; whence Isaias, the thirty-seventh chapter[^p347-5]: « He shall send that which shall be saved of the house of Juda, and that which is left, roots downward, and shall bear fruit upward ».

41. And because the knowledge of the faith, growing in itself, overflows into the salvation of others, therefore he subjoins: *And the birds of the air rested*[^p347-6] *in the branches thereof.* By *the birds of the air* are understood spiritual men; Isaias, the sixtieth chapter: « Who are these, that fly as clouds, and as doves to their windows »? of whom Proverbs, the first chapter: « A net is spread in vain before the eyes of them that have wings ». By the *branches* of the tree are understood the lessons of truth proceeding out of the mouth of the wise; whence in Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p347-7], Wisdom says: « I as the turpentine have stretched out my branches, and my branches are of honour and grace ». *The birds,* then, *of the air rest* in the branches of the growing mustard, because those who come to the knowledge of the truth are set at quiet under the doctrine of a faithful doctor. Whence the Gloss[^p347-8]: « *In the branches thereof,* that is, in the divers sentences, spiritual men rest, who mount up on high on the wings of the virtues ». In figure of which, Daniel, the fourth chapter[^p347-9]: « I saw, and behold a tree in the midst of the earth ». « The leaves thereof were most beautiful, and the fruit thereof exceeding much, and in it was food for all. Under it dwelt the beasts of the earth, and in the branches thereof the fowls of the air had their abode ». — Thus, then, the small[^p347-10] seed grows into the greatness of a tree through continual increase. He, then, who is small ought to labour continually toward the progress of merit, that thus he may become a tree whose top may reach to heaven through hope and the desire of eternal things.

42. (Vers. 20.). *And again he said: To what is it like* etc. Having first put forward an example spurring on to make progress continually in the knowledge of the truth, here he adds *an example spurring on to make progress in the fervour of charity,* and this under the metaphor of *leaven*[^p347-11], by likening to which he spurs on to make progress continually in charity as to its threefold property of likeness, namely that which leaven has on the side of *its own nature,* on the side of *another's industry,* and on the side of *both.*

First, then, as to the property which it has on the side of *its own nature,* through which he intends to stir us up to progress in charity, he says: *And again he said: Whereunto shall I esteem*[^p347-12] *the kingdom of God to be like?* And here by *the kingdom of God* is rightly understood the love of God, because God reigns in those alone who love him; to the Colossians, the first chapter[^p347-13]: « Who hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the kingdom of the Son of his charity »; because, as is said to the Romans, the fourteenth chapter, « the kingdom of God is not meat and drink, but justice and peace and joy in the Holy Spirit ».

43. (Vers. 21.). And since charity and joy dilate the heart and warm it from within, and this is the outward likeness[^p347-14] with leaven; therefore he subjoins: *It is like to leaven.* — And note that by *leaven* is sometimes understood the corruption of peace and unity, as above, in the twelfth chapter[^p347-15]: « Beware ye of the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy »; and this quite rightly, because leaven is old dough, corrupted by its oldness and turned to sourness, which corrupts the rest of the mass and turns it to sourness. Therefore it is said pointedly in the first to the Corinthians, the fifth chapter[^p347-16]: « Purge out the old leaven ». — Sometimes by *leaven* is understood fervour and dilection, as here[^p347-17], because leaven warms the dough and brings on a certain fervour as it were from
<!-- page 348 -->
what is hidden and inward; whence not unfittingly *leaven* is charity. Nor is it contrary to reason that *leaven* should be taken now this way and now that by reason of a diverse property. Whence Augustine, in the third book *On Christian Doctrine*[^p348-1]: « Since things appear like to things in many ways, let us not think it prescribed that what a thing has signified by likeness in some place, we should believe it always to signify. For the Lord put *leaven* both in *reproof,* when he said: *Beware of the leaven of the Pharisees;* and in *praise,* when he said: *The kingdom of heaven is like to a woman who hid leaven in three measures of meal* ». And many things of this kind, as he says[^p348-2], are like in Scripture, as « *the lion signifies Christ,* Apocalypse, the fifth chapter: *The lion of the tribe of Juda hath prevailed; the devil,* the first of Peter, the fifth chapter: *Your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion* etc.; and so too very many others ». The cause of this diversity is the multiplicity of properties, from which there arises the diversity of likenesses and representations in figures.

44. Secondly, as to the property on the side of *another's industry,* he subjoins: *Which a woman took and hid in three measures of meal.* According to the letter, as Bede says[^p348-3], « a *measure* (*satum*) is a kind of measure, according to the custom of Palestine, holding a modius and a half ».

And note that by this *woman* is understood the divine wisdom[^p348-4], or the Church; Proverbs, the last chapter: « Who shall find a valiant woman » etc. But the *meal* are the faithful, who are ground between two millstones, namely the upper and the lower, that is, fear and hope; Deuteronomy, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p348-5]: « Thou shalt not take the nether nor the upper millstone to pledge ». Of which meal is made *one bread,* which Christ passes into his mystical body; the first to the Corinthians, the tenth chapter: « We, being many, are one bread in Christ ». — But *the three measures* are *three differences of the faithful,* in whom the wisdom of God hides dilection on the way, namely good *prelates,* good *contemplatives* and good *actives.* Whence the Gloss[^p348-6]: « Three measures, three kinds of men: Noe, Daniel and Job »; Ezechiel, the fourteenth chapter: « If Noe, Daniel and Job shall be in the midst thereof, they shall deliver by their justice ». This first exposition, then, accords more with the letter; Bede in the Gloss expounds otherwise and in many ways[^p348-7], that from this there may appear « the manifold wisdom of God », « which is hidden in a mystery ».

Secondly, by *the three measures* are understood *three modes of loving,* namely with the whole heart, with the whole soul and with the whole mind. Whence the Gloss of Bede[^p348-8]: « The Church hides the leaven of dilection in three measures of meal, because she commands that we love God with the whole heart, with the whole soul and with the whole strength »; Deuteronomy, the sixth chapter: « Thou shalt love the Lord thy God » etc.; and Matthew, the twenty-second chapter: « Thou shalt love the Lord thy God » etc. « On this commandment » etc.

Thirdly, in this way: that by *the three measures* are understood *spirit* and *soul* and *body,* of which man is made whole. The Gloss[^p348-9]: « That spirit and soul and body, brought back into one, may not be at variance among themselves », according to that of the first to the Thessalonians, the fifth chapter: « That your whole spirit, and soul, and body, may be preserved blameless in the coming of our Lord » etc.

Fourthly, in this way: that by *the three measures* are understood *the three powers of the soul,* which are reformed by charity. The Gloss[^p348-10]: « Let the three powers of the soul be brought back into one, that in *reason* we may possess prudence, in *anger* the hatred of vices, in *cupidity* the desire of virtues », according to that of Micheas, the sixth chapter: « To do judgement, and to love mercy, and to walk solicitous with thy God ».

45. Fifthly, in this way: that charity be joined and in a certain manner mixed with *the faith of the Trinity,* so that one not only believe the true God, but also by believing tend into him. Whence the Gloss[^p348-11]: « The Church mixes the faith of man in three measures of meal, that is, with the belief
<!-- page 349 -->
of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit ». Whence of charity it is said in the first to the Corinthians, the thirteenth chapter, that it « believeth all things ».

Sixthly, in this way: by *the three measures, the threefold fruit,* to which charity orders; whence the Gloss[^p349-1]: « In these *measures* can be understood the fruits of that seed of the Lord, the thirtyfold, namely, the sixtyfold and the hundredfold, that is, of the married, of the continent and of virgins; of which Matthew, the thirteenth chapter: « He beareth fruit, and yieldeth the one an hundredfold, and another sixty, and another thirty ».

Seventhly, by *the three measures* of meal can be understood *the three parts of the human race,* scattered through the whole world from the three sons of Noe; of which Genesis, the tenth chapter[^p349-2]: « These are the generations of Noe: Sem, Cham and Japheth ».

Eighthly, according to Hilary[^p349-3], by *the meal* is understood Scripture; by *the three measures* the three parts of Scripture, namely the Mosaic, the prophetic and the evangelical. And in these the wisdom of God has hidden charity, because, Matthew, the twenty-second chapter, « on these two commandments dependeth the whole law and the prophets ».

Ninthly, according to Ambrose[^p349-4], thus: « *The woman,* the Church; *her meal* are we; who hides the Lord Jesus as *leaven* in our inward parts, until the warmth of heavenly wisdom draw them over ».

Tenthly, according to Bernard[^p349-5], thus: « *The woman,* the Virgin Mary; *the three measures,* the threefold nature or substance in the one person of Christ, namely flesh, soul and Divinity. The first measure is *ancient,* the second is *new,* but the third *eternal.* And these she mixed through the *leaven* of her faith and dilection ». Whence Hugh[^p349-6]: « Because love burned singularly in her mind, therefore it worked miracles in her flesh ». In figure of which it is said in Genesis, the eighteenth chapter: « Abraham made haste into the tent to Sara, and said to her: Make haste, temper together three measures of flour ». And so did the blessed Virgin, because at the Angel's word she straightway consented: « Behold, she said[^p349-7], the handmaid of the Lord » etc.

46. From these things, then, it appears how from one small thing the Holy Spirit has drawn out outwardly various understandings through his Saints, that from this there may appear « the manifold wisdom of God[^p349-8] », which after the manner of the woman hides the leaven in the measures. For, as Dionysius says[^p349-9], the whole of mystical theology, « which namely is hidden in a mystery », consists wholly in transporting dilection according to the threefold hierarchic power: purgative, illuminative and perfective.

47. Thirdly, as to the effect on the side of *both,* he subjoins: *Until the whole was leavened;* the Gloss[^p349-10]: « So long ought charity hidden in the mind to grow, until it change the whole mind into its own perfection, so that it love nothing besides God ». For as leaven is more and more diffused, so also the charity of God. Whence Gregory[^p349-11]: « The love of God is never idle; for it works great things, if it is; but if it refuses to work, it is not love »; and Bernard: « Charity either makes progress or falls away »; whence[^p349-12] « charity never falleth away », but after the manner of leaven and of fire turns the whole into its own nature. Whence Exodus, the nineteenth chapter: « All mount Sinai was on a smoke: because the Lord was come down upon it in fire ». — For this fire and warmth of charity is begun on the way, but occupies the whole heart in the fatherland; Isaias, the thirty-first chapter[^p349-13]: « Said the Lord, whose fire is in Sion, and his furnace in Jerusalem ». Whence, as from the heart, which is in the centre, vital warmth flows into the whole body, so from perfect charity all the gatherings of the works of the virtues receive warmth and vigour, through which they tend upward. And as it is said[^p349-14]: « If thy eye be single, thy whole body shall be lightsome »; so, if thy heart be *warm,* thy whole body will be warmed; if the heart be *divine,*
<!-- page 350 -->
the whole man is divine through deifying love, according to that of the Psalm: « My flesh and my heart hath fainted away: thou art the God of my heart, and the God that is my portion for ever ».
