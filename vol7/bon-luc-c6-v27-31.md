# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. VI, Vers. 27-31
## *Sed vobis dico, qui auditis etc. Post promissiones praemiorum et comminationes suppliciorum subdit hic tertio Evangelista leges praeceptorum sive documenta dirigentia*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 153 -->

64. *Sed vobis dico, qui auditis* etc. Post promissiones praemiorum et comminationes suppliciorum subdit hic tertio Evangelista *leges praeceptorum* sive documenta dirigentia, et habet haec pars duas. In quarum prima ponit documenta *propria et expressa;* in secunda vero *similitudinaria et translata,* ibi[^p153-1]: *Dicebat autem illis et similitudinem* etc.

Circa *prima documenta* auditoribus commendanda hoc modo procedit Christi doctrina. Primo namque ea *promulgat sub perfectione conveniente.* Secundo vero *probat ex conditione deficiente,* ibi[^p153-2]: *Et si diligitis eos qui vos diligunt.* Tertio vero *resumit cum ratione movente,* ibi: *Verumtamen diligite inimicos vestros.* Quarto *concludit pro retributione sufficiente,* ibi: *Estote ergo misericordes.*

Circa *documentorum promulgationem* notandum est, quod ad hoc, quod homo sit perfectus, necesse est, ipsum esse perfecte *benevolum,* perfecte *pacificum* et perfecte *beneficum.* Primum respicit *affectionem,* secundum *passionem* et tertium *operationem.*

65. (Vers. 27.). Dat igitur primo mandatum perfectae *benevolentiae,* cum ait: *Sed vobis dico, qui auditis: Diligite inimicos vestros.* Hoc namque mandatum est auditoribus, id est credentibus et obedientibus, proponendum, in quibus habitat Deus, quia dicitur primae Ioannis quarto[^p153-3]: « Qui manet in caritate in Deo manet, et Deus in eo ». Et talis audit mandatum caritatis, non solum aure corporis, sed cordis, secundum illud Ioannis octavo: « Qui ex Deo est verba Dei audit ». Hunc auditum excitabat Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto: « Audite audientes me et comedite bonum, et delectabitur in crassitudine anima vestra. Inclinate aurem vestram et venite ad me; audite, et vivet anima vestra ». *Vivet* utique per mandatum dilectionis, quia, sicut dicitur primae Ioannis tertio[^p153-4], « qui non diligit manet in morte »; et ante illud: « Nos scimus, quoniam translati sumus de morte ad vitam, quoniam diligimus fratres ». Ad hoc Apostolus ad Ephesios quinto: « Ambulate in dilectione, sicut et Christus dilexit nos et tradidit semetipsum pro nobis »; « quoniam, cum inimici essemus, Deo reconciliati sumus per mortem Filii eius », ad Romanos quinto. Et ita nos debemus inimicos diligere. — Et quoniam non sufficit « diligere verbo neque lingua solum, sed opere et veritate[^p153-5] »; ideo subdit: *Bene facite his qui oderunt vos,* ut sit « dilectio sine simulatione »; Proverbiorum vigesimo quinto: « Si esurit inimicus tuus, ciba illum; si sitit, potum da illi »; idem dicitur ad Romanos duodecimo.

66. (Vers. 28.). Et quia[^p153-6] « verbum melius est quam datum », et sermo declarat affectum, subdit: *Benedicite maledicentibus vobis,* in sermone *publico; et orate pro calumniantibus vos,* in sermone privato. *Benedicite,* in sermone manifesto. Ad hoc Apostolus ad Romanos duodecimo[^p153-7]: « Benedicite maledicentibus vobis, benedicite et nolite maledicere »; et primae Petri tertio: « Nulli malum pro malo reddentes nec maledictum pro maledicto, sed e contrario, benedicentes ». *Orate,* etiam in sermone *secreto,* exemplo illius, de quo Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p153-8]: « Pro transgressoribus oravit »; et exemplo illius protomartyris, Actuum septimo: « Positis autem genibus, clamavit voce magna: Domine, ne statuas illis hoc peccatum ».

67. Sed huic contrariari videtur, quod Eliseus, quarti Regum secundo[^p153-9], iratus est pueris irridentibus, et Elias quinquagenario, quarti Regum primo, et Paulus sacerdoti summo, Actuum vigesimo tertio. — Ad hoc respondet Beda[^p153-10], quod ista « non sunt vota optantium, sed praedictiones futurorum »; vel non irascebantur contra naturam, sed contra vitium. Ut enim dicit Augustinus[^p153-11]: « Sic homines sunt diligendi, ut tamen eorum non diligantur errores ». Sic igitur dat mandatum perfectae benevolentiae, quae non solum consistit in corde, sed etiam in opere et sermone. Unde Gregorius[^p153-12]: « De dilectione Conditoris lingua, mens et manus requiratur ».

De hoc autem *mandato benevolentiae* in summa notandum est, quod quantum ad *affectum* astringit universos, quantum ad *signum* astringit perfectos, quantum ad *effectum* in articulo necessitatis omnes astringit, citra necessitatem non astringit *imperfectos,* nisi quantum requirit debitum alicuius praecepti[^p153-13]. *Perfectos* autem *a statu praelationis* astringit,
<!-- page 154 -->
quantum requirit debitum officii assumti; *perfectos a statu perfectionis,* quantum requirit debitum boni exempli; *perfectos* autem *a perfectione virtutis,* quantum requirit intensio caritatis.

68. (Vers. 29.). Secundo dat mandatum de *perfecta patientia,* cum subdit: *Et qui te percusserit in unam maxillam*[^p154-1], inferendo scilicet iniuriam in *personam;* et ponit *maxillam* pro parte corporis nobiliori, quae cum percutitur, praecipua iniuria irrogatur; unde Iob decimo sexto: « Aperuerunt super me ora sua, exprobrantes percusserunt maxillam meam ». De tali et tanta iniuria non est exigenda vindicta, sed exhibenda patientia; unde subdit: *Praebe et alteram,* scilicet maxillam, id est, paratus sis aliam percussionem sustinere, exemplo Magistri, de quo Threnorum tertio[^p154-2]: « Dabit percutienti se maxillam, saturabitur opprobriis »; *dabit* utique, non invitando ad percussionem, sed non opponendo defensionem, secundum illud ad Romanos duodecimo: « Non vosmetipsos defendentes, carissimi, sed date locum irae ».

69. Unde in hoc mandato inhibetur *libidinosa defensio,* non *caritativa correctio,* cum dicatur Matthaei decimo octavo[^p154-3]: « Si peccaverit in te frater tuus, vade et corripe eum » etc. Et exemplum huius in Christo, Ioannis decimo octavo: « Unus minister assistens dedit alapam Iesu »; et subditur: « Respondit ei Iesus: Si male locutus sum, testimonium perhibe de malo; si autem bene, cur me caedis »? Unde Augustinus in quodam sermone de Verbis Domini[^p154-4]: « Si iniuriam pateris et taces et proximum non corripis, plus peccas negligendo quam ille affligendo ». — Per hoc etiam mandatum non arcentur personae laicales a sui vel aliorum defensione; unde Paulus, Actuum vigesimo tertio[^p154-5], petiit milites, ut illaesus servaretur. Unde Augustinus primo de Civitate Dei, exponens illud praeceptum Exodi vigesimo: *Non occides,* dicit, quod « contra hoc praeceptum non fecerunt qui Deo auctore bella gesserunt et secundum leges sceleratos punierunt ». Unde canon[^p154-6]: « Fortitudo, quae a barbaris tuetur patriam, vel domi defendit infirmos, vel a latronibus socios, plena iustitia est ». Unde Augustinus ad Marcellinum: « Ista praecepta patientiae sunt in *cordis praeparatione retinenda;* ipsa namque benevolentia, ne reddatur malum pro malo, semper in cordis voluntate complenda est. *Agenda* autem sunt multa etiam cum invitis benigna quadam asperitate plectendis, quorum potius est utilitati consulendum quam voluntati ». — Sic igitur intelligendum est mandatum de exhibenda patientia iniurianti ipsi personae.

70. Exhibenda est etiam patientia iniurianti *in re possessa,* propter quod addit: *Et ab eo qui aufert tibi vestimentum,* scilicet exterius per iniuriam, sicut illi, de quibus Iob vigesimo quarto[^p154-7]: « Nudos dimittunt homines, indumenta tollentes »; et Michaeae secundo: « Desuper tunicam pallium sustulistis ». Tali sic iniurianti exhibenda est patientia; unde subdit: *Etiam tunicam noli prohibere,* scilicet rixando, vel contradicendo, exemplo Salvatoris, de quo in Psalmo[^p154-8]: « Diviserunt sibi vestimenta mea » etc., sine repugnantia scilicet et contradictione. In hoc similiter mandato intelligendum est, prohiberi contentionem vel libidinem vindicandi. Unde Augustinus ad Marcellinum[^p154-9]: « Cavendum est, ne vindicandi cupiditate amittatur ipsa patientia, quae pluris habenda est quam omne, quod potest invito inimicus auferre ». — In summa hic tenendum est de praecepto *patientiae,* quod non prohibetur hic *correctio proximi,* seu *impeditio* vel prohibitio *mali,* vel *punitio sceleris* perpetrati ab eo qui potest et debet, cuiusmodi est legis minister; sed prohibetur et cohibetur hic *libido ulciscendi* et quantum ad *manum* et quantum ad *signum* et quantum ad *animum.* Et in hoc addit Evangelista supra Legem[^p154-10], quam perficit et consummat secundum spiritualem intelligentiam.

71. (Vers. 30.). Tertio[^p154-11] dat mandatum de *perfecta beneficentia,* cum subiungit: *Omni autem petenti te tribue.* Hoc est mandatum generale respectu omnis *personae* et omnis *dati* sive *obsequii,* sive
<!-- page 155 -->
*mutui,* sive *alterius muneris.* Et est proximo dandum munus *exterioris relevationis,* secundum illud Deuteronomii decimo quinto[^p155-1]: « Praecipio tibi, ut aperias manum tuam fratri tuo egeno et pauperi »; et Exodi vigesimo tertio: « Si videris asinum odientis te iacere sub onere, non pertransibis, sed sublevabis cum eo ». Et *internae compassionis;* Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo[^p155-2]: « Cum effuderis esurienti animam tuam » etc.; et ad Colossenses tertio: « Induite vos, sicut electi Dei, viscera misericordiae, supportantes invicem et donantes vobismetipsis » etc. Et etiam munus *dulcis locutionis;* Ecclesiastici decimo octavo[^p155-3]: « Nonne ardorem refrigerabit ros? Sic et verbum melius quam datum ».

72. Ad duo sequentia omnes obligantur; ad primum autem obligantur praecipue divites sive qui habent, unde possint alienam necessitatem relevare; primae ad Timotheum ultimo[^p155-4]: « Divitibus huius saeculi praecipe, non sublime sapere, sed bene agere, divites fieri in bonis operibus, facile tribuere, communicare, thesaurizare sibi fundamentum bonum in futurum, ut apprehendant veram vitam ».

73. Obligat etiam respectu *omnis personae,* considerata natura et necessitate, non autem pensato vitio; unde non contrariatur ei quod dicitur Ecclesiastici duodecimo[^p155-5]: « Da bono, et non receperis peccatorem; benefac humili, et non dederis impio »; hoc dicit: quia est *peccator* vel impius. Unde non est dandum histrionibus ratione sui ministerii, quia, ut dicit Hieronymus[^p155-6], « mimis et histrionibus dare nihil aliud est quam daemonibus immolare ». Si tamen sunt in necessitate constituti, dandum est eis, quia sunt ad imaginem Dei creati et quia fratres nostri; unde primae Ioannis tertio: « Qui viderit, fratrem suum necessitatem habere, et clauserit viscera sua ab eo, quomodo caritas Dei manet in eo »?

74. Et quia hoc mandatum beneficentiae se extendit ad omnes sine exceptione, ideo specificat etiam de *iniuriantibus,* cum adiungit: *Et qui aufert quae tua sunt, ne repetas;* Glossa[^p155-7]: « Cum litigio vel contentione », quae venit ex cupiditate; Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo: « Ecce, in die ieiunii vestri invenitur voluntas vestra, et omnes debitores vestros repetitis. Ecce, ad lites et contentiones ieiunatis »; Ecclesiastici trigesimo primo: « Verba improperii non dicas proximo, et non premas illum in repetendo ». Tunc autem fit, quando coram iudice alieno vel infideli causam trahit; propter quod primae ad Corinthios sexto[^p155-8]: « Frater adversus fratrem in iudicio contendit, et hoc apud infideles? Quare non magis fraudem patimini »? Hoc autem non dicit, quia in ullo non liceat repetere, sed ut ostendat, quod magis debemus diligere proximum et eius salutem quam rem temporalem. Unde Gregorius[^p155-9]: « Cum pro terrena re pax a corde cum proximo scinditur, apparet, quod plus res quam proximus amatur ».

75. Unde nota, quod repetere contingit tripliciter, scilicet ex *cupiditate* et *zelo iustitiae* et *timore casus.* Primum est *malorum,* non iustorum caritatem habentium, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios decimo tertio[^p155-10]: « Caritas non quaerit quae sua sunt »; Glossa: « Non repetit ablata », scilicet ex amore cupiditatis ad rem. — Est iterum *repetere zelo iustitiae;* et hoc modo licet *perfectis,* qui habent proprium *in communi,* nomine communitatis; perfectis vero, qui nec *in communi* nec *in speciali* habent proprium[^p155-11], non licet eis repetere, sed eis solum, qui habent proprietatem illarum rerum, quarum ipsi usum habent. Ille *zelo iustitiae repetit,* qui intendit consulere saluti rapientis et refrenationi cupiditatis alienae et paci universalis Ecclesiae. Unde Gregorius[^p155-12]: « Non solum debet esse cura, ne nostra subtrahant, sed ne rapientes non sua semetipsos perdant ». Ille etiam *zelo iustitiae repetit,* qui intendit ius suum defendere, vel personae, vel Ecclesiae, seu alicuius communitatis adiunctae, maxime cum illud ex causa teneatur defendere. — Est tertio repetere *timore casus,* scilicet ne propter paupertatem et indigentiam ruant in culpam; et sic spectat ad *infirmos.* Unde orabat in persona talium Sapiens Proverbiorum trigesimo[^p155-13]: « Divitias et paupertates ne dederis mihi; tribue tantum victui meo necessaria, ne forte satiatus illiciar ad negandum et dicam: Quis est Dominus? aut egestate compulsus furer et periurem nomen Dei mei ».
<!-- page 156 -->
— Vult ergo in hoc mandato dicere, quod homo sit paratus ad benefaciendum omnibus, non solum benefactoribus suis, sed etiam suis praedatoribus.

76. (Vers. 31.). Ad perfectionem etiam benevolentiae exigitur, quod homo benefaciat *in omnibus;* unde et subditur: *Et prout vultis, ut vobis faciant homines, et vos facite illis similiter.* Et nota, quod non dicit: prout *faciunt,* sed: prout *vultis,* quia quilibet vult sibi, cum indiget, benefieri et in omni necessitate relevari; Proverbiorum vigesimo quarto[^p156-1]: « Ne dicas: Quomodo fecit mihi, sic faciam ei et reddam unicuique secundum opus suum ». In hoc mandato est consummatio legis *naturalis;* cuius una pars negativa ponitur Tobiae quarto et implicatur hic: « Quod ab alio oderis tibi fieri, vide, ne tu aliquando alteri facias ». Vult ergo, quod semper bonum, quando possumus, cuilibet faciamus, sicut prompti sumus ad volendum recipere, secundum illud ad Galatas ultimo: « Bonum autem facientes, non deficiamus » etc. — Et nota, quod non dicit: facite, prout *volunt homines,* sed: prout *vultis;* tum quia voluntas nostra nobis notior est; tum etiam, quia magis nos movet consideratio nostri; tum etiam, quia loquitur ad eos qui voluntatem habent ordinatam, et secundum illam est operandum[^p156-2]. — In summa tenendum est de praecepto beneficentiae in generali, quod tantum astringit, quantum explicatur per speciales obligationes praeceptorum; et ideo supra legem naturae addita est lex scripta, ut per eam sciret homo, ad quid astringeretur ex iustitia.

## English
<!-- page 153 -->

64. *But I say to you that hear* etc. After the promises of rewards and the threatenings of penalties the Evangelist here, thirdly, adds the *laws of the precepts* or directing lessons, and this part has two. In the first of them he sets down lessons *proper and express;* but in the second, lessons *by way of likeness and transferred,* at[^p153-1]: *And he spoke also to them a similitude* etc.

Concerning the *first lessons,* to be commended to the hearers, the teaching of Christ proceeds in this manner. For first it *promulgates them under a fitting perfection.* But second it *proves them from a deficient condition,* at[^p153-2]: *And if you love them that love you.* But third it *resumes them with a moving reason,* at: *But love ye your enemies.* Fourth it *concludes for a sufficient retribution,* at: *Be ye therefore merciful.*

Concerning the *promulgation of the lessons* it is to be noted that, for a man to be perfect, it is necessary that he be perfectly *benevolent,* perfectly *peaceable* and perfectly *beneficent.* The first regards *affection,* the second *passion* and the third *operation.*

65. (Vers. 27.). First, then, he gives the commandment of perfect *benevolence,* when he says: *But I say to you that hear: Love your enemies.* For this commandment is to be set before the hearers, that is, before those who believe and obey, in whom God dwells, because it is said in the first of John, the fourth chapter[^p153-3]: « He that abideth in charity abideth in God, and God in him ». And such a one hears the commandment of charity, not only with the ear of the body, but with that of the heart, according to that of John, the eighth chapter: « He that is of God heareth the words of God ». This hearing he aroused, Isaias, the fifty-fifth chapter: « Hear, ye that hear me, and eat that which is good, and your soul shall be delighted in fatness. Incline your ear and come to me; hear, and your soul shall live ». *Shall live,* indeed, through the commandment of dilection, because, as is said in the first of John, the third chapter[^p153-4], « he that loveth not abideth in death »; and before that: « We know that we have passed from death to life, because we love the brethren ». To this the Apostle, to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter: « Walk in love, as Christ also hath loved us, and hath delivered himself for us »; « for, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son », to the Romans, the fifth chapter. And so we ought to love our enemies. — And since it is not enough « to love in word nor in tongue only, but in deed and in truth[^p153-5] »; therefore he adds: *Do good to them that hate you,* that it may be « love without dissimulation »; Proverbs, the twenty-fifth chapter: « If thy enemy be hungry, give him to eat; if he thirst, give him to drink »; the same is said to the Romans, the twelfth chapter.

66. (Vers. 28.). And because[^p153-6] « the word is better than the gift », and speech declares the affection, he adds: *Bless them that curse you,* in *public* speech; *and pray for them that calumniate you,* in private speech. *Bless,* in open speech. To this the Apostle, to the Romans, the twelfth chapter[^p153-7]: « Bless them that curse you; bless, and curse not »; and the first of Peter, the third chapter: « Not rendering evil for evil, nor railing for railing, but contrariwise, blessing ». *Pray,* also in *secret* speech, after the example of him of whom Isaias, the fifty-third chapter[^p153-8]: « He hath prayed for the transgressors »; and after the example of that protomartyr, Acts, the seventh chapter: « And falling on his knees, he cried with a loud voice: Lord, lay not this sin to their charge ».

67. But against this seems to be the fact that Eliseus, the fourth of Kings, the second chapter[^p153-9], was angry with the boys who mocked him, and Elias with the captain of fifty, the fourth of Kings, the first chapter, and Paul with the high priest, Acts, the twenty-third chapter. — To this Bede answers[^p153-10] that these « are not the wishes of those who desire, but predictions of things to come »; or they were not angry against nature, but against vice. For as Augustine says[^p153-11]: « Men are so to be loved that yet their errors be not loved ». Thus, then, he gives the commandment of perfect benevolence, which consists not only in the heart, but also in deed and in speech. Whence Gregory[^p153-12]: « Concerning the dilection of the Creator let the tongue, the mind and the hand be required ».

But concerning this *commandment of benevolence* it is to be noted, in sum, that as to the *affection* it binds all men, as to the *sign* it binds the perfect, as to the *effect* it binds all in the article of necessity; short of necessity it does not bind the *imperfect,* except so far as the debt of some precept requires[^p153-13]. But it binds *the perfect by the state of prelacy*
<!-- page 154 -->
so far as the debt of the office assumed requires; *the perfect by the state of perfection,* so far as the debt of good example requires; but *the perfect by the perfection of virtue,* so far as the intensity of charity requires.

68. (Vers. 29.). Secondly he gives the commandment of *perfect patience,* when he adds: *And he that shall strike thee on the one cheek*[^p154-1], namely by inflicting injury on the *person;* and he puts the *cheek* for the nobler part of the body, on which, when it is struck, a chief injury is inflicted; whence Job, the sixteenth chapter: « They have opened their mouths upon me, reproaching they have struck my cheek ». For such and so great an injury vengeance is not to be exacted, but patience is to be shown; whence he adds: *Offer also the other,* namely cheek, that is, be ready to sustain another stroke, after the example of the Master, of whom the Lamentations, the third chapter[^p154-2]: « He shall give his cheek to him that striketh him, he shall be filled with reproaches »; *he shall give,* indeed, not by inviting the stroke, but by not opposing a defence, according to that of the Romans, the twelfth chapter: « Not defending yourselves, my dearly beloved, but give place unto wrath ».

69. Whence in this commandment *lustful defence* is forbidden, not *charitable correction,* since it is said in Matthew, the eighteenth chapter[^p154-3]: « If thy brother shall offend against thee, go, and correct him » etc. And the example of this is in Christ, John, the eighteenth chapter: « One of the servants standing by gave Jesus a blow »; and it is added: « Jesus answered him: If I have spoken evil, give testimony of the evil; but if well, why strikest thou me »? Whence Augustine in a certain sermon on the Words of the Lord[^p154-4]: « If you suffer an injury and are silent and do not correct your neighbour, you sin more by neglecting than he by afflicting ». — By this commandment also lay persons are not barred from the defence of themselves or of others; whence Paul, Acts, the twenty-third chapter[^p154-5], asked for soldiers, that he might be kept unhurt. Whence Augustine in the first book of the City of God, expounding that precept of Exodus, the twentieth chapter: *Thou shalt not kill,* says that « they have not acted against this precept who have waged wars by God's authority and have punished the wicked according to the laws ». Whence the canon[^p154-6]: « Fortitude, which defends the fatherland from barbarians, or at home defends the weak, or companions from robbers, is full justice ». Whence Augustine to Marcellinus: « These precepts of patience are to be kept in the *preparation of the heart;* for that benevolence itself, that evil be not rendered for evil, is always to be fulfilled in the will of the heart. But many things are *to be done* even with the unwilling, who are to be punished with a certain kindly harshness, whose utility is rather to be consulted than their will ». — Thus, then, is to be understood the commandment of showing patience to him who does injury to the person itself.

70. Patience is also to be shown to him who does injury *in a thing possessed,* on account of which he adds: *And from him that taketh away from thee thy garment,* namely outwardly through injury, as those of whom Job, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p154-7]: « They send men away naked, taking away their clothes »; and Micheas, the second chapter: « You have taken away the cloak from off the coat ». To one doing injury thus patience is to be shown; whence he adds: *Forbid not thy coat also,* namely by quarrelling, or by gainsaying, after the example of the Saviour, of whom in the Psalm[^p154-8]: « They parted my garments among them » etc., without resistance, that is, and gainsaying. In this commandment likewise it is to be understood that contention or the lust of avenging is forbidden. Whence Augustine to Marcellinus[^p154-9]: « Care must be taken lest through the desire of avenging patience itself be lost, which is to be held of more worth than all that an enemy can take away from one unwilling ». — In sum it is to be held here concerning the precept of *patience* that here is not forbidden the *correction of the neighbour,* or the *hindering* or forbidding *of evil,* or the *punishment of a crime* committed, by him who can and ought, of which sort is the minister of the law; but there is forbidden and restrained here the *lust of taking vengeance,* both as to the *hand* and as to the *sign* and as to the *mind.* And in this the Evangelist adds above the Law[^p154-10], which he perfects and consummates according to the spiritual intelligence.

71. (Vers. 30.). Thirdly[^p154-11] he gives the commandment of *perfect beneficence,* when he subjoins: *And give to every one that asketh thee.* This is a general commandment in respect of every *person* and of every *gift* or *service,* whether
<!-- page 155 -->
*of a loan,* or *of another gift.* And there is to be given to the neighbour the gift *of outward relief,* according to that of Deuteronomy, the fifteenth chapter[^p155-1]: « I command thee to open thy hand to thy needy and poor brother »; and Exodus, the twenty-third chapter: « If thou see the ass of him that hateth thee lie underneath his burden, thou shalt not pass by, but shalt lift him up with him ». And that *of inward compassion;* Isaias, the fifty-eighth chapter[^p155-2]: « When thou shalt pour out thy soul to the hungry » etc.; and to the Colossians, the third chapter: « Put ye on, as the elect of God, the bowels of mercy, bearing with one another and forgiving one another » etc. And also the gift *of sweet speech;* Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter[^p155-3]: « Shall not the dew assuage the heat? So also the word is better than the gift ».

72. To the two following all are bound; but to the first the rich are chiefly bound, or those who have whence they may relieve another's necessity; the first to Timothy, the last chapter[^p155-4]: « Charge the rich of this world not to be highminded, but to do good, to be rich in good works, to give easily, to communicate, to lay up in store for themselves a good foundation against the time to come, that they may lay hold on the true life ».

73. It binds also in respect of *every person,* nature and necessity being considered, but vice not weighed; whence that is not contradicted which is said in Ecclesiasticus, the twelfth chapter[^p155-5]: « Give to the good, and receive not a sinner; do good to the humble, and give not to the ungodly »; this he says: because he is a *sinner* or ungodly. Whence there is not to be given to players by reason of their ministry, because, as Jerome says[^p155-6], « to give to mimes and players is nothing else than to sacrifice to demons ». If however they are set in necessity, there is to be given to them, because they are created to the image of God and because they are our brethren; whence the first of John, the third chapter: « He that shall see his brother in need, and shall shut up his bowels from him, how doth the charity of God abide in him »?

74. And because this commandment of beneficence extends itself to all without exception, therefore he specifies also concerning *those who do injury,* when he adjoins: *And of him that taketh away the things that are thine, ask them not again;* the Gloss[^p155-7]: « With litigation or contention », which comes from cupidity; Isaias, the fifty-eighth chapter: « Behold, in the day of your fast your own will is found, and you ask again of all your debtors. Behold, you fast for debates and strife »; Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-first chapter: « Speak not words of reproach to thy neighbour, and press him not in asking again ». Now it is done then, when one drags the case before an alien or unbelieving judge; on account of which the first to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter[^p155-8]: « Brother contendeth in judgement against brother, and that before unbelievers? Why do you not rather suffer yourselves to be defrauded »? But he does not say this because it is not lawful to ask again in any, but to show that we ought to love our neighbour and his salvation more than a temporal thing. Whence Gregory[^p155-9]: « When for an earthly thing peace with the neighbour is torn from the heart, it appears that the thing is loved more than the neighbour ».

75. Whence note that asking again happens in three ways, namely from *cupidity,* and from *zeal for justice,* and from *fear of falling.* The first belongs to the *wicked,* not to the just who have charity, according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the thirteenth chapter[^p155-10]: « Charity seeketh not her own »; the Gloss: « It does not ask again things taken away », namely from the love of cupidity for the thing. — Again there is *asking again from zeal for justice;* and in this manner it is lawful for the *perfect* who have property *in common,* in the name of the community; but for the perfect who have property neither *in common* nor *in particular*[^p155-11], it is not lawful for them to ask again, but only for those who have the ownership of those things of which they themselves have the use. He *asks again from zeal for justice* who intends to provide for the salvation of the one who seizes and for the restraint of another's cupidity and for the peace of the universal Church. Whence Gregory[^p155-12]: « There ought to be care not only lest they take away our things, but lest those who seize what is not theirs destroy themselves ». He also *asks again from zeal for justice* who intends to defend his right, either of his person, or of the Church, or of some community joined to him, especially when he is bound for a cause to defend it. — Thirdly there is asking again *from fear of falling,* namely lest on account of poverty and indigence they rush into fault; and so it belongs to the *weak.* Whence the Wise Man prayed in the person of such, Proverbs, the thirtieth chapter[^p155-13]: « Give me not riches and poverty; give me only the things necessary for my living, lest perhaps being filled I should be enticed to deny, and say: Who is the Lord? or being compelled by want I should steal, and forswear the name of my God ».
<!-- page 156 -->
— He means, then, in this commandment, that a man should be ready to do good to all, not only to his benefactors, but also to his plunderers.

76. (Vers. 31.). For the perfection of benevolence it is also required that a man do good *in all things;* whence also it is added: *And as you will that men should do to you, do you also to them in like manner.* And note that he does not say: as they *do,* but: as you *will,* because each one wills for himself, when he is in need, to have good done to him and to be relieved in every necessity; Proverbs, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p156-1]: « Say not: As he hath done to me, so will I do to him, and I will render to every one according to his work ». In this commandment is the consummation of the *natural* law; one part of which, the negative, is set down in Tobias, the fourth chapter, and is implied here: « What thou wouldst hate to have done to thee by another, see that thou do it not at any time to another ». He means, then, that we should always do good, when we can, to anyone, just as we are prompt to will to receive it, according to that of the Galatians, the last chapter: « And in doing good, let us not fail » etc. — And note that he does not say: do, as *men will,* but: as *you will;* both because our own will is better known to us; and also because the consideration of ourselves moves us more; and also because he speaks to those who have an ordered will, and according to it one is to act[^p156-2]. — In sum it is to be held concerning the precept of beneficence in general that it binds so far as it is unfolded through the special obligations of the precepts; and therefore above the law of nature the written law was added, that through it man might know to what he was bound by justice.
