# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XV, Vers. 11-20
## *Parabola de filio prodigo*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 389 -->

#### Parabola de filio prodigo.

21. *Ait autem: Homo quidam habuit duos filios.* Post parabolam de pastore et ovibus suis et de muliere et drachmis subditur hic tertio[^p389-10] parabola *de patre et filiis,* ut sic, cum maxima sit pietas patris ad suum filium, maxima ostendatur hic pietas Dei ad peccatorem conversum, qui intelligitur per *filium prodigum.* Et quoniam misericordia patris in hoc ostenditur, quod relevavit miseriam filii prius peccantis et postmodum poenitentis; ideo in hac parabola quatuor describit Evangelista. Primo namque describit filii prodigi *insolentiam;* secundo, *miseriam et indigentiam,* ibi[^p389-11]: *Et postquam consummasset omnia;* tertio, eiusdem *poenitentiam,* ibi: *Ipse autem in se reversus, dixit* etc.; quarto, *patris misericordiam,* ibi: *Cum autem adhuc longe esset.* Et hoc quidem facit rectissimo processu et ordine. Nam *insolentia* praecipitavit in miseriam et in indigentiam; *indigentia* autem excitavit ad poenitentiam, et *poenitentia* praeparavit ad patris misericordiam obtinendam.

Circa *filii prodigi insolentiam* exprimendam tria introducuntur ab Evangelista, scilicet *conditio libertatis humanae, perpetratio voluntariae culpae* et *dissipatio boni*[^p389-12] *gratiae et naturae.*

22. (Vers. 11.). Primo igitur quantum ad *conditionem libertatis humanae* dicit: *Ait autem: Homo
<!-- page 390 -->
quidam habuit duos filios.* Per *hominem* istum, sicut saepe dictum est[^p390-1], intelligimus Dominum benignum et pium. Nam ipse ratione summae pietatis, quae reddit eum benignum et humanum erga hominem, *homo* merito dici potest; unde Danielis decimo[^p390-2]: « Tetigit me quasi visio hominis et confortavit me ». Ad cuius etiam designationem Deus in effigie humana per subiectam creaturam frequenter apparuit sanctis Patribus et Prophetis. Per *duos* autem *filios* intelligimus humani generis universitatem, non solum quoad gentiles et Iudaeos, sicut Glossa[^p390-3] exponit, sed etiam generaliter quoad innocentes et poenitentes, sicut oportet intelligi ex ipsa applicatione parabolae. Unde Beda[^p390-4]: « Haec parabola de Iudaeo et gentili, generaliter de poenitente et iusto, vel qui sibi iustus videtur, potest intelligi ». Et hi dicuntur *filii,* quia in libertate arbitrii sunt creati et ad aeternam hereditatem habendam; Ioannis octavo[^p390-5]: « Servus non manet in domo in aeternum, filius autem manet in aeternum »; unde in Psalmo: « Sicut miseretur pater filiorum, sic misertus est Dominus timentibus se ».

23. (Vers. 12.). Et quia libertas arbitrii in vanis et peccatoribus frequenter de propriis viribus praesumit et sibi usurpat quod Dei est, ideo subdit: *Et dixit adolescentior ex illis patri: Pater*[^p390-6]*, da mihi portionem substantiae, quae me contingit.* Iste *adolescentior* dicitur ille qui vanior est et ad sensibilia bona magis intentus, secundum illud Ecclesiastae undecimo[^p390-7]: « Adolescentia enim et voluptas vana sunt »; et Ecclesiastae eodem capitulo supra: « Laetare, iuvenis, in adolescentia tua »; « et scito, quod pro omnibus his adducet te Deus in iudicium ». Hic adolescens petit *partem suam,* cum relinqui vult libertati suae, ut iam non secundum imperium gratiae, sed secundum nutum voluntatis suae incedat. Unde Beda[^p390-8]: « *Substantia* est omne, quod agimus, quod vivimus, sapimus, cogitamus et loquimur. Huius ergo portionem substantiae minor petit sibi dari, cum homo, rationali sensu suo delectatus, per liberum arbitrium se regere et a dominio Creatoris exire quaesivit ». Haec petitio non fuit pro se, sed magis contra se, quia petiit, se sibi exponi et suo iudicio derelinqui; sicut et petitio filiorum Israel, qua petierunt regem; primi Regum octavo[^p390-9]: « Dixerunt filii Israel ad Samuelem: Constitue nobis regem, ut iudicet nos ». « Dixitque Dominus ad Samuelem: Non te abiecerunt, sed me, ne regnem super eos ». — Et quia Dominus iudicio iusto eum qui de se praesumit, relinquit sibi; ideo subdit: *Et divisit illis substantiam.* Nam sic conveniebat, secundum illud Ecclesiastici decimo quinto[^p390-10]: « Deus ab initio constituit hominem et reliquit eum in manu consilii sui ». Nam sic Dominus iusto iudicio facit, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo quinto: « Uni dedit quinque talenta, alii autem duo, alii autem unum, unicuique secundum propriam virtutem » etc.

24. (Vers. 13.). Secundo quantum ad *perpetrationem voluntariae culpae* subdit: *Et non post multos dies, congregatis omnibus,* per liberi arbitrii dominium, quod habet respectu omnium virium naturalium; *adolescentior filius peregre profectus est in regionem longinquam,* et hoc perpetrando peccatum. *Regio* namque *longinqua* a Deo dicitur, non quantum ad situm localem, cum Deus sit ubique, sed per voluntatis dissimilitudinem. Unde Glossa[^p390-11]: « *Abiit in regionem longinquam,* non mutando locum, sed animum. Quanto enim quis in pravo opere plus delinquit, tanto a Dei gratia longius recedit »; unde Proverbiorum decimo quinto: « Longe est Dominus ab impiis et orationes iustorum exaudiet ». Est autem peccatum *regio longinqua* a Deo, quia, cum sit *iniquitas,* elongat a summa *bonitate,* secundum illud Isaiae quinquagesimo nono[^p390-12]: « Iniquitates vestrae diviserunt inter vos et Deum vestrum, et peccata vestra absconderunt faciem eius a vobis ». Cum sit *tenebra,* elongat a summa *luce et veritate;* primae Ioannis primo[^p390-13]: « Deus lux est, et tenebrae in eo non sunt ullae. Si dixerimus, quoniam societatem habemus cum eo, et in tenebris ambulamus; mentimur et veritatem non facimus »; et paulo post subdit, de quibus tenebris intelligit: « Qui odit fratrem suum in tenebris est » etc. Cum etiam peccatum sit *nihil,* elongat a summa *entitate;* propter quod Ieremiae secundo[^p390-14]: « Quid invenerunt in me patres vestri
<!-- page 391 -->
iniquitatis, quia elongaverunt se a me et ambulaverunt post vanitatem »? De his tribus simul secundae ad Corinthios sexto[^p391-1]: « Quae participatio iustitiae cum iniquitate? Aut quae societas lucis ad tenebras? Quae autem conventio Christi ad Belial »? « Qui autem consensus templo Dei cum idolis »? Et propterea merito peccator, qui elongat se a Deo per peccatum et vadit in regionem longinquam longe a caelesti regione, proiicitur in infernum, qui est regio mortis et tenebrarum, secundum illud Iob decimo[^p391-2]: « Antequam vadam ad terram tenebrosam et opertam mortis caligine, terram miseriae et tenebrarum, ubi umbra mortis et nullus ordo » etc.

25. Tertio quantum ad *dissipationem boni gratiae et naturae* subiungit: *Dissipavit*[^p391-3] *substantiam suam. Dissipavit,* inquam, per peccata *spiritualia,* quae maxime dissipant animam, et hoc quidem per *superbiam;* Nahum secundo[^p391-4]: « Sicut reddidit Dominus superbiam Iacob, sic superbiam Israel, quia vastatores dissipaverunt eos et propagines eorum corruperunt ». — Item, per *inanem gloriam;* Isaiae tertio: « Popule meus, qui te beatum dicunt, ipsi te decipiunt et viam gressuum tuorum dissipant ». — Item, per *invidiam,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p391-5]: « Dissipata sunt ossa nostra secus infernum », supple: per invidiam, de qua dicitur Proverbiorum decimo quarto: « Putredo ossium, invidia ». — Item, per *iracundiam;* Psalmus[^p391-6]: « Dissipati sunt nec compuncti, frenduerunt super me dentibus suis ». Unde iracundus similis est daemoniaco, de quo supra nono: « Subito clamat et elidit et dissipat eum cum spuma ». — Item dissipavit per *accidiam,* secundum illud Proverbiorum decimo octavo[^p391-7]: « Qui mollis et dissolutus est in opere suo frater est sua opera dissipantis ». — Et quia peccata *spiritualia* frequenter praecipitant in *carnalia,* ideo addit: *Vivendo luxuriose,* quantum ad *gulam* et *luxuriam,* secundum illud Proverbiorum vigesimo[^p391-8]: « Luxuriosa res vinum, et tumultuosa ebrietas; quicumque his delectatur, non erit sapiens ». Unde in his est consummatio dissipationis, secundum illud Iob trige-
simo primo: « Ignis est usque ad consumtionem devorans et omnia eradicans genimina ». Unde et his peccatis non solum dissipantur animae vires, verum etiam corporis membra et divitiae temporales, ac per hoc dissipatur tota hominis substantia, scilicet rerum, membrorum et morum. Propter quod Proverbiorum vigesimo nono[^p391-9]: « Vir, qui amat sapientiam, laetificat patrem suum; qui autem nutrit scorta perdet substantiam », quia in huiusmodi impletur illud Psalmi: « Homo, cum in honore esset, non intellexit » etc.

26. (Vers. 14.). *Et postquam consummasset omnia* etc. Post descriptam filii prodigi insolentiam hic describit *miseriam et indigentiam*[^p391-10], et hoc quantum ad tria, scilicet quantum ad *famis incommodum,* quantum ad *servitutis opprobrium* et quantum ad *mendicitatis genus extremum.*

Primo ergo quantum ad *incommodum famis* dicit: *Et postquam consummasset omnia,* scilicet *culpa totum devastante,* secundum illud Sapientiae tertio[^p391-11]: « Nationis iniquae dirae sunt consummationes »; de qua consummatione Iacobi primo: « Concupiscentia, cum conceperit, parit peccatum; peccatum vero, cum consummatum fuerit, generat mortem ». — *Facta est fames valida in regione illa,* divina scilicet *iustitia ulciscente.* Haec *fames* est inopia spiritualium bonorum; unde Glossa[^p391-12]: « *Fames valida in regione longinqua* est indigentia verbi veritatis in oblivione Creatoris ». Et de hac fame Amos octavo[^p391-13]: « Mittam famem in terram; non famem panis neque sitim aquae, sed audiendi verbum Dei »; et de hac in Psalmo: « Vocavit famem super terram » etc. — *Et ipse coepit egere, nullo* scilicet *auxiliante;* unde Ambrosius[^p391-14]: « Virtutibus, earum fonte relicto. Merito ergo eget qui thesauros sapientiae divitiarumque caelestium altitudinem dereliquit »; unde Proverbiorum decimo tertio[^p391-15]: « Egestas et ignominia ei qui deserit disciplinam »; et hoc propter culpam; quia, Proverbiorum decimo quarto, « miseros facit populos peccatum ». Propter quod dicit Beda[^p391-16]: « Omnis locus, absente patre, penuria est ».
<!-- page 392 -->
Sicut enim, sapientia adveniente, veniunt omnia bona sapientiae, Sapientiae septimo: « Venerunt mihi omnia bona pariter cum illa, et innumerabilis honestas per manus illius »; sic ipsa abeunte, omnia bona recedunt, et supervenit egestas, secundum illud Proverbiorum tertio: « Egestas a Domino in domo impii, habitacula iustorum benedicentur ».

27. (Vers. 15.). Secundo quantum ad *opprobrium servitutis* subdit: *Et abiit et adhaesit uni civium regionis illius; civis* autem in regione dissimilitudinis et peccati est ille qui in peccato mansionem perpetuam collocavit. Inter hos cives unus et primus est ipse diabolus; unde Glossa[^p392-1]: « Unus civium ille est, qui, concupiscentiis terrenis merito pravitatis suae praepositus, *princeps mundi* vocatur », secundum illud Ioannis decimo quarto: « Venit enim princeps mundi huius » etc. Huic civi adhaeret quis, quando vult per omnia eius facere voluntatem; quod faciunt peccatores, secundum quod designatur secundi Machabaeorum quarto[^p392-2] de quibusdam, de quibus dicitur, quod « illis similes esse cupiebant, quos hostes et peremptores habuerunt ». — Et nota, quod dicitur *adhaesisse,* quia diabolus non habet *posse,* nisi quantum peccator ei dat ex mera voluntate; unde ex persona diaboli dicitur Isaiae quinquagesimo primo[^p392-3]: « Incurvare, ut transeamus ». — Et quia, potestate accepta, diabolus semper incitat hominem ad peccata; ideo subdit: *Et misit illum in villam suam, ut pasceret porcos;* per *porcos* intelliguntur daemones propter *ruditatem,* ratione cuius Matthaei septimo[^p392-4]: « Nolite sanctum dare canibus nec margaritas spargere ante porcos, ne forte conculcent eas pedibus suis ». Item, propter *carnalitatem;* secundae Petri secundo[^p392-5]: « Contingit eis illud veri proverbii: Sus lota in volutabro luti »; et hoc facit carnalitas; unde Proverbiorum undecimo: « Circulus aureus in naribus suis, mulier pulcra et fatua », quia diabolus per illam inquinat multos. Hanc autem *ruditatem* habent daemones in se ipsis, sed *carnalitatem* suggerunt aliis; propter quod et dicuntur *spiritus immundi;* unde recte *porcis* cognati sunt, secundum illud Matthaei octavo[^p392-6]: « Si eiicis
nos, mitte nos in porcos ». Hi enim porci pascuntur immunditiis peccatorum, quia in hoc solo daemones delectantur; unde Ambrosius[^p392-7]: « Cibus daemonum est ebrietas, fornicatio et huiusmodi, quae blanda sunt et ad suum usum provocant, neque alia cura est luxuriosis, nisi ut ventrem suum impleant; *quorum Deus venter est* ». De his ad Philippenses tertio[^p392-8]: « Quorum Deus venter est, et gloria in confusione, qui terrena sapiunt ». Unde hos porcos pascunt homines porcini, in quibus regnat *ruditas* intelligentiae, *carnalitas* concupiscentiae, *cupiditas* avaritiae. Et tales ad modum porcorum nihil prorsus valent ad *vitam,* sed solum ad *interitum;* in cuius designationem dicitur supra octavo[^p392-9], quod « impetu magno abiit grex in stagnum et suffocatus est » etc.

28. (Vers. 16.). Tertio autem quantum ad *extremum genus mendicitatis* subdit: *Et cupiebat implere ventrem suum de siliquis, quas porci manducabant.* « *Siliqua,* ut dicit Glossa[^p392-10], est genus leguminis sonoris follibus et vacuis, quod ventrem magis onerat, quam reficiat ». Et ideo designat delectationes vitiorum, quae peccatori aliquando aliquid sonare videntur exterius, sed nihil interius sunt; unde huiusmodi delectationes somniis comparantur; Isaiae vigesimo nono[^p392-11]: « Sicut somniat esuriens et comedit; cum autem expergefactus fuerit, vacua est anima eius; et sicut somniat sitiens et bibit, et postquam expergefactus fuerit, lassus adhuc sitit, et anima eius vacua est; sic erit multitudo omnium gentium, quae dimicaverunt contra montem Sion ». Has *siliquas* porci manducant, quia, Proverbiorum secundo[^p392-12], « laetantur, cum male fecerint, et exsultant in rebus pessimis ». Et his delectationibus tanquam deliciis suis peccator quaerit et appetit satiari; unde de talibus Iob trigesimo: « Qui rodebant in solitudine, squallentes calamitate et miseria. Et mandebant herbas et arborum cortices, et radix iuniperorum erat cibus eorum ». « Qui inter huiusmodi laetabantur et esse sub sentibus delicias computabant ». — Et quia de talibus non potest homo satiari, ideo subdit: *Et nemo illi dabat;* quia frequenter, cum peccator huiusmodi quaerit delicias, iusto Dei iudicio perdit
<!-- page 393 -->
illas. Unde Osee secundo[^p393-1] dicit anima peccatrix: « Vadam post amatores meos, qui dant panes mihi et aquas meas et lanam meam et linum meum et oleum meum et potum meum. Propter hoc ecce, ego sepiam viam tuam spinis et sepiam eam maceria, et semitas suas non inveniet »; et post: « Cessare faciam omne gaudium eius et neomeniam eius et Sabbata eius et omnia festa eius ». Et hoc facit Deus ideo, ut peccatores a se aversos per delectationes ad se revocet per afflictiones. Unde Augustinus in libro Confessionum[^p393-2]: « Omnes, inquit, delectationes meas amaritudinibus respersisti, Domine Deus, ut quaererem sine offensione laetari et non invenirem nisi in te ». *Nemo* igitur *dat de istis siliquis,* quando peccatori subtrahitur consolatio carnalis, ut sic saltem revertatur ad cognitionem Dei et sui; et sicut inebriatus est per delectationem, sic evigilet per tribulationem, secundum illud Isaiae vigesimo octavo[^p393-3]: « Tantummodo vexatio intellectum dabit auditui »; unde et Levitici vigesimo sexto: « Immittam pestilentiam in medio vestri, et trademini in hostium manibus ». « Et comedetis et non saturabimini ».

29. (Vers. 17.). *Ipse autem in se reversus*[^p393-4]. Post descriptam filii prodigi insolentiam et miseriam describit hic tertio *eius poenitentiam*[^p393-5], quam describit valde sufficientem, scilicet quantum ad humilitatem *contritionis, confessionis* et *satisfactionis.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *humilitatem contritionis* dicit: *Ipse autem in se reversus,* scilicet per recognitionem propriae iniquitatis, quia dolor compunctionis facit *circumspicere internam conscientiam;* propter quod dicit: *In se reversus,* secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo sexto[^p393-6]: « Redite, praevaricatores, ad cor ». — Facit etiam dolor compunctionis *circumspicere felicitatem amissam;* propter quod addit: *Dixit: Quanti mercenarii in domo patris mei abundant panibus!* id est, in domo Dei feliciter et
suaviter vivunt. — Et nota, quod quidam sunt mercenarii *mali,* de quibus Ioannis decimo[^p393-7]: « Mercenarius autem fugit, quia mercenarius est ». Isti, etsi abundant aliquando in domo summi Patris panibus materialibus, non tamen spiritualibus; propter quod Matthaei sexto: « Amen dico vobis: Receperunt mercedem suam ». Et talibus dicitur Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto: « Quare appenditis argentum non in panibus » etc. Alii sunt *mercenarii boni,* de quibus Genesis penultimo[^p393-8]: « Issachar, asinus fortis, vidit requiem, quod esset bona, et terram, quod optima, et supposuit humeros suos ». Et isti sunt qui serviunt pro mercede aeterna, et tales abundant frequenter panibus fidei, spei et caritatis, de quibus supra undecimo[^p393-9]: « Amice, commoda mihi tres panes »; et de quibus in Psalmo: « Pauperes eius saturabo panibus ». — Facit nihilominus compunctio *conspicere calamitatem propriam;* unde addit: *Ego autem hic fame pereo,* scilicet ob defectum divinae gratiae et doctrinae; et haec est magna calamitas; Threnorum quarto[^p393-10]: « Melius fuit occisis gladio quam interfectis fame, quoniam isti extabuerunt, consumti a sterilitate terrae »; et pro istis dicitur Threnorum secundo: « Leva manus tuas pro animabus parvulorum tuorum, qui defecerunt in fame in capite omnium compitorum ». Ex hac autem afflictione compunctionis spiritus provocatur et excitatur; propter quod in Psalmo[^p393-11]: « Afflictus sum et humiliatus sum nimis, rugiebam a gemitu cordis mei »; et sequitur: « Quoniam ego in flagella paratus sum ».

30. (Vers. 18.). Secundo quantum ad *humilitatem confessionis* subdit: *Surgam et ibo ad patrem meum et dicam illi*[^p393-12]*: Pater, peccavi in caelum;* in quo confitetur, se peccasse in *ecclesiasticam unitatem,* quae intelligitur nomine *caeli.* Nam in hac habitat Deus; Isaiae ultimo[^p393-13]: « Caelum mihi sedes
<!-- page 394 -->
est »; et in Psalmo: « Dominus in templo sancto suo, Dominus in caelo sedes eius ». — Confitetur etiam, se peccasse contra *divinam maiestatem;* unde addit: *Et coram te,* quia illi omnia sunt aperta; ad Hebraeos quarto[^p394-1]: « Omnia nuda et aperta sunt oculis eius, ad quem nobis sermo ». Propter quod dicitur Proverbiorum decimo sexto: « Omnes viae hominum patent oculis eius, et spirituum ponderator est Dominus ».

31. (Vers. 19.). Et quia offensa tantae maiestatis privat nos dignitate divinae filiationis, ideo confitetur suam indignitatem, cum subdit: *Iam non sum dignus vocari filius tuus;* et hoc quidem recte et vere confitetur, quia esse filium Dei est maximae caritatis, secundum illud primae Ioannis tertio[^p394-2]: « Videte, fratres, qualem caritatem dedit nobis Pater, ut filii Dei nominemur et simus ». Hoc nomine peccator est indignus, quia contempsit Patrem suum, secundum illud Isaiae primo: « Filios enutrivi et exaltavi, ipsi autem spreverunt me ». — Et quoniam verae confessionis est, non solum confiteri peccati impietatem et peccantis indignitatem, verum etiam *implorare pietatem;* ideo addit: *Fac me sicut unum de*[^p394-3] *mercenariis tuis.* — Et nota, quod hic distinguitur *filius* et *servus* et *mercenarius:* quia *filius* est qui servit ex *amore;* ad Galatas quarto[^p394-4]: « Quoniam estis filii Dei, misit Deus spiritum Filii sui » etc.; *servus,* qui ex *timore;* Malachiae primo: « Filius honorat patrem, servus autem dominum suum timebit »; sed *mercenarius* est qui servit ex *spe hereditatis aeternae,* de quo mercenario Iob septimo: « Militia est vita hominis super terram, et sicut dies mercenarii dies eius. Sicut servus desiderat umbram, et sicut mercenarius praestolatur finem operis sui, ita et ego menses vacuos et noctes laboriosas enumeravi mihi ». — Ex his igitur colligitur, quod confessio debet esse *vera* et *humilis,* ut sic *recognoscatur gravitas peccati, indignitas peccantis,* et *imploretur misericordia pii patris,* affectu scilicet humili, quia dicitur Ecclesiastici trigesimo quinto[^p394-5]: « Oratio humiliantis se nubes penetrabit ». Huius exemplum est infra decimo octavo de publicano.

32. (Vers. 20.). Tertio quantum ad *humilitatem satisfactionis* subiungit: *Et surgens venit ad
*patrem suum.* Per *surrectionem* istam intelligitur peccati desertio et quaedam elongatio ab illo, quae est satisfactionis *initium.* Nam « satisfacere est causas peccatorum excidere et eis peccandi aditum non indulgere[^p394-6] »; unde et in huius figuram adolescenti adhuc *mortuo* dictum est supra septimo: « Adolescens, tibi dico, surge », ut sic deseratur *culpa.* Homini similiter *contracto* dictum est Ioannis quinto: « Surge, tolle grabatum tuum et ambula », ut sic relinquatur *peccatorum sequela.* Similiter Petro *compedito* dictum est Actuum duodecimo[^p394-7]: « Surge velociter »; ut sic relinquatur *peccati occasio* seu mala societas et consuetudo. Propter quae peccatori dicitur ad Ephesios quinto: « Surge, qui dormis, et exsurge a mortuis » etc. — Et quia[^p394-8] « minus est mala non agere, nisi quisque etiam studeat bonis operibus insudare », protinus subditur: *Venit ad patrem suum,* per ipsius imitationem; Ioannis decimo quarto[^p394-9]: « Nemo venit ad Patrem nisi per me ». *Venit,* inquam, ut qui prius recesserat superbe praevaricando, nunc ad patrem veniat humiliter satisfaciendo, secundum illud Isaiae sexagesimo[^p394-10]: « Venient ad te omnes, qui detrahebant tibi, et adorabunt vestigia pedem tuorum » etc. Omnis, qui sic venit, a Deo *trahitur;* Ioannis sexto: « Nemo venit ad me, nisi Pater meus traxerit eum »; et sic *tractus adiuvatur;* Iob vigesimo tertio[^p394-11]: « Quis mihi tribuat, ut cognoscam et inveniam illum et veniam usque ad solium eius »? et sic *adiutus suscipitur;* Ioannis sexto: « Eum qui venit ad me, non eiiciam foras ».

#### The parable of the prodigal son.

21. *And he said: A certain man had two sons.* After the parable of the shepherd and his sheep and of the woman and the groats there is subjoined here, in the third place[^p389-10], the parable *of the father and the sons,* so that, since the piety of a father toward his son is greatest, the piety of God toward the converted sinner, who is understood by *the prodigal son,* may here be shown to be greatest[^tr-pietas]. And since the mercy of the father is shown in this, that he relieved the misery of the son who first sinned and afterwards did penance; therefore in this parable the Evangelist describes four things. For first he describes the prodigal son's *insolence;* second, his *misery and want,* at[^p389-11]: *And after he had spent all;* third, his *penance,* at: *But he, returning to himself, said* etc.; fourth, the *mercy of the father,* at: *And when he was yet a great way off.* And this indeed he does in a most right procedure and order. For *insolence* cast him headlong into misery and into want; *want,* again, roused him to penance, and *penance* prepared him for obtaining the father's mercy.

Concerning the expression of the prodigal son's *insolence* three things are brought in by the Evangelist, namely *the condition of human liberty, the perpetration of voluntary fault* and *the wasting of the good*[^p389-12] *of grace and of nature.*

22. (Vers. 11.). First, then, as to *the condition of human liberty* he says: *And he said: A certain

<!-- page 390 -->
man had two sons.* By this *man,* as has often been said[^p390-1], we understand the Lord, kind and pious. For by reason of his highest piety, which renders him kind and humane toward man, he can deservedly be called *a man;* whence Daniel, the tenth chapter[^p390-2]: « There touched me as it were the appearance of a man, and strengthened me ». For the designation of this, too, God frequently appeared in human likeness, through a subject creature, to the holy Fathers and Prophets. But by *the two sons* we understand the whole of the human race, not only as to Gentiles and Jews, as the Gloss[^p390-3] expounds, but also generally as to the innocent and the penitent, as must be understood from the very application of the parable. Whence Bede[^p390-4]: « This parable can be understood of the Jew and the Gentile, and generally of the penitent and the just, or him who seems just to himself ». And these are called *sons,* because they were created in liberty of choice and for having an eternal inheritance; John, the eighth chapter[^p390-5]: « Now the servant abideth not in the house for ever; but the son abideth for ever »; whence in the Psalm: « As a father hath compassion on his children, so hath the Lord compassion on them that fear him ».

23. (Vers. 12.). And because liberty of choice in the vain and in sinners frequently presumes on its own strength and usurps to itself what is God's, therefore he subjoins: *And the younger of them said to his father: Father*[^p390-6]*, give me the portion of substance that falleth to me.* He is called *the younger* who is the vainer and the more intent on sensible goods, according to that of Ecclesiastes, the eleventh chapter[^p390-7]: « For youth and pleasure are vain »; and Ecclesiastes, in the same chapter above: « Rejoice, O young man, in thy youth »; « and know that for all these God will bring thee into judgment ». This young man asks for *his portion* when he wishes to be left to his own liberty, so as to go no longer according to the command of grace but according to the nod of his own will. Whence Bede[^p390-8]: « *Substance* is all that we do, that we live, know, think and speak. The younger, then, asks that the portion of this substance be given to him, when man, delighted with his own rational sense, sought to rule himself by free choice and to go out from the dominion of the Creator ». This petition was not for himself, but rather against himself, because he asked to be exposed to himself and left to his own judgement; as was also the petition of the children of Israel by which they asked for a king; the first of Kings, the eighth chapter[^p390-9]: « The children of Israel said to Samuel: Make us a king, to judge us ». « And the Lord said to Samuel: They have not rejected thee, but me, that I should not reign over them ». — And because the Lord by a just judgement leaves to himself him who presumes on himself; therefore he subjoins: *And he divided unto them his substance.* For so it was fitting, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the fifteenth chapter[^p390-10]: « God made man from the beginning, and left him in the hand of his own counsel ». For so the Lord does by a just judgement, according to that of Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter: « And to one he gave five talents, and to another two, and to another one, to every one according to his proper ability » etc.

24. (Vers. 13.). Second, as to *the perpetration of voluntary fault* he subjoins: *And not many days after, gathering all together,* through the dominion of free choice, which it has in respect of all the natural powers; *the younger son went abroad into a far country,* and this by perpetrating sin. For a *country* is called *far* from God not as to local situation, since God is everywhere, but through unlikeness of will. Whence the Gloss[^p390-11]: « *He went into a far country,* not by changing place but mind. For the more anyone offends in wicked work, the farther he withdraws from the grace of God »; whence Proverbs, the fifteenth chapter: « The Lord is far from the wicked: and he will hear the prayers of the just ». Now sin is *a far country* from God because, since it is *iniquity,* it removes far from the highest *goodness,* according to that of Isaias, the fifty-ninth chapter[^p390-12]: « But your iniquities have divided between you and your God, and your sins have hid his face from you ». Since it is *darkness,* it removes far from the highest *light and truth;* the first of John, the first chapter[^p390-13]: « God is light, and in him there is no darkness. If we say that we have fellowship with him, and walk in darkness, we lie, and do not the truth »; and a little after he subjoins, of what darkness he understands it: « He that hateth his brother, is in darkness » etc. Since sin is also *nothing,* it removes far from the highest *entity;* on account of which Jeremias, the second chapter[^p390-14]: « What iniquity have your fathers found in me,

<!-- page 391 -->
that they have removed themselves far from me, and have walked after vanity »? Of these three together the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter[^p391-1]: « For what participation hath justice with iniquity? Or what fellowship hath light with darkness? And what concord hath Christ with Belial »? « And what agreement hath the temple of God with idols »? And therefore deservedly the sinner, who removes himself far from God through sin and goes into a far country, far from the heavenly country, is cast forth into hell, which is the country of death and of darkness, according to that of Job, the tenth chapter[^p391-2]: « Before I go to a land that is dark and covered with the mist of death, a land of misery and darkness, where the shadow of death, and no order » etc.

25. Third, as to *the wasting of the good of grace and of nature* he subjoins: *He wasted*[^p391-3] *his substance. He wasted* it, I say, through *spiritual* sins, which most of all waste the soul, and this indeed through *pride;* Nahum, the second chapter[^p391-4]: « For the Lord hath rendered the pride of Jacob, as the pride of Israel: because the wasters have wasted them, and have marred their vine branches ». — Likewise, through *vainglory;* Isaias, the third chapter: « O my people, they that call thee blessed, the same deceive thee, and waste the way of thy steps ». — Likewise, through *envy,* according to that of the Psalm[^p391-5]: « Our bones are wasted by the side of hell », supply: through envy, of which it is said in Proverbs, the fourteenth chapter: « The rottenness of the bones, envy ». — Likewise, through *anger;* the Psalm[^p391-6]: « They were wasted, and were not pricked; they gnashed upon me with their teeth ». Whence the angry man is like the demoniac, of whom above, the ninth chapter: « It suddenly crieth out, and throweth him down, and wasteth him with foam ». — Likewise he wasted it through *acedia,* according to that of Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter[^p391-7]: « He that is loose and slack in his work, is the brother of him that wasteth his own works ». — And because *spiritual* sins frequently cast headlong into *carnal* ones, therefore he adds: *Living riotously,* as to *gluttony* and *lust,* according to that of Proverbs, the twentieth chapter[^p391-8]: « Wine is a luxurious thing, and drunkenness riotous: whosoever is delighted therewith shall not be wise ». Whence in these is the consummation of the wasting, according to that of Job, the thirty-first chapter: « It is a fire that devoureth even to consumption, and rooteth up all things that spring ». Whence by these sins not only are the powers of the soul wasted, but also the members of the body and temporal riches, and through this the whole substance of man is wasted, namely of things, of members and of morals. On account of which Proverbs, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p391-9]: « A man that loveth wisdom, rejoiceth his father: but he that maintaineth harlots, shall squander away his substance », because in such is fulfilled that of the Psalm: « Man when he was in honour did not understand » etc.

26. (Vers. 14.). *And after he had spent all* etc. After the prodigal son's insolence has been described, here he describes his *misery and want*[^p391-10], and this as to three things, namely as to *the hardship of famine,* as to *the reproach of servitude* and as to *the extreme kind of beggary.*

First, then, as to *the hardship of famine* he says: *And after he had spent all,* namely *with fault laying waste the whole,* according to that of Wisdom, the third chapter[^p391-11]: « For dreadful are the consummations of a wicked nation »; of which consummation James, the first chapter: « Then when concupiscence hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin. But sin, when it is consummated, begetteth death ». — *There came a mighty famine in that country,* namely *with the divine justice avenging.* This *famine* is the lack of spiritual goods; whence the Gloss[^p391-12]: « *A mighty famine in the far country* is the want of the word of truth in forgetfulness of the Creator ». And of this famine Amos, the eighth chapter[^p391-13]: « I will send forth a famine into the land: not a famine of bread, nor a thirst of water, but of hearing the word of God »; and of this in the Psalm: « And he called a famine upon the land » etc. — *And he began to be in want,* namely *with no one helping;* whence Ambrose[^p391-14]: « In want of virtues, their fount forsaken. Deservedly, then, he is in want who has forsaken the treasures of wisdom and the height of the heavenly riches »; whence Proverbs, the thirteenth chapter[^p391-15]: « Poverty and shame to him that forsaketh discipline »; and this on account of fault; because, Proverbs, the fourteenth chapter, « sin maketh peoples miserable ». On account of which Bede says[^p391-16]: « Every place, the father being absent, is penury ».

<!-- page 392 -->
For as, when wisdom comes, all the goods of wisdom come, Wisdom, the seventh chapter: « Now all good things came to me together with her, and innumerable riches through her hands »; so, when she goes away, all goods withdraw, and want comes upon one, according to that of Proverbs, the third chapter: « Want is from the Lord in the house of the wicked: but the habitations of the just shall be blessed ».

27. (Vers. 15.). Second, as to *the reproach of servitude* he subjoins: *And he went and cleaved to one of the citizens of that country;* but *a citizen* in the country of unlikeness and of sin is he who has set his perpetual dwelling in sin. Among these citizens one, and the first, is the devil himself; whence the Gloss[^p392-1]: « One of the citizens is he who, set over earthly concupiscences by the merit of his wickedness, is called *the prince of the world* », according to that of John, the fourteenth chapter: « For the prince of this world cometh » etc. To this citizen one cleaves when he wishes to do his will in all things; which sinners do, according to what is designated in the second of Machabees, the fourth chapter[^p392-2], of certain men, of whom it is said that « they desired to be like to those whom they had as enemies and destroyers ». — And note that he is said *to have cleaved,* because the devil has no *power* except so far as the sinner gives it to him of his mere will; whence in the person of the devil it is said in Isaias, the fifty-first chapter[^p392-3]: « Bow down, that we may go over ». — And because, the power received, the devil always incites man to sins; therefore he subjoins: *And he sent him into his farm to feed swine;* by *the swine* are understood the demons, on account of *rudeness,* by reason of which Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p392-4]: « Give not that which is holy to dogs; neither cast ye your pearls before swine, lest perhaps they trample them under their feet ». Likewise, on account of *carnality;* the second of Peter, the second chapter[^p392-5]: « That of the true proverb has happened to them: The sow that was washed, to her wallowing in the mire »; and this carnality does; whence Proverbs, the eleventh chapter: « A golden ring in a swine's snout, a woman fair and foolish », because the devil defiles many through her. Now the demons have this *rudeness* in themselves, but they suggest *carnality* to others; on account of which they are also called *unclean spirits;* whence they are rightly akin to *the swine,* according to that of Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p392-6]: « If thou cast us out, send us into the swine ». For these swine are fed on the uncleannesses of sinners, because in this alone do the demons take delight; whence Ambrose[^p392-7]: « The food of the demons is drunkenness, fornication and the like, which are alluring and provoke to their use; nor have the riotous any other care than to fill their belly; *whose God is their belly* ». Of these, to the Philippians, the third chapter[^p392-8]: « Whose God is their belly, and whose glory is in confusion, who mind earthly things ». Whence swinish men feed these swine, in whom reign *rudeness* of intelligence, *carnality* of concupiscence, *cupidity* of avarice. And such, after the manner of swine, avail nothing at all for *life,* but only for *destruction;* in designation of which it is said above, the eighth chapter[^p392-9], that « the herd went with great violence into the lake, and was stifled » etc.

28. (Vers. 16.). Third, as to *the extreme kind of beggary* he subjoins: *And he would fain have filled his belly with the husks the swine did eat.* « *A husk,* as the Gloss says[^p392-10], is a kind of pulse with sounding and empty pods, which loads the belly rather than refreshes it ». And therefore it designates the delights of the vices, which to the sinner sometimes seem to sound something outwardly, but within are nothing; whence such delights are compared to dreams; Isaias, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p392-11]: « And as he that is hungry dreameth, and eateth, but when he is awake, his soul is empty: and as he that is thirsty dreameth, and drinketh, and after he is awake, is yet faint with thirst, and his soul is empty: so shall be the multitude of all the Gentiles, that have fought against mount Sion ». These *husks* the swine eat, because, Proverbs, the second chapter[^p392-12], they « are glad when they have done evil, and exult in most wicked things ». And with these delights, as with his dainties, the sinner seeks and desires to be filled; whence of such Job, the thirtieth chapter: « Who gnawed in the wilderness, disfigured with calamity and misery. And they ate grass, and the barks of trees, and the root of junipers was their food ». « Who rejoiced among these things, and counted it delightful to be under the briers ». — And because man cannot be filled with such things, therefore he subjoins: *And no man gave unto him;* because frequently, when the sinner seeks dainties of this kind, by a just judgement of God he loses

<!-- page 393 -->
them. Whence in Osee, the second chapter[^p393-1], the sinful soul says: « I will go after my lovers, that give me my bread, and my waters, and my wool, and my flax, and my oil, and my drink. Wherefore behold I will hedge up thy way with thorns, and I will stop it up with a wall, and she shall not find her paths »; and after: « I will cause all her joy to cease, and her new moon, and her sabbaths, and all her feasts ». And God does this to the end that he may call back to himself through afflictions sinners turned away from him through delights. Whence Augustine in the book of the Confessions[^p393-2]: « Thou hast sprinkled, he says, all my delights with bitternesses, O Lord God, that I might seek to rejoice without offence and might find it nowhere but in thee ». *No man,* then, *gives of these husks,* when carnal consolation is withdrawn from the sinner, that so at least he may return to the knowledge of God and of himself; and as he was made drunk through delight, so he may awake through tribulation, according to that of Isaias, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p393-3]: « Vexation alone shall make you understand what you hear »; whence also Leviticus, the twenty-sixth chapter: « I will send the pestilence in the midst of you, and you shall be delivered into the hands of your enemies ». « And you shall eat, and shall not be filled ».

29. (Vers. 17.). *But he, returning to himself*[^p393-4]. After the prodigal son's insolence and misery have been described, he describes here, in the third place, *his penance*[^p393-5], which he describes as thoroughly sufficient, namely as to the humility *of contrition, of confession* and *of satisfaction.*

First, then, as to *the humility of contrition* he says: *But he, returning to himself,* namely through the recognition of his own iniquity, because the sorrow of compunction makes one *look round upon the inward conscience;* on account of which he says: *Returning to himself,* according to that of Isaias, the forty-sixth chapter[^p393-6]: « Return, ye prevaricators, to the heart ». — The sorrow of compunction also makes one *look round upon the happiness lost;* on account of which he adds: *He said: How many hired servants in my father's house abound with bread!* that is, in the house of God live happily and suavely. — And note that some are *bad* hired servants, of whom John, the tenth chapter[^p393-7]: « And the hireling flieth, because he is a hireling ». These, although they sometimes abound in the house of the highest Father with material breads, yet do not with spiritual ones; on account of which Matthew, the sixth chapter: « Amen I say to you, they have received their reward ». And to such it is said in Isaias, the fifty-fifth chapter: « Why do you weigh out silver not for bread » etc. Others are *good hired servants,* of whom Genesis, the next to last chapter[^p393-8]: « Issachar, a strong ass, saw rest that it was good, and the land that it was excellent, and he put under his shoulders ». And these are they who serve for an eternal reward, and such frequently abound with the breads of faith, hope and charity, of which above, the eleventh chapter[^p393-9]: « Friend, lend me three loaves »; and of which in the Psalm: « I will satisfy her poor with bread ». — Compunction makes one also *behold his own calamity;* whence he adds: *And I here perish with hunger,* namely because of the lack of divine grace and doctrine; and this is a great calamity; Lamentations, the fourth chapter[^p393-10]: « It was better with them that were slain by the sword, than with them that died with hunger; for these pined away, consumed by the barrenness of the earth »; and for these it is said in Lamentations, the second chapter: « Lift up thy hands for the souls of thy little children, that have fainted for hunger at the top of all the streets ». And from this affliction the spirit of compunction is provoked and roused; on account of which in the Psalm[^p393-11]: « I am afflicted and humbled exceedingly: I roared with the groaning of my heart »; and there follows: « For I am ready for scourges ».

30. (Vers. 18.). Second, as to *the humility of confession* he subjoins: *I will arise, and will go to my father, and say to him*[^p393-12]*: Father, I have sinned against heaven;* in which he confesses that he has sinned against *ecclesiastical unity,* which is understood by the name of *heaven.* For in this God dwells; Isaias, the last chapter[^p393-13]: « Heaven is my throne »;

<!-- page 394 -->
and in the Psalm: « The Lord is in his holy temple, the Lord's throne is in heaven ». — He confesses also that he has sinned against *the divine majesty;* whence he adds: *And before thee,* because to him all things are open; to the Hebrews, the fourth chapter[^p394-1]: « All things are naked and open to his eyes, to whom our speech is ». On account of which it is said in Proverbs, the sixteenth chapter: « All the ways of men are open to his eyes: the Lord is the weigher of spirits ».

31. (Vers. 19.). And because the offence of so great a majesty deprives us of the dignity of divine sonship, therefore he confesses his unworthiness, when he subjoins: *I am not worthy to be called thy son;* and this indeed he confesses rightly and truly, because to be a son of God is of the greatest charity, according to that of the first of John, the third chapter[^p394-2]: « See, brethren, what manner of charity the Father hath given us, that we should be named and should be the sons of God ». Of this name the sinner is unworthy, because he has despised his Father, according to that of Isaias, the first chapter: « I have brought up children, and exalted them: but they have despised me ». — And since it belongs to true confession not only to confess the impiety of the sin and the unworthiness of the sinner, but also *to implore piety;* therefore he adds: *Make me as one of*[^p394-3] *thy hired servants.* — And note that here *son* and *servant* and *hired servant* are distinguished: for *a son* is he who serves out of *love;* to the Galatians, the fourth chapter[^p394-4]: « Because you are sons of God, God hath sent the Spirit of his Son » etc.; *a servant,* he who serves out of *fear;* Malachias, the first chapter: « The son honoureth the father, but the servant shall fear his master »; but *a hired servant* is he who serves out of *hope of the eternal inheritance,* of which hired servant Job, the seventh chapter: « The life of man upon earth is a warfare, and his days are like the days of a hireling. As a servant longeth for the shade, and as the hireling looketh for the end of his work; so I also have numbered to myself empty months and wearisome nights ». — From these, then, it is gathered that confession ought to be *true* and *humble,* so that *the gravity of the sin* and *the unworthiness of the sinner* may be recognized, and *the mercy of the pious father* implored, namely with a humble affection, because it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-fifth chapter[^p394-5]: « The prayer of him that humbleth himself, shall pierce the clouds ». An example of this is below, the eighteenth chapter, of the publican.

32. (Vers. 20.). Third, as to *the humility of satisfaction* he subjoins: *And rising up he came to his father.* By this *rising* is understood the desertion of sin and a certain removal far from it, which is *the beginning* of satisfaction. For « to satisfy is to cut off the causes of sins and not to grant them an entrance for sinning[^p394-6] »; whence also in figure of this it was said above, the seventh chapter, to the young man still *dead:* « Young man, I say to thee, arise », that so *fault* may be deserted. To the *palsied* man likewise it was said in John, the fifth chapter: « Arise, take up thy bed, and walk », that so *the train of sins* may be left behind. Likewise to Peter *in fetters* it was said in Acts, the twelfth chapter[^p394-7]: « Arise quickly »; that so *the occasion of sin* may be left behind, or evil company and custom. On account of which it is said to the sinner, to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter: « Rise thou that sleepest, and arise from the dead » etc. — And because[^p394-8] « it is little not to do evil things, unless each one also strive to sweat over good works », it is forthwith subjoined: *He came to his father,* through imitation of him; John, the fourteenth chapter[^p394-9]: « No man cometh to the Father, but by me ». *He came,* I say, so that he who had before withdrawn by proudly prevaricating may now come to the father by humbly satisfying, according to that of Isaias, the sixtieth chapter[^p394-10]: « And all they that slandered thee shall come to thee, and shall worship the steps of thy feet [so printed: *pedem* for *pedum*] » etc. Everyone who so comes is *drawn* by God; John, the sixth chapter: « No man cometh to me, unless my Father draw him »; and so *drawn, he is helped;* Job, the twenty-third chapter[^p394-11]: « Who will grant me that I might know and find him, and come even to his throne »? and so *helped, he is received;* John, the sixth chapter: « Him that cometh to me, I will not cast out ».

[^tr-pietas]: **En.** Translator's note: *pietas* is rendered "piety" throughout, and *pius* "pious" (ruling 12: the six mercy nouns stay six words; ruling 86). Chapter XV ¶ 1 divides the chapter by the one Latin root: the *impietas* of the Jews murmuring against clemency, the *manifestatio pietatis divinae* of the three parables, and the *commendatio et persuasio pietatis humanae* of Chapter XVI — "impiety … divine piety … human piety", one English root, as the Latin has one. This third parable belongs to the second member, and here (¶ 21) Bonaventure opens it on the comparison itself: *cum maxima sit pietas patris ad suum filium, maxima ostendatur hic pietas Dei ad peccatorem conversum*, "since the piety of a father toward his son is greatest, the piety of God toward the converted sinner may here be shown to be greatest". The man of the parable is *Dominus benignus et pius*, "the Lord, kind and pious", called *a man* by reason of *summae pietatis*, "his highest piety" (¶ 22); and the son's confession ends by *implorare pietatem*, "to implore piety", the *misericordia pii patris*, "the mercy of the pious father" (¶ 31), set against *peccati impietatem*, "the impiety of the sin". The Latin word covers the tenderness of a parent toward a child as well as the devotion of a creature toward God; in these places Bonaventure is using the first sense, and the parable turns on it.

## Notes

**THE EIGHTY-FIFTH CHUNK OF THE *COMMENTARIUS IN EVANGELIUM S. LUCAE*, THE THIRD OF CAPITULUM XV: THE PARABLE OF THE PRODIGAL SON, ITS FIRST THREE MEMBERS.** ¶ 21 opens the third of the three parables named at ¶ 6 on the piety of a father toward his son as the greatest, and divides it four ways: the prodigal son's insolence; his misery and want (*Et postquam consummasset omnia*, ¶ 26); his penance (*Ipse autem in se reversus*, ¶ 29); the father's mercy (*Cum autem adhuc longe esset*, ¶ 33 — the next chunk).
