# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. IX, Vers. 10-17
## *Et reversi Apostoli nuntiaverunt. Post informationem Apostolorum factam per verbum sive praeceptum sequitur hic informatio eorundem per factum sive exemplum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 220 -->
15. *Et reversi Apostoli nuntiaverunt*[^p220-11]. Post informationem Apostolorum factam per verbum sive
<!-- page 221 -->
praeceptum sequitur hic informatio eorundem *per factum* sive exemplum. Habet autem haec pars duas. In prima enim informat per exemplum *familiare;* in secunda vero per *figurale,* ibi[^p221-1]: *Dies autem coeperat declinare.* Primum attenditur in turbarum *eruditione spirituali;* secundum, in eorum *refectione corporali,* quae formam et figuram gerit refectionis spiritualis.

*Eruditio* autem[^p221-2] turbarum a Domino, in qua tribuitur forma praedicatoribus in docendo fidelium populo, explicatur quantum ad quatuor, quae requiruntur in praedicando. Primum est *fidelitas consortii;* secundum est *opportunitas loci;* tertium, *docilitas populi;* quartum autem, *affabilitas Magistri;* ut sic nihil desit verbo Dei, nec ex parte assistentium nec ex parte adversantium nec ex parte audientium[^p221-3] nec ex parte docentium, quin verbum Domini habeat debitum effectum.

16. (Vers. 10.). Primo igitur quantum ad *fidelitatem consortii* dicitur: *Et reversi Apostoli, nuntiaverunt illi quaecumque fecerunt;* in quo apparet eorum fidelitas, quia ad divinum consortium *celeriter redierunt.* Unde fuerunt non sicut corvus, de quo Genesis octavo[^p221-4]: « Qui egrediebatur et non revertebatur »; sed sicut columba, de qua dicitur, quod « cum non invenisset, ubi requiesceret pes eius, reversa est ad Noe in arcam », tanquam fidelis amica. Sic et Apostoli columbina simplicitate praediti, sunt ad Christum celeriter reversi tanquam fideles nuntii. — Apparet etiam eorum fidelitas, quia nihil *Deo celaverunt,* sed *narraverunt ei quaecumque fecerunt;* non ut Dominum erudirent, quem nihil latet, sed ut ostenderent, se etiam nolle latere, si possent. Quocontra Isaiae vigesimo nono[^p221-5]: « Vae! qui profundi estis corde, ut a Domino abscondatis consilium » etc. Vel ideo *narraverunt,* ut de bonis factis non quaererent sibi gloriam, sed omnia referrent in Deum, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios decimo[^p221-6]: « Omnia in gloriam Dei facite »; contra illos qui dicunt illud Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo: « Manus nostra excelsa, et non Dominus, fecit haec omnia ».

17. Secundo vero quantum ad *opportunitatem loci* subdit: *Et assumtis illis,* tanquam fidelibus, ad consortium, *secessit seorsum in locum desertum, qui est Bethsaidae,* tanquam ad locum quietum et tranquillum; unde Marci sexto[^p221-7]: « Venite seorsum in desertum locum et requiescite pusillum ». Et hic locus est aptus eruditioni, secundum illud Osee secundo: « Adducam eam in solitudinem et loquar ad cor eius ». Hic autem locus desertus erat iuxta stagnum Genesareth, quod apparet ex hoc quod dicit: *Bethsaidae.* Est autem Bethsaida « civitas Andreae et Petri », sicut dicitur Ioannis primo[^p221-8]. Et nota, quod non dicit *Bethsaida,* quasi iuxta illam civitatem vel in civitate; sed *Bethsaidae,* quia remotus erat locus, in confinibus tamen civitatis Bethsaidae. Unde Marci sexto[^p221-9] dicitur, quod post multiplicationem panum iussit, eos praecedere « trans fretum ad Bethsaidam ». Unde non est contrarietas, sed unus explanavit quod alter tacuit.

18. (Vers. 11.). Tertio vero quantum ad *docilitatem populi* adiungit: *Quod cum cognovissent turbae, secutae sunt illum,* ut scilicet doctrinam eius exciperent, quem desiderabant, secundum illud Osee sexto[^p221-10]: « Sciemus sequemurque, ut cognoscamus Dominum ». Unde Beda in Glossa: « *Secutae sunt* non in iumentis, non in vehiculis, sed proprio labore pedum, ut ardorem mentis ostenderent ». Unde Marci sexto[^p221-11] dicitur, quod multi « pedestres de civitatibus venerunt illuc ». Ratio autem huius redditur Ioannis sexto, ubi dicitur: « Post haec abiit Iesus trans mare Tiberiadis, et sequebatur eum multitudo magna, quia videbant signa, quae faciebat » etc.

19. Quarto vero quantum ad *affabilitatem Magistri* subiungit: *Et excepit illos*[^p221-12]*;* in quo ostenditur benignitas Christi *in signo,* ad quam hortatur Apostolus ad Romanos decimo quinto: « Suscipite invicem, sicut et Christus suscepit vos in honorem Dei ». Nec tantum *in signo,* sed etiam *in verbo;* unde addit: *Et loquebatur illis de regno Dei,* quasi sermone affabili et familiari, ut vere ostendatur impletum illud Psalmi[^p221-13]: « Narrabo nomen tuum fratribus meis ». Licet autem loqueretur affabiliter, non tamen inutiliter, non carnaliter, quia de regno Dei, non de mundo; unde primae Petri quarto[^p221-14]: « Si quis loquitur quasi sermones Dei »; et Ioannis
<!-- page 222 -->
tertio: « Qui de terra est de terra loquitur », sed Christus de caelo; et Ioannis sexto: « Verba vitae aeternae habes ». — Nec tantum exhibuit se affabilem *verbo* et *signo,* sed etiam *facto.* Unde et subdit: *Et eos qui cura indigebant, sanabat;* in quo ostenditur perfectio dilectionis, de qua primae Ioannis tertio[^p222-1]: « Diligamus non verbo neque lingua, sed opere et veritate »; quia « qui viderit, fratrem suum necessitatem habere, et clauserit viscera sua ab eo, quomodo caritas Dei manet in eo »? Talem caritatem habuit Christus; unde Actuum decimo[^p222-2]: « Pertransiit benefaciendo et sanando omnes oppressos a diabolo », non solum bene loquendo. — Unde in hoc eruditur praedicator seu praelatus, ut se benignum exhibeat ovibus, scilicet signo, verbo, facto. In cuius rei signum et dictum est ter Petro: *Pasce,* Ioannis ultimo[^p222-3].

20. *Dies autem coeperat declinare* etc. Post informationem discipulorum per exemplum familiare subditur informatio per *factum figurale,* et hoc in turbarum refectione. Circa cuius expressionem duo introducuntur ab Evangelista. Primum est *opportunitas* ex parte indigentiae multitudinis; secundum est *liberalitas* ex parte misericordiae Salvatoris, et hoc ibi[^p222-4]: *Ait autem ad discipulos suos: Facite illos discumbere* etc.

*Opportunitas* autem *ex parte indigentiae multitudinis* exprimitur quantum ad quatuor, scilicet quantum ad *tarditatem diei,* quantum ad *ariditatem loci,* quantum ad *paucitatem cibi* et quantum ad *numerositatem populi.* Et sic conficitur ista opportunitas ex circumstantia temporis et loci, quantitatis cibi et personarum[^p222-5].

21. (Vers. 12.). Primo ergo quantum ad *diei tarditatem* dicitur: *Dies autem coeperat declinare;* et ita tempus erat sumendi refectionem, sicut dicitur infra ultimo[^p222-6]: « Quoniam advesperascit, et inclinata est iam dies ». In quo etiam apparet mira turbarum devotio, ut propter auditum divini verbi oblitae essent corporalis cibi. Simile legitur Actuum vigesimo, quod « Paulus protraxit sermonem usque ad mediam noctem ».

Secundo vero quantum ad *loci ariditatem* subditur: *Et accedentes duodecim dixerunt illi: Dimitte turbas;* et ad hoc persuadendum movebat loci ariditas. — Unde et subdit: *Ut euntes*[^p222-7] *in castella villasque, quae circa sunt, divertant et inveniant escas,* ne scilicet fame pereant. Et hoc est consilium sanum, quod fecerunt filii Israel; Genesis quadragesimo secundo: « Descenderunt filii Iacob in Aegyptum, ut emerent escas »; sicut et Apostoli, Ioannis quarto: « Discipuli autem abierant in civitatem, ut cibos emerent ». — Sic etiam isti consulebant nunc propter loci penuriam; unde et subdunt: *Quia hic in loco deserto sumus,* ubi est defectus cibi, secundum quod Exodi decimo sexto[^p222-8]: « Cur induxisti nos in desertum, ut occideres omnem multitudinem hanc fame »? Et quia difficile est invenire refectionem in deserto, ideo dicebant illud Psalmi: « Nunquid poterit Deus parare mensam in deserto »? Hoc dicebant increduli, detrahentes divinae potentiae; sed Apostoli dicebant consulendo humanae indigentiae, quia nolebant Dominum tentare.

22. (Vers. 13.). Tertio vero quantum ad *cibi modicitatem* subditur: *Ait autem ad illos: Vos date illis manducare; vos,* tanquam eorum praelati, secundum illud primae Petri quinto[^p222-9]: « Seniores, pascite qui in vobis est gregem ». Hoc autem dicit, ut ex eorum responsione cognoscatur cibi modicitas. — Propter quod additur: *At illi dixerunt: Non sunt nobis plus quam quinque panes et duo pisces.* Ex quo apparet Apostolorum inopia non solum ad reficiendam turbam, sed etiam ad propriam famem mitigandam; et ut magna ostendatur inopia, Ioannes exprimit genus panum. Dicitur enim Ioannis sexto[^p222-10]: « Est hic puer unus, qui habet quinque panes hordeaceos et duos pisces; sed haec quid sunt inter tantos »? Similes erant in hoc illi viduae, cuius farinam multiplicavit Elias; tertii Regum decimo septimo: « Vivit Dominus Deus, quia non habeo panem nisi quantum pugillus capere potest farinae »; et quarti Regum quarto dixit puer Eliseo: « Quantum est hoc, ut apponam centum viris »? Similiter responderunt hic Apostoli, vel Andreas in persona omnium, sicut dicitur Ioannis sexto[^p222-11]; vel ipse prius et post alii; inde non est contrarietas. Qualitercumque dicta sint haec verba, ostendunt impotentiam discipulorum ad reficiendam turbam. — Ne tamen crederentur hoc dicere non propter impotentiam, sed propter pigritiam; ideo offerunt obsequium ad emptionem ciborum, cum adiungunt: *Nisi forte nos*[^p222-12] *eamus et emamus in omnem turbam*
<!-- page 223 -->
*hanc escas.* Et hoc quidem potest esse verbum *offerentium obsequium,* iuxta illud Marci sexto: « Euntes emamus ducentorum denariorum panes, et dabimus illis manducare ». Vel potest esse verbum *ostendentium impotentiam* ad reficiendum, non solum quantum ad panes habitos, verum etiam quantum ad pretium. Unde dicitur Ioannis sexto[^p223-1]: « Ducentorum denariorum panes non sufficiunt eis, ut unusquisque modicum quid accipiat ». Et quidam de discipulis dixerunt uno modo, quidam alio; et Ioannes exprimit unum, Marcus alium, Lucas vero in praedicto sermone includit utrumque; quod patet ex ipsa sermonis varia prolatione.

23. (Vers. 14.). Quarto vero quantum ad *populi numerositatem* subditur: *Erant autem viri fere quinque millia,* ac per hoc tanta multitudo, ut pro uno pane essent mille viri. Unde *viros* nominat et numerat; ex quo apparet, quanta erat reliquorum turba prae sui multitudine non numerata; unde Matthaei decimo quarto[^p223-2] dicitur, quod erant « quinque millia, exceptis parvulis et mulieribus ». Ideo autem numerum exprimit, quia propter *quinque* competebat *Legi,* quae homines carnales et sensibiles informabat; propter *perfectionem millenarii* competebat ad susceptionem doctrinae perfectionis evangelicae[^p223-3].

24. (Vers. 14. 15.). *Ait autem ad discipulos* etc. Post opportunitatem ex parte indigentiae multitudinis subdit hic *liberalitatem ex parte munificentiae Salvatoris.* Manifestatur autem Salvatoris liberalitas in quatuor: in *convivantium ordinatione, victualium multitudine, ministrantium urbanitate, manducantium saturitate;* ut sic ostendatur perfecta liberalitas per ordinem discumbentium, multitudinem victualium, promptitudinem ministrantium et plenitudinem manducantium.

Primo quantum ad *discumbentium ordinationem* dicitur: *Ait autem ad discipulos suos: Facite illos discumbere per convivia quinquagenos. Et ita fecerunt.* Decet enim magnificum et liberalem dominum ordinem attendere in disponendo, iuxta quod dicitur tertii Regum decimo[^p223-4], quod « videns regina Saba sapientiam Salomonis et cibaria mensae eius et habitacula servorum et ordinem ministrantium, non habebat ultra spiritum ». Haec autem fuit dispositio ordinis cum determinatione numeri; unde in Marci sexto[^p223-5] dicitur, quod « discubuerunt in partes per centenos et quinquagenos ». Quinquagesies enim centum sunt quinque millia. Magis autem exprimitur numerus quinquagesimus, quia propter quinarium magis competebat Legi et etiam quinario panum refectionum. Ex hoc autem ostendit Christus suam miram sapientiam, qua numerum virorum praesciebat et discipulis ex sessione notificabat. Nec mirum, quia ipse erat, de quo dicitur Sapientiae undecimo[^p223-6]: « Omnia in mensura, numero et pondere disposuisti ».

25. (Vers. 16.). Secundo vero quantum ad *victualium multitudinem* dicitur: *Acceptis autem quinque panibus et duobus piscibus, respexit in caelum et benedixit illos*[^p223-7]*;* in qua benedictione fuit panum multiplicatio, iuxta illud Genesis primo: « Benedixit illis Deus et ait: Crescite et multiplicamini et replete terram »; et Genesis trigesimo dixit ad Iacob Laban: « Scio, quod benedixit mihi Dominus propter te ». Ideo autem benedicendo *respexit in caelum,* ut ostenderet, quod talis multiplicatio non erat virtute terrena sive tradita elementis, sed virtute caelesti, reservata in prioribus causis, de qua in Psalmo[^p223-8] dicitur: « Omnia, quaecumque voluit, fecit Dominus in caelo et in terra, in mari et in omnibus abyssis ». — Et nota, quod dicitur: *Respexit et benedixit: respexit* quidem ex humilitate humana, et *benedixit* ex virtute divina; Psalmus[^p223-9]: « Levavi oculos meos in montes ».

26. Tertio vero quantum ad *ministrantium urbanitatem* subditur: *Et fregit et distribuit discipulis suis, ut apponerent*[^p223-10] *ante turbas.* Ipsi enim erant nobiles Christi ministri et dispensatores et prompti ad serviendum, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios quarto: « Sic nos existimet homo ut ministros Christi et dispensatores mysteriorum Dei ». Decet enim nobilem dominum nobiles habere ministros, secundum illud Esther primo[^p223-11]: « Sic rex statuerat,
<!-- page 224 -->
praeponens singulis mensis singulos de principibus suis, ut sumeret unusquisque quod vellet ». In curia autem Iesu Christi nobiliores ministrant et serviunt, sicut dicitur Lucae vigesimo secundo: « Qui maior est in vobis fiat sicut minor; et qui praecessor est, sicut ministrator »; post: « Ego autem in medio vestrum sum, sicut qui ministrat ».

27. (Vers. 17.). Quarto vero quantum ad *manducantium saturitatem* subditur: *Et manducaverunt omnes et saturati sunt,* ita ut verificaretur illud Psalmi[^p224-1]: « Edent pauperes et saturabuntur, et laudabunt Dominum qui requirunt eum »; et illud: « Manducaverunt et saturati sunt nimis »; *saturati,* inquam, saturitate *iustitiae,* de qua Proverbiorum decimo tertio[^p224-2]: « Iustus comedit et replet animam suam »; « beati enim, qui esuriunt et sitiunt iustitiam, quoniam ipsi saturabuntur », Matthaei quinto; non saturitate *avaritiae,* de qua Ecclesiastae quinto: « Saturitas divitis non sinit eum dormire », quia sic saturatur, ut semper famescat. — Et quoniam divina abundantia excedit nostram indigentiam, ideo subditur: *Et sublatum est quod superfuit illis, fragmentorum cophini duodecim;* in quo ostenditur magnitudo superabundantiae ex continentia vasorum et numero, quia duodenarius est primus *numerus abundans,* secundum quod tradit arithmetica[^p224-3]. Et sic impletum est illud, cuius figura praecessit quarti Regum quarto. Dixit Eliseus de paucis panibus: « Haec dicit Dominus: Comedent, et supererit. Posuit itaque coram eis, qui comederunt, et superfuit iuxta verbum Domini ». Sed ibi non determinatur mensura tantae abundantiae, sicut hic; et hoc propter maiorem *perfectionem* in Evangelio et abundantiorem, secundum illud Matthaei quinto[^p224-4]: « Nisi abundaverit iustitia vestra plus quam scribarum et Pharisaeorum, non intrabitis in regnum caelorum ». Vel propter *mysterium.* Isti enim duodecim cophini significant duodecim Apostolos. *Cophini* sunt vasa humilia, et hi repleti sunt fragmentis quinque panum hordeaceorum, id est doctrina quinque librorum Moysi, de quibus primae ad Corinthios decimo quarto[^p224-5]: « Malo quinque verba in Ecclesia loqui sensu meo » etc.

28. Ex quo datur forma praedicantibus, ut non nova de cordibus suis adinveniant, quia Dominus non creavit novos panes ad reficiendam turbam; sed sicut Dominus multiplicavit divina benedictione quinque panes hordeaceos, sic omnis abundantia verae doctrinae sumi debet ex *fundamento sacrae Scripturae,* multiplicanda per *orationem,* qua in caelum respicitur[^p224-6], et *devotione,* qua benedicitur, *meditatione,* qua frangitur, et *praedicatione,* qua distribuitur et explicatur. — Doctrina enim catholica primo sumenda est de sacra Scriptura per *lectionem,* cuius verba sunt *panes;* Matthaei quarto[^p224-7]: « Scriptum est: Non in solo pane vivit homo, sed in omni verbo, quod procedit de ore Dei ». — Sed lectioni debet adiungi *oratio;* Matthaei septimo: « Quaerite, et invenietis; petite et accipietis; pulsate, et aperietur vobis »: *quaerimus* legendo, *petimus* orando, *pulsamus* manu operando. — Orationi debet iungi *devotio* et *gratiarum actio;* unde ad Colossenses quarto[^p224-8]: « Orationi instate, vigilantes in ea in gratiarum actione ». Unde Matthaei vigesimo sexto: « Elevatis oculis in caelum, gratias agens benedixit ». — Devotioni iungenda *meditatio* et *veritatis explicatio,* quae non est aliud quam *fractio* panis; Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo[^p224-9]: « Frange esurienti panem tuum et egenos vagosque induc in domum tuam ». — Et tunc *distribuitur,* quando pro capacitate audientium ministratur; Psalmus[^p224-10]: « Distribuite domos eius, ut enarretis ». Et ex hoc audientes saturantur; Ecclesiastici decimo quinto: « Cibabit illum pane vitae et intellectus et aqua sapientiae salutaris potabit illum ». — Et sic patet, quod Dominus praedicatores informavit non solum exemplo proprio et familiari, sed etiam mystico et figurali. Plus enim fecit Dominus hoc miraculum ad informationem mentium quam ad refectionem corporum, quoniam, sicut dicitur Sapientiae undecimo[^p224-11], ipse est amator praecipuus animarum.

## English
<!-- page 220 -->
15. *And the Apostles being returned announced*[^p220-11]. After the formation of the Apostles made through word or
<!-- page 221 -->
precept there follows here their formation *through deed* or example. Now this part has two. For in the first he forms them through a *familiar* example; but in the second through a *figural* one, at[^p221-1]: *Now the day began to decline.* The first is attended to in the *spiritual erudition* of the multitudes; the second, in their *bodily refection,* which bears the form and figure of the spiritual refection.

Now the *erudition*[^p221-2] of the multitudes by the Lord, in which a form is given to preachers in teaching the people of the faithful, is set out as to four things which are required in preaching. The first is the *faithfulness of the fellowship;* the second is the *opportunity of the place;* the third, the *docility of the people;* and the fourth, the *affability of the Master;* so that thus nothing may be wanting to the word of God, neither on the part of those assisting nor on the part of those opposing nor on the part of those hearing[^p221-3] nor on the part of those teaching, but that the word of the Lord may have its due effect.

16. (Vers. 10.). First, then, as to the *faithfulness of the fellowship* it is said: *And the Apostles, when they were returned, announced to him whatsoever they had done;* in which their faithfulness appears, because they *quickly returned* to the divine fellowship. Hence they were not like the raven, of which Genesis eight[^p221-4]: « Which went forth and did not return »; but like the dove, of which it is said that « when she had not found where her foot might rest, she returned to Noe into the ark », as a faithful friend. So also the Apostles, endowed with dovelike simplicity, quickly returned to Christ as faithful messengers. — Their faithfulness appears also because they *hid nothing from God,* but *told him whatsoever they had done;* not that they might teach the Lord, from whom nothing is hidden, but that they might show that they themselves would not be hidden, even if they could. Against which, Isaias twenty-nine[^p221-5]: « Woe! you that are deep of heart, to hide counsel from the Lord » etc. Or they *told* it for this reason, that they might not seek glory for themselves from good deeds, but refer all things to God, according to that of First Corinthians ten[^p221-6]: « Do all things to the glory of God »; against those who say that of Deuteronomy thirty-two: « Our mighty hand, and not the Lord, hath done all these things ».

17. But secondly, as to the *opportunity of the place* he subjoins: *And taking them,* as faithful ones, into fellowship, *he went aside into a desert place, apart, which is of Bethsaida,* as to a quiet and tranquil place; hence Mark six[^p221-7]: « Come apart into a desert place, and rest a little ». And this place is apt for erudition, according to that of Osee two: « I will lead her into the wilderness, and I will speak to her heart ». Now this desert place was beside the lake of Genesareth, which appears from this, that he says: *of Bethsaida.* For Bethsaida is « the city of Andrew and Peter », as is said in John one[^p221-8]. And note that he does not say *Bethsaida,* as though beside that city or in the city; but *Bethsaidae,* because the place was remote, yet in the borders of the city of Bethsaida. Hence in Mark six[^p221-9] it is said that after the multiplication of the loaves he bade them go before him « over the water to Bethsaida ». Hence there is no contrariety, but the one has explained what the other passed over in silence.

18. (Vers. 11.). But thirdly, as to the *docility of the people* he adds: *Which when the people knew, they followed him,* that is, that they might receive his teaching, whom they desired, according to that of Osee six[^p221-10]: « We shall know, and we shall follow on, that we may know the Lord ». Hence Bede in the Gloss: « *They followed* not on beasts, not in carriages, but by the proper labour of their feet, that they might show the ardour of their mind ». Hence in Mark six[^p221-11] it is said that many « came thither on foot from the cities ». And the reason for this is given in John six, where it is said: « After these things Jesus went over the sea of Tiberias, and a great multitude followed him, because they saw the miracles which he did » etc.

19. But fourthly, as to the *affability of the Master* he subjoins: *And he received them*[^p221-12]*;* in which the kindness of Christ is shown *in sign,* to which the Apostle exhorts in Romans fifteen: « Receive one another, as Christ also hath received you unto the honour of God ». Nor only *in sign,* but also *in word;* hence he adds: *And he spoke to them of the kingdom of God,* as it were in affable and familiar speech, that that of the Psalm[^p221-13] might be truly shown fulfilled: « I will declare thy name to my brethren ». But although he spoke affably, yet not unprofitably, not carnally, because of the kingdom of God, not of the world; hence First Peter four[^p221-14]: « If any man speak, as the words of God »; and John
<!-- page 222 -->
three: « He that is of the earth, of the earth he speaketh », but Christ from heaven; and John six: « Thou hast the words of eternal life ». — Nor did he show himself affable only *in word* and *in sign,* but also *in deed.* Hence he also subjoins: *And he healed them who had need of healing;* in which is shown the perfection of love, of which First John three[^p222-1]: « Let us not love in word, nor in tongue, but in deed, and in truth »; because « he that shall see his brother in need, and shall shut up his bowels from him, how doth the charity of God abide in him »? Such charity Christ had; hence Acts ten[^p222-2]: « He went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed by the devil », not only speaking well. — Hence in this the preacher or prelate is schooled to show himself kind to the sheep, that is, by sign, word, deed. In token of which thing it was also said thrice to Peter: *Feed,* John the last[^p222-3].

20. *Now the day began to decline* etc. After the formation of the disciples through a familiar example there is subjoined their formation through a *figural deed,* and this in the refection of the multitudes. About the expression of which two things are brought in by the Evangelist. The first is the *opportunity* on the part of the need of the multitude; the second is the *liberality* on the part of the mercy of the Saviour, and this at[^p222-4]: *And he said to his disciples: Make them sit down* etc.

Now the *opportunity on the part of the need of the multitude* is expressed as to four things, namely as to the *lateness of the day,* as to the *aridity of the place,* as to the *paucity of the food* and as to the *numerousness of the people.* And thus this opportunity is made up from the circumstance of time and place, of the quantity of food and of persons[^p222-5].

21. (Vers. 12.). First, then, as to the *lateness of the day* it is said: *Now the day began to decline;* and so it was the time for taking refection, as is said below in the last chapter[^p222-6]: « Because it is towards evening, and the day is now far spent ». In which there appears also the wonderful devotion of the multitudes, that for the hearing of the divine word they had forgotten bodily food. The like is read in Acts twenty, that « Paul continued his speech until midnight ».

But secondly, as to the *aridity of the place* it is subjoined: *And the twelve came and said to him: Send away the multitude;* and the aridity of the place moved them to urge this. — Hence he also subjoins: *That going*[^p222-7] *into the towns and villages round about, they may lodge and get victuals,* that is, lest they perish with hunger. And this is sound counsel, which the children of Israel took; Genesis forty-two: « The sons of Jacob went down into Egypt to buy victuals »; as did the Apostles also, John four: « But the disciples were gone into the city to buy meats ». — So too these now counselled on account of the penury of the place; hence they also subjoin: *For we are here in a desert place,* where there is want of food, according to that of Exodus sixteen[^p222-8]: « Why hast thou brought us into the desert, that thou mightest destroy all this multitude with famine »? And because it is difficult to find refection in the desert, therefore they said that of the Psalm: « Can God furnish a table in the desert »? This the unbelieving said, disparaging the divine power; but the Apostles said it in counselling for human need, because they would not tempt the Lord.

22. (Vers. 13.). But thirdly, as to the *scantiness of the food* it is subjoined: *But he said to them: Give you them to eat; you,* as their prelates, according to that of First Peter five[^p222-9]: « Ancients, feed the flock which is among you ». And he says this that from their answer the scantiness of the food might be known. — Because of which it is added: *And they said: We have no more than five loaves and two fishes.* From which there appears the want of the Apostles, not only for refreshing the multitude, but even for allaying their own hunger; and that the want may be shown to be great, John expresses the kind of loaves. For it is said in John six[^p222-10]: « There is one boy here that hath five barley loaves and two fishes; but what are these among so many »? Like in this were they to that widow whose meal Elias multiplied; Third Kings seventeen: « The Lord God liveth, I have no bread, but as much meal as a handful can hold »; and in Fourth Kings four the servant said to Eliseus: « How much is this, that I should set it before a hundred men »? Likewise the Apostles answered here, or Andrew in the person of all, as is said in John six[^p222-11]; or he first and the others after; hence there is no contrariety. In whatever way these words were said, they show the impotence of the disciples to refresh the multitude. — Yet lest they should be believed to say this not from impotence but from sloth, therefore they offer their service for the buying of food, when they add: *Unless perhaps we*[^p222-12] *should go and buy food for all this*
<!-- page 223 -->
*multitude.* And this indeed can be the word of *those offering service,* according to that of Mark six: « Going, let us buy bread for two hundred pence, and we will give them to eat ». Or it can be the word of *those showing impotence* to refresh, not only as to the loaves they had, but also as to the price. Hence it is said in John six[^p223-1]: « Two hundred pennyworth of bread is not sufficient for them, that every one may take a little ». And some of the disciples spoke in one way, some in another; and John expresses the one, Mark the other, but Luke in the aforesaid speech includes both; which is plain from the very varied utterance of the speech.

23. (Vers. 14.). But fourthly, as to the *numerousness of the people* it is subjoined: *For there were about five thousand men,* and accordingly so great a multitude that for one loaf there were a thousand men. Hence he names and numbers the *men;* from which it appears how great was the crowd of the rest, not numbered on account of its multitude; hence in Matthew fourteen[^p223-2] it is said that there were « five thousand, besides children and women ». Now he expresses the number for this reason, that by reason of the *five* it befitted the *Law,* which formed carnal and sensible men; by reason of the *perfection of the thousand* it befitted the receiving of the doctrine of evangelical perfection[^p223-3].

24. (Vers. 14. 15.). *And he said to his disciples* etc. After the opportunity on the part of the need of the multitude he here subjoins the *liberality on the part of the munificence of the Saviour.* Now the liberality of the Saviour is manifested in four things: in the *ordering of the banqueters,* the *multitude of the victuals,* the *courtesy of those ministering,* the *fulness of those eating;* so that thus a perfect liberality may be shown through the order of those sitting down, the multitude of the victuals, the promptness of those ministering and the plenitude of those eating.

First, as to the *ordering of those sitting down* it is said: *And he said to his disciples: Make them sit down by fifties in a company. And they did so.* For it becomes a magnificent and liberal lord to attend to order in disposing, according to what is said in Third Kings ten[^p223-4], that « the queen of Saba seeing the wisdom of Solomon, and the meat of his table, and the apartments of his servants, and the order of his ministers, had no longer any spirit ». Now this was a disposition of order with a determination of number; hence in Mark six[^p223-5] it is said that « they sat down in ranks by hundreds and by fifties ». For fifty times a hundred are five thousand. But the fiftieth number is the rather expressed, because on account of the quinary it rather befitted the Law and also the quinary of the loaves of the refections. And from this Christ showed his wonderful wisdom, by which he foreknew the number of the men and made it known to the disciples by the sitting. Nor is it a wonder, because he was the one of whom it is said in Wisdom eleven[^p223-6]: « Thou hast ordered all things in measure, and number, and weight ».

25. (Vers. 16.). But secondly, as to the *multitude of the victuals* it is said: *And taking the five loaves and the two fishes, he looked up to heaven, and blessed them*[^p223-7]*;* in which blessing there was the multiplication of the loaves, according to that of Genesis one: « God blessed them and said: Increase and multiply, and fill the earth »; and in Genesis thirty Laban said to Jacob: « I know that the Lord hath blessed me for thy sake ». Now in blessing he *looked up to heaven* for this reason, that he might show that such a multiplication was not by an earthly power or one handed down to the elements, but by a heavenly power, reserved in the prior causes, of which it is said in the Psalm[^p223-8]: « All things whatsoever he would, the Lord hath done, in heaven and in earth, in the sea and in all the deeps ». — And note that it is said: *He looked up and blessed: looked up* indeed from human humility, and *blessed* from divine power; the Psalm[^p223-9]: « I have lifted up my eyes to the mountains ».

26. But thirdly, as to the *courtesy of those ministering* it is subjoined: *And he broke, and distributed to his disciples, to set*[^p223-10] *before the multitude.* For they were noble ministers of Christ and dispensers, and prompt to serve, according to that of First Corinthians four: « Let a man so account of us as of the ministers of Christ, and the dispensers of the mysteries of God ». For it becomes a noble lord to have noble ministers, according to that of Esther one[^p223-11]: « So the king had appointed,
<!-- page 224 -->
setting over every table one of his princes, that every one might take what he would ». But in the court of Jesus Christ the nobler minister and serve, as is said in Luke twenty-two: « He that is the greater among you, let him become as the lesser; and he that is the leader, as the minister »; after: « But I am in the midst of you, as he that ministereth ».

27. (Vers. 17.). But fourthly, as to the *fulness of those eating* it is subjoined: *And they did all eat, and were filled,* so that that of the Psalm[^p224-1] was verified: « The poor shall eat and shall be filled, and they shall praise the Lord that seek him »; and that: « So they did eat, and were filled exceedingly »; *filled,* I say, with the fulness *of justice,* of which Proverbs thirteen[^p224-2]: « The just eateth and filleth his soul »; « for blessed are they that hunger and thirst after justice, for they shall be filled », Matthew five; not with the fulness *of avarice,* of which Ecclesiastes five: « The fulness of the rich will not suffer him to sleep », because he is so filled that he is ever hungry. — And since the divine abundance exceeds our need, therefore it is subjoined: *And there were taken up of fragments that remained to them, twelve baskets;* in which is shown the greatness of the superabundance from the content of the vessels and from their number, because twelve is the first *abundant number,* according as arithmetic teaches[^p224-3]. And so that was fulfilled whose figure went before in Fourth Kings four. Eliseus said of the few loaves: « Thus saith the Lord: They shall eat, and there shall be left. So he set it before them, and they ate, and there was left, according to the word of the Lord ». But there the measure of so great an abundance is not determined, as here; and this on account of the greater and more abundant *perfection* in the Gospel, according to that of Matthew five[^p224-4]: « Unless your justice abound more than that of the scribes and Pharisees, you shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven ». Or on account of the *mystery.* For these twelve baskets signify the twelve Apostles. *Baskets* are lowly vessels, and these were filled with the fragments of the five barley loaves, that is, with the doctrine of the five books of Moses, of which First Corinthians fourteen[^p224-5]: « I had rather speak five words in the church with my understanding » etc.

28. From which a form is given to those preaching, that they devise nothing new out of their own hearts, because the Lord did not create new loaves to refresh the multitude; but as the Lord multiplied the five barley loaves by the divine blessing, so all the abundance of true doctrine ought to be taken from the *foundation of sacred Scripture,* to be multiplied through *prayer,* by which one looks up to heaven[^p224-6], and through *devotion,* by which it is blessed, *meditation,* by which it is broken, and *preaching,* by which it is distributed and unfolded. — For catholic doctrine is first to be taken from sacred Scripture through *reading,* whose words are *loaves;* Matthew four[^p224-7]: « It is written: Not in bread alone doth man live, but in every word that proceedeth from the mouth of God ». — But to reading *prayer* ought to be joined; Matthew seven: « Seek, and you shall find; ask, and you shall receive; knock, and it shall be opened to you »: we *seek* by reading, we *ask* by praying, we *knock* by working with the hand. — To prayer ought to be joined *devotion* and *thanksgiving;* hence Colossians four[^p224-8]: « Be instant in prayer, watching in it with thanksgiving ». Hence Matthew twenty-six: « Lifting up his eyes to heaven, giving thanks, he blessed ». — To devotion are to be joined *meditation* and the *unfolding of the truth,* which is nothing else than the *breaking* of the bread; Isaias fifty-eight[^p224-9]: « Break thy bread to the hungry, and bring the needy and the harbourless into thy house ». — And then it is *distributed,* when it is ministered according to the capacity of the hearers; the Psalm[^p224-10]: « Distribute her houses, that ye may relate it ». And from this the hearers are filled; Ecclesiasticus fifteen: « She shall feed him with the bread of life and understanding, and shall give him the water of wholesome wisdom to drink ». — And so it is plain that the Lord formed preachers not only by his own and familiar example, but also by a mystical and figural one. For the Lord did this miracle more for the formation of minds than for the refection of bodies, since, as is said in Wisdom eleven[^p224-11], he is the special lover of souls.
