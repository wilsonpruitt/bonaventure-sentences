# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. IX, Vers. 18-27
## *Via proficiendi dupliciter explicatur*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 225 -->

#### Via proficiendi dupliciter explicatur.

29. *Et factum est, cum solus esset* etc. Postquam Evangelista expressit formam praedicandi, in parte ista exprimit *viam proficiendi*[^p225-1]. Ad profectum autem ista duo consideranda concurrunt, scilicet *asperitas viae,* in qua consistit meritum, et *suavitas patriae,* in qua consistit praemium. Ideo post expressionem *asperitatis viae* exprimit secundo Evangelista *suavitatem gloriae,* ibi[^p225-2]: *Factum est autem post haec verba* etc.

*Via* autem ad patriam potissime in duobus consistit, scilicet in *perfecta cognitione* Christi per fidem et *perfecta imitatione* per crucem. Ideo primo insinuatur nobis, qualiter Christus per *fidem est cognoscendus;* secundo vero, qualiter per *crucem est imitandus,* ibi[^p225-3]: *Dicebat autem ad omnes: Si quis* etc.

Quantum ad primum notandum, quod *perfecta notitia fidei* in quatuor consistit, scilicet in *approbatione divini honoris, reprobatione humani erroris, confessione Christi mediatoris, consideratione mysterii redemptionis.* Ad perfectam enim fidem concurrit approbatio religionis, detestatio superstitionis, confessio patens et contemplatio latens.

30. (Vers. 18.). Primo igitur quantum ad *approbationem divini honoris* dicitur: *Et factum est, cum solus esset orans,* ut Deo Patri debitum cultum et reverentiam exhiberet et ostenderet exhibendam; quae potissime exhibetur in oratione, iuxta illud Ioannis quarto[^p225-4]: « Venit hora, in qua veri adoratores adorabunt Patrem in spiritu et veritate. Nam et Pater tales quaerit »; unde in Psalmo: « Invoca me in die tribulationis, et eruam te, et honorificabis me ». — Quoniam autem haec reverentia debet carere *omni ostentatione,* ideo dicitur, quod *solus erat orans,* secundum illud Matthaei sexto[^p225-5]: « Tu autem, cum oraveris, intra in cubiculum tuum » etc. Carere etiam debet *omni inquietatione;* ideo etiam *solus* orabat, secundum illud Threnorum tertio[^p225-6]: « Sedebit solitarius et tacebit, quia levavit se super se », ne scilicet aliquo strepitu retrahatur. Et ideo Matthaei decimo quarto dicitur, quod « dimissa turba, ascendit in montem solus orare ». — Sed quoniam huius reverentiae non erant discipuli perturbatores ex inquietatione, sed approbatores per veram fidem; ideo subditur: *Erant cum illo et discipuli*[^p225-7]. Unde per hoc, quod dicitur *solus,* non excludit consortium discipulorum, sed tumultum turbarum. Nam *discipulos* tanquam filios admittebat; unde et dicebat de eis illud quod inducit Apostolus ad Hebraeos secundo[^p225-8]: « Ecce, ego et pueri mei, quos dedit mihi Deus »; *turbas* autem tanquam servos excludebat; cuius figuram Abraham gessit, Genesis vigesimo secundo, quando « dixit ad pueros suos: Exspectate hic in valle cum asino; ego autem et puer illuc usque properantes, postquam adoraverimus, revertemur ad vos ». — Vel hoc dicitur, quia non ratione eiusdem temporis sunt coniuncti, quia solus primo oravit, et postea immediate cum eo discipuli fuerunt. Tamen simul iunguntur, quia, etsi Apostoli erant absentes locali circumscriptione, tamen cum eo erant mentali affectu et fidei approbatione, secundum illud Pauli ad Colossenses secundo[^p225-9]: « Etsi corpore absens sum, in spiritu vobiscum sum, gaudens et videns ordinem vestrum » etc.

31. Secundo quantum ad *reprobationem humani erroris* subditur: *Et interrogavit illos dicens: Quem me esse dicunt turbae?* Quod non ideo quaerebat, ut veritatem addisceret, quam sciebat, secundum illud Ioannis secundo[^p225-10]: « Non erat opus, ut quis testimonium ei perhiberet de homine. Ipse enim sciebat, quid esset in homine »; sed ideo interrogat, ut erroneam mundanorum sententiam ostendat verae fidei inimicam. Ut plurimum enim plures contradicunt sapientibus, et turba turbatum habet iudicium; propter quod Exodi vigesimo tertio[^p225-11]: « Non sequeris turbam ad faciendum malum. Nec in iudicio plurimorum acquiesces sententiae, ut a vero devies ». Et ideo non est mirum, si animales homines de Christo Deo non recte sentiebant; quia, primae ad Corinthios secundo, « animalis homo non percipit ea quae Dei sunt ».

32. (Vers. 19.). Propter quod subiungitur triplex opinio sumta a triplici excellentia. Quantum
<!-- page 226 -->
ad primam dicit: *At illi responderunt et dixerunt: Ioannem Baptistam;* et hoc, quia videbant in eo *spiritum sanctificationis,* qui fuit in Ioanne, secundum illud supra primo[^p226-1]: « Erit magnus coram Domino et Spiritu sancto replebitur » etc. — Sed nec in hoc omnes consentiebant, ideo addit: *Alii autem: Eliam,* scilicet propter *zelum aemulationis,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici quadragesimo octavo[^p226-2]: « Surrexit Elias propheta quasi ignis, et verbum eius quasi facula ardebat ». Et illud videbatur probabile, quia Elias propheta excellens erat non solum in vita, sed etiam in potentia, et quia etiam adventus eius promissus erat, secundum illud Malachiae ultimo: « Ecce, ego mittam vobis Eliam prophetam, antequam veniat dies Domini magnus et horribilis ». — Nec tamen in hoc omnes concordabant, et ideo addit: *Alii*[^p226-3]*, quia Propheta unus de prioribus surrexit;* et hoc propter *sensum discretionis,* quem in eo attendebant; supra septimo: « Propheta magnus surrexit in nobis »; et Ioannis sexto: « Hic est qui venturus est vere Propheta ». Sed quia purum hominem credebant, ideo *unum de prioribus;* propter quod errabant, quia non erat unus inter priores, sed unus, propter quem praecesserant omnes priores; de quo Deuteronomii decimo octavo[^p226-4]: « Prophetam suscitabit Dominus de gente vestra; ipsum sicut me audietis ». — Ex tanta igitur varietate apparet, quod turbae alienae erant a veritate et fidei uniformitate; propter quod dicitur ad Ephesios quarto[^p226-5]: « Non simus sicut parvuli fluctuantes et circumferamur omni vento doctrinae, ad circumventionem erroris ».

33. (Vers. 20.). Tertio vero quantum ad *confessionem Christi mediatoris* subditur: *Dixit autem illis: Vos autem quem me esse dicitis? Vos,* scilicet *ab aliis selecti,* secundum illud Ioannis sexto[^p226-6]: « Ego duodecim vos elegi »; et decimo quinto: « Non vos me elegistis, sed ego elegi vos ». Vel *vos amici,* secundum illud Ioannis decimo quinto: « Vos autem dixi amicos ». Vel *vos speciales discipuli,* secundum illud supra octavo[^p226-7]: « Vobis datum est nosse mysterium regni Dei ». *Vos* autem, qui in me creditis, *quem me esse dicitis?* id est, confitemini, quia « corde creditur ad iustitiam, ore autem confessio fit ad salutem », ad Romanos decimo. — Ideo subditur: *Respondens Simon Petrus dixit: Christum Dei;* quod unus pro omnibus respondet, ut commendetur unitas Ecclesiae in fide, confessione et praelatione; unde Petrus tanquam unus praelatus universalis Ecclesiae unus pro omnibus respondet. Propter quod dicebat Paulus primae ad Corinthios primo[^p226-8]: « Obsecro, ut id ipsum dicatis omnes, et non sint in vobis schismata ». — Unde merito istius confessionis unius pro omnibus nomen *Petri* est confirmatum, et universalis praelatio super Ecclesiam est concessa, secundum quod dicitur Matthaei decimo sexto: « Et ego dico tibi ». Unde apparet, quod non ex impetu, sed sancto Spiritu incitatus, prior respondit Petrus. Et hoc est quod dicitur in Glossa[^p226-9]: « Unus pro omnibus et prae omnibus, quia senior et princeps Apostolorum ».

34. Confitendo autem ipsum *Christum Dei,* confitetur simul Deum verum et hominem. Unde Ambrosius in Glossa[^p226-10]: « Complexus est omnia qui et *naturam* et *nomen* expressit. In nomine enim *Christi* et Divinitatis et incarnationis est expressio et fides passionis ». *Christus* enim exprimit *personam unam in duabus naturis* et *dignitatem regiam* et *sacerdotalem* et *gratiae plenitudinem* propter *unctionis* praerogativam, de quo in Psalmo[^p226-11]: « Unxit te Deus, Deus tuus oleo laetitiae ». Unde in nomine *Christi* intelligitur *unctus, ungens* et *quo ungitur;* ac per hoc simul Pater, Verbum et Spiritus, secundum illud Isaiae sexagesimo primo: « Spiritus Domini super me, eo quod unxerit me ». — Intelligitur etiam uncti *natura, gratia,* qua ungitur, et *dignitas,* ad quam ungitur,
<!-- page 227 -->
sive officium; Danielis nono[^p227-1]: « Ungetur Sanctus Sanctorum, ut impleatur visio et prophetia ». — Intelligitur etiam *dignitas regalis et prophetalis et sacerdotalis,* secundum triplicem excellentiam suam, scilicet potestatis et veritatis et sanctitatis. In cuius rei figuram in Lege veteri hae tres personae consueverunt inungi[^p227-2]. — Et in hoc apparet, quomodo tres opiniones falsae, sicut aliquid habebant rationis moventis, ita defectus obnubilantis. Unde in hoc apparet, quod Christus in hoc verbo confutavit errores et stravit totius fidei fundamentum, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios tertio[^p227-3]: « Fundamentum aliud nemo potest ponere ». Hinc est, quod *Petrus* est vocatus, et ei concessum est, ut de Ecclesia eius nunquam deficiat vera fides; infra vigesimo secundo: « Ego pro te rogavi, Petre, ut non deficiat » etc.

35. (Vers. 21. 22.). Quarto vero quantum ad *cognitionem mysterii redemptionis* subditur: *At ille increpans eos*[^p227-4]*, praecepit, ne cui dicerent hoc;* et hoc, quia non erat consummatum redemptionis mysterium, quod iusto Dei iudicio erat mundi principibus occultandum. Unde primae ad Corinthios secundo[^p227-5]: « Loquimur Dei sapientiam in mysterio, quae abscondita est, quam nemo principum huius saeculi cognovit; si enim cognovissent, nunquam Dominum gloriae crucifixissent ». — Licet autem non esset omnibus publicandum, erat tamen discipulis praedicendum; propter quod addit: *Dicens, quia oportet, Filium hominis multa pati.* Ratio autem huius praedictionis assignatur Ioannis decimo quarto[^p227-6]: « Nunc dixi vobis, priusquam fiat, ut, cum factum fuerit, credatis ». Nam ipsi discipuli nondum hoc capiebant, secundum quod dicitur infra decimo octavo[^p227-7]: « Assumsit autem Iesus duodecim et ait illis: Ecce, ascendimus »; et post: « Et ipsi nihil horum intellexerunt, sed hoc acceperunt post consummationem ». Unde dicitur Lucae ultimo: « Haec sunt verba, quae locutus sum vobis, cum adhuc essem vobiscum, quoniam necesse est impleri omnia, quae scripta sunt de me »; et post subditur: « Aperuit illis sensum, ut intelligerent Scripturas ».

36. Dicit autem: *Oportet,* id est, *necessarium* est, non simpliciter, sed ex praesuppositione divinae dispositionis et promissionis, secundum illud Actuum tertio[^p227-8]: « Deus autem quae praenuntiavit per os omnium Prophetarum, pati Christum suum, implevit sic ». Vel *oportet,* id est, *opportunum* est et expediens, iuxta quod prophetavit Caiphas; Ioannis undecimo[^p227-9]: « Expedit, ut unus homo moriatur pro populo, et non tota gens pereat ». *Oportet,* inquam, *multa pati,* quia *pro multis* animabus redimendis, multis culpis remittendis, multis reatibus absolvendis; Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p227-10]: « Ipse vulneratus est propter iniquitates nostras, attritus propter scelera nostra ». « Omnes nos quasi oves erravimus; unusquisque in viam suam declinavit, et Dominus posuit in eo iniquitates omnium nostrum ». — Passus est etiam *multa* et *magna,* passus est etiam *a multis* et *magnis;* propter quod addit: *Et reprobari a senioribus et principibus sacerdotum et scribis. Seniores* praecellebant antiquitate, *principes* auctoritate, sed *scribae* ratione doctrinae, ut sic impleretur illud Isaiae primo[^p227-11]: « Filios enutrivi et exaltavi, ipsi autem spreverunt me ». — Et quia passio Christi terminanda in morte erat, ideo additur: *Et occidi,* scilicet oportet, secundum illud Danielis nono[^p227-12]: « Occidetur Christus ». Sed ne credatur, quod *invitus,* ideo Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio: « Tradidit in mortem animam suam ». — Sed quia passio haec vivificativa erat mortuorum, ideo addit: *Et tertia die resurgere,* secundum illud Osee sexto[^p227-13]: « Vivificabit nos post duos dies et in die tertia suscitabit nos ». Huius figuram dicit Salvator praecessisse in Iona Matthaei duodecimo: « Sicut fuit Ionas » etc. — Ex his igitur apparet, quae est perfecta fidei notitia, in qua Christi fundatur Ecclesia, scilicet per quam approbatur divinus honor, reprobatur humanus error, asseritur mediator et agnoscitur reparator.

37. (Vers. 23.). *Dicebat autem ad omnes* etc. Ostenso, qualiter Christus secundum fidem est cognoscendus, hic secundo ostenditur, qualiter *per crucem est imitandus,* quia[^p227-14] « fides mortua est sine
<!-- page 228 -->
operibus ». Quoniam autem difficile est ferre crucem, ideo ad hoc invitat quadruplici consideratione, scilicet ex consideratione *sui exempli, proprii periculi, futuri iudicii* et *regni divini;* ut, si non movent monita, moveant exempla, vel saltem pericula, vel futura iudicia, vel saltem promissa[^p228-1].

Primo igitur invitat *consideratione sui exempli,* cum dicit: *Dicebat autem ad omnes: Si quis vult post me venire,* id est me imitari, ad quod non cogo, sed invito; ideo dicit: *Si quis vult;* Ecclesiastici decimo quinto[^p228-2]: « Si volueris mandata servare, conservabunt te ». « Apposuit tibi aquam et ignem; ad quod volueris, porrige manum tuam ». Unde Chrysostomus[^p228-3]: « Si quis aurum daret, aut thesaurum apponeret, non vocaret cum violentia, sed currerent homines; multo magis ad ea quae sunt in caelis. Si enim natura rerum persuadet currere; si non curris, non es dignus accipere ». Ideo dicit: *Si quis vult* exemplum meum sequi. — *Abneget semetipsum,* per *omnimodam humilitatem,* captivando sensum, affectum et « intellectum in Christi obsequium[^p228-4] »; quod notat in hoc quod dicit: *Semetipsum.* Et hoc est consilium Ecclesiastici, septimo: « Humilia valde spiritum tuum »; et hoc quidem necessarium est volenti sequi humilem Christum. Unde Gregorius in Glossa[^p228-5]: « Nisi quis a se ipso deficiat, ad eum qui supra se est, non appropinquat ». — Sed hoc non sufficit; ideo addit: *Et tollat crucem suam quotidie,* per *continuam austeritatem,* ut possit dicere illud Pauli ad Galatas secundo[^p228-6]: « Christo confixus sum cruci »; et secundae ad Corinthios quarto: « Semper mortificationem Iesu Christi in corpore nostro circumferentes ». Et ideo dicitur ad Galatas quinto: « Qui autem sunt Christi carnem suam crucifixerunt cum vitiis et concupiscentiis ».

38. Nota, quod dicit: *Quotidie,* quia quotidie poenitentia crucis debet esse nova et recens, ut dicat semper[^p228-7]: « Dixi: Nunc coepi », sicut beatus Franciscus, qui in morte dicebat, quod tunc incipiebat bene facere: « Fratres, incipiamus et proficiamus, quia nunc usque parum profecimus ». Crux namque Christi renovativam habet naturam; unde secundae ad Corinthios quarto: « Licet is qui foris est noster homo corrumpatur, tamen is qui intus est, renovatur de die in diem ». — Ad perfectam autem Christi imitationem non sufficit humilitas et austeritas, sed etiam necessaria est *paupertas.* Ideo addit: *Et sequatur me,* scilicet per *altissimam paupertatem,* qua nihil omnino volebat portare in via, ut recte et expedite perveniret ad patriam. Ideo dicebat Petrus Matthaei decimo nono[^p228-8]: « Ecce, nos reliquimus omnia et secuti sumus te »; super quo Bernardus: « Bene, Petre, et non ad insipientiam tibi; quia currentem sequi non poteras oneratus ». Et hoc est valde honorabile, licet videatur vile, quia, Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio[^p228-9], « magna gloria est sequi Dominum ».

Ex hoc autem verbo elicitur triplex consilium et votum religiosorum, scilicet *obedientiae* in abnegatione, *castitatis* in cruce et *paupertatis* in subsecutione; sed « non omnes capiunt verbum illud, sed quibus datum est a Patre meo », sicut dicitur Matthaei decimo nono[^p228-10]; ideo praemittit: *Si quis vult* etc. Ut nemo se excuset, quasi non habeat gratiam, dicit: *Si quis vult* etc., quasi dicat, quod gratia Dei nobis praesto est, si non desit voluntas; ad Hebraeos duodecimo[^p228-11]: « Contemplantes, ne quis desit gratiae Dei ».

39. (Vers. 24.). Secundo invitat consideratione *proprii periculi,* cum ait: *Qui enim voluerit animam suam salvam facere perdet eam*[^p228-12], id est, qui voluerit salvare in praesenti vitam perdet vitam in futuro, scilicet pro vita momentanea perdet aeternam; unde Ioannis duodecimo: « Qui amat animam suam perdet eam »: quia qui nimis amat inique amat, et « qui diligit iniquitatem odit animam suam », sicut dicitur in Psalmo. Qui enim diligit animam suam supra Deum perdit Deum, et perdendo Deum perdit per consequens se ipsum. Et ideo dicebat Paulus Actuum vigesimo[^p228-13]: « Non facio animam meam pretiosiorem quam me ipsum ». — Et quia ista videtur esse dura sententia, ideo probat a contrario sensu: *Nam qui perdiderit animam suam propter me,* in hac scilicet vita, *salvam faciet illam,* scilicet in futura vita, secundum illud secundi Machabaeorum septimo[^p228-14]: « Tu quidem in praesenti vita nos perdis, sed Rex mundi defunctos nos pro suis
<!-- page 229 -->
legibus in aeternae vitae resurrectione suscitabit ». Et ideo dicebat Paulus Actuum vigesimo primo: « Ego non solum alligari, sed et mori in Ierusalem paratus sum propter nomen Domini Iesu ». Sic ergo *perdere animam* pro Christo est *salvare; velle animam salvare* est eam *perditioni exponere,* iuxta illud ad Romanos octavo[^p229-1]: « Si secundum carnem vixeritis, moriemini »; et hoc est maximum periculum.

40. (Vers. 25.). Et ideo in eius exaggeratione adiungit: *Quid enim proficit homini*[^p229-2]*, si lucretur universum mundum,* et hoc temporaliter, *se ipsum autem perdat et detrimentum sui faciat?* et hoc sempiternaliter; quasi dicat: nihil, quia qui perdit se omnia perdit, ideo non prodest, sed obest. Ideo Iacobi quinto[^p229-3]: « Agite nunc, divites, plorate ululantes in miseriis vestris, quae advenient vobis ». Et ratio huius est, quia, sicut dicit Augustinus in sermone de Innocentibus[^p229-4], « nemo habet iniustum lucrum sine iusto damno, lucrum in arca, damnum in conscientia, tulit vestem et perdidit fidem, acquirit pecuniam et perdidit iustitiam », et sic per consequens animam suam. Unde Isidorus[^p229-5]: « Si haberes sapientiam Salomonis, si pulcritudinem Absalonis, si fortitudinem Samsonis, si longaevitatem Henoch, si divitias et potestatem Octaviani; quid prodest tibi, si tandem caro datur vermibus, anima daemonibus cum divite crucianda sine fine »?

41. (Vers. 26.). Tertio invitat ad crucis portationem consideratione *futuri iudicii,* cum ait: *Nam qui me erubuerit,* scilicet imitari, *et meos sermones,* scilicet confiteri, contemnendo Christi crucem tanquam ignominiosam, et etiam doctrinam eius ex mala erubescentia, de qua dicitur Ecclesiastici quarto[^p229-6]: « Pro anima tua non confundaris dicere verum; est enim confusio adducens peccatum, et est confusio adducens gratiam et gloriam ». In quo dat intelligere, quod non debemus erubescere veritatem Christi, exemplo Apostoli, qui dicebat ad Romanos primo[^p229-7]: « Non erubesco Evangelium; virtus enim Dei est » etc. Sed erubescere debemus vilitatem peccati, iuxta illud ad Romanos sexto: « Quem fructum habuistis, in quibus nunc erubescitis »? Sed multi non erubescunt peccati deformitatem, de quibus Ieremiae octavo[^p229-8]: « Confusi sunt, quia abominationem fecerunt, quinimmo confusione non sunt confusi et erubescere nescierunt »; et iterum: « Frons mulieris meretricis facta est tibi » etc. Talis erubescit humilitatem Christi. — Et de hoc adiungit: *Hunc Filius hominis erubescet,* id est, erubescentem faciet, vel sicut erubescendum abiiciet iusto suo iudicio, quo dicitur primi Regum secundo[^p229-9]: « Qui me honorificaverit, honorificabo eum; qui autem contemnunt me erunt ignobiles », et hoc in die iudicii. — Et ideo subiungit: *Cum venerit in maiestate sua et Patris et sanctorum Angelorum;* in quo tangit iudiciariam potestatem quantum ad *principium causalitatis* in Patre, quantum ad *privilegium excellentiae* in se et quantum ad *ministerium obedientiae* in Angelorum multitudine. Unde Matthaei vigesimo quarto[^p229-10]: « Tunc apparebit signum Filii hominis in caelo, et tunc plangent omnes tribus terrae et videbunt Filium hominis » etc.; et Isaiae tertio: « Dominus ad iudicium veniet cum senioribus populi sui ». In hoc iudicio confundentur superbi, et exaltabuntur humiles, secundum illud Psalmi[^p229-11]: « Omnia cornua peccatorum confringam, et exaltabuntur cornua iusti ».

42. (Vers. 27.). Quarto invitat ad considerationem *divini regni,* quod suis imitatoribus ostendit, cum subdit: *Dico autem vobis vere: Sunt aliqui hic stantes,* per mentis erectionem, secundum illud Psalmi[^p229-12]: « Stantes erant pedes nostri in atriis tuis, Ierusalem ». — Talibus debet ostendi regnum, ut eorum compleatur desiderium; ideo addit: *Qui non gustabunt mortem,* ex animae et corporis separatione; *donec videant regnum Dei,* per revelationem, non per apertam visionem. Nam dictum est Moysi Exodi trigesimo tertio[^p229-13]: « Non videbit me homo, et vivet ». Sed hoc dictum est per aliquam specialem revelationem, qua dictum est Moysi: « Ego ostendam tibi omne bonum ». In quo datur intelligi, quod stantes in sermone Christi non gustabunt mortem, sed transibunt ad vitam, secundum illud Ioannis octavo[^p229-14]: « Si quis sermonem meum servaverit, mortem non gustabit in aeternum ». — Ex quo ergo Christus se per crucem imitantibus promittit tantum praemium, comminatur tam severum iudicium, praetendit tantum periculum, ostendit tantum exemplum; valde
<!-- page 230 -->
durus est qui non movetur ad ipsum imitandum. Propter quod dicitur ad Hebraeos duodecimo[^p230-1]: « Habentes tantam nubem testium impositam, curramus » etc.; et ultimo: « Exeamus ad eum extra castra, improperium eius portantes; non enim habemus hic manentem civitatem, sed futuram inquirimus ».

## English
<!-- page 225 -->

#### The way of making progress is explained in two ways.

29. *And it came to pass, as he was alone* etc. After the Evangelist has expressed the form of preaching, in this part he expresses *the way of making progress*[^p225-1]. Now for progress these two things concur to be considered, namely *the asperity of the way,* in which merit consists, and *the sweetness of the fatherland,* in which the reward consists. Therefore after the expression of *the asperity of the way* the Evangelist expresses secondly *the sweetness of glory,* at[^p225-2]: *And it came to pass after these words* etc.

Now *the way* to the fatherland consists chiefly in two things, namely in *the perfect cognition* of Christ through faith and in *the perfect imitation* of him through the cross. Therefore it is first intimated to us how Christ *is to be known through faith;* but secondly, how he *is to be imitated through the cross,* at[^p225-3]: *And he said to all: If any man* etc.

As to the first it is to be noted that *the perfect knowledge of faith* consists in four things, namely in *the approbation of the divine honour, the reprobation of human error, the confession of Christ the mediator, the consideration of the mystery of redemption.* For there concur to perfect faith the approbation of religion, the detestation of superstition, open confession and hidden contemplation.

30. (Vers. 18.). First, then, as to *the approbation of the divine honour* it is said: *And it came to pass, as he was alone praying,* that he might show to God the Father the worship and reverence due to him, and show that they are to be shown; which is shown chiefly in prayer, according to that of John the fourth[^p225-4]: « The hour cometh, in which the true adorers shall adore the Father in spirit and in truth. For the Father also seeketh such »; whence in the Psalm: « Call upon me in the day of trouble, and I will deliver thee, and thou shalt glorify me ». — But since this reverence ought to be free *of all ostentation,* therefore it is said that *he was alone praying,* according to that of Matthew the sixth[^p225-5]: « But thou, when thou shalt pray, enter into thy chamber » etc. It ought also to be free *of all disquiet;* therefore too he prayed *alone,* according to that of Lamentations the third[^p225-6]: « He shall sit solitary and hold his peace, because he hath lifted himself up above himself », that is, lest he be drawn back by any din. And therefore it is said in Matthew the fourteenth that « having dismissed the multitude, he went up into a mountain alone to pray ». — But since the disciples were not disturbers of this reverence out of disquiet, but approvers of it through true faith; therefore it is added: *The disciples also were with him*[^p225-7]. Hence by this, that *alone* is said, he does not exclude the fellowship of the disciples, but the tumult of the multitudes. For he admitted *the disciples* as sons; whence he also said of them that which the Apostle brings in, to the Hebrews the second[^p225-8]: « Behold, I and my children, whom God hath given me »; but *the multitudes* he excluded as servants; of which Abraham bore the figure, Genesis the twenty-second, when « he said to his young men: Wait here in the valley with the ass; but I and the boy, hastening as far as yonder, after we have worshipped, will return to you ». — Or this is said because they are not joined by reason of the same time, since he first prayed alone, and afterwards immediately the disciples were with him. Yet they are joined together, because, although the Apostles were absent by local circumscription, yet they were with him by the affection of the mind and the approbation of faith, according to that of Paul to the Colossians the second[^p225-9]: « Though I be absent in body, yet in spirit I am with you, rejoicing and beholding your order » etc.

31. Secondly, as to *the reprobation of human error* it is added: *And he asked them, saying: Whom do the multitudes say that I am?* Which he asked, not in order to learn the truth, which he knew, according to that of John the second[^p225-10]: « It was not needful that any should give testimony to him of man. For he himself knew what was in man »; but he asks for this reason, that he may show the erroneous opinion of worldly men to be an enemy of true faith. For for the most part the many contradict the wise, and the multitude has a troubled judgment; on account of which, Exodus the twenty-third[^p225-11]: « Thou shalt not follow the multitude to do evil. Neither shalt thou yield in judgment to the opinion of the most part, to stray from the truth ». And therefore it is not to be wondered at if sensual men did not think rightly of Christ God; because, as in the first to the Corinthians the second, « the sensual man perceiveth not the things that are of God ».

32. (Vers. 19.). On account of which there is subjoined a threefold opinion taken from a threefold excellence. As
<!-- page 226 -->
to the first he says: *But they answered and said: John the Baptist;* and this, because they saw in him *the spirit of sanctification,* which was in John, according to that above in the first[^p226-1]: « He shall be great before the Lord, and shall be filled with the Holy Spirit » etc. — But not all agreed in this, therefore he adds: *But some: Elias,* namely on account of *the zeal of emulation,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus the forty-eighth[^p226-2]: « Elias the prophet stood up as a fire, and his word burnt like a torch ». And that seemed probable, because Elias was an excellent prophet not only in life, but also in power, and because his coming also had been promised, according to that of Malachias the last: « Behold, I will send you Elias the prophet, before the great and dreadful day of the Lord come ». — Nor yet did all accord in this, and therefore he adds: *Others*[^p226-3]*, that one of the former Prophets is risen again;* and this on account of *the sense of discernment* which they marked in him; above in the seventh: « A great Prophet is risen up among us »; and John the sixth: « This is he that is to come, truly the Prophet ». But because they believed him a mere man, therefore *one of the former;* on account of which they erred, because he was not one among the former, but the one on account of whom all the former had gone before; of whom Deuteronomy the eighteenth[^p226-4]: « The Lord will raise up a Prophet of your nation; him you shall hear as me ». — From so great a variety, then, it appears that the multitudes were estranged from the truth and from the uniformity of faith; on account of which it is said to the Ephesians the fourth[^p226-5]: « Let us not be as little ones tossed to and fro, and carried about with every wind of doctrine, unto the circumvention of error ».

33. (Vers. 20.). But thirdly, as to *the confession of Christ the mediator* it is added: *And he said to them: But whom do you say that I am? You,* namely *selected from the others,* according to that of John the sixth[^p226-6]: « I have chosen you twelve »; and the fifteenth: « You have not chosen me, but I have chosen you ». Or *you friends,* according to that of John the fifteenth: « But I have called you friends ». Or *you special disciples,* according to that above in the eighth[^p226-7]: « To you it is given to know the mystery of the kingdom of God ». *But you,* who believe in me, *whom do you say that I am?* that is, confess, because « with the heart one believeth unto justice, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation », to the Romans the tenth. — Therefore it is added: *Simon Peter answering said: The Christ of God;* in that one answers for all, so that the unity of the Church may be commended in faith, confession and prelacy; whence Peter, as the one prelate of the universal Church, answers, one for all. On account of which Paul said in the first to the Corinthians the first[^p226-8]: « I beseech you, that you all speak the same thing, and that there be no schisms among you ». — Hence by the merit of this confession of one for all the name *of Peter* was confirmed, and the universal prelacy over the Church was granted, according to what is said in Matthew the sixteenth: « And I say to thee ». Whence it appears that Peter answered first not from impulse, but moved by the Holy Spirit. And this is what is said in the Gloss[^p226-9]: « One for all and before all, because the elder and the prince of the Apostles ».

34. Now in confessing him *the Christ of God,* he confesses him at once true God and man. Whence Ambrose in the Gloss[^p226-10]: « He embraced all things who expressed both *the nature* and *the name.* For in the name *of Christ* there is the expression both of the Divinity and of the incarnation, and faith in the passion ». For *Christ* expresses *one person in two natures,* and *the royal* and *priestly dignity,* and *the fulness of grace,* on account of the prerogative *of unction,* of which in the Psalm[^p226-11]: « God, thy God, hath anointed thee with the oil of gladness ». Whence in the name *of Christ* is understood *the anointed, the anointing* and *that with which he is anointed;* and by this at once the Father, the Word and the Spirit, according to that of Isaias the sixty-first: « The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me ». — There is understood also the *nature* of the anointed, the *grace* with which he is anointed, and the *dignity* to which he is anointed,
<!-- page 227 -->
or office; Daniel the ninth[^p227-1]: « The Saint of saints shall be anointed, that the vision and prophecy may be fulfilled ». — There is understood also *the royal and prophetic and priestly dignity,* according to his threefold excellence, namely of power and of truth and of sanctity. In figure of which thing, in the old Law these three persons were accustomed to be anointed[^p227-2]. — And in this it appears how the three false opinions, just as they had something of a reason that moved them, so had they a defect that clouded them. Whence in this it appears that Christ in this word confuted errors and laid the foundation of the whole faith, according to that of the first to the Corinthians the third[^p227-3]: « Other foundation no man can lay ». Hence it is that he was called *Peter,* and it was granted to him that true faith should never fail from his Church; below in the twenty-second: « I have prayed for thee, Peter, that it fail not » etc.

35. (Vers. 21. 22.). But fourthly, as to *the cognition of the mystery of redemption* it is added: *But he, rebuking them*[^p227-4]*, enjoined that they should tell this to no man;* and this, because the mystery of redemption had not been consummated, which by the just judgement of God was to be hidden from the princes of the world. Whence the first to the Corinthians the second[^p227-5]: « We speak the wisdom of God in a mystery, which is hidden, which none of the princes of this world knew; for if they had known it, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory ». — But although it was not to be published to all, yet it was to be foretold to the disciples; on account of which he adds: *Saying that the Son of man must suffer many things.* Now the reason of this foretelling is assigned in John the fourteenth[^p227-6]: « Now I have told you, before it come to pass, that when it shall come to pass, you may believe ». For the disciples themselves did not yet grasp this, according to what is said below in the eighteenth[^p227-7]: « Then Jesus took unto him the twelve, and said to them: Behold, we go up »; and after: « And they understood none of these things, but they received this after the consummation ». Whence it is said in Luke the last: « These are the words which I spoke to you while I was yet with you, that all things must needs be fulfilled which are written concerning me »; and after it is added: « He opened their understanding, that they might understand the Scriptures ».

36. Now he says: *Must,* that is, *it is necessary,* not simply, but from the presupposition of the divine disposition and promise, according to that of the Acts the third[^p227-8]: « But God, those things which he before had shewed by the mouth of all the Prophets, that his Christ should suffer, he hath so fulfilled ». Or *must,* that is, *it is opportune* and expedient, according to what Caiphas prophesied; John the eleventh[^p227-9]: « It is expedient that one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not ». *Must,* I say, *suffer many things,* because *for many* souls to be redeemed, many faults to be remitted, many guilts to be absolved; Isaias the fifty-third[^p227-10]: « He was wounded for our iniquities, he was bruised for our sins ». « All we like sheep have gone astray; every one hath turned aside into his own way, and the Lord hath laid on him the iniquities of us all ». — He suffered also *many* and *great* things, he suffered also *from many* and *great* men; on account of which he adds: *And be rejected by the ancients and the chiefs of the priests and the scribes. The ancients* excelled in antiquity, *the chiefs* in authority, but *the scribes* by reason of doctrine, so that thus might be fulfilled that of Isaias the first[^p227-11]: « I have brought up children and exalted them, but they have despised me ». — And because the passion of Christ was to be ended in death, therefore it is added: *And be killed,* that is, he must, according to that of Daniel the ninth[^p227-12]: « Christ shall be slain ». But lest it be believed that he was *unwilling,* therefore Isaias the fifty-third: « He hath delivered his soul unto death ». — But because this passion was quickening of the dead, therefore he adds: *And the third day rise again,* according to that of Osee the sixth[^p227-13]: « He will quicken us after two days, and on the third day he will raise us up ». The figure of this the Saviour says went before in Jonas, Matthew the twelfth: « For as Jonas was » etc. — From these things, then, it appears what is the perfect knowledge of faith, on which the Church of Christ is founded, namely that through which the divine honour is approved, human error is reprobated, the mediator is asserted and the restorer is acknowledged.

37. (Vers. 23.). *And he said to all* etc. Having shown how Christ is to be known according to faith, here secondly it is shown how he *is to be imitated through the cross,* because[^p227-14] « faith is dead without
<!-- page 228 -->
works ». But since it is difficult to bear the cross, therefore he invites to this by a fourfold consideration, namely from the consideration of *his own example, one's own peril, the future judgement* and *the divine kingdom;* so that, if admonitions do not move, examples may move, or at least perils, or future judgements, or at least promises[^p228-1].

First, then, he invites *by the consideration of his own example,* when he says: *And he said to all: If any man will come after me,* that is, imitate me, to which I do not compel, but invite; therefore he says: *If any man will;* Ecclesiasticus the fifteenth[^p228-2]: « If thou wilt keep the commandments, they shall preserve thee ». « He hath set before thee water and fire; stretch forth thy hand to which thou wilt ». Whence Chrysostom[^p228-3]: « If one were giving gold, or setting out a treasure, he would not call with violence, but men would run; much more to the things that are in heaven. For if the nature of things persuades one to run; if thou dost not run, thou art not worthy to receive ». Therefore he says: *If any man will* follow my example. — *Let him deny himself,* through *humility in every respect,* bringing into captivity sense, affection and « the understanding unto the obedience of Christ[^p228-4] »; which he notes in this, that he says: *Himself.* And this is the counsel of Ecclesiasticus, the seventh: « Humble thy spirit very much »; and this indeed is necessary for one willing to follow the humble Christ. Whence Gregory in the Gloss[^p228-5]: « Unless one fall away from himself, he does not draw near to him who is above himself ». — But this is not enough; therefore he adds: *And take up his cross daily,* through *continual austerity,* so that he may be able to say that of Paul to the Galatians the second[^p228-6]: « With Christ I am nailed to the cross »; and of the second to the Corinthians the fourth: « Always bearing about in our body the mortification of Jesus Christ ». And therefore it is said to the Galatians the fifth: « And they that are Christ's have crucified their flesh with the vices and concupiscences ».

38. Note that he says: *Daily,* because daily the penance of the cross ought to be new and fresh, so that one may always say[^p228-7]: « I said: Now have I begun », as blessed Francis, who at his death said that he was then beginning to do well: « Brothers, let us begin and make progress, because up to now we have made little progress ». For the cross of Christ has a renewing nature; whence the second to the Corinthians the fourth: « Though our man that is outward be corrupted, yet he that is inward is renewed day by day ». — But for the perfect imitation of Christ humility and austerity are not enough, but *poverty* also is necessary. Therefore he adds: *And follow me,* namely through *the most high poverty,* by which he willed to carry nothing at all on the way, so as to arrive at the fatherland rightly and unencumbered. Therefore Peter said in Matthew the nineteenth[^p228-8]: « Behold, we have left all things and have followed thee »; upon which Bernard: « Well, Peter, and not unto folly for thee; for thou couldst not follow one running while thou wast laden ». And this is very honourable, though it may seem vile, because, in Ecclesiasticus the twenty-third[^p228-9], « it is great glory to follow the Lord ».

Now from this word is drawn out the threefold counsel and vow of religious, namely *of obedience* in the denial, *of chastity* in the cross and *of poverty* in the following; but « all men take not this word, but they to whom it is given by my Father », as is said in Matthew the nineteenth[^p228-10]; therefore he puts first: *If any man will* etc. That no one may excuse himself, as though he had not grace, he says: *If any man will* etc., as if to say that the grace of God is at hand for us, if the will be not wanting; to the Hebrews the twelfth[^p228-11]: « Looking diligently, lest any man be wanting to the grace of God ».

39. (Vers. 24.). Secondly, he invites by the consideration *of one's own peril,* when he says: *For whosoever will save his soul shall lose it*[^p228-12], that is, he who will save life in the present shall lose life in the future, namely for a momentary life he shall lose the eternal; whence John the twelfth: « He that loveth his soul shall lose it »: because he who loves too much loves wickedly, and « he that loveth iniquity hateth his own soul », as is said in the Psalm. For he who loves his soul above God loses God, and in losing God loses in consequence himself. And therefore Paul said in the Acts the twentieth[^p228-13]: « Neither do I make my soul more precious than myself ». — And because this seems to be a hard saying, therefore he proves it by the contrary sense: *For he that shall lose his soul for my sake,* namely in this life, *shall save it,* namely in the future life, according to that of the second of Machabees the seventh[^p228-14]: « Thou indeed destroyest us in this present life, but the King of the world will raise us up, who have died for his
<!-- page 229 -->
laws, in the resurrection of eternal life ». And therefore Paul said in the Acts the twenty-first: « I am ready not only to be bound, but to die also in Jerusalem for the name of the Lord Jesus ». Thus, then, *to lose the soul* for Christ is *to save it; to will to save the soul* is *to expose it to perdition,* according to that to the Romans the eighth[^p229-1]: « If you live according to the flesh, you shall die »; and this is the greatest peril.

40. (Vers. 25.). And therefore, in amplification of it, he adds: *For what doth it advantage a man*[^p229-2]*, if he gain the whole world,* and this temporally, *but lose himself and cast away himself?* and this everlastingly; as if to say: nothing, because he who loses himself loses all things, therefore it does not avail, but harms. Therefore James the fifth[^p229-3]: « Go to now, ye rich men, weep and howl in your miseries, which shall come upon you ». And the reason of this is, because, as Augustine says in the sermon on the Innocents[^p229-4], « no one has an unjust gain without a just loss, the gain in the chest, the loss in the conscience; he took a garment and lost faith, he acquires money and lost justice », and so in consequence his soul. Whence Isidore[^p229-5]: « If thou hadst the wisdom of Solomon, if the beauty of Absalom, if the strength of Samson, if the long life of Henoch, if the riches and power of Octavian; what doth it avail thee, if in the end the flesh is given to the worms, the soul to the demons, to be tormented with the rich man without end »?

41. (Vers. 26.). Thirdly, he invites to the carrying of the cross by the consideration *of the future judgement,* when he says: *For he that shall be ashamed of me,* namely to imitate me, *and of my words,* namely to confess them, despising the cross of Christ as ignominious, and also his doctrine, out of an evil blushing, of which it is said in Ecclesiasticus the fourth[^p229-6]: « For thy soul be not ashamed to say the truth; for there is a shame that bringeth sin, and there is a shame that bringeth grace and glory ». In which he gives to understand that we ought not to be ashamed of the truth of Christ, after the example of the Apostle, who said to the Romans the first[^p229-7]: « I am not ashamed of the Gospel; for it is the power of God » etc. But we ought to be ashamed of the vileness of sin, according to that to the Romans the sixth: « What fruit had you in those things of which you are now ashamed »? But many are not ashamed of the deformity of sin, of whom Jeremias the eighth[^p229-8]: « They were confounded, because they committed abomination; yea rather they were not confounded with confusion, and they knew not how to blush »; and again: « The forehead of a harlot woman is become thine » etc. Such a one is ashamed of the humility of Christ. — And of this he adds: *Of him the Son of man shall be ashamed,* that is, will make him ashamed, or will cast him away as a thing to be ashamed of by his just judgement, by which it is said in the first of Kings the second[^p229-9]: « Whosoever shall glorify me, him will I glorify; but they that despise me shall be despised », and this in the day of judgement. — And therefore he subjoins: *When he shall come in his majesty, and that of his Father, and of the holy Angels;* in which he touches the judiciary power as to *the principle of causality* in the Father, as to *the privilege of excellence* in himself and as to *the ministry of obedience* in the multitude of the Angels. Whence Matthew the twenty-fourth[^p229-10]: « Then shall appear the sign of the Son of man in heaven, and then shall all tribes of the earth mourn, and they shall see the Son of man » etc.; and Isaias the third: « The Lord will come to judgement with the ancients of his people ». In this judgement the proud shall be confounded, and the humble shall be exalted, according to that of the Psalm[^p229-11]: « I will break all the horns of sinners, and the horns of the just shall be exalted ».

42. (Vers. 27.). Fourthly, he invites to the consideration *of the divine kingdom,* which he shows to his imitators, when he adds: *But I tell you of a truth: There are some standing here,* through the uprightness of the mind, according to that of the Psalm[^p229-12]: « Our feet were standing in thy courts, O Jerusalem ». — To such the kingdom ought to be shown, that their desire may be fulfilled; therefore he adds: *That shall not taste death,* by the separation of soul and body; *till they see the kingdom of God,* through revelation, not through open vision. For it was said to Moses in Exodus the thirty-third[^p229-13]: « Man shall not see me and live ». But this was said through some special revelation, by which it was said to Moses: « I will shew thee all good ». In which it is given to understand that those standing in the word of Christ shall not taste death, but shall pass to life, according to that of John the eighth[^p229-14]: « If any man keep my word, he shall not taste death for ever ». — Since, then, Christ promises so great a reward to those imitating him through the cross, threatens so severe a judgement, holds out so great a peril, shows so great an example; very
<!-- page 230 -->
hard is he who is not moved to imitate him. On account of which it is said to the Hebrews the twelfth[^p230-1]: « Having so great a cloud of witnesses laid over us, let us run » etc.; and the last: « Let us go forth to him without the camp, bearing his reproach; for we have not here a lasting city, but we seek one that is to come ».
