# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XVII, Vers. 31-37
## *In illa die qui fuerit in tecto etc.*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 444 -->

53. *In illa die*[^p444-1] *qui fuerit in tecto* etc. Postquam descripsit modum apparendi et modum veniendi, hic tertio describit *modum evadendi*[^p444-2]. Habet autem pars ista partes tres; in quarum prima describitur *evadendi modus;* in secunda vero, *evadentium status,* ibi[^p444-3]: *Dico vobis: In illa nocte;* in tertia vero, *evasionis terminus* sive locus, ibi: *Respondentes dicunt illi.*

Circa *modum evadendi* quatuor introducuntur, scilicet *modus effugiendi, modus periclitandi, ratio periculi* et *ratio remedii.*

54. (Vers. 31.). Primo igitur quantum ad *modum effugiendi* dicit: *In illa die qui fuerit in tecto,* per amorem spiritualium; unde Glossa[^p444-4]: « *In tecto est* qui, carnalia excedens, velut in aura libera spiritualiter vivit ». Super hoc tectum debet ascendere praedicator veritatis, ut faciat illud quod aliis persuadet; Matthaei decimo[^p444-5]: « Quod in aure auditis praedicate super tecta ». Nam Isaiae quadragesimo dicitur: « Super montem excelsum ascende tu, qui evangelizas Sion » etc. — *Et vasa eius in domo,* per contemptum carnalium; Glossa[^p444-6]: « *Vasa eius in domo* sunt concupiscentiae carnales, quas prius amaverat, sed superna petens, in mundo reliquerat », secundum illud primae ad Thessalonicenses quarto[^p444-7]: « Sciat unusquisque vas suum possidere in sanctificatione et honore et non in passione desiderii, sicut et gentes, quae ignorant Deum »; de quibus Isaiae sexagesimo quinto: « Comedunt carnem suillam, et ius profanum in vasis eorum ». — *Non*[^p444-8] *descendat tollere illa,* per peccati recidivum, sicut ille, de quo supra decimo: « Homo quidam descendebat ab Ierusalem in Iericho et incidit in latrones »; Isaiae trigesimo primo: « Vae! qui descendunt in Aegyptum ad auxilium, in equis sperantes » etc. *Et qui in agro,* per exercitium virtutum; Glossa[^p444-9]: « *In agro* est qui operatur in Ecclesia, plantans et rigans »; Proverbiorum vigesimo quarto: « Diligenter exerce agrum tuum, ut postea aedifices domum tuam »; *similiter non redeat retro,* ad opera vitiorum, quia, sicut dicitur supra nono[^p444-10], « nemo mittens manum suam ad aratrum et aspiciens retro » etc. Vult igitur dicere, quod qui vult illam iudicii diem evadere, ut non pereat, despiciendo mundana perseverantissime tendat ad aeterna, sicut Paulus et faciebat et dicebat ad Philippenses tertio: « Quae retro sunt obliviscens, ad ea quae sunt priora, extendens me ipsum, ad destinatum persequor, ad bravium supernae vocationis Dei in Christo Iesu ». Licet autem haec perseverantia sit omnibus necessaria, maxime tamen, imminente futuro iudicio, quando instabunt tribulationes et multa mala exempla et documenta; Matthaei vigesimo quarto[^p444-11]: « Surgent pseudoprophetae et seducent multos. Et quoniam abundabit iniquitas, refrigescet caritas multorum; qui autem perseveraverit usque in finem » etc.

55. (Vers. 32.). Secundo quantum ad *modum periclitandi,* qui debet declinari, subiungit: *Memores estote uxoris Lot,* de qua dicitur Genesis decimo nono[^p444-12]: « Respiciens uxor Lot post se, versa est in statuam salis ». Et ratio huius est, quia contra mandatum Domini fecit, quo praedicitur Genesis decimo nono: « Noli respicere post tergum tuum, neque stes in omni circa regione, sed in monte salvum te fac, ne et tu simul pereas ». In quo intelligitur, quod totum periculum salutis in conversis ad Christum consistit in retrocedendo; unde secundae Petri secundo[^p444-13]: « Melius est viam veritatis non agnoscere, quam post agnitionem retrorsum converti ». Et rationem huius reddit, cum praemittit[^p444-14]: « Si enim refugientes coinquinationes mundi in cognitione Domini nostri et Salvatoris Iesu Christi, his rursus implicati superantur, facta sunt eis posteriora deteriora prioribus ». Et propterea ad detestationem apostasiae et recidivi et retrocessionis Dominus memoriale illud posuit de uxore Lot; de qua Sapientiae decimo[^p444-15]: « Incredibilis animae memoria stans figmentum salis ». Ideo signanter dicit: *Memores estote uxoris Lot.* Nam ideo versa est in statuam salis, ut sic
<!-- page 445 -->
ostendendo suam fatuitatem det aliis sapientiae condimentum, ut nullus aspiciat retrorsum, secundum illud quod dicitur supra decimo quarto[^p445-1]: « Bonum est sal; quodsi sal evanuerit, in quo salietur? ad nihilum valet ultra » etc. Unde Glossa: « Uxor Lot significat eos qui in tribulatione retro respiciunt et se a spe divinae promissionis avertunt; et ideo statua salis facta est, quia admonendo homines, ne similia faciant, corda eorum condit, ne sint fatui ». Hoc autem non tantum est exemplum, verum etiam figura et[^p445-2] praesagium. Nam sicut illud *incendium* significat finale iudicium imminens, sic *uxor Lot* retro aspiciens animas effeminatas et carnales, quae retrocedent et apostatabunt in illa tribulatione.

56. (Vers. 33.). Tertio quantum ad *rationem periculi* subdit: *Quicumque quaesierit animam suam salvam facere,* per amorem privatum; accipitur enim hic *anima* pro vita carnali, de qua Levitici decimo septimo[^p445-3]: « Anima omnis carnis in sanguine est »; Glossa, ibi: « Vitam corporis vocavit *animam,* non animam, quae migrat a corpore, sed quae morte finitur ». Hanc quaerit salvare qui nimis eam diligit; unde Glossa[^p445-4] super illud *quicumque quaesierit animam suam salvam facere:* « Qui vitam carnalem negando, Christum in mundo salvare voluerit ». — De quo subdit: *Perdet eam*[^p445-5], id est per divinum iudicium; Ioannis duodecimo: « Qui amat animam suam perdet eam ». Et quidem hoc fiet iusto Dei iudicio, ut qui sui amore immoderato offendit Deum simul perdat divinam felicitatem et animae salutem. Et hoc est, quia, amando bonum commutabile supra incommutabile, diligit iniquitatem; Psalmus[^p445-6]: « Qui diligit iniquitatem odit animam suam »; et vere, quia, sicut dicitur Matthaei decimo sexto, « quid prodest homini, si mundum universum lucretur, animae vero suae detrimentum patiatur »? Totius igitur illius periculi ratio est conversio ad *privatum bonum,* quo quis nimis se ipsum diligit et se ipsum nimis diligendo, plus debito afficitur circa bonum privatum sperando et gaudendo de eius praesentia, timendo et dolendo de eius absentia. Et hoc totum venit ex amore privati boni, qui maxime regnabit in fine mundi; secundae ad Timotheum tertio[^p445-7]: « In novissimis diebus instabunt tempora periculosa, et erunt homines se ipsos amantes, cupidi, elati » etc.; et nimis se ipsos diligendo, Deo se reddent exosos; Isaiae vigesimo octavo: « Coangustatum est stratum, ita ut alter decidat, et pallium breve utrumque operire non potest » etc.

57. Quarto quantum ad *rationem remedii* subdit: *Et quicumque perdiderit illam,* per odium omnis carnalitatis. *Vitam* namque *perdere* dicitur qui carnalitatem ipsius crucifigendo persequitur, sicut Paulus dicit ad Galatas quinto[^p445-8]: « Qui autem sunt Christi carnem suam crucifixerunt cum vitiis et concupiscentiis ». Ideo ipse dicebat ad Galatas secundo: « Christo confixus sum cruci. Vivo autem ego, iam non ego, vivit vero in me Christus ». Huius exemplum ostendit Actuum vigesimo[^p445-9]: « Vincula et tribulationes Ierosolymis me manent. Sed nihil horum vereor; non enim facio animam meam pretiosiorem quam me ipsum, dummodo consummem cursum meum et verbum ministerii, quod accepi ». Ille igitur perdit, qui perditioni exponit pro amore Iesu Christi; et de hoc subditur: *Vivificabit eam,* per praemium aeternae felicitatis; Ioannis duodecimo[^p445-10]: « Qui odit animam suam in hoc mundo in vitam aeternam custodit eam ». Et ratio huius est, quia dignus est a Domino liberari qui diligit eum usque ad odium sui; Apocalypsis duodecimo[^p445-11]: « Ipsi vicerunt eum propter sanguinem Agni et propter verbum testimonii sui, et non dilexerunt animas suas usque in mortem ». Qui sic diligit Deum summe diligit, secundum illud Ioannis decimo quinto: « Maiorem caritatem nemo habet, quam ut animam suam ponat quis pro amicis suis ». Et quoniam sic est diligens, dignus est *diligi,* secundum illud Proverbiorum octavo[^p445-12]: « Ego diligentes me diligo »; etiam merito dignus est aeternaliter *vivificari* et ab omni periculo liberari; Apocalypsis septimo: « Hi qui amicti sunt stolis albis, qui sunt et unde venerunt? Et dixit mihi: Hi sunt qui venerunt de magna tribulatione et laverunt stolas suas et dealbaverunt eas in sanguine Agni. Ideo ante thronum Dei sunt, non esurient neque sitient amplius, neque cadet super illos sol neque ullus aestus, quoniam Agnus, qui in medio throni est, reget illos et deducet eos ad vitae fontes aquarum ».

58. (Vers. 34.). *Dico vobis: In illa nocte* etc. Postquam descripsit evadendi modum, hic describit
<!-- page 446 -->
*evadentium statum*[^p446-1], quem describit triplicem, scilicet *contemplativorum, activorum* et *praelatorum,* secundum illud Ezechielis decimo quarto: « Si fuerint in medio eius tres viri isti: Noe, Daniel et Iob; ipsi animas suas liberabunt »; in quibus intelligitur triplex status salvandorum, ita quod in Noe, qui praefuit arcae, *praelati;* in Daniele, *contemplativi;* et in Iob, *activi.* In omni tamen statu sunt aliqui boni et aliqui mali, aliqui iusti et aliqui iniusti, ut sic omnis status timore sollicitetur, « et[^p446-2] subditus fiat omnis mundus Deo ».

Primo igitur quantum ad *statum contemplativorum* dicit: *Dico vobis: In illa nocte erunt duo in lecto uno,* quantum ad *statum contemplantium,* qui recte dicitur *lectulus,* secundum illud Canticorum tertio[^p446-3]: « In lectulo meo quaesivi per noctes quem diligit anima mea » etc. Intelligitur autem per *lectulum* status contemplativorum, quia ipse est locus *quietis;* Canticorum tertio[^p446-4]: « En, lectulum Salomonis sexaginta fortes ambiunt »; item, locus *solitudinis;* Matthaei sexto: « Tu autem, cum oraveris, intra in cubiculum tuum » etc.; item, locus *contubernii nuptialis;* Canticorum primo: « Ecce, tu pulcra es, amica mea; ecce, tu pulcra es, oculi tui columbarum. Ecce, tu pulcher es, dilecte mi, et decorus. Lectulus noster floridus ». — In hoc igitur lecto admittuntur etiam aliquando et electi et reprobi; propter quod addit: *Unus assumetur,* scilicet ad divinum consortium. In cuius figuram Matthaei decimo septimo[^p446-5]: « Assumsit Iesus Petrum, Iacobum et Ioannem, fratrem eius, et duxit eos in montem excelsum seorsum ». Unde ille Sanctus contemplativus dicebat in Psalmo: « Tenuisti manum dexteram meam et in voluntate tua deduxisti me et cum gloria suscepisti me »; et iterum: « Quoniam Domini est assumtio nostra et sancti Israel regis nostri ». — *Et alter relinquetur,* scilicet ad aeternum supplicium; Iob vigesimo[^p446-6]: « Devorabit eum ignis, qui non succenditur, et affligetur relictus in tabernaculo suo ». — Et nota, quod cum prius praemisisset[^p446-7] *in illa die,* nunc dicit *in illa nocte;* nam finalis tribulatio dicitur *dies* propter ipsius iudicis revelationem, et dicitur *nox* propter tribulationum horribilitatem, secundum illud Sapientiae decimo septimo[^p446-8]: « Nec siderum limpidissimae flammae illuminare poterant illam noctem horrendam ». Unde Glossa: « *In illa nocte,* id est in illa tam tenebrosa tribulatione, *ut in errorem inducantur, si fieri potest, etiam electi* ». Et de hoc Psalmus[^p446-9]: « Posuisti tenebras, et facta est nox; in ipsa pertransibunt omnes bestiae silvae ». In hac non solum cadent simplices, verum etiam aliqui contemplativi et magni. In cuius figuram dicitur Matthaei vigesimo quarto: « Stellae cadent de caelo, et virtutes caelorum commovebuntur ».

59. (Vers. 35.). Secundo quantum ad *statum activorum* subdit: *Duae erunt molentes in unum,* per exercitium circa *activam,* quae recte intelligitur per *molam,* quia qui versantur circa actus vitae activae ad modum molae circumeunt et decurrunt nec in uno opere sistunt, sicut dictum est Marthae supra decimo[^p446-10]: « Martha, Martha, sollicita es et turbaris erga plurima ». — Vel quia tales debent *erigi* spe aeternorum praemiorum et *deprimi* terrore suppliciorum, per spem scilicet et timorem, quibus tanquam molis spirituale molendinum movetur; unde Deuteronomii vigesimo quarto[^p446-11]: « Non accipies loco
pignoris inferiorem et superiorem molam ». Et quoniam circa hanc versatur homo quantum ad inferiorem rationis portionem, ideo dicit in feminino genere: *Duae erunt molentes;* unde Glossa[^p446-12]: « Duae differentiae sequentium orbem rerum mobilium, quae feminino genere designantur, quia maiorum consilio reguntur, ut feminae a viris, quae tamen *in unum* molunt, in quantum de suis negotiis Ecclesiae usibus deserviunt ». — Et quia inter hos sunt aliqui boni, ideo additur: *Una assumetur,* scilicet ad gloriam, secundum illud Psalmi[^p446-13]: « Misit de summo et accepit me et assumsit me de aquis multis ». — *Et altera relinquetur,* scilicet ad gehennam; Isaiae primo: « Derelinquetur filia Sion sicut umbraculum in vinea et sicut tugurium in cucumerario et velut civitas, quae vastatur »; et Matthaei vigesimo tertio: « Ecce, relinquetur vobis domus vestra deserta ».
<!-- page 447 -->

60. Tertio quantum ad *statum praelatorum* subiungit: *Duo in agro,* per custodiam Ecclesiarum; Glossa[^p447-1]: « Duae differentiae praedicatorum in Ecclesia quasi in agro laborantium ». Unde et Apostolus praelatum et praedicatorem etiam *agricolam* vocat; secundae ad Timotheum secundo[^p447-2]: « Laborantem agricolam oportet primum de fructibus eius edere »; et ideo primae ad Corinthios tertio: « Dei agricultura estis, Dei aedificatio estis ». Propter quod dictum est Ieremiae primo: « Ecce, constitui te super gentes et super regna, ut evellas et destruas et disperdas et dissipes et aedifices et plantes ». In hoc agro Ecclesiae crescunt *fructus bonorum operum;* Genesis vigesimo septimo[^p447-3]: « Ecce, odor filii mei sicut odor agri pleni, cui benedixit Dominus »; crescunt etiam *flores* Sanctorum, scilicet rosae Martyrum, lilia Virginum et violae Confessorum; Psalmus: « Pulcritudo agri mecum est ». — Inter hos tamen agricultores aliqui salvabuntur, et ideo addit: *Unus assumetur,* scilicet ad culmen aeternorum bonorum; Aggaei secundo[^p447-4]: « Assumam te, Zorobabel, fili Salathiel, et ponam te quasi signaculum, quia elegi te ». — *Et alter relinquetur,* scilicet in calamitate infernalium opprobriorum; Isaiae vigesimo septimo[^p447-5]: « Civitas munita desolata erit, speciosa relinquetur et dimittetur quasi desertum ». — Ex his omnibus colligitur, quod in quolibet statu salvandorum aliqui erunt boni et aliqui mali; et sic omnes formident, secundum illud Ecclesiastae nono[^p447-6]: « Sunt iusti atque sapientes, et opera eorum in manu Dei; et tamen nescit homo, utrum amore, an odio dignus sit, sed omnia in futurum servantur incerta ». Propter quod, si quis vult futurum iudicium evadere, necessarius est ei non tantum mundi contemptus, verum etiam timor divinus, secundum illud Isaiae secundo:[^p447-7] « Ingredere in petram et abscondere in fossa humo a facie timoris Domini et a gloria Maiestatis »; « cuius enim vult miseretur, et quem vult indurat ». Ideo unusquisque territus cum Apostolo exclamet et dicat: « O altitudo divitiarum sapientiae et scientiae Dei! quam incomprehensibilia sunt iudicia eius » etc.

61. (Vers. 36.). *Respondentes dicunt illi* etc. Postquam descripsit evadendi modum et evadentium statum, subditur hic ultimo[^p447-8] *evasionis terminus* et locus quietis, quem Apostoli requirunt, cum dicitur: *Respondentes,* scilicet Apostoli, *dicunt illi: Ubi, Domine?* scilicet assumetur et relinquetur. Unde Apostoli utrumque requirunt, sed unum Dominus respondet expresse et aliud innuit tacite; unde Glossa[^p447-9]: « Duo quaerentibus unum respondet, Sanctos scilicet secum assumendos, aliud subticendo innuit, malos scilicet a se repulsos cum diabolo damnandos ».

62. (Vers. 37.). Et ideo subdit: *Qui dixit illis: Ubicumque fuerit corpus, illuc congregabuntur et aquilae.* Nota hic notabile exemplum: electi congregabuntur ad Christum sicut ad suae refectionis abundantiam, quemadmodum aquilae congregantur ad corpus sicut ad escam; Iob, trigesimo nono[^p447-10], loquens de aquila dicit: « Ubicumque fuerit cadaver, statim adest ». Unde Hieronymus[^p447-11]: « Aquilae et vultures transmarina sentire dicuntur cadavera ». Congregantur autem[^p447-12] per vivacissimum olfactum, limpidissimum aspectum, velocissimum motum et avidissimum appetitum. Sic Sancti ad Christum congregabuntur, ad Verbum incarnatum, per ardentissimum *desiderium,* limpidissimum *contuitum,* sapidissimum *gustum* et per *motum amoris* excessivum. Propter quod et Propheta David[^p447-13], exprimens harum aquilarum accessus ad Christum, exprimit in se ipso *desiderium:* « Quemadmodum desiderat cervus ad fontes aquarum, ita desiderat anima mea ad te, Deus »; *contuitum:* « Quia apud te est fons vitae, et in lumine tuo videbimus lumen » etc.; *gustum:* « Satiabor, cum apparuerit gloria tua »; *amorem:* « Quia inflammatum est cor meum ». In horum figuram Iob trigesimo nono[^p447-14] dicitur: « Nunquid ad praeceptum tuum elevabitur aquila et ponet in arduis nidum suum? In petris manet et in praeruptis silicibus
<!-- page 448 -->
commoratur »; « oculi eius de longe respiciunt, et pulli eius lambunt sanguinem » etc. De natura aquilae dicit Ambrosius[^p448-1], quod aquila alta petit, humilia derelinquit, longaevam fertur habere aetatem. Sic Sancti post resurrectionem, renovati ad modum aquilae, secundum illud Psalmi[^p448-2]: « Renovabitur ut aquilae iuventus tua », et sursum tendent et semper cum Domino vivent, secundum illud primae ad Thessalonicenses quarto: « Deinde nos, qui vivimus, qui relinquimur, simul rapiemur cum illis in nubibus obviam Christo in aëra, et sic semper cum Domino erimus ».

## English
<!-- page 444 -->

53. *In that day*[^p444-1] *he that shall be on the housetop* etc. After he has described the manner of appearing and the manner of coming, here third he describes *the manner of escaping*[^p444-2]. This part, then, has three parts; in the first of which is described *the manner of escaping;* in the second, *the state of those escaping,* there[^p444-3]: *I say to you: In that night;* in the third, *the term* or place *of the escape,* there: *They answering say to him.*

Concerning *the manner of escaping* four things are introduced, namely *the manner of fleeing, the manner of being imperilled, the reason of the peril* and *the reason of the remedy.*

54. (Vers. 31.). First, then, as to *the manner of fleeing* he says: *In that day he that shall be on the housetop,* through the love of spiritual things; whence the Gloss[^p444-4]: « *He is on the housetop* who, going beyond carnal things, lives spiritually as in a free air ». Upon this housetop the preacher of the truth ought to go up, that he may do that which he persuades to others; Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p444-5]: « That which you hear in the ear, preach ye upon the housetops ». For in Isaias, the fortieth chapter, it is said: « Get thee up upon a high mountain, thou that bringest good tidings to Sion[^tr-evangelizare] » etc. — *And his vessels in the house,* through the contempt of carnal things; the Gloss[^p444-6]: « *His vessels in the house* are the carnal concupiscences, which he had before loved, but, seeking the things above, had left behind in the world », according to that of the first to the Thessalonians, the fourth chapter[^p444-7]: « That every one should know how to possess his vessel in sanctification and honour, and not in the passion of lust, like the Gentiles that know not God »; of whom Isaias, the sixty-fifth chapter: « They eat swine's flesh, and profane broth is in their vessels ». — *Let him not*[^p444-8] *go down to take them away,* through the relapse of sin, like that man of whom above, the tenth chapter: « A certain man went down from Jerusalem to Jericho, and fell among robbers »; Isaias, the thirty-first chapter: « Woe to them that go down to Egypt for help, trusting in horses » etc. *And he that shall be in the field,* through the exercise of the virtues; the Gloss[^p444-9]: « *He is in the field* who works in the Church, planting and watering »; Proverbs, the twenty-fourth chapter: « Diligently till thy ground, that afterward thou mayst build thy house »; *in like manner let him not return back,* to the works of the vices, because, as is said above, the ninth chapter[^p444-10], « no man putting his hand to the plough, and looking back » etc. He means to say, then, that he who wishes to escape that day of judgement, so as not to perish, should, despising worldly things, tend most perseveringly to eternal things, as Paul both did and said, to the Philippians, the third chapter: « Forgetting the things that are behind, and stretching forth myself to those that are before, I press towards the mark, to the prize of the supernal vocation of God in Christ Jesus ». And although this perseverance is necessary for all, yet most of all when the future judgement is imminent, when tribulations and many evil examples and lessons will press upon us; Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p444-11]: « False prophets shall rise, and shall seduce many. And because iniquity shall abound, the charity of many shall grow cold; but he that shall persevere to the end » etc.

55. (Vers. 32.). Second, as to *the manner of being imperilled,* which ought to be shunned, he subjoins: *Remember Lot's wife,* of whom it is said in Genesis, the nineteenth chapter[^p444-12]: « Lot's wife, looking behind her, was turned into a statue of salt ». And the reason of this is that she acted against the commandment of the Lord, by which it is foretold in Genesis, the nineteenth chapter: « Look not back behind thee, neither stay thou in all the country about: but save thyself in the mountain, lest thou also perish together ». In which it is understood that the whole peril of salvation in those converted to Christ consists in going backward; whence the second of Peter, the second chapter[^p444-13]: « It is better not to know the way of truth than, after the knowledge of it, to be turned backward ». And he gives the reason of this when he sets before it[^p444-14]: « For if, flying from the pollutions of the world in the knowledge of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ, they be again entangled in them and overcome, the latter things are become unto them worse than the former ». And therefore, to the detestation of apostasy and of relapse and of going backward, the Lord set that memorial of Lot's wife; of whom Wisdom, the tenth chapter[^p444-15]: « The memorial of an incredulous soul, a standing pillar of salt ». Therefore he says pointedly: *Remember Lot's wife.* For she was turned into a statue of salt for this reason, that so
<!-- page 445 -->
by showing her folly she might give to others the seasoning of wisdom, that no one should look backward, according to that which is said above, the fourteenth chapter[^p445-1]: « Salt is good; but if the salt shall lose its savour, wherewith shall it be salted? It is good for nothing any more » etc. Whence the Gloss: « Lot's wife signifies those who in tribulation look back and turn themselves away from the hope of the divine promise; and therefore she was made a statue of salt, because, by warning men not to do the like, she seasons their hearts, lest they be fools ». This, however, is not only an example, but also a figure and[^p445-2] a presage. For as that *burning* signifies the final judgement imminent, so *Lot's wife* looking back signifies the effeminate and carnal souls, which will go backward and will apostatize in that tribulation.

56. (Vers. 33.). Third, as to *the reason of the peril* he subjoins: *Whosoever shall seek to save his soul,* through private love; for *soul* is taken here for the carnal life, of which Leviticus, the seventeenth chapter[^p445-3]: « The soul of all flesh is in the blood »; the Gloss, there: « He called the life of the body *soul,* not the soul which departs from the body, but that which is ended by death ». This he seeks to save who loves it too much; whence the Gloss[^p445-4] on that *whosoever shall seek to save his soul:* « He who by denying Christ shall have wished to save the carnal life in the world ». — Of whom he subjoins: *He shall lose it*[^p445-5], that is, through the divine judgement; John, the twelfth chapter: « He that loveth his soul shall lose it ». And indeed this will come about by the just judgement of God, that he who by an immoderate love of himself offends God loses at once the divine felicity and the salvation of the soul. And this is because, by loving the changeable good above the unchangeable, he loves iniquity; the Psalm[^p445-6]: « He that loveth iniquity hateth his own soul »; and truly, because, as is said in Matthew, the sixteenth chapter, « what doth it profit a man, if he gain the whole world, and suffer the loss of his own soul »? The reason, then, of that whole peril is the turning to the *private good,* by which one loves himself too much, and, by loving himself too much, is affected more than is due about the private good, hoping and rejoicing over its presence, fearing and grieving over its absence. And all this comes from the love of the private good, which will reign most of all at the end of the world; the second to Timothy, the third chapter[^p445-7]: « In the last days shall come dangerous times, and men shall be lovers of themselves, covetous, haughty » etc.; and by loving themselves too much they will render themselves hateful to God; Isaias, the twenty-eighth chapter: « The bed is straitened, so that one must fall out, and a short covering cannot cover both » etc.

57. Fourth, as to *the reason of the remedy* he subjoins: *And whosoever shall lose it,* through hatred of all carnality. For he is said *to lose* his *life* who pursues its carnality by crucifying it, as Paul says, to the Galatians, the fifth chapter[^p445-8]: « And they that are Christ's have crucified their flesh, with the vices and concupiscences ». Therefore he himself said, to the Galatians, the second chapter: « With Christ I am nailed to the cross. And I live, now not I; but Christ liveth in me ». The twentieth of the Acts shows an example of this[^p445-9]: « Bands and afflictions wait for me at Jerusalem. But I fear none of these things; for I do not count my soul more precious than myself, so that I may consummate my course and the ministry of the word which I received ». He, then, loses it, who exposes it to perdition for the love of Jesus Christ; and of this it is subjoined: *He shall quicken it,* through the reward of eternal felicity; John, the twelfth chapter[^p445-10]: « He that hateth his soul in this world keepeth it unto life eternal ». And the reason of this is that he is worthy to be delivered by the Lord who loves him even to the hatred of himself; the Apocalypse, the twelfth chapter[^p445-11]: « They overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony, and they loved not their souls unto death ». He who so loves God loves supremely, according to that of John, the fifteenth chapter: « Greater charity no man hath than that a man lay down his life for his friends ». And since he is so loving, he is worthy *to be loved,* according to that of Proverbs, the eighth chapter[^p445-12]: « I love them that love me »; and deservedly too he is worthy *to be quickened* eternally and to be delivered from every peril; the Apocalypse, the seventh chapter: « These that are clothed in white robes, who are they? and whence came they? And he said to me: These are they who are come out of great tribulation, and have washed their robes, and have made them white in the blood of the Lamb. Therefore they are before the throne of God, they shall no more hunger nor thirst, neither shall the sun fall on them, nor any heat, for the Lamb, which is in the midst of the throne, shall rule them, and shall lead them to the fountains of the waters of life ».

58. (Vers. 34.). *I say to you: In that night* etc. After he has described the manner of escaping, here he describes
<!-- page 446 -->
*the state of those escaping*[^p446-1], which he describes as threefold, namely *of the contemplatives, of the actives* and *of the prelates,* according to that of Ezechiel, the fourteenth chapter: « If these three men, Noe, Daniel and Job, shall be in the midst thereof, they shall deliver their own souls »; in whom is understood the threefold state of those to be saved, so that in Noe, who presided over the ark, *the prelates;* in Daniel, *the contemplatives;* and in Job, *the actives.* In every state, however, there are some good and some wicked, some just and some unjust, that so every state may be made solicitous by fear, « and[^p446-2] all the world may be made subject to God ».

First, then, as to *the state of the contemplatives* he says: *I say to you: In that night there shall be two in one bed,* as to *the state of those contemplating,* which is rightly called a *bed,* according to that of the Canticles, the third chapter[^p446-3]: « In my bed by night I sought him whom my soul loveth » etc. And by the *bed* is understood the state of the contemplatives, because it is the place of *rest;* the Canticles, the third chapter[^p446-4]: « Behold, threescore valiant ones surround the bed of Solomon »; likewise, the place of *solitude;* Matthew, the sixth chapter: « But thou, when thou shalt pray, enter into thy chamber » etc.; likewise, the place of *nuptial companionship;* the Canticles, the first chapter: « Behold, thou art fair, my love; behold, thou art fair, thy eyes are as those of doves. Behold, thou art fair, my beloved, and comely. Our bed is flourishing ». — Into this bed, then, both elect and reprobate are sometimes admitted; on account of which he adds: *The one shall be taken,* namely to the divine fellowship. In figure of which, Matthew, the seventeenth chapter[^p446-5]: « Jesus took Peter, James and John his brother, and brought them up into a high mountain apart ». Whence that holy contemplative said in the Psalm: « Thou hast held me by my right hand; and by thy will thou hast conducted me, and with glory thou hast received me »; and again: « For our taking up is of the Lord, and of the holy one of Israel our king ». — *And the other shall be left,* namely to eternal punishment; Job, the twentieth chapter[^p446-6]: « A fire that is not kindled shall devour him, and he shall be afflicted, being left in his tabernacle ». — And note that, whereas he had first set down[^p446-7] *in that day,* he now says *in that night;* for the final tribulation is called *day* on account of the revelation of the judge himself, and is called *night* on account of the horribleness of the tribulations, according to that of Wisdom, the seventeenth chapter[^p446-8]: « Neither could the most clear flames of the stars enlighten that horrible night ». Whence the Gloss: « *In that night,* that is, in that so dark tribulation, *that even the elect, if it can be, may be led into error* ». And of this the Psalm[^p446-9]: « Thou hast appointed darkness, and it is night: in it shall all the beasts of the woods go about ». In this not only the simple will fall, but also some contemplatives and great men. In figure of which it is said in Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter: « The stars shall fall from heaven, and the powers of heaven shall be moved ».

59. (Vers. 35.). Second, as to *the state of the actives* he subjoins: *Two women shall be grinding together,* through the exercise about *the active life,* which is rightly understood by the *millstone,* because those who are busied about the acts of the active life go round after the manner of a millstone and run to and fro and do not stand still in one work, as was said to Martha above, the tenth chapter[^p446-10]: « Martha, Martha, thou art careful and art troubled about many things ». — Or because such ought *to be raised up* by the hope of the eternal rewards and *pressed down* by the terror of the punishments, namely by hope and fear, by which as by millstones the spiritual mill is moved; whence Deuteronomy, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p446-11]: « Thou shalt not take the nether and the upper millstone in place of a pledge ». And since man is busied about this as to the inferior portion of reason, therefore he says in the feminine gender: *Two women shall be grinding;* whence the Gloss[^p446-12]: « Two differences of those who follow the round of movable things, which are designated by the feminine gender, because they are ruled by the counsel of their elders, as women by men, who yet grind *together,* inasmuch as from their own business they serve the uses of the Church ». — And because among these there are some good, therefore it is added: *The one shall be taken,* namely to glory, according to that of the Psalm[^p446-13]: « He sent from on high, and received me, and took me up out of many waters ». — *And the other shall be left,* namely to hell; Isaias, the first chapter: « The daughter of Sion shall be left as a covert in a vineyard, and as a lodge in a garden of cucumbers, and as a city that is laid waste »; and Matthew, the twenty-third chapter: « Behold, your house shall be left to you desolate ».
<!-- page 447 -->

60. Third, as to *the state of the prelates* he subjoins: *Two in the field,* through the keeping of the churches; the Gloss[^p447-1]: « Two differences of preachers labouring in the Church as in a field ». Whence the Apostle too calls the prelate and the preacher a *husbandman;* the second to Timothy, the second chapter[^p447-2]: « The husbandman that laboureth must first eat of his fruits »; and therefore the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter: « You are God's husbandry, you are God's building ». On account of which it was said to Jeremias, the first chapter: « Behold, I have set thee over the nations and over kingdoms, to root up and to pull down and to waste and to destroy and to build and to plant ». In this field of the Church grow *the fruits of good works;* Genesis, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p447-3]: « Behold, the smell of my son is as the smell of a plentiful field, which the Lord hath blessed »; there grow also the *flowers* of the Saints, namely the roses of the Martyrs, the lilies of the Virgins and the violets of the Confessors; the Psalm: « With me is the beauty of the field ». — Among these husbandmen, however, some will be saved, and therefore he adds: *The one shall be taken,* namely to the summit of the eternal goods; Aggeus, the second chapter[^p447-4]: « I will take thee, O Zorobabel, son of Salathiel, and will make thee as a signet, for I have chosen thee ». — *And the other shall be left,* namely in the calamity of the infernal reproaches; Isaias, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p447-5]: « The strong city shall be desolate, the beautiful shall be left and forsaken as a wilderness ». — From all these it is gathered that in every state of those to be saved some will be good and some wicked; and so let all fear, according to that of Ecclesiastes, the ninth chapter[^p447-6]: « There are just men and wise men, and their works are in the hand of God; and yet man knoweth not whether he be worthy of love or hatred, but all things are kept uncertain for the time to come ». On account of which, if anyone wishes to escape the future judgement, there is necessary to him not only the contempt of the world, but also divine fear, according to that of Isaias, the second chapter:[^p447-7] « Enter thou into the rock, and hide thee in the pit from the face of the fear of the Lord, and from the glory of his Majesty »; « for he hath mercy on whom he will, and whom he will he hardeneth ». Therefore let every one, terrified, cry out with the Apostle and say: « O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and of the knowledge of God! how incomprehensible are his judgements » etc.

61. (Vers. 36.). *They answering say to him* etc. After he has described the manner of escaping and the state of those escaping, there is subjoined here last[^p447-8] *the term of the escape* and the place of rest, which the Apostles ask after, when it is said: *They answering,* namely the Apostles, *say to him: Where, Lord?* namely shall they be taken and left. Whence the Apostles ask after both, but the Lord answers the one expressly and intimates the other tacitly; whence the Gloss[^p447-9]: « To them asking two things he answers one, namely that the Saints are to be taken up with him; the other he intimates by passing it over in silence, namely that the wicked, driven back from him, are to be damned with the devil ».

62. (Vers. 37.). And therefore he subjoins: *Who said to them: Wheresoever the body shall be, thither will the eagles also be gathered together.* Note here a notable example: the elect will be gathered together to Christ as to the abundance of their refreshment, as the eagles are gathered together to the body as to food; Job, the thirty-ninth chapter[^p447-10], speaking of the eagle, says: « Wheresoever the carcass shall be, she is immediately there ». Whence Jerome[^p447-11]: « Eagles and vultures are said to scent carcasses beyond the sea ». And they are gathered together[^p447-12] through a most lively scent, a most clear sight, a most swift motion and a most greedy appetite. So the Saints will be gathered together to Christ, to the Word incarnate, through a most ardent *desire,* a most clear *contuition,* a most savoury *taste* and through a transporting *motion of love.* On account of which the Prophet David too[^p447-13], expressing the approaches of these eagles to Christ, expresses in himself *desire:* « As the hart desireth the fountains of waters, so my soul desireth thee, O God »; *contuition:* « For with thee is the fountain of life, and in thy light we shall see light » etc.; *taste:* « I shall be satisfied when thy glory shall appear »; *love:* « For my heart hath been inflamed ». In figure of these it is said in Job, the thirty-ninth chapter[^p447-14]: « Will the eagle mount up at thy command, and make her nest in high places? She abideth among the rocks, and dwelleth among cragged flints
<!-- page 448 -->
»; « her eyes behold afar off, and her young ones suck up blood » etc. Of the nature of the eagle Ambrose says[^p448-1] that the eagle seeks high things, leaves lowly things behind, and is said to have a long-lived age. So the Saints after the resurrection, renewed after the manner of the eagle, according to that of the Psalm[^p448-2]: « Thy youth shall be renewed like the eagle's », both will tend upward and will live always with the Lord, according to that of the first to the Thessalonians, the fourth chapter: « Then we who are alive, who are left, shall be taken up together with them in the clouds to meet Christ, into the air, and so shall we be always with the Lord ».
