# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. I, Vers. 5-17
## *Tria dicuntur de conceptu praecursoris*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 12 -->

### Divisio generalis[^p12-8]

7. *Fuit in diebus Herodis regis* etc. Hoc Evangelium Lucae, sicut supra[^p12-9] dictum est, habet qua-
<!-- page 13 -->
tuor partes principales, in quarum prima agitur de *incarnationis mysterio* usque ad tertium capitulum; in secunda vero, de *eruditionis magisterio* usque ad vigesimum secundum; in tertia, de *passionis medicamento* sive remedio usque ad vigesimum quartum; in quarta de *resurrectionis tropaeo* usque in finem libri.

#### Pars. I. De incarnationis mysterio agitur tripliciter.

Prima pars, quae prosequitur incarnationis mysterium, tres habet partes. Explicat enim Christi nativitatem temporalem, quae fuit triplex, scilicet *in utero* quantum ad conceptum, *ex utero* quantum ad partum, *extra uterum* quantum ad baptismum[^p13-1]. Primam explicat in primo capitulo, secundam in secundo et tertiam in tertio.

#### *Primo, de nativitate in utero dupliciter.*

Intendit igitur in primo capitulo agere de *conceptione* Salvatoris; sed quia conceptum Virginis praecessit conceptus sterilis, et ante regis conceptum praecessit praecursoris exordium, et utrumque divinum est, et de utroque laudandus est Deus; ideo praesens capitulum duas habet partes, in quarum prima agitur de sterilis et Virginis *fecundatione;* in secunda vero, de *gratiarum actione,* ibi[^p13-2]: *Exsurgens Maria abiit in montana.*

#### Primo, de duplici fecundatione.

Prima pars habet duas, in quarum prima agitur de conceptu *praecursoris;* in secunda, de conceptu *Salvatoris,* ibi[^p13-3]: *In mense autem sexto missus est* etc.

#### Tria dicuntur de conceptu praecursoris.

Describens autem Evangelista *praecursoris* conceptum, ut ostendat, ipsum esse mirificum, primo ostendit *parentum conditionem;* secundo explicat *angelicam annuntiationem,* ibi[^p13-4]: *Factum est, cum sacerdotio* etc.; tertio, *denuntiationis ministerialem confirmationem,* ibi: *Et dixit Zacharias ad Angelum: Unde hoc sciam;* quarto, *mirabilem promissionis impletionem,* ibi: *Et factum est, ut impleti sunt dies officii sui* etc.

#### Primo describitur parentum conditio quadrupliciter.

Describens autem *parentum* ipsius *conditionem,* quatuor explicat: primo, *dignitatis officium;* secundo, *honestatis coniugium;* tertio, *sanctitatis meritum* sive privilegium; quarto, *sterilitatis opprobrium.*

8. (Vers. 5.). Primo igitur *dignitatis officium* ostendit, cum dicit: *Fuit in diebus Herodis, regis Iudaeae, sacerdos quidam nomine Zacharias.* Sacerdotale officium explicat, adiungendo regale dominium, ut insinuet, quod Ioannes praenuntiare debebat Christum, qui erat *sacerdos secundum ordinem Melchisedech*[^p13-5], id est simul rex et sacerdos; ad Hebraeos septimo: « Melchisedech, rex Salem, sacerdos Dei summi » etc. — Vel ideo explicat regnum, ut ostendat, tempore Legis regiam potestatem praeire sacerdotium, sed nunc est econtra. Unde primae Petri secundo[^p13-6]: « Vos autem estis genus electum, regale sacerdotium » etc.; sed Exodi decimo nono dicitur: « Vos eritis mihi in regnum sacerdotale et gens sancta ». — Vel ideo explicat regnum Herodis alienigenae, qui primus inter alienos imperavit Iudaeis, ut ostendat, advenisse tempus adventus Christi, et impletam esse illam prophetiam, Genesis penultimo[^p13-7]: « Non auferetur sceptrum de Iuda et dux » etc. — Vel ideo nominat Herodem, ut ostendat, ipsum in malitia fuisse insignem. Fuit enim triplex Herodes, scilicet pater, filius et nepos; primus Ascalonita, qui interfecit pueros, Matthaei secundo[^p13-8]; secundus Antipas, qui Ioannem decapitavit, Matthaei decimo quarto; tertius Agrippa, qui occidit Iacobum, claudens in carcere Petrum, Actuum duodecimo; sed primus infamior et inter omnes crudelior. Ideo, quia « contra malum bonum[^p13-9] », econtra nominatur *sacerdos* Zacharias, ut ostendat, ipsum famosum in bono et Dei amicum, secundum illud Exodi trigesimo tertio: « Invenisti gratiam coram me, et novi te ex nomine »; nec tantum ex nomine ipsum ostendit sacerdotem, sed etiam ex genere; unde dicit: *De vice Abia,* id est de genere, secundum quod exponit Beda in Glossa[^p13-10] dicens: « Cum David cultum Dei ampliaret, sicut di-
<!-- page 14 -->
citur primi Paralipomenon vigesimo quarto, ministeria sacerdotum in viginti quatuor sortes divisit; in quibus familiam Abia, de qua Zacharias, sors octava contigit, ut praeco novi testamenti in octavae sortis vice nascatur; quia per octo novum testamentum intelligitur, sicut per septem vetus ».

9. Secundo explicat *honestatis coniugium* in hoc quod dicit: *Et uxor illius de filiabus Aaron;* et ita uterque erat de genere sacerdotali, ac per hoc ad coniugium apti secundum mandatum Domini; Numerorum trigesimo sexto[^p14-1]: « Omnes viri de eadem tribu et cognatione uxores accipiant, sed et cunctae feminae maritos ». Quam explicat *nomine,* quia ita de ea frequenter erat facturus sermonem: *Et nomen eius Elisabeth,* et in conceptu et salutatione et partu; unde[^p14-2]: « Elisabeth impletum est » etc. Explicat autem honestatis coniugium iuxta dignitatis officium, ut ex utroque commendabilior fiat conceptus; Sapientiae quarto[^p14-3]: « O quam pulcra est casta generatio cum claritate » etc.

10. (Vers. 6.). Tertio describit *sanctitatis privilegium* in hoc quod dicit: *Erant autem ambo iusti ante Deum,* id est *interius* in oculis Dei; Sapientiae quinto[^p14-4]: « Iusti in perpetuum vivent »; et sequitur: « Cogitatio eorum apud Altissimum ». Non erant de genere illorum, de quibus dicitur infra decimo sexto: « Vos estis, qui iustificatis vos » etc.; sed similes Noe, de quo Genesis sexto: « Noe, vir iustus atque perfectus in generationibus suis, cum Deo ambulavit », et Ezechiae, de quo quarti Regum vigesimo: « Memento, Domine, quomodo ambulaverim coram te in veritate et in corde perfecto ». Secundum enim quod dicitur primi Regum decimo sexto: « Deus intuetur cor ». — Erant et iusti *exterius* in conspectu hominum; et propterea dicit: *Incedentes in omnibus mandatis et iustificationibus Domini sine querela;* ad Philippenses secundo[^p14-5]: « Omnia facite sine murmurationibus et haesitationibus, ut sitis sine querela et simplices filii Dei, sine reprehensione in medio nationis pravae et perversae ». Et recte dicit: *in omnibus;* quia, secundum quod dicitur Iacobi secundo[^p14-6], « quicumque autem totam Legem servaverit, offendat autem in uno, factus est omnium reus ». Et ideo dicebat Propheta: « Ad omnia mandata tua
dirigebar »; et ibidem: « Dirigantur viae meae ad custodiendas iustificationes tuas ».

11. (Vers. 7.). Quarto adiungit *sterilitatis opprobrium* in hoc quod dicit: *Et non erat illis filius;* et in hoc erat magnus defectus; unde Genesis decimo quinto[^p14-7] dicebat Abraham: « Domine Deus, quid dabis mihi? Ecce, ego vadam sine liberis ». Et ratio huius defectus redditur in matre, cum dicitur: *Eo quod esset Elisabeth sterilis;* quod erat opprobrium in Israel; unde poterat dicere illud Isaiae quadragesimo nono[^p14-8]: « Ego sterilis et non pariens ». Et ut maius ostendatur miraculum, subiungitur defectus in utroque parente: *Et ambo processerant*[^p14-9] *in diebus suis,* secundum quod Abraham et Sara; Genesis decimo octavo: « Postquam consenui, et dominus meus vetulus est, voluptati operam dabo »? quasi dicat: hoc non est naturale, sed mirabile. Unde Genesis vigesimo primo: « Quis auditurus crederet Abraham, quod Sara lactaret filium, quem peperit ei iam seni »? Hoc autem sterilitatis opprobrium non fuit in peccati poenam, sed ad manifestandam Dei gloriam, secundum quod dicitur Ioannis nono[^p14-10] de caeco nato.

#### Secundo describitur angelica annuntiatio.

12. *Factum est autem* etc. Postquam descripta est parentum conditio, describitur hic secundo *angelica denuntiatio,* et primo, quantum ad circumstantias adiacentes *denuntiationi angelicae;* secundo, quantum ad circumstantias adiacentes *proli denuntiatae,* ibi[^p14-11]: *Erit enim magnus coram Domino* etc.

Tria autem fuerunt adiacentia promissioni, scilicet *exsecutio sacerdotalis officii, apparitio caelestis nuntii, denuntiatio conceptus futuri.* De primo, ibi[^p14-12]: *Factum est autem, cum sacerdotio* etc.; de secundo, ibi: *Apparuit autem illi Angelus Domini;* de tertio, ibi: *Ait autem ad illum Angelus.*

Primo ergo describitur *exsecutio sacerdotalis officii* debito modo, quia secundum *congruentiam ordinis, exigentiam consuetudinis* et *assistentiam devotae multitudinis.*

13. (Vers. 8.). Insinuans ergo exsecutionem debitam secundum *congruentiam ordinis,* dicit: *Factum est autem, cum sacerdotio* etc.; unde sibi competit illud quod de Aaron dicitur Ecclesiastici
<!-- page 15 -->
quadragesimo quinto[^p15-1]: « Dedit illi Dominus fungi sacerdotio et habere laudem, et glorificare populum in nomine ipsius » etc. Et dicit: *In ordine,* quia illud maxime debet fieri ordinate; primae ad Corinthios decimo quarto[^p15-2]: « Omnia honeste et secundum ordinem fiant in vobis ». Tunc autem recte servatur ordo, quando mens sacerdotis digne assistit conspectui divino; et ideo dicit: *In ordine,* secundum quod de Christo dicitur ad Hebraeos nono[^p15-3], quod « introivit in caelum, ut appareat nunc vultui Dei pro nobis ».

14. (Vers. 9.). Insinuat etiam eam debitam secundum *exigentiam consuetudinis,* cum dicit: *Secundum consuetudinem sacerdotii sorte exiit, ut incensum poneret.* Haec enim consuetudo, ut non usurpatione, sed sorte vel electione pontifex introiret, est recta; quia dicitur ad Hebraeos quinto[^p15-4]: « Nec quisquam sibi sumit honorem » etc. Quocontra dicitur de Ozia secundi Paralipomenon vigesimo sexto, quod volens incensum ponere, percussus est lepra; et filii Core consumti sunt igne, Numerorum decimo sexto. Hic autem *sorte exiit;* unde illud Sapientiae tertio: « Dabitur enim illi fidei donum electum et sors in templo Dei acceptissima ». *Exiit,* inquam, *ut poneret incensum, ingressus in templum Domini;* Levitici decimo sexto: « Assumto thuribulo, hauriens manu incensum, ultra velum intrabit in sancta, ut, positis super ignem aromatibus, vapor operiat oraculum, quod est super testimonium, et non moriatur ».

15. (Vers. 10.). Tertio insinuat eam debito modo factam propter *assistentiam multitudinis,* cum dicit: *Et omnis multitudo populi erat foris, orans hora incensi.* Omnes debebant assistere et pariter orare, secundum quod dicitur Actuum primo[^p15-5]: « Omnes erant perseverantes unanimiter in oratione ». Et *foris* stabant propter reverentiam, secundum quod ille publicanus; Lucae decimo octavo: « Publicanus, a longe stans, non audebat » etc. Et hoc *hora incensi,* quia illa erat hora apta; secundi Machabaeorum primo: « Orationem faciebant omnes sacerdotes, dum consummaretur sacrificium »; in quo significa-
tur, quod omnis nostra oratio debet offerri per Christum, qui « exauditur in omnibus pro sua reverentia », ad Hebraeos quinto.

16. (Vers. 11.). *Apparuit autem illi Angelus* etc. Tangitur hic secundo *apparitio caelestis nuntii,* et hoc ut *venerabilis,* ut *admirabilis* et ut *terribilis.* — Ostenditur autem *venerabilis in situ;* propter quod dicit: *Apparuit autem illi Angelus Domini, stans a dextris altaris incensi,* qui scilicet erat locus magnae reverentiae; unde, Levitici decimo[^p15-6], interfecti sunt Nadab et Abiu propter loci irreverentiam; et statim subditur: « Sanctificabor in his qui appropinquant mihi ». Et *a dextris stabat,* ut significetur, quod de caelestibus civibus erat; Proverbiorum tertio: « Longitudo dierum in dextera eius »; et in Psalmo: « Dixit Dominus Domino meo: Sede a dextris meis ». Et ideo *stabat,* non sedebat, ut ostendatur, quod nuntius erat; unde infra dicit: « Ego sum Gabriel, qui assisto ante Deum ».

17. (Vers. 12.). Ostenditur *admirabilis in aspectu;* et propterea dicit: *Et Zacharias turbatus est videns,* scilicet ex admiratione; Esther decimo quinto[^p15-7]: « Vidi te, domine, quasi Angelum Dei, et turbatum est cor meum prae timore gloriae tuae. Valde enim es mirabilis, domine, et facies tua plena gratiarum ». Aspectus namque angelicus carnalibus admirabilis est, quia insolitus; unde Angelus ad Manue, Iudicum decimo tertio: « Cur quaeris nomen meum, quod est mirabile »?

18. Ostenditur *terribilis ex occursu;* et propterea dicit: *Et timor irruit super eum,* in quo significatur vehementia timoris, sicut Danielis decimo[^p15-8]: « Vidi autem ego Daniel solus visionem. Porro viri, qui erant mecum, non viderunt, sed terror nimius irruit super eos, et fugerunt in absconditum; ego autem relictus solus vidi visionem grandem hanc, et non remansit in me fortitudo ». Adhuc Angeli terribiles apparebant, quia nondum erant nobis reconciliati; unde superiores nobis erant et ut superiores apparebant, et ideo timorem incutiebant; Ambrosius[^p15-9]: « Solemus turbari et a sensu nostro alienari, quando praestringimur superioris potestatis occursu ».
<!-- page 16 -->

19. (Vers. 13.). *Ait autem ad illum Angelus.* Tangitur hic tertio *denuntiatio conceptus futuri,* ut *confortativa* irascibilis, ut *instructiva* rationalis et ut *consolativa* concupiscibilis[^p16-1]. — Ostenditur *confortativa* in hoc quod dicit: *Ne timeas, Zacharia, quoniam exaudita est deprecatio tua.* Si Deus exaudit homines, tunc sperandum est in ipso, et ideo confitendum, non formidandum; Genesis decimo quinto[^p16-2]: « Noli timere, Abram; ego protector tuus sum ». Et quod omnino sit exaudita, signum est missio nuntii; Tobiae tertio: « In illo tempore exauditae sunt preces amborum. Et missus est Angelus Raphael » etc. Simile dixit Angelus Danieli decimo: « Noli metuere, Daniel, quia ex die primo, quo posuisti cor tuum ad intelligendum, ut te affligeres in conspectu Dei tui, exaudita sunt verba tua ».

20. Describitur *instructiva,* unde praenuntiat *prolem* et praedicit *nomen. Et uxor tua Elisabeth pariet tibi filium, et vocabis nomen eius Ioannem; pariet,* inquam, *tibi,* quia de te et ad tuam consolationem, iuxta quod promissum est Abrahae Genesis decimo quinto[^p16-3]: « Qui egredietur de utero tuo, ipsum habebis heredem ». Et subiungit *nomen* ipsius, secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo nono, quod dicitur in persona ipsius Ioannis: « Dominus ab utero vocavit me, de ventre matris meae recordatus est nominis mei ».

21. (Vers. 14.). Describitur postremo ut *consolativa* in hoc quod dicitur: *Et erit tibi gaudium et exsultatio;* Proverbiorum vigesimo tertio[^p16-4]: « Exsultat gaudio pater iusti », id est Zacharias, pater Ioannis; et « qui sapientem genuit filium laetabitur in eo », quia Proverbiorum decimo: « Filius sapiens laetificat patrem ». — Nec erit illud gaudium singulare, sed *commune.* Et propterea dicit: *Multi in nativitate eius gaudebunt;* Proverbiorum vigesimo nono: « In multitudine iustorum laetabitur vulgus »; Ieremiae trigesimo tertio: « Erit mihi in nomen et in gaudium et in laudem et in exsultationem cunctis gentibus terrae ». Ambrosius[^p16-5]: « Habet Sanctorum editio laetitiam plurimorum, quia commune bonum est. Iustitia enim communis est virtus ». « Exsultate ergo iusti in Domino, rectos decet collaudatio ».

22. (Vers. 15.). *Erit enim magnus coram Domino.* Supra notificata est denuntiatio angelica quantum ad circumstantias adiacentes ex parte sui; hic notificatur quantum ad circumstantias[^p16-6] ex parte *promissi.* Commendatur enim filius nasciturus in tribus, scilicet in *vita, gratia* et *doctrina.* Et primum respicit *actum,* secundum respicit *affectum,* tertium respicit *intellectum;* primum, ibi: *Erit magnus coram Domino;* secundum, ibi[^p16-7]: *Et Spiritu sancto replebitur;* tertium, ibi: *Et multos filiorum Israel* etc.

23. Nasciturum igitur filium, quem patris dixit esse gaudium, primo commendat in *vita* quantum ad *existentiam virtutis* et quantum ad *evidentiam sanctitatis.* — *Existentiam virtutis* tangit, cum dicit: *Erit magnus coram Domino,* sicut Iob primo[^p16-8]: « Erat vir ille magnus inter omnes Orientales », non inter peccatores, qui sunt magni in conspectu hominum, sed inter iustos, ita ut in eius ortu possit dici illud infra septimo: « Propheta magnus surrexit in nobis » etc. Vere *magnus Propheta,* quia secundum laudem Salvatoris, Matthaei undecimo, ipse Propheta, « et plus quam Propheta », et adeo magnus, ut dicat ibidem Salvator: « Inter natos mulierum » etc.

24. *Evidentiam sanctitatis* tangit in hoc quod dicit: *Et vinum et siceram non bibet;* in quo designatur vitae austeritas, quae signum est sanctitatis interioris, secundum illud ad Galatas quinto[^p16-9]: « Qui autem sunt Christi carnem suam crucifixerunt » etc. Per ista autem duo excludit omnem potum, qui potest inebriare. « Decet enim vas caelesti gratiae mancipatum a saeculi illecebris abstinere »; unde dicitur Aaron et filiis eius Levitici decimo: « Vinum et omne, quod inebriare potest, non bibetis, quando intrabitis tabernaculum testimonii, ne moriamini ». Hoc etiam prohibebatur Nazaraeis a tempore consecrationis suae, Numerorum sexto. Magnum erat hoc in beato Ioanne, ut cum multa innocentia esset tanta abstinentia.

25. Secundo commendatur in *gratia* quantum ad *consummationem* et *accelerationem.* Propter *consummationem* dicit: *Spiritu sancto replebitur,* ut de ipso possit dici illud Ecclesiastici decimo quinto[^p16-10]:
<!-- page 17 -->
« Et implebit eum Spiritu sapientiae et intellectus » etc. Solus enim Spiritus sanctus replet animae capacitatem, secundum illud Sapientiae primo: « Spiritus Domini replevit orbem terrarum ». Et hoc est, quod orabat Apostolus, ad Ephesios tertio: « Ut impleamini in omnem plenitudinem Dei ».

26. Propter *accelerationem* dicit: *Adhuc ex utero matris suae.* In cuius rei testimonium dicitur infra eodem[^p17-1]: « Exsultavit in gaudio infans in utero meo ». Unde Ambrosius: « Nondum erat in eo spiritus vitae, et iam erat spiritus gratiae »; non, inquam, erat secundum apparentiam, erat tamen spiritus vitalis secundum existentiam. Simile praecessit in Ieremia, primo: « Priusquam te formarem in utero, novi te, et antequam exires de vulva, sanctificavi te ».

27. (Vers. 16.). Tertio commendat eum in *doctrina* quantum ad *efficaciam utilitatis* et quantum ad *eminentiam auctoritatis.* Propter *utilitatis efficaciam* dicit: *Et multos filiorum Israel convertet ad Dominum Deum ipsorum.* Et hoc est maxima utilitas; Iacobi ultimo[^p17-2]: « Qui converti fecerit peccatorem ab errore viae suae, salvabit animam eius a morte et operiet multitudinem peccatorum ». Hoc est quod maxime Dominus petit, ut ad se convertantur; Zachariae primo: « Convertimini ad me, ait Dominus exercituum, et ego convertar ad vos, dicit Dominus exercituum ». Convertimini ad me, per poenitentiam scilicet, et ego convertar ad vos, per indulgentiam; Ieremiae tertio: « Convertimini, filii revertentes, et sanabo aversiones vestras ».

28. (Vers. 17.). Propter *excellentiam auctoritatis* subdit: *Et praecedet ante illum in spiritu et virtute Eliae.* Spiritus et virtus Eliae describitur Ecclesiastici quadragesimo octavo[^p17-3]: « Surrexit Elias quasi ignis, et verbum ipsius sicut facula ardebat »; et de Ioanne dicitur Ioannis quinto: « Ille erat lucerna ardens et lucens »: et ideo propter nimiam conformitatem dicitur Elias; Matthaei undecimo: « Et si vultis recipere, ipse est Elias »; et etiam decimo septimo: « Elias iam venit » etc. Et subditur *exsecutio auctoritatis* in hoc quod dicitur: *Ut convertat corda patrum in filios,* id est intelligentiam Patriarcharum in imitatores ipsorum. Beda[^p17-4]: « Corda patrum in filios convertit, dum spiritualem Sanctorum intelligentiam populis praedicando infundit ». Hoc praedictum erat de Elia Malachiae ultimo: « Ecce, mittam vobis Eliam prophetam, antequam veniat dies Domini magnus et horribilis. Et convertet cor patrum in filios, et cor filiorum ad patres eorum, ne
forte veniam et percutiam terram anathemate ». Et ut per omnia ostendatur conformis Eliae, subdit: *Et incredulos ad prudentiam iustorum,* id est ad fidem, quae est ipsorum prudentia, per quam credunt se iustificari; ad Romanos tertio[^p17-5]: « Arbitramur, hominem iustificari per fidem sine operibus Legis »; unde Habacuc secundo: « Qui incredulus est non erit recta anima eius in semetipso, iustus autem in fide sua vivet ». — Et quia haec conversio praeparabat ad Christi adventum suscipiendum, ideo addit: *Parare Domino plebem perfectam.* Sic enim de ipso fuerat praedictum Isaiae quadragesimo[^p17-6]: « Vox clamantis in deserto: Parate viam Domini, rectas facite in solitudine semitas Dei nostri ». Quando semita recta est, tunc facit perfectum; Proverbiorum quarto: « Iustorum autem semita, quasi lux splendens, procedit et crescit usque ad perfectam diem »; Ioannes autem quasi lucerna lucebat et manuducebat populum ad videndam lucem veram et perfectam. Auctoritas igitur Ioannis et officium erat praecurrere et praenuntiare Christum; unde de ipso intelligitur illud Malachiae tertio[^p17-7]: « Ecce, ego mitto Angelum meum, et praeparabit viam ante faciem meam ». Unde praedicatio eius praefigurata fuit in praedicatione Moysi Exodi decimo nono: « Estote parati in diem tertium », ut accipiamus *diem* pro *anno,* quia tertio anno post praedicationem Ioannis Christus aperte praedicavit. Sic exorsus est Samuel primi Regum septimo: « Praeparate corda vestra Domino » etc.; sic et omnis praedicator; unde Beda: « Qui fidem et opera praedicat, quid aliud quam venienti Domino ad corda audientium viam parat »?

### General division[^p12-8]

7. *There was in the days of Herod the king* etc. This Gospel of Luke, as was said above[^p12-9], has four
<!-- page 13 -->
principal parts, in the first of which the *mystery of the incarnation* is treated, up to the third chapter; but in the second, the *teaching office of instruction,* up to the twenty-second; in the third, the *medicine* or remedy *of the passion,* up to the twenty-fourth; in the fourth, the *trophy of the resurrection,* up to the end of the book.

#### Part I. The mystery of the incarnation is treated in three ways.

The first part, which pursues the mystery of the incarnation, has three parts. For it explains the temporal nativity of Christ, which was threefold, namely *in the womb* as to the conception, *from the womb* as to the birth, *outside the womb* as to the baptism[^p13-1]. The first it explains in the first chapter, the second in the second and the third in the third.

#### *First, on the nativity in the womb, in two ways.*

He intends, then, in the first chapter to treat of the *conception* of the Saviour; but because the conception of the barren woman preceded the conception of the Virgin, and the beginning of the precursor preceded the conception of the king, and each is divine, and for each God is to be praised; therefore the present chapter has two parts, in the first of which the *fecundation* of the barren woman and of the Virgin is treated; but in the second, the *thanksgiving,* there[^p13-2]: *Mary rising up went into the hill country.*

#### First, on the twofold fecundation.

The first part has two, in the first of which the conception of the *precursor* is treated; in the second, the conception of the *Saviour,* there[^p13-3]: *And in the sixth month the angel was sent* etc.

#### Three things are said of the conception of the precursor.

But the Evangelist, describing the conception of the *precursor,* in order to show that it was wonderful, first shows *the condition of the parents;* secondly he explains *the angelic announcement*[^tr-annuntiare], there[^p13-4]: *And it came to pass, when he executed the priestly function* etc.; thirdly, *the ministerial confirmation of the declaration,* there: *And Zachary said to the angel: Whereby shall I know this;* fourthly, *the wonderful fulfilment of the promise,* there: *And it came to pass, after the days of his office were accomplished* etc.

#### First the condition of the parents is described in four ways.

But describing the *condition* of his *parents,* he explains four things: first, *the office of dignity;* secondly, *the marriage of honesty;* thirdly, *the merit* or privilege *of holiness;* fourthly, *the reproach of barrenness.*

8. (Vers. 5.). First, then, he shows *the office of dignity* when he says: *There was in the days of Herod, the king of Judea, a certain priest named Zachary.* He explains the priestly office by adjoining the royal dominion, to insinuate that John was to announce beforehand Christ, who was *a priest according to the order of Melchisedech*[^p13-5], that is, at once king and priest; to the Hebrews, the seventh chapter: « Melchisedech, king of Salem, priest of the most high God » etc. — Or he explains the reign for this reason, to show that in the time of the Law the royal power went before the priesthood, but now it is the contrary. Whence the first of Peter, the second chapter[^p13-6]: « But you are a chosen generation, a kingly priesthood » etc.; but in Exodus, the nineteenth chapter, it is said: « You shall be to me a priestly kingdom and a holy nation ». — Or he explains the reign of Herod the foreigner, who first among foreigners ruled over the Jews, for this reason, to show that the time of the coming of Christ had arrived, and that that prophecy was fulfilled, Genesis, the next to last chapter[^p13-7]: « The sceptre shall not be taken away from Juda, nor a ruler » etc. — Or he names Herod for this reason, to show that he was notable in malice. For there was a threefold Herod, namely father, son and grandson; the first the Ascalonite, who killed the children, Matthew, the second chapter[^p13-8]; the second Antipas, who beheaded John, Matthew, the fourteenth chapter; the third Agrippa, who killed James, shutting up Peter in prison, Acts, the twelfth chapter; but the first was the more infamous and the cruellest of all. Therefore, because « good is set against evil[^p13-9] », on the contrary *the priest* Zachary is named, to show that he was famous in good and a friend of God, according to that of Exodus, the thirty-third chapter: « Thou hast found grace in my sight, and I know thee by name »; nor does he show him to be a priest only by name, but also by lineage; whence he says: *Of the course of Abia,* that is, of the lineage, according as Bede expounds it in the Gloss[^p13-10], saying: « When David enlarged the worship of God, as is
<!-- page 14 -->
said in the first of Paralipomenon, the twenty-fourth chapter, he divided the ministries of the priests into twenty-four lots; among which the eighth lot fell to the family of Abia, of which Zachary was, so that the herald of the new testament might be born in the course of the eighth lot; because by eight the new testament is understood, as by seven the old ».

9. Secondly he explains *the marriage of honesty* in this, that he says: *And his wife was of the daughters of Aaron;* and so each was of priestly lineage, and through this fit for marriage according to the commandment of the Lord; Numbers, the thirty-sixth chapter[^p14-1]: « All the men shall take wives of their own tribe and kindred, and all the women husbands ». Her he explains by *name,* because he was going to make discourse of her so often: *And her name Elizabeth,* both in the conception and in the salutation and in the birth; whence[^p14-2]: « Elizabeth's full time was come » etc. And he explains the marriage of honesty next to the office of dignity, so that from both the conception might be made more commendable; Wisdom, the fourth chapter[^p14-3]: « O how beautiful is the chaste generation with glory » etc.

10. (Vers. 6.). Thirdly he describes *the privilege of holiness* in this, that he says: *And they were both just before God,* that is, *interiorly* in the eyes of God; Wisdom, the fifth chapter[^p14-4]: « The just shall live for evermore »; and there follows: « The care of them is with the most High ». They were not of the kind of those of whom it is said below, in the sixteenth chapter: « You are they who justify yourselves » etc.; but like Noe, of whom Genesis, the sixth chapter: « Noe was a just and perfect man in his generations, he walked with God », and Ezechias, of whom the fourth of Kings, the twentieth chapter: « Remember, O Lord, how I have walked before thee in truth and with a perfect heart ». For according to what is said in the first of Kings, the sixteenth chapter: « God beholdeth the heart ». — And they were just *exteriorly* also in the sight of men; and therefore he says: *Walking in all the commandments and justifications of the Lord without blame;* to the Philippians, the second chapter[^p14-5]: « Do ye all things without murmurings and hesitations, that you may be blameless and simple children of God, without reproof in the midst of a crooked and perverse generation ». And he rightly says: *in all;* because, according to what is said in James, the second chapter[^p14-6], « whosoever shall keep the whole Law, but offend in one point, is become guilty of all ». And therefore the Prophet said: « I was directed to all thy commandments »; and in the same place: « O that my ways may be directed to keep thy justifications ».

11. (Vers. 7.). Fourthly he adds *the reproach of barrenness* in this, that he says: *And they had no son;* and in this there was a great defect; whence in Genesis, the fifteenth chapter[^p14-7], Abraham said: « Lord God, what wilt thou give me? Behold, I shall go without children ». And the reason of this defect is given in the mother, when it is said: *For that Elizabeth was barren;* which was a reproach in Israel; whence she could say that of Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter[^p14-8]: « I am barren and bring not forth ». And so that the miracle may be shown greater, the defect in each parent is added: *And they both were well advanced*[^p14-9] *in years,* according as Abraham and Sara; Genesis, the eighteenth chapter: « After I am grown old, and my lord is an old man, shall I give myself to pleasure »? as if to say: this is not natural, but wonderful. Whence Genesis, the twenty-first chapter: « Who would believe that Abraham should hear that Sara gave suck to a son, whom she bore to him in his old age »? But this reproach of barrenness was not in punishment of sin, but for manifesting the glory of God, according to what is said in John, the ninth chapter[^p14-10], of the man born blind.

#### Secondly the angelic announcement is described.

12. *And it came to pass* etc. After the condition of the parents has been described, here secondly *the angelic declaration* is described, and first as to the circumstances attending *the angelic declaration;* secondly, as to the circumstances attending *the offspring declared,* there[^p14-11]: *For he shall be great before the Lord* etc.

Now there were three things attending the promise, namely *the execution of the priestly office, the apparition of the heavenly messenger, the declaration of the future conception.* Of the first, there[^p14-12]: *And it came to pass, when he executed the priestly function* etc.; of the second, there: *And there appeared to him an angel of the Lord;* of the third, there: *But the angel said to him.*

First, then, is described *the execution of the priestly office* in due manner, because according to *the fittingness of the order, the requirement of custom* and *the attendance of the devout multitude.*

13. (Vers. 8.). Insinuating, then, the due execution according to *the fittingness of the order,* he says: *And it came to pass, when he executed the priestly function* etc.; whence there befits him that which is said of Aaron in Ecclesiasticus,
<!-- page 15 -->
the forty-fifth chapter[^p15-1]: « And the Lord gave him to execute the office of the priesthood and to have praise, and to glorify his people in his name » etc. And he says: *In the order,* because that ought most of all to be done in an ordered way; the first to the Corinthians, the fourteenth chapter[^p15-2]: « Let all things be done decently and according to order among you ». And the order is rightly kept when the mind of the priest worthily stands before the divine sight; and therefore he says: *In the order,* according to what is said of Christ to the Hebrews, the ninth chapter[^p15-3], that he « entered into heaven, that he may appear now in the presence of God for us ».

14. (Vers. 9.). He insinuates it also as due according to *the requirement of custom,* when he says: *According to the custom of the priestly office, it was his lot to go out to offer incense.* For this custom, that the high priest should enter not by usurpation but by lot or election, is right; because it is said to the Hebrews, the fifth chapter[^p15-4]: « Neither doth any man take the honour to himself » etc. On the contrary it is said of Ozias in the second of Paralipomenon, the twenty-sixth chapter, that wishing to offer incense he was struck with leprosy; and the sons of Core were consumed by fire, Numbers, the sixteenth chapter. But this man *by lot went out;* whence that of Wisdom, the third chapter: « For the precious gift of faith shall be given to him, and a most acceptable lot in the temple of God ». *He went out,* I say, *to offer incense, going into the temple of the Lord;* Leviticus, the sixteenth chapter: « Taking the censer, and taking up with his hands the incense, he shall go in within the veil into the holies, that, the spices being put upon the fire, the vapour may cover the oracle, which is over the testimony, and he may not die ».

15. (Vers. 10.). Thirdly he insinuates that it was done in due manner on account of *the attendance of the multitude,* when he says: *And all the multitude of the people was praying without, at the hour of incense.* All ought to attend and pray together, according to what is said in Acts, the first chapter[^p15-5]: « All were persevering with one mind in prayer ». And they stood *without* on account of reverence, according as that publican; Luke, the eighteenth chapter: « The publican, standing afar off, would not » etc. And this *at the hour of incense,* because that was the fitting hour; the second of Machabees, the first chapter: « All the priests made prayer while the sacrifice was consuming »; in which it is signified that all our prayer ought to be offered through Christ, who « was heard for his reverence » in all things, to the Hebrews, the fifth chapter.

16. (Vers. 11.). *And there appeared to him an angel* etc. Here secondly is touched *the apparition of the heavenly messenger,* and this as *venerable,* as *admirable* and as *terrible.* — He is shown *venerable in his position;* on account of which he says: *And there appeared to him an angel of the Lord, standing on the right side of the altar of incense,* which namely was a place of great reverence; whence, in Leviticus, the tenth chapter[^p15-6], Nadab and Abiu were slain on account of irreverence for the place; and it is added at once: « I will be sanctified in them that approach to me ». And *he stood on the right side,* so that it might be signified that he was of the heavenly citizens; Proverbs, the third chapter: « Length of days is in her right hand »; and in the Psalm: « The Lord said to my Lord: Sit thou at my right hand ». And therefore *he stood,* he did not sit, so that it might be shown that he was a messenger; whence below he says: « I am Gabriel, who stand before God ».

17. (Vers. 12.). He is shown *admirable in his aspect;* and therefore he says: *And Zachary seeing him was troubled,* namely from wonder; Esther, the fifteenth chapter[^p15-7]: « I saw thee, my lord, as an angel of God, and my heart was troubled for fear of thy glory. For thou art very wonderful, my lord, and thy face is full of graces ». For the angelic aspect is admirable to carnal men, because unaccustomed; whence the angel to Manue, Judges, the thirteenth chapter: « Why askest thou my name, which is wonderful »?

18. He is shown *terrible from the encounter;* and therefore he says: *And fear fell upon him,* in which the vehemence of the fear is signified, as in Daniel, the tenth chapter[^p15-8]: « And I Daniel alone saw the vision. But the men that were with me saw it not, but an exceeding great terror fell upon them, and they fled into a hiding place; and I being left alone saw this great vision, and there remained no strength in me ». Moreover angels appeared terrible because they were not yet reconciled to us; whence they were superior to us and appeared as superiors, and therefore struck fear; Ambrose[^p15-9]: « We are wont to be troubled and estranged from our sense when we are dazzled by the encounter of a superior power ».
<!-- page 16 -->

19. (Vers. 13.). *But the angel said to him.* Here thirdly is touched *the declaration of the future conception,* as *strengthening* the irascible, as *instructing* the rational and as *consoling* the concupiscible[^p16-1]. — It is shown *strengthening* in this, that he says: *Fear not, Zachary, for thy prayer is heard.* If God hears men, then one must hope in him, and therefore be confident, not afraid; Genesis, the fifteenth chapter[^p16-2]: « Fear not, Abram; I am thy protector ». And that it was wholly heard, the sending of the messenger is a sign; Tobias, the third chapter: « At that same time the prayers of them both were heard. And the angel Raphael was sent » etc. The angel said the like to Daniel, in the tenth chapter: « Fear not, Daniel, for from the first day that thou didst set thy heart to understand, to afflict thyself in the sight of thy God, thy words have been heard ».

20. It is described as *instructing,* whence he announces beforehand the *offspring* and foretells the *name. And thy wife Elizabeth shall bear thee a son, and thou shalt call his name John; shall bear,* I say, *thee,* because of thee and for thy consolation, according to what was promised to Abraham in Genesis, the fifteenth chapter[^p16-3]: « He that shall come out of thy bowels, him shalt thou have for thy heir ». And he adds his *name,* according to that of Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter, which is said in the person of John himself: « The Lord hath called me from the womb, from the bowels of my mother he hath been mindful of my name ».

21. (Vers. 14.). It is described lastly as *consoling* in this, that it is said: *And thou shalt have joy and gladness;* Proverbs, the twenty-third chapter[^p16-4]: « The father of the just rejoiceth greatly », that is, Zachary, the father of John; and « he that hath begotten a wise son shall have joy in him », because Proverbs, the tenth chapter: « A wise son maketh the father glad ». — Nor will that joy be singular, but *common.* And therefore he says: *And many shall rejoice in his nativity;* Proverbs, the twenty-ninth chapter: « When just men increase, the people shall rejoice »; Jeremias, the thirty-third chapter: « It shall be to me a name, and a joy, and a praise, and a gladness before all the nations of the earth ». Ambrose[^p16-5]: « The bringing forth of Saints has the gladness of very many, because it is a common good. For justice is a common virtue ». « Rejoice, then, ye just, in the Lord; praise becometh the upright ».

22. (Vers. 15.). *For he shall be great before the Lord.* Above, the angelic declaration was made known as to the circumstances attending on its own part; here it is made known as to the circumstances[^p16-6] on the part of *the one promised.* For the son to be born is commended in three things, namely in *life, grace* and *teaching.* And the first regards *act,* the second regards *affection,* the third regards *understanding;* the first, there: *He shall be great before the Lord;* the second, there[^p16-7]: *And he shall be filled with the Holy Spirit;* the third, there: *And many of the children of Israel* etc.

23. The son to be born, then, whom he said to be the joy of his father, he first commends in *life,* as to *the existence of virtue* and as to *the evidence of holiness.* — *The existence of virtue* he touches when he says: *He shall be great before the Lord,* as Job, the first chapter[^p16-8]: « That man was great among all the people of the east », not among sinners, who are great in the sight of men, but among the just, so that at his rising it could be said that of the seventh chapter below: « A great prophet is risen up among us » etc. Truly *a great Prophet,* because according to the Saviour's praise, Matthew, the eleventh chapter, he is a Prophet, « and more than a Prophet », and so great that the Saviour says in the same place: « Among them that are born of women » etc.

24. *The evidence of holiness* he touches in this, that he says: *And shall drink no wine nor strong drink;* in which is designated the austerity of life, which is a sign of interior holiness, according to that to the Galatians, the fifth chapter[^p16-9]: « And they that are Christ's have crucified their flesh » etc. By these two he excludes every drink that can inebriate. « For it befits the vessel given over to heavenly grace to abstain from the allurements of the world »; whence it is said to Aaron and his sons in Leviticus, the tenth chapter: « You shall not drink wine nor anything that may make drunk, when you enter into the tabernacle of the testimony, lest you die ». This was also forbidden to the Nazarites from the time of their consecration, Numbers, the sixth chapter. This was a great thing in blessed John, that with much innocence there should be so great abstinence.

25. Secondly he is commended in *grace,* as to *consummation* and *acceleration.* On account of the *consummation* he says: *He shall be filled with the Holy Spirit,* so that of him there could be said that of Ecclesiasticus, the fifteenth chapter[^p16-10]:
<!-- page 17 -->
« And he shall fill him with the spirit of wisdom and understanding » etc. For the Holy Spirit alone fills the capacity of the soul, according to that of Wisdom, the first chapter: « The Spirit of the Lord hath filled the whole world ». And this is what the Apostle prayed, to the Ephesians, the third chapter: « That you may be filled unto all the fulness of God ».

26. On account of the *acceleration* he says: *Even from his mother's womb.* In testimony of which thing it is said below, in the same chapter[^p17-1]: « The infant in my womb leaped for joy ». Whence Ambrose: « There was not yet in him the spirit of life, and already there was the spirit of grace »; not, I say, was there according to appearance, but yet there was the vital spirit according to existence. The like went before in Jeremias, the first chapter: « Before I formed thee in the womb, I knew thee, and before thou camest forth out of the womb, I sanctified thee ».

27. (Vers. 16.). Thirdly he commends him in *teaching,* as to *the efficacy of utility* and as to *the eminence of authority.* On account of *the efficacy of utility* he says: *And he shall convert many of the children of Israel to the Lord their God.* And this is the greatest utility; James, the last chapter[^p17-2]: « He who causeth a sinner to be converted from the error of his way shall save his soul from death and shall cover a multitude of sins ». This is what the Lord most of all asks, that they be converted to him; Zacharias, the first chapter: « Turn ye to me, saith the Lord of hosts, and I will turn to you, saith the Lord of hosts ». Turn ye to me, namely by penance, and I will turn to you, by indulgence; Jeremias, the third chapter: « Return, ye revolting children, and I will heal your revoltings ».

28. (Vers. 17.). On account of *the excellence of authority* he adds: *And he shall go before him in the spirit and power of Elias.* The spirit and power of Elias is described in Ecclesiasticus, the forty-eighth chapter[^p17-3]: « Elias the prophet stood up as a fire, and his word burnt like a torch »; and of John it is said in John, the fifth chapter: « He was a burning and a shining lamp »: and therefore on account of his very great conformity he is called Elias; Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « And if you will receive it, he is Elias »; and also in the seventeenth chapter: « Elias is already come » etc. And *the execution of the authority* is added in this, that it is said: *That he may turn the hearts of the fathers unto the children,* that is, the intelligence of the Patriarchs into their imitators. Bede[^p17-4]: « He turns the hearts of the fathers unto the children, while by preaching he pours into the peoples the spiritual intelligence of the Saints ». This had been foretold of Elias in Malachias, the last chapter: « Behold, I will send you Elias the prophet, before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the Lord. And he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers, lest I come and strike the earth with anathema ». And so that he may be shown conformed to Elias in all things, he adds: *And the incredulous to the prudence of the just,* that is, to faith, which is their prudence, through which they believe that they are justified; to the Romans, the third chapter[^p17-5]: « We account a man to be justified by faith without the works of the Law »; whence Habacuc, the second chapter: « He that is unbelieving, his soul shall not be right in himself; but the just shall live in his faith ». — And because this conversion prepared for receiving the coming of Christ, therefore he adds: *To prepare unto the Lord a perfect people.* For thus it had been foretold of him in Isaias, the fortieth chapter[^p17-6]: « The voice of one crying in the desert: Prepare ye the way of the Lord, make straight in the wilderness the paths of our God ». When the path is straight, then it makes perfect; Proverbs, the fourth chapter: « But the path of the just, as a shining light, goeth forwards and increaseth even to perfect day »; but John as a lamp shone and led the people by the hand to seeing the true and perfect light. The authority and office of John, therefore, was to run before and to announce beforehand Christ; whence of him is understood that of Malachias, the third chapter[^p17-7]: « Behold, I send my angel, and he shall prepare the way before my face ». Whence his preaching was prefigured in the preaching of Moses, Exodus, the nineteenth chapter: « Be ready against the third day », so that we take *day* for *year,* because in the third year after the preaching of John Christ preached openly. Thus Samuel began, the first of Kings, the seventh chapter: « Prepare your hearts unto the Lord » etc.; and thus every preacher; whence Bede: « He who preaches faith and works, what else does he do than prepare the way for the Lord coming into the hearts of the hearers »?

     **En.** As to the notes placed hereafter at the foot of the page, we note the following beforehand. First, from here on we no longer note edition 1 throughout, which scarcely ever differs from the Vatican edition; indeed it is the exemplar which the Vatican edition printed again.
     Secondly it must be observed that the holy Doctor, in citing places of sacred Scripture, uses a greater freedom than in his other writings, so as to study brevity: so that here and there he omits some words of the sacred text which do not bear on the matter he is treating, at times contracts a proposition, and more often also indicates by the words *etc.* that what follows is to be supplied from the readers' good memory. Our old codices agree remarkably in places cited in this way, by which it is sufficiently shown that the author himself wrote so. In the editions, however, these texts were commonly supplied and transcribed entire from the old Vulgate. Although this was done for the reader's convenience, we nevertheless did not hesitate that the reading of the codices was to be followed by us. But since the number of places of sacred Scripture is so great, it would have been too burdensome to supply these lacunae in the notes *always.* It seemed for the most part sufficient to indicate the verses of the Vulgate, which, as is known, were not yet in use in the Middle Ages. Yet when the sense itself still seemed to be lacking, or not expressed clearly enough, especially where the author himself insinuated this by that *etc.*, then we supplied the lacunae from the Vulgate. Nor have we omitted to note variant readings of some moment which seemed to differ from the text of the Vulgate.
