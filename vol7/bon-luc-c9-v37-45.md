# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. IX, Vers. 37-45
## *Factum est autem in sequenti die etc. Post demonstrationem gloriae promissae et contemplationem gloriae demonstratae subditur hic confirmatio gloriae consideratae in mirabili curatione daemoniaci, muti et lunatici*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 239 -->
70. *Factum est autem in sequenti die* etc. Post demonstrationem gloriae promissae et contemplationem gloriae demonstratae subditur hic *confirmatio gloriae consideratae* in mirabili curatione daemoniaci, muti et lunatici[^p239-5]. Circa huius curationem tria introducuntur ab Evangelista ad ipsius explicationem perfectam. Primum est *opportunitas disponens;* secundum est *potestas efficiens,* ibi[^p239-6]: *Respondens autem Iesus, ait illis* etc.; tertium est *utilitas consequens,* et hoc ibi: *Stupebant autem omnes* etc.

*Opportunitas* autem *disponens* colligitur ex tribus, scilicet ex *praesentia multitudinis occurrentis,* ex *instantia hominis obsecrantis,* ex *violentia daemonis obsidentis.*

71. (Vers. 37.). Introducit igitur primo *multitudinis occurrentis praesentiam,* cum ait: *Factum est autem in sequenti die, descendentibus illis de monte,* revertendo scilicet ad plana; *occurrit illis turba multa,* quia credebant et sperabant videre signa, secundum illud Ioannis sexto[^p239-7]: « Sequebatur Iesum multitudo magna, videntes signa, quae faciebat super his qui infirmabantur ». — Et nota, quod non comitatur turba *ascendentem,* quia non capit sublimia; unde Exodi decimo nono[^p239-8]: « Non poterit vulgus ascendere in montem »; occurrit autem *descendenti,* quia libenter amplectitur Christi humilia; unde Matthaei octavo: « Cum descendisset Iesus de monte, secutae sunt eum turbae multae ».

72. Ex hoc etiam potest *spiritualiter* intelligi, quod paucorum est otium contemplationis, quod designatur per *ascensum in montem;* sed multorum est exercitium actionis, quod designatur per *eius descensum* et turbam occurrentem. Quod designatum fuit in arca Noe, quae, cum esset trecentorum cubitorum in imo, consummata est in summo in cubito uno, sicut patet Genesis sexto[^p239-9]. Et ideo apex montis competit *contemplantibus,* et campestria plana *laborantibus,* iuxta illud Psalmi: « Ascendunt montes, et descendunt campi ». Et hoc est quod dixit Beda in Glossa[^p239-10]: « In monte orat, docet, maiestatem suam demonstrat, Patris vocem Apostolis pandit; descendens autem a turba excipitur, miserorum fletu pulsatur, infidelitatis peccata exprobrat, malos spiritus expellit ». Ubi tangit quatuor actus *contemplativae,* quos designavit in montis *ascensu,* qui sunt *oratio, lectio, contuitio divinorum* et *revelatio secretorum;* et quatuor *activae,* qui ostenduntur in *descensu.* Quadrupliciter enim debet praelatus in exercitio actionis condescendere subditis suis, scilicet *visitando, compatiendo, vitia increpando* et *necessitates relevando*[^p239-11]. — Secundum *litteram* vero descendenti turba occurrit, quia cupiebant videre expulsionem daemonis, quae non potuit fieri a discipulis, sicut paulo post subditur: « Rogavi discipulos tuos, ut eiicerent illum[^p239-12], et non potuerunt ». Ex
<!-- page 240 -->
tanta igitur praesentia et desiderio populi colligitur opportunitas miraculi faciendi.

73. (Vers. 38.). Secundo vero introducit *hominis obsecrantis instantiam,* cum ait: *Et ecce, vir de turba exclamavit.* In hoc ostendit affectum in supplicando, secundum illud Psalmi[^p240-1]: « Dum clamarem ad Dominum, exaudivit vocem meam »; unde primi Machabaeorum quinto: « Exclamaverunt tubis in caelum et exclamaverunt in oratione »; et de Samuele dicitur primi Regum decimo quinto, quod « contristatus clamavit ad Dominum tota nocte ». — Et quod hic esset *clamor orationis,* patet per hoc quod sequitur: *Dicens: Magister, obsecro te, respice in filium meum, quia unicus est mihi.* In qua oratione fatetur *Christi excellentiam,* offert *reverentiam,* petit *clementiam* et allegat *indigentiam;* et sic est oratio satis perfecta. Cum dicit: *Magister, excellentiam confitetur,* iuxta quod dicitur discipulis: « Vocatis me *magister* et *domine,* et bene dicitis », Ioannis decimo tertio[^p240-2]. Ideo autem magis vocat eum *magistrum,* quia existimabat, eum plura posse et scire quam discipulos, qui non potuerant nec sciverant curare filium suum; et propterea dicebat Dominus discipulis Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p240-3]: « Ne vocemini magistri, quia unus est magister vester, Christus ». — In hoc autem, quod addit: *Obsecro, exhibet reverentiam.* Obsecrantis enim est se humiliare et revereri, iuxta quod dicitur primae ad Timotheum quinto[^p240-4]: « Seniorem ne increpaveris, sed obsecra ut patrem ». Et hoc potissime debet homo facere in oratione. Nam Ecclesiastici trigesimo quinto dicitur: « Oratio humiliantis se nubes penetrabit ». — In hoc vero, quod addit: *Respice in filium meum, petit misericordiam,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo sexto[^p240-5]: « Miserere nostri, Deus omnium, et respice in nos et ostende nobis lucem miserationum tuarum »; de quo respectu dicitur in Psalmo: « Respexit in orationem humilium et non sprevit precem eorum ». — In hoc, quod addit: *Quia unicus est mihi, allegat indigentiam,* quasi dicat illud Psalmi[^p240-6]: « Respice in me et miserere mei, quia unicus et pauper sum ego ». Unicus enim filius *tenere diligitur,* iuxta illud secundi Regum primo: « Sicut mater unicum amat filium suum, ita ego te diligebam »; et *cum dolore perditur;* unde Genesis quadragesimo secundo dixit Iacob de Beniamin: « Ipse solus remansit, si quid ei adversitatis acciderit in terra, deducetis canos meos cum dolore ad inferos ».

74. (Vers. 39.). Tertio vero adiungit *daemonis obsidentis violentiam,* cum addit: *Et ecce, spiritus apprehendit illum*[^p240-7]*, et subito clamat;* ex quo apparet, quod daemon affligebat afflictione repentina; unde Matthaei decimo septimo dicitur de isto eodem: « Miserere filio meo, quia lunaticus est et male patitur et saepe cadit in aquam et crebro in ignem »; unde talis infirmitas erat *repentina,* sicut est infirmitas arreptitiorum. Talis autem erat Saul, de quo primi Regum decimo sexto[^p240-8] dicitur, quod « quando spiritus Domini malus arripiebat Saul, quod David citharam percutiebat »; in quo designatur, quod verus David, Christus, daemoniacam violentiam domaturus erat. — Nec tantum erat repentina, sed etiam *afflictiva;* unde addit: *Et elidit et dissipat eum cum spuma;* quod est signum magni doloris et afflictionis; unde poterat iam dicere[^p240-9]: « Dissipata sunt ossa mea secus infernum », quia a spiritu infernali ille dissipabatur. — Nec tantum erat afflictiva, sed etiam *diuturna;* propter quod addit: *Et vix discedit dilanians eum;* unde poterat dicere illud Iob decimo nono[^p240-10]: « Simul venerunt latrones et fecerunt sibi viam per me et obsederunt in gyro tabernaculum meum ».

75. (Vers. 40.). Et quod passio ista magnae esset durationis[^p240-11], apparet propter difficultatem curationis; unde adiungit: *Et rogavi discipulos tuos, ut eiicerent illum, et non potuerunt.* Ratio autem huius fuit, quod non poterant per potestatem terrenam, secundum illud Iob quadragesimo primo[^p240-12]: « Non est potestas super terram » etc.; et tunc aberat potestas divinae efficaciae propter incredulitatem humanam. Unde Matthaei decimo septimo: « Dixerunt discipuli Domino: Quare nos non potuimus eum eiicere? At ille respondens dixit: Propter incredulitatem vestram ». Sed econtra Marci nono: « Si potes credere, omnia possibilia sunt credenti ».

76. Secundum *spiritualem* autem intelligentiam in hoc daemoniaco sic vexato intelligitur homo, qui possidetur a *peccato spirituali;* qui *apprehenditur* per *superbiam,* quae prima facit, quod diabolus manum extendat in hominem, secundum illud Iob
<!-- page 241 -->
quadragesimo primo[^p241-1]: « Ipse est rex super omnes filios superbiae ». — Secundo vero *clamat* per *iactantiam;* Isaiae quinto[^p241-2]: « Exspectavi, ut faceret iustitiam, et ecce, clamor ». Tales erant Pharisaei, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo tertio: « Dilatant phylacteria sua, magnificant fimbrias, amant primos accubitus in coenis » etc. — Tertio vero *elidit* per *invidiam,* secundum illud Iob trigesimo[^p241-3]: « Elevasti me, et quasi super ventum ponens », per arrogantiam, « elisisti valide », per invidiam, quia, Iob quinto, « parvulum occidit invidia ». — Quarto vero *dissipatur* per *iracundiam,* quae omnes cogitationes conturbat, secundum illud Iob decimo septimo[^p241-4]: « Cogitationes meae dissipatae sunt, torquentes cor meum »; et in Psalmo: « Dissipati nec compuncti, frenduerunt super me dentibus suis ». — Quinto *dilaniat* per *cordis moestitiam,* qua praecipitat in impatientiam, per quam dilaniatur et conscientia; unde Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo[^p241-5]: « Tristitia cordis flectit cervicem » etc. — Et hinc est, quod *vix discedit* propter *perfidiam;* Sapientiae duodecimo: « Nequam est natio illorum, et naturalis malitia eorum, et quoniam non poterat mutari cogitatio cordis eorum in perpetuum ».

77. (Vers. 41.). *Respondens autem Iesus ait illis*[^p241-6] etc. Post opportunitatem disponentem explicat secundo Evangelista *potestatem efficientem,* quae describitur hic ut *aequa,* ut *pia* et ut *magna.*

Primo describitur ut *aequa* in argutione cordis increduli, cum dicit: *O generatio infidelis et perversa!* ut *infidelitas* sit in cognitione et *perversitas* in affectione, secundum illud Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo[^p241-7]: « Generatio enim perversa est, et infideles filii ». Infidelitas reddebat eos *stultos* et perversitas *duros,* secundum illud Ecclesiastae primo: « Perversi difficile corriguntur, et stultorum infinitus est numerus »; ideo non solum sunt admonendi, sed etiam fortiter increpandi. — Propterea addit: *Usquequo ero apud vos,* vobis scilicet faciens bona, *et patiar vos?* sustinens scilicet vestra mala; quod non dicit propter impatientiam, cum ipse sit, de quo dicitur Matthaei quinto[^p241-8]: « Qui solem suum facit oriri super bonos et malos »; et supra sexto: « Ipse benignus est super ingratos et malos ». Sed hoc dicit, ut per increpationem duram expellat cordis duritiam, secundum illud ad Titum primo: « Increpa eos dure, ut sani sint in fide ».

78. Secundo describitur ut *pia* in advocatione infantis obsessi, cum dicit: *Adduc huc filium tuum,* scilicet ad praesentiam Salvatoris, ut iam videatur impletum illud Isaiae quadragesimo nono[^p241-9]: « Afferent filios suos in ulnis et filias suas super humeros portabunt ». *Adduc,* inquam, per fidem tuam eum qui non potest venire per fidem suam, sicut illi viri apportaverunt paralyticum, de quibus Matthaei nono[^p241-10] dicitur: « Quorum fidem ut vidit Iesus, dixit: Dimittuntur tibi peccata tua ». Unde Marci nono Dominus dixit illi viro: « Si potes credere, omnia possibilia sunt credenti ». Fides enim illa est, qua mediante quis ducitur ad Christum, quia ipsa est via deveniendi ad lucem; unde Apostolus ad Hebraeos undecimo[^p241-11]: « Accedentem oportet credere, quia sine fide impossibile est placere Deo ».

79. (Vers. 42.). Et quia[^p241-12] « non est conventio Christi ad Beliál nec lucis ad tenebras », ideo addit: *Et cum accederet, elisit illum daemonium et dissipavit;* in quo ostenditur, quod ipse invitus veniebat ad lucem tanquam male agens, secundum illud Ioannis tertio: « Qui male agit odit lucem ». Simul etiam cum hoc ostenditur, quod diabolus maxime adversatur, ne quis ab eo obsessus convertatur ad Christum, secundum quod dicit Beda in Glossa[^p241-13]: « Unde puer ad Dominum accedens eliditur, quia conversi ad Dominum plerumque a daemone gravius pulsantur, ut ad vitia revertantur ». Cuius exemplum habemus Exodi decimo quarto de Pharaone, qui persequebatur populum recedentem.

80. (Vers. 43.). Tertio describitur ut *magna* in expulsione spiritus immundi, cum additur: *Et increpavit Iesus spiritum immundum,* quasi solo verbo compescens eum, secundum illud supra quarto[^p241-14]: « Increpans non sinebat ea loqui »; unde Angelus Domini Zachariae tertio dicebat: « Increpet Dominus in te, satan ». — Et quia illa increpatione eiectum est daemonium, subditur: *Et sanavit puerum.* Unde illa increpatio erat salutaris; propter quod Iob quinto[^p241-15]: « Increpationem Domini ne reprobes, quia ipse vulnerat et medetur, percutit, et manus eius
<!-- page 242 -->
sanabunt ». — Et quia hoc meruerat fides patris, ideo additur: *Et reddidit illum patri suo*[^p242-1], ad leniendum dolorem suum, ut posset dicere unico suo illud Iacob, Genesis quadragesimo sexto: « Iam laetus moriar, quia vidi faciem tuam et superstitem te relinquo ».

81. Hoc autem facto *spiritualiter* eruditur praelatus, qualiter subditum a diabolo per peccatum obsessum reddere debet Regi caelorum, scilicet *increpando* et *sanando: increpando* per iustitiam et *sanando* per clementiam. Debet autem esse rigor iustitiae respectu culpae, sed dulcor clementiae respectu naturae. Unde Augustinus[^p242-2]: « Fiat correctio cum dilectione hominum in odio vitiorum »; et Gregorius[^p242-3]: « Districtionem debemus vitiis et compassionem naturae ». « Vera namque iustitia compassionem habet, falsa iustitia indignationem ». Unde etiam infra decimo dicitur de Samaritano curante vulneratum, quod plagis eius infudit vinum et oleum, vinum mordicativum et oleum lenitivum, secundum duo, quae dicta sunt.

82. (Vers. 44.). *Stupebant autem omnes* etc. Post opportunitatem disponentem et potestatem efficientem subdit hic Evangelista *utilitatem consequentem.* Et haec fuit duplex: prima *universalis,* secunda *particularis.* Prima fuit *excitatio universorum ad reverentiam maiestatis,* secunda fuit *sollicitatio discipulorum ad intelligentiam veritatis.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *excitationem universorum ad maiestatis reverentiam* dicitur: *Stupebant autem omnes in magnitudine Dei,* quia magna erat potentia, quae tam magnum morbum in tam parvo tempore, tam parvo verbo perfecte curavit. Unde decantabant illud Psalmi[^p242-4]: « Magnus Dominus noster et magna virtus eius »; et illud Ieremiae decimo: « Magnus es tu, Domine, et magnum nomen tuum, et in universis regnis terrarum nullus est similis »; et Exodi decimo quinto: « Quis similis tui in fortibus, Domine? Terribilis atque laudabilis et faciens mirabilia ».

83. Secundo vero quantum ad *sollicitationem discipulorum ad veritatis intelligentiam* additur: *Omnibus mirantibus in omnibus, quae faciebat,* et etiam dicebat et loquebatur[^p242-5]; *dixit ad discipulos suos: Ponite vos in cordibus vestris sermones istos;* quasi dicat eis: aliis recognoscentibus et admirantibus magnitudinem miraculorum, vos cogitate humilitatem futurorum opprobriorum et « pretiosi sanguinis, quo mundus redimendus est, eventum mente recondite, dum ceteri mirantur tantum facta divinae altitudinis[^p242-6] ». Unde *ponite,* id est, in memoria habete, secundum illud Canticorum ultimo: « Pone me ut signaculum supra cor tuum ». Ideo autem post ostensionem miraculi adiungit praedictionem futuri supplicii, ne subito veniens passio discipulos omnino concutiat; unde Ecclesiastici undecimo[^p242-7]: « In die bonorum ne immemor sis malorum »; et ne aliquis prosperitatis tempore extollatur, secundum illud Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo: « Ne glorieris in crastinum, ignorans, quid superventura pariat dies »; et ideo Ecclesiastici undecimo: « In die honoris tui ne extollaris ». — Hi *sermones,* quos vult ad memoriam reduci, sunt de *passione;* unde addit: *Filius*[^p242-8] *hominis futurum est ut tradatur in manus hominum. Tradatur,* inquam, ex *clementia* a Deo Patre; ad Romanos octavo: « Qui proprio Filio suo non pepercit, sed pro nobis omnibus tradidit illum » etc. Tradetur ex *obedientia* a se ipso; Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p242-9]: « Pro eo, quod tradidit in mortem animam suam ». Tradetur ex *malitia* a Iuda proditore; Matthaei vigesimo sexto: « Qui intingit mecum manum in paropside, hic me tradet ». Et in hoc implicat totam passionem.

84. (Vers. 45.). Et quoniam hoc erat occultum mysterium, ideo additur: *At illi ignorabant verbum istud, et erat velatum ante eos, ut non sentirent illud;* quia adhuc *rudes* erant et *animales* nec intelligebant praeterita, ideo nec futura; unde Ecclesiastae octavo[^p242-10]: « Qui ignorat praeterita nec futura ullo scire potest nuntio ». — Immo et quia
<!-- page 243 -->
adhuc non erant *perfecte eruditi,* ideo additur: *Et timebant eum interrogare de hoc verbo.* Nondum enim audierant ab eo illud Ioannis decimo quinto[^p243-1]: « Iam non dicam vos servos, quia servus nescit, quid faciat dominus eius » etc. — Huius autem ignorantiae triplex erat ratio. Partim enim veniebat ex amore caritatis, partim ex fide Divinitatis, partim ex consueto modo loquendi in parabolis. Et hoc tangit Glossa Bedae[^p243-2]: « Haec ignorantia » etc. Licet autem sciret Dominus, eos ignorare, praedixit tamen eis, ut postmodum narravit post resurrectionem, quando eos increpavit, infra ultimo[^p243-3]: « O stulti et tardi corde ad credendum »! et quando « aperuit illis sensum ». Unde huius rationem reddit Ioannis decimo quarto: « Hoc dixi vobis, priusquam fiat, ut, cum factum fuerit, credatis ». Tunc autem dicebat, ut, sicut dictum est[^p243-4], in omni nostra exaltatione doceat mortis et humilitatis nostrae habere memoriam; unde Ecclesiastici septimo: « Memorare novissima tua et in aeternum non peccabis ».

## English
<!-- page 239 -->
70. *And it came to pass the day following* etc. After the demonstration of the glory promised and the contemplation of the glory demonstrated there is subjoined here *the confirmation of the glory considered,* in the marvellous cure of the demoniac, dumb and lunatic[^p239-5]. About the cure of this one three things are brought in by the Evangelist for its perfect explanation. The first is *the disposing opportunity;* the second is *the effecting power,* at[^p239-6]: *And Jesus answering, said to them* etc.; the third is *the consequent utility,* and this at: *And all were astonished* etc.

Now *the disposing opportunity* is gathered from three things, namely from *the presence of the multitude coming to meet him,* from *the urgency of the man beseeching,* from *the violence of the demon besieging.*

71. (Vers. 37.). First, then, he brings in *the presence of the multitude coming to meet him,* when he says: *And it came to pass the day following, when they came down from the mountain,* returning, namely, to the plains; *there met them a great multitude,* because they believed and hoped to see signs, according to that of John, the sixth chapter[^p239-7]: « A great multitude followed Jesus, seeing the signs which he did on them that were diseased ». — And note that the multitude does not accompany him *ascending,* because it does not grasp lofty things; whence Exodus, the nineteenth chapter[^p239-8]: « The common people shall not be able to ascend into the mountain »; but it comes to meet him *descending,* because it gladly embraces the lowly things of Christ; whence Matthew, the eighth chapter: « When Jesus was come down from the mountain, great multitudes followed him ».

72. From this too it can be understood *spiritually* that the leisure of contemplation belongs to few, which is signified by *the ascent into the mountain;* but the exercise of action belongs to many, which is signified by *his descent* and the multitude coming to meet him. Which was signified in the ark of Noe, which, being of three hundred cubits at the bottom, was finished at the top in one cubit, as is plain from Genesis, the sixth chapter[^p239-9]. And therefore the summit of the mountain befits *those contemplating,* and the level plains *those labouring,* according to that of the Psalm: « The mountains ascend, and the plains descend ». And this is what Bede said in the Gloss[^p239-10]: « On the mountain he prays, teaches, shows his majesty, opens the Father's voice to the Apostles; but descending he is received by the multitude, is beaten upon by the weeping of the wretched, upbraids the sins of faithlessness, expels evil spirits ». Where he touches four acts of *the contemplative life,* which he signified in the *ascent* of the mountain, which are *prayer, reading, the contuition of divine things* and *the revelation of secrets;* and four of *the active,* which are shown in the *descent.* For in four ways ought a prelate, in the exercise of action, to condescend to his subjects, namely *by visiting, by suffering with them, by rebuking vices* and *by relieving necessities*[^p239-11]. — According to *the letter,* however, the multitude comes to meet him descending because they longed to see the expulsion of the demon, which could not be done by the disciples, as is subjoined a little after: « I desired thy disciples to cast him out[^p239-12], and they could not ». From
<!-- page 240 -->
so great a presence, then, and desire of the people, the opportunity for working the miracle is gathered.

73. (Vers. 38.). Secondly, he brings in *the urgency of the man beseeching,* when he says: *And behold a man among the crowd cried out.* In this he shows the affection in supplicating, according to that of the Psalm[^p240-1]: « When I cried to the Lord, he heard my voice »; whence the first of Machabees, the fifth chapter: « They cried out with trumpets to heaven and cried out in prayer »; and of Samuel it is said in the first of Kings, the fifteenth chapter, that « being grieved, he cried to the Lord all night ». — And that this was *the cry of prayer* is plain from what follows: *Saying: Master, I beseech thee, look upon my son, because he is my only one.* In which prayer he confesses *Christ's excellence,* offers *reverence,* asks *clemency* and alleges *need;* and so the prayer is quite perfect. When he says: *Master, he confesses the excellence,* according to what is said to the disciples: « You call me *Master* and *Lord,* and you say well », John, the thirteenth chapter[^p240-2]. And he calls him *Master* the rather because he reckoned that he could do and knew more than the disciples, who had not been able nor had known how to cure his son; and therefore the Lord said to the disciples, Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p240-3]: « Be not called masters, for one is your master, Christ ». — In this, however, that he adds: *I beseech thee, he shows reverence.* For it belongs to one beseeching to humble himself and to revere, according to what is said in the first to Timothy, the fifth chapter[^p240-4]: « An ancient man rebuke not, but beseech him as a father ». And this above all ought a man to do in prayer. For in Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-fifth chapter, it is said: « The prayer of him that humbleth himself shall pierce the clouds ». — But in this, that he adds: *Look upon my son, he asks mercy,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-sixth chapter[^p240-5]: « Have mercy upon us, O God of all, and look upon us, and shew us the light of thy mercies »; of which looking it is said in the Psalm: « He hath looked upon the prayer of the humble, and he hath not despised their petition ». — In this, that he adds: *Because he is my only one, he alleges need,* as if he said that of the Psalm[^p240-6]: « Look thou upon me, and have mercy on me, for I am an only one and poor ». For an only son *is tenderly loved,* according to that of the second of Kings, the first chapter: « As the mother loveth her only son, so did I love thee »; and *is lost with sorrow;* whence in Genesis, the forty-second chapter, Jacob said of Benjamin: « He only is left; if any mischief befall him in the land, you will bring down my grey hairs with sorrow to hell ».

74. (Vers. 39.). Thirdly, he adds *the violence of the demon besieging,* when he adds: *And lo, a spirit seizeth him*[^p240-7]*, and he suddenly crieth out;* from which it appears that the demon afflicted him with a sudden affliction; whence in Matthew, the seventeenth chapter, it is said of this same one: « Have pity on my son, for he is a lunatic and suffereth much, and often falleth into the water and often into the fire »; whence such an infirmity was *sudden,* as is the infirmity of the arreptitious. And such was Saul, of whom it is said in the first of Kings, the sixteenth chapter[^p240-8], that « when the evil spirit of the Lord caught Saul, David struck the harp »; in which it is signified that the true David, Christ, was to tame the demoniac violence. — Nor was it only sudden, but also *afflicting;* whence he adds: *And he dasheth him and dissipateth him with foam;* which is a sign of great pain and affliction; whence he could already say[^p240-9]: « My bones are dissipated by the side of hell », because he was dissipated by the spirit of hell. — Nor was it only afflicting, but also *lasting;* on account of which he adds: *And he hardly departeth, rending him;* whence he could say that of Job, the nineteenth chapter[^p240-10]: « The robbers came together, and made themselves a way by me, and besieged my tabernacle round about ».

75. (Vers. 40.). And that this affliction was of great duration[^p240-11] appears from the difficulty of the cure; whence he adds: *And I desired thy disciples to cast him out, and they could not.* And the reason of this was that they could not by an earthly power, according to that of Job, the forty-first chapter[^p240-12]: « There is no power upon the earth » etc.; and then the power of divine efficacy was absent because of human unbelief. Whence in Matthew, the seventeenth chapter: « The disciples said to the Lord: Why could not we cast him out? But he answering said: Because of your unbelief ». But on the contrary, in Mark, the ninth chapter: « If thou canst believe, all things are possible to him that believeth ».

76. Now according to the *spiritual* intelligence, by this demoniac so vexed is understood the man who is possessed by *spiritual sin;* who *is seized* through *pride,* which first brings it about that the devil stretches out his hand upon a man, according to that of Job,
<!-- page 241 -->
the forty-first chapter[^p241-1]: « He is king over all the children of pride ». — Secondly, he *crieth out* through *boasting;* Isaias, the fifth chapter[^p241-2]: « I looked that he should do justice, and behold a cry ». Such were the Pharisees, according to that of Matthew, the twenty-third chapter: « They make broad their phylacteries, they enlarge their fringes, they love the first places at suppers » etc. — Thirdly, he *dasheth* through *envy,* according to that of Job, the thirtieth chapter[^p241-3]: « Thou hast lifted me up, and set me as it were upon the wind », through arrogance, « thou hast mightily dashed me », through envy, because, Job, the fifth chapter, « envy slayeth the little one ». — Fourthly, he *is dissipated* through *wrath,* which troubles all the thoughts, according to that of Job, the seventeenth chapter[^p241-4]: « My thoughts are dissipated, tormenting my heart »; and in the Psalm: « Dissipated and not compunct, they gnashed upon me with their teeth ». — Fifthly, he *rends* through *grief of heart,* by which he casts headlong into impatience, through which the conscience too is rent; whence Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p241-5]: « The sadness of the heart boweth down the neck » etc. — And hence it is that *he hardly departeth* on account of *perfidy;* Wisdom, the twelfth chapter: « Their nation is wicked, and their malice natural, and that the thought of their heart could not be changed for ever ».

77. (Vers. 41.). *And Jesus answering said to them*[^p241-6] etc. After the disposing opportunity the Evangelist secondly sets out *the effecting power,* which is described here as *equitable,* as *pious*[^tr-pietas] and as *great.*

First it is described as *equitable* in the reproof of the unbelieving heart, when he says: *O faithless and perverse generation!* so that *faithlessness* be in the cognition and *perversity* in the affection, according to that of Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter[^p241-7]: « For it is a perverse generation, and faithless children ». Faithlessness made them *foolish* and perversity *hard,* according to that of Ecclesiastes, the first chapter: « The perverse are hard to be corrected, and the number of fools is infinite »; therefore they are not only to be admonished, but also to be strongly rebuked. — Therefore he adds: *How long shall I be with you,* namely, doing you good things, *and suffer you?* namely, bearing your evil things; which he does not say out of impatience, since he is the one of whom it is said in Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p241-8]: « Who maketh his sun to rise upon the good and bad »; and above, the sixth chapter: « He is kind to the unthankful and to the evil ». But he says this so that by a hard rebuke he may drive out the hardness of heart, according to that of the Epistle to Titus, the first chapter: « Rebuke them hard, that they may be sound in the faith ».

78. Secondly it is described as *pious* in the calling of the possessed child, when he says: *Bring hither thy son,* namely to the presence of the Saviour, so that that of Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter[^p241-9], may now seem fulfilled: « They shall bring their sons in their arms, and carry their daughters upon their shoulders ». *Bring,* I say, by thy faith him who cannot come by his own faith, as those men brought the man sick of the palsy, of whom it is said in Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p241-10]: « Whose faith when Jesus saw, he said: Thy sins are forgiven thee ». Whence in Mark, the ninth chapter, the Lord said to that man: « If thou canst believe, all things are possible to him that believeth ». For faith is that by means of which one is led to Christ, because it is the way of coming to the light; whence the Apostle to the Hebrews, the eleventh chapter[^p241-11]: « He that cometh must believe, because without faith it is impossible to please God ».

79. (Vers. 42.). And because[^p241-12] « there is no concord of Christ with Belial, nor of light with darkness », therefore he adds: *And as he was coming, the devil dashed him and dissipated him;* in which it is shown that he came to the light unwillingly, as one doing evil, according to that of John, the third chapter: « He that doth evil hateth the light ». At the same time it is also shown with this that the devil most of all opposes himself lest anyone possessed by him be converted to Christ, according to what Bede says in the Gloss[^p241-13]: « Whence the boy, approaching the Lord, is dashed, because those converted to the Lord are for the most part beaten upon more heavily by the demon, that they may return to their vices ». An example of which we have in Exodus, the fourteenth chapter, of Pharao, who pursued the people as it withdrew.

80. (Vers. 43.). Thirdly it is described as *great* in the expulsion of the unclean spirit, when it is added: *And Jesus rebuked the unclean spirit,* as it were restraining him by a word alone, according to that above, the fourth chapter[^p241-14]: « Rebuking, he suffered them not to speak »; whence the Angel of the Lord said in Zacharias, the third chapter: « The Lord rebuke thee, Satan ». — And because by that rebuke the demon was cast out, there is subjoined: *And he healed the boy.* Whence that rebuke was salutary; on account of which Job, the fifth chapter[^p241-15]: « Refuse not the rebuke of the Lord, for he woundeth and cureth, he striketh, and his hands
<!-- page 242 -->
shall heal ». — And because the father's faith had merited this, therefore it is added: *And restored him to his father*[^p242-1], to soothe his sorrow, that he might say to his only son that of Jacob, Genesis, the forty-sixth chapter: « Now shall I die with joy, because I have seen thy face, and leave thee alive ».

81. Now by this deed the prelate is *spiritually* schooled how he ought to restore to the King of heaven a subject possessed by the devil through sin, namely *by rebuking* and *by healing: by rebuking* through justice and *by healing* through clemency. For there ought to be the rigour of justice with respect to the fault, but the sweetness of clemency with respect to the nature. Whence Augustine[^p242-2]: « Let correction be made with dilection of men in hatred of vices »; and Gregory[^p242-3]: « We owe strictness to vices and compassion to nature ». « For true justice has compassion, false justice indignation ». Whence also below, in the tenth chapter, it is said of the Samaritan curing the wounded man that he poured wine and oil into his wounds, the biting wine and the soothing oil, according to the two things which have been said.

82. (Vers. 44.). *And all were astonished* etc. After the disposing opportunity and the effecting power the Evangelist here subjoins *the consequent utility.* And this was twofold: the first *universal,* the second *particular.* The first was *the arousing of all to reverence of the majesty,* the second was *the solicitation of the disciples to the intelligence of the truth.*

First, then, as to *the arousing of all to reverence of the majesty* it is said: *And all were astonished at the greatness of God,* because great was the power which in so short a time, by so short a word, perfectly cured so great a disease. Whence they sang that of the Psalm[^p242-4]: « Great is our Lord, and great is his power »; and that of Jeremias, the tenth chapter: « Thou art great, O Lord, and great is thy name, and in all the kingdoms of the earth there is none like »; and Exodus, the fifteenth chapter: « Who is like to thee among the strong, O Lord? Terrible and praiseworthy and doing wonders ».

83. Secondly, as to *the solicitation of the disciples to the intelligence of the truth* it is added: *And while all wondered at all the things he did,* and also said and spoke[^p242-5]; *he said to his disciples: Put you in your hearts these words;* as if he said to them: while the others recognize and admire the greatness of the miracles, do you think on the humility of the reproaches to come, and « store up in mind the event of the precious blood, by which the world is to be redeemed, while the rest wonder only at the deeds of the divine loftiness[^p242-6] ». Whence *put,* that is, have in memory, according to that of the Canticle, the last chapter: « Put me as a seal upon thy heart ». And he joins the foretelling of the punishment to come after the showing of the miracle, lest the passion, coming suddenly, should altogether shake the disciples; whence Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter[^p242-7]: « In the day of good things be not unmindful of evils »; and lest anyone be exalted in the time of prosperity, according to that of Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter: « Boast not for to morrow, not knowing what the day to come may bring forth »; and therefore Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter: « In the day of thy honour be not exalted ». — These *words,* which he wills to be brought back to memory, are about *the passion;* whence he adds: *The Son*[^p242-8] *of man, it is to come to pass that he be delivered into the hands of men. Be delivered,* I say, out of *clemency* by God the Father; to the Romans, the eighth chapter: « He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all » etc. He will be delivered out of *obedience* by himself; Isaias, the fifty-third chapter[^p242-9]: « For that he hath delivered his soul unto death ». He will be delivered out of *malice* by Judas the traitor; Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter: « He that dippeth his hand with me in the dish, he shall deliver me ». And in this he implies the whole passion.

84. (Vers. 45.). And since this was a hidden mystery, therefore it is added: *But they were ignorant of this word, and it was veiled before them, so that they perceived it not;* because they were as yet *untaught* and *animal* and did not understand things past, and therefore not things to come either; whence Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter[^p242-10]: « He that is ignorant of things past cannot know things to come by any messenger ». — Nay more, because
<!-- page 243 -->
they were not yet *perfectly schooled,* therefore it is added: *And they were afraid to ask him concerning this word.* For they had not yet heard from him that of John, the fifteenth chapter[^p243-1]: « I will not now call you servants, for the servant knoweth not what his lord doth » etc. — Now of this ignorance there was a threefold reason. For it came partly from the love of charity, partly from faith in the Divinity, partly from the accustomed manner of speaking in parables. And this the Gloss of Bede touches[^p243-2]: « This ignorance » etc. But although the Lord knew that they were ignorant, he nevertheless foretold it to them, as he afterward recounted after the resurrection, when he rebuked them, below, the last chapter[^p243-3]: « O foolish and slow of heart to believe »! and when « he opened their understanding ». Whence he gives the reason of this in John, the fourteenth chapter: « This I have told you before it come to pass, that when it shall come to pass, you may believe ». Now he said it then so that, as has been said[^p243-4], he might teach us in all our exaltation to have memory of our death and humility; whence Ecclesiasticus, the seventh chapter: « Remember thy last end, and thou shalt never sin ».
