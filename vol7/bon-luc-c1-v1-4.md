## *Prologus Evangelistae*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 11 -->

### Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae
### Capitulum I.

### Prologus Evangelistae.

1. *Quoniam quidem multi* etc. Hic est secundus prologus, auctoris scilicet beati Lucae, quem suo praemittit Evangelio, in quo suam manifestat intentionem quantum ad quatuor, scilicet quantum ad *motivum, promotivum, directivum* et *consummativum,* in quibus sufficienter manifestatur principale intentum.

2. (Vers. 1.). Primo ergo manifestat intentionem suam quantum ad *motivum,* quod quidem fuit *aliorum exemplum;* et hoc notat, cum dicit: *Quoniam multi conati sunt ordinare narrationem.* Inter quos quidam fuerunt boni, sicut Matthaeus et Marcus, qui ante scripserunt, sicut habitum est in prologo Hieronymi[^p11-4]; quidam vero mali, sicut dicit Glossa Bedae, « ut Basilides et Appelles, et qui sub nomine Thomae et Mathiae et aliorum Apostolorum falsa scripserunt »; quia, sicut dicitur secundae ad Corinthios undecimo, « huiusmodi pseudoapostoli sunt operarii subdoli, transfigurantes se in Apostolos Christi ». Primi conati sunt et perfecerunt, secundi conati sunt et defecerunt. Primi moverunt exemplo boni et veri, secundi moverunt odio mali. Ad *ordinandum narrationem,* earum scilicet rerum, *quae completae sunt in nobis*[^p11-5], id est inter nos et propter nos, sicut mysterium incarnationis, passionis et resurrectionis. Unde Ioannis primo: « Et Verbum caro factum est et habitavit in nobis; et vidimus gloriam eius » etc.

3. (Vers. 2.). Secundo, quantum ad *promotivum,* quod quidem fuit *Apostolorum documentum;* et hoc notat, cum dicit: *Sicut tradiderunt nobis qui ab initio ipsi viderunt,* scilicet Apostoli; unde primae Ioannis primo[^p11-6]: « Quod audivimus, quod vidimus oculis nostris, quod perspeximus, et manus nostrae contrectaverunt » etc.; et Ioannis decimo nono: « Qui vidit testimonium perhibuit, et verum est testimonium eius. Et ille scit, quia vera dicit, ut et vos credatis ». Et quia viderant, ideo testificabantur; unde et subdit: *Et ministri fuerunt sermonis;* Actuum primo[^p11-7]: « Oportet ergo ex his viris,
<!-- page 12 -->
qui nobiscum sunt congregati in omni tempore, quo intravit et exivit inter nos Dominus Iesus, incipiens a baptismate Ioannis usque in diem, qua assumtus est a nobis, testem resurrectionis eius nobiscum fieri unum ex istis ». Et tales erant Apostoli; unde primae ad Corinthios quarto: « Sic nos existimet homo ut ministros Christi et dispensatores mysteriorum Dei; hic iam quaeritur » etc.

4. (Vers. 3.). Tertio, quantum ad *directivum,* quod fuit *ordinatus, sufficiens* et *diligens processus;* et hoc notat, cum subdit: *Visum est et mihi,* Spiritu sancto scilicet instigante; sicut dicit Hieronymus[^p12-1], « sacro instigante Spiritu, hoc scripsit Evangelium ». *Assecuto,* id est perfecte secuto, quia a principio usque in finem; unde et subdit: *Omnia diligenter ex ordine tibi scribere, optime Theophile;* ubi simul *sufficientia, ordo* et *diligentia;* et haec quidem competunt historico narratori. Unde secundi Machabaeorum secundo[^p12-2]: « Intellectum colligere et ordinare sermonem et curiosius partes singulas quasque inquirere, historiae competit auctori ».

5. (Vers. 4.). Quarto, quantum ad *consummativum,* quod fuit *veritatis intellectus,* subdit: *Ut cognoscas eorum verborum de quibus eruditus es veritatem,* cognoscas scilicet magis plene. Et hoc fuit, in quo quievit intentio Evangelistae scribentis; unde secundae ad Timotheum tertio[^p12-3]: « Omnis Scriptura divinitus inspirata utilis est ad docendum, ad arguendum, ad corripiendum, ad erudiendum in iustitia, ut perfectus sit homo Dei ad omne opus bonum instructus »; et hunc finem ponit Lucas respectu omnium, quos intelligit in persona Theophili, cui scribit. « *Theophilus* interpretatur amans Deum, vel amatus a Deo[^p12-4] », et talibus manifestatur veritas; unde Ioannis decimo quarto: « Qui autem diligit me diligetur a Patre meo; et ego diligam eum et manifestabo ei me ipsum »; et decimo quinto: « Vos autem dixi amicos, quia omnia, quaecumque audivi a Patre meo, nota feci vobis ». — Et sic patet, quam plene et plane aperit Evangelista intentionem suam.

6. Et quod Lucas evangelista praemittit suo Evangelio prologum, quod non fecit aliquis aliorum Evangelistarum, hoc fuit ratione specialis personae, cui scribit, cum omnes alii scribant generaliter sine ratione specialis causae[^p12-5]; suntque ad hoc tres causae, quarum prima est, quia liber suus posset videri superfluus, cum iam duo Evangelistae scripsissent, et « in ore duorum testium stet omne verbum[^p12-6] ». Unde ut hoc removeat, primo assignat primam causam, quia non est superfluus, cum sit ad vana, falsa et superflua resecanda, quae scripta erant per pseudoevangelistas. — Secunda est, quia liber suus posset videri minus authenticus, utpote eius qui non fuit Apostolus; et ideo ostendit, se scripsisse secundum Apostolorum documentum et testimonium et aliorum, qui viderunt. — Tertia causa est, quia liber eius, cum sit historicus et narrativus diversarum parabolarum, posset videri inordinatus et quasi casualiter conscriptus; et ideo ad hoc removendum ostendit, processum in prologo esse sufficientem, diligentem et ordinatum. — Et sic patet, quod non est curiosum inquirere et perscrutari ordinem, distinctionem et sufficientiam in prosecutione eorum quae in hoc Evangelio conscribuntur; pari ratione nec in aliis. Si ordo attestatur sapientiae et bonitati, illi scripturae potissime debet competere[^p12-7], quae procedit a fonte sapientiae et ducit ad finem bonitatis completae. Et haec est Scriptura evangelica; et ideo non est curiositas, sed utilitas, si describantur in sequentibus divisio, ordo et sufficientia, quia hoc intenditur ab Evangelista.

## English
<!-- page 11 -->

### Commentary on the Gospel of St Luke
### Chapter I.

### The Prologue of the Evangelist.

1. *Forasmuch as many* etc. This is the second prologue, namely the author's, blessed Luke's, which he prefixes to his Gospel, in which he manifests his intention as to four things, namely as to the *motive, the promotive, the directive* and *the consummative,* in which the principal intent is sufficiently manifested.

2. (Vers. 1.). First, then, he manifests his intention as to the *motive,* which indeed was *the example of others;* and he notes this when he says: *Forasmuch as many have taken in hand to set in order a narration.* Among whom some were good, as Matthew and Mark, who wrote before, as was had in the prologue of Jerome[^p11-4]; but some were bad, as the Gloss of Bede says, « as Basilides and Apelles, and those who under the name of Thomas and of Matthias and of other Apostles wrote false things »; because, as is said in the second to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter, « such false apostles are deceitful workmen, transforming themselves into the Apostles of Christ ». The first took it in hand and accomplished it, the second took it in hand and failed. The first moved by the example of the good and true, the second moved by hatred of the evil. *To set in order a narration,* namely of those things *which have been accomplished in us*[^p11-5], that is, among us and on account of us, as the mystery of the incarnation, of the passion and of the resurrection. Whence John, the first chapter: « And the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us; and we saw his glory » etc.

3. (Vers. 2.). Secondly, as to the *promotive,* which indeed was *the instruction of the Apostles;* and he notes this when he says: *According as they have delivered them unto us, who from the beginning themselves saw,* namely the Apostles; whence the first of John, the first chapter[^p11-6]: « That which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled » etc.; and John, the nineteenth chapter: « He that saw it hath given testimony, and his testimony is true. And he knoweth that he saith true, that you also may believe ». And because they had seen, therefore they testified; whence he also adds: *And were ministers of the word;* Acts, the first chapter[^p11-7]: « Wherefore of these men,
<!-- page 12 -->
who have been gathered together with us all the time that the Lord Jesus came in and went out among us, beginning from the baptism of John until the day wherein he was taken up from us, one of these must be made a witness with us of his resurrection ». And such were the Apostles; whence the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « Let a man so account of us as of the ministers of Christ and the dispensers of the mysteries of God; here now it is required » etc.

4. (Vers. 3.). Thirdly, as to the *directive,* which was an *ordered, sufficient* and *diligent process;* and he notes this when he adds: *It seemed good to me also,* namely at the instigation of the Holy Spirit; as Jerome[^p12-1] says, « at the instigation of the sacred Spirit he wrote this Gospel ». *Having attained,* that is, having perfectly followed, because from the beginning to the end; whence he also adds: *To write to thee all things diligently in order, most excellent Theophilus;* where there are at once *sufficiency, order* and *diligence;* and these indeed befit a historical narrator. Whence the second of Machabees, the second chapter[^p12-2]: « To gather the understanding and to set the discourse in order and more curiously to inquire into every particular part befits the author of a history ».

5. (Vers. 4.). Fourthly, as to the *consummative,* which was *the understanding of the truth,* he adds: *That thou mayest know the truth of those words in which thou hast been instructed,* mayest know, namely, more fully. And this was that in which the intention of the Evangelist in writing rested; whence the second to Timothy, the third chapter[^p12-3]: « All Scripture, inspired of God, is profitable to teach, to reprove, to correct, to instruct in justice, that the man of God may be perfect, furnished to every good work »; and Luke sets this end with respect to all whom he understands in the person of Theophilus, to whom he writes. « *Theophilus* is interpreted loving God, or loved by God[^p12-4] », and to such the truth is manifested; whence John, the fourteenth chapter: « And he that loveth me shall be loved of my Father; and I will love him and will manifest myself to him »; and the fifteenth chapter: « But I have called you friends, because all things whatsoever I have heard of my Father, I have made known to you ». — And thus it is plain how fully and plainly the Evangelist opens his intention.

6. And that Luke the evangelist prefixes a prologue to his Gospel, which none of the other Evangelists did, this was by reason of the special person to whom he writes, whereas all the others write generally, without the reason of a special cause[^p12-5]; and there are three causes for this, of which the first is that his book could seem superfluous, since two Evangelists had already written, and « in the mouth of two witnesses every word shall stand[^p12-6] ». Whence, to remove this, he first assigns the first cause, that it is not superfluous, since it is for cutting away the vain, false and superfluous things which had been written by pseudo-evangelists. — The second is that his book could seem less authentic, as being of one who was not an Apostle; and therefore he shows that he wrote according to the instruction and testimony of the Apostles and of others who saw. — The third cause is that his book, since it is historical and narrative of diverse parables, could seem disordered and as if written by chance; and therefore, to remove this, he shows that the process in the prologue is sufficient, diligent and ordered. — And thus it is plain that it is not curious to inquire into and scrutinize the order, distinction and sufficiency in the pursuit of those things which are written in this Gospel; by equal reason, nor in the others. If order attests to wisdom and goodness, it ought most of all to belong to that scripture[^p12-7] which proceeds from the fount of wisdom and leads to the end of completed goodness. And this is the gospel Scripture; and therefore it is not curiosity but utility if the division, order and sufficiency are described in what follows, because this is intended by the Evangelist.
