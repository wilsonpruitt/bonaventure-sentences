# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. V, Vers. 17-26
## *Et factum est in una dierum. Confirmatis discipulis per curationem leprosi, hic ad maiorem firmitatem confirmat eosdem per mirabilem sanationem paralytici*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 123 -->

40. *Et factum est in una dierum.* Confirmatis discipulis per curationem leprosi, hic ad maiorem firmitatem confirmat eosdem per mirabilem *sanationem paralytici.* Cuius admirabilem sanationem describit Evangelista hoc ordine. Primo namque introducitur *opportunitas miraculi faciendi;* secundo vero, *integritas miraculi perfecti,* ibi[^p123-7]: *Quorum fidem* etc.; tertio vero, *celebritas miraculi publicati,* ibi: *Et stupor apprehendit* etc.

*Opportunitas* autem *miraculi faciendi* colligitur ex quatuor, scilicet ex *multitudine populi assistentis,* ex *promptitudine virtutis sanantis,* ex *gravedine morbi opprimentis,* ex *sollicitudine fidei offerentis.*

41. (Vers. 17.). Primo ergo ad designandum *multitudinem populi assistentis,* cui communicanda erat veritatis doctrina, dicitur: *Et factum est in una dierum, et Iesus*[^p123-8] *sedebat docens; sedebat,* inquam, tanquam magister et doctor praecipuus propter multitudinem sapientiae, sicut Iob, vigesimo nono: « Si volebam ire ad eos, sedebam primus; cumque sederem quasi rex, circumstante exercitu, eram tamen moerentium consolator ». Hoc utique modo sedebat Christus in medio multorum; unde et subditur: *Et erant Pharisaei sedentes et Legis doctores.* Nam ipsi inter alios sunt excellentiores, *Pharisaei* in observando et *Legis doctores* in erudiendo. Et propterea etiam ipsi dicuntur *sedentes,* quia Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p123-9]: « Super cathedram Moysi sederunt scribae et Pharisaei ». — Et quia « in multitudine plebis gloria principis », ideo subdit, quod erant multi, cum ait: *Qui venerant ex omni castello Galilaeae et Iudaeae et Ierusalem.*

42. Et nota, quod in his tribus exprimit regionem *propinquam,* scilicet in *Galilaea;* unde et discipulos inde elegit, secundum quod dicitur Actuum secundo[^p123-10]: « Nonne omnes isti, qui loquuntur, Galilaei sunt »? — Exprimit et *praecipuam* in *Iudaea,*
<!-- page 124 -->
in qua habitabat tribus Iuda, cui promissus erat Christus, secundum illud Psalmi[^p124-1]: « Et tribum Ephraim non elegit, sed elegit tribum Iuda ». — Tangit et *civitatem regiam* in *Ierusalem,* ubi maxime habitabant doctores, secundum illud Isaiae secundo: « De Sion exibit lex, et verbum Domini de Ierusalem ». — Et signanter haec tria nominat, quia in Galilaea Christus *concipitur,* sicut supra primo[^p124-2]; in Iudaea *nascitur,* sicut supra secundo; sed in Ierusalem *crucifigitur,* sicut infra vigesimo tertio. Et ideo habitatoribus horum trium locorum erat Christus promissus, et ipsi eum exspectabant, et ideo ad ipsum conveniebant.

43. Secundo vero ad denotandam *promptitudinem virtutis liberantis* adiungit: *Et virtus Domini erat,* scilicet prompta, *ad sanandum eos,* scilicet infirmos, quia ipsi erant prompti ad audiendum. De qua virtute ad Hebraeos primo[^p124-3]: « Portans omnia verbo virtutis suae »; quae quidem *virtus* est Christus, qui est Verbum Patris; primae ad Corinthios primo: « Christum dicimus Dei virtutem et Dei sapientiam ». Et haec *virtus apta* est ad sanandum, non ad perdendum; et ideo nomen eius est *Iesus,* id est Salvator[^p124-4]. Unde anima sancta in Psalmo: « Domine Deus, virtus salutis meae, obumbrasti super caput meum in die belli » etc. Et ideo Actuum decimo: « Unxit eum Deus Spiritu sancto et virtute », id est virtute Spiritus sancti, « qui pertransiit benefaciendo et sanando omnes oppressos » Hanc virtutem *promptam* reddit fides credentium; unde infra sexto[^p124-5]: « Convenerant, ut audirent eum »; « quia virtus de illo exibat et sanabat omnes »; sed econtra dicitur de civitate sua Matthaei decimo tertio: « Non fecit ibi virtutes multas propter incredulitatem eorum ».

44. (Vers. 18.). Tertio vero ad ostendendam *gravedinem morbi opprimentis* adiungit: *Et ecce, viri portantes in lecto hominem, qui erat paralyticus;* et ita omnino impotens, quia paralysis omnino dissolvit membrorum virtutem, sicut legitur de Alcimo primi Machabaeorum nono[^p124-6]: « Occlusum est os eius, et dissolutus est paralysi nec ultra potuit loqui verbum ». Magna vere infirmitas et miseranda, quae motum tollit et verbum. *Et in lecto* portabatur, non, inquam, in lecto quietis, sed potius laboris et doloris; unde poterat de eo orari: « Dominus opem ferat illi super lectum doloris eius » etc. Propterea misericorditer agebant viri, qui eum portabant, secundum documentum Apostoli ad Galatas sexto[^p124-7]: « Alter alterius onera portate »; unde poterat eis competere illud Ecclesiastici quadragesimo quarto: « Hi sunt viri misericordiae, quorum pietates non defuerunt »; et iam verificatur in hoc facto illud Isaiae quadragesimo nono: « Afferent filios tuos in ulnis et filias tuas super humeros portabunt ».

45. Quarto vero ad insinuandam *sollicitudinem fidei offerentis* subiungit: *Et quaerebant eum inferre et ponere ante eum,* ut scilicet ex eius praesentia sanaretur. Unde volebant implere exhortationem Psalmi[^p124-8]: « Quaerite Dominum et confirmamini; quaerite faciem eius semper ». Nec immerito, quia Iob decimo septimo: « Libera me, Domine, et pone me iuxta te, et cuiusvis manus pugnet contra me ».

46. (Vers. 19.). Et quia quaerenti per fidem impedimenta frequenter occurrunt, ideo subditur: *Et non invenientes, qua parte inferrent eum*[^p124-9] *prae turba;* quae solet impedire adventum ad Christum, sicut dicitur Matthaei vigesimo et infra decimo octavo, quod « turbae increpabant caecum, ut taceret »; sic et Zachaeus, infra decimo nono, « quaerebat videre Iesum, et non poterat prae turba ». — Sed certe veram fidem impedimenta non retrahunt, sed accendunt; in cuius testimonium subditur: *Ascenderunt supra tectum et per tegulas submiserunt illum*[^p124-10] *cum lecto.* Sic et Zachaeus fecit, sicut dicitur infra decimo nono, quod « ascendit in arborem sycomorum, ut Iesum transeuntem videret », quem prae turba videre non poterat. — Talibus adiutoribus indigebat ille languidus, qui dicebat Ioannis quinto: « Domine, hominem non habeo, qui, cum turbata fuerit aqua, ponat me in piscinam ». Tales adiutores sunt viri *simplices,* secundum quod Actuum nono[^p124-11] dicitur: « Accipientes discipuli Paulum nocte, per murum dimiserunt eum, submittentes in sporta ». Tales sunt viri *spirituales,* qui infirmos per orationem Domini conspectui repraesentant; Threnorum secundo: « Effunde sicut aquam cor tuum ante conspectum Domini Dei tui. Leva manus tuas pro animabus parvulorum, qui defecerunt fame in capite omnium compitorum ». Et ideo praesentaverunt eum *conspectui* Domini, ut eum videret, et *situi,* ut eum tangeret. — Et propterea subditur: *In medium ante Iesum,* quia Salvator semper medium quaerit; unde Ioannis primo[^p124-12]: « Medius vestrum stetit »; et infra
<!-- page 125 -->
vigesimo secundo: « Ego autem in medio vestrum sum »; et ideo in Psalmo: « Suscepimus misericordiam tuam in medio templi tui ».

47. (Vers. 20.). *Quorum fidem ut vidit* etc. Praemissa est miraculi faciendi opportunitas; hic vero subiungitur ipsius in *faciendo integritas,* quia simul Christus infirmi corpus et animam curat: *animam* a peccato et *corpus* a morbo. Consistit autem huius miraculi perfecta integritas in quatuor, quae sunt supra virtutem humanam, scilicet in *remissione peccatorum, discretione cogitationum, exstirpatione*[^p125-1] *errorum* et *consolidatione membrorum paralyticorum.* Haec enim quatuor miraculum reddunt perfectum.

Praemittitur ergo *remissio peccatorum* ex merito fidei offerentium, cum dicit: *Quorum fidem ut vidit,* quia, sicut dicitur primi Regum decimo sexto[^p125-2], « homo videt ea quae parent, Dominus autem intuetur cor ». *Vidit,* inquam, oculo misericordiae, sicut Exodi tertio: « Vidi afflictionem populi mei, qui est in Aegypto ». Ad quam visionem fides disponit; Ieremiae quinto: « Domine, oculi tui respiciunt fidem ». — Et quoniam, sicut dicitur Proverbiorum decimo quinto, « per misericordiam et fidem purgantur peccata »; ideo subditur: *Dixit: Homo, remittuntur tibi peccata tua.* In hoc, quod dicit *homo,* invitat ad *considerationem propriae miseriae;* Iob decimo quarto[^p125-3]: « Homo natus de muliere, brevi vivens tempore, repletur multis miseriis »; et rursus vigesimo quinto: « Homo putredo, et filius hominis vermis ». In hoc, quod adiungit *remittuntur,* invitat ad *spem divinae gratiae;* ad Ephesios primo[^p125-4]: « Gratificavit nos in dilecto Filio suo, in quo habemus redemptionem per sanguinem eius, remissionem peccatorum »; unde dicitur primae Ioannis secundo: « Remittuntur vobis peccata vestra propter nomen eius ». Haec autem remissio peccatorum inchoata fuit a fide *aliena,* sed in *propria* consummatur; unde Beda[^p125-5]: « Multum valet unicuique fides propria, cum per alienam homo sit interius et exterius salvatus », sed hoc non sine propria subsequente; unde Matthaei nono dicitur paralytico: « Confide, fili, remittuntur tibi peccata ».

48. Et nota, quod peccata dicuntur *remitti* et dicuntur *dimitti,* dicuntur *mundari,* dicuntur *sanari,* dicuntur *tegi,* dicuntur *redimi.* — Dicuntur *remitti* quantum ad *reatum poenae;* infra septimo[^p125-6]: « Remittuntur ei peccata multa, quoniam dilexit multum ». — Dicuntur *dimitti* ratione *offensae;* Matthaei sexto: « Si dimiseritis hominibus peccata eorum, dimittet et vobis Pater caelestis delicta vestra ». — Dicuntur *mundari* ratione *maculae;* Psalmus[^p125-7]: « Amplius lava me ab iniquitate mea, et a peccato meo munda me »; et Isaiae sexto: « Auferetur iniquitas tua, et peccatum tuum mundabitur ». — Dicuntur *sanari* quantum ad *vulnerationem naturae;* unde Ioannis quinto[^p125-8]: « Ecce, iam sanus factus es, iam amplius noli peccare », dicitur languido; et in Psalmo: « Sana animam meam, quia peccavi tibi ». — Dicuntur *tegi* propter *spoliationem virtutis gratuitae;* Psalmus[^p125-9]: « Beati, quorum remissae sunt iniquitates, et quorum tecta sunt peccata »; quod fit per caritatem; primae Petri quarto: « Caritas operit multitudinem peccatorum ». — Dicuntur *redimi* quantum ad *captivitatem servitutis diabolicae;* Danielis quarto[^p125-10]: « Peccata eleemosynis redime »; et Psalmus: « Ex usuris et iniquitate redimet animas eorum ».

49. (Vers. 21.). Additur autem secundo *discretio cogitationum* in animo blasphemantium, cum dicitur: *Et coeperunt cogitare scribae et Pharisaei,* cogitationes vanas et noxias; ad Romanos primo[^p125-11]: « Evanuerunt in cogitationibus suis, et obscuratum est insipiens cor eorum ». Et sequitur cogitationis expressio, cum addit: *dicentes,* scilicet in cordibus suis, secundum illud Psalmi: « Quae dicitis in cordibus vestris in cubilibus vestris compungimini ». — *Quis est hic, qui loquitur blasphemiam*[^p125-12]*?* In qua cogitatione *despiciunt* Salvatorem quasi *vilem,* in hoc quod dicunt: *Quis est hic?* Ecclesiastici decimo tertio: « Pauper locutus est, et dicunt: Quis est hic »? contra illud quod dicitur Ecclesiastici undecimo: « Ne spernas hominem in visu suo ». — *Redarguunt* ut *peccatorem* in hoc, quod imponunt blasphemiam. Est autem *blasphemare* Deo iniuriam irrogare[^p125-13]; quod fit tripliciter: vel cum *attribuitur Deo* quod sibi non convenit; vel cum *removetur ab eo* quod sibi convenit; vel cum *creaturae attribuitur* quod proprie Deo convenit; unde quando Christus attribuebat sibi Divinitatem, credebant eum blasphemare, sicut Marci decimo quarto[^p125-14]: « Quid adhuc desideramus testes? Audistis blasphemiam »; Ioannis decimo Salvator dixit Iudaeis: « Quem Pater sanctificavit et
<!-- page 126 -->
misit in mundum, vos dicitis, quia blasphemas, quia dixi: Filius Dei sum »? Similiter credebant, eum blasphemare, quando attribuebat sibi proprium Dei, scilicet peccata remittere. Unde subditur: *Quis potest peccata remittere*[^p126-1] *nisi solus Deus?* Et in hoc verum dicunt; Isaiae quadragesimo tertio: « Ego sum qui deleo iniquitates tuas propter me ». Sed certe, dum ipsi Christo imponebant blasphemiam, in suis cordibus blasphemabant; Isaiae quadragesimo sexto: « Cui assimilastis me et adaequastis et comparastis et fecistis similem »?

50. (Vers. 22.). Et quoniam haec cogitando blasphemabant, ideo *deprehenduntur* et *arguuntur,* cum subditur: *Ut autem cognovit Iesus cogitationes eorum,* tanquam Deus, quia, Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio[^p126-2], « oculi Domini multo plus lucidiores sunt sole, circumspicientes omnes vias hominum et profundum abyssi et hominum corda intuentes ». Nec mirum, quia est Verbum Dei, de quo ad Hebraeos quarto: « Vivus est sermo Dei et efficax et penetrabilior omni gladio ancipiti et discretor cogitationum et intentionum cordis ». — Nec solum deprehendit et discernit, sed etiam *redarguit: Respondens dixit ad illos: Quid cogitatis mala*[^p126-3] *in cordibus vestris?* quasi dicat illud Isaiae primo: « Auferte malum cogitationum vestrarum ab oculis meis ». Ipse enim videbat et arguebat, Ieremiae quarto: « Usquequo demorabuntur in te cogitationes noxiae »? Tales enim sunt cogitationes perversae, de quibus Sapientiae primo: « Perversae enim cogitationes separant a Deo ». Ideo Isaiae vigesimo nono: « Vae! qui profundi estis corde, ut a Domino abscondatis consilium »; et sequitur: « Perversa est haec vestra cogitatio, quasi si lutum contra figulum cogitet, et dicat opus factori suo: Non me fecisti; et figmentum dicat factori suo: Non intelligis ». Sed certe potest dici de eis illud Sapientiae secundo[^p126-4]: « Haec cogitaverunt et erraverunt; excaecavit enim illos malitia eorum ».

51. (Vers. 23.). Adiungitur autem tertio *confutatio errorum* male cogitantium[^p126-5] in hoc quod sequitur: *Quid est facilius dicere* etc. Facit enim argumentum, in quo ostendit, se verum Deum esse et peccata posse dimittere, ac per hoc illos errare. Et est argumentum tale: omnis, qui potest paralyticum solo verbo liberare, potest solo verbo peccata dimittere[^p126-6]. Huius autem rationis praemittit *maioris* propositionis probationem, subiungit *assumtionem* et interponit *conclusionem. Maioris* propositionis probationem praemittit in hoc quod dicit: *Quid est facilius dicere: Dimittuntur tibi peccata, an dicere: Surge et ambula?* quasi dicat: aequalis facilitatis sunt: ergo qui potest unum potest et reliquum; et haec erat propositio *maior.* Unde Glossa super illud Matthaei nono[^p126-7]: « Per remissionem poenae remissionem culpae intellige ». Intelligitur autem hoc per illud tanquam per simile et per notius. Et hoc est quod dicitur Ieremiae trigesimo secundo: « Non erit tibi difficile omne verbum, qui facis misericordiam in millibus »; unde Sapientiae undecimo: « Parcis autem omnibus, Domine, quia omnia potes ».

52. (Vers. 24.). *Conclusionem* autem interponit in hoc quod adiungit: *Ut autem sciatis, quia Filius hominis habet potestatem in terra dimittendi peccata.* Hoc enim est, quod intendit concludere finaliter; et est verum secundum testimonium Ioannis primo[^p126-8]: « Ecce, Agnus Dei; ecce, qui tollit peccata mundi »; et Apocalypsis primo: « Dilexit nos et lavit nos a peccatis nostris in sanguine suo ». — *Assumtionem* subiungit, cum addit: *Ait paralytico: Tibi dico: Surge, tolle grabatum*[^p126-9] *tuum* etc. — Et nota, quod verba praecedentia sunt Salvatoris et sequentia; sed hoc verbum, quod dicit: *Ait paralytico,* est verbum Evangelistae, quod interponit, quia prima verba erant Domini ad Pharisaeos, scilicet *propositio* et *conclusio;* sed *assumtionem* dixit, convertendo sermonem ad paralyticum, cui imperat actum, qui non potest competere nisi sano, scilicet *surgere,* cum dicit: *Surge;* sicut Petrus claudo ait Actuum tertio[^p126-10]: « In nomine Iesu Christi Nazareni surge et ambula ».

53. Praecipit tollere grabatum, cum ait: *Tolle grabatum tuum. Grabatum* autem est lectus pauperum infirmorum, ubi nihil sternitur nisi ad caput; unde dicitur a *graba,* quod est caput. Sic Ioannis quinto[^p126-11] praecipit illi languido triginta octo annorum:
<!-- page 127 -->
« Surge, tolle grabatum et ambula ». — Praecipit ambulare et in domum ire, cum subdit: *Et ambula*[^p127-1] *et vade in domum tuam,* ut scilicet cum tuis congratuleris, sicut Marci quinto: « Vade in domum tuam ad tuos et annuntia illis, quanta tibi Dominus fecerit ». — In his tribus includitur perfecta sanitas contra tria mala, quae facit aegritudo paralysis. Reddit enim inhabilem ad *standum* et inhabiliorem ad *operandum* et inhabilissimum ad *ambulandum;* et econtra imperat[^p127-2] *rectitudinem status, fortitudinem actus* et *promptitudinem motus;* quibus obtentis, efficitur perfecte sanus.

54. (Vers. 25.). Subiungitur ultimo *consolidatio*[^p127-3] *membrorum languentium* in hoc quod sequitur: *Et confestim consurgens coram illis* etc. Consolidata enim sunt verbo Christi membra paralytici quantum ad virtutem *sustentativam, operativam* et *motivam.* Surrexit enim, iam non indigens sustentamento lectuli aut baculi, et hoc virtute manus Dei omnipotentis; Danielis decimo[^p127-4]: « Ecce, manus tetigit me et erexit me super genua mea et super articulos manuum mearum ». — *Tulit lectum, in quo iacebat;* iam non indigens ab alio portari, et sic videtur adimpletum illud Amos tertio: « Eruentur filii Israel, qui habitant in Samaria, in plaga lectuli et Damasci grabato ». — *Et abiit in domum suam, magnificans Deum;* iam valens faciliter moveri, sicut et ille claudus a Petro curatus, Actuum tertio[^p127-5], « intravit cum eis in templum ambulans et exsiliens et laudans Deum ». Unde magnificabat Deum et ipsum laudando et eius miracula enarrando et ad laudandum alios invitando; unde poterat dicere: « Magnificate Dominum mecum, et exaltemus nomen eius in id ipsum ». Poterat etiam cantare cum Virgine: « Magnificat anima mea Dominum. Et exsultavit spiritus meus » etc.

55. (Vers. 26.). *Et stupor apprehendit omnes* etc. Hic tertio post miraculi faciendi opportunitatem et facti integritatem subiungit eiusdem *publicati*[^p127-6] *celebritatem,* per quam clarificata est divina virtus in Christo, quoniam ex praesentis miraculi consideratione ostensa est Christi virtus *admirabilis* et *venerabilis, formidabilis* et *praedicabilis.*

56. *Admirabilis,* inquam, ostensa est ex impossibilitate *comprehendendi;* propter quod dicit: *Et stupor apprehendit omnes,* id est *innocentes* ad *credendum;* Iob decimo septimo[^p127-7]: « Stupebunt iusti super hoc, et innocens contra hypocritam suscitabitur »; nam Pharisaei hypocritae sunt confusi, et Christus admirabilis est effectus. *Apprehendit malignantes* ad *confundendum;* Iob duodecimo: « Adducit consiliarios in stultum finem et iudices in stuporem ».

57. *Venerabilis* vero ostenditur ex impossibilitate *imitandi;* propter quod dicitur: *Et magnificabant Deum,* scilicet in opere suo, ut possent dicere illud Psalmi[^p127-8]: « Quam magnificata sunt opera tua, Domine! Omnia in sapientia fecisti »; et Isaiae trigesimo tertio: « Magnificatus est Dominus, quoniam habitavit in excelso et implevit Sion iustitia et iudicio ». Sic illi tres pueri ex aspectu miraculi; Danielis tertio: « Hi tres uno ore laudabant et magnificabant et benedicebant Deum ».

58. *Formidabilis* vero ostenditur virtus in Christo ex impossibilitate *evadendi;* unde additur: *Et repleti sunt timore,* considerantes immensitatem potentiae, quam nemo potest effugere; infra duodecimo[^p127-9]: « Ostendam vobis, quem timeatis; timete eum qui, postquam occiderit, habet potestatem mittere in gehennam ». Hanc potentiam timebat Iob, trigesimo primo: « Semper quasi tumentes fluctus super me timui Deum, et pondus eius ferre non potui ».

59. *Praedicabilis* autem ostenditur ex impossibilitate *contradicendi;* propter quod subditur: *Dicentes: Quia vidimus mirabilia hodie;* et iam non est locus contradicendi; unde Actuum quarto[^p127-10]: « Videntes Petri constantiam et Ioannis, admirabantur et cognoscebant eos, quoniam cum Iesu fuerant; hominem quoque videntes, qui fuerat curatus, nihil poterant contradicere »; unde Petrus et Ioannes dicebant Actuum quarto: « Non possumus quae audivimus et vidimus non loqui ». — Qui ergo post visionem horum signorum Domino contradicit excluditur a populo Dei et hereditate regni, secundum illud Numerorum decimo quarto[^p127-11]: « Homines, qui viderunt maiestatem meam et signa, quae feci in Aegypto, et tentaverunt me non videbunt terram, pro qua iuravi patribus eorum ».

60. *Spiritualiter* autem tria sunt hic attendenda: quis sit hic *morbus,* qui curatur; quae
<!-- page 128 -->
*domus,* in qua fit curatio; et quis curandi *modus.* — *Morbus* iste *paralysis* designat culpam originalem, sicut *lepra* actualem. Unde *paralysis* aufert *sensum* et *motum;* sic originale aufert *sensum* cognitionis per ignorantiam; Tobiae quinto[^p128-1]: « Quale mihi gaudium est, qui in tenebris sedeo et lumen caeli non video »? Aufert etiam *motum rectum* per concupiscentiam, quae reddit tremulum sicut paralysis; ad Romanos septimo: « Velle adiacet mihi, perficere autem non invenio »; unde et subditur: « Video aliam legem in membris meis, repugnantem legi mentis meae » etc. Unde Hugo[^p128-2]: « Vitium humanae corruptionis dupliciter naturam infecit humanam, scilicet *ignorantia* mentem et *concupiscentia* carnem ». De his duobus in Psalmo: « Dereliquit me virtus mea, et lumen oculorum meorum »; et Exodi decimo: « Factae sunt tenebrae in Aegypto, et tribus diebus non est motus homo de loco suo ».

61. *Domus,* in qua fit curatio, est Ecclesia, de qua Proverbiorum nono[^p128-3]: « Sapientia aedificavit sibi domum, excidit columnas septem »; hae *columnae* sunt septem Sacramenta. Huius domus *ianua* est potestas sacerdotalis, cuius potestatis auctoritas residet penes Petrum; unde Matthaei decimo sexto: « Tibi dabo claves regni caelorum ». Sed quando haec ianua impletur turbis terrenarum sollicitudinum, tunc non praebet aditum ad Iesum, quia non curant de animabus, quae sunt Christi, sed de promotione proprii commodi; ad Philippenses secundo[^p128-4]: « Omnes quae sua sunt quaerunt, non quae Iesu Christi ».

62. *Modus* curandi est per alienum suffragium et divinum imperium. Quantum ad curationem ab originali concurrit virtus alienae fidei et virtus divini Sacramenti. Unde *portitores* designant patrinos, qui offerunt; Isaiae quadragesimo nono[^p128-5]: « Afferent filios tuos in ulnis et filias tuas super humeros portabunt ». *Christus* vero, imperio sanans, designat Sacramenti officium, per quod fit *remissio culpae,* cum dicitur: *Remittuntur tibi peccata;* Actuum secundo[^p128-6]: « Baptizetur unusquisque vestrum in remissionem peccatorum ». Fit etiam *curatio sequelae;* propter quod dicit: *Surge et tolle grabatum* etc.; Actuum duodecimo Angelus dixit ad Petrum iacentem in compedibus: « Surge velociter. Et confestim ceciderunt catenae de manibus eius ». *Surge,* id est, sursum te erige ad videndum lumen, contra *ignorantiam;* ad Ephesios quinto[^p128-7]: « Surge, qui dormis, et exsurge a mortuis, et illuminabit te Christus »; Isaiae sexagesimo: « Surge, illuminare Ierusalem, quia venit lumen tuum ». — *Tolle grabatum tuum,* contra *infirmitatem;* Matthaei undecimo[^p128-8]: « Venite ad me, omnes, qui laboratis »; et post: « Tollite iugum meum super vos ». — *Et ambula,* contra *malitiam;* Genesis decimo septimo: « Ambula coram me, et esto perfectus »; ad Ephesios quinto: « Ambulate in dilectione, sicut et Christus dilexit nos » etc. — *Vade in domum,* contra *concupiscentiam;* Ecclesiastici trigesimo secundo[^p128-9]: « Praecurre autem prior in domum et illic age conceptiones tuas et non in delictis ». In domum *suam* vadit qui amat bona interiora; Sapientiae octavo: « Intrans in domum meam conquiescam » etc.; in domum *alienam,* qui concupiscit mundana; Ecclesiastici vigesimo nono: « Vita nequam hospitandi de domo in domum ».

## English

40. *And it came to pass on a certain day.* The disciples having been confirmed by the cure of the leper, here, for greater firmness, he confirms the same by the marvellous *healing of the paralytic.* Whose wonderful healing the Evangelist describes in this order. For first is introduced the *opportunity of the miracle to be done;* but second, the *integrity of the miracle accomplished,* there[^p123-7]: *Whose faith* etc.; but third, the *renown of the miracle published,* there: *And astonishment seized* etc.

Now the *opportunity of the miracle to be done* is gathered from four things, namely from the *multitude of the people standing by,* from the *readiness of the healing power,* from the *heaviness of the oppressing disease,* from the *solicitude of the faith of the one offering.*

41. (Vers. 17.). First, then, to designate the *multitude of the people standing by,* to whom the doctrine of truth was to be communicated, it is said: *And it came to pass on a certain day, and Jesus*[^p123-8] *sat teaching; he sat,* I say, as master and chief doctor on account of the multitude of his wisdom, as Job, the twenty-ninth chapter: « If I would go to them, I sat first; and when I sat as a king, with his army standing about him, yet I was the comforter of them that mourned ». In this manner surely Christ sat in the midst of many; whence it is also subjoined: *And there were Pharisees sitting and doctors of the Law.* For these are more excellent among the others, the *Pharisees* in observing and the *doctors of the Law* in schooling. And on this account they too are said to be *sitting,* because, Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p123-9]: « Upon the chair of Moses have sitten the scribes and the Pharisees ». — And because « in the multitude of the people is the glory of the prince », therefore he subjoins that they were many, when he says: *Who were come out of every town of Galilee, and Judea and Jerusalem.*

42. And note that in these three he expresses the *neighbouring* region, namely in *Galilee;* whence also he chose his disciples from there, according to what is said in the Acts, the second chapter[^p123-10]: « Are not all these, that speak, Galileans »? — He expresses also the *chief* region, in *Judea,*
<!-- page 124 -->
in which dwelt the tribe of Juda, to whom Christ had been promised, according to that of the Psalm[^p124-1]: « And he chose not the tribe of Ephraim, but he chose the tribe of Juda ». — He touches also the *royal city* in *Jerusalem,* where the doctors chiefly dwelt, according to that of Isaias, the second chapter: « Out of Sion shall go forth the law, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem ». — And he names these three pointedly, because in Galilee Christ *is conceived,* as above, the first chapter[^p124-2]; in Judea he *is born,* as above, the second chapter; but in Jerusalem he *is crucified,* as below, the twenty-third chapter. And therefore Christ had been promised to the inhabitants of these three places, and they were expecting him, and therefore they came together to him.

43. But second, to denote the *readiness of the freeing power,* he adds: *And the power of the Lord was,* namely ready, *to heal them,* namely the sick, because they themselves were ready to hear. Of which power to the Hebrews, the first chapter[^p124-3]: « Upholding all things by the word of his power »; which *power* indeed is Christ, who is the Word of the Father; the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter: « We call Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God ». And this *power is apt* for healing, not for destroying; and therefore his name is *Jesus,* that is, Saviour[^p124-4]. Whence the holy soul in the Psalm: « O Lord God, the power of my salvation: thou hast overshadowed my head in the day of battle » etc. And therefore Acts, the tenth chapter: « God anointed him with the Holy Spirit and with power », that is, with the power of the Holy Spirit, « who went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed » This power the faith of believers renders *ready;* whence below, the sixth chapter[^p124-5]: « They had come together, that they might hear him »; « for power went out from him, and healed all »; but on the contrary it is said of his own city, Matthew, the thirteenth chapter: « He wrought not many powers there, because of their unbelief ».

44. (Vers. 18.). But third, to show the *heaviness of the oppressing disease,* he adds: *And behold, men carrying in a bed a man who was paralytic;* and so wholly powerless, because paralysis wholly dissolves the power of the members, as is read of Alcimus in the first of the Machabees, the ninth chapter[^p124-6]: « His mouth was stopped, and he was dissolved by paralysis, nor could he any more speak a word ». A great infirmity truly and pitiable, which takes away movement and word. *And in a bed* he was carried, not, I say, in a bed of quiet, but rather of labour and pain; whence it could be prayed concerning him: « The Lord help him on his bed of sorrow » etc. On this account the men who were carrying him acted mercifully, according to the instruction of the Apostle to the Galatians, the sixth chapter[^p124-7]: « Bear ye one another's burdens »; whence there could befit them that of Ecclesiasticus, the forty-fourth chapter: « These were men of mercy, whose godly deeds have not failed »; and now there is verified in this deed that of Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter: « They shall bring thy sons in their arms, and carry thy daughters upon their shoulders ».

45. But fourth, to intimate the *solicitude of the faith of the one offering,* he subjoins: *And they sought to bring him in, and to lay him before him,* namely that he might be healed by his presence. Whence they wished to fulfil the exhortation of the Psalm[^p124-8]: « Seek ye the Lord, and be strengthened: seek his face evermore ». Not without merit, because, Job, the seventeenth chapter: « Deliver me, O Lord, and set me beside thee, and let any man's hand fight against me ».

46. (Vers. 19.). And because hindrances frequently meet one who seeks through faith, therefore it is subjoined: *And not finding by what way they might bring him in*[^p124-9] *because of the multitude;* which is wont to hinder coming to Christ, as is said in Matthew, the twentieth chapter, and below, the eighteenth chapter, that « the multitudes rebuked the blind man, that he should hold his peace »; so too Zacheus, below, the nineteenth chapter, « sought to see Jesus, and could not for the multitude ». — But surely hindrances do not hold back true faith, but kindle it; in testimony of which it is subjoined: *They went up upon the roof, and let him down through the tiles*[^p124-10] *with his bed.* So too Zacheus did, as is said below, the nineteenth chapter, that « he climbed up into a sycamore tree, that he might see Jesus passing by », whom he could not see for the multitude. — Of such helpers that languishing man had need who said, John, the fifth chapter: « Sir, I have no man, when the water is troubled, to put me into the pond ». Such helpers are *simple* men, according to what is said in the Acts, the ninth chapter[^p124-11]: « The disciples taking Paul by night, conveyed him away by the wall, letting him down in a basket ». Such are *spiritual* men, who by prayer present the sick to the sight of the Lord; Lamentations, the second chapter: « Pour out thy heart like water before the sight of the Lord thy God. Lift up thy hands for the souls of the little ones, that have fainted for hunger at the top of all the streets ». And therefore they presented him to the *sight* of the Lord, that he might see him, and to his *position,* that he might touch him. — And on this account it is subjoined: *Into the midst before Jesus,* because the Saviour always seeks the midst; whence John, the first chapter[^p124-12]: « There hath stood one in the midst of you »; and below,
<!-- page 125 -->
the twenty-second chapter: « But I am in the midst of you »; and therefore in the Psalm: « We have received thy mercy in the midst of thy temple ».

47. (Vers. 20.). *Whose faith when he saw* etc. The opportunity of the miracle to be done has been set forth first; but here is subjoined its *integrity in the doing,* because Christ at once cures the body and the soul of the sick man: the *soul* from sin and the *body* from disease. Now the perfect integrity of this miracle consists in four things, which are above human power, namely in the *remission of sins, the discernment of thoughts, the extirpation*[^p125-1] *of errors* and the *consolidation of the paralysed members.* For these four render the miracle perfect.

First, then, the *remission of sins* is set forth, from the merit of the faith of those offering, when he says: *Whose faith when he saw,* because, as is said in the first of Kings, the sixteenth chapter[^p125-2], « man seeth those things that appear, but the Lord beholdeth the heart ». *He saw,* I say, with the eye of mercy, as in Exodus, the third chapter: « I have seen the affliction of my people which is in Egypt ». For which seeing faith disposes; Jeremias, the fifth chapter: « O Lord, thy eyes look upon faith ». — And since, as is said in Proverbs, the fifteenth chapter, « by mercy and faith sins are purged away »; therefore it is subjoined: *He said: Man, thy sins are remitted thee.* In that he says *man,* he invites to the *consideration of one's own misery;* Job, the fourteenth chapter[^p125-3]: « Man born of a woman, living for a short time, is filled with many miseries »; and again, the twenty-fifth chapter: « Man is rottenness, and the son of man a worm ». In that he adds *are remitted,* he invites to the *hope of divine grace;* to the Ephesians, the first chapter[^p125-4]: « He hath graced us in his beloved Son, in whom we have redemption through his blood, the remission of sins »; whence it is said in the first of John, the second chapter: « Your sins are remitted you for his name's sake ». Now this remission of sins was begun from *another's* faith, but is consummated in *one's own;* whence Bede[^p125-5]: « Much avails to each one his own faith, when through another's a man has been saved inwardly and outwardly », but this not without his own following after; whence in Matthew, the ninth chapter, it is said to the paralytic: « Be of good heart, son, thy sins are remitted thee ».

48. And note that sins are said to be *remitted* and are said to be *forgiven,* are said to be *cleansed,* are said to be *healed,* are said to be *covered,* are said to be *redeemed.* — They are said to be *remitted* as regards the *guilt of punishment;* below, the seventh chapter[^p125-6]: « Many sins are remitted her, because she hath loved much ». — They are said to be *forgiven* by reason of the *offence;* Matthew, the sixth chapter: « If you forgive men their sins, your heavenly Father will forgive you also your offences ». — They are said to be *cleansed* by reason of the *stain;* the Psalm[^p125-7]: « Wash me yet more from my iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin »; and Isaias, the sixth chapter: « Thy iniquity shall be taken away, and thy sin shall be cleansed ». — They are said to be *healed* as regards the *wounding of nature;* whence John, the fifth chapter[^p125-8]: « Behold, now thou art made whole; now sin no more », it is said to the languishing man; and in the Psalm: « Heal my soul, for I have sinned against thee ». — They are said to be *covered* because of the *despoiling of gratuitous virtue;* the Psalm[^p125-9]: « Blessed are they whose iniquities are remitted, and whose sins are covered »; which is done through charity; the first of Peter, the fourth chapter: « Charity covereth a multitude of sins ». — They are said to be *redeemed* as regards the *captivity of diabolical servitude;* Daniel, the fourth chapter[^p125-10]: « Redeem sins with alms »; and the Psalm: « From usuries and iniquity he shall redeem their souls ».

49. (Vers. 21.). Now second is added the *discernment of thoughts* in the mind of the blasphemers, when it is said: *And the scribes and Pharisees began to think,* thoughts vain and noxious; to the Romans, the first chapter[^p125-11]: « They became vain in their thoughts, and their foolish heart was darkened ». And there follows the expression of the thought, when he adds: *saying,* namely in their hearts, according to that of the Psalm: « The things you say in your hearts, be sorry for them upon your beds ». — *Who is this that speaketh blasphemy*[^p125-12]*?* In which thought they *despise* the Saviour as *vile,* in that they say: *Who is this?* Ecclesiasticus, the thirteenth chapter: « The poor man spoke, and they say: Who is this »? against that which is said in Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter: « Despise not a man for his look ». — They *convict* him as a *sinner* in that they impute blasphemy. Now to *blaspheme* is to inflict injury on God[^p125-13]; which is done in three ways: either when there is *attributed to God* what does not befit him; or when there is *removed from him* what befits him; or when there is *attributed to a creature* what properly befits God; whence when Christ attributed Divinity to himself, they believed him to blaspheme, as Mark, the fourteenth chapter[^p125-14]: « What need we any further witnesses? You have heard the blasphemy »; in John, the tenth chapter, the Saviour said to the Jews: « Whom the Father hath sanctified and
<!-- page 126 -->
sent into the world, you say: Thou blasphemest, because I said: I am the Son of God »? Likewise they believed him to blaspheme when he attributed to himself what is proper to God, namely to remit sins. Whence it is subjoined: *Who can remit sins*[^p126-1] *but God alone?* And in this they say what is true; Isaias, the forty-third chapter: « I am he that blot out thy iniquities for my own sake ». But surely, while they imputed blasphemy to Christ, they blasphemed in their own hearts; Isaias, the forty-sixth chapter: « To whom have you likened me, and made me equal, and compared me, and made me like »?

50. (Vers. 22.). And since they blasphemed in thinking these things, therefore they *are detected* and *are reproved,* when it is subjoined: *But when Jesus knew their thoughts,* as God, because, Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third chapter[^p126-2], « the eyes of the Lord are far brighter than the sun, beholding round about all the ways of men, and the bottom of the deep, and looking into the hearts of men ». No wonder, because he is the Word of God, of whom to the Hebrews, the fourth chapter: « The word of God is living and effectual, and more piercing than any two-edged sword; and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart ». — And not only does he detect and discern, but he also *convicts: Answering he said to them: Why do you think evil things*[^p126-3] *in your hearts?* as if he were saying that of Isaias, the first chapter: « Take away the evil of your devices from my eyes ». For he was seeing and reproving, Jeremias, the fourth chapter: « How long shall hurtful thoughts abide in thee »? For such are perverse thoughts, of which Wisdom, the first chapter: « For perverse thoughts separate from God ». Therefore Isaias, the twenty-ninth chapter: « Woe to you that are deep of heart, to hide your counsel from the Lord »; and there follows: « This thought of yours is perverse, as if the clay should think against the potter, and the work should say to the maker thereof: Thou madest me not; and the thing framed should say to him that fashioned it: Thou understandest not ». But surely there can be said of them that of Wisdom, the second chapter[^p126-4]: « These things they thought, and were deceived: for their own malice blinded them ».

51. (Vers. 23.). Now third is added the *confutation of the errors* of those thinking evilly[^p126-5] in that which follows: *Which is easier to say* etc. For he makes an argument in which he shows that he is true God and can forgive sins, and by this that they err. And the argument is such: everyone who can free a paralytic by a word alone can forgive sins by a word alone[^p126-6]. Now of this reasoning he sets first the proof of the *major* proposition, subjoins the *assumption,* and interposes the *conclusion.* He sets first the proof of the *major* proposition in that he says: *Which is easier to say: Thy sins are forgiven thee, or to say: Arise and walk?* as if he were saying: they are of equal ease: therefore he who can do the one can do the other also; and this was the *major* proposition. Whence the Gloss on that of Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p126-7]: « By the remission of the punishment understand the remission of the fault ». Now this is understood through that as through what is like and better known. And this is what is said in Jeremias, the thirty-second chapter: « No word shall be hard to thee, who showest mercy unto thousands »; whence Wisdom, the eleventh chapter: « But thou sparest all, O Lord, because thou canst do all things ».

52. (Vers. 24.). Now he interposes the *conclusion* in that he adds: *But that you may know that the Son of man hath power on earth to forgive sins.* For this is what he intends to conclude finally; and it is true according to the testimony of John, the first chapter[^p126-8]: « Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who taketh away the sins of the world »; and the Apocalypse, the first chapter: « He hath loved us, and washed us from our sins in his own blood ». — He subjoins the *assumption,* when he adds: *He saith to the paralytic: I say to thee: Arise, take up thy pallet*[^p126-9] etc. — And note that the preceding words are the Saviour's, and the following; but this word that he says, *He saith to the paralytic,* is the word of the Evangelist, which he interposes, because the first words were the Lord's to the Pharisees, namely the *proposition* and the *conclusion;* but the *assumption* he spoke turning his speech to the paralytic, on whom he enjoins an act that cannot belong except to one healthy, namely *to arise,* when he says: *Arise;* as Peter says to the lame man, Acts, the third chapter[^p126-10]: « In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, arise and walk ».

53. He enjoins him to take up his pallet, when he says: *Take up thy pallet. Grabatum* now is the bed of poor sick men, where nothing is spread except at the head; whence it is named from *graba,* which is the head. So in John, the fifth chapter[^p126-11], he enjoins on that languishing man of thirty-eight years:
<!-- page 127 -->
« Arise, take up thy pallet, and walk ». — He enjoins him to walk and to go into his house, when he subjoins: *And walk*[^p127-1] *and go into thy house,* namely that thou mayest rejoice together with thine own, as in Mark, the fifth chapter: « Go into thy house to thine own, and announce to them how great things the Lord hath done for thee ». — In these three is included perfect health against the three evils which the sickness of paralysis causes. For it renders one unable for *standing,* and more unable for *working,* and most unable for *walking;* and on the contrary he commands[^p127-2] *rightness of stance, strength of act* and *readiness of motion;* which being obtained, he is made perfectly sound.

54. (Vers. 25.). Lastly is subjoined the *consolidation*[^p127-3] *of the languishing members* in that which follows: *And immediately rising up before them* etc. For the members of the paralytic were consolidated by the word of Christ as regards the *sustaining, operative* and *motive* power. For he arose, no longer needing the support of a couch or of a staff, and this by the power of the hand of almighty God; Daniel, the tenth chapter[^p127-4]: « Behold, a hand touched me, and lifted me up upon my knees, and upon the joints of my hands ». — *He took up the bed on which he lay;* no longer needing to be carried by another, and so there seems fulfilled that of Amos, the third chapter: « The children of Israel shall be taken out, that dwell in Samaria, in the corner of a couch, and in the pallet of Damascus ». — *And he went away to his own house, magnifying God;* now able to move easily, as that lame man too, cured by Peter, Acts, the third chapter[^p127-5], « went in with them into the temple, walking, and leaping, and praising God ». Whence he magnified God both by praising him and by recounting his miracles and by inviting others to praise; whence he could say: « Magnify the Lord with me, and let us extol his name together ». He could also sing with the Virgin: « My soul doth magnify the Lord. And my spirit hath exulted » etc.

55. (Vers. 26.). *And astonishment seized all* etc. Here third, after the opportunity of the miracle to be done and the integrity of the miracle done, he subjoins the *renown* of the same *published*[^p127-6], through which the divine power in Christ was glorified, since from the consideration of the present miracle the power of Christ was shown *wonderful* and *venerable, formidable* and *worthy to be preached.*

56. *Wonderful,* I say, it was shown from the impossibility of *comprehending;* on account of which he says: *And astonishment seized all,* that is, the *innocent* for *believing;* Job, the seventeenth chapter[^p127-7]: « The just shall be astonished at this, and the innocent shall be raised up against the hypocrite »; for the Pharisees, hypocrites, were confounded, and Christ was made wonderful. *It seized the malignant* for *confounding;* Job, the twelfth chapter: « He bringeth counsellors to a foolish end, and judges into astonishment ».

57. *Venerable,* on the other hand, it is shown from the impossibility of *imitating;* on account of which it is said: *And they magnified God,* namely in his work, so that they could say that of the Psalm[^p127-8]: « How magnified are thy works, O Lord! Thou hast made all things in wisdom »; and Isaias, the thirty-third chapter: « The Lord is magnified, for he hath dwelt on high; he hath filled Sion with justice and judgement ». So those three children at the sight of the miracle; Daniel, the third chapter: « These three as with one mouth praised, and magnified, and blessed God ».

58. *Formidable,* on the other hand, the power in Christ is shown from the impossibility of *escaping;* whence it is added: *And they were filled with fear,* considering the immensity of the power which no one can flee; below, the twelfth chapter[^p127-9]: « I will show you whom you shall fear: fear ye him, who after he hath killed, hath power to cast into hell ». This power Job feared, the thirty-first chapter: « For always, as waves swelling over me, I feared God, and his weight I was not able to bear ».

59. *Worthy to be preached,* now, it is shown from the impossibility of *contradicting;* on account of which it is subjoined: *Saying: We have seen marvellous things to-day;* and now there is no place for contradicting; whence Acts, the fourth chapter[^p127-10]: « Seeing the constancy of Peter and of John, they wondered; and they knew them, that they had been with Jesus; seeing the man also who had been healed, they could contradict nothing »; whence Peter and John said, Acts, the fourth chapter: « We cannot but speak the things which we have heard and seen ». — He, then, who after the sight of these signs contradicts the Lord is shut out from the people of God and from the inheritance of the kingdom, according to that of Numbers, the fourteenth chapter[^p127-11]: « The men who have seen my majesty, and the signs that I did in Egypt, and have tempted me, shall not see the land for which I swore to their fathers ».

60. *Spiritually,* now, three things are to be attended to here: what is the *disease* here which is cured; what the
<!-- page 128 -->
*house* in which the cure is done; and what the *manner* of curing. — This *disease* of *paralysis* designates original fault, as *leprosy* actual. Whence *paralysis* takes away *sense* and *motion;* so the original takes away the *sense* of cognition through ignorance; Tobias, the fifth chapter[^p128-1]: « What manner of joy is to me, who sit in darkness, and see not the lumen[^tr-lumen] of heaven »? It takes away also *right motion* through concupiscence, which renders one trembling as paralysis does; to the Romans, the seventh chapter: « To will is present with me, but to accomplish I find not »; whence it is also subjoined: « I see another law in my members, fighting against the law of my mind » etc. Whence Hugh[^p128-2]: « The vice of human corruption has infected human nature in two ways, namely the mind with *ignorance* and the flesh with *concupiscence* ». Of these two in the Psalm: « My power hath left me, and the lumen of my eyes »; and Exodus, the tenth chapter: « There came darkness in Egypt, and for three days no man moved from his place ».

61. The *house* in which the cure is done is the Church, of which Proverbs, the ninth chapter[^p128-3]: « Wisdom hath built herself a house, she hath hewn her out seven pillars »; these *pillars* are the seven Sacraments. The *door* of this house is the priestly power, the authority of which power resides with Peter; whence Matthew, the sixteenth chapter: « To thee will I give the keys of the kingdom of heaven ». But when this door is filled with crowds of earthly solicitudes, then it does not afford access to Jesus, because they do not care for souls, which are Christ's, but for the advancement of their own advantage; to the Philippians, the second chapter[^p128-4]: « All seek the things that are their own; not the things that are Jesus Christ's ».

62. The *manner* of curing is through another's suffrage and the divine command. As regards the cure from the original, there concur the power of another's faith and the power of the divine Sacrament. Whence the *bearers* designate the godparents, who offer; Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter[^p128-5]: « They shall bring thy sons in their arms, and carry thy daughters upon their shoulders ». But *Christ,* healing by command, designates the office of the Sacrament, through which is done the *remission of the fault,* when it is said: *Thy sins are remitted thee;* Acts, the second chapter[^p128-6]: « Let every one of you be baptized unto the remission of sins ». There is done also the *cure of the sequel;* on account of which he says: *Arise and take up thy pallet* etc.; in Acts, the twelfth chapter, the Angel said to Peter lying in fetters: « Arise quickly. And forthwith the chains fell off from his hands ». *Arise,* that is, raise thyself up to see the lumen, against *ignorance;* to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter[^p128-7]: « Arise, thou that sleepest, and rise up from the dead, and Christ shall enlighten thee »; Isaias, the sixtieth chapter: « Arise, be enlightened, O Jerusalem: for thy lumen is come ». — *Take up thy pallet,* against *infirmity;* Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p128-8]: « Come to me, all you that labour »; and after: « Take up my yoke upon you ». — *And walk,* against *malice;* Genesis, the seventeenth chapter: « Walk before me, and be perfect »; to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter: « Walk in love, as Christ also hath loved us » etc. — *Go into the house,* against *concupiscence;* Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-second chapter[^p128-9]: « But run before into thy house, and there carry out thy designs, and not in offences ». Into his *own* house goes he who loves interior goods; Wisdom, the eighth chapter: « Entering into my house, I shall repose » etc.; into *another's* house, he who covets worldly things; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-ninth chapter: « It is a miserable life to go as a guest from house to house ».
