---

## Latin

#### De laude et gratiarum actione ponuntur duo.

119. (Vers. 67.). *Et Zacharias pater eius* etc. Postquam descripta est Ioannis origo, subiungitur hic *gratiarum actio* pro redemptionis beneficio iam inchoato in ortu praecursoris et consummando in ortu Salvatoris. Hanc igitur gratiarum actionem dupliciter describit: primo, ex parte *motivi;* secundo, ex parte *modi,* ibi[^p38-2]: *Benedictus Dominus.*

Circa *motivum* nota, quod illud quod movit Zachariam ad laudandum, fuit Spiritus divinus, non suus; propter quod praemittit, eum Spiritu sancto fuisse afflatum et impletum. Et tangitur hic: *quis* impletur? scilicet *Zacharias,* qui fuit vir iustus, de quo supra eodem[^p38-3], quod erat « incedens in omnibus mandatis et iustificationibus Domini sine querela », et in hoc innuitur, quod *memoria Dei* impletur; Tobiae primo: « Quia memor fuit Domini in toto corde suo, dedit illi Deus gratiam in conspectu Salmanasar regis ». — *Quare* impletur? Quia *pater eius,* scilicet Ioannis; unde sicut mater spiritum prophetiae habuit propter filium, supra eodem[^p38-4], sic et pater; Proverbiorum vigesimo tertio: « Exsultat gaudio pater iusti, qui sapientem genuit filium laetabitur in ipso ». In quo intelligitur *allegorice,* quod lex vetus et sacerdotium non habebat Spiritum nisi ratione novi testamenti, quod praefigurabat[^p38-5]. — *Quomodo* impletur? Propter quod dicit: *Et impletus est Spiritu sancto;* Genesis quadragesimo primo[^p38-6]: « Nunquid invenire poterimus talem virum, qui spiritu Dei plenus sit »? Et Ecclesiastici decimo quinto: « Implevit eum spiritu sapientiae et intellectus ». In quo instruimur, *quo* debemus impleri; ad Ephesios
quinto: « Impleamini Spiritu sancto, loquentes vobismetipsis in psalmis et hymnis » etc. — *Ad quid* impletur? *Et prophetavit,* laudando et praedicando; Actuum secundo[^p38-7]: « Repleti sunt omnes Spiritu sancto et coeperunt loqui variis linguis, prout Spiritus sanctus dabat eloqui illis »; et Amos tertio: « Dominus locutus est, quis non prophetabit »? et secundae Petri primo: « Non enim voluntate humana allata est aliquando prophetia, sed Spiritu sancto inspirati locuti sunt sancti Dei homines ».

120. *Benedictus Dominus Deus Israel* etc. Hic describitur *gratiarum actio* quantum ad *modum* in hoc cantico, cuius quatuor sunt partes. In quarum prima fit gratiarum actio et laudis decantatio pro beneficio nostrae redemptionis, secundum quod *consummanda erat per Christum* dispensatio remediativa. Secundo, in quantum iam *promissa per Prophetas* Spiritus sancti vaticinio, ibi[^p38-8]: *Sicut locutus est per os sanctorum.* Tertio, in quantum *promissa Patriarchis,* veritatis iuramento firmata, ibi: *Ad faciendam misericordiam cum Patribus nostris* etc. Quarto, secundum quod iam *inchoata in praecursore* prophetali praeconio, ibi: *Et tu puer, Propheta Altissimi vocaberis.*

Agens autem gratias pro beneficio redemptionis *consummando*[^p38-9] *in Christo* ponit praeteritum pro futuro, more prophetico; in quo tangitur *mysterium incarnationis, pretium redemptionis, tropaeum resurrectionis;* in quibus est remedium nostrae salutis.

121. (Vers. 68.). Introducit ergo primo Zachariam in gratiarum actione per Spiritum sanctum prophetantem, quia agit gratias de futuro beneficio, cum dicit: *Benedictus Dominus Deus Israel.* Similiter dicitur Tobiae octavo[^p38-10]: « Benedicimus te, Domine Deus Israel, quia fecisti nobiscum misericordiam tuam »; et primi Paralipomenon ultimo: « Benedictus es, Domine Deus Israel, patris nostri ab aeterno in aeternum ».

Et nota, quod *Dominus* nominat *maiestatem* benedicendam cum *timore;* Malachiae primo[^p38-11]: « Si ego Dominus sum, ubi est timor meus »? Et ideo dicitur Deuteronomii decimo: « Dominum Deum tuum timebis »; et Psalmus: « Domine Dominus noster, quam admirabile est nomen tuum in universa terra »! — *Deus Israel* nominat *benignitatem* benedicendam cum *amore;* Isaiae quadragesimo quinto[^p38-12]: « Ego
<!-- page 39 -->
Dominus, qui voco nomen tuum, Deus Israel propter Iacob servum meum et Israel electum meum »; et Exodi tertio: « Ego sum Deus Abraham, Deus Isaac et Deus Iacob. Hoc nomen meum, et hoc memoriale ». Ideo autem *Israel* hic potius nominatur, quia est nomen electionis et gratiae.

122. Huius autem benedictionis primum motivum est *incarnationis mysterium,* quod notat, cum dicit: *Quia visitavit,* scilicet per incarnationem; Exodi tertio[^p39-1]: « Visitans visitavit nos Dominus et vidit omnia mala, quae acciderunt nobis in Aegypto, et educet nos de afflictione Aegypti » etc. Hoc petebat Propheta in Psalmo: « Memento nostri, Domine, in beneplacito populi tui, visita nos in salutari tuo ». Hoc autem fuit, quando carnem sumsit ex Virgine; Psalmus: « Visitasti terram et inebriasti eam, multiplicasti locupletare eam ».

123. Secundum motivum est *redemptionis pretium,* quod tangit, cum dicit: *Et fecit redemptionem plebiss uae;* ut impleatur illud Psalmi[^p39-2]: « Redemptionem misit Dominus populo suo »; et vere, « quia apud Dominum misericordia, et copiosa apud eum redemptio »; et primae Petri primo: « Non corruptibilibus auro vel argento redempti estis de vana vestra conversatione paternae traditionis, sed pretioso sanguine quasi Agni immaculati et incontaminati » etc. Et hoc est, quod dicitur Isaiae quinquagesimo secundo[^p39-3]: « Gratis venundati estis et sine argento redimemini »; Psalmus: « Redemisti in brachio tuo populum tuum ».

124. (Vers. 69.). Tertium motivum est *resurrectionis tropaeum,* quod tangit, cum dicit: *Et erexit cornu salutis nobis in domo David, pueri sui;* in quo intelligitur potestas Christi resurgentis; primi Regum secundo[^p39-4]: « Dominus dabit imperium regi suo et sublimabit cornu Christi sui ». Et hoc fuit in resurrectione; Matthaei ultimo: « Data est mihi omnis potestas in caelo et in terra ». Et quia illa potestas erat durabilis, ideo per *cornu* significatur; in cuius rei figuram vas, quo inunctus fuit Salomon, *cornu* fuit, tertii Regum primo; vas, quo inunctus fuit Saul, fuit *lenticula,* scilicet vas fragile, primi Regum decimo. Et dicitur: *Cornu salutis,* quia per illud « salvatus est Israel salute aeterna », Isaiae quadragesimo quinto[^p39-5]. — Et addit: *In domo David, pueri sui;* secundum illud Psalmi: « Illuc producam cornu David, paravi lucernam Christo meo »; et secundi Regum vigesimo secundo: « Scutum meum et cornu salutis meae, elevator meus et susceptor meus, ab iniquitate mea liberabis me ». Hoc autem dicitur propter incarnationem; infra secundo dicitur, quod Ioseph et Maria erant « de familia David ».

125. (Vers. 70.). *Sicut locutus est per os sanctorum.* Hic agit laudes et gratias pro nostra reparatione, secundum quod fuit *per Prophetas promissa* Spiritus sancti vaticinio. Et primo praemittit *vaticinium propheticum;* secundo vero subiungit *vaticinatum beneficium;* et tertio, *beneficii complementum.* Haec enim tria excitant ad benedicendum.

Primo igitur praemittit *vaticinium* in hoc quod dicit: *Sicut locutus est per os sanctorum qui a saeculo sunt Prophetarum eius.* Et debet iterari: « Benedictus Dominus Deus, quia erexit » etc. *Sicut locutus est;* primi Paralipomenon decimo septimo[^p39-6]: « Nunc, Domine, sermo, quem locutus es, confirmetur in perpetuum, et fac, sicut locutus es ». Hoc optabant Sancti praecedentes Christi adventum; Ecclesiastici trigesimo sexto[^p39-7]: « Suscita precationes, quas locuti sunt in nomine tuo Prophetae priores; et da mercedem sustinentibus te, ut Prophetae tui fideles inveniantur ». Quod et factum est; infra decimo octavo: « Ecce, ascendimus Ierosolymam, et consummabuntur omnia, quae dicta sunt per Prophetas de Filio hominis »; Amos tertio: « Quia non faciet Dominus verbum, nisi revelaverit secretum suum ad servos
suos Prophetas ».

126. (Vers. 71.). Secundo subiungit *beneficium,* quod Prophetae promiserunt, cum dicit: *Salutem ex inimicis nostris;* et debet repeti verbum hoc *fecit,* vel *erexit.* De hac enim locuti sunt Prophetae; primae Petri primo[^p39-8]: « De qua salute exquisierunt atque scrutati sunt Prophetae, qui de futura in vobis gratia prophetaverunt ». Haec autem salus est in *liberatione ab inimicis,* id est a daemonibus; Psalmus: « Eripe me de inimicis meis »; ad Colossenses primo: « Eruit nos de potestate tenebrarum ».

127. Et subdit tertio *beneficii complementum,* cum dicit: *Et de manu omnium, qui oderunt nos,* id est de manu sive potestate omnium persequentium, sive daemonum, sive hominum; Isaiae decimo nono[^p39-9]: « Clamabunt ad Dominum a facie tribulantis, et mittet eis salvatorem et propugnatorem, qui liberet eos »; Iob quinto: « Dissipat cogitationes malignorum, ne possint implere manus eorum quod coeperant ». Et de tanto beneficio laudandus est Deus;
<!-- page 40 -->
secundi Machabaeorum primo[^p40-1]: « De magnis periculis a Deo liberati, magnifice gratias agimus ipsi »; et in Psalmo: « Eripuit me de inimicis meis fortissimis, et ab his qui oderunt me ».

128. (Vers. 72.). *Ad faciendam misericordiam* etc. Haec est tertia pars principalis cantici, in qua gratias agit pro nostra reparatione, secundum quod *Patribus promissa* sub iureiurando. Et in hac parte primo praemittit *principium ad promittendum movens;* secundo, *iuramentum stabiliens;* et tertio, *remedium subsequens.*

*Principium* autem *movens ad promittendum* fuit divina misericordia, ex qua promissione ortum habuit testimonium Patrum; et hoc tangit, cum dicit: *Ad faciendam misericordiam cum Patribus nostris.* Et debet iterari verbum prius positum, scilicet benedictionis et erectionis: benedictus scilicet Deus, qui erexit cornu, ut faceret misericordiam; Exodi vigesimo[^p40-2]: « Faciet misericordiam in millia his qui diligunt illum »; et Tobiae tertio: « Benedictus es, Domine Deus patrum nostrorum, qui, cum iratus fueris, misericordiam facis »; Psalmus: « Magnificans salutes regis eius et faciens misericordiam Christo suo David et semini eius in sempiternum ». Et quia misericordiam promittendo quasi fecit debitum per pactum, ideo subdit: *Et memorari testamenti sui sancti;* Psalmus[^p40-3]: « Memor fuit in saeculum testamenti sui, verbi, quod mandavit in mille generationes ».

129. Et nota, quod testamentum vetus fuit sanctum per *figurationem,* novum vero per *continentiam* et impletionem; unde *sanctum* testamentum vocat testamentum novum, quod sanctificavit sanguine suo; Zachariae nono[^p40-4]: « Tu autem in sanguine testamenti tui eduxisti vinctos de lacu »; et ad Hebraeos nono: « Si sanguis hircorum et cinis vitulae aspersus inquinatos sanctificat ad emundationem carnis; quanto magis sanguis Christi, qui per Spiritum sanctum semetipsum obtulit immaculatum Deo, emundabit conscientiam nostram ab operibus mortuis ad serviendum Deo viventi? Et ideo novi testamenti mediator est, ut, morte intercedente, in redemptionem earum praevaricationum, quae erant sub priori testamento, repromissionem accipiant, qui vocati sunt, aeternae hereditatis ». Et hoc est quod ante promiserat, sicut dicitur ad Hebraeos octavo[^p40-5]: « Consummabo super domum Israel et domum Iuda testamentum novum ».

130. (Vers. 73.). Secundo subdit *iuramentum stabiliens,* cum ait: *Iusiurandum, quod iuravit ad Abraham, patrem nostrum;* et debet semper iterari verbum praemissum, scilicet *fecit;* Genesis vigesimo secundo[^p40-6]: « Per memetipsum iuravi, dicit Dominus, quia fecisti rem hanc et non pepercisti unigenito filio tuo propter me, et benedicens benedicam te ». Quod introducens Apostolus ad Hebraeos sexto inquit: « Abrahae namque promittens Deus, cum neminem haberet, per quem iuraret, maiorem, per semetipsum iuravit »; et sic, ut « ostenderet abundantius heredibus pollicitationis immobilitatem consilii sui, interposuit iusiurandum, ut per duas res immobiles, quibus impossibile est mentiri Deum, fortissimum haberemus solatium ». *Abrahae,* promisit, scilicet *daturum se nobis,* filiis eius, scilicet per promissionem, Unigenitum suum; Ioannis tertio[^p40-7]: « Sic Deus dilexit mundum, ut Filium suum unigenitum daret »; et: « Qui proprio Filio non pepercit, sed pro nobis omnibus tradidit illum, quomodo non omnia nobis cum illo donavit »? ad Romanos octavo. Huius autem figura Genesis decimo quinto: « Semini tuo dabo terram hanc a fluvio Aegypti usque ad fluvium magnum Euphraten ».

131. (Vers. 74.). Deinde subiungit beneficium sive *remedium subsequens,* cum ait: *Ut sine timore de manu inimicorum nostrorum liberati, serviamus illi.* Istud enim est remedium, quod assecuti sumus per Christum nobis donatum; ad Romanos sexto[^p40-8]: « Nunc liberati a peccato, servi autem facti Deo, habemus fructum in sanctificatione, finem vero vitam aeternam »; Sapientiae decimo sexto: « Ostendisti inimicis nostris, quia tu es, qui liberas ab omni malo », illos scilicet, qui te audiunt credendo et obediendo; Proverbiorum primo: « Qui autem me audierit absque terrore erit et abundantia perfruetur, malorum timore sublato ». Et isti sunt, qui serviunt Domino; primi Regum septimo: « Praeparate corda vestra Domino et servite illi soli »; et liberabit nos de manibus inimicorum nostrorum.

132. (Vers. 75.). Et quia non omnis modus serviendi est Deo acceptus: ideo subdit *modum,* scilicet *sancte* quoad se ipsum, *iuste* quoad proximum, *simpliciter* quoad Deum, *perseveranter* respectu omnium praedictorum. — Propter *sanctitatem* dicit: *In sanctitate* autem serviamus illi, quia, secundum quod dicitur in Psalmo[^p40-9], « ambulans in via immaculata, hic mihi ministrabat »; ideo Exodi vigesimo
<!-- page 41 -->
octavo: « Sanctificabis Aaron et filios eius, ut sacerdotio fungantur mihi », et « ministrent in sanctuario, ne iniquitatis rei moriantur »; primae Petri primo: « Secundum eum qui vocavit vos sanctum, ut et ipsi in omni conversatione sancti sitis, quoniam scriptum est: Sancti eritis, quoniam ego sanctus sum ».

133. Propter *iustitiam* quoad proximum dicit: *Et iustitia;* ad Romanos sexto[^p41-1]: « Sicut exhibuistis membra vestra servire iniquitati ad iniquitatem, ita nunc exhibete membra vestra servire iustitiae in sanctificationem »; ut sic possitis dicere illud Psalmi: « Ego autem in iustitia apparebo conspectui tuo ».

134. Propter *simplicitatem* dicit: *Coram ipso,* scilicet Deo; quod fit per simplicem intentionem, sicut Ezechias, quarti Regum vigesimo[^p41-2]: « Obsecro, Domine, memento, quomodo ambulaverim coram te in veritate et corde perfecto, et quod placitum est coram te fecerim ». Sic « puer Samuel ministrabat in conspectu Domini ante faciem Heli », primi Regum tertio et primi Paralipomenon ultimo: « Scio, Domine, quod probes corda et simplicitatem diligas ».

135. Propter *perseverantiam* subdit: *Omnibus diebus nostris,* sicut Apostolus, Actuum vigesimo[^p41-3]: « Vos scitis, qualiter per omne tempus fuerim vobiscum, serviens Domino cum omni humilitate et lacrymis et tentationibus »; et Iosue vigesimo secundo: « Adhaereatis Deo et serviatis ei in omni corde et in omni anima vestra ».

136. (Vers. 76.). *Et tu, puer, propheta Altissimi vocaberis* etc. Haec est quarta pars cantici, in qua recolit beneficium nostrae redemptionis, secundum quod iam *inchoata erat in praecursore;* et hoc *prophetali praeconio,* quod commendat tripliciter, scilicet ab *auctoritate prophetiae,* ab *utilitate doctrinae* et a *proximitate divinae praesentiae.*

Commendat igitur Ioannis praeconium nostrae reparationis inchoativum ab *auctoritate prophetiae,* convertens sermonem ad puerum. *Et tu, puer, propheta Altissimi; tu puer* et aetate et puritate; primae ad Corinthios decimo quarto[^p41-4]: « Malitia parvuli estote, sensibus autem perfecti estote ». *Vocaberis propheta;* Matthaei undecimo: « Etiam dico vobis, et plus quam prophetam »; et *propheta Altissimi,* id est Christi, ratione *Divinitatis;* Psalmus: « Homo natus est in ea, et ipse fundavit eam Altissimus ». — *Praeibis enim ante faciem Domini,* ratione *humanitatis;* Psalmus[^p41-5]: « Ostende faciem tuam, et salvi erimus »; et Malachiae tertio: « Ecce, ego mitto
Angelum meum, et praeparabit viam ante faciem meam ». — *Parare vias eius,* pluraliter dicit *vias,* id est viam cognitionis in *fide* et affectionis in *caritate.* De *prima* Iob decimo septimo[^p41-6]: « Tenebit iustus viam suam et mundis manibus addet fortitudinem »; ad Romanos primo: « Iustus autem ex fide vivit ». De via *caritatis;* primae ad Corinthios duodecimo: « Adhuc excellentiorem viam vobis demonstro ». Viam *fidei* praeparavit Ioannes, secundum quod *vox;* infra tertio: « Vox clamantis in deserto: Parate viam Domini »; viam *caritatis,* secundum quod *lucerna;* Ioannis quinto: « Ille erat lucerna ardens et lucens ».

137. (Vers. 77.). Commendat etiam secundo ab *utilitate doctrinae,* cum dicit: *Ad dandam scientiam salutis plebi eius; dandam,* scilicet gratis; Matthaei decimo[^p41-7]: « Gratis accepistis, gratis date »; et Proverbiorum secundo: « Dominus dat sapientiam, et ex ore eius prudentia et scientia ». *Dandam,* inquam, *scientiam,* non quamcumque, sed *salutis;* quia sine scientia non est salus; Isaiae quinto[^p41-8]: « Propterea captivus ductus est populus meus, eo quod non habuit scientiam ». Hanc debet habere omnis rector populi; Osee quarto: « Quia tu scientiam repulisti, repellam ego te, ne sacerdotio fungaris mihi »; Isaiae trigesimo tertio: « Divitiae salutis, sapientia et scientia », *salutis,* inquam, non cuiuscumque, sed *spiritualis;* propter quod subdit: *In remissionem peccatorum eorum.* Beda[^p41-9]: « Ne temporalem salutem promitti putares, addit in remissionem peccatorum eorum ». Nam de Salvatore dicitur Matthaei primo: « Ipse salvum faciet populum suum a peccatis eorum »; et Marci primo: « Fuit Ioannes praedicans baptismum poenitentiae in remissionem peccatorum ».

138. (Vers. 78.). Commendat etiam Ioannis praeconium a *proximitate divinae praesentiae,* cum dicit: *Per viscera misericordiae Dei nostri.* Et hanc describit tripliciter: primo, ut *visitantem* ad consolandum affectum; secundo, ut *illuminantem* ad docendum intellectum, ibi[^p41-10]: *Illuminare his* etc.; tertio, ut *dirigentem* ad rectificandum effectum, ibi: *Ad dirigendos pedes* etc.

Describit igitur primo divinam praesentiam coniunctam prophetiae praecursoris, cum dicit: *Per viscera misericordiae Dei nostri, in quibus visitavit nos oriens ex alto;* et debet continuari ad praecedens sic: Praeibis ad dandam scientiam salutis plebi
<!-- page 42 -->
eius in remissionem peccatorum, quae quidem fuit *per viscera misericordiae Dei,* quae accelerant divinam praesentiam in carne, in qua *nos visitavit. Viscera misericordiae* sunt nimia dilectio et nimia compassio; ad Ephesios secundo[^p42-1]: « Deus autem, qui dives est in misericordia, propter nimiam caritatem suam, qua dilexit nos, et cum essemus mortui peccatis, convivificavit nos Christo, cuius gratia salvati estis »; et Isaiae sexagesimo tertio: « Ubi est multitudo viscerum tuorum et miserationum tuarum? Super me continuerunt se ». At postquam Filius Dei incarnatus est, verificatum est illud Genesis quadragesimo tertio[^p42-2] de Ioseph: « Commota sunt viscera sua super fratre suo ». Ad cuius exemplum ad Colossenses tertio: « Induite sicut electi Dei viscera misericordiae, benignitatem, humilitatem, modestiam, patientiam ». Per *haec viscera misericordiae visitavit nos oriens ex alto;* Malachiae quarto[^p42-3]: « Vobis timentibus nomen meum orietur sol iustitiae et sanitas in pennis eius »; et Zachariae sexto: « Ecce vir, *oriens* nomen eius ». *Ex alto,* secundum illud Ioannis tertio: « Qui desursum venit super omnes est ». In quo mira fuit dignatio; Psalmus: « Quid est homo, quod memor es eius, aut filius hominis, quoniam visitas eum »?

139. (Vers. 79.). Haec autem visitatio superni solis fuit non solum causa *consolationis,* verum etiam *illuminationis;* et propterea subdit: *Illuminare his qui in tenebris et in umbra mortis sedent;* et debet construi: visitavit illuminare, id est ad illuminandum, his qui in tenebris sedent, id est gentibus errantibus; Isaiae nono[^p42-4]: « Populus, qui ambulabat in tenebris, vidit lucem magnam; habitantibus in regione umbrae mortis lux orta est eis »; et rursus quadragesimo secundo: « Dedi te in lucem gentium, ut aperires oculos caecorum et educeres de conclusione vinctum, de domo carceris sedentes in tenebris ». Et ideo Ioannis nono: « In iudicium ego in hunc mundum veni, ut qui non vident videant, et qui vident caeci fiant ».

140. Et quia per illuminationem ducimur ad *directionem,* ideo sequitur: *Ad dirigendos pedes nostros in viam pacis;* Proverbiorum decimo sexto[^p42-5]: « Cor hominis disponit viam suam, sed Domini est dirigere gressus eius ». Et ideo Proverbiorum tertio: « In omnibus viis tuis cogita illum, et ipse diriget
gressus tuos »; et Psalmus: « Spiritus tuus bonus deducet me in terram rectam », ubi est regnum pacis sine labore; Sapientiae decimo: « Iustum deduxit Dominus per vias rectas et ostendit illi regnum Dei »; de quo regno dicitur in Psalmo: « Lauda, Ierusalem, Dominum, qui posuit fines tuos pacem ». *Ierusalem* enim interpretatur visio pacis, ad quam ducimur per ducatum Christi et per viam mandatorum, quae ducit ad pacem; Psalmus[^p42-6]: « Pax multa diligentibus legem tuam »; et Isaiae quadragesimo octavo: « Utinam attendisses mandata mea, facta fuisset sicut flumen pax tua ».

141. Nota, quod Dominus visitat ad *corrigendum delinquentes,* ut legatus provinciam; Abdiae primo[^p42-7]: « Legatum ad gentes misit. Surgite, et consurgamus ad eum in proelium »; Psalmus: « Visitabo in virga iniquitates eorum ». — Ad *iustificandum poenitentes,* ut medicus aegrotum; Marci secundo: « Non egent qui sani sunt medico; sed qui male habent ». — Ad *docendum ignorantes,* ut magister discipulum, sicut hic: *Visitavit nos, illuminare* etc.; Psalmus[^p42-8]: « Visitasti terram et inebriasti eam »; sequitur: « Flumen Dei repletum est aquis ». — Ad *excitandum negligentes,* ut princeps populum; primi Regum decimo septimo: « Fratres tuos visitabis, si recte agant, et cum quibus ordinati sunt, disce »; Actuum decimo quinto: « Revertentes visitemus fratres per universas civitates ». — Ad *promovendum operantes,* sicut vinitor vineam; Psalmus[^p42-9]: « Respice de caelo et vide et visita vineam istam »; Isaiae quinto: « Vinea enim Domini exercituum domus Israel est ». — Ad *conservandum obedientes,* sicut pastor gregem; Ezechielis trigesimo quarto: « Visitabo oves meas, sicut visitat pastor gregem suum ». — Ad *fecundandum contemplantes,* ut sponsus sponsam; Genesis vigesimo primo[^p42-10]: « Visitavit Dominus Saram, et concepit »; Iudith decimo quinto: « Ioachim, summus pontifex, de Ierusalem venit in Bethuliam cum universis presbyteris suis, ut videret Iudith ». — Ad *adiuvandum pugnantes,* ut rex exercitum; Genesis ultimo[^p42-11]: « Post mortem meam Deus visitabit vos »; et Lucae ultimo: « State in civitate, quoadusque induamini virtute ex alto ». — Ad *consolandum patientes,* ut sanus infirmum; Iob decimo[^p42-12]: « Visitatio tua custodivit spiritum meum »; et Iob secundo et Iacobi primo: « Religio munda
<!-- page 43 -->
et immaculata apud Deum et Patrem haec est, visitare pupillos et viduas in tribulationibus eorum »; Ecclesiastici septimo: « Non te pigeat visitare infirmum ». — Ad *remunerandum perseverantes,* sicut paterfamilias; Matthaei vigesimo[^p43-1]: « Voca operarios et redde illis mercedem »; et Isaiae sexagesimo: « Ponam visitationem tuam pacem et praepositos tuos iustitiam ».

#### De educatione pueri et laudis amplificatione tria.

142. (Vers. 80.). *Puer autem crescebat* etc. Postquam descripta est praecursoris origo et pro origine gratiarum actio, hic tertio subditur ipsius *educatio,* in qua est divinae laudis amplificatio. Describitur autem educatio quantum ad profectum *aetatis in carne* et *virtutis in mente* et *religiositatis in conversatione.*

Quantum ad profectum *aetatis* dicit: *Puer autem crescebat,* scilicet, corpore, secundum quod dicitur de Samuele primi Regum secundo[^p43-2]: « Puer autem Samuel crescebat et placebat tam Deo quam hominibus ». Invenerat enim benedictionem, sicut Ioseph; de quo Genesis penultimo: « Filius accrescens Ioseph, filius accrescens et decorus aspectu »; sed econtra dicitur de Ruben in Genesis penultimo: « Effusus es sicut aqua, non crescas ». Unde et sibi competit illud Genesis vigesimo sexto: « Isaac proficiens atque succrescens, donec magnus vehementer effectus est ».

143. Quantum ad profectum *virtutis in mente* dicit: *Et confortabatur spiritu,* scilicet divinae virtutis, de quo Ezechielis tertio[^p43-3]: « Manus Domini erat mecum confortans me »; unde poterat dicere illud Apostoli ad Philippenses ultimo: « Omnia possum in eo qui me confortat ». Hic autem est Spiritus sanctus, qui dat virtutem ceteris Sanctis; Psalmus: « Verbo Domini caeli firmati sunt, et spiritu oris eius omnis virtus eorum ».

144. Quantum ad profectum *religiositatis in conversatione* subdit: *Et erat in desertis,* scilicet ut ibi religiosam duceret vitam, secundum quod de ipso cantatur[^p43-4]:

Antra deserti teneris sub annis, / Civium turmas fugiens petisti, / Ne levi saltem maculare vitam / Famine posses;

quia[^p43-5], « si quis se existimat religiosum esse, non refrenans linguam suam, sed seducens cor suum, huius vana est religio ». Et ideo faciebat illud propheticum in Psalmo: « Elongavi fugiens et mansi in solitudine ». Qui enim vult perfecte vivere debet, relictis urbibus, petere deserta; primi Machabaeorum secundo: « Multi descenderunt cum Mathathia, quaerentes iudicium et iustitiam in deserto, et sederunt ibi ». — Et quia religiositas non est laudabilis, nisi sit perseverans, ideo subdit: *Usque in diem ostensionis suae ad Israel,* cum scilicet ostendit se in praedicatione virtuosa; iuxta quod dicit Apostolus primae ad Corinthios secundo[^p43-6]: « Sermo meus et praedicatio mea non fuit in persuasibilibus humanae sapientiae verbis, sed in ostensione spiritus et virtutis ». Unde prius vixit sibi occulte per longum tempus, ut postmodum per suam ostensionem viveret ad salutem aliorum, secundum illud Iacobi tertio: « Quis sapiens et disciplinatus inter vos? Ostendat ex bona conversatione operationem suam in mansuetudine sapientiae ». — In hoc erudiuntur omnes praedicatores, ut prius vacent perfectioni propriae et deinde aedificationi alienae, quia[^p43-7] « cuius vita despicitur, restat, ut eius praedicatio contemnatur ». Huius figura praecessit in Elia, tertii Regum decimo nono, qui prius abiit in desertum et pervenit ad montem Dei, ubi edoctus est; et post sequitur, quod unxit reges et prophetas ad regimen populi Dei.

#### Of praise and thanksgiving two things are set down.

119. (Vers. 67.). *And Zachary his father* etc. After the origin of John has been described, there is subjoined here *the thanksgiving* for the benefit of redemption, already begun in the birth of the precursor and to be consummated in the birth of the Saviour. This thanksgiving, then, he describes in two ways: first, on the part of *the motive;* secondly, on the part of *the mode,* there[^p38-2]: *Blessed be the Lord.*

Concerning *the motive* note that that which moved Zachary to praise was the divine Spirit, not his own; on account of which he puts first that he was breathed upon and filled with the Holy Spirit. And it is touched on here *who* is filled: namely *Zachary,* who was a just man, of whom it is said above, in the same chapter[^p38-3], that he was « walking in all the commandments and justifications of the Lord without blame », and in this it is hinted that *the memory of God* is filled; Tobias, the first chapter: « Because he was mindful of the Lord with all his heart, God gave him favour in the sight of Salmanasar the king ». — *Why* is he filled? Because he is *his father,* namely John's; whence, as the mother had the spirit of prophecy on account of the son, above, in the same chapter[^p38-4], so also the father; Proverbs, the twenty-third chapter: « The father of the just rejoiceth greatly, he that hath begotten a wise son shall have joy in him ». In which is understood *allegorically* that the old law and priesthood did not have the Spirit except by reason of the new testament, which it prefigured[^p38-5]. — *How* is he filled? On account of which he says: *And he was filled with the Holy Spirit;* Genesis, the forty-first chapter[^p38-6]: « Can we find such another man, that is full of the spirit of God »? And Ecclesiasticus, the fifteenth chapter: « He hath filled him with the spirit of wisdom and understanding ». In which we are instructed *with what* we ought to be filled; to the Ephesians,
the fifth chapter: « Be ye filled with the Holy Spirit, speaking to yourselves in psalms and hymns » etc. — *For what* is he filled? *And he prophesied,* by praising and by preaching; Acts, the second chapter[^p38-7]: « They were all filled with the Holy Spirit, and they began to speak with divers tongues, according as the Holy Spirit gave them to speak »; and Amos, the third chapter: « The Lord hath spoken, who shall not prophesy »? and the second of Peter, the first chapter: « For prophecy came not by the will of man at any time, but the holy men of God spoke, inspired by the Holy Spirit ».

120. *Blessed be the Lord God of Israel* etc. Here is described *the thanksgiving* as to *the mode,* in this canticle, of which there are four parts. In the first of them is made the thanksgiving and the singing of praise for the benefit of our redemption, according as the remedial dispensation *was to be consummated through Christ.* Secondly, inasmuch as it was already *promised through the Prophets* by the foretelling of the Holy Spirit, there[^p38-8]: *As he spoke by the mouth of the holy ones.* Thirdly, inasmuch as it was *promised to the Patriarchs,* made firm by the oath of truth, there: *To perform mercy with our fathers* etc. Fourthly, according as it was already *begun in the precursor* by the prophetic proclamation, there: *And thou, child, shalt be called the Prophet of the Highest.*

Now giving thanks for the benefit of redemption *to be consummated*[^p38-9] *in Christ,* he puts the past for the future, in the prophetic manner; in which is touched on *the mystery of the incarnation, the price of the redemption, the trophy of the resurrection;* in which is the remedy of our salvation.

121. (Vers. 68.). First, then, he brings in Zachary, in the thanksgiving, prophesying through the Holy Spirit, because he gives thanks for a future benefit, when he says: *Blessed be the Lord God of Israel.* Likewise it is said in Tobias, the eighth chapter[^p38-10]: « We bless thee, O Lord God of Israel, because thou hast done thy mercy with us »; and the first of Paralipomenon, the last chapter: « Blessed art thou, O Lord the God of Israel our father, from eternity to eternity ».

And note that *Lord* names *the majesty* to be blessed with *fear;* Malachias, the first chapter[^p38-11]: « If I am the Lord, where is my fear »? And therefore it is said in Deuteronomy, the tenth chapter: « Thou shalt fear the Lord thy God »; and the Psalm: « O Lord our Lord, how admirable is thy name in the whole earth »! — *God of Israel* names *the kindness* to be blessed with *love;* Isaias, the forty-fifth chapter[^p38-12]: « I
<!-- page 39 -->
am the Lord who call thy name, the God of Israel, for the sake of Jacob my servant, and Israel my elect »; and Exodus, the third chapter: « I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob. This is my name, and this my memorial ». Now *Israel* is named here rather for this reason, that it is the name of election and of grace.

122. Now the first motive of this blessing is *the mystery of the incarnation,* which he notes when he says: *Because he hath visited,* namely through the incarnation; Exodus, the third chapter[^p39-1]: « The Lord visiting hath visited us, and hath seen all the evils that have befallen us in Egypt, and he will bring us out of the affliction of Egypt » etc. This the Prophet was asking in the Psalm: « Remember us, O Lord, in the favour of thy people; visit us with thy salvation ». And this was when he took flesh from the Virgin; the Psalm: « Thou hast visited the earth, and hast plentifully watered it; thou hast many ways enriched it ».

123. The second motive is *the price of the redemption,* which he touches on when he says: *And hath wrought the redemption of his people* [so printed: *plebiss uae* for *plebis suae*]; so that that of the Psalm may be fulfilled[^p39-2]: « He hath sent redemption to his people »; and truly, « because with the Lord there is mercy, and with him plentiful redemption »; and the first of Peter, the first chapter: « Not with corruptible things, gold or silver, were you redeemed from your vain conversation of the tradition of your fathers, but with the precious blood as of a Lamb unspotted and undefiled » etc. And this is what is said in Isaias, the fifty-second chapter[^p39-3]: « You were sold gratis, and you shall be redeemed without money »; the Psalm: « With thy arm thou hast redeemed thy people ».

124. (Vers. 69.). The third motive is *the trophy of the resurrection,* which he touches on when he says: *And hath raised up an horn of salvation to us, in the house of David his servant;* in which is understood the power of Christ rising again; the first of Kings, the second chapter[^p39-4]: « The Lord shall give empire to his king, and shall exalt the horn of his Christ ». And this was in the resurrection; Matthew, the last chapter: « All power is given to me in heaven and in earth ». And because that power was lasting, therefore it is signified by *a horn;* in figure of which thing the vessel with which Solomon was anointed was *a horn,* the third of Kings, the first chapter; the vessel with which Saul was anointed was *a little vial,* namely a fragile vessel, the first of Kings, the tenth chapter. And it is said: *A horn of salvation,* because through it « Israel is saved with an eternal salvation », Isaias, the forty-fifth chapter[^p39-5]. — And he adds: *In the house of David his servant;* according to that of the Psalm: « There will I bring forth a horn to David; I have prepared a lamp for my anointed »; and the second of Kings, the twenty-second chapter: « My shield, and the horn of my salvation, my lifter up and my receiver, from my iniquity thou wilt deliver me ». Now this is said on account of the incarnation; below, in the second chapter, it is said that Joseph and Mary were « of the family of David ».

125. (Vers. 70.). *As he spoke by the mouth of the holy ones.* Here he gives praises and thanks for our reparation, according as it was *promised through the Prophets* by the foretelling of the Holy Spirit. And first he puts forward *the prophetic foretelling;* secondly he subjoins *the foretold benefit;* and thirdly, *the complement of the benefit.* For these three stir up to blessing.

First, then, he puts forward *the foretelling* in this, that he says: *As he spoke by the mouth of his holy Prophets, who are from the beginning.* And there ought to be repeated: « Blessed be the Lord God, because he hath raised up » etc. *As he spoke;* the first of Paralipomenon, the seventeenth chapter[^p39-6]: « Now, O Lord, let the word which thou hast spoken be established for ever, and do as thou hast spoken ». This the Saints who went before the coming of Christ were longing for; Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-sixth chapter[^p39-7]: « Raise up the prayers which the former Prophets spoke in thy name; and reward them that patiently wait for thee, that thy Prophets may be found faithful ». Which also was done; below, the eighteenth chapter: « Behold, we go up to Jerusalem, and all things shall be accomplished which were spoken by the Prophets concerning the Son of man »; Amos, the third chapter: « For the Lord will not do a thing, unless he hath revealed his secret to his servants
the Prophets ».

126. (Vers. 71.). Secondly he subjoins *the benefit* which the Prophets promised, when he says: *Salvation from our enemies;* and there ought to be repeated this word *he hath wrought,* or *he hath raised up.* For of this the Prophets spoke; the first of Peter, the first chapter[^p39-8]: « Of which salvation the Prophets have inquired and diligently searched, who prophesied of the grace to come in you ». Now this salvation is in *the deliverance from enemies,* that is, from the demons; the Psalm: « Deliver me from my enemies »; to the Colossians, the first chapter: « He hath delivered us from the power of darkness ».

127. And thirdly he subjoins *the complement of the benefit,* when he says: *And from the hand of all that hate us,* that is, from the hand or power of all who persecute, whether demons or men; Isaias, the nineteenth chapter[^p39-9]: « They shall cry to the Lord because of the oppressor, and he shall send them a Saviour and a defender to deliver them »; Job, the fifth chapter: « He bringeth to nought the designs of the malignant, so that their hands cannot accomplish what they had begun ». And for so great a benefit God is to be praised;
<!-- page 40 -->
the second of Machabees, the first chapter[^p40-1]: « Being delivered by God out of great dangers, we give him great thanks »; and in the Psalm: « He delivered me from my strongest enemies, and from them that hated me ».

128. (Vers. 72.). *To perform mercy* etc. This is the third principal part of the canticle, in which he gives thanks for our reparation according as it was *promised to the Fathers* under oath. And in this part he first puts forward *the principle moving to promise;* secondly, *the oath establishing it;* and thirdly, *the remedy following.*

Now *the principle moving to promise* was the divine mercy, from which promise the testimony of the Fathers had its rise; and this he touches on when he says: *To perform mercy with our fathers.* And there ought to be repeated the word put before, namely of the blessing and of the raising up: blessed, namely, be God, who hath raised up a horn, that he might perform mercy; Exodus, the twentieth chapter[^p40-2]: « He will perform mercy unto thousands to them that love him »; and Tobias, the third chapter: « Blessed art thou, O Lord God of our fathers, who, when thou hast been angry, performest mercy »; the Psalm: « Magnifying the salvations of his king, and performing mercy to David his Christ, and to his seed for ever ». And because by promising mercy he made, as it were, a debt by covenant, therefore he subjoins: *And to remember his holy testament;* the Psalm[^p40-3]: « He hath remembered his testament for ever, the word which he commanded to a thousand generations ».

129. And note that the old testament was holy by *figuration,* but the new by *containing* and fulfilment; whence he calls the new testament the *holy* testament, which he sanctified with his blood; Zacharias, the ninth chapter[^p40-4]: « Thou also by the blood of thy testament hast sent forth the prisoners out of the pit »; and to the Hebrews, the ninth chapter: « If the blood of goats and the ashes of an heifer being sprinkled sanctify such as are defiled, to the cleansing of the flesh, how much more shall the blood of Christ, who by the Holy Spirit offered himself unspotted unto God, cleanse our conscience from dead works, to serve the living God? And therefore he is the mediator of the new testament, that by means of his death, for the redemption of those transgressions which were under the former testament, they that are called may receive the promise of eternal inheritance ». And this is what he had promised before, as is said to the Hebrews, the eighth chapter[^p40-5]: « I will perfect unto the house of Israel and unto the house of Juda a new testament ».

130. (Vers. 73.). Secondly he subjoins *the oath establishing it,* when he says: *The oath which he swore to Abraham our father;* and the word put before ought always to be repeated, namely *he hath wrought;* Genesis, the twenty-second chapter[^p40-6]: « By my own self have I sworn, saith the Lord, because thou hast done this thing, and hast not spared thy only begotten son for my sake, and blessing I will bless thee ». Which the Apostle bringing in, to the Hebrews, the sixth chapter, says: « For God making promise to Abraham, because he had no one greater by whom he might swear, swore by himself »; and thus, so as « more abundantly to shew to the heirs of the promise the immutability of his counsel, he interposed an oath, that by two immutable things, in which it is impossible for God to lie, we may have the strongest comfort ». *To Abraham* he promised, namely *that he would grant to us,* his sons, namely by promise, his Only-begotten; John, the third chapter[^p40-7]: « God so loved the world, as to give his only begotten Son »; and: « He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how hath he not also with him given us all things »? to the Romans, the eighth chapter. And the figure of this is in Genesis, the fifteenth chapter: « To thy seed will I give this land, from the river of Egypt even to the great river Euphrates ».

131. (Vers. 74.). Then he subjoins the benefit, or *the remedy following,* when he says: *That, without fear, being delivered from the hand of our enemies, we may serve him.* For this is the remedy which we have obtained through Christ given to us; to the Romans, the sixth chapter[^p40-8]: « Now being made free from sin, and become servants to God, we have our fruit unto sanctification, and the end life everlasting »; Wisdom, the sixteenth chapter: « Thou shewedst to our enemies that thou art he who deliverest from all evil », namely those who hear thee by believing and obeying; Proverbs, the first chapter: « But he that shall hear me shall be without terror, and shall enjoy abundance, without fear of evils ». And these are they who serve the Lord; the first of Kings, the seventh chapter: « Prepare your hearts unto the Lord, and serve him only »; and he will deliver us out of the hands of our enemies.

132. (Vers. 75.). And because not every mode of serving is acceptable to God, therefore he subjoins *the mode,* namely *holily* as to oneself, *justly* as to one's neighbour, *simply* as to God, *perseveringly* in respect of all the foregoing. — On account of *holiness* he says: *In holiness* then let us serve him, because, according to what is said in the Psalm[^p40-9], « the man that walked in the perfect way, he served me »; therefore in Exodus, the twentieth
<!-- page 41 -->
chapter: « Thou shalt sanctify Aaron and his sons, that they may do the office of priesthood unto me », and « minister in the sanctuary, lest being guilty of iniquity they die »; the first of Peter, the first chapter: « According to him that hath called you, who is holy, be you also in all manner of conversation holy, because it is written: You shall be holy, for I am holy ».

133. On account of *justice* as to one's neighbour he says: *And justice;* to the Romans, the sixth chapter[^p41-1]: « As you have yielded your members to serve iniquity, unto iniquity, so now yield your members to serve justice, unto sanctification »; so that you may thus say that of the Psalm: « But I in justice shall appear before thy sight ».

134. On account of *simplicity* he says: *Before him,* namely God; which comes about through a simple intention, as Ezechias, the fourth of Kings, the twentieth chapter[^p41-2]: « I beseech thee, O Lord, remember how I have walked before thee in truth and with a perfect heart, and have done that which is pleasing before thee ». So « the child Samuel ministered in the sight of the Lord before Heli », the first of Kings, the third chapter; and the first of Paralipomenon, the last chapter: « I know, O Lord, that thou provest hearts, and lovest simplicity ».

135. On account of *perseverance* he subjoins: *All our days,* as the Apostle, Acts, the twentieth chapter[^p41-3]: « You know in what manner I have been with you for all the time, serving the Lord with all humility, and with tears, and temptations »; and Josue, the twenty-second chapter: « That you cleave to God and serve him with all your heart and with all your soul ».

136. (Vers. 76.). *And thou, child, shalt be called the prophet of the Highest* etc. This is the fourth part of the canticle, in which he calls to mind the benefit of our redemption according as it *had already been begun in the precursor;* and this *by the prophetic proclamation,* which he commends in three ways, namely from *the authority of prophecy,* from *the usefulness of the doctrine,* and from *the nearness of the divine presence.*

He commends, then, John's proclamation, inchoative of our reparation, from *the authority of prophecy,* turning his speech to the child. *And thou, child, the prophet of the Highest; thou child* both in age and in purity; the first to the Corinthians, the fourteenth chapter[^p41-4]: « In malice be children, but in sense be perfect ». *Thou shalt be called a prophet;* Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « Yea, I tell you, and more than a prophet »; and *the prophet of the Highest,* that is, of Christ, by reason of *the Divinity;* the Psalm: « A man is born in her, and the Highest himself hath founded her ». — *For thou shalt go before the face of the Lord,* by reason of *the humanity;* the Psalm[^p41-5]: « Shew thy face, and we shall be saved »; and Malachias, the third chapter: « Behold, I send my Angel, and he shall prepare the way before my face ». — *To prepare his ways:* he says *ways* in the plural, that is, the way of cognition in *faith* and of affection in *charity.* Of *the first,* Job, the seventeenth chapter[^p41-6]: « The just shall hold on his way, and with clean hands he shall add strength »; to the Romans, the first chapter: « But the just man liveth by faith ». Of the way of *charity;* the first to the Corinthians, the twelfth chapter: « I shew unto you yet a more excellent way ». The way of *faith* John prepared according as he is *the voice;* below, the third chapter: « A voice of one crying in the wilderness: Prepare ye the way of the Lord »; the way of *charity,* according as he is *the lamp;* John, the fifth chapter: « He was a burning and a shining lamp ».

137. (Vers. 77.). He commends it also, secondly, from *the usefulness of the doctrine,* when he says: *To give knowledge of salvation to his people; to give,* namely freely; Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p41-7]: « Freely have you received, freely give »; and Proverbs, the second chapter: « The Lord giveth wisdom, and out of his mouth cometh prudence and knowledge ». *To give,* I say, *knowledge,* not any whatever, but *of salvation;* because without knowledge there is no salvation; Isaias, the fifth chapter[^p41-8]: « Therefore is my people led away captive, because they had not knowledge ». This every ruler of the people ought to have; Osee, the fourth chapter: « Because thou hast rejected knowledge, I will reject thee, that thou shalt not do the office of priesthood to me »; Isaias, the thirty-third chapter: « Riches of salvation, wisdom and knowledge »: *of salvation,* I say, not of any whatever, but *spiritual;* on account of which he subjoins: *Unto the remission of their sins.* Bede[^p41-9]: « Lest thou shouldst think that a temporal salvation was promised, he adds: unto the remission of their sins ». For of the Saviour it is said in Matthew, the first chapter: « He shall save his people from their sins »; and in Mark, the first chapter: « John was preaching the baptism of penance unto the remission of sins ».

138. (Vers. 78.). He commends John's proclamation also from *the nearness of the divine presence,* when he says: *Through the bowels of the mercy of our God.* And this he describes in three ways: first, as *visiting,* to console the affection; secondly, as *enlightening,* to teach the understanding, there[^p41-10]: *To enlighten them* etc.; thirdly, as *directing,* to set right the effect, there: *To direct our feet* etc.

First, then, he describes the divine presence joined to the prophecy of the precursor, when he says: *Through the bowels of the mercy of our God, in which the Orient from on high hath visited us;* and it ought to be continued with what precedes thus: Thou shalt go before to give knowledge of salvation to his
<!-- page 42 -->
people, unto the remission of sins; which indeed was *through the bowels of the mercy of God,* which hasten the divine presence in the flesh, in which *he hath visited us. The bowels of mercy* are an exceeding dilection and an exceeding compassion; to the Ephesians, the second chapter[^p42-1]: « But God, who is rich in mercy, for his exceeding charity wherewith he loved us, even when we were dead in sins, hath quickened us together in Christ, by whose grace you are saved »; and Isaias, the sixty-third chapter: « Where is the multitude of thy bowels and of thy mercies? They have held back themselves from me ». But after the Son of God was incarnate, that of Genesis, the forty-third chapter[^p42-2], concerning Joseph, was verified: « His bowels were moved upon his brother ». After whose example, to the Colossians, the third chapter: « Put ye on, as the elect of God, the bowels of mercy, kindness, humility, modesty, patience ». Through *these bowels of mercy the Orient from on high hath visited us;* Malachias, the fourth chapter[^p42-3]: « Unto you that fear my name the Sun of justice shall arise, and health in his wings »; and Zacharias, the sixth chapter: « Behold a man, *the Orient* is his name ». *From on high,* according to that of John, the third chapter: « He that cometh from above is above all ». In which there was a wonderful condescension; the Psalm: « What is man, that thou art mindful of him? or the son of man, that thou visitest him »?

139. (Vers. 79.). Now this visitation of the supernal sun was the cause not only of *consolation,* but also of *illumination;* and therefore he subjoins: *To enlighten them that sit in darkness and in the shadow of death;* and it ought to be construed: he hath visited to enlighten, that is, for the enlightening of, them that sit in darkness, that is, the wandering Gentiles; Isaias, the ninth chapter[^p42-4]: « The people that walked in darkness have seen a great light; to them that dwelt in the region of the shadow of death light is risen »; and again, the forty-second chapter: « I have given thee for a light of the Gentiles, that thou mightest open the eyes of the blind, and bring forth the prisoner out of prison, them that sit in darkness out of the prison house ». And therefore John, the ninth chapter: « For judgement I am come into this world, that they who see not may see, and they who see may become blind ».

140. And because through illumination we are led to *direction,* therefore it follows: *To direct our feet into the way of peace;* Proverbs, the sixteenth chapter[^p42-5]: « The heart of man disposeth his way, but the Lord must direct his steps ». And therefore Proverbs, the third chapter: « In all thy ways think on him, and he will direct
thy steps »; and the Psalm: « Thy good spirit shall lead me into the right land », where is the kingdom of peace without labour; Wisdom, the tenth chapter: « She conducted the just through the right ways, and shewed him the kingdom of God »; of which kingdom it is said in the Psalm: « Praise the Lord, O Jerusalem, who hath placed peace in thy borders ». For *Jerusalem* is interpreted vision of peace, to which we are led by the leading of Christ and by the way of the commandments, which leads to peace; the Psalm[^p42-6]: « Much peace have they that love thy law »; and Isaias, the forty-eighth chapter: « O that thou hadst hearkened to my commandments, thy peace had been as a river ».

141. Note that the Lord visits *to correct those who transgress,* as a legate his province; Abdias, the first chapter[^p42-7]: « He hath sent an ambassador to the nations. Arise, and let us rise up against him to battle »; the Psalm: « I will visit their iniquities with a rod ». — *To justify the penitent,* as a physician the sick man; Mark, the second chapter: « They that are well have no need of a physician, but they that are sick ». — *To teach the ignorant,* as a master his disciple, as here: *He hath visited us, to enlighten* etc.; the Psalm[^p42-8]: « Thou hast visited the earth, and hast plentifully watered it »; it follows: « The river of God is filled with water ». — *To rouse the negligent,* as a prince his people; the first of Kings, the seventeenth chapter: « Thou shalt visit thy brethren, whether they fare well, and learn with whom they are placed »; Acts, the fifteenth chapter: « Let us return and visit our brethren in all the cities ». — *To advance those who labour,* as a vinedresser his vineyard; the Psalm[^p42-9]: « Look down from heaven, and see, and visit this vineyard »; Isaias, the fifth chapter: « For the vineyard of the Lord of hosts is the house of Israel ». — *To preserve the obedient,* as a shepherd his flock; Ezechiel, the thirty-fourth chapter: « I will visit my sheep, as the shepherd visiteth his flock ». — *To make fruitful those who contemplate,* as a bridegroom his bride; Genesis, the twenty-first chapter[^p42-10]: « The Lord visited Sara, and she conceived »; Judith, the fifteenth chapter: « Joachim the high priest came from Jerusalem to Bethulia with all his ancients, to see Judith ». — *To help those who fight,* as a king his army; Genesis, the last chapter[^p42-11]: « After my death God will visit you »; and Luke, the last chapter: « Stay you in the city till you be endued with power from on high ». — *To console the suffering,* as a healthy man the sick; Job, the tenth chapter[^p42-12]: « Thy visitation hath preserved my spirit »; and Job, the second chapter, and James, the first chapter: « Religion clean
<!-- page 43 -->
and undefiled before God and the Father is this: to visit the fatherless and widows in their tribulations »; Ecclesiasticus, the seventh chapter: « Be not slow to visit the sick ». — *To reward those who persevere,* as a householder; Matthew, the twentieth chapter[^p43-1]: « Call the labourers and pay them their hire »; and Isaias, the sixtieth chapter: « I will make thy visitation peace, and thy overseers justice ».

#### Of the upbringing of the child and the amplification of the praise, three things.

142. (Vers. 80.). *And the child grew* etc. After the origin of the precursor has been described, and the thanksgiving for the origin, here thirdly is subjoined his *upbringing,* in which there is an amplification of the divine praise. Now the upbringing is described as to the progress *of age in the flesh,* and *of virtue in the mind,* and *of religiousness in the manner of life.*

As to the progress *of age* he says: *And the child grew,* namely in body, according to what is said of Samuel, the first of Kings, the second chapter[^p43-2]: « But the child Samuel grew, and pleased both God and men ». For he had found the blessing, as Joseph; of whom Genesis, the next to the last chapter: « Joseph is a growing son, a growing son and comely to behold »; but on the contrary it is said of Ruben in Genesis, the next to the last chapter: « Thou art poured out as water, grow thou not ». Whence there befits him also that of Genesis, the twenty-sixth chapter: « Isaac went on prospering and increasing, till he became exceeding great ».

143. As to the progress *of virtue in the mind* he says: *And was strengthened in spirit,* namely of the divine power, of which Ezechiel, the third chapter[^p43-3]: « The hand of the Lord was with me, strengthening me »; whence he could say that of the Apostle, to the Philippians, the last chapter: « I can do all things in him who strengtheneth me ». Now this is the Holy Spirit, who gives power to the other Saints; the Psalm: « By the word of the Lord the heavens were established, and all the power of them by the spirit of his mouth ».

144. As to the progress *of religiousness in the manner of life* he subjoins: *And was in the deserts,* namely so that there he might lead a religious life, according to what is sung of him[^p43-4]:

The caves of the desert, in thy tender years, / fleeing the throngs of the citizens, thou didst seek, / lest thou shouldst be able to stain thy life / even with a light word;

because[^p43-5] « if any man think himself to be religious, not bridling his tongue, but deceiving his own heart, this man's religion is vain ». And therefore he did that prophetic word in the Psalm: « Lo, I have gone far off flying away, and I abode in the wilderness ». For he who wishes to live perfectly ought, the cities being left behind, to seek the deserts; the first of Machabees, the second chapter: « Many went down with Mathathias, seeking after judgement and justice in the desert, and they abode there ». — And because religiousness is not praiseworthy unless it be persevering, therefore he subjoins: *Until the day of his manifestation to Israel,* namely when he showed himself in virtuous preaching; according to what the Apostle says, the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p43-6]: « My speech and my preaching was not in the persuasive words of human wisdom, but in the manifestation of the spirit and of power ». Whence he first lived hidden to himself for a long time, so that afterward through his manifestation he might live for the salvation of others, according to that of James, the third chapter: « Who is a wise man and endued with knowledge among you? Let him shew, by a good conversation, his work in the mildness of wisdom ». — In this all preachers are schooled, that they first be free for[^tr-vacare] their own perfection, and then for the edification of others, because[^p43-7] « he whose life is despised, it remains that his preaching be contemned ». The figure of this went before in Elias, the third of Kings, the nineteenth chapter, who first went away into the desert and came to the mount of God, where he was taught; and afterward it follows that he anointed kings and prophets for the ruling of the people of God.
