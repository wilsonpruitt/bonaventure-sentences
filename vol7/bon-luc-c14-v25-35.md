# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XIV, Vers. 25-35
## *Tertio erudiuntur turbae subsequentes, de quo duo*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 375 -->

#### Tertio erudiuntur turbae subsequentes, de quo duo.

51. (Vers. 25.). *Ibant autem turbae multae.* Postquam confutavit calumniantes et erudivit convivantes, hic iam tertio *informat comitantes*[^p375-3]. Dividitur autem pars ista in duas. In prima erudit *comitantes per verba expressa;* in secunda vero per *exempla parabolica,* ibi[^p375-4]: *Quis enim ex vobis volens aedificare turrem* etc. — Erudiens autem per *verba expressa* ad Christum spiritualiter comitandum procedit hoc ordine, exprimendo scilicet *spiritualis comitatus indicium, principium* et *complementum.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *spiritualis comitatus indicium* dicit: *Ibant autem turbae multae cum eo,* in signum spiritualis comitatus; unde Glossa[^p375-5]: « *Ibant turbae cum eo,* captae scilicet dulcedine praedicationis et miraculorum »; unde poterant dicere illud Ioannis sexto: « Domine, ad quem ibimus? Verba vitae aeternae habes ». Et nota hic, quod Pharisaei *calumniabantur* Christum, et turbae *comitabantur,* quia quem superbi despiciunt humiles comitantur; unde Ioannis septimo[^p375-6]: « Nunquid aliquis de principibus credidit in eum? Sed turba haec, quae ignorat Legem, maledicta est ». Sequebantur autem turbae Iesum tanquam *oves pastorem;* Matthaei nono[^p375-7]: « Videns Iesus turbas, misertus est eis, quia erant vexati et iacentes sicut oves non habentes pastorem ». Item, sequebantur sicut *populi regem;* Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p375-8]: « Turbae, quae praeibant et quae sequebantur, clamabant: Hosanna » etc. Item, sequebantur sicut *discipuli doctorem;* supra quinto: « Factum est, cum turbae irruerent in eum, ut audirent verbum Dei; et ipse stabat secus stagnum Genesareth ».

52. (Vers. 25. 26.). Secundo quantum ad *spiritualis comitatus principium* subdit: *Et conversus dixit ad illos; conversus,* inquam, per *benignitatem;* Psalmus[^p375-9]: « Deus, tu conversus vivificabis nos »; *dixit,* per *veritatem doctrinae: Si quis venit ad me,* per *supererogationem iustitiae;* Matthaei undecimo: « Venite ad me omnes, qui laboratis et onerati estis ». — *Et non odit patrem suum et matrem et uxorem et filios et fratres et sorores, adhuc autem et animam suam,* per *mortificationem affectus carnalis,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p375-10]: « Audi, filia, et vide et inclina aurem tuam » etc.; *non potest meus esse discipulus,* per *assumtionem evangelicae perfectionis,* de qua Lucae sexto: « Perfectus omnis erit, si sit sicut magister eius ». — Et ratio huius est, quia principium spiritualis vitae est exuere carnalem affectum, quia, primae ad Corinthios secundo[^p375-11], « animalis homo non percipit ea quae sunt spiritus Dei; stultitia enim est illi, et non potest intelligere » etc.; et Isaiae vigesimo octavo: « Quem docebit scientiam et quem intelligere faciet auditum? Ablactatos a lacte » etc., hoc est separatos a carnali affectione.

53. Unde notandum est hic, quod non praecipit hic odium parentum quantum ad *naturam,* quia hoc esset impietatis, cum dicatur primae Ioannis tertio[^p375-12]: « Qui odit fratrem suum homicida est »; sed quantum ad *culpam,* sicut dicit Augustinus: « Sic diligendi sunt homines, ut eorum errores non diligantur ». De quo odio in Psalmo[^p375-13]: « Nonne qui oderunt te, Domine, oderam et super inimicos tuos tabescebam »? et post: « Perfecto odio oderam illos » etc. Unde Gregorius: « Simul et odisse possumus et diligere, ut et cognatione nobis coniunctos ex praecepto divino diligamus, et quos adversarios in via Dei patimur odiendo et fugiendo nesciamus ». Unde odium illud non venit ex crudelitate, sed ex caritate, sicut et odium animae suae, quam debemus odisse, non quantum ad salutem, sed quantum ad carnalitatem. Unde Gregorius[^p375-14]: « Tunc animam nostram odimus, cum eius affectibus carnalibus reluctamur ». De hoc odio Ioannis duodecimo: « Qui odit

<!-- page 376 -->
animam suam in hoc mundo in vitam aeternam custodit eam ». Augustinus: « Si male amaveris, tunc odisti; si bene oderis, tunc amasti. Felices, qui sic oderunt custodiendo, ne perdant amando ». Hoc est quod expressius dicitur Ecclesiastici decimo octavo[^p376-1]: « Fili, post concupiscentias tuas non eas et a voluntate tua avertere. Si praestes animae tuae concupiscentias eius, faciet te in gaudium venire inimicis tuis ». Sicut igitur principium spiritualis *vitae* est *non concupiscere,* ubi omne malum prohibetur[^p376-2], et *declinare a malo;* sic fundamentum spiritualis *comitatus* est *carnalitatem omnem declinare,* sive ad se sive ad parentes. Propter quod ad Ephesios quarto[^p376-3]: « Deponite vos secundum pristinam conversationem veterem hominem, qui corrumpitur secundum desideria erroris ».

54. (Vers. 27.). Tertio quantum ad *spiritualis comitatus complementum* subiungit: *Et qui non baiulat crucem suam,* per *perfectam carnis mortificationem*[^p376-4]*; et venit post me,* per *rectam intentionem; non potest meus esse discipulus,* per *veram imitationem.* — Et nota, quod hic est *consummatio* discipulatus Christi et comitatus, scilicet in baiulando crucem, quia ibi Christus consummavit, sicut dicitur Ioannis decimo nono[^p376-5]: « Et baiulans sibi crucem, exiit in eum qui dicitur Calvariae locum ». In hac cruce Christi est summa *humilitas;* ad Philippenses secundo[^p376-6]: « Humiliavit semetipsum, factus obediens usque ad mortem, mortem autem crucis »; summa *paupertas,* ita ut vere posset dicere Christus illud Iob primo[^p376-7]: « Nudus egressus sum de utero matris meae, et nudus revertar illuc ». Unde Ioannis decimo nono dicitur, quod « milites, cum crucifixissent eum, acceperunt vestimenta eius et fecerunt quatuor partes ». Est etiam ibi summa *austeritas,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p376-8]: « Foderunt manus meas et pedes meos, dinumeraverunt omnia ossa mea ».

55. *Crucem* ergo *baiulare* est *humilitatem* in corde, *austeritatem* in carne et *paupertatem* in passione assumere, contra illa *tria radicalia* et contra *triplicem excusationem* eorum qui noluerunt venire ad coenam[^p376-9]. Haec crux fuit *Christi,* quia Christus voluntarie eam suscepit; similiter, qui vult Christum sequi, oportet, quod portet eam *voluntarie,* ut ferat eam sicut crucem *suam* ex voluntate propria, non in angaria sicut *alienam,* et ita non sit imitator Christi, sed potius Simonis, de quo Marci decimo quinto[^p376-10]: « Angariaverunt quendam Simonem Cyrenaeum, ut tolleret crucem eius ». Ad hanc crucem potissime nos *invitat* Christus, qui primus eam portavit sicut vexillum et signum regale; unde ad Hebraeos ultimo[^p376-11]: « Christus extra portam passus est. Exeamus ergo ad eum » etc. Qui ergo sic non vult sequi non est Christi discipulus, quia non vult imitari eum, in quo se praebuit exemplar nobis et magistrum; unde ad Galatas quinto[^p376-12]: « Qui autem sunt Christi carnem suam crucifixerunt cum vitiis et concupiscentiis ». Propter quod secundae ad Corinthios quarto: « Semper crucis mortificationem in corpore nostro circumferentes, ut et vita Iesu in corporibus nostris manifestetur ». Unde Chrysostomus[^p376-13]: « Nullus verecundetur crucem, circumferamus eam ut coronam; etenim omnia, quae secundum nos sunt, per crucem perficiuntur: regeneratio, consecratio. Propter hoc in domo, in ianuis, in viis, in frontibus et in mente eam scribimus ». Haec igitur baiulanda est tanquam signum Dei vivi, de quo Apocalypsis septimo[^p376-14]: « Vidi alterum Angelum ascendentem ab ortu solis, habentem signum Dei vivi; et clamavit voce magna quatuor Angelis, quibus datum est nocere terrae et mari, dicens: Nolite nocere terrae et mari neque arboribus, quoadusque signemus servos Dei in frontibus eorum », ut sciatur scilicet manifeste, quis sit Christi discipulus.

56. *Quis enim ex vobis, volens aedificare* etc. Postquam informavit comitantes, et hoc per verba expressa, hic secundo informat eosdem *per exempla parabolica*[^p376-15]. Et quoniam Christum debemus imitari in *actionibus* et *passionibus* — in primis similes sumus *aedificantibus,* in secundis similes *proeliantibus* — ideo primo in hac parte introducit parabolam de *homine aedificante.* Secundo vero adiungit parabolam et exemplum de *rege proeliante,* ibi[^p376-16]: *Aut quis rex iturus.* In tertia vero subiungit *adaptationem utriusque,* ibi: *Sic ergo omnis ex vobis, qui non renuntiaverit.*

<!-- page 377 -->
Circa parabolam de *homine aedificante* tria introducuntur, scilicet *conceptio propositi aedificandi, provisio sufficientis stipendii, declinatio imminentis opprobrii.*

57. (Vers. 28.). Primo igitur quantum ad *conceptionem propositi aedificandi* dicit: *Quis enim ex vobis, volens aedificare turrem*[^p377-1]. Per *turrem,* quae est aedificium altissimum, possumus intelligere cumulum perfectionis meritorum, quae gradatim ascendit ab imo usque ad summum, incipiens a *fundamento timoris* et perveniens usque ad *culmen sapientiae deiformis.* Et est primo aedificanda turris *doni timoris,* de qua potest intelligi illud Michaeae quarto[^p377-2]: « Tu, turris gregis nebulosa, usque ad te veniet », quia timor parvulum facit ex consideratione iudicii. — Secunda turris est *pietatis,* de qua Isaiae quinto[^p377-3]: « Aedificavit turrim in medio vineae et torcular exstruxit in ea », quia pietas oritur ex consideratione passionis. — Tertia est turris *scientiae;* Canticorum quarto: « Sicut turris David collum tuum, quae aedificata est cum propugnaculis. Mille clypei pendent ex ea, omnis armatura fortium » etc.; quod intelligitur de scientia sacrae Scripturae. — Quarta est turris *fortitudinis;* Proverbiorum decimo octavo[^p377-4]: « Turris fortissima, nomen Domini; ad eam currit iustus et exaltabitur »; et in Psalmo: « Deduxisti me, quia factus es spes mea, turris fortitudinis » etc. — Quinta est turris *consilii,* de qua Canticorum septimo[^p377-5]: « Nasus tuus sicut turris Libani, quae respicit contra Damascum »; quia consilium est contra insidias inimici. — Sexta turris est *intellectus,* de qua Canticorum septimo: « Collum tuum sicut turris eburnea; oculi tui sicut piscinae in Hesebon »; ubi datur intelligi munditia cordis et limpiditas intelligentiae spiritualis. — Septima est turris *sapientiae,* de qua Canticorum octavo[^p377-6]: « Ego murus, et ubera mea sicut turris »; quod dicitur propter consolationem, quae est in gustu lactis uberum divinae sapientiae.

Antequam autem huius aedificii altitudo erigatur, necessaria est aedificatio *aedificii septem virtutum,* de quo Proverbiorum nono[^p377-7]: « Sapientia aedificavit sibi domum, excidit columnas septem ». Huius autem aedificii Christus est *fundator* et *fundamentum* et etiam *complementum.* Huic tamen aedificationi vir spiritualis comparatur; unde primae ad Corinthios tertio[^p377-8]: « Dei coadiutores sumus, Dei agricultura estis, Dei aedificatio estis ». Omnis igitur, qui proponit ad culmen virtutis ascendere, hic vult turrem aedificare.

58. Secundo quantum ad *provisionem sufficientis stipendii* subdit: *Nonne*[^p377-9] *prius sedens computat sumptus, qui necessarii sunt, si habeat ad perficiendum? Sedere* illud est considerare intra se secreta conscientiae suae; Threnorum tertio: « Sedebit solitarius et tacebit ». *Computare* est praevidere per sollicitam considerationem; unde Glossa[^p377-10]: « Omne, quod agimus, praevenire per studium considerationis debemus ». Et hoc est quod dicitur Proverbiorum quarto: « Oculi tui videant recta, et palpebrae tuae praecedant gressus tuos ». *Sumptus* autem *necessarii* sunt sufficientes conatus mentis, quibus quis cooperatur gratiae Christi *viriliter,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p377-11]: « Viriliter agite, et confortetur cor vestrum, omnes, qui speratis in Domino »; *utiliter,* unde primae ad Corinthios tertio: « Si quis autem superaedificaverit super fundamentum hoc aurum, argentum et lapides pretiosos » etc.; *efficaciter;* ad Hebraeos duodecimo[^p377-12]: « Videte, ne quis desit gratiae Dei » etc.; *perseveranter;* tertii Regum septimo: « Perfecit omne opus, quod faciebat Salomon in domo Domini ». — Hac aedificatione perfecta, Dominus apparet ad habitandum; unde tertii Regum nono[^p377-13]: « Factum est, cum perfecisset Salomon aedificium domus Domini et aedificium regis et omne, quod optaverat, apparuit ei Dominus ». Hi igitur sumptus sufficientes ad perficiendum consistunt in proposito voluntatis agentis *viriliter, utiliter, efficaciter* et *perseveranter.* Sed si quid deest, Dominus supplet; unde ad Philippenses secundo[^p377-14]: « Deus est, qui operatur in nobis velle et perficere pro bona voluntate ». Qui vult ergo opus altum incipere debet praevidere, si velit perseverare; quia, sicut dicitur supra nono, « nemo mittens manum ad aratrum » etc.

<!-- page 378 -->
59. (Vers. 29.). Tertio quantum ad *declinationem imminentis opprobrii* subiungit: *Ne, posteaquam posuerit fundamentum,* scilicet altiorem statum aggrediendo in imitando Christum; et hoc est *ponere fundamentum;* primae ad Corinthios tertio[^p378-1]: « Fundamentum aliud nemo potest ponere praeter id quod positum est, quod est Christus Iesus ». Hoc fundamentum incipit quis ponere per fidem et bonam operationem, secundum illud Matthaei septimo[^p378-2]: « Omnis, qui audit verba mea haec et facit ea, similis est homini, qui aedificavit domum suam supra petram ». In hac *petra* fundamentum habitationis collocat qui Christum vult perfecte imitari, secundum illud Ieremiae quadragesimo octavo[^p378-3]: « Relinquite civitates et habitate in petra, habitatores Moab » etc. — *Et non potuerit perficere,* et hoc perseverando, sicut sunt illi, de quibus dicitur supra octavo[^p378-4]: « Ad tempus credunt et in tempore tentationis recedunt ». Tales sunt qui destruunt *recidivando,* quidquid aedificaverunt de bono *inchoando;* Ecclesiastici trigesimo quarto[^p378-5]: « Unus aedificans, et alter destruens, quid prodest illis nisi labor »? Tales sunt *dissoluti et remissi;* Proverbiorum decimo octavo: « Qui mollis et dissolutus est in opere suo frater est sua opera dissipantis ». — *Omnes, qui vident*[^p378-6], inceptam perfectionem circumspiciendo; primae ad Corinthios quarto: « Spectaculum facti sumus mundo, et Angelis et hominibus ». Quod enim in alto ponitur omnibus ostenditur; Matthaei quinto: « Non potest civitas abscondi supra montem posita, neque accendunt lucernam et ponunt eam sub modio, sed super candelabrum, ut luceat omnibus, qui in domo sunt ».

60. (Vers. 29. 30.). *Incipiant illudere ei, dicentes: Quia hic homo coepit aedificare et non potuit consummare;* et hoc contumelias inferendo et deridendo, quia valde est opprobriosum retrocedentibus et apostatantibus. Nam deridentur a daemonibus; Threnorum primo[^p378-7]: « Viderunt eam hostes et deriserunt Sabbata eius ». Hanc irrisionem abhorret vir spiritualis; Psalmus: « Neque irrideant me inimici mei » etc. Hi inimici derident imperfectionem, secundum illud Nehemiae quarto[^p378-8]: « Dixit Tobias Ammanites: Aedificent Iudaei, ascendent vulpes, transilient murum eorum lapideum ». Unde Ambrosius[^p378-9]: « In omni, quod agimus, occultos adversarios considerare debemus, qui nostris operibus insidiantur; et nisi contra eos vigilemus, patimur eos irrisores, quos ad malum habemus persuasores ». Qui autem retrocedit ab incepto deridetur pro *temeritate* in praesumendo, pro *instabilitate* in prosequendo, pro *pusillanimitate* in desistendo; et hoc non solum a diabolo, verum etiam a mundo; Ecclesiastici vigesimo[^p378-10]: « Fatuo non erit amicus, et non erit gratia in bonis illius »; et post: « Quoties et quanti deridebunt eum » etc.

61. (Vers. 31.). *Aut quis rex iturus committere* etc. Supra proposuit parabolam de homine aedificante, hic proponit parabolam *de rege proeliante*[^p378-11]; in qua quidem parte tria introducuntur, scilicet *propositio belli, praevisio periculi* et *provisio remedii.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *propositionem belli* dicit: *Aut quis rex iturus committere bellum adversus alium regem. Rex* iste est quilibet, qui se regit secundum legem Dei; Proverbiorum vigesimo[^p378-12]: « Rex, qui sedet in solio iudicii » etc.; et Apocalypsis quinto: « Fecisti nos Deo nostro regnum, et regnabimus super terram ». Istius est *committere bellum;* quia, Iob septimo[^p378-13], « militia est vita hominis super terram ». Hoc autem tripliciter habet intelligi: vel, quod quis committat bellum cum *rege superbiae,* de quo Iob quadragesimo primo[^p378-14]: « Ipse est rex super omnes filios superbiae »; vel cum *rege iustitiae,* de quo in Psalmo: « Reges eos in virga » etc.; vel cum *rege supernae providentiae,* de quo Ecclesiastae quinto: « Insuper rex universae terrae imperat » etc. — Committit autem bellum cum *rege superbiae,* quando ab ipso dissentit; ad Ephesios sexto[^p378-15]: « Non est nobis colluctatio adversus carnem et sanguinem, sed adversus mundi rectores tenebrarum harum ». — Committit etiam bellum cum *rege iustitiae,* quando vult in conspectu Dei se

<!-- page 379 -->
iustificare, quia dicitur Ieremiae secundo[^p379-1]: « Ecce, ego iudicio contendam tecum, pro eo quod dixeris: Non peccavi ». Sic et Iob volebat committere, qui dicebat decimo tertio: « Ad Omnipotentem loquar et disputare cum Deo cupio »; et Ieremiae duodecimo: « Iustus quidem tu es, Domine, si disputem tecum; verumtamen iusta loquar ad te ». — Committit etiam bellum cum *rege supernae providentiae,* cum se parat ad conflictum patientiae, sicut dicitur Iob sexto[^p379-2]: « Quis det, ut veniat petitio mea, et quod exspecto tribuat mihi Deus; et qui coepit, ipse me conterat »? Sic tripliciter cum rege tripliciter dicto committitur bellum. — Primum committit *poenitentia* de peccatis, secundum *confidentia* de meritis, tertium autem *patientia* in flagellis.

62. Secundo quantum ad *praevisionem periculi* subdit: *Nonne*[^p379-3] *sedens prius cogitat,* ad providendum, secundum illud Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo: « Utinam saperent et intelligerent ac novissima providerent »! Propter quod ad Ephesios quinto: « Videte, quomodo caute ambuletis, non quasi insipientes, sed ut sapientes, redimentes tempus, quoniam dies mali sunt. Propterea nolite fieri imprudentes » etc. — *Si possit cum decem millibus occurrere,* scilicet ad resistendum, *ei qui cum viginti millibus venit ad se,* ad impetendum. Et hoc tripliciter habet exponi sicut et praecedens[^p379-4]. Nam si exponatur de *rege superbiae,* occurrimus ei per opera virtutum, quae in medietate consistunt, quae ideo *decem millia* dicuntur propter observantiam decem mandatorum; et adversarius venit cum numero geminato, quia cuilibet virtuti respondet duplex vitium ex opposito secundum superabundantiam et defectum. Unde quia pluribus modis est obliquari quam dirigi, *plures* dicuntur esse in exercitu regis nobis contrarii; unde Iudith primo[^p379-5]: « Gloriabatur quasi potens in potentia exercitus sui et in gloria quadrigarum suarum ». Sed non est fugiendum nec timendum, quia, sicut dicitur primi Machabaeorum tertio[^p379-6], « facile est, concludi multos in manus paucorum; et non est differentia in conspectu Dei caeli liberare in paucis et in multis; quoniam non in multitudine exercitus victoria belli, sed de caelo fortitudo est ».

63. Si autem intelligatur de *rege iustitiae,* occurrimus cum *decem millibus,* id est cum confidentia meritorum ex observantia decem mandatorum; et ipse cum *viginti millibus,* hoc est cum severitate iudiciorum, in quibus fit discussio operum et cogitationum. Unde Beda[^p379-7]: « Cum decem millibus occurrit qui offert opera decem mandatorum, sed Deus quasi cum duplicato numero contra eum venit, cum vix praeparatum in solo opere discutit de opere simul et cogitatione ». — Ex quo datur intelligi, quod sicut non est tutum cum *decem millibus* impugnare *viginti millia;* sic non est securum cum iudice illo contendere. Propter quod Iob nono[^p379-8]: « Vere scio, quod non iustificetur homo comparatus Deo. Sed si voluerit contendere cum eo, non poterit ei respondere unum pro mille ». Unde Malachiae tertio: « Ecce, venit, dicit Dominus exercituum; et quis poterit cogitare diem adventus eius? Ipse enim quasi ignis conflans » etc.; et in Psalmo: « Tu terribilis es, et quis resistet tibi »? — Si vero intelligatur de *rege supernae providentiae,* cum *decem millibus* vadit qui vult pro Christo sustinere magna et multa, sed ipse cum *viginti millibus,* quia pro nobis sustinuit incomparabiliter plura, cum ipse sit[^p379-9] « gigas geminae substantiae »; propter quod in Psalmo: « Quid retribuam Domino pro omnibus, quae retribuit mihi »? et Michaeae sexto: « Quid dignum offeram Domino » etc.

64. (Vers. 32.). Tertio quantum ad *provisionem remedii* subdit: *Alioquin, adhuc illo longe agente, legationem mittens rogat ea quae pacis sunt,* videlicet si suam recognoscit infirmitatem respectu istius triplicis pugnae, laborat ad illius pugnae declinationem[^p379-10]. Missio autem istius legationis nihil aliud est quam missio orationis, quam pro nobis portant Angeli ante conspectum Dei tanquam nostri legati; Tobiae duodecimo[^p379-11]: « Cum orabas et eleemosynas faciebas et mortuos sepeliebas, ego obtuli orationem

<!-- page 380 -->
tuam coram Domino ». Haec legatio mittenda est pro *repellenda pugna tentationum,* quae veniunt a *rege superbiae;* sicut orabat Paulus, de quo secundae ad Corinthios duodecimo[^p380-1]: « Datus est mihi stimulus carnis meae, angelus satanae, qui me colaphizet; propter quod ter Dominum rogavi » etc. — Item, mittenda est haec legatio pro *repellenda pugna divinarum disceptationum,* quae venit a *rege iustitiae,* sicut orabat Propheta in Psalmo[^p380-2]: « Non intres in iudicium cum servo tuo, Domine, quia non iustificabitur in conspectu tuo omnis vivens » etc. — Mittenda etiam est legatio pro *repellenda pugna tribulationum,* quae venit a *rege providentiae,* sicut orabat Ionas Ionae secundo[^p380-3]: « Oravit Ionas ad Dominum Deum suum de utero piscis et dixit: Clamavi ad Dominum de tribulatione mea, et exaudivit me ». — Ex quo colligitur, quod consideratio periculi excitat ad procurationem remedii; unde Glossa[^p380-4]: « Nos fragilitatis nostrae insufficientiam attendentes, ad impetrandam severi iudicis pacem debemus praemittere legationem lacrymarum, bonorum operum et purarum affectionum ». Ideo dicitur Ecclesiastici decimo octavo[^p380-5]: « Ante languorem adhibe medicinam et ante iudicium interroga te ipsum, et in conspectu Dei invenies propitiationem. Ante languorem humilia te et in tempore infirmitatis ostende conversationem tuam », ut sic per legationem sacerdotum invenias Regem placatum.

65. (Vers. 33.). *Sic ergo omnis ex vobis* etc. Postquam praemisit exemplum parabolicum de aedificante et exemplum de proeliante, hic tertio ponit *adaptationem utriusque*[^p380-6]. Et quoniam haec exempla introducta erant ad excitandum nos ad providentiam rei opportunae et ad cautelam rei nocivae et ad dandam intelligentiam spiritualem utriusque; ideo in hac parte introducitur praedictorum exemplorum adaptatio quantum ad *providentiam rei opportunae,* quantum ad *cautelam rei nocivae* et quantum ad *spiritualem intelligentiam utriusque.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *providentiam rei opportunae* dicit: *Sic ergo omnis ex vobis, qui non renuntiaverit*[^p380-7] *omnibus, quae possidet,* per contemptum temporalium; *non potest meus esse discipulus,* per perfectionem spiritualium; quasi dicat: sicut aedificans non potest consummare sine sumtibus, nec pugnans secure pugnam aggredi sine militibus[^p380-8]; sic nullus perfecte potest sequi Christum, nisi renuntiet omnibus.

66. Et nota, quod duplex est Christi discipulatus: unus *necessitatis,* sicut dicitur Actuum nono[^p380-9] et sexto, quod *discipuli* consueverunt vocari illi qui nunc dicuntur *Christiani,* de qua disciplina Isaiae primo: « Quiescite agere perverse, discite benefacere ». Alius est discipulatus *supererogationis,* quo quis Christum sequitur in consiliis evangelicis, de quo Matthaei quinto[^p380-10]: « Videns Iesus turbas, ascendit in montem; et cum sedisset, accesserunt ad eum discipuli eius ». Primo discipulatui necessarium est *omnibus renuntiare,* quantum ad terrenorum contemptum; secundo autem necessarium est *omnia relinquere,* non solum quantum ad affectum, verum etiam quantum ad rem. Unde Glossa[^p380-11]: « Hoc distat inter *renuntiare omnibus* et *relinquere omnia,* quia *renuntiare* convenit omnibus, qui ita licite utuntur mundanis, quae possident, ut tamen mente tendant ad aeterna. *Relinquere* est tantummodo perfectorum, qui omnia temporalia postponunt et solis aeternis inhiant », et hoc necessarium est perfectis Christi discipulis, ut conformes sint paupertati Magistri. Unde super illud Matthaei decimo nono[^p380-12]: « Ecce, nos reliquimus omnia et secuti sumus te », Bernardus: « Bene, Petre, et non ad insipientiam tibi, quia currentem sequi non poteras oneratus. *Exsultavit* enim *ut gigas ad currendam viam* ». Cum enim primum sit inter Christi documenta[^p380-13]: « Beati pauperes spiritu »; qui hoc ignorat per consequens nescit, « quae sint elementa exordii sermonum » Christi; ac per hoc Christi discipulus non est nec ipsum intelligit. Et ideo Antiochus, qui interpretatur *silentium paupertatis*[^p380-14], subvertit fundamenta Ierusalem, quia non potest esse discipulus pauperis Crucifixi qui consilium paupertatis contemnit.

67. (Vers. 34.). Secundo quantum ad *cautelam rei nocivae* subdit: *Bonum est sal, si autem sal evanuerit.* Per *sal* intelligitur perfectus quisque discipulus, qui exemplo et verbo debet condire corda aliorum; unde Matthaei quinto[^p380-15]: « Vos estis sal terrae »; quod dicitur, quia *discretionem* summam

<!-- page 381 -->
debet habere in *conversatione,* secundum illud ad Colossenses quarto: « Sermo vester semper in gratia sale sit conditus »; quia, sicut non potest comedi cibus, nisi sit sale conditus, sic nec stomachus cordis verbum suscipit indiscretum; Iob sexto: « Nunquid poterit comedi insulsum, quod non est sale conditum »? Debet etiam habere *sapientiam* in *contemplatione;* unde Levitici secundo[^p381-1] praecipitur, quod in omni sacrificio offeratur sal. Et talis, qui prudens est et sapiens, recte verbo et exemplo condimentum est Ecclesiae; unde Glossa[^p381-2]: « Sal cibos condit, vermes necat, carnes siccat; sic praedicatio a vermibus vitiorum et putredine illaesam servat Creatori suo humanam naturam ». — Sed tunc *sal evanescit,* quando Christi discipulus efficitur indevotus in contemplatione et indiscretus in actione et in praedicatione, sicut de illis dicitur ad Romanos primo[^p381-3]: « Evanuerunt in cogitationibus suis, et obscuratum est insipiens cor eorum » etc.; et hoc est maximum periculum. — Ideo subdit: *In quo condietur?* quasi dicat: in nullo; unde Ecclesiastici duodecimo[^p381-4]: « Quis miserebitur incantatori a serpente percusso »? quasi dicat, quod talis prorsus est despectibilis et inutilis tam coram Deo quam coram hominibus, tam in actibus spiritualibus quam in temporalibus.

68. (Vers. 35.). Ideo addit: *Neque in terra neque in sterquilinio*[^p381-5] *utile est, sed foras mittetur.* Glossa: « Quia qui retro vadit neque ipse fructum ferre neque alios excolere valet »; immo, quod plus est, reddit terram sterilem, quia vir perfectus cadens suo malo exemplo alios reddit inutiles ad bonum faciendum. Et ideo vir retrocedens comparatur uxori Lot, de qua dicitur Genesis decimo nono[^p381-6], quod « respiciens retro, versa est in statuam salis »; et ideo Proverbiorum sexto: « Homo apostata, vir inutilis, gradiens ore perverso » etc. — Sicut igitur *aedificium inceptum et non consummatum* ad nihilum utile est, et homo, qui incepit et non perfecit, ab omnibus deridetur; et similiter ille qui *in proelio superatur:* sic et *discipulus apostatans* despicitur tanquam *sal infatuatum;* unde Matthaei quinto[^p381-7]: « Ad nihilum valet ultra, nisi ut mittatur foras et conculcetur ab hominibus ».

69. Tertio quantum ad *spiritualem intelligentiam utriusque* subiungit: *Qui habet aures audiendi audiat;* in quo invitat ad perfectam intelligentiam, ut non solum verba praedicta audiantur quoad *sonum vocalem,* de quo dicitur Matthaei decimo tertio[^p381-8]: « Incrassatum est cor populi huius, et auribus graviter audierunt »; verum etiam quoad *intellectum spiritualem,* secundum illud Iob ultimo: « Auditu auris audivi te, nunc oculus meus videt te »; Psalmus: « Audiam, quid loquatur in me Dominus Deus » etc.; et etiam quoad *effectum manualem;* Iacobi primo[^p381-9]: « Estote factores verbi, non auditores tantum ». Vult igitur dicere, quod verba praemissa sunt *parabolica;* et ideo sunt spiritualiter et non carnaliter audienda et discutienda, secundum illud Proverbiorum primo[^p381-10]: « Audiens sapiens sapientior erit, et intelligens gubernacula possidebit; animadvertet parabolam et interpretationem, verba sapientum et aenigmata eorum ». Qui hoc modo audiunt verbum Dei efficacissime suscipiunt, iuxta illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto[^p381-11]: « Qui audiunt me non confundentur, et qui operantur in me non peccabunt. Qui elucidant me vitam aeternam habebunt ».

#### Third the multitudes following are schooled, of which two things.

51. (Vers. 25.). *And there went great multitudes.* After he confuted those who calumniate and schooled the guests at table, here now thirdly he *forms those who accompany him*[^p375-3]. Now this part is divided into two. In the first he schools *those who accompany him through express words;* but in the second through *parabolic examples,* there[^p375-4]: *For which of you having a mind to build a tower* etc. — Now schooling through *express words* for accompanying Christ spiritually, he proceeds in this order, namely by expressing *the sign, the beginning* and *the completion of spiritual companionship.*

First, then, as to *the sign of spiritual companionship* he says: *And there went great multitudes with him,* in sign of spiritual companionship; whence the Gloss[^p375-5]: « *There went multitudes with him,* taken, namely, by the sweetness of the preaching and of the miracles »; whence they could say that of John, the sixth chapter: « Lord, to whom shall we go? Thou hast the words of eternal life ». And note here that the Pharisees *calumniated* Christ, and the multitudes *accompanied* him, because him whom the proud despise the humble accompany; whence John, the seventh chapter[^p375-6]: « Hath any one of the rulers believed in him? But this multitude, that knoweth not the law, are accursed ». Now the multitudes followed Jesus as *sheep their shepherd;* Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p375-7]: « Jesus seeing the multitudes, had compassion on them: because they were distressed, and lying like sheep that have no shepherd ». Again, they followed as *peoples their king;* Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p375-8]: « The multitudes that went before and that followed, cried: Hosanna » etc. Again, they followed as *disciples their teacher;* above, the fifth chapter: « It came to pass, that when the multitudes pressed upon him to hear the word of God, he stood by the lake of Genesareth ».

52. (Vers. 25. 26.). Second, as to *the beginning of spiritual companionship* he subjoins: *And turning, he said to them; turning,* I say, through *kindness;* the Psalm[^p375-9]: « O God, thou turning wilt bring us to life »; *he said,* through *the truth of doctrine: If any man come to me,* through *the supererogation of justice;* Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « Come to me all you that labour and are burdened ». — *And hate not his father, and mother, and wife, and children, and brethren, and sisters, yea and his own soul also,* through *the mortification of carnal affection,* according to that of the Psalm[^p375-10]: « Hearken, O daughter, and see, and incline thy ear » etc.; *he cannot be my disciple,* through *the taking up of evangelical perfection,* of which Luke, the sixth chapter: « Every one shall be perfect, if he be as his master ». — And the reason of this is that the beginning of the spiritual life is to put off carnal affection, because, the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p375-11], « the sensual man perceiveth not these things that are of the Spirit of God; for it is foolishness to him, and he cannot understand » etc.; and Isaias, the twenty-eighth chapter: « Whom shall he teach knowledge? and whom shall he make to understand the hearing? them that are weaned from the milk » etc., that is, separated from carnal affection.

53. Whence it is to be noted here that he does not here command hatred of parents as to *nature,* because this would be of impiety, since it is said in the first of John, the third chapter[^p375-12]: « Whosoever hateth his brother is a murderer »; but as to *fault,* as Augustine says: « Men are so to be loved, that their errors be not loved ». Of which hatred in the Psalm[^p375-13]: « Have I not hated them, O Lord, that hated thee: and pined away because of thy enemies »? and after: « I have hated them with a perfect hatred » etc. Whence Gregory: « We can both hate and love at once, so that we both love those joined to us by kinship out of the divine precept, and, by hating and fleeing, do not know those whom we suffer as adversaries in the way of God ». Whence that hatred comes not from cruelty, but from charity, as also the hatred of one's own soul, which we ought to hate, not as to salvation, but as to carnality. Whence Gregory[^p375-14]: « Then do we hate our soul, when we struggle against its carnal affections ». Of this hatred John, the twelfth chapter: « He that hateth his

<!-- page 376 -->
soul in this world, keepeth it unto life eternal ». Augustine: « If thou hast loved ill, then thou hast hated; if thou hast hated well, then thou hast loved. Happy they who so hate by keeping, lest they lose by loving ». This is what is said more expressly in Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter[^p376-1]: « My son, go not after thy lusts, but turn away from thy own will. If thou give to thy soul her desires, she will make thee come to be a joy to thy enemies ». As, then, the beginning of the spiritual *life* is *not to covet,* where all evil is forbidden[^p376-2], and *to decline from evil;* so the foundation of spiritual *companionship* is *to decline all carnality,* whether towards oneself or towards one's parents. On account of which to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter[^p376-3]: « Put off, according to former conversation, the old man, who is corrupted according to the desire of error ».

54. (Vers. 27.). Third, as to *the completion of spiritual companionship* he subjoins: *And whosoever doth not carry his cross,* through *perfect mortification of the flesh*[^p376-4]*; and come after me,* through *right intention; cannot be my disciple,* through *true imitation.* — And note that here is *the consummation* of the discipleship and companionship of Christ, namely in carrying the cross, because there Christ consummated, as is said in John, the nineteenth chapter[^p376-5]: « And bearing his own cross, he went forth to that place which is called Calvary ». In this cross of Christ is the highest *humility;* to the Philippians, the second chapter[^p376-6]: « He humbled himself, becoming obedient unto death, even to the death of the cross »; the highest *poverty,* so that Christ could truly say that of Job, the first chapter[^p376-7]: « Naked came I out of my mother's womb, and naked shall I return thither ». Whence in John, the nineteenth chapter, it is said that « the soldiers, when they had crucified him, took his garments, and they made four parts ». There is also there the highest *austerity,* according to that of the Psalm[^p376-8]: « They have dug my hands and feet. They have numbered all my bones ».

55. *To carry the cross,* then, is to take up *humility* in the heart, *austerity* in the flesh and *poverty* in suffering, against those *three radical things* and against the *threefold excuse* of those who would not come to the supper[^p376-9]. This cross was *Christ's,* because Christ took it up willingly; likewise he who wishes to follow Christ must carry it *willingly,* so as to bear it as *his own* cross out of his own will, not under constraint as *another's,* and so be not an imitator of Christ, but rather of Simon, of whom Mark, the fifteenth chapter[^p376-10]: « They forced one Simon a Cyrenian to take up his cross ». To this cross Christ most of all *invites* us, who first carried it as a standard and royal sign; whence to the Hebrews, the last chapter[^p376-11]: « Christ suffered without the gate. Let us go forth therefore to him » etc. He then who will not follow thus is not Christ's disciple, because he will not imitate him in whom he offered himself to us as exemplar and master; whence to the Galatians, the fifth chapter[^p376-12]: « And they that are Christ's have crucified their flesh with the vices and concupiscences ». On account of which the second to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « Always bearing about in our body the mortification of the cross, that the life also of Jesus may be made manifest in our bodies ». Whence Chrysostom[^p376-13]: « Let no one be ashamed of the cross; let us bear it about as a crown; for all things that concern us are accomplished through the cross: regeneration, consecration. For this reason we write it on the house, on the doors, on the ways, on the foreheads and in the mind ». This, then, is to be carried as the sign of the living God, of which the Apocalypse, the seventh chapter[^p376-14]: « I saw another Angel ascending from the rising of the sun, having the sign of the living God; and he cried with a loud voice to the four Angels, to whom it was given to hurt the earth and the sea, saying: Hurt not the earth, nor the sea, nor the trees, till we sign the servants of God in their foreheads », so that it may be known, namely, manifestly who is Christ's disciple.

56. *For which of you having a mind to build* etc. After he formed those who accompany him, and this through express words, here secondly he forms the same *through parabolic examples*[^p376-15]. And since we ought to imitate Christ in *actions* and *sufferings* — in the first we are like *those who build,* in the second like *those who do battle* — therefore first in this part he introduces the parable of *the man building.* But secondly he adjoins the parable and example of *the king doing battle,* there[^p376-16]: *Or what king, about to go.* But in the third he subjoins *the adaptation of both,* there: *So likewise every one of you that shall not have renounced.*

<!-- page 377 -->
Concerning the parable of *the man building* three things are introduced, namely *the conceiving of the purpose of building, the provision of a sufficient outlay, the avoidance of the threatening reproach.*

57. (Vers. 28.). First, then, as to *the conceiving of the purpose of building* he says: *For which of you having a mind to build a tower*[^p377-1]. By *the tower,* which is the highest building, we can understand the heap of the perfection of merits, which ascends by degrees from the lowest to the highest, beginning from *the foundation of fear* and coming even to *the summit of deiform wisdom.* And first is to be built the tower of *the gift of fear,* of which can be understood that of Micheas, the fourth chapter[^p377-2]: « Thou, cloudy tower of the flock, unto thee shall it come », because fear makes one a little one from the consideration of judgement. — The second tower is of *piety,* of which Isaias, the fifth chapter[^p377-3]: « He built a tower in the midst of the vineyard, and set up a winepress therein », because piety arises from the consideration of the passion. — The third is the tower of *knowledge;* the Canticles, the fourth chapter: « Thy neck is as the tower of David, which is built with bulwarks: a thousand bucklers hang upon it, all the armour of valiant men » etc.; which is understood of the knowledge of sacred Scripture. — The fourth is the tower of *fortitude;* Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter[^p377-4]: « A most strong tower, the name of the Lord: the just runneth to it, and shall be exalted »; and in the Psalm: « Thou hast conducted me, for thou art become my hope, a tower of strength » etc. — The fifth is the tower of *counsel,* of which the Canticles, the seventh chapter[^p377-5]: « Thy nose is as the tower of Libanus, that looketh toward Damascus »; because counsel is against the snares of the enemy. — The sixth tower is of *understanding,* of which the Canticles, the seventh chapter: « Thy neck as a tower of ivory. Thy eyes like the fishpools in Hesebon »; where is given to be understood the cleanness of heart and the limpidity of spiritual intelligence. — The seventh is the tower of *wisdom,* of which the Canticles, the eighth chapter[^p377-6]: « I am a wall: and my breasts are as a tower »; which is said on account of the consolation which is in the taste of the milk of the breasts of divine wisdom.

But before the height of this building is raised, there is necessary the building of *the building of the seven virtues,* of which Proverbs, the ninth chapter[^p377-7]: « Wisdom hath built herself a house, she hath hewn her out seven pillars ». Now of this building Christ is *the founder* and *the foundation* and also *the completion.* To this building, however, the spiritual man is compared; whence the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p377-8]: « We are God's coadjutors: you are God's husbandry; you are God's building ». Every one, therefore, who purposes to ascend to the summit of virtue, he wishes to build a tower.

58. Second, as to *the provision of a sufficient outlay* he subjoins: *Doth he not*[^p377-9] *first sit down, and reckon the charges that are necessary, whether he have wherewithal to finish it? To sit down* is to consider within himself the secrets of his conscience; Lamentations, the third chapter: « He shall sit solitary, and hold his peace ». *To reckon* is to foresee through solicitous consideration; whence the Gloss[^p377-10]: « Everything that we do we ought to forestall by the zeal of consideration ». And this is what is said in Proverbs, the fourth chapter: « Let thy eyes see right things, and let thy eyelids go before thy steps ». Now *the necessary charges* are sufficient endeavours of the mind, by which one cooperates with the grace of Christ *manfully,* according to that of the Psalm[^p377-11]: « Do ye manfully, and let your heart be strengthened, all ye that hope in the Lord »; *usefully,* whence the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter: « Now if any man build upon this foundation, gold, silver, precious stones » etc.; *efficaciously;* to the Hebrews, the twelfth chapter[^p377-12]: « See that no man be wanting to the grace of God » etc.; *perseveringly;* the third of Kings, the seventh chapter: « Solomon finished all the work that he was making in the house of the Lord ». — This building being finished, the Lord appears to dwell there; whence the third of Kings, the ninth chapter[^p377-13]: « It came to pass, when Solomon had finished the building of the house of the Lord, and the king's building, and all that he desired, the Lord appeared to him ». These sufficient charges for finishing, then, consist in the purpose of the will of one acting *manfully, usefully, efficaciously* and *perseveringly.* But if anything is wanting, the Lord supplies it; whence to the Philippians, the second chapter[^p377-14]: « It is God who worketh in us both to will and to accomplish, according to his good will ». He then who wishes to begin a lofty work ought to foresee whether he will persevere; because, as is said above, the ninth chapter, « no man putting his hand to the plough » etc.

59. (Vers. 29.). Third, as to *the avoidance of the threatening reproach* he subjoins: *Lest, after he hath laid the foundation,* namely by undertaking a higher state in imitating Christ; and this is *to lay the foundation;* the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p378-1]: « Other foundation no man can lay, but that which is laid; which is Christ Jesus ». This foundation one begins to lay through faith and good working, according to that of Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p378-2]: « Every one that heareth these my words, and doth them, is like to a man that built his house upon a rock ». On this *rock* he places the foundation of his dwelling who wishes to imitate Christ perfectly, according to that of Jeremias, the forty-eighth chapter[^p378-3]: « Leave the cities, and dwell in the rock, you that dwell in Moab » etc. — *And is not able to finish it,* and this by persevering, as are those of whom it is said above, the eighth chapter[^p378-4]: « For a while they believe, and in time of temptation they fall away ». Such are they who destroy *by relapsing* whatever they built of good *by beginning;* Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p378-5]: « One buildeth up, and another pulleth down: what profit have they but the labour »? Such are *the dissolute and remiss;* Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter: « He that is soft and dissolute in his work, is the brother of him that wasteth his own works ». — *All that see it*[^p378-6], by looking round upon the perfection begun; the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « We are made a spectacle to the world, and to Angels, and to men ». For what is placed on high is shown to all; Matthew, the fifth chapter: « A city seated on a mountain cannot be hid. Neither do men light a lamp and put it under a bushel, but upon a candlestick, that it may shine to all that are in the house ».

60. (Vers. 29. 30.). *Begin to mock him, saying: This man began to build, and was not able to finish;* and this by heaping reproaches and deriding, because it is very reproachful to those who go back and apostatize. For they are derided by the demons; Lamentations, the first chapter[^p378-7]: « The enemies have seen her, and have mocked at her sabbaths ». This mockery the spiritual man abhors; the Psalm: « Neither let my enemies laugh at me » etc. These enemies deride imperfection, according to that of Nehemias, the fourth chapter[^p378-8]: « Tobias the Ammonite said: Let the Jews build; foxes shall go up, they shall leap over their stone wall ». Whence Ambrose[^p378-9]: « In everything that we do we ought to consider the hidden adversaries who lie in wait for our works; and unless we watch against them, we suffer as mockers those whom we have as persuaders to evil ». But he who goes back from what he began is derided for *rashness* in presuming, for *instability* in pursuing, for *pusillanimity* in desisting; and this not only by the devil, but also by the world; Ecclesiasticus, the twentieth chapter[^p378-10]: « A fool shall have no friend, and there shall be no thanks for his good deeds »; and after: « How often, and how many will laugh him to scorn » etc.

61. (Vers. 31.). *Or what king, about to go to make war* etc. Above he set forth the parable of the man building, here he sets forth the parable *of the king doing battle*[^p378-11]; in which part three things are introduced, namely *the proposing of war, the foreseeing of danger* and *the provision of a remedy.*

First, then, as to *the proposing of war* he says: *Or what king, about to go to make war against another king. This king* is any one who rules himself according to the law of God; Proverbs, the twentieth chapter[^p378-12]: « The king, that sitteth on the throne of judgement » etc.; and the Apocalypse, the fifth chapter: « Thou hast made us to our God a kingdom, and we shall reign on the earth ». It is his *to make war;* because, Job, the seventh chapter[^p378-13], « the life of man upon earth is a warfare ». Now this has to be understood in three ways: either that one makes war with *the king of pride,* of whom Job, the forty-first chapter[^p378-14]: « He is king over all the children of pride »; or with *the king of justice,* of whom in the Psalm: « Thou shalt rule them with a rod » etc.; or with *the king of supernal providence,* of whom Ecclesiastes, the fifth chapter: « Moreover the king commandeth all the land » etc. — Now one makes war with *the king of pride,* when he dissents from him; to the Ephesians, the sixth chapter[^p378-15]: « Our wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but against the rulers of the world of this darkness ». — One makes war also with *the king of justice,* when he wishes to justify himself in the sight of God,

<!-- page 379 -->
because it is said in Jeremias, the second chapter[^p379-1]: « Behold, I will contend with thee in judgement, because thou hast said: I have not sinned ». Thus Job also wished to make war, who said, in the thirteenth chapter: « I will speak to the Almighty, and I desire to reason with God »; and Jeremias, the twelfth chapter: « Thou indeed, O Lord, art just, if I plead with thee; but yet I will speak what is just to thee ». — One makes war also with *the king of supernal providence,* when he prepares himself for the conflict of patience, as is said in Job, the sixth chapter[^p379-2]: « Who will grant that my request may come, and that God may give me what I look for; and that he that hath begun may destroy me »? Thus in three ways war is made with the king said in three ways. — The first penance makes concerning sins, the second confidence concerning merits, but the third patience in scourges.

62. Second, as to *the foreseeing of danger* he subjoins: *Doth he not*[^p379-3] *first sit down and think,* in order to provide, according to that of Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter: « O that they would be wise and would understand, and would provide for their last end »! On account of which to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter: « See how you walk circumspectly: not as unwise, but as wise: redeeming the time, because the days are evil. Therefore become not unwise » etc. — *Whether he be able, with ten thousand, to meet,* namely in order to resist, *him that, with twenty thousand, cometh against him,* in order to attack. And this has to be expounded in three ways, as also the preceding[^p379-4]. For if it is expounded of *the king of pride,* we meet him through the works of the virtues, which consist in a mean, which are therefore called *ten thousand* on account of the observance of the ten commandments; and the adversary comes with the number doubled, because to every virtue there answers a twofold vice from the opposite side, according to superabundance and defect. Whence, because there are more ways of going awry than of being directed, *more* are said to be in the army of the king contrary to us; whence Judith, the first chapter[^p379-5]: « He gloried as a mighty one in the might of his army, and in the glory of his chariots ». But one is not to flee nor to fear, because, as is said in the first of Machabees, the third chapter[^p379-6], « it is an easy matter for many to be shut up in the hands of a few: and there is no difference in the sight of the God of heaven to deliver with few or with many; for the victory of war is not in the multitude of the army, but strength cometh from heaven ».

63. But if it is understood of *the king of justice,* we meet him with *ten thousand,* that is, with confidence of merits from the observance of the ten commandments; and he with *twenty thousand,* that is, with the severity of judgements, in which is made the examination of works and of thoughts. Whence Bede[^p379-7]: « He meets him with ten thousand who offers the works of the ten commandments, but God comes against him as with a doubled number, when he examines him, scarcely prepared in work alone, concerning work and thought together ». — From which it is given to be understood that, as it is not safe to attack *twenty thousand* with *ten thousand;* so it is not secure to contend with that judge. On account of which Job, the ninth chapter[^p379-8]: « Indeed I know that man cannot be justified compared with God. If he will contend with him, he cannot answer him one for a thousand ». Whence Malachias, the third chapter: « Behold, he cometh, saith the Lord of hosts; and who shall be able to think of the day of his coming? For he is like a refining fire » etc.; and in the Psalm: « Thou art terrible, and who shall resist thee »? — But if it is understood of *the king of supernal providence,* he goes with *ten thousand* who wishes to sustain great things and many for Christ, but he with *twenty thousand,* because he sustained incomparably more for us, since he is[^p379-9] « the giant of twin substance »; on account of which in the Psalm: « What shall I render to the Lord, for all the things that he hath rendered to me »? and Micheas, the sixth chapter: « What shall I offer to the Lord that is worthy » etc.

64. (Vers. 32.). Third, as to *the provision of a remedy* he subjoins: *Or else, whilst the other is yet afar off, sending an embassy, he desireth conditions of peace,* that is, if he recognizes his own infirmity in respect of that threefold fight, he labours for the avoidance of that fight[^p379-10]. Now the sending of this embassy is nothing else than the sending of prayer, which the Angels carry for us before the sight of God as our ambassadors; Tobias, the twelfth chapter[^p379-11]: « When thou didst pray, and didst give alms, and didst bury the dead, I offered thy prayer

<!-- page 380 -->
before the Lord ». This embassy is to be sent for *repelling the fight of temptations,* which come from *the king of pride;* as Paul prayed, of whom the second to the Corinthians, the twelfth chapter[^p380-1]: « There was given me a sting of my flesh, an angel of Satan, to buffet me; for which thing thrice I besought the Lord » etc. — Again, this embassy is to be sent for *repelling the fight of the divine disputations,* which comes from *the king of justice,* as the Prophet prayed in the Psalm[^p380-2]: « Enter not into judgement with thy servant, O Lord: for in thy sight no man living shall be justified » etc. — The embassy is to be sent also for *repelling the fight of tribulations,* which comes from *the king of providence,* as Jonas prayed, Jonas, the second chapter[^p380-3]: « Jonas prayed to the Lord his God out of the belly of the fish, and he said: I cried to the Lord out of my tribulation, and he heard me ». — From which it is gathered that the consideration of danger rouses to the procuring of a remedy; whence the Gloss[^p380-4]: « We, attending to the insufficiency of our frailty, ought to send before us, for obtaining the peace of the severe judge, an embassy of tears, of good works and of pure affections ». Therefore it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter[^p380-5]: « Before sickness take a medicine, and before judgement examine thyself, and thou shalt find mercy in the sight of God. Humble thyself before thou art sick, and in the time of infirmity shew thy conversation », so that thus through the embassy of priests thou mayest find the King appeased.

65. (Vers. 33.). *So likewise every one of you* etc. After he set forth the parabolic example of the man building and the example of the man doing battle, here thirdly he sets down *the adaptation of both*[^p380-6]. And since these examples had been introduced to rouse us to providence for the thing that is opportune and to caution against the thing that is harmful, and to give the spiritual intelligence of both; therefore in this part is introduced the adaptation of the aforesaid examples as to *providence for the thing that is opportune,* as to *caution against the thing that is harmful* and as to *the spiritual intelligence of both.*

First, then, as to *providence for the thing that is opportune* he says: *So likewise every one of you that shall not have renounced*[^p380-7] *all that he possesseth,* through contempt of temporal things; *cannot be my disciple,* through the perfection of spiritual things; as if to say: as he who builds cannot finish without charges, nor he who fights safely undertake the fight without soldiers[^p380-8]; so no one can follow Christ perfectly, unless he renounce all things.

66. And note that the discipleship of Christ is twofold: one of *necessity,* as is said in the Acts, the ninth chapter[^p380-9], and the sixth, that those who are now called *Christians* were accustomed to be called *disciples,* of which discipline Isaias, the first chapter: « Cease to do perversely, learn to do well ». The other is the discipleship of *supererogation,* by which one follows Christ in the evangelical counsels, of which Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p380-10]: « Jesus seeing the multitudes, went up into a mountain; and when he was set down, his disciples came unto him ». To the first discipleship it is necessary *to renounce all things,* as to the contempt of earthly things; but to the second it is necessary *to relinquish all things,* not only as to affection, but also as to the thing itself. Whence the Gloss[^p380-11]: « This is the difference between *renouncing all things* and *relinquishing all things,* that *to renounce* befits all who so lawfully use the worldly things which they possess, that yet they tend in mind to the eternal things. *To relinquish* belongs only to the perfect, who put after them all temporal things and gape after the eternal alone », and this is necessary to the perfect disciples of Christ, that they may be conformed to the poverty of the Master. Whence upon that of Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p380-12]: « Behold, we have left all things, and have followed thee », Bernard: « Well done, Peter, and not unto folly for thee, because thou couldst not follow one running while thou wast burdened. For *he hath rejoiced as a giant to run the way* ». For since the first among the lessons of Christ is[^p380-13]: « Blessed are the poor in spirit »; he who is ignorant of this consequently does not know « what are the elements of the beginning of the words » of Christ; and on this account he is not Christ's disciple, nor does he understand him. And therefore Antiochus, who is interpreted *silence of poverty*[^p380-14], overthrew the foundations of Jerusalem, because he cannot be a disciple of the poor Crucified who despises the counsel of poverty.

67. (Vers. 34.). Second, as to *caution against the thing that is harmful* he subjoins: *Salt is good, but if the salt shall lose its savour.* By *salt* is understood every perfect disciple, who by example and word ought to season the hearts of others; whence Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p380-15]: « You are the salt of the earth »; which is said because

<!-- page 381 -->
he ought to have the highest *discretion* in *conversation,* according to that to the Colossians, the fourth chapter: « Let your speech be always in grace seasoned with salt »; because, as food cannot be eaten unless it be seasoned with salt, so neither does the stomach of the heart receive an undiscreet word; Job, the sixth chapter: « Can an unsavoury thing be eaten, that is not seasoned with salt »? He ought also to have *wisdom* in *contemplation;* whence in Leviticus, the second chapter[^p381-1], it is commanded that in every sacrifice salt be offered. And such a one, who is prudent and wise, is rightly by word and example the seasoning of the Church; whence the Gloss[^p381-2]: « Salt seasons foods, kills worms, dries flesh; so preaching keeps human nature unharmed for its Creator from the worms of vices and from rottenness ». — But then *the salt loses its savour,* when the disciple of Christ becomes undevout in contemplation and undiscreet in action and in preaching, as is said of those to the Romans, the first chapter[^p381-3]: « They became vain in their thoughts, and their foolish heart was darkened » etc.; and this is the greatest danger. — Therefore he subjoins: *Wherewith shall it be seasoned?* as if to say: with nothing; whence Ecclesiasticus, the twelfth chapter[^p381-4]: « Who will pity an enchanter struck by a serpent »? as if to say that such a one is altogether despicable and useless as well before God as before men, as well in spiritual acts as in temporal.

68. (Vers. 35.). Therefore he adds: *It is neither profitable for the land nor for the dunghill*[^p381-5]*, but shall be cast out.* The Gloss: « Because he who goes back is able neither himself to bear fruit nor to cultivate others »; nay, what is more, he renders the land barren, because a perfect man falling by his bad example renders others useless for doing good. And therefore the man who goes back is compared to Lot's wife, of whom it is said in Genesis, the nineteenth chapter[^p381-6], that « looking back, she was turned into a statue of salt »; and therefore Proverbs, the sixth chapter: « A man that is an apostate, an unprofitable man, walketh with a perverse mouth » etc. — As, then, *a building begun and not finished* is profitable for nothing, and a man who began and did not finish is derided by all; and likewise he who *is overcome in battle:* so also *the apostatizing disciple* is despised as *salt that has lost its savour;* whence Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p381-7]: « It is good for nothing any more but to be cast out, and to be trodden on by men ».

69. Third, as to *the spiritual intelligence of both* he subjoins: *He that hath ears to hear, let him hear;* in which he invites to perfect intelligence, that the aforesaid words be heard not only as to *the vocal sound,* of which it is said in Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p381-8]: « The heart of this people is grown gross, and with their ears they have been dull of hearing »; but also as to *the spiritual understanding,* according to that of Job, the last chapter: « With the hearing of the ear I have heard thee, but now my eye seeth thee »; the Psalm: « I will hear what the Lord God will speak in me » etc.; and also as to *the manual effect;* James, the first chapter[^p381-9]: « Be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only ». He means, then, to say that the foregoing words are *parabolic;* and therefore they are to be heard and discussed spiritually and not carnally, according to that of Proverbs, the first chapter[^p381-10]: « A wise man shall hear, and shall be wiser: and he that understandeth, shall possess governments. He shall understand a parable, and the interpretation, the words of the wise, and their mysterious sayings ». They who hear in this way receive the word of God most efficaciously, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p381-11]: « They that hear me shall not be confounded, and they that work by me shall not sin. They that explain me shall have life everlasting ».
