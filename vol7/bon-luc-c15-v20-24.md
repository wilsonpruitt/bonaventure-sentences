# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XV, Vers. 20-24
## *Cum autem adhuc longe esset. Post descriptam insolentiam, miseriam et poenitentiam hic quarto describitur pii patris misericordia*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 394 -->
33. *Cum autem adhuc longe esset.* Post descriptam insolentiam, miseriam et poenitentiam hic quarto describitur *pii patris misericordia*[^p394-12]. Dividitur autem haec pars in duas. In quarum prima describitur *misericordia pii patris* in *acceptando conversionem peccatoris;* secundo, in *placando indignationem obedientis,* ibi[^p394-13]: *Erat autem filius eius senior* etc.

*Misericordiam* autem *acceptantem filii prodigi conversionem* manifestat Evangelista quantum ad triplicem actum, scilicet primo, in *suscipiendo indignum;* secundo, *in vestiendo nudum,* ibi[^p394-14]: *Dixit autem pater ad servos suos;* tertio vero, in *reficiendo famelicum,* ibi: *Et adducite vitulum saginatum.* Nam is est ordo reparationis animae per
<!-- page 395 -->
donum misericordiae paternae, quod primo *suscipitur* ad benignitatem gratiae; secundo *vestitur* vestimento iustitiae; tertio *reficitur* suavitate laetitiae et iucunditatis internae. — Circa *misericordiam* igitur *suscipientem indignum* duo introducuntur, scilicet *clementia patris suscipientis* et *reverentia*[^p395-1] *filii se ipsum accusantis.*

34. Primo igitur quantum ad *clementiam patris suscipientis* dicit: *Cum autem adhuc longe esset,* propter culpam a Deo elongantem; Psalmus[^p395-2]: « Longe a salute mea verba delictorum meorum »; et rursus: « Longe a peccatoribus salus, quia iustificationes tuas non custodierunt ». — *Vidit illum pater ipsius et misericordia motus est,* infundendo *gratiam praevenientem,* de quo respectu infra vigesimo secundo[^p395-3]: « Respexit Dominus Petrum »; « et egressus foras Petrus flevit amare ». Et de hoc Exodi tertio: « Vidi afflictionem populi mei, qui est in Aegypto, et descendi, ut liberem eum ». Hunc respectum petebat Propheta, cum dicebat[^p395-4]: « Vide humilitatem meam et laborem meum et dimitte universa delicta mea »; et rursus: « Respice in me et miserere mei » etc. — *Et accurrens cecidit super collum eius,* per *gratiam concomitantem;* quasi dicat: amplexatus est eum, secundum illud Canticorum secundo[^p395-5]: « Laeva eius sub capite meo, et dextera illius amplexabitur me »; et hoc est per *gratiam concomitantem,* quae adiuvat ad portandum iugum divinae legis. Unde Ambrosius[^p395-6]: « *Super collum* filii cecidit, dum leve iugum dilectionis sibi imposuit ». Hoc enim *iugum* adiuvat ad portandum onus. *Amplexus* divini brachii est humanitas Verbi incarnati; unde Glossa[^p395-7]: « Brachium, id est Filium, in amplexum revertentis humiliavit »; de quo brachio in Psalmo: « Salvavit sibi dextera eius et brachium sanctum eius ». Et per istum *amplexum* solvuntur vincula peccatorum, secundum illud Isaiae quinquagesimo secundo: « Solve vincula colli tui, captiva filia Sion ». — *Et osculatus est eum,* per *gratiam* scilicet *subsequentem;* osculum enim signum est amoris et pacis. Unde
Beda[^p395-8]: « Rediens osculum caritatis a patre accipit, dum per gratiam certificatur de indulgentia », secundum illud ad Ephesios primo: « In quo credentes signati estis Spiritu promissionis sancto, qui est pignus hereditatis nostrae ». Hoc osculum amoris et pacis petit sponsa Canticorum primo: « Osculetur me osculo oris sui, quia meliora sunt » etc. In hoc nimius[^p395-9] apparet pietatis affectus, quo Deus Pater peccatori redeunti maximum ostendit dilectionis indicium. Et per hoc cognoscit Deus Pater, si sumus eius filii, cum libenter osculamur eum per amorem mutuum; Genesis vigesimo septimo[^p395-10]: « Accede ad me et da mihi osculum, fili mi; et accessit et osculatus est eum »; et sequitur, quod « benedixit ei »; quia benedictionis plenitudo ex hoc osculo procedit. Unde in Psalmo: « Benedixisti, Domine, terram tuam »; et subditur: « Misericordia et veritas obviaverunt sibi, iustitia et pax osculatae sunt ». Istius deosculationis origo est in Verbo incarnato, in quo est unio summi amoris et connexionis duplicis naturae, per quam Deus nos osculatur, et nos Deum deosculamur, secundum illud Canticorum octavo[^p395-11]: « Quis mihi det te fratrem meum, ut inveniam te foris et deosculer te, et iam me nemo despiciat » etc.

35. (Vers. 21.). Secundo quantum ad *reverentiam filii se ipsum accusantis* subdit: *Dixitque filius ei: Pater, peccavi in caelum,* scilicet per *contemptum Maiestatis* omnibus praesidentis; Iob undecimo[^p395-12]: « Excelsior caelo est, et quid facies? Profundior inferno, et unde cognosces »? et vigesimo secundo: « An ignoras, quod Deus excelsior caelo sit » etc. *In caelum* ergo peccat qui Deum caeli contemnit; unde dicitur primi Esdrae nono[^p395-13]: « Iniquitates meae multiplicatae sunt super caput meum, et delicta nostra creverunt usque ad caelum » etc. *Et coram te,* per *contemptum Veritatis* omnia speculantis; Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio[^p395-14]: « Oculi Domini multo plus lucidiores sunt super solem, circumspicientes omnes vias hominum et profundum
<!-- page 396 -->
abyssi, et omnium corda intuentes in absconditas partes. Domino enim Deo nostro, antequam crearentur, omnia sunt agnita, sic et post perfectum inspicit omnia »; Psalmus[^p396-1]: « Tibi soli peccavi et malum coram te feci ». — *Iam non sum dignus vocari filius tuus,* propter *contemptum Bonitatis* omnibus providentis; Sapientiae duodecimo[^p396-2]: « Tu autem, Dominator virtutis, cum tranquillitate iudicas et cum magna reverentia disponis nos. Subest enim tibi, cum volueris, *posse.* Docuisti autem populum tuum, quoniam oportet, iustum esse et humanum, et bonae spei fecisti filios tuos, quoniam iudicans das locum in peccatis poenitentiae ». Quia igitur contempsi hanc summam bonitatem, cuius est dare « spiritum adoptionis filiorum », secundum illud ad Romanos octavo[^p396-3]: « Non accepistis spiritum servitutis iterum in timore, sed spiritum adoptionis filiorum »; cuius etiam est regenerare per aquam et Spiritum sanctum in regnum filiorum Dei, secundum illud ad Colossenses primo: « Eripuit nos de potestate tenebrarum et transtulit in regnum filii caritatis suae »: *iam non sum dignus vocari filius tuus.*

36. Et nota, quod licet pater sibi occurrat, collum stringat per amplexum, licet suscipiat ad osculum, tamen verus poenitens nunquam obliviscitur suum peccatum, secundum illud Psalmi[^p396-4]: « Quoniam ego in flagella paratus sum, et dolor meus in conspectu meo semper. Quoniam iniquitatem meam annuntiabo et cogitabo pro peccato meo ». Licet autem recognoscat, nihilominus anhelat ad *caritatem perfectam.* Et ideo non petit hic *dilectionem mercenariam,* sicut supra[^p396-5] faciebat, cum non haberet adhuc gratiam infusam. Sed nunc ad patrem plene conversus, tanquam vere poenitens commendat et postulat gratiam divinam; et hoc est quod dicit Glossa[^p396-6]: « Vult, inquit, per gratiam fieri quod fatetur se indignum per meritum », sicut ille poenitens petiit secundi Paralipomenon ultimo: « Nunc flecto genua mea coram te, precans bonitatem tuam. Peccavi, Domine, et iniquitatem meam agnosco; peto rogans: Domine, remitte mihi, ne simul perdas me cum iniquitatibus meis neque in aeternum
reserves mala mihi, quia indignum salvabis me secundum magnam misericordiam tuam, et laudabo te semper omnibus diebus vitae meae; quoniam te laudat virtus caelorum, et tibi est gloria in saecula saeculorum ».

37. (Vers. 22.). *Dixit autem pater ad servos suos.* Post ostensam misericordiam patris in suscipiendo indignum subditur hic *misericordia in vestiendo nudum*[^p396-7]. Et quoniam peccator nudatur a *veste interioris puritatis* et *ornatu exterioris honestatis;* ideo quantum ad haec duo ostendit, filium prodigum per patris misericordiam relevari.

Primo igitur quantum ad *vestem interioris puritatis* dicit: *Dixit autem pater ad servos suos: Cito proferte stolam primam et induite illum. Prima stola* est vestis *interioris puritatis,* qua mens nostra debet indui et qua a principio suae conditionis induta fuit. Unde Beda[^p396-8]: « Stola prima vestis est innocentiae, in qua primus homo conditus est; sed post culpam se nudum novit et pellicium, id est, mortale sumsit indumentum ». De hoc puritatis indumento dicitur Apocalypsis tertio[^p396-9]: « Vestimentis albis induaris, ut non appareat confusio nuditatis tuae ». His vestimentis induitur qui se per divinae gratiae adiutorium in puritate custodit, secundum illud Apocalypsis tertio: « Habes pauca nomina in Sardis, qui non inquinaverunt vestimenta sua; et ambulabunt mecum in albis, quia digni sunt ». Haec autem puritatis vestis datur per susceptionem Sacramentorum, scilicet *baptismi,* qui omnes sordes abluit — in cuius designatione baptizatus alba veste vestitur — et etiam *poenitentiae,* quae « ad baptismi puritatem conatur reducere », ut dicit Augustinus[^p396-10]. Et quia haec dispensatio Sacramentorum fit per diligentiam ministrorum, ideo dicitur, quod *pater dixit servis,* id est ministris. Nam sic dixit Ioannis vigesimo[^p396-11]: « Accipite Spiritum sanctum; quorum remiseritis peccata » etc.

38. Secundo quantum ad *ornatum honestatis exterioris* subditur: *Et date anulum in manu*[^p396-12] *eius,* per *pulcritudinem pudicitiae; anulus* enim *in manibus* significat desponsationem per castum amorem[^p396-13]. Ex quo intelligimus, quod *anulum in manu*
<!-- page 397 -->
portat qui pudicitiam in operibus suis servat. Haec pudicitia, etsi appareat in integritate carnis, maxime consistit in integritate *fidei,* secundum illud secundae ad Corinthios undecimo[^p397-1]: « Despondi vos uni viro, virginem castam exhibere Christo ». Unde *anulus* est fidei signaculum, quo anima subarrhatur, ut opera eius placeant Christo; quia, ad Hebraeos undecimo, « sine fide impossibile est placere Deo ». Ideo et beata Agnes dicebat[^p397-2]: « Anulo fidei suae subarrhavit me ». Tunc autem anulus est *in manu,* quando fides relucet in operatione; quia, Iacobi secundo[^p397-3], « fides sine operibus mortua est ». Unde Glossa Ambrosii: « *Anulum,* id est signaculum fidei, quo signantur promissa in cordibus fidelium; *date in manu,* id est in operatione, ut per opera fides clarescat, et per fidem opera confirmentur ». In cuius rei designationem, Genesis quadragesimo primo[^p397-4], « tulit anulum Pharao de manu sua et dedit eum in manu Ioseph ». — Et quia non sufficit, quod aliquis sit pudicus in operibus suis, nisi etiam sit *obediens et humilis,* ideo subdit: *Et calceamenta in pedes*[^p397-5], per *promptitudinem* scilicet *obedientiae;* qui enim calceatus est promptus est ad itinerandum; propter quod ad Ephesios sexto: « Calceati pedes in praeparationem Evangelii pacis ». Tunc autem ista obedientia est maxime placita, quando fit ex affectu filiali; Canticorum septimo[^p397-6]: « Quam pulcri sunt gressus tui in calceamentis, filia principis »! Et quia nullus est idoneus ad transeundum « ex hoc mundo ad Patrem[^p397-7] », nisi qui habet promptitudinem obedientiae; ideo in esu paschalis agni haec datur forma Exodi duodecimo: « Renes vestros accingetis et calceamenta habebitis in pedibus, tenentes baculos in manibus, et comedetis festinanter: Est enim phase, id est transitus Domini ».

39. Et haec obedientia maxime requiritur in *praedicatoribus* exemplo Christi et Sanctorum; Isaiae quinquagesimo secundo[^p397-8]: « Quam pulcri super montes pedes annuntiantis et praedicantis pacem, annuntiantis salutem, dicentis Sion: Regnabit Dominus »! Unde Beda[^p397-9]: « *Calceamenta in pedes,* id est exempla priorum munite in officio praedicandi, ut et opus eius bene vivendo ornetur et cursus ad aeterna praeparando ». Unde quia calceamenta fiunt de pellibus animalium mortuorum et per hoc possunt designare *exempla patrum,* sicut hic dicit Beda in Glossa[^p397-10]; ideo dicitur, quod *dentur in pedes.* Quia designare possunt *vetustatem peccati,* ideo dicitur Moysi Exodi tertio[^p397-11]: « Tolle calceamentum de pedibus tuis »; et supra decimo: « Nolite portare neque sacculum neque calceamenta ». Unde supra[^p397-12] loquitur de calceamentis *ad litteram,* hic autem per *similitudinem parabolicam.*

40. (Vers. 23.). *Et adducite vitulum saginatum.* Post misericordiam in suscipiendo indignum et vestiendo nudum subditur hic *misericordia in reficiendo famelicum*[^p397-13]. Et hoc recto ordine, quia post remissionem culparum et vestitionem virtutum sequitur *divinarum oblectatio deliciarum,* quae consurgit ex duplici consideratione, scilicet ex consideratione *Salvatoris nostri* et *hominis salvati.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *considerationem Salvatoris nostri* Christi dicit: *Adducite vitulum saginatum et occidite. Vitulus* iste *saginatus* est Christus pro nobis occisus, qui nobis in Sacramento altaris proponitur in cibum suavissimum. Christus autem dicitur *vitulus,* quia pro peccatis oblatus; Levitici primo[^p397-14]: « Immolabit vitulum coram Domino, et offerent filii Aaron sacerdotes sanguinem eius, fundentes per altaris circuitum, quod est ante ostium
<!-- page 398 -->
tabernaculi ». Dicitur autem *saginatus,* quia omnibus bonis repletus et suavissimus ad spiritualem cibum; unde Glossa[^p398-1]: « Christus vitulus saginatus, omni spirituali virtute opimus, ut pro totius mundi salute sufficiat ». De hoc in figura Genesis decimo octavo de convivio Angelis facto ab Abraham dicitur, quod « cucurrit Abraham ad armentum et attulit inde vitulum tenerrimum et optimum ». Hunc vitulum *adducunt* servi Christi *et occidunt,* quando in altari immolant pro salute peccatorum et nobis exponunt in cibum; unde Glossa[^p398-2]: « Semper Christus credentibus immolatur ». Vel tunc *adducitur et occiditur,* quando mors eius et Sacramentum altaris ad memoriam per praedicatorem revocatur et ad sustentationem et cibum. Unde Beda[^p398-3]: « Praedicate Christum natum et mortem eius insinuate, ut et corde credat occisum imitando et ore percipiat passionis Sacramentum ad emendationem ». Unde etsi omnes Christiani habeant refectionem in passione Christi, illi potissime oblectantur, qui per illam se a maioribus malis et periculis et peccatis ereptos et liberatos vident. — Et ideo subdit: *Et manducemus et epulemur,* non convivio *carnali,* de quo dicunt impii: « Comedamus et bibamus, cras enim moriemur », secundum quod dicit Isaias vigesimo secundo[^p398-4]; sed *spirituali,* de quo primae ad Corinthios quinto: « Pascha nostrum immolatus est Christus. Itaque epulemur non in fermento veteri neque in fermento malitiae et nequitiae, sed in azymis sinceritatis et veritatis »; de quo Canticorum quinto: « Comedite, amici, et inebriamini, carissimi ». — In hoc convivio Christus est *cibus, conviva* et *dispensator,* quia ipse delectatur nobiscum, et nos cum ipso, et hoc faciente ipso; Apocalypsis tertio[^p398-5]: « Ecce, sto ad ostium et pulso. Si quis aperuerit mihi, intrabo et coenabo cum illo, et ipse mecum ».

41. (Vers. 24.). Secundo quantum ad *considerationem hominis salvati* subdit: *Quia hic filius meus mortuus fuerat*[^p398-6], per *culpam,* quae mortuos facit; Psalmus: « Non mortui laudabunt te, Domine »; et iterum Ioannis octavo: « In peccato vestro moriemini ». *Et revixit,* per *poenitentiam;* ad Ephesios
quinto[^p398-7]: « Surge, qui dormis, et exsurge a mortuis, et illuminabit te Christus »; et Psalmus: « Auferes spiritum eorum, et deficient ». « Emitte spiritum tuum » etc. *Perierat,* per *amissionem iustitiae;* ad Romanos secundo[^p398-8]: « Qui sine Lege peccaverunt sine Lege peribunt »; et secundae Petri secundo: « In corruptione sua peribunt, percipientes mercedem iniustitiae suae ». *Et inventus est,* per *recuperationem gratiae,* secundum illud Iob trigesimo tertio[^p398-9]: « Inveni, in quo ei propitier ». Et hoc facit Dominus, cum gratiam suam infundit in animam; Psalmus: « Inveni David servum meum; oleo sancto meo unxi eum ». — Et quia de hoc est omnibus gaudendum, ideo subdit: *Et coeperunt omnes manducare et epulari*[^p398-10]. Beda: « Non solum filius, pro quo vitulus occisus est, reficitur, sed et pater et servi eius, quia refectio Dei et Sanctorum est salus peccatorum ». In cuius rei figuram dicitur Deuteronomii decimo sexto[^p398-11]: « Epulaberis in festivitate tua, tu et filius tuus et filia tua et servus tuus et ancilla tua, Levites quoque, advena et pupillus et vidua, qui intra portas tuas sunt » etc.

## English
<!-- page 394 -->
33. *And when he was yet a great way off.* After the insolence, misery and penance have been described, here, in the fourth place, *the mercy of the pious father* is described[^p394-12][^tr-pietas]. Now this part is divided into two. In the first of which *the mercy of the pious father* is described in *accepting the conversion of the sinner;* second, in *placating the indignation of the obedient one,* at[^p394-13]: *Now his elder son* etc.

Now the Evangelist manifests the *mercy accepting the conversion of the prodigal son* as to a threefold act, namely first, in *receiving the unworthy;* second, *in clothing the naked,* at[^p394-14]: *And the father said to his servants;* third, in *refreshing the famished,* at: *And bring hither the fatted calf.* For this is the order of the reparation of the soul through
<!-- page 395 -->
the gift of the fatherly mercy, that first it *is received* to the kindness of grace; second, it *is clothed* with the garment of justice; third, it *is refreshed* with the sweetness of interior gladness and joy. — Concerning *the mercy receiving the unworthy,* then, two things are introduced, namely *the clemency of the father receiving* and *the reverence*[^p395-1] *of the son accusing himself.*

34. First, then, as to *the clemency of the father receiving* he says: *And when he was yet a great way off,* through fault that removes far from God; the Psalm[^p395-2]: « Far from my salvation are the words of my sins »; and again: « Salvation is far from sinners; because they have not kept thy justifications ». — *His father saw him, and was moved with mercy,* by infusing *prevenient grace,* of which regard below, the twenty-second chapter[^p395-3]: « The Lord looked on Peter »; « and Peter going out, wept bitterly ». And of this Exodus, the third chapter: « I have seen the affliction of my people which is in Egypt, and I have come down to deliver it ». This regard the Prophet was asking for when he said[^p395-4]: « See my abjection and my labour; and forgive me all my sins »; and again: « Look upon me, and have mercy on me » etc. — *And running to him fell upon his neck,* through *concomitant grace;* as if he said: he embraced him, according to that of the Canticles, the second chapter[^p395-5]: « His left hand is under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me »; and this is through *concomitant grace,* which helps toward bearing the yoke of the divine law. Whence Ambrose[^p395-6]: « He fell *upon the neck* of the son, when he laid upon him the light yoke of dilection ». For this *yoke* helps toward bearing the burden. *The embrace* of the divine arm is the humanity of the Word incarnate; whence the Gloss[^p395-7]: « The arm, that is, the Son, he bowed down into the embrace of the one returning »; of which arm in the Psalm: « His right hand hath wrought for him salvation, and his arm is holy ». And through this *embrace* the bonds of sins are loosed, according to that of Isaias, the fifty-second chapter: « Loose the bonds of thy neck, O captive daughter of Sion ». — *And kissed him,* namely through *subsequent grace;* for a kiss is a sign of love and of peace. Whence
Bede[^p395-8]: « Returning, he receives from the father the kiss of charity, while through grace he is certified of pardon », according to that to the Ephesians, the first chapter: « In whom believing you were signed with the holy Spirit of promise, who is the pledge of our inheritance ». This kiss of love and of peace the spouse asks for in the Canticles, the first chapter: « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth, for better are » etc. In this there appears an exceeding[^p395-9] affection of piety, by which God the Father shows to the returning sinner the greatest token of dilection. And through this God the Father knows whether we are his sons, when we gladly kiss him through mutual love; Genesis, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p395-10]: « Come near me, and give me a kiss, my son; and he came near, and kissed him »; and it follows that « he blessed him »; because the fulness of blessing proceeds from this kiss. Whence in the Psalm: « Lord, thou hast blessed thy land »; and it is subjoined: « Mercy and truth have met each other: justice and peace have kissed ». The origin of this kissing is in the Word incarnate, in whom is the union of the highest love and of the connexion of a twofold nature, through which God kisses us, and we kiss God, according to that of the Canticles, the eighth chapter[^p395-11]: « Who shall give thee to me for my brother, that I may find thee without, and kiss thee, and now no man may despise me » etc.

35. (Vers. 21.). Second, as to *the reverence of the son accusing himself* he subjoins: *And the son said to him: Father, I have sinned against heaven,* namely through *contempt of the Majesty* presiding over all things; Job, the eleventh chapter[^p395-12]: « He is higher than heaven, and what wilt thou do? He is deeper than hell, and how wilt thou know »? and the twenty-second: « Knowest thou not that God is higher than heaven » etc. He sins, then, *against heaven* who contemns the God of heaven; whence it is said in the first of Esdras, the ninth chapter[^p395-13]: « My iniquities are multiplied over my head, and our sins are grown up even unto heaven » etc. *And before thee,* through *contempt of the Truth* that beholds all things; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third chapter[^p395-14]: « The eyes of the Lord are far brighter above the sun, beholding round about all the ways of men, and the bottom of the
<!-- page 396 -->
deep, and looking into the hearts of all, into the hidden parts. For to the Lord our God all things were known before they were created; so also after they were perfected he beholdeth all things »; the Psalm[^p396-1]: « To thee only have I sinned, and have done evil before thee ». — *I am not now worthy to be called thy son,* on account of *contempt of the Goodness* that provides for all things; Wisdom, the twelfth chapter[^p396-2]: « But thou, Master of power, judgest with tranquillity, and with great favour disposest of us: for *to be able* is at hand with thee when thou wilt. But thou hast taught thy people that one must be just and humane, and hast made thy children to be of a good hope, because in judging thou givest place for repentance for sins ». Since, then, I have contemned this highest goodness, to which it belongs to give « the spirit of adoption of sons », according to that to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p396-3]: « You have not received the spirit of bondage again in fear, but the spirit of adoption of sons »; to which it belongs also to regenerate by water and the Holy Spirit unto the kingdom of the sons of God, according to that to the Colossians, the first chapter: « He hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the kingdom of the Son of his charity »: *I am not now worthy to be called thy son.*

36. And note that, although the father runs to meet him, clasps his neck in an embrace, receives him to a kiss, yet the true penitent never forgets his sin, according to that of the Psalm[^p396-4]: « For I am ready for scourges: and my sorrow is continually before me. For I will announce my iniquity: and I will think for my sin ». But although he acknowledges it, he none the less pants after *perfect charity.* And therefore he does not here ask for *the dilection of a hired servant,* as he was doing above[^p396-5], when he did not yet have infused grace. But now, fully converted to the father, as one truly penitent he commends and asks for divine grace; and this is what the Gloss says[^p396-6]: « He wills, it says, that what he confesses himself unworthy of by merit be done through grace », as that penitent asked in the second of Paralipomenon, the last chapter: « Now I bend my knees before thee, beseeching thy goodness. I have sinned, O Lord, and I acknowledge my iniquity; I ask, beseeching: O Lord, forgive me, destroy me not together with my iniquities, neither for ever
reserve evils for me, for thou wilt save me who am unworthy according to thy great mercy, and I will praise thee always all the days of my life; for the power of the heavens praiseth thee, and thine is the glory for ever and ever ».

37. (Vers. 22.). *And the father said to his servants.* After the mercy of the father in receiving the unworthy has been shown, there is subjoined here *the mercy in clothing the naked*[^p396-7]. And since the sinner is stripped of *the garment of interior purity* and of *the adornment of exterior honesty;* therefore he shows, as to these two, that the prodigal son is relieved through the father's mercy.

First, then, as to *the garment of interior purity* he says: *And the father said to his servants: Bring forth quickly the first robe, and put it on him. The first robe* is the garment *of interior purity,* with which our mind ought to be clothed and with which it was clothed from the beginning of its condition. Whence Bede[^p396-8]: « The first robe is the garment of innocence, in which the first man was made; but after the fault he knew himself naked and took a garment of skin, that is, a mortal one ». Of this clothing of purity it is said in the Apocalypse, the third chapter[^p396-9]: « Be thou clothed with white garments, that the shame of thy nakedness may not appear ». With these garments he is clothed who keeps himself in purity through the help of divine grace, according to that of the Apocalypse, the third chapter: « Thou hast a few names in Sardis, which have not defiled their garments; and they shall walk with me in white, because they are worthy ». Now this garment of purity is given through the receiving of the Sacraments, namely of *baptism,* which washes away all stains — in signification of which the baptized is clothed with a white garment — and also of *penance,* which « strives to lead back to the purity of baptism », as Augustine says[^p396-10]. And since this dispensing of the Sacraments is done through the diligence of the ministers, therefore it is said that *the father said to the servants,* that is, to the ministers. For so he said, John, the twentieth chapter[^p396-11]: « Receive ye the Holy Spirit; whose sins you shall forgive » etc.

38. Second, as to *the adornment of exterior honesty* it is subjoined: *And put a ring on his hand*[^p396-12], through *the beauty of purity;* for *a ring on the hands* signifies espousal through chaste love[^p396-13]. From which we understand that he bears *a ring on his hand*
<!-- page 397 -->
who keeps purity in his works. This purity, though it appears in integrity of the flesh, consists chiefly in integrity of *faith,* according to that of the second to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter[^p397-1]: « I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Christ ». Whence *the ring* is the seal of faith, by which the soul is betrothed, so that its works may please Christ; because, to the Hebrews, the eleventh chapter, « without faith it is impossible to please God ». Therefore blessed Agnes too said[^p397-2]: « With the ring of his faith he hath betrothed me ». Now the ring is *on the hand* when faith shines forth in operation; because, James, the second chapter[^p397-3], « faith without works is dead ». Whence the Gloss of Ambrose: « *A ring,* that is, the seal of faith, by which the promises are sealed in the hearts of the faithful; *put on his hand,* that is, in operation, that through works faith may grow bright, and through faith works may be confirmed ». In designation of this thing, Genesis, the forty-first chapter[^p397-4], « Pharao took the ring from his own hand, and gave it into the hand of Joseph ». — And since it does not suffice that one be pure in his works unless he be also *obedient and humble,* therefore he subjoins: *And shoes on his feet*[^p397-5], namely through *the promptness of obedience;* for he who is shod is prompt for journeying; on account of which to the Ephesians, the sixth chapter: « Your feet shod unto the preparation of the gospel of peace ». Now this obedience is most pleasing when it is done out of filial affection; the Canticles, the seventh chapter[^p397-6]: « How beautiful are thy steps in shoes, O prince's daughter »! And since no one is fit for passing « out of this world to the Father[^p397-7] » unless he who has the promptness of obedience; therefore in the eating of the paschal lamb this form is given in Exodus, the twelfth chapter: « You shall gird your reins, and you shall have shoes on your feet, holding staves in your hands, and you shall eat in haste: for it is the Phase, that is, the Passage of the Lord ».

39. And this obedience is most of all required in *preachers,* after the example of Christ and of the Saints; Isaias, the fifty-second chapter[^p397-8]: « How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him that announceth and preacheth peace, that announceth salvation, that saith to Sion: The Lord shall reign »! Whence Bede[^p397-9]: « *Shoes on the feet,* that is, furnish the examples of those who went before in the office of preaching, that his work may be adorned by living well and his course to eternal things by preparing ». Whence, since shoes are made of the skins of dead animals and through this can designate *the examples of the fathers,* as Bede says here in the Gloss[^p397-10]; therefore it is said that *they are given on the feet.* Since they can designate *the oldness of sin,* therefore it is said to Moses, Exodus, the third chapter[^p397-11]: « Put off the shoe from thy feet »; and above, the tenth chapter: « Carry neither purse nor shoes ». Whence above[^p397-12] he speaks of shoes *according to the letter,* but here by *parabolic similitude.*

40. (Vers. 23.). *And bring hither the fatted calf.* After the mercy in receiving the unworthy and clothing the naked there is subjoined here *the mercy in refreshing the famished*[^p397-13]. And this in right order, because after the remission of faults and the clothing with virtues there follows *the delighting in divine delights,* which arises from a twofold consideration, namely from the consideration *of our Saviour* and *of the man saved.*

First, then, as to *the consideration of our Saviour* Christ he says: *Bring hither the fatted calf, and kill it. This fatted calf* is Christ slain for us, who is set before us in the Sacrament of the altar as the sweetest food. Now Christ is called *a calf,* because he was offered for sins; Leviticus, the first chapter[^p397-14]: « He shall immolate the calf before the Lord, and the priests the sons of Aaron shall offer the blood thereof, pouring it round about the altar, which is before the door
<!-- page 398 -->
of the tabernacle ». Now he is called *fatted,* because he is filled with all goods and most sweet for spiritual food; whence the Gloss[^p398-1]: « Christ the fatted calf, rich in every spiritual virtue, so that he suffices for the salvation of the whole world ». Of this, in figure, it is said in Genesis, the eighteenth chapter, of the banquet made for the Angels by Abraham, that « Abraham ran to the herd, and took from thence a calf very tender and very good ». This calf the servants of Christ *bring hither and kill,* when on the altar they immolate it for the salvation of sinners and set it out for us as food; whence the Gloss[^p398-2]: « Christ is ever immolated for believers ». Or then he *is brought hither and killed* when his death and the Sacrament of the altar are recalled to memory by the preacher, and for sustenance and food. Whence Bede[^p398-3]: « Preach Christ born and make known his death, that one may both believe with the heart him slain, by imitating, and receive with the mouth the Sacrament of the passion unto amendment ». Whence, although all Christians have refection in the passion of Christ, those take delight most of all who see themselves through it snatched and delivered from greater evils and dangers and sins. — And therefore he subjoins: *And let us eat and make merry,* not with a *carnal* banquet, of which the impious say: « Let us eat and drink, for to morrow we shall die », according to what Isaias says, the twenty-second chapter[^p398-4]; but with a *spiritual* one, of which the first to the Corinthians, the fifth chapter: « Christ our pasch is immolated. Therefore let us feast, not with the old leaven, nor with the leaven of malice and wickedness, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth »; of which the Canticles, the fifth chapter: « Eat, O friends, and be inebriated, my dearly beloved ». — In this banquet Christ is *the food, the guest* and *the dispenser,* because he takes delight with us, and we with him, and this by his own doing; the Apocalypse, the third chapter[^p398-5]: « Behold, I stand at the gate, and knock. If any man shall open to me, I will come in, and will sup with him, and he with me ».

41. (Vers. 24.). Second, as to *the consideration of the man saved* he subjoins: *Because this my son was dead*[^p398-6], through *fault,* which makes men dead; the Psalm: « The dead shall not praise thee, O Lord »; and again John, the eighth chapter: « In your sin you shall die ». *And is come to life again,* through *penance;* to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter[^p398-7]: « Rise, thou that sleepest, and arise from the dead: and Christ shall enlighten thee »; and the Psalm: « Thou shalt take away their spirit, and they shall fail ». « Send forth thy spirit » etc. *He was lost,* through *the loss of justice;* to the Romans, the second chapter[^p398-8]: « Whosoever have sinned without the Law shall perish without the Law »; and the second of Peter, the second chapter: « In their corruption they shall perish, receiving the reward of their injustice ». *And is found,* through *the recovery of grace,* according to that of Job, the thirty-third chapter[^p398-9]: « I have found wherein I may be merciful to him ». And this the Lord does when he infuses his grace into the soul; the Psalm: « I have found David my servant: with my holy oil I have anointed him ». — And since all must rejoice over this, therefore he subjoins: *And they all began to eat and to make merry*[^p398-10]. Bede: « Not only the son, for whom the calf was slain, is refreshed, but both the father and his servants, because the refection of God and of the Saints is the salvation of sinners ». In figure of which thing it is said in Deuteronomy, the sixteenth chapter[^p398-11]: « Thou shalt make merry in thy festival, thou and thy son and thy daughter and thy manservant and thy maidservant, the Levite also, the stranger and the fatherless and the widow, that are within thy gates » etc.
