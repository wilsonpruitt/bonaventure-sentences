# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XI, Vers. 5-13
## *Et ait ad illos: Quis vestrum etc. Post exemplum et documentum subiungit hic tertio orandi incitamentum, per quod invitamur ad orandum frequenter et fiducialiter*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 282 -->
18. *Et ait ad illos: Quis vestrum* etc. Post exemplum et documentum subiungit hic tertio *orandi incitamentum*[^p282-12], per quod invitamur ad orandum frequenter et fiducialiter. Et habet haec pars duas: in prima proponitur incitamentum ad *orationis frequentiam;* in secunda vero, ad *orandi fiduciam,* ibi[^p282-13]: *Quis autem ex vobis patrem petit* etc. Ista enim duo sunt, quae maxime orationis fructum evacuant, scilicet *negligentia* et *diffidentia.*

Circa *incitamentum frequentiae* duo insinuantur. Primum est *similitudo incitativa;* secundum est *eruditio informativa,* ibi[^p282-14]: *Et ego dico vobis: Petite, et dabitur vobis.*

Quantum autem ad *similitudinem incitativam* ad orationis frequentiam introducuntur tria. Primum est *opportunitas postulans;* secundum est *difficultas recusans;* tertium est *importunitas impetrans,* ut ex hoc ostendatur, quantae efficaciae sit oratio importuna et continua.

<!-- page 283 -->
19. (Vers. 5.). Primo igitur quantum ad *opportunitatem postulantem* dicit: *Quis vestrum habebit amicum* etc. Hic legitur *interrogative,* sed Glossa[^p283-1] vult, quod legatur *remissive:* « *Quis,* hoc est *aliquis,* vel *si quis* ». Hoc tamen dicitur, ut ostendatur veri amici pretiositas, secundum illud Ecclesiastici sexto[^p283-2]: « Amico fideli nulla est comparatio, et non est digna ponderatio auri et argenti contra bonitatem fidei illius »; et iterum: « Amicus fidelis, medicamentum vitae et immortalitatis; et qui metuit Dominum inveniet illum ». — Ad istum ergo fiducialiter recurritur, et ideo additur: *Et ibit ad illum media nocte,* quia, sicut dicitur Proverbiorum decimo septimo[^p283-3], « omni tempore diligit qui amicus est, et frater in angustiis comprobatur ». — Et tali fiducialiter necessitas exponitur; propter quod additur: *Et dicet illi: Amice, commoda mihi tres panes;* et hoc lege *pietatis et caritatis communis,* de qua dicitur Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo[^p283-4]: « Frange esurienti panem tuum »; et Ecclesiastae undecimo: « Mitte panem tuum super transeuntes aquas ».

20. (Vers. 6.). Lege etiam *fidelitatis et amicitiae specialis;* propter quod addit: *Quia*[^p283-5] *amicus meus venit ad me de via, et non habeo quod ponam ante eum;* et ita fidelitate amicitiae teneor illi, ac per hoc et tu mihi; unde Ecclesiastici vigesimo secundo: « Fidem posside cum amico in paupertate illius, ut in bonis illius laeteris. In tempore tribulationis illius permane illi fidelis, ut in hereditate illius coheres sis ».

21. (Vers. 7.). Secundo quantum ad *difficultatem recusantem* subdit: *Et ille de intus respondens dicat: Noli mihi molestus esse,* inquietando scilicet quiescentem et a somno excitando, sicut dixit Abner David primi Regum vigesimo sexto[^p283-6]: « Quis es tu, qui clamas et inquietas regem »? — Et quod magna sit molestia, ostenditur ex difficultate adiuncta: *Iam ostium clausum est, et pueri mei mecum sunt in cubili,* id est filii, qui tenero amore debent amari; unde Isaiae octavo[^p283-7]: « Ecce, ego et pueri mei, quos dedit mihi Dominus », id est filii, quorum amor non potest negligi, secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo nono: « Nunquid oblivisci potest mulier infantem suum, ut non misereatur filio uteri sui »? — Ideo subdit: *Non possum surgere et dare tibi.* Et hoc non tollit omnino potestatem, sed ponit difficultatem, quasi dicat illud quod dixit Elias Eliseo quarti Regum secundo[^p283-8]: « Rem difficilem postulasti ». Et propterea dicit: *Non possum,* id est, non commode nec faciliter possum; sicut rex Israel respondit regi Syriae tertii Regum vigesimo: « Omnia, propter quae misisti servum tuum in initio, faciam; hanc autem rem facere non possum ».

22. (Vers. 8.). Tertio quantum ad *importunitatem impetrantem* subdit: *Et si ille perseveraverit pulsans,* precibus, non pudore victus nec taedio fatigatus nec desperatione fractus, tanquam verus amicus, secundum illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo secundo[^p283-9]: « Ad amicum si produxeris gladium, non desperes; est enim regressus ad amicum, et si aperueris os triste, non metuas ». — Et quia[^p283-10] « labor improbus omnia vincit », ideo subdit: *Dico vobis, etsi non dabit illi surgens, eo quod amicus eius sit,* id est, motus veritate dilectionis, quae interdum tepescit in amicis. Propter quod dicitur primae Ioannis tertio[^p283-11]: « Filioli, non diligamus verbo neque lingua, sed opere et veritate ». Si ergo non vincitur abundantia caritatis, vincitur tamen instantia importunitatis. — Propter quod addit: *Propter improbitatem tamen eius surget et dabit illi quotquot habet necessarios.* Huius exemplum proponitur infra decimo octavo[^p283-12] de iudice et vidua, quae illum infestabat; unde ibi dicitur, quod « iudex nolebat eam audire per multum tempus. Post haec autem dixit intra se: Etsi Deum non timeo nec hominem revereor, tamen, quia molesta est mihi, vindicabo illam, ne in novissimo veniens sugillet me ». Huius etiam exemplum habetur Iudicum decimo quarto[^p283-13] de Samsone et eius uxore, a quo cum peteret quod prius nolebat indicare, subditur, quod « septem diebus convivii flebat apud eum, tandemque septimo die, cum ei esset molesta, exposuit ».

23. Secundum *spiritualem* autem *intellectum* per *amicum* istum intelligitur Christus, de quo Ecclesiastici sexto[^p283-14]: « Amicus fidelis, fortis protectio; qui autem invenit illum invenit thesaurum ». De hoc Ioannis decimo quinto: « Iam non dicam vos servos, vos autem dixi amicos »; et iterum: « Vos
<!-- page 284 -->
amici mei estis » etc. — Ad istum amicum *eundum est nocte,* id est *nocturno silentio,* sicut Nicodemus venit, de quo Ioannis tertio[^p284-1] dicitur, quod « venit ad Iesum nocte » primum, quia in secreto noctis silentio est per orationem pulsandus, secundum illud Isaiae vigesimo sexto: « Anima mea desideravit te in nocte »; et Threnorum secundo: « Consurge in nocte, in principio vigiliarum; effunde sicut aquam cor tuum » etc. Vel *in nocte,* id est *in tribulatione,* secundum illud Osee sexto[^p284-2]: « In tribulatione sua mane consurgent ad me »; et in Psalmo: « Invoca me in die tribulationis, et eruam te » etc. — « *Amicus* autem, *qui venit de via,* animus noster est, secundum quod dicitur in Glossa[^p284-3], qui, quoties ad temporalia petenda vagatur, toties a nobis recedit ». Hunc amicum evagari facit delectatio, sed reducit tribulatio, secundum quod dicitur de filio prodigo infra decimo quinto[^p284-4], qui propter luxuriam recessit, sed propter inediam rediit. Unde Osee secundo: « Ego sepiam viam tuam spinis et sepiam eam maceria »; et post subditur: « Et dices: Vadam et revertar ad virum meum priorem, quia melius erat mihi tunc, magis quam nunc ». — Iste *revertitur,* quando ad interiora recurrit, secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo sexto[^p284-5]: « Redite praevaricatores ad cor », sed invenit ipsum vacuum a solatio spiritualium refectionum. Unde de peccatoribus dicitur Threnorum secundo[^p284-6]: « Matribus suis dixerunt: Ubi est triticum et vinum, cum deficerent quasi vulnerati in plateis civitatis; cum exhalarent animas suas in sinu matrum suarum ». « Et defecit gaudium cordis nostri. Vae nobis! quia peccavimus ».

24. Pro hoc igitur amico famelico *petendi sunt a vero amico tres panes,* id est, secundum quod dicit Beda et Augustinus[^p284-7], intelligentia Trinitatis sive trium personarum nomina, ut in solius Dei cognitione inveniat refectionem; unde Exodi vigesimo quarto: « Viderunt Dominum et comederunt et biberunt »; et Ecclesiastici decimo quinto: « Cibabit illum pane vitae et intellectus et aqua sapientiae salutaris potabit illum ». — Vel *tres panes* sunt fides, spes, caritas, quibus reficitur triplex virtus in anima; de quibus infra decimo quinto[^p284-8]: « Quanti mercenarii in domo patris mei abundant panibus » etc. De his primi Regum decimo: « Cum veneris ad quercum Thabor, invenient te tres viri ascendentes ad Dominum in Bethel: unus portans tres haedos, alius tres tortas panis, et alius portans lagenam vini »; ut in his intelligatur unitas gratiae et trinitas virtutum, per quas reformatur imago Dei in anima. — Sed haec petitio *non statim exauditur* a Christo, sed oportet cum multa instantia requirere; quia ad tam magna dona est *anima peccatrix* minus idonea, unde Matthaei decimo quinto[^p284-9]: « Non est bonum sumere panem filiorum et dare canibus »; verum etiam, si iam *filius* est, ne panis ille vilescat, cum semper ipsum oporteat esurire, secundum illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto: « Qui edunt me adhuc esurient »; sive ut petendo magis mereamur et disponamur. Unde Augustinus ad Probam de Orando Deo[^p284-10]: « Exerceri vult in orationibus desiderium nostrum, quo possumus capere illud quod praeparat dare ».

25. (Vers. 9.). *Et ego dico vobis.* Post similitudinem incitativam subdit *eruditionem informativam* ad orationis frequentiam, quam elicit ex similitudine praehabita; ubi primo hortatur[^p284-11] *discipulos,* secundo vero *generaliter omnes.*

Ideo quantum ad *exhortationem respectu discipulorum* dicit: *Et ego vobis dico,* qui scilicet non mentior, quia, Numerorum vigesimo tertio[^p284-12], « non est Deus ut homo, ut mentiatur, nec ut filius hominis, ut mutetur; dixit ergo, et non faciet »? Unde Glossa dicit: « Magnam spem tribuit qui promittendo non decipit ». — Monet ergo ad orationis instantiam et frequentiam, cum dicit: *Petite, et dabitur vobis; quaerite, et invenietis; pulsate, et aperietur vobis.*
<!-- page 285 -->
Simile habetur Matthaei septimo[^p285-1]; super quo loco dicit Augustinus: « Operose quidem, quid ista tria inter se differant, distinguendum putavi, sed longe melius ad instantissimam petitionem omnia referuntur. Hoc quippe ostendit, ubi eodem verbo cuncta conclusit: *Quanto magis Pater vester dabit spiritum bonum petentibus se* »? Unde vult Dominus dicere illud quod simpliciter dicitur primae ad Thessalonicenses quinto[^p285-2]: « Sine intermissione orate »; et ad Colossenses quarto: « Orationi instate ».

26. Possunt tamen multipliciter distingui, ut *petere* respiciat actum oris; *quaerere,* actum cordis; *pulsare,* operis. *Petite* ergo ore, *et dabitur vobis;* Isaiae sexagesimo secundo[^p285-3]: « Qui reminiscimini Domini, ne taceatis, donec stabiliat et ponat Ierusalem laudem in terra »; et Isaiae trigesimo: « Ad vocem clamoris tui statim, ut audierit, respondebit tibi, et dabit vobis Dominus panem » etc.; et sexagesimo quinto: « Eritque, antequam clament, ego exaudiam, adhuc illis loquentibus ». — *Quaerite* etiam corde, *et invenietis;* unde Ieremiae vigesimo nono[^p285-4]: « Quaeretis me, et invenietis, cum quaesieritis me in toto corde vestro »; et Sapientiae primo: « In simplicitate cordis quaerite illum; quoniam invenitur ab his qui non tentant illum ». — *Pulsate,* in opere; qui enim pulsat manu tangit et excitat; unde in Psalmo[^p285-5]: « In noctibus extollite manus vestras in sancta »; et iterum: « In die tribulationis meae Deum exquisivi manibus meis et non sum deceptus ».

27. Aliter etiam possunt distingui ratione *petiti. Petite,* scilicet veniam; *quaerite,* gratiam; *pulsate,* ad *gloriam,* secundum illud Zachariae decimo[^p285-6]: « Petite pluviam a Domino in tempore serotino, et Dominus faciet nives et pluviam imbris et dabit eis singulis herbam in agro ». *Nives dabit* ad exstinguendas malas herbas per veniam; et *pluviam imbris,* ad fecundandam terram per gratiam; et *dabit eis singulis herbam in agro,* decorando et vestiendo per gloriam. — Aliter ex parte *petentium. Petite,* incipientes, quorum est accipere; *quaerite,* proficientes, quorum est invenire; *pulsate,* perfecti, quorum est intrare. Unde *incipientibus* dicitur ad Philippenses quarto[^p285-7]: « Petitiones vestrae innotescant apud Deum ». *Proficientibus* dicitur illud Psalmi: « Quaerite Dominum, et confirmamini; quaerite faciem eius semper ». *Perfectis* dicitur Isaiae vigesimo sexto: « Aperite portas, et ingredietur gens iusta ». — Vel penes *modos perveniendi ad sapientiam,* sicut distinguit Augustinus[^p285-8]: « Ad sapientiam non venitur nisi, quemadmodum Dominus docet, petendo, quaerendo, pulsando, id est orando, legendo, plangendo ». *Petite* ergo orando; Iacobi primo[^p285-9]: « Si quis vestrum indiget sapientia, postulet a Deo, et dabitur ei ». *Quaerite* legendo in libro Scripturae et creaturae; Canticorum tertio: « Per vicos et plateas quaeram quem diligit anima mea ». *Pulsate* plangendo, sicut Ioannes Apocalypsis quinto, ubi dicit: « Ego flebam multum, quoniam nemo inventus est dignus aperire librum »; et subditur postea, quod vidit, librum aperiri per Agnum occisum. — Vel, sicut dicit Glossa[^p285-10], ex parte *modorum veniendi ad gloriam:* « *Petite,* orando; *quaerite,* recte vivendo; *pulsate,* perseverando ». — Vel, sicut dicit Glossa super Matthaei septimum[^p285-11], « *petimus* fide, per eam ad Christum euntes; *quaerimus* spe, qua attingimus usque ad interiora; *pulsamus* caritate, dum laboribus insudamus, ut quod petimus et quaerimus consequamur. Primum petere debes, ut *habeas;* post quaerere, ut *invenias;* inventa observare, ut *introeas* ».

28. (Vers. 10.). Secundo quantum ad *exhortationem respectu omnium* subdit: *Omnis enim, qui petit, accipit,* si *pie* petat. Unde hoc insinuat Dominus Ioannis decimo quinto[^p285-12]: « Si quid petieritis Patrem in nomine meo, dabit vobis », id est pro salute vestra; alioquin non dat; unde Iacobi quarto: « Petitis, et non accipitis, eo quod male petatis, ut in concupiscentiis vestris insumatis ». Unde Chrysostomus[^p285-13]: « Si temporalia petas, quomodo ille praestat, quae si habes, praecepit, ut contemnas »? —
<!-- page 286 -->
*Et qui quaerit invenit,* si tamen *debite et diligenter* quaerat, secundum illud Deuteronomii quarto[^p286-1]: « Cum quaesieris Dominum Deum tuum, invenies eum; si tamen toto corde quaesieris eum et tota tribulatione animae tuae »; et primi Paralipomenon vigesimo octavo: « Si quaesieris Deum, invenies; si autem dereliqueris, proiiciet te in aeternum ». Sed aliqui aliquando non debite quaerunt, et ideo non inveniunt; unde Osee quinto[^p286-2]: « In gregibus suis et in armentis vadent ad quaerendum Dominum et non invenient »; et hoc est, quia *in armentis* quaerebant ipsum; Ioannis septimo: « Quaeretis me, et non invenietis », quia mala intentione quaerebant, ut perderent. — *Et pulsanti aperietur,* si pulset *incessanter usque in finem,* secundum illud Matthaei decimo[^p286-3]: « Qui perseveraverit usque in finem, hic salvus erit ». Tali aperitur ostium gloriae, de qua Apocalypsis undecimo: « Apertum est templum Dei in caelo »; et Apocalypsis quarto: « Ecce, ostium apertum est in caelo ». Hoc aperietur, quando dicetur illud Matthaei vigesimo quinto: « Venite, benedicti, possidete » etc.

29. *Quis autem*[^p286-4] *ex vobis* etc. Post incitamentum ad orationis frequentiam subdit hic *incitamentum ad orationis fiduciam,* et hoc ostendendo ostendit *liberalitatem* in Deo Patre nos exaudiente. In hac ergo parte primo proponit liberalitatem *patris carnalis* respectu filii; secundo concludit liberalitatem *Patris caelestis* respectu nostri, ibi[^p286-5]: *Si ergo vos, cum sitis mali* etc.

*Liberalitatem* autem *patris carnalis* ostendit in communicatione cibi cum cautela contrarii, et hoc secundum triplicem differentiam nutrimenti: aut naturae *terreae,* aut *aqueae,* aut *aëreae.* Primum spectat ad vegetabilia; secundum, ad natatilia; tertium, ad volatilia.

30. (Vers. 11. et 12.). Quantum igitur ad *petitionem fructus vegetabilium* dicit: *Quis ex vobis patrem petit panem,* ad refectionem; *nunquid lapidem dabit illi?* scilicet ad laesionem. Homines enim panibus sustentantur, secundum illud Psalmi[^p286-6]: « Panis cor hominis confirmat »; sed lapidibus obruuntur, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo primo: « Qui ceciderit super lapidem istum confringetur, super quem autem ceciderit, conteret eum ». — Quantum ad *petitionem natatilium* subdit: *Aut piscem, nunquid pro pisce serpentem dabit illi?* Homines piscibus reficiuntur; unde infra ultimo[^p286-7]: « Habetis hic aliquid, quod manducetur? At illi obtulerunt ei partem piscis assi et favum mellis »; sed serpentibus occiduntur; unde Numerorum vigesimo primo: « Immisit Dominus in populum ignitos serpentes », quorum scilicet morsibus moriebantur. — Quantum ad *petitionem fructus volatilium* subditur: *Aut si petierit ovum,* scilicet in cibum; *nunquid porriget illi scorpionem?* in[^p286-8] venenum. Ova enim sustentant, sed scorpiones necant, sicut manifeste probat experientia. Haec quidem ad litteram sunt plana et similitudo ex his assumta.

31. Secundum *spiritualem* autem *intelligentiam* datur intelligi hic, *quid* petendum. Unde Augustinus ad Probam de Orando Deo[^p286-9] dicit, quod in *pisce* fides, in *ovo* spes, in *pane* autem caritas designatur. — Recte namque petitione *panis* intelligitur petitio *caritatis;* unde Augustinus[^p286-10]: « Caritas in *pane;* maior enim omnium bonorum est caritas, et in cibis vincit cetera panis utilitas ». Vel, quia caritas ad modum panis nutrit et confirmat; unde Canticorum ultimo[^p286-11]: « Fortis est ut mors dilectio »; unde Sacramentum caritatis traditur in specie panis. Vel, quia sine pane est omnis mensa vacua; sic et omne cor absque caritate; unde primae ad Corinthios decimo tertio[^p286-12]: « Si caritatem non habeam, nihil sum, et nihil mihi prodest ». « Huic, ut dicit Augustinus[^p286-13], opponitur *lapis,* quia corda dura respuunt caritatem ». Unde in lapide *duritia* designatur, secundum illud Ezechielis trigesimo sexto: « Auferam cor lapideum de carne vestra et dabo vobis » etc.; et Lucae tertio: « Potens est Deus de lapidibus istis » etc.

<!-- page 287 -->
32. Recte etiam « per *piscem* intelligitur *fides,* sicut dicit Augustinus[^p287-1], vel propter aquam baptismi, quia in huius saeculi fluctibus fides integra est »; primae Ioannis quinto: « Haec est victoria, quae vincit mundum, fides nostra »; et ad Hebraeos undecimo: « Per fidem vicerunt regna ». Vel, ut dicit Beda[^p287-2], « quia piscis sub tegumento aquarum nascitur, vivit et alitur; sic fides trahitur de occultis Scripturis et de occultis est et occulta »; unde ad Hebraeos undecimo: « Fides est substantia sperandarum rerum, argumentum non apparentium ». — « Huic, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p287-3], contrarius est *serpens,* qui, ut non crederetur Deo, venenosa fraude persuasit »; unde Ecclesiastae decimo: « Qui dissipat sepem », id est defensionem sacrae Scripturae, « mordebit eum coluber », id est infidelitas erroris diabolici.

33. Recte etiam per *ovum* intelligitur *spes;* unde Augustinus[^p287-4]: « *Spes* in *ovo,* quia vita pulli nondum est, sed futura est, nec iam videtur, sed adhuc speratur. *Spes enim, quae videtur, non est spes* », sicut dicitur ad Romanos octavo; et quia in ovo pullus est imperfecte, qui dum perficitur, testa ovi frangitur; sic spei succedit beatitudinis perfectio; unde primae ad Corinthios decimo tertio[^p287-5]: « Cum venerit quod perfectum est, evacuabitur quod ex parte est ». — « Huic, ut dicit Augustinus[^p287-6], contrarius est *scorpio* », id est *desperatio,* quae facit retrorsum respicere et ex illa parte nocet. « Scorpio enim ex ea parte cavendus est, quam venenatam et aculeatam retrorsum habet »; unde qui nititur a spe vitae subvertere scorpius est; unde Ezechielis secundo[^p287-7]: « Subversores sunt tecum, et cum scorpionibus habitas ». — Est igitur *sensus* verbi propositi *spiritualis,* quod si quis petat panem *caritatis* Spiritus sancti, piscem *fidei* et ovum *spei,* non dabit ei Deus, immo removebit ab eo, lapidem *obdurationis,* serpentem *infidelitatis* et scorpium *desperationis;* quae per divinum adiutorium elongantur a viris sanctis.

34. (Vers. 13.). *Si ergo vos, cum sitis mali* etc. Postquam proposuit liberalitatem in patre carnali, concludit *a minori* in *Patre spirituali.* Si enim bonum est communicativum[^p287-8], magis bonum magis communicativum, et maxime bonum maxime communicativum. Si ergo carnalis pater bonum communicat filio petenti, multo fortius caelestis homini supplicanti. Et hoc est quod dicit: *Si ergo vos, cum sitis mali, nostis bona data dare filiis vestris.* Beda[^p287-9]: « *Malos* vocat *saeculi amatores* »; hi autem dupliciter mali sunt malitia culpae; quia Ecclesiastici undecimo[^p287-10]: « Si dives fueris, non eris immunis a delicto »; et Augustinus: « Omnis dives aut est iniquus, aut heres iniqui ». — Vel, *malos* vocat ipsos *Apostolos,* qui comparatione divinae bonitatis *mali* dicuntur; unde Marci decimo[^p287-11]: « Nemo bonus nisi solus Deus »; et Isaiae sexagesimo quarto: « Facti sumus ut immundus omnes nos, iustitiae nostrae sicut pannus menstruatae »; et Iob vigesimo quinto: « Stellae non sunt mundae in conspectu eius, quanto magis homo » etc. Unde Gregorius[^p287-12]: « Saepe iustitia nostra, ad examen divinae iustitiae deducta, iniustitia est, et sordet in districtione iudicis quod in oculis fulget operantis ». — Vel, *mali* erant propter *peccata venialia,* sine quibus non ducitur praesens vita; unde dicitur ad Ephesios quinto[^p287-13]: « Redimentes tempus, quoniam dies mali sunt »; quia, secundum quod
<!-- page 288 -->
dicitur Genesis quadragesimo septimo, « dies peregrinationis vitae meae pauci et mali sunt »; quia, secundum quod dicitur primae Ioannis primo, « si dixerimus, quia peccatum non habemus, mendaces sumus, et veritas in nobis non est ». Minus ergo videtur, quod a malo bonum, quam a bono bonum.

35. Et ideo concludit: *Quanto magis Pater vester de caelo dabit spiritum bonum petentibus se?* Immo certe dabit, quia Iacobi primo[^p288-1]: « Omne datum optimum et omne donum perfectum desursum est ». Iste *spiritus bonus* est Spiritus sanctus, in quo omnia dona donantur; de quo Sapientiae septimo[^p288-2]: « Invocavi, et venit in me spiritus sapientiae »; et post: « Venerunt mihi omnia bona pariter cum illa »; et primae ad Corinthios duodecimo: « Alii datur per Spiritum » etc.; et post: « Haec omnia operatur unus atque idem Spiritus, dividens singulis, prout vult ». Iste Spiritus datur *petentibus* et desiderantibus, secundum illud Psalmi[^p288-3]: « Os meum aperui et attraxi spiritum »; Ieremiae secundo: « Onager assuetus in solitudine in desiderio animae suae attraxit ventum amoris sui ». Ad hunc enim Spiritum Domini suspirat Sanctorum spiritus; ad Romanos octavo: « Spiritus est qui postulat » etc.

## English
<!-- page 282 -->
18. *And he said to them: Which of you* etc. After the example and the instruction he subjoins here, thirdly, *the incitement of praying*[^p282-12], by which we are invited to pray frequently and confidently. And this part has two: in the first an incitement is put forward to *frequency of prayer;* in the second, to *confidence in praying,* at[^p282-13]: *And which of you, if he ask his father bread* etc. For these are the two things that most empty out the fruit of prayer, namely *negligence* and *diffidence.*

Concerning *the incitement of frequency* two things are suggested. The first is *the inciting likeness;* the second is *the informing erudition,* at[^p282-14]: *And I say to you: Ask, and it shall be given you.*

Now as to *the inciting likeness* toward frequency of prayer three things are introduced. The first is *the opportunity asking;* the second is *the difficulty refusing;* the third is *the importunity obtaining,* that from this it may be shown of how great efficacy is prayer importunate and continual.

<!-- page 283 -->
19. (Vers. 5.). First, then, as to *the opportunity asking* he says: *Which of you shall have a friend* etc. Here it is read *interrogatively,* but the Gloss[^p283-1] would have it read *remissively:* « *Which,* that is, *anyone,* or *if any* ». Yet this is said that the preciousness of a true friend may be shown, according to that of Ecclesiasticus six[^p283-2]: « Nothing can be compared to a faithful friend, and no weight of gold and silver is worthy to be set against the goodness of his fidelity »; and again: « A faithful friend is the medicine of life and immortality; and he that feareth the Lord shall find him ». — To such a one, then, recourse is had confidently, and therefore it is added: *And shall go to him at midnight,* because, as is said in Proverbs seventeen[^p283-3], « at all times he loveth who is a friend, and a brother is proved in distress ». — And to such a one the need is laid open confidently; for which reason it is added: *And shall say to him: Friend, lend me three loaves;* and this by the law of common *piety and charity,* of which it is said in Isaias fifty-eight[^p283-4]: « Deal thy bread to the hungry »; and Ecclesiastes eleven: « Cast thy bread upon the running waters ».

20. (Vers. 6.). By the law also *of fidelity and of special friendship;* for which reason he adds: *Because*[^p283-5] *a friend of mine is come off his journey to me, and I have not what to set before him;* and so by the fidelity of friendship I am bound to him, and through this thou also to me; whence Ecclesiasticus twenty-two: « Keep fidelity with a friend in his poverty, that in his good things thou mayst rejoice. In the time of his tribulation continue faithful to him, that thou mayst be a coheir in his inheritance ».

21. (Vers. 7.). Secondly, as to *the difficulty refusing* he subjoins: *And he from within should answer, and say: Trouble me not,* namely by disquieting one at rest and rousing him from sleep, as Abner said to David in the first of Kings twenty-six[^p283-6]: « Who art thou, that criest, and disquietest the king »? — And that the trouble is great is shown from the difficulty joined to it: *The door is now shut, and my children are with me in bed,* that is, sons, who ought to be loved with a tender love; whence Isaias eight[^p283-7]: « Behold I and my children, whom the Lord hath given me », that is, sons, whose love cannot be neglected, according to that of Isaias forty-nine: « Can a woman forget her infant, so as not to have pity on the son of her womb »? — Therefore he subjoins: *I cannot rise and give thee.* And this does not take away the power altogether, but puts a difficulty, as if to say what Elias said to Eliseus in the fourth of Kings two[^p283-8]: « Thou hast asked a difficult thing ». And for this reason he says: *I cannot,* that is, I cannot conveniently nor easily; as the king of Israel answered the king of Syria in the third of Kings twenty: « All things for which thou didst send to thy servant at the beginning, I will do: but this thing I cannot do ».

22. (Vers. 8.). Thirdly, as to *the importunity obtaining* he subjoins: *Yet if he shall continue knocking,* with prayers, not overcome by shame, nor wearied with tedium, nor broken by despair, as a true friend, according to that of Ecclesiasticus twenty-two[^p283-9]: « If thou hast drawn a sword at a friend, despair not; for there is a returning to a friend; and if thou hast opened a sad mouth, fear not ». — And because[^p283-10] « importunate labour conquers all things », therefore he subjoins: *I say to you, although he will not rise and give him, because he is his friend,* that is, moved by the truth of dilection, which at times grows lukewarm in friends. For which reason it is said in the first of John three[^p283-11]: « My little children, let us not love in word, nor in tongue, but in deed, and in truth ». If, then, he is not overcome by abundance of charity, he is overcome nevertheless by the instance of importunity. — For which reason he adds: *Yet, because of his importunity, he will rise, and give him as many as he needeth.* An example of this is set forth below in eighteen[^p283-12] of the judge and the widow, who harassed him; whence it is said there that « the judge would not hear her for a long time. But afterwards he said within himself: Although I fear not God, nor regard man, yet because she is troublesome to me, I will avenge her, lest coming at the last she weary me ». An example of this is also had in Judges fourteen[^p283-13] of Samson and his wife, who, when she asked of him what he before would not disclose, it is subjoined that « she wept before him the seven days of the feast, and at length on the seventh day, as she was troublesome to him, he told her ».

23. Now according to the *spiritual understanding* by this *friend* is understood Christ, of whom Ecclesiasticus six[^p283-14]: « A faithful friend is a strong defence; and he that hath found him hath found a treasure ». Of him John fifteen: « I will not now call you servants, but I have called you friends »; and again: « You are
<!-- page 284 -->
my friends » etc. — To this friend *one must go by night,* that is, *in the silence of night,* as Nicodemus came, of whom it is said in John three[^p284-1] that « he came to Jesus by night »: first, because he is to be knocked at through prayer in the secret silence of the night, according to that of Isaias twenty-six: « My soul hath desired thee in the night »; and Lamentations two: « Arise in the night, in the beginning of the watches; pour out thy heart like water » etc. Or *in the night,* that is, *in tribulation,* according to that of Osee six[^p284-2]: « In their tribulation they will rise early to me »; and in the Psalm: « Call upon me in the day of tribulation, and I will deliver thee » etc. — « Now *the friend who is come off his journey* is our mind, according to what is said in the Gloss[^p284-3], which, as often as it wanders to ask for temporal things, so often departs from us ». Delight makes this friend wander abroad, but tribulation brings him back, according to what is said of the prodigal son below in fifteen[^p284-4], who departed on account of riotousness, but returned on account of hunger. Whence Osee two: « I will hedge up thy way with thorns, and I will hedge it with a wall »; and afterwards it is subjoined: « And she shall say: I will go, and return to my first husband, because it was better with me then, more than now ». — He *returns* when he runs back to the things within, according to that of Isaias forty-six[^p284-5]: « Return, ye transgressors, to the heart », but he finds it empty of the solace of spiritual refections. Whence it is said of sinners in Lamentations two[^p284-6]: « They said to their mothers: Where is corn and wine? when they fainted away as the wounded in the streets of the city; when they breathed out their souls in the bosoms of their mothers ». « And the joy of our heart hath failed. Woe to us! because we have sinned ».

24. For this hungry friend, then, *three loaves are to be asked of the true friend,* that is, according to what Bede and Augustine say[^p284-7], the intelligence of the Trinity or the names of the three persons, that it may find refection in the knowledge of God alone; whence Exodus twenty-four: « They saw the Lord, and did eat and drink »; and Ecclesiasticus fifteen: « She shall feed him with the bread of life and understanding, and shall give him the water of wholesome wisdom to drink ». — Or *the three loaves* are faith, hope, charity, by which the threefold power in the soul is refreshed; of which below in fifteen[^p284-8]: « How many hired servants in my father's house abound with bread » etc. Of these the first of Kings ten: « When thou shalt come to the oak of Thabor, there shall find thee three men going up to the Lord in Bethel: one carrying three kids, another three loaves of bread, and another carrying a bottle of wine »; so that in these may be understood the unity of grace and the trinity of the virtues, through which the image of God is reformed in the soul. — But this petition *is not heard at once* by Christ, but one must seek it again with much instance; because for gifts so great *the sinful soul* is less fit, whence Matthew fifteen[^p284-9]: « It is not good to take the bread of the children, and to give it to the dogs »; yes, and even if he is already *a son,* lest that bread grow cheap, since he must always hunger for it, according to that of Ecclesiasticus twenty-four: « They that eat me shall yet hunger »; or that by asking we may merit more and be disposed. Whence Augustine to Proba, on Praying to God[^p284-10]: « He wills that our desire be exercised in prayers, by which we may be able to take that which he prepares to give ».

25. (Vers. 9.). *And I say to you.* After the inciting likeness he subjoins *the informing erudition* toward frequency of prayer, which he draws out from the likeness had before; where first he exhorts[^p284-11] *the disciples,* and secondly *all in general.*

Therefore as to *the exhortation in respect of the disciples* he says: *And I say to you,* I namely who do not lie, because, in Numbers twenty-three[^p284-12], « God is not as a man, that he should lie, nor as the son of man, that he should be changed; hath he said then, and will he not do »? Whence the Gloss says: « He gives great hope who in promising does not deceive ». — He admonishes, then, to instance and frequency of prayer, when he says: *Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and you shall find; knock, and it shall be opened to you.*
<!-- page 285 -->
The like is had in Matthew seven[^p285-1]; upon which place Augustine says: « Laboriously indeed I thought it to be distinguished how these three differ among themselves, but far better are they all referred to the most instant petition. For this he shows, where he concluded all with the same word: *How much more will your Father give the good spirit to them that ask him* »? Whence the Lord would say what is said simply in the first to the Thessalonians five[^p285-2]: « Pray without ceasing »; and to the Colossians four: « Be instant in prayer ».

26. They can, however, be distinguished in many ways, so that *to ask* regards the act of the mouth; *to seek,* the act of the heart; *to knock,* that of work. *Ask,* then, with the mouth, *and it shall be given you;* Isaias sixty-two[^p285-3]: « You that are mindful of the Lord, hold not your peace, till he establish and make Jerusalem a praise in the earth »; and Isaias thirty: « At the voice of thy cry, as soon as he shall hear, he will answer thee, and the Lord will give you bread » etc.; and sixty-five: « And it shall come to pass that before they call, I will hear, as they are yet speaking ». — *Seek* also with the heart, *and you shall find;* whence Jeremias twenty-nine[^p285-4]: « You shall seek me, and shall find me, when you shall seek me with all your heart »; and Wisdom one: « Seek him in simplicity of heart; for he is found by them that tempt him not ». — *Knock,* in work; for he who knocks touches with the hand and rouses; whence in the Psalm[^p285-5]: « In the nights lift up your hands to the holy places »; and again: « In the day of my trouble I sought God with my hands, and I was not deceived ».

27. They can be distinguished otherwise also by reason of *the thing asked. Ask,* namely pardon; *seek,* grace; *knock,* for *glory,* according to that of Zacharias ten[^p285-6]: « Ask ye of the Lord rain in the latter season, and the Lord will make snows and showers of rain, and will give them, to every one, grass in the field ». *He will give snows* to extinguish the evil herbs through pardon; and *showers of rain,* to make the earth fruitful through grace; and *will give them, to every one, grass in the field,* by adorning and clothing through glory. — Otherwise, on the part of *those who ask. Ask,* you beginners, whose part it is to receive; *seek,* you who make progress, whose part it is to find; *knock,* you perfect, whose part it is to enter. Whence to *beginners* is said in Philippians four[^p285-7]: « Let your petitions be made known to God ». To *those making progress* is said that of the Psalm: « Seek ye the Lord, and be strengthened; seek his face evermore ». To *the perfect* is said in Isaias twenty-six: « Open ye the gates, and the just nation shall enter in ». — Or according to *the ways of arriving at wisdom,* as Augustine distinguishes[^p285-8]: « One does not come to wisdom except, as the Lord teaches, by asking, seeking, knocking, that is, by praying, reading, lamenting ». *Ask,* then, by praying; James one[^p285-9]: « If any of you want wisdom, let him ask of God, and it shall be given him ». *Seek* by reading in the book of Scripture and of the creature; Canticles three: « Through the streets and the broad ways I will seek him whom my soul loveth ». *Knock* by lamenting, as John in the Apocalypse five, where he says: « And I wept much, because no man was found worthy to open the book »; and it is subjoined afterwards that he saw the book opened by the Lamb that was slain. — Or, as the Gloss says[^p285-10], on the part of *the ways of coming to glory:* « *Ask,* by praying; *seek,* by living rightly; *knock,* by persevering ». — Or, as the Gloss on Matthew seven says[^p285-11], « *we ask* by faith, going to Christ through it; *we seek* by hope, by which we reach as far as the things within; *we knock* by charity, while we sweat in labours, that we may obtain what we ask and seek. First thou must ask, that thou mayst *have;* afterwards seek, that thou mayst *find;* keep the things found, that thou mayst *enter in* ».

28. (Vers. 10.). Secondly, as to *the exhortation in respect of all* he subjoins: *For every one that asketh, receiveth,* if he ask *piously.* Whence the Lord suggests this in John fifteen[^p285-12]: « If you shall ask the Father any thing in my name, he will give it you », that is, for your salvation; otherwise he does not give; whence James four: « You ask, and receive not, because you ask amiss, that you may consume it on your concupiscences ». Whence Chrysostom[^p285-13]: « If thou ask temporal things, how does he grant what he has enjoined thee to despise if thou have them »? —
<!-- page 286 -->
*And he that seeketh, findeth,* if yet he seek *duly and diligently,* according to that of Deuteronomy four[^p286-1]: « When thou shalt seek the Lord thy God, thou shalt find him; if yet thou seek him with all thy heart, and all the affliction of thy soul »; and the first of Paralipomenon twenty-eight: « If thou seek God, thou shalt find; but if thou forsake, he will cast thee off for ever ». But some at times do not seek duly, and therefore do not find; whence Osee five[^p286-2]: « With their flocks and with their herds they shall go to seek the Lord, and shall not find »; and this is because they were seeking him *in herds;* John seven: « You shall seek me, and shall not find », because they were seeking with an evil intention, that they might destroy. — *And to him that knocketh, it shall be opened,* if he knock *unceasingly unto the end,* according to that of Matthew ten[^p286-3]: « He that shall persevere unto the end, he shall be saved ». To such a one the door of glory is opened, of which Apocalypse eleven: « The temple of God was opened in heaven »; and Apocalypse four: « Behold, a door was opened in heaven ». This will be opened when that of Matthew twenty-five shall be said: « Come, ye blessed, possess » etc.

29. *And which*[^p286-4] *of you* etc. After the incitement to frequency of prayer he subjoins here *the incitement to confidence of prayer,* and this by showing he shows *the liberality* in God the Father who hears us. In this part, then, he first puts forward the liberality *of a carnal father* in respect of his son; secondly he concludes the liberality *of the heavenly Father* in respect of us, at[^p286-5]: *If you then, being evil* etc.

Now *the liberality of the carnal father* he shows in the sharing of food with caution against its contrary, and this according to the threefold difference of nourishment: of nature either *earthy,* or *watery,* or *airy.* The first regards growing things; the second, swimming things; the third, flying things.

30. (Vers. 11. et 12.). As to *the asking for the fruit of growing things,* then, he says: *Which of you, if he ask his father bread,* for refection; *will he give him a stone?* namely for hurt. For men are sustained by bread, according to that of the Psalm[^p286-6]: « Bread strengtheneth man's heart »; but they are overwhelmed by stones, according to that of Matthew twenty-one: « Whosoever shall fall upon this stone shall be broken, but on whomsoever it shall fall, it shall grind him ». — As to *the asking for swimming things* he subjoins: *Or a fish, will he for a fish give him a serpent?* Men are refreshed by fishes; whence below in the last[^p286-7]: « Have you here any thing to eat? And they offered him a piece of a broiled fish, and a honeycomb »; but they are killed by serpents; whence Numbers twenty-one: « The Lord sent among the people fiery serpents », by whose bites, namely, they were dying. — As to *the asking for the fruit of flying things* it is subjoined: *Or if he shall ask an egg,* namely for food; *will he reach him a scorpion?* for[^p286-8] poison. For eggs sustain, but scorpions kill, as experience manifestly proves. These things indeed are plain according to the letter, and the likeness is taken from them.

31. Now according to the *spiritual intelligence* it is given to be understood here *what* is to be asked. Whence Augustine to Proba, on Praying to God[^p286-9], says that in the *fish* faith, in the *egg* hope, but in the *bread* charity is designated. — For rightly by the asking for *bread* is understood the asking for *charity;* whence Augustine[^p286-10]: « Charity in the *bread;* for charity is the greater of all goods, and among foods the usefulness of bread surpasses the rest ». Or, because charity after the manner of bread nourishes and strengthens; whence Canticles the last[^p286-11]: « Dilection is strong as death »; whence the Sacrament of charity is delivered under the species of bread. Or, because without bread every table is empty; so also every heart without charity; whence the first to the Corinthians thirteen[^p286-12]: « If I have not charity, I am nothing, and it profiteth me nothing ». « To this, as Augustine says[^p286-13], the *stone* is opposed, because hard hearts spit out charity ». Whence in the stone *hardness* is designated, according to that of Ezechiel thirty-six: « I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and will give you » etc.; and Luke three: « God is able of these stones » etc.

<!-- page 287 -->
32. Rightly also « by the *fish* is understood *faith,* as Augustine says[^p287-1], either on account of the water of baptism, or because amid the waves of this world faith is whole »; the first of John five: « This is the victory which overcometh the world, our faith »; and to the Hebrews eleven: « By faith they conquered kingdoms ». Or, as Bede says[^p287-2], « because the fish is born, lives and is nourished under the covering of the waters; so faith is drawn from the hidden things of the Scriptures, and is of hidden things, and is hidden »; whence to the Hebrews eleven: « Faith is the substance of things to be hoped for, the evidence of things that appear not ». — « To this, as Augustine says[^p287-3], the *serpent* is contrary, who by venomous fraud persuaded that God should not be believed »; whence Ecclesiastes ten: « He that breaketh a hedge », that is, the defence of sacred Scripture, « a serpent shall bite him », that is, the faithlessness of diabolical error.

33. Rightly also by the *egg* is understood *hope;* whence Augustine[^p287-4]: « *Hope* in the *egg,* because the life of the chick is not yet, but is to be, and is not yet seen, but is still hoped for. *For hope that is seen is not hope* », as is said to the Romans eight; and because in the egg the chick is imperfectly, and while it is being perfected the shell of the egg is broken; so to hope succeeds the perfection of beatitude; whence the first to the Corinthians thirteen[^p287-5]: « When that which is perfect is come, that which is in part shall be done away ». — « To this, as Augustine says[^p287-6], the *scorpion* is contrary », that is, *despair,* which makes one look backward and does harm from that side. « For the scorpion is to be guarded against from that side which it has venomous and stinging behind »; whence he who strives to overturn one from the hope of life is a scorpion; whence Ezechiel two[^p287-7]: « Subverters are with thee, and thou dwellest with scorpions ». — The *spiritual sense,* then, of the word put forward is that if any one ask for the bread of *the charity* of the Holy Spirit, the fish *of faith* and the egg *of hope,* God will not give him, nay, will remove from him, the stone *of obduracy,* the serpent *of faithlessness* and the scorpion *of despair;* which through the divine help are put far from holy men.

34. (Vers. 13.). *If you then, being evil* etc. After he has put forward the liberality in a carnal father, he concludes *from the lesser* in *the spiritual Father.* For if good is communicative[^p287-8], the greater good is the more communicative, and the greatest good the most communicative. If, then, a carnal father shares good with a son who asks, much more strongly does the heavenly one with a man who supplicates. And this is what he says: *If you then, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children.* Bede[^p287-9]: « He calls *evil* the *lovers of the world* »; and these are evil in a twofold way by the malice of fault; because Ecclesiasticus eleven[^p287-10]: « If thou be rich, thou shalt not be free from sin »; and Augustine: « Every rich man is either wicked, or the heir of a wicked man ». — Or, he calls *evil* the *Apostles* themselves, who in comparison with the divine goodness are called *evil;* whence Mark ten[^p287-11]: « None is good but God alone »; and Isaias sixty-four: « We are all become as one unclean, our justices as the rag of a menstruous woman »; and Job twenty-five: « The stars are not pure in his sight, how much less man » etc. Whence Gregory[^p287-12]: « Often our justice, brought to the examination of the divine justice, is injustice, and what shines in the eyes of the doer is filthy in the strictness of the judge ». — Or, they were *evil* on account of *venial sins,* without which the present life is not led; whence it is said to the Ephesians five[^p287-13]: « Redeeming the time, because the days are evil »; because, according to what
<!-- page 288 -->
is said in Genesis forty-seven, « the days of the pilgrimage of my life are few and evil »; because, according to what is said in the first of John one, « if we say that we have no sin, we are liars, and the truth is not in us ». It seems less, therefore, that good should come from the evil than good from the good.

35. And therefore he concludes: *How much more will your Father from heaven give the good spirit to them that ask him?* Nay, certainly he will give, because James one[^p288-1]: « Every best thing given and every perfect gift is from above ». This *good spirit* is the Holy Spirit, in whom all gifts are given; of whom Wisdom seven[^p288-2]: « I called upon him, and the spirit of wisdom came upon me »; and afterwards: « Now all good things came to me together with her »; and the first to the Corinthians twelve: « To one is given by the Spirit » etc.; and afterwards: « But all these things one and the same Spirit worketh, dividing to every one according as he will ». This Spirit is given *to them that ask* and desire, according to that of the Psalm[^p288-3]: « I opened my mouth, and drew in spirit »; Jeremias two: « A wild ass accustomed to the wilderness in the desire of his soul drew in the wind of his love ». For to this Spirit of the Lord the spirit of the Saints sighs; to the Romans eight: « It is the Spirit who asketh » etc.
