# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XII, Vers. 49-59
## *Secundo invitatur ad vigilantiam consideratione primi adventus*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 330 -->

#### Secundo invitatur ad vigilantiam consideratione primi adventus.

70. (Vers. 49.). *Ignem veni mittere in terram* etc. Postquam excitavit ad vigilantiam ex consideratione secundi adventus, hic excitat ad *considerationem primi adventus.* Et haec pars habet duas: in quarum prima ostendendo primi adventus efficaciam *excitat ad vigilantiam;* in secunda *reprehendit Iudaeorum desipientiam et negligentiam,* ibi[^p330-9]: *Dicebat autem et ad turbas* etc.

In exprimendo igitur *efficaciam primi adventus ad sollicitandum ad vigilantiam*[^p330-10], ostendit, se venisse ad tria, scilicet ad *immittendum dilectionis incendium,* ad *perficiendum passionis remedium* et ad *introducendum affectus carnalis divortium.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *immissionem incendii amoris* per efficaciam adventus primi dicit: *Ignem veni mittere in terram,* ignem scilicet *divini* amoris, qui igni comparatur Canticorum ultimo[^p330-11]: « Fortis est ut mors dilectio, dura sicut infernus aemulatio; lampades eius, lampades ignis » etc. Sed dilectio *carnalis* est sicut ignis *infernalis;* Iob trigesimo primo[^p330-12]: « Ignis est usque ad consumtionem devorans »; et Ecclesiastici tertio: « Sicut aqua exstinguit ignem » etc. Dilectio autem *spiritualis* est sicut ignis *caelestis;* Threnorum primo[^p330-13]: « De excelso misit ignem in ossibus meis » etc. Hunc *mittit* Dominus *communicando doctrinam;* Psalmus: « Ignitum eloquium tuum vehementer »; et Ieremiae vigesimo tertio: « Nonne verba mea sicut ignis, et sicut malleus
<!-- page 331 -->
conterens petram »? Item, *infundendo gratiam;* Deuteronomii quarto[^p331-1]: « De caelo te fecit audire vocem suam et in terra ostendit tibi ignem suum maximum ». Item, *inflammando ad iustitiam;* Ecclesiastici quadragesimo octavo[^p331-2]: « Surrexit Elias quasi ignis »; unde, quia in ipso erat ignis divini zeli, de quo Sophoniae tertio: « In igne zeli mei devorabitur omnis terra »; ideo merito ad verbum ipsius ignis de caelo descendit et super sacrificium, tertii Regum decimo octavo, et super principem quinquagenarium, quarti Regum primo: « Si homo Dei sum, descendat ignis de caelo » etc. — Et quoniam hic ignis frustra mittitur, nisi accendatur; ideo subdit: *Et quid volo, nisi ut accendatur?* Hunc *accendit meditatio beneficiorum praesentium;* Psalmus[^p331-3]: « Concaluit cor meum intra me, et in meditatione mea exardescet ignis »; et infra ultimo: « Nonne cor nostrum ardens erat in nobis » etc. Item, *rememoratio praeteritorum;* Levitici sexto[^p331-4]: « Ignis in altari meo semper ardebit, quem nutriet sacerdos, subiiciens ligna mane et per singulos dies » etc. Item, *praemeditatio futurorum promissorum;* Proverbiorum trigesimo: « Omnis sermo Dei ignitus, clypeus est omnibus sperantibus in se ».

71. (Vers. 50.). Secundo quantum ad *perfectionem remedii passionis* subditur: *Baptismo autem*[^p331-5] *habeo baptizari.* Nota, quod passio Christi dicitur *baptismus,* quia in eius sanguine purificamur quasi in lavacro baptismali; Apocalypsis septimo[^p331-6]: « Hi sunt, qui venerunt ex magna tribulatione et laverunt stolas suas in sanguine Agni »; unde ad Hebraeos nono: « Si sanguis hircorum et taurorum et cinis vitulae aspersus inquinatos sanctificat ad emundationem carnis; quanto magis sanguis Christi emundabit conscientiam nostram » etc. Hoc baptismate fuit Christus baptizatus, quia totum corpus eius fuit hoc sanguine rubricatum; Isaiae sexagesimo tertio[^p331-7]: « Quis est iste qui venit de Edom, tinctis vestibus de Bosra »? et post: « Quare ergo rubrum est indumentum tuum, et vestimenta tua sicut calcantium in torculari » etc. — Et nota, quod passio Christi comparatur *baptismo,* sicut hic, quia fuit *universalis;* Psalmus[^p331-8]: « Omnes fluctus tuos induxisti super me ». — Item comparatur *calici;* Matthaei vige
simo sexto[^p331-9]: « Pater, si fieri potest, transeat a me calix iste », quia *voluntaria;* Matthaei vigesimo: « Potestis bibere calicem, quem ego bibiturus sum »? — Item, quia *acerbissima* et gravis, comparatur *torculari;* Isaiae sexagesimo tertio[^p331-10]: « Torcular calcavi solus, et de gentibus non est vir mecum ».

72. Hoc ergo baptismo Christus baptizatus est, non pro indigentia necessitatis, sed pro abundantia caritatis; ideo subdit: *Et quomodo coarctor; coarctor* scilicet per amorem, qui habet cordis dominium; ad Ephesios secundo[^p331-11]: « Deus, qui dives est in misericordia, propter nimiam caritatem suam » etc. — Et nota, quod amor *coarctat per violentiam attractivam;* Ioannis tertio[^p331-12]: « Sic Deus dilexit mundum, ut Filium suum unigenitum daret »; Ieremiae trigesimo primo: « In caritate perpetua dilexi te, ideo attraxi te miserans tui » Unde Hugo[^p331-13]: « Magnam vim habes, caritas: tu sola Deum trahere potuisti de caelo ad terras. O quam forte vinculum tuum, quo et Deus ligari potuit et hoc ligatus vincula iniquitatis destruxit! Nescio, quid maius in tuam laudem dicere possum, quam quod Deum de caelo traheres, et hominem de terra ad caelum levares ». Unde Osee undecimo[^p331-14]: « In funiculis Adam traham eos, in vinculis caritatis ». — Item, *dilatat per benevolentiam diffusivam;* Psalmus: « Latum mandatum tuum nimis »; secundae ad Corinthios sexto: « Cor nostrum dilatatum est ». — *Elevat per adhaerentiam excessivam;* Canticorum ultimo[^p331-15]: « Quae est ista, quae ascendit de deserto, deliciis affluens »? Ioannis quarto: « Qui biberit, fiet in eo fons aquae salientis in vitam aeternam ». — *Inclinat per condescendentiam dignativam;* ad Galatas secundo[^p331-16]: « Qui dilexit me et tradidit semetipsum pro me ». — Propter ista quatuor ad Ephesios tertio: « In caritate radicati et fundati, ut possitis comprehendere cum omnibus Sanctis » etc. — Et quoniam haec caritas ostensa fuit in passione perfecte, ideo dicit: *Usquedum perficiatur,* scilicet in effusione sanguinis; Apocalypsis primo[^p331-17]: « Dilexit nos et lavit nos a peccatis nostris in sanguine suo »; et primae Ioannis tertio: « In hoc cognovimus caritatem Dei, quoniam ille pro nobis animam suam posuit ». Et hoc erat, quod Christus in cruce sitiebat; Ioannis decimo nono[^p331-18]:
<!-- page 332 -->
« Sciens Iesus, quia omnia iam consummata sunt, ut consummaretur Scriptura, dixit: Sitio ». « Cum ergo accepisset Iesus acetum, dixit: Consummatum est. Et inclinato capite, emisit spiritum ».

73. (Vers. 51.). Tertio quantum ad *introductionem divortii affectus carnalis* subiungit: *Putatis, quia*[^p332-1] *pacem veni dare in terram?* pacem scilicet terrenam. Hanc non venit dare, sed tollere, quia haec est pax *mala,* de qua Isaiae vigesimo octavo[^p332-2]: « Pepigimus foedus cum morte et cum inferno fecimus pactum ». — Hanc pacem vir iustus reprobat; in Psalmo[^p332-3]: « Zelavi super iniquos, pacem peccatorum videns »; quia talis pax est pax *prava;* Deuteronomii vigesimo nono: « Pax erit mihi, et ambulabo in pravitate cordis mei ». — Est pax *falsa;* Ieremiae sexto[^p332-4]: « Curabant contritionem filiae populi mei cum ignominia, dicentes: Pax, pax, et non erat pax ». — Est pax *momentanea;* primae ad Thessalonicenses quinto[^p332-5]: « Cum dixerint: Pax et securitas, tunc repentinus eis superveniet interitus ». — Est pax *deceptoria;* Ieremiae nono: « In ore pacem cum amico loquitur, et occulte ponit ei insidias ». — Est pax *turbativa;* in cantico, Isaiae trigesimo octavo[^p332-6]: « Ecce, in pace amaritudo mea amarissima »; Ecclesiastici quadragesimo primo: « O mors, quam amara est memoria tua homini habenti pacem in substantia sua »!

74. Haec pax est *inimica verae paci.* Nam Christi pax est pax *bona;* supra secundo[^p332-7]: « In terra pax hominibus bonae voluntatis »; ideo Apostolus: « Gratia vobis et pax ». — Est pax *vera;* ad Romanos decimo quarto[^p332-8]: « Regnum Dei est iustitia et pax et gaudium ». — Pax *permansiva;* Ioannis decimo quarto: « Pacem relinquo vobis, non quomodo mundus dat, ego do vobis ». — Est pax *directiva;* supra primo[^p332-9]: « Per viscera misericordiae, in quibus visitavit nos »; « ad dirigendos pedes nostros in viam pacis ». — Est pax *consolatoria;* Ioannis decimo sexto[^p332-10]: « Ut in me pacem habeatis; in mundo pressuram habebitis »; et ad Philippenses quarto: « Pax Dei, quae exsuperat omnem sensum, custodiat corda vestra et intelligentias vestras ». — Quoniam ergo per omnia contrariatur cum pace mundana; et contraria simul esse
non possunt[^p332-11]: ideo non potest introduci ab auctore pacis nisi per dissolutionem huius fictae pacis.

75. Ideo subdit: *Non, dico vobis,* id est, assero vobis, quod non veni pacem dare, sed separationem, secundum illud Isaiae quinquagesimo secundo[^p332-12]: « Recedite, recedite, exite inde, pollutum nolite tangere »; et secundae ad Corinthios sexto: « Exite de medio eorum et separamini, dicit Dominus »; et Matthaei decimo: « Veni enim separare hominem adversus patrem suum » etc. Et ratio huius est, quia dicitur ibidem: « Qui amat patrem aut matrem plus quam me non est me dignus »; et ideo infra decimo quarto[^p332-13]: « Si quis venit ad me et non odit patrem suum et matrem et uxorem et filios et fratres et sorores, adhuc autem et animam suam, non potest meus esse discipulus ».

76. (Vers. 52. 53.). Et propterea subdit: *Erunt enim ex hoc quinque in una domo divisi,* id est ab affectu carnali separati; et secundum illud Deuteronomii penultimo[^p332-14]: « Qui dixerit patri suo et matri suae: Nescio vos, et fratribus suis: Ignoro illos, et nescierunt filios suos. Hi custodierunt eloquium tuum et pactum tuum servaverunt ». — Et subdit *modum* divisionis: *Tres in duos, et duo in tres dividentur*[^p332-15], id est personae, quae maxime videntur per carnem coniunctae, per spiritum caritatis erunt a carnali amore seiunctae. Nec mirum, si hoc fiat per verbum divinum, quia ad Hebraeos quarto: « Vivus est sermo Dei et efficax et penetrabilior omni gladio ancipiti, pertingens usque ad divisionem animae ac spiritus, compagum quoque ac medullarum, et discretor cogitationum » etc. — Et subdit *modum et exemplum: Pater in filium et filius in patrem*[^p332-16], scilicet *dividentur.* Chrysostomus: « Solus Deus animarum pater est. Carnalibus patribus praebet carnis obsequium, spirituali patri animae sanctitatem ». Nec tantum hoc erit in sexu virili, verum etiam in muliebri; ideo subdit: *Mater in filiam, et filia in matrem, socrus in nurum suam, et nurus in socrum suam.*

77. Et nota, quod *quinque personas* praemisit Dominus et *sex necessitudines* posuit: patris et filii, matris et filiae, nurus et socrus; quia una persona
<!-- page 333 -->
est mater et socrus simul[^p333-1]. Et ponit duos viros et tres mulieres, sive quia mulieres magis pronae sunt ad divisionem propter earum instabilitatem, sive quia plures sunt effeminati et carnales quam spirituales; unde primae ad Corinthios undecimo[^p333-2]: « Ideo inter vos multi infirmi et imbecilles, et dormiunt multi ». *Ad litteram* ita factum est, sicut dicit, post eius praedicationem; unde Glossa Bedae: « Post baptisma passionis, post adventum ignis totus orbis contra se divisus est; unaquaeque domus habuit fideles et infideles, his contra fidem, illis pro fide pugnantibus », secundum illud Zachariae octavo[^p333-3]: « Divisi omnes homines, unumquemque contra proximum suum »; et Ecclesiastici trigesimo tertio: « Intuere omnia opera Altissimi: duo contra duo, et unum contra unum »; Matthaei decimo: « Tradet autem frater fratrem in mortem, et pater filium, et insurgent filii in parentes et morte eos afficient ».

78. *Spiritualiter* « quilibet *homo* est domus vel Dei, vel diaboli[^p333-4] »; secundum quod est in eo pax mala, quae est peccati, *diabolus* est pater; Ioannis octavo: « Vos ex patre diabolo estis »; *caro* autem est nurus et mater; Ezechielis decimo sexto: « Pater tuus Amorrhaeus, et mater tua Cethaea », id est garrula. Tunc autem *dividitur* filius contra patrem et matrem et socrum, quando carnis relinquit desideria et abrenuntiat diabolo et pompis eius et ab eis dividitur per baptismum; unde Psalmus[^p333-5]: « Quoniam pater meus et mater mea dereliquerunt me, Dominus autem assumsit me ». Ad istam divisionem faciendam oportet renasci denuo; Ioannis tertio: « Nisi quis renatus fuerit ex aqua et Spiritu sancto, non potest introire in regnum Dei ». Ignis enim divini amoris et baptismus tam flaminis quam fluminis et sanguinis omnes dividit a domesticis inimicis[^p333-6]; et haec est efficacia adventus primi.

79. (Vers. 54.). *Dicebat autem et ad turbas* etc. Postquam invitavit ad vigilantiam, hic secundo *arguit negligentiam,* quae de gratia Christi venientis non curat[^p333-7]. Et habet haec pars duas. In quarum prima *redarguit negligentiam respectu gratiae cognoscendae;* secundo vero, *respectu poenitentiae faciendae,* ibi[^p333-8]: *Aderant autem quidam ipso tempore.* Nam Christus venit ad gratiam conferendam et poenitentiam praedicandam.

*Negligentiam* autem *respectu gratiae cognoscendae redarguit* propter tria, scilicet propter commonitionem *exterioris indicii, interioris iudicii*[^p333-9] et *superioris edicti.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *commonitionem exterioris indicii,* propter quam negligentia cognoscendi est reprehensibilis, dicit: *Dicebat autem et ad turbas: Cum videritis nubem orientem ab occasu, statim dicitis: Nimbus venit,* id est imber pluviae, *et ita fiet*[^p333-10], quia hoc est signum, cum caelum nube operitur, quod terris pluvia praeparetur; Psalmus: « Qui operit caelum nubibus et parat terrae pluviam ».

80. (Vers. 55.). *Et cum austrum flantem, dicitis: Quia aestus erit, et ita*[^p333-11] *fit;* Iob trigesimo septimo: « Nonne vestimenta tua calida erunt, cum perflata fuerit terra austro »?

81. (Vers. 56.). Et quia ex his sensibilibus manuducitur homo ad intelligentiam illorum quae illi considerare negligebant, ideo subdit: *Hypocritae, faciem caeli et terrae nostis probare; hoc autem*[^p333-12]
<!-- page 334 -->
*tempus quomodo non probatis?* Ideo eos dicit *hypocritas,* quia magis profitebantur notitiam Scripturarum, sicut catholici, quam naturarum et stellarum, sicut astrologi; et tamen magis intendebant istis quam illis. Et hoc est hypocrisis in religione christiana, magis sollicitari circa naturalia quam circa gratuita. Unde Augustinus[^p334-1]: « Laudabilior est animus, cui nota est propria infirmitas, quam qui caelorum constellationes et terrarum fundamenta scrutatur ». Ideo Ecclesiastici tertio[^p334-2]: « Quae praecepit tibi Deus, cogita illa semper, et in pluribus operibus eius ne fueris curiosus ». Illi ergo sunt reprehensibiles, qui solliciti sunt considerare tempus huius pluviae et negligunt considerare tempus gratiae, cum per haec sensibilia manuduci possint ad spiritualia; unde reliquerunt patriam propter viam, et faciem Domini propter eius vestigia. Contra quos Augustinus in secundo de Libero Arbitrio[^p334-3]: « Vae his! qui dereliquerunt te ducem et oberrant in vestigiis tuis, qui nutus tuos pro te amant et obliviscuntur, quid innuas ».

82. Propter quod intelligendum, quod *nubes* insinuat carnem Christi; Isaiae decimo nono[^p334-4]: « Ascendet Dominus super nubem levem et ingredietur Aegyptum ». — *Apparitio nubis ab occasu* designat Christi carnem resurgentem et ascendentem; Psalmus[^p334-5]: « Qui ascendit super occasum, Dominus nomen illi ». — *Descensus nimbi et pluviae* descensum Spiritus sancti post Christi ascensionem designat; tertii Regum decimo octavo[^p334-6]: « Nubecula parva quasi vestigium hominis ascendebat de mari ». « Et facta est pluvia grandis ». — Per *austrum flantem* flatus Spiritus sancti venientis in discipulos; Canticorum quarto: « Surge aquilo, et veni auster, perfla hortum meum ». Hoc factum est, Actuum secundo, quando « factus est repente de caelo sonus tanquam advenientis spiritus vehementis ». — Per *aestum subsequentem* fervor intelligitur caritatis, qui omnes messes Ecclesiae maturavit; Proverbiorum decimo[^p334-7]: « Qui congregat in messe filius sapiens est; qui autem stertit aestate, filius confusionis » etc.

83. *Moraliter* autem per *nubem venientem ab occasu* intelligitur dolor compunctionis ex memoria mortis nascens; per *nimbum* intelligitur imber lacrymarum; per *austrum flantem* devotio laetificans; per *aestum* devotio inflammans; Tobiae tertio[^p334-8]: « Post tempestatem tranquillum facit et post lacrymationem et fletum exsultationem infundit ». — Unde is est ordo descensus gratiae in cor nostrum, quia primo homo considerat finem suum et mortem, et deinde concipit dolorem, post haec prorumpit in lacrymas, deinde sentit consolationem, tandem inflammatur ad dilectionem; unde supra septimo[^p334-9] de poenitente peccatrice dicitur, quod « stans retro secus pedes Domini, lacrymis coepit rigare pedes eius et unguento ungebat » etc.

84. (Vers. 57.). Secundo quantum ad *commonitionem interioris iudicii* subditur: *Quid autem et a vobis ipsis*[^p334-10] *non iudicatis quod iustum est?* quasi dicat: etsi negligitis exteriorem commonitionem, non negligatis interiorem. Unde Ecclesiastici trigesimo primo[^p334-11]: « Cognosce quae sunt proximi tui ex te ipso », id est ex tuo naturali iudicatorio. Insertum est enim homini naturale iudicatorium, per quod dirigitur ad videndum et eligendum; Psalmus[^p334-12]: « Signatum est super nos lumen vultus tui, Domine »; et Isaiae quadragesimo sexto: « Redite, praevaricatores, ad cor »; ibi enim dictatur quod iustum est. Et inter omnia, quae iusta sunt, haec dictat ratio naturalis, quae dixit etiam Antiochus, secundi Machabaeorum nono[^p334-13]: « Iustum est subditum esse Deo, et mortalem hominem non paria Deo sentire ». Hoc ergo potissime est iustum, quod debet iudicare quilibet, scilicet Deum esse colendum et Christum honorandum et Spiritus sancti donum cum reverentia suscipiendum; quia, Ioannis quinto, « qui non honorificat Filium non honorificat Patrem » etc. Iustum est etiam iudicare, quod[^p334-14] « Deus iudicabit orbem terrarum in aequitate », et se ipsum iudicando praevenire illud iudicium, quia, primae ad Corinthios undecimo, « si nosmetipsos diiudicaremus, non utique iudicaremur » etc. Unde conscientia naturaliter remurmurat,
<!-- page 335 -->
et ipsa etiam testatur, quod mala nostra non remanebunt inulta. Et ideo ex ipsa debemus sollicitari ad divinam gratiam promerendam; ad Romanos secundo[^p335-1]: « Cum gentes, quae Legem non habent, ea quae Legis sunt faciunt; eiusmodi Legem non habentes, ipsi sibi sunt lex, quia ostendunt opus Legis scriptum in cordibus suis, testimonium reddente illis conscientia eorum » etc.

85. (Vers. 58.). Tertium quantum ad *commonitionem superioris edicti* subdit: *Cum autem vadis cum adversario tuo ad principem in via*[^p335-2]. Beda: « *Adversarius* noster in via est sermo Dei contrarius nostris carnalibus desideriis in praesenti vita »; *princeps* noster est Christus; Isaiae trigesimo secundo: « Princeps ea quae digna sunt principe, cogitabit ». Ad hunc principem sermo Dei dirigit nos *triplici via,* scilicet *innocentiae,* de qua Psalmus[^p335-3]: « Ambulans in via immaculata, hic mihi ministrabat »; via *poenitentiae;* Exodi octavo: « Viam trium dierum ibimus in solitudine »; via *sapientiae;* Proverbiorum quarto: « Viam sapientiae monstrabo tibi, ducam te per semitas aequitatis ». — Has vias petebat Propheta demonstrari sibi, dicens[^p335-4]: « Vias tuas demonstra mihi ». Has autem ostendit *exemplo;* Ioannis decimo quarto: « Ego sum via, veritas et vita »; ostendit *verbo;* Isaiae trigesimo: « Audies verbum praeceptoris tui. Haec via bona, ambulate in ea »; ostendit etiam *dono divinitus inspirato;* Sapientiae decimo[^p335-5]: « Iustum deduxit Dominus per vias rectas et ostendit illi regnum Dei ».

86. Quoniam ergo sermo Dei dirigit nos per viam rectam, per quam carnalitas ire recusat; et ratio magis debet credere verbo Dei quam suggestioni carnali: ideo subdit: *Da operam liberari ab illo,* implendo *praecepta,* declinando *prohibita,* sperando *promissiones* et timendo *comminationes.* Qui haec facit securus est ab adversario; Ecclesiastici trigesimo sexto[^p335-6]: « Extolle adversarium et afflige
inimicum ». Qui enim verbum divinum extollit inimicum, id est carnem, mundum et diabolum, affligit. Beda[^p335-7]: « Cum in hac vita praeparas te ad placitam faciem iudicis videndam; quamvis sermo Dei sit contrarius tuae carni, tamen eo utere sicut custodia data tibi in hoc itinere, ne contemptus te accuset ». — Ideo subdit: *Ne forte trahat te ad*[^p335-8] *iudicem,* id est Christum; Actuum decimo: « Ipse est, qui constitutus est iudex vivorum et mortuorum » etc. — *Et iudex tradat te exactori,* id est diabolo, qui exiget pro culpa poenam[^p335-9]; Iob tertio: « Non exaudierunt vocem exactoris ». — *Et exactor mittat te in carcerem,* scilicet infernum[^p335-10]; Isaiae vigesimo quarto: « Congregabuntur in congregatione unius fascis in lacum et claudentur ibi in carcere »; quia poena ista est irremediabilis.

87. (Vers. 59.). Propterea subiungit: *Dico tibi, non exies*[^p335-11] *inde, donec novissimum minutum reddas,* id est nunquam, quia nunquam reddes. Beda[^p335-12]: « Reddit semper poenas pro peccatis patiendo, sed nunquam veniam assequendo ». Unde *donec* non ponit hic terminum, sicut nec ibi: « Dixit Dominus Domino meo etc., donec ponam » etc. Augustinus[^p335-13]: « *Donec* non terminum poenae significat, sed semper solves et nunquam persolves ». Et dicitur *persolvere novissimum minutum,* quia non tantum pro magnis, sed etiam pro minoribus reprobi puniuntur. Unde Chrysostomus[^p335-14]: « Si semel condemnatus missus fueris in carcerem, iam non solum pro gravibus peccatis, sed etiam pro verbo otioso, quod locutus es, exigentur a te supplicia ». In haec supplicia praecipitantur qui non custodiunt divina verba, quae mittunt hominem ad divinam gratiam et ad Christum; unde Ioannis quinto[^p335-15]: « Scrutamini Scripturas. Illae sunt quae testimonium perhibent de me ».

88. Ex his igitur apparet, quod negligentia hominis ad Dei gratiam cognoscendam et suscipiendam est inexcusabilis, pro eo quod ad illam commonitione
<!-- page 336 -->
triplici excitatur, scilicet per librum *creaturae, conscientiae* et *Scripturae,* quasi per tres testes, quorum unus *exterior,* alter *interior,* tertius quasi *superior.* Quapropter, sicut dicitur ad Hebraeos secundo[^p336-1], « abundantius oportet nos servare ea, quae audivimus, ne forte pereffluamus. Si enim qui per Angelos dictus est sermo factus est firmus, et omnis praevaricatio et inobedientia iustam mercedis accepit retributionem; quomodo nos effugiemus, si tantam neglexerimus salutem »?

#### Secondly they are invited to vigilance by the consideration of the first coming.

70. (Vers. 49.). *I am come to cast fire on the earth* etc. After he roused them to vigilance from the consideration of the second coming, here he rouses them to *the consideration of the first coming.* And this part has two: in the first of them, by showing the efficacy of the first coming, *he rouses them to vigilance;* in the second *he reproves the folly and negligence of the Jews,* there[^p330-9]: *And he said also to the multitudes* etc.

In expressing, then, *the efficacy of the first coming for soliciting to vigilance*[^p330-10], he shows that he came for three things, namely *to send in the burning of dilection,* *to accomplish the remedy of the passion* and *to bring in the divorce of carnal affection.*

First, then, as to *the sending in of the burning of love* through the efficacy of the first coming, he says: *I am come to cast fire on the earth,* the fire, namely, of *divine* love, which is compared to fire in the last of the Canticle[^p330-11]: « Dilection is strong as death, jealousy as hard as hell, the lamps thereof are lamps of fire » etc. But *carnal* dilection is as *infernal* fire; Job, the thirty-first chapter[^p330-12]: « It is a fire that devoureth even to consumption »; and Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter: « As water quencheth fire » etc. But *spiritual* dilection is as *heavenly* fire; Lamentations, the first chapter[^p330-13]: « From above he hath sent fire into my bones » etc. This the Lord *sends* by *communicating doctrine;* the Psalm: « Thy word is exceedingly fiery »; and Jeremias, the twenty-third chapter: « Are not my words as a fire, and as a hammer
<!-- page 331 -->
that breaketh the rock in pieces »? Again, by *pouring in grace;* Deuteronomy, the fourth chapter[^p331-1]: « Out of heaven he made thee hear his voice, and upon earth he shewed thee his exceeding great fire ». Again, by *inflaming to justice;* Ecclesiasticus, the forty-eighth chapter[^p331-2]: « Elias stood up as a fire »; whence, because in him was the fire of divine zeal, of which Sophonias, the third chapter: « In the fire of my zeal shall all the earth be devoured »; therefore deservedly at his word fire came down from heaven both upon the sacrifice, the third of Kings, the eighteenth chapter, and upon the captain of fifty, the fourth of Kings, the first chapter: « If I be a man of God, let fire come down from heaven » etc. — And since this fire is sent in vain unless it be kindled, therefore he subjoins: *And what will I, but that it be kindled?* This *the meditation of present benefits kindles;* the Psalm[^p331-3]: « My heart grew hot within me: and in my meditation a fire shall flame out »; and below, the last chapter: « Was not our heart burning within us » etc. Again, *the remembrance of things past;* Leviticus, the sixth chapter[^p331-4]: « The fire on my altar shall always burn, and the priest shall feed it, putting wood under it in the morning and every day » etc. Again, *the premeditation of the promises to come;* Proverbs, the thirtieth chapter: « Every word of God is fire tried: he is a buckler to all that hope in him ».

71. (Vers. 50.). Secondly, as to *the accomplishment of the remedy of the passion,* it is subjoined: *And I have a baptism*[^p331-5] *wherewith I am to be baptized.* Note that the passion of Christ is called *a baptism,* because in his blood we are purified as in the baptismal laver; the Apocalypse, the seventh chapter[^p331-6]: « These are they who are come out of great tribulation, and have washed their robes in the blood of the Lamb »; whence to the Hebrews, the ninth chapter: « If the blood of goats and of oxen, and the ashes of an heifer being sprinkled, sanctify such as are defiled, to the cleansing of the flesh: how much more shall the blood of Christ cleanse our conscience » etc. With this baptism Christ was baptized, because his whole body was reddened with this blood; Isaias, the sixty-third chapter[^p331-7]: « Who is this that cometh from Edom, with dyed garments from Bosra »? and after: « Why then is thy apparel red, and thy garments like theirs that tread in the winepress » etc. — And note that the passion of Christ is compared to *a baptism,* as here, because it was *universal;* the Psalm[^p331-8]: « All thy waves thou hast brought in upon me ». — Again, it is compared to *a chalice;* Matthew, the twenty-
sixth chapter[^p331-9]: « Father, if it be possible, let this chalice pass from me », because it was *voluntary;* Matthew, the twentieth chapter: « Can you drink the chalice that I shall drink »? — Again, because it was *most bitter* and grievous, it is compared to *a winepress;* Isaias, the sixty-third chapter[^p331-10]: « I have trodden the winepress alone, and of the Gentiles there is not a man with me ».

72. With this baptism, then, Christ was baptized, not for the want of necessity, but for the abundance of charity; therefore he subjoins: *And how am I straitened; straitened,* namely, by love, which has the lordship of the heart; to the Ephesians, the second chapter[^p331-11]: « God, who is rich in mercy, for his exceeding charity » etc. — And note that love *straitens by an attractive violence;* John, the third chapter[^p331-12]: « God so loved the world, as to give his only begotten Son »; Jeremias, the thirty-first chapter: « I have loved thee with an everlasting charity, therefore have I drawn thee, taking pity on thee » Whence Hugh[^p331-13]: « Great power hast thou, charity: thou alone couldst draw God from heaven to earth. O how strong is thy bond, by which both God could be bound, and, bound by it, destroyed the bonds of iniquity! I know not what greater thing I can say in thy praise, than that thou drewest God from heaven, and raisedst man from earth to heaven ». Whence Osee, the eleventh chapter[^p331-14]: « I will draw them with the cords of Adam, with the bands of charity ». — Again, *it enlarges by a diffusive benevolence;* the Psalm: « Thy commandment is exceeding broad »; the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter: « Our heart is enlarged ». — *It raises up by an excessive adherence;* the last of the Canticle[^p331-15]: « Who is this that cometh up from the desert, flowing with delights »? John, the fourth chapter: « He that shall drink, it shall become in him a fountain of water, springing up into life everlasting ». — *It inclines by a deigning condescension;* to the Galatians, the second chapter[^p331-16]: « Who loved me, and delivered himself for me ». — On account of these four, to the Ephesians, the third chapter: « Rooted and founded in charity, that you may be able to comprehend with all the saints » etc. — And since this charity was shown perfectly in the passion, therefore he says: *Until it be accomplished,* namely in the shedding of blood; the Apocalypse, the first chapter[^p331-17]: « He loved us, and washed us from our sins in his blood »; and the first of John, the third chapter: « In this we have known the charity of God, because he hath laid down his life for us ». And this it was for which Christ thirsted on the cross; John, the nineteenth chapter[^p331-18]:
<!-- page 332 -->
« Jesus knowing that all things were now accomplished, that the scripture might be fulfilled, said: I thirst ». « When Jesus therefore had taken the vinegar, he said: It is consummated. And bowing his head, he yielded up the ghost ».

73. (Vers. 51.). Thirdly, as to *the bringing in of the divorce of carnal affection,* he subjoins: *Think ye, that*[^p332-1] *I am come to give peace on earth?* earthly peace, namely. This he came not to give, but to take away, because this is an *evil* peace, of which Isaias, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p332-2]: « We have entered into a league with death, and we have made a covenant with hell ». — This peace the just man reproves; in the Psalm[^p332-3]: « I had a zeal on occasion of the wicked, seeing the peace of sinners »; because such a peace is a *depraved* peace; Deuteronomy, the twenty-ninth chapter: « I shall have peace, and will walk on in the depravity of my heart ». — It is a *false* peace; Jeremias, the sixth chapter[^p332-4]: « They cured the breach of the daughter of my people with disgrace, saying: Peace, peace: and there was no peace ». — It is a *momentary* peace; the first to the Thessalonians, the fifth chapter[^p332-5]: « When they shall say, peace and security; then shall sudden destruction come upon them ». — It is a *deceitful* peace; Jeremias, the ninth chapter: « With his mouth he speaketh peace with his friend, and secretly he layeth snares for him ». — It is a *troubling* peace; in the canticle, Isaias, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p332-6]: « Behold in peace is my bitterness most bitter »; Ecclesiasticus, the forty-first chapter: « O death, how bitter is the remembrance of thee to a man that hath peace in his possessions »!

74. This peace is *the enemy of true peace.* For the peace of Christ is a *good* peace; above, the second chapter[^p332-7]: « On earth peace to men of good will »; therefore the Apostle: « Grace to you and peace ». — It is a *true* peace; to the Romans, the fourteenth chapter[^p332-8]: « The kingdom of God is justice and peace and joy ». — A *permanent* peace; John, the fourteenth chapter: « Peace I leave with you: not as the world giveth, do I give unto you ». — It is a *directive* peace; above, the first chapter[^p332-9]: « Through the bowels of mercy, in which he hath visited us »; « to direct our feet into the way of peace ». — It is a *consoling* peace; John, the sixteenth chapter[^p332-10]: « That in me you may have peace. In the world you shall have distress »; and to the Philippians, the fourth chapter: « The peace of God, which surpasseth all understanding, keep your hearts and minds ». — Since, then, it is contrary in all things to worldly peace, and contraries cannot be
together[^p332-11], therefore it cannot be brought in by the author of peace save through the dissolution of this feigned peace.

75. Therefore he subjoins: *I tell you, no,* that is, I assure you that I came not to give peace, but separation, according to that of Isaias, the fifty-second chapter[^p332-12]: « Depart, depart, go ye out from thence, touch no unclean thing »; and the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter: « Go out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord »; and Matthew, the tenth chapter: « For I came to separate a man against his father » etc. And the reason of this is, because it is said in the same place: « He that loveth father or mother more than me, is not worthy of me »; and therefore below, the fourteenth chapter[^p332-13]: « If any man come to me, and hate not his father, and mother, and wife, and children, and brethren, and sisters, yea and his own life also, he cannot be my disciple ».

76. (Vers. 52. 53.). And on this account he subjoins: *For there shall be from henceforth five in one house divided,* that is, separated from carnal affection; and according to that of Deuteronomy, the last chapter but one[^p332-14]: « Who hath said to his father and to his mother: I know you not; and to his brethren: I know them not; and they have not known their own children. These have kept thy word, and observed thy covenant ». — And he subjoins *the manner* of the division: *Three shall be divided against two, and two against three*[^p332-15], that is, the persons who seem most joined through the flesh will be disjoined from carnal love through the spirit of charity. Nor is it wonderful if this is done through the divine word, because to the Hebrews, the fourth chapter: « The word of God is living and effectual, and more piercing than any two edged sword; and reaching unto the division of the soul and the spirit, of the joints also and the marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts » etc. — And he subjoins *the manner and an example: The father against the son, and the son against the father*[^p332-16], namely *shall be divided.* Chrysostom: « God alone is the father of souls. To carnal fathers one renders the service of the flesh, to the spiritual father the holiness of the soul ». Nor will this be in the male sex only, but also in the female; therefore he subjoins: *The mother against the daughter, and the daughter against the mother, the mother in law against her daughter in law, and the daughter in law against her mother in law.*

77. And note that the Lord first named *five persons* and set down *six relationships:* of father and son, of mother and daughter, of daughter in law and mother in law; because one person
<!-- page 333 -->
is mother and mother in law at once[^p333-1]. And he sets down two men and three women, either because women are more prone to division on account of their instability, or because more are effeminate and carnal than spiritual; whence the first to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter[^p333-2]: « Therefore are there many infirm and weak among you, and many sleep ». *According to the letter* it was so done, as he says, after his preaching; whence the Gloss of Bede: « After the baptism of the passion, after the coming of the fire, the whole world was divided against itself; each house had faithful and unfaithful, these fighting against the faith, those for the faith », according to that of Zacharias, the eighth chapter[^p333-3]: « I divided all men, every one against his neighbour »; and Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-third chapter: « Look upon all the works of the most High: two against two, and one against one »; Matthew, the tenth chapter: « The brother shall deliver up the brother to death, and the father the son: and the children shall rise up against their parents, and shall put them to death ».

78. *Spiritually* « every *man* is a house either of God or of the devil[^p333-4] »; according as there is in him the evil peace, which is that of sin, *the devil* is the father; John, the eighth chapter: « You are of your father the devil »; but *the flesh* is the daughter in law and the mother; Ezechiel, the sixteenth chapter: « Thy father was an Amorrhite, and thy mother a Cethite », that is, a chatterer. But then the son *is divided* against the father and the mother and the mother in law, when he leaves the desires of the flesh and renounces the devil and his pomps and is divided from them through baptism; whence the Psalm[^p333-5]: « For my father and my mother have left me: but the Lord hath taken me up ». To make this division one must be born again; John, the third chapter: « Unless a man be born again of water and the Holy Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God ». For the fire of divine love and the baptism as well of the Spirit as of water and of blood divides all from their domestic enemies[^p333-6]; and this is the efficacy of the first coming.

79. (Vers. 54.). *And he said also to the multitudes* etc. After he invited them to vigilance, here secondly *he reproves the negligence* that cares not for the grace of the coming Christ[^p333-7]. And this part has two. In the first of them *he convicts negligence in respect of grace to be known;* but in the second, *in respect of penance to be done,* there[^p333-8]: *And there were present some at that very time.* For Christ came to confer grace and to preach penance.

Now *he convicts negligence in respect of grace to be known* on account of three things, namely on account of the monition *of an exterior sign*[^tr-corrigenda], *of an interior judgement*[^p333-9] and *of a superior edict.*

First, then, as to *the monition of an exterior sign,* on account of which the negligence of knowing is reprehensible, he says: *And he said also to the multitudes: When you see a cloud rising from the west, presently you say: A shower is coming,* that is, a downpour of rain, *and so it shall be*[^p333-10], because this is the sign, when the heaven is covered with a cloud, that rain is being prepared for the earth; the Psalm: « Who covereth the heaven with clouds, and prepareth rain for the earth ».

80. (Vers. 55.). *And when ye see the south wind blow, you say: There will be heat: and so*[^p333-11] *it cometh to pass;* Job, the thirty-seventh chapter: « Are not thy garments hot, when the earth is blown upon by the south wind »?

81. (Vers. 56.). And because by these sensible things man is led by the hand to the understanding of those things which they neglected to consider, therefore he subjoins: *You hypocrites, you know how to discern the face of the heaven and of the earth: but*[^p333-12]
<!-- page 334 -->
*how is it that you do not discern this time?* Therefore he calls them *hypocrites,* because they professed the knowledge of the Scriptures, as catholics, more than that of natures and of the stars, as astrologers; and yet they attended more to these than to those. And this is hypocrisy in the Christian religion: to be more solicitous about natural things than about things of grace. Whence Augustine[^p334-1]: « More praiseworthy is the mind to which its own infirmity is known, than that which searches out the constellations of the heavens and the foundations of the earth ». Therefore Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter[^p334-2]: « The things that God hath commanded thee, think on them always, and in many of his works be not curious ». Those, then, are reprehensible who are solicitous to consider the time of this rain and neglect to consider the time of grace, although through these sensible things they could be led by the hand to spiritual things; whence they have left the fatherland for the way, and the face of the Lord for his footprints. Against whom Augustine in the second book *On Free Choice*[^p334-3]: « Woe to them who forsake thee as their guide and wander among thy footprints, who love thy nods instead of thee and forget what thou signifiest by them ».

82. On account of which it is to be understood that *the cloud* signifies the flesh of Christ; Isaias, the nineteenth chapter[^p334-4]: « The Lord will ascend upon a swift cloud, and will enter into Egypt ». — *The appearing of the cloud from the west* designates the flesh of Christ rising again and ascending; the Psalm[^p334-5]: « Who ascendeth upon the west, the Lord is his name ». — *The descent of the shower and of the rain* designates the descent of the Holy Spirit after the ascension of Christ; the third of Kings, the eighteenth chapter[^p334-6]: « A little cloud arose out of the sea like a man's foot ». « And there was a great rain ». — By *the south wind blowing,* the breath of the Holy Spirit coming upon the disciples; the Canticle, the fourth chapter: « Arise, O north wind, and come, O south wind, blow through my garden ». This was done, Acts, the second chapter, when « suddenly there came a sound from heaven, as of a mighty wind coming ». — By *the heat following* is understood the fervour of charity, which ripened all the harvests of the Church; Proverbs, the tenth chapter[^p334-7]: « He that gathereth in the harvest is a wise son: but he that snorteth in the summer is the son of confusion » etc.

83. *Morally,* however, by *the cloud coming from the west* is understood the sorrow of compunction springing from the memory of death; by *the shower* is understood the downpour of tears; by *the south wind blowing,* gladdening devotion; by *the heat,* inflaming devotion; Tobias, the third chapter[^p334-8]: « After a storm he maketh a calm, and after tears and weeping he poureth in joyfulness ». — Whence this is the order of the descent of grace into our heart: that first a man considers his end and death, and then conceives sorrow, after this breaks out into tears, then feels consolation, at last is inflamed to dilection; whence above, the seventh chapter[^p334-9], it is said of the penitent sinful woman that « standing behind at the feet of the Lord, she began to wash his feet with tears, and anointed them with ointment » etc.

84. (Vers. 57.). Secondly, as to *the monition of an interior judgement,* it is subjoined: *And why even of yourselves*[^p334-10] *do you not judge that which is just?* as if he should say: although you neglect the exterior monition, do not neglect the interior. Whence Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-first chapter[^p334-11]: « Judge of the disposition of thy neighbour by thyself », that is, by thy natural judicatory. For there is implanted in man a natural judicatory, through which he is directed to seeing and choosing; the Psalm[^p334-12]: « The light of thy countenance, O Lord, is signed upon us »; and Isaias, the forty-sixth chapter: « Return, ye transgressors, to the heart »; for there that which is just is dictated. And among all the things that are just, natural reason dictates these, which even Antiochus said, the second of Machabees, the ninth chapter[^p334-13]: « It is just to be subject to God, and that a mortal man should not equal himself to God ». This, then, is most especially just, which every one ought to judge: namely that God is to be worshipped and Christ to be honoured and the gift of the Holy Spirit to be received with reverence; because, John, the fifth chapter, « he who honoureth not the Son, honoureth not the Father » etc. It is just also to judge that[^p334-14] « God will judge the world in equity », and by judging oneself to forestall that judgement, because, the first to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter, « if we would judge ourselves, we should not be judged » etc. Whence conscience naturally murmurs back,
<!-- page 335 -->
and itself also testifies that our evils will not remain unpunished. And therefore by it we ought to be stirred to merit the divine grace; to the Romans, the second chapter[^p335-1]: « When the Gentiles, who have not the law, do those things that are of the law; these having not the law are a law to themselves: who shew the work of the law written in their hearts, their conscience bearing witness to them » etc.

85. (Vers. 58.). Thirdly, as to *the monition of a superior edict,* he subjoins: *And when thou goest with thy adversary to the prince, whilst thou art in the way*[^p335-2]. Bede: « Our *adversary* in the way is the word of God, contrary to our carnal desires in the present life »; our *prince* is Christ; Isaias, the thirty-second chapter: « The prince will devise such things as are worthy of a prince ». To this prince the word of God directs us by *a threefold way,* namely of *innocence,* of which the Psalm[^p335-3]: « The man that walked in the perfect way, he served me »; the way of *penance;* Exodus, the eighth chapter: « We will go three days' journey into the wilderness »; the way of *wisdom;* Proverbs, the fourth chapter: « I will shew thee the way of wisdom, I will lead thee by the paths of equity ». — These ways the Prophet asked to be shown to him, saying[^p335-4]: « Shew thy ways to me ». And these he shows *by example;* John, the fourteenth chapter: « I am the way, and the truth, and the life »; he shows them *by word;* Isaias, the thirtieth chapter: « Thou shalt hear the word of thy teacher. This is the good way, walk ye in it »; he shows them also *by a gift divinely inspired;* Wisdom, the tenth chapter[^p335-5]: « The Lord conducted the just through the right ways, and shewed him the kingdom of God ».

86. Since, then, the word of God directs us by the right way, by which carnality refuses to go, and reason ought to believe the word of God rather than carnal suggestion, therefore he subjoins: *Endeavour to be delivered from him,* by fulfilling *the precepts,* by declining *the things forbidden,* by hoping for *the promises* and by fearing *the threatenings.* He who does these things is secure from the adversary; Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-sixth chapter[^p335-6]: « Exalt the adversary, and afflict the enemy ». For he who exalts the divine word, an adversary, afflicts the enemy, that is, the flesh, the world and the devil. Bede[^p335-7]: « When in this life thou preparest thyself to see the well-pleased face of the judge, although the word of God be contrary to thy flesh, yet use it as a guard given thee on this journey, lest, despised, it accuse thee ». — Therefore he subjoins: *Lest perhaps he draw thee to*[^p335-8] *the judge,* that is, Christ; Acts, the tenth chapter: « It is he who is appointed judge of the living and of the dead » etc. — *And the judge deliver thee to the exacter,* that is, to the devil, who will exact the penalty for the fault[^p335-9]; Job, the third chapter: « They have not heard the voice of the exacter ». — *And the exacter cast thee into prison,* namely hell[^p335-10]; Isaias, the twenty-fourth chapter: « They shall be gathered together as in the bundle of one into the pit, and they shall be shut up there in prison »; because that penalty is irremediable.

87. (Vers. 59.). On this account he subjoins: *I say to thee, thou shalt not go out*[^p335-11] *thence, until thou pay the very last mite,* that is, never, because thou wilt never pay it. Bede[^p335-12]: « He pays the penalties for his sins always by suffering, but never by obtaining pardon ». Whence *until* does not set a term here, as neither there: « The Lord said to my Lord etc., until I make » etc. Augustine[^p335-13]: « *Until* signifies not a term of the penalty, but that thou wilt always be paying and never pay in full ». And he is said *to pay in full the very last mite,* because the reprobate are punished not only for great things, but also for lesser ones. Whence Chrysostom[^p335-14]: « If once condemned thou art sent into prison, now not only for grave sins, but even for an idle word that thou hast spoken, torments will be exacted of thee ». Into these torments are cast headlong those who keep not the divine words, which send man to the divine grace and to Christ; whence John, the fifth chapter[^p335-15]: « Search the scriptures. The same are they that give testimony of me ».

88. From these things, then, it is plain that the negligence of man in knowing and receiving the grace of God is inexcusable, for the reason that to it he is roused by a monition
<!-- page 336 -->
threefold, namely through the book *of the creature, of conscience* and *of Scripture,* as by three witnesses, of which one is *exterior,* another *interior,* the third as it were *superior.* Wherefore, as is said to the Hebrews, the second chapter[^p336-1], « we ought more diligently to observe the things which we have heard, lest perhaps we should let them slip. For if the word, spoken by angels, became steadfast, and every transgression and disobedience received a just recompense of reward; how shall we escape, if we neglect so great salvation »?
