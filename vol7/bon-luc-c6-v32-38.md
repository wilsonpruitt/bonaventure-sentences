# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. VI, Vers. 32-38
## *Et si diligitis illos etc. Postquam promulgavit leges evangelicas sub perfectione conveniente, hic secundo probat ex conditione deficiente*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 156 -->

77. *Et si diligitis illos*[^p156-3] etc. Postquam promulgavit leges evangelicas sub perfectione conveniente, hic secundo *probat ex conditione deficiente,* ubi ostendit, quod praecipere debuit secundum modum praemissum; alioquin nullum est meritum apud Deum. Unde ostendit hic tria, scilicet quod non sufficit ad meritum esse *benevolum* respectu *amantium,* nec *beneficum* solum respectu *retribuentium,* nec *communicativum* respectu *recompensantium.* Et in hoc ostendit, quod perfecta benevolentia et beneficentia non tantum debet se extendere ad *amicos,* verum etiam ad *adversarios* iuxta formam superius[^p156-4] promulgatam.

78. (Vers. 32.). Ostendit ergo primo, quod non sufficit esse *benevolum solum respectu diligentium,* quia hoc est peccatorum; propter quod ait: *Et si diligitis illos qui vos diligunt,* id est, quia vos diligunt, *quae vobis est gratia?* id est quod meritum retributione dignum? quasi dicat: nullum. Et rationem reddit: *Nam et peccatores diligentes se diligunt,* qui nullum habent meritum apud Deum; hoc enim est *naturale,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici decimo tertio[^p156-5]: « Omne animal diligit simile sibi, sic et omnis homo proximum sibi » etc.; vel etiam *carnale,* sicut Ecclesiastici trigesimo septimo: « Sodalis amico condolet causa ventris et contra hostem accipiet scutum ». Talis dilectio apud Deum non habet meritum, quia non impenditur propter Deum. Si enim propter Deum impenderetur, utique diligeret homo *diligentes se,* secundum illud Proverbiorum octavo[^p156-6]: « Ego diligentes me diligo »; *minus* etiam *diligentibus* plus dilectionis rependeret, secundum illud secundae ad Corinthios duodecimo: « Ego autem libentissime impendam et superimpendar pro animabus vestris, licet, plus vos diligens, minus diligar »; item, *nullo modo* etiam *diligentes* diligeret, secundum illud primae Ioannis quarto[^p156-7]: « In hoc apparuit caritas Dei, non quia nos dilexerimus Deum, sed quoniam ipse prior dilexit nos »; et ad Romanos quinto: « Commendat autem caritatem suam Deus in nobis, quoniam, cum adhuc peccatores essemus, Christus pro impiis mortuus est ». — Unde quando solum ad *diligentes* extenditur, non est dilectio divina nec gratuita, sed mercenaria et humana, ac per hoc nec retributione digna.

79. (Vers. 33.). Non sufficit etiam esse *beneficum respectu retribuentium,* quia hoc etiam est ipsorum mundanorum; propter quod subdit: *Et si benefeceritis his qui vobis benefaciunt;* Glossa[^p156-8]: « Id est, ideo, quia benefaciunt »; *quae vobis est gratia?* id est quod meritum? quasi dicat: nullum. Et ratio redditur: *Siquidem et peccatores hoc faciunt;* beneficium namque pro recompensatione factum non est gratuitum, sed venale, et ideo nec acceptabile est. Unde Ecclesiastici vigesimo[^p156-9]: « Datus insipientis non erit tibi utilis; oculi eius septemplices sunt; exigua dabit et multa improperabit ». Sed illud quod liberaliter et hilariter datur, illud acceptatur, secundum illud secundae ad Corinthios nono: « Unusquisque prout destinavit in corde suo, non ex tristitia, aut ex necessitate; hilarem enim datorem diligit Deus ». Si ergo benefacere benefacientibus solum est *debitum,* benefacere non benefacientibus *gratuitum;* videtur, quod malefacere benefacientibus est *impium* et crudelissimum; quod quidem facit peccator, cum offendit Deum; iuxta quod secundi Paralipomenon
<!-- page 157 -->
trigesimo secundo[^p157-1] dicitur de Ezechia, quod « non iuxta beneficia, quae accepit a Deo, retribuit, quia elevatum est cor eius ». Unde Christus potest dicere illud Psalmi: « Retribuebant mihi mala pro bonis », et peccator Christo illud primi Regum vigesimo quarto: « Tu tribuisti mihi bona, et ego reddidi tibi mala ». Et eo ipso, si vult Domino reconciliari, oportet, quod ipse benefaciat non tantum benefacienti, sed etiam adversanti; et tale est beneficium caritatis secundum illud primae ad Corinthios decimo tertio[^p157-2]: « Caritas patiens est, benigna est ».

80. (Vers. 34.). Non sufficit etiam esse *communicativum respectu recompensantium,* quia hoc est ipsorum feneratorum; unde et subiungit: *Et si mutuum dederitis his, a quibus speratis recipere,* scilicet recompensationem; *quae gratia est vobis?* quasi dicat: nulla. Et hoc ostendit: *Nam et peccatores peccatoribus fenerantur,* id est, commodant ad usuram; unde subdit: *Ut recipiant aequalia,* scilicet pro servitio, non solum pro debito. Et quia mercedem accipiunt, non habent meritum apud Deum; unde in Psalmo[^p157-3]: « Quis ascendet in montem Domini »? et subditur: « Qui pecuniam suam non dedit ad usuram et munera super innocentem non accepit ». Alioquin in montem Domini non ascendit; unde Ezechielis decimo octavo[^p157-4] ille iustificatur, de quo dicitur: « Qui ad usuram non accommodaverit et amplius non acceperit »; et ille e contrario, qui amplius accipit, reprobatur ibidem. Unde talibus potest dici illud Aggaei primo[^p157-5]: « Respexistis ad amplius, et ecce, factum est minus », quia in Psalmo dicitur: « Nihil invenerunt viri divitiarum in manibus suis ». Tales feneratores inter peccatores computantur, quia sunt oppressores aliorum; unde Proverbiorum vigesimo secundo[^p157-6]: « Qui accipit mutuum servus est fenerantis ». Et propterea inhibetur Exodi vigesimo secundo: « Si pecuniam mutuam dederis pauperi, non urgebis eum quasi exactor nec usuris opprimes ».

81. Sed contrarium huius videtur dici Deuteronomii vigesimo tertio[^p157-7]: « Non fenerabis fratri tuo, sed alieno ». — Sed intelligendum est, quod hoc est malum *secundum se;* sed permisit illis Lex hoc, sicut et libellum repudii, propter vitationem maioris mali[^p157-8]. — Ratio autem huius prohibitionis est multiplex: tum quia pecunia propter accommodationem non deterioratur; tum quia de se non fructificat, sed tota ratio utilitatis est ex parte utentis; tum quia continue consumit et devastat, et ita commodum mutui convertitur in incommodum damni; tum quia in mutuo pecuniae transit rei mutuatae dominium, quia non tenetur eandem[^p157-9] numero reddere; tum quia totum transfertur periculum in eum qui fuerat mutuatus; tum etiam, quia in tali modo mutuandi plus diligitur nummus quam proximus, dum nummos proximo accommodat non ex amore proximi, sed ex amore nummi.

82. (Vers. 35.). *Verumtamen diligite inimicos* etc. Hic post promulgationem et probationem resumit *mandata approbata cum ratione movente.* Sunt autem tria, quae debent nos movere ad praedicta facienda, scilicet *auctoritas praecepti, immensitas praemii* et *sublimitas exempli.* Primum movet irascibilem, secundum concupiscibilem, tertium vero rationalem.

Resumit igitur praedicta documenta per *modum praecepti,* cum ait: *Verumtamen diligite inimicos vestros et*[^p157-10] *benefacite,* scilicet ut non « diligatis verbo neque lingua, sed opere et veritate », primae Ioannis tertio. Et hoc imperando dicit, quia, sicut dicitur Ioannis decimo quinto, « hoc est praeceptum meum, ut diligatis invicem, sicut dilexi vos ». Ipse autem dilexit non solum affectu, sed etiam effectu, sine aliqua spe retributionis. — Ideo additur: *Et mutuum date, nihil inde sperantes,* scilicet recompensationis; Deuteronomii vigesimo tertio[^p157-11]: « Fratri tuo absque usura id quod indiget, accommodabis, ut benedicat tibi Dominus », id est, non quaeras retributionem de hoc ab homine, sicut usurarii, qui propter spem usurae perdunt spem vitae aeternae; contra quos Apostolus primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p157-12] ait: « Si tantum in hac vita sperantes sumus, miserabiliores sumus omnibus hominibus ». Tales sunt divites, qui in divitiis sperant; talium « spes est vacua et labores sine fructu », Sapientiae tertio. Et ideo Ecclesiastici trigesimo primo: « Beatus dives, qui inventus est sine macula, et qui post aurum non abiit nec speravit in pecunia et thesauris. Quis est hic, et laudabimus eum? Fecit enim mirabilia in vita sua ».

83. Nota, quod *spes in mutuo* potest esse ad simile *obsequium,* vel ad *lucrum.* Primo modo licet;
<!-- page 158 -->
secundo modo potest esse dupliciter: vel *expressa* per pactum, et haec simpliciter est prohibita, quia facit usurarium; vel *tacita* in corde, et hoc modo dupliciter: vel *principaliter movens* ad mutuum, et haec similiter prohibita, quia contra mandatum divinum est; vel *non principaliter movens,* sed quasi annexa, quia, licet non faciat propter commodum principaliter vel lucrum, credit tamen, accipientem non esse sibi ingratum, et ideo facit libentius. Hic vero non est talis spes commodi contra divinum mandatum[^p158-1].

84. Secundo subdit rationem motivam *ex parte praemii,* cum adiungit: *Et erit merces vestra multa;* Ecclesiastici duodecimo[^p158-2]: « Benefac iusto, et invenies retributionem magnam, et si non ab ipso, certe a Domino ». Et dicitur retributio *multa* et *magna,* quia aeterna merces omnia bona continet in excellentia, secundum illud Psalmi: « Quam magna multitudo dulcedinis tuae, Domine, quam abscondisti timentibus te »! Et vere *magna* et *multa* et *abscondita,* sed promissa, quia, primae ad Corinthios secundo[^p158-3] et Isaiae sexagesimo quarto, « oculus non vidit, et auris non audivit, et in cor hominis non ascendit, quae praeparavit Deus diligentibus se ».

85. Tertio subiungit rationem motivam *ex parte exempli,* cum subdit: *Et eritis filii Altissimi,* scilicet per imitationem, secundum illud ad Ephesios quinto[^p158-4]: « Estote imitatores Dei, sicut filii carissimi ». Sicut filii debemus Deum imitari, quia accepimus *gratiam fidei,* de qua Ioannis primo[^p158-5]: « Dedit eis potestatem filios Dei fieri, his qui credunt in nomine eius ». Accepimus etiam ab eo *spiritum adoptionis filiorum,* secundum illud ad Romanos octavo: « Non accepistis spiritum servitutis in timore, sed spiritum adoptionis filiorum ». Et ideo debemus Deum imitari, sicut filius patrem, secundum illud Ioannis octavo[^p158-6]: « Si filii Abrahae estis, opera Abrahae facite ». — Et propterea ad exemplum eius debemus vivere; quod adiungit, cum addit: *Quia ipse benignus est super ingratos et malos,* secundum illud Ioelis secundo[^p158-7]: « Benignus et misericors est, patiens et multae misericordiae et praestabilis super malitia ». Et haec benignitas se ad ingratos et malos extendit; ad Romanos secundo: « An ignoras, quod Dei benignitas ad poenitentiam te adducit? Tu autem secundum duritiam tuam et impoenitens cor thesaurizas tibi iram in die irae et revelationis iusti iudicii Dei, qui reddit unicuique secundum opera eius ». Et propterea dicitur secundae ad Timotheum tertio[^p158-8]: « Mali et seductores proficient in peius »; Ecclesiastae octavo: « Etenim, quia non profert cito contra malos sententiam, filii hominum absque ullo timore perpetrant mala ». Ab his omnibus malis liberat et salvat divina benignitas, excedens nostram malitiam; ad Titum tertio[^p158-9]: « Apparuit benignitas et humanitas Salvatoris nostri Dei ».

86. (Vers. 36.). *Estote ergo misericordes* etc. Postquam evangelicas leges promulgavit et probavit et etiam resumsit cum ratione movente, hic tandem *concludit pro retributione consequente*[^p158-10], quam ostendit sequi opera nostra in *sententia iudiciali,* in *gloria caelesti* et in *poena infernali;* in *iudicio* quidem respectu universorum, in *praemio* respectu bonorum et in *supplicio* respectu peccatorum.

Concludit igitur primo, quod debemus praemissa mandata implere secundum formam praescriptam, *praedicendo iudicialem sententiam,* cum dicit: *Estote ergo misericordes,* scilicet in exhibendo vos proximis *benevolos, pacificos* et *beneficos;* in quo tangit mandatorum praedictorum *substantiam.* — *Sicut et Pater vester misericors est,* in quo tangit *formam* sive circumstantiam, quia ipse non tantum amicis, sed adversariis condescendit. Hoc quippe necessarium est, secundum illud Matthaei decimo octavo[^p158-11]: « Oportuit, et te misereri conservi tui, sicut et ego tui misertus sum ». Unde illam misericordiam exigit, quam impendit; et secundum illam quam impendimus, in iudicio rependit, secundum illud Ecclesiastici quarto: « In iudicando esto pupillis misericors ut pater, et pro viro matri illorum; et eris tu velut filius Altissimi, et miserebitur tui magis quam mater »; Iacobi secundo: « Iudicium sine misericordia ei qui non fecerit misericordiam ».

Et ideo addit *retributionem iudicii* correspondentem operibus meriti secundum quadruplicem differentiam actus, scilicet *iudicandi, condemnandi, dimittendi* et *donandi;* quorum primus est *aequitatis,* secundus *severitatis,* tertius *pietatis* et quartus *liberalitatis.*

87. (Vers. 37.). Primum notatur cum subdit: *Nolite iudicare, et non iudicabimini;* Matthaei septimo[^p158-12] dicitur: « In quo iudicio iudicaveritis,
<!-- page 159 -->
iudicabimini ». — Et quia non intelligit de quocumque iudicio, sed de eo quod a pietate deviat in crudelitatem; ideo adiungit: *Nolite condemnare, et non condemnabimini;* ad Romanos secundo[^p159-1]: « In quo alterum iudicas, te ipsum condemnas », quia, sicut dicitur Proverbiorum decimo septimo, « et qui iustificat impium, et qui condemnat iustum, uterque est abominabilis apud Deum ». — Unde revocat a iudicio iniquitatis et hortatur ad beneficium pietatis, quod consistit in *dimittendo* et *dando:* in dimittendo iniuriam et in dando indulgentias, vel in dimittendo mala commissa et in dando bona concessa. — Quantum ad *dimissionem malorum* adiungit: *Dimittite, et dimittetur vobis*[^p159-2]. Alioquin non salubriter oratis, Matthaei sexto: « Dimitte nobis debita nostra » etc.; et post: « Si non dimiseritis hominibus, nec Pater vester dimittet vobis peccata vestra ». Unde Ecclesiastici vigesimo octavo: « Relinque proximo nocenti te, et tunc deprecanti tibi peccata tua solventur ».

88. (Vers. 38.). Quantum ad *collationem bonorum* subditur: *Date, et dabitur vobis,* scilicet in iudicio, in quo dicetur illud Proverbiorum ultimo[^p159-3]: « Date illi de fructu manuum suarum » etc., quia, Matthaei vigesimo quinto, « habenti dabitur, et abundabit ». Illa enim sola homo secum habet, quae pro Deo dedit, secundum illud Ecclesiastici decimo septimo[^p159-4]: « Eleemosyna viri quasi sacculus cum ipso, et gratiam hominis quasi pupillam conservabit, et postea resurget et retribuet ». Et ideo hoc attendens Zachaeus dicebat, infra decimo nono: « Ecce, dimidium bonorum meorum do pauperibus ».

In summa nota hic, quod non prohibetur hic iudicium quodcumque, scilicet *deliberatum, ordinatum* et *iustum,* sed *usurpatum, temerarium* et *iniquum. Usurpatum* autem dico, quando non est officium nec auctoritas in iudicante; *temerarium,* quando est de re, quae non constat, sed procedit ex sola suspicione; *iniquum,* quando est contra ius divinitus approbatum; et tale iudicium hic prohibetur, non primum[^p159-5].

89. Secundo, *promittendo supercaelestem gloriam* secundum mensuram perfectam, cum adiungit: *Mensuram bonam* etc. Et nota, quod ponit hic quatuor conditiones secundum quatuor partes beatitudinis, quarum prima est in gaudio *interiori* in beatitudine animae; propter quam dicit: *Mensuram bonam,* quia, secundum Psalmum[^p159-6], tunc « replebitur in bonis desiderium tuum »; et dices cum Petro, Matthaei decimo septimo: « Domine, bonum est nos hic esse », quia eadem erit « mensura hominis tunc, quae et Angeli », Apocalypsis vigesimo primo. — Secunda, ex gaudio *inferiori* in glorificatione corporis; propter quod dicit: *Confertam*[^p159-7], a *con* et *farcio, farcis;* Iob quinto: « Ingredieris in abundantia sepulcrum, sicut infertur acervus in tempore suo ». — Tertia, ex gaudio *exteriori,* scilicet ex societate Sanctorum et renovatione omnium creaturarum; propter quod addit: *et coagitatam;* Iob vigesimo secundo[^p159-8]: « Argentum tuum et aurum coacervabitur tibi », id est, imperfecti et perfecti in merito omnes colligentur in unum gaudium. — Quarta, ex gaudio *superiori* in visione Dei; propter quod dicit: *et supereffluentem;* Iob vigesimo secundo[^p159-9]: « Tunc super Omnipotentem deliciis afflues ». — Et de hoc quadruplici gaudio in praemio aeterno potest exponi illud ad Ephesios tertio: « In caritate radicati et fundati, ut possitis comprehendere, quae sit longitudo » gaudii *interioris,* « latitudo » *exterioris,* « sublimitas » *superioris* et « profunditas » *inferioris.* — Hanc quidem mensuram *dabunt in sinum vestrum,* illi scilicet, quibus misericordiam facitis in praesenti, secundum illud quod infra decimo sexto dicitur[^p159-10]: « Facite vobis amicos de mammona iniquitatis » etc. Et tales sunt *pauperes,* quos fecit Dominus iudices; Iob trigesimo sexto: « Iudicium pauperibus tribuet ». Hanc *dabunt in sinum,* id est in animae capacitatem stabilem et intrinsecam; Iob decimo nono[^p159-11]: « Reposita est haec spes mea in sinu meo »; quam si vis habere, fac consilium Sapientis, Ecclesiastici vigesimo nono: « Conclude eleemosynam in sinu pauperis, et haec pro te exorabit ab omni malo ».

90. Tertio, *comminando infernalem poenam* secundum aequiparantiam ad culpam, cum adiungit: *Eadem quippe mensura, qua mensi fueritis,* scilicet
<!-- page 160 -->
nocendo vel male agendo sive demerendo, *remetietur*[^p160-1] *vobis,* iuxta illud Deuteronomii vigesimo quinto: « Iuxta mensuram delicti erit et plagarum modus »; et Apocalypsis decimo octavo: « Quantum se glorificavit et in deliciis fuit, tantum date ei tormentum et luctum »; et hoc est quod comminatur Dominus Ioelis tertio: « Cito velociter reddam vicissitudinem vobis in caput vestrum ».

91. Retribuit autem Dominus in mensura *aequali,* pensato *reatu,* sed in *maiori,* pensata *duratione,* quia cito transit delectatio, sed sempiterna manet punitio; Iob septimo[^p160-2]: « Sicut consumitur nubes et pertransit, sic qui descendit ad inferos non ascendet nec revertetur ultra in domum suam ». In *minori* vero, pensata *offensa,* quia qui contra omne bonum peccavit omni bono debet *privari,* secundum illud Threnorum tertio[^p160-3]: « Misericordiae Domini, quia non sumus consumti, quia non defecerunt miserationes eius ». — Rursus, qui peccat in bonum infinitum *intensive* et *extensive,* deberet utroque modo infinite puniri; et quia non potest creatura habere infinitatem *intensionis,* suscipit alteram, scilicet infinitatem *durationis* cum finitate *acerbitatis.* Unde Augustinus[^p160-4]: « Non actus temporalis facit, ut peccatum mortale aeternaliter puniatur, sed circumstantia illius, in quem peccatur »; qui cum sit aeternus, implentibus mandata bona promittit aeterna, peccantibus vero poenam comminatur aeternam ». Et ideo Augustinus: « Dignus est malo aeterno, qui in se perimit bonum, quod posset esse aeternum ». Et Gregorius[^p160-5] dicit hoc ipsum: « Iuste qui aeternaliter peccato perfrui volunt aeternaliter poenam inveniunt, et quorum voluptas non habuit finem peccandi non habebit finem torquendi ». — Licet igitur *poena* sit maior *voluptate* et minor *offensa,* attamen aequatur *reatui obligationis* necessario. Nec mirum, si actionem temporalem comitatur reatus perpetuus, quia in contractu temporalis est actio, et tamen ius perpetuum acquirit emptori[^p160-6]; vulneratio etiam temporalis ad mortem deducit perpetuam, et bellum diurnum acquirit perpetuam servitutem, et temporale peccatum exsilium sempiternum.

## English
<!-- page 156 -->

77. *And if you love them*[^p156-3] etc. Having promulgated the evangelical laws under a fitting perfection, he here, secondly, *proves them from a deficient condition,* where he shows that he had to enjoin according to the manner set down before; otherwise there is no merit with God. Whence he shows here three things, namely that it does not suffice for merit to be *benevolent* in respect of *those who love,* nor *beneficent* only in respect of *those who make retribution,* nor *communicative* in respect of *those who recompense.* And in this he shows that perfect benevolence and beneficence must extend themselves not only to *friends,* but also to *adversaries,* according to the form promulgated above[^p156-4].

78. (Vers. 32.). He shows, then, first, that it does not suffice to be *benevolent only in respect of those who love,* because this belongs to sinners; on account of which he says: *And if you love them that love you,* that is, because they love you, *what thanks are to you?* that is, what merit worthy of retribution? as if to say: none. And he gives the reason: *For sinners also love those that love them,* who have no merit with God; for this is *natural,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirteenth chapter[^p156-5]: « Every beast loveth its like: so also every man him that is nearest to himself » etc.; or even *carnal,* as in Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-seventh chapter: « A companion condoleth with his friend for his belly's sake, and he will take up a shield against the enemy ». Such dilection has no merit with God, because it is not spent for God's sake. For if it were spent for God's sake, a man would indeed love *those who love him,* according to that of Proverbs, the eighth chapter[^p156-6]: « I love them that love me »; he would even repay *those who love less* with more dilection, according to that of the second to the Corinthians, the twelfth chapter: « But I most gladly will spend and be spent myself for your souls, although, loving you more, I be loved less »; likewise, he would love even those who *in no way* love, according to that of the first of John, the fourth chapter[^p156-7]: « In this appeared the charity of God: not as though we had loved God, but because he first loved us »; and to the Romans, the fifth chapter: « But God commendeth his charity towards us; because, when as yet we were sinners, Christ died for the ungodly ». — Whence, when it extends only to *those who love,* it is not a divine nor a gratuitous dilection, but a mercenary and human one, and by this very fact not worthy of retribution either.

79. (Vers. 33.). Nor does it suffice to be *beneficent in respect of those who make retribution,* because this too belongs to the worldly themselves; on account of which he adds: *And if you do good to them who do good to you;* the Gloss[^p156-8]: « That is, for this reason, because they do good »; *what thanks are to you?* that is, what merit? as if to say: none. And the reason is given: *For sinners also do this;* for a benefit done for the sake of recompense is not gratuitous, but venal, and therefore not acceptable either. Whence Ecclesiasticus, the twentieth chapter[^p156-9]: « The gift of the fool shall do thee no good; his eyes are sevenfold; he will give a few things, and upbraid much ». But that which is given liberally and cheerfully, that is accepted, according to that of the second to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter: « Every one as he hath determined in his heart, not with sadness, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver ». If, then, to do good to those who do good is only *due,* and to do good to those who do not do good is *gratuitous;* it seems that to do evil to those who do good is *impious* and most cruel; which indeed the sinner does when he offends God; according to which in the second of Paralipomenon,
<!-- page 157 -->
the thirty-second chapter[^p157-1], it is said of Ezechias that « he did not render again according to the benefits which he had received from God, for his heart was lifted up ». Whence Christ can say that of the Psalm: « They repaid me evil things for good », and the sinner can say to Christ that of the first of Kings, the twenty-fourth chapter: « Thou hast given me good things, and I have rendered thee evil things ». And by that very fact, if he wills to be reconciled to the Lord, it is needful that he do good not only to one who does him good, but also to one who opposes him; and such is the benefit of charity, according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the thirteenth chapter[^p157-2]: « Charity is patient, is kind ».

80. (Vers. 34.). Nor does it suffice to be *communicative in respect of those who recompense,* because this belongs to the lenders upon usury themselves; whence also he subjoins: *And if you lend to them of whom you hope to receive,* namely recompense; *what thanks are to you?* as if to say: none. And this he shows: *For sinners also lend to sinners,* that is, they lend out at usury; whence he adds: *For to receive as much,* namely for service, not only for the debt. And because they receive a reward, they have no merit with God; whence in the Psalm[^p157-3]: « Who shall ascend into the mountain of the Lord »? and it is added: « He that hath not put out his money to usury, nor taken bribes against the innocent ». Otherwise he does not ascend into the mountain of the Lord; whence in Ezechiel, the eighteenth chapter[^p157-4], that man is justified of whom it is said: « He that hath not lent upon usury, nor taken more »; and the contrary man, who takes more, is reprobated in the same place. Whence to such can be said that of Aggeus, the first chapter[^p157-5]: « You have looked for more, and behold it became less », because in the Psalm it is said: « The men of riches have found nothing in their hands ». Such lenders upon usury are counted among sinners, because they are oppressors of others; whence Proverbs, the twenty-second chapter[^p157-6]: « He that taketh a loan is servant to him that lendeth upon usury ». And on this account it is forbidden in Exodus, the twenty-second chapter: « If thou lend money to the poor, thou shalt not be hard upon him as an extortioner, nor oppress him with usuries ».

81. But the contrary of this seems to be said in Deuteronomy, the twenty-third chapter[^p157-7]: « Thou shalt not lend upon usury to thy brother, but to the stranger ». — But it must be understood that this is evil *in itself;* but the Law permitted them this, as also the bill of divorce, for the avoidance of a greater evil[^p157-8]. — But the reason of this prohibition is manifold: both because money is not made worse through lending; and because of itself it does not fructify, but the whole account of its utility is on the side of the one using it; and because it continually consumes and lays waste, and so the advantage of the loan is turned into the disadvantage of loss; and because in a loan of money the ownership of the thing lent passes over, since one is not bound to return the same[^p157-9] in number; and because the whole risk is transferred to him who had borrowed; and also because in such a manner of lending the coin is loved more than the neighbour, while one lends coins to the neighbour not out of love of the neighbour, but out of love of the coin.

82. (Vers. 35.). *But love ye your enemies* etc. Here, after the promulgation and the proof, he resumes *the commandments approved, with a moving reason.* Now there are three things which ought to move us to doing the things aforesaid, namely *the authority of the precept, the immensity of the reward* and *the sublimity of the example.* The first moves the irascible, the second the concupiscible, but the third the rational.

He resumes, then, the aforesaid lessons by *way of precept,* when he says: *But love ye your enemies and*[^p157-10] *do good,* namely so that you love « not in word nor in tongue, but in deed and in truth », the first of John, the third chapter. And he says this by commanding, because, as is said in John, the fifteenth chapter, « this is my precept, that you love one another, as I have loved you ». But he loved not only in affection, but also in effect, without any hope of retribution. — Therefore it is added: *And lend, hoping for nothing thereby,* namely of recompense; Deuteronomy, the twenty-third chapter[^p157-11]: « To thy brother thou shalt lend without usury that which he wanteth, that the Lord may bless thee », that is, do not seek retribution for this from man, as usurers do, who for the hope of usury lose the hope of eternal life; against whom the Apostle, in the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter[^p157-12], says: « If in this life only we are hoping, we are of all men most miserable ». Such are the rich who hope in riches; of such « the hope is vain, and their labours without fruit », Wisdom, the third chapter. And therefore Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-first chapter: « Blessed is the rich man that is found without blemish: and that hath not gone after gold, nor hoped in money nor in treasures. Who is he, and we will praise him? for he hath done marvellous things in his life ».

83. Note that *hope in a loan* can be for a like *service,* or for *gain.* In the first manner it is lawful;
<!-- page 158 -->
in the second manner it can be twofold: either *express* by a pact, and this is simply forbidden, because it makes a usurer; or *tacit* in the heart, and in this manner twofold: either *principally moving* to the loan, and this is likewise forbidden, because it is against the divine commandment; or *not principally moving,* but as it were annexed, because, although he does not do it principally for advantage or gain, he nevertheless believes that the one receiving will not be ungrateful to him, and therefore does it more willingly. But this is not such a hope of advantage as is against the divine commandment[^p158-1].

84. Secondly he subjoins the moving reason *on the part of the reward,* when he adds: *And your reward shall be much;* Ecclesiasticus, the twelfth chapter[^p158-2]: « Do good to the just, and thou shalt find great retribution: and if not of him, assuredly of the Lord ». And the retribution is called *much* and *great,* because the eternal reward contains all goods in excellence, according to that of the Psalm: « O how great is the multitude of thy sweetness, O Lord, which thou hast hidden for them that fear thee »! And truly *great* and *much* and *hidden,* but promised, because, in the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p158-3], and in Isaias, the sixty-fourth chapter, « eye hath not seen, nor ear heard, neither hath it entered into the heart of man, what things God hath prepared for them that love him ».

85. Thirdly he subjoins the moving reason *on the part of the example,* when he adds: *And you shall be the sons of the Highest,* namely by imitation, according to that of the Ephesians, the fifth chapter[^p158-4]: « Be ye imitators of God, as most dear children ». As sons we ought to imitate God, because we have received *the grace of faith,* of which John, the first chapter[^p158-5]: « He gave them power to be made the sons of God, to them that believe in his name ». We have received from him also *the spirit of adoption of sons,* according to that of the Romans, the eighth chapter: « You have not received the spirit of bondage in fear, but the spirit of adoption of sons ». And therefore we ought to imitate God, as a son his father, according to that of John, the eighth chapter[^p158-6]: « If you be the sons of Abraham, do the works of Abraham ». — And on this account we ought to live according to his example; which he adds, when he subjoins: *For he is kind to the unthankful and to the evil,* according to that of Joel, the second chapter[^p158-7]: « He is kind and merciful, patient and rich in mercy, and ready to repent of the evil ». And this kindness extends itself to the unthankful and the evil; to the Romans, the second chapter: « Knowest thou not that the kindness of God leadeth thee to penance? But according to thy hardness and impenitent heart, thou treasurest up to thyself wrath, against the day of wrath and revelation of the just judgement of God, who will render to every man according to his works ». And on this account it is said in the second to Timothy, the third chapter[^p158-8]: « Evil men and seducers shall grow worse and worse »; Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter: « For because sentence is not speedily pronounced against the evil, the children of men commit evils without any fear ». From all these evils the divine kindness frees and saves, exceeding our malice; to Titus, the third chapter[^p158-9]: « The kindness and humanity of our Saviour God appeared ».

86. (Vers. 36.). *Be ye therefore merciful* etc. Having promulgated and proved the evangelical laws, and also resumed them with a moving reason, he here at last *concludes for the consequent retribution*[^p158-10], which he shows follows our works in the *judicial sentence,* in *heavenly glory* and in *infernal punishment;* in the *judgement,* indeed, in respect of all, in the *reward* in respect of the good, and in the *penalty* in respect of sinners.

He concludes, then, first, that we ought to fulfil the foregoing commandments according to the form prescribed, *by foretelling the judicial sentence,* when he says: *Be ye therefore merciful,* namely in showing yourselves *benevolent, peaceable* and *beneficent* to your neighbours; in which he touches the *substance* of the aforesaid commandments. — *As your Father also is merciful,* in which he touches the *form* or circumstance, because he condescends not only to friends, but to adversaries. This indeed is necessary, according to that of Matthew, the eighteenth chapter[^p158-11]: « It behoved thee also to have mercy on thy fellow servant, even as I had mercy on thee ». Whence he exacts that mercy which he spends; and according to that which we spend, he repays in the judgement, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the fourth chapter: « In judging be merciful to the fatherless as a father, and as a husband to their mother; and thou shalt be as a son of the most High, and he will have mercy on thee more than a mother »; James, the second chapter: « Judgement without mercy to him that hath not done mercy ».

And therefore he adds *the retribution of the judgement* corresponding to the works of merit according to a fourfold difference of act, namely of *judging, condemning, forgiving* and *giving;* of which the first is of *equity,* the second of *severity,* the third of *piety* and the fourth of *liberality.*

87. (Vers. 37.). The first is noted when he subjoins: *Judge not, and you shall not be judged;* in Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p158-12], it is said: « For with what judgement you judge,
<!-- page 159 -->
you shall be judged ». — And because he does not mean any judgement whatever, but that which deviates from piety into cruelty; therefore he adds: *Condemn not, and you shall not be condemned;* to the Romans, the second chapter[^p159-1]: « Wherein thou judgest another, thou condemnest thyself », because, as is said in Proverbs, the seventeenth chapter, « he that justifieth the wicked, and he that condemneth the just, both are abominable before God ». — Whence he calls back from the judgement of iniquity and exhorts to the benefit of piety, which consists in *forgiving* and *giving:* in forgiving injury and in giving indulgences, or in forgiving evils committed and in giving goods granted. — As to the *forgiving of evils* he adds: *Forgive, and it shall be forgiven you*[^p159-2]. Otherwise you do not pray wholesomely, Matthew, the sixth chapter: « Forgive us our debts » etc.; and afterward: « If you will not forgive men, neither will your Father forgive you your sins ». Whence Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-eighth chapter: « Forgive thy neighbour if he hath hurt thee: and then shall thy sins be forgiven to thee when thou prayest ».

88. (Vers. 38.). As to the *conferring of goods* it is subjoined: *Give, and it shall be given to you,* namely in the judgement, in which that of Proverbs, the last chapter[^p159-3], will be said: « Give her of the fruit of her hands » etc., because, Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter, « to him that hath shall be given, and he shall abound ». For a man has with him only those things which he gave for God, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the seventeenth chapter[^p159-4]: « The alms of a man is as a purse with him, and shall preserve the grace of a man as the apple of the eye: and afterward he shall rise up, and shall render retribution ». And therefore, attending to this, Zacheus said, below, in the nineteenth chapter: « Behold, the half of my goods I give to the poor ».

In sum, note here that not every judgement whatever is forbidden here, namely the *deliberate, ordered* and *just,* but the *usurped, rash* and *iniquitous.* Now I call it *usurped* when there is no office nor authority in the one judging; *rash,* when it is about a matter which is not established, but proceeds from suspicion alone; *iniquitous,* when it is against right divinely approved; and such judgement is forbidden here, not the first[^p159-5].

89. Secondly, *by promising supercelestial glory* according to a perfect measure, when he adds: *Good measure* etc. And note that he sets down here four conditions according to four parts of beatitude, of which the first is in the *interior* joy in the beatitude of the soul; on account of which he says: *Good measure,* because, according to the Psalm[^p159-6], then « thy desire shall be filled with good things »; and thou shalt say with Peter, Matthew, the seventeenth chapter: « Lord, it is good for us to be here », because the same will be « the measure of a man then, which is also of an Angel », Apocalypse, the twenty-first chapter. — The second, from the *inferior* joy in the glorification of the body; on account of which he says: *Pressed down*[^p159-7], from *con* and *farcio, farcis;* Job, the fifth chapter: « Thou shalt enter into the grave in abundance, as a heap is brought in its season ». — The third, from the *exterior* joy, namely from the society of the Saints and the renewal of all creatures; on account of which he adds: *and shaken together;* Job, the twenty-second chapter[^p159-8]: « Thy silver and gold shall be heaped together for thee », that is, the imperfect and the perfect in merit shall all be gathered into one joy. — The fourth, from the *superior* joy in the vision of God; on account of which he says: *and running over;* Job, the twenty-second chapter[^p159-9]: « Then shalt thou abound in delights in the Almighty ». — And of this fourfold joy in the eternal reward that of the Ephesians, the third chapter, can be expounded: « Rooted and founded in charity, that you may be able to comprehend what is the length » of the *interior* joy, « the breadth » of the *exterior,* « the height » of the *superior* and « the depth » of the *inferior.* — This measure indeed *shall they give into your bosom,* those, namely, to whom you do mercy in the present, according to that which is said below, in the sixteenth chapter[^p159-10]: « Make unto you friends of the mammon of iniquity » etc. And such are the *poor,* whom the Lord has made judges; Job, the thirty-sixth chapter: « He will give judgement to the poor ». This *shall they give into your bosom,* that is, into the stable and intrinsic capacity of the soul; Job, the nineteenth chapter[^p159-11]: « This my hope is laid up in my bosom »; which if thou wilt have, take the counsel of the Wise man, Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-ninth chapter: « Shut up alms in the bosom of the poor, and it shall entreat for thee from all evil ».

90. Thirdly, *by threatening infernal punishment* according to equiparation to the fault, when he adds: *For with the same measure that you shall mete withal,* namely
<!-- page 160 -->
by harming or doing evil or demeriting, *it shall be measured to you again*[^p160-1], according to that of Deuteronomy, the twenty-fifth chapter: « According to the measure of the sin shall the measure also of the stripes be »; and the Apocalypse, the eighteenth chapter: « As much as she hath glorified herself and lived in delicacies, so much torment and sorrow give ye to her »; and this is what the Lord threatens in Joel, the third chapter: « I will very speedily return you a recompense upon your own head ».

91. Now the Lord makes retribution in an *equal* measure, the *guilt* being weighed, but in a *greater* one, the *duration* being weighed, because delight passes quickly, but the punishment remains everlasting; Job, the seventh chapter[^p160-2]: « As a cloud is consumed and passeth away, so he that shall go down to hell shall not come up, nor shall he return any more into his house ». But in a *lesser* one, the *offence* being weighed, because he who has sinned against every good ought to be *deprived* of every good, according to that of Lamentations, the third chapter[^p160-3]: « The mercies of the Lord, that we are not consumed: because his commiserations have not failed ». — Again, he who sins against the infinite good *intensively* and *extensively* ought to be punished infinitely in both ways; and because a creature cannot have the infinity of *intension,* it undergoes the other, namely the infinity of *duration* with a finiteness of *bitterness.* Whence Augustine[^p160-4]: « Not the temporal act brings it about that a mortal sin is punished eternally, but the circumstance of him against whom one sins »; who, since he is eternal, promises eternal goods to those who fulfil the commandments, but threatens eternal punishment to those who sin ». And therefore Augustine: « He is worthy of eternal evil who destroys in himself the good which could be eternal ». And Gregory[^p160-5] says this very thing: « Justly do those who will to enjoy sin eternally find punishment eternally, and those whose pleasure had no end of sinning will have no end of torment ». — Although, then, the *punishment* is greater than the *pleasure* and less than the *offence,* yet it is necessarily made equal to the *guilt of the obligation.* Nor is it a marvel if a perpetual guilt accompanies a temporal action, because in a contract the action is temporal, and yet it acquires a perpetual right for the buyer[^p160-6]; a temporal wounding also leads to perpetual death, and a war of a day acquires perpetual servitude, and a temporal sin an everlasting exile.
