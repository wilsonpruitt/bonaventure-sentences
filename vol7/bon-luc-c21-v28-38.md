# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XXI, Vers. 28-38
## *His autem fieri incipientibus etc.*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 536 -->

48. (Vers. 28.). *His autem fieri incipientibus* etc. Postquam discipulos erudivit ad providentiam, hic secundo[^p536-5] *erigit ad confidentiam,* et hoc quidem facit tripliciter, scilicet *exhortatione consolativa, similitudine manuductiva* et *assertione certificativa.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *exhortationem consolatoriam* dicit: *His autem fieri incipientibus, respicite et levate capita vestra;* Gregorius[^p536-6]: « *Levate capita,* exhilarate corda, quia, dum finitur mundus, cuius amici non estis, prope est redemptio, quam quaesiistis ». *Respicite* igitur per veram fidem, secundum illud supra decimo octavo[^p536-7]: « Respice, fides tua te salvum fecit ». *Respicite,* inquam, futura, quae non videntur, secundum illud secundae ad Corinthios quarto: « Non contemplantibus nobis quae videntur, sed quae non videntur. Quae enim videntur » etc. Et hoc quidem fit per *fidem,* de qua ad Hebraeos undecimo: « Fides est substantia rerum sperandarum, argumentum non apparentium ». — *Levate* autem per *spem;* et rationem huius subdit: *Quoniam appropinquat redemptio vestra,* ad quam spes vestra suspirat. Unde ad Romanos octavo[^p536-8]: « Creatura ingemiscit et parturit usque adhuc. Non solum autem illa, sed et nos ipsi primitias spiritus habentes, et ipsi intra nos gemimus, adoptionem filiorum Dei exspectantes, redemptionem corporis nostri. Spe enim salvi facti sumus ». Haec autem spes facit, tribulationes patienter ferri; ad Romanos duodecimo[^p536-9]: « Spe gaudentes, in tribulatione patientes »; et ratio huius, ad Romanos octavo, quod « non sunt condignae passiones huius temporis ad futuram gloriam, quae revelabitur in nobis. Nam exspectatio creaturae revelationem filiorum Dei exspectat ».

49. Debent igitur viri spirituales *levare capita* per firmam exspectationem; debent etiam *levare oculos* per claram considerationem, secundum illud Psalmi[^p536-10]: « Ad te levavi oculos meos » etc.; et Genesis decimo tertio: « Leva oculos tuos in directum ». « Omnem terram, quam conspicis, tibi dabo » etc. Debent *levare vocem* per Scripturae praedicationem; Isaiae quinquagesimo secundo[^p536-11]: « Vox speculatorum tuorum, levaverunt vocem, simul laudabunt » etc. Debent *levare manus* ad perfectam operationem; Psalmus[^p536-12]: « Sic benedicam te in vita mea et in nomine tuo levabo manus meas ». Et hoc est quod maxime nos sursum erigit, propter quod primae ad Timotheum secundo: « Volo, vos orare in omni loco, levantes puras manus sine omni ira et disceptatione ».

50. (Vers. 29. 30.). Secundo quantum ad *similitudinem manuductivam* subditur: *Et dixit illis similitudinem: Videte ficulneam et omnes arbores: cum producunt iam ex se fructum, scitis, quoniam*[^p536-13] *prope est aestas.* Comparat autem *arbores* humano generi, et maxime *ficulneam.* Arboribus enim huiusmodi homo assimilatur et quantum ad *ortum* et quantum ad *occasum* et quantum ad *fructum.* Quantum ad *ortum,* propter quod in Psalmo[^p536-14]: « Erit tanquam lignum, quod plantatum est secus decursus aquarum » etc. Quantum ad *occasum,* propter quod Iob decimo quarto: « Lignum habet spem; si praecisum fuerit, rursum virescit, et rami eius pullulant »; sic et homo, cum moritur, spem habet resurgendi. Quantum ad *fructum,* quia, sicut arbor infructuosa inciditur, sic et homo; unde Matthaei tertio[^p536-15]: « Omnis arbor, quae non facit fructum bonum, excidetur » etc.; unde supra decimo tertio dicitur de ficu infructuosa: « Succide illam » etc. Sicut enim arbor secus viam non fructificat, sic et homo occupatus circa multa; Matthaei
<!-- page 537 -->
vigesimo primo[^p537-1]: « Videns fici arborem secus viam, venit ad eam et nihil invenit in ea nisi folia tantum. Et ait illi: Nunquam ex te fructus nascatur ». Unde Chrysostomus[^p537-2]: « Sicut impossibile est, arborem secus viam fructus ad maturitatem servare; sic difficile est, hominem iuxta mundum viventem iuventutem immaculatam usque ad finem tenere ». Sicut etiam arbores fructificant, abscedente hieme; sic homo fructum habet post tribulationem; Canticorum secundo[^p537-3]: « Iam hiems transiit, imber abiit et recessit »; « ficus protulit grossos suos, vineae florentes dederunt odorem suum ». Hic fructus est per tolerantiam tribulationis, secundum illud ad Hebraeos duodecimo: « Omnis disciplina in praesenti non est gaudii, sed moeroris, postea autem affert fructum pacatissimum exercitatis per eam ».

51. (Vers. 31.). Et hic fructus colligitur in aestate claritatis aeternae; propter quod subdit: *Ita et vos, cum videritis haec fieri, scitote, quoniam*[^p537-4] *prope est regnum Dei;* Glossa: « *Regnum Dei* aestati comparat, quia tunc moeroris nostri nubila transibunt, et aeterni dies vitae sub claritate solis fulgebunt ». In hac aestate cessabit *pluvia lacrymarum;* Isaiae vigesimo quinto[^p537-5]: « Aufert Dominus Deus omnem lacrymam ab omni facie »; Psalmus: « Euntes ibant et flebant » etc. Cessabit *nubilum tribulationum;* Tobiae tertio[^p537-6]: « Post tempestatem tranquillum facis et post lacrymationem et fletum exsultationem infundis » etc. Cessabit etiam *frigus cupiditatum;* unde Bernardus[^p537-7]: « O vera meridies, plenitudo fervoris et lucis, solis statio, umbrarum exterminatio, desiccatio paludum, foetorum depulsio! O perenne solstitium, quando iam non inclinabitur dies; o lumen meridianum, o vernalis temperies, o aestiva venustas, o autumnalis ubertas! Et ne quid videar praeteriisse: O quies et feriatio hiemalis! aut certe, si hoc magis probas, sola tunc *hiems abiit et recessit* »!

52. (Vers. 32.). Tertio quantum ad *assertionem certificativam* subiungit: *Amen dico vobis, quod*[^p537-8] *non praeteribit generatio haec, donec omnia fiant;* Glossa: « Quae dico vobis sub certitudine exspectate ». *Hanc* autem *generationem* vocat generationem mortalem, secundum illud Ecclesiastae primo[^p537-9]: « Generatio praeterit, et generatio advenit ». Haec *praeterit,* quando transit ad immortalitatem per resurrectionem; Glossa[^p537-10]: « De mortalitate ad immortalitatem ». Et haec resurrectio non erit ante praedictorum consummationem, quia, Iob decimo quarto, « homo, cum dormierit, non resurget, donec atteratur caelum ».

53. (Vers. 33.). Et ideo subdit: *Caelum et terra transibunt,* quantum ad *exteriorem figuram;* Psalmus[^p537-11]: « Tu in principio, Domine, terram fundasti, et opera manuum tuarum sunt caeli. Ipsi peribunt, tu autem permanes » etc.; et ideo primae ad Corinthios septimo: « Praeterit figura huius mundi ». Unde Isaiae quinquagesimo primo: « Caeli sicut fumus liquescent, et terra sicut vestimentum atteretur »; et Isaiae sexagesimo quinto: « Ecce, ego creo caelos novos et terram novam ». — Et ideo transeunt haec omnia sicut temporalia, sed econtra, *verba autem mea non transibunt,* quantum ad *interiorem sententiam;* Psalmus[^p537-12]: « In aeternum, Domine, permanet verbum tuum ». Unde Gregorius: « Omne, quod apud vos durabile et sine mutatione est, ad aeternitatem durabile non est; et omne, quod apud me transire cernitur, fixum et sine transitu tenetur ». Unde Isaiae quadragesimo[^p537-13]: « Exsiccatum est foenum, et cecidit flos; verbum autem Domini nostri manet in aeternum ». Qui vult igitur aeternaliter manere, oportet, quod in transitoriis non moretur, sed potius deserendo transitoria per divina verba transeat ad aeterna.

54. Propter quod notandum, quod mundus iste transit quantum ad omne quod habet in se appetibile. Transit primo quoad *celsitudinem;* Sapientiae
<!-- page 538 -->
quinto[^p538-1]: « Quid nobis profuit superbia, aut divitiarum iactantia quid contulit nobis? Transierunt omnia » etc.; Psalmus: « Vidi impium superexaltatum » etc. — Secundo quoad *pulcritudinem;* Psalmus[^p538-2]: « Mane sicut herba transeat, mane floreat et transeat »; et Iacobi primo: « Glorietur frater humilis in exaltatione sua, dives autem in humilitate sua, quia sicut flos foeni transibit. Exortus est enim sol cum ardore et arefecit foenum ». — Tertio quoad *dulcedinem;* primae Ioannis secundo[^p538-3]: « Mundus transit et concupiscentia eius »; Proverbiorum quinto: « Favus distillans labia meretricis ». — Ergo qui his transitoriis adhaeret triplicem facit transitum. Primus est *a culpa in culpam;* Sapientiae secundo[^p538-4]: « Coronemus nos rosis, antequam marcescant; nullum sit pratum, quod non pertranseat luxuria nostra » etc. Secundus est *a culpa in poenam;* Sapientiae secundo[^p538-5]: « Umbrae transitus est tempus nostrum »; et Iob septimo: « Dies mei velocius transierunt, quam a texente tela succiditur ». Tertius est *a poena in poenam;* Iob vigesimo quarto[^p538-6]: « Transibunt ab aquis nivium ad nimium calorem ». — Qui autem transit a transitoriis triplicem facit transitum. Primus est *a culpa ad poenitentiam;* et de hoc Actuum duodecimo[^p538-7]: « Transeuntes primam et secundam custodiam venerunt ad portam ferream »; et hic designatus fuit in transitu *maris rubri,* Exodi decimo quarto. Secundus est *a poenitentia ad sapientiam;* Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto[^p538-8]: « Transite ad me omnes, qui concupiscitis me, et a generationibus meis adimplemini »; et hic designatur in transitu *per desertum,* in quo filii Israel gustaverunt manna, Exodi decimo sexto. Tertius est *a sapientia ad vitam aeternam;* Psalmus[^p538-9]: « Transivimus per ignem et aquam » etc.; et Ioannis decimo tertio: « Sciens, quia venit hora eius » etc.; et hic designatur in transitu *Iordanis,* quo ingressi sunt terram promissionis; Iosue quarto: « Festinavit populus, ut transiret; cumque transissent omnes, transivit et arca Dei ». — Qui hunc triplicem facit transitum, ille solus est Hebraeus et celebrat verum pascha; quia *pascha* nihil aliud est quam transitus[^p538-10].

55. (Vers. 34.). *Attendite autem vobis* etc. Postquam erudivit discipulos ad providentiam et erexit ad confidentiam, hic tertio[^p538-11] *excitat ad vigilantiam;* quod quidem facit quadrupliciter, scilicet *dissuadendo contrarium, insinuando periculum, persuadendo propositum* et *introducendo exemplum.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *dissuasionem oppositi vigilantiae* dicit: *Attendite autem vobis,* per vigilantiam, *ne forte graventur corda vestra,* per somnolentiam; Sapientiae nono[^p538-12]: « Corpus, quod corrumpitur, animam aggravat »; *in crapula et ebrietate et curis huius vitae,* per excessum in gula; Ecclesiastici trigesimo septimo: « Propter crapulam multi perierunt, qui autem abstinens est adiiciet vitam ». Huiusmodi autem superfluitas ineptos nos reddit ad vigilandum circa *studium veritatis;* Proverbiorum vigesimo[^p538-13]: « Luxuriosa res est vinum, et tumultuosa ebrietas. Quicumque his delectatur, non erit sapiens »; et ideo Ecclesiastae secundo: « Cogitavi a vino abstrahere carnem meam; ut transferrem animam meam » etc. Item, circa *exercitium virtutis;* ad Romanos decimo tertio[^p538-14]: « Sicut in die honeste ambulemus, non in comessationibus et ebrietatibus » etc.; et ad Philippenses tertio: « Multi ambulant, quos saepe dicebam vobis; quorum finis interitus, quorum Deus venter est, et gloria in confusione ipsorum »; et ad Romanos decimo sexto: « Huiusmodi Christo non serviunt, sed suo ventri ». Item, circa *excessum contemplationis;* ad Ephesios quinto[^p538-15]: « Nolite inebriari vino, in quo est luxuria, sed implemini Spiritu sancto, loquentes vobis » etc.; ad Hebraeos decimo tertio: « Optimum est gratia stabilire cor, non escis »; quia, ad Romanos decimo quarto, « regnum Dei non est esca et potus, sed iustitia et pax » etc.

56. Secundo quantum ad *insinuationem periculi* subdit: *Et superveniat in vos repentina dies illa,* propter inconsideratum eventum. *Repente* enim evenit quod non praevidetur; Isaiae trigesimo[^p538-16]: « Subito, dum non speratur, veniet contritio eius ». Venit enim ille dies iudicii repente his qui se non praeparaverunt contra ipsum; supra duodecimo: « Si dixerit servus ille: Moram facit dominus meus venire, et coeperit edere et bibere et inebriari; veniet dominus servi illius in die, qua non sperat, et hora, qua nescit; et dividet eum partemque eius ponet cum infidelibus ».

57. (Vers. 35.). Et quantum ad tales subdit: *Tanquam laqueus enim superveniet in omnes, qui sedent super faciem omnis terrae,* id est super omnes, qui terrenis incumbunt. Unde Ieremiae
<!-- page 539 -->
quadragesimo octavo[^p539-1]: « Pavor et fovea et laqueus super te, o habitator Moab », per quem quidem *carnales* intelliguntur, qui super terram sedent, de quibus in Psalmo[^p539-2]: « Quoniam humiliata est in pulvere anima nostra, conglutinatus est in terra venter noster »; Ezechielis vigesimo sexto: « In terra sedebunt, et attoniti super repentino casu tuo, admirabuntur ». De hoc autem posuit Dominus supra decimo septimo[^p539-3] duplex exemplum, scilicet de diebus Noe et de diebus Lot; quia primi perierunt *diluvio,* secundi *incendio.* Et de talibus Iob vigesimo primo: « Domus eorum securae sunt et pacatae ». « Tenent tympanum et citharam et gaudent ad sonitum organi, ducunt in bonis dies suos et in puncto ad inferna descendunt ».

58. (Vers. 36.). Tertio quantum ad *persuasionem propositi* subiungit: *Vigilate itaque, omni tempore*[^p539-4] *orantes.* Attendendum est autem, quod simul iungit *vigilantiam* et *orationem.* Nam *vigilare* oportet contra *hostem extrinsecum,* scilicet diabolum; primae Petri ultimo[^p539-5]: « Sobrii estote et vigilate, quia adversarius vester diabolus » etc.; et contra *domesticum,* scilicet carnem; et ideo Ecclesiastici trigesimo primo: « Vigilia honestatis tabefaciet carnes ». *Vigilare* oportet ad *profectum virtutis;* secundae ad Corinthios sexto[^p539-6]: « In omnibus exhibeamus nosmetipsos sicut Dei ministros in multa patientia, in vigiliis multis » etc. — *Vigilare* oportet per *desiderium contemplationis;* Canticorum quinto[^p539-7]: « Ego dormio, et cor meum vigilat »; et Isaiae vigesimo sexto: « Anima mea desideravit te in nocte » etc. Et quoniam nec possumus *hostes revincere* nec *in bono proficere* nec *consolationes supernas habere* nisi per donum gratiae divinae, ideo oportet *orare;* et propterea Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p539-8]: « Vigilate et orate, ut non intretis in tentationem »; et Ecclesiastici trigesimo nono: « Iustus cor suum tradet ad vigilandum diluculo » etc. — Quia igitur oportet vigilare propter *declinationem mali,* ideo addit: *Ut digni habeamini fugere ista omnia, quae futura sunt,* secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo quarto[^p539-9]: « Orate autem, ne fiat fuga vestra in hieme vel Sabbato ». Et hoc orabat Propheta in Psalmo: « Domine, exaudi orationem meam, auribus percipe obsecrationem meam » etc. — Oportet vigilare etiam propter *adeptionem boni,* et ideo addit: *Et stare ante Filium hominis;* Glossa[^p539-10]: « Haec est summa beatitudinis, in praesentia iudicis securum consistere »; Iob vigesimo tertio: « Quis mihi tribuat, ut cognoscam et inveniam illum et veniam usque ad solium eius »? Et ideo Apocalypsis septimo: « Hi sunt, qui venerunt de tribulatione magna et laverunt stolas suas in sanguine Agni. Ideo sunt ante thronum Dei et serviunt ei die ac nocte in templo eius ». Et hic semper est orandum; unde in Psalmo[^p539-11]: « Sitivit anima mea ad Deum, fontem vivum; quando veniam et apparebo ante faciem Dei »!

59. (Vers. 37.). Quarto quantum ad *introductionem exempli* subiungitur: *Erat autem diebus docens in templo,* ut vigilans circa *exercitium activae,* ut exemplum daret ad exercitium actionis perfectae, secundum illud secundae ad Timotheum quarto[^p539-12]: « Praedica verbum, insta opportune, importune » etc.; et iterum ibidem: « Tu vero vigila, in omnibus labora » etc. — *Noctibus vero exiens, morabatur in monte, qui vocatur Oliveti,* ut vigilans circa *otium contemplativae,* secundum illud Threnorum secundo[^p539-13]: « Consurge in nocte, in principio vigiliarum, effunde sicut aquam cor tuum ». Et ex his duobus exemplum dabat perfectae vigilantiae in activa et contemplativa cunctis praelatis, secundum illud Canticorum tertio[^p539-14]: « En lectulum Salomonis ambiunt sexaginta fortes ex fortissimis Israel, omnes tenentes gladios et ad bella doctissimi »; et Isaiae vigesimo primo: « Super speculam Domini ego sum, stans iugiter per diem, et super custodiam meam ego sum, stans totis noctibus ».

60. (Vers. 38.). Et quia talis praelatus et doctor libenter auditur, ideo subdit: *Et omnis populus manicabat*[^p539-15] *ad eum in templo, audire illum,* ut vigilans ad disciplinam, secundum illud Proverbiorum octavo: « Qui mane vigilaverint ad me
<!-- page 540 -->
invenient me ». *Manicare* autem idem est, quod mane accelerare[^p540-1], quia mane surgere oportet ad studium sapientiae; Isaiae quinquagesimo: « Erigit mihi mane aurem, ut audiam eum quasi magistrum »; et Proverbiorum octavo: « Beatus homo, qui audit me, et qui vigilat ad fores meas quotidie et observat ad postes ostii mei. Qui me invenerit inveniet vitam et hauriet salutem a Domino ».

## English
<!-- page 536 -->

48. (Vers. 28.). *But when these things begin to come to pass* etc. After he has schooled the disciples unto foresight, here, second[^p536-5], *he raises them up unto confidence,* and this indeed he does in three ways, namely *by a consolative exhortation, a manuductive similitude* and *a certifying assertion.*

First, then, as to *the consolatory exhortation* he says: *But when these things begin to come to pass, look up and lift up your heads;* Gregory[^p536-6]: « *Lift up your heads,* gladden your hearts, because, while the world is being ended, whose friends you are not, the redemption is near which you have sought ». *Look up,* then, through true faith, according to that above, the eighteenth chapter[^p536-7]: « Look up, thy faith hath made thee whole ». *Look up,* I say, at the things to come, which are not seen, according to that of the second to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « While we look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen. For the things which are seen » etc. And this indeed is done through *faith,* of which to the Hebrews, the eleventh chapter: « Faith is the substance of things to be hoped for, the evidence of things that appear not ». — But *lift up* through *hope;* and he subjoins the reason of this: *Because your redemption is at hand,* for which your hope sighs. Whence to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p536-8]: « The creature groaneth and travaileth in pain even till now. And not only it, but ourselves also, who have the firstfruits of the spirit, even we ourselves groan within ourselves, waiting for the adoption of the sons of God, the redemption of our body. For we are saved by hope ». Now this hope makes tribulations to be borne patiently; to the Romans, the twelfth chapter[^p536-9]: « Rejoicing in hope, patient in tribulation »; and the reason of this, to the Romans, the eighth chapter, that « the sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come, that shall be revealed in us. For the expectation of the creature waiteth for the revelation of the sons of God ».

49. Spiritual men, then, ought *to lift up their heads* through firm expectation; they ought also *to lift up their eyes* through clear consideration, according to that of the Psalm[^p536-10]: « To thee have I lifted up my eyes » etc.; and Genesis, the thirteenth chapter: « Lift up thy eyes straight forward ». « All the land which thou seest, I will give to thee » etc. They ought *to lift up the voice* through the preaching of Scripture; Isaias, the fifty-second chapter[^p536-11]: « The voice of thy watchmen: they have lifted up their voice, they shall praise together » etc. They ought *to lift up their hands* unto perfect operation; the Psalm[^p536-12]: « Thus will I bless thee all my life long, and in thy name I will lift up my hands ». And this is what most of all raises us upward, on account of which the first to Timothy, the second chapter: « I will that you pray in every place, lifting up pure hands, without all anger and contention ».

50. (Vers. 29. 30.). Second, as to *the manuductive similitude,* it is subjoined: *And he spoke to them a similitude: See the fig tree and all the trees: when they now shoot forth their fruit from themselves, you know that*[^p536-13] *summer is nigh.* Now he compares *the trees* to the human race, and most of all *the fig tree.* For man is likened to trees of this kind both as to *birth* and as to *going down* and as to *fruit.* As to *birth,* on account of which in the Psalm[^p536-14]: « He shall be as a tree which is planted near the running waters » etc. As to *going down,* on account of which Job, the fourteenth chapter: « A tree hath hope: if it be cut, it groweth green again, and the boughs thereof sprout »; so also man, when he dies, has hope of rising again. As to *fruit,* because, as an unfruitful tree is cut, so also man; whence Matthew, the third chapter[^p536-15]: « Every tree that doth not yield good fruit shall be cut down » etc.; whence above, the thirteenth chapter, it is said of the unfruitful fig tree: « Cut it down » etc. For as a tree by the way bears no fruit, so also a man busied about many things; Matthew,
<!-- page 537 -->
the twenty-first chapter[^p537-1]: « Seeing a fig tree by the way side, he came to it and found nothing on it but leaves only. And he saith to it: Never may fruit grow from thee ». Whence Chrysostom[^p537-2]: « As it is impossible for a tree by the way to keep its fruits to maturity, so it is difficult for a man living beside the world to keep an immaculate youth unto the end ». As trees also bear fruit when winter departs, so man has fruit after tribulation; the Canticles, the second chapter[^p537-3]: « Now winter is past, the rain is over and gone »; « the fig tree hath put forth her green figs, the vines in flower have yielded their smell ». This fruit is through the bearing of tribulation, according to that to the Hebrews, the twelfth chapter: « All discipline for the present is not of joy, but of sorrow; but afterwards it yieldeth the most peaceable fruit to them that are exercised by it ».

51. (Vers. 31.). And this fruit is gathered in the summer of eternal brightness; on account of which he subjoins: *So you also, when you shall see these things come to pass, know that*[^p537-4] *the kingdom of God is at hand;* the Gloss: « He compares *the kingdom of God* to summer, because then the clouds of our sorrow shall pass, and the days of eternal life shall shine under the brightness of the sun ». In this summer there shall cease *the rain of tears;* Isaias, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p537-5]: « The Lord God taketh away every tear from every face »; the Psalm: « Going they went and wept » etc. There shall cease *the cloud of tribulations;* Tobias, the third chapter[^p537-6]: « After a storm thou makest a calm, and after tears and weeping thou pourest in exultation » etc. There shall cease also *the cold of cupidities;* whence Bernard[^p537-7]: « O true noon, fulness of fervour and of light, standing of the sun, extermination of shadows, drying up of marshes, driving away of stenches! O perennial solstice, when the day shall now not decline; O lumen[^tr-lumen] of noon, O vernal temperateness, O summer loveliness, O autumnal plenty! And lest I seem to have passed anything over: O rest and holiday of winter! or certainly, if thou approvest this more, then only *winter is over and gone* »!

52. (Vers. 32.). Third, as to *the certifying assertion* he subjoins: *Amen, I say to you, that*[^p537-8] *this generation shall not pass away till all things be fulfilled;* the Gloss: « The things which I say to you, expect under certitude ». Now *this generation* he calls the mortal generation, according to that of Ecclesiastes, the first chapter[^p537-9]: « A generation passeth away, and a generation cometh ». This *passes away,* when it passes over to immortality through the resurrection; the Gloss[^p537-10]: « From mortality to immortality ». And this resurrection will not be before the consummation of the things foretold, because, Job, the fourteenth chapter, « man, when he is fallen asleep, shall not rise again, till the heaven be broken ».

53. (Vers. 33.). And therefore he subjoins: *Heaven and earth shall pass away,* as to *the exterior figure;* the Psalm[^p537-11]: « In the beginning, O Lord, thou foundedst the earth, and the heavens are the works of thy hands. They shall perish, but thou remainest » etc.; and therefore the first to the Corinthians, the seventh chapter: « The figure of this world passeth away ». Whence Isaias, the fifty-first chapter: « The heavens shall melt away like smoke, and the earth shall be worn away like a garment »; and Isaias, the sixty-fifth chapter: « Behold, I create new heavens and a new earth ». — And therefore all these things pass, as temporal things, but on the contrary, *but my words shall not pass away,* as to *the interior sense;* the Psalm[^p537-12]: « For ever, O Lord, thy word remaineth ». Whence Gregory: « Everything that with you is durable and without change, is not durable unto eternity; and everything that with me is seen to pass, is held fixed and without passing ». Whence Isaias, the fortieth chapter[^p537-13]: « The grass is withered, and the flower is fallen; but the word of our Lord remaineth for ever ». Whoever, then, wills to remain eternally, it behoves that he not tarry in transitory things, but rather, by deserting transitory things, pass over through the divine words to eternal things.

54. On account of which it is to be noted, that this world passes as to everything which it has in itself desirable. It passes, first, as to *loftiness;* Wisdom,
<!-- page 538 -->
the fifth chapter[^p538-1]: « What hath pride profited us? or what hath the boasting of riches brought us? All those things are passed away » etc.; the Psalm: « I have seen the wicked highly exalted » etc. — Second, as to *beauty;* the Psalm[^p538-2]: « In the morning let him pass like grass, in the morning let him flourish and pass »; and James, the first chapter: « Let the brother of low condition glory in his exaltation, and the rich in his being low, because as the flower of the grass shall he pass away. For the sun rose with a burning heat and parched the grass ». — Third, as to *sweetness;* the first of John, the second chapter[^p538-3]: « The world passeth away and the concupiscence thereof »; Proverbs, the fifth chapter: « The lips of a harlot are a honeycomb dropping ». — Therefore he who adheres to these transitory things makes a threefold passage. The first is *from fault into fault;* Wisdom, the second chapter[^p538-4]: « Let us crown ourselves with roses, before they be withered; let there be no meadow which our riot passeth not through » etc. The second is *from fault into punishment;* Wisdom, the second chapter[^p538-5]: « Our time is the passage of a shadow »; and Job, the seventh chapter: « My days have passed more swiftly than the web is cut by the weaver ». The third is *from punishment into punishment;* Job, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p538-6]: « They shall pass from the snow waters to excessive heat ». — But he who passes from transitory things makes a threefold passage. The first is *from fault to penance;* and of this, Acts, the twelfth chapter[^p538-7]: « Passing through the first and the second ward, they came to the iron gate »; and this was designated in the passage *of the Red Sea,* Exodus, the fourteenth chapter. The second is *from penance to wisdom;* Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p538-8]: « Pass over to me, all ye that desire me, and be filled with my fruits »; and this is designated in the passage *through the desert,* in which the children of Israel tasted manna, Exodus, the sixteenth chapter. The third is *from wisdom to eternal life;* the Psalm[^p538-9]: « We have passed through fire and water » etc.; and John, the thirteenth chapter: « Knowing that his hour was come » etc.; and this is designated in the passage *of the Jordan,* by which they entered the land of promise; Josue, the fourth chapter: « The people made haste, that they might pass over; and when they had all passed over, the ark of God also passed over ». — He who makes this threefold passage, he alone is a Hebrew and celebrates the true pasch; because *pasch* is nothing else than passage[^p538-10].

55. (Vers. 34.). *And take heed to yourselves* etc. After he has schooled the disciples unto foresight and raised them up unto confidence, here, third[^p538-11], *he stirs them up unto vigilance;* which indeed he does in four ways, namely *by dissuading from the contrary, by intimating the peril, by persuading to the purpose* and *by introducing an example.*

First, then, as to *the dissuasion from the opposite of vigilance* he says: *And take heed to yourselves,* through vigilance, *lest perhaps your hearts be overcharged,* through sleepiness; Wisdom, the ninth chapter[^p538-12]: « The body which is corrupted weigheth down the soul »; *with surfeiting and drunkenness and the cares of this life,* through excess in gluttony[^tr-excessus]; Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-seventh chapter: « By surfeiting many have perished, but he that is abstinent shall add to life ». Now superfluity of this kind renders us unfit for watching as regards *the study of truth;* Proverbs, the twentieth chapter[^p538-13]: « Wine is a luxurious thing, and drunkenness riotous: whosoever is delighted therewith shall not be wise »; and therefore Ecclesiastes, the second chapter: « I thought to withdraw my flesh from wine, that I might transfer my soul » etc. Likewise, as regards *the exercise of virtue;* to the Romans, the thirteenth chapter[^p538-14]: « Let us walk honestly, as in the day, not in rioting and drunkenness » etc.; and to the Philippians, the third chapter: « Many walk, of whom I have told you often, whose end is destruction, whose God is their belly, and whose glory is in their shame »; and to the Romans, the sixteenth chapter: « Such serve not Christ, but their own belly ». Likewise, as regards *the transport of contemplation;* to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter[^p538-15]: « Be not drunk with wine, wherein is luxury; but be ye filled with the Holy Spirit, speaking to yourselves » etc.; to the Hebrews, the thirteenth chapter: « It is best that the heart be established with grace, not with meats »; because, to the Romans, the fourteenth chapter, « the kingdom of God is not meat and drink, but justice and peace » etc.

56. Second, as to *the intimation of the peril* he subjoins: *And that day come upon you suddenly,* on account of the unconsidered outcome. For that comes about *suddenly* which is not foreseen; Isaias, the thirtieth chapter[^p538-16]: « Suddenly, when it is not looked for, shall come the destruction thereof ». For that day of judgement comes suddenly to those who have not prepared themselves against it; above, the twelfth chapter: « If that servant shall say: My lord delayeth his coming, and shall begin to eat and to drink and to be drunk; the lord of that servant shall come in the day that he hopeth not, and at the hour that he knoweth not, and shall separate him and shall appoint his portion with unbelievers ».

57. (Vers. 35.). And as to such he subjoins: *For as a snare shall it come upon all that sit upon the face of the whole earth,* that is, upon all who lie upon earthly things. Whence Jeremias,
<!-- page 539 -->
the forty-eighth chapter[^p539-1]: « Fear, and the pit, and the snare upon thee, O inhabitant of Moab », by which indeed *the carnal* are understood, who sit upon the earth, of whom in the Psalm[^p539-2]: « For our soul is humbled down to the dust, our belly cleaveth to the earth »; Ezechiel, the twenty-sixth chapter: « They shall sit on the ground, and, astonished at thy sudden fall, they shall wonder ». Now of this the Lord set above, the seventeenth chapter[^p539-3],, a twofold example, namely of the days of Noe and of the days of Lot; because the first perished *by the flood,* the second *by the burning.* And of such Job, the twenty-first chapter: « Their houses are secure and peaceable ». « They take the timbrel and the harp, and rejoice at the sound of the organ; they spend their days in goods, and in a moment they go down to hell ».

58. (Vers. 36.). Third, as to *the persuasion to the purpose* he subjoins: *Watch ye, therefore, praying at all times*[^p539-4]. Now it is to be attended to, that he joins together *vigilance* and *prayer.* For it behoves *to watch* against *the exterior enemy,* namely the devil; the first of Peter, the last chapter[^p539-5]: « Be sober and watch, because your adversary the devil » etc.; and against *the domestic one,* namely the flesh; and therefore Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-first chapter: « The watching of honesty shall consume the flesh ». It behoves *to watch* for *the advancement of virtue;* the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter[^p539-6]: « In all things let us exhibit ourselves as the ministers of God, in much patience, in many watchings » etc. — It behoves *to watch* through *the desire of contemplation;* the Canticles, the fifth chapter[^p539-7]: « I sleep, and my heart watcheth »; and Isaias, the twenty-sixth chapter: « My soul hath desired thee in the night » etc. And since we can neither *overcome the enemies* nor *advance in good* nor *have heavenly consolations* except through the gift of divine grace, therefore it behoves *to pray;* and on that account Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p539-8]: « Watch ye and pray, that ye enter not into temptation »; and Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-ninth chapter: « The just man will give his heart to watching at break of day » etc. — Since, then, it behoves to watch on account of *the declining of evil,* therefore he adds: *That you may be accounted worthy to escape all these things that are to come,* according to that of Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p539-9]: « But pray that your flight be not in the winter or on the sabbath ». And this the Prophet prayed in the Psalm: « O Lord, hear my prayer: give ear to my supplication » etc. — It behoves to watch also on account of *the obtaining of the good,* and therefore he adds: *And to stand before the Son of man;* the Gloss[^p539-10]: « This is the sum of beatitude, to stand secure in the presence of the judge »; Job, the twenty-third chapter: « Who will grant me that I might know and find him, and come even to his throne »? And therefore the Apocalypse, the seventh chapter: « These are they who are come out of great tribulation, and have washed their robes in the blood of the Lamb. Therefore they are before the throne of God, and they serve him day and night in his temple ». And here there must always be prayer; whence in the Psalm[^p539-11]: « My soul hath thirsted after God, the living fountain; when shall I come and appear before the face of God »!

59. (Vers. 37.). Fourth, as to *the introduction of an example* it is subjoined: *And in the daytime he was teaching in the temple,* as one watching about *the exercise of the active,* that he might give an example for the exercise of perfect action, according to that of the second to Timothy, the fourth chapter[^p539-12]: « Preach the word, be instant in season, out of season » etc.; and again in the same place: « But do thou watch, labour in all things » etc. — *But at night going out, he abode in the mount that is called Olivet,* as one watching about *the leisure of the contemplative,* according to that of Lamentations, the second chapter[^p539-13]: « Arise in the night, in the beginning of the watches, pour out thy heart like water ». And by these two he gave an example of perfect vigilance in the active and the contemplative to all prelates, according to that of the Canticles, the third chapter[^p539-14]: « Behold, threescore valiant ones of the most valiant of Israel surround the bed of Solomon, all holding swords, and most expert in war »; and Isaias, the twenty-first chapter: « I am upon the watchtower of the Lord, standing continually by day; and I am upon my ward, standing whole nights ».

60. (Vers. 38.). And because such a prelate and doctor is gladly heard, therefore he subjoins: *And all the people came early in the morning*[^p539-15] *to him in the temple, to hear him,* as one watching for discipline, according to that of Proverbs, the eighth chapter: « They that in the morning watch for me
<!-- page 540 -->
shall find me ». Now *manicare* ("to come early in the morning") is the same as to hasten in the morning[^p540-1], because it behoves to rise in the morning to the study of wisdom; Isaias, the fiftieth chapter: « He raiseth my ear in the morning, that I may hear him as a master »; and Proverbs, the eighth chapter: « Blessed is the man that heareth me, and that watcheth daily at my gates, and waiteth at the posts of my door. He that shall find me shall find life, and shall draw salvation from the Lord ».
