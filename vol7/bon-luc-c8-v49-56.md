# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. VIII, Vers. 49-56
## *Adhuc illo loquente etc. Postquam ostendit Evangelista Christum dominum corporum per sanationem haemorrhoissae, hic ostendit Christum dominum animarum per suscitationem puellae*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 213 -->
87. *Adhuc illo loquente* etc. Postquam ostendit Evangelista Christum dominum corporum per sanationem haemorrhoissae, hic ostendit Christum *dominum animarum* per suscitationem puellae[^p213-4]. Habet autem haec pars quatuor particulas; in quarum prima praemittitur *soliditas fidei* per modum *disponentis.* Secundo adiungitur *idoneitas testimonii* per modum *approbantis,* ibi[^p213-5]: *Et cum pervenisset ad domum* etc. Tertio ostenditur *sublimitas imperii* per modum *efficientis,* ibi: *Ipse autem tenens manum eius.* Quarto notatur *celebritas miraculi* per modum *terminantis,* ibi: *Et iussit illi dari manducare.* Et sic perfecte describitur miraculum quantum ad quadruplex genus causae.

*Soliditas* autem *fidei archisynagogi* describitur in duplici respectu: primo respectu *dissuasionis humanae* et respectu *persuasionis divinae;* per primam tentatur et per secundam solidatur.

88. (Vers. 49.). Primo igitur quantum ad *tentationem fidei* per *dissuasionem humanam* dicitur: *Adhuc illo loquente,* scilicet Iesu, *venit quidam ad principem synagogae; venit* autem non in adiutorium sicut Angelus bonus, sicut dicitur Danielis decimo[^p213-6]: « Michael, unus de principibus primis, venit in adiutorium »; sed sicut malus nuntius venit ad impediendum. Propter quod addit: *Dicens ei: Quia mortua est filia tua,* et ita non potest esse spes de vita, secundum illud secundi Regum duodecimo[^p213-7]: « Nunc, quia mortuus est infans, quare ieiuno? Nunquid potero revocare eum amplius? Ego vadam magis ad eum, ille non revertetur ad me ». — Et ideo subiungit: *Iam noli vexare Magistrum*[^p213-8], id est, frustra tecum ducere cum labore. Hoc quidem erat verbum non credentis, Christum posse mortuum suscitare. Ignorabat enim illud divinum verbum Ioannis quinto[^p213-9]: « Quia venit hora, ut qui in monumentis sunt audiant vocem Filii Dei, et qui audierint vivent ». Unde iste nuntius non dicebat ex reverentia, sicut centurio, supra septimo: « Domine, noli vexari », sed ex diffidentia, ut diceret illud Iob septimo: « Dies mei velocius transierunt, quam a texente tela succiditur, et consumti sunt absque ulla spe ».

89. (Vers. 50.). Secundo quantum ad *roborationem fidei* per *suasionem divinam* subditur: *Iesus autem, audito hoc verbo,* quod scilicet poterat concutere mentem archisynagogi, quia, secundum illud Proverbiorum vigesimo sexto[^p213-10], « verba susurronis quasi simplicia, et ipsa penetrant usque ad interiora ventris ». Ne ergo verbum mortis interius penetraret, opponit verbum vitae; et hoc est verbum solidans fidem, quia, ad Hebraeos decimo, « iustus meus ex fide vivit; quodsi subtraxerit se, non placebit animae meae ». — Ideo additur: *Dixit*[^p213-11] *patri puellae: Noli timere,* scilicet ex diffidentia; Iacobi primo: « Postulet autem in fide, nihil haesitans. Qui enim haesitat similis est fluctui maris, qui a vento movetur et circumfertur ». Sic de Abraham dicitur ad Romanos quarto, quod « in promissione non haesitavit diffidentia, sed confortatus fide est, plenissime sciens, quoniam quae promisit Deus potens est
<!-- page 214 -->
et facere ». — Et ideo subditur: *Sed*[^p214-1] *crede tantum,* per confidentiam; Ioannis undecimo: « Si credideris, videbis gloriam Dei ». Et ideo addit: *Et salva erit,* per divinam potentiam. Et ad hoc debebat moveri per illud quod statim ante hoc[^p214-2] dixerat mulieri curatae: « Fides tua te salvam fecit ». Unde in hoc apparet mirabilis dispensatio Dei, qui in miraculis suis nostram fidem requirit ad nostrum meritum. Unde Matthaei nono dixit caecis petentibus illuminari: « Creditis, quia hoc possum facere vobis? Dicunt ei: Utique, Domine. Tunc tetigit oculos eorum, dicens: Secundum fidem vestram fiat vobis ». Et quoniam difficile erat credere, mortuum suscitari; ideo Dominus in ipso itinere miraculum fecit, per quod adiuvit fidem archisynagogi; ut sic pateat, illud esse verum quod primae ad Corinthios decimo[^p214-3] dicitur: « Fidelis Deus, qui non patietur, vos tentari supra id quod potestis; sed faciet etiam cum tentatione proventum, ut possitis sustinere ».

90. (Vers. 51.). *Et cum pervenisset*[^p214-4] etc. Hic secundo post soliditatem fidei disponentis subiungit Evangelista *idoneitatem testimonii approbantis.* Ad probationem autem suscitationis requiritur testimonium de *vita reddita* et de *vita perdita;* ideo inducuntur testes respectu utriusque.

Primo igitur quantum ad personas idoneas ad testificandum de *suscitatione miraculosa* dicitur: *Et cum pervenisset ad domum,* ubi scilicet debebat puella suscitari, quae erat domus luctus, ad quam utile est ire, secundum illud Ecclesiastae septimo[^p214-5]: « Melius est ire ad domum luctus quam ad domum convivii »; quia « in illa finis cunctorum » etc. — Et quia non omnibus aperienda sunt sacramenta Dei; Ioannis decimo quinto: « Servus nescit, quid faciat dominus eius »; ideo subditur: *Non permisit intrare secum quemquam,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici sexto[^p214-6]: « Multi pacifici sint tibi, consiliarius sit tibi unus de mille ». — Quia vero discipuli erant eius amici, secundum quod dicit Ioannis decimo quinto: « Vos autem dixi amicos » etc.; ideo non omnes excludit, sed excipit ab exclusis, cum subdit: *Nisi Petrum, Iacobum*[^p214-7] *et Ioannem,* qui erant discipuli principales; et inde isti assumti sunt in transfiguratione; infra nono et Matthaei decimo septimo: « Assumsit Iesus Petrum et Iacobum et Ioannem, fratrem eius » etc. — Et quia « testimonium oportet habere » non tantum a domesticis, sed etiam « ab his qui foris sunt », secundum illud primae ad Timotheum tertio; ideo discipulis adiungit duas personas, cum dicit: *Et patrem et matrem puellae,* ut sic[^p214-8] « in ore duorum et trium testium stet » fixum veritatis testimonium. — Unde apparet, quod *secundum litteram* est idoneitas testimonii quantum ad *personas* et quantum ad *numerum,* quia nihil habet superfluum, nihil habet diminutum.

91. Secundum *spiritualem* intelligentiam hoc non vacat a mysterio. Nam *Petrus,* qui interpretatur agnoscens, designat *fidem; Iacobus,* qui luctator, designat *spem; Ioannes,* in quo gratia, *caritatem*[^p214-9]. Et hae tres virtutes sunt Domino familiarissimae et concurrunt ad suscitationem *peccatoris* a morte, quae designatur in suscitatione puellae. Nam *fides* concurrit; Ioannis undecimo[^p214-10]: « Qui credit in me, etiam si mortuus fuerit, vivet ». Similiter *spes;* Psalmus: « Ecce, oculi Domini super timentes eum, et in eis qui sperant super misericordia eius, ut eruat a morte ». Similiter *caritas;* primae Ioannis quarto: « In hoc apparuit caritas Dei, quia misit Filium suum unigenitum, ut vivamus per eum ». — *Pater* autem et *mater* sunt Legis doctores: *pater,* qui instruit in *fide,* secundum illud primae ad Corinthios quarto[^p214-11]: « In Christo Iesu per Evangelium ego vos genui »; *mater,* quae fovet in *moribus,* secundum illud ad Galatas quarto: « « Filioli mei, quos iterum parturio, donec formetur in vobis Christus ». Et de his Proverbiorum sexto: « Conserva, fili, praecepta patris tui, et ne dimittas legem matris tuae ». — Haec autem sunt cum Christo suscitante peccatorem.

92. (Vers. 52.). Secundo quantum ad personas idoneas testificantes *de morte naturali* subditur: *Flebant autem omnes et plangebant illam,* tanquam mortuam, secundum consilium Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo[^p214-12]: « Fili, in mortuum produc lacrymas et quasi dira passus incipe plorare ». *Flebant* autem lacrymis et *plangebant* vocibus lamentatoriis, sicut erat consuetudinis Iudaeorum; Ieremiae nono[^p214-13]: « Vocate
<!-- page 215 -->
lamentatrices, et assumant super vos lamentum ». Vel *flebant* propter vitae amissionem et *plangebant* propter desperationem. — Et ideo, quia excedebant, confortantur a Salvatore, cum dicitur: *At ille dixit: Nolite flere,* secundum illud primae ad Thessalonicenses quarto[^p215-1]: « Non contristemini sicut et ceteri, qui spem non habent ». — Et ideo additur: *Non est mortua puella, sed dormit;* in quo non removet mortis veritatem, sed dat resurgendi securitatem, quia poterat eam ita faciliter a mortuis suscitare, sicut suscitatur de somnis dormiens a vigilante[^p215-2]. Et propterea sic dicebat Ioannis undecimo: « Lazarus, amicus noster, dormit »; et post: « Lazarus mortuus est ». Unde Beda[^p215-3]: « Mortua est hominibus, qui suscitare non possunt, non Deo, cui anima vivit, et caro suscitanda quiescit ». Propter quod merito cantat Ecclesia: « Regem, cui omnia vivunt, venite, adoremus », in Officio defunctorum; infra vigesimo: « Non est Deus mortuorum, sed vivorum; omnes enim vivunt ei ». Quoniam ergo nihil Domino moritur, et praesens aderat Deus; ideo merito dicebat: *Non est mortua puella.*

93. (Vers. 53.). Sed quia « homo animalis non percipit ea quae Dei sunt », primae ad Corinthios secundo[^p215-4]; ideo additur: *Et deridebant eum, scientes, quod mortua esset.* Qui enim certus est de aliquo non solum non credit, sed etiam deridet persuadentem contrarium. Et quoniam divina sapientia frequenter persuadet contrarium humanae scientiae, hinc est, quod homo per humanam scientiam irridet Deum, et sic efficitur stultus; Ieremiae decimo[^p215-5]: « Stultus factus est omnis homo a scientia sua ». Stulta est enim scientia, quae deridet sapientiam; primae ad Corinthios primo: « Stultam fecit Deus sapientiam huius mundi ». Et quia, secundum quod dicitur Proverbiorum decimo octavo[^p215-6], « non recipit stultus verba prudentiae, nisi ea dixeris, quae versantur in corde eius »; ideo isti non recipiebant, sed deridebant; Proverbiorum decimo quinto: « Stultus irridet disciplinam patris sui »; et ideo non erant idonei testes ad *vitae restitutionem,* sed solum ad *vitae perditionem.* Ideo fuerunt eiecti, secundum quod dicitur Matthaei nono[^p215-7]: « Cum eiecta esset turba, intravit et tenuit manum puellae » etc.

94. (Vers. 54.). *Ipse autem tenens manum* etc. Hic iam tertio post soliditatem fidei et idoneitatem testimonii subiungitur *sublimitas imperii suscitantis* quantum ad *potentiam praecipientem* et quantum ad *efficaciam subsequentem.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *potentiam praecipientem* dicitur: *Ipse tenens manum eius,* ex *virtutis adiutorio,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p215-8]: « Dextera Domini fecit virtutem » etc. Et rursus poterat puella dicere: « Tenuisti manum dexteram meam ». — Et quoniam « non est abbreviata manus Domini, ut salvare non possit », sicut dicitur Isaiae quinquagesimo nono; ideo additur: *Clamavit dicens: Puella, surge,* ex *imperio maiestatis,* sicut dicitur Ioannis undecimo[^p215-9]: « Voce magna clamavit: Lazare, veni foras ». Clamor autem iste in voce exteriori designat immensitatem in virtute interiori, iuxta quod in Psalmo dicitur: « Dabit voci suae vocem virtutis », scilicet miraculi, secundum illud primae ad Thessalonicenses quarto: « Ipse Dominus in iussu et in voce Archangeli et in tuba Dei descendet de caelo, et mortui, qui in Christo sunt, resurgent primi ».

95. (Vers. 55.). Secundo quantum ad *efficaciam subsequentem* subditur: *Et reversus est spiritus eius,* per *intimam coniunctionem,* iuxta quod dicitur tertii Regum decimo septimo[^p215-10]: « Reversa est anima pueri intra ipsum, et revixit »; *et surrexit continuo,* per *vitalem operationem.* Surgere enim est operatio viventis, sicut cadere morientis.

96. Et nota, quod puella ista in resuscitatione sua dicitur *surrexisse,* et adolescens *resedisse,* supra septimo[^p215-11], Lazarus vero *ligatus manus et pedes prodiisse,* Ioannis undecimo; quia, sicut supra tactum fuit, per *puellam* mortuam *in domo* peccatum intelligitur *cogitationis;* per *adolescentem in porta,* peccatum *operationis;* per *Lazarum in sepulcro,* peccatum *consuetudinis.* Et quoniam, secundum quod peccata sunt graviora, maiores relinquunt sequelas; ideo qui solum *cogitatione* peccavit vivificatus *continuo surgit,* quia parva remanet difficultas; qui vero in *opere, sedet,* quia adhuc pronitas remanet; qui autem in *consuetudine, ligatus est,* quia valde habet pronitatem ligantem in malo et praepedientem
<!-- page 216 -->
a bono, postquam etiam redditus fuerit vitae. Et secundum hoc gravior debet imponi poenitentia diversis[^p216-1].

97. *Et iussit illi dari manducare.* Hic quarto et ultimo tangitur *claritas miraculi* ut *manifestandi* ad fidei utilitatem et *occultandi* contra gloriae vanitatem. Unde primo praemittitur *manifestatio utilis;* secundo vero, *occultatio humilis,* quia, sicut dicit Gregorius[^p216-2], « sic debet esse opus in manifesto, ut tamen intentio maneat in occulto ».

Primo ergo quantum ad *utilem miraculi manifestationem* dicitur: *Et iussit illi dari manducare,* ut non phantastica, sed vera suscitatio probaretur, sicut ipse probavit suam resurrectionem; infra ultimo[^p216-3]: « Habetis hic aliquid, quod manducetur »? et suscitationem Lazari, Ioannis duodecimo, ubi dicitur, quod « Lazarus unus erat ex discumbentibus ». Ideo autem signum dat sensibus verae suscitationis, ut mentes elevet in excessum admirationis.

98. (Vers. 56.). Unde et additur: *Et stupuerunt parentes eius,* quasi magnitudinem divinae potentiae ferre non valentes; Isaiae vigesimo nono[^p216-4]: « Ecce, ego addam, ut admirationem faciam populo huic miraculo grandi et stupendo ». Stupor autem iste fidem credentium elevavit in excessum, secundum illud Actuum decimo: « Obstupuerunt ex circumcisione fideles » etc.

99. Secundo vero quantum ad *humilem occultationem* subditur: *Quibus praecepit, ne cui*[^p216-5] *dicerent quod factum erat;* volens humiliter latere et exemplum humilitatis dare, sicut praecepit discipulis Matthaei decimo septimo: « Praecepit illis: Nemini dixeritis visionem » etc.; in quo ostendit, nos debere velle latere in magnis factis. Unde Augustinus[^p216-6]: « Occulta quod agis, in quantum potes; quod si ex toto non vales, sit in animo voluntas occultandi ». — Et nota, quod est praeceptum *exsecutionis,* sicut decalogus; *probationis,* sicut de immolando filio Abrahae, Genesis vigesimo secundo[^p216-7]; et *instructionis,* sicut hic. In primo invitatur *intellectus* ad cognoscendum, *affectus* ad volendum, *effectus* ad exsequendum. In secundo invitatur *affectus,* sed non *effectus.* In tertio tantum *intellectus* eruditur, non ligatur *affectus,* nec prohibetur *effectus.* Et sic intelligitur illud praeceptum.

## English
<!-- page 213 -->
87. *While he was yet speaking* etc. After the Evangelist has shown Christ the lord of bodies through the healing of the woman with the issue of blood, here he shows Christ *the lord of souls* through the raising of the girl[^p213-4]. Now this part has four little parts; in the first of which is set forth *the solidity of faith* by way of *disposing.* Secondly is joined *the fitness of the testimony* by way of *approving,* there[^p213-5]: *And when he was come to the house* etc. Thirdly is shown *the sublimity of the command* by way of *effecting,* there: *But he taking her by the hand.* Fourthly is noted *the renown of the miracle* by way of *terminating,* there: *And he bid them give her to eat.* And so the miracle is perfectly described as to the fourfold genus of cause.

Now *the solidity of the faith of the ruler of the synagogue* is described in a twofold respect: first in respect of *human dissuasion* and in respect of *divine persuasion;* by the first it is tempted and by the second it is made solid.

88. (Vers. 49.). First then, as to *the temptation of faith* through *human dissuasion,* it is said: *While he was yet speaking,* namely Jesus, *there cometh one to the ruler of the synagogue; there cometh* however not to help, as a good Angel, as is said in Daniel, the tenth chapter[^p213-6]: « Michael, one of the chief princes, came to help »; but as an evil messenger he comes to hinder. On account of which he adds: *Saying to him: Thy daughter is dead,* and so there can be no hope of life, according to that of the second of Kings, the twelfth chapter[^p213-7]: « Now, because the child is dead, why do I fast? Shall I be able to bring him back any more? I shall go to him rather, he shall not return to me ». — And therefore he subjoins: *Now trouble not the Master*[^p213-8], that is, to lead him with thee in vain with toil. This indeed was the word of one not believing that Christ could raise the dead. For he did not know that divine word of John, the fifth chapter[^p213-9]: « For the hour cometh, that they who are in the graves shall hear the voice of the Son of God, and they that hear shall live ». Whence this messenger did not speak out of reverence, as the centurion, above, the seventh chapter: « Lord, trouble not thyself », but out of distrust, so as to say that of Job, the seventh chapter: « My days have passed more swiftly than the web is cut by the weaver, and are consumed without any hope ».

89. (Vers. 50.). Secondly, as to *the strengthening of faith* through *divine persuading,* it is subjoined: *And Jesus, having heard this word,* which namely could shake the mind of the ruler of the synagogue, because, according to that of Proverbs, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p213-10], « the words of a talebearer are as it were simple, and they pierce even to the inner parts of the belly ». Lest then the word of death should pierce within, he opposes the word of life; and this is the word that makes faith solid, because, to the Hebrews, the tenth chapter, « my just man liveth by faith; but if he withdraw himself, he shall not please my soul ». — Therefore it is added: *He said*[^p213-11] *to the father of the girl: Fear not,* namely out of distrust; James, the first chapter: « But let him ask in faith, nothing wavering. For he that wavereth is like a wave of the sea, which is moved and carried about by the wind ». So it is said of Abraham, to the Romans, the fourth chapter, that « in the promise he staggered not by distrust, but was strengthened in faith, most fully knowing that whatsoever God hath promised, he is able also
<!-- page 214 -->
to perform ». — And therefore it is subjoined: *But*[^p214-1] *believe only,* through confidence; John, the eleventh chapter: « If thou believe, thou shalt see the glory of God ». And therefore he adds: *And she shall be safe,* through the divine power. And to this he ought to have been moved by that which just before this[^p214-2] he had said to the woman cured: « Thy faith hath made thee whole ». Whence in this appears the marvellous dispensation of God, who in his miracles requires our faith for our merit. Whence in Matthew, the ninth chapter, he said to the blind asking to be enlightened: « Do you believe that I can do this unto you? They say to him: Yea, Lord. Then he touched their eyes, saying: According to your faith, be it done unto you ». And since it was hard to believe that the dead is raised, therefore the Lord on the very way worked a miracle, by which he helped the faith of the ruler of the synagogue; that so it may be plain that that is true which is said in the first to the Corinthians, the tenth chapter[^p214-3]: « God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that which you are able; but will make also with temptation issue, that you may be able to bear it ».

90. (Vers. 51.). *And when he was come*[^p214-4] etc. Here secondly, after the solidity of faith disposing, the Evangelist subjoins *the fitness of the testimony approving.* Now for the proof of the raising, testimony is required concerning *the life given back* and concerning *the life lost;* therefore witnesses are brought in in respect of each.

First then, as to the persons fit to testify concerning *the miraculous raising,* it is said: *And when he was come to the house,* namely where the girl was to be raised, which was a house of mourning, to which it is profitable to go, according to that of Ecclesiastes, the seventh chapter[^p214-5]: « It is better to go to the house of mourning than to the house of feasting »; for « in that the end of all » etc. — And since the sacraments of God are not to be opened to all; John, the fifteenth chapter: « The servant knoweth not what his lord doth »; therefore it is subjoined: *He suffered not any man to go in with him,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the sixth chapter[^p214-6]: « Be in peace with many, let one of a thousand be thy counsellor ». — But since the disciples were his friends, according as John says, the fifteenth chapter: « But I have called you friends » etc.; therefore he does not shut out all, but excepts from those shut out, when he subjoins: *But Peter and James*[^p214-7] *and John,* who were the principal disciples; and hence these were taken up in the transfiguration; below, the ninth chapter, and Matthew, the seventeenth chapter: « Jesus took Peter and James and John his brother » etc. — And since « he must have testimony » not only from those of the household, but also « from them who are without », according to that of the first to Timothy, the third chapter; therefore to the disciples he joins two persons, when he says: *And the father and mother of the maiden,* that so[^p214-8] « in the mouth of two and three witnesses may stand » the fixed testimony of the truth. — Whence it appears that *according to the letter* there is fitness of the testimony as to *the persons* and as to *the number,* because it has nothing superfluous, it has nothing diminished.

91. According to the *spiritual* intelligence this is not free from mystery. For *Peter,* who is interpreted acknowledging, designates *faith; James,* who is a wrestler, designates *hope; John,* in whom is grace, *charity*[^p214-9]. And these three virtues are most familiar to the Lord and run together to the raising of the *sinner* from death, which is designated in the raising of the girl. For *faith* runs together; John, the eleventh chapter[^p214-10]: « He that believeth in me, although he be dead, shall live ». Likewise *hope;* the Psalm: « Behold, the eyes of the Lord are on them that fear him, and on them that hope in his mercy, to deliver from death ». Likewise *charity;* the first of John, the fourth chapter: « In this hath the charity of God appeared, because he sent his only begotten Son, that we may live by him ». — Now *the father* and *the mother* are the doctors of the Law: *the father,* who instructs in *faith,* according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter[^p214-11]: « In Christ Jesus, by the gospel, I have begotten you »; *the mother,* who cherishes in *morals,* according to that to the Galatians, the fourth chapter: « « My little children, of whom I am in labour again, until Christ be formed in you » [so printed: the opening guillemet doubled]. And of these, Proverbs, the sixth chapter: « My son, keep the commandments of thy father, and forsake not the law of thy mother ». — And these are with Christ when he raises the sinner.

92. (Vers. 52.). Secondly, as to the persons fit to testify *concerning the natural death,* it is subjoined: *And all wept and mourned for her,* as dead, according to the counsel of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p214-12]: « My son, shed tears over the dead, and begin to lament as if thou hadst suffered some great harm ». Now they *wept* with tears and *mourned* with lamenting voices, as was the custom of the Jews; Jeremias, the ninth chapter[^p214-13]: « Call for
<!-- page 215 -->
the mourning women, and let them take up a lamentation over you ». Or *they wept* because of the loss of life and *mourned* because of despair. — And therefore, since they went to excess, they are comforted by the Saviour, when it is said: *But he said: Weep not,* according to that of the first to the Thessalonians, the fourth chapter[^p215-1]: « Be not sorrowful, even as others who have no hope ». — And therefore it is added: *The maid is not dead, but sleepeth;* in which he does not remove the truth of death, but gives security of rising again, because he could raise her from the dead as easily as one sleeping is raised from sleep by one awake[^p215-2]. And for this reason he said thus in John, the eleventh chapter: « Lazarus our friend sleepeth »; and after: « Lazarus is dead ». Whence Bede[^p215-3]: « She is dead to men, who cannot raise her, not to God, to whom the soul lives, and the flesh, to be raised, rests ». On account of which the Church rightly sings: « The King, to whom all things live, come, let us adore », in the Office of the dead; below, the twentieth chapter: « He is not the God of the dead, but of the living; for all live to him ». Since then nothing dies to the Lord, and God was present at hand; therefore he rightly said: *The maid is not dead.*

93. (Vers. 53.). But since « the sensual man perceiveth not the things that are of God », the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p215-4]; therefore it is added: *And they laughed him to scorn, knowing that she was dead.* For he who is certain of something not only does not believe, but also laughs to scorn one persuading the contrary. And since the divine wisdom often persuades the contrary of human knowledge, hence it is that man through human knowledge mocks God, and so is made a fool; Jeremias, the tenth chapter[^p215-5]: « Every man is become a fool for his knowledge ». For foolish is the knowledge that laughs wisdom to scorn; the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter: « God hath made foolish the wisdom of this world ». And since, according to what is said in Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter[^p215-6], « a fool receiveth not the words of prudence, unless thou say those things which are in his heart »; therefore these did not receive, but laughed to scorn; Proverbs, the fifteenth chapter: « A fool laugheth at the instruction of his father »; and therefore they were not fit witnesses for *the restoring of life,* but only for *the loss of life.* Therefore they were put forth, according to what is said in Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p215-7]: « When the multitude was put forth, he went in and took the maid by the hand » etc.

94. (Vers. 54.). *But he taking her by the hand* etc. Here now thirdly, after the solidity of faith and the fitness of the testimony, is subjoined *the sublimity of the command that raises,* as to *the power that commands* and as to *the efficacy that follows.*

First then, as to *the power that commands,* it is said: *He taking her by the hand,* by *the aid of power,* according to that of the Psalm[^p215-8]: « The right hand of the Lord hath wrought power » etc. And again the girl could say: « Thou hast held me by my right hand ». — And since « the hand of the Lord is not shortened that it cannot save », as is said in Isaias, the fifty-ninth chapter; therefore it is added: *He cried out, saying: Maid, arise,* by *the command of majesty,* as is said in John, the eleventh chapter[^p215-9]: « He cried with a loud voice: Lazarus, come forth ». Now this cry in the exterior voice designates immensity in the interior power, according to what is said in the Psalm: « He will give to his voice the voice of power », namely of miracle, according to that of the first to the Thessalonians, the fourth chapter: « The Lord himself, with commandment, and with the voice of an archangel, and with the trumpet of God, shall come down from heaven, and the dead who are in Christ shall rise first ».

95. (Vers. 55.). Secondly, as to *the efficacy that follows,* it is subjoined: *And her spirit returned,* through *the intimate conjunction,* according to what is said in the third of Kings, the seventeenth chapter[^p215-10]: « The soul of the child returned into him, and he revived »; *and she arose immediately,* through *the vital operation.* For to rise is the operation of one living, as to fall is of one dying.

96. And note that this girl in her raising again is said *to have arisen,* and the young man *to have sat up,* above, the seventh chapter[^p215-11], but Lazarus *to have come forth bound hands and feet,* John, the eleventh chapter; because, as was touched on above, by *the girl* dead *in the house* is understood the sin *of thought;* by *the young man at the gate,* the sin *of working;* by *Lazarus in the sepulchre,* the sin *of custom.* And since, according as sins are graver, they leave greater consequences; therefore he who has sinned only *in thought,* made alive, *arises immediately,* because little difficulty remains; but he who has sinned in *work, sits,* because a proneness still remains; but he who has sinned in *custom, is bound,* because he has very much a proneness binding him in evil and hindering him
<!-- page 216 -->
from good, even after he has been given back to life. And according to this a graver penance ought to be imposed on different ones[^p216-1].

97. *And he bid them give her to eat.* Here fourthly and lastly is touched *the brightness of the miracle,* as *to be made manifest* for the profit of faith and *to be hidden* against the vanity of glory. Whence first is set forth *the profitable manifestation;* but secondly, *the humble concealment,* because, as Gregory says[^p216-2], « the work ought so to be in public, that yet the intention may remain in secret ».

First then, as to *the profitable manifestation of the miracle,* it is said: *And he bid them give her to eat,* that the raising might be proved not fantastic, but true, as he himself proved his own resurrection; below, the last chapter[^p216-3]: « Have you here any thing to eat »? and the raising of Lazarus, John, the twelfth chapter, where it is said that « Lazarus was one of them that were at table ». Now therefore he gives to the senses a sign of the true raising, that he may lift up minds into a transport of admiration.

98. (Vers. 56.). Whence it is also added: *And her parents were astonished,* as not able to bear the greatness of the divine power; Isaias, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p216-4]: « Behold, I will proceed to cause an admiration in this people, by a great and astonishing miracle ». Now this astonishment lifted up the faith of believers into transport, according to that of the Acts, the tenth chapter: « The faithful of the circumcision were astonished » etc.

99. But secondly, as to *the humble concealment,* it is subjoined: *Whom he enjoined*[^p216-5] *to tell no man what was done;* willing humbly to lie hid and to give an example of humility, as he enjoined the disciples in Matthew, the seventeenth chapter: « He enjoined them: Tell the vision to no man » etc.; in which he shows that we ought to will to lie hid in great deeds. Whence Augustine[^p216-6]: « Hide what thou doest, as far as thou canst; but if thou canst not altogether, let there be in the mind the will to hide ». — And note that there is a precept *of execution,* as the decalogue; *of probation,* as concerning the sacrificing of Abraham's son, Genesis, the twenty-second chapter[^p216-7]; and *of instruction,* as here. In the first *the intellect* is invited to knowing, *the affection* to willing, *the effect* to executing. In the second *the affection* is invited, but not *the effect.* In the third *the intellect* alone is schooled, *the affection* is not bound, nor is *the effect* forbidden. And so that precept is understood.
