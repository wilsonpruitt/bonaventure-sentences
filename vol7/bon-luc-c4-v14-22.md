# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. IV, Vers. 14-22
## *Primo testimonium Scripturae affertur ad eruditionem credentium*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 95 -->

#### Authenticatur doctrina Christi ex testimonio Scripturae.

26. *Et Iesus regressus* etc. Ostendit supra[^p95-11] Evangelista auctoritatem doctrinae Christi ex merito vitae; hic secundo ostendit ex *testimonio Scripturae.* Introducit ergo primo testimonium ut authenticum ad *eruditionem credentium;* secundo vero, testimonium authenticum ad *confutationem detrahentium,* ibi[^p95-12]: *Et dicebant: Nonne hic est filius Ioseph?*

#### Primo testimonium Scripturae affertur ad eruditionem credentium.

In explicatione autem *testimonii prophetici* quatuor introducuntur a beato Luca: primum est
<!-- page 96 -->
*celebritas in praedicando;* secundum est *opportunitas in testificando*[^p96-1]: *Et venit Nazareth;* tertium est *congruitas in testimonio,* ibi: *Spiritus Domini super me* etc.; quartum est *credulitas in populo,* et hoc notatur ibi: *Et cum plicuisset librum* etc.

Quantum ad primum nota, quod *celebritas praedicationis* Christi ex quatuor colligitur, scilicet ex *plenitudine gratiae, latitudine famae, certitudine doctrinae* et *magnitudine efficaciae.*

27. (Vers. 14.). Quantum ad *plenitudinem gratiae* dicitur: *Et regressus est Iesus in virtute Spiritus in Galilaeam. Regressus,* scilicet ad praedicandum, quia, secundum quod in Psalmo[^p96-2] dicitur, « Dominus dabit verbum evangelizantibus virtute multa ». Et licet infirmitatem assumsisset ex nobis, virtutem tamen ostendebat ex se, secundum illud Sapientiae duodecimo: « Virtutem enim ostendis, qui non crederis esse in virtute consummatus ». Et signanter dicitur: *In virtute Spiritus.* Talis enim debet esse praedicatio perfecta, ut sit virtuti spirituali coniuncta, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios secundo[^p96-3]: « Sermo noster et praedicatio non fuit in persuasibilibus humanae sapientiae verbis, sed in ostensione spiritus et virtutis, ut fides vestra non sit in sapientia hominis, sed in virtute Dei ».

28. Quantum ad *latitudinem famae* dicitur: *Et fama exiit per universam regionem de illo,* secundum illud Iob vigesimo octavo[^p96-4]: « Auribus nostris audivimus famam eius »; loquitur de sapientia Christi, qui est « Dei virtus et sapientia ». Huic concordat alius Evangelista; Matthaei quarto: « Exiit opinio de illo in universam Syriam ». Huius autem *figura* praecessit in Salomone, de quo dicitur secundi Paralipomenon nono[^p96-5], quod « omnes desiderabant videre faciem eius »; de quo Ecclesiastici quadragesimo septimo: « Ad insulas longe divulgatum est nomen tuum ».

29. (Vers. 15.). Quantum ad *certitudinem doctrinae* subditur: *Et ipse docebat in synagogis eorum,* non in occulto, sicut homines, qui habent suspectam doctrinam; Ioannis decimo octavo[^p96-6]: « Ego palam locutus sum mundo, ego semper docui in synagogis et in templo, ubi omnes Iudaei conveniunt, et in occulto locutus sum nihil ». Econtra dicitur sapientia haereticorum « terrena, animalis, diabolica », quae designatur per *mulierem malam;* Proverbiorum nono: « Aquae furtivae dulciores sunt, et panis absconditus suavior ». Unde Christus implebat illud Proverbiorum quinto[^p96-7]: « Deriventur fontes tui foras, et in plateis aquas tuas divide ». Et ideo dicitur Proverbiorum primo: « Sapientia foris clamitat, in plateis dat vocem suam » etc.

30. Quantum ad *magnitudinem efficaciae* subinfertur: *Et magnificabatur ab omnibus,* quia omnes magnitudinem efficaciae sentiebant adeo, ut possent dicere illud Psalmi[^p96-8]: « Magnificata est usque ad caelos misericordia tua, et usque ad nubes veritas tua », scilicet in praedicatione. Nec mirum, quia ipse erat ille qui designatur per illum Salomonem, de quo tertii Regum decimo: « Magnificatus est rex Salomon super omnes reges universae terrae prae divitiis et sapientia » etc.; infra septimo: « Propheta magnus surrexit in nobis ».

31. *Et venit Nazareth* etc. Hic vero notatur *opportunitas in testificando,* quae attenditur in circumstantiis concurrentibus ad hoc, quod Christus legeret in conspectu synagogae. Unde quatuor hic connotantur. Dicitur enim *intrasse, surrexisse, librum accepisse* et *acceptum revolvisse.* Haec quatuor introducuntur ad instructionem quadriformem hominum perscrutantium veritatem, scilicet *vacationis* ad meditandum, *promptitudinis* ad legendum, *mansuetudinis* ad discendum, *certitudinis* ad iudicandum.

32. (Vers. 16.). Ad instructionem igitur *vacationis ad meditandum* dicitur *synagogam intrasse,* cum ait: *Et venit Nazareth, ubi erat nutritus; et intravit secundum consuetudinem suam die Sabbati in synagogam.* Haec enim erat consuetudo approbata ex Lege Moysi et conversatione Christi. Hinc dicitur Actuum decimo septimo[^p96-9], quod « Paulus secundum consuetudinem apud Thessalonicam intrabat in synagogam et per tria Sabbata disserebat cum eis de Scripturis ». Illud enim erat tempus, quo debebant vacare legi Dei. In quo docemur, quod quando volumus Deo sabbatizare, debemus congregari *interius* et ibi quiescere; Sapientiae octavo[^p96-10]: « Intrans in domum meam, conquiescam cum illa ». Si igitur Sabbatum *exterius* agatur, est irrisio daemonum, secundum illud Threnorum primo: « Viderunt eam hostes et deriserunt Sabbata eius »; et huiusmodi Deo non placet, secundum illud Isaiae primo: « Neomeniam et Sabbatum et solemnitates vestras non feram ». Et ratio huius est, quia
<!-- page 97 -->
non intrabant interius, sed exterius, et carnaliter sapiebant.

33. Ad instructionem vero *promptitudinis ad legendum* subditur: *Et surrexit legere. Surrectio* ista promptitudinem et vigilantiam importat, quae necessaria est perscrutanti verba divina; unde dicitur Ezechielis secundo[^p97-1]: « Fili hominis, sta super pedes tuos, et loquar tecum ». Hinc est, quod dicitur animae sanctae Isaiae sexagesimo: « Surge, illuminare, Ierusalem » etc.; et Apostolus ad Ephesios quinto: « Surge, qui dormis, et exsurge a mortuis, et illuminabit te Christus ». Simul etiam cum promptitudine designat *humilitatem,* quia *surrexit legere,* tanquam minister, ut etiam exerceret lectoris officium; unde Beda[^p97-2]: « Qui venerat *ministrare et non ministrari* lectoris officium non dedignabatur suscipere ».

34. (Vers. 17.). Ad instructionem autem *mansuetudinis ad addiscendum* additur: *Et traditus est ei*[^p97-3] *liber Isaiae prophetae,* quem scilicet cum mansuetudine suscepit, non dedignans uti Scripturae testimonio et auctoritate. Sic et Ioannes, Apocalypsis decimo: « Accepi librum de manu Angeli et devoravi illum, et erat in ore meo tanquam mel dulce ». Liber autem *sacrae Scripturae* debuit ei *tradi,* quia, secundum illud Apocalypsis quinto[^p97-4], « dignus est Agnus, qui occisus est, accipere librum et solvere septem signacula eius », quia nullus alius poterat; et *liber Isaiae,* quia, sicut dicitur Isaiae vigesimo nono, « erit vobis visio sicut verba libri signati ». Et ideo signanter dicitur *traditus illi liber Isaiae prophetae.*

35. Postremo ad instructionem *certitudinis ad iudicandum* subiungit: *Et ut revolvit librum;* in quo significatur diligens discendi inquisitio, quae praevenire debet iudicium certum; propter quod dicitur Iob octavo[^p97-5]: « Interroga generationem pristinam et diligenter investiga patrum memoriam ». Non enim ex abrupto procedendum est ad iudicium, sed cum maturitate; propter quod dicitur Iob vigesimo nono: « Causam, quam nesciebam, diligentissime investigabam »; et secundi Paralipomenon decimo nono: « Praecipiens Iosaphat iudicibus ait: Cum diligentia cuncta facite ». — Et quoniam qui diligenter quaerit invenit, ideo additur: *Invenit locum, ubi scriptum erat: Spiritus Domini* etc.; hoc invenitur Isaiae sexagesimo primo[^p97-6]. Nec mirum, quia, secundum quod dicitur Baruch tertio, « hic est Deus noster, et non aestimabitur alius ad eum; hic adinvenit omnem viam disciplinae ». Ante ipsum quaerebatur, sed non inveniebatur; unde Iob vigesimo octavo: « Sapientia ubi invenitur? Et quis est locus intelligentiae » etc.

36. (Vers. 18.). *Spiritus Domini super me* etc. Hic notatur *congruitas in testimonio.* Exprimit enim quatuor conditiones excellentiae, quae fuerunt in Salvatore nostro. Est enim *mediator, eruditor, reparator* et *retributor;* et haec quatuor insinuantur in sermone proposito[^p97-7].

Primo igitur insinuatur esse *mediator per incarnationis mysterium,* cum dicit: *Spiritus Domini super me, propter quod unxit me; me* demonstrat ibi personam Christi in natura assumta, supra quam Spiritus sanctus requievit, secundum illud Isaiae undecimo[^p97-8]: « Et requiescet super eum Spiritus Domini »; et Ioannis primo: « Super quem videris Spiritum descendentem et manentem, hic est qui baptizat ». Et ideo dicitur Actuum decimo: « Dixit Petrus: Vos scitis, quod factum est verbum per universam Iudaeam; primo incipiens a Galilaea post baptismum, quod praedicavit Ioannes, Iesum a Nazareth, quomodo unxit eum Deus Spiritu sancto » etc. Unde hic est *Sanctus Sanctorum,* in cuius unctione impleri debet prophetia Danielis nono[^p97-9]: « Impleatur visio et prophetia, et impleatur iustitia sempiterna, et ungatur Sanctus Sanctorum ». — Et nota, quod humana natura in Christo per virtutem Spiritus sancti fuit concepta et per eius gratiam divinae naturae unita. Et propter haec duo dicit, Spiritum Domini *super eum esse* et *unxisse.* — Vel certe dicitur *esse super eum* quantum ad superexcellentiam gratiae singularis, propter quam dicitur Ioannis tertio[^p97-10]: « Non ad mensuram dat Deus spiritum ». Dicitur vero *unxisse eum* quantum ad praerogativam sacerdotalis et regalis dignitatis. Haec respiciunt assumtam naturam in Christo; et hoc praefigurabatur in unctione regum et sacerdotum in veteri testamento.

37. Secundo insinuatur esse *eruditor per praedicationis magisterium* in hoc quod sequitur: *Evangelizare pauperibus misit me. Evangelium* namque
<!-- page 98 -->
est bona annuntiatio[^p98-1], unde in nostra translatione dicitur: *Ad annuntiandum mansuetis misit me.* Et huic concordat illud Nahum primo: « Ecce, super montes pedes evangelizantis et annuntiantis pacem »; et Isaiae quinquagesimo secundo: « Quam pulcri super montes pedes annuntiantis et praedicantis pacem, annuntiantis bonum, praedicantis salutem ». Hanc autem dicitur maxime *pauperibus* nuntiare, quia ad pauperes inchoavit sermonem; Matthaei quinto[^p98-2]: « Beati pauperes spiritu », et per pauperes paupertatem aliis praedicavit; Matthaei undecimo: « Pauperes evangelizantur ». Nec mirum, quia dicitur in Psalmo: « Propter miseriam inopum et gemitum pauperum nunc exsurgam » etc. His utique annuntiat salutem, quia alibi[^p98-3]: « Parcet pauperi et inopi et animas pauperum salvas faciet » etc. Et propter hoc dicitur a Spiritu et a Domino missus; Isaiae quadragesimo octavo: « Sicut misit me Dominus et Spiritus eius ». « In hoc ergo cognovimus caritatem Dei », quia « misit Filium suum », primae Ioannis quarto.

38. Tertio insinuatur *reparator per passionis remedium,* cum additur: *Sanare contritos;* quod[^p98-4] quidem liberat a *malo culpae actualis* et *originalis,* a *caecitate rationis* et *infirmitate voluntatis.* — Liberat utique a *malo actualis culpae;* propter quod dicitur: *Sanare contritos corde,* misit scilicet. Ad curationem actualis culpae necessaria est cum passione Christi contritio poenitentiae; Psalmus[^p98-5]: « Qui sanat contritos corde ». — Vel hoc dicitur, quia ipsa culpa actualis totum robur animae frangit; Threnorum secundo: « Magna velut mare contritio tua » etc.; et Proverbiorum decimo quinto: « Lingua, quae immoderata est, conteret spiritum ».

39. (Vers. 19.). Liberat autem a *malo culpae originalis;* propter quod additur: *Et*[^p98-6] *praedicare captivis remissionem.* Merito causa originalis culpae captivum detinebatur genus humanum a diabolo; Isaiae quinquagesimo secundo: « Excutere de pulvere, consurge, sede, Ierusalem; solve vincula colli tui, captiva filia Sion. Quia haec dicit Dominus: Gratis venundati estis et sine argento redimemini » etc.; unde Zachariae nono: « Tu autem in sanguine testamenti tui emisisti vinctos de lacu » etc. Et hoc est quod dicitur Isaiae quadragesimo nono[^p98-7]: « Dedi te in foedus populi, ut diceres his qui vincti sunt: Exite; et his qui in tenebris sunt: Revelamini »; et post: « Equidem captivitas a forti tolletur »; quia « ascendens Christus in altum, captivam duxit captivitatem », ad Ephesios quarto. Et haec remissio in Cyro praefigurata fuit, qui dimisit captivitatem filiorum Israel; Isaiae quadragesimo quinto[^p98-8]: « Captivitatem meam dimittet, non in pretio, aut in muneribus ».

40. Liberat etiam a *caecitate rationis;* ideo subditur: *Caecis*[^p98-9] *visum,* misit scilicet me *praedicare;* quod et fecit, sicut dicitur Matthaei undecimo: « Euntes, renuntiate Ioanni quae vidistis et audistis: caeci vident » etc.; in quo significatur *interior* illuminatio. Unde Ioannis nono[^p98-10]: « In iudicium ego veni in mundum, ut qui non vident videant », id est gentiles, secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo nono: « Dedi te in lucem gentium ». Et hoc est quod dicitur Isaiae vigesimo nono: « Audient in die illa surdi verba libri, et de tenebris et caligine oculi caecorum videbunt ». Et hoc petebat David: « Illumina oculos meos ».

41. Liberat etiam ab *infirmitate voluntatis;* propter quod subiungitur: *Et*[^p98-11] *dimittere confractos in remissionem,* misit scilicet. *Confracti* sunt, quorum voluntas per peccatum est infirmata ad bonum, sicut sunt fatui peccatores; Ecclesiastici vigesimo primo[^p98-12]: « Cor fatui quasi vas confractum »; et Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo: « Dimitte eos qui confracti sunt, liberos ». Haec autem confractio est reparabilis, dum hic vivimus, sed irreparabilis efficitur in morte, secundum illud Isaiae trigesimo[^p98-13]: « Comminuetur quasi lagena figuli contritione pervalida, et non invenietur de fragmentis eius testa, in qua portetur igniculus de incendio »; quia non erit susceptibilis gratiae. Et hoc erit in iudicio, secundum illud Psalmi: « Reges eos in virga ferrea et tanquam vas figuli confringes eos ». Sed modo remissionem facit, quod figuratum est primi Machabaeorum decimo, ubi dicitur, quod sub Ionatha, fratre Simonis, factae sunt multae remissiones Iudaeis.

42. Postremo insinuatur *remunerator per retributionis iudicium,* in quo *bonis* fit universalis
<!-- page 99 -->
boni collatio. Propter quod dicit: *Praedicare annum Domini acceptum,* scilicet bonis, de quo in Psalmo[^p99-1]: « Benedices coronae anni benignitatis tuae » etc. Hic est *annus* significatus in iubilaeo; Levitici vigesimo quinto: « Anno iubilaei omnes redibunt ad possessiones suas »; vel etiam in septimo; Deuteronomii decimo quinto: « Septimo anno facies remissionem ». Hic annus superat annum meriti, quia supra condignum remunerabuntur boni; Ecclesiastici trigesimo tertio[^p99-2]: « Quare annus annum superat, dies diem, lux lucem a sole »? — In hoc etiam iudicio *malis* fit sempiternalis damnatio; ideo additur: *Et diem retributionis,* in quo retribuet unicuique secundum opera sua; Isaiae sexagesimo tertio[^p99-3]: « Dies ultionis in corde meo, annus retributionis meae venit ». De hac die dicitur primae ad Thessalonicenses quinto: « Dies Domini, sicut fur in nocte, ita veniet; cum enim dixerint: Pax et securitas » etc. Hanc, ut dicit Gregorius[^p99-4], debemus tanto sollicitius formidare, quanto eam non possumus praevidere.

43. (Vers. 20.). *Et cum plicuisset librum* etc. Hic quarto loco, postquam descripta est congruitas in testimonio, notatur *credulitas in populo.* Ad quam exprimendam insinuatur in *docente maturitas,* in *plebe assistente audiendi aviditas,* in *eruditione veritas,* in *eruditis credulitas.*

Ad exprimendam igitur *maturitatem in docente* dicitur: *Et cum plicuisset librum, reddidit ministro et sedit.* Prius stans legerat, accipiendo librum cum humilitate; nunc *reddit* et *sedet* cum auctoritate; *sedere* enim est indicium doctorum et regum; unde Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p99-5]: « Super cathedram Moysi sederunt scribae et Pharisaei »; et Iob vigesimo nono: « Si quando volebam ire ad eos, sedebam primus; cumque sederem quasi rex, circumstante exercitu, eram tamen moerentium consolator ». — Et nota: hic per *plicationem libri* debemus intelligere divinorum mysteriorum occultationem, quia non omnia sunt omnibus revelanda, secundum quod dicit Beda[^p99-6]: « *Plicatum librum ministro reddit,* quia non omnia omnibus communicanda, sed pro captu audientium committit doctori dispensandum verbum »; Danielis duodecimo: « Vade, Daniel, quia clausi sunt signatique sermones usque ad tempus praefinitum ». — Vel ideo plicatum ministro reddidit, quia, secundum illud secundae ad Corinthios tertio[^p99-7], « usque in hodiernum diem, cum legitur Moyses, velamen est positum super cor eorum ».

44. Ad exprimendam vero *in plebe aviditatem* subditur: *Et omnium in synagoga oculi erant intendentes in eum,* quasi desiderantes eum audire sicut sapientem; Sapientiae octavo[^p99-8] dicit sapiens: « In conspectu principum admirabilis ero; tacentem me sustinebunt et loquentem me respicient »; et Iob vigesimo nono: « Qui me audiebant exspectabant sententiam meam et intenti tacebant ad consilium meum »; quia, sicut dicitur Ecclesiastici tertio, « auris bona audiet cum omni concupiscentia sapientiam ».

45. (Vers. 21.). Ad insinuandam vero *veritatem* sive firmitatem *doctrinae* subditur: *Et coepit*[^p99-9] *dicere ad illos: Quia hodie impleta est Scriptura haec in auribus vestris.* Ideo dicitur *impleta Scriptura illa* tunc fuisse, quia, cum verba illa ad Christum pertinerent, nullus in propria persona poterat verbum praemissum vere dicere nisi Christus, qui eam legit *in auribus eorum;* infra ultimo[^p99-10]: « Haec sunt verba, quae dicebam vobis, cum adhuc essem vobiscum, quoniam necesse est impleri omnia, quae scripta sunt in Lege et Prophetis et Psalmis de me ». Unde Christus adimplevit *omnem iustitiam* quantum ad morum honestatem, secundum illud Matthaei tertio[^p99-11]: « Sic decet nos implere omnem iustitiam ». Implevit etiam *omnem Scripturam* fundendo Dei veritatem, sicut Matthaei quinto: « Putas, quia veni solvere Legem, aut Prophetas? Non veni solvere, sed adimplere ». Et ideo dicitur ad Ephesios primo: « Quia omnia in omnibus adimplevit ». Unde *verba* et *signa* veteris legis vacua implevit *veritate* et *gratia.* In cuius signum Ioannis secundo[^p99-12] in primo signo dicitur:
<!-- page 100 -->
« Implete hydrias aqua. Et impleverunt eas usque ad summum ».

46. (Vers. 22.). Ad denotandam vero *credulitatem in multitudine assistente* subditur: *Et omnes testimonium illi dabant; omnes,* id est aliqui de omnibus, testificabantur sanctitatem et innocentiam ipsius; Iob vigesimo nono[^p100-1]: « Auris audiens beatificabat me, et oculus videns testimonium reddebat mihi ». — Et quoniam verbis non poterant eius gratiam explicare, ideo subditur: *Et mirabantur in verbis gratiae, quae procedebant de ore eius*[^p100-2], sicut et doctores mirabantur « super prudentia et responsis eius », supra secundo. Et dicuntur *verba gratiae,* secundum illud Ecclesiastae decimo: « Verba oris sapientis gratia »; et maxime huius, ad quem Petrus dicebat Ioannis sexto: « Verba vitae aeternae habes »; et ipse de se: « Verba, quae ego locutus sum vobis, spiritus et vita sunt ». Et ideo in Psalmo: « Diffusa est gratia in labiis tuis, propterea benedixit te Deus in aeternum ».

## English
<!-- page 95 -->

#### The doctrine of Christ is authenticated from the testimony of Scripture.

26. *And Jesus returned* etc. Above[^p95-11] the Evangelist showed the authority of the doctrine of Christ from the merit of life; here, second, he shows it from the *testimony of Scripture.* He therefore introduces, first, the testimony as authentic for the *erudition of believers;* but second, the authentic testimony for the *confutation of detractors,* there[^p95-12]: *And they said: Is not this the son of Joseph?*

#### First the testimony of Scripture is brought forward for the erudition of believers.

But in the setting forth of the *prophetic testimony* four things are introduced by blessed Luke: the first is
<!-- page 96 -->
*renown in preaching;* the second is *opportuneness in testifying*[^p96-1]: *And he came to Nazareth;* the third is *fittingness in the testimony,* there: *The Spirit of the Lord is upon me* etc.; the fourth is *belief in the people,* and this is noted there: *And when he had folded the book* etc.

As to the first, note that the *renown of the preaching* of Christ is gathered from four things, namely from *fulness of grace, breadth of fame, certitude of doctrine* and *greatness of efficacy.*

27. (Vers. 14.). As to the *fulness of grace* it is said: *And Jesus returned in the power of the Spirit into Galilee. Returned,* namely to preach, because, according to what is said in the Psalm[^p96-2], « The Lord shall give the word to them that preach good tidings[^tr-evangelizare] with great power ». And though he had assumed weakness from us, yet he showed power from himself, according to that saying of Wisdom, the twelfth chapter: « For thou shewest power, who art not believed to be absolute in power ». And it is said pointedly: *In the power of the Spirit.* For perfect preaching ought to be such that it is joined to spiritual power, according to that saying of the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p96-3]: « Our speech and preaching was not in the persuasive words of human wisdom, but in shewing of the spirit and power, that your faith might not be in the wisdom of man, but in the power of God ».

28. As to the *breadth of fame* it is said: *And the fame of him went out through the whole country,* according to that saying of Job, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p96-4]: « With our ears we have heard the fame thereof »; he speaks of the wisdom of Christ, who is « the power of God and wisdom ». With this the other Evangelist agrees; Matthew, the fourth chapter: « His fame went out throughout all Syria ». But the *figure* of this went before in Solomon, of whom it is said in the second of Paralipomenon, the ninth chapter[^p96-5], that « all desired to see his face »; of whom Ecclesiasticus, the forty-seventh chapter: « Thy name was spread abroad to the islands far off ».

29. (Vers. 15.). As to the *certitude of doctrine* there is added: *And he taught in their synagogues,* not in secret, as men do who hold a suspect doctrine; John, the eighteenth chapter[^p96-6]: « I have spoken openly to the world: I have always taught in the synagogues and in the temple, whither all the Jews resort; and in secret I have spoken nothing ». On the contrary the wisdom of heretics is called « earthly, sensual, devilish », which is designated by the *evil woman;* Proverbs, the ninth chapter: « Stolen waters are sweeter, and hid bread is more pleasant ». Whence Christ fulfilled that saying of Proverbs, the fifth chapter[^p96-7]: « Let thy fountains be conveyed abroad, and in the streets divide thy waters ». And therefore it is said in Proverbs, the first chapter: « Wisdom crieth aloud abroad, she uttereth her voice in the streets » etc.

30. As to the *greatness of efficacy* there is added further: *And he was magnified by all,* because all felt the greatness of his efficacy, so much that they could say that saying of the Psalm[^p96-8]: « Thy mercy is magnified even to the heavens, and thy truth even to the clouds », namely in preaching. Nor is it a wonder, because he was the one who is designated by that Solomon, of whom the third of Kings, the tenth chapter: « King Solomon was magnified above all the kings of the whole earth in riches and wisdom » etc.; below, the seventh chapter: « A great prophet is risen up among us ».

31. *And he came to Nazareth* etc. Here is noted the *opportuneness in testifying,* which is regarded in the circumstances concurring to this, that Christ should read in the sight of the synagogue. Whence four things are connoted here. For he is said to have *entered, to have risen up, to have received the book* and *to have unfolded it when received.* These four are introduced for the fourfold instruction of men searching out the truth, namely of *freedom*[^tr-vacare] for meditating, of *promptness* for reading, of *meekness* for learning, of *certitude* for judging.

32. (Vers. 16.). For the instruction, then, of *freedom for meditating* he is said *to have entered the synagogue,* when it says: *And he came to Nazareth, where he was brought up; and he went into the synagogue according to his custom on the sabbath day.* For this was a custom approved by the Law of Moses and by the manner of life of Christ. Hence it is said in Acts, the seventeenth chapter[^p96-9], that « Paul according to custom at Thessalonica went into the synagogue, and for three sabbaths reasoned with them out of the Scriptures ». For that was the time in which they were to be free for the law of God. In which we are taught that when we wish to keep sabbath to God, we ought to be gathered *within* and there to rest; Wisdom, the eighth chapter[^p96-10]: « Entering into my house, I shall rest with her ». If, then, the Sabbath is kept *without,* it is a mockery of demons, according to that saying of Lamentations, the first chapter: « The enemies have seen her, and have mocked at her sabbaths »; and such a thing does not please God, according to that saying of Isaias, the first chapter: « The new moon and the sabbath and your solemnities I will not abide ». And the reason of this is, that
<!-- page 97 -->
they did not enter within, but without, and savoured carnally.

33. But for the instruction of *promptness for reading* there is added: *And he rose up to read.* This *rising* imports promptness and vigilance, which is necessary to one searching out the divine words; whence it is said in Ezechiel, the second chapter[^p97-1]: « Son of man, stand upon thy feet, and I will speak to thee ». Hence it is that it is said to the holy soul in Isaias, the sixtieth chapter: « Arise, be enlightened, O Jerusalem » etc.; and the Apostle to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter: « Rise, thou that sleepest, and arise from the dead, and Christ shall enlighten thee ». At the same time, together with promptness, it designates *humility,* because *he rose up to read,* as a minister, that he might also exercise the office of reader; whence Bede[^p97-2]: « He who had come *to minister and not to be ministered unto* did not disdain to take up the office of reader ».

34. (Vers. 17.). But for the instruction of *meekness for learning* there is added: *And the book of Isaias the prophet was delivered unto him*[^p97-3], which, namely, he received with meekness, not disdaining to use the testimony and authority of Scripture. So too John, Apocalypse, the tenth chapter: « I took the book from the hand of the angel and ate it up, and it was in my mouth as sweet honey ». But the book *of sacred Scripture* had to be *delivered* to him, because, according to that saying of the Apocalypse, the fifth chapter[^p97-4], « the Lamb that was slain is worthy to receive the book and to loose the seven seals thereof », because no other could; and *the book of Isaias,* because, as is said in Isaias, the twenty-ninth chapter, « the vision shall be unto you as the words of a book that is sealed ». And therefore it is said pointedly: *the book of Isaias the prophet was delivered unto him.*

35. Lastly, for the instruction of *certitude for judging* he adds: *And as he unfolded the book;* in which is signified the diligent inquiry of learning, which ought to go before a certain judgement; on account of which it is said in Job, the eighth chapter[^p97-5]: « Inquire of the former generation, and search diligently into the memory of the fathers ». For one is not to proceed to judgement abruptly, but with ripeness; on account of which it is said in Job, the twenty-ninth chapter: « The cause which I knew not, I searched out most diligently »; and the second of Paralipomenon, the nineteenth chapter: « Josaphat, charging the judges, said: Do all things with diligence ». — And since he who seeks diligently finds, therefore there is added: *He found the place where it was written: The Spirit of the Lord* etc.; this is found in Isaias, the sixty-first chapter[^p97-6]. Nor is it a wonder, because, according to what is said in Baruch, the third chapter, « this is our God, and there shall no other be accounted of in comparison of him; he found out all the way of discipline ». Before him it was sought, but it was not found; whence Job, the twenty-eighth chapter: « Where is wisdom found? And which is the place of understanding? » etc.

36. (Vers. 18.). *The Spirit of the Lord is upon me* etc. Here is noted the *fittingness in the testimony.* For it expresses four conditions of excellence, which were in our Saviour. For he is *mediator, schoolmaster, repairer* and *retributor;* and these four are intimated in the passage set forth[^p97-7].

First, then, he is intimated to be *mediator through the mystery of the incarnation,* when it says: *The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, wherefore he hath anointed me; me* there points out the person of Christ in the assumed nature, upon which the Holy Spirit rested, according to that saying of Isaias, the eleventh chapter[^p97-8]: « And the Spirit of the Lord shall rest upon him »; and John, the first chapter: « He upon whom thou shalt see the Spirit descending and remaining, he it is that baptizeth ». And therefore it is said in Acts, the tenth chapter: « Peter said: You know that the word was made throughout all Judea, beginning first from Galilee, after the baptism which John preached, Jesus of Nazareth, how God anointed him with the Holy Spirit » etc. Whence this is the *Saint of saints,* in whose anointing the prophecy of Daniel, the ninth chapter[^p97-9], ought to be fulfilled: « Let vision and prophecy be fulfilled, and let everlasting justice be fulfilled, and let the Saint of saints be anointed ». — And note that the human nature in Christ was conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit and united to the divine nature by his grace. And on account of these two he says that the Spirit of the Lord *is upon him* and *has anointed* him. — Or certainly he is said *to be upon him* as to the surpassing excellence of singular grace, on account of which it is said in John, the third chapter[^p97-10]: « For God doth not give the spirit by measure ». But he is said *to have anointed him* as to the prerogative of priestly and kingly dignity. These regard the assumed nature in Christ; and this was prefigured in the anointing of kings and priests in the old testament.

37. Second, he is intimated to be *schoolmaster through the teaching office of preaching* in what follows: *To preach the gospel to the poor he hath sent me.* For the *Gospel*
<!-- page 98 -->
is a good announcement[^p98-1], whence in our translation it is said: *To announce to the meek he hath sent me.* And with this agrees that saying of Nahum, the first chapter: « Behold upon the mountains the feet of him that bringeth good tidings and that announceth peace »; and Isaias, the fifty-second chapter: « How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him that announceth and preacheth peace, that announceth good, that preacheth salvation ». But this he is said to announce chiefly *to the poor,* because he began his discourse to the poor; Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p98-2]: « Blessed are the poor in spirit », and through the poor he preached poverty to others; Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « The poor have the gospel preached to them ». Nor is it a wonder, because it is said in the Psalm: « By reason of the misery of the needy and the groans of the poor, now will I arise » etc. To these he announces salvation indeed, because elsewhere[^p98-3]: « He shall spare the poor and needy, and he shall save the souls of the poor » etc. And on account of this he is said to be sent by the Spirit and by the Lord; Isaias, the forty-eighth chapter: « As the Lord hath sent me, and his Spirit ». « In this therefore we have known the charity of God », that « he sent his Son », the first of John, the fourth chapter.

38. Third, he is intimated to be *repairer through the remedy of the passion,* when there is added: *To heal the contrite;* which[^p98-4] indeed frees from the *evil of actual* and *original guilt,* from *blindness of reason* and *weakness of will.* — He frees, to be sure, from the *evil of actual guilt;* on account of which it is said: *To heal the contrite of heart,* he hath sent, namely. For the curing of actual guilt there is necessary, together with the passion of Christ, the contrition of penance; the Psalm[^p98-5]: « Who healeth the contrite of heart ». — Or this is said because actual guilt itself breaks the whole strength of the soul; Lamentations, the second chapter: « Great as the sea is thy contrition » etc.; and Proverbs, the fifteenth chapter: « The tongue that is immoderate shall crush the spirit ».

39. (Vers. 19.). But he frees from the *evil of original guilt;* on account of which there is added: *And*[^p98-6] *to preach remission to the captives.* Deservedly by reason of original guilt was the human race held captive by the devil; Isaias, the fifty-second chapter: « Shake thyself from the dust, arise, sit up, O Jerusalem; loose the bonds of thy neck, O captive daughter of Sion. For thus saith the Lord: You were sold gratis, and you shall be redeemed without money » etc.; whence Zacharias, the ninth chapter: « Thou also by the blood of thy testament hast sent forth thy prisoners out of the pit » etc. And this is what is said in Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter[^p98-7]: « I have given thee for a covenant of the people, that thou mightest say to them that are bound: Come forth; and to them that are in darkness: Shew yourselves »; and after: « Yea verily, the captivity shall be taken away from the strong »; because « Christ, ascending on high, led captivity captive », to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter. And this remission was prefigured in Cyrus, who let go the captivity of the children of Israel; Isaias, the forty-fifth chapter[^p98-8]: « He shall let go my captivity, not for a price, nor for presents ».

40. He frees also from the *blindness of reason;* therefore there is added: *Sight to the blind*[^p98-9], he hath sent me, namely, *to preach;* which also he did, as is said in Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « Going, relate to John what you have seen and heard: the blind see » etc.; in which is signified *interior* illumination. Whence John, the ninth chapter[^p98-10]: « For judgement I am come into this world, that they who see not may see », that is, the Gentiles, according to that saying of Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter: « I have given thee to be the light of the Gentiles ». And this is what is said in Isaias, the twenty-ninth chapter: « And in that day the deaf shall hear the words of the book, and out of darkness and obscurity the eyes of the blind shall see ». And this David asked: « Enlighten my eyes ».

41. He frees also from *weakness of will;* on account of which there is added: *And*[^p98-11] *to set at liberty them that are broken, unto remission,* he hath sent, namely. They are *broken* whose will has been weakened for the good through sin, as are foolish sinners; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-first chapter[^p98-12]: « The heart of a fool is like a broken vessel »; and Isaias, the fifty-eighth chapter: « Let them that are broken go free ». But this breaking is repairable while we live here, but becomes irreparable in death, according to that saying of Isaias, the thirtieth chapter[^p98-13]: « It shall be broken small as the potter's vessel with a mighty breaking, and there shall not be found of the pieces thereof a sherd wherein a little fire may be carried from the burning »; because it will not be capable of receiving grace. And this will be in the judgement, according to that saying of the Psalm: « Thou shalt rule them with a rod of iron, and shalt break them in pieces like a potter's vessel ». But now he makes remission, which was figured in the first of Machabees, the tenth chapter, where it is said that under Jonathan, the brother of Simon, many remissions were made to the Jews.

42. Lastly he is intimated to be *remunerator through the judgement of retribution,* in which for *the good* there is made the universal
<!-- page 99 -->
conferring of good. On account of which he says: *To preach the acceptable year of the Lord,* namely to the good, of which in the Psalm[^p99-1]: « Thou shalt bless the crown of the year of thy kindness » etc. This is the *year* signified in the jubilee; Leviticus, the twenty-fifth chapter: « In the year of the jubilee all shall return to their possessions »; or also in the seventh; Deuteronomy, the fifteenth chapter: « In the seventh year thou shalt make a remission ». This year surpasses the year of merit, because the good will be rewarded beyond what is condign; Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-third chapter[^p99-2]: « Why doth year surpass year, day day, light light from the sun »? — In this judgement also for *the wicked* there is made everlasting damnation; therefore there is added: *And the day of retribution,* in which he will render to every one according to his works; Isaias, the sixty-third chapter[^p99-3]: « The day of vengeance is in my heart, the year of my retribution is come ». Of this day it is said in the first to the Thessalonians, the fifth chapter: « The day of the Lord shall so come as a thief in the night; for when they shall say: Peace and security » etc. This, as Gregory says[^p99-4], we ought to dread the more anxiously, the less we are able to foresee it.

43. (Vers. 20.). *And when he had folded the book* etc. Here in the fourth place, after the fittingness in the testimony has been described, there is noted the *belief in the people.* To express which there is intimated in *the one teaching, ripeness;* in *the people standing by, eagerness to hear;* in *the erudition, truth;* in *those schooled, belief.*

To express, then, the *ripeness in the one teaching* it is said: *And when he had folded the book, he restored it to the minister and sat down.* Before, standing, he had read, receiving the book with humility; now he *restores* it and *sits* with authority; for *to sit* is the token of doctors and of kings; whence Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p99-5]: « The scribes and the Pharisees have sitten on the chair of Moses »; and Job, the twenty-ninth chapter: « If at any time I would go to them, I sat first; and when I sat as a king, with the army standing about, I was yet the comforter of them that mourned ». — And note: here by the *folding of the book* we ought to understand the hiding of the divine mysteries, because not all things are to be revealed to all, according to what Bede says[^p99-6]: « *He restores the folded book to the minister,* because not all things are to be communicated to all, but according to the capacity of the hearers he commits the word to the doctor to be dispensed »; Daniel, the twelfth chapter: « Go, Daniel, because the words are shut up and sealed until the appointed time ». — Or he restored it folded to the minister for this reason, that, according to that saying of the second to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p99-7], « even unto this day, when Moses is read, the veil is set upon their heart ».

44. But to express the *eagerness in the people* there is added: *And the eyes of all in the synagogue were fixed on him,* as desiring to hear him as one wise; in Wisdom, the eighth chapter[^p99-8], the wise man says: « In the sight of princes I shall be wonderful; they shall wait for me when I hold my peace, and they shall look upon me when I speak »; and Job, the twenty-ninth chapter: « They that heard me waited for my sentence, and being attentive held their peace for my counsel »; because, as is said in Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter, « a good ear will hear wisdom with all desire ».

45. (Vers. 21.). But to intimate the *truth* or firmness *of the doctrine* there is added: *And he began*[^p99-9] *to say to them: This day is fulfilled this scripture in your ears.* Therefore that *scripture* is said to have been *fulfilled* then, because, since those words pertained to Christ, no one in his own person could truly say the word set down before except Christ, who read it *in their ears;* below, the last chapter[^p99-10]: « These are the words which I spoke to you while I was yet with you, that all things must needs be fulfilled which are written in the Law and the Prophets and the Psalms concerning me ». Whence Christ fulfilled *all justice* as to uprightness of morals, according to that saying of Matthew, the third chapter[^p99-11]: « So it becometh us to fulfil all justice ». He fulfilled also *all Scripture* by pouring forth the truth of God, as in Matthew, the fifth chapter: « Thinkest thou that I came to destroy the Law or the Prophets? I came not to destroy, but to fulfil ». And therefore it is said to the Ephesians, the first chapter: « Because he hath fulfilled all in all ». Whence he filled the empty *words* and *signs* of the old law with *truth* and *grace.* In token of which, in John, the second chapter[^p99-12], at the first sign it is said:
<!-- page 100 -->
« Fill the waterpots with water. And they filled them up to the brim ».

46. (Vers. 22.). But to denote the *belief in the multitude standing by* there is added: *And all gave testimony to him; all,* that is, some of all, testified to his holiness and innocence; Job, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p100-1]: « The ear that heard me blessed me, and the eye that saw me gave witness to me ». — And since they could not set forth his grace in words, therefore there is added: *And they wondered at the words of grace that proceeded from his mouth*[^p100-2], just as the doctors also wondered « at his prudence and his answers », above, the second chapter. And they are called *words of grace,* according to that saying of Ecclesiastes, the tenth chapter: « The words of the mouth of a wise man are grace »; and above all his, to whom Peter said in John, the sixth chapter: « Thou hast the words of eternal life »; and he himself of himself: « The words that I have spoken to you are spirit and life ». And therefore in the Psalm: « Grace is poured abroad in thy lips; therefore hath God blessed thee for ever ».

[^tr-vacare]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin *vacare* holds together several senses that English has to split: being at leisure, being free, being free *for* something and being free *from* something. Bede's *Vacate et videte* (Ps. 45:11, in p. 96 n. 10) keeps the Douay's "Be still and see".
