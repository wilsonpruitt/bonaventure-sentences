# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XIII, Vers. 22-30
## *Et ibat per civitates etc. Supra ostendit, sine cessatione facienda esse bona opera, per manuductionem naturalis exempli; hic ostendit hoc ipsum per manifestationem divini decreti*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 350 -->
48. *Et ibat per civitates* etc. Supra[^p350-1] ostendit, sine cessatione facienda esse bona opera, per manuductionem naturalis exempli; hic ostendit hoc ipsum per *manifestationem divini decreti.* Quod quidem manifestat dupliciter: primo, quoad *severitatem divinae districtionis in iudicando;* secundo, quoad *sublimitatem divinae dispositionis in redimendo,* ibi[^p350-2]: *In ipsa die accesserunt quidam.*

*Severitas autem districtionis in iudicando* ostenditur tripliciter: primo, quantum ad *difficultatem in merito;* secundo, quantum ad *rigiditatem in iudicio,* ibi[^p350-3]: *Cum autem intraverit paterfamilias;* tertio, quantum ad *calamitatem in supplicio,* ibi: *Ibi erit fletus.* — *Difficultatem,* quae *in merito* est, ostendit dupliciter, *exemplo* scilicet in facto et *documento* in verbo.

49. (Vers. 22.). Primo igitur quantum ad *exemplum,* quo ostendit difficultatem ingrediendi in caelum, dicit: *Et ibat per civitates et castella, docens et iter faciens in Ierusalem.* In hoc, quod *ibat et circuibat,* patet, quod sine cessatione *laborabat;* unde poterat dicere illud Psalmi[^p350-4]: « Pauper sum ego et in laboribus a iuventute mea ». Et *exemplum* aliis dabat ad *laborandum;* Proverbiorum sexto: « Discurre, festina, suscita amicum tuum ». In hoc autem quod *in Ierusalem* ibat, patet, quod ad *passionem* currebat; unde Matthaei vigesimo[^p350-5]: « Ecce, ascendimus Ierosolymam, et Filius hominis tradetur principibus sacerdotum et scribis, et condemnabunt eum morte ». Et in hoc exemplum aliis dabat ad *sustinendum;* quia, primae Petri secundo, « Christus passus est pro nobis, vobis relinquens exemplum, ut sequamini vestigia eius ». Unde Iacobi quinto[^p350-6]: « Exemplum accipite, fratres, exitus mali et longanimitatis et laboris et patientiae Prophetas, qui locuti sunt in nomine Domini. Sufferentiam Iob audistis et finem Domini vidistis ». — Ex hoc igitur exemplo Domini, qui tantum laborem et in fine passionem sustinuit, apparet, quod difficilis est aditus caeli; unde infra ultimo[^p350-7]: « Nonne oportuit pati Christum, et ita intrare in gloriam suam »? Et hinc est, quod opportunum est per tribulationes intrare in caelum; Actuum decimo quarto: « Per multas tribulationes oportet nos intrare in regnum Dei ».

50. Et nota, quod in his verbis tria nobis insinuantur exemplo Domini, per quae pervenit quis ad gloriam paradisi, scilicet *actio* bona, *praedicatio* vera et *contemplatio* tranquilla. *Operatio* insinuatur in hoc, quod *ibat per civitates et castella; praedicatio* vero in hoc, quod *ibat docens; contemplatio* in hoc, quod *iter faciens in Ierusalem,* quae est *visio pacis,* per anagogiam; Psalmus[^p350-8]: « Ierusalem, quae aedificatur ut civitas ». « Illuc enim ascenderunt tribus, tribus Domini ». Primum horum spectat ad *prudentiam,* secundum ad *intelligentiam,* tertium autem ad *sapientiam.* Vel primum ad *bonitatem,* secundum ad *disciplinam* et tertium ad *scientiam;* Psalmus[^p350-9]: « Bonitatem et disciplinam et scientiam doce me ».

51. (Vers. 23.). Secundo quantum ad *documentum in verbo,* quo respondet ad quaestionem de paucitate salvandorum, subdit: *Ait autem illi quidam: Domine, si pauci sunt qui salvantur?* Hoc quaerit iste, quia non audierat divinam sententiam, qua dicitur Matthaei vigesimo[^p350-10]: « Multi sunt vocati, pauci vero electi »; in cuius figuram dicitur Michaeae septimo: « Vae mihi! quia factus sum sicut qui colligit in autumno racemos vindemiae ». — Vel ideo hanc quaestionem faciebat, ut occasionem daret docendi verbo quod ostendebat exemplo.

52. (Vers. 23. 24.). Unde subdit: *Dixit autem ad illos*[^p350-11]*: Contendite intrare per angustam portam;* Matthaei septimo: « Quam angusta porta et arcta via est, quae ducit ad vitam, et pauci sunt qui inveniunt eam »! *Porta* ista est Christus; Ioannis decimo[^p350-12]: « Ego sum ostium »; ipse est *via;* Ioannis decimo quarto: « Ego sum via »; « nemo venit ad Patrem nisi per me ». Haec *via arcta* est et *porta angusta,* non propter parvitatem potentiae, sed propter arctitudinem modestiae et rectitudinem iustitiae; unde Chrysostomus[^p350-13]: « Porta arcta est Christus, non potestatis parvitate, sed humilitatis ratione. Christus in se non recipit, nisi qui se exuerint a peccatis et deposuerint omnem sarcinam mundi ». Et propterea Matthaei decimo nono: « Facilius est camelum per foramen acus transire, quam divitem intrare in regnum caelorum ». Ideo ergo *porta* caeli dicitur *angusta,* quia, nisi quis se redigat ad parvitatem et austeritatem, non potest intrare.

53. Et quia pauci sunt tales, ideo subdit: *Quia multi, dico vobis, quaerent intrare;* Glossa[^p350-14]:
<!-- page 351 -->
« Salutis et praemiorum amore provocati »; *et non poterunt,* Glossa: « itineris asperitate perterriti », quia non quaerunt voluntate integra et plena. Nam volunt Christum consequi et nolunt eum sequi, secundum illud Proverbiorum decimo tertio[^p351-1]: « Vult et non vult piger ». Unde Chrysostomus: « Nisi ambulaverit quis per viam, non poterit pervenire ad portam. Si non fuisti nec es in via iustitiae, et putas, te cognoscere Christum, mentiris; sicut qui audit, mel esse dulce, et non gustat, nomen mellis scit, gratiam autem et saporem mellis ignorat »; unde pigri, qui volunt et nolunt, intrare non possunt; similiter nec illi qui volunt simul praesens saeculum et Deum; de quibus Osee quinto[^p351-2]: « In gregibus suis et armentis vadent ad quaerendum Dominum, et non invenient ». Et de talibus dicitur Matthaei sexto: « Nemo potest duobus dominis servire »; « non potestis Deo servire et mammonae ». Hi non vadunt recte in caelum; unde tertii Regum decimo octavo: « Usquequo claudicatis in duas partes? Si Dominus est Deus, sequimini eum; si autem Baal, sequimini illum ».

54. Quia impossibile est cum cupiditatibus terrenorum introire in regnum caelorum, et difficile est has deponere; ideo oportet conari contra appetitum; et propterea dicit: *Contendite intrare per angustam portam,* quasi dicat: impossibile est intrare cum desideriis, quod multi quaerunt; sed vos saltem nitamini, desideriis calcatis. Unde et Matthaei undecimo[^p351-3]: « A diebus Ioannis Baptistae regnum caelorum vim patitur, et violenti rapiunt illud ». Nec obstat, quod ibidem[^p351-4] dicitur: « Iugum meum suave », quia porta et via caelestis arcta est *inchoantibus,* sed dilatatur *proficientibus* et ampla est *pervenientibus.* Pro primis dicebat Psalmus[^p351-5]: « Propter verba labiorum tuorum ego custodivi vias duras »; pro secundis: « Viam mandatorum tuorum cucurri, cum dilatasti cor meum »; pro tertiis: « In via testimoniorum tuorum delectatus sum, sicut in omnibus divitiis ». Pro tribus his dicitur Proverbiorum quarto[^p351-6]: « Iustorum semita quasi lux splendens procedit et crescit usque ad perfectam diem »; et iterum post: « Ducam te per semitas aequitatis, quas cum ingressus fueris, non arctabuntur gressus tui, et currens non habebis offendiculum ».

55. (Vers. 25.). *Cum autem*[^p351-7] *intraverit paterfamilias.* Postquam excitavit ad continue proficiendum in opere bono propter difficultatem in merito, hic secundo excitat ad hoc propter *rigiditatem in futuro iudicio,* quam ostendit dupliciter, videlicet, quia repellet *supplicationem precum* et *allegationem rationum.*

Primo quantum ad *repulsam supplicationis* dicit: *Cum autem intraverit paterfamilias et clauserit ostium,* per iudiciariam districtionem; Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p351-8]: « Quae paratae erant intraverunt cum eo ad nuptias, et clausa est ianua ». — Et nota, quod Christus dicitur *paterfamilias* respectu *iustorum;* unde Matthaei vigesimo[^p351-9]: « Simile est regnum caelorum homini patrifamilias, qui exiit primo mane conducere operarios in vineam suam ». Dicitur autem *paterfamilias,* quia multam habet familiam; in cuius figura Iob primo: « Fuit possessio Iob septem millia ovium, tria millia camelorum, quingenta quoque iuga boum et quingentae asinae, ac familia multa nimis ». — Hic etiam est *iudex* respectu *malorum,* quibus claudit ostium misericordiae; unde dicitur Deuteronomii undecimo[^p351-10]: « Cavete, ne recedatis a Domino, iratusque Dominus claudat caelum, et pluviae non descendant ». — Haec porta aperta est per totum spatium viae, sed clauditur in fine; Ezechielis quadragesimo sexto[^p351-11]: « Porta autem non claudetur usque ad vesperam; et adorabit populus terrae ad ostium portae illius in Sabbatis et in Calendis coram Domino ».

56. Et quia subtractio misericordiae ducit hominem in recognitionem propriae miseriae, et haec iterum ducit ad precum importunitatem; ideo subdit: *Et*[^p351-12] *incipietis foris stare et pulsare ostium,* per serotinam poenitudinem; Sapientiae quinto: « Videntes turbabuntur timore horribili »; « dicentes intra se, poenitentiam agentes »; et ad Hebraeos duodecimo: « Non invenit locum poenitentiae, quamquam cum lacrymis inquisisset eam ». — *Dicentes: Aperi nobis, Domine,* per importunam supplicationem;
<!-- page 352 -->
Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p352-1]: « Novissime veniunt et reliquae virgines dicentes: Domine, Domine, aperi nobis ». — *Et respondens dicet vobis: Nescio vos, unde sitis,* per finalem reprobationem; Proverbiorum primo[^p352-2]: « Tunc clamabunt, et non exaudiam; mane consurgent et non invenient me, eo quod exosam habuerunt disciplinam et timorem Domini non susceperunt »; unde et Proverbiorum sexto: « Zelus et furor viri non parcet in die vindictae nec acquiescet cuiusquam precibus ». — Et nota, quod Dominus dicitur *nos nescire,* non quia non cognoscat per *simplicem notitiam,* cum dicatur Iob duodecimo[^p352-3]: « Ipse novit et decipientem, et eum qui decipitur »; et in Psalmo: « In libro tuo omnes scribentur »; sed quia non novit *notitia approbationis,* quia dicitur secundae ad Timotheum secundo[^p352-4]: « Novit Dominus qui sunt eius »; Iob octavo: « Si absorbuerit eum de loco suo, negabit eum et dicet: Non novi te ».

57. (Vers. 26.). Secundo quantum ad *repulsionem allegationis rationum* subdit: *Tunc incipietis dicere: Manducavimus coram te et bibimus;* ecce, allegatio familiaritatis in *vita; et in plateis nostris docuisti,* quantum ad familiaritatem in *doctrina.* Vel potest primum referri ad *miracula,* quae fecit Christus inter Iudaeos, quando panes multiplicavit, Ioannis sexto[^p352-5], et aquam in vinum convertit, Ioannis secundo; secundum ad *documenta.* Nam isti viderant miracula et audierant documenta; unde Matthaei undecimo: « Vae tibi Corozain! vae tibi Bethsaida! quia, si in Tyro et Sidone factae essent virtutes, quae factae sunt in vobis, olim in cilicio et cinere poenitentiam egissent ». Sic allegant notitiam Christi, quia cognoverunt eius *opera et miracula,* cognoverunt etiam *verba et documenta.* Tales erunt mali Christiani in iudicio, de quibus ad Titum primo[^p352-6]: « Confitentur, se nosse Deum, factis autem negant ».

58. (Vers. 27.). Et quoniam parum valet allegatio *cognitionis* sine allegatione *imitationis et operis,* ideo subdit: *Et dicet vobis: Nescio vos, unde sitis.* Chrysostomus[^p352-7]: « Imaginem meam non cognosco in vobis. Non potestis meorum stipendia militum accipere, qui tyranni vexilla portastis ». Hoc autem dicit Chrysostomus, non quantum ad imaginem *creationis,* de qua in Psalmo[^p352-8]: « In imagine pertransit homo »; sed quantum ad imaginem *recreationis,* de qua Augustinus: « Non novit eos Dominus, dum apud eos characterem fidei et dilectionis non invenit ». Unde *negatio notitiae* non respicit extraneitatem personarum, contra quam ipsi allegant familiaritatem, sed solum diversitatem morum, secundum illud Habacuc primo[^p352-9]: « Mundi sunt oculi tui, Domine, et respicere ad iniquitatem non poteris ». — Ideo subdit: *Discedite a me, omnes operarii iniquitatis;* Psalmus[^p352-10]: « Discedite a me, omnes, qui operamini iniquitatem ». — Ratio autem huius discessionis est distantia inter iustitiam et iniquitatem; secundae ad Corinthios sexto: « Quae participatio iustitiae cum iniquitate? Aut quae societas lucis ad tenebras? Quae autem conventio Christi ad Belial »? Quoniam ergo reprobi erunt iniusti et tenebrosi et viri Belial; Christus autem *iudex* erit *iustus,* erit *lux vera,* erit *summe bonus:* ideo compellet malos descendere in infernum per finale iudicium, dicendo[^p352-11]: « Discedite a me, maledicti, in ignem aeternum, qui paratus est diabolo et angelis eius ». Quoniam ergo homo iudicabitur non secundum magnitudinem cognitionis, sed fortitudinem operis; continue intendendum est operibus bonis; unde ad Romanos secundo[^p352-12]: « Non auditores Legis, sed factores iusti sunt apud Deum »; propter quod in Psalmo: « Semel locutus est Deus; duo haec audivi, quia potestas Dei est, et tibi, Domine, misericordia, quia tu reddes unicuique iuxta opera sua ».

59. (Vers. 28.). *Ibi erit fletus.* Hic iam tertio[^p352-13], postquam excitavit ad continue proficiendum, ostendendo difficultatem in merito et rigiditatem in iudicio, excitat ad hoc ipsum ostendendo *calamitatem in supplicio.* Quod quidem facit dupliciter, scilicet ex consideratione *deiectionis propriae* et ex consideratione *glorificationis alienae.*
<!-- page 353 -->
Primo igitur quantum ad *considerationem deiectionis propriae* dicit: *Ibi erit fletus et stridor dentium,* quantum ad *poenam sensus.* Et tangitur poena *caloris,* ex qua *fletus oculorum,* et poena *frigoris,* ex qua *stridor dentium.* Ideo autem hae duae poenae corporales magis exprimuntur, sive quia istae duae qualitates sunt activae et magis afflictivae; propter quod Iob vigesimo quarto[^p353-1]: « Transibunt ab aquis nivium ad calorem nimium ». Sive quia dupliciter peccatur in corpore, scilicet per concupiscentiam oculorum et per concupiscentiam carnis, unde in his puniuntur; Sapientiae undecimo[^p353-2]: « Per quae peccat quis, per haec et torquetur ». Sive quia *fletus* venit ab interiori, et *stridor dentium* venit ab exteriori; et in his intelligitur universitas poenarum, maxime corporalium. Unde Glossa Bedae[^p353-3]: « Nota, per *fletum,* qui est oculorum, et *stridorem,* qui est dentium, veram resurrectionem intelligi corporum impiorum ». Hic autem fletus[^p353-4] non erit resolutionis humoris, sed per dolorem et gemitum; vel si erit, fiet mirabiliter restitutio humoris, sicut et mirabiliter in tantis cruciatibus habebunt, corpora a spiritibus vegetari.

60. Et quia *poena sensus* coniuncta est *poenae damni,* quae erit amissio societatis Sanctorum in gloria paradisi; ideo subdit: *Cum videritis Abraham et Isaac et Iacob et omnes Prophetas in regno Dei,* id est omnes Patriarchas et Prophetas. Primi fuerunt exemplar vivendi in vita; unde Ioannis octavo[^p353-5]: « Si filii Abrahae estis, opera Abrahae facite »; secundi in vita et doctrina; secundae Petri primo: « Firmiorem habemus propheticum sermonem, cui bene facitis attendentes ». Vel *Patriarchae* fuerunt illi quibus facta est promissio; *Prophetae* illi per quos facta est promissionis promulgatio, secundum illud supra primo[^p353-6]: « Sicut locutus est per os sanctorum qui a saeculo sunt Prophetarum eius ». Et tam illi quam isti excitaverunt et vocaverunt ad regnum Dei intrandum, quod non intrabunt, sed excludentur qui sunt filii carnis et Sanctorum imitatores esse recusaverunt. — Ideo subdit: *Vos autem expelli foras;* Apocalypsis ultimo[^p353-7]: « Foris canes et venefici et impudici et idolis servientes et omnis, qui amat et facit mendacium ». In cuius figuram Genesis vigesimo primo dicitur: « Eiice ancillam et filium eius; non enim erit heres filius ancillae cum filio liberae »; quia dicitur Ioannis octavo: « Servus non manet in domo in aeternum, filius autem manet in aeternum ».

61. (Vers. 29.). Secundo quantum ad *considerationem glorificationis alienae* subdit: *Et venient ab oriente et occidente, ab*[^p353-8] *aquilone et austro,* hoc est de universitate gentium ex omni parte mundi congregatarum. Iudaeis enim repulsis propter suam carnalitatem, convocavit Dominus gentes tanquam filios spirituales; unde dicitur Ioannis decimo[^p353-9]: « Alias oves habeo, quae non sunt de hoc ovili, et illas oportet me adducere ». Hae congregatae sunt ab omni parte mundi per ministerium Apostolorum, secundum illud Psalmi: « Omnes gentes, quascumque fecisti, venient et adorabunt coram te, Domine, et glorificabunt nomen tuum »; et Isaiae quadragesimo tertio: « Ab oriente adducam semen tuum et ab occidente congregabo te; dicam aquiloni: da; et austro: noli prohibere ». Ideo dicuntur de tam distantibus partibus convocari propter diversitatem morum, a quibus vocantur ad unitatem caritatis et felicitatis. — Propter quod subdit: *Et accumbent in regno Dei;* Isaiae vigesimo quinto[^p353-10]: « Faciet Dominus in monte hoc omnibus populis convivium pinguium, convivium vindemiae, pinguium medullatorum, vindemiae defaecatae ».

62. (Vers. 30.). Et quia hoc erit ad confusionem reproborum et gloriam electorum, ideo subdit: *Et ecce, sunt novissimi qui erant*[^p353-11] *primi, et primi qui erant novissimi. Primi* sunt novissimi propter superbiam, et *novissimi* primi propter humilitatem; quia dicitur infra decimo octavo[^p353-12]: « Omnis, qui se humiliat, exaltabitur, et qui se exaltat humiliabitur ». Quare hoc? Quia in Psalmo dicitur: « Excelsus Dominus et humilia respicit, et alta a longe cognoscit ». Exemplum huius maxime apparet in
<!-- page 354 -->
duplici populo, scilicet Iudaeorum, qui sunt excaecati, et gentilium, qui sunt electi; ad Romanos undecimo[^p354-1]: « Nolo enim vos ignorare, fratres, mysterium hoc, quia caecitas ex parte contigit in Israel, donec plenitudo gentium intraret, et sic omnis Israel salvus fieret ». Exemplum etiam huius apparet in Iuda, qui fuit Apostolus et damnatus, et in latrone, qui fuit salvatus, infra vigesimo tertio[^p354-2]. Et exemplo huius omnes excitantur ad timorem divinum, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios decimo: « Qui se existimat stare videat, ne cadat ».

63. Ex hac igitur consideratione futuri iudicii inducimur ad insistendum continue operibus bonis propter triplicem rationem praemissam: primo, quia *difficile est in conspectu Domini iustificari* propter difficultatem in merito; unde Psalmus[^p354-3]: « Non intres in iudicium cum servo tuo, Domine, quia non iustificabitur in conspectu tuo omnis vivens »; et primae Petri quarto: « Et si iustus vix salvabitur, impius et peccator ubi parebunt »? — Secundo, quia *impossibile est excusari* propter rigiditatem in iudicio; Proverbiorum vigesimo octavo[^p354-4]: « Qui declinat aurem suam, ne audiat legem, oratio eius fiet exsecrabilis »; et Iob quadragesimo primo: « Non parcam ei, verbis potentibus et ad deprecandum compositis ». — Tertio, quia *necesse habet tribulari* propter calamitatem in supplicio; Apocalypsis vigesimo[^p354-5]: « Qui non inventus est in libro vitae scriptus missus est in stagnum ignis et sulphuris »; Matthaei vigesimo secundo dicitur de eo qui non habebat vestem nuptialem: « Ligatis manibus et pedibus eius, mittite eum in tenebras exteriores; ibi erit fletus et stridor dentium ». — Et propterea bonum est consilium Sapientis Ecclesiastae nono[^p354-6]: « Quodcumque facere potest manus tua, instanter operare; quia non est opus nec ratio nec sapientia nec scientia apud inferos, quo tu properas ».

## English
<!-- page 350 -->
48. *And he went through the cities* etc. Above[^p350-1] he showed that good works are to be done without cessation, through the leading by the hand of a natural example; here he shows this same thing through *the manifestation of a divine decree.* Which indeed he manifests in two ways: first, as to *the severity of the divine strictness in judging;* secondly, as to *the sublimity of the divine disposition in redeeming,* there[^p350-2]: *In the same day there came some.*

*Now the severity of the strictness in judging* is shown in three ways: first, as to *the difficulty in merit;* secondly, as to *the rigidity in judgment,* there[^p350-3]: *But when the master of the house shall be gone in;* thirdly, as to *the calamity in punishment,* there: *There shall be weeping.* — *The difficulty* which is *in merit* he shows in two ways, namely by *example* in deed and by *instruction* in word.

49. (Vers. 22.). First, then, as to the *example* by which he shows the difficulty of entering into heaven, he says: *And he went through the cities and towns, teaching and making his journey to Jerusalem.* In this, that *he went and went about,* it is plain that he *laboured* without cessation; whence he could say that of the Psalm[^p350-4]: « I am poor, and in labours from my youth ». And he gave others an *example* for *labouring;* Proverbs, the sixth chapter: « Run about, make haste, stir up thy friend ». But in this, that he went *to Jerusalem,* it is plain that he was running to his *passion;* whence Matthew, the twentieth chapter[^p350-5]: « Behold, we go up to Jerusalem, and the Son of man shall be betrayed to the chief priests and the scribes, and they shall condemn him to death ». And in this he gave others an example for *bearing;* because, in the first of Peter, the second chapter, « Christ suffered for us, leaving you an example, that you should follow his steps ». Whence James, the fifth chapter[^p350-6]: « Take, brethren, for an example of suffering evil, and of long-suffering, and of labour and patience, the prophets, who spoke in the name of the Lord. You have heard of the patience of Job, and you have seen the end of the Lord ». — From this example of the Lord, then, who bore so great a labour and at the end the passion, it appears that the entrance of heaven is difficult; whence below, in the last chapter[^p350-7]: « Ought not Christ to have suffered, and so to enter into his glory »? And hence it is that it is fitting to enter into heaven through tribulations; Acts, the fourteenth chapter: « Through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God ».

50. And note that in these words three things are suggested to us by the example of the Lord, through which one comes to the glory of paradise, namely good *action,* true *preaching* and tranquil *contemplation.* *Operation* is suggested in this, that *he went through the cities and towns; preaching,* in this, that *he went teaching; contemplation,* in this, that *he was making his journey to Jerusalem,* which is *the vision of peace,* by anagogy; the Psalm[^p350-8]: « Jerusalem, which is built as a city ». « For thither did the tribes go up, the tribes of the Lord ». The first of these pertains to *prudence,* the second to *intelligence,* but the third to *wisdom.* Or the first to *goodness,* the second to *discipline* and the third to *knowledge;* the Psalm[^p350-9]: « Teach me goodness and discipline and knowledge ».

51. (Vers. 23.). Secondly, as to *the instruction in word,* by which he answers the question concerning the fewness of those to be saved, he adds: *And a certain man said to him: Lord, are they few that are saved?* This man asks this because he had not heard the divine sentence by which it is said, Matthew, the twentieth chapter[^p350-10]: « Many are called, but few chosen »; in figure of which it is said, Micheas, the seventh chapter: « Woe is me! for I am become as one that gleaneth in the autumn the grapes of the vintage ». — Or he put this question so that he might give occasion for teaching by word what he was showing by example.

52. (Vers. 23. 24.). Whence he adds: *But he said to them*[^p350-11]*: Strive to enter by the narrow gate;* Matthew, the seventh chapter: « How narrow is the gate, and strait is the way that leadeth to life, and few there are that find it »! This *gate* is Christ; John, the tenth chapter[^p350-12]: « I am the door »; he is the *way;* John, the fourteenth chapter: « I am the way »; « no man cometh to the Father, but by me ». This *way is strait* and the *gate narrow,* not on account of smallness of power, but on account of the straitness of modesty and the rectitude of justice; whence Chrysostom[^p350-13]: « Christ is the strait gate, not by smallness of power, but by reason of humility. Christ does not receive into himself any but those who have stripped themselves of sins and have laid down every pack of the world ». And therefore Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « It is easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle, than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of heaven ». Therefore, then, the *gate* of heaven is called *narrow,* because unless one bring himself down to smallness and austerity, he cannot enter.

53. And because such are few, therefore he adds: *For many, I say to you, shall seek to enter;* the Gloss[^p350-14]:
<!-- page 351 -->
« drawn on by the love of salvation and of rewards »; *and shall not be able,* the Gloss: « terrified by the roughness of the journey », because they do not seek with an entire and full will. For they wish to attain Christ and do not wish to follow him, according to that of Proverbs, the thirteenth chapter[^p351-1]: « The sluggard willeth and willeth not ». Whence Chrysostom: « Unless one have walked along the way, he will not be able to come to the gate. If you have not been, nor are, on the way of justice, and you think that you know Christ, you lie; just as he who hears that honey is sweet, and does not taste it, knows the name of honey, but is ignorant of the grace and the savour of honey »; whence the sluggards, who will and will not, cannot enter; likewise neither can those who wish at once for the present world and for God; of whom Osee, the fifth chapter[^p351-2]: « With their flocks and with their herds they shall go to seek the Lord, and shall not find him ». And of such it is said, Matthew, the sixth chapter: « No man can serve two masters »; « you cannot serve God and mammon ». These do not go rightly into heaven; whence the third of Kings, the eighteenth chapter: « How long do you halt between two sides? If the Lord be God, follow him; but if Baal, follow him ».

54. Because it is impossible to enter into the kingdom of heaven with the cupidities of earthly things, and it is difficult to lay these down, therefore one must strive against the appetite; and for that reason he says: *Strive to enter by the narrow gate,* as if he were saying: it is impossible to enter with the desires, which many seek; but do you at least struggle, the desires trampled underfoot. Whence also Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p351-3]: « From the days of John the Baptist the kingdom of heaven suffereth violence, and the violent bear it away ». Nor does it stand in the way that it is said in the same place[^p351-4]: « My yoke is sweet », because the heavenly gate and way is strait *for those who begin,* but is widened *for those who make progress* and is ample *for those who arrive.* For the first the Psalm said[^p351-5]: « For the sake of the words of thy lips, I have kept hard ways »; for the second: « I have run the way of thy commandments, when thou didst enlarge my heart »; for the third: « I have been delighted in the way of thy testimonies, as in all riches ». For these three it is said, Proverbs, the fourth chapter[^p351-6]: « The path of the just, as a shining light, goeth forwards and increaseth even to perfect day »; and again after: « I will lead thee by the paths of equity, which when thou shalt have entered, thy steps shall not be straitened, and when thou runnest thou shalt not meet a stumblingblock ».

55. (Vers. 25.). *But when*[^p351-7] *the master of the house shall be gone in.* After he had stirred up to making progress continually in good work on account of the difficulty in merit, here secondly he stirs up to this on account of *the rigidity in the future judgment,* which he shows in two ways, namely, because it will repel *the supplication of prayers* and *the allegation of reasons.*

First, as to *the repulse of supplication,* he says: *But when the master of the house shall be gone in and shall shut the door,* through judicial strictness; Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p351-8]: « They that were ready went in with him to the marriage, and the door was shut ». — And note that Christ is called *master of the house* in respect of *the just;* whence Matthew, the twentieth chapter[^p351-9]: « The kingdom of heaven is like to a man that is a master of a house, who went out early in the morning to hire labourers into his vineyard ». And he is called *master of the house* because he has a great family; in figure of which Job, the first chapter: « The possession of Job was seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, and five hundred yoke of oxen, and five hundred she-asses, and a family exceeding great ». — He is also *judge* in respect of *the wicked,* for whom he shuts the door of mercy; whence it is said, Deuteronomy, the eleventh chapter[^p351-10]: « Beware lest you depart from the Lord, and the Lord being angry shut up heaven, and the rain come not down ». — This gate is open through the whole space of the way, but it is shut at the end; Ezechiel, the forty-sixth chapter[^p351-11]: « But the gate shall not be shut till the evening; and the people of the land shall adore at the door of that gate on the Sabbaths and on the new moons before the Lord ».

56. And because the withdrawal of mercy leads a man into the recognition of his own misery, and this in turn leads to importunity of prayers; therefore he adds: *And*[^p351-12] *you shall begin to stand without and knock at the door,* through late repentance; Wisdom, the fifth chapter: « Seeing it, they shall be troubled with terrible fear »; « saying within themselves, repenting »; and to the Hebrews, the twelfth chapter: « He found no place of repentance, although with tears he had sought it ». — *Saying: Lord, open to us,* through importunate supplication;
<!-- page 352 -->
Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p352-1]: « At last come also the other virgins, saying: Lord, Lord, open to us ». — *And he answering shall say to you: I know you not, whence you are,* through final reprobation; Proverbs, the first chapter[^p352-2]: « Then shall they cry, and I will not hear; they shall rise in the morning and shall not find me, because they have hated discipline and received not the fear of the Lord »; whence also Proverbs, the sixth chapter: « The jealousy and rage of the husband will not spare in the day of revenge, nor will he yield to any man's prayers ». — And note that the Lord is said *not to know us,* not because he does not know by *simple knowledge,* since it is said, Job, the twelfth chapter[^p352-3]: « He knoweth both the deceiver and him that is deceived »; and in the Psalm: « In thy book all shall be written »; but because he does not know by *the knowledge of approbation,* because it is said, the second to Timothy, the second chapter[^p352-4]: « The Lord knoweth who are his »; Job, the eighth chapter: « If he pluck him out of his place, he shall deny him, and shall say: I know thee not ».

57. (Vers. 26.). Secondly, as to *the repulsion of the allegation of reasons,* he adds: *Then you shall begin to say: We have eaten and drunk in thy presence;* behold, the allegation of familiarity in *life; and thou hast taught in our streets,* as to familiarity in *doctrine.* Or the first can be referred to the *miracles* which Christ did among the Jews, when he multiplied the loaves, John, the sixth chapter[^p352-5], and turned water into wine, John, the second chapter; the second to the *lessons.* For these had seen the miracles and had heard the lessons; whence Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « Woe to thee, Corozain! woe to thee, Bethsaida! for if in Tyre and Sidon had been wrought the miracles that have been wrought in you, they had long ago done penance in sackcloth and ashes ». Thus they allege their knowledge of Christ, because they knew his *works and miracles,* they knew also his *words and lessons.* Such will be bad Christians in the judgment, of whom to Titus, the first chapter[^p352-6]: « They profess that they know God, but in their works they deny him ».

58. (Vers. 27.). And since the allegation of *knowledge* avails little without the allegation of *imitation and of work,* therefore he adds: *And he shall say to you: I know you not, whence you are.* Chrysostom[^p352-7]: « My image I do not recognise in you. You cannot receive the wages of my soldiers, who have carried the standards of a tyrant ». Now Chrysostom says this not as to the image of *creation,* of which in the Psalm[^p352-8]: « In an image man passeth »; but as to the image of *re-creation,* of which Augustine: « The Lord does not know them, while he does not find among them the character of faith and dilection ». Whence *the denial of knowledge* does not regard the strangeness of the persons, against which they themselves allege familiarity, but only the diversity of morals, according to that of Habacuc, the first chapter[^p352-9]: « Thy eyes are clean, O Lord, and thou canst not look on iniquity ». — Therefore he adds: *Depart from me, all ye workers of iniquity;* the Psalm[^p352-10]: « Depart from me, all ye that work iniquity ». — Now the reason of this departure is the distance between justice and iniquity; the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter: « What participation hath justice with iniquity? Or what fellowship hath light with darkness? And what concord hath Christ with Belial »? Since, then, the reprobate will be unjust and darksome and men of Belial, but Christ will be a *just judge,* will be *the true light,* will be *supremely good:* therefore he will compel the wicked to go down into hell through the final judgment, saying[^p352-11]: « Depart from me, you cursed, into everlasting fire, which was prepared for the devil and his angels ». Since, then, a man will be judged not according to the greatness of his knowledge, but the fortitude of his work, one must attend continually to good works; whence to the Romans, the second chapter[^p352-12]: « Not the hearers of the Law, but the doers are just before God »; for which reason in the Psalm: « God hath spoken once, these two things have I heard, that power belongeth to God, and mercy to thee, O Lord; for thou wilt render to every man according to his works ».

59. (Vers. 28.). *There shall be weeping.* Here now thirdly[^p352-13], after he had stirred up to making progress continually by showing the difficulty in merit and the rigidity in judgment, he stirs up to this same thing by showing *the calamity in punishment.* Which indeed he does in two ways, namely from the consideration of *one's own casting down* and from the consideration of *another's glorification.*
<!-- page 353 -->
First, then, as to *the consideration of one's own casting down,* he says: *There shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth,* as to *the pain of sense.* And there is touched the pain of *heat,* from which comes *the weeping of the eyes,* and the pain of *cold,* from which comes *the gnashing of teeth.* Now these two bodily pains are expressed rather than others, either because these two qualities are active and more afflictive; for which reason Job, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p353-1]: « They shall pass from the snow waters to excessive heat ». Or because one sins in two ways in the body, namely through the concupiscence of the eyes and through the concupiscence of the flesh, whence they are punished in these; Wisdom, the eleventh chapter[^p353-2]: « By what things a man sinneth, by the same also he is tormented ». Or because *weeping* comes from within, and *the gnashing of teeth* comes from without; and in these is understood the totality of pains, chiefly of the bodily ones. Whence the Gloss of Bede[^p353-3]: « Note that by *weeping,* which is of the eyes, and by *gnashing,* which is of the teeth, the true resurrection of the bodies of the impious is understood ». But this weeping[^p353-4] will not be of the dissolving of humour, but through pain and groaning; or if it shall be, there will be a marvellous restoration of the humour, just as also, marvellously, in such great torments they will have their bodies quickened by their spirits.

60. And because *the pain of sense* is joined to *the pain of loss,* which will be the loss of the society of the Saints in the glory of paradise; therefore he adds: *When you shall see Abraham and Isaac and Jacob and all the prophets in the kingdom of God,* that is, all the Patriarchs and Prophets. The first were the exemplar of living in life; whence John, the eighth chapter[^p353-5]: « If you be the children of Abraham, do the works of Abraham »; the second in life and doctrine; the second of Peter, the first chapter: « We have the more firm prophetical word, whereunto you do well to attend ». Or the *Patriarchs* were those to whom the promise was made; the *Prophets,* those through whom the promulgation of the promise was made, according to that above, in the first chapter[^p353-6]: « As he spoke by the mouth of his holy prophets, who are from the beginning ». And both the former and the latter stirred up and called to entering the kingdom of God, which they shall not enter, but they shall be shut out who are children of the flesh and refused to be imitators of the Saints. — Therefore he adds: *And you yourselves thrust out;* the Apocalypse, the last chapter[^p353-7]: « Without are dogs and sorcerers and the unchaste and servers of idols, and every one that loveth and maketh a lie ». In figure of which it is said, Genesis, the twenty-first chapter: « Cast out the bondwoman and her son; for the son of the bondwoman shall not be heir with the son of the free woman »; because it is said, John, the eighth chapter: « The servant abideth not in the house for ever, but the son abideth for ever ».

61. (Vers. 29.). Secondly, as to *the consideration of another's glorification,* he adds: *And there shall come from the east and the west, from*[^p353-8] *the north and the south,* that is, from the totality of the nations gathered together from every part of the world. For the Jews having been repelled on account of their carnality, the Lord called together the nations as spiritual children; whence it is said, John, the tenth chapter[^p353-9]: « Other sheep I have, that are not of this fold, and them also I must bring ». These were gathered together from every part of the world through the ministry of the Apostles, according to that of the Psalm: « All the nations thou hast made shall come and adore before thee, O Lord, and they shall glorify thy name »; and Isaias, the forty-third chapter: « I will bring thy seed from the east, and gather thee from the west; I will say to the north: Give; and to the south: Keep not back ». They are said to be called together from parts so distant on account of the diversity of morals, from which they are called to the unity of charity and of felicity. — For which reason he adds: *And shall sit down in the kingdom of God;* Isaias, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p353-10]: « The Lord shall make unto all people in this mountain a feast of fat things, a feast of wine, of fat things full of marrow, of wine purified from the lees ».

62. (Vers. 30.). And because this will be to the confusion of the reprobate and the glory of the elect, therefore he adds: *And behold, they are last who were*[^p353-11] *first, and first who were last. The first* are last on account of pride, and *the last* first on account of humility; because it is said below, the eighteenth chapter[^p353-12]: « Every one that humbleth himself shall be exalted, and he that exalteth himself shall be humbled ». Why this? Because it is said in the Psalm: « The Lord is high, and looketh on the low; and the high he knoweth afar off ». The example of this appears most of all in
<!-- page 354 -->
the twofold people, namely of the Jews, who are blinded, and of the Gentiles, who are elect; to the Romans, the eleventh chapter[^p354-1]: « For I would not have you ignorant, brethren, of this mystery, that blindness in part has happened in Israel, until the fulness of the Gentiles should come in, and so all Israel should be saved ». The example of this appears also in Judas, who was an Apostle and damned, and in the thief, who was saved, below, the twenty-third chapter[^p354-2]. And by the example of this all are stirred up to the divine fear, according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the tenth chapter: « He that thinketh himself to stand, let him take heed lest he fall ».

63. From this consideration of the future judgment, then, we are led to insist continually on good works on account of the threefold reason set forth before: first, because *it is difficult to be justified in the sight of the Lord* on account of the difficulty in merit; whence the Psalm[^p354-3]: « Enter not into judgment with thy servant, O Lord, for in thy sight no man living shall be justified »; and the first of Peter, the fourth chapter: « And if the just man shall scarcely be saved, where shall the ungodly and the sinner appear »? — Secondly, because *it is impossible to be excused* on account of the rigidity in judgment; Proverbs, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p354-4]: « He that turneth away his ear from hearing the law, his prayer shall be an abomination »; and Job, the forty-first chapter: « I will not spare him, his mighty words and framed to make supplication ». — Thirdly, because *one must needs be afflicted* on account of the calamity in punishment; the Apocalypse, the twentieth chapter[^p354-5]: « Whosoever was not found written in the book of life was cast into the pool of fire and brimstone »; Matthew, the twenty-second chapter, it is said of him who had not on a wedding garment: « Bind his hands and feet, and cast him into the exterior darkness; there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth ». — And therefore good is the counsel of the Wise Man, Ecclesiastes, the ninth chapter[^p354-6]: « Whatsoever thy hand is able to do, do it earnestly; for neither work, nor reason, nor wisdom, nor knowledge is in hell, whither thou art hastening ».
