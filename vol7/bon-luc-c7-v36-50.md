# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. VII, Vers. 36-50
## *Tertio declaratur sublimitas Doctoris propter affluentiam pietatis tripliciter*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 182 -->

#### Tertio declaratur sublimitas Doctoris propter affluentiam pietatis tripliciter.

62. *Rogabat autem illum quidam Pharisaeus*[^p182-4]. Supra ostendit Evangelista sublimitatem Domini Salvatoris quantum ad excellentiam potestatis et quantum ad evidentiam veritatis; hic tertio ostendit quantum ad *affluentiam pietatis.* Ostenditur autem haec pietatis affluentia in Domino Iesu magna in peccatricis *susceptione,* maior in *approbatione,* sed maxima in *absolutione.* Suscepit enim accedentem, commendavit diligentem et absolvit poenitentem.

Introducitur ergo primo in hac parte benigna *susceptio peccatricis accedentis;* secundo vero, benignior *approbatio diligentis,* ibi[^p182-5]: *Et respondens Iesus dixit ad illum* etc.; tertio vero, benignissima *absolutio poenitentis confitentis,* ibi: *Dixit autem ad illam: Remittuntur tibi peccata.*

Quantum ad primum notandum, quod *susceptio peccatricis* ad Dominum accedentis ostenditur esse magnae pietatis et misericordiae, tum propter *solemnitatem convivii,* in quo eam suscepit; tum propter *familiaritatem obsequii,* ad quod admisit; tum propter *temeritatem alieni iudicii,* ob quod[^p182-6] eam non dimisit.

63. (Vers. 36.). Primo ergo quantum ad *solemnitatem convivii,* in quo peccatrix fuit pie a Domino suscepta, dicitur: *Rogabat autem illum quidam Pharisaeus, ut manducaret cum illo;* non est dubium, quin Dominum Iesum; et recte, secundum consilium et propositum Sapientis; Sapientiae octavo[^p182-7]: « Proposui hanc », id est sapientiam, Christum, « adducere ad convivendum, sciens, quoniam mecum communicabit de bonis suis ». Hic autem Pharisaeus iam non erat a Domino divisus, sed magis unitus, et ideo secure Dominum rogabat et ad convivium invitabat, secundum illud Iob vigesimo secundo[^p182-8]: « Si reversus fueris ad Omnipotentem, aedificaberis »; et sequitur: « Rogabis eum, et exaudiet te ». — Quod et iste consecutus est; unde et subditur: *Et ingressus domum Pharisaei discubuit.* Resedit ad manducandum et sic implevit illud visibiliter, quod spiritualiter promittit in Apocalypsis tertio[^p182-9]: « Si quis aperuerit mihi, intrabo ad illum et coenabo cum illo, et ipse mecum ». Nec mirum, quia, sicut ipse dicit Proverbiorum octavo, « deliciae meae esse cum filiis hominum ». Hoc dicit quantum ad nimietatem caritatis, propter quam homo factus est et humanas indigentias libenter suscepit et pertulit inter homines, secundum illud Baruch tertio[^p182-10]: « Post haec in terris visus est et cum hominibus conversatus est ». Haec ergo introducta sunt propter solemnitatem convivii.

64. (Vers. 37.). Quantum ad *familiaritatem obsequii,* ad quod fuit admissa, subditur: *Et ecce, mulier, quae erat in civitate peccatrix,* cuius infamia innotuerat toti civitati, ita ut posset ei dici illud Ieremiae tertio[^p182-11]: « Frons mulieris meretricis facta est tibi, noluisti erubescere ». Haec est illa mulier, de qua dicitur Ecclesiastae septimo: « Inveni amariorem morte mulierem, quae laqueus est venatorum, et sagena cor eius »; et hoc, quia fornicaria manifesta, sicut dicit Ravennas[^p182-12], quod « infamia eius totam laeserat civitatem », ita ut impletum esset in ea quod comminatur Dominus per Amos ad Amasiam sacerdotem, Amos septimo: « Uxor tua in civitate fornicabitur ». Et ideo merito dicitur *peccatrix* et ratione infamiae et ratione frequentiae, secundum illud Ecclesiastici tertio[^p183-1]: « Cor nequam gravabitur in doloribus, et peccator adiecit ad peccandum »; et Apocalypsis ultimo: « Qui in sordibus est sordescat adhuc ». Non solum autem culpam iteraverat, sed et diversis modis peccaverat; unde super hoc quod dicitur Marci ultimo[^p183-2], quod « eiecerat Christus de Maria septem daemonia », Gregorius: « Per septenarium recte universitas designatur. Septem ergo daemonia Maria habuit, quae universis vitiis plena fuit ».
<!-- page 183 -->

65. Quoniam ergo sic erat peccatrix et immunda, merito deberet repelli, maxime de loco convivii; et tamen propter pietatem Domini, quam cognovit, attracta fuit; unde et subditur: *Ut cognovit, quod Iesus*[^p183-3] *accubuisset in domo Pharisaei, attulit alabastrum unguenti,* id est pyxidem seu vas alabastri, quod « est de quodam genere marmoris, in quo incorrupta servantur unguenta ». Hoc attulit peccatrix ad domum Pharisaei, in qua Christum cognovit esse et a quo suscepta fuit, secundum illud Psalmi[^p183-4]: « Deus in domibus eius cognoscetur, cum suscipiet eam ». *Attulit,* inquam, unguentum *humilitatis,* ubi Christus accubuit, ut impleretur illud Canticorum primo: « Dum esset rex in accubitu suo, nardus mea dedit odorem suum ». Haec autem aromata sive unguenta, quae attulit Magdalena Christo, designata sunt secundi Paralipomenon nono[^p183-5]: « Non fuerunt aromata talia, qualia haec, quae dedit regina Saba Salomoni ». — Vel Magdalena Salvatori unguenta attulit propter impendendum familiare obsequium, ad quod admissa fuit, scilicet ad exhibendum Christo ex *devotione* quod sibi exhibuerat ex *libidine,* hoc est ad abluendum, detergendum, deosculandum et ungendum sacratissimam et mundissimam carnem Christi, ad quod admissa fuit propter affectum *poenitentiae* et *benevolentiae.*

66. (Vers. 38.). Quantum ad affectum *poenitentiae* dicitur: *Et stans retro secus pedes eius, lacrymis coepit rigare pedes eius,* per dolorem. In poenitente enim anima debet esse *pudor, timor* et *dolor.* *Pudor* erat, quia stabat *retro; timor,* quia *secus pedes; dolor,* quia *lacrymis rigabat,* sicut in illo publicano infra decimo octavo[^p183-6]: « Publicanus autem a longe stans, nolebat nec oculos ad caelum levare, sed percutiebat pectus suum dicens: Deus, propitius esto mihi peccatori »; Ieremiae trigesimo primo: « Converte me, Domine, et convertar, quia tu Deus meus. Postquam convertisti me, egi poenitentiam, et postquam ostendisti mihi, percussi femur meum. Confusus sum et erubui » etc.; ubi tanguntur tria praemissa. — Quantum autem ad affectum *benevolentiae* additur: *Et capillis capitis sui tergebat, et osculabatur pedes eius, et ungebat unguento,* ubi ostenditur dilectio benevolentiae *pura, affectuosa* et *devota.* Debet enim caritas procedere « de corde puro[^p183-7] ». Magis autem curabat de infima parte corporis sui quam de suprema. Iam enim non quaerebat placere mundo, sed soli Christo, ut posset dicere illud Canticorum octavo: « Quis det te mihi fratrem meum, ut inveniam te solum foris et deosculer te, et iam me nemo despiciat »?

67. Et in his duobus consistit *perfecta conversio* ad Christum. Sicut enim in peccato est conversio per concupiscentiam et aversio per superbiam[^p183-8]; sic in remedio est aversio a peccato per *poenitentiam* et conversio ad Deum per *benevolentiam.* Et vere haec mulier fuit perfecte conversa et reversa, quia, ut dicit Gregorius, « quot in se habuit oblectamenta, tot de se invenit holocausta ». — Solent enim peccatrices mulieres in his quatuor maxime delinquere, scilicet in *nutibus oculorum;* Isaiae tertio[^p183-9]: « Nutibus oculorum ibant »; *superfluitatibus capillorum;* primae Petri tertio: « Non sit exterius capillatura »; in *voluptatibus osculorum;* Proverbiorum septimo: « Apprehensumque deosculatur iuvenem »; *suavitatibus unguentorum,* sicut dicitur Esther secundo, quod puellae regis « ungebantur oleo myrrhino » etc. — Et ideo perfecta poenitens *oculis* serviebat ad lacrymandum, ita ut posset ei Christus dicere illud quarti Regum vigesimo[^p183-10]: « Audivi orationem tuam: vidi lacrymam tuam »; *capillis* ad abstergendum, ut sic posset ei dici illud Canticorum quarto: « Vulnerasti cor meum in uno crine colli tui »; *ore* ad osculandum, ita ut posset ei dici a Christo illud Canticorum quarto: « Favus distillans labia tua »; *unguentis* serviebat ad ungendum, ut posset sibi dici: « In odorem unguentorum tuorum curremus ».
<!-- page 184 -->

68. (Vers. 39.). Tertio quantum ad *temeritatem alieni iudicii,* propter quod non fuit repulsa, dicit: *Videns autem Pharisaeus, qui vocaverat eum,* scilicet ad convivium; *videns,* inquam, oculo non simplici, sed nequam, secundum illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo primo[^p184-1]: « Memento, quoniam malus est oculus nequam. Nequius oculo quid creatum est »? Et vere *nequam,* quia, secundum illud Ecclesiastici undecimo, « bona in mala convertens insidiatur et in electis imponet maculam ». Et ideo talium convivium non est multum acceptum, secundum illud Proverbiorum vigesimo tertio: « Ne comedas cum homine invido neque desideres cibos eius, quoniam in similitudinem harioli et coniectoris aestimat quod ignorat. Comede et bibe, dicet tibi, et mens eius non est tecum ». Et talis erat iste Pharisaeus; quod patet per illud quod subditur: *Ait intra se*[^p184-2], scilicet cogitando sicut hypocrita, de quo Iob decimo quinto: « Venter eius praeparat dolos ». — Unde sequitur iudicium: *Hic si esset Propheta, sciret utique, quae et qualis est mulier, quae tangit eum.* Et ita iudicabat, eum non esse Prophetam, sed hominem ignorantem, dicens in corde suo[^p184-3]: « Signa nostra non vidimus, iam non est Propheta, et non cognoscet amplius ». Sed huic potest dici illud Iob vigesimo secundo: « An non cogitas, quod Deus excelsior caelo sit et super stellarum verticem sublimetur? Et dicis: Quid enim novit Deus? Et quasi per caliginem iudicat ». Sed certe ipse « novit in tenebris constituta, et lux cum eo est », sicut dicitur Danielis secundo.

69. Et nota, quod hoc modo ex *vero* cadebat in *falsum.* Manifestum est enim, quod Deus et homo divinus novit occulta non solum bonorum, sed etiam malorum; Proverbiorum decimo quinto[^p184-4]: « Oculi Domini in omni loco contemplantur bonos et malos ». Sed in hoc errabat, quia hoc credebat ignorare Christum, dum se permittebat a muliere peccatrice contingi. Unde etiam de ipsa male iudicabat; propter quod addit: *Quia peccatrix est,* quasi dicat: non admitti, sed repelli deberet. Hic erat de illis, de quibus Isaiae sexagesimo quinto[^p184-5]: « Qui dicunt: Recede a nobis et ne appropinques, quia immundus es ». Sic etiam alter Pharisaeus dicebat de publicano infra decimo octavo: « Deus, gratias tibi ago, quia non sum sicut ceteri hominum, velut etiam hic publicanus ». Et male iudicabat, et tamen Christus propter eius malum iudicium non dimisit facere misericordiam; de quo supra quinto[^p184-6] dixit Pharisaeis: « Non egent qui sani sunt medico, sed qui male habent. Non enim veni vocare iustos, sed peccatores ad poenitentiam ». — Magna igitur misericordia Christi ostensa fuit in susceptione peccatricis accedentis ad Christi contactum inter epulas convivantium et versutias iudicantium. In quo simul commendatur Christi *clementia* et peccatricis *poenitentia.* Unde Gregorius[^p184-7]: « Cogitanti mihi de Mariae poenitentia flere magis libet, quam aliquid dicere. Cuius enim vel saxeum pectus istae huius peccatricis lacrymae ad exemplum poenitendi non emolliant? Super convivantes ingressa est, inter epulas lacrymas fudit; quid ergo miramur? Mariam venientem, vel Dominum suscipientem? *Suscipientem* dicam, an *trahentem?* Sed melius *suscipientem et trahentem,* quia nimirum ille per misericordiam traxit intus, qui per mansuetudinem suscepit foris ».

70. Et est hic *moraliter* notandum, quod in his sex, quae dicuntur de hac peccatrice poenitente, scilicet quod *retro stabat* et *secus pedes* iacebat; *lacrymis rigabat, capillis tergebat* et *osculabatur* et *ungebat;* ista intelliguntur, quae debent esse in vero poenitente, scilicet *pudor erubescentiae,* quod notat, cum dicit *stans retro;* infra decimo octavo[^p184-8]: « Publicanus a longe stans ». — *Timor reverentiae,* quod notatur ibi: *Secus pedes eius;* Psalmus: « Adorate scabellum pedum eius, quoniam sanctum est ». — *Dolor poenitentiae: Lacrymis coepit rigare pedes eius;* Threnorum secundo: « Deduc quasi torrentem lacrymas ». — *Nitor pudicitiae: Capillis suis tergebat;* Isaiae primo[^p184-9]: « Lavamini, mundi estote, auferte malum ». — *Ardor benevolentiae: Osculabatur pedes eius;* et hic: « Osculum mihi non dedisti » etc., usque ibi: « Quoniam dilexit multum ». — *Dulcor devotionis internae: Et unguento ungebat;* Canticorum primo[^p184-10]: « Meliora sunt ubera tua vino, fragrantia unguentis optimis ». Bonum unguentum *compunctionis,* melius *compassionis,* sed optimum *devotionis;* primo pedes, secundo corpus, sed tertio ungitur caput. Et ideo Magdalena ter legitur venisse ad Dominum ungendum, hic et Ioannis duodecimo[^p185-1] et Matthaei vigesimo sexto et Marci decimo sexto.
<!-- page 185 -->

71. (Vers. 40.). *Et respondens Iesus dixit* etc. Post benignam susceptionem accedentis subditur hic *benignior approbatio diligentis.* Approbat Dominus hanc peccatricem diligentem et eius dilectionem, primo perquirens *generale iudicium;* secundo assumens *speciale propositum,* ibi[^p185-2]: *Et conversus ad mulierem* etc.; tertio vero concludens *principale intentum,* ibi: *Propter quod dico tibi: Remittuntur ei* etc. — In *perquirendo* autem *generale iudicium* procedit hoc ordine. *Excitat* enim *Pharisaei attentionem, insinuat quaestionem* et *approbat responsionem.*

Primo igitur *Pharisaei excitat attentionem,* ut a falsitate revocetur, cum dicit: *Respondens Iesus dixit ad illum: Simon, habeo tibi aliquid dicere;* in quo excitat eum, ut attendat. Et nota, quod dicitur *respondere Pharisaeo,* cum tamen non dicatur aliquid quaesivisse a Christo, ut ostendatur, quod ipse audiebat eius cogitationem, ut eius iudicium refellatur, quo credebat Christum ignarum, iuxta quod in Psalmo[^p185-3] dicitur: « Intelligite insipientes in populo, et stulti aliquando sapite. Qui plantavit aurem, non audiet? Aut qui finxit oculum, non considerat »? In hoc, quod quasi respondendo alloquitur, excitat ad cogitandam Christi sapientiam; in hoc vero, quod spondet, se aliquid dicturum, excitat ad percipiendam doctrinam; sicut et discipulis dixit Ioannis decimo sexto[^p185-4]: « Multa habeo vobis dicere, sed non potestis portare modo ». — Et quoniam efficaciter eum excitavit, ideo subditur eius attentio, cum dicitur: *At ille ait: Magister, dic.* In qua responsione profitetur scientiam et petit doctrinam, iuxta consilium sapientis, Ecclesiastici sexto[^p185-5]: « Fili, si attenderis mihi, disces sapientiam, et si accommodaveris animum tuum, sapiens eris; si inclinaveris aurem tuam, excipies doctrinam; et si dilexeris audire, sapiens eris ». Et sequitur post: « Et si videris sensatum, evigila ad illum ». Hic est Christus Dominus, quem recte vocat *magistrum,* secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p185-6]: « Unus est magister vester, Christus »; et Ioannis decimo tertio: « Vocatis me *magister* et *domine,* et bene dicitis; sum etenim ».

72. (Vers. 41.). Secundo autem *Pharisaeo insinuat quaestionem,* ut ad veritatem dirigatur, cum subditur: *Duo debitores erant uni*[^p185-7] *foeneratori* etc. Casum proponit in generali, intelligens per istos *debitores* et intelligi dans peccatores, qui magnis debitis sunt astricti, secundum illud Matthaei sexto: « Dimitte nobis debita », id est peccata, « sicut et nos dimittimus debitoribus nostris » etc. Per *foeneratorem* intelligimus Christum, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p185-8]: « Serve male et piger, nonne oportuit pecuniam meam mittere nummulariis, et ego veniens exegissem quod meum est cum usuris »? — Et quia non omnes aequaliter peccant nec aequaliter obligantur, ideo additur: *Unus debebat denarios quingentos;* in quo ostenditur *plena* obligatio propter quinqueformem transgressionem perfectionis evangelicae; completio enim intelligitur per centenarium. *Et alius quinquaginta;* hoc dicitur quantum ad *semiplenam* obligationem, quae est in transgressione quinqueformi ipsius decalogi.

73. (Vers. 42.). Sed quia Deus miseretur omni generi peccatorum, qui ad ipsum convertuntur; ideo subditur: *Non habentibus illis unde redderent, donavit utrisque,* scilicet ex misericordia condonante, sicut dicitur Matthaei decimo octavo[^p185-9]: « Misertus dominus servi illius, dimisit eum et debitum dimisit ei », et hoc compassione miseriae, qua impotentes sumus solvere. Omnia enim, quae habemus, sine obligatione peccati Deo debemus; primae ad Corinthios quarto[^p185-10]: « Quid habes, quod non accepisti »? et primi Paralipomenon vigesimo nono: « Tua sunt, Domine, omnia, et quae de manu tua accepimus dedimus tibi ». Si ergo remittuntur peccata, oportet, quod liberaliter et misericorditer remittantur, quia nullum possumus rependere beneficium. Obligamur tamen ad dilectionis affectum, de quo requirit propter collatum beneficium. — Unde subdit: *Quis ergo eum plus diligit?* cum uterque diligere teneatur. Unde sic quaerebat Dominus a Petro post condonationem sui peccati, Ioannis vigesimo primo[^p185-11]: « Simon Ioannis, diligis me plus his »? Et rationabiliter quaerit, quia inaequalitatem beneficiorum sequi debet inaequalitas affectionum, secundum quod dicit Gregorius: « Cum augentur dona, rationes etiam crescunt donorum. Tanto ergo humilior atque ad serviendum Deo promptior quisque esse debet ex munere, quanto se obligatiorem esse conspicit in reddenda ratione ».

74. (Vers. 43.). Tertio vero Pharisaei *approbat responsionem,* ut in veritate confirmetur; propter quod additur: *Respondit Simon et dixit*[^p186-1]: *Aestimo, quia is cui plus donavit,* quod scilicet amplius diligat, ut verificetur illud ad Romanos quinto: « Ubi autem abundavit delictum, superabundavit et gratia ». — Et quoniam, sicut dicitur Ecclesiastici vigesimo primo, « verbum sapiens, quodcumque audierit, scius laudabit et ad se adiiciet »; ideo subditur: *At ille dixit ei: Recte iudicasti.* Hoc enim solum iudicium approbat Veritas Christus, secundum illud Ioannis septimo[^p186-2]: « Nolite iudicare secundum faciem, sed iustum iudicium iudicate ». Unde qui falsum iudicium fecerat ex propria aestimatione rectum profert divina manuductione, et qui male prius iudicaverat intra se nunc recte iudicat contra se. Unde Gregorius[^p186-3]: « Sua sententia Pharisaeus convincitur, quia sicut phreneticus funem portat, ex quo ligatur ». — Et nota, quod commendatur hic Pharisaei iudicium; nec tamen ille qui amplius peccaverat, amplius approbatur, sed ostenditur, quod quantum est ratione *obligationis,* quae venit ex dimisso, amplius obligatur[^p186-4]. Unde intelligenda est haec sententia *cum reduplicatione* et quantum ad obligationem, et supposita gratia et rectitudine in voluntate; quia multi ad magnas gratias tenentur, qui paucas aut nullas reddunt. Unde Beda[^p186-5]: « Secundum omnes plus offendit qui plus debuit; sed per misericordiam omnis causa mutatur, ut amplius diligat qui amplius debuit, si tamen gratiam consequatur ».
<!-- page 186 -->

75. (Vers. 44.). *Et conversus ad mulierem* etc. Hic post generale iudicium assumit *speciale propositum,* in quo ostendit, peccatricem maiorem affectum[^p186-6] habuisse ad Christum quam Pharisaeum, proponendo triplex dilectionis indicium, scilicet *fletum, osculum* et *ablutionem pedum,* in quibus mulier praecellit Pharisaeum.

Primo igitur praeponit peccatricem Simoni propter *fletum,* qui erat expressum indicium *contritionis;* et hoc notat, cum dicitur: *Et conversus ad mulierem,* propter eius contritionem et poenitentiam, secundum illud Zachariae primo[^p186-7]: « Convertimini ad me, et ego convertar ad vos ». Hoc autem fit, cum diligimus et rediligimur, secundum illud Canticorum secundo: « Dilectus meus mihi, et ego illi »; et Canticorum septimo: « Ego dilecto meo, et ad me conversio eius ». — Ideo autem se corporaliter convertit ad mulierem, ut in ipsa erudiret Simonem, ad quem loquebatur; et ideo subditur: *Dixit Simoni: Vides hanc mulierem?* Ideo quaerit, utrum *videret,* quia, licet videret corporaliter, eius[^p186-8] facta non approbabat, sed reprobabat eius merita; et licet videret peccatricis fletum, non tamen attendebat affectum. — Et ideo a Domino commonetur et ex comparatione illius arguitur in hoc quod sequitur: *Intravi in domum tuam, aquam pedibus meis non dedisti;* ex quo reprehensibilis es, quia non fuisti imitator Abrahae; Genesis decimo octavo[^p186-9]: « Loquebatur viris transeuntibus: Laventur pedes vestri, et requiescite sub arbore ». — Sed econtra haec est commendabilis, unde additur: *Haec autem lacrymis rigavit pedes meos et capillis suis tersit,* id est, lacrymas in tanta copia fudit ad modum fontis et rivuli, iuxta quod dicitur Ieremiae nono[^p186-10]: « Quis dabit capiti meo aquam et oculis meis fontem lacrymarum »? Et in hoc significatur mirabilis affectus mulieris et gratia interior, quae ita in lacrymas profluebat exterius, ut impleretur illud Psalmi: « Posuit desertum in stagna aquarum »; et Isaiae trigesimo quinto: « Et quae erat arida facta est in stagnum, et sitiens in fontes aquarum ». Et in hoc praeponitur Pharisaeo quantum ad *luctum*[^p186-11], in quo est expressum signum compunctionis.

76. (Vers. 45.). Secundo vero praeponit eam quantum ad *osculum,* quod erat signum *dilectionis,* cum additur: *Osculum mihi non dedisti,* in signum amoris, iuxta Apostolum ad Romanos ultimo[^p186-12]: « Salutate invicem in osculo sancto »; et discipuli osculati sunt Paulum, Actuum vigesimo. — Sed econtra de hac; unde additur: *Haec autem, ex quo intravit, non cessavit osculari pedes meos;* in quo signatur *fervens* dilectio et *permanens.* Ideo non cessabat ab osculo, quia non cessabat ab interiori desiderio. Nam de ipsa dicit Gregorius[^p186-13], quod « amoris eius, id est Christi, igne succensa ardebat desiderio ». Sicut enim dicitur Canticorum ultimo: « Fortis est ut mors dilectio »; et post: « Lampades eius lampades ignis atque flammarum ». Unde sicut ignis semper urit, sic caritas incessanter accendit; ut enim dicit Gregorius[^p186-14]: « Nunquam est amor Dei otiosus ».

77. (Vers. 46.). Tertio praeponit eam quantum ad *unctionem pedum,* quod erat signum *devotionis,* cum subiungit: *Oleo caput meum non unxisti;* quod erat moris tunc temporis in signum laetitiae et exsultationis, secundum illud Matthaei sexto[^p187-1]: « Tu autem, cum ieiunas, unge caput tuum et faciem tuam lava ». — *Haec autem unguento unxit pedes meos,* quod est pretiosius oleo. Unde pretiosiora offerebat haec mulier Christo, secundum illud Ioannis duodecimo[^p187-2]: « Maria ergo accepit libram unguenti nardi pistici pretiosi, et unxit pedes Iesu ». In his ergo tribus dilectionis indiciis probat Dominus, quod peccatrix verius et ferventius dilexit.
<!-- page 187 -->

78. (Vers. 47.). *Propter quod dico tibi* etc. Hic post generale iudicium et speciale propositum concludit *principale intentum,* quod est commendatio mulieris consequens ex praemissis. Commendatur autem haec mulier peccatrix a Domino commendatione plena, quia[^p187-3] ut *purgata a peccato* propter dilectionis affectum, ut *perfecta in merito* propter dilectionis effectum, ut *praeposita Pharisaeo* propter dilectionis excessum.

Unde approbat hic Dominus dilectionem eius primo, quia dilectio eam *a peccato purgavit;* quod notat, cum dicit: *Propter quod dico tibi,* id est, propter nunc praemissa dilectionis indicia assero tibi: *Remittuntur ei peccata multa* etc.; et ita iam non debet a te condemnari, sed magis beatificari, quia in Psalmo scriptum est[^p187-4]: « Beati, quorum remissae sunt iniquitates, et quorum tecta sunt peccata »; et ad Romanos quarto: « Ei qui non operatur, credenti in eum qui iustificat impium, reputatur fides eius ad iustitiam secundum propositum gratiae Dei. Sicut et David dicit beatitudinem hominis, cui Deus accepto fert iustitiam sine operibus » Legis. Unde in ista impletum est illud Psalmi[^p187-5]: « Dixi, confitebor adversum me iniustitiam meam Domino; et tu remisisti impietatem peccati mei ».

79. Secundo, quia dilectio *in merito perfecit*[^p187-6]; propter quod addit: *Quoniam dilexit multum.* Magnitudo enim dilectionis amplificat meritum et expellit peccatum; primae Petri quarto[^p187-7]: « Caritas operit multitudinem peccatorum »; et Proverbiorum decimo: « Odium suscitat rixas, et universa delicta operit caritas ». Unde et in Glossa dicitur: « Ardor caritatis in ea rubiginem peccatorum consumsit ». Nec solum purgat a peccato, sed etiam *conservat* in merito, secundum illud ad Colossenses tertio[^p187-8]: « Super omnia haec caritatem habete, quod est vinculum perfectionis ».

80. Tertio vero approbat, quia dilectio eam *Pharisaeo praeposuit;* propter quod addit: *Cui autem minus dimittitur,* scilicet Pharisaeo, *minus diligit,* quam ista cui dimissa sunt multa. Et ita ex hoc concluditur, quod longe plus quam Pharisaeus Dominum amavit, et ideo amplius Deo accepta fuit; et propterea « eam laudavit vir eius[^p187-9] », quasi dicens sibi illud Proverbiorum ultimo: « Multae filiae congregaverunt divitias, tu supergressa es universas »; quia « fallax gratia, et vana est pulcritudo; mulier timens Dominum, ipsa laudabitur ». — Patet igitur, quomodo in hac conclusione clauditur[^p187-10] perfecta commendatio et approbatio diligentis contra iudicium Pharisaei ipsam contemnentis, quia reputabat eam plenam peccatis et vacuam meritis, et sic viluisse in conspectu superni iudicis. Quod totum exsufflatum est in hac conclusione, cum ipsa ostenditur iustificata a peccato et multiplicata in merito et anteposita Pharisaeo.

81. Et nota, quod in hac conclusione infertur commendatio dilectionis Christi respectu remissionis peccati per modum *causae* et per modum *effectus.* Unde remissio peccati *praemittitur,* et remissio peccati *postponitur,* et dilectio *interponitur.* Est enim *remissio* quoad *poenam,* et haec causatur a dilectione; et quoad *culpam,* et haec inducit dilectionem. Et rursus est *dilectio affectus,* et hanc inducit peccati remissio, secundum quod supra[^p187-11] ostensum fuit in Pharisaei iudicio. Et est *dilectio effectus,* et haec inducit peccati remissionem quoad *poenam,* secundum quod colligitur ex triplici dilectionis indicio[^p187-12]. Unde ista conclusio quantum ad primam partem respondet inductioni factae in bonis operibus; quantum ad secundam respondet quaestioni de duobus debitoribus et respondet simul omnibus praemissis. — Super hoc etiam advertenda est Christi *sapientia* in approbando peccatricem, quam Pharisaeus condemnaverat. Approbat eam ex datis a Pharisaeo et ex concessis[^p187-13], deinde ex manifestis indiciis; primo quasi syllogizando, secundo quasi inducendo; primo in generali, secundo in speciali; primo parabolice, secundo expresse. Ad haec omnia respondet illatio conclusionis praemissae.

82. (Vers. 48.). *Dixit autem ad illam.* Post benignam susceptionem accedentis et benigniorem approbationem diligentis subiungitur hic tertio *benignissima absolutio confitentis.* Circa quam primo introducitur *divinae largitatis donum plenarium;* secundo, *humanae impietatis iudicium perversum;* tertio, *devotae credulitatis meritum perfectum.*
<!-- page 188 -->

Primo igitur quantum ad *divinae largitatis donum plenarium* dicitur: *Dixit autem ad illam.* Non est dubium, quin dixerit Christus ad peccatricem: *Remittuntur tibi peccata,* non solum quoad *culpam,* sed etiam quoad *poenam,* secundum quod petebat Manasses[^p188-1]: « Peto rogans te, Domine, remitte mihi, remitte mihi, ne simul perdas me cum iniquitatibus meis neque in aeternum reserves mala mea ». Hanc remissionem Christus fecit sua virtute; Matthaei nono: « Ut sciatis, quia Filius hominis habet potestatem in terra dimittendi peccata » etc.; Actuum decimo: « Huic omnes Prophetae testimonium perhibent, remissionem peccatorum accipere per nomen eius omnes, qui credunt in eum ».

83. (Vers. 49.). Secundo quantum ad *humanae impietatis iudicium perversum* subditur: *Et coeperunt qui simul accumbebant, dicere intra se,* scilicet male iudicando et ipsum despiciendo: *Quis est hic, qui etiam peccata dimittit?* quasi dicant: cum sit parvus et nullus, quomodo sibi attribuit quod potest solus Deus? iuxta illud Isaiae quadragesimo tertio[^p188-2]: « Ego sum, ego sum ipse, qui deleo iniquitates tuas propter me ». Et sic verificatum est illud Ecclesiastici decimo tertio: « Pauper locutus est, et dicunt: Quis est hic »? Sic etiam in cordibus suis qui simul manducabant dolum cogitabant, ut impleretur illud Psalmi: « Si is qui oderat me, super me magna locutus fuisset, abscondissem me forsitan ab eo. Tu vero homo unanimis » etc. Unde isti male iudicando de sanitate illius infirmabantur, sicut dicit Beda[^p188-3]: « Sanata aegrota, de eius salute alii aegrotant ». Et sic verificatum est illud Osee septimo: « Cum sanare vellem Israel, revelata est iniquitas Ephraim et malitia Samariae, quia mendacium operati sunt ».

84. (Vers. 50.). Tertio quantum ad *devotae credulitatis meritum perfectum* subditur: *Dixit autem ad mulierem: Fides tua te salvam fecit,* scilicet a peccatis; *fides,* inquam, Christi, de qua Matthaei primo[^p188-4]: « Ipse salvum faciet populum suum a peccatis eorum »; et hoc per fidem; ad Romanos tertio: « Deus iustificat eum qui ex fide est Iesu Christi ». Et quia iustitia facit pacem cum Deo et cum conscientia, ideo subdit: *Vade in pace;* ad Romanos quinto[^p188-5]: « Iustificati ergo ex fide, pacem habeamus ad Deum »; et Isaiae quinquagesimo septimo: « Veniat pax et requiescat in cubili suo »; ut de illa possit dici illud Malachiae secundo: « In pace et in aequitate ambulavit mecum et multos avertit ab iniquitate ».

## English
<!-- page 182 -->

#### Thirdly, the sublimity of the Doctor is declared on account of the affluence of piety, in three ways.

62. *And a certain Pharisee desired him*[^p182-4]. Above, the Evangelist showed the sublimity of the Lord Saviour as to the excellence of power and as to the evidence of truth; here, thirdly, he shows it as to the *affluence of piety*[^tr-pietas]. And this affluence of piety is shown in the Lord Jesus as great in the *reception* of the sinner, greater in the *approbation,* but greatest in the *absolution.* For he received her approaching, commended her loving, and absolved her doing penance.

First, then, there is introduced in this part the kind *reception of the sinner approaching;* but secondly, the kinder *approbation of her loving,* there[^p182-5]: *And Jesus answering, said to him* etc.; but thirdly, the kindest *absolution of her doing penance and confessing,* there: *And he said to her: Thy sins are remitted thee.*

As to the first, it must be noted that the *reception of the sinner* approaching the Lord is shown to be of great piety and mercy, as well on account of the *solemnity of the feast,* in which he received her; as on account of the *familiarity of the service,* to which he admitted her; as on account of the *rashness of another's judgement,* on account of which[^p182-6] he did not dismiss her.

63. (Vers. 36.). First, then, as to the *solemnity of the feast,* in which the sinner was piously received by the Lord, it is said: *And a certain Pharisee desired him to eat with him;* there is no doubt but that it was the Lord Jesus; and rightly, according to the counsel and purpose of the Wise man; Wisdom, the eighth chapter[^p182-7]: « I purposed to take her », that is, wisdom, Christ, « to live with me, knowing that she will communicate to me of her good things ». And this Pharisee was now not divided from the Lord, but rather united, and therefore he securely besought the Lord and invited him to the feast, according to that of Job, the twenty-second chapter[^p182-8]: « If thou wilt return to the Almighty, thou shalt be built up »; and there follows: « Thou shalt beseech him, and he will hear thee ». — Which this man too obtained; whence there is also added: *And he went into the house of the Pharisee, and sat down to meat.* He sat down to eat, and so fulfilled visibly that which he promises spiritually in Apocalypse, the third chapter[^p182-9]: « If any man shall open to me, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me ». Nor is it a wonder, because, as he himself says in Proverbs, the eighth chapter, « my delights were to be with the children of men ». This he says as to the excess of charity, on account of which he was made man and willingly took up and bore human needs among men, according to that of Baruch, the third chapter[^p182-10]: « Afterwards he was seen upon earth, and conversed with men ». These things, then, have been introduced on account of the solemnity of the feast.

64. (Vers. 37.). As to the *familiarity of the service,* to which she was admitted, there is added: *And behold a woman that was in the city, a sinner,* whose infamy had become known to the whole city, so that there could be said to her that of Jeremias, the third chapter[^p182-11]: « Thou hadst a harlot's forehead, thou wouldst not blush ». This is that woman of whom it is said in Ecclesiastes, the seventh chapter: « I have found a woman more bitter than death, who is the hunters' snare, and her heart is a net »; and this, because she was a manifest fornicatress, as he of Ravenna[^p182-12] says, that « her infamy had injured the whole city », so that there was fulfilled in her what the Lord threatens through Amos to Amasias the priest, Amos, the seventh chapter: « Thy wife shall play the harlot in the city ». And therefore she is deservedly called a *sinner,* both by reason of infamy and by reason of frequency, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter[^p183-1]: « A wicked heart shall be laden with sorrows, and the sinner hath added sin to sin »; and Apocalypse, the last chapter: « He that is filthy, let him be filthy still ». And not only had she repeated the fault, but she had also sinned in diverse ways; whence, on this, that it is said in Mark, the last chapter[^p183-2], that « Christ had cast seven devils out of Mary », Gregory says: « By the number seven the totality is rightly designated. Mary, then, had seven devils, she who was full of all vices ».
<!-- page 183 -->

65. Since, then, she was thus a sinner and unclean, she deservedly ought to have been repelled, most of all from the place of the feast; and yet, on account of the piety of the Lord, which she knew, she was drawn; whence too there is added: *When she knew that Jesus*[^p183-3] *sat at meat in the Pharisee's house, she brought an alabaster box of ointment,* that is, a box or vessel of alabaster, which « is of a certain kind of marble, in which ointments are kept incorrupt ». This the sinner brought to the house of the Pharisee, in which she knew Christ to be and by whom she was received, according to that of the Psalm[^p183-4]: « In her houses shall God be known, when he shall receive her ». *She brought,* I say, the ointment of *humility,* where Christ sat at meat, that there might be fulfilled that of the Canticle, the first chapter: « While the king was at his repose, my spikenard sent forth the odour thereof ». And these spices or ointments, which Magdalen brought to Christ, were designated in the second of Paralipomenon, the ninth chapter[^p183-5]: « There were no such spices as these, which the queen of Saba gave to Solomon ». — Or Magdalen brought ointments to the Saviour on account of the familiar service to be rendered, to which she was admitted, namely to render to Christ out of *devotion* what she had rendered to herself out of *lust,* that is, to wash, to wipe, to kiss and to anoint the most sacred and most clean flesh of Christ; to which she was admitted on account of the affection of *penance* and of *benevolence.*

66. (Vers. 38.). As to the affection of *penance* it is said: *And standing behind at his feet, she began to wash his feet with tears,* through sorrow. For in the soul doing penance there ought to be *shame, fear* and *sorrow.* There was *shame,* because she stood *behind; fear,* because *at his feet; sorrow,* because *she washed with tears,* as in that publican below, the eighteenth chapter[^p183-6]: « And the publican, standing afar off, would not so much as lift up his eyes towards heaven; but struck his breast, saying: O God, be merciful to me a sinner »; Jeremias, the thirty-first chapter: « Convert me, O Lord, and I shall be converted, for thou art my God. After thou didst convert me, I did penance: and after thou didst shew unto me, I struck my thigh. I am confounded and ashamed » etc.; where the three foregoing are touched. — But as to the affection of *benevolence* there is added: *And wiped them with the hairs of her head, and kissed his feet, and anointed them with the ointment,* where the dilection of benevolence is shown, *pure, affectionate* and *devout.* For charity ought to proceed « from a pure heart[^p183-7] ». And she cared more for the lowest part of his body than for the highest. For now she sought to please not the world, but Christ alone, so that she could say that of the Canticle, the eighth chapter: « Who shall give thee to me for my brother, that I may find thee alone without, and kiss thee, and now no man may despise me »?

67. And in these two consists *perfect conversion* to Christ. For as in sin there is conversion through concupiscence and aversion through pride[^p183-8]; so in the remedy there is aversion from sin through *penance* and conversion to God through *benevolence.* And truly this woman was perfectly converted and turned back, because, as Gregory says, « as many delights as she had had in herself, so many holocausts did she find of herself ». — For sinful women are wont to offend most of all in these four things, namely in *the nods of the eyes;* Isaias, the third chapter[^p183-9]: « They walked with nods of the eyes »; *the superfluities of the hair;* the first of Peter, the third chapter: « Let it not be the outward plaiting of the hair »; in *the pleasures of kisses;* Proverbs, the seventh chapter: « And catching the young man, she kisseth him »; *the sweetnesses of ointments,* as it is said in Esther, the second chapter, that the king's maidens « were anointed with oil of myrrh » etc. — And therefore the perfect penitent served with her *eyes* for weeping, so that Christ could say to her that of the fourth of Kings, the twentieth chapter[^p183-10]: « I have heard thy prayer, and I have seen thy tears »; with her *hair* for wiping, so that thus there could be said to her that of the Canticle, the fourth chapter: « Thou hast wounded my heart with one hair of thy neck »; with her *mouth* for kissing, so that there could be said to her by Christ that of the Canticle, the fourth chapter: « Thy lips are as a dropping honeycomb »; with *ointments* she served for anointing, so that there could be said to her: « We will run to the odour of thy ointments ».
<!-- page 184 -->

68. (Vers. 39.). Thirdly, as to the *rashness of another's judgement,* on account of which she was not repulsed, he says: *And the Pharisee, who had invited him, seeing it,* namely to the feast; *seeing,* I say, with an eye not single, but wicked, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-first chapter[^p184-1]: « Remember that a wicked eye is evil. What is created more wicked than an eye »? And truly *wicked,* because, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter, « turning good into evil he lieth in wait, and on the elect he will lay a blot ». And therefore the feast of such men is not much accepted, according to that of Proverbs, the twenty-third chapter: « Eat not with an envious man, and desire not his meats: because like a soothsayer and diviner, he thinketh that which he knoweth not. Eat and drink, will he say to thee: and his mind is not with thee ». And such was this Pharisee; which is plain from that which is added: *He said within himself*[^p184-2], namely thinking as a hypocrite, of whom Job, the fifteenth chapter: « His bowels prepare deceits ». — Whence there follows the judgement: *This man, if he were a Prophet, would know surely who and what manner of woman it is that toucheth him.* And so he judged that he was not a Prophet, but an ignorant man, saying in his heart[^p184-3]: « Our signs we have not seen, there is now no Prophet, and he will know no more ». But to him there can be said that of Job, the twenty-second chapter: « Dost not thou think that God is higher than heaven, and is elevated above the height of the stars? And thou sayest: What doth God know? And he judgeth as it were through a mist ». But surely he « knoweth what is set in darkness, and light is with him », as is said in Daniel, the second chapter.

69. And note that in this way he fell from the *true* into the *false.* For it is manifest that God and the godly man knows the hidden things not only of the good but also of the wicked; Proverbs, the fifteenth chapter[^p184-4]: « The eyes of the Lord in every place behold the good and the evil ». But in this he erred, that he believed Christ to be ignorant of this, while he let himself be touched by a sinful woman. Whence he also judged ill of her; on account of which he adds: *That she is a sinner,* as if he should say: she ought not to be admitted, but repelled. He was of those of whom Isaias, the sixty-fifth chapter[^p184-5]: « That say: Depart from us, and come not near, because thou art unclean ». So too the other Pharisee said of the publican, below, the eighteenth chapter: « O God, I give thee thanks that I am not as the rest of men, as also is this publican ». And he judged ill, and yet Christ, on account of his evil judgement, did not forbear to do mercy; about which, above, the fifth chapter[^p184-6], he said to the Pharisees: « They that are whole need not the physician, but they that are sick. For I came not to call the just, but sinners to penance ». — Great, therefore, was the mercy of Christ shown in the reception of the sinner approaching to the touch of Christ, amid the banquet of the feasters and the wiles of the judgers. In which are commended at once the *clemency* of Christ and the *penance* of the sinner. Whence Gregory says[^p184-7]: « When I think of Mary's penance, I would rather weep than say anything. For whose breast, even of stone, would not these tears of this sinner soften to the example of doing penance? She went in upon the feasters, she shed tears amid the banquet; what then do we wonder at? Mary coming, or the Lord receiving? Shall I say *receiving,* or *drawing?* But better *receiving and drawing,* because surely he drew her within by mercy, who received her without by meekness ».

70. And here it must be noted *morally* that in these six things which are said of this sinner doing penance, namely that she *stood behind* and lay *at his feet;* *washed with tears, wiped with her hair* and *kissed* and *anointed;* there are understood those things which ought to be in the true penitent, namely the *shame of blushing,* which he marks when he says *standing behind;* below, the eighteenth chapter[^p184-8]: « The publican, standing afar off ». — The *fear of reverence,* which is marked there: *At his feet;* the Psalm: « Adore his footstool, for it is holy ». — The *sorrow of penance: She began to wash his feet with tears;* Lamentations, the second chapter: « Let tears run down like a torrent ». — The *cleanness of purity: She wiped them with her hair;* Isaias, the first chapter[^p184-9]: « Wash yourselves, be clean, take away the evil ». — The *ardour of benevolence: She kissed his feet;* and here: « Thou gavest me no kiss » etc., as far as there: « Because she hath loved much ». — The *sweetness of inward devotion: And she anointed them with ointment;* the Canticle, the first chapter[^p184-10]: « Thy breasts are better than wine, smelling sweet of the best ointments ». Good is the ointment of *compunction,* better that of *compassion,* but best that of *devotion;* first the feet, secondly the body, but thirdly the head is anointed. And therefore Magdalen is read three times to have come to anoint the Lord: here, and in John, the twelfth chapter[^p185-1], and in Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter, and in Mark, the sixteenth chapter.
<!-- page 185 -->

71. (Vers. 40.). *And Jesus answering, said* etc. After the kind reception of her approaching there is added here the *kinder approbation of her loving.* The Lord approves this sinner loving, and her dilection, first searching out a *general judgement;* secondly, taking up a *special proposition,* there[^p185-2]: *And turning to the woman* etc.; but thirdly, concluding the *principal intent,* there: *Wherefore I say to thee: There are remitted her* etc. — And in *searching out* the *general judgement* he proceeds in this order. For he *rouses the Pharisee's attention, intimates a question* and *approves the answer.*

First, then, he *rouses the Pharisee's attention,* that he may be called back from falsity, when he says: *Jesus answering, said to him: Simon, I have somewhat to say to thee;* in which he rouses him to attend. And note that he is said *to answer the Pharisee,* though the Pharisee is not said to have asked anything of Christ, that it may be shown that he heard his thought, so that his judgement, by which he believed Christ ignorant, may be refuted, according to what is said in the Psalm[^p185-3]: « Understand, ye senseless among the people: and, you fools, be wise at last. He that planted the ear, shall he not hear? Or he that formed the eye, doth he not consider »? In this, that he addresses him as though answering, he rouses him to think on Christ's wisdom; but in this, that he promises that he will say something, he rouses him to receive doctrine; as he also said to the disciples, John, the sixteenth chapter[^p185-4]: « I have many things to say to you: but you cannot bear them now ». — And since he roused him effectually, therefore his attention is added, when it is said: *But he said: Master, say it.* In which answer he professes knowledge and asks for doctrine, according to the counsel of the wise man, Ecclesiasticus, the sixth chapter[^p185-5]: « My son, if thou wilt attend to me, thou shalt learn wisdom: and if thou wilt apply thy mind, thou shalt be wise. If thou wilt incline thy ear, thou shalt receive doctrine: and if thou love to hear, thou shalt be wise ». And there follows after: « And if thou see a man of understanding, go to him early in the morning ». This is Christ the Lord, whom he rightly calls *master,* according to that of Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p185-6]: « One is your master, Christ »; and John, the thirteenth chapter: « You call me *Master* and *Lord;* and you say well, for so I am ».

72. (Vers. 41.). But secondly, he *intimates a question to the Pharisee,* that he may be directed to the truth, when there is added: *There were two debtors to one*[^p185-7] *lender upon usury* etc. He proposes the case in general, understanding by these *debtors,* and giving to be understood, sinners, who are bound by great debts, according to that of Matthew, the sixth chapter: « Forgive us our debts », that is, sins, « as we also forgive our debtors » etc. By the *lender upon usury* we understand Christ, according to that of Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p185-8]: « Wicked and slothful servant, oughtest thou not to have committed my money to the bankers, and at my coming I should have received my own with usury »? — And because not all sin equally nor are equally bound, therefore there is added: *The one owed five hundred pence;* in which is shown a *full* obligation, on account of the fivefold transgression of evangelical perfection; for completion is understood through the hundred. *And the other fifty;* this is said as to a *half-full* obligation, which is in the fivefold transgression of the decalogue itself.

73. (Vers. 42.). But because God has mercy on every kind of sinners who are converted to him; therefore there is added: *And whereas they had not wherewith to pay, he pardoned them both,* namely out of pardoning mercy, as is said in Matthew, the eighteenth chapter[^p185-9]: « The lord of that servant, being moved with pity, let him go and forgave him the debt », and this out of compassion for the misery by which we are powerless to pay. For all things that we have we owe to God, without the obligation of sin; the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter[^p185-10]: « What hast thou that thou hast not received »? and the first of Paralipomenon, the twenty-ninth chapter: « All things are thine, O Lord: and we have given thee what we received of thy hand ». If, then, sins are remitted, they must be remitted liberally and mercifully, because we can repay no benefit. Yet we are bound to the affection of dilection, which he requires on account of the benefit conferred. — Whence he adds: *Which therefore of the two loveth him most?* since both are bound to love. Whence thus the Lord asked of Peter after the pardoning of his sin, John, the twenty-first chapter[^p185-11]: « Simon son of John, lovest thou me more than these »? And reasonably he asks, because the inequality of affections ought to follow the inequality of benefits, according to what Gregory says: « When gifts are increased, the accounts of the gifts also grow. Each one, therefore, ought to be the humbler and the readier to serve God by reason of his gift, the more he sees himself bound in the rendering of account ».

74. (Vers. 43.). But thirdly, he *approves the Pharisee's answer,* that he may be confirmed in the truth; on account of which is added: *Simon answered and said*[^p186-1]: *I suppose that he to whom he pardoned most,* namely that he would love more, so that there may be verified that to the Romans, the fifth chapter: « But where sin abounded, grace did more abound ». — And since, as is said in Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-first chapter, « a wise word whatsoever a skilful man shall hear, he will commend it, and will apply it to himself »; therefore there is added: *And he said to him: Thou hast judged rightly.* For this judgement alone does Christ the Truth approve, according to that of John, the seventh chapter[^p186-2]: « Judge not according to the appearance, but judge just judgement ». Whence he who had made a false judgement out of his own estimation brings forth a right one by divine manuduction, and he who before had judged ill within himself now judges rightly against himself. Whence Gregory says[^p186-3]: « The Pharisee is convicted by his own sentence, because, like a madman, he carries the rope by which he is bound ». — And note that the Pharisee's judgement is commended here; and yet he who had sinned more is not approved more, but it is shown that, as far as concerns the reckoning of *obligation,* which comes from what was forgiven, he is bound more[^p186-4]. Whence this sentence is to be understood *with reduplication,* both as to obligation, and supposing grace and rectitude in the will; because many are held to great thanks who render few or none. Whence Bede says[^p186-5]: « According to all, he offended more who owed more; but through mercy the whole case is changed, so that he loves more who owed more, if yet he obtain grace ».
<!-- page 186 -->

75. (Vers. 44.). *And turning to the woman* etc. Here, after the general judgement, he takes up a *special proposition,* in which he shows that the sinner had a greater affection[^p186-6] toward Christ than the Pharisee had, by proposing a threefold indication of dilection, namely *weeping, the kiss* and *the washing of the feet,* in which the woman excelled the Pharisee.

First, then, he sets the sinner before Simon on account of her *weeping,* which was an express indication of *contrition;* and this he marks when it is said: *And turning to the woman,* on account of her contrition and penance, according to that of Zacharias, the first chapter[^p186-7]: « Turn ye to me, and I will turn to you ». And this comes about when we love and are loved in return, according to that of the Canticle, the second chapter: « My beloved to me, and I to him »; and the Canticle, the seventh chapter: « I to my beloved, and his turning is towards me ». — And he turned himself bodily to the woman for this reason, that in her he might school Simon, to whom he was speaking; and therefore there is added: *He said to Simon: Dost thou see this woman?* He asks whether he *saw* for this reason, that, although he saw bodily, he did not approve her deeds[^p186-8], but reprobated her merits; and although he saw the sinner's weeping, yet he did not attend to the affection. — And therefore he is admonished by the Lord, and by comparison with her he is reproved, in this which follows: *I entered into thy house, thou gavest me no water for my feet;* by which thou art reprehensible, because thou wast not an imitator of Abraham; Genesis, the eighteenth chapter[^p186-9]: « He spoke to the men passing by: Let your feet be washed, and rest ye under the tree ». — But on the contrary this woman is commendable, whence there is added: *But she with tears hath washed my feet, and with her hairs hath wiped them,* that is, she shed tears in such plenty, after the manner of a fountain and a brook, according to what is said in Jeremias, the ninth chapter[^p186-10]: « Who will give water to my head, and a fountain of tears to my eyes »? And in this is signified the marvellous affection of the woman and the inward grace, which so flowed forth outwardly in tears that there was fulfilled that of the Psalm: « He hath turned a wilderness into pools of waters »; and Isaias, the thirty-fifth chapter: « And that which was dry land is become a pool, and the thirsty land springs of waters ». And in this she is set before the Pharisee as to *mourning*[^p186-11], in which is the express sign of compunction.

76. (Vers. 45.). But secondly, he sets her before him as to the *kiss,* which was a sign of *dilection,* when there is added: *Thou gavest me no kiss,* in sign of love, according to the Apostle to the Romans, the last chapter[^p186-12]: « Salute one another with a holy kiss »; and the disciples kissed Paul, Acts, the twentieth chapter. — But on the contrary concerning her; whence there is added: *But she, since she came in, hath not ceased to kiss my feet;* in which there is signified a *fervent* dilection, and an *abiding* one. She did not cease from the kiss for this reason, that she did not cease from inward desire. For of her Gregory says[^p186-13] that « set on fire with the fire of his love, that is, Christ's, she burned with desire ». For as it is said in the Canticle, the last chapter: « Love is strong as death »; and after: « The lamps thereof are lamps of fire and of flames ». Whence as fire always burns, so charity unceasingly kindles; for, as Gregory says[^p186-14]: « The love of God is never idle ».

77. (Vers. 46.). Thirdly, he sets her before him as to the *anointing of the feet,* which was a sign of *devotion,* when he subjoins: *My head with oil thou didst not anoint;* which was the custom at that time in sign of gladness and exultation, according to that of Matthew, the sixth chapter[^p187-1]: « But thou, when thou fastest, anoint thy head, and wash thy face ». — *But she with ointment hath anointed my feet,* which is more precious than oil. Whence this woman offered more precious things to Christ, according to that of John, the twelfth chapter[^p187-2]: « Mary therefore took a pound of ointment of right spikenard, of great price, and anointed the feet of Jesus ». In these three indications of dilection, then, the Lord proves that the sinner loved more truly and more fervently.
<!-- page 187 -->

78. (Vers. 47.). *Wherefore I say to thee* etc. Here, after the general judgement and the special proposition, he concludes the *principal intent,* which is the commendation of the woman following from the premises. And this sinful woman is commended by the Lord with a full commendation, because[^p187-3] as *purged from sin* on account of the affection of dilection, as *perfected in merit* on account of the effect of dilection, as *set before the Pharisee* on account of the excess of dilection.

Whence the Lord here approves her dilection, first, because dilection *purged her from sin;* which he marks when he says: *Wherefore I say to thee,* that is, on account of the indications of dilection now set forth, I assert to thee: *Many sins are remitted her* etc.; and so she ought now not to be condemned by thee, but rather to be called blessed, because in the Psalm it is written[^p187-4]: « Blessed are they whose iniquities are remitted, and whose sins are covered »; and to the Romans, the fourth chapter: « To him that worketh not, yet believeth in him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is reputed to justice, according to the purpose of the grace of God. As David also termeth the blessedness of a man, to whom God reputeth justice without works » of the Law. Whence in her was fulfilled that of the Psalm[^p187-5]: « I said, I will confess against myself my injustice to the Lord: and thou hast remitted the wickedness of my sin ».

79. Secondly, because dilection *perfected her in merit*[^p187-6]; on account of which he adds: *Because she hath loved much.* For the greatness of dilection enlarges merit and expels sin; the first of Peter, the fourth chapter[^p187-7]: « Charity covereth a multitude of sins »; and Proverbs, the tenth chapter: « Hatred stirreth up strifes: and charity covereth all sins ». Whence it is said in the Gloss too: « The ardour of charity in her consumed the rust of sins ». And it not only purges from sin, but also *keeps* in merit, according to that to the Colossians, the third chapter[^p187-8]: « Above all these things have charity, which is the bond of perfection ».

80. But thirdly, he approves her because dilection *set her before the Pharisee;* on account of which he adds: *But to whom less is forgiven,* namely the Pharisee, *he loveth less,* than this woman to whom many things are forgiven. And so from this it is concluded that she loved the Lord far more than the Pharisee did, and therefore was more accepted by God; and on that account « her husband praised her[^p187-9] », as if saying to her that of Proverbs, the last chapter: « Many daughters have gathered together riches: thou hast surpassed them all »; because « favour is deceitful, and beauty is vain: the woman that feareth the Lord, she shall be praised ». — It is plain, therefore, how in this conclusion there is enclosed[^p187-10] the perfect commendation and approbation of her loving, against the judgement of the Pharisee who despised her, because he reckoned her full of sins and empty of merits, and thus to have grown vile in the sight of the Judge above. All of which is blown away in this conclusion, since she is shown justified from sin, and multiplied in merit, and set before the Pharisee.

81. And note that in this conclusion there is inferred a commendation of dilection toward Christ in respect of the remission of sin, by way of *cause* and by way of *effect.* Whence the remission of sin is *set before,* and the remission of sin is *set after,* and dilection is *set between.* For there is *remission* as to the *punishment,* and this is caused by dilection; and as to the *fault,* and this induces dilection. And again there is *dilection of affection,* and this the remission of sin induces, according as was shown above[^p187-11] in the Pharisee's judgement. And there is *dilection of effect,* and this induces the remission of sin as to the *punishment,* according as is gathered from the threefold indication of dilection[^p187-12]. Whence this conclusion, as to its first part, answers to the induction made in good works; as to the second, it answers to the question about the two debtors, and it answers at once to all the premises. — On this, too, there must be noticed the *wisdom* of Christ in approving the sinner whom the Pharisee had condemned. He approves her from what was given by the Pharisee and from what was granted[^p187-13], then from manifest indications; first as it were by syllogizing, secondly as it were by inducing; first in general, secondly in particular; first in a parable, secondly expressly. To all these the inference of the foregoing conclusion answers.

82. (Vers. 48.). *And he said to her.* After the kind reception of her approaching and the kinder approbation of her loving, there is subjoined here, thirdly, the *kindest absolution of her confessing.* Concerning which there is introduced, first, the *plenary gift of divine largess;* secondly, the *perverse judgement of human impiety;* thirdly, the *perfect merit of devout belief.*
<!-- page 188 -->

First, then, as to the *plenary gift of divine largess* it is said: *And he said to her.* There is no doubt but that Christ said to the sinner: *Thy sins are remitted thee,* not only as to the *fault,* but also as to the *punishment,* according to what Manasses asked[^p188-1]: « I beseech thee, praying, O Lord, remit to me, remit to me; destroy me not together with my iniquities, neither reserve my evils for ever ». This remission Christ made by his own power; Matthew, the ninth chapter: « That you may know that the Son of man hath power on earth to forgive sins » etc.; Acts, the tenth chapter: « To him all the Prophets give testimony, that all receive remission of sins through his name, who believe in him ».

83. (Vers. 49.). Secondly, as to the *perverse judgement of human impiety* there is added: *And they that sat at meat with him began to say within themselves,* namely judging ill and despising him: *Who is this that forgiveth sins also?* as if they should say: since he is little and nothing, how does he attribute to himself what God alone can do? according to that of Isaias, the forty-third chapter[^p188-2]: « I am, I am he that blot out thy iniquities for my own sake ». And thus there was verified that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirteenth chapter: « The poor man spoke, and they say: Who is this »? So too those who ate with him were thinking guile in their hearts, that there might be fulfilled that of the Psalm: « If he that hated me had spoken great things against me, I would perhaps have hidden myself from him. But thou a man of one mind » etc. Whence these, by judging ill of her health, grew sick, as Bede says[^p188-3]: « The sick woman being healed, others grow sick over her health ». And thus there was verified that of Osee, the seventh chapter: « When I would have healed Israel, the iniquity of Ephraim was discovered, and the wickedness of Samaria, for they have committed falsehood ».

84. (Vers. 50.). Thirdly, as to the *perfect merit of devout belief* there is added: *And he said to the woman: Thy faith hath made thee safe,* namely from sins; *faith,* I say, in Christ, of whom Matthew, the first chapter[^p188-4]: « He shall save his people from their sins »; and this through faith; to the Romans, the third chapter: « God justifieth him who is of the faith of Jesus Christ ». And because justice makes peace with God and with the conscience, therefore he adds: *Go in peace;* to the Romans, the fifth chapter[^p188-5]: « Being justified therefore by faith, let us have peace with God »; and Isaias, the fifty-seventh chapter: « Let peace come, and let him rest in his bed »; so that of her there may be said that of Malachias, the second chapter: « In peace and in equity he walked with me, and turned many away from iniquity ».
