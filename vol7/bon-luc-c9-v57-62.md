# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. IX, Vers. 57-62
## *Factum est autem, ambulantibus etc. Post informationem Apostolorum ad normam praesidendi quantum ad humilitatem animi et aequanimitatem zeli hic tertio loco informat eos quantum ad perspicacitatem iudicii; et hoc per exemplum Iesu Christi*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 248 -->
102. *Factum est autem, ambulantibus* etc. Post informationem Apostolorum ad normam praesidendi quantum ad humilitatem animi et aequanimitatem zeli hic tertio loco informat eos *quantum ad perspicacitatem iudicii*[^p248-11]*;* et hoc per exemplum Iesu Christi. Perspicacitas autem iudicii manifestatur in parte ista tripliciter: primo in *repulsione duplicium;* secundo, *advocatione simplicium,* ibi[^p248-12]: *Ait autem ad alterum* etc.; tertio vero, *redargutione mobilium,* ibi: *Et alter ait: Sequar te, Domine* etc.
<!-- page 249 -->

Circa *repulsionem duplicium* introducuntur duo: primum est *simulatio simplicitatis,* secundum est *detectio*[^p249-1] *duplicitatis.*

103. (Vers. 57.). Primo igitur quantum ad *simplicitatis simulationem* dicit: *Factum est autem, ambulantibus illis in via,* scilicet discipulis ambulantibus cum Domino in via perfectionis, sicut dicitur de Noe Genesis sexto[^p249-2]: « Noe vir iustus atque perfectus cum Deo ambulavit »; et similiter Dominus ad Abraham, Genesis decimo septimo: « Ambula coram me, et esto perfectus ». De qua via Isaiae trigesimo: « Audies verbum praeceptoris tui. Haec est via bona; ambulate in ea ». — Et quia in hac via frequenter mali simulatione iungi volunt bonis, ideo additur: *Dixit quidam ad Iesum*[^p249-3]*: Sequar te, quocumque ieris.* In quo ostendebat, se habere voluntatem sequendi perfecte, cum tamen aliud in corde haberet, sicut Simon magus, de quo dicitur Actuum octavo, quod « cum baptizabantur viri et mulieres, tunc Simon et ipse credidit et baptizatus est », malo tamen corde et duplici, sicut ex sequentibus patet. Unde Hieronymus[^p249-4]: « Iste hac intentione volebat sequi Iesum, qua Simon magus desiderabat habere potestatem miracula faciendi, ut de miraculis divitias lucraretur ». Sed contra Ecclesiastici primo[^p249-5]: « Ne accesseris ad illum duplici corde et ne fueris hypocrita in conspectu hominum ». Tales sunt qui magna promittunt et parva faciunt, contra quos Ecclesiastici quarto: « Ne fueris citatus in lingua tua et inutilis et remissus in operibus tuis ».

104. (Vers. 58.). Secundo vero quantum ad *duplicitatis detectionem* adiungitur: *Et ait illi*[^p249-6] *Iesus: Vulpes foveas habent,* in quibus scilicet latent, *et volucres caeli nidos,* in quibus pernoctant; *Filius autem hominis non habet, ubi caput reclinet;* in quo miram *ostendit inopiam,* quasi dicat, se nolle habere comitem, qui diligat terrena; unde infra decimo quarto[^p249-7]: « Qui non renuntiaverit omnibus, quae possidet, non potest meus esse discipulus », hoc est imitator perfectus. Unde Chrysostomus: « Respondet Dominus ad mentem eius, quasi dicat: Quid me sequeris propter pecunias, qui vides, non tantum mihi diversorium esse, quantum avibus »? — Simul etiam cum hoc notat eum *de simulatione exteriori,* quae intelligitur in *vulpe,* secundum quod infra decimo tertio[^p249-8] dicitur de Herode: « Ite, dicite vulpi illi ». Et propterea per *vulpes* intelliguntur haeretici, quoniam tortuosi et subdoli sunt et ignem habent in caudis, sicut dicitur de vulpibus Samsonis Iudicum decimo quinto.

105. Notat etiam eum *de simulatione interiori* in *volucre,* quae alta petit; talis est superbus, sicut dicitur Abdiae primo[^p249-9]: « Si exaltatus fueris ut aquila et inter sidera posueris nidum tuum; inde detraham te, dicit Dominus ». — Et propter haec duo ostendit Dominus eius consortium repudiandum, quia, primae ad Corinthios primo[^p249-10], « ignobilia mundi et contemptibilia elegit Deus, et ea quae non sunt, ut ea quae sunt, destrueret, ut non glorietur omnis caro in conspectu eius ». — Et propterea in exemplum imitationis factus est mendicus, iuxta illud secundae ad Corinthios octavo: « Factus est pro nobis egenus, ut eius inopia divites essemus »; unde imitatores Christi inopia Christi divites facti sunt. Valde enim dives est qui tantum habet, quantum Rex caeli et terrae. Huiusmodi autem est, qui nihil habet proprietate et totum possidet caritate, sicut illi, secundae ad Corinthios sexto[^p249-11], « tanquam nihil habentes et omnia possidentes ». Beatus, qui terrenam foveam commutat in aeternam aulam; « beati autem pauperes spiritu » etc. — Simul etiam cum hoc attende, quod paupertas non est placita nisi *simplex* contra *dolositatem vulpis,* et *humilis* contra *elationem volucris.* Unde Isaiae sexagesimo sexto[^p249-12]: « Ad quem autem respiciam nisi ad pauperculum et contritum spiritu et trementem sermones meos »? ubi iungit paupertatem simplicitati et humilitati. Unde Augustinus[^p249-13]: « Quid prodest divitias reliquisse, cum miser homo superbior efficitur divitias contemnendo, quam fuerat possidendo »? Nam pauper superbus odiosus
<!-- page 250 -->
est Deo et hominibus, secundum illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo quinto: « Tres species odivit anima mea »: « Pauperem superbum » etc.

106. (Vers. 59.). *Ait autem ad alterum.* Post repulsionem duplicium subditur hic *advocatio simplicium,* circa quam duo introducuntur: primum est *vocatio innocentis;* secundum est *eruditio ignorantis.*

Primum igitur quantum ad *innocentis vocationem* dicitur: *Ait autem ad alterum: Sequere me,* sicut *filius patrem;* Ieremiae tertio[^p250-1]: « Patrem vocabis me et post me ingredi non cessabis ». Vel sicut *discipulus magistrum,* sicut dixit Dominus adolescenti consilium quaerenti Matthaei decimo nono: « Si vis perfectus esse, vade, vende omnia, quae habes, et da pauperibus; et veni et sequere me ». Et hunc discretive vocabat, sicut Petrum, Ioannis ultimo: « Tu me sequere ». — Et signanter *alterum* dicit, quia alterius erat dispositionis; erat autem *simplex* et *humilis;* ad quod designandum adiungit: *Ille autem dixit: Domine, permitte mihi primum ire et sepelire patrem meum;* in quo apparet *puritas,* quia non celabat cordis affectum, non refutabat consilium. Unde Isidorus[^p250-2]: « Non respuit esse discipulus, sed expleta pietate paterni funeris, vult liberior sequi ». Apparet *humilitas,* quia mortuo patri impendere volebat obsequium, iuxta illud Ecclesiastici tertio[^p250-3]: « Qui timet Dominum honorat parentes, et quasi dominis serviet his qui se genuerunt, in opere et sermone et omni patientia ». Ad hoc autem movebat eum *obedientia divini praecepti;* Exodi vigesimo[^p250-4]: « Honora patrem tuum »; movebat etiam *clementia animi;* sepelire enim mortuum opus est misericordiae, de qua commendatur Tobias, primo: « Cum occideret rex multos de filiis Israel, Tobias sepeliebat corpora eorum »; movebat etiam *consuetudo laudabilis,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo: « In mortuum produc lacrymas, et quasi dira passus, incipe plorare, et secundum iudicium contege corpus illius et non despicias sepulturam illius ». Unde apparet, quod non dixit hoc ex carnalitate, sed ex quadam pietate et humilitate, sicut Eliseus dixit Eliae tertii Regum decimo nono[^p250-5]: « Osculer, oro, patrem meum et matrem meam, et sic sequar te ».

107. (Vers. 60.). Secundo vero quantum ad *ignorantis eruditionem* subditur: *Dixitque illi*[^p250-6] *Iesus: Sine, ut mortui sepeliant mortuos suos.* In quo primo erudit eum *peccatorum consortium declinare;* nam *mortui* vocantur hic peccatores et infideles; unde ad Ephesios secundo: « Vos cum essetis mortui in delictis et peccatis vestris, in quibus aliquando ambulastis ». Talium consortium deserendum est viro sancto, secundum illud Ecclesiastici decimo tertio[^p250-7]: « Quae communicatio homini sancto ad canem »? id est ad peccatorem et infidelem. Unde quantumcumque sit coniunctus, tamen deserendus, maxime cum impedit bonum. Unde Deuteronomii trigesimo tertio: « Qui dixerit patri suo et matri suae: Nescio vos; et fratribus: Ignoro illos; hi sunt qui custodierunt iudicia tua, o Iacob, et legem tuam, o Israel ».

108. Erudit etiam eum, *minora bona pro maioribus postponenda esse;* unde Ambrosius[^p250-8] docet, « minora bona pro utilitate maiorum esse praetermittenda. Maius est enim animas mortuorum praedicando suscitare, quam corpus mortui in terra abscondere ». — Et propterea subdit: *Tu autem vade et annuntia regnum Dei;* quasi dicat: minus bonum pro maiori intermitte, iuxta quod dicitur primae ad Timotheum quarto[^p250-9]: « Exerce temetipsum ad pietatem. Nam corporalis exercitatio ad modicum utilis est » etc. Ex quo apparet, quod opera pietatis spiritualis omnino secundum sententiam Apostoli et Domini sunt corporalibus operibus anteponenda. Quamvis enim pietatis magnum sit opus sepelire mortuum, et hoc patrem proprium; tamen Christus vult, pro praedicationis officio esse dimittendum. Propter quod dicebant Apostoli Actuum sexto[^p250-10]: « Non est aequum, nos derelinquere verbum Dei et ministrare mensis ». Quantum enim anima melior est corpore, tantum melius est animam pascere praedicando quam corpus alimenta ministrando; vel maxime, quia plures sunt idonei ad sepulturam praebendam quam ad dandam doctrinam. Unde Apostoli
<!-- page 251 -->
dicebant Actuum sexto[^p251-1]: « Considerate viros boni testimonii, quos constituamus super hoc opus. Nos autem orationi et ministerio verbi instantes erimus »; quia dicitur infra decimo: « Maria optimam partem elegit »; et ideo Danielis duodecimo: « Qui ad iustitiam erudiunt multos quasi stellae » etc. — Et nota, quod dicit: *Annuntia regnum Dei,* volentibus scilicet poenitere; iuxta quod dicebat Christus Matthaei quarto[^p251-2]: « Poenitentiam agite; appropinquavit enim regnum caelorum ». Sicut ergo est magnum peccatum, cum indigenti dives non dat eleemosynam; sic maius est, cum sciens non communicat doctrinam; unde Proverbiorum undecimo: « Qui abscondit frumenta maledicetur in populis ». Ideo dicit: *Nuntia,* non abscondas.

109. (Vers. 61.). *Et alter ait: Sequar te* etc. Post repulsionem duplicium et advocationem simplicium subiungit hic *redargutionem mobilium,* circa quam duo introducuntur: primum est *vacillatio voluntatis,* secundum est *redargutio mobilitatis.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *voluntatis vacillationem* praemittit: *Et alter ait: Sequar te*[^p251-3]*;* ecce, voluntas adhaerendi Deo tanquam magistro. Attendebat enim illud dominicum Ioannis octavo: « Qui sequitur me non ambulat in tenebris »; et Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio: « Magna gloria est sequi Dominum ». — Sed in hac voluntate fortis non erat; propter quod addit: *Sed primum permitte, me ire et nuntiare his qui domi sunt*[^p251-4]. Ex quo apparet, quod adhuc afficiebatur erga suos, contra id quod in Psalmo dicitur: « Obliviscere populum tuum et domum patris tui ». Non erat hic similis Paulo, qui dicebat ad Galatas primo: « Cum placuit ei qui me vocavit per gratiam suam, continuo non acquievi carni et sanguini ». Carnales amici spiritualibus consiliis adversantur, et ideo tanquam adversarii sunt dimittendi. Ideo Matthaei decimo[^p251-5] dicitur: « Non veni pacem mittere, sed gladium. Veni separare hominem adversus patrem »; « et inimici hominis domestici eius ». Et ideo dicitur in persona boni religiosi Ieremiae duodecimo: « Reliqui domum meam, dimisi hereditatem meam ». Non talis iste, quia oculum habebat ad domesticos. Huic similes sunt religiosi curiosi de factis consanguineorum; contra quod Beda in Glossa[^p251-6]: « Si discipulus Dominum secuturus arguitur, quod domui renuntiare velit; quid fiet illis qui nulla utilitate saepe visitant domos, quas reliquerant »? Tales non attendunt quod dictum est Abrahae Genesis duodecimo[^p251-7]: « Egredere de terra tua et de cognatione tua et de domo patris tui et veni in terram, quam monstravero tibi ».

110. (Vers. 62.). Secundo vero quantum ad *redargutionem mobilitatis* adiungit: *Ait*[^p251-8] *ad illum Iesus: Nemo mittens manum suam ad aratrum* etc., id est ad divinae perfectionis et praedicationis exercitium, quod recte per *aratrum* designatur propter culturam et fructum, iuxta illud Osee decimo: « Arabit Iudas, et confringet sibi sulcos Iacob ». — *Nemo,* inquam, talis existens, *et aspiciens*[^p251-9] *retro,* id est ad mundum, *aptus est regno Dei,* id est ad regnum Dei possidendum; quia, secundum illud secundae ad Timotheum secundo, « nemo militans Deo implicat se negotiis saecularibus, ut ei placeat, cui se probavit ». — Cuius rei figura praecessit in uxore Lot, quae[^p251-10], « respiciens post se, versa est in statuam salis »; quia talis homo efficitur inutilis et sterilis. Et ideo dicitur infra decimo septimo: « Qui fuerit in agro non redeat retro. Memores estote uxoris Lot ».

111. Talis homo non est aptus ad regnum, sed potius *ad opprobrium;* unde secundae Petri secundo[^p251-11]: « Melius est viam veritatis non cognoscere,
<!-- page 252 -->
quam post agnitam retrorsum converti ab eo, quod illis fuerat traditum sancto mandato. Contingit enim eis illud veri proverbii: Canis reversus ad suum vomitum » etc.; quod recte intelligi potest de recidivantibus et apostatantibus. Et ideo e contrario suadet Ecclesiasticus, quinto[^p252-1]: « Esto firmus in via Domini ». Talis erat Apostolus, sicut dicitur ad Philippenses tertio: « Quae retro obliviscens, ad ea vero, quae sunt priora, extendens me ipsum, ad destinatum persequor, ad bravium supernae vocationis ». Et ideo ipse monebat ad Ephesios tertio[^p252-2]: « In caritate radicati et fundati, ut possitis comprehendere » etc. Sciebat enim, a Domino dictum esse Matthaei decimo: « Qui perseveraverit usque in finem, hic salvus erit ». Ad hoc potissime Scriptura nos invitat; unde per totam Legem et Prophetas[^p252-3] prohibet filiis Israel, ne revertantur in Aegyptum, id est, ne de divino servitio convertatur homo ad mundum. Unde et propter illud delictum fere omnes filii Israel a Domino in deserto interfecti fuerunt.

## English
<!-- page 248 -->
102. *And it came to pass, as they walked* etc. After the formation of the Apostles to the norm of presiding as to humility of mind and equanimity of zeal, here in the third place he forms them *as to perspicacity of judgement*[^p248-11]*;* and this through the example of Jesus Christ. Now perspicacity of judgement is manifested in this part in three ways: first, in *the repelling of the double;* secondly, in *the calling of the simple,* there[^p248-12]: *But he said to another* etc.; but thirdly, in *the convicting of the fickle,* there: *And another said: I will follow thee, Lord* etc.
<!-- page 249 -->

Concerning *the repelling of the double* two things are introduced: the first is *the dissembling of simplicity,* the second is *the detection*[^p249-1] *of duplicity.*

103. (Vers. 57.). First, then, as to *the dissembling of simplicity* he says: *And it came to pass, as they walked in the way,* namely, the disciples walking with the Lord in the way of perfection, as is said of Noe in Genesis, the sixth chapter[^p249-2]: « Noe, a just and perfect man, walked with God »; and likewise the Lord to Abraham, Genesis, the seventeenth chapter: « Walk before me, and be perfect ». Of which way Isaias, the thirtieth chapter: « Thou shalt hear the word of thy teacher. This is the good way; walk ye in it ». — And because in this way the wicked frequently wish to be joined to the good by dissembling, therefore it is added: *A certain man said to Jesus*[^p249-3]*: I will follow thee withersoever thou goest.* In which he showed that he had the will to follow perfectly, while yet he had something else in his heart, like Simon Magus, of whom it is said in Acts, the eighth chapter, that « when men and women were baptized, then Simon himself also believed and was baptized », yet with an evil and double heart, as is plain from what follows. Whence Jerome[^p249-4]: « This man wished to follow Jesus with the intention with which Simon Magus desired to have the power of doing miracles, that he might gain riches from the miracles ». But against this, Ecclesiasticus, the first chapter[^p249-5]: « Come not to him with a double heart, and be not a hypocrite in the sight of men ». Such are they who promise great things and do little things, against whom Ecclesiasticus, the fourth chapter: « Be not hasty in thy tongue, and unprofitable and remiss in thy works ».

104. (Vers. 58.). But secondly, as to *the detection of duplicity,* it is subjoined: *And Jesus said*[^p249-6] *to him: The foxes have holes,* in which, namely, they lie hid, *and the birds of the air nests,* in which they pass the night; *but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head;* in which he *shows* a marvellous *want,* as if to say that he does not wish to have a companion who loves earthly things; whence below, in the fourteenth chapter[^p249-7]: « He that doth not renounce all that he possesseth cannot be my disciple », that is, a perfect imitator. Whence Chrysostom: « The Lord answers to his mind, as if to say: Why dost thou follow me for the sake of money, who seest that I have not so much as a lodging, as much as the birds have »? — At the same time also with this he marks him *for the outward dissembling,* which is understood in *the fox,* according to what is said below, in the thirteenth chapter[^p249-8], of Herod: « Go and tell that fox ». And therefore by *foxes* are understood heretics, since they are crooked and crafty and have fire in their tails, as is said of the foxes of Samson in Judges, the fifteenth chapter.

105. He marks him also *for the inward dissembling* in *the bird,* which seeks high things; such is the proud man, as is said in Abdias, the first chapter[^p249-9]: « If thou be exalted as an eagle, and set thy nest among the stars, thence will I bring thee down, saith the Lord ». — And on account of these two the Lord shows that his fellowship is to be repudiated, because, in the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p249-10], « the base things of the world and the contemptible hath God chosen, and the things that are not, that he might bring to nought the things that are, that no flesh should glory in his sight ». — And therefore, for an example of imitation, he was made a beggar, according to that of the second to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter: « He became needy for us, that through his want we might be rich »; whence the imitators of Christ were made rich by the want of Christ. For he is very rich who has as much as the King of heaven and earth. But such is he who has nothing by ownership and possesses all by charity, as those, in the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter[^p249-11], « as having nothing and possessing all things ». Blessed is he who exchanges an earthly hole for an eternal hall; « but blessed are the poor in spirit » etc. — At the same time also with this attend that poverty is not pleasing unless it is *simple,* against *the guile of the fox,* and *humble,* against *the elation of the bird.* Whence Isaias, the sixty-sixth chapter[^p249-12]: « But to whom shall I have respect, but to the poor little one, and the contrite of spirit, and him that trembleth at my words »? where he joins poverty to simplicity and humility. Whence Augustine[^p249-13]: « What doth it profit to have left riches, when the wretched man is made prouder by despising riches than he had been by possessing them »? For the proud poor man is hateful
<!-- page 250 -->
to God and to men, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fifth chapter: « Three sorts my soul hateth »: « A poor man that is proud » etc.

106. (Vers. 59.). *But he said to another.* After the repelling of the double there is subjoined here *the calling of the simple,* concerning which two things are introduced: the first is *the calling of the innocent;* the second is *the erudition of the ignorant.*

First, then, as to *the calling of the innocent* it is said: *But he said to another: Follow me,* as *a son his father;* Jeremias, the third chapter[^p250-1]: « Thou shalt call me father, and shalt not cease to walk after me ». Or as *a disciple his master,* as the Lord said to the young man seeking counsel, Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « If thou wilt be perfect, go, sell all that thou hast, and give to the poor; and come and follow me ». And this man he called individually, as Peter, John, the last chapter: « Follow thou me ». — And significantly he says *another,* because he was of another disposition; for he was *simple* and *humble;* to designate which he adds: *But he said: Lord, suffer me first to go and to bury my father;* in which there appears *purity,* because he did not conceal the affection of his heart, he did not refuse the counsel. Whence Isidore[^p250-2]: « He does not refuse to be a disciple, but, the piety of his father's funeral fulfilled, he wishes to follow the more freely ». There appears *humility,* because he wished to render a service to his dead father, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter[^p250-3]: « He that feareth the Lord honoureth his parents, and will serve them as his masters that brought him into the world, in work and word and all patience ». And to this there moved him *obedience to the divine precept;* Exodus, the twentieth chapter[^p250-4]: « Honour thy father »; there moved him also *clemency of mind;* for to bury the dead is a work of mercy, for which Tobias is commended, in the first chapter: « When the king slew many of the children of Israel, Tobias buried their bodies »; there moved him also *a praiseworthy custom,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter: « Shed tears over the dead, and, as one that hath suffered dire things, begin to lament, and according to judgment cover his body, and neglect not his burial ». Whence it appears that he did not say this out of carnality, but out of a certain piety and humility, as Eliseus said to Elias, in the third of Kings, the nineteenth chapter[^p250-5]: « Let me kiss, I pray, my father and my mother, and so I will follow thee ».

107. (Vers. 60.). But secondly, as to *the erudition of the ignorant,* it is subjoined: *And Jesus said to him*[^p250-6]*: Let the dead bury their dead.* In which first he schools him *to shun the fellowship of sinners;* for here sinners and unbelievers are called *the dead;* whence to the Ephesians, the second chapter: « You, when you were dead in your offences and sins, wherein in time past you walked ». The fellowship of such is to be forsaken by a holy man, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirteenth chapter[^p250-7]: « What communion hath a holy man with a dog »? that is, with a sinner and unbeliever. Whence however closely he be joined, yet he is to be forsaken, most of all when he hinders the good. Whence Deuteronomy, the thirty-third chapter: « Who hath said to his father and to his mother: I know you not; and to his brethren: I know them not; these are they who have kept thy judgments, O Jacob, and thy law, O Israel ».

108. He schools him also that *the lesser goods are to be put after the greater;* whence Ambrose[^p250-8] teaches that « the lesser goods are to be passed over for the profit of the greater. For it is greater to raise up the souls of the dead by preaching than to hide the body of a dead man in the earth ». — And therefore he subjoins: *But go thou, and announce the kingdom of God;* as if to say: leave off the lesser good for the greater, according to what is said in the first to Timothy, the fourth chapter[^p250-9]: « Exercise thyself unto piety. For bodily exercise is profitable to little » etc. From which it appears that the works of spiritual piety are altogether to be set before bodily works, according to the judgement of the Apostle and of the Lord. For although it is a great work of piety to bury the dead, and this one's own father; yet Christ wills that it be let go for the office of preaching. On account of which the Apostles said in Acts, the sixth chapter[^p250-10]: « It is not reason that we should leave the word of God and serve tables ». For as much as the soul is better than the body, so much is it better to feed the soul by preaching than the body by ministering food; or most of all because more are fit for affording burial than for giving doctrine. Whence the Apostles
<!-- page 251 -->
said in Acts, the sixth chapter[^p251-1]: « Look ye out men of good testimony, whom we may appoint over this business. But we will give ourselves continually to prayer and to the ministry of the word »; because it is said below, in the tenth chapter: « Mary hath chosen the best part »; and therefore Daniel, the twelfth chapter: « They that school many unto justice, as stars » etc. — And note that he says: *Announce the kingdom of God,* namely, to those willing to do penance; according to what Christ said, Matthew, the fourth chapter[^p251-2]: « Do penance, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand ». As, then, it is a great sin when a rich man does not give alms to one in need; so it is a greater when one who knows does not communicate doctrine; whence Proverbs, the eleventh chapter: « He that hideth up corn shall be cursed among the people ». Therefore he says: *Announce,* do not hide.

109. (Vers. 61.). *And another said: I will follow thee* etc. After the repelling of the double and the calling of the simple he subjoins here *the convicting of the fickle,* concerning which two things are introduced: the first is *the wavering of the will,* the second is *the convicting of fickleness.*

First, then, as to *the wavering of the will* he puts first: *And another said: I will follow thee*[^p251-3]*;* behold, the will to cleave to God as to a master. For he attended to that saying of the Lord, John, the eighth chapter: « He that followeth me walketh not in darkness »; and Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third chapter: « It is great glory to follow the Lord ». — But in this will he was not strong; on account of which he adds: *But first suffer me to go and to tell them that are at home*[^p251-4]. From which it appears that he was still attached to his own, against what is said in the Psalm: « Forget thy people and thy father's house ». This man was not like Paul, who said to the Galatians, the first chapter: « When it pleased him who called me by his grace, immediately I condescended not to flesh and blood ». Carnal friends oppose spiritual counsels, and therefore they are to be let go as adversaries. Therefore in Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p251-5], it is said: « I came not to send peace, but the sword. I came to set a man at variance against his father »; « and a man's enemies shall be they of his own household ». And therefore it is said in the person of the good religious, Jeremias, the twelfth chapter: « I have forsaken my house, I have left my inheritance ». Not such was this man, for he had an eye to those of his household. Like him are religious curious about the doings of their kinsfolk; against which Bede in the Gloss[^p251-6]: « If a disciple about to follow the Lord is reproved because he wishes to bid farewell to his house; what will become of those who, with no profit, often visit the houses which they had left »? Such do not attend to what was said to Abraham, Genesis, the twelfth chapter[^p251-7]: « Go forth out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and out of thy father's house, and come into the land which I shall shew thee ».

110. (Vers. 62.). But secondly, as to *the convicting of fickleness,* he adds: *Jesus said*[^p251-8] *to him: No man putting his hand to the plough* etc., that is, to the exercise of divine perfection and of preaching, which is rightly designated by *the plough* on account of the tillage and the fruit, according to that of Osee, the tenth chapter: « Juda shall plough, and Jacob shall break the furrows for himself ». — *No man,* I say, being such, *and looking*[^p251-9] *back,* that is, to the world, *is fit for the kingdom of God,* that is, for possessing the kingdom of God; because, according to that of the second to Timothy, the second chapter, « no man, being a soldier to God, entangleth himself with secular businesses, that he may please him to whom he hath approved himself ». — A figure of this thing went before in Lot's wife, who[^p251-10], « looking behind her, was turned into a statue of salt »; because such a man is made unprofitable and barren. And therefore it is said below, in the seventeenth chapter: « He that shall be in the field, let him not return back. Remember Lot's wife ».

111. Such a man is not fit for the kingdom, but rather *for reproach;* whence the second of Peter, the second chapter[^p251-11]: « It is better not to know the way of truth,
<!-- page 252 -->
than, after having known it, to turn back from that which had been delivered to them by the holy commandment. For that of the true proverb hath befallen them: The dog is returned to his vomit » etc.; which can rightly be understood of those who relapse and apostatize. And therefore, on the contrary, Ecclesiasticus urges, in the fifth chapter[^p252-1]: « Be steadfast in the way of the Lord ». Such was the Apostle, as is said to the Philippians, the third chapter: « Forgetting the things that are behind, and stretching forth myself to those that are before, I pursue towards the mark, to the prize of the supernal vocation ». And therefore he himself admonished, to the Ephesians, the third chapter[^p252-2]: « Rooted and founded in charity, that you may be able to comprehend » etc. For he knew that it had been said by the Lord, Matthew, the tenth chapter: « He that shall persevere unto the end, he shall be saved ». To this Scripture invites us most of all; whence through the whole Law and the Prophets[^p252-3] it forbids the children of Israel to return into Egypt, that is, that man be not turned from the divine service to the world. Whence also on account of that offence almost all the children of Israel were slain by the Lord in the desert.
