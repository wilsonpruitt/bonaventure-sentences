# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. I, Vers. 26-38
## *Tria dicuntur etiam de conceptu Salvatoris*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 20 -->

#### Tria dicuntur etiam de conceptu Salvatoris.

40. *In mense autem sexto* etc. Egit auctor supra de conceptione praecursoris, quae fuit in fecundatione sterilis; in hac parte agit de conceptione *Salvatoris,* quae fuit in fecundatione Virginis. Haec autem fecundatio est facta, *Deo efficiente, Angelo nuntiante* et *Virgine consentiente,* ut reparatio lapsui responderet, sicut dicit Bernardus[^p20-7]: « Placuit Deo eodem modo et ordine sibi reconciliare hominem, quo noverat cecidisse ». Ideo pars ista habet tres partes; in prima insinuatur *destinatio nuntii* a Deo; in secunda, *exsecutio ministerii* ab Angelo, ibi[^p20-8]: *Et ingressus Angelus ad eam;* in tertia, *assecutio propositi,* mediante consensu virgineo, ibi: *Dixit autem Maria ad Angelum: Ecce ancilla* etc.

#### Primo describitur destinatio nuntii quoad quatuor.

*Destinatio* autem *nuntii* describitur debito modo facta ex parte *temporis, legati, loci* et *personae,* ad quam mittitur nuntius Dei.

41. (Vers. 26.). Congruentiam *temporis* insinuat, cum dicit: *In mense autem sexto,* scilicet a conceptione Ioannis, qui fuit Martius[^p20-9], in quo mense homo et mundus fuerat conditus, in quo etiam per Christi incarnationem erat renovandus, ut sic reparatio responderet primae conditioni, ut impleri videatur illud Isaiae sexagesimo sexto: « Sicut caeli novi et terra nova, quae ego facio stare coram me, dicit Dominus; sic stabit semen vestrum et nomen vestrum. Et erit mensis ex mense, et Sabbatum ex Sabbato ». Nec etiam senarius numerus vacat a mysterio; quia enim perfectus est, ideo homo, inter cetera opera Dei perfectus, sexta die est conditus[^p20-10]. Ideo et Christus venit sexta aetate et sexto millenario annorum et sexto mense conceptus et sexta feria
<!-- page 21 -->
passus et sexta hora in cruce suspensus, ut sic significetur advenire in *plenitudine temporum* et perfectione, qui conceptus est sexto mense; ad Galatas quarto: « At ubi venit plenitudo temporis, misit Deus Filium suum » etc.

42. Congruentiam *legati* notat, cum dicit: *Missus est Angelus Gabriel a Deo,* ut sic respondeat reparatio lapsui, et, quemadmodum homo lapsus est per suggestionem angeli mali, sic resurgat per ministerium boni; Proverbiorum decimo tertio[^p21-1]: « Nuntius impii corruet in malum; legatus fidelis, sanitas »; respondeat etiam impletio promissioni, ut, sicut per Gabrielem mysterium illud praedictum fuit Danieli, sic apertum fuit Virgini; Danielis nono: « Ecce, vir Gabriel, quem videram in visione, a principio, cito volans, tetigit me in tempore sacrificii vespertini et docuit me »; et post: « Septuaginta hebdomadae abbreviatae sunt super populum tuum et super urbem sanctam tuam, ut consummetur praevaricatio, et finem accipiat peccatum, et deleatur iniquitas, et adducatur iustitia sempiterna, et impleatur visio et prophetia, et ungatur Sanctus Sanctorum ». Et nota, quod *missus* dicitur *a Deo.* Bernardus[^p21-2]: « Ab ipso Deo mitti perhibetur »; in quo notatur, quod « nulli beatorum spirituum suum ante quam Virgini revelasse putetur consilium, excepto dumtaxat Archangelo Gabriele »; et quia a Deo erat, denuntiationi divini mysterii congruebat.

43. Congruentiam *loci* notat in hoc quod ait: *In civitatem Galilaeae, cui nomen Nazareth,* iuxta quod praedixerat Dominus per Isaiam, nono[^p21-3]: « Aggravata est via maris trans Iordanem Galilaeae gentium, populus, qui ambulabat in tenebris, vidit lucem magnam ». In hoc ergo, quod in civitatem *Galilaeae* missus est, quae erat confinium Iudaeorum et gentium, significatur, quod illum venit annuntiare, qui promissus fuerat Abrahae Genesis vigesimo secundo[^p21-4]: « In semine tuo benedicentur universae cognationes terrae »; et illud Iacob, Genesis penultimo: « Ipse erit exspectatio gentium ». In hoc, quod civitas illa *Nazareth* vocatur, quae *flos* interpretatur, significatur, quod illum veniebat praenuntiare, qui est « flos campi et lilium convallium », Canticorum secundo; et Isaiae undecimo: « Egredietur virga de radice Iesse, et flos de radice eius ascendet »; ubi dicit Hieronymus[^p21-5], quod secundum Hebraicam veritatem habetur: « Nazareus de radice Iesse ascendet ». Igitur a Nazareth potest aliquid boni esse, immo flos omnis boni. Congruebat enim, florem in flore concipi, in flore nutriri, in florum tempore, id est in vere et Martio, nuntiari. Unde Bernardus[^p21-6]: « Florigeram amat patriam flos de radice Iesse ». Et ideo iam poterat decantare: « Flores apparuerunt in terra nostra », Canticorum secundo.

44. (Vers. 27.). Congruentiam *personae* insinuat in hoc quod dicit: *Ad virginem desponsatam;* cuius congruentia ostenditur *pudica,* cum dicit: *Virginem;* primae ad Corinthios septimo[^p21-7]: « Mulier innupta et virgo cogitat quae Dei sunt, quomodo sit sancta corpore et spiritu ». Bernardus: « Missus est ad Virginem, virginem carne, virginem mente, virginem professione, virginem, qualem describit Apostolus, mente et corpore sanctam ». — Ostenditur etiam *probata,* cum dicit: *Desponsatam viro, cui nomen erat Ioseph* etc., ut scilicet « Ioseph, vir iustus », secundum quod dicitur Matthaei primo[^p21-8], esset testis castitatis ipsius, secundum illud Proverbiorum ultimo: « Et vir eius laudabit eam ». Bernardus: « *Virum* nominat, non quia maritus, sed quia homo virtutis et iustus erat »; et ideo fuit testis legitimus. Bernardus: « Uno tali consilio secretis caelestibus et admittitur testis, et exluditur hostis, et integra servatur fama Virginis Matris; alioquin, quomodo pepercisset iustus adulterae » ? « Quia zelus et furor viri non parcet », Proverbiorum sexto. — Ostenditur etiam *promissa* in hoc quod dicit: *De domo David;* quod refertur ad Virginem et Ioseph, quia uterque de semine David, cui facta erat repromissio in Psalmo[^p21-9]: « Iuravit Dominus David veritatem et non frustrabitur eam; de fructu ventris tui » etc. Bernardus: « Ambo erant de domo et familia David; sed in altera completa est veritas, quam iuravit Dominus David, altero tantum teste et conscio », scilicet ipso Ioseph. — Ostenditur etiam *praenominata* in hoc quod dicit: *Et nomen Virginis Maria. Maria* enim stella maris interpretatur, et sic ostenditur impleta illa prophetia Balaam; Numerorum vigesimo quarto[^p21-10]: « Orietur stella ex Iacob, et con-
<!-- page 22 -->
surget virga de Israel »; Ecclesiastici quinquagesimo: « Quasi stella matutina in medio nebulae »; Apocalypsis ultimo: « Ego sum radix et genus David, stella splendida et matutina ».

45. Ideo ergo nominat Virginem, ut ostendatur, ipsam fuisse praeparatam, non casu inventam. Bernardus[^p22-1]: « Non est noviter, aut fortuito inventa, sed a saeculo electa, ab Altissimo praecognita, sibi praeparata, sibi et servata, a Patribus praesignata, a Prophetis promissa ». Quod enim Moysi monstratum est in rubo et igne, Aaron in virga et flore, Gedeoni in vellere et rore, hoc Salomon praevidit in forti muliere et eius pretio, Ieremias praececinit de femina et viro, apertissime declarat Isaias de virgine et Deo, Gabriel tandem exhibuit ipsam *Virginem* salutando. — Vel ideo nominat Virginem, ut nos doceat nomen ipsius in necessitatibus invocare; unde Bernardus in Homilia[^p22-2]: « O quisquis te intelligis in huius saeculi profluvio magis inter procellas et tempestates fluctuare quam super terram ambulare; respice stellam, voca Mariam! Si iactaris superbiae undis, si ambitionis, si detractionis, si aemulationis; respice stellam, voca Mariam. Si ira, aut avaritia, aut carnis illecebra naviculam concusserit mentis; respice stellam, voca Mariam. Si criminum immanitate turbatus, si conscientiae foeditate confusus, barathro desperationis incipias absorberi; respice ad Mariam ». Unde et dicit: « Ipsa tenente, non corruis; ipsa protegente, non metuis; ipsa duce, non fatigaris; ipsa propitia, pervenis ». — Vel ideo nominat, ut insinuet nomen eius mysterio plenum secundum triplicem interpretationem[^p22-3], per quam triplicem statum intelligimus salvandorum: *activos* per mare amarum, *contemplativos* per stellam, *praelatos* per dominium.

#### Secundo describitur executio ministerii quoad tria.

46. *Et ingressus Angelus* etc. Postquam descripta est destinatio nuntii, hic describitur *exsecutio ministerii,* quae quidem fuit in denuntiatione conceptus futuri, in qua procedit Angelus, tanquam bonus rhetor, valde ordinate, et Evangelista explicat perfecte. Praemittitur enim primo *salutationis ingressus;* secundo additur *narrationis processus,* ibi[^p22-4]: *Et ait ei Angelus: Ne timeas;* subiungitur tertio *conclusionis terminus,* ibi: *Ideoque et quod nascetur ex te sanctum.* Istae enim sunt partes principaliores orationis rhetoricae, scilicet exordium, narratio et conclusio, secundum Tullium[^p22-5].

Primo ergo praemittitur *salutationis ingressus,* secundo additur *narrationis processus,* in quo introducitur primo *Angelus salutans,* et deinde *Virgo auscultans,* ibi[^p22-6]: *Quae cum audisset* etc. — Introducit ergo Evangelista, Angelum in suo *ingressu* a salutatione sumsisse exordium; in qua salutatione ipsius *captat benevolentiam, erigit confidentiam* et *exhibet reverentiam.* Ostendit enim eam commendabilem a *bonitatis plenitudine,* et ideo amabilem; a *dignitatis altitudine,* et ideo venerabilem; a *laudis amplitudine,* et ideo praedicabilem. Et propter haec tria designata fuit per arcam foederis, de qua dicitur ad Hebraeos nono[^p22-7], quod continebat tria, scilicet « virgam Aaron, tabulas Legis et manna »; ut sic ostendatur, quod ipsa erat dulcis et amabilis per manna, venerabilis per virgam, praedicabilis et commendabilis per legem divinam. Et secundum hoc fuit in ipsa triplex excellentia, scilicet *dignitatis, virtutis* et *caritatis;* Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto: « Ego mater pulcrae dilectionis et timoris et agnitionis et sanctae spei ».

47. (Vers. 28.). Ut ergo ostendat eam *amabilem gratiositate,* dicit: *Ingressus Angelus ad eam, dixit: Ave, gratia plena.* Et ideo bene per Esther designata est, de qua Esther secundo[^p22-8] dicitur, quod erat formosa valde et incredibili pulcritudine, omnium oculis gratiosa et amabilis videbatur. Unde de ipsa potest dici illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto: « In me omnis gratia vitae et veritatis, in me omnis spes vitae et virtutis ». Nec mirum, quia illum erat conceptura, de quo dicitur Ioannis primo: « Vidimus gloriam eius, gloriam quasi Unigeniti a Patre, plenum gratiae et veritatis »; et ideo post subditur: « De plenitudine eius nos omnes accepimus ».

48. Ut autem ostendat *venerabilem ex dignitate,* addit: *Dominus tecum;* discretive dictum est, quia excellenti et singulari modo, tanquam in proprio tabernaculo; Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto[^p22-9]: « Qui creavit me requievit in tabernaculo meo »; Psalmus: « Dominus in templo sancto suo », hoc est in Vir-
<!-- page 23 -->
ginis utero; et rursus: « Sanctificavit tabernaculum suum Altissimus, Deus in medio eius, non commovebitur ». Bernardus[^p23-1]: « *Dominus tecum;* illius eris mater, cuius est Deus pater; Filius paternae caritatis erit corona tuae castitatis »; ex quo super omnes sortiris inaestimabilem dignitatem, et propterea *regina* dicitur in Psalmo: « Astitit regina a dextris tuis ».

49. Ut etiam ostendat ipsam *laudabilem ex benedictione,* subiungit: *Benedicta tu in mulieribus,* propter universale remedium; primi Regum vigesimo quinto[^p23-2]: « Dixit David ad Abigail: Benedicta tu, quae prohibuisti, ne ulciscerer me manu mea ». *Benedicta,* inquam, *in mulieribus,* id est *inter mulieres;* Iudicum quinto: « Benedicta inter mulieres Iahel, benedicatur in tabernaculo ». Vel: benedicta *in mulieribus,* id est super omnes mulieres; Iudith decimo tertio: « Benedicta es, filia, a Domino Deo excelso prae omnibus mulieribus super terram »; et iterum decimo quinto: « Benedixerunt illam omnes una voce dicentes: Tu gloria Ierusalem, tu laetitia Israel, tu honorificentia populi nostri, quia fecisti viriliter, et confortatum est cor tuum, eo quod castitatem amaveris, et post virum tuum alterum nescieris; ideo et manus Domini confortavit te, et ideo eris benedicta ». Vel: benedicta *a mulieribus;* Canticorum sexto[^p23-3]: « Viderunt eam filiae Sion et beatissimam praedicaverunt, et reginae et concubinae laudaverunt eam ».

50. (Vers. 29.). *Quae cum audisset* etc. Introduxit prius Angelum salutantem, hic introducit *Virginem auscultantem,* in qua auscultatione ostendit, Virginem commendabilem esse tripliciter, in *auditu* scilicet, *affectu* et *cogitatu.* — In *auditu* commendatur *modestia,* cum dicitur: *Quae cum audisset,* in silentio et taciturnitate auscultasset, secundum illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo secundo[^p23-4]: « Audi tacens, et pro reverentia accedet tibi bona gratia ». Sic prompta fuit ad audiendum, licet non ad loquendum, secundum illud Iacobi primo: « Sit omnis homo velox ad audiendum, tardus autem ad loquendum ». Et propterea dicit: *audisset,* id est perfecte audientiam praebuisset; « auris enim bona cum omni concupiscentia audiet sapientiam », Ecclesiastici tertio.

51. In *affectu* commendatur *verecundia,* cum dicitur: *Turbata est in sermone eius,* scilicet ex verecundia. Beda[^p23-5]: « Trepidare virginum est et ad omnes viri ingressus pavere, omnes viri aspectus vereri »; Habacuc tertio: « Audivi, et conturbatus est venter meus, a voce contremuerunt labia mea »; ut posset dicere spiritus eius illud Iob vigesimo tertio: « A facie eius turbatus sum, et considerans eum, timore sollicitor ». Exemplum huius Esther, penultimo: « Vidi te, domine, quasi Angelum Dei, et turbatum est cor meum prae timore gloriae tuae ».

52. In *cogitatu* laudatur *prudentia,* cum dicitur: *Et cogitabat, qualis esset ista salutatio;* et hoc erat magna prudentia. Continebat enim profunditatem sapientiae, de qua Sapientiae sexto[^p23-6]: « Cogitare de illa sensus est consummatus »; unde Danielis quarto: « Daniel, cuius nomen Balthassar, coepit intra semetipsum tacitus cogitare quasi una hora, et cogitationes eius conturbabant eum ». Cogitationes autem Virginis, etsi turbarent propter pudorem innocentiae, non tamen perturbabant propter splendorem intelligentiae. Bernardus[^p23-7]: « Turbata est, sed non perturbata; immo secundum illud Psalmi: Turbatus sum et non sum locutus, sed cogitavi dies antiquos » etc.

53. *Et ait Angelus ei* etc. Postquam praemissus est salutationis ingressus, hic secundo loco sequitur *narrationis tractatus* sive processus, in quo primo introducit Evangelista *Angelum narrantem;* secundo, *Virginem interrogantem,* ibi[^p23-8]: *Dixit autem Maria ad Angelum: Quomodo fiet istud* etc.

*Narrans* autem *Angelus,* denuntiat id, propter quod venerat, et hoc quidem facit hoc ordine. Primo exprimit *divinae acceptionis beneplacitum;* secundo, *salutiferae fecundationis beneficium*[^p23-9]: *Ecce, concipies;* tertio, *nasciturae prolis fastigium: Hic erit magnus et Filius Altissimi vocabitur* etc.

54. (Vers. 30.). Exprimens igitur Angelus *divinae acceptionis beneplacitum,* confortat Virginis animum et nominat: *Ne timeas, Maria. Ne timeas,* inquam, sed gaudeas, quia nomen tuum scriptum est in caelo, secundum quod infra dicitur ad Apostolos, decimo[^p23-10]: « Gaudete, quod nomina vestra scripta sunt in caelis ». Et rationem reddit: *Invenisti enim gratiam apud Deum,* gratiam scilicet *electionis,* sicut Moyses; Exodi trigesimo tertio: « Invenisti gratiam apud me, et te ipsum novi ex nomine »; sic dicitur Virgini Mariae. Gratiam etiam *perfectionis,* sicut Noe; Genesis sexto: « Noe invenit gratiam coram Deo »; et paulo post: « Noe vir iustus atque perfectus cum Deo ambulavit ». Gratiam *desponsationis,* ut fieret sponsa Dei et mater Filii Dei; Esther secundo[^p23-11]: « Habuit Esther gratiam et misericordiam coram Assuero super omnes mulieres,
<!-- page 24 -->
et posuit diadema regni in capite eius ». Invenit autem gratiam propter nimiam humilitatem et mansuetudinem; Iacobi quarto: « Deus superbis resistit, humilibus autem dat gratiam »; et Ecclesiastici tertio: « Quanto magnus es, humilia te in omnibus, et coram Deo invenies gratiam ».

55. (Vers. 31.). Exprimens etiam *salutiferae conceptionis* seu fecundationis *beneficium,* ostendit, ipsum esse proximum, cum dicit: *Ecce, concipies in utero et paries filium;* ut sic impleatur in te illud Isaiae septimo[^p24-1]: « Ecce, virgo concipiet et pariet filium ». Et signanter dicit: *Ecce, concipies in utero,* ut ostendat, hoc ipsum esse magnum et mirum et novum, ut concipiat intra, nihil recipiendo ab extra; Ieremiae trigesimo primo: « Novum fecit Dominus super terram: femina circumdabit virum ». Et quia conceptum sine libidine sequitur partus sine parturitione et dolore, ideo addit: *Et paries filium,* secundum illud Isaiae ultimo[^p24-2]: « Antequam parturiret, peperit. Quis audivit unquam tale, aut quis vidit huic simile »? Et quia partum sine dolore sequitur fructus uteri cum salute, ideo subdit: *Vocabis nomen eius Iesum;* quia, secundum quod dicitur Actuum quarto[^p24-3], « nec aliud nomen est sub caelo datum hominibus, in quo oporteat nos salvos fieri ». Huius nominis praefiguratio praecessit in Iesu, filio Nave; Ecclesiastici quadragesimo sexto: « Fortis in bello Iesus Nave, qui fuit magnus secundum nomen suum, maximus in salutem electorum Dei ». Huius etiam praefiguratio praecessit in Ioseph, de quo dicitur Genesis quadragesimo primo, quod « Pharao vertit nomen eius et vocavit eum lingua Aegyptiaca *Salvatorem mundi* ».

56. (Vers. 32.). Postremo exprimens *nasciturae prolis fastigium,* ostendit ipsum magnum, cum dicit: *Hic erit magnus.* Ostendit autem, magnitudinem eius excellentissimam esse propter *gratiam singularem,* propter *excellentiam regalem,* propter *potentiam aeternalem.* — *Singularem gratiam* tangit, cum dicit: *Et Filius Altissimi vocabitur,* scilicet per gratiam unionis; *Filius,* inquam, unigenitus, secundum illud Ioannis primo[^p24-4]: « Vidimus gloriam eius, gloriam quasi Unigeniti a Patre ». Et ita aequalis erit Altissimo, secundum illud Psalmi: « Tu Dominus altissimus super omnem terram ». Quis est hic nisi Christus Dominus? de quo ad Philippenses secundo: « Dedit illi nomen, quod est super omne nomen, ut in nomine Iesu omne genu flectatur » etc. Hoc autem nulli alii dedit, quia in solo Filio Virginis est gratia unionis.

57. *Regalem excellentiam* insinuat, cum dicit: *Et dabit illi Dominus Deus sedem David, patris eius,* id est regale solium, secundum quod David erat *promissum*[^p24-5]: « De fructu ventris tui ponam super sedem tuam »; et secundum quod per Ieremiam Prophetam, Ieremiae vigesimo tertio, promissum fuit: « Ecce, dies veniunt, dicit Dominus. Et suscitabo David germen iustum, et regnabit rex et sapiens erit ». Hoc etiam primo *ostensum* fuit Danieli septimo, de Filio hominis, de quo dicit, quod « antiquus dierum dedit ei potestatem et honorem et regnum, et omnes populi et tribus et linguae servient ei ».

58. *Aeternalem potentiam* notat, cum dicit: *Et regnabit in domo Iacob in aeternum,* secundum quod promissum erat David, secundum illud secundi Regum septimo[^p24-6]: « Suscitabo semen, quod egredietur de utero tuo, et firmabo regnum eius; et stabiliam thronum regni eius usque in sempiternum ». Hoc ipsum ait Daniel septimo: « Potestas eius, potestas aeterna, quae non auferetur, et regnum eius, quod non corrumpetur ».

59. (Vers. 33.). Ut autem ostendat, quod aeternitas ista est proprie dicta per carentiam finis, addit: *Et regni eius non erit finis;* ut sic impleatur illud Isaiae nono[^p24-7]: « Super solium David et super regnum eius sedebit, ut confirmet illud et corroboret in iudicio et iustitia, amodo et usque in sempiternum ». Et ideo recte dicit Psalmista: « Regnum tuum regnum omnium saeculorum, et dominatio tua in omni generatione et generationem ».

60. (Vers. 34.). *Dixit autem Maria ad Angelum.* Introduxit prius Evangelista Angelum narrantem; subiungit hic prudentissimam *Virginem percunctantem;* in qua percunctatione tria nobis explicantur, scilicet *dubitatio conveniens, ratio movens* et *solutio satisfaciens.* — *Conveniens dubitatio* notatur, cum dicitur: *Quomodo fiet istud?* Cum enim triplex sit concipiendi modus: unus carnalis, alius spiritualis et tertius admirabilis et singularis; quaero, quo istorum modorum fiat? Nicodemus sic quaerebat a Domino, cum audiret, quod oporteret, eum renasci denuo; Ioannis tertio[^p24-8]: « Quomodo, inquit, possunt haec fieri »? Et nota, quod non quaerit modum *sciendi* sive signum, quod fidem faciat, sicut Zacharias, qui punitus fuit, supra eodem: « Unde hoc sciam? Ego enim sum senex, et uxor mea processit in diebus suis »; sed quaerit modum *fiendi,* ut consentiat, secundum illud Proverbiorum quarto: « Palpebrae tuae praecedant gressus tuos ».

61. *Rationem moventem* tangit, cum dicit: *Quoniam virum non cognosco,* id est, non cognoscituram propono, et ita sum virgo mente et carne et proposito, ut sibi recte competat illud Rebeccae, Ge-
<!-- page 25 -->
nesis vigesimo quarto[^p25-1], quod erat « puella decora nimis virgoque pulcherrima et incognita viro ». Et secundum Apostolum, primae ad Corinthios septimo, iam non cogitabat, quae carnis erant, sed « quae Domini sunt, quomodo esset sancta et corpore et spiritu »; et ideo non irrationabiliter quaerebat, quomodo debeat prolem concipere, quae non proponebat virum cognoscere, ut, si posset esse, quod haberet simul virginitatem et fecunditatem, tunc consensum praeberet.

62. (Vers. 35.). Et quoniam dubitatio sua rationem habebat recte moventem; ideo meruit *responsionem satisfacientem,* quae notatur ibi: *Et respondens Angelus dixit ei: Spiritus sanctus superveniet in te, et virtus Altissimi obumbrabit tibi;* quasi dicat Angelus: quaeris, quomodo concipies? Ad hoc respondeo, quod fecundaberis sine corruptione, concipies sine libidine, paries sine dolore, quia non ex virili semine, sed ex Spiritus sancti virtute. Ita dictum est Matthaei primo[^p25-2]: « Quod in ea natum est de Spiritu sancto est ». Et recte dicit: *Spiritus sanctus superveniet,* id est, desursum veniet; Iacobi primo: « Omne datum optimum et omne donum perfectum desursum est, descendens a Patre luminum ». Propter hoc dicitur Actuum primo: « Accipietis virtutem supervenientis Spiritus sancti in vos »; et ita non erit *corruptio,* quia de Spiritu sancto. Non etiam erit *libido,* quia *virtus Altissimi obumbrabit tibi,* scilicet ne sentias aestum concupiscentiae, secundum illud Canticorum secundo[^p25-3]: « Sub umbra illius, quem desideraveram, sedi ».

63. Et nota, trium praecedentium triplicem praecessisse figuram: quia *sine virili semine,* in virga Aaron, quae floruit, Numerorum decimo septimo[^p25-4]; Isaiae undecimo: « Flos de radice eius ascendet » etc.; quia *sine libidine,* in rubo et igne, Exodi tertio, quia rubus ardebat et non comburebatur; quia *partus sine dolore* designatur in rore et vellere Gedeonis, Iudicum sexto. « Vellus enim, ut dicit Hieronymus[^p25-5], cum sit de corpore, nescit tamen corporis passionem »; et ideo in Psalmo: « Descendet sicut pluvia in vellus ».

64. *Ideoque et quod nascetur ex te sanctum* etc. Postquam descriptus est ingressus salutationis et processus narrationis, ponitur hic ultimo *terminus conclusionis;* et conclusio est, quod virgo Maria concipiat, et ex ea nascatur Filius Dei. In hac autem illatione[^p25-6] primo praemittit Angelus suam *conclusionem,* et deinde *approbationem,* et postremo utriusque subiungit *rationem.*

*Conclusionem* infert, cum dicit: *Ideoque et quod nascetur ex te sanctum vocabitur Filius Dei.* Quia enim fecundaris virtute Spiritus sancti, non prolem viri generabis, sed prolem Dei, non peccatorem, sed sanctum; Danielis nono[^p25-7]: « Impleatur visio et prophetia, et ungatur Sanctus Sanctorum ». Si enim, quemadmodum dicitur Ioannis tertio, « quod natum est ex carne caro est, et quod natum est de spiritu spiritus est »: ergo quod nascitur de Spiritu sancto necessario sequitur esse sanctum. Bernardus[^p25-8]: « Si diceret: sancta caro, vel sanctus homo, parum dixisse videretur; posuit ergo indefinite *sanctum,* quia quidquid fuit, quod Virgo genuit, sanctum procul dubio ac singulariter sanctum fuit ». Et quia filii hominum nascuntur polluti, ad Ephesios secundo[^p25-9]: « Omnes nascimur natura filii irae »; ideo iam non filius viri, sed *Filius Dei vocabitur;* Isaiae nono: « Et vocabitur nomen eius admirabilis, consiliarius, Deus, fortis » etc. *Vocabitur* ab ipso *Deo;* Marci nono[^p25-10]: « Hic est Filius meus carissimus »; et Matthaei decimo septimo: « Hic est Filius meus dilectus, in quo mihi bene complacui, ipsum audite ». Vocabitur ab ipsis *credentibus;* Matthaei decimo sexto: « Tu es Christus, Filius Dei vivi »; et ad Romanos primo: « Qui praedestinatus est Filius Dei in virtute ». Vocabitur ab *omnibus;* Matthaei penultimo dixit centurio: « Vere Filius Dei erat iste ».

65. (Vers. 36.). *Approbationem* autem sive confirmationem adiungit, cum dicit: *Ecce, Elisabeth, cognata tua, et ipsa concepit filium in senectute sua.* Si ergo Deus dedit conceptum seni, dare potest et iuveni, licet istud novum, illud iam factum; Genesis vigesimo primo[^p25-11]: « Concepit Sara et peperit filium in senectute sua ». — Ut melius constet Virgini, additur: *Et hic mensis est sextus illi quae vocatur sterilis.* Et ita cum manifesta fuerit eius sterilitas, iam est manifesta eius fecunditas. Iam ergo incipiunt signa et mirabilia fieri in terra, secundum illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo sexto[^p25-12]: « Innova signa et immuta mirabilia ». Et ideo firmum et certum est, quod et tu, Virgo, iam concipias supra naturam; et hoc est quod dicit Beda in Glossa: « Ne Virgo desperet de partu, accipit exemplum sterilis et anus pariturae, ut discat, omnia Deo esse
<!-- page 26 -->
possibilia, quae ordini naturae videntur esse contraria ». Et ideo Dominus tot sterilium conceptus praemisit, ut istum conceptum figurarent et figurando praenuntiarent et praenuntiando testimonium darent et credibilem facerent.

66. (Vers. 37.). *Rationem* autem huius subiungit, cum dicit: *Quia non erit impossibile apud Deum omne verbum;* Ecclesiastae octavo[^p26-1]: « Omne, quod voluerit, faciet, et sermo illius potestate plenus est »; Matthaei decimo nono: « Apud Deum omnia possibilia sunt », sicut dictum est Sarae, Genesis decimo octavo: « Nunquid Deo quidquam est difficile »? Bernardus[^p26-2]: « Apud Dominum idem est facere quod loqui, et loqui quod velle. Merito ergo noveris, non esse impossibile omne verbum »; et ideo, cum aliquid promittit Deus, sine dubitatione credendum est, quia, cum omnia possit et mentiri non possit, necesse est, ut fiat ita, sicut promittit; Numerorum vigesimo tertio[^p26-3]: « Non est Deus quasi homo, ut mentiatur; nec ut filius hominis, ut mutetur. Dixit ergo, et non faciet? Locutus est, et non implebit »? — Et sic ostendit Angelus suam conclusionem necessariam, ut iam Virgo nec dubitare possit nec valeat dissentire.

#### Tertio describitur assecutio propositi quoad tria.

67. (Vers. 38.). *Dixit autem Maria* etc. Hic ultimo ponitur *assecutio propositi,* quod fuit in consensu Virginis, per quem mox concepit Filium Dei. Hunc autem consensum describit debite et ordinate factum: quia ex *humilitate praedisponente* et *caritate perficiente* et *credulitate pronuntiante.*

Introducitur ergo primo *pronuntiatio ex credulitate,* cum dicitur: *Dixit autem Maria* ad Angelum. Quia enim Angeli dictum credidit esse verum, ideo dat expresse responsum consentaneum; ad Romanos decimo[^p26-4]: « Corde creditur ad iustitiam, ore autem confessio fit ad salutem »; et in Psalmo: « Credidi, propter quod locutus sum ». *Dixit;* de hoc verbo fidei dicitur ad Romanos decimo: « Prope est verbum in ore tuo et in corde tuo, hoc est verbum fidei, quod praedicamus ». Quia ergo corde concepit verbum fidei, ventre concepit Filium Dei; infra eodem: « Beata, quae credidisti » etc.; et undecimo: « Beatus venter, qui te portavit, et ubera, quae suxisti ».

68. Deinde sequitur *praeparatio ex humilitate,* cum dicitur: *Ecce, ancilla Domini.* Simile responsum dedit Abigail, mulier prudens, cum David vellet eam uxorem accipere; primi Regum vigesimo quinto[^p26-5]: « Ecce, famula tua sit in ancillam, ut lavet pedes servorum domini mei »; et illa Ruth, quae designat beatam Virginem, Ruth secundo: « Unde hoc mihi, ut invenirem gratiam ante oculos tuos »? quoniam « locutus es ad cor ancillae tuae, quae non sum similis unius puellarum tuarum ». Et quia se virgo Maria humiliavit, se ad gratiam praeparavit. Bernardus[^p26-6]: « Humiliter respondetur, ut sedes praeparetur ». Et quia singularis humilitas, ideo et singularis fuit gratia; unde Bernardus: « Non est magnum, esse humilem in abiectione; magna prorsus et rara virtus humilitas honorata »; hoc « est luminare, quod minuitur in consummatione », Ecclesiastici quadragesimo tertio.

69. Postremo subditur *consensus perfectus ex caritate,* cum dicitur: *Fiat mihi secundum verbum tuum.* « *Fiat,* ut dicit Bernardus[^p26-7], est *desiderii signum,* non dubitationis indicium »; etiam est *signum assertionis,* sicut in Psalmo dicitur: « Dicet omnis populus: Fiat, fiat »; et iterum *affectionis,* ut in Psalmo: « Fiat manus tua, ut salvet me » — idem enim est *verbum* et *manus* Patris — et potest esse *orationis verbum,* ut sic sit *desideratorium,* quia ex caritate; *oratorium,* quia ex humilitate; *assertorium,* quia ex credulitate[^p26-8]. — Haec est vox dulcissima hominibus, Angelis et ipsi Sponso. Hanc petebat in Canticorum secundo: « Sonet vox tua in auribus meis; vox enim tua dulcis, et facies tua decora »; quod facit, cum dixit Angelo: *Fiat mihi secundum verbum tuum.* « Verbum, quod erat in principio apud Deum, fiat caro de carne mea, secundum verbum tuum[^p26-9] ». Unde Bernardus: « Fiat mihi, obsecro, verbum non prolatum, ut transeat, sed conceptum, ut maneat. Fiat verbum non tantum audibile auribus, sed et visibile oculis, palpabile manibus, gesta-
<!-- page 27 -->
bile humeris », ut verbo virtutis suae me portet[^p27-1]. « Porro *multifariam multisque modis olim locutus est Deus Patribus in Prophetis,* et aliis in corde, aliis in aure, aliis in ore, aliis in visu, aliis in manu; mihi, oro, ut fiat iuxta verbum tuum; nec volo, ut fiat mihi aut declamatorie praedicatum, aut figuraliter signatum, aut imaginaliter somniatum, sed silenter inspiratum, personaliter incarnatum, corporaliter invisceratum » « Verbum caro factum »[^p27-2]. Et si ita fiet, tunc potero dicere tibi, quod dixit mulier Sarephtana Eliae tertii Regum decimo septimo: « Nunc cognovi, quoniam vir Dei es tu, et verbum Dei verum in ore tuo est ».

70. Et quoniam in tali consensu conceptus est Dei Filius, et propositum suum assecutus est Angelus; ideo sequitur: *Et discessit ab illa Angelus.* Hoc enim ostendebat facto, quod Raphael Tobiae dicebat verbo, Tobiae duodecimo[^p27-3]: « Tempus est, ut revertar ad eum qui me misit »; et de Petro dicitur Actuum duodecimo, quod ipso educto de carcere, « continuo discessit Angelus ab eo ». Recessit ab ea Angelus, sed remansit cum ea Dei Filius; discessit quantum ad apparentiam, sed remanserunt multi quantum ad custodiam; Canticorum tertio: « En, lectulum Salomonis sexaginta fortes ambiunt ex fortissimis Israel ». Isti sunt Angeli beatissimi, qui custodiebant eam tanquam « locum divinae habitationis[^p27-4] » electissimum; unde etsi beneficium custodiae sibi impenderent propter infirmitatem carnis, cultum tamen reverentiae exhibebant propter dignitatem Matris, et ideo quodam modo per ipsam ascendebant et descendebant. Unde designata fuit per illam *scalam,* cui Dominus erat innixus, et Angeli per illam ascendebant, quam vidit Iacob, Genesis vigesimo octavo[^p27-5]; et post sequitur: « Non est hic aliud nisi *domus* Dei et *porta* caeli », quia nullus potest iam caelum intrare, nisi per Mariam transeat tanquam per *portam.* Sicut enim Deus ad nos venit per ipsam, ita per ipsam nos oportet redire in Deum. Et ideo dicitur *domus, porta* et *scala: domus* propter Christi conceptionem, *porta* propter Christi partum et *scala* propter ascensum ad Deum. — Non ergo discedamus ab ea, sed eius pedibus provoluti, semper eam salutemus: *Ave, gratia plena,* ut per eam quae invenit gratiam et misericordiam super omnes mulieres in conspectu illius magni Assueri, nos « gratiam inveniamus et misericordiam consequamur in auxilio opportuno[^p27-6] ».

#### Three things are also said of the conception of the Saviour.

40. *And in the sixth month* etc. The author treated above of the conception of the precursor, which was in the fecundation of a barren woman; in this part he treats of the conception of the *Saviour,* which was in the fecundation of a Virgin. Now this fecundation was brought about, *God effecting, the angel announcing* and *the Virgin consenting,* so that the restoration might answer to the fall, as Bernard[^p20-7] says: « It pleased God to reconcile man to himself in the same mode and order in which he knew he had fallen ». Therefore this part has three parts: in the first is insinuated *the destining of the messenger* by God; in the second, *the execution of the ministry* by the angel, there[^p20-8]: *And the angel being come in unto her;* in the third, *the attainment of the purpose,* by means of the virginal consent, there: *And Mary said to the angel: Behold the handmaid* etc.

#### Firstly the destining of the messenger is described as to four things.

Now *the destining* of *the messenger* is described as made in due mode on the part of *the time,* of *the envoy,* of *the place* and of *the person* to whom the messenger of God is sent.

41. (Vers. 26.). He insinuates the fittingness of *the time* when he says: *And in the sixth month,* namely from the conception of John, which was March[^p20-9], in which month man and the world had been founded, in which also it was to be renewed through the incarnation of Christ, so that thus the restoration might answer to the first founding, that there may be seen to be fulfilled that of Isaias, the sixty-sixth chapter: « For as the new heavens and the new earth, which I make to stand before me, saith the Lord: so shall your seed stand, and your name. And there shall be month after month, and sabbath after sabbath ». Nor yet is the number six empty of mystery; for because it is perfect, therefore man, perfect among the other works of God, was founded on the sixth day[^p20-10]. Therefore also Christ came in the sixth age and in the sixth millenary of years, and was conceived in the sixth month, and on the sixth day of the week
<!-- page 21 -->
suffered and at the sixth hour hung upon the cross, so that thus it might be signified that he came in *the fulness of times* and in perfection, he who was conceived in the sixth month; to the Galatians, the fourth chapter: « But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent his Son » etc.

42. He notes the fittingness of *the envoy* when he says: *The angel Gabriel was sent from God,* so that thus the restoration might answer to the fall, and, just as man fell through the suggestion of an evil angel, so he might rise again through the ministry of a good one; Proverbs, the thirteenth chapter[^p21-1]: « The messenger of the wicked shall fall into evil; a faithful ambassador, health »; and that the fulfilment might also answer to the promise, so that, as that mystery was foretold to Daniel through Gabriel, so it was opened to the Virgin; Daniel, the ninth chapter: « Behold, the man Gabriel, whom I had seen in the vision, at the beginning, flying swiftly, touched me at the time of the evening sacrifice and instructed me »; and afterwards: « Seventy weeks are shortened upon thy people and upon thy holy city, that transgression may be finished, and sin may have an end, and iniquity may be abolished, and everlasting justice may be brought, and vision and prophecy may be fulfilled, and the Saint of saints may be anointed ». And note that he is said to be *sent from God.* Bernard[^p21-2]: « He is declared to be sent by God himself »; in which it is noted that « God is not to be thought to have revealed his counsel to any of the blessed spirits before the Virgin, save only the Archangel Gabriel »; and because he was from God, he was fitting for the declaration of the divine mystery.

43. He notes the fittingness of *the place* in this, that he says: *Into a city of Galilee, called Nazareth,* according to what the Lord had foretold through Isaias, the ninth chapter[^p21-3]: « The way of the sea beyond the Jordan of the Galilee of the Gentiles was heavily loaded; the people that walked in darkness saw a great light ». In this, then, that he was sent into a city of *Galilee,* which was the border of the Jews and of the Gentiles, it is signified that he came to announce him who had been promised to Abraham, Genesis, the twenty-second chapter[^p21-4]: « In thy seed shall all the kindreds of the earth be blessed »; and that of Jacob, Genesis, the next to last chapter: « He shall be the expectation of nations ». In this, that that city is called *Nazareth,* which is interpreted *flower,* it is signified that he was coming to announce beforehand him who is « the flower of the field and the lily of the valleys », the Canticle, the second chapter; and Isaias, the eleventh chapter: « There shall come forth a rod out of the root of Jesse, and a flower shall rise up out of his root »; where Jerome[^p21-5] says that according to the Hebrew truth it reads: « A Nazarene shall rise up out of the root of Jesse ». Therefore something good can come from Nazareth, nay, the flower of all good. For it was fitting that the flower should be conceived in the flower, nourished in the flower, announced in the time of flowers, that is, in spring and in March. Whence Bernard[^p21-6]: « The flower from the root of Jesse loves a flower-bearing homeland ». And therefore she could already sing: « The flowers have appeared in our land », the Canticle, the second chapter.

44. (Vers. 27.). He insinuates the fittingness of *the person* in this, that he says: *To a virgin espoused;* whose fittingness is shown as *chaste,* when he says: *A virgin;* the first to the Corinthians, the seventh chapter[^p21-7]: « The unmarried woman and the virgin thinketh on the things of God, that she may be holy both in body and in spirit ». Bernard: « He was sent to a Virgin, a virgin in flesh, a virgin in mind, a virgin in profession, a virgin such as the Apostle describes, holy in mind and in body ». — She is shown also as *proved,* when he says: *Espoused to a man whose name was Joseph* etc., namely so that « Joseph, being a just man », according to what is said in Matthew, the first chapter[^p21-8], might be the witness of her chastity, according to that of Proverbs, the last chapter: « And her husband shall praise her ». Bernard: « He names him *a man,* not because he was a husband, but because he was a man of virtue and just »; and therefore he was a lawful witness. Bernard: « By one such counsel in the heavenly secrets both a witness is admitted and the enemy is shut out [so printed: *exluditur* for *excluditur*], and the fame of the Virgin Mother is kept whole; otherwise, how would the just man have spared an adulteress »? « Because the jealousy and rage of the husband will not spare », Proverbs, the sixth chapter. — She is shown also as *promised* in this, that he says: *Of the house of David;* which is referred to the Virgin and to Joseph, because both were of the seed of David, to whom the promise had been made in the Psalm[^p21-9]: « The Lord hath sworn truth to David, and he will not make it void: of the fruit of thy womb » etc. Bernard: « Both were of the house and family of David; but in the one was fulfilled the truth which the Lord swore to David, the other being only witness and privy to it », namely Joseph himself. — She is shown also as *named beforehand* in this, that he says: *And the virgin's name was Mary.* For *Mary* is interpreted star of the sea, and thus that prophecy of Balaam is shown fulfilled; Numbers, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p21-10]: « A star shall rise out of Jacob, and a sceptre
<!-- page 22 -->
shall spring up from Israel »; Ecclesiasticus, the fiftieth chapter: « As the morning star in the midst of a cloud »; the Apocalypse, the last chapter: « I am the root and stock of David, the bright and morning star ».

45. Therefore, then, he names the Virgin, that it may be shown that she was prepared, not found by chance. Bernard[^p22-1]: « She was not newly or by chance found, but chosen from of old, foreknown by the Most High, prepared for himself, and kept for himself, foreshown by the Fathers, promised by the Prophets ». For what was shown to Moses in the bush and the fire, to Aaron in the rod and the flower, to Gedeon in the fleece and the dew, this Solomon foresaw in the valiant woman and her price, Jeremias foretold in song of the woman and the man, Isaias most openly declares of the virgin and God[^tr-corrigenda], Gabriel at last showed forth in saluting the *Virgin* herself. — Or he names the Virgin for this reason, to teach us to call upon her name in our necessities; whence Bernard in the Homily[^p22-2]: « O whoever thou art that perceivest thyself in the flood of this world to be tossed among storms and tempests rather than to walk upon the land: look to the star, call upon Mary! If thou art tossed by the waves of pride, of ambition, of detraction, of envy: look to the star, call upon Mary. If anger, or avarice, or the allurement of the flesh have shaken the little ship of the mind: look to the star, call upon Mary. If, troubled by the enormity of crimes, confounded by the foulness of conscience, thou beginnest to be swallowed up in the abyss of despair: look to Mary ». Whence also he says: « While she holds thee, thou fallest not; while she protects thee, thou fearest not; while she leads thee, thou art not wearied; while she is propitious, thou arrivest ». — Or he names her for this reason, to insinuate that her name is full of mystery according to a threefold interpretation[^p22-3], through which we understand the threefold state of those to be saved: *the active* through the bitter sea, *the contemplative* through the star, *prelates* through the lordship.

#### Secondly the execution of the ministry is described as to three things.

46. *And the angel being come in* etc. After the destining of the messenger has been described, here is described *the execution of the ministry,* which indeed consisted in the declaration of the conception to come, in which the angel proceeds most orderly, as a good rhetorician, and the Evangelist sets it out perfectly. For first is put forward *the entry of the salutation;* secondly is added *the course of the narration,* there[^p22-4]: *And the angel said to her: Fear not;* thirdly is subjoined *the term of the conclusion,* there: *And therefore also the Holy which shall be born of thee.* For these are the more principal parts of a rhetorical oration, namely exordium, narration and conclusion, according to Tully[^p22-5].

First, then, is put forward *the entry of the salutation,* secondly is added *the course of the narration,* in which is introduced first *the angel saluting,* and then *the Virgin listening,* there[^p22-6]: *Who having heard* etc. — The Evangelist, then, introduces the angel as having taken the exordium, at his *entry,* from the salutation; in which salutation of his he *captures goodwill, raises up confidence* and *shows reverence.* For he shows her to be commendable from *the fulness of goodness,* and therefore lovable; from *the height of dignity,* and therefore venerable; from *the amplitude of praise,* and therefore worthy to be preached. And on account of these three she was designated by the ark of the covenant, of which it is said to the Hebrews, the ninth chapter[^p22-7], that it contained three things, namely « the rod of Aaron, the tables of the Law and the manna »; so that thus it may be shown that she was sweet and lovable through the manna, venerable through the rod, worthy to be preached and commendable through the divine law. And according to this there was in her a threefold excellence, namely of *dignity,* of *virtue* and of *charity;* Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter: « I am the mother of fair love, and of fear, and of knowledge, and of holy hope ».

47. (Vers. 28.). In order, then, to show her *lovable by graciousness,* he says: *The angel being come in unto her, said: Hail, full of grace.* And therefore she is well designated by Esther, of whom it is said in Esther, the second chapter[^p22-8], that she was exceeding fair and of incredible beauty, and appeared gracious and lovable in the eyes of all. Whence that of Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter, can be said of her: « In me is all grace of life and of truth, in me is all hope of life and of virtue ». Nor is it a wonder, because she was to conceive him of whom it is said in John, the first chapter: « We saw his glory, the glory as it were of the Only-begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth »; and therefore it is added afterwards: « Of his fulness we all have received ».

48. But in order to show her *venerable from dignity,* he adds: *The Lord is with thee;* it is said distinctively, because in an excellent and singular mode, as in his own tabernacle; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p22-9]: « He that made me rested in my tabernacle »; the Psalm: « The Lord is in his holy temple », that is, in the Vir-
<!-- page 23 -->
gin's womb; and again: « The Most High hath sanctified his own tabernacle; God is in the midst thereof, it shall not be moved ». Bernard[^p23-1]: « *The Lord is with thee;* thou shalt be the mother of him whose father is God; the Son of the Father's charity shall be the crown of thy chastity »; whence thou obtainest an inestimable dignity above all, and therefore she is called *queen* in the Psalm: « The queen stood on thy right hand ».

49. In order also to show her *praiseworthy from blessing,* he subjoins: *Blessed art thou among women,* on account of the universal remedy; the first of Kings, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p23-2]: « David said to Abigail: Blessed art thou, who hast kept me from revenging me with my own hand ». *Blessed,* I say, *among women,* that is, *amid women;* Judges, the fifth chapter: « Blessed among women be Jahel, blessed be she in her tent ». Or: blessed *among women,* that is, above all women; Judith, the thirteenth chapter: « Blessed art thou, O daughter, by the Lord the most high God, above all women upon the earth »; and again in the fifteenth: « They all blessed her with one voice, saying: Thou art the glory of Jerusalem, thou art the joy of Israel, thou art the honour of our people, for thou hast done manfully, and thy heart has been strengthened, because thou hast loved chastity, and after thy husband hast not known any other; therefore also the hand of the Lord hath strengthened thee, and therefore thou shalt be blessed ». Or: blessed *by women;* the Canticle, the sixth chapter[^p23-3]: « The daughters of Sion saw her and preached her most blessed, and the queens and concubines praised her ».

50. (Vers. 29.). *Who having heard* etc. He introduced first the angel saluting; here he introduces *the Virgin listening,* in which listening he shows that the Virgin is commendable in three ways, namely in *hearing,* in *affection* and in *thought.* — In *hearing* her *modesty* is commended, when it is said: *Who having heard,* that is, had listened in silence and stillness, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-second chapter[^p23-4]: « Hear in silence, and for thy reverence good grace shall come to thee ». Thus she was prompt to hear, though not to speak, according to that of James, the first chapter: « Let every man be swift to hear, but slow to speak ». And therefore he says: *having heard,* that is, had perfectly given audience; « for a good ear will hear wisdom with all desire », Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter.

51. In *affection* her *shamefastness* is commended, when it is said: *She was troubled at his saying,* namely out of shamefastness. Bede[^p23-5]: « It is the part of virgins to tremble, and to be afraid at all the entries of a man, to fear all the looks of a man[^tr-corrigenda-2] »; Habacuc, the third chapter: « I have heard, and my bowels were troubled; my lips trembled at the voice »; so that her spirit could say that of Job, the twenty-third chapter: « I am troubled at his presence, and when I consider him, I am made pensive with fear ». An example of this is Esther, the next to last chapter: « I saw thee, my lord, as an angel of God, and my heart was troubled for fear of thy glory ».

52. In *thought* her *prudence* is praised, when it is said: *And thought what manner of salutation this should be;* and this was great prudence. For it contained the depth of wisdom, of which Wisdom, the sixth chapter[^p23-6]: « To think upon her is perfect understanding »; whence Daniel, the fourth chapter: « Daniel, whose name was Baltassar, began silently to think within himself for about one hour, and his thoughts troubled him ». But the thoughts of the Virgin, although they troubled her on account of the shame of innocence, yet did not perturb her, on account of the splendour of intelligence. Bernard[^p23-7]: « She was troubled, but not perturbed; nay, according to that of the Psalm: I was troubled and I spoke not, but I thought upon the days of old » etc.

53. *And the angel said to her* etc. After the entry of the salutation has been put forward, here in the second place follows *the treatment of the narration,* or its course, in which first the Evangelist introduces *the angel narrating;* secondly, *the Virgin asking,* there[^p23-8]: *And Mary said to the angel: How shall this be done* etc.

Now *the angel, narrating,* declares that for which he had come, and this indeed he does in this order. First he expresses *the good pleasure of the divine acceptance;* secondly, *the benefit of the salutary fecundation*[^p23-9]: *Behold, thou shalt conceive;* thirdly, *the eminence of the offspring to be born: He shall be great and shall be called the Son of the Most High* etc.

54. (Vers. 30.). The angel, then, expressing *the good pleasure of the divine acceptance,* strengthens the Virgin's mind and names her: *Fear not, Mary. Fear not,* I say, but rejoice, because thy name is written in heaven, according to what is said below to the Apostles, in the tenth chapter[^p23-10]: « Rejoice that your names are written in heaven ». And he gives the reason: *For thou hast found grace with God,* namely the grace *of election,* as Moses did; Exodus, the thirty-third chapter: « Thou hast found grace with me, and I know thee by name »; so it is said to the Virgin Mary. The grace also *of perfection,* as Noe did; Genesis, the sixth chapter: « Noe found grace before God »; and a little after: « Noe, a just and perfect man, walked with God ». The grace *of espousal,* that she might become the spouse of God and the mother of the Son of God; Esther, the second chapter[^p23-11]: « Esther had grace and mercy before Assuerus above all women,
<!-- page 24 -->
and he set the diadem of the kingdom on her head ». Now she found grace on account of her exceeding humility and meekness; James, the fourth chapter: « God resisteth the proud, but to the humble he giveth grace »; and Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter: « The greater thou art, humble thyself in all things, and thou shalt find grace before God ».

55. (Vers. 31.). Expressing also *the benefit of the salutary conception,* or fecundation, he shows that it is near, when he says: *Behold, thou shalt conceive in thy womb and shalt bring forth a son;* so that thus there may be fulfilled in thee that of Isaias, the seventh chapter[^p24-1]: « Behold, a virgin shall conceive and bear a son ». And he says pointedly: *Behold, thou shalt conceive in thy womb,* to show that this itself is great and wonderful and new, that she should conceive within, receiving nothing from without; Jeremias, the thirty-first chapter: « The Lord hath made a new thing upon the earth: a woman shall compass a man ». And because upon a conception without lust there follows a birth without travail and pain, therefore he adds: *And shalt bring forth a son,* according to that of Isaias, the last chapter[^p24-2]: « Before she was in labour, she brought forth. Who hath ever heard such a thing, or who hath seen the like to this »? And because upon a birth without pain there follows the fruit of the womb with salvation, therefore he subjoins: *Thou shalt call his name Jesus;* because, according to what is said in the Acts, the fourth chapter[^p24-3], « there is no other name under heaven given to men, whereby we must be saved ». A prefiguring of this name went before in Jesus, the son of Nave; Ecclesiasticus, the forty-sixth chapter: « Valiant in war was Jesus the son of Nave, who was great according to his name, very great for the saving of the elect of God ». A prefiguring of it also went before in Joseph, of whom it is said in Genesis, the forty-first chapter, that « Pharao turned his name and called him in the Egyptian tongue *the Saviour of the world* ».

56. (Vers. 32.). Lastly, expressing *the eminence of the offspring to be born,* he shows him to be great, when he says: *He shall be great.* And he shows that his greatness is most excellent on account of *singular grace,* on account of *royal excellence,* on account of *eternal power.* — He touches upon *singular grace* when he says: *And shall be called the Son of the Most High,* namely through the grace of union; *the Son,* I say, only-begotten, according to that of John, the first chapter[^p24-4]: « We saw his glory, the glory as it were of the Only-begotten of the Father ». And so he will be equal to the Most High, according to that of the Psalm: « Thou art the Lord most high over all the earth ». Who is this but Christ the Lord? of whom to the Philippians, the second chapter: « He hath given him a name which is above every name, that in the name of Jesus every knee should bow » etc. But this he gave to no other, because in the Son of the Virgin alone is the grace of union.

57. He insinuates *royal excellence* when he says: *And the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of David his father,* that is, the royal seat, according as it had been *promised* to David[^p24-5]: « Of the fruit of thy womb I will set upon thy throne »; and according as it was promised through Jeremias the Prophet, Jeremias, the twenty-third chapter: « Behold, the days come, saith the Lord. And I will raise up to David a just branch, and a king shall reign and shall be wise ». This also was first *shown* to Daniel, the seventh chapter, concerning the Son of man, of whom he says that « the Ancient of days gave him power and honour and a kingdom, and all peoples and tribes and tongues shall serve him ».

58. He notes *eternal power* when he says: *And he shall reign in the house of Jacob for ever,* according as it had been promised to David, according to that of the second of Kings, the seventh chapter[^p24-6]: « I will raise up the seed which shall proceed out of thy womb, and I will establish his kingdom; and I will establish the throne of his kingdom for ever ». This same thing Daniel says, the seventh chapter: « His power is an everlasting power that shall not be taken away, and his kingdom that shall not be destroyed ».

59. (Vers. 33.). But to show that this eternity is properly so called through the lack of an end, he adds: *And of his kingdom there shall be no end;* so that thus that of Isaias, the ninth chapter[^p24-7], may be fulfilled: « He shall sit upon the throne of David and upon his kingdom, to establish it and strengthen it with judgement and with justice, from henceforth and for ever ». And therefore the Psalmist rightly says: « Thy kingdom is a kingdom of all ages, and thy dominion in every generation and generation ».

60. (Vers. 34.). *And Mary said to the angel.* The Evangelist first introduced the angel narrating; here he subjoins *the Virgin,* most prudent, *inquiring;* in which inquiry three things are set out for us, namely *a fitting doubt, a moving reason* and *a satisfying solution.* — *A fitting doubt* is noted when it is said: *How shall this be done?* For since the mode of conceiving is threefold, one carnal, another spiritual and the third wonderful and singular, I ask by which of these modes it is to be done. Nicodemus asked thus of the Lord when he heard that he must be born again; John, the third chapter[^p24-8]: « How, he says, can these things be done »? And note that she does not ask for a mode *of knowing,* or a sign to produce faith, as Zachary did, who was punished, above, in the same chapter: « Whereby shall I know this? For I am an old man, and my wife is advanced in her days »; but she asks the mode *of its being done,* that she may consent, according to that of Proverbs, the fourth chapter: « Let thy eyelids go before thy steps ».

61. He touches upon *the moving reason* when he says: *Because I know not man,* that is, I purpose that I will not know one, and so I am a virgin in mind and in flesh and in purpose, so that that of Rebecca rightly befits her, Ge-
<!-- page 25 -->
nesis, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p25-1], that she was « an exceeding comely maid, and a most beautiful virgin, and not known to man ». And according to the Apostle, the first to the Corinthians, the seventh chapter, she was no longer thinking on the things that are of the flesh, but on « the things of the Lord, that she may be holy both in body and in spirit »; and therefore not unreasonably did she ask how she should conceive offspring, she who did not purpose to know man, so that, if it could be that she should have virginity and fecundity together, she would then give her consent.

62. (Vers. 35.). And since her doubt had a reason rightly moving it, therefore it merited *a satisfying response,* which is noted there: *And the angel answering said to her: The Holy Spirit shall come upon thee, and the power of the Most High shall overshadow thee;* as if the angel were to say: thou askest how thou shalt conceive? To this I respond that thou shalt be made fruitful without corruption, shalt conceive without lust, shalt bring forth without pain, because not from the seed of man but from the power of the Holy Spirit. So it is said in Matthew, the first chapter[^p25-2]: « That which is born in her is of the Holy Spirit ». And he says rightly: *The Holy Spirit shall come upon thee,* that is, shall come from above; James, the first chapter: « Every best gift and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights ». For this reason it is said in the Acts, the first chapter: « You shall receive the power of the Holy Spirit coming upon you »; and so there will be no *corruption,* because it is of the Holy Spirit. Nor will there be *lust,* because *the power of the Most High shall overshadow thee,* namely so that thou feel not the heat of concupiscence, according to that of the Canticle, the second chapter[^p25-3]: « I sat down under his shadow, whom I desired ».

63. And note that a threefold figure went before the three preceding things: because *without the seed of man,* in the rod of Aaron which flowered, Numbers, the seventeenth chapter[^p25-4]; Isaias, the eleventh chapter: « A flower shall rise up out of his root » etc.; because *without lust,* in the bush and the fire, Exodus, the third chapter, because the bush was burning and was not burnt; because *a birth without pain* is designated in the dew and the fleece of Gedeon, Judges, the sixth chapter. « For the fleece, as Jerome[^p25-5] says, although it is of the body, yet knows not the passion of the body »; and therefore in the Psalm: « He shall come down like rain upon the fleece ».

64. *And therefore also the Holy which shall be born of thee* etc. After the entry of the salutation and the course of the narration have been described, here lastly is put *the term of the conclusion;* and the conclusion is that the virgin Mary conceives, and that from her the Son of God is born. Now in this inference[^p25-6] the angel first puts forward his *conclusion,* and then *the approval,* and lastly subjoins *the reason* of both.

He infers *the conclusion* when he says: *And therefore also the Holy which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God.* For because thou art made fruitful by the power of the Holy Spirit, thou shalt not beget the offspring of a man but the offspring of God, not a sinner but the Holy; Daniel, the ninth chapter[^p25-7]: « That vision and prophecy may be fulfilled, and the Saint of saints may be anointed ». For if, as is said in John, the third chapter, « that which is born of the flesh is flesh, and that which is born of the spirit is spirit », then it necessarily follows that what is born of the Holy Spirit is holy. Bernard[^p25-8]: « If he had said: holy flesh, or holy man, he would seem to have said too little; he therefore put *holy* indefinitely, because whatever it was that the Virgin bore was beyond doubt holy, and singularly holy ». And because the sons of men are born defiled, to the Ephesians, the second chapter[^p25-9]: « We are all born by nature children of wrath »; therefore he is now not the son of a man, but *shall be called the Son of God;* Isaias, the ninth chapter: « And his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, God, Mighty » etc. He *shall be called* so by *God* himself; Mark, the ninth chapter[^p25-10]: « This is my most beloved Son »; and Matthew, the seventeenth chapter: « This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him ». He shall be called so by *believers* themselves; Matthew, the sixteenth chapter: « Thou art Christ, the Son of the living God »; and to the Romans, the first chapter: « Who was predestinated the Son of God in power ». He shall be called so by *all;* in Matthew, the next to last chapter, the centurion said: « Indeed this was the Son of God ».

65. (Vers. 36.). And he adds *the approval,* or confirmation, when he says: *And behold thy cousin Elizabeth, she also hath conceived a son in her old age.* If, then, God gave conception to an old woman, he can give it also to a young one, though this is new and that already done; Genesis, the twenty-first chapter[^p25-11]: « Sara conceived and bore a son in her old age ». — That it may be the clearer to the Virgin, it is added: *And this is the sixth month with her that is called barren.* And so, as her barrenness had been manifest, now her fecundity is manifest. Now, then, signs and wonders begin to be done on the earth, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-sixth chapter[^p25-12]: « Renew thy signs and work new wonders ». And therefore it is firm and certain that thou too, Virgin, now conceivest above nature; and this is what Bede says in the Gloss: « Lest the Virgin despair of childbearing, she receives the example of a barren and aged woman who is to bring forth, that she may learn that all things are possible
<!-- page 26 -->
to God which seem to be contrary to the order of nature ». And therefore the Lord sent before so many conceptions of barren women, that they might figure this conception, and by figuring announce it beforehand, and by announcing it beforehand give testimony and make it credible.

66. (Vers. 37.). And he subjoins *the reason* of this when he says: *Because no word shall be impossible with God;* Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter[^p26-1]: « He will do all that he will, and his word is full of power »; Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « With God all things are possible », as was said to Sara, Genesis, the eighteenth chapter: « Is there anything hard to God »? Bernard[^p26-2]: « With the Lord it is the same to do as to speak, and to speak as to will. Rightly, then, mayest thou know that no word is impossible »; and therefore, when God promises anything, it is to be believed without doubting, because, since he can do all things and cannot lie, it is necessary that it be done just as he promises; Numbers, the twenty-third chapter[^p26-3]: « God is not as a man, that he should lie, nor as the son of man, that he should be changed. Hath he said then, and will he not do? Hath he spoken, and will he not fulfil »? — And thus the angel shows his conclusion to be necessary, so that the Virgin now can neither doubt nor is able to dissent.

#### Thirdly the attainment of the purpose is described as to three things.

67. (Vers. 38.). *And Mary said* etc. Here lastly is put *the attainment of the purpose,* which consisted in the consent of the Virgin, through which she forthwith conceived the Son of God. And he describes this consent as made duly and in order: because from *humility disposing beforehand,* and *charity perfecting,* and *credulity pronouncing.*

First, then, is introduced *the pronouncing from credulity,* when it is said: *And Mary said* to the angel. For because she believed the angel's saying to be true, therefore she gives an expressly consenting response; to the Romans, the tenth chapter[^p26-4]: « With the heart we believe unto justice, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation »; and in the Psalm: « I have believed, therefore have I spoken ». *She said;* of this word of faith it is said to the Romans, the tenth chapter: « The word is nigh thee, in thy mouth and in thy heart; this is the word of faith which we preach ». Because, then, she conceived the word of faith in her heart, she conceived the Son of God in her womb; below, in the same chapter: « Blessed art thou that hast believed » etc.; and in the eleventh: « Blessed is the womb that bore thee, and the paps that thou hast sucked ».

68. Then follows *the preparation from humility,* when it is said: *Behold the handmaid of the Lord.* A like response was given by Abigail, a prudent woman, when David wished to take her to wife; the first of Kings, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p26-5]: « Behold, let thy servant be a handmaid, to wash the feet of the servants of my lord »; and that Ruth, who designates the blessed Virgin, Ruth, the second chapter: « Whence is this to me, that I should find grace before thy eyes »? since « thou hast spoken to the heart of thy handmaid, who am not like to one of thy maids ». And because the virgin Mary humbled herself, she prepared herself for grace. Bernard[^p26-6]: « She responds humbly, that a seat may be prepared ». And because her humility was singular, therefore her grace too was singular; whence Bernard: « It is no great thing to be humble in abjection; a great and rare virtue indeed is humility honoured »; this « is a light that decreaseth in its perfection », Ecclesiasticus, the forty-third chapter.

69. Lastly is subjoined *the consent perfected from charity,* when it is said: *Be it done to me according to thy word.* « *Be it done,* as Bernard[^p26-7] says, is *a sign of desire,* not an indication of doubt »; it is also *a sign of assertion,* as is said in the Psalm: « All the people shall say: So be it, so be it »; and again *of affection,* as in the Psalm: « Let thy hand be done, to save me » — for the *Word* and the *hand* of the Father are the same — and it can be *a word of prayer,* so that thus it is *desiderative,* because from charity; *oratory,* because from humility; *assertory,* because from credulity[^p26-8]. — This is a voice most sweet to men, to angels and to the Spouse himself. This he was asking for in the Canticle, the second chapter: « Let thy voice sound in my ears, for thy voice is sweet, and thy face comely »; which she does when she said to the angel: *Be it done to me according to thy word.* « Let the Word, which was in the beginning with God, be made flesh of my flesh according to thy word[^p26-9] ». Whence Bernard: « Be it done to me, I beseech, the word, not uttered so as to pass, but conceived so as to remain. Be it done, the word, not only audible to the ears but also visible to the eyes, palpable to the hands, porta-
<!-- page 27 -->
ble on the shoulders », that he may bear me up by the word of his power[^p27-1]. « Now *at sundry times and in divers manners God spoke in times past to the Fathers by the Prophets,* and to some in the heart, to some in the ear, to some in the mouth, to some in sight, to some in the hand; to me, I pray, be it done according to thy word; nor do I will that it be done to me either preached in declamation, or signified in figure, or dreamed in image, but silently inspired, personally incarnate, bodily embowelled » « the Word made flesh »[^p27-2]. And if it shall be done so, then I shall be able to say to thee what the woman of Sarephta said to Elias, the third of Kings, the seventeenth chapter: « Now I know that thou art a man of God, and the word of God in thy mouth is true ».

70. And since in such a consent the Son of God was conceived, and the angel had attained his purpose, therefore there follows: *And the angel departed from her.* For he was showing by deed what Raphael was saying to Tobias by word, Tobias, the twelfth chapter[^p27-3]: « It is time that I return to him that sent me »; and it is said of Peter in the Acts, the twelfth chapter, that when he had been led out of prison, « immediately the angel departed from him ». The angel withdrew from her, but the Son of God remained with her; he departed as to appearance, but many remained as to guard; the Canticle, the third chapter: « Behold, threescore valiant ones of the most valiant of Israel surround the little bed of Solomon ». These are the most blessed angels, who guarded her as the most choice « place of the divine dwelling[^p27-4] »; whence, although they bestowed on her the benefit of guarding on account of the infirmity of the flesh, yet they showed her the worship of reverence on account of the dignity of the Mother, and therefore in a certain mode they were ascending and descending by her. Whence she was designated by that *ladder* on which the Lord was leaning, and by which the angels were ascending, which Jacob saw, Genesis, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p27-5]; and after it follows: « This is no other but the *house* of God and the *gate* of heaven », because no one can now enter heaven unless he pass through Mary as through *the gate.* For as God came to us through her, so through her we must return to God. And therefore she is called *house, gate* and *ladder: house* on account of Christ's conception, *gate* on account of Christ's birth, and *ladder* on account of the ascent to God. — Let us not, then, depart from her, but, prostrate at her feet, let us ever salute her: *Hail, full of grace,* so that through her who found grace and mercy above all women in the sight of that great Assuerus, we may « find grace and obtain mercy in seasonable aid[^p27-6] ».
