# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. VII, Vers. 24-35
## *Et cum discessissent etc. Supra comprobavit testimonium Ioannis per evidentiam veritatis in se; hic secundo comprobat per evidentiam virtutis in testificante Ioanne*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 177 -->

47. *Et cum discessissent* etc. Supra[^p177-8] comprobavit testimonium Ioannis per evidentiam veritatis in se; hic secundo comprobat per *evidentiam virtutis in testificante Ioanne.* Et haec pars habet duas partes, in quarum prima multipliciter *extollitur praerogativa virtutis in Ioanne;* in secunda vero *redarguitur perfidia incredulitatis in plebe,* ibi[^p177-9]: *Cui ergo similes dicam homines?*

*Praerogativa* autem *virtutis* ostenditur fuisse quadrupliciter in Ioanne; primo scilicet quantum ad *meritum vitae;* secundo vero, quantum ad *officium doctrinae,* ibi[^p177-10]: *Sed quid existis videre? Prophetam* etc.; tertio, quantum ad *donum gratiae,* ibi: *Dico enim vobis: Inter natos mulierum* etc.; quarto, quantum ad *praeconium famae,* ibi: *Et omnis populus audiens* etc.

Quantum ad primum notandum, quod meritum vitae dupliciter commendatur in Ioanne: primo, quantum ad *constantiam* respectu difficilium; secundo vero, quantum ad *abstinentiam* respectu delectabilium.

48. (Vers. 24.). Introducitur ergo primo commendatio *constantiae* Ioannis in absentia discipulorum; propter quod dicit: *Et cum discessissent nuntii Ioannis*[^p177-11], solutione quaestionis obtenta, revertentes tanquam boni nuntii ad eum, a quo missi fuerant; de quibus Iob trigesimo octavo: « Nunquid mittes fulgura, et ibunt et revertentia dicent tibi: Adsumus »? — In horum absentia laudari debebat Ioannes, ut ostendatur laus esse vera, non adulatoria; ideo dicitur: *Coepit de Ioanne dicere ad turbas; coepit* utique ipsum turbis laudare, ne videretur Ioannes ex dubitatione discipulos ad Christum transmisisse. — Ideo incipit eum de constantia commendare: *Quid existis in desertum videre? Arundinem vento agitatam?* quasi dicat: non est Ioannes similis arundini, ut quolibet impulsu trepidet et concutiatur, sed fortis et fixus in fide et in omni bonitate; iuxta quod Iacobi primo[^p177-12]: « Postulet in fide nihil haesitans; qui enim haesitat similis est fluctui maris, qui a vento movetur et circumfertur ». « Vir autem duplex animo inconstans est in omnibus viis suis ». Talis vere est *arundo,* non talis erat Ioannes, cum esset sanctissimus, cum dicatur Ecclesiastici vigesimo septimo[^p177-13]: « Homo sanctus in sapientia remanet sicut sol; nam stultus sicut luna mutatur ». Tales sunt omnes Sancti, secundum illud ad Ephesios quarto: « Non simus parvuli fluctuantes et circumferamur omni vento doctrinae in nequitia hominum ». Ideo rogabat Paulus secundae ad Thessalonicenses secundo: « Rogamus vos, fratres, per adventum Domini nostri Iesu Christi, ut non cito moveamini a vestro sensu ». Hanc autem constantiam qui vult adipisci debet relinquere mundum et exire ad Ioannem in desertum, secundum quod dicitur primi Machabaeorum secundo[^p177-14]: « Exclamavit Mathathias voce magna: Omnis, qui habet zelum Legis, exeat post me »; et sequitur, quod « multi exierunt in desertum ».
<!-- page 178 -->

49. (Vers. 25.). Secundo vero subditur commendatio *abstinentiae* Ioannis respectu delectabilium; propter quod dicit: *Sed quid existis videre?* id est considerare: *Hominem mollibus vestimentis vestitum*[^p178-1]? quasi dicat: non; Ioannes enim sanctissimus ab omni mollitie carnalitatis erat alienus, sicut dicitur Matthaei tertio: « Habebat autem Ioannes vestimentum de pilis camelorum, esca autem eius erant locustae ». Hic vere erat de numero virorum evangelicorum, in quorum persona primae ad Timotheum sexto[^p178-2] dicitur: « Habentes alimenta et quibus tegamur, his contenti simus »; *quibus tegamur* dicit, non: quibus ornemur. Bernardus ad Eugenium: « Apostoli fortes fuerunt in bello, non molles in serico; quodsi filius es Apostolorum et Prophetarum, et tu fac similiter ». Sed qui hoc vult facere, oportet quod deserat mundum et cum Ioanne exeat in desertum. — Propter quod adiungit: *Ecce, qui in veste pretiosa sunt et in*[^p178-3] *deliciis in domibus regum sunt;* quasi dicat: vestis pretiosa et vita deliciosa sunt hominum mundanorum, non virorum spiritualium et christianorum. Unde primae Petri tertio[^p178-4] dicitur de mulieribus: « Quarum non sit extrinsecus capillatura aut circumdatio auri et argenti aut indumenti vestimentorum cultus ». Super quo Gregorius: « Nemo putet in cultu pretiosarum vestium deesse peccatum. Pensate, quae culpa sit hoc etiam viros appetere, a quo curavit pastor Ecclesiae etiam feminas prohibere ». Huiusmodi namque vestimenta pretiosa sunt carnalia et deliciosa, in deliciis autem periclitatur spiritualis vita; unde primae ad Timotheum quinto[^p178-5]: « Vidua, quae in deliciis est, vivens mortua est »; Ieremiae trigesimo primo: « Usquequo deliciis dissolveris, filia vaga »? Huiusmodi autem vestes pretiosae et deliciosae consueverunt esse vanae gloriae occasio; et ideo addit: *In domibus regum sunt;* de quo Ecclesiastici undecimo[^p178-6]: « In vestitu ne glorieris unquam, nec in die honoris tui extollaris ».

50. (Vers. 26.). *Sed quid existis videre?* Postquam commendavit Salvator Ioannem quantum ad meritum vitae, hic commendat quantum ad *officium doctrinae,* et recto ordine, quia prior debet esse vita bona quam doctrina authentica. Commendatur autem *doctrinae officium* in Ioanne dupliciter, scilicet quantum ad *limpiditatem in cognoscendo* et quantum ad *auctoritatem in docendo,* quae duo perfectum reddunt doctoris officium.

Primo ergo quantum ad *limpiditatem in cognoscendo* divinum mysterium dicitur: *Sed quid existis videre? Prophetam?* cui scilicet Dominus revelet futura, secundum illud Amos tertio[^p178-7]: « Neque enim facit Dominus Deus verbum, nisi prius revelaverit secretum suum ad servos suos Prophetas ». Huic autem non solum futura praedixit, sed etiam se praesentialiter ostendit; et ideo addit: *Utique, dico vobis, et plus quam Prophetam.* De quo Ambrosius[^p178-8]: « Quia multi cupierunt eum videre, quem iste prophetavit, quem iste conspexit, quem iste baptizavit »; ideo, ostendendo Christum digito et videndo oculo, aliis Prophetis fuit excellentior et beatior, secundum illud infra decimo[^p178-9]: « Beati oculi; qui vident quae vos videtis. Dico enim vobis, quod multi Prophetae et reges voluerunt videre quae vos videtis, et non viderunt ». Et ideo, quia excellentius fuit eius officium quam Prophetarum, ideo Baptista, Ioannis primo[^p178-10], Pharisaeis quaerentibus: « Propheta es tu? » respondet: « Non sum, sed vox clamantis in deserto ». Nec est aliqua contradictio, sed consonantia, quia *propheta* praedicit futura et absentia, sed *vox* patefacit praesentia.

51. (Vers. 27.). Secundo vero quantum ad *auctoritatem in docendo* per divinum mandatum dicitur: *Hic est, de quo scriptum est,* scilicet Malachiae tertio[^p178-11], ubi loquitur Pater ad Filium: « Ecce, ego mitto angelum meum », id est nuntium authenticum, Ioannem, qui *angelus* dicitur non simplicitate naturae, sed auctoritate doctrinae, secundum illud Malachiae secundo: « Labia sacerdotis custodient scientiam, et legem requirent de ore eius, quia angelus Domini exercituum est ». Ideo autem dicitur Ioannes *nuntius* ad modum Angeli, quia, sicut Angeli « vident faciem Patris[^p178-12] » in Deitate, sic ipse vidit faciem Christi et monstravit in carne. — Propter quod addit: *Ante faciem tuam,* id est apparitionem in carne, de qua in Psalmo[^p178-13]: « Ostende nobis faciem tuam, et salvi erimus ». Hanc faciem desiderabat Isaias in persona Patrum, sexagesimo quarto: « Utinam dirumperes caelos et descenderes;
<!-- page 179 -->
a facie tua montes defluerent ». — Et quia non erant parati ad istam faciem recipiendam, nisi praemunirentur; ideo dicitur: *Qui praeparavit viam tuam*[^p179-1] *ante te,* scilicet nascendo, conversando, baptizando, praedicando. Omnibus enim his modis manuducebat ad Christum, sicut vox ad verbum. Ideo recte de ipso dictum est Isaiae quadragesimo[^p179-2], et resumtum est supra tertio et Ioannis primo: « Ego vox clamantis in deserto: Parate viam Domini »; ut impleatur illud Isaiae trigesimo quinto: « Erit vobis directa via, ut stulti non errent per eam ».

52. (Vers. 28.). *Dico enim vobis: Maior* etc. Hic tertio commendat Christus suum praecursorem quantum ad *donum gratiae divinae,* qua fuit « magnus coram Domino », secundum quod dicitur supra primo[^p179-3]. Et quoniam donum gratiae in Ioanne *praerogativam* habuit in *gradu* et *mensuram* finitatis in *statu;* ideo haec duo insinuat, cum donum gratiae in Ioanne commendat.

Primo igitur commendat Ioannem quantum ad *praerogativam doni gratuiti* respectu hominis puri, cum dicit: *Dico enim vobis: Maior inter natos mulierum propheta Ioanne Baptista nemo est.* Non est ergo inter homines in *gradù gratiae* infimo vel medio, necesse est igitur, quod sit in supremo. Unde non solum ex hoc dicitur, quod sit magnus, sed quod sit maximus, ut possit de eo dici illud Ecclesiastici quadragesimo sexto[^p179-4], quod dicitur de Iosue: « Qui fuit magnus secundum nomen suum, maximus in salutem electorum Dei ». Quia enim magnam habuit gratiam, ideo *Ioannes* vocatus est[^p179-5]. Magnus etiam fuit per *vitae meritum,* maior per *doctrinae magisterium,* maximus per *gratiae privilegium.* Non autem dicitur *maior omnibus,* ut omnibus *praeferatur,* sed quod alius non fuerit maior eo; nec dicitur de omnibus *universaliter* praeteritis et futuris, sed tunc praeteritis. Non dicitur etiam respectu *utriusque sexus,* sed tantum virilis; unde non includitur virgo Maria, quae « exaltata est super choros Angelorum[^p179-6] », nullam puram creaturam habens superiorem nec etiam aequalem, secundum illud Canticorum sexto: « Una est columba mea, perfecta mea, una est matris suae, electa genetrici suae ».

53. Secundo vero insinuat *doni illius mensuram* respectu hominis Christi, quantum ad quem dicit: *Qui autem minor est in regno caelorum maior est eo*[^p179-7]. *Regnum caelorum* hic vocat Ecclesiam, secundum quod exponit Gregorius illud Matthaei decimo tertio: « Mittet Angelos suos, et colligent de regno eius omnia scandala ». *Minor* in hoc regno dicitur humilior; hic autem est Christus, de quo in Psalmo[^p179-8]: « Minuisti eum paulo ab Angelis, gloria et honore coronasti eum ». Quia enim, sicut dicitur ad Philippenses secundo, « humiliavit semetipsum, factus est obediens usque ad mortem »; « dedit illi Dominus nomen, quod est super omne nomen ». Et sic verificatum est illud Isaiae sexagesimo[^p179-9]: « Minimus erit in mille et parvulus in gentem fortissimam ». Et haec est lex divina, quam ipse dedit Matthaei decimo octavo: « Quicumque humiliaverit se sicut parvulus iste, hic est maior in regno caelorum ». Sic utique, immo plus per omnem modum humiliavit se Christus; infra vigesimo secundo: « Quis maior est, qui recumbit, an qui ministrat? Ego autem in medio vestrum sum, sicut qui ministrat ». Unde Gregorius[^p179-10]: « Humilia te, quantumcumque potes, adhuc Christus erit humilior ». — Posset tamen exponi de Beatis, quod minor bonorum Angelorum maior esset Ioanne pro statu illo; est enim « ignis caritatis in Sion, sed caminus in Ierusalem », Isaiae trigesimo primo[^p179-11].

54. (Vers. 29.). *Et omnis populus audiens* etc. Hic quarto, ut nihil desit commendationi Ioannis, commendatur a Domino quantum ad *praeconium famae.* Et quoniam fama bonorum salutifera est electis et perniciosa reprobis, secundum illud secundae ad Corinthios secundo[^p179-12]: « Christi bonus odor sumus Deo in his qui salvi fiunt et in his qui pereunt;
<!-- page 180 -->
aliis quidem odor mortis in mortem, aliis odor vitae in vitam »; ideo commendat dupliciter praeconium famae in Ioanne: primo ostendendo ipsam *salutiferam credentibus,* secundo *mortiferam despicientibus.* Primis enim videbatur Ioannes laudabilis, secundis vero contemptibilis; primi erant humiles et secundi superbi.

Primo ergo introducit commendationem Ioannis de humilibus assentientibus, cum dicit: *Et omnis populus audiens,* Ioannem scilicet praedicantem, *iustificaverunt*[^p180-1] *Deum,* id est, iustum esse dixerunt, se ipsos ostendendo iniustos, peccata confitendo, secundum illud Psalmi: « Iustificeris in sermonibus tuis et vincas, cum iudicaris ». Peccator enim, dum confitetur peccatum, Deum *dicit iustum* et *iustificat* se ipsum, secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo tertio[^p180-2]: « Narra, si quid habes, ut iustificeris »; alia translatio: « Dic tu prior iniquitates tuas, ut iustificeris ». Et hoc modo publicani et peccatores fecerunt, audientes Ioannem, secundum illud Matthaei tertio: « Exibat ad eum omnis Ierosolyma et omnis Iudaea et omnis regio circa Iordanem, ut baptizarentur ab eo, confitentes peccata sua ». Haec enim duo requiruntur ad iustificationem peccatorum, scilicet poenitentia et baptismus. — Et ideo subdit: *Baptizati baptismo Ioannis;* supra tertio[^p180-3]: « Venerunt autem et publicani, ut baptizarentur », ab eo quaerentes, quid agerent; unde signo et verbo approbabant Ioannem tanquam Dei angelum et gloriosum habebant nomen eius. Unde cum Dominus quaereret a Pharisaeis Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p180-4]: « Baptismus Ioannis unde erat, e caelo, an ex hominibus »? dixerunt inter se: « Si dixerimus: E caelo, dicet nobis: Quare non credidistis illi? Si autem dixerimus: ex hominibus, timemus turbam; omnes enim Ioannem sicut Prophetam habebant ». Et ideo subditur: « Publicani et meretrices praecedent vos in regnum caelorum ».

55. (Vers. 30.). Secundo vero adiungit contemptum Ioannis a Pharisaeis superbientibus, cum dicit: *Pharisaei autem,* in quibus fastus sanctitatis, *et legis periti,* in quibus arrogantia notitiae veritatis, *consilium Dei spreverunt in semetipsos, non baptizati ab eo,* quia, secundum illud infra decimo[^p180-5], « qui vos spernit me spernit ». Unde qui spreverunt Ioannem, qui erat Dei nuntius, spreverunt divinum consilium. Tales fuerunt Pharisaei, ita ut posset eis Sapientia Dei improperare[^p180-6]: « Despexistis omne consilium nostrum »; et verificatum sit in eis illud Isaiae primo: « Filios enutrivi et exaltavi; ipsi autem spreverunt me ». Et ideo consuluit Apostolus primae ad Thessalonicenses ultimo: « Spiritum nolite exstinguere, prophetias nolite spernere ». — In hoc autem divinum consilium spreverunt, quia baptismum recusaverunt, per quod divina sapientia consuluit humanae saluti. Cuius figura praecessit in arca Noe in diluvio fabricata, Genesis sexto[^p180-7]. Propter quod dicitur primae Petri tertio: « Quod et vos nunc similis formae salvos facit baptisma ».

56. Et nota, quod *Pharisaei spreverunt consilium Dei in semetipsos,* propter sex rationes. Primo, quia praetulerunt statuta humana divinis; Matthaei decimo quinto[^p180-8]: « Quare vos transgredimini Legem Dei propter traditionem vestram »? — Secundo, quia praetulerunt iustitiam Legis iustitiae fidei; ad Romanos decimo: « Ignorantes Dei iustitiam et suam quaerentes statuere » etc. — Tertio, quia praetulerunt apparentiam veritati; Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p180-9]: « Vae vobis! qui mundatis quod deforis est ». — Quarto, quia praeferebant affluentiam paupertati; infra decimo sexto: « Audiebant omnia haec Pharisaei, qui erant avari, et deridebant illum » etc. — Quinto, quia vanam gloriam humilitati; Ioannis duodecimo[^p180-10]: « Dilexerunt magis gloriam hominum quam gloriam Dei ». — Sexto, quia commodum honestati; Matthaei vigesimo tertio: « Vae vobis! qui dicitis: Qui iuraverit per templum, nihil est; qui autem iuraverit in auro templi, debet. Stulti et caeci » etc.

57. (Vers. 31.). *Ait autem Dominus: Cui ergo similes dicam* etc. Postquam Salvator noster extulit praerogativam virtutis in Ioanne, hic *redarguit perfidiam incredulitatis in plebe,* cuius incredulitatem redarguit quadrupliciter. Notat enim eos de *infidelitate,* de *duritia,* de *detractione* et de *blasphemia.*

Primo igitur quantum ad notam *infidelitatis in cogitatione* dicitur: *Cui ergo similes dicam homines generationis huius, et cui similes sunt?* qui scilicet noluerunt credere Ioanni propter mentis caecitatem; *huius,* inquam, *generationis* incredulae, secundum illud Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo[^p180-11]: « Generatio enim perversa est et infideles filii »; et Marci nono: « O generatio incredula! quamdiu ero apud vos? quamdiu vos patiar »? Psalmus: « Ne fiant, sicut patres eorum, generatio prava et exasperans, generatio, quae non direxit cor suum, et non est cum Deo spiritus eius ». Et ideo Ieremiae secundo: « Considerate vehementer, si mutavit gens deos suos; populus vero meus mutavit gloriam suam in idolum »
<!-- page 181 -->

58. (Vers. 32.). Secundo, quantum ad notam *duritiae in affectione* subdit: *Similes sunt pueris,* ubi adiungit similitudinem, per quam ostenditur duritia eorum, quia nec moti sunt ad *cantum* promissionum nec ad *lamentum* comminationum, sicut pueri stulti in foro non respondent aliis, nec cantantibus nec lamentantibus; quos introducit per modum similitudinis. Propter quod dicit: *Similes sunt pueris sedentibus in foro.* Similiter competit eis dici et improperari quod improperant pueri sedentes in foro suis coaetaneis. Propter quod addit: *Loquentibus*[^p181-1] *ad invicem et dicentibus: Cantavimus vobis tibiis, et non saltastis;* quia sic possunt eis praedicatores dicere: *Cantavimus vobis tibiis,* scilicet promissa Dei concrepando; *et non saltastis,* proficiendo. Et recta est comparatio, quia praedicator tibicini comparatur, secundum illud Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo[^p181-2]: « Clama, ne cesses, quasi tuba exalta vocem tuam »; et Ieremiae quarto: « Canite tuba in Sion ». Nec immerito, quia, Ecclesiastici quadragesimo[^p181-3], « tibiae et psalterium suavem faciunt melodiam, et super utraque lingua suavis ». Ad haec duri corde non moventur nec saltant per profectum bonorum operum; de quo saltu Malachiae quarto: « Egrediemini, et salietis sicut vitulus de armento et calcabitis impios ». Et de hac Ambrosius[^p181-4]: « Honesta saltatio, quia tripudiat animus bonis operibus elevatus »; unde qui ad cantum promissionum moti non fuerunt duri sunt corde, sed opere duriores, quia nec ad comminationes suppliciorum. — Propter quod addit: *Lamentavimus* vobis, scilicet per comminationem poenae; Ezechielis secundo[^p181-5]: « Scriptae erant in libro lamentationes et carmen et vae »; et Ieremiae nono: « Contemplamini et vocate lamentatrices, et assumant super nos lamentum »; Ioelis secundo: « Convertimini ad me in ieiunio et fletu et planctu, et scindite corda vestra et non vestimenta vestra ». Et tamen ad hoc moti non sunt; unde addit: *Et non plorastis,* per compunctionem poenitentiae; Isaiae vigesimo secundo[^p181-6]: « Vocabit Dominus ad fletum et planctum »; et post: « Et ecce, gaudium et laetitia, iugulare arietes » etc.; cum tamen dicat Apostolus ad Romanos duodecimo: « Gaudere cum gaudentibus, et flere cum flentibus ».

59. (Vers. 33.). Tertio, quantum ad notam *detractionis in locutione,* qua bonum ponunt in malum, dicitur: *Venit Ioannes Baptista non manducans*[^p181-7] *panem neque bibens vinum,* et ita in via *severitatis* et *iustitiae,* sicut dicitur Matthaei vigesimo primo: « Venit ad vos Ioannes in via iustitiae ». Unde quia venit cum austeritate poenitentiae, ideo venit sicut lamentans et gemens propter salutem populi, secundum illud Danielis decimo: « Ego Daniel lugebam trium hebdomadarum diebus, panem desiderabilem non comedi, et caro et vinum non introierunt in os meum ». Sed tamen per eum non est emollita eorum duritia ad plorandum, sed aucta malitia ad detrahendum. — Unde subditur: *Et dicitis: Daemonium habet,* quia dissimilis est vitae alienae. Sic dicebant Salvatori Ioannis decimo[^p181-8]: « Daemonium habet; quid eum auditis »? Unde opera virtutum, quae sunt a Spiritu sancto, ipsi ex nequitia cordis attribuebant daemonio, sicut Matthaei nono dicebant: « In principe daemoniorum eiicit daemonia ». Et sic verificatum est in eis illud Ecclesiastici undecimo[^p181-9]: « Bona in mala convertens insidiatur, et in electis imponet maculam ». Et propterea vae eis, secundum illud Isaiae quinto: « Vae! qui dicitis bonum malum et malum bonum ».

60. (Vers. 34.). Quarto, quantum ad notam *blasphemiae in obduratione* contra ipsum Christum subditur: *Venit Filius hominis manducans et bibens,* et ita in via *clementiae* quasi medicus et tanquam sponsus, sicut supra quinto[^p181-10]: « Non poteritis filios sponsi facere ieiunare, quamdiu cum illis est sponsus ». — Et tamen non sunt moti ad exsultandum, sed depravati ad blasphemandum; et ideo subditur: *Et dicitis: Ecce, homo vorator*[^p181-11] *et bibens vinum;* hoc dicunt quantum ad *superfluitatem victus,* quasi dicant: non potest esse sapiens, secundum illud Proverbiorum vigesimo: « Luxuriosa res vinum et tumultuosa ebrietas ». *Amicus publicanorum et peccatorum,* quantum ad *inhonestatem convictus,* quasi dicant: non potest esse bonus, quia, sicut dicitur Ecclesiastici decimo tertio[^p181-12], « qui tetigerit picem inquinabitur ab ea, et qui communicaverit superbo induet superbiam ». Sed male, quia Ieremiae decimo quinto: « Ipsi convertentur ad te, et non converteris ad eos »; et Matthaei nono: « Euntes discite, quid est: Misericordiam volo et non sacrificium. Non enim veni vocare iustos, sed peccatores », ad poenitentiam.

61. (Vers. 35.). Et quia blasphemia Iudaeorum aggravatur ex vera praedicatione Apostolorum, ideo
<!-- page 182 -->
subditur: *Et iustificata est Sapientia ab omnibus filiis suis. Sapientiam* namque Evangelista vocat Christum, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios primo[^p182-1]: « Qui factus est nobis a Deo sapientia et iustitia ». Filii istius Sapientiae sunt Apostoli per verbum istius Sapientiae geniti et per Spiritum sanctum vivificati, secundum illud Ecclesiastici quarto[^p182-2]: « Sapientia filiis suis vitam inspirat », id est Christus Apostolis. Unde et Petrus dicebat Ioannis sexto: « Domine, ad quem ibimus? Verba vitae aeternae habes ». Isti *iustificaverunt,* id est, Christum iustum reputaverunt, dum eius verba per iustitiam et obedientiam impleverunt, secundum illud Ecclesiastici tertio[^p182-3]: « Filii sapientiae, ecclesia iustorum, et natio illorum, obedientia et dilectio ». Ad istorum exemplum Pharisaei debuerunt moveri, sed propter pertinaciam suam remanserunt in faecibus suis. Unde ipsis dicebat Matthaei duodecimo: « Si ego in Beelzebub eiicio daemones, filii vestri in quo eiiciunt? Ideoque ipsi iudices vestri erunt », quia ipsi iusti reputantur a Sapientia, quam iustificant, et Pharisaei iniusti et reprobi ab eadem Sapientia, quam condemnant propter pertinaciam et blasphemiam.

## English
<!-- page 177 -->

47. *And when they were departed* etc. Above[^p177-8], he proved the testimony of John through the evidence of truth in itself; here, secondly, he proves it through the *evidence of virtue in John, the one testifying.* And this part has two parts, in the first of which *the prerogative of virtue in John is extolled* in many ways; but in the second *the perfidy of unbelief in the people is reproved,* there[^p177-9]: *Whereunto then shall I liken the men?*

Now the *prerogative of virtue* is shown to have been in John in four ways: first, namely, as to the *merit of life;* but secondly, as to the *office of doctrine,* there[^p177-10]: *But what went you out to see? A prophet* etc.; thirdly, as to the *gift of grace,* there: *For I say to you: Amongst those that are born of women* etc.; fourthly, as to the *proclamation of fame,* there: *And all the people hearing* etc.

As to the first it is to be noted that the merit of life is commended in John in two ways: first, as to *constancy* with respect to difficult things; but secondly, as to *abstinence* with respect to delightful things.

48. (Vers. 24.). First, then, is brought in the commendation of John's *constancy* in the absence of the disciples; on account of which he says: *And when the messengers of John were departed*[^p177-11], the solution of the question having been obtained, returning like good messengers to him by whom they had been sent; of whom Job, the thirty-eighth chapter: « Canst thou send lightnings, and will they go, and will they return and say to thee: Here we are »? — In their absence John ought to be praised, that the praise might be shown to be true, not flattering; therefore it is said: *He began to speak to the multitudes concerning John; he began,* to be sure, to praise him to the multitudes, lest John should seem to have sent his disciples to Christ out of doubt. — Therefore he begins to commend him for constancy: *What went ye out into the desert to see? A reed shaken with the wind?* as if he were to say: John is not like a reed, that he should tremble and be shaken at any impulse whatever, but strong and fixed in faith and in all goodness; according to that of James, the first chapter[^p177-12]: « Let him ask in faith, nothing wavering; for he that wavereth is like a wave of the sea, which is moved and carried about by the wind ». « A double minded man is inconstant in all his ways ». Such a one truly is a *reed;* not such was John, since he was most holy, since it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p177-13]: « A holy man continueth in wisdom as the sun; but a fool is changed as the moon ». Such are all the Saints, according to that to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter: « Let us not be children tossed to and fro, and let us not be carried about with every wind of doctrine in the wickedness of men ». Therefore Paul asked in the second to the Thessalonians, the second chapter: « We beseech you, brethren, by the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, that you be not easily moved from your sense ». But he who wishes to obtain this constancy ought to leave the world and go out to John into the desert, according to what is said in the first of Machabees, the second chapter[^p177-14]: « Mathathias cried out with a loud voice: Every one that hath zeal for the Law, let him go out after me »; and it follows that « many went out into the desert ».
<!-- page 178 -->

49. (Vers. 25.). But secondly is subjoined the commendation of John's *abstinence* with respect to delightful things; on account of which he says: *But what went you out to see?* that is, to consider: *A man clothed in soft garments*[^p178-1]? as if he were to say: no; for John, most holy, was a stranger to all softness of carnality, as is said in Matthew, the third chapter: « And John had a garment of camels' hair, and his meat was locusts ». He truly was of the number of the evangelical men, in whose person it is said in the first to Timothy, the sixth chapter[^p178-2]: « Having food, and wherewith to be covered, with these let us be content »; *wherewith to be covered,* he says, not: wherewith to be adorned. Bernard to Eugene: « The Apostles were strong in battle, not soft in silk; but if thou art a son of the Apostles and of the Prophets, do thou also likewise ». But he who wishes to do this must forsake the world and go out with John into the desert. — On account of which he adds: *Behold, they that are in costly apparel and in*[^p178-3] *delights are in the houses of kings;* as if he were to say: costly apparel and a life of delights belong to worldly men, not to spiritual and Christian men. Whence in the first of Peter, the third chapter[^p178-4], it is said of women: « Whose adorning let it not be the outward plaiting of the hair, or the wearing of gold and silver, or the adorning of garments ». On which Gregory: « Let no one think that there is no sin in the adorning of precious garments. Consider what fault it is for men too to seek this, from which the shepherd of the Church took care to forbid even women ». For garments of this kind, precious ones, are carnal and delightful, and in delights the spiritual life is imperilled; whence in the first to Timothy, the fifth chapter[^p178-5]: « The widow that is in delights, living, is dead »; Jeremias, the thirty-first chapter: « How long wilt thou be dissolved in delights, O wandering daughter »? But garments of this kind, precious and delightful, have been wont to be an occasion of vainglory; and therefore he adds: *Are in the houses of kings;* of which Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter[^p178-6]: « Glory not in apparel at any time, and be not exalted in the day of thy honour ».

50. (Vers. 26.). *But what went you out to see?* After the Saviour has commended John as to the merit of life, here he commends him as to the *office of doctrine,* and in right order, because a good life ought to come before authentic doctrine. Now the *office of doctrine* is commended in John in two ways, namely as to *limpidity in knowing* and as to *authority in teaching,* which two render the office of a doctor perfect.

First, then, as to *limpidity in knowing* the divine mystery it is said: *But what went you out to see? A prophet?* namely, one to whom the Lord should reveal things to come, according to that of Amos, the third chapter[^p178-7]: « For the Lord God doth nothing without first revealing his secret to his servants the Prophets ». But to this one he not only foretold things to come, but also showed himself present; and therefore he adds: *Yea, I say to you, and more than a prophet.* Of whom Ambrose[^p178-8]: « Because many desired to see him whom this one prophesied, whom this one beheld, whom this one baptized »; therefore, by pointing out Christ with the finger and seeing him with the eye, he was more excellent and more blessed than the other Prophets, according to that below, the tenth chapter[^p178-9]: « Blessed are the eyes; that see the things which you see. For I say to you, that many prophets and kings have desired to see the things that you see, and have not seen them ». And therefore, because his office was more excellent than that of the Prophets, therefore the Baptist, John, the first chapter[^p178-10], to the Pharisees asking: « Art thou the prophet? » answers: « I am not, but the voice of one crying in the desert ». Nor is there any contradiction, but consonance, because a *prophet* foretells things to come and absent, but a *voice* makes manifest things present.

51. (Vers. 27.). But secondly, as to *authority in teaching* by divine mandate, it is said: *This is he of whom it is written,* namely in Malachias, the third chapter[^p178-11], where the Father speaks to the Son: « Behold, I send my angel », that is, an authentic messenger, John, who is called an *angel* not by simplicity of nature, but by authority of doctrine, according to that of Malachias, the second chapter: « The lips of the priest shall keep knowledge, and they shall seek the law at his mouth, because he is the angel of the Lord of hosts ». Now John is called a *messenger* after the manner of an Angel for this reason, that, as the Angels « see the face of the Father[^p178-12] » in the Godhead, so he saw the face of Christ and showed it in the flesh. — On account of which he adds: *Before thy face,* that is, the appearing in the flesh, of which in the Psalm[^p178-13]: « Shew us thy face, and we shall be saved ». This face Isaias desired in the person of the Fathers, the sixty-fourth chapter: « O that thou wouldst rend the heavens and wouldst come down;
<!-- page 179 -->
the mountains would melt away at thy face ». — And because they were not ready to receive this face unless they were forearmed; therefore it is said: *Who hath prepared thy way*[^p179-1] *before thee,* namely by being born, by his manner of life, by baptizing, by preaching. For by all these modes he led by the hand to Christ, as the voice to the word. Therefore rightly was it said of him in Isaias, the fortieth chapter[^p179-2], and taken up again above, the third chapter, and in John, the first chapter: « I am the voice of one crying in the desert: Prepare the way of the Lord »; that there might be fulfilled that of Isaias, the thirty-fifth chapter: « It shall be unto you a straight way, so that fools shall not err therein ».

52. (Vers. 28.). *For I say to you: Greater* etc. Here, thirdly, Christ commends his precursor as to the *gift of divine grace,* by which he was « great before the Lord », according to what is said above, the first chapter[^p179-3]. And since the gift of grace in John had a *prerogative* in *degree* and a *measure* of finiteness in *state;* therefore he intimates these two, when he commends the gift of grace in John.

First, then, he commends John as to the *prerogative of the gratuitous gift* with respect to mere man, when he says: *For I say to you: Amongst those that are born of women there is not a greater prophet than John the Baptist.* He is not, therefore, among men in the lowest or the middle *degree of grace;* it is necessary, therefore, that he be in the highest. Whence from this it is said not only that he is great, but that he is the greatest, so that there could be said of him that of Ecclesiasticus, the forty-sixth chapter[^p179-4], which is said of Josue: « Who was great according to his name, greatest for the salvation of the elect of God ». For because he had great grace, therefore he was called *John*[^p179-5]. He was great, too, by the *merit of life,* greater by the *teaching office of doctrine,* greatest by the *privilege of grace.* But he is not called *greater than all,* so as to be *preferred* to all, but in that no other was greater than he; nor is it said of all *universally,* past and to come, but of those past at that time. Nor is it said, either, with respect to *both sexes,* but only the male; whence the Virgin Mary is not included, who « was exalted above the choirs of Angels[^p179-6] », having no mere creature superior to her, nor even equal, according to that of the Canticle, the sixth chapter: « One is my dove, my perfect one, she is the only one of her mother, the chosen of her that bore her ».

53. But secondly he intimates the *measure of that gift* with respect to the man Christ, as to whom he says: *But he that is the lesser in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he*[^p179-7]. *The kingdom of heaven* he here calls the Church, according as Gregory expounds that of Matthew, the thirteenth chapter: « He shall send his Angels, and they shall gather out of his kingdom all scandals ». *The lesser* in this kingdom is called the humbler; and this is Christ, of whom in the Psalm[^p179-8]: « Thou hast made him a little less than the Angels, thou hast crowned him with glory and honour ». For because, as is said to the Philippians, the second chapter, « he humbled himself, becoming obedient unto death »; « the Lord gave him a name which is above every name ». And thus was verified that of Isaias, the sixtieth chapter[^p179-9]: « The least shall become a thousand, and a little one a most strong nation ». And this is the divine law which he himself gave in Matthew, the eighteenth chapter: « Whosoever shall humble himself as this little child, he is the greater in the kingdom of heaven ». So, to be sure, nay more, in every way Christ humbled himself; below, the twenty-second chapter: « Which is greater, he that sitteth at table, or he that serveth? But I am in the midst of you, as he that serveth ». Whence Gregory[^p179-10]: « Humble thyself as much as thou canst, Christ will still be humbler ». — It could, however, be expounded of the Blessed: that the lesser of the good Angels would be greater than John for that state; for there is « the fire of charity in Sion, but the furnace in Jerusalem », Isaias, the thirty-first chapter[^p179-11].

54. (Vers. 29.). *And all the people hearing* etc. Here, fourthly, that nothing might be lacking to the commendation of John, he is commended by the Lord as to the *proclamation of fame.* And since the fame of the good is health-bearing to the elect and pernicious to the reprobate, according to that of the second to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p179-12]: « We are the good odour of Christ unto God, in them that are saved, and in them that perish;
<!-- page 180 -->
to the one indeed the odour of death unto death, to the others the odour of life unto life »; therefore he commends the proclamation of fame in John in two ways: first, by showing it *health-bearing to those who believe,* secondly, *death-bearing to those who despise.* For to the first John seemed praiseworthy, but to the second contemptible; the first were humble and the second proud.

First, then, he brings in the commendation of John by the humble who assent, when he says: *And all the people hearing,* namely John preaching, *justified*[^p180-1] *God,* that is, they said that he is just, showing themselves unjust, by confessing their sins, according to that of the Psalm: « That thou mayest be justified in thy words, and mayest overcome when thou art judged ». For the sinner, while he confesses his sin, *says that God is just* and *justifies* himself, according to that of Isaias, the forty-third chapter[^p180-2]: « Tell, if thou hast anything, that thou mayest be justified »; another translation: « Tell thou first thy iniquities, that thou mayest be justified ». And in this way the publicans and sinners did, hearing John, according to that of Matthew, the third chapter: « There went out to him all Jerusalem and all Judea and all the country about the Jordan, that they might be baptized by him, confessing their sins ». For these two are required for the justification of sinners, namely penance and baptism. — And therefore he subjoins: *Baptized with the baptism of John;* above, the third chapter[^p180-3]: « And the publicans also came to be baptized », asking of him what they should do; whence by sign and word they approved John as an angel of God and held his name glorious. Whence when the Lord asked the Pharisees, in Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p180-4]: « The baptism of John, whence was it, from heaven, or from men »? they said among themselves: « If we shall say: From heaven, he will say to us: Why did you not believe him? But if we shall say: From men, we fear the multitude; for all held John as a Prophet ». And therefore it is subjoined: « The publicans and the harlots shall go before you into the kingdom of heaven ».

55. (Vers. 30.). But secondly he adds the contempt of John by the Pharisees in their pride, when he says: *But the Pharisees,* in whom was the haughtiness of sanctity, *and the lawyers,* in whom was the arrogance of the knowledge of truth, *despised the counsel of God against themselves, being not baptized by him,* because, according to that below, the tenth chapter[^p180-5], « he that despiseth you despiseth me ». Whence those who despised John, who was God's messenger, despised the divine counsel. Such were the Pharisees, so that the Wisdom of God could upbraid them[^p180-6]: « You have despised all our counsel »; and that of Isaias, the first chapter, might be verified in them: « I have brought up children, and exalted them; but they have despised me ». And therefore the Apostle counselled in the first to the Thessalonians, the last chapter: « Extinguish not the spirit, despise not prophecies ». — Now in this they despised the divine counsel, that they refused baptism, by which the divine wisdom took counsel for human salvation. Its figure went before in the ark of Noe built at the flood, Genesis, the sixth chapter[^p180-7]. On account of which it is said in the first of Peter, the third chapter: « Whereunto baptism being of the like form now saveth you also ».

56. And note that *the Pharisees despised the counsel of God against themselves* for six reasons. First, because they preferred human statutes to divine ones; Matthew, the fifteenth chapter[^p180-8]: « Why do you transgress the Law of God for your tradition »? — Secondly, because they preferred the justice of the Law to the justice of faith; to the Romans, the tenth chapter: « Not knowing the justice of God, and seeking to establish their own » etc. — Thirdly, because they preferred appearance to truth; Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p180-9]: « Woe to you! who make clean that which is without ». — Fourthly, because they preferred affluence to poverty; below, the sixteenth chapter: « Now the Pharisees, who were covetous, heard all these things, and they derided him » etc. — Fifthly, because they preferred vainglory to humility; John, the twelfth chapter[^p180-10]: « They loved the glory of men more than the glory of God ». — Sixthly, because they preferred advantage to uprightness; Matthew, the twenty-third chapter: « Woe to you! who say: Whosoever shall swear by the temple, it is nothing; but he that shall swear by the gold of the temple is a debtor. Ye foolish and blind » etc.

57. (Vers. 31.). *And the Lord said: Whereunto then shall I liken* etc. After our Saviour has extolled the prerogative of virtue in John, here *he reproves the perfidy of unbelief in the people,* whose unbelief he reproves in four ways. For he marks them for *infidelity,* for *hardness,* for *detraction* and for *blasphemy.*

First, then, as to the mark of *infidelity in thought* it is said: *Whereunto then shall I liken the men of this generation, and to what are they like?* those, namely, who would not believe John on account of the blindness of their mind; *of this,* I say, unbelieving *generation,* according to that of Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter[^p180-11]: « For it is a perverse generation, and unfaithful children »; and Mark, the ninth chapter: « O unbelieving generation! how long shall I be with you? how long shall I suffer you »? The Psalm: « That they might not become like their fathers, a perverse and exasperating generation, a generation that set not their heart aright, and its spirit is not with God ». And therefore Jeremias, the second chapter: « Consider diligently, whether a nation hath changed their gods; but my people hath changed their glory into an idol »
<!-- page 181 -->

58. (Vers. 32.). Secondly, as to the mark of *hardness in affection* he subjoins: *They are like to children,* where he adds a similitude by which their hardness is shown, because they were moved neither by the *song* of the promises nor by the *lament* of the threatenings, as foolish children in the marketplace do not answer the others, neither those who sing nor those who lament; whom he brings in by way of a similitude. On account of which he says: *They are like to children sitting in the marketplace.* Likewise it fits them to be told and upbraided with what the children sitting in the marketplace upbraid their fellows with. On account of which he adds: *Speaking*[^p181-1] *one to another and saying: We have piped to you, and you have not danced;* because thus can preachers say to them: *We have piped to you,* namely by sounding forth the promises of God; *and you have not danced,* by making progress. And the comparison is right, because the preacher is compared to a piper, according to that of Isaias, the fifty-eighth chapter[^p181-2]: « Cry, cease not, lift up thy voice like a trumpet »; and Jeremias, the fourth chapter: « Blow the trumpet in Sion ». And not undeservedly, because, Ecclesiasticus, the fortieth chapter[^p181-3], « the pipes and the psaltery make a sweet melody, but a pleasant tongue is above them both ». At these the hard of heart are not moved, nor do they dance by progress in good works; of which leaping Malachias, the fourth chapter: « You shall go forth, and shall leap like calves of the herd, and you shall tread down the wicked ». And of this Ambrose[^p181-4]: « Honest is the dancing, because the mind leaps for joy, raised up by good works »; whence those who were not moved at the song of the promises are hard of heart, but harder in deed, because they were not moved even at the threatenings of penalties. — On account of which he adds: *We have lamented* to you, namely through the threatening of punishment; Ezechiel, the second chapter[^p181-5]: « There were written in the book lamentations, and a canticle, and woe »; and Jeremias, the ninth chapter: « Consider ye, and call for the lamenting women, and let them take up a lamentation over us »; Joel, the second chapter: « Be converted to me in fasting, and in weeping, and in mourning, and rend your hearts and not your garments ». And yet they were not moved by this; whence he adds: *And you have not wept,* through the compunction of penance; Isaias, the twenty-second chapter[^p181-6]: « The Lord shall call to weeping and to mourning »; and after: « And behold, joy and gladness, to slay rams » etc.; though the Apostle says to the Romans, the twelfth chapter: « Rejoice with them that rejoice, and weep with them that weep ».

59. (Vers. 33.). Thirdly, as to the mark of *detraction in speech,* by which they put good for evil, it is said: *John the Baptist came not eating*[^p181-7] *bread nor drinking wine,* and so in the way of *severity* and *justice,* as is said in Matthew, the twenty-first chapter: « John came to you in the way of justice ». Whence, because he came with the austerity of penance, therefore he came as one lamenting and groaning for the salvation of the people, according to that of Daniel, the tenth chapter: « I Daniel mourned for the days of three weeks, I ate no desirable bread, and neither flesh nor wine entered into my mouth ». But yet through him their hardness was not softened to weeping, but their malice was increased to detracting. — Whence it is subjoined: *And you say: He hath a devil,* because he is unlike the life of others. So they said of the Saviour, John, the tenth chapter[^p181-8]: « He hath a devil; why hear you him »? Whence the works of power, which are from the Holy Spirit, they from the wickedness of their heart attributed to the devil, as they said in Matthew, the ninth chapter: « By the prince of devils he casteth out devils ». And thus was verified in them that of Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter[^p181-9]: « Turning good into evil he lieth in wait, and on the elect he will lay a blot ». And therefore woe to them, according to that of Isaias, the fifth chapter: « Woe to you that call good evil, and evil good ».

60. (Vers. 34.). Fourthly, as to the mark of *blasphemy in obduracy* against Christ himself, it is subjoined: *The Son of man is come eating and drinking,* and so in the way of *clemency,* as a physician and as a bridegroom, as above, the fifth chapter[^p181-10]: « You cannot make the children of the bridegroom fast, whilst the bridegroom is with them ». — And yet they were not moved to exult, but depraved to blaspheme; and therefore it is subjoined: *And you say: Behold, a man that is a glutton*[^p181-11] *and drinking wine;* this they say as to *superfluity of living,* as if they said: he cannot be wise, according to that of Proverbs, the twentieth chapter: « Wine is a luxurious thing, and drunkenness riotous ». *A friend of publicans and sinners,* as to *unseemliness of company,* as if they said: he cannot be good, because, as is said in Ecclesiasticus, the thirteenth chapter[^p181-12], « he that toucheth pitch shall be defiled with it, and he that hath fellowship with the proud shall put on pride ». But wrongly, because Jeremias, the fifteenth chapter: « They shall be turned to thee, and thou shalt not be turned to them »; and Matthew, the ninth chapter: « Going, learn what this is: I will have mercy and not sacrifice. For I am not come to call the just, but sinners », to penance.

61. (Vers. 35.). And because the blasphemy of the Jews is aggravated by the true preaching of the Apostles, therefore
<!-- page 182 -->
it is subjoined: *And Wisdom is justified by all her children. Wisdom,* indeed, the Evangelist calls Christ, according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p182-1]: « Who of God is made unto us wisdom and justice ». The children of this Wisdom are the Apostles, begotten through the word of this Wisdom and quickened through the Holy Spirit, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the fourth chapter[^p182-2]: « Wisdom inspireth life into her children », that is, Christ into the Apostles. Whence Peter too said, John, the sixth chapter: « Lord, to whom shall we go? Thou hast the words of eternal life ». These *justified,* that is, they reputed Christ just, while they fulfilled his words through justice and obedience, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter[^p182-3]: « The children of wisdom are the church of the just, and their generation, obedience and love ». By their example the Pharisees ought to have been moved, but on account of their pertinacity they remained in their dregs. Whence he said to them, Matthew, the twelfth chapter: « If I by Beelzebub cast out devils, your children, by whom do they cast them out? Therefore they shall be your judges », because they are reputed just by Wisdom, whom they justify, and the Pharisees unjust and reprobate by the same Wisdom, whom they condemn on account of their pertinacity and blasphemy.
