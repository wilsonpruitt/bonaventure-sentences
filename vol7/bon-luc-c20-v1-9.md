# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XX, Vers. 1-9
## *Confutatio Pharisaeorum errantium ex malitia per tria*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 501 -->

### Capitulum XX.

#### Secundo confutat Iudaeorum incredulitatem per verba et documenta et hoc in tribus partibus.

1. *Et factum est in una dierum* etc. Postquam confutavit incredulitatem Iudaeorum per facta, hic *confutat per verba et documenta*[^p501-1]. Dividitur autem haec pars in tres. In quarum prima documentis confutat *Pharisaeos errantes ex malitia.* In secunda confutat *Sadducaeos errantes ex ignorantia,* ibi[^p501-2]: *Accesserunt autem quidam Sadducaeorum* etc. In tertia informat *discipulos ad sapientiam,* ibi: *Audiente autem omni populo* etc.

#### Confutatio Pharisaeorum errantium ex malitia per tria.

Prima pars, in qua *Pharisaeos confutat,* tres habet partes. In quarum prima *repellit obiectam calumniam.* In secunda *arguit occultam perfidiam,* ibi[^p501-3]: *Coepit autem dicere ad plebem* etc. In tertia vero *convincit versutiam adinventam,* ibi: *Et quaerebant principes sacerdotum* etc.

*Confutationem* igitur *calumniae obiectae* describit Evangelista hoc ordine. Primo introducitur *Pharisaeorum protervitas in impetendo*[^p501-4]; secundo, *Christi sagacitas in resistendo;* tertio, *Pharisaeorum dolositas in fugiendo;* quarto, *Christi veracitas adversarios refellendo.*

2. (Vers. 1.). Primo igitur quantum ad *protervitatem Pharisaeorum in impetendo* praemittit: *Et factum est in una dierum, docente illo populum in templo et evangelizante,* secundum evangelicam veritatem, quae omnibus declaranda est, secundum quod ipse discipulis suis dixit Matthaei decimo[^p501-5]: « Quod in aure auditis praedicate super tecta »; et Marci ultimo: « Praedicate Evangelium omni creaturae ». Sic et ipse faciebat, secundum illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto: « Doctrinam quasi antelucanum illumino omnibus et enarrabo illam usque in longinquum »; et post: « Videte, quoniam non solum mihi laboravi, sed omnibus exquirentibus sapientiam ». — Haec autem doctrina veritatis odiosa est malis, quia « qui male agit odit lucem », Ioannis tertio[^p501-6]; ideo subditur: *Convenerunt principes sacerdotum et scribae cum senioribus,* per fastuosam protervitatem. In principibus namque erat fastus propter excellentiam *dignitatis;* in scribis, propter excellentiam *cognitionis;* in senioribus, propter *canitiem senectutis.* Ideo non conveniebant sicut humiles ad addiscendum, sed sicut superbi ad calumniandum, secundum illud Psalmi[^p501-7]: « Principes convenerunt in unum adversus Dominum et adversus Christum eius ». Unde in his impletur illud Iob vigesimo quarto: « Ipsi rebelles fuerunt lumini et ignoraverunt vias eius ». Rebellio autem ortum habuit ex invidia, quae est filia superbiae. Haec est « fera pessima, quae devoravit Ioseph[^p501-8] »; et de hac Chrysostomus: « Dicebant sacerdotes apud se: Nos sumus columnae templi; et ecce, super illum tota reclinatur Ecclesia. Nos fuimus tacentium Scripturarum quasi lingua visibilis; et ecce, ille resonat in medio templi, et nos contempti quasi cithara dissipata tacemus. Nos fuimus patres, ille generat filios, et nos steriles sumus ». Quod dicit quantum ad praedicta tria genera personarum, quae per invidiam excaecata sunt, et propter hoc manifesta dederunt[^p501-9] in dubium et calumniantur.

3. (Vers. 2.). Propter quod adiungitur: *Et aiunt dicentes ad illum: Dic nobis: In qua potestate haec facis? Aut quis est, qui dedit tibi hanc potestatem?* Hoc autem requirunt ut dubium, cum tamen per evidentissima miracula sit manifestum, secundum illud Ioannis quinto[^p501-10]: « Opera, quae ego facio, testimonium perhibent de me »; sed ipsi quaerendo totam plebem in dubitationem ducere volebant, quasi illa miracula non essent a Deo, sed potius a diabolo. Unde Glossa[^p501-11]: « De Dei potestate
<!-- page 502 -->
dubitant et subintelligi volunt, diaboli esse quod facit ». Haec autem dubitatio veniebat illis ex corruptione conscientiae suae; unde Chrysostomus[^p502-1]: « Omnis homo secundum se existimat alterum, et fornicarius neminem putat castum, castus non facile de fornicario suspicatur, superbus neminem putat humilem; sic qui non est ex Deo sacerdos nullius sacerdotium putat esse ex Deo ». Unde et caecus illuminatus arguebat Pharisaeorum caecitatem, qui dicebant, se nescire, unde Christus esset, secundum illud Ioannis nono[^p502-2]: « In hoc mirabile est, quod vos nescitis, unde sit ». « Scimus autem, quod peccatores Deus non audit, sed si quis Dei voluntatem facit » etc. Ideo dicitur Ecclesiastici decimo: « In manu Dei potestas hominis »; et ad Romanos decimo tertio: « Non est potestas nisi a Deo » etc.

4. (Vers. 3.). Secundo quantum ad *sagacitatem Christi in resistendo* subditur: *Respondens autem Iesus dixit ad illos: Interrogabo vos et ego unum verbum.* Cum deberet respondere, interrogabat, non quia nesciret, sed quia melius ligantur per interrogationem factam quam per responsionem absolutam. Unde Beda[^p502-3]: « Poterat aperta responsione tentatorum calumniam confutare, sed prudenter interrogat, ut vel suo silentio, vel sua sententia condemnentur », sicut supra decimo nono: « Ex ore tuo te iudico, serve nequam ». Cautela enim est, quod « omnino adversanti omnino adversandum est[^p502-4] », et contra tergiversantes oportet ex datis procedere, ut vincantur.

5. (Vers. 4.). Ideo caute quaerit et interrogat, ut, quidquid respondeant, convincantur, cum subdit: *Respondete mihi: Baptismus Ioannis unde erat*[^p502-5]*? De caelo, an ex hominibus?* Ad hoc non poterant respondere nisi tripliciter: vel simpliciter *tacendo,* et sic erant confusi; vel dicendo, quod erat *de hominibus,* et sic ab hominibus essent lapidati; vel dicendo, quod *de Deo,* et sic Christus haberet propositum, quia Ioannes perhibuit testimonium de Christo in suo baptismo, secundum illud Ioannis quinto[^p502-6]: « Vos misistis ad Ioannem, et ipse testimonium perhibuit veritati ». Nam dicitur Ioannis primo: « Testimonium perhibuit Ioannes dicens: Quia vidi Spiritum sanctum sicut columbam descendentem de caelo et manentem super eum »; « et ego vidi et testimonium perhibui, quia hic est Filius Dei ». Unde Glossa[^p502-7]: « Quem confitemini de caelo habuisse prophetiam, mihi perhibuit testimonium, et ab illo audistis, in qua potestate haec faciam ». Quoniam ergo, ut dicitur Ioannis primo[^p502-8], « non erat ille lux, sed ut testimonium perhiberet de lumine », dubitatores de *lumine* remittuntur ad considerationem *lucernae;* nam, sicut dicitur Ioannis quinto, « ille erat lucerna ardens et lucens »; et dubitatores de *Verbo* remittuntur ad *vocem;* Ioannis primo: « Ego vox clamantis in deserto ».

6. (Vers. 5.). Tertio quantum ad *Pharisaeorum dolositatem in subterfugiendo* subiungitur: *At illi cogitabant intra se dicentes, quia, si dixerimus: De caelo,* confitendo veritatem; *dicet* nobis: *Quare ergo non credidistis ei*[^p502-9]*?* arguendo scilicet nostram *incredulitatem,* qua scilicet viro caelesti credere recusabant, secundum illud Matthaei undecimo: « Venit Ioannes non manducans neque bibens, et dicunt: Daemonium habet ».

7. (Vers. 6.). *Si autem dixerimus: Ex hominibus, plebs universa lapidabit nos,* abhorrendo *mendacitatem.* Nam blasphemi lapidandi erant secundum Legem; Levitici vigesimo quarto[^p502-10]: « Educ blasphemum extra castra, et ponant omnes, qui audierunt, manus suas super caput eius, et lapidet eum omnis populus ». — *Certi sunt enim, Ioannem Prophetam esse,* propter evidentissimam sanctitatem, ob quam de Ioanne dicitur Matthaei undecimo[^p502-11]: « Quid existis in desertum videre? Prophetam? Etiam, dico vobis, et plus quam Prophetam ». Unde supra tertio: « Existimante omni populo de Ioanne, ne forte ipse esset Christus; dixit Ioannes: Ego baptizo vos aqua, veniet fortior me post me, cuius non sum dignus solvere corrigiam calceamentorum eius ».
<!-- page 503 -->

8. (Vers. 7.). Et quia odium veritatis ex una parte urgebat et ex alia timor lapidationis; ideo praetendunt ignorantiam responsionis, cum subditur: *Et responderunt, se nescire, unde esset;* Glossa[^p503-1]: « Timentes lapidationem, sed magis timentes veritatis confessionem ». Huius autem confessio ignorantiae superba erat et dolosa, quia falsum dicebant et contra conscientiam, ut vitarent redargutionem apertam. Unde Chrysostomus[^p503-2]: « Mendacia, etsi non habent quem decipiant, tamen ipsa sibi mentiuntur »; Psalmus: « Mentita est iniquitas sibi »; et iterum: « Filii alieni mentiti sunt mihi »; et ideo Ioannis octavo: « Si dixero, quia non scio eum, ero similis vobis, mendax ».

9. (Vers. 8.). Quarto quantum ad *Christi veracitatem in refellendo* subiungit: *Et Iesus ait illis: neque ego dico vobis, in qua potestate haec facio.* Quia enim veritatem denegant, digni sunt, ut eis a Veritate notitiae veritas denegetur, secundum illud ad Romanos primo[^p503-3]: « Quia non probaverunt Deum habere in notitia, tradidit eos Deus in reprobum sensum ». — Et nota, quod Dominus eis veritatem occultavit duplici de causa; unde Glossa[^p503-4]: « Propter duas causas veritas quaerentibus est abscondenda: vel quia minus capaces sunt ad audiendum; unde dicitur Ioannis decimo sexto: *Multa habeo vobis dicere, sed non potestis portare modo;* vel quia ex odio vel contemptu quaerunt; unde dicitur Matthaei septimo: *Nolite dare sanctum canibus* ». Hinc Chrysostomus[^p503-5] dicit: « Quia ipse dixit: *Nolite sanctum dare canibus;* condecebat, ne ipse transgrederetur praeceptum, quod posuit »; Psalmus: « Praeceptum posuit et non praeteribit ». Informamur ergo ex hoc ad celandum *mysteria sacra* malevolis; Isaiae vigesimo quarto[^p503-6]: « Secretum meum mihi, secretum meum mihi »; et ad occultandum *nostram excellentiam;* Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo: « Laudet te alienus, et non os tuum; extraneus, et non labia tua ».

10. *Coepit autem dicere ad plebem* etc. Postquam repulit Pharisaeorum calumniam, hic consequenter arguit et detegit *occultam ipsorum perfidiam per parabolam* ad plebem propositam[^p503-7]. In hac autem parabola tria insinuantur. Primum est *benignitas liberalitatis divinae.* Secundum est *malignitas perversitatis Iudaicae,* ibi[^p503-8]: *Et in tempore vindemiae misit* etc. Tertium est *severitas ultionis iudiciariae,* de qua ibi: *Quid ergo faciet illis dominus vineae?*

*Benignitatem* autem *liberalitatis divinae circa synagogam* ostendit tripliciter, scilicet in *vocando*[^p503-9] *synagogam ad sui culturam;* in *dando debitam custodiam,* et in *commitiendo libertatem voluntariam.*

11. (Vers. 9.). Primo igitur quantum ad *parabolicam expressionem vocationis synagogae ad divinam culturam* dicit: *Coepit autem dicere ad plebem parabolam hanc;* quia scilicet maiores non erant digni audire. — *Homo quidam*[^p503-10] *plantavit vineam;* per *hominem* istum Deus intelligitur, ad cuius imaginem homo est formatus; Genesis primo[^p503-11]: « Faciamus hominem ad imaginem » etc. *Vinea* autem ista est synagoga; Isaiae quinto: « Vinea Domini exercituum domus Israel est ». Hanc vineam Dominus *plantavit,* quando in Patriarchis eos ad culturam suam elegit et vocavit; propter quod Ieremiae secundo[^p503-12]: « Ego plantavi te vineam electam, omne semen verum ». De istius vineae plantatione Isaiae quinto: « Vinea facta est dilecto meo in cornu, filio olei. Et sepivit eam et lapides elegit ex ea et plantavit vineam electam et aedificavit turrim in medio eius ». Per *sepem* autem huius vineae intelligitur Angelorum custodia; Psalmus[^p503-13]: « Angelis suis mandavit de te, ut custodiant te » etc. Per *lapides electos* intelligitur Patriarcharum stabilitas; unde in duodecim lapidibus *rationalis* erant nomina duodecim filiorum Israel, Exodi penultimo[^p503-14]. Per *turrim* autem, sacra Scriptura; Canticorum quarto: « Sicut turris
<!-- page 504 -->
David collum tuum, quae aedificata est cum propugnaculis » etc.

12. Secundo quantum ad *debitam custodiam datam* subdit: *Et locavit eam colonis.* Per *colonos* intelliguntur duces et proceres synagogae; Glossa[^p504-1]: « Coloni sunt operarii, qui ad colendam vineam hora prima, tertia, sexta et nona sunt conducti », secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo: « Simile est regnum caelorum homini patrifamilias, qui exiit primo mane conducere operarios in vineam suam ». Et de his in Canticorum ultimo[^p504-2]: « Vinea fuit pacifico in ea quae habuit populos; tradidit eam Dominus custodibus ». Ideo autem dicitur *locasse,* quia exigit pensionem; unde Canticorum octavo; « Vir offert pro fructu eius mille argenteos. Vinea mea coram me est. Mille tui pacifici, et ducenti his qui custodiunt fructus ». Huius coloni in Ecclesia, quae vinea Domini est, sunt episcopi et sacerdotes; unde primae ad Corinthios tertio[^p504-3]: « Ego plantavi, Apollo rigavit » etc. De hoc dicit Chrysostomus: « Coloni sunt sacerdotes, qui curam populi susceperunt, vel ad magnam gloriam, vel ad magnum periculum: ad gloriam, si diligenter; ad periculum, si negligenter egerunt ». Hanc igitur coloniam debent isti coloni diligentissime exercere, quia ad hoc instituti sunt, ut dicit Chrysostomus[^p504-4]: « Sacerdos non tantum placet Deo propter suam iustitiam, quantum pro populi sanctitate, quia sua iustitia una est, populi autem multiplex ». Valde igitur timendum est illud Proverbiorum vigesimo quarto[^p504-5]: « Per agrum hominis pigri transivi et per vineam viri stulti; et ecce, totum repleverant urticae », id est peccata luxuriae, « et operuerunt superficiem eius spinae », id est peccata avaritiae. Et ideo Augustinus Contra quinque Haereses[^p504-6]: « Ubi estis, o boni agricolae? Quid agitis? Quare vacatis? Videtis, quot malis plena sit terra: hinc spinae, hinc tribuli, hinc foenum surgit. Spinas incendite, tribulos eradicate, semina bona spargite ».

13. Tertio quantum ad *libertatem voluntariam unicuique concessam* subiungitur: *Et ipse peregre fuit multis temporibus*[^p504-7]; Beda: « *Peregre fuit* qui ubique praesens est, quia vinitoribus liberum operandi arbitrium reliquit »; Ecclesiastici decimo quinto[^p504-8]: « Deus ab initio constituit hominem et reliquit illum in manu consilii sui ». « Apposuit tibi aquam et ignem, ad quodcumque volueris, extende manum tuam. Ante hominem vita et mors, bonum et malum; quod placuerit ei dabitur illi ». Dicitur autem *peregre esse diebus multis,* quia magnam habet patientiam in exspectando ad poenitentiam. Unde Ambrosius[^p504-9]: « Longo tempore abfuit, ne praepropera videretur exactio. Et ideo, quo indulgentior liberalitas, eo inexcusabilior pervicacia ». Ideo ad Romanos secundo: « An ignoras, quoniam benignitas Dei ad poenitentiam te adducit »? et secundae Petri ultimo: « Patienter agit propter vos, nolens aliquem perire, sed omnes ad poenitentiam reverti et ad agnitionem veritatis eius venire ».

## English
<!-- page 501 -->

### Chapter XX.

#### Second, he confutes the unbelief of the Jews by words and instructions, and this in three parts.

1. *And it came to pass on one of the days* etc. After he has confuted the unbelief of the Jews by deeds, here he *confutes by words and instructions*[^p501-1]. Now this part is divided into three. In the first of them by instructions he confutes *the Pharisees erring out of malice.* In the second he confutes *the Sadducees erring out of ignorance,* there[^p501-2]: *And there came some of the Sadducees* etc. In the third he forms *the disciples to wisdom,* there: *And in the hearing of all the people* etc.

#### The confutation of the Pharisees erring out of malice, through three things.

The first part, in which *he confutes the Pharisees,* has three parts. In the first of them *he repels the calumny cast at him.* In the second *he reproves their hidden perfidy,* there[^p501-3]: *And he began to speak to the people* etc. But in the third *he convinces the craftiness they devised,* there: *And the chief priests sought* etc.

*The confutation,* then, *of the calumny cast at him* the Evangelist describes in this order. First is brought in *the frowardness of the Pharisees in assailing*[^p501-4]; second, *the sagacity of Christ in resisting;* third, *the guile of the Pharisees in fleeing;* fourth, *the veracity of Christ in refuting his adversaries.*

2. (Vers. 1.). First, then, as to *the frowardness of the Pharisees in assailing* he sets down first: *And it came to pass on one of the days, as he was teaching the people in the temple and preaching the gospel,* according to the gospel truth, which is to be declared to all, according to what he himself said to his disciples in Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p501-5]: « That which you hear in the ear, preach ye upon the housetops »; and in Mark, the last chapter: « Preach the Gospel to every creature ». So he also did himself, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter: « I make doctrine to shine forth to all as the morning light, and will declare it even afar off »; and after: « See ye that I have not laboured for myself only, but for all that seek out wisdom ». — But this doctrine of truth is hateful to the wicked, because « he that doth evil hateth the light », John, the third chapter[^p501-6]; therefore it is added: *The chief priests and the scribes, with the ancients, met together,* through a haughty frowardness. For in the chief priests there was haughtiness on account of the excellence of their *dignity;* in the scribes, on account of the excellence of their *cognition;* in the ancients, on account of *the hoariness of old age.* Therefore they did not come together as the humble, to learn, but as the proud, to calumniate, according to that of the Psalm[^p501-7]: « The princes met together against the Lord and against his Christ ». Whence in these is fulfilled that of Job, the twenty-fourth chapter: « They have been rebellious to the light, and have not known his ways ». Now the rebellion had its rise from envy, which is the daughter of pride. This is « the most evil wild beast, which devoured Joseph[^p501-8] »; and of it Chrysostom: « The priests said among themselves: We are the pillars of the temple; and behold, the whole Church leans upon him. We were as the visible tongue of the silent Scriptures; and behold, he resounds in the midst of the temple, and we, despised, are silent like a broken harp. We were fathers, he begets sons, and we are barren ». Which he says as to the three kinds of persons mentioned before, which were blinded through envy, and on account of this brought[^p501-9] manifest things into doubt and calumniate them.

3. (Vers. 2.). On account of which it is added: *And they speak, saying to him: Tell us, by what power dost thou these things? Or who is he that hath given thee this power?* Now this they ask as a doubtful thing, although it is manifest through most evident miracles, according to that of John, the fifth chapter[^p501-10]: « The works which I do give testimony of me »; but they by asking wished to lead the whole people into doubt, as though those miracles were not from God, but rather from the devil. Whence the Gloss[^p501-11]: « Of the power of God
<!-- page 502 -->
they doubt, and wish it to be understood that what he does is the devil's ». Now this doubt came to them from the corruption of their conscience; whence Chrysostom[^p502-1]: « Every man reckons another according to himself, and the fornicator thinks no one chaste, the chaste does not easily suspect the fornicator, the proud man thinks no one humble; so the priest who is not of God thinks the priesthood of no one to be of God ». Whence also the blind man enlightened reproved the blindness of the Pharisees, who said that they knew not whence Christ was, according to that of John, the ninth chapter[^p502-2]: « Herein is a wonderful thing, that you know not whence he is ». « Now we know that God doth not hear sinners, but if a man doth the will of God » etc. Therefore it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the tenth chapter: « In the hand of God is the power of man »; and to the Romans, the thirteenth chapter: « There is no power but from God » etc.

4. (Vers. 3.). Second, as to *the sagacity of Christ in resisting* it is added: *And Jesus answering said to them: I also will ask you one word.* When he ought to answer, he asked, not because he did not know, but because men are better bound by a question put than by an answer given outright. Whence Bede[^p502-3]: « He could have confuted the calumny of the tempters by an open answer, but he prudently asks, that they may be condemned either by their own silence or by their own sentence », as above, the nineteenth chapter: « Out of thy own mouth I judge thee, thou wicked servant ». For it is a caution that « one who altogether opposes is altogether to be opposed[^p502-4] », and against those who evade one must proceed from things granted, that they may be overcome.

5. (Vers. 4.). Therefore he cautiously seeks and asks, so that, whatever they answer, they may be convinced, when he adds: *Answer me: The baptism of John, whence was it*[^p502-5]*? From heaven, or of men?* To this they could not answer but in three ways: either simply *by keeping silence,* and so they were confounded; or by saying that it was *of men,* and so they would have been stoned by men; or by saying that it was *of God,* and so Christ would have his proposition, because John gave testimony of Christ in his baptism, according to that of John, the fifth chapter[^p502-6]: « You sent to John, and he gave testimony to the truth ». For it is said in John, the first chapter: « John gave testimony, saying: I saw the Holy Spirit coming down as a dove from heaven, and remaining upon him »; « and I saw, and I gave testimony, that this is the Son of God ». Whence the Gloss[^p502-7]: « He whom you confess to have had prophecy from heaven gave testimony to me, and you heard from him by what power I do these things ». Since then, as is said in John, the first chapter[^p502-8], « he was not the light, but was to give testimony of the lumen[^tr-lumen] », those who doubt of *the lumen* are sent back to the consideration of *the lamp;* for, as is said in John, the fifth chapter, « he was a burning and a shining lamp »; and those who doubt of *the Word* are sent back to *the voice;* John, the first chapter: « I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness ».

6. (Vers. 5.). Third, as to *the guile of the Pharisees in slipping away* it is subjoined: *But they thought within themselves, saying that, if we shall say: From heaven,* by confessing the truth; *he will say* to us: *Why then did you not believe him*[^p502-9]*?* reproving, namely, our *unbelief,* by which, namely, they refused to believe a heavenly man, according to that of Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « John came neither eating nor drinking, and they say: He hath a devil ».

7. (Vers. 6.). *But if we say: Of men, the whole people will stone us,* abhorring *the lying.* For blasphemers were to be stoned according to the Law; Leviticus, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p502-10]: « Bring forth the blasphemer without the camp, and let all that heard him put their hands upon his head, and let all the people stone him ». — *For they are certain that John is a prophet,* on account of his most evident sanctity, on account of which it is said of John in Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p502-11]: « What went you out into the desert to see? A prophet? Yea, I tell you, and more than a prophet ». Whence above, the third chapter: « All the people thinking of John, that perhaps he might be the Christ, John said: I baptize you with water; there shall come after me one mightier than I, the latchet of whose shoes I am not worthy to loose ».
<!-- page 503 -->

8. (Vers. 7.). And because hatred of the truth pressed them on the one side and fear of stoning on the other, therefore they put forward ignorance of the answer, when it is added: *And they answered that they knew not whence it was;* the Gloss[^p503-1]: « Fearing stoning, but fearing more the confession of the truth ». Now this confession of ignorance was proud and guileful, because they spoke falsely and against conscience, that they might avoid an open conviction. Whence Chrysostom[^p503-2]: « Lies, even if they have none to deceive, nevertheless lie to themselves »; the Psalm: « Iniquity hath lied to itself »; and again: « The children that are strangers have lied to me »; and therefore in John, the eighth chapter: « If I shall say that I know him not, I shall be like to you, a liar ».

9. (Vers. 8.). Fourth, as to *the veracity of Christ in refuting* he subjoins: *And Jesus said to them: neither do I tell you by what power I do these things.* For because they deny the truth, they are worthy that the truth of knowledge be denied them by the Truth, according to that to the Romans, the first chapter[^p503-3]: « Because they liked not to have God in their knowledge, God delivered them up to a reprobate sense ». — And note that the Lord hid the truth from them for a twofold cause; whence the Gloss[^p503-4]: « For two causes the truth is to be hidden from those who seek it: either because they are less capable of hearing; whence it is said in John, the sixteenth chapter: *I have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now;* or because they seek out of hatred or contempt; whence it is said in Matthew, the seventh chapter: *Give not that which is holy to dogs* ». Hence Chrysostom[^p503-5] says: « Because he himself said: *Give not that which is holy to dogs;* it was becoming that he should not himself transgress the precept which he set »; the Psalm: « He hath set a precept, and it shall not pass away ». We are formed, then, by this to conceal *the sacred mysteries* from the ill-willed; Isaias, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p503-6]: « My secret to myself, my secret to myself »; and to hide *our own excellence;* Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter: « Let another praise thee, and not thy own mouth; a stranger, and not thy own lips ».

10. *And he began to speak to the people* etc. After he has repelled the calumny of the Pharisees, here consequently he reproves and lays bare *their hidden perfidy by a parable* set before the people[^p503-7]. Now in this parable three things are intimated. The first is *the kindness of the divine liberality.* The second is *the malignity of the Jewish perversity,* there[^p503-8]: *And in the time of the vintage he sent* etc. The third is *the severity of the judicial vengeance,* of which there: *What therefore will the lord of the vineyard do to them?*

Now *the kindness of the divine liberality toward the synagogue* he shows in three ways, namely in *calling*[^p503-9] *the synagogue to his cultivation;* in *giving due custody,* and in *committing* [so printed: *commitiendo* for *committendo*] *voluntary liberty.*

11. (Vers. 9.). First, then, as to *the parabolic expression of the calling of the synagogue to the divine cultivation* he says: *And he began to speak to the people this parable;* because, namely, the greater ones were not worthy to hear. — *A certain*[^p503-10] *man planted a vineyard;* by this *man* God is understood, to whose image man was formed; Genesis, the first chapter[^p503-11]: « Let us make man to our image » etc. And this *vineyard* is the synagogue; Isaias, the fifth chapter: « The vineyard of the Lord of hosts is the house of Israel ». This vineyard the Lord *planted* when in the Patriarchs he chose and called them to his cultivation; on account of which in Jeremias, the second chapter[^p503-12]: « I planted thee a chosen vineyard, all true seed ». Of the planting of this vineyard, Isaias, the fifth chapter: « A vineyard was made for my beloved on a horn, the son of oil. And he fenced it in, and chose stones out of it, and planted a chosen vineyard, and built a tower in the midst of it ». Now by the *fence* of this vineyard is understood the custody of the Angels; the Psalm[^p503-13]: « He hath given his angels charge over thee, to keep thee » etc. By *the chosen stones* is understood the stability of the Patriarchs; whence on the twelve stones of the *rational* were the names of the twelve sons of Israel, Exodus, the last chapter but one[^p503-14]. And by *the tower,* sacred Scripture; Canticles, the fourth chapter: « As the tower
<!-- page 504 -->
of David is thy neck, which is built with bulwarks » etc.

12. Second, as to *the due custody given* he adds: *And he let it out to husbandmen.* By *the husbandmen* are understood the leaders and nobles of the synagogue; the Gloss[^p504-1]: « The husbandmen are the labourers who were hired to till the vineyard at the first, third, sixth and ninth hour », according to that of Matthew, the twentieth chapter: « The kingdom of heaven is like to a householder, who went out early in the morning to hire labourers into his vineyard ». And of these in Canticles, the last chapter[^p504-2]: « The peaceable had a vineyard, in that which had peoples; the Lord let out the same to keepers ». And he is said *to have let it out* because he requires a rent; whence Canticles, the eighth chapter; « A man offereth for the fruit thereof a thousand pieces of silver. My vineyard is before me. A thousand are thine, O peaceable, and two hundred for them that keep the fruit ». The husbandmen of this vineyard in the Church, which is the vineyard of the Lord, are the bishops and priests; whence the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p504-3]: « I have planted, Apollo watered » etc. Of this Chrysostom says: « The husbandmen are the priests, who have taken up the care of the people either to great glory or to great peril: to glory, if they have acted diligently; to peril, if negligently ». This husbandry, then, these husbandmen ought to exercise most diligently, because they have been appointed for this, as Chrysostom says[^p504-4]: « The priest pleases God not so much on account of his own justice as for the sanctity of the people, because his own justice is one, but the people's manifold ». Greatly, then, is that of Proverbs, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p504-5], to be feared: « I passed by the field of the sluggish man, and by the vineyard of the foolish man; and behold, nettles had filled it all », that is, the sins of lust, « and thorns had covered the face thereof », that is, the sins of avarice. And therefore Augustine, *Against Five Heresies*[^p504-6]: « Where are you, O good husbandmen? What are you doing? Why are you free[^tr-vacare]? You see with how many evils the earth is full: hence thorns, hence thistles, hence hay springs up. Burn the thorns, root up the thistles, scatter good seeds ».

13. Third, as to *the voluntary liberty granted to each one* it is subjoined: *And he was abroad for a long time*[^p504-7]; Bede: « *He was abroad* who is present everywhere, because he left to the vinedressers the free choice of working »; Ecclesiasticus, the fifteenth chapter[^p504-8]: « God made man from the beginning, and left him in the hand of his own counsel ». « He hath set water and fire before thee; stretch forth thy hand to whatsoever thou wilt. Before man is life and death, good and evil; that which shall please him shall be given him ». And he is said *to be abroad many days* because he has great patience in waiting for penance. Whence Ambrose[^p504-9]: « He was absent a long time, lest the exaction should seem over-hasty. And therefore, the more indulgent the liberality, the more inexcusable the obstinacy ». Therefore to the Romans, the second chapter: « Knowest thou not that the kindness of God leadeth thee to penance »? and the second of Peter, the last chapter: « He dealeth patiently for your sake, not willing that any should perish, but that all should return to penance and come to the knowledge of his truth ».

[^tr-lumen]: **En.** Translator's note: this translation keeps *lux* as "light" and *lumen* as "lumen", except where a verse is quoted as a verse and no surrounding prose leans on the word. Here (¶ 5) John 1:8 sets the two words side by side, *non erat ille lux, sed ut testimonium perhiberet de lumine*, and Bonaventure takes up the second at once: *dubitatores de lumine remittuntur ad considerationem lucernae*, "those who doubt of the lumen are sent back to the consideration of the lamp". So the verse reads "he was not the light, but was to give testimony of the lumen", and the *lucerna* of John 5:35, on which the same sentence leans, is "lamp" rather than the Douay's "light". Elsewhere in this pericope the word is *lux* (John 3:20 *odit lucem*, "hateth the light", ¶ 2), and Job 24:13 *rebelles fuerunt lumini* (¶ 2) is a verse adduced as a verse, with no prose leaning on the word, so it keeps "rebellious to the light".

[^tr-vacare]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin *vacare* holds together several senses that English has to split: being at leisure, being free, being free *for* something and being free *from* something. Here (¶ 12) Augustine's reproach of the idle husbandmen, *Quid agitis? Quare vacatis?*, uses the verb absolutely, with nothing named that they are free for: "What are you doing? Why are you free?", that is, why are you at leisure while the vineyard runs to thorns. The absolute use keeps "to be free" (as at Cap. X ¶ 67). No *vaca-* word is rendered "rest" in this translation, because *quies* and *requies* take that word.
