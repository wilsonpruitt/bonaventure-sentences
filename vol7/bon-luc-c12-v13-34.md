# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XII, Vers. 13-34
## *A sollicitudine avaritiae revocatur quadrupliciter*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 316 -->

#### Secundo ad cavendam cupiditatem venientem ex timiditate monet dupliciter.

21. *Ait autem ei*[^p316-5] *quidam* etc. Postquam dissuasit dolositatem venientem ex humana timiditate et ipsam timiditatem, in hac parte dissuadet *cupiditatem.* Et habet haec pars duas partes. In quarum prima *revocat a sollicitudine avaritiae;* in secunda vero *invitat ad sollicitudinem providentiae,* infra eodem[^p316-6]: *Sint lumbi vestri praecincti* etc.

#### A sollicitudine avaritiae revocatur quadrupliciter.

A *sollicitudine* autem *avaritiae* revocat quadrupliciter: primo scilicet *documento rationali;* secundo vero, *exemplo terribili,* ibi[^p316-7]: *Dixit autem ad illos similitudinem* etc.; tertio, *argumento irrefragabili,* ibi: *Dixitque ad discipulos suos* etc.; quarto, *promisso desiderabili,* ibi: *Et vos nolite quaerere, quid manducetis* etc.

Circa *documentum rationale,* per quod revocat a sollicitudine avaritiae, tria introducuntur: primum est *confutatio petitionis avarae*[^p316-8], secundum est *dissuasio avaritiae,* tertium est *assignatio causae.*

22. (Vers. 13.). Primo igitur quantum ad *confutationem petitionis avarae: Ait autem ei quidam de turba: Magister, dic fratri meo, ut dividat mecum hereditatem.* Vere iste *de turba* erat, quia *de terra sollicitus erat,* secundum illud Isaiae vigesimo nono[^p316-9]: « De humo mussitabit eloquium tuum » etc. Contra se etiam loquebatur, dum *magistrum* eum vocabat, quia Christus non fatebatur se magistrum in docendo, temporalia acquiri et dividi, sed potius derelinqui; unde adolescenti quaerenti et dicenti[^p316-10]: « Magister bone, quid faciendo vitam aeternam possidebo »? respondit: « Si vis perfectus esse, vade et vende » etc. Unde Christus non docebat, quomodo augeretur pecunia, sed quomodo servaretur inopia. — Contra se etiam loquitur, dum vult *dividi a fratre,* cui debet coniungi. Veniebat autem haec quaestio ex amore privato, qui dividit coniuncta; quod maxime facit diabolus, non Dominus; Osee decimo tertio[^p316-11]: « Ipse inter fratres dividet ».

23. (Vers. 14.). Et quoniam, sicut dicit Beda in Glossa[^p316-12], « Christus non est Deus dissensionis, sed pacis et unitatis »; ideo confutat petitionem praedictam, cum subdit: *At ille dixit ei*[^p316-13], scilicet Iesus: *Homo, quis me constituit iudicem, aut divisorem super vos?* iudicem scilicet litium et divisorem facultatum; Glossa[^p316-14]: « Dedignatur esse iudex litium aut arbiter facultatum, qui vivorum et mortuorum habet iudicium et meritorum arbitrium ». Verum est utique, sicut dicitur in Psalmo[^p316-15], quod « ipse constitutus est rex ab eo super Sion, montem sanctum eius »; et quod « in ditione eius cuncta sunt posita »; sed quia Deus eum miserat ad spiritualia communicanda: ideo descendere recusabat ad temporalia dividenda, insinuans in hoc illud secundae ad Timotheum secundo: « Nemo militans Deo implicat se negotiis saecularibus ». — Et nota, quod Christus vocat eum *hominem,* animalem et brutalem, propter *amorem in terrenis* et *litem divisionis.* Unde primae ad Corinthios tertio[^p316-16]: « Cum sit inter vos zelus et
<!-- page 317 -->
contentio » etc.; et Psalmus: « Filii hominum, usquequo gravi corde »? Unde in hoc confutatio insinuatur petitionis avarae.

24. (Vers. 15.). Secundo quantum ad *dissuasionem avaritiae* subdit: *Dixitque ad illos: Videte et cavete ab omni avaritia. Videte* scilicet, ut providi, et *cavete,* ut cauti; ad Ephesios quinto[^p317-1]: « Videte, fratres, quomodo caute ambuletis, non quasi insipientes, sed ut sapientes » etc. *Providentia* namque facit cavere pericula, in quae incidit quis per avaritiam; primae ad Timotheum ultimo[^p317-2]: « Qui volunt divites fieri incidunt in tentationem et in laqueum diaboli et desideria multa inutilia et nociva, quae mergunt homines in interitum et perditionem. Radix enim malorum est cupiditas » etc. Unde ipsa excaecat hominem, quod significatum fuit in excaecatione Tobiae per stercora hirundinum, Tobiae secundo[^p317-3]. Et ideo dicit: *Videte,* scilicet aeterna, *et cavete ab avaritia,* quae quaerit temporalia; secundae ad Corinthios quarto: « Non contemplantibus vobis, quae videntur, sed quae non videntur. Quae enim videntur temporalia sunt, quae autem non videntur aeterna sunt ». Et quoniam appetitus cuiuslibet boni transitorii, scilicet pecuniae et potentiae et honoris, *avaritia* potest dici; ideo dicit: *Cavete ab omni avaritia;* Isaiae trigesimo tertio[^p317-4]: « Quis habitare poterit cum ardoribus sempiternis »? et sequitur: « Qui proiicit avaritiam ex calumnia et excutit manus suas ab omni munere ».

25. Tertio quantum ad *assignationem rationis et causae* subdit: *Quia non in abundantia cuiusquam vita eius est*[^p317-5] *ex his quae possidet;* hoc est, vita alicuius non est *in abundantia; abundantia,* inquam, *ex his quae possidet,* id est, nullus propter abundantiam divitiarum vivere potest diutius; Psalmus[^p317-6]: « Relinquent alienis divitias suas, et sepulcra eorum domus illorum in aeternum »; et Iob vigesimo septimo: « Dives, cum dormierit, nihil secum auferet » etc.; et Psalmus: « Dormierunt somnum suum, et nihil invenerunt omnes viri divitiarum in manibus suis ». Unde *abundantia* vitam non prolongat, sed frequenter abbreviat; Ecclesiastici octavo[^p317-7]: « Multos
perdidit aurum et argentum »; et Actuum octavo: « Pecunia tua tecum sit in perditionem ». Et propterea ipsa perdenda est, ne perdat possessorem; Ecclesiastici vigesimo nono: « Perde pecuniam propter fratrem et amicum, et non abscondas illam sub lapide in perditionem ».

26. (Vers. 16.). *Dixit autem ad illos similitudinem, dicens* etc. Hic secundo revocat a sollicitudine avaritiae *per exemplum terribile*[^p317-8]; circa quod tria introducuntur, scilicet vanae securitatis *occasio,* vanae securitatis *conceptio* et vanae securitatis *subtractio.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *vanae securitatis occasionem* venientem ex fructuum ubertate dicit: *Hominis cuiusdam divitis uberes fructus ager attulit.* Haec enim consueverunt esse bona divitum ex ubertate fructuum temporalium; Psalmus[^p317-9]: « Promptuaria eorum plena, eructantia ex hoc in illud; oves » etc.; unde Iob vigesimo primo: « Quare impii vivunt, sublevati sunt confortatique divitiis »? « Domus eorum securae sunt et pacatae, et non est virga Dei super illos ».

27. (Vers. 17.). Et quia divitiae sic faciunt securum, quod *reddunt sollicitum;* ideo subdit: *Et cogitabat intra se dicens: Quid faciam, quia non habeo, quo congregem fructus meos?* Ecclesiastae quarto[^p317-10]: « Est alia vanitas, quam vidi sub sole: unus est et secundum non habet, et tamen laborare non cessat, nec satiantur oculi eius divitiis », immo certe sollicitantur; Ecclesiastae quinto: « Saturitas divitis non sinit eum dormire ».

28. (Vers. 18.). Et huius ratio est, quia cogitur *cogitare;* et ideo subdit: *Et dixit: Hoc faciam: destruam horrea mea et maiora faciam; et illuc congregabo omnia, quae nata sunt mihi et bona mea.* Glossa[^p317-11]: « Ecce, magna sollicitudo ex avaritia », quia sollicitudo destruendi facta et aedificandi nova. Licet enim essent horrea plena, cupiditas adhuc vacua quaerebat nova receptacula; Ecclesiastae quinto[^p317-12]: « Avarus non implebitur pecunia » etc.; et Ecclesiastici decimo quarto: « Insatiabilis oculus cupidi, in parte iniquitatis non satiabitur ». Impletum
<!-- page 318 -->
erat horreum, sed cor erat vacuum, tum quia animam Trinitatis capacem solus Deus potest implere[^p318-1]; tum quia temporalia non intrant cor nisi secundum phantasticam similitudinem; tum quia concupiscentiam augent; tum quia animam non faciunt meliorem; tum etiam, quia sunt omnino alterius naturae, sicut Angelus et corporalis locus, sic anima et corporalis thesaurus; tum etiam, quia anima, cum sit quanta non *mole,* sed *virtute,* non impletur quantitate materiali, sed virtuali, sicut est gratia Spiritus sancti, Sapientiae primo et Actuum secundo[^p318-2].

29. (Vers. 19.). Secundo vero quantum ad *vanae securitatis conceptionem* per stolidam promissionem subditur: *Et dicam animae meae,* id est animalitati; Ioannis duodecimo[^p318-3]: « Qui amat animam suam perdet eam ». — *Anima, habes multa bona posita in annos plurimos;* Apocalypsis tertio: « Dicis, quia dives sum et locupletatus et nullius egeo » etc.; et Zachariae undecimo: « Benedictus Dominus, quia divites facti sumus ». — Et quia abundantia cum securitate parit malum lasciviae, ideo subdit: *Requiesce*[^p318-4]; ecce, *negligentia;* Ecclesiastae quarto: « Stultus complicat manus suas et dicit: Melior est pugillus cum requie quam plena utraque manus cum labore et afflictione animi ». *Comede;* ecce, *gula;* Ecclesiastici undecimo[^p318-5]: « Inveni requiem mihi, et tunc manducabo de bonis meis solus ». *Bibe,* quantum ad *crapulam;* quocontra Proverbiorum vigesimo tertio: « Noli esse in conviviis potatorum »; « quia vacantes potibus et dantes symbola consumentur ». *Epulare;* ecce, *lascivia;* Iacobi quinto[^p318-6]: « Epulati estis super terram et in luxuriis enutristis corda vestra ». Haec enim consueverunt esse vitia divitum; Amos sexto: « Vae! qui opulenti estis in Sion »; et post: « Qui dormitis in lectis eburneis et lascivitis in stratis vestris; qui comeditis agnum de grege, bibentes vinum in phialis, et optimo unguento delibuti ». Istius autem lasciviae causa est securitas vana, quam anima stulte concipit, vel *promittendo* sibi longam vitam temporalem per *praesumtionem,* secundum illud Isaiae vigesimo octavo[^p318-7]: « Percussimus foedus cum morte et cum inferno fecimus pactum; flagellum inundans, cum transierit, non veniet super nos »; vel *contemnendo* per *desperationem;* Isaiae vigesimo secundo: « Comedamus et bibamus; cras enim moriemur ».

30. (Vers. 20.). Tertio quantum ad *vanae securitatis subtractionem* per divinae sententiae aequitatem subditur: *Dixit autem illi Deus.* Glossa[^p318-8]: « *Dicere* Dei ad hominem est pravas eius machinationes subita animadversione compescere ». — Propter quod addit sententiam: *Stulte, hac nocte animam tuam repetent*[^p318-9] *a te;* primae ad Thessalonicenses quinto: « Cum dixerint: Pax et securitas, tunc repentinus superveniet eis interitus ». *Stultum* eum vocat pro sapientia terrena, quia, primae ad Corinthios primo, « stultam fecit Deus sapientiam huius mundi ». Stultus est qui non praevidet futura pericula; Ecclesiastae secundo[^p318-10]: « Sapientis oculi in capite eius; stultus in tenebris ambulat »; quia, de futuris incertus, securus dormit; quocontra Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo: « Ne glorieris in crastinum, ignorans, quid superventura pariat dies »; et Iacobi quarto: « Quae est vita nostra? Vapor ad modicum parens ». — Et quia, vita perdita, perduntur haec temporalia, ideo subdit: *Quae autem parasti cuius erunt?* Ambrosius[^p318-11]: « Non sunt hominis bona, quae secum auferre non potest; sola misericordia comes est defunctorum »; unde primae ad Timotheum ultimo: « Nihil intulimus in hunc mundum, haud dubium, quod nec auferre quid possumus »; et Iob vigesimo: « Cum habuerit quod concupierat, possidere non poterit; cum satiatus fuerit, arctabitur, et omnis dolor irruet super eum ».

31. (Vers. 21.). Et quia haec sententia communis est omnibus avaris, ideo subdit: *Sic est omnis*[^p318-12]*, qui thesaurizat sibi, et non est in Deum dives. Thesaurizat sibi* qui thesauros sibi in terra multiplicat; Psalmus: « Thesaurizat et ignorat cui congregabit ea »; *in Deum* autem *dives est* qui abundat
<!-- page 319 -->
in meritis et operibus pietatis; primae ad Corinthios primo[^p319-1]: « In omnibus divites facti estis in illo, ut nihil vobis desit in ulla gratia ». Ideo dicitur Matthaei sexto: « Nolite vobis thesaurizare thesauros in terra, ubi aerugo et tinea demolitur ». « Thesaurizate autem vobis thesauros in caelo, ubi neque aerugo neque tinea demolitur, et ubi fures non effodiunt nec furantur ». — Facit autem nos *divites esse in Deum* spes, quam in ipsum habemus, secundum illud primae Petri primo[^p319-2]: « Regeneravit nos in spem vivam, in hereditatem incorruptibilem, conservatam in caelis ». Haec autem spes radicatur in paupertate; unde secundae ad Corinthios sexto: « Sicut egentes, multos autem locupletantes; tanquam nihil habentes, et omnia possidentes ». Talis erat Paulus; unde ad Philippenses ultimo[^p319-3]: « Habeo omnia et abundo ». Tales nos fecit Christus; unde secundae ad Corinthios octavo: « Factus est pro vobis egenus, ut illius inopia vos divites essetis ». Ideo talibus dicit: « Beati pauperes spiritu, quoniam ipsorum est regnum caelorum ».

32. *Dixitque ad discipulos suos* etc. Hic iam tertio[^p319-4] revocat a sollicitudine avaritiae *per argumentum irrefragabile,* quod sumtum est a triplici genere creaturae: primo a creatura *rationali;* secundo, a *sensibili,* ibi[^p319-5]: *Considerate corvos;* tertio, a *vegetabili,* ibi: *Considerate lilia agri.*

Primo igitur a creatura *rationali* sumit tale argumentum: qui dat quod maius est, dabit quod est minus; sed anima plus est quam esca, et corpus plus quam vestimentum: ergo qui dedit animam et corpus dabit victum et vestitum: non oportet ergo de his sollicitum esse. In qua ratione primo praemittit *principalem conclusionem,* deinde subiungit *probationem.*

33. (Vers. 22.). Praemittit ergo primo *principalem conclusionem,* cum dicit: *Ideo dico vobis: Nolite solliciti esse animae vestrae, quid manducetis, neque corpori vestro*[^p319-6]*, quid vestiamini,* id est, nolite sollicitari de victu et vestitu, quae sunt maxime necessaria vitae, multo minus nec de aliis,
quae spectant ad vitam praesentem; ad Philippenses quarto: « Nihil solliciti sitis, sed in omni oratione et obsecratione cum gratiarum actione petitiones vestrae innotescant apud Deum ». — Et nota, quod non prohibet sollicitudinem, quae provenit ex *providentia animi,* sed illam quae venit ex *diffidentia Dei,* quasi Deus non habeat curam de nobis; quocontra primae Petri ultimo[^p319-7]: « Omnem sollicitudinem vestram proiicientes in eum, quia ipsi cura est de vobis »; et in Psalmo: « Iacta cogitatum tuum in Domino » etc.; et Ambrosius: « Indecorum est homini curare de cibo vel de veste, qui militat regno; novit enim rex, quemadmodum familiam suam pascat, alat et vestiat ».

34. (Vers. 23.). Deinde addit *probationem,* cum dicit: *Anima plus est quam esca, et corpus plus quam vestimentum;* ex quo sequitur: qui dedit quod plus, supplebit etiam quod minus est. Ambrosius[^p319-8]: « Absurdum est, ut victus copiam nobis defuturam putemus, qui vivendi iugem substantiam a Deo consequimur ». Anima enim et corpus sunt partes hominis constitutivae, propter quem factae sunt ceterae creaturae, secundum illud Genesis primo[^p319-9]: « Faciamus hominem ad imaginem et similitudinem nostram, et praesit volatilibus caeli » etc. Unde Chrysostomus[^p319-10]: « Omnia fecit Deus propter hominem, hominem autem propter se; omnia fecit Deus in sapientia, hominem autem non solum in sapientia, sed etiam secundum sapientiam suam ». Unde Sapientiae sexto: « Pusillum et magnum ipse fecit, et aequaliter est illi cura de omnibus ». Et Augustinus de Agone christiano[^p319-11]: « Rationales animas per se ipsum Deus curat et Angelos, cetera vero per ipsos gubernat ». — Et nota, quod recte comparat animam et praefert escae, et corpus vestimento, quia anima vigorem dat corpori illud vegetando, et corpus est quasi indumentum animae, quod extra apparet. Si ergo Deus providet corporalem vigorem animae vegetantis, multo fortius sufficientiam alimoniae sustentantis. Rursus, si animae providet indumentum corporis, multo fortius tegumentum vestis exterioris[^p319-12].
<!-- page 320 -->

35. (Vers. 24.). *Considerate corvos.* Hic sumit argumentum a *creatura sensibili;* et primo *ponit exemplum,* deinde elicit *argumentum.*

Proponit igitur *exemplum* considerandum, cum dicit: *Considerate corvos, quia non seminant neque metunt;* ecce, quod non habent laboris exercitium; *quibus non est cellarium,* quantum ad vinum reponendum; *neque horreum,* quantum ad frumentum condendum; ecce, quod non habent repositorium. *Et Deus pascit illos;* ecce, quod non patiuntur defectum; Psalmus[^p320-1]: « Qui dat iumentis escam ipsorum, et pullis corvorum invocantibus eum ». — Et nota, quod magis proponit exemplum de corvis quam de aliis avibus, et hoc tum propter hoc, quod maximae sunt edacitatis; unde Genesis octavo[^p320-2] dicitur, quod « Noe dimisit corvum per fenestram arcae, qui egrediebatur et non revertebatur »; et ratio huius est, quia insederat super cadaver; tum etiam, quia haec fertur esse natura corvi, quod pullos suos in principio non pascit, quia non putat suos esse, donec videat, eos esse nigros; Iob trigesimo octavo[^p320-3]: « Quis praeparat corvo escam suam, quando pulli eius clamant ad Deum, vagientes, eo quod non habeant cibos »? Unde per *corvos* merito alia volatilia intelligi possunt; Matthaei sexto[^p320-4]: « Respicite volatilia caeli, quoniam non serunt » etc.

36. Deinde *elicit argumentum,* cum addit: *Quanto magis vos pluris estis illis?* Glossa[^p320-5]: « Propter rationem et immortalitatem, quae vobis promittitur, plus valetis »; primae ad Corinthios nono: « Nunquid de bobus cura est Deo? Nam propter nos scripta sunt ». — Sed quia posset aliquis contra arguere, quod nobis magis competit sollicitudo quam corvis propter excellentiam rationis; ostendit, quod hoc nihil est. Si enim *augmentum* corporis non fit per humanam cogitationem, sed per divinam dispositionem, pari ratione et *alimentum.*

37. (Vers. 25.). Propter quod addit: *Quis autem vestrum cogitando*[^p320-6] *potest adiicere ad staturam suam cubitum unum?* quasi dicat: nullus, quia hoc non est hominis cogitantis, sed opus Dei producentis; unde secundi Machabaeorum septimo[^p320-7] dicitur: « Nescio, qualiter in utero meo apparuistis; neque ego vitam donavi vobis, et singulorum membra non ego ipsa compegi »; et primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto: « Deus dat illi corpus, prout vult, et unicuique seminum proprium corpus ». Unde et Philosophus[^p320-8] dicit, quod « omnium natura constantium terminus est et ratio magnitudinis et augmenti ». Si ergo haec minima non sunt in humana, sed in divina providentia constituta, multo minus nec alia.

38. (Vers. 26.). Ideo addit: *Si ergo neque quod minimum est potestis, quid de ceteris solliciti estis?* quasi dicat: frustra et stulte. Beda[^p320-9]: « Illi tegendum corpus relinquite, qui illud ad hanc mensuram fecit pervenire ». — Verumtamen ista ratio non videtur valere, quia, licet non oporteat sollicitari de *augmento,* possumus tamen et debemus de *nutrimento.* Licet enim vis *augmentativa* non sit subiecta voluntati et rationi, nihil tamen impedit de *nutritiva,* quin sit ei opus regimine et providentia.

Verumtamen ad hoc est responsio, quod ratio est bona: quia, si illa quae sunt commissa *providentiae naturae,* non oportet hominem regere et curare[^p320-10]; pari ratione nec illa quae commissa sunt *providentiae supernae.* — Rursus, sicut non potest homo corpus augere, sic nec vitae terminum, quem praefixit Dominus, protelare. — Postremo; si non
<!-- page 321 -->
oportet sollicitari de ipsius corporis mensura, sed est homo contentus ea quam Dominus dedit; quare non similiter de alimonia extrinseca?

39. (Vers. 27.). *Considerate lilia* etc. Hic iam tertio *sumit argumentum de vegetabili.* Et primo praemittit *similitudinem sensibilem,* deinde subdit *adaptationem.*

Praemittit ergo *similitudinem,* cum dicit: *Considerate lilia agri*[^p321-1]*, quomodo crescunt; non laborant neque nent.* Ambrosius: « Aperta comparatio; lilia non, sicut ceteri fructus, aliquem agricolarum cultum requirunt »; unde ipsa crescunt non humano studio, sed divino imperio, quod datum fuit ab ipso mundi exordio; Genesis primo[^p321-2]: « Germinet terra herbam virentem » etc. — Et quia opus summi Artificis est excellentius omni opere humanae artis, ideo addit: *Dico autem vobis, quia*[^p321-3] *nec Salomon in omni gloria sua vestiebatur, sicut unum ex istis;* et tamen Salomon circa ornatum vestium fuit curiosissimus, simul habens voluntatem, industriam et facultatem ad ornandum se. Unde de ipso dicitur tertii Regum decimo[^p321-4]: « Videns regina Saba omnem sapientiam Salomonis et habitacula servorum et ordines ministrantium et vestes eorum, non habebat ultra spiritum »; et hoc, quia peritissime omnia ordinaverat secundum humanam industriam; sed tamen operibus et industriae naturae, quae est opus Dei, aequari non poterat. Unde Beda[^p321-5]: « Quae purpura regum, quae pictura textricum potest floribus comparari »?

40. (Vers. 28.). Deinde post similitudinem subdit *adaptationem* et infert *conclusionem: Si autem foenum, quod hodie est in agro et cras in clibanum mittitur,* id est, cito marcescit; Psalmus[^p321-6]: « Fiant sicut foenum tectorum, quod, priusquam evellatur, exaruit »; et Iacobi primo: « Exortus est sol cum ardore et arefecit foenum, et flos eius decidit, et decor vultus eius deperiit »; *Deus sic vestit,* pulcro scilicet ornatu et decoro: « Ipse color dicitur vestimentum floris[^p321-7] ». — *Quanto magis vos, pusillae fidei?* id est, quanto magis vos vestiet, etiam si modicum habueritis de fide, quia tam ratione naturae quam ratione fidei praecellitis. Unde Petro de omnipotentia in tempestate dubitanti dicitur Matthaei decimo quarto[^p321-8]: « Modicae fidei, quare dubitasti »? *Modicam fidem* habet de regimine paternae providentiae qui timet quotidie mori fame; Psalmus[^p321-9]: « Non crediderunt in Deo nec speraverunt in salutari eius »; et sequitur: « Et mandavit nubibus desuper et ianuas caeli aperuit, et pluit illis manna ad manducandum » etc. Si quis igitur verae fidei est, credens, omnia gubernari superna providentia; cum providentia Dei maiorem curam gerat et gerere debeat de pretiosioribus, dignioribus et durabilioribus, de rationalibus quam de irrationalibus vel de vegetabilibus; et de his gerit talem providentiam, quod nil eis deest: multo minus ergo dubitandum[^p321-10] de his quae spectant ad regimen hominis, tanquam pretiosioris, melioris et dignioris inter ceteras creaturas, quia rationalis est et ad imaginem Dei factus. Est igitur ratio ista irrefragabilis, quia, si quis contrarium huius dicat, destruit regimen divinae providentiae et ordinem universi. Et ideo non tantum est modicae fidei, verum etiam existimationis[^p321-11] perversae.

41. (Vers. 29.). *Et vos nolite quaerere, quid manducetis.* Hic quarto revocat a sollicitudine avaritiae per *promissum desiderabile*[^p321-12]; circa quod tria introducuntur: dissuadet enim sollicitudinem avaritiae et cupiditatis promittendo *sufficientiam viatici, superexcellentiam praemii* et *superabundantiam thesauri.*

Primo igitur dissuadet sollicitudinem avaritiae et cupiditatis *promittendo sufficientiam viatici,* cum dicit: *Et vos nolite quaerere, quid manducetis, aut quid bibatis,* videlicet ut cupidi et curiosi quaerunt, ut abundent et superbiant. — Ideo addit: *Et nolite in sublime tolli,* pro acquisitione terrenorum; primae ad Timotheum ultimo[^p321-13]: « Divitibus huius saeculi
<!-- page 322 -->
praecipe, non sublime sapere nec sperare in incerto divitiarum ». Nam sic terrena quaerere non est hominum caelestium, sed terrenorum.

42. (Vers. 30.). Ideo addit: *Haec enim omnia gentes mundi quaerunt,* quia mundanam habent sapientiam; Baruch tertio[^p322-1]: « Filii Agar, qui exquisierunt prudentiam, quae de terra est, negotiatores Merrhae et Theman ». Viri autem caelestes non debent his esse intenti, quia sunt de familia summi Patris. — Unde addit: *Pater autem vester scit, quoniam*[^p322-2] *his indigetis;* et non est dubium, quin *possit* providere; ad Romanos decimo: « Idem Dominus omnium, dives in omnes, qui invocant illum ». Non est dubium etiam, quin *velit;* Isaiae quadragesimo nono: « Nunquid oblivisci potest mulier infantem suum, ut non misereatur filio uteri sui » etc. Et ideo volentes tendere ad patriam non derelinquentur in via ob defectum cibi.

43. (Vers. 31.). Propter quod addit: *Verumtamen quaerite primum regnum Dei*[^p322-3], tanquam principale intentum; *et haec omnia adiicientur vobis,* tanquam viaticum, quia qui paratus est dare *regnum* absque dubio non denegabit *cibum,* qui dare paratus est aeterna non denegabit temporalia. Unde Augustinus[^p322-4]: « Ostendit Dominus, temporalia non esse petenda tanquam bona nostra, etsi necessaria. Regnum autem Dei petendum est, et in illo finis ponendus est, propter quem omnia faciamus ». Ideo sollicitudo nostra non debet esse pro acquirendo cibo, sed pro acquirendo regno aeterno; ad Romanos decimo quarto[^p322-5]: « Regnum Dei non est esca et potus, sed iustitia et pax et gaudium in Spiritu sancto ». Chrysostomus[^p322-6]: « Regnum Dei est *retributio;* iustitia est *via,* per quam venimus ad regnum ». Ideo Matthaei sexto: « Quaerite primum regnum Dei *et iustitiam eius* » etc. — Et nota, quod haec promissio est satis recta, quia qui quaerit *regnum et iustitiam eius* est Dei *servus,* Dei *amicus* et Dei *filius;* ad Romanos octavo[^p322-7]: « Qui spiritu Dei aguntur, hi filii Dei sunt »; quod autem Deus desit servo suo, amico et filio, ut ei non provideat quae sunt necessaria; valde est perversum sentire. Nam talis habet Deum, talis habetur a Deo, et ideo habet per
consequens quae habet Deus; unde Apostolus primae ad Corinthios tertio[^p322-8]: « Omnia vestra sunt, vos autem Christi, Christus autem Dei ».

44. (Vers. 32.). Secundo dissuadet sollicitudinem avaritiae *promittendo superexcellentiam praemii,* cum subdit: *Nolite timere, pusillus grex; pusillus grex* dicitur respectu *multitudinis reproborum;* Matthaei vigesimo[^p322-9]: « Multi sunt vocati, pauci vero electi ». Vel *pusillus* ratione *modicitatis propriae;* primae ad Corinthios primo[^p322-10]: « Videte vocationem vestram, fratres, quia non multi sapientes secundum carnem, non multi potentes » etc. Vel *pusillus* ratione *humilitatis voluntariae;* Ezechielis trigesimo quarto[^p322-11]: « Vos autem greges mei homines estis, et ego Deus vester ». Est enim Deus humilium; Ecclesiastici tertio: « Magna potentia Dei solius, et ab humilibus honoratur ». Et talibus promittit Deus regnum Matthaei decimo nono: « Sinite parvulos venire ad me; talium enim est regnum caelorum ». — Ideo subdit: *Quia complacuit Patri vestro*[^p322-12] *dare vobis regnum;* Proverbiorum vigesimo nono: « Humilem spiritu suscipiet gloria »; et Iob vigesimo secundo: « Qui humiliatus fuerit erit in gloria ». Haec autem superexcellentia regni promissi inducit *spem,* et inducendo spem inducit *securitatem,* et per hoc aufert *pusillanimitatem timoris* et *ardorem cupiditatis;* secundae ad Corinthios sexto[^p322-13]: « Sicut egentes, multos autem locupletantes, tanquam nihil habentes, et omnia possidentes ». Solum enim illud regnum est vera cordis possessio, quae cor implet et auferri non potest, quia intus est; infra decimo septimo: « Ecce, regnum Dei intra vos est ».

45. Et nota, quod Patri complacuit pusillis, id est pauperibus spiritu, *dare regnum gloriae;* unde Matthaei quinto[^p322-14]: « Beati pauperes spiritu, quoniam ipsorum est regnum caelorum »; quia tales *desiderant aeterna;* Proverbiorum decimo: « Desiderium suum iustis dabitur »; et Psalmus: « Desiderium pauperum exaudivit Dominus »; quoniam *contemnunt temporalia;* Matthaei decimo nono[^p322-15]: « Qui reliquerit patrem aut matrem centuplum accipiet » etc.; *amplectuntur spiritualia;* ad Galatas quinto: « Si spiritu vivimus, spiritu et ambulemus; non efficiamur
<!-- page 323 -->
inanis gloriae cupidi ». — Item complacuit *dare eis veniam;* Iudith nono[^p323-1]: « Humilium et mansuetorum semper tibi placuit deprecatio », scilicet ad dandum veniam; Exodi trigesimo tertio: « Misereor cui miserebor ». — Placuit *dare gratiam;* Isaiae quadragesimo secundo[^p323-2]: « Ecce, servus meus, suscipiam eum, electus meus » etc. — Placuit *dare sapientiam;* Matthaei undecimo: « Abscondisti haec a sapientibus et prudentibus et revelasti ea parvulis ». — Placuit *dare gloriam aeternam,* sicut hic: *Nolite timere, pusillus* etc.; Psalmus[^p323-3]: « Beneplacitum est Domino super timentes eum, et in eis qui sperant super misericordia eius ».

46. (Vers. 33.). Tertio dissuadet sollicitudinem avaritiae *promittendo superabundantiam thesauri* pro dimissione saeculi, cum dicit: *Vendite quae possidetis et date eleemosynam,* id est, distribuite bona vestra pauperibus, secundum illud Matthaei decimo nono[^p323-4]: « Vade et vende omnia, quae habes, et da pauperibus ». — Et quia durum est vendere et dare absque recompensatione, ideo addit: *Facite vobis sacculos, qui non veterascunt;* Ecclesiastici decimo septimo[^p323-5]: « Eleemosyna viri quasi sacculus cum eo, et gratiam hominis quasi pupillam conservabit; et postea resurget et retribuet illis retributionem, unicuique in caput ipsorum ». — Et quia ista retributio est opulentissima, quae est in his *sacculis;* ideo subdit: *Thesaurum non deficientem*[^p323-6] *in caelis;* subintelligitur *facite,* et hoc fit per eleemosynam. Unde Tobiae duodecimo: « Bona est oratio cum ieiunio et eleemosyna magis, quam thesauros auri condere; quoniam eleemosyna a morte liberat et facit invenire vitam aeternam ». — Et quod iste thesaurus caelestis sit *indeficiens,* ostendit: quia non potest *amitti per fures,* non potest *corrumpi in se;* ideo addit: *Quo fur non appropiat, neque tinea corrumpit.* Chrysostomus[^p323-7]: « Omnia bona mundi triplex interitus tollit: aut enim ex se ipsis veterascunt, aut dominorum luxu consumuntur, aut ab extraneis dolo, vi, vel calumnia diripiuntur ». Et propterea in terris thesaurus indeficiens non potest haberi. Qui vult ergo indeficientem habere, dispergat in terris, ut abundet in caelis; Psalmus[^p323-8]: « Dispersit, dedit pauperibus, iustitia eius manet in saeculum saeculi ». Unde Augustinus: « Non iussit Dominus, ut thesaurum perderemus, sed locum ostendit, ubi poneremus ».

47. (Vers. 34.). Est autem magna vis facienda, ubi locetur thesaurus, quia et ibidem locatur animus; ideo subiungit: *Ubi enim thesaurus vester est, ibi et cor vestrum erit*[^p323-9]. *Thesaurus* est illud quod principaliter amat animus, secundum illud Matthaei decimo tertio: « Simile est regnum caelorum thesauro abscondito » etc. Ubi autem est principale amatum, ibi habitabit animus; unde Bernardus[^p323-10]: « Verius est anima ubi amat, quam ubi animat ». Et ideo ubi est thesaurus tuus, ibi et cor tuum. Beda[^p323-11]: « Si in terra est, cor deorsum est; si in caelestibus, in Christo fixum est; necesse est enim, ut quo praecesserit dilectionis thesaurus, illuc et cogitationis sequatur affectus ». Et quia sapiens thesaurum habet in caelis, et stultus in terris; ideo Ecclesiastae decimo[^p323-12]: « Cor sapientis in dextera eius, et cor stulti in sinistra illius » etc.; secundae ad Corinthios quarto: « Non contemplantibus nobis quae videntur, sed quae non videntur. Quae enim videntur, temporalia sunt, quae autem non videntur, aeterna ». Thesaurus enim iste *visibilis* et terrestris consistit in *pecunia;* sed thesaurus *invisibilis* consistit in *sapientia;* Sapientiae septimo[^p323-13]: « Venerunt mihi omnia bona pariter cum illa »; et post: « Infinitus est enim thesaurus hominibus, quo qui usi sunt, participes facti sunt amicitiae Dei ».

48. Et nota, quod iste thesaurus, qui consistit in sapientia, *incipit* a *timore reverentiae;* Isaiae trigesimo tertio[^p323-14]: « Divitiae salutis, sapientia et scientia, timor Domini ipse thesaurus eius ». *Proficit* autem in *studio disciplinae;* Matthaei decimo tertio: « Omnis scriba doctus in regno caelorum » etc. *Conservatur* in *sanctitate conscientiae;* Lucae sexto[^p323-15]: « Bonus homo de bono thesauro cordis » etc. *Consummatur* autem in *sublimitate gloriae;* Matthaei decimo nono: « Si vis perfectus esse, vade et vende omnia, quae habes, et habebis thesaurum in caelo ». — Et de talibus loquitur hic; unde pauperibus promittit *viaticum refectionis, regnum excellentiae* et
<!-- page 324 -->
*thesaurum abundantiae,* quia pauperes consueverunt esse afflicti et despecti et egeni pro nomine Domini nostri Iesu Christi.

#### Secondly, to guard against cupidity coming from timidity, he warns in two ways.

21. *And one said to him*[^p316-5] etc. After he dissuaded them from the guile coming from human timidity and from timidity itself, in this part he dissuades them from *cupidity.* And this part has two parts. In the first of them he *calls them back from the solicitude of avarice;* but in the second he *invites them to the solicitude of providence,* below in the same chapter[^p316-6]: *Let your loins be girt* etc.

#### They are called back from the solicitude of avarice in four ways.

Now he calls them back from the *solicitude* of *avarice* in four ways: first, namely, *by a rational instruction;* but secondly, *by a terrible example,* there[^p316-7]: *And he spoke a similitude to them* etc.; thirdly, *by an irrefragable argument,* there: *And he said to his disciples* etc.; fourthly, *by a desirable promise,* there: *And seek not you what you shall eat* etc.

Concerning the *rational instruction,* by which he calls them back from the solicitude of avarice, three things are introduced: the first is *the confutation of an avaricious petition*[^p316-8], the second is *the dissuasion from avarice,* the third is *the assigning of the cause.*

22. (Vers. 13.). First, then, as to *the confutation of an avaricious petition: And one of the multitude said to him: Master, speak to my brother that he divide the inheritance with me.* Truly this man was *of the multitude,* because he *was solicitous about the earth,* according to that of Isaias, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p316-9]: « Out of the earth thy speech shall mutter » etc. He was speaking against himself also, while he called him *Master,* because Christ did not profess himself a master in teaching temporal things to be acquired and divided, but rather to be forsaken; whence to the young man asking and saying[^p316-10]: « Good master, what shall I do to possess everlasting life »? he answered: « If thou wilt be perfect, go and sell » etc. Whence Christ did not teach how money might be increased, but how want might be kept. — He speaks against himself also, while he wishes *to be divided from his brother,* to whom he ought to be joined. Now this question came from private love, which divides things joined; which the devil most of all does, not the Lord; Osee, the thirteenth chapter[^p316-11]: « He shall divide between brothers ».

23. (Vers. 14.). And since, as Bede says in the Gloss[^p316-12], « Christ is not the God of dissension, but of peace and of unity »; therefore he confutes the aforesaid petition, when he subjoins: *But he said to him*[^p316-13], namely Jesus: *Man, who hath appointed me judge, or divider, over you?* a judge, namely, of lawsuits and a divider of possessions; the Gloss[^p316-14]: « He disdains to be the judge of lawsuits or the arbiter of possessions, who has the judgement of the living and the dead and the arbitration of merits ». It is true indeed, as is said in the Psalm[^p316-15], that « he is appointed king by him over Sion, his holy mountain »; and that « all things are set in his power »; but because God had sent him to communicate spiritual things: therefore he refused to descend to dividing temporal things, intimating in this that of the second to Timothy, the second chapter: « No man, being a soldier to God, entangleth himself with secular businesses ». — And note that Christ calls him *man,* animal and brutish, on account of *love in earthly things* and *the lawsuit of division.* Whence the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p316-16]: « Whereas there is among you envying and
<!-- page 317 -->
contention » etc.; and the Psalm: « O ye sons of men, how long will you be dull of heart »? Whence in this the confutation of an avaricious petition is intimated.

24. (Vers. 15.). Secondly, as to *the dissuasion from avarice,* he subjoins: *And he said to them: See and beware of all avarice. See,* namely, as men provident, and *beware,* as men wary; to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter[^p317-1]: « See, brethren, how you walk circumspectly: not as unwise, but as wise » etc. For *providence* makes one beware of the dangers into which one falls through avarice; the first to Timothy, the last chapter[^p317-2]: « They that will become rich, fall into temptation, and into the snare of the devil, and into many unprofitable and hurtful desires, which drown men into destruction and perdition. For the root of evils is cupidity » etc. Whence it blinds a man, which was signified in the blinding of Tobias through the dung of swallows, Tobias, the second chapter[^p317-3]. And therefore he says: *See,* namely eternal things, *and beware of avarice,* which seeks temporal things; the second to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « While we look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen. For the things which are seen, are temporal; but the things which are not seen, are eternal ». And since the appetite of any transitory good whatever, namely of money and of power and of honour, can be called *avarice;* therefore he says: *Beware of all avarice;* Isaias, the thirty-third chapter[^p317-4]: « Who can dwell with everlasting burnings »? and there follows: « He that casteth away avarice by oppression, and shaketh his hands from all bribes ».

25. Thirdly, as to *the assigning of the reason and the cause,* he subjoins: *For the life of any man is not in the abundance*[^p317-5] *of the things which he possesseth;* that is, the life of anyone is not *in abundance; abundance,* I say, *of the things which he possesseth,* that is, no one can live longer on account of an abundance of riches; the Psalm[^p317-6]: « They shall leave their riches to strangers: and their sepulchres shall be their houses for ever »; and Job, the twenty-seventh chapter: « The rich man when he shall sleep shall take away nothing with him » etc.; and the Psalm: « They have slept their sleep; and all the men of riches have found nothing in their hands ». Whence *abundance* does not prolong life, but frequently shortens it; Ecclesiasticus, the eighth chapter[^p317-7]: « Gold and silver
hath destroyed many »; and Acts, the eighth chapter: « Thy money be with thee unto perdition ». And for this reason it is to be lost, lest it destroy its possessor; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-ninth chapter: « Lose thy money for thy brother and thy friend: and hide it not under a stone to be lost ».

26. (Vers. 16.). *And he spoke a similitude to them, saying* etc. Here secondly he calls them back from the solicitude of avarice *by a terrible example*[^p317-8]; concerning which three things are introduced, namely the *occasion* of vain security, the *conception* of vain security and the *withdrawal* of vain security.

First, then, as to *the occasion of vain security* coming from the plenty of fruits, he says: *The land of a certain rich man brought forth plenty of fruits.* For these are wont to be the goods of the rich, from the plenty of temporal fruits; the Psalm[^p317-9]: « Their storehouses full, flowing out of this into that; their sheep » etc.; whence Job, the twenty-first chapter: « Why then do the wicked live, are they advanced, and strengthened with riches »? « Their houses are secure and peaceable, and the rod of God is not upon them ».

27. (Vers. 17.). And because riches make a man secure in such a way that they *render him solicitous;* therefore he subjoins: *And he thought within himself, saying: What shall I do, because I have no room where to bestow my fruits?* Ecclesiastes, the fourth chapter[^p317-10]: « There is another vanity, which I saw under the sun: there is but one, and he hath not a second, and yet he ceaseth not to labour, neither are his eyes satisfied with riches », nay rather they are certainly made solicitous; Ecclesiastes, the fifth chapter: « The fulness of the rich will not suffer him to sleep ».

28. (Vers. 18.). And the reason of this is that he is compelled *to think;* and therefore he subjoins: *And he said: This will I do: I will pull down my barns, and will build greater; and into them will I gather all things that are grown to me, and my goods.* The Gloss[^p317-11]: « Behold, great solicitude from avarice », because there is solicitude of destroying things made and of building new ones. For although the barns were full, cupidity, still empty, sought new receptacles; Ecclesiastes, the fifth chapter[^p317-12]: « A covetous man shall not be satisfied with money » etc.; and Ecclesiasticus, the fourteenth chapter: « The eye of the covetous man is insatiable: in his portion of iniquity he will not be satisfied ». The barn
<!-- page 318 -->
was filled, but the heart was empty: both because God alone can fill the soul capable of the Trinity[^p318-1]; and because temporal things do not enter the heart save according to a phantastic likeness; and because they increase concupiscence; and because they do not make the soul better; and also because they are altogether of another nature, as an angel and a bodily place, so the soul and a bodily treasure; and also because the soul, since it is great not in *bulk* but in *power,* is not filled by a material quantity, but by a virtual one, such as is the grace of the Holy Spirit, Wisdom, the first chapter, and Acts, the second chapter[^p318-2].

29. (Vers. 19.). But secondly, as to *the conception of vain security* through a stolid promise, there is subjoined: *And I will say to my soul,* that is, to the animal part; John, the twelfth chapter[^p318-3]: « He that loveth his soul shall lose it ». — *Soul, thou hast much goods laid up for many years;* the Apocalypse, the third chapter: « Thou sayest: I am rich, and made wealthy, and have need of nothing » etc.; and Zacharias, the eleventh chapter: « Blessed be the Lord, we are become rich ». — And because abundance with security brings forth the evil of wantonness, therefore he subjoins: *Take thy rest*[^p318-4]; behold, *negligence;* Ecclesiastes, the fourth chapter: « The fool foldeth his hands together, and saith: Better is a handful with rest, than both hands full with labour, and vexation of mind ». *Eat;* behold, *gluttony;* Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter[^p318-5]: « I have found me rest, and now I will eat of my goods alone ». *Drink,* as to *surfeiting;* against which Proverbs, the twenty-third chapter: « Be not in the feasts of great drinkers »; « because they that give themselves to drinking, and that club together shall be consumed ». *Make good cheer;* behold, *wantonness;* James, the fifth chapter[^p318-6]: « You have feasted upon earth: and in riotous living have you nourished your hearts ». For these are wont to be the vices of the rich; Amos, the sixth chapter: « Woe to you that are wealthy in Sion »; and after: « You that sleep upon beds of ivory, and are wanton on your couches; that eat the lamb out of the flock, drinking wine in bowls, and anointed with the best ointment ». Now the cause of this wantonness is vain security, which the soul foolishly conceives, either *by promising* itself a long temporal life through *presumption,* according to that of Isaias, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p318-7]: « We have entered into a league with death, and we have made a covenant with hell; the overflowing scourge, when it shall pass through, shall not come upon us »; or *by contemning* it through *despair;* Isaias, the twenty-second chapter: « Let us eat and drink; for to morrow we shall die ».

30. (Vers. 20.). Thirdly, as to *the withdrawal of vain security* through the equity of the divine sentence, there is subjoined: *But God said to him.* The Gloss[^p318-8]: « For God *to say* to a man is to restrain his crooked machinations by a sudden chastisement ». — For which reason he adds the sentence: *Thou fool, this night they shall require thy soul*[^p318-9] *of thee;* the first to the Thessalonians, the fifth chapter: « When they shall say, Peace and security; then shall sudden destruction come upon them ». He calls him *fool* for his earthly wisdom, because, in the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter, « God hath made foolish the wisdom of this world ». He is a fool who does not foresee future dangers; Ecclesiastes, the second chapter[^p318-10]: « The eyes of a wise man are in his head: the fool walketh in darkness »; because, uncertain of things to come, he sleeps secure; against which Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter: « Boast not for to morrow, not knowing what the day to come may bring forth »; and James, the fourth chapter: « What is our life? A vapour which appeareth for a little while ». — And because, when life is lost, these temporal things are lost, therefore he subjoins: *And whose shall those things be which thou hast provided?* Ambrose[^p318-11]: « They are not a man's goods, which he cannot carry away with him; mercy alone is the companion of the dead »; whence the first to Timothy, the last chapter: « We brought nothing into this world: and certainly we can carry nothing out »; and Job, the twentieth chapter: « When he hath what he coveted, he shall not be able to possess it; when he shall be filled, he shall be straitened, and every sorrow shall fall upon him ».

31. (Vers. 21.). And because this sentence is common to all the avaricious, therefore he subjoins: *So is every one*[^p318-12]*, that layeth up treasure for himself, and is not rich towards God. He layeth up treasure for himself* who multiplies treasures for himself on earth; the Psalm: « He storeth up: and he knoweth not for whom he shall gather these things »; but he *is rich towards God* who abounds
<!-- page 319 -->
in the merits and works of piety; the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p319-1]: « In all things you are made rich in him, so that nothing is wanting to you in any grace ». Therefore it is said in Matthew, the sixth chapter: « Lay not up to yourselves treasures on earth: where the rust, and moth consume ». « But lay up to yourselves treasures in heaven: where neither the rust nor moth doth consume, and where thieves do not break through, nor steal ». — Now the hope which we have in him makes us *to be rich towards God,* according to that of the first of Peter, the first chapter[^p319-2]: « He hath regenerated us unto a lively hope, unto an inheritance incorruptible, reserved in heaven ». And this hope is rooted in poverty; whence the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter: « As needy, yet enriching many; as having nothing, and possessing all things ». Such was Paul; whence to the Philippians, the last chapter[^p319-3]: « I have all, and abound ». Such Christ made us; whence the second to the Corinthians, the eighth chapter: « He became needy for you, that through his want you might be rich ». Therefore he says to such: « Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven ».

32. *And he said to his disciples* etc. Here now thirdly[^p319-4] he calls them back from the solicitude of avarice *by an irrefragable argument,* which is taken from a threefold kind of creature: first from the *rational* creature; secondly, from the *sensible,* there[^p319-5]: *Consider the ravens;* thirdly, from the *vegetable,* there: *Consider the lilies of the field.*

First, then, from the *rational* creature he takes such an argument: he who gives what is greater will give what is less; but the soul is more than meat, and the body more than raiment: therefore he who gave the soul and the body will give food and clothing: therefore one ought not to be solicitous about these. In which reasoning he first sets down *the principal conclusion,* then subjoins *the proof.*

33. (Vers. 22.). He sets down, then, first *the principal conclusion,* when he says: *Therefore I say to you, be not solicitous for your soul, what you shall eat; nor for your body*[^p319-6]*, what you shall put on,* that is, be not made solicitous about food and clothing, which are most necessary to life, much less about other things,
which regard the present life; to the Philippians, the fourth chapter: « Be nothing solicitous; but in every prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your petitions be made known to God ». — And note that he does not forbid the solicitude which proceeds from *providence of mind,* but that which comes from *distrust of God,* as if God had no care of us; against which the first of Peter, the last chapter[^p319-7]: « Casting all your solicitude upon him, for he hath care of you »; and in the Psalm: « Cast thy care upon the Lord » etc.; and Ambrose: « It is unbecoming for a man who soldiers for the kingdom to care about food or clothing; for the king knows how to feed, nourish and clothe his household ».

34. (Vers. 23.). Then he adds *the proof,* when he says: *The soul is more than the meat, and the body is more than the raiment;* from which it follows: he who gave what is more will supply also what is less. Ambrose[^p319-8]: « It is absurd that we should think an abundance of food will fail us, who obtain from God the perpetual substance of living ». For the soul and the body are the constitutive parts of man, for whose sake the other creatures were made, according to that of Genesis, the first chapter[^p319-9]: « Let us make man to our image and likeness: and let him have dominion over the fowls of the air » etc. Whence Chrysostom[^p319-10]: « God made all things for the sake of man, but man for his own sake; God made all things in wisdom, but man not only in wisdom, but also according to his wisdom ». Whence Wisdom, the sixth chapter: « He made the little and the great, and he hath equally care of all ». And Augustine, *On the Christian Combat*[^p319-11]: « God cares for rational souls and the angels through himself, but governs the rest through them ». — And note that he rightly compares the soul and prefers it to meat, and the body to raiment, because the soul gives vigour to the body by vegetating it, and the body is as it were the garment of the soul, which appears outwardly. If, then, God provides the bodily vigour of the vegetating soul, much more the sufficiency of sustaining nourishment. Again, if he provides for the soul the garment of the body, much more the covering of the outer vesture[^p319-12].
<!-- page 320 -->

35. (Vers. 24.). *Consider the ravens.* Here he takes an argument from the *sensible creature;* and first he *sets down an example,* then draws out *the argument.*

He proposes, then, an *example* to be considered, when he says: *Consider the ravens, for they sow not, neither do they reap;* behold, that they have no exercise of labour; *which have no storehouse,* as to laying up wine; *nor barn,* as to storing corn; behold, that they have no repository. *And God feedeth them;* behold, that they suffer no want; the Psalm[^p320-1]: « Who giveth to beasts their food: and to the young ravens that call upon him ». — And note that he proposes the example of ravens rather than of other birds, and this both because they are of the greatest voracity; whence it is said in Genesis, the eighth chapter[^p320-2], that « Noe sent forth a raven through the window of the ark, which went forth and did not return »; and the reason of this is that it had settled[^tr-corrigenda] upon a carcass; and also because this is said to be the nature of the raven, that it does not feed its young at the beginning, because it does not think them its own until it sees that they are black; Job, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p320-3]: « Who provideth food for the raven, when her young ones cry to God, wandering about, because they have no meat »? Whence by *ravens* other birds can fittingly be understood; Matthew, the sixth chapter[^p320-4]: « Behold the birds of the air, for they neither sow » etc.

36. Then he *draws out the argument,* when he adds: *How much are you more valuable than they?* The Gloss[^p320-5]: « On account of reason and the immortality which is promised you, you are worth more »; the first to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter: « Doth God take care for oxen? For these things are written for our sakes ». — But because someone could argue against this, that solicitude befits us more than ravens on account of the excellence of reason; he shows that this is nothing. For if the *growth* of the body does not come about through human thought, but through divine disposition, by like reason neither does *nourishment.*

37. (Vers. 25.). For which reason he adds: *And which of you, by taking thought*[^p320-6]*, can add to his stature one cubit?* as if to say: no one, because this is not the work of man thinking, but of God producing; whence it is said in the second of Machabees, the seventh chapter[^p320-7]: « I know not how you appeared in my womb: neither did I give you life, and the limbs of every one of you I myself did not frame »; and the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter: « God giveth it a body as he will: and to every seed its proper body ». Whence the Philosopher too[^p320-8] says that « of all things constituted by nature there is a limit and a ratio of magnitude and of growth ». If, then, these least things are not established in human but in divine providence, much less are the others.

38. (Vers. 26.). Therefore he adds: *If then ye be not able to do so much as the least thing, why are you solicitous for the rest?* as if to say: in vain and foolishly. Bede[^p320-9]: « Leave the covering of the body to him who made it reach this measure ». — Yet this reason does not seem to hold, because, although we ought not to be made solicitous about *growth,* yet we can and ought to be about *nutriment.* For although the *augmentative* power is not subject to will and reason, yet nothing prevents the *nutritive* from needing government and providence.

Yet to this the answer is that the reason is good: because, if man ought not to rule and care for[^p320-10] those things which are committed to *the providence of nature;* by like reason neither those which are committed to *the providence on high.* — Again, as man cannot increase his body, so neither can he prolong the term of life which the Lord has fixed. — Lastly, if
<!-- page 321 -->
one need not be made solicitous about the very measure of the body, but man is content with that which the Lord has given; why not likewise about outward nourishment?

39. (Vers. 27.). *Consider the lilies* etc. Here now thirdly he *takes an argument from the vegetable.* And first he sets down *a sensible similitude,* then subjoins *the adaptation.*

He sets down, then, *the similitude,* when he says: *Consider the lilies of the field*[^p321-1]*, how they grow: they labour not, neither do they spin.* Ambrose: « An open comparison; lilies do not, like other fruits, require any tillage of husbandmen »; whence they grow not by human study, but by the divine command, which was given from the very beginning of the world; Genesis, the first chapter[^p321-2]: « Let the earth bring forth the green herb » etc. — And because the work of the highest Artificer is more excellent than every work of human art, therefore he adds: *But I say to you, that*[^p321-3] *not even Solomon in all his glory was clothed like one of these;* and yet Solomon was most careful about the adornment of garments, having at once the will, the industry and the means to adorn himself. Whence it is said of him in the third of Kings, the tenth chapter[^p321-4]: « The queen of Saba, seeing all the wisdom of Solomon and the apartments of his servants and the orders of his ministers and their apparel, had no longer any spirit »; and this because he had ordered all things most skilfully according to human industry; but yet he could not be equalled to the works and the industry of nature, which is the work of God. Whence Bede[^p321-5]: « What purple of kings, what painting of weavers can be compared to the flowers »?

40. (Vers. 28.). Then after the similitude he subjoins *the adaptation* and infers *the conclusion: Now if the grass, which is to day in the field, and to morrow is cast into the oven,* that is, quickly withers; the Psalm[^p321-6]: « Let them be as grass upon the tops of houses: which withereth before it be plucked up »; and James, the first chapter: « The sun rose with a burning heat, and parched the grass, and the flower thereof fell off, and the beauty of the shape thereof perished »; *God so clothe,* namely with fair adornment and comeliness: « The colour itself is called the garment of the flower[^p321-7] ». — *How much more you, O ye of little faith?* that is, how much more will he clothe you, even if you have little of faith, because you excel both by reason of nature and by reason of faith. Whence to Peter, doubting of the omnipotence in the storm, it is said in Matthew, the fourteenth chapter[^p321-8]: « O thou of little faith, why didst thou doubt »? He has *little faith* concerning the government of the fatherly providence who fears daily to die of hunger; the Psalm[^p321-9]: « They believed not in God: and trusted not in his salvation »; and there follows: « And he had commanded the clouds from above, and had opened the doors of heaven, and had rained down manna upon them to eat » etc. If, then, anyone is of true faith, believing that all things are governed by the providence on high; since the providence of God takes and ought to take greater care of the more precious, the worthier and the more durable things, of rational than of irrational or of vegetable things; and of these it takes such providence that nothing is wanting to them: much less, then, is there to be doubt[^p321-10] about the things which regard the government of man, as of the more precious, the better and the worthier among the other creatures, because he is rational and made to the image of God. This reason, then, is irrefragable, because, if anyone say the contrary of it, he destroys the government of the divine providence and the order of the universe. And therefore he is not only of little faith, but also of a perverse judgement[^p321-11].

41. (Vers. 29.). *And seek not you what you shall eat.* Here fourthly he calls them back from the solicitude of avarice by *a desirable promise*[^p321-12]; concerning which three things are introduced: for he dissuades them from the solicitude of avarice and of cupidity by promising *the sufficiency of provision for the way, the surpassing excellence of the reward* and *the superabundance of the treasure.*

First, then, he dissuades them from the solicitude of avarice and of cupidity *by promising the sufficiency of provision for the way,* when he says: *And seek not you what you shall eat, or what you shall drink,* namely as the cupidinous and the curious seek, that they may abound and be proud. — Therefore he adds: *And be not lifted up on high,* for the acquiring of earthly things; the first to Timothy, the last chapter[^p321-13]: « Charge the rich of this world
<!-- page 322 -->
not to be highminded, nor to trust in the uncertainty of riches ». For so to seek earthly things belongs not to heavenly men, but to earthly.

42. (Vers. 30.). Therefore he adds: *For all these things do the nations of the world seek,* because they have a worldly wisdom; Baruch, the third chapter[^p322-1]: « The children of Agar, that sought after the wisdom that is of the earth, the merchants of Merrha, and of Theman ». But heavenly men ought not to be intent upon these things, because they are of the household of the highest Father. — Whence he adds: *But your Father knoweth that*[^p322-2] *you have need of these things;* and there is no doubt that he *can* provide; to the Romans, the tenth chapter: « The same is Lord over all, rich unto all that call upon him ». There is no doubt also that he *wills* to; Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter: « Can a woman forget her infant, so as not to have pity on the son of her womb » etc. And therefore those who wish to make for the fatherland will not be forsaken on the way for want of food.

43. (Vers. 31.). For which reason he adds: *Yet seek ye first the kingdom of God*[^p322-3], as the principal intent; *and all these things shall be added unto you,* as provision for the way, because he who is ready to give *the kingdom* will without doubt not deny *food,* he who is ready to give eternal things will not deny temporal things. Whence Augustine[^p322-4]: « The Lord shows that temporal things are not to be sought as our goods, even if necessary. But the kingdom of God is to be sought, and in it the end is to be set, for the sake of which we do all things ». Therefore our solicitude ought not to be for acquiring food, but for acquiring the eternal kingdom; to the Romans, the fourteenth chapter[^p322-5]: « The kingdom of God is not meat and drink; but justice, and peace, and joy in the Holy Spirit ». Chrysostom[^p322-6]: « The kingdom of God is *the retribution;* justice is *the way* by which we come to the kingdom ». Therefore Matthew, the sixth chapter: « Seek ye first the kingdom of God *and his justice* » etc. — And note that this promise is quite right, because he who seeks *the kingdom and his justice* is God's *servant,* God's *friend* and God's *son;* to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p322-7]: « Whosoever are led by the Spirit of God, they are the sons of God »; but that God should fail his servant, his friend and his son, so as not to provide for him the things that are necessary; this is a very perverse thing to think. For such a one has God, such a one is had by God, and therefore he has through
consequence the things which God has; whence the Apostle in the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p322-8]: « All are yours; and you are Christ's; and Christ is God's ».

44. (Vers. 32.). Secondly, he dissuades them from the solicitude of avarice *by promising the surpassing excellence of the reward,* when he subjoins: *Fear not, little flock; little flock* is said with respect to *the multitude of the reprobate;* Matthew, the twentieth chapter[^p322-9]: « Many are called, but few chosen ». Or *little* by reason of *their own littleness;* the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p322-10]: « See your vocation, brethren, that there are not many wise according to the flesh, not many mighty » etc. Or *little* by reason of *voluntary humility;* Ezechiel, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p322-11]: « And you my flocks are men, and I am your God ». For God is the God of the humble; Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter: « Great is the power of God alone, and he is honoured by the humble ». And to such God promises the kingdom, Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « Suffer the little children to come to me: for the kingdom of heaven is for such ». — Therefore he subjoins: *For it hath pleased your Father*[^p322-12] *to give you a kingdom;* Proverbs, the twenty-ninth chapter: « Glory shall uphold the humble of spirit »; and Job, the twenty-second chapter: « He that hath been humbled, shall be in glory ». Now this surpassing excellence of the kingdom promised induces *hope,* and by inducing hope it induces *security,* and through this takes away *the faintheartedness of fear* and *the ardour of cupidity;* the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter[^p322-13]: « As needy, yet enriching many; as having nothing, and possessing all things ». For that kingdom alone is the true possession of the heart, which fills the heart and cannot be taken away, because it is within; below in the seventeenth chapter: « Behold, the kingdom of God is within you ».

45. And note that it pleased the Father *to give the kingdom of glory* to the little ones, that is, to the poor in spirit; whence Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p322-14]: « Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven »; because such men *desire eternal things;* Proverbs, the tenth chapter: « To the just their desire shall be given »; and the Psalm: « The Lord hath heard the desire of the poor »; because they *contemn temporal things;* Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p322-15]: « He that hath left father or mother shall receive an hundredfold » etc.; they *embrace spiritual things;* to the Galatians, the fifth chapter: « If we live in the Spirit, let us also walk in the Spirit. Let us not be made
<!-- page 323 -->
desirous of vainglory ». — Likewise it pleased him *to give them pardon;* Judith, the ninth chapter[^p323-1]: « The prayer of the humble and the meek hath always pleased thee », namely for giving pardon; Exodus, the thirty-third chapter: « I have mercy on whom I will have mercy ». — It pleased him *to give grace;* Isaias, the forty-second chapter[^p323-2]: « Behold my servant, I will uphold him: my elect » etc. — It pleased him *to give wisdom;* Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « Thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them to little ones ». — It pleased him *to give eternal glory,* as here: *Fear not, little* etc.; the Psalm[^p323-3]: « The Lord taketh pleasure in them that fear him: and in them that hope in his mercy ».

46. (Vers. 33.). Thirdly, he dissuades them from the solicitude of avarice *by promising the superabundance of the treasure* in return for the leaving of the world, when he says: *Sell what you possess and give alms,* that is, distribute your goods to the poor, according to that of Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p323-4]: « Go and sell all that thou hast, and give to the poor ». — And because it is hard to sell and give without recompense, therefore he adds: *Make to yourselves bags which grow not old;* Ecclesiasticus, the seventeenth chapter[^p323-5]: « The alms of a man is as a bag with him, and shall preserve the grace of a man as the apple of the eye: and afterward he shall rise up, and shall render them their reward, to every one upon their own head ». — And because this retribution which is in these *bags* is most opulent; therefore he subjoins: *A treasure which faileth not*[^p323-6] *in heaven;* *make* is understood, and this is done through alms. Whence Tobias, the twelfth chapter: « Prayer is good with fasting and alms, more than to lay up treasures of gold; for alms delivereth from death, and maketh to find life everlasting ». — And that this heavenly treasure is *unfailing,* he shows: because it cannot *be lost through thieves,* it cannot *be corrupted in itself;* therefore he adds: *Where no thief approacheth, nor moth corrupteth.* Chrysostom[^p323-7]: « A threefold destruction takes away all the goods of the world: for either they grow old of themselves, or they are consumed by the luxury of their owners, or they are plundered by strangers by guile, force, or calumny ». And therefore an unfailing treasure cannot be had on earth. Let him, then, who wishes to have an unfailing one scatter on earth, that he may abound in heaven; the Psalm[^p323-8]: « He hath distributed, he hath given to the poor: his justice remaineth for ever and ever ». Whence Augustine: « The Lord did not command that we should lose our treasure, but he showed the place where we should put it ».

47. (Vers. 34.). Now great effort must be made as to where the treasure is placed, because in the same place the mind too is placed; therefore he subjoins: *For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also*[^p323-9]. *The treasure* is that which the mind principally loves, according to that of Matthew, the thirteenth chapter: « The kingdom of heaven is like unto a treasure hidden » etc. And where the principal thing loved is, there the mind will dwell; whence Bernard[^p323-10]: « The soul is more truly where it loves than where it animates ». And therefore where thy treasure is, there also is thy heart. Bede[^p323-11]: « If it is on earth, the heart is below; if in heavenly places, it is fixed in Christ; for it is necessary that whither the treasure of dilection has gone before, thither the affection of thought should follow ». And because the wise man has his treasure in heaven, and the fool on earth; therefore Ecclesiastes, the tenth chapter[^p323-12]: « The heart of a wise man is in his right hand, and the heart of a fool is in his left » etc.; the second to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « While we look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen. For the things which are seen, are temporal; but the things which are not seen, are eternal ». For this *visible* and earthly treasure consists in *money;* but the *invisible* treasure consists in *wisdom;* Wisdom, the seventh chapter[^p323-13]: « Now all good things came to me together with her »; and after: « For she is an infinite treasure to men! which they that use, become the friends of God ».

48. And note that this treasure, which consists in wisdom, *begins* from *the fear of reverence;* Isaias, the thirty-third chapter[^p323-14]: « Riches of salvation, wisdom and knowledge: the fear of the Lord is his treasure ». But it *makes progress* in *the study of discipline;* Matthew, the thirteenth chapter: « Every scribe instructed in the kingdom of heaven » etc. It *is preserved* in *the sanctity of conscience;* Luke, the sixth chapter[^p323-15]: « A good man out of the good treasure of his heart » etc. But it *is consummated* in *the sublimity of glory;* Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « If thou wilt be perfect, go and sell all that thou hast, and thou shalt have treasure in heaven ». — And he speaks here of such men; whence he promises to the poor *the provision of refreshment for the way, the kingdom of excellence* and
<!-- page 324 -->
*the treasure of abundance,* because the poor are wont to be afflicted and despised and needy for the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.
