# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XI, Vers. 42-54
## *Sed vae vobis Pharisaeis etc. — Post increpationem duplicitatis subditur hic duplicibus et hypocritis comminatio calamitatis; unde Matthaei vigesimo tertio quasi superadditur ista determinatio: « Vae vobis, scribae et Pharisaei, hypocritae »!*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 304 -->
85. *Sed vae vobis Pharisaeis* etc. — Post increpationem duplicitatis subditur hic duplicibus et hypocritis *comminatio calamitatis;* unde Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p304-13] quasi superadditur ista determinatio: « Vae vobis, scribae et Pharisaei, hypocritae »! Et quoniam haec duo genera personarum, scilicet Pharisaeorum et scribarum, simulabant maxime religionem; ideo contra has duas personas facit suam invectionem, sed in Matthaeo simul, hic autem distincte. Ideo pars ista habet duas. In prima comminatur *vae* ipsis *Pharisaeis;* in secunda vero, *scribis* sive legisperitis, ibi[^p304-14]: *Respondens autem quidam ex legisperitis* etc.

*Comminatio* autem damnationis aeternae tertio replicatur contra *Pharisaeos* propter triplicem causam, scilicet propter *negligentiam,* propter *arrogantiam* et propter *fraudulentiam,* ut sit *negligentia* in omissione boni, *arrogantia* in commissione mali, *fraudulentia* in occultatione mali et ostentatione boni.

86. (Vers. 42.). Primo igitur comminatur *Pharisaeis* poenam aeternam propter *negligentiam,* qua
<!-- page 305 -->
minimis intenti, omittebant maxima. Et hoc, cum dicit: *Sed vae vobis Pharisaeis! qui*[^p305-1] *decimatis mentham et rutam et omne olus,* quasi multum sitis intenti ad impletionem divinorum mandatorum; unde infra decimo octavo se iactabat Pharisaeus: « Decimas do omnium, quae possideo ». Sed tamen per hoc non iustificabantur propter omissionem optimorum; unde addit: *Et praeteritis omne*[^p305-2] *iudicium et caritatem Dei,* id est iustitiam et misericordiam. Et hoc est quod maxime requirit Deus, secundum illud Michaeae sexto: « Indicabo tibi, o homo, quidquid sit bonum, et Dominus requirat a te; utique facere iudicium et diligere misericordiam ». Et hanc vocat *caritatem;* Zachariae septimo: « Iudicium verum iudicate et misericordiam et miserationes facite, unusquisque cum proximo suo »; ita quod sit *iudicium* respectu sui, et *caritas* sive misericordia respectu proximi. — Vel, per *iudicium* intelligatur divinum *praeceptum,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p305-3]: « Iudicia Domini vera, iustificata in semetipsa » etc.; *caritas* autem dicitur *praecepti finis* et complementum; primae ad Timotheum primo: « Finis praecepti est caritas » etc. — Vel, per *iudicium* intelligantur *praecepta iudicialia,* secundum illud Exodi vigesimo primo[^p305-4]: « Haec sunt iudicia, quae propones eis: Si emeris servum » etc.; per *caritatem, moralia;* unde ad Romanos decimo tertio: « Qui diligit proximum Legem implevit. Nam: Non occides » etc. Arguit igitur eos, quia propter *caeremonialia* dimittebant *iudicialia* et *moralia,* quae magis erant sollicite facienda. — Et propterea addit: *Haec autem oportuit facere,* scilicet iudicium et caritatem primo et principaliter; unde Osee sexto[^p305-5]: « Misericordiam volo et non sacrificium, et scientiam Dei plus quam holocaustum ». — *Et illa non omittere,* quia, Ecclesiastici decimo nono, « qui spernit modica paulatim decidet »; et Ecclesiastae septimo: « Qui timet Deum nihil negligit ».

87. (Vers. 43.). Secundo hanc *comminationem iterat* propter *arrogantiam,* cum subdit: *Vae vobis Pharisaeis! quia*[^p305-6] *diligitis primas cathedras in synagogis,* quantum ad *ambitionem dignitatum.* Unde Glossa: « Arrogantiam redarguit, quia prima-
tus appetebant ». Haec autem non est appetenda; unde Ecclesiastici septimo[^p305-7]: « Noli quaerere ab homine ducatum neque a rege cathedram honoris ». Nam talis cathedra non est verae *excellentiae,* sed magis *pestilentiae,* secundum illud Psalmi: « Beatus vir, qui in cathedra pestilentiae non sedit »; super quam tamen sedebant scribae et Pharisaei; verumtamen ut indigni, quia verba habebant et non facta idonea. Unde dicitur Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p305-8]: « Super cathedram Moysi sederunt scribae et Pharisaei; quae dicunt facite, secundum opera eorum nolite facere ». — Et quia ad ambitionem dignitatis sequitur *appetitus laudis,* propterea subdit: *Et salutationes in foro,* id est laudes in conspectu multorum, ut ceteris honorabiliores viderentur; unde Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p305-9]: « Amant salutationes in foro et vocari ab hominibus *rabbi* ». Unde hic arguuntur, quia vanam gloriam quaerebant, propter quam etiam veritatem cognitam confiteri non audebant, secundum illud Ioannis duodecimo: « Ex principibus autem multi crediderunt in eum, sed propter Pharisaeos non confitebantur, ut de synagoga non eiicerentur. Dilexerunt enim magis gloriam hominum quam gloriam Dei ». — Ex quo apparet, quod laudes et honores secundum *divinam formam* omnino debemus contemnere, non secundum *Pharisaeos* appetere. Qui enim haec amat, ut dicit Hieronymus[^p305-10], non est Christi discipulus, sed scriba et Pharisaeus. Christus enim, cum petebatur ad regnum, « fugit in montem », Ioannis sexto; cum laudaretur, secedebat in desertum ad orandum, supra quinto.

88. (Vers. 44.). Tertio hanc *comminationem replicat* propter *fraudulentiam,* cum subdit: *Vae vobis! qui*[^p305-11] *estis ut monumenta, quae non apparent,* scilicet qualia sunt intus, quia foetida et immunda sunt intus, sed extra sunt pulcra; unde addit: *Et homines ambulantes supra nesciunt.* Tales sunt hypocritae, qui cum intra sint mali, extra videntur perfecti; unde merito dicuntur *sepulcra,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p305-12]: « Sepulcrum patens est guttur eorum, linguis suis » etc. Et ideo non sunt *filii Sponsi,* immo omnino oppositi. Nam Canticorum
<!-- page 306 -->
primo: « Nigra sum, sed formosa », isti autem albi sunt, sed foetidi; ideo merito dicuntur *monumenta;* unde Matthaei vigesimo tertio: « Vae vobis, scribae et Pharisaei hypocritae! quia similes estis sepulcris dealbatis, quae a foris parent hominibus speciosa, intus autem plena sunt ossibus mortuorum et omni spurcitia ». Unde Chrysostomus[^p306-1]: « Iustorum corpora *templa* dicuntur, corpora vero peccatorum *sepulcra,* quia intus est anima mortua; nec enim viva putanda est quae nihil vivum, id est spirituale, egit in corpore ». Et post: « Dic, hypocrita, si bonum est bonum esse, cur non vis *esse* quod vis *apparere?* Quod autem turpe est *apparere* turpius est *esse.* Aut ergo esto quod appares, aut appare quod es ».

Ex his Domini verbis potest colligi, qui sint[^p306-2] illi qui debent *hypocritae* iudicari, scilicet in quibus regnat *negligentia, arrogantia, fraudulentia.* Hi autem sunt qui minima praeferunt maximis, transitoria aeternis, apparentia veris. De his autem iudicare non est hominis cuiuslibet, sed Christi, qui cor aspicit, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios quarto[^p306-3]: « Nolite ante tempus iudicare, quousque veniat Dominus, qui illuminabit abscondita tenebrarum » etc. Nam Dominus non reprehendit bonitatem apparentem et evidentem, sed sophisticam et non existentem.

89. *Respondens autem quidam ex legisperitis*[^p306-4] etc. Postquam comminatus est vae Pharisaeis, hic secundo comminatur *legisperitis,* et hoc tribus de causis: primo, propter *desidiam* contra veritatem vitae; secundo, propter *perfidiam* contra veritatem iustitiae, ibi[^p306-5]: *Vae vobis! qui aedificatis monumenta;* tertio, propter *fallaciam* contra veritatem doctrinae, ibi: *Vae vobis legisperitis! qui tulistis clavem* etc. Haec enim triplex veritas maxime debet esse in doctoribus Legis, sed in legisperitis nihil erat nisi hypocrisis.

In comminando igitur gehennam propter *desidiam* primo praemittitur *ostentatio auctoritatis,* secundo adiungitur *comminatio calamitatis.*

90. (Vers. 45.). Primo igitur quantum ad *ostentationem auctoritatis* ex parte legisperiti dicitur: *Respondens autem quidam*[^p306-6] *ex legisperitis,* qui scilicet sibi peritus esse videbatur, secundum illud Isaiae quinto: « Vae! qui estis sapientes in oculis vestris et coram vobismetipsis prudentes ». Et quia tales videntur sibi docti et magni, ideo contumelio-
sum putant ab aliquo argui. Propter quod addit: *Ait illi: Magister, haec dicens etiam nobis contumeliam facis;* quod non diceret, nisi ex conscientia sentiret, se similiter transgredi, et ex superbia nollet reprehendi; unde poterat sibi dici illud ad Romanos secundo[^p306-7]: « Confidis, te ipsum esse ducem caecorum, lumen eorum qui in tenebris sunt, eruditorem insipientium, magistrum infantium ». « Qui ergo alium doces te ipsum non doces ». Doctrinam sanam putant *contumeliam,* quia, ut dicitur Proverbiorum decimo octavo[^p306-8], « non recipit stultus verba prudentiae, nisi ea dixeris, quae versantur in corde suo ». Et vere talis erat stultus, qui reputabat se eruditum, quoniam Proverbiorum vigesimo sexto: « Vidisti hominem sapientem sibi videri? Magis illo spem habebit stultus ».

91. (Vers. 46.). Secundo vero quantum ad *comminationem calamitatis* propter desidiam bene agendi dicitur: *At ille ait: Et vobis legisperitis vae!* scilicet damnationis aeternae. Et ratio huius est, quia multa dicebant et pauca faciebant. — Ideo subdit: *Quia oneratis homines oneribus, quae portare non possunt,* id est praeceptis et traditionibus superstitiosis et inutilibus, ut merito dicatur illud Actuum decimo quinto[^p306-9]: Hoc est onus, « quod neque nos neque patres nostri portare potuimus ». Unde Matthaei vigesimo tertio: « Alligant onera gravia et importabilia et imponunt in humeros hominum »; ad quae scilicet aliquis non tenetur, et ipsi dicunt teneri. — Et quia haec onera imponebant, *docebant*[^p306-10], sed non *portabant* faciendo; addit: *Et ipsi uno digito vestro non tangitis sarcinas.*

92. Nota signanter quod dicit: *Uno digito non tangitis;* non dicit: *non portatis,* quia posset esse excusatio de impotentia. Frequenter doctor docet quod facere *non potest;* verumtamen non est doctrina eius contemnenda, si facere *vult,* quia voluntas ei computatur pro facto. Unde primi Regum trigesimo[^p306-11]: « Aequa pars erit descendentis ad proelium et remanentis ad sarcinas, et similiter divident »; et hoc dicitur pro viris, qui lassi substiterant; sed qui non vult digitum ad sarcinas apponere, manifestum, quod nihil laboris vult sustinere. Et talis, sive imponat per modum magistri, sive per modum praelati, omnino est reprehensibilis nec est imitator Christi, de quo dicitur Actuum primo[^p306-12]: « Coepit Iesus facere et docere ».
<!-- page 307 -->
Unde contra istos Deuteronomii vigesimo quinto: « Non habebis in sacculo diversa pondera »; et Proverbiorum vigesimo: « Pondus et pondus, mensura et mensura; utrumque abominabile est apud Deum ». Hoc autem est, cum homo sibi est misericors et proximo vel subdito durus; cum nihil portat, sed totum aliis portandum imponit. Unde Chrysostomus[^p307-1]: « Vis apparere et esse sanctus? Circa vitam tuam esto austerus, circa aliorum benignus. Audiant te homines parva mandantem et gravia facientem ».

93. (Vers. 47.). *Vae vobis! qui aedificatis* etc. Postquam comminatus est eis *vae* pro culpa desidiae ex defectu veritatis vitae, hic secundo replicat idem *pro culpa perfidiae contra veritatem iustitiae,* quae quidem erat in impia iustorum persecutione. Circa quam primo *exaggeratur ipsa culpa,* deinde *aggravatur vindicta.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *exaggerationem culpae* dicit: *Vae vobis! qui aedificatis monumenta Prophetarum,* praetendentes pietatem in facto, sed gerentes impietatem in animo, sicut et patres eorum. — Propter quod subdit: *Patres autem vestri occiderunt eos*[^p307-2], scilicet tanquam impii iustos. Unde Actuum septimo dixit beatus Stephanus: « Quem Prophetarum non sunt persecuti patres vestri? Et occiderunt eos qui prophetabant de adventu iusti ». Sed et isti, licet aliud simularent, tamen erant imitatores eorum.

94. (Vers. 48.). Propter quod ait: *Sed profecto testificamini, quod consentitis operibus patrum vestrorum,* id est, consentitis, haec fuisse opera patrum vestrorum; unde Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p307-3]: « Testimonio estis vobismetipsis, quod estis filii eorum qui occiderunt Prophetas ». — Ideo addit: *Quoniam ipsi quidem eos occiderunt, vos autem aedificatis eorum sepulcra;* et sic signo et verbo testes erant, quod patres eorum ipsos occiderant. Et hoc quidem *simulabant* se verbo et signo reprobare; unde Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p307-4]: « Ornatis monumenta iustorum et dicitis: Si fuissemus in diebus patrum nostrorum, non fuissemus socii eorum »; sed *approbabant* consimili facto et voto, quod ipsi, ut patres
eorum, erant persecutores bonorum; et ex hoc testes erant, se consentire operibus patrum. Unde Hieronymus[^p307-5]: « Contra vosmetipsos testes estis; opere enim vos esse imitatores patrum testificamini, quorum facinora detestamini verbo ». Unde Actuum septimo[^p307-6]: « Dura cervice et incircumcisis cordibus et auribus, vos semper Spiritui sancto restitistis, sicut patres vestri, ita et vos ».

95. (Vers. 49.). Et quia malitia, quam gerebant in corde, permissione Dei propalanda erat in opere, ideo subdit: *Propterea et sapientia Dei dixit,* « id est, ab aeterno disposuit[^p307-7] », vel ipse Christus disposuit vel praevidit, qui est « Dei virtus et Dei sapientia ». Vel *dixit,* id est, praedixit voce prophetica. — *Mittam ad illos Prophetas et Apostolos, et ex illis occident et persequentur,* secundum illud Ieremiae septimo[^p307-8]: « Misi ad vos omnes servos meos Prophetas per diem, consurgens diluculo et mittens; non audierunt me, sed nec inclinaverunt aurem suam et obduraverunt suam cervicem et peius operati sunt quam patres eorum ». Hoc verificatum est in istis, qui non tantum Prophetas, sed etiam *Dominum proprium interfecerunt.* Unde Actuum tertio[^p307-9] dicit eis Petrus: « Vos autem iustum et sanctum negastis et petistis, virum homicidam donari vobis; auctorem vero vitae interemistis ». Occiderunt etiam et persecuti sunt omnes *testes ipsius.* Nam Iacobus occisus est, et Petrus comprehensus, Actuum duodecimo[^p307-10]. Persecuti sunt etiam ipsi Paulum et Barnabam, ut transmigrarent ad gentes, Actuum decimo tertio et decimo quarto; et omnes etiam discipuli flagellati, Actuum quinto.

96. (Vers. 50.). Secundo vero quantum ad *aggravationem vindictae* dicit: *Ut inquiratur sanguis omnium Prophetarum. Inquiritur* namque per vindictam; unde in Psalmo[^p307-11]: « Quoniam requirens sanguinem eorum, recordatus est » etc.; et Genesis nono: « De manu viri et fratris eius requiram animam hominis. Quicumque effuderit humanum sanguinem, fundetur sanguis illius ». — *Inquiretur* igitur *sanguis,* id est vindicta effusionis sanguinis; propter
<!-- page 308 -->
quod addit: *Qui effusus est a constitutione mundi,* id est ab initio usque in finem. *Inquiretur,* inquam, *a generatione ista,* quasi dicat, quod vindicta omnis sanguinis sancti viri effusi erit super generationem Iudaeorum, quia ipsi effuderunt sanguinem illius qui erat « Sanctus Sanctorum », scilicet Christus, Danielis nono[^p308-1]; et post: « Occidetur Christus, et non erit eius populus, qui eum negaturus est ». Unde ipsi dixerunt Ioannis decimo nono: « Non habemus regem nisi caesarem »; et Matthaei vigesimo septimo: « Sanguis eius super nos et super filios nostros ». — Vel hoc dicitur, ut ipsi puniantur in exemplum aliorum, quia, cum viderent, malum in aliis puniri, se non correxerunt. Unde Chrysostomus[^p308-2]: « Quid aliorum peccata vindicat Deus in istis, si soli sunt passi talia, cum non soli peccassent? Sicut, multis servis peccantibus, castigatur unus pro suo peccato, et tamen pro omnibus flagellatus pertulit solus, quod omnibus est promissum ». Ratio autem, quare magis ad istos poena pervenit, redditur ab eodem Ioanne Chrysostomo[^p308-3]: « Quia, inquit, vos audistis de Cain et de ceteris malis vindictam factam, et si non estis per eorum exempla correcti, graviori poena feriemini, et venient super vos exempla priorum ».

97. (Vers. 51.). Et propterea exemplificat secundum testimonia scripta, cum subdit: *A sanguine Abel usque ad sanguinem Zachariae. Abel* namque primus fuit iustus a fratre occisus, secundum quod dicitur Genesis quarto[^p308-4]. *Zacharias* autem iste fuit filius Ioiadae, qui occisus fuit a rege Ioas, qui multa bona acceperat ab eius patre, secundum quod historia narrat secundi Paralipomenon vigesimo quarto. Verumtamen iste non fuit interfectus ultimo, sed ideo magis coniungitur cum primo, « quia[^p308-5] Abel pastor ovium fuit et necatus in agro, Zacharias vero sacerdos et necatus in atrio templi; qui duos ordines Martyrum designant, scilicet laicos, et eos qui altaris officio mancipantur ». — Ideo locum mortis huius totum designat, cum subdit: *Qui periit inter*
*altare et aedem,* id est, occisus fuit inter templum et altare holocausti. « Duo namque altaria erant in templo: unum interius, scilicet incensi; aliud exterius, scilicet holocausti[^p308-6] »; secundum quod legitur Exodi trigesimo quinto. Et inter hoc et templum Zacharias a Iudaeis fuit occisus, secundum quod scribitur secundi Paralipomenon vigesimo quarto: « Congregati adversus eum, miserunt lapides iuxta regis imperium in atrio domus Dei ». « Qui, cum moreretur, ait: Videat Dominus et requirat ». Hoc dixit attendens aequitatem divinae iustitiae, quae nihil dimittit inultum.

98. Ad cuius infallibilitatem notandam iterat futuram vindictam, cum subdit: *Ita, dico vobis, requiretur ab hac generatione;* ita ut per pronomen fiat demonstratio generationis malignae. Dicit enim Glossa Bedae[^p308-7]: « Omnes maligni una generatio sunt, una civitas, unum corpus diaboli, sicut omnes boni unum corpus, una generatio sunt Dei ». Unde de hac generatione iniqua intelligi potest illud Proverbiorum trigesimo[^p308-8]: « Generatio, quae pro dentibus gladios habet et commandit molaribus suis, ut perdat inopes de terra et pauperes ex hominibus ». Haec est generatio, a qua requiretur vindicta; sed in hac specialiter a gente Iudaica, quia consummata fuit in eis perfidia. Unde Chrysostomus[^p308-9]: « Sicut *bonis* per multas generationes promisit Deus Christum venturum, praestitit autem novissimis Sanctis; ita per singulas generationes comminatus est *malis,* reddet autem generationi novissimae. Nunquam enim talis *gratia* data est hominibus, qualis in Christo, aut talis *interitus* venit super impios, sicut super Iudaeos ». Unde possunt ipsi dicere optime illud Genesis quadragesimo secundo[^p308-10]: « Merito haec patimur, quia peccavimus in fratrem nostrum. En, sanguis eius exquiritur ».

99. (Vers. 52.). *Vae vobis legisperitis* etc. Hic tertio replicat *vae* propter *fraudulentiam contra veritatem doctrinae.* Et quia mali et seductores
<!-- page 309 -->
proficiunt in peius, ideo primo praemittitur *comminatio gehennae;* secundo vero adiungitur *obduratio nequitiae.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *comminationem poenae* propter defectum veritatis doctrinae dicit: *Vae vobis legisperitis! qui*[^p309-1] *tulistis clavem scientiae,* scilicet quantum ad officium dispensationis doctrinae. Haec autem clavis principaliter est Christi tanquam summi doctoris: « Unus est enim magister vester, Christus », secundum quod dicitur Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p309-2]. Et de hac Isaiae vigesimo secundo: « Dabo clavem domus David super humerum eius, et aperiet » etc. Hanc tulerant scribae, simulantes, se recte docere; sed tamen non recte docebant, quia negligebant se ipsos et impediebant alios.

100. Propter quod subdit: *Ipsi non introistis et eos qui introibant, prohibuistis.* Ipsi namque *non introibant,* quia, licet haberent verba, non habebant facta; unde poterat eis dici illud ad Romanos secundo[^p309-3]: « Qui alios doces te ipsum non doces; qui praedicas non furandum furaris ». Et quia sola verba habebant, *intrare non poterant;* Matthaei septimo[^p309-4]: « Non omnis, qui dicit mihi: Domine, Domine, intrabit in regnum caelorum » etc.; ideo non per sola verba intrabant; et *alios etiam impediebant* per mala exempla; unde verificatum est illud Ieremiae quinquagesimo[^p309-5]: « Grex perditus factus est populus meus; pastores seduxerunt eos », scilicet a recta via, vel *malis exemplis,* vel *falsis doctrinis,* secundum illud Threnorum secundo: « Prophetae tui viderunt falsa tibi et stulta nec aperiebant iniquitatem tuam tibi, ut te ad poenitentiam provocarent; viderunt autem tibi assumtiones falsas et eiectiones »; quia ex falsis assumtionibus fere omnes conscientiae distortae formantur, per quas homines a regno Dei eiiciuntur et in illud intrare prohibentur. Unde Chrysosto-
mus[^p309-6]: « Lex et Prophetae Christi adventum manifeste praedicabant, sed hanc ianuam intellectus pravis expositionibus claudebant »; et ideo prohibebant intrare, revocando a via veritatis exemplo et verbo. Quocontra Proverbiorum tertio: « Noli prohibere eum benefacere qui potest; si potes, et ipse benefac ».

101. (Vers. 53.). Secundo quantum ad *obdurationem nequitiae* subditur: *Cum autem haec ad illos diceret, coeperunt Pharisaei et legisperiti graviter insistere;* in quo ostendunt, cor suum esse *obduratum et aggravatum,* audiendo verbum divinum; ut ostendatur, in eis esse verificatum illud Isaiae sexto[^p309-7]: « Excaeca cor populi huius et oculos eius claude et aures eius aggrava »; et Matthaei decimo tertio: « Incrassatum est cor populi huius, et auribus graviter audierunt ». — Nec tantum erat in eis obduratio, sed *veritatis impugnatio;* unde subdit: *Et os eius opprimere de multis,* quasi os illud mundissimum locutum esset malum vel falsum. Unde verificatum est illud Amos quinto[^p309-8]: « Odio habuerunt corripientem in porta et loquentem perfecte abominati sunt »; et ideo Proverbiorum nono: « Qui erudit derisorem, ipse sibi iniuriam facit »; quia convertitur ad convicia propter veritatem auditam.

102. (Vers. 54.). Et quia non sufficit obstinatae malitiae conviciari quem odit, sed etiam nititur machinari ad mortis interitum; propterea subdit: *Insidiantes ei et quaerentes aliquid capere ex ore*[^p309-9] *eius, ut accusarent illum.* Non enim audebant aperte ipsum occidere propter timorem turbarum, sed volebant occulte occidere propter odium veritatis auditae. Unde Ioannis octavo[^p309-10]: « Quid me quaeritis interficere, hominem, qui veritatem locutus sum vobis »? quod quaerebant caute et malitiose; unde Matthaei vigesimo secundo: « Abeuntes Pharisaei, consilium inierunt, ut Iesum caperent in sermone ».

## English
<!-- page 304 -->
85. *But woe to you, Pharisees* etc. — After the rebuke of duplicity *the threatening of calamity* is subjoined here for the double-minded and the hypocrites; whence in Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p304-13], this determination is as it were added over: « Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites »! And since these two kinds of persons, namely of the Pharisees and of the scribes, dissembled religion most of all; therefore he makes his invective against these two persons, but in Matthew together, here, however, distinctly. Therefore this part has two. In the first he threatens *woe* to the *Pharisees* themselves; but in the second, to the *scribes* or lawyers, there[^p304-14]: *But one of the lawyers answering* etc.

Now *the threatening* of eternal damnation is repeated three times against the *Pharisees* for a threefold cause, namely for *negligence,* for *arrogance* and for *fraudulence,* so that *negligence* is in the omission of good, *arrogance* in the commission of evil, *fraudulence* in the hiding of evil and the showing of good.

86. (Vers. 42.). First, then, he threatens the *Pharisees* with eternal punishment for *negligence,* by which,
<!-- page 305 -->
intent on the least things, they omitted the greatest. And this, when he says: *But woe to you, Pharisees, who*[^p305-1] *tithe mint and rue and every herb,* as if you were much intent on the fulfilling of the divine commandments; whence below, in the eighteenth chapter, the Pharisee boasted: « I give tithes of all that I possess ». But yet by this they were not justified, on account of the omission of the best things; whence he adds: *And pass over all*[^p305-2] *judgment and the charity of God,* that is, justice and mercy. And this is what God most of all requires, according to that of Micheas, the sixth chapter: « I will shew thee, O man, what is good, and what the Lord requireth of thee: verily, to do judgment and to love mercy ». And this he calls *charity;* Zacharias, the seventh chapter: « Judge ye true judgment, and shew ye mercy and compassion every man to his neighbour »; so that *judgment* is with respect to oneself, and *charity* or mercy with respect to the neighbour. — Or, by *judgment* let the divine *precept* be understood, according to that of the Psalm[^p305-3]: « The judgments of the Lord are true, justified in themselves » etc.; but *charity* is called *the end of the precept* and its completion; the first to Timothy, the first chapter: « The end of the commandment is charity » etc. — Or, by *judgment* let *the judicial precepts* be understood, according to that of Exodus, the twenty-first chapter[^p305-4]: « These are the judgments which thou shalt set before them: If thou buy a servant » etc.; by *charity, the moral ones;* whence to the Romans, the thirteenth chapter: « He that loveth his neighbour hath fulfilled the Law. For: Thou shalt not kill » etc. He reproves them, then, because for the sake of *the ceremonial precepts* they let go *the judicial* and *the moral,* which were rather to be done with care. — And therefore he adds: *Now these things you ought to have done,* namely judgment and charity, first and principally; whence Osee, the sixth chapter[^p305-5]: « I will have mercy and not sacrifice, and the knowledge of God more than holocausts ». — *And not to leave the other undone,* because, Ecclesiasticus, the nineteenth chapter, « he that contemneth small things shall fall by little and little »; and Ecclesiastes, the seventh chapter: « He that feareth God neglecteth nothing ».

87. (Vers. 43.). Secondly he *reiterates this threatening* for *arrogance,* when he subjoins: *Woe to you, Pharisees, who*[^p305-6] *love the uppermost seats in the synagogues,* as to *the ambition of dignities.* Whence the Gloss: « He rebukes arrogance, because they seek the primacies ». Now this is not to be sought; whence Ecclesiasticus, the seventh chapter[^p305-7]: « Seek not of man a preeminence, nor of the king the seat of honour ». For such a seat is not one of true *excellence,* but rather of *pestilence,* according to that of the Psalm: « Blessed is the man who hath not sat in the chair of pestilence »; upon which, however, the scribes and Pharisees sat; but yet as unworthy, because they had words and not fitting deeds. Whence it is said in Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p305-8]: « The scribes and the Pharisees have sitten on the chair of Moses; what they say, do ye; according to their works do ye not ». — And because on the ambition of dignity there follows *the appetite for praise,* therefore he subjoins: *And salutations in the marketplace,* that is, praises in the sight of many, that they might seem more honourable than the rest; whence Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p305-9]: « They love salutations in the marketplace, and to be called by men *Rabbi* ». Whence here they are reproved because they sought vainglory, on account of which they did not even dare to confess the truth they knew, according to that of John, the twelfth chapter: « But of the chief men many believed in him, but because of the Pharisees they did not confess, that they might not be cast out of the synagogue. For they loved the glory of men more than the glory of God ». — From which it appears that we ought altogether to contemn praises and honours according to *the divine form,* not to seek them after the manner of the *Pharisees.* For he who loves these things, as Jerome says[^p305-10], is not a disciple of Christ, but a scribe and Pharisee. For Christ, when he was sought for a kingdom, « fled into the mountain », John, the sixth chapter; when he was praised, he withdrew into the desert to pray, above, in the fifth chapter.

88. (Vers. 44.). Thirdly he *repeats this threatening* for *fraudulence,* when he subjoins: *Woe to you, who*[^p305-11] *are as monuments which appear not,* namely such as they are within, because they are fetid and unclean within, but without they are fair; whence he adds: *And men that walk over are not aware.* Such are the hypocrites, who, though they be evil within, seem perfect without; whence they are rightly called *sepulchres,* according to that of the Psalm[^p305-12]: « Their throat is an open sepulchre; with their tongues » etc. And therefore they are not *sons of the Bridegroom,* nay, altogether opposed. For in Canticles,
<!-- page 306 -->
the first chapter: « I am black but beautiful », but these are white, but fetid; therefore they are rightly called *monuments;* whence Matthew, the twenty-third chapter: « Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! because you are like to whited sepulchres, which outwardly appear to men beautiful, but within are full of dead men's bones and of all filthiness ». Whence Chrysostom[^p306-1]: « The bodies of the just are called *temples,* but the bodies of sinners *sepulchres,* because within is a dead soul; for that soul is not to be thought living which has done nothing living, that is, spiritual, in the body ». And after: « Tell me, hypocrite, if it is good to be good, why wilt thou not *be* what thou wilt *appear?* But what is base to *appear* is baser to *be.* Either, then, be what thou appearest, or appear what thou art ».

From these words of the Lord it can be gathered who those are[^p306-2] that ought to be judged *hypocrites,* namely those in whom *negligence, arrogance, fraudulence* reign. Now these are they who prefer the least things to the greatest, the transitory to the eternal, the apparent to the true. But to judge of these is not for any man whatever, but for Christ, who looks upon the heart, according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter[^p306-3]: « Judge not before the time, until the Lord come, who will bring to light the hidden things of darkness » etc. For the Lord does not reprehend goodness that is apparent and evident, but that which is sophistical and not existing.

89. *But one of the lawyers answering*[^p306-4] etc. After he has threatened woe to the Pharisees, here secondly he threatens the *lawyers,* and this for three causes: first, for *sloth* against the truth of life; secondly, for *perfidy* against the truth of justice, there[^p306-5]: *Woe to you, who build the monuments;* thirdly, for *deceit* against the truth of doctrine, there: *Woe to you, lawyers, who have taken away the key* etc. For this threefold truth ought most of all to be in the doctors of the Law, but in the lawyers there was nothing but hypocrisy.

In threatening hell, then, for *sloth,* first is set *the display of authority,* secondly is joined *the threatening of calamity.*

90. (Vers. 45.). First, then, as to *the display of authority* on the part of the lawyer it is said: *But one*[^p306-6] *of the lawyers answering,* one, namely, who seemed to himself to be learned, according to that of Isaias, the fifth chapter: « Woe to you who are wise in your own eyes, and prudent in your own sight ». And because such men seem to themselves learned and great, therefore they think it a reproach
to be reproved by anyone. On account of which he adds: *Saith to him: Master, saying these things thou reproachest us also;* which he would not have said, unless he felt in his conscience that he likewise transgressed, and out of pride would not be reprehended; whence that of the Romans, the second chapter[^p306-7], could be said to him: « Thou art confident that thou thyself art a guide of the blind, a light of them that are in darkness, an instructor of the foolish, a teacher of infants ». « Thou therefore that teachest another teachest not thyself ». They think sound doctrine a *reproach,* because, as it is said in Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter[^p306-8], « a fool receiveth not the words of prudence, unless thou say those things which are in his heart ». And truly such a one was a fool, who reckoned himself learned, since Proverbs, the twenty-sixth chapter: « Hast thou seen a man seem wise to himself? The fool shall have more hope than he ».

91. (Vers. 46.). But secondly, as to *the threatening of calamity* for sloth in doing well, it is said: *But he said: Woe to you lawyers also!* namely of eternal damnation. And the reason of this is that they said much and did little. — Therefore he subjoins: *Because you load men with burdens which they cannot bear,* that is, with superstitious and useless precepts and traditions, so that that of the Acts, the fifteenth chapter[^p306-9], may rightly be said: This is the burden « which neither we nor our fathers have been able to bear ». Whence Matthew, the twenty-third chapter: « They bind heavy and insupportable burdens, and lay them on men's shoulders »; to which, namely, no one is bound, and they say he is bound. — And because they laid on these burdens — they *taught* them[^p306-10], but did not *bear* them by doing — he adds: *And you yourselves touch not the packs with one of your fingers.*

92. Note pointedly that he says: *You touch not with one finger;* he does not say: *You bear not,* because there could be an excuse from impotence. Often a doctor teaches what he *cannot* do; yet his doctrine is not to be contemned, if he *wills* to do it, because the will is reckoned to him for the deed. Whence the first of Kings, the thirtieth chapter[^p306-11]: « Equal shall be the portion of him that went down to battle and of him that abode at the packs, and they shall divide alike »; and this is said for the men who had stayed behind, weary; but he who will not put a finger to the packs, it is manifest that he will sustain no labour. And such a one, whether he lay burdens on by way of a master or by way of a prelate, is altogether reprehensible, and is not an imitator of Christ, of whom it is said in the Acts, the first chapter[^p306-12]: « Jesus began to do and to teach ».
<!-- page 307 -->
Whence against these, Deuteronomy, the twenty-fifth chapter: « Thou shalt not have divers weights in thy bag »; and Proverbs, the twentieth chapter: « Weight and weight, measure and measure: both are abominable before God ». Now this is when a man is merciful to himself and hard to his neighbour or subject; when he bears nothing, but lays the whole on others to be borne. Whence Chrysostom[^p307-1]: « Wilt thou appear and be holy? Be austere about thy own life, kind about the life of others. Let men hear thee commanding small things and doing great ones ».

93. (Vers. 47.). *Woe to you, who build* etc. After he has threatened them with *woe* for the fault of sloth, from the defect of the truth of life, here secondly he repeats the same *for the fault of perfidy against the truth of justice,* which was in the impious persecution of the just. Concerning which, first *the fault itself is magnified,* then *the vengeance is made heavier.*

First, then, as to *the magnifying of the fault* he says: *Woe to you, who build the monuments of the Prophets,* making a pretence of piety in deed, but bearing impiety in mind, as their fathers also did. — On account of which he subjoins: *And your fathers killed them*[^p307-2], namely, as the impious the just. Whence in the Acts, the seventh chapter, blessed Stephen said: « Which of the Prophets have not your fathers persecuted? And they have slain them who foretold of the coming of the Just One ». And these too, though they dissembled otherwise, were nevertheless their imitators.

94. (Vers. 48.). On account of which he says: *Truly you bear witness that you consent to the works of your fathers,* that is, you agree that these were the works of your fathers; whence Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p307-3]: « Wherefore you are witnesses against yourselves, that you are the sons of them that killed the Prophets ». — Therefore he adds: *For they indeed killed them, and you build their sepulchres;* and so by sign and by word they were witnesses that their fathers had killed them. And this indeed they *dissembled,* as though they reproved it by word and sign; whence Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p307-4]: « You adorn the monuments of the just, and say: If we had been in the days of our fathers, we would not have been partakers with them »; but they *approved* it by a like deed and wish, in that they themselves, like their fathers,
were persecutors of the good; and from this they were witnesses that they consented to the works of their fathers. Whence Jerome[^p307-5]: « You are witnesses against yourselves; for by deed you bear witness that you are imitators of the fathers whose crimes you detest by word ». Whence the Acts, the seventh chapter[^p307-6]: « You of stiff neck and uncircumcised in hearts and ears, you have always resisted the Holy Spirit; as your fathers, so you also ».

95. (Vers. 49.). And because the malice which they bore in the heart was, by the permission of God, to be made public in deed, therefore he subjoins: *Therefore also the wisdom of God said,* « that is, disposed from eternity[^p307-7] », or Christ himself disposed or foresaw, who is « the power of God and the wisdom of God ». Or *said,* that is, foretold by the prophetic voice. — *I will send to them Prophets and Apostles, and some of them they will kill and persecute,* according to that of Jeremias, the seventh chapter[^p307-8]: « I have sent to you all my servants the Prophets by day, rising up early and sending; they have not hearkened to me, nor inclined their ear, but have hardened their neck and have done worse than their fathers ». This was verified in these, who killed not only the Prophets, but also *their own Lord.* Whence in the Acts, the third chapter[^p307-9], Peter says to them: « But you denied the Holy One and the Just, and desired a murderer to be granted unto you; but the author of life you killed ». They also killed and persecuted all *his witnesses.* For James was slain, and Peter seized, the Acts, the twelfth chapter[^p307-10]. They themselves also persecuted Paul and Barnabas, so that they passed over to the gentiles, the Acts, the thirteenth and the fourteenth chapter; and all the disciples too were scourged, the Acts, the fifth chapter.

96. (Vers. 50.). But secondly, as to *the making heavier of the vengeance* he says: *That the blood of all the Prophets may be required. It is required,* namely, by vengeance; whence in the Psalm[^p307-11]: « For requiring their blood he hath remembered » etc.; and Genesis, the ninth chapter: « At the hand of man and of his brother will I require the life of man. Whosoever shall shed man's blood, his blood shall be shed ». — *The blood,* then, *shall be required,* that is, the vengeance for the shedding of blood; on account of
<!-- page 308 -->
which he adds: *Which was shed from the foundation of the world,* that is, from the beginning even to the end. *It shall be required,* I say, *of this generation,* as if he should say that the vengeance for all the blood of holy men that was shed shall be upon the generation of the Jews, because they shed the blood of him who was « the Saint of Saints », namely Christ, Daniel, the ninth chapter[^p308-1]; and after: « Christ shall be slain, and the people that shall deny him shall not be his ». Whence they themselves said, John, the nineteenth chapter: « We have no king but Caesar »; and Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter: « His blood be upon us and upon our children ». — Or this is said that they may be punished for an example to others, because, though they saw evil punished in others, they did not correct themselves. Whence Chrysostom[^p308-2]: « Why does God avenge the sins of others in these, if they alone suffered such things, though they alone had not sinned? As, when many servants sin, one is chastised for his own sin, and yet, scourged for all, he alone has borne what was promised to all ». But the reason why the punishment came rather upon these is given by the same John Chrysostom[^p308-3]: « Because, he says, you have heard of the vengeance done upon Cain and upon the other wicked, and if you are not corrected by their examples, you shall be struck with a heavier punishment, and the examples of those before you shall come upon you ».

97. (Vers. 51.). And therefore he gives examples according to the written testimonies, when he subjoins: *From the blood of Abel unto the blood of Zacharias. Abel,* namely, was the first just man slain by a brother, according to what is said in Genesis, the fourth chapter[^p308-4]. *Zacharias,* however, this one was the son of Joiada, who was slain by king Joas, who had received many good things from his father, according to what the history relates in the second of Paralipomenon, the twenty-fourth chapter. Yet this man was not slain last, but he is joined with the first for this reason, « because[^p308-5] Abel was a shepherd of sheep and was slain in the field, but Zacharias a priest and slain in the court of the temple; and they designate the two orders of Martyrs, namely the lay, and those who are bound to the office of the altar ». — Therefore he designates the whole place of this death, when he subjoins: *Who perished between*
*the altar and the temple,* that is, he was slain between the temple and the altar of holocaust. « For there were two altars in the temple: one within, namely of incense; the other without, namely of holocaust[^p308-6] »; according to what is read in Exodus, the thirty-fifth chapter. And between this and the temple Zacharias was slain by the Jews, according to what is written in the second of Paralipomenon, the twenty-fourth chapter: « Gathered together against him, they cast stones at him according to the king's command in the court of the house of God ». « And when he was dying, he said: The Lord see and require it ». This he said attending to the equity of the divine justice, which leaves nothing unavenged.

98. For noting its infallibility he reiterates the future vengeance, when he subjoins: *Yea, I say to you, it shall be required of this generation;* so that by the pronoun there is made a pointing out of the malignant generation. For the Gloss of Bede says[^p308-7]: « All the malignant are one generation, one city, one body of the devil, as all the good are one body, one generation of God ». Whence of this wicked generation that of Proverbs, the thirtieth chapter[^p308-8], can be understood: « A generation that for teeth hath swords, and grindeth with its jaw teeth, to destroy the needy from off the earth and the poor from among men ». This is the generation of which the vengeance shall be required; but in this, specially of the Jewish nation, because perfidy was consummated in them. Whence Chrysostom[^p308-9]: « As God through many generations promised *the good* that Christ would come, but gave him to the last Saints; so through the several generations he threatened *the wicked,* but will render it to the last generation. For never was such *grace* given to men as in Christ, or such *destruction* came upon the impious as upon the Jews ». Whence they themselves can most fittingly say that of Genesis, the forty-second chapter[^p308-10]: « Deservedly do we suffer these things, because we have sinned against our brother. Lo, his blood is required ».

99. (Vers. 52.). *Woe to you lawyers* etc. Here thirdly he repeats *woe* for *fraudulence against the truth of doctrine.* And because wicked men and seducers
<!-- page 309 -->
grow worse and worse, therefore first is set *the threatening of hell;* but secondly is joined *the hardening of wickedness.*

First, then, as to *the threatening of punishment* for the defect of the truth of doctrine he says: *Woe to you lawyers, who*[^p309-1] *have taken away the key of knowledge,* namely as to the office of dispensing doctrine. Now this key belongs principally to Christ as the supreme doctor: « For one is your master, Christ », according to what is said in Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p309-2]. And of this Isaias, the twenty-second chapter: « I will give the key of the house of David upon his shoulder, and he shall open » etc. This the scribes had taken away, dissembling that they taught rightly; but yet they did not teach rightly, because they neglected themselves and hindered others.

100. On account of which he subjoins: *You yourselves have not entered in, and those that were entering in you have hindered.* For they themselves *did not enter in,* because, though they had words, they had not deeds; whence that of the Romans, the second chapter[^p309-3], could be said to them: « Thou that teachest others teachest not thyself; thou that preachest that men should not steal stealest ». And because they had words only, *they could not enter;* Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p309-4]: « Not every one that saith to me: Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven » etc.; therefore they did not enter by words alone; and *they hindered others also* by evil examples; whence that of Jeremias, the fiftieth chapter[^p309-5], was verified: « My people is become a lost flock; the shepherds have seduced them », namely from the right way, either *by evil examples* or *by false doctrines,* according to that of Lamentations, the second chapter: « Thy prophets have seen false and foolish things for thee, and they have not laid open thy iniquity, to excite thee to penance; but they have seen for thee false assumptions and banishments »; because from false assumptions almost all distorted consciences are formed, by which men are cast out of the kingdom of God and are hindered from entering into it. Whence Chrysostom[^p309-6]: « The Law and the Prophets plainly preached the coming of Christ, but they shut this door of understanding with perverse expositions »; and therefore they hindered from entering, calling back from the way of truth by example and by word. Against which Proverbs, the third chapter: « Do not withhold him from doing good who is able; if thou art able, do good thyself also ».

101. (Vers. 53.). Secondly, as to *the hardening of wickedness,* there is subjoined: *And as he was saying these things to them, the Pharisees and the lawyers began violently to urge him;* in which they show their heart to be *hardened and made heavy* in hearing the divine word; so that it may be shown that that of Isaias, the sixth chapter[^p309-7], was verified in them: « Blind the heart of this people, and shut its eyes, and make its ears heavy »; and Matthew, the thirteenth chapter: « The heart of this people is grown gross, and with their ears they have been dull of hearing ». — Nor was there in them hardening only, but *assault upon the truth;* whence he subjoins: *And to oppress his mouth about many things,* as if that most clean mouth had spoken evil or falsehood. Whence that of Amos, the fifth chapter[^p309-8], was verified: « They have hated him that rebuketh in the gate, and have abhorred him that speaketh perfectly »; and therefore Proverbs, the ninth chapter: « He that teacheth a scorner doth an injury to himself »; because he turns to railing on account of the truth he has heard.

102. (Vers. 54.). And because it is not enough for obstinate malice to rail at him whom it hates, but it strives also to plot unto destruction by death; therefore he subjoins: *Lying in wait for him, and seeking to catch something from his mouth*[^p309-9]*, that they might accuse him.* For they did not dare to kill him openly for fear of the crowds, but they wished to kill him secretly out of hatred of the truth they had heard. Whence John, the eighth chapter[^p309-10]: « Why seek you to kill me, a man who have spoken the truth to you »? which they sought warily and maliciously; whence Matthew, the twenty-second chapter: « The Pharisees going away took counsel how they might catch Jesus in his speech ».
