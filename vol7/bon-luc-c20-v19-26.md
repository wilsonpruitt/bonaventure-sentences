# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XX, Vers. 19-26
## *Et quaerebant principes sacerdotum etc. Post repulsam calumniam et detectam perfidiam sequitur hic tertia pars, in qua deprehendit adinventam versutiam*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 509 -->

25. (Vers. 19.). *Et quaerebant principes sacerdotum* etc. Post repulsam calumniam et detectam perfidiam sequitur hic tertia pars[^p509-5], in qua *deprehendit adinventam versutiam.* Huius autem versutiae primo describit Evangelista *adinventionem fallacem;* secundo vero, *confutationem veracem,* ibi[^p509-6]: *Considerans autem dolum illorum* etc.

*Adinventionem autem versutiae* describit Evangelista quantum ad tria, scilicet quantum ad Pharisaeorum *indignationem malevolam, insidiationem malignam* et *interrogationem malitiosam.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *Pharisaeorum indignationem malevolam* dicit: *Et quaerebant principes sacerdotum et scribae mittere in illum manus illa*[^p509-7] *hora; quaerebant,* inquam, ad ipsum occidendum, secundum illud Ioannis septimo: « Quid me quaeritis interficere, hominem, qui vera locutus sum vobis »? Ex animi ergo indignatione quaerebant, sed ex populi formidine non audebant. — Unde addit: *Et timuerunt populum,* non Deum; contra quod Proverbiorum vigesimo nono[^p509-8]: « Qui timet hominem cito corruet ». Populum autem magis timebant quam Deum, quia plus amabant populi laudem quam Christi veritatem; laus enim populi ungebat, sed veritas Christi arguebat. — Unde subditur: *Cognoverunt enim, quod ad ipsos dixerit similitudinem istam*[^p509-9]. Et sic veritas odium peperit, et verificatum est illud Amos quinto: « Odio habuerunt corripientem in porta et loquentem perfecte abominati sunt ». In huius figuram dicitur Ieremiae vigesimo sexto: « Locuti sunt sacerdotes et Prophetae ad principes et omnem populum dicentes: Iudicium mortis est viro isti, quia prophetavit adversus civitatem istam, sicut audistis auribus vestris ». Isti autem Pharisaei, dum volebant Christum occidere, impugnando veritatem auditam ipsam veritatem implebant, secundum illud Actuum decimo tertio[^p509-10]: « Qui habitabant Ierusalem et principes eius, hunc Iesum ignorantes et voces Prophetarum, quae per omne Sabbatum leguntur, iudicantes impleverunt; et nullam causam mortis invenientes in eo, petierunt a Pilato, ut interficerent eum. Cumque consummassent omnia, quae de eo scripta erant, deponentes eum de ligno, posuerunt eum in monumento ».

26. (Vers. 20.). Secundo quantum ad *insidiationem malignam* subdit: *Et observantes miserunt insidiatores, qui se iustos simularent,* contra illud Proverbiorum vigesimo quarto[^p509-11]: « Ne insidieris et quaeras iniquitatem in domo iusti et ne vastes requiem eius ». Insidiae autem fiunt per simulationes; contra quos Iob trigesimo sexto: « Simulatores et callidi provocant iram Dei »; quia, Proverbiorum undecimo, « simulator ore decipit amicum suum ». — Et quia simulatio adiunctam habet intentionem perversam, ideo addit: *Ut caperent eum in sermone et*[^p509-12] *traderent illum principatui et potestati praesidis,* scilicet Herodis. Unde Marci duodecimo dicitur: « Mittunt ad eum quosdam ex Pharisaeis et Herodianis, ut eum caperent in verbo ». Et haec fuit
<!-- page 510 -->
magna versutia; unde Glossa[^p510-1]: « Quod prae timore populi per se non possunt manibus praesidis efficere volunt, ut velut immunes a morte ipsius videantur ». Ideo in persona talium dicitur Sapientiae secundo[^p510-2]: « Circumveniamus iustum, quoniam inutilis est nobis et contrarius operibus nostris, et improperat in nos peccata legis et infamat in nos peccata disciplinae ». Unde etiam in figuram huius dixerunt Philisthiim ad Dalilam Iudicum decimo sexto: « Decipe eum et disce ab eo, in quo habeat tantam fortitudinem, et quomodo eum superare valeamus ».

27. (Vers. 21.). Tertio quantum ad *interrogationem malitiosam* subiungit: *Et interrogaverunt illum*[^p510-3] *dicentes: Magister, scimus, quia recte dicis et doces.* Quantum ad *verbum* verum quidem dicunt, quia Ioannis decimo septimo: « Sermo tuus veritas est ». Hoc ipsi dicebant blandiendo, ut extorquerent secretum cordis ab ipso. Unde Chrysostomus[^p510-4]: « Haec est prima hypocritarum prudentia, ficta laudatio, sicut qui taurum non potest viribus subiugare mollibus manibus fricat cervicem eius, ut per blandimenta comprehendat ». Et de talibus Proverbiorum decimo sexto[^p510-5]: « Vir malus lactat amicum suum et ducit eum per viam non bonam »; et Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo: « Qui benedicit proximo suo voce grandi de nocte consurgens maledicenti similis erit ». — *Et non accipis personam hominis*[^p510-6], quantum ad *animum;* et hoc verum, secundum illud Actuum decimo: « In veritate comperi, quoniam non est personarum acceptor Deus »; et Deuteronomii decimo: « Deus magnus et potens et terribilis, qui personam non accipit nec munera ». — *Sed viam Dei in veritate doces,* quantum ad *exemplum;* propter quod ipse dicebat Ioannis decimo quarto[^p510-7]: « Ego sum via, veritas et vita ». — Vel: *recte dicis,* quantum ad *veritatem vitae; personam non accipis,* quoad *veritatem iustitiae;* sed *viam Dei in veritate doces,* quoad *veritatem doctrinae.* In quo laudant Christum laude vera, sed fallaci corde.

28. (Vers. 22.). Et hoc patet ex ipsorum versuta interrogatione, cum subditur: *Licet nobis dare tributum Caesari, an non?* secundum divinae legis decretum; in quo quaestionem dolosam proponunt, quia, utrolibet dato, ipsum ad inconveniens deducere intendunt. Nam aut diceret, quod sic, et ita videretur dicere contra Legem et contra populum Iudaeorum et opinionem communem; aut diceret, quod non, et tunc traderent eum principatui, sicut Caesari rebellem et totius imperii hostem. Unde Glossa[^p510-8]: « Pharisaei, qui de iustitia sibi applaudebant, qui decimas et primitias Deo dabant, tributa homini solvenda negabant ». Et hinc est, quod Christo blandiebantur, ut hoc ex ore Christi extorquerent huiusmodi documentum, ut sic post possent dicere illud Ioannis decimo nono[^p510-9]: « Si hunc dimittis, non es amicus Caesaris; omnis enim, qui se regem facit, contradicit Caesari ».

29. (Vers. 23.). *Considerans autem dolum illorum.* Postquam descripsit versutiae adinventionem fallacem, hic secundo[^p510-10] describit *confutationem veracem.* Circa quam tria introducuntur, scilicet *circumspecta consideratio sapientiae respondentis, verax dissolutio propositae quaestionis* et *evidens confutatio astutiae versipellis.*

Primo igitur quantum ad *circumspectam considerationem sapientiae respondentis* dicit: *Considerans autem dolum illorum,* in animo; unde Hieronymus[^p510-11]: « Prima virtus respondentis est interrogantium mentes cognoscere ». In horum mentibus ipsa Sapientia videbat dolum; unde Sapientiae septimo: « Sapientiam non vincit malitia »; et Ecclesiastici decimo sexto: « Omne cor intelligitur ab illo, et vias illius quis intelligit »? quia, ad Hebraeos quarto, « vivus est sermo Dei et efficax et discretor cogitationum et intentionum cordis ». — Ideo iste Sermo *dixit ad eos: Quid me tentatis?* scilicet in verbo, secundum illud Ecclesiastici decimo tertio[^p510-12]: « Ex multa loquela tentabit te et subridens interrogabit te de absconditis tuis ». In persona horum dicitur Sapientiae secundo: « Tentemus, quae ventura sunt illi, et sciemus, quae erunt novissima illius ».

30. (Vers. 24.). Et quia cautela tentationis revincitur per manifestationem veritatis, ideo subditur: *Ostendite mihi denarium. Cuius habet imaginem et
<!-- page 511 -->
*superscriptionem*[^p511-1]*? Respondentes dixerunt ei: Caesaris,* scilicet in exteriori signo. Hoc Dominus non quaerit, quia dubitet, sed ut ex eorum responsione ipsos confutet. — Vel ideo quaerit imaginem et superscriptionem denarii, ut ex ipsa forma et figura et qualitate rei ostendat manare debere iudicium aequitatis. — Vel ideo hoc requirit, ut ostendat, quod unusquisque iudicandus est secundum superscriptionem, quam superscriptam habet imagini mentis suae. Nam per *denarium* intelligitur homo, quia denarius est de materia terrestri, scilicet de metallo, et homo de limo; Genesis secundo[^p511-2]: « Formavit Deus hominem de limo terrae »; et ipse *decimus* est post novenarium Angelorum et per *drachmam unam* designatur supra decimo quinto: « Aut quae mulier » etc. Per *imaginem denarii* intelligitur Trinitas personarum, de qua Ecclesiastici decimo septimo[^p511-3]: « Deus de terra creavit hominem et secundum imaginem suam fecit illum »; et Genesis primo: « Faciamus hominem ad imaginem » etc. Per *superscriptionem* intelligitur qualitas morum, et haec est duplex: una *divina,* alia *mundana. Divina* est per lumen gratiae et iustitiae; Psalmus[^p511-4]: « Signatum est super nos lumen vultus tui, Domine »; *mundana* autem, quae est Caesaris, est per appetitum cupiditatis et avaritiae. De hac Ambrosius[^p511-5]: « Imago Caesaris non reperitur nisi in mari, ubi dracones »; in quo tangit illud quod legitur Matthaei decimo septimo, ubi Christus misit Petrum ad mare; et invenit piscem et in pisce staterem, quem Caesari solvit; in quo intelligitur, quod superscriptio terrenitatis non est nisi in mundanis mentibus, in quibus pro cupiditatibus variis est procella et impetus tempestatis.

31. (Vers. 25.). Secundo quantum ad *veracem dissolutionem propositae quaestionis* subdit: *Et ait illis: Reddite ergo quae sunt Caesaris Caesari;* in quo manifeste iustam dat responsionem; est enim actus iustitiae « reddere unicuique quod suum est[^p511-6] ». Si ergo res est eius cuius habet superscriptionem; cum temporalia superscriptionem Caesaris habeant, non est contra Deum, si terrenis principibus persolvantur. Ideo ad Romanos decimo tertio[^p511-7] dicitur: « Reddite omnibus debita: cui tributum, tributum; cui vectigal, vectigal. Nemini quidquam debeatis, nisi ut invicem diligatis ». — Et quia non est sufficiens iustitia, nisi suum unicuique reddatur; ideo addit: *Et quae sunt Dei Deo;* Glossa[^p511-8]: « Sicut Caesar exigit impressionem suae imaginis, sic et Deus animam lumine vultus sui insignitam »; propter quod primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto: « Sicut portavimus imaginem terreni, sic portemus imaginem caelestis ».

32. Et nota, quod prius praemittit, ut reddantur quae sunt mundi, quia, sicut dicitur primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p511-9], « non prius quod spirituale est, sed quod animale, deinde quod spirituale ». Prius etiam deponendum est pondus, quam quis erigatur sursum; unde Glossa[^p511-10]: « Si non vis esse obnoxius Caesari, noli habere quae sunt mundi; et si habes divitias, obnoxius es Caesari. Si vis terreno regi nihil debere, omnia tua relinque, Christum sequere. Et bene prius quae sunt Caesaris reddenda decrevit, quia non potest quis esse Domini, nisi prius mundo renuntiet »; supra decimo quarto[^p511-11]: « Nisi quis renuntiaverit omnibus, quae possidet, non potest meus esse discipulus ». Dei autem sunt anima et corpus, quia ipse corpus et animam formavit, corpus de limo terrae, animam de nihilo; et ideo utrumque reddendum et offerendum Deo. Quod designatur in illa vidua, quae misit « duo aera minuta in gazophylacium » Domini, infra vigesimo primo[^p511-12]. Et ideo ad Romanos duodecimo: « Exhibeatis corpora vestra hostiam viventem, sanctam, Deo placentem, rationabile obsequium vestrum. Et nolite conformari huic saeculo, sed reformamini in novitate sensus vestri ». Unde in hoc verbo clauditur summa totius iustitiae et perfectionis[^p511-13].

33. (Vers. 26.). Tertio quantum ad *evidentem confutationem astutiae versipellis* subiungit: *Et non potuerunt reprehendere verbum eius coram plebe,* per apparentem contradictionem. Nec mirum, quia ad Romanos nono[^p511-14]: « Tu quis es, qui respondeas
<!-- page 512 -->
Deo »? Unde Iob nono: « Si repente interroget, quis respondebit ei? Aut quis potest ei dicere: Cur ita facis »? — Et ideo qui cum Christo contendunt necessario confunduntur; propter quod subdit: *Et mirati in responso eius tacuerunt,* per evidentem confusionem; unde Glossa[^p512-1]: « Qui credere debuerunt mirati sunt, quod non habent locum tentandi ». Unde Chrysostomus[^p512-2]: « Malitia confunditur aliquando, corrigitur autem nunquam; sicut qui vult claudere aquae currentis meatum, aliunde semitam rumpit, ita et malitia ». Unde non tacebant ex *prudentia,* sicut dicitur Ecclesiastici vigesimo[^p512-3]: « Est tacens qui invenitur sapiens ». « Homo sapiens tacebit usque ad tempus ». Nec ex *reverentia,* sicut ille, de quo Ecclesiastici trigesimo secundo: « Audi tacens, et pro reverentia accedet tibi bona gratia ». Nec ex *patientia,* sicut ille Isaiae quadragesimo secundo[^p512-4]: « Tacui, semper silui, patiens fui ». Nec ex *pace intrinseca;* Threnorum tertio: « Sedebit solitarius et tacebit ». Sed ex *confusione aperta,* sicut illi, a quibus Dominus quaesivit Marci tertio[^p512-5]: « Si licet Sabbatis bene facere, an male; animam salvam facere, an perdere? At illi tacebant »; et primi Regum secundo: « Impii in tenebris conticescent » etc.

## English
<!-- page 509 -->

25. (Vers. 19.). *And the chief priests sought* etc. After the calumny repelled and the perfidy laid bare, there follows here the third part[^p509-5], in which *he catches out the craftiness they devised.* Now of this craftiness the Evangelist describes first *the fallacious devising;* and second, *the truthful confutation,* there[^p509-6]: *But he considering their guile* etc.

*The devising of the craftiness* the Evangelist describes as to three things, namely as to the Pharisees' *malevolent indignation, malignant lying in wait* and *malicious questioning.*

First, then, as to *the malevolent indignation of the Pharisees* he says: *And the chief priests and the scribes sought to lay hands on him the same*[^p509-7] *hour; they sought,* I say, to kill him, according to that of John, the seventh chapter: « Why seek you to kill me, a man who have spoken true things to you »? Out of indignation of mind, then, they sought it, but out of dread of the people they dared not. — Whence he adds: *And they feared the people,* not God; against which, Proverbs, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p509-8]: « He that feareth man shall quickly fall ». Now they feared the people more than God, because they loved the praise of the people more than the truth of Christ; for the praise of the people anointed them, but the truth of Christ reproved them. — Whence it is subjoined: *For they knew that he spoke this parable to them*[^p509-9]. And so truth brought forth hatred, and that of Amos, the fifth chapter, was verified: « They have hated him that rebuketh in the gate, and have abhorred him that speaketh perfectly ». In figure of this it is said in Jeremias, the twenty-sixth chapter: « And the priests and the Prophets spoke to the princes and to all the people, saying: The judgement of death is for this man, because he hath prophesied against this city, as you have heard with your ears ». But these Pharisees, while they wished to kill Christ, by attacking the truth they had heard fulfilled that very truth, according to that of Acts, the thirteenth chapter[^p509-10]: « They that inhabited Jerusalem and the rulers thereof, not knowing him, nor the voices of the Prophets which are read every sabbath, judging him have fulfilled them; and finding no cause of death in him, they desired of Pilate that they might kill him. And when they had fulfilled all things that were written of him, taking him down from the tree, they laid him in a sepulchre ».

26. (Vers. 20.). Second, as to *the malignant lying in wait* he subjoins: *And being upon the watch, they sent liers in wait, who should feign themselves just,* against that of Proverbs, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p509-11]: « Lie not in wait, nor seek after wickedness in the house of the just, nor spoil his rest ». Now lyings in wait are made through dissemblings; against which, Job, the thirty-sixth chapter: « Dissemblers and crafty men prove the wrath of God »; because, Proverbs, the eleventh chapter, « the dissembler with his mouth deceiveth his friend ». — And because dissembling has a perverse intention joined to it, therefore he adds: *That they might take hold of him in his words, and*[^p509-12] *deliver him up to the authority and power of the governor,* namely of Herod. Whence it is said in Mark, the twelfth chapter: « They send to him some of the Pharisees and of the Herodians, that they should catch him in his words ». And this was
<!-- page 510 -->
great craftiness; whence the Gloss[^p510-1]: « What for fear of the people they cannot do of themselves they wish to do by the hands of the governor, so that they may seem as it were free from his death ». Therefore in the person of such it is said in Wisdom, the second chapter[^p510-2]: « Let us lie in wait for the just, because he is not for our turn, and he is contrary to our doings, and upbraideth us with transgressions of the law, and divulgeth against us the sins of our way of life ». Whence also in figure of this the Philistines said to Dalila, Judges, the sixteenth chapter: « Deceive him, and learn of him wherein he hath so great strength, and how we may be able to overcome him ».

27. (Vers. 21.). Third, as to *the malicious questioning* he subjoins: *And they asked him*[^p510-3]*, saying: Master, we know that thou speakest and teachest rightly.* As to *the word*, indeed, they say what is true, because John, the seventeenth chapter: « Thy word is truth ». This they said by flattering, that they might wring from him the secret of his heart. Whence Chrysostom[^p510-4]: « This is the first prudence of hypocrites, feigned praise, like one who cannot subdue a bull by strength and rubs its neck with soft hands, that he may take it by blandishments ». And of such Proverbs, the sixteenth chapter[^p510-5]: « An evil man allureth his friend, and leadeth him into a way that is not good »; and Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter: « He that blesseth his neighbour with a loud voice, rising in the night, shall be like to him that curseth ». — *And thou acceptest not the person of man*[^p510-6], as to *the mind;* and this is true, according to that of Acts, the tenth chapter: « In very deed I perceive that God is not a respecter of persons »; and Deuteronomy, the tenth chapter: « A great God and mighty and terrible, who accepteth no person nor bribes ». — *But thou teachest the way of God in truth,* as to *example;* on account of which he himself said, John, the fourteenth chapter[^p510-7]: « I am the way, the truth and the life ». — Or: *thou speakest rightly,* as to *the truth of life; thou acceptest not the person,* as regards *the truth of justice;* but *thou teachest the way of God in truth,* as regards *the truth of doctrine.* In which they praise Christ with true praise, but with a deceitful heart.

28. (Vers. 22.). And this is plain from their crafty questioning, when it is subjoined: *Is it lawful for us to give tribute to Caesar, or no?* according to the decree of the divine law; in which they propose a guileful question, because, whichever were granted, they intend to lead him to something unfitting. For either he would say yes, and so he would seem to speak against the Law and against the people of the Jews and the common opinion; or he would say no, and then they would deliver him up to the authority, as a rebel against Caesar and an enemy of the whole empire. Whence the Gloss[^p510-8]: « The Pharisees, who applauded themselves for justice, who gave tithes and first-fruits to God, denied that tributes were to be paid to man ». And hence it is that they flattered Christ, that they might wring from the mouth of Christ this instruction of the kind, so that afterwards they could say that of John, the nineteenth chapter[^p510-9]: « If thou release this man, thou art not Caesar's friend; for whosoever maketh himself a king speaketh against Caesar ».

29. (Vers. 23.). *But he considering their guile.* After he has described the fallacious devising of the craftiness, here second[^p510-10] he describes *the truthful confutation.* About which three things are brought in, namely *the circumspect consideration of the wisdom of the one answering, the truthful dissolving of the question proposed* and *the evident confutation of a wily cunning.*

First, then, as to *the circumspect consideration of the wisdom of the one answering* he says: *But he considering their guile,* in mind; whence Jerome[^p510-11]: « The first virtue of one answering is to know the minds of those questioning ». In their minds Wisdom itself saw the guile; whence Wisdom, the seventh chapter: « Malice overcometh not wisdom »; and Ecclesiasticus, the sixteenth chapter: « Every heart is understood by him, and his ways who shall understand »? because, to the Hebrews, the fourth chapter, « the word of God is living and effectual, and a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart ». — Therefore this Word *said to them: Why tempt you me?* namely in word, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirteenth chapter[^p510-12]: « By much talk he will tempt thee, and smiling will examine thee concerning thy secrets ». In the person of these it is said in Wisdom, the second chapter: « Let us prove what shall happen to him, and we shall know what his end shall be ».

30. (Vers. 24.). And because the wariness of the temptation is overcome through the manifestation of the truth, therefore it is subjoined: *Shew me a penny. Whose image and
<!-- page 511 -->
*superscription hath it*[^p511-1]*? They answering said to him: Caesar's,* namely in the outward sign. This the Lord does not ask because he doubts, but that from their answer he may confute them. — Or he asks the image and superscription of the penny for this, that from the very form and figure and quality of the thing he may show that the judgement of equity must flow. — Or he requires this for this, that he may show that each one is to be judged according to the superscription which he has written over the image of his mind. For by *the penny* is understood man, because a penny is of earthly matter, namely of metal, and man of slime; Genesis, the second chapter[^p511-2]: « God formed man of the slime of the earth »; and he is *the tenth* after the ninefold of the Angels, and is signified by *the one groat* above, the fifteenth chapter: « Or what woman » etc. By *the image of the penny* is understood the Trinity of persons, of which Ecclesiasticus, the seventeenth chapter[^p511-3]: « God created man of the earth, and made him after his own image »; and Genesis, the first chapter: « Let us make man to our image » etc. By *the superscription* is understood the quality of morals, and this is twofold: one *divine,* the other *worldly.* *The divine* is through the lumen[^tr-lumen] of grace and of justice; the Psalm[^p511-4]: « The lumen of thy countenance, O Lord, is signed upon us »; but *the worldly,* which is Caesar's, is through the appetite of cupidity and avarice. Of this Ambrose[^p511-5]: « The image of Caesar is not found save in the sea, where the dragons are »; in which he touches that which is read in Matthew, the seventeenth chapter, where Christ sent Peter to the sea; and he found a fish and in the fish a stater, which he paid to Caesar; in which it is understood that the superscription of earthliness is not save in worldly minds, in which, on account of various cupidities, there is the storm and the onrush of the tempest.

31. (Vers. 25.). Second, as to *the truthful dissolving of the question proposed* he subjoins: *And he said to them: Render therefore to Caesar the things that are Caesar's;* in which he plainly gives a just answer; for it is the act of justice « to render to each one what is his[^p511-6] ». If, then, a thing is his whose superscription it has; since temporal things have the superscription of Caesar, it is not against God if they are paid to earthly princes. Therefore it is said to the Romans, the thirteenth chapter[^p511-7]: « Render to all men their dues: tribute to whom tribute, custom to whom custom. Owe no man any thing, but to love one another ». — And because justice is not sufficient unless to each one his own is rendered; therefore he adds: *And to God the things that are God's;* the Gloss[^p511-8]: « As Caesar exacts the impression of his image, so God too exacts the soul marked with the lumen of his countenance »; on account of which the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter: « As we have borne the image of the earthly, so let us bear the image of the heavenly ».

32. And note that he puts first that the things of the world be rendered, because, as is said in the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter[^p511-9], « that was not first which is spiritual, but that which is natural; afterwards that which is spiritual ». Also, the weight is to be laid down before one is raised upward; whence the Gloss[^p511-10]: « If thou wilt not be beholden to Caesar, do not have the things of the world; and if thou hast riches, thou art beholden to Caesar. If thou wilt owe nothing to the earthly king, leave all thine, follow Christ. And well did he decree that the things of Caesar be rendered first, because no one can be the Lord's unless he first renounce the world »; above, the fourteenth chapter[^p511-11]: « Unless one renounce all that he possesseth, he cannot be my disciple ». But the soul and the body are God's, because he himself formed the body and the soul, the body of the slime of the earth, the soul of nothing; and therefore both are to be rendered and offered to God. Which is signified in that widow who cast « two brass mites into the treasury » of the Lord, below, the twenty-first chapter[^p511-12]. And therefore to the Romans, the twelfth chapter: « Present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, pleasing unto God, your reasonable service. And be not conformed to this world, but be reformed in the newness of your mind ». Whence in this word is enclosed the sum of all justice and perfection[^p511-13].

33. (Vers. 26.). Third, as to *the evident confutation of a wily cunning* he subjoins: *And they could not reprehend his word before the people,* by an apparent contradiction. Nor is it to be wondered at, because, to the Romans, the ninth chapter[^p511-14]: « Who art thou that repliest against
<!-- page 512 -->
God »? Whence Job, the ninth chapter: « If he examine on a sudden, who shall answer him? Or who can say to him: Why dost thou so »? — And therefore those who contend with Christ are of necessity confounded; on account of which he subjoins: *And wondering at his answer, they held their peace,* through evident confusion; whence the Gloss[^p512-1]: « Those who ought to have believed wondered that they have no place for tempting ». Whence Chrysostom[^p512-2]: « Malice is confounded sometimes, but corrected never; as one who wishes to shut the channel of running water, it breaks a path for itself elsewhere, so too malice ». Whence they did not hold their peace out of *prudence,* as is said in Ecclesiasticus, the twentieth chapter[^p512-3]: « There is one that holdeth his peace, that is found wise ». « A wise man will hold his peace until the time ». Nor out of *reverence,* like him of whom Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-second chapter: « Hear in silence, and for reverence good grace shall come to thee ». Nor out of *patience,* like him in Isaias, the forty-second chapter[^p512-4]: « I have held my peace, I have always kept silence, I have been patient ». Nor out of *inward peace;* Lamentations, the third chapter: « He shall sit solitary, and hold his peace ». But out of *open confusion,* like those whom the Lord asked, Mark, the third chapter[^p512-5]: « Is it lawful to do good on the sabbath days, or to do evil; to save life, or to destroy? But they held their peace »; and the first of Kings, the second chapter: « The wicked shall be silent in darkness » etc.
