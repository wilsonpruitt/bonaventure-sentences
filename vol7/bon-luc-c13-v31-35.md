# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XIII, Vers. 31-35
## *In ipsa autem die etc. Postquam excitavit ad faciendum incessanter opera bona propter severitatem districtionis in iudicando, hic secundo excitat ad idem ipsum propter sublimitatem dispositionis in redimendo*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 354 -->
64. *In ipsa autem die* etc. Postquam excitavit ad faciendum incessanter opera bona propter severitatem districtionis in iudicando, hic secundo excitat ad idem ipsum *propter sublimitatem dispositionis in redimendo*[^p354-7]. Habet autem pars ista partes duas, in quarum prima exprimitur sublimitas dispositionis divinae *in redemptione humani generis;* in secunda vero, *in reprobatione plebis rebellis,* ibi[^p354-8]: *Ierusalem, Ierusalem* etc.

*Sublimitas autem divinae dispositionis in redimendo* ostenditur tripliciter, scilicet quia passio Christi ante tempus fuit *frustra ab homine*[^p354-9] *intentata;* in tempore debito fuit *a Christo voluntarie suscepta;* ab aeterno vero fuit *infallibiliter praeordinata.*

65. (Vers. 31.). Primo igitur insinuatur passio Christi ab homine *ante tempus frustra intentata,* cum dicitur: *In ipsa die accesserunt quidam Pharisaeorum, dicentes illi: Exi et vade hinc*[^p354-10]*, quia Herodes vult te occidere.* Hic Herodes erat filius Herodis, qui, volens infantulum Iesum occidere ante tempus, interfecit pueros Bethlehemitas, secundum quod dicitur Matthaei secundo: « Tunc Herodes, videns, quoniam illusus esset a Magis, mittens occidit omnes pueros, qui erant in Bethlehem ». Sic et iste filius, paterni sceleris imitator, volebat eum occidere ante tempus, sicut interfecerat praecursorem suum; sicut dicitur Matthaei decimo quarto[^p354-11]: « Misit Herodes et decollavit Ioannem in carcere ». Sic etiam nunc facere volebat Christo iam adulto, et deinde nepos hoc facere voluit iam glorificato; unde primus Herodes fuit malus, secundus peior, sed tertius pessimus. Et omnes persecuti sunt Christum, sed frustra; quia dicitur in Psalmo[^p354-12]: « Dominus dissipat consilia gentium, reprobat autem cogitationes populorum et reprobat consilia principum ». — Et nota, quod Pharisaei hoc nuntiaverunt Christo, sive quia aliqui ex eis fortassis erant boni, sive quia volebant Christum deterrere et a se repellere; sive etiam, quia volebant eum tentare, si pro timore desisteret a praedicatione veritatis. Et illud est magis credibile, quia hoc consueverunt facere; unde Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p354-13]: « Abeuntes Pharisaei, consilium inierunt, ut Iesum caperent in sermone; et mittunt ei discipulos suos cum Herodianis ».

66. Et nota hic secundum *mysticum intellectum,* quod *Herodes* interpretatur glorians in pellibus[^p354-14], et designat quemlibet superbum et vanegloriosum, qui gloriatur in ostentatione exteriorum operum et verborum. Et talis *persequitur Christum nascentem* in Bethlehem, qui nascitur tanquam pauper, quia vanegloriosi consueverunt persequi pauperes Christi. Unde Bernardus in quodam sermone[^p354-15]: « Patet, quantum nocet iniqua potestas, quomodo caput impium subiectos quosque suae conformet impietati. Misera plane civitas, in qua regnat Herodes, quoniam Herodianae particeps erit malitiae. Herodiana vero malitia est nascentem velle exstinguere religionem. Si quid ergo ad salutem pertinens, si quid religionis
<!-- page 355 -->
exoritur, quicumque resistit, nascentem persequitur Salvatorem ».

67. (Vers. 32.). Secundo vero insinuatur passio Christi *ut ab ipso voluntarie suscepta,* cum subditur: *Et ait illis: Ite et dicite vulpi illi. Vulpem* vocat Herodem propter malitiam et dolositatem, quae tamen sapientiae Christi non praevalet; Sapientiae septimo[^p355-1]: « Sapientiam non vincit malitia. Attingit ergo a fine usque ad finem fortiter ». — Et quia mors Christi non erat consummanda secundum machinationem humanae malitiae, sed secundum dispositionem propriae sapientiae et voluntatis suae; ideo subdit: *Ecce, eiicio daemonia et sanitates perficio hodie et cras,* per miraculorum operationem; Actuum decimo[^p355-2]: « Pertransiit bene faciendo et sanando omnes oppressos a diabolo ». — *Et tertia die consummor,* per passionis perpessionem, in qua dixit Ioannis decimo nono[^p355-3]: « Consummatum est »; et ad Hebraeos secundo: « Decebat autem eum, propter quem omnia et per quem omnia, qui multos filios in gloriam adduxerat, per passionem consummari ». Unde per istos *tres dies* possunt intelligi tres anni, in quibus Dominus praedicavit et miracula fecit, et ita quod in ultimo voluntarie passus fuit et secundum suam voluntatem; Ioannis decimo[^p355-4]: « Potestatem habeo ponendi animam meam, et potestatem habeo iterum sumendi eam ». Unde quando noluit, nemo eum laedere potuit, secundum illud Ioannis septimo: « Nemo in eum manus misit, quia nondum venerat hora eius ». Unde vulpina malitia machinari potuit mortem Christi, verumtamen non potuit perficere, nisi sicut ipse voluit; de quo Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p355-5]: « Oblatus est, quia ipse voluit ». — Et per hoc etiam *spiritualiter intelligitur,* quod Christi *corpus mysticum* non patitur, nisi secundum quod Deus disponit, ab impiis haereticis, quos dat Christus intelligi per *Herodem,* attribuens ei nomen et proprietatem vulpis.

68. (Vers. 33.). Tertio insinuatur Christi passio *ut ab aeterno infallibiliter praeordinata;* unde subiungit: *Verumtamen oportet, me hodie et cras et sequenti die ambulare.* Opportunitas ista non fuit ex compulsione, sed ex superna dispositione, secundum illud infra ultimo[^p355-6]: « O stulti et tardi corde ad credendum in omnibus, quae locuti sunt Prophetae » etc. Hoc enim Deus praeviderat, secundum quod dicitur Actuum secundo: « Hunc definito consilio et praescientia Dei traditum, per manus iniquorum affligentes, interemistis ». Hoc etiam Prophetae praedixerunt, secundum quod dicitur Actuum tertio: « Deus autem, qui per os Prophetarum nuntiavit, pati Christum suum, sic implevit ». Istam opportunitatem divinae dispositionis insinuavit ipse in passione sua, cum oravit, sicut dicitur Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p355-7]: « Pater mi, si non potest transire hic calix, nisi bibam illum, fiat voluntas tua ».

69. Et quia divina dispositio non tantum praeordinavit tempus, verum etiam locum; ideo subdit: *Quia non capit, Prophetam perire extra Ierusalem; non capit,* « id est, non convenit[^p355-8] », non capit *Scriptura prophetalis,* vel non capit *divina providentia.* Propheta iste per excellentiam Christus est, qui cuncta futura praenoscit; de quo Deuteronomii decimo octavo[^p355-9]: « Prophetam de fratribus vestris sicut me suscitabit vobis Dominus »; et supra septimo: « Propheta magnus surrexit in nobis ». Istum non convenit mori nisi in Ierusalem, secundum Scripturas, et quia illa erat civitas regalis, magistralis et sacerdotalis, et Christus erat summus rex et magister et sacerdos. Et ideo non debebat mori nisi in illo loco; unde infra decimo octavo[^p355-10]: « Ecce, ascendimus Ierosolymam, et consummabuntur omnia, quae scripta sunt per Prophetas de Filio hominis ». Nam Prophetae hoc universaliter praedixerunt, quod de Ierusalem veniret lex nova; unde Isaiae secundo[^p355-11]: « Venite, ascendamus ad montem Domini et ad domum Dei Iacob; quia de Sion exibit lex, et verbum Domini de Ierusalem »; et Zachariae decimo tertio: « In die illa erit fons patens domui David et habitantibus Ierusalem in ablutionem peccatoris et menstruatae ». — Et nota, quod Christus in Ierusalem condemnatus est, sed extra portam passus est; unde ad Hebraeos decimo tertio[^p355-12]: « Iesus, ut sanctificaret per suum sanguinem populum, extra portam passus est ».

70. *Mystice* vero notandum est hic, quod istud triduum secundum diversos expositores exponitur
<!-- page 356 -->
tripliciter: *allegorice, moraliter* et *anagogice.* — *Allegorice* sic: prima dies est lex *naturae,* secunda est lex *Scripturae* et tertia est lex *gratiae;* de quo Marci octavo[^p356-1]: « Misereor super turbam, quia ecce, iam triduo sustinent me ». — Vel, primus dies est *passionis;* secundus, *sepulturae;* sed tertius, *resurrectionis;* Ioannis secundo[^p356-2]: « Solvite templum hoc, et in tribus diebus excitabo illud ». — Vel, prima dies est *decursus sextae aetatis;* secunda, *quies animarum;* sed tertia, *resurrectio corporum,* quae est in octava; Osee sexto[^p356-3]: « Vivificabit nos post duos dies et in die tertia suscitabit nos ».

71. *Moraliter* sic, ut prima dies sit *compunctionis;* secunda, *confessionis;* tertia, *satisfactionis;* Exodi quinto[^p356-4]: « Dominus Deus Hebraeorum vocavit nos; ibimus viam trium dierum in solitudinem ». — Vel, prima dies bona *cogitatio,* secunda est bona *locutio* et tertia est bona *operatio;* Ionae tertio[^p356-5] secundum translationem Septuaginta: « Adhuc tres dies, et Ninive subvertetur ». — Vel, tres dies sunt triplex votum religionis, scilicet *castitatis, obedientiae* et *paupertatis;* Genesis quadragesimo[^p356-6]: « Tres dies sunt, post quos recordabitur Pharao ministerii tui et restituet te in gradum pristinum ».

72. *Anagogice* sic, ut prima dies sit *purgatio;* secunda, *illuminatio,* et tertia, *perfectio;* supra secundo[^p356-7]: « Factum est, post triduum invenerunt eum in templo ». — Vel, ut prima dies sit contemplatio Dei in suo *vestigio,* secunda sit Dei in *imagine* sive in speculo, tertia, in *se ipso;* Numerorum decimo[^p356-8]: « Arca Domini praecedebat eos per tres dies, providens castrorum locum ». — Vel, prima dies sit contemplatio *hierarchiae subcaelestis;* secunda, *caelestis,* et tertia, *supercaelestis*[^p356-9]. In prima est *daemoniorum eiectio;* in secunda est *sanitatis perfectio,* sed in tertia, *omnis boni consummatio;* et de hoc triduo, Iosue secundo[^p356-10]: « Exploratores venerunt ad montana et manserunt ibi per tres dies ». — Haec *arca* Christus est, qui in qualibet istarum hierarchiarum est hierarcha altissimus et dux noster, ut veniamus ad terram promissionis nobis repromissam[^p356-11]. In cuius *figuram* dicit, se per *triduum ambulare,* quia facit nos per hanc triplicem hierarchiam semper sursum ascendere, nisi forte descendamus ad actiones. In cuius figuram Genesis vigesimo octavo[^p356-12] dicitur, quod vidit « Iacob Angelos Dei *ascendentes* et *descendentes* in scala »; nullus vidit eos *stantes.* In quo signatur, quod semper in bono proficiendum est. Hoc enim est appropinquare ad supernam Ierusalem, cui non appropinquamus passibus corporis, sed affectibus cordis et mentis.

73. (Vers. 34.). *Ierusalem, Ierusalem* etc. Postquam manifestavit sublimitatem divinae dispositionis in redemptione humani generis, hic secundo manifestat in *reprobatione plebis rebellis*[^p356-13]. Et primo insinuat *rebellionem,* deinde *obdurationem* et tertio *desertionem.*

Primo igitur quantum ad illius gentis *rebellionem* dicit: *Ierusalem, Ierusalem, quae occidis Prophetas et lapidas eos qui mittuntur ad te*[^p356-14]. Hic accipitur continens pro contento; Actuum septimo: « Dura cervice et incircumcisis cordibus et auribus, vos semper Spiritui sancto restitistis ». « Quem Prophetarum non sunt persecuti patres vestri »? Unde quarti Regum vigesimo primo: « Sanguinem innocentem fudit Manasses multum nimis, donec impleretur Ierusalem usque ad os ». Et hoc faciebant, quia habebant odio veritatem, secundum illud Amos
<!-- page 357 -->
quinto[^p357-1]: « Odio habuerunt corripientem in porta et loquentem perfecte abominati sunt ». Et hoc erat, quia nolebant argui; « qui enim male agit odit lucem et non venit ad lucem ». Propter quod dicitur Ezechielis secundo: « Fili hominis, mitto ego te ad gentes apostatrices, quae recesserunt a me; patres praevaricati sunt pactum meum, et filii dura cervice et indomabili corde sunt, ad quos ego mitto te ». Et hoc significat[^p357-2] ipsa *lapidatio.*

74. Secundo quantum ad illius gentis *obdurationem* subdit: *Quoties volui congregare filios tuos, quemadmodum avis nidum suum sub pennis, et noluisti*[^p357-3]*!* In quo nimia eorum apparet obduratio, quia tanta benignitate noluit emolliri. — Christus autem comparavit se *avi* in tantum[^p357-4], quia proprietatem multiplicem habet avis quantum ad *superabundantiam pietatis.* Comparatur autem *gallinae* in *conversatione,* quia gallina sua pietate et vigilantia movetur ad pullos et eos congregat et eos alis defendit contra rapacitatem milvi. Sic et Christus fecit, quamdiu in carne fuit; ideo Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p357-5]: « Quoties volui congregare filios tuos, sicut gallina congregat pullos suos sub alas, et noluisti »! — Comparatur *pellicano* in *passione,* quia pellicanus fertur pullos suos occisos triduo lugere et post rostro sanguinem suum fundere, et sic vivificare[^p357-6]. Hoc modo Christus ter flevit pro salute humani generis: primo super Lazarum, Ioannis undecimo; secundo super Ierusalem, infra decimo nono; tertio in cruce, ad Hebraeos quinto. Et post sanguinem suum fudit, de quo et nos vivificavit; unde Psalmus: « Similis factus sum pellicano solitudinis ». — Tertio comparatur *aquilae* in *resurrectione* et *ascensione,* quae pullos excitat, ut sursum tendant et in solem aspiciant et in sublimia volent. Et ut hoc possint, lambunt eius sanguinem; unde Iob trigesimo nono[^p357-7]: « Nunquid ad praeceptum tuum elevabitur aquila et in arduis ponet nidum suum » etc., usque ibi: « Pulli eius lambunt sanguinem ». Haec autem avis, licet dura sit extraneis, valde tamen intenta est providentiae suae prolis; propter quod merito assimilatur ei Christus, redemptor noster. Unde Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo[^p357-8]: « Eduxit eum et docuit et custodivit eum quasi pupillam oculi sui. Sicut aquila, provocans ad volandum pullos suos et super eos volitans, expandit alas suas et assumsit eum atque portavit in humeris suis ». — Hanc benignitatem recusare est summae obdurationis et perfidiae; propter quod Isaiae primo[^p357-9]: « Vae genti peccatrici, populo gravi iniquitate, semini nequam, filiis sceleratis! Dereliquerunt Dominum, blasphemaverunt Sanctum Israel, abalienati sunt retrorsum ».

75. (Vers. 35.). Tertio quantum ad illius gentis *desertionem* subdit: *Ecce, relinquetur vobis domus vestra deserta;* Isaiae primo[^p357-10]: « Terra vestra deserta, civitates vestrae succensae sunt igni ». « Et derelinquetur filia Sion ut umbraculum in vinea et sicut tugurium in cucumerario et velut civitas, quae vastatur ». — Et quoniam, sicut obdurationi coniungitur desertio, ita devotioni iuncta est visitatio; ideo subiungit: *Dico autem vobis: Quia non videbitis me, donec veniat, cum dicetis: Benedictus, qui venit in nomine Domini;* Psalmus[^p357-11]: « Benedictus, qui venit in nomine Domini. Benediximus vobis de domo Domini; Deus Dominus et illuxit nobis ». Istud autem referri potest ad *visionem fidei,* qua non videt, nisi qui credit eum verum hominem et verum Deum; Ioannis nono[^p357-12]: « Dixit Dominus caeco nato: Tu credis in Filium Dei? Respondit ille et dixit: Quis est, Domine, ut credam in eum? Et dixit ei Iesus: Et vidisti eum, et qui loquitur tecum, ipse est. Et ille ait: Credo, Domine; et procidens adoravit eum ». — Potest etiam referri ad *visionem speciei,* ad quam non pervenit, nisi qui benedicit vita et lingua; Isaiae vigesimo sexto[^p357-13]: « Tollatur impius, ne videat gloriam Dei », secundum aliam translationem; secundum autem nostram dicitur: « In terra Sanctorum iniqua gessit et non videbit gloriam Domini ».
<!-- page 358 -->
76. Et nota, quod, secundum quod exponit Glossa[^p358-1], loquitur hic de *visione in iudicio,* quia post visionem Christi in humilitate non exspectatur nisi visio in maiestate, et haec erit duplex: *Divinitatis* et *humanitatis: Divinitatis,* et haec erit bonorum omnino, quia habebit gaudium adiunctum maximum; Iob trigesimo tertio[^p358-2]: « Videbit faciem eius in iubilo, et reddet homini iustitiam suam »; et Isaiae trigesimo tertio: « Regem in decore suo videbunt » etc. Alia est visio *humanitatis,* et haec erit omnibus communis. Omnes enim certitudinaliter videbunt potentiam et dominium Christi; Apocalypsis primo[^p358-3]: « Ecce, veniet cum nubibus, et videbit eum omnis oculus, et qui eum pupugerunt ». Vult ergo Dominus dicere, quod ille populus Iudaicus non videbit Christum ad suam salutem, nisi convertatur ad fidem et ipsius laudem; quod tandem exspectatur in finali tempore post gentium plenitudinem; unde ad Romanos undecimo[^p358-4]: « Caecitas ex parte contigit in Israel, donec plenitudo gentium intraret, et sic omnis Israel salvus fieret »; et ad Romanos nono: « Isaias clamat pro Israel: Si fuerit numerus filiorum Israel tanquam arena maris, reliquiae salvae fient ». Et in hoc manifeste apparet altitudo dispensationis divinae, propter quam exclamat Apostolus ad Romanos undecimo[^p358-5]: « O altitudo divitiarum sapientiae et scientiae Dei! quam incomprehensibilia sunt iudicia eius et investigabiles viae eius »!

## English
<!-- page 354 -->
64. *In the same day* etc. After he stirred up to doing good works incessantly on account of the severity of the strictness in judging, here secondly he stirs up to the very same *on account of the sublimity of the disposition in redeeming*[^p354-7]. Now this part has two parts, in the first of which is expressed the sublimity of the divine disposition *in the redemption of the human race;* but in the second, *in the reprobation of the rebellious people,* there[^p354-8]: *Jerusalem, Jerusalem* etc.

*Now the sublimity of the divine disposition in redeeming* is shown in three ways, namely because the passion of Christ before the time was *in vain attempted by man*[^p354-9]*;* in the due time it was *voluntarily undertaken by Christ;* but from eternity it was *infallibly preordained.*

65. (Vers. 31.). First, then, is insinuated the passion of Christ *attempted in vain* by man *before the time,* when it is said: *The same day there came some of the Pharisees, saying to him: Depart, and get thee hence*[^p354-10]*, for Herod hath a mind to kill thee.* This Herod was the son of Herod who, wishing to kill the little child Jesus before the time, slew the children of Bethlehem, according to what is said in Matthew, the second chapter: « Then Herod, perceiving that he was deluded by the wise men, sending killed all the men children that were in Bethlehem ». So this son too, an imitator of his father's crime, wished to kill him before the time, as he had slain his forerunner; as is said in Matthew, the fourteenth chapter[^p354-11]: « Herod sent, and beheaded John in the prison ». So too now he wished to do to Christ, already grown up, and afterwards the grandson wished to do this to him when already glorified; whence the first Herod was evil, the second worse, but the third worst. And all persecuted Christ, but in vain; for it is said in the Psalm[^p354-12]: « The Lord bringeth to nought the counsels of nations; and he rejecteth the devices of people, and casteth away the counsels of princes ». — And note that the Pharisees announced this to Christ, either because some of them were perhaps good, or because they wished to deter Christ and drive him away from them; or else because they wished to tempt him, whether out of fear he would desist from the preaching of the truth. And this is the more credible, because they were accustomed to do this; whence Matthew, the twenty-second chapter[^p354-13]: « The Pharisees going, consulted among themselves how to insnare Jesus in his speech; and they send to him their disciples with the Herodians ».

66. And note here, according to the *mystical understanding,* that *Herod* is interpreted glorying in skins[^p354-14], and he signifies any proud and vainglorious man, who glories in the display of outward works and words. And such a one *persecutes Christ at his birth* in Bethlehem, who is born as a poor man, because the vainglorious are accustomed to persecute Christ's poor. Whence Bernard in a certain sermon[^p354-15]: « It is plain how much wicked power harms, how an impious head conforms all its subjects to its own impiety. Wretched indeed is the city in which Herod reigns, since it will be a partaker of Herodian malice. But Herodian malice is to wish to extinguish religion at its birth. If, then, anything pertaining to salvation, if anything of religion
<!-- page 355 -->
arises, whoever resists persecutes the Saviour at his birth ».

67. (Vers. 32.). But secondly is insinuated the passion of Christ *as voluntarily undertaken by him,* when it is subjoined: *And he said to them: Go and tell that fox. He calls* Herod *a fox* on account of his malice and guile, which nevertheless does not prevail against the wisdom of Christ; Wisdom, the seventh chapter[^p355-1]: « No evil can overcome wisdom. She reacheth therefore from end to end mightily ». — And because the death of Christ was not to be consummated according to the machination of human malice, but according to the disposition of his own wisdom and will; therefore he subjoins: *Behold, I cast out devils, and do cures today and tomorrow,* by the working of miracles; Acts, the tenth chapter[^p355-2]: « He went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed by the devil ». — *And the third day I am consummated,* by the suffering of the passion, in which he said, John, the nineteenth chapter[^p355-3]: « It is consummated »; and to the Hebrews, the second chapter: « For it became him, for whom are all things, and by whom are all things, who had brought many children into glory, to be consummated by his passion ». Whence by these *three days* can be understood the three years in which the Lord preached and worked miracles, and so that in the last he suffered voluntarily and according to his own will; John, the tenth chapter[^p355-4]: « I have power to lay down my life, and I have power to take it up again ». Whence when he did not will it, no one could harm him, according to that of John, the seventh chapter: « No man laid hands on him, because his hour was not yet come ». Whence the fox's malice could plot the death of Christ, but nevertheless could not accomplish it, save as he himself willed; of which Isaias, the fifty-third chapter[^p355-5]: « He was offered because it was his own will ». — And by this too is *spiritually understood* that Christ's *mystical body* does not suffer, save according as God disposes, from impious heretics, whom Christ gives to be understood by *Herod,* attributing to him the name and the property of a fox.

68. (Vers. 33.). Thirdly is insinuated Christ's passion *as infallibly preordained from eternity;* whence he subjoins: *Nevertheless I must walk today and tomorrow, and the day following.* This opportunity was not from compulsion, but from the supernal disposition, according to that below, the last chapter[^p355-6]: « O foolish, and slow of heart to believe in all things which the Prophets have spoken » etc. For God had foreseen this, according to what is said in Acts, the second chapter: « This same being delivered up by the determinate counsel and foreknowledge of God, you by the hands of wicked men have crucified and slain ». The Prophets too foretold this, according to what is said in Acts, the third chapter: « But God, who foreshewed by the mouth of the Prophets that his Christ should suffer, hath so fulfilled ». This opportunity of the divine disposition he himself insinuated in his passion, when he prayed, as is said in Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p355-7]: « My Father, if this chalice may not pass away, but I must drink it, thy will be done ».

69. And because the divine disposition preordained not only the time, but also the place; therefore he subjoins: *Because it cannot be that a Prophet perish out of Jerusalem; it cannot be,* « that is, it is not fitting[^p355-8] », the *prophetic Scripture* does not admit it, or *divine providence* does not admit it. This Prophet by excellence is Christ, who foreknows all things to come; of whom Deuteronomy, the eighteenth chapter[^p355-9]: « The Lord will raise up to you a Prophet of your brethren like unto me »; and above, the seventh chapter: « A great Prophet is risen up among us ». It is not fitting that this one die save in Jerusalem, according to the Scriptures, and because that was the royal, magisterial and priestly city, and Christ was the supreme king and master and priest. And therefore he ought not to die save in that place; whence below, the eighteenth chapter[^p355-10]: « Behold, we go up to Jerusalem, and all things shall be accomplished which were written by the Prophets concerning the Son of man ». For the Prophets universally foretold this, that the new law would come from Jerusalem; whence Isaias, the second chapter[^p355-11]: « Come, and let us go up to the mountain of the Lord, and to the house of the God of Jacob; for the law shall come forth from Sion, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem »; and Zacharias, the thirteenth chapter: « In that day there shall be a fountain open to the house of David, and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem, for the washing of the sinner, and of the unclean woman ». — And note that Christ was condemned in Jerusalem, but suffered without the gate; whence to the Hebrews, the thirteenth chapter[^p355-12]: « Jesus, that he might sanctify the people by his own blood, suffered without the gate ».

70. But *mystically* it is to be noted here that this space of three days is expounded according to diverse expositors
<!-- page 356 -->
in three ways: *allegorically, morally* and *anagogically.* — *Allegorically* thus: the first day is the law of *nature,* the second is the law of *Scripture* and the third is the law of *grace;* of which Mark, the eighth chapter[^p356-1]: « I have compassion on the multitude, for behold they have now been with me three days ». — Or, the first day is that *of the passion;* the second, *of the burial;* but the third, *of the resurrection;* John, the second chapter[^p356-2]: « Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up ». — Or, the first day is *the course of the sixth age;* the second, *the rest of souls;* but the third, *the resurrection of bodies,* which is in the eighth; Osee, the sixth chapter[^p356-3]: « He will revive us after two days; on the third day he will raise us up ».

71. *Morally* thus, so that the first day is that *of compunction;* the second, *of confession;* the third, *of satisfaction;* Exodus, the fifth chapter[^p356-4]: « The Lord God of the Hebrews hath called us; we will go three days' journey into the wilderness ». — Or, the first day is good *thought,* the second is good *speech* and the third is good *work;* Jonas, the third chapter[^p356-5], according to the translation of the Septuagint: « Yet three days, and Ninive shall be destroyed ». — Or, the three days are the threefold vow of religion, namely *of chastity, of obedience* and *of poverty;* Genesis, the fortieth chapter[^p356-6]: « Three days there are, after which Pharao will remember thy service, and will restore thee to thy former place ».

72. *Anagogically* thus, so that the first day is *purgation;* the second, *illumination,* and the third, *perfection;* above, the second chapter[^p356-7]: « It came to pass, that, after three days, they found him in the temple ». — Or, so that the first day is the contemplation of God in his *vestige,* the second is that of God in his *image* or in a mirror, the third, in *himself;* Numbers, the tenth chapter[^p356-8]: « The ark of the Lord went before them for three days, providing a place for the camp ». — Or, the first day is the contemplation *of the subcelestial hierarchy;* the second, *of the celestial,* and the third, *of the supercelestial*[^p356-9]. In the first is *the casting out of devils;* in the second is *the perfecting of health,* but in the third, *the consummation of every good;* and of this space of three days, Josue, the second chapter[^p356-10]: « The spies came to the mountains, and stayed there three days ». — This *ark* is Christ, who in each of these hierarchies is the most high hierarch and our leader, that we may come to the land of promise promised again to us[^p356-11]. In *figure* of this he says that he *walks* for *three days,* because he makes us through this threefold hierarchy always ascend upward, unless perhaps we descend to actions. In figure of this, Genesis, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p356-12], it is said that « Jacob saw the Angels of God *ascending* and *descending* by the ladder »; no one saw them *standing.* In which it is signified that one must always make progress in good. For this is to draw near to the supernal Jerusalem, to which we do not draw near by the steps of the body, but by the affections of the heart and mind.

73. (Vers. 34.). *Jerusalem, Jerusalem* etc. After he manifested the sublimity of the divine disposition in the redemption of the human race, here secondly he manifests it in *the reprobation of the rebellious people*[^p356-13]. And first he insinuates the *rebellion,* then the *obduracy* and thirdly the *desertion.*

First, then, as to the *rebellion* of that nation he says: *Jerusalem, Jerusalem, that killest the Prophets, and stonest them that are sent to thee*[^p356-14]. Here the container is taken for the contained; Acts, the seventh chapter: « You stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, you always have resisted the Holy Ghost ». « Which of the Prophets have not your fathers persecuted »? Whence the fourth of Kings, the twenty-first chapter: « Manasses shed also very much innocent blood, till he filled Jerusalem up to the mouth ». And they did this because they held the truth in hatred, according to that of Amos,
<!-- page 357 -->
the fifth chapter[^p357-1]: « They have hated him that rebuketh in the gate, and have abhorred him that speaketh perfectly ». And this was because they were unwilling to be reproved; « for he that doth evil hateth the light, and cometh not to the light ». On account of which it is said in Ezechiel, the second chapter: « Son of man, I send thee to the apostatizing nations, that have departed from me; the fathers have transgressed my covenant, and the children are of a hard neck, and of an obstinate heart, to whom I send thee ». And this the *stoning* itself signifies[^p357-2].

74. Secondly, as to the *obduracy* of that nation he subjoins: *How often would I have gathered thy children, as the bird doth her brood under her wings, and thou wouldst not*[^p357-3]*!* In which their excessive obduracy appears, because it would not be softened by so great kindness. — But Christ compared himself to a *bird* in so far[^p357-4] as a bird has a manifold property as to *the superabundance of piety*[^tr-pietas]. Now he is compared *to the hen* in *conversation,* because the hen by her piety and vigilance is moved toward her chicks, and gathers them and defends them with her wings against the rapacity of the kite. So too Christ did, as long as he was in the flesh; therefore Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p357-5]: « How often would I have gathered together thy children, as the hen doth gather her chickens under her wings, and thou wouldst not »! — He is compared *to the pelican* in *the passion,* because the pelican is said to mourn its slain young for three days and afterwards to shed its blood with its beak, and so to revive them[^p357-6]. In this manner Christ wept three times for the salvation of the human race: first over Lazarus, John, the eleventh chapter; secondly over Jerusalem, below, the nineteenth chapter; thirdly on the cross, to the Hebrews, the fifth chapter. And afterwards he shed his blood, by which he also revived us; whence the Psalm: « I am become like to a pelican of the wilderness ». — Thirdly, he is compared *to the eagle* in *the resurrection* and *ascension,* which rouses its young to tend upward and to look into the sun and to fly to the heights. And that they may be able to do this, they lick its blood; whence Job, the thirty-ninth chapter[^p357-7]: « Will the eagle mount up at thy command, and make her nest in high places » etc., as far as there: « Her young ones shall suck up blood ». Now this bird, though she be hard to strangers, is nevertheless very intent on the providing for her offspring; on account of which Christ, our redeemer, is deservedly likened to her. Whence Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter[^p357-8]: « He led him about, and taught him, and he kept him as the apple of his eye. As the eagle enticing her young to fly, and hovering over them, he spread his wings, and hath taken him and carried him on his shoulders ». — To refuse this kindness is of the uttermost obduracy and perfidy; on account of which Isaias, the first chapter[^p357-9]: « Woe to the sinful nation, a people laden with iniquity, a wicked seed, ungracious children! They have forsaken the Lord, they have blasphemed the Holy One of Israel, they are gone away backwards ».

75. (Vers. 35.). Thirdly, as to the *desertion* of that nation he subjoins: *Behold, your house shall be left to you desolate;* Isaias, the first chapter[^p357-10]: « Your land is desolate, your cities are burnt with fire ». « And the daughter of Sion shall be left as a covert in a vineyard, and as a lodge in a garden of cucumbers, and as a city that is laid waste ». — And since, as desertion is conjoined to obduracy, so visitation is joined to devotion; therefore he subjoins: *And I say to you, that you shall not see me till the time come, when you shall say: Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord;* the Psalm[^p357-11]: « Blessed be he that cometh in the name of the Lord. We have blessed you out of the house of the Lord; the Lord is God, and he hath shone upon us ». Now this can be referred to *the vision of faith,* by which none sees save he who believes him true man and true God; John, the ninth chapter[^p357-12]: « The Lord said to the man born blind: Dost thou believe in the Son of God? He answered, and said: Who is he, Lord, that I may believe in him? And Jesus said to him: Thou hast both seen him; and it is he that talketh with thee. And he said: I believe, Lord; and falling down, he adored him ». — It can also be referred to *the vision of sight,* at which none arrives save he who blesses by life and tongue; Isaias, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p357-13]: « Let the wicked be taken away, that he may not see the glory of God », according to another translation; but according to ours it is said: « In the land of the saints he hath done wicked things, and he shall not see the glory of the Lord ».
<!-- page 358 -->
76. And note that, according as the Gloss[^p358-1] expounds, he speaks here of *the vision in the judgment,* because after the vision of Christ in humility there is not awaited save the vision in majesty, and this will be twofold: *of the Divinity* and *of the humanity:* *of the Divinity,* and this will be of the good alone, because it will have the greatest joy joined to it; Job, the thirty-third chapter[^p358-2]: « He shall see his face with joy, and he will render to man his justice »; and Isaias, the thirty-third chapter: « They shall see the king in his beauty » etc. The other is the vision *of the humanity,* and this will be common to all. For all will see with certitude the power and dominion of Christ; the Apocalypse, the first chapter[^p358-3]: « Behold, he cometh with the clouds, and every eye shall see him, and they also that pierced him ». The Lord, then, means to say that that Jewish people will not see Christ unto its salvation, unless it be converted to faith and to his praise; which at length is awaited in the final time after the fulness of the Gentiles; whence to the Romans, the eleventh chapter[^p358-4]: « Blindness in part has happened in Israel, until the fulness of the Gentiles should come in, and so all Israel should be saved »; and to the Romans, the ninth chapter: « Isaias crieth out concerning Israel: If the number of the children of Israel be as the sand of the sea, a remnant shall be saved ». And in this the depth of the divine dispensation manifestly appears, on account of which the Apostle exclaims, to the Romans, the eleventh chapter[^p358-5]: « O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and of the knowledge of God! how incomprehensible are his judgments, and how unsearchable his ways »!
