# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. VIII, Vers. 40-48
## *Factum est autem, cum rediisset Iesus etc. Supra ostendit Evangelista dominium et auctoritatem Christi super creaturam corporalem in sedatione tempestatis et super spiritualem in expulsione daemonis; hic ostendit, ipsum dominium habere super creaturam compositam ex utraque in sanatione haemorrhoissae et suscitatione puellae*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 208 -->
72. *Factum est autem, cum rediisset Iesus* etc. Supra[^p208-10] ostendit Evangelista dominium et auctoritatem Christi super creaturam corporalem in sedatione tempestatis et super spiritualem in expulsione daemonis; hic ostendit, ipsum dominium habere super *creaturam compositam ex utraque* in sanatione haemorrhoissae et suscitatione puellae. Habet autem haec pars duas, secundum quod humana natura ex duobus est composita, corpore videlicet et anima. Nam primo ostenditur *dominus corporum* in haemorrhoissae sanatione; secundo, *dominus animarum* in puellae suscitatione, ibi[^p208-11]: *Adhuc illo loquente* etc. Introducitur autem explicatio utriusque miraculi ad manifestandam dignitatem virtutis Christi, quam demonstrat satis perfecte in miraculo curationis haemorrhoissae.
<!-- page 209 -->
Nam primo demonstratur in parte ista *benignitas praesentiae Christi desiderabilis;* secundo vero, *sublimitas potentiae inaestimabilis,* ibi[^p209-1]: *Et mulier quaedam erat in fluxu sanguinis* etc.; tertio, *profunditas sapientiae ineffabilis,* ibi: *Et ait Iesus: Quis est qui me tetigit* etc.

*Benignitas* autem *praesentiae Christi* ostenditur *desiderabilis* ex *plebe suscipiente cum laetitia,* ex *principe supplicante cum fiducia,* ex *multitudine concomitante cum benevolentia.* Primum respicit Christi adventum, cui plebs *occurrit;* secundum, Christi statum, ad quem princeps *recurrit;* tertium, Christi progressum, ad quem multitudo *concurrit.* Ex occursu ergo et recursu et concursu multitudinis multipliciter ostenditur praesentia et adventus Christi desiderabilis.

73. (Vers. 40.). Primo igitur quantum ad *susceptionem laetitiae factam a plebe* dicitur: *Factum est autem, cum rediisset Iesus,* trans fretum scilicet in Galilaeam, cui ad tempus corporalem subtraxerat praesentiam desideratam; iuxta quod figuratum est tertii Regum decimo[^p209-2] in Salomone: « Et universa terra desiderabat videre vultum Salomonis, ut audiret sapientiam eius, quam dederat ei Deus ». Per istum *Salomonem* intelligimus Christum, de quo in Psalmo: « Speciosus forma prae filiis hominum » etc. — Et ideo subditur: *Excepit illum turba multa*[^p209-3], scilicet cum laetitia et gaudio, ut sic impleretur illud Isaiae vigesimo nono: « Pauperes homines in sancto Israel exsultabunt ». — Et ideo subditur: *Erant enim omnes exspectantes eum,* scilicet cum desiderio, quia, Threnorum tertio[^p209-4], « bonum est praestolari cum silentio salutare Dei ». Nam « bonus Dominus sperantibus in eum, animae quaerenti illum »; « ipse enim erit exspectatio gentium », Genesis penultimo; Psalmus: « Exspecta igitur Dominum et viriliter age » etc.

74. (Vers. 41.). Secundo quantum ad *supplicationem fiduciae factam a principe* subditur: *Et ecce, venit vir, cui nomen Iairus;* cuius nomen exprimit, eo quod persona erat notabilis ex principatu, iuxta quod dicitur secundi Regum septimo[^p209-5]: « Feci tibi nomen grande iuxta nomen magnorum, qui sunt in terra »; vel etiam, quia nomen respondebat officio et dignitati. Iairus enim *illuminatus* interpretatur, et talis in plebe debet principari. — Unde subditur: *Et ipse princeps synagogae erat;* Ecclesiastici quadragesimo quinto[^p209-6]: « Statuit illi testamentum pacis et principem sanctorum et gentis suae ». Hic autem princeps, licet esset exaltatus in gloria, ita ut ei posset competere illud primi Regum secundo[^p209-7]: « Ut sedeat cum principibus et solium gloriae teneat »; non inde est elatus per superbiam, sed humiliatus per *reverentiam,* attendens illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo secundo: « Rectorem te posuerunt? Noli extolli ». — Ideo subditur: *Et cecidit ad pedes Iesu,* sciens scriptum, Isaiae quadragesimo quinto[^p209-8]: « Mihi curvabitur omne genu, et iurabit omnis lingua »; et in Psalmo: « Venite, adoremus et procidamus ante Dominum ». Ideo autem procidit, ut Christi maiestatem honoret sua reverentia; Ecclesiastici tertio[^p209-9]: « Quam magna potentia Dei solius, et ab humilibus honoratur »; et ut etiam imploret pietatem cum fiducia. — Unde et subdit: *Rogans*[^p209-10]*, ut intraret in domum eius.* Hoc faciebat cum *instantia,* ut posset dicere illud Canticorum tertio: « Tenui eum nec dimittam, donec introducam illum in domum matris meae ».

75. (Vers. 42.). Ad istam autem instantiam propellebat *indigentia,* quam exprimit, cum dicit: *Quia unica filia erat ei,* et ita multum amabilis. Nam, secundi Regum primo[^p209-11], « sicut mater unicum amat filium suum, ita te diligebam », dixit David. Non solum autem erat amabilis, quia *unica,* sed etiam quia *iuvencula;* et ideo addit: *Fere annorum duodecim;* et quia tenera, ideo tenero amore amanda, secundum illud Genesis trigesimo septimo[^p209-12]: « Israel autem diligebat Ioseph super omnes filios suos, eo quod in senectute genuisset eum ». Et quia vita erat multum amabilis, ideo mors erat multum tristabilis. — Propter quod subdit: *Et haec moriebatur;* quod non poterat esse sine magno dolore, qui est in luctu unicae; Ieremiae sexto[^p209-13]: « Luctum unigeniti fac tibi planctum amarum ». Ideo dicebat Iacob de Beniamin Genesis quadragesimo secundo: « Non descendet, inquit, filius meus vobiscum; frater enim suus mortuus est, et ipse solus remansit. Si quid adversi acciderit in via, deducetis canos meos cum dolore ad inferos ». Sic et hic dicere poterat de unica filia.

76. Quoniam igitur Christi praesentiam adiit cum reverentia, rogavit ex confidentia, desideravit ex indigentia; ideo oratio eius fuit exaudita, secundum
<!-- page 210 -->
illud Ecclesiastici quarto[^p210-1]: « Rogationem contribulati ne abiicias ». Hinc est, quod Dominus non reprehendit istum qui rogabat, quod iret ad domum, sicut regulum, Ioannis quarto; quia regulus credebat, quod magis posset infirmum sanare quam mortuum suscitare, unde dixit: « Domine, descende, priusquam moriatur filius meus ». Hic autem credidit, quod aeque bene posset super utrumque, unde filiam suam quasi mortuam reputabat. — Et ideo alius Evangelista, scilicet Matthaeus[^p210-2], introducit eum rogantem pro mortua, licet secundum Lucam et Marcum aliquid superesset de vita. Unde Augustinus de Concordia Evangelistarum dicit, quod Marcus et Lucas expresserunt principis verbum, Matthaeus vero intellectum expressit, quia eam mortuam reputabat.

77. Tertio quantum ad *comitatum benevolentiae factum a multitudine* subditur: *Et contigit, dum iret, a turbis comprimebatur;* quoniam ex amoris affectu omnes volebant contingere ipsum, sicut supra sexto[^p210-3]: « Omnis turba quaerebat eum tangere ». In hoc etiam versa vice ostenditur benevolentia Salvatoris; qui etiam a multitudine vult comprimi, quasi nullum a se repellens. Ipse enim est, qui *dicit* Ioannis sexto[^p210-4]: « Omnis, qui venerit ad me, non eiiciam foras »; ipse, qui *vocat* Matthaei undecimo: « Venite ad me, omnes, qui laboratis et onerati estis, et ego reficiam vos »; ipse est, qui *ad ostium pulsat;* Apocalypsis tertio: « Ego sto ad ostium et pulso ». Hanc compressionem facit mutuus amor, qui est copula amantis et amati; quia, primae ad Corinthios sexto[^p210-5], « qui adhaeret Domino, unus spiritus est »; et Ioannis decimo quinto: « Manete in me, et ego in vobis ». Unde perfectus amor non est contentus aliquo sensu sine tactu; propter quod dicit sponsa Canticorum primo[^p210-6]: « Trahe me post te »; Ieremiae trigesimo primo: « In caritate perpetua dilexi te, ideo attraxi te ».

78. (Vers. 43.). *Et mulier quaedam erat in fluxu* etc. Postquam ostensa est praesentia Christi desiderabilis, ostenditur *potentia eius admirabilis* in mirabili curatione a fluxu sanguinis. Hanc autem curationem ostendit Evangelista processisse ex admirabili omnipotentia tum propter *diuturnitatem morbi* in muliere, tum propter *impossibilitatem curandi* ab arte, tum propter *facilitatem remedii* a Salvatore.

Primo igitur quantum ad *diuturnitatem immundae passionis* in muliere dicitur: *Et mulier quaedam erat in fluxu sanguinis ab annis duodecim.* Haec erat immunda passio, et ideo cum difficultate portabilis; Levitici decimo quinto[^p210-7]: « Mulier, quae patitur fluxum sanguinis, si fluere non cessat, quamdiu huic passioni subiacet, immunda est ». Erat cum difficultate sanabilis propter adhaerentiam, quia iam erat inveterata; Ecclesiastici decimo: « Languor prolixior gravat medicum ». Erat igitur haec passio immunda, continua et diuturna; ideo vilis ad cohabitandum, gravis ad sustinendum et difficilis ad sanandum. Unde poterat dicere illud Threnorum tertio[^p210-8]: « Vetustam fecit pellem meam et carnem meam »; et illud Psalmi: « Lumbi mei impleti sunt illusionibus, et non est sanitas in carne mea ». Haec autem mulier secundum Ambrosium videtur fuisse Martha. Ait enim[^p210-9]: « Christus largum fluxum sanguinis siccavit in Martha et daemones expulit de Maria ».

79. Secundo quantum ad *impossibilitatem humanae subventionis* ex arte subditur: *Quae in medicos erogaverat omnem substantiam suam,* propter curationis remedium, secundum consilium Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo[^p210-10]: « Honora medicum propter necessitatem, etenim illum creavit Altissimus ». Hoc autem dicit de honore *munerum,* non sermonum; unde haec eos honoraverat, quia omnem substantiam expenderat. Et ideo probabile est, quodsi curabilis fuisset aegritudo, quod per artem esset curata. — Attamen ars non profuit; unde subdit: *Nec ab ullo potuit curari.* Ita nec a natura nec ab arte potuit ei subveniri, ut verificaretur in ea illud Sapientiae decimo sexto[^p210-11]: « Neque herba neque malagma sanavit eos, sed tuus, Domine, sermo, qui sanat omnia ».

80. Unde nota, quod natura nihil potest sine superna potentia, nec ars aliquid potest sine natura; unde ubi natura non subvenit, ars non proficit, sed magis nocet, sicut in hac muliere, de qua Marci quinto[^p210-12] dicitur, quod « fuerat multa perpessa a
<!-- page 211 -->
pluribus medicis et erogaverat omnia sua, nec quidquam profecerat, sed magis deterius habebat ». Sed Lucas medicus hoc tacet, de medicis curialius loquens. — Ex quo colligitur, quod medicorum ars est *laudabilis,* quando sequitur supernam potentiam et naturam substratam. Unde dicitur Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo[^p211-1]: « Da locum medico, ne recedat a te ». Sed tunc erit *vituperabilis* operatio medici, quando negligit vel considerare naturam, vel non anteponit divinam potentiam, sicut dicitur de Asa secundi Paralipomenon decimo sexto[^p211-2]: « Aegrotavit Asa dolore pedum vehementer nec sic Dominum requisivit, sed magis medicorum arte confisus est »; et sequitur, quod « mortuus est ».

81. (Vers. 44.). Tertio quantum ad *facilitatem perfectae curationis* a Salvatore subditur: *Accessit retro et tetigit fimbriam vestimenti eius. Accessit,* quia *credebat;* ad Hebraeos undecimo[^p211-3]: « Oportet accedentem credere »; *retro,* quia *timebat,* sicut soror eiusdem Maria, supra septimo: « Stans retro secus pedes Domini ». *Tetigit,* quia *speravit curari;* Matthaei nono: « Dicebat intra se: Si tetigero fimbriam vestimenti, salva ero ». *Tetigit* autem *fimbriam*[^p211-4], id est infimam partem *vestimenti,* reputans se immundam et honorificans Christi potentiam, qui potest salutem dare per infima. — Et quia credidit et speravit, ideo faciliter obtinuit. Propter hoc additur: *Et confestim stetit fluxus sanguinis eius;* ex quo apparet mira Dei virtus, quae morbum tam diuturnum et tam gravem curavit statim ad tactum unius particulae. Mira est ergo virtus Christi, quae credulitate sui impossibilia facit facilia, secundum illud Marci nono[^p211-5]: « Si potes credere, omnia possibilia sunt credenti »; et Ioannis decimo quarto: « Qui credit in me, opera, quae ego facio, et ipse faciet et maiora horum faciet »; et Actuum decimo nono: « Virtutes non modicas faciebat Deus per manum Pauli, ut etiam de corpore eius deferrentur sudaria et semicinctia, et recedebant ab eis languores ». Si ergo tam salubre est per fidem tangere fimbriam vestimenti, quam salubre erit ipsum Christum tenere? Unde Bernardus[^p211-6]: « Dulce satis est te, bone Iesu, quaerere, dulcius tenere »; unde sponsa Canticorum tertio: « Tenui eum nec dimittam ». « Dulcis utique est attrectatio tui; nam et ipse tactus fructu non caret. Mulier enim evangelica felici furto *attigit fimbriam* Iesu, *et statim stetit* in illa *fluxus* carnalis illecebrae, carnalis delectationis et curae ».

82. (Vers. 45.). *Et ait Iesus* etc. Postquam ostensa est Christi praesentia esse desiderabilis et potentia admirabilis, hic tertio ostenditur eius *sapientia infallibilis;* et hoc tripliciter, scilicet ex *deprehensione veritatis occultae,* ex *detectione veritatis deprehensae,* ex *approbatione veritatis detectae.*

Primo igitur introducitur *deprehensio veritatis occultae* a Christo per propriam inquisitionem, cum dicitur: *Et ait Iesus: Quis*[^p211-7] *me tetigit?* Hoc autem non dicit, ut discat, sed ut quod notum est sibi aliis detegat; iuxta quod in Psalmo dicitur: « Palpebrae eius interrogant filios hominum, Dominus interrogat iustum et impium »; non ut ab eis aliquid addiscat, sed ut doceat quod ignorant. — Unde quia ignorabant quaestionis causam, subditur: *Negantibus autem omnibus, dixit Petrus, et qui cum illo erant,* id est alii discipuli, causam huius quaestionis non intelligentes; unde poterat eis dici illud Matthaei decimo quinto[^p211-8]: « Adhuc et vos sine intellectu estis »? quia quaestionem illam carnaliter, non spiritualiter intelligebant. — Quod patet in hoc quod adducit: *Praeceptor, turbae te comprimunt et affligunt, et tu*[^p211-9] *dicis: Quis me tetigit?* quasi dicant: cum omnes te tangant indifferenter, quomodo quaeris de aliquo specialiter? Unde mirabantur, quomodo Christus, magister et praeceptor, quaereret quasi de dubio quod omnibus erat in manifesto; et ideo merito mirabantur, quomodo Christus proponeret quaestionem, quae apparet stulta, cum dicatur secundae ad Timotheum secundo[^p211-10]: « Stultas et sine disciplina quaestiones devita ». Sed quia intellectum habebant carnalem, manuducendi erant ad spiritualem.

83. (Vers. 46.). Propter quod addit: *Et dixit Iesus: Aliquis me tetigit,* id est aliqua persona, tactu scilicet spirituali; et hoc probat: *Nam et*[^p211-11] *ego novi, virtutem de me exiisse.* Hoc dicit quantum ad effectum curationis; et sic verificatur illud Sapientiae duodecimo: « Virtutem autem tuam ostendis,
<!-- page 212 -->
qui non crederis in virtute consummatus ». Ostendit autem effectum egredientem ab ipso, secundum illud quod supra sexto dicitur, quod « virtus de illo exibat et sanabat omnes ». Nec mirum, quia, primae ad Corinthios primo[^p212-1], « Christum dicimus Dei virtutem et Dei sapientiam »; ad Hebraeos primo: « Portans omnia verbo virtutis suae ».

84. (Vers. 47.). Secundo vero adiungitur *detectio veritatis deprehensae* per alienam confessionem, cum dicitur: *Videns autem mulier, quia non latuit eum*[^p212-2]*;* unde poterat dicere illud mulieris Samaritanae, Ioannis quarto: « Domine, ut video, Propheta es tu ». Propter quod et ipsa poterat simile testimonium cum illa perhibere et ideo dicere: « Venite et videte hominem, qui dixit mihi omnia, quaecumque feci ». Iste vere erat alter Salomon, immo sapientior eo, de quo dicitur tertii Regum decimo[^p212-3]: « Non fuit sermo qui regem posset latere »; quia ipse est, de quo Danielis secundo: « Ipse novit in tenebris constituta, et lux cum eo est ». Et quia iam cognoverat potentiam in effectu et sapientiam percipiebat in sermone, ideo attendebat, se non posse effugere. — Et propterea subditur: *Et*[^p212-4] *tremens venit et procidit ad pedes eius,* ad honorificandam Maiestatem; Michaeae sexto: « Quid dignum offeram Domino? Curvabo genu Deo excelso ». Et hoc cum *tremore,* quia solos tales respicit Deus; Isaiae ultimo[^p212-5]: « Ad quem respiciam nisi ad pauperculum et trementem sermones meos »? Et ideo ad Philippenses secundo: « Cum metu et tremore vestram salutem operamini, quia Deus operatur in vobis et velle et perficere pro bona voluntate ». — Et quia nihil magis spectat ad honorem Maiestatis quam manifestatio veritatis, ideo subditur: *Et ob quam causam tetigerit eum, indicavit coram omni populo,* scilicet *quomodo confestim sanata est*[^p212-6]*;* quia, Tobiae duodecimo, « opera Dei revelare et confiteri honorificum est ». Et ideo dicebat Angelus: « Benedicite Deum caeli et coram omnibus viventibus confitemini ei, quia fecit vobiscum misericordiam suam ».

85. (Vers. 48.). Tertio adiungitur *approbatio veritatis detectae* per definitivam commendationem, cum subditur: *At ipse dixit illi*[^p212-7]*: Filia, fides tua te salvam fecit,* quasi dicat: iuste hoc assecuta es, quia credidisti; quasi dicat: fides fecit te *filiam,* secundum illud Ioannis primo: « Dedit eis potestatem filios Dei fieri, his qui credunt in nomine eius ». Fides etiam fecit *salvam,* quia, ad Romanos decimo, « omnis, quicumque invocaverit nomen Domini, salvus erit ». Unde in hoc apparet mira dispensatio nostrae salutis, quia *mulier sanata* totum attribuit *divinae virtuti,* secundum illud ad Romanos nono[^p212-8]: « Non est volentis neque currentis, sed Dei miserentis »; *Deus salvans* totum attribuit *merito fidei,* sicut dicitur Marci nono: « Si potestis credere, omnia possibilia sunt credenti ». — Et quoniam in acquisitione salutis per consequens fit acquisitio *pacis,* ideo subditur: *Vade in pace,* tanquam Dei amica et filia; Sapientiae tertio[^p212-9]: « Donum et pax est electis Dei ». Pax est illud quod Dominus omnibus *optat* et optari mandat, secundum illud infra decimo: « In quamcumque domum intraveritis, primum dicite: Pax huic domui ». Hoc est, quod prae aliis *donat;* Ioannis decimo quarto[^p212-10]: « Pacem relinquo vobis, pacem meam do vobis »; hoc est, ad quod specialiter *vocat;* Matthaei undecimo: « Tollite iugum meum super vos, et invenietis requiem »; et Ioannis decimo sexto: « Haec locutus sum vobis, ut in me pacem habeatis. In mundo pressuram habebitis, sed confidite, ego vici mundum ». De hac ad Philippenses quarto: « Pax Dei, quae exsuperat omnem sensum, custodiat corda vestra et intelligentias vestras in Christo Iesu », Domino meo.

86. Et nota, quod trium mulierum Dominus fidem commendat, quas exaudit et salvat. Commendavit Magdalenam supra septimo[^p212-11], dicens consimile verbum: « Fides tua te salvam fecit »; et hanc commendat hic; et mulierem Chananaeam, Matthaei decimo quinto, cui dixit: « Mulier, magna est fides tua ». — In his tribus mulieribus tres poenitentiae partes *spiritualiter* intelliguntur. Nam prima, quae flevit et lacrymis rigavit, dat intelligere *contritionem;* Chananaea, quae clamavit, dat intelligere *confessionem;* haec autem, quae tetigit vestimentum, dat intelligere *satisfactionem.* De Magdalena legitur supra septimo[^p212-12], quod « stans retro secus pedes Domini,
<!-- page 213 -->
lacrymis coepit rigare » etc. De Chananaea Matthaei decimo quinto dicunt discipuli: « Dimitte eam, quia clamat post nos ». De ista dicitur hic, quod « accessit retro et tetigit fimbriam eius vestimenti ». Per *tactum* autem *vestimenti* intelligitur imitatio in labore sanctae conversationis. — Unde nota, quod legimus Christum tactum in *pede* a Magdalena, supra septimo[^p213-1]; tactum in *veste* ab haemorrhoissa, ut hic; tactum in *latere* a Thoma; Ioannis vigesimo: « Mitte manum tuam in latus meum ». In quo intelligitur triplex imitatio approximantium ad Christum, scilicet in *effectu operationis bonae,* in *habitu conversationis sanctae,* in *tolerantia passionis acerbae,* in quibus consistit integritas satisfactionis perfectae. Per *pedum* namque *incessum* intelligitur rectitudo operationis bonae; Ezechielis primo[^p213-2]: « Pedes eorum pedes recti ». Per *vestimentum* autem intelligitur honestas conversationis sanctae; Apocalypsis decimo sexto: « Beatus, qui custodit vestimenta sua, ne nudus ambulet ». Talis est qui imitatur Christum; ad Romanos decimo tertio: « Abiiciamus opera tenebrarum et induamur arma lucis ». Per *latus* Christi vulneratum et *manus* et *pedes* perforatos intelligitur tolerantia passionis acerbae, secundum illud Psalmi[^p213-3]: « Foderunt manus meas et pedes meos ». In his enim Christum contingimus, cum eius vulneribus ad tolerantiam armamur; primae Petri quarto: « Christo igitur passo in carne, et vos eadem cogitatione armamini ».

## English
<!-- page 208 -->
72. *And it came to pass, that when Jesus was returned* etc. Above[^p208-10] the Evangelist showed the dominion and authority of Christ over the corporeal creature in the calming of the tempest, and over the spiritual in the expulsion of the demon; here he shows that he has the same dominion over *the creature composed of both,* in the healing of the woman with the issue of blood and the raising of the girl. Now this part has two, according as human nature is composed of two, namely of body and soul. For first he is shown *the lord of bodies* in the healing of the woman with the issue of blood; secondly, *the lord of souls* in the raising of the girl, there[^p208-11]: *While he was yet speaking* etc. And the setting forth of each miracle is brought in to make manifest the dignity of the power of Christ, which he shows full perfectly in the miracle of the cure of the woman with the issue of blood.
<!-- page 209 -->
For first there is shown in this part *the desirable kindness of Christ's presence;* secondly, *the inestimable sublimity of his power,* there[^p209-1]: *And a certain woman was in an issue of blood* etc.; thirdly, *the ineffable depth of his wisdom,* there: *And Jesus said: Who is it that touched me* etc.

*The kindness* of *Christ's presence* is shown *desirable* from *the people receiving him with gladness,* from *the ruler beseeching him with confidence,* from *the multitude accompanying him with benevolence.* The first regards Christ's coming, which the people *run to meet;* the second, Christ's standing, to which the ruler *runs back;* the third, Christ's going forward, to which the multitude *runs together.* From the running to meet, then, and the running back and the running together of the multitude, the presence and coming of Christ is shown in many ways to be desirable.

73. (Vers. 40.). First, then, as to *the reception with gladness made by the people,* it is said: *And it came to pass, that when Jesus was returned,* namely across the sea into Galilee, from which for a time he had withdrawn his bodily presence, desired as it was; according to what was prefigured in Solomon, the third of Kings, the tenth chapter[^p209-2]: « And all the earth desired to see the face of Solomon, to hear his wisdom, which God had given him ». By this *Solomon* we understand Christ, of whom it is said in the Psalm: « Beautiful in form above the sons of men » etc. — And therefore it is subjoined: *A great multitude received him*[^p209-3], namely with gladness and joy, that so there might be fulfilled that of Isaias, the twenty-ninth chapter: « The poor men shall exult in the Holy One of Israel ». — And therefore it is subjoined: *For they were all waiting for him,* namely with desire, because, Lamentations, the third chapter[^p209-4], « it is good to wait with silence for the salvation of God ». For « the Lord is good to them that hope in him, to the soul that seeketh him »; « for he shall be the expectation of nations », Genesis, the last chapter but one; the Psalm: « Expect therefore the Lord, and do manfully » etc.

74. (Vers. 41.). Secondly, as to *the supplication with confidence made by the ruler,* it is subjoined: *And behold there came a man whose name was Jairus;* whose name he expresses because he was a person of note by reason of his rule, according to what is said in the second of Kings, the seventh chapter[^p209-5]: « I have made thee a great name, like unto the name of the great ones that are on the earth »; or also because the name answered to his office and dignity. For Jairus is interpreted *enlightened,* and such a one ought to rule among the people. — Whence it is subjoined: *And he was a ruler of the synagogue;* Ecclesiasticus, the forty-fifth chapter[^p209-6]: « He made to him a covenant of peace, and the prince of the sanctuary and of his people ». But this ruler, though he was exalted in glory, so that there could befit him that of the first of Kings, the second chapter[^p209-7]: « That he may sit with princes, and hold the throne of glory »; was not on that account lifted up through pride, but humbled through *reverence,* heeding that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-second chapter: « Have they made thee ruler? Be not lifted up ». — Therefore it is subjoined: *And he fell down at the feet of Jesus,* knowing that it is written, Isaias, the forty-fifth chapter[^p209-8]: « To me every knee shall be bowed, and every tongue shall swear »; and in the Psalm: « Come, let us adore and fall down before the Lord ». And he fell down for this, that he might honour the majesty of Christ by his reverence; Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter[^p209-9]: « How great is the power of God alone, and he is honoured by the humble »; and that he might also implore his piety[^tr-pietas] with confidence. — Whence also he subjoins: *Beseeching*[^p209-10]*, that he would come into his house.* This he did with *urgency,* so that he could say that of the Canticle, the third chapter: « I held him, and I will not let him go, till I bring him into my mother's house ».

75. (Vers. 42.). To this urgency *need* drove him, which he expresses when he says: *For he had an only daughter,* and so one much to be loved. For, the second of Kings, the first chapter[^p209-11], « as the mother loveth her only son, so did I love thee », said David. And she was to be loved not only because she was *only,* but also because she was *a young girl;* and therefore he adds: *Almost twelve years old;* and because she was tender, therefore she was to be loved with a tender love, according to that of Genesis, the thirty-seventh chapter[^p209-12]: « Now Israel loved Joseph above all his sons, because he had begotten him in his old age ». And because her life was much to be loved, therefore her death was much to be grieved. — On account of which he subjoins: *And she was dying;* which could not be without great sorrow, which is in the mourning for an only child; Jeremias, the sixth chapter[^p209-13]: « Make thee the mourning of an only begotten, a bitter lamentation ». Therefore Jacob said of Benjamin, Genesis, the forty-second chapter: « My son, said he, shall not go down with you; for his brother is dead, and he is left alone. If any mischief befall him in the way, you will bring down my grey hairs with sorrow to hell ». So this man also could say of his only daughter.

76. Since, then, he came into the presence of Christ with reverence, besought him out of confidence, desired out of need; therefore his prayer was heard, according to
<!-- page 210 -->
that of Ecclesiasticus, the fourth chapter[^p210-1]: « Reject not the petition of the afflicted ». Hence it is that the Lord does not reprove this man who asked that he go to his house, as he did the ruler, John, the fourth chapter; because the ruler believed that he could rather heal one sick than raise one dead, whence he said: « Lord, come down before that my son die ». But this man believed that he could do the one equally well as the other, whence he reckoned his daughter as good as dead. — And therefore another Evangelist, namely Matthew[^p210-2], brings him in asking for her as dead, although according to Luke and Mark something of life still remained. Whence Augustine, *On the Harmony of the Evangelists,* says that Mark and Luke expressed the ruler's word, but Matthew expressed his understanding, because he reckoned her dead.

77. Thirdly, as to *the company of benevolence made by the multitude,* it is subjoined: *And it happened as he went, that he was thronged by the multitudes;* since out of the affection of love all wished to touch him, as above, the sixth chapter[^p210-3]: « All the multitude sought to touch him ». In this, too, the benevolence of the Saviour is shown in turn, who wills even to be thronged by the multitude, as one repelling no one from himself. For he it is who *says,* John, the sixth chapter[^p210-4]: « Every one that shall come to me, I will not cast out »; he who *calls,* Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « Come to me, all you that labour and are burdened, and I will refresh you »; he it is who *knocks at the gate;* the Apocalypse, the third chapter: « I stand at the gate, and knock ». This thronging is made by mutual love, which is the bond of lover and beloved; because, the first to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter[^p210-5], « he who is joined to the Lord is one spirit »; and John, the fifteenth chapter: « Abide in me, and I in you ». Whence perfect love is not content with any sense without touch; on account of which the spouse says, the Canticle, the first chapter[^p210-6]: « Draw me after thee »; Jeremias, the thirty-first chapter: « I have loved thee with a perpetual charity, therefore have I drawn thee ».

78. (Vers. 43.). *And a certain woman was in an issue* etc. After the presence of Christ has been shown to be desirable, *his wonderful power* is shown in the marvellous cure from the issue of blood. And the Evangelist shows that this cure proceeded from a wonderful omnipotence, as well on account of *the long duration of the disease* in the woman, as on account of *the impossibility of curing it* by art, as on account of *the ease of the remedy* from the Saviour.

First, then, as to *the long duration of the unclean affliction* in the woman, it is said: *And a certain woman was in an issue of blood twelve years.* This was an unclean affliction, and therefore borne with difficulty; Leviticus, the fifteenth chapter[^p210-7]: « The woman that hath an issue of blood, if it cease not to flow, as long as she is subject to this affliction is unclean ». It was healable with difficulty on account of its clinging, because it was already grown old; Ecclesiasticus, the tenth chapter: « A long sickness is troublesome to the physician ». This affliction, then, was unclean, continual and of long duration; therefore vile to dwell with, heavy to bear and difficult to heal. Whence she could say that of Lamentations, the third chapter[^p210-8]: « My skin and my flesh he hath made old »; and that of the Psalm: « My loins are filled with illusions, and there is no health in my flesh ». Now this woman, according to Ambrose, seems to have been Martha. For he says[^p210-9]: « Christ dried up the copious issue of blood in Martha, and cast out demons from Mary ».

79. Secondly, as to *the impossibility of human succour* from art, it is subjoined: *Who had bestowed all her substance on physicians,* for a remedy of cure, according to the counsel of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p210-10]: « Honour the physician for the need thou hast of him, for the Most High hath created him ». Now he says this of the honour *of gifts,* not of words; whence this woman had honoured them, because she had spent all her substance. And therefore it is probable that, if the sickness had been curable, it would have been cured by art. — But art did not avail; whence he subjoins: *And could not be cured by any.* Thus she could be succoured neither by nature nor by art, that there might be verified in her that of Wisdom, the sixteenth chapter[^p210-11]: « Neither herb nor mollifying plaster healed them, but thy word, O Lord, which healeth all things ».

80. Whence note that nature can do nothing without the supernal power, nor can art do anything without nature; whence where nature does not succour, art does not profit, but rather harms, as in this woman, of whom it is said, Mark, the fifth chapter[^p210-12], that « she had suffered many things from
<!-- page 211 -->
many physicians, and had bestowed all that she had, and found no benefit, but rather grew worse ». But Luke the physician is silent about this, speaking more courteously of physicians. — From which it is gathered that the art of physicians is *praiseworthy* when it follows the supernal power and the nature that underlies it. Whence it is said, Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p211-1]: « Give place to the physician, let him not depart from thee ». But the work of the physician will then be *blameworthy* when he neglects either to consider nature or to put the divine power first, as is said of Asa, the second of Paralipomenon, the sixteenth chapter[^p211-2]: « Asa fell sick of a most violent pain in his feet, and yet he did not seek the Lord, but rather trusted in the art of physicians »; and it follows that « he died ».

81. (Vers. 44.). Thirdly, as to *the ease of the perfect cure* from the Saviour, it is subjoined: *She came behind and touched the hem of his garment. She came,* because *she believed;* to the Hebrews, the eleventh chapter[^p211-3]: « He that cometh must believe »; *behind,* because *she feared,* as did the sister of the same, Mary, above, the seventh chapter: « Standing behind at the feet of the Lord ». *She touched,* because *she hoped to be cured;* Matthew, the ninth chapter: « She said within herself: If I shall touch the hem of the garment, I shall be healed ». *She touched,* however, *the hem*[^p211-4], that is, the lowest part *of the garment,* reckoning herself unclean and honouring the power of Christ, who can give salvation through the lowest things. — And because she believed and hoped, therefore she obtained easily. On account of this it is added: *And immediately the issue of her blood stopped;* from which there appears the wondrous power of God, which cured a disease so long-lasting and so grave at once at the touch of one small part. Wondrous, then, is the power of Christ, which through belief in him makes impossible things easy, according to that of Mark, the ninth chapter[^p211-5]: « If thou canst believe, all things are possible to him that believeth »; and John, the fourteenth chapter: « He that believeth in me, the works that I do, he also shall do, and greater than these shall he do »; and the Acts, the nineteenth chapter: « God wrought no small powers by the hand of Paul, so that even there were brought from his body handkerchiefs and aprons, and the diseases departed from them ». If, then, it is so wholesome to touch the hem of the garment by faith, how wholesome will it be to hold Christ himself? Whence Bernard[^p211-6]: « Sweet enough it is to seek thee, good Jesus, sweeter to hold thee »; whence the spouse, the Canticle, the third chapter: « I held him, and I will not let him go ». « Sweet indeed is the handling of thee; for even the touch itself does not lack fruit. For the woman of the Gospel by a happy theft *touched the hem* of Jesus, *and immediately* there *stopped* in her *the issue* of carnal allurement, of carnal delight and care ».

82. (Vers. 45.). *And Jesus said* etc. After Christ's presence has been shown to be desirable and his power wonderful, here, thirdly, his *infallible wisdom* is shown; and this in three ways, namely from *the detecting of the hidden truth,* from *the uncovering of the truth detected,* from *the approving of the truth uncovered.*

First, then, there is brought in *the detecting of the hidden truth* by Christ through his own inquiry, when it is said: *And Jesus said: Who*[^p211-7] *touched me?* Now he does not say this that he may learn, but that he may uncover to others what is known to himself; according to what is said in the Psalm: « His eyelids question the sons of men, the Lord questions the just and the wicked »; not that he may learn anything from them, but that he may teach what they do not know. — Whence, because they did not know the cause of the question, it is subjoined: *And all denying, Peter and they that were with him said,* that is, the other disciples, not understanding the cause of this question; whence that of Matthew, the fifteenth chapter[^p211-8], could be said to them: « Are you also yet without understanding »? because they understood that question carnally, not spiritually. — Which is plain in this, that he adduces: *Master,*[^tr-praeceptor] *the multitudes throng and press thee, and thou*[^p211-9] *sayest: Who touched me?* as if they said: since all touch thee indifferently, how dost thou ask about some one in particular? Whence they wondered how Christ, master and preceptor, should ask as of a doubtful thing what was manifest to all; and therefore they wondered with reason how Christ should put forward a question which appears foolish, since it is said, the second to Timothy, the second chapter[^p211-10]: « Avoid foolish and unlearned questions ». But because they had a carnal understanding, they were to be led by the hand to a spiritual one.

83. (Vers. 46.). On account of which he adds: *And Jesus said: Somebody hath touched me,* that is, some person, namely by a spiritual touch; and this he proves: *For*[^p211-11] *I also know that power is gone out from me.* This he says as to the effect of the cure; and so there is verified that of Wisdom, the twelfth chapter: « For thou shewest thy power,
<!-- page 212 -->
who art not believed to be consummate in power ». And he shows the effect going out from him, according to that which is said above, the sixth chapter, that « power went out from him and healed all ». Nor is it a wonder, because, the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p212-1], « we call Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God »; to the Hebrews, the first chapter: « Upholding all things by the word of his power ».

84. (Vers. 47.). Secondly, there is joined *the uncovering of the truth detected* through another's confession, when it is said: *And the woman seeing that she was not hid from him*[^p212-2]*;* whence she could say that of the Samaritan woman, John, the fourth chapter: « Lord, as I see, thou art a Prophet ». On account of which she too could bear like witness with her, and therefore say: « Come and see a man who hath told me all things whatsoever I have done ». This man was truly another Solomon, nay wiser than he, of whom it is said, the third of Kings, the tenth chapter[^p212-3]: « There was not any word that could be hid from the king »; because he it is of whom Daniel, the second chapter, speaks: « He knoweth the things set in darkness, and light is with him ». And because she had now known his power in its effect and perceived his wisdom in his word, therefore she attended that she could not escape. — And therefore it is subjoined: *And*[^p212-4] *she came trembling, and fell down at his feet,* to honour the Majesty; Micheas, the sixth chapter: « What worthy thing shall I offer to the Lord? I will bow the knee to the high God ». And this with *trembling,* because God has respect to such alone; Isaias, the last chapter[^p212-5]: « To whom shall I have respect, but to him that is poor and little, and that trembleth at my words »? And therefore to the Philippians, the second chapter: « With fear and trembling work out your salvation, for it is God who worketh in you both to will and to accomplish, according to his good will ». — And because nothing pertains more to the honour of the Majesty than the manifestation of the truth, therefore it is subjoined: *And declared before all the people for what cause she had touched him,* namely *how she was immediately healed*[^p212-6]*;* because, Tobias, the twelfth chapter, « it is honourable to reveal and confess the works of God ». And therefore the Angel said: « Bless ye the God of heaven, and confess to him in the sight of all that live, because he hath shewn his mercy to you ».

85. (Vers. 48.). Thirdly, there is joined *the approving of the truth uncovered* by a definitive commendation, when it is subjoined: *But he said to her*[^p212-7]*: Daughter, thy faith hath made thee whole,* as if he said: justly hast thou obtained this, because thou hast believed; as if he said: faith hath made thee *a daughter,* according to that of John, the first chapter: « He gave them power to be made the sons of God, to them that believe in his name ». Faith also made her *whole,* because, to the Romans, the tenth chapter, « whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved ». Whence in this there appears the wondrous dispensation of our salvation, that *the healed woman* attributes the whole *to the divine power,* according to that to the Romans, the ninth chapter[^p212-8]: « It is not of him that willeth, nor of him that runneth, but of God that sheweth mercy »; *God saving* attributes the whole *to the merit of faith,* as is said in Mark, the ninth chapter: « If you can believe, all things are possible to him that believeth ». — And since in the acquiring of salvation there follows as a consequence the acquiring of *peace,* therefore it is subjoined: *Go thy way in peace,* as a friend and daughter of God; Wisdom, the third chapter[^p212-9]: « Gift and peace is to the elect of God ». Peace is that which the Lord *wishes* to all and commands to be wished, according to that below, the tenth chapter: « Into whatsoever house you enter, first say: Peace be to this house ». This is what he *gives* before other things; John, the fourteenth chapter[^p212-10]: « Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you »; this is that to which he specially *calls;* Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « Take up my yoke upon you, and you shall find rest »; and John, the sixteenth chapter: « These things I have spoken to you, that in me you may have peace. In the world you shall have distress, but have confidence, I have overcome the world ». Of this, to the Philippians, the fourth chapter: « The peace of God, which surpasseth all understanding, keep your hearts and minds in Christ Jesus », my Lord.

86. And note that the Lord commends the faith of three women, whom he hears and saves. He commended the Magdalen above, the seventh chapter[^p212-11], saying a like word: « Thy faith hath made thee whole »; and he commends this one here; and the woman of Chanaan, Matthew, the fifteenth chapter, to whom he said: « O woman, great is thy faith ». — In these three women the three parts of penance are understood *spiritually.* For the first, who wept and watered with tears, gives to understand *contrition;* the woman of Chanaan, who cried out, gives to understand *confession;* but this one, who touched the garment, gives to understand *satisfaction.* Of the Magdalen it is read above, the seventh chapter[^p212-12], that « standing behind at the feet of the Lord,
<!-- page 213 -->
she began to water them with tears » etc. Of the woman of Chanaan, Matthew, the fifteenth chapter, the disciples say: « Send her away, for she crieth after us ». Of this woman it is said here that « she came behind and touched the hem of his garment ». And by *the touch of the garment* is understood imitation in the labour of a holy manner of life. — Whence note that we read that Christ was touched on *the foot* by the Magdalen, above, the seventh chapter[^p213-1]; touched on *the garment* by the woman with the issue of blood, as here; touched on *the side* by Thomas; John, the twentieth chapter: « Put thy hand into my side ». In which is understood the threefold imitation of those who draw near to Christ, namely in *the effect of good working,* in *the habit of a holy manner of life,* in *the endurance of bitter passion,* in which consists the integrity of perfect satisfaction. For by *the going of the feet* is understood the uprightness of good working; Ezechiel, the first chapter[^p213-2]: « Their feet were straight feet ». And by *the garment* is understood the seemliness of a holy manner of life; the Apocalypse, the sixteenth chapter: « Blessed is he that keepeth his garments, lest he walk naked ». Such is he who imitates Christ; to the Romans, the thirteenth chapter: « Let us cast off the works of darkness, and put on the armour of light ». By the wounded *side* of Christ, and the pierced *hands* and *feet,* is understood the endurance of bitter passion, according to that of the Psalm[^p213-3]: « They have dug my hands and my feet ». For in these we touch Christ, when we are armed by his wounds to endurance; the first of Peter, the fourth chapter: « Christ therefore having suffered in the flesh, be you also armed with the same thought ».
