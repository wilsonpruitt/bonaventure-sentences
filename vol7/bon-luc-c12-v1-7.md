# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. XII, Vers. 1-7
## *Informatio duplex ad cavendam dolositatem*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 309 -->

### Capitulum XII.

#### Secundo informat ad fallaciam confutatam vitandam duplici modo.

1. *Multis autem turbis circumstantibus* etc. Postquam Dominus in Pharisaeis redarguit dolositatem simulationis malignae, hic discipulos *invitat ad eautelam simulationis redargutae*[^p309-11]. Et quoniam huiusmodi duplicitatem causat *timor* male humilians, vel *amor* male accendens; ideo pars ista dividitur in duas. In quarum prima monet ad cavendam
<!-- page 310 -->
*duplicitatem ex timiditate venientem;* in secunda vero, *cupiditatem,* infra eodem capitulo[^p310-1]: *Ait autem ei quidam de turba* etc.

#### Primo ad cavendam duplicitatem venientem ex timiditate monet quadrupliciter.

Prima autem pars habet quatuor partes, in quarum prima *informat ad cavendam dolositatem;* in secunda *confortat ad repellendam timiditatem,* ibi[^p310-2]: *Dico autem vobis amicis* etc.; in tertia vero *invitat ad conservandam veracitatem,* ibi: *Dico autem vobis: Omnis, quicumque confessus* etc.; in quarta vero *animat ad habendam securitatem,* ibi: *Cum autem inducent vos in synagogas.* Et sunt istae partes sibi correspondentes, quia duae postremae correspondent duabus primis.

#### Informatio duplex ad cavendam dolositatem.

Informat autem discipulos ad *cavendam dolositatem* informatione *celebri* et informatione *credibili: celebri,* inquam, propter *multitudinem turbae*[^p310-3]*; credibili,* propter *certitudinem causae.*

2. (Vers. 1.). Primo igitur quantum ad *multitudinem turbae reddentis admonitionem celebrem* dicitur: *Multis autem turbis circumstantibus, ita ut se invicem conculcarent;* et hoc, quia unusquisque volebat ei appropinquare, sive propter *salutem habendam,* sicut supra sexto[^p310-4]: « Omnis turba quaerebat eum tangere » etc.; sive propter *doctrinam suscipiendam,* sicut infra decimo quinto: « Erant appropinquantes ad Iesum publicani » etc.; sive propter ipsius Christi *videndam benignitatem,* sicut in figura dicitur Iob vigesimo nono: « Cumque sederem quasi rex, circumstante exercitu, eram tamen moerentium consolator ». In tanta multitudinis praesentia celebris erat doctrina. — Propter quod subdit: *Coepit dicere ad discipulos suos,* in aperto, quia veritas non requirit latebras, secundum illud Ioannis decimo octavo[^p310-5]: « Ego palam locutus sum mundo, ego semper docui in synagogis et in templo » etc. Et talem decebat esse doctrinam ad confutandam Pharisaeorum malitiam, quae semper quaerit latebram, quia « contraria contrariis curantur[^p310-6] », et tenebra per lumen expellitur.

3. Propter quod additur: *Attendite a fermento Pharisaeorum, quod est hypocrisis,* id est cavete, ne sitis hypocritae, sicut Pharisaei, quorum vita et doctrina propter malitiam et fallaciam *fermentum* dicitur, quia corrumpit *imperceptibiliter;* Osee septimo[^p310-7]: « Quievit paululum civitas a fermento, donec fermentaretur totum ». Corrumpit etiam *universaliter,* sicut dicitur primae ad Corinthios quinto[^p310-8]: « Modicum fermenti corrumpit totam massam »; et ideo subditur: « Expurgate vetus fermentum, ut sitis nova conspersio ». Hoc autem est *vetus fermentum,* quod consistit in *fraudulentia doctrinae* et *malitia vitae.* Unde ibidem subditur: « Neque in fermento malitiae et nequitiae, sed in azymis sinceritatis et veritatis ». Ab hoc fermento revocat in hoc loco; et de hoc in figura dicitur Deuteronomii decimo sexto[^p310-9]: « Non apparebit fermentatum in omnibus finibus tuis », quia haec simulatio et hypocrisis debet elongari a cunctis operibus nostris, et maxime a *devotione spirituali;* Levitici secundo[^p310-10]: « Omnis oblatio, quae offeretur Domino, absque fermento fiet »; ab *afflictione corporali;* Deuteronomii decimo sexto: « Septem diebus comedes afflictionis panem absque fermento »; a *praedicatione divini verbi;* Exodi vigesimo tertio: « Non immolabis super fermentum sanguinem hostiae ».

4. Unde Scriptura docet, hoc vitium esse vitandum tanquam maxime corruptivum. Licet autem sit maxime corruptivum, non tamen est capitale peccatum, quia eius corruptio aliunde causatur. Nam *hypocrisis* originem trahit a vana gloria, et haec a superbia; et ideo merito comparatur hypocrisis *fermento.* Nam sicut fermentum inflat et inflando vanum facit et sic corrumpit, sic superbia inflando introducit vanitatem gloriae, et haec vanitas introducit falsitatem corrumpentem. Unde hoc fermentum cavetur per veritatem *humiliationis voluntariae, gloriationis internae, signationis extrinsecae,* secundum illud secundae ad Corinthios primo[^p310-11]: « Gloria nostra haec est, testimonium conscientiae nostrae, quod in
<!-- page 311 -->
simplicitate cordis et sinceritate Dei et non in sapientia carnali, sed in gratia Dei conversati sumus in hoc mundo ».

5. (Vers. 2.). Secundo vero quantum ad *certitudinem causae reddentis informationem credibilem* subdit: *Nihil autem opertum est, quod non reveletur, neque absconditum, quod non sciatur;* ut per *opertum* intelligatur *cognitio* latens in intellectu, et per *absconditum, delectatio* latitans in affectu; quorum utrumque manifestum est Deo, secundum illud Ieremiae decimo septimo[^p311-1]: « Ego Dominus scrutans corda et renes, qui do unicuique » etc.; in Psalmo: « Scrutans corda et renes Deus ». Et ideo, quia ipsius erit omnia abscondita manifestare et revelare in iudicio, secundum illud Sapientiae sexto[^p311-2]: « Data est vobis potestas a Domino et virtus ab Altissimo, qui interrogabit opera vestra et cogitationes scrutabitur »; et primae ad Corinthios quarto: « Veniet Dominus, qui illuminabit abscondita tenebrarum et manifestabit consilia cordium » etc.: et sic vitanda sunt omnia mala, quantumcumque occulta, quia omnia fient manifesta in iudicio.

6. (Vers. 3.). Et quod plus est, ad maiorem confusionem fient etiam manifesta omnibus; propter quod subdit: *Quoniam quae in tenebris dixistis in lumine dicentur.* Hoc dicit quantum ad manifestationem respectu *visus.* Huius exemplum in David habetur secundi Regum duodecimo[^p311-3], ubi dixit Dominus ad David per Nathan: « Tu fecisti verbum illud abscondite; ego autem faciam verbum istud in conspectu omnis Israel et in conspectu solis huius ». Lumen enim solis visui cetera manifestat. — Quantum autem ad manifestationem respectu *auditus* dicitur: *Et quod in aurem*[^p311-4] *locuti estis in cubiculis praedicabitur in tectis,* id est, audientibus ceteris. Hoc quidem in iudicio fiet, quando scilicet, secundum illud Ioannis quinto[^p311-5], « omnes, qui in monumentis sunt, audient vocem Filii Dei ». Et tunc manifestabuntur susurrationes detractionum, secundum illud Sapientiae primo: « Qui loquitur iniqua non potest latere, nec praeteriet illum corripiens iudicium. In cogitationibus » etc. Et ideo consilium datur Ecclesiastae decimo: « In cogitatione tua regi ne detrahas, et in secreto cubiculi tui non maledixeris diviti, quia aves caeli portabunt vocem tuam ».

7. Propter iudicium ergo manifestans cuncta in futuro vitanda est omnis hypocrisis in praesenti saeculo; Ecclesiastici primo[^p311-6]: « Ne fueris hypocrita in conspectu hominum; ne revelet Deus abscondita tua et in medio synagogae elidat te ». — Et nota, quod haec est nobilissima[^p311-7] causa, propter quam hypocrisis est vitanda, scilicet universalis manifestatio et plena interiorum, quae maxime opponitur simulationi hypocritarum. Haec autem manifestatio fiet per Christum, « qui est splendor gloriae paternae et figura substantiae eius, portans omnia verbo virtutis suae[^p311-8] ». Et quia ipse est splendor intimus, ideo ipsius est omnia detegere; Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio: « Oculi Domini multo plus lucidiores super solem, circumspicientes omnes vias hominum et profundum abyssi et hominum corda intuentes » etc. Quia vero ipse est Sermo vivus, ideo sibi omnia patent, et omnia patefacit ut Verbum; ad Hebraeos quarto[^p311-9]: « Vivus est sermo Dei et efficax et penetrabilior omni gladio ancipiti, pertingens usque ad divisionem animae » etc., usque ibi: « Non est ulla creatura invisibilis in conspectu eius ». Et ideo Christus iudex, quia omnia deteget in iudicio, dicitur[^p311-10] venire « sicut fulgur », et « descendere in voce Archangeli », quia omnia omnibus clarissime reserabit.

#### Informatio ad repellendam timiditatem per duo.

8. (Vers. 4.). *Dico autem vobis amicis* etc. Postquam informavit ad cavendam dolositatem, hic secundo *confortat ad repellendam timiditatem*[^p311-11]. Ad hoc autem inducit secundum duplicem considerationem, scilicet *gehennalis punitionis,* in quam nullus hominum potest deiicere; et *generalis provisionis,* quam nullus homo potest effugere. Primum respicit divinam *potentiam;* secundum, divinam *providentiam;* et primum incutit verum *timorem;* secundum, veram *spem;* et utrumque horum est medicamentum contra timorem mundanum.

Primo igitur revocat a timore hominum, quia *nihil possunt in gehennale supplicium,* quod solum
<!-- page 312 -->
est timendum. Propter quod dicit: *Dico autem vobis amicis meis,* id est, qui me amatis et a me amamini, secundum illud Ioannis decimo sexto[^p312-1]: « Ipse Pater amat vos, quia vos me amastis » etc. Talibus dicit: *Ne terreamini ab his qui occidunt corpus, et post haec non habent, quid amplius faciant*[^p312-2]. Non timeatis *corporalis mortis supplicium,* quia particulare est et momentaneum; unde Isaiae quinquagesimo primo: « Quis tu, ut timeas ab homine mortali et a filio hominis, qui quasi foenum ita exarescet »? et primi Machabaeorum secundo: « A verbis viri peccatoris ne timueritis, quia gloria eius stercus et vermis » etc.; unde quia transitoria est eorum potentia, ideo non est timenda, nec eorum supplicia. Unde Seneca[^p312-3]: « Mors, exsilium, luctus, dolor non sunt supplicium, sed tributa vivendi »; « quod debeo paratus sum solvere, ubi me fenerator appellat ». Et Chrysostomus[^p312-4]: « Si gratis post modicum morituri sumus, quare non et ante modicum in causa Dei cum gloria morimur? Si mutuo acceperis bovem aut equum, operaris instanter et dicis: Forte cras tolletur a me. Quare hoc non facis in corpore »? — Non sunt etiam timenda spiritui, *quia fiunt in carne* et ita quasi *extra,* ut in vestimento; iuxta quod dicitur secundae ad Corinthios quinto[^p312-5]: Nolumus exspoliari, sed supervestiri ». Ideo Chrysostomus: « Propter animam Deus fecit corpus, et multum hanc zelat et persequitur inimicus; corpus vero vestimentum est animae. Sicut ergo, si quis scindit vestem alterius, ille quidem iniuriam sentit, damnum autem in natura non patitur; sic anima occisionis sentit dolorem, damnum autem non patitur in natura ». Et propterea *corporale* supplicium non est timendum nec ille qui infert; sed *spirituale* est maxime formidandum et solus ille qui infert ipsum.

9. (Vers. 5.). Propter quod addit: *Ostendam autem vobis quem timeatis,* ut eius scilicet timore malum declinetis, secundum illud Ieremiae decimo[^p312-6]: « Nolite timere ea quae nec male facere possunt nec bene »; et post: « Quis non timebit te, o Rex gentium » etc. — Ut ergo non timeamus mundanam potentiam, oportet nos timere divinam; propter quod dicit: *Timete eum qui, postquam occiderit, habet potestatem mittere in gehennam;* unde in Psalmo: « Timeat Dominum omnis terra, ab eo autem commoveantur omnes inhabitantes orbem ». — Et quia huiusmodi timor maxime utilis est et necessarius, ideo iterat: *Ita dico vobis, hunc timete.* Unde Isaiae octavo[^p312-7]: « Dominum exercituum ipsum sanctificate, et ipse sit pavor vester, et ipse terror vester »; et Ieremiae quinto: « Me ergo non timebitis? et a facie mea » etc.

10. (Vers. 6.). Secundo vero revocat a timore hominum, quia *nihil possunt contra generale Dei patrocinium;* quantum ad quod dicit: *Nonne quinque passeres veneunt dipondio? Dipondius* hic nomen est monetae habentis duos obolos sive asses. Unde Beda[^p312-8]: « Dipondius est pondus ex duobus assibus compositum. Quod autem in numeris est *unum,* hoc in ponderibus est as; quod *duo,* hoc *dipondius* ». Huic concordat nec discordat quod dicitur Matthaei decimo[^p312-9]: « Nonne duo passeres asse veneunt »? Quia, sicut videmus, cum quinque ova venduntur pro uno denario, duo dantur pro obolo. Parvi igitur pretii est passer, cum tot inveniantur pro uno denario; tamen nec ipse est alienus a divino patrocinio. — Propter quod addit: *Et unus ex illis non est in oblivione coram Deo,* immo divina providentia gubernatur, secundum illud Sapientiae undecimo[^p312-10]: « Diligis omnia, quae sunt, et nihil odisti eorum quae fecisti; neque enim odiens aliquid constituisti ». Ex quo apparet, quod divina providentia usque ad minima se extendit; nec contradicit illud primae ad Corinthios nono[^p312-11]: « Nunquid de bobus cura est Deo »? Illud enim intelligitur de cura providentiae *specialis,* qua manuducit per viam aeternae salutis; hoc autem intelligitur de providentia *generalis* administrationis et conservationis, de qua in Psalmo: « Qui dat iumentis escam ipsorum et pullis corvorum invocantibus eum ». — Et nota quod dicit: *Non est in oblivione coram Deo;* minus dicens et plus significans, quia nihil eis evenit nisi secundum divinam providentiam ordinantem; unde Matthaei
<!-- page 313 -->
decimo[^p313-1]: « Unus ex illis non cadet super terram sine Patre vestro ». Et tamen passeres facillime cadunt; unde morbum caducum habere dicuntur; et propterea potissime ponit de eis exemplum tanquam de re manifesta. Ex quo colligitur tanquam *a minori,* quod maxime circa regimen hominis divina providentia sit intenta.

11. (Vers. 7.). Ad cuius expressionem addit: *Sed et capilli capitis vestri omnes numerati sunt;* ex quo datur intelligi, quod maxime est cura illi de homine. Unde Beda[^p313-2]: « *Sed et capilli,* ut de massa corporis taceam; nam et minutae particulae Dei providentia conservantur ». Unde *numeratio* non tam refertur ad cognitionem quam ad diligentissimam conservationem, quia de corporibus Sanctorum patientium nec capillus perire potest; unde infra vigesimo primo[^p313-3]: « Capillus de capite vestro non peribit ». Tanta igitur est Deo cura de nobis, ut nec capillus capitis nostri sine eius nutu possit auferri. Ideo non oportet, nos sollicitari per timorem, secundum illud primae Petri ultimo: « Omnem sollicitudinem vestram proiicientes in eum, quia ipsi cura est de vobis ». — Et hoc est quod infert: *Nolite ergo timere, multis passeribus pluris estis vos,* « id est maioris pretii[^p313-4] »; in quo simul tangitur conclusio et ratio illationis per *locum a minori.* Si enim cura est Deo de minori, multo fortius de maiori; et si cura est de nobis, non oportet, mundum timeri a nobis. Maioris autem pretii sumus quam creaturae brutales, quam volucres, quia rationales creaturae, et hoc, quia ad imaginem Dei facti; propter quod cetera subiecta sunt homini; Genesis primo[^p313-5]: « Faciamus hominem ad imaginem et similitudinem nostram, et praesit volucribus caeli » etc. Et ideo homo immediatissime est sub protectione Dei, et maxime homo iustus et Dei amicus. Et propterea non oportet, quod habeat auxilium alicuius caduci; unde Genesis decimo quinto[^p313-6]: « Noli timere, Abram, ego protector tuus sum et merces tua magna nimis »; quasi dicat: noli timere, quia nihil pati potes pro defectu auxilii, nihil etiam pati potes sine retributione praemii, quamdiu adhaeseris mihi.

### Chapter XII.

#### Secondly, he forms them to shun the confuted deceit, in two ways.

1. *And when many multitudes stood about* etc. After the Lord convicted in the Pharisees the guile of malignant dissembling, here he *invites* the disciples *to caution against the convicted dissembling* [so printed: *eautelam* for *cautelam*][^p309-11]. And since such duplicity is caused by *fear* that wrongly humbles, or by *love* that wrongly inflames; therefore this part is divided into two. In the first of which he warns them to guard against
<!-- page 310 -->
*duplicity coming from timidity;* but in the second, against *cupidity,* below in the same chapter[^p310-1]: *And one of the multitude said to him* etc.

#### First, to guard against duplicity coming from timidity, he warns in four ways.

Now the first part has four parts, in the first of which he *forms them to guard against guile;* in the second he *strengthens them to repel timidity,* there[^p310-2]: *And I say to you, my friends* etc.; but in the third he *invites them to preserve truthfulness,* there: *And I say to you: Whosoever shall confess* etc.; but in the fourth he *heartens them to have security,* there: *And when they shall bring you into the synagogues.* And these parts correspond to one another, because the two last correspond to the two first.

#### A twofold formation for guarding against guile.

Now he forms the disciples to *guard against guile* by a formation *public* and by a formation *credible: public,* I say, on account of *the multitude of the crowd*[^p310-3]*; credible,* on account of *the certainty of the cause.*

2. (Vers. 1.). First, then, as to *the multitude of the crowd rendering the admonition public* it is said: *And when many multitudes stood about, so that they trod one upon another;* and this, because each one wished to draw near to him, either for the sake of *obtaining health,* as above, the sixth chapter[^p310-4]: « And all the multitude sought to touch him » etc.; or for the sake of *receiving doctrine,* as below, the fifteenth chapter: « The publicans were drawing near unto Jesus » etc.; or for the sake of *seeing the kindliness* of Christ himself, as is said in figure in Job, the twenty-ninth chapter: « And when I sat as a king, with the army standing about, yet I was the comforter of them that mourned ». In the presence of so great a multitude the doctrine was public. — On account of which he subjoins: *He began to say to his disciples,* in the open, because truth does not require hiding-places, according to that of John, the eighteenth chapter[^p310-5]: « I have spoken openly to the world: I have always taught in the synagogues and in the temple » etc. And such it befitted the doctrine to be for confuting the malice of the Pharisees, which always seeks a hiding-place, because « contraries are cured by contraries[^p310-6] », and darkness is driven out by lumen[^tr-lumen].

3. On account of which there is added: *Beware ye of the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy,* that is, take care that you be not hypocrites, as the Pharisees, whose life and doctrine on account of malice and deceit is called *leaven,* because it corrupts *imperceptibly;* Osee, the seventh chapter[^p310-7]: « The city rested a little from the leaven, till the whole was leavened ». It corrupts also *universally,* as is said in the first to the Corinthians, the fifth chapter[^p310-8]: « A little leaven corrupteth the whole lump »; and therefore there is subjoined: « Purge out the old leaven, that you may be a new paste ». Now this is *the old leaven,* which consists in *fraudulence of doctrine* and *malice of life.* Whence there is subjoined in the same place: « Nor with the leaven of malice and wickedness, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth ». From this leaven he calls them back in this place; and of this it is said in figure in Deuteronomy, the sixteenth chapter[^p310-9]: « There shall not appear anything leavened in all thy coasts », because this dissembling and hypocrisy ought to be put far from all our works, and most of all from *spiritual devotion;* Leviticus, the second chapter[^p310-10]: « Every oblation that is offered to the Lord shall be made without leaven »; from *bodily affliction;* Deuteronomy, the sixteenth chapter: « Seven days shalt thou eat the bread of affliction without leaven »; from *the preaching of the divine word;* Exodus, the twenty-third chapter: « Thou shalt not sacrifice the blood of the victim upon leaven ».

4. Whence Scripture teaches that this vice is to be shunned as most corruptive. But although it is most corruptive, yet it is not a capital sin, because its corruption is caused from elsewhere. For *hypocrisy* draws its origin from vainglory, and this from pride; and therefore hypocrisy is rightly compared to *leaven.* For as leaven puffs up and by puffing up makes empty and so corrupts, so pride by puffing up brings in the vanity of glory, and this vanity brings in corrupting falsity. Whence this leaven is guarded against through the truth *of voluntary humiliation, of inward glorying, of outward signing,* according to that of the second to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p310-11]: « For our glory is this, the testimony of our conscience, that in
<!-- page 311 -->
simplicity of heart and sincerity of God, and not in carnal wisdom, but in the grace of God, we have conversed in this world ».

5. (Vers. 2.). But secondly, as to *the certainty of the cause rendering the formation credible,* he subjoins: *For there is nothing covered, that shall not be revealed: nor hidden, that shall not be known;* so that by *covered* is understood *cognition* lying hid in the intellect, and by *hidden, delight* lurking in the affection; each of which is manifest to God, according to that of Jeremias, the seventeenth chapter[^p311-1]: « I the Lord, who search the hearts and reins, who give to every one » etc.; in the Psalm: « The searcher of hearts and reins is God ». And therefore, because it will be his to make manifest and reveal all hidden things in the judgment, according to that of Wisdom, the sixth chapter[^p311-2]: « Power is given you by the Lord, and strength by the most High, who will examine your works, and search out your thoughts »; and of the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « The Lord will come, who will bring to light the hidden things of darkness, and will make manifest the counsels of the hearts » etc.: and so all evils, however secret, are to be shunned, because all will be made manifest in the judgment.

6. (Vers. 3.). And what is more, to their greater confusion they will be made manifest also to all; on account of which he subjoins: *For whatsoever things you have spoken in darkness shall be spoken in the lumen.* This he says as to the manifestation with respect to *sight.* An example of this is had in David, in the second of Kings, the twelfth chapter[^p311-3], where the Lord said to David through Nathan: « Thou didst that thing secretly; but I will do this thing in the sight of all Israel, and in the sight of this sun ». For the lumen of the sun makes other things manifest to sight. — But as to the manifestation with respect to *hearing* it is said: *And that which you have spoken in the ear*[^p311-4] *in the chambers shall be preached on the housetops,* that is, in the hearing of the rest. This indeed will be done in the judgment, namely when, according to that of John, the fifth chapter[^p311-5], « all that are in the graves shall hear the voice of the Son of God ». And then the whisperings of detractions will be made manifest, according to that of Wisdom, the first chapter: « He that speaketh unjust things cannot be hid, neither shall the chastising judgment pass him by. Into the thoughts » etc. And therefore counsel is given in Ecclesiastes, the tenth chapter: « In thy thought detract not the king, and in the secret of thy chamber speak not evil of the rich man, because the birds of the air will carry thy voice ».

7. On account of the judgment, then, that makes all things manifest in the future, every hypocrisy is to be shunned in the present age; Ecclesiasticus, the first chapter[^p311-6]: « Be not a hypocrite in the sight of men; lest God discover thy secrets, and cast thee down in the midst of the congregation ». — And note that this is the most noble[^p311-7] cause on account of which hypocrisy is to be shunned, namely the universal and full manifestation of inward things, which is most of all opposed to the dissembling of hypocrites. Now this manifestation will be made through Christ, « who is the brightness of the Father's glory and the figure of his substance, upholding all things by the word of his power[^p311-8] ». And because he is the inmost brightness, therefore it is his to uncover all things; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third chapter: « The eyes of the Lord are far brighter than the sun, beholding round about all the ways of men, and the bottom of the deep, and looking into the hearts of men » etc. But because he is the living Word, therefore all things lie open to him, and he lays all things open as the Word; to the Hebrews, the fourth chapter[^p311-9]: « The word of God is living and effectual, and more piercing than any two-edged sword; and reaching unto the division of the soul » etc., as far as there: « Neither is there any creature invisible in his sight ». And therefore Christ the judge, because he will uncover all things in the judgment, is said[^p311-10] to come « as lightning », and « to descend with the voice of an archangel », because he will unlock all things to all most clearly.

#### A formation for repelling timidity through two things.

8. (Vers. 4.). *And I say to you, my friends* etc. After he formed them to guard against guile, here secondly he *strengthens them to repel timidity*[^p311-11]. Now he leads them to this according to a twofold consideration, namely *of the punishment of hell,* into which none of men can cast down; and *of the general provision,* which no man can escape. The first regards the divine *power;* the second, the divine *providence;* and the first strikes in true *fear;* the second, true *hope;* and each of these is a medicine against worldly fear.

First, then, he calls them back from the fear of men, because *they can do nothing towards the penalty of hell,* which alone
<!-- page 312 -->
is to be feared. On account of which he says: *And I say to you, my friends,* that is, you who love me and are loved by me, according to that of John, the sixteenth chapter[^p312-1]: « The Father himself loveth you, because you have loved me » etc. To such he says: *Be not afraid of them who kill the body, and after that have no more that they can do*[^p312-2]. Fear not *the penalty of bodily death,* because it is particular and momentary; whence Isaias, the fifty-first chapter: « Who art thou, that thou shouldst be afraid of a mortal man, and of the son of man, who shall wither away like grass »? and the first of Machabees, the second chapter: « Fear not the words of a sinful man, for his glory is dung and worms » etc.; whence, because their power is transitory, therefore it is not to be feared, nor their penalties. Whence Seneca[^p312-3]: « Death, exile, mourning, pain are not a penalty, but the tributes of living »; « what I owe I am ready to pay, when the lender calls upon me ». And Chrysostom[^p312-4]: « If gratis, after a little while, we are going to die, why do we not die even a little while before in the cause of God with glory? If thou hast received an ox or a horse on loan, thou workest it hard and sayest: Perhaps it will be taken from me tomorrow. Why dost thou not do this in the body »? — Nor are they to be feared by the spirit, *because they are done in the flesh* and so as it were *outside,* as in a garment; according to what is said in the second to the Corinthians, the fifth chapter[^p312-5]: We would not be unclothed, but clothed upon ». Therefore Chrysostom: « For the sake of the soul God made the body, and the enemy is greatly jealous of this and persecutes it; but the body is the garment of the soul. As, then, if anyone tears another's garment, he indeed feels the injury, but does not suffer loss in his nature; so the soul feels the pain of the slaying, but does not suffer loss in its nature ». And therefore *bodily* penalty is not to be feared, nor he who inflicts it; but *spiritual* penalty is most of all to be dreaded, and he alone who inflicts it.

9. (Vers. 5.). On account of which he adds: *But I will show you whom you shall fear,* namely that by fear of him you may turn aside from evil, according to that of Jeremias, the tenth chapter[^p312-6]: « Fear not those things which can neither do evil nor good »; and after: « Who shall not fear thee, O King of nations » etc. — That we may not fear worldly power, then, we must fear the divine; on account of which he says: *Fear ye him who, after he hath killed, hath power to cast into hell;* whence in the Psalm: « Let all the earth fear the Lord, and let all the inhabitants of the world be in awe of him ». — And because such fear is most useful and necessary, therefore he repeats: *Yea, I say to you, fear him.* Whence Isaias, the eighth chapter[^p312-7]: « Sanctify the Lord of hosts himself, and let him be your fear, and let him be your dread »; and Jeremias, the fifth chapter: « Will you not then fear me? and at my face » etc.

10. (Vers. 6.). But secondly he calls them back from the fear of men, because *they can do nothing against the general patronage of God;* as to which he says: *Are not five sparrows sold for a dipondius? Dipondius* here is the name of a coin having two oboli or asses. Whence Bede[^p312-8]: « A dipondius is a weight composed of two asses. For what in numbers is *one,* this in weights is an as; what is *two,* this is a *dipondius* ». With this agrees, and does not disagree, what is said in Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p312-9]: « Are not two sparrows sold for an as »? Because, as we see, when five eggs are sold for one denarius, two are given for an obolus. Of small price, then, is a sparrow, since so many are found for one denarius; yet not even it is a stranger to the divine patronage. — On account of which he adds: *And not one of them is forgotten before God,* rather it is governed by divine providence, according to that of Wisdom, the eleventh chapter[^p312-10]: « Thou lovest all things that are, and hatest none of the things which thou hast made; for thou didst not appoint anything hating it ». From which it appears that divine providence extends even to the least things; nor does that of the first to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter[^p312-11], contradict it: « Doth God take care for oxen »? For that is understood of the care of *special* providence, by which he leads by the hand along the way of eternal salvation; but this is understood of the providence of *general* administration and conservation, of which in the Psalm: « Who giveth to beasts their food, and to the young ravens that call upon him ». — And note that he says: *Is not forgotten before God;* saying less and signifying more, because nothing befalls them except according to divine providence that orders it; whence Matthew,
<!-- page 313 -->
the tenth chapter[^p313-1]: « One of them shall not fall on the ground without your Father ». And yet sparrows fall most easily; whence they are said to have the falling sickness; and therefore he most of all sets down the example of them as of a manifest thing. From which it is gathered, as *from the lesser,* that divine providence is most of all intent upon the governance of man.

11. (Vers. 7.). For the expression of which he adds: *But even the hairs of your head are all numbered;* from which it is given to be understood that he has the greatest care of man. Whence Bede[^p313-2]: « *But even the hairs,* that I may be silent of the mass of the body; for even the tiny particles are preserved by the providence of God ». Whence the *numbering* refers not so much to cognition as to most diligent conservation, because of the bodies of the suffering Saints not even a hair can perish; whence below, the twenty-first chapter[^p313-3]: « A hair of your head shall not perish ». So great, then, is God's care of us, that not even a hair of our head can be taken away without his nod. Therefore we ought not to be made anxious through fear, according to that of the first of Peter, the last chapter: « Casting all your care upon him, for he hath care of you ». — And this is what he infers: *Fear not therefore: you are of more value than many sparrows,* « that is, of greater price[^p313-4] »; in which are touched at once the conclusion and the ground of the inference, by *the place from the lesser.* For if God has care of the lesser, much more strongly of the greater; and if he has care of us, the world ought not to be feared by us. Now we are of greater price than the brute creatures, than the birds, because we are rational creatures, and this, because we are made to the image of God; on account of which the other things are made subject to man; Genesis, the first chapter[^p313-5]: « Let us make man to our image and likeness: and let him have dominion over the fowls of the air » etc. And therefore man is most immediately under the protection of God, and most of all the just man and the friend of God. And on that account he need not have the help of anything perishing; whence Genesis, the fifteenth chapter[^p313-6]: « Fear not, Abram, I am thy protector, and thy reward exceeding great »; as if he should say: fear not, because thou canst suffer nothing for want of help, and thou canst suffer nothing without the recompense of reward, so long as thou cleavest to me.
