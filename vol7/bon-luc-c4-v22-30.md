# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. IV, Vers. 22-30
## *Secundo affertur testimonium Scripturae ad confutationem detrahentium*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 100 -->

#### Secundo affertur testimonium Scripturae ad confutationem detrahentium.

47. *Et dicebant: Nonne* etc. Postquam introduxit testimonium propheticum ad eruditionem credentium, hic introducit *exemplum authenticum* ad *confutationem detrahentium.* Et quia detractores, cum confutantur, fiunt deteriores, quia efficiuntur persecutores; ideo in hac parte sunt duae. In quarum prima ponitur *confutatio detrahentium;* in secunda vero, *declinatio persequentium,* ibi[^p100-3]: *Et repleti sunt omnes in synagoga* etc.

Prima pars habet duas. In prima proponuntur *detrahentium convicia;* in secunda vero introducuntur *exempla prophetica* detractores confutantia, ibi[^p100-4]: *Ait autem: Non est propheta* etc.

48. (Vers. 22.). Exprimit autem *convicia detrahentium* tripliciter, scilicet per modum *apertae contumeliae,* per modum *occultae ironiae,* per modum *invectionis expressae.* — Per modum, inquam, *apertae contumeliae* exprimit, cum ait: *Et dicebant: Nonne hic est filius Ioseph?* Filium Dei vocabant filium fabri, conceptum de Spiritu sancto et utero virginali dicebant genitum concubitu coniugali; unde Matthaei decimo tertio[^p100-5] dicitur, quod dicebant: « Nonne hic est fabri filius »? Beda: « Magna caecitas, cum, quem verbis et factis Christum esse cognoscunt, ob solam generis notitiam contemnunt ». Simile huic dicebatur de Saul, primi Regum decimo[^p100-6]: « Quaenam res accidit filio Cis? Num et Saul inter Prophetas »? et secundi Regum vigesimo: « Non est nobis pars in David, nec hereditas in filio Isai ». Haec autem dicebant, non omnes, sed mali, scilicet scribae et Pharisaei; unde non contradicit ei quod prius[^p100-7] dictum est, quod « omnes testimonium illi dabant ». Similis modus loquendi est in Scriptura: « Cum occideret eos, quaerebant eum », non mortui, sed alii. Haec autem est consuetudo malorum, ut, quando aliquem laudari audiunt, statim se opponunt; et si non possunt ex parte morum et scientiae, saltem ex parte naturae, secundum illud Ecclesiastici undecimo[^p100-8]: « Bona in mala convertens, insidiatur, et in electis imponet maculam ». — Vel etiam illi iidem, qui prius laudabant, post despiciebant, secundum illud Psalmi: « Laudaverunt laudem eius. Cito fecerunt et obliti sunt operum eius ». — Vel aliter intelligi potest, ut, licet immediate hic narretur, non tamen fiat immediate, sed post factum[^p100-9].

49. (Vers. 23.). Per modum *occultae ironiae* exprimit eorum convicia, cum adiungit: *Et ait illis: Utique dicetis mihi hanc similitudinem;* hoc enim videbat eos occulte cogitare. Dixit ergo: hoc ironice proponitis contra me, quod occulte in cordibus concepistis, quia, secundum quod dicitur Matthaei duodecimo[^p100-10], « ex abundantia cordis os loquitur »; proponitis hanc similitudinem, scilicet per ironiam: *Medice, cura te ipsum;* quod consuevit dici medicis infirmantibus, cum sibi subvenire non possunt, vel nesciunt, aut negligunt; quod est ipsorum derisorium verbum. Sic et tu te ipsum, id est infirmum, cura. Quod dicebant potius ex incredulitate et derisione
<!-- page 101 -->
quam ex humilitate et devotione, sicut Matthaei decimo tertio[^p101-1] dicitur, quod « non fecit ibi virtutes multas, propter incredulitatem eorum ». Unde Beda dicit in Glossa: « Frustra opem misericordiae caelestis exspectas, si alienis virtutibus invides. Aspernator enim est Dominus invidorum, et ab his qui divina beneficia in aliis persequuntur, miracula suae potentiae avertit ». — Et nota, quod ironice vocant *medicum,* quem veraciter deberent confiteri medicum, secundum illud Iob quinto[^p101-2]: « Ipse vulnerat et medetur ». Despiciunt quem deberent colere et honorare, secundum illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo: « Honora medicum propter necessitatem »; et post: « Altissimus de terra creavit medicinam, et vir prudens non abhorrebit illam ».

50. Per modum vero *invectionis expressae* exprimit, cum subiungit: *Quanta audivimus facta in Capharnaum,* scilicet opera miraculosa; unde Matthaei undecimo[^p101-3]: « Vae tibi Capharnaum! quia, si in Tyro et Sidone factae fuissent virtutes » etc. — Sed quomodo haec audierunt[^p101-4], cum Evangelista nondum expresserit, aliqua miracula ipsum fecisse? — Propter quod attendendum, quod Evangelista non prosequitur ordinem rei gestae, sed ordinem intentionis suae, et hoc quidem facit pluries; et ideo, cum unus Evangelista narrat post, quod alius ante, non est repugnantia, quia non intendunt dicere, eo ordine esse facta, quo sunt scripta, sicut dicit Augustinus, de Concordantia Evangelistarum[^p101-5]. Praeterea, Lucas cum aliis duobus, Matthaeo et Marco, nihil narrat de primo adventu Christi in Galilaeam, sed de secundo; de primo autem determinat solus Ioannes[^p101-6]. Ex fama ergo miraculorum factorum in terra aliena arguebant eum, quia non faciebat in terra propria. — Unde additur: *Fac et hic in patria tua;* et hoc est dicere: cura te ipsum, id est, illos cura de patria tua per miracula, sicut alios, secundum illud Ecclesiastici decimo quarto[^p101-7]: « Qui sibi nequam est, cui alii bonus erit »? et primae ad Timotheum quinto: « Si quis suorum et maxime domesticorum curam non habet, fidem negavit »; dicitur Ecclesiastici decimo septimo: « Unicuique mandavit de proximo suo ».

51. (Vers. 24.). *Ait autem: Amen dico* etc. Hic post detrahentium convicia subiunguntur *exempla prophetica,* detractores[^p101-8] confutantia. Ad plenam autem ipsorum confutationem praemittitur *generale proverbium;* secundo, *speciale exemplum* in *Elia,* eximio Prophetarum; tertio, in *Eliseo,* discipulo ipsius. Primum est ad commonendum, secundum ad confutandum, tertium ad confirmandum.

*Generale* igitur *proverbium* in omni Propheta et populo praemittit, cum dicit: *Ait autem,* scilicet Christus: *Amen dico vobis,* id est certitudinaliter, quia *amen* signum est affirmationis. Et vere, quia, sicut ipse dicit Matthaei vigesimo quarto[^p101-9], « caelum et terra transibunt, verba autem mea non transibunt ». — *Quia nemo propheta acceptus est in patria sua,* scilicet nec quantum ad doctrinam nec quantum ad vitam, in quibus Prophetae consueverunt habere excellentiam. Et ideo non est mirum, si in patria sua non facit miracula, quae pro ingratis et indignis non sunt facienda. Huic Salvatoris invectioni attestatur, quod Ioseph a fratribus venditur, Genesis trigesimo septimo[^p101-10], ab Aegyptiis honoratur, Genesis quadragesimo primo. Sic Moyses ab Aaron et Maria contumeliam patitur, Numerorum duodecimo, et a contribulibus obiurgatur, Numerorum decimo sexto. Sic Abraham a compatriotis patitur; unde dicitur ei: « Egredere de terra tua et de cognatione », Genesis duodecimo. — Rationem autem huius assignat Beda in Glossa[^p101-11]: « Ceteri Prophetae minus in patria quam in ceteris civitatibus sunt honorati, quia propemodum naturale est, cives concivibus invidere. Non enim considerant praesentia viri opera, non virtutem, sed fragilem recordantur infantiam, quasi non et ipsi per eosdem aetatis gradus ad maturam aetatem pervenerint ».

52. (Vers. 25.). *Speciale* vero *exemplum* in *Elia,* Prophetarum eximio, adiungit, cum dicit: *In veritate dico vobis;* Ioannis decimo septimo[^p101-12]: « Sermo
<!-- page 102 -->
tuus veritas est ». *In veritate* dicebat, cui credendum, et tamen non credebant; unde illud Ioannis octavo: « Si veritatem dico, quare non creditis mihi »? et illud Iob sexto: « Quare detraxistis sermonibus veritatis »? Et haec est veritas certa, quia sumitur de Scriptura. — Unde additur: *Multae viduae erant in diebus Eliae in Israel,* quae scilicet erant in necessitate constitutae propter *defectum matrimonialis societatis;* Ieremiae decimo quinto[^p102-1]: « Multiplicatae sunt viduae eius super arenam maris ». — Erant in necessitate constitutae propter *defectum pluviae caelestis;* unde addit: *Quando clausum est caelum, et non pluit*[^p102-2] *annis tribus et mensibus sex;* sicut Dominus comminatus fuerat Levitici vigesimo sexto: « Sin autem nec sic obedieritis mihi, dabo caelum desuper sicut ferreum »; et Deuteronomii vigesimo octavo: « Sit caelum, quod supra te est, aeneum ». Et sic factum est propter peccata eorum; unde Ieremiae tertio: « Polluisti terram in fornicationibus tuis. Idcirco prohibitae sunt stillae pluviarum, et serotinus imber non fuit ». — Erant in necessitate constitutae propter *defectum terrestris alimoniae;* et ideo additur: *Cum facta est*[^p102-3] *fames magna in omni terra;* sicut dicitur tertii Regum decimo octavo: « Erat fames vehemens » etc.; unde verificatum erat de filiis Israel illud Psalmi: « Famem patientur ut canes et circuibunt civitatem », quia circuibat rex et dispensator domus suae ad invenienda pascua, tertii Regum decimo octavo.

53. (Vers. 26.). Et licet essent in tanta necessitate, subiungitur: *Et ad nullam illarum missus est Elias,* ut eas per miraculum relevaret a penuria famis, ut sic verificaretur in eis illud Psalmi[^p102-4]: « Esurientes et sitientes, anima eorum in ipsis defecit ». Sed *extraneam* relevavit, unde addit: *Nisi in Sarepta Sidoniae ad mulierem viduam,* secundum quod scribitur tertii Regum decimo septimo: Dictum est Eliae: « Surge et vade in Sarepta Sidoniorum et manebis ibi; praecepi enim ibi mulieri viduae, ut pascat te », immo ipsa per te.

54. (Vers. 27.). Aliud exemplum ad confirmationem praedicti de *Eliseo* introducitur, cum ait: *Et multi leprosi erant in Israel sub Eliseo propheta;* unde quarti Regum septimo[^p102-5] dicitur, quod tempore Elisei « quatuor viri leprosi erant iuxta portam Samariae », et multi alii in necessitate consimili. *Et nemo eorum mundatus est nisi Naaman Syrus* — quarti Regum quinto, ubi dicitur, quod Naaman ad verbum Elisei lavit se septies in Iordane et restitutus est plenariae sanitati — et hoc, iusto iudicio Dei, quia non erant digni. Et *a simili* et secundum imitationem praedecessorum ostendebat, eos esse indignos ad miraculorum susceptionem. Unde simile in Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p102-6]: « Testes estis vobismetipsis, quia estis filii eorum qui occiderunt Prophetas; et vos implete mensuram patrum vestrorum »; et Actuum septimo: « Dura cervice et incircumcisi cordibus et auribus, vos semper Spiritui sancto resistitis, sicut patres vestri, et vos. Quem enim Prophetarum non sunt persecuti patres vestri » etc. Et sic manifeste sunt convicti, quia defectus miraculorum non erat imputandus impotentiae vel negligentiae Christi, sed potius suae invidiae et incredulitati; unde digni erant miraculis privari, sicut privati etiam fuerant patres sui.

55. Et nota hic *spiritualiter,* quod in his duobus exemplis ostendit indignitatem Iudaeorum respectu gentilium quantum ad universalem *defectum* poenitentiae et universale *contagium* malitiae, quod in Iudaeis remansit non curatum, sed in gentibus est remediatum per Christum. *Clausio* enim *caeli* designat defectum gratiae et doctrinae; Deuteronomii undecimo[^p102-7]: « Cavete, ne recedatis a Domino »; et deinceps: « Et iratus Dominus claudat caelum, et pluvia non descendat, et terra non det germen suum ». De qua pluvia in Psalmo: « Pluviam voluntariam segregabit Deus »; et ex hoc fit fames et indigentia omnis boni; Iob decimo octavo: « Attenuetur fame robur eius, et inedia invadat costas eius ». — Et nota, quod dicitur subtracta esse pluvia annis tribus et mensibus sex, quia tribus annis et dimidio, quibus Dominus praedicavit, non receperunt pluviam gratiae, exigentibus peccatis suis. Quamvis enim super se veniret imber, Iudaea tamen non recepit ad fructificandum; ad Hebraeos sexto[^p102-8]: « Terra super se venientem bibens imbrem et germinans herbam opportunam illis, a quibus colitur, accipit benedictionem a Deo; proferens autem spinas et tribulos, reproba est et maledicto proxima ». — In hoc autem tempore mittitur verus Elias praedicator ad viduam in Sarepta Sidoniae, quae recte designat gentilitatem, sicut ostendit Beda in magna Glossa[^p102-9], et salvata est, populo derelicto Iudaeorum, secundum illud Actuum decimo
<!-- page 103 -->
tertio: « Quoniam repellitis verbum Dei et indignos vos iudicatis aeternae vitae; ecce, convertimur ad gentes ».

56. *Infectio* autem *leprae* designat corruptionem malitiae, quae fuit in omnibus ante adventum Christi, secundum illud Psalmi[^p103-1]: « Corrupti sunt et abominabiles facti sunt » etc. Ab hac non est curata Iudaea; Ezechielis vigesimo quarto: « Mundare te volui, et non es mundata a sordibus; sed nec mundaberis prius, donec quiescere faciam indignationem meam in te ». Sed Naaman mundatur, qui interpretatur *decor,* et designat gentium populum Sacramento baptismi decorum factum et omni foeditate mentis et corporis expurgatum. Et Osee octavo[^p103-2]: « Usquequo non poterit emundari »? secundum illud Ezechielis trigesimo sexto: « Effundam super vos aquam mundam, et mundabimini ab omnibus inquinamentis vestris ». — Et sic secundum utrumque intellectum fuit hic confutatio infidelium Iudaeorum.

57. (Vers. 28.). *Et repleti sunt omnes* etc. Postquam descripta est confutatio detrahentium, subiungitur hic *declinatio persequentium.* Habet autem haec pars duas. In prima describitur *malignantium persecutio;* in secunda vero, *persequentium declinatio,* ibi[^p103-3]: *Ipse autem transiens* etc. — Ad descriptionem ergo *persecutionis malignantium* tria introducuntur, scilicet *iracundia mentis, violentia operis* et *perfidia ex parte finis intenti.*

Insinuat ergo *iracundiam mentis* in hoc quod dicit: *Et repleti sunt omnes in synagoga ira, haec audientes.* Unde verificatum est illud Amos quinto[^p103-4]: « Odio habuerunt corripientem in porta » etc.; propter quod dicitur Proverbiorum nono: « Noli arguere derisorem, ne oderit te; argue sapientem, et diliget te ». Unde ex auditu veritatis non sunt eruditi, sed potius excaecati et irati; contra quod dicitur Ecclesiastae septimo[^p103-5]: « Noli esse ad irascendum facilis, quia ira in sinu stulti requiescit »; et Iob quinto: « Vere stultum interficit iracundia », quia, sicut dicitur Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo, « ira non habet misericordiam nec erumpens furor ».

58. (Vers. 29.). *Violentiam* autem *operis* insinuat, cum addit: *Et surrexerunt et eiecerunt illum extra civitatem,* tanquam blasphemum; sic enim erat praeceptum; Levitici vigesimo quarto[^p103-6]: « Educ blasphemum extra castra, et lapidet eum omnis populus ». Et sic verificata est illa parabola, Matthaei vigesimo primo, qua dicitur, quod filium patrisfamilias « eiecerunt coloni extra vineam; vinea enim Domini exercituum domus Israel est », Isaiae quinto. Sic fecerunt Stephano, Actuum septimo: « Impetum fecerunt unanimiter in eum et eiecerunt eum extra civitatem » etc. Unde Salvator poterat eis dicere illud Iudicum undecimo[^p103-7]: « Nonne vos estis, qui odistis me et eiecistis extra domum patris mei »?

59. *Perfidiam* autem *ex parte finis intenti* explicat, cum adiungit: *Et duxerunt eum*[^p103-8] *usque ad supercilium montis,* quasi dicat: non per portam, *super quem civitas illorum erat aedificata.* Ad locum praeruptum ducebant ad praecipitium; unde poterat dici populo illi insano illud sapientis mulieris, secundi Regum vigesimo: « Quare praecipitas hereditatem Domini »? In hoc autem, quod praecipitare eum volebant, « peiores diabolo erant, sicut dicit Beda[^p103-9], quia ille dixit tantum verbo: *Mitte te deorsum;* isti conati sunt facto »; sed non valuerunt, quia ipse potestatem habebat ponendi animam suam, sicut dicitur Ioannis decimo. Unde Beda[^p103-10]: « Non a Nazaraeis praecipitari, non a Ierosolymitis lapidari, non inter pueros ab Herode perimi; sed crucis exspectare voluit triumphum. Ipsa enim est typus monarchiae singularis, *ut in nomine Iesu omne genu flectatur* etc., ad Philippenses secundo. Et hinc est, quod cacumina crucis caelos ostendunt, ima petunt inferos, cornua terram tegunt ».

60. (Vers. 30.). *Ipse autem transiens* etc. Hic notatur *declinatio persequentium* perfecta, quia fuit sine *detentione,* sine *impulsione,* sine *portatione*[^p103-11]; unde non poterant sibi violentiam facere nec trahendo nec impellendo nec portando.

Ad ostendendum igitur, quod non fuit *violentia detentionis,* dicitur: *Ipse autem transiens;* unde Ambrosius[^p103-12]: « Quod capitur a paucis et tenetur, voluntatis est; quod a populo non tenetur, sed transit, maiestatis ». Unde nihil poterat actum virtutis suae
<!-- page 104 -->
retardare; Canticorum secundo[^p104-1]: « Ecce, iste venit saliens in montibus, transiliens colles ». Fuit autem iste transitus a virtute propria, sed Petri in virtute aliena; Actuum duodecimo: « Transeuntes autem primam et secundam custodiam, venerunt » etc.

61. Ad ostendendum, quod non fuit *violentia impulsionis,* subditur: *Per medium eorum*[^p104-2], certus, quod non posset ab aliquo superari; unde poterat dicere illud Psalmi: « Si consistant adversum me castra, non timebit cor meum ». Simile dicitur Ioannis decimo: « Quaerebant eum Iudaei apprehendere », scilicet ad lapidandum, « et exivit de manibus eorum ». *Per medium* vadit, quia « mediator Dei et hominum[^p104-3] » semper diligit medium; unde Ioannis primo: « Medius vestrum stetit quem vos nescitis ».

62. Ad ostendendum ulterius, quod non fuit *violentia portationis,* subditur: *ibat,* scilicet propria virtute, sicut animalia, de quibus Ezechielis primo[^p104-4] dicitur, quod « ibant et revertebantur in similitudinem fulguris coruscantis ». *Ibat,* scilicet non in praeceps, sed descendebat, sicut dicit Beda[^p104-5], sub rupe, illi cedente petra et ipsum suscipiente, ut illi ad eum non possent attingere; unde poterat dicere illud Ioannis octavo: « Quo ego vado, vos non potestis venire ». In quo ostenditur simul Domini *potentia,* quia non potuerunt illum laedere, sed etiam lapides obediebant ei; ostenditur et *patientia,* quia cessit nec eos laesit, sicut Ioannis octavo[^p104-6] dicitur, quod « tulerunt lapides Iudaei, ut iacerent in eum; Iesus autem abscondit se et exivit de templo ».

63. Nota hic, quod quatuor modis est Domino Iesu mors intentata. Quidam tentaverunt *gladio* perimere, sicut Herodes, Matthaei secundo[^p104-7]; quidam *praecipitio,* sicut hic; quidam *lapidatione,* sicut Iudaei, Ioannis octavo; quidam *crucifixione,* Matthaei vigesimo septimo. — *Spiritualiter* enim Christus *crucifigitur* in nostra *recidivatione;* unde dicitur de his qui prolapsi sunt, ad Hebraeos sexto[^p104-8]: « Rursus sibimetipsis crucifigentes Filium Dei » etc. *Lapidatur* in *obduratione;* Matthaei vigesimo tertio: « Ierusalem, Ierusalem, quae occidis Prophetas et lapidas eos » etc., ubi ostenditur obduratio Iudaeorum. *Praecipitatur* autem in *desperatione;* Ieremiae vigesimo secundo[^p104-9]: « Succident electam cedrum tuam et praecipitabunt eam in ignem »; et hoc est in desperatione. Sed *gladio perimitur* in *blasphematione,* secundum illud Psalmi: « Filii hominum, dentes eorum arma et sagittae, et lingua eorum gladius acutus ».

## English
<!-- page 100 -->

#### Second, the testimony of Scripture is brought forward for the confutation of those who detract.

47. *And they said: Is not* etc. After he introduced the prophetic testimony for the erudition of believers, here he introduces an *authentic example* for the *confutation of those who detract.* And since detractors, when they are confuted, become worse, because they are made persecutors, therefore in this part there are two. In the first of which is set down the *confutation of those who detract;* but in the second, the *turning aside from those who persecute,* there[^p100-3]: *And all they in the synagogue were filled* etc.

The first part has two. In the first are set forth the *insults of those who detract;* but in the second are introduced *prophetic examples* confuting the detractors, there[^p100-4]: *And he said: No prophet* etc.

48. (Vers. 22.). Now he expresses the *insults of those who detract* in three ways, namely by way of *open contumely,* by way of *hidden irony,* by way of *express invective.* — By way, I say, of *open contumely* he expresses it, when he says: *And they said: Is not this the son of Joseph?* The Son of God they called the son of a carpenter; him conceived of the Holy Spirit and of a virginal womb they said was begotten by conjugal intercourse; hence in Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p100-5], it is said that they said: « Is not this the carpenter's son »? Bede: « Great blindness, when him whom by words and deeds they know to be the Christ, they despise solely on account of the knowledge of his kindred ». Like this was said of Saul, the first of Kings, the tenth chapter[^p100-6]: « What is this that hath happened to the son of Cis? Is Saul also among the Prophets »? and the second of Kings, the twentieth chapter: « We have no part in David, nor inheritance in the son of Isai ». But these things were said, not by all, but by the wicked, namely the scribes and Pharisees; hence it does not contradict what was said before[^p100-7], that « all gave testimony to him ». There is a like manner of speaking in Scripture: « When he slew them, then they sought him », not the dead, but others. Now this is the custom of the wicked, that, when they hear someone praised, at once they set themselves against it; and if they cannot on the side of morals and knowledge, at least on the side of nature, according to that saying of Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter[^p100-8]: « Turning good into evil, he lieth in wait, and on the elect he will lay a blot ». — Or else those very same, who before were praising, afterwards were despising, according to that saying of the Psalm: « They sang his praises. They had quickly done, and they forgot his works ». — Or it can be understood otherwise, that, although it is narrated here immediately, yet it did not happen immediately, but after the deed[^p100-9].

49. (Vers. 23.). By way of *hidden irony* he expresses their insults, when he adds: *And he said to them: Doubtless you will say to me this similitude;* for he saw that they were thinking this in hidden fashion. He said therefore: this you put forward ironically against me, which you have conceived hiddenly in your hearts, because, according to what is said in Matthew, the twelfth chapter[^p100-10], « out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh »; you put forward this similitude, namely by irony: *Physician, heal thyself;* which is wont to be said to physicians who are sick, when they cannot help themselves, or do not know how, or neglect to; which is a word of derision among them. So do thou too heal thyself, that is, the sick one. Which they said rather out of unbelief and derision
<!-- page 101 -->
than out of humility and devotion, just as it is said in Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p101-1], that « he wrought not many miracles there, because of their unbelief ». Hence Bede says in the Gloss: « In vain dost thou expect the help of heavenly mercy, if thou envy the powers of others. For the Lord is a despiser of the envious, and from those who persecute the divine benefits in others he turns away the miracles of his power ». — And note that ironically they call him *physician* whom they ought truly to confess a physician, according to that saying of Job, the fifth chapter[^p101-2]: « He woundeth and cureth ». They despise him whom they ought to worship and honour, according to that saying of Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter: « Honour the physician for the need thou hast of him »; and after: « The most High hath created medicine out of the earth, and a prudent man will not abhor it ».

50. But by way of *express invective* he expresses it, when he subjoins: *As great things as we have heard done in Capharnaum,* namely miraculous works; hence Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p101-3]: « Woe to thee, Capharnaum! for if in Tyre and Sidon the miracles had been wrought » etc. — But how did they hear these things[^p101-4], when the Evangelist has not yet expressed that he did any miracles? — On account of which it must be attended to that the Evangelist does not pursue the order of the thing done, but the order of his intention, and this indeed he does many times; and therefore, when one Evangelist narrates after what another narrates before, there is no contradiction, because they do not intend to say that things were done in the order in which they are written, as Augustine says, *On the Agreement of the Evangelists*[^p101-5]. Moreover, Luke with the other two, Matthew and Mark, narrates nothing of the first coming of Christ into Galilee, but of the second; but of the first John alone treats[^p101-6]. From the fame of the miracles done in a foreign land, therefore, they were reproaching him, because he was not doing them in his own land. — Hence there is added: *Do also here in thy own country;* and this is to say: heal thyself, that is, heal those of thy own country by miracles, as thou didst others, according to that saying of Ecclesiasticus, the fourteenth chapter[^p101-7]: « He that is evil to himself, to whom else will he be good »? and of the first to Timothy, the fifth chapter: « If any man have not care of his own, and especially of those of his house, he hath denied the faith »; it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the seventeenth chapter: « He gave to every one commandment concerning his neighbour ».

51. (Vers. 24.). *And he said: Amen I say* etc. Here after the insults of those who detract are subjoined *prophetic examples,* confuting the detractors[^p101-8]. Now for their full confutation there is put first a *general proverb;* second, a *special example* in *Elias,* the most eminent of the Prophets; third, in *Eliseus,* his disciple. The first is for admonishing, the second for confuting, the third for confirming.

The *general proverb,* then, about every Prophet and people he puts first, when he says: *And he said,* namely Christ: *Amen I say to you,* that is, with certitude, because *amen* is a sign of affirmation. And truly, because, as he himself says in Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p101-9], « heaven and earth shall pass, but my words shall not pass ». — *That no prophet is accepted in his own country,* namely neither as to doctrine nor as to life, in which the Prophets were wont to have excellence. And therefore it is no wonder if in his own country he does not do miracles, which are not to be done for the ungrateful and unworthy. To this invective of the Saviour it bears witness that Joseph is sold by his brethren, Genesis, the thirty-seventh chapter[^p101-10], is honoured by the Egyptians, Genesis, the forty-first chapter. So Moses suffers contumely from Aaron and Mary, Numbers, the twelfth chapter, and is chidden by his fellow-tribesmen, Numbers, the sixteenth chapter. So Abraham suffers from his fellow-countrymen; hence it is said to him: « Go forth out of thy country, and from thy kindred », Genesis, the twelfth chapter. — But Bede assigns the reason of this in the Gloss[^p101-11]: « The other Prophets were less honoured in their own country than in the other cities, because it is almost natural for citizens to envy fellow-citizens. For they do not consider the present works of the man, nor his virtue, but they recall his frail infancy, as if they themselves had not come by the same steps of age to mature age ».

52. (Vers. 25.). But the *special example* in *Elias,* the most eminent of the Prophets, he adds, when he says: *In truth I say to you;* John, the seventeenth chapter[^p101-12]: « Thy word
<!-- page 102 -->
is truth ». *In truth* he was speaking, he who was to be believed, and yet they did not believe; hence that saying of John, the eighth chapter: « If I say the truth, why do you not believe me »? and that of Job, the sixth chapter: « Why have you detracted the words of truth »? And this is certain truth, because it is taken from Scripture. — Hence there is added: *There were many widows in the days of Elias in Israel,* who namely were set in need on account of the *lack of matrimonial fellowship;* Jeremias, the fifteenth chapter[^p102-1]: « Her widows are multiplied above the sand of the sea ». — They were set in need on account of the *lack of heavenly rain;* hence he adds: *When heaven was shut up, and it rained not*[^p102-2] *three years and six months;* as the Lord had threatened in Leviticus, the twenty-sixth chapter: « But if not even so you will obey me, I will make the heaven above as iron »; and in Deuteronomy, the twenty-eighth chapter: « Be the heaven that is over thee, of brass ». And so it was done on account of their sins; hence Jeremias, the third chapter: « Thou hast polluted the land with thy fornications. Therefore the drops of rain were withholden, and there was no latter rain ». — They were set in need on account of the *lack of earthly nourishment;* and therefore there is added: *When there was*[^p102-3] *a great famine throughout all the earth;* as is said in the third of Kings, the eighteenth chapter: « There was a grievous famine » etc.; hence that saying of the Psalm had been verified of the children of Israel: « They shall suffer hunger like dogs, and shall go round about the city », because the king and the steward of his house went round to find pasture, the third of Kings, the eighteenth chapter.

53. (Vers. 26.). And although they were in such great need, there is subjoined: *And to none of them was Elias sent,* to relieve them by a miracle from the penury of famine, so that thus might be verified in them that saying of the Psalm[^p102-4]: « Hungry and thirsty, their soul fainted in them ». But he relieved a *foreign* woman, hence he adds: *But to Sarepta of Sidon, to a widow woman,* according to what is written in the third of Kings, the seventeenth chapter: It was said to Elias: « Arise, and go to Sarephta of the Sidonians, and dwell there; for I have commanded a widow woman there to feed thee », nay rather, she through thee.

54. (Vers. 27.). Another example, for the confirmation of the foregoing, is introduced about *Eliseus,* when he says: *And there were many lepers in Israel under Eliseus the prophet;* hence in the fourth of Kings, the seventh chapter[^p102-5], it is said that in the time of Eliseus « four leprous men were by the gate of Samaria », and many others in like need. *And none of them was cleansed but Naaman the Syrian* — the fourth of Kings, the fifth chapter, where it is said that Naaman at the word of Eliseus washed himself seven times in the Jordan and was restored to full health — and this, by the just judgement of God, because they were not worthy. And *from the like* and according to the imitation of their predecessors he was showing that they were unworthy of receiving miracles. Hence the like in Matthew, the twenty-third chapter[^p102-6]: « You are witnesses to yourselves, that you are the sons of them that killed the Prophets; and do you fill up the measure of your fathers »; and in Acts, the seventh chapter: « You stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, you always resist the Holy Spirit, as your fathers did, so do you also. For which of the Prophets have not your fathers persecuted » etc. And so they are manifestly convicted, because the lack of miracles was not to be imputed to the impotence or negligence of Christ, but rather to their own envy and unbelief; hence they were worthy to be deprived of miracles, just as their fathers too had been deprived.

55. And note here *spiritually* that in these two examples he shows the unworthiness of the Jews in respect of the Gentiles as to the universal *lack* of penance and the universal *contagion* of malice, which remained uncured in the Jews, but in the Gentiles has been remedied through Christ. For the *shutting of heaven* designates the lack of grace and of doctrine; Deuteronomy, the eleventh chapter[^p102-7]: « Beware lest you depart from the Lord »; and next: « And the Lord being angry shut up heaven, and the rain come not down, and the earth yield not her fruit ». Of which rain in the Psalm: « A free rain God shall set aside »; and from this comes famine and the want of every good; Job, the eighteenth chapter: « Let his strength be wasted with famine, and let hunger invade his ribs ». — And note that the rain is said to have been withdrawn for three years and six months, because for the three years and a half in which the Lord preached they did not receive the rain of grace, their sins requiring it. For although the shower came upon them, Judea yet did not receive it for bearing fruit; to the Hebrews, the sixth chapter[^p102-8]: « The earth that drinketh in the rain which cometh upon it, and bringeth forth herbs meet for them by whom it is tilled, receiveth blessing from God; but bringing forth thorns and briers, it is reprobate and very near unto a curse ». — Now in this time the true Elias, the preacher, is sent to the widow in Sarepta of Sidon, who rightly designates the Gentile world, as Bede shows in the great Gloss[^p102-9], and she was saved, the people of the Jews being forsaken, according to that saying of Acts, the thirteenth
<!-- page 103 -->
chapter: « Because you reject the word of God and judge yourselves unworthy of eternal life, behold, we turn to the Gentiles ».

56. But the *infection of leprosy* designates the corruption of malice, which was in all before the coming of Christ, according to that saying of the Psalm[^p103-1]: « They are corrupt and are become abominable » etc. From this Judea was not cured; Ezechiel, the twenty-fourth chapter: « I desired to cleanse thee, and thou art not cleansed from thy filthiness; but neither shalt thou be cleansed before I cause my indignation to rest in thee ». But Naaman is cleansed, who is interpreted *comeliness,* and he designates the people of the Gentiles made comely by the Sacrament of baptism and purged of every foulness of mind and body. And Osee, the eighth chapter[^p103-2]: « How long will he be incapable of being cleansed »? according to that saying of Ezechiel, the thirty-sixth chapter: « I will pour upon you clean water, and you shall be cleansed from all your filthiness ». — And so according to each understanding there was here a confutation of the unbelieving Jews.

57. (Vers. 28.). *And all they were filled* etc. After the confutation of those who detract has been described, there is subjoined here the *turning aside from those who persecute.* Now this part has two. In the first is described the *persecution of the malignant;* but in the second, the *turning aside from those who persecute,* there[^p103-3]: *But he passing through* etc. — For the description, then, of the *persecution of the malignant* three things are introduced, namely *wrath of mind, violence of deed* and *perfidy on the part of the end intended.*

He intimates, then, *wrath of mind* in this, that he says: *And all they in the synagogue, hearing these things, were filled with anger.* Hence was verified that saying of Amos, the fifth chapter[^p103-4]: « They have hated him that rebuketh in the gate » etc.; on account of which it is said in Proverbs, the ninth chapter: « Rebuke not a scorner, lest he hate thee; rebuke a wise man, and he will love thee ». Hence from the hearing of the truth they were not schooled, but rather blinded and angered; against which it is said in Ecclesiastes, the seventh chapter[^p103-5]: « Be not easy to anger, for anger resteth in the bosom of a fool »; and Job, the fifth chapter: « Truly anger killeth the foolish », because, as is said in Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter, « anger hath no mercy, nor fury when it breaketh forth ».

58. (Vers. 29.). But he intimates *violence of deed,* when he adds: *And they rose up and thrust him out of the city,* as a blasphemer; for so it had been commanded; Leviticus, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p103-6]: « Bring forth the blasphemer without the camp, and let all the people stone him ». And so was verified that parable, Matthew, the twenty-first chapter, in which it is said that the son of the householder « the husbandmen cast forth out of the vineyard; for the vineyard of the Lord of hosts is the house of Israel », Isaias, the fifth chapter. So they did to Stephen, Acts, the seventh chapter: « They ran violently with one accord upon him and cast him forth without the city » etc. Hence the Saviour could say to them that saying of Judges, the eleventh chapter[^p103-7]: « Are not you they who hated me and cast me out of the house of my father »?

59. But he sets forth the *perfidy on the part of the end intended,* when he adds: *And they brought him*[^p103-8] *to the brow of the hill,* as if to say: not by the gate, *whereon their city was built.* To a precipitous place they were leading him for casting him down headlong; hence there could be said to that mad people that saying of the wise woman, the second of Kings, the twentieth chapter: « Why dost thou cast down headlong the inheritance of the Lord »? Now in this, that they wished to cast him down headlong, « they were worse than the devil, as Bede says[^p103-9], because he said only in word: *Cast thyself down;* these attempted it in deed »; but they did not prevail, because he had power to lay down his life, as is said in John, the tenth chapter. Hence Bede[^p103-10]: « Not to be cast down headlong by the Nazarenes, not to be stoned by those of Jerusalem, not to be slain among the children by Herod; but he willed to await the triumph of the cross. For it is the type of a singular monarchy, *that in the name of Jesus every knee should bow* etc., to the Philippians, the second chapter. And hence it is that the summits of the cross show the heavens, its lowest part seeks the lower regions, its arms cover the earth ».

60. (Vers. 30.). *But he passing through* etc. Here is noted the *turning aside from those who persecute,* complete, because it was without *detention,* without *impulsion,* without *carrying*[^p103-11]; hence they could not do him violence either by dragging or by pushing or by carrying.

To show, then, that there was not the *violence of detention,* it is said: *But he passing through;* hence Ambrose[^p103-12]: « That he is taken by a few and held, is of his will; that he is not held by the people, but passes through, is of his majesty ». Hence nothing could retard the act of his power;
<!-- page 104 -->
the Canticle, the second chapter[^p104-1]: « Behold, he cometh leaping upon the mountains, skipping over the hills ». Now this passing was by his own power, but Peter's by another's power; Acts, the twelfth chapter: « And passing through the first and the second ward, they came » etc.

61. To show that there was not the *violence of impulsion,* there is subjoined: *Through the midst of them*[^p104-2], certain that he could not be overcome by anyone; hence he could say that saying of the Psalm: « If armies in camp should stand together against me, my heart shall not fear ». The like is said in John, the tenth chapter: « The Jews sought to take him », namely for stoning, « and he went forth out of their hands ». *Through the midst* he goes, because « the mediator of God and men[^p104-3] » always loves the middle; hence John, the first chapter: « One stood in the midst of you, whom you know not ».

62. To show further that there was not the *violence of carrying,* there is subjoined: *went his way,* namely by his own power, like the living creatures of which it is said in Ezechiel, the first chapter[^p104-4], that « they went and returned after the likeness of flashing lightning ». *He went,* namely not headlong, but he was descending, as Bede says[^p104-5], beneath the rock, the stone yielding to him and receiving him, so that they could not reach him; hence he could say that saying of John, the eighth chapter: « Whither I go, you cannot come ». In which is shown at once the Lord's *power,* because they could not hurt him, but even the stones obeyed him; there is shown also his *patience,* because he gave way and did not hurt them, just as it is said in John, the eighth chapter[^p104-6], that « the Jews took up stones to cast at him; but Jesus hid himself and went out of the temple ».

63. Note here that in four ways was death attempted against the Lord Jesus. Some tried to slay him by the *sword,* as Herod, Matthew, the second chapter[^p104-7]; some by *casting down headlong,* as here; some by *stoning,* as the Jews, John, the eighth chapter; some by *crucifixion,* Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter. — For *spiritually* Christ *is crucified* in our *relapse;* hence it is said of those who have fallen away, to the Hebrews, the sixth chapter[^p104-8]: « Crucifying again to themselves the Son of God » etc. He *is stoned* in *obduracy;* Matthew, the twenty-third chapter: « Jerusalem, Jerusalem, thou that killest the Prophets and stonest them » etc., where the obduracy of the Jews is shown. But he *is cast down headlong* in *despair;* Jeremias, the twenty-second chapter[^p104-9]: « They shall cut down thy chosen cedar and shall cast it headlong into the fire »; and this is in despair. But he *is slain by the sword* in *blasphemy,* according to that saying of the Psalm: « The sons of men, whose teeth are weapons and arrows, and their tongue a sharp sword ».
