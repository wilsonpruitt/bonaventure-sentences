# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Lucae — Cap. V, Vers. 27-39
## *Secundo, quomodo vocentur discipuli de statu culpae*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 128 -->

#### Secundo, quomodo vocentur discipuli de statu culpae.

63. *Et post haec exiit* etc. Supra[^p128-10] egit Evangelista de vocatione discipulorum de statu licito sive iustitiae; hic agit de vocatione ipsorum de *statu periculoso* sive iniustitiae, ubi ostendit, quomodo vocavit Matthaeum sedentem ad telonium. Habet autem pars ista duas, in quarum prima agit de *vocationis perfectione;* in secunda vero, de *vocatorum defensione,* ibi: *Factum est autem in Sabbato* etc., proximo capitulo[^p128-11].

Circa *vocationis perfectionem* introducuntur tria: primum est *vocatio peccatorum;* secundum est *confutatio superborum,* ibi[^p128-12]: *Et murmurabant Pharisaei* etc.; tertium est *consolatio infirmorum,* ibi: *At illi dixerunt ad eum* etc.

*Vocatio* autem *publicani* ostenditur perfecta ex tribus, scilicet ex parte *divini doni praevenientis* et ex parte *liberi arbitrii consentientis* et ex parte *devoti obsequii consequentis.*

64. (Vers. 27.). Primo igitur ostenditur perfecta ex parte *divini doni praevenientis,* cum dicitur: *Et post haec exiit et vidit publicanum* etc. Et nota,
<!-- page 129 -->
quod dicitur *exire, videre* et *vocare. Exit* quidem, ut requirat; *videt,* ut attrahat; *affatur* et *vocat,* ut ad se convertat. *Exiit* quidem per sollicitudinem requirendi opus suum, sicut in Psalmo[^p129-1] dicitur: « Exibit homo ad opus suum et ad operationem suam usque ad vesperam ». Unde significatur per patremfamilias, de quo Matthaei vigesimo dicitur, quod « exiit primo mane conducere operarios in vineam suam ». Hic est vere Dominus Iesus, qui dicit de se ipso Ioannis decimo sexto: « Exivi a Patre et veni in mundum », quia de secreto sinu venit in manifestum. — Nec tantum exiit, sed *et vidit publicanum,* ut per respectum misericordiae ipsum ad se attrahat, sicut petebat ille *videri* in Psalmo[^p129-2]: « Vide humilitatem meam et laborem meum » etc. Et hoc modo *vidit* istum, quia, licet esset peccator in facto, erat tamen electus in divino proposito; Sapientiae quarto: « Respectus eius in electos ipsius »; unde dicitur infra vigesimo secundo: « Conversus Dominus, respexit Petrum, et egressus foras Petrus, flevit amare ».

65. Hoc respectu *vidit* Iesus *publicanum nomine Levi,* id est Matthaeum, qui, ut dicit Hieronymus[^p129-3], fuit binomius; sed Lucas et Marcus propter honorem Evangelistae nomen tacent vulgatum, Matthaeus autem in sermonis principio accusator sui factus, Matthaeum se et publicanum nominat. — Et nota, quod *publicani* dicebantur qui publice peccabant, sicut accipitur infra decimo octavo[^p129-4]: « Duo homines ascenderunt in templum: unus Pharisaeus, et alter publicanus »; vel, qui merces vendendo omnibus publicabant, sicut mercatores facere solent, sicut accipitur supra tertio; vel, qui publica vectigalia exigebant; et sic videtur proprie accipi hic.

66. Unde subditur: *Sedentem ad telonium.* Secundum Bedam[^p129-5] « telos Graece, vectigal Latine »; secundum Isidorum locus est, « ubi merces navium vel nautarum emolumenta redduntur »; secundum Ambrosium aequivoce potest accipi ad *vectigal* et ad *domum,* in qua redditur; unde dicit[^p129-6]: « Ille prius dura de laboribus, avara de mercedibus multorum emolumenta extorquens, vocatus verbo, propria reliquit qui rapiebat aliena ». « Nemo ergo, ut dicit Glossa Bedae[^p129-7], de immanitate peccatorum desperet, cum Matthaeus de publicano in Apostolum, de telonario in Evangelistam sit mutatus ».

67. Nec tantum vidit, ut attraheret, sed etiam *allocutus est* eum, ut converteret, cum subditur: *Et ait illi: Sequere me,* scilicet per omnium temporalium *abrenuntiationem,* quia, secundum quod dicit Ioannis octavo[^p129-8], « qui sequitur me non ambulat in tenebris »; et Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio: « Magna gloria est sequi Dominum ». *Sequere me — discretive* dicitur — non *lucra temporalia;* quia, Ecclesiastici trigesimo quarto[^p129-9], « sicut qui apprehendit umbram et sequitur ventos, sic qui attendit ad visa mendacia ». — *Sequere me,* non *desideria carnalia;* Ecclesiastici quinto: « Ne sequaris in fortitudine tua concupiscentiam cordis tui »; et Proverbiorum septimo: « Sequitur eam sicut bos ductus ad victimam » etc. — *Sequere me,* non *flagitia mundana,* sicut illi, de quibus secundi Regum decimo quinto[^p129-10]: « Toto corde universus Israel sequitur Absalom »; Osee duodecimo: « Ephraim pascit ventum et sequitur aestum, tota die mendacia et vastitatem multiplicat ». Et tales sunt vane gloriosi[^p129-11], qui pascunt ventum mundanae vanitatis.

68. (Vers. 28.). Secundo autem ostenditur perfecta ex parte *liberi arbitrii consentientis* obtemperando, cum subditur: *Et relictis omnibus, surgens, secutus est eum;* in quo designatur perfectus consensus liberi arbitrii et quantum ad *recessum a malo* et *profectum in bono* et *consummationem in statu perfecto.* Ideo dicitur, quod *omnia reliquit,* quod *surrexit,* et quod *Christum secutus fuit,* ut posset dicere illud Petri Matthaei decimo nono[^p129-12]: « Ecce, nos reliquimus omnia et secuti sumus te ». Et hoc est, ad quod hortatur nos Apostolus ad Hebraeos duodecimo: « Deponentes omne pondus et circumstans nos peccatum, per patientiam curramus » etc.
<!-- page 130 -->

69. Et nota, quod sub ista distributione includuntur omnia, quae impediunt ad hoc, quod homo Christum perfecte sequatur, quae sunt septem, scilicet criminale *flagitium,* corporale *solatium,* temporale *negotium,* personale[^p130-1] *litigium,* populare *consortium,* paternale *hospitium* et puerile *desiderium.* — Relinquendum est *criminale flagitium;* Proverbiorum vigesimo octavo[^p130-2]: « Qui abscondit scelera sua non dirigetur ». — Secundo, *corporale solatium,* sicut Tobiae secundo: « Reliquit prandium, ieiunus pervenit ad corpus ». — Tertio, *temporale negotium;* sic Ioseph, *pallium* in manu mulieris Aegyptiacae; Genesis trigesimo nono[^p130-3]: « Relicto pallio, fugit »; sic Eliseus, *boves et aratrum,* tertii Regum decimo nono; sic Apostoli, *retia,* sicut dicitur Matthaei quarto: « Relictis retibus, secuti sunt eum ». Per *retia* recte intelliguntur negotia terrena, quia « qui volunt divites fieri incidunt in tentationem » etc., primae ad Timotheum sexto. — Quarto, relinquendum est *personale litigium;* Ecclesiastici vigesimo octavo[^p130-4]: « Relinque proximo tuo nocenti te, et tunc deprecanti tibi peccata solventur ». — Quinto vero, *populare consortium;* Ieremiae quadragesimo octavo: « Relinquite civitates et habitate in petra ». — Sexto, *paternale hospitium;* Ieremiae duodecimo: « Reliqui domum meam et dimisi hereditatem meam » etc. — Septimo, *puerile desiderium;* Proverbiorum nono: « Relinquite infantiam » etc. — Qui haec omnia relinquit, ille est qui bene *surgit;* qui designatur per Paulum conversum, de quo Actuum nono[^p130-5]: « Surrexit autem Saulus de terra, apertisque oculis, nihil videbat »; quia homini ad Christum converso et luce eius illuminato nihil esse videtur terrenum; ad Ephesios quinto: « Surge, qui dormis, et exsurge a mortuis, et illuminabit te Christus ». Et qui sic expeditus surgit potest per viam perfectionis *sequi,* ut possit dicere illud Iob vigesimo tertio[^p130-6]: « Vestigia eius secutus est pes meus ».

70. Et *velociter* hoc fecit; sciebat enim, quod « nocuit differre paratis »; unde nolebat facere contra illud Proverbiorum tertio[^p130-7]: « Ne dicas amico tuo: Vade et revertere, et cras dabo tibi, cum statim possis dare »; sed illud Proverbiorum vigesimo secundo: « Vidisti virum velocem in opere suo? Coram regibus stabit ».

71. Verumtamen de hoc arguit eum Porphyrius haereticus[^p130-8], quia statim sine deliberatione secutus est eum tanquam stultus. — Ad quem respondet Glossa[^p130-9], quod « non levitate ductus, sed virtute operum secutus est eum »; viderat enim Christi miracula. Unde Hieronymus[^p130-10] dicit, quod « fulgor Divinitatis occultae, quae in humana facie Christi relucebat, extemplo ad se videntes trahere poterat aspectu. Sicut vis in magnete lapide esse dicitur, ut anulos et stipulam sibi copulet; quanto magis creaturarum Dominus ad se trahere poterat quos volebat ». Iuxta quod dicit sponsa in Canticis illud: « Trahe me post te, in odorem curremus unguentorum tuorum ».

72. (Vers. 29.). Tertio vero ostenditur vocatio perfecta ex parte *devoti obsequii* subsequentis in ministrando, cum dicitur: *Et fecit ei convivium magnum Levi in domo sua.* Faciebat Matthaeus secundum propositum Sapientis, qui dicebat Sapientiae octavo[^p130-11]: « Proposui sapientiam adducere mihi ad convivendum, sciens, quoniam communicabit mecum de bonis suis ». Iam in eo impletum erat desiderium Christi, secundum illud Apocalypsis tertio: « Ecce, sto ad ostium et pulso; si quis aperuerit mihi, intrabo ad illum et coenabo cum ipso » etc. Iam manifestum est gaudium Patris aeterni, de quo infra decimo quinto[^p130-12]: « Epulari oportebat et gaudere, quia filius hic mortuus fuerat et resurrexit ».

73. Et quoniam magnitudo convivii requirit multitudinem convivantium, ideo subdit: *Et erat turba multa publicanorum et aliorum, qui cum ipsis*[^p130-13] *erant discumbentes.* Invitaverat enim illos Matthaeus, ut, sicut fuerant socii in culpa, sic etiam essent in poenitentia, secundum illud Apocalypsis ultimo: « Qui audit dicat: Veni »; unde Hieronymus: « Videbant publicanum, a peccatis ad meliora conversum, locum invenisse poenitentiae, et ob illud etiam ipsi non desperant salutem ». Attendebant enim, quod « melius est vocari ad olera cum caritate quam ad vitulum saginatum cum odio », Proverbiorum decimo quinto[^p130-14]. Haec autem turba publicanorum ad
<!-- page 131 -->
convivium Christi vocata designat illud implendum, quod dicitur Matthaei octavo: « Multi ab oriente et occidente venient et recumbent cum Abraham in regno caelorum; filii autem regni eiicientur in tenebras exteriores »; unde vigesimo primo: « Publicani et peccatores praecedent vos ». Et in huius figuram dicitur infra decimo[^p131-1], quod « mulier quaedam, Martha nomine, excepit illum in domum suam, et huic erat soror, nomine Maria ».

74. Sed huic videtur obviare illud primae ad Corinthios quinto[^p131-2]: « Si quis frater nominatus inter vos est fornicator aut avarus, cum huiusmodi nec cibum sumere ». — Sed illud dicitur infirmis, ne ex mala communicatione corrumpantur, quia dicitur Ecclesiastici decimo tertio[^p131-3]: « Qui communicaverit superbo induet superbiam ». Vel certe dicitur de publicis obstinatis et convictis, secundum quod dicit Glossa. Hi autem poterant praesentia Christi conteri. Unde Hieronymus[^p131-4]: « Ibat Iesus ad convivia peccatorum, ut occasionem haberet docendi et spirituales invitatoribus suis praeberet cibos. Denique, cum frequenter pergere ad convivia describatur, nihil aliud refertur, quod ibi fecerit, nisi quod docuerit, ut et humilitas Domini eundo ad peccatores, et potentia doctrinae eius in conversione poenitentium demonstretur ».

75. *Et murmurabant* etc. Supra[^p131-5] egit de vocatione peccatorum; in hac parte agit de *confutatione superborum,* quoniam, sicut dicit Apostolus primae ad Corinthios primo, « infirma mundi elegit Deus, ut confundat fortia, et ignobilia et contemptibilia mundi elegit Deus, et ea quae non sunt, ut ea quae sunt, destrueret, ut non glorietur omnis caro in conspectu eius ». Describitur ergo *confutatio Pharisaeorum* superbientium de propriis meritis et ceteros contemnentium hoc ordine. Nam primo superbia eorum *detegitur;* secundo detecta *redarguitur;* tertio redarguta *deiicitur.*

76. (Vers. 30.). *Detegitur* ergo primo ex murmure loquacitatis propriae; et hoc notatur, cum dicit: *Et murmurabant Pharisaei et scribae eorum,* contra consilium Sapientis, Sapientiae primo[^p131-6]: « Custodite vos a murmuratione, quae nihil prodest, et a detractione parcite linguae ». Quod isti non faciebant; unde et subditur: *Dicentes*[^p131-7]*: Quare cum publicanis et peccatoribus manducatis et bibitis?* Unde non poterant mordere in peccato proprio, mordebant tamen ex alieno; hoc enim est hominis iniqui; Ecclesiastici undecimo: « Bona in mala convertens, insidiatur et in electis imponit maculam ». In hoc manifestatur Pharisaeorum superbia, quia se non reputabant peccatores. Unde Glossa[^p131-8]: « Dupliciter peccant, et quia putant, se esse iustos, cum sint superbi, et illos reos, cum iam poeniteant ». Unde erant similes illi Pharisaeo, de quo infra decimo octavo: « Non sum sicut ceteri hominum, raptores, iniusti, adulteri, velut etiam hic publicanus ». Et ideo infra decimo sexto dicebat eis Dominus: « Vos estis qui iustificatis vos coram hominibus; Deus autem novit corda vestra ». De talibus Isaias sexagesimo quinto[^p131-9]: « Qui dicunt: Recede a me, non appropinques mihi, quia immundus es ». Propter talium verba et oblocutiones non debet homo devitare opera misericordiae; unde primi Machabaeorum secundo: « A verbis viri peccatoris ne timueritis, quia gloria eius stercus et vermis est ». Unde Hieronymus[^p131-10]: « Non potest fieri, ut absque morsu hominum huius vitae curricula quis pertranseat. Malorum solatium est bonorum vitam carpere, et de peccantium multitudine putant suorum culpam minui peccatorum ».

77. (Vers. 31.). *Arguitur* vero secundo ex lumine veritatis perspicuae, cum additur: *Respondens*[^p131-11] *Iesus dixit ad illos: Non egent qui sani sunt medico, sed qui male habent.* Haec est per se nota, quia, secundum quod dicitur Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo, « honora medicum propter necessitatem; etenim creavit illum Altissimus ». Si medicina propter aegritudinem, igitur non est redarguendus aegrotus, qui confugit ad medicum, nec medicus, qui accedit ad aegrotum; sed magis illi redarguendi sunt, qui haec redarguunt. Vocat autem Christus peccatores *infirmos,* quoniam peccatum infirmum facit, secundum illud Psalmi[^p131-12]: « Quoniam lumbi mei impleti sunt illusionibus, et non est sanitas in carne mea ». De tali infirmo dicitur ad Romanos decimo quarto: « Infirmum autem in fide assumite » etc.; et decimo quinto: « Debemus nos firmiores imbecillitates infirmorum sustinere ». Se autem vocat *medicum,* quia,
<!-- page 132 -->
Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p132-1], « livore eius sanati sumus »; et sexagesimo primo: « Spiritus Domini super me, eo quod unxerit me, ut mederer contritos corde »; et Actuum decimo: « Pertransiit benefaciendo et sanando omnes oppressos a diabolo ».

78. (Vers. 32.). *Deiicitur* autem tertio ex rectitudine aequitatis supernae, qua magis condescendit humili peccatori quam superbo iusto; et ideo subdit: *Non veni vocare iustos, sed peccatores ad poenitentiam; iustos,* id est, qui se reputant; Glossa[^p132-2]: « Qui iustitiae suae confidunt ». Unde Beda: « *Iustos* eos vocat, qui *Dei ignorantes iustitiam et suam volentes statuere, iustitiae Dei non sunt subiecti,* ad Romanos decimo, qui ex Lege praesumentes, Evangelii gratiam non quaerunt ». Tales enim non venit Dominus vocare, sed magis iusto iudicio excaecare; unde Ioannis nono[^p132-3]: « In iudicium ego veni in mundum, ut qui non vident videant, et qui vident caeci fiant ». Venit autem Dominus vocare omnes ad poenitentiam, quia, sicut dicitur ad Romanos tertio et acceptum est de Psalmo, « non est iustus quisquam, omnes declinaverunt, simul inutiles facti sunt »; unde et ibidem: « Omnes peccaverunt et egent gloria Dei ». Et ideo a poenitentia incepit praedicare, secundum quod dicitur Matthaei quarto[^p132-4]. De hac vocatione dicitur Isaiae vigesimo secundo: « Vocabit Dominus Deus exercituum in die illa ad fletum et planctum et calvitium et ad cingulum sacci ». Sed hanc vocem non audiunt qui se reputant iustos; contra quos Iob nono[^p132-5]: « Si iustificare me voluero, os meum condemnabit me; si innocentem ostendero, pravum me comprobabit ». Sed hanc audiunt humiles peccatores; infra septimo: « Et omnis populus audiens et publicani iustificaverunt Dominum, baptizati baptismo Ioannis. Pharisaei autem et legisperiti spreverunt consilium Dei in semetipsos, non baptizati ab eo ». — Et sic patet, quomodo in hoc facta est confutatio superborum; unde divino iudicio peiores reputantur illis quos despiciunt.

79. (Vers. 33.). *At illi dixerunt.* Postquam descripta est vocatio peccatorum et confutatio superborum, subiungitur hic tertio *consolatio infirmorum* in hoc, quod non imponuntur ipsis discipulis onera gravia et difficultates ieiuniorum. Introducitur ergo *consolatio* manuductoria discipulorum ut *dissonans severitati legalis observantiae,* ut *consonans benignitati divinae praesentiae,* ut *expediens ruditati imperfectionis humanae*[^p132-6].

Primo ergo introducitur ut *dissonans severitati legalis observantiae* in quaestione Pharisaeorum, cum dicitur: *At illi dixerunt ad eum: Quare discipuli Ioannis ieiunant frequenter et obsecrationes faciunt? Similiter autem et*[^p132-7] *Pharisaeorum* etc. Dicitur autem et Matthaei nono, quod hoc discipuli Ioannis quaesierunt; sed, ut dicit Augustinus in libro de Concordia Evangelistarum, non est contrarietas, quia utrique quaesierunt. Et utrique quaerebant, sed discipuli Ioannis volentes discere veritatem, Pharisaei vero volebant eorum explorare libertatem, sicut illi, de quibus dicitur ad Galatas secundo[^p132-8]: « Propter subintroductos falsos fratres, qui subintroierunt explorare libertatem nostram ». Hoc autem non quaerebant, quia discipulos Ioannis approbarent, sed ut comparatione eorum Christi discipulos reprehenderent.

80. Unde et subdunt: *Tui autem edunt et bibunt;* et ideo conquerebatur Dominus de eis Matthaei undecimo[^p132-9]: « Venit Ioannes neque manducans neque bibens, et dicunt: Daemonium habet; venit Filius hominis manducans et bibens, et dicunt: Ecce, homo vorax et potator vini, publicanorum et peccatorum amicus ». Et ideo spernebant Pharisaei eos tanquam discordes a legali et populari observatione; sed male, quia Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo[^p132-10]: « Nolite ieiunare sicut usque ad hanc diem, ut audiatur in excelso clamor vester. Nunquid tale est ieiunium, quod elegi? Hoc enim est ieiunium, quod elegi? Dissolve colligationes impietatis » etc.; et ad Romanos decimo quarto: « Is qui non manducat, manducantem non spernat », quia, sicut ibidem subditur, « regnum Dei non est esca et potus ».

81. (Vers. 34.). Secundo vero introducitur ut *conveniens benignitati divinae praesentiae* in dissolutione obiectorum, cum dicitur: *Quibus ipse ait:*
<!-- page 133 -->
*Nunquid possunt filii sponsi ieiunare* etc. Secundum Bedam[^p133-1] vero haec est littera: *Nunquid potestis filios sponsi facere ieiunare? Sponsus* iste Christus est, de quo Ioannis tertio: « Qui habet sponsam sponsus est »; et in Psalmo: « Et ipse tanquam sponsus procedens de thalamo suo ». *Filii sponsi* sunt Apostoli; unde Isaiae octavo: « Ecce, ego et pueri mei, quos dedit mihi Dominus »; et in Psalmo: « Pro patribus tuis nati sunt tibi filii; constitues eos principes » etc. Isti non possunt nec debent ieiunare ieiunio moeroris et afflictionis, quamdiu cum illis est sponsus, quia tunc est tempus gaudii; propter quod Canticorum tertio[^p133-2] dicitur: « Filiae Ierusalem, egredimini et videte regem Salomonem in diademate »; et Isaiae sexagesimo primo: « Gaudens gaudebo in Domino, et exsultabit anima mea in Deo meo, quia induit me Dominus vestimento salutis et indumento iustitiae circumdedit me, quasi sponsum decoratum corona et quasi sponsam ornatam monilibus ». Unde tunc erat tempus *gaudii,* non moeroris, quia, sicut dicitur Ioannis tertio[^p133-3], « amicus sponsi stat et gaudio gaudet propter vocem sponsi ».

82. (Vers. 35.). Et ideo discipulorum *consolatio* conveniens est praesentiae sponsi Christi, sicut et *desolatio* absentiae. Propter quod addit: *Venient autem dies, cum ablatus fuerit ab eis*[^p133-4], scilicet per mortem et passionem; Osee quinto: « Vadent ad quaerendum Dominum et non invenient; ablatus est enim ab eis », et *tunc ieiunabunt in illis diebus,* scilicet ad tempus illud; quia Ioannis decimo sexto[^p133-5]: « Plorabitis et flebitis vos, mundus autem gaudebit, vos vero contristabimini, sed tristitia vestra convertetur in gaudium »; et tunc erit tempus moeroris et ieiunii, unde Tobiae secundo: « Comedit panem suum in luctu et tremore, memorans illud propheticum: Dies festi vestri convertentur in luctum »; Threnorum primo: « Idcirco ego plorans, et oculus meus deducens aquas, quia longe factus est a me consolator meus, convertens animam meam ».

83. (Vers. 36.). Tertio autem introducitur ut *expediens ruditati imperfectionis humanae,* cum dicitur: *Dicebat autem illis similitudinem*[^p133-6]; ubi ostendit, discipulos ad ieiunium non esse arctandos tum propter *vetustatem conversationis,* tum propter *fragilitatem carnis,* tum etiam propter *dissuetudinem affectionis,* quae tria exprimit sub triplici metaphora, scilicet veteris *vestis,* veteris *utris* et veteris *vini.*

Rudes igitur discipulos expediens erat confovere, non ieiuniis aggravare, propter *vetustatem conversationis,* propter quam dicit: *Quia nemo commissuram,* id est particulam, quae coniungit, *a vestimento novo,* scilicet ablatam, *immittit in vestimentum vetus,* quia duplex sequitur inconveniens; et ideo addit: *Alioquin et novum rumpit,* quod est *damnosum, et veteri non convenit commissura*[^p133-7], quod est *indecorum.* — Sic *spiritualiter* intelligendum, quod non sunt imponendae novae observantiae convenientes novae conversationi, quamdiu homo non est priori exutus, scilicet in principio conversionis. Et ideo dicebat Apostolus ad Ephesios quarto[^p133-8]: « Deponentes secundum pristinam conversationem veterem hominem, renovamini spiritu mentis vestrae et induite novum hominem, qui secundum Deum creatus est »; et ad Colossenses tertio: « Exspoliantes vos veterem hominem cum actibus suis, induite novum »; alioquin erit monstruosum. — Et ideo in figura huius prohibita est in Lege commixtio diversorum; Deuteronomii vigesimo secundo[^p133-9]: « Non indueris vestimento, quod ex lana linoque contextum est », quia hinc contemnitur Dominus; Ieremiae duodecimo: « Nunquid avis discolor hereditas mea mihi »?

84. (Vers. 37. 38.). Hoc ipsum erat expediens propter *fragilitatem carnis,* propter quam dicit: *Et nemo mittit vinum novum in utres veteres;* quia ex hoc sequitur duplex inconvenientia, scilicet effusio vini, propter quam dicit: *Alioquin rumpet vinum novum utres, et ipsum effundetur;* alia, perditio utris; unde et addit: *Et utres peribunt*[^p133-10]. Et ideo novo vino novum etiam vas providendum; propter quod dicit: *Sed vinum novum in utres novos mittendum est et*
<!-- page 134 -->
*utraque servantur.* — Sic spirituales observantiae sunt spiritualibus, non carnalibus imponendae, qui designantur per veteres utres, secundum illud Psalmi[^p134-1]: « Factus sum sicut uter in pruina »; unde designantur per utres Gabaonitarum ruptos et solutos; Iosue nono: « Utres vini novos implevimus, qui nunc rupti sunt atque soluti sunt », et ita ad continendum vinum inepti. Et ideo primae ad Corinthios tertio: « Et ego, fratres, non potui vobis loqui quasi spiritualibus, sed quasi carnalibus, tanquam parvulis in Christo. Lac vobis potum dedi, non escam ». Antequam ergo eis spiritualia iniungantur, necesse est, ut corda eorum renoventur, iuxta illud Ezechielis trigesimo sexto[^p134-2]: « Ecce, dabo vobis cor novum et spiritum novum ponam in medio vestri »; quia ad Galatas sexto Apostolus ait: « In Christo Iesu neque circumcisio aliquid valet neque praeputium, sed nova creatura ». Et ideo in Psalmo[^p134-3] petitur: « Cor mundum crea in me, Deus, et spiritum rectum innova in visceribus meis »; quod fit in viris spiritualibus; secundae ad Corinthios quarto: « Licet is qui foris est, noster homo corrumpatur, tamen is qui intus est, renovatur de die in diem ».

85. (Vers. 39.). Hoc etiam ipsum nihilominus[^p134-4] expediens erat propter *dissuetudinem affectionis;* propter quam designandam dicit: *Et nemo bibens vetus statim vult novum.* Et rationem huius reddit propter iudicium sensus, cum dicit: *Dicit enim: Vetus melius est.* — In quo per simile in delectatione potus vult ostendere, quod affectus noster non statim delectatur in novis servandis, nisi paulative manuducatur. Et ideo dicebant quidam Actuum decimo quinto[^p134-5]: « Nisi circumcidamini secundum morem Moysi, non potestis salvari ». Hanc autem affectionem innovat caritas, secundum illud Ioannis decimo tertio: « Mandatum novum do vobis, ut diligatis invicem ». Hoc facit nova sapere ut vetera; unde primae Ioannis secundo[^p134-6]: « Non mandatum novum scribo vobis, sed mandatum vetus, quod audistis ab initio ». « Iterum mandatum novum scribo vobis, quod est verum et in ipso et in vobis ». Vere hoc est *novum mandatum,* quod vetera evacuat et quod facit sapere nova; secundae ad Corinthios quinto[^p134-7]: « Si qua in Christo nova creatura, vetera transierunt; ecce, facta sunt omnia nova »; quia, sicut dicitur Levitici vigesimo sexto, « novis supervenientibus, vetera proiicietis ». — Sic ergo propter ruditatem discipulorum tum ex parte *conversationis,* tum ex parte *carnis,* tum ex parte *mentis,* erant discipuli benigne tractandi, nondum ad ieiunium arctandi, maxime ad ieiunium exsultationis, quod non convenit nisi renovatis per gratiam Spiritus sancti.

Unde ex praemissis colligitur, quod discipuli Christi non erant arctandi, nec ad ieiunium *moeroris* propter praesentiam sponsi, nec *exsultationis* propter absentiam Spiritus sancti; et ita erant benigne consolandi tanquam adhuc tenelli et infirmi. Ex praemissis igitur sufficienter apparet, quomodo a Domino sunt *peccatores* misericorditer *vocati,* et *superbi* rationabiliter *confutati,* et *infirmi* suaviter *consolati.*

#### Second, how the disciples are called from the state of fault.

63. *And after these things he went forth* etc. Above[^p128-10] the Evangelist treated of the calling of the disciples from the licit state or the state of justice; here he treats of their calling from the *perilous state* or the state of injustice, where he shows how he called Matthew sitting at the receipt of custom. Now this part has two, in the first of which he treats of the *perfection of the calling;* but in the second, of the *defence of those called,* there: *And it came to pass on the sabbath* etc., in the next chapter[^p128-11].

Concerning the *perfection of the calling* three things are introduced: the first is the *calling of sinners;* the second is the *confutation of the proud,* there[^p128-12]: *And the Pharisees murmured* etc.; the third is the *consolation of the infirm,* there: *And they said to him* etc.

Now the *calling* of the *publican* is shown to be perfect from three things, namely on the part of the *divine gift going before,* and on the part of the *free will consenting,* and on the part of the *devout service following.*

64. (Vers. 27.). First, then, it is shown to be perfect on the part of the *divine gift going before,* when it is said: *And after these things he went forth and saw a publican* etc. And note
<!-- page 129 -->
that *to go forth, to see* and *to call* are said. He *goes forth,* indeed, to seek; he *sees,* to draw; he *addresses* and *calls,* to turn to himself. He *went forth,* indeed, through solicitude in seeking out his work, as is said in the Psalm[^p129-1]: « Man shall go forth to his work, and to his labour until the evening ». Whence he is signified by the householder, of whom it is said in Matthew, the twentieth chapter, that « he went out early in the morning to hire labourers into his vineyard ». This is truly the Lord Jesus, who says of himself in John, the sixteenth chapter: « I came forth from the Father, and am come into the world », because from the secret bosom he came into the manifest. — Nor did he only go forth, but *he also saw a publican,* so as to draw him to himself through the regard of mercy, as that man asked *to be seen* in the Psalm[^p129-2]: « See my abjection and my labour » etc. And in this manner he *saw* this man, because, although he was a sinner in deed, yet he was elect in the divine purpose; Wisdom, the fourth chapter: « His regard is upon his elect »; whence it is said below, in the twenty-second chapter: « The Lord turning looked on Peter, and Peter going out wept bitterly ».

65. With this regard Jesus *saw a publican named Levi,* that is, Matthew, who, as Jerome says[^p129-3], had two names; but Luke and Mark, on account of the honour of the Evangelist, are silent about his common name, whereas Matthew, made his own accuser at the beginning of his discourse, names himself Matthew and a publican. — And note that those were called *publicans* who sinned publicly, as it is taken below, in the eighteenth chapter[^p129-4]: « Two men went up into the temple: the one a Pharisee, and the other a publican »; or those who made public their wares to all by selling, as merchants are wont to do, as it is taken above, in the third chapter; or those who exacted the public tolls; and so it seems to be properly taken here.

66. Whence it is subjoined: *Sitting at the receipt of custom.* According to Bede[^p129-5], « *telos* in Greek, *vectigal* in Latin »; according to Isidore it is the place « where the wares of ships or the earnings of sailors are rendered »; according to Ambrose it can be taken equivocally for the *toll* and for the *house* in which it is rendered; whence he says[^p129-6]: « He who before, harsh about labours, greedy about wages, extorted the earnings of many, being called by a word, left his own, he who used to seize what was another's ». « Let no one, therefore, as the Gloss of Bede says[^p129-7], despair on account of the enormity of his sins, since Matthew was changed from a publican into an Apostle, from a toll-gatherer into an Evangelist ».

67. Nor did he only see, so as to draw, but he also *addressed* him, so as to convert him, when it is subjoined: *And he said to him: Follow me,* namely through the *renunciation* of all temporal things, because, according to what he says in John, the eighth chapter[^p129-8], « he that followeth me walketh not in darkness »; and Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third chapter: « It is great glory to follow the Lord ». *Follow me —* it is said *discretively —* not *temporal gains;* because, Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p129-9], « as he that catcheth at a shadow and followeth after the wind, so is he also that giveth heed to lying visions ». — *Follow me,* not *carnal desires;* Ecclesiasticus, the fifth chapter: « Follow not in thy strength the concupiscence of thy heart »; and Proverbs, the seventh chapter: « He followeth her as an ox led to be a victim » etc. — *Follow me,* not *worldly outrages,* like those of whom the second of Kings, the fifteenth chapter[^p129-10]: « All Israel with their whole heart followeth Absalom »; Osee, the twelfth chapter: « Ephraim feedeth on the wind, and followeth the burning heat; all the day long he multiplieth lies and desolation ». And such are the vainglorious[^p129-11], who feed on the wind of worldly vanity.

68. (Vers. 28.). But second, it is shown to be perfect on the part of the *free will consenting* by obeying, when it is subjoined: *And leaving all things, rising up, he followed him;* in which is designated the perfect consent of free will, both as regards the *withdrawal from evil* and the *progress in good* and the *consummation in the perfect state.* Therefore it is said that he *left all things,* that he *rose up,* and that he *followed Christ,* so that he could say that word of Peter in Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p129-12]: « Behold, we have left all things and have followed thee ». And this is that to which the Apostle exhorts us to the Hebrews, the twelfth chapter: « Laying aside every weight and sin which surrounds us, let us run by patience » etc.
<!-- page 130 -->

69. And note that under this distribution are included all things that hinder a man from following Christ perfectly, which are seven, namely the criminal *outrage,* bodily *solace,* temporal *business,* personal[^p130-1] *strife,* the people's *company,* the paternal *dwelling* and childish *desire.* — The *criminal outrage* is to be left; Proverbs, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p130-2]: « He that hideth his crimes shall not prosper ». — Second, *bodily solace,* as in Tobias, the second chapter: « He left his dinner, and came fasting to the body ». — Third, *temporal business;* so Joseph left the *garment* in the hand of the Egyptian woman; Genesis, the thirty-ninth chapter[^p130-3]: « Leaving the garment, he fled »; so Eliseus the *oxen and the plough,* the third of Kings, the nineteenth chapter; so the Apostles the *nets,* as is said in Matthew, the fourth chapter: « Leaving their nets, they followed him ». By the *nets* are rightly understood earthly businesses, because « they that will become rich fall into temptation » etc., the first to Timothy, the sixth chapter. — Fourth, *personal strife* is to be left; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p130-4]: « Leave to thy neighbour that hurteth thee, and then shall thy sins be loosed to thee when thou prayest ». — But fifth, *the people's company;* Jeremias, the forty-eighth chapter: « Leave the cities, and dwell in the rock ». — Sixth, the *paternal dwelling;* Jeremias, the twelfth chapter: « I have left my house, I have forsaken my inheritance » etc. — Seventh, *childish desire;* Proverbs, the ninth chapter: « Leave childishness » etc. — He who leaves all these is he who *rises* well; who is designated by Paul converted, of whom in Acts, the ninth chapter[^p130-5]: « And Saul arose from the ground, and when his eyes were opened, he saw nothing »; because to a man converted to Christ and enlightened by his light nothing earthly seems to be; to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter: « Arise, thou that sleepest, and rise up from the dead, and Christ shall enlighten thee ». And he who rises thus unencumbered can *follow* by the way of perfection, so that he can say that word of Job, the twenty-third chapter[^p130-6]: « My foot hath followed his steps ».

70. And he did this *speedily;* for he knew that « to delay has harmed those who are ready »; whence he did not wish to act against that word of Proverbs, the third chapter[^p130-7]: « Say not to thy friend: Go, and come again, and tomorrow I will give to thee, when thou canst give at present »; but according to that of Proverbs, the twenty-second chapter: « Hast thou seen a man swift in his work? He shall stand before kings ».

71. Nevertheless Porphyry the heretic[^p130-8] reproves him for this, because he followed him at once, without deliberation, like a fool. — To whom the Gloss responds[^p130-9] that « not led by levity, but by the power of the works, did he follow him »; for he had seen the miracles of Christ. Whence Jerome says[^p130-10] that « the splendour of the hidden Divinity, which shone forth in the human face of Christ, could draw those who saw it to himself at once by the sight. As a force is said to be in the magnet stone, so that it joins rings and straw to itself; how much more could the Lord of creatures draw to himself whom he would ». According to which the bride says that word in the Canticles: « Draw me after thee; we will run to the odour of thy ointments ».

72. (Vers. 29.). But third, the calling is shown to be perfect on the part of the *devout service* following in ministering, when it is said: *And Levi made him a great feast in his own house.* Matthew did according to the purpose of the Wise man, who said in Wisdom, the eighth chapter[^p130-11]: « I purposed to bring wisdom to me to live with me, knowing that she will communicate to me of her good things ». Now in him was fulfilled the desire of Christ, according to that word of the Apocalypse, the third chapter: « Behold, I stand at the gate and knock; if any man shall open to me, I will come in to him, and will sup with him » etc. Now manifest is the joy of the eternal Father, of which below, in the fifteenth chapter[^p130-12]: « It was fit that we should make merry and be glad, for this son was dead, and is risen again ».

73. And since the greatness of a feast requires a multitude of guests, therefore he subjoins: *And there was a great multitude of publicans and of others, who were at table with them*[^p130-13]. For Matthew had invited them, that, as they had been companions in fault, so also they might be in penance, according to that word of the Apocalypse, the last chapter: « He that heareth, let him say: Come »; whence Jerome: « They saw the publican, converted from sins to better things, to have found a place of penance, and on that account they too do not despair of salvation ». For they considered that « it is better to be invited to herbs with charity than to a fatted calf with hatred », Proverbs, the fifteenth chapter[^p130-14]. Now this multitude of publicans called to
<!-- page 131 -->
the feast of Christ designates that which is to be fulfilled, which is said in Matthew, the eighth chapter: « Many shall come from the east and the west, and shall sit down with Abraham in the kingdom of heaven; but the children of the kingdom shall be cast out into the exterior darkness »; whence in the twenty-first chapter: « Publicans and sinners shall go before you ». And in figure of this it is said below, in the tenth chapter[^p131-1], that « a certain woman, named Martha, received him into her house, and she had a sister, named Mary ».

74. But that word of the first to the Corinthians, the fifth chapter[^p131-2], seems to stand against this: « If any man that is named a brother among you be a fornicator or covetous, with such a one not so much as to eat ». — But that is said to the infirm, lest they be corrupted by evil communication, because it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the thirteenth chapter[^p131-3]: « He that communicateth with the proud shall put on pride ». Or certainly it is said of those who are public, obstinate and convicted, according to what the Gloss says. But these could be made contrite by the presence of Christ. Whence Jerome[^p131-4]: « Jesus went to the feasts of sinners, that he might have occasion of teaching and might offer spiritual food to those who invited him. In short, though he is described as going often to feasts, nothing else is reported that he did there except that he taught, so that both the humility of the Lord in going to sinners and the power of his doctrine in the conversion of the penitent might be shown ».

75. *And they murmured* etc. Above[^p131-5] he treated of the calling of sinners; in this part he treats of the *confutation of the proud,* since, as the Apostle says in the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter, « the weak things of the world hath God chosen, that he may confound the strong, and the base and contemptible things of the world hath God chosen, and the things that are not, that he might bring to nought the things that are, that no flesh should glory in his sight ». So the *confutation of the Pharisees,* who prided themselves on their own merits and despised the rest, is described in this order. For first their pride is *uncovered;* second, uncovered, it is *convicted;* third, convicted, it is *cast down.*

76. (Vers. 30.). It is *uncovered,* then, first from the murmur of their own talkativeness; and this is noted when he says: *And the Pharisees and their scribes murmured,* against the counsel of the Wise man, Wisdom, the first chapter[^p131-6]: « Keep yourselves from murmuring, which profiteth nothing, and refrain your tongue from detraction ». Which these did not do; whence it is also subjoined: *Saying*[^p131-7]*: Why do you eat and drink with publicans and sinners?* Whence they could not bite in their own sin, yet they bit from another's; for this belongs to the wicked man; Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter: « Turning good into evil, he lieth in wait, and on the elect he will lay a blot ». In this the pride of the Pharisees is made manifest, because they did not reckon themselves sinners. Whence the Gloss[^p131-8]: « They sin in two ways, both because they think themselves just, when they are proud, and those guilty, when they are now doing penance ». Whence they were like that Pharisee of whom below, in the eighteenth chapter: « I am not as the rest of men, extortioners, unjust, adulterers, as also is this publican ». And therefore below, in the sixteenth chapter, the Lord said to them: « You are they who justify yourselves before men; but God knoweth your hearts ». Of such Isaias, the sixty-fifth chapter[^p131-9]: « Who say: Depart from me, come not near me, because thou art unclean ». On account of the words and the gainsayings of such a man ought not to shun the works of mercy; whence the first of Machabees, the second chapter: « Fear ye not the words of a sinful man, for his glory is dung and worms ». Whence Jerome[^p131-10]: « It cannot be that anyone should pass through the course of this life without the bite of men. It is the solace of the wicked to carp at the life of the good, and from the multitude of those who sin they think the guilt of their own sins is lessened ».

77. (Vers. 31.). But second, it is *reproved* from the lumen[^tr-lumen] of perspicuous truth, when it is added: *Jesus answering*[^p131-11] *said to them: They that are whole need not the physician, but they that are sick.* This is known through itself, because, according to what is said in Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter, « honour the physician for necessity; for the most High hath created him ». If medicine is on account of sickness, then the sick man is not to be convicted who flees to the physician, nor the physician who approaches the sick man; but rather those are to be convicted who convict these things. Now Christ calls sinners *infirm,* since sin makes one infirm, according to that word of the Psalm[^p131-12]: « For my loins are filled with illusions, and there is no health in my flesh ». Of such an infirm man it is said to the Romans, the fourteenth chapter: « Now him that is infirm in faith, take unto you » etc.; and in the fifteenth chapter: « We that are stronger ought to bear the infirmities of the infirm ». But he calls himself a *physician,* because,
<!-- page 132 -->
Isaias, the fifty-third chapter[^p132-1], « by his bruises we are healed »; and the sixty-first chapter: « The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me, that I might heal the contrite of heart »; and Acts, the tenth chapter: « He went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed by the devil ».

78. (Vers. 32.). But third, it is *cast down* from the rectitude of the supernal equity, by which he condescends more to the humble sinner than to the proud just man; and therefore he subjoins: *I came not to call the just, but sinners to penance; the just,* that is, those who reckon themselves so; the Gloss[^p132-2]: « Who trust in their own justice ». Whence Bede: « He calls those *just* who, *not knowing the justice of God and seeking to establish their own, have not submitted themselves to the justice of God,* to the Romans, the tenth chapter, who, presuming on the Law, do not seek the grace of the Gospel ». For such the Lord did not come to call, but rather to blind by just judgement; whence John, the ninth chapter[^p132-3]: « For judgement I am come into this world, that they who see not may see, and they who see may become blind ». But the Lord came to call all to penance, because, as is said to the Romans, the third chapter, and is taken from the Psalm, « there is not any just man; all have turned aside, they are become unprofitable together »; whence also in the same place: « All have sinned, and do need the glory of God ». And therefore he began to preach from penance, according to what is said in Matthew, the fourth chapter[^p132-4]. Of this calling it is said in Isaias, the twenty-second chapter: « The Lord God of hosts shall call in that day to weeping, and to mourning, and to baldness, and to girding with sackcloth ». But those who reckon themselves just do not hear this voice; against whom Job, the ninth chapter[^p132-5]: « If I would justify myself, my own mouth shall condemn me; if I would shew myself innocent, it shall prove me wicked ». But the humble sinners hear it; below, in the seventh chapter: « And all the people hearing, and the publicans, justified the Lord, being baptized with John's baptism. But the Pharisees and the lawyers despised the counsel of God against themselves, being not baptized by him ». — And so it is plain how in this the confutation of the proud was made; whence by divine judgement they are reckoned worse than those whom they despise.

79. (Vers. 33.). *And they said.* After the calling of sinners and the confutation of the proud have been described, here is subjoined third the *consolation of the infirm,* in this, that heavy burdens and the difficulties of fasts are not laid upon the disciples themselves. The manuductory *consolation* of the disciples is therefore introduced as *discordant with the severity of the legal observance,* as *concordant with the kindness of the divine presence,* as *expedient for the rawness of human imperfection*[^p132-6].

First, then, it is introduced as *discordant with the severity of the legal observance,* in the question of the Pharisees, when it is said: *And they said to him: Why do the disciples of John fast often and make prayers? And in like manner*[^p132-7] *those of the Pharisees* etc. But it is said also in Matthew, the ninth chapter, that the disciples of John asked this; but, as Augustine says in the book on the Concord of the Evangelists, there is no contrariety, because both asked it. And both asked, but the disciples of John wishing to learn the truth, while the Pharisees wished to spy out their liberty, like those of whom it is said to the Galatians, the second chapter[^p132-8]: « Because of false brethren unawares brought in, who came in privately to spy our liberty ». Now they did not ask this because they approved the disciples of John, but that by comparison with them they might reprehend the disciples of Christ.

80. Whence they also subjoin: *But thine eat and drink;* and therefore the Lord complained of them in Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p132-9]: « John came neither eating nor drinking, and they say: He hath a devil; the Son of man came eating and drinking, and they say: Behold a man that is a glutton and a wine drinker, a friend of publicans and sinners ». And therefore the Pharisees despised them as discordant from the legal and popular observance; but wrongly, because Isaias, the fifty-eighth chapter[^p132-10]: « Do not fast as until this day, to make your cry to be heard on high. Is this such a fast as I have chosen? For is this the fast that I have chosen? Loose the bands of wickedness » etc.; and to the Romans, the fourteenth chapter: « He that eateth not, let him not despise him that eateth », because, as is subjoined in the same place, « the kingdom of God is not meat and drink ».

81. (Vers. 34.). But second it is introduced as *fitting the kindness of the divine presence,* in the dissolving of the objections, when it is said: *To whom he said:*
<!-- page 133 -->
*Can the children of the bridegroom fast* etc. But according to Bede[^p133-1] this is the letter: *Can you make the children of the bridegroom fast? The bridegroom* is Christ, of whom John, the third chapter: « He that hath the bride is the bridegroom »; and in the Psalm: « And he, as a bridegroom coming out of his bride chamber ». *The children of the bridegroom* are the Apostles; whence Isaias, the eighth chapter: « Behold, I and my children, whom the Lord hath given me »; and in the Psalm: « Instead of thy fathers, sons are born to thee; thou shalt make them princes » etc. These cannot and ought not to fast with the fast of mourning and affliction, as long as the bridegroom is with them, because then is the time of joy; on account of which it is said in the Canticle, the third chapter[^p133-2]: « Go forth, ye daughters of Jerusalem, and see king Solomon in the diadem »; and Isaias, the sixty-first chapter: « I will greatly rejoice in the Lord, and my soul shall exult in my God, for the Lord hath clothed me with the garment of salvation, and with the robe of justice he hath covered me, as a bridegroom decked with a crown, and as a bride adorned with her jewels ». Whence then was the time of *joy,* not of mourning, because, as is said in John, the third chapter[^p133-3], « the friend of the bridegroom standeth and rejoiceth with joy because of the bridegroom's voice ».

82. (Vers. 35.). And therefore the *consolation* of the disciples is fitting to the presence of Christ the bridegroom, as also *desolation* to his absence. On account of which he adds: *But the days will come, when he shall be taken away from them*[^p133-4], namely through death and passion; Osee, the fifth chapter: « They shall go to seek the Lord, and shall not find him; for he is taken away from them », and *then shall they fast in those days,* namely at that time; because John, the sixteenth chapter[^p133-5]: « You shall lament and weep, but the world shall rejoice, and you shall be made sorrowful, but your sorrow shall be turned into joy »; and then will be the time of mourning and of fasting, whence Tobias, the second chapter: « He ate his bread with mourning and trembling, remembering that prophetic word: Your festival days shall be turned into mourning »; Lamentations, the first chapter: « Therefore do I weep, and my eye runneth down with water, because my comforter, the relief of my soul, is far from me ».

83. (Vers. 36.). But third it is introduced as *expedient for the rawness of human imperfection,* when it is said: *And he spoke to them a similitude*[^p133-6]; where he shows that the disciples were not to be constrained to fasting, both on account of the *oldness of their conversation,* and on account of the *frailty of the flesh,* and also on account of the *disuse of the affection,* which three he expresses under a threefold metaphor, namely of the old *garment,* the old *bottle* and the old *wine.*

It was expedient, then, to cherish the raw disciples, not to weigh them down with fasts, on account of the *oldness of their conversation,* on account of which he says: *For no man putteth a piece,* that is, a small part that joins, *from a new garment,* namely taken, *upon an old garment,* because a twofold unfittingness follows; and therefore he adds: *Otherwise he both rendeth the new,* which is *damaging, and the piece agreeth not with the old*[^p133-7], which is *unseemly.* — So it is to be understood *spiritually,* that new observances befitting the new conversation are not to be imposed as long as a man has not put off the former, namely in the beginning of conversion. And therefore the Apostle said to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter[^p133-8]: « Putting off, according to the former conversation, the old man, be renewed in the spirit of your mind, and put on the new man, who according to God is created »; and to the Colossians, the third chapter: « Stripping yourselves of the old man with his deeds, put on the new »; otherwise it will be monstrous. — And therefore in figure of this the mixing of diverse things was forbidden in the Law; Deuteronomy, the twenty-second chapter[^p133-9]: « Thou shalt not wear a garment that is woven of wool and linen », because hence the Lord is despised; Jeremias, the twelfth chapter: « Is my inheritance to me as a speckled bird »?

84. (Vers. 37. 38.). This same thing was expedient on account of the *frailty of the flesh,* on account of which he says: *And no man putteth new wine into old bottles;* because from this follows a twofold unfittingness, namely the spilling of the wine, on account of which he says: *Otherwise the new wine will break the bottles, and it will be spilled;* the other, the loss of the bottle; whence he also adds: *And the bottles will be lost*[^p133-10]. And therefore for new wine a new vessel also is to be provided; on account of which he says: *But new wine must be put into new bottles, and*
<!-- page 134 -->
*both are preserved.* — So spiritual observances are to be imposed on the spiritual, not on the carnal, who are designated by the old bottles, according to that word of the Psalm[^p134-1]: « I am become like a bottle in the frost »; whence they are designated by the bottles of the Gabaonites, rent and burst; Josue, the ninth chapter: « We filled new bottles of wine, which now are rent and burst », and so unfit for holding wine. And therefore the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter: « And I, brethren, could not speak to you as unto spiritual, but as unto carnal, as unto little ones in Christ. I gave you milk to drink, not meat ». Before, then, spiritual things are enjoined on them, it is necessary that their hearts be renewed, according to that word of Ezechiel, the thirty-sixth chapter[^p134-2]: « Behold, I will give you a new heart, and put a new spirit in the midst of you »; because to the Galatians, the sixth chapter, the Apostle says: « In Christ Jesus neither circumcision availeth any thing, nor uncircumcision, but a new creature ». And therefore in the Psalm[^p134-3] it is asked: « Create a clean heart in me, O God, and renew a right spirit within my bowels »; which is done in spiritual men; the second to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « Though our man that is outward be corrupted, yet he that is inward is renewed day by day ».

85. (Vers. 39.). This same thing nonetheless[^p134-4] was expedient on account of the *disuse of the affection;* to designate which he says: *And no man drinking old hath presently a mind to new.* And he gives the reason of this on account of the judgement of the sense, when he says: *For he saith: The old is better.* — In which, by a likeness in the delight of drink, he wishes to show that our affection does not at once delight in the new things to be observed, unless it be led by the hand little by little. And therefore some said in Acts, the fifteenth chapter[^p134-5]: « Except you be circumcised after the manner of Moses, you cannot be saved ». But charity renews this affection, according to that word of John, the thirteenth chapter: « A new commandment I give unto you, that you love one another ». This makes the new taste as the old; whence the first of John, the second chapter[^p134-6]: « I write not a new commandment to you, but an old commandment, which you have heard from the beginning ». « Again a new commandment I write unto you, which is true both in him and in you ». Truly this is the *new commandment,* which empties out the old things and which makes the new taste well; the second to the Corinthians, the fifth chapter[^p134-7]: « If any be in Christ a new creature, the old things are passed away; behold, all things are made new »; because, as is said in Leviticus, the twenty-sixth chapter, « the new coming upon you, you shall cast away the old ». — So therefore, on account of the rawness of the disciples, on the part of *conversation,* on the part of the *flesh,* and on the part of the *mind,* the disciples were to be treated kindly, not yet to be constrained to fasting, above all to the fast of exultation, which befits none but those renewed through the grace of the Holy Spirit.

Whence it is gathered from the foregoing that the disciples of Christ were not to be constrained, neither to the fast of *mourning,* on account of the presence of the bridegroom, nor to that of *exultation,* on account of the absence of the Holy Spirit; and so they were to be kindly consoled, as being still tender and infirm. From the foregoing, then, it sufficiently appears how by the Lord the *sinners* were mercifully *called,* and the *proud* reasonably *confuted,* and the *infirm* sweetly *consoled.*
