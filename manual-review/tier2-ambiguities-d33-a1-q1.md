# Tier-2 ambiguities — d.33 a.1 q.1 (*Utrum proprietas sit persona*)

Logged 2026-05-07 during Tier-2 promotion.

## Inline `[?]` flags

1. **`[^11]` apparatus entry placeholder.** OCR shows footnote markers in the body for both *substantia* (where the relation "non videtur esse in substantia") and *subiecto* (where it "nec praedicare aliquid in subiecto"). The standalone fn 6 entry in the printed page-bottom apparatus reads only "In cod. bb et ed. 1 hic additur *vere*." — which would naturally key to the prior *vere* clause (`differentes sunt`), already taken as `[^10]`. The body anchor pair (`[^11]` for *substantia*, `[^12]` for *subiecto*) is preserved for OCR-marker parity, but `[^11]`'s apparatus content is left as a tagged placeholder pending PDF-eyes-on resolution. → Resolve at next decade-polish pass via 600 dpi PDF on p. 572 (PDF p. 162).

2. **Body, p. 573, *Ad 5*: "ordo erat in potentia in hoc, non[?] ratione sui, sed ratione eius ad quod est."** OCR shows "in hoc non' ratione sui" with a marker after *non*; codd. M P Q X read *et hoc non*, codd. *nec hoc*, cod. T more briefly *in hoc, tamen ratione sui*. The Vatican-text reading retained verbatim; flagged because the negation placement is variant-sensitive and the literal English ("not by reason of itself") is the syntactically natural reading but does not square neatly with the Boethian context. → Resolve at next decade-polish pass.

3. **Body, p. 573, top: "transit in substantiam[?]".** OCR fn 5 (`[^20]` here) reads "Supple cum Vat. *in substantiam*" — i.e. the word *substantiam* is supplied from the Vatican edition; many codices presumably elide it. Translation kept as "passes over into substance" with the [?] retained at the body anchor as a reminder. → Resolve at next decade-polish pass.
