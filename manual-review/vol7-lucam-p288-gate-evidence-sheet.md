### 15. ⭐ *excessus*: "excess" (Luke 9:31, ¶ 54) beside "transport" (¶ 47) — and the corpus's frozen rule

**Question.** The corpus rule in CLAUDE.md reads "*excessus* → 'transport' (NEVER 'excess')". `c9-v28-31` renders Luke 9:31
*dicebant excessum eius* "his excess" (Douay "his decease") and ¶ 54's four members "the excess of humility … of poverty … of
sorrow … of love", with a new `tr-excessus`. Conform to "transport", or keep "excess" with the note?

**Where the frozen rule comes from.** Both CLAUDE.md statements are **work registers**: the Itinerarium ("Register (exercised
in the pilot, locked) … *excessus* → 'transport' (NEVER 'excess'; 'ecstasy' is blocked by § 3's *ecstaticos excessus*)"; "★
load-bearing here: c4 § 3's *ecstaticus amor*") and the Sermones tractatus ("*contemplatio / meditatio / devotio* as the
Itinerarium, *excessus* → transport"). In both works every *excessus* is the mind's going-out. **No CLAUDE.md line rules the
word outside that sense**, and the corpus has never applied "NEVER 'excess'" to the other senses.
