## ⛔ `COLLATIO I.` CARRIES NO APPARATUS ANCHOR — the *septem donis* answer, not the Hexaemeron's

Read at 4× on the 450 dpi plate. Nothing stands after the period; there is no raised mark of any
kind, and none of p. 507's footer notes documents the title or the codices — n. 1 is `Cap. 19, 17.`
answering the Matthew lemma in ¶ 1. **Two works in a row now come out this way, so the Hexaemeron's
anchored heading is the exception in Vol V, not the rule.** As there: the collatio is the chunk
unit, settled by the index and by Quaracchi's citation practice, not by an anchor. Do not go
looking for one.

⚠ The raw cannot answer it either way — Vol V's OCR has no footnote numerals at all, and both
headings render as `COLLATIO<space><numeral>`. **The anchor question is only ever answerable on the plate.**

## The Summarium is present, full-measure, and UNANCHORED

short-Summarium mechanism is a property of the genre, not a spent one.

⭐ **This is the *septem donis*' closing lesson used FORWARDS for the first time.** That work wrote:
*"boundary shape is a function of unit size against leaf size — predict it, don't be surprised by
it."* Here the unit is **~3.7 printed pp**, thoroughly non-integral, so mid-page openings were
predicted before a single heading was located — and **all six interior boundaries are shared
pages.** No clean crossing anywhere in the work. Consequences, all mandatory:

- **Every interior chunk both RECEIVES a runover hand-off and MAKES one.** c2–c6 each begin at a
  footer-register number greater than 1 on their opening page and each stop short on their closing
  page. Only c1 makes without receiving; only c7 receives without making.
- **⚠ But the p. 498 rule still governs each one individually.** A shared page forwards a runover
  only when the incoming collatio's **numbered BODY** reaches it — not when only its heading and
  Summarium do. On this band every incoming collatio's ¶ 1 does appear to fall on the shared page
  (Coll. VII's heading, Summarium and ¶ 1 are all inside p. 529), so **expect a hand-off at all
  six — and verify it at all six.** Read anchors, only anchors.
- **⚠ NEVER derive a span's last page by subtracting one from the next collatio's index page.**
  The index gives a HEADING page and a heading page may be shared. Every span above is stated as
  *inclusive of its shared last page*, which is what the index pairs would have got wrong.

## ⚠ Header hazards — and one that is new

Running head garbles: `DE DF.CEM` · `DE DFXEM` · `DE DFrEM` · `DE DECEM PRAECEPTES` ·
`DE DECEM PKAECEPTIS` · `DE DECEM PRAECEFnS` · `COLL\TIO` · `C0LL\T10` · `COIJ.ATK)` ·
`COLLATIO K.` (= II) · `COLLATIO \l` (= II) · `COLLATIO 1.` (= I) · `COLLATIO 111.` (= III).
Real bare headers garble too: `COLLATIO L` (= I) · `IL` (= II) · `IIL` (= III) · `VL` (= VI) ·
`VIL` (= VII).

★★ **NEW, AND IT MATTERS: THE RUNNING HEAD NAMES THE COLLATIO THAT BEGINS MID-PAGE.** On every one
of the six shared pages the running head is already set to the *incoming* collatio while the top of
the page is still the outgoing one's text — p. 510's head reads `COLLATIO II.` above four columns of
Collatio I. **The running head is therefore not evidence of who owns the top of a page.** The frozen
rule already says *fix every span from the next real bare `COLLATIO N.` header, never from a running
head*; this work is the case that shows why, and the reason is not OCR garbling but the printing.

## Gutter — p. 507

`colcrop.py vol5 507` measures **1178 on a 15 px run** — a loud failure, and the expected one: the
full-width heading, subtitle and Summarium stack across the gutter at the head of the leaf. The
skew screen reports a 41 px walk with slices 0.17/0.25/0.34 returning no band at all — **that is
the stack, not skew** (`--skew` cannot tell them apart; the standing warning).

Profiling below the Summarium settles it. Four windows (0.42–0.95, 0.45–0.92, 0.50–0.92,
0.55–0.90) return the **identical band 1127/1129–1185/1186 (57–59 px), midpoint 1156–1157**, with a
10 px centre-rule island at **1151–1160**. The 0.38-start windows drag the band (1145–1183) because
they still catch the Summarium's last line. **Adopt 1157 for p. 507.** `colcrop`'s 1178 is the
frozen rule-5 case: the centre rule inked heavily enough to truncate the zero-ink run, leaving a
15 px remnant in the band's right half.

**Expect the same at every collatio opening — and at every mid-page opening too**, which here is
all six of them. p. 473 vs p. 479 in the *septem donis* is the governing precedent: **profile above
and below only when the mid-page heading falls inside the default 45–92 % window.**

## Register — the two *septem donis* rulings BIND, unchanged

1. ***pietas* → "piety" everywhere**, body prose and inside scripture quotations alike.
2. ***intellectus* → understanding, *intelligentia* → intelligence in BODY PROSE**; inside a
   scripture quotation the Douay's "understanding" stands.

⚠ They pull opposite ways on purpose. **The discriminator is whether the English is carrying a
Latin distinction — not whether the sentence is a quotation.** Both are stated in full in repo
`CLAUDE.md` § SEPTEM DONIS.

⚠ Watch for a third of the same shape here: the decalogue vocabulary (*praeceptum* / *mandatum* /
*lex*) sits under continuous Douay pressure across all seven collationes, and Collatio I ¶ 20
enumerates the ten. **Do not settle it chunk by chunk** — carry it to the gate the way *pietas* was
carried, and settle it once for the work.
