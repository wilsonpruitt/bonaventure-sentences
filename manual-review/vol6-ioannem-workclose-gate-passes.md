## APPENDIX A — the ruling-98 conform run over ALL of *Ioannem*: every bare ordinal, by chunk

**Census method.** Every `the <ordinal>` in each chunk's `## English` body (first … ninety-ninth, compound forms, *last*, *next to last*)
was classed: CHAPTER (followed by "chapter(s)", a page comment allowed between) · PSALM ("… Psalm") · BARE (a book name, *above* or
*below* within the clause before it, and a stop, colon, guillemet, anchor, bracket, or a clause word after it) · OTHER (enumeration,
book ordinal "the first of John", shared plural "the twelfth and the sixteenth chapters"). Every BARE outside the standard
`Book, the Nth: «` shape was read by eye (≈130), every OTHER standing after a book name was read by eye (≈30), and the Latin's ordinal
citations were counted paragraph by paragraph against the English (2,003 Latin regex hits; 61 paragraphs disagreed and every one was
read — Latin regex misses, nominative-evangelist and genitive forms, `[so printed]` digits). Found by that reading and added by hand:
one chained second ordinal (`c16-v23-33` L141 *the fifteenth*).
**Result: whole work 1,286 "the Nth chapter" · 755 bare · 4 Psalm numbers (§ 1 of the docket) · in scope (c17–Schemata) 372 / 0 / 3.**

"Job the twenty-sixth, that", "the first to the Corinthians, the thirteenth." were not caught), not drift since.

(the c17–c21 form: "Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p411-4]:"). Chains take it on each member ("in the seventh chapter and the
eighth chapter"; "in the fourteenth chapter and in the fifteenth chapter"), as `c17-v1-5` ¶ 5 already prints "the second chapter and the
