# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XVIII, Vers. 4-12
## *Hic ostenditur, quod comprehensio Christi voluntaria fuit ex parte Christi quadrupliciter*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 479 -->

11. *Iesus autem*[^p479-4]*, sciens.* Hic ostenditur, quod comprehensio Christi *voluntaria* fuit ex parte Christi quadrupliciter: primo in hoc, quod cum posset latitare, *se ipsum obtulit;* secundo, quod se offerendo *inimicos prostravit;* tertio, quod se offerendo *solo imperio discipulos liberavit;* quarto, quod *eos resistere non permisit.*

(Vers. 4.). Notatur ergo primo, quod voluntarie comprehensus est, quia *latitare potuit;* unde dicit: *Iesus autem, sciens omnia, quae ventura erant super eum,* ac per hoc valens eos declinare, antequam venirent; Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio[^p479-5]: « Oculi Domini multo plus lucidiores sunt super solem » etc. Et tamen se ipsum obtulit; unde dicitur: *Processit et dixit eis: Quem quaeritis? Processit,* id est, se voluntarie obtulit; Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p479-6]: « Oblatus est, quia ipse voluit » etc.; in Psalmo: « Confundantur et revereantur quaerentes animam meam ». Augustinus: « Illi eum quaerebant ad occidendum saeviendo, sed quaerebat nos ille moriendo ».

12. (Vers. 5.). *Responderunt*[^p479-7]*: Iesum Nazarenum,* in hoc patet, quod eum non agnoscebant. Chrysostomus: « In medio existens excaecavit eos, et sic latere potuit ».

*Dicit eis Iesus: Ego sum.* Hic notatur, quod voluntarie comprehensus est, quia se offerendo *illos prostravit;* unde sequitur: *Stabat autem et Iudas, qui tradebat eum, cum ipsis,* scilicet cum aliis stantibus; sed audito verbo, non steterunt, immo ceciderunt. Propter quod dicit:

13. (Vers. 6.). *Ut ergo dixit eis: Ego sum, abierunt retrorsum,* avertendo faciem suam ab eo, secundum illud Psalmi[^p479-8]: « Avertantur retrorsum et confundantur qui cogitant mihi mala ». *Et ceciderunt in terram.* Augustinus: « Una vox tantam turbam odiis ferocem, armis terribilem sine ullo telo percussit, repulit, stravit virtute latentis Divinitatis. Quid iudicaturus faciet, qui iudicandus hoc fecit? Quid regnaturus poterit, qui moriturus hoc potuit »? Iob vigesimo sexto[^p479-9]: « Et cum vix stillam sermonis eius audierimus, tonitruum magnitudinis illius quis poterit intueri »?

14. (Vers. 7.). *Iterum ergo interrogavit eos.* Hic notatur tertia ratio, quare voluntarie comprehensus, quia se ipsum manifestando *discipulos liberavit.* Manifestat ergo se eis, cum iterum interrogat: *Quem quaeritis? Illi autem dixerunt,* sicut prius: *Iesum Nazarenum.* Et sicut iterum interrogavit, ita secundo offert et manifestat se.

15. (Vers. 8.). *Respondit eis*[^p479-10] *Iesus: Dixi vobis, quia ego sum.* Et quia tunc non cognovistis, iterum dico, ut cognoscatis. Excaecati erant, ne eum cognoscerent, sicut Lucae ultimo: « Oculi eorum tenebantur, ne eum agnoscerent ». Postquam se obtulit, discipulos liberavit *solo imperio;* unde subdit: *Si*[^p479-11] *me quaeritis, sinite hos abire.* Augustinus: « Inimicis iubet, et hoc faciunt, quod iubet; sinunt eos abire, quos non vult perire ». Et ostendit
<!-- page 480 -->
Evangelista, quod hoc propria virtute fecit; unde dicit:

16. (Vers. 9.). *Ut impleretur sermo, quem dixit,* supra decimo septimo[^p480-1]: *Quia quos dedisti mihi,* o Pater, *non perdidi ex eis quemquam;* quia solus pati voluit; Isaiae sexagesimo tertio: « Torcular calcavi solus, et de gentibus non est vir mecum ».

17. Sed quid est quod dicit: *Non perdidi ex eis quemquam?* Nonne dicitur Matthaei decimo sexto[^p480-2]: *Qui perdiderit animam suam propter me inveniet eam?*

Propterea exponit Augustinus[^p480-3] de perditione *aeterna,* « quia nondum sic credebant in eum, sicut credunt quicumque non pereunt ». Illud autem dictum est credentibus perfecte.

18. (Vers. 10.). *Simon autem*[^p480-4] *Petrus.* Hic notatur quartum signum, quod voluntarie comprehensus: quia *Petrum resistentem compescuit.* Innuitur ergo fervor Petri in tuendo Dominum in hoc, quod *habens gladium, eduxit eum,* quasi non potens continere; *et percussit servum pontificis,* quasi non discernens personam, *et amputavit*[^p480-5] *auriculam eius dexteram.* Chrysostomus: « Innuit impetum Apostoli, quia ad ipsum caput impetum fecit ». *Erat autem nomen servo Malchus;* hoc, ut dicit Chrysostomus[^p480-6], dixit Evangelista ad maiorem certitudinem habendam. Petrus, zelo Legis accensus, percussit illos maleficos; primi Machabaeorum secundo: « Vidit Mathathias et doluit, et accensus est zelus secundum iudicium Legis, et accessit et trucidavit eum ». — *Spiritualiter:* Petrus, agnoscens[^p480-7], amputavit auriculam dexteram, id est obedientiam. — Petrum ergo sic resistentem compescuit; unde sequitur:

19. (Vers. 11.). *Dixit ergo Iesus Petro: Mitte gladium tuum in vaginam.* Victor[^p480-8]: « Cesset vindicta, exhibeatur patientia », iuxta illud Matthaei quinto: « Ego dico vobis, non resistere malo, sed si quis te percusserit in unam maxillam tuam, praebe ei alteram ». Et rationem reddit: *Calicem, quem dedit mihi Pater, non vis, ut*[^p480-9] *bibam illum?* Quasi dicat: si recte sapis, necesse habes, velle quod vult Pater. De hoc calice Matthaei vigesimo sexto: « Pater, si fieri potest, transeat a me calix iste ». Hunc calicem Filius bibit; unde Matthaei vigesimo: « Potestis bibere calicem, quem ego bibiturus sum »? et de voluntate Patris; unde ad Romanos octavo: « Proprio Filio suo non pepercit, sed pro nobis omnibus tradidit illum ».

20. (Vers. 12.). *Cohors ergo et tribunus* etc. Hic tertio ostenditur, quod comprehensio Christi fuit *ignominiosa* ex parte cohortis, quia ipsum comprehenderunt et ligaverunt tanquam latronem. Ideo dicit: *Cohors ergo,* id est multitudo militum in una societate constituta, *et tribunus,* qui eis praeerat, *et ministri Iudaeorum comprehenderunt Iesum et ligaverunt eum.* Victor[^p480-10] dicit, quod « mos erat Iudaeis, ut, quem morte dignum iudicarent, vinctum praesidi traderent », et ita tanquam malefactorem; ideo Matthaei vigesimo sexto dicit Dominus eis: « Tanquam ad latronem existis cum gladiis et fustibus comprehendere me ». Et ligaverunt, ne fugeret, quia dixerat Iudas Marci decimo quarto: « Quem osculatus fuero, ipse est; tenete eum et ducite caute ».

### Quaestiones.

21. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc, quod Dominus se ipsum obtulit Iudaeis. — Videtur, quod dederit eis occasionem male faciendi.
<!-- page 481 -->

Respondet Chrysostomus[^p481-1]: « Ostendit Dominus omnia, quae sufficientia erant eos revocare », detinendo scilicet visum eorum, ne eum agnoscerent, et deiiciendo eos ad terram solo verbo. « Quia vero in malitia persistebant et nullam habebant excusationem, tunc deinde manibus eorum tradidit se ».

22. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur: cum Petrus tantum irritaret eos, quare non detinuerunt eum?

Respondet Chrysostomus[^p481-2], quod « nullus alius eos detinuit, sed ea virtus, quae prius proiecit eos resupinos ».

23. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur: cum iussum esset Petro, quod non haberet *peram*[^p481-3]; quomodo tunc habebat *gladium?*

Ad hoc respondet Victor[^p481-4], quod Dominus mandatum illud dedit eis, quando misit ad praedicandum; instante vero persecutionis tempore, praecepit, ut ferrent necessaria victui et gladios emerent. Et accepit hoc a Lucae vigesimo secundo[^p481-5]: *Qui habet sacculum tollat et peram, et qui non habet gladium vendat tunicam suam et emat gladium.* — Vel potest dici, quod Dominus hoc non praecepit, quia vellet se defendere; sed ut ostenderet comprehensoribus patientiam, quia, cum haberet arma, noluit eis uti[^p481-6].

24. Quaest. IV. Item, cum dictum esset Apostolis Matthaei quinto[^p481-7]: *Si quis te percusserit in unam maxillam tuam, praebe ei et alteram;* videtur, quod male fecerit Petrus in hoc, quod percussit.

Respondet Chrysostomus[^p481-8]: « Hic non se ipsum ulciscebatur, sed Magistrum. Deinde, neque perfecti quodam modo et consummati erant », sicut postmodum fuerunt post adventum Spiritus sancti; et tunc impleverunt perfectionem sibi pro tempore illo praeceptam.

25. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur, utrum successor Petri possit gladium materialem educere et percutere. — Et quod sic, videtur exemplo Petri.

Ad hoc respondet Bernardus ad Eugenium[^p481-9], loquens de materiali gladio: « Tuus est gladius, tuo forsitan nutu, etsi non manu, evaginandus. Alioquin, si nullo modo ad te pertineret, dicentibus Apostolis: *Ecce, duo gladii hic,* non respondisset Dominus: *Satis,* sed: nimis est. Uterque ergo est Ecclesiae, sed materialis exserendus pro Ecclesia, spiritualis autem ab Ecclesia exserendus ».

## English
<!-- page 479 -->

11. *But Jesus*[^p479-4]*, knowing.* Here it is shown that the seizure of Christ was *voluntary* on Christ's part in four ways: first in this, that though he could have lain hid, *he offered himself;* second, that by offering himself *he laid his enemies prostrate;* third, that by offering himself *he freed the disciples by command alone;* fourth, that *he did not permit them to resist.*

(Vers. 4.). It is noted, then, first, that he was seized voluntarily, because *he could have lain hid;* hence he says: *But Jesus, knowing all things that were to come upon him,* and through this being able to avoid them before they came; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third chapter[^p479-5]: « The eyes of the Lord are much brighter above the sun » etc. And yet he offered himself; hence it is said: *He went forth and said to them: Whom seek ye? He went forth,* that is, he offered himself voluntarily; Isaias, the fifty-third chapter[^p479-6]: « He was offered, because he himself willed it » etc.; in the Psalm: « Let them be confounded and ashamed that seek my soul ». Augustine: « They were seeking him to kill him, raging, but he was seeking us by dying ».

12. (Vers. 5.). *They answered*[^p479-7]*: Jesus of Nazareth,* in this it is plain that they did not recognize him. Chrysostom: « Standing in the midst he blinded them, and so he could lie hid ».

*Jesus saith to them: I am he.* Here it is noted that he was seized voluntarily, because by offering himself *he laid them prostrate;* hence there follows: *And Judas also, who betrayed him, stood with them,* namely with the others standing; but when the word was heard, they did not stand, nay rather they fell. On account of which he says:

13. (Vers. 6.). *When therefore he had said to them: I am he, they went backward,* turning their face away from him, according to that of the Psalm[^p479-8]: « Let them be turned backward and be confounded that devise evils against me ». *And they fell to the ground.* Augustine: « One voice, without any weapon, struck, drove back, laid prostrate so great a multitude, fierce with hatreds, terrible with arms, by the power of the hidden Divinity. What will he do when he is to judge, who did this when he was to be judged? What will he be able to do when he is to reign, who was able to do this when he was to die »? Job, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p479-9]: « And whereas we have heard scarce a little drop of his word, who shall be able to behold the thunder of his greatness »?

14. (Vers. 7.). *Again therefore he asked them.* Here is noted the third reason why he was seized voluntarily, because by manifesting himself *he freed the disciples.* He manifests himself to them, then, when he asks again: *Whom seek ye? And they said,* as before: *Jesus of Nazareth.* And as he asked again, so a second time he offers and manifests himself.

15. (Vers. 8.). *Jesus answered them*[^p479-10]*: I have told you that I am he.* And because you did not know then, I say it again, that you may know. They were blinded, that they should not know him, as in Luke, the last chapter: « Their eyes were held, that they should not recognize him ». After he offered himself, he freed the disciples *by command alone;* hence he subjoins: *If*[^p479-11] *you seek me, let these go their way.* Augustine: « He commands his enemies, and they do what he commands; they let those go whom he wills not to perish ». And the Evangelist
<!-- page 480 -->
shows that he did this by his own power; hence he says:

16. (Vers. 9.). *That the word might be fulfilled which he said,* above, the seventeenth chapter[^p480-1]: *Because of those whom thou gavest me,* O Father, *I have not lost any one;* because he willed to suffer alone; Isaias, the sixty-third chapter: « I have trodden the winepress alone, and of the Gentiles there is not a man with me ».

17. But what is it that he says: *I have not lost any one of them?* Is it not said in Matthew, the sixteenth chapter[^p480-2]: *He that shall lose his soul for my sake shall find it?*

Therefore Augustine[^p480-3] expounds it of *eternal* loss, « because they did not yet so believe in him as all believe who do not perish ». But that was said of those believing perfectly.

18. (Vers. 10.). *But Simon*[^p480-4] *Peter.* Here is noted the fourth sign that he was seized voluntarily: because *he restrained Peter when he resisted.* The fervour of Peter in defending the Lord is intimated, then, in this, that *having a sword, he drew it,* as one not able to contain himself; *and struck the servant of the high priest,* as one not discerning the person, *and cut off*[^p480-5] *his right ear.* Chrysostom: « He intimates the onset of the Apostle, because he made his onset at the very head ». *And the name of the servant was Malchus;* this, as Chrysostom says[^p480-6], the Evangelist said for the having of greater certitude. Peter, kindled with zeal for the Law, struck those evildoers; the first of Machabees, the second chapter: « Mathathias saw and was grieved, and zeal was kindled according to the judgement of the Law, and he went up and slew him ». — *Spiritually:* Peter, recognizing[^p480-7], cut off the right ear, that is, obedience. — So, then, he restrained Peter resisting; hence there follows:

19. (Vers. 11.). *Jesus therefore said to Peter: Put up thy sword into the scabbard.* Victor[^p480-8]: « Let vengeance cease, let patience be shown », according to that of Matthew, the fifth chapter: « I say to you, not to resist evil, but if any one strike thee on thy one cheek, offer him the other ». And he gives the reason: *The chalice which the Father hath given me, wilt thou not that*[^p480-9] *I drink it?* As if he were to say: if you are rightly wise, you must needs will what the Father wills. Of this chalice, in Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter: « Father, if it be possible, let this chalice pass from me ». This chalice the Son drank; hence in Matthew, the twentieth chapter: « Can you drink the chalice that I shall drink »? and of the will of the Father; hence to the Romans, the eighth chapter: « He spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all ».

20. (Vers. 12.). *Then the band and the tribune* etc. Here, third, it is shown that the seizure of Christ was *ignominious* on the part of the band, because they seized him and bound him as a robber. Therefore he says: *Then the band,* that is, a multitude of soldiers established in one fellowship, *and the tribune,* who was over them, *and the servants of the Jews seized Jesus and bound him.* Victor[^p480-10] says that « it was the custom of the Jews that whom they judged worthy of death they handed over bound to the governor », and so as an evildoer; therefore in Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter, the Lord says to them: « You are come out as it were to a robber with swords and clubs to seize me ». And they bound him, lest he should flee, because Judas had said, in Mark, the fourteenth chapter: « Whomsoever I shall kiss, that is he; hold him and lead him away carefully ».

### Questions.

21. Quaest. I. But it is asked here first about this, that the Lord offered himself to the Jews. — It seems that he gave them occasion of doing evil.
<!-- page 481 -->

Chrysostom responds[^p481-1]: « The Lord showed all things which were sufficient to call them back », namely by holding back their sight, that they should not recognize him, and by casting them to the ground by a word alone. « But because they persisted in malice and had no excuse, then thereafter he delivered himself into their hands ».

22. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked: since Peter so provoked them, why did they not hold him?

Chrysostom responds[^p481-2] that « no other held them back, but that power which before threw them on their backs ».

23. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked: since it had been commanded to Peter that he should not have a *scrip*[^p481-3]; how then did he have a *sword?*

To this Victor responds[^p481-4] that the Lord gave them that command when he sent them to preach; but when the time of persecution was at hand, he enjoined that they should carry the necessaries of living and buy swords. And he took this from Luke, the twenty-second chapter[^p481-5]: *He that hath a purse, let him take it, and a scrip; and he that hath not a sword, let him sell his coat and buy a sword.* — Or it can be said that the Lord did not enjoin this because he wished to defend himself; but that he might show patience to those who seized him, because, though he had arms, he would not use them[^p481-6].

24. Quaest. IV. Likewise, since it had been said to the Apostles, in Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p481-7]: *If any one strike thee on thy one cheek, offer him also the other;* it seems that Peter did evil in this, that he struck.

Chrysostom responds[^p481-8]: « Here he was not avenging himself, but the Teacher. Further, they were not yet in a certain manner perfect and consummated », as afterwards they were after the coming of the Holy Spirit; and then they fulfilled the perfection enjoined on them for that time.

25. Quaest. V. Likewise it is asked whether the successor of Peter can draw the material sword and strike. — And that he can, seems so by the example of Peter.

To this Bernard responds to Eugene[^p481-9], speaking of the material sword: « The sword is yours, to be unsheathed perhaps at your nod, though not by your hand. Otherwise, if it in no way pertained to you, when the Apostles said: *Behold, here are two swords,* the Lord would not have responded: *It is enough,* but: it is too much. Both, therefore, are the Church's, but the material is to be drawn for the Church, but the spiritual to be drawn by the Church ».
