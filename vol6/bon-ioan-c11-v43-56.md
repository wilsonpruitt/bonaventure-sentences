# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XI, Vers. 43-56
## *Pars secunda capituli, de ipso miraculo*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 405 -->

#### Pars secunda capituli, de ipso miraculo.

59. *Haec cum dixisset* etc. Posita sunt praeambula ad mirabilem Lazari suscitationem, hic ponuntur *concomitantia* et *consequentia*[^p405-5]; et procedit in hac particula sic. Primo ponitur ipsius Lazari virtuosa *vocatio;* secundo, ipsius *resuscitatio;* tertio, ipsius *solutio;* quarto, consequens *aliorum aedificatio, aliorum* vero *destructio.*

(Vers. 43.). Lazari vero potestativa *vocatio* innuitur in hoc, quod *voce magna clamavit: Lazare, veni foras;* imperiose dicit: *Veni,* et *voce magna clamat,* eo quod ad vocem eius fiet resurrectio generalis, secundum quod praedictum est supra quinto[^p405-6]: « Venit hora, in qua omnes, qui in monumentis sunt, audient vocem Filii Dei, et procedent qui bona fecerunt in resurrectionem vitae, qui vero mala egerunt in resurrectionem iudicii ».

60. (Vers. 44.). *Et statim prodiit.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet ad Domini imperium Lazari *suscitatio;* propter quod dicit: *Et statim prodiit qui fuerat mortuus;* in quo notatur obedientia creaturae ad Creatorem; Marci quarto[^p405-7]: « Quis, putas, est iste, quia ventus et mare obediunt ei »? Origenes: « Cum omnia obediant Creatori, sola rationalis creatura inobediens invenitur ». Nec obstiterunt vincula; unde dicit: *Ligatus manus et pedes institis. Ligatus;* synecdochica[^p405-8] est constructio; *institis,* id est ligaminibus, quibus involvebantur manus et pedes; *et facies eius*[^p405-9] *erat ligata sudario;* et ideo non proprio egressu, sed divina virtute egressus est. Hac virtute usus est Christus in morte sua; Matthaei vigesimo septimo[^p405-10]: « Monumenta aperta sunt, et multa corpora Sanctorum, qui dormierant, surrexerunt ».

*Dixit eis Iesus: Solvite eum.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *ligati solutio.* Ad litteram ideo solvitur a ministris, quia Dominus, etsi totum possit facere, vult tamen aliis suam potestatem communicare. Beda[^p405-11]: « Potuit ligamenta solvere qui potuit mortuum suscitare; sed propter unitatem et individuam caritatem dicit ministris: *Solvite eum et sinite abire* ». Chrysostomus[^p405-12]: « Non voluit resuscitatum secum ducere, ut pompam mundi nos evitare doceret ».

61. (Vers. 45.). *Multi ergo ex Iudaeis.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *quorundam aedificatio* et
<!-- page 406 -->
*aliorum destructio.* Propter illos qui aedificati sunt ad fidem, dicit: *Multi ergo ex Iudaeis, qui venerant ad Mariam et Martham et viderunt quae fecit*[^p406-1]*, crediderunt in eum. Qui venerant;* Proverbiorum decimo tertio: « Qui cum sapientibus graditur sapiens erit »; supra quarto: « Multi crediderunt in eum » etc. Propter alios, qui peiores effecti sunt, dicit:

62. (Vers. 46.). *Quidam autem ex ipsis abierunt ad Pharisaeos et dixerunt eis quae fecit Iesus;* unde eodem uno alii profecerunt, alii defecerunt; secundae ad Corinthios secundo[^p406-2]: « Aliis sumus odor mortis in mortem, aliis odor vitae in vitam »; ideo Lucae secundo: « Ecce, positus est hic in ruinam et in resurrectionem multorum in Israel ».

63. *Moraliter* sunt in suscitatione Lazari quatuor notanda: primo, quod Dominus *quaerit, ubi sit;* secundo, *lapidem amoveri praecipit;* tertio, *ipsum prodire facit;* quarto, *discipulis solvendum exponit.*

*Quaerit* igitur primo Dominus, *ubi peccator sit,* cum ei vilitatem peccati sui ostendit. Sic quaerebat Dominus ab Adam Genesis tertio[^p406-3]: *Adam, ubi es?* Et Ieremiae tertio: *Leva oculos tuos in directum et vide, ubi non prostrata sis.*

Secundo, *lapidem amoveri praecipit,* cum a consuetudine peccati hominem cessare facit, quae tanquam lapis ponderosus deorsum trahit. Ideo dicebat anima poenitens in Psalmo[^p406-4]: *Iniquitates meae supergressae sunt caput meum, et sicut onus grave gravatae sunt super me.* Augustinus: « Quam difficile surgit quem moles malae consuetudinis premit »! Threnorum tertio: *Lapsa est in lacum,* id est in mortem culpae, *vita mea, et posuerunt super me lapidem,* id est sepulturam. — Unde notandum, quod Dominus suscitavit mortuam *in domo,* Matthaei nono[^p406-5], id est peccatorem peccatis mortuum per *consensum; extra domum,* id est per *opus malum; in sepulcro,* per *consuetudinem malam,* ut hic.

Tertio, ipsum *prodire iniungit,* cum propriam iniquitatem confiteri facit. *Prodire* est de occulto in apertum venire; unde Proverbiorum vigesimo octavo[^p406-6]: *Qui abscondit scelera sua non dirigetur; qui autem confessus fuerit et reliquerit ea misericordiam consequetur.* Augustinus: « Quando contemnis, mortuus iaces; si maxime contemnis, sepulcro iaces; quando confiteris, procedis ».

Quarto, *discipulis solvendum exponit,* quia tunc venit potestas sacerdotalis, quae ante confessionem locum non habet. Sed Dominus praecipit, ut ostendant se sacerdotibus ad solvendum; Lucae decimo septimo[^p406-7] de leprosis mundatis dictum est eis: *Ite, ostendite vos sacerdotibus;* et Dominus eis dedit officium, infra vigesimo: *Quorum remiseritis peccata, remittuntur eis; et quorum retinueritis, retenta sunt.* — Et notandum est, quod Lazarus resuscitatus triplici vinculo ligatus prodiit, quia triplex difficultas remanet in homine post remissionem peccati. Nam *facies ligata* dicit difficultatem in *cognoscendo, ligamen pedum* dicit difficultatem in *bene volendo, ligamen manuum* in *bene operando.* De his Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p406-8]: *Ligatis manibus et pedibus eius, proiicite eum in tenebras exteriores.* Sed ab his vinculis solvitur, quando per satisfactionem iniunctam ab hoc triplici vinculo solvitur et in pristinum statum restituitur. Hunc statum petebat Samson, Iudicum decimo sexto[^p406-9]: *Memento mei, Deus, in bono et redde nunc mihi pristinam fortitudinem meam.*

64. *Collegerunt ergo pontifices* etc. Ista pars libri, quae ibi[^p406-10] incipit: *Fuit homo missus a Deo,* in qua agitur de Verbo incarnato, fuit divisa in tres partes. In prima agitur de Christi incarnatione et de incarnati manifestatione; in secunda vero, de passione; in tertia, de resurrectione.

#### *Secundo tractatur de Verbi incarnati passione dupliciter.*

Hic ergo incipit secunda pars. Intentio igitur huius partis usque ad vigesimum capitulum[^p406-11] est agere de his quae spectant ad passionem; et dividitur haec pars in duas: quia quaedam spectant ad
<!-- page 407 -->
passionem ut *praeambula,* quaedam vero ut *circumadiacentia.* Primo ergo agitur de *antecedentibus;* secundo vero, de *concomitantibus,* in principio decimi octavi[^p407-1]: *Et cum haec dixisset Iesus, egressus est* etc.

#### Primo agitur de iis quae ad passionem spectant ut praeambula, tripliciter.

*Praeambula* vero ad passionem sunt tria, scilicet *Iudaeorum machinatio, passionis Christi praedictio, discipulorum consolatio.* Ideo prima pars habet tres partes. Primo enim agitur de *Iudaeorum conspiratione;* secundo, de *passionis praedictione,* ibi: *Erant autem quidam gentiles* etc., infra duodecimo capitulo[^p407-2]; tertio, de *discipulorum confirmatione,* infra decimo tertio: *Ante diem festum.*

#### Primo, de Iudaeorum machinatione ponuntur quatuor.

Prima pars, in qua agitur de *machinatione Iudaeorum,* qui mortem Christi machinabantur, habet quatuor partes. In prima describitur *Iudaeorum conspiratio;* in secunda, *conspirationis corroboratio,* ibi[^p407-3]: *Iesus ergo ante sex dies;* tertio, *conspirationis amplificatio,* ibi: *Cognovit ergo turba multa;* quarto, *in conspiratione obstinatio,* ibi: *In crastinum autem turba multa.*

*Conspiratio* ergo *Iudaeorum* hoc ordine describitur: primo ponitur *humani consilii indagatio;* secundo, *consilii determinatio;* tertio, ex hoc *Christi occultatio;* quarto, *Christi occultati inquisitio*[^p407-4].

65. (Vers. 47.). Ponitur ergo primo *Iudaeorum conspiratio,* quae facta fuit occasione manifestationis Christi; propter quod dicit: *Collegerunt ergo pontifices et Pharisaei consilium adversus Iesum*[^p407-5]. Unita fuit simul ambitio principum cum malitia Pharisaeorum, ut per vim facerent consilium iniquum; Ecclesiastici vigesimo primo: « Stuppa collecta synagoga peccantium ». *Et dicebant: Quid facimus, quia hic homo multa signa facit?* Chrysostomus[^p407-6]: « Adhuc *hominem* vocant, qui tantam Divinitatis fecerat demonstrationem ».

66. (Vers. 48.). *Si dimittimus eum sic, omnes credent in eum;* et ex hoc sequitur inconveniens, *et venient Romani et tollent locum nostrum et gentem.* Ideo hoc dicebant, quia Romani tunc imperabant eis; et si ipsi sibi novum imperatorem vel regem invenirent, timebant eorum indignationem incurrere; ideo magis voluerunt facere contra Deum quam contra hominem; propterea Proverbiorum vigesimo nono[^p407-7]: « Qui timet hominem cito corruet ». Ratio autem huius fuit, quia magis diligebant temporalia quam aeterna; unde Augustinus: « Temporalia amittere noluerunt et vitam aeternam non cogitaverunt, ac sic utrumque amiserunt ». Et quod timebant, hoc eis accidit; unde Chrysostomus[^p407-8] dicit, in eis esse impletum illud Psalmi: « Infixae sunt gentes in interitu, quem fecerunt »; Proverbiorum decimo: « Quod timet impius veniet super eum; desiderium suum iustis dabitur ».

67. (Vers. 49.). *Unus autem ex ipsis* etc. Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *consilii determinatio,* quae facta est *auctoritate humana, dispensatione divina* et *communi concordia. Auctoritate humana;* propter quod dicit: *Unus ex ipsis, Caiphas nomine, cum esset pontifex anni illius,* et ita auctoritatem haberet, et per hoc consilium authenticum daret, *dixit*[^p407-9]*: Vos nescitis quidquam.*

68. (Vers. 50.). *Nec cogitatis, quia expedit vobis, ut unus moriatur homo pro populo, et non tota gens pereat. Unus;* ad Romanos quinto[^p407-10]: « Si enim per unius hominis delictum multi mortui sunt, multo magis gratia Dei et donum in gratia unius hominis in plures abundavit ». Iste non dedit consilium, quod interficeretur propter suam culpam, quia non habuisset robur; sed propter aliorum salutem, ut nullus posset iam huic resistere propter Christi innocentiam; Ieremiae decimo octavo[^p407-11]: « Venite, et cogitemus contra Ieremiam cogitationes; venite, et percutiamus eum lingua et non attendamus ad universos sermones eius ». Illud consilium
<!-- page 408 -->
mortis Christi processit a sacerdotibus secundum illud Danielis decimo tertio: « Egressa est iniquitas a senioribus iudicibus, qui videbantur regere populum ». Huius consilii determinatio non solum facta est *auctoritate*[^p408-1] *humana,* sed etiam *dispensatione divina;* propterea subdit Evangelista:

69. (Vers. 51.). *Hoc autem non dixit a semetipso,* hoc est propria adinventione, sed divina inspiratione; propterea dicit: *Sed cum esset pontifex anni illius, prophetavit.* Spiritum propheticum honori sacerdotali attribuit[^p408-2]. In hoc patet, quod Spiritus sanctus multa nobilia opera etiam per malos sacerdotes facit; unde in reverentia habendi sunt; propterea, cum Paulus malediceret summo pontifici, comperto, quod summus pontifex esset, dixit: « Nesciebam, quod summus sacerdos esset », Actuum vigesimo tertio[^p408-3]; et Saul, cum fuit inunctus, prophetavit, primi Regum decimo. Et prophetiam eius explanat subdens: *Prophetavit, quia Iesus moriturus erat pro gente.*

70. (Vers. 52.). *Et non tantum pro gente,* scilicet Iudaeorum; *sed ut filios Dei, qui erant dispersi, congregaret in unum,* id est, ut praedestinatos ad unitatem Ecclesiae colligeret. Haec congregatio praedicta est Isaiae undecimo[^p408-4]: « Congregabit profugos Israel et dispersos Iuda colliget a quatuor plagis terrae »; et Isaiae sexagesimo: « Filii tui de longe venient »; supra decimo: « Alias oves habeo, quae non sunt ex hoc ovili, et illas oportet me adducere, et vocem meam audient, et fiet unum ovile et unus pastor ». Cum *auctoritate humana* et *dispensatione divina* concurrit *subditorum concordia;* propterea subdit:

71. (Vers. 53.). *Ab illo igitur*[^p408-5] *die cogitaverunt, ut interficerent eum,* nullo iam contradicente; quia, etsi prius quaererent, sicut dicitur supra septimo et octavo: « Quid me quaeritis interficere »? tamen non ita definito consilio. De hoc Proverbiorum decimo octavo[^p408-6].

72. (Vers. 54.). *Iesus ergo iam non palam*[^p408-7] *ambulabat.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *Christi occultatio.* Occultat, id est declinat, non timore, sed nostra instructione; non ut mortem fugiat, sed ut tempus congruum veniat; unde dicit: *Iesus ergo iam non palam ambulabat apud Iudaeos, sed abiit in regionem iuxta desertum,* secundum humanum modum loca secretiora quaerens, *in civitatem, quae dicitur Ephrem; et ibi morabatur cum discipulis suis.* De hoc Augustinus[^p408-8]: « Apparet, non esse peccatum, si fideles, qui sunt membra Christi, oculis persequentium se subtrahant et furorem sceleratorum potius latendo devitent, quam se offerendo magis accendant ». Iudaeos fugiebat et cum discipulis manebat; supra decimo[^p408-9]: « Oves illum sequuntur et vocem eius audiunt ».

73. (Vers. 55.). *Proximum autem erat Pascha.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *Christi latentis inquisitio,* quae fiebat ab eis qui ascenderant ad diem festum; quia Christus in diebus festivis solebat ascendere. Propterea dicit: *Erat autem proximum Pascha,* dies festus, *Iudaeorum.* Per antonomasiam Pascha *festiva dies* dicitur, quod fiebat mense primo, scilicet Aprili; Exodi duodecimo[^p408-10]: « Mensis iste vobis principium mensium; primus erit in mensibus anni. Decima die mensis huius tollat unusquisque agnum per familias et domos suas » etc. *Et ascenderunt multi Ierosolymam de regione ante Pascha,* ante scilicet diem festum. Et ratio subditur: *Ut sanctificarent se ipsos,* quia nullus audebat immundus comedere Pascha; Numerorum nono[^p408-11]: « Homo, qui
<!-- page 409 -->
fuerit immundus super anima, vel in via, faciat phase in mense secundo »; infra decimo octavo[^p409-1]: « Ut non contaminarentur, sed manducarent Pascha ». Hi venerunt se exterius mundare, sed interius per voluntatis homicidium inquinare; unde dicit:

74. (Vers. 56.). *Quaerebant ergo Iesum,* scilicet ad perdendum; Psalmus[^p409-2]: « Confundantur et revereantur qui quaerunt animam meam, ut auferant eam ». *Et colloquebantur ad invicem, in templo stantes.* Et modus quaerendi subditur: *Quid putatis, quia non venit ad diem festum?* Ista quaestio fiebat in templo. Unde impletum est illud Ieremiae septimo[^p409-3]: « Spelunca latronum facta est domus ista, in qua invocatum est nomen meum »; unde Matthaei vigesimo primo: « Vos fecistis eam speluncam latronum ». Quare autem sic quaererent, insinuat Evangelista: *Dederant autem pontifices et Pharisaei mandatum, ut, si quis cognoverit, ubi sit, indicet, ut apprehendant eum.* Simile mandatum perversum dedit Saul de Davide primi Regum vigesimo tertio[^p409-4]: « Considerate et videte omnia latibula eius, in quibus absconditur. Quod si in terra se obstruxerit, perscrutabor eum in cunctis millibus Iuda ».

### Quaestiones.

75. Quaest. I. Quaeritur, utrum Iudaei peccaverint in dando isto consilio? — Quod *sic,* videtur, quia reprehenduntur principes sacerdotum et Pharisaei, quod Dominum occiderunt sive occidi fecerunt, et ipse Dominus reprehendit eos supra septimo[^p409-5].

Quod *non,* videtur: 1. Quia voluntas Dei erat, quod Christus occideretur: ergo in volendo ipsum occidere conformabant voluntatem suam voluntati divinae; et si hoc: ergo non peccabant[^p409-6].

2. Item, ipse Dominus voluit Filium suum pati[^p409-7]; sed non potuit pati, nisi interficeretur; et Iudaei interfecerunt: ergo voluit, quod Iudaei interficerent.

3. Item, ad hoc venit, ut pateretur: ergo si Iudaei non interficerent, adventus eius frustraretur: ergo cum adventus eius efficaciam habuerit ex Iudaeorum consilio, videtur, quod Iudaei non peccaverint.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Iudaei peccaverunt et ratione *actus* et ratione *intentionis.* Ratione *actus,* quia voluerunt occidere eum quem sciebant innocentem esse, quod nullo modo bene potest fieri[^p409-8]. Ratione *intentionis,* quia non intendebant implere divinam voluntatem, sed suam satiare invidiam.

1. 2. Quod ergo obiicitur, quod Deo placuit mors Christi; dicendum, quod *activa* occisio fuit permissa, sed *passiva* fuit beneplacita, sicut est in passionibus Sanctorum; quia illi occidebant mala voluntate, et Christus patiebatur bona[^p409-9].

3. Quod quaeritur, quod frustra; dicendum, quod non; quia alius modus erat Deo possibilis[^p409-10], et Deus per malos ministros multa bona implet, sicut per diabolum probat Sanctos; et tamen diabolus non facit nisi demereri, sed Deus actum ordinat in bonum.

76. Quaest. II. Quaeritur, utrum Caiphas peccaverit in dando illud consilium? — Quod *non,* videtur:

1. Per textum, quia dicitur in littera[^p409-11], quod *non dixit a semetipso;* et per hoc innuitur, quod dixit a Spiritu sancto: ergo si quod est a Spiritu sancto non est peccatum etc.

2. Item, dicit[^p409-12], quod *prophetavit, cum esset pontifex:* ergo si actus prophetandi bonus est, non dedit malum consilium, sed bonum: ergo non peccavit.

**Sed contra:** Ipse hoc verbo intendebat dare consilium, quod Christus occideretur: ergo in dando istud consilium erat homicida etc.

Respondet Praepositivus[^p409-13], quod Caiphas in *mala voluntate* peccavit, sed in illius *consilii propalatione* non peccavit; quia Spiritus sanctus usus est eo sicut instrumento, et sicut usus fuit asina Balaam[^p409-14]. Unde illius locutio non fuit fructus malae
<!-- page 410 -->
voluntatis; quia voluntas non fuit nisi mala, locutio non fuit nisi bona. — Dicendum tamen, quod Caiphas non tantum protulit ut asinus, sed ut homo perversus, intendens per hoc persuadere malum[^p410-1].

Unde dicendum, quod istius sermonis principium duplex fuit: *interius formativum,* scilicet divina inspiratio, et haec simpliciter bona; et *Caiphae intentio,* et haec simpliciter mala. Et secundum illud duplex principium duplex respondet intellectus in sermone: primus est *carnalis,* et iste fuit malus, ad vitandam Romanorum offensionem; secundus est *spiritualis* et bonus, propter humani generis salvationem. Et sic concedendum, quod peccavit[^p410-2]; et aliquis propheta potest peccare, sic praedicendo, sicut miracula faciendo, quando suam pervertit intentionem.

## English

#### The second part of the chapter, on the miracle itself.

59. *When he had said these things* etc. The preambles to the wonderful raising of Lazarus have been set down; here are set down the *concomitants* and *consequents*[^p405-5]; and he proceeds in this portion thus. First is set down the mighty *calling* of Lazarus himself; second, his *raising;* third, his *loosing;* fourth, the consequent *edification of some,* but *the destruction of others.*

(Vers. 43.). Now the powerful *calling* of Lazarus is hinted in this, that *he cried with a loud voice: Lazarus, come forth;* imperiously he says: *Come,* and he *cries with a loud voice,* because at his voice the general resurrection will come to be, according to what was foretold above, in the fifth chapter[^p405-6]: « The hour cometh, wherein all that are in the graves shall hear the voice of the Son of God, and they that have done good things shall come forth unto the resurrection of life, but they that have done evil unto the resurrection of judgement ».

60. (Vers. 44.). *And presently he came forth.* Here is touched the second, namely, at the Lord's command, the *raising* of Lazarus; on account of which he says: *And presently he came forth that had been dead;* in which is noted the obedience of the creature to the Creator; Mark, the fourth chapter[^p405-7]: « Who, thinkest thou, is this, that both wind and sea obey him »? Origen: « Though all things obey the Creator, the rational creature alone is found disobedient ». Nor did the bonds stand in the way; whence he says: *Bound hands and feet with winding bands. Bound;* it is a synecdochical[^p405-8] construction; *with winding bands,* that is, with the bindings in which the hands and feet were wrapped; *and his face*[^p405-9] *was bound about with a napkin;* and therefore he came forth not by his own going forth, but by divine virtue. This virtue Christ used in his death; Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p405-10]: « The graves were opened, and many bodies of the Saints that had slept arose ».

*Jesus said to them: Loose him.* Here is touched the third, namely, *the loosing of the bound man.* To the letter he is loosed by ministers for this reason, that the Lord, although he could do the whole, yet wills to communicate his power to others. Bede[^p405-11]: « He who could raise the dead man could loose the bindings; but on account of unity and undivided charity he says to the ministers: *Loose him and let him go* ». Chrysostom[^p405-12]: « He would not lead the raised man with him, that he might teach us to avoid the pomp of the world ».

61. (Vers. 45.). *Many therefore of the Jews.* Here is touched the fourth, namely, *the edification of some* and
<!-- page 406 -->
*the destruction of others.* On account of those who were edified to faith, he says: *Many therefore of the Jews, who were come to Mary and Martha, and had seen the things that he did*[^p406-1]*, believed in him. Who were come;* Proverbs, the thirteenth chapter: « He that walketh with the wise, shall be wise »; above, in the fourth chapter: « Many believed in him » etc. On account of the others, who were made worse, he says:

62. (Vers. 46.). *But some of them went to the Pharisees, and told them the things that Jesus had done;* whence from one and the same thing some profited, others fell away; the second to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p406-2]: « To some we are the odour of death unto death, to others the odour of life unto life »; therefore Luke, the second chapter: « Behold, this child is set for the fall and for the resurrection of many in Israel ».

63. *Morally,* four things are to be noted in the raising of Lazarus: first, that the Lord *asks where he is;* second, he *commands the stone to be moved away;* third, he *causes him to come forth;* fourth, he *sets him before the disciples to be loosed.*

*He asks,* then, first, the Lord, *where the sinner is,* when he shows him the vileness of his sin. Thus the Lord asked of Adam, Genesis, the third chapter[^p406-3]: *Adam, where art thou?* And Jeremias, the third chapter: *Lift up thy eyes on high, and see where thou hast not prostrated thyself.*

Second, he *commands the stone to be moved away,* when he causes a man to cease from the habit of sin, which like a heavy stone drags him downward. Therefore the penitent soul said in the Psalm[^p406-4]: *My iniquities are gone over my head, and as a heavy burden are become heavy upon me.* Augustine: « How hardly rises he whom the mass of evil habit presses down »! Lamentations, the third chapter: *Fallen into the pit,* that is, into the death of guilt, *is my life, and they have laid a stone over me,* that is, burial. — Whence it is to be noted that the Lord raised the dead girl *in the house,* Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p406-5], that is, the sinner dead in sins by *consent; outside the house,* that is, by *evil work; in the sepulchre,* by *evil habit,* as here.

Third, he *enjoins him to come forth,* when he causes him to confess his own iniquity. *To come forth* is to come from what is hidden into the open; whence Proverbs, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p406-6]: *He that hideth his sins, shall not prosper; but he that shall confess, and forsake them, shall obtain mercy.* Augustine: « When you despise, you lie dead; if you despise greatly, you lie in the sepulchre; when you confess, you come forth ».

Fourth, he *sets him before the disciples to be loosed,* because then comes the priestly power, which before confession has no place. But the Lord commands that they show themselves to the priests to be loosed; Luke, the seventeenth chapter[^p406-7], of the cleansed lepers it was said to them: *Go, show yourselves to the priests;* and the Lord gave them the office, below, in the twentieth chapter: *Whose sins you shall forgive, they are forgiven them; and whose sins you shall retain, they are retained.* — And it is to be noted that Lazarus, raised, came forth bound with a threefold bond, because a threefold difficulty remains in a man after the remission of sin. For *the bound face* speaks of difficulty in *knowing, the binding of the feet* speaks of difficulty in *willing well, the binding of the hands* in *working well.* Of these, Matthew, the twenty-second chapter[^p406-8]: *His hands and feet being bound, cast him into the exterior darkness.* But he is loosed from these bonds when through enjoined satisfaction he is loosed from this threefold bond and restored to his former state. This state Samson sought, Judges, the sixteenth chapter[^p406-9]: *Remember me, O God, for good, and restore to me now my former strength.*

64. *The chief priests therefore gathered* etc. This part of the book, which begins there[^p406-10]: *There was a man sent from God,* in which there is treatment of the incarnate Word, was divided into three parts. In the first there is treatment of the incarnation of Christ and of the manifestation of him incarnate; in the second, of the passion; in the third, of the resurrection.

#### *Second, there is treatment of the passion of the incarnate Word in two ways.*

Here, then, begins the second part. The intention of this part up to the twentieth chapter[^p406-11], then, is to deal with the things that belong to the passion; and this part is divided into two: because some things belong to the
<!-- page 407 -->
passion as *preambles,* but others as *circumjacent things.* First, then, there is treatment of the *antecedents;* but second, of the *concomitants,* at the beginning of the eighteenth chapter[^p407-1]: *And when Jesus had said these things, he went forth* etc.

#### First there is treatment of the things that belong to the passion as preambles, in three ways.

Now the *preambles* to the passion are three, namely, *the machination of the Jews, the prediction of Christ's passion, the consolation of the disciples.* Therefore the first part has three parts. For first there is treatment of *the conspiracy of the Jews;* second, of *the prediction of the passion,* there: *Now there were certain Gentiles* etc., below, in the twelfth chapter[^p407-2]; third, of *the confirmation of the disciples,* below, in the thirteenth chapter: *Before the festival day.*

#### First, on the machination of the Jews, four things are set down.

The first part, in which there is treatment of *the machination of the Jews,* who were machinating the death of Christ, has four parts. In the first is described *the conspiracy of the Jews;* in the second, *the corroboration of the conspiracy,* there[^p407-3]: *Jesus therefore, six days before;* third, *the amplification of the conspiracy,* there: *A great multitude therefore knew;* fourth, *obstinacy in the conspiracy,* there: *And on the next day a great multitude.*

*The conspiracy,* then, *of the Jews* is described in this order: first is set down *the investigation of human counsel;* second, *the determination of the counsel;* third, as a result of this, *the hiding of Christ;* fourth, *the search for the hidden Christ*[^p407-4].

65. (Vers. 47.). First, then, is set down *the conspiracy of the Jews,* which was made on the occasion of the manifestation of Christ; on account of which he says: *The chief priests therefore and the Pharisees gathered a counsel against Jesus*[^p407-5]. The ambition of the princes was united together with the malice of the Pharisees, that by force they might carry out a wicked counsel; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-first chapter: « The congregation of sinners is like tow heaped together ». *And they said: What do we, for this man doth many signs?* Chrysostom[^p407-6]: « Still they call him a *man,* who had made so great a demonstration of Divinity ».

66. (Vers. 48.). *If we let him alone so, all will believe in him;* and from this there follows an unfitting thing, *and the Romans will come, and take away our place and nation.* They said this for this reason, that the Romans then ruled over them; and if they should find for themselves a new emperor or king, they feared to incur their indignation; therefore they chose rather to act against God than against man; for this reason Proverbs, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p407-7]: « He that feareth man, shall quickly fall ». Now the reason of this was that they loved temporal things more than eternal; whence Augustine: « They would not lose temporal things, and they gave no thought to eternal life, and thus they lost both ». And what they feared, this befell them; whence Chrysostom[^p407-8] says that in them was fulfilled that of the Psalm: « The Gentiles have stuck fast in the destruction which they made »; Proverbs, the tenth chapter: « That which the wicked feareth, shall come upon him; to the just their desire shall be given ».

67. (Vers. 49.). *But one of them* etc. Here is touched the second, namely, *the determination of the counsel,* which was made *by human authority, by divine dispensation* and *by common concord. By human authority;* on account of which he says: *One of them, named Caiphas, being the high priest that year,* and so he had authority, and through this might give an authentic counsel, *said*[^p407-9]*: You know nothing.*

68. (Vers. 50.). *Neither do you consider that it is expedient for you that one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not. One;* to the Romans, the fifth chapter[^p407-10]: « For if by the offence of one man many died, much more the grace of God and the gift, in the grace of one man, hath abounded unto many ». This man did not give the counsel that he should be killed on account of his own fault, because it would not have had strength; but on account of the salvation of others, that no one could now resist this on account of the innocence of Christ; Jeremias, the eighteenth chapter[^p407-11]: « Come, and let us invent devices against Jeremias; come, and let us strike him with the tongue, and let us give no heed to all his words ». That counsel
<!-- page 408 -->
of the death of Christ proceeded from the priests according to that of Daniel, the thirteenth chapter: « Iniquity came out from the ancient judges, that seemed to govern the people ». The determination of this counsel was made not only *by human authority*[^p408-1], but also *by divine dispensation;* for this reason the Evangelist adds:

69. (Vers. 51.). *And this he spoke not of himself,* that is, by his own invention, but by divine inspiration; for this reason he says: *But being the high priest of that year, he prophesied.* He attributes the prophetic Spirit to the priestly honour[^p408-2]. In this it is plain that the Holy Spirit does many noble works even through evil priests; whence they are to be held in reverence; for this reason, when Paul cursed the high priest, on learning that he was the high priest, he said: « I knew not that he was the high priest », Acts, the twenty-third chapter[^p408-3]; and Saul, when he had been anointed, prophesied, the first of Kings, the tenth chapter. And he explains his prophecy by adding: *He prophesied that Jesus should die for the nation.*

70. (Vers. 52.). *And not only for the nation,* namely of the Jews; *but to gather together in one the children of God that were dispersed,* that is, to collect the predestined into the unity of the Church. This gathering was foretold in Isaias, the eleventh chapter[^p408-4]: « He shall assemble the fugitives of Israel, and shall gather together the dispersed of Juda from the four quarters of the earth »; and Isaias, the sixtieth chapter: « Thy sons shall come from afar »; above, in the tenth chapter: « Other sheep I have, that are not of this fold; them also I must bring, and they shall hear my voice, and there shall be one fold and one shepherd ». With *human authority* and *divine dispensation* concurs *the concord of the subjects;* for this reason he adds:

71. (Vers. 53.). *From that day therefore*[^p408-5] *they devised to put him to death,* no one now speaking against it; because, although they sought it before, as is said above, in the seventh and eighth chapters: « Why seek you to kill me »? yet not by so definite a counsel. Of this, Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter[^p408-6].

72. (Vers. 54.). *Wherefore Jesus walked no more openly*[^p408-7]. Here is touched the third, namely, *the hiding of Christ.* He hides, that is, turns aside, not out of fear, but for our instruction; not that he may flee death, but that the fitting time may come; whence he says: *Wherefore Jesus walked no more openly among the Jews, but he went into a country near the desert,* according to the human mode seeking more secret places, *unto a city that is called Ephrem; and there he abode with his disciples.* Of this Augustine[^p408-8]: « It appears that it is not a sin if the faithful, who are members of Christ, withdraw themselves from the eyes of those who persecute them, and avoid the fury of the wicked rather by hiding than kindle it the more by offering themselves ». He was fleeing the Jews and remaining with the disciples; above, in the tenth chapter[^p408-9]: « The sheep follow him and hear his voice ».

73. (Vers. 55.). *Now the pasch was at hand.* Here is touched the fourth, namely, *the search for the Christ who lay hidden,* which was made by those who had gone up to the festival day; because Christ was accustomed to go up on festival days. For this reason he says: *Now the pasch was at hand,* the festival day, *of the Jews.* By antonomasia the Pasch is called the *festival day,* which was held in the first month, namely in April; Exodus, the twelfth chapter[^p408-10]: « This month shall be to you the beginning of months; it shall be the first in the months of the year. The tenth day of this month let every man take a lamb by their families and houses » etc. *And many went up to Jerusalem from the country before the pasch,* before, namely, the festival day. And the reason is added: *To sanctify themselves,* because no one dared to eat the Pasch unclean; Numbers, the ninth chapter[^p408-11]: « The man that
<!-- page 409 -->
shall be unclean by occasion of one that is dead, or in a journey, let him make the phase in the second month »; below, in the eighteenth chapter[^p409-1]: « That they might not be defiled, but that they might eat the pasch ». These came to cleanse themselves outwardly, but to defile themselves inwardly through the homicide of the will; whence he says:

74. (Vers. 56.). *They sought therefore for Jesus,* namely to destroy him; the Psalm[^p409-2]: « Let them be confounded and ashamed, that seek after my soul to take it away ». *And they discoursed one with another, standing in the temple.* And the mode of seeking is added: *What think you that he is not come to the festival day?* This question was raised in the temple. Whence was fulfilled that of Jeremias, the seventh chapter[^p409-3]: « A den of robbers is this house become, in which my name hath been called upon »; whence Matthew, the twenty-first chapter: « You have made it a den of robbers ». But why they were seeking thus the Evangelist intimates: *And the chief priests and Pharisees had given a commandment, that if any man knew where he was, he should tell, that they might apprehend him.* A like perverse commandment Saul gave concerning David, the first of Kings, the twenty-third chapter[^p409-4]: « Consider and see all his lurking holes wherein he is hid. And if he should hide himself in the earth, I will search him out in all the thousands of Juda ».

### Questions.

75. Quaest. I. It is asked whether the Jews sinned in giving this counsel? — That they *did,* it seems, because the chief priests and the Pharisees are reprehended, that they killed the Lord or caused him to be killed, and the Lord himself reprehends them above, in the seventh chapter[^p409-5].

That they did *not,* it seems: 1. Because it was the will of God that Christ should be killed: therefore in willing to kill him they were conforming their will to the divine will; and if this: therefore they did not sin[^p409-6].

2. Likewise, the Lord himself willed his Son to suffer[^p409-7]; but he could not suffer unless he were put to death; and the Jews put him to death: therefore he willed that the Jews should put him to death.

3. Likewise, he came for this, that he might suffer: therefore if the Jews had not put him to death, his coming would have been frustrated: therefore since his coming had its efficacy from the counsel of the Jews, it seems that the Jews did not sin.

**I respond:** It must be said that the Jews sinned both by reason of the *act* and by reason of the *intention.* By reason of the *act,* because they willed to kill him whom they knew to be innocent, which in no mode can be done well[^p409-8]. By reason of the *intention,* because they did not intend to fulfill the divine will, but to satiate their own envy.

1. 2. To what, then, is objected, that the death of Christ pleased God; it must be said that the *active* killing was permitted, but the *passive* was well-pleasing, as it is in the passions of the Saints; for those killed with an evil will, and Christ suffered with a good one[^p409-9].

3. As to what is asked, that it was in vain; it must be said that it was not; for another mode was possible to God[^p409-10], and God fulfills many good things through evil ministers, as through the devil he proves the Saints; and yet the devil does nothing but demerit, but God orders the act to good.

76. Quaest. II. It is asked whether Caiphas sinned in giving that counsel? — That he did *not,* it seems:

1. Through the text, because it is said in the letter[^p409-11] that *he spoke not of himself;* and through this it is hinted that he spoke from the Holy Spirit: therefore if what is from the Holy Spirit is not a sin etc.

2. Likewise, he says[^p409-12] that *he prophesied, being the high priest:* therefore if the act of prophesying is good, he did not give an evil counsel, but a good one: therefore he did not sin.

**On the contrary:** By this word he intended to give the counsel that Christ should be killed: therefore in giving this counsel he was a homicide etc.

Praepositivus responds[^p409-13] that Caiphas sinned in his *evil will,* but in the *publishing of that counsel* he did not sin; because the Holy Spirit used him as an instrument, and as he used the ass of Balaam[^p409-14]. Whence his speech was not the fruit of an evil
<!-- page 410 -->
will; for the will was nothing but evil, the speech nothing but good. — It must be said, however, that Caiphas uttered it not only as an ass, but as a perverse man, intending by this to persuade evil[^p410-1].

Whence it must be said that the principle of this speech was twofold: the *interior formative one,* namely divine inspiration, and this simply good; and *the intention of Caiphas,* and this simply evil. And according to that twofold principle there answers a twofold understanding in the speech: the first is *carnal,* and this was evil, for avoiding the offense of the Romans; the second is *spiritual* and good, on account of the salvation of the human race. And thus it is to be conceded that he sinned[^p410-2]; and a prophet can sin, in foretelling thus as in working miracles, when he perverts his intention.
