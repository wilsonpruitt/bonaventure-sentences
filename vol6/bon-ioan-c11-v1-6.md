# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XI, Vers. 1-6
## *Pars prima, de antecedentibus ad miraculum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 394 -->

### Capitulum XI.

#### Quarto, Filius Dei se manifestat perfectum reparatorem.

1. *Erat autem quidam languens* etc. Supra ostendit Dominus se curatorem et conservatorem et directorem, hic ostendit, se esse *perfectum reparatorem*[^p394-8]; quod fit in animae et corporis iterata coniunctione; et hoc ostendit in Lazari quatriduani suscitatione; et durat haec pars usque circa finem capituli, ibi[^p394-9]: *Collegerunt ergo pontifices* etc. Dividitur autem in duas: quia primo ponuntur *antecedentia ad miraculum;* secundo ponitur *ipsum miraculum,* ibi[^p394-10]: *Haec cum dixisset, voce magna clamavit.*

#### Pars prima, de antecedentibus ad miraculum.

Prima pars habet quatuor partes secundum quatuor antecedentia, quae determinat. In prima notatur *denuntiatio infirmitatis;* in secunda, *adventus Medici,* ibi[^p394-11]: *Deinde post haec dicit discipulis suis;* in tertia, *intercessio* sive meritum *fidei,* ibi: *Martha ergo, ut audivit, quia Iesus venit* etc.; in quarta, *exauditio pietatis,* ibi: *Iesus ergo, ut vidit eam plorantem.*

Describitur ergo primo *infirmitatis denuntiatio* hoc ordine: primo tangitur *languentis conditio;* secundo, *languoris denuntiatio;* tertio, *infirmitatis ratio;* quarto, *sanitatis dilatio*[^p394-12].

2. (Vers. 1.). *Languentis* igitur *conditio* describitur a *nomine,* cum dicitur: *Erat autem quidam languens Lazarus;* a *patria,* cum subditur: *a Bethania,* in quem locum solebat Dominus venire[^p394-13]; a *parentela,* cum subditur: *de castello Mariae et Marthae, sororum eius;* Lucae decimo: « Intravit Iesus in quoddam castellum, et mulier quaedam, Martha nomine, excepit illum in domum suam ». Et specialiter describit Mariam:

3. (Vers. 2.). *Erat autem Maria, quae unxit Dominum unguento et extersit pedes eius capillis suis,* in quo miro modo laudatur eius devotio; infra
<!-- page 395 -->
duodecimo[^p395-1]: « Maria ergo accepit libram unguenti nardi pistici, pretiosi »; Lucae septimo: « Ecce, mulier attulit alabastrum unguenti ». *Cuius frater Lazarus infirmabatur,* cuius etiam precibus fuit resuscitatus, sicut notatur infra eodem: « Iesus ergo, ut vidit Mariam plorantem ».

4. (Vers. 3.). *Miserunt ergo sorores eius.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *infirmitatis denuntiatio* facta a sororibus Lazari Domino; propter quod dicit: *Miserunt ergo sorores eius ad eum, dicentes: Domine, ecce, quem amas infirmatur. Dominum* vocant, non *magistrum,* quia petebant miraculum, non documentum; quasi dicant: Domine, qui potens es; Matthaei octavo[^p395-2]: « Domine, si vis, potes me mundare ». Et rationem adiutorii exprimunt: *Ecce, quem amas;* Psalmus: « Sperent in te qui noverunt nomen tuum, quoniam non dereliquisti quaerentes te, Domine ». Et opportunitas est: *infirmatur,* ideo miserere; Psalmus: « Miserere mei, Domine, quoniam infirmus sum ».

5. (Vers. 4.). *Audiens autem Iesus, dixit eis.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *infirmitatis ratio,* quam Dominus insinuat salutem petentibus; propter quod *dixit eis: Infirmitas haec non est ad mortem, sed pro gloria Dei.* Hoc dicit secundum divinam dispensationem; propter quod subdit: *Ut glorificetur Filius Dei per eam,* id est, per opus gloriam suam manifestet, quia in resuscitatione manifestavit potentiam suam, ac per hoc gloriam; unde supra secundo[^p395-3]: « Hoc signum fecit Iesus et manifestavit gloriam suam » coram discipulis suis. Quare autem erat in eo manifestanda gloria, occulte innuit Evangelista: propter specialem amorem erga illas personas; propter quod dicit:

6. (Vers. 5.). *Diligebat autem Iesus Martham et sororem eius Mariam et Lazarum.* Ideo diligebat eos, quia ipsi diligebant eum, secundum illud Proverbiorum octavo[^p395-4]: « Ego diligentes me diligo »; nec mirum, quia omnia, Sapientiae undecimo, « diligis, Domine, quae sunt, et nihil odisti eorum quae fecisti ».

7. (Vers. 6.). *Ut ergo audivit.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *curationis dilatio.* Quamvis enim vellet curare, non tamen *statim* voluit, sed maluit exspectare. Propterea dicit: *Ut ergo audivit,* id est, postquam audivit, *quia infirmabatur; tunc quidem mansit in eodem loco duobus diebus.* Ideo Dominus, ab amicis vocatus, non statim venit, sed exspectat, ut instantius et ardentius requiratur; ad Colossenses quarto[^p395-5]: « Orationi instate, vigilantes in ea ».

8. Est multiplex infirmitas[^p395-6], scilicet *gratiae honestatis amplectenda;* et haec quadruplex: *humilitatis;* Proverbiorum trigesimo[^p395-7]: *Formicae, populus infirmus, qui praeparat in aestate cibum sibi;* primae ad Corinthios primo: *Infirma mundi elegit Deus et contemptibilia* etc.; Matthaei undecimo: *Quia abscondisti haec a sapientibus et prudentibus, et revelasti ea parvulis.* — *Poenitentiae;* Ecclesiastici trigesimo primo[^p395-8]: *Infirmitas gravis sobriam reddit animam;* de hac in Psalmo: *Qui sanat contritos corde et alligat contritiones,* vel infirmitates *eorum,* secundum aliam litteram. — *Patientiae;* secundae ad Corinthios duodecimo[^p395-9]: *Virtus in infirmitate perficitur,* scilicet patientiae, quia Iacobi primo: *Patientia opus perfectum habet.* Ideo dicitur secundae ad Corinthios duodecimo: *Libenter gloriabor in infirmitatibus meis, ut inhabitet in me virtus Christi.* — *Compatientiae* sive compassionis; secundae ad Corinthios undecimo[^p395-10]: *Quis infirmatur, et ego non infirmor?* et primae ad Corinthios nono: *Factus sum infirmis infirmus, ut infirmos lucrifacerem.*

9. Item, est infirmitas *perversitatis culpae fugienda;* et haec quadruplex est, scilicet *cupiditatis;* Ecclesiastae quinto[^p395-11]: *Est et alia infirmitas pessima, quam vidi sub sole: divitiae congregatae in malum domini sui; pereunt enim in afflictione pessima.* — *Carnalis voluptatis;* Isaiae vigesimo quarto[^p395-12]: *Luxit vindemia, infirmata est vitis, ingemuerunt omnes, qui laetabantur corde.* — *Elationis;* Isaiae vigesimo quarto[^p395-13]: *Infirmata est altitudo populi terrae, et terra infecta est ab habitatoribus suis.*
<!-- page 396 -->
— *Obstinationis;* Deuteronomii septimo[^p396-1]: *Auferet Dominus omnes languores; et omnes infirmitates Aegypti pessimas, quas novisti, non inferet tibi.* Infirmitates Aegypti sunt obstinationes in malo.

10. Item, est infirmitas *difficultatis sequelae relevanda;* et haec quadruplex: ad *resistendum malo culpae;* Threnorum primo[^p396-2]: *Infirmata est virtus mea; dedit me Dominus in manu, de qua non potero surgere;* et Isaiae quadragesimo: *Iuvenes in infirmitate sua cadent.* — Ad *sustinendum malum poenae;* primae ad Thessalonicenses ultimo[^p396-3]: *Consolamini pusillanimes, suscipite infirmos;* quocontra Ezechielis trigesimo quarto: *Quod infirmum erat non consolidastis.* — Ad *aggrediendum bonum;* Ecclesiastici trigesimo primo[^p396-4]: *In omnibus operibus tuis esto velox, et omnis infirmitas non occurret tibi;* ad Romanos decimo quarto: *Infirmum autem in fide suscipite.* — Ad *perseverandum in bono;* primi Regum secundo[^p396-5]: *Quae multos habebat filios infirmata est.* Hae omnes infirmitates sunt *relevandae;* ad Romanos octavo: *Ipse Spiritus adiuvat infirmitatem nostram.*

11. Item, est infirmitas *passibilitatis poenae supportanda;* et haec quadruplex, scilicet *contractae* propter praevaricationem; Psalmus[^p396-6]: *Qui propitiatur omnibus iniquitatibus tuis, qui sanat omnes infirmitates tuas;* ad Romanos decimo quarto: *Qui infirmus est olus manducet.* — *Assumtae* ad nostram redemptionem; Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p396-7]: *Vidimus eum novissimum virorum, virum dolorum et scientem infirmitatem.* — *Inflictae* ad purgationem; Psalmus[^p396-8]: *Multiplicatae sunt infirmitates eorum, postea acceleraverunt.* — *Illatae* ad gloriae divinae manifestationem, ut supra eodem[^p396-9]: *Infirmitas haec non est ad mortem, sed pro gloria Dei, ut glorificetur Filius Dei per eam.*

### Quaestiones.

12. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod dicitur in littera, quod *Lazarus erat de castello Mariae et Marthae, sororum eius.* — Cum enim *caput mulieris sit vir*[^p396-10], potius debet dici econtra: Martha et Maria de castello Lazari.

Dicendum ad hoc, quod non sine causa introducuntur hic Maria et Martha ut principales, quia, etsi mulieres essent sexu, tamen firmitate animi et virtute Lazarum excedebant; quia etiam merito fidei ipsarum, sicut infra[^p396-11] patebit, est Lazarus suscitatus. Unde attendendum, quod Evangelista duo dicit: *de castello Mariae et Marthae,* ut eas sibi praeferat in virtute; *sororum eius,* ut eas sibi[^p396-12] postponat in sexus fragilitate.

13. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc, quod mulieres mittentes ad Iesum non rogaverunt, sed tantum insinuaverunt. — Videtur, quod aut fratris salutem neglexerint, aut Dominum contempserint.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod mulieres illae optimae et fideles *cognoscebant* Christi *potestatem,* ideo non rogabant, quod veniret, sicut regulus rogavit; *reverebantur maiestatem,* et ideo non audebant rogare, quod ibi iuberet, et hic fieret, sicut centurio[^p396-13]; *sperabant* de *bonitate,* ideo insinuabant: *Ecce, quem amas infirmatur.* Unde magister Hugo[^p396-14] dicit, quod *insinuatio* est una species orationis, quae fit tripliciter: *confidentia,* ut Mater Domini, supra se-
<!-- page 397 -->
cundo; *reverentia,* sicut hic, ut dicit ipse Hugo de Sancto Victore; *contemptu,* sicut superbus dominus aliquando de[^p397-1] famulo.

14. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Infirmitas haec non est ad mortem;* quia mortuus est.

Unde quaerit Chrysostomus[^p397-2]: Quomodo non fuerunt scandalizatae mulieres, quando non evenit quod Dominus eis praedixerat?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod sensus est: *non est ad mortem,* id est ad finalem terminum vitae, ut amplius non vivat; erat tamen *ad mortem,* id est ad dissolutionem animae a corpore[^p397-3].

Quod quaeritur: quare mulieres non fuerunt scandalizatae? dicendum, quod adhuc sperabant, quod Dominus deberet ipsum resuscitare[^p397-4]. — Vel crediderunt, ex quo non evenit ad litteram, quod Dominus parabolice esset locutus.

15. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc, quod Dominus *mansit duobus diebus* et exspectavit. — Videtur, quod non debuit, quia ipse erat amicus; et Proverbiorum tertio[^p397-5] dicitur: *Ne dicas amico tuo: Vade et revertere; cras dabo tibi, cum statim possis dare.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod distulit, ut *maius* quid faceret, quam infirmum curaret, et *magis acceptabile. Maius* enim fuit mortuum suscitare quam infirmum curare[^p397-6]. — Praeterea distulit ad *maiorem certitudinem* miraculi, quia, sicut dicit Chrysostomus[^p397-7], iam certum erat, quod non erat stupidus, sed quod vere mortuus; ad *maiorem potentiae ostensionem;* quia et maius fuit animam unire corpori iam putrido quam adhuc recenter mortuo. Ipse vero tempus congruum in omnibus operibus suis considerat.

16. Quaest. V. Item quaerit Chrysostomus[^p397-8] de descriptione istius Mariae, quae unxit Dominum unguento, utrum fuerit illa peccatrix, de qua dicitur Lucae septimo, quod unxit pedes Domini in domo Simonis leprosi. — Et respondet, quod non est illa, quia ista erat bona mulier et famosa, sed illa erat meretrix. Glossa[^p397-9] autem dicit contrarium, et similiter Gregorius.

Dicendum, quod assentiendum est Glossae et Gregorio in parte ista. — Quod autem Chrysostomus sentit contrarium, dicendum, quod non est inconveniens, expositores contraria sentire, maxime in nominibus personarum, quia non est ibi vis.

## English

### Chapter XI.

#### Fourth, the Son of God manifests himself as perfect restorer.

1. *Now there was a certain man languishing* etc. Above, the Lord showed himself curer and preserver and director; here he shows himself to be the *perfect restorer*[^p394-8]; which is done in the repeated conjoining of soul and body; and this he shows in the raising of Lazarus, four days dead; and this part lasts until about the end of the chapter, there[^p394-9]: *The chief priests therefore gathered* etc. And it is divided into two: for first are set down *the things antecedent to the miracle;* second is set down *the miracle itself,* there[^p394-10]: *When he had said these things, he cried with a loud voice.*

#### The first part, on the things antecedent to the miracle.

The first part has four parts according to the four antecedents which it determines. In the first is noted *the announcing of the infirmity;* in the second, *the coming of the Physician,* there[^p394-11]: *Then after that he says to his disciples;* in the third, *the intercession,* or merit, *of faith,* there: *Martha therefore, as soon as she heard that Jesus had come* etc.; in the fourth, *the hearing granted to piety,* there: *When Jesus therefore saw her weeping.*

First, then, *the announcing of the infirmity* is described in this order: first is touched *the condition of the one languishing;* second, *the announcing of the languor;* third, *the reason of the infirmity;* fourth, *the delay of health*[^p394-12].

2. (V. 1.). *The condition of the one languishing,* then, is described from his *name,* when it is said: *Now there was a certain man languishing, Lazarus;* from his *native place,* when it is added: *of Bethania,* to which place the Lord was accustomed to come[^p394-13]; from his *kindred,* when it is added: *of the town of Mary and of Martha, his sisters;* Luke, the tenth chapter: « Jesus entered into a certain town: and a certain woman named Martha received him into her house ». And he describes Mary specially:

3. (V. 2.). *And Mary was she that anointed the Lord with ointment and wiped his feet with her hair,* in which her devotion is praised in a wonderful manner; below,
<!-- page 395 -->
the twelfth chapter[^p395-1]: « Mary therefore took a pound of ointment of right spikenard, precious »; Luke, the seventh chapter: « Behold, a woman brought an alabaster box of ointment ». *Whose brother Lazarus was infirm,* who also was raised again by her prayers, as is noted below in the same chapter: « When Jesus therefore saw Mary weeping ».

4. (V. 3.). *His sisters therefore sent.* Here is touched the second, namely *the announcing of the infirmity* made by the sisters of Lazarus to the Lord; on account of which he says: *His sisters therefore sent to him, saying: Lord, behold, he whom thou lovest is infirm.* They call him *Lord,* not *teacher,* because they were asking for a miracle, not a lesson; as if they should say: Lord, who art mighty; Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p395-2]: « Lord, if thou wilt, thou canst make me clean ». And they express the reason for help: *Behold, he whom thou lovest;* the Psalm: « Let them trust in thee who know thy name: for thou hast not forsaken them that seek thee, O Lord ». And there is the fitness of the moment: *he is infirm,* therefore have mercy; the Psalm: « Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I am infirm ».

5. (V. 4.). *And Jesus hearing it, said to them.* Here is touched the third, namely *the reason of the infirmity,* which the Lord intimates to those asking for health; on account of which *he said to them: This infirmity is not unto death, but for the glory of God.* This he says according to the divine dispensation; on account of which he adds: *That the Son of God may be glorified by it,* that is, that by the work he may manifest his glory, because in the raising he manifested his power, and by this his glory; whence above, the second chapter[^p395-3]: « This sign did Jesus, and manifested his glory » before his disciples. But why the glory was to be manifested in him the Evangelist hints in a hidden way: on account of a special love toward those persons; on account of which he says:

6. (V. 5.). *Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister Mary, and Lazarus.* He loved them for this reason, that they loved him, according to that of Proverbs, the eighth chapter[^p395-4]: « I love them that love me »; nor is it a wonder, since all things, Wisdom, the eleventh chapter, « thou lovest, O Lord, that are, and hatest none of the things which thou hast made ».

7. (V. 6.). *When he had heard therefore.* Here is touched the fourth, namely *the delay of the cure.* For although he willed to cure, yet he did not will to do so *at once,* but preferred to wait. Therefore he says: *When he had heard therefore,* that is, after he heard, *that he was infirm, he still remained in the same place two days.* The Lord, therefore, called by his friends, does not come at once, but waits, so that he may be sought more instantly and more ardently; Colossians, the fourth chapter[^p395-5]: « Be instant in prayer, watching in it ».

8. There is a manifold infirmity[^p395-6]: namely *of the grace of uprightness, to be embraced;* and this is fourfold: *of humility;* Proverbs, the thirtieth chapter[^p395-7]: *The ants, a people infirm, which prepareth its food in the summer;* First Corinthians, the first chapter: *The infirm things of the world hath God chosen, and the contemptible* etc.; Matthew, the eleventh chapter: *Because thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them to little ones.* — *Of penance;* Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-first chapter[^p395-8]: *A grievous infirmity maketh the soul sober;* of this in the Psalm: *Who healeth the contrite of heart, and bindeth up their contritions,* or their infirmities, according to another reading. — *Of patience;* Second Corinthians, the twelfth chapter[^p395-9]: *Virtue is made perfect in infirmity,* namely, of patience, because James, the first chapter: *Patience hath a perfect work.* Therefore it is said, Second Corinthians, the twelfth chapter: *Gladly will I glory in my infirmities, that the virtue of Christ may dwell in me.* — *Of co-suffering,* or of compassion; Second Corinthians, the eleventh chapter[^p395-10]: *Who is infirm, and I am not infirm?* and First Corinthians, the ninth chapter: *I became infirm to the infirm, that I might gain the infirm.*

9. Likewise, there is the infirmity *of the perversity of fault, to be fled;* and this is fourfold, namely *of cupidity;* Ecclesiastes, the fifth chapter[^p395-11]: *There is also another most grievous infirmity, which I have seen under the sun: riches gathered to the hurt of their lord; for they perish with a most grievous affliction.* — *Of carnal pleasure;* Isaiah, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p395-12]: *The vintage hath mourned, the vine is made infirm, all the merryhearted have sighed.* — *Of elation;* Isaiah, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p395-13]: *The height of the people of the earth is made infirm, and the earth is infected by the inhabitants thereof.*
<!-- page 396 -->
— *Of obstinacy;* Deuteronomy, the seventh chapter[^p396-1]: *The Lord will take away all languors; and all the most grievous infirmities of Egypt, which thou knowest, he will not bring upon thee.* The infirmities of Egypt are obstinacies in evil.

10. Likewise, there is the infirmity *of the difficulty that follows upon sin, to be relieved;* and this is fourfold: for *resisting the evil of fault;* Lamentations, the first chapter[^p396-2]: *My power is made infirm: the Lord hath delivered me into a hand out of which I am not able to rise;* and Isaiah, the fortieth chapter: *Young men shall fall in their infirmity.* — For *sustaining the evil of penalty;* First Thessalonians, the last chapter[^p396-3]: *Comfort the feebleminded, support the infirm;* against which, Ezekiel, the thirty-fourth chapter: *That which was infirm you have not strengthened.* — For *undertaking good;* Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-first chapter[^p396-4]: *In all thy works be quick, and no infirmity shall come to thee;* Romans, the fourteenth chapter: *Now him that is infirm in faith, take unto you.* — For *persevering in good;* First Kings, the second chapter[^p396-5]: *She that had many children is made infirm.* All these infirmities are *to be relieved;* Romans, the eighth chapter: *The Spirit himself helpeth our infirmity.*

11. Likewise, there is the infirmity *of the passibility of penalty, to be borne;* and this is fourfold, namely *contracted* on account of transgression; the Psalm[^p396-6]: *Who forgiveth all thy iniquities: who healeth all thy infirmities;* Romans, the fourteenth chapter: *He that is infirm, let him eat herbs.* — *Assumed* for our redemption; Isaiah, the fifty-third chapter[^p396-7]: *We have seen him the most abject of men, a man of sorrows, and acquainted with infirmity.* — *Inflicted* for purgation; the Psalm[^p396-8]: *Their infirmities were multiplied: afterwards they made haste.* — *Brought on* for the manifestation of the divine glory, as above in the same chapter[^p396-9]: *This infirmity is not unto death, but for the glory of God: that the Son of God may be glorified by it.*

### Questions.

12. Quaest. I. But it is asked here first about what is said in the letter, that *Lazarus was of the town of Mary and of Martha, his sisters.* — For since *the head of the woman is the man*[^p396-10], it ought rather to be said the other way round: Martha and Mary of the town of Lazarus.

It must be said to this that not without cause are Mary and Martha introduced here as the principal ones, because, although they were women in sex, yet in firmness of mind and in virtue they surpassed Lazarus; because also by the merit of their faith, as will be plain below[^p396-11], Lazarus was raised. Whence it is to be attended to that the Evangelist says two things: *of the town of Mary and of Martha,* to set them before himself in virtue; *his sisters,* to set them after himself[^p396-12] in the frailty of sex.

13. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked about this, that the women, sending to Jesus, did not ask, but only intimated. — It seems that either they neglected their brother's health, or they held the Lord in contempt.

**I respond:** It must be said that those most excellent and faithful women *knew* Christ's *power,* therefore they did not ask that he come, as the ruler asked; *they revered his majesty,* and therefore they did not dare to ask that he command there and it be done here, as the centurion[^p396-13]; *they hoped* in his *goodness,* therefore they intimated: *Behold, he whom thou lovest is infirm.* Whence Master Hugh[^p396-14] says that *insinuation* is a species of prayer, which is done in three ways: *by confidence,* as the Mother of the Lord, above, the se-
<!-- page 397 -->
cond chapter; *by reverence,* as here, as Hugh of Saint Victor himself says; *by contempt,* as a proud lord sometimes of[^p397-1] a servant.

14. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *This infirmity is not unto death;* since he died.

Whence Chrysostom asks[^p397-2]: How were the women not scandalized, when what the Lord had foretold them did not come to pass?

**I respond:** It must be said that the sense is: *it is not unto death,* that is, unto the final term of life, so that he should live no more; yet it was *unto death,* that is, unto the dissolution of the soul from the body[^p397-3].

As to what is asked, why the women were not scandalized, it must be said that they still hoped that the Lord would raise him again[^p397-4]. — Or they believed, from the fact that it did not come to pass according to the letter, that the Lord had spoken in a parable.

15. Quaest. IV. Likewise it is asked about this, that the Lord *remained two days* and waited. — It seems that he ought not to have, since he was a friend; and in Proverbs, the third chapter[^p397-5], it is said: *Say not to thy friend: Go, and come again: and to morrow I will give to thee: when thou canst give at present.*

**I respond:** It must be said that he put it off so as to do something *greater* than to cure a sick man, and *more acceptable. For it was greater* to raise a dead man than to cure a sick one[^p397-6]. — Moreover, he put it off for the *greater certainty* of the miracle, because, as Chrysostom says[^p397-7], it was now certain that he was not stupefied, but that he was truly dead; for *a greater showing of power;* because it was also greater to unite the soul to a body already putrid than to one still recently dead. And he considers the fitting time in all his works.

16. Quaest. V. Likewise Chrysostom asks[^p397-8] about the description of this Mary, who anointed the Lord with ointment, whether she was that sinner of whom it is said in Luke, the seventh chapter, that she anointed the Lord's feet in the house of Simon the leper. — And he answers that she is not that one, because this one was a good woman and of good repute, but that one was a harlot. But the Gloss[^p397-9] says the contrary, and likewise Gregory.

It must be said that one must assent to the Gloss and to Gregory in this part. — But as to Chrysostom's holding the contrary, it must be said that it is not unfitting that expositors hold contrary views, especially in the names of persons, because the force does not lie there.
