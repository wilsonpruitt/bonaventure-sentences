# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XX, Vers. 19-25
## *De prima manifestatione notantur tria*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 511 -->

#### Secundo, de manifestatione facta discipulis.

36. *Cum ergo esset sero die illo* etc. Supra egit de manifestatione Domini facta mulieribus, hic agit de manifestatione facta *discipulis*[^p511-14]. Et sunt in hac parte tres particulae secundum tres manifestatio-
<!-- page 512 -->
nes, quae diversificantur quantum ad *modum* manifestandi et quantum ad *rationem:* quantum ad *modum;* nam prima est per *aspectum,* secunda per *tactum,* tertia per *gustum.* Primo enim se *ostendit;* secundo, *palpabilem exhibuit;* tertio, *comedit.* — Quantum ad *rationem* etiam manifestandi; nam primo ad *removendum incredulitatem omnium discipulorum;* secundo, ad *removendum incredulitatem Thomae;* tertio vero, ad *confirmationem dilectionis in Petro.* Et incipit secunda manifestatio ibi[^p512-1]: *Et post dies octo;* tertia vero, ibi: *Postea manifestavit se,* in principio vigesimi primi capituli.

#### De prima manifestatione notantur tria.

In prima manifestatione tria determinantur. Primum est *ipsa manifestatio;* secundum, ad manifestationem consequens *discipulorum missio,* ibi[^p512-2]: *Dixit ergo eis iterum: Pax vobis;* tertio, incidens *alterius manifestationis occasio,* quae fuit absentia Thomae, ibi: *Thomas autem* etc.

*Ipsa* ergo *manifestatio* hoc ordine describitur. Ponuntur enim quinque[^p512-3], quae ipsam declarant, scilicet *discipulorum congregatio, Christi apparitio, apparentis allocutio,* post allocutionem *sui ostensio,* post ostensionem *discipulorum congratulatio.*

37. (Vers. 19.). Primo ergo tangitur *discipulorum congregatio,* quae erat et propter *tempus,* quia sero; et propter *Iudaeos,* qui persequebantur; ideo dicit: *Cum ergo esset sero die illo,* scilicet die dominico[^p512-4], in quo apparuit Magdalenae, *una Sabbatorum,* quia in illis diebus sabbatizabant Iudaei, comedentes azyma; *et fores essent clausae,* ad sui tuitionem, unde: *ubi erant discipuli congregati propter metum Iudaeorum;* ideo simul in unum clausis ianuis convenerant, et per hoc digni erant, quibus Dominus appareret, secundum illud Matthaei decimo octavo[^p512-5]: « Ubi sunt duo, vel tres congregati in nomine meo, ibi sum in medio eorum ».

38. Cum, inquam, ita essent, *venit Iesus et stetit in medio.* Hic notatur secundum, scilicet *Domini apparitio;* ideo *stetit in medio,* ut ab omnibus videretur; ideo *stetit in medio,* ut se Mediatorem et reconciliatorem esse ostenderet; supra primo[^p512-6]: « Medius autem vestrum stetit quem vos nescitis », dictum est Iudaeis. Ipse enim, sicut dicitur ad Ephesios secundo, « est pax nostra, qui fecit utraque unum ».

39. Ideo sequitur: *Et dixit eis: Pax vobis.* Hic notatur tertium, scilicet *benigna allocutio,* in qua allocutione ipsos confortavit; unde Lucae ultimo[^p512-7]: « Dixit eis: Pax vobis; ego sum, nolite timere ». Unde Chrysostomus: « Voce firmavit fluctuantem mentem, dicens: *Pax vobis;* hoc est, ne tumultuemini »; ad Philippenses ultimo: « Pax Dei, quae exsuperat omnem sensum, custodiat corda vestra ».

40. (Vers. 20.). *Et cum haec*[^p512-8] *dixisset, ostendit eis manus et latus.* Hic notatur quartum, scilicet *Christi ostensio,* in qua ostendit, se esse eundem, qui passus fuerat, non tantum in spiritu, sed et in corpore; et ideo ostendit manus et latus, in quibus fuerant vulnera, et cicatrices remanserant; unde Lucae ultimo[^p512-9]: « Videte manus meas et pedes meos, quia ego ipse sum ».

41. *Gavisi sunt ergo discipuli, viso Domino.* Hic tangitur quintum, scilicet *discipulorum congratulatio* ex aspectu Domini; quod gaudium promiserat eis Dominus supra decimo sexto[^p512-10]: « Iterum videbo vos, et gaudebit cor vestrum ».

42. (Vers. 21.). *Dixit ergo eis iterum.* Hic tangitur secundum, scilicet ad manifestationem consequens *discipulorum missio*[^p512-11], in qua tria innuit Evangelista, quae Dominus mittendis discipulis contulit, scilicet *idoneitatem, auctoritatem* et *potestatem: idoncitatem,* cum pacem tribuit; unde dicit: *Dixit ergo eis iterum: Pax vobis,* id est tranquillitas, sine qua nullus ad persecutiones mitti est idoneus. Unde Chrysostomus[^p512-12]: « Quia praelium inexpugnabile habebant ad Iudaeos, ideo dicit rursus: *Pax vobis,* parem praelio dans consolationem » — Tribuit etiam *auctoritatem,* cum mittit; unde dicit: *Sicut misit me Pater, et ego mitto vos,* id est,
<!-- page 513 -->
sicut ego a me ipso non veni, sed missus, et ideo auctoritate veni; sic ego vos mitto, dans vobis auctoritatem; unde ad Romanos decimo[^p513-1]: « Quomodo praedicabunt, nisi mittantur »? Unde et Isaias, sexto, petebat hanc auctoritatem, cum dicebat: « Ecce ego, Domine; mitte me »; contra de malis prophetis Ieremiae vigesimo tertio: « Currebant, et ego non mittebam eos, non loquebar ad eos » etc. — Tribuit etiam *potestatem,* cum Spiritum sanctum contulit; unde et dicit:

43. (Vers. 22.). *Haec cum dixisset, insufflavit in eos*[^p513-2]. Augustinus: « Insufflando significavit, Spiritum sanctum non solius Patris esse, sed et suum ». *Et dixit eis: Accipite Spiritum sanctum,* id est donum Spiritus sancti, non ad omnia, sed ad remittenda peccata; et in hoc contulit potestatem clavium; propterea dicit:

44. (Vers. 23.). *Quorum remiseritis peccata, remittuntur eis,* id est, quos solveritis soluti sunt; *et quorum retinueritis, retenta erunt*[^p513-3], id est, quos ligaveritis ligati sunt; Matthaei decimo sexto: « Quodcumque ligaveris super terram, erit ligatum et in caelis; et quodcumque solveris super terram, erit solutum et in caelis ».

45. *Thomas autem, unus ex duodecim.* Hic ponitur tertium, scilicet incidens *tertiae manifestationis occasio,* quae hic describitur quantum ad tria, scilicet *Thomae absentationem, discipulorum relationem* et *ipsius indurationem.*

46. (Vers. 24.). Tangitur *eius absentatio,* cum dicit: *Thomas autem, unus ex duodecim,* id est unus ex specialiter electis; supra sexto[^p513-4]: « Nonne ego duodecim vos elegi »? Lucae sexto: « Elegit duodecim, quos et Apostolos nominavit ». *Qui dicitur Didymus,* quia dubius erat[^p513-5], *non erat cum eis, quando venit Iesus;* et ita Dominum venientem non vidit, quia a congregatione recesserat.

47. (Vers. 25.). *Dixerunt ergo ei alii discipuli.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *discipulorum relatio.* Referunt enim quod viderant: *Vidimus Dominum.* Ideo hoc referebant, ut audiens testimonium videntium credat resurrectionem; sed verificatum est quod scribitur supra tertio[^p513-6]: « Quod vidimus testamur, et testimonium nostrum non accipitis ».

48. Unde sequitur tertium, scilicet *Thomae induratio. Ille autem dixit eis: Nisi videro in manibus eius* etc. Duritia ostenditur in hoc, quod non credit per *auditum,* nisi ipse videat, *in manibus* scilicet, *fixuram clavorum.* Duritia etiam ostenditur in hoc, quod non tantum nolebat credere per *auditum,* sed nec etiam per *visum,* nisi adesset *tactus;* ideo dicit: *Et mittam digitum meum in locum clavorum et mittam manum meam in latus eius,* quod scilicet fuit lancea perforatum; *non credam.* In hoc durus fuit; unde Chrysostomus[^p513-7]: « Sicut simpliciter credere, et ut contingit, facilitatis est; ita circumscrutari et multum investigare grossissimae mentis est ». Sed haec duritia divina dispensatione in Apostolo permissa est ad tollendum nostram; unde Gregorius[^p513-8]: « Minus mihi praestitit Maria Magdalene, quae citius credidit, quam Thomas, qui diu dubitavit. Ille enim, dum in Magistro vulnera palpavit carnis, in nobis vulnera sanavit infidelitatis ».

### Quaestiones.

49. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic de potestate discipulis collata. Dicitur enim: *Quorum remiseritis peccata, remittuntur eis* etc.

**Contra:** 1. Isaiae quadragesimo tertio[^p513-9]: *Ego sum, qui deleo iniquitates tuas propter me:* ergo solius Dei est peccata remittere.

2. Item, Matthaei nono[^p513-10]: *Ut autem sciatis, quia Filius hominis habet potestatem in terra remittendi peccata; ait paralytico: Surge* etc.: ergo eiusdem potestatis est remittere peccata et curare incurabilem. Sed illud est potentiae infinitae et solius Christi-hominis et Dei: ergo et remittere peccata. Quaeritur igitur, quomodo differenter sit Dei, Christi-hominis et sacerdotis?
<!-- page 514 -->

3. Item, de hoc quod subditur: *Quorum retinueritis, retenta sunt:* ergo videtur, quod sacerdotes possint nobis claudere ingressum regni.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod in peccato duo considerantur, scilicet *culpa* et *reatus poenae; culpam* remittere solius Dei est *effective,* quia solius est dare *gratiam,* quae delet culpam; *meritorie* est Christi-hominis; *dispositive* vero sacerdotis est, quia facit, quo facto, Deus remittit, conferendo scilicet Sacramenta[^p514-1]. — Est autem alia remissio quantum ad *poenam,* et super hanc habet potestatem sacerdos, facta remissione culpae, et Christus-homo; sed potestas Christi-hominis est *excellentiae* et *universalis,* potestas vero sacerdotis est *ministerialis* et *particularis*[^p514-2]. Et sunt hae potestates ordinatae, quia sacerdos non habet effectum in remittendo poenam nisi per passionem Christi, nec rursus passio Christi alicui confertur, nisi cui Deus remittit culpam.

3. Quod obiicitur, quod salus nostra est in manu sacerdotis; dicendum, quod potestas ista intelligenda est, *clave non errante.* Tunc autem errat, vel quando solvit quem Deus non vivificavit; vel quando minus ligat, quam sit ligandus. Unde Gregorius[^p514-3]: « Illum discipuli viventem solvunt, quem Magister resuscitaverat mortuum. Si enim discipuli solverent mortuum, foetorem magis ostenderent quam virtutem ».

50. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Accipite Spiritum sanctum.* — Videtur enim, quod nondum deberet eis dare Spiritum sanctum, quia dicitur supra decimo sexto[^p514-4]: *Si non abiero, Paracletus non veniet ad vos:* ergo si nondum ascenderat, nondum debebat dare Spiritum sanctum.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Spiritus sanctus dicitur *accipi* vel *dari,* non ratione *essentiae,* sed *effectus.* Habuerunt ergo discipuli Spiritum sanctum *ante* passionem, sed ad *salutis suae operationem,* quae est per gratiam; habuerunt *post* passionem *ante ascensionem* ad *peccatorum remissionem;* habuerunt *post ascensionem* ad *fidei nostrae publicationem;* ideo tunc confirmati fuerunt, et in linguis igneis Spiritus sanctus descendit[^p514-5].

51. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur: quibus dicitur[^p514-6] pluraliter: *Accipite Spiritum sanctum?* — Videtur, quod pluribus.

**Contra:** Matthaei decimo sexto[^p514-7]: *Tibi dabo claves regni caelorum,* dixit Dominus Petro: ergo videtur, quod potestas relaxandi peccata fuit specialiter in Petro, hic autem videtur, quod in omnibus.

Et dicendum, quod potestas *dimittendi* sive potestas *solvendi* data est omnibus Apostolis; tamen specialius Petro data est plenitudo potestatis et eius successoribus, tanquam principi et capiti omnium Apostolorum[^p514-8]. Quia ergo caput erat, dicitur in Mat-
<!-- page 515 -->
thaeo: *Tibi dabo;* quia vero alii suae potestatis erant participes, ideo dicitur: *Accipite* etc.

52. Quaest. IV. Item, cum Dominus non dixerit nisi undecim: *Accipite Spiritum sanctum; quorum remiseritis* etc.; videtur, quod non omnes sacerdotes habeant hanc potestatem, sed solum illi qui tenent locum Apostolorum, quales sunt episcopi, non simplices sacerdotes. — Item, videtur Gregorius[^p515-1] hoc dicere, quod « solvendi et ligandi auctoritatem accipiunt qui locum regiminis sortiuntur ».

Ad hoc dicendum, quod dupliciter est *ligare* et *solvere:* vel in foro *poenitentiali,* vel in foro *iudiciali.* Primo modo convenit presbyteris ratione ordinis; secundo modo convenit episcopis et ceteris superioribus ratione dignitatis. Unde Hugo de sancto Victore[^p515-2]: « Alia est ligatio, qua ligant ministri Ecclesiae veraciter poenitentes debito iniunetae satisfactionis, et alia, qua ligantur intolerabiliter delinquentes vinculo anathematis ».

53. Quaest. V. Item obiiciunt haeretici[^p515-3] de hoc quod prius dicitur: *Accipite Spiritum sanctum;* et post: *Quorum remiseritis peccata* etc., quod nullus sacerdos habet potestatem ligandi et solvendi, nisi habeat Spiritum sanctum inhabitantem: ergo mali sacerdotes non absolvunt, nec etiam summus Pontifex, si malus est. Quodsi hoc, tunc in periculo est nostra salus.

Ad hoc multipliciter respondetur. — Quidam enim dicunt, quod prius datur Spiritus sanctus, et post exsecutio potestatis solvendi, quia solutio vel remissio non fit nisi in caritate Ecclesiae; unde dicunt, quod non est necesse, quod sacerdos habeat caritatem, sed sufficit, quod saltem sit in Ecclesia[^p515-4].

Aliter respondetur, quod dupliciter est solvere, scilicet *merito et officio* et *officio tantum.* Ad hoc, quod *merito* solvat, necesse est, quod habeat Spiritum sanctum; sed ad hoc, quod solvat *officio,* non oportet. Quod ergo Dominus prius dat Spiritum sanctum, hoc est, ut *digne* exsequatur, vel ad significandum, quod sine gratia Spiritus sancti non datur remissio peccatorum. Sed non oportet, quod illa gratia sit in sacerdote, sed in Sacramentis[^p515-5].

Potest autem et aliter dici, quod Spiritus sanctus dicitur dari, quando dantur dona sua. Sunt igitur quaedam dona, quae sunt *a* Spiritu sancto et *cum* Spiritu sancto et nunquam *sine,* ut *caritas.* Sunt et alia, quae sunt *a* Spiritu sancto et nunquam *cum* Spiritu sancto, ut *timor servilis.* Sunt et alia, quae sunt et *a* Spiritu sancto et possunt esse *cum* Spiritu sancto et *sine,* et talia sunt *characteres*[^p515-6]. Et quia potestas conficiendi et ligandi et solvendi sunt tales, manent et in iustis et in iniustis; et hoc divina dispensatione actum est, ut peccatum praelati subdito non praeiudicet.

54. Quaest. VI. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *Thomas non erat cum discipulis, quando venit Iesus;* quia Lucae ultimo[^p515-7] dicitur de duobus discipulis euntibus in Emmaus, quod *regressi sunt in Ierusalem et invenerunt congregatos undecim;* et dum ipsi narrarent quod viderant, dicit, quod *stetit Iesus in medio eorum:* ergo si non erant undecim nisi cum Thoma, videtur, quod Thomas tunc ibi esset.

Ad hoc respondet Augustinus in tertio de Concordia Evangelistarum[^p515-8]: « Intelligendum est, inquit, quod Thomas inde exierat, antequam eis loquentibus Dominus appareret »; unde verum est, quod discipuli illi invenerant Thomam, sed dum illi in sermone persisterent, Thomas exivit, et Dominus intravit.

## English

#### Second, on the manifestation made to the disciples.

36. *When therefore it was late that same day* etc. Above he treated of the manifestation of the Lord made to the women; here he treats of the manifestation made *to the disciples*[^p511-14]. And there are in this part three portions according to the three manifesta-
<!-- page 512 -->
tions, which are diversified as to the *manner* of manifesting and as to the *reason:* as to the *manner;* for the first is through *beholding,* the second through *touch,* the third through *taste.* For first he *shows himself;* second, he *offered himself palpable;* third, he *eats.* — As to the *reason* of manifesting also; for the first is for *removing the incredulity of all the disciples;* the second, for *removing the incredulity of Thomas;* but the third, for *the confirmation of dilection in Peter.* And the second manifestation begins there[^p512-1]: *And after eight days;* but the third, there: *After this he manifested himself,* at the beginning of the twenty-first chapter.

#### On the first manifestation three things are noted.

In the first manifestation three things are determined. The first is *the manifestation itself;* the second, the *sending of the disciples* following upon the manifestation, there[^p512-2]: *He said therefore to them again: Peace be to you;* third, the incidental *occasion of the other manifestation,* which was the absence of Thomas, there: *Now Thomas* etc.

*The manifestation itself,* then, is described in this order. For five things are set down[^p512-3] which declare it, namely the *gathering of the disciples,* the *appearing of Christ,* the *address of the one appearing,* after the address *the showing of himself,* after the showing *the congratulation of the disciples.*

37. (Vers. 19.). First, then, is touched the *gathering of the disciples,* which was both on account of the *time,* because it was late; and on account of the *Jews,* who were persecuting; therefore he says: *When therefore it was late that same day,* namely on the Lord's day[^p512-4], on which he appeared to Magdalen, *one of the Sabbaths,* because in those days the Jews kept sabbath, eating unleavened bread; *and the doors were shut,* for their own protection, whence: *where the disciples were gathered together for fear of the Jews;* therefore they had come together into one, with the doors shut, and by this they were worthy that the Lord should appear to them, according to that of Matthew, the eighteenth chapter[^p512-5]: « Where there are two, or three gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them ».

38. When, I say, they were so, *Jesus came and stood in the midst.* Here is noted the second, namely the *appearing of the Lord;* therefore he *stood in the midst,* that he might be seen by all; therefore he *stood in the midst,* that he might show himself to be the Mediator and reconciler; above, the first chapter[^p512-6]: « But there hath stood one in the midst of you, whom you know not », said to the Jews. For he himself, as is said to the Ephesians, the second chapter, « is our peace, who hath made both one ».

39. Therefore there follows: *And he said to them: Peace be to you.* Here is noted the third, namely the *kindly address,* in which address he strengthened them; whence Luke, the last chapter[^p512-7]: « He said to them: Peace be to you; it is I, fear not ». Whence Chrysostom: « By his voice he steadied the wavering mind, saying: *Peace be to you;* that is, be not in tumult »; to the Philippians, the last chapter: « The peace of God, which surpasseth all understanding, keep your hearts ».

40. (Vers. 20.). *And when he had said this*[^p512-8], *he showed them his hands and his side.* Here is noted the fourth, namely the *showing of Christ,* in which he showed that he was the same who had suffered, not only in spirit, but also in body; and therefore he showed his hands and side, in which the wounds had been, and the scars had remained; whence Luke, the last chapter[^p512-9]: « See my hands and my feet, that it is I myself ».

41. *The disciples therefore were glad, when they saw the Lord.* Here is touched the fifth, namely the *congratulation of the disciples* from the beholding of the Lord; which joy the Lord had promised them above, the sixteenth chapter[^p512-10]: « I will see you again, and your heart shall rejoice ».

42. (Vers. 21.). *He said therefore to them again.* Here is touched the second, namely the *sending of the disciples*[^p512-11] following upon the manifestation, in which the Evangelist intimates three things which the Lord conferred on the disciples to be sent, namely *fitness, authority* and *power: fitness* [so printed: *idoncitatem* for *idoneitatem*], when he bestows peace; whence he says: *He said therefore to them again: Peace be to you,* that is, tranquillity, without which no one is fit to be sent to persecutions. Whence Chrysostom[^p512-12]: « Because they had an unconquerable battle against the Jews, therefore he says again: *Peace be to you,* giving a consolation equal to the battle » — He bestows also *authority,* when he sends; whence he says: *As the Father hath sent me, I also send you,* that is,
<!-- page 513 -->
as I came not of myself, but sent, and therefore came with authority; so I send you, giving you authority; whence to the Romans, the tenth chapter[^p513-1]: « How shall they preach, unless they be sent »? Whence Isaias too, in the sixth chapter, asked for this authority, when he said: « Behold me, Lord; send me »; on the contrary, of the evil prophets, Jeremias, the twenty-third chapter: « They ran, and I did not send them, I did not speak to them » etc. — He bestows also *power,* when he conferred the Holy Spirit; whence he also says:

43. (Vers. 22.). *When he had said this, he breathed on them*[^p513-2]. Augustine: « By breathing on them he signified that the Holy Spirit is not of the Father alone, but also his own ». *And he said to them: Receive ye the Holy Spirit,* that is, the gift of the Holy Spirit, not for all things, but for the remitting of sins; and in this he conferred the power of the keys; for this reason he says:

44. (Vers. 23.). *Whose sins you shall remit, they are remitted to them,* that is, those whom you shall loose are loosed; *and whose sins you shall retain, they shall be retained*[^p513-3], that is, those whom you shall bind are bound; Matthew, the sixteenth chapter: « Whatsoever thou shalt bind upon earth, it shall be bound also in heaven; and whatsoever thou shalt loose upon earth, it shall be loosed also in heaven ».

45. *Now Thomas, one of the twelve.* Here is set down the third, namely the incidental *occasion of the third manifestation,* which is described here as to three things, namely *the absenting of Thomas, the report of the disciples* and *his own hardening.*

46. (Vers. 24.). His *absenting* is touched when he says: *Now Thomas, one of the twelve,* that is, one of those specially chosen; above, the sixth chapter[^p513-4]: « Have not I chosen you twelve »? Luke, the sixth chapter: « He chose twelve, whom also he named Apostles ». *Who is called Didymus,* because he was doubtful[^p513-5], *was not with them when Jesus came;* and so he did not see the Lord coming, because he had withdrawn from the gathering.

47. (Vers. 25.). *The other disciples therefore said to him.* Here is touched the second, namely the *report of the disciples.* For they report what they had seen: *We have seen the Lord.* They reported this so that, hearing the testimony of those who saw, he might believe the resurrection; but there was verified what is written above, the third chapter[^p513-6]: « What we have seen we testify, and you receive not our testimony ».

48. Whence there follows the third, namely *the hardening of Thomas. But he said to them: Except I shall see in his hands* etc. Hardness is shown in this, that he does not believe through *hearing,* unless he himself see, *in his hands,* namely, *the print of the nails.* Hardness is shown also in this, that he was unwilling to believe not only through *hearing,* but not even through *sight,* unless *touch* were present; therefore he says: *And put my finger into the place of the nails, and put my hand into his side,* which namely was pierced by the lance; *I will not believe.* In this he was hard; whence Chrysostom[^p513-7]: « As to believe simply, and as it chances, is a mark of facility; so to scrutinize round about and to investigate much is a mark of a most gross mind ». But this hardness was permitted in the Apostle by the divine dispensation for taking away ours; whence Gregory[^p513-8]: « Mary Magdalen, who believed more quickly, did less for me than Thomas, who doubted long. For he, while he felt the wounds of the flesh in the Teacher, healed in us the wounds of unbelief ».

### Questions.

49. Quaest. I. But it is asked here about the power conferred on the disciples. For it is said: *Whose sins you shall remit, they are remitted to them* etc.

**On the contrary:** 1. Isaias, the forty-third chapter[^p513-9]: *I am he that blot out thy iniquities for my own sake:* therefore it belongs to God alone to remit sins.

2. Likewise, Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p513-10]: *But that you may know that the Son of man hath power on earth to remit sins; he saith to the man sick of the palsy: Arise* etc.: therefore it is of the same power to remit sins and to cure the incurable. But that belongs to infinite might and to Christ-man and God alone: therefore also to remit sins. It is asked, then, how it belongs differently to God, to Christ-man and to the priest?
<!-- page 514 -->

3. Likewise, about this which is added: *Whose sins you shall retain, they are retained:* therefore it seems that priests can close against us the entrance of the kingdom.

**I respond:** It must be said that in sin two things are considered, namely the *fault* and the *guilt of punishment;* to remit the *fault* belongs to God alone *effectively,* because it belongs to him alone to give *grace,* which blots out the fault; *meritoriously* it belongs to Christ-man; but *dispositively* it belongs to the priest, because he does that by which, once done, God remits, namely by conferring the Sacraments[^p514-1]. — But there is another remission as to the *punishment,* and over this the priest has power, once the remission of the fault has been made, and Christ-man; but the power of Christ-man is *of excellence* and *universal,* while the power of the priest is *ministerial* and *particular*[^p514-2]. And these powers are ordered, because the priest has no effect in remitting punishment except through the passion of Christ, nor again is the passion of Christ conferred on anyone, except on him to whom God remits the fault.

3. As to what is objected, that our salvation is in the hand of the priest; it must be said that this power is to be understood *with the key not erring.* But it errs then, either when it looses one whom God has not quickened; or when it binds less than he is to be bound. Whence Gregory[^p514-3]: « The disciples loose him living whom the Teacher had raised dead. For if the disciples loosed him dead, they would show stench rather than power ».

50. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *Receive ye the Holy Spirit.* — For it seems that he ought not yet to give them the Holy Spirit, because it is said above, the sixteenth chapter[^p514-4]: *If I go not, the Paraclete will not come to you:* therefore if he had not yet ascended, he ought not yet to give the Holy Spirit.

**I respond:** It must be said that the Holy Spirit is said *to be received* or *to be given,* not by reason of the *essence,* but of the *effect.* The disciples therefore had the Holy Spirit *before* the passion, but for the *working of their own salvation,* which is through grace; they had him *after* the passion *before the ascension* for the *remission of sins;* they had him *after the ascension* for the *making public of our faith;* therefore they were then confirmed, and the Holy Spirit descended in fiery tongues[^p514-5].

51. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked: to whom is it said[^p514-6] in the plural: *Receive ye the Holy Spirit?* — It seems, to several.

**On the contrary:** Matthew, the sixteenth chapter[^p514-7]: *To thee will I give the keys of the kingdom of heaven,* the Lord said to Peter: therefore it seems that the power of releasing sins was specially in Peter, while here it seems that it is in all.

And it must be said that the power of *forgiving,* or the power of *loosing,* was given to all the Apostles; yet more specially to Peter was given the plenitude of power, and to his successors, as to the prince and head of all the Apostles[^p514-8]. Because, then, he was the head, it is said in Mat-
<!-- page 515 -->
thew: *To thee will I give;* but because the others were partakers of his power, therefore it is said: *Receive ye* etc.

52. Quaest. IV. Likewise, since the Lord said only to the eleven: *Receive ye the Holy Spirit; whose sins you shall remit* etc.; it seems that not all priests have this power, but only those who hold the place of the Apostles, such as are bishops, not simple priests. — Likewise, Gregory[^p515-1] seems to say this, that « they receive the authority of loosing and binding who obtain the place of rule ».

To this it must be said that to *bind* and to *loose* is twofold: either in the *penitential* forum, or in the *judicial* forum. In the first way it belongs to presbyters by reason of order; in the second way it belongs to bishops and the other superiors by reason of dignity. Whence Hugh of St. Victor[^p515-2]: « One is the binding by which the ministers of the Church bind the truly penitent with the debt of an enjoined [so printed: *iniunetae* for *iniunctae*] satisfaction, and another, by which the intolerably delinquent are bound with the chain of anathema ».

53. Quaest. V. Likewise the heretics[^p515-3] object about this, that first it is said: *Receive ye the Holy Spirit;* and after: *Whose sins you shall remit* etc., that no priest has the power of binding and loosing unless he have the Holy Spirit indwelling: therefore evil priests do not absolve, nor even the supreme Pontiff, if he is evil. And if this, then our salvation is in peril.

To this it is responded in many ways. — For some say that first the Holy Spirit is given, and after, the execution of the power of loosing, because loosing or remission is not done except in the charity of the Church; whence they say that it is not necessary that the priest have charity, but it suffices that he be at least in the Church[^p515-4].

Otherwise it is responded that to loose is twofold, namely *by merit and by office* and *by office only.* For one to loose *by merit,* it is necessary that he have the Holy Spirit; but for one to loose *by office,* it is not needed. That the Lord, then, first gives the Holy Spirit, this is so that he may execute *worthily,* or to signify that without the grace of the Holy Spirit the remission of sins is not given. But it is not needed that that grace be in the priest, but in the Sacraments[^p515-5].

It can also be said otherwise, that the Holy Spirit is said to be given when his gifts are given. There are, then, certain gifts which are *from* the Holy Spirit and *with* the Holy Spirit and never *without,* as *charity.* There are others which are *from* the Holy Spirit and never *with* the Holy Spirit, as *servile fear.* There are others which are both *from* the Holy Spirit and can be *with* the Holy Spirit and *without,* and such are the *characters*[^p515-6]. And because the power of confecting and of binding and of loosing are such, they remain both in the just and in the unjust; and this was done by the divine dispensation, that the sin of the prelate may not prejudice the subject.

54. Quaest. VI. Likewise it is asked about this, that he says that *Thomas was not with the disciples when Jesus came;* because in Luke, the last chapter[^p515-7], it is said of the two disciples going to Emmaus that *they returned into Jerusalem and found the eleven gathered together;* and while they were telling what they had seen, he says that *Jesus stood in the midst of them:* therefore if there were not eleven except with Thomas, it seems that Thomas was there then.

To this Augustine responds in the third book *On the Agreement of the Evangelists*[^p515-8]: « It is to be understood, he says, that Thomas had gone out from there before the Lord appeared to them as they were speaking »; whence it is true that those disciples had found Thomas, but while they persisted in speech, Thomas went out, and the Lord came in.
