# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XII, Vers. 24-36
## *Postquam descripta est vocationis gentium praesignatio, ponitur hic fructus passionis praedictio et hoc quidem per hunc modum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 417 -->

36. *Amen, amen dico vobis.* Postquam descripta est vocationis gentium praesignatio, ponitur hic *fructus passionis praedictio*[^p417-7] et hoc quidem per hunc modum. Primo *praedicitur passionis fructificatio.* Secundo vero, quia passio non habet effectum nisi in imitatoribus Christi, *ponitur imitationis exhortatio,* ibi[^p417-8]: *Qui amat animam suam* etc. Tertio vero, quia nulli imitantur, nisi qui a Domino vocantur; *notatur pro eis facta oratio,* ibi: *Nunc anima mea turbata est.* Quarto, quia ipse impetrat omne, quod petit; *notatur exauditio,* ibi: *Venit ergo vox* etc.

(Vers. 24.). Primo igitur tangitur *passionis fructificatio* exemplo grani; propter quod dicit: *Amen, amen dico vobis;* asserendo loquitur gentilibus, qui eum quaesierunt, ut ipsos in fide confirmet; *nisi granum frumenti cadens in terram mortuum fuerit;*

37. (Vers. 25.). *Ipsum solum manet.* Ad *litteram* non multiplicatur, nisi moriatur; primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p417-9]: « Insipiens, tu quod seminas non vivificatur, nisi prius moriatur ». *Si autem mortuum fuerit, multum fructum affert;* quia tunc producit messem. — Sic intelligendum in Christo, qui est *granum frumenti* propter puritatem et adipis multitudinem, propter quod optimus ex eo fit panis; sic et Christi caro, qui est « panis vivus, qui de caelo descendit », supra sexto[^p417-10]. De hoc dicitur in Psalmo: « Adipe frumenti satiat te ». Hoc granum, *cadens in terram,* per vilitatem, *mortuum est* per passionis acerbitatem; ideo in Psalmo[^p417-11] dicitur: « Omnes fluctus tuos induxisti super me. Longe fecisti notos meos a me, posuerunt me abominationem sibi ». Hoc granum *attulit multum fructum,* quia multos filios adduxit in gloriam; ad Hebraeos secundo[^p417-12]: « Decebat eum qui multos filios adduxit in gloriam, auctorem salutis eorum per passionem
<!-- page 418 -->
consummare »; Isaiae sexagesimo: « Quando conversa fuerit ad te multitudo maris, fortitudo gentium venerit tibi ».

*Qui amat animam suam.* Hic tangitur secundum, scilicet *ad passionem imitandam exhortatio;* et exhortatur, proponens *commodum* et *exemplum.* Propter *commodum et incommodum* dicit: *Qui amat animam suam perdet eam;* et *animam* vocat vitam corporalem, sicut infra decimo tertio[^p418-1] dixit Petrus: « Animam meam ponam pro te ». *Et qui odit animam suam in hoc mundo,* dando scilicet eam pro Christo, *in vitam aeternam custodit eam;* Matthaei decimo sexto[^p418-2]: « Qui voluerit animam suam salvam facere perdet eam, et qui perdiderit eam propter me inveniet eam ». Augustinus: « Si cupis vitam tenere cum Christo, noli mortem timere pro Christo. Felices, qui vitam custodiunt perdendo, ne perdant amando ». Ad hoc idem hortatur *per exemplum:*

38. (Vers. 26.). *Si quis mihi ministrat, me sequatur,* sicut servus Dominum suum, alioquin non est verus servus; *sequatur,* scilicet per passionis vestigia; primae Petri secundo[^p418-3]: « Christus passus est pro nobis, vobis relinquens exemplum, ut sequamini vestigia eius »; ut, sicut imitatur in poena, sic et in gloria. Propter quod subdit: *Ut ubi ego sum, ibi sit et minister meus*[^p418-4]; secundae ad Timotheum secundo: « Fidelis sermo: Si commortui sumus, et convivemus; si compatimur, et conregnabimus »; ideo *fidelis,* quia promissus a Domino; Apocalypsis tertio: « Qui vicerit, dabo illi sedere mecum in throno meo ». Ne aliquis dedignetur sibi ministrare, subdit honoris culmen: *Si quis mihi ministraverit, honorificabit eum Pater meus;* quia ipse honorificavit me; primi Regum secundo[^p418-5]: « Qui honorificaverit me, honorificabo eum; qui autem contemnunt me erunt ignobiles ». *Honorificabit,* inquam, quia de servo faciet filium; ad Romanos octavo: « Ipse Spiritus testimonium reddit spiritui nostro, quod sumus filii Dei; quodsi filii, et heredes; heredes quidem Dei, coheredes autem Christi ».

39. (Vers. 27.). *Nunc anima mea turbata est* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet pro *vocandis exoratio;* et cum Dominus sit inter duo positus, quia caro refugiebat mortem; oportebat, eum pro illa orare; sed quia rursus propter salutem nostram venerat, praefert orationem pro nostra conversione orationi pro sua liberatione, pro qua orat primo; unde dicit: *Nunc anima mea turbata est,* scilicet timore mortis; Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p418-6]: « Coepit turbari et moestus esse ». *Et quid dicam?* Quasi dicat: nunquid pro ipsa petam, ut mortem evadam? Petam quidem, ut infirmitatem ostendam; propter quod dicit: *Pater, salvifica me ex hac hora,* scilicet passionis; Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p418-7]: « Pater, si possibile est, transeat a me calix iste ». Sed hoc non petiit, ut exaudiretur; propter quod subdit: *Sed propterea veni in hanc horam,* ut scilicet moriar, ideo de hoc nolo exaudiri[^p418-8], sed de hoc:

40. (Vers. 28.). *Pater, clarifica nomen tuum;* et hoc est petere, ut nomen Dei notum fiat gentibus per passionem. Orat pro *nobis,* pro quibus etiam se offert; unde ad Hebraeos quinto[^p418-9] de Christo dicitur: « In diebus carnis suae preces supplicationesque ad Deum, qui posset eum salvare a morte, cum clamore valido et lacrymis offerens, exauditus est pro sua reverentia ».

*Venit ergo vox de caelo.* Tangitur hic ultimum, scilicet *exauditio,* in cuius signum dicit: *Vox de caelo venit, dicens*[^p418-10]: *Et clarificavi,* scilicet per opera miraculosa, *et iterum clarificabo,* per opera mirabiliora, sicut sunt nostra redemptio et Christi ad caelos sublimatio. A Patre facta est clarificatio; unde ad Hebraeos quinto[^p418-11]: « Christus non semetipsum clarificavit, ut pontifex fieret, sed qui locutus est ad eum: Filius meus es tu, ego hodie genui te ». Unde haec vox Patri attribuitur, sicut Matthaei tertio in baptismo, Matthaei decimo septimo in transfiguratione et nunc. Unde vox Christo testimonium perhibuit in *adventu* sive incarnatione, in *passione* et in *resurrectione.*

### Quaestiones.

41. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Qui amat animam suam perdet eam.* — Sed omnes amant animam suam, quia dicitur ad Ephesios quinto[^p419-1]: *Nemo unquam carnem suam odio habuit, sed nutrit* etc.: ergo multo minus *animam odit.*

Respondetur, quod *anima* dat intelligere *vitam* secundum triplicem differentiam: et haec uno modo dicitur vita *carnalis,* secundum quod dicitur[^p419-2], quod *animalis homo non percipit ea quae sunt spiritus Dei.* Et haec non est amanda, quia sic importat vitium; unde Ecclesiastici decimo octavo: *Si praestes animae tuae concupiscentias eius, faciet te in gaudium inimicis tuis.* — Alio modo dicitur anima vita *naturalis,* secundum illud infra decimo tertio[^p419-3]: *Animam meam ponam pro te.* — Tertio modo, vita *spiritualis,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p419-4]: *Qui diligit iniquitatem odit animam suam.* — Modo dicitur de vita, secundum quod sonat in *vitium.* — Sed hoc non solvit, quia Dominus hortatur ad mortem: ergo vult, quod homo det se morti, et ita habeat odio vitam *naturalem.* Quod si ita est, videntur mereri qui se interficiunt, ut Christo obediant. — Sed hoc manifeste falsum, quia nulli licet interficere se ipsum, sicut probatur primo de Civitate Dei[^p419-5].

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod intelligit de vita *naturae.* Sed attendendum, quod non est *absolute* accipiendum *amare* nec *odire,* sed *in respectu,* ut scilicet ille dicatur *amare,* qui magis eam diligit quam divinum praeceptum implere; *odire* vero, qui magis implere praeceptum quam servare vitam. Unde non tenetur quis mori vel se occidere nec in mortem se praecipitare debet; unde Augustinus[^p419-6]: « Si cogatur homo alterum eligere, mortem comminante persecutore, potius eligat Deo dilecto mori quam offenso vivere ».

42. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc, quod petit *salvari ex hora mortis.*

1. Ipse sciebat, quod Deus nolebat exaudire: ergo indiscrete petebat.

2. Item, hoc petiit et non fuit exauditus: constat ergo, quod non in omnibus est exauditus. — Contrarium supra undecimo[^p419-7]: *Ego sciebam, quod semper me audis.*

3. *Si dicas,* quod petitio ista fuit *sensualitatis;* *contra:* Sensualitas non se convertit ad Deum Patrem: ergo si fuit, fuit *rationis.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod in Christo duplex fuit voluntas, scilicet *rationis* et *sensualitatis.* Voluntas *rationis* semper conformis est Deo in *volito,* et propterea semper exauditur; sed voluntas *sensualitatis* in *volito* discordat a voluntate Dei, sed non in *modo volendi;* et ideo voluntas *sensualitatis* in Christo non est semper assecuta quod voluit, quia non evenit, nisi quod Deus voluit. — Petitio ergo ista non fuit ex voluntate *rationis* procedens, sed *sensualitatis;* et ideo non est exaudita[^p419-8].

1. Quod ergo obiicitur: ad quid petebat? dicendum, quod sicut Dominus aliqua *praecipit* non ad *implendum,* sed *nos instruendum;* sic aliquid *petiit* non ad *impetrandum,* sed *nos instruendum.* Et instruxit nos in hac petitione, quod licet nobis voluntate *sensualitatis* aliquid proprium velle; et iterum, quod ipsa subdenda est voluntati divinae; et iterum, quod diffidere non debemus, si formidamus mortem.

3. Quod obiicitur, quod *ratio* petiit; dicendum, quod sermo ille formatus est secundum imperium rationis, et ratio proposuit petitionem. Et sicut advocatus aliquando non petit pro se, nec quia illud velit, sed pro alio, et quia alius vult; sic et *ratio* pro *sensualitate* petebat; nec hoc volebat, immo potius mori pro salute nostra[^p419-9].

43. *Turba ergo, quae stabat.* Postquam determinata est passionis futurae fructificatio, subiungitur hic tertium, scilicet *illius explanatio,* in qua procedit Evangelista hoc ordine. Primo innuitur *turbae admiratio;* secundo *a Christo fit explanatio;* tertio, *turbarum dubitatio;* quarto, *dubitationis amotio.*

(Vers. 29.). Tangitur ergo *turbarum admiratio* de auditu vocis illius, quia nesciebant, unde esset; propter quod dicit: *Turba ergo, quae stabat et audiebat*[^p419-10], illam scilicet vocem; *dicebat tonitruum factum esse,* attendentes vocis magnitudinem et sensibilitatem; et hi erant de Sadducaeis, de quibus Matthaei vigesimo secundo et Actuum vigesimo tertio: « Non dicunt esse Angelum neque spiritum » *Alii* vero *dicebant: Angelus ei locutus est,* attendentes
<!-- page 420 -->
vocis articulationem, tamen eius significatum non intelligebant. Isti erant de secta Pharisaeorum, qui dicebant, Angelos esse; unde Actuum vigesimo tertio[^p420-1]: « Quid, si spiritus ei locutus est, aut Angelus »?

44. (Vers. 30.). *Respondit Iesus et dixit.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *illius vocis explanatio,* quia ipsi nesciebant, nec propter quid esset facta nec quid significaret; ideo dicit, quare facta est: *Non propter me haec vox venit, sed propter vos,* quia non ad instructionem meam, sed vestram; ideo dat vocis intellectum:

45. (Vers. 31.). *Nunc iudicium est mundi,* scilicet in passione; *nunc princeps huius mundi eiicietur foras,* scilicet qui creditur esse princeps, quia a multis gentibus adorabatur; potius est daemon pessimus reputatus; Lucae undecimo[^p420-2]: « Cum fortis armatus custodit atrium suum, si fortior illo supervenerit et vicerit eum, universa arma eius auferet, in quibus confidebat, et spolia eius distribuet ». Et ipse vicit mundum cum suo principe; unde infra decimo sexto: « Confidite, ego vici mundum ». Et modum eiiciendi principem dicit esse per passionem; propter quod dicit:

46. (Vers. 32.). *Et ego, si exaltatus fuero a terra,* per passionem crucis; *omnia traham ad me ipsum,* per fidem et dilectionem; Cantici Canticorum primo[^p420-3]: « Trahe me post te »; infra vigesimo primo: « Traxit Simon Petrus rete in terram, plenum magnis piscibus » etc.; supra tertio: « Sicut exaltavit Moyses serpentem in deserto, ita exaltari oportet Filium hominis »; et hoc exponit Evangelista:

47. (Vers. 33.). *Hoc autem dicebat, significans, qua morte esset moriturus;* quoniam morte crucis, quae erat vilissima; Sapientiae secundo[^p420-4]: « Morte turpissima condemnemus eum ».

48. (Vers. 34.). *Respondit ei turba.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *turbarum dubitatio* quantum ad illos qui in Lege instructi erant: quia audierant Christum immortalem, et hic dicebat, se esse Christum, et dicebat, se moriturum. Propter quod dicunt: *Nos audivimus ex Lege, quia Christus manet in aeternum.* — Sed ubi hoc in Lege audierant? Intelligendum, quod Lex hic comprehendit Prophetas; et dicitur Michaeae quinto[^p420-5]: « Egressus eius a diebus aeternitatis » etc.; Danielis septimo: « Potestas eius, potestas aeterna, et regnum illius, quod non corrumpetur »; secundi Regum septimo: « Suscitabo semen tuum post te, et stabiliam thronum regni eius usque in sempiternum ». — *Et quomodo tu dicis: Oportet, exaltari Filium hominis? Quis est iste Filius hominis?* Hoc Christus modo non dixit sub hac forma, sed supra octavo[^p420-6]: « Cum exaltaveritis Filium hominis, tunc *cognoscetis* ». Modo vero dicit: « Et ego, si exaltatus fuero a terra ». Non quaerebant hoc quod audiebant, sed quod prius conceperant.

49. (Vers. 35.). *Dixit ergo eis Iesus.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *dubitationis illius amotio;* et ostendit Dominus, quod illa dubitatio non veniebat de malo intellectu, sed de defectu intelligentiae, quia Scriptura utrumque de Christo dicit, et quod aeternus et quod mortalis. Ideo dicit: *Adhuc modicum lumen in vobis est. Lumen est,* quia creditis Christum *aeternum;* sed *modicum,* quia non creditis *moriturum*[^p420-7]. Ideo hortatur, ut in fide proficiant, dum tempus habent; propter quod dicit: *Ambulate, dum lucem habetis.* Illud *ambulare* est per fidem ad Christum accedere; Psalmus[^p420-8]: « Accedite ad eum, et illuminamini ». Et ratio subditur: *Ut tenebrae vos non comprehendant,* id est excaecatio infidelitatis, de quibus supra tertio: « Lux venit in mundum, et dilexerunt homines magis tenebras quam lucem ». Et periculosum est ab eis comprehendi, quia *qui*[^p420-9] *ambulat in tenebris nescit, quo vadat;* Proverbiorum quarto: « Via impiorum tenebrosa; nesciunt, ubi corruant ». Et explanat, quid est quod dixerat *ambulate,* quod est *credite;* unde dicit:

50. (Vers. 36.). *Dum lucem habetis, credite in lucem, ut filii lucis sitis. Lucis filii* sunt filii Dei, quia ipse « lux vera[^p420-10] »; et hoc fit per fidem; supra primo: « Dedit eis potestatem, filios Dei fieri,
<!-- page 421 -->
his qui credunt in nomine eius ». Optime satisfacit, quia ab illa dubitatione non poterant liberari nisi accedendo ad eum per fidem; unde Augustinus[^p421-1]: « Accedite, totum intelligite, et Christum moriturum et Christum in aeternum victurum et sanguinem effusurum, quo redimat, et ascensurum ad sublimia, quo perducat ». Hoc est *ambulare.*

### Quaestiones.

51. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Nunc iudicium est mundi;* quia dictum est[^p421-2], quod non iudicat quemquam nec venit ad iudicandum.

**Respondeo:** Est iudicium *condemnationis* et *discretionis*[^p421-3]; hoc iudicio iudicatur mundus in passione Christi, per quam discernuntur oves a lupis, non primo iudicio.

52. Quaest. II. Sed quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *princeps huius mundi eiicietur foras.* Quis est iste princeps? — Si diabolus: ergo videtur, quod mundus iste sensibilis pertineat ad dominium diaboli; et tunc vera erit impietas Manichaeorum.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *mundus* hic dicuntur mundo dediti per amicitiam. Horum princeps est diabolus, non quoad *naturam,* sed quantum ad *praeexcellentiam in culpa,* quia *ipse est rex super omnes filios superbiae,* Iob quadragesimo primo[^p421-4].

53. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *eiicietur foras;* quia non videtur; adhuc enim dominatur in his qui mundo dediti sunt, et fortius premit, quia gravius peccant.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod diabolus dicitur *eiici de mundo,* non quin tentet, sed quod non intrinsecus regnet; nec dicitur *eiici,* quod omnino in nullo intrinsecus regnet, sed quod non in tot; nec ita potestative regnat, quia manum *trahentem* habet abscissam, propter hoc quod *chirographum, quod erat adversum nos*[^p421-5], in passione deletum est et affixum cruci. Manus illius *impellens* est debilitata, nostra manus ad resistendum fortificata per fidem; primae Petri ultimo[^p421-6]: *Cui resistite fortes in fide.*

54. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *omnia trahet ad se;* quia non omnes, nec maior pars, conversi sunt ad fidem, immo sunt[^p421-7] plures mali quam boni.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p421-8], qui tendunt ad *non-esse* non debent numerari; et tales sunt mali; unde per hoc quod est *omnia* non fit distributio nisi pro *electis,* qui erant Dei filii per electionem; et hos traxit ad se et *congregavit in unum*[^p421-9]. — Vel: *omnia traham,* id est de omni gente et regno, non tantum Iudaeos, sed etiam gentiles, sine discretione nationum.

## English
<!-- page 417 -->

36. *Amen, amen I say to you.* After the foreshowing of the calling of the Gentiles has been described, there is set down here *the prediction of the fruit of the passion*[^p417-7], and this indeed in this way. First *the fructification of the passion is foretold.* Second, because the passion has no effect except in the imitators of Christ, *the exhortation to imitation is set down,* there[^p417-8]: *He that loveth his soul* etc. Third, because none imitate save those who are called by the Lord, *the prayer made for them is noted,* there: *Now is my soul troubled.* Fourth, because he himself obtains everything that he asks, *the hearing is noted,* there: *A voice therefore came* etc.

(V. 24.). First, then, is touched *the fructification of the passion* by the example of a grain; on account of which he says: *Amen, amen I say to you;* speaking by assertion to the Gentiles who sought him, that he might confirm them in faith; *unless the grain of wheat falling into the ground die;*

37. (V. 25.). *Itself remaineth alone.* To the *letter*, it is not multiplied unless it die; the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth[^p417-9]: « Senseless man, that which thou sowest is not quickened, except it die first ». *But if it die, it bringeth forth much fruit;* because then it produces the harvest. — So it is to be understood in Christ, who is the *grain of wheat* on account of purity and abundance of fatness, on account of which the best bread is made from it; so also the flesh of Christ, who is « the living bread, which came down from heaven », above, the sixth[^p417-10]. Of this it is said in the Psalm: « He filleth thee with the fat of wheat ». This grain, *falling into the ground,* through vileness, *died* through the bitterness of the passion; therefore in the Psalm[^p417-11] it is said: « All thy waves thou hast brought in upon me. Thou hast put away my acquaintance far from me, they have set me an abomination to themselves ». This grain *brought forth much fruit,* because it brought many children into glory; to the Hebrews, the second[^p417-12]: « It became him who brought many children into glory, by his passion
<!-- page 418 -->
to perfect the author of their salvation »; Isaias, the sixtieth: « When the multitude of the sea shall be converted to thee, the strength of the Gentiles shall come to thee ».

*He that loveth his soul.* Here is touched the second, namely, *the exhortation to imitate the passion;* and he exhorts, setting forth *advantage* and *example.* On account of *advantage and disadvantage* he says: *He that loveth his soul shall lose it;* and he calls bodily life *soul*, as below, in the thirteenth[^p418-1], Peter said: « I will lay down my soul for thee ». *And he that hateth his soul in this world,* namely, by giving it for Christ, *keepeth it unto life eternal;* Matthew, the sixteenth[^p418-2]: « Whosoever will save his soul shall lose it, and whosoever shall lose it for my sake shall find it ». Augustine: « If thou desirest to hold life with Christ, do not fear death for Christ. Happy they who keep life by losing, lest they lose by loving ». To this same he exhorts *by example:*

38. (V. 26.). *If any man minister to me, let him follow me,* as a servant his Lord, otherwise he is not a true servant; *let him follow,* namely, through the steps of the passion; the first of Peter, the second[^p418-3]: « Christ suffered for us, leaving you an example, that you should follow his steps »; that, as he imitates in penalty, so also in glory. On account of which he adds: *That where I am, there also may my minister be*[^p418-4]; the second to Timothy, the second: « A faithful saying: If we be dead with him, we shall live also with him; if we suffer with him, we shall also reign with him »; therefore *faithful,* because promised by the Lord; the Apocalypse, the third: « To him that shall overcome, I will give to sit with me in my throne ». Lest anyone disdain to minister to him, he adds the summit of honour: *If any man minister to me, him will my Father honour;* because he himself honoured me; the first of Kings, the second[^p418-5]: « Whosoever shall honour me, him will I honour; but they that despise me shall be ignoble ». *He will honour,* I say, because of a servant he will make a son; to the Romans, the eighth: « The Spirit himself giveth testimony to our spirit, that we are the sons of God; and if sons, heirs also; heirs indeed of God, and joint heirs with Christ ».

39. (V. 27.). *Now is my soul troubled* etc. Here is touched the third, namely, *the entreaty* for *those to be called;* and since the Lord is placed between two things, because the flesh shrank from death; it behoved him to pray for it; but because, again, he had come on account of our salvation, he prefers the prayer for our conversion to the prayer for his own deliverance, for which he prays first; whence he says: *Now is my soul troubled,* namely, by the fear of death; Matthew, the twenty-sixth[^p418-6]: « He began to be troubled and to be sad ». *And what shall I say?* As if to say: shall I ask for it, that I may escape death? I will ask indeed, that I may show infirmity; on account of which he says: *Father, save me from this hour,* namely, of the passion; Matthew, the twenty-sixth[^p418-7]: « Father, if it be possible, let this chalice pass from me ». But he did not ask this that he might be heard; on account of which he adds: *But for this cause I came unto this hour,* namely, that I may die, therefore in this I do not wish to be heard[^p418-8], but in this:

40. (V. 28.). *Father, glorify thy name;* and this is to ask that the name of God may become known to the Gentiles through the passion. He prays for *us,* for whom he also offers himself; whence to the Hebrews, the fifth[^p418-9], it is said of Christ: « In the days of his flesh, offering up prayers and supplications to God, who was able to save him from death, with a strong cry and tears, he was heard for his reverence ».

*A voice therefore came from heaven.* Here is touched the last, namely, *the hearing,* in sign of which he says: *A voice came from heaven, saying*[^p418-10]: *I have both glorified,* namely, through miraculous works, *and will glorify again,* through more wonderful works, such as are our redemption and the raising of Christ to the heavens. The glorification was made by the Father; whence to the Hebrews, the fifth[^p418-11]: « Christ did not glorify himself, that he might be made a high priest, but he that spoke to him: Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee ». Whence this voice is attributed to the Father, as in Matthew, the third, at the baptism, in Matthew, the seventeenth, at the transfiguration, and now. Whence the voice gave testimony to Christ at his *coming* or incarnation, at his *passion* and at his *resurrection.*

<!-- page 419 -->

### Questions.

41. Quaest. I. But it is asked concerning what he says: *He that loveth his soul shall lose it.* — But all love their soul, because it is said to the Ephesians, the fifth[^p419-1]: *No man ever hated his own flesh, but nourisheth it* etc.: therefore much less *does he hate his soul.*

It is responded that *soul* gives one to understand *life* according to a threefold difference: and in one way this is called *carnal* life, according to that which is said[^p419-2], that *the animal man perceiveth not those things that are of the Spirit of God.* And this is not to be loved, because so it imports vice; whence Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth: *If thou give to thy soul her concupiscences, she will make thee a joy to thy enemies.* — In another way soul is called *natural* life, according to that below, in the thirteenth[^p419-3]: *I will lay down my soul for thee.* — In the third way, *spiritual* life, according to that of the Psalm[^p419-4]: *He that loveth iniquity hateth his own soul.* — Now it is said of life according as it sounds in *vice.* — But this does not solve it, because the Lord exhorts to death: therefore he wills that a man give himself to death, and so hold *natural* life in hatred. And if this is so, those seem to merit who kill themselves that they may obey Christ. — But this is manifestly false, because it is lawful for no one to kill himself, as is proved in the first *On the City of God*[^p419-5].

**I respond:** It must be said that he understands it of the life *of nature.* But it must be attended to that *to love* and *to hate* are not to be taken *absolutely,* but *in respect,* so that, namely, he is said *to love* it who loves it more than to fulfil the divine precept; and *to hate,* he who would more fulfil the precept than keep life. Whence no one is bound to die or to kill himself, nor ought he to cast himself headlong into death; whence Augustine[^p419-6]: « If a man be compelled to choose one of the two, the persecutor threatening death, let him rather choose to die with God loved than to live with God offended ».

42. Quaest. II. Again, it is asked concerning this, that he asks *to be saved from the hour of death.*

1. He himself knew that God did not will to hear: therefore he asked indiscreetly.

2. Again, he asked this and was not heard: it is established, therefore, that he was not heard in all things. — The contrary, above, the eleventh[^p419-7]: *I knew that thou hearest me always.*

3. *If thou say* that this petition was of *sensuality;* *on the contrary:* Sensuality does not turn itself to God the Father: therefore if it was, it was of *reason.*

**I respond:** It must be said that in Christ there was a twofold will, namely, of *reason* and of *sensuality.* The will of *reason* is always conformed to God in *the thing willed,* and on that account is always heard; but the will of *sensuality* is at discord with the will of God in *the thing willed,* but not in *the mode of willing;* and therefore the will of *sensuality* in Christ did not always attain what it willed, because nothing comes about save what God has willed. — This petition, therefore, did not proceed from the will of *reason,* but of *sensuality;* and therefore it was not heard[^p419-8].

1. To what is objected, then: to what end did he ask? it must be said that as the Lord *commands* some things not *to be fulfilled,* but *to instruct us;* so he *asked* something not *to obtain it,* but *to instruct us.* And he instructed us in this petition that it is lawful for us by the will of *sensuality* to will something of our own; and again, that it is to be subjected to the divine will; and again, that we ought not to despair if we dread death.

3. To what is objected, that *reason* asked; it must be said that that speech was formed according to the command of reason, and reason put forward the petition. And as an advocate sometimes asks not for himself, nor because he wills that thing, but for another, and because the other wills it; so also *reason* asked for *sensuality;* nor did it will this, but rather to die for our salvation[^p419-9].

43. *The multitude therefore that stood.* After the fructification of the future passion has been determined, there is subjoined here the third, namely, *the explanation of it,* in which the Evangelist proceeds in this order. First is hinted *the wonder of the multitude;* second, *the explanation is given by Christ;* third, *the doubt of the multitudes;* fourth, *the removal of the doubt.*

(V. 29.). There is touched, then, *the wonder of the multitudes* at the hearing of that voice, because they did not know whence it was; on account of which he says: *The multitude therefore that stood and heard*[^p419-10], that voice, namely; *said that it thundered,* attending to the magnitude and sensibility of the voice; and these were of the Sadducees, of whom Matthew, the twenty-second, and Acts, the twenty-third: « They do not say that there is an Angel nor spirit » *Others* however *said: An Angel spoke to him,* attending
<!-- page 420 -->
to the articulation of the voice, yet they did not understand what it signified. These were of the sect of the Pharisees, who said that there are Angels; whence Acts, the twenty-third[^p420-1]: « What if a spirit hath spoken to him, or an Angel »?

44. (V. 30.). *Jesus answered, and said.* Here is touched the second, namely, *the explanation of that voice,* because they did not know either on account of what it had been made or what it signified; therefore he says why it was made: *This voice came not because of me, but for your sakes,* because not for my instruction, but yours; therefore he gives the understanding of the voice:

45. (V. 31.). *Now is the judgement of the world,* namely, in the passion; *now shall the prince of this world be cast out,* namely, he who is believed to be prince, because he was adored by many Gentiles; rather he is reputed the worst demon; Luke, the eleventh[^p420-2]: « When a strong man armed keepeth his court, if a stronger than he come upon him and overcome him, he will take away all his armour wherein he trusted, and will distribute his spoils ». And he himself overcame the world with its prince; whence below, in the sixteenth: « Have confidence, I have overcome the world ». And he says that the mode of casting out the prince is through the passion; on account of which he says:

46. (V. 32.). *And I, if I be lifted up from the earth,* through the passion of the cross; *will draw all things to myself,* through faith and dilection; the Canticle of Canticles, the first[^p420-3]: « Draw me after thee »; below, in the twenty-first: « Simon Peter drew the net to land, full of great fishes » etc.; above, in the third: « As Moses lifted up the serpent in the desert, so must the Son of man be lifted up »; and this the Evangelist expounds:

47. (V. 33.). *Now this he said, signifying what death he should die;* since by the death of the cross, which was the vilest; Wisdom, the second[^p420-4]: « Let us condemn him to a most shameful death ».

48. (V. 34.). *The multitude answered him.* Here is touched the third, namely, *the doubt of the multitudes,* as regards those who were instructed in the Law: because they had heard that Christ was immortal, and here he was saying that he was the Christ, and was saying that he would die. On account of which they say: *We have heard out of the Law, that Christ abideth for ever.* — But where had they heard this in the Law? It must be understood that the Law here comprehends the Prophets; and it is said in Micheas, the fifth[^p420-5]: « His going forth is from the days of eternity » etc.; Daniel, the seventh: « His power is an everlasting power, and his kingdom, that shall not be destroyed »; the second of Kings, the seventh: « I will raise up thy seed after thee, and I will establish the throne of his kingdom for ever ». — *And how sayest thou: The Son of man must be lifted up? Who is this Son of man?* Christ did not say this now under this form, but above, in the eighth[^p420-6]: « When you shall have lifted up the Son of man, then shall you *know* ». But now he says: « And I, if I be lifted up from the earth ». They were not inquiring into what they were hearing, but into what they had conceived before.

49. (V. 35.). *Jesus therefore said to them.* Here is touched the fourth, namely, *the removal of that doubt;* and the Lord shows that that doubt did not come from a bad understanding, but from a defect of intelligence, because Scripture says both of Christ, both that he is eternal and that he is mortal. Therefore he says: *Yet a little lumen*[^tr-lumen] *is in you. There is lumen,* because you believe Christ *eternal;* but *a little,* because you do not believe *that he will die*[^p420-7]. Therefore he exhorts them to prevail in faith, while they have time; on account of which he says: *Walk whilst you have the light.* That *walking* is to approach Christ through faith; the Psalm[^p420-8]: « Come ye to him, and be enlightened ». And the reason is added: *That the darkness overtake you not,* that is, the blinding of unbelief, of which above, in the third: « The light is come into the world, and men loved darkness rather than the light ». And it is perilous to be overtaken by it, because *he that*[^p420-9] *walketh in darkness knoweth not whither he goeth;* Proverbs, the fourth: « The way of the wicked is darksome; they know not where they fall ». And he explains what it is that he had said, *walk,* which is *believe;* whence he says:

50. (V. 36.). *Whilst you have the light, believe in the light, that you may be the sons of light. The sons of light* are the sons of God, because he is « the true light[^p420-10] »; and this comes about through faith; above, in the first: « He gave them power to be made the sons of God,
<!-- page 421 -->
to them that believe in his name ». He satisfies excellently, because they could not be freed from that doubt except by approaching him through faith; whence Augustine[^p421-1]: « Approach, understand the whole, both that Christ will die and that Christ will live for ever, both that he will shed the blood by which he may redeem, and that he will ascend to the heights to which he may lead ». This is *to walk.*

### Questions.

51. Quaest. I. But it is asked concerning what he says: *Now is the judgement of the world;* because it was said[^p421-2] that he judges no one and did not come to judge.

**I respond:** There is a judgement of *condemnation* and of *discernment*[^p421-3]; by this judgement the world is judged in the passion of Christ, through which the sheep are discerned from the wolves, not by the first judgement.

52. Quaest. II. But it is asked concerning what he says, that *the prince of this world shall be cast out.* Who is this prince? — If the devil: therefore it seems that this sensible world pertains to the dominion of the devil; and then the impiety of the Manichees will be true.

**I respond:** It must be said that *the world* here means those given to the world through friendship. Of these the devil is prince, not as to *nature,* but as to *pre-eminence in fault,* because *he is king over all the children of pride,* Job, the forty-first[^p421-4].

53. Quaest. III. Again, it is asked concerning what he says, that he *shall be cast out;* because it does not seem so; for he still has dominion in those who are given to the world, and presses harder, because they sin more gravely.

**I respond:** It must be said that the devil is said *to be cast out of the world,* not that he does not tempt, but that he does not reign inwardly; nor is he said *to be cast out* because he reigns inwardly in no one at all, but because not in so many; nor does he reign so powerfully, because he has the *drawing* hand cut off, on account of this, that *the handwriting, which was against us*[^p421-5], was blotted out in the passion and fastened to the cross. His *impelling* hand has been weakened, our hand fortified for resisting through faith; the first of Peter, the last[^p421-6]: *Whom resist ye, strong in faith.*

54. Quaest. IV. Again, it is asked concerning what he says, that *he will draw all things to himself;* because not all, nor the greater part, have been converted to the faith, but there are[^p421-7] more bad than good.

**I respond:** It must be said that, as Augustine says[^p421-8], those who tend to *non-being* ought not to be counted; and such are the bad; whence by this that is *all things* a distribution is not made save for *the elect,* who were sons of God by election; and these he drew to himself and *gathered together in one*[^p421-9]. — Or: *I will draw all things,* that is, from every nation and kingdom, not only the Jews, but also the Gentiles, without discernment of nations.
