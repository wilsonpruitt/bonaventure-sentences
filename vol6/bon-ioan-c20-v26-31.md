# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XX, Vers. 26-31
## *De secunda manifestatione notantur duo*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 515 -->

#### De secunda manifestatione notantur duo.

55. *Et post dies octo.* Hic agitur de *secunda manifestatione* Domini, facta ad removendum incredulitatem Thomae[^p515-9]; et duo in hac parte notantur primo *manifestatio;* secundo, ex manifestatione consequens *fidei aedificatio,* ibi: *Respondit Thomas.*

In *manifestatione* plura nobis insinuantur, scilicet *discipulorum congregatio, Domini apparitio, eiusdem allocutio* et *sui ad se palpandum exhibitio* sive ad Thomam perfecta ostensio.

(Vers. 26.). Discipulorum *congregatio* tangitur, cum dicitur: *Post dies octo,* scilicet post resurre-
<!-- page 516 -->
ctionem et primam apparitionem; *iterum erant discipuli eius intus,* in eadem domo quieti a strepitu; *et Thomas cum eis;* et sic, quia simul erant et intus erant, ad videndum Dominum habiles erant, secundum illud Matthaei decimo octavo[^p516-1]: « Ubi sunt duo vel tres congregati in nomine meo, ibi sum in medio eorum ».

56. *Venit Iesus, ianuis clausis, et stetit in medio.* Hic tangitur secundum, scilicet *Domini apparitio;* unde veniens stetit in medio, ut nullus sit, qui non videat.

*Et dixit eis*[^p516-2]: *Pax vobis.* Hic notatur tertium, scilicet *Domini allocutio.* Iste modus alloquendi ipsum manifestabat, quia sic salutare consueverat, pacem optando, et suos discipulos sic docuerat salutare; Matthaei decimo[^p516-3]: « In quamcumque domum intraveritis, primum dicite: Pax huic domui ».

57. (Vers. 27.). *Deinde dicit Thomae.* Hic notatur quartum, scilicet *sui ad se palpandum exhibitio.* Quia enim Thomas dixerat[^p516-4]: « Nisi mittam digitum meum in locum clavorum » etc.; ideo Dominus dicit ei: *Infer digitum tuum huc et vide manus meas.* Quia etiam dixerat: « Nisi mittam manum meam in latus eius »; ideo Dominus dicit ei: *Et affer manum tuam et mitte in latus meum.* Et quia etiam dixerat: Nisi hoc fecero, « non credam »; ideo Dominus dicit: *Et noli esse incredulus, sed fidelis;* ex quo per omnia satisfeci tuae voluntati. Unde Gregorius[^p516-5]: « Divina dispensatione gestum est, ut electus discipulus tunc deesset, quando Dominus aliis apparuit; post autem veniens audiret, audiens dubitaret, dubitans palparet, palpans crederet ». Habacuc secundo[^p516-6]: « Qui autem incredulus est, non erit anima eius recta in semetipso; iustus autem meus ex fide vivit ».

58. *Respondit Thomas.* Hic notatur *fidei aedificatio* ex Domini manifestatione procedens. Tripliciter autem astruitur nostra fides ex divina manifestatione: aut per *praesentiam* sive per visum, aut per *absentiam* sive per auditum, aut per *Scripturae testimonium.* Primo ergo modo aedificata est fides in Thoma, qui credebat; secundo modo, in aliis, qui referentibus crediderant; tertio modo, in his qui Scripturis credituri erant.

Primo ergo notatur fides *Thomae credentis per visum;* secundo commendatur fides aliorum *credentium per auditum;* tertio, illorum qui *credituri erant per Scripturae testimonium.*

59. (Vers. 28.). Tangitur ergo fides Thomae, quae innuitur in recta eius confessione; unde dicit: *Respondit Thomas et dixit*[^p516-7], post tantam ostensionem: *Dominus meus,* quantum ad humanitatem, *et Deus meus,* quantum ad Divinitatem; ideo enim *Deus,* quia creavit; ideo *Dominus,* quia nos comparavit ut servos; primae Petri primo[^p516-8]: « Non corruptibilibus auro vel argento redempti estis, sed pretioso sanguine, quasi agni immaculati et incontaminati Christi Iesu ».

60. (Vers. 29.). *Dicit*[^p516-9] *ei Iesus.* Hic commendatur fides eorum qui *aedificati sunt per auditum,* et praefertur fidei Thomae, quae fuit per praesentiam et visum; et ideo dicit: *Quia vidisti me, Thoma, credidisti; beati qui non viderunt et crediderunt;* quia maioris fuit libertatis et plus habuit de suo talis fides. Chrysostomus[^p516-10]: « Cum quis dicit: nunc vellem esse secundum tempora illa et videre Christum mirificantem; excogitet, quoniam *beati qui non viderunt et crediderunt* »; primae Petri primo: « Quem cum non videritis, diligitis; in quem nunc quoque non videntes creditis ».

61. (Vers. 30.). *Multa quidem et alia signa.* Hic tangitur fides *credentium per Scripturam* talium manifestationum[^p516-11]; ideo dicit, huiusmodi manifestationes conscriptas esse ad astruendam fidem eorum qui eas videre non potuerunt. Et ideo non omnia scripta sunt, sed quae sufficiunt ad fidem nostram; ideoque dicit: *Multa quidem et alia signa fecit Iesus in conspectu discipulorum*[^p516-12], *quae non sunt scripta in libro hoc;* quia non est nostrum omnia narrare, sed fidem aedificare; unde subdit:

62. (Vers. 31.). *Haec autem scripta sunt, ut credatis;* ad Romanos decimo quinto[^p516-13]: « Quaecumque scripta sunt, ad nostram doctrinam scripta sunt ». *Credatis,* inquam, *quia Iesus est Filius Dei,* id est in Christo humanitatem et Divinitatem; Lucae primo[^p516-14]: « Vocabis nomen eius Iesum. Hic erit magnus et Filius Altissimi vocabitur »; primae Ioannis quinto: « Quis est autem, qui vincit
<!-- page 517 -->
mundum, nisi qui credit, quoniam Iesus est Filius Dei »? Et quia non est status in fide, ideo finem istum ordinat ad ultimum: *Et ut credentes vitam habeatis,* quia, sicut dicitur ad Hebraeos undecimo[^p517-1], « sine fide impossibile est placere Deo », sed credentes *vitam* possident; primae Petri primo: « Credentes exsultabitis laetitia inenarrabili et glorificata. reportantes finem fidei vestrae salutem animarum vestrarum ». *In nomine eius,* id est in fide nominis eius; Actuum quarto[^p517-2]: « Non est aliud nomen sub caelo datum hominibus, in quo oporteat nos salvos fieri »; primae Ioannis ultimo: « Haec scribo vobis, ut sciatis, quoniam vitam aeternam habetis, qui creditis in nomine Filii Dei ».

### Quaestiones.

63. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic de hoc quod dicit: *Venit Iesus, ianuis clausis.* — Si enim ad hoc veniebat, ut per tactum Thomae cognosceretur; videtur, quod in ipso modo veniendi fidem, quam astruebat, infirmaret; quia, sicut spiritus non potest tangi, sic nec quod potest tangi clausis potest ianuis introire; et ideo videri poterat totum phantasticum[^p517-3].

Respondent quidam, quod Christus debuit ostendere non solum, se habere corpus, in quo passus est, sed etiam corpus *divinum,* id est Divinitati unitum. Et ut ostenderet, esse divinum, clausis venit ianuis. Unde Augustinus[^p517-4]: « Moli corporis, ubi Divinitas erat, ostia clausa non obstiterunt. Ille quippe, non eis apertis, intrare potuit, quo nascente, virginitas Matris inviolata permansit ».

Alii vero respondent, quod per hoc non ostendit, se corpus habere divinum, sed *gloriosum;* quia hoc non erat suum, sed omnium corporum glorificatorum. Et verbum Augustini intelligendum est per locum *a minori;* et ita exponit illud Gregorius[^p517-5]: « Quid mirum, si, ianuis clausis, post resurrectionem suam intravit, qui antea veniens, non aperto Virginis utero, exiit »? Et ita per locum *a minori* arguit.

Respondendum ergo ad rationem, quod sic debebat probare idem esse corpus, ut etiam ostenderet gloriosum.

64. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Infer digitum tuum huc et mitte manum in latus.*

1. Ex hoc videtur, quod corpus Christi esset vulneratum; sed si hoc, non erat gloriosum.

2. Item, si dicas, quod cicatrices remanserunt; videtur, quod non plenam gloriam habeat, quia perfectius curatur, in quo nulla remanent imperfectionis vestigia: ergo videtur, quod corpus Christi non sit perfecte glorificatum[^p517-6]. — *Si dicas,* quod remanserant propter discipulos tantum; tunc videtur, quod non verae, sed falsae cicatrices essent.

Respondet Augustinus in libro de Sex Solutionibus contra paganos[^p517-7]: « Sciant, inquit, qui has quaestiones proposuerunt, Christum non vulnera, sed cicatrices discipulis demonstrasse, quae tunc falsae essent, si nulla vulnera praecessissent; nec ex impotentia sunt, qui divina dispensatione et arte optimi medici sunt relictae ». Exemplum ponit Augustinus de milite vulnerato, qui non vult sanari a medico, quin signa vulnerum remaneant.

Ratio autem, quare servatae sunt, triplex fuit, sicut dicit Victor[^p517-8]: « Sic, inquiens, cicatrices vulnerum non ex impotentia curandi servavit, sed ut in perpetuum victoriae suae circumferat triumphum; et ad fidem resurrectionis astruendam, ut idem corpus, quod moritur, resurgere demonstraret; et ut sua morte redemptis, quam misericorditer sint adiuti, propositis et ostensis eiusdem mortis innuat indi-
<!-- page 518 -->
ciis ». Posset quarta ratio reddi: ad confusionem damnandorum; unde Apocalypsis primo[^p518-1]: *Videbit eum omnis oculus, et qui eum pupugerunt.*

65. Quaest. III. Item, de hoc quod dicit: *Et mitte manum tuam in latus meum* etc. — Ergo corpus Christi erat palpabile; sed, sicut dicit Gregorius[^p518-2], « omne palpabile est corruptibile »: ergo corpus illud erat corruptibile. — *Si dicas,* sicut dicit Chrysostomus, quod « hoc erat condescensionis quod fiebat »; *tunc obiicitur:* quia, si *palpari* est contra naturam corporis glorificati, ergo manifestum, quod in hoc non ostendebat, se habere corpus *glorificatum,* sed potius *non glorificatum.*

Et respondetur, quod ista ratio non valet, quia intrando, ianuis clausis, ostendit *gloriosum,* sed per tactum ostendit esse *idem,* quod fuerat passum.

Potest tamen aliter dici, quod, sicut dupliciter aliquid est *visibile:* uno modo, quia visum latere non potest, ut lapis; alio modo, quia, cum latere possit, potest etiam immutare[^p518-3] — primum est quodam modo *infirmitatis* et est in corpore non glorioso, secundum *virtutis* et est in corpore glorioso — sic *tangi,* quia tactum non potest refugere, est *infirmitatis* quodam modo et corporis non gloriosi; tangi vero, quia terminat impulsum manus ut solidum, quod quidem potest sua virtute manum penetrare, hoc *magnae virtutis.* Et hoc fuit in corpore glorioso, et maxime in corpore Christi[^p518-4].

66. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Noli esse incredulus, sed fidelis.*

Opponit Gregorius[^p518-5]: « Cum Paulus dicat ad Hebraeos undecimo: *Fides est argumentum non apparentium;* fides est illarum rerum, quae apparere non possunt; quae enim apparent fidem non habent, sed agnitionem: ergo si Thomas vidit Dominum, non iam habuit fidem, sed scientiam ».

Respondet Gregorius[^p518-6] et Augustinus, quod « aliud *vidit,* aliud *credidit; vidit* hominem et Deum confessus est; unde dicit: *Dominus meus et Deus meus* ».

67. Sed tunc *obiicitur:* 1. Quia saltem vidit eum quem prius viderat mortuum: ergo *sciebat* resurrectionem, non credebat.

2. Item, Thomas non credebat, nisi quia videbat: ergo « humana ratio fidei suae praebebat experimentum »: ergo « fides sua non habebat meritum[^p518-7] ».

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod quia Apostoli sciebant ludificationes daemonum, poterant aestimare totum illud, quod faciebat Christus, esse a spiritu aliquo in assumto corpore; et inde erat, quod etiam aliqui dubitabant[^p518-8]. Et propterea dico, quod per visionem illam non fuit certa cognitio, nec habuerant per illas ostensiones rationes *cogentes,* quae auferrent fidei meritum, sed solum *inducentes,* quae adiuvant animum infirmum et nondum in fide stabilitum[^p518-9].

68. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Quia vidisti me, Thoma, credidisti.* — Videtur, quod debuisset dicere: quia *tetigisti* me, quia ei non suffecerat, quod videret.

Ad hoc respondet Augustinus[^p518-10] dupliciter: vel quia vere *visus* intelligitur tactus, quia *visus* dicitur communiter sensus; vel quia Thomas tangere non fuit ausus; unde dicit: « Non ait *tetigisti,* sed *vidisti,* quoniam generalis quodam modo est sensus visus; quamvis dici possit, non ausum fuisse discipulum tangere[^p518-11] ».

## English

#### On the second manifestation two things are noted.

55. *And after eight days.* Here he treats of the *second manifestation* of the Lord, made for removing the incredulity of Thomas[^p515-9]; and two things are noted in this part: first the *manifestation;* second, the *building-up of faith* following from the manifestation, there: *Thomas answered.*

In the *manifestation* several things are intimated to us, namely the *gathering of the disciples, the appearing of the Lord, his address* and *the offering of himself to be felt,* or the perfect showing to Thomas.

(Vers. 26.). The *gathering* of the disciples is touched when it is said: *After eight days,* namely after the resur-
<!-- page 516 -->
rection and the first appearing; *again his disciples were within,* in the same house, quiet from the din; *and Thomas with them;* and so, because they were together and were within, they were apt for seeing the Lord, according to that of Matthew, the eighteenth chapter[^p516-1]: « Where there are two or three gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them ».

56. *Jesus cometh, the doors being shut, and stood in the midst.* Here is touched the second, namely the *appearing of the Lord;* whence coming he stood in the midst, that there might be no one who does not see.

*And said to them*[^p516-2]: *Peace be to you.* Here is noted the third, namely the *address of the Lord.* This manner of addressing manifested him, because he had been accustomed to greet thus, wishing peace, and had taught his disciples to greet thus; Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p516-3]: « Into whatsoever house you shall enter, first say: Peace be to this house ».

57. (Vers. 27.). *Then he saith to Thomas.* Here is noted the fourth, namely *the offering of himself to be felt.* For because Thomas had said[^p516-4]: « Except I put my finger into the place of the nails » etc.; therefore the Lord says to him: *Put in thy finger hither, and see my hands.* Because he had also said: « Except I put my hand into his side »; therefore the Lord says to him: *And bring hither thy hand, and put it into my side.* And because he had also said: Unless I do this, « I will not believe »; therefore the Lord says: *And be not incredulous, but faithful;* since in all things I have satisfied thy will. Whence Gregory[^p516-5]: « It was done by the divine dispensation that the chosen disciple should then be absent, when the Lord appeared to the others; but coming afterwards should hear, hearing should doubt, doubting should feel, feeling should believe ». Habacuc, the second chapter[^p516-6]: « But he that is incredulous, his soul shall not be right in himself; but my just man liveth by faith ».

58. *Thomas answered.* Here is noted the *building-up of faith* proceeding from the manifestation of the Lord. But our faith is established from the divine manifestation in three ways: either through *presence* or through sight, or through *absence* or through hearing, or through *the testimony of Scripture.* In the first way, then, faith was built up in Thomas, who believed; in the second way, in the others, who had believed those who reported; in the third way, in those who were to believe the Scriptures.

First, then, is noted the faith *of Thomas believing through sight;* second is commended the faith of the others *believing through hearing;* third, of those who *were to believe through the testimony of Scripture.*

59. (Vers. 28.). The faith of Thomas, then, is touched, which is intimated in his right confession; whence he says: *Thomas answered, and said*[^p516-7], after so great a showing: *My Lord,* as to the humanity, *and my God,* as to the Divinity; for he is *God* because he created; he is *Lord* because he purchased us as servants; the first of Peter, the first chapter[^p516-8]: « Not with corruptible things, gold or silver, were you redeemed, but with the precious blood, as of a lamb unspotted and undefiled, Christ Jesus ».

60. (Vers. 29.). *Jesus saith*[^p516-9] *to him.* Here is commended the faith of those who *were built up through hearing,* and it is preferred to the faith of Thomas, which was through presence and sight; and therefore he says: *Because thou hast seen me, Thomas, thou hast believed; blessed are they that have not seen, and have believed;* because such faith was of greater liberty and had more of its own. Chrysostom[^p516-10]: « When anyone says: now I would wish to be in those times and to see Christ working wonders; let him consider that *blessed are they that have not seen, and have believed* »; the first of Peter, the first chapter: « Whom though you have not seen, you love; in whom now also, not seeing, you believe ».

61. (Vers. 30.). *Many other signs also.* Here is touched the faith *of those believing through the Scripture* of such manifestations[^p516-11]; therefore he says that manifestations of this kind were written down for establishing the faith of those who could not see them. And therefore not all things were written, but those which suffice for our faith; and so he says: *Many other signs also did Jesus in the sight of his disciples*[^p516-12], *which are not written in this book;* because it is not ours to narrate all things, but to build up faith; whence he subjoins:

62. (Vers. 31.). *But these are written, that you may believe;* to the Romans, the fifteenth chapter[^p516-13]: « Whatsoever things were written, were written for our learning[^tr-doctrina] ». *That you may believe,* I say, *that Jesus is the Son of God,* that is, in Christ humanity and Divinity; Luke, the first chapter[^p516-14]: « Thou shalt call his name Jesus. He shall be great, and shall be called the Son of the Most High »; the first of John, the fifth chapter: « Who is he that overcometh
<!-- page 517 -->
the world, but he that believeth that Jesus is the Son of God »? And because there is no stopping-place in faith, therefore he orders this end to the last: *And that believing you may have life,* because, as is said to the Hebrews, the eleventh chapter[^p517-1], « without faith it is impossible to please God », but believers possess *life;* the first of Peter, the first chapter: « Believing, you shall rejoice with joy unspeakable and glorified, receiving the end of your faith, the salvation of your souls ». *In his name,* that is, in faith in his name; Acts, the fourth chapter[^p517-2]: « There is no other name under heaven given to men, whereby we must be saved »; the first of John, the last chapter: « These things I write to you, that you may know that you have eternal life, you who believe in the name of the Son of God ».

### Questions.

63. Quaest. I. But it is asked here about this, that he says: *Jesus cometh, the doors being shut.* — For if he came for this, that he might be known through the touch of Thomas; it seems that in the very manner of coming he weakened the faith which he was establishing; because, as a spirit cannot be touched, so neither can that which can be touched enter with the doors shut; and therefore the whole could seem phantasmal[^p517-3].

Some respond that Christ ought to show not only that he had a body in which he suffered, but also a *divine* body, that is, one united to the Divinity. And to show that it was divine, he came with the doors shut. Whence Augustine[^p517-4]: « The doors being shut did not stand in the way of the mass of the body where the Divinity was. For he could enter, they not being opened, at whose birth the virginity of his Mother remained inviolate ».

But others respond that by this he did not show that he had a divine body, but a *glorious* one; because this was not his own, but belongs to all glorified bodies. And the word of Augustine is to be understood by the topic *from the lesser;* and so Gregory expounds it[^p517-5]: « What wonder if, the doors being shut, he entered after his resurrection, who before, coming, went forth with the Virgin's womb not opened »? And so he argues by the topic *from the lesser.*

It must be responded, then, to the argument, that he ought so to prove the body to be the same as also to show it glorious.

64. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *Put in thy finger hither, and put thy hand into my side.*

1. From this it seems that the body of Christ was wounded; but if this, it was not glorious.

2. Likewise, if you say that scars remained; it seems that it does not have full glory, because that is more perfectly healed in which no traces of imperfection remain: therefore it seems that the body of Christ is not perfectly glorified[^p517-6]. — *If you say* that they had remained on account of the disciples only; then it seems that they were not true, but false scars.

Augustine responds in the book *On the Six Solutions against the Pagans*[^p517-7]: « Let them know, he says, who proposed these questions, that Christ showed to the disciples not wounds but scars, which would then be false if no wounds had gone before; nor are they from impotence, which were left by the divine dispensation and the art of the best physician ». Augustine sets down the example of a wounded soldier, who does not wish to be healed by the physician without the marks of the wounds remaining.

But the reason why they were kept was threefold, as Victor says[^p517-8]: « Thus, he says, he kept the scars of the wounds not from impotence of healing, but that he might carry about for ever the triumph of his victory; and for establishing faith in the resurrection, that he might show the same body which dies to rise again; and that to those redeemed by his death he might intimate how mercifully they have been helped, by the tokens of that same death set forth
<!-- page 518 -->
and shown ». A fourth reason could be given: for the confusion of the damned; whence the Apocalypse, the first chapter[^p518-1]: *Every eye shall see him, and they also that pierced him.*

65. Quaest. III. Likewise, about this, that he says: *And put thy hand into my side* etc. — Therefore the body of Christ was palpable; but, as Gregory says[^p518-2], « everything palpable is corruptible »: therefore that body was corruptible. — *If you say,* as Chrysostom says, that « what was done was of condescension »; *then it is objected:* because, if *to be felt* is against the nature of a glorified body, then it is manifest that in this he did not show that he had a *glorified* body, but rather a *not glorified* one.

And it is responded that this argument does not hold, because by entering with the doors shut he showed it *glorious,* but through touch he showed it to be *the same* which had suffered.

It can nevertheless be said otherwise, that, as something is *visible* in two ways: in one way, because it cannot be hidden from sight, as a stone; in another way, because, though it can be hidden, it can also change the sight[^p518-3] — the first is in a manner *of infirmity* and is in a body not glorious, the second *of power* and is in a glorious body — so *to be touched,* because it cannot flee from touch, is in a manner *of infirmity* and of a body not glorious; but to be touched because it terminates the impulse of the hand as a solid, which nevertheless can by its own power penetrate the hand, this is *of great power.* And this was in the glorious body, and most of all in the body of Christ[^p518-4].

66. Quaest. IV. Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *Be not incredulous, but faithful.*

Gregory objects[^p518-5]: « Since Paul says to the Hebrews, the eleventh chapter: *Faith is the evidence of things that appear not;* faith is of those things which cannot appear; for the things which appear have not faith, but recognition: therefore if Thomas saw the Lord, he had no longer faith, but knowledge ».

Gregory[^p518-6] and Augustine respond that « he *saw* one thing, he *believed* another; he *saw* a man and confessed God; whence he says: *My Lord and my God* ».

67. But then *it is objected:* 1. Because at least he saw him whom he had before seen dead: therefore he *knew* the resurrection, he did not believe it.

2. Likewise, Thomas did not believe except because he saw: therefore « human reason furnished experiment to his faith »: therefore « his faith did not have merit[^p518-7] ».

**I respond:** It must be said that, because the Apostles knew the illusions of demons, they could reckon that all that which Christ was doing was from some spirit in an assumed body; and hence it was that some also doubted[^p518-8]. And for this reason I say that through that vision there was not certain cognition, nor had they through those showings *compelling* reasons, which would take away the merit of faith, but only *inducing* ones, which help a mind infirm and not yet established in faith[^p518-9].

68. Quaest. V. Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *Because thou hast seen me, Thomas, thou hast believed.* — It seems that he ought to have said: because thou hast *touched* me, because it had not sufficed him to see.

To this Augustine responds[^p518-10] in two ways: either because by *sight* touch is truly understood, because *sight* is said of sense in common; or because Thomas did not dare to touch; whence he says: « He says not *thou hast touched,* but *thou hast seen,* because sight is in a certain manner the general sense; although it could be said that the disciple did not dare to touch[^p518-11] ».
