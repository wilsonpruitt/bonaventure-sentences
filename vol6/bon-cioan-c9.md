# Collationes in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Capitulum IX
## *Et praeteriens Iesus · Collationes XXXVII–XXXVIII*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 578 -->

### Capitulum IX. *Et praeteriens Iesus.*

### Collatio XXXVII.

(Vers. 1.). *Et praeteriens Iesus vidit hominem caecum a nativitate.*

1. Nota, quod est multiplex *caecitas.* Est enim caecitas *naturae,* caecitas *ignorantiae,* caecitas *poenae* et caecitas *culpae.* — Caecitas *naturae* est *miseranda;* de qua hic: *Praeteriens Iesus vidit hominem caecum a nativitate;* et sequitur infra[^p578-6], qualiter misertus est eius; Deuteronomii vigesimo septimo: « Maledictus, qui errare fecit caecum in itinere ». — Caecitas *ignorantiae* est *relevanda;* de hac caecitate Matthaei decimo quinto: « Caecus si caeco ducatum praestet, ambo in foveam cadunt ». Haec relevanda est; unde Isaiae quadragesimo secundo: « Dedi te in foedus populi et in lucem gentium, ut aperias oculos caecorum » etc. — Caecitas *poenae* est *timenda;* de qua Sophoniae primo[^p578-7]: « Tribulabo homines, et ambulabunt ut caeci, quia Domino peccaverunt »; et Deuteronomii vigesimo octavo: « Percutiat te Dominus amentia et caecitate ac furore mentis, et palpes in meridie sicut palpare solet caecus in tenebris ». — Caecitas *culpae* est *abominanda;* de qua secundae Petri primo: « Cui enim non praesto sunt haec, caecus est et manu tentans »; loquitur de operibus virtutum; et Sapientiae secundo: « Excaecavit eos malitia eorum ».

2. Quoniam autem negotiandum est circa caecitatem *culpae,* notandum, quod haec est multiplex. Est enim caecitas *erroris* in *intellectu;*
<!-- page 579 -->
caecitas *praesumtionis* in *affectu;* caecitas *deviationis* in *effectu;* caecitas *obstinationis* in *peccandi consuetudine* et excessu. — Est ergo caecitas *erroris;* de qua ad Romanos undecimo[^p579-1]: « Caecitas ex parte contigit in Israel, donec plenitudo gentium intraret »; et ad Ephesios quarto: « Alienati a via Dei per ignorantiam, quae est in illis, propter caecitatem cordis ipsorum ». — Est caecitas *praesumtionis;* de qua Apocalypsis tertio: « Tu dicis, quia dives sum et locupletatus sum et nullius egeo, et nescis, quia miser es et miserabilis et pauper et nudus et caecus ». — Est etiam caecitas *deviationis;* de qua Threnorum quarto[^p579-2]: « Erraverunt caeci in plateis, polluti sunt in sanguine ». — Est etiam caecitas *obstinationis;* de qua Matthaei decimo quinto: « Sinite eos; caeci sunt ducesque caecorum ». Sic ergo est quadruplex caecitas culpae.

3. Dicitur autem *caecitas culpae* quadruplici ratione: quia ad modum caecitatis naturalis facit hominem *ignarum* in discernendo, *incautum* in evitando, *ineptum* in agendo, *praecipitem* in eundo. — Facit igitur hominem *ignarum in discernendo,* ut nesciat bonum eligere; propter quod dicitur Isaiae quadragesimo secundo[^p579-3]: « Audite, caeci, et intuemini ad videndum ». — Facit *incautum in evitando,* ut non possit malum cavere; Lucae sexto: « Nunquid potest caecus caecum ducere? Nonne ambo in foveam cadunt »? — Facit *ineptum in agendo,* ut non possit operari bonum; unde Isaiae quinquagesimo nono: « Palpavimus sicut caeci parietem, et quasi absque oculis attrectavimus »; et de hoc Deuteronomii vigesimo octavo: « Palpari te faciet » etc. — Facit *praecipitem in eundo,* ut non possit prospicere; Tobiae undecimo: « Exsurgens caecus pater coepit offendens pedibus currere ». — Haec ergo incommoda caecitatis spiritualis, quae facit *impediendo bonum,* ne scilicet caecus spiritualis possit[^p579-4] *discernere,* vel *cavere,* vel *operari,* vel *proficere.*

4. Sunt etiam alia incommoda, quae facit caecitas spiritualis *inferendo malum,* quia scilicet *depauperat, molestat, vilificat* et *deformat.* — Reddit enim caecitas hominem *pauperem;* Marci decimo[^p579-5]: « Caecus sedebat iuxta viam mendicans »; et Apocalypsis tertio: « Pauper es et nudus et caecus ». Paupertas enim conscientiae et nuditas vitae comitantur caecitatem culpae. — Item reddit hominem *tristem;* Tobiae quinto: « Quale gaudium mihi erit, qui in tenebris sedeo et lumen caeli non video »? Cuius signum fuit, quia, cum quaereret Dominus a caeco, Marci decimo: « Quid vis, ut faciam tibi »? respondit nihil aliud nisi: « Rabboni, ut videam ». — Item, reddit etiam hominem *vilem et indignum* aliorum communitate; unde secundi Regum quinto[^p579-6]: « Caecus et claudus non intrabunt in templum ». Revera caecus spiritualis non intrat templum gloriae. — Item reddit hominem *deformem;* unde ad Ephesios quarto dicitur de peccatoribus: « Obscuratum habentes intellectum, alienati a via Dei per ignorantiam, quae est in illis, propter caecitatem cordis ipsorum ».

5. *Vidit caecum a nativitate.* — Nota, quod est caecitas *innata,* et haec est caecitas *culpae originalis,* quae significatur per istum caecum, qui natus est caecus — et est caecitas *contracta* vel acquisita, et haec est caecitas *culpae actualis,* de qua Sapientiae secundo[^p579-7]: « Excaecavit eos malitia eorum ». Contrahitur autem haec caecitas a tribus. Contrahitur enim a *concupiscentia carnis* et a *concupiscentia oculorum* et a *superbia vitae.* Nocet enim oculis spiritualibus *sanguis luxuriae, pulvis avaritiae et ventus superbiae.*

6. Excaecantur ergo quidam per *concupiscentiam carnis;* quod figuratum est, Iudicum decimo sexto[^p579-8], de Samsone, qui excaecatus fuit per Dalilam. Et nota primo, quomodo *blandiendo decepit;* secundo *crines,* id est ornatum virtutum, *rapuit;* tertio *manibus inimicorum tradidit,* qui eum excaecaverunt, ne statum suum cognosceret, et vinctum duabus catenis, pronitate scilicet ad malum et difficultate ad bonum, molere fecerunt per peccandi consuetudinem. — Excaecantur alii per *concupiscentiam oculorum;* et hoc figuratum est, Tobiae secundo[^p579-9], in excaecatione Tobiae, qui excaecatus est stercore hirundinum, quae significant temporalia, quae sancti viri arbitrantur « ut stercora », secundum quod habetur ad Philippenses tertio. — Excaecantur alii per *superbiam;* et hoc figuratum est, quarti Regum ultimo[^p579-10], de Sedecia, quem excaecavit Nabuchodonosor, id est diabolus, in Reblatha, cum fugeret de Ierusalem. *Reblatha* interpretatur *multum commissum,* et significat superbiam, de qua dicitur in Psalmo: « Emundabor a delicto maximo ». — Sic ergo caecitas spiritualis tripliciter *contrahitur.*

7. Sed notandum est, quod haec eadem caecitas tripliciter *sanatur.* Sanatur enim caecitas in Scriptura
<!-- page 580 -->
aliquando *collyrio,* aliquando *felle,* aliquando *luto.* — *Collyrium,* quod humores restringit, significat carnis mortificationem, quae fluxum concupiscentiae restringit; et haec est medicina caecitatis contractae per *luxuriam;* de qua dicitur Apocalypsis tertio[^p580-1]: « Collyrio inunge oculos tuos, ut videas ». *Inunge,* inquit; nam carnis maceratio et est corpori afflictio et unctio sive illuminatio spiritui. — *Fellis amaritudo* significat memoriam mortis; de qua Ecclesiastici quadragesimo primo: « O mors, quam amara est memoria tua » etc. Et haec est medicina caecitatis contractae per *avaritiam,* quia, sicut dicit Hieronymus[^p580-2], « facile contemnit omnia qui se semper cogitat moriturum ». Et hoc figuratum est, Tobiae undecimo, in illuminatione Tobiae, qui sanatus est a caecitate sua, linitis oculis felle. — *Lutum* significat vilitatem humanae conditionis, quae, superposita oculis spiritualibus, id est reducta in mentem, medicina est caecitatis contractae per *superbiam.* Et hoc significari potest in illuminatione huius caeci, qui primo fuit *luto linitus,* considerando propriam vilitatem; secundo, *lotus* per lacrymarum effusionem; tertio, *illuminatus* per gratiam.

### Collatio XXXVIII.

(Vers. 11.). *Ille homo, qui dicitur Iesus, lutum fecit* etc.

1. Distinguitur multiplex lutum. Est enim lutum *naturae* — lutum *culpae* — et lutum *poenae.* — Est autem lutum naturae triplex, quia natura est aut natura *instituta,* aut natura *lapsa,* aut natura *reparata.* — De luto naturae *institutae,* quae dicitur *lutum* propter eius *ductibilitatem,* dicitur Isaiae sexagesimo quarto[^p580-3]: « Domine, pater noster es tu, nos vero lutum; et fictor noster tu, et opera manuum tuarum omnes nos »; et Ieremiae decimo octavo: « Sicuti lutum in manu figuli, sic vos in manu mea, domus Israel ». — De luto naturae *lapsae,* quae dicitur *lutum* propter eius *ignobilitatem,* dicitur in Iob quadragesimo primo: « Sternet sibi aurum quasi lutum ». — De luto naturae *reparatae,* quae dicitur lutum propter eius *fragilitatem;* Iob trigesimo: « Comparatus sum luto, et assimilatus sum favillae et cineri ».

2. Est praeterea lutum *culpae,* et dicitur culpa *lutum* propter eius *foeditatem;* secundae Petri secundo[^p580-4]: « Canis reversus ad vomitum, et sus lota in volutabro luti »; Psalmus: « Eduxit me de lacu miseriae et de luto faecis ». — Item, propter eius *lubricitatem;* Ieremiae trigesimo octavo: « Demerserunt te in coeno et in lubrico pedes tuos »; Psalmus: « Eripe me de luto, ut non infigar ». — Item, propter eius *laboriositatem;* unde Exodi primo dicitur, quod Aegyptii « ad amaritudinem perducebant filios Israel operibus duris luti et lateris ».

3. Est praeterea lutum *poenae,* et haec est triplex, secundum quod est triplex poena, scilicet poena *inventa,* poena *inflicta* et poena *assumta.* — Poena *inventa* dicitur *lutum* propter eius *difficultatem* ad transeundum; unde Habacuc tertio[^p580-5]: « Viam fecisti in mari equis tuis, in luto aquarum multarum ». — Poena *inflicta* dicitur *lutum* propter eius *expositionem* ad conculcandum; Isaiae decimo: « Mandabo illi, ut auferat spolia et dividat praedam et ponat illum in conculcationem quasi lutum platearum »; Psalmus: « Ut lutum platearum delebo eos ». — Poena *assumta* dicitur *lutum* propter eius *utilitatem;* de quo luto Nahum tertio: « Intra in lutum et calca, et tene subigens laterem ». — Est autem huiusmodi lutum utile ad tria: ad *lavandum;* unde Ioannis nono[^p580-6]: *Ille homo, qui dicitur Iesus, lutum fecit et unxit oculos meos et dixit mihi: Vade ad natatoria Siloe et lava* etc. — Est etiam utile ad *aedificandum;* unde Levitici decimo quarto dicitur de domo leprosa: « Iubebit sacerdos, lapides alios reponi pro his qui ablati fuerant, et luto alio liniri domum ». — Est etiam utile ad *impinguandum;* unde Lucae decimo tertio dicitur de ficu infructuosa: « Domine, dimitte anno isto, usque dum fodiam circa illam et mittam stercora ». — Lutum igitur est poena, quia utilis est ad *lavandam* culpam, ad *aedificandam* vitam, ad *impinguandam* animam.

4. *Lutum posuit super oculos meos, et lavi et video*[^p580-7]. — *Lutum posuit super oculos,* peccatum ad memoriam reducendo; *et lavi,* peccatum deflendo; *et video,* gratiam accipiendo. — Lutum ergo super oculos ponitur in culpae recordatione.

5. Et nota, quod est multiplex lutum secundum multiplex genus culpae. Est namque lutum *superbiae,* de quo Isaiae vigesimo nono[^p580-8]: « Perversa est haec vestra cogitatio, quasi si lutum contra figulum cogitet, et dicat opus factori suo: Non fecisti me »; hoc est lutum ampullosum. — Est lutum *irae,* de quo Isaiae quinquagesimo septimo: « Cor impii quasi mare fervens, quod quiescere non potest, et redundant fluctus eius in conculcationem et lutum ». — Est praeterea lutum *invidiae,* de quo Iob quadragesimo primo: « Sternet sibi aurum quasi lutum ».
<!-- page 581 -->
— Est praeterea lutum *avaritiae;* Habacuc secundo[^p581-1]: « Qui multiplicat non sua, usquequo aggravat contra se densum lutum »? — Est et lutum *accidiae,* de quo Ecclesiastici vigesimo secundo: « In lapide luteo lapidabitur piger ». — Est lutum *luxuriae,* de quo Ieremiae trigesimo octavo: « Demerserunt in coeno et in lubrico pedes tuos »; Psalmus: « Eripe me de luto, ut non infigar ». — Est etiam lutum *gulae;* secundae Petri secundo: « Canis reversus ad vomitum, et sus lota in volutabro luti ». — Et potest dici per appropriationem *gula* lutum *foetens* vel foetidum; *luxuria,* lutum *viscosum* vel lubricum; *accidia,* lutum *grave* vel ponderosum; *avaritia,* lutum *terrenum* et opacum; *invidia,* lutum *fragile* et passivum; *ira,* lutum *fluctuans* et inquietatum; *superbia,* lutum *tumidum* et ampullosum. — Hoc lutum ponendum est super oculos et lavandum, ut spiritualem spiritualis caecus recuperet visum.

### Chapter IX. *And Jesus passing by.*

### Collation XXXVII.

(Verse 1.). *And Jesus passing by saw a man blind from his birth.*

1. Note that *blindness* is manifold. For there is blindness *of nature,* blindness *of ignorance,* blindness *of punishment* and blindness *of fault.* — Blindness *of nature* is *to be pitied;* of which here: *Jesus passing by saw a man blind from his birth;* and it follows below[^p578-6] how he had mercy on him; Deuteronomy, the twenty-seventh chapter: « Cursed be he that made the blind to wander in the way ». — Blindness *of ignorance* is *to be relieved;* of this blindness Matthew, the fifteenth chapter: « If the blind lead the blind, both fall into the pit ». This is to be relieved; whence Isaias, the forty-second chapter: « I have given thee for a covenant of the people, for a light of the Gentiles, that thou mightest open the eyes of the blind » etc. — Blindness *of punishment* is *to be feared;* of which Sophonias, the first chapter[^p578-7]: « I will distress men, and they shall walk like blind men, because they have sinned against the Lord »; and Deuteronomy, the twenty-eighth chapter: « The Lord strike thee with madness and blindness and fury of mind, and mayst thou grope at midday as the blind is wont to grope in the dark ». — Blindness *of fault* is *to be abominated;* of which the second of Peter, the first chapter: « For he to whom these things are not present is blind, and groping with his hand »; he speaks of the works of the virtues; and Wisdom, the second chapter: « Their malice blinded them ».

2. But since we must deal with the blindness *of fault,* it is to be noted that this is manifold. For there is the blindness *of error* in *the understanding;*
<!-- page 579 -->
the blindness *of presumption* in *the affection;* the blindness *of deviation* in *the effect;* the blindness *of obstinacy* in *the custom of sinning* and excess. — There is, then, the blindness *of error;* of which to the Romans, the eleventh chapter[^p579-1]: « Blindness in part has happened in Israel, until the fulness of the Gentiles should come in »; and to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter: « Being alienated from the way of God through the ignorance that is in them, because of the blindness of their hearts ». — There is the blindness *of presumption;* of which the Apocalypse, the third chapter: « Thou sayest: I am rich, and made wealthy, and have need of nothing; and knowest not that thou art wretched, and miserable, and poor, and naked, and blind ». — There is also the blindness *of deviation;* of which Lamentations, the fourth chapter[^p579-2]: « They have wandered as blind men in the streets, they were defiled with blood ». — There is also the blindness *of obstinacy;* of which Matthew, the fifteenth chapter: « Let them alone; they are blind, and leaders of the blind ». Thus, then, the blindness of fault is fourfold.

3. But the *blindness of fault* is so called on a fourfold account: because, after the manner of natural blindness, it makes a man *ignorant* in discerning, *incautious* in avoiding, *inept* in acting, *headlong* in going. — It makes a man, then, *ignorant in discerning,* so that he knows not how to choose the good; on account of which it is said in Isaias, the forty-second chapter[^p579-3]: « Hear, ye blind, and behold that you may see ». — It makes him *incautious in avoiding,* so that he cannot beware of evil; Luke, the sixth chapter: « Can the blind lead the blind? do they not both fall into the ditch »? — It makes him *inept in acting,* so that he cannot work the good; whence Isaias, the fifty-ninth chapter: « We have groped for the wall like the blind, and we have groped as if we had no eyes »; and of this Deuteronomy, the twenty-eighth chapter: « He will make thee grope » etc. — It makes him *headlong in going,* so that he cannot look ahead; Tobias, the eleventh chapter: « The blind father, rising up, began to run, stumbling with his feet ». — These, then, are the disadvantages of spiritual blindness which it causes *by hindering the good,* namely that the spiritually blind man cannot[^p579-4] *discern,* or *beware,* or *work,* or *make progress.*

4. There are also other disadvantages which spiritual blindness causes *by inflicting evil,* namely because it *impoverishes, troubles, debases* and *deforms.* — For blindness renders a man *poor;* Mark, the tenth chapter[^p579-5]: « The blind man sat by the way side begging »; and the Apocalypse, the third chapter: « Thou art poor, and naked, and blind ». For poverty of conscience and nakedness of life accompany the blindness of fault. — Likewise it renders a man *sad;* Tobias, the fifth chapter: « What manner of joy shall be to me, who sit in darkness, and see not the light[^tr-lumen] of heaven »? A sign of which was that, when the Lord asked the blind man, Mark, the tenth chapter: « What wilt thou that I should do to thee »? he answered nothing else but: « Rabboni, that I may see ». — Likewise it also renders a man *vile and unworthy* of the fellowship of others; whence the second of Kings, the fifth chapter[^p579-6]: « The blind and the lame shall not come into the temple ». In truth the spiritually blind man does not enter the temple of glory. — Likewise it renders a man *deformed;* whence to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter, it is said of sinners: « Having their understanding darkened, being alienated from the way of God through the ignorance that is in them, because of the blindness of their hearts ».

5. *He saw a man blind from his birth.* — Note that there is a blindness *inborn,* and this is the blindness *of original fault,* which is signified by this blind man, who was born blind — and there is a blindness *contracted* or acquired, and this is the blindness *of actual fault,* of which Wisdom, the second chapter[^p579-7]: « Their malice blinded them ». But this blindness is contracted from three things. For it is contracted from *the concupiscence of the flesh* and from *the concupiscence of the eyes* and from *the pride of life.* For the spiritual eyes are harmed by *the blood of lust, the dust of avarice and the wind of pride.*

6. Some, then, are blinded through *the concupiscence of the flesh;* which is figured, Judges, the sixteenth chapter[^p579-8], in Samson, who was blinded through Dalila. And note first how she *deceived him by flattering;* secondly she *carried off his locks,* that is, the adornment of the virtues; thirdly she *delivered him into the hands of his enemies,* who blinded him, so that he might not know his state, and, having bound him with two chains, namely proneness to evil and difficulty toward the good, made him grind through the custom of sinning. — Others are blinded through *the concupiscence of the eyes;* and this is figured, Tobias, the second chapter[^p579-9], in the blinding of Tobias, who was blinded by the dung of swallows, which signify temporal things, which holy men count « but as dung », according to what is had to the Philippians, the third chapter. — Others are blinded through *pride;* and this is figured, the fourth of Kings, the last chapter[^p579-10], in Sedecias, whom Nabuchodonosor, that is, the devil, blinded in Reblatha, when he was fleeing from Jerusalem. *Reblatha* is interpreted *much committed,* and signifies pride, of which it is said in the Psalm: « I shall be cleansed from the greatest sin ». — Thus, then, spiritual blindness *is contracted* in three ways.

7. But it is to be noted that this same blindness *is healed* in three ways. For blindness is healed in Scripture
<!-- page 580 -->
sometimes *with eyesalve,* sometimes *with gall,* sometimes *with clay.* — *Eyesalve,* which restrains the humours, signifies the mortification of the flesh, which restrains the flux of concupiscence; and this is the medicine of blindness contracted through *lust;* of which it is said in the Apocalypse, the third chapter[^p580-1]: « Anoint thy eyes with eyesalve, that thou mayst see ». *Anoint,* he says; for the maceration of the flesh is both an affliction to the body and an anointing or illumination to the spirit. — *The bitterness of gall* signifies the remembrance of death; of which Ecclesiasticus, the forty-first chapter: « O death, how bitter is the remembrance of thee » etc. And this is the medicine of blindness contracted through *avarice,* because, as Jerome says[^p580-2], « he easily despises all things who always thinks that he is to die ». And this is figured, Tobias, the eleventh chapter, in the illumination of Tobias, who was healed of his blindness, his eyes being smeared with gall. — *Clay* signifies the vileness of the human condition, which, laid upon the spiritual eyes, that is, brought back to mind, is the medicine of blindness contracted through *pride.* And this can be signified in the illumination of this blind man, who first was *smeared with clay,* by considering his own vileness; secondly, *washed* through the shedding of tears; thirdly, *illumined* through grace.

### Collation XXXVIII.

(Verse 11.). *That man that is called Jesus made clay* etc.

1. Manifold clay is distinguished. For there is the clay *of nature* — the clay *of fault* — and the clay *of punishment.* — But the clay of nature is threefold, because nature is either nature *instituted,* or nature *fallen,* or nature *repaired.* — Of the clay of nature *instituted,* which is called *clay* on account of its *ductility,* it is said in Isaias, the sixty-fourth chapter[^p580-3]: « O Lord, thou art our father, and we are clay; and thou art our maker, and we all are the works of thy hands »; and Jeremias, the eighteenth chapter: « As clay is in the hand of the potter, so are you in my hand, O house of Israel ». — Of the clay of nature *fallen,* which is called *clay* on account of its *ignobility,* it is said in Job, the forty-first chapter: « He shall strew gold for himself as clay ». — Of the clay of nature *repaired,* which is called clay on account of its *fragility;* Job, the thirtieth chapter: « I am compared to clay, and am likened to embers and ashes ».

2. There is besides the clay *of fault,* and fault is called *clay* on account of its *foulness;* the second of Peter, the second chapter[^p580-4]: « The dog is returned to his vomit, and the sow that was washed to her wallowing in the clay »; the Psalm: « He brought me out of the pit of misery and the clay of dregs ». — Likewise, on account of its *slipperiness;* Jeremias, the thirty-eighth chapter: « They have plunged thee in the mire, and thy feet in a slippery place »; the Psalm: « Draw me out of the clay, that I may not stick fast ». — Likewise, on account of its *laboriousness;* whence in Exodus, the first chapter, it is said that the Egyptians « brought the children of Israel to bitterness with hard works of clay and brick ».

3. There is besides the clay *of punishment,* and this is threefold, according as punishment is threefold, namely punishment *found,* punishment *inflicted* and punishment *assumed.* — Punishment *found* is called *clay* on account of its *difficulty* for crossing; whence Habacuc, the third chapter[^p580-5]: « Thou madest a way in the sea for thy horses, in the clay of many waters ». — Punishment *inflicted* is called *clay* on account of its *exposure* to being trodden down; Isaias, the tenth chapter: « I will give him a charge to take away the spoils, and to divide the prey, and to lay him to be trodden down as the clay of the streets »; the Psalm: « As the clay of the streets I will destroy them ». — Punishment *assumed* is called *clay* on account of its *usefulness;* of which clay Nahum, the third chapter: « Go into the clay, and tread, and hold it, working the brick ». — But clay of this kind is useful for three things: for *washing;* whence John, the ninth chapter[^p580-6]: *That man that is called Jesus made clay, and anointed my eyes, and said to me: Go to the pool of Siloe, and wash* etc. — It is useful also for *building;* whence in Leviticus, the fourteenth chapter, it is said of the leprous house: « The priest shall command other stones to be put in the place of those that were taken away, and the house to be daubed with other clay ». — It is useful also for *fattening;* whence in Luke, the thirteenth chapter, it is said of the unfruitful fig tree: « Lord, let it alone this year, until I dig about it and lay dung ». — Clay, therefore, is punishment, because it is useful for *washing* fault, for *building* life, for *fattening* the soul.

4. *He put clay upon my eyes, and I washed, and I see*[^p580-7]. — *He put clay upon my eyes,* by bringing sin back to memory; *and I washed,* by weeping for sin; *and I see,* by receiving grace. — Clay, then, is put upon the eyes in the recollection of fault.

5. And note that clay is manifold according to the manifold kind of fault. For there is the clay *of pride,* of which Isaias, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p580-8]: « This thought of yours is perverse, as if the clay should think against the potter, and the work should say to the maker thereof: Thou madest me not »; this is the puffed-up clay. — There is the clay *of anger,* of which Isaias, the fifty-seventh chapter: « The heart of the wicked is as the raging sea, which cannot rest, and its waves overflow into treading down and clay ». — There is besides the clay *of envy,* of which Job, the forty-first chapter: « He shall strew gold for himself as clay ».
<!-- page 581 -->
— There is besides the clay *of avarice;* Habacuc, the second chapter[^p581-1]: « He that heapeth together that which is not his own, how long doth he load himself with thick clay »? — There is also the clay *of sloth,* of which Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-second chapter: « The slothful man shall be stoned with a stone of clay ». — There is the clay *of lust,* of which Jeremias, the thirty-eighth chapter: « They have plunged thy feet in the mire and in a slippery place »; the Psalm: « Draw me out of the clay, that I may not stick fast ». — There is also the clay *of gluttony;* the second of Peter, the second chapter: « The dog is returned to his vomit, and the sow that was washed to her wallowing in the clay ». — And by appropriation it can be said that *gluttony* is a *stinking* or fetid clay; *lust,* a *sticky* or slippery clay; *sloth,* a *heavy* or weighty clay; *avarice,* an *earthy* and dark clay; *envy,* a *brittle* and passive clay; *anger,* a *surging* and unquieted clay; *pride,* a *swollen* and puffed-up clay. — This clay is to be put upon the eyes and washed off, that the spiritually blind man may recover spiritual sight.
