# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. II, Vers. 1-11
## *Postquam descripta est discipulorum vocatio, describitur hic facta per Verbum et de Verbo incarnato manifestatio*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 269 -->

### Capitulum II.

1. *Et die tertio*[^p269-2] *nuptiae* etc. Postquam descripta est discipulorum vocatio, describitur hic facta per Verbum et de Verbo incarnato *manifestatio;* unde Glossa[^p269-3] dicit, quod post ostensiones factas per Ioannem ipse Dominus miraculo se ostendit. — Hanc igitur manifestationem describit Evangelista hoc ordine. Primo innuitur miraculi faciendi *occasio;* secundo vero, miraculi *impetratio;* tertio, mirabilis aquae in vinum *conversio;* quarto, miraculi *approbatio;* quinto, Christi *manifestatio* et discipulorum *aedificatio,* quibus Dominus per hoc miraculum se ipsum manifestare dignatus est.

(Vers. 1.). Primo igitur innuitur *miraculi faciendi occasio,* quae fuit, quod in nuptiis Dominus erat praesens cum discipulis. Propter quod dicit: *Et die tertio,* scilicet post discipulorum vocationem[^p269-4], *nuptiae factae sunt in Cana Galilaeae;* tangitur locus. *Et erat Mater Iesu ibi;* non dicitur, quod fuerit vocata, quia ratione affinitatis ivit tanquam officiosa, sicut etiam abiit in montana ad Elisabeth ad serviendum ei, ut dicitur Lucae primo[^p269-5]. Ratione huius vocatus fuit etiam Dominus. Propterea dicit:

2. (Vers. 2.). *Vocatus est autem et Iesus et discipuli eius ad nuptias.* Chrysostomus[^p269-6]: « Vocatus est ad nuptias, non ut aliquis magnus, sed ut consanguineus, et ipse ut humilis invitationem non respuit, sed ivit », iuxta quod docet Lucae decimo quarto: « Cum vocatus fueris ad nuptias, vade, recumbe in novissimo loco ».

3. (Vers. 3.). *Et deficiente vino.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *miraculi impetratio,* quae facta est a Matre miserante sponsi inopiam. Propterea dicit: *Et deficiente vino, dicit Mater Iesu ad eum: Vinum non habent;* quasi dicat: Tua abundantia supple eorum inopiam; sciebat enim, quod esset « dives in misericordia[^p269-7] » et potentia, quamvis appareret egenus; secundae ad Corinthios octavo: « Cum esset dives in omnibus, factus est pro nobis egenus ». Sed Dominus, innuens Matri, quod non poterat hoc ipsa praecipere secundum quod mater, nec ipse facere secundum quod eius filius, nec debebat facere, nisi cum adesset tempus proprium; hinc sequitur:

4. (Vers. 4.). *Et dicit ei Iesus: Quid mihi et tibi est, mulier?* Glossa[^p269-8]: « Id est, quae natura est mihi et tibi communis, secundum quam hoc faciam »? *Mulierem* vocat, non a fragilitate, sed a natura et sexu. Haec enim est illa mulier, de qua Proverbiorum ultimo[^p269-9]: « Mulierem fortem quis inveniet »? Quasi dicat: Hoc petere potens es ut sancta mulier, non praecipere ut mater, nec adhuc oportet facere. *Nondum venit hora mea,* id est meae clarificationis et passionis, de qua infra decimo septimo: « Pater, venit hora, clarifica Filium tuum ». Sed Mater sciens, hanc responsionem non esse ex indignatione, sed ex humilitate et instructione, fiducialiter praecipit; unde sequitur:

5. (Vers. 5.). *Dicit Mater eius ministris: Quodcumque dixerit vobis, facite.* Sciebat enim, quod posset; sciebat etiam, quod sciret; sciebat, quod vellet, quia semper ei obediebat; unde Lucae secundo[^p269-10]: « Descendit cum eis et venit Nazareth et erat subditus illis », Iesus scilicet parentibus; et ex hoc certa erat, quod faceret. Ideo dicit: *Quodcumque dixerit vobis, facite,* tanquam boni ministri, ut miraculum consequamini, sicut et Naaman, quarti Regum quinto[^p269-11]: « Pater, etsi rem grandem dixisset tibi Propheta, facere debuisses ». *Facite, quodcumque dixerit vobis;* non diffidatis; infra undecimo: « Si credideris, videbis gloriam Dei ».

6. (Vers. 6.). *Erant autem hydriae sex* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *miraculi operatio.* Et quia Deus omnia facit in certo « pondere, numero et mensura[^p269-12] »; dicit mensuram, in qua hoc miraculum factum est. Propter quod dicit: *Erant autem ibi hydriae lapideae sex. Hydriae* sunt vasa aquae receptibilia; Genesis vigesimo quarto[^p269-13]: « Da mihi
<!-- page 270 -->
pauxillum aquae de hydria tua ». Hae hydriae *positae secundum purificationem Iudaeorum,* id est secundum morem Iudaeorum ad se purificandum. De hoc more, Marci septimo[^p270-1]: « Et a foro redeuntes, nisi baptizentur, non comedunt; et alia multa sunt, quae tradita sunt illis servare, baptismata calicum et urceorum ». *Capientes singulae metretas binas vel ternas. Metreta* dicitur a *metron,* quod est mensura[^p270-2], et vocatur hic mensura communis et usitata, sicut Parisius sextarium vini dicimus. Secundum has igitur mensuras factum est miraculum ad Domini imperium; propter quod subdit:

7. (Vers. 7.). *Dicit eis Iesus: Implete hydrias aqua. Et impleverunt eas usque ad summum.* Et intelligendum, quod cum impletae fuerunt, Domini virtute in vinum conversa est aqua; ideo *impleri* praecepit, ut plenum opus faceret, secundum quod in conditione rerum, Genesis primo[^p270-3], « vidit Deus cuncta, quae fecerat, et erant valde bona »; et Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo: « Dei perfecta sunt opera ».

8. (Vers. 8.). *Et dixit*[^p270-4] *eis Iesus.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *miraculi approbatio,* in approbatione vini facta testimonio architriclini, cuius erat de vino probare. Propter quod dicit: *Et dixit eis Iesus,* scilicet ministris: *Haurite nunc et ferte architriclino. Et tulerunt,* secundum eius imperium, scilicet ideo ad ipsum praecepit ferri, quia nuptiis praeerat et iudicabat de huiusmodi architriclinus. « Triclinium, id est, tres ordines discumbentium, altitudine distantes[^p270-5] »; inde *architriclinus,* quia praeerat convivio ordinis trium mensarum; vel *architriclinus,* princeps trium camerarum; vel generaliter, qui praeest servientibus, ut dicit Chrysostomus[^p270-6]. Ad hunc praecepit ferri, ut de vino iudicaret; propter quod dicit:

9. (Vers. 9.). *Ut autem gustavit architriclinus aquam vinum factam,* id est vinum factum ex aqua; *et non sciebat, unde esset, ministri autem sciebant, qui hauserant aquam; vocat sponsum architriclinus,* ut scilicet ipsum redarguat et vinum commendet et extollat.

10. (Vers. 10.). *Et dicit ei: Omnis homo primum bonum vinum ponit; primum,* id est in principio; *et cum inebriati fuerint, tunc id quod deterius est,* quia tunc non valent diiudicare, sed omne vinum dicunt bonum. *Tu autem servasti bonum vinum usque adhuc;* id est usque ad finem, et ita inordinate et contra morem fecisti. Malus mos erat iste, quia contra veritatem et contra sobrietatem; unde ad Ephesios quinto[^p270-7]: « Nolite inebriari vino, in quo est luxuria, sed impleamini Spiritu sancto, loquentes » etc.

11. (Vers. 11.). *Hoc initium signorum.* Tangitur hic quintum, scilicet *divinae gloriae manifestatio* et *fidei discipulorum aedificatio.* Ratione *manifestationis suae* dicit: *Hoc initium signorum fecit Iesus in Cana Galilaeae et manifestavit gloriam suam;* Glossa[^p270-8]: « Id est latentem Divinitatem »; infra decimo quarto: « Ego diligam eum et manifestabo ei me ipsum ». — Ratione *aedificationis discipulorum* dicit: *Et crediderunt in eum discipuli eius,* id est, firmius crediderunt. Beda[^p270-9]: « Discumbente Domino ad nuptias, vinum defecit, ut, vino meliori per ipsum mirabili ordine facto, manifestaretur in illo latentis gloria Dei, et credentium in eum fides aucta proficeret ».

12. *Allegorice:* Triplex est *dies* secundum triplex tempus, naturae scilicet, Legis et gratiae. In hoc *tertio* nuptiae factae sunt inter Christum et Ecclesiam in carnis assumtione, de quibus Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p270-10]: *Simile est regnum caelorum homini regi, qui fecit nuptias filio suo.* In his nuptiis *sex hydriae aquae* insipidae et sine nutrimento conversae sunt in vinum sapidum et iucundum: quia *umbra Legis* in veritatem; ad Hebraeos decimo[^p270-11]: *Umbram habens Lex futurorum bonorum* etc. — *Varietas sacrificiorum* in unitatem; ad Hebraeos decimo: *Omnis quidem sacerdos praesto est quotidie ministrans, et easdem saepe offerens hostias, quae nunquam possunt auferre peccata: hic autem unam pro peccatis offerens hostiam* etc. — *Asperitas poenarum* in levitatem; Actuum decimo quinto[^p270-12]: *Quid tentatis Deum, imponere iugum super cervices discipulorum* etc.? — *Aenigmata* in lucem; secundae ad Corinthios tertio: *Usque in hodiernum diem velamen est positum super cor eorum, nos autem, revelata facie glorium Domini speculantes* etc. — *Terrores* in amorem; ad Romanos octavo[^p270-13]: *Non accepistis spiritum servitutis iterum in timore, sed
<!-- page 271 -->
accepistis spiritum adoptionis filiorum* etc. — *Promissiones* in adeptionem; Lucae primo[^p271-1] in cantico Zachariae: *Benedictus Dominus Deus Israel, quia visitavit et fecit redemptionem plebis suae.* — Et haec facta sunt ad preces Virginis, ipsa intercedente; Lucae primo: *Ne timeas Maria; invenisti enim gratiam apud Deum.*

13. *Moraliter: Nuptiae* istae sunt Dei et animae in ipsa reconciliatione animae ad Deum, de quibus Osee secundo[^p271-2]: *Sponsabo te mihi in fide, et scies, quia ego Dominus.* — Ad has nuptias faciendas *venit Dominus Iesus,* sed invitatus; Apocalypsis tertio[^p271-3]: *Ecce, sto ad ostium et pulso; si quis audierit vocem meam et aperuerit mihi ianuam, intrabo ad illum et coenabo cum illo.* — *Vinum* in his nuptiis *deficit;* hoc vinum est interna devotio, de quo in Psalmo[^p271-4]. *Vinum laetificatcor hominis;* et Matthaei nono: *Nemo mittit vinum novum in utres veteres.* Illud vinum *deficit,* cum homo efficitur aridus et indevotus, sicut dicit anima sancta in Psalmo: *Anima mea sicut terra sine aqua tibi.* — Sed ad preces Virginis, quae compatitur miseris, Deus *implet hydrias aqua* compunctionis, quae convertitur in dulcedinem devotionis; unde Exodi decimo quinto[^p271-5] dicitur, quod aquae Marath *in dulcedinem conversae sunt.*

Nota igitur, quod *hydria,* in qua hauritur aqua, est consideratio; *hydria vacua* est consideratio defectus et vacuitatis[^p271-6], quam incurrit homo per peccatum. Ista hydria *impletur aqua,* cum homo ex consideratione tali lacrymis perfunditur. — Est igitur prima hydria consideratio defectus *cognitionis;* Tobiae quinto[^p271-7]: *Quale mihi gaudium esse potest, qui in tenebris sedeo et lumen caeli non video?* — Secunda hydria, consideratio *defectus circumspectionis* circa hostem; secundae ad Corinthios undecimo[^p271-8]: *Timeo autem, ne, sicut serpens seduxit Evam astutia sua, ita corrumpantur sensus vestri et excidant a simplicitate, quae est in Christo.* — Tertia est consideratio defectus *internae consolationis;* Ieremiae secundo[^p271-9]: *Vide, quam malum et amarum est, te reliquisse Dominum Deum tuum.* — Quarta est consideratio defectus *virtutis ad resistendum:* Psalmus[^p271-10]: *Dereliquit me virtus mea et lumen oculorum, et ipsum non est mecum.* — Quinta est consideratio defectus *stabilitatis ad permanendum;* Genesis octavo[^p271-11]: *Sensus et cogitatio hominis in malum prona sunt ab adolescentia sua.* — Sexta est consideratio defectus *securitatis ad exspectandum;* Ecclesiastis nono[^p271-12]: *Nescit homo, utrum amore, an odio dignus sit.*

Tunc *implentur* istae hydriae *usque ad summum aqua,* quando anima poenitens lavat per singulas noctes ex his considerationibus lectum suum[^p271-13]. Et ex aqua *vinum fit optimum* devotionis, de quo Zachariae nono: *Quid bonum, et quid pulcrum eius nisi frumentum electorum et vinum germinans virgines?* In hoc convertitur aqua compunctionis; unde sancta anima in Psalmo[^p271-14]: *Convertisti planctum meum in gaudium mihi.*

### Quaestiones.

14. Incidit hic quaestio inter expositores.

Quaest. I. Primo de *tempore* nuptiarum; dicitur enim a quibusdam, quod factae fuerunt eodem die, quo Dominus baptizatus. Sed quomodo potest hoc stare, cum dicatur, quod *die tertio factae sunt,* postquam Dominus venit de Iudaea?

Respondent, quod eodem die, anno revoluto[^p271-15]. — Sed tunc repugnat sequens littera; dicitur enim infra eodem[^p271-16], quod *descendit Capharnaum,* et quod non mansit ibi diu, quia erat proximum Pascha: ergo nuptiae istae factae fuerunt prope Pascha, non ergo in Epiphania. — Quod si conceditur, in contrarium est mos Ecclesiae, quia Ecclesia eodem die tria festa celebrat.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod, sicut dictum est, hic sunt diversae opiniones, et utraque probabilis et sustineri potest. Si enim velimus sustinere, quod
<!-- page 272 -->
factum fuerit hoc miraculum post baptismum Domini et reditum eius de deserto et adventum in Galilaeam[^p272-1]; dicemus, quod fuit prope Pascha. — Nec obstat mos Ecclesiae, quia Ecclesia simul ista tria celebrat de stella et columba et vino mutato, quia Dominus in his tribus primo innotuit; vel quia aquae mutatio significat vim regenerativam aquis collatam, Domino baptizato[^p272-2]. — Si autem velimus sustinere aliam opinionem, respondebimus, quod cum dicitur, quod *post haec*[^p272-3] *descendit Capharnaum,* intelligendum, quod non statim *post haec;* sed aliquanto tempore interposito, ita quod Pascha appropinquabat.

15. Quaest. II. Quaestio est de eo cuius fuerunt nuptiae. — Et quod fuerunt *Ioannis,* dicitur in Prologo supra[^p272-4], et Augustinus dixit in Glossa similiter super principium. — Sed *contra* hoc opponit Victor[^p272-5]: « Cum virginitas sit perpetuae incorruptionis meditatio, non est credendum, quod Ioannes, cui testimonium de virginitate perhibetur, unquam consenserit in actum carnis ».

Respondendum ad hoc, quod quia maior auctoritas in hoc concurrit, tenendum, quod fuerunt Ioannis[^p272-6]. Et huius signum potest haberi ex littera: quia Ioannes erat consanguineus Domini, et ob hanc causam ipse et Mater eius ad nuptias sunt vocati[^p272-7].

Quod ergo obiicit Victor, dupliciter solvitur: uno modo, quod non consenserit in carnis actum sive copulam carnalem absolute; sed, sicut Maria Virgo, dispositioni sancti Spiritus se commisit, ut sic virgo, qui erat Virginem servaturus, in modo virginitatis cum Virgine conveniret[^p272-8]. — Aliter respondetur, quod esto, quod consenserit absolute, *virtus* non est minuta, quia licite consensit; similiter *dignitas virginitatis,* quia, cum virginitas dicat incorruptionem mentis et carnis, potest tripliciter perdi: aut perditur incorruptio *utriusque,* et tunc perditur aeternaliter et temporaliter; aut perditur incorruptio *mentis tantum,* et tunc perditur recuperabiliter; aut incorruptio *corporis tantum,* et tunc perditur temporaliter, sed non aeternaliter, quia gloriam virginitatis habebit; sed tamen, sicut dicit Leo Papa[^p272-9], non debent tales inter sacras virgines se computare. Quia igitur Ioannes carne mansit incorruptus, etsi mens consentiret, postmodum in idem rediit.

16. Quaest. III. Quaeritur de hoc, quod Dominus voluit ire ad nuptias. — Et videtur, quod non debuerit. Si enim locus nuptiarum est locus comessationis; et ipse Dominus omnem comessationem debuit fugere: ergo nec illuc ire.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod ivit, ut *fidem* astrueret et *haeresim* exstirparet. *Fidem* discipulorum vocatorum firmavit per miraculum, fidem etiam sponsi adeptus est. *Haeresim* exstirpavit, scilicet Iuliani[^p272-10], qui nuptias damnat. Quocontra Dominus voluit esse praesens et nuptias firmare et approbare miraculo. Si vero Dominus vocatus ire recusasset, tunc videretur haeresi illi favisse et nuptias damnasse.

17. Quaest. IV. Quaeritur de responsione Domini ad Matrem: *Quid mihi et tibi est, mulier? Nondum venit hora mea.*

1. Aut enim divina[^p272-11] Virgo petebat *petendum,* aut *non petendum.* Si petebat *petendum:* ergo non debuit ei ita acriter respondere. Si petebat *non petendum:* ergo petentem non debuit exaudire.

2. Item, quid est quod dicit: *Nondum venit hora?* Aut enim *venit,* et ita falsum dicit; aut *non venit,* et ita male facit, quod ante horam facit.

3. Item, ex hoc arguunt haeretici, quod Dominus fuit subiectus fato et astris[^p272-12].
<!-- page 273 -->

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Domini responsio non fuit *insultatoria,* sed *instructoria.* Ostendit enim, qua ratione Matri petenti satisfaciat. Unde in petitione tria considerantur: *petens,* et haec est Mater Domini; *pro quibus* petit, quia pro consanguineis pauperibus; *quid* petit, quia miraculum. Dominus ostendit in responsione, quod hoc non debebat petere ut *mater;* quia non ex natura ab ipsa tracta hoc poterat, ideo dicit ei *mulier,* non dicit *mater*[^p273-1]. Non debebat petere pro *consanguineis,* tanquam curam habens parentum carnalium; ideo dicit: *Quid mihi et tibi est?* Ostendit, quod miraculum non est faciendum propter indigentiam, sed ad manifestandam suam gloriam, cuius manifestandae nondum erat necessitas; ideo dicit: *Nondum venit hora mea.* Quia tamen petens mulier *sancta* erat; quia illi, pro quibus petebat, *pauperes* erant; quia discipulis *gloria* manifestanda erat: ideo exaudit eam.

2. Quod obiicitur de hora, intelligendum, nondum venerat hora perfectae manifestationis, qualis fuit circa mortem: de hora, dicit Augustinus[^p273-2], non qua cogeretur mori, sed qua dignaretur occidi; unde infra decimo: *Potestatem habeo ponendi animam meam, et nemo eam tollit a me.*

## English
<!-- page 269 -->

### Chapter II.

1. *And the third day*[^p269-2] *a marriage* etc. After the calling of the disciples has been described, there is described here the *manifestation* made through the Word and concerning the Word incarnate; whence the Gloss[^p269-3] says that after the showings made through John, the Lord himself showed himself by a miracle. — This manifestation, therefore, the Evangelist describes in this order. First is intimated the *occasion* of working the miracle; second, the *obtaining* of the miracle by petition; third, the wonderful *conversion* of water into wine; fourth, the *approval* of the miracle; fifth, the *manifestation* of Christ and the *edification* of the disciples, to whom the Lord deigned to manifest himself through this miracle.

(Verse 1.). First, then, is intimated the *occasion of working the miracle,* which was that the Lord was present at the marriage with his disciples. On account of which he says: *And the third day,* namely after the calling of the disciples[^p269-4], *there was a marriage in Cana of Galilee;* the place is touched upon. *And the Mother of Jesus was there;* it is not said that she was invited, because by reason of kinship she went as one ready to serve, as she also went into the hill country to Elizabeth to serve her, as is said in Luke, the first[^p269-5]. By reason of her the Lord too was invited. Therefore he says:

2. (Verse 2.). *And Jesus also was invited, and his disciples, to the marriage.* Chrysostom[^p269-6]: « He was invited to the marriage, not as someone great, but as a kinsman, and he himself, as humble, did not spurn the invitation, but went », according to what Luke, the fourteenth, teaches: « When thou art invited to a wedding, go, sit down in the lowest place ».

3. (Verse 3.). *And the wine failing.* Here is touched the second, namely the *obtaining of the miracle by petition,* which was made by the Mother pitying the bridegroom's want. Therefore he says: *And the wine failing, the Mother of Jesus saith to him: They have no wine;* as if to say: Let thy abundance supply their want; for she knew that he was « rich in mercy[^p269-7] » and in power, although he appeared needy; II Corinthians, the eighth: « Being rich in all things, he became needy for us ». But the Lord, intimating to his Mother that she could not command this according as she was mother, nor could he do it according as he was her son, nor ought he to do it except when the proper time was at hand; hence there follows:

4. (Verse 4.). *And Jesus saith to her: What is that to me and to thee, woman?* The Gloss[^p269-8]: « That is, what nature is common to me and to thee, according to which I should do this »? He calls her *woman,* not from frailty, but from nature and sex. For this is that woman of whom Proverbs, the last[^p269-9]: « Who shall find a valiant woman »? As if to say: This thou art able to ask as a holy woman, not to command as a mother, nor is it yet fitting to do it. *My hour is not yet come,* that is, the hour of my glorification and passion, of which below, the seventeenth: « Father, the hour is come, glorify thy Son ». But the Mother, knowing that this answer was not from indignation, but from humility and instruction, confidently gives command; whence there follows:

5. (Verse 5.). *His Mother saith to the waiters: Whatsoever he shall say to you, do ye.* For she knew that he could; she knew too that he knew; she knew that he willed, because he always obeyed her; whence Luke, the second[^p269-10]: « He went down with them and came to Nazareth and was subject to them », Jesus, namely, to his parents; and from this she was certain that he would do it. Therefore she says: *Whatsoever he shall say to you, do ye,* as good waiters, that you may obtain the miracle, as also Naaman, IV Kings, the fifth[^p269-11]: « Father, if the Prophet had bid thee do some great thing, thou shouldst have done it ». *Do ye whatsoever he shall say to you;* do not distrust; below, the eleventh: « If thou believe, thou shalt see the glory of God ».

6. (Verse 6.). *Now there were six waterpots* etc. Here is touched the third, namely the *working of the miracle.* And because God makes all things in definite « weight, number and measure[^p269-12] », he states the measure in which this miracle was made. On account of which he says: *Now there were set there six waterpots of stone. Waterpots* are vessels for receiving water; Genesis, the twenty-fourth[^p269-13]: « Give me
<!-- page 270 -->
a little water of thy pitcher ». These waterpots were *set according to the purifying of the Jews,* that is, according to the custom of the Jews for purifying themselves. Of this custom, Mark, the seventh[^p270-1]: « And when they come from the market, unless they be washed, they eat not; and many other things there are that have been delivered to them to observe, the washings of cups and of pots ». *Containing each two or three measures. Metreta* ("measure") is so called from *metron,* which is measure[^p270-2], and here is called a common and customary measure, as at Paris we speak of a sextary of wine. According to these measures, then, the miracle was made at the Lord's command; on account of which he adds:

7. (Verse 7.). *Jesus saith to them: Fill the waterpots with water. And they filled them up to the brim.* And it is to be understood that when they had been filled, by the power of the Lord the water was converted into wine; therefore he commanded that they *be filled,* that he might make a full work, according to what is said of the founding of things, Genesis, the first[^p270-3]: « God saw all the things that he had made, and they were very good »; and Deuteronomy, the thirty-second: « The works of God are perfect ».

8. (Verse 8.). *And Jesus said*[^p270-4] *to them.* Here is touched the fourth, namely the *approval of the miracle,* in the approval of the wine made by the testimony of the chief steward, whose part it was to approve the wine. On account of which he says: *And Jesus said to them,* namely to the waiters: *Draw out now, and carry to the chief steward of the feast. And they carried it,* according to his command; namely, he commanded it to be carried to him for this reason, because the *architriclinus* ("chief steward") presided over the marriage and judged of things of this kind. « *Triclinium,* that is, three rows of those reclining, distant in height[^p270-5] »; hence *architriclinus,* because he presided over the banquet of the order of three tables; or *architriclinus,* the chief of three chambers; or generally, one who is set over those serving, as Chrysostom says[^p270-6]. He commanded it to be carried to him, that he might judge of the wine; on account of which he says:

9. (Verse 9.). *And when the chief steward had tasted the water made wine,* that is, wine made from water, *and knew not whence it was, but the waiters knew who had drawn the water; the chief steward calleth the bridegroom,* namely to reprove him and to commend and extol the wine.

10. (Verse 10.). *And saith to him: Every man at first setteth forth good wine; at first,* that is, at the beginning; *and when they have become drunk, then that which is worse,* because then they are not able to discern, but call every wine good. *But thou hast kept the good wine until now;* that is, until the end, and so thou hast acted inordinately and against custom. This was a bad custom, because it was against truth and against sobriety; whence Ephesians, the fifth[^p270-7]: « Be not drunk with wine, wherein is luxury, but be ye filled with the Holy Spirit, speaking » etc.

11. (Verse 11.). *This beginning of signs.* Here is touched the fifth, namely the *manifestation of the divine glory* and the *edification of the faith of the disciples.* By reason of *his manifestation* he says: *This beginning of signs did Jesus in Cana of Galilee, and manifested his glory;* the Gloss[^p270-8]: « That is, his hidden Divinity »; below, the fourteenth: « I will love him and will manifest myself to him ». — By reason of the *edification of the disciples* he says: *And his disciples believed in him,* that is, believed more firmly. Bede[^p270-9]: « As the Lord reclined at the marriage, the wine failed, so that, a better wine being made by him in a wonderful order, the hidden glory of God might be manifested in him, and the faith of those believing in him, being increased, might advance ».

12. *Allegorically:* The *day* is threefold according to a threefold time, namely of nature, of the Law and of grace. On this *third* a marriage was made between Christ and the Church in the assumption of flesh, of which Matthew, the twenty-second[^p270-10]: *The kingdom of heaven is likened to a king, who made a marriage for his son.* In this marriage *six waterpots of water,* insipid and without nourishment, were converted into wine savoury and pleasant: because the *shadow of the Law* into truth; Hebrews, the tenth[^p270-11]: *The Law having a shadow of the good things to come* etc. — The *variety of sacrifices* into unity; Hebrews, the tenth: *Every priest indeed standeth daily ministering, and often offering the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins: but this man offering one sacrifice for sins* etc. — The *harshness of penalties* into lightness; Acts, the fifteenth[^p270-12]: *Why tempt you God, to put a yoke upon the necks of the disciples* etc.? — *Riddles* into light; II Corinthians, the third: *Even until this day the veil is put upon their heart, but we, beholding the glory* [so printed: *glorium* for *gloriam*] *of the Lord with open face* etc. — *Terrors* into love; Romans, the eighth[^p270-13]: *You have not received the spirit of bondage again in fear, but
<!-- page 271 -->
you have received the spirit of adoption of sons* etc. — *Promises* into attainment; Luke, the first[^p271-1], in the canticle of Zachary: *Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, because he hath visited and wrought the redemption of his people.* — And these things were done at the prayers of the Virgin, she herself interceding; Luke, the first: *Fear not, Mary; for thou hast found grace with God.*

13. *Morally: The marriage* is that of God and the soul in the very reconciliation of the soul to God, of which Osee, the second[^p271-2]: *I will espouse thee to me in faith, and thou shalt know that I am the Lord.* — To make this marriage *the Lord Jesus comes,* but invited; Apocalypse, the third[^p271-3]: *Behold, I stand at the gate and knock; if any man shall hear my voice and open to me the door, I will come in to him and will sup with him.* — The *wine* in this marriage *fails;* this wine is interior devotion, of which in the Psalm[^p271-4]. *Wine maketh glad* [so printed: *laetificatcor* for *laetificat cor*] *the heart of man;* and Matthew, the ninth: *Neither do they put new wine into old bottles.* That wine *fails* when a man is made dry and undevout, as the holy soul says in the Psalm: *My soul is as earth without water unto thee.* — But at the prayers of the Virgin, who has compassion on the wretched, God *fills the waterpots with the water* of compunction, which is converted into the sweetness of devotion; whence in Exodus, the fifteenth[^p271-5], it is said that the waters of Marath *were turned into sweetness.*

Note, therefore, that the *waterpot,* in which water is drawn, is consideration; the *empty waterpot* is the consideration of defect and of emptiness[^p271-6], which a man incurs through sin. This waterpot *is filled with water* when a man from such consideration is suffused with tears. — The first waterpot, then, is the consideration of the defect of *knowledge;* Tobias, the fifth[^p271-7]: *What manner of joy can be to me, who sit in darkness and see not the light*[^tr-lumen] *of heaven?* — The second waterpot, the consideration of the *defect of circumspection* concerning the enemy; II Corinthians, the eleventh[^p271-8]: *But I fear lest, as the serpent seduced Eve by his subtilty, so your minds should be corrupted and fall from the simplicity that is in Christ.* — The third is the consideration of the defect of *interior consolation;* Jeremias, the second[^p271-9]: *See how evil and bitter a thing it is for thee to have left the Lord thy God.* — The fourth is the consideration of the defect of *power to resist:* the Psalm[^p271-10]: *My power hath left me, and the light of my eyes itself is not with me.* — The fifth is the consideration of the defect of *stability to persevere;* Genesis, the eighth[^p271-11]: *The imagination and thought of man's heart are prone to evil from his youth.* — The sixth is the consideration of the defect of *security in waiting;* Ecclesiastes, the ninth[^p271-12]: *Man knoweth not whether he be worthy of love, or hatred.*

Then these waterpots *are filled with water up to the brim* when the penitent soul every night washes its bed from these considerations[^p271-13]. And from the water there *becomes the best wine* of devotion, of which Zacharias, the ninth: *For what is the good thing of him, and what is his beautiful thing, but the corn of the elect, and wine springing forth virgins?* Into this the water of compunction is converted; whence the holy soul in the Psalm[^p271-14]: *Thou hast turned for me my mourning into joy.*

### Questions.

14. A question arises here among the expositors.

Quest. I. First, concerning the *time* of the marriage; for it is said by some that it was made on the same day on which the Lord was baptized. But how can this stand, since it is said that *on the third day it was made,* after the Lord came from Judaea?

They answer: on the same day, a year having come round[^p271-15]. — But then the following text is against it; for it is said below in the same chapter[^p271-16] that *he went down to Capharnaum,* and that he did not stay there long, because the Pasch was near: therefore this marriage was made near the Pasch, not therefore at the Epiphany. — And if this is conceded, the custom of the Church is to the contrary, because the Church celebrates three feasts on the same day.

**I respond:** It must be said that, as has been said, there are different opinions here, and each is probable and can be sustained. For if we wish to sustain that
<!-- page 272 -->
this miracle was done after the baptism of the Lord and his return from the desert and his coming into Galilee[^p272-1], we shall say that it was near the Pasch. — Nor does the custom of the Church stand in the way, because the Church celebrates these three together, of the star and of the dove and of the wine changed, because in these three the Lord first became known; or because the changing of the water signifies the regenerative force conferred on the waters when the Lord was baptized[^p272-2]. — But if we wish to sustain the other opinion, we shall answer that when it is said that *after this*[^p272-3] *he went down to Capharnaum,* it is to be understood not immediately *after this,* but after some time had intervened, so that the Pasch was drawing near.

15. Quest. II. The question is about whose the marriage was. — And that it was *John's* is said in the Prologue above[^p272-4], and Augustine said likewise in the Gloss on the beginning. — But Victor objects *against* this[^p272-5]: « Since virginity is the meditation of perpetual incorruption, it is not to be believed that John, to whom testimony of virginity is given, ever consented to the act of the flesh ».

It must be answered to this that, because the greater authority concurs on this, it is to be held that it was John's[^p272-6]. And a sign of this can be had from the text: because John was a kinsman of the Lord, and for this cause he and his Mother were invited to the marriage[^p272-7].

What Victor objects, then, is solved in two ways: in one way, that he did not consent to the act of the flesh or carnal union absolutely; but, as the Virgin Mary committed herself to the disposition of the Holy Spirit, so he, a virgin, who was to keep the Virgin, consented in the manner of virginity to come together with a virgin[^p272-8]. — It is answered otherwise, that, granting that he consented absolutely, his *virtue* was not diminished, because he consented licitly; likewise the *dignity of virginity,* because, since virginity means incorruption of mind and of flesh, it can be lost in three ways: either the incorruption *of both* is lost, and then it is lost eternally and temporally; or the incorruption *of the mind only* is lost, and then it is lost recoverably; or the incorruption *of the body only,* and then it is lost temporally, but not eternally, because she will have the glory of virginity; but nevertheless, as Pope Leo says[^p272-9], such should not count themselves among the sacred virgins. Because, therefore, John remained incorrupt in the flesh, even if the mind consented, it afterwards returned to the same.

16. Quest. III. It is asked about this, that the Lord willed to go to the marriage. — And it seems that he ought not. For the place of a marriage is a place of revelling; and the Lord himself ought to have fled all revelling: therefore neither ought he to go there.

**I respond:** It must be said that he went to build up *faith* and to root out *heresy.* He strengthened the *faith* of the disciples who had been called through the miracle; he also gained the faith of the bridegroom. He rooted out the *heresy,* namely that of Julian[^p272-10], who condemns marriage. Against which the Lord willed to be present and to strengthen marriage and to approve it by a miracle. But if the Lord, when invited, had refused to go, then he would have seemed to favour that heresy and to have condemned marriage.

17. Quest. IV. It is asked about the Lord's answer to his Mother: *What is that to me and to thee, woman? My hour is not yet come.*

1. For either the divine[^p272-11] Virgin was asking for *what was to be asked,* or for *what was not to be asked.* If she was asking for *what was to be asked:* then he ought not to have answered her so sharply. If she was asking for *what was not to be asked:* then he ought not to have heard her as she asked.

2. Likewise, what is it that he says: *My hour is not yet come?* For either it *has come,* and so he says what is false; or it *has not come,* and so he does ill, because he acts before the hour.

3. Likewise, from this the heretics argue that the Lord was subject to fate and to the stars[^p272-12].
<!-- page 273 -->

**I respond:** It must be said that the Lord's answer was not *insulting,* but *instructive.* For he shows by what reasoning he satisfies his Mother as she asks. Whence in a petition three things are considered: *the one asking,* and this is the Mother of the Lord; *for whom* she asks, since for poor kinsfolk; *what* she asks, since a miracle. The Lord showed in his answer that she ought not to ask this as *mother;* because he did not have power for this from the nature drawn from her, therefore he says to her *woman,* he does not say *mother*[^p273-1]. She ought not to ask for *kinsfolk,* as one having care of fleshly relatives; therefore he says: *What is that to me and to thee?* He shows that a miracle is not to be done on account of need, but to manifest his glory, of whose manifesting there was not yet necessity; therefore he says: *My hour is not yet come.* Because, however, the woman asking was *holy;* because those for whom she was asking were *poor;* because glory was to be manifested to the disciples: therefore he hears her.

2. As to what is objected about the hour, it is to be understood that the hour of the perfect manifestation had not yet come, such as was about the time of his death: of the hour, Augustine says[^p273-2], not that in which he would be compelled to die, but that in which he would deign to be slain; whence below, the tenth: *I have power to lay down my life, and no man taketh it away from me.*
