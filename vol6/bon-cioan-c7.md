### Capitulum VII. *Post haec autem ambulavit.*

### Collatio XXX.

(Vers. 7.). *Me autem odit mundus.*

1. Notandum, quod mundus Christum *ignoravit* ut caecus — *odivit* ut invidus — *occidit* ut sacrilegus. Fuit *caecus* mundus in *ignorando;* Ioannis primo[^p570-1]: « Mundus per ipsum factus est, et mundus eum non cognovit ». — *Invidus* in *odiendo;* Ioannis decimo quinto: « Si mundus vos odit, scitote, quia me priorem vobis odio habuit ». — *Sacrilegus* in *occidendo;* Matthaei vigesimo primo dicunt mundani: « Hic est heres; venite, occidamus eum, et habebimus hereditatem eius ». Ideo « qui voluerit esse amicus huius saeculi inimicus Dei constituitur », Iacobi quarto. « Nolite ergo diligere mundum, neque ea quae in mundo sunt », primae Ioannis secundo.

2. *Mundus,* inquit, *me odit.* Odivit eum sicut *invidus,* quia Christus *magnus* erat in mundo; odivit eum sicut *adversarius,* quia Christus erat *contrarius* mundo; odivit eum sicut *mali conscius,* quia Christus erat *alienus* a mundo. — Christus ergo *magnus* reputabatur, et ideo odivit eum mundus; Ioannis decimo quinto[^p570-2]: « Si opera non fecissem in eis, quae nemo alius fecit, peccatum non haberent. Nunc autem viderunt et oderunt me et Patrem meum ». — Item, Christus fuit mundo *contrarius,* et ideo odivit eum mundus; unde hic: *Me autem odit mundus.* — Item, Christus fuit a mundo *alienus,* et ideo suspectus habebatur a mundo; propter quod odiebat eum mundus; Ioannis decimo quinto[^p570-3]: « Si de mundo fuissetis, mundus quod suum erat diligeret »; nunc autem, « quia vero non estis de mundo, propterea odit vos mundus »; et de se ipso dicit: « Si mundus vos odit, scitote, quia me priorem vobis odio habuit ».

### Collatio XXXI.

(Vers. 24.). *Nolite iudicare secundum faciem, sed iustum iudicium iudicate.*

1. In verbis istis *interdicit* Dominus *iudicium malum,* cum dicit: *Nolite iudicare secundum faciem* — et *commendat iustum iudicium,* cum subiungit: *Sed iustum iudicium* etc.

*Nolite iudicare secundum faciem.* Nota, quod multiplex est iudicium *malum.* Est enim iudicium *temerarium,* et hoc est malum propter defectum *scientiae;* Ioannis septimo[^p570-4]: « Nunquid Lex nostra iudicat hominem, nisi prius audierit ab ipso et cognoverit, quid faciat »? quasi dicat: iudicaret temerarie, si iudicaret sine causae cognitione. — Est etiam iudicium *praesumtuosum,* et hoc malum est propter defectum *vitae.* Non enim debet homo reprehensibilis alium iudicare; ad Romanos secundo[^p570-5]: « In quo alterum iudicas, te ipsum condemnas; eadem enim agis, quae iudicas ». — Est etiam iudicium *usurpativum,* et hoc est malum propter defectum *potestatis iudiciariae;* quod reprehendit Apostolus ad Romanos decimo quarto: « Tu quis es, qui iudicas alienum servum? Domino suo aut stat, aut cadit »; et primae ad Corinthios quarto: « Nolite ante tempus iudicare » etc. Et nota, quod hoc triplex iudicium habet defectum ex parte *iudicantis.*

2. Est etiam triplex iudicium malum, quod deficit ex parte *ordinis iudicandi,* utpote quando in iudicato non est causa iudicii. Iudicium ergo illud aliquando procedit usque ad *suspicionem* tantum, et appellatur iudicium *suspiciosum,* ut quando ex levi coniectura alius iudicat alium; de quo hic: *Nolite iudicare secundum faciem.* — Aliquando procedit usque ad *definitionem,* et appellatur iudicium *definitivum;* de quo Matthaei septimo[^p570-6]: « Nolite iudicare, et non iudicabimini; in quo enim iudicio iudicaveritis, iudicabimini ». — Aliquando procedit iudicium usque ad *punitionem,* et appellatur iudicium *iniquum;* de quo Actuum vigesimo tertio: « Percutiet te Deus, paries dealbate; et tu sedens iudicas me secundum Legem, et contra Legem iubes, me percuti »; et Isaiae quadragesimo nono: « Eos qui te iudicaverunt, ego iudicabo ». — Recollige ergo et attende, quod triplex est iudicium malum, ex eo quod in *iudicante* non est facultas iudicandi; et triplex est iudicium malum, ex eo quod non est in *iudicato* causa iudicii.

3. *Nolite iudicare secundum faciem.* — Nota, quod quatuor inducunt hominem ad male iudicandum. Primum est *timor,* quem inhibet Scriptura, Levitici decimo nono[^p570-7]: « Non consideres personam pauperis nec honores vultum potentis; iuste iudica
<!-- page 571 -->
proximo tuo ». — Secundum est *odium,* quod detestabatur Iob sexto: « Respondete, obsecro, absque contentione, et loquentes id quod iustum est, iudicate »; Iacobi tertio: « Sapientia, quae » etc. — Tertium est *dilectio* sive favor *popularis,* qui prohibetur Exodi vigesimo tertio[^p571-1]: « Pauperis non misereberis in iudicio »; et ibidem: « Non sequeris turbam ad faciendum malum, nec in iudicio plurimorum acquiesces sententiae, ut a vero devies ». — Quartum est *cupiditas,* quae prohibetur Exodi vigesimo tertio: « Non accipies munera, quae excaecant oculos prudentium et subvertunt verba iustorum »; et Ecclesiastici vigesimo: « Exenia et dona excaecant oculos iudicum ». — Quatuor ista continentur his versibus[^p571-2]:

> Quatuor ista: timor, odium, dilectio, census,
> Saepe solent hominum rectos pervertere sensus.

4. Sequitur: *Sed iustum iudicium iudicate.* — Hic *commendat* Dominus *iudicium iustum.* Ubi notandum est, quod ad iuste iudicandum[^p571-3] quaedam requiruntur in *iudice* — et quaedam in *iudicii ordine.* — Ex parte *iudicis* requiritur iudicis *sapientia* in cognitione, *iustitia* in intentione, *irreprehensibilitas* in conversatione, *auctoritas* in exsecutione.

5. Primo ergo requiritur *sapientia in cognitione,* ut ius scilicet noverit et causam sciat, quia sine hoc esset iudicium *temerarium.* Hanc postulabat Salomon tertii Regum tertio[^p571-4]: « Dabis, inquit, servo tuo cor docile, ut possit iudicare populum » etc.; et sequitur in eodem: « Qui timuerunt regem, videntes, sapientiam Dei esse in eo ad faciendum iudicium ».

6. Requiritur etiam *iustitia in intentione,* ut scilicet iudicet non libidine vindictae, vel irae, vel cuiusque malitiae, sed amore iustitiae, quia sine hoc esset iudicium *iniquum;* et ad hoc hortatur Scriptura secundi Paralipomenon decimo nono[^p571-5]: « Non exercetis, ait, iudicium hominis, sed Dei, et quodcumque iudicaveritis, in vos redundabit »; et adiungit: « Non enim est apud Deum nostrum iniquitas nec personarum acceptio nec cupido munerum ». Et ideo dicitur Sapientiae primo: « Diligite iustitiam qui iudicatis terram »; et in Psalmo: « Recta iudicate, filii hominum ».

7. Requiritur etiam *irreprehensibilitas in conversatione;* non enim congrue potest qui damnandus est, alium damnare; alioquin iudicium erit *praesumtuosum;* propter quod dicitur ad Romanos secundo[^p571-6]: « Inexcusabilis es, o homo, qui iudicas. In quo enim iudicas alterum, te ipsum condemnas; eadem enim agis, quae iudicas »; Matthaei septimo: « Hypocrita, eiice primum trabem de oculo tuo » etc.

8. Requiritur etiam *auctoritas in exsecutione,* sine qua esset etiam iudicium *usurpatum;* propter quod dicitur ad Romanos decimo quarto[^p571-7]: « Tu quis es, qui iudicas alienum servum »? et Lucae duodecimo: « Homo, quis constituit me iudicem super vos aut divisorem »? quasi dicat: non debes iudicare non constitutus a superiore. — Haec ergo quatuor requiruntur in iudice.

9. Sunt et alia quatuor, quae requiruntur in *iudicii exsecutione,* scilicet *inquisitio* diligens, *definitio* iusta, *exsecutio* constans, *punitio* modesta. — Requiritur *inquisitio diligens;* unde Iob vigesimo nono[^p571-8]: « Causam, quam nesciebam, diligentissime investigabam »; et secundi Paralipomenon decimo nono dicitur iudicibus: « Cum diligentia cuncta facite »; et Ioannis septimo: « Nunquid Lex nostra iudicat hominem, nisi prius audierit ab ipso et cognoverit, quid faciat »? — Requiritur *definitio iusta;* Levitici decimo nono: « Non consideres personam pauperis »; et sequitur: « Iuste iudica proximo tuo ». — Requiritur *exsecutio constans;* Ecclesiastici septimo[^p571-9]: « Noli quaerere fieri iudex, nisi virtute valeas irrumpere iniquitatem, ne forte extimescas faciem potentis ». — Requiritur *punitio modesta;* Ecclesiastae septimo: « Noli esse iustus multum, neque plus sapias, quam necesse est »; et ideo statuitur modus poenae Deuteronomii vigesimo quinto, cum dicitur: « Pro mensura peccati erit et plagarum modus; ita dumtaxat, ut quadragenarium numerum non excedat ».

### Collatio XXXII.

(Vers. 34.). *Quaeretis me et non invenietis.*

1. Nota, quod a bonis quaeritur Christus et invenitur ab illis; quaeritur autem, sed non invenitur a reprobis, quibus hic dicitur: *Quaeretis me et non invenietis.* — Quare autem non invenitur Dominus a quaerentibus, causa triplex est: una est ex parte *temporis* — alia, ex parte *loci* — tertia, ex parte *modi quaerendi.* Quaerunt ergo et non inveniunt illi qui quaerunt, non *quando* inveniri potest — et qui quaerunt, non *ubi* inveniri potest — et qui quaerunt, non *qualiter* inveniri potest.

2. Quaerunt, non *quando* inveniri potest, qui non quaerunt *de die,* vel qui non quaerunt *continue,* vel qui non quaerunt *tempestive.* — Quaerunt
<!-- page 572 -->
ergo et non inveniunt qui non quaerunt *de die gratiae,* sed in nocte culpae; unde Canticorum tertio[^p572-1]: « Per noctes quaesivi quem diligit anima mea »; et sequitur: « Quaesivi et non inveni ». Nec mirum, quia quaesivit obscuro tempore. — Quaerunt et non inveniunt qui quaerunt *interrupte,* cum ipse sit quaerendus *continue;* unde in Psalmo: « Quaerite Dominum et confirmamini; quaerite faciem eius semper ». Nota fabulam de vetula, quae dicebat: Quaere pulcrum maritum. — Quaerunt et non inveniunt qui non quaerunt *tempestive,* sicut illa, de qua Canticorum quinto[^p572-2]: « Pessulum ostii mei aperui dilecto meo; at ille declinaverat atque transierat »; et sequitur: « Quaesivi et non inveni illum »; et Ioannis septimo dicitur: « Adhuc modicum tempus vobiscum sum, et vado ad eum qui me misit »; et subiungit: *Quaeretis me et non invenietis;* quasi dicat: quia tarde quaeritis. — Propter haec tria dicitur Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto: « Quaerite Dominum, dum inveniri potest ».

3. Quaerunt, non *ubi* inveniri potest, qui quaerunt in *lecto carnalis voluptatis,* vel qui in *foro mundanae negotiationis,* vel qui in *choro pompae saecularis.* — Quaeritur ergo in *lecto carnalis voluptatis;* Canticorum tertio[^p572-3]: « In lectulo meo quaesivi quem diligit anima mea »; sed non invenitur, quia sequitur: « Quaesivi illum et non inveni »; unde Iob vigesimo octavo: « Non invenitur in terra suaviter viventium ». — Quaeritur in *foro mundanae negotiationis;* Canticorum tertio[^p572-4]: « Surgam et circuibo civitatem; per vicos et plateas quaeram quem diligit anima mea ». Expone de civitate mundi, de qua Psalmus: « Vidi iniquitatem et contradictionem in civitate ». Sed nec ibi invenitur; unde sequitur: « Quaesivi illum et non inveni »; Osee undecimo: « In medio tui Sanctus, et non ingrediar civitatem »; loquitur de se ipso Dominus. — Quaeritur in *choro pompae saecularis;* Lucae secundo[^p572-5]: « Existimantes, illum esse in comitatu, venerunt iter diei unius, et requirebant eum inter cognatos et notos »; sed nec ibi invenitur; unde sequitur: « Et non invenientes regressi sunt in Ierusalem ». — Propter haec tria corripit Dominus quaerentes se Lucae secundo: « Quid est, quod me quaerebatis? Nesciebatis, quia in his quae Patris mei sunt oportet me esse »?

4. Quaerunt et non inveniunt qui non quaerunt, *qualiter* inveniri potest, ut in quibus est *evagatio rationis,* vel *obliquitas intentionis,* vel *remissio operis.* — Non inveniunt in quibus est *evagatio rationis,* de quibus Osee quinto[^p572-6]: « In gregibus suis et in armentis suis vadent ad quaerendum Dominum »; supple: per curiosam cogitationem; « et non invenient; ablatus est ab eis »; Sapientiae primo: « In simplicitate cordis quaerite Dominum, quoniam invenitur ab his qui non tentant illum ». — Item, non invenitur ab his, in quibus est *obliquitas intentionis,* sive sit intentio *maligna,* qualem habuit Herodes; Matthaei secundo[^p572-7]: « Futurum est enim, ut Herodes quaerat puerum ad perdendum eum »; sive sit intentio *carnalis,* qualem habebant turbae; Ioannis sexto: « Quaeritis me, non quia vidistis signa, sed quia de panibus manducastis »; sive sit *curiosa,* qualem habebant Pharisaei, Ioannis undecimo, « qui quaerebant Iesum et colloquebantur ad invicem in templo stantes: Quid putatis, quia non venit ad diem festum »? — Item, non invenitur ab his, in quibus est *remissio operis;* propter quod dicitur Lucae decimo tertio[^p572-8]: « Contendite intrare per angustam portam, quia dico vobis, multi quaerent intrare et non poterunt ». Propter hoc dicitur Isaiae vigesimo primo: « Si quaeritis, quaerite; convertimini et venite ».

### Annotatio 28.

(Vers. 16.). *Doctrina mea non est mea.*

Nota, qualiter docet nos vitare arrogantiam, qui, cum exhibuerit nobis in se ipso sapientiam, potentiam et iustitiam, attribuit tamen Patri omnium istorum honorem. — Patri attribuit honorem *sapientiae;* unde hic: *Mea doctrina non est mea, sed eius qui misit me,* Patris; et infra octavo[^p572-9]: « Sicut docuit me Pater, haec loquor ». — Patri attribuit honorem *potentiae;* unde Ioannis quinto: « Non potest Filius a se quidquam facere, nisi quod viderit Patrem facientem »; et infra octavo: « A me ipso facio nihil ». — Patri attribuit honorem *iustitiae;* unde Ioannis quinto: « Non possum ego a me ipso facere quidquam, sed sicut audio, iudico ».

### Annotatio 29.

(Vers. 18.). *Qui a semetipso loquitur gloriam propriam quaerit.*

Et hoc malum est, quia gloria propria est gloria *vana,* gloria *vilis* et *momentanea.* — *Vana;* unde Isaiae quadragesimo[^p572-10]: « Omnis caro foenum, et omnis gloria eius quasi flos foeni ». — *Vilis;* primi Machabaeorum secundo: « A verbis viri peccatoris ne timueris, quia gloria eius stercus et vermis est ».
<!-- page 573 -->
— *Momentanea;* unde in Psalmo: « Ne timueris, cum dives factus fuerit homo, et cum multiplicata fuerit gloria domus eius; quoniam, cum interierit, non sumet omnia, neque descendet cum eo gloria eius ».

### Annotatio 30.

(Vers. 38.). *Flumina de ventre eius fluent aquae vivae.*

Nota, quod gratia Dei dicitur *aqua viva,* quia vitam *tribuit,* vitam *custodit,* ad vitam *perducit.* — Vitam *tribuit;* Ezechielis quadragesimo septimo[^p573-1]: « Omnia, ad quae pervenerit torrens, vivent ». — Vitam *custodit;* Numerorum vigesimo: « Aperi nobis thesaurum tuum, fontem aquae vivae, ut satiati, cesset murmuratio eorum ». — Ad vitam *perducit;* Ioannis quarto: « Fiet in eo fons aquae salientis in vitam aeternam ».

### Annotatio 31.

(Vers. 46.). *Nunquam sic locutus est homo, sicut hic homo.*

*Nunquam locutus est sic* potenter; Matthaei septimo[^p573-2]: « Admirabantur turbae super doctrina eius; erat enim docens eos sicut potestatem habens, non sicut scribae et Pharisaei ». — Item, *nunquam locutus est homo sic* sapienter; Lucae secundo: « Stupebant autem omnes, qui eum audiebant, super prudentia et responsis eius »; Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto: « Ego sapientia effudi flumina ». — Item, *nunquam locutus est homo sic* dulciter; quam dulcedinem sentiens, « mulier extollens vocem, dixit illi: Beatus venter, qui te portavit, et ubera, quae suxisti[^p573-3] ». — Item, *nunquam locutus est homo sic* utiliter; Ioannis sexto: « Verba, quae locutus sum vobis, spiritus et vita sunt ».

### Annotatio 32.

(Vers. 51.). *Nunquid Lex nostra iudicat hominem?*

Require supra de iudicio eodem capitulo septimo[^p573-4].

### Chapter VII. *Now after these things he walked.*

### Collation XXX.

(Verse 7.). *But me the world hateth.*

1. It is to be noted that the world *knew not* Christ as one blind — *hated* him as one envious — *killed* him as one sacrilegious. The world was *blind* in *not knowing;* John, the first chapter[^p570-1]: « The world was made by him, and the world knew him not ». — *Envious* in *hating;* John, the fifteenth chapter: « If the world hate you, know ye that it hath hated me before you ». — *Sacrilegious* in *killing;* in Matthew, the twenty-first chapter, the worldly say: « This is the heir; come, let us kill him, and we shall have his inheritance ». Therefore « whosoever will be a friend of this world is made an enemy of God », James, the fourth chapter. « Love not therefore the world, nor the things which are in the world », the first of John, the second chapter.

2. *The world,* he says, *hateth me.* It hated him as one *envious,* because Christ was *great* in the world; it hated him as an *adversary,* because Christ was *contrary* to the world; it hated him as one *conscious of evil,* because Christ was *estranged* from the world. — Christ therefore was reputed *great,* and so the world hated him; John, the fifteenth chapter[^p570-2]: « If I had not done among them the works that no other man hath done, they would not have sin. But now they have seen and hated both me and my Father ». — Likewise, Christ was *contrary* to the world, and so the world hated him; whence here: *But me the world hateth.* — Likewise, Christ was *estranged* from the world, and so he was held suspect by the world; on which account the world hated him; John, the fifteenth chapter[^p570-3]: « If you had been of the world, the world would love its own »; but now, « because you are not of the world, therefore the world hateth you »; and of himself he says: « If the world hate you, know ye that it hath hated me before you ».

### Collation XXXI.

(Verse 24.). *Judge not according to the appearance, but judge just judgment.*

1. In these words the Lord *forbids evil judgment,* when he says: *Judge not according to the appearance* — and *commends just judgment,* when he adds: *But judge just judgment* etc.

*Judge not according to the appearance.* Note that evil judgment is manifold. For there is *rash* judgment, and this is evil through a defect of *knowledge;* John, the seventh chapter[^p570-4]: « Doth our Law judge a man, unless it first hear him and know what he doth »? as if to say: it would judge rashly, if it judged without knowledge of the cause. — There is also *presumptuous* judgment, and this is evil through a defect of *life.* For a man who is reprehensible ought not to judge another; to the Romans, the second chapter[^p570-5]: « Wherein thou judgest another, thou condemnest thyself; for thou dost the same things which thou judgest ». — There is also *usurping* judgment, and this is evil through a defect of *judicial power;* which the Apostle reproves to the Romans, the fourteenth chapter: « Who art thou that judgest another man's servant? To his own lord he standeth or falleth »; and the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « Judge not before the time » etc. And note that this threefold judgment has a defect on the part of *the one judging.*

2. There is also a threefold evil judgment which fails on the part of *the order of judging,* as when in the one judged there is no cause for judgment. That judgment, then, sometimes proceeds only as far as *suspicion,* and is called *suspicious* judgment, as when from a slight conjecture one judges another; of which here: *Judge not according to the appearance.* — Sometimes it proceeds as far as *definition,* and is called *definitive* judgment; of which Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p570-6]: « Judge not, and you shall not be judged; for with what judgment you judge, you shall be judged ». — Sometimes judgment proceeds as far as *punishment,* and is called *unjust* judgment; of which Acts, the twenty-third chapter: « God shall strike thee, thou whited wall; and thou sitting judgest me according to the Law, and contrary to the Law commandest me to be struck »; and Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter: « Those that have judged thee, I will judge ». — Gather up therefore and attend: there is a threefold evil judgment from this, that in *the one judging* there is not the faculty of judging; and there is a threefold evil judgment from this, that in *the one judged* there is no cause for judgment.

3. *Judge not according to the appearance.* — Note that four things lead a man to judge wrongly. The first is *fear,* which Scripture forbids, Leviticus, the nineteenth chapter[^p570-7]: « Respect not the person of the poor, nor honour the countenance of the mighty; judge justly
<!-- page 571 -->
thy neighbour ». — The second is *hatred,* which Job detested, in Job, the sixth chapter: « Answer, I beseech you, without contention, and speaking that which is just, judge ye »; James, the third chapter: « The wisdom which » etc. — The third is *dilection* or *popular* favour, which is forbidden in Exodus, the twenty-third chapter[^p571-1]: « Thou shalt not pity the poor man in judgment »; and in the same place: « Thou shalt not follow the multitude to do evil, neither shalt thou yield in judgment to the opinion of the most part, to stray from the truth ». — The fourth is *cupidity,* which is forbidden in Exodus, the twenty-third chapter: « Thou shalt not take gifts, which blind the eyes of the prudent and pervert the words of the just »; and Ecclesiasticus, the twentieth chapter: « Presents and gifts blind the eyes of judges ». — These four are contained in these verses[^p571-2]:

> These four: fear, hatred, dilection, wealth,
> Are often wont to pervert the upright senses of men.

4. There follows: *But judge just judgment.* — Here the Lord *commends just judgment.* Where it is to be noted that for judging justly[^p571-3] certain things are required in *the judge* — and certain things in *the order of judgment.* — On the part of *the judge* there is required the judge's *wisdom* in cognition, *justice* in intention, *irreprehensibility* in conversation, *authority* in execution.

5. First, then, there is required *wisdom in cognition,* namely that he know the law and know the cause, because without this the judgment would be *rash.* This Solomon asked for in the third of Kings, the third chapter[^p571-4]: « Thou wilt give, he says, to thy servant a docile heart, that he may judge the people » etc.; and there follows in the same: « They feared the king, seeing that the wisdom of God was in him to do judgment ».

6. There is required also *justice in intention,* namely that he judge not from lust of vengeance, or of anger, or of any malice, but from love of justice, because without this the judgment would be *unjust;* and to this Scripture exhorts in the second of Paralipomenon, the nineteenth chapter[^p571-5]: « You exercise not, it says, the judgment of man, but of God, and whatsoever you judge, it shall redound to you »; and it adds: « For there is no iniquity with our God, nor respect of persons, nor desire of gifts ». And therefore it is said in Wisdom, the first chapter: « Love justice, you that judge the earth »; and in the Psalm: « Judge right things, ye sons of men ».

7. There is required also *irreprehensibility in conversation;* for he who is to be condemned cannot fittingly condemn another; otherwise the judgment will be *presumptuous;* on which account it is said to the Romans, the second chapter[^p571-6]: « Thou art inexcusable, O man, that judgest. For wherein thou judgest another, thou condemnest thyself; for thou dost the same things which thou judgest »; Matthew, the seventh chapter: « Thou hypocrite, cast out first the beam out of thy own eye » etc.

8. There is required also *authority in execution,* without which the judgment would also be *usurped;* on which account it is said to the Romans, the fourteenth chapter[^p571-7]: « Who art thou that judgest another man's servant »? and Luke, the twelfth chapter: « Man, who hath appointed me judge over you, or divider »? as if to say: you ought not to judge if not appointed by a superior. — These four, then, are required in the judge.

9. There are also four others which are required in *the execution of judgment,* namely diligent *inquiry,* just *definition,* constant *execution,* modest *punishment.* — There is required *diligent inquiry;* whence Job, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p571-8]: « The cause which I knew not, I searched out most diligently »; and in the second of Paralipomenon, the nineteenth chapter, it is said to the judges: « Do all things with diligence »; and John, the seventh chapter: « Doth our Law judge a man, unless it first hear him and know what he doth »? — There is required *just definition;* Leviticus, the nineteenth chapter: « Respect not the person of the poor »; and there follows: « Judge justly thy neighbour ». — There is required *constant execution;* Ecclesiasticus, the seventh chapter[^p571-9]: « Seek not to be made a judge, unless thou be able by strength to break through iniquity, lest perhaps thou fear the face of the powerful ». — There is required *modest punishment;* Ecclesiastes, the seventh chapter: « Be not over just, neither be more wise than is necessary »; and therefore the measure of punishment is fixed in Deuteronomy, the twenty-fifth chapter, when it is said: « According to the measure of the sin shall the measure also of the stripes be; yet so that they exceed not the number of forty ».

### Collation XXXII.

(Verse 34.). *You shall seek me, and shall not find me.*

1. Note that Christ is sought by the good and is found by them; but he is sought and not found by the reprobate, to whom it is said here: *You shall seek me, and shall not find me.* — Now why the Lord is not found by those who seek him, the cause is threefold: one is on the part of *time* — another, on the part of *place* — the third, on the part of *the manner of seeking.* They therefore seek and do not find who seek not *when* he can be found — and who seek not *where* he can be found — and who seek not *how* he can be found.

2. They seek not *when* he can be found who do not seek *by day,* or who do not seek *continually,* or who do not seek *in season.* — They
<!-- page 572 -->
therefore seek and do not find who seek not *in the day of grace,* but in the night of fault; whence Canticles, the third chapter[^p572-1]: « By night I sought him whom my soul loveth »; and there follows: « I sought, and found not ». No wonder, since she sought in a dark time. — They seek and do not find who seek *interruptedly,* since he is to be sought *continually;* whence in the Psalm: « Seek ye the Lord, and be strengthened; seek his face evermore ». Note the fable of the old woman who used to say: Seek a handsome husband. — They seek and do not find who do not seek *in season,* like her of whom Canticles, the fifth chapter[^p572-2]: « I opened the bolt of my door to my beloved; but he had turned aside and was gone »; and there follows: « I sought him, and found him not »; and in John, the seventh chapter, it is said: « Yet a little while I am with you, and I go to him that sent me »; and he adds: *You shall seek me, and shall not find me;* as if to say: because you seek late. — On account of these three it is said in Isaias, the fifty-fifth chapter: « Seek ye the Lord, while he may be found ».

3. They seek not *where* he can be found who seek in *the bed of carnal pleasure,* or who in *the market of worldly business,* or who in *the throng of worldly pomp.* — He is sought, then, in *the bed of carnal pleasure;* Canticles, the third chapter[^p572-3]: « In my bed I sought him whom my soul loveth »; but he is not found, because there follows: « I sought him, and found him not »; whence Job, the twenty-eighth chapter: « It is not found in the land of them that live in delights ». — He is sought in *the market of worldly business;* Canticles, the third chapter[^p572-4]: « I will rise, and will go about the city; in the streets and the broad ways I will seek him whom my soul loveth ». Expound it of the city of the world, of which the Psalm: « I have seen iniquity and contradiction in the city ». But neither is he found there; whence there follows: « I sought him, and found him not »; Osee, the eleventh chapter: « The Holy One in the midst of thee, and I will not enter into the city »; the Lord speaks of himself. — He is sought in *the throng of worldly pomp;* Luke, the second chapter[^p572-5]: « Thinking that he was in the company, they came a day's journey, and sought him among their kinsfolks and acquaintance »; but neither is he found there; whence there follows: « And not finding him, they returned into Jerusalem ». — On account of these three the Lord rebukes those seeking him, Luke, the second chapter: « How is it that you sought me? Did you not know that I must be in the things that are my Father's »?

4. They seek and do not find who seek not *how* he can be found, as those in whom there is *wandering of reason,* or *obliquity of intention,* or *slackness of work.* — They do not find in whom there is *wandering of reason,* of whom Osee, the fifth chapter[^p572-6]: « With their flocks and with their herds they shall go to seek the Lord »; supply: by curious thought; « and they shall not find him; he is taken away from them »; Wisdom, the first chapter: « In simplicity of heart seek the Lord, for he is found by them that tempt him not ». — Likewise, he is not found by those in whom there is *obliquity of intention,* whether the intention be *malign,* such as Herod had; Matthew, the second chapter[^p572-7]: « For it will come to pass that Herod will seek the child to destroy him »; or whether the intention be *carnal,* such as the crowds had; John, the sixth chapter: « You seek me, not because you have seen signs, but because you did eat of the loaves »; or whether it be *curious,* such as the Pharisees had, John, the eleventh chapter, « who sought Jesus and discoursed one with another, standing in the temple: What think you, that he is not come to the festival day »? — Likewise, he is not found by those in whom there is *slackness of work;* on which account it is said in Luke, the thirteenth chapter[^p572-8]: « Strive to enter by the narrow gate; for I say to you, many shall seek to enter, and shall not be able ». On account of this it is said in Isaias, the twenty-first chapter: « If you seek, seek; return, come ».

### Annotation 28.

(Verse 16.). *My teaching*[^tr-doctrina] *is not mine.*

Note how he teaches us to avoid arrogance, who, though he displayed to us in himself wisdom, power and justice, yet attributed to the Father the honour of all these. — He attributed to the Father the honour of *wisdom;* whence here: *My teaching is not mine, but his that sent me,* the Father's; and below, in the eighth chapter[^p572-9]: « As the Father hath taught me, these things I speak ». — He attributed to the Father the honour of *power;* whence John, the fifth chapter: « The Son cannot do any thing of himself, but what he seeth the Father doing »; and below, in the eighth chapter: « Of myself I do nothing ». — He attributed to the Father the honour of *justice;* whence John, the fifth chapter: « I cannot of myself do any thing, but as I hear, I judge ».

### Annotation 29.

(Verse 18.). *He that speaketh of himself seeketh his own glory.*

And this is evil, because one's own glory is glory *vain,* glory *vile* and *momentary.* — *Vain;* whence Isaias, the fortieth chapter[^p572-10]: « All flesh is grass, and all the glory thereof as the flower of the grass ». — *Vile;* the first of Machabees, the second chapter: « Fear not the words of a sinful man, for his glory is dung and worms ».
<!-- page 573 -->
— *Momentary;* whence in the Psalm: « Be not thou afraid, when a man shall be made rich, and when the glory of his house shall be increased; for when he shall die, he shall not take all things away, nor shall his glory descend with him ».

### Annotation 30.

(Verse 38.). *Out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water.*

Note that the grace of God is called *living water,* because it *gives* life, *guards* life, *leads* to life. — It *gives* life; Ezechiel, the forty-seventh chapter[^p573-1]: « All things to which the torrent shall come shall live ». — It *guards* life; Numbers, the twentieth chapter: « Open to us thy treasure, a fountain of living water, that being satisfied, their murmuring may cease ». — It *leads* to life; John, the fourth chapter: « There shall be in him a fountain of water springing up into life everlasting ».

### Annotation 31.

(Verse 46.). *Never did man so speak as this man.*

*Never did he speak so* powerfully; Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p573-2]: « The multitudes were in admiration at his teaching; for he was teaching them as one having power, and not as the scribes and Pharisees ». — Likewise, *never did man speak so* wisely; Luke, the second chapter: « And all that heard him were astonished at his prudence and his answers »; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter: « I, wisdom, have poured out rivers ». — Likewise, *never did man speak so* sweetly; perceiving which sweetness, « a woman, lifting up her voice, said to him: Blessed is the womb that bore thee, and the paps that thou didst suck[^p573-3] ». — Likewise, *never did man speak so* usefully; John, the sixth chapter: « The words that I have spoken to you are spirit and life ».

### Annotation 32.

(Verse 51.). *Doth our Law judge a man?*

Look above, concerning judgment, in the same seventh chapter[^p573-4].
