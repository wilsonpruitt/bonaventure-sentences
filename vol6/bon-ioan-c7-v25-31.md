# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. VII, Vers. 25-31
## *Confutavit malignantes, hic confutat obloquentes; et procedit hoc ordine*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 346 -->

36. *Dicebant ergo quidam ex Ierosolymis.* Confutavit malignantes, hic *confutat obloquentes;* et procedit hoc ordine. Primo ponitur ipsa *oblocutio;* secundo, *obloquentium confutatio;* tertio, *confutatorum obstinatio;* quarto, parvulorum seu *minorum aedificatio.*

(Vers. 25.). Primo ergo ponitur *oblocutio* illorum qui magis cognoscere debebant; et isti erant qui in civitate regia manebant; propter quod dicit: *Dicebant ergo quidam ex Ierosolymis,* qui conscii erant machinationis Pharisaeorum: *Nonne hic est, quem quaerebant Iudaei interficere*[^p346-10]? tanquam malum; erant enim de illis, de quibus Proverbiorum primo dicitur: « Pedes illorum ad malum currunt, et festinant, ut effundant sanguinem ».

37. (Vers. 26.). *Et ecce, palam loquitur, et nihil ei dicunt,* et ex hoc admirantur et ex admiratione, cum deberent excitari ad fidem, procedunt in mendacem oblocutionem: *Nunquid vere cognoverunt principes, quoniam*[^p346-11] *hic est Christus?* Quasi dicant: suspicari possunt, sed vere scire non possunt; unde subdit:

38. (Vers. 27.). *Sed hunc scimus, unde sit,* quia a Nazareth; supra primo[^p346-12]: « Invenimus Iesum, filium Ioseph a Nazareth ». *Christus autem cum venerit, nemo scit, unde sit;* et ita derogabant eius origini. Hoc dicebant propter illud quod scribitur Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio: « Generationem eius
<!-- page 347 -->
quis enarrabit »? quasi generatio Christi sit indicibilis, et ita imperscrutabilis; vel quia occulte venit; Sapientiae decimo octavo: « Dum medium silentium tenerent omnia » etc.

39. (Vers. 28.). *Clamabat ergo* etc. Hic ponitur secundum, scilicet *obloquentium confutatio.* Hos quidem obloquentes de Christi generatione confutat, duplicem suam ostendens generationem: *temporalem,* quam ipsi sciebant, et *aeternam,* quam ipse sciebat. Propter *temporalem* dicit: *Clamabat ergo Iesus, docens in templo,* ut attonitos faceret mussitantes; unde Proverbiorum primo[^p347-1]: « Sapientia foris clamitat, in plateis dat vocem suam »; quia aperte et libere docet; Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo: « Clama, ne cesses, quasi tuba exalta vocem tuam ». *Et dicens: Et me scitis, et unde sim scitis,* secundum generationem *temporalem;* unde Matthaei decimo tertio: « Mater eius dicitur Maria »; et hoc sciebant, quod erat homo et quod ex Maria natus; hoc dictum est secundum generationem *temporalem.* Sed quantum ad *aeternam* dicit, quod ipse est ab alio, quem ipsi nesciunt; propter quod dicit: *Et a me ipso non veni,* in quo notatur a Patre processio; *sed est verus, qui misit me,* quia Deus est; est autem Deus verax, omnis autem homo mendax, secundum illud Psalmi[^p347-2]: « Omnis homo mendax »; Numerorum vigesimo tertio: « Non est Deus ut homo, ut mentiatur ». *Quem vos nescitis;* supra quinto: « Testimonium perhibuit de me qui misit me Pater, neque vocem eius audistis neque speciem vidistis ».

40. (Vers. 29.). *Ego autem scio eum, et si dixero, quia nescio eum, ero similis vobis, mendax*[^p347-3]; qui mentimini dicentes, vos ignorare nativitatem *temporalem;* ego vero non mentior, ut dicam, me nescire *aeternam.* *Ego enim scio eum, quia ab ipso sum, et ipse me misit;* tanquam Filius cognoscit Patrem; Matthaei undecimo[^p347-4]: « Nemo novit Patrem nisi Filius, et cui voluerit Filius revelare ». *Scio eum,* per apertam cognitionem; supra primo: « Deum nemo vidit » etc. *Ab ipso sum,* per generationem; infra decimo sexto[^p347-5]: « Exivi a Patre et veni in mundum ». *Et ipse me misit,* per incarnationem; Isaiae sexagesimo primo: « Spiritus Domini super me » etc.

41. (Vers. 30.). *Quaerebant ergo eum apprehendere.* Notatur hic tertium, scilicet *confutatorum obstinatio.* Quia enim ostenderat eos mendaces et ignorantes, ideo in malo confirmati et non valentes verbo resistere, *quaerebant ergo eum apprehendere* quidam scilicet maiores, sicut dicit Chrysostomus[^p347-6]. Sed tamen effectum non habuit eorum malitia, divina faciente potentia. Non enim apprehenderunt Christum, veritatem, sed mendacium; Ieremiae octavo[^p347-7]: « Apprehenderunt mendacium et noluerunt reverti »; unde dicit: *Sed nemo misit in eum manus,* ad capiendum scilicet, *quia nondum venerat hora eius,* in qua pati dignaretur. Unde Augustinus[^p347-8]: « Non dixit horam, qua cogeretur mori, sed qua dignaretur occidi »; de qua dicitur infra decimo septimo: « Pater, venit hora, clarifica Filium tuum ».

42. (Vers. 31.). *De turba autem multi crediderunt in eum.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet in *parvulis fidei aedificatio,* unde dicit: *De turba autem multi crediderunt in eum,* non de principibus et maioribus; Matthaei undecimo[^p347-9]: « Abscondisti haec a sapientibus et prudentibus et revelasti ea parvulis »; primae ad Corinthios primo: « Videte vocationem vestram, fratres, quia non multi sapientes » etc. Et subditur vera confessio: *Et dicebant: Christus, cum veniet*[^p347-10], *nunquid plura signa faciet, quam quae hic facit?* Unde quia signa maxima et multa eis visa sunt, credebant et confitebantur, quod alius non esset exspectandus; infra decimo quinto: « Si non fecissem in eis opera, quae nemo alius fecit, peccatum non haberent ».

### Quaestiones.

43. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur primo de hoc quod dicunt, quod *Christus cum venerit, nemo scit, unde sit.* — Falsum videntur dicere, quia ex Scripturis habebant, quod *ex semine David et de Bethlehem castello;* sicut dicitur infra eodem capitulo[^p347-11].

Respondendum ad hoc, quod doctores Legis secundum diversas Scripturas coniiciebant, Christum non sic nasciturum quemadmodum alii; unde scire poterant, quod ex virgine, et tamen ignorare modum[^p347-12]. Unde Scriptura *modum* generationis Christi innuit esse occultum, sed *ipsam propaginem* manifestavit et *locum.* Et ipsi sciebant de Christo Domino, unde esset, quia de Maria Virgine; sed ignorabant quomodo, quia credebant, quod per Ioseph.
<!-- page 348 -->

Aliter autem exponitur iuxta duplicem generationem, scilicet *temporalem* et *aeternam.* Sciebant aliquo modo *temporalem,* aliquo modo ignorabant; sed *aeternam* simpliciter ignorabant[^p348-1].

44. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicitur: *Hunc autem scimus, unde sit;* quia infra[^p348-2] dicitur: *Hunc nescimus, unde sit:* ergo contradicunt sibi.

Respondet Chrysostomus[^p348-3], quod non est mirum, si plebs tumultuans contradicat sibi, quia non erat stabilitas in verbis eius.

Aliter dicitur, quod hoc dicitur de *simplici* notitia; sed infra de notitia *approbationis,* quia nesciebant ipsum tanquam approbatum a Deo, sicut Moyses, cui *locutus est Deus,* sicut ibidem[^p348-4] dicitur.

45. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Clamabat Iesus* etc.; quia dicitur Isaiae quadragesimo secundo[^p348-5]: *Non clamabit, et non audietur vox eius foris.*

**Respondeo:** Est clamor *impatientiae* et *contentionis,* et de hoc intelligit Isaias[^p348-6]; et est clamor *affectuosae praedicationis,* et sic accipitur hic.

46. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Me scitis.* — *Contra:* Infra octavo[^p348-7]: *Si me sciretis, et Patrem meum sciretis:* ergo nesciebant eum.

**Respondeo:** Duplex erat cognitio de Christo: quaedam per *sensum exteriorem,* sicut quod esset et homo et natus in tali villa et ex tali parente, hoc sciebant; alia per *revelationem interiorem,* quae erat de invisibili Divinitate; et hac quidem nesciebant eum; et de hac dicitur Ioannis octavo.

## English
<!-- page 346 -->

36. *Some therefore of Jerusalem said.* He refuted the malicious; here *he refutes those who speak against him;* and he proceeds in this order. First is set down the *obloquy* itself; second, *the refutation of those who speak against him;* third, *the obstinacy of the refuted;* fourth, of the little ones or *the lesser, the edification.*

(Verse 25.). First, then, is set down the *obloquy* of those who ought more to have known; and these were those who stayed in the royal city; on account of which he says: *Some therefore of Jerusalem said,* who were privy to the machination of the Pharisees: *Is not this he whom the Jews sought to kill*[^p346-10]? as an evil man; for they were of those of whom it is said in Proverbs, the first: « Their feet run to evil, and make haste to shed blood ».

37. (Verse 26.). *And behold, he speaketh openly, and they say nothing to him,* and from this they wonder, and from wonder, when they ought to have been roused to faith, they proceed to a lying obloquy: *Have the rulers known for a truth that*[^p346-11] *this is the Christ?* As if they were to say: they can suspect it, but they cannot truly know it; whence he adds:

38. (Verse 27.). *But we know this man, whence he is,* because he is from Nazareth; above, in the first[^p346-12]: « We have found Jesus, the son of Joseph of Nazareth ». *But the Christ, when he cometh, no man knoweth whence he is;* and thus they detracted from his origin. This they said on account of that which is written in Isaias, the fifty-third: « His generation
<!-- page 347 -->
who shall declare »? as though the generation of Christ were unspeakable, and so unsearchable; or because he comes secretly; Wisdom, the eighteenth: « While all things were in quiet silence » etc.

39. (Verse 28.). *Cried out therefore* etc. Here the second is set down, namely, *the refutation of those who speak against him.* These who speak against him about the generation of Christ he refutes, showing his twofold generation: the *temporal,* which they themselves knew, and the *eternal,* which he himself knew. On account of the *temporal* he says: *Jesus therefore cried out, teaching in the temple,* so as to make the mutterers astonished; whence Proverbs, the first[^p347-1]: « Wisdom crieth aloud abroad, she uttereth her voice in the streets »; because he teaches openly and freely; Isaias, the fifty-eighth: « Cry, cease not, lift up thy voice like a trumpet ». *And saying: You both know me, and you know whence I am,* according to the *temporal* generation; whence Matthew, the thirteenth: « His mother is called Mary »; and this they knew, that he was a man and that he was born of Mary; this is said according to the *temporal* generation. But as to the *eternal* he says that he is from another, whom they do not know; on account of which he says: *And I am not come of myself,* in which the procession from the Father is noted; *but he that sent me is true,* because he is God; and God is truthful, but every man a liar, according to that of the Psalm[^p347-2]: « Every man is a liar »; Numbers, the twenty-third: « God is not as a man, that he should lie ». *Whom you know not;* above, in the fifth: « The Father who sent me hath given testimony of me; neither have you heard his voice, nor seen his shape ».

40. (Verse 29.). *But I know him, and if I shall say that I know him not, I shall be like to you, a liar*[^p347-3]; you who lie, saying that you are ignorant of the *temporal* nativity; but I do not lie, so as to say that I do not know the *eternal.* *For I know him, because I am from him, and he hath sent me;* as the Son knows the Father; Matthew, the eleventh[^p347-4]: « No one knoweth the Father but the Son, and he to whom the Son will reveal him ». *I know him,* by open knowledge; above, in the first: « No man hath seen God » etc. *I am from him,* by generation; below, in the sixteenth[^p347-5]: « I came forth from the Father, and am come into the world ». *And he hath sent me,* by incarnation; Isaias, the sixty-first: « The Spirit of the Lord is upon me » etc.

41. (Verse 30.). *They sought therefore to apprehend him.* Here the third is noted, namely, *the obstinacy of the refuted.* For because he had shown them to be liars and ignorant, therefore, confirmed in evil and not able to resist the word, *they sought therefore to apprehend him* — certain ones, namely the greater ones, as Chrysostom says[^p347-6]. But yet their malice did not have its effect, the divine power bringing this about. For they did not apprehend Christ, the truth, but a lie; Jeremias, the eighth[^p347-7]: « They have apprehended a lie, and have refused to return »; whence he says: *But no man laid hands on him,* to take him, namely, *because his hour was not yet come,* in which he would deign to suffer. Whence Augustine[^p347-8]: « He did not say the hour in which he would be compelled to die, but that in which he would deign to be slain »; of which it is said below, in the seventeenth: « Father, the hour is come, glorify thy Son ».

42. (Verse 31.). *And of the people many believed in him.* Here the fourth is touched upon, namely, in *the little ones, the edification of faith,* whence he says: *And of the people many believed in him,* not of the rulers and the greater ones; Matthew, the eleventh[^p347-9]: « Thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them to little ones »; the first to the Corinthians, the first: « See your vocation, brethren, that not many are wise » etc. And there is added a true confession: *And they said: The Christ, when he shall come*[^p347-10], *will he do more signs than those which this man doth?* Whence, because very great and many signs were seen by them, they believed and confessed that another was not to be looked for; below, in the fifteenth: « If I had not done among them the works that no other man hath done, they would not have sin ».

### Questions.

43. Question I. But it is asked first about this, that they say that *the Christ, when he cometh, no man knoweth whence he is.* — They seem to say what is false, because they had from the Scriptures that he would be *of the seed of David, and from Bethlehem the town;* as is said below, in the same chapter[^p347-11].

It is to be responded to this, that the doctors of the Law, according to diverse Scriptures, conjectured that the Christ would not be born in the way that others are; whence they could know that he would be of a virgin, and yet be ignorant of the manner[^p347-12]. Whence Scripture intimates that the *manner* of Christ's generation is hidden, but it made manifest *the lineage itself* and *the place.* And they themselves knew of Christ the Lord whence he was, because of the Virgin Mary; but they were ignorant how, because they believed that it was through Joseph.
<!-- page 348 -->

Otherwise, however, it is expounded according to the twofold generation, namely the *temporal* and the *eternal.* The *temporal* they knew in some way, in some way they were ignorant of it; but the *eternal* they were simply ignorant of[^p348-1].

44. Question II. Likewise it is asked about this, that it is said: *But we know this man, whence he is;* because below[^p348-2] it is said: *This man we know not, whence he is:* therefore they contradict themselves.

Chrysostom responds[^p348-3] that it is no wonder if a tumultuous populace contradicts itself, because there was no stability in its words.

Otherwise it is said that this is said of *simple* knowledge; but below, of the knowledge of *approbation,* because they did not know him as one approved by God, as Moses was, to whom *God spoke,* as is said in the same place[^p348-4].

45. Question III. Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *Jesus cried out* etc.; because it is said in Isaias, the forty-second[^p348-5]: *He shall not cry, and his voice shall not be heard abroad.*

**I respond:** There is a cry of *impatience* and *contention,* and Isaias understands it of this[^p348-6]; and there is a cry of *affectionate preaching,* and so it is taken here.

46. Question IV. Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *You know me.* — *On the contrary:* below, in the eighth[^p348-7]: *If you did know me, you would know my Father also:* therefore they did not know him.

**I respond:** There was a twofold knowledge of Christ: one through *exterior sense,* as that he was both a man and born in such a town and of such a parent — this they knew; another through *interior revelation,* which was of the invisible Divinity; and by this one they did not know him; and of this it is said in John, the eighth.
