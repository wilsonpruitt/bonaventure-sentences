# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XI, Vers. 33-42
## *Supra posita sunt tria praeambula ad mirabilem suscitationem, hic ponitur quartum, quod est exauditionis pietas; propter pietatem enim motus est Dominus exaudire et Lazarum suscitare; et describitur hoc ordine*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 402 -->

46. *Iesus ergo, ut vidit eam plorantem.* Supra[^p402-15] posita sunt tria praeambula ad mirabilem suscitationem, hic ponitur quartum, quod est *exauditionis pietas;* propter pietatem enim motus est Dominus exaudire et Lazarum suscitare; et describitur hoc ordine. Primo innuitur *pietas exaudientis;* secundo, *signum exauditionis;* tertio, *virtus* vel potestas *exaudiendi.*

(Vers. 33.). *Pietas* ergo *exaudientis* notatur in hoc, quod Christus ad fletum Mariae et Iudaeorum flevit et doluit; propterea dicit: *Iesus ergo, ut vi-*
<!-- page 403 -->
*dit eam plorantem,* scilicet Mariam, *et Iudaeos, qui venerant cum ea, plorantes. Vidit,* oculo scilicet pietatis; quarti Regum vigesimo[^p403-1]: « Audivi orationem tuam et vidi lacrymas tuas ». *Infremuit spiritu et turbavit semetipsum,* eis compatiendo; et ex hac compassione approximat; unde:

47. (Vers. 34.). *Dixit*[^p403-2]*: Ubi posuistis eum? Dicunt ei: Domine, veni et vide;* et sequitur compassionis ostensio; unde:

48. (Vers. 35.). *Et lacrymatus est Iesus;* unde ex visu oritur *turbatio,* ex turbatione *approximatio,* ex approximatione *doloris et compassionis expressio.* Iob trigesimo[^p403-3]: « Flebam quondam super eo qui afflictus erat, et compatiebatur anima mea pauperi »; Lucae decimo nono: « Videns Iesus civitatem, flevit super illam ». Flevit, cum vidit flentem Mariam; ad Romanos duodecimo: « Flere cum flentibus »; et haec compassio signum erat doloris, et dolor signum amoris; propterea:

49. (Vers. 36.). *Dixerunt ergo Iudaei: Ecce, quomodo amabat eum,* quia de eius morte flebat; et verum dicebant, secundum quod Sapientiae undecimo[^p403-4] dicitur: « Domine, diligis omnia, quae sunt, et nihil odisti eorum quae fecisti », et post: « Tua sunt, Domine, qui amas animas ». Sed ex hoc in dubitationem incidebant; quia enim amans alium non vult, eum mori; videbatur eis, quod contra voluntatem eius mortuus esset, et admirabantur; propter quod dicit:

50. (Vers. 37.). *Quidam autem ex ipsis dixerunt: Non poterat hic qui aperuit oculos caeci nati* — supra nono[^p403-5] — *facere, ut hic non moreretur?* Dubitando dicunt, quia rationem ad utramque partem habere videntur. Et bene quidem *poterat,* quia, Ecclesiastae octavo[^p403-6], « omne, quod voluerit, faciet »; et Lucae primo: « Non erit impossibile apud Deum omne verbum ». Sed si non esset mortuus, non manifestaretur gloria Dei in illo; ideo iste est *mortuus*[^p403-7], sicut ille *caecus* natus; supra nono: « Neque hic peccavit neque parentes eius, sed ut manifestentur opera Dei in illo »; ideo *caecus* est natus, sic Lazarus *mortuus.*

51. (Vers. 38.). *Iesus ergo rursum fremens.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *signum exauditionis,* quod in hoc manifestatur, quia ad monumentum venit et lapidem amoveri fecit; et compassio movebat haec facere. Ideo dicit: *Iesus ergo rursum fremens in semetipso,* per compassionem, *venit ad monumentum.* Et quia venerat suscitare, ideo monumentum praecepit aperire, quia clausum erat; ideo dicit: *Erat autem spelunca, et lapis superpositus erat ei,* propter foetorem scilicet amovendum.

52. (Vers. 39.). *Ait Iesus: Tollite lapidem.* Et quia Martha sollicita erat de Domino quantum ad sensibilia et corporalia; ideo: *Dicit ei Martha, soror eius qui mortuus fuerat: Domine, iam foetet;* ideo non est aperienda; *quatriduanus enim est.* Et quia Martha credebat, quod Dominus ex compassione vellet videre, non ex potentia suscitare[^p403-8]; ideo:

53. (Vers. 40.). *Dicit ei Iesus: Nonne dixi tibi, quoniam, si credideris, videbis gloriam Dei?* id est divinae virtutis magnitudinem; Actuum septimo[^p403-9]: « Cum esset Stephanus plenus Spiritu sancto, intendens in caelum, vidit gloriam Dei ». Et in hoc sciverunt, quod non ideo aperiri fecerat, ut videret mortuum, sed ut suscitaret; ideo sequitur obedientia:

54. (Vers. 41.). *Tulerunt ergo lapidem,* in quo iam manifestatio facta est, quod Dominus vellet eum suscitare; ideo tunc crediderunt et obtinere meruerunt; unde dicitur Mariae Lucae primo[^p403-10]: « Beata, quae credidisti, quoniam perficientur in te quae dicta sunt tibi a Domino ».
<!-- page 404 -->

*Iesus autem elevatis* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *potestas exaudiendi* in Christo, quae insinuatur ex verbis eius, in quibus ostendit, se exauditum et semper exaudiri et non ex impotentia Patrem rogare; propter quod dicit: *Iesus autem, elevatis oculis in caelum*[^p404-1]*, dixit: Pater, gratias ago tibi, quoniam audisti me;* gratias agit pro exauditione, non quia Pater audiret eum tunc et non alias; propterea dicit:

55. (Vers. 42.). *Ego autem sciebam, quoniam*[^p404-2] *semper me audis;* ad Hebraeos quinto: « Cum lacrymis et clamore valido offerens preces supplicationesque ad Deum, exauditus est pro sua reverentia ». Ideo non tanquam indigens petii vel gratias egi; *sed propter populum, qui circumstat, dixi, ut credant, quia tu me misisti;* et ego in operibus meis non sum tibi contrarius, sed per omnia concors; supra octavo[^p404-3]: « Sicut docuit me Pater, sic loquor; et qui me misit mecum est et non reliquit me solum, quia quae placita sunt ei facio semper ». Et sic gratias egit et oravit, non propter impotentiam, sed ad insinuandam sui et Dei concordiam, de qua supra quinto: « Non potest Filius a se facere quidquam, nisi quod viderit Patrem facientem; quaecumque enim ille facit, haec et Filius similiter facit ».

### Quaestiones.

56. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod dicit, quod *turbavit semetipsum Iesus.*

Contrarium videtur: 1. quod dicitur Isaiae quadragesimo secundo[^p404-4]: *Non erit tristis neque turbulentus.* — *Si dicas,* quod voluntarie turbavit se, non involuntarie, sicut nos[^p404-5]; tunc *obiicitur:*

2. Quia perturbatio obscurat oculum mentis[^p404-6]: ergo si in Christo mentis oculus non potuit obscurari, ergo non potuit perturbari.

3. Item, quando perturbatio est voluntaria, tunc non solum est sensualitatis, sed rationis, et completa est perturbatio: ergo si Christus voluntarie fuit perturbatus, patet ex hoc, quod plene perturbatus fuit.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod turbatio uno modo est *passio,* sicut dicit Hieronymus[^p404-7], alio modo *propassio: passio,* secundum quod ascendit in rationem, *propassio,* secundum quod sistit in sensualitate; et hoc potest dupliciter esse: quia aut est in sentiente de *necessitate,* quod non potest facere aliud; aut *voluntate.* Sic igitur triplex est perturbatio, scilicet *rationis,* et haec mala et in malis; *sensualitatis,* et haec *necessitate* in bonis, *voluntate* in Christo[^p404-8].

1. Quod ergo dicitur: *Non erit tristis neque turbulentus,* intelligendum de illa quae est *rationis,* vel *sensualitatis involuntaria*[^p404-9].

2. Quod ergo obiicitur, quod illa quae est sensualitatis, obscurat mentem, saltem ad tempus; dicendum, quod[^p404-10] verum est, ubi est *necessitas;* sed in Christo ascendebat, quantum dictante ratione volebat.

3. Quod obiicitur, quod voluntaria est plena; intelligitur, quando ratio turbat se ipsam.

57. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc, quod Christus *lacrymatus est.* — Aut enim *volebat,* Lazarum esse mortuum, aut *non;* si *sic:* ergo fictitie flebat; si *non:* ergo voluntas eius erat discors a divina et aliquid voluit quod non fecit.

**Respondeo:** Triplex fuit in Christo voluntas humana, scilicet *rationis, pietatis* et *sensualitatis,* sicut dicit Hugo[^p404-11]; et voluntate *pietatis,* quae est velleitas, volebat aliquid, quod tamen absolute non volebat; et propter illam compatiebatur et flebat, sicut *flevit super Ierusalem*[^p404-12]. Intelligendum tamen, quod Christus non flevit propter mortem Lazari, sed propter nostram miseriam, quae in morte Lazari significabatur.

Unde legitur Dominus flevisse primo in suscitatione Lazari, et tunc deflevit humanam *infirmitatem.* — Secundo, super Ierusalem, Lucae decimo nono[^p404-13], et tunc deflevit nostram *caecitatem;* unde: *Si cognovisses et tu.* — Tertio, in cruce; ad Hebraeos quinto[^p404-14]: *Cum lacrymis offerens;* et tunc deflevit nostram *malignitatem.* — Quae sunt tres *origines* peccatorum et tres *poenae* inflictae nobis pro peccatis[^p404-15], quae merito lacrymarum eius mitigatae sunt.
<!-- page 405 -->

58. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc, quod *oravit:* quia oravit secundum quod homo; et secundum quod oravit, suscitavit: ergo secundum quod homo suscitavit: ergo non differt modus eius resuscitandi a modo Petri[^p405-1].

**Respondeo:** Sicut patet in littera[^p405-2], nec oravit nec gratias egit, ut potestatem suscitandi acciperet, ut alii, nec ut se minorem Patre credi faceret; quia, ut dicit Chrysostomus[^p405-3], non oportebat; sed ut Deo se conformem et virtutem suam a Patre non discordantem ostenderet.

Quod ergo obiicit: suscitavit, secundum quod oravit; falsum est; quia suscitavit secundum quod Deus, oravit secundum quod homo[^p405-4].

## English
<!-- page 402 -->

46. *Jesus therefore, when he saw her weeping.* Above[^p402-15] were set down three preambles to the wonderful raising; here is set down the fourth, which is *the piety of the hearing granted;* for on account of piety the Lord was moved to hear and to raise Lazarus; and it is described in this order. First is hinted *the piety of him who hears;* second, *the sign of the hearing granted;* third, *the virtue* or power *of hearing.*

(Vers. 33.). *The piety,* then, *of him who hears* is noted in this, that Christ at the weeping of Mary and of the Jews wept and grieved; for this reason he says: *Jesus therefore, when he*
<!-- page 403 -->
*saw her weeping,* namely Mary, *and the Jews that were come with her, weeping. He saw,* namely with the eye of piety; the fourth of Kings, the twentieth chapter[^p403-1]: « I have heard thy prayer, and I have seen thy tears ». *He groaned in the spirit and troubled himself,* having compassion on them; and from this compassion he draws near; whence:

47. (Vers. 34.). *He said*[^p403-2]*: Where have you laid him? They say to him: Lord, come and see;* and there follows the showing of compassion; whence:

48. (Vers. 35.). *And Jesus wept;* whence from the sight arises *troubling,* from troubling *drawing near,* from drawing near *the expression of grief and compassion.* Job, the thirtieth chapter[^p403-3]: « I wept heretofore for him that was afflicted, and my soul had compassion on the poor »; Luke, the nineteenth chapter: « Jesus seeing the city, wept over it ». He wept when he saw Mary weeping; to the Romans, the twelfth chapter: « Weep with them that weep »; and this compassion was a sign of grief, and grief a sign of love; for this reason:

49. (Vers. 36.). *The Jews therefore said: Behold how he loved him,* because he was weeping over his death; and they spoke the truth, according to what is said in Wisdom, the eleventh chapter[^p403-4]: « Lord, thou lovest all things that are, and hatest none of the things which thou hast made », and afterward: « Thine they are, O Lord, who lovest souls ». But from this they fell into doubt; for since one who loves another does not wish him to die, it seemed to them that he had died against his will, and they wondered; on account of which he says:

50. (Vers. 37.). *But some of them said: Could not he that opened the eyes of the man born blind* — above, in the ninth chapter[^p403-5] — *have caused that this man should not die?* They say it doubting, because they seem to have a reason for either side. And well indeed he *could,* because, Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter[^p403-6], « he will do all that he pleaseth »; and Luke, the first chapter: « No word shall be impossible with God ». But if he had not died, the glory of God would not be made manifest in him; therefore this man has *died*[^p403-7], as that man was born *blind;* above, in the ninth chapter: « Neither hath this man sinned, nor his parents; but that the works of God should be made manifest in him »; therefore he was born *blind,* so Lazarus *died.*

51. (Vers. 38.). *Jesus therefore again groaning.* Here is touched the second, namely, *the sign of the hearing granted,* which is made manifest in this, that he came to the sepulchre and caused the stone to be moved away; and compassion moved him to do these things. Therefore he says: *Jesus therefore again groaning in himself,* through compassion, *cometh to the sepulchre.* And because he had come to raise, therefore he commanded the sepulchre to be opened, because it was closed; therefore he says: *Now it was a cave; and a stone was laid over it,* namely to move away the stench.

52. (Vers. 39.). *Jesus saith: Take away the stone.* And because Martha was solicitous about the Lord as regards sensible and bodily things; therefore: *Martha, the sister of him that was dead, saith to him: Lord, by this time he stinketh;* therefore it is not to be opened; *for he is four days dead.* And because Martha believed that the Lord wished to see out of compassion, not to raise out of power[^p403-8]; therefore:

53. (Vers. 40.). *Jesus saith to her: Did not I say to thee, that if thou believe, thou shalt see the glory of God?* that is, the greatness of the divine virtue; Acts, the seventh chapter[^p403-9]: « When Stephen was full of the Holy Spirit, looking up steadfastly to heaven, he saw the glory of God ». And in this they knew that he had caused it to be opened not in order to see the dead man, but in order to raise him; therefore there follows obedience:

54. (Vers. 41.). *They took therefore the stone away,* in which it was now made manifest that the Lord wished to raise him; therefore they then believed and merited to obtain; whence it is said to Mary, Luke, the first chapter[^p403-10]: « Blessed art thou that hast believed, because those things shall be accomplished in thee that were spoken to thee by the Lord ».
<!-- page 404 -->

*And Jesus lifting up* etc. Here is touched the third, namely, *the power of hearing* in Christ, which is intimated by his words, in which he shows that he has been heard and is always heard, and that he does not ask the Father out of impotence; on account of which he says: *And Jesus, lifting up his eyes to heaven*[^p404-1]*, said: Father, I give thee thanks that thou hast heard me;* he gives thanks for the hearing granted, not because the Father heard him then and not at other times; for this reason he says:

55. (Vers. 42.). *And I knew that*[^p404-2] *thou hearest me always;* to the Hebrews, the fifth chapter: « With tears and a strong cry, offering up prayers and supplications to God, he was heard for his reverence ». Therefore not as one in need have I asked or given thanks; *but because of the people who stand about have I said it, that they may believe that thou hast sent me;* and I in my works am not contrary to thee, but in all things in concord; above, in the eighth chapter[^p404-3]: « As the Father hath taught me, so I speak; and he that sent me is with me, and he hath not left me alone, for I do always the things that please him ». And thus he gave thanks and prayed, not on account of impotence, but to intimate the concord of himself and God, of which above, in the fifth chapter: « The Son cannot do any thing of himself, but what he seeth the Father doing; for what things soever he doth, these the Son also doth in like manner ».

### Questions.

56. Quaest. I. But it is asked here first about what he says, that *Jesus troubled himself.*

The contrary seems: 1. what is said in Isaias, the forty-second chapter[^p404-4]: *He shall not be sad, nor troublesome.* — *If you say* that he troubled himself voluntarily, not involuntarily, as we do[^p404-5]; then *it is objected:*

2. Because perturbation darkens the eye of the mind[^p404-6]: therefore if in Christ the eye of the mind could not be darkened, therefore he could not be perturbed.

3. Likewise, when perturbation is voluntary, then it belongs not only to sensuality, but to reason, and the perturbation is complete: therefore if Christ was voluntarily perturbed, it is plain from this that he was fully perturbed.

**I respond:** It must be said that troubling in one mode is a *passion,* as Jerome says[^p404-7], in another mode a *propassion: a passion,* according as it ascends into reason, *a propassion,* according as it stops in sensuality; and this can be in two ways: because either it is in the one sensing of *necessity,* so that he cannot do otherwise; or by *will.* Thus therefore perturbation is threefold, namely, of *reason,* and this is evil and in the evil; of *sensuality,* and this by *necessity* in the good, by *will* in Christ[^p404-8].

1. To what, then, is said: *He shall not be sad, nor troublesome,* it is to be understood of that which is of *reason,* or of the *involuntary one of sensuality*[^p404-9].

2. To what, then, is objected, that that which is of sensuality darkens the mind, at least for a time; it must be said that[^p404-10] this is true where there is *necessity;* but in Christ it ascended so far as, at the dictate of reason, he willed.

3. As to what is objected, that the voluntary one is full; this is understood when reason troubles itself.

57. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked about this, that Christ *wept.* — For either he *willed* Lazarus to be dead, or *not;* if *so:* therefore he was weeping fictitiously; if *not:* therefore his will was discordant from the divine and he willed something that he did not do.

**I respond:** The human will in Christ was threefold, namely, of *reason,* of *piety* and of *sensuality,* as Hugh says[^p404-11]; and by the will of *piety,* which is a velleity, he willed something which nevertheless he did not absolutely will; and on account of that he had compassion and wept, as he *wept over Jerusalem*[^p404-12]. It is to be understood, however, that Christ did not weep on account of the death of Lazarus, but on account of our misery, which was signified in the death of Lazarus.

Whence the Lord is read to have wept first at the raising of Lazarus, and then he wept over human *infirmity.* — Second, over Jerusalem, Luke, the nineteenth chapter[^p404-13], and then he wept over our *blindness;* whence: *If thou also hadst known.* — Third, on the cross; to the Hebrews, the fifth chapter[^p404-14]: *With tears offering;* and then he wept over our *malignity.* — Which are the three *origins* of sins and the three *punishments* inflicted on us for sins[^p404-15], which were mitigated by the merit of his tears.
<!-- page 405 -->

58. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked about this, that he *prayed:* for he prayed according as he is man; and according as he prayed, he raised: therefore according as he is man he raised: therefore his mode of raising does not differ from the mode of Peter[^p405-1].

**I respond:** As is plain in the letter[^p405-2], he neither prayed nor gave thanks in order to receive the power of raising, as others do, nor to make himself believed less than the Father; for, as Chrysostom says[^p405-3], it was not fitting; but that he might show himself conformed to God and his virtue not discordant from the Father.

As to what, then, he objects: he raised according as he prayed; it is false; for he raised according as he is God, he prayed according as he is man[^p405-4].
