# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. VI, Vers. 22-33
## *Secundo, manifestat se conservatorem spirituum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 324 -->

#### Secundo, manifestat se conservatorem spirituum.

37. *Altera die turba* etc. Ostendit supra Dominus, se esse conservatorem[^p324-7] refectione corporali; in hac parte ostendit, se esse conservatorem *refectione spirituali.* Et quoniam nemo reficitur spiritualiter nisi consentiens; et liberum arbitrium non consentit, nisi alliciatur et excitetur[^p324-8]: ideo haec pars duas habet, quia primo fit *turbarum excitatio,* secundo vero proponitur eis *spiritualis refectio,* ibi[^p324-9]: *Dixerunt ei: Domine, semper da nobis panem* etc.

*Excitatio* igitur *turbarum* hoc ordine describitur. Primo notatur turbarum ad quaerendum Dominum Christum *sollicitudo;* secundo vero, inordinate quaerentium *correctio;* tertio, dubitantium *instructio;* quarto, superbientium *humiliatio.*

Insinuatur ergo primo *turbarum sollicitudo* ad Christum quaerendum proveniens ex viso miraculo; et quia eum nesciebant, ideo quaerebant, quia nesciebant, quando et quomodo recesserat. Propterea dicit:
<!-- page 325 -->

38. (Vers. 22.). *Altera die turba, quae stabat trans mare; stabat,* inquam, per *exspectationem;* unde dicere poterant illud Petri, Matthaei decimo septimo[^p325-1]: « Domine, bonum est nos hic esse », ideo non statim recesserant; *trans mare,* ubi scilicet refecti fuerant. *Vidit,* per *considerationem,* secundum illud[^p325-2]: « Dedi tibi cor sapiens et intelligens ». *Vidit, quod navicula alia non erat ibi nisi una,* in qua scilicet Iesus potuisset transire, nec in illa transivit. *Et, vidit* supple, *quia non introisset Iesus cum discipulis suis in navim, sed solum discipuli abiissent.* Et quia eum non inveniebant, mirabiliter eum transisse credebant; et quaerere volebant et poterant, quia naves advenerant. Propterea subdit:

39. (Vers. 23.). *Aliae vero supervenerunt naves a Tiberiade,* a civitate, a qua denominabatur lacus ille; *iuxta locum, ubi manducaverant panem, gratias agentes Deo*[^p325-3], quia sic manducandum est.

40. (Vers. 24.). *Cum ergo vidisset turba, quia Iesus non esset ibi, nec discipuli eius;* et ipsi propter Iesum venerant; *ascenderunt naviculas*[^p325-4] *et venerunt Capharnaum, quaerentes Iesum;* ideo eum quaerebant, quia eos refecerat in praesentia, secundum illud Ecclesiastici[^p325-5]: « Qui edunt me adhuc esurient ». In *ligno* quaerebant, qui salutem operatus est, iuxta illud Sapientiae decimo quarto: « Exiguo ligno committunt homines animas suas, et transeuntes mare per ratem, liberati sunt ».

41. (Vers. 25.). *Et cum invenissent* etc. Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *inordinate quaerentium correctio.* Quaerunt enim Iudaei Iesum et ei invento quaestionem faciunt; propterea dicit: *Et cum invenissent eum trans mare,* qui transire eum non viderant; *dixerunt ei: Rabbi, quando huc venisti?* Ex admiratione quaerunt, nescientes, quomodo venerit; et tempus curiose quaerunt; sicut discipuli, Matthaei vigesimo quarto[^p325-6]: « Dic nobis, quando haec erunt » ? Et Dominus non respondet inquisitioni, sed inordinate quaerentium intentioni. Propterea subdit:

42. (Vers. 26.). *Respondit*[^p325-7] *Iesus et dixit: Amen, amen dico vobis: Quaeritis me, non quia vidistis signa,* quae feci, ut per signa veniatis ad fidem, et ita nihil spirituale quaeritis, sed carnale; *sed quia cx panibus meis,* id est a me multiplicatis, *manducastis et saturati estis.* Et hoc Iudaei carnales requirebant, scilicet corporis saturitatem, sicut et patres eorum; Exodi decimo sexto[^p325-8]: « Utinam mortui essemus per manum Domini in terra Aegypti, quando sedebamus super ollas carnium et comedebamus panem in saturitate ». Et quia inordinate quaerebant, ideo Dominus eos *dirigit et ordinat.*

43. (Vers. 27.). *Operamini,* id est, operose requirite, *non cibum, qui perit,* id est carnalem; quia primae ad Corinthios sexto[^p325-9]: « Esca ventri, et venter escis; Deus autem et hunc et has destruet »; Matthaei decimo quinto: « Omne, quod in os intrat, in ventrem vadit » etc.; *sed cibum*[^p325-10]*, qui permanet in vitam aeternam,* id est cibum spiritualem. Iste cibus est verbum Dei, qui in aeternum permanet; unde Isaiae quadragesimo dicitur: « Verbum Domini manet in aeternum »; et ideo dat vitam aeternam; infra eodem: « Domine, ad quem ibimus? Verba vitae aeternae habes ». Hunc cibum dat Christus; propterea dicit: *Quem Filius hominis dabit vobis,* tanquam bonus pastor; infra decimo[^p325-11]: « Per me si quis introierit, salvabitur et ingredietur et egredietur et pascua inveniet ». Et quod ipsius sit hunc cibum dare, ostendit: *Hunc enim Pater signavit Deus; signavit,* signo sui testimonii ipsum esse suum Filium ostendit, qui possit vitam et cibum dare; Matthaei tertio[^p325-12], in baptismo: « Hic est Filius meus dilectus ».

Vel: *signavit,* id est, proprio signo *discrevit* ab aliis, ut sit singularis et « in omnibus primatum tenens[^p325-13] ». Discrevit enim Pater Christum triplici discretione, scilicet *Verbi unione,* supra primo[^p325-14]: « Verbum caro factum est »; *donorum plenitudine,* supra tertio: « Non ad mensuram dat Deus spiritum »; *peccati immunitate,* supra primo: « Ecce, Agnus Dei; ecce, qui tollit peccata mundi ». Ista signatio praesignata est Aggaei ultimo[^p325-15]: « Assumam te, Zorobabel, serve meus, dicit Dominus, et ponam te quasi signaculum; quia te elegi, dicit Dominus exercituum ». Per *Zorobabel* intelligitur *Christus;* ideo de agno, Exodi decimo tertio: « Erit quasi signum in manu tua ».
<!-- page 326 -->

44. (Vers. 28.). *Dixerunt ergo*[^p326-1]*: Quid faciemus* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *dubitantium instructio;* non enim intelligentes Christi correctionem, incipiunt dubitare et quaerere. Propter hoc ait: *Dixerunt ergo ad eum,* scilicet Iudaei: *Quid faciemus, ut operemur opera bona Dei?* Ideo quaerebant de operatione, quia per illam volebant venire in refectionem. Is enim est ordo; secundae ad Thessalonicenses ultimo[^p326-2]: « Qui non laborat non manducet »; Proverbiorum duodecimo: « Qui operatur terram suam saturabitur panibus ». Et Dominus quaerentes eos instruit; unde subdit:

45. (Vers. 29.). *Respondit Iesus et dixit eis,* scilicet quaerentibus et dubitantibus: *Hoc est opus Dei, ut credatis in eum, quem misit ille. Hoc est opus* bonum, principium omnium bonorum operum, quoniam dicitur ad Hebraeos undecimo[^p326-3]: « Sine fide impossibile est placere Deo ». Qui vult bene operari a fide debet incipere; Genesis decimo quinto: « Credidit Abraham Deo, et reputatum est ei ad iustitiam ».

46. (Vers. 30.). *Dixerunt ergo ei* etc. Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *contemnentium et superbientium humiliatio;* nolebant enim Christo credere per signa, quae viderant, quasi maiora signa vidissent patres eorum. Propterea dicunt: *Quod ergo tu signum facis, ut videamus et credamus tibi?* Non enim credunt Hebraei, nisi signa videant; primae ad Corinthios primo[^p326-4]: « Iudaei signa quaerunt ». *Quid operaris?* Quasi dicant: quid magnum fecisti? Modicum est, quod fecisti respectu eius quod factum est in deserto.

47. (Vers. 31.). *Patres nostri manducaverunt manna in deserto,* non panem hordeaceum, sed caelestem; unde et subdunt: *Sicut scriptum est: Panem de caelo dedit eis manducare.* Scriptum est in Psalmo[^p326-5]: « Panem Angelorum manducavit homo, cibaria misit eis in abundantia ». Sic efferebantur de miraculo ostenso patribus, contemnentes miraculum Christi; et Dominus eos humiliat ostendens, quod Scriptura illa non est impleta per Moysen, sed impletur per ipsum. Propterea subdit:

48. (Vers. 32.). *Dicit*[^p326-6] *ergo eis Iesus: Amen, amen dico vobis: Non Moyses dedit vobis panem de caelo,* sicut illa Scriptura loquitur; non, inquam, *vere,* sed *typice,* quia, sicut dicitur primae ad Corinthios decimo, « omnia in figura contingebant illis ». *Sed Pater meus dat vobis panem verum de caelo;* et ideo ab ipso petimus: « Panem nostrum supersubstantialem da nobis hodie », Matthaei sexto. Et rationem huius reddit:

49. (Vers. 33.). *Panis enim verus*[^p326-7] *est, qui de caelo descendit et dat vitam mundo;* et hic panis non fuit manna, sed Christus; infra decimo: « Ego veni, ut vitam habeant et abundantius habeant ». *Descendere de caelo,* hoc est in carnem venire; unde Augustinus[^p326-8]: « Verbum, quod erat panis Angelorum, dum descendit in carnem, vertitur in lac parvulorum ».

### Quaestiones.

50. Quaest. I. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Quaeritis me, non quia vidistis signa.* — *Contra:* Supra eodem[^p326-9]: *Sequebatur eum multitudo magna, quia videbant signa* etc.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod contrarietas haec solvitur dupliciter: uno modo per relationem ad *diversa tempora:* quia illi iidem, qui primo secuti sunt propter *signa,* postmodum refecti, secuti sunt propter *escas.* Alio modo solvitur per relationem ad *diversas personas:* quia utrumque verum est; quidam enim sequebantur propter *signa,* quidam propter *escas.* — Unde notandum, quod quinque fuerunt causae, quare aliqui sequebantur Dominum, quae his versibus continentur:

Morbus, signa, cibus, blasphemia, dogma fuere  
Causae, cur Dominum turba secuta fuit[^p326-10].

51. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Operamini non cibum, qui perit.* Quaerit Chrysostomus[^p326-11]: utrum debeat homo operari pro cibo corporali? — Quod non, videtur:

Per litteram istam. — Item, Matthaei sexto[^p326-12]: *Nolite solliciti esse, quid manducetis* etc. — Item, Lucae decimo[^p326-13] reprehendit Dominus Martham, quae circa cibum corporalem sollicita erat. Ergo non videtur, quod debeat homo pro cibo corporali operari.

**Contra:** Primae ad Thessalonicenses quarto[^p326-14]: *Operamini manibus vestris, sicut praecepimus*
<!-- page 327 -->
*vobis.* — Item, secundae ad Thessalonicenses ultimo[^p327-1]: *Qui non laborat non manducet;* ubi etiam Apostolus dicit, se praebuisse exemplum.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod bonum est operari pro habendo cibo corporali, sicut dicitur[^p327-2]: *Labores manuum tuarum manducabis; beatus es* etc. Sed circa hanc manuum operationem triplex contingit esse inordinatio: in *intentione,* ut cum finaliter hunc cibum requirit, et Dominus hoc prohibet hic; in *sollicitudine,* ut quando absorbetur mens circa terrena, et sic prohibet Dominus in Matthaeo; in *tempore,* ut quando debet vacare auditioni verbi Dei, vacat operi vel cibo corporali; et sic Christus reprehendit Martham, quae debuisset audire Dominum praedicantem[^p327-3].

52. Quaest. III. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Opus Dei est, ut credatis in eum quem ille misit.*

**Contra:** Dicit Apostolus ad Romanos tertio[^p327-4]: *Arbitramur, hominem iustificari per fidem sine operibus Legis:* ergo credere non est *opus.*

**Respondeo:** *Opus* accipitur *communiter* ad opus *operans* et ad opus *operatum, spirituale* et *corporale, interius* et *exterius;* alio modo *proprie* secundum alteram horum differentiam. Primo modo *credere* est *opus,* secundo modo minime[^p327-5].

53. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Moyses non dedit vobis panem de caelo:*

Quia dicitur Sapientiae decimo sexto[^p327-6]: *Paratum panem de caelo praestitisti eis.*

Respondent quidam, quod est caelum *empyreum* et est *aëreum;* a primo non habuerunt panem, sed a secundo[^p327-7]. — Haec solutio non solvit, quia Christus non fuit panis de caelo empyreo; et praeterea dicitur in Psalmo[^p327-8]: *Panem Angelorum manducavit homo,* sed panis Angelorum non est de *aëre.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *manna* cibus fuit materialis et de caelo materiali, gerens tamen *figuram* cibi veri, caelestis et spiritualis[^p327-9]. Auctoritates ergo, quae dicunt illum cibum *caelestem,* intelligendae sunt secundum *figuram* et typum[^p327-10]; oppositae vero secundum *veritatem* apertam.

## English

#### Second, he manifests himself as preserver of spirits.

37. *The next day, the multitude* etc. The Lord showed above that he is the preserver[^p324-7] by bodily refection; in this part he shows that he is the preserver by *spiritual refection.* And since no one is refreshed spiritually unless he consents; and free choice does not consent unless it be allured and stirred up[^p324-8]: therefore this part has two, since first there takes place *the stirring up of the multitudes,* and second *a spiritual refection* is set before them, at[^p324-9]: *They said to him: Lord, give us always bread* etc.

*The stirring up,* then, *of the multitudes* is described in this order. First is noted the *solicitude* of the multitudes to seek the Lord Christ; second, the *correction* of those seeking inordinately; third, the *instruction* of those doubting; fourth, the *humiliation* of those growing proud.

First, then, is intimated *the solicitude of the multitudes* to seek Christ, arising from the miracle they had seen; and because they did not know where he was, therefore they were seeking, because they did not know when and how he had withdrawn. Therefore he says:
<!-- page 325 -->

38. (Verse 22.). *The next day, the multitude that stood on the other side of the sea; stood,* I say, by *expectation;* whence they could say that saying of Peter, Matthew, the seventeenth[^p325-1]: « Lord, it is good for us to be here », therefore they had not withdrawn at once; *on the other side of the sea,* namely where they had been refreshed. *Saw,* by *consideration,* according to that saying[^p325-2]: « I have given thee a wise and understanding heart ». *Saw that there was no other little ship there but one,* in which, namely, Jesus could have crossed, and he did not cross in it. *And, saw* supply, *that Jesus had not entered into the ship with his disciples, but that the disciples only were gone away.* And because they did not find him, they believed that he had crossed wonderfully; and they wished to seek him and were able, because ships had come. Therefore he adds:

39. (Verse 23.). *But other ships came in from Tiberias,* from the city from which that lake was named; *nigh unto the place where they had eaten the bread, giving thanks to God*[^p325-3], because so must one eat.

40. (Verse 24.). *When therefore the multitude saw that Jesus was not there, nor his disciples;* and they had come on account of Jesus; *they boarded the little ships*[^p325-4] *and came to Capharnaum, seeking for Jesus;* they were seeking him for this reason, because he had refreshed them in the present, according to that saying of Ecclesiasticus[^p325-5]: « They that eat me shall yet hunger ». In *wood* they were seeking him who wrought salvation, according to that saying of Wisdom, the fourteenth chapter: « Men trust their souls to a little wood, and passing over the sea by a raft are delivered ».

41. (Verse 25.). *And when they had found* etc. Here is touched the second, namely *the correction of those seeking inordinately.* For the Jews seek Jesus, and having found him they put a question to him; therefore he says: *And when they had found him on that side of the sea,* who had not seen him cross; *they said to him: Rabbi, when camest thou hither?* They ask out of wonder, not knowing how he came; and they ask the time curiously; as the disciples did, Matthew, the twenty-fourth[^p325-6]: « Tell us when shall these things be » ? And the Lord does not answer the inquiry, but the intention of those seeking inordinately. Therefore he adds:

42. (Verse 26.). *Jesus answered*[^p325-7] *and said: Amen, amen I say to you, you seek me, not because you have seen signs,* which I did, that through the signs you might come to faith, and so you seek nothing spiritual, but something carnal; *but because you did eat of* [so printed: *cx* for *ex*] *my loaves,* that is, loaves multiplied by me, *and were filled.* And this the carnal Jews were seeking, namely the filling of the body, as their fathers also did; Exodus, the sixteenth[^p325-8]: « Would to God we had died by the hand of the Lord in the land of Egypt, when we sat over the flesh pots, and ate bread to the full ». And because they were seeking inordinately, therefore the Lord *directs and orders* them.

43. (Verse 27.). *Labour,* that is, seek laboriously, *not for the food which perisheth,* that is, the carnal; because the first to the Corinthians, the sixth[^p325-9]: « The meat for the belly, and the belly for the meats; but God shall destroy both it and them »; Matthew, the fifteenth: « All that entereth into the mouth, goeth into the belly » etc.; *but for the food*[^p325-10]*, which endureth unto life everlasting,* that is, the spiritual food. This food is the word of God, which endures for ever; whence it is said in Isaias, the fortieth chapter: « The word of the Lord endureth for ever »; and therefore it gives life everlasting; below, in the same chapter: « Lord, to whom shall we go? Thou hast the words of eternal life ». This food Christ gives; therefore he says: *Which the Son of man will give you,* as the good shepherd; below, the tenth[^p325-11]: « By me, if any man enter in, he shall be saved: and he shall go in, and go out, and shall find pastures ». And that it is his to give this food, he shows: *For him hath God the Father signed; signed,* with the sign of his testimony he showed him to be his own Son, who can give life and food; Matthew, the third[^p325-12], at the baptism: « This is my beloved Son ».

Or: *signed,* that is, *distinguished* him from others by a proper sign, that he may be singular and « holding the primacy in all things[^p325-13] ». For the Father distinguished Christ by a threefold distinction, namely by *the union of the Word,* above, the first[^p325-14]: « The Word was made flesh »; by *the fulness of gifts,* above, the third: « God doth not give the Spirit by measure »; by *immunity from sin,* above, the first: « Behold, the Lamb of God; behold, him who taketh away the sins of the world ». This signing was foresigned in Aggeus, the last chapter[^p325-15]: « I will take thee, O Zorobabel, my servant, saith the Lord, and will make thee as a signet; for I have chosen thee, saith the Lord of hosts ». By *Zorobabel* is understood *Christ;* therefore of the lamb, Exodus, the thirteenth: « It shall be as a sign in thy hand ».
<!-- page 326 -->

44. (Verse 28.). *They said therefore*[^p326-1]*: What shall we do* etc. Here is touched the third, namely *the instruction of those doubting;* for, not understanding Christ's correction, they begin to doubt and to ask. On this account he says: *They said therefore unto him,* namely the Jews: *What shall we do, that we may work the good works of God?* They were asking about working for this reason, because through it they wished to come to the refection. For this is the order; the second to the Thessalonians, the last chapter[^p326-2]: « He that laboureth not, let him not eat »; Proverbs, the twelfth: « He that tilleth his land shall be satisfied with bread ». And the Lord instructs them as they ask; whence he adds:

45. (Verse 29.). *Jesus answered, and said to them,* namely to those asking and doubting: *This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he hath sent. This is the* good *work,* the principle of all good works, since it is said to the Hebrews, the eleventh[^p326-3]: « Without faith it is impossible to please God ». He who wishes to work well must begin from faith; Genesis, the fifteenth: « Abraham believed God, and it was reputed to him unto justice ».

46. (Verse 30.). *They said therefore to him* etc. Here is touched the fourth, namely *the humiliation of those despising and growing proud;* for they would not believe Christ through the signs which they had seen, as though their fathers had seen greater signs. Therefore they say: *What sign therefore dost thou shew, that we may see, and may believe thee?* For the Hebrews do not believe unless they see signs; the first to the Corinthians, the first[^p326-4]: « The Jews require signs ». *What dost thou work?* As if they said: what great thing hast thou done? What thou hast done is small in respect of that which was done in the desert.

47. (Verse 31.). *Our fathers did eat manna in the desert,* not barley bread, but heavenly; whence they also add: *As it is written: He gave them bread from heaven to eat.* It is written in the Psalm[^p326-5]: « Man ate the bread of Angels: he sent them provisions in abundance ». So they were puffed up over the miracle shown to the fathers, despising the miracle of Christ; and the Lord humbles them, showing that that Scripture was not fulfilled through Moses, but is fulfilled through himself. Therefore he adds:

48. (Verse 32.). *Jesus therefore saith*[^p326-6] *to them: Amen, amen I say to you; Moses gave you not bread from heaven,* as that Scripture says; not, I say, *truly,* but *in type,* because, as is said in the first to the Corinthians, the tenth, « all things happened to them in figure ». *But my Father giveth you the true bread from heaven;* and therefore we ask it of him: « Give us this day our supersubstantial bread », Matthew, the sixth. And he gives the reason of this:

49. (Verse 33.). *For the true bread*[^p326-7] *is that which cometh down from heaven, and giveth life to the world;* and this bread was not the manna, but Christ; below, the tenth: « I came that they may have life, and may have it more abundantly ». *To come down from heaven,* that is, to come into flesh; whence Augustine[^p326-8]: « The Word, which was the bread of Angels, while he comes down into flesh, is turned into the milk of little ones ».

### Questions.

50. Question I. It is asked about what he says: *You seek me, not because you have seen signs.* — *On the contrary:* Above, in the same chapter[^p326-9]: *A great multitude followed him, because they saw the signs* etc.

**I respond:** It must be said that this contrariety is solved in two ways: in one way by relation to *different times:* because those same people who at first followed on account of the *signs,* afterwards, having been refreshed, followed on account of the *food.* In another way it is solved by relation to *different persons:* because both are true; for some were following on account of the *signs,* some on account of the *food.* — Whence it is to be noted that there were five causes why some followed the Lord, which are contained in these verses:

Disease, signs, food, blasphemy, dogma were  
The causes why the multitude followed the Lord[^p326-10].

51. Question II. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *Labour not for the food which perisheth.* Chrysostom asks[^p326-11]: whether a man ought to work for bodily food? — That he ought not, it seems:

By this letter. — Likewise, Matthew, the sixth[^p326-12]: *Be not solicitous what you shall eat* etc. — Likewise, in Luke, the tenth[^p326-13], the Lord reproves Martha, who was solicitous about bodily food. Therefore it does not seem that a man ought to work for bodily food.

**On the contrary:** The first to the Thessalonians, the fourth[^p326-14]: *Work with your own hands, as we commanded*
<!-- page 327 -->
*you.* — Likewise, the second to the Thessalonians, the last chapter[^p327-1]: *He that laboureth not, let him not eat;* where the Apostle also says that he gave himself as an example.

**I respond:** It must be said that it is good to work for the sake of having bodily food, as it is said[^p327-2]: *Thou shalt eat the labours of thy hands; blessed art thou* etc. But about this working of the hands a threefold inordinateness can occur: in the *intention,* as when one seeks this food as an end, and the Lord forbids this here; in the *solicitude,* as when the mind is absorbed about earthly things, and so the Lord forbids it in Matthew; in the *time,* as when one ought to be free[^tr-vacare] for the hearing of the word of God, and is free for work or bodily food; and so Christ reproves Martha, who ought to have listened to the Lord preaching[^p327-3].

52. Question III. It is asked about what he says: *This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he hath sent.*

**On the contrary:** The Apostle says to the Romans, the third[^p327-4]: *For we account a man to be justified by faith, without the works of the law:* therefore to believe is not a *work.*

**I respond:** *Work* is taken *commonly* for the work as *working* and for the work as *wrought, spiritual* and *bodily, interior* and *exterior;* in another way *properly,* according to the other of these differences. In the first way *to believe* is a *work,* in the second way not at all[^p327-5].

53. Question IV. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *Moses gave you not bread from heaven:*

Because it is said in Wisdom, the sixteenth[^p327-6]: *Bread prepared from heaven thou gavest them.*

Some answer that there is an *empyrean* heaven and an *aerial* one; from the first they did not have bread, but from the second[^p327-7]. — This solution does not solve, because Christ was not bread from the empyrean heaven; and besides, it is said in the Psalm[^p327-8]: *Man ate the bread of Angels,* but the bread of Angels is not from the *air.*

**I respond:** It must be said that the *manna* was material food and from the material heaven, yet bearing the *figure* of the true food, heavenly and spiritual[^p327-9]. The authorities therefore which call that food *heavenly* are to be understood according to *figure* and type[^p327-10]; the opposite ones according to open *truth.*
