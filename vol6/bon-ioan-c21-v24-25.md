# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XXI, Vers. 24-25
## *Tertio, de nostrae fidei confirmatione*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 529 -->

#### Tertio, de nostrae fidei confirmatione.

54. *Hic est discipulus ille* etc. Supra posita est discipulorum sollicitudo et Christi manifestatio; hic ponitur *fidei nostrae confirmandae ratio*[^p529-8]. Hanc autem confirmat in nobis Evangelista dupliciter: *certificando in veritate* et *solidando in humilitate. Certificat* in veritate, dum se dicit testem verum et securum; *solidat* in humilitate, dum ostendit, se non posse omnia scribere ad plenum, ibi[^p529-9]: *Sunt autem et alia multa* etc.

55. (Vers. 24.). Dicit igitur: *Hic est discipulus ille, qui testimonium perhibet de his et scripsit haec,* cui credendum est, omni dubitatione postposita, quia *et scimus, quia verum est testimonium eius.* Chrysostomus[^p529-10]: « Quia multa certitudine scripsit, non refutat sui ipsius testimonium in medium ferre, provocans unumquemque examinare et scrutari quae facta sunt. Consuetudo nobis est, cum valde vera dicimus, neque nostrum testimonium denegare; unde Actuum quinto dixerunt Apostoli: Testes sumus horum verborum ».

56. (Vers. 25.). *Sunt autem et alia multa, quae fecit Iesus.* Ostendit, se non omnia scripsisse ad plenum; in quo intellectum nostrum humiliat, ne credamus nos ad omnia sciendum capaces. Propterea dicit, se non scripsisse[^p529-11]. Ideo dicit: *Quae si scribantur per singula, nec ipsum mundum arbitror posse capere eos qui scribendi sunt libros* — construi debet sic littera: *capere eos libros, qui scri-*
<!-- page 530 -->
*bendi sunt* — quia parva est nostra capacitas; unde supra decimo sexto[^p530-1]: « Multa habeo vobis dicere, sed non potestis portare modo ».

### Quaestio.

57. Sed quid est, quod dicit, quod signa[^p530-2] Christi non possent capi in toto mundo, cum non solum acta sua, sed omnia, quae gesta sunt ab initio saeculi, si scribantur, caperet mundus libros?

Et dicendum, quod duplex est capacitas: *corporalis* et *spiritualis,* quae est per intellectum. Si de *corporali* dicatur, *per hyperbolem* dictum est, sicut in aliis locis Scripturae, ut in Psalmo[^p530-3]: *Posuerunt in caelum os suum;* et de Salomone, quod fecit tantam copiam *argenti, quanta est lapidum in Ierusalem,* tertii Regum decimo. Si autem de captu *intelligentiae* dicatur vel accipiatur, verum est ad litteram; unde Augustinus[^p530-4]: « Nos scientes, quod intelligentia nostra non caperet quae de Christo scribi possent; curemus, recta fide intelligendo quae scripsit, recta operatione exercendo quae docuit, ad dona sempiterna pervenire, quae Dominus ipse promisit », ipso adiuvante, qui vivit et regnat in saecula saeculorum. Amen[^p530-5].

**Explicit.**

## English
<!-- page 529 -->

#### Third, on the confirmation of our faith.

54. *This is that disciple* etc. Above there was set down the solicitude of the disciples and the manifestation of Christ; here there is set down *the reason for the confirming of our faith*[^p529-8]. Now the Evangelist confirms this in us in two ways: *by certifying in truth* and *by making solid in humility. He certifies* in truth when he calls himself a true and secure witness; *he makes solid* in humility when he shows that he could not write all things in full, there[^p529-9]: *But there are also many other things* etc.

55. (Vers. 24.). He says, then: *This is that disciple who giveth testimony of these things, and hath written these things,* who is to be believed, every doubt set aside, because *we also know that his testimony is true.* Chrysostom[^p529-10]: « Because he wrote with much certitude, he does not refuse to bring his own testimony into the midst, challenging each one to examine and scrutinize the things that were done. It is our custom, when we say things that are very true, not to deny our own testimony either; hence in Acts, the fifth chapter, the Apostles said: We are witnesses of these words ».

56. (Vers. 25.). *But there are also many other things which Jesus did.* He shows that he has not written all things in full; in which he humbles our understanding, lest we believe ourselves capable of knowing all things. On that account he says that he has not written them[^p529-11]. Therefore he says: *Which, if they were written every one, I think that not even the world itself could contain those which are to be written, books* — the letter ought to be construed thus: *contain those books which are to be*
<!-- page 530 -->
*written* — because our capacity is small; hence above, the sixteenth chapter[^p530-1]: « I have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now ».

### Question.

57. But what is it that he says, that the signs[^p530-2] of Christ could not be contained in the whole world, since, if not only his acts but all things that have been done from the beginning of the world were written, the world would contain the books?

And it must be said that capacity is twofold: *bodily* and *spiritual,* which is through the understanding. If it is said of the *bodily,* it is said *by hyperbole,* as in other places of Scripture, as in the Psalm[^p530-3]: *They have set their mouth against heaven;* and of Solomon, that he made so great an abundance *of silver as there is of stones in Jerusalem,* the third of Kings, the tenth chapter. But if it is said or taken of the grasp of *intelligence,* it is true according to the letter; hence Augustine[^p530-4]: « We, knowing that our intelligence would not contain the things that could be written of Christ, let us take care, by understanding with right faith the things he wrote, by exercising with right operation the things he taught, to come to the everlasting gifts which the Lord himself promised », he helping, who lives and reigns for ever and ever. Amen[^p530-5].

**Here it ends.**

Augustine, *On the Gospel of John*, tractate 124, n. 8: It is not to be believed that the world could not contain them by the space of places — for how could they be written in it, if it would not bear them when written? But perhaps they could not be comprehended by the capacity of the readers, although, the faith of the things being kept safe, words very often seem to exceed belief. This does not happen when something that was obscure or doubtful is expounded by a cause and reason given; but when that which is plain is either increased or diminished, and yet there is no straying from the path of the truth to be signified...
