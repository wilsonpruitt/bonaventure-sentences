# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. I, Vers. 15-18
## *Primo, de manifestatione Verbi incarnati in generali*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 254 -->

#### *Secundo, de ipsa incarnatione quoad eius manifestationem dupliciter.*

35. *Et vidimus gloriam eius.* Supra determinata est Verbi incarnatio; in hac parte determinatur *incarnati Verbi cognitio* sive manifestatio. Habet autem haec pars duas, quia primo agit de manifestatione eius in *generali;* secundo vero, in *speciali*, ibi[^p254-4]: *Et hoc est testimonium Ioannis* etc.

#### Primo, de manifestatione Verbi incarnati in generali.

Prima pars habet duas, quia primo tangitur ipsius Verbi incarnati *cognitio;* secundo vero, *cognitionis ratio*, ibi[^p254-5]: *Ioannes testimonium perhibet* etc.

*Cognitio* vero Verbi incarnati, quam habuerunt Apostoli et credentes, fuit quantum ad utramque naturam; in quorum persona dicit Evangelista: *Et vidimus gloriam eius*, id est, cognovimus; *gloriam*, scilicet quantum ad Divinitatem. Propterea dicit: *Gloriam quasi Unigeniti a Patre;* de qua gloria secundae Petri primo[^p254-6]: « Accepit a Deo Patre honorem et gloriam, voce delapsa ad eum a magnifica gloria: Hic est Filius meus dilectus ». — Quantum vero ad humanitatem dicitur *plenum gratiae et veritatis*, id est, *vidimus*, supple: eum, *plenum gratiae et veritatis*, vere *plenum*, quia in ipso, sicut dicitur ad Colossenses secundo[^p254-7], « omnis plenitudo Divinitatis corporaliter inhabitat »; *plenum gratiae*, ad exhaurienda peccata; *plenum veritatis*, ad implenda promissa; Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto: « In me omnis gratia vitae et veritatis ». Et notandum, quod hoc quod dicit: *quasi Unigeniti* non est nota comparationis vel similitudinis, sed expressionis[^p254-8].

36, *Ioannes testimonium.* Postquam posita est cognitio, ponitur hic *cognoscendi ratio.* Nam triplex fuit in eis ratio cognoscendi: prima, per *testimonium;* secunda, per *singularem effectum;* tertia, per *singulare documentum.*

(Vers. 15.). Prima ergo ratio cognoscendi fuit per *testimonium authenticum*, scilicet personae authenticae, Ioannis videlicet, qui sanctus fuit ex utero matris. Propterea dicit: *Ioannes testimonium perhibet de ipso;* Proverbiorum decimo quarto[^p254-9]: « Testis fidelis non mentitur ». *Et clamat*, Ioannes aperte et secure, secundum illud Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo: « Clama, ne cesses, quasi tuba exalta vocem tuam »; dicens: *Qui post me venit ante me factus est;* ideo dignior me secundum humanam naturam effectus; ad Ephesios primo[^p254-10]: « Omnia subiecit sub pedibus eius et ipsum dedit caput supra omnem Ecclesiam ». Et ratio huius est: *quia prior me erat*, scilicet secundum Divinitatem; infra octavo: « Amen, amen dico vobis, antequam Abraham fieret, ego sum »; et sic prior Ioanne.

37. (Vers. 16.). *Et de plenitudine eius.* Tangitur hic secunda ratio cognoscendi, quod Christus erat Dei Filius, scilicet propter *singularem effectum*, et iste fuit collatio gratiae. Propterea dicit: *Et de plenitudine*, quasi dicat: vere cognovimus eum *ple-*
<!-- page 255 -->
*num;* quia *de plenitudine eius nos omnes accepimus gratiam pro gratia*, gratiam scilicet retributionis pro gratia iustificationis, vel e converso gratiam iustificationis accepimus pro obtinenda gratia retributionis, quae recte dicitur *gratia*, quia, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p255-1], « nihil Deus in nobis praeter sua dona coronat »; ad Romanos sexto: « Stipendia peccati mors; gratia autem Dei, vita aeterna »; et Zachariae quarto: « Exaequabit gratiam gratiae ». Et quod iste effectus sit singularis Christi, ostendit:

38. (Vers. 17.). *Quia Lex per Moysen data est*, quae tantum *promisit* gratiam et veritatem et non dedit; quia, ad Hebraeos septimo[^p255-2], « Lex neminem ad perfectum duxit ». Sed quod Lex promisit Christus *exhibuit;* ideo dicit: *Gratia et veritas per Iesum Christum facta est*, id est donata. Propterea clamat Apostolus ad Romanos septimo[^p255-3]: « Infelix ego homo! quis me liberabit de corpore mortis huius? Gratia Dei per Iesum Christum », non lex Moysaica.

39. (Vers. 18.). *Deum nemo vidit unquam.* Tangitur hic tertia ratio cognoscendi, quae scilicet est *singulare documentum;* quia Christus solus de Deo potuit docere et docuit certitudinaliter et aperte, quia solus vidit; ideo dicit: *Deum nemo vidit unquam;* primae ad Timotheum ultimo[^p255-4]: « Lucem habitat inaccessibilem »; et Exodi trigesimo tertio: « Non videbit me homo et vivet ».

40. *Nisi Unigenitus*[^p255-5]. Aliqui libri habent: *sed Unigenitus;* utraque littera bona. *Nemo vidit nisi Unigenitus* debet construi cum praecedenti verbo; si est ibi *sed*, debet construi cum sequenti: *Sed Unigenitus, qui est in sinu Patris, ipse enarravit. In sinu Patris*, quia ab ipso genitus non recedit ab ipso; infra tertio[^p255-6]: « Nemo ascendit in caelum, nisi qui de caelo descendit, Filius hominis, qui est in caelo ». *Ipse enarravit*, quia vidit; infra tertio: « Quod scimus loquimur, et quod vidimus testamur »; ad Hebraeos primo: « Novissime diebus istis locutus est nobis in Filio, quem constituit heredem universorum, per quem fecit et saecula ».

### Quaestiones.

41. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Plenum gratiae et veritatis:* secundum quam naturam hoc dictum est? Aut secundum *divinam*, aut secundum *humanam.* Secundum *divinam*, non; quia non est in eo gratia, sed natura est quidquid habet. Nec secundum *humanitatem*, videtur, quia subdit: *De plenitudine eius gratiam accepimus pro gratia;* sed gratia a solo Deo datur, non ab homine: ergo etc.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod in Christo fuit gratia *singularis* maxima, quae potuit esse in homine; sicut etiam gratia *unionis*, qua nulla maior potest excogitari. Ratione utriusque dicitur fuisse *plenus gratia*, et ratione huius duplicis gratiae fuit caput Ecclesiae. Et quia caput in omnia membra redundat, ideo *de plenitudine eius accepimus omnes*[^p255-7].

Quod ergo quaerit: utrum secundum divinam naturam, vel humanam? dico, quod Christus dicitur caput secundum humanam unitam divinae. Sic plenitudo *redundantiae* est intelligenda[^p255-8].

42. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *De plenitudine eius accepimus omnes.* — *Contra* hoc est, quia multi praecesserunt Christum: ergo multi fuerunt ante plenitudinem Christi: ergo non omnes acceperunt[^p255-9].

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod omnes acceperunt, quia Christus fuit caput omnium iustorum ab Abel usque ad ultimum iustum[^p255-10]. Huic capiti uniuntur membra ligamine fidei et dilectionis. Quia ergo nullus fuit salvatus sine fide Christi, sicut dicit beatus Augustinus[^p255-11]; nullus fuit salvatus, qui non receperit de plenitudine Christi.

Quod obiicitur, quod secutus est; dicendum, quod fide fuit semper omnibus praesens, licet corporaliter sit secutus[^p255-12].

43. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Deum nemo vidit unquam.*

**Contra:** 1. Numerorum duodecimo[^p255-13]: « Ore ad os loquor ei, et palam et non per figuras et aenigmata Dominum videt »; dicitur de Moyse: ergo Dominum vidit.

2. Item, Isaiae sexto[^p255-14]: « Vidi Dominum sedentem super solium excelsum et elevatum ».

**Responsio** est ad hoc, quod est cognoscere Deum *in se* et *in suo effectu*[^p255-15]. Et cognoscere Deum *in effectu*, hoc est videre *per speculum*, et hoc dupliciter: aut per speculum *lucidum* et oculum, et sic videbat primus homo ante lapsum; aut per specu-
<!-- page 256 -->
lum *obscuratum*, et sic videmus nos modo, quia propter peccatum et oculi nostri caligaverunt, et omnes creaturae obtenebratae sunt; unde videmus nunc per *speculum in aenigmate*, primae ad Corinthios decimo tertio[^p256-1].

Alio modo cognoscitur Deus *in se;* et hoc dupliciter: aut *clare*, et hoc modo a solo Filio et a Beatis; alio modo *in caligine*, sicut dicit beatus Dionysius de Mystica Theologia[^p256-2]; et sic vidit Moyses, et sublimiter contemplantes, in quorum aspectu nulla figitur imago creaturae. Et tunc revera magis sentinnt, quam cognoscant. Unde Bernardus[^p256-3] dicit, quod aliquid in se sentiebat aliquando, de quo quando volebat videre, statim amittebat.

## English
<!-- page 254 -->

#### *Second, on the incarnation itself as to its manifestation, in two ways.*

35. *And we saw his glory.* Above, the incarnation of the Word was determined; in this part there is determined the *knowledge of the incarnate Word*, or its manifestation. This part, moreover, has two, because first it treats of his manifestation in *general;* but second, in *special*, there[^p254-4]: *And this is the testimony of John* etc.

#### First, on the manifestation of the incarnate Word in general.

The first part has two, because first there is touched the *knowledge* of the incarnate Word himself; but second, the *reason for the knowledge*, there[^p254-5]: *John giveth testimony* etc.

But the *knowledge* of the incarnate Word, which the Apostles and believers had, was as to each nature; in whose person the Evangelist says: *And we saw his glory*, that is, we knew; *glory*, namely as to the Divinity. Therefore he says: *The glory as it were of the only begotten of the Father;* of which glory the second of Peter, the first[^p254-6]: « He received from God the Father honour and glory, this voice coming down to him from the excellent glory: This is my beloved Son ». — But as to the humanity it is said *full of grace and truth*, that is, *we saw*, supply: him, *full of grace and truth*, truly *full*, because in him, as is said to the Colossians, the second[^p254-7], « all the fulness of the Divinity dwelleth corporeally »; *full of grace*, for draining away sins; *full of truth*, for fulfilling promises; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth: « In me is all grace of life and of truth ». And it is to be noted that what he says, *as it were of the only begotten*, is not a mark of comparison or of likeness, but of expression[^p254-8].

36. *John testimony.* After the knowledge has been set down, there is set down here the *reason for knowing.* For the reason for knowing was threefold in them: the first, through *testimony;* the second, through a *singular effect;* the third, through a *singular teaching.*

(Vers. 15.). The first reason for knowing, then, was through *authentic testimony*, namely of an authentic person, that is, of John, who was holy from his mother's womb. Therefore he says: *John giveth testimony of him;* Proverbs, the fourteenth[^p254-9]: « A faithful witness lieth not ». *And crieth out*, John openly and securely, according to that saying of Isaiah, the fifty-eighth: « Cry, cease not, lift up thy voice like a trumpet »; saying: *He that came after me was made before me;* therefore made worthier than I according to human nature; to the Ephesians, the first[^p254-10]: « He hath subjected all things under his feet, and hath made him head over all the Church ». And the reason for this is: *because he was before me*, namely according to the Divinity; below in the eighth: « Amen, amen I say to you, before Abraham was made, I am »; and thus he was before John.

37. (Vers. 16.). *And of his fulness.* Here is touched the second reason for knowing that Christ was the Son of God, namely on account of a *singular effect*, and this was the conferring of grace. Therefore he says: *And of the fulness*, as if he were saying: truly we knew him to be *full;*
<!-- page 255 -->
because *of his fulness we all have received, grace for grace*, namely the grace of retribution for the grace of justification, or conversely we have received the grace of justification for obtaining the grace of retribution, which is rightly called *grace*, because, as Augustine says[^p255-1], « God crowns nothing in us but his own gifts »; to the Romans, the sixth: « The wages of sin is death; but the grace of God, life everlasting »; and Zechariah, the fourth: « He shall make grace equal to grace ». And that this effect is singular to Christ, he shows:

38. (Vers. 17.). *Because the Law was given by Moses*, which only *promised* grace and truth and did not give it; because, to the Hebrews, the seventh[^p255-2], « The Law brought no one to perfection ». But what the Law promised, Christ *displayed;* therefore he says: *Grace and truth was made by Jesus Christ*, that is, given. Therefore the Apostle cries out to the Romans, the seventh[^p255-3]: « Unhappy man that I am! who shall deliver me from the body of this death? The grace of God by Jesus Christ », not the Mosaic law.

39. (Vers. 18.). *No man hath seen God at any time.* Here is touched the third reason for knowing, which namely is a *singular teaching;* because Christ alone could teach about God, and taught, with certitude and openly, because he alone saw; therefore he says: *No man hath seen God at any time;* the first to Timothy, the last[^p255-4]: « He dwelleth in light inaccessible »; and Exodus, the thirty-third: « Man shall not see me and live ».

40. *Except the only begotten*[^p255-5]. Some books have: *but the only begotten;* each reading is good. *No man hath seen except the only begotten* must be construed with the preceding verb; if *but* stands there, it must be construed with what follows: *But the only begotten, who is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him. In the bosom of the Father*, because, begotten by him, he does not withdraw from him; below in the third[^p255-6]: « No man hath ascended into heaven, but he that descended from heaven, the Son of man who is in heaven ». *He hath declared him*, because he saw; below in the third: « We speak what we know, and we testify what we have seen »; to the Hebrews, the first: « Last of all, in these days he hath spoken to us in the Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds ».

### Questions.

41. Quaest. I. But it is asked about what he says: *Full of grace and truth:* according to which nature was this said? Either according to the *divine*, or according to the *human.* Not according to the *divine*, because in him there is not grace, but whatever he has is nature. Nor according to the *humanity*, it seems, because he adds: *Of his fulness we have received grace for grace;* but grace is given by God alone, not by a man: therefore etc.

**I respond:** It must be said that in Christ there was *singular* grace, the greatest that could be in a man; just as also the grace of *union*, than which no greater can be thought out. By reason of both he is said to have been *full of grace*, and by reason of this twofold grace he was the head of the Church. And because the head overflows into all the members, therefore *of his fulness we all have received*[^p255-7].

As to what he asks, then: whether according to the divine nature, or the human? I say that Christ is called head according to the human nature united to the divine. Thus the fulness of *overflow* is to be understood[^p255-8].

42. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *Of his fulness we all have received.* — *Against* this is that many preceded Christ: therefore many were before the fulness of Christ: therefore not all received[^p255-9].

**I respond:** It must be said that all received, because Christ was the head of all the just from Abel to the last just man[^p255-10]. To this head the members are united by the bond of faith and dilection. Since, then, no one was saved without faith in Christ, as blessed Augustine says[^p255-11], no one was saved who did not receive of the fulness of Christ.

As to what is objected, that he followed; it must be said that by faith he was always present to all, although bodily he followed[^p255-12].

43. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *No man hath seen God at any time.*

**On the contrary:** 1. Numbers, the twelfth[^p255-13]: « I speak to him mouth to mouth, and plainly, and not by figures and riddles doth he see the Lord »; it is said of Moses: therefore he saw the Lord.

2. Likewise, Isaiah, the sixth[^p255-14]: « I saw the Lord sitting upon a throne high and elevated ».

**The response** to this is that there is knowing God *in himself* and *in his effect*[^p255-15]. And to know God *in effect* is to see *through a mirror*, and this in two ways: either through a *bright* mirror and eye, and thus the first man saw before the fall; or through a mirror
<!-- page 256 -->
*darkened*, and thus we see now, because on account of sin both our eyes have grown dim and all creatures have been darkened; hence we see now through a *mirror in an enigma*, the first to the Corinthians, the thirteenth[^p256-1].

In another way God is known *in himself;* and this in two ways: either *clearly*, and in this way by the Son alone and by the Blessed; in the other way *in darkness*, as blessed Dionysius says in the *Mystical Theology*[^p256-2]; and thus Moses saw, and those who contemplate sublimely, in whose gaze no image of a creature is fixed. And then indeed they sense [so printed: *sentinnt* for *sentiunt*] more than they know. Hence Bernard[^p256-3] says that he sometimes sensed something in himself which, when he wished to see it, he at once lost.
