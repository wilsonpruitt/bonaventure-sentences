# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. V, Vers. 10-18
## *Postquam descripta est miraculosa paralytici curatio, describitur hic secundo curationis calumniatio; in cuius descriptione procedit Evangelista hoc ordine*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 306 -->

19. *Erat autem Sabbatum* etc. Postquam descripta est miraculosa paralytici curatio, describitur hic secundo *curationis calumniatio;* in cuius descriptione[^p306-8] procedit Evangelista hoc ordine. Primo insinuatur *curati reprehensio;* secundo, *calumniae in curantem devolutio;* tertio, *curantis manifestatio;* quarto, *manifestati persecutio;* quinto, in *persecutione obstinatio.*

20. (Vers. 9.). Insinuatur ergo primo *curati reprehensio* ex hoc, quod tollebat grabatum in die Sabbati, in qua sanus factus fuerat. Propter quod innuens occasionem calumniandi dicit Evangelista: *Erat autem Sabbatum in die illa,* scilicet in qua curatus fuerat et grabatum portabat, et occasione huius Iudaei eum reprehendebant. Ideo subdit:

21. (Vers. 10.). *Dicebant ergo Iudaei ei qui sanus factus fuerat*[^p306-9], ipsum calumniando: *Sabbatum est, non licet tibi tollere grabatum tuum. Tollere enim grabatum* opus servile videbatur, quod prohibitum erat fieri in Sabbato; Levitici vigesimo tertio: « Dies septimus erit celebrior et sanctior; omne opus servile non facietis in eo ».

22. (Vers. 11.). *Respondit eis.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *calumniae in curantem devolutio,* quia ille infirmus sanatus se excusat per mandatum curantis. Ideo dicit: *Qui me sanum fecit,* per potestatem, *ille mihi dixit: Tolle grabatum tuum et ambula,* per propriam praecipiens auctoritatem. Et certe, qui tantam habebat potestatem bene habuit tanti mandati auctoritatem; unde dixit Matthaei nono[^p306-10]: « Ut autem sciatis, quia Filius hominis habet potestatem in terra dimittendi peccata; ait paralytico: Surge, tolle lectum tuum et vade in domum tuam ». Si peccata poterat dimittere, ergo in praecepto de Sabbato dispensare. Sed Iudaei, hoc non attendentes, de ipso interrogant, ut eum possint calumniari. Propter quod dicit:

23. (Vers. 12.). *Interrogaverunt ergo eum: Quis est homo ille,* homo infirmus, non Deus omnipotens, *qui dixit tibi: Tolle grabatum tuum et ambula?* qui contra Legem praecepit, non per virtutem sanavit. Mala quaerunt, bona tacent; Proverbiorum undecimo[^p306-11]: « Bene surgit diluculo qui quaerit bona; investigator malorum opprimetur ab eis ». Et quia male quaerunt, ideo in inquisitione deficiunt. Ideo dicit:

24. (Vers. 13.). *Is autem, qui sanus effectus fuerat, nesciebat, quis esset;* ideo in inquirendo necesse habuerunt deficere; Psalmus[^p306-12]: « Defecerunt scrutantes scrutinio ». Ideo dictum est Iudaeis infra
<!-- page 307 -->
septimo: « Quaeretis me, et non invenietis »; quia non quaerebant ad habendum, sed ad persequendum.

25. *Iesus autem*[^p307-1] *declinavit.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *Christi quaesiti et non inventi manifestatio,* quae facta est non in turba, sed in ecclesia. Ideo dicit: *Iesus autem declinavit a turba constituta in loco,* scilicet a multitudine, quae erat in loco curationis; ideo nesciebat eum ille, quia in turba remanserat.

26. (Vers. 14.). *Postea invenit eum Iesus in templo,* quando scilicet a turba recesserat. Augustinus[^p307-2]: « In turba positus non cognovit Iesum, sed post in templo »; in quo docemur, ut, quisquis ad visionem Dei vult pervenire, fugiat turbam suorum affectuum et nequitiam hominum et adeat internae orationis templum. Unde in suscitatione puellae praecepit, eiici turbam tumultuantem, Matthaei nono[^p307-3]. Et quia Domini misericordia nos praevenit, non dicit, quod *ipse* invenerit Iesum, sed quod *Iesus invenit eum;* unde nec cognovit ipsum nisi per eius eruditionem, quam dedit[^p307-4]. Propterea subdit: *Dicit ei Iesus,* instruens ut bonus medicus: *Ecce, sanus factus es,* recolit beneficium; *iam noli peccare,* dissuadet peccatum; *ne deterius tibi aliquid contingat,* comminatur periculum; Lucae undecimo: « Facta sunt novissima hominis illius peiora prioribus ».

27. (Vers. 15.). *Abiit ille homo.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *Christi manifestati persecutio.* Cognoverunt enim Iudaei, illo homine referente, quod Christus eum sanum fecisset. Unde dicit: *Abiit ille homo et nuntiavit Iudaeis,* qui scilicet ab eo quaesierant, *quia Iesus esset, qui fecit eum sanum.* Non hoc dixit detrahendo vel accusando, sed nomen Christi annuntiando; unde Glossa[^p307-5]: « Annuntiat iste salutem Iudaeis, ut sequantur; sed illi e contrario persequuntur ». Ideo subdit:

28. (Vers. 16.). *Propterea persequebantur Iudaei Iesum, quia haec faciebat in Sabbato.* Videbatur enim Legem eorum velle destruere et Domino, qui praeceptum de custodiendo Sabbato dederat, contraire; infra nono[^p307-6]: « Non est hic homo a Deo, qui Sabbatum non custodit »; Lucae sexto: « Observabant scribae et Pharisaei Iesum, si in Sabbato curaret ».

29. (Vers. 17.). *Iesus autem respondit.* Tangitur hic quintum, scilicet *in persecutione obstinatio,* quae consecuta est ad Domini responsionem ostendentis, se non solvere Sabbatum. Propter quod dicit: *Iesus autem respondit eis,* scilicet Iudaeis persequentibus: *Pater meus usque modo operatur,* etiam in Sabbato, et tamen non solvit Sabbatum; *et ego operor,* scilicet Patri cooperando etiam in Sabbato. Si ergo Deus non solvit Sabbatum, nec ego solvo; Matthaei duodecimo[^p307-7]: « Dominus est Filius hominis etiam Sabbati »; et quia *Dominus,* potest in eo sine reprehensione aliqua operari. Ex hac responsione Iudaei sunt in malitia obfirmati, ex qua debuerunt pacificari. Propterea subdit, scilicet propter istam responsionem:

30. (Vers. 18.). *Propterea ergo magis quaerebant eum Iudaei interficere;* quia non poterant responsioni contradicere, quaerebant eum interficere; infra septimo[^p307-8]: « Quid me quaeritis interficere, hominem, qui vera locutus sum vobis »? *Quia non solum solvebat Sabbatum,* secundum suam sententiam, *sed etiam Patrem suum dicebat Deum; suum,* singulariter; *aequalem se faciens Deo,* quod ipsi reputabant blasphemiam; infra decimo[^p307-9]: « De bono opere non lapidamus te, sed de blasphemia, et quia tu, homo cum sis, facis te ipsum Deum »; *faciens,* quasi non esset, sed se Deum faceret.

### Quaestiones.

31. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic: cum praeceptum de Sabbato sit praeceptum de prima tabula, in quibus, sicut dicit Bernardus[^p307-10], non potest Deus dispensare; videtur, quod Dominus male fecerit solvendo Sabbatum. — *Si dicas,* quod in praecepto est aliquid *morale* et aliquid *caeremoniale;* et secundum quod *morale* spectat ad primam tabulam, et sic non solvit; secundum quod *caeremoniale* non spectat ad Decalogum, et sic solvit[^p307-11];

Tunc 1. est quaestio: quid est in eo *morale,* et quid *caeremoniale?*

2. Item est quaestio: quare praeceptum tertium magis habet secum annexam observantiam caeremonialem quam aliquod aliud mandatum decalogi? — Et videtur, quod non deberet ei admisceri, sicut nec umbra veritati.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod, sicut dictum est, mandatum de Sabbato aliquid habet *morale,* habet
<!-- page 308 -->
etiam aliquid *caeremoniale;* et ideo in Lege non tantum ponitur inter moralia, sed etiam inter caeremonialia.

1. Quod ergo quaerit, quid sit in eo morale et caeremoniale; dico, quod in mandato illo praecipitur homo *vacare Deo,* ut templum illius fiat per dilectionem, et hoc est *morale.* Ulterius est ibi *significatio diei,* et praeter hoc *quietatio* ab omnibus operibus servilibus; et ista duo sunt *caeremonialia*[^p308-1].

2. Quod quaeritur: quare magis habet coniunctam caeremoniam? respondet Augustinus, ad Ianuarium[^p308-2], quod est praeceptum dilectionis, quia tertium praeceptum respicit tertiam in Trinitate personam, et praecipitur ibi dilectio. Ut ergo ostendatur, quod omnia caeremonialia sunt referenda ad dilectionem Dei et proximi et ad illum finem exponenda; ideo dilectionis mandato observantiam caeremonialem adiunxit. — Alia ratio potest reddi, quod in dilectionis mandato mandata cetera diriguntur et implentur[^p308-3]. — Rursus, in vacando Deo et considerando legem divinam diligens efficitur homo in eiusmodi observantia legis. Quia ergo in hoc mandato praecipitur dilectio et Deo vacatio, omnium mandatorum observantia pendet ex hoc; et ideo, hoc neglecto et in oblivionem tradito, cetera negligerentur. Ideo, ne *oblivioni traderetur,* significavit Dominus diem; ne etiam ob alia *negligeretur,* ab omnibus aliis cessare praecepit; et hoc *caeremoniale.*

32. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur, utrum Dominus mandatum de Sabbato solverit quantum ad observantiam caeremonialem. — Quod non, videtur: quia non prohibetur ibi nisi opus servile, et Dominus nullum opus servile in eo faciebat; unde Lucae decimo quarto[^p308-4] Dominus ostendit, quod non solvit.

Quod autem solverit, videtur: quia Iudaei propter hoc eum persequebantur.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod observatio eius quod erat caeremoniale in Sabbato, triplex erat, scilicet *spiritualis, litteralis* et *consuetudinalis. Spiritualem* non solvit nec solvere potuit, sed implevit; *litteralem* solvere potuit, non tamen solvit, quia nullum opus servile fecit[^p308-5]; *consuetudinalem* solvit et solvi praecepit: *solvit,* quando *fecit lutum ex sputo*[^p308-6]; *solvi praecepit,* quando grabatum praecepit sustolli. Hoc enim non erat contra observantiam litteralem, sed consuetudinalem et superinductam.

33. Quaest. III. Item obiicitur de hoc quod dicit Evangelista, quod *Iudaei persequebantur Iesum, quia haec faciebat in Sabbato:* si Dominus aeque bene hoc facere poterat in aliis diebus, ut in Sabbato; videtur, quod Dominus ipse sibi persecutionem excitaverit Iudaeorum nulla causa opportuna.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod ratio huius fuit *doctrinae veritas*[^p308-7]. Quia enim observantiae caeremoniales debebant in Christi adventu cessare, ipse, qui magister fuit veritatis, *debuit* hoc docere et per suam vitam ostendere, ut apud nos veritas Evangelii remaneret. — Secunda ratio fuit divini *iudicii severitas:* quia, sicut dicitur infra nono[^p308-8], *in iudicium ego veni in mundum* etc. Quia ergo ex hoc mali excaecabantur ad procurandam Christi mortem, quae fuit nostra salus; boni vero illuminabantur ad credendum, eum esse qui Legem dederat et solvere poterat: ideo etc. — Tertia ratio *communis necessitas,* quia tunc pauperes et simplices conveniebant ad synagogas, quos debebat Dominus attrahere per miracula.

34. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod Dominus respondit: *Pater meus usque modo operatur, et ego operor.* — *Contra:* Genesis secundo[^p308-9]: *Requievit Deus ab omni opere, quod patrarat:* ergo videtur, quod sit ibi contradictio.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod est opus primae *conditionis,* et hoc consistit in *creatione* eorum quae spectant ad complementum universi et includit in se *creationem, distinctionem* et *ornatum;* et hoc perfectum fuit in sex diebus. Ab hoc opere Deus requievit. Aliud est opus *conservationis*[^p308-10]; et quia res non possunt conservari in se ipsis, quia natae sunt per naturam corrumpi; consistit hoc opus in *transmutatione* et *propagatione* et *animarum creatione;* et hoc opere operatur Deus et cooperatur naturae cooperanti. Et de hoc opere intelligit hic, a quo non cessat in Sabbato[^p308-11].

## English
<!-- page 306 -->

19. *And it was the Sabbath* etc. After the miraculous cure of the paralytic has been described, there is described here, second, *the calumniation of the cure;* in the description[^p306-8] of which the Evangelist proceeds in this order. First there is intimated *the reproof of the one cured;* second, *the turning of the calumny upon the one curing;* third, *the manifestation of the one curing;* fourth, *the persecution of the one manifested;* fifth, *obstinacy* in *the persecution.*

20. (Verse 9.). First, then, there is intimated *the reproof of the one cured,* from this, that he was taking up his bed on the day of the Sabbath on which he had been made whole. On account of which, intimating the occasion of calumniating, the Evangelist says: *And it was the Sabbath on that day,* namely the day on which he had been cured and was carrying his bed, and on occasion of this the Jews were reproving him. Therefore he adds:

21. (Verse 10.). *The Jews therefore said to him that had been made whole*[^p306-9], calumniating him: *It is the Sabbath; it is not lawful for thee to take up thy bed. To take up a bed* seemed a servile work, which it was forbidden to do on the Sabbath; Leviticus, the twenty-third chapter: « The seventh day shall be more solemn and more holy; no servile work shall you do therein ».

22. (Verse 11.). *He answered them.* Here there is touched on the second, namely *the turning of the calumny upon the one curing,* because that infirm man, now healed, excuses himself through the command of the one curing. Therefore he says: *He that made me whole,* through power, *he said to me: Take up thy bed and walk,* commanding by his own authority. And certainly, he who had so great a power well had the authority of so great a command; whence he said, Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p306-10]: « But that you may know that the Son of man hath power on earth to forgive sins, he saith to the man sick of the palsy: Arise, take up thy bed, and go into thy house ». If he could forgive sins, then he could dispense in the precept about the Sabbath. But the Jews, not attending to this, ask about him, that they might be able to calumniate him. On account of which he says:

23. (Verse 12.). *They asked him therefore: Who is that man,* an infirm man, not God almighty, *who said to thee: Take up thy bed and walk?* who commanded against the Law, did not heal by virtue. They seek evil things, they keep silent about good things; Proverbs, the eleventh chapter[^p306-11]: « He riseth early well who seeketh good things; the searcher after evil things shall be oppressed by them ». And because they seek evilly, therefore they fail in the inquiry. Therefore he says:

24. (Verse 13.). *But he who had been made whole knew not who it was;* therefore in inquiring they of necessity failed; the Psalm[^p306-12]: « They that searched have failed in their search ». Therefore it was said to the Jews below, the
<!-- page 307 -->
seventh chapter: « You shall seek me, and shall not find me »; because they did not seek in order to have, but in order to persecute.

25. *But Jesus*[^p307-1] *went aside.* Here there is touched on the third, namely *the manifestation of Christ, sought and not found,* which was made not in the multitude, but in the church. Therefore he says: *But Jesus went aside from the multitude standing in the place,* namely from the multitude which was in the place of the cure; therefore that man did not know him, because he had remained in the multitude.

26. (Verse 14.). *Afterwards Jesus findeth him in the temple,* namely when he had withdrawn from the multitude. Augustine[^p307-2]: « Placed in the multitude he did not know Jesus, but afterwards in the temple »; in which we are taught that whoever wishes to come to the vision of God should flee the multitude of his own affections and the wickedness of men and go to the temple of inward prayer. Whence in the raising of the girl he commanded the tumultuous multitude to be cast out, Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p307-3]. And because the mercy of the Lord goes before us, he does not say that *he* found Jesus, but that *Jesus findeth him;* whence neither did he know him except through his erudition, which he gave[^p307-4]. On account of this he adds: *Jesus saith to him,* instructing him as a good physician: *Behold, thou art made whole,* he recalls the benefit; *sin no more,* he dissuades from sin; *lest some worse thing happen to thee,* he threatens the danger; Luke, the eleventh chapter: « The last state of that man was made worse than the first ».

27. (Verse 15.). *The man went his way.* Here there is touched on the fourth, namely *the persecution of Christ manifested.* For the Jews came to know, that man reporting it, that Christ had made him whole. Whence he says: *The man went his way and told the Jews,* namely those who had asked of him, *that it was Jesus who made him whole.* He did not say this by detracting or accusing, but by announcing the name of Christ; whence the Gloss[^p307-5]: « This man announces salvation to the Jews, that they may follow; but they on the contrary persecute ». Therefore he adds:

28. (Verse 16.). *Therefore did the Jews persecute Jesus, because he did these things on the Sabbath.* For he seemed to wish to destroy their Law and to go against the Lord, who had given the precept about keeping the Sabbath; below, the ninth chapter[^p307-6]: « This man is not of God, who keepeth not the Sabbath »; Luke, the sixth chapter: « The scribes and Pharisees watched Jesus, if he would cure on the Sabbath ».

29. (Verse 17.). *But Jesus answered.* Here there is touched on the fifth, namely *obstinacy in the persecution,* which followed upon the answer of the Lord showing that he did not break the Sabbath. On account of which he says: *But Jesus answered them,* namely the Jews who were persecuting: *My Father worketh until now,* even on the Sabbath, and yet does not break the Sabbath; *and I work,* namely by cooperating with the Father even on the Sabbath. If, then, God does not break the Sabbath, neither do I break it; Matthew, the twelfth chapter[^p307-7]: « The Son of man is Lord even of the Sabbath »; and because he is *Lord,* he can work on it without any reproach. By this answer the Jews were confirmed in malice, by which they ought to have been pacified. On account of this he adds, namely on account of this answer:

30. (Verse 18.). *Hereupon therefore the Jews sought the more to kill him;* because they could not contradict the answer, they sought to kill him; below, the seventh chapter[^p307-8]: « Why seek you to kill me, a man who have spoken true things to you »? *Because he did not only break the Sabbath,* according to their opinion, *but also said God was his own Father; his own,* singularly; *making himself equal to God,* which they themselves reckoned blasphemy; below, the tenth chapter[^p307-9]: « For a good work we stone thee not, but for blasphemy; and because that thou, being a man, makest thyself God »; *making,* as if he were not [God], but made himself God.

### Questions.

31. Question I. But it is asked here: since the precept about the Sabbath is a precept of the first table, in which, as Bernard says[^p307-10], God cannot dispense; it seems that the Lord did ill in breaking the Sabbath. — *If you say* that in the precept there is something *moral* and something *ceremonial;* and according as the *moral* pertains to the first table, so he did not break it; according as the *ceremonial* does not pertain to the Decalogue, so he did break it[^p307-11];

Then 1. the question is: what in it is *moral,* and what *ceremonial?*

2. Likewise the question is: why does the third precept have annexed to it a ceremonial observance more than any other commandment of the decalogue? — And it seems that it ought not to be mingled with it, as neither a shadow with the truth.

**I respond:** It must be said that, as has been said, the commandment about the Sabbath has something *moral,* it has
<!-- page 308 -->
also something *ceremonial;* and therefore in the Law it is placed not only among the moral precepts, but also among the ceremonial.

1. As to what, then, he asks, what in it is moral and ceremonial: I say that in that commandment man is commanded *to be free for God*[^tr-vacare], that he may become his temple through dilection, and this is *moral.* Further there is in it *the signification of the day,* and besides this *the quieting* from all servile works; and these two are *ceremonial*[^p308-1].

2. As to what is asked: why it has a ceremony more joined to it? Augustine answers, to Januarius[^p308-2], that it is the precept of dilection, because the third precept regards the third person in the Trinity, and dilection is commanded there. In order, then, that it might be shown that all ceremonial things are to be referred to the dilection of God and of neighbour and set forth for that end; therefore he joined the ceremonial observance to the commandment of dilection. — Another reason can be given, that in the commandment of dilection the other commandments are directed and fulfilled[^p308-3]. — Again, in being free for God and in considering the divine law a man is made diligent in observance of the law of this kind. Since, then, in this commandment dilection and being free for God are commanded, the observance of all the commandments hangs on this; and therefore, if this were neglected and given over to oblivion, the others would be neglected. Therefore, lest it *should be given over to oblivion,* the Lord signified the day; lest, again, it *should be neglected* on account of other things, he commanded a ceasing from all other things; and this is *ceremonial.*

32. Question II. Likewise it is asked whether the Lord broke the commandment about the Sabbath as regards the ceremonial observance. — That he did not, it seems: because nothing is forbidden there except servile work, and the Lord did no servile work on it; whence in Luke, the fourteenth chapter[^p308-4], the Lord shows that he does not break it.

But that he did break it, it seems: because the Jews persecuted him on account of this.

**I respond:** It must be said that the observance of what was ceremonial in the Sabbath was threefold, namely *spiritual, literal* and *customary.* The *spiritual* he did not break nor could he break it, but he fulfilled it; the *literal* he could break, yet he did not break it, because he did no servile work[^p308-5]; the *customary* he broke and commanded to be broken: *he broke it,* when *he made clay of the spittle*[^p308-6]; *he commanded it to be broken,* when he commanded the bed to be taken up. For this was not against the literal observance, but against the customary and superinduced.

33. Question III. Likewise an objection is made about what the Evangelist says, that *the Jews persecuted Jesus, because he did these things on the Sabbath:* if the Lord could do this equally well on other days as on the Sabbath; it seems that the Lord himself stirred up the persecution of the Jews against himself with no fitting cause.

**I respond:** It must be said that the reason of this was *the truth*[^p308-7] *of the teaching*[^tr-doctrina]. For since the ceremonial observances had to cease at the coming of Christ, he, who was the teacher of truth, *had* to teach this and to show it by his life, that the truth of the Gospel might remain with us. — The second reason was the *severity* of the divine *judgement:* because, as is said below, the ninth chapter[^p308-8], *For judgement I came into the world* etc. Since, then, from this the wicked were blinded to procuring the death of Christ, which was our salvation, but the good were enlightened to believing that he was the one who had given the Law and could break it: therefore etc. — The third reason, *a common necessity,* because at that time the poor and simple used to come together to the synagogues, whom the Lord had to draw by miracles.

34. Question IV. Likewise it is asked about this, that the Lord answered: *My Father worketh until now, and I work.* — *On the contrary:* Genesis, the second chapter[^p308-9]: *God rested from all the work which he had done:* therefore it seems that there is a contradiction there.

**I respond:** It must be said that there is the work of the first *founding,* and this consists in the *creation* of those things which pertain to the completion of the universe, and it includes in itself *creation, distinction* and *adornment;* and this was completed in six days. From this work God rested. Another is the work of *preservation*[^p308-10]; and because things cannot be preserved in themselves, since they are apt by nature to be corrupted, this work consists in *transmutation* and *propagation* and *the creation of souls;* and by this work God works and cooperates with nature cooperating. And of this work he understands here, from which he does not cease on the Sabbath[^p308-11].

[^tr-vacare]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin *vacare* holds together several senses that English has to split: being at leisure, being free, being free *for* something and being free *from* something. Here the commandment of the Sabbath is said to command man *vacare Deo*, with the dative, so the English takes the side of being free *for* God, and keeps it when the same reply speaks of *vacando Deo* and *Deo vacatio*: the point is what the day is given over to, as the dilection by which man becomes God's temple, while the ceasing from servile works is named separately as *quietatio*, "quieting", a ceremonial thing. No *vaca-* word is rendered "rest" in this translation, because *quies* and *requies* take that word.

[^tr-doctrina]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin *doctrina* holds together what English divides among doctrine, teaching and learning. Here the first reason for the Lord's working on the Sabbath is *doctrinae veritas*, and the next sentence explains it by the act of one *qui magister fuit veritatis* and *debuit hoc docere*: the truth of what he taught by teaching it and showing it in his life, so the English takes "teaching". Elsewhere this translation renders the same word "doctrine" where it names a body of tenets and "learning" where it names what a learner acquires.

**⭐ CONSTRUALS (rulings 74, 79–82 applied).**
- (a) ***vacare* — THE DIKAISUNE RULE, ONE `tr-vacare` NOTE.** Three sites in Quaest. I's reply: *praecipitur homo vacare Deo* (dative), *in vacando Deo*, *Deo vacatio* — all → "be free for God" / "being free for God", the note at the first. **Never "rest"**: the same reply sets *quietatio ab omnibus operibus servilibus* beside it as a separate, ceremonial member → **"quieting"** (not *quies*), and Quaest. IV's *Requievit Deus* (Gen. 2:2) / *requievit* → "rested" (the *requies* family keeps "rest").
- (b) ***doctrina* — ONE SITE, `tr-doctrina`** (Quaest. III *doctrinae veritas* → "the truth of the teaching", the English anchors split *the truth*⁷ / *of the teaching* so the Quaracchi anchor mirrors the Latin's *veritas*⁷). *magister fuit veritatis* → "the teacher of truth" (ruling 74). *eruditio* (¶ 26) → "instruction" (*instruens* → "instructing").
- (c) ***dilectio* → "dilection"** (ruling 74; six sites in Quaest. I's reply); *diligens efficitur homo* → "is made diligent" (the adjective, another word).
- (d) **Decalogue vocabulary — the *praeceptis* ruling**: *praeceptum* → precept, *mandatum* → commandment (including ¶ 22 *mandatum curantis* / *tanti mandati auctoritatem* → "command", the act, not the Decalogue — disclosed), *lex* / *Lex* → law / the Law; *Decalogus* / *decalogus* → the Decalogue / the decalogue (as printed). *praecipere* → command. *caeremonialis* / *caeremonia* / *moralis* → ceremonial / ceremony / moral. *solvere Sabbatum* → **"break the Sabbath"** (Douay 5:18) at every site, Quaestiones included (*solvi praecepit* → "commanded to be broken"; *Legem … solvere poterat* → "could break [the Law]"). *spiritualis / litteralis / consuetudinalis* → spiritual / literal / customary; *superinductam* → "superinduced".
- (e) ***virtus* (ruling 80(a), by sense)**: ¶ 23 *non per virtutem sanavit* → "did not heal by virtue", against ¶ 22's *per potestatem* / *potestatem* ×3 → "power" (Matt. 9:6 Douay "power") and *auctoritas* → authority.
- (f) ***curare* / *sanare* kept apart (the `c5-v1-9` register)**: *curatio* → cure, *curatus* → "the one cured", *curans* → "the one curing", Luke 6:7 *curaret* → "would cure" (Douay "heal" adjusted to keep the root); *sanare* / *sanatus* → heal / healed; *sanus* → whole (Douay 5:10, 5:13, 5:14, 5:15). *salus* → **"salvation"** at all three sites (¶ 27's Gloss *Annuntiat iste salutem Iudaeis*, ¶ 33 *quae fuit nostra salus*, p. 307 n. 5 Augustine *salutem suam* ×2) — here it is the saving news, not the bodily healing `c5-v1-9`'s Quaest. IV rendered "health"; disclosed.
- (g) ***turba* and *multitudo* both → "multitude"** (Douay 5:13 *a turba* "from the multitude"; ¶ 25 glosses *turba* by *multitudo*); *ecclesia* (¶ 25, lower case) → "the church".
