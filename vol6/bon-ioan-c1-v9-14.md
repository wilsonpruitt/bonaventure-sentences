## *De adventu Christi*

## Latin
<!-- page 251 -->

#### De adventu Christi.

24. *Erat lux vera* etc. Determinato adventu praecursoris, determinatur adventus *Regis* et determinatur cum suis proprietatibus et circumstantiis. Hunc ergo adventum Christi describit hoc ordine. Primo notatur *adveniendi ratio;* secundo, *advenientis despectio;* tertio vero, ipsius *adventus utilitas* sive fructus; quarto, *adveniendi modus.*

(Vers. 9.). *Ratio* igitur *adveniendi* in carnem primo tangitur, quae fuit: quia ipse Deus ante incarnationem, licet esset summa lux omnibus praesens, non cognoscebatur a mundo; ideo visibilis effectus est,
<!-- page 252 -->
ut cognosceretur. Propterea dicit: *Erat lux vera*, scilicet per *essentiam; quae illuminat omnem hominem venientem in hunc mundum;* infra octavo[^p252-1]: « Ego sum lux mundi; qui sequitur me non ambulat in tenebris ». Haec lux erat praesens mundo; unde in mundo erat per praesentiam; Sapientiae septimo: « Attingit ubique propter sui munditiam ».

25. (Vers. 10.). *In mundo erat*, per *effectum;* unde *et mundus per ipsum factus est*, per quem cognosci poterat; Sapientiae decimo tertio[^p252-2]: « A magnitudine speciei et creaturae poterat Creator horum cognoscibiliter videri ». Et tamen *mundus eum non cognovit; mundus*, id est mundani, metonymia est; infra decimo septimo[^p252-3]: « Pater iuste, mundus te non cognovit ». Haec est ratio, quare venit; unde Chrysostomus[^p252-4]: « Quia in mundo existens non putabatur adesse, eo quod nondum cognoscebatur; dignatus est induere carnem ».

26. (Vers. 11.). *In propria venit.* Tangitur hic *advenientis despectio*, et hoc maxime ab his qui debebant eum honorare, et de quorum genere assumsit carnem. Propter quod dicit: *In propria venit*, id est, de genere Iudaeorum, quos prae ceteris praeelegerat, carnem assumsit. *Et sui*, id est Iudaei, *eum non receperunt*, non quia nulli, sed quia pauci; Isaiae primo[^p252-5]: « Filios enutrivi et exaltavi, ipsi autem spreverunt me ». Iudaei dicti sunt *sui*, quia secundum carnem erat ex eorum genere, quia de semine David erat, qui erat rex in Israel usque in sempiternum. — Vel: *sui* per specialem electionem, secundum illud Deuteronomii vigesimo sexto[^p252-6]: « Elegit te Dominus in populum peculiarem ».

27. (Vers. 12.). *Quotquot autem.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *adventus ipsius fructus* sive utilitas, quae est divinae filiationis adeptio. Quia haec utilitas non se extendit, nisi ad eos qui eum receperunt et in eum crediderunt; ideo dicit: *Quotquot autem receperunt.* Ita *sui* eum non receperunt, sed *quotquot receperunt eum, dedit eis potestatem filios Dei fieri.* Et haec magna *utilitas;* primae Ioannis tertio[^p252-7]: « Videte, qualem caritatem dedit nobis Pater, ut filii Dei nominemur et simus »; et in hoc magna utilitas; unde ad Romanos octavo[^p252-8]: « Si filii, et heredes, heredes quidem Dei, coheredes autem Christi ». Ita ergo hanc potestatem filiationis Dei dedit *his qui receperunt.* Sed quia haec receptio est per fidem, ideo explicat: *His qui credunt in nomine eius;* ita *dedit eis qui receperunt, his qui credunt in nomine eius*, id est in re nominis eius. Nomen eius est *Emmanuel*, id est *nobiscum Deus*[^p252-9]. Ille igitur *credit in nomine*, qui credit eum Deum et hominem; et hic est filius Dei; primae Ioannis quinto: « Omnis, qui credit, quoniam Iesus est Filius Dei, ex Deo natus est ». Quia vero filii Dei non fiunt nisi per generationem spiritualem, non carnalem, ideo dicit:

28. (Vers. 13.). *Qui non ex sanguinibus*, id est commixtione seminum; *neque ex voluntate carnis*, id est mulieris; *neque ex voluntate viri*, id est delectatione et pruritu; *sed ex Deo nati sunt;* quia infra tertio[^p252-10]: « Quod natum est ex carne caro est, et quod natum est ex spiritu spiritus est »; et primae Petri primo: « Renati non ex semine corruptibili, sed incorruptibili, per verbum Dei vivi et permanentis in aeternum », hoc est semper.

29. (Vers. 14.). *Et Verbum caro factum est.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *adveniendi modus*, qui fuit per carnis assumtionem, in qua fuit *summa* unio; ideo dicit: *Verbum caro factum est;* id est carni unitum; ad Philippenses secundo[^p252-11]: « Humiliavit semetipsum, formam servi accipiens ». Et quia haec unio fuit *inconfusa*, fuit etiam *indivisa;* ideo dicit: *Et habitavit in nobis*, id est in nostra natura: *habitavit*, ergo non est *conversus; habitavit*, ergo non est *separatus.* De hac habitatione Levitici vigesimo sexto[^p252-12]: « Ponam tabernaculum meum in medio vestri, et non abiiciet vos anima mea »; et in Psalmo: « In pace factus est locus eius, et habitatio eius in Sion ».

### Quaestiones.

30. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur primo de eo quod dicit: *Erat lux vera, quae illuminat omnem hominem venientem* etc.; quia dictum est supra[^p252-13], quod aliqui homines sunt *tenebrae:* ergo non omnes illuminat. — *Si tu dicas:* quantum in se est: ergo si
<!-- page 253 -->
voluntati eius nullus resistere potest[^p253-1], necesse est, omnes illuminari.

Ad hoc respondet Augustinus[^p253-2] sic: « Illuminat omnem, scilicet qui illuminatur, quia nullus illuminatur nisi per ipsum ». Exemplum de magistro docente pueros. — Beda[^p253-3] respondet, quod illud intelligitur de lumine *naturae*, non gratiae; et quilibet habet signatum lumen vultus Dei. — Chrysostomus[^p253-4] vero exponit: quia paratus est illuminare; sed non omnes illuminantur, quia sibi claudunt viam luminis. Exemplum de caeco existente in sole.

Quod tu obiicis: si vult, facit; dicendum, quod *absolute* non vult illuminare nisi eos qui volunt; sed voluntate *conditionali* omnes vult illuminari, sicut *omnes homines salvos fieri*[^p253-5].

31. Quaest. II. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *In propria venit.*

1. Si enim eius est *venire*, cuius est *abesse;* et Deus nusquam abest: ergo non venit.

2. Item, si Deus potest omnia ubique, ergo non indiget propter aliquid venire: ergo si non venisset, idem facere potuisset.

Ad hoc respondet Beda[^p253-6]: « In mundo erat per Divinitatem, in mundum venit per incarnationem. *Venire* quippe vel *abire* est humanitatis; sed *manere* Divinitatis »; unde sensus est: *Venit*, id est, visibilis apparuit.

Quod tu quaeris: quae necessitas veniendi? Respondeo[^p253-7]: Non propter se, sed propter nos; et hoc bene innuit Evangelista: quia non cognoscebat mundus sublimitatem, apparuit in humilitate et humanitate.

32. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Dedit eis potestatem filios Dei fieri, his qui credunt* etc.

**Contra:** 1. Augustinus de Praedestinatione Sanctorum[^p253-8]: « Posse credere naturae est »: ergo a natura habent hanc potestatem, non ergo datam a Christo.

2. Item, quare magis dicit de credentibus quam de amantibus, vel aliis?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod est *posse* perfectum et activum, et hoc dicitur *posse potestatis;* et est *posse* imperfectum et passivum, et hoc dicitur *possibilitatis*[^p253-9]. Primum *posse* non est nisi per gratiam, et de illo loquitur hic; ideo dicit: *Dedit eis potestatem.* De alio loquitur Augustinus.

2. Quod quaerit: quare magis fidei? Responsio est: quia fides est prima virtutum et fundamentum[^p253-10], ideo ei attribuit ut ianuae.

33. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Verbum caro factum est.* Hic dicuntur tria verba, et quaestio est: quare magis dixit *verbum* quam *lux*, cum descriptum sit Verbum et sub ratione verbi et sub ratione lucis? — *Si tu dicas*, quod *lux* est communis tribus personis, *verbum* soli Filio, qui solus est incarnatus; tunc est quaestio: quare non dixit *Filius?* Et quare solus Filius est incarnatus, cum divina bonitas sit summe communicativa in qualibet hypostasum?

Respondeo ad hoc, quod solus Filius est incarnatus sumitur ratio ex parte *nativitatis*, *operis* et *effectus.* Ratione *nativitatis:* quia[^p253-11] *filius Virginis*, et non debuit *filius* alii personae attribui. Ratione *operis:* quia *recreatio*, et per ipsum est recreatio[^p253-12], per quem creatio. Ex parte *effectus:* quia *nos fecit filios* et coheredes, per Filium naturalem debemus in hereditatem et filiationem induci[^p253-13].

Magis autem dixit *verbum* quam *filius*, ut excludat intellectum carnalem et manifestet spiritualem. *Filius* enim carnaliter est generatus in creatu-
<!-- page 254 -->
ris, sed *verbum* spiritualem habet generationem. Manifestat spiritualem, quia, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p254-1], verbum induit vocem et non transit in vocem; sic Verbum aeternum induit carnem.

34. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Factum est caro;* quia aut est sensus: *factum est caro*, id est mutatum in carnem; aut *factum caro*, id est factum, ut sit caro. Neuter intellectus sanus est. — *Si tu dicas*, quod synecdochica est constructio, et *caro* ibi tantum valet quantum *homo*[^p254-2]; unde *factum caro*, id est *homo;* tunc quaeritur: quare magis voluit humanitatem intelligi per *carnem* quam per *animam*, cum anima sit nobilior pars? — Et quare usus est hac locutione figurativa, cum posset uti propria?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod illa unio fuit ad nostram *eruditionem* et nostram *salutem;* et eruditio fuit, quia *visibilis;* et liberatio, quia *passibilis;* et caro dicit partem visibilem et maxime passibilem: ideo[^p254-3] etc.

#### On the coming of Christ.

24. *That was the true light* etc. The coming of the forerunner having been determined, there is determined the coming of the *King*, and it is determined with its properties and circumstances. This coming of Christ, then, he describes in this order. First there is noted the *reason for coming;* second, the *contempt of the one coming;* third, the *usefulness* or fruit *of the coming* itself; fourth, the *mode of coming.*

(Vers. 9.). The *reason*, then, *for coming* into flesh is touched first, which was this: because God himself before the incarnation, although he was the highest light present to all things, was not known by the world; therefore he was made visible,
<!-- page 252 -->
that he might be known. Therefore he says: *That was the true light*, namely by *essence; which illumines every man that cometh into this world;* below in the eighth[^p252-1]: « I am the light of the world; he that followeth me walketh not in darkness ». This light was present to the world; hence he was in the world by presence; Wisdom, the seventh: « It reacheth everywhere by reason of its purity ».

25. (Vers. 10.). *He was in the world*, by *effect;* hence *and the world was made by him*, through which he could be known; Wisdom, the thirteenth[^p252-2]: « By the greatness of the beauty and of the creature the Creator of them could be seen, so as to be known thereby ». And yet *the world knew him not; the world*, that is, worldly men, is a metonymy; below in the seventeenth[^p252-3]: « Just Father, the world hath not known thee ». This is the reason why he came; hence Chrysostom[^p252-4]: « Because, existing in the world, he was not thought to be present, since he was not yet known, he deigned to put on flesh ».

26. (Vers. 11.). *He came unto his own.* Here is touched the *contempt of the one coming*, and this chiefly by those who ought to have honoured him, and of whose race he assumed flesh. On account of which he says: *He came unto his own*, that is, he assumed flesh of the race of the Jews, whom he had chosen before the rest. *And his own*, that is, the Jews, *received him not*, not because none did, but because few did; Isaiah, the first[^p252-5]: « I have brought up children and exalted them, but they have despised me ». The Jews are called *his own* because according to the flesh he was of their race, because he was of the seed of David, who was king in Israel unto everlasting. — Or: *his own* by special election, according to that saying of Deuteronomy, the twenty-sixth[^p252-6]: « The Lord hath chosen thee to be a peculiar people ».

27. (Vers. 12.). *But as many as.* Here is touched the third, namely the *fruit of the coming itself* or its usefulness, which is the obtaining of divine sonship. Because this usefulness does not extend itself except to those who received him and believed in him; therefore he says: *But as many as received.* Thus *his own* received him not, but *as many as received him, he gave them power to be made the sons of God.* And this is a great *usefulness;* the first of John, the third[^p252-7]: « Behold what manner of charity the Father hath bestowed upon us, that we should be called and should be the sons of God »; and in this is a great usefulness; hence to the Romans, the eighth[^p252-8]: « If sons, heirs also; heirs indeed of God, and joint heirs with Christ ». Thus, then, he gave this power of the sonship of God *to those who received.* But because this receiving is through faith, therefore he explains: *To them that believe in his name;* thus *he gave to those who received, to them that believe in his name*, that is, in the reality of his name. His name is *Emmanuel*, that is, *God with us*[^p252-9]. He, then, *believes in the name* who believes him to be God and man; and this man is a son of God; the first of John, the fifth: « Whosoever believeth that Jesus is the Son of God, is born of God ». But because sons of God are not made except through spiritual generation, not carnal, therefore he says:

28. (Vers. 13.). *Who not of blood*, that is, of the commingling of seeds; *nor of the will of the flesh*, that is, of the woman; *nor of the will of man*, that is, of delight and itching; *but are born of God;* because below in the third[^p252-10]: « That which is born of the flesh is flesh, and that which is born of the spirit is spirit »; and the first of Peter, the first: « Being born again not of corruptible seed, but incorruptible, by the word of God who liveth and remaineth for ever », that is, always.

29. (Vers. 14.). *And the Word was made flesh.* Here is touched the fourth, namely the *mode of coming*, which was through the assumption of flesh, in which there was the *highest* union; therefore he says: *The Word was made flesh;* that is, united to flesh; to the Philippians, the second[^p252-11]: « He humbled himself, taking the form of a servant ». And because this union was *unconfused*, it was also *undivided;* therefore he says: *And dwelt in us*, that is, in our nature: *he dwelt*, therefore he was not *converted; he dwelt*, therefore he was not *separated.* Of this dwelling, Leviticus, the twenty-sixth[^p252-12]: « I will set my tabernacle in the midst of you, and my soul shall not cast you off »; and in the Psalm: « His place is in peace, and his abode in Sion ».

### Questions.

30. Quaest. I. But it is asked first about what he says: *That was the true light, which illumines every man that cometh* etc.; because it was said above[^p252-13] that some men are *darkness:* therefore he does not illumine all. — *If you say:* as far as is in him: then if
<!-- page 253 -->
no one can resist his will[^p253-1], it is necessary that all be illumined.

To this Augustine[^p253-2] responds thus: « He illumines every one, namely who is illumined, because no one is illumined except through him ». An example of a teacher teaching boys. — Bede[^p253-3] responds that it is understood of the lumen[^tr-lumen] of *nature*, not of grace; and each one has the lumen of the countenance of God signed upon him. — But Chrysostom[^p253-4] expounds: because he is ready to illumine; but not all are illumined, because they close to themselves the way of the lumen. An example of a blind man standing in the sun.

As to what you object: if he wills, he does; it must be said that *absolutely* he does not will to illumine any but those who will; but by a *conditional* will he wills all to be illumined, just as *all men to be saved*[^p253-5].

31. Quaest. II. It is asked about what he says: *He came unto his own.*

1. For if to *come* belongs to him to whom it belongs to *be absent;* and God is nowhere absent: therefore he did not come.

2. Likewise, if God can do all things everywhere, then he does not need to come for anything: therefore if he had not come, he could have done the same.

To this Bede[^p253-6] responds: « He was in the world by the Divinity, he came into the world by the incarnation. For to *come* or to *go away* belongs to the humanity; but to *remain*, to the Divinity »; hence the sense is: *He came*, that is, he appeared visible.

As to what you ask: what necessity was there of coming? I respond[^p253-7]: Not on account of himself, but on account of us; and the Evangelist well intimates this: because the world did not know his sublimity, he appeared in humility and humanity.

32. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *He gave them power to be made the sons of God, to them that believe* etc.

**On the contrary:** 1. Augustine, *On the Predestination of the Saints*[^p253-8]: « To be able to believe is of nature »: therefore they have this power from nature, therefore not given by Christ.

2. Likewise, why does he speak of those believing rather than of those loving, or of others?

**I respond:** It must be said that there is a *being able* that is perfect and active, and this is called the *being able of power;* and there is a *being able* that is imperfect and passive, and this is called *of possibility*[^p253-9]. The first *being able* is not except through grace, and of that he speaks here; therefore he says: *He gave them power.* Of the other Augustine speaks.

2. As to what he asks: why of faith rather? The response is: because faith is the first of the virtues and the foundation[^p253-10], therefore he attributes it to faith as to the door.

33. Quaest. IV. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *The Word was made flesh.* Here three words are said, and the question is: why did he say *word* rather than *light*, since the Word was described both under the account of word and under the account of light? — *If you say* that *light* is common to the three persons, *word* proper to the Son alone, who alone is incarnate; then the question is: why did he not say *Son?* And why is the Son alone incarnate, since the divine goodness is supremely communicative in each one of the hypostases?

I respond to this, that the reason why the Son alone is incarnate is taken on the part of *nativity*, of *work* and of *effect.* By reason of *nativity:* because[^p253-11] *son of the Virgin*, and *son* ought not to be attributed to another person. By reason of *work:* because *re-creation*, and through him is re-creation[^p253-12] through whom is creation. On the part of *effect:* because *he made us sons* and joint heirs, we ought to be brought into the inheritance and the sonship through the natural Son[^p253-13].

But he said *word* rather than *son*, in order to exclude a carnal understanding and to manifest a spiritual one. For a *son* is generated carnally in creatures,
<!-- page 254 -->
but a *word* has a spiritual generation. It manifests a spiritual one, because, as Augustine says[^p254-1], a word puts on a voice and does not pass into the voice; so the eternal Word put on flesh.

34. Quaest. V. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *Was made flesh;* because either the sense is: *was made flesh*, that is, changed into flesh; or *made flesh*, that is, made so as to be flesh. Neither understanding is sound. — *If you say* that it is a synecdochic construction, and *flesh* there is worth as much as *man*[^p254-2]; hence *made flesh*, that is, *man;* then it is asked: why did he wish the humanity to be understood by *flesh* rather than by *soul*, since the soul is the nobler part? — And why did he use this figurative locution, when he could have used a proper one?

**I respond:** It must be said that that union was for our *instruction* and our *salvation;* and the instruction was because it was *visible;* and the liberation, because it was *passible;* and flesh names the part that is visible and most passible: therefore[^p254-3] etc.
