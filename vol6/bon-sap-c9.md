# Commentarius in librum Sapientiae — Cap. IX, Vers. 1-19
## *Tertio ostendit, quam devote sapientiam a Deo petierit*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 166 -->

### Capitulum IX.

#### *Tertio ostendit, quam devote sapientiam a Deo petierit.*

*Deus patrum meorum* etc. Postquam ostendit, qualiter sapientiam acceperit et quantum acceptam amaverit; hic ostendit, quam devote eam a Deo *petierit.* Et primo tangit *petitionis formam;* secundo, *exauditionis rationem: Tu autem*[^p166-9] *elegisti;* tertio, *petitionis finem: Mitte illam;* quarto, *finis difficultatem: Quis enim* etc.

#### Primo tangitur petitionis forma.

In prima parte primo *decorem laudat;* secundo, *donum specificat: Da mihi sedium;* tertio, *se humiliat: Quoniam servus;* quarto, *indigentiam insinuat: Et*[^p166-10] *si quis* etc.

(Vers. 1.). *Deus patrum meorum,* scilicet per gratiam electionis; *patrum,* scilicet antiquorum; Exodi tertio[^p166-11]: « Ego sum Deus patris tui, Deus Abraham » etc. Vel: *Deus patrum meorum,* id est, qui fecisti patres meos et elegisti et sanctificasti eos. *Et Domine misericordiae,* id est Domine misericors, ita quod sit intransitiva constructio; vel: *Domine misericordiae,* auctor, ita quod sit transitiva; in Psalmo[^p166-12]: « Miserator et misericors Dominus ». *Domine,* inquam, *misericordiae,* scilicet per effectum remissionis peccatorum et iustificationis; misericordia enim respicit miseriam; Proverbiorum decimo quarto: « Miseros autem facit populos peccatum ». *Qui fecisti*
<!-- page 167 -->
*omnia,* scilicet per opus creationis, ut patet Genesis primo[^p167-1]; in Psalmo: « Dixit, et facta sunt »; *Verbo tuo,* id est per Filium tuum; Ioannis primo: « In principio erat Verbum » etc., et post: « Omnia per ipsum facta sunt ».

(Vers. 2.). *Et sapientia tua,* id est eodem Filio tuo, secundum illud Psalmi[^p167-2]: « Omnia in sapientia fecisti »; *constituisti,* id est, ex partibus valde diversis composuisti, scilicet ex spiritu rationali et luto terrestri; *hominem;* Ecclesiastici decimo septimo: « Deus de terra creavit hominem ». Et notandum, quod cetera dicit facta solo *Verbo,* sed hominem *sapientia,* quasi opus nobilius, quia capax sapientiae tam creatae quam increatae, et ideo quasi opus maiori excogitatione faciendum; propter quod Dominus, quasi deliberans, ait Genesis primo[^p167-3]: « Faciamus hominem ad imaginem » etc. *Ut dominaretur creaturae, quae a te facta est,* creaturae, scilicet non spirituali vel angelicae, sed corporali, et hoc per potentiam; Genesis primo: « Dominamini piscibus maris » etc.

(Vers. 3.). *Ut disponat orbem terrarum;* scilicet per sapientiam; est enim homo positus in hoc mundo, sicut rex in regno. *In aequitate,* id est misericordia, *et iustitia;* haec enim duo in disponendo iungi debent; Gregorius[^p167-4]: « Disciplina et misericordia multum destituitur, si una sine altera teneatur ». *Et in directione cordis,* scilicet per bonitatem, et hoc per rectam ad Deum intentionem; in Psalmo[^p167-5]: « Confitebor tibi in directione cordis »; Iob trigesimo quarto: « Si direxerit ad Deum cor suum, spiritum illius et flatum ad se trahet »; *iudicium iudicet,* scilicet super subditos. Nomine autem *iudicii* simpliciter dicti intelligit iudicium « verum[^p167-6] » vel iustum. Sicut enim homo pictus vel mortuus non est homo, sic falsum vel iniustum iudicium non est iudicium.

(Vers. 4.). *Da mihi,* « a quo omne datum optimum », Iacobi primo[^p167-7]; *sapientiam,* scilicet intellectum, *sedium tuarum assistricem,* id est sanctarum animarum inhabitatricem; supra septimo: « Per nationes in animas sanctas se transfert ». Sanctae autem animae dicuntur *sedes Dei,* quia in eis sedet Deus et quiescit praesidens et imperans omnibus earum motibus et affectionibus; Isaiae ultimo[^p167-8]: « Caelum mihi sedes est »; Glossa: « Anima iusti sedes est sapientiae ». *Et noli me reprobare,* dando mihi bonitatem et gratiam in affectu; *noli,* inquam, *reprobare me,* sicut illos, de quibus ad Romanos primo[^p167-9]: « Tradidit illos in reprobum sensum »; Ieremiae sexto: « Argentum reprobum vocate eos ». *A pueris tuis,* scilicet a puerorum tuorum numero et consortio excludendo, de quibus Isaiae octavo: « Ecce, ego et pueri mei » etc.

(Vers. 5.). *Quoniam* etc., quasi dicat: ideo peto, ut des mihi sapientiam, *quoniam servus tuus sum ego,* id est tibi paratus servire; in Psalmo[^p167-10]: « Servus tuus sum ego, da mihi intellectum »; tertii Regum decimo: « Beati servi tui, qui audiunt sapientiam tuam ». *Et filius ancillae tuae,* id est synagogae, de qua ad Galatas quarto: « Scriptum est, quoniam Abraham duos filios habuit ». Secundum Glossam[^p167-11] verba ista sunt « Filii ad Patrem », secundum quod homo sapientiam pro membris petentis, se servum eius secundum humanitatem, non filium allegantis; Isaiae quadragesimo nono: « Servus meus es tu, Israel, quia in te gloriabor ». *Et filius ancillae tuae,* id est Virginis Mariae, secundum illud Lucae primo[^p167-12]: « Ecce, ancilla Domini ». *Homo infirmus,* ex parte corporis propter corporis fragilitatem; in Psalmo: « Miserere mei, Domine, quoniam infirmus sum ». *Et exigui temporis,* propter vitae brevitatem; Iob decimo quarto: « Homo natus de muliere, brevi vivens tempore »; *et minor,* ex parte animae, id est ex naturali virtute insufficiens, *ad intellectum;* Glossa[^p167-13]: « Ad percipiendum »; *iudicii,* id est iudiciorum tuorum, quorum exemplo iudicandum est; *et legum,* tuarum scilicet aeternarum, quarum regulatione iudicandum est; ad Romanos undecimo: « O altitudo divitiarum sapientiae et scientiae Dei, quam incomprehensibilia sunt iudicia eius, et investigabiles viae eius »!

(Vers. 6.). *Et*[^p167-14] *si quis* etc., in hoc innuitur caritas, *erit consummatus,* id est perfectus; Glossa: « In iudicio »; *inter filios hominum,* qui ex viro
<!-- page 168 -->
scilicet et muliere nascuntur ad differentiam filii hominis, id est solius Virginis, de quo Matthaei decimo sexto: « Quem dicunt homines esse filium hominis »? Et dicit: *consummatus inter filios hominum,* quoniam potest esse purus homo per naturam. *Si ab illo fugerit* vel *aufugerit*[^p168-1], scilicet tanquam ab indigno et peccatore, secundum illud supra primo: « In malevolam animam non introibit sapientia »; *sapientia tua;* Glossa: « Divina », non mundana. Mundana enim propter peccatum non fugit; Ieremiae quarto: « Sapientes sunt, ut faciant mala ». *In nihilum,* scilicet moris, non naturae[^p168-2], *computabitur,* scilicet apud Deum et Sanctos eius; infra decimo tertio: « Vani sunt omnes homines, in quibus non subest scientia Dei »; per gratiam enim est homo id quod est quantum ad *esse* moris; unde primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto: « Gratia Dei sum id quod sum »; item, sine caritate homo nihil est, ut patet primae ad Corinthios decimo tertio; et secundum Augustinum peccator nihil est, scilicet in eo, quod talis.

#### Secundo, exauditionis ratio duplex.

*Tu autem*[^p168-3] *elegisti* etc. Hic subdit multiplicem *rationem exauditionis,* quarum prima est iniunctum *officium regni;* secunda est *mandatum templi aedificandi: Et dixisti* etc.

(Vers. 7.). *Tu autem* etc., quasi dicat ita: sum per me insufficiens sine sapientia. *Autem,* pro sed; nihilominus *tu elegisti me;* Glossa[^p168-4]: « Salomonem, vel Christum ». *Regem populo tuo,* id est in te credenti; in Psalmo: « Ego autem constitutus sum rex ab eo super Sion » etc.; *et iudicem filiorum tuorum et filiarum,* scilicet tuarum, id est fidelium, secundum illud Ioannis primo: « Dedit eis potestatem filios Dei fieri, his qui credunt in nomine eius ». *Regis* officium est et in negotiis aliis regni suum exercere iudicium; *iudicis* vero specialiter est in causis populi.

(Vers. 8.). *Et dixisti;* Glossa[^p168-5]: « Iussisti »; David enim, qui vir sanguinum erat, non fuit permissus templum aedificare; sed Salomon pacificus ad hoc fuit electus, ut patet secundi Regum septimo et primi Paralipomenon vigesimo nono. *Et dixisti,* inquam, *aedificare me templum in monte sancto tuo,* id est in monte Moria, ubi sancta fieri voluisti; *et in civitate habitationis tuae,* scilicet Ierusalem, ubi habitare dicebatur, quia ibi excellentius colebatur[^p168-6]; *altare,* scilicet holocaustorum, *holocaustorum,* dico, ad differentiam altaris aenei, quod erat in atrio ante templum sub divo. *Similitudinem tabernaculi tui sancti,* id est ad eius exemplar, de quo Exodi vigesimo quinto[^p168-7], quod factum est ad exemplar caelestis tabernaculi; unde ibidem: « Inspice et fac secundum exemplar, quod tibi in monte monstratum est ». *Quod praeparasti;* Glossa[^p168-8]: « Per Moysen »; *ab initio,* non creationis, sed dationis Legis; unde Glossa: « Cum educeres filios Israel de Aegypto »; *tabernaculum* in deserto, ut dicit Glossa, signabat Ecclesiam militantem, sed *templum* in Ierusalem triumphantem. Ratio huius est, quia tabernaculum in deserto erat mobile, sed templum in Ierusalem erat immobile.

(Vers. 9.). *Et tecum,* scilicet praeparavit, *sapientia tua,* scilicet increata, « id est Filius tuus[^p168-9] », de quo primae ad Corinthios primo: « Christum Dei virtutem atque sapientiam ». *Quae novit opera tua,* quasi cooperatrix omnium, secundum illud Psalmi: « Omnia in sapientia fecisti ». *Quae affuit,* tecum praesens, *tunc,* scilicet *cum orbem terrarum faceres;* Proverbiorum octavo: « Quando appendebat fundamenta terrae, eram cum eo cuncta componens »; et in eodem: « Delectabar cum eo per singulos dies, ludens in orbe terrarum ». *Et sciebat, quod id*[^p168-10] *placitum esset oculis tuis,* scilicet quoad consilia; *oculis tuis,* scilicet intellectus et affectus tui, id est misericordiae et veritatis tuae, « in omni enim opere Dei misericordia et veritas[^p168-11] »; Ecclesiastici quadragesimo secundo: « Invenit gratiam coram oculis Domini »,
<!-- page 169 -->
secundum aliam translationem. *Et quod directum;* Glossa: « Id est rectum et iustum »; *in praeceptis tuis;* in Psalmo: « Iustitiae Domini rectae, laetificantes corda ».

#### Tertio, petitionis finis dupliciter tangitur.

*Mitte illam* etc. Hic ostendit *finem petitionis,* id est, ad quid sapientiam quaesierit; et primo *petit sapientiam* ad sciendum et faciendum Dei voluntatem. Secundo *ostendit,* quod *valet* ad talis finis adeptionem: *Scit enim.*

(Vers. 10.). Dicit ergo: *Mitte illam,* scilicet sapientiam etc. Notandum, quod duplex est missio Sapientiae, scilicet una visibilis in carnem, de qua Ioannis decimo[^p169-1]: « Quem Pater sanctificavit et misit in mundum »; alia in mentem, quam petit hic. *De caelis sanctis tuis,* id est de te ipso, qui caelum es omnia continendo; in Psalmo: « A summo caelo egressio eius »; item Iacobi tertio: « Quae desursum est sapientia primum quidem pudica est » etc. Dicit autem *de caelis* pluraliter propter multitudinem contentorum et effectuum in contentis, sicut dicuntur *dies aeternitatis* pluraliter; Michaeae quinto[^p169-2]: « Et egressus eius, sicut a principio dierum aeternitatis ». *Et a sede magnitudinis tuae;* Glossa: « A te ipso, qui sedens et quietus, omnia gubernans »; Isaiae decimo octavo: « Ego quiescam et considerabo in loco meo, sicut lux meridiana clara est ».

Et notandum, quod Sapientia increata quandoque dicitur *esse in sinu Patris,* ut Ioannis primo[^p169-3]: « Unigenitus, qui est in sinu Patris, enarravit nobis »; aliquando *gigni ex utero,* in Psalmo: « Ex utero ante luciferum genui te »; aliquando *ex ore Patris procedere,* Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto: « Ego ex ore Altissimi prodii primogenita ante omnem creaturam »; aliquando *de caelo mitti,* ut hic; aliquando *sedere in sanctis animabus,* unde supra eodem: « Da mihi sedium tuarum assistricem sapientiam » etc. Primum propter *occultationem,* secundum propter *consubstantialitatem,* tertium propter *manifestationem,* quartum propter *illuminationem,* quintum propter *tranquillitatem.*

*Ut mecum sit,* scilicet intus in animae essentia et per gratiam *inhabitando* et informando; Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto[^p169-4]: « In Iacob inhabita » etc. *Et mecum laboret,* motivam animae potentiam ad opus *perficiendo;* Ecclesiastici ultimo: « Memoratus sum misericordiae tuae, Domine, et operationis tuae, quae a saeculo sunt ». *Ut sciam,* id est, ut scire me facias meae animae potentiam cognitivam *illustrando, quid acceptum sit coram te,* vel *apud te*[^p169-5]; in Psalmo: « Doce me facere voluntatem tuam ».

(Vers. 11.). *Scit enim omnia illa,* omnia scilicet ad scientiam rationis pertinentia, sicut sunt inferiora; *et intelligit,* omnia scilicet pertinentia ad intelligentiam intellectus, ut sunt superiora, supra septimo[^p169-6]: « Omnia prospiciens ». *Et deducet me in operibus meis sobrie;* Glossa[^p169-7]: « Ne offendam », dirigendo in opere bono; supra octavo: « Sobrietatem docet » etc. *Et custodiet me in sua potentia;* Glossa: « Ab adversariis » protegendo; in Psalmo: « Nisi Dominus custodierit civitatem » etc.; item: « Ecce, non dormitabit neque dormiet » etc.

(Vers. 12.). *Et erunt accepta opera mea;* Glossa[^p169-8]: « Coram Deo », tanquam facta pure ex amore suo. *Et disponam populum tuum iuste,* scilicet recte subditos gubernando; unde supra octavo: « Disponam populos et nationes ». *Et dignus ero sedium patris mei,* ipsum imitando; *sedium* dicit pluraliter, quia sedes praesentis regni, secundum illud Proverbiorum octavo: « Per me reges regnant »; et futuri; Glossa: « Remuneratione caelestis regni ».

#### Quarto, finis huius difficultas dupliciter ostenditur.

*Quis enim hominum* etc. Hic ostendit *finis difficultatem,* primo ostendens, quod Dei voluntas *sine sapientia sciri non potest;* secundo, quod solum *per eam sciri potest: Sensum autem tuum.* — Quod *sine illa sciri non possit,* ostendit primo ex *divini sensus profunditate;* secundo, ex *sensus humani imperfectione: Cogitationes enim;* tertio, ex *rerum sensibilium comparatione,* ibi: *Et difficile aestimamus* etc.

(Vers. 13.). Bene dixi: *Mitte illam* etc.; *quis enim hominum,* scilicet purus homo, *poterit scire,* scilicet sine sapientia tua, *consilium Dei?* quod est in praevidendo et disponendo fienda; Isaiae quadragesimo[^p169-9]: « Quis adiuvit spiritum Domini, aut quis
<!-- page 170 -->
consiliarius eius fuit »? *Aut quis poterit cogitare,* id est cogitando nosse, *quid velit Deus?* scilicet operando praevisa, quasi dicat: nullus; unde ad Romanos undecimo: « O altitudo divitiarum sapientiae et scientiae Dei » etc.

(Vers. 14.). *Cogitationes enim mortalium,* scilicet quamdiu sumus in statu mortalitatis, *timidae;* Glossa[^p170-1]: « Id est fragiles »; et hoc quantum ad ea quae sunt supra rationem. *Et incertae providentiae nostrae;* Glossa: « Quia anima mutabilis est, et caro corruptibilis », et hoc quantum ad ea quae sunt sub ratione et veri cogitatione.

(Vers. 15.). *Corpus enim, quod corrumpitur,* id est, quod continuae corruptioni subiacet, propter quod putredini comparatur; Iob vigesimo quinto[^p170-2]: « Homo putredo, et filius hominis vermis ». *Aggravat animam,* scilicet affectum eius a caelestibus retrahendo; ad Galatas quinto: « Caro concupiscit adversus spiritum ».

*Corpus, quod corrumpitur, aggravat animam.* Notandum, quod corpus animam peccato *originali maculat;* Iob decimo quarto[^p170-3]: « Quis potest facere mundum de immundo conceptum semine »? Item, necessitati peccati *venialis obligat;* ad Romanos septimo: « Quod odi malum, illud facio ». Item, ad peccatum *mortale inclinat;* Genesis octavo[^p170-4]: « Proni sensus, et cogitatio humani cordis in malum prona sunt ab adolescentia ». Item, *intellectum* a contemplatione veritatis *obnubilat;* supra secundo: « Umbrae transitus tempus nostrum », *umbrae,* scilicet interpositione corporis opaci inter animam et solem iustitiae. Item, *affectum* ab amore caelestium *retardat,* ut hic: « Corpus, quod corrumpitur, aggravat animam ». Item, *potentias motivas* a bono *impedit et ligat;* in Psalmo[^p170-5]: « Educ de custodia », vel de carcere, « animam meam »; item ad Romanos septimo: « Non, quod volo bonum, hoc ago ». Item, spiritui continuum *bellum suscitat;* ad Galatas quinto: « Caro concupiscit adversus spiritum ». Item, cura sui *spiritum sollicitat,* contra quod Matthaei sexto[^p170-6]: « Nolite solliciti esse » etc. Item, suis molestiis *animam perturbat;* Danielis decimo tertio: « Angustiae mihi sunt undique ». Item, sui mutabilitate eius *quietem turbat et variat;* Iob decimo quarto: « Et nunquam in eodem statu permanet ».

*Et terrena inhabitatio deprimit,* id est, deorsum premit a contemplatione aeternorum retrahendo, id est ad terrena inclinando; *sensum,* id est intellectum humanum; Genesis octavo[^p170-7]: « Sensus et cogitatio humani cordis prona sunt in malum ab adolescentia »; ideo ad Romanos septimo: « Infelix » etc. *Sensum,* inquam, *multa cogitantem,* id est temporalia, quae multa sunt, non aeterna, quae unum sunt, secundum illud Lucae decimo: « Turbaris erga plurima, porro unum est necessarium ».

(Vers. 16.). *Et difficile* etc., quasi dicat: Et hoc patet, quia *difficile aestimamus,* id est aestimative, non certitudinaliter cognoscimus; Ecclesiastae primo[^p170-8]: « Cunctae res difficiles »; *quae in terra sunt,* id est sensibilia inferiora. *Et quae in prospectu sunt,* « id est in aperto », sicut naturas rerum sensibilium superiorum; *invenimus cum labore;* Ecclesiastae octavo: « Est homo, qui diebus ac noctibus somnum non capit oculis; et intellexi, quod omnium operum Dei nullam possit homo invenire rationem ». Philosophus[^p170-9]: « Sicut se habet oculus noctuae ad lucem solis, sic intellectus noster ad manifestissima naturae ». *Quae autem in caelis sunt quis investigabit?* cum sint tam remota a nobis; Ecclesiastici tertio[^p170-10]: « Altiora te ne quaesieris »; Ecclesiastae quinto: « Deus in caelo, et tu super terram; idcirco pauci sint sermones tui ».

*Sensum autem tuum* etc. Hic ostendit, quod voluntas divina potest solum sciri *per sapientiam.* Hoc autem ostendit appropriando ei triplicem effectum, scilicet effectum *instruendi intelligentiam; corrigendi culpam: Et sic correctae; sanandi naturam,* ibi: *Nam per sapientiam* etc.

(Vers. 17.). *Sensum autem tuum,* id est consilium et voluntatem, *quis sciet?* quasi dicat: nullus. *Nisi tu dederis sapientiam,* illuminantem *intellectum;* Ecclesiastici primo[^p170-11]: « Secundum datum suum praebet illam diligentibus se »; Iacobi primo: « Dat omnibus affluenter »; Danielis secundo: « Dat sapientiam sapientibus et scientiam intelligentibus disciplinam ». *Et miseris Spiritum sanctum tuum,* inflammantem *affectum,* quasi dicat: nullus. Sicut enim nemo « scit quae sunt hominis, nisi spiritus hominis, qui est in ipso », ut habetur primae ad Corinthios secundo[^p170-12]; « sic nec ea quae Dei sunt, nisi Deus, et cui voluerit revelare ». *De altissimis,* id est de te, qui altissimus es, et de Filio, qui altissimus est, et vos dico *altissimi,* adiective, non substantive.

(Vers. 18.). *Et sic,* scilicet per donum sapientiae et Spiritus sancti missionem, *correctae sunt*[^p170-13]
<!-- page 171 -->
*semitae,* id est operationes, *eorum qui in terris sunt,* id est hominum, et hoc per revocationem *a malo;* Ioannis decimo sexto[^p171-1]: « Ille arguet mundum de peccato »; et bene dicit: *Semitae eorum qui in terris sunt,* id est peccantium in terra; semitae enim angelorum peccantium corrigi non possunt. *Et quae tibi placent didicerunt homines,* per informationem *in bono;* Ioannis decimo quarto: « Spiritus paracletus etc., docebit vos omnia ».

(Vers. 19.). *Nam per sapientiam* etc. Bene dixi: *Et sic correctae sunt* etc.; *nam per sapientiam sanati sunt,* scilicet a caecitate intellectus et corruptione affectus; Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto[^p171-2]: « Ego quasi fluvius Dorix », quod interpretatur *medicamentum generationis. Quicumque placuerunt tibi, Domine, a principio,* « scilicet mundi », secundum Glossam[^p171-3]; infra decimo sexto: « Etenim neque herba neque malagma sanavit eos » etc.; in Psalmo: « Misit verbum suum et sanavit eos »; Proverbiorum tertio: « Lignum vitae est his qui apprehenderint eam ». Hinc est, quod canes spirituales, id est sapientiae doctores et praedicatores, habent linguas medicinales; et dicit *omnes,* quia, secundum Augustinum[^p171-4], eadem fides Mediatoris salvavit antiquos, quae salvat et nos.

## English
<!-- page 166 -->

### Chapter IX.

#### *Thirdly he shows how devoutly he asked wisdom of God.*

*God of my fathers* etc. After he has shown how he received wisdom and how much he loved her once received; here he shows how devoutly he *asked* her of God. And first he touches the *form of the petition;* secondly, *the reason for its being heard: But*[^p166-9] *thou hast chosen;* thirdly, *the end of the petition: Send her;* fourthly, *the difficulty of the end: For who* etc.

#### First is touched the form of the petition.

In the first part he first *praises the comeliness* (*decorem*); secondly, *specifies the gift: Give me … of thy thrones;* thirdly, *humbles himself: For I am thy servant;* fourthly, *intimates his need: And*[^p166-10] *if one* etc.

(Vers. 1.). *God of my fathers,* namely by the grace of election; *of my fathers,* namely of the ancients; Exodus, the third[^p166-11]: « I am the God of thy father, the God of Abraham » etc. Or: *God of my fathers,* that is, who didst make my fathers and didst choose and sanctify them. *And Lord of mercy,* that is, merciful Lord, so that the construction is intransitive; or: *Lord of mercy,* the author, so that it is transitive; in the Psalm[^p166-12]: « The Lord is compassionate and merciful ». *Lord,* I say, *of mercy,* namely through the effect of the remission of sins and of justification; for mercy looks to misery; Proverbs, the fourteenth: « But sin maketh nations miserable ». *Who hast made*
<!-- page 167 -->
*all things,* namely through the work of creation, as is clear from Genesis, the first[^p167-1]; in the Psalm: « He spoke, and they were made »; *by thy word,* that is, by thy Son; John, the first: « In the beginning was the Word » etc., and after: « All things were made by him ».

(Vers. 2.). *And by thy wisdom,* that is, by the same thy Son, according to that of the Psalm[^p167-2]: « Thou hast made all things in wisdom »; *hast appointed* (*constituisti*), that is, hast composed out of very diverse parts, namely out of a rational spirit and earthly clay; *man;* Ecclesiasticus, the seventeenth: « God created man of the earth ». And it is to be noted that he says the other things were made by the *Word* alone, but man by *wisdom,* as a nobler work, because capable of wisdom both created and uncreated, and therefore as a work to be made with greater deliberation; on account of which the Lord, as if deliberating, says in Genesis, the first[^p167-3]: « Let us make man to our image » etc. *That he should have dominion over the creature that was made by thee,* over the creature, namely not the spiritual or angelic, but the bodily, and this by power; Genesis, the first: « Rule over the fishes of the sea » etc.

(Vers. 3.). *That he should order the world;* namely by wisdom; for man is placed in this world as a king in his kingdom. *In equity,* that is, mercy, *and justice;* for these two ought to be joined in ordering; Gregory[^p167-4]: « Discipline and mercy are much impaired, if one be held without the other ». *And in uprightness of heart,* namely through goodness, and this through a right intention towards God; in the Psalm[^p167-5]: « I will praise thee in uprightness of heart »; Job, the thirty-fourth: « If he turn his heart to God, he shall draw his spirit and breath unto himself »; *should judge judgement,* namely over his subjects. And by the name of *judgement* said without qualification he understands « true[^p167-6] » or just judgement. For just as a painted or dead man is not a man, so a false or unjust judgement is not a judgement.

(Vers. 4.). *Give me,* « from whom is every best gift », James, the first[^p167-7]; *wisdom,* namely understanding, *the assistant of thy thrones* (*sedium tuarum assistricem*), that is, the indweller of holy souls; above, the seventh: « Through nations she conveyeth herself into holy souls ». And holy souls are called *thrones of God,* because in them God sits and rests, presiding over and commanding all their motions and affections; Isaias, the last[^p167-8]: « Heaven is my throne »; the Gloss: « The soul of the just man is the seat of wisdom ». *And do not reject me* (*noli me reprobare*), by giving me goodness and grace in the affection; *do not,* I say, *reject me,* as those of whom it is said to the Romans, the first[^p167-9]: « He delivered them up to a reprobate sense »; Jeremias, the sixth: « Call them reprobate silver ». *From thy children* (*a pueris tuis*), namely by excluding me from the number and fellowship of thy children, of whom Isaias, the eighth: « Behold I and my children » etc.

(Vers. 5.). *For* etc., as if he were saying: therefore I ask that thou give me wisdom, *for I am thy servant,* that is, ready to serve thee; in the Psalm[^p167-10]: « I am thy servant: give me understanding »; III Kings, the tenth: « Happy are thy servants, who hear thy wisdom ». *And the son of thy handmaid,* that is, of the synagogue, of which it is said to the Galatians, the fourth: « It is written that Abraham had two sons ». According to the Gloss[^p167-11] these words are « of the Son to the Father », inasmuch as he is man, asking wisdom for his members, alleging himself his servant according to the humanity, not his son; Isaias, the forty-ninth: « Thou art my servant, Israel, for in thee will I glory ». *And the son of thy handmaid,* that is, of the Virgin Mary, according to that of Luke, the first[^p167-12]: « Behold the handmaid of the Lord ». *A weak man,* on the part of the body, on account of the frailty of the body; in the Psalm: « Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I am weak ». *And of short time,* on account of the brevity of life; Job, the fourteenth: « Man born of a woman, living for a short time »; *and less,* on the part of the soul, that is, insufficient by natural power, *as to the understanding;* the Gloss[^p167-13]: « For perceiving »; *of judgement,* that is, of thy judgements, by whose example one must judge; *and of laws,* thy eternal ones, namely, by whose regulation one must judge; to the Romans, the eleventh: « O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and of the knowledge of God! How incomprehensible are his judgements, and how unsearchable his ways »!

(Vers. 6.). *And*[^p167-14] *if one* etc., in this charity is intimated; *shall be consummate,* that is, perfect; the Gloss: « In judgement »; *among the sons of men,* who are born, namely,
<!-- page 168 -->
of man and woman, in distinction from the Son of man, that is, of the Virgin alone, of whom Matthew, the sixteenth: « Whom do men say that the Son of man is »? And he says: *consummate among the sons of men,* since he can be a mere man by nature. *If thy wisdom shall flee from him,* or *shall flee away*[^p168-1], namely as from one unworthy and a sinner, according to that above, the first: « Into a malicious soul wisdom shall not enter »; *thy wisdom;* the Gloss: « The divine », not the worldly. For the worldly does not flee on account of sin; Jeremias, the fourth: « They are wise to do evils ». *As nothing,* namely of conduct, not of nature[^p168-2], *he shall be counted,* namely before God and his Saints; below, the thirteenth: « All men are vain, in whom there is not the knowledge of God »; for it is by grace that a man is what he is as to the *being* of conduct; whence I Corinthians, the fifteenth: « By the grace of God I am what I am »; likewise, without charity man is nothing, as is clear from I Corinthians, the thirteenth; and according to Augustine the sinner is nothing, namely in that he is such.

#### Secondly, the twofold reason for his being heard.

*But*[^p168-3] *thou hast chosen* etc. Here he adds a manifold *reason for his being heard,* of which the first is the *office of the kingdom* enjoined; the second is the *command to build the temple: And thou hast said* etc.

(Vers. 7.). *But thou* etc., as if he were saying thus: I am of myself insufficient without wisdom. *But* (*autem*) stands for *sed;* nevertheless *thou hast chosen me;* the Gloss[^p168-4]: « Solomon, or Christ ». *King to thy people,* that is, to the people believing in thee; in the Psalm: « But I am appointed king by him over Sion » etc.; *and judge of thy sons and daughters,* thine, namely, that is, of the faithful, according to that of John, the first: « He gave them power to be made the sons of God, to them that believe in his name ». It is the office of the *king* to exercise his judgement in the other affairs of the kingdom as well; but that of the *judge* is specially in the causes of the people.

(Vers. 8.). *And thou hast said;* the Gloss[^p168-5]: « Thou hast commanded »; for David, who was a man of blood, was not permitted to build the temple; but Solomon the peaceable was chosen for this, as is clear from II Kings, the seventh, and I Paralipomenon, the twenty-ninth. *And thou hast said,* I say, *that I should build a temple on thy holy mount,* that is, on mount Moria, where thou didst will that holy things be done; *and in the city of thy dwelling place,* namely Jerusalem, where he was said to dwell, because there he was worshipped more excellently[^p168-6]; *an altar,* namely of holocausts; *of holocausts,* I say, in distinction from the brazen altar, which was in the court before the temple, under the open sky. *A resemblance of thy holy tabernacle,* that is, after its pattern, of which Exodus, the twenty-fifth[^p168-7], which was made after the pattern of the heavenly tabernacle; whence in the same place: « Look, and make it according to the pattern that was shown thee in the mount ». *Which thou hast prepared;* the Gloss[^p168-8]: « Through Moses »; *from the beginning,* not of creation, but of the giving of the Law; whence the Gloss: « When thou didst lead the children of Israel out of Egypt »; the *tabernacle* in the desert, as the Gloss says, signified the Church militant, but the *temple* in Jerusalem the Church triumphant. The reason of this is that the tabernacle in the desert was movable, but the temple in Jerusalem was immovable.

(Vers. 9.). *And with thee,* namely prepared, *thy wisdom,* namely the uncreated, « that is, thy Son[^p168-9] », of whom I Corinthians, the first: « Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God ». *Which knoweth thy works,* as the co-worker of all things, according to that of the Psalm: « Thou hast made all things in wisdom ». *Which was present,* present with thee, *then,* namely *when thou madest the world;* Proverbs, the eighth: « When he balanced the foundations of the earth, I was with him forming all things »; and in the same: « I was delighted with him every day, playing in the world ». *And knew that this*[^p168-10] *was agreeable to thy eyes,* namely as to counsels; *to thy eyes,* namely of thy understanding and affection, that is, of thy mercy and truth, « for in every work of God there is mercy and truth[^p168-11] »; Ecclesiasticus, the forty-second: « He found grace before the eyes of the Lord »,
<!-- page 169 -->
according to another translation. *And what is right* (*Et quod directum*); the Gloss: « That is, right and just »; *in thy precepts;* in the Psalm: « The justices of the Lord are right, rejoicing hearts ».

#### Thirdly, the end of the petition is touched in two ways.

*Send her* etc. Here he shows the *end of the petition,* that is, for what he sought wisdom; and first *he asks for wisdom* for knowing and doing the will of God. Secondly *he shows* that she *avails* for the attainment of such an end: *For she knoweth.*

(Vers. 10.). He says therefore: *Send her,* namely wisdom etc. It is to be noted that the mission of Wisdom is twofold, namely one visible, into the flesh, of which John, the tenth[^p169-1]: « Whom the Father hath sanctified and sent into the world »; the other into the mind, which he asks for here. *Out of thy holy heavens* (*De caelis sanctis tuis*), that is, out of thyself, who art heaven by containing all things; in the Psalm: « His going out is from the end of heaven »; likewise James, the third: « The wisdom that is from above, first indeed is chaste » etc. And he says *out of the heavens* in the plural on account of the multitude of the things contained and of the effects in the things contained, just as *the days of eternity* are spoken of in the plural; Micheas, the fifth[^p169-2]: « And his going forth is as from the beginning of the days of eternity ». *And from the throne of thy greatness;* the Gloss: « From thyself, who, seated and at rest, governest all things »; Isaias, the eighteenth: « I will take my rest, and consider in my place, as the noonday light is clear ».

And it is to be noted that uncreated Wisdom is sometimes said *to be in the bosom of the Father,* as John, the first[^p169-3]: « The only begotten, who is in the bosom of the Father, hath declared to us »; sometimes *to be begotten from the womb,* in the Psalm: « From the womb before the day star I begot thee »; sometimes *to proceed from the mouth of the Father,* Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth: « I came out of the mouth of the most High, the firstborn before all creatures »; sometimes *to be sent from heaven,* as here; sometimes *to sit in holy souls,* whence above, in the same chapter: « Give me wisdom, the assistant of thy thrones » etc. The first on account of *hiddenness,* the second on account of *consubstantiality,* the third on account of *manifestation,* the fourth on account of *illumination,* the fifth on account of *tranquillity.*

*That she may be with me,* namely within, in the essence of the soul, and *by indwelling* and informing it through grace; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth[^p169-4]: « Let thy dwelling be in Jacob » etc. *And may labour with me,* by *bringing* the moving power of the soul *to perfection* in the work; Ecclesiasticus, the last: « I remembered thy mercy, O Lord, and thy work, which are from the beginning of the world ». *That I may know,* that is, that thou mayest make me know, *by enlightening* the cognitive power of my soul, *what is acceptable before thee,* or *with thee*[^p169-5]; in the Psalm: « Teach me to do thy will ».

(Vers. 11.). *For she knoweth all those things,* all things, namely, pertaining to the knowledge of reason, such as are the inferior things; *and understandeth,* all things, namely, pertaining to the intelligence of the understanding, as are the superior things, above, the seventh[^p169-6]: « Beholding all things ». *And she shall lead me soberly in my works;* the Gloss[^p169-7]: « Lest I stumble », by directing me in good work; above, the eighth: « She teacheth sobriety » etc. *And she shall preserve me by her power;* the Gloss: « From adversaries », by protecting me; in the Psalm: « Unless the Lord keep the city » etc.; likewise: « Behold he shall neither slumber nor sleep » etc.

(Vers. 12.). *And my works shall be acceptable;* the Gloss[^p169-8]: « Before God », as done purely out of love of him. *And I shall govern* (*disponam*) *thy people justly,* namely by ruling subjects rightly; whence above, the eighth: « I shall set the people in order, and nations ». *And I shall be worthy of the thrones of my father,* by imitating him; he says *of the thrones* in the plural, because the throne of the present kingdom, according to that of Proverbs, the eighth: « By me kings reign »; and of the future; the Gloss: « By the reward of the heavenly kingdom ».

#### Fourthly, the difficulty of this end is shown in two ways.

*For who among men* etc. Here he shows the *difficulty of the end,* first showing that the will of God *cannot be known without wisdom;* secondly, that it *can be known through her* alone: *But thy mind* (*Sensum autem tuum*). — That it *cannot be known without her,* he shows first from the *depth of the divine mind;* secondly, from the *imperfection of the human mind: For the thoughts;* thirdly, from *comparison with sensible things,* at: *And hardly do we guess* etc.

(Vers. 13.). Well did I say: *Send her* etc.; *for who among men,* namely a mere man, *can know,* namely without thy wisdom, *the counsel of God?* which consists in foreseeing and disposing the things to be done; Isaias, the fortieth[^p169-9]: « Who hath helped the spirit of the Lord, or who
<!-- page 170 -->
hath been his counsellor »? *Or who can think,* that is, know by thinking, *what God willeth?* namely the things foreseen in working, as if he were saying: no one; whence to the Romans, the eleventh: « O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and of the knowledge of God » etc.

(Vers. 14.). *For the thoughts of mortal men,* namely as long as we are in the state of mortality, *are fearful;* the Gloss[^p170-1]: « That is, frail »; and this as to the things that are above reason. *And our foresights* (*providentiae*) *uncertain;* the Gloss: « Because the soul is mutable, and the flesh corruptible », and this as to the things that are under reason and the thinking of the true.

(Vers. 15.). *For the body which is corrupted,* that is, which is subject to continual corruption, on account of which it is compared to rottenness; Job, the twenty-fifth[^p170-2]: « Man that is rottenness, and the son of man a worm ». *Weigheth down the soul,* namely by drawing back its affection from heavenly things; to the Galatians, the fifth: « The flesh lusteth against the spirit ».

*The body which is corrupted weigheth down the soul.* It is to be noted that the body *stains* the soul with *original* sin; Job, the fourteenth[^p170-3]: « Who can make him clean that is conceived of unclean seed »? Likewise, it *binds* it to the necessity of *venial* sin; to the Romans, the seventh: « The evil which I hate, that I do ». Likewise, it *inclines* it to *mortal* sin; Genesis, the eighth[^p170-4]: « The imagination and thought of man's heart are prone to evil from his youth ». Likewise, it *clouds over* the *understanding* from the contemplation of truth; above, the second: « Our time is as the passing of a shadow », *of a shadow,* namely by the interposition of the opaque body between the soul and the sun of justice. Likewise, it *slows* the *affection* from the love of heavenly things, as here: « The body which is corrupted weigheth down the soul ». Likewise, it *hinders and binds* the *moving powers* from the good; in the Psalm[^p170-5]: « Bring out of prison », or out of the gaol, « my soul »; likewise to the Romans, the seventh: « The good which I will, I do not ». Likewise, it *stirs up* continual *war* against the spirit; to the Galatians, the fifth: « The flesh lusteth against the spirit ». Likewise, by care of itself it *makes the spirit anxious,* against which Matthew, the sixth[^p170-6]: « Be not solicitous » etc. Likewise, by its troubles it *disturbs the soul;* Daniel, the thirteenth: « I am straitened on every side ». Likewise, by its own mutability it *disturbs and varies* the soul's *rest;* Job, the fourteenth: « And never continueth in the same state ».

*And the earthly habitation presseth down,* that is, presses downward by drawing back from the contemplation of eternal things, that is, by inclining to earthly things; *the mind* (*sensum*), that is, the human understanding; Genesis, the eighth[^p170-7]: « The imagination and thought of man's heart are prone to evil from his youth »; therefore to the Romans, the seventh: « Unhappy » etc. *The mind,* I say, *that museth upon many things,* that is, temporal things, which are many, not eternal things, which are one, according to that of Luke, the tenth: « Thou art troubled about many things: but one thing is necessary ».

(Vers. 16.). *And hardly* etc., as if he were saying: And this is clear, because *hardly do we guess,* that is, we know by guessing, not with certainty; Ecclesiastes, the first[^p170-8]: « All things are hard »; *the things that are upon earth,* that is, the lower sensible things. *And the things that are within our view* (*in prospectu*), « that is, in the open », such as the natures of the higher sensible things; *with labour do we find;* Ecclesiastes, the eighth: « There is a man that day and night taketh no sleep with his eyes; and I understood that man can find no reason of all those works of God ». The Philosopher[^p170-9]: « As the eye of the owl is to the light of the sun, so is our understanding to the most manifest things of nature ». *But the things that are in heaven, who shall search out?* since they are so remote from us; Ecclesiasticus, the third[^p170-10]: « Seek not the things that are too high for thee »; Ecclesiastes, the fifth: « God is in heaven, and thou upon earth: therefore let thy words be few ».

*But thy mind* etc. Here he shows that the divine will can be known *through wisdom* alone. And he shows this by appropriating to her a threefold effect, namely the effect *of instructing the intelligence; of correcting fault: And so … corrected; of healing nature,* at: *For by wisdom* etc.

(Vers. 17.). *But thy mind* (*Sensum autem tuum*), that is, thy counsel and will, *who shall know?* as if he were saying: no one. *Unless thou give wisdom,* enlightening the *understanding;* Ecclesiasticus, the first[^p170-11]: « According to his gift he giveth her to them that love him »; James, the first: « Who giveth to all abundantly »; Daniel, the second: « He giveth wisdom to the wise, and knowledge and discipline to them that understand ». *And send thy Holy Spirit,* inflaming the *affection,* as if he were saying: no one. For just as no one « knoweth the things of a man, but the spirit of a man that is in him », as is had in I Corinthians, the second[^p170-12]; « so neither the things that are of God, but God, and he to whom he will reveal them ». *From the most high* (*De altissimis*), that is, from thee, who art most high, and from the Son, who is most high, and I call you *most high* (*altissimi*) adjectivally, not substantively.

(Vers. 18.). *And so,* namely through the gift of wisdom and the mission of the Holy Spirit, *are corrected*[^p170-13]
<!-- page 171 -->
*the paths* (*semitae*), that is, the operations, *of them that are upon earth,* that is, of men, and this by calling them back *from evil;* John, the sixteenth[^p171-1]: « He will convince the world of sin »; and well does he say: *The paths of them that are upon earth,* that is, of those sinning on earth; for the paths of the angels who sinned cannot be corrected. *And men have learned the things that please thee,* through being formed *in good;* John, the fourteenth: « The Paraclete, the Holy Ghost etc., will teach you all things ».

(Vers. 19.). *For by wisdom* etc. Well did I say: *And so … are corrected* etc.; *for by wisdom they were healed,* namely from blindness of the understanding and corruption of the affection; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth[^p171-2]: « I, like a river Dorix », which is interpreted *medicine of generation. Whosoever have pleased thee, O Lord, from the beginning,* « namely of the world », according to the Gloss[^p171-3]; below, the sixteenth: « For it was neither herb nor mollifying plaster that healed them » etc.; in the Psalm: « He sent his word, and healed them »; Proverbs, the third: « She is a tree of life to them that lay hold on her ». Hence it is that the spiritual dogs, that is, the teachers and preachers of wisdom, have medicinal tongues; and he says *all,* because, according to Augustine[^p171-4], the same faith in the Mediator saved the ancients which saves us also.
