# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. X, Vers. 1-10
## *Pars prima demonstrat bonum pastorem in parabola*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 382 -->

### Capitulum X.

#### Tertio Filius Dei se ostendit lucem et directorem exemplo bonae vitae.

1. *Amen, amen dico vobis* etc. Manifestavit Dominus supra[^p382-10] se directorem verbo sapientiae et miraculo potentiae; hic manifestat *exemplo bonae vitae.* Primis enim duobus modis dirigebat ut *doctor,* sed hic tertio dirigit ut *pastor.* In hoc igitur capitulo intendit Dominus se ostendere *verum et bonum pastorem;* et dividitur in duas partes: quia primo Dominus bonum pastorem *in proverbio et parabola demonstrat;* secundo vero *proverbium exponit et sibi adaptat,* ut per hoc se verum pastorem ostendat, ibi[^p382-11]: *Dixit ergo eis iterum Iesus.*

#### Pars prima demonstrat bonum pastorem in parabola.

Describit igitur bonum pastorem in proverbio hoc ordine: nam primo determinat boni et veri[^p382-12] pastoris *ingressum;* secundo, *indicium;* tertio, boni pastoris *officium;* quarto dicit, hoc proverbium *esse Iudaeis occultum.*

2. (Vers. 1.). Primo igitur determinat boni et veri pastoris *ingressum* per comparationem ad suum
<!-- page 383 -->
oppositum, quia « opposita iuxta se posita magis elucescunt[^p383-1] »; et ingressus pastoris est *per ostium,* sed furis per alium locum. Propterea dicit: *Amen, amen dico vobis* — sermonem continuat Dominus Pharisaeis loquens — *Qui non intrat per ostium in ovile ovium,* id est in Ecclesiam Dei, in qua grex dominicus continetur; *sed ascendit aliunde,* ut superbus et ambitiosus; *ille fur est et latro: fur,* quia alienum suum dicit; *latro,* quia bona alterius destruit et occidit. De hoc ascensu mali pastoris dicit Hieronymus[^p383-2]: « Laetamur ad ascensum, timeamus descensum; non est tanti gaudii excelsa tenuisse, quanti moeroris est de excelsis corruisse ». Sic ascendit ille princeps latronum et ambitiosorum, de quo dicitur Isaiae decimo quarto[^p383-3]: « Ascendam in caelum, super astra caeli exaltabo solium meum ». Qui sic inordinate ingreditur fur est et non pastor.

3. (Vers. 2.). *Qui autem intrat per ostium pastor est ovium;* ille intrat per ostium, qui intrat per veritatem. De hoc introitu primae ad Thessalonicenses secundo[^p383-4]: « Ipsi scitis, fratres, introitum nostrum ad vos »; et subditur ibidem: « Neque enim aliquando fuimus in sermone adulationis, sicut scitis, neque in occasione avaritiae neque quaerentes ab hominibus gloriam[^p383-5].

4. (Vers. 3.). *Huic ostiarius aperit.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet veri pastoris *indicium* in hoc, quod recognoscitur ab ostiario et grege. Propterea dicit: *Huic ostiarius aperit,* sciens, eum esse pastorem. Ostiarius iste est Christus, qui habet clavem; unde Isaiae vigesimo secundo[^p383-6]: « Dabo clavem domus David super humerum eius; et aperiet, et non erit qui claudat; et claudet, et non erit qui aperiat ». *Et oves vocem eius audiunt,* quia bono pastori libenter obediunt; ad Hebraeos ultimo[^p383-7]: « Obedite praepositis vestris et subiacete eis; ipsi enim pervigilant, quasi rationem pro animabus vestris reddituri ».

*Et proprias oves vocat nominatim.* Tangitur hic boni pastoris *officium,* quod est triplex: *vocare,*
<!-- page 384 -->
*eduoere* et *dirigere;* nominatim *vocat* cognoscendo; *educit* ad pascua instruendo; sed *ante eas vadit* bonum exemplum praebendo. Hoc convenit pastori Christo per *excellentiam,* aliis per *imitationem.* Unde primo dicit: *Et proprias oves vocat nominatim,* scilicet Christus; secundae ad Timotheum secundo[^p384-1]: « Novit Dominus, qui sunt eius », et de *eius imitatione;* Proverbiorum duodecimo: « Novit iustus animas iumentorum suorum ». *Et educit eas,* ad pascua, scilicet Christus; Ezechielis trigesimo quarto[^p384-2]: « Educam eos de populis et congregabo eos de terris et inducam in terram suam », quae scilicet erat lacte manans. Sic et *imitator Christi,* ut Moyses et Aaron; Psalmus: « Deduxisti sicut oves populum tuum in manu Moysi et Aaron ».

5. (Vers. 4.). *Et cum proprias oves emiserit, ante eas vadit,* bonum exemplum ostendendo praeit, ut Christus; Michaeae secundo[^p384-3]: « Ascendit pandens iter ante eos ». Unde dixit infra decimo tertio: « Exemplum dedi vobis, ut quemadmodum ego feci vobis, ita et vos faciatis ». Sic et *imitator Christi;* unde primae ad Corinthios undecimo[^p384-4]: « Imitatores mei estote, sicut et ego Christi ». Sed pauci sunt tales; unde Isaiae vigesimo quarto: « Erit sicut populus, sic sacerdos ». — Hoc triplex boni pastoris officium magnum habet effectum in ovibus, qui scilicet est ovium directio per *imitationem;* propter quod dicit: *Oves*[^p384-5] *illum sequuntur,* scilicet verum pastorem. *Oves* sunt simplices et humiles, de quibus dicit Hugo[^p384-6]: « Humilitas ovis est, ut praeesse non appetas et subesse diligas. Multi fugientes laborem volunt praeesse, subesse dedignantur; isti non sunt *oves* », quia non sequuntur. Ideo oves sequuntur, *quia sciunt vocem eius,* esse scilicet vocem consolationis, secundum illud Matthaei undecimo[^p384-7]: « Venite ad me omnes, qui laboratis et onerati estis, et ego reficiam vos ». Bonus pastor ad reficiendum vocat.

6. (Vers. 5.). *Alienum autem non sequuntur, sed fugiunt ab eo,* hoc est malum pastorem vel lupum[^p384-8], *quia non noverunt vocem alienorum,* id est, non approbant. Isti alieni sunt pseudochristi et pseudoprophetae et pseudoapostoli, de quibus dicitur secundae ad Corinthios undecimo[^p384-9]; quod « sunt operarii subdoli, transfigurantes se in apostolos Christi ». Hos non sequuntur; moniti enim sunt a suo pastore; Matthaei septimo: « Attendite a falsis prophetis, qui veniunt ad vos in vestimentis ovium, intrinsecus autem sunt lupi rapaces ».

7. (Vers. 6.). *Hoc proverbium dixit illis*[^p384-10] *Iesus.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet quod *proverbium fuit illis occultum;* unde dicit: *Hoc proverbium dixit illis Iesus. Illi autem non cognoverunt, quid loqueretur eis;* unde Matthaei decimo tertio[^p384-11]: « Ideo in parabolis loquor eis, ut videntes non videant, et audientes non audiant neque intelligant ».

8. Notandum, quod « *proverbium* secundum Chrysostomum[^p384-12] est sermo utilis, aliquid utilitatis continens in aperto, plurimum sensum retinens in occulto ». Secundum Basilium[^p384-13] « *proverbium* est moralis eruditio, emendatio vitiorum, vitae probabilis regula, humanos actus linea dirigens altiore ». Secundum communem dicendi modum *proverbium* est locutio generalis et brevis, aliud in sententia et aliud in verborum superficie continens[^p384-14]. — Notandum etiam ad intelligentiam praedictorum, quod veri
<!-- page 385 -->
pastoris privatur officio qui per *ostium non ingreditur,* et hoc multipliciter.

#### Pars secunda exponit parabolam et applicat ad Christum.

9. *Dixit ergo eis iterum Iesus.* Haec est secunda pars capituli, in qua Dominus *proverbium* propositum *sibi adaptando explanat,* ostendens se verum pastorem quantum ad illa tria, quae praedicta sunt in proverbio: primo, *quoad veri pastoris ingressum;* secundo, *quoad veri pastoris affectum,* ibi[^p385-1]: *Ego sum pastor bonus;* tertio, *quoad pastoris indicium,* ibi: *Facta sunt encaenia* etc.

Primo ergo ostendit se verum pastorem *quoad ingressum* hoc ordine: primo, quod *nullus nisi per ipsum recte ingreditur;* secundo, quod *per ipsum quicumque ingreditur, recte*[^p385-2] *ingreditur;* tertio, ipse non tantum est *via ingrediendi,* sed etiam *recte ingreditur.*

(Vers. 7.). Ostendit ergo primo, quod *nullus nisi per ipsum recte in ovile ingreditur;* propter quod dicit: *Amen, amen dico vobis: Ego*[^p385-3] *sum ostium ovium; ego* discretive, et nullus alius, quia non est ingressus nisi per me; unde dicit:

10. (Vers. 8.). *Omnes, quotquot venerunt, fures sunt et latrones,* quia scilicet per me non intraverunt; et huius signum subdit: *Sed*[^p385-4] *non audierunt eos oves.* Istud ostium clausum fuit diu, sed in passione est reseratum, ut « plenitudo gentium intraret ». De hoc ostio Apocalypsis quarto[^p385-5]: « Post haec vidi; et ecce, ostium apertum in caelo »; vere apertum, quia, sicut dicitur supra sexto, « qui venit ad me, non eiiciam eum foras ».

11. (Vers. 9.). *Ego sum ostium.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet quod *quicumque per ipsum ingreditur, salubriter ingreditur.* Propterea dicit: *Ego sum ostium,* per quod scilicet salubriter intratur; et ratio subditur: *Per me si quis introierit, salvabitur;* de quo ingressu Matthaei septimo[^p385-6]: « Intrate per angustam portam. Quam angusta est porta, et arcta via est, quae ducit ad vitam »! quia Christus fuit pauper et modicus. Per hoc ostium parvulum non intrant divites pleni divitiis; propter quod dicitur Matthaei decimo nono[^p385-7]: « Facilius est, camelum transire per foramen acus, quam divitem intrare in regnum caelorum ». Ingressus iste est per *fidem* et *baptismatis Sacramentum;* quoniam haec est ianua virtutum, et illud Sacramentorum. Qui hoc modo ingreditur salvabitur; Marci ultimo[^p385-8]: « Qui crediderit et baptizatus fuerit salvus erit ». *Et ingredietur et egredietur et pascua inveniet: ingredietur,* per contemplationem, quae revocat ad interiora; *et egredietur,* per actionem; Numerorum vigesimo septimo[^p385-9]: « Provideat Dominus Deus spiritum universae carnis, hominem, qui possit intrare et exire ante eos ». Vel, sicut exponit Augustinus[^p385-10], *ingredietur* ad contemplationem Divinitatis, *egredietur* ad aspectum humanitatis, *et pascua inveniet,* quia in omnibus reficitur, intellectus in contemplatione Divinitatis, et sensus in contemplatione humanitatis; de quibus pascuis Ezechielis trigesimo quarto[^p385-11]: « Pascam eos in montibus Israel, in pascuis uberrimis pascam eos ».

12. (Vers. 10.). *Fur non venit, nisi ut furetur.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet quod ipse Christus *ut verus pastor ingreditur,* non ut fur; propter quod dicit: *Fur non venit, nisi ut furetur,* bona temporalia extorquendo; *et mactet,* subditos temporaliter affligendo; *et perdat,* per malum exemplum in infernum praecipitando. *Ego veni, ut vitam habeant et abundantius habeant,* id est ad conservandam vitam ut pastor; *ut habeant vitam,* scilicet gratiae, de qua vita supra sexto[^p385-12]: « Hic est panis de caelo descendens, qui dat vitam mundo »; *et abundantius habeant,* scilicet vitam gloriae, de qua infra decimo septimo[^p385-13]: « Haec est vita aeterna, ut cognoscant te solum verum Deum et quem misisti Iesum Christum ». Haec dicitur *abundans,* quia dici-
<!-- page 386 -->
tur Lucae sexto: « Mensuram bonam et confertam et coagitatam et supereffluentem dabunt in sinum vestrum ».

### Quaestiones.

13. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc, quod Dominus se comparat hic *ostio,* quia supra[^p386-1] se comparavit *ostiario;* quomodo idem est *ostium* et *ostiarius* et *pastor?*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod, sicut dicitur infra decimo quarto[^p386-2], Christus est *via, veritas et vita:* quia *via* ad Patrem, ideo *ostium;* quia vero *veritas,* quae docet viam, ideo *ostiarius;* quia *vita,* ideo *pastor,* qui pascit et conservat vitam[^p386-3].

14. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Quotquot venerunt, fures sunt.*

**Contra:** Venerunt Prophetae et Patriarchae et Ioannes Baptista: ergo secundum hoc omnes mali, ut dicunt haeretici[^p386-4].

Respondetur ad hoc, ut fiat vis in hoc quod dicitur *venerunt,* scilicet propria auctoritate, non divina, sicut pseudoprophetae, de quibus Ieremiae vigesimo tertio[^p386-5]: *Non mittebam cos, et ipsi currebant;* boni autem non *venerunt,* sed *missi sunt.* Unde Augustinus: « Non praeter illum venerunt, sed cum illo venerunt ». Ipse enim est veritas; et ideo cum illo venerunt, quicumque veritatem praedicaverunt.

15. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *egredietur et pascua inveniet.*

**Contra:** *Nullus mittens manum ad aratrum debet retro aspicere*[^p386-6]: ergo nullus ingrediens egredietur.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod duplex est egressus: unus *contrarius ingressui,* et iste est egressus de Ecclesia per infidelitatem; et de hoc obiicit, et de hoc dicit Augustinus[^p386-7]: « Ingredi in Ecclesiam bonum est, sed egredi pessimum »; et de hoc primae Ioannis secundo: *Ex nobis exierunt, sed ex nobis non erant.* Alius est *a contemplatione ad actionem;* et iste non est retrocessionis, sed exercitationis. De hoc in Psalmo: « Exibit homo ad opus suum et ad operationem suam usque ad vesperam ».

## English

### Chapter X.

#### Third, the Son of God shows himself light and director by the example of a good life.

1. *Amen, amen I say to you* etc. The Lord made himself manifest above[^p382-10] as director by the word of wisdom and by the miracle of power; here he makes himself manifest *by the example of a good life.* For in the first two modes he directed as *doctor,* but here, in the third, he directs as *shepherd.* In this chapter, then, the Lord intends to show himself *the true and good shepherd;* and it is divided into two parts: for first the Lord *shows the good shepherd in a proverb and parable;* second, *he expounds the proverb and adapts it to himself,* so that by this he may show himself the true shepherd, there[^p382-11]: *Jesus therefore said to them again.*

#### The first part shows the good shepherd in a parable.

He describes, then, the good shepherd in the proverb in this order: for first he determines the *entrance* of the good and true[^p382-12] shepherd; second, his *mark;* third, the good shepherd's *office;* fourth he says that this proverb *was hidden from the Jews.*

2. (V. 1.). First, then, he determines the *entrance* of the good and true shepherd by comparison with its
<!-- page 383 -->
opposite, since « opposites set beside one another shine out the more[^p383-1] »; and the entrance of the shepherd is *by the door,* but that of the thief by another place. Therefore he says: *Amen, amen I say to you* — the Lord continues his discourse, speaking to the Pharisees — *He that entereth not by the door into the sheepfold,* that is, into the Church of God, in which the Lord's flock is contained; *but climbeth up another way,* as the proud and ambitious man does; *the same is a thief and a robber: a thief,* because he calls another's his own; *a robber,* because he destroys and kills the goods of another. Of this climbing up of the evil shepherd Jerome says[^p383-2]: « We rejoice at the ascent, let us fear the descent; there is not so much joy in having held the heights as there is grief in having fallen from the heights ». So climbed up that prince of robbers and of the ambitious, of whom it is said in Isaias, the fourteenth chapter[^p383-3]: « I will ascend into heaven, above the stars of heaven I will exalt my throne ». He who enters in thus inordinately is a thief and not a shepherd.

3. (V. 2.). *But he that entereth in by the door is the shepherd of the sheep;* he enters by the door who enters by the truth. Of this entrance First Thessalonians, the second chapter[^p383-4]: « You yourselves know, brethren, our entrance in unto you »; and it is added in the same place: « For neither were we at any time in the speech of flattery, as you know, nor in an occasion of covetousness, nor seeking glory of men[^p383-5].

4. (V. 3.). *To him the porter openeth.* Here the second is touched on, namely the *mark* of the true shepherd, in this, that he is recognised by the porter and by the flock. Therefore he says: *To him the porter openeth,* knowing that he is the shepherd. This porter is Christ, who has the key; whence Isaias, the twenty-second chapter[^p383-6]: « I will give the key of the house of David upon his shoulder; and he shall open, and none shall shut; and he shall shut, and none shall open ». *And the sheep hear his voice,* because they willingly obey a good shepherd; to the Hebrews, the last chapter[^p383-7]: « Obey your prelates, and be subject to them; for they watch, as being to render an account for your souls ».

*And he calleth his own sheep by name.* Here is touched on the good shepherd's *office,* which is threefold: *to call,*
<!-- page 384 -->
*to eduoere* [so printed: *eduoere* for *educere*] and *to direct;* he *calls* by name by knowing; he *leads out* to pasture by instructing; but he *goeth before them* by affording a good example. This befits Christ the shepherd by *excellence,* the others by *imitation.* Whence first he says: *And he calleth his own sheep by name,* namely Christ; Second Timothy, the second chapter[^p384-1]: « The Lord knoweth who are his », and of *the imitation of him;* Proverbs, the twelfth chapter: « The just knoweth the lives of his beasts ». *And leadeth them out,* to pasture, namely Christ; Ezechiel, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p384-2]: « I will bring them out from the peoples, and will gather them out of the countries, and will bring them into their own land », which, namely, was flowing with milk. So also the *imitator of Christ,* as Moses and Aaron; the Psalm: « Thou hast conducted thy people like sheep by the hand of Moses and Aaron ».

5. (V. 4.). *And when he hath let out his own sheep, he goeth before them,* he goes ahead by showing a good example, as Christ does; Micheas, the second chapter[^p384-3]: « He went up, opening the way before them ». Whence he said below, in the thirteenth chapter: « I have given you an example, that as I have done to you, so you do also ». So also the *imitator of Christ;* whence First Corinthians, the eleventh chapter[^p384-4]: « Be ye imitators of me, as I also am of Christ ». But few are such; whence Isaias, the twenty-fourth chapter: « And it shall be as with the people, so with the priest ». — This threefold office of the good shepherd has a great effect in the sheep, which, namely, is the direction of the sheep by *imitation;* on account of which he says: *The sheep*[^p384-5] *follow him,* namely the true shepherd. *Sheep* are the simple and humble, of whom Hugh says[^p384-6]: « The humility of the sheep is this, that you do not desire to be over others and love to be under. Many, fleeing labour, wish to be over others, and disdain to be under; these are not *sheep* », because they do not follow. Therefore the sheep follow, *because they know his voice,* namely that it is the voice of consolation, according to that of Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p384-7]: « Come to me, all you that labour, and are burdened, and I will refresh you ». The good shepherd calls in order to refresh.

6. (V. 5.). *But a stranger they follow not, but fly from him,* that is, the evil shepherd or the wolf[^p384-8], *because they know not the voice of strangers,* that is, they do not approve it. These strangers are the false christs and false prophets and false apostles, of whom it is said in Second Corinthians, the eleventh chapter[^p384-9]; that « they are deceitful workmen, transforming themselves into the apostles of Christ ». These they do not follow; for they have been warned by their shepherd; Matthew, the seventh chapter: « Beware of false prophets, who come to you in the clothing of sheep, but inwardly they are ravening wolves ».

7. (V. 6.). *This proverb Jesus spoke to them*[^p384-10]. Here the fourth is touched on, namely that *the proverb was hidden from them;* whence he says: *This proverb Jesus spoke to them. But they understood not what he spoke to them;* whence Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p384-11]: « Therefore do I speak to them in parables: that seeing they may not see, and hearing they may not hear, neither understand ».

8. It is to be noted that « a *proverb,* according to Chrysostom[^p384-12], is a useful saying, containing something of usefulness in the open, retaining a very great sense in the hidden ». According to Basil[^p384-13], « a *proverb* is a moral erudition, an amendment of vices, a rule of approved life, directing human acts by a higher line ». According to the common manner of speaking, a *proverb* is a general and brief locution, containing one thing in its sense and another on the surface of its words[^p384-14]. — It is to be noted also, for the understanding of the foregoing, that he is deprived of the office of the true
<!-- page 385 -->
shepherd who *entereth not by the door,* and this in many ways.

#### The second part expounds the parable and applies it to Christ.

9. *Jesus therefore said to them again.* This is the second part of the chapter, in which the Lord *explains* the proverb put forward *by adapting it to himself,* showing himself the true shepherd as regards those three things which were said beforehand in the proverb: first, *as regards the entrance of the true shepherd;* second, *as regards the affection of the true shepherd,* there[^p385-1]: *I am the good shepherd;* third, *as regards the shepherd's mark,* there: *And it was the feast of the dedication* etc.

First, then, he shows himself the true shepherd *as regards the entrance* in this order: first, that *no one enters rightly except through him;* second, that *whoever enters through him enters rightly*[^p385-2]; third, that he himself is not only *the way of entering,* but also *enters rightly.*

(V. 7.). He shows, then, first, that *no one enters rightly into the sheepfold except through him;* on account of which he says: *Amen, amen I say to you: I*[^p385-3] *am the door of the sheep; I* discretively, and no one else, because there is no entrance except through me; whence he says:

10. (V. 8.). *All, as many as have come, are thieves and robbers,* namely because they did not enter through me; and he adds the sign of this: *But*[^p385-4] *the sheep heard them not.* This door was long shut, but in the passion it was unbarred, so that « the fulness of the Gentiles should come in ». Of this door, the Apocalypse, the fourth chapter[^p385-5]: « After these things I saw; and behold, a door opened in heaven »; truly opened, because, as is said above, in the sixth chapter, « him that cometh to me, I will not cast out ».

11. (V. 9.). *I am the door.* Here the second is touched on, namely that *whoever enters through him enters healthfully.* Therefore he says: *I am the door,* through which, namely, one enters healthfully; and the reason is added: *By me, if any man enter in, he shall be saved;* of which entrance Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p385-6]: « Enter ye in at the narrow gate. How narrow is the gate, and strait is the way, that leadeth to life »! because Christ was poor and lowly. Through this little door the rich, full of riches, do not enter; on account of which it is said in Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p385-7]: « It is easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle, than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of heaven ». This entrance is through *faith* and *the Sacrament of baptism;* since the one is the gate of the virtues, and the other that of the Sacraments. He who enters in this way shall be saved; Mark, the last chapter[^p385-8]: « He that believeth and is baptized shall be saved ». *And he shall go in, and go out, and shall find pastures: he shall go in,* by contemplation, which calls back to the things within; *and he shall go out,* by action; Numbers, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p385-9]: « May the Lord, the God of the spirit of all flesh, provide a man, who may go in and go out before them ». Or, as Augustine expounds it[^p385-10], *he shall go in* to the contemplation of the Divinity, *he shall go out* to the beholding of the humanity, *and shall find pastures,* because he is refreshed in all, the understanding in the contemplation of the Divinity, and the sense in the contemplation of the humanity; of which pastures Ezechiel, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p385-11]: « I will feed them in the mountains of Israel, in most fruitful pastures will I feed them ».

12. (V. 10.). *The thief cometh not, but for to steal.* Here the third is touched on, namely that Christ himself *enters as the true shepherd,* not as a thief; on account of which he says: *The thief cometh not, but for to steal,* by extorting temporal goods; *and to kill,* by afflicting his subjects temporally; *and to destroy,* by casting them headlong into hell through evil example. *I am come that they may have life, and may have it more abundantly,* that is, to preserve life, as a shepherd; *that they may have life,* namely of grace, of which life above, in the sixth chapter[^p385-12]: « This is the bread coming down from heaven, which giveth life to the world »; *and may have it more abundantly,* namely the life of glory, of which below, in the seventeenth chapter[^p385-13]: « This is eternal life, that they may know thee, the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent ». This is called *abundant,* because it is said
<!-- page 386 -->
in Luke, the sixth chapter: « Good measure and pressed down and shaken together and running over shall they give into your bosom ».

### Questions.

13. Quaest. I. But here it is asked first about this, that the Lord here compares himself to a *door,* whereas above[^p386-1] he compared himself to a *porter;* how is the same one *door* and *porter* and *shepherd?*

**I respond:** It must be said that, as is said below, in the fourteenth chapter[^p386-2], Christ is *the way, the truth, and the life:* because *the way* to the Father, therefore *the door;* but because *the truth,* which teaches the way, therefore *the porter;* because *the life,* therefore *the shepherd,* who feeds and preserves life[^p386-3].

14. Quaest. II. Again it is asked about what he says: *As many as have come are thieves.*

**On the contrary:** The Prophets and Patriarchs and John the Baptist came: therefore according to this all were evil, as the heretics say[^p386-4].

To this it is answered that the force lies in what is said, *have come,* namely by their own authority, not by divine, as the false prophets, of whom Jeremias, the twenty-third chapter[^p386-5]: *I did not send cos* [so printed: *cos* for *eos*]*, and they ran;* but the good did not *come,* but *were sent.* Whence Augustine: « They did not come apart from him, but came with him ». For he himself is the truth; and therefore all who preached the truth came with him.

15. Quaest. III. Again it is asked about what he says, that *he shall go out, and shall find pastures.*

**On the contrary:** *No man putting his hand to the plough ought to look back*[^p386-6]: therefore no one who enters shall go out.

**I respond:** It must be said that going out is twofold: one *contrary to entrance,* and this is the going out from the Church through unbelief; and of this the objection argues, and of this Augustine says[^p386-7]: « To enter into the Church is good, but to go out is very bad »; and of this First John, the second chapter: *They went out from us, but they were not of us.* The other is *from contemplation to action;* and this is not of going back, but of exercise. Of this in the Psalm: « Man shall go forth to his work, and to his labour until the evening ».
