# Commentarius in Ecclesiasten — Cap. III, Vers. 1

## *Proponitur et probatur generaliter varietas et mutabilitas rerum in tempore*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 28 -->

### Capitulum III.

#### *Partis primae membrum II. Ostenditur vanitas rerum quoad varietatem in tempore, et hoc tribus articulis.*

*Omnia tempus habent* etc. Supra egit de vanitate rerum ostendens, in iis esse variabilitatem ratione *motus*; hic secundo vult ostendere vanitatem quantum ad determinationem et varietatem *in tempore*[^p28-1]. Et quia ostendit mutabilitatem rerum et varietatem non ad ipsarum contemptum, secundum quod sunt opera Dei, sed secundum quod amantur a nobis; ideo in hac parte ostendit primo *temporum varietatem*; secundo reprehendit *nostram curiositatem*, ibi: *Quid habet amplius homo*; tertio ostendit *divinorum operum perpetuitatem*, ibi: *Et didici, quod opera Dei.*

#### Art. 1. *De varietate et mutabilitate rerum in tempore tum generaliter tum specialiter.*

Prima pars habet duas, quia primo agit de varietate et mutabilitate rerum in tempore *generaliter*, secundo *specialiter*, inducendo et probando, ibi: *Tempus nascendi.*

#### Proponitur et probatur generaliter varietas et mutabilitas rerum in tempore.

(Vers. 1.). Proponit igitur *generaliter* dicens: *Omnia tempus habent*, id est temporis varietatem, quia ad Romanos octavo[^p28-2]: « Vanitati subiecta est creatura non volens ». *Et suis spatiis transeunt universa sub sole*, quantum ad praefixi temporis determinationem; Sapientiae undecimo: « Omnia in numero, pondere et mensura disposuisti »; primae ad Corinthios septimo: « Praeterit figura huius mundi »; et maxime hoc temporis spatio sibi praefixo; Iob decimo quarto: « Constituisti terminos eius, qui praeteriri non poterunt ».

### Quaestiones

I. Hic est quaestio de hoc quod dicit: *Omnia tempus habent.*

1. Instantia est: quoniam « substantiae spirituales, ut dicit Hieronymus[^p28-3], nec loco nec tempore continentur ».

2. Item, omne, quod est in tempore et habet tempus, est corporale[^p28-4]; sed substantiae spirituales sunt incorporales: ergo non sunt in tempore nec habent tempus.

Quod autem sint in tempore, videtur: 1. Quia dicit Augustinus ad Dardanum[^p28-5]: « Substantia spiritualis increata non movetur per tempus nec per locum; substantia spiritualis creata movetur per tempus, sed non per locum; corporalis creata per tempus et locum ».

2. Item, quod est in loco est in tempore; sed « solus Deus est incircumscriptibilis », ut dicit Damascenus[^p28-6], ergo solus est intemporalis: ergo omnia alia tempus habent.

Respondeo: dicendum ad hoc, quod ista *distributio* est *accommoda*, sicut, quando dicitur: caelum tegit omnia, intelligitur de his quae sunt sub caelo[^p28-7]; et ideo non est ferenda instantia in substantia *spirituali*. — Si autem velimus distributionem extendere, dicemus, quod *tempus* dicitur tripliciter: *communiter* et *proprie* et *magis proprie*. *Communiter*, secundum quod dicit mensuram exitus de *non-esse* ad *esse*; et hoc modo est in omnibus, quia omnia initium habuerunt. Et hoc videtur littera innuere, quia ipse dicit, quod *omnia tempus habent*, sed non, quod omnia *transeant* nisi *sub sole*. — Alio modo *proprie*, prout importat variationem sive in substantia, sive in affectione; et sic adhuc substantia spiritualis habet tempus quantum ad mutationem affectuum. — Tertio modo *magis proprie*, prout est mensura excellens; et sic est corruptibilium, et non omnium.

<!-- page 29 -->
Quod ergo quaeritur, utrum substantiae spirituales habeant tempus; solvendum, quod primo modo habeant quantum ad *substantiam*; secundo modo, quantum ad aliquas *affectiones*; tertio modo non habent[^p29-1]. — Et per hoc solvuntur contrarietates utriusque partis.

II. Quaeritur de secunda parte propositionis: *Et suis spatiis transeunt universa sub sole*, utrum transeant elementa. — Quod sic, videtur.

1. Apocalypsis vigesimo primo[^p29-2]: *Vidi caelum novum et terram novam*: ergo vetus transierat.

2. Item, primae Ioannis secundo[^p29-3]: *Transit mundus et concupiscentia eius*; et Matthaei vigesimo quarto[^p29-4]: *Caelum et terra transibunt.*

**Contra:** Sunt de constitutione universi: ergo cum universum maneat, manifestum est, quod etiam elementa.

Respondeo: dicendum, quod aliquid *transire* suo spatio est tripliciter: vel quantum ad corruptionem secundum *totam substantiam*, sicut corrumpuntur animalia bruta; vel quoad corruptionem sui secundum *partem*, sicut elementa generantur et corrumpuntur in suis terminis, in quibus se tangunt[^p29-5]; vel *transire*, hoc est a statu corruptibilitatis ad statum alium transmutari, et hoc modo mundus iste sensibilis innovabitur. — Quod ergo dicitur hic, quod *suis spatiis transeunt*, altero istorum trium modorum intelligitur.

2. Quod rursus obiicitur: *Transit mundus et concupiscentia eius,* determinatio solvit, scilicet mundus, prout est occasio voluptatis.

1. Quod dicitur: *Vidi caelum novum;* Glossa[^p29-6]: « id est innovatum ».

2. Quod dicitur in Matthaeo: *Caelum et terra transibunt;* Glossa exponit: transibunt, a forma mutabili ad immutabilem.

## English
<!-- page 28 -->

### Chapter III.

#### *Member II of the first part. The vanity of things is shown as to their variety in time, and this in three articles.*

*All things have their time* etc. Above he treated of the vanity of things, showing that there is in them variability by reason of *motion*; here secondly he wishes to show their vanity as to their determination and variety *in time*[^p28-1]. And because he shows the mutability and variety of things not to their contempt, according as they are the works of God, but according as they are loved by us; therefore in this part he shows first *the variety of times*; secondly he reproves *our curiosity*, there: *What hath a man more*; thirdly he shows *the perpetuity of the divine works*, there: *And I have learned that the works of God.*

#### Art. 1. *On the variety and mutability of things in time, both generally and specially.*

The first part has two, for first he treats of the variety and mutability of things in time *generally*, secondly *specially*, by inducing and proving, there: *A time to be born.*

#### The variety and mutability of things in time is proposed and proved in general.

(V. 1.). He proposes, therefore, *generally*, saying: *All things have their time*, that is, the variety of time, because to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p28-2]: « The creature was made subject to vanity, not willingly ». *And in their own spaces all things pass under the sun*, as to the determination of the time appointed beforehand; Wisdom, the eleventh chapter: « Thou hast ordered all things in number, weight and measure »; the first to the Corinthians, the seventh chapter: « The fashion of this world passeth away »; and most of all within this space of time appointed to it; Job, the fourteenth chapter: « Thou hast appointed his bounds, which cannot be passed ».

### Questions

I. Here is a question concerning that which he says: *All things have their time.*

1. There is an instance against it: since « spiritual substances, as Jerome says[^p28-3], are contained neither by place nor by time ».

2. Likewise, everything that is in time and has time is corporeal[^p28-4]; but spiritual substances are incorporeal: therefore they are not in time nor have they time.

But that they are in time, it seems: 1. Because Augustine says to Dardanus[^p28-5]: « Uncreated spiritual substance is not moved through time nor through place; created spiritual substance is moved through time, but not through place; created corporeal substance through time and place ».

2. Likewise, what is in place is in time; but « God alone is uncircumscribable », as Damascene says[^p28-6], therefore he alone is untemporal: therefore all other things have time.

I respond: it must be said to this, that that *distribution* is an *accommodated* one, just as, when it is said: heaven covers all things, it is understood of those things which are under heaven[^p28-7]; and therefore the instance is not to be pressed in the case of *spiritual* substance. — But if we wish to extend the distribution, we shall say that *time* is spoken of in three ways: *commonly* and *properly* and *more properly*. *Commonly*, according as it states the measure of the going forth from *non-being* to *being*; and in this way it is in all things, since all things had a beginning. And this the letter seems to hint, since he himself says that *all things have their time*, but not that all things *pass* except *under the sun*. — In another way *properly*, inasmuch as it implies variation either in substance or in affection; and thus even spiritual substance has time as to the change of its affections. — In a third way *more properly*, inasmuch as it is an excelling measure; and thus it belongs to corruptible things, and not to all.

<!-- page 29 -->
That, therefore, which is asked, whether spiritual substances have time; it is to be solved that in the first way they do have it as to *substance*; in the second way, as to certain *affections*; in the third way they do not have it[^p29-1]. — And through this the contrarieties of both sides are solved.

II. There is a question concerning the second part of the proposition: *And in their own spaces all things pass under the sun*, whether the elements pass away. — That they do, it seems.

1. The Apocalypse, the twenty-first chapter[^p29-2]: *I saw a new heaven and a new earth*: therefore the old had passed away.

2. Likewise, the first of John, the second chapter[^p29-3]: *The world passeth away, and the concupiscence thereof*; and Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p29-4]: *Heaven and earth shall pass away.*

**On the contrary:** They belong to the constitution of the universe: therefore, since the universe remains, it is manifest that the elements also remain.

I respond: it must be said that for a thing *to pass* in its own space is threefold: either as to corruption according to the *whole substance*, as brute animals are corrupted; or as to the corruption of itself according to a *part*, as the elements are generated and corrupted in their own boundaries, in which they touch one another[^p29-5]; or *to pass*, that is, to be transmuted from the state of corruptibility to another state, and in this way this sensible world will be made new. — What therefore is said here, that *in their own spaces they pass*, is understood in one or other of those three ways.

2. As to what is objected again: *The world passeth away, and the concupiscence thereof,* the determination solves it, namely the world inasmuch as it is the occasion of pleasure.

1. As to what is said: *I saw a new heaven;* the Gloss[^p29-6]: « that is, made new ».

2. As to what is said in Matthew: *Heaven and earth shall pass away;* the Gloss expounds: they shall pass away, from a mutable form to an immutable one.
