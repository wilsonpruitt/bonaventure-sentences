# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XV, Vers. 18-27
## *Tertio hortatur discipulos ad tolerantiam inimicorum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 453 -->

#### Tertio hortatur discipulos ad tolerantiam inimicorum.

31. *Si mundus vos odit* etc. Haec est tertia pars capituli[^p453-5], in qua Dominus hortatur ad *tolerantiam inimicorum persequentium;* et hoc quidem facit per hunc modum. Primo *praedicit mundi inimicitiam;* secundo vero, ex hoc *procedentem iniuriam;* tertio vero *ostendit persequentium* sive odientium *malitiam;* quarto vero, *suam innocentiam;* quinto vero *discipulos armat ad patientiam.*

(Vers. 18.). Primo ergo *praedicit mundi inimicitiam,* quae futura erat contra membra ad exemplum capitis. Fuerat enim odium mundi respectu *capitis,* quod est Christus; propter quod dicit: *Si mundus vos odit, scitote,* id est, considerate, *quia me priorem vobis odio habuit.* Rationem huius odii assignavit Dominus supra capitulo tertio[^p453-6]: « Omnis, qui male agit, odit lucem »; et supra septimo: « Me odit mundus, quoniam testimonium illi perhibeo, quod opera eius mala sunt ». Propter hanc eandem rationem ostendit odium *membrorum;* unde rationem huius odii reddit: quia ipsi disconveniebant cum mundo; propter quod dicit:

32. (Vers. 19.). *Si de mundo fuissetis,* id est, in mundo mansissetis per pravam consuetudinem; *mundus quod suum erat diligeret;* unde supra septimo[^p453-7] dicitur mundanis: « Tempus autem vestrum semper est paratum. Non potest vos odisse mundus ». *Quia vero de mundo non estis,* id est mundanae conversationis; *sed ego elegi vos de mundo, propterea odit vos mundus.* Illud odium significatum fuit Genesis vigesimo septimo[^p453-8] in odio Esau, ubi dicitur: « Oderat Esau Iacob pro benedictione, qua benedixerat ei pater eius ». *Esau* mundanos et reprobos significat, sed *Iacob* electos.

33. (Vers. 20.). *Mementote sermonis.* Hic tangitur secundum, scilicet *iniuria,* eodem modo, quo inimicitia, scilicet *capitis* primo et *membrorum* propter caput et ad exemplum capitis. Propter quod dicit: *Mementote sermonis, quem dixi*[^p453-9] *vobis: Non est servus maior domino suo;* supra decimo tertio: « Amen, amen dico vobis: Non est discipulus supra magistrum, nec apostolus maior domino suo ». Et ideo arguit: *Si me persecuti sunt, et vos persequentur.* Et illud est consequens, ut, si odiunt et persequuntur *caput,* quod etiam *membra.* Unde Augustinus[^p453-10]: « Recusat esse in corpore qui odium mundi non vult sustinere cum capite ». De persecutione *capitis* habetur in Psalmo: « Persecuti sunt hominem inopem et mendicum et compunctum corde ». Non solum persequentur in facto, sed contemnent in corde: *Si sermonem meum servaverunt, et vestrum servabunt,* id est, sicut meum sermonem contempserunt, ita et vestrum; Lucae decimo[^p453-11]: « Qui vos audit me audit, et qui vos spernit me spernit. — Vel *servaverunt,* id est, observaverunt ad calumniandum; Matthaei vigesimo secundo: « Abeuntes Pharisaei consilium inierunt, ut Iesum caperent in sermone »; et in Psalmo: « Observabit peccator iustum ». — Et etiam persequuntur mundani caput et membra, non persequuntur caput propter membra, sed e converso; unde dicit:

34. (Vers. 21.). *Sed haec omnia facient vobis propter nomen meum,* scilicet delendum, pro quo beati estis; unde Matthaei quinto[^p453-12]: « Beati estis, cum vos persecuti fuerint homines et dixerint omne malum adversum vos, mentientes propter me ». Ratio
<!-- page 454 -->
huius est caecitas infidelitatis; unde: *quia nesciunt eum qui misit me;* supra octavo: « Neque me scitis neque Patrem meum scitis »; et infra decimo septimo: « Pater iuste, mundus te non cognovit ».

35. (Vers. 22.). *Si non venissem.* Hic iam tertio *ostenditur odientium et persequentium malitia.* Ostendit ergo eorum malitiam inexcusabilem tum per ea quae *audierunt,* tum per ea quae *viderunt:* per ea quae *audierunt;* unde dicit: *Si non venissem et locutus eis non*[^p454-1] *fuissem,* verba scilicet fidei et eruditionis, *peccatum non haberent,* supple: ita grave et inexcusabile, quia, Lucae duodecimo, « servus, qui cognovit voluntatem domini sui et non facit, vapulabit multis; qui autem non cognovit vapulabit paucis »; et ideo, si non audissent, excusabiles essent aliquo modo per ignorantiam[^p454-2]. *Nunc autem excusationem non habent de peccato suo;* quia non ex ignorantia non credunt, sed ex odio, quod odium redundat in Patrem; propter quod dicit:

36. (Vers. 23.). *Qui me odit et Patrem meum odit;* superbia sua elevati, secundum illud Psalmi[^p454-3]: « Superbia eorum qui te oderunt, ascendit semper ». Quia ergo ex malitia audire recusaverunt, ideo inexcusabiles fuerunt; Matthaei duodecimo: « Regina Austri surget in iudicio cum generatione hac et condemnabit eam, quia venit a finibus terrae audire sapientiam Salomonis »; ad Romanos secundo: « Inexcusabilis es, o homo omnis, qui iudicas; in quo enim alterum iudicas, te ipsum condemnas ». Inexcusabiles etiam sunt Iudaei propter ea quae *viderunt;* ideo dicit:

37. (Vers. 24.). *Si non fecissem in eis opera, quae nemo alius fecit; peccatum non haberent,* scilicet non credendo ita magnum; quia, sicut supra quinto[^p454-4], « opera, quae ego facio, testimonium perhibent de me ». *Nunc autem et viderunt et oderunt et me et Patrem meum;* et ideo scienter et ex malitia recusant credere et persequuntur; propterea inexcusabiles sunt; Matthaei undecimo: « Vae tibi, Corozain! vae tibi, Bethsaida! quia, si in Tyro et Sidone factae fuissent virtutes, quae in te factae sunt, olim in cilicio et cinere poenitentiam egissent. Verumtamen dico vobis: Tyro et Sidoni remissius erit in die iudicii quam vobis ». Et haec Iudaeorum malignitas in Scriptura expressa est; propterea dicit:

38. (Vers. 25.). *Sed ut impleatur*[^p454-5] *sermo, qui in Lege eorum scriptus est* — sumitur large *Lex,* prout comprehendit Psalmos — *quia odio habuerunt me gratis;* in Psalmo scribitur: « Qui adversantur mihi inique, qui oderunt me gratis et annuunt oculis »; et in alio Psalmo: « Dum loquebar illis, impugnabant me gratis ». Illud odium praesignatum fuit Genesis trigesimo septimo, ubi dicitur, quod « fratres Ioseph oderant eum et non poterant ei quidquam pacifice loqui ».

39. (Vers. 26.). *Cum autem venerit.* Hic quarto suam *insinuat innocentiam* exprimendam[^p454-6] testimonio Spiritus sancti et Apostolorum; propter quod dicit: *Cum autem venerit;* ita me persecuti sunt tanquam virum nequam et morte dignum; *cum autem venerit Paracletus, quem ego mittam vobis a Patre, Spiritum veritatis, qui a Patre procedit*[^p454-7]*, ille testimonium perhibebit de me;* Actuum quinto: « Spiritus testis est, quem dedit Deus omnibus obedientibus sibi »; et perhibendo de mea innocentia testimonium, arguet mundum de malitia; infra decimo sexto: « Cum venerit ille, arguet mundum de peccato », quia non credit. — Non solum ille[^p454-8], sed et vos illo confirmati; unde:

40. (Vers. 27.). *Et vos testimonium perhibebitis, quia ab initio mecum estis;* Actuum primo[^p454-9]: « Oportet ergo ex his viris, qui nobiscum sunt congregati ab eo tempore, quo intravit et exivit inter nos Dominus Iesus, testem fieri resurrectionis eius nobiscum unum ex istis ». Hoc testimonium non dederunt Apostoli ante Spiritus sancti adventum, sed post. Unde Petrus, qui ad vocem ancillae Christum negavit[^p454-10], cum magna auctoritate principi sacerdotum post missionem Spiritus sancti respondit Actuum quinto: « Obedire oportet Deo magis quam hominibus ».

## English
<!-- page 453 -->

#### Third he exhorts the disciples to the toleration of enemies.

31. *If the world hate you* etc. This is the third part of the chapter[^p453-5], in which the Lord exhorts to the *toleration of persecuting enemies;* and this indeed he does in this way. First he *foretells the enmity of the world;* second, the *injury proceeding* from this; third he *shows the malice of those persecuting* or hating; fourth, *his own innocence;* fifth he *arms the disciples to patience.*

(Vers. 18.). First, then, he *foretells the enmity of the world,* which was to be against the members after the example of the head. For there had been a hatred of the world with respect to the *head,* which is Christ; on account of which he says: *If the world hate you, know ye,* that is, consider, *that it hath hated me before you.* The reason of this hatred the Lord assigned above, in chapter three[^p453-6]: « Every one that doth evil hateth the light »; and above, the seventh chapter: « Me the world hateth: because I give testimony of it, that the works thereof are evil ». For this same reason he shows the hatred of the *members;* hence he gives the reason of this hatred: because they disagreed with the world; on account of which he says:

32. (Vers. 19.). *If you had been of the world,* that is, had remained in the world through depraved custom; *the world would love what was its own;* hence above, the seventh chapter[^p453-7], it is said to worldly men: « But your time is always ready. The world cannot hate you ». *But because you are not of the world,* that is, of worldly conversation; *but I have chosen you out of the world, therefore the world hateth you.* That hatred was signified, Genesis, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p453-8], in the hatred of Esau, where it is said: « Esau hated Jacob, for the blessing wherewith his father had blessed him ». *Esau* signifies worldly men and the reprobate, but *Jacob* the chosen.

33. (Vers. 20.). *Remember the word.* Here is touched the second, namely the *injury,* in the same way as the enmity, namely first of the *head* and of the *members* on account of the head and after the example of the head. On account of which he says: *Remember the word that I said*[^p453-9] *to you: The servant is not greater than his lord;* above, the thirteenth chapter: « Amen, amen I say to you: The disciple is not above the teacher, nor the apostle greater than his lord ». And therefore he argues: *If they have persecuted me, they will also persecute you.* And that is a consequence, that, if they hate and persecute the *head,* they do so to the *members* also. Hence Augustine[^p453-10]: « He refuses to be in the body who will not bear the hatred of the world with the head ». Of the persecution of the *head* it is had in the Psalm: « They have persecuted the poor man and the beggar, and the broken in heart ». Not only shall they persecute in deed, but they shall contemn in heart: *If they have kept my word, they will keep yours also,* that is, as they have contemned my word, so also yours; Luke, the tenth chapter[^p453-11]: « He that heareth you, heareth me; and he that despiseth you, despiseth me. — Or *they have kept,* that is, they have observed in order to calumniate; Matthew, the twenty-second chapter: « The Pharisees going, consulted among themselves how to insnare Jesus in his speech »; and in the Psalm: « The sinner shall observe the just man ». — And also the worldly persecute the head and the members, they do not persecute the head on account of the members, but the reverse; hence he says:

34. (Vers. 21.). *But all these things they will do to you for my name's sake,* namely to blot it out, for which you are blessed; hence Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p453-12]: « Blessed are ye when men shall persecute you, and speak all that is evil against you, untruly, for my sake ». The reason
<!-- page 454 -->
of this is the blindness of unbelief; hence: *because they know not him that sent me;* above, the eighth chapter: « Neither me do you know, nor my Father »; and below, the seventeenth chapter: « Just Father, the world hath not known thee ».

35. (Vers. 22.). *If I had not come.* Here now third is *shown the malice of those hating and persecuting.* He shows, then, their malice as inexcusable both through the things which they *heard* and through the things which they *saw:* through the things which they *heard;* hence he says: *If I had not come, and had not*[^p454-1] *spoken to them,* the words, namely, of faith and of erudition, *they would not have sin,* supply: so grave and inexcusable, because, Luke, the twelfth chapter, « the servant, who knew the will of his lord, and doth it not, shall be beaten with many stripes; but he that knew not shall be beaten with few stripes »; and therefore, if they had not heard, they would be excusable in some way through ignorance[^p454-2]. *But now they have no excuse for their sin;* because it is not out of ignorance that they do not believe, but out of hatred, which hatred redounds upon the Father; on account of which he says:

36. (Vers. 23.). *He that hateth me, hateth my Father also;* lifted up by their pride, according to that of the Psalm[^p454-3]: « The pride of them that hate thee ascendeth continually ». Since, then, out of malice they refused to hear, therefore they were inexcusable; Matthew, the twelfth chapter: « The queen of the south shall rise in judgement with this generation, and shall condemn it: because she came from the ends of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon »; to the Romans, the second chapter: « Thou art inexcusable, O man, whosoever thou art that judgest. For wherein thou judgest another, thou condemnest thyself ». Inexcusable also are the Jews on account of the things which they *saw;* therefore he says:

37. (Vers. 24.). *If I had not done among them the works that no other man hath done, they would not have sin,* namely so great a one, by not believing; because, as above, the fifth chapter[^p454-4], « the works, which I do, give testimony of me ». *But now they have both seen and hated both me and my Father;* and therefore knowingly and out of malice they refuse to believe and they persecute; therefore they are inexcusable; Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « Woe to thee, Corozain, woe to thee, Bethsaida: for if in Tyre and Sidon had been wrought the miracles that have been wrought in thee, they had long ago done penance in sackcloth and ashes. But I say unto you, it shall be more tolerable for Tyre and Sidon in the day of judgement, than for you ». And this malignity of the Jews is expressed in Scripture; therefore he says:

38. (Vers. 25.). *But that the word may be fulfilled*[^p454-5] *which is written in their Law* — *Law* is taken broadly, as it comprehends the Psalms — *They hated me without cause;* in the Psalm it is written: « They that are my enemies wrongfully, who have hated me without cause, and wink with the eyes »; and in another Psalm: « When I spoke to them they fought against me without cause ». That hatred was foreshown, Genesis, the thirty-seventh chapter, where it is said that « the brethren of Joseph hated him, and could not speak anything peaceably to him ».

39. (Vers. 26.). *But when he cometh.* Here fourth he *intimates* his own *innocence,* to be expressed[^p454-6] by the testimony of the Holy Spirit and of the Apostles; on account of which he says: *But when he cometh;* thus they have persecuted me as a wicked man and worthy of death; *but when the Paraclete cometh, whom I will send you from the Father, the Spirit of truth, who proceedeth from the Father*[^p454-7]*, he shall give testimony of me;* Acts, the fifth chapter: « The Spirit is a witness, whom God hath given to all that obey him »; and by giving testimony of my innocence, he will reprove the world of malice; below, the sixteenth chapter: « When he is come, he will reprove the world of sin », because it does not believe. — Not only he[^p454-8], but also you, confirmed by him; hence:

40. (Vers. 27.). *And you shall give testimony, because you are with me from the beginning;* Acts, the first chapter[^p454-9]: « Wherefore of these men who have companied with us from that time in which the Lord Jesus came in and went out among us, one of these must be made a witness with us of his resurrection ». This testimony the Apostles did not give before the coming of the Holy Spirit, but after. Hence Peter, who at the voice of a maidservant denied Christ[^p454-10], with great authority, after the mission of the Holy Spirit, responded to the prince of the priests, Acts, the fifth chapter: « We ought to obey God, rather than men ».
