# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. I, Vers. 1-2
## *De Verbo in se, et primo in comparatione ad dicentem*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 246 -->

### Commentarius in Evangelium Ioannis.
### Capitulum I.

1. *In principio erat Verbum* etc. Iste liber, qui est de Verbo incarnato, in quo duplex consideratur natura, divina scilicet et humana, dividitur primo in duas partes. In prima agit de Verbo in se; in secunda vero, in quantum est carni unitum, et incipit illa pars ibi[^p246-1]: *Fuit homo missus a Deo.* Et quia *verbum* dicit operativam potentiam, ut dicit Augustinus, et habet respectum ad dicentem, et ad id quod per verbum dicitur; ideo prima pars habet duas. In prima determinatur de Verbo in comparatione ad dicentem; in secunda in comparatione ad ea quae dicuntur per Verbum: *Omnia per ipsum facta sunt.*

#### Pars. I. De Verbo in se, et primo in comparatione ad dicentem.

In prima parte ponuntur quatuor clausulae[^p246-2], in quibus describitur Verbum incarnatum quantum ad quatuor conditiones, quae sunt: in essentia *unitas*, in persona *alietas*, in maiestate *aequalitas*, in duratione *coaeternitas*. Et per has conditiones eliduntur quatuor errores[^p246-3].

2. (Vers. 1.). Prima igitur conditio est in *essentia unitas*, et notatur in prima clausula, cum dicitur: *In principio erat Verbum;* sicut dicit Augustinus[^p246-4]: « Est principium non de principio, et hoc est Pater; et est principium de principio, et hoc Filius ». Hic accipitur *principium* per antonomasiam de Patre; unde sensus est: *In principio*, hoc est in Patre, est Filius, non separatus a Patre per essentiam, infra decimo quarto[^p246-5]: « Ego in Patre, et Pater in me est », tanquam indivisi; quia sicut dicitur infra decimo: « Ego et Pater unum sumus ». Et in hoc eliditur *infidelitas paganorum*, qui nobis indignantur, quod dicimus tres personas, et non tres deos; sicut dicit Augustinus de Agone christiano[^p246-6]; et hoc, quia non intelligunt, quomodo *Verbum erat in principio.*

3. Secunda conditio est in *persona alietas*, et haec tangitur in secunda clausula, cum dicitur: *Verbum erat apud Deum;* quia, sicut dicit Grammaticus[^p246-7], et etiam Victor, praepositiones sunt transitivae; ideo haec praepositio *apud* importat *distinctionem* secundum quod praepositio, ratione vero specialis significationis dicit *auctoritatem* in Patre et *subauctoritatem* in Filio, quia dicitur minor esse
<!-- page 247 -->
apud maiorem et non e converso, secundum communem modum loquendi. Ibi vero, etsi non sit minus et maius, tamen est auctoritas in Patre et subauctoritas in Filio[^p247-1]. Nomine vero *verbi* intelligitur *generationis emanatio.* Et haec tria tanguntur Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto[^p247-2]: « Ego ex ore Altissimi prodii primogenita ante omnem creaturam »: *Altissimi*, ecce auctoritas; *ex ore*, ecce generatio; *prodii*, ecce distinctio. Et in hoc eliditur *haeresis Sabellianorum*, qui ponunt, quod unus et idem sint Pater et Filius; quia non intellexerunt, quomodo *Verbum erat apud Deum*[^p247-3].

4. Tertia conditio est in *maiestate aequalitas*, et haec tangitur in tertia clausula, ibi: *Et Deus erat Verbum.* Si enim, sicut dicit Anselmus[^p247-4], « Deus est quo maius excogitari non potest »; si Verbum erat Deus: ergo erat, quo maius excogitari non poterat, et ita in maiestate Patri aequalis erat; infra decimo quarto dixit discipulis: « Creditis in Deum, et in me credite ». Si in solum Deum credendum tanquam in summam veritatem, et in Verbum credendum; restat, quod Deus erat Verbum. Et in hoc eliditur *haeresis Arianorum*, qui posuerunt Filium creaturam et minorem Patre, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p247-5]. Non enim intellexerunt, quomodo *Deus erat Verbum.*

5. (Vers. 2.). Quarta conditio, in *duratione coaeternitas*, et haec tangitur, cum dicitur: *Hoc erat in principio apud Deum*, in quo notatur coaeternitas. Nam Deus Pater ab aeterno erat; quodsi Filius non erat coaeternus, ante fuit Pater quam Filius: non ergo Verbum erat in principio apud Deum; sed erat in principio, et ideo coaeternum. Unde Proverbiorum octavo[^p247-6]: « Dominus possedit me in initio viarum suarum, antequam quidquam faceret a principio. Ab aeterno ordinata sum ». Et in hoc eliditur *haeresis Photinianorum* et *Ebionitarum*, qui dixerunt, Christum ex Maria prorsus sumsisse exordium, sicut dicit Augustinus de Agone christiano[^p247-7]. Non enim intellexerunt, quomodo *Verbum erat in principio apud Deum.*

### Quaestiones.

6. Ad intelligentiam praedictorum potest hic quaeri:

Quaest. I. Cum Filius importet proprietatem maxime distinctivam, quare potius describit Dei Filium sub nomine *verbi* quam sub nomine *filii?* Videtur enim, quod deberet facere e contrario.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *filius* solum dicit comparationem ad patrem, *verbum* vero dicit comparationem ad dicentem, dicit comparationem ad id quod per verbum dicitur, dicit comparationem ad vocem, quam induit, dicit etiam comparationem ad doctrinam, quae mediante verbo in altero efficitur; et cum Dei Filius debeat hic describi non tantum in comparatione ad Patrem, a quo procedit, sed etiam ad creaturas, quas fecit, et ad carnem, quam induit, et ad documenta, quae praebuit: nobilissime et decentissime sub nomine verbi describi debuit, quia nomen illud ad omnia respicit, nec posset in mundo nomen convenientius inveniri[^p247-8].

7. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod per hoc verbum *erat* describitur. Si enim *erat* est temporis praeteriti imperfecti, omnino repugnat aeternitati, quia nulla ibi praeteritio. *Si dicas*, sicut dicit Augustinus in Glossa[^p247-9], quod hoc verbum *est* aliquando importat tempus secundum analogiam aliorum verborum; hic autem non dicit tempus, sed essentiam sine ullo temporali motu designat: hoc non solvit, quia, cum dicitur: *Filius generatur* vel *est genitus*, non importat ibi tempus; tamen melius dicitur: *semper genitus* quam *semper generatur*[^p247-10]. — Item
<!-- page 248 -->
Dominus ita dicit: *Antequam Abraham fieret, ego sum*, infra octavo[^p248-1]; non dicit *fui* vel *eram:* ergo et nunc per *praesens* deberet dicere.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod quia *esse* Verbi describitur hic ut *permanens*, describitur ut *immutabile*, ut *cetera antecedens*, quia hoc verbum *erat* secundum quod huiusmodi haec tria importat: ideo elegantissime dixit *erat.* Cum enim sit medium inter praesens et praeteritum, et « medium naturam sapiat extremorum[^p248-2] »; a praesenti habet *permanentiam*, a praeterito *immutabilitatem*, a se ipso *antecessionem* et ordinem, scilicet aeternitatis ad tempus. Ideo recte dicitur *erat.*

8. Sed opponit haereticus: si Verbum procedit a dicente ut filius a patre; et filius est patre posterior: ergo Verbum non erat in principio apud Deum. *Si tu dicas*, quod comparatur sicut splendor ad lucem; opponit alius haereticus: si lux multo luminosior et nobilior est splendore, qui ab ipsa procedit: ergo et Pater Verbo[^p248-3].

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod acceptio similitudinis, scilicet in creaturis, nusquam plene reperitur respectu Dei, quia omnis creatura plus est dissimilis quam similis; et ideo erraverunt haeretici, qui omnino assimilare voluerunt; sed in pluribus est accipere[^p248-4]. *Verbum* enim comparatur ad dicentem sicut *filius* ad patrem propter similitudinem in natura et perfectionem aequalem, comparatur ut *splendor* ad lucem propter coaeternitatem. Et ideo in omnibus talibus argumentis est *consequens*[^p248-5].

## English
<!-- page 246 -->

### Commentary on the Gospel of John.
### Chapter I.

1. *In the beginning was the Word* etc. This book, which is about the incarnate Word, in whom a twofold nature is considered, namely the divine and the human, is divided first into two parts. In the first he treats of the Word in himself; in the second, however, of the Word insofar as he is united to flesh, and that part begins there[^p246-1]: *There was a man sent from God.* And because *word* expresses an operative power, as Augustine says, and has a relation to the one speaking and to that which is said through the word; therefore the first part has two. In the first a determination is made concerning the Word in comparison to the one speaking; in the second, in comparison to the things which are said through the Word: *All things were made by him.*

#### Part I. On the Word in himself, and first in comparison to the one speaking.

In the first part four clauses[^p246-2] are set down, in which the incarnate Word is described as to four conditions, which are: in essence *unity*, in person *otherness*, in majesty *equality*, in duration *coeternity*. And through these conditions four errors are struck down[^p246-3].

2. (Vers. 1.). The first condition, then, is *unity in essence*, and it is noted in the first clause, when it is said: *In the beginning was the Word;* as Augustine says[^p246-4]: « There is a beginning not from a beginning, and this is the Father; and there is a beginning from a beginning, and this is the Son ». Here *beginning* is taken by antonomasia of the Father; whence the sense is: *In the beginning*, that is, in the Father, is the Son, not separated from the Father by essence, below in the fourteenth[^p246-5]: « I am in the Father, and the Father in me », as being undivided; because, as is said below in the tenth: « I and the Father are one ». And in this is struck down *the unbelief of the pagans*, who are indignant with us because we say three persons and not three gods; as Augustine says in *On the Christian Combat*[^p246-6]; and this, because they do not understand how *the Word was in the beginning.*

3. The second condition is *otherness in person*, and this is touched on in the second clause, when it is said: *The Word was with God;* because, as the Grammarian says[^p246-7], and Victor too, prepositions are transitive; therefore this preposition *with* implies *distinction* according as it is a preposition, but by reason of its special signification it expresses *authority* in the Father and *subauthority* in the Son, because the lesser is said to be
<!-- page 247 -->
with the greater and not conversely, according to the common manner of speaking. There, however, although there is not lesser and greater, yet there is authority in the Father and subauthority in the Son[^p247-1]. By the name *word*, moreover, is understood *the emanation of generation.* And these three are touched on in Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth[^p247-2]: « I came out of the mouth of the most High, the firstborn before all creatures »: *of the most High*, behold the authority; *out of the mouth*, behold the generation; *I came out*, behold the distinction. And in this is struck down *the heresy of the Sabellians*, who posit that the Father and the Son are one and the same; because they did not understand how *the Word was with God*[^p247-3].

4. The third condition is *equality in majesty*, and this is touched on in the third clause, there: *And the Word was God.* For if, as Anselm says[^p247-4], « God is that than which a greater cannot be thought »; if the Word was God: then he was that than which a greater could not be thought, and so he was equal to the Father in majesty; below in the fourteenth he said to the disciples: « You believe in God, believe also in me ». If one must believe in God alone as in the highest truth, and one must believe in the Word; it remains that the Word was God. And in this is struck down *the heresy of the Arians*, who held the Son to be a creature and less than the Father, as Augustine says[^p247-5]. For they did not understand how *the Word was God.*

5. (Vers. 2.). The fourth condition, *coeternity in duration*, and this is touched on when it is said: *The same was in the beginning with God*, in which coeternity is noted. For God the Father was from eternity; but if the Son was not coeternal, the Father was before the Son: then the Word was not in the beginning with God; but he was in the beginning, and therefore coeternal. Whence Proverbs, the eighth[^p247-6]: « The Lord possessed me in the beginning of his ways, before he made any thing from the beginning. I was set up from eternity ». And in this is struck down *the heresy of the Photinians* and *of the Ebionites*, who said that Christ took his beginning wholly from Mary, as Augustine says in *On the Christian Combat*[^p247-7]. For they did not understand how *the Word was in the beginning with God.*

### Questions.

6. For the understanding of the foregoing it can be asked here:

Quaest. I. Since Son implies a most distinctive property, why does he describe the Son of God under the name of *word* rather than under the name of *son?* For it seems that he ought to do the contrary.

**I respond:** It must be said that *son* expresses only a comparison to the father, but *word* expresses a comparison to the one speaking, expresses a comparison to that which is said through the word, expresses a comparison to the voice which it puts on, expresses also a comparison to the teaching[^tr-doctrina] which is effected in another by means of the word; and since the Son of God ought here to be described not only in comparison to the Father, from whom he proceeds, but also to the creatures which he made, and to the flesh which he put on, and to the lessons which he furnished: most nobly and most fittingly he ought to have been described under the name of word, because that name looks to them all, nor could a more fitting name be found in the world[^p247-8].

7. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked about this, that he is described by this word *was*. For if *was* is of the imperfect past tense, it is altogether repugnant to eternity, because there is no pastness there. *If you say*, as Augustine says in the Gloss[^p247-9], that this word *is* sometimes implies time according to the analogy of other words; but here it does not express time, but designates the essence without any temporal motion: this does not solve it, because when it is said: *The Son is generated* or *is begotten*, time is not implied there; yet it is better said: *always begotten* than *always generated*[^p247-10]. — Likewise
<!-- page 248 -->
the Lord says thus: *Before Abraham was made, I am*, below in the eighth[^p248-1]; he does not say *I have been* or *I was:* therefore now too he ought to speak by the *present*.

**I respond:** It must be said that because the *being* of the Word is described here as *permanent*, it is described as *immutable*, as *preceding the rest*, because this word *was*, insofar as it is such, implies these three: therefore most elegantly he said *was.* For since it is a mean between the present and the past, and « the mean savours of the nature of the extremes[^p248-2] »; from the present it has *permanence*, from the past *immutability*, from itself *precedence* and order, namely of eternity to time. Therefore it is rightly said *was.*

8. But a heretic objects: if the Word proceeds from the one speaking as a son from a father; and a son is later than the father: then the Word was not in the beginning with God. *If you say* that it is compared as brightness to light; another heretic objects: if light is much more luminous and nobler than the brightness which proceeds from it: then the Father too is so than the Word[^p248-3].

**I respond:** It must be said that the taking of a likeness, namely among creatures, is nowhere fully found with respect to God, because every creature is more unlike than like; and therefore the heretics erred, who wished to liken altogether; but it is to be taken in several[^p248-4]. For the *Word* is compared to the one speaking as a *son* to a father on account of likeness in nature and equal perfection; it is compared as *brightness* to light on account of coeternity. And therefore in all such arguments there is a *consequent*[^p248-5].

Confutetur autem per hoc quod dicit: *In principio erat, et Deus erat illud Verbum.* Sabellius vero negans personarum trinitatem, sed asserens unitatem, reiiciatur per hoc quod dicitur: *Et illud Verbum erat apud Deum.* Manifeste enim hoc loco alium esse Verbum, et alium esse Patrem, declarat magnus Ioannes, etiamsi non sit aliud et aliud.
