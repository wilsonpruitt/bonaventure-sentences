# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. III, Vers. 22-26
## *Manifestavit se Dominus Iudaeis virtutis signo et eruditionis verbo; hic manifestat se regenerationis Sacramento, recipiendo Iudaeos ad baptismum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 285 -->

39. *Post haec venit Iesus* etc. Manifestavit se Dominus Iudaeis virtutis signo et eruditionis verbo; hic manifestat se *regenerationis Sacramento,* recipiendo Iudaeos ad baptismum. Et quia tam Dominus quam Ioannes baptizabat, et tamen solus Dominus regenerabat; ideo primo introducitur Christi et Ioannis *purificatio;* secundo, de praecellentia nascens *quaestio,* ibi[^p285-1]: *Facta est ergo quaestio;* tertio vero, *quaestionis terminatio,* ibi: *Respondit Ioannes.*

40. (Vers. 22.). Describitur ergo *Christi purificatio* per baptismum primo, per quam Iudaeis se manifestat; ideo dicit: *Post haec,* id est post praedictas manifestationes, *venit Iesus et discipuli eius in Iudaeam terram,* ut scilicet Iudaeis se manifestum faceret per baptismum. Unde et subdit: *Et illic demorabatur cum illis*[^p285-2], scilicet discipulis, *et baptizabat;* secundum quod Ioannes ei testimonium perhibuerat supra primo: « Super quem videris Spiritum descendentem, hic est qui baptizat »; nec ipse solus baptizabat, immo etiam eius praecursor. Propter quod dicit:

41. (Vers. 23.). *Erat autem Ioannes*[^p285-3] *baptizans in Aennon iuxta Salim,* id est Salem, quod fuit castrum Melchisedech, ad Hebraeos septimo. Et ratio subditur: *Quia erant illic aquae multae.* Bona ratio, quia baptizabat Ioannes tantum in aqua; Marci primo[^p285-4]: « Ego baptizavi vos aqua », dicebat Ioannes. *Et veniebant,* ad eum, *et baptizabantur,* etiam Christo baptizante; Matthaei tertio: « Exibat ad eum Ierosolyma et omnis Iudaea et omnis regio circa Iordanem, et baptizabantur ab eo ».

42. (Vers. 24.). *Nondum enim missus erat*[^p285-5] *Ioannes in carcerem;* quia tunc cessavit eius baptismus, absente magistro; erat enim temporalis. De incarceratione Ioannis Matthaei decimo quarto: « Herodes tenuit Ioannem et alligavit eum et misit in carcerem » etc.

43. *Facta est ergo quaestio*[^p285-6] etc. Hic introducitur ex duplici purificatione *procedens quaestio,* quae ortum habuit a discipulis Ioannis calumniantibus Iudaeos, quod, relicto Ioannis baptismate, ibant ad Christum. Propter quod dicit:

(Vers. 25.). *Facta est ergo quaestio ex discipulis Ioannis,* quasi ab ipsis trahens originem, *cum Iudaeis,* contradicentibus, scilicet *de purificatione:* ubi scilicet esset maior purificatio, utrum in baptismo Ioannis, vel Christi. Et dicebant discipuli Ioannis, baptismum Ioannis esse maiorem, Iudaei e contrario. Propter hoc, videntes, se non posse praevalere, ipsum Christum per invidiam accusant. Propter quod subdit:

44. (Vers. 26.). *Et venerunt ad Ioannem et dixerunt ei: Rabbi! qui erat trans Iordanem tecum,* tecum erat tanquam minor, quia tu ipsum baptizasti, quem etiam exaltasti; unde subdunt: *Cui testimonium perhibuisti;* supra primo[^p285-7]: « Ioannes testimonium perhibet de ipso » etc.; *ecce, hic baptizat,* quasi, officium tuum usurpat. Et ut provocent in aemulationem, addunt: *Et omnes veniunt ad eum,* quasi de te amplius non curantes. Aemulatione movebantur. Simile fecit Iosue; Numerorum undecimo[^p285-8] de viris prophetantibus dixit Moysi: « Domine mi, prohibe eos. Et Moyses respondit: Quare tu aemularis pro me? Quis tribuat, ut omnis populus prophetet, et det eis Dominus spiritum suum »?

### Quaestiones.

45. Quaest. I. Sed incidit hic quaestio primo de hoc quod dicit, quod Iesus baptizabat. — **Contra:** Infra quarto[^p285-9]: *Iesus non baptizabat, sed discipuli eius:* ergo est ibi contradictio.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod ille facit, cuius auctoritate fit; unde *facere aliquid,* hoc est dupli-
<!-- page 286 -->
citer, scilicet *auctoritate,* vel *ministerio.* Christus baptizabat *auctoritate,* sed discipuli eius *ministerio*[^p286-1]; vel baptizabat interius, discipuli autem exterius.

46. Quaest. II. Sed tunc est quaestio: unde est hoc, quod Dominus in propria persona non baptizabat exterius? — Et videtur, quod deberet, ut aliis praeberet exemplum et formam et modum.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod ipse aliis praebuit modum et docuit, sed tamen non baptizabat causa vitandi *erroris* et *praesumtionis: erroris,* quia credidissent homines, quod alii, qui non essent ab eo baptizati, vere baptizati non essent; *praesumtionis,* ne baptizati ab ipso extollerentur et despicerent baptizatos a discipulis et per hoc baptismum discipulorum.

47. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur: cum baptismus Ioannis esset praeparatorius ad baptismum Christi[^p286-2], et adveniente veritate, cessat umbra; videtur, quod debuit cessare baptizare, ex quo Christus incepit.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod cessavit, sed non statim. Et ratio fuit quadruplex: ne videretur ei contrarius; ne videretur instabilis; ne videretur indignanter tulisse; ne discipulos suos in invidiam et zelum mitteret[^p286-3].

48. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur: quis baptismus potior, utrum Christi, vel Ioannis? — Quod Ioannis, videtur:

1. Quia Christus fuit baptizatus baptismo Ioannis: ergo sicut nobilius fuit Melchisedech sacerdotium sacerdotio Levi, quia Levi fuit a Melchisedech decimatus[^p286-4]; eadem ratione videtur de baptismate.

2. Item, *Christus non baptizabat, sed discipuli eius*[^p286-5]; sed discipuli Christi non erant maiores Ioanne: ergo nec baptismus discipulorum Christi maior baptismate Ioannis.

3. Item quaeritur, utrum baptismus ille daret gratiam? — Et quod non, videtur; quia dicitur infra septimo[^p286-6]: *Spiritus nondum erat datus, quia Iesus nondum erat glorificatus;* et si aliquis baptizatus fuisset mortuus ante passionem, non intrasset regnum Dei.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod secundum Chrysostomum[^p286-7] baptismus discipulorum Christi ante passionem fuit tantum praeparatorius, sicut et baptismus Ioannis; unde uterque erat expers gratiae. — Tamen Beda et Augustinus[^p286-8] dicunt, quod « baptizavit Ioannes, et rebaptizatum est; baptizavit Iudas, et rebaptizatum non est ». Unde dicunt, quod baptismus ille gratiam dabat, et ideo nobilior, quia nobilioris efficaciae.

1. Quod ergo obiicitur, quod Christus fuit baptizatus; dicendum, quod baptismus Ioannis nullum omnino in Christum habuit effectum[^p286-9].

2. Quod obiicitur, quod discipuli baptizabant; dicendum, quod verum est, *exterius;* sed tamen in eo baptismo Christus baptizabat interius; non sic autem in baptismate Ioannis.

3. Quod obiicitur: Spiritus nondum erat datus; intelligendum est de datione *visibili* et aperta, quomodo dabatur per manuum impositionem, non de datione invisibili[^p286-10].

Quod obiicit, quod non evolaret baptizatus mortuus; dicendum, quod non erat propter defectum gratiae in baptismo, sed quia pretium eius nondum erat solutum. — Et si obiiciat, quod baptismus habet efficaciam a passione[^p286-11]; intelligendum, quod efficaciam habet *collationis gratiae* a virtute divina ibi invisibiliter operante, a passione vero quantum ad *ianuae apertionem*[^p286-12]. Vel: si nondum erat passus, tamen fides passionis erat praesens, ratione cuius efficaciam habebat, licet non aperiendi ianuam, tamen dandi gratiam[^p286-13].

## English
<!-- page 285 -->

39. *After these things Jesus came* etc. The Lord manifested himself to the Jews by the sign of virtue and the word of erudition; here he manifests himself by *the Sacrament of regeneration,* receiving the Jews to baptism. And because both the Lord and John baptized, and yet the Lord alone regenerated; therefore first there is introduced the *purification* of Christ and of John; second, the *question* arising about pre-eminence, there[^p285-1]: *There arose therefore a question;* but third, *the settling of the question,* there: *John answered.*

40. (Verse 22.). The *purification of Christ* through baptism is therefore described first, through which he manifests himself to the Jews; therefore he says: *After these things,* that is, after the aforesaid manifestations, *Jesus came and his disciples into the land of Judea,* namely so that he might make himself manifest to the Jews through baptism. Whence he also adds: *And there he abode with them*[^p285-2], namely with the disciples, *and baptized;* according as John had given testimony to him above, in the first chapter: « He upon whom thou shalt see the Spirit descending, he it is that baptizeth »; nor did he alone baptize, but his forerunner also. On account of which he says:

41. (Verse 23.). *And John was*[^p285-3] *baptizing in Ennon near Salim,* that is, Salem, which was the stronghold of Melchisedech, to the Hebrews, the seventh chapter. And the reason is added: *Because there was much water there.* A good reason, because John baptized only in water; Mark, the first chapter[^p285-4]: « I have baptized you with water », John said. *And they came,* to him, *and were baptized,* even while Christ was baptizing; Matthew, the third chapter: « There went out to him Jerusalem and all Judea and all the country about Jordan, and were baptized by him ».

42. (Verse 24.). *For John was not yet*[^p285-5] *cast into prison;* because then his baptism ceased, the teacher being absent; for it was temporal. On the imprisonment of John, Matthew, the fourteenth chapter: « Herod laid hold on John and bound him and sent him into prison » etc.

43. *There arose therefore a question*[^p285-6] etc. Here is introduced the *question proceeding* from the twofold purification, which had its origin from the disciples of John, who were calumniating the Jews because, leaving the baptism of John, they were going to Christ. On account of which he says:

(Verse 25.). *There arose therefore a question from the disciples of John,* as though drawing its origin from them, *with the Jews,* who contradicted, namely *concerning purification:* namely, where the greater purification was, whether in the baptism of John, or of Christ. And the disciples of John said that the baptism of John was the greater, the Jews the contrary. On account of this, seeing that they could not prevail, they accuse Christ himself out of envy. On account of which he adds:

44. (Verse 26.). *And they came to John and said to him: Rabbi! he that was with thee beyond the Jordan,* was with thee as the lesser, because thou didst baptize him, whom also thou didst exalt; whence they add: *To whom thou gavest testimony;* above, in the first chapter[^p285-7]: « John giveth testimony of him » etc.; *behold, he baptizeth,* as if to say, he usurps thy office. And so that they may provoke him to emulation, they add: *And all men come to him,* as if no longer caring about thee. They were moved by emulation. Josue did the like; Numbers, the eleventh chapter[^p285-8], about the men prophesying he said to Moses: « My lord, forbid them. And Moses answered: Why hast thou emulation for me? Who will grant that all the people may prophesy, and that the Lord may give them his spirit »?

### Questions.

45. Question I. But a question arises here first about this that he says, that Jesus baptized. — **On the contrary:** Below, in the fourth chapter[^p285-9]: *Jesus baptized not, but his disciples:* therefore there is a contradiction there.

**I respond:** It must be said that he does a thing by whose authority it is done; whence *to do something* is in two
<!-- page 286 -->
ways, namely *by authority,* or *by ministry.* Christ baptized *by authority,* but his disciples *by ministry*[^p286-1]; or he baptized inwardly, but the disciples outwardly.

46. Question II. But then there is the question: whence is it that the Lord in his own person did not baptize outwardly? — And it is seen that he ought to have, so that he might offer to others an example and a form and a mode.

**I respond:** It must be said that he himself offered others the mode and taught it, but yet he did not baptize, for the sake of avoiding *error* and *presumption: error,* because men would have believed that others, who had not been baptized by him, were not truly baptized; *presumption,* lest those baptized by him should be lifted up and despise those baptized by the disciples, and through this the baptism of the disciples.

47. Question III. Likewise it is asked: since the baptism of John was preparatory to the baptism of Christ[^p286-2], and when the truth comes, the shadow ceases; it is seen that he ought to have ceased to baptize from the time Christ began.

**I respond:** It must be said that he ceased, but not at once. And the reason was fourfold: lest he should seem contrary to him; lest he should seem unstable; lest he should seem to have borne it indignantly; lest he should send his disciples into envy and jealousy[^p286-3].

48. Question IV. Likewise it is asked: which baptism is the more powerful, that of Christ or that of John? — That it is John's, is seen:

1. Because Christ was baptized with the baptism of John: therefore, just as the priesthood of Melchisedech was nobler than the priesthood of Levi, because Levi was tithed by Melchisedech[^p286-4], by the same reason it seems so of the baptism.

2. Likewise, *Christ baptized not, but his disciples*[^p286-5]; but the disciples of Christ were not greater than John: therefore neither was the baptism of the disciples of Christ greater than the baptism of John.

3. Likewise it is asked whether that baptism gave grace? — And that it did not, is seen; because it is said below, in the seventh chapter[^p286-6]: *The Spirit was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified;* and if someone baptized had died before the passion, he would not have entered the kingdom of God.

**I respond:** It must be said that, according to Chrysostom[^p286-7], the baptism of the disciples of Christ before the passion was only preparatory, just as the baptism of John also was; whence each was without a share of grace. — Yet Bede and Augustine[^p286-8] say that « John baptized, and it was baptized again; Judas baptized, and it was not baptized again ». Whence they say that that baptism gave grace, and was therefore nobler, because of nobler efficacy.

1. To what is objected, therefore, that Christ was baptized; it must be said that the baptism of John had no effect at all upon Christ[^p286-9].

2. To what is objected, that the disciples baptized; it must be said that it is true, *outwardly;* but yet in that baptism Christ baptized inwardly; but not so in the baptism of John.

3. To what is objected: the Spirit was not yet given; it is to be understood of the *visible* and open giving, as he was given through the imposition of hands, not of the invisible giving[^p286-10].

To what he objects, that one baptized who died would not fly away; it must be said that this was not on account of a defect of grace in the baptism, but because its price had not yet been paid. — And if he objects that baptism has efficacy from the passion[^p286-11]; it is to be understood that it has the efficacy *of the conferring of grace* from the divine power invisibly working there, but from the passion as to *the opening of the gate*[^p286-12]. Or: if he had not yet suffered, yet faith in the passion was present, by reason of which it had efficacy, though not of opening the gate, yet of giving grace[^p286-13].
