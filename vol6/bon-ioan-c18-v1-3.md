# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XVIII, Vers. 1-3
## *Primo, de Christi comprehensione*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 477 -->

### Capitulum XVIII.

#### Secundo agitur de his quae passionem concomitantur, quadrupliciter.

1. *Haec cum dixisset* etc. Supra egit de antecedentibus ad passionem, scilicet a fine undecimi[^p477-9]; hic agit de his, in quibus *passio consummatur.* Et quia in passione comprehensus est et iudicatus, occisus et sepultus; ideo dividitur haec pars in quatuor partes. In prima agitur de Christi *comprehensione;* in secunda, de eius *condemnatione,* ibi[^p477-10]: *Et adduxerunt eum ad Annam;* in tertia, de eius *passione,* ibi: *Susceperunt autem Iesum* etc.; in quarta, de *sepulturae conditione,* in fine decimi noni, ibi: *Post haec autem rogavit Pilatum.*

#### Primo, de Christi comprehensione.

In prima igitur parte, in qua manifestatur, qualis fuit Christi *comprehensio,* tria ostenduntur: primo, quod fuit *proditiosa;* secundo, quod *voluntaria,* ibi[^p477-11]: *Iesus autem sciens quae ventura erant;* tertio, quod *ignominiosa,* ibi: *Cohors ergo et tribunus* etc.

In prima igitur parte procedit sic. Ostendit primo *adventum Christi* ad locum cognitum proditori; se-
<!-- page 478 -->
cundo, *adventum proditoris* ad illum locum, ibi[^p478-1]: *Iudas ergo* etc.

2. (Vers. 1.). Continuans igitur dicta dicendis ait: *Haec cum dixisset,* supple: quae praedicta sunt; *Iesus egressus est cum discipulis suis trans torrentem Cedron.* Iste transitus praesignatus fuit secundi Regum decimo quinto[^p478-2]: « Rex quoque transgrediebatur torrentem Cedron ». Genitivus pluralis est *Cedron,* id est trans torrentem cedrorum, quia ibi erant arbores illae. *Egressus,* de civitate, quia extra comprehensus et passus; ad Hebraeos ultimo: « Christus extra portam passus est » etc. *Ubi erat hortus, in quem introivit ipse et discipuli eius,* non tanquam fugiens vel abscondens se, sed quietem quaerens; Canticorum sexto[^p478-3]: « Descendi in hortum meum, ut viderem »; Psalmus: « In pace factus est locus eius »; unde subdit:

3. (Vers. 2.). *Sciebat autem et Iudas, qui tradebat eum, locum.* Et ratio subditur: *Quia frequenter Iesus conveniebat*[^p478-4] *illuc cum discipulis suis.* Victor: « Iudas hunc locum notaverat, eo quod ad illum Dominus saepe venire consueverat ». Locus iste erat in pede montis Oliveti; illuc ibat Iesus frequenter; Lucae vigesimo primo[^p478-5]: « Erat diebus docens in templo, noctibus autem exiens morabatur in monte Oliveti ».

4. (Vers. 3.). *Iudas ergo, cum accepisset cohortem.* Hic notatur *adventus proditoris* cum talibus, qui possent capere et tenere et ducere. Ideo, *cum accepisset cohortem,* a gentilibus scilicet, *et a principibus*[^p478-6] *et Pharisaeis ministros,* quia illis eum tradebat; Matthaei vigesimo sexto dicitur, quod « abiit ad principes sacerdotum et dixit illis: Quid vultis mihi dare, et ego vobis eum tradam »? *Venit illuc,* quasi ipse ductor aliorum, *cum laternis et facibus et armis,* ne posset *latere* in nocte; quia enim in tenebris ambulabant, ideo lumen a facibus mendicabant; Iob vigesimo quarto[^p478-7]: « Si subito apparuerit aurora, arbitrantur umbram mortis; et sic in tenebris quasi in luce ambulant »; vel se *defendere;* et hoc faciebant, quia nihil divinum in eo credebant, sed humanam tantum potentiam.

### Quaestiones.

5. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod dicit Ioannes, quod post orationem *egressus est trans torrentem Cedron;* quia, sicut dicit Augustinus, de Concordia Evangelistarum[^p478-8], finito sermone, *facta est contentio, quis eorum videretur esse maior.*

Et dicendum, quod verbum beati Ioannis non est intelligendum praecise, quod statim post orationem, sed quod post orationem et non ante[^p478-9].

6. Quaest. II. Item, Matthaeus[^p478-10] vigesimo sexto dicit, quod *venit Iesus in villam, quae dicitur Gethsemani.* Lucas vero vigesimo secundo dicit, quod *egressus ibat secundum consuetudinem in montem Olivarum;* Ioannes, quod *trans torrentem Cedron.* — Videtur hic esse contrarietas.

Respondet Augustinus in tertio de Concordia Evangelistarum[^p478-11]: « *Mons,* inquit, *olivarum* iste locus est, cuius nomen Matthaeus et Marcus dixerunt *Gethsemani;* et ibi intelligimus fuisse hortum, quem commemorat Ioannes ». Et Victor[^p478-12] dicit, quod « villa illa sita est in pede montis Oliveti ».

7. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur: quare Dominus egressus est urbem nocte? — Videtur, quod voluerit latitare.

Respondet Chrysostomus[^p478-13], quod hoc non tantum fecit tunc, sed etiam saepe facere consuevit,
<!-- page 479 -->
ut a tumultu hominum se subtraheret et de necessariis loqueretur.

8. Quaest. IV. Item, cum Iudas reliquisset eum domi, quomodo non duxit illuc cohortem, sed ad hortum?

Respondet Chrysostomus[^p479-1], quod libentius eum domi quaereret, ut dormientem inveniret; sed sciebat, eum consuetudinem habere frequenter extra pernoctare.

9. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur: cum cohors illa esset militum gentilium, quomodo sequebatur Iudam?

**Respondeo:** Quod corrupti erant per pecuniam datam, vel promissam. Unde Chysostomus[^p479-2]: « Veri erant milites, pecuniarum gratia omnia facere meditantes ».

10. Quaest. VI. Item quaestio est: quare de die non venerunt ad comprehendendum eum, cum haberent tantam cohortem?

Et respondet Chrysostomus[^p479-3], quod timebant turbas, quae eum de die sequebantur.

## English
<!-- page 477 -->

### Chapter XVIII.

#### Second, there is treatment of the things that accompany the passion, in four ways.

1. *When he had said these things* etc. Above he treated of the things antecedent to the passion, namely from the end of the eleventh chapter[^p477-9]; here he treats of those things in which *the passion is consummated.* And because in the passion he was seized and judged, slain and buried; therefore this part is divided into four parts. In the first there is treatment of Christ's *seizure;* in the second, of his *condemnation,* there[^p477-10]: *And they led him away to Annas;* in the third, of his *passion,* there: *And they took Jesus* etc.; in the fourth, of *the condition of the burial,* at the end of the nineteenth chapter, there: *And after these things he besought Pilate.*

#### First, on the seizure of Christ.

In the first part, then, in which it is manifested of what kind the *seizure* of Christ was, three things are shown: first, that it was *traitorous;* second, that it was *voluntary,* there[^p477-11]: *But Jesus knowing the things that were to come;* third, that it was *ignominious,* there: *Then the band and the tribune* etc.

In the first part, then, he proceeds thus. He shows first *the coming of Christ* to the place known to the traitor; se-
<!-- page 478 -->
cond, *the coming of the traitor* to that place, there[^p478-1]: *Judas therefore* etc.

2. (Vers. 1.). Continuing, then, the things said with the things to be said, he says: *When he had said these things,* supply: which were said before; *Jesus went forth with his disciples over the brook Cedron.* This crossing was foreshown in the second of Kings, the fifteenth chapter[^p478-2]: « The king also passed over the brook Cedron ». *Cedron* is a genitive plural, that is, over the brook of the cedars, because those trees were there. *He went forth,* from the city, because he was seized and suffered outside; to the Hebrews, the last chapter: « Christ suffered without the gate » etc. *Where there was a garden, into which he entered, and his disciples,* not as fleeing or hiding himself, but seeking quiet; Canticles, the sixth chapter[^p478-3]: « I went down into my garden, to see »; the Psalm: « In peace is his place made »; hence he subjoins:

3. (Vers. 2.). *And Judas also, who betrayed him, knew the place.* And the reason is subjoined: *Because Jesus used often to resort*[^p478-4] *thither with his disciples.* Victor: « Judas had noted this place, because the Lord had been accustomed often to come to it ». This place was at the foot of the mount of Olivet; thither Jesus used to go often; Luke, the twenty-first chapter[^p478-5]: « He was in the days teaching in the temple, but in the nights going out he abode in the mount of Olivet ».

4. (Vers. 3.). *Judas therefore having received a band.* Here is noted *the coming of the traitor* with such as could take and hold and lead. Therefore, *having received a band,* namely from the Gentiles, *and servants from the princes*[^p478-6] *and the Pharisees,* because he was betraying him to them; in Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter, it is said that he « went to the princes of the priests and said to them: What will you give me, and I will deliver him unto you »? *He cometh thither,* as himself the leader of the others, *with lanterns and torches and weapons,* lest he should be able *to lie hid* in the night; for because they were walking in darkness, therefore they begged lumen[^tr-lumen] from the torches; Job, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p478-7]: « If the morning suddenly appear, they deem it the shadow of death; and so they walk in darkness as in light »; or lest he should be able *to defend* himself; and this they did because they believed nothing divine in him, but only human power.

### Questions.

5. Quaest. I. But it is asked here first about this, that John says that after the prayer *he went forth over the brook Cedron;* because, as Augustine says, *On the Agreement of the Evangelists*[^p478-8], when the discourse was finished, *a strife arose, which of them should seem to be the greater.*

And it must be said that the word of blessed John is not to be understood precisely, that immediately after the prayer, but that after the prayer and not before[^p478-9].

6. Quaest. II. Likewise, Matthew in the twenty-sixth chapter[^p478-10] says that *Jesus came into a country place which is called Gethsemani.* But Luke in the twenty-second chapter says that *going out, he went according to his custom to the mount of Olives;* John, that *over the brook Cedron.* — There seems to be a contrariety here.

Augustine responds in the third book *On the Agreement of the Evangelists*[^p478-11]: « *The mount,* he says, *of Olives* is this place, whose name Matthew and Mark called *Gethsemani;* and there we understand to have been the garden which John records ». And Victor[^p478-12] says that « that country place is situated at the foot of the mount of Olivet ».

7. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked: why did the Lord go forth from the city by night? — It seems that he wished to lie hid.

Chrysostom responds[^p478-13] that he did this not only then, but was also accustomed to do it often,
<!-- page 479 -->
that he might withdraw himself from the tumult of men and speak of necessary things.

8. Quaest. IV. Likewise, since Judas had left him at home, how is it that he did not lead the band thither, but to the garden?

Chrysostom responds[^p479-1] that he would more gladly have sought him at home, to find him sleeping; but he knew that he had the custom of often passing the night outside.

9. Quaest. V. Likewise it is asked: since that band was of gentile soldiers, how did it follow Judas?

**I respond:** That they were corrupted by money given, or promised. Hence Chysostom [*so printed*][^p479-2]: « They were true soldiers, intent on doing all things for the sake of money ».

10. Quaest. VI. Likewise the question is: why did they not come by day to seize him, since they had so great a band?

And Chrysostom responds[^p479-3] that they feared the multitudes, which followed him by day.

[^tr-doctrina]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin *doctrina* holds together what English divides among doctrine, teaching and learning. Here it stands once, in p. 478 n. 13, where Chrysostom says the Lord sought out empty places *ne auditores a doctrina interpellarentur*: what is interrupted is a teaching being given and heard (the Greek behind it speaks of the hearing, τῆς ἀκροάσεως), not a body of propositions, so "teaching" is the English.

[^tr-lumen]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin *lumen* is left standing here, against *lux*, which this translation renders "light". *Lux* is light at its source and *lumen* light as given off and received. This pericope's one *lumen* is Bonaventure's own prose in ¶ 4, *lumen a facibus mendicabant*, the light borrowed from the torches by those who walked in darkness; the verse adduced at once for it, Job 24:17, has *lux* (*in tenebris quasi in luce ambulant*), and keeps "light". The body leans on the difference, so the English keeps both words.
