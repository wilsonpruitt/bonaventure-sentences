# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. III, Vers. 17-21
## *Supra determinavit, quae sit salutis via, et quod salutis principium; hic tertio iam determinat, quod est salutis impedimentum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 283 -->

30. *Non enim misit Deus.* Supra determinavit, quae sit salutis via, et quod salutis principium; hic tertio iam determinat, quod est *salutis impedimentum.* Et primo ostendit, quod impedimentum nostrae salutis *non est ex parte Dei;* secundo, quod *est ex parte defectus fidei;* tertio vero, quod *est ex parte defectus operis boni.*

(Vers. 17.). Ostendit igitur primo, quod *non est ex parte Dei* nostra damnatio, cum dicit: *Non enim misit* etc.; et debet ita continuari ad praecedens: vere dixi, quod Deus dedit Filium, *ut omnis, qui credit in eum, habeat vitam aeternam,* non, inquam, ut damnetur. *Non enim misit Deus Filium suum in mundum, ut iudicet mundum,* scilicet ipsum condemnando, *sed ut salvetur mundus per ipsum;* primae Ioannis tertio[^p283-9]: « In hoc cognovimus caritatem Dei, quoniam ipse prior dilexit nos et misit Filium suum propitiationem pro peccatis nostris », ideo ad propitiandum, non ad damnandum.

31. (Vers. 18.). *Qui credit in eum non iudicatur.* Tangitur hic secundum, quod damnatio et iudicium venit *ex parte defectus fidei.* Et hoc ostendit per hoc, quod qui credit non iudicabitur, sed qui non credit iam est iudicatus. Propter hoc dicit: *Qui credit in eum,* scilicet in Dei Filium, *non iudicatur;* infra quinto[^p283-10]: « In iudicium non veniet, sed transiet de morte ad vitam ». *Qui autem non credit iam iudicatus est,* id est, pro damnato
<!-- page 284 -->
habetur; *quia non credit in nomine unigeniti Filii Dei;* quia, sicut dictum est Actuum quarto[^p284-1], « non est aliud nomen sub caelo datum hominibus, in quo oporteat nos salvos fieri ». Et ideo qui in eius nomine non credit merito est damnatus.

32. (Vers. 19.). *Et hoc est iudicium*[^p284-2] etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet quod impedimentum salutis et ratio damnationis et iudicii est *ex parte defectus operis boni,* ratione cuius homo refugit salutem. Propterea dicit: *Et hoc est iudicium;* Glossa[^p284-3]: « Id est causa damnationis »; *quia lux venit in mundum,* id est Verbum incarnatum; infra duodecimo: « Ego lux in mundum veni, ut qui credit in me in tenebris non maneat ». *Et dilexerunt homines magis tenebras,* scilicet erroris, *quam lueem,* veritatis; Iob vigesimo quarto: « Ipsi fuerunt rebelles lumini ». Et ratio huius caecitatis est defectus operis boni; propter quod dicit: *Erant enim eorum mala opera.* Et quod malum opus sit ratio fugiendi lucem, ostendit.

33. (Vers. 20.). *Omnis enim, qui male agit, odit lucem;* Iob vigesimo quarto[^p284-4]: « Oculus adulteri observat caliginem ». *Et non venit ad lucem, ut non arguantur opera eius,* id est, manifestentur esse mala; ad Ephesios quinto: « Nolite communicare operibus infructuosis tenebrarum, magis autem illa redarguite; omnia autem, quae arguuntur, a lumine manifestantur ». Sed e contrario bonum opus ducit ad lucem. Ideo dicit:

34. (Vers. 21.). *Qui autem facit veritatem venit ad lucem;* Matthaei quinto[^p284-5]: « Nemo abscondit lucernam sub modio, sed super candelabrum »; quia bona opera lux sunt. Unde et subdit: *Ut manifestentur opera eius, quia in Deo sunt facta.* In Deo fiunt, quae in caritate; primae Ioannis quarto[^p284-6]: « Qui manet in caritate in Deo manet, et Deus in eo », qui est summa lux, et ideo ad eius honorem sunt revelanda; Matthaei quinto: « Sic luceat lux vestra coram hominibus, ut videant opera vestra bona et glorificent Patrem vestrum, qui in caelis est ».

### Quaestiones.

35. Quaest. I. Sed incidit hic quaestio primo de hoc quod dicit, quod *non misit Filium suum in mundum, ut iudicet.* — *Contra:* Habetur infra quinto[^p284-7]: *Pater omne iudicium dedit Filio.*

**Respondeo:** Est duplex adventus: *primus* et *secundus,* primus *misericordiae,* secundus *iustitiae.* In *primo* venit ad propitiandum, non ad iudicandum, sed in *secundo* venturus est ad iudicandum.

36. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Qui non credit iam iudicatus est.*

**Contra:** Si *Deus non iudicat bis in id ipsum*[^p284-8]; et infideles iam sunt iudicati: ergo in die iudicii non iudicabuntur. — *Si dicas,* quod iudicati sunt merito; similiter mali Christiani.

**Respondeo:** Est iudicium *praesentis punitionis;* et hoc omnes mali iudicantur — quia « non est dedecus peccati sine decore iustitiae[^p284-9] » — maxime infideles, quia infidelitas magna poena est sibi. Est iudicium *extremae discussionis,* et hoc non iudicabuntur infideles. Et est iudicium *futurae retributionis.* Primo iudicantur omnes mali; secundo, mediocriter boni et mali; ultimo, omnes boni et omnes mali. Dicitur ergo infidelis *iam iudicatus* vel propter excaecationem, vel quia non iudicabitur per discussionem, ut dicit Gregorius[^p284-10].

37. Quaest. III. Item obiicitur de hoc quod dicit: *Omnis, qui male agit, odit lucem.*

**Contra:** « Omnes homines naturaliter desiderant *scire*[^p284-11] », sicut et *beati esse;* sed malitia operis non inducit odium beatitudinis: ergo nec lucis.

**Respondeo:** dicendum, quod lux dupliciter potest considerari: aut ratione effectus *primi,* secundum quod est principium cognitionis, et sic a nullo oditur; aut ratione effectus *secundi,* qui est turpitudinem manifestare, quod quia omnes mali abhorrent, ideo et lucem quantum ad hunc effectum. Sed *beatitudo* non habet effectum in anima contrarium alicui naturali desiderio, ut ostendit Augustinus[^p284-12].

38. Quaest. IV. Item[^p284-13], si *qui male agit non
<!-- page 285 -->
venit ad lucem:* ergo nullus unquam peccator convertitur ad fidem.

Respondendum, quod quamdiu diligit manere in malo opere, ad lucem non venit; sed si incipiat malum odire, tunc potest ad lucem venire; et sic veniunt peccatores per contritionem.

## English
<!-- page 283 -->

30. *For God sent not.* Above he determined what is the way of salvation, and what the principle of salvation; here, third, he now determines what is *the impediment of salvation.* And first he shows that the impediment of our salvation *is not on the part of God;* second, that it *is on the part of a defect of faith;* but third, that it *is on the part of a defect of good work.*

(Verse 17.). He shows, therefore, first, that our damnation *is not on the part of God,* when he says: *For God sent not* etc.; and it ought to be joined to what precedes in this way: truly I said that God gave his Son, *that whosoever believeth in him may have life everlasting,* not, I say, that he may be damned. *For God sent not his Son into the world, to judge the world,* namely by condemning it, *but that the world may be saved by him;* the first of John, the third chapter[^p283-9]: « In this we have known the charity of God, because he first loved us and sent his Son a propitiation for our sins », therefore to propitiate, not to damn.

31. (Verse 18.). *He that believeth in him is not judged.* Here the second thing is touched on, that damnation and judgement come *on the part of a defect of faith.* And he shows this by this, that he who believes will not be judged, but he who does not believe is already judged. On account of this he says: *He that believeth in him,* namely in the Son of God, *is not judged;* below, the fifth chapter[^p283-10]: « He shall not come into judgement, but shall pass from death to life ». *But he that doth not believe is already judged,* that is, he is held as one
<!-- page 284 -->
damned; *because he believeth not in the name of the only begotten Son of God;* because, as is said in the Acts, the fourth chapter[^p284-1], « there is no other name under heaven given to men, whereby we must be saved ». And therefore he who does not believe in his name is deservedly damned.

32. (Verse 19.). *And this is the judgement*[^p284-2] etc. Here the third thing is touched on, namely that the impediment of salvation and the reason of damnation and of judgement is *on the part of a defect of good work,* by reason of which a man flees from salvation. Therefore he says: *And this is the judgement;* the Gloss[^p284-3]: « That is, the cause of damnation »; *because the light is come into the world,* that is, the Word incarnate; below, the twelfth chapter: « I am come a light into the world, that he who believeth in me may not remain in darkness ». *And men loved darkness rather,* namely of error, *than the light* [so printed: *lueem* for *lucem*], of truth; Job, the twenty-fourth chapter: « They have been rebellious to the light[^tr-lumen] ». And the reason of this blindness is the defect of good work; on account of which he says: *For their works were evil.* And that evil work is the reason for fleeing the light, he shows.

33. (Verse 20.). *For every one that doth evil hateth the light;* Job, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p284-4]: « The eye of the adulterer observeth darkness ». *And cometh not to the light, that his works may not be reproved,* that is, made manifest to be evil; to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter: « Have no fellowship with the unfruitful works of darkness, but rather reprove them; but all things that are reproved are made manifest by the light ». But on the contrary good work leads to the light. Therefore he says:

34. (Verse 21.). *But he that doth truth cometh to the light;* Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p284-5]: « No man hideth a candle under a bushel, but upon a candlestick »; because good works are light. Whence he also adds: *That his works may be made manifest, because they are done in God.* Those things are done in God which are done in charity; the first of John, the fourth chapter[^p284-6]: « He that abideth in charity abideth in God, and God in him », who is the highest light, and therefore they are to be revealed to his honour; Matthew, the fifth chapter: « So let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works and glorify your Father who is in heaven ».

### Questions.

35. Question I. But a question arises here first about this that he says, that *he sent not his Son into the world, to judge.* — *On the contrary:* It is had below, in the fifth chapter[^p284-7]: *The Father hath given all judgement to the Son.*

**I respond:** There is a twofold coming: the *first* and the *second,* the first of *mercy,* the second of *justice.* In the *first* he came to propitiate, not to judge, but in the *second* he is to come to judge.

36. Question II. Likewise it is asked about this that he says: *He that doth not believe is already judged.*

**On the contrary:** If *God does not judge twice in the same thing*[^p284-8], and unbelievers are already judged: therefore on the day of judgement they will not be judged. — *If thou sayest* that they are judged deservedly; in like manner evil Christians.

**I respond:** There is a judgement *of present punishment;* and by this all evil men are judged — because « there is no disgrace of sin without the comeliness of justice[^p284-9] » — unbelievers most of all, because unbelief is a great punishment to itself. There is a judgement *of final examination,* and by this unbelievers will not be judged. And there is a judgement *of future retribution.* By the first all evil men are judged; by the second, the middling good and the evil; by the last, all the good and all the evil. The unbeliever is therefore said to be *already judged* either on account of blinding, or because he will not be judged through examination, as Gregory says[^p284-10].

37. Question III. Likewise it is objected about this that he says: *Every one that doth evil hateth the light.*

**On the contrary:** « All men by nature desire *to know*[^p284-11] », just as also *to be blessed;* but the malice of a work does not induce hatred of beatitude: therefore neither of light.

**I respond:** It must be said that light can be considered in two ways: either by reason of its *first* effect, according as it is the principle of knowledge, and in this way it is hated by no one; or by reason of its *second* effect, which is to make turpitude manifest, which because all evil men abhor, therefore they abhor the light also as to this effect. But *beatitude* does not have an effect in the soul contrary to any natural desire, as Augustine shows[^p284-12].

38. Question IV. Likewise[^p284-13], if *he that doth evil cometh not
<!-- page 285 -->
to the light:* therefore no sinner is ever converted to the faith.

It must be responded that as long as he loves to remain in evil work, he does not come to the light; but if he begins to hate evil, then he can come to the light; and in this way sinners come through contrition.

[^tr-lumen]: **En.** Translator's note: this translation keeps *lux* as "light" and *lumen* as "lumen", except where a verse is quoted as a verse and no surrounding prose leans on the word.
