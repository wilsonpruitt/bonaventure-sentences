# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. I, Vers. 43-51
## *Prima manifestatio, quae est facta discipulis*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 265 -->

#### De manifestatione Verbi per se ipsum.

89. *In crastinum* etc. Ista pars, quae incipit ab illo loco[^p265-3]: *Hoc est testimonium Ioannis* etc., in qua agitur de Verbi manifestatione, divisa fuit in duas partes. In prima agitur de manifestatione Verbi facta per vocem Ioannis usque ad locum istum. Deinceps vero usque ad finem undecimi[^p265-4] agitur de manifestatione facta *per ipsum Verbum.* Et quia manifestatio respectum habet ad eos quibus fit, et ad eum, de quo fit; ideo haec pars habet duas partes. In prima agitur de manifestatione, secundum quod respicit conditiones a parte eorum *quibus fit.* In secunda agitur de manifestatione, secundum quod respicit conditiones a parte *Verbi manifestati;* et incipit illa pars in principio quinti capituli[^p265-5]: *Post haec erat dies.*

#### Primo, de hac manifestatione quoad conditiones a parte eorum quibus fit.

Prima pars habet quatuor partes secundum quatuor genera personarum, quibus se manifestat. In prima manifestat se suis *discipulis.* In secunda parte manifestat se *Iudaeis,* in medio secundi capituli[^p265-6]: *Post haec descendit* etc. In tertia manifestat se *Samaritanis,* in principio quarti capituli: *Ut ergo cognovit Iesus* etc. In quarta parte manifestat se *Galilaeis,* circa finem quarti capituli, ibi: *Post duos dies exiit.*

#### Prima manifestatio, quae est facta discipulis.

Prima pars, in qua manifestat se suis *discipulis,* habet duas: in prima agitur de discipulorum *vocatione;* in secunda, de *manifestatione* facta discipulis vocatis, ibi[^p265-7]: *Et die tertio nuptiae factae sunt.*

*Vocatio* igitur discipulorum a Domino facta describitur hoc ordine. Primo notatur *vocatio simplicis;* secundo, *vocatio sapientis;* tertio, *vocati attractio;* quarto, *attracti confirmatio.* — *Vocatio* ergo *simplicis,* scilicet Philippi, primo facta a Domino describitur, quia ab ipso Domino de Galilaea vocatus est. Propterea dicit:

90. (Vers. 43.). *In crastinum,* scilicet post conversionem Andreae et Petri, *voluit exire in Galilaeam et invenit Philippum,* non casualiter, sed ex proposito invenitur quod perditum est. Sic Dominus discipulos suos invenit prius perditos, quia a perditione eripuit; Lucae decimo quinto[^p265-8]: « Mulier, quae perdidit drachmam unam, quaerit diligenter, donec inveniat ». *Et dicit ei: Sequere me,* tanquam servus dominum, tanquam discipulus magistrum, ut
<!-- page 266 -->
possis consequi; ad Philippenses tertio[^p266-1]: « Sequor autem, si quo modo comprehendam, in quo et comprehensus sum ». Et tangit Evangelista locum, unde erat Philippus oriundus, ad maiorem manifestationem. Propterea dicit:

91. (Vers. 44.). *Erat autem Philippus a Bethsaida, civitate Andreae et Petri.* Ed ideo intelligendum, quod motus est sequi, quia vidit illos sequentes. Cortina enim cortinam trahit, secundum quod Exodi vigesimo sexto[^p266-2] in compositione tabernaculi signatum est. — *Moraliter* vero *Philippus* os lampadis interpretatur, *Bethsaida* domus venatorum[^p266-3]. Per *os* sapientia notatur eloquentiae, de qua Lucae vigesimo primo[^p266-4]: « Ego dabo vobis os et sapientiam, cui non poterunt resistere et contradicere omnes adversarii vestri ». Per *lampadem,* honestas conversationis, Matthaei quinto: « Luceat lux vestra coram hominibus, ut videant » etc. Per *venatores,* praedicatores; Ieremiae decimo sexto: « Mittam eis venatores multos, et venabuntur eos ». In vocatione igitur Philippi de Bethsaida significatur, quod qui assumit officium praedicationis debet habere sapientiam eloquentiae et honestatem vitae, « quia, cuius vita despicitur, restat, ut eius praedicatio contemnatur[^p266-5] ».

92. (Vers. 45.). *Invenit Philippus* etc. Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *vocatio sapientis,* quae facta est mediante simplici. Propterea dicit: *Invenit Philippus Nathanael.* Sicut ipse inventus fuerat a Christo, sic et Nathanaelem invenit; Beda[^p266-6]: « Vide venatorem istum, quantum capiendis animabus esset intentus ». Unde et sequitur: *Et dicit ei: Quem scripsit Moyses in Lege, et Prophetae;* supple: scripserunt; aliqui libri[^p266-7] habent *et Prophetis,* et debet suppleri littera: *Quem scripsit Moyses in Lege, et Prophetis:* scriptum est. *Invenimus Iesum, filium Ioseph a Nazareth.* Iste erat Christus, qui praenuntiatus fuit a Lege et Prophetis; Deuteronomii decimo octavo[^p266-8]: « Prophetam suscitabit tibi Dominus de gente tua » etc.; Isaiae septimo: « Ecce, Virgo concipiet et pariet filium » etc. Hunc dicebat *filium Ioseph, reputatione;* Lucae tertio: « Ipse Iesus erat incipiens quasi annorum triginta, ut putabatur filius Ioseph ». Et hoc quidem dixit Nathanaeli, tanquam ei qui peritus erat in Lege et Prophetis. Et hoc patet ex ipsius responsione.

93. (Vers. 46.). *Et dixit ei Nathanael: A Nazareth potest aliquid boni esse. Dicit ei Philippus: Veni et vide.* Ista littera legitur *remissive*[^p266-9], ut sit sensus, quod aliquid potest esse boni a Nazareth, propter illud quod scribitur Matthaei secundo[^p266-10]: « Quod scriptum est per Prophetam, quoniam Nazaraeus vocabitur », et accipitur de Isaiae undecimo, secundum Hebraicam veritatem; ubi nos habemus: « Flos de radice eius ascendet », ipsi habent: « Nazaraeus ». Ideo hoc intelligens Nathanael tanquam peritus asseruit. — Aliter legitur *interrogative,* quasi dubitans quaerat: *A Nazareth potest aliquid boni esse?* scilicet tantum bonum, quia — sicut scribitur Michaeae quinto[^p266-11]: « Et tu Bethlehem Ephrata, parvulus » etc. — de Bethlehem nasciturus erat Christus. Ideo Iudaei dixerunt infra septimo: « Nunquid a Galilaea venit Christus » ? Sive legatur *remissive,* sive *interrogative,* asserit hic Philippus et hortatur, ut veniat. Propter quod sequitur: *Dicit ei Philippus: Veni et vide;* Chrysostomus[^p266-12]: « Philippus divitias, quas invenit, per verba demonstrare non potens, ad inventum ducit thesaurum fratrem suum ». Et intelligendum, quod secutus est Philippum.

94. (Vers. 47.). *Vidit Iesus Nathanael* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *attractio vocati.* Attrahit per *commendationem* et per *secretorum revelationem.* Per *commendationem;* unde dicit: *Vidit Iesus Nathanael venientem ad se,* scilicet per admonitionem Philippi simplicis; *et dicit de eo: Ecce, vere Israelita, in quo dolus non est.* Ideo dolus non est, quia, cum sit sapiens, non erubuit sequi simplicem; quia quod credebat in corde, hoc
<!-- page 267 -->
protulit ore, quia non veniebat voluntate tentandi, sed studio proficiendi; quocontra Proverbiorum duodecimo[^p267-1]: « Dolus in corde cogitantium mala ». Sed Nathanael non credit commendationi suae, nisi super hoc audiat *certitudinem,* quia non debet homo esse pronus credere de se bona. Ideo

95. (Vers. 48.). *Dicit ei Nathanael: Unde me nosti?* Quid me sic commendas? Et Dominus attrahit per *secretorum revelationem;* ideo sequitur: *Respondit Iesus et dixit ei: Priusquam te Philippus vocaret, cum esses sub ficu, vidi te. Ad litteram:* ipse in loco secreto, ut in horto, erat, unde eum Philippus vocavit. Vel: *ficus* dicitur consuetudo peccandi, ut primi parentes sub fici foliis se texerunt, Genesis tertio[^p267-2]; ubi est prius visus a Domino et quaesitus, quam ipse Dominum quaereret. Hac revelatione Nathanael attractus est; unde sequitur eius fidelis confessio.

96. (Vers. 49.). *Respondit Nathanael et dixit ei: Rabbi, tu es Filius Dei,* quantum ad Divinitatem, quia nosti secreta cordis; Ieremiae decimo septimo[^p267-3]: « Ego Dominus scrutans corda et renes ». *Tu es rex Israel,* quantum ad humanitatem. Similiter confessa est turba, infra duodecimo: « Benedictus, qui venit in nomine Domini, rex Israel ».

97. (Vers. 50.). *Respondit Iesus* etc. Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet iam *attracti confirmatio;* confirmat enim eum per promissionem ostensionis maiorum; propter quod dicit: *Respondit Iesus et dixit ei: Quia dixi tibi: Vidi te sub ficu, credis;* et ita pro modico es tractus ad fidem. *Maius his videbis;* infra quinto[^p267-4]: « Maiora his demonstrabit ei opera, ut vos miremini ». Et specificat illud *maius.*

98. (Vers. 51.). *Et dicit ei: Amen, amen dico vobis, quia*[^p267-5] *videbitis caelum apertum,* secundum quod dicitur Matthaei tertio, quod Ioannes vidit, quod caeli aperti sunt super eum. *Et Angelos Dei ascendentes et descendentes super Filium hominis. Ad litteram* Angeli ad ipsum descenderunt, secundum quod dicitur Matthaei quarto[^p267-6]: « Accesserunt Angeli et ministrabant ei »; *spiritualiter* vero, ut dicit Augustinus, quod dictum est Nathanaeli modo impletur in Christianis, quia, postquam Deus homo ascendit, credentibus in eum aperitur aditus caeli, et vident Angelos, id est praedicatores, *ascendentes,* dum arcana Deitatis considerant; *et descendentes,* dum humana de eo praedicant. Ista visio signata est Genesis vigesimo octavo[^p267-7], ubi dicitur, quod Iacob vidit Dominum innixum scalae et Angelos ascendentes et descendentes.

### Quaestiones.

99. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic de adventu Domini in Galilaeam: quare dicitur hic, quod *in crastinum,* post testimonium Ioannis, venit in Galilaeam?

**Contra:** 1. Alii Evangelistae dicunt, quod statim *ductus est in desertum a Spiritu,* Matthaei quarto[^p267-8] et Marci primo.

2. Item ibidem[^p267-9] dicitur, quod *Iesus venit in Galilaeam, postquam Ioannes traditus est;* sed constat, quod adhuc non erat traditus, sicut dicitur infra tertio, quod Ioannes longo post tempore baptizavit.

Ad hoc respondet Augustinus in libro de Concordia Evangelistarum secundo[^p267-10], quod duplex fuit adventus Domini in Galilaeam. Primus fuit post testimonium Ioannis, antequam missus fuit in carcerem; et de hoc omnes alii Evangelistae tacent. Secundus vero fuit, postquam Ioannes missus est in carcerem, et de hoc dicitur infra quarto capitulo[^p267-11] In primo adventu fecit miraculum de mutatione aquae in vinum in nuptiis, in secundo adventu miraculum de filio reguli.

1. Quod ergo dicitur, quod ductus est in desertum; intelligendum est, quod testimonium illud fuit datum post reditum Domini de deserto.

100. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de *vocatione* discipulorum, quia dubitatur similiter.

1. Dicitur enim hic, quod vocati fuerunt, antequam Ioannes traderetur. Matthaeus[^p267-12] vero dicit, quod post traditionem Ioannis vocati fuerunt.
<!-- page 268 -->

2. Item Matthaeus dicit, quod vocavit Petrum et Andream *de navi,* ut dicitur Matthaei quarto[^p268-1]; hic dictum est, quod ad testimonium Ioannis venerunt ad eum, primo Andreas, deinde Petrus.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p268-2], quod Ioannes servat ordinem historiae de *tempore* vocationis, sed Matthaeus illud dicit per *recapitulationem.*

2. Quod obiicitur de *modo* vocationis, dicendum ad hoc, quod discipuli Domini dupliciter sunt vocati: uno modo ad fidem, alio modo ad perfectionem. Primo modo vocati fuerunt[^p268-3] Petrus et Andreas, ut Dominum cognoscerent et in eum crederent testimonio Ioannis. Secundo modo vocati sunt de navibus, ut omnia relinquerent et Christum sequerentur. De prima loquitur hic Ioannes, de secunda vero loquitur Matthaeus[^p268-4].

101. Quaest. III. Item, de *loco* vocationis: quia Dominus vocavit discipulos de Galilaea, quaerit Chrysostomus[^p268-5], cum Iudaei essent peritiores in Lege; unde est hoc, quod de Ierosolymis non elegit? — Et iuxta hoc quaeritur: quare vocavit Nathanaelem per Philippum medium? — Et iterum, cum Nathanael esset persona tam sancta, ut Dominus dicit, et etiam in Lege peritissimus; talem Dominus videtur debuisse constituere in Apostolum. — *Si dicas,* quod voluit eligere simplices: ergo si « omnis Christi actio nostra est instructio[^p268-6] », videtur, quod nos debemus eligere praelatos simplices et idiotas.

Ad hoc docet nos respondere Apostolus primae ad Corinthios primo[^p268-7]: *Non multi sapientes secundum carnem, non multi potentes, non multi nobiles, sed quae stulta sunt mundi elegit Deus, et ea quae non sunt.* Et ratio huius est, ut confundat alta mundi, et *ut non glorietur in conspectu eius omnis caro,* et ut ostendatur, totum, quod factum est per Apostolos, esse virtutis divinae, non humanae prudentiae. Hinc est, quod Galilaeos elegit Apostolos et piscatores et rusticos simplices; quia, sicut dicit Chrysostomus[^p268-8], Galilaea habet incolas rusticanos. Hinc etiam est, quod per se vocavit Philippum simplicem et per illum sapientem fratrem[^p268-9]. Hinc est etiam, quod Nathanaelem, magnum doctorem in Lege, non fecit Apostolum[^p268-10]. Hinc est, quod Paulum non inter primos Apostolos vocavit; sed *novissime omnium tanquam abortivo visus est et ei*[^p268-11], ut humiliaretur.

Quod ergo obiicis, quod nos deberemus eligere simplices; respondendum, quod Dominus elegit simplices, ut faceret sapientes; sed quia non est in potestate nostra facere sapientes, ideo sapientes factos debemus nobis praeficere.

102. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de obedientia Apostolorum, quia statim secuti, nullis visis signis. — Videtur, quod stulte moti fuerunt, sicut dicitur hic de Philippo et de aliis; unde haeretici[^p268-12] reprehendunt Matthaeum.

Responderi potest, quod illuminati fuerunt interius; vel etiam dicendum, quod exterius fuerunt inducti, quidam *testimonio,* ut Andreas et eius socius; quidam *miraculo,* ut dicitur de Petro Lucae quinto[^p268-13]; quidam *exemplo,* et sic Philippus, quia vidit Petrum et Andream compatriotas suos sequentes, quos noverat esse bonos.

103. Quaest. V. Item quaerit Beda[^p268-14]: cum Nathanael vocet Christum *Filium Dei,* unde est hoc, quod ipse se vocat *filium hominis?*

Respondet Beda[^p268-15], quod « iusto dispensationis moderamine hoc actum est, ut, cum utraque natura commemoratur, ipse suam humilitatem, ille eius altitudinem fateatur »; ut per hoc innotescat utraque natura in Christo, et exemplum humilitatis donetur, unde homo semper in se humilia recognoscat et dicat. Quod maxime est contra eos qui habentes aliquos consanguineos nobiles, cum quaeritur de origine, non respondent nomen patris, sed
<!-- page 269 -->
avunculi, vel alicuius famosi consanguinei. Dominus noster Iesus Christus, cum sit nobilis ex parte Patris, sed infimus ex parte Matris, vocat se *filium hominis* saepe, raro se *filium Dei,* nisi necessitate compulsus. Et nota ad differentiam aliorum, quod alii dicuntur filii hominum, qui sunt ex commixtione seminum generati; Christus vero unicum solum habuit parentem, et ideo recte filium hominis se appellat[^p269-1].

## English

#### On the manifestation of the Word through himself.

89. *On the following day* etc. This part, which begins from that place[^p265-3]: *This is the testimony of John* etc., in which the manifestation of the Word is treated, was divided into two parts. In the first is treated the manifestation of the Word made through the voice of John, up to this place. From here on, however, up to the end of the eleventh[^p265-4], is treated the manifestation made *through the Word himself.* And because manifestation has a relation to those to whom it is made, and to him of whom it is made; therefore this part has two parts. In the first is treated the manifestation insofar as it regards the conditions on the part of those *to whom it is made.* In the second is treated the manifestation insofar as it regards the conditions on the part of *the Word manifested;* and that part begins at the beginning of the fifth chapter[^p265-5]: *After these things was a day.*

#### First, on this manifestation as to the conditions on the part of those to whom it is made.

The first part has four parts, according to the four kinds of persons to whom he manifests himself. In the first he manifests himself to his *disciples.* In the second part he manifests himself to the *Jews,* in the middle of the second chapter[^p265-6]: *After this he went down* etc. In the third he manifests himself to the *Samaritans,* at the beginning of the fourth chapter: *When Jesus therefore understood* etc. In the fourth part he manifests himself to the *Galileans,* about the end of the fourth chapter, there: *After two days he departed.*

#### The first manifestation, which was made to the disciples.

The first part, in which he manifests himself to his *disciples,* has two: in the first is treated the *calling* of the disciples; in the second, the *manifestation* made to the disciples once called, there[^p265-7]: *And the third day there was a marriage.*

The *calling,* then, of the disciples, made by the Lord, is described in this order. First is noted the *calling of the simple man;* second, the *calling of the wise man;* third, the *drawing of the one called;* fourth, the *confirmation of the one drawn.* — The *calling,* then, *of the simple man,* namely of Philip, made first by the Lord, is described, because he was called by the Lord himself from Galilee. On this account he says:

90. (Vers. 43.). *On the following day,* namely after the conversion of Andrew and Peter, *he would go forth into Galilee, and he findeth Philip,* not by chance, but on purpose is that found which was lost. So the Lord finds his disciples, who were before lost, because he snatched them from perdition; Luke, the fifteenth[^p265-8]: « A woman who hath lost one groat seeketh diligently until she find it ». *And he saith to him: Follow me,* as a servant his lord, as a disciple his teacher, that
<!-- page 266 -->
thou mayest attain; to the Philippians, the third[^p266-1]: « But I follow after, if I may by any means apprehend, wherein I am also apprehended ». And the Evangelist touches on the place whence Philip came, for the greater manifestation. On this account he says:

91. (Vers. 44.). *Now Philip was of Bethsaida, the city of Andrew and Peter.* And therefore it is to be understood that he was moved to follow because he saw them following. For curtain draws curtain, according as is signified in Exodus, the twenty-sixth[^p266-2], in the making of the tabernacle. — *Morally,* however, *Philip* is interpreted mouth of a lamp, *Bethsaida* house of hunters[^p266-3]. By the *mouth* is denoted the wisdom of eloquence, of which Luke, the twenty-first[^p266-4]: « I will give you a mouth and wisdom, which all your adversaries shall not be able to resist and gainsay ». By the *lamp,* honesty of conduct, Matthew, the fifth: « Let your light shine before men, that they may see » etc. By the *hunters,* preachers; Jeremiah, the sixteenth: « I will send them many hunters, and they shall hunt them ». In the calling, then, of Philip from Bethsaida it is signified that he who takes up the office of preaching ought to have the wisdom of eloquence and the honesty of life, « because, when one's life is despised, it remains that his preaching be contemned[^p266-5] ».

92. (Vers. 45.). *Philip findeth* etc. Here is touched the second, namely the *calling of the wise man,* which was made through the mediation of the simple man. On this account he says: *Philip findeth Nathanael.* As he himself had been found by Christ, so too he finds Nathanael; Bede[^p266-6]: « See this hunter, how intent he was on catching souls ». Whence it also follows: *And he saith to him: Him of whom Moses in the Law, and the Prophets;* supply: wrote; some books[^p266-7] have *and in the Prophets,* and the letter ought to be supplied: *Of whom Moses in the Law, and in the Prophets:* it is written. *We have found Jesus the son of Joseph of Nazareth.* This was the Christ, who was foretold by the Law and the Prophets; Deuteronomy, the eighteenth[^p266-8]: « The Lord will raise up to thee a Prophet of thy nation » etc.; Isaiah, the seventh: « Behold, a Virgin shall conceive and bear a son » etc. He called him *the son of Joseph, by repute;* Luke, the third: « Jesus himself was beginning about the age of thirty years, as it was supposed the son of Joseph ». And this indeed he said to Nathanael as to one who was skilled in the Law and the Prophets. And this is plain from his response.

93. (Vers. 46.). *And Nathanael said to him: From Nazareth there can be something of good. Philip saith to him: Come and see.* This letter is read *remissively*[^p266-9], so that the sense is that something of good can be from Nazareth, on account of that which is written in Matthew, the second[^p266-10]: « What was written by the Prophet, that he shall be called a Nazarite », and it is taken from Isaiah, the eleventh, according to the Hebrew truth; where we have: « A flower shall rise up out of his root », they have: « Nazarite ». Therefore Nathanael, understanding this, as one skilled, asserted it. — Otherwise it is read *interrogatively,* as if, doubting, he asks: *Can anything of good be from Nazareth?* namely so great a good, because — as is written in Micah, the fifth[^p266-11]: « And thou Bethlehem Ephrata, a little one » etc. — the Christ was to be born of Bethlehem. Therefore the Jews said below, the seventh: « Doth the Christ come out of Galilee » ? Whether it be read *remissively* or *interrogatively,* Philip here asserts and exhorts him to come. On account of which it follows: *Philip saith to him: Come and see;* Chrysostom[^p266-12]: « Philip, not being able to show by words the riches which he found, leads his brother to the treasure found ». And it is to be understood that he followed Philip.

94. (Vers. 47.). *Jesus saw Nathanael* etc. Here is touched the third, namely the *drawing of the one called.* He draws him by *commendation* and by the *revelation of secrets.* By *commendation;* whence he says: *Jesus saw Nathanael coming to him,* namely through the admonition of the simple Philip; *and he saith of him: Behold an Israelite indeed, in whom there is no guile.* Therefore there is no guile, because, though he is wise, he did not blush to follow a simple man; because what he believed in his heart, this he
<!-- page 267 -->
brought forth with his mouth, because he came not with a will to tempt, but with a study[^tr-studium] of profiting; against which, Proverbs, the twelfth[^p267-1]: « Deceit is in the heart of them that think evil things ». But Nathanael does not believe his own commendation unless he hear a *certitude* upon it, because a man ought not to be prone to believe good things of himself. Therefore

95. (Vers. 48.). *Nathanael saith to him: Whence knowest thou me?* Why dost thou so commend me? And the Lord draws him by the *revelation of secrets;* therefore it follows: *Jesus answered, and said to him: Before that Philip called thee, when thou wast under the fig tree, I saw thee. According to the letter:* he was in a secret place, as in a garden, whence Philip called him. Or: the *fig tree* is called the custom of sinning, as the first parents covered themselves under the leaves of the fig, Genesis, the third[^p267-2]; where he was seen and sought by the Lord before he himself sought the Lord. By this revelation Nathanael was drawn; whence there follows his faithful confession.

96. (Vers. 49.). *Nathanael answered and said to him: Rabbi, thou art the Son of God,* as to the Divinity, because thou knowest the secrets of the heart; Jeremiah, the seventeenth[^p267-3]: « I the Lord searching the hearts and reins ». *Thou art the King of Israel,* as to the humanity. In like manner the crowd confessed, below, the twelfth: « Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord, the King of Israel ».

97. (Vers. 50.). *Jesus answered* etc. Here is touched the fourth, namely the *confirmation of the one* now *drawn;* for he confirms him by the promise of showing greater things; on account of which he says: *Jesus answered, and said to him: Because I said unto thee, I saw thee under the fig tree, thou believest;* and so thou wast drawn to faith for a small thing. *A greater thing than these shalt thou see;* below, the fifth[^p267-4]: « Greater works than these will he shew him, that you may wonder ». And he specifies that *greater thing.*

98. (Vers. 51.). *And he saith to him: Amen, amen I say to you, that*[^p267-5] *you shall see the heaven opened,* according as it is said in Matthew, the third, that John saw that the heavens were opened above him. *And the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of man. According to the letter* the angels descended to him, according as it is said in Matthew, the fourth[^p267-6]: « Angels came and ministered to him »; *spiritually,* however, as Augustine says, what was said to Nathanael is now fulfilled in Christians, because, after God made man ascended, the entrance of heaven is opened to those who believe in him, and they see the angels, that is, the preachers, *ascending,* while they consider the hidden things of the Deity; *and descending,* while they preach the human things concerning him. This vision was signified in Genesis, the twenty-eighth[^p267-7], where it is said that Jacob saw the Lord leaning upon the ladder, and the angels ascending and descending.

### Questions.

99. Quaest. I. But it is asked here about the Lord's coming into Galilee: why is it said here that *on the following day,* after the testimony of John, he came into Galilee?

**Contra:** 1. The other Evangelists say that immediately *he was led into the desert by the Spirit,* Matthew, the fourth[^p267-8], and Mark, the first.

2. Likewise, in the same place[^p267-9] it is said that *Jesus came into Galilee after John was delivered up;* but it is established that he had not yet been delivered up, as is said below, the third, that John baptized a long time after.

To this Augustine responds in the book *On the Concord of the Evangelists*, the second[^p267-10], that the coming of the Lord into Galilee was twofold. The first was after the testimony of John, before he was sent into prison; and of this all the other Evangelists are silent. The second, however, was after John was sent into prison, and of this it is said below, in the fourth chapter[^p267-11] In the first coming he worked the miracle of the change of water into wine at the wedding, in the second coming the miracle of the ruler's son.

1. To what is said, then, that he was led into the desert; it is to be understood that that testimony was given after the Lord's return from the desert.

100. Quaest. II. Likewise, it is asked about the *calling* of the disciples, because there is a like doubt.

1. For it is said here that they were called before John was delivered up. But Matthew[^p267-12] says that they were called after John's delivering up.
<!-- page 268 -->

2. Likewise, Matthew says that he called Peter and Andrew *from the ship,* as is said in Matthew, the fourth[^p268-1]; here it has been said that at the testimony of John they came to him, first Andrew, then Peter.

**Respondeo:** It must be said, as Augustine[^p268-2] says, that John keeps the order of history as to the *time* of the calling, but Matthew says it by *recapitulation.*

2. To what is objected about the *manner* of the calling, it must be said to this that the disciples of the Lord were called in two ways: in one way to faith, in another way to perfection. In the first way Peter and Andrew were called[^p268-3], that they might know the Lord and believe in him by the testimony of John. In the second way they were called from the ships, that they might leave all things and follow Christ. Of the first John speaks here, but of the second Matthew speaks[^p268-4].

101. Quaest. III. Likewise, about the *place* of the calling: because the Lord called the disciples from Galilee, Chrysostom asks[^p268-5], since the Jews were more skilled in the Law, whence is it that he did not choose from Jerusalem? — And next to this it is asked: why did he call Nathanael through Philip as a mean? — And again, since Nathanael was so holy a person, as the Lord says, and also most skilled in the Law, the Lord seems to have been obliged to appoint such a man an Apostle. — *If thou say* that he willed to choose simple men: then, if « every action of Christ is our instruction[^p268-6] », it seems that we ought to choose simple and unlettered prelates.

To this the Apostle teaches us to respond, in First Corinthians, the first[^p268-7]: *Not many wise according to the flesh, not many mighty, not many noble, but the foolish things of the world hath God chosen, and the things that are not.* And the reason of this is, that he may confound the high things of the world, and *that no flesh should glory in his sight,* and that it may be shown that all that was done through the Apostles is of divine power, not of human prudence. Hence it is that he chose Galileans as Apostles, both fishermen and simple rustics; because, as Chrysostom[^p268-8] says, Galilee has rustic inhabitants. Hence also it is that he called the simple Philip by himself, and through him his wise brother[^p268-9]. Hence it is too that he did not make Nathanael, a great doctor in the Law, an Apostle[^p268-10]. Hence it is that he did not call Paul among the first Apostles; but *last of all, as by one born out of due time, he was seen also by him*[^p268-11], that he might be humbled.

To what thou objectest, then, that we should choose simple men; it must be responded that the Lord chose simple men that he might make them wise; but because it is not in our power to make men wise, therefore we ought to set over ourselves men made wise.

102. Quaest. IV. Likewise, it is asked about the obedience of the Apostles, because they followed immediately, with no signs seen. — It seems that they were moved foolishly, as is said here of Philip and of the others; whence the heretics[^p268-12] reprehend Matthew.

It can be responded that they were illumined inwardly; or it must also be said that they were induced outwardly, some by *testimony,* as Andrew and his companion; some by a *miracle,* as is said of Peter in Luke, the fifth[^p268-13]; some by *example,* and so Philip, because he saw Peter and Andrew, his fellow countrymen, following, whom he knew to be good.

103. Quaest. V. Likewise, Bede[^p268-14] asks: since Nathanael calls Christ *the Son of God,* whence is it that he calls himself *the son of man?*

Bede[^p268-15] responds that « this was done by the just governance of the dispensation, that, when each nature is mentioned, he should confess his own humility, the other his height »; that thereby each nature in Christ may be made known, and an example of humility be given, whence a man should always acknowledge and speak of lowly things in himself. Which is most of all against those who, having some noble kinsmen, when they are asked about their origin, do not answer with the name of their father, but
<!-- page 269 -->
of an uncle, or of some famous kinsman. Our Lord Jesus Christ, though he is noble on the part of the Father, but lowest on the part of the Mother, calls himself *the son of man* often, rarely *the Son of God,* unless compelled by necessity. And note, for the difference from others, that others are called sons of men who are begotten of the commingling of seeds; but Christ had one single parent, and therefore rightly calls himself the son of man[^p269-1].
