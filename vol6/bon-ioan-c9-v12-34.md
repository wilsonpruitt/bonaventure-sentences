# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. IX, Vers. 12-34
## *In parte secunda describitur Pharisaeorum disceptatio*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 376 -->

#### In parte secunda describitur Pharisaeorum disceptatio.

18. *Et dixerunt ei: Ubi est ille?* Postquam insinuata est mirabilis caeci illuminatio, sequitur hic secunda pars, in qua describitur ex hoc oriens *Iudaeorum calumniatio.* Et dividitur haec pars in tres. In prima ostenditur, quomodo Pharisaei nituntur *Christum reprehendere.* Secundo, quomodo nituntur *miraculum negare,* ibi[^p376-5]: *Dicunt ergo iterum caeco: Tu quid dicis* etc. Tertio, quomodo, in his deficientes, nituntur *occultare,* ibi: *Vocaverunt ergo rursum hominem.*

Primo ergo describitur, quomodo nituntur *reprehendere* miraculum cum auctoritate per hunc modum. Primo enim insinuatur *reprehendentium sollicitudo;* secundo, *reprehendendi occasio;* tertio, *ipsa reprehensio*[^p376-6].

(Vers. 12.). *Reprehendentium sollicitudo* in hoc notatur, quod curantem requirunt, et non invenientes, curatum non dimittunt; ideo dicit: *Et dixerunt ei: Ubi est ille?* scilicet curator. *Ait: Nescio.* Et quia curantem non possunt invenire, curatum ad Pharisaeos perducunt; ideo dicit:

19. (Vers. 13.). *Adducunt eum ad Pharisaeos, qui caecus fuerat,* quia illi erant ad reprehendendum solliciti; Lucae sexto[^p376-7]: « Observabant scribae et Pharisaei, si in Sabbato curaret, ut invenirent, unde accusarent eum ».

20. (Vers. 14.). *Erat autem Sabbatum.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *reprehendendi occasio,* quae erat *ex die festo* et *modo curandi. Ratione diei* dicit: *Erat autem Sabbatum, quando lutum fecit Iesus et aperuit oculos eius;* supra quinto[^p376-8]: « Propter hoc persequebantur Iudaei Iesum, quia hoc », scilicet opus curationis, « faciebat in Sabbato », quasi Legem contemnens. Alia occasio erat *ex modo curandi,* quem ideo quaerunt, quia nisi
<!-- page 377 -->
per confessionem caeci scire non possunt. Quaerunt de modo, ut, quia factum non valent reprehendere, calumnientur modum[^p377-1]; ideo dicit:

21. (Vers. 15.). *Et iterum interrogabant eum Pharisaei, quomodo videret*[^p377-2]*;* et modum quaerunt et modum audiunt; unde: *Ille autem dixit eis: Lutum posuit mihi super oculos meos, et lavi et modo video;* breviter repetit, quia ipsi iam ab aliis audierant, sed ad maiorem certitudinem ex ipsius ore requirebant. Et apparet hic constantia caeci, qui veritatem non timuit confiteri, servans illud Ecclesiastici quarto[^p377-3]: « Pro anima tua non confundaris dicere verum ».

22. (Vers. 16.). *Dicebant ergo ex Pharisaeis quidam.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *in reprehendendo dissensio;* unde dicit: *Dicebant ergo ex Pharisaeis quidam,* fatui zelatores Legis; ad Romanos decimo[^p377-4]: « Aemulationem quidem Dei habent, sed non secundum scientiam ». Dicebant: *Non est hic homo a Deo,* per conformitatem et complacentiam; *qui Sabbatum non custodit,* quod custodiri a Deo praeceptum est, Exodi vigesimo[^p377-5] et in pluribus aliis locis. *Alii autem dicebant: Quomodo potest homo peccator haec signa facere?* Et isti erant magis illuminati; unde isti habebant aliquid luminis; sed alii obtenebrati; ideo sequitur, quod *schisma*[^p377-6] *erat inter eos,* id est divisio, iusto Dei iudicio dividentis lucem a tenebris et bonos a malis; propter quod dixit Matthaei decimo: « Non veni pacem mittere, sed gladium ».

23. *Dicunt ergo*[^p377-7] *caeco.* Tentaverunt supra Pharisaei Christum reprehendere; et quia in reprehendendo non poterant concordare, tentant et satagunt *miraculum negare.* Et hoc quidem describitur hoc ordine: primo notatur *Pharisaeorum versutia ad caecum tentandum;* secundo, *duritia ad credendum;* tertio, *diligentia ad quaerendum;* quarto, *parentum caeci timiditas ad respondendum.*

(Vers. 17.). Notatur ergo primo *Pharisaeorum versutia ad tentandum;* tentant enim caecum, quid de Christo sentiat, ut, si male, eum negare faciant Christi miraculum; si bene, verbis eius non credant. Propterea dicitur: *Dicunt ergo iterum caeco,* scilicet Pharisaei: *Tu quid dicis de eo*[^p377-8] *qui aperuit oculos tuos?* utrum scilicet sit bonus, vel malus; et malitiose requirunt, caecus autem respondit simpliciter. *Ille autem dixit, quia Propheta est;* Glossa[^p377-9]: « Constanter exprimit quod sentit », sicut turbae, Lucae septimo: « Propheta magnus surrexit in nobis ».

24. (Vers. 18.). *Non crediderunt ergo Iudaei de illo, quia caecus* etc. Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *Iudaeorum duritia ad credendum;* ideo enim credere nolebant, propter hoc scilicet, quia Christum laudabat; ideo suspectum habebant. *Non crediderunt*[^p377-10], scilicet *donec vocaverunt parentes eius qui viderat;* sperabant enim per ipsos miraculum infirmare; unde Chrysostomus[^p377-11]: « A parentibus putabant abortivum facere miraculum »; unde semper rebellare nitebantur, secundum illud Iob vigesimo quarto: « Ipsi rebelles fuerunt lumini et ignoraverunt vias eius ».

25. (Vers. 19.). *Et interrogaverunt eos dicentes.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *Iudaeorum diligentia ad inquirendum;* quaerunt enim a parentibus tria: *personam, conditionem* et mirabilem *immutationem.* Propter quod dicitur: *Hic est filius vester?* Quaerunt de *persona. Quem vos dicitis, quia caecus natus est?* Id est, de quo dicitis, quia caecus natus est; interrogative quaerunt *conditionem. Quomodo ergo nunc videt?* Quaerunt *immutationem;* prudenter et diligenter requirunt, quia, sicut dicitur Lucae decimo sexto[^p377-12], « filii huius saeculi prudentiores filiis lucis in generatione sua sunt ».

26. (Vers. 20.). *Responderunt parentes eius.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *parentum timiditas ad respondendum.* Et cum tria quaesivissent, ad duo respondent, ad tertium non audent respondere[^p377-13]. Ad hoc, utrum sit eorum filius et caecus natus; *responderunt eis parentes eius,* id est pater et mater, *et dixerunt: Scimus, quia hic est filius noster et quia caecus natus est.* Et in hoc confutantur Pharisaei certissimo parentum testimonio; unde
<!-- page 378 -->
Chrysostomus[^p378-1]: « Quis crederet, parentes talia mentiri de filio »? Et licet veritatem dixerint, tamen non totam; unde:

27. (Vers. 21.). *Quomodo autem nunc videat, nescimus; aut quis eius oculos aperuit, nos nescimus;* quia scilicet praesentes non fuimus, neque magis scimus quam vos, nec oportet nos interrogare, sed ipsum; unde dicunt: *Ipsum interrogate,* quia potest respondere; *aetatem habet, ipse de se loquatur;* quia ipse certior est nobis. Rationem huius responsionis assignat Evangelista. Illius responsionis ratio fuit timor Iudaicae conspirationis, unde dicit Evangelista:

28. (Vers. 22.). *Haec autem dixerunt parentes eius, quia*[^p378-2] *timebant Iudaeos;* Isaiae quinquagesimo primo: « Quis tu, ut timeas ab homine mortali et a filio hominis, qui quasi foenum ita arescet »? Et primi Machabaeorum secundo: « A verbis viri peccatoris ne timueritis, quia gloria eius stercus et vermis est ». Et ratio timendi redditur: *Iam enim conspiraverant Iudaei, ut, si quis eum confiteretur Christum*[^p378-3]*, extra synagogam fieret.* Conspiratio est colligatio in malum, sicut, Ieremiae undecimo, « inventa est coniuratio in viris Iuda et in habitatoribus Ierusalem ». Reversi sunt ad iniquitates patrum suorum et *conspiraverant* nomen Christi delere; Ieremiae undecimo: « Eradamus eum de terra viventium, et nomen eius non memoretur amplius ».

29. (Vers. 23.). *Propterea parentes eius dixerunt: Aetatem*[^p378-4] *habet; ipsum interrogate;* adhuc enim erant de illis, de quibus dicitur infra duodecimo, quod « multi crediderunt in eum, sed propter Pharisaeos non confitebantur, ut de synagoga non eiicerentur ».

30. *Vocaverunt ergo rursum hominem* illum. Iam frustrato conatu Pharisaeorum ad reprehendendum et negandum, hic conantur *ad miraculum obumbrandum,* ut ipsum Christo Domino non attribuatur, sed nomen eius taceatur. Ideo conantur hic caecum a confessione nominis Christi retrahere hoc ordine: primo scilicet *blanditiis;* secundo, *astutiis;* tertio, *conviciis;* quarto, *iniuriis.*

(Vers. 24.). Primo ergo conantur ipsum retrahere *blanditiis.* Blande enim admonent ipsum caecum, ut Christum non tanquam bonum credat hoc fecisse miraculum et illud confiteatur. Propterea dicit: *Vocaverunt ergo rursum hominem, qui caecus fuerat, et dixerunt ei: Da gloriam Deo,* scilicet confitendo veritatem. *Dare* enim *gloriam Deo* est veritatem de aliquo confiteri, quasi Deo praesente; Iosue septimo[^p378-5]: « Fili, confitere et da gloriam Deo ». Et veritas illa erat, ut hoc miraculum Christo non attribuat; propter quod dicunt: *Nos scimus, quia hic homo peccator est;* confitere igitur, te non ab hoc curatum, sed a Deo. Chrysostomus[^p378-6]: « Sub specie religionis hoc facere tentabant »; et tamen ad malum inducebant. Unde Augustinus[^p378-7]: « Negare quod accepisti non est gloriam Deo dare, sed Deum potius blasphemare ». Et licet blandiantur, non tamen praevalent; quia caecus admonitionem spernit et veritatem dicit. Unde:

31. (Vers. 25.). *Dicit homo ille*[^p378-8]*: Si peccator est, nescio; unum scio, quia, cum essem caecus, modo video.* Quasi dicat: auctorem non iudico, sed beneficium recognosco. Augustinus[^p378-9]: « Nec favet calumniae nec celat veritatem ». Sapienter caecus respondit, iuxta illud Ecclesiastici quinto[^p378-10]: « Si est tibi intellectus, responde proximo; sin autem, sit manus tua super os tuum, ne capiaris in verbo indisciplinato et confundaris ».

32. (Vers. 26.). *Dixerunt ergo illi.* Tangitur hic secundum, quomodo scilicet tentant retrahere *per astutias;* ideo malitiose quaerunt, ut aliquid miraculo obiiciant; propter quod dicit: *Quid fecit tibi? Quomodo aperuit oculos tuos*[^p378-11]*?* Chrysostomus: « Curationis modum scrutantur, quemadmodum canes undique investigantes ». Sed in hac astutia reprehenduntur a caeco, ideo renuit respondere, sed arguit; unde subdit:

33. (Vers. 27.). *Respondit eis: Iam dixi vobis, et audistis; quid iterum vultis audire?* Quasi dicat: si malitiose quaeritis, non respondebo; si autem studiose, respondebo; unde dicit: *Nunquid et vos vultis discipuli eius fieri?* Quasi dicat: credo, quod
<!-- page 379 -->
non vultis; sed si velletis, iterum dicerem; attendens illud Proverbiorum nono[^p379-1]: « Qui erudit derisorem, ipse sibi iniuriam facit ».

34. (Vers. 28.). *Maledixerunt ergo* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet quod tentant retrahere *conviciis;* unde sic paulatim procedunt: *Maledixerunt ergo ei et dixerunt: Tu discipulus illius sis,* id est, maledicendo et conviciando dixerunt ei, quod esset Christi discipulus. Augustinus[^p379-2]: « In me sit ista maledictio; tale maledictum sit super nos et super filios nostros ». Discipulatum Christi contemnebant et ei praeferebant discipulatum Moysi; unde dicunt: *Nos*[^p379-3] *Moysi discipuli sumus,* non istius; et rationem subdunt:

35. (Vers. 29.). *Nos scimus, quia Moysi locutus est Deus,* sicut dicitur in Exodo et Levitico quasi per totum; Exodi trigesimo tertio[^p379-4]: « Loquebatur Dominus Moysi facie ad faciem ». *Hunc autem nescimus, unde sit;* ideo discipuli sumus illius quem Dominus approbat. Sed tamen falsum dicebant, quia, sicut dicitur supra quinto[^p379-5], « si crederetis Moysi, crederetis forsitan et mihi ». Et in hac maledictione contra caecum non praevalent; immo arguit maledictionis rationem in hoc, quod dicebant, se nescire Christum; propter quod dicit:

36. (Vers. 30.). *Respondit ille homo et dixit eis: In hoc enim mirabile est, quod*[^p379-6] *vos nescitis, unde sit, et aperuit oculos meos.* Non dixit *impium,* ne provocaret eos, sed *mirabile,* quia ipse admirabatur eorum duritiam; et rationem reddit, quia vident signum evidentissimum et non credunt. Ideo verificatum est illud Isaiae sexto[^p379-7]: « Ut videntes non videant, et audientes non intelligant ».

37. (Vers. 31.). *Scimus*[^p379-8]*, quia peccatores Deus non audit;* unde Isaiae primo Dominus ad peccatores: « Cum multiplicaveritis orationem, non exaudiam »; Iob octavo: « Deus non proiiciet simplicem nec porriget manum malignis ». *Sed si quis Dei cultor est et voluntatem eius facit, hunc exaudit;* infra decimo quinto[^p379-9]: « Si manseritis in me, et verba mea in vobis manserint; quodcumque volueritis, petetis, et fiet vobis »; primae Ioannis tertio: « Fiduciam habemus ad Deum, ut, quodcumque petierimus, accipiamus, quoniam mandata eius custodimus et ea quae sunt placita coram eo, facimus ». Sed hunc praecipue exaudit; et hoc probat:

38. (Vers. 32.). *A saeculo non est auditum, quod*[^p379-10] *quis aperuit oculos caeci nati;* sed hic aperuit: ergo est a Deo, quia *a destructione consequentis:*

39. (Vers. 33.). *Nisi esset hic homo a Deo, non posset*[^p379-11] *facere quidquam;* infra decimo quinto: « Sine me nihil potestis facere ».

40. (Vers. 34.). *Responderunt et dixerunt.* Tangitur hic quartum: quia non valentes retrahere blanditiis nec astutiis nec conviciis, aggrediuntur *iniuriis;* unde ex indignatione moti, *responderunt et dixerunt ei: In peccatis natus es totus, et tu doces nos?* qui scilicet iusti reputamur. Vere isti erant Pharisaei similes illi qui se in oratione laudabat, Lucae decimo octavo[^p379-12]. *Et eiecerunt eum foras,* pro veritate iniuriam intulerunt. Chrysostomus: « Vides praeconem veritatis, quanta passus est. Haec scripta sunt, ut et nos similiter imitemur ». Simile de Stephano Actuum septimo: « Et eiicientes eum extra civitatem, lapidabant ».

### Quaestiones.

41. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod caecus dixit: *Si peccator est, nescio.* — Videtur fuisse mentitus, quia infra[^p379-13] ostendit, quod non erat peccator.

Respondetur dupliciter: ex Chrysostomo[^p379-14] primo, quod *nescio,* id est, modo non enuntio; vel *nescio,* quia ipsius vitam ignoro; porro ut homo caecus, et qui nunquam eum vidi nec secutus fui.

42. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Scimus, quia peccatores Deus non audit.* — *Contra:* Nonne audivit publicanum[^p379-15]?
<!-- page 380 -->

Respondetur dupliciter ex Glossa[^p380-1]: primo modo, ut fiat vis in hoc quod dicit: *peccatores non audit,* quia persistentes in peccato non audit, ad salutem scilicet. — Aliter respondet Glossa[^p380-2], quod caecus nondum inunctus erat nec interius illuminatus; et ideo falsum dixit, licet crederet verum dicere.

43. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Nisi esset hic homo a Deo, non posset facere quidquam.* — *Contra:* Multi mali faciunt miracula plura, sicut ipse Dominus dicit Matthaei septimo[^p380-3]: *Domine, nonne in nomine tuo daemonia eiecimus et in nomine tuo virtutes multas fecimus?*

Respondetur, quod caecus intellexit, quod faceret propria auctoritate; vel potest responderi, sicut de priori[^p380-4].

44. Quaest. IV. Sed tunc quaeritur: cum Dominus omnem, quem sanavit exterius, sanaverit simul interius[^p380-5]; quare iste caecus non fuit interius illuminatus?

Responderi potest dupliciter: aut quod caecus iste fidelis erat, sed tamen nondum fidem explicitam habebat, sicut catechumeni, et ideo infra[^p380-6] instruitur; aut quod non oportuit, eum tunc simul sanari interius, quia non ex culpa sua infirmabatur exterius, sicut Dominus testatur[^p380-7].

45. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur, quare dicant ei Pharisaei: *In peccatis natus es totus.*

Responderi potest, quod non dicunt ex ratione, sed ex indignatione. Vel quia a peccatis, in quibus natus fuerat, non recesserat per religionem, sicut Pharisaei; vel propter caecitatem, quae erat poena peccati, vocabant eum in peccatis natum[^p380-8].

## English
<!-- page 376 -->

#### In the second part is described the disputing of the Pharisees.

18. *And they said to him: Where is he?* After the wonderful illumination of the blind man has been intimated, there follows here the second part, in which is described *the calumniating of the Jews* arising from it. And this part is divided into three. In the first is shown how the Pharisees strive *to reprehend Christ.* Second, how they strive *to deny the miracle,* there[^p376-5]: *They say therefore to the blind man again: What sayest thou* etc. Third, how, failing in these, they strive *to conceal,* there: *They therefore called the man again.*

First, then, is described how they strive *to reprehend* the miracle together with its author, in this manner. For first is intimated *the solicitude of those reprehending;* second, *the occasion of reprehending;* third, *the reprehension itself*[^p376-6].

(V. 12.). *The solicitude of those reprehending* is noted in this, that they seek the one who cured, and not finding him, they do not let go the one cured; therefore he says: *And they said to him: Where is he?* that is, the curer. *He saith: I know not.* And because they cannot find the one who cured, they lead the one cured to the Pharisees; therefore he says:

19. (V. 13.). *They bring him that had been blind to the Pharisees,* because these were solicitous to reprehend; Luke, the sixth chapter[^p376-7]: « The scribes and Pharisees watched if he would heal on the sabbath, that they might find an accusation against him ».

20. (V. 14.). *Now it was the sabbath.* Here the second is touched on, namely *the occasion of reprehending,* which was *from the feast day* and *the manner of curing. By reason of the day* he says: *Now it was the sabbath, when Jesus made the clay, and opened his eyes;* above, in the fifth chapter[^p376-8]: « Therefore did the Jews persecute Jesus, because he did these things », namely the work of curing, « on the sabbath », as though despising the Law. The other occasion was *from the manner of curing,* which they ask about for this reason, that unless
<!-- page 377 -->
through the confession of the blind man they cannot know it. They ask about the manner, so that, since they are not able to reprehend the deed, they may calumniate the manner[^p377-1]; therefore he says:

21. (V. 15.). *And again the Pharisees asked him, how he saw*[^p377-2]*;* and they ask the manner and they hear the manner; whence: *But he said to them: He put clay upon my eyes, and I washed, and now I see;* he repeats it briefly, because they had already heard it from others, but for greater certainty they sought it from his own mouth. And here appears the constancy of the blind man, who did not fear to confess the truth, keeping that of Ecclesiasticus, the fourth chapter[^p377-3]: « For thy soul be not ashamed to say the truth ».

22. (V. 16.). *Some therefore of the Pharisees said.* Here the third is touched on, namely *the dissension in reprehending;* whence he says: *Some therefore of the Pharisees said,* foolish zealots of the Law; to the Romans, the tenth chapter[^p377-4]: « They have indeed a zeal of God, but not according to knowledge ». They said: *This man is not of God,* by conformity and good pleasure; *who keepeth not the sabbath,* which God commanded to be kept, Exodus, the twentieth chapter[^p377-5], and in many other places. *But others said: How can a man that is a sinner do these signs?* And these were more illuminated; whence these had something of lumen[^tr-lumen]; but the others were darkened; therefore it follows that *there was a schism*[^p377-6] *among them,* that is, a division, by the just judgement of God dividing the light from the darkness and the good from the wicked; because of which he said in Matthew, the tenth chapter: « I came not to send peace, but the sword ».

23. *They say therefore*[^p377-7] *to the blind man.* The Pharisees tried above to reprehend Christ; and because in reprehending they could not agree, they try and busy themselves *to deny the miracle.* And this indeed is described in this order: first is noted *the cunning of the Pharisees in testing the blind man;* second, *their hardness to believing;* third, *their diligence in asking;* fourth, *the timidity of the blind man's parents in answering.*

(V. 17.). First, then, is noted *the cunning of the Pharisees in testing;* for they test the blind man as to what he thinks of Christ, so that, if ill, they may make him deny Christ's miracle; if well, they may not believe his words. Therefore it is said: *They say therefore to the blind man again,* namely the Pharisees: *What sayest thou of him*[^p377-8] *that hath opened thy eyes?* that is, whether he be good or wicked; and they ask it maliciously, but the blind man answered simply. *And he said: He is a prophet;* the Gloss[^p377-9]: « He constantly expresses what he thinks », as did the crowds, Luke, the seventh chapter: « A great prophet is risen up among us ».

24. (V. 18.). *The Jews then did not believe concerning him, that he had been blind* etc. Here the second is touched on, namely *the hardness of the Jews to believing;* for they were unwilling to believe for this reason, namely because he praised Christ; therefore they held him suspect. *They did not believe*[^p377-10], that is, *until they called the parents of him that had seen;* for they hoped through them to weaken the miracle; whence Chrysostom[^p377-11]: « Through the parents they thought to make the miracle miscarry »; whence they always strove to rebel, according to that of Job, the twenty-fourth chapter: « They have been rebellious to the light, and have not known its ways ».

25. (V. 19.). *And they asked them, saying.* Here the third is touched on, namely *the diligence of the Jews in inquiring;* for they ask three things of the parents: *the person, the condition* and the wonderful *change.* Because of which it is said: *Is this your son?* They ask about *the person. Who you say was born blind?* That is, of whom you say that he was born blind; by questioning they ask about *the condition. How then doth he now see?* They ask about *the change;* they inquire prudently and diligently, because, as is said in Luke, the sixteenth chapter[^p377-12], « the children of this world are wiser in their generation than the children of light ».

26. (V. 20.). *His parents answered.* Here the fourth is touched on, namely *the timidity of the parents in answering.* And though they had asked three things, they answer to two; to the third they do not dare to answer[^p377-13]. To this, whether he is their son and born blind, *his parents answered them,* that is, father and mother, *and said: We know that this is our son, and that he was born blind.* And in this the Pharisees are confuted by the most certain testimony of the parents; whence
<!-- page 378 -->
Chrysostom[^p378-1]: « Who would believe that parents would lie such things about a son »? And although they told the truth, yet not the whole; whence:

27. (V. 21.). *But how he now seeth, we know not; or who hath opened his eyes, we know not;* that is, because we were not present, nor do we know more than you, nor ought you to ask us, but him; whence they say: *Ask himself,* because he can answer; *he is of age, let him speak for himself;* because he is more certain than we. The Evangelist assigns the reason of this response. The reason of that response was fear of the conspiracy of the Jews, whence the Evangelist says:

28. (V. 22.). *These things his parents said, because*[^p378-2] *they feared the Jews;* Isaias, the fifty-first chapter: « Who art thou, that thou shouldst be afraid of a mortal man, and of the son of man, who shall wither away like grass »? And First Machabees, the second chapter: « Fear ye not the words of a sinful man, for his glory is dung and worms ». And the reason for fearing is given: *For the Jews had already conspired, that if any man should confess him to be Christ*[^p378-3]*, he should be put out of the synagogue.* A conspiracy is a binding together unto evil, as, in Jeremias, the eleventh chapter, « a conspiracy is found among the men of Juda, and among the inhabitants of Jerusalem ». They have returned to the iniquities of their fathers and *had conspired* to blot out the name of Christ; Jeremias, the eleventh chapter: « Let us cut him off from the land of the living, and let his name be remembered no more ».

29. (V. 23.). *Therefore did his parents say: He is of age*[^p378-4]*; ask him;* for they were still of those of whom it is said below, in the twelfth chapter, that « many believed in him, but because of the Pharisees they did not confess him, that they might not be cast out of the synagogue ».

30. *They therefore called the man again,* that one. The Pharisees' attempt to reprehend and to deny having now been frustrated, here they attempt *to overshadow the miracle,* so that it be not attributed to Christ the Lord, but his name be passed over in silence. Therefore here they attempt to draw the blind man back from the confession of the name of Christ in this order: first, namely, *by blandishments;* second, *by cunning tricks;* third, *by revilings;* fourth, *by injuries.*

(V. 24.). First, then, they attempt to draw him back *by blandishments.* For they blandly admonish the blind man himself not to believe that Christ, as one good, did this miracle, and not to confess it. Therefore he says: *They therefore called the man again that had been blind, and said to him: Give glory to God,* namely by confessing the truth. For *to give glory to God* is to confess the truth about something, as though God were present; Josue, the seventh chapter[^p378-5]: « My son, confess, and give glory to God ». And that truth was that he should not attribute this miracle to Christ; because of which they say: *We know that this man is a sinner;* confess, therefore, that you were cured not by this man, but by God. Chrysostom[^p378-6]: « Under the appearance of religion they tried to do this »; and yet they were leading him to evil. Whence Augustine[^p378-7]: « To deny what you have received is not to give glory to God, but rather to blaspheme God ». And although they use blandishments, yet they do not prevail; because the blind man spurns the admonition and speaks the truth. Whence:

31. (V. 25.). *That man saith*[^p378-8]*: If he be a sinner, I know not: one thing I know, that whereas I was blind, now I see.* As if he were to say: I do not judge the author, but I acknowledge the benefit. Augustine[^p378-9]: « He neither favours the calumny nor hides the truth ». Wisely the blind man answered, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the fifth chapter[^p378-10]: « If thou have understanding, answer thy neighbour: but if not, let thy hand be upon thy mouth, lest thou be surprised in an unskilful word, and be confounded ».

32. (V. 26.). *They said then to him.* Here the second is touched on, namely how they try to draw him back *by cunning tricks;* therefore they ask maliciously, so as to object something against the miracle; because of which he says: *What did he to thee? How did he open thy eyes*[^p378-11]*?* Chrysostom: « They scrutinize the manner of the curing, like dogs tracking on every side ». But in this cunning they are reprehended by the blind man; therefore he refuses to answer, but argues against them; whence he adds:

33. (V. 27.). *He answered them: I have told you already, and you have heard: why would you hear it again?* As if he were to say: if you ask maliciously, I will not answer; but if studiously, I will answer; whence he says: *Will you also become his disciples?* As if he were to say: I believe that
<!-- page 379 -->
you do not wish it; but if you wished it, I would tell it again; heeding that of Proverbs, the ninth chapter[^p379-1]: « He that teacheth a scorner, doth an injury to himself ».

34. (V. 28.). *They reviled him therefore* etc. Here the third is touched on, namely that they try to draw him back *by revilings;* whence they proceed thus little by little: *They reviled him therefore, and said: Be thou his disciple,* that is, reviling and abusing they said to him that he was the disciple of Christ. Augustine[^p379-2]: « Let this curse be on me; such a curse be upon us and upon our children ». They despised the discipleship of Christ and preferred to it the discipleship of Moses; whence they say: *We*[^p379-3] *are the disciples of Moses,* not of this man; and they add the reason:

35. (V. 29.). *We know that God spoke to Moses,* as is said in Exodus and Leviticus almost throughout; Exodus, the thirty-third chapter[^p379-4]: « The Lord spoke to Moses face to face ». *But as to this man, we know not from whence he is;* therefore we are disciples of him whom the Lord approves. But yet they spoke falsely, because, as is said above, in the fifth chapter[^p379-5], « if you did believe Moses, you would perhaps believe me also ». And in this reviling against the blind man they do not prevail; rather he argues against the reason of the reviling in this, that they said they did not know Christ; because of which he says:

36. (V. 30.). *The man answered, and said to them: Why, herein is a wonderful thing, that*[^p379-6] *you know not from whence he is, and he hath opened my eyes.* He did not say *impious,* lest he provoke them, but *wonderful,* because he himself wondered at their hardness; and he gives the reason, because they see a most evident sign and do not believe. Therefore that of Isaias, the sixth chapter[^p379-7], has been verified: « That seeing they may not see, and hearing they may not understand ».

37. (V. 31.). *We know*[^p379-8]*, that God doth not hear sinners;* whence in Isaias, the first chapter, the Lord to sinners: « When you multiply prayer, I will not hear »; Job, the eighth chapter: « God will not cast away the simple, nor reach out his hand to the evildoer ». *But if a man be a server of God, and doth his will, him he heareth;* below, in the fifteenth chapter[^p379-9]: « If you abide in me, and my words abide in you, you shall ask whatever you will, and it shall be done unto you »; First John, the third chapter: « We have confidence towards God: and whatsoever we shall ask, we shall receive, because we keep his commandments, and do those things which are pleasing in his sight ». But this man he hears especially; and he proves this:

38. (V. 32.). *From the beginning of the world it hath not been heard, that*[^p379-10] *any man hath opened the eyes of one born blind;* but this man opened them: therefore he is from God, because *by the destruction of the consequent:*

39. (V. 33.). *Unless this man were of God, he could not*[^p379-11] *do any thing;* below, in the fifteenth chapter: « Without me you can do nothing ».

40. (V. 34.). *They answered, and said.* Here the fourth is touched on: that, not being able to draw him back by blandishments nor by cunning tricks nor by revilings, they attack *with injuries;* whence, moved with indignation, *they answered, and said to him: Thou wast wholly born in sins; and dost thou teach us?* — we, that is, who are reputed just. Truly these Pharisees were like that man who praised himself in prayer, Luke, the eighteenth chapter[^p379-12]. *And they cast him out,* they inflicted injury for the truth. Chrysostom: « You see the herald of truth, how much he suffered. These things are written that we too may imitate him likewise ». Similar is that of Stephen, Acts, the seventh chapter: « And casting him forth without the city, they stoned him ».

### Questions.

41. Question I. But here it is asked first about what the blind man said: *If he be a sinner, I know not.* — He seems to have lied, because below[^p379-13] he shows that he was not a sinner.

It is responded in two ways: from Chrysostom[^p379-14], first, that *I know not* means: I do not declare it now; or *I know not,* because I do not know his life; further, as a blind man, and one who never saw him nor followed him.

42. Question II. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *We know that God doth not hear sinners.* — **On the contrary:** Did he not hear the publican[^p379-15]?
<!-- page 380 -->

It is responded in two ways from the Gloss[^p380-1]: in the first way, that the force be placed on what he says: *doth not hear sinners,* because he does not hear those persisting in sin, unto salvation, that is. — Otherwise the Gloss[^p380-2] responds that the blind man was not yet anointed nor inwardly illuminated; and therefore he spoke falsely, although he believed he was speaking the truth.

43. Question III. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *Unless this man were of God, he could not do any thing.* — **On the contrary:** Many wicked men do many miracles, as the Lord himself says in Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p380-3]: *Lord, have not we cast out devils in thy name, and done many mighty works in thy name?*

It is responded that the blind man understood: that he did it by his own authority; or it can be responded as to the former[^p380-4].

44. Question IV. But then it is asked: since the Lord healed inwardly at the same time everyone whom he healed outwardly[^p380-5], why was this blind man not inwardly illuminated?

It can be responded in two ways: either that this blind man was a believer, but yet did not yet have explicit faith, like catechumens, and therefore he is instructed below[^p380-6]; or that it was not fitting for him to be healed inwardly at that time, because he was not infirm outwardly through his own fault, as the Lord testifies[^p380-7].

45. Question V. Likewise it is asked why the Pharisees say to him: *Thou wast wholly born in sins.*

It can be responded that they do not say it from reason, but from indignation. Or because he had not withdrawn from the sins in which he had been born by religion, as the Pharisees had; or because of the blindness, which was the penalty of sin, they called him born in sins[^p380-8].
