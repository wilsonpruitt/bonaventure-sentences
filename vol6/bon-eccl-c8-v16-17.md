# Commentarius in Ecclesiasten — Cap. VIII, Vers. 16-17

## *Tertio, de indistinctione bonorum et malorum, de qua agitur dupliciter*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 70 -->

#### Tertio, de indistinctione bonorum et malorum, de qua agitur dupliciter.

*Et apposui cor meum* etc. Ostensum est, quomodo vanitas securitatis oritur ex *sublimatione malorum* et etiam ex *depressione bonorum.* Hic tertio ostenditur, quomodo oritur ex *indistinctione horum et illorum.* Quia enim mali non possunt distingui a bonis, ideo sunt securi; et quoniam haec securitas ortum habet ex huiusmodi indistinctione, et ipsa etiam est principium voluptatis; ideo habet pars ista duas partes. Primo ostenditur, quomodo ex indistinctione oritur *securitas;* secundo, quomodo ex securitate *voluptas,* ibi[^p70-13]: *Nemo est, qui semper vivat* etc.

<!-- page 71 -->
#### Primo ostenditur, quomodo inde nascitur securitas.

Primum ergo hoc ordine describitur: primo ergo notatur discernendi *cassa sollicitudo;* secundo vero, *discretionis incertitudo;* tertio, *incertitudinis ratio;* quarto notatur ex hoc *vanae securitatis origo.*

(Vers. 16. 17.). Insinuatur igitur primo discernendi *cassa sollicitudo,* quae sollicitudo fuit *diligens;* unde dicit: *Et apposui cor meum,* id est, intellectum applicui; *ut scirem sapientiam,* quantum ad invisibilia, *et intelligerem distinctionem eorum quae versantur in terra,* quantum ad visibilia, quia ad Romanos primo[^p71-1]: « Invisibilia Dei per ea quae facta sunt, a creatura mundi intellecta conspiciuntur ». Et ista sollicitudo *cassa* est, quia, quantumcumque homo laboret, non potest invenire; ideo subdit: *Est homo, qui diebus et noctibus somnum non capit oculis,* immo laborat continue ad inquirendum; supra tertio: « Vidi afflictionem, quam dedit Deus filiis hominum, ut distendantur in ea »; et subdit: « Cuncta fecit bona et mundum tradidit disputationi eorum »; et licet tantum laboret, nihil proficit. — Ideo subdit: *Et intellexi, quod omnium operum Dei nullam possit homo invenire rationem, eorum,* scilicet operum, *quae fiunt sub sole;* Ecclesiastici quadragesimo tertio[^p71-2]: « Ne laboretis, non enim comprehendetis ». *Et quanto plus laboraverit ad quaerendum, tanto minus inveniet;* unde in Psalmo: « Accedet homo ad cor altum, et exaltabitur Deus », id est elongabitur; Augustinus: « Altus est Deus; elevas te, et fugit a te; humilias te, et venit ad te ». *Etiam si dixerit sapiens, se nosse, non poterit reperire,* id est, si se credat scire, minus intelligit, quia dicitur ad Romanos primo[^p71-3]: « Dicentes, se esse sapientes, stulti facti sunt »; supra septimo: « Dixi: sapiens efficiar, et ipsa longius recessit a me ». Et repetit sollicitudinem, addens, quod fuit *curiosa;* adhibere enim sollicitudinem, ubi non est utilitas, est curiositas[^p71-4].

Ideo dicit: *Omnia haec tractavi in corde meo, ut intelligerem curiose*[^p71-5]; et hoc reprehenditur Ecclesiastici tertio[^p71-6]: « In supervacuis rebus noli scrutari multum, et in pluribus operibus eius ne fueris curiosus ». Et sic patet distinguendi sollicitudo, quae fuit *diligens* et *cassa,* et ideo *curiosa.*

## English
<!-- page 70 -->

#### Thirdly, on the indistinction of the good and the evil, which is treated in two ways.

*And I applied my heart* etc. It has been shown how the vanity of security arises from *the raising up of the evil* and also from *the casting down of the good.* Here, thirdly, it is shown how it arises from *the indistinction of these and those.* For since the evil cannot be distinguished from the good, they are therefore secure; and since this security has its origin from such indistinction, and is itself also the beginning of pleasure, therefore this part has two parts. First it is shown how *security* arises from indistinction; secondly, how *pleasure* arises from security, there[^p70-13]: *There is no man that liveth always* etc.

<!-- page 71 -->
#### First it is shown how security is born from it.

The first, then, is described in this order: first, then, is noted the *fruitless solicitude* of discerning; secondly, *the uncertainty of discernment;* thirdly, *the reason of the uncertainty;* fourthly is noted from this *the origin of vain security.*

(Vers. 16. 17.). First, then, is intimated the *fruitless solicitude* of discerning, which solicitude was *diligent;* whence he says: *And I applied my heart,* that is, I applied my understanding; *to know wisdom,* as regards invisible things, *and to understand the distinction of the things that are busied upon earth,* as regards visible things, since to the Romans, the first[^p71-1]: « The invisible things of God are clearly seen, from the creation of the world, being understood by the things that are made ». And this solicitude is *fruitless,* because, however much a man labours, he cannot find; therefore he adds: *There is a man that day and night taketh no sleep with his eyes,* nay rather, he labours continually at inquiring; above, the third: « I have seen the trouble which God hath given the sons of men, to be exercised in it »; and he adds: « He hath made all things good, and hath delivered the world to their consideration »; and though he labour so much, he profits nothing. — Therefore he adds: *And I understood that man can find no reason of all the works of God, of those,* namely of the works, *that are done under the sun;* Ecclesiasticus, the forty-third[^p71-2]: « Labour not, for you shall not comprehend ». *And the more he shall labour to seek, so much the less shall he find;* whence in the Psalm: « Man shall come to a high heart, and God shall be exalted », that is, shall be removed far off; Augustine: « God is high: thou liftest thyself up, and he flees from thee; thou humblest thyself, and he comes to thee ». *Yea, though the wise man shall say that he knoweth it, he shall not be able to find it,* that is, if he believes that he knows, he understands less, since it is said to the Romans, the first[^p71-3]: « Saying that they were wise, they became fools »; above, the seventh: « I said: I will be made wise: and it departed farther from me ». And he repeats the solicitude, adding that it was *curious;* for to apply solicitude where there is no usefulness is curiosity[^p71-4].

Therefore he says: *All these things have I treated in my heart, that I might understand curiously*[^p71-5]; and this is reproved in Ecclesiasticus, the third[^p71-6]: « In superfluous things search not much, and in his many works be not curious ». And thus the solicitude of distinguishing is clear, which was *diligent* and *fruitless,* and therefore *curious.*
