# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XVII, Vers. 12-19
## *Postquam petiit conservationem in bono, petit hic liberationem sive ereptionem a malo; et ponitur hic ipsa petitio vel oratio per hunc modum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 472 -->

23. *Cum essem cum eis* etc. Postquam petiit conservationem in bono, petit hic *liberationem* sive ereptionem *a malo;* et ponitur hic ipsa petitio vel oratio per hunc modum. Primo enim notatur *necessitas petendi;* secundo vero, *forma petitionis;* tertio vero, *ratio exaudiendi.* — *Necessitas* ergo *petendi* conservationem venit ex duplici causa: tum ex *absentatione conservatoris,* tum ex *immersione tribulationis.*

24. (Vers. 12.). *Absentabatur ab eis Christus,* qui eos conservaverat; propterea dicit: *Cum essem cum eis, ego servabam eos in nomine tuo.* Ipse conservavit discipulos in *nomine Patris,* quia *in virtute* Patris et quia *ad gloriam* Patris; et post Pater servavit eos in nomine Filii; unde supra decimo sexto[^p472-4]: « Si quid petieritis Patrem in nomine meo, dabit vobis ». Et bene utique servabat; propterea dicit: *Quos dedisti mihi ego*[^p472-5] *custodivi,* tanquam bonus praelatus, cui dicitur tertii Regum vigesimo: « Custodi virum istum, qui si lapsus fuerit, erit anima tua pro anima illius ». De hac custodia in Psalmo[^p472-6]: « Ecce, non dormitabit neque dormiet qui custodit Israel »; et rursus: « Nisi Dominus custodierit civitatem, frustra vigilat qui custodit eam »; unde Psalmus: « Custodi nos, Domine, ut pupillam oculi »; Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo: « Circumduxit eum et docuit et custodivit quasi pupillam oculi sui ». Et bene utique; unde dicit: *Et nemo ex his*[^p472-7] *periit nisi filius perditionis,* id est Iudas, qui *perditionis* dicitur, quia praescitus fuit ad mortem. Ipse enim perdidit se ipsum *temporaliter;* Matthaei vigesimo septimo[^p472-8]: « Abiit et laqueo se suspendit »; perdidit *aeternaliter;* Matthaei vigesimo sexto: « Vae homini illi! per quem Filius hominis tradetur; bonum erat ei, si natus non fuisset homo ille ». Eadem ratione de antichristo secundae ad Thessalonicenses secundo dicitur: « Tunc revelabitur homo peccati, filius perditionis ». Hic non periit casu nec Christi negligentia, sed propria malitia, quae ab aeterno fuit praescita et per Scripturam praenuntiata; ideo dicit: *Ut Scriptura impleatur,* illius Psalmi[^p472-9] scilicet: « Deus, laudem meam ». Ita ergo ego bene eos servabam, et tunc non indigebant, ut pro eis orarem.

25. (Vers. 13.). *Nunc autem ad te venio,* et eos dimitto, et ideo oro, ut ipsi mea oratione fiduciam concipiant; ideo dicit: *Haec*[^p472-10] *loquor in mundo,* id est, hanc orationem facio, ipsis audientibus; *ut habeant gaudium meum impletum in semetipsis,* ut per diffidentiam non turbentur, sed per spem exauditionis in se ipsis gaudeant, secundum illud supra decimo sexto: « Petite, et accipietis, ut gaudium vestrum sit plenum ». Ideo ipse petebat, ut ipsi concipiant gaudium plenum.

Haec est igitur prima necessitas, scilicet absentatio conservatoris; alia autem est *immersio tribulationis,* quae ex hoc irruitura erat super eos propter divini sermonis observantiam, propter quam mundus eos odio habebat. Propterea dicit:

26. (Vers. 14.). *Ego dedi eis sermonem tuum;* supra decimo quarto[^p472-11]: « Sermonem, quem audistis, non est meus, sed eius qui misit me, Patris ». Et ex hoc consecuti sunt odium mundi; ideo dicit: *Et mundus eos odio habuit.* Et ratio est, quia ipsi reliquerunt mundum; ideo dicit: *Quia non sunt de mundo, sicut ego*[^p472-12] *non sum de mundo,* id est a mundana conversatione segregati, sicut ego; supra decimo quinto[^p472-13]: « Si de mundo fuissetis, mundus quod suum erat diligeret; quia vero ego vos elegi de mundo, propterea odit vos mundus ». Apostoli segregati erant a mundo; unde ad Romanos primo dixit « Paulus, Apostolus Iesu Christi, segregatus », vere segregatus, quia ad Galatas ultimo dixit: « Mihi mundus crucifixus est, et ego mundo ».

27. (Vers. 15.). *Non rogo, ut tollas.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *forma petitionis,* in qua pe-
<!-- page 473 -->
tit, eos removeri a duplici malo, scilicet *perversae affectionis* et *erroris.* — Petit igitur, eos conservari a *perversa affectione,* ad quam mundus pertrahit, in quo conversari debebant; propterea dicit: *Non rogo, ut tollas eos de mundo,* per mortem scilicet carnis, sicut dicitur de Henoch Genesis quinto[^p473-1]: « Ambulavit Henoch cum Deo et non apparuit, quia tulit eum Deus ». Ad hoc tulit, ut servaret a malo; Sapientiae quarto: « Raptus est, ne malitia mutaret intellectum eius ». Non sic petebat eos servari, sed *in mundo;* ideo subdit: *Sed ut serves eos a malo.* Et ratio est, quare debent conservari in mundo:

28. (Vers. 16.). *De mundo non sunt, sicut ego*[^p473-2] *non sum de mundo,* scilicet conversatione; ideo petebat, eos salvari et servari, ne converterentur ad mundum per amorem; de qua servatione Apocalypsis tertio: « Quia servasti verbum patientiae meae, servabo et ego te ab hora tentationis, quae superventura est super orbem universum, tentare habitantes in terra ». Sic petit, eos servari a *malo praevaricationis;* petit etiam, servari a *malo erroris;* propter quod dicit:

29. (Vers. 17.). *Sanctifica eos in veritate. Sanctum,* hoc est sine terra[^p473-3]; *sanctificare,* hoc est mundare et purgare; *veritas* autem haec est in sermone Dei. Ideo dicit: *Sermo tuus veritas est;* et hoc sermone Dei sanctificantur discipuli, id est, ab errore purgantur. Chrysostomus[^p473-4]: « *Sanctifica eos in veritate,* id est, erudi eos, doce veritatem; recta enim dogmata sanctificant animam ». Novit enim verbum Dei purgare; ideo dictum est supra decimo quinto[^p473-5]: « Vos mundi estis propter sermonem, quem locutus sum vobis »; et ad Ephesios quinto: « Christus sanctificavit Ecclesiam, mundans eam lavacro aquae in verbo vitae ». De sanctificatione huiusmodi, secundae ad Timotheum secundo: « Si quis emundaverit se ab istis, erit vas sanctificatum, utile Domino, ad omne opus bonum paratum ».

30. (Vers. 18.). *Sicut tu me misisti.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *ratio exaudiendi,* quae duplex est, scilicet *commissum eis officium* et *pro eis oblatum sacrificium.* Ratione *commissi officii* debent servari et sanctificari, quia missi erant inter malos; ideo dicit: *Sicut tu me misisti in mundum,* ad passionem; *sic*[^p473-6] *et ego misi eos in mundum,* ad patiendum scilicet; unde Matthaei decimo: « Ecce, ego mitto vos sicut oves in medio luporum ». Ratione igitur *officii* dignum erat, ut servarentur; ratione etiam *sacrificii oblati;* ideo subdit:

31. (Vers. 19.). *Et ego pro eis sanctifico me ipsum;* Chrysostomus[^p473-7]: « Id est, hostiam sanctam offero. *Hostiae* omnes sanctae dicebantur »; ad Romanos duodecimo: « Exhibeatis corpora vestra hostiam viventem, sanctam, Deo placentem »; et sicut dicit Chrysostomus[^p473-8], « quia antiquitus sanctificatio erat in ove in typo, nunc autem non est in typo, sed in ipsa veritate; ideo ait: *Ut sint sanctificati in veritate* »; ad Hebraeos nono: « Si cinis vitulae aspersus et sanguis hircorum inquinatos sanctificat ad emundationem carnis; quanto magis sanguis Christi emundabit conscientiam nostram ab operibus mortuis ad serviendum Deo viventi »?

### Quaestiones.

32. Quaest. I. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Cum essem cum eis, ego servabam eos.*

1. Videtur ex hoc innuere in se impotentiam, quia in ipsa absentia eos servare non poterat.

2. Videtur etiam ex hoc, quod opera Trinitatis sint divisa[^p473-9] et alternatim fiant, quia prius Filius servavit, modo rogat Patrem, ut eos servet.
<!-- page 474 -->

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod dupliciter est *servare,* scilicet *effective* et *dispositive.* Primo modo est eius servare, cuius est *esse* dare; secundo modo potest esse alterius[^p474-1]. Primo modo Deus conservat hominem in bono; secundo, unus homo alium bona instructione et exemplo, ut praelatus subditos. — Dico igitur, quod Christus loquitur hic de conservatione, quae fuit *dispositive,* per modum dispositivum, scilicet exemplo et exhortatione et eruditione; et haec subtrahenda erat cum praesentia corporali[^p474-2]. Alio modo servabat *interius* cum Patre in quantum Deus; sed de ista non loquitur et hanc non subtrahebat, sed simul cum Patre exhibebat.

33. Quaest. II. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Quos dedisti mihi custodivi; et nemo periit ex eis nisi filius perditionis.*

**Contra:** Supra sexto[^p474-3]: *Omne, quod dat mihi Pater, ad me veniet;* et supra decimo: *Nemo rapit oves de manibus meis:* ergo nullus debuit de ovibus perire.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Pater dat Filio aliquos dupliciter, scilicet *aeterna praescientia quos praedestinavit et praescivit conformes fieri imaginis Filii sui*[^p474-4]; aliquos dat *secundum praesentem iustitiam.* — Cum igitur dicitur, quod non perdidit quemquam ex datis; loquitur *secundum praedestinationem;* cum excipit Iudam, loquitur *secundum iustitiam praesentem.*

34. Quaest. III. Quaeritur de hoc, quod petit, discipulos suos conservari *a malo.* — Videtur, quod inordinate petat:

1. Quia ipsi erant in caritate; sed ex quantulacumque gratia vel caritate potest quis quantaecumque tentationi resistere[^p474-5]: ergo etc.

2. Item, ab illa gratia non potest cadere nisi per peccatum mortale; non potest peccare nisi volens: ergo quod unusquisque conservetur, est in libertate nostrae voluntatis: ergo superflue petit.

Respondetur ad hoc, quod, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p474-6], *permanere* in bono accepto *divini* est *muneris* et *nostrae sollicitudinis;* gratia enim conservatur in nobis per continuam influentiam; et ideo petitur a Domino, ut continue adiuvet. Unde si possimus sola voluntate deficere; nisi interveniat divinum subsidium, non possumus permanere.

## English
<!-- page 472 -->

23. *While I was with them* etc. After he asked for conservation in good, he asks here for *liberation* or rescue *from evil;* and here is set down the petition itself or prayer in this manner. For first is noted *the necessity of asking;* second, *the form of the petition;* third, *the reason for being heard.* — The *necessity,* then, *of asking* for conservation comes from a twofold cause: both from *the absenting of the conserver,* and from *the immersion of tribulation.*

24. (Vers. 12.). *Christ was absenting himself from them,* he who had conserved them; therefore he says: *While I was with them, I kept them in thy name.* He conserved the disciples in *the name of the Father,* because *in the power* of the Father and because *to the glory* of the Father; and afterwards the Father kept them in the name of the Son; hence above, the sixteenth chapter[^p472-4]: « If you ask the Father any thing in my name, he will give it you ». And well indeed did he keep them; therefore he says: *Those whom thou gavest me I*[^p472-5] *have guarded,* as a good prelate, to whom it is said in the third of Kings, the twentieth chapter: « Guard this man, who, if he shall slip away, thy life shall be for his life ». Of this guarding in the Psalm[^p472-6]: « Behold, he shall neither slumber nor sleep, that guardeth Israel »; and again: « Unless the Lord guard the city, he watcheth in vain that guardeth it »; hence the Psalm: « Guard us, O Lord, as the apple of the eye »; Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter: « He led him about and taught him and guarded him as the apple of his eye ». And well indeed; hence he says: *And none of these*[^p472-7] *is lost but the son of perdition,* that is Judas, who is called *of perdition,* because he was foreknown unto death. For he lost himself *temporally;* Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p472-8]: « He went and hanged himself with a halter »; he lost himself *eternally;* Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter: « Woe to that man! by whom the Son of man shall be betrayed; it were better for him, if that man had not been born ». By the same reason it is said of the antichrist in the second to the Thessalonians, the second chapter: « Then shall be revealed the man of sin, the son of perdition ». This one was not lost by chance nor by the negligence of Christ, but by his own malice, which from eternity was foreknown and through Scripture foretold; therefore he says: *That the Scripture may be fulfilled,* namely of that Psalm[^p472-9]: « O God, my praise ». Thus, then, I kept them well, and then they did not need that I should pray for them.

25. (Vers. 13.). *But now I come to thee,* and I leave them, and therefore I pray, that they may conceive trust by my prayer; therefore he says: *These things*[^p472-10] *I speak in the world,* that is, I make this prayer, they hearing it; *that they may have my joy filled in themselves,* that they may not be troubled through distrust, but may rejoice in themselves through the hope of being heard, according to that above, the sixteenth chapter: « Ask, and you shall receive; that your joy may be full ». Therefore he himself asked, that they might conceive full joy.

This, then, is the first necessity, namely the absenting of the conserver; but the other is *the immersion of tribulation,* which from this was about to rush upon them because of the observance of the divine word, on account of which the world held them in hatred. Therefore he says:

26. (Vers. 14.). *I have given them thy word;* above, the fourteenth chapter[^p472-11]: « The word which you have heard is not mine, but his who sent me, the Father's ». And from this they incurred the hatred of the world; therefore he says: *And the world hath hated them.* And the reason is, because they left the world; therefore he says: *Because they are not of the world, as I*[^p472-12] *am not of the world,* that is, separated from worldly conversation, as I am; above, the fifteenth chapter[^p472-13]: « If you had been of the world, the world would love its own; but because I have chosen you out of the world, therefore the world hateth you ». The Apostles were separated from the world; hence to the Romans, the first chapter, Paul said « Paul, an Apostle of Jesus Christ, separated », truly separated, because to the Galatians, the last chapter, he said: « To me the world is crucified, and I to the world ».

27. (Vers. 15.). *I pray not that thou shouldst take.* Here is touched the second, namely *the form of the petition,* in which he asks
<!-- page 473 -->
that they be removed from a twofold evil, namely *of perverse affection* and *of error.* — He asks, then, that they be conserved from *perverse affection,* to which the world draws, in which they were to have their conversation; therefore he says: *I pray not that thou shouldst take them out of the world,* namely by the death of the flesh, as is said of Henoch in Genesis, the fifth chapter[^p473-1]: « Henoch walked with God and was seen no more, because God took him ». He took him for this, that he might keep him from evil; Wisdom, the fourth chapter: « He was taken away, lest wickedness should alter his understanding ». Not so did he ask that they be kept, but *in the world;* therefore he subjoins: *But that thou shouldst keep them from evil.* And the reason why they ought to be conserved in the world is:

28. (Vers. 16.). *They are not of the world, as I*[^p473-2] *am not of the world,* namely by conversation; therefore he asked that they be saved and kept, lest they should be turned to the world through love; of which keeping in the Apocalypse, the third chapter: « Because thou hast kept the word of my patience, I also will keep thee from the hour of temptation, which shall come upon the whole world, to try them that dwell upon the earth ». Thus he asks that they be kept from *the evil of prevarication;* he asks also that they be kept from *the evil of error;* on account of which he says:

29. (Vers. 17.). *Sanctify them in truth. Holy,* that is, without earth[^p473-3]; *to sanctify,* that is, to cleanse and to purge; and this *truth* is in the word of God. Therefore he says: *Thy word is truth;* and by this word of God the disciples are sanctified, that is, are purged from error. Chrysostom[^p473-4]: « *Sanctify them in truth,* that is, instruct them, teach the truth; for right dogmas sanctify the soul ». For the word of God knows how to purge; therefore it was said above, the fifteenth chapter[^p473-5]: « You are clean by reason of the word which I have spoken to you »; and to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter: « Christ sanctified the Church, cleansing it by the laver of water in the word of life ». Of sanctification of this kind, the second to Timothy, the second chapter: « If any man shall cleanse himself from these, he shall be a vessel sanctified, profitable to the Lord, prepared unto every good work ».

30. (Vers. 18.). *As thou hast sent me.* Here is touched the third, namely *the reason for being heard,* which is twofold, namely *the office committed to them* and *the sacrifice offered for them.* By reason of *the office committed* they ought to be kept and sanctified, because they were sent among the wicked; therefore he says: *As thou hast sent me into the world,* unto the passion; *so*[^p473-6] *I also have sent them into the world,* namely unto suffering; hence Matthew, the tenth chapter: « Behold, I send you as sheep in the midst of wolves ». By reason, then, of *the office* it was fitting that they should be kept; by reason also *of the sacrifice offered;* therefore he subjoins:

31. (Vers. 19.). *And I for them sanctify myself;* Chrysostom[^p473-7]: « That is, I offer a holy victim. All *victims* were called holy »; to the Romans, the twelfth chapter: « That you present your bodies a living victim, holy, pleasing unto God »; and as Chrysostom says[^p473-8], « because of old sanctification was in the sheep in type, but now it is not in type, but in the truth itself; therefore he says: *That they may be sanctified in truth* »; to the Hebrews, the ninth chapter: « If the ashes of a heifer sprinkled and the blood of goats sanctify the defiled to the cleansing of the flesh; how much more shall the blood of Christ cleanse our conscience from dead works, to serve the living God »?

### Questions.

32. Quaest. I. It is asked concerning what he says: *While I was with them, I kept them.*

1. He seems by this to intimate impotence in himself, because in his very absence he could not keep them.

2. It seems also from this that the works of the Trinity are divided[^p473-9] and are done alternately, because first the Son kept them, now he asks the Father to keep them.
<!-- page 474 -->

**I respond:** It must be said that *to keep* is twofold, namely *effectively* and *dispositively.* In the first mode, to keep belongs to him to whom it belongs to give *being;* in the second mode it can belong to another[^p474-1]. In the first mode God conserves man in good; in the second, one man another by good instruction and example, as a prelate his subjects. — I say, then, that Christ speaks here of the conservation which was *dispositive,* by a dispositive mode, namely by example and exhortation and erudition; and this was to be withdrawn with the bodily presence[^p474-2]. In the other mode he kept them *interiorly* together with the Father inasmuch as he is God; but of this he does not speak, and this he did not withdraw, but showed it together with the Father.

33. Quaest. II. It is asked concerning what he says: *Those whom thou gavest me I have guarded; and none of them is lost but the son of perdition.*

**On the contrary:** Above, the sixth chapter[^p474-3]: *All that the Father giveth to me shall come to me;* and above, the tenth chapter: *No one snatches the sheep out of my hands:* therefore none of the sheep ought to have perished.

**I respond:** It must be said that the Father gives some to the Son in two ways, namely *by eternal foreknowledge those whom he predestined and foreknew to be made conformable to the image of his Son*[^p474-4]; some he gives *according to present justice.* — When, then, it is said that he lost none of those given, he speaks *according to predestination;* when he excepts Judas, he speaks *according to present justice.*

34. Quaest. III. It is asked concerning this, that he asks that his disciples be conserved *from evil.* — It seems that he asks inordinately:

1. Because they were in charity; but by however little grace or charity one can resist however great a temptation[^p474-5]: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, from that grace one cannot fall except through mortal sin; one cannot sin except willingly: therefore that each one is conserved lies in the liberty of our will: therefore he asks superfluously.

It is answered to this that, as Augustine says[^p474-6], *to remain* in the good received is *of the divine gift* and *of our solicitude;* for grace is conserved in us through a continual influence; and therefore it is asked of the Lord, that he help continually. Hence if we can fail by the will alone, unless the divine aid intervene, we cannot remain.
