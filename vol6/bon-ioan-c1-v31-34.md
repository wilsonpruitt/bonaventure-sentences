# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. I, Vers. 31-34
## *Et ego nesciebam*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 260 -->

65. *Et ego nesciebam.* Posita est testificatio, ponitur hic eius *corroboratio* sive certitudo, quae describitur hoc ordine. Primo tangitur *modus* deveniendi in certitudinem; secundo, *signum* certificans; tertio, *testimonium* certum.

(Vers. 31.). Tangitur igitur primo *modus deveniendi in certitudinem,* qui fuit per actum baptizandi; ideo dicit: *Ego nesciebam eum*, id est, non eram certus de ipso; *sed ut manifestaretur in Israel, propterea veni ego in aqua baptizans.* Hoc ipsum dicebat Marci primo[^p260-6]: « Ego baptizavi vos aqua, ille autem baptizabit vos Spiritu sancto ».

66. (Vers. 32.). *Et testimonium perhibuit Ioannes.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *signum certificans,* quod quidem fuit descensus Spiritus sancti; ideo dicit: *Et testimonium perhibuit Ioannes*, tanquam certus, *dicens: Quia vidi Spiritum descendentem quasi columbam de caelo;* Lucae tertio[^p260-7]: « Descendit Spiritus sanctus corporali specie sicut columba in ipsum ». *Et manentem super eum*, quia in eo requievit; Isaiae undecimo: « Requiescet super eum Spiritus Domini ». Et hoc fuit signum certificans ipsum; ideo dicit:

67. (Vers. 33.). *Et ego nesciebam eum,* prius, sed hoc signo cognovi, quia datum fuit mihi a Deo; ideo subdit: *Sed qui misit me baptizare in aqua*, scilicet Deus; supra eodem[^p260-8]: « Fuit homo missus a Deo ». *Ille*, inquam, *mihi dixit: Super quem videris Spiritum descendentem et manentem super eum, ille est, qui baptizat in Spiritu sancto*, quia in eius solo nomine et virtute datur baptismus; Actuum secundo[^p260-9]: « Baptizetur unusquisque vestrum in nomine Christi ». Signum vero huius fuit descensio et mansio Spiritus sancti; unde Beda[^p260-10]: « In solo Mediatore Spiritus perpetuo mansit; quia ex eo tempore, ex quo homo fieri incepit, eius ministerio et opere conceptus est ». Ideo hoc signo fuit certificatus.

68. (Vers. 34.). *Et ego vidi* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *testimonium certum.* Certum enim est testimonium, quando homo testificatur de hoc quod vidit. Ideo dicit: *Et ego vidi et testimonium*
<!-- page 261 -->
*perhibui, quia hic est Filius Dei;* infra decimo nono[^p261-1]: « Et qui vidit testimonium perhibuit, et verum est testimonium eius ».

### Quaestiones.

69. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de descensu Spiritus sancti. — Si enim Spiritus sanctus est Deus; et Deus ubique est: ergo non descendit. — *Si tu dicas*, quod non descendit per *essentiam,* sed per *effectum; contra:* Nullus effectus de novo collatus est Christo, quia nullum munus gratiae habuit tunc, quod non habuisset a conceptione: ergo non descendit per *effectum.*

Quaest. II. Item quaero: quare magis dicitur descendisse in illa columba quam in alia? Aut enim uniebatur illi columbae, aut non; si sic: ergo assumsit illam creaturam; si non: ergo aeque erat in illa sicut in alia: ergo descendit in qualibet columba.

Quaest. III. Item, quare magis descendit in columba quam in aliquo alio?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Deus dicitur *esse in creaturis* quatuor modis: primo modo, pure per rationem *essendi;* et sic est in omnibus et aequaliter, nec descendit nec movetur nec recedit. — Secundo modo, per rationem *signandi* vel ostendendi; sic Deus dicebatur esse in his creaturis, quae ipsum signabant, sicut in monte Sinai et in nube[^p261-2]. — Tertio modo, per rationem *gratificandi;* sic dicitur esse in iis quos amat, et qui eum amant. — Quarto, per rationem *uniendi* vel assumendi; sic in Christo.

Primo igitur modo existendi, quia universalis est et communis, non respondet descensus vel ascensus; aliis modis sic. Unde Deus dicitur aliquando *descendere*, quando descendit creatura, quae ipsum signat; aliquando, quando confert gratiam; aliquando, quando descendit natura assumta[^p261-3]. — Quia igitur ad hoc erat illa columba instituta et specialiter ostendebat Spiritum, ideo dicitur *descendisse in ipsa*, non in alia, non ratione *effectus* vel *unionis*, sed *ostensionis*[^p261-4].

70. Quod quaeritur: quare magis se ostendit per columbam? Responsio est: propter proprietates columbae, quia columba est simplex animal, mansuetum et fructuosum. Spiritus vero sanctus est benignus et fructuosus et sine dolo; « Spiritus enim sanctus effugiet fictum[^p261-5] ».

71. Quaest. IV. Quaeritur etiam hic de hoc quod dicit: *Ego nesciebam eum.* — *Contra:* Testimonium perhibuit Ioannes de Christo, antequam baptizaret eum, sicut habitum est supra[^p261-6]: ergo aut cognovit ante, aut tulit testimonium de eo quod nesciebat.

Respondetur secundum Chrysostomum[^p261-7], quod cognovit eum in *generali;* bene enim sciebat, quod Christus venerat, et quod ipse eius praecursor erat; sed tamen eum personaliter et *singulariter* non agnoscebat, quia in deserto ab infantia fuerat et eum non viderat.

Sed haec responsio non potest stare: quia Ioannes cognovit Dominum in utero matris[^p261-8]; quomodo non cognovit, cum iam tantum creverat? — Item, Matthaei tertio[^p261-9] dicitur: « Ego a te debeo baptizari ». — Responsio est, quod cognovit, ipsum esse Christum; sed non cognovit, quod potestatem baptizandi reservasset sibi.

Sed tunc *obiicitur:* quia aut de potestate baptizandi exterius, aut de potestate baptizandi interius; utroque modo falsum, quia utrumque debebat scire.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quodsi loquatur de Christo quantum ad *substantiam,* quod non cognoscebat; intelligendum est: supple: ita certitudinaliter[^p261-10], ut cognovit post signum. Si quantum ad *virtutem baptizandi*, intelligendum, quod quintuplex[^p261-11] est potestas baptizandi, scilicet *auctoritatis, cooperationis, invocationis, excellentiae* et *ministerii.* — Primam nulli dedit nec dare potuit, ultimam dedit, tres intermedias non dedit; sed potestatem excellentiae et invocationis dare potuit, sed noluit, ne unitas Ecclesiae rumperetur. De potestate *cooperationis* dicit Magister Sententiarum[^p261-12], quod dubitavit, sed tamen communiter tenetur, quod illam non potuit dare; et de illa non dubitavit, sed de tertia et quarta potuit dubitare.

## English
<!-- page 260 -->

65. *And I knew not.* The testifying has been set down; here is set down its *corroboration* or certitude, which is described in this order. First is touched the *mode* of arriving at certitude; second, the certifying *sign;* third, the certain *testimony.*

(Vers. 31.). First, then, is touched the *mode of arriving at certitude,* which was through the act of baptizing; therefore he says: *I knew him not*, that is, I was not certain about him; *but that he may be made manifest in Israel, therefore am I come baptizing in water.* This very thing he was saying, Mark, the first[^p260-6]: « I have baptized you with water; but he shall baptize you with the Holy Spirit ».

66. (Vers. 32.). *And John gave testimony.* Here is touched the second, namely the *certifying sign,* which indeed was the descent of the Holy Spirit; therefore he says: *And John gave testimony*, as one certain, *saying: I saw the Spirit coming down as a dove from heaven;* Luke, the third[^p260-7]: « The Holy Spirit descended in a bodily shape, as a dove, upon him ». *And remaining upon him*, because he rested in him; Isaiah, the eleventh: « The Spirit of the Lord shall rest upon him ». And this was the sign certifying him; therefore he says:

67. (Vers. 33.). *And I knew him not,* before, but by this sign I knew him, because it was given to me by God; therefore he adds: *But he who sent me to baptize in water*, namely God; above in the same[^p260-8]: « There was a man sent from God ». *He*, I say, *said to me: He upon whom thou shalt see the Spirit descending and remaining upon him, he it is that baptizeth in the Holy Spirit*, because in his name and power alone is baptism given; Acts, the second[^p260-9]: « Let every one of you be baptized in the name of Christ ». But the sign of this was the descent and the abiding of the Holy Spirit; whence Bede[^p260-10]: « In the Mediator alone did the Spirit remain perpetually; because from that time from which he began to become man, he was conceived by his ministry and work ». Therefore he was certified by this sign.

68. (Vers. 34.). *And I saw* etc. Here is touched the third, namely the *certain testimony.* For testimony is certain when a man testifies about that which he has seen. Therefore he says: *And I saw, and*
<!-- page 261 -->
*I gave testimony, that this is the Son of God;* below, the nineteenth[^p261-1]: « And he that saw it hath given testimony, and his testimony is true ».

### Questions.

69. Quaest. I. But it is asked here first about the descent of the Holy Spirit. — For if the Holy Spirit is God, and God is everywhere: therefore he does not descend. — *If you say* that he does not descend by *essence,* but by *effect; on the contrary:* No effect was newly conferred on Christ, because he had no gift of grace then which he had not had from his conception: therefore he did not descend by *effect.*

Quaest. II. Likewise I ask: why is he said to have descended in that dove rather than in another? For either he was united to that dove, or not; if so: therefore he assumed that creature; if not: therefore he was equally in that one as in another: therefore he descended in any dove whatever.

Quaest. III. Likewise, why did he descend in a dove rather than in some other thing?

**I respond:** It must be said that God is said *to be in creatures* in four modes: in the first mode, purely by reason of *being;* and thus he is in all things, and equally, and neither descends nor is moved nor withdraws. — In the second mode, by reason of *signifying* or showing; thus God was said to be in those creatures which signified him, as on Mount Sinai and in the cloud[^p261-2]. — In the third mode, by reason of *gracing;* thus he is said to be in those whom he loves and who love him. — Fourth, by reason of *uniting* or assuming; thus in Christ.

To the first mode of existing, then, because it is universal and common, descent or ascent does not correspond; to the other modes it does. Hence God is said sometimes *to descend* when a creature descends which signifies him; sometimes, when he confers grace; sometimes, when an assumed nature descends[^p261-3]. — Because, then, that dove was appointed for this and specially showed the Spirit, therefore he is said *to have descended in it*, not in another, not by reason of *effect* or of *union*, but of *showing*[^p261-4].

70. As to what is asked: why does he show himself rather through a dove? The answer is: on account of the properties of the dove, because the dove is a simple animal, gentle and fruitful. But the Holy Spirit is benign and fruitful and without guile; « For the Holy Spirit will flee from the deceitful[^p261-5] ».

71. Quaest. IV. It is also asked here about what he says: *I knew him not.* — *On the contrary:* John bore testimony about Christ before he baptized him, as was had above[^p261-6]: therefore either he knew him before, or he bore testimony about what he did not know.

The reply is made according to Chrysostom[^p261-7] that he knew him in *general;* for he well knew that Christ had come, and that he himself was his forerunner; but yet he did not recognize him personally and *singularly*, because he had been in the desert from infancy and had not seen him.

But this reply cannot stand: because John knew the Lord in his mother's womb[^p261-8]; how did he not know him, when he had now grown so much? — Likewise, in Matthew, the third[^p261-9], it is said: « I ought to be baptized by thee ». — The reply is that he knew that he was the Christ; but he did not know that he had reserved the power of baptizing to himself.

But then it is *objected:* because either about the power of baptizing outwardly, or about the power of baptizing inwardly; in either way it is false, because he ought to have known both.

**I respond:** It must be said that, if he speaks of Christ as to *substance,* that he did not know him, it is to be understood — supply: so certitudinally[^p261-10] as he knew him after the sign. If as to the *virtue of baptizing*, it is to be understood that the power of baptizing is fivefold[^p261-11], namely of *authority,* of *cooperation,* of *invocation,* of *excellence* and of *ministry.* — The first he gave to no one, nor could he give it; the last he gave; the three intermediate ones he did not give; but the power of excellence and of invocation he could have given, but he did not will to, lest the unity of the Church be broken. Of the power of *cooperation* the Master of the Sentences[^p261-12] says that he doubted, but yet it is commonly held that he could not give it; and about that one he did not doubt, but about the third and fourth he could have doubted.
