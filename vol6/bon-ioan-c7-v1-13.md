# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. VII, Vers. 1-13
## *Pars prima de Doctoris propalatione agit dupliciter*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 338 -->

### Capitulum VII.

#### Tertio, Filius Dei se manifestat directorem et illustratorem tripliciter.

1. *Post haec*[^p338-9] *ambulabat Iesus* etc. Ostendit Dominus supra se curatorem et conservatorem; hic incipit tertia pars, in qua se manifestat et ostendit *directorem et illustratorem;* et haec pars extenditur usque ad undecimum capitulum. Ostendit Dominus se lucem et directorem tripliciter: primo, *verbo doctrinae;* secundo, *miraculo potentiae,* in principio noni, ibi[^p338-10]: *Et praeteriens Iesus vidit hominem caecum;* tertio, *exemplo bonae vitae,* infra in principio decimi: *Amen, amen dico vobis, qui non intrat per ostium.*

<!-- page 339 -->
#### Primo se ostendit lucem et directorem verbo doctrinae.

Prima igitur pars, in qua manifestat se *directorem verbo doctrinae*[^p339-1], continet duo capitula, scilicet septimum et octavum, et dividitur in duas, quia primo tangitur *Doctoris propalatio;* secundo, *doctrinae communicatio,* ibi[^p339-2]: *Nonne Moyses dedit vobis Legem* etc.

#### Pars prima de Doctoris propalatione agit dupliciter.

*Doctoris* autem *propalatio* vel manifestatio necessaria erat ad hoc, quod doceret. Et quia praecesserat eius occultatio, ideo primo agitur de *Doctoris occultatione;* secundo, de *occulti manifestatione,* ibi[^p339-3]: *Iam autem die festo mediante* etc.

*Doctoris* igitur summi *occultatio* hoc ordine describitur. Quia enim facta est per Iudaeorum persecutionem, primo insinuatur *Iudaeorum perfidia* ad persequendum; secundo vero, *consanguineorum vanitas* ad honorem persuadendum; tertio, *Christi humilitas* ad latendum; quarto, *Iudaeorum curiositas* ad Christum quaerendum.

2. (Vers. 1.). Insinuatur ergo primo *Iudaeorum perfidia* ad Christum occidendum, propter quam Dominus in Galilaea stabat et Iudaeam reliquerat, vitans furorem persequentium. Propterea dicit: *Post haec,* scilicet quae dicta sunt de miraculo panum et doctrina Christi[^p339-4], *ambulabat Iesus in Galilaeam;* quia ibi non erant ita zelatores Legis, ac per hoc nec persecutores. *Non enim volebat in Iudaeam ambulare;* et ratio redditur: *quia quaerebant eum Iudaei*
<!-- page 340 -->
interficere, a diabolo excitati, in cuius figura, Exodi secundo[^p340-1], « quaerebat Pharao occidere Moysen, qui fugiens de conspectu eius » etc. Hos vitando, furorem vitare persequentium docuit, secundum illud Matthaei decimo: « Cum vos persecuti fuerint in una civitate, fugite in aliam ».

3. (Vers. 2.). *Erat autem in proximo.* Notatur hic secundum, scilicet *consanguineorum vanitas* ad persuadendum honorem terrenum et vanum; et rationem persuadendi accipiunt ab *opportunitate temporis, aedificatione proximi* et *exaltatione nominis.* — *Opportunum tempus* aderat; unde dicit: *Erat autem in proximo dies festus Iudaeorum Scenopegia*[^p340-2]. Istud erat festum tabernaculorum, quod debebat fieri in Septembri, secundum illud Levitici vigesimo tertio: « Mense septimo festa celebrabitis et habitabitis in tabernaculis » etc. Ex hac opportunitate fit ei persuasio; unde dicit:

4. (Vers. 3.). *Dixerunt autem fratres eius ad eum: Transi hinc et vade in Iudaeam.* *Fratres* isti erant Domini cognati vel consanguinei in secundo gradu; et secundum Chrysostomum[^p340-3] fuerunt Iacobus et Iudas, qui erant fratres Domini; sed melius est dicere, quod essent alii cognati, propter litteram sequentem[^p340-4]. Credendum est enim, quod isti duo Apostoli credebant in eum. — Nota, quod est frater *natura,* sic omnes fratres Christi sumus; Psalmus[^p340-5]: « Narrabo nomen tuum fratribus meis ». *Affectione,* sic boni; Matthaei duodecimo: « Qui sunt fratres mei? Omnis, qui fecerit voluntatem » etc. *Cognatione,* sic consanguinei, ut Iacobus; ad Galatas primo: « Iacobum, fratrem Domini ». — Et rationem persuasionis aliam addunt, scilicet *proximi aedificationem;* propter quod dicunt: *Ut et discipuli tui videant opera tua, quae tu*[^p340-6] *facis;* discipuli, inquam, illi, qui scandalizati recesserant, ut videntes magna, quae faciebat in Galilaea, iterum redeant. De his dictum est supra sexto: « Multi ex discipulis eius abierunt retro ». — Et rationem tertiam subdunt, *nominis* scilicet *manifestationem.*

5. (Vers. 4.). *Nemo quippe in occulto quid facit, et quaerit ipse in palam esse,* per opus illud, scilicet tales adhuc non recesserant a consideratione hypocritarum, de quibus dicitur Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p340-7]: « Omnia opera sua faciunt, ut videantur ab hominibus ». Ideo dicebant: *Si haec facis, manifesta te ipsum mundo,* id est, si vera sunt quae facis miracula ut Deus, manifesta te ipsum. Dubitando hoc dicunt, et rationem reddit Evangelista:

6. (Vers. 5.). *Neque enim fratres eius credebant in eum;* supra quarto[^p340-8]: « Non est propheta sine honore nisi in patria sua »; supra primo: « In propria venit, et sui eum non receperunt », et hoc propter familiaritatem; hac causa, quia familiaris erat fratribus suis, non credebant in eum.

7. (Vers. 6.). *Dixit*[^p340-9] *ergo eis Iesus.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *Christi humilitas* in latitando; propter quod se apud eos excusat, primo a *defectu temporis opportuni;* non enim erat tempus opportunum sibi ad manifestationem. Propter quod dicit: *Tempus meum nondum advenit,* scilicet manifestationis meae; et ego congruitatem temporis considero; unde in Psalmo[^p340-10]: « Cum accepero tempus, ego iustitias iudicabo »; Ecclesiastae tertio: « Cuncta fecit Deus bona in tempore suo ». *Tempus autem vestrum semper est paratum,* id est gloriae mundi, quam affectatis; de quo tempore Ieremiae decimo septimo[^p340-11]: « Diem hominis non desideravi »; Ecclesiastici undecimo: « In die honoris tui ne extollaris ». — Sic igitur non mihi, sed vobis est opportunitas temporis; non mihi etiam, sed vobis est *exaltatio gloriae temporalis;* unde:

8. (Vers. 7.). *Non potest vos odisse mundus,* vituperando, quia ipsum et gloriam eius diligitis; *me autem odit,* inhonorando; ad Galatas ultimo[^p340-12]: « Mihi mundus crucifixus est, et ego mundo ». *Quia*
<!-- page 341 -->
*ego testimonium perhibeo de illo, quia opera eius mala sunt,* veritatem annuntiando; Sapientiae secundo: « Circumveniamus ergo iustum, quoniam inutilis est nobis et contrarius operibus nostris et improperat nobis peccata Legis et diffamat in nos peccata disciplinae nostrae »; Amos quinto: « Odio habuerunt corripientem in porta ». Ideo dixit quidam[^p341-1]: « Veritas odium parit ». Quia ergo vos mundus non odit;

9. (Vers. 8.). *Vos ascendite ad diem festum hunc;* quia vero odit me et persequitur, *ego autem non ascendam ad diem festum hunc*[^p341-2]*, quia tempus meum nondum est impletum;* in quo pati debeam, de quo supra secundo: « Mulier, nondum venit hora mea ». Sicut in incarnatione observavit temporis plenitudinem, sic et in passione; ad Galatas quarto: « At ubi venit plenitudo temporis, misit Deus Filium suum » etc. Et sic eis se excusavit, ut posset latenter ascendere; unde et post eos solus ascendit, non stipatus multitudine consanguineorum.

10. (Vers. 9.). *Haec cum dixisset,* id est, se apud fratres excusasset, *mansit*[^p341-3] *in Galilaea,* donec scilicet ascenderent fratres eius.

11. (Vers. 10.). *Ut autem ascenderunt fratres eius, tunc et ipse ascendit ad diem festum, non manifeste, sed quasi in occulto;* ideo *in occulto,* ut humanitatis ostenderet infirmitatem[^p341-4], et sic invitaret ad fidem. Vel: *in occulto,* ut doceat vanam vitare laudem; Matthaei sexto[^p341-5]: « Pater tuus, qui videt in abscondito, reddet tibi »; Gregorius: « Ita sit opus in manifesto, quatenus intentio maneat in occulto ».

12. (Vers. 11.). *Iudaei ergo quaerebant eum.* Tangitur quartum, scilicet *Iudaeorum curiositas* ad quaerendum Christum, cum latenter ascendisset; quia prius manifeste ascendebat; ideo dicit: *Iudaei ergo quaerebant eum in die festo;* quia tunc consueverat ascendere, et ex odio quaerebant; unde subdit: *Et dicebant: Ubi est ille?* Non eum nomine proprio nominabant ex odio vel contemptu; quocontra Sapientiae primo[^p341-6]: « Sentite de Domino in bonitate, et in simplicitate cordis quaerite illum ». Et ex hac inquisitione tacita fiebat collocutio de absente; unde dicit:

13. (Vers. 12.). *Et murmur multum erat in turba de eo,* et oriebatur ex controversia; unde subdit: *Quidam enim dicebant, quia bonus est,* ut parvuli; *alii autem*[^p341-7]*: Non, sed seducit turbas,* et isti erant Pharisaei maiores, qui vocaverunt eum *seductorem;* Matthaei vigesimo septimo[^p341-8]: « Recordati sumus, quia seductor ille dixit » etc.; infra eodem: « Nunquid et vos seducti estis »? Et licet esset controversia, erat sub murmure, non manifesta; unde dicit:

14. (Vers. 13.). *Nemo tamen palam de illo loquebatur propter metum Iudaeorum.* Sicut enim dicitur infra nono[^p341-9]: « Conspiraverant Iudaei, ut, si quis eum confiteretur Christum, extra Synagogam fieret ». Ideo timebant; quocontra Matthaei decimo: « Nolite timere eos qui occidunt corpus, animam autem non possunt occidere; sed potius timete eum qui potest et animam et corpus perdere in gehennam ».

### Quaestiones.

15. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic: cum Dominus venisset non tantum docere, verum etiam pati, videtur, quod propter passionem vel persecutionem Iudaeorum non debuit a doctrina cessare: ergo nec Iudaeam relinquere, ubi maxime locus erat doctrinae.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Dominus, etsi pati vellet et pati non timeret, tempus tamen passionis congruum praefixerat, et ante illud manibus persequentium se tradere non volebat; et ideo in Iudaeam non ambulabat[^p341-10].

16. Quaest. II. Sed quaeritur tunc secundo: cum Iudaei tempus determinatum a Domino non possent praevenire, videtur, quod ante illud non possent in eum manus mittere: ergo nec propter hoc debuit se absentare.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Dominus sic se manifestavit bonis, ut tamen se celaret malis; et e converso sic se occultavit malis, ut tamen non lateret bonos. Et ideo aliquando vicit per potentiam, aliquando per prudentiam, sicut dicitur Iob vigesimo sexto[^p341-11], quod *prudentia eius percussit superbum.* Et ideo, quamvis potenter posset cohibere, maluit tunc tamen sapienter declinare. — Alia ratio est, ut in se ostenderet veram humanitatem; ideo aliquando potentem, aliquando infirmum se ostendebat. Unde
<!-- page 342 -->
alia littera[^p342-1] habet: *Non enim poterat in Iudaea ambulare.* — Tertia ratio est nostra instructio, ut non miremur, si viri sancti timent et declinant pericula, nec etiam de nobis ipsis desperemus[^p342-2].

17. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Ego non ascendam;* et post dicitur, quod cum illi ascendissent, *tunc et ipse ascendit.* — Videtur, quod faciat contrarium eius quod praedixerat.

Respondetur ad hoc multipliciter: primo sic: *Ego non ascendam,* scilicet vobiscum, *vos* autem *ascendite*[^p342-3]. — Sed haec expositio habet quaestionem, quare scilicet noluerit ascendere cum eis.

Propter hoc respondent alii et faciunt vim in hoc quod dicitur: *ad diem festum hunc; interlinearis*[^p342-4]: « Id est ad primum diem », qui erat solemnior, sed ad dies intermedios. — Sed hoc similiter habet quaestionem, quare scilicet non ascendat ad primum diem.

Ideo tertio modo exponitur, quod « sermones accipiendi sunt secundum materiam[^p342-5] »; et Dominus respondebat suasioni fratrum, quod se manifestaret. Propterea dicit: *Non ascendam* ad me ipsum manifestandum; ideo dicitur in littera: *Non ascendit in manifesto.* Et ideo non ascendit cum fratribus nec in primo die, ne ascensus eius esset manifestus[^p342-6].

18. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de responsione Domini, qua dicit: *Tempus meum nondum advenit.* — Quamvis hoc non debeat intelligi de fato[^p342-7], videtur tamen, quod operatio Domini a tempore dependeat; quod videtur inconveniens, cum sit omnipotens.

Respondetur uno modo, quod duplex est Domini operatio: una secundum *Divinitatem,* et haec a tempore non dependet; alia vero secundum *humanitatem,* et haec certum tempus requirit. Et haec erat operatio redemptionis, et quantum ad hanc dicebat: *Tempus meum nondum advenit*[^p342-8].

Aliter respondetur, quod *determinato tempore operari,* hoc est dupliciter: vel ex *indigentia,* vel ex *congruentia.* Primo modo arbores in aestate tempore determinato fructificant; secundo modo Deus miseretur et iudicat et salvat[^p342-9]. Unde quia tempus paschale redemptioni nostrae optime congruebat, ut Lex in Evangelio secundum optimam convenientiam finiretur: ideo tempus paschale exspectabat.

## English
<!-- page 338 -->

### Chapter VII.

#### Third, the Son of God manifests himself as director and enlightener in three ways.

1. *After these things*[^p338-9] *Jesus walked* etc. Above, the Lord showed himself as curer and preserver; here begins the third part, in which he manifests and shows himself as *director and enlightener;* and this part extends as far as the eleventh chapter. The Lord shows himself light and director in three ways: first, *by the word of teaching*[^tr-doctrina]*;* second, *by the miracle of power,* at the beginning of the ninth, at the place[^p338-10]: *And Jesus passing by saw a man who was blind;* third, *by the example of a good life,* below, at the beginning of the tenth: *Amen, amen I say to you, he that entereth not by the door.*

<!-- page 339 -->
#### First he shows himself light and director by the word of teaching.

The first part, then, in which he manifests himself as *director by the word of teaching*[^p339-1], contains two chapters, namely the seventh and the eighth, and is divided into two, because first the *Doctor's disclosure* is touched on; second, *the communication of teaching,* at the place[^p339-2]: *Did not Moses give you the Law* etc.

#### The first part treats of the Doctor's disclosure in two ways.

Now the *Doctor's disclosure* or manifestation was necessary for this, that he should teach. And because his concealment had gone before, therefore first there is treated *the Doctor's concealment;* second, *the manifestation of the one concealed,* at the place[^p339-3]: *Now about the midst of the feast* etc.

The concealment, then, of the supreme *Doctor* is described in this order. For because it was brought about through the persecution of the Jews, first is intimated *the perfidy of the Jews* in persecuting; but second, *the vanity of his kinsmen* in persuading him to honour; third, *Christ's humility* in lying hidden; fourth, *the curiosity of the Jews* in seeking Christ.

2. (Verse 1.). First, then, is intimated *the perfidy of the Jews* in seeking to kill Christ, on account of which the Lord was staying in Galilee and had left Judea, avoiding the fury of his persecutors. Therefore he says: *After these things,* namely those that were said of the miracle of the loaves and of the teaching of Christ[^p339-4], *Jesus walked into Galilee;* because there were not such zealots of the Law there, and by that token not persecutors either. *For he would not walk in Judea;* and the reason is given: *because the Jews sought*
<!-- page 340 -->
to kill him, stirred up by the devil, in figure of whom, Exodus the second[^p340-1], « Pharao sought to kill Moses, who fleeing from his sight » etc. By avoiding these he taught us to avoid the fury of persecutors, according to that of Matthew the tenth: « When they shall persecute you in one city, flee into another ».

3. (Verse 2.). *Now there was at hand.* Here the second is noted, namely *the vanity of his kinsmen* in persuading him to an earthly and vain honour; and they take the grounds of their persuading from *the opportuneness of the time, the edification of the neighbour* and *the exaltation of his name.* — *The opportune time* was there; whence he says: *Now there was at hand the festival day of the Jews, Scenopegia*[^p340-2]. This was the feast of tabernacles, which was to be held in September, according to that of Leviticus the twenty-third: « In the seventh month you shall celebrate the feasts, and shall dwell in tabernacles » etc. From this opportuneness the persuasion is made to him; whence he says:

4. (Verse 3.). *And his brethren said to him: Pass from hence, and go into Judea.* These *brethren* were the Lord's kinsmen or blood relations in the second degree; and according to Chrysostom[^p340-3] they were James and Jude, who were brethren of the Lord; but it is better to say that they were other kinsmen, on account of the text that follows[^p340-4]. For it is to be believed that those two Apostles believed in him. — Note that one is a brother *by nature,* and so we are all brethren of Christ; the Psalm[^p340-5]: « I will declare thy name to my brethren ». *By affection,* and so are the good; Matthew the twelfth: « Who are my brethren? Whosoever shall do the will » etc. *By kinship,* and so are blood relations, as James; to the Galatians, the first: « James, the brother of the Lord ». — And they add another ground of persuasion, namely *the edification of the neighbour;* on account of which they say: *That thy disciples also may see thy works which thou*[^p340-6] *dost;* the disciples, I say, those who had been scandalized and had withdrawn, that seeing the great things which he was doing in Galilee, they might come back again. Of these it was said above, in the sixth: « Many of his disciples went back ». — And they subjoin the third ground, namely the *manifestation* of his *name.*

5. (Verse 4.). *For there is no man that doth any thing in secret, and he himself seeketh to be known openly,* by that work, namely such men had not yet withdrawn from the regard of hypocrites, of whom it is said in Matthew the twenty-third[^p340-7]: « All their works they do for to be seen of men ». Therefore they said: *If thou do these things, manifest thyself to the world,* that is, if the miracles which thou dost as God are true, manifest thyself. They say this doubting, and the Evangelist gives the reason:

6. (Verse 5.). *For neither did his brethren believe in him;* above, in the fourth[^p340-8]: « A prophet is not without honour, save in his own country »; above, in the first: « He came unto his own, and his own received him not », and this on account of familiarity; for this cause, because he was familiar with his brethren, they did not believe in him.

7. (Verse 6.). *Then*[^p340-9] *Jesus said to them.* Here the third is touched on, namely *Christ's humility* in lying hidden; on account of which he excuses himself before them, first by *the want of an opportune time;* for it was not an opportune time for him for his manifestation. On account of which he says: *My time is not yet come,* namely of my manifestation; and I consider the fittingness of the time; whence in the Psalm[^p340-10]: « When I shall take a time, I will judge justices »; Ecclesiastes the third: « God made all things good in their time ». *But your time is always ready,* that is, of the glory of the world, which you aspire to; of which time Jeremias the seventeenth[^p340-11]: « I have not desired the day of man »; Ecclesiasticus the eleventh: « In the day of thy honour be not exalted ». — So, then, the opportuneness of the time is not for me, but for you; nor again is *the exaltation of temporal glory* for me, but for you; whence:

8. (Verse 7.). *The world cannot hate you,* by reviling you, because you love it and its glory; *but me it hateth,* by dishonouring me; to the Galatians, the last[^p340-12]: « The world is crucified to me, and I to the world ». *Because*
<!-- page 341 -->
*I give testimony of it, that the works thereof are evil,* by announcing the truth; Wisdom the second: « Let us therefore lie in wait for the just, because he is not for our turn, and he is contrary to our doings, and upbraideth us with the sins of the Law, and divulgeth against us the sins of our discipline »; Amos the fifth: « They have hated him that rebuketh in the gate ». Therefore a certain man said[^p341-1]: « Truth begets hatred ». Since, then, the world does not hate you;

9. (Verse 8.). *Go you up to this festival day;* but since it hates me and persecutes me, *but I will not go up to this festival day*[^p341-2]*, because my time is not accomplished;* in which I am to suffer, of which above, in the second: « Woman, my hour is not yet come ». As in the incarnation he observed the fulness of time, so also in the passion; to the Galatians, the fourth: « But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent his Son » etc. And so he excused himself to them, that he might go up hiddenly; whence also he went up alone after them, not thronged by a multitude of kinsmen.

10. (Verse 9.). *When he had said these things,* that is, had excused himself before his brethren, *he stayed*[^p341-3] *in Galilee,* namely until his brethren should go up.

11. (Verse 10.). *But after his brethren were gone up, then he also went up to the feast, not openly, but, as it were, in secret;* therefore *in secret,* that he might show the infirmity of his humanity[^p341-4], and so invite to faith. Or: *in secret,* that he might teach us to avoid vain praise; Matthew the sixth[^p341-5]: « Thy Father, who seeth in secret, will repay thee »; Gregory: « Let the work be so in the open that the intention remains in secret ».

12. (Verse 11.). *The Jews therefore sought him.* The fourth is touched on, namely *the curiosity of the Jews* in seeking Christ, when he had gone up hiddenly; because before he used to go up openly; therefore he says: *The Jews therefore sought him on the festival day;* because at that time he had been accustomed to go up, and they sought him out of hatred; whence he subjoins: *And said: Where is he?* They did not name him by his proper name, out of hatred or contempt; against which Wisdom the first[^p341-6]: « Think of the Lord in goodness, and seek him in simplicity of heart ». And from this silent seeking there arose a talking together about the absent one; whence he says:

13. (Verse 12.). *And there was much murmuring among the multitude concerning him,* and it arose from controversy; whence he subjoins: *For some said: He is good,* as the little ones; *and others*[^p341-7]*: No, but he seduceth the multitudes,* and these were the greater Pharisees, who called him *seducer;* Matthew the twenty-seventh[^p341-8]: « We have remembered, that that seducer said » etc.; below, in the same: « Are you also seduced »? And though there was controversy, it was under a murmur, not open; whence he says:

14. (Verse 13.). *Yet no man spoke openly of him, for fear of the Jews.* For as it is said below, in the ninth[^p341-9]: « The Jews had conspired, that if any man should confess him to be Christ, he should be put out of the Synagogue ». Therefore they were afraid; against which Matthew the tenth: « Fear ye not them that kill the body, and are not able to kill the soul: but rather fear him that can destroy both soul and body in hell ».

### Questions.

15. Question I. But it is asked here: since the Lord had come not only to teach, but also to suffer, it seems that he ought not to have ceased from teaching on account of the passion or persecution of the Jews: therefore neither to have left Judea, where the place for teaching was greatest.

**I respond:** It must be said that the Lord, though he willed to suffer and did not fear to suffer, had nevertheless fixed beforehand a fitting time of the passion, and before it he did not will to hand himself over to the hands of his persecutors; and therefore he did not walk in Judea[^p341-10].

16. Question II. But it is then asked second: since the Jews could not forestall the time determined by the Lord, it seems that before it they could not lay hands on him: therefore neither ought he to have absented himself on that account.

**I respond:** It must be said that the Lord so manifested himself to the good as nevertheless to conceal himself from the wicked; and conversely so hid himself from the wicked as nevertheless not to be hidden from the good. And therefore he conquered sometimes through power, sometimes through prudence, as it is said in Job the twenty-sixth[^p341-11], that *his prudence hath struck the proud.* And therefore, although he could powerfully have restrained them, he nevertheless preferred then to withdraw wisely. — Another reason is, that he might show true humanity in himself; therefore he showed himself sometimes powerful, sometimes weak. Whence
<!-- page 342 -->
another text[^p342-1] has: *For he could not walk in Judea.* — The third reason is our instruction, that we should not wonder if holy men fear and avoid dangers, nor even despair of ourselves[^p342-2].

17. Question III. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *I will not go up;* and afterwards it is said that when they had gone up, *then he also went up.* — It seems that he does the contrary of what he had foretold.

This is answered in many ways: first thus: *I will not go up,* namely with you, but *you go up*[^p342-3]. — But this exposition raises a question, namely why he would not go up with them.

On account of this others answer and lay stress on what is said: *to this festival day;* the *interlinear*[^p342-4]: « That is, to the first day », which was the more solemn, but to the intermediate days. — But this likewise raises a question, namely why he does not go up to the first day.

Therefore it is expounded in a third way, that « words are to be taken according to their matter[^p342-5] »; and the Lord was answering the urging of his brethren, that he should manifest himself. Therefore he says: *I will not go up* to manifest myself; therefore it is said in the text: *He went up not openly.* And therefore he did not go up with his brethren nor on the first day, lest his going up should be manifest[^p342-6].

18. Question IV. Likewise it is asked about the Lord's answer, in which he says: *My time is not yet come.* — Although this ought not to be understood of fate[^p342-7], yet it seems that the Lord's operation depends on time; which seems unfitting, since he is omnipotent.

It is answered in one way, that the Lord's operation is twofold: one according to the *Divinity,* and this does not depend on time; but the other according to the *humanity,* and this requires a certain time. And this was the operation of redemption, and as regards this he said: *My time is not yet come*[^p342-8].

It is answered otherwise, that *to operate at a determined time* is in two ways: either from *need,* or from *fittingness.* In the first way trees bear fruit in summer at a determined time; in the second way God has mercy and judges and saves[^p342-9]. Hence, because the paschal time was best fitting for our redemption, that the Law might be ended in the Gospel according to the best fittingness: therefore he waited for the paschal time.
