# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XVI, Vers. 5-15
## *Primo consolatur discipulos ex Spiritus S. missione*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 457 -->

#### Tertio, circa exspectationem spei tria ponuntur.

10. *Haec autem vobis ab initio* etc. Supra[^p457-7] Dominus verbo eruditionis confirmavit discipulos in fide et in dilectione; in hac parte intendit eos confirmare *in spei exspectatione,* consolans eos multipliciter contra turbationem et desolationem de suo recessu. Et haec pars tertia partitur secundum triplex genus consolationis. Prima consolatio est ex *Spiritus sancti missione;* secunda vero, ex *Filii visitatione* et determinatur ibi[^p457-8]: *Modicum, et non videbitis me;* tertia est ex *Patris exauditione,* ibi: *Amen, amen dico vobis: Si quid petieritis Patrem* etc.; ut sic animam tota Trinitas consoletur. Prima consolatio est ad dandam *intelligentiam;* secunda, ad *laetitiam;* tertia, ad *pacem et confidentiam.* Prima respicit[^p457-9] *rationalem;* secunda, *concupiscibilem;* tertia vero respicit *irascibilem.*

#### Primo consolatur discipulos ex Spiritus S. missione.

Primo igitur consolatur eos de *Spiritus sancti missione;* et describitur haec consolatio per hunc modum. Primo praemittitur *discipulorum desolatio;* secundo, *Spiritus sancti promissio;* tertio, *missi officium;* quarto, *missionis principium.*

(Vers. 5.). Praemittit ergo primo *discipulorum desolationem* occasione sui recessus, cuius ratione etiam praedictas admonitiones fecerat; ideo dicit:

11. *Haec ab initio non dixi vobis*[^p457-10], scilicet quae praedicta sunt de Spiritus sancti missione et vestra persecutione; *quia vobiscum eram,* consolans vos mea praesentia, et ideo non dicebam vobis tristia; unde Matthaei nono[^p457-11]: « Non possunt filii sponsi lugere, quamdiu cum illis est sponsus ». *Et nunc vado ad eum qui misit me,* scilicet ad Patrem; ideo vobis praedico. Et ex hoc recessu facti erant discipuli non diligentes, sed tristes; unde dicit: *Et nemo ex vobis interrogat me: Quo vadis?* Audiebant et non intelligebant et quaerere negligebant; et hoc erat prae tristitia.
<!-- page 458 -->

12. (Vers. 6.). *Sed quia haec locutus sum vobis, tristitia implevit cor vestrum;* in quo significatur abundantia tristitiae, quia solum considerabant absentiam et non absentiae fructum; contra hoc dicitur primae ad Thessalonicenses quarto[^p458-1]: « Non contristemini sicut et ceteri, qui spem non habent ». Tales implentur tristitia; secundae ad Corinthios secundo: « Consolemini, ne forte abundantiori absorbeatur tristitia qui huiusmodi est »; Threnorum tertio: « Replevit me amaritudinibus, inebriavit me absinthio ».

13. (Vers. 7.). *Sed ego veritatem dico vobis* etc. Hic tangitur secundum, scilicet *Spiritus sancti promissio,* quae impletur in suo recessu, ex qua debent consolari; ideo dicit: *Sed ego veritatem* etc., quasi dicat: ita tristamini, sed e contrario debetis consolari de recessu. *Expedit vobis, ut ego vadam;* et ratio redditur: *Si enim non abiero, Paracletus non veniet ad vos.* Et ratio huius est: quia, nisi fueritis desolati, non consolabimini; Matthaei quinto[^p458-2]: « Beati qui lugent, quoniam ipsi consolabuntur ». Bernardus: « Delicata est nimis divina consolatio nec datur habentibus alienam ». *Si autem abiero, mittam eum ad vos;* quia, subtracta consolatione visibili, dabitur vobis consolator invisibilis; Isaiae vigesimo octavo[^p458-3]: « Quem docebit scientiam? Et quem intelligere faciet auditum? Ablactatos a lacte, avulsos ab uberibus ». Et ratio huius redditur ad Hebraeos quinto: « Omnis, qui lactis est particeps, expers est sermonis iustitiae; parvulus enim est ».

14. (Vers. 8.). *Et cum venerit ille.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *Spiritus sancti officium,* tum per comparationem ad *mundanos,* et hoc est *arguere;* tum per comparationem ad *bonos,* et hoc est *docere.* Officium igitur per comparationem ad *malos* exprimit, cum dicit: *Et cum venerit ille, arguet mundum de peccato et de iustitia et de iudicio.* Et ipse Salvator exponit:

15. (Vers. 9.). *De peccato quidem, quia non crediderunt in me;* et hoc est peccatum infidelitatis, de quo supra octavo[^p458-4]: « Vos in peccato vestro moriemini; si enim non credideritis, quia ego sum, moriemini in peccato vestro ».

16. (Vers. 10.). *De iustitia vero, quia ad Patrem vado, et iam non videbitis me;* Glossa[^p458-5]: « Non de iustitia, quam fecerint, sed quam nolunt imitari ». Et haec est iustitia fidei, quae credit quod non videt; ideo dicit: *Quia ad Patrem vado*[^p458-6] etc.; et tunc creditis, quia « fides est substantia rerum sperandarum » etc., ad Hebraeos undecimo. Et haec est iustitia, scilicet fidei, per quam iustus salvatur; ad Romanos tertio: « Arbitramur, hominem iustificari per fidem sine operibus Legis ».

17. (Vers. 11.). *De iudicio vero*[^p458-7]*, quia princeps huius mundi iam iudicatus est;* supra duodecimo: « Nunc iudicium est mundi; nunc princeps huius mundi eiicietur foras ».

18. Potest tamen aliter exponi. *Arguere* enim *mundum,* hoc est manifestare mundo, secundum illud ad Ephesios quinto[^p458-8]: « Quae arguuntur a lumine manifestantur ». Per adventum Spiritus sancti tria manifestata sunt mundo: *peccatum, iustitia* et *iudicium:* peccatum *mundi,* iustitia *Christi,* iudicium *diaboli.* Et ratio horum trium est: quia Christus *redemptor* erat mundi, diabolus *deceptor,* et mundus *detinebatur a diabolo* propter peccatum infidelitatis; ad Ephesios sexto[^p458-9]: « Adversus mundi rectores tenebrarum harum ». — Et de hoc *peccato* Spiritus sanctus *arguit mundum:* dum ostendit ei suam *infidelitatem,* arguit de peccato. Et huic concordat littera dicens: *De peccato, quia non crediderunt*[^p458-10].

*Christus* vero mundum redemit *sua iustitia.* Quia enim iustus fuit et pati non meruit, patiendo
<!-- page 459 -->
pro mundo ipsum salvavit; in cuius iustitiae attestationem sublimatus est ad dexteram Patris. Et huic concordat littera: *De iustitia,* scilicet mea, *quia vado ad Patrem,* per sublimationem, et hoc merito *iustitiae;* ad Philippenses secundo[^p459-1]: « Humiliavit semetipsum factus obediens usque ad mortem, mortem autem crucis. Propter quod et Deus exaltavit illum et donavit illi nomen, quod est super omne nomen ».

*Diabolus* vero iniuste mundum detinebat et iniuste contra Christum pugnavit; ideo *iudicatus* est, quia amisit dominium in genus humanum[^p459-2]. Et huic consonat sequens littera: *De iudicio vero, quia princeps huius mundi iam iudicatus est,* id est, in causa Salvatoris reus factus est. — Legatur ergo sic littera: *Arguet mundum de peccato,* scilicet suo, *quia non crediderunt* etc.; *de iustitia,* scilicet mea, *quia ad Patrem vado,* in huius testimonium; *de iudicio,* scilicet diaboli, *quia princeps huius mundi iam iudicatus est.*

Sic determinavit officium eius ad *mundanos;* deinde subiungit officium eius ad *discipulos,* et hoc est *docere,* quia non plene potuerunt doceri in praesentia Christi; ideo dicit:

19. (Vers. 12.). *Adhuc habeo multa vobis dicere; sed non potestis portare modo;* et ratio huius erat, quia adhuc carnales erant; unde primae ad Corinthios tertio[^p459-3]: « Non potui vobis loqui quasi spiritualibus, sed quasi carnalibus. Nondum enim poteratis, sed nec adhuc potestis ». Sed ista carnalitas amovenda erat per adventum Spiritus sancti; ideo subdit:

20. (Vers. 13.). *Cum autem venerit ille Spiritus veritatis, docebit vos omnem veritatem;* unde primae Ioannis secundo[^p459-4]: « Unctio docebit vos de omnibus ». Et quod doceat *veritatem,* ostendit per hoc, quod non a se, sed ab alio; propterea dicit: *Non enim loquitur*[^p459-5] *a semetipso,* sicut diabolus, qui « mendax est et pater eius », supra octavo: « Cum loquitur mendacium, ex propriis loquitur ». *Sed quaecumque audiet, loquetur,* ideo vera loquetur; supra octavo[^p459-6]: « Qui misit me verax est, et ego quae audivi ab eo, haec loquor ». Nec tantum docebit veritatem de praeteritis, sed etiam de futuris; unde: *Et quae ventura sunt annuntiabit vobis;* unde primae ad Timotheum quarto: « Spiritus manifeste dicit, quod in novissimis diebus discedent quidam a fide ».

21. (Vers. 14.). *Ille me clarificabit.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *Spiritus sancti principium;* et hoc quidem non tantum est Pater, verum etiam Filius; et ideo, sicut Filius, quia procedit a Patre, clarificat in suis operibus Patrem, a quo est[^p459-7]; sic et Spiritus sanctus Filium. Propterea dicit: *Ille me clarificabit;* et ratio redditur: *Quia de meo accipiet et annuntiabit vobis.* Cum Spiritus sanctus sit summe simplex, est quidquid habet: ergo si accipit aliquid a Filio, totum accipit, et ideo sequitur, quod a Filio procedit[^p459-8]. Sed ne credas ex hoc, quod ab ipso *solo* procedat, vel ab ipso *principaliter;* ideo ostendit, quod ab ipso procedit, una cum procedit a Patre. Propterea subdit:

22. (Vers. 15.). *Omnia, quae*[^p459-9] *habet Pater, mea sunt;* infra decimo septimo: « Mea omnia tua sunt, et tua mea ». *Propterea dixi: Quia de meo accipiet et annuntiabit vobis; de meo,* quod tamen a Patre accepi. Sic Spiritus sanctus enuntiat Filium, a quo procedit, sicut et Filius Patrem; supra primo[^p459-10]: « Unigenitus Filius, qui est in sinu Patris, ipse enarravit ».

### Quaestiones.

23. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod dicit: *Nemo ex vobis interrogat me: Quo vadis?*

**Contra:** Supra decimo tertio[^p459-11]: *Dicit ei Petrus: Domine, quo vadis?*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Dominus ibat ad ignominiam passionis, ibat ad gloriam resurrectionis; et cum prius loqueretur, quod iret ad passionem, quaesivit Petrus: *Quo vadis?* Sed modo vult eis dicere, quod vadit ad gloriam resurrectionis; unde: *Vado ad eum qui misit me.* Et ipsi tantum turbati erant de itinere ad passionem, quod nihil cogitabant de gloria. Ideo de hac non quaerebant; sed Dominus volebat, quod quaererent, ut ipsos consolaretur[^p459-12].

24. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Si non abiero, Paracletus non veniet ad vos.*

Videtur ex hoc, quod Christus non fuerit omnipotens, quia Spiritum non poterat dare, nisi personaliter iret. — *Si tu dicas,* quod hoc non fuit propter Christi impotentiam, sed propter discipulorum ineptitudinem[^p459-13]; *contra* hoc est, quia Christi
<!-- page 460 -->
praesentia potius disponebat ad gratiam, quam impediret; quod patet, quia ante adventum Christi non fuit tempus gratiae, sed post. — Respondetur, quod impediebantur, quia carnaliter ipsum amabant[^p460-1]; sed hoc nihil est, quia in illo amore non peccabant: ergo non repugnabat gratiae, unde simul manebat cum caritate. — *Si tu dicas,* quod non repugnat ut incompossibile, sed tamen ut impediens; hoc nihil est, quia, quando aliquis amat aliquem sensibiliter et spiritualiter, ferventius diligit, quia dilectio naturalis intendit spiritualem.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod triplici ex causa non fuit datus Spiritus sanctus ante ascensum Christi. Una ratio est ex parte *suscipientium,* quia, consolati sensibiliter praesentia corporali Christi, non desiderabant aliam consolationem. Deus autem non vult dona sua contemni, et ideo non dat nisi merentibus; ideo oportuit, Christum ab eis separari[^p460-2]. — Alia ratio ex parte *mittentis,* quia non decet, miserum dotare suos servitores, nisi ipse appareat gloriosus; ideo dicitur supra septimo[^p460-3]: *Spiritus nondum erat datus, quia Iesus nondum erat glorificatus.* — Tertia ratio est ex parte *utriusque,* quia adhuc manebant inimicitiae inter nos et Deum; et ideo primo oportuit fieri reconciliationem[^p460-4] quam Spiritus sancti donationem. Ideo prius oportuit Christum pati, quam Spiritum sanctum mitti.

25. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *cum venerit Spiritus, docebit vos omnem veritatem.*

1. Primo videtur male dicere, quod *docebit;* quia *docere* est actus sapientiae, et sapientia appropriatur Filio, non Spiritui sancto[^p460-5].

2. Item, quomodo *docebit omnem veritatem?* Secundum hoc videtur, quod Apostoli sciverunt geometriam et omnes artes; et illud falsum est, quia etiam male sciebant grammaticam; unde Apostolus dicebat: *Etsi imperitus* sum *sermone, non tamen scientia,* secundae ad Corinthios undecimo[^p460-6].

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *docere* totius Trinitatis est, tamen aliquando tribuitur Filio, aliquando Spiritui sancto; quia duplex est cognitio, scilicet *speculationis,* et haec attribuitur Filio; et *experientiae et devotionis*[^p460-7], et haec attribuitur unctioni Spiritus sancti; et de hac loquitur hic.

2. Et per hoc patet solutio ad sequens. Intelligit enim non de *omni veritate simpliciter,* sed *secundum pietatem*[^p460-8]; et haec est veritas necessaria ad salutem, quam plene cognoverunt Apostoli.

Vel dicendum, quod non intelligit hoc, secundum quod *omnis* distribuit pro *singulis generum,* sed pro *generibus singulorum*[^p460-9]; est enim veritas *doctrinae, vitae* et *iustitiae,* et has docuit. De veritate *doctrinae;* Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p460-10]: *Magister, scimus, quia verax es et viam Dei in veritate doces.* — De veritate *vitae;* Isaiae trigesimo octavo: *Memento, Domine, quomodo ambulaverim coram te in veritate.* — De veritate *iustitiae;* Proverbiorum decimo octavo: *Accipere personam in iudicio non est bonum, ut declines a veritate iudicii*[^p460-11].

26. Quaest. IV. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *De meo accipiet.*

1. Videtur falsum; quia, si Spiritus sanctus est Deus; et Deus nihil novum potest habere: ergo nihil amplius potest accipere. Quid est ergo, quod dicit: *De meo accipiet?*
<!-- page 461 -->

2. Item quaeritur de ratione Domini: *Propterea,* inquit, *dixi: De meo accipiet, quia omnia, quae habet Pater, mea sunt.* — Aut *omnia* distribuit pro *essentialibus,* aut pro *proprietatibus;* si pro *proprietatibus,* falsum est, quia Filius non habet innascibilitatem. Si pro *essentialibus;* sed ex hoc non sequitur, quodsi Spiritus sanctus procedat a Patre, quod propter hoc procedat a Filio, quia *procedere* est personale; per eandem rationem posset dicere Spiritus sanctus de Filio: *De meo accipiet,* quia omnia, quae habet Pater, habet Spiritus sanctus[^p461-1].

Ad haec respondent Graeci, quod duplex est Spiritus sancti processio, *aeterna* scilicet et *temporalis;* quantum ad *aeternam* a solo Patre procedit, et sic accipit a Patre et non a Filio; quantum ad *temporalem* a Filio procedit; in cuius rei testimonium dicitur: *accipiet,* quia temporaliter procedit. Et rationem erroris sui accipiunt ab hoc sermone Domini; quia Dominus aliquando dicit, quod *mittet* Spiritum sanctum, sed nunquam dicit, quod *a se procedat,* semper, quod a Patre[^p461-2]. — Sed tamen, salva pace sua, nihil dicunt, quia, sicut patet ex hac littera, Spiritus sanctus *accipit a Filio.* Quid accipit? Quidquid accipiat, hoc non est alterum quam ipse, cum summe simplex sit: ergo si *se accipit,* quomodo potest accipere ex tempore et non esse temporalis?

Propterea dicendum, quod Spiritus sanctus *procedit* a Filio aeternaliter et temporaliter *mittitur* in creaturam[^p461-3].

1. Quod ergo dicitur *accipiet,* respondetur in Glossa[^p461-4]: « Quod aeternitas comprehendit omne tempus propter diversas proprietates; unde de futuro dicitur *accipiet,* non quia novum accipiat, sed quia non desinit ». — Dicendum tamen, quod *accipiet* idem est, ac si diceret: se accepisse monstrabit.

2. Ad aliud dicendum, quod illa non est ratio *cogens,* quare Spiritus sanctus procedat a Filio; sed illa est ratio *dicti,* scilicet quomodo dixerat: *De meo accipiet,* non quia de proprio, excludendo Patrem, sed quia simul cum Patre[^p461-5]. Unde ea ratione dictum est, qua dicitur: *Omnia, quae habet Pater, mea sunt.*

## English

#### Third, concerning the expectation of hope three things are set down.

10. *But these things to you from the beginning* etc. Above[^p457-7] the Lord by the word of erudition confirmed the disciples in faith and in dilection; in this part he intends to confirm them *in the expectation of hope,* consoling them manifoldly against the troubling and desolation over his withdrawal. And this third part is divided according to a threefold kind of consolation. The first consolation is from the *mission of the Holy Spirit;* the second, from the *visitation of the Son,* and it is settled there[^p457-8]: *A little while, and you shall not see me;* the third is from the *hearing of the Father,* there: *Amen, amen I say to you: If you ask the Father anything* etc.; so that thus the whole Trinity may console the soul. The first consolation is for giving *intelligence;* the second, for *joy;* the third, for *peace and confidence.* The first regards[^p457-9] the *rational;* the second, the *concupiscible;* but the third regards the *irascible.*

#### First he consoles the disciples from the mission of the Holy Spirit.

First, then, he consoles them concerning the *mission of the Holy Spirit;* and this consolation is described in this mode. First there is set before us the *desolation of the disciples;* second, the *promise of the Holy Spirit;* third, the *office of the one sent;* fourth, the *principle of the mission.*

(Verse 5.). He sets before us, then, first the *desolation of the disciples* on the occasion of his withdrawal, by reason of which he had also made the aforesaid admonitions; therefore he says:

11. *These things I told you not from the beginning*[^p457-10], namely those which have been foretold of the mission of the Holy Spirit and of your persecution; *because I was with you,* consoling you with my presence, and therefore I did not tell you sad things; whence Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p457-11]: « The children of the bridegroom cannot mourn, as long as the bridegroom is with them ». *And now I go to him that sent me,* namely to the Father; therefore I foretell to you. And from this withdrawal the disciples had become not loving, but sad; whence he says: *And none of you asketh me: Whither goest thou?* They heard and did not understand, and neglected to ask; and this was for sorrow.

12. (Verse 6.). *But because I have spoken these things to you, sorrow hath filled your heart;* in which is signified the abundance of sorrow, because they considered only the absence and not the fruit of the absence; against this it is said in the first to the Thessalonians, the fourth chapter[^p458-1]: « Be not sorrowful, even as others who have no hope ». Such men are filled with sorrow; the second to the Corinthians, the second chapter: « Comfort, lest perhaps such an one be swallowed up with overmuch sorrow »; Lamentations, the third chapter: « He hath filled me with bitterness, he hath inebriated me with wormwood ».

13. (Verse 7.). *But I tell you the truth* etc. Here the second is touched, namely the *promise of the Holy Spirit,* which is fulfilled in his withdrawal, from which they ought to be consoled; therefore he says: *But I the truth* etc., as if to say: so you are sorrowful, but on the contrary you ought to be consoled over the withdrawal. *It is expedient to you that I go;* and the reason is given: *For if I go not, the Paraclete will not come to you.* And the reason of this is: because, unless you have been made desolate, you shall not be consoled; Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p458-2]: « Blessed are they that mourn, for they shall be consoled ». Bernard: « Divine consolation is exceedingly delicate, nor is it given to those who have an alien one ». *But if I go, I will send him to you;* because, the visible consolation being withdrawn, an invisible consoler will be given to you; Isaias, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p458-3]: « Whom shall he teach knowledge? And whom shall he make to understand the hearing? Them that are weaned from the milk, that are drawn away from the breasts ». And the reason of this is given to the Hebrews, the fifth chapter: « Every one that is a partaker of milk is unskilful in the word of justice: for he is a little child ».

14. (Verse 8.). *And when he is come.* Here the third is touched, namely the *office of the Holy Spirit,* both in comparison to *worldly men,* and this is *to reprove;* and in comparison to the *good,* and this is *to teach.* The office, then, in comparison to the *wicked* he expresses, when he says: *And when he is come, he will reprove the world of sin, and of justice, and of judgement.* And the Saviour himself expounds:

15. (Verse 9.). *Of sin indeed, because they believed not in me;* and this is the sin of unbelief, of which above, the eighth chapter[^p458-4]: « You shall die in your sin; for if you believe not that I am he, you shall die in your sin ».

16. (Verse 10.). *And of justice, because I go to the Father, and you shall see me no longer;* the Gloss[^p458-5]: « Not of the justice which they have done, but which they will not imitate ». And this is the justice of faith, which believes what it does not see; therefore he says: *Because I go to the Father*[^p458-6] etc.; and then you believe, because « faith is the substance of things to be hoped for » etc., to the Hebrews, the eleventh chapter. And this is the justice, namely of faith, by which the just man is saved; to the Romans, the third chapter: « We account a man to be justified by faith without the works of the Law ».

17. (Verse 11.). *And of judgement*[^p458-7]*, because the prince of this world is already judged;* above, the twelfth chapter: « Now is the judgement of the world; now shall the prince of this world be cast out ».

18. It can, however, be expounded otherwise. For *to reprove the world* is to manifest to the world, according to that to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter[^p458-8]: « The things that are reproved are made manifest by the light »[^tr-lumen]. By the coming of the Holy Spirit three things were made manifest to the world: *sin, justice* and *judgement:* the sin *of the world,* the justice *of Christ,* the judgement *of the devil.* And the reason of these three is: because Christ was the *redeemer* of the world, the devil the *deceiver,* and the world *was held by the devil* on account of the sin of unbelief; to the Ephesians, the sixth chapter[^p458-9]: « Against the rulers of the world of this darkness ». — And of this *sin* the Holy Spirit *reproves the world:* while he shows it its *unbelief,* he reproves of sin. And with this the letter agrees, saying: *Of sin, because they believed not*[^p458-10].

But *Christ* redeemed the world *by his justice.* For because he was just and did not deserve to suffer, by suffering
<!-- page 459 -->
for the world he saved it; in attestation of which justice he was exalted to the right hand of the Father. And with this the letter agrees: *Of justice,* namely mine, *because I go to the Father,* by exaltation, and this by the merit of *justice;* to the Philippians, the second chapter[^p459-1]: « He humbled himself, becoming obedient unto death, even to the death of the cross. For which cause God also hath exalted him, and hath given him a name which is above all names ».

But the *devil* unjustly held the world and unjustly fought against Christ; therefore he is *judged,* because he lost dominion over the human race[^p459-2]. And with this the following letter is in accord: *And of judgement, because the prince of this world is already judged,* that is, in the Saviour's cause he was made guilty. — Let the letter, then, be read thus: *He will reprove the world of sin,* namely its own, *because they believed not* etc.; *of justice,* namely mine, *because I go to the Father,* in testimony of this; *of judgement,* namely the devil's, *because the prince of this world is already judged.*

Thus he has settled his office toward *worldly men;* next he adds his office toward the *disciples,* and this is *to teach,* because they could not be fully taught in the presence of Christ; therefore he says:

19. (Verse 12.). *I have yet many things to say to you; but you cannot bear them now;* and the reason of this was, because they were still carnal; whence the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p459-3]: « I could not speak to you as unto spiritual, but as unto carnal. For you were not able as yet. But neither indeed are you now able ». But this carnality was to be removed by the coming of the Holy Spirit; therefore he adds:

20. (Verse 13.). *But when he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will teach you all truth;* whence the first of John, the second chapter[^p459-4]: « The unction will teach you of all things ». And that he teaches the *truth,* he shows by this, that it is not from himself, but from another; therefore he says: *For he speaketh not*[^p459-5] *of himself,* as the devil, who « is a liar, and the father thereof », above, the eighth chapter: « When he speaketh a lie, he speaketh of his own ». *But what things soever he shall hear, he shall speak,* therefore he shall speak true things; above, the eighth chapter[^p459-6]: « He that sent me is true; and the things I have heard of him, these same I speak ». Nor will he teach truth only of things past, but also of things to come; whence: *And the things that are to come he shall shew you;* whence the first to Timothy, the fourth chapter: « The Spirit manifestly saith, that in the last days some shall depart from the faith ».

21. (Verse 14.). *He shall glorify me.* Here the fourth is touched, namely the *principle of the Holy Spirit;* and this indeed is not only the Father, but also the Son; and therefore, as the Son, because he proceeds from the Father, glorifies in his works the Father, from whom he is[^p459-7]; so also the Holy Spirit the Son. Therefore he says: *He shall glorify me;* and the reason is given: *Because he shall receive of mine, and shew it to you.* Since the Holy Spirit is supremely simple, he is whatever he has: therefore if he receives anything from the Son, he receives the whole, and therefore it follows that he proceeds from the Son[^p459-8]. But lest you believe from this that he proceeds from him *alone,* or from him *principally;* therefore he shows that he proceeds from him, together with his proceeding from the Father. Therefore he adds:

22. (Verse 15.). *All things, that*[^p459-9] *the Father hath, are mine;* below, the seventeenth chapter: « All my things are thine, and thine are mine ». *Therefore I said: Because he shall receive of mine, and shew it to you; of mine,* which nevertheless I received from the Father. Thus the Holy Spirit declares the Son, from whom he proceeds, as also the Son the Father; above, the first chapter[^p459-10]: « The only begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him ».

### Questions.

23. Question I. But it is asked here first concerning what he says: *None of you asketh me: Whither goest thou?*

**On the contrary:** Above, the thirteenth chapter[^p459-11]: *Peter saith to him: Lord, whither goest thou?*

**I respond:** It must be said that the Lord was going to the ignominy of the passion, was going to the glory of the resurrection; and when he spoke earlier, that he was going to the passion, Peter asked: *Whither goest thou?* But now he wishes to say to them that he goes to the glory of the resurrection; whence: *I go to him that sent me.* And they themselves were so troubled over the journey to the passion that they thought nothing of the glory. Therefore of this they did not ask; but the Lord wished that they should ask, that he might console them[^p459-12].

24. Question II. Likewise it is asked concerning what he says: *If I go not, the Paraclete will not come to you.*

It seems from this that Christ was not omnipotent, because he could not give the Spirit, unless he went personally. — *If thou say* that this was not on account of Christ's impotence, but on account of the disciples' unfitness[^p459-13]; *on the contrary* this is, because Christ's
<!-- page 460 -->
presence disposed to grace rather than hindered it; which is plain, because before the coming of Christ it was not the time of grace, but after. — It is responded that they were hindered, because they loved him carnally[^p460-1]; but this is nothing, because in that love they did not sin: therefore it was not repugnant to grace, whence it remained at once with charity. — *If thou say* that it is not repugnant as incompossible, but yet as hindering; this is nothing, because, when one loves someone sensibly and spiritually, he loves more fervently, because natural dilection intensifies the spiritual.

**I respond:** It must be said that for a threefold cause the Holy Spirit was not given before the ascent of Christ. One reason is on the part of *those receiving,* because, consoled sensibly by the bodily presence of Christ, they did not desire another consolation. But God does not wish his gifts to be contemned, and therefore he gives only to the deserving; therefore it behoved that Christ be separated from them[^p460-2]. — Another reason on the part of the *sender,* because it is not fitting that a wretched man endow his servitors, unless he himself appear glorious; therefore it is said above, the seventh chapter[^p460-3]: *As yet the Spirit was not given, because Jesus was not yet glorified.* — The third reason is on the part of *both,* because enmities still remained between us and God; and therefore it behoved that reconciliation[^p460-4] come first rather than the donation of the Holy Spirit. Therefore it behoved Christ to suffer before the Holy Spirit was sent.

25. Question III. Likewise it is asked concerning what he says, that *when the Spirit is come, he will teach you all truth.*

1. First, it seems that he says ill that *he will teach;* because *to teach* is the act of wisdom, and wisdom is appropriated to the Son, not to the Holy Spirit[^p460-5].

2. Likewise, how *will he teach all truth?* According to this it seems that the Apostles knew geometry and all the arts; and that is false, because they even knew grammar ill; whence the Apostle said: *Although* I am *rude in speech, yet not in knowledge,* the second to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter[^p460-6].

**I respond:** It must be said that *to teach* belongs to the whole Trinity, yet it is sometimes attributed to the Son, sometimes to the Holy Spirit; because cognition is twofold, namely of *speculation,* and this is attributed to the Son; and of *experience and devotion*[^p460-7], and this is attributed to the unction of the Holy Spirit; and of this he speaks here.

2. And by this the solution to the following is plain. For he understands it not of *all truth simply,* but *according to piety*[^p460-8]; and this is the truth necessary for salvation, which the Apostles knew fully.

Or it must be said that he does not understand this according as *all* distributes over the *individuals of kinds,* but over the *kinds of individuals*[^p460-9]; for there is the truth of *teaching*[^tr-doctrina], *of life* and *of justice,* and these he taught. Of the truth of *teaching;* Matthew, the twenty-second chapter[^p460-10]: *Teacher, we know that thou art a true speaker, and teachest the way of God in truth.* — Of the truth *of life;* Isaias, the thirty-eighth chapter: *Remember, O Lord, how I have walked before thee in truth.* — Of the truth *of justice;* Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter: *To accept a person in judgement is not good, that thou shouldst decline from the truth of judgement*[^p460-11].

26. Question IV. It is asked concerning what he says: *He shall receive of mine.*

1. It seems false; because, if the Holy Spirit is God; and God can have nothing new: therefore he can receive nothing further. What is it, then, that he says: *He shall receive of mine?*
<!-- page 461 -->

2. Likewise it is asked concerning the Lord's reason: *Therefore,* he says, *I said: He shall receive of mine, because all things, that the Father hath, are mine.* — Either *all things* distributes over *essentials,* or over *properties;* if over *properties,* it is false, because the Son does not have innascibility. If over *essentials;* but from this it does not follow, if the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father, that on this account he proceeds from the Son, because *to proceed* is personal; by the same reason the Holy Spirit could say of the Son: *He shall receive of mine,* because all things that the Father has, the Holy Spirit has[^p461-1].

To this the Greeks respond that the procession of the Holy Spirit is twofold, namely *eternal* and *temporal;* as to the *eternal* he proceeds from the Father alone, and so he receives from the Father and not from the Son; as to the *temporal* he proceeds from the Son; in testimony of which thing it is said: *he shall receive,* because he proceeds temporally. And they take the reason of their error from this word of the Lord; because the Lord sometimes says that he *will send* the Holy Spirit, but never says that *he proceeds from himself,* always that it is from the Father[^p461-2]. — But nevertheless, saving their peace, they say nothing, because, as is plain from this letter, the Holy Spirit *receives from the Son.* What does he receive? Whatever he receives, this is not other than himself, since he is supremely simple: therefore if *he receives himself,* how can he receive in time and not be temporal?

Therefore it must be said that the Holy Spirit *proceeds* from the Son eternally and is temporally *sent* into the creature[^p461-3].

1. As to what is said, then, *he shall receive,* it is responded in the Gloss[^p461-4]: « That eternity comprehends all time on account of diverse properties; whence *he shall receive* is said of the future, not because he receives anything new, but because he does not cease ». — It must be said, however, that *he shall receive* is the same as if he said: he will show that he has received.

2. To the other it must be said that that is not a *compelling* reason why the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Son; but that is the reason *of the saying,* namely how he had said: *He shall receive of mine,* not because from his own, excluding the Father, but because together with the Father[^p461-5]. Whence it has been said by that reason by which it is said: *All things, that the Father hath, are mine.*
