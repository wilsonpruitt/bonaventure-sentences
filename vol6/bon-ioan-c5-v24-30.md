# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. V, Vers. 24-30
## *Declaravit Dominus suam potentiam in communi; hic incipit eam declarare in speciali, descendens ad potentiam vivificandi et ad potentiam iudicandi; ideo habet haec pars duas partes*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 311 -->

47. *Amen, amen dico vobis, quia qui* etc. Declaravit Dominus suam potentiam in communi; hic incipit eam declarare *in speciali,* descendens ad *potentiam vivificandi* et ad *potentiam iudicandi;* ideo habet haec pars duas partes. In prima declarat suam potentiam in *vivificando;* in secunda, suam potentiam in *iudicando,* ibi[^p311-5]: *Et potestatem dedit ei iudicium facere* etc. Potentia vivificandi respicit *benignitatem misericordiae;* potentia iudicandi *severitatem iustitiae.*

*Potentiam* igitur *vivificandi* hoc ordine describit, primo scilicet, *quos* vivificat; secundo, *quomodo* vivificat; tertio, *qua virtute* vivificat. Et primum respicit *obiectum,* secundum respicit *actum,* tertium vero *virtutem.* In his tribus consistit potentiae perfecta cognitio, scilicet in virtute, opere et obiecto.

48. (Vers. 24.). Primo ergo determinat, *quos vivificat,* vita scilicet animae, quoniam non omnes, sed credentes. Hi sunt qui verbum Dei audiunt et audito credunt[^p311-6]; ideo dicit: *Amen, amen dico vobis,* id est vere; *quia qui verbum meum audit,* se ad fidem disponendo, quia, ad Romanos decimo, « fides ex auditu »; *et credit ei qui misit me,* audito consentiendo; infra duodecimo: « Qui credit in me, non credit in me, sed in eum qui misit me »; ille quidem *habet vitam aeternam;* supra tertio: « Qui credit in Filium Dei habet vitam aeternam », vitam aeternam, non poenam. Unde etiam subdit: *Et in iudicium non venit,* in iudicium scilicet mortis; *sed transit de morte ad vitam*[^p311-7]; *de morte,* id est de praesenti vita, quae mors est, transit ad vitam aeternam sine iudicio condemnationis; supra tertio: « Qui credit in eum non iudicatur ». Vita vero praesens *mors* dicitur, quia, sicut dicitur ad Romanos octavo, « corpus mortuum est propter peccatum ». Ab hac morte transeunt ad vitam iusti, quibus temporaliter mori *transire* est; infra decimo tertio[^p311-8] de Christo capite: « Sciens, quia venit hora, ut transeat ex hoc mundo ad Patrem ». De hoc iusti in Psalmo gloriantur: « Et laetentur omnes, qui sperant in te; in aeternum exsultabunt, et habitabis in eis. Et gloriabuntur in te omnes, qui diligunt nomen tuum, quoniam tu benedices iusto ».

49. (Vers. 25.). *Amen, amen dico vobis.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *quomodo vivificat,* scilicet per internam inspirationem, quam qui audit vivificatur; ideo dicit: *Venit hora et nunc est,* scilicet gratiae, quae *nunc est;* de qua ad Romanos decimo tertio[^p311-9]: « Hora est, iam nos de somno surgere », de somno scilicet peccati et mortis; *quando mortui,* per culpam, *audient vocem Filii Dei,* per internam inspirationem; ad Ephesios quinto: « Surge, qui dormis, et exsurge a mortuis, et illuminabit te Christus ». *Et qui audierint vivent,* per gratiam, quia sermo eius vivificat audientes; infra decimo[^p311-10]: « Oves meae vocem meam audiunt, et ego vitam aeternam do eis »; et hanc *verbo* do et conservo; Deuteronomii octavo: « Non in solo pane vivit homo, sed in omni verbo, quod egreditur de ore Dei ».

50. (Vers. 26.). *Sicut enim Pater* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, *qua* scilicet *virtute vivificat,* quoniam virtute propria secundum Divinitatem sibi a Patre datam per generationem; unde dicit: *Sicut enim* etc. Bene dixi, quod Filius *vivificat; sicut enim Pater habet vitam in semetipso,* id est per essentiam; *sic dedit et Filio vitam habere in semetipso,* id est per essentiam, non per participationem. Et quia illud quod est vita per essentiam, omnibus influit vitam, ut dicit Dionysius[^p311-11]; ideo
<!-- page 312 -->
Filius potens est se ipso dare vitam. Unde infra undecimo: « Ego sum resurrectio et vita; qui credit in me, etiamsi mortuus fuerit, vivet »; similiter infra decimo quarto: « Ego sum via, veritas et vita »; non sum vivens *aliena* vita, sed sum vita *aliis dando vivere.*

51. *Et potestatem dedit ei.* Declaravit potentiam suam in vivificando, hic declarat suam potentiam *in iudicando;* et hoc quidem facit hoc ordine. Primo enim tangit, *qua virtute* iudicat; secundo, *quos* iudicat; tertio, *quomodo* iudicat, secundum illa tria, secundum quae descripta est *potentia vivificandi.*

(Vers. 27.). Primo ergo determinat, *qua virtute iudicat,* quoniam potestate sibi secundum humanitatem a Patre collata. Propterea dicit: *Et potestatem dedit ei,* scilicet Pater Filio, *iudicium facere, quia Filius hominis est;* ideo in humanitate, ut possint eum *reprobi videre* in iudicio, qui Deum videre non possunt; Apocalypsis primo[^p312-1]: « Videbit eum omnis oculus, et qui eum pupugerunt »; ideo in humanitate, quia *in humanitate iudicatus est;* Iob trigesimo sexto: « Causa tua quasi impii iudicata est, iudicium causamque recipies ».

52. (Vers. 28.). *Nolite mirari hoc.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *quos iudicat;* quia, licet omnes debeat suscitare, non tamen omnes iudicabit, sed malos[^p312-2]; unde dicit: *Nolite mirari hoc,* scilicet quod dictum est[^p312-3], quod « potestatem dedit ei iudicium facere, quia Filius hominis est »; *quia venit hora,* scilicet finis saeculi; Iob decimo quarto: « Homo, cum dormierit, non resurget, donec atteratur caelum »; *in qua omnes, qui in monumentis sunt, audient vocem Filii Dei.* Si mortui audient, constat, quod resurgent ad eius vocem; primae ad Thessalonicenses quarto[^p312-4]: « Ipse Dominus in iussu et in voce Archangeli et in tuba Dei descendet de caelo, et mortui, qui in Christo sunt, resurgent primi ». Et licet omnes resurgant, non tamen omnes iudicabuntur; ideo dicit:

53. (Vers. 29.). *Et procedent qui bona fecerunt in resurrectionem vitae;* quia ad hoc resuscitabuntur, ut vivant; *qui vero mala egerunt in resurrectionem iudicii,* id est damnationis, hos scilicet iudicabit Dominus; secundi Machabaeorum septimo[^p312-5]: « Potius est, ab hominibus morti datos spem exspectare a Deo, ab ipso iterum esse resuscitandos; tibi autem resurrectio non erit ad vitam »; dicitur Antiocho; *non ad vitam,* sed ad iudicium; primae ad Corinthios quinto[^p312-6]: « Eos qui foris sunt, iudicabit Deus », qui foris sunt per malam vitam; Danielis duodecimo: « Multi de his qui dormiunt in terrae pulvere, evigilabunt, alii in vitam aeternam, et alii in opprobrium ».

54. (Vers. 30.). *Non possum ego a me ipso.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *quomodo iudicat:* quia iuste, non secundum suam voluntatem a voluntate Dei declinando, sicut homines, qui pervertunt iudicium[^p312-7]. Ideo dicit: *Non possum ego a me ipso facere quidquam,* et ita nec iudicare; sed *sicut audio, iudico,* id est, sicut a Patre accipio, et ex hoc sequitur, quod iuste iudicat. Ideo dicit: *Et iudicium meum iustum est,* quia non declinat a regula iustitiae per discordiam voluntatis; et hoc est: *Quia non quaero voluntatem meam,* in iudicando scilicet, *sed voluntatem eius qui misit me,* id est Dei Patris, qui iustus est; unde infra octavo[^p312-8]: « Qui misit me verax est »; et post: « Quae placita sunt ei facio semper »; et ideo *iuste,* quia, Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo, « Deus fidelis et absque ulla iniquitate, iustus et rectus »; in Psalmo: « Iustus Dominus in omnibus viis suis » etc.

### Quaestiones.

55. Quaest. I. Quaeritur hic: secundum quam naturam data est Filio iudicandi potestas? — Quod secundum *humanam,* videtur:

1. Per litteram[^p312-9]: *Et potestatem dedit ei iudicium facere, quia Filius hominis est.*

2. Item, Augustinus[^p312-10]: « Potestatem iudicii dedit ei in carnem mittendo ».

**Sed contra:** 1. Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo[^p312-11]: *Mea est ultio, et ego retribuam,* dicit Dominus: ergo solius Dei est vindicta: ergo si *iudicium* est vindicta, est ergo Filii secundum divinam naturam.

2. Et hoc ostenditur per litteram[^p312-12]: *Pater omne iudicium dedit Filio, ut omnes honorificent Filium, sicut et Patrem;* sed Filius non est honorificandus sicut Pater secundum humanam naturam, sed solum secundum divinam: ergo etc.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod potestas iudicandi est duplex, scilicet *auctoritatis* et *ministerii.* Prima
<!-- page 313 -->
est totius Trinitatis, ministerii vero est Filii sedentis et apparentis in iudicio, et hoc secundum humanam naturam, et de hac hic loquitur[^p313-1].

1. Quod ergo obiicit: Mihi vindicta etc., non excludit Filium incarnatum, sed hominem purum.

2. Quod obiicit de honorificatione, dicendum, quod persona Filii propter humiliationem incarnationis videbatur descendisse; ideo Pater voluit, quod in eadem humanitate, in qua apparebat humilis, iudicaret, ut nemo ipsum incarnatum contemneret, sed sicut Patrem timeret tanquam eum cuius sententia inviolabilis perseverat; unde iudicium per humanitatem facit timeri per Divinitatem[^p313-2].

56. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur, utrum solius Filii sit iudicare. — Quod sic, videtur per litteram: *Pater omne iudicium dedit Filio;* et Actuum decimo[^p313-3]: *Ipse est, qui constitutus est a Deo iudex vivorum et mortuorum;* et primae ad Corinthios quarto: *Qui iudicat me Dominus est:* ergo si ipse solus est Dominus noster, ipsius solius est iudicare.

**Contra:** 1. Primae ad Corinthios undecimo[^p313-4]: *Si nosmetipsos diiudicaremus, non utique iudicaremur:* ergo nostrum est iudicare nos.

2. Item, quod ad alios, videtur Matthaei decimo nono[^p313-5]: *Sedebitis super sedes duodecim* etc. dictum est Apostolis; item, primae ad Corinthios sexto: *Nescitis, quia Angelos iudicabimus?*

Ad hoc dicendum, quod in iudicio est *examinatio,* est *approbatio,* est etiam *sententiae latio* sive definitio. — Primus actus, scilicet *examinationis,* ad omnes pertinet, et ratione huius dicit Apostolus, quoniam debent *se probare,* id est examinare. — Secundus, scilicet *approbationis,* vel *reprobationis,* ad perfectos pertinet, quia ipsi approbabunt et reprobabunt, et eorum comparatione mali reprobabuntur; sed hoc non in praesenti, sed in futuro; unde Apostolus dicit, tale iudicium in praesenti tempore esse *ante tempus;* primae ad Corinthios quarto[^p313-6]: *Nolite ante tempus iudicare.* — Tertius actus, scilicet *definitionis,* est solius Filii, quia ipse sententiam promulgabit. Hoc actu non utitur aliquis nisi usurpative et temerarie; unde Dominus prohibet Matthaei septimo[^p313-7]: *Nolite iudicare;* et Apostolus ad Romanos decimo quarto: *Tu quis es, qui iudicas alienum servum?* — Dico igitur, quod quando dicit, quod *Pater omne iudicium dedit Filio,* intelligitur de iudicio *definitionis,* non *examinationis* vel *approbationis*[^p313-8].

57. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de iudicandis, qui scilicet iudicabuntur. — Quod *mali* tantum, videtur ex littera[^p313-9]: *In iudicium non venit, sed transit;* et supra tertio: *Qui credit in eum non iudicatur.*

**Contra:** Secundae ad Corinthios quinto[^p313-10]: *Omnes stabimus ante tribunal Christi.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod iudicium *definitionis* accipitur *communiter,* et sic comprehendit sententiam salutis et poenae, et sic *omnes* iudicabuntur; accipitur *proprie* pro damnatione, et sic accipitur hic: *Procedent qui mala egerunt in resurrectionem iudicii,* id est damnationis; similiter supra tertio[^p313-11].

58. Quaest. IV. Item[^p313-12], de modo iudicandi:

1. Quod iudicat, sicut audit. — *Contra:* Isaiae undecimo[^p313-13]: *Non secundum auditum aurium arguet.*

2. Item dicit[^p313-14], quod non secundum suam *voluntatem,* sed *Dei:* ergo voluntas sua discordat a voluntate Dei.

**Respondeo** ad primum: Non iudicat secundum auditum exteriorem et relationem[^p313-15], sed secundum interiorem et paternae illuminationis.

Ad secundum dicendum, quod hoc habet duas causas veritatis: *non quaero voluntatem meam* singularem, aut quia habet, sed non quaerit; aut quia non habet nec quaerit; et in hoc sensu loquitur, et ipse obiicit sic in primo sensu[^p313-16].

## English
<!-- page 311 -->

47. *Amen, amen I say unto you, that he who* etc. The Lord has declared his power in common; here he begins to declare it *in special,* descending to *the power of giving life* and to *the power of judging;* therefore this part has two parts. In the first he declares his power in *giving life;* in the second, his power in *judging,* at[^p311-5]: *And he hath given him power to do judgement* etc. The power of giving life regards *the benignity of mercy;* the power of judging, *the severity of justice.*

*The power,* then, *of giving life* he describes in this order: first, namely, *whom* he gives life; second, *how* he gives life; third, *by what virtue* he gives life. And the first regards *the object,* the second regards *the act,* but the third *the virtue.* In these three consists the perfect cognition of a power, namely in the virtue, the work and the object.

48. (Verse 24.). First, then, he determines *whom he gives life,* namely the life of the soul, since not all, but those who believe. These are they who hear the word of God and, having heard, believe[^p311-6]; therefore he says: *Amen, amen I say unto you,* that is, truly; *that he who heareth my word,* by disposing himself to faith, because, to the Romans, the tenth chapter, « faith is by hearing »; *and believeth him that sent me,* by consenting to what is heard; below, the twelfth chapter: « He that believeth in me, doth not believe in me, but in him that sent me »; he indeed *hath life everlasting;* above, the third chapter: « He that believeth in the Son of God hath life everlasting », life everlasting, not punishment. Whence also he adds: *And cometh not into judgement,* namely into the judgement of death; *but passeth from death to life*[^p311-7]; *from death,* that is, from the present life, which is death, he passes to life everlasting without the judgement of condemnation; above, the third chapter: « He that believeth in him is not judged ». But the present life is called *death,* because, as is said to the Romans, the eighth chapter, « the body is dead because of sin ». From this death the just pass to life, for whom to die temporally is *to pass;* below, the thirteenth chapter[^p311-8], of Christ the head: « Knowing that the hour was come, that he should pass out of this world to the Father ». Of this the just glory in the Psalm: « And let all them be glad that hope in thee: they shall rejoice for ever, and thou shalt dwell in them. And all they that love thy name shall glory in thee: for thou wilt bless the just ».

49. (Verse 25.). *Amen, amen I say unto you.* Here there is touched on the second, namely *how he gives life,* namely through internal inspiration, which whoever hears is given life; therefore he says: *The hour cometh, and now is,* namely of grace, which *now is;* of which to the Romans, the thirteenth chapter[^p311-9]: « It is the hour now for us to rise from sleep », namely from the sleep of sin and of death; *when the dead,* through fault, *shall hear the voice of the Son of God,* through internal inspiration; to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter: « Rise, thou that sleepest, and arise from the dead: and Christ shall enlighten thee ». *And they that shall have heard shall live,* through grace, because his speech gives life to those who hear; below, the tenth chapter[^p311-10]: « My sheep hear my voice, and I give them life everlasting »; and this I give and preserve by the *word;* Deuteronomy, the eighth chapter: « Not in bread alone doth man live, but in every word that proceedeth from the mouth of God ».

50. (Verse 26.). *For as the Father* etc. Here there is touched on the third, *by what virtue,* namely, *he gives life,* since by his own virtue according to the Divinity, given him by the Father through generation; whence he says: *For as* etc. I said well that the Son *gives life; for as the Father hath life in himself,* that is, through essence; *so he hath given to the Son also to have life in himself,* that is, through essence, not through participation. And because that which is life through essence pours life into all things, as Dionysius says[^p311-11]; therefore
<!-- page 312 -->
the Son is able of himself to give life. Whence below, the eleventh chapter: « I am the resurrection and the life: he that believeth in me, although he be dead, shall live »; likewise below, the fourteenth chapter: « I am the way, the truth and the life »; I am not living by *another's* life, but I am the life *giving others to live.*

51. *And he hath given him power.* He has declared his power in giving life; here he declares his power *in judging;* and this indeed he does in this order. For first he touches on *by what virtue* he judges; second, *whom* he judges; third, *how* he judges, according to those three according to which *the power of giving life* was described.

(Verse 27.). First, then, he determines *by what virtue he judges,* since by a power conferred on him by the Father according to the humanity. On account of this he says: *And he hath given him,* namely the Father to the Son, *power to do judgement, because he is the Son of man;* therefore in the humanity, so that *the reprobate* may *see* him in the judgement, who cannot see God; Apocalypse, the first chapter[^p312-1]: « Every eye shall see him, and they also that pierced him »; therefore in the humanity, because *in the humanity he was judged;* Job, the thirty-sixth chapter: « Thy cause hath been judged as that of the wicked, judgement and cause thou shalt recover ».

52. (Verse 28.). *Wonder not at this.* Here there is touched on the second, namely *whom he judges;* because, although he must raise up all, yet he will not judge all, but the wicked[^p312-2]; whence he says: *Wonder not at this,* namely what was said[^p312-3], that « he hath given him power to do judgement, because he is the Son of man »; *for the hour cometh,* namely the end of the world; Job, the fourteenth chapter: « Man, when he shall have slept, shall not rise again, till the heaven be broken »; *wherein all that are in the graves shall hear the voice of the Son of God.* If the dead shall hear, it is evident that they will rise again at his voice; the first to the Thessalonians, the fourth chapter[^p312-4]: « The Lord himself shall come down from heaven with commandment, and with the voice of an Archangel, and with the trumpet of God: and the dead who are in Christ shall rise first ». And although all rise again, yet not all will be judged; therefore he says:

53. (Verse 29.). *And they that have done good things shall come forth unto the resurrection of life;* because they will be raised up again for this, that they may live; *but they that have done evil, unto the resurrection of judgement,* that is, of damnation; these, namely, the Lord will judge; the second of Machabees, the seventh chapter[^p312-5]: « It is better, being put to death by men, to look for hope from God, to be raised up again by him: for, as to thee, thou shalt have no resurrection unto life »; it is said to Antiochus; *not unto life,* but unto judgement; the first to the Corinthians, the fifth chapter[^p312-6]: « Them that are without, God will judge », those who are without through a wicked life; Daniel, the twelfth chapter: « Many of those that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake: some unto life everlasting, and others unto reproach ».

54. (Verse 30.). *I cannot of myself.* Here there is touched on the third, namely *how he judges:* that is, justly, not by declining according to his own will from the will of God, as men do who pervert judgement[^p312-7]. Therefore he says: *I cannot of myself do any thing,* and so neither judge; but *as I hear, so I judge,* that is, as I receive from the Father, and from this it follows that he judges justly. Therefore he says: *And my judgement is just,* because it does not decline from the rule of justice through discord of will; and this is: *Because I seek not my own will,* namely in judging, *but the will of him that sent me,* that is, of God the Father, who is just; whence below, the eighth chapter[^p312-8]: « He that sent me is true »; and afterwards: « I do always the things that please him »; and therefore *justly,* because, Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter, « God is faithful and without any iniquity, just and right »; in the Psalm: « The Lord is just in all his ways » etc.

### Questions.

55. Question I. It is asked here: according to which nature was the power of judging given to the Son? — That it was according to the *human,* it seems:

1. Through the letter[^p312-9]: *And he hath given him power to do judgement, because he is the Son of man.*

2. Likewise, Augustine[^p312-10]: « He gave him the power of judgement in sending him into the flesh ».

**On the contrary:** 1. Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter[^p312-11]: *Revenge is mine, and I will repay,* saith the Lord: therefore vengeance belongs to God alone: therefore if *judgement* is vengeance, it belongs, therefore, to the Son according to the divine nature.

2. And this is shown through the letter[^p312-12]: *The Father hath given all judgement to the Son, that all men may honour the Son, as they also honour the Father;* but the Son is not to be honoured as the Father according to the human nature, but only according to the divine: therefore etc.

**I respond:** It must be said that the power of judging is twofold, namely of *authority* and of *ministry.* The first
<!-- page 313 -->
belongs to the whole Trinity, but that of ministry belongs to the Son sitting and appearing in the judgement, and this according to the human nature, and of this he speaks here[^p313-1].

1. As to what he objects, then: Vengeance is mine etc., it does not exclude the incarnate Son, but a mere man.

2. As to what he objects concerning the honouring, it must be said that the person of the Son, on account of the humiliation of the incarnation, seemed to have descended; therefore the Father willed that he should judge in the same humanity in which he appeared humble, so that no one should despise him incarnate, but should fear him as the Father, as him whose sentence abides inviolable; whence he makes the judgement through the humanity to be feared through the Divinity[^p313-2].

56. Question II. Likewise it is asked whether to judge belongs to the Son alone. — That it does, it seems through the letter: *The Father hath given all judgement to the Son;* and Acts, the tenth chapter[^p313-3]: *It is he who is appointed by God judge of the living and of the dead;* and the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: *He that judgeth me is the Lord:* therefore if he alone is our Lord, to judge belongs to him alone.

**On the contrary:** 1. The first to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter[^p313-4]: *If we would judge ourselves, we should not be judged:* therefore it is ours to judge ourselves.

2. Likewise, that it belongs to others, it seems from Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p313-5]: *You shall sit on twelve seats* etc., which was said to the Apostles; likewise, the first to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter: *Know you not that we shall judge Angels?*

To this it must be said that in judgement there is *examination,* there is *approbation,* there is also *the passing of sentence* or definition. — The first act, namely of *examination,* pertains to all, and by reason of this the Apostle says that they ought *to prove themselves,* that is, to examine. — The second, namely of *approbation,* or of *reprobation,* pertains to the perfect, because they themselves will approve and reprove, and by comparison with them the wicked will be reproved; but this not in the present, but in the future; whence the Apostle says that such judgement in the present time is *before the time;* the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter[^p313-6]: *Judge not before the time.* — The third act, namely of *definition,* belongs to the Son alone, because he himself will promulgate the sentence. No one uses this act except usurpingly and rashly; whence the Lord forbids it, Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p313-7]: *Judge not;* and the Apostle to the Romans, the fourteenth chapter: *Who art thou that judgest another man's servant?* — I say, then, that when he says that *the Father hath given all judgement to the Son,* it is understood of the judgement *of definition,* not *of examination* or *of approbation*[^p313-8].

57. Question III. Likewise it is asked about those to be judged, namely who will be judged. — That only *the wicked,* it seems from the letter[^p313-9]: *He cometh not into judgement, but passeth;* and above, the third chapter: *He that believeth in him is not judged.*

**On the contrary:** The second to the Corinthians, the fifth chapter[^p313-10]: *We shall all stand before the judgement seat of Christ.*

**I respond:** It must be said that the judgement *of definition* is taken *commonly,* and so it comprehends the sentence of salvation and of punishment, and so *all* will be judged; it is taken *properly* for damnation, and so it is taken here: *They that have done evil shall come forth unto the resurrection of judgement,* that is, of damnation; likewise above, the third chapter[^p313-11].

58. Question IV. Likewise[^p313-12], concerning the mode of judging:

1. That he judges as he hears. — *On the contrary:* Isaias, the eleventh chapter[^p313-13]: *He shall not reprove according to the hearing of the ears.*

2. Likewise he says[^p313-14] that not according to his own *will,* but *God's:* therefore his will is in discord with the will of God.

**I respond** to the first: He does not judge according to exterior hearing and report[^p313-15], but according to the interior one, and that of the paternal illumination.

To the second it must be said that this has two causes of truth: *I seek not my own will,* singular, either because he has it, but does not seek it; or because he neither has it nor seeks it; and in this sense he speaks, and he himself objects thus in the first sense[^p313-16].
