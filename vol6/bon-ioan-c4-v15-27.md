# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. IV, Vers. 15-27
## *Excitavit Dominus mulierem ad consentiendum; hic iam mulieri consentienti et petenti verbum fidei proponit ad credendum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 293 -->

28. *Dicit ad eum mulier* etc. Excitavit Dominus mulierem ad consentiendum; hic iam mulieri consentienti et petenti *verbum fidei proponit ad
<!-- page 294 -->
credendum.* Quia mulier, etsi esset excitata, non tamen erat illuminata; ideo Dominus non statim verbum fidei proponit, sed hoc ordine procedit: primo *revelat secretum;* secundo *manifestat dubium;* tertio vero *aperit fidei sacramentum.*

29. (Vers. 15.). Mulieri igitur iam petenti aquam ita promissam et ex hoc consentienti *revelat secretum;* propter quod ostendit petitionem mulieris: *Dicit ei*[^p294-1] *mulier* etc. Ita promiserat ei Dominus aquam indeficientem; hanc petit mulier: *Domine, da mihi hanc aquam, ut non sitiam neque veniam huc haurire.* Et sitis erat ei poenalis et labor hauriendi; et ideo petebat donum Dei, quia, sicut dicit Gregorius[^p294-2], « mala, quae nos hic premunt, ad Deum ire compellunt ». Propterea Dominus vocat oppressos Matthaei undecimo: « Venite ad me, omnes, qui laboratis et onerati estis, et ego reficiam vos ». Mulieri igitur sic petenti Dominus *aperit secretum,* sed non statim, ne indignetur, quia conviciosum; sed occasionem dicendi quaerit, unde sequitur:

30. (Vers. 16.). *Dicit ei Iesus: Vade, voca virum tuum,* non per matrimonium, sed per abusum, *et veni huc.* Non hoc dicebat, quia crederet, eam habere virum, sed ut respondenti secretum de viro revelet. Unde:

31. (Vers. 17.). *Respondit mulier et dixit: Non habeo virum.* Ex hac veraci responsione Dominus, occasione accepta, subdit secreti revelationem. Unde et sequitur: *Dicit ei Iesus: Bene dixisti; non habeo virum,* quia verum confessa est.

32. (Vers. 18.). *Quinque enim viros habuisti,* ecce, revelat praeterita, *et hunc quem habes, non est tuus vir; hunc,* id est *hic;* antiptosis est[^p294-3]. Revelat praesentia, et ut non indignetur, verum commendat: *Hoc vere dixisti.* In quo traditur forma praedicatoribus, ut, cum volunt audientibus dicere aspera, immisceant et dulcia; unde Paulus hanc formam servabat primae ad Corinthios undecimo[^p294-4]: « Laudo, inquit, vos? in hoc non laudo »; non enim omnino reprehendebat.

33. *Mystice. Mulier* est pars inferior rationis; *vir* eius naturalis et legitimus est pars superior, recte diiudicans; primae ad Corinthios undecimo[^p294-5]: *Vir est imago et gloria Domini, mulier autem est gloria viri.* — Haec mulier reprehenditur, quod habuit *quinque viros,* quando quinque carnis sensibus in infantia deservivit, qui ei principabantur tanquam viri[^p294-6]. *Pueri* sunt qui sequuntur desideria iuvenilia, propter quod dicit Apostolus secundae ad Timotheum secundo[^p294-7]: *Iuvenilia desideria fuge.* Haec desideria iuvenilia, etsi videantur dulcia, tamen sunt aspera; unde significantur per quinque iuga boum; Lucae decimo quarto: *Iuga boum emi quinque,* scilicet quinque sensuum gravia desideria. — Primus vir, *gustus;* huic serviunt gulosi, in quorum persona Ecclesiastae secundo[^p294-8]: *Melius est comedere et bibere et ostendere animae suae bona;* Isaiae vigesimo secundo: *Comedamus et bibamus.* — Secundus, *tactus;* huic serviunt *luxuriosi,* in quorum persona Sapientiae secundo[^p294-9]: *Nullum sit pratum, quod non pertranseat luxuria nostra. Nemo nostrum exsors sit luxuriae nostrae.* — Tertius, *olfactus;* huic deserviunt *deliciosi,* in quorum persona dicitur Sapientiae secundo[^p294-10]: *Unguentis nos impleamus, et non praetereat nos flos temporis; coronemus nos rosis* etc. — Quartus, *visus;* huic deserviunt *avari;* Ecclesiastici decimo quarto[^p294-11]: *Insatiabilis est oculus cupidi in partem iniquitatis; non satiabitur, donec consumat arefaciens animam suam.* — Quintus, *auditus;* huic deserviunt *curiosi;* Actuum decimo septimo[^p294-12]: *Athenienses ad nihil aliud vacabant nisi aut dicere, aut audire aliquid novi.*

Redarguitur de viro sexto *non suo.* Hic vir est *error,* quo anima abducitur et seducitur. Hoc igitur
<!-- page 295 -->

errore reiecto, hortatur Dominus, ut vocet *virum,* id est naturale rationis iudicium, ut possit fidei sacramenta percipere, et non sit *sicut equus et mulus, quibus non est intellectus*[^p295-1]. Tali animae dat Dominus *aquam salientem in vitam aeternam.* Haec est aqua *gratiae,* quae a Deo descendit; ideo ad Deum salire facit; proprietas enim aquae est, ut tantum ascendat, quantum descendit[^p295-2].

34. (Vers. 19.). *Dicit ei mulier.* Tangitur hic secundum; postquam enim revelavit secretum, *manifestat dubium.* Mulier enim videns, quod Dominus esset cognitor secretorum, suam dubitationem terminandam proponit; propter quod ait: *Dicit ei mulier: Domine, video, quia propheta es tu,* quia scilicet nosti secreta, et ita potes respondere dubitationi[^p295-3].

35. (Vers. 20.). *Patres nostri in monte hoc adoraverunt,* scilicet Abraham et Iacob[^p295-4]; *et vos dicitis, quia Ierosolymis est locus, ubi adorare oportet.* Si igitur patres sunt imitandi, quomodo ergo vos hoc dicitis, quasi morem patrum contemnentes? Hoc dicebant Iudaei propter illud Deuteronomii duodecimo[^p295-5]: « Cave, ne in omni loco offeras Domino, sed in loco, quem elegerit Dominus ». Huius quaestionis dubio Dominus satisfacit, ostendens, quod neque ibi neque in Iudaea perfecte adorabant; ideo ait:

36. (Vers. 21.). *Dicit ei Iesus: Mulier, crede mihi, quia veniet*[^p295-6] *hora, quando neque in monte hoc neque Ierosolymis adorabitis Patrem.* Ista hora est *tempus gratiae,* in quo debet evacuari error gentilium et umbra mandatorum legalium. De hac hora ad Romanos decimo tertio[^p295-7]: « Hora est iam nos de somno surgere; nox praecessit, dies autem appropinquavit » etc. Haec hora locum orationis non determinat alicubi, sed ubique; primae ad Timotheum secundo: « Volo, viros orare in omni loco, levantes puras manus » etc. Et licet nec hic nec Ierosolymis perfecte nunc adoretur, tamen melius Ierosolymis. Ideo dicit:

37. (Vers. 22.). *Vos adoratis quod nescitis;* quia Legem non habetis; item, Deum simul et idola colitis et Deum localem creditis; quarti Regum decimo septimo[^p295-8] dicitur de Samaritanis, quod serviebant Deo terrae et idolis suis. *Nos adoramus quod scimus; nos,* scilicet Iudaei; *quia salus ex Iudaeis est,* id est Christus salvator; ad Galatas tertio: « Abrahae dictae sunt promissiones et semini eius ». Quia ergo nec Ierosolymis perfecte adoratur, docet mulierem, quando et quomodo perfecte adoretur, ne eam detineat in suspenso. Ideo dicit:

38. (Vers. 23.). *Sed venit hora.* Dixi, quod non perfecte adoratis, quia Deum *localem* creditis; nec Iudaei perfecte, quia *sub figuris. Sed venit hora,* scilicet gratiae, *et nunc est,* quia Christus iam venerat; *quando veri adoratores adorabunt Patrem in spiritu et veritate: in spiritu,* contra localitatem et corpulentiam; *in veritate,* reiecta umbra; secundum quod dicebat Apostolus secundae ad Corinthios tertio[^p295-9]: « Nos revelata facie gloriam Domini speculantes, transformamur in eandem imaginem a claritate » etc. Quod sint isti veri adoratores, ostendit dicens: *Nam et Pater tales quaerit, qui adorent eum;* supple: sicut dictum est, *in spiritu et veritate.*

39. (Vers. 24.). *Spiritus est Deus,* non corpus, ut vos creditis; Isaiae quadragesimo[^p295-10]: « Cui similem existimastis Deum? Aut quam imaginem ponetis ei »? *Et eos qui adorant eum, in spiritu et veritate oportet adorare;* ut sic ei conformentur, ut sint in *spiritu,* non in carne; ad Romanos octavo: « Qui in carne sunt Deo placere non possunt »; sint in *veritate,* non in fictione; Sapientiae primo: « Spiritus sanctus disciplinae effugiet fictum ».

40. (Vers. 25.). *Dicit ei mulier.* Hic iam tertio, ex quo manifestavit dubium, *aperit fidei sacramentum.* Mulier enim, a Domino instructa, ad Christum et tempus gratiae oculum considerationis elevat; propter quod ait: *Dicit ei mulier: Scio, quia Messias venit, qui dicitur Christus; cum ergo venerit ille, nobis annuntiabit omnia*[^p295-11]. Sciebat hoc mulier; quia Samaritani Legem admittebant, credebant, maxime quia Christus in Lege promittebatur non tantum Iudaeis, sed et gentibus; Genesis quadragesimo nono[^p295-12]: « Non auferetur sceptrum de Iuda et dux de femore eius, donec veniat qui mittendus est, et ipse erit exspectatio gentium ». Ex quo igitur mulier per-
<!-- page 296 -->
ducta est usque ad Christum et in eum iam parata credere; quid restat, nisi ut Dominus aperiat ei se ipsum? Propter hoc sequitur:

41. (Vers. 26.). *Dicit ei Iesus: Ego sum, qui loquor tecum.* Similiter Dominus respondit caeco infra nono[^p296-1], cum caecus quaereret, quis esset Dei Filius, in quem deberet credere, Dominus respondit: « Vidisti eum, et qui loquitur tecum, ipse est ». Iam fide pronuntiata, terminatur praedicatio et colloquium, quia discipuli revertuntur. Ideo subdit:

42. (Vers. 27.). *Et continuo venerunt discipuli eius et mirabantur, quia cum muliere loquebatur.* Admirabantur eius clementiam et dignationem; Augustinus[^p296-2]: « Bonum admirabantur, non malum suspicabantur ». Et haec admiratio non duxit in quaestionem, sed in reverentiam et timorem. Ideo dicit: *Nemo tamen dixit ei*[^p296-3]*: Quid quaeris, aut quid loqueris cum ea? Quid quaeris?* quasi, quae est huius locutionis necessitas? *Quid loqueris?* quae scilicet est huius locutionis utilitas? « Verbum enim, quod caret ratione iustae necessitatis vel piae utilitatis, ut dicit Gregorius[^p296-4], otiosum est ».

### Quaestiones.

43. Quaest. I. Incidit hic quaestio primo de hoc quod dicit: *Venit hora, quando neque in monte hoc neque Ierosolymis adorabitis Patrem.*

1. Si hoc verum est: ergo non deberent construi templa ad orationem.

2. Item, si eos qui adorant, *in spiritu* oportet adorare: ergo peccant, qui ad orandum faciunt imagines corporales.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod conditiones orationis quaedam sunt *essentiales,* ut recta intentio et iusta petitio; quaedam *accidentales,* ut tempus et locus. Dico igitur, quod Dominus non dixit, locum determinatum ab oratione vera esse resecandum ut superfluum; sed cognoscendum, non esse de orationis essentia, quia veri adoratores ubique Deum adorant. Et licet non sit *necessarius* locus, non tamen est *superfluus;* valet enim ad *memoriam* habendam, ad *devotionem* excitandam, ad *unitatem* servandam, ad *societatem Angelorum* obtinendam, quia *praevenerunt principes coniuncti psallentibus*[^p296-5].

2. Quod obiicit de imaginibus, dicendum, quod modo non obsunt, quia non creditur, aliquid in eis esse *divinum,* nec Deum esse ibi *corporaliter,* sed solum *significative*[^p296-6].

44. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod Dominus de se et[^p296-7] de Iudaeis dicit: *Nos adoramus quod scimus. — Contra:* Infra octavo[^p296-8]: *Neque me scitis neque Patrem meum* scitis, dicit Dominus Iesus Iudaeis.

**Respondeo:** Infra de infidelibus intelligit, qui erant Iudaei solo nomine; hic autem de Iudaeis veris loquitur, qui re et merito *Iudaei* appellantur[^p296-9].

45. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur: cum « verbum abbreviatum fecerit Dominus super terram[^p296-10] »; unde hoc est, quod Samaritanam per tot verba protraxit?

Respondet Chrysostomus[^p296-11], quod si statim dixisset mulieri: Ego sum Christus, videretur delirare; et ideo paulatim tanquam bonus doctor provexit et nobis exemplum tribuit, ut non dedignemur paucis auditoribus praedicare, quando ipse uni vetulae tam longum sermonem fecit.

46. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur: cum discipuli dubitarent, et Petrus audacter soleret a Domino quaerere[^p296-12]; quare non quaesierunt a Domino, quid loqueretur?

Ad hoc est responsio, quod si quaesissent, viderentur iam ipsum habere suspectum; ideo timebant argui de stulta quaestione et de indisciplina; et ideo non fuerunt ausi quaerere.

## English
<!-- page 293 -->

28. *The woman saith to him* etc. The Lord stirred up the woman to consenting; here now to the woman consenting and asking he *proposes the word of faith for
<!-- page 294 -->
believing.* Because the woman, although she had been stirred up, had not yet been enlightened; therefore the Lord does not at once propose the word of faith, but proceeds in this order: first *he reveals a secret;* second *he makes plain a doubt;* but third *he opens the sacrament of faith.*

29. (Verse 15.). To the woman therefore now asking for the water thus promised, and from this consenting, *he reveals a secret;* on account of which he shows the woman's request: *The woman saith to him*[^p294-1] etc. The Lord had thus promised her unfailing water; this the woman asks for: *Sir, give me this water, that I may not thirst, nor come hither to draw.* And thirst was a penalty to her, and the labour of drawing; and therefore she asked for the gift of God, because, as Gregory[^p294-2] says, « the evils which press us here compel us to go to God ». For this reason the Lord calls the oppressed in Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « Come to me, all you that labour and are burdened, and I will refresh you ». To the woman therefore so asking the Lord *opens the secret,* but not at once, lest she be indignant, because it is reproachful; but he seeks an occasion of saying it, whence there follows:

30. (Verse 16.). *Jesus saith to her: Go, call thy husband,* not through matrimony, but through abuse, *and come hither.* He did not say this because he believed that she had a husband, but that he might reveal to her, answering, the secret about her husband. Whence:

31. (Verse 17.). *The woman answered and said: I have no husband.* From this truthful answer the Lord, the occasion having been taken, adds the revelation of the secret. Whence also there follows: *Jesus said to her: Thou hast said well, I have no husband,* because she confessed the truth.

32. (Verse 18.). *For thou hast had five husbands,* behold, he reveals things past, *and him whom thou hast, is not thy husband; him,* that is *he;* it is antiptosis[^p294-3]. He reveals things present, and that she may not be indignant, he commends the truth: *This thou hast said truly.* In which a form is handed down to preachers, that when they wish to say harsh things to their hearers, they mix in sweet things also; whence Paul kept this form in the first to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter[^p294-4]: « Do I praise you, he says? In this I praise you not »; for he was not altogether reproving.

33. *Mystically. The woman* is the inferior part of reason; *her* natural and lawful *husband* is the superior part, judging rightly; the first to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter[^p294-5]: *The man is the image and glory of the Lord, but the woman is the glory of the man.* — This woman is reproved, because she had *five husbands,* when in infancy she served the five senses of the flesh, which ruled over her as husbands[^p294-6]. *Boys* are those who follow youthful desires, on account of which the Apostle says in the second to Timothy, the second chapter[^p294-7]: *Flee youthful desires.* These youthful desires, although they seem sweet, yet are harsh; whence they are signified by the five yoke of oxen; Luke, the fourteenth chapter: *I have bought five yoke of oxen,* namely the heavy desires of the five senses. — The first husband, *taste;* to it the gluttonous are slaves, in whose person it is said in Ecclesiastes, the second chapter[^p294-8]: *It is better to eat and drink, and to shew his soul good things;* Isaias, the twenty-second chapter: *Let us eat and drink.* — The second, *touch;* to it the *luxurious* are slaves, in whose person it is said in Wisdom, the second chapter[^p294-9]: *Let there be no meadow which our luxury doth not pass through. Let none of us be without his part in our luxury.* — The third, *smell;* to it the *voluptuous* are given over, in whose person it is said in Wisdom, the second chapter[^p294-10]: *Let us fill ourselves with ointments, and let not the flower of the time pass by us; let us crown ourselves with roses* etc. — The fourth, *sight;* to it the *avaricious* are given over; Ecclesiasticus, the fourteenth chapter[^p294-11]: *Insatiable is the eye of the covetous in his portion of iniquity; he will not be satisfied till he consume his own soul, drying it up.* — The fifth, *hearing;* to it the *curious* are given over; Acts, the seventeenth chapter[^p294-12]: *The Athenians were free for nothing else*[^tr-vacare] *but either to tell or to hear some new thing.*

She is reproved concerning the sixth husband, *not her own.* This husband is *error,* by which the soul is led away and seduced. This
<!-- page 295 -->
error therefore having been cast off, the Lord exhorts her to call *her husband,* that is, the natural judgement of reason, that she may be able to receive the sacraments of faith, and not be *as the horse and the mule, which have no understanding*[^p295-1]. To such a soul the Lord gives *water springing up into life everlasting.* This is the water *of grace,* which comes down from God; therefore it makes one spring up to God; for it is the property of water that it rises as high as it comes down[^p295-2].

34. (Verse 19.). *The woman saith to him.* Here the second is touched on; for after he has revealed the secret, *he makes plain a doubt.* For the woman, seeing that the Lord was a knower of secrets, puts forward her doubt to be settled; on account of which she says: *The woman saith to him: Sir, I see that thou art a prophet,* namely because thou knowest secrets, and so thou canst answer a doubt[^p295-3].

35. (Verse 20.). *Our fathers adored on this mountain,* namely Abraham and Jacob[^p295-4]; *and you say, that at Jerusalem is the place where men must adore.* If therefore the fathers are to be imitated, how then do you say this, as though despising the custom of the fathers? The Jews said this on account of that text of Deuteronomy, the twelfth chapter[^p295-5]: « Beware lest thou offer to the Lord in every place, but in the place which the Lord shall choose ». The Lord satisfies the doubt of this question, showing that neither there nor in Judea did they adore perfectly; therefore he says:

36. (Verse 21.). *Jesus saith to her: Woman, believe me, that the hour shall come*[^p295-6]*, when you shall neither on this mountain, nor in Jerusalem, adore the Father.* That hour is *the time of grace,* in which the error of the Gentiles and the shadow of the legal commandments must be emptied out. Of this hour, to the Romans, the thirteenth chapter[^p295-7]: « It is now the hour for us to rise from sleep; the night is passed, and the day is at hand » etc. This hour does not fix the place of prayer anywhere in particular, but everywhere; the first to Timothy, the second chapter: « I will that men pray in every place, lifting up pure hands » etc. And although now one does not adore perfectly either here or in Jerusalem, yet better in Jerusalem. Therefore he says:

37. (Verse 22.). *You adore that which you know not;* because you do not have the Law; likewise, you worship God and idols together and believe God to be local; in the fourth of Kings, the seventeenth chapter[^p295-8], it is said of the Samaritans that they served the God of the land and their idols. *We adore that which we know; we,* namely the Jews; *for salvation is of the Jews,* that is, Christ the saviour; to the Galatians, the third chapter: « To Abraham were the promises spoken, and to his seed ». Since, therefore, one does not adore perfectly even in Jerusalem, he teaches the woman when and how one is to adore perfectly, that he may not hold her in suspense. Therefore he says:

38. (Verse 23.). *But the hour cometh.* I have said that you do not adore perfectly, because you believe God to be *local;* nor do the Jews adore perfectly, because they adore *under figures. But the hour cometh,* namely of grace, *and now is,* because Christ had now come; *when the true adorers shall adore the Father in spirit and in truth: in spirit,* against locality and corporeality; *in truth,* the shadow cast off; according to what the Apostle said in the second to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p295-9]: « We, beholding the glory of the Lord with open face, are transformed into the same image from glory » etc. That these are the true adorers, he shows, saying: *For the Father also seeketh such to adore him;* supply: as has been said, *in spirit and in truth.*

39. (Verse 24.). *God is a spirit,* not a body, as you believe; Isaias, the fortieth chapter[^p295-10]: « To whom have you thought God like? Or what image will you set for him »? *And they that adore him, must adore in spirit and in truth;* that so they may be conformed to him, that they may be in *spirit,* not in the flesh; to the Romans, the eighth chapter: « They who are in the flesh cannot please God »; that they may be in *truth,* not in feigning; Wisdom, the first chapter: « The Holy Spirit of discipline will flee from what is feigned ».

40. (Verse 25.). *The woman saith to him.* Here now, third, after he made plain the doubt, *he opens the sacrament of faith.* For the woman, instructed by the Lord, raises the eye of consideration to Christ and the time of grace; on account of which she says: *The woman saith to him: I know that the Messias cometh, who is called Christ; therefore, when he is come, he will announce to us all things*[^p295-11]. The woman knew this; because the Samaritans admitted the Law, they believed it, chiefly because Christ was promised in the Law not only to the Jews, but also to the Gentiles; Genesis, the forty-ninth chapter[^p295-12]: « The sceptre shall not be taken away from Juda, nor a ruler from his thigh, till he come that is to be sent, and he shall be the expectation of the Gentiles ». Since therefore the woman has been
<!-- page 296 -->
led all the way to Christ and is now ready to believe in him, what remains, except that the Lord open himself to her? On account of this there follows:

41. (Verse 26.). *Jesus saith to her: I am he, who speak with thee.* In like manner the Lord answered the blind man below, in the ninth chapter[^p296-1], when the blind man asked who was the Son of God, in whom he should believe; the Lord answered: « Thou hast seen him, and he that speaketh with thee, he it is ». Faith now having been pronounced, the preaching and the conversation are ended, because the disciples return. Therefore he adds:

42. (Verse 27.). *And immediately his disciples came, and they wondered that he talked with the woman.* They wondered at his clemency and condescension; Augustine[^p296-2]: « They wondered at a good thing, they did not suspect an evil one ». And this wonder did not lead to a question, but to reverence and fear. Therefore he says: *Yet no man said to him*[^p296-3]*: What seekest thou, or what talkest thou with her? What seekest thou?* as if: what is the necessity of this talk? *What talkest thou?* namely, what is the usefulness of this talk? « For a word which lacks the ground of a just necessity or of a pious usefulness, as Gregory[^p296-4] says, is idle ».

### Questions.

43. Question I. A question falls in here, first, about this, that he says: *The hour cometh, when you shall neither on this mountain, nor in Jerusalem, adore the Father.*

1. If this is true: then temples ought not to be built for prayer.

2. Likewise, if they that adore must adore *in spirit:* then they sin who make bodily images for praying.

**Respondeo:** It must be said that of the conditions of prayer some are *essential,* such as a right intention and a just petition; some *accidental,* such as time and place. I say therefore that the Lord did not say that a determinate place is to be cut away from true prayer as superfluous; but that it is to be recognised as not being of the essence of prayer, because the true adorers adore God everywhere. And although place is not *necessary,* yet it is not *superfluous;* for it avails for having *memory,* for stirring up *devotion,* for keeping *unity,* for obtaining *the company of the Angels,* because *princes went before joined with singers*[^p296-5].

2. As to what he objects about images, it must be said that now they do no harm, because it is not believed that anything *divine* is in them, nor that God is there *bodily,* but only *by way of signifying*[^p296-6].

44. Question II. Likewise it is asked about this, that the Lord says of himself and[^p296-7] of the Jews: *We adore that which we know. — Contra:* Below, in the eighth chapter[^p296-8]: *Neither me do you know, nor my Father* do you know, says the Lord Jesus to the Jews.

**Respondeo:** Below he means the unbelievers, who were Jews in name only; but here he speaks of the true Jews, who in reality and by merit are called *Jews*[^p296-9].

45. Question III. Likewise it is asked: since « the Lord has made a shortened word upon the earth[^p296-10] »; whence is it that he drew out the Samaritan woman through so many words?

Chrysostom answers[^p296-11] that if he had said to the woman at once: I am the Christ, he would have seemed to rave; and therefore little by little, as a good doctor, he led her on and gave us an example, that we should not disdain to preach to few hearers, when he himself made so long a discourse to one old woman.

46. Question IV. Likewise it is asked: since the disciples were in doubt, and Peter was accustomed boldly to ask the Lord[^p296-12]; why did they not ask the Lord what he was speaking about?

To this the answer is that if they had asked, they would have seemed already to hold him suspect; therefore they feared to be rebuked for a foolish question and for indiscipline; and therefore they did not dare to ask.
