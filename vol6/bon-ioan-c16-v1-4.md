# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XVI, Vers. 1-4
## *Hic iam quinto discipulos suos armat ad patientiam, ut scilicet propter tribulationes non deficiant, sed sustineant; propterea dicit: Haec locutus sum vobis, ut non scandalizemini; quia, sicut dicit Gregorius, « minus feriunt iacula, quae praevidentur, et tolerabilius mundi mala suscipimus, si contra haec per providentiae clypeum munimur »*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 454 -->

### Capitulum XVI.

1. (Vers. 1.). *Haec locutus sum vobis* etc. Hic iam quinto[^p454-11] *discipulos suos armat ad patientiam,* ut scilicet propter tribulationes non deficiant, sed sustineant; propterea dicit: *Haec locutus sum vobis,*
<!-- page 455 -->
*ut non scandalizemini;* quia, sicut dicit Gregorius[^p455-1], « minus feriunt iacula, quae praevidentur, et tolerabilius mundi mala suscipimus, si contra haec per providentiae clypeum munimur ». Ideo praemunit eos de tribulatione futura.

2. (Vers. 2.). *Absque synagogis facient vos;* Actuum octavo[^p455-2]: « Facta est persecutio magna in Ecclesia, quae erat Ierosolymis, et omnes dispersi sunt per regiones Iudaeae et Samariae ». Et ratio huius acerbae persecutionis subiungitur: *Sed venit hora, ut omnis, qui interficit vos, arbitretur, se obsequium praestare Deo.* Hora ista fuit tempus praedicationis Apostolorum; tunc occidendo eos arbitrabantur se bene facere; unde exemplum est in Paulo, Actuum nono[^p455-3], qui « petiit litteras a principibus sacerdotum in Damascum, ut, si quos invenisset » etc. Talis autem cogitatio ortum habet ab infidelitate; et ideo dicit:

3. (Vers. 3.). *Et haec facient vobis, quia non noverunt Patrem neque me,* id est propter ignorantiam et incredulitatem. Exemplum est in Paulo, primae ad Timotheum primo[^p455-4]: « Fui blasphemus et persecutor et contumeliosus, sed ignorans hoc feci in incredulitate ». Contra igitur haec mala armat discipulos praedicens eis; unde dicit:

4. (Vers. 4.). *Sed haec locutus sum vobis, ut, cum venerit hora eorum*[^p455-5], qua scilicet persequentur, *reminiscamini, quia ego dixi vobis.* Reminiscentia et memoria verborum Dei multum valet ad omnia mala perferenda, e contrario oblivio nocet; ideo Deuteronomii octavo[^p455-6]: « Cave, ne quando obliviscaris Domini Dei tui et negligas mandata eius ». Recordatio sermonum Dei hominem erigit et dirigit; unde dicitur Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p455-7]: « Recordatus est Petrus verbi Iesu, et egressus foras, flevit amare »; Psalmus: « Reminiscentur et convertentur ad Dominum omnes fines terrae ».

### Quaestiones.

5. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod dicit: *Non noverunt me;* quaeritur, utrum ipsi Pharisaei cognoverint Christum. — Quod sic, videtur:

1. Quia dicitur Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p455-8] in parabola de vinea: *Illi cultores dixerunt: Hic est heres; venite, occidamus eum.*

2. Item, in littera[^p455-9] dicitur: *Nunc autem viderunt et oderunt et me et Patrem meum:* ergo scienter ipsum oderant.

**Sed contra** hoc est: 1. Quia Actuum tertio[^p455-10]: *Et nunc, fratres, scio, quia per ignorantiam fecistis, sicut et principes vestri;* ergo principes per ignorantiam occiderunt Christum: non ergo cognoverunt.

2. Item, primae ad Corinthios secundo[^p455-11]: *Si cognovissent, nunquam Dominum gloriae crucifixissent:* ergo non cognoverunt Christum.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Christus et Deus erat et Messias seu promissus princeps in Lege. Dicendum ergo, quod Iudaei mali cognoverunt, quod ipse erat Christus eis promissus et verax et innocens; sed quod esset Dei Filius, non cognoverunt[^p455-12]. Quia enim veritatem agnitam impugnaverunt, volentes ostendere, ipsum non esse neque bonum neque sanctum; ideo excaecati sunt, ut in eo Divinitatem non cognoscerent, in quo humanitatem odirent.

Quod ergo dicitur, aut quod cognoverunt, aut quod non; referenda est cognitio ad *diversa cognita.* — Alii referunt ad *diversa tempora,* quod in *principio* cognoverunt; sed *postmodum,* impugnando per malitiam, sunt excaecati[^p455-13].

6. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit[^p455-14]: *Si non venissem et locutus non fuissem; peccatum non haberent.*
<!-- page 456 -->

1. Ergo secundum istam rationem illi qui non audiunt et non credunt, non damnantur propter infidelitatem; quod manifeste falsum est, quia nullus salvatur sine fide.

2. Item, secundum hoc Tyrii et Sidonii, qui non crediderunt, non damnabuntur; quod manifeste falsum est per testimonium Evangelii[^p456-1].

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod per excellentiam diversa genera peccatorum nomine *peccati* generaliter dantur intelligi, praecipue tamen inter alia peccatum in Spiritum sanctum, quod est mere peccatum, nullam praetendens excusationem[^p456-2]. Nomine igitur *peccati* intelligitur hic peccatum *in Spiritum sanctum,* quod accrevit peccato *infidelitatis;* et hoc non habuissent Iudaei, sed solum remansissent in *infidelitate,* si Dominus non per aperta signa manifestasset se eis.

7. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit[^p456-3]: *Si opera non fecissem in eis, quae nemo alius fecit* etc.

1. Istud videtur falsum, quia maiora et mirabiliora signa legimus in veteri testamento. Legimus enim mare rubrum divisum et Iordanem, homines quadraginta annis de caelo pastos, mortuos suscitatos[^p456-4] et multa talia.

2. Item, ipse Dominus dicit[^p456-5]: *Qui credit in me maiora horum faciet;* ergo signum operum non erat certum signum nec singulare, quod ipse esset Christus.

Ad hoc Glossa[^p456-6] respondet, quod non intelligitur de operibus *miraculosis,* sed de *mirabilibus,* ut quod *mulier circumdedit virum,* et quod ipse de Virgine natus, et consimilia. — Sed illud non solvit, quia haec non erant eis nota[^p456-7].

Propterea dicendum aliter: vel quod hoc dicitur quantum ad operum multitudinem et diversitatem, quia nullus unquam tot miracula et tam varia et diversa fecerat, sicut Christus[^p456-8]. — Vel facienda est vis in hoc quod dicit: *Fecissem, quae nemo alius fecit,* quia non erat Deus, sed ad eius invocationem facta sunt; Christus autem fecit tanquam ille qui erat omnipotens.

8. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit[^p456-9]: *Viderunt nunc et oderunt me et Patrem meum.*

**Contra** hoc est: 1. Quia, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p456-10], « nullius conscientia potest odisse Deum »; quomodo ergo oderunt Patrem?

2. Item, nemo odit nisi quod novit; sed ipse dicit, quod *non noverunt Patrem,* et nunc[^p456-11] et in multis locis: ergo Deum non habebant odio.

Respondetur ad hoc dupliciter: primo, ut distinguatur *odium:* quia est odium *verum,* quod est in affectus controversia[^p456-12] adversus aliquem; et sic nullus potest odisse Deum. Et est odium *interpretativum,* quando aliquis ad modum odientis se habet. Sic dicitur aliquis *odire animam suam*[^p456-13], quando facit contra salutem propriam; sic etiam dicitur *odisse Deum,* quando voluntati eius adversatur.

Aliter dicitur, quod Deum est considerare ut *bonum summum,* a quo omne bonum; est considerare ipsum ut *iustum* in suis operibus. Nullus potest Deum odisse nec odit in *se;* odit tamen secundum quod *iustus*[^p456-14].
<!-- page 457 -->

1. Quod ergo obiicitur de Augustino, dicendum, quod Augustinus[^p457-1] illud retractat; tamen verbum illud verum est, accipiendo odium, sicut expositum est.

2. Quod obiicit, quod non cognoverunt; dicendum est, quod est cognitio *certitudinis* et *claritatis per speciem;* et hac nullus Deum cognoscit, nisi sit comprehensor. Et est cognitio *certitudinis,* sed *aenigmatis*[^p457-2]*;* sed hac nullus Deum cognoscit nisi fidelis. Et est cognitio *aestimationis,* et hac infideles et multi mali cognoscunt. Et sic cognoverunt Patrem et per consequens odire possunt[^p457-3].

9. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Venit hora, ut omnis, qui interficit vos, arbitretur, se obsequium praestare Deo.*

Ergo secundum hoc illi qui occidebant Apostolos, bona intentione occidebant: ergo merebantur. — *Si tu dicas,* quod non poterat bona intentione fieri; illud falsum est, quia mala *in genere,* quae non sunt mala *secundum se,* bene fieri possunt; et tale est *occidere.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod quaedam sunt opera, quae *nullo modo* possunt bene fieri, ut *mentiri,* sicut dicit Augustinus[^p457-4], quia « mox nominata coniuncta sunt malo », ut dicit Philosophus. Quaedam autem sunt, quae *statim* possunt bene fieri, cum adest bona intentio, ut sunt bona in genere[^p457-5]. Quaedam, quae non fiunt bona *sola intentione,* nisi adsit *causa* et *ordo,* et talia sunt *occidere* et consimilia. Unde quandocumque homo occidat alium pro Deo, nisi adsit ordo iudicii et causa, nullo modo bene fit. Tali modo fiebat in Apostolis, quia iusta causa et inquisitio deerat[^p457-6].

## English
<!-- page 454 -->

### Chapter XVI.

1. (Vers. 1.). *These things have I spoken to you* etc. Here now fifth[^p454-11] he *arms his disciples to patience,* namely that on account of tribulations they may not fail, but may endure; therefore he says: *These things have I spoken to you,*
<!-- page 455 -->
*that you may not be scandalized;* because, as Gregory says[^p455-1], « darts strike less which are foreseen, and we take up the evils of the world more tolerably, if against these we are armed by the shield of providence ». Therefore he forearms them concerning the tribulation to come.

2. (Vers. 2.). *They will put you out of the synagogues;* Acts, the eighth chapter[^p455-2]: « There was raised a great persecution against the Church, which was at Jerusalem, and all were dispersed through the countries of Judea and Samaria ». And the reason of this bitter persecution is subjoined: *Yea, the hour cometh, that whosoever killeth you, will think that he doth a service to God.* That hour was the time of the preaching of the Apostles; then by killing them they thought that they did well; hence there is an example in Paul, Acts, the ninth chapter[^p455-3], who « asked letters from the princes of the priests to Damascus, that, if he found any » etc. But such a thought has its rise from unbelief; and therefore he says:

3. (Vers. 3.). *And these things will they do to you; because they have not known the Father, nor me,* that is, on account of ignorance and unbelief. There is an example in Paul, the first to Timothy, the first chapter[^p455-4]: « I was a blasphemer and a persecutor and contumelious, but I did it ignorantly in unbelief ». Against these evils, then, he arms the disciples by foretelling to them; hence he says:

4. (Vers. 4.). *But these things I have told you, that when their hour shall come*[^p455-5], namely that in which they shall persecute, *you may remember that I told you.* Reminiscence and memory of the words of God avail much for bearing all evils, on the contrary forgetfulness harms; therefore Deuteronomy, the eighth chapter[^p455-6]: « Beware lest at any time thou forget the Lord thy God, and neglect his commandments ». The remembrance of the words of God raises a man up and directs him; hence it is said in Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p455-7]: « Peter remembered the word of Jesus, and going forth, he wept bitterly »; the Psalm: « All the ends of the earth shall remember, and shall be converted to the Lord ».

### Questions.

5. Quaest. I. But it is asked here first concerning this which he says: *They have not known me;* it is asked whether the Pharisees themselves knew Christ. — That they did, it seems:

1. Because it is said in Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p455-8], in the parable of the vineyard: *Those husbandmen said: This is the heir; come, let us kill him.*

2. Likewise, in the letter[^p455-9] it is said: *But now they have both seen and hated both me and my Father:* therefore they hated him knowingly.

**On the contrary** this is: 1. Because Acts, the third chapter[^p455-10]: *And now, brethren, I know that you did it through ignorance, as did also your princes;* therefore the princes killed Christ through ignorance: they did not, then, know.

2. Likewise, the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p455-11]: *If they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory:* therefore they did not know Christ.

**I respond:** It must be said that Christ was both God and the Messias, or the prince promised in the Law. It must be said, then, that the wicked Jews knew that he was the Christ promised to them, and truthful and innocent; but that he was the Son of God, they did not know[^p455-12]. For because they attacked the truth they had acknowledged, wishing to show that he was neither good nor holy; therefore they were blinded, so that they should not know the Divinity in him in whom they hated the humanity.

What is said, then, either that they knew or that they did not: the cognition is to be referred to *diverse things known.* — Others refer it to *diverse times,* that in the *beginning* they knew; but *afterwards,* attacking through malice, they were blinded[^p455-13].

6. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked concerning this which he says[^p455-14]: *If I had not come, and had not spoken, they would not have sin.*
<!-- page 456 -->

1. Therefore according to this reason those who do not hear and do not believe are not damned on account of unbelief; which is manifestly false, because no one is saved without faith.

2. Likewise, according to this the Tyrians and Sidonians, who did not believe, shall not be damned; which is manifestly false through the testimony of the Gospel[^p456-1].

**I respond:** It must be said that by excellence diverse kinds of sins are generally given to be understood by the name of *sin,* yet principally among the others the sin against the Holy Spirit, which is purely sin, putting forward no excuse[^p456-2]. By the name of *sin,* then, is understood here the sin *against the Holy Spirit,* which grew upon the sin of *unbelief;* and this the Jews would not have had, but would only have remained in *unbelief,* if the Lord had not manifested himself to them through open signs.

7. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked concerning this which he says[^p456-3]: *If I had not done among them the works that no other man hath done* etc.

1. That seems false, because we read of greater and more wonderful signs in the old testament. For we read of the Red Sea divided and the Jordan, of men fed from heaven forty years, of the dead raised[^p456-4] and many such things.

2. Likewise, the Lord himself says[^p456-5]: *He that believeth in me shall do greater than these;* therefore the sign of the works was not a certain sign nor a singular one that he was the Christ.

To this the Gloss[^p456-6] responds that it is not understood of *miraculous* works, but of *wonderful* ones, as that *a woman compassed a man,* and that he was born of a Virgin, and the like. — But that does not solve it, because these things were not known to them[^p456-7].

Therefore it must be said otherwise: either that this is said as to the multitude and diversity of the works, because no one ever had done so many miracles and so varied and diverse, as Christ[^p456-8]. — Or store must be set by this which he says: *I had done what no other man hath done,* because he was not God, but they were done at his invocation; but Christ did them as he who was omnipotent.

8. Quaest. IV. Likewise it is asked concerning this which he says[^p456-9]: *They have now seen and hated me and my Father.*

**On the contrary** this is: 1. Because, as Augustine says[^p456-10], « no one's conscience can hate God »; how then did they hate the Father?

2. Likewise, no one hates except what he knows; but he himself says that *they have not known the Father,* both now[^p456-11] and in many places: therefore they did not hold God in hatred.

It is responded to this in two ways: first, that *hatred* be distinguished: because there is *true* hatred, which is in the affection's controversy[^p456-12] against someone; and thus no one can hate God. And there is *interpretative* hatred, when someone bears himself in the manner of one hating. Thus someone is said to *hate his own soul*[^p456-13], when he acts against his own salvation; thus also he is said to *hate God,* when he opposes his will.

Otherwise it is said that God is to be considered as the *highest good,* from whom is all good; he is to be considered as *just* in his works. No one can hate God nor does he hate him in *himself;* yet he hates him according as he is *just*[^p456-14].
<!-- page 457 -->

1. As to what, then, is objected from Augustine, it must be said that Augustine[^p457-1] retracts that; yet that word is true, taking hatred as has been expounded.

2. As to what he objects, that they did not know; it must be said that there is a cognition of *certitude* and of *clarity through sight;* and by this no one knows God, unless he be a comprehensor. And there is a cognition of *certitude,* but of *enigma*[^p457-2]*;* but by this no one knows God except the faithful man. And there is a cognition of *estimation,* and by this unbelievers and many wicked men know. And thus they knew the Father and consequently can hate him[^p457-3].

9. Quaest. V. Likewise it is asked concerning this which he says: *The hour cometh, that whosoever killeth you, will think that he doth a service to God.*

Therefore according to this those who killed the Apostles killed with a good intention: therefore they merited. — *If you say* that it could not be done with a good intention; that is false, because things evil *in genus,* which are not evil *according to themselves,* can be done well; and such is *killing.*

**I respond:** It must be said that there are some works which *in no way* can be done well, as *to lie,* as Augustine says[^p457-4], because « as soon as named, they are conjoined with evil », as the Philosopher says. But there are some which *straightway* can be done well, when a good intention is present, as are the things good in genus[^p457-5]. Some, which do not become good by *intention alone,* unless *cause* and *order* be present, and such are *killing* and the like. Hence whenever a man kills another for God, unless the order of judgement and cause be present, it is in no way done well. In such a way it was being done in the case of the Apostles, because just cause and inquiry were lacking[^p457-6].
