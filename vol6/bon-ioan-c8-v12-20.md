# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. VIII, Vers. 12-20
## *Confutata Iudaeorum perversitate, in hac secunda parte manifestat Dominus suae doctrinae dignitatem et nobilitatem*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 357 -->

17. *Iterum ergo locutus est eis Iesus*[^p357-7]*: Ego sum lux mundi.* Confutata Iudaeorum perversitate, in hac secunda parte manifestat Dominus suae *doctrinae dignitatem et nobilitatem.* Nobilitas autem doctrinae suae consistit in hoc, quod liberat a *caecitate erroris,* a *servitute peccati,* a *damnatione mortis.* Primo ergo ostenditur, quomodo liberat a *caecitate erroris* et infidelitatis. Secundo, quomodo liberat a *servitute peccati,* ibi[^p357-8]: *Dixit ergo eis iterum Iesus* etc. Tertio, quomodo liberat a *damnatione mortis,* ibi: *Amen, amen dico vobis: Si quis sermonem meum servaverit, mortem non gustabit in aeternum.*

Primo igitur commendat suam doctrinam in hoc, quod liberat *a tenebrositate erroris,* et hoc quidem facit per hunc modum: primo insinuatur *doctrinae commendatio;* secundo, propter Iudaeorum reprehensionem *commendationis approbatio;* tertio, *manifestatio Iudaicae ignorantiae;* quarto vero, *evasio perfidiae.*

18. (Vers. 12.). Insinuatur igitur primo *doctrinae commendatio* in hoc, quod Dominus se comitantes et doctrinae suae adhaerentes dicit a tenebris liberari; propter quod dicit: *Iterum ergo locutus est eis Iesus;* quia eorum confutaverat perversitatem, rursus resumsit sermonem, ut doctrinae ostendat nobilitatem[^p357-9].[^p357-nota]
<!-- page 358 -->

Commendat igitur doctrinam suam, *dicens: Ego sum lux mundi,* id est hominum existentium in mundo; supra primo[^p358-1]: « Illuminat omnem hominem » etc. *Ego sum lux mundi,* per *instructionem;* Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto: « Doctrinam quasi antelucanum illumino omnibus ». *Qui sequitur me,* per *intellectus captivitatem;* secundae ad Corinthios decimo[^p358-2]: « In captivitatem redigentes omnem intellectum in obsequium Christi »; talis sequitur; infra duodecimo: « Qui mihi ministrat me sequatur ». *Non ambulat in tenebris,* per *erroris excaecationem;* ad Ephesios quarto[^p358-3]: « Non ambuletis, sicut gentes ambulant in vanitate sensus sui »; infra duodecimo: « Ambulate, dum lucem habetis, ut tenebrae vos non comprehendant ». *Sed habebit lumen vitae,* per divinae *claritatis visionem; sed habebit,* quia in *futuro* erit lux vitae, quae non poterit exstingui; sed *modo* potest; unde in Psalmo[^p358-4]: « Apud te est fons vitae, et in lumine tuo videbimus lumen ». Vel ideo dicit *habebit,* quia nunc ambulamus per fidem, sed tunc habebimus per speciei comprehensionem[^p358-5]; in praesenti vero non comprehenditur, nisi carne obumbretur. Unde Bernardus[^p358-6]: « Splendorem illius aeternae lucis nullus ferre posset aspectus, nisi obumbraretur levi nubecula carnis »; ideo, ait, esse dictum Lucae primo: « Virtus Altissimi obumbrabit tibi ».

19. (Vers. 13.). *Dixerunt ergo ei Pharisaei.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *commendationis approbatio* propter Iudaeorum reprehensionem. Reprehendebant enim eum, quia se ipsum commendabat. Propterea dicunt: *Tu de te ipso testimonium perhibes; testimonium tuum non est verum,* id est, non tanquam verum est acceptandum; quia dicitur Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo[^p358-7]: « Laudet te alienus, et non os tuum; extraneus, non labia tua ». Ad hanc reprehensionem subdit Dominus suam responsionem; ideo ait:

20. (Vers. 14.). *Respondit Iesus et dixit eis,* et defendit testimonium suum tanquam verum, quia *certum,* quia *legitimum;* propterea dicit: *Et si ego testimonium perhibeo de me ipso, verum est testimonium meum,* quia sum Deus; Numerorum vigesimo tertio[^p358-8]: « Non est Deus quasi homo, ut mentiatur ». Ideo verum et acceptabile est, quia *certum;* unde dicit: *Quia scio, unde venio*[^p358-9] *et quo vado;* Proverbiorum duodecimo: « Qui quod novit loquitur iudex iustitiae est, qui autem mentitur testis est fraudulentus »; infra decimo tertio: « Sciens, quia a Deo exivit et ad Deum vadit ». Sed vos male arguitis; unde subdit: *Vos autem nescitis, unde venio, aut quo vado;* Psalmus[^p358-10]: « Nescierunt neque intellexerunt, in tenebris ambulant ». Et quod nesciant illud, monstrat; unde dicit:

21. (Vers. 15.). *Vos secundum carnem iudicatis;* primae ad Corinthios tertio[^p358-11]: « Cum sit inter vos zelus et contentio, nonne carnales estis et secundum hominem ambulatis »? sive iudicatis, quia ipsum purum hominem esse credebant et gloriam suam ut homo quaerere; et ideo eius testimonium arguebant. *Ego autem*[^p358-12] *non iudico quemquam,* in praesenti scilicet; supra tertio: « Non misit Deus Filium suum in mundum, ut iudicet mundum ». *Non iudico,* condemnando, sicut vos me modo condemnatis; et hoc non, quia non possim vel nesciam, immo scio et possum; propterea dicit:

22. (Vers. 16.). *Et si iudico ego, iudicium meum verum est,* non carnale, non incertum neque secundum faciem; Isaiae undecimo[^p358-13]: « Non secundum visionem oculorum iudicabit, neque secundum auditum aurium arguet »; et ideo in iudicio errare non possum: *Quia solus non sum, sed ego et qui misit me Pater.* Unde quia in iudicando ab ipso non discedo, ideo non erro; supra quinto[^p358-14]: « Non possum a me ipso facere quidquam, sed sicut audio, iudico, et iudicium meum iustum est ». — Non tantum testimonium meum est *certum,* sed etiam *legitimum,* quia secundum Legem erat sufficiens; unde dicit:

23. (Vers. 17.). *Et in Lege vestra scriptum est, quia duorum hominum testimonium verum est,* id est acceptabile; Deuteronomii decimo nono[^p358-15]: « In ore duorum vel trium testium stat omne verbum ».

24. (Vers. 18.). *Ego sum, qui testimonium perhibeo de me ipso, et testimonium perhibet de me qui misit me Pater,* ideo testimonium meum non est repudiandum; primae Ioannis quinto[^p358-16]: « Tres sunt, qui testimonium dant in caelo: Pater, Verbum et Spiritus sanctus ».

25. (Vers. 19.). *Dicebant ergo ei.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *manifestatio Iudaicae ignorantiae:*
<!-- page 359 -->
primo in *sua interrogatione.* Quia enim non intelligebant quod dicebat, quod Pater testificaretur de ipso Christo; ideo quaerunt: *Ubi est Pater tuus?* Non quaerunt, *quis* sit, sed *ubi* sit; et inordinate requirunt, quia ubique est; Ieremiae vigesimo tertio[^p359-1]: « Caelum et terram ego impleo »; ideo magis merentur reprehensionem quam satisfactionem; et ideo subditur: *Respondit eis Iesus.* Manifestatur eorum ignorantia in *Christi responsione,* qua eos reprehendit et ostendit, quod stulte requirunt de Patre, quia, ipsum ignorando Filium, Patrem nosse non possunt. Propterea dicit: *Neque me scitis neque Patrem meum scitis*[^p359-2]. Et ratio huius est, quia ego sum via cognoscendi Patrem; unde dicit: *Si me sciretis, forsitan et Patrem meum sciretis;* quia Matthaei undecimo: « Nemo novit Filium nisi Pater, neque Patrem quis novit nisi Filius, et cui Filius voluerit revelare »; unde primae Ioannis secundo: « Omnis, qui negat Filium, nec Patrem habet; qui autem confitetur Filium et Patrem habet ».

26. (Vers. 20.). *Haec verba locutus est Iesus.* Tangitur hic mirabilis *evasio Iudaicae perfidiae,* quia sic eos reprehendit et erat inter manus eorum, et eum non ceperunt; ideo dicit: *Haec verba locutus est Iesus in gazophylacio, docens in templo;* quia gazophylacium erat in dextera parte templi; *et nemo apprehendit eum,* et hoc divino miraculo, non humano consilio; *quia nondum*[^p359-3] *venerat hora eius;* illud verbum saepe repetit Evangelista, ut ostendat Christum propria voluntate captum et passum, ut dicitur infra decimo[^p359-4].

Notandum est, quod *gazophylacium* dicebatur locus, ubi fiebant oblationes; et triplex erat locus ad hoc deputatus: unus dicebatur *gazophylacium,* ubi inferebantur oblationes ad sarta tecta templi; et dicitur *gazophylacium* a *gaza,* quod est divitiae, et a *phylaxe,* quod est *servare.* De hoc Lucae vigesimo primo[^p359-5]: « Respiciens Iesus vidit eos qui mittebant munera sua in gazophylacium ». — Alius locus sive repositorium dicebatur *corban* vel *corbona,* quod erat deputatum ad oblationes, quae contingebant sacerdotes; de quo Matthaei vigesimo septimo[^p359-6]: « Non licet eos mittere in corbonam, quia pretium sanguinis est ». — Tertium repositorium dicebatur *musach,* ubi reponebantur tributa regum; quarti Regum decimo sexto: « Musach Sabbati ». — Vel aliter: *musach* ad oblationes solemnitatum, *corban* ad oblationes votivas, *gazophylacium* ad oblationes spontaneas.

### Quaestiones.

27. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod Dominus dicit: *Si ego testimonium perhibeo de me ipso, testimonium meum verum est.*

**Contra:** Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo[^p359-7]: *Laudet te alienus, non os tuum; extraneus, non labia tua.* — Item, nullius testimonium recipitur pro se ipso, et Dominus dixit supra quinto[^p359-8]: *Si ego testimonium perhibeo de me ipso, testimonium meum non est verum;* ergo contradicit sibi.

Item quaeritur de hoc, quod Dominus nititur testimonium suum approbare per Legem[^p359-9]: *quia duorum testimonium verum est.*

Sed 1. hoc argumentum videtur nihil valere, quia in Lege, quando aliquis testatur de se, et alius de eo, non reputantur nisi pro uno testimonio: ergo Filius secundum hoc non habebat nisi unicum testimonium.
<!-- page 360 -->

2. Item Pater eius erat persona coniunctissima; et talis persona secundum iura[^p360-1] ad testimonium non admittitur.

3. Item, infra decimo[^p360-2]: *Ego et Pater unum sumus,* ergo unum erant: non ergo duo testes; aut si duo testes, non unum sunt Pater et Filius.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod ipse Filius Dei est lux, sed omnis creatura est tenebra et potest, quantum est de se, obscurari. Quia ergo lucis est manifestare se et alia, ideo lux ipsa potest de se testimonium perhibere; sed creatura, quia tenebra est, alio manifestante indiget[^p360-3]. Item, quia lux est, non potest obscurari nec obliquari, ideo simul *iudicare* et *testificari* potest, simul etiam *laudare* et *laudari.* Quia vero creatura potest obliquari, non potest nec debet ista duo simul obtinere[^p360-4].

Illud igitur Proverbiorum dicitur ad homines fragiles et pronos ad vanam laudem. — Illud quod dictum est supra quinto, intelligitur de Christo secundum humanam naturam[^p360-5].

Ad illud vero, quod obiicitur de testimonio secundum iura, similiter respondendum: quia nullus potest testificari pro se; sed si ita esset, quod aliquis pro se posset testificari, tunc sufficeret unum aliud[^p360-6] testimonium. Sed Christus poterat de se testimonium perhibere; et hoc supposito, suum argumentum bonum est.

2. Similiter de persona coniuncta: quia declinare potest ad falsitatem, non recipitur testimonium; sed Pater caelestis, qui *verax est*[^p360-7], declinare non potest.

3. Quod autem obiicitur, quod Pater et Filius unum sunt; dicendum, quod sunt unum in essentia et distinguuntur in personis. Quia ergo in veris testibus est distinctio personarum et concordia testimonii; et hoc ibi reperitur: ideo authenticum valde est testimonium.

28. Quaest. II. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Neque me scitis* etc.; quia supra septimo[^p360-8]: *Et me scitis, et unde sim scitis.*

Et responsum est[^p360-9], quod illud dictum est secundum *humanitatem;* hoc autem dictum est secundum *Divinitatem;* quia *qui videt Filium videt et Patrem.*

29. Quaest. III. Sed de hoc quaeritur: *Si me sciretis, forsitan et Patrem meum sciretis;* quia si in Deo nulla cadit opinio, ergo nec dubitatio; quid est ergo hoc quod dicit *forsitan?*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod hoc adverbio *forsitan* « arguitur hic infidelitas, non opinatur Divinitas[^p360-10] »; insinuatur etiam nobis humani arbitrii libertas.

## English
<!-- page 357 -->

17. *Again therefore Jesus spoke to them*[^p357-7]*: I am the light of the world.* The perversity of the Jews having been refuted, in this second part the Lord manifests *the dignity and nobility of his teaching*[^tr-doctrina]*.* Now the nobility of his teaching consists in this, that it frees from *the blindness of error,* from *the servitude of sin,* from *the damnation of death.* First, therefore, it is shown how it frees from *the blindness of error* and of unbelief. Second, how it frees from *the servitude of sin,* there[^p357-8]: *Jesus therefore said to them again* etc. Third, how it frees from *the damnation of death,* there: *Amen, amen I say to you: If any man keep my word, he shall not taste death for ever.*

First, then, he commends his teaching in this, that it frees *from the darkness of error,* and this indeed he does in this manner: first is intimated *the commendation of the teaching;* second, because of the reproof of the Jews, *the approbation of the commendation;* third, *the manifestation of the Jewish ignorance;* but fourth, *the escape from perfidy.*

18. (V. 12.). First, then, is intimated *the commendation of the teaching* in this, that the Lord says that those who accompany him and cleave to his teaching are freed from darkness; on account of which he says: *Again therefore Jesus spoke to them;* because he had refuted their perversity, he took up his discourse again, that he might show the nobility of his teaching[^p357-9].[^p357-nota]
<!-- page 358 -->

He commends, then, his teaching, *saying: I am the light of the world,* that is, of the men existing in the world; above, in the first chapter[^p358-1]: « He illumines every man » etc. *I am the light of the world,* through *instruction;* Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter: « I make teaching to shine forth to all as the morning light ». *He that followeth me,* through *the captivity of the understanding;* the second to the Corinthians, the tenth chapter[^p358-2]: « Bringing into captivity every understanding unto the obedience of Christ »; such a one follows; below, in the twelfth chapter: « He that ministereth to me, let him follow me ». *Walketh not in darkness,* through *the blinding of error;* to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter[^p358-3]: « Walk not as the Gentiles walk in the vanity of their mind »; below, in the twelfth chapter: « Walk whilst you have the light, that the darkness overtake you not ». *But shall have the lumen of life*[^tr-lumen], through the vision of the divine *clarity; but shall have,* because in *the future* there will be a light of life which cannot be extinguished; but *now* it can; whence in the Psalm[^p358-4]: « With thee is the fountain of life, and in thy lumen we shall see lumen ». Or he says *shall have* for this reason, because now we walk by faith, but then we shall have it through the comprehension of sight[^p358-5]; but in the present it is not comprehended unless it be overshadowed by flesh. Whence Bernard[^p358-6]: « No sight could bear the splendour of that eternal light, unless it were overshadowed by the light cloud of the flesh »; therefore, he says, it was said in Luke, the first chapter: « The power of the Most High shall overshadow thee ».

19. (V. 13.). *The Pharisees therefore said to him.* Here the second is touched, namely *the approbation of the commendation* because of the reproof of the Jews. For they reproved him because he commended himself. Therefore they say: *Thou givest testimony of thyself; thy testimony is not true,* that is, it is not to be accepted as true; because it is said in Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p358-7]: « Let another praise thee, and not thy own mouth; a stranger, and not thy own lips ». To this reproof the Lord subjoins his response; therefore he says:

20. (V. 14.). *Jesus answered, and said to them,* and defends his testimony as true, because *certain,* because *legitimate;* therefore he says: *And if I give testimony of myself, my testimony is true,* because I am God; Numbers, the twenty-third chapter[^p358-8]: « God is not as a man, that he should lie ». Therefore it is true and acceptable, because *certain;* whence he says: *Because I know whence I come*[^p358-9] *and whither I go;* Proverbs, the twelfth chapter: « He that speaketh that which he knoweth is a judge of justice; but he that lieth is a deceitful witness »; below, in the thirteenth chapter: « Knowing that he came forth from God, and goeth to God ». But you argue ill; whence he subjoins: *But you know not whence I come, or whither I go;* the Psalm[^p358-10]: « They have not known nor understood; they walk in darkness ». And that they do not know it, he shows; whence he says:

21. (V. 15.). *You judge according to the flesh;* the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p358-11]: « Whereas there is among you envying and contention, are you not carnal, and walk according to man »? or judge, because they believed him to be a mere man and to seek his own glory as a man; and therefore they found fault with his testimony. *But I*[^p358-12] *judge not any man,* in the present, namely; above, in the third chapter: « God sent not his Son into the world, to judge the world ». *I judge not,* by condemning, as you now condemn me; and this not because I could not or know not how, nay rather I know and I can; therefore he says:

22. (V. 16.). *And if I do judge, my judgment is true,* not carnal, not uncertain, nor according to the face; Isaias, the eleventh chapter[^p358-13]: « He shall not judge according to the sight of the eyes, nor reprove according to the hearing of the ears »; and therefore I cannot err in judgment: *Because I am not alone, but I and the Father that sent me.* Whence, because in judging I do not depart from him, therefore I do not err; above, in the fifth chapter[^p358-14]: « I cannot of myself do any thing, but as I hear, I judge, and my judgment is just ». — Not only is my testimony *certain,* but also *legitimate,* because according to the Law it was sufficient; whence he says:

23. (V. 17.). *And in your Law it is written, that the testimony of two men is true,* that is, acceptable; Deuteronomy, the nineteenth chapter[^p358-15]: « In the mouth of two or three witnesses every word standeth ».

24. (V. 18.). *I am he that give testimony of myself, and the Father that sent me giveth testimony of me,* therefore my testimony is not to be repudiated; the first of John, the fifth chapter[^p358-16]: « There are three who give testimony in heaven: the Father, the Word and the Holy Spirit ».

25. (V. 19.). *They said therefore to him.* Here the third is touched, namely *the manifestation of the Jewish ignorance:*
<!-- page 359 -->
first, in *their questioning.* For because they did not understand what he was saying, that the Father gave testimony of Christ himself; therefore they ask: *Where is thy Father?* They do not ask *who* he is, but *where* he is; and they inquire inordinately, because he is everywhere; Jeremias, the twenty-third chapter[^p359-1]: « I fill heaven and earth »; therefore they deserve reproof rather than satisfaction; and therefore it is subjoined: *Jesus answered them.* Their ignorance is manifested in *Christ's response,* by which he reproves them and shows that they inquire foolishly about the Father, because, being ignorant of the Son himself, they cannot know the Father. Therefore he says: *Neither do you know me, nor do you know my Father*[^p359-2]. And the reason of this is, because I am the way of knowing the Father; whence he says: *If you knew me, perhaps you would know my Father also;* because in Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « No one knoweth the Son but the Father, neither doth any one know the Father but the Son, and he to whom the Son will reveal him »; whence in the first of John, the second chapter: « Whosoever denieth the Son hath not the Father either; but he that confesseth the Son hath the Father also ».

26. (V. 20.). *These words Jesus spoke.* Here is touched the wondrous *escape from the Jewish perfidy,* because he reproved them thus and was among their hands, and they did not take him; therefore he says: *These words Jesus spoke in the treasury, teaching in the temple;* because the treasury was on the right side of the temple; *and no man laid hands on him,* and this by divine miracle, not by human counsel; *because*[^p359-3] *his hour was not yet come;* that word the Evangelist often repeats, that he may show that Christ was taken and suffered by his own will, as is said below, in the tenth chapter[^p359-4].

It is to be noted that the place where the offerings were made was called *gazophylacium;* and the place appointed for this was threefold: one was called *gazophylacium,* where the offerings were brought in for the repairs of the temple; and it is called *gazophylacium* from *gaza,* which is riches, and from *phylaxe,* which is *to keep.* Of this, Luke, the twenty-first chapter[^p359-5]: « Jesus looking on saw them that cast their gifts into the treasury ». — Another place or repository was called *corban* or *corbona,* which was appointed for the offerings which fell to the priests; of which Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p359-6]: « It is not lawful to put them into the corbona, because it is the price of blood ». — The third repository was called *musach,* where the tributes of kings were deposited; the fourth of Kings, the sixteenth chapter: « The musach of the Sabbath ». — Or otherwise: the *musach* for the offerings of the solemnities, the *corban* for votive offerings, the *gazophylacium* for spontaneous offerings.

### Questions.

27. Quaest. I. But here it is asked, first, concerning this which the Lord says: *If I give testimony of myself, my testimony is true.*

**Against:** Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p359-7]: *Let another praise thee, not thy own mouth; a stranger, not thy own lips.* — Again, no one's testimony is received for himself, and the Lord said above, in the fifth chapter[^p359-8]: *If I give testimony of myself, my testimony is not true;* therefore he contradicts himself.

Again it is asked concerning this, that the Lord endeavours to approve his testimony through the Law[^p359-9]: *that the testimony of two is true.*

But 1. this argument seems to be worth nothing, because in the Law, when someone testifies of himself, and another of him, they are not reckoned except as one testimony: therefore the Son, according to this, had only a single testimony.
<!-- page 360 -->

2. Again, his Father was a person most closely joined; and such a person, according to the laws[^p360-1], is not admitted to testimony.

3. Again, below, in the tenth chapter[^p360-2]: *I and the Father are one,* therefore they were one: not, therefore, two witnesses; or if two witnesses, the Father and the Son are not one.

**I answer:** It must be said that the Son of God himself is light, but every creature is darkness and can, as far as it is of itself, be obscured. Because, then, it belongs to light to manifest itself and other things, therefore light itself can give testimony of itself; but the creature, because it is darkness, needs another to manifest it[^p360-3]. Again, because he is light, he cannot be obscured nor be made oblique; therefore he can at once *judge* and *testify,* and at once also *praise* and *be praised.* But because the creature can be made oblique, it cannot and ought not to hold these two at once[^p360-4].

That, then, from Proverbs is said to men who are frail and prone to vain praise. — That which was said above, in the fifth chapter, is understood of Christ according to the human nature[^p360-5].

But to that which is objected concerning testimony according to the laws, it must likewise be answered: that no one can testify for himself; but if it were so, that someone could testify for himself, then one other[^p360-6] testimony would suffice. But Christ could give testimony of himself; and this supposed, his argument is good.

2. Likewise of the person joined: because he can decline to falsehood, his testimony is not received; but the heavenly Father, who *is true*[^p360-7], cannot decline.

3. But as to what is objected, that the Father and the Son are one; it must be said that they are one in essence and are distinguished in persons. Because, then, in true witnesses there is distinction of persons and concord of testimony; and this is found there: therefore the testimony is very authentic.

28. Quaest. II. It is asked concerning this which he says: *Neither do you know me* etc.; because above, in the seventh chapter[^p360-8]: *You both know me, and you know whence I am.*

And it has been answered[^p360-9] that that was said according to *the humanity;* but this is said according to *the Divinity;* because *he that seeth the Son seeth the Father also.*

29. Quaest. III. But concerning this it is asked: *If you knew me, perhaps you would know my Father also;* because if no opinion befalls God, then neither does doubt; what, then, is this which he says, *perhaps?*

**I answer:** It must be said that by this adverb *perhaps* « unbelief is here reproved, the Divinity does not opine[^p360-10] »; there is also intimated to us the liberty of human choice.

[^tr-doctrina]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin *doctrina* holds together what English divides among doctrine, teaching and learning. Here it is what the Lord speaks and commends in 8:12 — *suae doctrinae dignitatem et nobilitatem*, *doctrinae commendatio*, those *doctrinae suae adhaerentes* — the word of the speaker, whose nobility is that it frees from the blindness of error, the servitude of sin and the damnation of death; and Ecclus. 24:44's *Doctrinam quasi antelucanum illumino omnibus* is adduced for the same thing. [^tr-lumen]: **En.** Translator's note: this translation keeps the Latin *lux* and *lumen* apart, rendering *lux* "light" and leaving *lumen* standing as "lumen". *Lux* is light at its source and *lumen* light as given off and received. The lemma itself sets them side by side — *Ego sum lux mundi … sed habebit lumen vitae* — the Lord is the light, and the one who follows him shall have the lumen of life; and the exposition keeps the pair, saying that in the future there will be a *lux vitae* that cannot be extinguished, and adducing Ps. 35:10, *in lumine tuo videbimus lumen*, for the lemma's *lumen*. Because the exposition leans on the word there, the Psalm's received "in thy light we shall see light" is not kept at this site, and it reads "in thy lumen we shall see lumen". Bernard's *aeternae lucis* and John 12:35's *dum lucem habetis* are *lux*, and "light"; so is the *Respondeo* of Quaest. I, where the Son of God *est lux* against the creature that is darkness.
