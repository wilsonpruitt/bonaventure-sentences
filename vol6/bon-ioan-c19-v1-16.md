# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XIX, Vers. 1-16
## *Hic intendit eum Pilatus liberare iudicio severitatis*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 490 -->

### Capitulum XIX.

1. *Tunc*[^p490-5] *apprehendit eum* etc. Hic intendit eum Pilatus liberare *iudicio severitatis,* flagellans innocentem, ut zelum mitiget Iudaeorum; et sunt hic tres particulae. In prima describitur *Christi ignominiosa flagellatio;* secundo, *flagellati ostensio,* ibi[^p490-6]: *Exivit iterum Pilatus foras;* tertio, *Iudaeorum induratio,* ibi: *Cum ergo vidissent eum pontifices.*

(Vers. 1.). *Ignominiosa Christi flagellatio* insinuatur primo in *afflictione,* deinde in *derisione.* — *Afflictionem* tangit, cum dicit: *Tunc apprehendit Pilatus Iesum et flagellavit.* Chrysostomus[^p490-7]: « Flagellavit eum Pilatus, fortassis absolvere volens et mitigare Iudaicum zelum »; unde Lucae vigesimo tertio dixit Pilatus: « Emendatum illum dimittam », id est flagellis afflictum ad magis mitigandum Iudaeorum zelum. — Afflictioni addita est *irrisio:*

2. (Vers. 2.). *Et milites plectentes* etc. Et quia reges solent honorari signo, habitu et verbo sive salutatione et reverentiae exhibitione; ideo quadrupliciter[^p490-8] irrident: primo in *signo coronae,* quod est signum regale, unde: *Plectentes,* id est plicantes, *coronam de spinis,* id est iuncis marinis acutis et pungitivis ad modum spinarum; *imposuerunt capiti eius.* De hac corona Canticorum tertio[^p490-9]: « Egredimini, filiae Sion, et videte regem Salomonem in diademate, quo coronavit eum mater sua », id est synagoga. — Deinde illudunt eum in *habitu* vel vestitu; unde dicitur: *Et veste purpurea circumdederunt eum,* quasi esset rex; in quo significatur, quod caro eius erat sanguine purpuranda; quod significatum est Genesis trigesimo septimo[^p490-10]: « Tulerunt tunicam Ioseph et in sanguine haedi, quem occiderant, tinxerunt »; et Isaiae sexagesimo tertio: « Quare rubrum est indumentum tuum, et vestimenta tua sicut calcantium in torculari »? — Deinde illusio in *salutatione;* unde dicit:

3. (Vers. 3.). *Et veniebant ad eum et dicebant: Ave, rex Iudaeorum,* et hoc quidem in opprobrium; Threnorum tertio[^p490-11]: « Saturabitur opprobriis ». — Non solum in verbo, sed in *reverentia,* quia loco reverentiae dabant alapas; unde dicit: *Et dabant ei alapas;* Threnorum tertio: « Dabit percutienti se maxillam »; et Isaiae quinquagesimo: « Corpus meum dedi percutientibus et genas meas vellentibus ».

4. (Vers. 4.). *Exivit*[^p490-12] *iterum Pilatus foras.* Hic ponitur *Christi flagellati et illusi ostensio,* ut moveantur ad pietatem. Et primum quidem replicatur innocentia; unde: *Dicit: Ecce, adduco vobis eum foras.* Rationem subdit: *Ut cognoscatis, quia nul-*
<!-- page 491 -->
*lam causam invenio in eo,* scilicet « mortis[^p491-1] » vel afflictionis; et tamen afflixi eum.

5. (Vers. 5.). *Exiit*[^p491-2] *ergo Iesus.* Augustinus: « Non clarus imperio, sed plenus opprobrio »; unde sequitur: *Portans spineam coronam et purpureum vestimentum,* in quibus illuserant ei, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo[^p491-3]: « Tradent eum gentibus ad illudendum et flagellandum ». *Et dicit eis: Ecce, homo; ecce,* ostensio flagellati et vituperati in hoc, quod se regem fecerat. Augustinus: « Spinis coronatus est, ludibriosa veste amictus est, amaris conviciis illusus est, alapis caesus est; fervet ignominia, frigescat invidia ».

6. (Vers. 6.). *Cum ergo vidissent eum.* Hic ad Christi ostensionem sequitur tertio *induratio pertinaciae Iudaeorum,* maxime pontificum; unde dicit: *Cum ergo vidissent eum pontifices et ministri, clamabant dicentes: Crucifige, crucifige eum.* Isaiae quinto[^p491-4]: « Exspectavi, ut faceret iudicium; et ecce, clamor ». Bis dicunt *crucifige* ad ostendendam mentis pertinaciam; Ieremiae duodecimo: « Facta est hereditas mea quasi leo in silva; dedit contra me vocem »; et Psalmus: « Aperuerunt super me os suum sicut leo rapiens et rugiens », propter clamorem.

7. *Dicit eis Pilatus.* Supra nisus est Pilatus liberare Christum iudicio benignitatis et severitatis; hic nititur *iudicio aequitatis*[^p491-5], recurrens ad innocentiam, quod aequum est dimittere innocentem et nunquam sine causa occidere. Et huic Iudaei dupliciter resistunt: primo *accusando* et postea *comminando.* Unde in hac parte innuuntur plura: primo, *Christi iustificatio;* secundo, *ipsius accusatio,* ibi[^p491-6]: *Responderunt Iudaei;* tertio, *accusationis inquisitio;* ibi: *Cum ergo audisset Pilatus hos sermones;* quarto vero subiungitur *Iudaeorum comminatio,* ibi: *Iudaei autem clamabant.*

Primo ergo ponitur *Christi iustificatio,* propter quam Pilatus non vult eum damnare, sed offert Iudaeis; unde dicit: *Dicit eis Pilatus,* qui quaerebant, Christum crucifigi: *Accipite eum vos et crucifigite; ego enim non invenio in eo causam;* et ideo sine causa nolo crucifigere. Vere non inveniebat causam, quia, primae Petri secundo[^p491-7], « peccatum non fecit, nec inventus est dolus in ore eius ».

8. (Vers. 7.). *Responderunt ei Iudaei.* Hic ponitur *accusatio Iudaeorum;* unde dixerunt: *Nos Legem habemus,* quam scilicet observamus de voluntate Romanorum principum. Legem allegant pro se, cum sit contra se; supra septimo[^p491-8]: « Nonne Moyses dedit vobis Legem, et nemo ex vobis facit Legem »? *Et secundum Legem debet mori, quia Filium Dei se fecit,* et ita blasphemavit; Levitici vigesimo quarto: « Qui blasphemaverit morte moriatur »; unde supra decimo: « De bono opere non lapidamus te, sed de blasphemia, et quia tu, homo cum sis, facis te ipsum Deum ».

9. (Vers. 8.). *Cum ergo audisset.* Hic ponitur tertio *inquisitio*[^p491-9]. Quaerit igitur, motus ex accusatione Iudaeorum; unde dicit: *Pilatus, cum audisset hunc sermonem, magis timuit,* ne, si dimitteret, contra Legem faceret; et si non dimitteret, Filium Dei crucifigeret. Non debet bonus iudex timere; unde Ecclesiastici septimo[^p491-10]: « Noli quaerere fieri iudex, nisi virtute valeas irrumpere iniquitates ». Ideo subdit:

10. (Vers. 9.). *Iterum ingressus in praetorium, dicit ad Iesum*[^p491-11]*: Unde es tu?* Quaerit de origine, quia ipsum de hoc accusabant; magnam et supra vires quaerit quaestionem; unde Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio: « Generationem eius quis enarrabit »? Et ideo non audit responsionem; Ecclesiastici tertio: « Altiora te ne quaesieris, et fortiora te ne scrutatus fueris ». Unde dicit: *Iesus autem responsum non dedit ei,* ut verificaretur illud Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p491-12]: « Quasi agnus coram tondente se obmutescet et non aperiet os suum ». Augustinus: « Ad hoc data est de agno similitudo, ut in suo silentio non reus, sed innocens haberetur »; Threnorum tertio: « De ore Altissimi non egredientur nec bona nec mala ». Sic ergo tacentem inducit ad respondendum; propterea:

11. (Vers. 10.). *Dicit*[^p491-13] *ei Pilatus: Mihi non loqueris?* quasi dicat: erras, quod quaestionem
<!-- page 492 -->
meam contemnis, cum deberes potius meam captare benevolentiam tanquam in manibus meis positus; et hoc infert: *Nescis, quia potestatem habeo crucifigere te, et potestatem habeo dimittere te?* et ideo et timendus et obsecrandus. Unde de hoc mirabatur, quod Christus in tanta necessitate non captabat eius gratiam; Matthaei vigesimo septimo[^p492-1]: « Et non respondit ei ullum verbum, ita ut miraretur praeses ». Et quia Pilatus se iactavit, Christus prudenter respondit quod eius superbiam humiliavit, docens « non altum sapere ».

12. (Vers. 11.). *Respondit Iesus: Non haberes in me potestatem*[^p492-2]*, nisi datum tibi esset desuper;* Augustinus: *Desuper,* id est a Deo, secundum illud ad Romanos decimo tertio: « Non est potestas nisi a Deo ». — Sed secundum istam expositionem nullam videtur habere consequentiam quod sequitur: *Propterea qui tradidit me* etc.; ideo exponit Victor[^p492-3]: *Desuper,* id est a Caesare; et quia timore exerces, *qui tradidit me tibi maius peccatum habet;* quia magis peccat qui ex malitia, quam qui ex infirmitate vel impotentia. Et quia prudenter responderat, Pilatus eum liberare intendebat; unde dicit:

13. (Vers. 12.). *Et exinde quaerebat Pilatus dimittere eum.* Intelligens, potestatem suam sub alterius potestate constitutam, nolebat damnare innocentem; ideo volebat dimittere eum.

*Iudaei autem clamabant.* Hic ponitur quartum, scilicet *comminatio Iudaeorum;* et quia non poterant Christum damnare per accusationem, convertunt se ad clamosam comminationem; unde imponunt Pilato, si eum dimittat, infidelitatem contra Caesarem; ideo dicit: *Iudaei autem clamabant,* contra Pilatum scilicet, eo quod vellet Christum dimittere, *dicentes: Si hunc dimittis, non es amicus Caesaris,* immo proditor; quia dimittis inimicum Caesaris. Et hoc probant: *Omnis enim, qui se regem facit, contradicit Caesari;* quia tunc Caesar erat monarcha, unde ipsius erat dare potestatem regnandi; qui ergo se ipsum regem constituebat, contradicebat Caesari. Et haec comminatio Pilatum retraxit a veritate iudicii in liberando, quia timuit accusari apud Caesarem; Proverbiorum vigesimo nono[^p492-4]: « Qui timet hominem cito corruet ». Propter hoc dicebat Dominus discipulis Matthaei decimo: « Nolite timere eos qui occidunt corpus ».

14. *Pilatus ergo*[^p492-5]*, cum audisset.* Ostensa est perversitas Iudaeorum in malignitate resistendo iudicio veritatis; hic ostenditur eorum perversitas *in extorquendo iudicium falsitatis* de condemnatione Christi. Describitur hic iudicium condemnationis Christi, quod quidem describitur hoc ordine. Primo notatur *loci et temporis circumstantia;* secundo, *condemnationis causa;* tertio vero, *ipsa sententia.*

(Vers. 13.). *Locus* notatur, cum dicit: *Pilatus ergo, cum audisset hos sermones.* In quo notatur, quod ipse iam ceciderat a iusta voluntate timore Caesaris, quasi dicat: timore perterritus; *adduxit Iesum foras,* id est ad locum publicum; ideo *foras,* ut in conspectu omnium condemnaretur. *Et sedit pro tribunali in loco, qui dicitur Lithostratos*[^p492-6]. Hic describitur locus specialiter; *pro tribunali,* id est in iudiciali. *Tribunal* enim est iudicum, *solium* regum, sed *cathedra* doctorum. *Lithos,* sicut dicit Victor[^p492-7],
<!-- page 493 -->
interpretatur *iudicium; stratos* a *sterno* valet pavimentum vario stratum lapide, in cuiusmodi loco erat consistorium, ubi iudicabatur. Et hic locus *Hebraice autem Gabbatha,* id est sublimitas, quia solent iudices in sublimi loco sedere. — Vel *Gabbatha* interpretatur *picturatio* vel politio et significat idem quod *Lithostratos,* id est locus iudicialis, pictus vel politus[^p493-1].

15. (Vers. 14.). *Erat autem parasceve.* Ecce, subiungitur *tempus,* quia erat feria sexta, quae dicitur *parasceve paschae,* id est praeparatio paschae, quia fiebat praeparatio in die Veneris pro die Sabbati, sicut dicitur Exodi decimo sexto[^p493-2], quod illo die colligebant pro duobus diebus. *Hora quasi sexta.* Hic tempus tangitur speciale, et dicit *quasi,* quia difficile est temporis horam determinare[^p493-3].

*Et dicit Iudaeis.* Hic notatur *causa* condemnationis Christi, et haec non fuit eius culpa, sed *duritia perversitatis* in Iudaeis et *negatio veritatis: duritia perversitatis* in hoc, quod petunt, eum damnari morte crudelissima; unde dicit: *Ecce, rex vester,* quasi dicat Pilatus: non habetis aliquid aliud obiicere contra ipsum.

16. (Vers. 15.). *Illi autem clamabant: Tolle, tolle, crucifige eum.* Geminatio significat intensionem affectus ad mortem Christi; Ieremiae vigesimo sexto[^p493-4]: « Locuti sunt sacerdotes et prophetae ad principes et ad omnem populum dicentes: Iudicium mortis est viro huic ». Et quia non sufficiebat *duritia perversitatis,* nisi adderetur *negatio veritatis;* ideo sequitur inquisitio Pilati: *Regem*[^p493-5] *vestrum crucifigam?* quasi in hoc verbo notans eis voluntatem perversam in hoc, quod suam ignominiam appetebant. Sed vituperium regis redundat in populum, et propterea, ut vituperium videantur fugere, *negant verum;* unde: *Responderunt pontifices dicentes*[^p493-6]*: Non habemus regem nisi Caesarem;* et ita hic non est rex noster, nec ignominia eius est nostra. Et ex hac causa condemnavit eum Pilatus; unde dicit:

17. (Vers. 16.). *Tunc*[^p493-7] *tradidit eis illum, ut crucifigeretur.* Hic tangitur tertium, scilicet *ipsa sententia,* qua condemnatus est poena crucis; et bene dicit: *Tradidit eis,* quia ad eorum petitionem fecit; Actuum tertio[^p493-8]: « Deus glorificavit Filium suum Iesum, quem vos quidem tradidistis et negastis ante faciem Pilati, iudicante illo dimitti »; et Lucae vigesimo tertio: « Pilatus adiudicavit fieri petitionem ipsorum », scilicet Iudaeorum. Fecit Pilatus contra Legem; Exodi vigesimo tertio: « Non sequeris turbam ad faciendum malum, nec in iudicio acquiesces sententiae plurimorum, ut a vero devies ».

### Quaestiones.

18. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic de illa consuetudine, quam habebant de dimissione vincti[^p493-9], utrum esset laudabilis. — Et videtur, quod sic, quia Dominus misericordiam acceptat magis quam iudicium, sicut patet de muliere adultera, supra octavo[^p493-10].

Sed tunc *obiicitur:* quia ex Lege praeceptum erat, ut malefici occiderentur[^p493-11]: ergo absolvere nocentes erat contra Legem et consuetudo mala.

Dicendum, quod consuetudo bona erat, dum tamen liberaretur qui magis congruebat; quia, etsi tunc esset tempus iustitiae, tamen debebant esse memores misericordiae in eis *factae* et *futurae* in paschate; et ideo quasi in signum hoc erat eis licitum[^p493-12].

Quod obiicit de Lege, dicendum, quod excellentia solemnitatis praeponderabat acerbitati Legis[^p493-13].
<!-- page 494 -->

19. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur; utrum peccaverint petendo Barabbam[^p494-1]. — Quod *sic,* videtur: Actuum tertio improperatur eis: *Petistis virum homicidam dari vobis; auctorem vero vitae interemistis* ».

Sed quod *non peccaverint,* videtur: quia licebat eis petere quem volebant sine distinctione[^p494-2].

**Respondeo,** quod non peccaverunt in hoc, quod liberaverunt eum qui dignus erat morte; sed in hoc, quod eum innocenti praetulerunt; unde fuit ibi personae acceptatio. Unde Augustinus[^p494-3]: « Non reprehendimus, o Iudaei, quod per pascha liberatis nocentem, sed quod occiditis innocentem ».

20. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc, quod milites ita irriserunt Christum, cum tamen non dicantur habuisse mandatum a Pilato. Unde hoc fuit?

Respondet Chrysostomus[^p494-4], quod hoc pecunia faciebant, quia corrupti erant a principibus sacerdotum.

21. Quaest. IV. Item de hoc quaeritur, quod hic Ioannes dicit Christum a militibus illusum *ante* sententiam condemnationis, cum Matthaei vigesimo septimo[^p494-5] dicatur, quod milites *post* condemnationem illuserunt Domino: *Tradidit illis Iesum, ut crucifigeretur;* et tunc dicitur, quod milites illuserunt ei.

Potest dici, quod bis factum est: et ante condemnationem et post[^p494-6]. — Vel Matthaeus hoc dicit per recapitulationem, quia ante factum fuerat.

22. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur: quare Dominus non respondit Pilato quaerenti: *Unde es tu?* — Videtur hoc fuisse arrogantiae.

**Respondeo,** quod ideo ad quaestionem illam non respondit, quia eam capere non poterat; unde Dominus discipulis suis dicebat supra decimo sexto[^p494-7]: *Multa habeo vobis dicere, sed non potestis portare modo.* Sic et nunc.

23. Quaest. VI. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Non haberes in me potestatem, nisi datum esset tibi desuper.* — Ergo videtur, quod in hoc non peccaverit, quod Christum crucifixit, quia hoc sibi a Deo donatum.

Dicendum, quod in potestate duo considerantur, scilicet ipsa *praesidentia* et *exsecutio. Prima* est a Deo, sicut dicit Apostolus[^p494-8], sed *exsecutio* non semper a Deo. Unde Dominus voluit dicere Pilato, quod potestatem a se non habebat; ideo de ipsa non debebat gloriari. *Exsecutio* vero a Deo fuit *permissive,* sicut dicit Chrysostomus[^p494-9].

24. Quaest. VII. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Qui tradidit me tibi maius peccatum habet.* — Videtur falsum, quia Iudaei peccaverunt ex ignorantia; credebant enim, eum esse blasphemum et dignum morte; sed Pilatus scienter occidit innocentem[^p494-10].

**Respondeo:** Utrique peccaverunt, sed Iudaei magis, quia ex malitia, sed Pilatus ex infirmitate timoreque Caesaris[^p494-11].

Et quod obiicit, quod *ignoranter;* dicendum, quod ignorabant, ipsum esse Deum, sed non, innocentem; vel si utrumque ignorabant, ex malitia erat; unde Sapientiae secundo[^p494-12]: *Excaecavit eos malitia eorum.*

25. Quaest. VIII. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod Christus condemnatus fuit *hora quasi sexta;* quia Marci decimo quinto[^p494-13] dicitur: *Erat autem hora diei quasi tertia, et crucifixerunt eum.* — Item, Matthaei vigesimo septimo dicitur, quod *tenebrae factae sunt ab hora sexta usque in horam nonam:* ergo ante horam sextam fuit iudicatus.

Respondet Augustinus[^p494-14]: « Hora tertia crucifixus est Dominus linguis Iudaeorum, hora sexta manibus militum; ut intelligamus, horam quintam fuisse transactam, et aliquid de sexta coeptum, quando Pilatus sedit pro tribunali; et cum duceretur, et gererentur quae gesta narrantur; hora sexta completa est, ex qua hora usque in nonam sol obscuratus est ».
<!-- page 495 -->

Aliter respondet Victor[^p495-1], quod Dominus hora media inter tertiam et sextam crucifixus est; et quia extrema sunt nominata, et medium saepe sortitur nomen extremorum; ideo dicit Marcus hora *tertia,* sed Ioannes *sexta,* non simpliciter, sed cum determinatione: *quasi sexta*[^p495-2].

26. Quaest. IX. Item quaeritur de hoc, quod Iudaei petierunt, Dominum crucifigi; quare magis illo genere mortis quam alio voluerunt Dominum occidi?

Respondet Chrysostomus[^p495-3], quod « exprobratissima haec erat mors. Formidantes igitur, ne aliqua eius post ipsum fiat memoria, student ad supplicium ducere maledictum, nescientes, quoniam per prohibitiones elevatur veritas ». — Ex parte Dei ratio fuit et *mysterium,* quia in ligno peccavimus, et *exemplum,* ne quis turpem formidaret mortem, et *iudicium,* quia[^p495-4] *placuit Deo per stultitiam praedicationis salvos facere credentes.*

## English
<!-- page 490 -->

### Chapter XIX.

1. *Then he took him*[^p490-5] etc. Here Pilate intends to free him by *the judgement of severity,* scourging the innocent one, that he may mitigate the zeal of the Jews; and there are three portions here. In the first is described *the ignominious scourging of Christ;* second, *the showing of the scourged one,* there[^p490-6]: *Pilate went forth again;* third, *the hardening of the Jews,* there: *When therefore the high priests had seen him.*

(Vers. 1.). *The ignominious scourging of Christ* is intimated first in *affliction,* then in *derision.* — He touches *the affliction,* when he says: *Then Pilate took Jesus and scourged him.* Chrysostom[^p490-7]: « Pilate scourged him, perhaps wishing to absolve him and to mitigate the Jewish zeal »; hence in Luke, the twenty-third chapter, Pilate said: « Having chastised him, I will release him », that is, afflicted with scourges, the more to mitigate the zeal of the Jews. — To the affliction was added *mockery:*

2. (Vers. 2.). *And the soldiers platting* etc. And because kings are wont to be honoured by sign, by habit and by word, or salutation, and by the showing of reverence; therefore they mock him in four ways[^p490-8]: first in *the sign of the crown,* which is a royal sign, hence: *Platting,* that is, folding, *a crown of thorns,* that is, of sharp and pricking sea rushes after the manner of thorns; *they put it upon his head.* Of this crown, Canticles, the third chapter[^p490-9]: « Go forth, ye daughters of Sion, and see king Solomon in the diadem, wherewith his mother crowned him », that is, the synagogue. — Then they mock him in *habit* or clothing; hence it is said: *And they put about him a purple garment,* as though he were a king; in which it is signified that his flesh was to be empurpled with blood; which was signified in Genesis, the thirty-seventh chapter[^p490-10]: « They took Joseph's coat and dipped it in the blood of a kid, which they had killed »; and Isaias, the sixty-third chapter: « Why then is thy apparel red, and thy garments like theirs that tread in the winepress »? — Then the mocking in *salutation;* hence he says:

3. (Vers. 3.). *And they came to him and said: Hail, king of the Jews,* and this indeed in reproach; Lamentations, the third chapter[^p490-11]: « He shall be filled with reproaches ». — Not only in word, but in *reverence,* because in place of reverence they gave blows; hence he says: *And they gave him blows;* Lamentations, the third chapter: « He shall give his cheek to him that striketh him »; and Isaias, the fiftieth chapter: « I have given my body to the strikers, and my cheeks to them that plucked them ».

4. (Vers. 4.). *Pilate went forth*[^p490-12] *again.* Here is set *the showing of Christ scourged and mocked,* that they might be moved to piety. And first indeed innocence is repeated; hence: *He saith: Behold, I bring him forth unto you.* He subjoins the reason: *That you may know that I find*
<!-- page 491 -->
*no cause in him,* namely « of death[^p491-1] » or of affliction; and yet I have afflicted him.

5. (Vers. 5.). *Jesus therefore came forth*[^p491-2]. Augustine: « Not glorious in command, but full of reproach »; hence there follows: *Bearing the crown of thorns and the purple garment,* in which they had mocked him, according to that of Matthew, the twentieth chapter[^p491-3]: « They shall deliver him to the Gentiles to be mocked and scourged ». *And he saith to them: Behold, the man; behold,* the showing of one scourged and vituperated in this, that he had made himself king. Augustine: « He was crowned with thorns, he was clothed in a garment of mockery, he was mocked with bitter revilings, he was struck with blows; ignominy seethes, let envy grow cold ».

6. (Vers. 6.). *When therefore they had seen him.* Here, upon the showing of Christ, there follows third *the hardening of the pertinacity of the Jews,* chiefly of the high priests; hence he says: *When therefore the high priests and the ministers had seen him, they cried out, saying: Crucify, crucify him.* Isaias, the fifth chapter[^p491-4]: « I looked that he should do judgement, and behold a cry ». They say *crucify* twice to show the pertinacity of the mind; Jeremias, the twelfth chapter: « My inheritance is become as a lion in the wood; it hath given forth its voice against me »; and the Psalm: « They have opened their mouth against me, as a lion ravening and roaring », on account of the cry.

7. *Pilate saith to them.* Above Pilate strove to free Christ by the judgement of benignity and of severity; here he strives by *the judgement of equity*[^p491-5], having recourse to innocence, because it is equitable to release the innocent and never to kill without cause. And to this the Jews resist in two ways: first *by accusing* and afterwards *by threatening.* Hence in this part several things are hinted: first, *the justification of Christ;* second, *his accusation,* there[^p491-6]: *The Jews answered;* third, *the inquiry into the accusation;* there: *When Pilate therefore had heard these words;* fourth, there is subjoined *the threat of the Jews,* there: *But the Jews cried out.*

First, then, is set *the justification of Christ,* on account of which Pilate will not condemn him, but offers him to the Jews; hence he says: *Pilate saith to them,* who were seeking that Christ be crucified: *Take him you, and crucify him; for I find no cause in him;* and therefore I will not crucify without cause. Truly he found no cause, because, the first of Peter, the second chapter[^p491-7], « he did no sin, neither was guile found in his mouth ».

8. (Vers. 7.). *The Jews answered him.* Here is set *the accusation of the Jews;* hence they said: *We have a Law,* which namely we observe by the will of the Roman princes. They allege the Law for themselves, though it is against them; above, the seventh chapter[^p491-8]: « Did not Moses give you the Law, and yet none of you keepeth the Law »? *And according to the Law he ought to die, because he made himself the Son of God,* and so he blasphemed; Leviticus, the twenty-fourth chapter: « He that blasphemeth, dying let him die »; hence above, the tenth chapter: « For a good work we stone thee not, but for blasphemy, and because that thou, being a man, makest thyself God ».

9. (Vers. 8.). *When therefore he had heard.* Here is set third *the inquiry*[^p491-9]. He inquires, then, moved by the accusation of the Jews; hence he says: *Pilate, when he had heard this saying, feared the more,* lest, if he released him, he should act against the Law; and if he did not release him, he should crucify the Son of God. A good judge ought not to fear; hence Ecclesiasticus, the seventh chapter[^p491-10]: « Seek not to be made a judge, unless thou have strength enough to break through iniquities ». Therefore he subjoins:

10. (Vers. 9.). *Having entered into the praetorium again, he saith to Jesus*[^p491-11]*: Whence art thou?* He asks about his origin, because they were accusing him of this; he asks a great question, and beyond his powers; hence Isaias, the fifty-third chapter: « Who shall declare his generation »? And therefore he does not hear a response; Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter: « Seek not the things that are too high for thee, and search not into things above thy ability ». Hence he says: *But Jesus gave him no answer,* that there might be verified that of Isaias, the fifty-third chapter[^p491-12]: « As a lamb before his shearer, he shall be dumb, and he shall not open his mouth ». Augustine: « For this was the likeness of a lamb given, that in his silence he might be held not guilty, but innocent »; Lamentations, the third chapter: « Out of the mouth of the Highest there shall come forth neither good things nor evil ». So, then, he induces the silent one to respond; therefore:

11. (Vers. 10.). *Pilate*[^p491-13] *saith to him: Speakest thou not to me?* as if to say: you err, in that you
<!-- page 492 -->
contemn my question, when you ought rather to court my benevolence, as one placed in my hands; and this he infers: *Knowest thou not that I have power to crucify thee, and I have power to release thee?* and therefore I am both to be feared and to be entreated. Hence he wondered at this, that Christ in so great a necessity was not courting his favour; Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p492-1]: « And he answered him not to any word, so that the governor wondered ». And because Pilate vaunted himself, Christ prudently answered what humbled his pride, teaching « not to be highminded ».

12. (Vers. 11.). *Jesus answered: Thou shouldst not have power against me*[^p492-2]*, unless it were given thee from above;* Augustine: *From above,* that is, from God, according to that to the Romans, the thirteenth chapter: « There is no power but from God ». — But according to this exposition what follows seems to have no consequence: *Therefore he that hath delivered me* etc.; therefore Victor expounds[^p492-3]: *From above,* that is, from Caesar; and because you exercise it out of fear, *he that hath delivered me to thee hath the greater sin;* because he sins more who sins out of malice than he who sins out of infirmity or impotence. And because he had answered prudently, Pilate intended to free him; hence he says:

13. (Vers. 12.). *And from thenceforth Pilate sought to release him.* Understanding that his power was constituted under the power of another, he was unwilling to condemn the innocent; therefore he wished to release him.

*But the Jews cried out.* Here is set the fourth, namely *the threat of the Jews;* and because they could not condemn Christ by accusation, they turn to a clamorous threat; hence they impute to Pilate, if he release him, infidelity against Caesar; therefore he says: *But the Jews cried out,* against Pilate, namely, because he wished to release Christ, *saying: If thou release this man, thou art not Caesar's friend,* nay, a traitor; because you release Caesar's enemy. And this they prove: *For whosoever maketh himself a king, speaketh against Caesar;* because Caesar was then monarch, whence it was his to give the power of reigning; whoever therefore constituted himself king, spoke against Caesar. And this threat drew Pilate back from the truth of judgement in freeing him, because he feared to be accused before Caesar; Proverbs, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p492-4]: « He that feareth man shall quickly fall ». On account of this the Lord said to the disciples, Matthew, the tenth chapter: « Fear ye not them that kill the body ».

14. *Pilate therefore*[^p492-5]*, when he had heard.* The perversity of the Jews was shown in malignity, in resisting the judgement of truth; here their perversity is shown *in extorting the judgement of falsity* concerning the condemnation of Christ. Here is described the judgement of Christ's condemnation, which indeed is described in this order. First is noted *the circumstance of place and time;* second, *the cause of the condemnation;* third, *the sentence itself.*

(Vers. 13.). *The place* is noted, when he says: *Pilate therefore, when he had heard these words.* In which it is noted that he had already fallen from a just will by fear of Caesar, as if to say: terrified by fear; *he brought Jesus forth,* that is, to a public place; therefore *forth,* that he might be condemned in the sight of all. *And he sat down in the judgement seat, in the place that is called Lithostratos*[^p492-6]. Here the place is described specially; *in the judgement seat,* that is, in the judicial seat. For the *tribunal* is of judges, the *throne* of kings, but the *chair* of doctors. *Lithos,* as Victor says[^p492-7],
<!-- page 493 -->
is interpreted *judgement; stratos,* from *sterno,* means a pavement strewn with varied stone, in which kind of place was the consistory, where judgement was given. And this place, *but in Hebrew Gabbatha,* that is, height, because judges are wont to sit in a high place. — Or *Gabbatha* is interpreted *picturing* or polishing, and signifies the same as *Lithostratos,* that is, a judicial place, pictured or polished[^p493-1].

15. (Vers. 14.). *And it was the parasceve.* Behold, *the time* is subjoined, because it was the sixth day of the week, which is called *the parasceve of the pasch,* that is, the preparation of the pasch, because the preparation was made on Friday for the day of the Sabbath, as is said in Exodus, the sixteenth chapter[^p493-2], that on that day they gathered for two days. *About the sixth hour.* Here the special time is touched, and he says *about,* because it is difficult to determine the hour of the time[^p493-3].

*And he saith to the Jews.* Here is noted *the cause* of the condemnation of Christ, and this was not his fault, but *the hardness of perversity* in the Jews and *the denial of truth: the hardness of perversity* in this, that they ask that he be condemned to a most cruel death; hence he says: *Behold your king,* as if Pilate said: you have nothing else to object against him.

16. (Vers. 15.). *But they cried out: Away with him, away with him, crucify him.* The doubling signifies the intensity of the affection toward the death of Christ; Jeremias, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p493-4]: « The priests and the prophets spoke to the princes, and to all the people, saying: The judgement of death is for this man ». And because the hardness of perversity did not suffice, unless *the denial of truth* were added; therefore there follows Pilate's inquiry: *Shall I crucify your king*[^p493-5]*?* as if in this word noting to them their perverse will in this, that they were craving their own ignominy. But the vituperation of the king redounds upon the people, and therefore, that they may seem to flee the vituperation, *they deny the true;* hence: *The high priests answered, saying*[^p493-6]*: We have no king but Caesar;* and so this man is not our king, nor is his ignominy ours. And from this cause Pilate condemned him; hence he says:

17. (Vers. 16.). *Then*[^p493-7] *he delivered him to them to be crucified.* Here the third is touched, namely *the sentence itself,* by which he was condemned to the punishment of the cross; and he says well: *He delivered him to them,* because he did it at their petition; Acts, the third chapter[^p493-8]: « God hath glorified his Son Jesus, whom you indeed delivered up and denied before the face of Pilate, when he judged he should be released »; and Luke, the twenty-third chapter: « Pilate gave sentence that their petition should be done », namely of the Jews. Pilate acted against the Law; Exodus, the twenty-third chapter: « Thou shalt not follow the multitude to do evil: neither shalt thou yield in judgement to the opinion of the most part, to stray from the truth ».

### Questions.

18. Quaest. I. But it is asked here about that custom which they had concerning the release of a prisoner[^p493-9], whether it was laudable. — And it seems that it was, because the Lord accepts mercy more than judgement, as is clear from the woman taken in adultery, above, the eighth chapter[^p493-10].

But then *it is objected:* because by the Law it was commanded that evildoers be killed[^p493-11]: therefore to absolve the nocent was against the Law, and the custom bad.

It must be said that the custom was good, provided that he was freed who was the more fitting; because, although it was then the time of justice, yet they ought to be mindful of the mercy *done* among them and *to be done* at the pasch; and therefore, as it were in sign, this was permitted to them[^p493-12].

To what he objects about the Law, it must be said that the excellence of the solemnity outweighed the bitterness of the Law[^p493-13].
<!-- page 494 -->

19. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked; whether they sinned in asking for Barabbas[^p494-1]. — That they *did,* it seems: in Acts, the third chapter, it is cast up against them: *You asked that a man who was a murderer be given unto you; but the author of life you killed* ».

But that they *did not sin,* it seems: because it was permitted them to ask for whom they would, without distinction[^p494-2].

**I respond** that they did not sin in this, that they freed one who was worthy of death; but in this, that they preferred him to the innocent one; whence there was there an acceptance of persons. Hence Augustine[^p494-3]: « We do not reprehend you, O Jews, that through the pasch you free the nocent, but that you kill the innocent ».

20. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked about this, that the soldiers so mocked Christ, though they are not said to have had a mandate from Pilate. Whence was this?

Chrysostom responds[^p494-4] that they did this for money, because they had been corrupted by the princes of the priests.

21. Quaest. IV. Likewise about this it is asked, that here John says that Christ was mocked by the soldiers *before* the sentence of condemnation, whereas in Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p494-5], it is said that the soldiers mocked the Lord *after* the condemnation: *He delivered Jesus to them to be crucified;* and then it is said that the soldiers mocked him.

It can be said that it was done twice: both before the condemnation and after[^p494-6]. — Or Matthew says this by recapitulation, because it had been done before.

22. Quaest. V. Likewise it is asked: why did the Lord not answer Pilate when he asked: *Whence art thou?* — This seems to have been of arrogance.

**I respond** that he therefore did not answer that question, because he could not take it in; hence the Lord said to his disciples, above, the sixteenth chapter[^p494-7]: *I have yet many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now.* So also now.

23. Quaest. VI. Likewise it is asked about this that he says: *Thou shouldst not have power against me, unless it were given thee from above.* — Therefore it seems that he did not sin in this, that he crucified Christ, because this was granted to him by God.

It must be said that in power two things are considered, namely the *presidency* itself and the *execution. The first* is from God, as the Apostle says[^p494-8], but the *execution* not always from God. Hence the Lord wished to say to Pilate that he did not have power from himself; therefore he ought not to glory in it. But the *execution* was from God *permissively,* as Chrysostom says[^p494-9].

24. Quaest. VII. Likewise it is asked about this that he says: *He that hath delivered me to thee hath the greater sin.* — It seems false, because the Jews sinned out of ignorance; for they believed that he was a blasphemer and worthy of death; but Pilate knowingly killed the innocent[^p494-10].

**I respond:** Both sinned, but the Jews more, because out of malice, but Pilate out of infirmity and fear of Caesar[^p494-11].

And as to what he objects, that *ignorantly;* it must be said that they were ignorant that he was God, but not that he was innocent; or if they were ignorant of both, it was out of malice; hence Wisdom, the second chapter[^p494-12]: *Their own malice blinded them.*

25. Quaest. VIII. Likewise it is asked about this that he says, that Christ was condemned *about the sixth hour;* because in Mark, the fifteenth chapter[^p494-13], it is said: *And it was the third hour of the day, and they crucified him.* — Likewise, in Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter, it is said that *there was darkness from the sixth hour until the ninth hour:* therefore he was judged before the sixth hour.

Augustine responds[^p494-14]: « At the third hour the Lord was crucified by the tongues of the Jews, at the sixth hour by the hands of the soldiers; that we may understand that the fifth hour had passed, and something of the sixth had begun, when Pilate sat in the judgement seat; and while he was being led, and the things were being done which are narrated as done, the sixth hour was completed, from which hour until the ninth the sun was darkened ».
<!-- page 495 -->

Victor responds otherwise[^p495-1], that the Lord was crucified at the hour midway between the third and the sixth; and because the extremes are named, and the middle often takes the name of the extremes; therefore Mark says at the *third* hour, but John the *sixth,* not simply, but with a determination: *about the sixth*[^p495-2].

26. Quaest. IX. Likewise it is asked about this, that the Jews asked that the Lord be crucified; why did they wish the Lord to be killed by that kind of death rather than by another?

Chrysostom responds[^p495-3] that « this was the most opprobrious death. Fearing, then, lest any memory of him should remain after him, they are eager to lead him to an accursed penalty, not knowing that through prohibitions the truth is raised up ». — On the part of God the reason was both a *mystery,* because in wood we sinned, and an *example,* lest anyone should dread a shameful death, and a *judgement,* because[^p495-4] *it pleased God by the foolishness of preaching to save them that believe.*
