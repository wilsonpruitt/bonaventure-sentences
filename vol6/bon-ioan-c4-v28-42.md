# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. IV, Vers. 28-42
## *Iam terminata est pars, in qua Dominus praedicavit et se ostendit mulieri Samaritanae singulariter et private; hic incipit secunda pars, in qua Dominus communiter Samaritanis praedicat et eis se ipsum manifestat*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 296 -->

47. *Reliquit ergo hydriam suam* etc. Iam terminata est pars, in qua Dominus praedicavit et se ostendit mulieri Samaritanae singulariter et private; hic incipit secunda pars, in qua Dominus *communiter Samaritanis* praedicat et eis se ipsum manifestat. Et quia haec manifestatio partim facta est praeconio mulieris, partim sermone Christi, et hoc discipulis audientibus, qui ad hoc idem debebant esse solliciti; ideo proceditur in hac manifestatione secundum hunc ordinem. Primo enim notatur *diligentia mulieris* ad nomen Christi annuntiandum; secundo, *desiderium Christi* ad[^p296-13] suscipiendum; tertio, *animatio discipulo-
<!-- page 297 -->
rum* ad praedicandum; quarto, *Samaritanorum devotio* ad credendum, ad quam ordinantur omnia, quae hic determinantur.

48. (Vers. 28.). Primo igitur describitur *strenuitas diligentiae mulieris,* quam habuit ad manifestationem nominis Christi et conversionem suae gentis, quia statim ivit annuntiare veritatem, quam perceperat; propter quod dicit: *Reliquit ergo hydriam suam mulier.* Audito verbo fidei, reliquit hydriam, ut citius curreret et annuntiaret, ne forte Dominus recederet; vel quia prae fervore oblita eius, pro quo venerat, Christum praeconizare volebat. Unde Chrysostomus[^p297-1]: « Docet nos haec mulier in spiritualium auditione omnia contemnere temporalia ». *Et abiit in civitatem,* ad vulgandum scilicet nomen Christi. Unde et sequitur: *Et dicit hominibus illis:*

49. (Vers. 29.). *Venite et videte hominem, qui dixit mihi*[^p297-2] *quaecumque feci.* Eos attrahebat per hoc, quod secreta revelaverat; non dixit: Venite et videte prophetam; sed vitam suam divulgat, ut omnes evellat et attrahat. *Nunquid ipse est Christus?* Non asseruit, ne decepta videretur; non asseruit, sed dubitando proposuit, ut ipsi pariter dubitent, et per hoc veniant certificari. Chrysostomus[^p297-3]: « Nolebat ex propria admonitione eos inducere, sed ex auditione illius »; propter quod excitati veniunt. Unde sequitur:

50. (Vers. 30.). *Exierunt*[^p297-4] *de civitate et veniebant ad eum.* Recte *exibant,* quia Christus ante portam erat tanquam ignotus et extra portam passus est; unde ad Hebraeos ultimo: « Christus extra portam passus est; exeamus igitur ad eum extra castra, improperium eius portantes ». In hoc innuitur, quod qui volunt doceri a Christo debent relinquere civitatis tumultum; Ieremiae quadragesimo octavo[^p297-5]: « Relinquite civitates et habitate in petra, habitatores Moab »; quia dicitur in Psalmo: « In terra deserta et invia et inaquosa, sic in sancto apparui tibi ».

51. (Vers. 31.). *Interea rogabant* etc. Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *Christi desiderium ad vocatos suscipiendum;* quia eorum salutem desiderat ut cibum, immo amplius quam cibum corporalem in tempore indigentiae, quando erat famelicus. Propterea dicit: *Interea,* id est, dum mulier ivit, et Samaritani veniebant; *rogabant eum discipuli eius*[^p297-6], quasi de Magistro solliciti, *dicentes: Rabbi, manduca;* quia cibos attulerant, et hora comedendi iam transierat. Chrysostomus[^p297-7]: « Videntes eum fatigatum ex itinere et calore et ieiunio, rogabant, ut manducaret »; sed Dominus magis cibum spiritualem desiderans quam corporalem, manducare recusat. Propter quod dicit:

52. (Vers. 32.). *Ille autem dixit*[^p297-8] *eis: Ego cibum habeo manducare, quem vos nescitis;* illum huic praefero. Illum nesciebant discipuli, quia erat spiritualis, cum ipsi essent carnales; primae ad Corinthios secundo: « Animalis homo non percipit ea quae sunt spiritus Dei ». Ideo patet, quod nesciebant, quia carnaliter intelligebant. Unde ait:

53. (Vers. 33.). *Dicebant ergo discipuli ad invicem: Nunquid aliquis attulit illi*[^p297-9] *manducare?* Augustinus: « Quid mirum, si mulier illa non intelligebat *aquam?* Ecce, discipuli non intelligunt *escam* ». Et quia magistri est docere discipulos non intelligentes, ideo sequitur explanatio.

54. (Vers. 34.). *Dicit eis Iesus: Meus cibus est,* quem scilicet vos nescitis, *ut faciam voluntatem eius qui misit me.* Voluntas Dei nostra salus; infra sexto[^p297-10]: « Haec est voluntas Patris mei, qui misit me, ut omnis, qui videt Filium et credit in eum, habeat vitam aeternam ». Hanc voluntatem volebat Dominus consummare per opus redemptionis; ideo dicit: *Ut perficiam opus eius. Opus Dei* dicitur per excellentiam opus misericordiosissimae reconciliationis; unde in Psalmo[^p297-11]: « Miserationes eius super omnia opera eius ». *Opus* igitur *Dei* est nostra ad Deum per fidem conversio; infra sexto: « Opus Dei est, ut credatis in eum, quem ille misit ».

55. *Nonne vos dicitis* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *animatio discipulorum ad praedicandum* propter salutem Samaritanorum. Et ad hoc animat eos tum ex consideratione *habilitatis eorum ad credendum,* tum ex consideratione *mercedis praedicatorum.*

(Vers. 35.). Ad considerandam *habilitatem credentium* instigat sub parabola dicens: *Nonne vos dicitis, quod adhuc quatuor menses sunt, et messis venit?* Ad litteram aestas erat propinqua[^p297-12]; et hoc quidem dicunt homines, cum vident, tempus metendi
<!-- page 298 -->
appropinquare, quod per totum annum exspectant[^p298-1]. *Ecce, dico vobis: Levate oculos,* id est considerationem, *et videte regiones,* id est multitudines gentilium; *quia albae sunt ad messem,* id est propinquae ad fidem, quia parati ad credendum, si est qui annuntiet; Matthaei nono[^p298-2]: « Messis quidem multa, operarii autem pauci. Rogate ergo dominum messis, ut mittat operarios in messem suam ». Sic igitur animat ex consideratione promptitudinis ad credendum; animat etiam ex consideratione *fructus metentium.* Ideo dicit:

56. (Vers. 36.). *Et qui metit mercedem accipit,* non temporalem, sed aeternam. Unde dicit: *Et congregat fructum in vitam aeternam,* « ubi neque aerugo nec tinea demolitur, nec fures effodiunt nec furantur », Matthaei sexto[^p298-3]. De hac mercede dicitur Lucae sexto: « Gaudete et exsultate; ecce enim, merces vestra multa est in caelo ». Et haec merces est communis et metentium et seminantium. Ideo dicit: *Ut et qui seminat* etc. Bene dico, quod fructum congregat qui metit non seminanti tantum, sed etiam sibi; *ut et qui seminat simul gaudeat, et qui metit;* Psalmus[^p298-4]: « Euntes ibant et flebant, mittentes semina sua; venientes autem venient cum exsultatione » etc. Et rationem reddit, quare tam metentes quam seminantes gaudere debent; quia secundum proverbium alii sunt isti et illi. Ideo dicit:

57. (Vers. 37.). *In hoc verbum verum est,* scilicet quod consuevit dici: *Quia alius est qui seminat, alius*[^p298-5] *qui metit;* nam seminaverunt Prophetae, sed post messuerunt Apostoli, et hoc quidem Domini beneficio et imperio, cuius erat messis. Ideo dicit:

58. (Vers. 38.). *Ego misi vos metere quod non laborastis.* De hac messione et missione Matthaei nono[^p298-6]: « Rogate ergo dominum messis, ut mittat operarios in messem suam ». *Alii laboraverunt, et vos in labores eorum introistis;* quia fructus praedicationis eorum colligitis; primae Petri primo: « Revelatum est » Prophetis, « quia non sibi ipsis » prophetabant, « vobis autem annuntiabant ea quae nunc nuntiata sunt vobis ».

59. (Vers. 39.). *Ex civitate autem illa.* Tangitur hic *Samaritanorum devotio ad credendum,* quae inchoata est in sermone mulieris; propter quod dicit: *Ex civitate autem illa multi crediderunt in eum Samaritanorum.* Et ratio subditur: *Propter verbum mulieris testimonium perhibentis,* de eius Divinitate: *Quia dixit mihi*[^p298-7] *quaecumque feci.* Ad testimonium mulieris crediderunt Samaritani, sed Iudaei recusaverunt credere Ioanni; unde Dominus dixit Iudaeis Matthaei vigesimo primo: « Amen dico vobis, quod publicani et meretrices praecedent vos in regno Dei ». Et licet non crederent firmiter, credebant tamen devote. Ideo subdit:

60. (Vers. 40.). *Cum ergo venissent ad eum*[^p298-8] *Samaritani, rogaverunt eum, ut ibi maneret;* Lucae ultimo: « Mane nobiscum, quoniam advesperascit »; quocontra Iudaei eum expulerunt. Chrysostomus[^p298-9]: « Pharisaei, multa signa videntes, non solum non crediderunt, sed a regione abiecerunt ». Unde Matthaei octavo dicitur, quod « tota civitas Gerasenorum exiit obviam Iesu, et viso eo, rogabant eum, ut transiret a finibus eorum ». Et Dominus Samaritanorum precibus condescendit; unde dicitur: *Et mansit ibi duos dies,* ad fidem eorum confirmandam et multiplicandam; quod et fecit. Unde dicit:

61. (Vers. 41.). *Et multo plures crediderunt*[^p298-10] *propter sermonem eius.* Nec mirum, quia, sicut dicit ei Petrus infra sexto, « Domine, ad quem ibimus? Verba vitae aeternae habes ». Nec tantum multiplicavit, sed etiam confirmavit. Unde sequitur:

62. (Vers. 42.). *Et mulieri dicebant: Quia iam non propter loquelam tuam credimus,* quasi mendicantes rationem humanam; *ipsi enim audivimus et scimus, quia hic est vere Salvator mundi;* id est, audiendo scivimus, quia, sicut dicitur ad Romanos decimo[^p298-11], « fides ex auditu, auditus autem
<!-- page 299 -->
per verbum Dei ». *Vere Salvator mundi,* non tantum Iudaeorum; unde supra tertio[^p299-1]: « Sic Deus dilexit mundum, ut Filium suum unigenitum daret » etc. Ideo primae Ioannis secundo: « Ipse est propitiatio pro peccatis nostris, non tantum pro nostris, sed etiam pro totius mundi ». Iste signatus est per Ioseph, de quo dicitur Genesis quadragesimo primo, quod « Pharao vertit nomen eius vocavitque eum lingua Aegyptiaca *Salvatorem mundi* ».

### Quaestiones.

63. Quaest. I. Incidit hic quaestio: cum dicatur in Psalmo[^p299-2]: *Testimonia tua credibilia facta sunt nimis;* et testimonium mulieris sit vanum et fragile, sicut sexus infirmus; videtur, quod Dominus non debuerit per mulierem se ipsum Samaritanis manifestare.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Christus non accipit humanum testimonium propter suam indigentiam, sed solum propter indigentiam audientium[^p299-3]. Quia igitur testimonium mulieris minime erat suspectum Samaritanis, voluit Dominus per mulierem eis innotescere, non quia eius testimonium haberet robur, sed quia Samaritanis credibile. — Alia ratio fuit ad Iudaeorum duritiam condemnandam in iudicio: quia ipsi non crediderunt Ioanni Baptistae, sed Samaritani uni mulierculae, sicut ipse Dominus dicit eis, quod *regina austri et Ninivitae surgent in iudicio* contra Iudaeos, Matthaei duodecimo[^p299-4].

64. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de testimonio mulieris, quod dixit: *Venite et videte virum, qui dixit mihi omnia, quaecumque feci. — Contra:* Non dixit ei nisi de viris, quos habuerat. Aut ergo mulier plura non fecerat, quod falsum est; aut[^p299-5] mentiebatur et falsum dicebat.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod mulier, quando hoc dixit, non tantum respexit ad sermonem dicentis, sed ad sapientiam, per quam dicebat. Et sicut ei unum secretum dixerat, eadem virtute poterat dicere *omnia, quaecumque fecerat.* Ideo mulier volens Christi virtutem exprimere, dixit in universali etc.

65. Quaest. III. Item obiicitur: cum lex evangelica non sit umbrae vel figurae, sed potius veritatis apertae; unde est hoc, quod Dominus discipulis suis loquitur in parabolis? — Videtur, quod sic non deberet loqui, sed aperte.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *loqui in figuris* est dupliciter: aut ita, quod veritas ibi claudatur et *non explicetur* audientibus; et hoc dicit umbram et obscuritatem, et hoc pertinet ad legem veterem, non ad novam. Alio modo est *loqui in similitudinibus,* ita quod similitudo illa *explicetur* et manifestetur; et tunc non est loqui in umbra, sed potius in luce; et hoc modo loquitur Dominus, ut res obscure proposita in figura per expositionem clare intelligatur, et sensus humanus humilietur, et veritas ardentius ametur et firmius teneatur. Veritas enim aliquantulum latens gratior efficitur, dum aperitur[^p299-6].

66. Quaest. IV. Item obiicitur de hoc, quod Dominus nostram salutem suum vocat *cibum,* similiter fidem nostram supra[^p299-7] vocavit suum *potum.* — Hoc non videtur debite dictum. *Cibus* enim conservat eum qui cibatur; sed Christus conservat nos, non nos Christum: ergo non sumus *cibus Christi.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod triplex est in cibo proprietas. Prima est, quod vehementer desideratur; secunda, quod per ipsum ille qui cibatur, reficitur; tertia, quod cibus in corpus traiicitur. — Ratione primae proprietatis est Christus *cibus noster,* quia eum desideramus, et nos, *suus,* quia ipse nostram salutem desiderat[^p299-8]. Ratione secundae proprietatis, quia nutrit et reficit, Christus est *cibus noster,* non e converso. Ratione tertiae nos sumus *cibus Christi,* quia ipse traiicit nos in corpus suum; unde Augustinus[^p299-9] dicit, quod in cruce manducavit latronem.

67. Quaest. V. Est etiam quaestio: cum Dominus videret eos habiles ad credendum, quid est, quod tantum per *duos dies* apud eos voluit morari? — Videtur, quod amplius et diutius debuisset cum eis esse.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod hoc fecit propter vitandum scandalum Iudaeorum, ne indignarentur, quod eis relictis, transiret ad alienigenas.

68. Quaest. VI. Item quaeritur de hoc quod Dominus dicit discipulis suis: *Alius est qui seminat, alius qui metit;* unde dicit Apostolos *messores,* non *seminatores.*

**Sed contra:** 1. *Verbum Dei est semen*[^p299-10]*:* ergo qui praedicat *seminat;* sed Apostoli fuerunt praedicatores: ergo etc.

2. Item, secundae ad Corinthios nono[^p299-11]: *Qui parce seminat parce et metet:* ergo qui nihil seminat nihil metit.

<!-- page 300 -->
3. Item videtur, quod Apostoli non sint *messores,* sed *Angeli;* unde Matthaei decimo tertio[^p300-1]: *Messis est consummatio saeculi, messores sunt Angeli:* non ergo Apostoli.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod haec tria tempora habent ordinem: tempus *legis* Moysaicae, *gratiae* et *gloriae.* Tempus *gratiae* est sicut fructus respectu temporis *legis* Moysaicae; tempus *gloriae* est sicut fructus respectu temporis *gratiae.* — Secundum hoc, quia *messis* dicit collectionem fructus, duplex est messis: prima in tempore *gratiae;* et huius sunt messores Apostoli, sed seminatores Prophetae, qui de hac gratia praedixerunt et populum exspectare fecerunt[^p300-2]. Alia est in tempore *gloriae;* et huius sunt messores Angeli, et seminatores Apostoli quoad aliquos, sed omnes quoad se ipsos. — Et procedunt rationes per diversas vias, quia Dominus loquitur de messe gratiae.

## English
<!-- page 296 -->

47. *The woman therefore left her waterpot* etc. Now the part is ended in which the Lord preached and showed himself to the Samaritan woman singly and privately; here begins the second part, in which the Lord preaches *to the Samaritans in common* and manifests himself to them. And because this manifestation was made partly by the woman's proclaiming, partly by the discourse of Christ, and this with the disciples listening, who ought to have been solicitous for this same thing; therefore one proceeds in this manifestation according to this order. For first is noted *the diligence of the woman* in announcing the name of Christ; second, *the desire of Christ* to[^p296-13] receive them; third, *the encouraging of the disci-
<!-- page 297 -->
ples* to preach; fourth, *the devotion of the Samaritans* to believing, to which all the things that are determined here are ordered.

48. (Verse 28.). First, then, is described *the vigour of the woman's diligence,* which she had in making known the name of Christ and in the conversion of her people, because she went at once to announce the truth which she had perceived; on account of which he says: *The woman therefore left her waterpot.* The word of faith having been heard, she left her waterpot, that she might run and announce the more quickly, lest perhaps the Lord should depart; or because in her fervour, forgetful of that for which she had come, she wished to proclaim Christ. Whence Chrysostom[^p297-1]: « This woman teaches us, in the hearing of spiritual things, to despise all temporal things ». *And she went her way into the city,* namely to make known the name of Christ. Whence also there follows: *And she saith to those men:*

49. (Verse 29.). *Come, and see a man who has told me*[^p297-2] *whatsoever I have done.* She drew them by this, that he had revealed secrets; she did not say: Come and see a prophet; but she makes her own life public, that she may draw all out and draw them on. *Is not he the Christ?* She did not assert it, lest she should seem deceived; she did not assert it, but put it forward by doubting, that they too might doubt, and through this come to be made certain. Chrysostom[^p297-3]: « She did not wish to lead them on by her own admonition, but by hearing him »; on account of which, stirred up, they come. Whence there follows:

50. (Verse 30.). *They went out*[^p297-4] *of the city, and were coming to him.* Rightly *were they going out,* because Christ was before the gate as one unknown, and suffered outside the gate; whence to the Hebrews, the last chapter: « Christ suffered without the gate; let us go forth therefore to him without the camp, bearing his reproach ». In this it is intimated that those who wish to be taught by Christ ought to leave the tumult of the city; Jeremias, the forty-eighth chapter[^p297-5]: « Leave the cities, and dwell in the rock, you that dwell in Moab »; because it is said in the Psalm: « In a desert land, and where there is no way, and no water: so in the holy place have I appeared to thee ».

51. (Verse 31.). *In the mean time they prayed* etc. Here the second is touched on, namely *the desire of Christ to receive those called;* because he desires their salvation as food, nay more than bodily food in the time of need, when he was hungry. For this reason he says: *In the mean time,* that is, while the woman went, and the Samaritans were coming; *his disciples*[^p297-6] *prayed him,* as though solicitous about their Teacher, *saying: Rabbi, eat;* because they had brought food, and the hour of eating had already passed. Chrysostom[^p297-7]: « Seeing him wearied from the journey and the heat and the fasting, they prayed that he would eat »; but the Lord, desiring spiritual food more than bodily food, refuses to eat. On account of which he says:

52. (Verse 32.). *But he said*[^p297-8] *to them: I have meat to eat, which you know not;* that I prefer to this. That the disciples did not know, because it was spiritual, whereas they were carnal; the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter: « The animal man perceiveth not those things that are of the spirit of God ». Therefore it is plain that they did not know it, because they understood carnally. Whence he says:

53. (Verse 33.). *The disciples therefore said one to another: Hath any man brought him*[^p297-9] *to eat?* Augustine: « What wonder, if that woman did not understand the *water?* Behold, the disciples do not understand the *food* ». And because it belongs to a teacher to teach disciples who do not understand, therefore the explanation follows.

54. (Verse 34.). *Jesus saith to them: My meat is,* namely the one which you know not, *to do the will of him that sent me.* The will of God is our salvation; below, in the sixth chapter[^p297-10]: « This is the will of my Father that sent me: that every one who seeth the Son, and believeth in him, may have life everlasting ». This will the Lord wished to consummate through the work of redemption; therefore he says: *That I may perfect his work. The work of God* is called by excellence the work of most merciful reconciliation; whence in the Psalm[^p297-11]: « His tender mercies are over all his works ». *The work,* then, *of God* is our conversion to God through faith; below, in the sixth chapter: « The work of God is that you believe in him whom he hath sent ».

55. *Do not you say* etc. Here the third is touched on, namely *the encouraging of the disciples to preach* for the salvation of the Samaritans. And to this he encourages them both from the consideration of *their aptness for believing,* and from the consideration of *the reward of preachers.*

(Verse 35.). To the consideration of *the aptness of those believing* he urges them under a parable, saying: *Do not you say, that there are yet four months, and the harvest cometh?* According to the letter, summer was near[^p297-12]; and this indeed men say when they see that the time of reaping
<!-- page 298 -->
is drawing near, which they await through the whole year[^p298-1]. *Behold, I say to you: Lift up the eyes,* that is, consideration, *and see the countries,* that is, the multitudes of the Gentiles; *for they are white to harvest,* that is, near to faith, because ready for believing, if there is one who announces; Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p298-2]: « The harvest indeed is great, but the labourers are few. Pray ye therefore the Lord of the harvest, that he send forth labourers into his harvest ». Thus, then, he encourages from the consideration of their promptness for believing; he encourages also from the consideration of *the fruit of those who reap.* Therefore he says:

56. (Verse 36.). *And he that reapeth receiveth wages,* not temporal, but eternal. Whence he says: *And gathereth fruit unto life everlasting,* « where neither the rust nor moth doth consume, and where thieves do not break through nor steal », Matthew, the sixth chapter[^p298-3]. Of this reward it is said in Luke, the sixth chapter: « Rejoice and be glad; for behold, your reward is great in heaven ». And this reward is common both to those who reap and to those who sow. Therefore he says: *That both he that soweth* etc. I say well that he who reaps gathers fruit not for the sower only, but also for himself; *that both he that soweth, and he that reapeth, may rejoice together;* the Psalm[^p298-4]: « Going they went and wept, casting their seeds; but coming they shall come with joyfulness » etc. And he gives the reason why both those who reap and those who sow ought to rejoice; because according to the proverb these are other than those. Therefore he says:

57. (Verse 37.). *For in this the saying is true,* namely what is accustomed to be said: *That it is one that soweth, and another*[^p298-5] *that reapeth;* for the Prophets sowed, but afterwards the Apostles reaped, and this indeed by the kindness and command of the Lord, whose the harvest was. Therefore he says:

58. (Verse 38.). *I have sent you to reap that in which you did not labour.* Of this reaping and sending, Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p298-6]: « Pray ye therefore the Lord of the harvest, that he send forth labourers into his harvest ». *Others have laboured, and you have entered into their labours;* because you gather the fruit of their preaching; the first of Peter, the first chapter: « It was revealed » to the Prophets, « that not to themselves » they prophesied, « but to you they announced those things which are now declared to you ».

59. ### Questions.

63. Question I. A question falls in here: since it is said in the Psalm[^p299-2]: *Thy testimonies are become exceedingly credible;* and the testimony of a woman is vain and frail, as the sex is weak; it seems that the Lord ought not to have manifested himself to the Samaritans through a woman.

**I respond:** It must be said that Christ does not accept human testimony on account of his own need, but only on account of the need of those who hear[^p299-3]. Since, then, the testimony of the woman was in no way suspect to the Samaritans, the Lord willed to become known to them through the woman, not because her testimony had strength, but because it was credible to the Samaritans. — Another reason was for condemning the hardness of the Jews in the judgement: because they did not believe John the Baptist, but the Samaritans believed one poor woman, just as the Lord himself says to them that *the queen of the south and the Ninivites shall rise in judgement* against the Jews, Matthew, the twelfth chapter[^p299-4].

64. Question II. Likewise it is asked about the testimony of the woman, that she said: *Come, and see a man who has told me all things whatsoever I have done. — On the contrary:* He spoke to her only about the husbands whom she had had. Either, then, the woman had not done more things, which is false; or[^p299-5] she was lying and saying what was false.

**I respond:** It must be said that the woman, when she said this, looked not only to the discourse of the one speaking, but to the wisdom through which he spoke. And just as he had told her one secret, by the same power he could tell *all things whatsoever she had done.* Therefore the woman, wishing to express the power of Christ, spoke in the universal etc.

65. Question III. Likewise it is objected: since the law of the Gospel is not of shadow or of figure, but rather of open truth; whence is it that the Lord speaks to his disciples in parables? — It seems that he ought not to speak so, but openly.

**I respond:** It must be said that *to speak in figures* is twofold: either so that the truth is shut up there and *is not unfolded* to the hearers; and this bespeaks shadow and obscurity, and this belongs to the old law, not to the new. In another way it is *to speak in likenesses,* so that that likeness *is unfolded* and made manifest; and then it is not to speak in shadow, but rather in light; and in this way the Lord speaks, that a thing set forth obscurely in a figure may be clearly understood through exposition, and human sense may be humbled, and the truth may be loved more ardently and held more firmly. For the truth, somewhat hidden, is made more pleasing when it is opened[^p299-6].

66. Question IV. Likewise it is objected about this, that the Lord calls our salvation his *meat,* just as above[^p299-7] he called our faith his *drink.* — This does not seem fittingly said. For *meat* preserves him who is fed; but Christ preserves us, not we Christ: therefore we are not *the meat of Christ.*

**I respond:** It must be said that there is a threefold property in meat. The first is that it is vehemently desired; the second, that through it he who is fed is refreshed; the third, that the meat is passed over into the body. — By reason of the first property Christ is *our meat,* because we desire him, and we are *his,* because he desires our salvation[^p299-8]. By reason of the second property, because he nourishes and refreshes, Christ is *our meat,* not conversely. By reason of the third we are *the meat of Christ,* because he passes us over into his body; whence Augustine[^p299-9] says that on the cross he ate the thief.

67. Question V. There is also a question: since the Lord saw them apt for believing, what is it that he wished to stay with them only for *two days?* — It seems that he ought to have been with them more and longer.

**I respond:** It must be said that he did this for avoiding the scandal of the Jews, lest they should be indignant that, they having been left, he passed over to strangers.

68. Question VI. Likewise it is asked about this, that the Lord says to his disciples: *One is he that soweth, another he that reapeth;* whence he calls the Apostles *reapers,* not *sowers.*

**On the contrary:** 1. *The word of God is the seed*[^p299-10]*:* therefore he who preaches *sows;* but the Apostles were preachers: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, in the second to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter[^p299-11]: *He who soweth sparingly shall also reap sparingly:* therefore he who sows nothing reaps nothing.

<!-- page 300 -->
3. Likewise it seems that the Apostles are not *reapers,* but *the Angels;* whence Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p300-1]: *The harvest is the consummation of the world, the reapers are the Angels:* therefore not the Apostles.

**I respond:** It must be said that these three times have an order: the time of the Mosaic *law,* of *grace* and of *glory.* The time of *grace* is as the fruit in respect of the time of the Mosaic *law;* the time of *glory* is as the fruit in respect of the time of *grace.* — According to this, since *harvest* bespeaks the gathering of fruit, the harvest is twofold: the first in the time of *grace;* and of this the Apostles are the reapers, but the Prophets the sowers, who foretold this grace and made the people expect it[^p300-2]. The other is in the time of *glory;* and of this the Angels are the reapers, and the Apostles the sowers as regards some, but all as regards themselves. — And the arguments proceed along different ways, because the Lord speaks of the harvest of grace.
