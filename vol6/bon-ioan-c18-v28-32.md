# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XVIII, Vers. 28-32
## *Hic agitur de tertia Christi examinatione*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 486 -->

51. *Adducunt ergo Iesum* etc. Hic agitur de *tertia Christi examinatione,* quae facta est sub Pilato[^p486-4]. Et in hoc ostenditur malitia Iudaeorum primo, quia nolentem Pilatum *inducunt ad iudicandum;* secundo in hoc, quod volentem Christum tanquam innocentem liberare *inducunt ad condemnandum,* ibi[^p486-5]: *Et cum hoc dixisset, iterum exivit* etc.

Prima in duas; primo enim ponitur *Christi oblatio;* secundo, *oblati examinatio,* ibi[^p486-6]: *Introivit ergo iterum* etc.

In *hac oblatione* notatur primo Iudaeorum *perversitas;* secundo, *falsitas;* tertio, *improbitas*[^p486-7]; quarto, quomodo ex hoc *impletur divina voluntas.*

52. (Vers. 28.). *Perversitas,* quia innocentem in die festo ad condemnationis locum adducunt; propterea dicit: *Adducunt ergo Iesum,* scilicet ministri, *ad Caipham pontificem in praetorium*[^p486-8], quia illuc venerat, ut Christum traderet Pilato. *Erat autem mane;* tempus auget eorum malitiam, quia matutinum; tantum sitiebant mortem Christi, quod diu non poterant exspectare. Erat etiam tempus festivum; unde subdit: *Et ipsi non introierunt in praetorium, ut non contaminarentur, sed*[^p486-9] *manducarent pascha,* quia non poterant contaminati; Numerorum nono: « Immundi super animam hominis non poterant facere phase ». Augustinus: « O impia caecitas! Alienigenae iudicis praetorio contaminari timebant, et fratris innocentis sanguine, non timebant ».

53. (Vers. 29.). *Exivit ergo Pilatus ad eos foras et dixit.* Tangitur hic *Iudaeorum falsitas,* quia Christo crimen falsum imponunt ad inquisitionem Pilati; unde dicit: *Quam accusationem affertis adversus hominem hunc?* quasi diceret: sine causa nolo eum suscipere; Actuum vigesimo quinto[^p486-10]: « Non est consuetudo Romanis condemnare aliquem hominem, priusquam is qui accusatur, praesentes habeat accusatores locumque defendendi accipiat ad
<!-- page 487 -->
abluenda crimina ». Et respondent Iudaei, quod causa est maleficium; unde subdit:

54. (Vers. 30.). *Responderunt et dixerunt ei: Si non esset hic malefactor, non tibi tradidissemus eum,* volentes illam legem servare: « Innocentem et iustum non interficies », Exodi vigesimo tertio[^p487-1]; quasi dicant: est malefactor. Non probant, sed falso imponunt. Augustinus: « Respondeant ab immundis spiritibus liberati, languidi sanati, utrum sit malefactor Iesus. Sed ista dicebant, de quibus per Prophetam praedixerat: Retribuebant mihi mala pro bonis ». Iob trigesimo sexto: « Causa tua quasi impii iudicata est ».

55. (Vers. 31.). *Dixit ergo eis Pilatus.* Hic notatur tertium, scilicet *Iudaeorum dolositas,* quia culpam volebant in alterum refundere; sed Pilatus recusat iudicare eum, non videns, eos habere causam debitam; unde: *Dixit ergo eis Pilatus: Accipite eum vos et secundum Legem vestram iudicate eum,* quae dicit Exodi vigesimo secundo[^p487-2]: « Maleficos non patieris vivere ». Sed Iudaei e contrario ostendunt, quod oportet, ipsum iudicare, quia causa est mortis, et eis non licebat quemquam occidere; propterea dicit: *Dixerunt ergo ei Iudaei: Nobis non licet interficere quemquam;* sed ad te spectat, et hic « reus est mortis[^p487-3] »: ideo necesse habes de hac causa cognoscere. Dolose volunt per alium occidere, sed non excusantur; unde dicitur eis Actuum septimo: « Cuius vos proditores et homicidae fuistis ».

56. (Vers. 32.). *Ut sermo Iesu impleretur.* Hic notatur quartum, scilicet quod *impletur Christi voluntas.* Volebat enim mori morte crucis, qua occidebant gentiles; ideo dicit: *Ut impleretur,* id est, ita factum, ut impleretur sermo; *quem dixit,* supra tertio[^p487-4]: « Sicut Moyses exaltavit serpentem in deserto, ita exaltari oportet Filium hominis », et supra duodecimo; *significans, qua morte moriturus esset,* quia crucis; Matthaei vigesimo: « Tradetur gentibus ad illudendum et flagellandum et crucifigendum ». Et hoc elegit Dominus tanquam vilissimum.

### Quaestiones.

57. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic de hoc quod dicit, quod *adducunt* eum *ad Caipham in praetorium;* quia statim dicit, quod *non intraverunt in praetorium.*

Et respondet Augustinus[^p487-5], quod *praetorium* dicitur primo locus, « ubi habitabat praeses »; secundo vero dicitur locus, ubi iudicabat, in quod si intrarent, reputabant se immundos.

58. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicitur: *Erat mane;* cum supra[^p487-6] dictum sit, quod ante cantum galli *misit eum Annas ligatum ad Caipham.*

**Respondeo:** In hoc discordant Chrysostomus et Augustinus. Nam Augustinus[^p487-7] dicit, quod adduxerunt eum ab Anna ad Caipham in praetorium; et dicit, quod hoc potest intelligi, vel quia Pilatus curiam teneret in domo Caiphae, vel quod Caiphas ad domum Pilati ivisset. Chrysostomus[^p487-8] dicit, quod non adducunt eum modo primo ab Anna, sed in galli cantu duxerunt ad domum Caiphae et postmodum mane a domo eius ad ipsum, qui iverat ad locum praetorii.

59. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Ne contaminarentur, sed manducarent pascha.* — Ergo nondum comederant pascha: ergo cum Christus comederit die praecedenti, non comedit tempore debito.

Ad hoc respondent Graeci, quod verum est, quod Christus praevenit pascha propter mortem[^p487-9]; unde dicunt, quod comedit *fermentatum* et ex eo confecit, et ideo ipsi conficiunt; et ex hoc verbo accipiunt causam erroris.

Nos vero exponimus, quod *pascha* idem est, quod cibus paschalis, et haec sunt azyma, quae comedebant per septem dies[^p487-10].

<!-- page 488 -->

60. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Nobis non licet interficere quemquam.* — Dicitur enim Exodi vigesimo secundo[^p488-1]: *Maleficos non patieris vivere;* et Lex multoties concedit.

Respondet Augustinus[^p488-2]: « Intelligendum est, eos hoc dixisse propter diei festi sanctitatem, quem iam celebrare coeperant ». — Vel dicendum, quod hoc dicunt propter imperium Romanorum, qui eis abstulerant potestatem occidendi, a quibus etiam emebant sacerdotium[^p488-3].

## English
<!-- page 486 -->

51. *They bring therefore Jesus* etc. Here it treats of *the third examination of Christ,* which was made under Pilate[^p486-4]. And in this is shown the malice of the Jews, first, because they *bring* the unwilling Pilate *to judging;* second in this, that they *bring* him, willing to free Christ as innocent, *to condemning,* there[^p486-5]: *And when he had said this, he went out again* etc.

The first in two; for first is set down *the offering of Christ;* second, *the examination of the one offered,* there[^p486-6]: *He went in therefore again* etc.

In *this offering* there is noted, first, the *perversity* of the Jews; second, their *falsity;* third, their *improbity*[^p486-7]; fourth, how from this *the divine will is fulfilled.*

52. (Vers. 28.). *Perversity,* because on the feast day they bring an innocent man to the place of condemnation; therefore he says: *They bring therefore Jesus,* namely the ministers, *to Caiphas the high priest into the praetorium*[^p486-8], because he had come there to deliver Christ to Pilate. *And it was morning;* the time increases their malice, because it was early morning; so greatly did they thirst for the death of Christ that they could not wait long. It was also a festal time; hence he adds: *And they went not into the praetorium, that they might not be defiled, but*[^p486-9] *might eat the pasch,* because, defiled, they could not; Numbers, the ninth chapter: « The unclean by the soul of a man could not make the phase ». Augustine: « O impious blindness! They feared to be defiled by the praetorium of a foreign judge, and by the blood of an innocent brother they did not fear ».

53. (Vers. 29.). *Pilate therefore went out to them outside and said.* Here is touched *the falsity of the Jews,* because they impose a false crime on Christ at the inquiry of Pilate; hence he says: *What accusation bring you against this man?* as if he said: without cause I will not take him up; Acts, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p486-10]: « It is not the custom of the Romans to condemn any man, before that he who is accused have his accusers present and receive a place of defending himself for
<!-- page 487 -->
washing away the crimes ». And the Jews respond that the cause is evildoing; hence he adds:

54. (Vers. 30.). *They answered and said to him: If this man were not a malefactor, we would not have delivered him up to thee,* wishing to keep that law: « The innocent and just thou shalt not put to death », Exodus, the twenty-third chapter[^p487-1]; as if they said: he is a malefactor. They do not prove it, but impose it falsely. Augustine: « Let those answer who were freed from unclean spirits, the languishing who were healed, whether Jesus is a malefactor. But they were saying these things of whom he had foretold through the Prophet: They repaid me evil for good ». Job, the thirty-sixth chapter: « Thy cause hath been judged as that of the wicked ».

55. (Vers. 31.). *Pilate therefore said to them.* Here is noted the third thing, namely *the deceitfulness of the Jews,* because they wished to pour the fault back upon another; but Pilate refuses to judge him, not seeing that they have a due cause; hence: *Pilate therefore said to them: Take him you, and judge him according to your Law,* which says in Exodus, the twenty-second chapter[^p487-2]: « Malefactors thou shalt not suffer to live ». But the Jews on the contrary show that it is fitting that he judge, because it is a cause of death, and it was not lawful for them to kill anyone; therefore he says: *The Jews therefore said to him: It is not lawful for us to put any man to death;* but it pertains to thee, and this man « is guilty of death[^p487-3] »: therefore thou must take cognizance of this cause. Deceitfully they wish to kill through another, but they are not excused; hence it is said to them in Acts, the seventh chapter: « Of whom you have been the traitors and murderers ».

56. (Vers. 32.). *That the word of Jesus might be fulfilled.* Here is noted the fourth thing, namely that *the will of Christ is fulfilled.* For he willed to die by the death of the cross, by which the Gentiles put men to death; therefore he says: *That it might be fulfilled,* that is, it was so done that the word might be fulfilled; *which he said,* above, the third chapter[^p487-4]: « As Moses lifted up the serpent in the desert, so must the Son of man be lifted up », and above, the twelfth chapter; *signifying what death he should die,* namely that of the cross; Matthew, the twentieth chapter: « He shall be delivered to the Gentiles to be mocked and scourged and crucified ». And this the Lord chose as the vilest.

### Questions.

57. Quaest. I. But it is asked here about this, that he says that they *bring* him *to Caiphas into the praetorium;* because straightway he says that *they entered not into the praetorium.*

And Augustine responds[^p487-5] that *praetorium* is said, first, of the place « where the governor dwelt »; but second it is said of the place where he judged, into which, if they entered, they counted themselves unclean.

58. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked about this that is said: *It was morning;* since it was said above[^p487-6] that before the crowing of the cock *Annas sent him bound to Caiphas.*

**I respond:** In this Chrysostom and Augustine disagree. For Augustine[^p487-7] says that they brought him from Annas to Caiphas into the praetorium; and he says that this can be understood either because Pilate held court in the house of Caiphas, or that Caiphas had gone to the house of Pilate. Chrysostom[^p487-8] says that they do not now bring him first from Annas, but at the crowing of the cock they led him to the house of Caiphas, and afterwards in the morning from his house to him who had gone to the place of the praetorium.

59. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked about this that he says: *That they might not be defiled, but might eat the pasch.* — Therefore they had not yet eaten the pasch: therefore, since Christ ate it on the preceding day, he did not eat at the due time.

To this the Greeks respond that it is true that Christ anticipated the pasch on account of his death[^p487-9]; hence they say that he ate *leavened bread* and confected from it, and therefore they themselves confect; and from this word they take the cause of their error.

But we expound that *pasch* is the same as the paschal food, and this is the unleavened bread, which they ate for seven days[^p487-10].

<!-- page 488 -->

60. Quaest. IV. Likewise it is asked about this that he says: *It is not lawful for us to put any man to death.* — For it is said in Exodus, the twenty-second chapter[^p488-1]: *Malefactors thou shalt not suffer to live;* and the Law many times concedes it.

Augustine responds[^p488-2]: « It is to be understood that they said this on account of the sanctity of the feast day, which they had already begun to celebrate ». — Or it must be said that they say this on account of the empire of the Romans, who had taken from them the power of killing, from whom they also bought the priesthood[^p488-3].
