# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. VII, Vers. 14-18
## *Postquam actum est de occultatione, agitur de Doctoris nostri propalatione, qui supra latitaverat*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 342 -->

19. *Iam*[^p342-10] *die festo mediante.* Postquam actum est de occultatione, agitur de *Doctoris nostri propalatione,* qui supra latitaverat. Et proceditur hoc ordine: primo ponitur Doctoris *propalatio in docendo;* secundo, Iudaeorum *admiratio in audiendo;* tertio, doctrinae *commendatio in respondendo.*

(Vers. 14.). Ponitur ergo primo *Doctoris propalatio;* propter quod dicit: *Iam autem die festo mediante,* et haec fuit « quarta dies festi », sicut dicit Glossa[^p342-11], quae est media septem dierum; *ascendit Iesus in templum et docebat,* in loco publico, non occulto, ne suspecta esset doctrina; infra decimo octavo: « Ego semper docui in Synagoga et in templo, quo omnes Iudaei conveniunt, et in occulto locutus sum nihil ».

20. (Vers. 15.). *Et mirabantur Iudaei* etc. Hic tangitur secundum, scilicet *Iudaeorum admiratio.* Ratio admirationis erat, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p342-12], quod « nunquam viderant eum litteras discentem, audiebant autem de Lege disputantem ». Propter quod dicit: *Et mirabantur Iudaei dicentes: Quomodo hic litteras scit, cum non didicerit?* Unde poterat Dominus dicere illud Proverbiorum trigesimo[^p342-13]: « Sapientiam non didici, et novi scientiam Sanctorum »; Lucae secundo: « Mirabantur super prudentia et responsis eius ».

21. (Vers. 16.). *Respondit eis Iesus.* Hic ponitur tertium, scilicet *Christi responsio,* qua admirationi eorum satisfacit, ostendens, se non habere scientiam per acquisitionem, sicut habent alii homines, sed a sua origine. Propter quod dicit: *Mea doctrina,* scilicet quam doceo, *non est mea,* id est a me, vel ad meam gloriam, vel a me acquisita; *sed eius qui*
<!-- page 343 -->
*misit me,* id est a Patre, qui misit me. Omnia enim, quae habet Filius, a Patre accipit; supra quinto[^p343-1]: « Non possum a me ipso facere quidquam », ita nec docere quidquam. Et quia haec responsio manifesta non erat nisi his qui recte audiunt; et hi sunt, qui audiunt, ut faciant: propterea dicit:

22. (Vers. 17.). *Si quis voluerit voluntatem eius,* scilicet Dei Patris, *facere, cognoscet de doctrina, utrum ex Deo sit, an ego a me ipso loquar;* quia aliter nec me nec doctrinam meam potest cognoscere vere. Ideo dicitur primae Ioannis secundo[^p343-2]: « Qui dicit, se Deum nosse, et mandata eius non custodit, mendax est ». Et sic *commendabilis ab origine,* et est etiam *commendabilis a fine* in hoc, quod in docendo non quaerit gloriam propriam; et hoc manifestat sic:

23. (Vers. 18.). *Qui loquitur a semetipso propriam gloriam quaerit;* sed qui quaerit propriam gloriam, mendax est, quia alienum sibi usurpat; Isaiae quadragesimo octavo[^p343-3]: « Gloriam meam alteri non dabo »; sed ego in docendo propriam gloriam non quaero, sed quaero gloriam eius qui misit me: ideo doctrina mea verax est. Propterea dicit: *Qui autem quaerit gloriam eius qui misit illum, hic verax est, et iniustitia in eo*[^p343-4] *non est.* Christus gloriam Divinitatis quaesivit, non suae humanitatis, quia ei omnis gloria debetur; primae ad Timotheum primo: « Regi saeculorum immortali, invisibili, soli Deo honor et gloria » etc. *Est iustus et verax,* quia e contrario usurpare sibi alienum honorem est falsitas et iniustitia. Sic usurpabit antichristus, de quo dicitur secundae ad Thessalonicenses secundo[^p343-5]: « Exaltabitur et extolletur super omne, quod dicitur Deus[^p343-6] ».

### Quaestiones.

24. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic: cum Dominus occulte Ierusalem ascenderit propter vitandum furorem Iudaicum; unde est, quod palam docet in templo?

Respondet Augustinus[^p343-7], « quod latebat, erat causa exempli; quod palam loquitur et non tenetur, est indicium potestatis ».

Aliter potest dici, quod ipsa sua doctrina furorem declinabat Iudaicum, dum eos elevat in admirationem[^p343-8].
<!-- page 344 -->

25. Quaest. II. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod litteras non didicit et litteras sciebat:

1. Quomodo ipsi sciebant, quod Dominus litteras sciret?

2. Item, cum Dominus exemplum deberet nobis dare non solum humilitatis in opere, verum etiam in cognitione; videtur, quod sicut propter nostrum exemplum fuit baptizatus et circumcisus, quod debuerit esse discipulus prius quam doctor. — *Si dicas,* quod nihil potuit addiscere, cum omnia sciret; *obiicitur* quod dicitur ad Hebraeos quinto[^p344-1]: « Didicit ex iis quae passus est, obedientiam ». Si didicit obedientiam, quare non scientiam?

Respondet Glossa[^p344-2], quod quae dicebat auctoritate Scripturae dicebat; ideo sciebant, ipsum scire litteras. Sciebant etiam, ipsum non didicisse, quia eius educationem, ut dicit Augustinus[^p344-3], et locum et parentes cognoverant.

2. Quod obiicit, quod debuit discere; dico primo, quod non eguit nec egere debuit, quia omnes passiones nostras assumere debuit praeter ignorantiam et peccatum; quia nobis omnino et saluti nostrae essent inutiles. Nec in hoc *exemplum* dare debuit, quia in hoc, quod disceret, se ostenderet ignorantem, et tunc opus esset simulatum[^p344-4]. — Quod ergo dicit de *circumcisione,* dicendum, quod erat ad distinguendum; ne igitur alterius ritus et gentis videretur, ideo observavit circumcisionem et baptismum Ioannis, ut aquas sanctificaret et innotesceret[^p344-5]. — Quod obiicit, quod *didicit;* breviter dicendum, quod *discere* uno modo est in ignoti notitiam per doctrinam devenire, alio modo *discere* est cognitum experiri. Primo modo non didicit, quia ponit ignorantiam; secundo modo didicit, quia ponit passiones, quas Christus assumsit et expertus est[^p344-6].

26. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Mea doctrina non est mea:*

1. Quia ita removetur a se, et ideo falsa[^p344-7] videtur.

2. Item, quomodo per hoc satisfit Iudaeis? Quia similiter possem ego dicere istud, quod mea doctrina non est a me, sed a Deo, quia *omnis sapientia a Domino Deo est*[^p344-8].

Respondet Chrysostomus[^p344-9], quod doctrinam suam dicit non esse a se inventam, sed a Patre esse; unde sensus est: mea doctrina, scilicet quam doceo, non est mea, sed a Patre est. — Et in hoc satisfacit quaestioni eorum, mittendo eos ad Patrem, et sedat eorum furorem per humilitatem; unde *sua* erat doctrina, quia est ipsa sapientia per essentiam; sed *non suam esse* dicebat, quia ab alio accepit.

Sed quare non simpliciter dixit: Ego sum ipsa sapientia, sed humiliavit se?

Quinque causas assignat Chrysostomus[^p344-10]: prima, ut ostendat, se esse ab alio et Filium, non ingenitum. — Secunda, ut ostendat, se non esse Deo contrarium, sicut erit antichristus, qui *extolletur super omne, quod dicitur Deus*[^p344-11]. — Tertia, ut ostendat, se esse hominem verum. — Quarta, propter audientium imbecillitatem. — Quinta, ad docendum humilitatem, iuxta illud ad Romanos undecimo[^p344-12]: *Noli altum sapere.*

Aliter respondetur, quod contradictio ista solvitur secundum regulam Augustini[^p344-13], aspiciendo ad duplicem naturam, ut sit sensus: mea doctrina secundum Divinitatem non est mea secundum humanitatem. Illud dicitur *nostrum,* quod a nobis est acquisitum, et sic satisfacit quaestioni.

Tertio modo fit vis in hoc quod est *meum; meum* enim dicit libertatem possidendi et principium efficiendi[^p344-14], ut *ager meus, liber meus,* quem feci. Sed hoc potest dupliciter dici: vel *simpliciter,* vel *discretive*[^p344-15], sicut et *mei* et *mihi. Simpliciter* ergo loquendo, doctrina illa erat Christi, qui eam libere possidebat et ab ipso ad audientes emanabat. Sed non erat *discretive* Christi solius, quia et haec eadem erat *Patris,* et etiam haec eadem erat *a Patre.* — Vel aliter respondeo: est triplex *meum: meum* secundum *naturam* vel originem, *meum* secundum divinam naturam vel humanam, *meum simpliciter,* vel *discretive.*

## English
<!-- page 342 -->

19. *Now*[^p342-10] *about the midst of the festival day.* After the concealment has been treated, there is treated *the disclosure of our Doctor,* who had lain hidden above. And it proceeds in this order: first is set down the Doctor's *disclosure in teaching;* second, the Jews' *wonder in hearing;* third, the *commendation* of the teaching[^tr-doctrina] *in answering.*

(Verse 14.). First, then, is set down *the Doctor's disclosure;* on account of which he says: *Now about the midst of the festival day,* and this was « the fourth day of the feast », as the Gloss says[^p342-11], which is the middle of the seven days; *Jesus went up into the temple and taught,* in a public place, not a hidden one, lest his teaching should be suspect; below, in the eighteenth: « I have always taught in the synagogue and in the temple, whither all the Jews resort, and in secret I have spoken nothing ».

20. (Verse 15.). *And the Jews wondered* etc. Here the second is touched upon, namely, *the wonder of the Jews.* The reason for the wonder was, as Augustine says[^p342-12], that « they had never seen him learning letters, but they heard him disputing about the Law ». On account of which he says: *And the Jews wondered, saying: How doth this man know letters, since he has not learned?* Whence the Lord could say that of Proverbs, the thirtieth[^p342-13]: « I have not learned wisdom, and I have known the science of the saints »; Luke, the second: « They wondered at his prudence and his answers ».

21. (Verse 16.). *Jesus answered them.* Here the third is set down, namely, *the answer of Christ,* by which he satisfies their wonder, showing that he does not have knowledge by acquisition, as other men have it, but from his origin. On account of which he says: *My teaching,* namely, that which I teach, *is not mine,* that is, from me, or for my glory, or acquired by me; *but his that*
<!-- page 343 -->
*sent me,* that is, from the Father, who sent me. For all things that the Son has, he receives from the Father; above, in the fifth[^p343-1]: « I cannot of myself do any thing », so neither teach any thing. And because this answer was not manifest except to those who hear rightly; and these are they who hear in order to do: therefore he says:

22. (Verse 17.). *If any man will do his will,* namely, of God the Father, *he shall know of the teaching, whether it be of God, or whether I speak of myself;* because otherwise he cannot truly know either me or my teaching. Therefore it is said in the first of John, the second[^p343-2]: « He who saith that he knoweth God, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar ». And so it is *commendable from its origin,* and it is also *commendable from its end* in this, that in teaching he does not seek his own glory; and he manifests this thus:

23. (Verse 18.). *He that speaketh of himself seeketh his own glory;* but he who seeks his own glory is a liar, because he usurps for himself what belongs to another; Isaias, the forty-eighth[^p343-3]: « I will not give my glory to another »; but I in teaching do not seek my own glory, but I seek the glory of him who sent me: therefore my teaching is true. On account of this he says: *But he that seeketh the glory of him that sent him, he is true, and there is no injustice in him*[^p343-4]. Christ sought the glory of the Divinity, not of his humanity, because to it all glory is due; the first to Timothy, the first: « To the King of ages, immortal, invisible, the only God, be honour and glory » etc. *He is just and true,* because, on the contrary, to usurp for oneself another's honour is falsity and injustice. So will antichrist usurp it, of whom it is said in the second to the Thessalonians, the second[^p343-5]: « He shall be exalted and lifted up above all that is called God[^p343-6] ».

### Questions.

24. Question I. But it is asked here: since the Lord went up to Jerusalem secretly in order to avoid the fury of the Jews, how is it that he teaches openly in the temple?

Augustine answers[^p343-7], « that he lay hidden was for an example; that he speaks openly and is not held is a sign of power ».

It can be said otherwise, that his very teaching turned aside the fury of the Jews, while it lifts them up into wonder[^p343-8].
<!-- page 344 -->

25. Question II. It is asked about what he says, that he had not learned letters and he knew letters:

1. How did they themselves know that the Lord knew letters?

2. Likewise, since the Lord ought to give us an example of humility not only in work but also in cognition, it seems that, just as for our example he was baptized and circumcised, he ought to have been a disciple before he was a doctor. — *If you say* that he could learn nothing, since he knew all things, *it is objected* what is said to the Hebrews, the fifth[^p344-1]: « He learned obedience by the things which he suffered ». If he learned obedience, why not knowledge?

The Gloss answers[^p344-2] that what he said he said by the authority of Scripture; therefore they knew that he knew letters. They knew also that he had not learned, because they had known his upbringing, as Augustine says[^p344-3], and his place and his parents.

2. As to the objection that he ought to have learned: I say first, that he did not need to, nor ought he to have needed to, because he ought to have assumed all our passions except ignorance and sin; because these would have been altogether useless to us and to our salvation. Nor ought he to give an *example* in this, because in this, that he should learn, he would show himself ignorant, and then the work would be feigned[^p344-4]. — As to what he says, then, about *circumcision,* it must be said that it was for distinguishing; lest, then, he should seem to be of another rite and nation, he therefore observed circumcision and the baptism of John, that he might sanctify the waters and make himself known[^p344-5]. — As to the objection that *he learned,* it must be said briefly that *to learn* in one way is to come into the knowledge of the unknown through teaching; in another way *to learn* is to experience what is known. In the first way he did not learn, because it implies ignorance; in the second way he learned, because it implies passions, which Christ assumed and experienced[^p344-6].

26. Question III. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *My teaching is not mine:*

1. Because thus it is removed from himself, and therefore it seems false[^p344-7].

2. Likewise, how by this are the Jews satisfied? For I could likewise say this, that my teaching is not from me but from God, because *all wisdom is from the Lord God*[^p344-8].

Chrysostom answers[^p344-9] that he says that his teaching was not invented by himself, but is from the Father; whence the sense is: my teaching, namely, that which I teach, is not mine, but is from the Father. — And in this he satisfies their question, by sending them to the Father, and he calms their fury by humility; whence the teaching was *his,* because he is wisdom itself by essence; but he said it was *not his,* because he received it from another.

But why did he not say simply: I am wisdom itself, but humbled himself?

Chrysostom assigns five causes[^p344-10]: the first, that he might show that he is from another, and the Son, not unbegotten. — The second, that he might show that he is not contrary to God, as antichrist will be, who *shall be lifted up above all that is called God*[^p344-11]. — The third, that he might show that he is a true man. — The fourth, on account of the weakness of the hearers. — The fifth, to teach humility, according to that to the Romans, the eleventh[^p344-12]: *Be not highminded.*

It is answered otherwise, that this contradiction is resolved according to Augustine's rule[^p344-13], by looking to the twofold nature, so that the sense is: my teaching according to the Divinity is not mine according to the humanity. That is called *ours* which is acquired by us, and so he satisfies the question.

In a third way the force is laid on this, that it is *mine; mine* in fact expresses freedom of possessing and principle of effecting[^p344-14], as *my field, my book,* which I made. But this can be said in two ways: either *simply,* or *discretively*[^p344-15], as also *of me* and *to me. Simply,* then, speaking, that teaching was Christ's, who possessed it freely and from whom it flowed forth to the hearers. But it was not *discretively* Christ's alone, because this same teaching was also *the Father's,* and also this same teaching was *from the Father.* — Or I answer otherwise: *mine* is threefold: *mine* according to *nature* or origin, *mine* according to the divine nature or the human, *mine simply,* or *discretively.*
