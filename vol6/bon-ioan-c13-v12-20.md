# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XIII, Vers. 12-20
## *Postquam posuit exemplum, hortatur ad imitandum ipsum, et hoc quidem facit per hunc modum: primo ex consideratione dignitatis Magistri; secundo, magnitudinis praemii; tertio, excellentiae meriti*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 427 -->

16. *Postquam ergo lavit pedes* etc. Postquam posuit exemplum, *hortatur ad imitandum ipsum,* et hoc quidem facit per hunc modum: primo ex consideratione *dignitatis Magistri;* secundo, *magnitudinis praemii;* tertio, *excellentiae meriti.*

(Vers. 12.). Hortatur ergo ex consideratione *dignitatis Magistri:* quia ipse Magister et Dominus fecit, ideo debent et ipsi facere; et ideo, cum surrexerit ut minister, iam incipit docere ut magister. Ideo et vestimenta resumit, audientiam petit; propter quod dicit: *Postquam ergo lavit pedes eorum, accepit*[^p427-8] *vestimenta sua,* quae scilicet deposuerat, ut serviret; *et cum recubuisset, iterum dicit eis: Scitis, quid fecerim vobis?* Facit eos sollicitos ad attendendum, quia debet docere ut Magister; et est doctrina sua, ut ipsi imitentur; propterea dicit:

17. (Vers. 13.). *Vos vocatis me Magister et Domine, et bene dicitis; sum etenim.* Est enim *Magister;* Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p427-9]: « Unus est Magister vester, Christus ». Est etiam *Dominus,* unde David vocabat eum *Dominum suum* in Psalmo: « Dixit Dominus Domino meo: Sede a dextris meis ».

18. (Vers. 14.). *Si ergo ego lavi pedes vestros, Dominus et Magister; et vos debetis alter alterius lavare pedes;* ad Philippenses secundo[^p427-10]: « In humilitate superiores sibi invicem arbitrantes »; et post: « Hoc sentite in vobis, quod et in Christo Iesu » etc. Et ratio huius subditur: quia hoc fecit ad se imitandum. Fecit enim miracula, quae non possumus imitari, quia non fecit in exemplum, sed in potentiae indicium; fecit opera humilia ad imitandum, ut hic; unde:

19. (Vers. 15.). *Exemplum*[^p427-11] *dedi vobis, ut quemadmodum ego feci vobis, ita et vos faciatis;*
<!-- page 428 -->
Matthaei undecimo: « Discite a me, quia mitis sum et humilis corde ». Et quod in operibus humilitatis sit imitandus, ostendit tali ratione: quia non est servus maior domino suo: ergo si dominus non dedignatur humiliari, multo minus servus. Et hoc est quod dicit:

20. (Vers. 16.). *Amen, amen dico vobis,* tanquam verum et certum: *Non est servus maior domino suo, neque apostolus maior est eo qui misit illum;* Matthaei decimo[^p428-1]: « Non est discipulus super magistrum », et infra decimo quinto: « Nec servus maior domino suo ». Et ideo omnes servi Christi debent conservis servire; ideo consuetudo est Papae, ut scribat se *servum servorum Dei.* Unde Chrysostomus[^p428-2]: « Ubi sunt qui despiciunt conservos? Ubi sunt qui honores expetunt? Proditoris pedes lavit Dominus et sacrilegi et furis tempore proditionis; et tu magna sapis et in sublime erigeris »?

21. (Vers. 17.). *Si haec scitis* etc. Hic secundo hortatur ad imitandum consideratione *magnitudinis praemii:* quia qui hoc facit beatus erit. Propter quod dicit: *Si haec scitis, beati eritis, si feceritis ea,* non si tantum scitis, immo etiam si facitis; Iacobi primo[^p428-3]: « Qui factor est operis, hic beatus in facto suo erit »; Matthaei quinto: « Qui solverit unum de mandatis istis minimis et docuerit sic homines, minimus vocabitur in regno caelorum ». Et post: « Qui autem fecerit et docuerit, hic magnus vocabitur in regno caelorum ». Et quia promiserat eis beatitudinem; ne credas, eam proditori promisisse, subdit:

22. (Vers. 18.). *Non de omnibus*[^p428-4] *dico,* scilicet quod beati eritis, sed de electis; unde ait: *Ego scio quos elegerim;* Matthaei vigesimo: « Multi sunt vocati, pauci vero electi »; secundae ad Timotheum secundo: « Novit Dominus qui sunt eius »; unde quod dixi: « Beati eritis », de aliquibus vestrum et pluribus est intelligendum. *Sed ut impleatur*[^p428-5] *Scriptura: Qui manducat mecum panem,* id est socius commensalis, *levabit calcaneum suum contra me,* in Psalmo; secundum aliam translationem: « Homo pacis meae, in quo speravi, qui edebat panes meos, magnificavit super me supplantationem ». *Levare autem calcaneum,* ut dicit Chrysostomus[^p428-6], est levare calcitrationem, dolum et fraudem et occultationem insidiarum. Et loquitur per similitudinem: sicut homo, quando vult conculcare rem, levat calcaneum super eam; sic Iudas conculcavit Christum. Et hoc dicebat de Iuda, non eum accusando, sed discipulos instruendo, ne crederent eum traditum ut hominem purum. Propterea subdit:

23. (Vers. 19.). *Amodo*[^p428-7] *dico vobis, priusquam fiat; ut, cum factum fuerit, credatis, quia ego sum,* scilicet Deus, qui scio omnia, antequam fiant; Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio: « Domino Deo, antequam crearentur, omnia sunt agnita » etc.

24. (Vers. 20.). *Amen, amen dico vobis.* Hic tertio hortatur ad imitandum se in honore servorum suorum consideratione *magnitudinis meriti.* Magnum enim meritum habet qui Dominum honorat, et ipse Dominus honorem factum servo reputat suum; et ita magnum meritum habet qui honorat Christi servum. Propterea dicit: *Qui accipit, si quem misero, me accipit,* id est, qui honorat missum honorat me mittentem; Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p428-8]: « Quamdiu uni ex minimis meis fecistis, mihi fecistis ». *Et qui accipit me accipit eum qui misit me,* id est, qui honorat Filium honorat Patrem, qui misit eum; Matthaei decimo dicitur simile: « Qui vos recipit me recipit, et qui me recipit recipit eum qui me misit ». Et ex hoc arguitur, quod sicut est magna *transgressio* Deum despicere, et magnum *meritum* ipsum honorare; sic etiam in servis Dei.

### Quaestiones.

25. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo: quare Dominus dedit discipulis suis magis exemplum hu-
<!-- page 429 -->
militatis quam alicuius alterius virtutis, cum sint aliae virtutes excellentes.

Dicendum, quod Dominus super omnia commendavit discipulis suis unitatem; et quia superbia recte frangit unitatem, quia *inter superbos semper sunt iurgia*[^p429-1], et discipuli contendebant, *quis eorum videretur esse maior;* ideo, ne per illam contentionem unitas Ecclesiae rumperetur, dedit exemplum humilitatis, ut quilibet alteri subiiciatur. — Alia ratio est, quia, sicut damnatio ortum habuit a superbia, ita salus ab humilitate[^p429-2].

26. Quaest. II. Sed quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Non de omnibus dico; ego scio, quos elegerim.*

**Contra:** Supra sexto[^p429-3] dicitur: *Nonne duodecim vos elegi?* Si elegit duodecim, Iudas erat electus, cuius contrarium dicit modo.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod est eligi ad *praesentem dignitatem;* et sic Iudas fuit electus, sicut eliguntur mali episcopi et praelati, non errore, sed mirabili dispensatione. Est electio *ad aeternam solemnitatem;* et hoc modo Iudas non fuit electus, quia haec pertinet ad finaliter bonos[^p429-4].

27. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicitur: *Qui recipit, si quem misero, me recipit.*

1. Secundum hoc qui honorat nuntium honorat mittentem: ergo sicut « honor *imaginis* refertur ad prototypum[^p429-5] », sic *nuntii* ad Dominum: ergo si imaginem Christi debemus adorare *latria,* sic et nuntios Christi.

2. Hoc ipsum videtur per litteram[^p429-6], quia apponit pro simili, quod *qui honorat ipsum honorat Patrem, qui misit eum;* sed eodem honore honorandus est uterque.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod honorem referri[^p429-7] ad aliquem est dupliciter: aut sicut ad *obiectum,* aut sicut ad *finem.* Dico ergo, quod honor *imaginis* refertur ad prototypum sicut ad *obiectum,* quia non honoratur lapis, sed Deus. Sed honor *missi* refertur ad *mittentem* sicut ad *motivum et finem;* et quia homo non honorat *missum* nisi propter *mittentem* et ex consequenti, non primo sicut ad obiectum; quia ex hoc Deus reputat se honoratum, vel dehonoratum: ideo imago est adoranda *latria,* sed nuntius *dulia*[^p429-8].

2. Quod obiicitur de simili, dicendum, quod non est omnino simile; quia ibi est conformitas *naturae,* hic autem *gratiae.*

## English
<!-- page 427 -->

16. *After therefore he had washed their feet* etc. After he has set down the example, *he exhorts to imitating him,* and he does this in this way: first from the consideration of *the dignity of the Teacher;* second, of *the greatness of the reward;* third, of *the excellence of the merit.*

(V. 12.). He exhorts, then, from the consideration of *the dignity of the Teacher:* because he who is himself Teacher and Lord did it, therefore they themselves also ought to do it; and therefore, since he has risen up as a minister, he now begins to teach as a teacher. Therefore he also takes up his garments again and asks for a hearing; on which account he says: *After therefore he had washed their feet, he took*[^p427-8] *his garments,* those, namely, which he had laid aside in order to serve; *and being set down again, he says to them: Know you what I have done to you?* He makes them solicitous to attend, because he is to teach as Teacher; and his teaching[^tr-doctrina] is that they themselves should imitate; therefore he says:

17. (V. 13.). *You call me Teacher and Lord, and you say well, for so I am.* For he is *Teacher;* Matthew, the twenty-third[^p427-9]: « One is your Teacher, Christ ». He is also *Lord,* whence David called him *his Lord* in the Psalm: « The Lord said to my Lord: Sit thou at my right hand ».

18. (V. 14.). *If then I being Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet; you also ought to wash one another's feet;* to the Philippians, the second chapter[^p427-10]: « In humility, let each esteem others better than themselves »; and after: « Let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus » etc. And the reason of this is added: because he did this that he might be imitated. For he did miracles, which we cannot imitate, because he did not do them as an example, but as a mark of power; he did humble works to be imitated, as here; whence:

19. (V. 15.). *I have given you an example*[^p427-11]*, that as I have done to you, so you do also;*
<!-- page 428 -->
Matthew, the eleventh: « Learn of me, because I am meek, and humble of heart ». And that he is to be imitated in works of humility, he shows by such a reason: because the servant is not greater than his lord: therefore if the lord does not disdain to be humbled, much less the servant. And this is what he says:

20. (V. 16.). *Amen, amen I say to you,* as something true and certain: *The servant is not greater than his lord; neither is the apostle greater than he that sent him;* Matthew, the tenth[^p428-1]: « The disciple is not above the teacher », and below, in the fifteenth: « Nor the servant greater than his lord ». And therefore all the servants of Christ ought to serve their fellow servants; therefore it is the custom of the Pope to write himself *servant of the servants of God.* Whence Chrysostom[^p428-2]: « Where are they who despise their fellow servants? Where are they who seek after honours? The Lord washed the feet of the traitor, both sacrilegious and a thief, at the time of the betrayal; and you mind great things and are raised on high »?

21. (V. 17.). *If you know these things* etc. Here second he exhorts to imitation by the consideration of *the greatness of the reward:* because he who does this shall be blessed. On which account he says: *If you know these things, you shall be blessed if you do them,* not if you only know, but rather if you also do; James, the first[^p428-3]: « He who is a doer of the work, this man shall be blessed in his deed »; Matthew, the fifth: « He that shall break one of these least commandments, and shall so teach men, shall be called the least in the kingdom of heaven ». And after: « But he that shall do and teach, he shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven ». And because he had promised them beatitude; lest you believe that he promised it to the traitor, he adds:

22. (V. 18.). *I speak not of all*[^p428-4]*,* namely that you shall be blessed, but of the chosen; whence he says: *I know whom I have chosen;* Matthew, the twentieth: « Many are called, but few chosen »; the second to Timothy, the second chapter: « The Lord knoweth who are his »; hence what I said: « You shall be blessed », is to be understood of some of you and of the greater number. *But that the scripture may be fulfilled*[^p428-5]*: He that eateth bread with me,* that is, a companion at table, *shall lift up his heel against me,* in the Psalm; according to another translation: « The man of my peace, in whom I trusted, who ate my bread, hath greatly supplanted me ». *But to lift up the heel,* as Chrysostom[^p428-6] says, is to lift up a kicking, guile and fraud and the concealment of snares. And he speaks by a similitude: as a man, when he wishes to trample a thing, lifts his heel over it; so Judas trampled Christ. And this he said of Judas, not accusing him, but instructing the disciples, lest they should believe that he was betrayed as a mere man. Therefore he adds:

23. (V. 19.). *At present*[^p428-7] *I tell you, before it come to pass: that when it shall come to pass, you may believe that I am,* namely God, who know all things before they come to pass; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third: « To the Lord God, before they were created, all things were known » etc.

24. (V. 20.). *Amen, amen I say to you.* Here third he exhorts to imitating him in the honour of his servants by the consideration of *the greatness of the merit.* For he who honours the Lord has great merit, and the Lord himself counts as his own the honour done to a servant; and so he who honours a servant of Christ has great merit. Therefore he says: *He that receiveth whomsoever I send, receiveth me,* that is, he who honours the one sent honours me the sender; Matthew, the twenty-fifth[^p428-8]: « As long as you did it to one of these my least, you did it to me ». *And he that receiveth me, receiveth him that sent me,* that is, he who honours the Son honours the Father, who sent him; in Matthew, the tenth, a like thing is said: « He that receiveth you, receiveth me: and he that receiveth me, receiveth him that sent me ». And from this it is argued that, as it is a great *transgression* to despise God, and a great *merit* to honour him; so also in the servants of God.

### Questions.

25. Quaest. I. But it is asked here first: why the Lord gave his disciples an example of humility rather
<!-- page 429 -->
than of some other virtue, since there are other excellent virtues.

It must be said that the Lord above all things commended unity to his disciples; and because pride rightly breaks unity, because *among the proud there are always contentions*[^p429-1], and the disciples were contending *which of them should seem to be the greater;* therefore, lest through that contention the unity of the Church should be broken, he gave an example of humility, that each might be subject to another. — Another reason is that, as damnation had its origin from pride, so salvation from humility[^p429-2].

26. Quaest. II. But it is asked concerning what he says: *I speak not of all; I know whom I have chosen.*

**On the contrary:** Above, in the sixth[^p429-3], it is said: *Have not I chosen you twelve?* If he chose twelve, Judas was chosen, the contrary of which he says now.

**I respond:** It must be said that there is being chosen for *present dignity;* and in this way Judas was chosen, as bad bishops and prelates are chosen, not by error, but by a wonderful dispensation. There is election *to eternal solemnity;* and in this way Judas was not chosen, because this pertains to those who are finally good[^p429-4].

27. Quaest. III. Again, it is asked concerning what is said: *He that receiveth whomsoever I send, receiveth me.*

1. According to this, he who honours a messenger honours the sender: therefore, as « the honour of an *image* is referred to the prototype[^p429-5] », so that of a *messenger* to the Lord: therefore if we ought to adore the image of Christ with *latria,* so also the messengers of Christ.

2. The same seems from the letter[^p429-6], because he adds as a like case that *he who honours him honours the Father, who sent him;* but both are to be honoured with the same honour.

**I respond:** It must be said that for honour to be referred[^p429-7] to someone is in two ways: either as to an *object,* or as to an *end.* I say therefore that the honour of an *image* is referred to the prototype as to an *object,* because it is not the stone that is honoured, but God. But the honour of the *one sent* is referred to the *sender* as to the *motive and end;* and because a man does not honour the *one sent* except on account of the *sender* and consequently, not primarily as to an object; because from this God counts himself honoured or dishonoured: therefore an image is to be adored with *latria,* but a messenger with *dulia*[^p429-8].

2. As for what is objected about the like case, it must be said that it is not altogether alike; because there there is a conformity of *nature,* but here of *grace.*
