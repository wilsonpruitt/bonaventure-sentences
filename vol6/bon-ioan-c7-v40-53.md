# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. VII, Vers. 40-53
## *Determinavit persequentium per doctrinam Christi confutationem, hic determinat persequentium dispersionem; et hoc quidem facit hoc ordine*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 350 -->

58. *Ex illa autem*[^p350-12] *turba.* Determinavit persequentium per doctrinam Christi confutationem, hic determinat *persequentium dispersionem;* et hoc quidem facit hoc ordine. Primo insinuatur *dissensio turbarum audientium;* secundo vero, *reprehensio ministrorum redeuntium;* tertio, *reprehensio Pharisaeorum in malitia persistentium,* quae facta est a Nicodemo.

(Vers. 40.). Insinuatur igitur primo *multitudinis dissensio* propter doctrinam Christi; propter quod dicit: *Ex illa turba, cum audissent hos sermones eius,* propter auditum doctrinae Christi quidam illuminati, *dicebant: Hic est vere Propheta,* quia tam sapienter loquebatur.
<!-- page 351 -->

59. (Vers. 41.). *Alii autem*[^p351-1] *dicebant: Hic est Christus;* et hi erant magis illuminati, unde divini erant; Matthaei decimo sexto: « Quem dicunt homines esse Filium hominis? At illi dixerunt: Alii Ioannem Baptistam, alii autem Eliam, alii vero Ieremiam, aut unum ex Prophetis. Dicit illis Iesus: Vos autem, quem me esse dicitis? Respondens Simon Petrus dixit: Tu es Christus, Filius Dei vivi ». *Quidam autem dicebant,* in contrarium, scilicet excaecati ex lumine Scripturae: *Nunquid a Galilaea venit Christus?* Quasi dicant: non; unde et Nathanael dixit supra primo[^p351-2]: « A Nazareth potest aliquid boni esse »? Et moti sunt per Scripturam:

60. (Vers. 42.). *Nonne Scriptura dicit: Quia ex semine David?* secundi Regum septimo[^p351-3]: « Suscitabo semen tuum post te, quod egredietur de utero tuo, et firmabo regnum eius et stabiliam thronum regni eius usque in sempiternum »; et Psalmus: « De fructu ventris tui ponam super sedem tuam ». *Et de Bethlehem castello, ubi erat David, venit Christus?* Michaeae quinto[^p351-4]: « Et tu Bethlehem Ephrata, parvulus es in millibus Iuda; ex te enim egredietur, qui sit dominator in Israel ». Et sic ad invicem contendebant; unde:

61. (Vers. 43.). *Dissensio itaque facta est in turba propter eum.* Ipse non erat causa, sed eorum malitia; Chrysostomus[^p351-5]: « Discissi sunt, non compuncti; malitia enim nulli vult cedere ». Propter istam dissensionem quidam volebant eum capere tanquam malum, quidam vero dimittere tanquam bonum; et ex ista contentione frustrata est machinatio Pharisaeorum, quia ministri manus non extenderunt ad capiendum eum, licet aliqui vellent. Propterea dicit:

62. (Vers. 44.). *Quidam autem ex ipsis,* scilicet ministris, *volebant apprehendere eum,* secundum imperium scilicet sibi datum; supra eodem[^p351-6]: « Miserunt Pharisaei et pontifices ministros, ut apprehenderent Iesum ». *Sed nemo misit in eum manus*[^p351-7], quia Deus misit; Psalmus: « Emitte manum tuam de alto »; et sic frustrati sunt a sua intentione. Et ratio huius est: quia « cogitationes hominum vanae sunt[^p351-8] », sed « consilium Domini manet in aeternum », quod quidem nondum decreverat apprehendendum Iesum Christum; Psalmus: « Dominus dissipat consilia gentium, reprobat autem cogitationes populorum et reprobat consilia principum ».

63. (Vers. 45.). *Venerunt ergo ministri.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *reprehensio ministrorum redeuntium,* quia nihil eorum fecerant, propter quae missi erant; ideo ab eis Pharisaei causam requirunt, ut eos redarguant. Propterea dicit: *Venerunt ergo ministri ad pontifices et Pharisaeos,* non adduxerunt tamen eum; *et dixerunt eis illi: Quare non adduxistis eum*[^p351-9]*?* quia ad hoc missi fuistis; sic frustrati estis.

64. (Vers. 46.). *Responderunt ministri: Nunquam sic locutus est homo, sicut hic homo loquitur*[^p351-10]; quasi dicant: haec ratio est, quare non adduximus eum, quia magnitudine doctrinae suae perterriti sumus, quoniam *nunquam sic locutus est homo. Nunquam* sic *dulciter;* unde mulier eum audiens dixit: « Beatus venter, qui te portavit, et ubera, quae suxisti », Lucae undecimo[^p351-11]; et Canticorum quarto: « Mel et lac sub lingua eius ». *Nunquam* sic *doctrinaliter* et *sapienter;* unde Lucae secundo[^p351-12]: « Stupebant omnes super prudentia et responsis eius »; et Marci primo: « Stupebant super doctrina eius ». *Nunquam* sic *potenter;* Matthaei septimo: « Erat docens tanquam potestatem habens »; et ideo admirabantur super doctrina eius. *Nunquam* sic *utiliter;* supra sexto: « Verba, quae locutus sum vobis, spiritus et vita sunt »; et eodem: « Domine, verba vitae aeternae habes ».

65. (Vers. 47.). *Responderunt ergo eis Pharisaei.* Audita ministrorum responsione, Pharisaei reprehendunt eos, quod doctrinae eius fidem adhibuerunt; unde dicunt: *Nunquid et vos seducti estis?* Et quod *seducti* erant[^p351-13], nituntur ostendere exemplo maiorum.

66. (Vers. 48.). *Nunquid aliquis ex principibus credidit in eum?* Quasi dicant: nullus; *aut ex Pharisaeis,* qui scilicet Legem sciunt; nulli scientes[^p351-14], sed ignorantes.

67. (Vers. 49.). *Sed turba haec, quae ignorat*[^p351-15] *Legem,* credit scilicet; et quia ignorant quod
<!-- page 352 -->
debent, et credunt quod non debent, *maledicti sunt;* Deuteronomii vigesimo septimo: « Maledictus qui non permanserit in sermonibus Legis huius ». Chrysostomus[^p352-1]: « Nihil veritate manifestius, nihil simplicius; attamen malignantibus nihil difficilius. Ecce enim, sapientes, scribae et Pharisaei, qui signa viderant et Scripturas legerant, veritate laesi sunt et excaecati sunt. Ministri vero, qui nihil viderant, ab una sola locutione capti sunt, et qui ligare venerant abierunt ligati ». Sed ipsos postmodum Pharisaei nitebantur a fide avertere; et propterea dicitur eis Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p352-2]: « Vae vobis, scribae et Pharisaei! qui clauditis regnum caelorum ante homines; vos vero non intratis nec introeuntes sinitis intrare »; Lucae undecimo: « Vae vobis, Legisperiti! quia tulistis clavem scientiae; ipsi non introistis et eos qui introibant, prohibuistis ».

68. (Vers. 50.). *Dixit Nicodemus* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *redargutio Pharisaeorum* facta auctoritate Legis, per quam ostenditur, quod non debuerunt eum capere, quia nondum erat adiudicatus morti per Legem. Ideo *dixit Nicodemus,* scilicet discipulus *ille, qui venit ad Iesum*[^p352-3] *nocte, qui unus erat ex ipsis,* scilicet Pharisaeis; supra tertio: « Erat homo ex Pharisaeis Nicodemus nomine » etc.

69. (Vers. 51.). *Nunquid Lex nostra iudicat hominem, nisi audierit ab eo*[^p352-4] *prius et cognoverit, quid faciat?* Quasi dicat: non; Deuteronomii decimo septimo: « Cum audiens inquisieris diligenter » etc. Hanc legem observabat Iob, vigesimo nono: « Causam, quam nesciebam, diligenter investigabam ». Hanc legem observabant Romani; Actuum vigesimo quinto: « Non est consuetudo Romanis damnare aliquem hominem prius, quam is qui accusatur, praesentes habeat accusatores locumque defendendi accipiat ad abluenda crimina ».

70. (Vers. 52.). *Responderunt et dixerunt*[^p352-5], quia incorrigibiles erant; et quamvis non possent calumniari verbum, calumniantur Nicodemum quasi deceptum, quod ipse in Christum crederet. Propterea dicunt: *Nunquid et tu Galilaeus es?* Id est, in Galilaeum credis — sicut dicuntur *Christiani,* quia credunt in Christum; unde Iulianus apostata[^p352-6] vocabat Christianos Galilaeos pannosos — et stulte in eum credis. Unde dicunt: *Scrutare Scripturas et vide, quoniam*[^p352-7] *a Galilaea propheta non surgit,* ille quem exspectamus. *Contra:* Augustinus: « Non tantum a Galilaea surgit propheta, sed etiam Dominus Prophetarum »; ipse *Nazaraeus* dictus est, quia in Nazareth nutritus.

71. (Vers. 53.). *Et reversi sunt,* quia reprehensionem evitare et etiam conceptum malum consummare non poterant. Propterea dicit: *Et reversi sunt unusquisque in domum suam,* ita quod unus non remansit cum alio; unde Ecclesiastici vigesimo primo[^p352-8]: « Stuppa collecta synagoga peccantium, et consummatio illius flamma ignis »; Psalmus: « Dissipa gentes, quae bella volunt ».

### Quaestiones.

72. Quaest. I. Sed hic quaeritur de ministris: si crediderunt, quare Christum non sunt secuti?

Et dicendum secundum Chrysostomum[^p352-9], quod crediderunt et praecones eius fuerunt; nec tamen secuti sunt, quia ad statum perfectionis non ascenderunt.

73. Quaest. II. Sed quaeritur hic: quae magna et sublimia fuerat Dominus locutus, propter quae dicebant: *Nunquam sic locutus est homo?*

Respondet Chrysostomus[^p352-10]: « Cum mens, inquit, incorrupta fuerit, non est opus longis sermonibus; tale enim quid est veritas, quod quando invenit animam bene dispositam, mirabiliter adhaeret et allicit ».

74. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur hic: cum Pharisaei omnia opera sua facerent in conspectu hominum et nollent mali iudicari[^p352-11]; quomodo sic iuris ordinem relinquebant, cum talis culpa sit manifesta?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod ad iustum iudicium tria concurrunt: *iustus animus,* unde secundi Paralipomenon decimo nono[^p352-12]: *Cum diligentia cuncta facite, non enim est apud Deum iniq[u]itas nec personarum acceptio nec cupido munerum.* — Secundum est *iusta causa;* Exodi vigesimo tertio[^p352-13]: *Innocentem et iustum non interficies;* et vigesimo
<!-- page 353 -->
secundo: *Maleficos non patieris vivere.* — Tertium est *iustus ordo,* qui est per rectam inquisitionem; Deuteronomii decimo sexto[^p353-1]: *Iuste quod iustum est persequeris.*

Quoniam igitur Pharisaeis hypocritis inerat *iniustus animus,* quo mortem Christi sitiebant; et aberat *iusta causa,* quia per ius ipsum condemnare non poterant: ideo necessario cogebantur *ordinem iudicii* praeterire. — Quatuor autem sunt, quae maxime animum pervertunt, quae fuerunt in Pharisaeis, quae his versibus continentur:

Quatuor ista: timor, odium, dilectio, census,  
Saepe solent hominum rectos pervertere sensus.

Et haec omnia fuerunt in Pharisaeis, ut patet inducendo. Contra haec quatuor dicitur Exodi decimo octavo[^p353-2]: *Elige tibi viros sapientes et timentes Deum, in quibus sit veritas, et qui oderint avaritiam.*

75. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur: cum Dominus, quantum est ex se, voluerit omnium fidem; quid est, quod ortum suum occultavit, ut crederetur esse natus in Nazareth, et propter hoc non crederetur Christus?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod istud factum est *divino iudicio, divina dispensatione* et *Scripturae adimpletione. Divino iudicio:* quia tempore Herodis natum cognoverunt et despexerunt, immo in mortem consenserunt; ideo sibi nativitatis locum per conversationem in Nazareth celari meruerunt. — Secundo, ex *divina dispensatione:* quia voluit latere malos, ut patiendo a malis redimeret bonos. — Tertio, *Scripturae adimpletione:* quia Scriptura non tantum dicit, quod in Bethlehem natus, sed quod *Nazaraeus vocabitur,* sicut scribitur Matthaei secundo[^p353-3]. Et quia Iudaei non fuerunt Scripturas scrutati, ideo erraverunt[^p353-4].

## English
<!-- page 350 -->

58. *But of that multitude*[^p350-12]. He determined the refutation of the persecutors through the teaching[^tr-doctrina] of Christ, here he determines *the dispersion of the persecutors;* and this indeed he does in this order. First is intimated *the dissension of the multitudes hearing;* second, *the reproof of the ministers returning;* third, *the reproof of the Pharisees persisting in malice,* which was made by Nicodemus.

(Verse 40.). First, then, is intimated *the dissension of the multitude* on account of the teaching of Christ; on account of which he says: *Of that multitude, when they had heard these words of his,* some, illumined through hearing the teaching of Christ, *said: This is the Prophet indeed,* because he spoke so wisely.
<!-- page 351 -->

59. (Verse 41.). *But others*[^p351-1] *said: This is the Christ;* and these were more illumined, whence they were divine; Matthew, the sixteenth: « Whom do men say that the Son of man is? But they said: Some John the Baptist, and other some Elias, and others Jeremias, or one of the Prophets. Jesus saith to them: But whom do you say that I am? Simon Peter answering said: Thou art Christ, the Son of the living God ». *But some said,* to the contrary, namely those blinded by the lumen[^tr-lumen] of Scripture: *Doth the Christ come out of Galilee?* As if they said: no; whence Nathanael also said above, in the first[^p351-2]: « Can any thing of good be from Nazareth »? And they were moved through Scripture:

60. (Verse 42.). *Doth not the Scripture say: That of the seed of David?* the second of Kings, the seventh[^p351-3]: « I will raise up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels, and I will establish his kingdom, and I will make firm the throne of his kingdom for ever »; and the Psalm: « Of the fruit of thy womb I will set upon thy throne ». *And from Bethlehem the town where David was, the Christ cometh?* Micheas, the fifth[^p351-4]: « And thou, Bethlehem Ephrata, art a little one among the thousands of Juda; for out of thee shall he come forth that is to be the ruler in Israel ». And so they contended with one another; whence:

61. (Verse 43.). *So there arose a dissension among the people because of him.* He himself was not the cause, but their malice; Chrysostom[^p351-5]: « They were divided, not compunct; for malice will yield to no one ». On account of this dissension some wished to take him as an evil man, but some to let him go as a good one; and from this contention the machination of the Pharisees was frustrated, because the ministers did not stretch out their hands to take him, although some wished it. On account of which he says:

62. (Verse 44.). *And some of them,* namely of the ministers, *would have apprehended him,* namely according to the command given them; above, in the same[^p351-6]: « The Pharisees and the chief priests sent ministers to apprehend Jesus ». *But no man put hands upon him*[^p351-7], because God put forth his; the Psalm: « Put forth thy hand from on high »; and so they were frustrated of their intention. And the reason of this is: because « the thoughts of men are vain[^p351-8] », but « the counsel of the Lord abideth for ever », which had not yet decreed that Jesus Christ should be apprehended; the Psalm: « The Lord bringeth to nought the counsels of nations, and he rejecteth the thoughts of peoples, and he rejecteth the counsels of princes ».

63. (Verse 45.). *The ministers therefore came.* Here the second is touched upon, namely *the reproof of the ministers returning,* because they had done none of the things for which they had been sent; therefore the Pharisees ask the cause of them, that they may rebuke them. On account of which he says: *The ministers therefore came to the chief priests and the Pharisees,* yet they did not bring him; *and they said to them: Why have you not brought him*[^p351-9]*?* because you were sent for this; so you have been frustrated.

64. (Verse 46.). *The ministers answered: Never did man speak so, as this man speaketh*[^p351-10]; as if they said: this is the reason why we have not brought him, because we were terrified by the greatness of his teaching, since *never did man speak so. Never* so *sweetly;* whence the woman hearing him said: « Blessed is the womb that bore thee, and the paps that thou didst suck », Luke, the eleventh[^p351-11]; and the Canticle, the fourth: « Honey and milk are under his tongue ». *Never* so *teachingly* and *wisely;* whence Luke, the second[^p351-12]: « All were astonished at his prudence and his answers »; and Mark, the first: « They were astonished at his teaching ». *Never* so *powerfully;* Matthew, the seventh: « He was teaching as one having power »; and therefore they were in admiration at his teaching. *Never* so *profitably;* above, in the sixth: « The words that I have spoken to you, are spirit and life »; and in the same: « Lord, thou hast the words of eternal life ».

65. (Verse 47.). *The Pharisees therefore answered them.* Having heard the response of the ministers, the Pharisees reprove them, because they had given faith to his teaching; whence they say: *Are you also seduced?* And that they had been *seduced*[^p351-13], they strive to show by the example of their elders.

66. (Verse 48.). *Hath any one of the rulers believed in him?* As if they said: none; *or of the Pharisees,* namely who know the Law; none of those knowing[^p351-14], but the ignorant.

67. (Verse 49.). *But this multitude, which is ignorant of*[^p351-15] *the Law,* believes, namely; and because they are ignorant of what they
<!-- page 352 -->
ought to know, and believe what they ought not, *they are accursed;* Deuteronomy, the twenty-seventh: « Cursed is he who abideth not in the words of this Law ». Chrysostom[^p352-1]: « Nothing is more manifest than the truth, nothing simpler; and yet to the malignant nothing is more difficult. For behold, the wise, the scribes and Pharisees, who had seen signs and had read the Scriptures, were hurt by the truth and were blinded. But the ministers, who had seen nothing, were taken by one single speech, and those who had come to bind went away bound ». But afterwards the Pharisees strove to turn them away from the faith; and therefore it is said to them in Matthew, the twenty-third[^p352-2]: « Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees! who shut the kingdom of heaven against men; but you do not enter, nor do you suffer those going in to enter »; Luke, the eleventh: « Woe to you, lawyers! for you have taken away the key of knowledge; you yourselves have not entered in, and those that were entering in, you have hindered ».

68. (Verse 50.). *Nicodemus said* etc. Here the third is touched upon, namely *the rebuke of the Pharisees* made by the authority of the Law, by which it is shown that they ought not to have taken him, because he had not yet been adjudged to death by the Law. Therefore *Nicodemus said,* namely that disciple, *he that came to Jesus*[^p352-3] *by night, who was one of them,* namely of the Pharisees; above, in the third: « There was a man of the Pharisees, named Nicodemus » etc.

69. (Verse 51.). *Doth our Law judge a man, unless it first hear from him*[^p352-4] *and know what he doth?* As if he said: no; Deuteronomy, the seventeenth: « When hearing it thou hast inquired diligently » etc. This law Job observed, the twenty-ninth: « The cause which I knew not, I searched out diligently ». This law the Romans observed; Acts, the twenty-fifth: « It is not the custom of the Romans to condemn any man, before that he who is accused have his accusers present, and have liberty to make his defence, to clear himself of the crimes ».

70. (Verse 52.). *They answered and said*[^p352-5], because they were incorrigible; and although they could not calumniate the word, they calumniate Nicodemus as one deceived, because he himself believed in Christ. On account of which they say: *Art thou also a Galilean?* That is, thou believest in the Galilean — as those are called *Christians* because they believe in Christ; whence Julian the apostate[^p352-6] used to call the Christians ragged Galileans — and thou believest in him foolishly. Whence they say: *Search the Scriptures, and see, that*[^p352-7] *out of Galilee a prophet riseth not,* he whom we await. *On the contrary:* Augustine: « Not only a prophet riseth out of Galilee, but even the Lord of the Prophets »; he himself was called *a Nazarite,* because he was brought up in Nazareth.

71. (Verse 53.). *And they returned,* because they could neither avoid the reproof nor even consummate the evil conceived. On account of which he says: *And they returned, every man to his own house,* so that one did not remain with another; whence Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-first[^p352-8]: « The synagogue of sinners is tow heaped together, and the consummation of it a flame of fire »; the Psalm: « Scatter thou the nations that will wars ».

### Questions.

72. Question I. But here it is asked about the ministers: if they believed, why did they not follow Christ?

And it must be said, according to Chrysostom[^p352-9], that they believed and were his heralds; and yet they did not follow, because they did not ascend to the state of perfection.

73. Question II. But it is asked here: what great and sublime things had the Lord spoken, on account of which they said: *Never did man speak so?*

Chrysostom responds[^p352-10]: « When the mind, he says, is incorrupt, there is no need of long speeches; for such a thing is the truth, that when it finds a soul well disposed, it wonderfully adheres to it and draws it ».

74. Question III. Likewise it is asked here: since the Pharisees did all their works in the sight of men and did not wish to be judged evil[^p352-11], how did they so leave the order of law, since such a fault is manifest?

**I respond:** It must be said that three things concur for a just judgment: *a just mind,* whence the second of Paralipomenon, the nineteenth[^p352-12]: *Do all things with diligence, for there is no iniquity with God, nor respect of persons, nor desire of gifts.* — The second is *a just cause;* Exodus, the twenty-third[^p352-13]: *The innocent and just thou shalt not put to death;* and the twenty-
<!-- page 353 -->
second: *Wizards thou shalt not suffer to live.* — The third is *a just order,* which is through right inquiry; Deuteronomy, the sixteenth[^p353-1]: *Thou shalt follow justly after that which is just.*

Since, then, there was in the hypocrite Pharisees *an unjust mind,* by which they thirsted for the death of Christ; and *a just cause* was lacking, because through the law itself they could not condemn him: therefore they were necessarily forced to pass over *the order of judgment.* — Now there are four things which most pervert the mind, which were in the Pharisees, which are contained in these verses:

These four: fear, hatred, dilection, wealth,  
Are often wont to pervert the right senses of men.

And all these were in the Pharisees, as is plain by going through them. Against these four it is said in Exodus, the eighteenth[^p353-2]: *Choose for thyself wise men, and such as fear God, in whom there is truth, and that hate avarice.*

75. Question IV. Likewise it is asked: since the Lord, as far as is from himself, willed the faith of all, why is it that he hid his birth, so that he was believed to have been born in Nazareth, and on account of this was not believed to be the Christ?

**I respond:** It must be said that this was done *by divine judgment, by divine dispensation* and *for the fulfilment of Scripture. By divine judgment:* because in the time of Herod they knew him born and despised him, nay consented to his death; therefore they deserved that the place of his nativity should be hidden from them through his dwelling in Nazareth. — Second, by *divine dispensation:* because he willed to lie hidden from the wicked, that by suffering from the wicked he might redeem the good. — Third, *for the fulfilment of Scripture:* because Scripture says not only that he was born in Bethlehem, but that *he shall be called a Nazarite,* as is written in Matthew, the second[^p353-3]. And because the Jews had not searched the Scriptures, therefore they erred[^p353-4].

     Est iudicium laudandum

     - cum in iudice est
       - sapientia quantum ad intellectum. Hanc petebat Salomon, ut recte posset iudicare, III. Reg. 3, 9: « Dabis servo tuo cor docile, ut iudicare possit populum tuum »; et ibid. v. 28: « Timuerunt regem, videntes, sapientiam Dei esse in eo ad faciendum iudicium ».
       - puritas quantum ad affectum, II. Paralip. 19, 6. seq.: « Non hominis exercetis iudicium, sed Domini »; et post: « Non est enim apud Dominum Deum nostrum iniquitas nec personarum acceptio nec cupido munerum »; Eccli. 20, 31: « Xenia et dona excaecant oculos iudicum ».
       - honestas quantum ad actum, Rom. 2, 1: « Inexcusabilis es, o omnis homo, qui iudicas; in quo enim alterum iudicas, te ipsum condemnas; eadem enim agis, quae iudicas »; Matth. 7, 5: « Hypocrita, eiice primum trabem de oculo tuo, et tunc videbis eiicere festucam de oculo fratris tui ».
       - auctoritas quantum ad officium, Rom. 14, 4: « Tu quis es, qui iudicas alienum servum »? Luc. 12, 14. dixit Dominus homini petenti, ut iudicaret: « Homo, quis me constituit iudicem aut divisorem super vos »?
       - Sine auctoritate est iudicium *usurpatorium;* sine honestate, *praesumtuosum;* sine puritate, *iniquum;* sine sapientia, *temerarium.* Ideo ista quatuor notantur Exod. 18, 21. in illa auctoritate: « Provide de omni plebe viros », ecce, auctoritas quantum ad officium; « sapientes », ecce, sapientia quantum ad intellectum; « timentes Deum », ecce, puritas quantum ad affectum, quia timor Dei expellit voluntatem peccandi; « in quibus sit veritas » etc., ecce, honestas quantum ad actum.
     - cum in ipso iudicio est
       - inquisitio cum diligentia, Iob 29, 16: « Causam, quam nesciebam, diligentissime investigabam »; II. Paralip. 19, 7: « Sit timor Domini vobiscum, et cum diligentia cuncta facite ».
       - definitio cum iustitia, Levit. 19, 15: « Non consideres personam pauperis nec honores vultum potentis; iuste proximo tuo iudica »; et Exod. 23, 3: « Pauperis quoque non misereberis in iudicio ».
       - exsecutio cum patientia, quia non debet commoveri; Sap. 12, 18: « Tu autem, dominator virtutis, cum tranquillitate iudicas nos et cum magna reverentia disponis nos ».
       - punitio cum moderantia, Eccle. 7, 17: « Noli esse multum iustus, neque plus sapias, quam necesse est »; Deut. 25, 2: « Pro mensura peccati erit et plagarum modus, ita dumtaxat, ut quadragenarium numerum non excedant, ne foede laceratus ante oculos tuos abeat frater tuus ».

     **En.** With the codices against it, the edition presents the following schema.

     Judgment is praiseworthy

     - when there is in the judge
       - wisdom as to the intellect. This Solomon asked for, that he might be able to judge rightly, III Kings 3:9: « Thou wilt give to thy servant a docile heart, that he may judge thy people »; and ibid., v. 28: « They feared the king, seeing that the wisdom of God was in him to do judgment ».
       - purity as to the affection, II Paralip. 19:6 f.: « You exercise not the judgment of man, but of the Lord »; and after: « For there is no iniquity with the Lord our God, nor respect of persons, nor desire of gifts »; Ecclus. 20:31: « Presents and gifts blind the eyes of judges ».
       - honesty as to the act, Rom. 2:1: « Thou art inexcusable, O man, whosoever thou art that judgest; for wherein thou judgest another, thou condemnest thyself; for thou dost the same things which thou judgest »; Matt. 7:5: « Thou hypocrite, cast out first the beam out of thy own eye, and then shalt thou see to cast out the mote out of thy brother's eye ».
       - authority as to the office, Rom. 14:4: « Who art thou that judgest another man's servant »? Luke 12:14, the Lord said to the man who asked him to judge: « Man, who hath appointed me judge or divider over you »?
       - Without authority judgment is *usurping;* without honesty, *presumptuous;* without purity, *iniquitous;* without wisdom, *rash.* Therefore these four are noted in Exod. 18:21, in that authority: « Provide out of all the people men », behold, authority as to the office; « wise », behold, wisdom as to the intellect; « such as fear God », behold, purity as to the affection, because the fear of God expels the will to sin; « in whom there is truth » etc., behold, honesty as to the act.
     - when there is in the judgment itself
       - inquiry with diligence, Job 29:16: « The cause which I knew not, I searched out most diligently »; II Paralip. 19:7: « Let the fear of the Lord be with you, and do all things with diligence ».
       - definition with justice, Lev. 19:15: « Respect not the person of the poor, nor honour the countenance of the mighty; judge thy neighbour justly »; and Exod. 23:3: « Neither shalt thou favour a poor man in judgment ».
       - execution with patience, because it ought not to be stirred; Wis. 12:18: « But thou, master of power, judgest us with tranquillity, and with great reverence disposest of us ».
       - punishing with moderation, Eccle. 7:17: « Be not over just, and be not more wise than is necessary »; Deut. 25:2: « According to the measure of the sin shall the measure also of the stripes be, yet so that they exceed not the number of forty, lest thy brother depart shamefully torn before thy eyes ».

[^tr-doctrina]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin *doctrina* holds together what English divides among doctrine, teaching and learning. In this pericope it is what the Lord says in the temple and what the multitude and the ministers hear — *propter auditum doctrinae Christi*, *magnitudine doctrinae suae perterriti*, *doctrinae eius fidem adhibuerunt* — and the Gospels' *stupebant super doctrina eius* (Mark 1:22; Matt. 7:28) are adduced for the same thing; the adverb *doctrinaliter*, set beside *sapienter*, *potenter* and *utiliter* as one of the ways in which no man ever spoke so, is "teachingly". It is a teaching spoken and heard, not a body of propositions, so "teaching" is the English throughout this chunk, the two Douay "doctrine"s included, as in the pericopes before it.

[^tr-lumen]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin *lumen* is left standing here — *excaecati ex lumine Scripturae*, "blinded by the lumen of Scripture" — against *lux*, which this translation renders "light" and which does not occur in this chunk. *Lux* is light at its source and *lumen* light as given off and received; here it is the light that Scripture gives, by which some read it rightly and from which others were blinded. The participle *illuminati* (¶ 58, ¶ 59) is "illumined", as at John 1:9.
