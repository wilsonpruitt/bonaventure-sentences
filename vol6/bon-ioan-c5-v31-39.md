# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. V, Vers. 31-39
## *Haec est quarta pars capituli, in qua determinatur praedictae confutationis corroboratio, quae facta est multiplici testimonio hoc approbante, quod supra Dominus dixit*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 313 -->

59. *Si ego testimonium perhibeo* etc. Haec est quarta pars capituli, in qua determinatur praedictae *confutationis corroboratio,* quae facta est multiplici testimonio hoc approbante, quod supra Dominus dixit. Et haec pars habet duas: quia primo adducitur *testimoniorum certitudo;* secundo vero, Iudaeorum non credentium *reprehensio,* ibi[^p313-17]: *Et non vultis venire ad me.*

Prima pars habet duas: quia primo Dominus, etsi possit suum testimonium adducere, *renuit;* secundo, testimonia aliorum *adducit,* ibi[^p313-18]: *Vos misistis* etc.
<!-- page 314 -->

(Vers. 31.). Renuit ergo *testimonium suum,* quia ipsi non crederent tanquam incertum; unde dicit: *Si ego testimonium perhibeo de me ipso, testimonium meum non est verum,* id est, non est efficax ad credendum, maxime si ego solus; unde infra octavo[^p314-1]: « Dixerunt ei Pharisaei: Tu de te ipso testimonium perhibes; testimonium tuum non est verum ». Nec tamen desunt mihi testimonia vera; unde subdit:

60. (Vers. 32.). *Alius est, qui testimonium perhibet de me,* scilicet Pater, vel etiam Ioannes, et de utroque recte intelligitur quod sequitur: *Et scio, quia verum est testimonium, quod perhibet de me;* quia tanquam certus testificatur. Pater enim verum dat testimonium, quia, sicut dicitur infra octavo[^p314-2], « qui misit me verax est ». Ioannes similiter certus; unde supra primo dixit Ioannes: « Ego vidi et testimonium perhibui, quia hic est Filius Dei »; Proverbiorum duodecimo: « Qui quod novit loquitur iudex iustitiae est, qui autem mentitur testis est fraudulentus ».

61. *Vos misistis* etc. Renuit Dominus testimonium suum, hic secundo adducit *testimonium alienum.* Et quia in ore plurium testium est testimonium firmum[^p314-3], ideo pars ista habet quatuor partes secundum quatuor testimonia, quae adducit. Primum est *Ioannis;* secundum est *operis;* tertium, *divinum;* et quartum, *Scripturarum.* Et distinguuntur haec testimonia: quia primum est *pronuntiatum,* secundum *exhibitum,* tertium *inspiratum* et quartum *scriptum.*

(Vers. 33.). Primo igitur adducit *testimonium Ioannis,* quod eis fuerat perhibitum ad suam petitionem; ideo dicit: *Vos misistis ad Ioannem;* non ille se ingessit, sed ad vestram petitionem respondit, supra primo[^p314-4]: « Et hoc est testimonium Ioannis, quando miserunt Iudaei ad eum » etc. *Et ille testimonium perhibuit veritati,* tanquam testis fidelis; Proverbiorum decimo quarto: « Testis fidelis non mentietur ». Et ne videretur Dominus humanum testimonium mendicare, subdit:

62. (Vers. 34.). *Ego autem ab homine testimonium non accipio,* supple: propter meam indigentiam; Psalmus[^p314-5]: « Dixi Domino: Deus meus es tu, quoniam bonorum meorum non eges »; sed recolo propter salutem vestram. Unde et dicit: *Sed haec dico, ut vos salvi sitis,* scilicet per testimonium eius credendo in me, quia, sicut dicitur Marci ultimo, « qui crediderit et baptizatus fuerit, salvus erit ». Et vere ad Ioannis testimonium credere debuissent; unde et subdit:

63. (Vers. 35.). *Ille erat lucerna ardens,* per gratiam, *et lucens,* per doctrinam; vel: *ardens,* per internam caritatem, *lucens,* per exteriorem vitae honestatem. Bernardus[^p314-6]: « Ardere tantum parum est, lucere vanum, sed lucere et ardere perfectum ». Ideo optime Ioannes signatus est per Eliam; Ecclesiastici quadragesimo octavo: « Surrexit Elias quasi ignis, et verbum eius quasi facula ardebat ». Tali igitur sic perfecte lucenti fides adhibenda erat, quod Iudaei non fecerunt; unde dicit: *Vos autem voluistis exsultare ad horam in luce eius,* ut scilicet eius doctrinae et testimonio crederetis et vitam imitaremini, quam ad modicum vidistis; Matthaei undecimo[^p314-7]: « Venit Ioannes non manducans neque bibens, et dicunt: Daemonium habet »; Matthaei vigesimo primo: « Venit ad vos Ioannes in via iustitiae, et non credidistis ei. Publicani autem et meretrices crediderunt ei ».

64. (Vers. 36.). *Ego autem habeo testimonium.* Tangitur hic secundum *testimonium,* scilicet *operum,* quod praedicto maius est. Propterea dicit: *Ego autem habeo testimonium maius Ioanne,* quia magis est motivum ad credendum in me. *Ipsa enim opera*[^p314-8], *quae dedit mihi Pater,* quia ab ipso accepi *esse* et *operari; ut perficiam ea,* id est, perfecte faciam; quia « Dei perfecta sunt opera », Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo. *Ipsa opera, quae ego facio,* per virtutem propriam, *testimonium perhibent de me, quia Pater misit me,* sicut Deus Filium suum, quia ei tanta opera communicavit; infra decimo quinto[^p314-9]: « Si non venissem et opera in eis non fecissem, quae nemo alius fecit; peccatum non haberent ». Istis operibus credendum, quod Pater misit eum; infra decimo quarto: « Pater in me manens ipse facit opera »; et iterum: « Si mihi non creditis, operibus credite ».

65. (Vers. 37.). *Et qui misit me Pater* etc. Tangitur hic tertium *testimonium,* scilicet *divinum,* quod et *paternum* dicit; propter quod ait: *Et qui misit me Pater,* per incarnationem, *testimonium perhibuit de me,* per internam inspirationem; quia, sicut dicitur infra sexto[^p314-10], « nemo venit ad me, nisi Pater traxerit eum ». Unde voce intelligibili revelat
<!-- page 315 -->
et testificatur, quam Iudaei carnales nec audire poterant nec ipsum loquentem videre. Ideo dicit: *Neque vocem eius unquam audistis,* per mentis revelationem; sicut Petrus audivit, Matthaei decimo sexto[^p315-1]: « Beatus es, Simon Bar Iona, quia caro et sanguis non revelavit tibi, sed Pater meus, qui est in caelis ». *Neque speciem vidistis,* per apertam visionem; Exodi trigesimo tertio: « Non videbit me homo et vivet »; et supra primo: « Deum nemo vidit unquam ».

66. (Vers. 38.). *Et verbum eius non habetis in vobis manens,* scilicet per fidem; unde et ratio subditur: *Quia quem misit ille, huic vos non creditis.* Et ille quem misit, Verbum est et in cordibus manet per fidem; ad Ephesios tertio[^p315-2]: « Habitare Christum per fidem in cordibus vestris »; Isaiae nono: « Verbum misit Dominus in Iacob, et cecidit in Israel »; non mansit in Iacob, quia « sui eum non receperunt ».

67. (Vers. 39.). *Scrutamini Scripturas.* Tangitur hic *testimonium* quartum, scilicet *Scripturarum,* ad quod monet Dominus et excitat ad diligentiam et spiritualem intelligentiam; propter quod dicit: *Scrutamini Scripturas,* id est, interiorem intellectum perspicite, quia, sicut dicit Apostolus secundae ad Corinthios tertio[^p315-3], « littera occidit, spiritus autem vivificat ». Et rationem, quare debent perscrutari, subdit: *In quibus vos putatis vitam aeternam habere,* id est, per quas putatis cognoscere, et per hoc vitam aeternam habere, quia per illas putatis Deum cognoscere, qui est vita aeterna; Sapientiae decimo quinto[^p315-4]: « Nosse te consummata iustitia est, et scire iustitiam tuam et virtutem tuam radix est immortalitatis ». Has Scripturas Christus dicit sibi testificari; unde et subdit: *Illae sunt, quae testimonium perhibent de me,* omnes scilicet Scripturae veteris testamenti; Lucae ultimo[^p315-5]: « Oportet, impleri omnia, quae scripta sunt in lege Moysi et Prophetis et Psalmis de me », dixit Dominus Iesus; Actuum decimo octavo dicitur de Apollo, quod « vehementer Iudaeos revincebat publice, ostendens per Scripturas, esse Iesum Christum[^p315-6] ».
<!-- page 316 -->

### Quaestiones.

68. Quaest. I. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Si ego testimonium perhibeo de me ipso, testimonium meum non est verum.*

**Contra:** 1. Numerorum vigesimo tertio[^p316-1]: *Non est Deus ut homo, ut mentiatur:* ergo si Christus est Deus, ergo etc.

2. Item, infra octavo[^p316-2]: *Si ego testimonium perhibeo de me ipso, testimonium meum verum est;* et hic dicit, quod *non est verum:* ergo etc.

Respondetur ad hoc tripliciter. Prima responsio est, faciendo vim in *subiecto:* si ego secundum *humanam* naturam testimonium perhibeo de me ipso, testimonium meum non est verum; est tamen verum secundum *divinam.* — Sed istud non solvit, quia, sicut nec secundum *divinam* mentitur, ita nec secundum *humanam.*

Propterea respondetur, ut fiat vis in *attributo: testimonium meum non est verum,* id est, non est efficax nec acceptabitur tanquam verum; tamen verum est, quia verum dico. Et sic accipitur, ita ut dicatur verum secundum *rem,* sed non verum secundum *Iudaeorum acceptionem*[^p316-3].

Aliter respondetur, quod verbum istud intelligendum est praecise: *si ego testimonium perhibeo de me ipso,* supple: ego solus, testimonium meum non est efficax; sed si ego cum alio, efficax est; unde dicit infra[^p316-4]: *Quia non sum solus.* Ideo, cum supra se suo testimonio commendasset, suam ostendens virtutem; ad hoc roborandum adducit aliena testimonia.

69. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de *testimonio operum: Opera,* inquit, *quae ego facio, testimonium perhibent de me.* — Sed eadem opera fecerunt Prophetae, ut miracula, eadem Apostoli ipsum sequentes et maiora[^p316-5]: ergo si per opera poterat sufficienter cognosci, quod esset Christus, eadem ratione de quolibet.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod miracula bonorum et malorum discerni possunt, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p316-6]. Cognoscere igitur poterant, quod miracula Christi et opera essent boni viri et sancti; sed Christus dicebat, se esse Deum; sed nullus bonus homo, manens bonus, potest se Deo facere aequalem, immo fur est: ergo si Christum cognoscere poterant *sanctum* per opera, et per verba, quod esset *Christus;* opera fuerunt sufficiens testimonium, quod Christus diceret verum, ac per hoc, quod esset Christus. Similiter de aliis bonis, quod ipsi dicerent *verum;* sed ipsi non dicebant de se[^p316-7], sed de Christo, ut Apostoli.

Aliter dicitur, quod opera non fuerunt testimonium *cogens,* sed *inducens,* et similiter testimonium Ioannis et Scripturae; sed testimonium Patris per revelationem est *compellens*[^p316-8].

70. Quaest. III. Quaeritur de *testimonio Patris.* — Dicit enim, quod *neque vocem eius audistis neque speciem vidistis;* dicit Iudaeis.

**Contra:** Nonne multi aderant, quando Lucae tertio[^p316-9] vox de caelo facta est sensibiliter: *Tu es Filius meus dilectus?* — Item, Deuteronomii quinto: *Vocem eius audivimus de medio ignis.* — Item, Exodi vigesimo: *Vos vidistis, quod de caelo locutus sum vobis.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod dupliciter loquitur Deus, et dupliciter apparet: uno modo *in se* et *per se;* et sic loquitur per internam mentis revelationem modo quodam indicibili[^p316-10], sed tamen quodam modo intelligibili, et sic non loquitur malis; alio modo *per subiectam creaturam,* et sic locutus est in Legis datione. Hoc secundo modo *viderunt* in subiecta creatura et *audierunt* sensibiliter, sed primo modo non; et de hoc loquitur hic. Unde Augustinus dicit super hoc[^p316-11]: *Pater testimonium perhibet:* « Qui me exterius oculis hominum visibilem exhibuit interius corda hominum ad me cognoscendum accendit ».

71. Quaest. IV. Quaeri etiam potest de *sufficientia et ordine* istorum testimoniorum. — Et notandum, quod primum testimonium, scilicet Ioannis, fuit *inducens;* secundum, scilicet operum, *promovens;* tertium, scilicet Patris, *compellens*[^p316-12]; quartum, scilicet Scripturae, *in veritate stabiliens.* Et ex his resultat unum testimonium completum et perfectum, et gradatim ordinantur secundum dictas conditiones.

## English
<!-- page 313 -->

59. *If I give testimony* etc. This is the fourth part of the chapter, in which is determined the *corroboration of the foregoing confutation,* which is made by manifold testimony approving what the Lord said above. And this part has two: for first is adduced the *certainty of the testimonies;* but second, the *reproof* of the unbelieving Jews, at[^p313-17]: *And you will not come to me.*

The first part has two: for first the Lord, although he could adduce his own testimony, *declines* it; second, he *adduces* the testimonies of others, at[^p313-18]: *You sent* etc.
<!-- page 314 -->

(Verse 31.). He declines, then, *his own testimony,* because they would not believe it, as being uncertain; whence he says: *If I give testimony of myself, my testimony is not true,* that is, it is not efficacious for believing, above all if I am alone; whence below, the eighth chapter[^p314-1]: « The Pharisees said to him: Thou givest testimony of thyself; thy testimony is not true ». Yet true testimonies are not lacking to me; whence he adds:

60. (Verse 32.). *There is another that giveth testimony of me,* namely the Father, or also John, and of both is rightly understood what follows: *And I know that the testimony is true which he giveth of me;* for he testifies as one certain. For the Father gives true testimony, because, as is said below, the eighth chapter[^p314-2], « he that sent me is true ». John is likewise certain; whence above, the first chapter, John said: « I saw, and I gave testimony that this is the Son of God »; Proverbs, the twelfth chapter: « He that speaketh what he knoweth is a judge of justice, but he that lieth is a deceitful witness ».

61. *You sent* etc. The Lord has declined his own testimony; here, second, he adduces *the testimony of another.* And because in the mouth of several witnesses testimony is firm[^p314-3], therefore this part has four parts according to the four testimonies which he adduces. The first is *John's;* the second is *of the work;* the third, *the divine;* and the fourth, *of the Scriptures.* And these testimonies are distinguished: for the first is *pronounced,* the second *exhibited,* the third *inspired* and the fourth *written.*

(Verse 33.). First, then, he adduces *the testimony of John,* which had been given to them at their own petition; therefore he says: *You sent to John;* he did not thrust himself forward, but answered at your petition, above, the first chapter[^p314-4]: « And this is the testimony of John, when the Jews sent to him » etc. *And he gave testimony to the truth,* as a faithful witness; Proverbs, the fourteenth chapter: « A faithful witness will not lie ». And lest the Lord should seem to beg human testimony, he adds:

62. (Verse 34.). *But I receive not testimony from man,* supply: on account of my own need; the Psalm[^p314-5]: « I have said to the Lord, thou art my God, for thou hast no need of my goods »; but I recall it on account of your salvation. Whence he also says: *But I say these things, that you may be saved,* namely by believing in me through his testimony, because, as is said in Mark, the last chapter, « he that believeth and is baptized shall be saved ». And truly they ought to have believed at John's testimony; whence he also adds:

63. (Verse 35.). *He was a lamp burning,* through grace, *and shining,* through teaching[^tr-doctrina]; or: *burning,* through inward charity, *shining,* through the outward honesty of life. Bernard[^p314-6]: « To burn only is little, to shine is vain, but to shine and to burn is perfect ». Therefore John was most fittingly signified by Elias; Ecclesiasticus, the forty-eighth chapter: « Elias stood up as a fire, and his word burnt like a torch ». To one, then, who shone so perfectly, faith was to be given, which the Jews did not do; whence he says: *But you were willing to rejoice for a time in his light,* namely that you might believe his teaching and testimony and imitate the life which you saw for a little while; Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p314-7]: « John came not eating nor drinking, and they say: He hath a devil »; Matthew, the twenty-first chapter: « John came to you in the way of justice, and you did not believe him. But the publicans and the harlots believed him ».

64. (Verse 36.). *But I have a testimony.* Here there is touched on the second *testimony,* namely *of the works,* which is greater than the foregoing. On this account he says: *But I have a greater testimony than John,* because it is more moving to belief in me. *For the works themselves*[^p314-8], *which the Father hath given me,* because from him I have received *to be* and *to work; that I may perfect them,* that is, do them perfectly; because « the works of God are perfect », Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter. *The works themselves, which I do,* by my own virtue, *give testimony of me, that the Father hath sent me,* as God his Son, because he communicated such works to him; below, the fifteenth chapter[^p314-9]: « If I had not come and had not done among them the works which no other man hath done, they would not have sin ». These works are to be believed, that the Father sent him; below, the fourteenth chapter: « The Father who abideth in me, he doth the works »; and again: « If you believe not me, believe the works ».

65. (Verse 37.). *And the Father who sent me* etc. Here there is touched on the third *testimony,* namely *the divine,* which he also calls *the paternal;* on account of which he says: *And the Father who sent me,* through the incarnation, *hath given testimony of me,* through inward inspiration; because, as is said below, the sixth chapter[^p314-10], « no man cometh to me, unless the Father draw him ». Whence by an intelligible voice he reveals
<!-- page 315 -->
and testifies, which the carnal Jews could neither hear nor see him speaking. Therefore he says: *Neither have you heard his voice at any time,* through revelation to the mind; as Peter heard, Matthew, the sixteenth chapter[^p315-1]: « Blessed art thou, Simon Bar-Jona, because flesh and blood hath not revealed it to thee, but my Father who is in heaven ». *Nor seen his shape,* through open vision; Exodus, the thirty-third chapter: « Man shall not see me and live »; and above, the first chapter: « No man hath seen God at any time ».

66. (Verse 38.). *And you have not his word abiding in you,* namely through faith; whence the reason is also added: *For whom he hath sent, him you believe not.* And he whom he sent is the Word, and abides in hearts through faith; to the Ephesians, the third chapter[^p315-2]: « That Christ dwell by faith in your hearts »; Isaias, the ninth chapter: « The Lord sent a word into Jacob, and it hath lighted upon Israel »; it did not abide in Jacob, because « his own received him not ».

67. (Verse 39.). *Search the Scriptures.* Here there is touched on the fourth *testimony,* namely *of the Scriptures,* to which the Lord admonishes and stirs up, to diligence and to spiritual intelligence; on account of which he says: *Search the Scriptures,* that is, look through to the interior understanding, because, as the Apostle says in the second to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p315-3], « the letter killeth, but the spirit giveth life ». And the reason why they ought to search them through he adds: *In which you think to have life everlasting,* that is, through which you think to know, and through this to have life everlasting, because through them you think to know God, who is life everlasting; Wisdom, the fifteenth chapter[^p315-4]: « For to know thee is perfect justice, and to know thy justice and thy power is the root of immortality ». These Scriptures Christ says testify to him; whence he also adds: *The same are they that give testimony of me,* namely all the Scriptures of the old testament; Luke, the last chapter[^p315-5]: « All things must needs be fulfilled which are written in the law of Moses and in the Prophets and in the Psalms concerning me », said the Lord Jesus; in Acts, the eighteenth chapter, it is said of Apollo that « he vehemently convinced the Jews openly, showing by the Scriptures that Jesus is the Christ[^p315-6] ».
<!-- page 316 -->

### Questions.

68. Quaest. I. It is asked concerning this which he says: *If I give testimony of myself, my testimony is not true.*

**On the contrary:** 1. Numbers, the twenty-third chapter[^p316-1]: *God is not as a man, that he should lie:* therefore if Christ is God, therefore etc.

2. Likewise, below, the eighth chapter[^p316-2]: *If I give testimony of myself, my testimony is true;* and here he says that *it is not true:* therefore etc.

It is responded to this in three ways. The first response is by putting the force in the *subject:* if I give testimony of myself according to *human* nature, my testimony is not true; yet it is true according to the *divine.* — But this does not solve it, because, just as he does not lie according to the *divine,* so neither according to the *human.*

Therefore it is responded so that the force falls on the *attribute: my testimony is not true,* that is, it is not efficacious, nor will it be accepted as true; yet it is true, because I speak the truth. And it is taken thus, so that it is called true according to *the thing,* but not true according to *the acceptance of the Jews*[^p316-3].

Otherwise it is responded that this saying is to be understood precisely: *if I give testimony of myself,* supply: I alone, my testimony is not efficacious; but if I with another, it is efficacious; whence he says below[^p316-4]: *Because I am not alone.* Therefore, since above he had commended himself by his own testimony, showing his virtue, to corroborate this he adduces the testimonies of others.

69. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked concerning *the testimony of the works: The works,* he says, *which I do, give testimony of me.* — But the Prophets did the same works, such as miracles, and the Apostles following him did the same and greater[^p316-5]: therefore if through the works he could sufficiently be known to be the Christ, by the same reason so could anyone.

**I respond:** It must be said that the miracles of the good and of the wicked can be discerned, as Augustine says[^p316-6]. They could know, then, that the miracles and works of Christ were those of a good and holy man; but Christ said that he was God; but no good man, remaining good, can make himself equal to God, nay rather he is a thief: therefore if they could know Christ to be *holy* through the works, and through the words that he was *the Christ,* the works were sufficient testimony that Christ spoke the truth, and by this, that he was the Christ. Similarly of the other good men, that they spoke *the truth;* but they did not speak of themselves[^p316-7], but of Christ, as the Apostles did.

Otherwise it is said that the works were not a *coercive* testimony, but an *inducing* one, and likewise the testimony of John and of the Scripture; but the testimony of the Father through revelation is *compelling*[^p316-8].

70. Quaest. III. It is asked concerning *the testimony of the Father.* — For he says that *neither have you heard his voice nor seen his shape;* he says it to the Jews.

**On the contrary:** Were not many present when, in Luke, the third chapter[^p316-9], a voice came from heaven sensibly: *Thou art my beloved Son?* — Likewise, Deuteronomy, the fifth chapter: *We have heard his voice out of the midst of the fire.* — Likewise, Exodus, the twentieth chapter: *You have seen that I have spoken to you from heaven.*

**I respond:** It must be said that God speaks in two ways, and appears in two ways: in one mode *in himself* and *through himself;* and thus he speaks through inward revelation to the mind in a certain unspeakable mode[^p316-10], but yet in a certain intelligible mode, and thus he does not speak to the wicked; in another mode *through a subject creature,* and thus he spoke in the giving of the Law. In this second mode they *saw* in a subject creature and *heard* sensibly, but in the first mode not; and of this he speaks here. Whence Augustine says on this[^p316-11]: *The Father giveth testimony:* « He who exhibited me outwardly, visible to the eyes of men, kindles inwardly the hearts of men to know me ».

71. Quaest. IV. It can also be asked concerning *the sufficiency and order* of these testimonies. — And it is to be noted that the first testimony, namely John's, was *inducing;* the second, namely of the works, *promoting;* the third, namely the Father's, *compelling*[^p316-12]; the fourth, namely of the Scripture, *establishing in truth.* And from these results one testimony, complete and perfect, and they are ordered by degrees according to the said conditions.

[^tr-lumen]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin here is *lumen,* which this translation leaves untranslated to keep it apart from *lux* ("light"). It stands in the apparatus only, in the Gloss's sentence *Christus verum lumen*, which echoes John 1:9 (where the Vulgate has *lux vera*) but is the Gloss's own prose, setting Christ the *lumen* against the Prophets and Apostles who are *lucernae*, "lamps"; so the verse test does not apply and "lumen" stands. In the body of this pericope the word does not occur; *lux* occurs once, at 5:35 (*in luce eius*), and is rendered "light", while *lucerna* is rendered "lamp".

[^tr-doctrina]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin *doctrina* holds together what English divides among doctrine, teaching and learning. Here it is what makes John *lucens*, "shining", set beside *ardens*, "burning", through grace: the light he gives outward to others, and three sentences later it is what the Jews ought to have believed (*eius doctrinae et testimonio*); so the English takes "teaching", the act of John's preaching, rather than "doctrine", a body of teaching held.
