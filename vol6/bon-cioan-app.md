### Appendix.[^p632-4]

### Annotatio 1.

Dicitur Filius Dei *lux* propter quinque. Quia maxime se *communicat* et etiam maxime se diffundit, unde « et in tenebris lucet[^p632-5] »; Dionysius: « Imago est bonitatis illius ». — Quia omnia *existentia ornat;* Genesis primo: « Fiat lux »; Damascenus: « Ibi incipit ornatus mundi et decor »; similiter et lumen intelligibile. — Quia omnia *viventia format,* ut patet in floribus et plantis; propter hoc dicitur *lumen vitae.* — Quia *animalia laetificat,* ut patet in avibus, aurora rutilante, quia spiritus luminosae naturae congaudet suo simili et delectatur in coniunctione convenientis; Ecclesiastae undecimo[^p632-6]: « Dulce lumen et delectabile est oculis videre solem »; Tobiae quinto: « Quale gaudium mihi erit, quia in tenebris sedeo et lumen caeli non video »? — Quia *volentia illuminari illuminat,* suos splendores immittens; Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo: « Implebit animam tuam splendoribus ».

### Collatio I.

(Cant. 6, 1.). *Dilectus meus descendit in hortum suum ad areolam aromatum, ut pascatur in hortis et lilia colligat.*

1. Hortus iste est *Ecclesia* et fidelis *anima;* et bene dixit *suum,* quem ipse *fecit,* ipse *excoluit,* ipse virtutum pigmentis *consevit,* fonte gratiae suae, ne deficeret, *rigavit,* muro custodiae suae, ne ab extraneis profanaretur, *conclusit.* — Hortus iste est *areola aromatum,* vernans *virore gratiarum,* fragrans *odore virtutum.*

2. In hac autem *areola,* quae est Ecclesia, *pascitur* Christus *in hortis,* id est mentibus fidelium, tribus pascuis: *operibus misericordiae, munda virginitate, devotione suavi.* — De primo dicitur Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p632-7]: « Esurivi, et dedistis mihi manducare ». — De secundo, Canticorum secundo: « Qui pascitur inter lilia, donec aspiret dies, et inclinentur umbrae ». — De tertio Apocalypsis tertio:
<!-- page 633 -->
« Cum apperuerit mihi, ego intrabo ad eum et coenabo cum illo, et ipse mecum ».

3. Haec tria figurata sunt in tribus conviviis, quae celebravit Dominus post resurrectionem, scilicet cum discipulis suis. — Comedit enim cum *euntibus* in Emmaus[^p633-1], qui « coegerunt eum » ad hospitium, quod pertinet ad *opera misericordiae.* — Comedit etiam cum *piscantibus* super litus « partem piscis assi et favum mellis », quod pertinet ad *mundam virginitatem.* In *pisce* enim *asso* designatur contra libidinem carnis *mortificatio;* in *melle,* quod non provenit ex coitu, sed ex mundis floribus, *virginitatis munda refectio.* — Comedit quoque cum discipulis *ascensurus in caelum*[^p633-2], « praecipiens eis, ab Hierosolymis ne discederent », quod interpretatur *pacis visio,* ubi permanent devoti, dum supernam contemplantur pacem; et haec devotio Christi est refectio.

4. In hac quoque areola *lilia colligit,* cum plures ad se quadam familiaritate spirituali trahit. Tria sunt lilia: *innocentiae, virginitatis* et *perfectionis.* — De primo in Canticis[^p633-3]: « Sicut lilium inter spinas, sic amica mea inter filias »; sicut *lilium inter spinas,* sic innocens, qui est pacificus « cum his qui oderunt pacem ». — De secundo: « Qui pascitur inter lilia », « septus choreis virginum ». — De tertio dicit Osee in fine[^p633-4]: « Ero quasi ros, Israel germinabit quasi lilium ». Quod autem hoc lilium perfectionem significet, sequens docet littera: « Et erumpet radix eius ut Libani; ibunt rami eius », id est virtutes, crescendo usque ad perfectionem gloriae suscipiendae proficient etc.

### Collatio II.

*Renovamini spiritu mentis vestrae,* ad Ephesios quarto[^p633-5]. Ad renovationem spiritus nos invitat Apostolus.

1. *Ferrum* renovatur in igne, deposita rubigine, et spiritus noster renovatur in igne tribulationum et afflictionum, nisi sit exsecrabilis immunditia nostra; de qua Ezechielis vigesimo quarto[^p633-6]: « Multo labore sudatum est, et non exivit de ea rubigo, neque per ignem exsecrabilis immunditia tua ». Accendit Deus caminum tribulationum et infirmitatum, ut renovetur spiritus noster, deposita rubigine peccatorum; sed ita exsecrabilis est immunditia nostra, quod neque per hunc ignem tollitur.

2. Renovatur iterum spiritus mentis nostrae, sicut renovatur *aurum* vel *argentum* in conflatorio, quia, sicut dicitur Ieremiae sexto[^p633-7], succensum est conflatorium, appositum est plumbum, ut purgetur aurum. *Succensum* fuit *conflatorium* in passione Christi; *appositum* fuit *plumbum,* scilicet caro Christi, ut, carne ipsa in passione consumta, purgaremur sicut aurum vel argentum. Sed conqueritur Deus de eo, quod non sumus purgati; unde sequitur[^p633-8]: « Universi sunt consumti; defecit conflatorium. In igne consumtum est plumbum, frustra conflavit conflator; malitiae eorum non sunt consumtae. Argentum reprobum vocate eos, quia Dominus proiecit eos ». Ecce, quam dura sententia Domini, cuius *proiectio* est reprobatio, id est ad aeternum supplicium reservatio.

3. Item renovatur spiritus noster, sicut renovatur « *cervus* desiderans ad fontes aquarum[^p633-9] ». *Cervus,* comesto serpente, aestuat, sitit et festinat ad aquas; sic homo, hausto peccati veneno, interius circa se aestuans sitiat et desideret et desiderando festinet ad aquas poenitentiae.

4. Item renovatur spiritus noster ad similitudinem *accipitris;* de quo Iob trigesimo nono[^p633-10]: « Nunquid per sapientiam tuam plumescit accipiter, expandens alas suas ad austrum »? *Accipiter expandit alas suas ad austrum,* qui est ventus calidus, ut ad teporem austri veteres plumas deponat et accipiat novas. Ad hoc ergo nos invitat Apostolus in hoc loco, ut ad similitudinem accipitris deponamus veteres pennas, id est veterem hominem, scilicet pristinam conversationem nostram, et accipiamus novum.

5. Unde sequitur[^p633-11]: *Induite novum hominem,* id est Christum, quem *induere* est ei novis moribus conformari. — Debemus autem induere Christum, sicut *aër* induit lucem, quia ipse « est *lux,* et tenebrae in eo non sunt ullae ». — Item sicut *ferrum* ignem, quia ipse « ignis consumens est[^p633-12] ». — Item sicut *lana* colorem, quia ipse sanguine suo omnes nos tinxit; unde et passionem suam appellat *baptismum,* id est tinctionem. — *Aër,* induendo lucem, totus fit lux; *ferrum,* induendo ignem, to-
<!-- page 634 -->
tum fit ignis; *lana,* induendo colorem, tota fit colorata: sic Ecclesia tota Christi sanguine tingi debet; unde Ieremiae duodecimo[^p634-1]: « Nunquid avis discolor hereditas mea mihi? Nunquid avis tincta per totum »? quasi dicat: Nunquid Ecclesia velut avis virtutum pennis volans, si non sit tota sanguine meo tincta, erit hereditas mea? quasi dicat: Non.

### Annotatio 2.

*Nolite conformari huic saeculo*[^p634-2], id est in forma huius saeculi formati a diabolo tanquam fusore; de quo Isaiae quinquagesimo quarto[^p634-3] dicit, loquens in persona Domini; « Ego creavi fabrum » id est angelum, qui nunc diabolus, « sufflantem in igne prunas et proferentem vas in opus suum ». Habet diabolus *fornacem* mundum, ubi format *vasa* sua. Haec est *fornax Babylonis,* quam fecit incendi Nabuchodonosor quatuor fomentis, scilicet *naphta, malleolis, pice* et *stuppa*[^p634-4]. *Naphta* est ossa olivarum cum amurca; siquidem cum de oliva exprimitur oleum, remanet dura testa cum faece, scilicet amurca, et istud est *naphta,* et significat peccatum. Nam si ab ipsa separetur usus operum misericordiae, quod significatur in oleo, nihil aliud remanet quam incendium gehennae. — *Malleoli* sunt vinearum sarmenta. *Vinea* potentiam saecularem significat, quae se ferre non potest sine obedientia subditorum, sicut vinea, quantumcumque generosa sit, tamen indiget, ut sustentetur paxillis. *Rami* huius vineae sunt potentes huius saeculi, qui si adhaereant stipiti, id est Deo, a quo omnis potestas, nihil est speciosius in arboribus, id est hominibus. Si autem separentur a stipite, ut sint sarmenta, ignis sunt materia. — *Pix,* quae nigra est et inquinat et accensa tetrum facit odorem, *luxuria* est. *Stuppa* vanitas gloriae saecularis, quae cito transit sicut stuppa ad ignem.

2. His prunis fervet fornax diaboli, in qua funditur homo velut metallum, et vadit alius in forma *luxuriae,* et alius in forma *avaritiae;* et sic de aliis. Sed pueri[^p634-5], qui missi fuerant in fornacem Babylonis, exierunt illaesi, ita quod nec « capillus capitis eorum adustus est, nec sarabala eorum fuerunt immutata ». Et beatus homo, cuius in hac fornace capillus, id est cognitio, et sarabala, id est continentia, non fuerint immutata[^p634-6].

### Appendix.[^p632-4]

### Annotation 1.

The Son of God is called *light* on account of five things. Because it *communicates* itself most of all and also diffuses itself most of all, whence « and it shineth in darkness[^p632-5] »; Dionysius: « It is the image of that goodness ». — Because it *adorns all existing things;* Genesis, the first chapter: « Be light made »; Damascene: « There begins the adornment of the world and its comeliness »; and likewise the intelligible lumen[^tr-lumen]. — Because it *forms all living things,* as is plain in flowers and plants; on account of this it is called *the lumen of life.* — Because it *gladdens animals,* as is plain in birds, when the dawn glows red, since a spirit of luminous nature rejoices together with its like and delights in the conjunction of what is fitting; Ecclesiastes, the eleventh chapter[^p632-6]: « The light is sweet, and delightful it is for the eyes to see the sun »; Tobias, the fifth chapter: « What manner of joy shall be to me, since I sit in darkness and see not the light of heaven »? — Because it *illuminates those willing to be illuminated,* sending in its splendours; Isaias, the fifty-eighth chapter: « He will fill thy soul with splendours ».

### Collation I.

(Cant. 6, 1.). *My beloved is gone down into his garden, to the bed of aromatical spices, to feed in the gardens, and to gather lilies.*

1. This garden is the *Church* and the faithful *soul;* and he said well *his,* which he himself *made,* he himself *tilled,* he himself *sowed* with the pigments of the virtues, *watered* with the fountain of his grace, lest it should fail, *enclosed* with the wall of his custody, lest it should be profaned by strangers. — This garden is a *bed of aromatical spices,* flourishing *with the greenness of graces,* fragrant *with the odour of virtues.*

2. And in this *bed,* which is the Church, Christ *feeds in the gardens,* that is, in the minds of the faithful, on three pastures: *on works of mercy, on clean virginity, on sweet devotion.* — Of the first it is said in Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p632-7]: « I was hungry, and you gave me to eat ». — Of the second, Canticles, the second chapter: « Who feedeth among the lilies, till the day break, and the shadows retire ». — Of the third, the Apocalypse, the third chapter:
<!-- page 633 -->
« When he shall have opened to me, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me ».

3. These three are figured in the three banquets which the Lord celebrated after the resurrection, namely with his disciples. — For he ate with those *going* to Emmaus[^p633-1], who « constrained him » to lodge with them, which pertains to *the works of mercy.* — He ate also with those *fishing,* on the shore, « a piece of a broiled fish, and a honeycomb », which pertains to *clean virginity.* For in the *broiled fish* is designated the *mortification* of the flesh against lust; in the *honey,* which does not come from coition but from clean flowers, *the clean refreshment of virginity.* — He ate too with the disciples *when about to ascend into heaven*[^p633-2], « commanding them that they should not depart from Jerusalem », which is interpreted *the vision of peace,* where the devout remain while they contemplate the peace on high; and this devotion is the refreshment of Christ.

4. In this bed too he *gathers lilies,* when he draws many to himself by a certain spiritual familiarity. There are three lilies: *of innocence, of virginity* and *of perfection.* — Of the first, in the Canticles[^p633-3]: « As the lily among thorns, so is my love among the daughters »; as *the lily among thorns,* so the innocent man, who is peaceable « with them that hated peace ». — Of the second: « Who feedeth among the lilies », « hedged about with choirs of virgins ». — Of the third Osee says at the end[^p633-4]: « I will be as the dew, Israel shall spring as the lily ». And that this lily signifies perfection, the letter following teaches: « And his root shall shoot forth as that of Libanus; his branches shall spread », that is, the virtues, by growing will make progress even to the perfection of the glory to be received etc.

### Collation II.

*Be renewed in the spirit of your mind,* to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter[^p633-5]. The Apostle invites us to the renewal of the spirit.

1. *Iron* is renewed in the fire, its rust laid aside, and our spirit is renewed in the fire of tribulations and afflictions, unless our uncleanness be execrable; of which Ezechiel, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p633-6]: « With much labour hath it been sweated at, and its rust is not gone out of it, nor by fire thy execrable uncleanness ». God kindles the furnace of tribulations and infirmities, that our spirit may be renewed, the rust of sins laid aside; but so execrable is our uncleanness, that not even by this fire is it taken away.

2. The spirit of our mind is renewed again, as *gold* or *silver* is renewed in the crucible, since, as is said in Jeremias, the sixth chapter[^p633-7], the crucible is kindled, the lead is put to it, that the gold may be purged. *The crucible was kindled* in the passion of Christ; *the lead was put to it,* namely the flesh of Christ, that, that flesh being consumed in the passion, we might be purged as gold or silver. But God complains of this, that we are not purged; whence there follows[^p633-8]: « They are all consumed; the crucible hath failed. The lead is consumed in the fire, the refiner hath refined in vain; their wickednesses are not consumed. Call them reprobate silver, because the Lord hath cast them away ». Behold how hard is the sentence of the Lord, whose *casting away* is reprobation, that is, a reserving for eternal penalty.

3. Likewise our spirit is renewed, as is renewed « the *hart* longing for the fountains of waters[^p633-9] ». The *hart,* having eaten a serpent, burns, thirsts and hastens to the waters; so man, having drunk the poison of sin, burning inwardly within himself, should thirst and long and, longing, hasten to the waters of penance.

4. Likewise our spirit is renewed after the likeness of the *hawk;* of which Job, the thirty-ninth chapter[^p633-10]: « Doth the hawk wax feathered by thy wisdom, spreading her wings to the south »? *The hawk spreads her wings to the south,* which is a warm wind, that at the warmth of the south wind she may lay down her old feathers and take new ones. To this, then, the Apostle invites us in this place, that after the likeness of the hawk we should lay down our old feathers, that is, the old man, namely our former conversation, and take the new.

5. Whence there follows[^p633-11]: *Put on the new man,* that is, Christ, whom *to put on* is to be conformed to him by new manners. — And we ought to put on Christ as the *air* puts on light, since he « is *light,* and in him there is no darkness ». — Likewise as *iron* puts on fire, since he « is a consuming fire[^p633-12] ». — Likewise as *wool* puts on colour, since he has dyed us all with his blood; whence he also calls his passion a *baptism,* that is, a dyeing. — *The air,* by putting on light, becomes wholly light; *iron,* by putting on fire, be-
<!-- page 634 -->
comes wholly fire; *wool,* by putting on colour, becomes wholly coloured: so the Church ought to be wholly dyed with the blood of Christ; whence Jeremias, the twelfth chapter[^p634-1]: « Is my inheritance to me a speckled bird? Is it a bird dyed throughout »? as if he were to say: Will the Church, flying like a bird with the feathers of the virtues, be my inheritance, if she be not wholly dyed with my blood? as if he were to say: No.

### Annotation 2.

*Be not conformed to this world*[^p634-2], that is, to the form of this world, formed by the devil as by a founder; of whom Isaias, the fifty-fourth chapter[^p634-3], says, speaking in the person of the Lord; « I have created the smith » that is, the angel, who is now the devil, « that bloweth the coals in the fire, and bringeth forth a vessel for his work ». The devil has the world as a *furnace,* where he forms his *vessels.* This is the *furnace of Babylon,* which Nabuchodonosor had kindled with four fuels, namely *naphtha, faggots, pitch* and *tow*[^p634-4]. *Naphtha* is the stones of olives with the lees; for when the oil is pressed out of the olive, the hard husk remains with the dregs, namely the lees, and this is *naphtha,* and it signifies sin. For if the use of the works of mercy, which is signified in the oil, be separated from it, nothing else remains but the burning of Gehenna. — *Faggots* are the cuttings of vines. The *vine* signifies secular power, which cannot bear itself up without the obedience of subjects, as a vine, however noble it be, nevertheless needs to be held up by stakes. The *branches* of this vine are the powerful of this world, who, if they cleave to the stock, that is, to God, from whom is all power, nothing is more comely among the trees, that is, among men. But if they are separated from the stock, so as to be cuttings, they are matter for the fire. — *Pitch,* which is black and defiles and, when kindled, gives off a foul odour, is *lust.* *Tow* is the vanity of worldly glory, which passes quickly as tow at the fire.

2. With these coals the devil's furnace glows, in which man is cast like metal, and one goes off in the form of *lust,* and another in the form of *avarice;* and so of the rest. But the children[^p634-5], who had been cast into the furnace of Babylon, came out unhurt, so that neither « was a hair of their head singed, nor were their garments altered ». And blessed the man whose hair in this furnace, that is, knowledge, and whose garments, that is, continence, shall not have been altered[^p634-6].

     *Vigilate in orationibus* (I. Petr. 4, 7.). In verbis istis circa nostram instructionem duo facit Apostolus. Primo monet nos ad vigilandum; et hoc tangitur, cum dicitur: *Vigilate.* Secundo ostendit, in quibus debemus vigilare; et hoc tangitur, cum dicitur: *in orationibus.* — Notandum, quod duplex est vigilia, *bona* et *mala.* De *bona* dicitur in octavo Proverbiorum (v. 34.): « Beatus homo, qui audit et qui vigilat ad fores meas quotidie et observat ad postes ostii ».      *Watch in prayers* (I Pet. 4:7). In these words the Apostle does two things concerning our instruction. First he admonishes us to watch; and this is touched when it is said: *Watch.* Secondly he shows in what we ought to watch; and this is touched when it is said: *in prayers.* — It is to be noted that watching is twofold, *good* and *bad.* Of the *good* it is said in Proverbs, the eighth chapter (v. 34): « Blessed is the man that heareth and that watcheth daily at my gates, and waiteth at the posts of my doors ». Of the *bad* it is said in Isaias (29:20) « The scorner is consumed, and they are all cut off that watched for iniquity ». The first is *to be sought,* because by it the kingdom of heaven is acquired. Of this we have in Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter (v. 46): « Blessed is that servant, whom when his Lord shall come he shall find watching. Amen I say to you, he shall place him over all his goods ». — The second is *to be fled,* because it leads to the watching of hell. Of this we have in Job (21:30–32), where it is said: « The wicked man is reserved to the day of destruction, and he shall be brought to the day of wrath: Who shall reprove his way to his face? and who shall repay him what he hath done »? And there follows: « He shall be brought to the graves, and shall watch in the heap of the dead ».

     To the good watching the Apostle invites us when he says: *Watch;* and therefore it is to be noted that we ought to watch for three things: on account of *the uncertainty of death;* and of this we have in Matthew (24:42), where it is said: « Watch ye; for you know not when your Lord will come »; whether at even, or at midnight, or at the cockcrowing, or in the morning; and in Mark (13:33): « See ye therefore and watch, for ye know not when the time is ». As Seneca says: (cf. above, p. 588, note 9; cf. ibid., note 8) « Nothing is more certain than death, but nothing more uncertain than the hour of death ». — On account of *avoiding the assault of the devil;* and of this we have in the Epistle of blessed Peter (I, 5:8): « Be sober and watch; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, goeth about seeking whom he may devour, whom resist ye, strong in faith ». — On account of *acquiring eternal beatitude;* and of this we have in Ecclesiasticus (32:18), where it is said: « He that feareth God will receive his doctrine, and he that shall watch for him shall find blessing ». — On account of *the uncertainty of death* we ought to watch *perseveringly.*

[^tr-lumen]: **En.** Translator's note: this translation keeps *lux* as "light" and *lumen* as "lumen", except where a verse is quoted as a verse and no surrounding prose leans on the word. The first annotation of the Appendix is built on *lux* (*Dicitur Filius Dei lux propter quinque*), and *lumen* stands in it four times. Twice it is the annotation's own word, *similiter et lumen intelligibile* and *propter hoc dicitur lumen vitae*, set by the prose beside *lux* as a second name of the same thing, and there it stays "lumen" ("the intelligible lumen", "the lumen of life"). Twice it is inside a verse adduced for the fourth property, that light gladdens: Eccle. 11:7, *Dulce lumen et delectabile est oculis videre solem*, and Tob. 5:12, *lumen caeli non video*. The prose that brings them in leans on *delectatur* and on the gladdening, not on *lumen*, so under the verse test the received wording stands at both, "The light is sweet" and "see not the light of heaven", as the first chunk of this work already gave Tob. 5:12.

[^tr-doctrina]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin *doctrina* holds together what English divides among doctrine, teaching and learning. Its two sites in this chunk are both in the apparatus. The first is Jerome's gloss on Jeremias in p. 633, n. 7, *omne eloquium doctrinarum et sermo propheticus*, every utterance of those who teach set beside the prophetic word and perishing for those who scorn to hear it: that is teaching delivered, so the English takes the side of "teaching". The second is the quotation of Ecclus. 32:18 in the codex B collation printed in p. 634, n. 6, *Qui timet Deum excipiet doctrinam eius*, adduced for its second half, *qui vigilaverit ad illum*; nothing in the surrounding prose leans on *doctrina*, so the Douay's "will receive his doctrine" stands, the quotation branch of this translation's practice.
