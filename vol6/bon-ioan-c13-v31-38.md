# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XIII, Vers. 31-38
## *Postquam ostensa est Iudae perversitas ad deviandum, ostenditur discipulorum infirmitas ad Dominum sequendum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 433 -->

44. *Cum ergo exisset.* Postquam ostensa est Iudae perversitas ad deviandum, ostenditur *discipulorum infirmitas* ad Dominum sequendum. Et haec est quarta pars capituli, et hoc facit[^p433-2] hoc ordine. Primo enim praedicitur futura *Christi passio;* secundo, *discipulorum imperfectio;* tertio, *imperfectorum eruditio;* quarto, *reprimitur Petri praesumtio.*

(Vers. 31.). Praedicitur ergo primo *Christi futura passio* et per passionem *clarificatio,* quae accelerabat[^p433-3], proditore accelerante. Propter quod dicit: *Cum ergo exisset,* scilicet Iudas, ut perficeret quod conceperat; *dixit Iesus: Nunc clarificatus est Filius hominis. Nunc* dicit propter passionem instantem, cuius merito erat sublimandus; ad Philippenses secundo[^p433-4]: « Humiliavit semetipsum, propter quod et Deus illum exaltavit ». *Et Deus clarificatus est in illo,* quia per Christum omnibus innotuit; infra decimo septimo: « Pater, manifestavi nomen tuum hominibus, quos dedisti mihi de mundo ».

45. (Vers. 32.). *Et*[^p433-5] *si Deus clarificatus est in illo,* in passione per multam virtutis manifestationem; quia mors sua et passio valde fuit in gloriam Dei, sicut dicitur de Petro infra ultimo[^p433-6]: « Significans, qua morte clarificaturus esset Deum ». *Et Deus clarificabit eum in semetipso,* per gloriam resurrectionis; *et continuo clarificabit eum,* quia non poterat eius resurrectio differri; unde in Psalmo: « Non dabis Sanctum tuum videre corruptionem ». Et vult dicere, quod Christus erat cito glorificandus a Patre per resurrectionem, quia ad Patris claritatem manifestandam ipse sustinere debebat passionem. Unde infirmitas Christi-hominis Deum clarificavit; et ideo potentia Dei Christum mortuum suscitavit et clarificavit.

46. (Vers. 33.). *Filioli, adhuc modicum* etc. Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *discipulorum ad sequendum Christum imperfectio;* ideo vocat eos *filiolos,* id est in fide infirmos, quia firma fides facit *filios,* sed infirma *filiolos.* — Vel *filiolos* vocat prae amoris teneritudine, sicut dicit Priscianus[^p433-7], quod diminutiva aliquando prae familiaritate et amore dicuntur, ut *Sergiolus.* Horum igitur imperfectionem manifestat, cum subdit: *Adhuc modicum vobiscum sum,* per corporalem praesentiam; *et*[^p433-8] *sicut dixi Iudaeis* — supra septimo et octavo — *quaeretis me; quo ego vado, vos non potestis venire; et vobis dico modo;* quia passionis arduitatem ferre non potestis. Ideo non poterant, quia virtutem ex alto non susceperant; ideo dicitur eis Lucae ultimo[^p433-9]: « Sedete in civitate, donec induamini virtute ex alto »; quia sine virtutis robore in praelio stare non est possibile.

47. (Vers. 34.). *Mandatum novum do vobis.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *discipulorum imperfectorum instructio.* Et quia erant imperfecti, Dominus dat eis remedium dilectionis, ut mutuo se diligant et sustentent; propter quod dicit: *Mandatum*
<!-- page 434 -->
*novum do vobis, ut diligatis invicem;* ideo *novum,* quia semper debet esse recens in corde, quia semper debetur dilectio et nunquam inveteratur. Unde ad Romanos decimo tertio[^p434-1]: « Nemini quidquam debeatis, nisi ut invicem diligatis ». Et *modus* diligendi exprimitur: *Sicut dilexi vos, ut et vos diligatis invicem.* Ipse autem sic dilexit, ut magis diligeret salutem nostram quam vitam suam; sic unusquisque magis animam proximi quam corpus suum; primae Ioannis tertio[^p434-2]: « In hoc cognovimus caritatem Dei, quoniam ille pro nobis animam suam posuit; et nos debemus pro fratribus animas ponere ». Et *ratio movens* ad dilectionem additur:

48. (Vers. 35.). *In hoc cognoscent omnes, quia discipuli mei estis, si dilectionem ad invicem habueritis;* quia, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p434-3], « dilectio propria et specialis virtus est piorum atque sanctorum. Haec est, quae discernit filios regni a filiis perditionis »; ad Ephesios quarto[^p434-4]: « Nolite contristare Spiritum sanctum Dei, in quo signati estis in die redemptionis ». Hoc autem signaculum Spiritus est dilectio; ad Romanos quinto: « Caritas Dei diffusa est in cordibus nostris per Spiritum sanctum, qui datus est nobis ».

49. (Vers. 36.). *Dicit ei Simon Petrus.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *praesumtionis Petri repressio,* qua se offerebat ad Dominum imitandum. Ideo quaerit Petrus ab eo, ut se offerat: *Domine, quo vadis?* Et quia se offerre volebat, ostendit ei Dominus suam impotentiam, ne in verbis prorumpat: *Dicit ei Iesus*[^p434-5]*: Quo ego vado, non potes me sequi modo,* propter infirmitatem; *sequeris autem postea,* confirmatus virtute. Sed Petrus admonitione non flectitur; ideo subdit:

50. (Vers. 37.). *Respondit ei Petrus: Quare non possum sequi*[^p434-6]*? Animam meam ponam pro te;* et hoc est perfectum sequi; infra decimo quinto: « Maiorem caritatem nemo habet, ut animam suam ponat quis pro amicis suis ». Si ergo hoc possum, bene possum sequi. Et quia non desistit Petrus a praesumtione per admonitionem fragilitatis suae, nunc reprimitur per praedictionem praevaricationis futurae. Propter quod Dominus resumit verbum Petri, ut arguat:

51. (Vers. 38.). *Respondit ei Iesus: Animam tuam pro me pones?* Augustinus[^p434-7]: « Praeire potes, qui sequi non potes? Quid te esse credis? Audi, quid sis ». *Amen, amen dico tibi, non cantabit gallus, donec ter me neges.* Augustinus: « Qui mihi promittis mortem tuam, ter negabis vitam tuam. Quanta enim vita est confiteri Christum, tanta mors est negare Christum. Timendo mortem carnis tuae, dabis mortem animae tuae ».

### Quaestiones.

52. Quaest. I. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit primo: *Nunc clarificatus est Filius hominis;* quia in passione et proditione Iudae potius dicitur *minoratus* quam *clarificatus.*

Respondet Augustinus[^p434-8], quod hoc dicitur in *signo,* quia in egressu Iudae remanserunt tantum boni; et tunc significata est futura claritas, in qua tantum cum bonis erit Deus. — Aliter potest dici, quod hoc dicit secundum *meritum;* quia in passione meruit clarificari et quantum in se clarificavit Dominum[^p434-9].

53. Quaest. II. Sed quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Quo ego vado, vos non potestis venire.*

**Contra:** 1. Supra duodecimo[^p434-10] dictum est: *Qui mihi ministrat me sequatur:* ergo hortabatur eos ad impossibile.

2. Item, Petrus dicit primae Canonicae secundo[^p434-11]: *Christus passus est pro nobis, vobis relinquens exemplum, ut sequamini vestigia eius:* ergo omnibus dabatur exemplum. Quomodo ergo dicit: *Vos non potestis venire?*

Dicendum ad hoc, quod sicut *posse credere* dicitur dupliciter: uno modo, prout dicit *possibilitatem,* et sic est naturae; alio modo, prout dicit *potestatem activam,* et sic gratiae; sic et *posse imitari passionem;* quia *posse imperfectum* est in nobis, sed *potestas completa* per virtutem Spiritus sancti, qua nondum erant induti Apostoli[^p434-12].

<!-- page 435 -->
Sed *contra* hoc est, quia minima caritate potest quis resistere quantaecumque tentationi: ergo si Apostoli minimam haberent, ergo poterant.

Respondetur ad hoc secundum duplicem opinionem. Quidam dicunt, quod *ex minima,* non tamen *manente minima,* quia augetur; et secundum istos solvendum, ut prius, quod non poterant per id quod habebant. — Secundum aliam opinionem respondeo: secundum minimam *potest,* sed non *faciliter,* immo *valde difficiliter;* et ideo pauci resistunt[^p435-1].

54. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Mandatum novum do vobis* etc.

1. Quare non praecepit eis de dilectione Dei, quod *est primum et summum mandatum*[^p435-2]?

2. Item, quomodo dicit *novum,* quia ab initio datum fuit? Unde primae Ioannis secundo[^p435-3], loquens de hoc mandato, dicit: *Non mandatum novum scribo vobis, sed mandatum vetus.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum ad primum, quod in dilectione proximi dilectio Dei intelligitur[^p435-4].

Ad secundum dicendum, quod etsi istud mandatum fuerit in veteri testamento datum, tamen in novo est innovatum quantum ad modum: quia ibi diligere amicos, hic inimicos; ibi amicis benefacere, hic inimicis mandatur[^p435-5]; hoc importat quod subdit: *Sicut dilexi vos.*

55. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod Dominus dixit Petro: *Ter me negabis*[^p435-6]. — Ex hoc videtur, quod revelaverit Petro lapsum suum, et quod coegerit Petrum desperare.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod differt *praedicere* et *revelare.* Deus nulli *revelat,* sed tamen bene *praedicit;* quia quod *revelatur* certitudinaliter scitur, quod vero *praedicitur,* non; quia aliquando creditur *ex comminatione* dictum, aliquando vero *sub conditione.* Unde et Petrus sub conditione intellexit: si non caveris tibi; et ideo non desperavit[^p435-7].

## English
<!-- page 433 -->

44. *When he therefore was gone out.* After the perversity of Judas in going astray has been shown, there is shown *the infirmity of the disciples* in following the Lord. And this is the fourth part of the chapter, and it does this[^p433-2] in this order. For first there is predicted the future *passion of Christ;* second, *the imperfection of the disciples;* third, *the erudition of the imperfect;* fourth, *the presumption of Peter is repressed.*

(Vers. 31.). There is predicted, then, first the *future passion of Christ* and through the passion *glorification,* which was hastening[^p433-3], the traitor hastening it. On which account he says: *When he therefore was gone out,* namely Judas, that he might accomplish what he had conceived; *Jesus said: Now is the Son of man glorified. Now* he says on account of the passion at hand, by whose merit he was to be exalted; to the Philippians, the second chapter[^p433-4]: « He humbled himself, for which cause God also hath exalted him ». *And God is glorified in him,* because through Christ he became known to all; below, in the seventeenth chapter: « Father, I have manifested thy name to the men whom thou hast given me out of the world ».

45. (Vers. 32.). *And*[^p433-5] *if God is glorified in him,* in the passion through much manifestation of virtue; because his death and passion were greatly to the glory of God, as is said of Peter below, in the last chapter[^p433-6]: « Signifying by what death he should glorify God ». *And God will glorify him in himself,* through the glory of the resurrection; *and immediately will he glorify him,* because his resurrection could not be deferred; whence in the Psalm: « Thou wilt not give thy Holy One to see corruption ». And he means to say that Christ was soon to be glorified by the Father through the resurrection, because to manifest the clarity of the Father he had to endure the passion. Whence the infirmity of Christ-man glorified God; and therefore the power of God raised up Christ dead and glorified him.

46. (Vers. 33.). *Little sons, yet a little while* etc. Here is touched the second, namely *the imperfection of the disciples in following Christ;* therefore he calls them *little sons,* that is, infirm in faith, because firm faith makes *sons,* but infirm faith *little sons.* — Or he calls them *little sons* out of tenderness of love, as Priscian says[^p433-7], that diminutives are sometimes said out of familiarity and love, as *Sergiolus.* Their imperfection, then, he manifests when he adds: *Yet a little while I am with you,* by bodily presence; *and*[^p433-8] *as I said to the Jews* — above, in the seventh chapter and the eighth chapter — *you shall seek me; whither I go, you cannot come; and I say to you now;* because you cannot bear the arduousness of the passion. They could not for this reason, that they had not received virtue from on high; therefore it is said to them in Luke, the last chapter[^p433-9]: « Stay you in the city, till you be endued with virtue from on high »; because without the strength of virtue it is not possible to stand in battle.

47. (Vers. 34.). *A new commandment I give unto you.* Here is touched the third, namely *the instruction of the imperfect disciples.* And because they were imperfect, the Lord gives them the remedy of dilection, that they may mutually love and sustain one another; on which account he says: *A new commandment*
<!-- page 434 -->
*I give unto you: That you love one another;* *new* for this reason, that it must always be fresh in the heart, because dilection is always owed and never grows old. Whence to the Romans, the thirteenth chapter[^p434-1]: « Owe no man any thing, but to love one another ». And the *mode* of loving is expressed: *As I have loved you, that you also love one another.* But he so loved as to love our salvation more than his own life; so let each one love the soul of his neighbour more than his own body; the first of John, the third chapter[^p434-2]: « In this we have known the charity of God, because he hath laid down his soul for us; and we ought to lay down our souls for the brethren ». And the *moving reason* for dilection is added:

48. (Vers. 35.). *By this shall all men know that you are my disciples, if you have dilection one for another;* because, as Augustine says[^p434-3], « dilection is the proper and special virtue of the pious and of the saints. It is this that distinguishes the sons of the kingdom from the sons of perdition »; to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter[^p434-4]: « Grieve not the Holy Spirit of God, whereby you are sealed unto the day of redemption ». Now this seal of the Spirit is dilection; to the Romans, the fifth chapter: « The charity of God is poured forth in our hearts by the Holy Spirit, who is given to us ».

49. (Vers. 36.). *Simon Peter saith to him.* Here is touched the fourth, namely *the repression of the presumption of Peter,* by which he offered himself to imitate the Lord. Therefore Peter asks him, that he may offer himself: *Lord, whither goest thou?* And because he wished to offer himself, the Lord shows him his impotence, lest he break out in words: *Jesus saith to him*[^p434-5]*: Whither I go, thou canst not follow me now,* on account of infirmity; *but thou shalt follow hereafter,* when confirmed by virtue. But Peter is not bent by the admonition; therefore he adds:

50. (Vers. 37.). *Peter answered him: Why cannot I follow*[^p434-6]*? I will lay down my soul for thee;* and this is perfect following; below, in the fifteenth chapter: « Greater charity no man hath, that a man lay down his soul for his friends ». If then I can do this, I can well follow. And because Peter does not desist from presumption through the admonition of his frailty, now he is repressed through the prediction of his future prevarication. On which account the Lord takes up again the word of Peter, that he may reprove him:

51. (Vers. 38.). *Jesus answered him: Wilt thou lay down thy soul for me?* Augustine[^p434-7]: « Canst thou go before, who canst not follow? What dost thou believe thyself to be? Hear what thou art ». *Amen, amen I say to thee, the cock shall not crow, till thou deny me thrice.* Augustine: « Thou who promisest me thy death, thou wilt thrice deny thy life. For as great a life as it is to confess Christ, so great a death it is to deny Christ. By fearing the death of thy flesh, thou wilt give death to thy soul ».

### Questions.

52. Quaest. I. It is asked about what he says first: *Now is the Son of man glorified;* because in the passion and the betrayal of Judas he is rather said to be *lessened* than *glorified.*

Augustine responds[^p434-8] that this is said in *sign,* because at the going out of Judas only the good remained; and then was signified the future clarity, in which God will be with the good only. — Otherwise it can be said that he says this according to *merit;* because in the passion he merited to be glorified and, as far as in him lay, he glorified the Lord[^p434-9].

53. Quaest. II. But it is asked about what he says: *Whither I go, you cannot come.*

**On the contrary:** 1. Above, in the twelfth[^p434-10], it was said: *He that ministereth to me, let him follow me:* therefore he was exhorting them to the impossible.

2. Likewise, Peter says in the first Canonical, the second chapter[^p434-11]: *Christ suffered for us, leaving you an example, that you should follow his steps:* therefore the example was given to all. How then does he say: *You cannot come?*

It must be said to this that just as *being able to believe* is said in two ways: in one way, as it says *possibility,* and thus it is of nature; in another way, as it says *active power,* and thus of grace; so also *being able to imitate the passion;* because an *imperfect being able* is in us, but *complete power* through the virtue of the Holy Spirit, with which the Apostles were not yet endued[^p434-12].

<!-- page 435 -->
But *on the contrary* this is, because with the least charity one can resist any temptation however great: therefore if the Apostles had the least, then they were able.

It is responded to this according to a twofold opinion. Some say that one can *from the least,* yet not *with the least remaining,* because it is increased; and according to these the solution is as before, that they were not able through that which they had. — According to the other opinion I respond: according to the least one *can,* but not *easily,* rather *with great difficulty;* and therefore few resist[^p435-1].

54. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *A new commandment I give unto you* etc.

1. Why did he not give them a precept about the dilection of God, which *is the first and highest commandment*[^p435-2]?

2. Likewise, how does he say *new,* since it was given from the beginning? Whence the first of John, the second chapter[^p435-3], speaking of this commandment, says: *I write not a new commandment to you, but an old commandment.*

**I respond:** It must be said to the first that in the dilection of neighbour the dilection of God is understood[^p435-4].

To the second it must be said that although that commandment was given in the old testament, yet in the new it was renewed as to mode: because there it is to love friends, here enemies; there to do good to friends, here it is commanded to do good to enemies[^p435-5]; this is what he implies in adding: *As I have loved you.*

55. Quaest. IV. Likewise it is asked about what the Lord said to Peter: *Thou wilt deny me thrice*[^p435-6]. — From this it seems that he revealed to Peter his fall, and that he compelled Peter to despair.

**I respond:** It must be said that to *predict* and to *reveal* differ. God reveals to no one, but yet he does well *predict;* because what *is revealed* is known with certitude, but what *is predicted,* not; because sometimes it is believed to be said *by way of threat,* sometimes *under condition.* Whence Peter also understood under condition: if thou dost not take care for thyself; and therefore he did not despair[^p435-7].
