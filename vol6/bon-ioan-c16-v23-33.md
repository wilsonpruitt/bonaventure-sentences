# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XVI, Vers. 23-33
## *Tertio consolatur discipulos ex petitionum suarum exauditione*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 464 -->

#### Tertio consolatur discipulos ex petitionum suarum exauditione.

37. *Amen, amen dico vobis.* Haec est tertia pars capituli, in qua Dominus discipulos consolatur ex *petitionum suarum exauditione*[^p464-2]; et procedit in hac parte per hunc modum. Primo notatur Domini *liberalis promissio;* secundo vero, *discipulorum consolatio,* ibi[^p464-3]: *Dicunt ei discipuli eius* etc.

Primum igitur describitur sic. Primo notatur *Domini liberalitas* ad promittendum; secundo, *temporis opportunitas* ad petendum; tertio, *voluntatis promptitudo* ad exaudiendum.

(Vers. 23.). Primo ergo fit sive notatur *Domini liberalitas ad promittendum* sub certitudine; propter quod dicit: *Amen, amen dico vobis,* signum est certitudinis; *si quid petieritis Patrem in nomine meo, dabit vobis. Si quid,* id est, si aliquid; illud *aliquid* est vita aeterna; unde Augustinus[^p464-4]: « Quidquid aliud petitur, nihil petitur, non quia res nulla omnino sit, sed quia in tantae rei comparatione, quidquid aliud concupiscitur, nihil est »; ideo Iacobi primo: « Si quis vestrum indiget sapientia, postulet a Deo, qui dat omnibus affluenter, et dabitur ei ».

38. (Vers. 24.). *Usque modo non petistis.* Hic tangitur secundum, scilicet *aptitudo temporis ad petendum,* quae hactenus non fuerat, sed deinceps erat futura; propterea dicit: *Usque modo non petistis quidquam in nomine meo,* quia non erat tempus opportunum; quia Dominus ipse erat cum eis, qui omnia dabat; unde infra decimo septimo[^p464-5]: « Cum essem cum eis, ego servabam eos in nomine tuo ». Sed amodo venit tempus; ideo dicit: *Petite, et accipietis, ut gaudium vestrum sit plenum,* quia omnis vestra petitio ad illud gaudium referri debet. Hoc *gaudium plenum* consistit in dotibus animae et corporis; de quo Isaiae ultimo[^p464-6]: « Videbitis, et gaudebit cor vestrum, et ossa vestra quasi herba germinabunt ». Et quod tempus aptum ad petendum adveniat, patet, quia usque modo nescierunt nec cognoverunt, a quo deberent petere; sed amodo cognoscent; ideo dicit:

39. (Vers. 25.). *Haec in proverbiis locutus sum vobis;* ideo in similitudinibus, quia, primae ad Corinthios tertio[^p464-7], « non potui vobis loqui quasi spiritualibus, sed quasi carnalibus ». Sed *venit hora, cum iam non in proverbiis loquar vobis,* occulte scilicet, *sed palam de Patre meo*[^p464-8] *annuntiabo vobis.* Hora ista erat tempus missionis Spiritus sancti.

40. (Vers. 26.). *In illo die,* hoc est tempore, *petetis in nomine meo,* quia tunc me et Patrem cognoscetis; unde Ieremiae trigesimo primo[^p464-9]: « Non dicet vir fratri suo: Cognosce Dominum; omnes enim cognoscent me a minimo usque ad maximum ». Et hoc fuit in tempore post acceptionem Spiritus sancti, qui non tantum docebat, sed etiam petere compellebat; ad Romanos octavo: « Spiritus postulat pro nobis gemitibus inenarrabilibus », id est, postulare facit.

*Et non dico vobis.* Notatur hic tertium, scilicet *ad exaudiendum promptitudo,* quia Pater paratus erat exaudire Apostolos rogantes sine aliquo intercessore medio; propterea dicit: *Et non dico vobis, quia ego rogabo Patrem de vobis,* quia non oportet[^p464-10].

41. (Vers. 27.). *Ipse enim Pater amat vos,* et ideo libenter exaudit; et ratio huius est: *Quia vos me amastis;* supra decimo quarto[^p464-11]: « Qui diligit me diligetur a Patre meo »; non solum amor erat in causa, immo etiam fides; *et credidistis, quia a Deo exivi.* Et merito fidei merentur exaudiri, quia
<!-- page 465 -->
dicitur Matthaei vigesimo primo: « Omnia, quae petieritis in oratione credentes, accipietis ». Quia ergo credidistis, meremini exaudiri. Sed usque tunc suffecerat credere, quod *exiisset* a Deo; deinceps oportebat credere, quod *rediret* ad Deum. Unde ad fidem eorum perficiendam subdit:

42. (Vers. 28.). *Exivi a Patre et veni in mundum: exivi,* per aeternam generationem, *veni,* per incarnationem, et hoc credidistis; unde supra undecimo[^p465-1]: « Tu es Christus, Filius Dei vivi, qui in hunc mundum venisti ». *Iterum relinquo mundum,* per passionem, *et vado ad Patrem,* per gloriosam resurrectionem. Unde cum resurrexit, misit discipulis suis dicere per Mariam, infra vigesimo[^p465-2]: « Dic fratribus meis: Ascendo ad Patrem meum et Patrem vestrum, Deum meum et Deum vestrum ». De his duobus dicitur in Psalmo: « A summo caelo egressio eius, et occursus eius usque ad summum eius ».

43. *Dicunt ei discipuli eius.* Postquam posita est Christi promissio, ponitur *discipulorum consolatio*[^p465-3], quae describitur hoc ordine. Primo notatur *consolatio;* deinde, quia decipiebantur, ponitur *instructio;* tertio, quia vacillabant, ponitur *confirmatio.*

(Vers. 29.). Primo igitur insinuatur *discipulorum consolatio,* quam habebant de verbis Domini, quae iam intellexerant, et iam tempus consolationis promissum adesse credebant. Propter hoc *dicunt ei discipuli eius: Ecce, nunc palam loqueris et proverbium nullum dicis;* et ita venit tempus, de quo dicis[^p465-4]: « Venit hora, cum iam non in proverbiis loquar vobis, sed palam annuntiabo ».

44. (Vers. 30.). *Nunc scimus, quia scis omnia, et non opus est tibi, ut quis te interroget.* Et ita venit hora, de qua dixisti supra[^p465-5]: « In illo die me non rogabitis quidquam ». *In hoc credimus, quia a Deo existi,* quia scilicet omnia nosti; supra sexto: « Nos credidimus et cognovimus, quia tu es Christus, Filius Dei » etc. Solius enim Dei est scire occulta; Ieremiae decimo septimo: « Pravum est cor hominis et inscrutabile, et quis cognoscet illud? Ego Dominus scrutans corda et probans renes ».

45. (Vers. 31.). *Respondit Iesus.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *discipulorum eruditio.* Quia enim, ex sermone Domini confortati, credebant se consolatos et in fide perfectos; docet eos Dominus humilia sapere, sicut supra fecit Petro; ideo resumit verbum eorum, ut corrigat. Propterea dicit: *Respondit eis Iesus: Modo creditis*[^p465-6], id est, iactatis vos de hoc, quod creditis.

46. (Vers. 32.). *Ecce, venit hora et iam venit,* id est, propinquat; *ut dispergamini unusquisque in propria,* id est, ut *dispergamini,* ita quod unusquisque *in propria,* quia unus cum altero non remansit nec fide nec corpore; Zachariae decimo tertio[^p465-7]: « Percute pastorem, et dispergentur oves gregis ». *Et me solum relinquatis;* Isaiae sexagesimo tertio: « Torcular calcavi solus, et de gentibus non est vir mecum ». Sed haec solitudo non erat per defectum divini auxilii, sed humani; ideo dicit: *Sed non*[^p465-8] *sum solus, quia Pater mecum est;* supra octavo: « Qui me misit mecum est et non reliquit me solum ». Nec est contrarium quod dicitur in Psalmo: « Ut quid me dereliquisti »? quia istud intelligitur de expositione ad poenam.

47. (Vers. 33.). *Haec locutus sum vobis.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *discipulorum confirmatio.* Quia praedixerat dispersionem futuram, subiungit confortationem, quia hoc non dixerat, ut desperarent, sed ut per confidentiam pacem haberent. Propterea dicit: *Haec locutus sum vobis, ut in me pacem habeatis;* de qua Isaiae ultimo[^p465-9]: « Declinabo super eam quasi flumen pacis ». Ad hanc pacem non venitur nisi per pressuras; ideo dicit: *In mundo pressuram habebitis;* Actuum decimo quarto: « Per multas tribulationes oportet nos intrare in regnum Dei ». In his pressuris non est desperandum, ideo subdit: *Sed confidite, ego vici mundum;* primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p465-10]: « Gratias Deo, qui dedit nobis victoriam per Iesum Christum Dominum nostrum ». Hanc victoriam adipiscuntur credentes in Christum; primae Ioannis quinto: « Haec est victoria, quae vincit mundum, fides nostra ».

### Quaestiones.

48. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod dicit: *Si quid petieritis Patrem in nomine meo, dabit vobis.*

1. Quaeritur: cum illud idem dixerit supra decimo quarto[^p465-11] et decimo quinto, ut quid nunc iterat decimo sexto? — Videtur *superfluum:* ergo si in verbis Christi nihil fuit superfluum, quare toties dicit?
<!-- page 466 -->

2. Item quaeritur, quia hoc videtur *falsum;* quia Paulus petiit stimulum amoveri carnis a se, et tamen non obtinuit, secundae ad Corinthios duodecimo[^p466-1]. — Item, tota die videmus, quod Sancti petunt, et tamen non exaudiuntur. Quid est quod dicit: *Si quid* etc.?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod ad hoc, quod aliqua oratio sit digna exauditione, requiritur, quod orans sit *dignus,* sit *assiduus;* quod petat quod sit *ad salutem;* quod petat *pro se*[^p466-2]. His conditionibus concurrentibus, intelligitur Christi promissio; et tunc non fallit, quin exaudiatur. — Ad hoc autem, quod sit *dignus,* necesse est, quod habeat *fidem, spem* et *caritatem;* ideo ter ista promissio facta est. Primo merito *fidei,* secundo *caritatis* et hic iam tertio *spei*[^p466-3]. — Ad hoc, quod sit *assiduus,* necesse est, quod pluries petat; ideo multoties iteravit: *Si quid petieritis,* ut ostenderet, multoties idem esse petendum. — Necesse est, quod petitio sua sit *salutaris;* ideo saepe dicit, quod *petieritis in nomine meo;* Augustinus[^p466-4]: Hoc est in re nominis, quia Iesus *salus. Petere in nomine Iesu,* hoc est petere quod est ad salutem; et ideo Paulus non fuit exauditus[^p466-5]. — Quarto, *pro se,* ideo dicit: *Dabit vobis,* non dicit: dabit aliis, sed vobis[^p466-6].

49. Quaest. II. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Haec in proverbiis locutus sum vobis.*

1. Quia dicitur Matthaei decimo tertio[^p466-7]: *Vobis datum est nosse mysterium regni Dei, ceteris autem in parabolis:* ergo non debebat discipulis loqui in proverbiis.

2. Item, quomodo loquitur in proverbiis, quia statim sequitur[^p466-8]: *Ecce, palam loqueris et proverbium nullum dicis?* Ergo aut discipuli dixerunt falsum, aut Dominus.

3. Item, cum ipsi haberent modicum intellectum[^p466-9], quare in proverbiis et occulte eis loquebatur? — Videtur, quod vellet amittere tempus.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *proverbium* aliquando dicitur *dictio parabolica,* aliquando dicitur *obscura sententia*[^p466-10]. Dominus in *sermonibus parabolicis* loquebatur turbis, tamen nihilominus discipulis loquebatur in *obscuris sententiis;* turbis quidem in *parabolis,* ut videntes non viderent; Apostolis in *obscuris sententiis,* ut intelligentibus postmodum magis doctrina placeret, sicut dicit Augustinus in libro de Doctrina christiana[^p466-11].

2. Quod ergo dixerunt discipuli, quod palam loquebatur, dixerunt tanquam non intelligentes, quia credebant, se ipsum intelligere, et non intelligebant[^p466-12].

3. Quod obiicit: quare dicebat discipulis, ex quo non intelligebant? respondeo, quod tunc aliquo modo cognoscebant, sed postmodum per adventum Spiritus sancti, qui verba Christi postillavit, clare
<!-- page 467 -->
viderunt[^p467-1]; sicut quis magis diligit aliquod occultum, quando intelligit, quam si omnino esset apertum.

50. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod discipuli dicunt: *Nunc scimus, quia scis omnia.*

1. Per quid sciebant discipuli, quod Dominus sciret omnia?

2. Item, quid est quod sequitur, quod *non opus est, ut quisquam te interroget?* Immo totum contrarium est, quia, si scit omnia, tunc est bonus doctor, et a tali debet unusquisque quaerere.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod ideo sciebant, quod ipse sciret omnia, quia non erat opus, ut quisquam eum interrogaret *exprimendo suam dubitationem;* ipse enim sciebat, de quo homo dubitaret; et hoc quidem non poterat, nisi esset scrutans corda ut Deus[^p467-2], et ideo omnia sciebat. — Et quod non esset opus, ut quis ei aperiret suam dubitationem; sciebant per hoc, quod ipsi prius dubitaverant de hoc quod dixerat[^p467-3]: *Modicum;* et ipse non exspectavit interrogationem, sed praecognovit, *quia volebant eum interrogare,* et satisfecit. Ideo modo dicunt: *Nunc scimus* etc.

#### Third he consoles the disciples from the hearing of their petitions.

37. *Amen, amen, I say to you.* This is the third part of the chapter, in which the Lord consoles the disciples from the *hearing of their petitions*[^p464-2]; and he proceeds in this part by this mode. First there is noted the Lord's *liberal promise;* second, the *consolation of the disciples,* there[^p464-3]: *His disciples say to him* etc.

The first, then, is described thus. First there is noted the *Lord's liberality* for promising; second, the *opportuneness of the time* for asking; third, the *promptness of the will* for hearing.

(Vers. 23.). First, then, there is done or noted the *Lord's liberality for promising* under certitude; on account of which he says: *Amen, amen, I say to you,* this is a sign of certitude; *if you ask the Father any thing in my name, he will give it you. If any thing,* that is, if something; that *something* is eternal life; whence Augustine[^p464-4]: « Whatever else is asked, nothing is asked, not because the thing is nothing at all, but because in comparison with so great a thing, whatever else is coveted is nothing »; therefore James, the first chapter: « If any of you want wisdom, let him ask of God who giveth to all men abundantly, and it shall be given him ».

38. (Vers. 24.). *Hitherto, you have not asked.* Here the second is touched, namely, the *aptness of the time for asking,* which had not been up to now, but was to be thereafter; on this account he says: *Hitherto, you have not asked any thing in my name,* because the time was not opportune; because the Lord himself was with them, who gave all things; whence below, the seventeenth chapter[^p464-5]: « While I was with them, I kept them in thy name ». But from now on the time comes; therefore he says: *Ask, and you shall receive; that your joy may be full,* because every petition of yours ought to be referred to that joy. This *full joy* consists in the dowries of the soul and of the body; of which Isaias, the last chapter[^p464-6]: « You shall see and your heart shall rejoice, and your bones shall flourish like an herb ». And that a time apt for asking is coming is plain, because hitherto they did not know nor had they come to know from whom they ought to ask; but from now on they will know; therefore he says:

39. (Vers. 25.). *These things I have spoken to you in proverbs;* therefore in similitudes, because, the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p464-7], « I could not speak to you as unto spiritual, but as unto carnal ». But *the hour cometh when I will no longer speak to you in proverbs,* hiddenly, namely, *but will shew you plainly of my Father*[^p464-8]. That hour was the time of the mission of the Holy Spirit.

40. (Vers. 26.). *In that day,* that is, time, *you shall ask in my name,* because then you will know me and the Father; whence Jeremias, the thirty-first chapter[^p464-9]: « A man shall not say to his brother: Know the Lord; for all shall know me from the least even to the greatest ». And this was in the time after the receiving of the Holy Spirit, who not only taught, but also compelled them to ask; to the Romans, the eighth chapter: « The Spirit asketh for us with unspeakable groanings », that is, makes them ask.

*And I say not to you.* Here the third is noted, namely, the *promptness for hearing,* because the Father was ready to hear the Apostles asking without any intermediary intercessor; on this account he says: *And I say not to you that I will ask the Father for you,* because it is not needful[^p464-10].

41. (Vers. 27.). *For the Father himself loveth you,* and therefore he willingly hears; and the reason of this is: *Because you have loved me;* above, the fourteenth chapter[^p464-11]: « He that loveth me shall be loved of my Father »; not only was love in the cause, nay, faith also; *and have believed that I came out from God.* And by the merit of faith they merit to be heard, because
<!-- page 465 -->
it is said in Matthew, the twenty-first chapter: « All things whatsoever you shall ask in prayer believing, you shall receive ». Because, then, you have believed, you merit to be heard. But up to then it had sufficed to believe that he *had come out* from God; from then on it was needful to believe that he *was returning* to God. Whence, to perfect their faith, he adds:

42. (Vers. 28.). *I came forth from the Father and am come into the world: I came forth,* through the eternal generation, *am come,* through the incarnation, and this you have believed; whence above, the eleventh chapter[^p465-1]: « Thou art Christ, the Son of the living God, who art come into this world ». *Again I leave the world,* through the passion, *and I go to the Father,* through the glorious resurrection. Whence when he had risen, he sent to say to his disciples through Mary, below, the twentieth chapter[^p465-2]: « Say to my brethren: I ascend to my Father and to your Father, to my God and to your God ». Of these two it is said in the Psalm: « His going out is from the end of heaven, and his circuit even to the end thereof ».

43. *His disciples say to him.* After the promise of Christ has been set down, there is set down the *consolation of the disciples*[^p465-3], which is described in this order. First there is noted the *consolation;* then, because they were deceived, there is set down the *instruction;* third, because they were wavering, there is set down the *confirmation.*

(Vers. 29.). First, then, the *consolation of the disciples* is intimated, which they had from the words of the Lord, which they had now understood, and they believed that the promised time of consolation was now present. On this account *his disciples say to him: Behold, now thou speakest plainly and speakest no proverb;* and thus the time has come of which thou sayest[^p465-4]: « The hour cometh when I will no longer speak to you in proverbs, but will shew plainly ».

44. (Vers. 30.). *Now we know that thou knowest all things and thou needest not that any man should question thee.* And thus the hour has come of which thou saidst above[^p465-5]: « In that day you shall not ask me any thing ». *By this we believe that thou camest forth from God,* because, namely, thou knowest all things; above, the sixth chapter: « We have believed and have known that thou art the Christ, the Son of God » etc. For it belongs to God alone to know hidden things; Jeremias, the seventeenth chapter: « The heart of man is perverse and unsearchable, and who shall know it? I the Lord search the hearts and prove the reins ».

45. (Vers. 31.). *Jesus answered.* Here the second is touched, namely, the *erudition of the disciples.* For because, comforted by the word of the Lord, they believed themselves consoled and perfect in faith, the Lord teaches them to savour humble things, as he did above with Peter; therefore he takes up again their word, that he may correct it. On this account he says: *Jesus answered them: Now you believe*[^p465-6], that is, you boast of this, that you believe.

46. (Vers. 32.). *Behold, the hour cometh, and it is now come,* that is, draws near; *that you shall be scattered every man to his own,* that is, that you shall be *scattered,* in such a way that every man *to his own,* because one did not remain with another either in faith or in body; Zacharias, the thirteenth chapter[^p465-7]: « Strike the shepherd, and the sheep of the flock shall be scattered ». *And shall leave me alone;* Isaias, the sixty-third chapter: « I have trodden the winepress alone, and of the Gentiles there is not a man with me ». But this solitude was not through a defect of divine help, but of human; therefore he says: *But I am not*[^p465-8] *alone, because the Father is with me;* above, the eighth chapter: « He that sent me is with me: and he hath not left me alone ». Nor is that contrary which is said in the Psalm: « Why hast thou forsaken me »? because this is understood of the exposure to punishment.

47. (Vers. 33.). *These things I have spoken to you.* Here the third is touched, namely, the *confirmation of the disciples.* Because he had foretold the future scattering, he adds comforting, because he had not said this that they should despair, but that through confidence they should have peace. On this account he says: *These things I have spoken to you, that in me you may have peace;* of which Isaias, the last chapter[^p465-9]: « I will bring upon her as it were a river of peace ». To this peace one does not come except through distresses; therefore he says: *In the world you shall have distress;* Acts, the fourteenth chapter: « Through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God ». In these distresses one must not despair, therefore he adds: *But have confidence. I have overcome the world;* the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter[^p465-10]: « Thanks be to God, who hath given us the victory through Jesus Christ our Lord ». This victory those believing in Christ obtain; First John, the fifth chapter: « This is the victory which overcometh the world: Our faith ».

### Questions.

48. Quaest. I. But it is asked here first concerning what he says: *If you ask the Father any thing in my name, he will give it you.*

1. It is asked: since he said that same thing above, in the fourteenth chapter[^p465-11] and in the fifteenth chapter, why does he now repeat it in the sixteenth? — It seems *superfluous:* therefore, if in the words of Christ nothing was superfluous, why does he say it so many times?
<!-- page 466 -->

2. Likewise it is asked, because this seems *false;* because Paul asked that the sting of the flesh be removed from him, and yet he did not obtain it, the second to the Corinthians, the twelfth chapter[^p466-1]. — Likewise, all day long we see that the Saints ask, and yet they are not heard. What is it that he says: *If any thing* etc.?

**I respond:** It must be said that for this, that any prayer be worthy of hearing, it is required that the one praying be *worthy,* be *assiduous;* that he ask what is *for salvation;* that he ask *for himself*[^p466-2]. These conditions concurring, the promise of Christ is understood; and then it does not fail that he is heard. — For this, however, that he be *worthy,* it is necessary that he have *faith, hope* and *charity;* therefore this promise was made three times. First by the merit of *faith,* second of *charity,* and here now third of *hope*[^p466-3]. — For this, that he be *assiduous,* it is necessary that he ask many times; therefore he repeated many times: *If you ask any thing,* to show that the same thing must be asked many times. — It is necessary that his petition be *salutary;* therefore he often says that *you ask in my name;* Augustine[^p466-4]: This is in the reality of the name, because Jesus is *salvation. To ask in the name of Jesus,* this is to ask what is for salvation; and therefore Paul was not heard[^p466-5]. — Fourth, *for himself,* therefore he says: *He will give it you,* he does not say: he will give it to others, but to you[^p466-6].

49. Quaest. II. It is asked concerning what he says: *These things I have spoken to you in proverbs.*

1. Because it is said in Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p466-7]: *To you it is given to know the mystery of the kingdom of God, but to the rest in parables:* therefore he ought not to have spoken to the disciples in proverbs.

2. Likewise, how does he speak in proverbs, since there follows immediately[^p466-8]: *Behold, thou speakest plainly and speakest no proverb?* Therefore either the disciples said what was false, or the Lord.

3. Likewise, since they had small understanding[^p466-9], why did he speak to them in proverbs and hiddenly? — It seems that he wished to lose time.

**I respond:** It must be said that a *proverb* is sometimes called a *parabolic saying,* and sometimes is called an *obscure sentence*[^p466-10]. The Lord spoke in *parabolic words* to the multitudes, but nevertheless he spoke to the disciples in *obscure sentences;* to the multitudes in *parables,* that seeing they might not see; to the Apostles in *obscure sentences,* that the *teaching*[^tr-doctrina] might please the more those understanding it afterwards, as Augustine says in the book *On Christian Doctrine*[^p466-11].

2. What the disciples said, then, that he was speaking plainly, they said as men not understanding, because they believed that they understood him, and they did not understand[^p466-12].

3. What he objects: why did he speak to the disciples, since they did not understand? I respond that then they knew in some way, but afterwards, through the coming of the Holy Spirit, who postillated the words of Christ, they clearly
<!-- page 467 -->
saw[^p467-1]; as someone loves some hidden thing more when he understands it than if it were entirely open.

50. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked concerning what the disciples say: *Now we know that thou knowest all things.*

1. By what did the disciples know that the Lord knew all things?

2. Likewise, what is it that follows, that *thou needest not that any man should question thee?* Nay, the whole contrary is so, because, if he knows all things, then he is a good doctor, and from such a one every man ought to inquire.

**I respond:** It must be said that they knew that he knew all things for this reason, that it was not needful that any man question him *by expressing his doubt;* for he knew of what a man was doubting; and this indeed he could not do, unless he were searching the hearts as God[^p467-2], and therefore he knew all things. — And that it was not needful that anyone open his doubt to him, they knew through this, that they themselves had earlier doubted of what he had said[^p467-3]: *A little while;* and he did not wait for the questioning, but foreknew *that they had a mind to question him,* and gave satisfaction. Therefore now they say: *Now we know* etc.
