### Capitulum IV. *Ut autem cognovit.*

### Collatio XIV.

(Vers. 6.). *Iesus autem, fatigatus ex itinere, sedebat supra fontem.*

1. Notandum hic, quod Iesus *fatigatus* dat nobis *exemplum sustinendi* laborem; Iesus autem *sedens* dat nobis *documentum inveniendi quietem.* — Si igitur gravat labor, consideremus Christum laborantem; si delectat quies, sedeamus ad fontem, ad Christum scilicet, qui est fons quantum ad *Divinitatem* et quantum ad *humanitatem.* Quantum ad *Divinitatem* Christus est fons de *summo descendens;*
<!-- page 555 -->
Canticorum quarto[^p555-1]: « Fons hortorum, puteus aquarum viventium, quae fluunt impetu de Libano ». Quantum ad *humanitatem.* Christus est fons de *imo ascendens;* Genesis secundo: « Fons ascendebat de terra, irrigans universam superficiem terrae ».

2. Latius autem considerando fontis huius apertionem, inveniemus, a fonte Christo fontem derivari *sapientiae*, fontem *misericordiae*, fontem *gratiae*, fontem *spiritualis laetitiae*, fontem *vitae aeternae.* — Fons *sapientiae* purgat ignorantiam intellectus; de quo Ecclesiastici primo[^p555-2]: « Fons sapientiae verbum Dei in excelsis ». — Fons *misericordiae* culpam purgat, quae est in affectu; Zachariae decimo tertio: « Erit fons patens domui David et habitantibus Ierusalem in ablutionem peccatoris et menstruatae ». — Fons *gratiae* reficit virtutem quantum ad *activos;* de quo fonte Ieremiae secundo[^p555-3]: « Dereliquerunt me, fontem aquae vivae », id est vivificantis, « et foderunt sibi cisternas dissipatas ». — Fons *spiritualis laetitiae* delectat mentem quantum ad *contemplativos;* quem sitiebat Psalmista dicens: « Sitivit anima mea ad Deum, fontem vivum ». — Fons *vitae aeternae* beatificat animam; de quo Psalmus: « Apud te est fons vitae, et in lumine tuo videbimus lumen ». — Sic ergo habes quinque fontes, quos si diligenter consideras, duo-primi sunt contra malum, duo sequentes promovent in bono, quintus vero fons effundit praemium.

3. Notandum, quod diversitatem *fontium* sequitur diversitas *aquarum.* Habemus namque de fonte *sapientiae* aquas *instructionis;* Ecclesiastici decimo quinto[^p555-4]: « Cibavit illum pane vitae et intellectus, et aqua sapientiae salutaris potavit illum ». — De fonte *misericordiae*, aquas *remissionis*, de quibus Ezechielis trigesimo sexto: « Effundam super vos aquam mundam, et mundabimini ab omnibus inquinamentis vestris ». — De fonte *gratiae*, aquam *refectionis;* Isaiae duodecimo: « Haurietis aquas in gaudio de fontibus Salvatoris »; Psalmus: « Super aquam refectionis educavit me ». — De fonte *spiritualis laetitiae*, aquas *consolationis;* Proverbiorum vigesimo quinto[^p555-5]: « Aqua frigida animae sitienti, nuntius bonus de terra longinqua ». — De fonte *vitae aeternae*, aquas *aeternae felicitatis*, de quibus Psalmus: « De torrente voluptatis tuae potabis eos »; et Apocalypsis septimo: « Non esurient neque sitient amplius, nec cadet super illos sol neque ullus aestus; quoniam Agnus, qui in medio throni est, reget illos et deducet eos ad fontes vitae aquarum ». — Sic ergo habes in Salvatore quinque genera *fontium* et quinque genera *aquarum*, de quibus Isaiae duodecimo: « Haurietis aquas in gaudio de fontibus Salvatoris ».

4. Possunt autem adaptari isti quinque fontes quinque *vulneribus* Christi, quorum quatuor inflicta sunt Christo ante mortem, quintum vero post mortem. Sic et istorum fontium quatuor aperiuntur nobis in vita ista, quintum vero post in gloria[^p555-6].

### Annotatio 12.

(Vers. 8.). *Discipuli eius abierunt, ut cibos emerent.*

Notandum, quod secundum Scripturam multiplex distinguitur *cibus.* Est enim cibus *naturalis, legalis, carnalis, spiritualis, sacramentalis, aeternalis.* — Cibus *naturalis* ministratur nobis in mensa *naturae*, de quo Matthaei decimo[^p555-7]: « Dignus est operarius cibo suo ». — Cibus *legalis*, in mensa *synagogae*, quem interdicit Apostolus ad Romanos decimo quarto: « Si propter cibum tuum frater tuus contristatur, iam non secundum caritatem ambulas ». — Cibus *carnalis*, in mensa *concupiscentiae;* Proverbiorum trigesimo: « Per tria movetur terra », quorum unum est, cum « stultus saturatus fuerit cibo ». — Cibus *spiritualis*, in mensa *conscientiae*, de quo Ioannis sexto: « Operamini non cibum, qui perit, sed qui permanet in vitam aeternam ». — Cibus *sacramentalis*, in mensa *eucharistiae;* Ioannis sexto[^p555-8]: « Caro mea vere est cibus » etc. — Cibus *aeternalis*, in mensa *gloriae*, de quo Tobiae duodecimo: « Ego cibo invisibili et potu, qui ab hominibus videri non potest, utor ».

### Collatio XV.

(Vers. 32.). *Ego cibum habeo manducare, quem vos nescitis.*

1. Ut sciamus, quo vesci debeat homo iustus, notandum, quod triplex est cibus, qui nobis apponitur in praesenti: cibus *naturalis* — cibus *carnalis* — et cibus *artificialis.* Primus *sustentat naturam*, secundus *nutrit concupiscentiam*, tertius *confortat animam.*

2. Circa cibum *naturalem* nota, quod *condigne est promerendus;* Matthaei decimo[^p555-9]: « Dignus est
<!-- page 556 -->
operarius cibo suo »; quasi dicat: et non alius; secundae ad Thessalonicenses ultimo: « Si quis non vult operari, non manducet »; Genesis tertio: « In sudore vultus tui vesceris pane tuo ». — Item, *modeste est accipiendus;* Ezechielis quarto: « Cibus tuus, quo vesceris, erit in pondere viginti stateres in die; a tempore usque ad tempus comedes illud »; quasi dicat: comedes ad pondus et ad tempus; Ecclesiastici trigesimo septimo: « Noli esse avidus in omni epulatione, et non te effundas super omnem escam ». — Item, *liberaliter est distribuendus;* quocontra de avaro, Iob vigesimo[^p556-1]: « Non remansit de cibo eius, propterea nihil remanebit de bonis eius ». Exemplum de divite epulone, Lucae decimo sexto.

3. Circa cibum *carnalem* attende, quare fugiendus: quia comedentem reddit *egenum*, eo quod nimis est venalis; Threnorum primo[^p556-2]: « Omnis populus gemens et quaerens panem; dederunt pretiosa quaeque pro cibo ad refocillandam animam ». — Comedentem facit *immundum;* est enim pollutus; Levitici undecimo: « Omnis cibus, quem comedetis, si fusa fuerit super eum aqua, immundus erit »; *aqua*, id est concupiscentia. — Comedentem facit *debilem* et infirmum; est enim male nutritivus; Iob trigesimo[^p556-3]: « Radix iuniperorum cibus eorum »; Threnorum primo: « Sacerdotes vestri in urbe consumti sunt, quia quaesierunt cibum sibi, ut refocillarent animam suam »; Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto: « Quare appenditis argentum vestrum non in panibus, et laborem vestrum non in saturitate »? — *Depauperat* expellendo gratiam, *polluit* ingerendo culpam seu maculam, *infirmat* corrumpendo sive vulnerando naturam.

4. Circa *spiritualem* cibum attende, quare *appetendus:* quia *nobilis* est; Sapientiae decimo sexto[^p556-4]: « Angelorum esca nutrivisti populum tuum, et paratum panem de caelo praestitisti eis sine labore » etc.; et Psalmus: « Panem Angelorum manducavit homo; cibaria misit eis in abundantia ». — Quia *utilis;* bene enim reficit et confortat; tertii Regum decimo nono: « Cum surrexisset Elias, comedit et bibit et ambulavit in fortitudine cibi illius quadraginta diebus et quadraginta noctibus usque ad montem Dei Horeb ». — Quia *delectabilis;* Sapientiae decimo sexto[^p556-5]: « Paratum panem de caelo praestitisti eis sine labore, habentem omne delectamentum et omnis saporis suavitatem »; Psalmus: « Cibavit eos ex adipe frumenti et de petra melle saturavit eos ».

5. Nota autem tria, quae auferunt homini delectationes cibi spiritualis. Primum est *sensus defectio;* secundi Regum decimo nono[^p556-6] de Berzellai: « Nunquid vigent sensus mei ad discernendum suave vel amarum? aut delectare potest servum tuum potus et cibus? vel audire possum ultra vocem cantorum et cantatricum »? Psalmus: « Aruit cor meum, quia oblitus sum comedere panem meum ». — Secundum est *sensus infectio*, quod solet esse in infirmis, quorum aduritur palatum; Iob trigesimo tertio[^p556-7]: « Increpat Dominus per dolorem in lectulo et omnia ossa eius marcescere facit; abominabilis ei fit in vita sua panis, et cibus animae suae ante desiderabilis »; Psalmus: « Omnem escam abominata est anima eorum, et appropinquaverunt usque ad portas mortis ». — Tertium est *sensus repletio;* Numerorum vigesimo primo: « Cur eduxisti nos de Aegypto, ut moreremur in solitudine? Deest panis, non sunt aquae, nauseat anima nostra super cibo isto levissimo ». — Attende, quia sensus male *repletur* lascivia malae operationis, male *inficitur* per concupiscentiam malae affectionis, *deficit* autem propter inconsuetudinem devotionis; et sunt illi quasi *saturati*, quasi *adusti* illi, illi quasi *deficientes* et languidi.

### Collatio XVI.

(Cap. VI, 27.). *Operamini non cibum, qui perit, sed qui permanet in vitam aeternam*[^p556-8].

1. In hoc verbo innuitur nobis quaedam cibi distinctio. Est enim cibus, qui *perit*, et ille est *culpae* — et est cibus, qui *permanet in vitam aeternam*, et ille est *gratiae.*

Est autem cibus *culpae* multiplex: cibus *luxuriae;* secundi Regum decimo tertio[^p556-9]: « Veniat, obsecro, Thamar, soror mea, ut det mihi cibum et faciat mihi pulmentum, et comedam de manu eius »; nota de amore. — Cibus *gulae;* Numerorum undecimo: « Adhuc carnes erant in faucibus eorum, nec defecerat huiusmodi cibus, et ecce, furor Domini concitatus in populum, percussit eum plaga magna nimis. Vocatus est locus ille sepulcra concupiscentiae »; Psalmus: « Adhuc escae eorum erant in ore ipsorum, et ira Dei ascendit super eos ». — Cibus *superbiae;* Danielis primo[^p556-10]: « Tenta nos, obsecro, decem diebus, et dentur nobis legumina ad vescendum et aqua ad bibendum, et contemplare vultus nostros et vultus puerorum, qui vescuntur cibo
<!-- page 557 -->
regio; et sicut videris, sic facies cum servis tuis »; Osee duodecimo: « Ephraim pascit ventum et sequitur aestum ». — Cibus *invidiae;* Proverbiorum vigesimo tertio: « Ne comedas cum homine invido, et ne desideres cibos eius, quoniam in similitudine arioli et coniectoris aestimat quod ignorat ». — Cibus *irae*, quem prohibet Dominus in figura Levitici septimo[^p557-1]: « Sanguinem omnis animalis non sumetis in cibo, tam de avibus quam de pecoribus ». — Cibus *avaritiae;* Proverbiorum vigesimo tertio: « Ne desideres de cibis eius », id est mundi, « in quo est panis mendacii »; Ecclesiastici decimo tertio: « Confundet te in cibis suis », id est mundus in divitiis, « donec te exinaniat bis et ter, et in novissimo deridebit te, et postea videns derelinquet te et caput suum movebit ad te ». Expone totum de *mundo*, cuius cibi divitiae sunt, quas propinat avaris ad credentiam[^p557-2]. — Cibus *accidiae;* Iudicum decimo nono: « Mane facto parabat Levites iter; cui rursus socer: Oro te, inquit, ut paululum cibi capias, et assumtis viribus, donec increscat dies, postea proficiscaris ». — Nota, quod tantum fecit eum morari, quod venit ad malum hospitium. Hic cibus est cavendus.

2. Cibus *gratiae* est similiter multiplex. — Cibus *gratiae*, hic reddit *vitam;* Psalmus[^p557-3]: « Flumen Dei repletum est aquis, parasti cibum illorum, quoniam ita est praeparatio eius. Rivos eius inebria, multiplica genimina eius ». — Cibus *sapientiae*, hic reddit *sensum;* Ecclesiastici decimo quinto: « Cibavit illum pane vitae et intellectus et aqua sapientiae salutaris potabit illum »; Matthaei vigesimo quarto: « Quis, putas, est fidelis servus et prudens, quem constituit Dominus super familiam suam, ut det illis cibum in tempore »? Proverbiorum decimo tertio: « Multi cibi in novalibus patrum, et aliis congregantur absque iudicio ». — Cibus *spiritualis laetitiae*, hic reddit *motum;* Ecclesiastici decimo quarto[^p557-4]: « Ante obitum tuum operare iustitiam, quoniam non est apud inferos invenire cibum »; Psalmus: « Qui simul mecum dulces capiebas cibos; in domo Dei ambulavimus cum consensu ». — Hic est cibus triplex *interior* in conscientia.

3. Sequitur cibus triplex *exterior* in vita: cibus *poenitentiae;* Psalmus[^p557-5]: « Cibabis nos pane lacrymarum et potum dabis nobis in lacrymis in mensura ». Hic est omnibus *medicinalis* sive sanativus cibus *aegrotantium.* — Cibus *iustitiae;* Ioannis quarto: « Meus cibus est, ut faciam voluntatem Patris mei, qui misit me, ut perficiam opus eius ». Hic est cibus *nutritivus et augmentativus* cibus *laborantium.* — Cibus *patientiae;* ad Hebraeos quinto: « *Perfectorum* est solidus cibus, eorum qui pro ipsa consuetudine exercitatos habent sensus ad discretionem boni et mali ». — Hic est triplex cibus exterior in vita.

4. Sequitur cibus *superior* in altari, et hic est septimus, scilicet cibus *eucharistiae;* Ioannis sexto[^p557-6]: « Caro mea vere est cibus, et sanguis meus vere est potus ». *Omnibus* huiusmodi cibus est quaerendus.

### Annotatio 13.

(Vers. 42.). *Hic est vere salvator mundi;* qui salvat a malo *culpae;* Matthaei primo[^p557-7]: « Ipse salvum faciet populum suum a peccatis eorum »; Lucae secundo: « Annuntio vobis gaudium magnum » etc.; « quia natus est vobis hodie salvator, qui est Christus Dominus ». — Qui salvat a malo *tentationis* sive pugnae; Psalmus: « Salvasti nos de affligentibus nos, et odientes nos confudisti ». — Qui salvat a malo *poenae* sive miseriae; ad Philippenses tertio: « Salvatorem exspectamus, qui reformabit corpus humilitatis nostrae, configuratum corpori claritatis suae ».

A malo *culpae* salvat *poenitentes;* Lucae septimo[^p557-8], exemplum de Magdalena, cui dictum est: « Fides tua te salvam fecit; vade in pace ». — A malo *pugnae, constantes;* primi Machabaeorum quarto: « Mementote, qualiter salvi facti sunt patres nostri; et nunc clamemus in caelum, et miserebitur nostri Deus noster ». — A malo *miseriae, perseverantes;* Matthaei decimo: « Qui perseveraverit usque in finem, hic salvus erit ». — Habe ergo *poenitentiam* de culpa, *constantiam* in pugna, *perseverantiam* in gratia.

### Annotatio 14.

(Vers. 18.). *Quinque viros habuisti, et hunc quem habes, non est tuus vir.*

1. *Mulier*, anima fidelis; *vir* eius legitimus, Christus; quoties ergo anima ad Deum convertitur, vero sponso coniungitur; quoties ad alium, fornicatur. — Mulieri isti improperantur *quinque viri* — et de *sexto* etiam reprehenditur — cum dicitur: *Quinque viros habuisti*, et spiritualiter possunt dici *quinque viri*, quibus anima abutitur, *quinque sensus.* — Primus igitur vir est *gustus*, cui serviunt *gulosi;* in quorum persona dicitur Ecclesiastae secundo[^p557-9]: « Melius est
<!-- page 558 -->
comedere et bibere et ostendere animae suae bona »; Isaiae vigesimo secundo: « Comedamus et bibamus, cras enim moriemur ». — Secundus est *tactus*, cui serviunt *luxuriosi;* in quorum persona dicitur Sapientiae secundo: « Nullum sit pratum, quod non pertranseat luxuria nostra. Nemo nostrum sit exsors luxuriae nostrae ». — Tertius est *odoratus*, cui serviunt *deliciosi;* in quorum persona dicitur Sapientiae secundo: « Unguentis nos impleamus, et non praetereat nos flos temporis; coronemus nos rosis » etc. — Quartus est *visus*, cui serviunt *avari;* de quibus Ecclesiastici decimo quarto[^p558-1]: « Insatiabilis oculus cupidi in parte iniquitatis; non satiabitur ». — Quintus est *auditus*, cui serviunt curiosi; tales erant Athenienses, Actuum decimo septimo: « Ad nihil aliud vacabant, nisi aut dicere aut audire aliquid novi »; et secundae ad Timotheum quarto: « Ad sua desideria coacervabunt sibi magistros prurientes auribus ».

Isti ergo sunt quinque viri, quos reprehenditur anima fidelis habuisse; qui etiam significantur per quinque iuga boum, de quibus Lucae decimo quarto[^p558-2] dicitur: « Iuga boum emi quinque ».

2. Adhuc arguitur Samaritana virum sextum, qui non est suus, habere, cum dicitur: *Et hunc quem habes, non est tuus vir;* et hic vir est *error.* Voluptates enim corporis sequitur error mentis, secundum quod dicitur in Psalmo[^p558-3]: « Nolite fieri sicut equus et mulus, quibus non est intellectus ». Virum istum monetur anima dimittere et ad sponsum proprium se transferre, cum dicitur ei a Christo: « Voca virum tuum », supra eodem.

### Collatio XVII.

(Vers. 46.). *Erat quidam regulus, cuius filius infirmabatur Capharnaum.*

1. Considerandum est in verbis istis, *quae sit* infirmitas ista — et *unde* contracta.

Infirmitas ista est infirmitas *culpae;* et dicitur *infirmitas culpae* propter tria, scilicet quia *deformat, depauperat, debilitat. Deformat* polluendo *conscientiam;* debilitat vulnerando *naturam;* depauperat expellendo *gratiam.*

2. Culpa igitur est infirmitas, quia *deformat;* primi Machabaeorum primo[^p558-4]: « Virgines et iuvenes infirmati sunt, et omnis speciositas mulierum immutata est »; Threnorum primo: « Egressus est a filia Sion omnis decor eius ». — Quia *depauperat;* Ecclesiastae quinto: « Sicut egressus est nudus de utero matris suae, sic revertetur »; et sequitur: « Mirabilis prorsus infirmitas, quomodo venit, sic revertetur »; Threnorum primo: « Manum suam misit hostis ad omnia desiderabilia eius ». — Quia *debilitat;* Threnorum primo: « Infirmata est virtus mea, dedit me Dominus in manu, de qua non potero surgere »; Psalmus: « Infirmata est in paupertate virtus mea ».

3. Videndum est praeterea, *a quo* infirmitas ista sit *contracta.* Notandum autem, quod aliquando infirmitas contrahitur a *loco*, aliquando a *cibo*, aliquando a *labore inordinato.* In loco nota malam *occasionem;* in cibo, malam *delectationem;* in labore inordinato, malam *operationem*[^p558-5]. — Contrahitur igitur infirmitas a *loco;* unde cum describitur infirmus in praesenti Evangelio, notabiliter subiungitur *locus*, cum dicitur: *Infirmabatur Capharnaum*, quod interpretatur ager pinguedinis, vel villa consolationis[^p558-6]. In his enim solet contrahi infirmitas spiritualis. Iterum, Osee quarto: « Maledictum et mendacium et furtum et homicidium inundaverunt, et sanguis sanguinem tetigit; propter hoc lugebit terra, et infirmabitur omnis qui habitat in ea ». — Contrahitur etiam infirmitas a *cibo;* Ecclesiastici trigesimo septimo: « In multis escis erit infirmitas, et aviditas appropinquat usque ad choleram ». — Contrahitur etiam a *labore inordinato;* Isaiae quadragesimo: « Deficiunt pueri et laborabunt, et iuvenes in infirmitate cadunt »; Psalmus: « Humiliatum est in laboribus cor eorum, infirmati sunt, nec fuit qui adiuvaret ».

### Collatio XVIII.

(Vers. 47.). *Incipiebat enim mori.*

1. Non solum describit Evangelium aegroti *infirmitatem*, sed infirmitatis *gravitatem* in hoc, quod appropinquabat aegrotus ad mortem; quod notat, cum dicit: *Incipiebat enim mori.* Ubi notandum est, quod mortis spiritualis quadruplex signum est, scilicet amissio *motus*, amissio *gustus*, amissio *loquelae* et amissio *sensus.* — Tunc enim incipit homo spiritualiter mori, quando amittit *motum bonae operationis;* unde Iacobi secundo[^p558-7]: « Fides sine operibus mortua est ». — Quando amittit *gustum devotionis;* Psalmus: « Omnem escam abominata est anima eorum, et appropinquaverunt usque ad portas mortis ». — Item, quando amittit *loquelam confessionis;* Psalmus: « Non est in morte qui memor sit tui; in inferno autem quis confitebitur tibi »? — Item, quando amittit *sensum discretionis;* propter quod clamavit David: « Illumina oculos meos, ne unquam obdormiam in morte »; et quarti Regum quarto dicitur de filio mulieris, ad quem venerat Giezi, quod « non erat vox neque sensus », ut significaretur esse mortuus.

2. Contra, nota, quod *vitae spiritualis* signa quatuor sunt, scilicet recuperatio *sensus discretionis;*
<!-- page 559 -->
unde quarti Regum quarto[^p559-1] de puero, quem suscitavit Eliseus, quia « oscitavit septies aperuitque oculos ». — Item, recuperatio *loquelae confessionis;* unde Lucae septimo de adolescente, quem suscitavit Iesus, dicitur, quod « resedit qui erat mortuus et coepit loqui ». — Item, recuperatio *gustus devotionis;* unde Lucae octavo, postquam resuscitavit Iesus filiam archisynagogi, « iussit illi dari manducare ». — Item, recuperatio *bonae operationis;* unde Ioannis undecimo, suscitato Lazaro, « dixit Iesus: Solvite eum et sinite eum abire ».

### Collatio XIX.

(Vers. 49.). *Domine, descende, priusquam moriatur filius meus.*

1. Duo tanguntur in verbis istis: imploratur *pietas medici*, cum dicitur: *Domine, descende* — et ostenditur *necessitas infirmi*, cum dicitur: *priusquam moriatur filius meus.*

*Necessitas* ergo *infirmi* est imminentia mortis; ubi notandum est, quod quidam moriuntur *egestate*, quidam *infirmitate*, quidam *adversitate.* — *Egestate* moriebatur qui clamabat in Psalmo[^p559-2]: « Aruit tanquam testa virtus mea, et lingua mea adhaesit faucibus meis, et in pulverem mortis deduxisti me ». — *Infirmitate* moriebatur iste filius reguli, qui infirmabatur Capharnaum; de quo prius dicitur: « Incipiebat enim mori ». — *Adversitate* moriebantur, de quibus dicitur Threnorum primo: « Foris interficit gladius, et domi mors similis est ». — In *egestate* intellige indurationem mentis; in *infirmitate*, corruptionem carnis; in *adversitate*, impugnationem hostis.

2. Propter hanc triplicem necessitatem infirmi imploratur *descensus medici;* ubi nota, quod Dominus descendit ad *reficiendum*, ne moriatur *egestate;* ad *curandum*, ne moriatur *infirmitate;* ad *liberandum*, ne moriatur *adversitate.*

Dicit ergo regulus: *Descende, Domine,* ne *moriatur filius meus* per *egestatem indevotionis;* de quo descensu in Psalmo[^p559-3]: « Descendit sicut pluvia in vellus et sicut stillicidia stillantia super terram ». — Item, *descende, Domine,* ne *moriatur filius meus* per *infirmitatem carnalis corruptionis;* de quo descensu Matthaei octavo: « Ait Iesus: Ego veniam et curabo eum », loquitur de filio centurionis; et hic, Ioannis quarto, rogabat regulus Iesum, ut descenderet et sanaret filium eius. — Item, *descende, Domine,* ne *moriatur filius meus* per *impugnationem hostis;* de quo descensu Exodi tertio: « Vidi afflictionem populi mei, qui est in Aegypto, et descendi, ut liberem eum ». — Descende ergo, Domine, ad *reficiendam mentem*, ad *curandam carnem*, ad *repellendum hostem*, ne moriatur filius meus.

### Annotatio 15.

(Vers. 13.). *Omnis, qui bibit ex aqua hac, sitiet iterum.*

Notandum, quod est sitis multiplex. Est autem sitis *naturae;* Ioannis quarto[^p559-4]: *Omnis, qui bibit ex aqua hac, sitiet iterum.* — Est sitis *culpae;* Ecclesiastici vigesimo sexto: « Sicut viator sitiens ad fontem os aperiet et ab omni aqua proxima bibet et contra omnem palum sedebit ». — Est sitis *poenae;* Isaiae sexagesimo quinto: « Servi mei comedent, et vos esurietis; servi mei bibent, et vos sitietis ». — Est sitis *gratiae;* Psalmus: « Sitivit anima mea ad Deum fortem, vivum. — Est sitis *gloriae;* Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto: « Qui edunt me adhuc esurient, et qui bibunt me adhuc sitient ».

### Annotatio 16.

(Vers. 36.). *Et qui metit mercedem accipit.*

Nota, quod merces ista est merces *magna*, merces *multa* et merces *aeterna.* — Merces *magna;* Genesis decimo quinto[^p559-5]: « Noli timere, Abraham, ego protector tuus sum, et merces tua magna nimis. — Merces *multa;* Lucae sexto: « Gaudete et exsultate, quoniam merces vestra multa est in caelo ». — Merces *aeterna;* Ioannis quarto: *Qui metit mercedem accipit et congregat fructum in vitam aeternam.*

### Annotatio 17.

(Vers. 41.). *Et multo plures crediderunt propter sermonem eius.*

Nota, quod quidam crediderunt propter *verba veritatis*, ut isti, de quibus hic dicitur: *Multo plures crediderunt* etc. — Alii, propter *signa potestatis;* supra secundo[^p559-6]: « Multi crediderunt in nomine eius, videntes signa, quae faciebat ». — Alii, propter *exemplum sanctitatis;* Ioannis decimo: « Si non vultis mihi credere, operibus credite » etc.

### Chapter IV. *Now when he understood.*

### Collation XIV.

(Verse 6.). *But Jesus, being wearied with his journey, sat on the well.*

1. It is to be noted here that Jesus *wearied* gives us *an example of sustaining* labour; but Jesus *sitting* gives us *a lesson in finding rest.* — If therefore labour weighs upon us, let us consider Christ labouring; if rest delights us, let us sit at the well, that is, at Christ, who is a well as to *the Godhead* and as to *the humanity.* As to *the Godhead* Christ is a well *descending from on high;*
<!-- page 555 -->
the Canticles, the fourth chapter[^p555-1]: « The fountain of gardens, the well of living waters, which run with a strong stream from Libanus ». As to *the humanity.* Christ is a well *ascending from below;* Genesis, the second chapter: « A spring rose out of the earth, watering all the surface of the earth ».

2. But considering more broadly the opening of this well, we shall find that from the well that is Christ is derived a fountain of *wisdom*, a fountain of *mercy*, a fountain of *grace*, a fountain of *spiritual gladness*, a fountain of *eternal life.* — The fountain of *wisdom* purges the ignorance of the understanding; of which Ecclesiasticus, the first chapter[^p555-2]: « The word of God on high is the fountain of wisdom ». — The fountain of *mercy* purges fault, which is in the affection; Zacharias, the thirteenth chapter: « There shall be a fountain open to the house of David, and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem, for the washing of the sinner and of the unclean woman ». — The fountain of *grace* refreshes virtue as to *the active;* of which fountain Jeremias, the second chapter[^p555-3]: « They have forsaken me, the fountain of living water », that is, of vivifying water, « and have digged to themselves cisterns, broken cisterns ». — The fountain of *spiritual gladness* delights the mind as to *the contemplative;* which the Psalmist thirsted for, saying: « My soul hath thirsted after God, the living fountain ». — The fountain of *eternal life* beatifies the soul; of which the Psalm: « For with thee is the fountain of life; and in thy light we shall see light[^tr-lumen] ». — So therefore you have five fountains, of which, if you consider them diligently, the first two are against evil, the two following promote in good, and the fifth fountain pours forth the reward.

3. It is to be noted that the diversity of *the fountains* is followed by a diversity of *the waters.* For we have from the fountain of *wisdom* waters of *instruction;* Ecclesiasticus, the fifteenth chapter[^p555-4]: « With the bread of life and understanding she shall feed him, and give him the water of wholesome wisdom to drink ». — From the fountain of *mercy*, waters of *remission*, of which Ezechiel, the thirty-sixth chapter: « I will pour upon you clean water, and you shall be cleansed from all your filthiness ». — From the fountain of *grace*, the water of *refection;* Isaias, the twelfth chapter: « You shall draw waters with joy out of the saviour's fountains »; the Psalm: « He hath brought me up on the water of refreshment ». — From the fountain of *spiritual gladness*, waters of *consolation;* Proverbs, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p555-5]: « As cold water to a thirsty soul, so is good tidings from a far country ». — From the fountain of *eternal life*, waters of *eternal felicity*, of which the Psalm: « Thou shalt make them drink of the torrent of thy pleasure »; and the Apocalypse, the seventh chapter: « They shall no more hunger nor thirst, neither shall the sun fall on them, nor any heat; for the Lamb, which is in the midst of the throne, shall rule them, and shall lead them to the fountains of the waters of life ». — So therefore you have in the Saviour five kinds of *fountains* and five kinds of *waters*, of which Isaias, the twelfth chapter: « You shall draw waters with joy out of the saviour's fountains ».

4. Now these five fountains can be fitted to the five *wounds* of Christ, of which four were inflicted on Christ before death, but the fifth after death. So also of these fountains four are opened to us in this life, but the fifth afterwards in glory[^p555-6].

### Annotation 12.

(Verse 8.). *His disciples were gone, to buy meats.*

It is to be noted that according to Scripture *food* is distinguished as manifold. For there is food *natural, legal, carnal, spiritual, sacramental, eternal.* — *Natural* food is ministered to us at the table *of nature*, of which Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p555-7]: « The workman is worthy of his meat ». — *Legal* food, at the table *of the synagogue*, which the Apostle forbids in to the Romans, the fourteenth chapter: « If, because of thy meat, thy brother be grieved, thou walkest not now according to charity ». — *Carnal* food, at the table *of concupiscence;* Proverbs, the thirtieth chapter: « By three things the earth is disturbed », of which one is, when « a fool is filled with meat ». — *Spiritual* food, at the table *of conscience*, of which John, the sixth chapter: « Labour not for the meat which perisheth, but for that which endureth unto life everlasting ». — *Sacramental* food, at the table *of the eucharist;* John, the sixth chapter[^p555-8]: « My flesh is meat indeed » etc. — *Eternal* food, at the table *of glory*, of which Tobias, the twelfth chapter: « I use an invisible meat and drink, which cannot be seen by men ».

### Collation XV.

(Verse 32.). *I have meat to eat, which you know not.*

1. That we may know what the just man ought to feed on, it is to be noted that threefold is the food that is set before us in the present: *natural* food — *carnal* food — and *artificial* food. The first *sustains nature*, the second *nourishes concupiscence*, the third *strengthens the soul.*

2. Concerning *natural* food note that *it is to be worthily earned;* Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p555-9]: « The workman is worthy
<!-- page 556 -->
of his meat »; as if he were to say: and not another; the second to the Thessalonians, the last chapter: « If any man will not work, neither let him eat »; Genesis, the third chapter: « In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread ». — Likewise, *it is to be taken modestly;* Ezechiel, the fourth chapter: « Thy meat, wherewith thou shalt be fed, shall be by weight twenty stateres a day; from time to time thou shalt eat it »; as if he were to say: thou shalt eat by weight and at the time; Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-seventh chapter: « Be not greedy in any feasting, and pour not out thyself upon any meat ». — Likewise, *it is to be distributed liberally;* against which, of the avaricious man, Job, the twentieth chapter[^p556-1]: « There was nothing left of his meat, and therefore nothing shall continue of his goods ». The example of the rich glutton, Luke, the sixteenth chapter.

3. Concerning *carnal* food attend why it is to be fled: because it renders the one who eats it *needy*, for it is too dear to buy; Lamentations, the first chapter[^p556-2]: « All her people sigh, and seek bread; they have given all their precious things for food to relieve the soul ». — It makes the one who eats it *unclean;* for it is polluted; Leviticus, the eleventh chapter: « Any meat which you eat, if water be poured upon it, shall be unclean »; *water*, that is, concupiscence. — It makes the one who eats it *weak* and infirm; for it is ill-nourishing; Job, the thirtieth chapter[^p556-3]: « The root of junipers was their food »; Lamentations, the first chapter: « Your priests were consumed in the city, because they sought food for themselves, to relieve their souls »; Isaias, the fifty-fifth chapter: « Why do you spend money for that which is not bread, and your labour for that which doth not satisfy you »? — It *impoverishes* by expelling grace, it *pollutes* by bringing in fault or stain, it *makes infirm* by corrupting or wounding nature.

4. Concerning *spiritual* food attend why it is *to be desired:* because it is *noble;* Wisdom, the sixteenth chapter[^p556-4]: « Thou didst feed thy people with the food of angels, and gavest them bread from heaven prepared without labour » etc.; and the Psalm: « Man ate the bread of angels; he sent them provisions in abundance ». — Because it is *useful;* for it well refreshes and strengthens; the third of Kings, the nineteenth chapter: « When Elias arose, he ate and drank, and walked in the strength of that food forty days and forty nights, unto the mount of God, Horeb ». — Because it is *delightful;* Wisdom, the sixteenth chapter[^p556-5]: « Thou gavest them bread from heaven prepared without labour, having in it all that is delicious, and the sweetness of every taste »; the Psalm: « He fed them with the fat of wheat, and filled them with honey out of the rock ».

5. But note three things that take away from man the delights of spiritual food. The first is *the failing of the sense;* the second of Kings, the nineteenth chapter[^p556-6], of Berzellai: « Are my senses quick to discern sweet or bitter? or can meat or drink delight thy servant? or can I hear any more the voice of singing men and singing women »? The Psalm: « My heart is withered, because I forgot to eat my bread ». — The second is *the infection of the sense*, which is wont to be in the infirm, whose palate is burned; Job, the thirty-third chapter[^p556-7]: « The Lord rebuketh by sorrow in the bed, and he maketh all his bones to wither; bread becometh abominable to him in his life, and to his soul the meat which before he desired »; the Psalm: « Their soul abhorred all manner of meat, and they drew nigh even to the gates of death ». — The third is *the surfeit of the sense;* Numbers, the twenty-first chapter: « Why didst thou bring us out of Egypt, to die in the wilderness? There is no bread, nor have we any waters; our soul now loatheth this very light food ». — Attend that the sense is evilly *surfeited* by the wantonness of evil working, is evilly *infected* through the concupiscence of evil affection, but *fails* through the unaccustomedness of devotion; and those are as it were *surfeited*, those as it were *burned*, those as it were *failing* and languid.

### Collation XVI.

(Chapter VI, 27.). *Labour not for the meat which perisheth, but for that which endureth unto life everlasting*[^p556-8].

1. In this word there is intimated to us a certain distinction of food. For there is a food that *perisheth*, and that is the food *of fault* — and there is a food that *endureth unto life everlasting*, and that is the food *of grace.*

But the food *of fault* is manifold: the food *of lust;* the second of Kings, the thirteenth chapter[^p556-9]: « Let my sister Thamar come, I pray thee, to give me to eat, and to make me a mess, and I may eat it at her hand »; note it of love. — The food *of gluttony;* Numbers, the eleventh chapter: « As yet the flesh was between their teeth, neither had that kind of meat failed, when behold, the wrath of the Lord being provoked against the people, struck them with an exceeding great plague. And that place was called the graves of lust »; the Psalm: « As yet their meat was in their mouth, and the wrath of God came upon them ». — The food *of pride;* Daniel, the first chapter[^p556-10]: « Try us, I beseech thee, for ten days, and let pulse be given us to eat, and water to drink, and look upon our faces, and the faces of the children that eat of the king's
<!-- page 557 -->
meat; and as thou shalt see, deal with thy servants »; Osee, the twelfth chapter: « Ephraim feedeth on the wind, and followeth the burning heat ». — The food *of envy;* Proverbs, the twenty-third chapter: « Eat not with an envious man, and desire not his meats, because like a soothsayer and diviner, he thinketh that which he knoweth not ». — The food *of anger*, which the Lord forbids in figure in Leviticus, the seventh chapter[^p557-1]: « You shall not take for food the blood of any creature, whether of birds or of beasts ». — The food *of avarice;* Proverbs, the twenty-third chapter: « Desire not of his meats », that is, of the world, « in which is the bread of deceit »; Ecclesiasticus, the thirteenth chapter: « He will shame thee by his meats », that is, the world by its riches, « until he have drawn thee dry twice or thrice, and at the last he will laugh at thee, and afterwards when he seeth thee he will forsake thee, and shake his head at thee ». Expound the whole of *the world*, whose meats are riches, which it pours out to the avaricious for the *credentia*[^p557-2]. — The food *of sloth;* Judges, the nineteenth chapter: « When it was morning, the Levite prepared to depart; to whom the father-in-law said again: I pray thee, he said, take a little meat, and having gathered strength, until the day be farther advanced, afterwards thou shalt depart ». — Note that he made him tarry so long that he came to an evil lodging. For

> Languid sloth puts off today's deeds to the morrow.

This food is to be shunned.

2. The food *of grace* is likewise manifold. — The food *of grace*, this renders *life;* the Psalm[^p557-3]: « The river of God is filled with water, thou hast prepared their food, for so is its preparation. Fill up plentifully the streams thereof, multiply its fruits ». — The food *of wisdom*, this renders *sense;* Ecclesiasticus, the fifteenth chapter: « With the bread of life and understanding she shall feed him, and give him the water of wholesome wisdom to drink »; Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter: « Who, thinkest thou, is a faithful and wise servant, whom his lord hath appointed over his family, to give them meat in season »? Proverbs, the thirteenth chapter: « Much food is in the tillage of fathers, and for others it is gathered without judgement ». — The food *of spiritual gladness*, this renders *movement;* Ecclesiasticus, the fourteenth chapter[^p557-4]: « Before thy death work justice, for in hell there is no finding food »; the Psalm: « Who didst take sweetmeats together with me; in the house of God we walked with consent ». — This is the threefold food *interior* in the conscience.

3. There follows the threefold food *exterior* in life: the food *of penance;* the Psalm[^p557-5]: « Thou wilt feed us with the bread of tears, and give us for our drink tears in measure ». This is for all the *medicinal* or healing food *of the sick.* — The food *of justice;* John, the fourth chapter: « My meat is to do the will of my Father who sent me, that I may perfect his work ». This is the *nourishing and increasing* food, the food *of those who labour.* — The food *of patience;* to the Hebrews, the fifth chapter: « Strong meat is for *the perfect*, for them who by custom have their senses exercised to the discerning of good and evil ». — This is the threefold food exterior in life.

4. There follows the *superior* food on the altar, and this is the seventh, namely the food *of the eucharist;* John, the sixth chapter[^p557-6]: « My flesh is meat indeed, and my blood is drink indeed ». *By all* food of this kind is to be sought.

### Annotation 13.

(Verse 42.). *This is indeed the saviour of the world;* who saves from the evil *of fault;* Matthew, the first chapter[^p557-7]: « He shall save his people from their sins »; Luke, the second chapter: « I bring you good tidings of great joy » etc.; « for, this day, is born to you a saviour, who is Christ the Lord ». — Who saves from the evil *of temptation* or of combat; the Psalm: « Thou hast saved us from them that afflict us, and hast put them to shame that hate us ». — Who saves from the evil *of punishment* or of misery; to the Philippians, the third chapter: « We look for the Saviour, who will reform the body of our lowness, made like to the body of his glory ».

From the evil *of fault* he saves *the penitent;* Luke, the seventh chapter[^p557-8], the example of Magdalen, to whom it was said: « Thy faith hath made thee safe; go in peace ». — From the evil *of combat, the constant;* the first of Machabees, the fourth chapter: « Remember in what manner our fathers were saved; and now let us cry to heaven, and our God will have mercy on us ». — From the evil *of misery, the persevering;* Matthew, the tenth chapter: « He that shall persevere unto the end, he shall be saved ». — Have therefore *penance* for fault, *constancy* in combat, *perseverance* in grace.

### Annotation 14.

(Verse 18.). *Thou hast had five husbands, and he whom thou hast, is not thy husband.*

1. *The woman*, the faithful soul; her lawful *husband*, Christ; as often therefore as the soul is converted to God, it is joined to its true spouse; as often as to another, it commits fornication. — To this woman are cast up *five husbands* — and she is reproved also of a *sixth* — when it is said: *Thou hast had five husbands*, and spiritually the *five husbands* whom the soul abuses can be called *the five senses.* — The first husband therefore is *taste*, whom *the gluttonous* serve; in whose person it is said in Ecclesiastes, the second chapter[^p557-9]: « It is better to
<!-- page 558 -->
eat and drink, and to shew his soul good things »; Isaias, the twenty-second chapter: « Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we shall die ». — The second is *touch*, whom *the lustful* serve; in whose person it is said in Wisdom, the second chapter: « Let no meadow escape our riot. Let none of us go without his part in luxury ». — The third is *smell*, whom *the voluptuous* serve; in whose person it is said in Wisdom, the second chapter: « Let us fill ourselves with ointments, and let not the flower of the time pass by us; let us crown ourselves with roses » etc. — The fourth is *sight*, whom *the avaricious* serve; of whom Ecclesiasticus, the fourteenth chapter[^p558-1]: « The eye of the covetous man is insatiable in his portion of iniquity; he will not be satisfied ». — The fifth is *hearing*, whom the curious serve; such were the Athenians, the Acts, the seventeenth chapter: « They employed themselves in nothing else[^tr-vacare], but either in telling or in hearing some new thing »; and the second to Timothy, the fourth chapter: « According to their own desires they will heap to themselves teachers, having itching ears ».

These therefore are the five husbands whom the faithful soul is reproved for having had; who also are signified by the five yoke of oxen, of which it is said in Luke, the fourteenth chapter[^p558-2]: « I have bought five yoke of oxen ».

2. Further, the Samaritan woman is charged with having a sixth husband, who is not her own, when it is said: *And he whom thou hast, is not thy husband;* and this husband is *error.* For error of mind follows the pleasures of the body, according to what is said in the Psalm[^p558-3]: « Do not become like the horse and the mule, who have no understanding ». The soul is admonished to dismiss this husband and to transfer herself to her own spouse, when it is said to her by Christ: « Call thy husband », above in the same place.

### Collation XVII.

(Verse 46.). *There was a certain ruler, whose son was sick at Capharnaum.*

1. It is to be considered in these words *what* this infirmity *is* — and *whence* it was contracted.

This infirmity is the infirmity *of fault;* and it is called *the infirmity of fault* on account of three things, namely because it *deforms, impoverishes, weakens. It deforms* by polluting *the conscience;* it weakens by wounding *nature;* it impoverishes by expelling *grace.*

2. Fault therefore is an infirmity, because it *deforms;* the first of Machabees, the first chapter[^p558-4]: « The virgins and young men were made feeble, and the beauty of the women was changed »; Lamentations, the first chapter: « From the daughter of Sion all her beauty is departed ». — Because it *impoverishes;* Ecclesiastes, the fifth chapter: « As he came forth naked from his mother's womb, so shall he return »; and there follows: « A most deplorable infirmity, as he came, so shall he return »; Lamentations, the first chapter: « The enemy hath put out his hand to all her desirable things ». — Because it *weakens;* Lamentations, the first chapter: « My strength is weakened, the Lord hath delivered me into a hand out of which I am not able to rise »; the Psalm: « My strength is weakened through poverty ».

3. It is to be seen besides *from what* this infirmity was *contracted.* But it is to be noted that infirmity is sometimes contracted from *the place*, sometimes from *the food*, sometimes from *inordinate labour.* In the place note the evil *occasion;* in the food, the evil *delectation;* in inordinate labour, the evil *working*[^p558-5]. — Infirmity therefore is contracted from *the place;* whence when the sick man is described in the present Gospel, *the place* is notably added, when it is said: *He was sick at Capharnaum*, which is interpreted field of fatness, or town of consolation[^p558-6]. For in these spiritual infirmity is wont to be contracted. Again, Osee, the fourth chapter: « Cursing and lying and theft and murder have overflowed, and blood hath touched blood; therefore shall the land mourn, and every one that dwelleth in it shall be weakened ». — Infirmity is contracted also from *the food;* Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-seventh chapter: « In many meats there will be sickness, and greediness will turn to choler ». — It is contracted also from *inordinate labour;* Isaias, the fortieth chapter: « Youths shall faint, and labour, and young men shall fall by infirmity »; the Psalm: « Their heart was humbled with labours, they were weakened, and there was none to help them ».

### Collation XVIII.

(Verse 47.). *For he was beginning to die.*

1. The Gospel describes not only the *infirmity* of the sick man, but the *gravity* of the infirmity in this, that the sick man was drawing near to death; which it notes when it says: *For he was beginning to die.* Where it is to be noted that of spiritual death the sign is fourfold, namely the loss *of movement*, the loss *of taste*, the loss *of speech* and the loss *of sense.* — For then a man begins to die spiritually, when he loses *the movement of good working;* whence James, the second chapter[^p558-7]: « Faith without works is dead ». — When he loses *the taste of devotion;* the Psalm: « Their soul abhorred all manner of meat, and they drew nigh even to the gates of death ». — Likewise, when he loses *the speech of confession;* the Psalm: « For there is no one in death, that is mindful of thee; and who shall confess to thee in hell »? — Likewise, when he loses *the sense of discretion;* on account of which David cried: « Enlighten my eyes, that I never sleep in death »; and in the fourth of Kings, the fourth chapter, it is said of the son of the woman, to whom Giezi had come, that « there was no voice nor sense », so that he was signified to be dead.

2. On the contrary, note that the signs *of spiritual life* are four, namely the recovery *of the sense of discretion;*
<!-- page 559 -->
whence in the fourth of Kings, the fourth chapter[^p559-1], of the child whom Eliseus raised up, that « he gaped seven times, and opened his eyes ». — Likewise, the recovery *of the speech of confession;* whence in Luke, the seventh chapter, of the young man whom Jesus raised up, it is said that « he that was dead sat up, and began to speak ». — Likewise, the recovery *of the taste of devotion;* whence in Luke, the eighth chapter, after Jesus raised up the daughter of the ruler of the synagogue, « he bid them give her to eat ». — Likewise, the recovery *of good working;* whence in John, the eleventh chapter, when Lazarus had been raised, « Jesus said: Loose him, and let him go ».

### Collation XIX.

(Verse 49.). *Lord, come down before that my son die.*

1. Two things are touched on in these words: *the piety of the physician* is implored, when it is said: *Lord, come down* — and *the need of the sick* is shown, when it is said: *before that my son die.*

*The need* therefore *of the sick* is the imminence of death; where it is to be noted that some die *of want*, some *of infirmity*, some *of adversity.* — *Of want* he was dying who cried in the Psalm[^p559-2]: « My strength is dried up like a potsherd, and my tongue hath cleaved to my jaws, and thou hast brought me down into the dust of death ». — *Of infirmity* this son of the ruler was dying, who was sick at Capharnaum; of whom it is said before: « For he was beginning to die ». — *Of adversity* they were dying of whom it is said in Lamentations, the first chapter: « Abroad the sword destroyeth, and at home there is death alike ». — In *want* understand the hardening of the mind; in *infirmity*, the corruption of the flesh; in *adversity*, the assault of the enemy.

2. On account of this threefold need of the sick man *the coming down of the physician* is implored; where note that the Lord comes down *to refresh*, lest one die *of want;* *to cure*, lest one die *of infirmity;* *to deliver*, lest one die *of adversity.*

The ruler says therefore: *Come down, Lord,* lest *my son die* through *the want of indevotion;* of which coming down in the Psalm[^p559-3]: « He shall come down like rain upon the fleece, and as showers falling gently upon the earth ». — Likewise, *come down, Lord,* lest *my son die* through *the infirmity of carnal corruption;* of which coming down Matthew, the eighth chapter: « Jesus saith: I will come and heal him », he speaks of the centurion's son; and here, in John, the fourth chapter, the ruler was asking Jesus to come down and heal his son. — Likewise, *come down, Lord,* lest *my son die* through *the assault of the enemy;* of which coming down Exodus, the third chapter: « I have seen the affliction of my people in Egypt, and I am come down to deliver them ». — Come down therefore, Lord, *to refresh the mind*, *to cure the flesh*, *to repel the enemy*, lest my son die.

### Annotation 15.

(Verse 13.). *Whosoever drinketh of this water, shall thirst again.*

It is to be noted that thirst is manifold. For there is the thirst *of nature;* John, the fourth chapter[^p559-4]: *Whosoever drinketh of this water, shall thirst again.* — There is the thirst *of fault;* Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-sixth chapter: « As a thirsty traveller that cometh to a fountain will open his mouth, and will drink of every water near him, and will sit down by every hedge ». — There is the thirst *of punishment;* Isaias, the sixty-fifth chapter: « My servants shall eat, and you shall be hungry; my servants shall drink, and you shall be thirsty ». — There is the thirst *of grace;* the Psalm: « My soul hath thirsted after the strong living God. — There is the thirst *of glory;* Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter: « They that eat me shall yet hunger, and they that drink me shall yet thirst ».

### Annotation 16.

(Verse 36.). *And he that reapeth receiveth reward.*

Note that this reward is a reward *great*, a reward *much* and a reward *eternal.* — A reward *great;* Genesis, the fifteenth chapter[^p559-5]: « Fear not, Abraham, I am thy protector, and thy reward exceeding great. — A reward *much;* Luke, the sixth chapter: « Be glad and rejoice, for your reward is much in heaven ». — A reward *eternal;* John, the fourth chapter: *He that reapeth receiveth reward, and gathereth fruit unto life everlasting.*

### Annotation 17.

(Verse 41.). *And many more believed because of his word.*

Note that some believed on account of *the words of truth*, as these, of whom it is said here: *Many more believed* etc. — Others, on account of *the signs of power;* above, in the second chapter[^p559-6]: « Many believed in his name, seeing his signs which he did ». — Others, on account of *the example of holiness;* John, the tenth chapter: « Though you will not believe me, believe the works » etc.
