# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. VI, Vers. 41-52
## *Posuit supra instructionem ad Iudaeorum petitionem, hic secundo ponit confirmationem ad compescendam eorum murmurationem; et procedit in hac parte hoc ordine: primo ponitur Iudaeorum murmuratio; secundo vero, murmurationis cohibitio; tertio, erroris amotio; quarto, veracis doctrinae confirmatio*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 329 -->

65. *Murmurabant ergo Iudaei* etc. Posuit supra[^p329-8] instructionem ad Iudaeorum petitionem, hic secundo ponit *confirmationem* ad compescendam eorum murmurationem; et procedit in hac parte hoc ordine: primo ponitur *Iudaeorum murmuratio;* secundo vero, *murmurationis cohibitio;* tertio, *erroris amotio;* quarto, *veracis doctrinae confirmatio.*

(Vers. 41.). Insinuatur ergo primo *Iudaeorum murmuratio* de hoc, quod Dominus se de caelo dixerat descendisse; propter quod dicit: *Murmurabant ergo Iudaei de illo.* Murmuratio est occulta detractio; unde Chrysostomus[^p329-9]: « Non aperte, sed occulte contradicebant, quia signum de panum multiplicatione adhuc recens erat ». Morem patrum suorum servabant, de quibus Numerorum decimo quarto: « Usquequo multitudo haec pessima murmurat contra me » ? Et subdit de quo murmurabant: *Quia dixisset: Ego sum panis vivus, qui de caelo descendi.* Ipsi enim quaerebant panem temporalem ad manducandum; unde, quia spe sua frustrati erant in hac responsione, ideo murmurabant.

66. (Vers. 42.). *Et dicebant: Nonne hic est*[^p329-10] *filius Ioseph?* Lucae tertio: « Erat incipiens quasi annorum triginta, ut putabatur, filius Ioseph »; *cuius nos novimus patrem et matrem,* de terris, non de caelis; Matthaei decimo tertio: « Nonne hic est fabri filius, et mater eius dicitur Maria, et fratres eius et sorores apud nos sunt » ? *Quomodo ergo dicit hic, quia de caelo descendi?* cum sciamus, eum natum esse in terris. Et sic verificatum est illud quod dictum est supra quarto[^p329-11]: « Propheta in patria sua honorem non habet ». Quia enim cognoscebant temporalem generationem, negabant aeternam; et quia contempserunt Christum humilem, non potuerunt videre sublimem.

67. (Vers. 43.). *Respondit ergo Iesus et dixit eis.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *murmurationis cohibitio.* Cohibet enim eos a murmuratione; unde dicit: *Nolite murmurare ad invicem*[^p329-12]*;* et vere, quia nihil prodest; Sapientiae primo: « Custodite vos a murmuratione, quae nihil prodest », sed obest; primae ad Corinthios decimo: « Neque murmuraveritis, sicut quidam eorum murmuraverunt ». Et ratio, quare non debent murmurare, est, quia Christum non intelligunt; et quia eum non intelligunt, stulte reprehendunt; et vere non intelligunt, quia non credunt; et non credunt, quia non eis est a Patre datum. Propterea dicit:

68. (Vers. 44.). *Nemo potest venire ad me,* per fidem et dilectionem et cognitionem; *nisi Pater, qui
<!-- page 330 -->
misit me, traxerit eum,* per *revelationem,* sicut Petrus, cui dicitur Matthaei decimo sexto[^p330-1]: « Beatus es, Simon Bariona, quia caro et sanguis non revelavit tibi, sed Pater meus, qui est in caelis ». Ita Pater trahit ad me per *revelationem. Et ego resuscitabo eum in novissimo die,* per *redemptionem* et liberationem a morte; primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto: « Quoniam quidem per hominem mors, et per hominem resurrectio mortuorum; et sicut in Adam omnes moriuntur, ita in Christo omnes vivificabuntur ». Et ut magis eis silentium imponat, confirmat per Scripturam.

69. (Vers. 45.). *Scriptum est in Prophetis: Erunt*[^p330-2] *omnes docibiles Dei,* id est, omnes docebuntur a Deo; *in Prophetis* dicitur, quia in pluribus invenitur; Ieremiae trigesimo primo: « Dabo legem meam in visceribus eorum et in cordibus eorum scribam eam, et ero eis in Deum, et ipsi erunt mihi in populum; et non docebit ultra vir proximum suum, et vir fratrem suum dicens: Cognosce Dominum; omnes enim cognoscent me a minimo usque ad maximum »; item, Isaiae quinquagesimo quarto[^p330-3]: « Ponam omnes filios tuos doctos a Domino »; et Ioelis secundo: « Effundam de spiritu meo super omnem carnem, et prophetabunt filii vestri et filiae vestrae, senes vestri somnia somniabunt ».

*Omnis, qui audivit a Patre* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *erroris amotio.* Quia enim dixerat, quod omnes docentur a Deo; ne quis crederet, quod videant Deum, a quo docentur, dicit, quod non *vident,* sed *audiunt.* Unde dicit: *Omnis, qui audivit a Patre meo*[^p330-4] *et didicit,* per inspirationem, iuxta illud Psalmi: « Audiam, quid loquatur in me Dominus Deus »; ille, inquam, *venit ad me,* per fidem et dilectionem, iuxta illud Apocalypsis ultimo: « Et qui audit dicat: Veni ».

70. (Vers. 46.). *Et*[^p330-5] *non quia Patrem vidit quisquam,* scilicet mortalis et passibilis, quia Exodi trigesimo tertio dicitur: « Non videbit me homo et vivet »; *nisi is qui est a Deo, hic videt Patrem.* Hic est unigenitus Filius, de quo supra primo: « Deum nemo vidit unquam, sed unigenitus Filius, qui est in sinu Patris » etc.

71. (Vers. 47.). *Amen, amen dico vobis* etc. Hic ponitur *praecedentis instructionis*[^p330-6] *confirmatio;* unde iterat quod dixerat et adverbium affirmandi geminat; propter quod dicit: *Amen, amen dico vobis,* id est, vere, vere dico: *Qui credit in me habet vitam aeternam;* et hoc probat, quoniam ipse est panis, qui vivificat credentes; unde dicit:

72. (Vers. 48.). *Ego sum panis vitae;* et hoc credentibus, quia, sicut dicitur Proverbiorum tertio[^p330-7], « lignum vitae est his qui apprehenderint eam », scilicet per *fidem;* « et qui tenuerit eam beatus », qui tenuerit per *comprehensionem.* Et quod sit *panis vitae,* ostendit per effectum, quia scilicet dat *vitam gratiae* et *vitam gloriae;* vitam *gratiae,* quam non dabat panis ille typicus; unde dicit:

73. (Vers. 49.). *Patres vestri manducaverunt manna in deserto,* per quadraginta annos scilicet, sicut dicitur Deuteronomii octavo[^p330-8]: « Dedit tibi cibum manna, quod ignorabas »; *et mortui sunt,* morte scilicet culpae et infidelitatis; ad Hebraeos quarto: « Non profuit eis sermo auditus, non admixtus fidei ».

74. (Vers. 50.). *Hic est panis de caelo descendens,* per incarnationem, *ut, si quis ex eo*[^p330-9] *manducaverit,* per fidem et dilectionem; *non moriatur,* per praevaricationem; quia, sicut dicitur Habacuc secundo, « iustus in fide sua vivit ». Sicut fides a morte liberat, sic etiam dilectio; primae Ioannis tertio: « Nos scimus, quoniam translati sumus de morte ad vitam, quoniam diligimus fratres; qui non diligit manet in morte ». Nec tantum dat vitam *gratiae,* immo etiam vitam *gloriae;* propter quod subdit:

75. (Vers. 51.). *Ego sum panis vivus,* per essentiam, quia vitam habens in semetipso; supra quinto[^p330-10]: « Sicut Pater habet vitam in semetipso, sic dedit et Filio vitam habere in semetipso ». *Qui de caelo descendi,* per misericordiae abundantiam; Exodi tertio: « Vidi afflictionem populi mei in Aegypto et dolorem eius et descendi, ut liberem eum ».

76. (Vers. 52.). *Si quis manducaverit ex hoc pane, vivet in aeternum,* per gloriam. De hac comestione Canticorum quinto[^p330-11]: « Comedite, amici, et bibite et inebriamini, carissimi »; hoc erit in gloria. Ab huiusmodi comestione prohibitus est peccator in figura Adae; Genesis tertio: « Nunc ergo, ne forte sumat de ligno vitae et comedat et vivat in aeternum ».

### Quaestiones.

77. Quaest. I. Quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod dicit, quod *Iudaei murmurabant.*

1. Dominus sciebat, quod Iudaei de doctrina veritatis cadere debebant in murmurationem: ergo
<!-- page 331 -->
cum sciret, doctrinam non proficere; cum esset medicus salutis, non debuit dare.

2. Ad hoc facit quod scribitur Matthaei septimo[^p331-1]: *Nolite sanctum dare canibus neque spargere margaritas ante porcos;* ad hoc ipsum Proverbiorum nono: *Qui erudit derisorem, ipse sibi iniuriam facit:* ergo videtur, quod talibus non deberet dari instructio, qui cadunt in detractionem.

**Respondeo:** Sicut dicit Augustinus in libro de Bono perseverantiae[^p331-2], salus unius boni praeferenda est multis malis; hoc elicitur ex verbis eius; unde dicit ibidem, quod cum scimus, doctrinam nostram prodesse bonis, etsi sciamus, inde scandalizari malos, non debemus tacere. Quoniam igitur opportuna erat doctrina de pane vitae et perutilis valde bonis, et[^p331-3] qui tunc aderant et nobis, qui legimus; et tempus se offerebat, quia mirabilis et magna refectio facta fuerat: ideo bene fecit docendo, non propter malos, sed propter bonos. — Unde solvendum, quod etsi Dominus malis loqueretur, tamen erudiebat bonos.

78. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Omnis, qui audivit a Patre; non quia Patrem viderit* etc.

**Contra:** 1. Constat, quod hic loquitur de *videre* et *audire* ipsius intellectus; sed in intellectu non differt *visus* et *auditus,* et qui audit intelligibiliter videt intelligibiliter[^p331-4]: ergo si auditur Pater interius, et videtur.

2. Praeterea, *si dicas,* quod *aliud*[^p331-5]*;* certum est, quod *intelligere* est *videre;* qui igitur audit, aut intelligit, aut non; si intelligit, videt; si audit et non intelligit et venit ad auditum: ergo venit non intelligens tanquam brutum.

**Respondeo**, quod *visio* respicit loquentem, sed *auditus* locutionem; proprie *locutio* Dei *inspiratio* Dei est, secundum quod hic accipitur. Haec inspiratio effectus Dei est in anima; hunc effectum potest anima in se ipsa cognoscere, quamvis non plene cognoscat, a quo est. Unde dicendum, quod illam inspirationem et *videt* et *audit,* sed tamen ex hoc non sequitur, quod videat *Deum.* Unde optime dicit: *Omnis, qui audit a Patre,* id est inspirationem provenientem a Patre, et *illam,* inquam, videt, sicut et audit. Sed non ex hoc sequitur, quod videat *inspirantem;* quia auditus non *eum* respicit, sed *effectum,* sicut et visus[^p331-6].

79. Quaest. III. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Nemo potest ad me venire, nisi Pater traxerit eum.*

**Contra:** 1. *Tractio* est motus violentus[^p331-7]; omne meritum est voluntarium: ergo nullus meretur, cum facit aliquid tractus.

2. Item, si non potest nisi *tractus,* ergo non est in sua potestate ad Deum venire; et si hoc: ergo cum nullus sit reprehensibilis, si non facit impossibile, nullus est reprehensibilis, si ad Deum non venit.

3. Item, cum *trahere* sit totius Trinitatis, quod est per revelationem, quae proprie est Filii, vel Spiritus sancti; quomodo attribuitur Patri? quia non per *proprietatem,* nec per *appropriationem*[^p331-8].

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod est *manere in se,* et hoc naturae est institutae; *descendere* vel *inclinari infra se,* hoc naturae corruptae; sed *supra se elevari,* hoc est naturae per gratiam sublimatae[^p331-9]. Unde *stare* est naturae, *descendere* culpae, *ascendere* gratiae. — Quoniam igitur natura corrupta est et ad malum prona; si supra se elevetur, non tantum erit *elevatio,* sed propter inclinationem ad contrarium, *tractio.* Quia igitur omnis, qui ad Deum venit, *supra se elevatur;* nemo venit, nisi *trahatur.* Propterea bene dicit: *Nemo venit* etc.

1. Quod obiicit de motu violento, respondetur, quod est tractio *coactionis* exterioris, et est tractio *inductionis;* prima violentiam ponit, secunda minime. — Vel aliter: est tractio *affectionis,* secundum quod dicit Augustinus[^p331-10], quod poeta dicit: « Trahit sua quemque voluptas »; et haec meritoria est et demeritoria; et est tractio *contra voluntatem,* et hac non trahit, et de hac non loquitur.

2. Ad secundum dicendum, quod non est in plena potestate venire, sed in potestate est se ad divinum auxilium praeparare[^p331-11].

3. Quod appropriat Patri, dico, quod appropriatio fit in Trinitate ratione *intelligentiae dandae,* ra-
<!-- page 332 -->
tione *erroris amovendi,* ratione *humilitatis ostendendae*[^p332-1]. Sic dico, quod hoc tertio modo Filius ea quae sunt Trinitatis, Patri appropriat, ut nos humiliari doceat; et sic patet.

80. Quaest. IV. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Erunt omnes docibiles Dei:*

1. Quia hoc certissime falsum est in omni statu.

2. Item, quomodo verum est illud quod dicitur Ioelis secundo[^p332-2]: *Effundam de spiritu meo super omnem carnem;* et similiter illud Isaiae: *Ponam omnes filios tuos doctos a Domino?*

**Respondeo** secundum Augustinum[^p332-3], quod *omnes* distribuit pro *generibus* singulorum. — Sed tunc auctoritas parum valeret ad propositum.

Propter hoc aliter exponitur, quod qui docti erunt docebuntur a Deo, et nullus nisi per ipsum; ideo *omnes docibiles*[^p332-4]. Et sic auctoritas valet ad propositum.

## English
<!-- page 329 -->

65. *The Jews therefore murmured* etc. He set down above[^p329-8] the instruction at the petition of the Jews; here, second, he sets down the *confirmation* to restrain their murmuring; and he proceeds in this part in this order: first is set down *the murmuring of the Jews;* second, *the restraining of the murmuring;* third, *the removal of the error;* fourth, *the confirmation of the truthful teaching.*[^tr-doctrina]

(Verse 41.). First, then, is intimated *the murmuring of the Jews* about this, that the Lord had said that he had come down from heaven; on account of which he says: *The Jews therefore murmured at him.* Murmuring is a hidden detraction; whence Chrysostom[^p329-9]: « Not openly, but in secret they contradicted, because the sign of the multiplication of the loaves was still fresh ». They kept the manner of their fathers, of whom Numbers, the fourteenth: « How long doth this most wicked multitude murmur against me » ? And he adds about what they murmured: *Because he had said: I am the living bread, which came down from heaven.* For they were seeking temporal bread to eat; whence, because they had been cheated of their hope in this answer, therefore they murmured.

66. (Verse 42.). *And they said: Is not this*[^p329-10] *the son of Joseph?* Luke, the third: « He was beginning about the age of thirty years, as it was supposed, the son of Joseph »; *whose father and mother we know,* from the earth, not from heaven; Matthew, the thirteenth: « Is not this the carpenter's son, and his mother called Mary, and his brethren and sisters are they not with us » ? *How then saith this man, I came down from heaven?* since we know that he was born on earth. And so that was verified which was said above, in the fourth[^p329-11]: « A prophet hath no honour in his own country ». For because they knew the temporal generation, they denied the eternal; and because they despised Christ humble, they could not see him sublime.

67. (Verse 43.). *Jesus therefore answered and said to them.* Here is touched the second, namely *the restraining of the murmuring.* For he restrains them from murmuring; whence he says: *Murmur not among yourselves*[^p329-12]*;* and truly, because it profits nothing; Wisdom, the first: « Keep yourselves from murmuring, which profiteth nothing », but harms; the first to the Corinthians, the tenth: « Neither do you murmur, as some of them murmured ». And the reason why they ought not to murmur is, because they do not understand Christ; and because they do not understand him, they foolishly find fault; and truly they do not understand, because they do not believe; and they do not believe, because it is not given them by the Father. Therefore he says:

68. (Verse 44.). *No man can come to me,* through faith and dilection and knowledge; *except the Father, who
<!-- page 330 -->
hath sent me, draw him,* through *revelation,* as Peter, to whom it is said in Matthew, the sixteenth[^p330-1]: « Blessed art thou, Simon Bar-Jona, because flesh and blood hath not revealed it to thee, but my Father who is in heaven ». So the Father draws to me through *revelation. And I will raise him up in the last day,* through *redemption* and deliverance from death; the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth: « For by a man came death, and by a man the resurrection of the dead; and as in Adam all die, so in Christ all shall be made alive ». And that he may impose silence on them the more, he confirms it through Scripture.

69. (Verse 45.). *It is written in the Prophets: They shall*[^p330-2] *all be taught of God,* that is, all shall be taught by God; *in the Prophets* is said, because it is found in several; Jeremiah, the thirty-first: « I will give my law in their bowels, and in their hearts I will write it; and I will be their God, and they shall be my people; and man shall teach no more his neighbour, and man his brother, saying: Know the Lord; for all shall know me from the least even to the greatest »; likewise Isaiah, the fifty-fourth[^p330-3]: « I will make all thy children taught of the Lord »; and Joel, the second: « I will pour out of my spirit upon all flesh, and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, your old men shall dream dreams ».

*Every one that hath heard of the Father* etc. Here is touched the third, namely *the removal of the error.* For because he had said that all are taught by God; lest anyone should believe that they see God, by whom they are taught, he says that they do not *see,* but *hear.* Whence he says: *Every one that hath heard of my Father*[^p330-4] *and hath learned,* through inspiration, according to that of the Psalm: « I will hear what the Lord God will speak in me »; he, I say, *cometh to me,* through faith and dilection, according to that of the Apocalypse, the last: « And he that heareth, let him say: Come ».

70. (Verse 46.). *And*[^p330-5] *not that any man hath seen the Father,* namely one mortal and passible, since it is said in Exodus, the thirty-third: « Man shall not see me and live »; *but he who is of God, he seeth the Father.* This is the only begotten Son, of whom above, in the first: « No man hath seen God at any time, but the only begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father » etc.

71. (Verse 47.). *Amen, amen I say unto you* etc. Here is set down *the confirmation of the preceding instruction*[^p330-6]; whence he repeats what he had said and doubles the adverb of affirming; on account of which he says: *Amen, amen I say unto you,* that is, truly, truly I say: *He that believeth in me hath everlasting life;* and he proves this, since he himself is the bread which quickens those who believe; whence he says:

72. (Verse 48.). *I am the bread of life;* and this to those who believe, since, as is said in Proverbs, the third[^p330-7], « she is a tree of life to them that lay hold on her », namely through *faith;* « and he that shall retain her is blessed », he that shall retain her through *comprehension.* And that he is *the bread of life,* he shows through the effect, since namely he gives *the life of grace* and *the life of glory;* the life *of grace,* which that bread in type did not give; whence he says:

73. (Verse 49.). *Your fathers did eat manna in the desert,* through forty years namely, as is said in Deuteronomy, the eighth[^p330-8]: « He gave thee manna for food, which thou knewest not »; *and are dead,* by the death namely of fault and of unbelief; to the Hebrews, the fourth: « The word of hearing did not profit them, not being mixed with faith ».

74. (Verse 50.). *This is the bread which cometh down from heaven,* through the incarnation, *that if any man eat of it*[^p330-9]*,* through faith and dilection, *he may not die,* through transgression; since, as is said in Habakkuk, the second, « the just liveth in his faith ». As faith frees from death, so also does dilection; the first of John, the third: « We know that we have passed from death to life, because we love the brethren; he that loveth not abideth in death ». Nor does he give only the life *of grace,* but also the life *of glory;* on account of which he adds:

75. (Verse 51.). *I am the living bread,* through essence, since having life in himself; above, in the fifth[^p330-10]: « As the Father hath life in himself, so he hath given to the Son also to have life in himself ». *Which came down from heaven,* through the abundance of mercy; Exodus, the third: « I have seen the affliction of my people in Egypt and their sorrow, and I have come down to deliver them ».

76. (Verse 52.). *If any man eat of this bread, he shall live for ever,* through glory. Of this eating, the Canticle, the fifth[^p330-11]: « Eat, O friends, and drink, and be inebriated, my dearly beloved »; this will be in glory. From such eating the sinner was forbidden in the figure of Adam; Genesis, the third: « Now therefore, lest perhaps he take of the tree of life, and eat, and live for ever ».

### Questions.

77. Question I. It is asked here first about what he says, that *the Jews murmured.*

1. The Lord knew that the Jews were to fall into murmuring over the teaching of the truth: therefore,
<!-- page 331 -->
since he knew that the teaching would not profit; since he was the physician of salvation, he ought not to have given it.

2. To this makes what is written in Matthew, the seventh[^p331-1]: *Give not that which is holy to dogs, neither scatter pearls before swine;* to this very point Proverbs, the ninth: *He that teacheth a scorner, doth an injury to himself:* therefore it seems that instruction ought not to be given to such as fall into detraction.

**I respond:** As Augustine says in the book *On the Good of Perseverance*[^p331-2], the salvation of one good man is to be preferred to many evil ones; this is drawn from his words; whence he says in the same place that when we know that our teaching profits the good, even if we know that the evil are scandalized by it, we ought not to be silent. Since, then, the teaching about the bread of life was opportune and very useful to the good, both[^p331-3] to those who were then present and to us who read; and the time offered itself, since a wonderful and great refection had been made: therefore he did well in teaching, not on account of the evil, but on account of the good. — Whence it is to be solved that, although the Lord was speaking to the evil, yet he was teaching the good.

78. Question II. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *Every one that hath heard of the Father; not that any man hath seen the Father* etc.

**On the contrary:** 1. It is evident that here he speaks of the *seeing* and *hearing* of the understanding itself; but in the understanding *sight* and *hearing* do not differ, and he who hears intelligibly sees intelligibly[^p331-4]: therefore if the Father is heard inwardly, he is also seen.

2. Further, *if you say* that it is *something else*[^p331-5]*;* it is certain that *to understand* is *to see;* he who hears, then, either understands or not; if he understands, he sees; if he hears and does not understand and comes by hearing: therefore he comes not understanding, like a brute.

**I respond** that *vision* looks to the one speaking, but *hearing* to the speech; properly the *speech* of God is the *inspiration* of God, according as it is taken here. This inspiration is an effect of God in the soul; this effect the soul can know in itself, although it does not fully know from whom it is. Whence it must be said that it both *sees* and *hears* that inspiration, but yet from this it does not follow that it sees *God.* Whence he says excellently: *Every one that hath heard of the Father,* that is, the inspiration coming from the Father, and *that,* I say, he sees, as he also hears it. But from this it does not follow that he sees *the one inspiring;* since hearing does not look to *him,* but to *the effect,* as sight also does[^p331-6].

79. Question III. It is asked about what he says: *No man can come to me, except the Father draw him.*

**On the contrary:** 1. *Drawing* is a violent motion[^p331-7]; all merit is voluntary: therefore no one merits when he does something drawn.

2. Likewise, if he cannot except *drawn,* then it is not in his power to come to God; and if this: then, since no one is reprehensible if he does not do the impossible, no one is reprehensible if he does not come to God.

3. Likewise, since *to draw* belongs to the whole Trinity, which is through revelation, which properly belongs to the Son, or to the Holy Spirit; how is it attributed to the Father? since not through *property,* nor through *appropriation*[^p331-8].

**I respond:** It must be said that there is *to remain in oneself,* and this belongs to nature as instituted; *to descend* or *to be inclined below oneself,* this to nature corrupted; but *to be raised above oneself,* this belongs to nature exalted through grace[^p331-9]. Whence *to stand* belongs to nature, *to descend* to fault, *to ascend* to grace. — Since, then, nature is corrupted and prone to evil; if it is raised above itself, it will be not only an *elevation,* but, on account of the inclination to the contrary, a *drawing.* Since, then, every one who comes to God *is raised above himself;* no one comes unless he *is drawn.* Therefore he says well: *No man cometh* etc.

1. As to the objection about violent motion, it is answered that there is a drawing of exterior *coaction,* and there is a drawing of *induction;* the first posits violence, the second not at all. — Or otherwise: there is a drawing of *affection,* according to what Augustine says[^p331-10], that the poet says: « His own pleasure draws each one »; and this is meritorious and demeritorious; and there is a drawing *against the will,* and by this he does not draw, and of this he does not speak.

2. To the second it must be said that it is not in one's full power to come, but it is in one's power to prepare oneself for the divine help[^p331-11].

3. As to his appropriating it to the Father, I say that appropriation is made in the Trinity by reason of *intelligence to be given,* by
<!-- page 332 -->
reason of *error to be removed,* by reason of *humility to be shown*[^p332-1]. So I say that in this third way the Son appropriates to the Father the things which belong to the Trinity, so that he may teach us to be humbled; and so it is plain.

80. Question IV. It is asked about what he says: *They shall all be taught of God:*

1. Because this is most certainly false in every state.

2. Likewise, how is that true which is said in Joel, the second[^p332-2]: *I will pour out of my spirit upon all flesh;* and likewise that of Isaiah: *I will make all thy children taught of the Lord?*

**I respond** according to Augustine[^p332-3], that *all* distributes over the *kinds* of individuals. — But then the authority would avail little for the purpose.

On this account it is expounded otherwise, that those who will be taught will be taught by God, and no one except through him; therefore *all taught*[^p332-4]. And so the authority avails for the purpose.
