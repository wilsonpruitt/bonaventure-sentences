# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. IX, Vers. 35-41
## *In tertia parte describitur spiritualis caeci illuminatio*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 380 -->

#### In tertia parte describitur spiritualis caeci illuminatio.

46. *Audivit Iesus.* Haec est tertia pars capituli, in qua, iam descripta mirabili caeci illuminatione et Iudaeorum disceptatione, tertio introducitur de *spirituali illius illuminatione.* Hanc autem describit Evangelista hoc ordine: primo enim notatur ipsius *caeci despecti vocatio benigna;* secundo, *consentientis illuminatio interna;* tertio vero innuitur ipsorum *Iudaeorum superborum excaecatio iusta.*

(Vers. 35.). Primo ergo insinuatur *caeci abiecti vocatio benigna.* Eiecerunt enim eum Pharisaei, et Dominus suscepit; propter quod dicit: *Audivit Iesus, quia eiecerunt eum foras;* per se sciebat, sed nihilominus est ei relatum. *Et cum invenisset eum Iesus,* non casu, sed studio suscipiendi; Lucae decimo quinto[^p380-9]: « Accendit lucernam et quaerit diligenter, donec inveniat »; *dixit ei: Tu credis in Filium Dei?* Quaerendo excitat et excitando vocat, ut abiectum suscipiat, secundum illud Psalmi[^p380-10]: « Pater meus et mater mea dereliquerunt me, Dominus autem assumsit me »; Ieremiae quadragesimo nono: « Relinque pupillos tuos, et ego vivere te faciam ».

47. (Vers. 36.). *Respondit ille et dixit.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *consentientis instructio vel illuminatio interna.* Consensit enim caecus in fidem; unde dixit: *Quis est, Domine, ut credam in eum?* Quasi dicat: paratus sum credere; unde Chrysostomus[^p380-11]: « Verbum inquirentis est et desiderantis »; ideo instruitur. Propterea:

48. (Vers. 37.). *Dixit*[^p380-12] *ei Iesus: Et vidisti eum, et qui loquitur tecum, ipse est. Vidisti,* per
<!-- page 381 -->
miraculosam operationem; *loquitur tecum,* per mirabilem instructionem; supra octavo[^p381-1]: « Ego principium, qui et loquor vobis ». Simili modo instruxit Dominus Samaritanam supra quarto: « Ego sum, qui loquor tecum ». Et quia « corde creditur ad iustitiam, ore autem confessio fit ad salutem »; ideo sequitur vera caeci illuminati confessio; et confessus est fidem suam *verbo,* dicens:

49. (Vers. 38.). *Credo*[^p381-2]*, Domine;* et *devotionis obsequio,* unde: *Et procidens adoravit eum,* tanquam Deum, qui solus latria est adorandus; unde respondit Dominus diabolo Matthaei quarto: « Dominum Deum tuum adorabis et illi soli servies »; Deuteronomii sexto: « Dominum Deum tuum timebis et illi soli servies ».

50. (Vers. 39.). *Et dixit ei*[^p381-3] *Iesus.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *Iudaeorum excaecatio iusta,* quam Dominus manifestat caeco, ad quam etiam manifestandam ipsum corporaliter illuminavit. Propterea dicit: *In iudicium ego veni in hunc mundum, ut qui non vident videant, et qui vident caeci fiant. In iudicium,* scilicet discretionis, *ut qui vident,* id est, se videre reputant, sicut Iudaei, *caeci fiant.* De hoc iudicio dicitur ad Romanos undecimo[^p381-4]: « Nolo vos ignorare, fratres, ut non sitis vobismetipsis sapientes, quia caecitas ex parte contigit in Israel, donec plenitudo gentium intraret »; et iterum ad Romanos nono: « Gentes, quae non sectabantur iustitiam, apprehenderunt iustitiam; Israel vero, sectando legem iustitiae, in legem iustitiae non pervenit ». Et quod illud iudicium iustum sit, patet ex inquisitione Pharisaeorum et responsione Domini, in qua innuitur malitia et pertinacia Iudaeorum; quia, cum viderent, noluerunt credere et intelligere: et ideo iudicio Dei et suo peccato sunt excaecati[^p381-5]; ideo dicit:

51. (Vers. 40.). *Audierunt*[^p381-6] *quidam ex Pharisaeis, qui erant cum ipso,* id est, praesentes aderant; *et dixerunt: Nunquid et nos caeci sumus?* Indignabantur verbo Domini, quia credebant, propter se fuisse dictum, quia se sapientes reputabant: et ex hoc ipso ostendit Dominus divinum iudicium, quod *iuste* sunt excaecati; propter quod:

52. (Vers. 41.). *Dixit eis Iesus: Si caeci essetis, non haberetis peccatum;* quia tunc vos Dominus illuminaret; unde primae ad Corinthios tertio[^p381-7]: « Si quis videtur inter vos sapiens esse in hoc saeculo, stultus fiat, ut sit sapiens ». *Nunc autem dicitis: Quia videmus,* et vos sapientes reputatis; *peccatum vestrum manet,* scilicet infidelitatis, secundum illud ad Romanos primo[^p381-8]: « Dicentes, se esse sapientes, stulti facti sunt ». Unde alloquitur Dominus Ieremiae quinto: « Audi, popule stulte, non habens cor, qui habentes oculos non videtis, et aures, et non auditis ». Et quia ipsi videntes noluerunt credere, ideo sunt excaecati; Psalmus: « Obscurentur oculi eorum, ne videant » etc.

### Quaestiones.

53. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod Dominus quaerit, utrum credat *in Filium Dei.*

1. Constans est, quod stat ibi pro divina natura; sed post subdit: *Vidisti eum; eum* est relativum[^p381-9], ergo refert secundum divinam naturam: ergo caecus vidit divinam naturam.

2. Item, si fides est de his quae non videntur[^p381-10], quomodo hortatur Dominus, ut credat in eum quem videt?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod idiomatum est communicatio propter convenientiam in una hypostasi[^p381-11]. Unde sicut dicitur idem mortuus et aeternus, sic idem, in quem credendum, quia aequalis Patri; idem qui loquitur et videtur, sed in forma servi[^p381-12].

54. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *In iudicium ego veni in mundum;* quia supra tertio[^p381-13] dicitur: *Non misit Deus Filium suum, ut iudicet mundum.*

Respondetur, quod venit in iudicium *discretionis,* sed non *condemnationis*[^p381-14].

Aliter tamen potest dici, quod differt dicere *veni in iudicium,* et *ad iudicium: venire ad iudicium*
<!-- page 382 -->
dicit finalem intentionem ad iudicandum; sic non venit in primo adventu; sed *venire in iudicium* est ex adventu eius aliquod fieri iudicium in superbos, quia excaecantur ex eius adventu, quamvis ad hoc non veniat[^p382-1].

55. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Ut qui non vident videant, et qui vident caeci fiant.* — Videtur ergo, quod a Domino Iesu Christo sit[^p382-2] *excaecatio.* Et hoc quidem videtur dici debere per rationem: quia, si excaecatio est poena, et omnis poena iusta est, et quod iustum est bonum est, et quod bonum est a Deo est[^p382-3]: ergo excaecatio est a Deo.

**Sed contra:** 1. *Deus est lux*[^p382-4], sicut et calor: ergo si caloris non est infrigidare, nec lucis est obscurare: ergo nec excaecare.

2. Item, Deus appetit semper, suam imaginem sibi conformari: ergo si excaecatio deformat, Dei non est excaecare.

Respondetur, quod *excaecatio* potest considerari aut secundum *id quod est;* et sic *privatio* est et non habet causam efficientem, sed deficientem. Deus autem nullius est causa deficiens; et sic eius est causa malitia nostra; unde Sapientiae secundo[^p382-5]: *Excaecavit eos malitia eorum.* Quia tamen *excaecatio* et *induratio poenae* sunt, ponunt *esse* secundum id quod sunt; et *poena* secundum id quod est, iusta est, et sic, secundum id quod est, potest esse a Deo[^p382-6].

Amplius videtur dicendum, quod dupliciter est aliqua poena *a Deo:* uno modo, sicut *ab ordinante;* et sic peccatum, quod est *poena* peccati, secundum quod est *poena,* a Deo est, et sic non est a Deo secundum *id quod est*[^p382-7], sed in quantum *poena.* — Alio modo aliqua poena dicitur esse *a Deo* secundum *id quod est,* et hoc vel *a Deo infligente;* et hoc modo *mors* et *poenae aliae*[^p382-8], per quas contingit mereri. — Alio modo, *a Deo aliquid subtrahente* nostro merito; et sic fomes a Deo. Cessavit enim Deus dare animae gratiam, per quam regeret carnem, et hoc merito nostro; qua gratia subtracta, caro ad inferius declinavit. — Tertio modo, sicut *a non apponente* nostris meritis; et sic *excaecatio* et *obduratio.* Aliquis enim *obduratur* in malo, quia a Domino non visitatur, iusto iudicio; aliquis *excaecatur,* quia interius non illuminatur; ita dicit Augustinus[^p382-9].

## English
<!-- page 380 -->

#### In the third part is described the spiritual illumination of the blind man.

46. *Jesus heard.* This is the third part of the chapter, in which, the wonderful illumination of the blind man and the disputing of the Jews having now been described, there is brought in, third, *his spiritual illumination.* And the Evangelist describes it in this order: for first is noted *the kindly calling of the despised blind man* himself; second, *the inward illumination of him consenting;* third is intimated *the just blinding of the proud Jews* themselves.

(V. 35.). First, then, is intimated *the kindly calling of the outcast blind man.* For the Pharisees cast him out, and the Lord took him up; therefore he says: *Jesus heard that they had cast him out;* of himself he knew it, but nonetheless it was reported to him. *And when Jesus had found him,* not by chance, but with an eagerness to take him up; Luke, the fifteenth chapter[^p380-9]: « She lighteth a candle and seeketh diligently, until she find it »; *he said to him: Dost thou believe in the Son of God?* By asking he rouses and by rousing he calls, that he may take up the outcast, according to that of the Psalm[^p380-10]: « My father and my mother have left me, but the Lord hath taken me up »; Jeremiah, the forty-ninth chapter: « Leave thy fatherless children, and I will make thee live ».

47. (V. 36.). *He answered and said.* Here the second is touched on, namely *the instruction or inward illumination of him consenting.* For the blind man consented to the faith; whence he said: *Who is he, Lord, that I may believe in him?* As if he should say: I am ready to believe; whence Chrysostom[^p380-11]: « It is the word of one inquiring and desiring »; therefore he is instructed. Therefore:

48. (V. 37.). *Jesus said*[^p380-12] *to him: Thou hast both seen him; and he that talketh with thee, he it is. Thou hast seen,* through
<!-- page 381 -->
the miraculous working; *he talketh with thee,* through the wonderful instruction; above, in the eighth chapter[^p381-1]: « The beginning, who also speak unto you ». In like manner the Lord instructed the Samaritan woman, above, in the fourth chapter: « I am he, who am speaking with thee ». And because « with the heart we believe unto justice, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation », therefore there follows the true confession of the illuminated blind man; and he confessed his faith *in word,* saying:

49. (V. 38.). *I believe*[^p381-2]*, Lord;* and *by the homage of devotion,* whence: *And falling down, he adored him,* as God, who alone is to be adored with latria; whence the Lord answered the devil in Matthew, the fourth chapter: « The Lord thy God shalt thou adore, and him only shalt thou serve »; Deuteronomy, the sixth chapter: « Thou shalt fear the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve ».

50. (V. 39.). *And Jesus said to him*[^p381-3]. Here the third is touched on, namely *the just blinding of the Jews,* which the Lord makes manifest to the blind man, for the manifesting of which he also illuminated him bodily. Therefore he says: *For judgement I am come into this world; that they who see not, may see; and they who see, may become blind. For judgement,* namely of discernment, *that they who see,* that is, reckon that they see, as the Jews, *may become blind.* Of this judgement it is said in Romans, the eleventh chapter[^p381-4]: « I would not have you ignorant, brethren, lest you should be wise in your own conceits, that blindness in part has happened in Israel, until the fulness of the Gentiles should come in »; and again in Romans, the ninth chapter: « The Gentiles, who followed not after justice, have attained to justice; but Israel, by following after the law of justice, is not come unto the law of justice ». And that that judgement is just is plain from the inquiry of the Pharisees and the response of the Lord, in which is intimated the malice and pertinacity of the Jews; because, although they saw, they would not believe and understand: and therefore by the judgement of God and by their own sin they were blinded[^p381-5]; therefore he says:

51. (V. 40.). *Some of the Pharisees heard*[^p381-6]*, who were with him,* that is, were there present; *and they said: Are we also blind?* They were indignant at the word of the Lord, because they believed it had been said on their account, because they reckoned themselves wise: and from this very thing the Lord shows the divine judgement, that they were *justly* blinded; on account of which:

52. (V. 41.). *Jesus said to them: If you were blind, you should not have sin;* because then the Lord would illuminate you; whence First Corinthians, the third chapter[^p381-7]: « If any man among you seem to be wise in this world, let him become a fool, that he may be wise ». *But now you say: We see,* and you reckon yourselves wise; *your sin remaineth,* namely of unbelief, according to that of Romans, the first chapter[^p381-8]: « Saying that they were wise, they became fools ». Whence the Lord addresses them in Jeremiah, the fifth chapter: « Hear, O foolish people, and without understanding: who have eyes, and see not: and ears, and hear not ». And because, seeing, they would not believe, therefore they were blinded; the Psalm: « Let their eyes be darkened that they see not » etc.

### Questions.

53. Question I. But it is asked here, first, about this, that the Lord asks whether he believes *in the Son of God.*

1. It is certain that it stands there for the divine nature; but afterwards he adds: *Thou hast seen him; him* is a relative[^p381-9], therefore it refers according to the divine nature: therefore the blind man saw the divine nature.

2. Likewise, if faith is of those things which are not seen[^p381-10], how does the Lord exhort him to believe in him whom he sees?

**I respond:** It must be said that there is a communication of idioms because of the agreement in one hypostasis[^p381-11]. Whence, just as the same one is said to be dead and eternal, so the same one is he in whom one must believe, because equal to the Father; the same one who speaks and is seen, but in the form of a servant[^p381-12].

54. Question II. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *For judgement I am come into the world;* because above, in the third chapter[^p381-13], it is said: *God sent not his Son, to judge the world.*

It is responded that he came for the judgement of *discernment,* but not of *condemnation*[^p381-14].

Yet it can be said otherwise, that it differs to say *I am come for judgement* and *to judgement: to come to judgement*
<!-- page 382 -->
expresses the final intention of judging; so he did not come at the first coming; but *to come for judgement* is that from his coming some judgement comes about upon the proud, because they are blinded from his coming, although he does not come for this[^p382-1].

55. Question III. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *That they who see not, may see; and they who see, may become blind.* — It seems, then, that *blinding* is[^p382-2] from the Lord Jesus Christ. And this indeed it seems must be said by reason: because, if blinding is a penalty, and every penalty is just, and what is just is good, and what is good is from God[^p382-3]: therefore blinding is from God.

**On the contrary:** 1. *God is light*[^p382-4], as also heat: therefore if it is not of heat to chill, neither is it of light to darken: therefore neither to blind.

2. Likewise, God always desires his image to be conformed to himself: therefore if blinding deforms, it is not God's to blind.

It is responded that *blinding* can be considered either according to *that which it is;* and so it is a *privation* and has not an efficient cause, but a deficient one. But God is the deficient cause of nothing; and so its cause is our malice; whence Wisdom, the second chapter[^p382-5]: *Their malice blinded them.* Yet because *blinding* and *induration are penalties,* they posit *being* according to that which they are; and a *penalty,* according to that which it is, is just, and so, according to that which it is, it can be from God[^p382-6].

It seems, further, that it must be said that a penalty is *from God* in two ways: in one way, as *from one ordaining;* and so sin, which is the *penalty* of sin, according as it is a *penalty,* is from God, and so it is not from God according to *that which it is*[^p382-7], but inasmuch as it is a *penalty.* — In another way some penalty is said to be *from God* according to *that which it is,* and this either *from God inflicting;* and in this way *death* and *the other penalties*[^p382-8], through which it befalls us to merit. — In another way, *from God withdrawing something* by our desert; and so the tinder is from God. For God ceased to give the soul the grace by which it might rule the flesh, and this by our desert; which grace being withdrawn, the flesh declined to what is lower. — In the third way, as *from one not adding* to our merits; and so *blinding* and *obduration.* For someone *is obdurate* in evil because he is not visited by the Lord, by just judgement; someone *is blinded* because he is not illuminated within; so says Augustine[^p382-9].
