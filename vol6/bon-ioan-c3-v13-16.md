# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. III, Vers. 13-16
## *Determinavit supra salutis viam, hic determinat salutis principium, ut per hoc plene satisfiat Nicodemo quaerenti, quomodo illa possint fieri*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 282 -->

23. *Et nemo ascendit in caelum.* Determinavit supra salutis viam, hic determinat *salutis principium,* ut per hoc plene satisfiat Nicodemo quaerenti, quomodo illa possint fieri. Et describitur hic salutis principium hoc ordine: primo determinatur, quod est *principium salvans;* secundo, quis *modus salvandi;* tertio, quae *ratio movet ad salvandum.*

(Vers. 13.). Principium salvans nos est ille qui solus potest introire in caelum per se, et omnes per illum; et hic est Christus Dominus. Propterea dicit: *Et nemo ascendit in caelum,* id est, nemo habet virtutem illuc ascendendi; *nisi qui descendit de caelo,* per assumtionem humanitatis; *Filius hominis, qui est in caelo,* per praesentiam et immensitatem Divinitatis: ergo omnis, qui ascendit et salvatur, ascendit per eum. De hac potentia ascendendi et descendendi ad Ephesios quarto[^p282-3]: « Qui descendit, ipse est et qui ascendit super omnes caelos, ut adimpleret omnia ». De existentia eius in caelo supra primo: « Unigenitus Filius, qui est in sinu Patris, ipse enarravit ». Ipse est, qui facit ascendere a morte ad vitam; infra octavo: « Si Filius vos liberaverit, vere liberi eritis ».

24. (Vers. 14.). *Et sicut Moyses exaltavit* etc. Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *quomodo* Filius Dei *salvat,* quoniam per passionem. Ideo dicit: *Sicut Moyses exaltavit serpentem in deserto;* Numerorum vigesimo primo[^p282-4]: « Fecit Moyses serpentem aeneum et posuit illum pro signo, quem percussi aspicientes sanabantur ». *Ita exaltari oportet Filium hominis,* scilicet in crucis patibulo, ut omnes in eum aspicientes per fidem sanentur. Ideo dicit:

25. (Vers. 15.). *Ut omnis, qui credit in eum*[^p282-5], *non pereat, sed habeat vitam aeternam;* infra undecimo: « Qui credit in me, etiamsi mortuus fuerit, vivet; et omnis, qui vivit et credit in me, non morietur in aeternum ». Optime describit passionem in relatione ad figuram, ut fiat credibilior; et optime praecessit figura, quia, sicut ibi non sanabantur per serpentem nisi aspicientes, sic nec per Christum nisi in eum credentes.

26. (Vers. 16.). *Sic enim Deus* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *ratio movens* ad sic salvandum; et haec fuit immensitas divini amoris ad hominem perditum. Propter quod dicit: *Sic enim Deus dilexit mundum,* id est hominem mundanum et peccatorem; *ut Filium suum unigenitum daret;* ad Ephesios secundo[^p282-6]: « Deus, qui dives est in misericordia, propter nimiam caritatem suam, qua dilexit nos, cum essemus mortui peccatis, convivificavit nos Christo ». Ideo *nimia,* quia plus dedit, quam oportuit; ad Romanos octavo[^p282-7]: « Et qui proprio Filio suo non pepercit, sed pro nobis omnibus tradidit illum ». *Daret,* inquam, pro omnibus quantum ad *sufficientiam,* sed quantum ad *efficaciam* pro electis et credentibus[^p282-8]. Ideo subdit: *Ut omnis, qui credit in eum, non pereat, sed habeat vitam aeternam.* Aliis non valet passio nisi credentibus, quia, sicut dicitur ad Hebraeos undecimo[^p282-9], « sine fide impossibile est placere Deo ».

### Quaestiones.

27. Quaest. I. Sed quaestio hic incidit primo de hoc quod dicit: *Nemo ascendit in caelum, nisi qui descendit de caelo.*

**Contra:** 1. Omnes Sancti ascendunt, et tamen nullus descendit.

2. Item, corpus Christi ascendit in caelum, et tamen non descendit de caelo.

3. Item obiicitur: si hoc intelligitur de Christo; aut intelligitur secundum naturam *divinam,* aut *humanam.* Secundum *humanam,* non; quia illa non descendit de caelo, et praeterea tunc, cum loquebatur, non erat in caelo. Secundum *divinam* non potest, quia non ascendit, quia maior esse non potuit nec locum mutavit.
<!-- page 283 -->

Respondent quidam, quod exceptio hic non tantum comprehendit Christum, qui est caput, immo totum corpus Christi; et illi omnes et soli, qui ad corpus eius pertinent, ascendunt[^p283-1]. — Sed hoc non potest stare, quia corpus non descendit de caelo[^p283-2].

Propterea intelligendum, quod *ascendere* hic dicitur propria virtute, non aliunde data, et sic solus Christus potuit[^p283-3].

2. Quod obiicit de corpore, respondet Augustinus[^p283-4], quod non obstat, quia corpus est tanquam indumentum; unde si aliquis descendit nudus de monte et ascendit vestitus, dicimus: nemo ascendit, nisi qui descendit; sic et in proposito.

3. Quod quaerit: secundum quam naturam hoc dicitur? respondeo: Dicendum, quod in illa oratione tria dicuntur de una hypostasi: *ascendere* convenit ei ratione humanae naturae; *descendere,* ratione divinae relate ad humanam, quia se humiliavit assumendo carnem; *esse* vero *in caelo,* ut tunc, secundum divinam pure. Et sic non potest totum retorqueri ad unam naturam, sed ad unam hypostasim[^p283-5].

28. Quaest. II. Item quaerit Chrysostomus[^p283-6] de hoc quod dicit: *Sicut exaltavit* etc.: quare, cum exprimat passionem, non dixit *pendere in cruce,* ut apertius loqueretur?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Dominus revelabat passionis mysterium Nicodemo ut *honestum,* ut *credibile,* ut *secretum:* ut *honestum,* ne homo adhuc carnalis despiceret et horreret; ideo dixit *exaltari,* non *suspendi.* Ut *credibile,* ut fidem adhiberet; ideo per figuram praecedentem mirabilem passionis effectum mirabilem insinuat. Ut etiam *secretum,* ut carius habeat; et ideo non explanat, sed relinquit ei locum meditandi, et per hoc proficiendi et ad donum intellectus perveniendi[^p283-7].

29. Quaest. III. Quaestio est de hoc quod dicit: *Ut omnis, qui credit in eum, non pereat.* Ergo nullus, qui habet fidem, damnabitur, quantumcumque malus. — *Si tu dicas,* quod intelligitur de fide formata: ergo secundum hoc nullus, qui habet fidem formatam, potest damnari.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod hic loquitur de fide vera; vera autem fides excludit fictionem duplicem. Fides enim est ficta, aut quia credit, sed *non amat;* aut quia credit et amat, sed *non perseverat.* Vera est igitur fides, quae quod credit amat et perseverat; et omnis talis salvabitur[^p283-8].

## English
<!-- page 282 -->

23. *And no man hath ascended into heaven.* Above he determined the way of salvation; here he determines *the principle of salvation,* so that through this full satisfaction may be given to Nicodemus, who asked how those things could be done. And the principle of salvation is described here in this order: first it is determined what is *the saving principle;* second, what is *the mode of saving;* third, what *reason moves to saving.*

(Verse 13.). The principle that saves us is he who alone can enter into heaven by himself, and all others through him; and this is Christ the Lord. On account of this he says: *And no man hath ascended into heaven,* that is, no man has the virtue of ascending thither; *but he that descended from heaven,* through the assumption of humanity; *the Son of man who is in heaven,* through the presence and immensity of the Divinity: therefore every one who ascends and is saved ascends through him. Of this power of ascending and descending, to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter[^p282-3]: « He that descended is the same also that ascended above all the heavens, that he might fill all things ». Of his existence in heaven, above, the first chapter: « The only begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared ». He it is who makes men ascend from death to life; below, the eighth chapter: « If the Son shall make you free, you shall be free indeed ».

24. (Verse 14.). *And as Moses lifted up* etc. Here the second thing is touched on, namely *how* the Son of God *saves,* since it is through the passion. Therefore he says: *As Moses lifted up the serpent in the desert;* Numbers, the twenty-first chapter[^p282-4]: « Moses made a brazen serpent and set it up for a sign; which they that were bitten looking upon were healed ». *So must the Son of man be lifted up,* namely on the gibbet of the cross, so that all looking upon him through faith may be healed. Therefore he says:

25. (Verse 15.). *That whosoever believeth in him*[^p282-5] *may not perish, but may have life everlasting;* below, the eleventh chapter: « He that believeth in me, although he be dead, shall live; and every one that liveth and believeth in me shall not die for ever ». Most excellently he describes the passion in relation to the figure, so that it may become more credible; and most excellently did the figure go before, because, just as there none were healed through the serpent except those looking upon it, so neither through Christ except those believing in him.

26. (Verse 16.). *For God so* etc. Here the third thing is touched on, namely *the reason moving* to saving thus; and this was the immensity of the divine love towards lost man. On account of which he says: *For God so loved the world,* that is, worldly and sinful man, *as to give his only begotten Son;* to the Ephesians, the second chapter[^p282-6]: « God, who is rich in mercy, for his exceeding charity wherewith he loved us, when we were dead in sins, hath quickened us together with Christ ». Therefore *exceeding,* because he gave more than was needful; to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p282-7]: « And he that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all ». *He gave,* I say, for all as to *sufficiency,* but as to *efficacy* for the elect and the believing[^p282-8]. Therefore he adds: *That whosoever believeth in him may not perish, but may have life everlasting.* The passion avails others only if they believe, because, as is said to the Hebrews, the eleventh chapter[^p282-9], « without faith it is impossible to please God ».

### Questions.

27. Question I. But a question arises here first about this that he says: *No man hath ascended into heaven, but he that descended from heaven.*

**On the contrary:** 1. All the Saints ascend, and yet none descended.

2. Likewise, the body of Christ ascended into heaven, and yet it did not descend from heaven.

3. Likewise it is objected: if this is understood of Christ, either it is understood according to the *divine* nature, or the *human.* According to the *human,* no; because that did not descend from heaven, and moreover at that time, when he was speaking, it was not in heaven. According to the *divine* it cannot be, because it did not ascend, since it could not be greater nor did it change place.
<!-- page 283 -->

Some respond that the exception here comprehends not only Christ, who is the head, but the whole body of Christ; and all those and only those who belong to his body ascend[^p283-1]. — But this cannot stand, because the body did not descend from heaven[^p283-2].

Therefore it must be understood that *to ascend* is said here of ascending by one's own virtue, not given from elsewhere, and in this way Christ alone could do it[^p283-3].

2. As to what he objects about the body, Augustine responds[^p283-4] that it is no obstacle, because the body is as it were a garment; whence if someone descends from a mountain naked and ascends clothed, we say: no one ascended but he who descended; so also in the matter at hand.

3. As to what he asks, according to which nature this is said, I respond: It must be said that in that statement three things are said of one hypostasis: *to ascend* belongs to him by reason of the human nature; *to descend,* by reason of the divine as related to the human, because he humbled himself by assuming flesh; but *to be in heaven,* as at that time, according to the divine purely. And so the whole cannot be referred back to one nature, but to one hypostasis[^p283-5].

28. Question II. Likewise Chrysostom asks[^p283-6] about this that he says: *As he lifted up* etc.: why, since he expresses the passion, did he not say *to hang on the cross,* so as to speak more openly?

**I respond:** It must be said that the Lord revealed the mystery of the passion to Nicodemus as *honourable,* as *credible,* as *secret:* as *honourable,* lest a man still carnal should despise it and shudder at it; therefore he said *to be lifted up,* not *to be hanged.* As *credible,* so that he might give faith to it; therefore through the figure that went before he intimates the wonderful effect of the passion as wonderful. As *secret,* too, so that he might hold it more dear; and therefore he does not explain it, but leaves him room for meditating, and through this for profiting and for arriving at the gift of understanding[^p283-7].

29. Question III. There is a question about this that he says: *That whosoever believeth in him may not perish.* Therefore no one who has faith will be damned, however evil he may be. — *If thou sayest* that it is understood of formed faith: then according to this no one who has formed faith can be damned.

**I respond:** It must be said that here he speaks of true faith; but true faith excludes a twofold feigning. For faith is feigned either because one believes but *does not love;* or because one believes and loves but *does not persevere.* True faith, therefore, is that which loves and perseveres in what it believes; and every such one will be saved[^p283-8].
