# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. X, Vers. 22-42
## *Ostendit se Dominus verum pastorem esse quoad pastoris ingressum et affectum; hic tertio ostendit quoad veri pastoris indicium, quod consistit in hoc, quod oves eum agnoscunt et sequuntur*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 390 -->

33. *Facta sunt*[^p390-3] *encaenia in Ierosolymis.* Ostendit se Dominus verum pastorem esse quoad pastoris ingressum et affectum; hic tertio ostendit quoad *veri pastoris indicium,* quod consistit in hoc, quod oves eum agnoscunt et sequuntur. Et tria notantur hic: primo, a Iudaeis *certi indicii postulatio,* secundo, *certi indicii expressio,* ibi[^p390-4]: *Respondit eis Iesus: Loquor* etc.; tertio vero, ex hoc *Iudaeorum persecutio,* ibi: *Sustulerunt lapides Iudaei.*

*Veri indicii postulatio,* quod ipse esset pastor et ductor in Israel, fit a Iudaeis quaerentibus, in quorum quaestionis descriptione innuuntur *tempus* et *locus* et *quaerendi modus.*

(Vers. 22.). Quantum ad *tempus* dicit: *Facta sunt encaenia in Ierosolymis; et hiems erat.* Notandum est, quod encaenia dicuntur a *caenon,* quod est *novum,* unde *encaeniare,* id est innovare[^p390-5]; quia tunc celebrabatur festum innovationis templi, prius profanati; et dicitur pluraliter, quia per octo dies durabat ista solemnitas; primi Machabaeorum quarto[^p390-6]: « Statuit Iudas et fratres eius et universa ecclesia Israel, ut agatur dies dedicationis templi ab anno in annum per dies octo ». — Quantum ad *locum* dicit:

34. (Vers. 23.). *Et ambulabat Iesus*[^p390-7] *in porticu Salomonis,* id est in loco, ubi Salomon prius fecerat porticum ad orandum[^p390-8], quae tamen destructa fuit. — Quantum ad *modum quaerendi* dicit:

35. (Vers. 24.). *Circumdederunt ergo eum Iudaei et dicebant ei: Quousque animam nostram tollis?* id est, retines in suspenso[^p390-9]. *Si tu es Christus, dic nobis palam,* certum de te da indicium. Et ut responsionem extorqueant, nituntur et *potentia* et *malitia: potentia,* ideo *circumdederunt;* Psalmus[^p390-10]: « Circumdederunt me vituli multi », ut scilicet non posset effugere; Psalmus: « Circumdederunt me sicut apes et exarserunt sicut ignis in spinis ». *Malitia,* ideo praetendunt desiderium addiscendi: *Quousque animam* etc.; Psalmus[^p390-11]: « Ascendunt usque ad caelos »; Augustinus: « Non veritatem desiderabant, sed calumniam praeparabant ».

36. *Respondit eis Iesus.* Postquam posita est inquisitio, ponitur hic *certi indicii exhibitio,* et hoc quidem per hunc modum. Primo igitur[^p390-12] in Domini responsione ponitur *Iudaeorum arguitio;* secundo, *certi indicii expressio;* tertio, illius *indicii ratio.*

(Vers. 25.). Ponitur *infidelitatis Iudaeorum arguitio;* propter quod dicit: *Loquor vobis, et non creditis,* et ideo stulte et malitiose quaeritis; et aperte loquor, quia non tantum verbo, sed facto: *Opera, quae ego facio in nomine Patris mei, ipsa*[^p390-13] *testimonium perhibent de me;* supra septimo: « Nunquid Christus, cum venerit, plura signa faciet », quibus deberetis credere?

37. (Vers. 26.). *Sed vos non creditis, quia non estis ex ovibus meis;* unde si essent oves, indicium sui pastoris haberent in verbo et opere. Augustinus[^p390-14]: « Eos dicit non esse de ovibus suis, quia videbat eos ad sempiternum interitum ordinatos, non ad vitam aeternam sui sanguinis pretio comparandos ».

38. (Vers. 27.). *Oves meae* etc. Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *certi indicii exhibitio,* quae quidem est in hoc, quod auditur ab ovibus, et oves
<!-- page 391 -->
eum sequuntur, et ipse eas conservat; hoc est certe pastoris. Propterea dicit: *Oves meae vocem meam audiunt,* scilicet credendo, quia « fides ex auditu », ad Romanos decimo[^p391-1]. *Et ego cognosco eas,* a malis discernendo, non facie, sed corde; ad Hebraeos quarto[^p391-2]: « Discretor est cogitationum et intentionum cordis, et non est ulla creatura invisibilis in conspectu eius; omnia autem nuda et aperta sunt oculis eius »; Matthaei vigesimo quinto: « Nescio vos », dicitur de malis; econtra de bonis secundae ad Timotheum secundo: « Novit Dominus qui sunt eius ». *Et sequuntur me,* per imitationem; Matthaei decimo sexto[^p391-3]: « Qui vult venire post me abneget semetipsum et tollat crucem suam et sequatur me ».

39. (Vers. 28.). *Et ego vitam aeternam do eis,* sicut bonus pastor retribuendo; supra sexto[^p391-4]: « Ut omnis, qui videt Filium et credit in eum, habeat vitam aeternam »; Ecclesiastici quarto: « Sapientia filiis suis vitam inspiravit ». *Et non peribunt in aeternum, nec*[^p391-5] *rapiet eas quisquam de manu mea,* propter meam potentiam. Pastor iste recte signatus est per David, qui interpretatur *manu fortis,* qui praedam eripiebat de ore leonis et ursi, sicut dicitur primi Regum decimo septimo.

40. (Vers. 29.). *Pater meus quod dedit mihi.* Notatur hic tertium, scilicet illius *indicii approbatio,* cuius duplex ratio assignatur. Prima talis est: quod Pater dedit mihi maius omnibus est; sed quod maius est omnibus non patitur violentiam: ergo impossibile est, quod aliquis rapiat de manu mea. Huius rationis ponit *medium,* cum dicit: *Pater meus quod dedit mihi,* id est, illud quod dedit mihi Pater meus, *maius omnibus est,* quia dedit totum, quod potuit; infra decimo sexto[^p391-6]: « Omnia, quae habet Pater, mea sunt ». Alia ratio: nullus potest rapere de manu Patris; sed una est manus mea et Patris: ergo nullus de manu mea rapere potest. Propterea dicit: *Nemo*[^p391-7] *potest rapere de manu Patris mei,* quia omnipotens est; Psalmus: « Tu terribilis es, et quis resistet tibi »? Ecclesiastae octavo: « Sermo eius potestate plenus est ».

41. (Vers. 30.). *Ego et Pater unum sumus; unum,* scilicet in essentiae unitate; *sumus,* in personarum pluralitate; ideo una est manus mea et Patris. Chrysostomus[^p391-8]: « Cum *manum* audis, nihil sensibile aestimes, sed tantum virtutem et potentiam intelligas ».

42. *Sustulerunt lapides.* Postquam facta est veri pastoris per certum indicium declaratio, tangitur *malarum ovium,* immo luporum *persecutio;* quia, sicut oves pastorem sequuntur, ita lupi persequuntur. Et determinatur persecutio hoc ordine: primo, *ipsa persecutio;* secundo, *persequendi ratio;* tertio, *prudens Christi excusatio;* quarto, *declinatio furoris persequentium;* quinto, *multiplicatio credentium.*

(Vers. 31.). Primo *ipsa persecutio* tangitur in hoc, quod Christum lapidare volebant; propter quod dicit: *Sustulerunt*[^p391-9] *lapides Iudaei, ut lapidarent eum;* sed non habuerunt effectum; Augustinus[^p391-10]: « Quia nondum venerat tempus passionis, non pervenerunt ad effectum lapidationis ». Statim voluerunt occidere, quemadmodum illi, de quibus Proverbiorum primo[^p391-11]: « Pedes eorum ad mala currunt, et festinant, ut effundant sanguinem ».

43. (Vers. 32.). *Respondit eis Iesus.* Notatur hic secundum, scilicet *persecutionis ratio.* Et primo Dominus ostendit, eos irrationabiliter moveri; propterea dicit: *Multa bona opera ostendi vobis ex Patre meo; propter quod eorum opus me lapidatis?* Id est, propter quod opus eorum, id est de numero eorum; partitiva est constructio; Psalmus[^p391-12]: « Retribuebant mihi mala pro bonis »; Proverbiorum decimo septimo: « Qui reddit mala pro bonis, non recedet malum de domo eius ». Sed malitia, quae latitat sub pelle ovina, rationem assignat; unde:
<!-- page 392 -->

44. (Vers. 33.). *Responderunt ergo*[^p392-1] *ei Iudaei: De bono opere non lapidamus te, sed de blasphemia,* quia, Levitici vigesimo quarto, « qui blasphemaverit nomen Domini morte moriatur ». Et ostendunt, in quo blasphemat: *Et quia tu, homo cum sis, facis te ipsum Deum;* et sic homo, faciens se Deum, blasphemat, quia plures dicit esse deos. Et hoc est contra Iudaeorum Legem, in qua praeceptum erat unum solum Deum credere et confiteri. De consimili, Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p392-2]: « Audistis blasphemiam! quid adhuc egemus testibus »? Non se fecit de homine Deum, sed cum Deus esset, factus est homo; ad Philippenses secundo: « Non rapinam arbitratus est, esse se aequalem Deo; sed semetipsum exinanivit formam servi accipiens, in similitudinem hominum factus et habitu inventus ut homo ».

45. (Vers. 34.). *Respondit eis Iesus.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *prudens sui excusatio,* in qua Dominus ostendit, se non blasphemasse, duplici testimonio, scilicet *Scripturarum* et *operum.* Testimonio *Scripturarum* sic: Scriptura vocat *deos,* ad quos Deus locutus est; sed multo magis dicendus est *deus,* quem Pater sanctificavit et misit: ergo si Scriptura non blasphemat, nec ego. *Nonne scriptum est in Lege vestra*[^p392-3]*: Quia ego dixi: dii estis? Lex* hic communiter accipitur, prout comprehendit Psalmos, in quibus scriptum est.

46. (Vers. 35.). *Si illos dixit deos, ad quos sermo Dei factus est,* ut Prophetas et alios Sanctos; Ezechielis primo[^p392-4]: « Et factum est verbum Domini ad Ezechielem sacerdotem » etc.; *et non potest solvi Scriptura,* id est falsitatis argui, vel de blasphemia reprehendi; Matthaei quinto: « Iota unum, aut unus apex non praeteribit a Lege, donec omnia fiant ».

47. (Vers. 36.). *Quem Pater sanctificavit,* id est, « sanctum genuit[^p392-5] », *et misit in mundum,* non vocavit de mundo et tenebris mundi, ut alios Sanctos; *vos dicitis: Quia blasphemas, quia dixi: Filius Dei sum?* Quasi dicat: irrationabiliter dicitis. « Si enim *illuminati* dii sunt, nonne *lumen,* quod illuminat, Deus est[^p392-6] »? Non Dominus blasphemabat, sed ipsi, qui de Deo dicebant, non esse Deum. Unde Dominus dixit Matthaei duodecimo: « Omne peccatum et blasphemia remittetur hominibus; spiritus autem blasphemia non remittetur, neque in hoc saeculo neque in futuro »; unde omnes erant lapidandi. — Deinde ostendit hoc idem, quod non blasphemavit, testimonio *operum,* quae ipsum Patri aequalem dicunt[^p392-7]. Propterea dicit:

48. (Vers. 37.). *Si non facio opera Patris mei,* scilicet opera omnipotentiae; *nolite credere mihi,* dicenti scilicet, me esse aequalem Patri; Exodi quarto[^p392-8] dixit Moyses: « Non credent mihi et non audient vocem meam, sed dicent » etc.; et ideo Dominus dedit ei signa.

49. (Vers. 38.). *Si autem facio, et si mihi non vultis credere,* id est sermoni meo; *operibus credite,* id est, per opera in me credite, *ut credatis et cognoscatis, quia Pater in me est, et ego in Patre;* et ita verum esse quod supra[^p392-9] dixi: « Ego et Pater unum sumus ». *Ut credatis,* id est, credendo *cognoscatis,* quia, secundum quod dicit Isaias septimo[^p392-10], secundum aliam litteram, « nisi credideritis, non intelligetis »; infra decimo quarto dicit Philippo: « Non credis, quia ego in Patre, et Pater in me »? « Alioquin propter opera ipsa credite ».

50. (Vers. 39.). *Quaerebant ergo eum.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *declinatio persequentium,* quia nondum tempus advenerat patiendi; ideo dicit: *Quaerebant ergo eum apprehendere,* sed non potuerunt; unde dicit: *Et exivit de manibus eorum.* Simile dicitur Lucae quarto[^p392-11]: « Ipse autem transiens per medium illorum, ibat », quando voluerunt eum praecipitare.

51. (Vers. 40.). *Et abiit*[^p392-12] *trans Iordanem in eum locum, ubi erat Ioannes baptizans primum,* de quo loco dicitur supra tertio: « Erat Ioannes baptizans in Aennon ». *Et mansit illic,* in quiete scilicet; Chrysostomus[^p392-13]: « Monet tumultus fugere et in quiete orare »; *mansit,* absconditus a Iudaeis, secundum illud Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo: « Abscondam faciem meam ab eis ».
<!-- page 393 -->

52. (Vers. 41.). *Et multi venerunt* etc. Tangitur hic ultimum, scilicet ex ipsius recessu *multiplicatio fidelium;* propter quod dicit: *Et multi venerunt ad eum,* scilicet per fidem; Isaiae sexagesimo[^p393-1]: « Filii tui de longe venient »; *et dicebant: Quia Ioannes nullum quidem signum fecit;* propter quod ostenderet, se esse Christum; sed tantum testimonium de Christo perhibuit[^p393-2].

53. (Vers. 42.). *Omnia autem, quaecumque dixit Ioannes de hoc, vera erant. Et,* ita isto duplici testimonio inducti, *multi crediderunt in eum,* secundum quod ipse Dominus supra eodem[^p393-3] dixerat: « Et si mihi non creditis, saltem operibus credite »; *multi,* secundum illud Psalmi: « Dinumerabo eos, et super arenam multiplicabuntur »; et Genesis decimo septimo: « Eris pater multarum gentium ».

### Quaestiones.

54. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic de hoc quod dicit: *Nemo rapiet eas de manu mea.* — Nonne sunt in manu Filii qui habent *praesentem iustitiam?* Qui tamen disperguntur, secundum illud quod supra[^p393-4] dictum est: *Lupus venit et dispergit oves.*

Respondetur ad hoc, quod rapiuntur non ex *imbecillitate custodientis*[^p393-5], sed ex *infirmitate custoditi.* — Vel secundum Augustinum *oves* hic dicuntur secundum *aeternam electionem,* qui non possunt rapi finaliter. Unde Augustinus[^p393-6]: « Nec lupus rapit, nec fur tollit, nec latro interficit; securus est de numero earum qui eas numeravit ».

55. Quaest. II. Sed tunc quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Nemo potest rapere de manu Patris mei.* — Ergo qui sunt in manu Dei, ut praedestinati, non possunt damnari: ergo non habent libertatem peccandi finaliter.

Ad hoc videtur dicere Augustinus simpliciter, quod non possunt *perire,* in libro de Bono perseverantiae[^p393-7]; nec tamen perit liberum arbitrium, sicut etiam non perit in confirmatis. — Dicendum tamen, quod nemo potest *rapere,* quia Pater electis suis non permittit violentiam fieri; unde[^p393-8]: *fidelis Deus, qui non permittet, vos tentari supra id quod potestis;* et diabolus non potest Deo violentiam facere, ut servos suos tentet ultra, quam ipse permittat. Manet ergo in eis liberi arbitrii *libertas ad peccandum,* sed non manet in diabolo *potestas ad violentandum* et vi surripiendum[^p393-9].

56. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de responsione Domini: quia Dominus se dixerat Deum et Dei Filium, ostendit, quod non blasphemavit, per Scripturam, quae servos Dei[^p393-10] *deos* vocat. — Ista responsio videtur nihil valere, quia Scriptura vocat illos *deos per adoptionem;* sed ipse se dicebat *Deum per essentiam;* unde dicebat: *Ego et Pater unum sumus.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum ad hoc, quod quando Dominus disputabat cum Iudaeis, aliquando respondebat *ad orationem,* aliquando respondebat *ad hominem,* eo quod « omnino adversanti omnino adversandum[^p393-11] ». Unde quando ipsi petierunt, *in qua potestate* faceret, et Dominus respondit: *Baptismus Ioannis e caelo erat, an ex hominibus?* eos sua interrogatione ligavit, Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p393-12]. Sic et nunc respondet *ad hominem,* ut mitiget eorum iracundiam, sicut supra octavo confutavit malitiam: *Qui sine peccato est vestrum, primus in eam lapidem mittat.*

Potest tamen dici, quod revera optime respondit, quia, cum hoc nomen *deus* dicatur tripliciter: *natura, participatione* et *nuncupatione,* ut in idolis[^p393-13]; ista multiplicitas habet ortum ab una radice,
<!-- page 394 -->
sicut multiplicitas huius nominis *sanum.* Unde verissimo modo dicitur *Deus* per *naturam,* minus vero modo dicendi per *participationem,* minime per *nuncupationem,* quia secundum solam aestimationem. Quia ergo calumniabantur ex hoc, quod nomen *dei* sibi attribuerat; probat, quod sibi potest sine blasphemia attribuere; quia, si amicus vel filius Dei adoptivus *deus* dicitur, et vere Deus honoratur sine blasphemia; multo magis ille qui est Filius per naturam; et est locus *a minori*[^p394-1].

57. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicunt: *Ioannes quidem nullum signum fecit:*

1. Quia dicitur Lucae primo[^p394-2]: *Ipse praecedet ante eum in spiritu et virtute Eliae:* ergo si virtus Eliae fuit in miracula faciendo, videtur, quod Dominus debuerit ei dare potestatem miraculorum.

2. Item, si non fuit *maior Ioanne* quisquam[^p394-3], et Dominus discipulis de ipso testificantibus potentiam dedit miraculorum; quare non similiter et Ioanni?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Ioannes a pluribus aestimabatur esse Christus, sicut patet ex primo capitulo[^p394-4]; ideo quantum ad homines sensibiles debuit differentia sensibilis esse inter Ioannem et Christum, ut possent discernere; sed non debuit in *sanctitate vitae,* quia necesse est, testem fidelem sanctum reputari: ergo oportuit, quod esset in *miraculorum virtute*[^p394-5].

1. Quod obiicitur de *spiritu et virtute,* dicendum, quod verbum Eliae erat igneum; sic et verbum Ioannis, sicut ipse Dominus dixit supra quinto[^p394-6]: *Ipse erat lucerna ardens et lucens.*

2. Quod obiicitur de discipulis, dicendum, quod de nullo discipulorum erat suspicio, quod ipse esset Christus; et quia omnibus constabat, quod Christum secuti fuerunt, totum, quod erat in eis, et sanctitatis et virtutis et scientiae, Magistro attribuebant[^p394-7]. Sed Ioannes non fuerat Dominum secutus nec eius discipulus.

## English
<!-- page 390 -->

33. *The encaenia were held*[^p390-3] *at Jerusalem.* The Lord has shown himself to be the true shepherd as to the shepherd's entrance and affection; here, third, he shows it as to *the true shepherd's mark,* which consists in this, that the sheep acknowledge him and follow him. And three things are noted here: first, the *asking for a sure mark* by the Jews; second, the *expression of the sure mark,* there[^p390-4]: *Jesus answered them: I speak* etc.; but third, the *persecution of the Jews* arising from this, there: *The Jews took up stones.*

The *asking for the true mark,* whether he himself were the shepherd and leader in Israel, is made by the Jews inquiring, in the description of whose question are intimated the *time* and the *place* and the *manner of inquiring.*

(V. 22.). As to the *time* he says: *The encaenia were held at Jerusalem; and it was winter.* It is to be noted that the encaenia are so called from *caenon,* which is *new,* whence *encaeniare,* that is, to renew[^p390-5]; because at that time was celebrated the feast of the renewal of the temple, which had before been profaned; and it is said in the plural, because that solemnity lasted through eight days; the first of Machabees, the fourth chapter[^p390-6]: « Judas and his brethren and all the church of Israel decreed that the day of the dedication of the temple should be kept from year to year for eight days ». — As to the *place* he says:

34. (V. 23.). *And Jesus walked*[^p390-7] *in Solomon's porch,* that is, in the place where Solomon had before made a porch for praying[^p390-8], which nevertheless was destroyed. — As to the *manner of inquiring* he says:

35. (V. 24.). *The Jews therefore came round about him, and said to him: How long dost thou hold up our soul?* that is, thou keepest it in suspense[^p390-9]. *If thou be the Christ, tell us plainly,* give a sure mark of thyself. And to wring out an answer they strive both by *power* and by *malice: by power,* therefore *they came round about;* the Psalm[^p390-10]: « Many calves have surrounded me », namely so that he could not escape; the Psalm: « They surrounded me like bees, and they burned like fire among thorns ». *By malice,* therefore they pretend a desire of learning: *How long dost thou hold up our soul* etc.; the Psalm[^p390-11]: « They mount up to the heavens »; Augustine: « They did not desire the truth, but were preparing a calumny ».

36. *Jesus answered them.* After the inquiry has been set down, here is set down the *showing of the sure mark,* and this indeed in this way. First, then[^p390-12], in the Lord's answer is set down the *reproving of the Jews;* second, the *expression of the sure mark;* third, the *reason* of that *mark.*

(V. 25.). There is set down the *reproving of the Jews' unbelief;* on account of which he says: *I speak to you, and you believe not,* and therefore you ask foolishly and maliciously; and I speak openly, because not only by word, but by deed: *The works that I do in the name of my Father, they*[^p390-13] *give testimony of me;* above, in the seventh chapter: « When the Christ cometh, shall he do more signs », by which you ought to believe?

37. (V. 26.). *But you do not believe, because you are not of my sheep;* whence, if they were sheep, they would have the mark of their shepherd in word and work. Augustine[^p390-14]: « He says that they are not of his sheep, because he saw them ordained to everlasting destruction, not to be purchased for life eternal at the price of his blood ».

38. (V. 27.). *My sheep* etc. Here the second is touched on, namely the *showing of the sure mark,* which indeed is in this, that he is heard by the sheep, and the sheep
<!-- page 391 -->
follow him, and he preserves them; this is surely the shepherd's. Therefore he says: *My sheep hear my voice,* namely by believing, because « faith cometh by hearing », to the Romans, the tenth chapter[^p391-1]. *And I know them,* by discerning them from the wicked, not by the face, but by the heart; to the Hebrews, the fourth chapter[^p391-2]: « He is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart, and there is not any creature invisible in his sight; but all things are naked and open to his eyes »; Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter: « I know you not », is said of the wicked; on the contrary, of the good, the second to Timothy, the second chapter: « The Lord knoweth who are his ». *And they follow me,* by imitation; Matthew, the sixteenth chapter[^p391-3]: « He that will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me ».

39. (V. 28.). *And I give them life everlasting,* as a good shepherd by repaying; above, in the sixth chapter[^p391-4]: « That every one who seeth the Son and believeth in him may have life everlasting »; Ecclesiasticus, the fourth chapter: « Wisdom hath inspired life into her children ». *And they shall not perish for ever, nor*[^p391-5] *shall any man pluck them out of my hand,* on account of my power. This shepherd was rightly signified by David, which is interpreted *strong of hand,* who snatched the prey from the mouth of the lion and of the bear, as is said in the first of Kings, the seventeenth chapter.

40. (V. 29.). *That which my Father hath given me.* Here the third is noted, namely the *approving* of that *mark,* of which a twofold reason is assigned. The first is such: what the Father hath given me is greater than all; but what is greater than all does not suffer violence: therefore it is impossible that anyone snatch out of my hand. Of this reason he sets down the *middle term,* when he says: *That which my Father hath given me,* that is, that which my Father hath given me, *is greater than all,* because he gave all that he could; below, in the sixteenth chapter[^p391-6]: « All things whatsoever the Father hath are mine ». The other reason: no one can snatch out of the hand of the Father; but my hand and the Father's are one: therefore no one can snatch out of my hand. Therefore he says: *No one*[^p391-7] *can snatch out of the hand of my Father,* because he is almighty; the Psalm: « Thou art terrible, and who shall resist thee »? Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter: « His word is full of power ».

41. (V. 30.). *I and the Father are one; one,* namely in the unity of essence; *are,* in the plurality of persons; therefore my hand and the Father's are one. Chrysostom[^p391-8]: « When thou hearest *hand,* think of nothing sensible, but understand only virtue and power ».

42. *They took up stones.* After the declaration of the true shepherd by a sure mark has been made, there is touched on the *persecution* of the wicked sheep, nay rather, of the wolves; because, as the sheep follow the shepherd, so the wolves persecute him. And the persecution is set out in this order: first, *the persecution itself;* second, *the reason for persecuting;* third, *Christ's prudent excusing;* fourth, *the evading of the persecutors' fury;* fifth, *the multiplying of believers.*

(V. 31.). First *the persecution itself* is touched on in this, that they wished to stone Christ; on account of which he says: *The Jews took up*[^p391-9] *stones to stone him;* but they had not the effect; Augustine[^p391-10]: « Because the time of the passion had not yet come, they did not attain to the effect of the stoning ». They wished to kill him at once, like those of whom Proverbs, the first chapter[^p391-11]: « Their feet run to evil, and they make haste to shed blood ».

43. (V. 32.). *Jesus answered them.* Here the second is noted, namely the *reason of the persecution.* And first the Lord shows that they are moved without reason; therefore he says: *Many good works I have shewed you from my Father; for which work of them do you stone me?* That is, for which work of them, that is, of their number; the construction is partitive; the Psalm[^p391-12]: « They repaid me evil for good »; Proverbs, the seventeenth chapter: « He that rendereth evil for good, evil shall not depart from his house ». But malice, which lurks under the sheep's skin, assigns the reason; whence:
<!-- page 392 -->

44. (V. 33.). *The Jews therefore*[^p392-1] *answered him: For a good work we stone thee not, but for blasphemy,* because, Leviticus, the twenty-fourth chapter, « he that blasphemeth the name of the Lord, dying let him die ». And they show in what he blasphemes: *And because thou, being a man, makest thyself God;* and so a man, making himself God, blasphemes, because he says that there are many gods. And this is against the Law of the Jews, in which it was a precept to believe and confess one only God. Of the like, Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p392-2]: « You have heard the blasphemy! what further need have we of witnesses »? He did not make himself God out of man, but, since he was God, he was made man; to the Philippians, the second chapter: « He thought it not robbery to be equal with God; but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, being made in the likeness of men, and in habit found as a man ».

45. (V. 34.). *Jesus answered them.* Here the third is touched on, namely his *prudent excusing* of himself, in which the Lord shows that he did not blaspheme, by a twofold testimony, namely of the *Scriptures* and of the *works.* By the testimony of the *Scriptures* thus: Scripture calls those *gods* to whom God spoke; but much more is he to be called *god* whom the Father sanctified and sent: therefore if Scripture does not blaspheme, neither do I. *Is it not written in your Law*[^p392-3]*: I said, you are gods? Law* is here taken commonly, as it includes the Psalms, in which it is written.

46. (V. 35.). *If he called them gods, to whom the word of God was spoken,* as the Prophets and other Saints; Ezechiel, the first chapter[^p392-4]: « And the word of the Lord came to Ezechiel the priest » etc.; *and the Scripture cannot be broken,* that is, be convicted of falsity, or be reproved of blasphemy; Matthew, the fifth chapter: « One jot, or one tittle shall not pass of the Law, till all be fulfilled ».

47. (V. 36.). *Whom the Father hath sanctified,* that is, « begot holy[^p392-5] », *and sent into the world,* not called out of the world and the darkness of the world, as the other Saints; *do you say: Thou blasphemest, because I said: I am the Son of God?* As if he should say: you say it without reason. « For if the *illuminated* are gods, is not the *lumen*[^tr-lumen] which illuminates God[^p392-6] »? The Lord was not blaspheming, but they, who said of God that he was not God. Whence the Lord said, Matthew, the twelfth chapter: « Every sin and blasphemy shall be forgiven men; but the blasphemy of the Spirit shall not be forgiven, neither in this world nor in the world to come »; whence all were to be stoned. — Then he shows this same thing, that he did not blaspheme, by the testimony of the *works,* which declare him equal to the Father[^p392-7]. Therefore he says:

48. (V. 37.). *If I do not the works of my Father,* namely the works of omnipotence; *believe me not,* namely when I say that I am equal to the Father; Exodus, the fourth chapter[^p392-8], Moses said: « They will not believe me, nor hear my voice, but they will say » etc.; and therefore the Lord gave him signs.

49. (V. 38.). *But if I do, though you will not believe me,* that is, my word; *believe the works,* that is, through the works believe in me, *that you may believe and know that the Father is in me, and I in the Father;* and so that what I said above[^p392-9] is true: « I and the Father are one ». *That you may believe,* that is, by believing *may know,* because, according to what Isaias says, the seventh chapter[^p392-10], according to another text, « unless you believe, you shall not understand »; below, in the fourteenth chapter, he says to Philip: « Do you not believe that I am in the Father, and the Father in me »? « Otherwise believe for the very works' sake ».

50. (V. 39.). *They sought therefore for him.* Here the fourth is touched on, namely the *evading of the persecutors,* because the time of suffering had not yet come; therefore he says: *They sought therefore to take him,* but they could not; whence he says: *And he escaped out of their hands.* The like is said in Luke, the fourth chapter[^p392-11]: « But he passing through the midst of them, went his way », when they wished to cast him down headlong.

51. (V. 40.). *And he went*[^p392-12] *beyond the Jordan, into that place where John was baptizing first,* of which place it is said above, in the third chapter: « John was baptizing in Aennon ». *And there he abode,* namely in quiet; Chrysostom[^p392-13]: « He counsels us to flee tumult and to pray in quiet »; *he abode,* hidden from the Jews, according to that of Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter: « I will hide my face from them ».
<!-- page 393 -->

52. (V. 41.). *And many came* etc. Here the last is touched on, namely, from his withdrawal, the *multiplying of the faithful;* on account of which he says: *And many came to him,* namely through faith; Isaias, the sixtieth chapter[^p393-1]: « Thy sons shall come from afar »; *and they said: John indeed did no sign;* by which he might show that he was the Christ; but he only gave testimony of Christ[^p393-2].

53. (V. 42.). *But all things whatsoever John said of this man were true. And,* being thus led on by this twofold testimony, *many believed in him,* according to what the Lord himself had said above, in the same chapter[^p393-3]: « And if you believe not me, at least believe the works »; *many,* according to that of the Psalm: « I will number them, and they shall be multiplied above the sand »; and Genesis, the seventeenth chapter: « Thou shalt be a father of many nations ».

### Questions.

54. Quaest. I. But it is asked here about what he says: *No man shall pluck them out of my hand.* — Are not they in the hand of the Son who have *present justice?* Who nevertheless are scattered, according to that which was said above[^p393-4]: *The wolf cometh and scattereth the sheep.*

It is responded to this that they are snatched not from the *weakness of the one guarding*[^p393-5], but from the *infirmity of the one guarded.* — Or, according to Augustine, *sheep* are here so called according to *eternal election,* who cannot be snatched finally. Whence Augustine[^p393-6]: « Neither does the wolf snatch, nor the thief take away, nor the robber kill; he is secure of their number who numbered them ».

55. Quaest. II. But then it is asked about what he says: *No one can snatch out of the hand of my Father.* — Then those who are in the hand of God, as the predestined, cannot be damned: therefore they have not the liberty of sinning finally.

To this Augustine seems to say simply that they cannot *perish,* in the book *On the Gift of Perseverance*[^p393-7]; nor yet does free choice perish, as it does not perish in the confirmed either. — It must, however, be said that no one can *snatch,* because the Father does not permit violence to be done to his elect; whence[^p393-8]: *God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that which you are able;* and the devil cannot do violence to God, so as to tempt his servants beyond what he himself permits. There remains, then, in them the *liberty* of free choice *for sinning,* but there does not remain in the devil the *power for doing violence* and for carrying off by force[^p393-9].

56. Quaest. III. Again it is asked about the Lord's answer: because the Lord had called himself God and the Son of God, he shows that he did not blaspheme through the Scripture, which calls the servants of God[^p393-10] *gods.* — That answer seems to be worth nothing, because Scripture calls them *gods by adoption;* but he called himself *God by essence;* whence he said: *I and the Father are one.*

**I respond:** It must be said to this that when the Lord disputed with the Jews, sometimes he answered *to the argument,* sometimes he answered *to the man,* for this reason, that « one who opposes altogether is altogether to be opposed[^p393-11] ». Whence, when they asked *by what authority* he did these things, and the Lord answered: *Was the baptism of John from heaven, or from men?* he bound them by his own question, Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p393-12]. So now too he answers *to the man,* to soften their anger, as above, in the eighth chapter, he confuted their malice: *He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone at her.*

It can, however, be said that in truth he answered excellently, because, since this name *god* is said in three ways: by *nature,* by *participation* and by *nuncupation,* as in idols[^p393-13]; this multiplicity has its rise from one root,
<!-- page 394 -->
as the multiplicity of this name *healthy.* Whence one is called *God* most truly by *nature,* but in a less true mode of speaking by *participation,* least of all by *nuncupation,* because it is according to opinion alone. Since, then, they made a calumny of this, that he had attributed the name *god* to himself; he proves that he can attribute it to himself without blasphemy; because, if a friend or an adopted son of God is called *god,* and is truly honoured as God without blasphemy; much more he who is the Son by nature; and it is an argument *from the lesser*[^p394-1].

57. Quaest. IV. Again it is asked about what they say: *John indeed did no sign:*

1. Because it is said in Luke, the first chapter[^p394-2]: *He shall go before him in the spirit and virtue of Elias:* therefore, if the virtue of Elias lay in doing miracles, it seems that the Lord ought to have given him the power of miracles.

2. Again, if there was not anyone *greater than John*[^p394-3], and the Lord gave the power of miracles to the disciples who testified of him; why not likewise to John also?

**I respond:** It must be said that John was thought by many to be the Christ, as is plain from the first chapter[^p394-4]; therefore, as regards men of sense, there ought to have been a sensible difference between John and Christ, so that they might discern; but it ought not to have been in *holiness of life,* because it is necessary that a faithful witness be reputed holy: therefore it was fitting that it be in the *virtue of miracles*[^p394-5].

1. As to what is objected about *the spirit and virtue,* it must be said that the word of Elias was fiery; so too the word of John, as the Lord himself said above, in the fifth chapter[^p394-6]: *He was a lamp burning and shining.*

2. As to what is objected about the disciples, it must be said that there was no suspicion of any of the disciples that he was the Christ; and because it was established for all that they had followed Christ, all that was in them, of holiness and of virtue and of knowledge, they attributed to their Teacher[^p394-7]. But John had not followed the Lord, nor was he his disciple.
