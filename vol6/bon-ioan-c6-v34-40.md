# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. VI, Vers. 34-40
## *Excitavit Dominus Iudaeos ad quaerendam spiritualem refectionem, hic iam excitatis et petentibus tribuit instructionem*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 327 -->

54. *Dixerunt ergo ad eum.* Excitavit Dominus Iudaeos ad quaerendam spiritualem refectionem, hic iam excitatis et petentibus *tribuit instructionem.* Et quoniam Christus est panis reficiens secundum divinam naturam et secundum humanam, ideo haec pars habet duas. In prima agitur de refectione huius caelestis panis secundum *divinam* naturam; in secunda, quantum ad *humanam,* ibi[^p327-11]: *Et panis, quem ego dabo, caro mea est* etc.

Prima pars habet duas partes. In prima ponitur *instructio* ad Iudaeorum petitionem; in secunda vero, *instructionis confirmatio* propter Iudaeorum murmurationem, ibi[^p327-12]: *Murmurabant ergo Iudaei* etc.

*Instruit* igitur Iudaeos de huius panis caelestis refectione hoc ordine: primo ostendit, *quis sit* iste panis reficiens; secundo vero, *qui* hoc pane *reficiuntur;* tertio, *quare* illos et non alios *reficit.*

55. (Vers. 34.). Primo igitur ad Iudaeorum petitionem docet, *quis sit* iste panis reficiens; propter quod introducit Iudaeos petentes: *Dixerunt ergo ad eum,* Iudaei scilicet: *Domine, semper da nobis hunc panem;* quia eis promiserat panem *vivum,* ideo petunt, ut non esuriant, sicut mulier aquam; supra quarto[^p327-13]: « Domine, da mihi hanc aquam, ut non sitiam neque veniam huc haurire ». Sic igitur petentibus Dominus *spiritualem* panem ostendit.

56. (Vers. 35.). *Dixit autem eis Iesus: Ego sum panis vitae,* id est panis *dans vitam;* quia ipse est Sapientia, de qua Ecclesiastici quarto[^p327-14]: « Sapientia filiis suis vitam inspirat »; nec tantum est vitam dans, immo etiam *satians* et affectum et intellectum; unde dicit: *Qui venit ad me,* per caritatem scilicet, *non esuriet;* Matthaei undecimo: « Venite ad me omnes, qui laboratis et onerati estis, et ego reficiam vos ». *Et qui credit in me non sitiet in aeternum*[^p327-15], quia, sicut dicitur supra quarto, « qui biberit ex aqua, quam ego dabo ei, non
<!-- page 328 -->
sitiet in aeternum; sed aqua, quam ego dabo, fiet in eo fons aquae salientis in vitam aeternam ». — Et notandum, quod *caritas pascit,* quia est in affectu, qui magis unitur; *fides potat,* quia respicit actum intellectus, qui minus unitur. Et propterea, quemadmodum potus ducit cibum per membra, sic fides et operatio intellectus viam dirigit virtutibus existentibus in affectu, in quo est cibatio[^p328-1].

57. (Vers. 36.). *Sed dixi vobis.* Hic notatur secundum, *qui* scilicet *hoc pane reficiuntur:* quia non increduli, sed fideles a Deo electi et vocati. Propterea dicit: Ita dixi, quod « qui venit ad me non esuriet »; sed vos, quia non creditis, refici non potestis; unde ait: *Sed dixi vobis: Quia et vidistis me et non creditis.* Supra tertio[^p328-2] dixit: « Nos quod scimus loquimur, et quod vidimus testamur, et testimonium nostrum non accipitis »; item supra quinto: « Quomodo vos potestis credere, qui gloriam ab invicem accipitis, et gloriam, quae a solo Deo est, non quaeritis » ? Item supra eodem: « Quaeritis me, non quia vidistis signa, sed quia de panibus meis manducastis ». Et quia *non creditis,* refici non potestis. Unde Beda[^p328-3]: « Panem de caelo desideratis? Ante vos habetis et non comeditis », quia non creditis; sed illi comedunt, qui electi a Deo sunt et vocati credunt. Unde dicit:

58. (Vers. 37.). *Omne, quod dat mihi Pater,* per aeternam electionem et temporalem vocationem; *ad me veniet,* per fidem et dilectionem. De hac *datione* in Psalmo[^p328-4]: « Postula a me, et dabo tibi gentes hereditatem tuam ». Has postulavit Dominus infra decimo septimo: « Pater, quos dedisti mihi, serva eos in nomine tuo » etc. De isto *adventu* ad Christum dicitur Isaiae secundo: « Venite, ascendamus ad montem Domini et ad domum Dei Iacob, et docebit nos vias suas ». Ad hunc adventum hortatur Isaias quinquagesimo quinto: « Omnes sitientes, venite ad aquas ». Sic venientes reficit; unde et subdit: *Et eum qui venit ad me, non eiiciam foras; non eiiciam,* sed reficiam; *non eiiciam foras,* sed *introducam,* iuxta illud Canticorum secundo[^p328-5]: « Introduxit me in cellam vinariam » etc. — Vel: *non eiiciam* eum, sed *intrabo* ad eum; Apocalypsis tertio: « Ecce, ego sto ad ostium et pulso; si quis audierit vocem meam et aperuerit mihi ianuam, introibo ad eum et coenabo cum illo ». Et in hoc ostenditur Dei *iustitia,* quia non reficit nisi venientes, et *benignitas,* quia omnes suscipit et nullum abiicit.

59. (Vers. 38.). *Quia descendi de caelo* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, *quare* scilicet *reficit* tantum praedestinatos et a Deo vocatos. Ratio est conformitas voluntatis suae ad divinam; propterea dicit: *Quia descendi de caelo,* carnem assumendo, *non ut faciam voluntatem meam,* a Patre discordando; *sed voluntatem eius qui misit me,* per omnia ipsi obediendo; unde ad Philippenses secundo[^p328-6]: « Factus est obediens » Patri « usque ad mortem, mortem autem crucis »; supra quinto: « Non quaero voluntatem meam, sed voluntatem eius qui misit me ». Sed voluntas Patris est, ut electi et vocati hoc pane reficiantur; propter quod subdit:

60. (Vers. 39.). *Haec est enim voluntas Patris mei, qui misit me*[^p328-7]*, ut omne, quod dedit mihi,* per electionem; ad Romanos octavo: « Quos praescivit et praedestinavit conformes fieri imaginis Filii sui »; *non perdam ex eo,* per reprobationem, iuxta illud primae ad Corinthios primo: « Perdam sapientiam sapientium et prudentiam prudentium reprobabo ». *Sed resuscitem illud in novissimo die,* ad aeternae beatitudinis participationem; ad Romanos octavo[^p328-8]: « Si Spiritus eius qui suscitavit Iesum a mortuis, habitat in vobis; qui suscitavit Iesum Christum a mortuis vivificabit et mortalia corpora vestra ». Hanc voluntatem implevit pro parte; infra decimo octavo: « Pater, quos dedisti mihi, non perdidi ex eis quemquam ». Et quia sic eligit nos Deus, ut per merita fidei beatitudinem consequamur; ut ostendat arbitrii nostri cooperationem, iterum repetit:

61. (Vers. 40.). *Haec est autem voluntas Patris mei, qui misit me, ut omnis, qui videt Filium,* secundum humanitatem, *et credit in eum,* per sui arbitrii libertatem, quia, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p328-9], « non potest homo credere nisi volens »; *habeat vitam aeternam,* in praemium; *et ego resuscitabo eum in novissimo die,* et ita hanc vitam consequetur per Filium; ad Ephesios secundo[^p328-10]: « Cum essemus mortui peccatis, convivificavit nos in Christo et conresuscitavit et consedere fecit in caelestibus in Christo Iesu »; et hoc *in novissimo die,* quia, sicut dicitur primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto, « novissima omnium destruetur inimica mors ».

### Quaestiones.

62. Quaest. I. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Qui venit ad me non esuriet*[^p328-11].
<!-- page 329 -->

**Contra:** Habetur infra eodem[^p329-1], quod *multi ex discipulis eius abierunt retro;* et constat, quod prius ad ipsum venerunt: ergo falsum dicit.

**Respondeo:** Intelligendum est de illis qui venerunt corde perfecto, non ficto; et isti sunt, qui veniunt cum caritate perseverante; alioquin habentur, ac si non venerint, eo quod *omnes iustitiae in oblivionem dantur*[^p329-2].

63. Quaest. II. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Vidistis me et non creditis.* — Videtur, quod nihil dicat, quia fides est earum rerum quae non videntur hypostasis[^p329-3]: ergo nihil potest eos reprehendere de hoc, quod non credunt, cum videant. — *Si dicas,* quod *videre* secundum humanitatem est, sed *credere* secundum Divinitatem; hoc nihil est, quia visio humanitatis potius impedit fidem Divinitatis, quam adiuvet: ergo non debet eos arguere ex hoc.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod intelligit de visione non humanitatis purae vel infirmae, sed de visione virtutis et potentiae in effectu. Et quoniam videbant divinam virtutem[^p329-4] in effectu, ideo credere debebant.

Quod obiicit, quod visio repugnat fidei; dicendum, quod verum est de visione essentiae, sed non est verum de visione, quae est in effectu tanquam in speculo, quia fidei est videre *per speculum et in aenigmate*[^p329-5].

64. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Omne, quod dedit mihi Pater, non perdam ex eo.*

**Contra:** Infra decimo septimo[^p329-6]: *Nemo ex eis quos dedisti mihi, periit nisi filius perditionis:* ergo aliquis periit ex sibi datis.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod vis potest fieri in hoc quod dicit *dedit,* quia *dare* est dupliciter: vel secundum *praesentem iustitiam,* et sic datus fuit Iudas, et sic periit; vel *dare* est secundum *electionem aeternam,* et sic dati sunt praedestinati, ex quibus nullus perit[^p329-7].

Aliter respondetur, quod fiat vis in hoc quod dicit *perdam,* quia ipse Christus diligentissime custodit; unde nullus perit propter defectum pastoris; sed quia neminem invitum salvat, aliqui pereunt propter malitiam suam. Unde Christus non eos *perdit,* sed ipsi se.

## English
<!-- page 327 -->

54. *They said therefore unto him.* The Lord stirred up the Jews to seek a spiritual refection; here, to those now stirred up and asking, he *grants instruction.* And since Christ is the bread that refreshes according to the divine nature and according to the human, therefore this part has two. In the first is treated the refection of this heavenly bread according to the *divine* nature; in the second, as regards the *human,* at[^p327-11]: *And the bread that I will give, is my flesh* etc.

The first part has two parts. In the first is set down the *instruction* at the petition of the Jews; in the second, the *confirmation of the instruction* on account of the murmuring of the Jews, at[^p327-12]: *The Jews therefore murmured* etc.

He *instructs,* then, the Jews about the refection of this heavenly bread in this order: first he shows *who* this refreshing bread *is;* second, *who* are *refreshed* by this bread; third, *why* he *refreshes* those and not others.

55. (Verse 34.). First, then, at the petition of the Jews he teaches *who* this refreshing bread *is;* on account of which he brings in the Jews asking: *They said therefore unto him,* the Jews, namely: *Lord, give us always this bread;* since he had promised them *living* bread, therefore they ask, that they may not hunger, as the woman asked for water; above, the fourth[^p327-13]: « Sir, give me this water, that I may not thirst, nor come hither to draw ». So then to those asking the Lord shows the *spiritual* bread.

56. (Verse 35.). *And Jesus said to them: I am the bread of life,* that is, the bread *giving life;* since he himself is Wisdom, of whom Ecclesiasticus, the fourth[^p327-14]: « Wisdom inspireth life into her children »; nor is he only giving life, but also *satisfying* both the affection and the understanding; whence he says: *He that cometh to me,* through charity namely, *shall not hunger;* Matthew, the eleventh: « Come to me, all you that labour and are burdened, and I will refresh you ». *And he that believeth in me shall not thirst for ever*[^p327-15], since, as is said above, the fourth, « he that shall drink of the water that I will give him, shall not
<!-- page 328 -->
thirst for ever; but the water that I will give, shall become in him a fountain of water springing up into life everlasting ». — And it is to be noted that *charity feeds,* because it is in the affection, which is more united; *faith gives drink,* because it regards the act of the understanding, which is less united. And therefore, just as drink carries food through the members, so faith and the working of the understanding direct the way for the virtues existing in the affection, in which the feeding is[^p328-1].

57. (Verse 36.). *But I said unto you.* Here is noted the second, namely *who are refreshed by this bread:* because not the unbelieving, but the faithful, chosen and called by God. Therefore he says: So I said, that « he that cometh to me shall not hunger »; but you, because you do not believe, cannot be refreshed; whence he says: *But I said unto you, that you also have seen me, and you believe not.* Above, the third[^p328-2], he said: « We speak what we know, and we testify what we have seen, and you receive not our testimony »; likewise above, the fifth: « How can you believe, who receive glory one from another, and the glory which is from God alone, you do not seek » ? Likewise above, in the same chapter: « You seek me, not because you have seen signs, but because you did eat of my loaves ». And because *you believe not,* you cannot be refreshed. Whence Bede[^p328-3]: « Do you desire bread from heaven? You have it before you and you do not eat », because you do not believe; but those eat who are chosen by God and, being called, believe. Whence he says:

58. (Verse 37.). *All that the Father giveth to me,* through eternal election and temporal calling; *shall come to me,* through faith and dilection. Of this *giving* in the Psalm[^p328-4]: « Ask of me, and I will give thee the Gentiles for thy inheritance ». These the Lord asked for below, the seventeenth: « Father, those whom thou hast given me, keep them in thy name » etc. Of this *coming* to Christ it is said in Isaias, the second: « Come, let us go up to the mountain of the Lord, and to the house of the God of Jacob, and he will teach us his ways ». To this coming Isaias exhorts, the fifty-fifth: « All you that thirst, come to the waters ». So he refreshes those coming; whence he also adds: *And him that cometh to me, I will not cast out; I will not cast out,* but will refresh; *I will not cast out,* but *will bring in,* according to that saying of the Canticles, the second[^p328-5]: « He brought me into the cellar of wine » etc. — Or: *I will not cast* him *out,* but *will enter* to him; Apocalypse, the third: « Behold, I stand at the gate and knock; if any man shall hear my voice and open to me the door, I will come in to him and will sup with him ». And in this is shown the *justice* of God, because he refreshes none but those coming, and his *benignity,* because he receives all and casts away none.

59. (Verse 38.). *Because I came down from heaven* etc. Here is touched the third, namely *why* he *refreshes* only the predestined and those called by God. The reason is the conformity of his will to the divine; therefore he says: *Because I came down from heaven,* by assuming flesh, *not to do my own will,* by disagreeing with the Father; *but the will of him that sent me,* by obeying him in all things; whence to the Philippians, the second[^p328-6]: « He became obedient » to the Father « unto death, even to the death of the cross »; above, the fifth: « I seek not my own will, but the will of him that sent me ». But the will of the Father is that the chosen and called be refreshed by this bread; on account of which he adds:

60. (Verse 39.). *For this is the will of my Father, who sent me*[^p328-7]*, that all that he hath given me,* through election; to the Romans, the eighth: « Whom he foreknew and predestinated to be made conformable to the image of his Son »; *I should lose nothing of it,* through reprobation, according to that saying of the first to the Corinthians, the first: « I will destroy the wisdom of the wise, and the prudence of the prudent I will reject ». *But should raise it up in the last day,* unto the participation of eternal beatitude; to the Romans, the eighth[^p328-8]: « If the Spirit of him that raised up Jesus from the dead, dwell in you; he that raised up Jesus Christ from the dead, shall quicken also your mortal bodies ». This will he fulfilled in part; below, the eighteenth: « Father, of those whom thou hast given me, I have not lost any one ». And because God so chooses us that we may attain beatitude through the merits of faith; that he may show the cooperation of our choice, he repeats again:

61. (Verse 40.). *And this is the will of my Father, who sent me, that every one who seeth the Son,* according to the humanity, *and believeth in him,* through the liberty of his own choice, since, as Augustine says[^p328-9], « a man cannot believe unless willing »; *may have life everlasting,* as a reward; *and I will raise him up in the last day,* and so he will attain this life through the Son; to the Ephesians, the second[^p328-10]: « When we were dead in sins, he hath quickened us together in Christ, and hath raised us up together, and hath made us sit together in the heavenly places in Christ Jesus »; and this *in the last day,* since, as is said in the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth, « last of all the enemy death shall be destroyed ».

### Questions.

62. Question I. It is asked about what he says: *He that cometh to me shall not hunger*[^p328-11].
<!-- page 329 -->

**On the contrary:** It is had below, in the same chapter[^p329-1], that *many of his disciples went back;* and it is certain that they had first come to him: therefore he says what is false.

**I respond:** It is to be understood of those who came with a perfect heart, not a feigned one; and these are those who come with persevering charity; otherwise they are held as if they had not come, because *all justices are given over to oblivion*[^p329-2].

63. Question II. It is asked about what he says: *You have seen me, and you believe not.* — It seems that he says nothing, since faith is the hypostasis of those things which are not seen[^p329-3]: therefore nothing can reprove them for this, that they do not believe, since they see. — *If you say* that *to see* is according to the humanity, but *to believe* according to the Divinity; this is nothing, since the vision of the humanity rather hinders faith in the Divinity than helps it: therefore he ought not to accuse them on this ground.

**I respond:** It must be said that he understands it of the vision not of the humanity pure or infirm, but of the vision of virtue and power in effect. And since they saw the divine virtue[^p329-4] in effect, therefore they ought to have believed.

As to the objection that vision is repugnant to faith; it must be said that this is true of the vision of the essence, but it is not true of the vision which is in the effect as in a mirror, since it belongs to faith to see *through a mirror and in an enigma*[^p329-5].

64. Question III. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *All that the Father hath given me, I shall lose nothing of it.*

**On the contrary:** Below, the seventeenth[^p329-6]: *None of them whom thou gavest me perished, but the son of perdition:* therefore someone of those given to him perished.

**I respond:** It must be said that the stress can be laid on what he says, *hath given,* since *to give* is in two ways: either according to *present justice,* and so Judas was given, and so he perished; or *to give* is according to *eternal election,* and so the predestined were given, of whom none perishes[^p329-7].

It is answered otherwise, that the stress be laid on what he says, *I shall lose,* since Christ himself guards most diligently; whence none perishes on account of a defect of the shepherd; but since he saves no one unwilling, some perish on account of their own malice. Whence Christ does not *lose* them, but they lose themselves.
