# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XX, Vers. 10-18
## *Primo, de manifestatione facta mulieri notantur quinque*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 507 -->

#### Secundo, de manifestatione resurrectionis.

17. *Abierunt ergo*[^p507-6] *discipuli ad semetipsos* etc. Supra egit de sollicitudine, hic agit de *manifestatione;* et quia manifestatio utrique sexui facta est, ideo dividitur haec pars in duas. In prima agitur de manifestatione facta *mulieri;* in secunda, de manifestatione facta *discipulis,* ibi[^p507-7]: *Cum ergo esset sero* etc.

#### Primo, de manifestatione facta mulieri notantur quinque.

Manifestatio igitur facta *mulieri* hoc ordine describitur. Primo insinuatur Mariae *desolatio;* secundo, desolatae offertur *consolatio,* ibi[^p507-8]: *Dum ergo fleret* etc.; tertio, nolenti consolari offertur *Christi manifestatio,* ibi: *Haec cum dixisset, conversa* etc.; quarto, de manifestatione publicanda sibi datur *praeceptio,* ibi: *Dicit ei Iesus: Noli me tangere;* quinto, manifestationis seu apparitionis *relatio,* ibi: *Venit Maria Magdalene.*

18. (Vers. 10.). Insinuatur ergo vehemens Mariae *desolatio,* quae ipsam inquietam non sinebat recedere a sepulcro, etiam aliis discipulis recedentibus; propterea dicit: *Abierunt ergo discipuli ad semetipsos,* « id est, ubi habitabant[^p507-9] », priusquam ad monumentum venerant, ut aliquam consolationem reciperent.

19. (Vers. 11.). *Maria autem stabat ad monumentum foris,* « id est ante illum saxei sepulcri locum[^p507-10] », et perseveranter manebat; ideo invenit; Lucae undecimo: « At ille si perseveraverit pulsans, dico vobis, etsi non dabit illi surgens, eo quod amicus eius sit, propter improbitatem tamen eius surget et dabit illi, quotquot habet panes necessarios »; ad Colossenses quarto: « Orationi instate, vigilantes in ea » etc. *Plorans;* Augustinus[^p507-11]: « Oculi, qui Dominum quaesierant et non invenerant, iam lacrymis vacabant, et plus dolet mulier, quod fuerat Dominus ablatus de monumento, quam quod fuerat occisus in ligno ».

Notandum, quod Maria flevit *compunctione,* Lucae septimo[^p507-12]; flevit *compassione,* supra undecimo: « Iesus, ut vidit eam plorantem »; flevit *devotione,* ut hic, de quo Psalmus: « Fuerunt mihi lacrymae meae panes die ac nocte, dum dicitur mihi quotidie: Ubi est Deus tuus » ?

*Dum ergo fleret.* Hic desolatae *consolatio offertur* et in *visu* et in *affatu,* sed ipsa neutro consolatur. In *visu* consolatur in aspectu Angelorum[^p507-13];
<!-- page 508 -->
unde dicit: *Dum ergo fleret,* quasi desolata, *inclinavit se et prospexit in monumentum,* ut saltem ex aspectu loci consolationem aliquam reciperet; sed maiorem recepit. *Inclinavit se,* per humilitatem; Iob vigesimo secundo[^p508-1]: « Qui humiliatus fuerit erit in gloria, et qui inclinaverit oculos, ipse salvabitur ». Unde:

20. (Vers. 12.). *Et vidit duos Angelos in albis sedentes.* Ideo *in albis sedent,* quia nuntiare venerant splendorem nostrae solemnitatis, scilicet resurrectionis. *Unum ad caput et alium*[^p508-2] *ad pedes, ubi positum fuerat corpus Iesu.* Gregorius exponit, quare sic positi erant. « Ille, inquit, ex passione annuntiandus erat, qui Deus est ante saecula et homo in fine saeculorum; quasi *ad caput* sedet Angelus, dum per Apostolum Ioannem praedicatur, quia *in principio erat Verbum* etc.; et quasi *ad pedes,* cum dicitur: *Verbum caro factum est* ». Et sic facta est ei consolatio *ex visu;* Lucae ultimo[^p508-3]: « Mulieres quaedam ex nostris terruerunt nos, quae ante lucem fuerunt ad monumentum, et non invento corpore eius, venerunt dicentes, se etiam visionem Angelorum vidisse, qui dicunt, eum vivere ».

21. (Vers. 13.). Non solum consolatur eam Dominus in visu, sed etiam in *affatu* Angelorum; unde dicit: *Dicunt ei illi: Mulier, quid ploras?* quasi dicant: noli flere. Non enim erat iam tempus fletus, sed gaudii, Domino resurgente. Sed illa *consolari renuit,* causam sui doloris recolens: *Quia*[^p508-4] *tulerunt Dominum meum,* id est corpus, per synecdochen dicitur; *et nescio, ubi posuerunt eum.* Augustinus: « Haec erat maior causa doloris, quia nesciebat, quo iret ad consolandum dolorem ». Unde poterat dicere illud Psalmi[^p508-5]: « Renuit consolari anima mea » etc.; Threnorum primo: « Idcirco oculus meus plorans et deducens lacrymas, quia recessit a me consolator meus, vertens animam meam ».

22. (Vers. 14.). *Haec cum dixisset.* Hic nolenti consolari fit *Christi manifestatio;* et primo se manifestat sensibus *carnis,* deinde *mentis*[^p508-6]: sensibus *corporis, visui* scilicet per aspectum et *auditui* per colloquium. — *Aspicit* ergo primo Iesum, unde dicit: *Haec cum dixisset,* per quae ostendebat, se nolle consolari; *conversa est retrorsum et vidit Iesum stantem.* Ideo *conversa* est, quia amor non sinebat eam sistere, immo modo huc, modo illuc respiciebat. Vel, sicut dicit Chrysostomus[^p508-7], « Christus, silenter post ipsam apparens, obstupescit Angelos, et illi motu et signo ostenderunt, se aliquid magnum vidisse; et hoc mulierem convertit retrorsum ». *Et vidit Iesum,* oculis corporis, non cordis; unde: *Et nesciebat, quia Iesus esset*[^p508-8].

23. (Vers. 15.). Sic manifestavit se per aspectum, manifestat etiam per *colloquium;* unde: *Dicit ei Iesus: Mulier, quid ploras?* quasi dicat: noli plorare. *Quem quaeris?* Illum quaerebat, quem et sponsa Canticorum tertio[^p508-9]: « In lectulo meo per noctes quaesivi quem diligit anima mea »; quasi dicat: non debes eum cum mortuis quaerere, quia « resurgens a mortuis iam non moritur, mors illi ultra non dominabitur », ad Romanos sexto. Sed nec adhuc per colloquium recognoscit; unde dicit: *Illa aestimans, quod*[^p508-10] *hortulanus esset,* quia sic diluculo venerat in hortum; *dicit ei: Domine, si tu sustulisti eum,* id est Iesum. Non fecerat mentionem de Iesu ipsi, cum quo loquebatur, et tantum dicebat *eum;* quia, sicut dicit Gregorius[^p508-11], « hoc agere solet in animo vis amoris, ut quem ille semper cogitat, nullum alium ignorare credat ». *Dicito mihi, ubi posuisti eum*[^p508-12]; *et ego eum tollam.* Non ei sufficiebat videre, nisi eum tolleret, secundum illud Canticorum tertio: « Tenui eum nec dimittam, donec introducam illum in domum matris meae et in cubiculum genitricis meae »; ideo tollere volebat.
<!-- page 509 -->

24. (Vers. 16.). *Dicit ei Iesus.* Manifestavit se sensui exteriori, hic se manifestat *interiori* ipsam nominatim vocando; unde: *Dicit ei Iesus: Maria.* Gregorius[^p509-1]: « Postquam eam communi vocabulo appellavit ex sexu et agnitus non est; vocat ex nomine, ut eum recognoscat, a quo recognoscitur ». *Conversa illa,* scilicet per intellectus captivationem; Apocalypsis primo[^p509-2]: « Conversus sum, ut viderem vocem, quae loquebatur mecum »; *dicit ei: Rabboni, quod interpretatur magister. Conversa,* non *corpore,* quia prius corpore erat conversa, sed *corde;* unde Augustinus[^p509-3]: « Tunc, inquit, conversa corpore, quod non erat putavit; nunc corde conversa, quod erat agnovit ». Recognoscens autem eum, vocat *magister;* quia sic consueverat eum vocare; supra undecimo[^p509-4] dixit Martha Mariae: « Magister adest et vocat te ».

25. (Vers. 17.). *Dicit ei Iesus.* Hic Mariae iam cognoscenti iniungitur *manifestationis publicatio;* et primo eam *corrigit;* secundo ad *annuntiandum mittit.* — *Corrigit* in hoc quod dicit: *Noli me tangere;* et rationem reddit: *Nondum enim ascendi ad Patrem meum.*

26. Sed haec littera videtur habere *falsitatem* et *dubietatem:*

1. Quia alii Evangelistae[^p509-5] dicunt, quod « accesserunt mulieres et tenuerunt pedes eius ».

2. Item, quae ratio est ista: *Nondum enim ascendi ad Patrem meum?* Immo haec est ratio, quare tangere debuit, quia, si ascendisset, non posset tangere[^p509-6].

Propter hoc littera ista multipliciter exponitur. Augustinus[^p509-7] exponit de tactu *cordis* seu fidei; unde dicit: « *Noli me tangere,* id est, noli me sic credere, quemadmodum adhuc sapis. Quomodo enim non carnaliter adhuc in eum credebat, quem sicut hominem flebat? Et hoc dicit sequens littera: *Nondum enim ascendi ad Patrem meum,* id est ad Patris aequalitatem in corde tuo ».

Secundum Gregorium[^p509-8] intelligitur de tactu *corporali;* sed hoc Dominus non prohibet, ut dicit, « quia tactum horruit feminarum, cum scriptum sit: *Accesserunt mulieres et tenuerunt pedes eius* »; sed in hoc voluit ipsam indignam ad tangendum ostendere tanquam non recte credentem, vel plene; et hoc probat per litteram sequentem: « *Nondum enim ascendi ad Patrem.* In corde nostro tunc Iesus ascendit ad Patrem, cum aequalis creditur Patri ».

Secundum Chrysostomum[^p509-9] intelligitur de tactu *corporali;* et Dominus non prohibet *tangere,* sed innuit, se cum reverentia magis, quam cum erat passibilis, esse tangendum; et hoc indicat ratio sequens, in qua Dominus dicit: *Nondum ascendi,* sed tamen praeparo me ad ascendendum, et ideo non sum vobiscum ut prius.

Potest etiam et aliter illud exponi: quia Maria fervens erat videre Dominum, unde et diligentissime quaesierat, volebat in oscula pedum ruere et eum non dimittere; sed Dominus eam redarguit, ostendens, hic non esse locum fruitionis et tactus Christi, sed cum Patre, unde dicit: *Noli me tangere,* id est, tangendo me hic frui velle; *nondum enim ascendi,* ubi est locus fruitionis, ubi nunquam frustraberis; sed nunc oportet sequestrari corporaliter[^p509-10]. Unde eam mittit ad annuntiandum quod viderat.

*Vade autem ad fratres meos,* Apostolos scilicet, de quibus in Psalmo[^p509-11]: « Narrabo nomen tuum fratribus meis; in medio Ecclesiae laudabo te »; *et dic eis,* annuntia *veritatem Christi resurgentis,* quae
<!-- page 510 -->
consistit in *dignitate glorificationis,* qua iam erat idoneus ire in caelum; ideo dicit: *Dic eis: Ascendo;* ad Ephesios quarto[^p510-1]: « Qui descendit, ipse est qui ascendit super omnes caelos ». Consistit etiam veritas resurrectionis Christi in *excellentia Divinitatis;* unde dicit: *Ad Patrem meum et Patrem vestrum,* non eodem modo *meum,* quo *vestrum;* unde Gregorius[^p510-2]: « *Meum* per naturam, *vestrum* per gratiam ». Consistit etiam veritas resurrectionis Christi in *excellentia humanitatis,* et hanc tangit, cum dicit: *Deum meum et Deum vestrum;* Augustinus[^p510-3]: « *Deum meum,* sub quo et ego *homo* sum, *Deum vestrum,* inter quos et ipsum *Mediator* sum ».

27. (Vers. 18.). *Venit ergo*[^p510-4] *Maria Magdalene.* Hic notatur quintum, scilicet *manifestationis relatio* facta a Magdalena; unde dicit: *Annuntians discipulis: Quia vidi Dominum, et haec dixit mihi,* supple: quae praedicta sunt; Chrysostomus[^p510-5]: « Annuntiat et visum et verba, ut in utroque erudiantur ».

### Quaestiones.

28. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de ordine istius manifestationis. — Si enim discipuli digniores erant, videtur, quod primo eis Dominus apparere debebat, et iterum discipuli docere mulieres, quam mulieres discipulos[^p510-6].

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod hoc factum est *ordine divinae dispensationis* et *merito humanae sollicitudinis: ordo divinae dispensationis;* propter quod dicit Gregorius[^p510-7]: « Quia in paradiso mulier viro propinavit mortem, a sepulcro mulier viris annuntiat vitam, et dicta vivificatoris narrat quae mortiferi serpentis verba narraverat ». *Meritum humanae sollicitudinis,* quia, discipulis recedentibus, mulier remansit afflicta et desolata; et ideo citius meruit apparitione dominica consolari et refici[^p510-8].

29. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur: cum Petrus Dominum tam ferventer diligeret, quare non remansit ad monumentum plorans, ut Maria, sed recessit?

Et respondet Chrysostomus[^p510-9]: « Compassibile quodam modo est muliebre genus »; et ideo non mireris, quod Maria amare flebat ad sepulcrum, Petrus vero nihil tale compassus est. — Alii sumunt rationem ex parte *fidei:* quia enim mulier minus de resurrectione sperabat, ideo magis de sublato corpore dolebat; Petrus autem, etsi non plene, tamen aliquo modo confidebat; unde dicitur in Luca[^p510-10], quod « abiit secum mirans ». — Alii sumunt rationem a parte *amoris,* scilicet quia pro tempore illo vehementius discipulis ardebat et ferventior erat, ideo et sollicitior existebat[^p510-11].

30. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod dum Maria fleret, *inclinavit se et prospexit in monumentum.* — Ad quid respiciebat, ex quo ipsum non esse sciebat?

Respondet Gregorius[^p510-12]: « Amanti semel aspexisse non sufficit, quia vis amoris intentionem multiplicat inquisitionis ». Et Augustinus[^p510-13] dicit, quod « quia vehementer amabat, ideo nec suis nec aliorum oculis putavit facile esse credendum ».

31. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod post recessum discipulorum vidit Angelos; quia dicitur Lucae vigesimo quarto[^p510-14], quod mulieres retulerunt Apostolis, quod viderant Angelos; et tunc dicitur quod discipuli iverunt ad monumentum.

Respondet Augustinus in tertio de Concordia Evangelistarum[^p510-15], quod « Lucas illud dicit per reca-
<!-- page 511 -->
pitulationem; tunc enim cucurrerunt ad monumentum, quando tantummodo annuntiatum fuit de corpore ablato; et post factum est de visione Angelorum, quam postmodum retulerunt ».

32. Quaest. V. Item dubitatur: 1. Quia Marci decimo sexto[^p511-1] dicitur de *uno solo* Angelo: *Introeuntes in monumentum viderunt iuvenem sedentem in dextris coopertum stola candida*

2. Item, Marcus[^p511-2] dicit, Mariam *intrasse,* iste, tantum *aspexisse.*

Ad hoc respondet Augustinus in tertio de Concordia Evangelistarum[^p511-3]: « Prima, inquit, Sabbati, id est die dominico, diluculo venerunt mulieres ad monumentum, sicut omnes Evangelistae narrant; et ut viderunt lapidem sublatum, antequam diligentius inspicerent, cucurrerunt et nuntiaverunt Petro et Ioanni, qui cucurrerunt ad monumentum et postea reversi sunt. *Maria autem* cum aliis *stabat ad monumentum foris plorans*[^p511-4], id est ante illum saxei sepulcri locum, sed tamen intra illud spatium, quo iam ingressae fuerant. Tunc viderunt *Angelum sedentem in dexteris* super lapidem revolutum a monumento, de quo Angelo narrat Matthaeus vigesimo octavo[^p511-5] et Marcus decimo sexto. Tunc dixit eis: *Nolite timere vos* etc., quae dicit Matthaeus et Marcus. Ad haec verba Maria, dum fleret, *inclinavit se et prospexit in monumentum,* sicut hic[^p511-6] dicitur; ubi vidit *duos Angelos in albis sedentes, qui dicunt ei: Mulier, quid ploras?* Et tunc intelligendum, quod Angeli surrexerunt et *steterunt,* sicut Lucas[^p511-7] dicit, quod *duo viri steterunt iuxta illas in veste fulgenti* ».

Unde solvitur contrarietas ex hoc, quod unus dicit quod alter omittit; et ex hoc, quod Marcus vocat *monumentum* aliquod spatium ante sepulcrum maceria communitum, Ioannes vero ipsum locum, in quo erat corpus Iesu.

33. Quaest. VI. Item quaeritur de hoc, quod Dominus se non statim manifestavit mulieri in propria effigie, sed visus est quasi hortulanus.

Respondet Gregorius[^p511-8], quod ratio huius erat modus quaerendi Mariae. « Quia enim Maria amabat et dubitabat, videbat et non cognoscebat ». — Bernardus[^p511-9] autem reddit rationem, quod hoc erat ad magis accendendum eam; unde dicit: « O delectabile pietatis spectaculum! Ipse qui quaeritur et desideratur, *occultat* se et *manifestatur;* occultat se, ut ardentius requiratur, et requisitus cum gaudio inveniatur, et inventus cum sollicitudine teneatur, et tentus non dimittatur ».

34. Quaest. VII. Item quaeritur, utrum Maria Christum crederet esse Deum? — Et quod sic, videtur, quia supra undecimo[^p511-10] dixit Martha: *Tu es Christus, Filius Dei vivi.* — Et etiam, quomodo fuissent ei remissa peccata sine fide recta?

Sed contrarium videtur dicere Gregorius et Augustinus super illud[^p511-11]: *Nondum enim ascendi ad Patrem meum,* sicut expositum est.

Respondendum, quod Maria ante passionem Christum Deum esse credebat, tamen vehementissime eum in carne amabat; et ideo in passione tantum dolorem concepit, ut iam absorpta non nisi de humanitate et morte cogitaret, et ideo non recoleret opera Maiestatis, sed poenas humanitatis.

35. Quaest. VIII. Item, quid est quod dicit: *Ascendo ad Patrem,* cum Pater sit ubique?

Respondetur, quod *causaliter* intelligitur, quia faciebat vel facturus erat se credere aequalem Patri per omnia; ideo dicitur *ascendere*[^p511-12] etc. — Vel ad locum, ubi Pater magis manifestatur in suis operibus; unde dicitur *a dexteris sedere,* id est in potioribus bonis[^p511-13].

## English

#### Second, on the manifestation of the resurrection.

17. *The disciples therefore departed*[^p507-6] *to their own home* etc. Above he treated of the solicitude, here he treats of the *manifestation;* and because the manifestation was made to both sexes, therefore this part is divided in two. In the first there is treatment of the manifestation made *to the woman;* in the second, of the manifestation made *to the disciples,* there[^p507-7]: *Now when it was late* etc.

#### First, on the manifestation made to the woman five things are noted.

The manifestation made *to the woman,* then, is described in this order. First is intimated the *desolation* of Mary; second, *consolation* is offered to her in her desolation, there[^p507-8]: *Now as she was weeping* etc.; third, to her who would not be consoled is offered *the manifestation of Christ,* there: *When she had thus said, she turned* etc.; fourth, concerning the manifestation to be made public, a *precept* is given her, there: *Jesus saith to her: Do not touch me;* fifth, the *report* of the manifestation or apparition, there: *Mary Magdalen cometh.*

18. (Vers. 10.). There is intimated, then, the vehement *desolation* of Mary, which did not suffer her, disquieted, to withdraw from the sepulchre, even while the other disciples were withdrawing; on this account he says: *The disciples therefore departed to their own home,* « that is, where they were dwelling[^p507-9] », before they had come to the sepulchre, that they might receive some consolation.

19. (Vers. 11.). *But Mary stood at the sepulchre without,* « that is, before that place of the stone sepulchre[^p507-10] », and she remained perseveringly; therefore she finds; Luke, the eleventh chapter: « Yet if he shall continue knocking, I say to you, although he will not rise and give him because he is his friend, yet because of his importunity he will rise and give him as many loaves as he needeth »; to the Colossians, the fourth chapter: « Be instant in prayer, watching in it » etc. *Weeping;* Augustine[^p507-11]: « The eyes, which had sought the Lord and had not found him, were now free for tears[^tr-vacare], and the woman grieves more that the Lord had been taken away from the sepulchre than that he had been slain on the wood ».

It is to be noted that Mary wept with *compunction,* Luke, the seventh chapter[^p507-12]; she wept with *compassion,* above, the eleventh chapter: « Jesus, when he saw her weeping »; she wept with *devotion,* as here, of which the Psalm says: « My tears have been my bread day and night, whilst it is said to me daily: Where is thy God » ?

*Now as she was weeping.* Here *consolation is offered* to her in her desolation both in *sight* and in *speech,* but she is consoled by neither. In *sight* she is consoled in the beholding of the Angels[^p507-13];
<!-- page 508 -->
whence he says: *Now as she was weeping,* as one desolate, *she stooped down and looked into the sepulchre,* that at least from the sight of the place she might receive some consolation; but she received a greater. *She stooped down,* through humility; Job, the twenty-second chapter[^p508-1]: « He that hath been humbled shall be in glory, and he that shall bow down his eyes, he shall be saved ». Whence:

20. (Vers. 12.). *And she saw two Angels in white, sitting.* Therefore they *sit in white,* because they had come to announce the splendour of our solemnity, namely of the resurrection. *One at the head and the other*[^p508-2] *at the feet, where the body of Jesus had been laid.* Gregory expounds why they were placed so. « He, he says, was to be announced from the passion, who is God before the ages and man in the end of the ages; as it were *at the head* the Angel sits, when he is preached through the Apostle John, because *in the beginning was the Word* etc.; and as it were *at the feet,* when it is said: *The Word was made flesh* ». And so consolation was given her *from sight;* Luke, the last chapter[^p508-3]: « Certain women also of our company affrighted us, who before it was light were at the sepulchre, and not finding his body, came saying that they had also seen a vision of Angels, who say that he is alive ».

21. (Vers. 13.). The Lord consoles her not only in sight, but also in the *speech* of the Angels; whence he says: *They say to her: Woman, why weepest thou?* as if they said: do not weep. For it was no longer a time of weeping, but of joy, the Lord rising again. But she *refused to be consoled,* recalling the cause of her sorrow: *Because*[^p508-4] *they have taken away my Lord,* that is, the body, it is said by synecdoche; *and I know not where they have laid him.* Augustine: « This was the greater cause of sorrow, that she did not know where she might go to console her sorrow ». Whence she could say that of the Psalm[^p508-5]: « My soul refused to be consoled » etc.; Lamentations, the first chapter: « Therefore my eye is weeping and shedding tears, because my consoler is departed from me, turning my soul ».

22. (Vers. 14.). *When she had thus said.* Here to her who would not be consoled comes *the manifestation of Christ;* and first he manifests himself to the senses *of the flesh,* then *of the mind*[^p508-6]: to the senses *of the body,* namely *to the sight* through beholding and *to the hearing* through converse. — She *beholds* Jesus, then, first, whence he says: *When she had thus said,* by which she showed that she would not be consoled; *she turned herself back and saw Jesus standing.* Therefore she *turned* herself, because love did not suffer her to stand still, but rather she looked about, now hither, now thither. Or, as Chrysostom says[^p508-7], « Christ, appearing silently behind her, struck the Angels with amazement, and they by movement and sign showed that they had seen something great; and this turned the woman back ». *And she saw Jesus,* with the eyes of the body, not of the heart; whence: *And she knew not that it was Jesus*[^p508-8].

23. (Vers. 15.). So he manifested himself through beholding; he manifests himself also through *converse;* whence: *Jesus saith to her: Woman, why weepest thou?* as if he said: do not weep. *Whom seekest thou?* She was seeking him whom the spouse also sought, in the Canticle, the third chapter[^p508-9]: « In my bed by night I sought him whom my soul loveth »; as if he said: thou oughtest not to seek him with the dead, because « rising again from the dead he dieth now no more, death shall no more have dominion over him », to the Romans, the sixth chapter. But not yet does she recognize him through converse; whence he says: *She, thinking that*[^p508-10] *it was the gardener,* because she had come into the garden so at daybreak; *saith to him: Sir, if thou hast taken him away,* that is, Jesus. She had made no mention of Jesus to the very one with whom she was speaking, and she said only *him;* because, as Gregory says[^p508-11], « the force of love is wont to do this in the mind, that it believes no one else ignorant of him whom it always thinks of ». *Tell me where thou hast laid him*[^p508-12]; *and I will take him away.* It did not suffice her to see, unless she took him away, according to that of the Canticle, the third chapter: « I held him, and I will not let him go, till I bring him into my mother's house, and into the chamber of her that bore me »; therefore she wished to take him away.
<!-- page 509 -->

24. (Vers. 16.). *Jesus saith to her.* He manifested himself to the exterior sense; here he manifests himself to the *interior,* calling her by name; whence: *Jesus saith to her: Mary.* Gregory[^p509-1]: « After he had called her by the common word, from her sex, and was not recognized, he calls her by name, that she may recognize him by whom she is recognized ». *She turning,* namely through the captivation of the understanding; Apocalypse, the first chapter[^p509-2]: « I turned to see the voice that spoke with me »; *saith to him: Rabboni, which is interpreted teacher. Turning,* not *in body,* because before she had turned in body, but *in heart;* whence Augustine[^p509-3]: « Then, he says, turned in body, she thought him what he was not; now, turned in heart, she recognized what he was ». And recognizing him, she calls him *teacher;* because so she had been accustomed to call him; above, the eleventh chapter[^p509-4], Martha said to Mary: « The Teacher is come and calleth for thee ».

25. (Vers. 17.). *Jesus saith to her.* Here on Mary, now knowing him, is enjoined the *making public of the manifestation;* and first he *corrects* her; second he *sends* her *to announce.* — He *corrects* her in this, that he says: *Do not touch me;* and he gives the reason: *For I am not yet ascended to my Father.*

26. But this text seems to have *falsity* and *doubtfulness:*

1. Because the other Evangelists[^p509-5] say that « the women came up and took hold of his feet ».

2. Likewise, what reason is this: *For I am not yet ascended to my Father?* Rather this is the reason why she ought to have touched him, because, if he had ascended, she could not touch him[^p509-6].

On account of this, this text is expounded in many ways. Augustine[^p509-7] expounds it of the touch *of the heart,* or of faith; whence he says: « *Do not touch me,* that is, do not so believe me as thou yet savourest. For how did she not still believe in him carnally, whom she was weeping as a man? And this the text following says: *For I am not yet ascended to my Father,* that is, to equality with the Father in thy heart ».

According to Gregory[^p509-8] it is understood of *bodily* touch; but the Lord does not forbid this, as he says, « because he shrank from the touch of women, since it is written: *The women came up and took hold of his feet* »; but in this he wished to show her unworthy to touch, as one not believing rightly, or fully; and he proves this by the text following: « *For I am not yet ascended to the Father.* In our heart Jesus ascends to the Father then, when he is believed equal to the Father ».

According to Chrysostom[^p509-9] it is understood of *bodily* touch; and the Lord does not forbid *touching,* but intimates that he is to be touched with more reverence than when he was passible; and the reason following indicates this, in which the Lord says: *I am not yet ascended,* but yet I am preparing myself to ascend, and therefore I am not with you as before.

It can also be expounded otherwise: because Mary was fervent to see the Lord, whence she had also sought most diligently, she wished to rush to kiss his feet and not to let him go; but the Lord rebukes her, showing that here is not the place of fruition and of the touch of Christ, but with the Father; whence he says: *Do not touch me,* that is, do not wish to enjoy me here by touching; *for I am not yet ascended,* where is the place of fruition, where thou shalt never be disappointed; but now it is necessary to be sequestered bodily[^p509-10]. Whence he sends her to announce what she had seen.

*But go to my brethren,* namely the Apostles, of whom it is said in the Psalm[^p509-11]: « I will declare thy name to my brethren; in the midst of the Church will I praise thee »; *and say to them,* announce the *truth of Christ rising again,* which
<!-- page 510 -->
consists in the *dignity of glorification,* by which he was now fit to go into heaven; therefore he says: *Say to them: I ascend;* to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter[^p510-1]: « He that descended is the same also that ascended above all the heavens ». The truth of the resurrection of Christ consists also in the *excellence of the Divinity;* whence he says: *To my Father and to your Father,* not *mine* in the same way as *yours;* whence Gregory[^p510-2]: « *Mine* by nature, *yours* by grace ». The truth of the resurrection of Christ consists also in the *excellence of the humanity,* and he touches on this when he says: *To my God and your God;* Augustine[^p510-3]: « *My God,* under whom I too am *man; your God,* between whom and you I am the *Mediator* ».

27. (Vers. 18.). *Mary Magdalen therefore*[^p510-4] *cometh.* Here is noted the fifth, namely the *report of the manifestation* made by Magdalen; whence he says: *Announcing to the disciples: I have seen the Lord, and these things he said to me,* supply: the things foretold; Chrysostom[^p510-5]: « She announces both the sight and the words, that they may be instructed in both ».

### Questions.

28. Quaest. I. But it is asked here first about the order of this manifestation. — For if the disciples were worthier, it seems that the Lord ought to have appeared to them first, and again that the disciples should teach the women, rather than the women the disciples[^p510-6].

**I respond:** It must be said that this was done by the *order of the divine dispensation* and by the *merit of human solicitude: the order of the divine dispensation;* for which reason Gregory says[^p510-7]: « Because in paradise the woman gave the man death to drink, from the sepulchre the woman announces life to the men, and narrates the words of the life-giver who had narrated the words of the death-dealing serpent ». *The merit of human solicitude,* because, the disciples withdrawing, the woman remained afflicted and desolate; and therefore she merited the sooner to be consoled and refreshed by the Lord's apparition[^p510-8].

29. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked: since Peter loved the Lord so fervently, why did he not remain at the sepulchre weeping, as Mary did, but withdrew?

And Chrysostom responds[^p510-9]: « The womanly kind is in a certain way compassionate »; and therefore do not wonder that Mary wept bitterly at the sepulchre, while Peter suffered nothing of the kind. — Others take the reason from the side of *faith:* for because the woman hoped less of the resurrection, therefore she grieved more over the body taken away; but Peter, though not fully, yet in some way was confident; whence it is said in Luke[^p510-10] that « he went away wondering in himself ». — Others take the reason from the side of *love,* namely because for that time she burned more vehemently than the disciples and was more fervent, therefore she was also more solicitous[^p510-11].

30. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked about this, that he says that while Mary wept, *she stooped down and looked into the sepulchre.* — For what was she looking, since she knew that he was not there?

Gregory responds[^p510-12]: « For one who loves, to have looked once does not suffice, because the force of love multiplies the intention of searching ». And Augustine[^p510-13] says that « because she loved vehemently, therefore she thought that neither her own eyes nor those of others were easily to be believed ».

31. Quaest. IV. Likewise it is asked about this, that he says that after the withdrawal of the disciples she saw the Angels; since it is said in Luke, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p510-14], that the women reported to the Apostles that they had seen Angels; and then it is said that the disciples went to the sepulchre.

Augustine responds in the third book *On the Agreement of the Evangelists*[^p510-15] that « Luke says that by reca-
<!-- page 511 -->
pitulation; for they ran to the sepulchre then, when it had only been announced about the body taken away; and afterwards came the vision of the Angels, which they reported later ».

32. Quaest. V. Likewise it is doubted: 1. Because in Mark, the sixteenth chapter[^p511-1], it is said of *one only* Angel: *Entering into the sepulchre, they saw a young man sitting on the right side, clothed with a white robe*

2. Likewise, Mark[^p511-2] says that Mary *entered,* this one, that she only *looked in.*

To this Augustine responds in the third book *On the Agreement of the Evangelists*[^p511-3]: « On the first, he says, of the Sabbath, that is, on the Lord's day, at daybreak the women came to the sepulchre, as all the Evangelists narrate; and when they saw the stone taken away, before they looked in more diligently, they ran and announced it to Peter and John, who ran to the sepulchre and afterwards returned. *But Mary* with the others *stood at the sepulchre without, weeping*[^p511-4], that is, before that place of the stone sepulchre, but yet within that space which they had already entered. Then they saw *the Angel sitting on the right* upon the stone rolled away from the sepulchre, of which Angel Matthew narrates in the twenty-eighth chapter[^p511-5] and Mark in the sixteenth chapter. Then he said to them: *Fear not you* etc., which Matthew and Mark say. At these words Mary, while she wept, *stooped down and looked into the sepulchre,* as is said here[^p511-6]; where she saw *two Angels in white sitting, who say to her: Woman, why weepest thou?* And then it is to be understood that the Angels rose and *stood,* as Luke[^p511-7] says that *two men stood by them in shining apparel* ».

Whence the contrariety is solved from this, that one says what another omits; and from this, that Mark calls *the sepulchre* some space before the tomb enclosed by a wall, but John the very place in which the body of Jesus was.

33. Quaest. VI. Likewise it is asked about this, that the Lord did not manifest himself at once to the woman in his own likeness, but was seen as a gardener.

Gregory responds[^p511-8] that the reason of this was Mary's manner of seeking. « For because Mary loved and doubted, she saw and did not know ». — But Bernard[^p511-9] gives the reason that this was to kindle her the more; whence he says: « O delightful spectacle of piety! He who is sought and desired *hides* himself and *is manifested;* he hides himself, that he may be sought more ardently, and being sought may be found with joy, and being found may be held with solicitude, and being held may not be let go ».

34. Quaest. VII. Likewise it is asked whether Mary believed Christ to be God? — And that she did, it seems, because above, in the eleventh chapter[^p511-10], Martha said: *Thou art Christ, the Son of the living God.* — And also, how would her sins have been forgiven her without right faith?

But Gregory and Augustine seem to say the contrary upon that[^p511-11]: *For I am not yet ascended to my Father,* as has been expounded.

It must be responded that Mary before the passion believed Christ to be God, yet she loved him most vehemently in the flesh; and therefore in the passion she conceived such sorrow that, now absorbed, she thought only of the humanity and the death, and therefore did not recall the works of the Majesty, but the punishments of the humanity.

35. Quaest. VIII. Likewise, what is it that he says: *I ascend to the Father,* since the Father is everywhere?

It is responded that it is understood *causally,* because he was making or was going to make himself believed equal to the Father in all things; therefore he is said *to ascend*[^p511-12] etc. — Or to the place where the Father is more manifested in his works; whence he is said *to sit at the right hand,* that is, in the more excellent goods[^p511-13].
