### Capitulum V. *Post haec erat dies festus.*

### Collatio XX.

(Vers. 2.). *Est autem Ierosolymis probatica piscina* etc.

1. Moraliter: *piscina* est humana conscientia; *aqua* huius piscinae, lacrymarum abundantia; *Angelus* movens aquam, inspiratio divina; *commotio aquae* ad sanationem infirmorum, commotio mentis humanae ad dolorem vel compunctionem peccatorum; de qua commotione Psalmus[^p560-1]: « Sana contritiones eius, quia commota est ». — In hac piscina lavanda sunt per contritionem corda nostra, ut placeant Deo nostra sacrificia, quia « sacrificium Deo spiritus contribulatus ».

2. Haec piscina habet *quinque porticus*, quia poenitentia habet quinque introitus. — Primus introitus, per quem descendit peccator ad poenitentiam, est consideratio *gratiae*, quam *amisit*, ad *dolendum.* — Secundus est consideratio *culpae*, quam *commisit*, ad *erubescendum.* — Tertius, consideratio *divinae iustitiae*, quam *offendit*, ad *timendum.* — Quartus, consideratio *poenae aeternae*, quam *meruit*, ad *horrendum.* — Quintus, consideratio *divinae misericordiae*, quae illi *occurrit*, ad *amandum.*

3. Primus igitur est consideratio *status gratiae*, quem amisit peccator. Per hunc introivit Petrus, Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p560-2], quando, « recordatus verbi, quod dixerat ei Iesus, exivit foras et flevit amare »; Apocalypsis secundo: « Memor esto, unde excideris, et poenitentiam age » etc. — Secundus est consideratio *culpae commissae.* Per hunc introitum intravit David in piscinam, secundi Regum ultimo, quando percussit cor suum post enumerationem populi dicens: « Peccavi valde in hoc facto; sed precor, Domine, transfer iniquitatem servi tui, quia stulte egi nimis »; Ieremiae secundo: « Vide vias tuas in convalle; scito, quid feceris »; Psalmus: « Cogitavi et converti pedes meos » etc. — Tertius est consideratio *offensae iustitiae divinae.* Per hunc intravit publicanus in piscinam, Lucae decimo octavo[^p560-3], qui prae timore iustitiae « nolebat oculos levare ad caelum, sed percutiebat pectus suum dicens: Deus, propitius esto mihi peccatori »; quia timeo iustitiam, ideo imploro misericordiam; ad Hebraeos decimo ait Apostolus: « Scimus, qui dixit: Mihi vindicta, et ego retribuam; et iterum: Dominus iudicabit populum suum. Horrendum est incidere in manus Dei viventis »; Iob quadragesimo secundo: « Auditu auris audivi te; nunc autem oculus meus videt te. Idcirco me reprehendo et ago poenitentiam » etc. — Quartus est consideratio *poenae meritae.* Per hunc introivit rex Ninivae, Ionae tertio[^p560-4], qui ad vocem Ionae comminantis subversionem civitatis « surrexit de solio suo » etc. Simile de Achab, tertii Regum penultimo. — Quintus est consideratio *divinae misericordiae*, quae hominem praevenit. Per hunc introivit Magdalena, Lucae septimo, cui « dimissa sunt peccata multa, quoniam dilexit multum »; Ieremiae tertio: « Revertere ad me, et ego te suscipiam ». Gregorius[^p560-5]: « Debemus misericordiam vocantis erubescere, cuius iustitiam nolumus formidare » etc.

4. *Considera* igitur gratiam amissam et *dole;* culpam commissam et *erubesce;* divinam iustitiam et *time;* poenam aeternam et *horre;* divinam clementiam et *dilige.* Per haec descende in piscinam poenitentiae.

### Annotatio 18.

(Vers. 3.). *In his iacebat multitudo languentium, caecorum, claudorum, aridorum.*

1. Nota: peccant *caeci* per ignorantiam contra *Filii sapientiam;* Matthaei decimo quinto[^p560-6]: « Caeci sunt et duces caecorum ». — *Claudi*, per impotentiam contra *Patris potentiam;* ad Hebraeos duodecimo: « Gressus rectos facite pedibus vestris, ut non quis claudicans erret, magis autem sanetur ». — *Aridi*, per malitiam contra *Spiritus sancti bonitatem;* Ecclesiastici trigesimo septimo: « O praesumtio nequissima! unde creata es, cooperire aridam malitia vel dolositate illius »?

2. Vel sic: *caecitas* est *ignorantia intellectus;* Psalmus[^p560-7]: « Dereliquit me virtus mea; et lumen oculorum meorum, et ipsum non est mecum ». — *Ariditas* est *indevotio in affectu;* Psalmus: « Aruit tanquam testa » etc. — *Claudicatio* est *inordinatio in effectu;* Psalmus: « Filii alieni mentiti sunt » etc.

### Collatio XXI.

(Vers. 14.). *Ecce, sanus factus es; iam amplius noli peccare, ne deterius tibi aliquid contingat.*
<!-- page 561 -->

1. In istis verbis duo facit Dominus. Primo memorat sanato *beneficium gratiae*, cum dicit: *Ecce, sanus factus es.* — Secundo interdicit *recidivum culpae*, comminando supplicium poenae, cum dicit: *Iam amplius noli peccare* etc. Primum necessarium est sanato, ne sit *ingratus*, scilicet commemorare gratiam; secundum, ne sit *incautus*, scilicet devitare culpam.

2. *Ecce sanus factus es.* Considerantibus paralytici sanationem occurrit *quadruplex medicina.* Est enim medicina *praeparativa*, medicina *purgativa*, medicina *reparativa* et medicina *praeservativa.* — Medicina *praeparativa* est *dolor contritionis*, et per hanc sanatur infirmus; unde Psalmista[^p561-1]: « Sana contritiones eius, quia commota est »; idem: « Sana me, Domine, quoniam conturbata sunt omnia ossa mea »; et iterum: « Qui sanat contritos corde ». — Medicina *purgativa* est *gratia remissionis*, et per hanc sanat Dominus; unde in Psalmo: « Qui propitiatur omnibus iniquitatibus tuis, qui sanat omnes infirmitates tuas ». — Medicina *reparativa* est *profectus bonae operationis*, et per hanc efficitur homo sanus; unde Proverbiorum quarto[^p561-2] dicitur de praeceptis disciplinae: « Vita sunt invenientibus ea et universae carnis sanitas ». — Medicina *praeservativa* est *modestia conversationis*, et per hanc etiam custoditur homo sanus; Ecclesiastici trigesimo primo: « Vigilia et tortura et cholera homini infrunito; somnus sanitatis in homine parco ».

3. Sequitur: *Iam amplius noli peccare, ne deterius tibi aliquid contingat;* ubi interdicitur *recidivum culpae.* Circa quod notandum est, quod recidivanti[^p561-3] accidit *deterius* quadrupliciter: primo, quia homo fit impotentior *ad resurgendum;* secundo, quia culpa fit difficilior *ad remittendum;* tertio, quia Deus fit difficilior *ad placandum;* quarto, quia diabolus fit difficilior *ad expellendum.*

4. *Homo* igitur fit *impotentior ad resurgendum;* unde ad Hebraeos sexto[^p561-4]: « Impossibile est, eos, qui semel illuminati sunt, caeleste etiam donum gustaverunt et participes facti sunt Spiritus sancti etc., et prolapsi sunt, rursus renovari ad poenitentiam ». — *Culpa* etiam fit *difficilior ad remittendum;* Ieremiae secundo: « Quam vilis facta es, nimis iterans vias tuas »! et secundae Petri secundo: « Si refugientes coinquinationes mundi in cognitionem Domini et Salvatoris Iesu Christi, his rursus implicati superantur, facta sunt eis posteriora deteriora prioribus. Contingit enim eis illud veri Proverbii: Canis reversus ad vomitum et sus lota in volutabro luti ». — Deus etiam fit *difficilior ad placandum;* quod figuratum est tertii Regum secundo[^p561-5] de Semei, qui primum peccans invenit misericordiam, eo pacto, quod staret in Ierusalem; secundo vero peccans sine ulla misericordia occisus est. — *Diabolus* etiam fit *difficilior ad expellendum;* quod figuratum est Lucae undecimo de spiritu immundo, revertente in domum suam, « qui assumit septem spiritus nequiores se, et fiunt novissima hominis illius peiora prioribus ».

### Collatio XXII.

(Vers. 21.). *Sicut Pater suscitat mortuos et vivificat, sic et Filius quos vult vivificat.*

1. Nota, quod mortui vivificantur a *Deo*, a *Christo* et ab *homine.* — *Deus* vivificat mortuos *effective;* ad Romanos quarto[^p561-6]: « Ipse vivificat mortuos », scilicet effective, « et vocat ea quae non sunt, sicut ea quae sunt ». — *Christus, meritorie;* primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto: « Sicut in Adam omnes moriuntur, ita et in Christo omnes vivificabuntur ». — *Homo, dispositive;* unde Isaiae quinquagesimo septimo: « Vivificat spiritum humilium et vivificat cor contritorum »

2. *Pater*, inquit, *suscitat mortuos et vivificat.* — Nota, quod Pater vivificat a morte *naturae*, a morte *culpae*, a morte *miseriae.* — A morte *naturae* suscitantur tam iusti quam iniusti; a morte *culpae* omnes, tam electi quam praesciti; a morte *miseriae* tantummodo electi. — Vivificat ergo a morte *naturae;* ad Romanos quarto[^p561-7]: « Ipse est qui vivificat mortuos ». — A morte *culpae;* Isaiae quinquagesimo septimo: « Habitat cum contrito et humili spiritu, ut vivificet spiritum humilium et vivificet cor contritorum ». — Vivificat a morte *miseriae;* ad Romanos octavo: « Qui suscitavit Iesum a mortuis vivificabit mortalia corpora nostra propter eius spiritum inhabitantem in vobis ». — *Pater*, inquit, *vivificat mortuos.* Require supra quarto capitulo[^p561-8] de signis vitae et mortis spiritualis.

3. *Filius quos vult vivificat.* — Nota, quod quaedam sunt, quae vitam spiritualem *ostendunt*, de quibus supra[^p561-9] habitum est; quaedam vero, quae ad vitam spiritualem *disponunt.* Ad vitam spiritualem *disponit* se homo et etiam *disponitur* a Deo. — *Disponit* autem se homo tripliciter: *Peccatum dimittendo;* Ezechielis decimo octavo[^p561-10]: « Si averterit se impius ab impietate sua, quam operatus est,
<!-- page 562 -->
et fecerit iudicium et iustitiam, animam suam vivificabit ». — De *peccato dolendo;* Isaiae quinquagesimo septimo: « Dominus habitat cum contrito et humili spiritu » etc. — In *bono se exercendo;* Psalmus: « In aeternum non obliviscar iustificationes tuas, quia in ipsis vivificasti me ».

*Disponitur* etiam homo a Deo tripliciter: *flagello correptionis;* primi Regum secundo[^p562-1]: « Dominus mortificat et vivificat ». *Verbo praedicationis;* Psalmus: « Eloquium tuum vivificavit me ». *Gratia inspirationis;* Psalmus: « Conversus vivificasti me, et de abyssis terrae iterum reduxisti me ».

### Collatio XXIII.

(Vers. 32.). *Alius est, qui testimonium perhibet de me.*

1. Nota, quod Christus habuit quadruplex testimonium. Habuit enim testimonium *vitae*, testimonium *famae*, testimonium *Scripturae* et testimonium *vocis paternae.* Primum testimonium est quasi per *signa;* secundum, quasi per *nuntium;* tertium, quasi per *litteras*, et quartum, quasi *viva voce.* His enim quatuor modis solet testimonium perhiberi.

2. Habuit ergo Christus testimonium *vitae;* Ioannis quinto[^p562-2]: « Opera, quae ego facio, ipsa testimonium perhibent de me ». — Testimonium *famae;* Ioannis quinto; « Vos misistis ad Ioannem, et ipse testimonium perhibuit veritati »; et Ioannis duodecimo: « Testimonium perhibebat ei turba, quae fuerat cum eo, quando vocavit Lazarum de monumento ». — Testimonium *Scripturae;* Ioannis quinto: « Scrutamini Scripturas, in quibus putatis vitam aeternam habere; et illa sunt, quae testimonium perhibent de me ». — Testimonium *vocis paternae;* Ioannis quinto: « Qui misit me Pater, ipse testimonium perhibuit de me »; item: *Alius est, qui testimonium perhibet de me.*

3. Nota, quod testimonium habuit Christus a *Scripturis*, a *creaturis*, a *personis divinis.* — A *Scripturis*, scilicet *Moysaica, prophetica* et *evangelica.* — A Scriptura *Moysaica;* et de hoc loquitur hic[^p562-3]: « Scrutamini Scripturas »; et post sequitur: « De me ille », scilicet Moyses, « scripsit ». — A Scriptura *prophetica;* Actuum decimo: « Huic omnes Prophetae testimonium perhibent, accipere remissionem » etc. — A Scriptura *evangelica;* Ioannis decimo nono: « Et qui vidit testimonium perhibuit »; et ultimo: « Hic est discipulus ille » etc.

4. A creaturis, *intellectuali*, scilicet Angelo; *rationali*, scilicet homine; *insensibili*, scilicet obediente. — A creatura *intellectuali*, scilicet Angelo, qui perhibet testimonium obsequendo; supra primo[^p562-4]: « Videbitis Angelos ascendentes » etc. — A creatura *rationali*, scilicet homine; Ioannis duodecimo: « Testimonium perhibebat illi turba, quando Lazarum vocavit de monumento ». — A creatura *insensibili* obediente; Matthaei vigesimo primo: « Maledixit ficulneae, et continuo arefacta est ».

5. A *personis divinis*, scilicet Patris et Filii et Spiritus sancti. A persona *Patris;* Ioannis quinto[^p562-5]: « Testimonium perhibet de me qui misit me Pater ». A persona *Filii;* Ioannis octavo: « Ego sum, qui testimonium perhibeo de me ipso ». A persona *Spiritus sancti;* Ioannis decimo quinto: « Cum venerit ille Spiritus veritatis, testimonium perhibebit de me ». — A tota Trinitate insimul; primae Ioannis ultimo: « Tres sunt, qui testimonium dant in caelo ».

### Collatio XXIV.

(Vers. 40.). *Non vultis venire ad me, ut vitam habeatis*, Ioannis quinto.

1. In his verbis duo facit Dominus. Primo *increpat malitiam* ad se venire nolentium, cum dicit: *Non vultis.* — Secundo *tangit fructum* ad se venientium, cum dicit: *Ut vitam* aeternam *habeatis.*

2. Dicit ergo: *Non vultis venire.* — Nota, quod veniendum est *ad* Christum, *post* Christum et *per* Christum. — Veniendum est *ad* Christum *docentem* tanquam *magistrum*, ipsum *audiendo;* Matthaei undecimo[^p562-6]: « Venite ad me, omnes, qui laboratis et oneratis estis, et ego reficiam vos ». — Veniendum *post* Christum *conversantem*, tanquam post *paedagogum*, ipsum *imitando;* Matthaei quarto: « Venite post me; faciam, vos fieri piscatores hominum ». — Veniendum est *per* Christum *patientem* tanquam per *ostium*, in ipsum *credendo;* Ioannis decimo quarto: « Nemo venit ad Patrem nisi per me ». — De omnibus istis Ioannis decimo quarto: « Ego sum via », dirigens in conversatione; « veritas », instruens in praedicatione; « vita », ad vitam aeternam introducens in passione.

3. *Non vultis*, inquit, *venire ad me.* Item tangit *fructum* veniendi ad Deum, cum dicit: *Ut vitam aeternam habeatis.* Fructus autem iste triplex est. Primus est *impetratio veniae;* secundus est *adeptio gratiae;* tertius est *perceptio gloriae.* — Primus
<!-- page 563 -->
igitur fructus est *impetratio veniae;* Isaiae primo[^p563-1]: « Quaerite iudicium, subvenite oppresso, iudicate pupillo, defendite viduam; et venite et arguite me, dicit Dominus. Si fuerint peccata vestra ut coccinum, quasi nix dealbabuntur; et si fuerint rubra quasi vermiculus, velut lana alba erunt ». — Secundus fructus est *adeptio gratiae;* Matthaei undecimo[^p563-2]: « Venite ad me, omnes, qui laboratis et onerati estis, et ego reficiam vos »; et Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto: « Omnes sitientes, venite ad aquas, et qui non habetis argentum properate, emite et comedite. Venite, emite absque argento et absque ulla commutatione vinum et lac ». — Tertius fructus est *perceptio aeternae gloriae;* Ioannis quinto[^p563-3]: *Non vultis venire ad me, ut vitam aeternam habeatis;* et Canticorum quarto: « Veni de Libano, veni, coronaberis ».

4. Sequitur de *modo* veniendi ad Christum. Modus autem veniendi ad Christum triplex est, scilicet per *fidem veritatis*, per *amorem bonitatis*, per *operationem virtutis.* — Primus modus est per *fidem veritatis;* Ioannis sexto[^p563-4]: « Nemo potest venire ad me, nisi Pater, qui misit me, traxerit eum »; Glossa: « Id est, credit in me ». — Secundus modus est per *amorem bonitatis;* Canticorum secundo: « Veni, columba mea, in foraminibus petrae, in caverna maceriae ». — Tertius modus est per *operationem virtutis;* Ioannis tertio: « Qui facit veritatem venit ad lucem, ut manifestentur opera eius »; et Lucae nono: « Si quis vult venire post me, abneget semetipsum et tollat crucem suam et sequatur me ».

5. Ista autem *operatio virtutis*, per quam veniendum est ad Christum, triplex est. — Prima est via *mandatorum;* secunda, semita *consiliorum;* tertia, compendium *supererogationum.* — Prima operatio virtutis est *via mandatorum;* Matthaei decimo nono[^p563-5]: « Si vis ad vitam ingredi, serva mandata »; et Matthaei vigesimo secundo: « Omnia parata sunt, venite ad nuptias »; et Lucae decimo quarto: « Misit servum suum hora coenae dicere invitatis, ut venirent ». — Secunda est *semita consiliorum;* Matthaei decimo nono: « Si vis perfectus esse, vade et vende omnia, quae habes, et da pauperibus, et habebis thesaurum in caelo, et veni et sequere me ». — Tertia est *compendium supererogationum;* Matthaei decimo quarto[^p563-6]: « Domine, si tu es, iube, me venire ad te super aquas ». Nota de sancto Benedicto. Et ad Hebraeos duodecimo: « Curramus ad propositum nobis certamen, aspicientes in auctorem fidei et consummatorem Iesum, qui, proposito sibi gaudio, sustinuit crucem, confusione contempta, atque in dextera sedis Dei sedet. Recogitate eum qui talem sustinuit a peccatoribus adversum semetipsum contradictionem, ut non fatigemini, animis vestris deficientes ».

### Collatio XXV.

(Vers. 28. 25.). *Qui in monumentis sunt audient vocem Filii Dei, et qui audierint vivent.*

1. In istis verbis tangitur ordo spiritualis resuscitationis, et tanguntur ibi tria. Tangitur enim ibi *status mortis* et *status vitae* et *via*[^p563-7] perveniendi ad vitam de morte. — *Status mortis* est status culpae, et hic tangitur, cum dicitur: *Qui in monumentis sunt.* — *Via* perveniendi ad vitam est verbum doctrinae, et haec tangitur, cum dicitur: *Audient vocem Filii Dei* vivi. — *Status vitae* est status gratiae, et hic tangitur, cum dicitur: *Et qui audierint vivent;* ac si diceretur: qui sunt in morte[^p563-8] culpae audient vocem doctrinae et vivent vita gratiae.

2. *Qui in monumentis sunt audient* etc. — Nota, quod mortuus spiritualis, id est peccator, aliquando significatur in Scriptura esse quasi in *thalamo*, aliquando quasi in *vico*, aliquando quasi in *monumento.* Quasi in *thalamo* est mortuus, quando *occiditur* peccator interius, id est in *consensu*[^p563-9]; quasi in *vico*, quando exterius *defertur* in *opere;* quasi in *monumento*, quando ulterius *sepelitur* in mala consuetudine. — Mortuus ergo spiritualis aliquando *iacet in thalamo*, quando peccat in solo consensu; et talis significatur per filiam archisynagogi, quam suscitavit Iesus in thalamo, Matthaei nono[^p563-10], Lucae octavo. — Item, mortuus spiritualis aliquando *effertur in vico*, quando peccat in opere; et talis significatur per « adolescentem, filium unicum viduae, matris suae », quem suscitavit in porta civitatis, Lucae septimo. — Item, mortuus spiritualis aliquando *sepelitur in monumento*, quando peccat ex consuetudine; et talis significatur per Lazarum, quem suscitavit Iesus quatuor dies habentem in monumento, Ioannis undecimo. — Non ergo desperet peccator, sive peccaverit in voluntate, sive in opere, sive in consuetudine, quia etiam qui in monumentis sunt vivent, *si audierint* vocem doctrinae.

<!-- page 564 -->
3. *Audient*, inquit, *vocem Filii Dei. Audient*, inquam, ad *intelligendum*, ad *credendum*, ad *obediendum.* Sic enim est audiendum verbum, ut *intelligamus*, secundum quod dicitur in Psalmo[^p564-1]: « Audi, filia, et vide », id est, intellige, « et inclina aurem tuam » etc. — Sic etiam audiendum est, ut *credamus;* Ioannis quinto: « Qui verbum meum audit et credit in me habet vitam aeternam ». — Sic audiendum est, ut *obediamus.* Taliter audiebat de quo dicitur in Psalmo: « In auditu auris obedivit mihi ». — Propter haec tria dicitur Apocalypsis secundo: « Qui habet aures audiendi audiat ».

4. Primo modo non audiunt qui verbo Dei non *intendunt;* de quibus in Psalmo[^p564-2]: « Non audivit populus meus vocem meam, et Israel non intendit mihi ». — Secundo modo, qui verbo Dei non *credunt*, de quibus Isaiae sexto: « Excaeca cor populi huius et aures eius aggrava, ne forte videat oculis suis et auribus suis audiat et corde suo intelligat et convertatur, et sanem eum ». — Tertio modo non audiunt, si qui verbo Dei non *obediunt;* similes sunt illi, de quo dicitur Iacobi primo: « Quia, si quis auditor est verbi et non factor, hic comparabitur viro consideranti vultum nativitatis suae in speculo »; et talibus dicitur Ioannis octavo: « Propterea vos non auditis, quia ex Deo non estis ». — Nos ergo audiamus *vocem Filii Dei* ad *intelligendum*, ad *credendum* et ad *obediendum*, ut simus « auditores et factores verbi, non auditores tantum »; et sic poterimus vivere per verbum.

5. Sequitur: *Et qui audient vivent. — Vivent*, inquam, per *fidem veritatis*, per *amorem bonitatis* et per *opera virtutis.* Hic enim est triplex vivendi modus spiritualis, ut vivamus per *fidem*, secundum quod dicitur Habacuc secundo[^p564-3]: « Iustus meus in sua fide vivit ». Vivamus etiam per *caritatem;* primae Ioannis tertio: « Scimus, quoniam translati sumus de morte ad vitam, quoniam diligimus fratres. Qui enim non diligit manet in morte ». Vivamus etiam per *opera virtutis*, de quibus Proverbiorum quarto: « Vita enim sunt invenientibus ea »; Ezechielis decimo octavo: « Si fuerit vir iustus et fecerit iudicium et iustitiam etc.; vita vivet et non morietur ». — Exeamus ergo de monumento culpae, et audiamus verbum doctrinae, et vivemus vita gratiae[^p564-4].

### Annotatio 19.

(Vers. 14.). *Ecce, sanus factus es.*

1. Nota, quod quosdam sanavit Dominus *manu*, quosdam *verbo*, quosdam autem *nutu* solo. — *Tactu* manus sanavit leprosum; Lucae quinto[^p564-5]: « Extendens Iesus manum, tetigit eum dicens: Volo, mundare; et confestim discessit lepra ab eo ». — *Virtute*, scilicet *verbi*, sanavit paralyticum; Ioannis quinto: « Dixit enim Iesus: Tolle grabatum tuum et ambula. Et statim sanus factus est. — *Nutu* solius voluntatis sanavit filium reguli, qui « infirmabatur Capharnaum », Ioannis quarto, quem nec vidit nec tetigit, sed solius voluntatis imperio sanavit eum, cum dixit patri suo: « Filius tuus vivit ».

2. Ita intellige, quod Dominus sanat quosdam *flagello correctionis;* Iob quinto[^p564-6]: « Vulnerat et medetur, percutit, et manus eius sanabunt ». — Quosdam sanat alloquendo solo *verbo praedicationis;* Sapientiae decimo sexto: « Neque herba neque malagma sanavit eos, at sermo tuus, Domine, qui sanat omnia ». — Quosdam autem sanat illustrando *gratia inspirationis*, secundum quod dicitur Malachiae quarto: « Orietur vobis timentibus Deum sol iustitiae, et sanitas in pennis eius ».

### Annotatio 20.

(Vers. 13.). *Is autem, qui sanus fuerat effectus, nesciebat, quis esset*, supple: qui sanaverat eum.

Attende, quam magna est misericordia Dei solius: sanaverat eum, et nondum *cognoscebatur* ab eo. Quanta faceret pro illo, si ab illo diligeretur, si ab illo rogaretur, qui ipsum sanabat, antequam ab eo cognosceretur? In hoc ergo est magna misericordia Dei erga nos, quia beneficia eius praeveniunt nostram *cognitionem*, nostram *dilectionem*, nostrum *obsequium* sive nostram operationem. — Nostram *cognitionem* praevenit, ut hic patet, cum dicitur: *Is qui sanus effectus fuerat, nesciebat, quis esset.* — Nostram *dilectionem;* unde ad Romanos quinto[^p564-7]: « Cum adhuc inimici essemus, reconciliati sumus per mortem Filii eius ». — Nostrum *obsequium* sive operationem; ad Titum tertio: « Non ex operibus iustitiae, quae fecimus nos, sed secundum suam misericordiam salvos nos fecit ».

### Annotatio 21.

(Vers. 19.). *Non potest Filius a se facere quidquam, nisi quod viderit Patrem facientem.*

Nota, quod aliquem esse alterius filium probant tria: *mansio, aemulatio* et *imitatio.* Solet enim filius *imitari* patrem, *aemulari* pro patre, *succedere* patri in mansione. Haec tria probaverunt, Christum esse Filium Dei. Tenuit enim Patris *mansionem;* unde Lucae secundo[^p564-8]: « Quid est, quod me quaerebatis; nesciebatis,
<!-- page 565 -->
quia in his quae Patris mei sunt, oportet me esse »? — Habuit Patris *zelum* sive aemulationem, ex cuiusmodi zelo Ioannis secundo dicebat: « Auferte ista hinc, et nolite facere domum Patris mei domum negotiationis. Recordati sunt discipuli, quia scriptum est: Zelus domus tuae comedit me ». — Habuit etiam Patris *imitationem*, de qua hic dicitur: *Non potest Filius a se facere quidquam, nisi quod viderit Patrem facientem, et quaecumque ille facit, haec similiter et Filius facit.*

### Chapter V. *After these things was a festival day.*

### Collation XX.

(Verse 2.). *Now there is at Jerusalem a probatic pond* etc.

1. Morally: the *pond* is the human conscience; the *water* of this pond, the abundance of tears; the *Angel* moving the water, the divine inspiration; the *moving of the water* for the healing of the sick, the moving of the human mind to sorrow or compunction for sins; of which moving the Psalm[^p560-1]: « Heal thou the breaches thereof, for it has been moved ». — In this pond our hearts are to be washed through contrition, so that our sacrifices may please God, because « a sacrifice to God is an afflicted spirit ».

2. This pond has *five porches*, because penance has five entrances. — The first entrance, through which the sinner goes down to penance, is the consideration of *the grace* that he *has lost*, for *sorrowing.* — The second is the consideration of *the fault* that he *has committed*, for *blushing.* — The third, the consideration of *the divine justice* that he *has offended*, for *fearing.* — The fourth, the consideration of *the eternal punishment* that he *has deserved*, for *shuddering.* — The fifth, the consideration of *the divine mercy* that *comes to meet* him, for *loving.*

3. The first, then, is the consideration of *the state of grace* that the sinner has lost. By this one Peter entered, Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p560-2], when, « remembering the word that Jesus had said to him, he went forth and wept bitterly »; the Apocalypse, the second chapter: « Be mindful from whence thou art fallen, and do penance » etc. — The second is the consideration of *the fault committed.* By this entrance David entered into the pond, the second of Kings, the last chapter, when he struck his heart after the numbering of the people, saying: « I have sinned very much in what I have done; but I pray thee, O Lord, take away the iniquity of thy servant, because I have done exceeding foolishly »; Jeremias, the second chapter: « See thy ways in the valley; know what thou hast done »; the Psalm: « I have thought, and turned my feet » etc. — The third is the consideration of *the divine justice offended.* By this one the publican entered into the pond, Luke, the eighteenth chapter[^p560-3], who for fear of justice « would not lift up his eyes towards heaven, but struck his breast, saying: O God, be merciful to me a sinner »; because I fear justice, therefore I implore mercy; to the Hebrews, the tenth chapter, the Apostle says: « We know him that hath said: Vengeance belongeth to me, and I will repay; and again: The Lord shall judge his people. It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God »; Job, the forty-second chapter: « With the hearing of the ear I have heard thee, but now my eye seeth thee. Therefore I reprehend myself, and do penance » etc. — The fourth is the consideration of *the punishment deserved.* By this one the king of Ninive entered, Jonas, the third chapter[^p560-4], who at the voice of Jonas threatening the overthrow of the city « rose up out of his throne » etc. The like of Achab, the third of Kings, the last chapter but one. — The fifth is the consideration of *the divine mercy*, which goes before man. By this one Magdalen entered, Luke, the seventh chapter, to whom « many sins are forgiven, because she hath loved much »; Jeremias, the third chapter: « Return to me, and I will receive thee ». Gregory[^p560-5]: « We ought to blush at the mercy of him who calls, whose justice we are unwilling to dread » etc.

4. *Consider*, then, the grace lost and *sorrow;* the fault committed and *blush;* the divine justice and *fear;* the eternal punishment and *shudder;* the divine clemency and *love.* By these go down into the pond of penance.

### Annotation 18.

(Verse 3.). *In these lay a great multitude of sick, of blind, of lame, of withered.*

1. Note: the *blind* sin through ignorance against *the Son's wisdom;* Matthew, the fifteenth chapter[^p560-6]: « They are blind, and leaders of the blind ». — The *lame*, through impotence against *the Father's power;* to the Hebrews, the twelfth chapter: « Make straight steps with your feet, that no one, halting, may go out of the way, but rather be healed ». — The *withered*, through malice against *the goodness of the Holy Spirit;* Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-seventh chapter: « O wicked presumption! whence wast thou created, to cover the dry land with malice and the deceitfulness thereof »?

2. Or thus: *blindness* is *ignorance in the understanding;* the Psalm[^p560-7]: « My strength hath left me; and the light[^tr-lumen] of my eyes, and it is not with me ». — *Withering* is *indevotion in the affection;* the Psalm: « It is dried up like a potsherd » etc. — *Lameness* is *disorder in the effect;* the Psalm: « The children that are strangers have lied » etc.

### Collation XXI.

(Verse 14.). *Behold, thou art made whole; sin no more, lest some worse thing happen to thee.*

<!-- page 561 -->

1. In these words the Lord does two things. First he recalls to the man healed *the benefit of grace*, when he says: *Behold, thou art made whole.* — Secondly he forbids *the relapse into fault*, threatening the penalty of punishment, when he says: *Sin no more* etc. The first is necessary for the man healed, lest he be *ungrateful*, namely to call grace to mind; the second, lest he be *incautious*, namely to shun fault.

2. *Behold thou art made whole.* To those who consider the healing of the paralytic a *fourfold medicine* presents itself. For there is a *preparative* medicine, a *purgative* medicine, a *reparative* medicine and a *preservative* medicine. — The *preparative* medicine is *the sorrow of contrition*, and by it the sick man is healed; whence the Psalmist[^p561-1]: « Heal thou the breaches thereof, for it has been moved »; the same: « Heal me, O Lord, for all my bones are troubled »; and again: « Who healeth the broken of heart ». — The *purgative* medicine is *the grace of remission*, and by it the Lord heals; whence in the Psalm: « Who forgiveth all thy iniquities, who healeth all thy diseases ». — The *reparative* medicine is *the progress of good working*, and by it a man is made whole; whence in Proverbs, the fourth chapter[^p561-2], it is said of the precepts of discipline: « They are life to those that find them, and health to all flesh ». — The *preservative* medicine is *the modesty of conversation*, and by it too a man is kept whole; Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-first chapter: « Watching, and gripes, and choler are with an intemperate man; the sleep of health in a sparing man ».

3. There follows: *Sin no more, lest some worse thing happen to thee;* where *the relapse into fault* is forbidden. Concerning which it is to be noted that to one who relapses[^p561-3] *something worse* happens in four ways: first, because the man becomes less able *to rise again;* secondly, because the fault becomes more difficult *to remit;* thirdly, because God becomes more difficult *to appease;* fourthly, because the devil becomes more difficult *to expel.*

4. *The man*, then, becomes *less able to rise again;* whence to the Hebrews, the sixth chapter[^p561-4]: « It is impossible for those who were once illuminated, have tasted also the heavenly gift, and were made partakers of the Holy Spirit etc., and are fallen away, to be renewed again to penance ». — *The fault* also becomes *more difficult to remit;* Jeremias, the second chapter: « How base art thou become, going over thy ways again exceedingly »! and the second of Peter, the second chapter: « If, flying from the pollutions of the world, into the knowledge of the Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ, they be again entangled in them and overcome, their latter state is become unto them worse than the former. For that of the true proverb has happened to them: The dog is returned to his vomit, and the sow that was washed to her wallowing in the mire ». — God also becomes *more difficult to appease;* which is figured in the third of Kings, the second chapter[^p561-5], of Semei, who, sinning the first time, found mercy, on the condition that he should stay in Jerusalem; but sinning the second time he was slain without any mercy. — *The devil* also becomes *more difficult to expel;* which is figured in Luke, the eleventh chapter, of the unclean spirit returning into his house, « who taketh with him seven spirits more wicked than himself, and the last state of that man becomes worse than the first ».

### Collation XXII.

(Verse 21.). *As the Father raiseth up the dead and giveth life, so the Son also giveth life to whom he will.*

1. Note that the dead are given life by *God*, by *Christ* and by *man.* — *God* gives life to the dead *effectively;* to the Romans, the fourth chapter[^p561-6]: « He giveth life to the dead », namely effectively, « and calleth those things that are not, as those that are ». — *Christ, meritoriously;* the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter: « As in Adam all die, so also in Christ all shall be made alive ». — *Man, dispositively;* whence Isaias, the fifty-seventh chapter: « He giveth life to the spirit of the humble and giveth life to the heart of the contrite »

2. *The Father*, he says, *raiseth up the dead and giveth life.* — Note that the Father gives life from the death *of nature*, from the death *of fault*, from the death *of misery.* — From the death *of nature* the just and the unjust alike are raised; from the death *of fault* all, the elect and the foreknown alike; from the death *of misery* only the elect. — He gives life, then, from the death *of nature;* to the Romans, the fourth chapter[^p561-7]: « It is he who giveth life to the dead ». — From the death *of fault;* Isaias, the fifty-seventh chapter: « He dwelleth with a contrite and humble spirit, to give life to the spirit of the humble and to give life to the heart of the contrite ». — He gives life from the death *of misery;* to the Romans, the eighth chapter: « He that raised up Jesus from the dead shall give life to our mortal bodies, because of his Spirit that dwelleth in you ». — *The Father*, he says, *giveth life to the dead.* Look above in the fourth chapter[^p561-8] concerning the signs of spiritual life and death.

3. *The Son giveth life to whom he will.* — Note that some things there are which *show* spiritual life, of which it was treated above[^p561-9]; but some which *dispose* to spiritual life. To spiritual life a man *disposes* himself and also *is disposed* by God. — Now a man disposes himself in three ways: *by forsaking sin;* Ezechiel, the eighteenth chapter[^p561-10]: « If the wicked turn himself away from his wickedness, which he hath wrought,

<!-- page 562 -->
and do judgement and justice, he shall give life to his soul ». — *By sorrowing for sin;* Isaias, the fifty-seventh chapter: « The Lord dwelleth with a contrite and humble spirit » etc. — *By exercising himself in good;* the Psalm: « Thy justifications I will never forget, for by them thou hast given me life ».

Man *is disposed* also by God in three ways: *by the scourge of correction;* the first of Kings, the second chapter[^p562-1]: « The Lord killeth and giveth life ». *By the word of preaching;* the Psalm: « Thy word hath given me life ». *By the grace of inspiration;* the Psalm: « Turning, thou hast given me life, and hast brought me back again from the depths of the earth ».

### Collation XXIII.

(Verse 32.). *There is another that giveth testimony of me.*

1. Note that Christ had a fourfold testimony. For he had the testimony *of life*, the testimony *of fame*, the testimony *of Scripture* and the testimony *of the Father's voice.* The first testimony is as it were by *signs;* the second, as it were by *a messenger;* the third, as it were by *letters*, and the fourth, as it were *by living voice.* For in these four ways testimony is wont to be given.

2. Christ had, then, the testimony *of life;* John, the fifth chapter[^p562-2]: « The works which I do, they give testimony of me ». — The testimony *of fame;* John, the fifth chapter; « You sent to John, and he gave testimony to the truth »; and John, the twelfth chapter: « The multitude gave testimony to him, which was with him when he called Lazarus out of the grave ». — The testimony *of Scripture;* John, the fifth chapter: « Search the scriptures, in which you think to have life everlasting; and the same are they that give testimony of me ». — The testimony *of the Father's voice;* John, the fifth chapter: « The Father who sent me, he hath given testimony of me »; likewise: *There is another that giveth testimony of me.*

3. Note that Christ had testimony from *the Scriptures*, from *creatures*, from *the divine persons.* — From *the Scriptures*, namely *the Mosaic, the prophetic* and *the evangelical.* — From the *Mosaic* Scripture; and of this he speaks here[^p562-3]: « Search the scriptures »; and afterwards there follows: « Of me he », namely Moses, « wrote ». — From the *prophetic* Scripture; the Acts, the tenth chapter: « To him all the prophets give testimony, to receive remission » etc. — From the *evangelical* Scripture; John, the nineteenth chapter: « And he that saw it hath given testimony »; and in the last chapter: « This is that disciple » etc.

4. From creatures: *the intellectual*, namely the Angel; *the rational*, namely man; *the insensible*, namely in obeying. — From the *intellectual* creature, namely the Angel, who gives testimony by doing service; above, in the first chapter[^p562-4]: « You shall see the Angels ascending » etc. — From the *rational* creature, namely man; John, the twelfth chapter: « The multitude gave testimony to him, when he called Lazarus out of the grave ». — From the *insensible* creature obeying; Matthew, the twenty-first chapter: « He cursed the fig tree, and immediately it withered away ».

5. From *the divine persons*, namely of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. From the person *of the Father;* John, the fifth chapter[^p562-5]: « The Father who sent me giveth testimony of me ». From the person *of the Son;* John, the eighth chapter: « I am one that give testimony of myself ». From the person *of the Holy Spirit;* John, the fifteenth chapter: « When he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he shall give testimony of me ». — From the whole Trinity together; the first of John, the last chapter: « There are three who give testimony in heaven ».

### Collation XXIV.

(Verse 40.). *You will not come to me that you may have life*, John, the fifth chapter.

1. In these words the Lord does two things. First he *rebukes the malice* of those unwilling to come to him, when he says: *You will not.* — Secondly he *touches on the fruit* of those who come to him, when he says: *That you may have* eternal *life.*

2. He says, then: *You will not come.* — Note that one must come *to* Christ, *after* Christ and *through* Christ. — One must come *to* Christ *teaching*, as to *a teacher*, by *hearing* him; Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p562-6]: « Come to me, all you that labour and are burdened, and I will refresh you ». — One must come *after* Christ *conversing* among us, as after *a pedagogue*, by *imitating* him; Matthew, the fourth chapter: « Come ye after me; I will make you to be fishers of men ». — One must come *through* Christ *suffering*, as through *a door*, by *believing* in him; John, the fourteenth chapter: « No man cometh to the Father but by me ». — Of all these, John, the fourteenth chapter: « I am the way », directing in conversation; « the truth », instructing in preaching; « the life », leading into eternal life in the passion.

3. *You will not*, he says, *come to me.* Likewise he touches on *the fruit* of coming to God, when he says: *That you may have eternal life.* Now this fruit is threefold. The first is *the obtaining of pardon;* the second is *the acquiring of grace;* the third is *the receiving of glory.* — The first

<!-- page 563 -->
fruit, then, is *the obtaining of pardon;* Isaias, the first chapter[^p563-1]: « Seek judgement, relieve the oppressed, judge for the fatherless, defend the widow; and come and accuse me, saith the Lord. If your sins be as scarlet, they shall be made as white as snow; and if they be red as crimson, they shall be white as wool ». — The second fruit is *the acquiring of grace;* Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p563-2]: « Come to me, all you that labour and are burdened, and I will refresh you »; and Isaias, the fifty-fifth chapter: « All you that thirst, come to the waters, and you that have no money make haste, buy and eat. Come, buy without money and without any exchange wine and milk ». — The third fruit is *the receiving of eternal glory;* John, the fifth chapter[^p563-3]: *You will not come to me that you may have eternal life;* and the Canticles, the fourth chapter: « Come from Libanus, come, thou shalt be crowned ».

4. There follows the *manner* of coming to Christ. Now the manner of coming to Christ is threefold, namely by *the faith of truth*, by *the love of goodness*, by *the working of virtue.* — The first manner is by *the faith of truth;* John, the sixth chapter[^p563-4]: « No man can come to me, except the Father, who hath sent me, draw him »; the Gloss: « That is, believes in me ». — The second manner is by *the love of goodness;* the Canticles, the second chapter: « Come, my dove, in the clefts of the rock, in the hollow places of the wall ». — The third manner is by *the working of virtue;* John, the third chapter: « He that doth truth cometh to the light, that his works may be made manifest »; and Luke, the ninth chapter: « If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me ».

5. Now this *working of virtue*, by which one must come to Christ, is threefold. — The first is the way *of the commandments;* the second, the path *of the counsels;* the third, the short cut *of supererogations.* — The first working of virtue is *the way of the commandments;* Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p563-5]: « If thou wilt enter into life, keep the commandments »; and Matthew, the twenty-second chapter: « All things are ready; come ye to the marriage »; and Luke, the fourteenth chapter: « He sent his servant at the hour of supper to say to them that were invited, that they should come ». — The second is *the path of the counsels;* Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « If thou wilt be perfect, go and sell all that thou hast, and give to the poor, and thou shalt have treasure in heaven, and come, follow me ». — The third is *the short cut of supererogations;* Matthew, the fourteenth chapter[^p563-6]: « Lord, if it be thou, bid me come to thee upon the waters ». Note concerning Saint Benedict. And to the Hebrews, the twelfth chapter: « Let us run to the fight proposed to us, looking on Jesus, the author and finisher of faith, who, having joy set before him, endured the cross, despising the shame, and sitteth on the right hand of the throne of God. Think diligently upon him that endured such opposition from sinners against himself, that you be not wearied, fainting in your minds ».

### Collation XXV.

(Verse 28. 25.). *They that are in the graves shall hear the voice of the Son of God, and they that hear shall live.*

1. In these words the order of spiritual resuscitation is touched on, and three things are touched on there. For there is touched on there *the state of death* and *the state of life* and *the way*[^p563-7] of coming to life from death. — *The state of death* is the state of fault, and it is touched on when it is said: *They that are in the graves.* — *The way* of coming to life is the word of teaching[^tr-doctrina], and it is touched on when it is said: *They shall hear the voice of the Son of God* living. — *The state of life* is the state of grace, and it is touched on when it is said: *And they that hear shall live;* as if it were said: they who are in the death[^p563-8] of fault shall hear the voice of teaching and shall live the life of grace.

2. *They that are in the graves shall hear* etc. — Note that the spiritually dead man, that is, the sinner, is signified in Scripture sometimes as being as it were in *the chamber*, sometimes as it were in *the street*, sometimes as it were in *the grave.* As it were in *the chamber* he is dead when the sinner *is slain* inwardly, that is, in *consent*[^p563-9]; as it were in *the street*, when outwardly he *is carried out* in *deed;* as it were in *the grave*, when further he *is buried* in evil custom. — The spiritually dead man, then, sometimes *lies in the chamber*, when he sins in consent alone; and such a one is signified by the daughter of the ruler of the synagogue, whom Jesus raised in the chamber, Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p563-10], Luke, the eighth chapter. — Likewise, the spiritually dead man sometimes *is carried out in the street*, when he sins in deed; and such a one is signified by « the young man, the only son of the widow, his mother », whom he raised at the gate of the city, Luke, the seventh chapter. — Likewise, the spiritually dead man sometimes *is buried in the grave*, when he sins from custom; and such a one is signified by Lazarus, whom Jesus raised having been four days in the grave, John, the eleventh chapter. — Let not the sinner despair, then, whether he has sinned in will, or in deed, or in custom, because even they that are in the graves shall live, *if they hear* the voice of teaching.

<!-- page 564 -->
3. *They shall hear*, he says, *the voice of the Son of God. They shall hear*, I say, for *understanding*, for *believing*, for *obeying.* For the word is to be heard so that we *understand*, according to what is said in the Psalm[^p564-1]: « Hear, O daughter, and see », that is, understand, « and incline thy ear » etc. — It is to be heard also so that we *believe;* John, the fifth chapter: « He that heareth my word and believeth in me hath life everlasting ». — It is to be heard so that we *obey.* So he heard of whom it is said in the Psalm: « At the hearing of the ear he hath obeyed me ». — Because of these three it is said in the Apocalypse, the second chapter: « He that hath ears to hear, let him hear ».

4. In the first manner they do not hear who do not *attend* to the word of God; of whom in the Psalm[^p564-2]: « My people heard not my voice, and Israel attended not to me ». — In the second manner, they who do not *believe* the word of God, of whom Isaias, the sixth chapter: « Blind the heart of this people and make its ears heavy, lest perhaps it see with its eyes and hear with its ears and understand with its heart and be converted, and I heal it ». — In the third manner they do not hear, if any do not *obey* the word of God; they are like him of whom it is said in James, the first chapter: « For if any one be a hearer of the word and not a doer, he shall be compared to a man beholding the countenance of his birth in a glass »; and to such it is said in John, the eighth chapter: « Therefore you hear not, because you are not of God ». — Let us, then, hear *the voice of the Son of God* for *understanding*, for *believing* and for *obeying*, that we may be « hearers and doers of the word, not hearers only »; and so we shall be able to live by the word.

5. There follows: *And they that hear shall live. — They shall live*, I say, by *the faith of truth*, by *the love of goodness* and by *the works of virtue.* For this is the threefold spiritual manner of living, that we live by *faith*, according to what is said in Habacuc, the second chapter[^p564-3]: « My just one liveth in his faith ». Let us live also by *charity;* the first of John, the third chapter: « We know that we have passed from death to life, because we love the brethren. For he that loveth not remaineth in death ». Let us live also by *the works of virtue*, of which Proverbs, the fourth chapter: « For they are life to those that find them »; Ezechiel, the eighteenth chapter: « If a man be just, and do judgement and justice etc.; living he shall live and shall not die ». — Let us go out, then, from the grave of fault, and hear the word of teaching, and we shall live the life of grace[^p564-4].

### Annotation 19.

(Verse 14.). *Behold, thou art made whole.*

1. Note that some the Lord healed *by the hand*, some *by the word*, but some *by a nod* alone. — *By the touch* of his hand he healed the leper; Luke, the fifth chapter[^p564-5]: « Jesus, stretching forth his hand, touched him, saying: I will, be thou cleansed; and immediately the leprosy departed from him ». — *By the power*, namely *of the word*, he healed the paralytic; John, the fifth chapter: « For Jesus said: Take up thy bed and walk. And immediately he was made whole. — *By the nod* of his will alone he healed the ruler's son, who « was sick at Capharnaum », John, the fourth chapter, whom he neither saw nor touched, but healed him by the command of his will alone, when he said to his father: « Thy son liveth ».

2. Understand it so, that the Lord heals some *by the scourge of correction;* Job, the fifth chapter[^p564-6]: « He woundeth and cureth, he striketh, and his hands shall heal ». — Some he heals by addressing them with the word alone *of preaching;* Wisdom, the sixteenth chapter: « It was neither herb nor mollifying plaster that healed them, but thy word, O Lord, which healeth all things ». — But some he heals by enlightening them *with the grace of inspiration*, according to what is said in Malachias, the fourth chapter: « Unto you that fear God the sun of justice shall arise, and health in his wings ».

### Annotation 20.

(Verse 13.). *But he who had been made whole knew not who it was*, supply: who had healed him.

Consider how great is the mercy of God alone: he had healed him, and was not yet *known* by him. How much would he do for him, if he were loved by him, if he were besought by him, who was healing him before he was known by him? In this, then, is the great mercy of God towards us, that his benefits go before our *knowledge*, our *dilection*, our *service* or our working. — He goes before our *knowledge*, as is plain here, when it is said: *He who had been made whole knew not who it was.* — Our *dilection;* whence to the Romans, the fifth chapter[^p564-7]: « When as yet we were enemies, we were reconciled by the death of his Son ». — Our *service* or working; to Titus, the third chapter: « Not by the works of justice, which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us ».

### Annotation 21.

(Verse 19.). *The Son cannot do any thing of himself, but what he seeth the Father doing.*

Note that three things prove that someone is another's son: *abiding, emulation* and *imitation.* For a son is wont *to imitate* his father, *to be emulous* for his father, *to succeed* his father in his abiding. These three proved that Christ is the Son of God. For he held the Father's *abiding;* whence Luke, the second chapter[^p564-8]: « How is it that you sought me? did you not know
<!-- page 565 -->
that I must be in the things that are my Father's »? — He had the Father's *zeal* or emulation, out of which zeal, in John, the second chapter, he said: « Take these things hence, and make not the house of my Father a house of traffic. And his disciples remembered that it was written: The zeal of thy house hath eaten me up ». — He had also the Father's *imitation*, of which it is said here: *The Son cannot do any thing of himself, but what he seeth the Father doing; for what things soever he doth, these the Son also doth in like manner.*
