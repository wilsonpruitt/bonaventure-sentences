# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. III, Vers. 27-36
## *Posita est supra Christi et Ioannis per baptismum purificatio et ex hoc procedens dubitatio; ponitur hic quaestionis terminatio facta a Ioanne, discipulis requirentibus*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 286 -->

49. *Respondit Ioannes et dixit.* Posita est supra[^p286-14] Christi et Ioannis per baptismum purificatio et ex hoc procedens dubitatio; ponitur hic *quaestionis terminatio* facta a Ioanne, discipulis requirentibus. Unde determinat, quod Christus est regenerator, non ipse; et procedit hoc ordine. Primo Ioannes se ipsum humiliat et Christo postponit per *considerationem fragilitatis humanae;* secundo Christum sibi praeponit in *baptizandi potentia;* tertio, in *praedicandi sapientia;* quarto vero, in *omnis gratiae excellentia.*

50. (Vers. 27.). Primo igitur se humiliat per *considerationem fragilitatis humanae* contra discipulos volentes, quod se ipsum et baptismum suum magnificaret. Propter quod dicit: *Non potest homo*
<!-- page 287 -->
*accipere quidquam, nisi fuerit ei datum de caelo;* Iacobi primo[^p287-1]: « Omne datum optimum et omne donum perfectum desursum est, descendens a Patre luminum ». Et ita, cum sim ego purus homo, non possum mihi attribuere, nisi quod mihi datum est; et propterea Christi officium non mihi attribuo, sed praeconis; primae ad Corinthios quarto: « Quid habes, quod non accepisti »?

51. (Vers. 28.). *Ipsi vos mihi testimonium perhibetis, quod dixerim: Non sum ego Christus;* supra primo[^p287-2]: « Et confessus est, quia non sum ego Christus ». *Sed quoniam missus sum ante illum,* tanquam vox ante verbum; supra primo: « Ego vox clamantis in deserto: Dirigite » etc.

52. (Vers. 29.). *Qui habet sponsam* etc. Ostendit hic secundo Christum sibi praepositum in *potentia baptizandi.* Illius enim est *baptizare,* cuius est *regenerare;* et illius *regenerare,* qui habet sponsam, ex qua generet. Propterea dicit: *Qui habet sponsam sponsus est,* et ex ea generare potest et baptizare; ad Ephesios quinto[^p287-3]: « Viri, diligite uxores vestras, sicut et Christus dilexit Ecclesiam et semetipsum tradidit pro ea, ut illam sanctificaret, mundans eam lavacro aquae in verbo vitae ». Beda[^p287-4]: « Christus est sponsus, sponsa est Ecclesia de gentibus congregata; secundae ad Corinthios undecimo: Despondi vos uni viro, virginem castam exhibere Christo ». Illius igitur est regenerare; mei autem non est regenerare nec invidere, sed potius gaudere tanquam amici. Ideo subdit: *Amicus autem sponsi, qui stat,* per gratiam, *et audit illum,* per inspirationem internam, *gaudio gaudet propter vocem sponsi. Gaudio gaudet,* id est, plene gaudet, quia vult secum gaudere. *Amicus;* infra decimo quinto[^p287-5]: « Vos amici mei estis » etc. *Stat;* ad Romanos decimo quarto: « Suo Domino stat, aut cadit ». *Audit;* Psalmus: « Audiam, quid loquatur in me Dominus Deus » etc. *Gaudio gaudet;* ad Philippenses ultimo: « Gaudete in Domino semper » etc. Et hoc modo gaudebat Ioannes tanquam amicus, non dolebat ut invidus[^p287-6]. Ideo dicit: *In hoc ergo gaudium meum impletum est,* id est perfectum, scilicet tunc gaudium est impletum, cum habet quod desiderat; infra decimo sexto[^p287-7]: « Petite, et accipietis, ut gaudium vestrum sit plenum ».

53. (Vers. 30.). *Illum oportet crescere, me autem minui.* Hoc optimum est signum[^p287-8], cum quis gaudet de alterius exaltatione cum sua depressione. *Illum oportet crescere,* in morte, quia exaltatus in cruce; *me autem minui,* in amputato capite. Vel: *illum oportet crescere,* in fama, ut credatur Christus; *me autem minui,* ut qui credor Christus credar eius praecursor. Vel: *illum oportet crescere,* in baptizando et in numero discipulorum; *me minui,* quia baptismus eius et discipulatus eius in proximo cessare debebat, sed Christi e contrario ampliari. De hac ampliatione gaudebat Apostolus ad Philippenses primo[^p287-9]: « Sive per occasionem, sive per veritatem annuntietur Christus, in hoc gaudeo, sed et gaudebo ». Rationem autem, quare Christum oportet crescere, reddit, quia *desursum venit;* propter quod dicit:

54. (Vers. 31.). *Qui desursum venit super omnes est;* Glossa[^p287-10]: « Ideo merito crescit », et propter hoc omnibus supereffertur; Psalmus: « Omnia subiecisti sub pedibus eius » etc.

*Qui de terra est de terra loquitur.* Hic tertio praeponit sibi Christum in *praedicandi sapientia,* quia Ioannes ut terrenus de terrenis loqui poterat, sed Christus caelestia. Propterea dicit: *Qui de terra est de terra loquitur;* talis est Ioannes, qui in peccatis conceptus. *Qui de caelo venit super omnes est,* in dignitate docendi; Christus de caelo venit; primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p287-11]: « Primus homo de terra terrenus, secundus de caelo caelestis »; ideo *de caelo,* quia spiritualis, quia sublimis Deitate; infra octavo: « Vos de deorsum estis, ego de supernis sum ». Et quia de supernis est, de supernis loquitur certitudinaliter. Propter quod dicit:

55. (Vers. 32.). *Et quod vidit et audivit, hoc testatur,* tanquam certus. *Vidit,* ut Dei sapientia, quia se ipso vidit; *audivit,* ut Filius accipiendo a Patre; infra decimo quinto[^p287-12]: « Omnia, quaecumque audivi a Patre meo, nota feci vobis ». Et licet eius doctrina sit excellentissima, tamen a carnalibus despicitur. Propterea subdit: *Et testimonium eius nemo accipit,* id est nullus homo *mundanus;* infra de-
<!-- page 288 -->
cimo quinto[^p288-1]: « Et viderunt et oderunt et me et Patrem meum »; sed *spirituales,* qui accipiunt, de doctrina eius certi fiunt. Propterea dicit:

56. (Vers. 33.). *Qui autem acceperit*[^p288-2] *eius testimonium signavit, quia Deus verax est,* id est, tanquam sigillum in corde certitudinaliter imprimet.

57. (Vers. 34.). *Quem enim misit Deus verba Dei loquitur;* et sic verax est, sicut Deus; infra septimo[^p288-3]: « Si quis voluerit voluntatem Dei facere, cognoscet de doctrina, utrum ex Deo sit, an ego a me ipso loquar ».

*Non enim ad mensuram* etc. Hic quarto praeponit Christum sibi in *gratiae excellentia,* quia omnes in gratia excellit, et ista excellentia est ratio praedictorum. Propterea dicit: *Non enim ad mensuram.* Vere dixi, quod ipse *est sponsus,* ipse *caelestia loquitur,* et *super omnes est: Non enim ad mensuram dat Deus spiritum,* scilicet Christo; non *ad mensuram certam,* sicut aliis[^p288-4]. Et rationem subdit:

58. (Vers. 35.). *Pater enim diligit Filium et omnia dedit in manu eius;* Matthaei undecimo[^p288-5]: « Omnia mihi tradita sunt a Patre meo ». Et ideo nullus salvatur nisi per Filium. Propterea subdit:

59. (Vers. 36.). *Qui credit in Filium habet vitam aeternam,* in merito scilicet; *qui autem incredulus est Filio, non videbit vitam,* non habebit glori[a]m, sed poenam. Unde dicit: *Sed ira Dei manet super eum;* infra duodecimo[^p288-6]: « Qui spernit me et non accipit verba mea habet qui iudicet eum », *ira,* scilicet vindictae, de qua Proverbiorum sexto: « Zelus et furor viri in die vindictae non parcet » etc.; vel *ira,* id est culpa originalis, per quam filius irae; ad Ephesios secundo: « Eramus natura filii irae ».

### Quaestiones.

60. Quaest. I. Sed quaerit Chrysostomus[^p288-7] hic primo de hoc, quod Ioannes vocat se *sponsi amicum,* cum supra non fuerit ausus dicere se dignum servum; supra primo: *Cuius non sum dignus* etc.

Respondet Chrysostomus[^p288-8], quod responsiones aptantur interrogationibus; et supra Ioannes incitabatur ad *ambitionem;* et ideo se humiliavit et indignum servum vocavit. Hic incitatur ad *aemulationem;* et ideo se amicum vocat et gaudere de bono eius dicit.

61. Quaest. II. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit Ioannes: *Qui de terra est de terra loquitur.* — Hoc dixit pro se, et videtur, quod inconvenienter dixerit; quia ipse non loquebatur *terrena,* sed *caelestia.*

Chrysostomus[^p288-9] respondet ad hoc, quod hoc non dixit *absolute,* sed *comparative;* non quia essent terrena, sed quia terrena erant comparata doctrinae Christi. — Augustinus[^p288-10] respondet, quod propositio illa intelligenda est secundum se sive cum reduplicatione: *Qui de terra est,* secundum quod terrenus, *loquitur de terra;* si autem de caelestibus, hoc est secundum quod spiritu imbutus, desursum venit.

62. Quaest. III. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *De caelo venit* etc. — Videtur ex hoc, quod Christus non assumserit carnem humanam, sed caelestem; aut si humanam, quare non dicitur venire de terra ut alii homines?

Respondet Augustinus[^p288-11]: « *De caelo* sive desursum, hoc est de altitudine humanae naturae ante peccatum primi parentis ». — Sed hoc videtur consonare illi haeresi, quod aliqua particula mansit in
<!-- page 289 -->
Adam incorrupta, quae per successiones usque ad Virginem traducta est, et de illa facta est caro Christi[^p289-1].

Propter hoc sane intelligendum est verbum, quod illud dicitur per quandam *assimilationem:* quia, sicut caro in Adam ante peccatum fuit pura et sine macula, sic Christus carnem immaculatam assumsit. Et notandum, quod *terram* non vocat hic naturam *passibilem,* sed *corruptam* et generatam per corruptionem vitiosam[^p289-2], secundum quam non est Christus genitus, sed per virtutem divinam Spiritus sancti conceptus; ideo *desursum.*

63. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Non ad mensuram dat Deus Spiritum.* — Aut intelligit de Christo secundum *divinam* naturam, aut secundum *humanam;* secundum *divinam* naturam non potest, quia ipse non recipit Spiritum, immo ab ipso[^p289-3] procedit. Si secundum *humanam; contra:* Gratia Christi hominis est creata; sed omnia creat Deus in certo *pondere, numero et mensura*[^p289-4], unde nihil creatum est infinitum: ergo ad mensuram.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod est mensura *limitationis,* et est mensura *divisionis.* Mensuram limitationis voco, qua unumquodque creatum finitum est in sua *substantia* et *virtute* et limitatum. Mensuram *divisionis* voco determinationem alicuius ad aliquem actum, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios duodecimo[^p289-5]: *Divisiones gratiarum sunt, idem autem Spiritus,* quando scilicet gratia datur alicui ad aliquem effectum determinatum, sicut dicit Apostolus. Christus gratiam habuit ad mensuram *primo* modo, quia gratia eius non fuit infinita; *secundo* modo habuit non ad mensuram, quia non fuit in eo arctata ad aliquam operationem, sed ad omnem, sicut caput habet omnes sensus. Unde Chrysostomus[^p289-6]: « *Spiritum* hic dicit *actionem Spiritus;* haec enim est, quae dividitur ». Unde patet, quod loquitur de mensura per gratiarum divisionem.

64. Quaest. V. Item de hoc quod dicit: *Qui credit habet vitam aeternam;* quia, si habet, non credit.

**Respondeo:** *Habere spiritualia* est per amorem; et tunc perfecte habentur, quando perfecte amantur[^p289-7].

## English
<!-- page 286 -->

49. *John answered and said.* There was set down above[^p286-14] the purification of Christ and of John through baptism, and the doubt proceeding from this; here is set down *the settling of the question,* made by John, his disciples inquiring. Whence he determines that Christ is the regenerator, not he himself; and he proceeds in this order. First John humbles himself and sets himself after Christ by *the consideration of human frailty;* second he sets Christ before himself in *the power of baptizing;* third, in *the wisdom of preaching;* but fourth, in *the excellence of all grace.*

50. (Verse 27.). First, then, he humbles himself by *the consideration of human frailty* against the disciples, who wished that he should magnify himself and his baptism. On account of which he says: *A man cannot*
<!-- page 287 -->
*receive any thing, unless it be given him from heaven;* James, the first chapter[^p287-1]: « Every best gift and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights[^tr-lumen] ». And so, since I am a mere man, I cannot attribute to myself anything but what is given to me; and therefore I do not attribute to myself the office of Christ, but that of the herald; first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « What hast thou that thou hast not received »?

51. (Verse 28.). *You yourselves give me testimony, that I said: I am not Christ;* above, in the first chapter[^p287-2]: « And he confessed, that I am not the Christ ». *But since I am sent before him,* as the voice before the word; above, in the first chapter: « I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness: Make straight » etc.

52. (Verse 29.). *He that hath the bride* etc. Here he shows, second, that Christ is set before him in *the power of baptizing.* For to baptize belongs to him to whom it belongs *to regenerate;* and *to regenerate* belongs to him who has the bride, from whom he may generate. Therefore he says: *He that hath the bride is the bridegroom,* and from her he can generate and baptize; to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter[^p287-3]: « Husbands, love your wives, as Christ also loved the Church and delivered himself up for it, that he might sanctify it, cleansing it by the laver of water in the word of life ». Bede[^p287-4]: « Christ is the bridegroom, the bride is the Church gathered from the gentiles; second to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter: I have espoused you to one husband, to present you as a chaste virgin to Christ ». To him, therefore, it belongs to regenerate; but to me it belongs not to regenerate nor to envy, but rather to rejoice as a friend. Therefore he subjoins: *But the friend of the bridegroom, who standeth,* through grace, *and heareth him,* through inward inspiration, *rejoiceth with joy because of the bridegroom's voice. Rejoiceth with joy,* that is, rejoices fully, because he wills to rejoice with him. *Friend;* below, in the fifteenth chapter[^p287-5]: « You are my friends » etc. *Standeth;* to the Romans, the fourteenth chapter: « To his own lord he standeth or falleth ». *Heareth;* the Psalm: « I will hear what the Lord God will speak in me » etc. *Rejoiceth with joy;* to the Philippians, the last chapter: « Rejoice in the Lord always » etc. And in this way John rejoiced as a friend, he did not grieve as one envious[^p287-6]. Therefore he says: *This my joy therefore is fulfilled,* that is, perfected; namely, joy is fulfilled then, when one has what one desires; below, in the sixteenth chapter[^p287-7]: « Ask, and you shall receive, that your joy may be full ».

53. (Verse 30.). *He must increase, but I must decrease.* This is the best sign[^p287-8], when someone rejoices at another's exaltation together with his own lowering. *He must increase,* in death, because he was exalted on the cross; *but I must decrease,* in the head cut off. Or: *he must increase,* in renown, so that he may be believed to be the Christ; *but I must decrease,* so that I who am believed to be the Christ may be believed to be his forerunner. Or: *he must increase,* in baptizing and in the number of disciples; *I must decrease,* because his baptism and his discipleship were soon to cease, but Christ's, on the contrary, to be enlarged. In this enlargement the Apostle rejoiced, to the Philippians, the first chapter[^p287-9]: « Whether by occasion or by truth Christ be preached, in this I rejoice, yea, and will rejoice ». And he gives the reason why Christ must increase: because *he cometh from above;* on account of which he says:

54. (Verse 31.). *He that cometh from above is above all;* the Gloss[^p287-10]: « Therefore deservedly he increases », and on account of this he is borne up above all; the Psalm: « Thou hast subjected all things under his feet » etc.

*He that is of the earth speaketh of the earth.* Here, third, he sets Christ before himself in *the wisdom of preaching,* because John, as earthly, could speak of earthly things, but Christ heavenly things. Therefore he says: *He that is of the earth speaketh of the earth;* such is John, who was conceived in sins. *He that cometh from heaven is above all,* in the dignity of teaching; Christ comes from heaven; first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter[^p287-11]: « The first man of the earth, earthly; the second from heaven, heavenly »; therefore *from heaven,* because spiritual, because sublime by the Deity; below, in the eighth chapter: « You are from beneath, I am from above ». And because he is from above, he speaks of things above with certitude. On account of which he says:

55. (Verse 32.). *And what he hath seen and heard, that he testifieth,* as one certain. *He hath seen,* as the wisdom of God, because he saw by himself; *heard,* as the Son, by receiving from the Father; below, in the fifteenth chapter[^p287-12]: « All things whatsoever I have heard of my Father, I have made known to you ». And though his teaching[^tr-doctrina] be most excellent, yet it is despised by the carnal. Therefore he subjoins: *And no man receiveth his testimony,* that is, no *worldly* man; below, in the fif-
<!-- page 288 -->
teenth chapter[^p288-1]: « They have both seen and hated both me and my Father »; but the *spiritual,* who receive it, become certain of his teaching. Therefore he says:

56. (Verse 33.). *He that shall receive*[^p288-2] *his testimony hath set to his seal, that God is true,* that is, he will imprint it with certitude in the heart as a seal.

57. (Verse 34.). *For he whom God hath sent speaketh the words of God;* and so he is true, as God is; below, in the seventh chapter[^p288-3]: « If any man will do the will of God, he shall know of the doctrine, whether it be of God, or whether I speak of myself ».

*For not by measure* etc. Here, fourth, he sets Christ before himself in *the excellence of grace,* because he excels all in grace, and this excellence is the reason of the things said before. Therefore he says: *For not by measure.* Truly I said that he *is the bridegroom,* he *speaketh heavenly things,* and he *is above all: For not by measure doth God give the spirit,* namely to Christ; not *by a fixed measure,* as to others[^p288-4]. And he subjoins the reason:

58. (Verse 35.). *For the Father loveth the Son, and hath given all things into his hand;* Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p288-5]: « All things are delivered to me by my Father ». And therefore no one is saved except through the Son. Therefore he subjoins:

59. (Verse 36.). *He that believeth in the Son hath life everlasting,* namely in merit; *but he that is incredulous to the Son shall not see life,* shall not have glory, but punishment. Whence he says: *But the wrath of God abideth on him;* below, in the twelfth chapter[^p288-6]: « He that despiseth me and receiveth not my words hath one that judgeth him », *wrath,* namely of vengeance, of which Proverbs, the sixth chapter: « The jealousy and the rage of the man will not spare in the day of vengeance » etc.; or *wrath,* that is, original guilt, through which one is a child of wrath; to the Ephesians, the second chapter: « We were by nature children of wrath ».

### Questions.

60. Question I. But Chrysostom[^p288-7] asks here, first, about this: that John calls himself *the friend of the bridegroom,* when above he did not dare to call himself a worthy servant; above, in the first chapter: *Whose I am not worthy* etc.

Chrysostom[^p288-8] answers that the answers are fitted to the questionings; and above John was being incited to *ambition;* and therefore he humbled himself and called himself an unworthy servant. Here he is being incited to *emulation;* and therefore he calls himself a friend and says that he rejoices at his good.

61. Question II. It is asked about this that John says: *He that is of the earth speaketh of the earth.* — He said this of himself, and it is seen that he said it unfittingly; because he did not speak *earthly things,* but *heavenly things.*

Chrysostom[^p288-9] answers to this that he did not say this *absolutely,* but *comparatively;* not because they were earthly, but because they were earthly when compared to the teaching of Christ. — Augustine[^p288-10] answers that that proposition is to be understood in itself, or with a reduplication: *He that is of the earth,* insofar as he is earthly, *speaketh of the earth;* but if he speaks of heavenly things, that is insofar as he is imbued with the spirit, he comes from above.

62. Question III. It is asked about this that he says: *He cometh from heaven* etc. — It is seen from this that Christ did not assume human flesh, but heavenly; or, if human, why is he not said to come from the earth like other men?

Augustine[^p288-11] answers: « *From heaven,* or from above, that is, from the height of human nature before the sin of the first parent ». — But this seems to accord with that heresy, that some particle remained in
<!-- page 289 -->
Adam uncorrupted, which was handed down through the successions as far as the Virgin, and from it the flesh of Christ was made[^p289-1].

On account of this the word is to be understood soundly, that it is said by a certain *assimilation:* because, just as the flesh in Adam before sin was pure and without stain, so Christ assumed immaculate flesh. And it is to be noted that he does not here call *earth* a *passible* nature, but a *corrupt* one, and one generated through vicious corruption[^p289-2], according to which Christ was not begotten, but conceived by the divine power of the Holy Spirit; therefore *from above.*

63. Question IV. Likewise it is asked about this that he says: *Not by measure doth God give the Spirit.* — Either he understands it of Christ according to the *divine* nature, or according to the *human;* according to the *divine* nature he cannot, because he does not receive the Spirit, nay, the Spirit proceeds from him[^p289-3]. If according to the *human; on the contrary:* The grace of Christ the man is created; but God creates all things in definite *weight, number and measure*[^p289-4], whence nothing created is infinite: therefore by measure.

**I respond:** It must be said that there is a measure *of limitation,* and there is a measure *of division.* I call the measure of limitation that by which each created thing is finite in its *substance* and *power,* and limited. I call the measure *of division* the determination of something to some act, according to that of first to the Corinthians, the twelfth chapter[^p289-5]: *There are diversities of graces, but the same Spirit,* namely when grace is given to someone for some determinate effect, as the Apostle says. Christ had grace by measure in the *first* way, because his grace was not infinite; in the *second* way he had it not by measure, because it was not narrowed in him to some one operation, but to every one, just as the head has all the senses. Whence Chrysostom[^p289-6]: « *Spirit* here he calls *the action of the Spirit;* for it is this which is divided ». Whence it is clear that he speaks of measure through the division of graces.

64. Question V. Likewise, about this that he says: *He that believeth hath life everlasting;* because, if he has it, he does not believe.

**I respond:** *To have spiritual things* is through love; and they are had perfectly then, when they are loved perfectly[^p289-7].
