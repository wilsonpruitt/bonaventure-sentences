---

## Latin
<!-- page 581 -->

### Capitulum X. *Amen, amen dico vobis.*

### Collatio XXXIX.

(Vers. 1.). *Amen, amen dico vobis, qui non intrat per ostium in ovile ovium, sed ascendit aliunde, ille fur est et latro.*

1. In praesenti Evangelio distinguitur pastor *non verus* a pastore *vero,* et ex *modo ingrediendi* — et ex *modo praesidendi.* Ex *modo intrandi,* hic: *Qui non intrat;* ex *modo praesidendi,* infra[^p581-2]: « Bonus pastor animam suam dat pro ovibus suis ». — Ex modo ingrediendi agnoscitur *verus* pastor, quando *intrat per ostium,* id est Christum; unde hic dicitur: *Qui intrat per ostium pastor est ovium; non verus* vero probatur qui *ascendit aliunde;* unde praemittitur: *Qui non intrat per ostium in ovile ovium, sed ascendit aliunde, ille* etc.

2. *Qui non intrat per ostium.* Nota, quod Christus est huiusmodi ostium, qui se ipsum significat in praesenti Evangelio et sub figura *ostii* — et sub figura *pastoris* — et sub figura *ostiarii;* et congrue. — Ipse enim fuit *pastor,* loquendo per appropriationem, in *praedicatione,* quando pavit oves suas cibo doctrinae; unde Isaiae quadragesimo[^p581-3]: « Sicut pastor gregem suum pascet » etc.; et in praesenti Evangelio ipse dicit: « Ego sum pastor bonus ». — Fuit *ostium* in *passione,* quia tunc per ipsum nobis patuit iter vitae; unde ipse dicit in praesenti Evangelio[^p581-4]: « Ego sum ostium »; et Ioannis decimo quarto: « Nemo venit ad Patrem nisi per me ». — Fuit *ostiarius* in *resurrectione;* tunc enim data est illi potestas alios introducendi in ovile; Matthaei ultimo: « Data est mihi omnis potestas » etc. Hic est ostiarius, qui dicit supra sexto: « Eum qui venit ad me, non eiiciam foras »; et de quo hic dicitur: « Huic ostiarius aperit ». — Sic ergo Christus est *pastor* et *ostiarius* et *ostium,* per quod ingreditur quisquis est pastor ovium.

3. Sed attende, quod multi, relicto ostio, *ascendunt aliunde.* Aliunde enim ascendunt qui intrant *simoniace,* sicut illi qui per pecuniam vel per munera quaecumque intrant in ovile; de quibus tertii Regum decimo tertio[^p581-5]: « Quicumque implebat manum Ieroboam, fiebat sacerdos excelsorum ». De his dicitur Osee octavo: « Ipsi regnaverunt, sed non ex me; principes exstiterunt, et non cognovi; argentum suum et aurum suum fecerunt sibi idola, ut interirent ». Isti volunt habere patrimonium Christi titulo *emptionis;* quibus dicetur Actuum octavo: « Pecunia tua tecum sit in perditionem ». — Item, aliunde ascendunt et non intrant per ostium qui intrant *pompatice,* ut illi qui praeficiuntur ratione parentelae; quibus dicitur Amos sexto[^p581-6]: « Vae vobis! qui opulenti estis in Sion et confiditis in monte Samariae, optimates capita populorum, ingredientes pompatice domum Israel ». Isti volunt possidere patrimonium Christi titulo *hereditariae successionis,* qui dixerunt, secundum quod habetur in Psalmo: « Hereditate possideamus sanctuarium Dei ». — Aliunde ascendunt et non per ostium qui intrant per *fallaciam,* sicut hypocritae; de quibus Matthaei septimo[^p581-7]: « Attendite a falsis prophetis, qui veniunt ad vos in vestimentis ovium, intrinsecus autem sunt lupi rapaces ». Huiusmodi luporum ingressum timebat Apostolus, Actuum vigesimo: « Scio, quoniam post discessionem meam intrabunt lupi rapaces in vos » etc. Isti volunt possidere patrimonium Christi titulo *mercenariae conductionis.* Volunt enim, ut suis reddatur meritis. — Isti ergo non intrant per ostium, sed *fures sunt et latrones,* non veri possessores, qui non possident sanctuarium Dei titulo sanctae vocationis.

4. Non sic intrat *verus pastor,* sed per ostium, id est per Christum. Et nota, quod *verus* pastor debet intrare *per* Christum — *post* Christum — et *propter* Christum. — *Per Christum* debet intrare quisquam vocationi eius obediendo. « Neque enim quisquam assumat sibi honorem, sed qui vocatur a Deo tanquam Aaron. Sic et Christus non semetipsum clarificavit, ut pontifex fieret[^p581-8] ». Sic ergo intrandum
<!-- page 582 -->
est per Christum; Ioannis decimo: « Ego sum ostium. Per me si quis introierit, ingredietur et egredietur et pascua inveniet ». — Debet ingredi *post Christum* ipsum imitando, ut possit dicere illud Apostoli[^p582-1]: « Imitatores mei estote, sicut et ego Christi ». De hoc Ieremiae tertio: « Patrem vocabis me, et post me ingredi non cessabis ». Talem enim vult Deus « habitare in tabernaculo », « qui ingreditur sine macula et operatur iustitiam ». — Debet etiam ingredi *propter Christum* nihil aliud praeter Christum quaerendo; unde primae Petri quinto[^p582-2]: « Pascite qui in vobis est gregem Dei, providentes non coacte, sed spontanee secundum Deum, neque turpis lucri gratia, sed voluntarie, neque ut dominantes in cleris, sed forma facti gregis ex animo, ut, cum apparuerit Princeps pastorum, percipiatis immarcescibilem gloriae coronam ».

5. *Bonus pastor animam suam dat pro ovibus suis*[^p582-3].

Hic distinguitur pastor bonus a non bono ex *modo praesidendi.* Notandum igitur, quod pastor bene praesidens debet esse in cognoscendo *sollicitus,* in supportando *pius,* in deducendo *primus,* in protegendo *probus.* — Debet esse *sollicitus in cognoscendo* per vigiliam intuitionis, qualis fuit Christus; Ioannis decimo[^p582-4]: « Ego sum pastor bonus et cognosco oves meas »; et Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo: « Diligenter agnosce vultum pecoris tui, tuosque greges considera ». — Debet esse in *supportando pius* per affectum pietatis, qualis fuit Christus; Isaiae quadragesimo[^p582-5]: « Sicut pastor gregem suum pascet, in brachio suo congregabit agnos et in sinu suo levabit, foetas ipse portabit ». Quocontra Ezechielis trigesimo quarto: « Vae pastoribus Israel »! et sequitur causa: « Gregem, inquit, meum non pascebatis; quod infirmum erat non consolidastis, et quod aegrotum erat non sanastis, quod fractum erat non alligastis et quod abiectum erat non reduxistis; sed cum austeritate imperabatis eis et cum potentia ». — Debet etiam esse in *deducendo primus* per exemplum honestatis; Ioannis decimo[^p582-6] dicitur de bono pastore: « Cum proprias oves emiserit, ante eas vadit, et oves illum sequuntur ». Talis erat Apostolus, qui dicebat primae ad Corinthios undecimo: « Imitatores mei estote, sicut et ego Christi. — Debet etiam esse in *protegendo probus* per constantiam virtutis, qualis fuit Christus; Ioannis decimo: *Bonus pastor animam suam dat pro ovibus suis.* Talis fuit Iacob; Genesis trigesimo primo: « Arietem gregis tui non comedi, nec captum a bestia ostendi tibi. Ego damnum omne reddebam; quidquid furto perierat, a me exigebas ». — Istae igitur sunt conditiones boni pastoris.

6. Sed nota, quod multi sunt pastores, qui non habent vigilantiam rationis, sed sunt quasi *idola,* gregem negligentes. — Sunt alii, qui non habent affectum pietatis, sed sunt *lupi* gregem devorantes. — Sunt et alii, qui non exhibent exemplum honestatis, sed sunt *fures* gregem abigentes[^p582-7]. — Sunt et alii, qui non adhibent praesidium defensionis, sed sunt *mercenarii* gregem in necessitate dimittentes.

7. Sunt ergo sicut *idola* gregem non curantes, quibus dicitur Zachariae undecimo[^p582-8]: « O pastor et idolum, derelinquens gregem »! et Isaiae vigesimo secundo: « Quis tu hic, aut quasi quis hic »? — Sunt alii sicut *lupi* gregem devorantes; de quibus Actuum vigesimo[^p582-9]: « Scio, inquit Paulus, quoniam post discessionem meam intrabunt lupi rapaces in vos, non parcentes gregi »; de talibus Ieremiae duodecimo: « Pastores multi demoliti sunt vineam meam »; et ideo dicitur ibidem: « Venite et congregamini, omnes bestiae terrae; properate ad devorandum ». — Sunt alii ut *fures* gregem abigentes; Ezechielis trigesimo quarto[^p582-10]: « Vae pastoribus Israel! qui pascebant semetipsos; nonne greges a pastoribus pascuntur? Lac comedebatis et lanis operiebamini, gregem autem meum non pascebatis »; et post paululum: « Dispersae sunt oves meae, eo quod non esset pastor ». Rationem huius reddit Dominus Ioannis decimo; quia scilicet « fur non venit, nisi ut furetur et mactet et perdat ». — Sunt et alii *mercenarii* gregem in necessitate dimittentes; Ioannis decimo[^p582-11]: « Mercenarius, et qui non est pastor, cuius non sunt oves propriae, videt lupum venientem et dimittit oves et fugit, et lupus rapit et dispergit oves. Mercenarius autem fugit, quia mercenarius est, et non pertinet ad eum de ovibus ».

8. *Cognosco oves meas, et cognoscunt me meae*[^p582-12].

Describitur hic bonitas *pastoris* ex *comparatione ad oves,* cum dicit Dominus: *Ego sum pastor bonus et cognosco oves meas* — et bonitas *ovis* ex *comparatione ad pastorem,* cum dicit: *Et cognoscunt me meae. Cognosco,* inquit, eas, *et cognoscunt me meae.* Cognosco eas per *electionem* et per *approbationem,* et cognoscunt me per *fidem* et *dilectionem.*
<!-- page 583 -->

9. *Cognosco,* inquit, *oves meas.* Nota, quod cognoscitur ovis ex quatuor. Cognoscitur enim *vultu, balatu, vellere* et *signo.* — Cognoscitur *vultu,* secundum illud Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo[^p583-1]: « Diligenter agnosce vultum pecoris tui »; *vultus spiritualis* est conscientia, de qua Canticorum secundo: « Ostende mihi faciem tuam ». Faciem istam non habentibus mundanis dicitur Matthaei septimo: « Non novi vos ». — Cognoscitur *balatu;* per *balatum* intellige *confessionis vocem;* unde in Psalmo[^p583-2]: « Populus eius et oves pascuae eius; introite portas eius in confessione ». De hoc balatu Canticorum secundo: « Sonet vox tua in auribus meis ». — Cognoscitur *vellere;* unde quando ovis devoratur a bestia, solet ostendi pellis in signum, secundum illud Genesis trigesimo primo[^p583-3]: « Captum a bestia non ostendi tibi »; quasi: si captum fuisset, ostendissem tibi. Per *vellus* intellige *honestam conversationem.* Debet enim ovis spiritualis habere *vellus* agninae conversationis, secundum illud Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo: « Sint tibi agni ad vestimentum tuum ». — Cognoscitur etiam *signo.* Per signum intellige *dominicam passionem.* Debet enim signari ovis spiritualis signo dominicae passionis; Canticorum octavo[^p583-4]: « Pone me ut signaculum super cor tuum ».

10. Sequitur: *Et cognoscunt me meae.*

Ut scias, qualiter ovis se debet habere ad pastorem, nota, quod ovis debet pastorem *cognoscere,* ad pastorem *accedere,* pastorem *audire* et post pastorem *ire.* — Pastorem *cognoscit* ovis per *fidem,* per gratiarum actionem; de quo Ioannis decimo[^p583-5]: « Cognosco oves meas, et cognoscunt me meae ». — Ad pastorem *accedit* per *caritatem;* unde primae Petri secundo: « Eratis sicut oves errantes, sed conversi estis nunc ad pastorem et episcopum animarum vestrarum ». — Oberrant enim oves peccando, sed convertuntur, quando pastorem *audiunt* per *obedientiam;* Ioannis decimo[^p583-6]: « Oves meae vocem meam audiunt ». — *Sequitur* pastorem per *imitationem;* Ioannis decimo: « Cum proprias oves emiserit, ante eas vadit, et oves illum sequuntur, quia sciunt vocem eius »; et infra eodem: « Cognosco oves meas, et sequuntur me, et ego vitam aeternam do eis ».

11. *Proprias oves vocat nominatim*[^p583-7].

Nota hic tria de *pastore bono.* Primo, quod oves *vocat nominatim,* cum dicitur: *Proprias oves vocat nominatim.* Secundo, quod *educit,* cum dicitur[^p583-8]: *Et educit.* Tertio, quod *deducit,* cum subiungitur: *Et cum proprias oves emiserit, ante eas vadit.* Bonus enim pastor debet oves *vocare* verbo praedicationis et *educere* Sacramento confessionis et *deducere* exemplo sanctae conversationis.

12. Nota iuxta hoc tria de *bona ove.* Debet enim ovis esse *humilis,* ut pastori obediat; debet esse *mitis,* ne gregem offendat; debet esse *foetosa,* ut patrifamilias fructum afferat. — Debet ergo esse *humilis* per obedientiam; unde in Psalmo[^p583-9]: « Deducis velut ovem Ioseph »; velut ovis debet esse ducibilis Ioseph: aliquando *virga* correptionis, aliquando *pane* consolationis, aliquando *cane* tentationis. — Debet esse *mitis* per innocentiam, iuxta illud secundi Regum ultimo[^p583-10]: « Isti qui oves sunt, quid fecerunt »? Unde illud:

> Quae meruistis, oves, animal sine fraude dolisque?

Debet esse *foetosa* per bonorum operum abundantiam, iuxta illud Psalmi[^p583-11]: « Oves eorum foetosae, abundantes in egressibus suis »; et Canticorum quarto: « Omnes gemellis foetibus, et sterilis non est inter eas ».

### Collatio XL.

(Vers. 27. 28.). *Oves meae sequuntur me; et ego vitam aeternam do eis.*

1. In verbis istis praemittit Dominus ovis spiritualis, id est animae fidelis, *meritum,* cum dicit: *Oves meae sequuntur me* — et promittit *praemium,* cum adiungit: *Et ego vitam aeternam do eis.*

Praemittens ergo *meritum,* dicit: *Sequuntur me.* Nota, quod sequuntur quidam per *planitiem mandatorum,* quidam per *montana consiliorum,* quidam per *excelsa supererogationum;* quod figuratum est in Evangelio in tribus generibus sequentium Dominum. Sequuntur Dominum *turbae — discipuli* — et *Apostoli: turbae* per *plana mandatorum;* Matthaei octavo[^p583-12]: « Cum descendisset Iesus de monte, secutae sunt eum turbae multae ». — Discipuli per *montana;* Lucae vigesimo secundo: « Egressus Iesus ibat secundum consuetudinem suam in montem Olivarum; secuti sunt autem eum discipuli eius ». — Apostoli per *excelsa supererogationum;* Matthaei decimo nono et Marci decimo et Lucae decimo octavo: « Ecce, nos reliquimus omnia et secuti sumus te ». Primi, *incipientes;* secundi, *proficientes;* tertii, *perfecti.* Primi sequuntur quasi per *viam;* secundi, quasi per *semitam;* tertii, quasi per *compendium.*

2. Dicit ergo: *Sequuntur me* cum *turbis* per planitiem mandatorum, et hoc tripliciter: per *fidem* in intellectu; tertii Regum decimo octavo[^p583-13], verba Eliae
<!-- page 584 -->
ad populum: « Usquequo claudicatis in duas partes? Si Dominus est Deus, sequimini eum; si autem Baal, sequimini illum ». — Per *caritatem* in affectu; tertii Regum decimo quarto, verba Ahiae ad Ieroboam: « Non fuisti sicut servus meus David, qui secutus est me in toto corde suo, faciens quod placitum esset in conspectu meo » etc. — Per *obedientiam* in effectu; Iob vigesimo tertio[^p584-1]: « Vestigia eius secutus est pes meus. Viam eius custodivi et non declinavi ex ea. A mandatis labiorum eius non recessi » etc.; Ioannis decimo: « Cum proprias oves emiserit, vadit ante eas, et oves eum sequuntur, quia audiunt vocem eius ». — Sequuntur ergo Dominum per *fidem,* per *caritatem* et per *obedientiam;* quia sine his nullus sequitur; ad Galatas quinto: « In Christo enim Iesu neque circumcisio aliquid valet neque praeputium, sed fides, quae per caritatem operatur ». — *Fides* quidem aspectum dirigit, *caritas* affectum attrahit, *operatio* effectum deducit.

3. Sequuntur ergo Dominum cum *discipulis* per *montana consiliorum,* et hoc tripliciter secundum triplex consilium: per *carnis mortificationem* contra concupiscentiam carnis; Matthaei decimo[^p584-2]: « Qui non accipit crucem suam et sequitur me non est me dignus ». — Per *temporalium* dimissionem contra concupiscentiam oculorum; Matthaei decimo nono, Marci decimo, Lucae decimo octavo: « Si vis perfectus esse, vade et vende omnia, quae habes, et da pauperibus, et habebis thesaurum in caelo, et veni et sequere me ». — Per *propriae voluntatis abiectionem* contra superbiam vitae; Matthaei decimo sexto[^p584-3]: « Si quis vult post me venire, abneget semetipsum et tollat crucem suam et sequatur me »; et Matthaei octavo.

4. Sequuntur iterum Dominum cum *Apostolis,* per excellentiam supererogationum, et hoc tripliciter: per excellentiam *puritatis* cum *virginibus,* de quibus Apocalypsis decimo quarto[^p584-4]: « Hi sunt, qui cum mulieribus non sunt coinquinati, virgines enim sunt; hi sequuntur Agnum, quocumque ierit ». — Per excellentiam *probitatis* cum *Apostolis,* qui « profecti ubique praedicaverunt, Domino cooperante et sermonem confirmante sequentibus signis ». Huiusmodi *sequi* figuratum est primi Regum decimo quarto[^p584-5], dicente « Ionatha ad armigerum suum: Sequere me »; et sequitur ibidem, quia « ascendit Ionathas contra stationem Philisthinorum, manibus et pedibus reptans, et armiger eius post eum ». — Per *imitationem passionis* cum *Martyribus;* primae Petri secundo[^p584-6]: « Christus passus est pro nobis, vobis relinquens exemplum, ut sequamini vestigia eius »; et Ioannis vigesimo primo dixit Dominus Petro: « Sequere me »; et infra de Ioanne: « Sic eum volo manere, donec veniam; quid ad te? Tu me sequere ». — Sequuntur ergo Dominum per obedientiam mandatorum cum *turbis,* per eminentiam consiliorum cum *discipulis,* per excellentiam supererogationum cum *Apostolis.*

5. Nota, quod sunt quidam, qui sequi Dominum *nolunt* — sunt qui *negligunt* — et sunt qui non *possunt.* — Sunt autem duo impedimenta, quae reddunt homines *nolentes.* Primum *amor praesentis prosperitatis,* qui avertit illum, de quo Matthaei decimo nono[^p584-7], Marci decimo, Lucae decimo octavo, cum dixit Dominus: « Vade et vende etc., et veni, sequere me. At ille abiit tristis; erat enim multas possessiones habens ». — Secundum est *timor futurae adversitatis,* qui avertit illos quibus iuravit Dominus Numerorum trigesimo secundo[^p584-8]: « Si videbunt homines isti, qui ascenderunt ex Aegypto a viginti annis et supra, terram, quam sub iuramento pollicitus sum Abraham, Isaac et Iacob; et noluerunt sequi me praeter Caleb, filium Iephone, et Iosue, filium Nun; isti impleverunt voluntatem meam ». Nota causam in historia.

6. Sunt similiter duo impedimenta, quae reddunt homines *negligentes.* Primum est *affectio carnalis,* quae notatur in illo, de quo Lucae nono[^p584-9], cui dixit Dominus: « Sequere me. Ille autem dixit: Domine, dimitte, me primum sepelire patrem meum ». Et nota, quid Dominus dixit illi. — Secundum est *sollicitudo temporalis,* quae notatur in illo, de quo eodem Lucae nono, qui dixit Domino: « Sequar te, Domine; sed primum permitte, me renuntiare his quae domi sunt. Ait ad illum Iesus: Nemo mittens manum ad aratrum » etc.

7. Sunt similiter duo impedimenta, quae reddunt homines *impotentes.* Primum est *perversitas intentionis,* quae impedit progressum *affectionis.* Secundum est *infirmitas virtutis,* quae impedit
<!-- page 585 -->
progressum *operationis.* Primum notatur in illo qui dixit Lucae nono[^p585-1]: « Sequar te, quocumque ieris », cuius intentionem cognoscens, Dominus dixit: « Vulpes foveas habent et volucres caeli nidos » etc. — Secundum notatur in Petro, Ioannis decimo tertio, cui, cum dixit Domino: « Domine, quo vadis? respondit Iesus: Quo ego vado, non potes me modo sequi; sequeris autem postea ».

8. Et nota, quomodo primo Petrus se obtulit non solum ad sequendum, sed ad praecedendum, vel comitandum, dicens[^p585-2]: « Domine, quare non possum te sequi modo? Animam meam pro te ponam ». Secundo, quando « de longe sequebatur », non solum non praecessit, sed nec de prope secutus est, sed « a longe, ut videret finem », Matthaei vigesimo sexto. Tertio, nec de longe nec de prope secutus est, quando eum negavit, eodem; et hoc, quia infirmus fuit.

9. *Sequuntur Dominum* etc. Habito superius[^p585-3] de *merito* in sequendo Dominum, hic tangitur *praemium* de sequendo ipsum. Nota, quod sequi Dominum *fructum* habet in *praesenti* — et *praemium* in *futuro.* — In *praesenti,* quia *honorificum;* Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio[^p585-4]: « Gloria magna est sequi Dominum ». — Quia *tutum;* Ioannis octavo: « Qui sequitur me non ambulat in tenebris »; Ioannis decimo: « Cognosco oves meas, et sequuntur me; et ego vitam aeternam do eis, et non peribunt in aeternum, neque rapiet eas quisquam de manu mea ». — Quia *proficuum.* Dominus enim sequentibus se triplex dat beneficium: *sanationis;* Matthaei decimo quarto[^p585-5]: « Secutae sunt eum turbae multae pedestres de civitatibus, et exiens vidit turbam magnam et misertus est eis et curavit languidos eorum »; simile Matthaei nono, Marci decimo, Lucae decimo octavo de duobus caecis. — *Instructionis;* Lucae nono: « Secutae sunt eum turbae, et excepit eos et loquebatur illis de regno Dei, et eos qui cura indigebant, sanabat ». — *Refectionis;* infra eodem[^p585-6] de miraculo quinque panum et duorum piscium; simile Marci secundo: « Multi publicani et peccatores discumbebant cum Iesu et discipulis eius. Erant enim multi, qui sequebantur eum ».

10. Similiter sequi Dominum habet *praemium* in *futuro:* et promittitur sequentibus *sublimitas honoris;* Matthaei decimo nono[^p585-7]: « Vos, qui secuti estis me etc.; sedebitis et vos super sedes duodecim, iudicantes duodecim tribus Israel ». — *Possessio hereditatis;* Iosue decimo quarto: « Terra, quam calcavit pes tuus, erit possessio tua et filiorum tuorum usque in sempiternum, quia secutus es Dominum Deum meum ». — *Lumen iucunditatis;* Ioannis octavo: « Qui sequitur me non ambulat in tenebris, sed habebit lumen vitae ». — *Securitas aeternitatis;* Ioannis decimo[^p585-8]: « Cognosco oves meas, et sequuntur me, et ego vitam aeternam do eis, et non peribunt in aeternum, neque rapiet eas quisquam de manu mea »; Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio: « Gloria magna sequi Dominum; longitudo dierum assumetur ab eo ».

### Chapter X. *Amen, amen I say to you.*

### Collation XXXIX.

(Verse 1.). *Amen, amen I say to you, he that entereth not by the door into the fold of the sheep, but climbeth up another way, the same is a thief and a robber.*

1. In the present Gospel the shepherd *not true* is distinguished from the shepherd *true,* both from *the mode of entering* — and from *the mode of presiding.* From *the mode of entering,* here: *He that entereth not;* from *the mode of presiding,* below[^p581-2]: « The good shepherd giveth his life for his sheep ». — From the mode of entering the *true* shepherd is recognized, when he *entereth by the door,* that is, Christ; whence it is said here: *He that entereth in by the door is the shepherd of the sheep;* but he is proved *not true* who *climbeth up another way;* whence it is set down before: *He that entereth not by the door into the fold of the sheep, but climbeth up another way, the same* etc.

2. *He that entereth not by the door.* Note that Christ is a door of this kind, who signifies himself in the present Gospel both under the figure *of the door* — and under the figure *of the shepherd* — and under the figure *of the porter;* and fittingly. — For he was *the shepherd,* speaking by appropriation, in *the preaching,* when he fed his sheep with the food of teaching[^tr-doctrina]; whence Isaias, the fortieth chapter[^p581-3]: « As a shepherd he shall feed his flock » etc.; and in the present Gospel he himself says: « I am the good shepherd ». — He was *the door* in *the passion,* because then through him the way of life lay open to us; whence he himself says in the present Gospel[^p581-4]: « I am the door »; and John, the fourteenth chapter: « No man cometh to the Father, but by me ». — He was *the porter* in *the resurrection;* for then power was given to him of bringing others into the fold; Matthew, the last chapter: « All power is given to me » etc. This is the porter who says above, in the sixth chapter: « Him that cometh to me, I will not cast out »; and of whom it is said here: « To him the porter openeth ». — Thus, then, Christ is *the shepherd* and *the porter* and *the door,* through which whoever is shepherd of the sheep enters in.

3. But attend that many, the door being left, *climb up another way.* For they climb up another way who enter *simoniacally,* as those who enter into the fold through money or through gifts of whatever kind; of whom the third of Kings, the thirteenth chapter[^p581-5]: « Whosoever filled the hand of Jeroboam became a priest of the high places ». Of these it is said in Osee, the eighth chapter: « They have reigned, but not by me; they have been princes, and I knew not; their silver and their gold they have made idols to themselves, that they might perish ». These wish to have the patrimony of Christ by title *of purchase;* to whom it will be said, Acts, the eighth chapter: « Thy money be with thee unto perdition ». — Likewise, they climb up another way and do not enter by the door who enter *pompously,* as those who are set over others by reason of kinship; to whom it is said, Amos, the sixth chapter[^p581-6]: « Woe to you who are wealthy in Sion, and have confidence in the mountain of Samaria, ye great men, heads of the peoples, going in pompously into the house of Israel ». These wish to possess the patrimony of Christ by title *of hereditary succession,* who have said, according to what is had in the Psalm: « Let us possess the sanctuary of God for an inheritance ». — They climb up another way and not by the door who enter through *deceit,* as hypocrites; of whom Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p581-7]: « Beware of false prophets, who come to you in the clothing of sheep, but inwardly they are ravening wolves ». The entrance of wolves of this kind the Apostle feared, Acts, the twentieth chapter: « I know that after my departure ravening wolves will enter in among you » etc. These wish to possess the patrimony of Christ by title *of mercenary hire.* For they wish that it be rendered to their merits. — These, then, do not enter by the door, but *are thieves and robbers,* not true possessors, who do not possess the sanctuary of God by title of holy vocation.

4. Not so does *the true shepherd* enter, but by the door, that is, through Christ. And note that the *true* shepherd ought to enter *through* Christ — *after* Christ — and *for the sake of* Christ. — *Through Christ* one ought to enter by obeying his calling. « For neither let any man take the honour to himself, but he that is called by God, as Aaron was. So Christ also did not glorify himself, that he might be made a high priest[^p581-8] ». Thus, then, one must enter
<!-- page 582 -->
through Christ; John, the tenth chapter: « I am the door. By me, if any man enter in, he shall go in, and go out, and shall find pastures ». — He ought to go in *after Christ* by imitating him, so that he can say that word of the Apostle[^p582-1]: « Be ye imitators of me, as I also am of Christ ». Of this Jeremias, the third chapter: « Thou shalt call me father, and shalt not cease to go in after me ». For such a one God wills « to dwell in the tabernacle », « who goeth in without blemish, and worketh justice ». — He ought also to go in *for the sake of Christ,* seeking nothing else besides Christ; whence the first of Peter, the fifth chapter[^p582-2]: « Feed the flock of God which is among you, taking care of it, not by constraint, but willingly, according to God; not for filthy lucre's sake, but voluntarily; neither as lording it over the clergy, but being made a pattern of the flock from the heart; that, when the Prince of pastors shall appear, you may receive a never fading crown of glory ».

5. *The good shepherd giveth his life for his sheep*[^p582-3].

Here the good shepherd is distinguished from the one not good by *the mode of presiding.* It is to be noted, then, that the shepherd presiding well ought to be *solicitous* in knowing, *pious* in bearing with, *first* in leading, *upright* in protecting. — He ought to be *solicitous in knowing* through the watchfulness of regard, such as Christ was; John, the tenth chapter[^p582-4]: « I am the good shepherd, and I know my sheep »; and Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter: « Diligently know the countenance of thy cattle, and consider thy flocks ». — He ought to be *pious in bearing with* through the affection of piety, such as Christ was; Isaias, the fortieth chapter[^p582-5]: « As a shepherd he shall feed his flock, in his arm he shall gather together the lambs, and shall take them up in his bosom; he himself shall carry them that are with young ». Against which Ezechiel, the thirty-fourth chapter: « Woe to the shepherds of Israel »! and the cause follows: « My flock, he says, you did not feed; that which was weak you did not strengthen, and that which was sick you did not heal, that which was broken you did not bind up, and that which was cast away you did not bring back; but you ruled over them with severity and with might ». — He ought also to be *first in leading* through the example of uprightness; in John, the tenth chapter[^p582-6], it is said of the good shepherd: « When he hath let out his own sheep, he goeth before them, and the sheep follow him ». Such was the Apostle, who said, the first to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter: « Be ye imitators of me, as I also am of Christ. — He ought also to be *upright in protecting* through the constancy of virtue, such as Christ was; John, the tenth chapter: *The good shepherd giveth his life for his sheep.* Such was Jacob; Genesis, the thirty-first chapter: « The ram of thy flock I did not eat, neither did I shew thee that which the beast had taken. I made good all the damage; whatsoever was lost by theft, thou didst exact it of me ». — These, therefore, are the conditions of the good shepherd.

6. But note that there are many shepherds who have not the watchfulness of reason, but are as *idols,* neglecting the flock. — There are others who have not the affection of piety, but are *wolves* devouring the flock. — There are also others who do not show the example of uprightness, but are *thieves* driving off the flock[^p582-7]. — There are also others who do not apply the protection of defence, but are *hirelings* forsaking the flock in necessity.

7. There are, then, those who are as *idols,* not caring for the flock, to whom it is said, Zacharias, the eleventh chapter[^p582-8]: « O shepherd, and idol, that forsaketh the flock »! and Isaias, the twenty-second chapter: « Who art thou here, or as if thou wert somebody here »? — There are others as *wolves* devouring the flock; of whom Acts, the twentieth chapter[^p582-9]: « I know, says Paul, that after my departure ravening wolves will enter in among you, not sparing the flock »; of such Jeremias, the twelfth chapter: « Many pastors have destroyed my vineyard »; and therefore it is said in the same place: « Come ye, assemble yourselves, all the beasts of the earth; make haste to devour ». — There are others as *thieves* driving off the flock; Ezechiel, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p582-10]: « Woe to the shepherds of Israel, that fed themselves! should not the flocks be fed by the shepherds? You ate the milk, and you clothed yourselves with the wool, but my flock you did not feed »; and a little after: « And my sheep were scattered, because there was no shepherd ». The Lord gives the reason of this in John, the tenth chapter; namely because « the thief cometh not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy ». — There are also others, *hirelings,* forsaking the flock in necessity; John, the tenth chapter[^p582-11]: « The hireling, and he that is not the shepherd, whose own the sheep are not, seeth the wolf coming, and leaveth the sheep, and flieth; and the wolf catcheth, and scattereth the sheep. And the hireling flieth, because he is a hireling, and he hath no care for the sheep ».

8. *I know my sheep, and mine know me*[^p582-12].

Here is described the goodness *of the shepherd* from *the comparison to the sheep,* when the Lord says: *I am the good shepherd, and I know my sheep* — and the goodness *of the sheep* from *the comparison to the shepherd,* when he says: *And mine know me. I know,* he says, them, *and mine know me.* I know them through *election* and through *approbation,* and they know me through *faith* and *dilection.*
<!-- page 583 -->

9. *I know,* he says, *my sheep.* Note that a sheep is known from four things. For it is known *by the countenance, by the bleating, by the fleece* and *by the sign.* — It is known *by the countenance,* according to that word of Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p583-1]: « Diligently know the countenance of thy cattle »; *the spiritual countenance* is conscience, of which the Canticles, the second chapter: « Shew me thy face ». To worldlings who have not this face it is said, Matthew, the seventh chapter: « I know you not ». — It is known *by the bleating;* by *the bleating* understand *the voice of confession;* whence in the Psalm[^p583-2]: « His people and the sheep of his pasture; go ye into his gates with confession ». Of this bleating the Canticles, the second chapter: « Let thy voice sound in my ears ». — It is known *by the fleece;* whence, when a sheep is devoured by a beast, the skin is wont to be shown as a sign, according to that word of Genesis, the thirty-first chapter[^p583-3]: « That which the beast had taken I did not shew thee »; as if to say: had it been taken, I would have shown it to thee. By *the fleece* understand *upright conversation.* For the spiritual sheep ought to have *the fleece* of a lamb-like conversation, according to that word of Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter: « Let there be lambs for thy clothing ». — It is known also *by the sign.* By the sign understand *the Lord's passion.* For the spiritual sheep ought to be signed with the sign of the Lord's passion; the Canticles, the eighth chapter[^p583-4]: « Put me as a seal upon thy heart ».

10. There follows: *And mine know me.*

That thou mayst know how the sheep ought to bear itself toward the shepherd, note that the sheep ought *to know* the shepherd, *to approach* the shepherd, *to hear* the shepherd and *to go* after the shepherd. — The sheep *knows* the shepherd through *faith,* through the giving of thanks; of which John, the tenth chapter[^p583-5]: « I know my sheep, and mine know me ». — It *approaches* the shepherd through *charity;* whence the first of Peter, the second chapter: « You were as sheep going astray, but you are now converted to the shepherd and bishop of your souls ». — For the sheep go astray by sinning, but they are converted when they *hear* the shepherd through *obedience;* John, the tenth chapter[^p583-6]: « My sheep hear my voice ». — It *follows* the shepherd through *imitation;* John, the tenth chapter: « When he hath let out his own sheep, he goeth before them, and the sheep follow him, because they know his voice »; and below in the same: « I know my sheep, and they follow me, and I give them life everlasting ».

11. *He calleth his own sheep by name*[^p583-7].

Note here three things concerning *the good shepherd.* First, that he *calls* the sheep *by name,* when it is said: *He calleth his own sheep by name.* Secondly, that he *leads them out,* when it is said[^p583-8]: *And leadeth them out.* Thirdly, that he *leads them on,* when it is added: *And when he hath let out his own sheep, he goeth before them.* For the good shepherd ought *to call* the sheep by the word of preaching and *to lead them out* by the Sacrament of confession and *to lead them on* by the example of holy conversation.

12. Note alongside this three things concerning *the good sheep.* For the sheep ought to be *humble,* that it may obey the shepherd; it ought to be *meek,* lest it offend the flock; it ought to be *fruitful,* that it may bring fruit to the householder. — It ought, then, to be *humble* through obedience; whence in the Psalm[^p583-9]: « Thou leadest Joseph like a sheep »; like a sheep Joseph ought to be easy to lead: sometimes *by the rod* of correction, sometimes *by the bread* of consolation, sometimes *by the dog* of temptation. — It ought to be *meek* through innocence, according to that word of the second of Kings, the last chapter[^p583-10]: « These that are the sheep, what have they done »? Whence that line:

> What have you deserved, O sheep, a creature without fraud and guile?

It ought to be *fruitful* through the abundance of good works, according to that word of the Psalm[^p583-11]: « Their sheep fruitful in young, abounding in their goings forth »; and the Canticles, the fourth chapter: « All with twin offspring, and there is none barren among them ».

### Collation XL.

(Verse 27. 28.). *My sheep follow me; and I give them life everlasting.*

1. In these words the Lord sets down first the *merit* of the spiritual sheep, that is, of the faithful soul, when he says: *My sheep follow me* — and promises the *reward,* when he adds: *And I give them life everlasting.*

Setting down first, then, the *merit,* he says: *They follow me.* Note that some follow through *the plain of the commandments,* some through *the mountains of the counsels,* some through *the heights of the supererogations;* which is figured in the Gospel in three kinds of those following the Lord. The Lord is followed by *the multitudes — the disciples* — and *the Apostles: the multitudes* through *the plains of the commandments;* Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p583-12]: « When Jesus was come down from the mountain, great multitudes followed him ». — The disciples through *the mountains;* Luke, the twenty-second chapter: « Jesus going out went, according to his custom, to the mount of Olives; and his disciples also followed him ». — The Apostles through *the heights of the supererogations;* Matthew, the nineteenth chapter, and Mark, the tenth chapter, and Luke, the eighteenth chapter: « Behold, we have left all things, and have followed thee ». The first, *beginners;* the second, *proficients;* the third, *the perfect.* The first follow as by *the road;* the second, as by *the path;* the third, as by *the short way.*

2. He says, then: *They follow me* with *the multitudes* through the plain of the commandments, and this in three ways: through *faith* in the understanding; the third of Kings, the eighteenth chapter[^p583-13], the words of Elias
<!-- page 584 -->
to the people: « How long do you halt between two sides? If the Lord be God, follow him; but if Baal, follow him ». — Through *charity* in the affection; the third of Kings, the fourteenth chapter, the words of Ahias to Jeroboam: « Thou hast not been as my servant David, who followed me with all his heart, doing that which was well pleasing in my sight » etc. — Through *obedience* in the effect; Job, the twenty-third chapter[^p584-1]: « My foot hath followed his steps. I have kept his way, and have not declined from it. I have not departed from the commandments of his lips » etc.; John, the tenth chapter: « When he hath let out his own sheep, he goeth before them, and the sheep follow him, because they hear his voice ». — They follow the Lord, then, through *faith,* through *charity* and through *obedience;* because without these no one follows; to the Galatians, the fifth chapter: « For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision availeth any thing, nor uncircumcision, but faith that worketh by charity ». — *Faith* indeed directs the regard, *charity* draws the affection, *working* leads on the effect.

3. They follow the Lord, then, with *the disciples* through *the mountains of the counsels,* and this in three ways according to the threefold counsel: through *the mortification of the flesh* against the concupiscence of the flesh; Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p584-2]: « He that taketh not up his cross, and followeth me, is not worthy of me ». — Through the relinquishing of *temporal things* against the concupiscence of the eyes; Matthew, the nineteenth chapter, Mark, the tenth chapter, Luke, the eighteenth chapter: « If thou wilt be perfect, go and sell all that thou hast, and give to the poor, and thou shalt have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me ». — Through *the casting off of one's own will* against the pride of life; Matthew, the sixteenth chapter[^p584-3]: « If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me »; and Matthew, the eighth chapter.

4. Again they follow the Lord with *the Apostles,* through the excellence of the supererogations, and this in three ways: through the excellence *of purity* with *the virgins,* of whom the Apocalypse, the fourteenth chapter[^p584-4]: « These are they who were not defiled with women, for they are virgins; these follow the Lamb whithersoever he goeth ». — Through the excellence *of uprightness* with *the Apostles,* who « going forth preached every where, the Lord working withal, and confirming the word with signs that followed ». Following of this kind is figured in the first of Kings, the fourteenth chapter[^p584-5], when « Jonathan said to his armourbearer: Follow me »; and it follows in the same place that « Jonathan went up against the station of the Philistines, creeping on his hands and feet, and his armourbearer after him ». — Through *the imitation of the passion* with *the Martyrs;* the first of Peter, the second chapter[^p584-6]: « Christ suffered for us, leaving you an example, that you should follow his steps »; and in John, the twenty-first chapter, the Lord said to Peter: « Follow me »; and below, of John: « So I will have him to remain till I come; what is it to thee? Follow thou me ». — They follow the Lord, then, through the obedience of the commandments with *the multitudes,* through the eminence of the counsels with *the disciples,* through the excellence of the supererogations with *the Apostles.*

5. Note that there are some who *will* not follow the Lord — there are those who *neglect* to — and there are those who *cannot.* — Now there are two impediments which render men *unwilling.* The first is *the love of present prosperity,* which turned away that man of whom Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p584-7], Mark, the tenth chapter, Luke, the eighteenth chapter, when the Lord said: « Go and sell etc., and come, follow me. But he went away sad; for he had great possessions ». — The second is *the fear of future adversity,* which turned away those to whom the Lord swore, Numbers, the thirty-second chapter[^p584-8]: « If these men shall see, that came up out of Egypt from twenty years old and upward, the land which I promised with an oath to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob; and they would not follow me, except Caleb the son of Jephone, and Josue the son of Nun; these have fulfilled my will ». Note the cause in the history.

6. There are likewise two impediments which render men *negligent.* The first is *carnal affection,* which is noted in that man of whom Luke, the ninth chapter[^p584-9], to whom the Lord said: « Follow me. But he said: Lord, permit me first to bury my father ». And note what the Lord said to him. — The second is *temporal solicitude,* which is noted in that man of whom, in the same place, Luke, the ninth chapter, who said to the Lord: « I will follow thee, Lord; but first permit me to take my leave of them that are at my house. Jesus said to him: No man putting his hand to the plough » etc.

7. There are likewise two impediments which render men *unable.* The first is *the perversity of intention,* which impedes the progress *of the affection.* The second is *the infirmity of virtue,* which impedes
<!-- page 585 -->
the progress *of the working.* The first is noted in that man who said, Luke, the ninth chapter[^p585-1]: « I will follow thee whithersoever thou goest », whose intention the Lord knowing said: « The foxes have holes, and the birds of the air nests » etc. — The second is noted in Peter, John, the thirteenth chapter, to whom, when he said to the Lord: « Lord, whither goest thou? Jesus answered: Whither I go, thou canst not follow me now; but thou shalt follow hereafter ».

8. And note how Peter first offered himself not only to follow, but to go before, or to accompany, saying[^p585-2]: « Lord, why cannot I follow thee now? I will lay down my life for thee ». Secondly, when « he followed afar off », not only did he not go before, but he did not even follow close by, but « afar off, to see the end », Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter. Thirdly, he followed neither afar off nor close by, when he denied him, in the same chapter; and this because he was infirm.

9. *They follow the Lord* etc. Having treated above[^p585-3] of the *merit* in following the Lord, here the *reward* of following him is touched upon. Note that following the Lord has *fruit* in *the present* — and a *reward* in *the future.* — In *the present,* because it is *honourable;* Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third chapter[^p585-4]: « It is great glory to follow the Lord ». — Because it is *safe;* John, the eighth chapter: « He that followeth me walketh not in darkness »; John, the tenth chapter: « I know my sheep, and they follow me; and I give them life everlasting, and they shall not perish for ever, and no man shall pluck them out of my hand ». — Because it is *profitable.* For the Lord gives to those following him a threefold benefit: *of healing;* Matthew, the fourteenth chapter[^p585-5]: « Great multitudes followed him on foot out of the cities, and coming forth he saw a great multitude, and had compassion on them, and healed their sick »; the like in Matthew, the ninth chapter, Mark, the tenth chapter, Luke, the eighteenth chapter, concerning the two blind men. — *Of instruction;* Luke, the ninth chapter: « The multitudes followed him, and he received them, and spoke to them of the kingdom of God, and healed them who had need of healing ». — *Of refreshment;* below in the same[^p585-6], concerning the miracle of the five loaves and the two fishes; the like in Mark, the second chapter: « Many publicans and sinners sat down together with Jesus and his disciples. For they were many, who followed him ».

10. Likewise following the Lord has a *reward* in *the future:* and there is promised to those following *the sublimity of honour;* Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p585-7]: « You who have followed me etc.; you also shall sit on twelve seats, judging the twelve tribes of Israel ». — *The possession of the inheritance;* Josue, the fourteenth chapter: « The land which thy foot hath trodden upon shall be thy possession, and thy children's for ever, because thou hast followed the Lord my God ». — *The lumen of joyfulness*[^tr-lumen]; John, the eighth chapter: « He that followeth me walketh not in darkness, but shall have the lumen of life ». — *The security of eternity;* John, the tenth chapter[^p585-8]: « I know my sheep, and they follow me, and I give them life everlasting, and they shall not perish for ever, and no man shall pluck them out of my hand »; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third chapter: « It is great glory to follow the Lord; length of days shall be received from him ».

[^tr-lumen]: **En.** Translator's note: in this translation *lux* is "light" and *lumen* is left standing as "lumen", because Bonaventure often sets the two words against each other. This chapter's one *lumen* is Bonaventure's own: in the fortieth collation the third member of the reward promised to those who follow is the *Lumen iucunditatis*, "the lumen of joyfulness", and its proof-text is John 8:12, *sed habebit lumen vitae*. The verse is not quoted only as a verse: it is adduced for the prose's own term, which takes its noun from it, so under the test this translation applies to quoted *lumen* the verse keeps "lumen" with the prose ("the lumen of life") rather than the Douay's "the light of life". The chapter has no *lux*.
