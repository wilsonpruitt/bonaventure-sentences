# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. II, Vers. 12-17
## *Secunda manifestatio, quae facta est Iudaeis*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 273 -->

#### Secunda manifestatio, quae facta est Iudaeis.

18. *Post haec*[^p273-3] *descendit* etc. Manifestavit se supra Dominus discipulis. In hac parte usque ad quartum capitulum agitur de manifestatione facta Iudaeis, quibus se tripliciter manifestat, scilicet *signo, verbo* et *Sacramento.* Ideo habet haec pars tres partes.

In prima agitur de manifestatione facta per *signum* virtutis; in secunda, per *verbum* eruditionis, ibi[^p273-4]: *Erat autem quidam homo ex Pharisaeis;* in tertia, per *Sacramentum* regenerationis, ibi: *Post haec venit Iesus in Iudaeam.*

Prima pars habet duas partes. In prima manifestat se signo *auctoritatis;* in secunda, signo *virtutis et potestatis,* ibi[^p273-5]: *Responderunt Iudaei et dixerunt.*

Signum *auctoritatis* fuit in potestativa transgredientium correctione, quae describitur hoc ordine. Primo innuitur *Domini ad corripiendum expeditio;* secundo vero, manifesta *Iudaeorum transgressio;* tertio, *transgredientium correctio;* quarto, *correctionis approbatio.*

19. (Vers. 12). Domini *ad corripiendum expeditio* in hoc notatur, quod descendit Capharnaum, ut ibi Matrem et fratres relinqueret; propterea dicit: *Post haeo,* scilicet quae narrata sunt, *descendit Capharnaum ipse et Mater eius et fratres eius et discipuli eius.* Cum omnibus descendit, quia ad habitandum ibi venit; Matthaei quarto[^p273-6]: « Relicta civitate Nazareth, venit et habitavit Capharnaum ». Ideo cum omnibus descendit, ut expeditius ascenderet; propterea dicit: *Et ibi manserunt non multis diebus.* Et ratio huius subditur:

20. (Vers. 13.). *Et prope erat Pascha Iudaeorum, et ascendit Iesus Ierosolymam.* Et propterea ad se praeparandum descendit; Chrysostomus[^p273-7]: « Ideo descendit, paulo post Ierosolymam ascensurus, ut non ubique fratres et Matrem traheret ». Ascendit autem secundum morem Iudaeorum et mandatum Legis; Deuteronomii decimo sexto[^p273-8]: « Tribus vicibus per annum apparebit omne masculinum tuum coram Domino ». Et licet Dominus non esset Legi subiectus, observabat tamen, ne videretur contrarius.

21. (Vers. 14.). *Et invenit in templo.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *Iudaeorum manifesta transgressio,* quia de templo fecerant forum; propter quod dicit: *Invenit in templo vendentes oves et boves et columbas.* Hoc enim introduxerat avaritia sacerdotum, ut volentibus offerre non deesset oblatio. *Et nummularios sedentes,* supple: invenit; et hos introduxerant sacerdotes, ut volentibus offerre non deesset pretium; et sic totaliter avaritiae deserviebant. Ideo conquerebatur Dominus per Ieremiam sexto[^p273-9]: « A maiore usque ad minorem omnes avaritiae student ».

22. (Vers. 15.). *Cum fecisset quasi flagellum.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *Domini potestativa correctio,* quia non tantum *verbo* corripuit, sed etiam *facto.* Propterea dicit: *Et cum fecisset quasi flagellum de funiculis,* scilicet ad percutiendum, *omnes eiecit de templo, oves quoque et boves,* tanquam indignos esse in templo; sicut dixit Dominus ad Sobnam, templi praepositum, Isaiae vigesimo secundo[^p273-10]: « Expellam te de statione tua, et de ministerio tuo deponam te ». *Et nummulariorum effudit aes et mensas subvertit.* Et sic aspere corripuit *facto.* Corripuit etiam *verbo,* et hoc vendentes columbas. Propterea subdit:

23. (Vers. 16.). *Et his qui vendebant columbas, dixit: Auferte ista hinc, et nolite facere
<!-- page 274 -->
domum Patris mei domum negotiationis.* De hac domo dicitur in Psalmo[^p274-1]: « Domum tuam, Domine, decet sanctitudo », non negotiatio. Augustinus[^p274-2]: « Si talia prohibet in loco orationis, quanto magis potationes et ebrietates et cetera consimilia »? Ideo praecipit in Regula sua[^p274-3], quod « in oratorio nihil fiat praeter id, ad quod institutum est ».

24. (Vers. 17.). *Recordati sunt discipuli.* Hic notatur quartum, scilicet *correctionis approbatio* facta per Scripturam. Propterea dicit: *Recordati sunt vero discipuli eius,* non tunc, sed post resurrectionem, cum intellexerunt Scripturam; tunc, inquam, recordati sunt, *quia scriptum est: Zelus domus tuae comedit me,* in illo Psalmo sexagesimo octavo[^p274-4]: « Salvum me fac, Deus ». De huius zeli comestione dicit Augustinus[^p274-5]: « Ille comeditur zelo domus Dei, qui omnia, quae videt ibi perversa, corrigere cupit, emendare non quiescit; si emendare non potest, tolerat et gemit ».

25. *Mystice* sunt hic tria notanda, scilicet *adventus Domini* in templum, *inventio transgressorum* et *eorum eiectio.* — De *adventu* Hieronymus[^p274-6]: « Secundum mysticum intellectum Iesus quotidie ingreditur templum Patris et eiicit omnes de Ecclesia, habens unius criminis vendentes pariter et ementes. Scriptum est enim: *Gratis accepistis, gratis date* », Matthaei decimo. — *Invenit vendentes boves;* Beda[^p274-7]: « *Boves* sunt praedicatores, quibus aratur et seminatur terra Domini semine verbi »; primae ad Corinthios nono: *Non alligabis os bovi trituranti.* Item Beda[^p274-8]: « Hos *vendunt* qui non divino amore, sed quaestus intuitu verbum praedicationis audientibus impendunt ». — *Invenit etiam vendentes oves.* « *Oves,* sicut dicit Beda[^p274-9], sunt opera pietatis et misericordiae. Has *vendunt,* qui humanae laudis gratia opera pietatis exercent, sicut hypocritae ». — *Invenit vendentes columbas.* « *Columbae* sunt dona Spiritus sancti », quia super Christum in specie columbae apparuit, Matthaei tertio[^p274-10]. « Has columbas *vendunt* qui acceptam Spiritus sancti gratiam ad *pretium* dant; qui impositionem manuum, qua Spiritus sanctus accipitur, etsi non ad quaestum pecuniae, ad vulgi tamen *favorem* tribuunt; qui sacros ordines non ad vitae meritum, sed ad *gratiam* largiuntur ». — *Invenit nummularios;* Beda[^p274-11]: « Nummos mutuo dant in templo qui non simulate caelestibus, sed aperte terrenis rebus in Ecclesia deserviunt, sua quaerentes, non quae Iesu Christi »; ad Philippenses secundo: *Omnes quaerunt quae sua sunt.* — *Eiecit vendentes oves et boves,* ut dicit Beda[^p274-12], « quia talium vitam pariter et doctrinam ostendit esse reprobam ». — *Nummulariorum effudit aes et mensas subvertit;* « quia damnatis in fine reprobis, etiam ipsarum rerum, quas dilexerant, tollet figuram, secundum quod scriptum est: *Transibit mundus et concupiscentia eius* », primae Ioannis secundo[^p274-13]; et: *Praeterit figura huius mundi,* primae ad Corinthios septimo. — *Columbas auferri* praecepit; Hieronymus in Matthaeum[^p274-14]: « *Cathedras* eorum *evertit,* quia talium negotiatorum sacerdotium destruit ». Hinc est, quod sacri Canones[^p274-15] simoniacam haeresim damnant, et eos sacerdotio privari praecipiunt, qui de sacerdotio pretium quaerunt.
<!-- page 275 -->

### Quaestiones.

26. Quaest. I. Quaeritur hic de ordine historiae. Hic enim dicitur, quod haec eiectio fuit statim post primum miraculum; Matthaei vero vigesimo primo[^p275-1] narratur, hoc fuisse circa passionem, quando sedit super asellum.

Ad hoc respondet Augustinus de Concordia Evangelistarum[^p275-2], quod bis hoc factum est, sed primum commemoratum est a Ioanne, secundum ab aliis. — Et per hoc solvuntur repugnantiae, quae possent obiici, quia ibi duriori modo corripit et alia verba dicit quam hic[^p275-3].

27. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de inventione ovium in templo. Sacerdotes non patiebantur, quod immundi homines intrarent in templum; quanto ergo minus de ovibus et bobus, quae templum foedarent? Multo minus videtur probabile.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod quatuor atria erant in templo: primum erat sacerdotum; secundum, mundorum virorum; tertium, mundarum mulierum; quartum, immundorum et gentilium. Et quodlibet istorum large appellabatur *templum,* et in hoc quarto invenit huiusmodi animalia[^p275-4].

28. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de correctione. Quia dicitur Matthaei decimo quinto[^p275-5]: *Sinite illos; caeci sunt et duces caecorum;* non videtur curasse de Iudaeorum correctione. Ut quid ergo tam acriter eos modo redarguit, cum essent incorrigibiles?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod etsi sciret eos incorrigibiles, volebat tamen zelum suum erga domum Dei ostendere, ut praelatis exemplum daret et ut Patri se consonum ostenderet et Legis amicum, *quam non venit solvere, sed ut adimpleret*[^p275-6].

29. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur: quare illos qui vendebant columbas, solo verbo corripuit, alios etiam facto? Et videtur, quod magis, quia per illos simoniaci designantur[^p275-7], qui sunt acrius corripiendi.

**Respondeo:** Ad hoc est ratio *litteralis,* quia columbae minus dehonestabant templum; *mystica* vero ratio est, quia per illos intelliguntur simoniaci, qui non sunt parva poena *corripiendi,* sed *sententialiter de Ecclesia eiiciendi;* vel quia per *columbam* intelligitur donum Spiritus sancti, quod non est eiiciendum.

30. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur de hoc: cum Iudaei essent multi et furiosi, et Christus unicus et inermis; quomodo non surrexerunt in eum et occiderunt[^p275-8], quando ita eiiciebat eos et res eorum?

Ad hoc dicunt aliqui, quod etsi mali essent, tamen erant hypocritae; et ideo, ut non ostenderent avaritiam nec malitiam, nec turbatos se ostendunt nec eum laedunt. — Hieronymus[^p275-9] aliter respondet, quod « igneum quiddam atque sidereum radiabat ex oculis eius, et Divinitatis maiestas lucebat in facie », immutans eorum animum, ut non audeant in ipsum mittere manum. Unde Hieronymus maius reputat istud signum quam de Lazari suscitatione, quod unus homo inermis tot expulit et non expavit[^p275-10].

31. Quaest. VI. Quaeritur etiam de hoc quod dicit, quod *zelus* fuit in Christo. Si enim zelus importat iram, et ira perturbationem, et in Christo non potuit esse perturbatio: ergo nec zelus.

Ad hoc respondet Victor[^p275-11], quod *zelus* dicitur dupliciter: uno modo importat animi perturbationem, quemadmodum et ira, et sic non fuit in Christo; Isaiae quadragesimo secundo[^p275-12]: *Non erit tristis neque turbulentus.* Alio modo *zelus* idem est quod fervens amor, et sic fuit in Christo; unde dicit Glossa Augustini[^p275-13]: « Bonus zelus est fervor animi, quo mens, abiecto timore humano, pro defensione veritatis accenditur ».

## English
<!-- page 273 -->

#### The second manifestation, which was made to the Jews.

18. *After this*[^p273-3] *he went down* etc. The Lord manifested himself above to the disciples. In this part, up to the fourth chapter, there is treatment of the manifestation made to the Jews, to whom he manifests himself in three ways, namely by *sign, word* and *Sacrament.* Therefore this part has three parts.

In the first there is treatment of the manifestation made through a *sign* of virtue; in the second, through the *word* of instruction, there[^p273-4]: *And there was a certain man of the Pharisees;* in the third, through the *Sacrament* of regeneration, there: *After these things Jesus came into Judea.*

The first part has two parts. In the first he manifests himself by a sign *of authority;* in the second, by a sign *of virtue and power,* there[^p273-5]: *The Jews therefore answered, and said.*

The sign *of authority* was in the powerful correction of transgressors, which is described in this order. First is intimated *the Lord's setting out to correct;* second, the manifest *transgression of the Jews;* third, *the correction of the transgressors;* fourth, *the approval of the correction.*

19. (Verse 12). *The Lord's setting out to correct* is noted in this, that he went down to Capharnaum, so as to leave his Mother and brethren there; therefore he says: *After this* [so printed: *haeo* for *haec*], namely the things that have been narrated, *he went down to Capharnaum, he and his Mother, and his brethren, and his disciples.* He went down with all of them, because he came to dwell there; Matthew, the fourth chapter[^p273-6]: « Leaving the city Nazareth, he came and dwelt in Capharnaum ». Therefore he went down with all of them, that he might go up the more unencumbered; therefore he says: *And they remained there not many days.* And the reason for this is added:

20. (Verse 13.). *And the pasch of the Jews was at hand, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem.* And therefore he went down to prepare himself; Chrysostom[^p273-7]: « He went down for this reason, being about to go up to Jerusalem a little after, that he might not draw his brethren and his Mother everywhere with him ». Now he went up according to the custom of the Jews and the command of the Law; Deuteronomy, the sixteenth chapter[^p273-8]: « Three times in a year shall all thy males appear before the Lord ». And although the Lord was not subject to the Law, he nevertheless observed it, lest he should seem contrary to it.

21. (Verse 14.). *And he found in the temple.* Here is touched the second, namely *the manifest transgression of the Jews,* because they had made a market of the temple; on account of which he says: *He found in the temple them that sold sheep and oxen and doves.* For the avarice of the priests had brought this in, so that an offering should not be lacking to those wishing to offer. *And the changers of money sitting,* supply: he found; and the priests had brought these in, so that the price should not be lacking to those wishing to offer; and so they were wholly in the service of avarice. Therefore the Lord complained through Jeremias, the sixth chapter[^p273-9]: « From the greatest even to the least, all are given to covetousness ».

22. (Verse 15.). *When he had made, as it were, a scourge.* Here is touched the third, namely *the Lord's powerful correction,* because he rebuked not only *by word,* but also *by deed.* Therefore he says: *And when he had made, as it were, a scourge of little cords,* namely for striking, *he drove them all out of the temple, the sheep also and the oxen,* as unworthy to be in the temple; as the Lord said to Sobna, the overseer of the temple, Isaias, the twenty-second chapter[^p273-10]: « I will expel thee from thy station, and depose thee from thy ministry ». *And the money of the changers he poured out, and the tables he overthrew.* And so he rebuked harshly *by deed.* He rebuked also *by word,* and this those who sold doves. Therefore he adds:

23. (Verse 16.). *And to them that sold doves he said: Take these things hence, and make not
<!-- page 274 -->
the house of my Father a house of traffic.* Of this house it is said in the Psalm[^p274-1]: « Holiness becometh thy house, O Lord », not traffic. Augustine[^p274-2]: « If he forbids such things in the place of prayer, how much more drinkings and drunkenness and other like things »? Therefore he prescribes in his Rule[^p274-3] that « in the oratory nothing be done except that for which it was instituted ».

24. (Verse 17.). *And his disciples remembered.* Here is noted the fourth, namely *the approval of the correction* made through Scripture. Therefore he says: *And his disciples remembered,* not then, but after the resurrection, when they understood the Scripture; then, I say, they remembered *that it was written: The zeal of thy house hath eaten me up,* in that sixty-eighth Psalm[^p274-4]: « Save me, O God ». Of the eating up by this zeal Augustine says[^p274-5]: « He is eaten up by the zeal of the house of God who desires to correct all the perverse things he sees there, and does not rest from amending them; if he cannot amend them, he bears with them and groans ».

25. *Mystically* three things are to be noted here, namely *the coming of the Lord* into the temple, *the finding of the transgressors,* and *their casting out.* — On *the coming,* Jerome[^p274-6]: « According to the mystical understanding Jesus daily enters the temple of the Father and casts out of the Church all, having those who sell and those who buy alike as of one crime. For it is written: *Freely have you received, freely give* », Matthew, the tenth chapter. — *He found them that sold oxen;* Bede[^p274-7]: « The *oxen* are the preachers, by whom the land of the Lord is ploughed and sown with the seed of the word »; the first to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter: *Thou shalt not muzzle the mouth of the ox that treadeth out the corn.* Likewise Bede[^p274-8]: « These *sell* who bestow the word of preaching on their hearers not out of divine love, but with a view to gain ». — *He found also them that sold sheep.* « The *sheep,* as Bede says[^p274-9], are works of piety and mercy. They *sell* these who perform works of piety for the sake of human praise, as the hypocrites do ». — *He found them that sold doves.* « The *doves* are the gifts of the Holy Spirit », because he appeared over Christ in the form of a dove, Matthew, the third chapter[^p274-10]. « They sell doves who give the grace of the Holy Spirit they have received for a *price;* who bestow the imposition of hands, by which the Holy Spirit is received, if not for the gain of money, yet for the *favour* of the crowd; who confer sacred orders not for merit of life, but as a *kindness* ». — *He found the changers of money;* Bede[^p274-11]: « They give money on loan in the temple who, not feignedly to heavenly things, but openly to earthly things, are in service in the Church, seeking their own, not the things that are Jesus Christ's »; to the Philippians, the second chapter: *All seek the things that are their own.* — *He cast out them that sold sheep and oxen,* as Bede says[^p274-12], « because he showed the life and likewise the teaching[^tr-doctrina] of such men to be reprobate ». — *The money of the changers he poured out, and the tables he overthrew;* « because, when the reprobate have been condemned at the end, he will take away even the figure of the very things they had loved, according as it is written: *The world passeth away, and the concupiscence thereof* », the first of John, the second chapter[^p274-13]; and: *The figure of this world passeth away,* the first to the Corinthians, the seventh chapter. — He commanded *the doves to be taken away;* Jerome on Matthew[^p274-14]: « He *overthrew* their *chairs,* because he destroys the priesthood of such traffickers ». Hence it is that the sacred Canons[^p274-15] condemn the simoniacal heresy, and prescribe that those be deprived of the priesthood who seek a price for the priesthood.

### Questions.

26. Question I. Here it is asked about the order of the history. For here it is said that this casting out was immediately after the first miracle; but in Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p275-1], it is narrated that this was about the time of the passion, when he sat upon the ass.

To this Augustine answers in *On the Harmony of the Evangelists*[^p275-2] that this was done twice, but the first is recorded by John, the second by the others. — And by this are solved the inconsistencies that could be objected, because there he rebukes in a harsher manner and says other words than here[^p275-3].

27. Question II. Likewise it is asked about the finding of the sheep in the temple. The priests did not suffer unclean men to enter into the temple; how much less, then, sheep and oxen, which would defile the temple? It seems much less probable.

**I respond:** It must be said that there were four courts in the temple: the first was that of the priests; the second, of clean men; the third, of clean women; the fourth, of the unclean and the gentiles. And each of these was broadly called *the temple,* and in this fourth he found animals of this kind[^p275-4].

28. Question III. Likewise it is asked about the correction. Since it is said in Matthew, the fifteenth chapter[^p275-5]: *Let them alone; they are blind, and leaders of the blind;* he seems not to have cared about the correction of the Jews. Why then does he now rebuke them so sharply, since they were incorrigible?

**I respond:** It must be said that although he knew them to be incorrigible, he nevertheless wished to show his zeal for the house of God, that he might give an example to prelates, and that he might show himself in accord with the Father and a friend of the Law, *which he came not to destroy, but that he might fulfil it*[^p275-6].

29. Question IV. Likewise it is asked: why did he rebuke those who sold doves by word alone, the others by deed also? And it seems that he should have done so the more, because by them the simoniacs are designated[^p275-7], who are to be rebuked more sharply.

**I respond:** To this there is a *literal* reason, that the doves dishonoured the temple less; but the *mystical* reason is that by them are understood the simoniacs, who are not to be *rebuked* with a small penalty, but are *to be cast out of the Church by sentence;* or because by the *dove* is understood the gift of the Holy Spirit, which is not to be cast out.

30. Question V. Likewise it is asked about this: since the Jews were many and furious, and Christ alone and unarmed, how did they not rise up against him and kill him[^p275-8], when he was so casting them out and their goods?

To this some say that although they were wicked, yet they were hypocrites; and therefore, so as not to show their avarice nor their malice, they neither show themselves disturbed nor harm him. — Jerome[^p275-9] answers otherwise, that « something fiery and starlike shone forth from his eyes, and the majesty of the Divinity gleamed in his face », changing their mind, so that they do not dare to lay a hand on him. Hence Jerome reckons this sign greater than that of the raising of Lazarus, that one unarmed man drove out so many and did not take fright[^p275-10].

31. Question VI. It is asked also about this, that he says that *zeal* was in Christ. For if zeal implies anger, and anger disturbance, and in Christ there could be no disturbance: therefore neither zeal.

To this Victor[^p275-11] answers that *zeal* is said in two ways: in one way it implies disturbance of the mind, as anger also does, and so it was not in Christ; Isaias, the forty-second chapter[^p275-12]: *He shall not be sad nor troublesome.* In another way *zeal* is the same as fervent love, and so it was in Christ; whence the Gloss of Augustine says[^p275-13]: « Good zeal is a fervour of the mind, by which the mind, human fear being cast away, is kindled for the defence of truth ».
