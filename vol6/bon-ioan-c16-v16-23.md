# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XVI, Vers. 16-23
## *Secundo consolatur discipulos ex suae visitationis promissione*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 461 -->

#### Secundo consolatur discipulos ex suae visitationis promissione.

27. *Modicum, et iam non videbitis me.* Haec est secunda pars capituli, in qua consolatur eos ex *suae visitationis promissione*[^p461-6], et procedit in hac parte secundum hunc modum. Primo ponitur *visitationis Christi promissio;* secundo, *discipulorum dubitatio;* tertio vero, *dubietatis explanatio;* quarto, *exemplificatio;* quinto vero, *exempli adaptatio.*

(Vers. 16.). *Visitationem* igitur primo *promittit* post recessum; propterea dicit: *Modicum, et iam non videbitis me,* quia recedam a vobis scilicet; *et iterum modicum, et videbitis me,* quia statim post passionem visitaturus erat eos; unde *modicum* erat usque ad passionem, in qua debebat non videri; et *modicum* a passione usque ad resurrectionem, in qua debebat apparere; Isaiae quinquagesimo quarto[^p461-7]: « Ad punctum et in modico dereliqui te, et in miserationibus magnis congregabo te ». Et rationem, quare non essent eum visuri, reddit: *Quia vado ad Patrem*[^p461-8]. — Aliter exponitur de visione *per gloriam,* ut sit sensus, quod modicum tempus erat usque ad recessum Christi et ascensum ad Patrem, et iterum modicum usque ad adventum eius ad eos, ut assumat in gloriam; unde supra decimo quarto[^p462-1]: « Et si abiero et praeparavero vobis locum, iterum venio et accipiam vos ad me ipsum, ut ubi sum ego, et vos sitis ».
<!-- page 462 -->

28. (Vers. 17.). *Dixerunt ergo ex discipulis eius* etc. Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *discipulorum dubitatio,* quae procedebat ex hoc quod dixerat: in modico non videndus et videndus; propter quod dicit: *Dixerunt ergo ex discipulis eius ad invicem: Quid est hoc quod dicit nobis: Modicum, et non videbitis me; et iterum modicum, et videbitis me?* De hoc dubitant primo, quia videtur dicere duo contradictorie opposita; et de alio dubitant, scilicet quo iret; unde: *et quia vado ad Patrem?* Nesciebant enim, quomodo intelligeret hoc[^p462-2]; dubitabant de significatione verbi.

29. (Vers. 18.). *Dicebant ergo: Quid est hoc quod dicit nobis: Modicum? Nescimus, quid loquitur.* Nesciebant, quid per hoc volebat dicere; parvum habebant adhuc discipuli intellectum; unde increpavit eos Dominus Matthaei decimo quinto[^p462-3]: « Adhuc et vos sine intellectu estis »?

30. (Vers. 19.). *Cognovit autem Iesus.* Hic tangitur tertium, scilicet *dubietatis amotio,* quae facta est per Magistrum ante inquisitionem discipulorum; propter quod dicit: *Cognovit Iesus, quia volebant eum interrogare* etc.; Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio[^p462-4]: « Oculi Domini multo plus lucidiores super solem, circumspicientes omnes vias hominum et profundum abyssi et hominum corda intuentes in absconditas partes »; et Ecclesiastici decimo sexto: « Omne cor intelligitur ab illo; et vias illius quis intelligit »? Et praevenit ipsorum dubitationem sive interrogationem, *et dixit eis: De hoc quaeritis inter vos, quia dixi vobis*[^p462-5]*: Modicum, et non videbitis me; et iterum modicum, et videbitis me.* Huic dubitationi satisfacit ostendens duplex tempus, scilicet *tribulationis* et *consolationis,* et propter unum dictum esse: *Non videbitis,* et propter aliud *videbitis.* Et propter hoc non fuit contradictio, quia non refertur ad idem tempus, sed propter diversa tempora bis dictum est *modicum.* Propterea dicit:

31. (Vers. 20.). *Amen, amen dico vobis: Quia plorabitis et flebitis vos: plorabitis* exterius, *flebitis* interius, pro tempore, in quo me non videbitis; et ideo beati; Lucae sexto[^p462-6]: « Beati, qui nunc fletis, quia ridebitis ». *Mundus autem gaudebit, vos vero contristabimini.* Mundus gaudet; Iob vigesimo primo: « Gaudent ad sonitum tympani ». Sed boni tristantur; primae Petri primo: « Modicum nunc si oportet contristari in variis tentationibus ». *Sed tristitia vestra vertetur in gaudium,* et ideo dictum est: *Modicum, et videbitis me;* Tobiae tertio[^p462-7]: « Post lacrymationem et fletum exsultationem infundis ». Ideo dicitur Ecclesiastae septimo: « Cor stultorum, ubi laetitia; cor sapientium, ubi tristitia est ».

32. (Vers. 21.). *Mulier, cum parit.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *exemplificatio.* Et exemplificatio est de hoc, quomodo cito et in modico tempore tristitia convertetur in gaudium; et est exemplum de muliere parturiente, quae, cum parit, tristatur, quamvis bonum faciat; sed cum peperit, gaudet statim. Propterea dicit: *Mulier, cum parit, tristitiam habet, quia venit hora eius,* scilicet pariendi, quam adhuc vellet differri prae dolore; quia vehemens dolor est in parturitione; Isaiae decimo tertio[^p462-8]: « Quasi parturiens dolebunt »; et primae ad Thessalonicenses quinto: « Et sicut dolor in utero habenti, et non effugient ». *Cum autem peperit*[^p462-9] *puerum, iam non meminit pressurae propter gaudium, quia natus est homo in mundum;* prae gaudio, quod habet de prole; Genesis vigesimo primo: « Risum fecit mihi Dominus; quicumque audierit, corridebit mihi », dixit Sara.

33. (Vers. 22.). *Et vos igitur.* Hic est *exempli adaptatio,* quod ipsi tristabuntur similiter in Christi passione, sed gavisuri erant in eius glorificatione sive resurrectione; propter quod dicit: *Et vos igitur nunc quidem tristitiam habetis,* et hoc de recessu sponsi; Matthaei nono[^p462-10]: « Venient dies, quando ab eis auferetur sponsus; et tunc lugebunt ». Sed ista tristitia modicum durabit; ideo dicit: *Iterum*[^p462-11] *videbo vos, et gaudebit cor vestrum;* infra vigesimo: « Gavisi sunt discipuli, viso Domino ». Et hoc non ad tempus, sed in aeternum; ideo dicit: *Et gaudium vestrum nemo tollet a vobis;* quia Christus, qui erat ipsorum gaudium, « resurgens a mortuis, iam non moritur »; Apocalypsis septimo: « Absterget Deus omnem lacrymam ab oculis Sanctorum » etc. Et tunc erunt terminatae quaestiones vestrae nec turbabunt vos, ut modo; ideo dicit:
<!-- page 463 -->

34. (Vers. 23.). *Et in illo die me non rogabitis*[^p463-1] *quidquam,* id est, non interrogabitis. Si hoc refertur ad diem gloriae, verum est, quia tunc omnia videbunt clare; Isaiae quinquagesimo quarto: « Ponam omnes filios tuos doctos a Domino ». Si autem intelligatur post glorificationem Christi, adhuc verum est; quia tunc habuerunt internum doctorem, iuxta illud quod dictum est supra eodem[^p463-2]: « Cum venerit ille Spiritus veritatis, docebit vos omnem veritatem »; et: « Unctio docebit vos de omnibus ». — Vel: *non rogabitis,* quia non indigebitis, sed eritis beati, quibus ad votum cuncta succedunt.

### Quaestiones.

35. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod dicit: *Modicum, et non videbitis me, quia vado ad Patrem.*

1. Quia dicitur Matthaei ultimo[^p463-3]: *Ecce, ego vobiscum sum usque ad consummationem saeculi.*

2. Item, quid est quod dicit: *Modicum, et videbitis me?* quia dicitur supra eodem[^p463-4]: *Ad Patrem vado, et iam non videbitis me.* Quomodo recedit et manet, videtur et non videtur?

**Respondeo:** Intelligendum, quod in Christo est *esse* divinum et *esse* humanum *visibile* et *invisibile, miserum* et *gloriosum.* Quantum igitur ad *esse* divinum nunquam recedit ab electis; quantum ad *esse humanum et invisibile* sub Sacramento non relinquet *usque ad consummationem saeculi;* quantum ad *esse humanum visibile et impassibile* non reliquit usque ad ascensionem; quantum ad *esse humanum visibile et passibile* reliquit in passione; quia non visus fuit passibilis nec cum eis ut passibilis postea conversatus[^p463-5].

36. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Plorabitis et flebitis vos* etc. — Quaeritur, utrum Apostoli in illa tristitia *meruerint,* vel *demeruerint.* Quod *meruerint,* videtur:

1. Quia dicitur secundae ad Timotheum secundo[^p463-6]: *Si compatimur, et conregnabimus:* ergo compati erat eis meritorium.

2. Item, tristitia mala convertitur in maiorem tristitiam, sed illa quae convertitur in gaudium, illa, inquam, bona tristitia est; sed tristitia Apostolorum conversa est in gaudium, sicut dicitur in littera[^p463-7]: *Tristitia vestra convertetur in gaudium:* ergo fuit bona et meritoria.

**Contra** hoc est: 1. Quia aut *volebant,* quod Christus moreretur, aut *non.* Si *non,* tunc peccabant, unde Petrus fuit de hoc reprehensus[^p463-8]; si vero *volebant:* ergo non dolebant.

2. Item videtur per textum[^p463-9], quia Dominus hortatur eos, ut *non turbentur;* sed Dominus non dehortatur bonum: ergo turbari non est bonum.

3. Item, supra decimo quarto[^p463-10] dicitur: *Si diligeretis me, gauderetis utique:* ergo gaudendum erat de passione; sed qui tristatur, unde gaudendum est, inordinate tristatur: ergo Apostoli inordinate et culpabiliter turbabantur.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod dupliciter est iudicare de tristitia, cuiusmodi sit, scilicet ex parte *originis* et ex parte *modi.* — Ex parte *originis* tristitia ortum habet ex *dilectione*[^p463-11]. Quaedam igitur est ex dilectione *voluptuosa* et carnali, ut gulosus tristatur in amissione boni morselli; quaedam ex *naturali,* ut anima in separatione a corpore, aut mater in morte filii; quaedam ex *sociali,* ut tristatur amicus, quando corporaliter seiungitur ab amico, cuius conversatio placet; quaedam ex *spirituali,* ut quando aliquis diligit ex caritate et compatitur patienti. — Prima tristitia est *culpabilis;* secunda et tertia *supportabilis;* sed quarta *laudabilis.*

Intelligendum est ergo, quod Apostoli dupliciter diligebant Dominum, scilicet amore *sociali,* quo diligit aliquis socium, quia non vult ab eo separari; et amore *spirituali;* ideo fuit in eis tristitia quaedam *laudabilis,* qua compatiebantur Christo; et quaedam *supportabilis,* qua nolebant separari a Christo[^p463-12].

A parte *modi tristandi* intelligendum, quod modus habendus est in tristitia; unde dicit Apostolus[^p463-13]: *Non contristemini sicut et ceteri, qui spem non habent;* unde debet tristitia temperari gaudio. Et
<!-- page 464 -->
quantum ad hoc excesserunt Apostoli et reprehensibiles fuerunt, quia nullo modo gaudebant, immo ut desperantes dolebant. Et quantum ad hunc modum sive immoderantiam amovendam hortabatur Dominus, ut *non tristarentur;* hortabatur, ut *gauderent.* Unde Chrysostomus[^p464-1]: « Magna est tristitiae tyrannis, et multa nobis virilitate opus est, ut resistamus huic passioni; et quod *utile* castificantes, quod est *superfluum* dimittamus ». Ideo laudabiles erant in tristitia, sed vituperabiles in modo.

## English

#### Second he consoles the disciples from the promise of his visitation.

27. *A little while, and now you shall not see me.* This is the second part of the chapter, in which he consoles them from *the promise of his visitation*[^p461-6], and he proceeds in this part according to this mode. First there is set down the *promise of Christ's visitation;* second, the *doubt of the disciples;* third, the *explanation of the doubtfulness;* fourth, the *exemplification;* fifth, the *adaptation of the example.*

(Verse 16.). The *visitation,* then, he first *promises* after the withdrawal; on this account he says: *A little while, and now you shall not see me,* because I shall withdraw from you, namely; *and again a little while, and you shall see me,* because immediately after the passion he was going to visit them; whence it was *a little while* up to the passion, in which he was to be not seen; and *a little while* from the passion up to the resurrection, in which he was to appear; Isaias, the fifty-fourth chapter[^p461-7]: « For a moment and in a little while have I forsaken thee, and with great mercies will I gather thee ». And the reason why they were not going to see him, he gives: *Because I go to the Father*[^p461-8]. — Otherwise it is expounded of the vision *through glory,* so that the sense is that it was a little time up to the withdrawal of Christ and his ascent to the Father, and again a little while up to his coming to them, that he may take them up into glory; whence above, the fourteenth chapter[^p462-1]: « And if I shall go and prepare a place for you, I will come again and will take you to myself: that where I am, you also may be ».
<!-- page 462 -->

28. (Verse 17.). *Then some of his disciples said* etc. Here the second is touched, namely, the *doubt of the disciples,* which proceeded from this, that he had said: in a little while not to be seen and to be seen; on account of which he says: *Then some of his disciples said one to another: What is this that he saith to us: A little while, and you shall not see me: and again a little while, and you shall see me?* Of this they doubt first, because he seems to say two things contradictorily opposed; and of another thing they doubt, namely, whither he was going; whence: *and, Because I go to the Father?* For they did not know how he understood this[^p462-2]; they were doubting of the signification of the word.

29. (Verse 18.). *They said therefore: What is this that he saith to us: A little while? We know not what he speaketh.* They did not know what he meant to say by this; the disciples as yet had small intellect; whence the Lord rebuked them, Matthew, the fifteenth chapter[^p462-3]: « Are you also yet without intellect »?

30. (Verse 19.). *And Jesus knew.* Here the third is touched, namely, the *removal of the doubtfulness,* which was done by the Teacher before the inquiry of the disciples; on account of which he says: *Jesus knew that they had a mind to ask him* etc.; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third chapter[^p462-4]: « The eyes of the Lord far brighter than the sun, beholding round about all the ways of men, and the bottom of the deep, and looking into the hearts of men, into the most hidden parts »; and Ecclesiasticus, the sixteenth chapter: « Every heart is understood by him; and who understandeth his ways »? And he forestalled their doubt or inquiry, *and said to them: Of this do you inquire among yourselves, because I said to you*[^p462-5]*: A little while, and you shall not see me; and again a little while, and you shall see me.* To this doubt he gives satisfaction by showing a twofold time, namely, of *tribulation* and of *consolation,* and that on account of the one it was said: *You shall not see,* and on account of the other *you shall see.* And on this account there was no contradiction, because it is not referred to the same time, but on account of diverse times *a little while* was said twice. On this account he says:

31. (Verse 20.). *Amen, amen, I say to you, that you shall lament and weep: you shall lament* outwardly, *you shall weep* inwardly, for the time in which you shall not see me; and therefore blessed; Luke, the sixth chapter[^p462-6]: « Blessed are ye that weep now: for you shall laugh ». *But the world shall rejoice, and you shall be made sorrowful.* The world rejoices; Job, the twenty-first chapter: « They rejoice at the sound of the timbrel ». But the good are sorrowful; First Peter, the first chapter: « If now you must be for a little time made sorrowful in divers temptations ». *But your sorrow shall be turned into joy,* and therefore it was said: *A little while, and you shall see me;* Tobias, the third chapter[^p462-7]: « After tears and weeping thou pourest in joyfulness ». Therefore it is said in Ecclesiastes, the seventh chapter: « The heart of fools, where there is mirth; the heart of the wise, where there is sorrow ».

32. (Verse 21.). *A woman, when she is in labour.* Here the fourth is touched, namely, the *exemplification.* And the exemplification is of this, how quickly and in a little time sorrow will be turned into joy; and the example is of a woman giving birth, who, when she is in labour, is sorrowful, although she does a good thing; but when she has brought forth, she rejoices immediately. On this account he says: *A woman, when she is in labour, hath sorrow, because her hour is come,* namely, of bringing forth, which she would still wish put off because of the pain; because the pain in giving birth is vehement; Isaias, the thirteenth chapter[^p462-8]: « They shall be in pain as a woman in labour »; and the first to the Thessalonians, the fifth chapter: « And as the pains upon her that is with child, and they shall not escape ». *But when she hath brought forth*[^p462-9] *the child, she remembereth no more the anguish, for joy that a man is born into the world;* for joy, which she has of her offspring; Genesis, the twenty-first chapter: « The Lord hath made a laughter for me: whosoever shall hear of it will laugh with me », said Sara.

33. (Verse 22.). *So also you.* Here is the *adaptation of the example,* that they themselves would likewise be sorrowful in the passion of Christ, but were going to rejoice in his glorification or resurrection; on account of which he says: *So also you now indeed have sorrow,* and this over the withdrawal of the bridegroom; Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p462-10]: « The days will come, when the bridegroom shall be taken away from them, and then they shall mourn ». But this sorrow will last a little while; therefore he says: *Again*[^p462-11] *I will see you and your heart shall rejoice;* below, the twentieth chapter: « The disciples therefore were glad, when they saw the Lord ». And this not for a time, but for ever; therefore he says: *And your joy no man shall take from you;* because Christ, who was their joy, « rising again from the dead, dieth now no more »; Apocalypse, the seventh chapter: « God shall wipe away all tears from the eyes of the Saints » etc. And then your questions will be ended, nor will they trouble you, as now; therefore he says:
<!-- page 463 -->

34. (Verse 23.). *And in that day you shall not ask*[^p463-1] *me any thing,* that is, you shall not question. If this is referred to the day of glory, it is true, because then they will see all things clearly; Isaias, the fifty-fourth chapter: « I will make all thy children taught of the Lord ». But if it is understood after the glorification of Christ, it is still true; because then they had an interior teacher, according to that which was said above, the same[^p463-2]: « When that Spirit of truth is come, he will teach you all truth »; and: « The unction will teach you of all things ». — Or: *you shall not ask,* because you will not need to, but you will be blessed, to whom all things turn out according to wish.

### Questions.

35. Question I. But it is asked here first concerning what he says: *A little while, and you shall not see me, because I go to the Father.*

1. Because it is said in Matthew, the last chapter[^p463-3]: *Behold I am with you even to the consummation of the world.*

2. Likewise, what is it that he says: *A little while, and you shall see me?* because it is said above, the same[^p463-4]: *I go to the Father, and you shall see me no longer.* How does he withdraw and remain, is he seen and not seen?

**I respond:** It must be understood that in Christ there is a divine *being* and a human *being, visible* and *invisible, wretched* and *glorious.* As to the divine *being,* then, he never withdraws from the elect; as to the *human and invisible being* under the Sacrament, he will not leave *even to the consummation of the world;* as to the *human visible and impassible being,* he did not leave until the ascension; as to the *human visible and passible being,* he left in the passion; because he was not seen passible, nor did he afterwards converse with them as passible[^p463-5].

36. Question II. Likewise it is asked concerning what he says: *You shall lament and weep* etc. — It is asked whether the Apostles in that sorrow *merited* or *demerited.* That they *merited,* it seems:

1. Because it is said in the second to Timothy, the second chapter[^p463-6]: *If we suffer with him, we shall also reign with him:* therefore to suffer with him was meritorious for them.

2. Likewise, evil sorrow is converted into greater sorrow, but that which is converted into joy, that, I say, is good sorrow; but the sorrow of the Apostles was converted into joy, as is said in the letter[^p463-7]: *Your sorrow shall be converted into joy:* therefore it was good and meritorious.

**On the contrary** this is: 1. Because either they *willed* that Christ should die, or *not.* If *not,* then they sinned, whence Peter was reprehended for this[^p463-8]; but if they *willed* it: therefore they did not grieve.

2. Likewise it appears through the text[^p463-9], because the Lord exhorts them that they *be not troubled;* but the Lord does not dissuade from what is good: therefore to be troubled is not good.

3. Likewise, above, the fourteenth chapter[^p463-10], it is said: *If you loved me you would indeed rejoice:* therefore they ought to have rejoiced over the passion; but he who is sorrowful where he ought to rejoice is inordinately sorrowful: therefore the Apostles were inordinately and culpably troubled.

**I respond:** It must be said that there are two ways of judging of sorrow, of what kind it is, namely, on the part of its *origin* and on the part of its *mode.* — On the part of its *origin,* sorrow has its rise from *dilection*[^p463-11]. Some sorrow, then, is from *voluptuous* and carnal dilection, as a glutton is sorrowful at the loss of a good morsel; some from *natural* dilection, as the soul in separation from the body, or a mother at the death of her son; some from *social* dilection, as a friend is sorrowful when he is bodily parted from a friend whose conversation pleases him; some from *spiritual* dilection, as when someone loves out of charity and suffers with one suffering. — The first sorrow is *culpable;* the second and third *supportable;* but the fourth *praiseworthy.*

It must be understood, then, that the Apostles loved the Lord in two ways, namely, with a *social* love, by which someone loves a companion, because he does not wish to be separated from him; and with a *spiritual* love; therefore there was in them a certain *praiseworthy* sorrow, by which they suffered with Christ; and a certain *supportable* sorrow, by which they did not wish to be separated from Christ[^p463-12].

On the part of the *mode of being sorrowful* it must be understood that a mode must be held in sorrow; whence the Apostle says[^p463-13]: *Be not sorrowful, even as others who have no hope;* whence sorrow ought to be tempered with joy. And
<!-- page 464 -->
as to this the Apostles exceeded and were reprehensible, because they rejoiced in no way, nay, they grieved as men despairing. And as to removing this mode or immoderation the Lord exhorted them that they *be not sorrowful;* he exhorted that they *rejoice.* Whence Chrysostom[^p464-1]: « Great is the tyranny of sorrow, and we have need of much manliness, that we may resist this passion; and, chastening what is *useful,* let us dismiss what is *superfluous* ». Therefore they were praiseworthy in sorrow, but blameworthy in the mode.
