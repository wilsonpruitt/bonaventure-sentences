# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. X, Vers. 11-21
## *Ostendit se Dominus bonum pastorem quoad boni pastoris ingressum, hic ostendit secundo quoad boni pastoris affectum; et hoc quidem facit per hunc modum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 386 -->

16. *Ego sum pastor bonus.* Ostendit se Dominus bonum pastorem quoad boni pastoris ingressum, hic ostendit secundo *quoad boni pastoris affectum;* et hoc quidem facit per hunc modum. Primo ostenditur Christi ad suas oves *amicitia;* secundo, *diligentia;* tertio, *providentia;* quarto, *munificentia;* quinto, ex hoc *Iudaeorum discordia.*

(Vers. 11.). Primo ergo ostenditur Christi ad oves vera *amicitia* in comparatione ad amorem mercenariorum, qui non est amor verus. Propterea dicit: *Ego sum pastor bonus,* et hoc monstrat; *bonus pastor animam suam dat pro ovibus suis,* ex vehementi dilectione, quam habet circa eas; unde ipse dixit infra decimo quinto[^p386-8]: « Maiorem dilectionem nemo habet, ut animam suam ponat quis pro amicis suis ». Talis pastor erat Paulus, qui dicebat secundae ad Corinthios duodecimo: « Libentissime impendam et superimpendar ipse pro animabus vestris ». Non sic mercenarius; propter quod dicit:

17. (Vers. 12.). *Mercenarius autem et qui non est pastor, cuius non sunt oves propriae,* quia eas non amat, sed lucrum, pro quo servit. De hoc Gregorius[^p386-9]: « Mercenarius est, qui locum quidem pastoris tenet, sed lucra animarum non quaerit, terrenis commodis inhiat, honore praelationis gaudet, impensa sibi ab hominibus reverentia laetatur »; de quo potest dici illud Matthaei sexto: « Amen dico vobis, receperunt mercedem suam ». Hic scilicet *videt lupum venientem et dimittit oves et fugit,* quia lupum timet et oves non amat. « Lupus, ut dicit Gregorius[^p386-10], super oves venit, cum quilibet iniustus
<!-- page 387 -->
fideles et humiles opprimit ». De talibus lupis Actuum vigesimo: « Scio », inquit Paulus, « quod post discessum meum intrabunt in vos lupi rapaces, non parcentes gregi ». Ad adventum talis lupi mercenarius dimittit oves; Zachariae undecimo: « O pastor et idolum, derelinquens gregem »! Ad huius mercenarii fugam sequitur ovium dispersio; et ideo dicit: *Et lupus rapit et dispergit oves;* Ezechielis trigesimo quarto[^p387-1]: « Super faciem terrae dispersi sunt greges mei, et non erat qui requireret ». Et ratio praedictorum redditur, defectus scilicet verae amicitiae; propter quod dicit:

18. (Vers. 13.). *Mercenarius autem fugit, quia mercenarius est, et non pertinet ad eum de ovibus,* hoc est, quia diligit mercedem et non oves. Unde Gregorius[^p387-2]: « Stare in periculo ovium non potest qui in eo, quod ovibus praeest, non oves diligit, sed lucrum terrenum quaerit ». De talibus dicitur Ezechielis decimo tertio: « Non ascendistis ex adverso nec opposuistis vos murum pro domo Israel, ut staretis in praelio in die Domini ». Gregorius[^p387-3]: « Fugit, quia tacuit », quia timuit; timor enim fuga est.

19. (Vers. 14.). *Ego sum pastor bonus.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet Christi circa oves *diligentia,* quae consistit in ovium discretione et cognitione, propter quam dicit se *pastorem bonum;* propter quod dicit: *Ego sum pastor bonus et cognosco oves*[^p387-4] *meas, et cognoscunt me meae;* et in hoc notatur *diligentia,* secundum illud Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo: « Diligenter agnosce vultum pecoris tui tuosque greges considera »; secundae ad Timotheum secundo: « Novit Dominus qui sunt eius[^p387-5] ». — Hanc diligentiam manifestat per simile[^p387-6]; unde subdit:

20. (Vers. 15.). *Sicut novit me Pater, et ego agnosco Patrem,* sic, supple, agnosco meas, et cognoscunt me meae. Chrysostomus[^p387-7]: « *Sicut* est nota similitudinis, non aequalitatis », sicut infra decimo septimo Filius, orans Patrem pro discipulis, dicit, « ut sint unum, sicut et nos ». Hanc etiam diligentiam manifestat per *effectum;* unde dicit: *Et animam meam pono pro ovibus meis.* Sic Apostolus primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p387-8]: « Quotidie morior propter gloriam vestram, fratres »; et ipse Dominus, Ieremiae duodecimo: « Dedi dilectam animam meam in manus iniquorum ».

21. (Vers. 16.). *Et alias oves habeo.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *Christi erga oves*[^p387-9] *providentia,* quae consistit in ovium suarum adunatione, sicut pastor in unum colligit oves, ne patiantur incursum.
<!-- page 388 -->
Propterea dicit: *Et alias oves habeo, quae non sunt ex hoc ovili,* ut fideles ex gentibus praedestinatos; *et illas oportet me adducere,* quasi errantes; unde primae Petri secundo[^p388-1]: « Eratis sicut oves errantes, sed conversi estis nunc ad pastorem et episcopum animarum vestrarum ». *Et vocem meam audient, et fiet unum ovile et unus pastor,* propter unionem Ecclesiae ex Iudaeis et gentibus; unde ad Ephesios secundo[^p388-2]: « Ipse est pax nostra, qui fecit utraque unum », scilicet gentes et Iudaeos in unum ovile. Et ipse unus pastor; Ezechielis trigesimo quarto: « Suscitabo super eas pastorem unum, qui pascat eas, servum meum David ».

22. (Vers. 17.). *Propterea me Pater diligit.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *Christi erga oves munificentia,* in hoc, quod ipse, cum non esset debitor mortis, non necessitate coactus, sed mera liberalitate posuit animam suam pro ovibus; et hoc ad Patris praeceptum, cuius gratia meruit Patris dilectionem. Ideo dicit: *Propterea me diligit Pater, quia ego pono animam meam,* in morte scilicet et passione; *ut iterum sumam eam,* id est in resurrectione[^p388-3], et ita ex potestate, quia meum est *ponere* et meum est *resumere;* et hoc manifestat:

23. (Vers. 18.). *Nemo tollit eam a me; sed ego pono eam a me*[^p388-4], et ita libere pono; unde dicit: *Et potestatem habeo ponendi eam,* id est deponendi, *et potestatem habeo iterum sumendi eam;* unde Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio: « Oblatus est quia ipse voluit ». Et quare? Ponit rationem: quia *hoc mandatum accepi a Patre meo;* unde ad Philippenses secundo[^p388-5]: « Factus est obediens » Patri « usque ad mortem » etc.; unde infra decimo octavo dixit Petro: « Calicem, quem dedit mihi Pater, non vis, ut bibam illum »? Quasi dicat: obedire volo.

24. (Vers. 19.). *Dissensio iterum*[^p388-6] *facta est.* Tangitur hic quintum, scilicet *Iudaeorum discordia* ex Domini instructione exorta, quia aliqui non intelligentes putabant, ipsum insanire; alii vero, sublimia dicere. Propterea dicit: *Facta est iterum dissensio inter Iudaeos* — quia supra fuit facta propter *miraculum,* supra nono[^p388-7] — *propter sermones hos,* quia non intelligebant; propter quod:

25. (Vers. 20.). *Dicebant autem multi ex ipsis: Daemonium habet et insanit;* unde putabant, eum insanire. Isti erant de illis, de quibus primae ad Corinthios secundo[^p388-8]: « Animalis homo non percipit ea quae Dei sunt; stultitia enim est illi, et non potest intelligere, quia spiritualiter examinantur ». Unde recusabant audire; propterea dicunt: *Quid eum auditis?* Unde Dominus dicebat supra octavo[^p388-9]: « Quia non potestis audire sermonem meum ».

26. (Vers. 21.). *Alii dicebant: Haec verba non sunt daemonium habentis;* hi aliquo modo incipiebant illuminari, unde ad illuminationis miraculum confugiunt: *Nunquid daemonium potest caecorum oculos aperire?* Chrysostomus[^p388-10]: « Quia verbis non poterant obstruere ora illorum, demonstrationem ab operibus faciebant »; unde opera dant fidem verbis; ideo infra eodem[^p388-11]: « Et si mihi non creditis, operibus credite ».

### Quaestiones.

27. Quaest. I. Quaeritur hic de mercenario, utrum sit abiiciendus et prohibendus[^p388-12].

Quod *prohibendus* ab ovibus, videtur hic[^p388-13], quia vituperatur. — Quod autem *sustinendus,* videtur; ad Philippenses primo[^p388-14]: *Sive per occasionem, sive per veritatem Christus annuntietur, et in hoc gaudeo et gaudebo.* — Quod autem *laudandus;* Lucae decimo quinto[^p388-15]: *Quanti mercenarii in domo patris mei abundant panibus?* Ibi dicit Ambrosius, quod abundant fide, spe et caritate.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod differt *mercenarius* et *fur;* quia *mercenarius* veritatem dicit et oves conservat, sed *fur,* falsitatem et oves dilacerat, ut haereticus; et iste est omnino *abiiciendus,* sed mercenarius *tolerandus.* — Sed hic est duplex: quia quidam servit propter mercedem *temporalem,* et talis est *sustinendus,* sed tamen *vituperandus;* quidam, propter *aeternam,* et talis *sustinendus* et *approbandus,* sed tamen non est omnino *extollendus;* sed qui *solum amore,* est praeconiis extollendus[^p388-16].

28. Quaest. II. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Bonus pastor animam suam dat pro ovibus suis.*

<!-- page 389 -->

Ex hoc videtur, 1. quod *praelatus* teneatur mori pro subditis. — *Sed contra:* Pati martyrium est supererogationis; sed ad opera supererogationis nullus tenetur, nisi voto se astrinxerit: ergo videtur, quod praelatus ad hoc non teneatur.

2. Item videtur, quod *omnes* teneantur ad hoc; primae Ioannis tertio[^p389-1]: *Nos debemus pro fratribus animas ponere.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *mori* pro grege dominico potest esse tripliciter: aut pro ipso *de bono in melius promovendo,* et sic est *supererogationis* quantum ad omnes *praelatos;* aut pro ipso *ab imminenti periculo liberando,* et sic tenetur quilibet *praelatus,* quia suscepit curam gregis dominici, et *de manu eius requiretur sanguis*[^p389-2]; aut pro ipso *in extremae necessitatis articulo constituto,* quod non potest evadere damnationem, nisi homo se morti exponat; et sic dico, quod est *necessitatis quantum ad omnes,* sicut vendere sua et dare pauperibus, quando sunt in extrema necessitate. — Et secundum has vias currunt rationes.

29. Quaest. III. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Alias oves habeo, quae non sunt ex hoc ovili;* quia nulla ovis est *ovis,* cum est extra Ecclesiam[^p389-3], nulla *innocens.*

Item, quomodo dicit: *Illas oportet me adducere?* Quia Matthaei decimo quinto[^p389-4]: *Non sum missus nisi ad oves quae perierunt domus Israel:* ergo non debebat illas adducere.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod illas oves de gentibus nondum vocatas secundum praesentem iustitiam *suas* dicit, quia electae secundum praedestinationem aeternam[^p389-5]. Illas adduxit merito passionis et verbo praedicationis, non suo, sed Apostolorum, quia ipse in propria persona specialiter et principaliter venerat populo Israelitico praedicare, quibus promissus erat et a quibus occidendus.

Et secundum hoc intelligendum est illud Matthaei decimo quinto: *Non sum missus*[^p389-6] etc.

30. Quaest. IV. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Ego pono* animam meam *a me ipso;* quia sic videtur quilibet facere; unde Augustinus[^p389-7]: « Quilibet, qui moritur, animam ponit ». — *Si dicas,* quod ille necessario; *obiicitur* de Martyre, qui moritur voluntarie.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod aliter ponit animam *quilibet homo,* aliter *Martyr,* aliter *Christus:* quilibet homo *necessitate,* Martyr *voluntate,* sed Christus *voluntate simul et potestate.* Voluntas enim Martyris, etsi sit absoluta a moriendo *ut tunc,* non tamen a moriendo *simpliciter;* sed Christus omnino fuit liber[^p389-8].

31. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Potestatem habeo ponendi animam meam et iterum sumendi eam.*

1. Aut hoc dicit secundum *divinam naturam,* aut secundum *corpus,* quod deponit animam. Si secundum *divinam:* ergo Divinitas dimisit animam, quod nefas est dicere, et qui hoc dicit anathema est[^p389-9]. — Aut dicit secundum *corpus,* et illud non habet potestatem resumendi eam.

2. Item obiicitur, si corpus deposuit animam; et sicut dicit Damascenus[^p389-10], anima erat medium uniendi divinam naturam corpori: ergo divina natura deposuit corpus, quod nefas est.

Dicendum, quod *anima* hic dicitur *vita;* unde sensus est: potestatem habeo ponendi animam meam, id est vitam meam, scilicet deponendi per mortem, et potestatem habeo resumendi eam per resurrectionem. — Si autem dicatur *anima proprie;* tunc dicitur Christus *animam ponere,* id est, de carne deponere, non quia hoc corpus faciat, sed ipse Dei Filius, qui potest super utrumque, scilicet animam et corpus[^p389-11].

2. Quod vero obiicitur de medio, dicendum, quod est medium *distantiae, colligantiae* et *congruentiae.* Anima non est medium *distantiae* in illa unione, quia immediate Divinitas unitur corpori; nec *colligantiae,* quia non ligat unum cum alio; sed *congruentiae,* quia corpus, per hoc quod animabile, est Divinitati unibile de congruo; et ideo non oportet, quod solvatur[^p389-12].

32. Quaest. VI. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Propterea diligit me Pater, quia ego pono animam meam.* — Ergo videtur, quod Christus per passionem meruit divinam dilectionem.

**Contra:** Ab instanti conceptionis dignus erat super omnes diligi.

Dicendum, quod loquitur secundum humanam naturam; et *propterea* dicit ibi causam *meritoriam,*
<!-- page 390 -->
sicut ibi[^p390-1]: *Propter quod et Deus exaltavit illum.* Et meritum attenditur, non quia de indebito faciat *debitum,* nec quia de debito *magis debitum;* sed quia quod *debetur una via* et uno modo, scilicet optima voluntate interiori, fit *debitum alia,* scilicet opere exteriori[^p390-2].

## English
<!-- page 386 -->

16. *I am the good shepherd.* The Lord has shown himself the good shepherd as to the good shepherd's entrance; here he shows, second, *as to the good shepherd's affection;* and he does this in this way. First is shown Christ's *friendship* toward his sheep; second, his *diligence;* third, his *providence;* fourth, his *munificence;* fifth, *the discord of the Jews* arising from this.

(V. 11.). First, then, is shown Christ's true *friendship* toward the sheep, in comparison with the love of hirelings, which is not true love. Therefore he says: *I am the good shepherd,* and he shows this; *the good shepherd giveth his life for his sheep,* out of the vehement dilection which he has for them; whence he himself said below, in the fifteenth chapter[^p386-8]: « Greater dilection no man hath, that a man lay down his life for his friends ». Such a shepherd was Paul, who said in the second to the Corinthians, the twelfth chapter: « Most gladly will I spend and be spent myself for your souls ». Not so the hireling; on account of which he says:

17. (V. 12.). *But the hireling, and he that is not the shepherd, whose own the sheep are not,* because he does not love them, but the gain for which he serves. Of this Gregory[^p386-9]: « He is a hireling who holds indeed the place of the shepherd, but does not seek the gains of souls; he gapes after earthly advantages, rejoices in the honour of prelacy, is glad at the reverence paid to him by men »; of whom that of Matthew, the sixth chapter, can be said: « Amen I say to you, they have received their reward ». This one, namely, *seeth the wolf coming, and leaveth the sheep, and flieth,* because he fears the wolf and does not love the sheep. « The wolf, as Gregory says[^p386-10], comes upon the sheep when any unjust man
<!-- page 387 -->
oppresses the faithful and the humble ». Of such wolves, Acts, the twentieth chapter: « I know », says Paul, « that after my departure ravening wolves will enter in among you, not sparing the flock ». At the coming of such a wolf the hireling leaves the sheep; Zacharias, the eleventh chapter: « O shepherd and idol, that forsaketh the flock »! Upon the flight of this hireling follows the scattering of the sheep; and therefore he says: *And the wolf catcheth and scattereth the sheep;* Ezechiel, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p387-1]: « Upon the face of the earth my flocks were scattered, and there was none that sought them ». And the reason of the foregoing is given, namely the lack of true friendship; on account of which he says:

18. (V. 13.). *But the hireling flieth, because he is a hireling, and he hath no care for the sheep,* that is, because he loves the reward and not the sheep. Whence Gregory[^p387-2]: « He cannot stand in the danger of the sheep who, in that he is set over the sheep, loves not the sheep, but seeks earthly gain ». Of such it is said in Ezechiel, the thirteenth chapter: « You have not gone up to face the enemy, nor have you set yourselves as a wall for the house of Israel, to stand in battle in the day of the Lord ». Gregory[^p387-3]: « He fled, because he was silent », because he feared; for fear is flight.

19. (V. 14.). *I am the good shepherd.* Here the second is touched on, namely Christ's *diligence* about the sheep, which consists in the discerning and knowing of the sheep, on account of which he calls himself *the good shepherd;* on account of which he says: *I am the good shepherd, and I know my sheep*[^p387-4], *and mine know me;* and in this *diligence* is noted, according to that of Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter: « Be diligent to know the countenance of thy cattle, and consider thy own flocks »; the second to Timothy, the second chapter: « The Lord knoweth who are his[^p387-5] ». — This diligence he manifests by a likeness[^p387-6]; whence he adds:

20. (V. 15.). *As the Father knoweth me, and I know the Father,* so, supply, I know mine, and mine know me. Chrysostom[^p387-7]: « *As* is a mark of likeness, not of equality », as below, in the seventeenth chapter, the Son, praying the Father for the disciples, says, « that they may be one, as we also are ». This diligence he also manifests by its *effect;* whence he says: *And I lay down my life for my sheep.* So the Apostle in the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter[^p387-8]: « I die daily for your glory, brethren »; and the Lord himself, Jeremias, the twelfth chapter: « I have given my beloved soul into the hands of the wicked ».

21. (V. 16.). *And other sheep I have.* Here the third is touched on, namely *Christ's providence toward the sheep*[^p387-9], which consists in the gathering together of his sheep, as a shepherd gathers the sheep into one, lest they suffer an attack.
<!-- page 388 -->
Therefore he says: *And other sheep I have, that are not of this fold,* as the faithful from the Gentiles who are predestined; *and them I must bring,* as if wandering; whence the first of Peter, the second chapter[^p388-1]: « You were as sheep going astray, but you are now converted to the shepherd and bishop of your souls ». *And they shall hear my voice, and there shall be one fold and one shepherd,* on account of the union of the Church out of Jews and Gentiles; whence to the Ephesians, the second chapter[^p388-2]: « He is our peace, who hath made both one », namely Gentiles and Jews into one fold. And he himself is the one shepherd; Ezechiel, the thirty-fourth chapter: « I will set up over them one shepherd, who shall feed them, my servant David ».

22. (V. 17.). *Therefore doth the Father love me.* Here the fourth is touched on, namely *Christ's munificence toward the sheep,* in this, that he, though he was not a debtor of death, not compelled by necessity, but by pure liberality, laid down his life for the sheep; and this at the Father's precept, by reason of which he merited the Father's dilection. Therefore he says: *Therefore doth the Father love me, because I lay down my life,* namely in death and passion; *that I may take it again,* that is, in the resurrection[^p388-3], and so by power, because it is mine *to lay down* and mine *to take up again;* and he makes this plain:

23. (V. 18.). *No man taketh it away from me; but I lay it down from me*[^p388-4], and so I lay it down freely; whence he says: *And I have power to lay it down,* that is, to put it off, *and I have power to take it up again;* whence Isaias, the fifty-third chapter: « He was offered because it was his own will ». And why? He gives the reason: because *this commandment have I received of my Father;* whence to the Philippians, the second chapter[^p388-5]: « He became obedient » to the Father « unto death » etc.; whence below, in the eighteenth chapter, he said to Peter: « The chalice which the Father hath given me, wilt thou not that I drink it »? As if he should say: I will to obey.

24. (V. 19.). *A dissension again*[^p388-6] *arose.* Here the fifth is touched on, namely *the discord of the Jews* arisen from the Lord's instruction, because some, not understanding, thought that he was mad; but others, that he spoke sublime things. Therefore he says: *A dissension arose again among the Jews* — for above it arose on account of the *miracle,* above, in the ninth chapter[^p388-7] — *for these words,* because they did not understand; on account of which:

25. (V. 20.). *And many of them said: He hath a devil, and is mad;* whence they thought that he was mad. These were of those of whom the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p388-8]: « The sensual man perceiveth not those things that are of God; for it is foolishness to him, and he cannot understand, because they are spiritually examined ». Whence they refused to hear; therefore they say: *Why hear you him?* Whence the Lord said above, in the eighth chapter[^p388-9]: « Because you cannot hear my word ».

26. (V. 21.). *Others said: These are not the words of one that hath a devil;* these in some manner were beginning to be illuminated, whence they take refuge in the miracle of the illumination: *Can a devil open the eyes of the blind?* Chrysostom[^p388-10]: « Because they could not stop their mouths by words, they made their demonstration from the works »; whence works give faith to words; therefore below, in the same chapter[^p388-11]: « And if you believe not me, believe the works ».

### Questions.

27. Quaest. I. Here it is asked about the hireling, whether he is to be cast off and forbidden[^p388-12].

That he is *to be forbidden* the sheep, seems so here[^p388-13], because he is blamed. — But that he is *to be borne with,* it seems; to the Philippians, the first chapter[^p388-14]: *Whether by occasion, or by truth, Christ be preached, and in this I rejoice, and will rejoice.* — But that he is *to be praised;* Luke, the fifteenth chapter[^p388-15]: *How many hirelings in my father's house abound with bread?* There Ambrose says that they abound in faith, hope and charity.

**I respond:** It must be said that the *hireling* and the *thief* differ; because the *hireling* speaks the truth and preserves the sheep, but the *thief* speaks falsehood and tears the sheep, as the heretic; and this one is altogether *to be cast off,* but the hireling *to be tolerated.* — But this one is twofold: because one serves for a *temporal* reward, and such a one is *to be borne with,* but nevertheless *to be blamed;* another, for an *eternal* one, and such a one is *to be borne with* and *to be approved,* but nevertheless is not altogether *to be extolled;* but he who serves *by love alone* is to be extolled with praises[^p388-16].

28. Quaest. II. It is asked about what he says: *The good shepherd giveth his life for his sheep.*

<!-- page 389 -->

From this it seems, 1. that a *prelate* is bound to die for his subjects. — *On the contrary:* To suffer martyrdom is of supererogation; but no one is bound to works of supererogation unless he has bound himself by vow: therefore it seems that a prelate is not bound to this.

2. Again it seems that *all* are bound to this; the first of John, the third chapter[^p389-1]: *We ought to lay down our lives for the brethren.*

**I respond:** It must be said that *to die* for the Lord's flock can be in three ways: either for the flock *to be advanced from good to better,* and so it is *of supererogation* as regards all *prelates;* or for the flock *to be freed from an imminent danger,* and so every *prelate* is bound, because he has undertaken the care of the Lord's flock, and *at his hand the blood shall be required*[^p389-2]; or for the flock *set at the point of extreme necessity,* which cannot escape damnation unless a man expose himself to death; and so I say that it is *of necessity as regards all,* as to sell one's goods and give to the poor when they are in extreme necessity. — And the arguments run according to these ways.

29. Quaest. III. It is asked about what he says: *Other sheep I have, that are not of this fold;* because no sheep is a *sheep* while it is outside the Church[^p389-3], none *innocent.*

Again, how does he say: *Them I must bring?* Because Matthew, the fifteenth chapter[^p389-4]: *I was not sent but to the sheep that are lost of the house of Israel:* therefore he ought not to bring them.

**I respond:** It must be said that he calls those sheep of the Gentiles, not yet called according to present justice, *his own,* because they are chosen according to eternal predestination[^p389-5]. He brought them by the merit of his passion and by the word of preaching, not his own, but the Apostles', because he himself in his own person had come specially and principally to preach to the people of Israel, to whom he had been promised and by whom he was to be killed.

And according to this is to be understood that of Matthew, the fifteenth chapter: *I was not sent*[^p389-6] etc.

30. Quaest. IV. It is asked about what he says: *I lay down* my life *of myself;* because so it seems that anyone does; whence Augustine[^p389-7]: « Anyone who dies lays down his life ». — *If you say* that he does so of necessity, *objection is made* of the Martyr, who dies voluntarily.

**I respond:** It must be said that *any man* lays down his life in one way, the *Martyr* in another, *Christ* in another: any man *by necessity,* the Martyr *by will,* but Christ *by will and power together.* For the will of the Martyr, although it be released from dying *as at that time,* is not released from dying *simply;* but Christ was altogether free[^p389-8].

31. Quaest. V. Again it is asked about what he says: *I have power to lay down my soul, and to take it up again.*

1. Either he says this according to the *divine nature,* or according to the *body,* which puts off the soul. If according to the *divine:* then the Divinity let go of the soul, which it is impious to say, and whoever says this is anathema[^p389-9]. — Or he says it according to the *body,* and that has not the power of taking it up again.

2. Again it is objected, if the body put off the soul; and, as Damascene says[^p389-10], the soul was the medium of uniting the divine nature to the body: then the divine nature put off the body, which is impious.

It must be said that *soul* is here called *life;* whence the sense is: I have power to lay down my soul, that is, my life, namely to put it off by death, and I have power to take it up again by resurrection. — But if *soul* be said *properly;* then Christ is said *to lay down his soul,* that is, to put it off from the flesh, not because the body does this, but the Son of God himself, who has power over both, namely soul and body[^p389-11].

2. As to what is objected about the medium, it must be said that there is a medium *of distance, of binding together* and *of congruity.* The soul is not the medium *of distance* in that union, because the Divinity is united to the body immediately; nor *of binding together,* because it does not bind one thing with another; but *of congruity,* because the body, by this, that it is animable, is unitable to the Divinity by congruity; and therefore it is not necessary that it be dissolved[^p389-12].

32. Quaest. VI. Again it is asked about what he says: *Therefore doth the Father love me, because I lay down my life.* — Then it seems that Christ by his passion merited the divine dilection.

**On the contrary:** From the instant of his conception he was worthy to be loved above all.

It must be said that he speaks according to the human nature; and *therefore* he states there a *meritorious* cause,
<!-- page 390 -->
as there[^p390-1]: *For which cause God also hath exalted him.* And merit is regarded not because it makes of what is not owed *what is owed,* nor because of what is owed *what is more owed;* but because what *is owed in one way* and in one manner, namely by the best interior will, *is made owed in another,* namely by the exterior work[^p390-2].
