# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XII, Vers. 1-8
## *Postquam descripta est Iudaeorum conspiratio, describitur hic secundo ipsius roboratio; robur accepit ipsa conspiratio ex proditoris indignatione*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 410 -->

### Capitulum XII.

1. *Iesus ergo ante sex dies* etc. Postquam descripta est Iudaeorum conspiratio, describitur hic secundo *ipsius roboratio;* robur accepit ipsa conspiratio ex proditoris indignatione. Haec igitur proditoris indignatio ortum habuit ex avaritia, sed occasionem ex effusione unguenti facta in convivio. Ideo describitur per hunc modum: primo describitur *convivium Domini;* secundo, *effusio unguenti;* tertio, *indignatio Iudae;* quarto, *excusatio Mariae.*

(Vers. 1.). *Convivium* Christo Domino factum est, ubi erat suscitatus Lazarus, ante diem festum per sex dies; unde dicit: *Iesus ergo ante sex dies Paschae,* id est sexto die ante Pascha[^p410-3], *venit Bethaniam, ubi Lazarus fuerat mortuus, quem suscitavit Iesus.* Ibi factum est convivium in praesentia Lazari suscitati; unde dicit:

2. (Vers. 2.). *Fecerunt autem ei coenam ibi;* quia Apocalypsis tertio[^p410-4]: « Si quis aperuerit mihi ianuam, introibo ad illum et coenabo cum illo, et ipse mecum ». Et licet in praesentia Lazari fieret, Lazarus non ministrabat, sed manducabat, ut resurrectionis veritas probaretur; Marci quinto de puella suscitata, « iussit illi dari manducare » etc. Propter quod dicit: *Et Martha ministrabat, Lazarus vero unus erat ex discumbentibus cum ipso*[^p410-5]. Semper Martha ministrat et sollicita est tanquam prudens mulier; unde Lucae decimo: « Domine, non est tibi curae, quod soror mea reliquit me solam ministrare »? Ideo per eam recte vitam *activam* intelligimus, quae sollicitudinibus anxiatur.

3. (Vers. 3.). *Maria ergo accepit libram.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *effusio unguenti,* quae facta est a Maria, quae non discumbebat nec ministrabat, sed ipsa Dominum speciali refectione pascebat; unde circa eum vehementi devotione flagrabat[^p410-6], cuius devotio exprimitur in unguento effuso. Propter quod dicit: *Maria ergo accepit libram unguenti;* magna fuit unguenti quantitas, quia magna erat devotio; non cuiuslibet modi, sed *nardi,* id est de nardo. *Nardus* est herba aromatica, sicut dicit Isidorus[^p410-7], quae vehementis est odoris; propter quod dicitur in Canticis primo: « Dum esset rex in accubitu suo, nardus mea dedit odorem suum ». De spicis nardi fit pretiosum unguentum; unde dicitur
<!-- page 411 -->
Marci decimo quarto[^p411-1]: « Nardi spicati ». *Pistici;* Glossa: « id est fidelis », ad differentiam sophisticati; *pretiosi,* quia magni pretii erat et valoris. *Et unxit pedes Iesu*[^p411-2]*; et domus impleta est ex odore unguenti.* Ad litteram, Iudaei unguentis valde odoriferis utebantur; ideo Dominus pretiosissimum unguentum sibi offerri praecepit, Exodi trigesimo[^p411-3], et aliis illud prohibuit; unde vocavit illud *sanctum sanctorum,* quod esset « in odorem suavissimum Domino ».

4. (Vers. 4.). *Dixit ergo unus ex discipulis eius, Iudas.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *indignatio Iudae* contra mulierem, quia unguentum effuderat; propter quod dicit: *Dixit ergo unus ex discipulis eius, Iudas Iscariotes, qui erat eum traditurus,* propter avaritiam scilicet; et propter avaritiam dicit:

5. (Vers. 5.). *Quare hoc unguentum non veniit trecentis denariis et datum est egenis?* Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p411-4]: « Ut quid perditio haec? Potuit enim venumdari multo et dari pauperibus ». Et quia videbatur hoc dixisse ex pietate, ostendit Evangelista, quod hoc dixit ex cupiditate; propter quod dicit:

6. (Vers. 6.). *Dixit autem hoc, non quia de egenis pertinebat ad eum,* quia de pauperibus non curabat; quocontra Ecclesiastici quarto[^p411-5]: « Fili, eleemosynam pauperis ne defraudes, et oculos tuos ne transvertas a paupere » etc.; *sed quia fur erat et loculos habens,* tanquam dispensator, *ea quae mittebantur, portabat,* id est, asportabat et furabatur, et ideo gaudebat, quando plura dabantur; unde propter avaritiam suam vendidit Dominum; unde dicitur Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p411-6], quod « abiit ad principes sacerdotum et dixit: Quid vultis mihi dare, et ego vobis eum tradam »? Unde Chrysostomus: « Avaritia cor in servitutem redigit, haec proditorem fecit Iudam et Giezi leprosum et Ananiam morti tradidit ».

7. (Vers. 7.). *Dixit ergo eis*[^p411-7] *Iesus.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *excusatio Mariae,* quia ei Iudas insultabat quasi de male facto; et vult Dominus ostendere, quod melius fecit, quam si dedisset pauperibus; propter quod dicit: *Sinite*[^p411-8] *illam,* id est, nolite esse molesti ei. Non eos culpat, sed Mariam excusat: *Ut in diem sepulturae meae servet illud,* quod quidem melius est quam dare egenis.

8. (Vers. 8.). *Pauperes enim semper vobiscum habebitis*[^p411-9]; Deuteronomii decimo quinto: « Non deerunt pauperes in terra habitationis vestrae ». *Me autem non semper habebitis,* secundum corporalem praesentiam; infra decimo sexto: « Ad Patrem vado, et iam non videbitis me »; et ideo magis expedit, mihi humanitatis beneficium exhiberi; ideo non debetis esse molesti; Matthaei vigesimo sexto: « Quid molesti estis huic mulieri? Opus enim bonum operata est in me ».

9. *Spiritualiter* sunt hic duo notanda, quod scilicet fuerit illud *convivium,* et quod *unguentum.* — Et notandum, quod *triplex convivium* legimus Domino factum: primum, a Matthaeo, publicano converso; Matthaei nono[^p411-10]: *Eo discumbente in domo, ecce, multi publicani et peccatores discumbebant cum eo;* hoc est convivium *poenitentium.* — Secundum, a Simone, Pharisaeo; Lucae septimo[^p411-11]: *Rogabat Iesum quidam Pharisaeus, ut manducaret cum illo;* hoc est *proficientium,* quia Pharisaei Legem servabant. — Tertium, a Maria, Martha et Lazaro in Bethania, ut hic. *Bethania* est domus obedientiae[^p411-12], et haec est religio, in qua sunt homines in statu perfectionis, secundum illud Matthaei decimo nono: *Si vis perfectus esse, vade et vende omnia, quae habes, et da pauperibus;* et hoc est convivium *perfectorum.*

10. Nota similiter de *unguento* secundum spiritualem intelligentiam, quod *triplex* est, unde et ter Maria unxisse Dominum legitur. Est enim
<!-- page 412 -->
unguentum *contritionis,* quod significatum est in illo, Lucae septimo[^p412-1]: *Lacrymis coepit rigare pedes eius et unguento ungebat.* — *Devotionis,* et hoc significatum est hic et Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p412-2]: *Cum esset Iesus in domo Simonis leprosi, accessit ad eum mulier habens alabastrum unguenti pretiosi et effudit super caput ipsius recumbentis.* — *Compassionis,* et hoc Marci ultimo[^p412-3]: *Maria Magdalena et Maria Iacobi et Salome emerunt aromata, ut venientes ungerent Iesum.* — Primo unguento inunguntur *pedes;* secundo, *caput;* tertio vero, *totum Domini corpus*[^p412-4].

### Quaestiones.

11. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod dicit, quod *ante sex dies Paschae venit Bethaniam,* ubi unctus est a Maria:

1. Quia Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p412-5] dicitur: *Scitis, quia post biduum Pascha fiet,* et post, quod *in domo Simonis* unctus est a Maria: ergo videtur per *biduum ante,* et hic dicitur, quod per *sex dies.*

2. Item obiicitur de hoc quod dicit, quod *unxit pedes,* quia dicitur Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p412-6], quod *effudit super caput eius.* Si effudit super caput, quomodo unxit pedes?

3. Item, de alia contrarietate, quia ibi[^p412-7] dicitur, quod *videntes discipuli indignati sunt;* hic autem dicitur, quod *unus indignatus,* scilicet proditor.

Ad has tres contrarietates solvitur per tres regulas Augustini, de Consensu Evangelistarum[^p412-8]:

Prima, quod notandum, quod aliquando unus Evangelista aliquid dicit secundum *seriem historiae,* alter dicit per *recapitulationem.* Et per hoc solvitur primum; quia Matthaeus recapitulando dicit, sicut ibidem ostendit[^p412-9].

Secunda regula est, quod unus *omittit,* alter *dicit;* et utrumque factum est, sed non utrumque ab utroque dictum; sic solvitur secundum: quia et pedes unxit et deinde totum super caput effudit[^p412-10].

Tertia regula est, quod mos est Evangelistarum pluralem numerum pro singulari ponere per synecdochen; et ideo dixerunt alii, quod discipuli indignati, Ioannes exprimit proprie, quis fuerit[^p412-11].

12. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit Evangelista, 1. quod Iudas sic *dixit, non quia de egenis pertinebat ad eum.* — *Sed contra:* Erat dispensator: ergo pertinebat.

2. Item dicit, quod *fur erat et loculos habebat;* unde est, quod Dominus volebat furi loculos committere? — Videtur, quod daret ei occasionem peccandi.

3. Item, quare non corripuit eum Dominus, ex quo sciebat, eum esse in peccato?

4. Item, cum Dominus esset in extrema paupertate et praecepisset discipulis non recipere pecuniam[^p412-12]; unde est, quod loculos habebat?

1. Dico ad primum, quod *pertinere* dupliciter est: aut quantum ad affectum, aut quantum ad officium[^p412-13]; primo modo non pertinebat, secundo modo sic.

2. Ad secundum dico, quod duplex fuit ratio, scilicet *divini iudicii dispensatio* et *nostra instructio: divini iudicii dispensatio* in hoc, quod Dominus malo eius desiderio satisfaciebat, sicut avaros in praesenti ditat; ut etiam Iudas non haberet excusationem, quod proderet Dominum propter inopiam[^p412-14]. *Nostra instructio,* quia ostendit Dominus, quod
<!-- page 413 -->
parvam vim facit in divitiis Ecclesiarum, sed magnam in thesauro animarum[^p413-1].

3. Ad aliud dicendum, quod non *corripuit,* quia sciebat, quod non melioraretur, sed peior efficeretur; nec *officium abstulit,* ne proderet crimen et ad maius crimen incitaret[^p413-2].

4. Ad aliud dicendum, quod, sicut dicit Hieronymus[^p413-3], Dominus habuit loculos, non ad usum suum, sed magis ad usum pauperum. Unde quando solvit didrachma, misit Petrum ad mare, Matthaei decimo septimo, ubi invenit pretium in ore piscis; quia nolebat ea quae data erant pauperibus, in usum suum convertere. — Alia ratio, ut innotesceret contra haereticos[^p413-4], quod liceret Ecclesiae suae divitias possidere.

13. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit in littera: *Sinite illam, ut in diem sepulturae meae servet illud.* Si enim effuderat[^p413-5] super caput, quomodo in diem sepulturae poterat conservare?

Ad hoc respondetur tripliciter: uno modo littera exponitur sic, *ut servet illud,* id est partem ex illo[^p413-6]. Sed illud non valet; quia non unxit Maria corpus Domini in die sepulturae. — Ideo exponit Victor[^p413-7]: *servet illud,* id est devotionem circa illud, scilicet devotionem ungendi, quia voluit corpus Domini ungere, quamvis non unxerit. — Alio modo sic, ut fiat vis in verbo *servandi; servare*[^p413-8] dicitur contra *perdere;* unguentum, quod in terram effunditur, perditur, sed quo mortuorum corpora condiuntur, *perdi* non dicitur, sed *servari.* Modo voluit Dominus contra Iudam dicere, quod non est facta unguenti *perditio,* sed *conservatio* in diem sepulturae praeveniendo; unde dicitur Marci decimo quarto[^p413-9]: *Praevenit corpus meum ungere in sepulturam.* Et sic intelligendum est.

## English
<!-- page 410 -->

### Chapter XII.

1. *Jesus therefore, six days before* etc. After the conspiracy of the Jews has been described, here is described second *its strengthening;* the conspiracy itself received strength from the indignation of the traitor. This indignation of the traitor, then, had its rise from avarice, but its occasion from the pouring out of the ointment done at the banquet. Therefore it is described in this manner: first is described *the banquet of the Lord;* second, *the pouring out of the ointment;* third, *the indignation of Judas;* fourth, *the excusing of Mary.*

(V. 1.). A *banquet* was made for Christ the Lord, where Lazarus had been raised, six days before the festal day; whence he says: *Jesus therefore, six days before the pasch,* that is, on the sixth day before the Pasch[^p410-3], *came to Bethania, where Lazarus had been dead, whom Jesus raised to life.* There the banquet was made in the presence of Lazarus raised; whence he says:

2. (V. 2.). *And they made him a supper there;* because Apocalypse, the third[^p410-4]: « If any man shall open to me the door, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me ». And although it was done in the presence of Lazarus, Lazarus was not serving, but eating, that the truth of the resurrection might be proved; Mark, the fifth, of the girl raised, « he commanded that something should be given her to eat » etc. On account of which he says: *And Martha served, but Lazarus was one of them that were at table with him*[^p410-5]. Martha always serves and is solicitous, as a prudent woman; whence Luke, the tenth: « Lord, hast thou no care that my sister hath left me alone to serve »? Therefore through her we rightly understand the *active* life, which is made anxious with solicitudes.

3. (V. 3.). *Mary therefore took a pound.* Here is touched the second, namely, *the pouring out of the ointment,* which was done by Mary, who neither reclined at table nor served, but herself fed the Lord with a special refreshment; whence she was burning[^p410-6] about him with vehement devotion, whose devotion is expressed in the ointment poured out. On account of which he says: *Mary therefore took a pound of ointment;* great was the quantity of the ointment, because great was the devotion; not of any kind whatever, but *of nard,* that is, from nard. *Nard* is an aromatic herb, as Isidore[^p410-7] says, which is of vehement odour; on account of which it is said in the Canticles, the first: « While the king was at his repose, my nard sent forth the odour thereof ». From the spikes of nard is made a precious ointment; whence it is said in
<!-- page 411 -->
Mark, the fourteenth[^p411-1]: « Of spiked nard ». *Pistic;* the Gloss: « that is, faithful », as distinct from the adulterated; *precious,* because it was of great price and value. *And anointed the feet of Jesus*[^p411-2]*; and the house was filled with the odour of the ointment.* To the letter, the Jews made use of very odoriferous ointments; therefore the Lord commanded the most precious ointment to be offered to him, Exodus, the thirtieth[^p411-3], and forbade it to others; whence he called it *the holy of holies,* because it was « for a most sweet odour to the Lord ».

4. (V. 4.). *Then one of his disciples, Judas, said.* Here is touched the third, namely, *the indignation of Judas* against the woman, because she had poured out the ointment; on account of which he says: *Then one of his disciples, Judas Iscariot, he that was about to betray him, said,* on account of avarice, namely; and on account of avarice he says:

5. (V. 5.). *Why was not this ointment sold for three hundred pence, and given to the poor?* Matthew, the twenty-sixth[^p411-4]: « To what purpose is this loss? For it might have been sold for much, and given to the poor ». And because he seemed to have said this out of piety, the Evangelist shows that he said this out of cupidity; on account of which he says:

6. (V. 6.). *Now he said this, not because anything concerning the poor pertained to him,* because he did not care for the poor; against which Ecclesiasticus, the fourth[^p411-5]: « Son, defraud not the poor of alms, and turn not away thy eyes from the poor » etc.; *but because he was a thief, and having the purse,* as a dispenser, *carried the things that were put therein,* that is, carried them away and stole them, and therefore he rejoiced when more were given; whence on account of his avarice he sold the Lord; whence it is said in Matthew, the twenty-sixth[^p411-6], that « he went to the chief priests and said: What will you give me, and I will deliver him unto you »? Whence Chrysostom: « Avarice brings the heart into servitude; this made Judas a traitor and Giezi a leper, and delivered Ananias to death ».

7. (V. 7.). *Jesus therefore said to them*[^p411-7]. Here is touched the fourth, namely, *the excusing of Mary,* because Judas was insulting her as for a thing ill done; and the Lord wills to show that she did better than if she had given to the poor; on account of which he says: *Let*[^p411-8] *her alone,* that is, do not be troublesome to her. He does not blame them, but excuses Mary: *That she may keep it against the day of my burial,* which indeed is better than to give to the poor.

8. (V. 8.). *For the poor you shall have always with you*[^p411-9]; Deuteronomy, the fifteenth: « There will not be wanting poor in the land of your habitation ». *But me you shall not have always,* according to bodily presence; below, the sixteenth: « I go to the Father, and now you shall not see me »; and therefore it is more expedient that the benefit of humanity be shown to me; therefore you ought not to be troublesome; Matthew, the twenty-sixth: « Why are you troublesome to this woman? For she hath wrought a good work upon me ».

9. *Spiritually* there are two things to be noted here, namely, what that *banquet* was, and what the *ointment.* — And it is to be noted that we read of a *threefold banquet* made for the Lord: the first, by Matthew, the converted publican; Matthew, the ninth[^p411-10]: *As he was sitting at meat in the house, behold, many publicans and sinners sat down with him;* this is the banquet *of the penitent.* — The second, by Simon, the Pharisee; Luke, the seventh[^p411-11]: *One of the Pharisees desired Jesus to eat with him;* this is the banquet *of those making progress,* because the Pharisees kept the Law. — The third, by Mary, Martha and Lazarus in Bethania, as here. *Bethania* is the house of obedience[^p411-12], and this is religion, in which men are in the state of perfection, according to that of Matthew, the nineteenth: *If thou wilt be perfect, go sell all that thou hast, and give to the poor;* and this is the banquet *of the perfect.*

10. Note similarly concerning the *ointment* according to the spiritual intelligence, that it is *threefold,* whence also Mary is read to have anointed the Lord three times. For there is
<!-- page 412 -->
the ointment *of contrition,* which is signified in that of Luke, the seventh[^p412-1]: *With tears she began to wash his feet, and anointed them with ointment.* — *Of devotion,* and this is signified here and in Matthew, the twenty-sixth[^p412-2]: *When Jesus was in the house of Simon the leper, there came to him a woman having an alabaster box of precious ointment, and poured it on his head as he was at table.* — *Of compassion,* and this in Mark, the last[^p412-3]: *Mary Magdalen, and Mary the mother of James, and Salome, bought sweet spices, that coming, they might anoint Jesus.* — With the first ointment the *feet* are anointed; with the second, the *head;* but with the third, *the whole body of the Lord*[^p412-4].

### Questions.

11. Quaest. I. But it is asked here first about this that he says, that *six days before the pasch he came to Bethania,* where he was anointed by Mary:

1. Because in Matthew, the twenty-sixth[^p412-5], it is said: *You know that after two days shall be the pasch,* and afterwards, that *in the house of Simon* he was anointed by Mary: therefore it seems to have been *two days before,* and here it is said, *six days.*

2. Again, it is objected concerning this that he says, that she *anointed the feet,* because it is said in Matthew, the twenty-sixth[^p412-6], that she *poured it on his head.* If she poured it on the head, how did she anoint the feet?

3. Again, of another contrariety, because there[^p412-7] it is said that *the disciples seeing it had indignation;* but here it is said that *one had indignation,* namely, the traitor.

To these three contrarieties the solution is by three rules of Augustine, On the Agreement of the Evangelists[^p412-8]:

The first, that it is to be noted that sometimes one Evangelist says something according to *the series of the history,* another says it by *recapitulation.* And by this the first is solved; because Matthew speaks by recapitulating, as he shows in the same place[^p412-9].

The second rule is that one *omits,* another *says;* and both things were done, but both were not said by both; thus the second is solved: because she both anointed the feet and afterwards poured out the whole on the head[^p412-10].

The third rule is that it is the custom of the Evangelists to put the plural number for the singular by synecdoche; and therefore the others said that the disciples had indignation, John expresses properly who it was[^p412-11].

12. Quaest. II. Again, it is asked concerning this that the Evangelist says, 1. that Judas thus *said, not because anything concerning the poor pertained to him.* — *On the contrary:* He was the dispenser: therefore it pertained to him.

2. Again, he says that *he was a thief and had the purse;* whence is it that the Lord willed to commit the purse to a thief? — It seems that he would be giving him an occasion of sinning.

3. Again, why did the Lord not correct him, since he knew that he was in sin?

4. Again, since the Lord was in extreme poverty and had commanded the disciples not to receive money[^p412-12]; whence is it that he had the purse?

1. I say to the first, that *to pertain* is twofold: either as to affection, or as to office[^p412-13]; in the first mode it did not pertain, in the second mode it did.

2. To the second I say that the reason was twofold, namely, *the dispensation of divine judgement* and *our instruction: the dispensation of divine judgement* in this, that the Lord was satisfying his evil desire, as he enriches the avaricious in the present; so that Judas also might have no excuse, that he betrayed the Lord on account of want[^p412-14]. *Our instruction,* because the Lord showed that he
<!-- page 413 -->
sets small store by the riches of the Churches, but great store by the treasure of souls[^p413-1].

3. To the other it must be said that he did not *correct* him, because he knew that he would not be bettered, but made worse; nor did he *take away his office,* lest he should betray his crime and incite him to a greater crime[^p413-2].

4. To the other it must be said that, as Jerome[^p413-3] says, the Lord had a purse, not for his own use, but rather for the use of the poor. Whence when he paid the didrachma, he sent Peter to the sea, Matthew, the seventeenth, where he found the price in the mouth of a fish; because he would not turn the things that had been given to the poor to his own use. — Another reason, that it might be made known against the heretics[^p413-4] that it was lawful for his Church to possess riches.

13. Quaest. III. Again, it is asked concerning this that he says in the letter: *Let her alone, that she may keep it against the day of my burial.* For if she had poured[^p413-5] it out on the head, how could she conserve it against the day of burial?

To this it is answered in three ways: in one mode the letter is expounded thus, *that she may keep it,* that is, a part of it[^p413-6]. But that does not hold; because Mary did not anoint the body of the Lord on the day of burial. — Therefore Victor[^p413-7] expounds: *may keep it,* that is, the devotion about it, namely, the devotion of anointing, because she willed to anoint the body of the Lord, although she did not anoint it. — In another mode thus, so that the force is in the word *of keeping; to keep*[^p413-8] is said against *to lose;* ointment which is poured out on the ground is lost, but that with which the bodies of the dead are embalmed is not said to be *lost,* but *kept.* Now the Lord willed to say against Judas that there was not made a *loss* of the ointment, but a *conserving* against the day of burial, by coming beforehand; whence it is said in Mark, the fourteenth[^p413-9]: *She is come beforehand to anoint my body for the burial.* And thus it is to be understood.
