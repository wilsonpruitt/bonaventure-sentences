# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XV, Vers. 1-11
## *Primo hortatur discipulos ad dilectionem Dei*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 446 -->

### Capitulum XV.

#### Secundo, circa constantiam in vera dilectione tria ponuntur.

1. *Ego sum vitis vera* etc. In praecedenti capitulo hortatus est Dominus discipulos suos ad fidei constantiam, in hoc hortatur eos ad *dilectionem probatam et veram*[^p446-9]. Et quoniam vera dilectio primo diligit Deum, deinde et amicum in Deo et inimicum diligit et tolerat propter Deum; ideo primo hortatur discipulos ad *dilectionem Dei;* secundo, ad *dilectionem amicorum,* ibi[^p446-10]: *Hoc est praeceptum meum, ut diligatis* etc.; tertio, ad *dilectionem et tolerantiam inimicorum,* ibi: *Si mundus vos odit, scitote* etc.

#### Primo hortatur discipulos ad dilectionem Dei.

Hortatur eos primo ad *dilectionem Dei;* et quia « qui manet in caritate in Deo manet[^p446-11] », hortatur ad *manendum in se,* et hoc quidem facit hoc ordine. Primo describitur *mansionis locus;* secundo, *fructus;* tertio, *modus;* quarto, *effectus.*

2. (Vers. 1.). *Mansionis* igitur *locus* exprimitur primo *ipse Christus,* in quo manere debent fideles, sicut palmes manet in vite. Propterea dicit: *Ego sum vitis vera,* ideo *vera,* quia vinum verum producit, de qua Genesis penultimo[^p446-12]: « Lavabit in vino stolam suam et in sanguine uvae pallium suum, ligans ad vineam pullum suum et ad vitem asinam
<!-- page 447 -->
suam ». Haec vitis non est inculta, ideo dicit: *Et Pater meus agricola est,* non tantum qui exterius colat, sed qui interius augeat; primae ad Corinthios tertio[^p447-1]: « Ego plantavi, Apollo rigavit, Deus autem incrementum dedit »; et paulo post: « Dei agricultura estis, Dei aedificatio estis ». Ideo Pater verus agricola, quia opus eius exercet; unde dicit:

3. (Vers. 2.). *Omnem palmitem in me non ferentem fructum, tollet eum;* Matthaei tertio[^p447-2]: « Securis ad radicem arboris posita est; omnis arbor, quae non facit fructum bonum, excidetur et in ignem mittetur ». *Et omnem, qui fert fructum, purgabit eum, ut fructum plus afferat. Purgabit,* per *poenitentiam;* unde primae Ioannis primo[^p447-3]: « Si confessi fuerimus peccata, fidelis et iustus est, qui dimittat nobis omnia peccata nostra et emundet nos ab omni iniquitate ». Purgat etiam per *patientiam;* unde in Psalmo: « Quoniam probasti nos, Deus; igne nos examinasti, sicut examinatur argentum; induxisti nos in laqueum » etc. In hac igitur vite optime locantur *rami mundi;* ideo Apostolos iam mundos hortatur, ut in se maneant; propter quod dicit:

4. (Vers. 3.). *Iam vos mundi estis propter sermonem, quem locutus sum vobis.* Iste fuit sermo fidei, qui purificat credentes; Actuum decimo quinto[^p447-4]: « Fide purificans corda eorum ». Quia ergo mundi estis:

5. (Vers. 4.). *Manete in me,* ut in vite, *et ego in vobis,* ut in templo; de hoc primae Ioannis quarto[^p447-5]: « Qui manet in caritate in Deo manet, et Deus in eo ». Ideo mutua est mansio, quia mutua dilectio; supra deeimo quarto: « Qui diligit me diligetur a Patre meo, et ego diligam eum et manifestabo ei me ipsum, et ad eum veniemus et mansionem apud eum faciemus ».

*Sicut palmes* etc. Tetigit mansionis locum, tangit hic secundo *mansionis fructum,* quem habet palmes, non sine vite, sed in vite. Ideo monstrat, quod fructus ex palmite originem habet a vite, primo *in opposito,* scilicet per separationem, secundo *in proposito*[^p447-6], per mansionem. Propter quod dicit: *Sicut palmes non potest ferre fructum a semetipso, nisi manserit in vite; sic et*[^p447-7] *vos, nisi in me manseritis.* Et ideo, si volunt fructificare, necesse est, quod maneant in eo, unde fructus habet originem; ad Ephesios primo: « Ipsum dedit caput super omnem Ecclesiam, quae est corpus eius et plenitudo eius, qui omnia in omnibus adimplevit »; Ioannis primo: « De plenitudine eius nos omnes accepimus gratiam pro gratia ». Ideo vos non potestis fructificare, nisi manseritis in me; sed in me potestis, quia:

6. (Vers. 5.). *Ego sum vitis, et*[^p447-8] *vos palmites;* et ideo *qui manet in me, hic fert fructum multum.* Et ideo manete in me tanquam in fructus origine; *quia sine me nihil potestis facere;* Isaiae vigesimo sexto[^p447-9]: « Omnia opera nostra in nobis operatus es, Domine ». Et quod nihil possint sine ipso, ostendit per similitudinem:

7. (Vers. 6.). *Si quis in me non manserit, mittetur foras sicut palmes,* scilicet a vite separatus. Extra vineam proiicitur per separationem ab unitate Ecclesiae; Apocalypsis ultimo[^p447-10]: « Foris canes et venefici et impudici » etc. Et non faciet fructum, unde *et arescet,* per amissionem humoris gratiae; *et colligent eum et in ignem mittent, et ardet,* quia ardebit per inflictionem poenae.

Unde quatuor mala consequuntur: primo, quod *proiicitur;* Matthaei decimo quinto[^p447-11]: « Omnis plantatio, quam non plantavit Pater meus caelestis, eradicabitur. — Secundo, *arescet;* Matthaei vigesimo primo dixit Dominus ficulneae, in qua non invenit nisi folia: « Nunquam ex te fructus nascatur in sempiternum. Et continuo exaruit »; Psalmus: « Aruit tanquam testa virtus mea ». — Tertio, *colligitur* per ministerium angelicum; unde Matthaei decimo tertio[^p447-12]: « Colligent de regno eius omnia scandala ». — Quarto, *igne comburitur;* Ezechielis decimo quinto: « Fili hominis, quid fiet de ligno vitis » ? Et sequitur: « Ecce, igni datum est in escam ».

8. (Vers. 7.). *Si manseritis in me.* Supra[^p447-13] ostendit, se esse fructus originem per *oppositum*
<!-- page 448 -->
*mansionis;* hic nunc ostendit per *ipsam mansionem,* quae fructum dat et respectu *sui* et respectu *proximi.* Respectu *sui: Si manseritis in me, et verba mea in vobis manserint;* non sicut illi, de quibus Lucae octavo[^p448-1]: « Ad tempus credunt et in tempore tentationis recedunt »; sed sicut illi, de quibus in Psalmo: « Qui servant testamentum eius et memores sunt mandatorum ipsius ad faciendum ea »; *quodcumque volueritis, petetis, et fiet vobis;* supra nono: « Si quis voluntatem Dei facit, hunc exaudit ». Non solum est fructus respectu *sui,* sed etiam *proximi* convertendi ad Dei gloriam; ideo subdit:

9. (Vers. 8.). *In hoc clarificatus est Pater meus, ut fructum plurimum afferatis;* ut scilicet homines convertantur vestro verbo et exemplo; Matthaei quinto[^p448-2]: « Sic luceat lux vestra coram hominibus, ut videant opera vestra bona et glorificent Patrem vestrum, qui in caelis est »; *et* ex hoc *efficiamini mei discipuli,* id est imitatores mei, qui plurimum fructum attuli per meam mortem, sicut supra duodecimo[^p448-3]: « Si granum frumenti mortuum fuerit, multum fructum affert ». Sic etiam Apostoli; unde Ecclesia cantat de eis, quod « plantaverunt Ecclesiam sanguine suo »; ad Colossenses primo: « Adimpleo ea quae desunt passionum Christi ».

10. (Vers. 9.). *Sicut dilexit me Pater.* Hic tertio tangitur *manendi modus,* qui est per dilectionem, ad quam nos debet invitare quod ipse prior dilexit; propterea dixit: *Sicut dilexit me Pater, et ego dilexi vos;* et ideo *manete in dilectione mea;* primae Ioannis quarto[^p448-4]: « Nos ergo diligamus Deum, quoniam ipse prior dilexit nos ». Et quia vera dilectio per opus manifestatur, subiungit:

11. (Vers. 10.). *Si praecepta mea servaveritis, manebitis in dilectione mea; sicut ego praecepta Patris mei servavi et maneo in eius dilectione;* supra decimo quarto[^p448-5]: « Ut cognoscat mundus, quia diligo Patrem, et sicut mandatum dedit mihi Pater, sic facio ». Sic et nos debemus facere; primae Ioannis secundo: « Qui dicit, se in illo manere, debet, sicut ille ambulavit, et ipse ambulare »; Deuteronomii decimo: « Et nunc, Israel, quid Dominus Deus tuus petit a te, nisi ut timeas Dominum Deum tuum et ambules in viis eius et diligas eum ac servias Domino Deo tuo in toto corde tuo et in tota anima tua custodiasque mandata Domini et caeremonias eius, quas ego hodie praecipio tibi, ut bene sit tibi » ?

12. (Vers. 11.). *Haec locutus sum vobis.* Hic tangitur quartum, scilicet *effectus mansionis;* et effectus est *gaudium in praesenti* et *gaudium in futuro.* Propter *gaudium in praesenti* dicit: *Ut gaudium meum in vobis sit,* hoc est gaudium purum, quo homo gaudet in Domino, non in mundo; de quo ad Philippenses ultimo[^p448-6]: « Gaudete in Domino semper; iterum dico: gaudete ». — Propter *gaudium in futuro* dicit: *Et gaudium vestrum impleatur,* de quo infra decimo sexto[^p448-7]: « Petite, et accipietis, ut gaudium vestrum sit plenum ». Gaudium nostrum impletur a gaudio Domini; de quo Matthaei vigesimo quinto: « Intra in gaudium Domini tui ».

### Quaestiones.

13. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic de hoc quod dicit: *Ego sum vitis vera.*

Obiicit Augustinus[^p448-8]: Si *vitis* de Christo dicitur, sicut dicitur *leo, lapis;* et ista dicuntur non per proprietatem, sed per similitudinem; et illa dicuntur *vera,* quae dicuntur per proprietatem: non videtur, quod deberet dicere: *Ego sum vitis vera,* sed similitudinaria.

Et respondet Augustinus[^p448-9], quod addens *vera,* « ab illa se discernit, cui dictum est: *Quomodo conversa es in amaritudinem, vitis aliena* » ? Ieremiae secundo: *Quomodo conversa es in pravum, vinea aliena?* Unde non dicitur *vera,* quia non per similitudinem; sed *vera,* quia non aliena.

Potest tamen dici, quod sicut *vinum verum* est, quod habet operationem vini; sic *vitis vera,* quae habet operationem vitis[^p448-10]. *Vitis* igitur *ad litteram* tunc est *vera,* quando vinum producit; *non vera,* quae non fructificat. Sic et *similitudinaria* dicitur *vera,* quae habet actum vitis; *non vera,* quae non habet. Sic et *Christus,* quia verum habet actum vitis, *vera vitis* est. Sed synagoga non, de qua Isaiae quinto[^p448-11]: « Exspectavit, ut faceret uvas, fecit autem spinas ».
<!-- page 449 -->

14. Quaest. II. Sed tunc restat quaestio: quare Dominus magis *grano frumenti* se comparat inter grana, et *ligno vitis* inter ligna?

**Respondeo:** Hoc est propter corpus *verum* et *mysticum. Verum* corpus Christi verus cibus est, et verus sanguis est verus potus[^p449-1]. Quia igitur ex granis frumenti fit panis optime reficiens, et ex vite vinum optime potans; ideo comparavit se viti et grano. Et inde est, quod in his duabus speciebus Sacramentum altaris conficitur. — Alia ratio est propter corpus *mysticum,* quia, sicut ex purissimis granis frumenti et acinis uvae conficitur panis et vinum; sic ex puris fidelibus corpus Christi mysticum[^p449-2].

15. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur, secundum quam naturam Christus sit *vitis,* utrum secundum *divinam,* aut secundum *humanam.* — Et quod secundum *humanam,* dicit Augustinus[^p449-3]: « Unius naturae sunt vitis et palmites; propter quod, cum esset Deus, cuius naturae non sumus, factus est homo, ut in illo esset vitis humana natura, cuius et nos palmites esse possemus ».

*Sed contra* hoc est: 1. Quia secundum quod vitis influit in palmites humorem: ergo si humorem gratiae non influit nisi solus Deus et secundum quod Deus[^p449-4], ergo est vitis secundum *divinam naturam.*

2. Item, in hac vite omnes Sancti ab Abel usque ad ultimum iustum manserunt et processerunt[^p449-5]; sed Christus secundum *humanam* naturam secutus est Abel: ergo non fuerunt in eo palmites secundum humanam naturam.

**Respondeo:** Intelligendum est, quod quaedam dicuntur de Christo secundum *divinam* naturam *pure,* ita quod nihilominus dicuntur, abstracta humana natura[^p449-6], sicut esse aeternum et impassibilem; quaedam secundum *humanam pure,* sicut esse mortalem et passibilem; quaedam secundum *humanam, prout unita divinae,* vel e converso, ut esse mediatorem. Nam si tantum Deus, iam non mediator; si tantum homo, iam non mediator; si Deus et homo, iam mediator[^p449-7]. Et secundum hoc *vitis* est et *caput* Ecclesiae secundum quod mediator. Ideo quod dicitur *vitis,* hoc non est secundum *humanam naturam pure,* sed secundum quod coniuncta est divinae in unitate hypostasis. Et propter unitatem hypostasis Christus est obiectum fidei secundum utramque naturam, unde in quantum credimus Deum et hominem[^p449-8]. Et quia sine hac fide implicita vel explicita nullus fuit salvus, humor gratiae ab hac vite in omnes Sanctos redundavit.

2. Et sic patet, quomodo fuit *vitis* respectu praecedentium et sequentium; unde[^p449-9] *et qui praeibant et qui sequebantur clamabant: Hosanna Filio David.* Unde optime signatus est Christus per *botrum,* qui allatus est de terra promissionis, Numerorum decimo tertio[^p449-10], quem afferebant in humeris, et qui praeibant portabant, sed non videbant; sequentes portabant et videbant.

16. Quaest. IV. Item quaerit Augustinus[^p449-11] de hoc quod dicit: *Vos mundi estis propter sermonem, quem locutus sum vobis;* quia sermo est exterius, munditia interius. Quomodo ergo potest exterior sermo interius mundare?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod hoc vel est propter *fidem,* vel propter *fidei Sacramentum:* propter *fidem,* quia[^p449-12] *fides ex auditu,* quae cor purificat; propter *fidei Sacramentum,* quod est baptismus. Unde Augustinus[^p449-13]: « In aqua verbum mundat; detrahe verbum, quid est aqua nisi aqua? Accedit verbum ad elementum, et fit Sacramentum ». Istud autem verbum fidei et Sacramenti, etsi sit exterius,
<!-- page 450 -->
tamen mundat, ut dicit Augustinus, « non quia dicitur, sed quia creditur; ad Romanos decimo: *Si confitearis in ore tuo,* ascendisse in caelum Dominum, *et in corde tuo credideris,* quia *Deus excitavit illum a mortuis, salvus eris* ».

17. Quaest. V. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Pater tollet omnem palmitem non ferentem fructum.*

Aut tollet de *vite,* aut tollet de *vita.* De *vite, non,* quia nullum Pater a Filio suo separat; aut si tollit, quomodo tollit de vite? quia qui non operatur bonum hoc ipso separatus est a vite, Christo, quia gratiam non habet, immo perdit. — Si de *vita;* istud falsum est, quia plus vivunt mali quam boni, ut videmus. — Item, bonos etiam tollit de vita corporali sicut malos.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod palmites a Christo trahunt non vitam *naturae,* sed vitam *gratiae* et vitam *gloriae.* Dicitur *auferri* vel separari palmes ab hac vite non per mortem *naturae,* sed per mortem *culpae,* vel *damnationis extremae.* Unde palmites infructuosi et qui in vacuum gratiam Dei recipiunt a vite separantur *merito suae culpae* iudicio divinae iustitiae; unde Apocalypsis tertio[^p450-1]: *Quia tepidus es, incipiam te evomere;* quia iusto iudicio gratiam non apponit eis Deus. *Iudicio damnationis extremae* tollentur et *in ignem mittentur,* quando tempus merendi praecluditur; unde dicit: *tollet,* non quia statim auferat, cum locum poenitendi tribuat[^p450-2].

18. Quaest. VI. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Sicut dilexit me Pater, et ego dilexi vos.*

**Contra** hoc est, quia dilectio Patris est naturalis; dilectio Filii ad nos est pure gratuita: ergo non videtur similitudo. Quaeritur ergo: pro quanto dicit: *Sicut dilexit me Pater* etc.?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod, sicut dicit Richardus de sancto Victore[^p450-3], in divinis est amor *pure gratuitus, debitus* et *ex utroque permixtus.* Amor Patris ad Filium est *pure gratuitus,* quia Pater dat Filio et nihil recipit. Amor vero Spiritus sancti ad Patrem et Filium est *pure debitus,* quia tantum recipit. Amor vero Filii ad Patrem est *debitus,* quia recipit; ad Spiritum sanctum *gratuitus,* quia dat. Quoniam igitur Filius dilexit nos *gratuito pure,* ideo amorem, quo diligit nos, comparat amori Patris ad se[^p450-4].

### Chapter XV.

#### Second, concerning constancy in true dilection three things are set down.

1. *I am the true vine* etc. In the preceding chapter the Lord exhorted his disciples to constancy of faith, in this one he exhorts them to a *proved and true dilection*[^p446-9]. And since true dilection first loves God, then both loves the friend in God and loves and tolerates the enemy on account of God; therefore he exhorts the disciples first to the *dilection of God;* second, to the *dilection of friends,* at[^p446-10]: *This is my precept, that you love* etc.; third, to the *dilection and toleration of enemies,* at: *If the world hate you, know ye* etc.

#### First he exhorts the disciples to the dilection of God.

He exhorts them first to the *dilection of God;* and because « he that abideth in charity, abideth in God[^p446-11] », he exhorts them to *abide in him,* and this indeed he does in this order. First is described the *place of the mansion;* second, the *fruit;* third, the *mode;* fourth, the *effect.*

2. (V. 1.). The *place of the mansion,* then, is expressed first as *Christ himself,* in whom the faithful ought to abide, as the branch abides in the vine. Therefore he says: *I am the true vine,* therefore *true,* because it produces true wine, of which Genesis, the next to last[^p446-12]: « He shall wash his robe in wine, and his garment in the blood of the grape, tying his foal to the vineyard, and his ass
<!-- page 447 -->
to the vine ». This vine is not uncultivated, therefore he says: *And my Father is the husbandman,* not only one who cultivates exteriorly, but one who gives increase interiorly; the first to the Corinthians, the third[^p447-1]: « I have planted, Apollo watered, but God gave the increase »; and a little after: « You are God's husbandry, you are God's building ». Therefore the Father is the true husbandman, because he exercises his work; hence he says:

3. (V. 2.). *Every branch in me, that beareth not fruit, he will take it away;* Matthew, the third[^p447-2]: « The axe is laid to the root of the tree; every tree that yieldeth not good fruit, shall be cut down, and cast into the fire ». *And every one that beareth fruit, he will purge it, that it may bring forth more fruit. He will purge,* through *penance;* hence the first of John, the first[^p447-3]: « If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just, to forgive us all our sins, and to cleanse us from all iniquity ». He purges also through *patience;* hence in the Psalm: « For thou, O God, hast proved us; thou hast tried us by fire, as silver is tried; thou hast brought us into a net » etc. In this vine, then, the *clean boughs* are best placed; therefore he exhorts the Apostles, now clean, that they abide in him; on account of which he says:

4. (V. 3.). *Now you are clean by reason of the word, which I have spoken to you.* This was the word of faith, which purifies those believing; the Acts, the fifteenth[^p447-4]: « By faith purifying their hearts ». Because, then, you are clean:

5. (V. 4.). *Abide in me,* as in the vine, *and I in you,* as in a temple; of this the first of John, the fourth[^p447-5]: « He that abideth in charity, abideth in God, and God in him ». Therefore the mansion is mutual, because the dilection is mutual; above, the fourteenth [so printed: *deeimo* for *decimo*]: « He that loveth me, shall be loved of my Father: and I will love him, and will manifest myself to him, and we will come to him, and will make our mansion with him ».

*As the branch* etc. He has touched the place of the mansion, here second he touches the *fruit of the mansion,* which the branch has, not without the vine, but in the vine. Therefore he shows that fruit from the branch has its origin from the vine, first *in the opposite,* namely through separation, second *in the proposed*[^p447-6], through the mansion. On account of which he says: *As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, unless it abide in the vine, so also*[^p447-7] *you, unless you abide in me.* And therefore, if they wish to bear fruit, it is necessary that they abide in him, whence the fruit has its origin; to the Ephesians, the first: « And hath made him head over all the Church, which is his body, and the fulness of him who hath filled all in all »; John, the first: « Of his fulness we all have received grace for grace ». Therefore you cannot bear fruit, unless you abide in me; but in me you can, because:

6. (V. 5.). *I am the vine, and*[^p447-8] *you the branches;* and therefore *he that abideth in me, the same beareth much fruit.* And therefore abide in me as in the origin of fruit; *for without me you can do nothing;* Isaias, the twenty-sixth[^p447-9]: « Thou hast wrought all our works in us, O Lord ». And that they can do nothing without him, he shows through a similitude:

7. (V. 6.). *If any one abide not in me, he shall be cast forth as a branch,* namely, separated from the vine. He is thrown outside the vineyard through separation from the unity of the Church; the Apocalypse, the last[^p447-10]: « Without are dogs, and sorcerers, and unchaste » etc. And he shall not bear fruit, hence *and he shall wither,* through the loss of the moisture of grace; *and they shall gather him up, and cast him into the fire, and he burneth,* because he shall burn through the infliction of penalty.

Hence four evils follow: first, that *he is thrown out;* Matthew, the fifteenth[^p447-11]: « Every plant which my heavenly Father hath not planted, shall be rooted up. — Second, *he shall wither;* Matthew, the twenty-first, the Lord said to the fig tree, on which he found nothing but leaves: « May no fruit grow on thee henceforward for ever. And immediately it withered away »; the Psalm: « My strength is dried up like a potsherd ». — Third, *he is gathered* through the ministry of angels; hence Matthew, the thirteenth[^p447-12]: « They shall gather out of his kingdom all scandals ». — Fourth, *he is burned with fire;* Ezechiel, the fifteenth: « Son of man, what shall be made of the wood of the vine » ? And there follows: « Behold it is cast into the fire for fuel ».

8. (V. 7.). *If you abide in me.* Above[^p447-13] he showed that he is the origin of fruit through the *opposite*
<!-- page 448 -->
*of the mansion;* here now he shows it through the *mansion itself,* which gives fruit both in respect of *oneself* and in respect of one's *neighbour.* In respect of *oneself: If you abide in me, and my words abide in you;* not as those of whom Luke, the eighth[^p448-1]: « They believe for a while, and in time of temptation fall away »; but as those of whom in the Psalm: « Who keep his covenant, and are mindful of his commandments to do them »; *you shall ask whatever you will, and it shall be done unto you;* above, the ninth: « If a man doth the will of God, him he heareth ». Not only is there fruit in respect of *oneself,* but also of one's *neighbour* to be converted to the glory of God; therefore he adds:

9. (V. 8.). *In this is my Father glorified; that you bring forth very much fruit;* namely, that men be converted by your word and example; Matthew, the fifth[^p448-2]: « So let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven »; *and* from this *become my disciples,* that is, imitators of me, who brought forth very much fruit through my death, as above, the twelfth[^p448-3]: « If the grain of wheat die, it bringeth forth much fruit ». So also the Apostles; hence the Church sings of them, that « they planted the Church with their blood »; to the Colossians, the first: « I fill up those things that are wanting of the sufferings of Christ ».

10. (V. 9.). *As the Father hath loved me.* Here third is touched the *mode of abiding,* which is through dilection, to which that he himself first loved ought to invite us; therefore he said: *As the Father hath loved me, I also have loved you;* and therefore *abide in my dilection;* the first of John, the fourth[^p448-4]: « Let us therefore love God, because he first hath loved us ». And because true dilection is manifested through work, he subjoins:

11. (V. 10.). *If you keep my commandments, you shall abide in my dilection; as I also have kept my Father's commandments, and do abide in his dilection;* above, the fourteenth[^p448-5]: « That the world may know, that I love the Father, and as the Father hath given me commandment, so do I ». So we too ought to do; the first of John, the second: « He that saith he abideth in him, ought himself also to walk, even as he walked »; Deuteronomy, the tenth: « And now, Israel, what doth the Lord thy God require of thee, but that thou fear the Lord thy God, and walk in his ways, and love him, and serve the Lord thy God, with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and keep the commandments of the Lord, and his ceremonies, which I command thee this day, that it may be well with thee » ?

12. (V. 11.). *These things I have spoken to you.* Here is touched the fourth, namely the *effect of the mansion;* and the effect is *joy in the present* and *joy in the future.* On account of *joy in the present* he says: *That my joy may be in you,* that is, pure joy, by which a man rejoices in the Lord, not in the world; of which to the Philippians, the last[^p448-6]: « Rejoice in the Lord always; again, I say, rejoice ». — On account of *joy in the future* he says: *And your joy may be filled,* of which below, the sixteenth[^p448-7]: « Ask, and you shall receive; that your joy may be full ». Our joy is filled by the joy of the Lord; of which Matthew, the twenty-fifth: « Enter thou into the joy of thy lord ».

### Questions.

13. Quaest. I. But it is asked here concerning this which he says: *I am the true vine.*

Augustine objects[^p448-8]: If *vine* is said of Christ, as *lion, stone* are said; and these are said not by property, but by similitude; and those things are called *true* which are said by property: it does not seem that he ought to say: *I am the true vine,* but the similitudinary one.

And Augustine responds[^p448-9] that by adding *true,* « he distinguishes himself from that one to which it is said: *How art thou turned into bitterness, O strange vine* » ? Jeremias, the second: *How art thou turned into that which is good for nothing, O strange vineyard?* Hence it is not called *true* because not by similitude; but *true* because not strange.

Yet it can be said that, as that is *true wine* which has the operation of wine; so that is a *true vine* which has the operation of a vine[^p448-10]. A *vine,* then, *to the letter* is *true* when it produces wine; *not true,* that which does not bear fruit. So also a *similitudinary* one is called *true* which has the act of a vine; *not true,* that which does not have it. So also *Christ,* because he has the true act of a vine, is a *true vine.* But the synagogue not, of which Isaias, the fifth[^p448-11]: « He looked that it should bring forth grapes, but it brought forth thorns ».
<!-- page 449 -->

14. Quaest. II. But then there remains the question: why does the Lord compare himself rather to the *grain of wheat* among grains, and to the *wood of the vine* among woods?

**I respond:** This is on account of the body, *true* and *mystical. True* body of Christ is true food, and true blood is true drink[^p449-1]. Because, then, from grains of wheat is made bread best refreshing, and from the vine wine best giving drink; therefore he compared himself to the vine and to the grain. And hence it is that under these two species the Sacrament of the altar is confected. — The other reason is on account of the *mystical* body, because, as from the purest grains of wheat and berries of the grape bread and wine are confected; so from the pure faithful the mystical body of Christ[^p449-2].

15. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked according to which nature Christ is the *vine,* whether according to the *divine,* or according to the *human.* — And that it is according to the *human,* Augustine says[^p449-3]: « Of one nature are the vine and the branches; on account of which, since he was God, of whose nature we are not, he was made man, that in him human nature might be the vine, of which we too could be the branches ».

*On the contrary* this is: 1. Because according as he is the vine he flows moisture into the branches: therefore if no one flows in the moisture of grace except God alone, and according as God[^p449-4], therefore he is the vine according to the *divine nature.*

2. Likewise, in this vine all the Saints from Abel to the last just man have abode and have proceeded[^p449-5]; but Christ according to the *human* nature came after Abel: therefore they were not branches in him according to the human nature.

**I respond:** It must be understood that some things are said of Christ according to the *divine* nature *purely,* so that they are said nonetheless, human nature being abstracted[^p449-6], as to be eternal and impassible; some according to the *human purely,* as to be mortal and passible; some according to the *human, as united to the divine,* or conversely, as to be mediator. For if only God, then not mediator; if only man, then not mediator; if God and man, then mediator[^p449-7]. And according to this he is *vine* and *head* of the Church according as he is mediator. Therefore what is called *vine,* this is not according to the *human nature purely,* but according as it is conjoined to the divine in unity of hypostasis. And on account of the unity of hypostasis Christ is the object of faith according to both natures, whence insofar as we believe God and man[^p449-8]. And because without this faith, implicit or explicit, no one has been saved, the moisture of grace has redounded from this vine into all the Saints.

2. And thus it is evident how he was the *vine* in respect of those preceding and following; hence[^p449-9] *and they that went before and they that followed, cried: Hosanna to the Son of David.* Hence Christ was best signified by the *cluster,* which was brought from the land of promise, Numbers, the thirteenth[^p449-10], which they brought upon their shoulders, and those who went before carried it, but did not see it; those following carried it and saw it.

16. Quaest. IV. Likewise Augustine asks[^p449-11] concerning this which he says: *You are clean by reason of the word, which I have spoken to you;* because the word is exteriorly, cleanness interiorly. How then can an exterior word cleanse interiorly?

**I respond:** It must be said that this is either on account of *faith,* or on account of the *Sacrament of faith:* on account of *faith,* because[^p449-12] *faith cometh by hearing,* which purifies the heart; on account of the *Sacrament of faith,* which is baptism. Hence Augustine[^p449-13]: « In water the word cleanses; take away the word, what is water but water? The word comes to the element, and it becomes a Sacrament ». But this word of faith and of the Sacrament, although it be exterior,
<!-- page 450 -->
yet cleanses, as Augustine says, « not because it is said, but because it is believed; to the Romans, the tenth: *If thou confess with thy mouth,* that the Lord has ascended into heaven, *and believe in thy heart,* that *God hath raised him up from the dead, thou shalt be saved* ».

17. Quaest. V. It is asked concerning this which he says: *The Father will take away every branch not bearing fruit.*

Either he will take away from the *vine,* or he will take away from *life.* From the *vine, no,* because the Father separates no one from his Son; or if he takes away, how does he take away from the vine? because he who does not work good is by that very fact separated from the vine, Christ, because he does not have grace, indeed loses it. — If from *life;* that is false, because more evil men live than good, as we see. — Likewise, he takes away the good too from bodily life, just as the evil.

**I respond:** It must be said that the branches draw from Christ not the life of *nature,* but the life of *grace* and the life of *glory.* A branch is said *to be removed* or separated from this vine not through the death of *nature,* but through the death of *fault,* or of *final damnation.* Hence unfruitful branches and those who receive the grace of God in vain are separated from the vine *by the merit of their fault* by the judgement of divine justice; hence the Apocalypse, the third[^p450-1]: *Because thou art lukewarm, I will begin to vomit thee out;* because by just judgement God does not apply grace to them. *By the judgement of final damnation* they will be taken away and *cast into the fire,* when the time of meriting is closed; hence he says: *he will take away,* not because he removes at once, since he grants a place for penance[^p450-2].

18. Quaest. VI. Likewise it is asked concerning this which he says: *As the Father hath loved me, I also have loved you.*

**On the contrary** this is, because the dilection of the Father is natural; the dilection of the Son toward us is purely gratuitous: therefore there does not seem to be a likeness. It is asked, then: in what respect does he say: *As the Father hath loved me* etc.?

**I respond:** It must be said that, as Richard of Saint Victor says[^p450-3], in divine things there is love *purely gratuitous, due* and *mixed of both.* The love of the Father toward the Son is *purely gratuitous,* because the Father gives to the Son and receives nothing. But the love of the Holy Spirit toward the Father and the Son is *purely due,* because he only receives. But the love of the Son toward the Father is *due,* because he receives; toward the Holy Spirit *gratuitous,* because he gives. Since, then, the Son has loved us *purely gratuitously,* therefore the love by which he loves us he compares to the love of the Father toward himself[^p450-4].
