# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Prooemium
## *Prooemium · Quaestiones I–III*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 239 -->

### Prooemium commentarii in Ioannem[^p239-1].

1. *Ecce, intelliget servus meus, et exaltabitur et elevabitur et sublimis erit valde.* Quia commendatio auctoris redundat in opus, et commendatio operis in auctorem; ideo verbum propositum, quod scribitur Isaiae quinquagesimo secundo[^p239-2], assumtum est, in quo beatus Ioannes evangelista commendatur a tribus, scilicet a *sanctitate vitae*, cum dicitur: *Ecce, servus meus*; a *claritate intelligentiae*, cum subditur: *intelliget*; ab *excellentia doctrinae*, cum subinfertur: *et exaltabitur et elevabitur et sublimis erit valde.* Nam is est ordo; sanctitas enim vitae meretur donum intelligentiae, et donum intelligentiae disponit ad excellentiam doctrinae.

2. Commendatur igitur a *sanctitate vitae* in hoc, quod Dominus vocat eum *servum suum*; non enim merentur dici *servi Dei*, nisi qui in sanctitate illi serviunt omnibus diebus. Servum autem *suum* vocat eum Dominus: *ratione obsequii*, quia ei fideliter est obsecutus; unde recte de eo potest intelligi illud Numerorum duodecimo[^p239-3]: *Servus meus Moyses in omni domo mea fidelissimus est.* Recte beatus Ioannes in *omni domo* Christi fuit *fidelissimus*; in cuius rei testimonium illum nobilissimum thesaurum, scilicet Matris virginitatem, Dominus ei servandum commisit[^p239-4]. — *Ratione officii*, quod sibi a Deo fuit impositum, et ad quod praeordinatus fuit ab aeterno, secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo nono[^p239-5]: *Dominus ab utero vocavit me, de ventre matris meae recordatus est nominis mei*; ecce, praedestinatio. *Et posuit os meum quasi gladium acutum, in umbra manus suae protexit me, et posuit me sicut sagittam electam, in pharetra sua abscondit me*; ecce, officii praedicationis commissio. *Et dixit mihi: Servus meus es tu, Israel, quia in te gloriabor*;
<!-- page 240 -->
ecce, servitutis acceptatio[^p240-1], quia non suscepit officium illud ad gloriam suam, sed ad divinam. — *Ratione beneficii*, quod sibi Dominus contulit, quia a Deo electus et praeelectus et dilectus et speciali munere gratiae ditatus fuit, secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo secundo[^p240-2]: *Ecce, servus meus, suscipiam eum; electus meus, complacuit sibi in illo anima mea, dedi spiritum meum super eum.* Recte complacuit sibi in beato Ioanne anima Domini; nam hic est *discipulus ille, quem diligebat Iesus*, Ioannis ultimo. — Sic igitur *servus Christi* fuit beatus Ioannes propter *obsequium*, *officium* et *beneficium.*

3. Ob hanc autem sanctitatem vitae meruit *donum intelligentiae*, quo multipliciter fuit ditatus. Intellexit enim Spiritus sancti *unctione*, angelica *revelatione*, Christi *eruditione.* Spiritus sancti unctione intellexit *utilia*; angelica revelatione intellexit *abscondita*; Christi eruditione *sublimia*; et in his tribus[^p240-3] omnis appetitus et desiderium nostrae intelligentiae consummatur et perficitur.

Intellexit igitur Spiritus sancti unctione *utilia*, quia ille Spiritus veritatis est spiritus salutis; de quo intellectu Isaiae vigesimo octavo[^p240-4]: *Quem docebit scientiam? Et quem intelligere faciet auditum? Ablactatos a lacte, avulsos ab uberibus.* Ablactati et semoti a dulcedine praesentis consolationis merentur intelligere utilia, alii vero non. Unde primae ad Corinthios secundo: *Animalis homo non percipit ea quae spiritus Dei sunt; stultitia enim est illi, et non potest intelligere quae spiritualiter examinantur.* Beatus vero Ioannes *ablactatus* fuit, quia patrem et matrem et etiam sponsam, quam duxerat, amore Dei dereliquit[^p240-5].

Intellexit angelica revelatione *abscondita*, ut quae ventura erant; unde recte convenit ei quod dicitur Danielis primo[^p240-6] de ipso Daniele: *Dedit Dominus Danieli intelligentiam omnium visionum et somniorum*; et hoc quidem ministerio revelationis angelicae; unde Danielis octavo dixit unus Angelus alii: *Gabriel, fac istum intelligere visionem.* Sed hoc Angelus non faciebat *auctoritative*; in cuius rei testimonium, dum Ioannes vellet adorare Angelum, qui sibi illa ostendebat, prohibuit eum dicens: *Vide, ne feceris; conservus enim tuus sum et fratrum tuorum*, Apocalypsis decimo nono[^p240-7]; in quo manifestatur, quod non revelabat ut dominus, sed ut conservus.

Intellexit etiam Christi eruditione *sublimia.* Etsi enim Christus omnes suos discipulos edoceret, tamen beatum Ioannem, quem prae ceteris diligebat, prae ceteris erudivit et intelligere fecit. Unde recte potest de eo intelligi quod dixit Dominus Salomoni tertii Regum tertio[^p240-8]: *Dedi tibi cor sapiens et intelligens in tantum, ut ante te nullus similis tui fuerit nec post te surrecturus sit.* Nullus enim ante eum fuit nec post eum secutus est, qui ad tantam Divinitatis cognitionem ascenderet.

4. Sic igitur intellexit beatus Ioannes *utilia* et *abscondita* et *sublimia*; et ratione huius triplicis intelligentiae sublimatus fuit ad triplicem dignitatem doctrinae. Ideo dicitur *exaltabitur*, scilicet ad *cathedram apostolicam*; *elevabitur* ad *denuntiationem propheticam*; et *sublimis erit valde* per *doctrinam evangelicam.* Propter quod tria scripta condidit, scilicet Epistolas canonicas, tanquam *praedicator* authenticus; Apocalypsim, tanquam *propheta* eximius; et Evangelium, tanquam *scriba* doctissimus.

Primo igitur *exaltabitur* ad *cathedram apostolicam.* Propter quod recte convenit ei quod dicitur in Psalmo[^p240-9]: *Exaltent eum in ecclesia plebis, et in cathedra seniorum laudent eum. Cathedra seniorum* est cathedra Apostolorum, in qua beatus Ioannes non sedit ut otiosus, immo erudivit praesentes verbis et absentes epistolis; Ecclesiastici decimo quinto: *Firmabitur in illo, et non flectetur; et continebit illum, et non confundetur; et exaltabit illum apud proximos suos.*

*Elevabitur* ad *denuntiationem propheticam*, secundum illud Ezechielis octavo[^p240-10]: *Elevavit me Spiritus inter caelum et terram, et adduxit me in Ierusalem in visione Dei.* Hac elevatione beatus Ioannes elevatus est; unde Apocalypsis vigesimo primo: *Sustulit me in spiritu in montem magnum et altum et ostendit mihi civitatem sanctam Ierusalem descendentem de caelo.* Et hoc quidem vidit ad denuntiandum; unde dictum est ei Apocalypsis primo[^p240-11]: *Scribe quae vidisti et quae sunt et quae oportet fieri post haec*; quia floruit triplici genere prophetiae.
<!-- page 241 -->

*Et sublimis erit valde* per *doctrinam evangelicam*, in qua omnes alios antecellit. Ideo recte exponi potest de eo quod dicitur Genesis septimo[^p241-1]: *Multiplicatae sunt aquae et elevaverunt arcam in sublime a terra. Arca* ista beatus Ioannes, in quo salvatum est semen verbi divini. *Aquae* illae *multiplicatae* sunt aquae sapientiae salutaris, quae multiplicatae fuerunt in beato Ioanne, quia, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p241-2], « fluenta Evangelii de ipso dominici pectoris fonte potavit ». Istae aquae sapientiae ipsum *elevaverunt in sublime*, super omnes montes, quia in Evangelio altius omnibus est locutus. Unde dicit Augustinus[^p241-3]: « Si altius intonuisset, totus mundus capere non potuisset ». Sed quia doctoris sublimitas manifestatur in sublimitate doctrinae, recte videtur *sublimis valde*, si Evangelii eius sublimitas explicetur.

5. Intelligendum est igitur, quod aliqua doctrina dicitur *sublimis*, aut quia de sublimibus *agit*, aut quia sublimiter *procedit*, aut quia ad sublimia *perducit.* — Evangelium beati Ioannis sublime est, quia de sublimibus *agit*, quia de Verbo incarnato secundum utramque naturam, quae valde sublimes sunt, maxime divina. Quod bene exprimitur parabolice Ezechielis decimo septimo[^p241-4]: *Haec dicit Dominus Deus: Ego sumam de medulla cedri sublimis, et plantabo super montem excelsum et eminentem, in monte sublimi Israel. Medulla cedri sublimis* est latens Divinitas Verbi. Hanc plantavit Deus Pater *in monte sublimi Israel*, quia humanae naturae in Christo univit, qui est *lapis abscissus de monte sine manibus*[^p241-5]. De hac medulla cedri sublimis est Evangelium beati Ioannis; unde in eodem capitulo dicitur: *Aquila grandis magnarum alarum, longo membrorum ductu, plena plumis et varietate, venit ad Libanum et tulit medullam cedri. Summitatem frondium eius avulsit et transportavit eam in terram Chanaan. Aquila grandis*, per caritatis latitudinem; *magnarum alarum*, per contemplationis altitudinem; *longo membrorum ductu*, per exspectationis longitudinem; *plena plumis et varietate*, per virtutum multitudinem; *venit ad Libanum*, id est ad montem montium Christum[^p241-6]; *tulit medullam cedri*, id est latentem Divinitatem Verbi; et *summitatem frondium avulsit*, quia operum divinorum excellentiam descripsit, sicut nobilissima miracula et altissima documenta, quae alii Evangelistae reliquerunt intacta. Si igitur *Libanus* est mons altus in mundo, et *cedrus* arbor alta in Libano, et *summitas frondium* altissima in cedro; utique sequitur, quod qui *summitatem frondium cedri avulsit* de sublimibus tractavit.

6. Non tantum de sublimibus *agit*, sed etiam sublimiter *procedit.* Modus procedendi sublimis est modus certitudinalis. Illae scientiae dicuntur sublimiores, quae certiores. Iste modus procedendi sublimis describitur Ezechielis primo[^p241-7] in figura animalium, per quae Evangelistae intelliguntur: *Audiebam*, inquit, *sonum alarum quasi sonum aquarum multarum, quasi sonum sublimis Dei. Sonus sublimis Dei* est certitudinalis, non variabilis, quia *non est Deus ut homo, ut mentiatur*[^p241-8]. Ideo Dominus dicebat de se Ioannis tertio: *Quod scimus loquimur et quod vidimus testamur.* Hoc modo certitudinali loquebatur beatus Ioannes; unde Ioannis decimo nono: *Qui vidit testimonium perhibuit, et testimonium eius verum est; et ille scit, quia vera dicit.* Unde ipse dicit in prima canonica sua, primo capitulo in principio: *Quod fuit ab initio, quod audivimus, quod vidimus oculis nostris, quod perspeximus, et manus nostrae contrectaverunt de Verbo vitae, testamur et annuntiamus vobis.*

7. Non solum sublimiter *procedit*, sed etiam ad sublimia *perducit.* Sublimia sunt bona aeterna; nam bona temporalia infima sunt et momentanea; unde secundae ad Corinthios quarto[^p241-9]: *Id quod est in praesenti momentaneum et leve tribulationis nostrae, supra modum in sublimitate aeternum gloriae pondus operatur in nobis.* Ad hoc *sublime pondus gloriae* perducit doctrina Evangelii
<!-- page 242 -->
secundum Ioannem. Unde Ioannis vigesimo[^p242-1]: *Haec scripta sunt, ut credatis, quia Iesus est Filius Dei, et ut credentes vitam habeatis in nomine eius*; illam scilicet vitam aeternam et sublimem, quam *nec oculus vidit, nec auris audivit, nec in cor hominis ascendit.*

Sic igitur doctrina huius Evangelii est sublimis; propterea non immerito et eius auctor dicitur esse sublimis, nec tantum sublimis, sed etiam *sublimis valde*, in superlativo gradu. Si enim divina Scriptura scripturas ceteras antecellit, et Evangelium omnes partes divinae Scripturae[^p242-2], et Evangelium Ioannis omnia Evangelia; consequens est, quod liber iste singulariter libros ceteros antecellat.

8. Ex dictis patent quae in principio cuiuslibet libri consueverunt quaeri, scilicet causa *efficiens*, Ioannes evangelista, sanctus, intelligens, exaltatus et elevatus et sublimior ceteris effectus; causa *materialis*, Verbum incarnatum, maxime quantum ad ea quae Divinitatis sunt; causa *formalis* sive *modus agendi*, etsi sit per modum narrationis, est tamen certitudinalis; causa vero formalis, quae est *divisio libri*, infra[^p242-3] manifestabitur, quia ordinate valde procedit; causa vero *finalis* proxima est fides, ultima vero est vita aeterna, quae est finis desideratissimus.

### Quaestiones.

9. Sed quantum ad haec tria, in quibus est haec doctrina commendata, scilicet in *utilitate*, *certitudine* et *sublimitate*, tria quaeruntur.

Quaest. I. Quaeritur ergo: quae necessitas fuit scribendi hoc Evangelium? Et videtur, quod nulla:

1. Quia Gregorius super Ezechielem dicit super illud[^p242-4]: *Quatuor facies uni*: « Si quaeris, quid Ioannes sentiat; hoc quod Lucas, Marcus et Matthaeus ». Ergo secundum hoc Ioannes nihil fecit, nisi quod replicavit hoc quod alii Evangelistae dixerant: ergo si non scripsisset, nihil deesset.

2. Item, aut Matthaeus sufficienter scripsit Evangelium, aut non; si non: ergo cum ipse a se non scripserit, sed a Spiritu sancto, Spiritus sanctus fuit insufficiens. Quodsi Matthaeus sufficienter tradidit evangelicam doctrinam: ergo superfluunt omnia alia Evangelia, tam Ioannis quam alia.

3. Item, si necesse fuit Ecclesiae, ut quid tantum tardavit, distulit et retinuit verbum tempore salutis[^p242-5]? Ergo videtur, quod negligenter fecerit et peccaverit, quod tantum distulit.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Evangelistae sunt testes Domini, et hoc non propter indigentiam claritatis divinae, sed infirmitatis humanae. Quia ergo nostra infirmitas firmius et certius credit pluribus quam uni; ideo disposuit divina sapientia, ut plures essent Evangelistae. — Si vero[^p242-6] omnino diversa dicerent, fidem non firmarent; necesse est enim, ut testimonium sit solidum, testes concordare. Si vero omnino idem dicerent, iam certe totum unum esset et superfluum. Ut ergo certum esset testimonium et nihil contineret superfluum, disposuit Spiritus sanctus, ut aliqua *singulariter* narrarent, aliqua *communiter*, nulla vero *discorditer.*

1. Quod obiicitur, quod sensit unus, et omnis; intelligendum, non quia omnino idem dixerint, sed ubi concurrerunt in idem narrandum, non discordant, sicut dicit Augustinus de Concordia Evangelistarum[^p242-7]. — Vel facienda est vis in hoc, quod dicit *sentiunt*; quia idem sentiunt tanquam uno spiritu et fide illuminati; sed tamen non omnino eadem scribunt.

2. Quod quaeritur, utrum Matthaeus sufficienter dixerit; dicendum ad hoc, quod *sufficienter de aliquo tractare*, hoc est dupliciter: aut quantum exigit *materiae dignitas*; et sic de Christo sufficienter tractare est impossibile, non tantum uni homini, sed etiam toti mundo, si converteretur in linguas, quia ad eius narrationem deficiunt omnes linguae. Unde Ioannis ultimo[^p242-8]: *Quodsi scribantur per singula quae fecit Iesus, nec ipsum arbitror mundum posse capere eos qui scribendi sunt libros.* Alio modo est *sufficienter tractare*, quantum sufficit ad *deveniendum in finem*; et sic in quolibet Evangelistarum substantia fidei, quantum sufficit, continetur. Sed tamen alii non superfluunt, quia faciunt ad fidei claritatem, explicationem, maiorem certitudinem et defensionem.

3. Quod obiicitur: quare tantum distulit? dicendum, quod ratio fuit ex parte *scribentis*; quia erat virgo et iuvenis; ideo ut verecundus se ingerere noluit, quousque ab episcopis Asiae rogaretur et
<!-- page 243 -->
ab haereticis excitaretur. Unde in Prologo[^p243-1] dicitur, quod ultimo scripsit Evangelium, et hoc virgini debebatur. — Alia ratio fuit ex parte *Scripturae*, quia alii sunt versati circa humanitatem et circa vitam activam, beatus Ioannes circa Divinitatem et vitam contemplativam, sicut dicit Augustinus in Glossa[^p243-2]. Quia ergo per visibilia venitur ad invisibilia, et per activam ad contemplativam; debuerunt primo alii scribere, ultimo vero Ioannes. — Tertia ratio fuit ex parte *dispensationis divinae*; quia enim Ioannes ceteris melius servaverat Evangelium, si ipse primo scripsisset, aut totum scripsisset et aliis locum scribendi abstulisset; aut si non totum, alii ipsum supplere non possent. Ideo permisit instinctu Spiritus sancti, alios prius scribere, ut quae alii omiserant, postmodum ipse narraret[^p243-3]. — Graeci autem reddunt aliam causam, ut alios corrigeret; sed mentiuntur, quia, si eodem Spiritu Evangelistae locuti sunt, impossibile fuit, eos dicere falsum; ideo aliquid fuit addendum, sed nihil corrigendum.

10. Quaest. II. Secundo quaeritur de huius libri certitudine, et videtur, quod male procedat.

1. Si enim modus narrationis est debilis et infirmus, quia quis[^p243-4] potest contradicere, nec habet unde refellatur; et Ioannes modo narratorio procedit: ergo valde debiliter et infirme.

2. Item, quod debeat certitudinaliter procedere, videtur: quia « nihil est certius homini sua fide[^p243-5] »: ergo nulla cognitio vel doctrina certius debet tradi ea quae generat fidem. Sed doctrina Ioannis est ad generandum fidem, sicut dicitur Ioannis vigesimo: ergo debet esse certissima et certitudinaliter procedere.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod quadruplex est certitudo, scilicet *demonstrationis*, *auctoritatis*, *internae illuminationis* et *exterioris persuasionis.* Prima certitudo est, cui resisti non potest; in illa cogitur homo assentire, velit nolit; et ideo talis certitudo, ubi dominatur, evacuat fidem. Alia est certitudo *auctoritatis*, et haec fidem generat; unde Augustinus[^p243-6]: « Quod credimus debemus auctoritati ». Tertia est certitudo *illuminationis interioris*, et haec fidem perficit et consummat. Quarta est *persuasionis*, quando homo fidelis rationes assignat congruentiae et efficaciae, ut quod credit intelligat; et haec certitudo fidem consequitur. Nam rationes huiusmodi fideli sunt optimae et efficaces, infideli vero prorsus inutiles et infirmae[^p243-7].

1. 2. Quod ergo quaeritur de certitudine, dicendum, quod « quia finis imponit necessitatem his quae sunt ad finem[^p243-8] »; cum haec doctrina sit ad generandam fidem, non debuit habere certitudinem *demonstrativam*; non potuit habere certitudinem *internam*, quia illa est gratiae; non debuit habere *persuasoriam*: ideo oportuit, quod haberet certitudinem *auctoritatis.* Et haec est ratio, quare omnes libri sacrae Scripturae traduntur per modum narrationis, non ratiocinationis, quia sunt ad generandam *fidem*, quae est per assensum liberum. Libri autem doctorum traduntur per ratiocinationes, quia sunt ad generandam *intelligentiam* et ad fidem confovendam et nutriendam. Sed rationes illae non habent robur, nisi fundatae super fidem[^p243-9].

Dicendum est ergo, quod certitudo auctoritatis in aliis scientiis inventis ab hominibus, qui mentiuntur saepe, parvum habet robur; sed in sacra Scriptura, quae a Spiritu sancto data est, magnam habet firmitatem, quia ipse mentiri non potest. Unde Augustinus super Genesim[^p243-10]: « Maior est huius Scripturae auctoritas quam omnis humani ingenii perspicacitas ».

11. Quaest. III. Quaeritur de sublimitate perscrutationis et sermonis. Et videtur, quod male agat beatus Ioannes, qui[^p243-11] tam sublimia perscrutatur:
<!-- page 244 -->

1. Quia Ecclesiastici tertio[^p244-1]: *Altiora te ne quaesieris, et fortiora te ne scrutatus fueris*; et iterum Proverbiorum vigesimo quinto: *Perscrutator maiestatis opprimetur a gloria.* Quid maius quam aeterna Verbi generatio? — *Si dicas*, quod ipse non perscrutatur, sed narrat; *contra*: Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p244-2]: *Generationem eius quis enarrabit?* Et Hieronymus exponit de generatione aeterna.

2. Item Apostolus primae ad Corinthios secundo[^p244-3] commendat se de hoc, quod non venit ad Corinthios *in sublimitate sermonis*, sed in humilitate. Ergo videtur, quod beatus Ioannes reprehensibilis sit, quod tam alte intonuit, ita quod altius non posset.

**Respondeo:** Intelligendum, quod perscrutatio maiestatis et sublimium potest esse tripliciter, scilicet in spiritu diabolico, humano et divino. Prima est *malignitatis*, et fuit in haereticis; secunda vero *curiositatis*, et fuit in philosophis; tertia vero *pietatis*, et fuit in Apostolis et Prophetis. Prima est omnino reprehendenda, secunda est cavenda, sed tertia amplectenda; quia et prima et secunda frequenter ducunt in errorem, sed tertia non, quia Spiritus *scrutatur profunda Dei*, primae ad Corinthios secundo[^p244-4]. Et quia Ioannes Spiritu divino afflatus fuit, ideo in caelestium perscrutatione et descriptione reprehendi non potest; et ideo Spiritus sanctus generationem aeternam enarravit, non homo.

2. Quod quaeritur de sublimitate sermonis, dicendum, quod *sublimitas sermonis* aliquando reprehenditur propter *pravam intentionem*, ut cum homo loquitur sublimia, ut gloriosus appareat; et sic prohibetur primi Regum secundo[^p244-5]: *Nolite multiplicare loqui sublimia gloriantes.* — Aliquando reprehenditur propter *materiam*, ut quando materia levis et modica est, non est verbis altis aggravanda et obscuranda; de quo ad Colossenses secundo[^p244-6]: *Hoc autem dico, ut nemo vos decipiat in sublimitate sermonum*; et post: *Videte, ne quis vos decipiat per philosophiam et inanem fallaciam.* — Aliquando reprehenditur, quia *audientes non sunt capaces*; propter hoc dicebat Apostolus primae ad Corinthios secundo[^p244-7]: *Non veni ad vos in sublimitate sermonis*; et ratio erat, quia non poterant intelligere. Econtra dicit Hieronymus de quibusdam: « Alii adducto supercilio grandia verba trutinantes, inter mulierculas de sacris Litteris philosophantur ». — Beatus vero Ioannes et *materiam* habuit altam et *intentionem* bonam et audientes provectos, quia iam Ecclesia creverat; quare recte et debite sublimiter locutus est. Tamen hoc est mirabile, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p244-8], quod in sermone suo invenit parvulus lac, quod sugat, invenit etiam perfectus solidum cibum, qui eum reficiat. Et verificatur illud Gregorii, quod ibi elephas natat, et agnus peditat. Et ideo parvuli, qui non possunt natare cum elephantibus, peditare debent cum agnis.

### Prooemium of the commentary on John[^p239-1].

1. *Behold, my servant shall understand, he shall be exalted and extolled and shall be exceeding high.* Since the commendation of the author redounds upon the work, and the commendation of the work upon the author; therefore the proposed word, which is written in Isaias, the fifty-second chapter[^p239-2], has been taken up, in which blessed John the Evangelist is commended from three things, namely from the *sanctity of his life*, when it is said: *Behold, my servant*; from the *clarity of his intelligence*, when it is added: *shall understand*; from the *excellence of his teaching*[^tr-doctrina], when it is further subjoined: *and he shall be exalted and extolled and shall be exceeding high.* For this is the order; for sanctity of life merits the gift of intelligence, and the gift of intelligence disposes to excellence of teaching.

2. He is commended, then, from the *sanctity of his life* in this, that the Lord calls him *his servant*; for none deserve to be called *servants of God* save those who serve him in sanctity all their days. And the Lord calls him *his* servant: *by reason of obedience*, because he obeyed him faithfully; whence that word of Numbers, the twelfth chapter[^p239-3], can rightly be understood of him: *My servant Moses, who is most faithful in all my house.* Rightly was blessed John *most faithful* in *all the house* of Christ; in testimony of which thing the Lord committed to him that most noble treasure, namely the virginity of his Mother, to be kept[^p239-4]. — *By reason of office*, which was laid upon him by God, and to which he was preordained from eternity, according to that word of Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter[^p239-5]: *The Lord hath called me from the womb, from the bowels of my mother he hath been mindful of my name*; behold, predestination. *And he hath made my mouth like a sharp sword, in the shadow of his hand he hath protected me, and hath made me as a chosen arrow, in his quiver he hath hidden me*; behold, the committing of the office of preaching. *And he said to me: Thou art my servant, Israel, for in thee will I glory*;
<!-- page 240 -->
behold, the acceptance of servitude[^p240-1], because he did not take up that office for his own glory, but for the divine. — *By reason of benefit*, which the Lord conferred upon him, because he was chosen and pre-chosen and loved by God and enriched with a special gift of grace, according to that word of Isaias, the forty-second chapter[^p240-2]: *Behold my servant, I will uphold him; my elect, my soul delighteth in him, I have given my spirit upon him.* Rightly did the soul of the Lord delight in blessed John; for this is *that disciple whom Jesus loved*, John, the last chapter. — Thus, then, blessed John was the *servant of Christ* on account of *obedience*, *office* and *benefit.*

3. And on account of this sanctity of life he merited the *gift of intelligence*, with which he was enriched in many ways. For he understood by the *anointing* of the Holy Spirit, by angelic *revelation*, by the *instruction* of Christ. By the anointing of the Holy Spirit he understood *useful things*; by angelic revelation he understood *hidden things*; by the instruction of Christ *sublime things*; and in these three[^p240-3] every appetite and desire of our intelligence is consummated and perfected.

He understood, then, by the anointing of the Holy Spirit *useful things*, because that Spirit of truth is the spirit of salvation; of which understanding Isaias, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p240-4]: *Whom shall he teach knowledge? And whom shall he make to understand the hearing? Them that are weaned from the milk, that are drawn away from the breasts.* Those who are weaned and set apart from the sweetness of present consolation merit to understand useful things, but others not. Whence the first [epistle] to the Corinthians, the second chapter: *The sensual man perceiveth not these things that are of the Spirit of God; for it is foolishness to him, and he cannot understand, because they are spiritually examined.* But blessed John was *weaned*, because he left father and mother and even the bride whom he had wedded, for the love of God[^p240-5].

He understood by angelic revelation *hidden things*, as the things that were to come; whence there rightly agrees with him what is said in Daniel, the first chapter[^p240-6], of Daniel himself: *The Lord gave to Daniel understanding of all visions and dreams*; and this indeed by the ministry of angelic revelation; whence in Daniel, the eighth chapter, one Angel said to another: *Gabriel, make this man to understand the vision.* But the Angel did not do this *with authority*; in testimony of which, when John wished to adore the Angel who was showing him those things, he forbade him, saying: *See thou do it not; for I am thy fellow servant and of thy brethren*, Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter[^p240-7]; in which it is made manifest that he did not reveal as a lord, but as a fellow servant.

He understood also by the instruction of Christ *sublime things.* For although Christ taught all his disciples, yet blessed John, whom he loved beyond the rest, he instructed beyond the rest and made to understand. Whence what the Lord said to Solomon in Third Kings, the third chapter[^p240-8], can rightly be understood of him: *I have given thee a wise and understanding heart, insomuch that there hath been no one like thee before thee, nor shall arise after thee.* For no one was before him nor followed after him who ascended to so great a cognition of the Divinity.

4. Thus, then, blessed John understood *useful things* and *hidden things* and *sublime things*; and by reason of this threefold intelligence he was raised up to a threefold dignity of teaching. Therefore it is said *he shall be exalted*, namely to the *apostolic chair*; *he shall be extolled* to *prophetic announcement*; and *he shall be exceeding high* through *evangelical teaching.* On account of which he composed three writings, namely the canonical Epistles, as an authentic *preacher*; the Apocalypse, as an eminent *prophet*; and the Gospel, as a most learned *scribe.*

First, then, *he shall be exalted* to the *apostolic chair.* On account of which there rightly agrees with him what is said in the Psalm[^p240-9]: *Let them exalt him in the church of the people, and praise him in the chair of the ancients. The chair of the ancients* is the chair of the Apostles, in which blessed John did not sit as one idle, but instructed those present by words and those absent by letters; Ecclesiasticus, the fifteenth chapter: *He shall be made strong in her, and shall not be moved; and she shall hold him fast, and he shall not be confounded; and she shall exalt him among his neighbours.*

*He shall be extolled* to *prophetic announcement*, according to that word of Ezechiel, the eighth chapter[^p240-10]: *The Spirit lifted me up between the earth and the heaven, and brought me in the vision of God into Jerusalem.* With this lifting up was blessed John lifted up; whence Apocalypse, the twenty-first chapter: *He took me up in spirit to a great and high mountain, and he showed me the holy city Jerusalem coming down out of heaven.* And this indeed he saw in order to announce it; whence it was said to him, Apocalypse, the first chapter[^p240-11]: *Write the things which thou hast seen, and the things which are, and the things which must be done hereafter*; because he flourished in a threefold kind of prophecy.
<!-- page 241 -->

*And he shall be exceeding high* through *evangelical teaching*, in which he surpasses all the others. Therefore what is said in Genesis, the seventh chapter[^p241-1], can rightly be expounded of him: *The waters increased and lifted up the ark on high from the earth. The ark* is blessed John, in whom the seed of the divine word was saved. *Those waters increased* are the waters of saving wisdom, which were increased in blessed John, because, as Augustine says[^p241-2], « he drank the streams of the Gospel from the very fountain of the Lord's breast ». These waters of wisdom *lifted* him *up on high*, above all the mountains, because in the Gospel he spoke more loftily than all. Whence Augustine says[^p241-3]: « Had he thundered more loftily, the whole world could not have grasped it ». But since the sublimity of a doctor is made manifest in the sublimity of his teaching, he is rightly seen to be *exceeding high* if the sublimity of his Gospel is unfolded.

5. It must be understood, then, that a teaching is called *sublime* either because it *treats* of sublime things, or because it *proceeds* sublimely, or because it *leads* to sublime things. — The Gospel of blessed John is sublime because it *treats* of sublime things, because it treats of the Word incarnate according to each nature, which are exceedingly sublime, above all the divine. Which is well expressed in a parable in Ezechiel, the seventeenth chapter[^p241-4]: *Thus saith the Lord God: I myself will take of the marrow of the sublime cedar, and will plant it on a mountain high and eminent, on the sublime mountain of Israel. The marrow of the sublime cedar* is the hidden Divinity of the Word. This God the Father planted *on the sublime mountain of Israel*, because he united it to human nature in Christ, who is *the stone cut out of a mountain without hands*[^p241-5]. Of this marrow of the sublime cedar is the Gospel of blessed John; whence in the same chapter it is said: *A large eagle with great wings, long-limbed, full of feathers and variety, came to Libanus and took away the marrow of the cedar. He cropped off the top of the twigs thereof and carried it away into the land of Chanaan. A large eagle*, through the breadth of charity; *with great wings*, through the height of contemplation; *long-limbed*, through the length of expectation; *full of feathers and variety*, through the multitude of the virtues; *came to Libanus*, that is, to Christ the mountain of mountains[^p241-6]; *took away the marrow of the cedar*, that is, the hidden Divinity of the Word; and *cropped off the top of the twigs*, because he described the excellence of the divine works, such as the most noble miracles and the most lofty lessons, which the other Evangelists left untouched. If, then, *Libanus* is a high mountain in the world, and the *cedar* a high tree on Libanus, and the *top of the twigs* the highest thing on the cedar; it certainly follows that he who *cropped off the top of the twigs of the cedar* treated of sublime things.

6. Not only does it *treat* of sublime things, but it also *proceeds* sublimely. The sublime manner of proceeding is the manner of certitude. Those sciences are called more sublime which are more certain. This sublime manner of proceeding is described in Ezechiel, the first chapter[^p241-7], in the figure of the living creatures, by which the Evangelists are understood: *I heard*, he says, *the noise of their wings, like the noise of many waters, as it were the noise of the sublime God. The noise of the sublime God* is certain, not variable, because *God is not as a man, that he should lie*[^p241-8]. Therefore the Lord said of himself, John, the third chapter: *We speak what we know, and we testify what we have seen.* In this manner of certitude blessed John spoke; whence John, the nineteenth chapter: *He that saw it hath given testimony, and his testimony is true; and he knoweth that he saith true.* Whence he himself says in his first canonical [epistle], in the first chapter, at the beginning: *That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled, of the Word of life, we testify and declare unto you.*

7. Not only does it proceed sublimely, but it also *leads* to sublime things. Sublime things are the eternal goods; for temporal goods are the lowest and momentary; whence the second [epistle] to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter[^p241-9]: *That which is at present momentary and light of our tribulation worketh for us above measure in sublimity an eternal weight of glory.* To this *sublime weight of glory* the teaching of the Gospel
<!-- page 242 -->
according to John leads. Whence John, the twentieth chapter[^p242-1]: *These are written, that you may believe that Jesus is the Son of God, and that believing you may have life in his name*; that is, that eternal and sublime life which *eye hath not seen, nor ear heard, neither hath it entered into the heart of man.*

Thus, then, the teaching of this Gospel is sublime; for this reason its author too is not undeservedly said to be sublime, and not only sublime, but even *exceeding high*, in the superlative degree. For if the divine Scripture surpasses the other scriptures, and the Gospel all the parts of the divine Scripture[^p242-2], and the Gospel of John all the Gospels; it follows that this book singularly surpasses the other books.

8. From what has been said, the things are plain which are wont to be asked at the beginning of any book, namely the *efficient* cause, John the Evangelist, holy, understanding, exalted and extolled and made more sublime than the rest; the *material* cause, the Word incarnate, above all as regards the things that belong to the Divinity; the *formal* cause or *manner of proceeding*, which, although it is by way of narration, is nevertheless certain; but the formal cause which is the *division of the book* will be made manifest below[^p242-3], because it proceeds in a very orderly way; and the *final* cause, the proximate one, is faith, but the ultimate is eternal life, which is the most desired end.

### Questions.

9. But as regards these three things in which this teaching has been commended, namely in *utility*, *certitude* and *sublimity*, three things are asked.

Quaest. I. It is asked, then: what necessity was there of writing this Gospel? And it seems that there was none:

1. Because Gregory on Ezechiel says, on the words[^p242-4] *One had four faces*: « If thou askest what John thinks; the same as Luke, Mark and Matthew ». Therefore according to this John did nothing but repeat what the other Evangelists had said: therefore had he not written, nothing would be lacking.

2. Likewise, either Matthew wrote the Gospel sufficiently, or not; if not: then, since he did not write of himself but from the Holy Spirit, the Holy Spirit was insufficient. But if Matthew handed down the evangelical teaching sufficiently: then all the other Gospels are superfluous, John's as much as the others.

3. Likewise, if it was necessary to the Church, why did he so long delay, put off and hold back the word in the time of salvation[^p242-5]? Therefore it seems that he acted negligently and sinned, in that he put it off so long.

**I respond:** It must be said that the Evangelists are witnesses of the Lord, and this not on account of a want of the divine clarity, but of human infirmity. Since, then, our infirmity believes more firmly and more certainly several than one; therefore the divine wisdom disposed that there should be several Evangelists. — But[^p242-6] if they said things altogether different, they would not confirm the faith; for it is necessary, for testimony to be solid, that the witnesses agree. But if they said altogether the same, then certainly the whole would be one and superfluous. In order, then, that the testimony might be certain and contain nothing superfluous, the Holy Spirit disposed that they should narrate some things *singly*, some things *in common*, but nothing *discordantly.*

1. As to the objection that one thought the same as all; it must be understood, not that they said altogether the same, but that where they concurred in narrating the same thing, they do not disagree, as Augustine says in the *Concord of the Evangelists*[^p242-7]. — Or force must be laid on this, that he says *they think*; for they think the same as illumined by one spirit and faith; but nevertheless they do not write altogether the same things.

2. As to the question whether Matthew spoke sufficiently; it must be said to this, that *to treat sufficiently of something* is twofold: either as much as the *dignity of the matter* requires; and so to treat sufficiently of Christ is impossible, not only for one man, but even for the whole world, were it turned into tongues, because for his narration all tongues fall short. Whence John, the last chapter[^p242-8]: *If they were written every one of the things which Jesus did, the world itself, I think, would not be able to contain the books that should be written.* In another way *to treat sufficiently* is as much as suffices *for arriving at the end*; and so in each of the Evangelists the substance of the faith is contained, as much as suffices. But nevertheless the others are not superfluous, because they make for the clarity of the faith, its unfolding, its greater certitude and its defence.

3. As to the objection: why did he put it off so long? it must be said that the reason was on the part of *the writer*; because he was a virgin and young; therefore out of modesty he would not put himself forward until he was asked by the bishops of Asia and
<!-- page 243 -->
stirred up by the heretics. Whence it is said in the Prologue[^p243-1] that he wrote the Gospel last, and this was owed to a virgin. — Another reason was on the part of *the Scripture*, because the others were occupied with the humanity and with the active life, blessed John with the Divinity and the contemplative life, as Augustine says in the Gloss[^p243-2]. Since, then, one comes through visible things to invisible, and through the active to the contemplative; the others ought to write first, and John last. — The third reason was on the part of *the divine dispensation*; for since John had kept the Gospel better than the rest, had he written first, either he would have written the whole and taken away from the others the room for writing; or, if not the whole, the others could not have supplied for him. Therefore he allowed, by the prompting of the Holy Spirit, the others to write first, so that what the others had omitted he himself might afterwards narrate[^p243-3]. — The Greeks, however, give another cause, that he might correct the others; but they lie, because, if the Evangelists spoke by the same Spirit, it was impossible that they should say what is false; therefore something was to be added, but nothing to be corrected.

10. Quaest. II. Secondly it is asked concerning the certitude of this book, and it seems that it proceeds badly.

1. For the manner of narration is weak and infirm, because anyone[^p243-4] can contradict it, and it has nothing whereby it may be refuted; and John proceeds in the narrative manner: therefore very weakly and infirmly.

2. Likewise, that it ought to proceed with certitude seems so: because « nothing is more certain to a man than his faith[^p243-5] »: therefore no cognition or teaching ought to be handed down more certainly than that which generates faith. But the teaching of John is for generating faith, as is said in John, the twentieth chapter: therefore it ought to be most certain and to proceed with certitude.

**I respond:** It must be said that certitude is fourfold, namely of *demonstration*, of *authority*, of *internal illumination* and of *exterior persuasion.* The first certitude is that which cannot be resisted; in it a man is compelled to assent, willing or unwilling; and therefore such certitude, where it holds sway, empties faith. Another is the certitude of *authority*, and this generates faith; whence Augustine[^p243-6]: « What we believe we owe to authority ». The third is the certitude of *interior illumination*, and this perfects and consummates faith. The fourth is of *persuasion*, when a faithful man assigns reasons of fittingness and of efficacy, so that he may understand what he believes; and this certitude follows upon faith. For reasons of this kind are excellent and efficacious to the faithful, but to the unbeliever wholly useless and infirm[^p243-7].

1. 2. As to the question, then, concerning certitude, it must be said that « since the end imposes necessity on the things which are for the end[^p243-8] »; since this teaching is for generating faith, it ought not to have *demonstrative* certitude; it could not have *internal* certitude, because that belongs to grace; it ought not to have *persuasive* certitude: therefore it was fitting that it should have the certitude of *authority.* And this is the reason why all the books of sacred Scripture are handed down by way of narration, not of reasoning, because they are for generating *faith*, which is by free assent. But the books of the doctors are handed down by reasonings, because they are for generating *intelligence* and for fostering and nourishing faith. But those reasons have no strength unless they are founded upon faith[^p243-9].

It must be said, therefore, that the certitude of authority in the other sciences, devised by men, who often lie, has little strength; but in sacred Scripture, which was given by the Holy Spirit, it has great firmness, because he cannot lie. Whence Augustine on Genesis[^p243-10]: « Greater is the authority of this Scripture than all the perspicacity of human wit ».

11. Quaest. III. It is asked concerning the sublimity of searching and of speech. And it seems that blessed John acts badly, who[^p243-11] searches into things so sublime:
<!-- page 244 -->

1. Because Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter[^p244-1]: *Seek not the things that are too high for thee, and search not into things above thy ability*; and again Proverbs, the twenty-fifth chapter: *He that is a searcher of majesty shall be overwhelmed by glory.* What is greater than the eternal generation of the Word? — *If thou sayest* that he does not search into it but narrates it; *on the contrary*: Isaias, the fifty-third chapter[^p244-2]: *Who shall declare his generation?* And Jerome expounds it of the eternal generation.

2. Likewise the Apostle in the first [epistle] to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p244-3], commends himself for this, that he did not come to the Corinthians *in sublimity of speech*, but in humility. Therefore it seems that blessed John is reprehensible, in that he thundered so loftily that he could not have done so more loftily.

**I respond:** It must be understood that the searching of majesty and of sublime things can be in three ways, namely in a diabolical, a human and a divine spirit. The first is of *malignity*, and was in the heretics; the second of *curiosity*, and was in the philosophers; the third of *piety*, and was in the Apostles and Prophets. The first is altogether to be reprehended, the second to be guarded against, but the third to be embraced; because both the first and the second frequently lead into error, but the third not, because the Spirit *searcheth the deep things of God*, the first [epistle] to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p244-4]. And because John was inspired by the divine Spirit, therefore he cannot be reprehended in the searching and description of heavenly things; and therefore it was the Holy Spirit that declared the eternal generation, not a man.

2. As to the question concerning the sublimity of speech, it must be said that *sublimity of speech* is sometimes reprehended on account of a *wicked intention*, as when a man speaks sublime things in order to appear glorious; and so it is forbidden in First Kings, the second chapter[^p244-5]: *Do not multiply to speak lofty things, boasting.* — Sometimes it is reprehended on account of the *matter*, as when the matter is light and slight, it is not to be weighed down and obscured with lofty words; of which the [epistle] to the Colossians, the second chapter[^p244-6]: *Now this I say, that no man may deceive you by sublimity of words*; and after: *Beware lest any man deceive you by philosophy and vain deceit.* — Sometimes it is reprehended because *the hearers are not capable*; on account of this the Apostle said in the first [epistle] to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p244-7]: *I came not to you in sublimity of speech*; and the reason was that they could not understand. Contrariwise Jerome says of certain men: « Others, with brows drawn up, weighing out grand words, philosophize among silly women about the sacred Letters ». — But blessed John had both a lofty *matter* and a good *intention* and advanced hearers, because the Church had now grown; wherefore he rightly and duly spoke sublimely. Yet this is marvellous, as Augustine says[^p244-8], that in his speech the little one finds milk to suck, and the perfect man finds solid food to refresh him. And that saying of Gregory is verified, that there the elephant swims and the lamb wades. And therefore the little ones, who cannot swim with the elephants, ought to wade with the lambs.

     *Sermon of brother Bonaventure in the schools at Paris, when he began John.*

     *Behold, my servant shall understand, he shall be exalted and extolled and shall be exceeding high*, Isa. 52:13. In these words blessed John is commended from the sanctity of an excellent life, in that it is said: *my servant*; from the clarity of a surpassingly splendid intelligence, in that it is said: *shall understand*; from the sublimity of a singular teaching, in that it is said: *he shall be exalted and extolled and shall be exceeding high*, as will be said in its place.

     The Lord calls him his *servant*, moreover, by reason of the most faithful obedience with which he obeyed him; Num. 12:7: My servant Moses, who is most faithful in all my house. Of the most solemn office to which he was preordained; Isa. 49:5: Thus saith the Lord, that formed me from the womb to be his servant: I have given thee etc. Of the most special benefit by which he was privileged beyond the rest; Isa. 42:1: Behold my servant etc., my elect etc.

     Blessed John *understood*, moreover, by the anointing of the Holy Spirit *useful things*; Isa. 28:9: Whom shall he teach knowledge? and whom shall he make to understand the hearing? them that are weaned from the milk etc. By the instruction of Christ *sublime things*; III Kings 3:12: I have given thee a wise and understanding heart etc. By angelic revelation *hidden things*; Dan. 1:17: But to Daniel the Lord gave the understanding of visions and dreams.

[^tr-doctrina]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin *doctrina* holds together what English divides among doctrine, teaching and learning — the body of what is taught, the act of teaching it, and what a learner takes away. Here the Prooemium builds its whole commendation of John on the word: Isaias' *sublimis erit valde* is read as the *excellentia doctrinae* of a doctor, and the Gospel is then praised as a *doctrina* that treats of sublime things, proceeds sublimely and leads to sublime things — a teaching given and received, not a body of propositions. The English therefore takes the side of "teaching" at every one of the word's fourteen sites in this chunk, so that the argument can be seen running on one word. Where this translation renders the same word "doctrine" or "learning", it is because the Latin there names the body of what is taught or the thing learned.
