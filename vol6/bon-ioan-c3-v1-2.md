# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. III, Vers. 1-2
## *Supra manifestavit se Dominus Iudaeis signo virtutis, hic secundo manifestat se verbo eruditionis, et hoc instruens omnes Iudaeos in persona Nicodemi principis Iudaeorum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 278 -->

### Capitulum III.

1. *Erat autem quidam*[^p278-1] *homo* etc. Supra manifestavit se Dominus Iudaeis signo virtutis, hic secundo manifestat se *verbo eruditionis,* et hoc instruens omnes Iudaeos in persona Nicodemi principis Iudaeorum. Et quia verbum eruditionis tradi non debet nisi auri attendenti; ideo primo hic notatur *diligentia Nicodemi;* secundo vero, *doctrina Domini,* ibi[^p278-2]: *Respondit Iesus et dixit ei: Amen.*

Describitur ergo Nicodemus a quadruplici conditione, ut ostendatur idoneus ad disciplinam: describitur a *vitae honestate,* ab *officii dignitate,* a *discendi sollicitudine,* a *discendi aptitudine.*

2. (Vers. 1.). Describitur igitur a *vitae honestate* primo, cum dicitur: *Erat homo ex Pharisaeis, Nicodemus nomine. Ex Pharisaeis,* quae secta erat probabilior et honestior super omnes sectas Iudaeorum; unde Apostolus gloriatur ad Philippenses tertio[^p278-3]: « Secundum legem Pharisaeus ». — Describitur etiam ab *officii dignitate,* cum dicitur: *Princeps Iudaeorum.* Unde et ipse restitit furori Iudaeorum; infra septimo[^p278-4]: « Nunquid lex nostra iudicat hominem, nisi audierit ab eo prius »? — Describitur etiam a *discendi sollicitudine,* cum dicitur:

3. (Vers. 2.). *Hic venit ad Iesum nocte.* Ideo nocte venit, ut quietius posset instrui et doceri, quia tunc hora est quieta; unde in Psalmo[^p278-5]: « Media nocte surgebam ad confitendum tibi ». — Describitur a *discendi aptitudine,* cum dicitur: *Et dixit ei,* scilicet Iesu: *Rabbi, scimus, quia a Deo venisti magister.* Aptus enim erat doceri, quia doceri venerat et Christum perfectum doctorem credebat et eius discipulus esse volebat, et propter hoc *magistrum* vocabat, sicut discipuli; infra decimo tertio[^p278-6]: « Vos vocatis me *magister* et *Domine* ». Et ratio, quare hoc dicit, infertur: *Nemo potest*[^p278-7] *haec signa facere, quae tu facis, nisi fuerit Deus cum eo.* Ideo patet, quod erat ex his, de quibus dictum est supra secundo: « Multi crediderunt in nomine eius, videntes signa, quae faciebat ». Signa erant ratio credendi, quod esset a Deo; unde infra nono dixit caecus: « Nisi esset hic homo a Deo, non posset facere quidquam ».

### Quaestiones.

4. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod dicitur, quod *Nicodemus* erat *princeps Iudaeorum* et *credebat* in Iesum; quia dicitur infra septimo[^p278-8]: *Nunquid aliquis ex principibus credidit in eum?* Quasi dicat: non.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Nicodemus *credidit* et erat *ex principibus.* Et illud verbum, quod dicitur infra septimo, fuit verbum Pharisaeorum, qui decepti fuerunt. Nicodemus enim credebat, etsi non palam, tamen occulte[^p278-9].

5. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur: cum hora illa sit hora malefactorum, quia *qui male agit odit lucem*[^p278-10]; unde est hoc, quod Nicodemus venire voluit *nocte?* Et videtur, quod inordinate venerit.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod in causa fuit *verecundia* quaedam, *infirmitas* et *diligentia.* De *verecundia,* Victor[^p278-11]: « Nocte venit, quia magister in Israel palam discere erubuit ». De *infirmitate,* Chrysostomus[^p278-12]: « Adhuc Iudaica detinebatur infirmitate; propterea et nocte venit, trepidans in die hoc facere ». De *diligentia* dicit Beda[^p278-13]: « Nocte venit, cupiens secreta allocutione mysteria fidei percipere, cuius iam aliquatenus rudimenta perceperat ».

6. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Nemo potest haec signa facere, quae tu facis, nisi
<!-- page 279 -->
fuerit Deus cum eo.* — *Contra:* Matthaei septimo[^p279-1]: *Multi dicent mihi in illa die: Domine, in nomine tuo daemonia eiecimus* etc.

Respondent quidam ad hoc, facientes vim in verbo: *Nisi fuerit Deus cum illo.* Differt enim dicere: Deus est cum homine, et homo cum Deo; sicut videtur velle Augustinus dicere in libro Confessionum[^p279-2]: « Mecum eras, sed ego non eram tecum ». Sic et in proposito homo est cum Deo, quando habet caritatem, per quam adhaeret Deo; sed Deus cum homine, quando aliquem specialem effectum et donum facit in homine. Sic est cum illis quibus tribuit virtutem faciendi miracula. — Sed istud non solvit, quia Nicodemus hoc non intendebat dicere, immo vere, quod esset a Deo[^p279-3].

Propterea dicendum, quod etsi boni et mali faciant miracula, distinguuntur tamen hi ab illis, *modo* scilicet et *fine: modo,* quia boni per publicam iustitiam, mali per signa publicae iustitiae; *fine,* quia boni ad aedificationem, mali ad gloriam[^p279-4]. Et quia Nicodemus in modo faciendi et fine miracula distinguebat; maxime hoc scire poterat in Christo, quia non per invocationem faciebat miracula[^p279-5].

## English
<!-- page 278 -->

### Chapter III.

1. *Now there was a certain*[^p278-1] *man* etc. Above, the Lord manifested himself to the Jews by a sign of virtue; here, second, he manifests himself *by a word of instruction,* and this instructing all the Jews in the person of Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews. And because a word of instruction ought not to be handed on except to an attentive ear; therefore here there is noted first *the diligence of Nicodemus;* but second, *the teaching*[^tr-doctrina] *of the Lord,* at the place[^p278-2]: *Jesus answered, and said to him: Amen.*

Nicodemus, then, is described by a fourfold condition, that he may be shown fit for discipline: he is described by *the uprightness of his life,* by *the dignity of his office,* by *his solicitude to learn,* by *his aptitude to learn.*

2. (Verse 1.). He is described, then, first by *the uprightness of his life,* when it is said: *There was a man of the Pharisees, named Nicodemus. Of the Pharisees,* which sect was more approved and more upright above all the sects of the Jews; whence the Apostle glories, to the Philippians, the third chapter[^p278-3]: « According to the law, a Pharisee ». — He is described also by *the dignity of his office,* when it is said: *A ruler of the Jews.* Whence he too withstood the fury of the Jews; below, the seventh chapter[^p278-4]: « Doth our law judge any man, unless it first hear him »? — He is described also by *his solicitude to learn,* when it is said:

3. (Verse 2.). *This man came to Jesus by night.* He came by night for this reason, that he might be instructed and taught more quietly, because then the hour is quiet; whence in the Psalm[^p278-5]: « I rose at midnight to give praise to thee ». — He is described by *his aptitude to learn,* when it is said: *And said to him,* namely to Jesus: *Rabbi, we know that thou art come a teacher from God.* For he was apt to be taught, because he had come to be taught, and he believed Christ a perfect doctor and wished to be his disciple, and on account of this he called him *teacher,* as the disciples did; below, the thirteenth chapter[^p278-6]: « You call me *Teacher* and *Lord* ». And the reason why he says this is brought in: *No man can*[^p278-7] *do these signs which thou dost, unless God be with him.* Hence it is plain that he was of those of whom it was said above, in the second chapter: « Many believed in his name, seeing the signs which he did ». The signs were the reason for believing that he was from God; whence below, in the ninth chapter, the blind man said: « Unless this man were of God, he could not do any thing ».

### Questions.

4. Question I. But here it is asked first about this that is said, that *Nicodemus* was *a ruler of the Jews* and *believed* in Jesus; because it is said below, in the seventh chapter[^p278-8]: *Hath any one of the rulers believed in him?* As if he were saying: no.

**I respond:** It must be said that Nicodemus *believed* and was *of the rulers.* And that word which is said below, in the seventh chapter, was a word of the Pharisees, who had been deceived. For Nicodemus believed, though not openly, yet secretly[^p278-9].

5. Question II. Likewise it is asked: since that hour is the hour of evildoers, because *he that doth evil hateth the light*[^p278-10]; whence is it that Nicodemus wished to come *by night?* And it seems that he came in a disordered way.

**I respond:** It must be said that the cause of it was a certain *shame, weakness* and *diligence.* Of *shame,* Victor[^p278-11]: « He came by night, because, being a teacher in Israel, he blushed to learn openly ». Of *weakness,* Chrysostom[^p278-12]: « He was still held by Jewish weakness; and therefore he came by night, fearing to do this by day ». Of *diligence* Bede says[^p278-13]: « He came by night, desiring to take in by a secret conversation the mysteries of the faith, whose rudiments he had already in some measure taken in ».

6. Question III. Likewise it is asked about this that he says: *No man can do these signs which thou dost, unless
<!-- page 279 -->
God be with him.* — *On the contrary:* Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p279-1]: *Many will say to me in that day: Lord, in thy name we have cast out devils* etc.

Some answer to this by laying stress on the word: *Unless God be with him.* For it is different to say: God is with a man, and a man is with God; as Augustine seems to wish to say in the book of the *Confessions*[^p279-2]: « Thou wast with me, but I was not with thee ». So also in the matter at hand a man is with God when he has charity, through which he cleaves to God; but God is with a man when he works some special effect and gift in the man. So it is with those to whom he grants the virtue of working miracles. — But this does not solve it, because Nicodemus did not mean to say this, but rather truly that he was from God[^p279-3].

On account of this it must be said that, although both the good and the wicked work miracles, yet these are distinguished from those, namely *by mode* and *by end: by mode,* because the good work them through public justice, the wicked through signs of public justice; *by end,* because the good work them for edification, the wicked for glory[^p279-4]. And because Nicodemus distinguished miracles by the mode of working and by the end; most of all could he know this in Christ, because he did not work miracles by invocation[^p279-5].

[^tr-doctrina]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin *doctrina* holds together what English divides among doctrine, teaching and learning. Here *doctrina Domini* names the second member of the chapter's division, the Lord's answer to Nicodemus beginning at verse 3, which ¶ 7, opening the next pericope, calls the *doctrina Christi, in qua docet Nicodemum*: the act of teaching one who came to be taught, set against Nicodemus's diligence in coming to learn, so the English takes "teaching". Elsewhere this translation renders the same word "doctrine" where it names a body of tenets and "learning" where it names what a learner acquires.
