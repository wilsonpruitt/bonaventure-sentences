# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. V, Vers. 19-23
## *Supra descripta est miraculosa curatio et eius calumniatio; hic incipit tertia pars capituli, in qua determinatur calumniationis confutatio*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 308 -->

35. *Respondit itaque Iesus* etc. Supra descripta est miraculosa curatio et eius calumniatio; hic incipit tertia pars capituli, in qua determinatur *calumniationis confutatio.* Confutat enim Dominus Iudaeos ipsum calumniantes de opere curationis; et hoc ostendendo suam in operando omnipotentem potentiam. — Et dividitur haec pars in duas, quia primo ostenditur eius potentia et declaratur in *generali;* secundo vero, in *speciali,* ibi[^p308-12]: *Amen, amen dico vobis, quia qui verbum meum audit.*

Filii igitur Dei *potentia* hoc ordine declaratur et manifestatur. Primo notatur *potestatis origo;*
<!-- page 309 -->
secundo, *potentiae magnitudo;* tertio, *magnitudinis declaratio;* quarto vero, *declarandi ratio.*

36. (Vers. 18. 19.). Primo igitur notatur *potentiae Filii Dei origo.* Dicit enim Filius Dei, quod a se ipso non habet potentiam operandi, sed a Patre. Propterea dicit: *Respondit itaque Iesus et dixit eis,* scilicet Iudaeis persequentibus et calumniantibus: *Amen, amen dico vobis,* id est vere et certitudinaliter, *non potest Filius a se facere quidquam.* Sicut enim non habet *esse* a se, sed a Patre; sic nec *operari* a se, sed a Patre; infra octavo[^p309-1]: « A me ipso facio nihil ». *Nisi quod viderit Patrem suum facientem,* id est, solum hoc potest, quod a Patre potente accepit, ut posset; infra octavo: « Ego quae vidi apud Patrem meum, haec loquor ». Et ita, quidquid potest, a Patre potest, et nihil aliud potest, nisi quod a Patre accepit, ut posset.

37. *Quaecumque enim* etc. Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *potentiae magnitudo,* in hoc, quod quamvis Filius accipiat *posse* a Patre, tamen ita magnum *posse* habet, ut Pater. Ideo dicit: *Quaecumque enim;* bene dixi, quod Filius facit « quod videt Patrem facientem »; *quaecumque enim ille facit*[^p309-2], *haec et similiter Filius facit: similiter,* uniformiter et concorditer; *similiter,* quia aeque potenter. Unde ad Colossenses primo de Filio dicitur: « Omnia per ipsum et in ipso creata sunt »; quia, sicut dicitur supra primo, « sine ipso factum est nihil ». Et *ratio* indivisionis in opere est indivisio in essentia et in dilectione. Ideo dicit:

38. (Vers. 20.). *Pater enim diligit Filium.* Bene dixi, quod Filius potest omnia facere, quae et Pater; *Pater enim diligit Filium.* Ideo summe *diligit,* quia summe unum; infra decimo[^p309-3]: « Ego et Pater unum sumus »; et quia summe unum, ideo omnia *communicat* Filio; ideo subdit: *Et omnia demonstrat ei, quae ipse facit; omnia demonstrat,* id est, omnis operationis potentiam communicat. Et huius communicationis ratio est unitas et dilectio; supra tertio[^p309-4]: « Pater diligit Filium et omnia dedit in manu eius ».

39. *Et maiora his* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *magnitudinis potentiae declaratio,* quae fit per magnitudinem operum, quae Dominus Iudaeis ostensurus erat per Filium. Ideo dicit: *Et maiora his,* supple: quae vobis ostensa sunt, *demonstrabit ei opera,* id est, per eum demonstrabit vobis, *ut vos miremini,* prae magnitudine, supple; infra decimo[^p309-5]: « Multa bona opera ostendi vobis ». Et quae sint ista opera, ostendit: quia suscitatio mortuorum. Unde dicit:

40. (Vers. 21.). *Sicut enim Pater suscitat mortuos et vivificat,* per potentiam omnipotentem; *sic et Filius quos vult vivificat,* per potentiam ei aequalem; infra undecimo[^p309-6] habetur de Lazari suscitatione in corpore, et de suscitatione in anima dicitur: « Ego sum resurrectio et vita; qui credit in me, etiamsi mortuus fuerit, vivet ». Exemplum suscitationis corporalis habetur similiter Lucae septimo.

41. (Vers. 22.). *Neque enim Pater iudicat quemquam.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *declarandi potentiam ratio;* quare scilicet Deus potentiam suam declarat in Filio et per Filium. Ut ostendat eum sibi aequalem et aeque honorandum; et ideo tribuit Filio *iudicium.* Propter quod dicit: *Neque enim Pater;* bene dixi[^p309-7], quod « maiora his demonstrabit opera, ut vos miremini », quia etiam propter hoc iudicium tribuit. Ideo dicit: *Neque enim Pater iudicat quemquam,* in iudicio scilicet apparendo, *sed omne iudicium dedit Filio;* Actuum decimo: « Ipse est constitutus a Deo iudex vivorum et mortuorum ». Et ratio huius redditur Filii honorificatio; unde dicit:

42. (Vers. 23.). *Ut omnes honorificent Filium, sicut honorificant Patrem.* De hoc Proverbiorum septimo[^p309-8]: « Fili, honora Dominum, et valebis; praeter eum ne timueris alienum ». Filius Dei est honorandus et timendus ratione iudiciariae potestatis, quia « potest et animam et corpus perdere in gehennam », Matthaei decimo[^p309-9]. Et ratio, quare vult Filium Pater honorari: quia honor Filii est Patris; ideo dicit: *Qui non honorificat Filium non honorificat Patrem, qui misit illum;* infra octavo: « Honorifico Patrem meum, et vos inhonorastis me ». Augustinus[^p309-10]: « Si minor crederetur Filius Patre, Pater inhonoraretur, quia vel invidus nollet genuisse aequalem, vel infirmus non posset ».

### Quaestiones.

43. Quaest. I. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Filius non potest a se facere quidquam.*
<!-- page 310 -->

**Contra:** Potentior est qui potest a se, quam qui ab alio; sed Pater potest a se ipso, Filius ab alio: ergo Pater est potentior Filio. — *Si tu dicas:* Maioritas potentiae consistit in virtute, non consistit in modo habendi, et *habere a se* dicit solum modum habendi, non virtutem, et ita non sequitur, quod Pater sit potentior Filio; *obiicitur,* quod nobiliori modo habetur quod habetur a se, quam quod ab alio: ergo si Pater habet potentiam a se, nobiliori modo habet eam quam Filius, qui habet ab alio.

Respondetur ad hoc, quod *habere ab alio* est duobus modis, per *participationem* scilicet, vel per solam *emanationem. Habere ab alio per participationem* minus nobilis modus est; modus enim participationis modo habendi per essentiam non aequatur. Alio modo est *habere ab alio tantum per emanationem,* ita quod ille qui emanat, habet per *essentiam,* sicut ille qui dat; et hoc nullam omnino ponit ignobilitatem, immo omnimodam aequalitatem. *Habere enim a Patre* ita nobilitatis est in Filio, sicut *generare* in Patre. Et sic patet illud[^p310-1].

44. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *Filius nihil potest, nisi quod viderit Patrem facientem;* et iterum, quod *Pater demonstrat Filio quaecumque facit.*

1. Ergo si per naturam prius est aliquid, quam videatur[^p310-2]; prius Pater operatur, et post Filius ad eius operationem tanquam ad exemplar.

2. Item, si Pater nihil omnino potest sine Filio operari, quomodo dicit, quod Filius operatur, sicut videt Patrem operantem?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod idem dicitur, cum dicitur: Pater demonstrat Filio, et: Filius videt Patrem operantem; solum differentia est quoad terminos. Intelligendum autem, quod ista verba translata sunt a creaturis ad Deum. Duplicem autem importat verbum *demonstrandi* similiter et *videndi* proprietatem. Una scilicet est *emanatio cognitionis;* quia video te sic operantem, relinquitur in me cognitio operandi; alia est *exemplaritas antecessionis;* sic[^p310-3] operatio tua et demonstratio praecedit meam cognitionem tanquam exemplar. Dico ergo, quod ratione primae conditionis transfertur ad divina, scilicet ratione *emanationis,* ratione *antecessionis* non. Unde *Patrem demonstrare Filio* non est, quod Pater prius cognoscat quam Filius, sed quod Pater dat Filio; similiter *Filium videre Patrem operantem* non est, quod Pater prius operetur, sed quod Filius habet a Patre[^p310-4].

45. Quaest. III. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Quaecumque Pater facit, haec et Filius similiter facit.*

**Contra:** 1. Pater generat Filium: ergo Filius generat Filium. — *Si dicas,* quod *generare* non dicit operationem, sed relationem; *obiicitur,* quod *mittere Filium* dicit operationem: ergo si, quamcumque operationem facit Pater, hanc facit et Filius; si Pater mittit Filium: ergo et Filius[^p310-5].

2. Item, si Trinitas omnino est indivisa in substantia et operatione; cum *manifestatio* et *apparitio* fiat per operationem, quomodo potest *apparere* vel se manifestare Trinitas in aliqua suarum hypostasum determinate? Nullo modo videtur.

Intelligendum ad haec, quod quaedam verba dicta de Deo dicunt *puram relationem,* sicut *generare;* quaedam *puram*[^p310-6] *actionem,* sicut *creare;* quaedam *actionem et relationem,* sicut *mittere;* quaedam *actionem et significationem,* sicut *apparere.* — Dico igitur, quod quando dicitur: *Quaecumque Pater facit,* intelligitur quantum ad *operationem,* quae communis est et essentialis. Si igitur inferatur verbum significans *mere actionem,* illatio bona est; si vero *mere relationem,* illatio omnino sophistica per figuram dictionis[^p310-7]. Si autem verbum significans *actionem et relationem;* etsi ratione *actionis* teneret illatio, fallit ratione *relationis* inclusae, sicut *mittere* et *incarnari.* Similiter de verbo importante *actionem et significationem,* fallit ratione *significationis.* Sicut enim dicit Augustinus[^p310-8], etsi omnis actio et species sit a tribus, tamen alia specialiter significat Patrem, alia Filium. — Exemplum est de hoc nomine *intelligentia,* quod profertur operatione trium potentiarum, scilicet memoriae, intelligentiae et voluntatis, et tamen alteram illarum significat. Sic et est in proposito et sic patet[^p310-9].
<!-- page 311 -->

46. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de ratione illa, quod *Pater omnia demonstrat Filio,* quia *Filium diligit. Demonstrare Filio* hoc est Filium generare, ut dicit Augustinus[^p311-1]. Aut ergo *diligere* sumitur ibi *notionaliter,* aut *essentialiter.* Si *notionaliter,* falsa est, quia communis spiratio non est ratio emanandi per generationem, quia generatio secundum rationem intelligendi prior[^p311-2]. Si *essentialiter,* adhuc falsa, quia essentia non est ratio emanationis nec relationis; tunc enim Filius generaret.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod cum dicitur: *Pater omnia demonstrat Filio,* et dico, quod *demonstrat,* et dico, quod *omnia.* Dico igitur, quod ratio *demonstrandi omnia* est *dilectio,* non quia demonstrandi *simpliciter,* sed quia demonstrandi *omnia*[^p311-3]. *Dilectio* enim accipitur *essentialiter* et dicit unitatem summam Patris ad Filium; et quia summa est unitas in *essentia,* ideo omnimoda indivisio in *operatione;* et ideo necesse est, quod in omnibus Filius Patri cooperetur. Ideo bene dicit: *Omnia demonstrat,* quia summe *diligit*[^p311-4].

## English
<!-- page 308 -->

35. *Jesus therefore answered* etc. Above, the miraculous cure and its calumniation have been described; here begins the third part of the chapter, in which there is determined *the confutation of the calumniation.* For the Lord confutes the Jews who calumniate him over the work of the cure; and this by showing his omnipotent power in working. — And this part is divided in two, because first his power is shown and declared in *general;* but second, in *special,* there[^p308-12]: *Amen, amen I say unto you, that he who heareth my word.*

The *power* of the Son of God, then, is declared and manifested in this order. First there is noted *the origin of the power;*
<!-- page 309 -->
second, *the greatness of the power;* third, *the declaration of the greatness;* but fourth, *the reason for declaring.*

36. (Verses 18. 19.). First, then, there is noted *the origin of the power of the Son of God.* For the Son of God says that he does not have the power of working from himself, but from the Father. On account of this he says: *Jesus therefore answered and said to them,* namely to the Jews who were persecuting and calumniating: *Amen, amen I say unto you,* that is, truly and with certainty, *the Son cannot do any thing of himself.* For just as he does not have *being* from himself, but from the Father; so neither *working* from himself, but from the Father; below, the eighth chapter[^p309-1]: « I do nothing of myself ». *But what he hath seen his Father doing,* that is, he can do only this, which he received from the Father who is able, that he might be able; below, the eighth chapter: « The things which I have seen with my Father, these I speak ». And so, whatever he can do, he can do from the Father, and he can do nothing else except what he received from the Father, that he might be able.

37. *For what things soever* etc. Here there is touched on the second, namely *the greatness of the power,* in this, that although the Son receives *being able* from the Father, yet he has a *being able* as great as the Father. Therefore he says: *For what things soever;* I said well that the Son does « what he seeth the Father doing »; *for what things soever he doth*[^p309-2], *these the Son also doth in like manner: in like manner,* uniformly and concordantly; *in like manner,* because equally powerfully. Whence to the Colossians, the first chapter, it is said of the Son: « All things were created by him and in him »; because, as is said above, the first chapter, « without him was made nothing ». And the *reason* for the undividedness in work is the undividedness in essence and in dilection. Therefore he says:

38. (Verse 20.). *For the Father loveth the Son.* I said well that the Son can do all things which the Father also can; *for the Father loveth the Son.* Therefore he *loves* supremely, because they are supremely one; below, the tenth chapter[^p309-3]: « I and the Father are one »; and because supremely one, therefore he *communicates* all things to the Son; therefore he adds: *And sheweth him all things which himself doth; sheweth all things,* that is, communicates the power of every operation. And the reason of this communication is unity and dilection; above, the third chapter[^p309-4]: « The Father loveth the Son, and he hath given all things in his hand ».

39. *And greater than these* etc. Here there is touched on the third, namely *the declaration of the greatness of the power,* which is made through the greatness of the works which the Lord was to show the Jews through the Son. Therefore he says: *And greater than these,* supply: than those which have been shown to you, *will he shew him works,* that is, through him he will show to you, *that you may wonder,* supply: at their greatness; below, the tenth chapter[^p309-5]: « Many good works I have shewed you ». And what these works are, he shows: namely the raising of the dead. Whence he says:

40. (Verse 21.). *For as the Father raiseth up the dead and giveth life,* through omnipotent power; *so the Son also giveth life to whom he will,* through a power equal to him; below, the eleventh chapter[^p309-6], there is had the raising of Lazarus in the body, and of the raising in the soul it is said: « I am the resurrection and the life; he that believeth in me, although he be dead, shall live ». An example of bodily raising is had likewise in Luke, the seventh chapter.

41. (Verse 22.). *For neither doth the Father judge any man.* Here there is touched on the fourth, namely *the reason for declaring the power;* namely why God declares his power in the Son and through the Son. That he might show him equal to himself and equally to be honoured; and therefore he assigns *judgement* to the Son. On account of which he says: *For neither doth the Father;* I said well[^p309-7] that « greater works will he shew him, that you may wonder », because on account of this too he assigned judgement. Therefore he says: *For neither doth the Father judge any man,* namely by appearing in the judgement, *but hath given all judgement to the Son;* Acts, the tenth chapter: « He is appointed by God judge of the living and of the dead ». And the reason of this is given as the honouring of the Son; whence he says:

42. (Verse 23.). *That all men may honour the Son, as they honour the Father.* Of this, Proverbs, the seventh chapter[^p309-8]: « Son, honour the Lord, and thou shalt be strong; but besides him fear thou no stranger ». The Son of God is to be honoured and feared by reason of his judiciary power, because « he can destroy both soul and body in hell », Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p309-9]. And the reason why the Father wills the Son to be honoured: because the honour of the Son is the Father's; therefore he says: *He who honoureth not the Son honoureth not the Father, who hath sent him;* below, the eighth chapter: « I honour my Father, and you have dishonoured me ». Augustine[^p309-10]: « If the Son were believed less than the Father, the Father would be dishonoured, because either out of envy he would not have willed to beget an equal, or out of weakness he could not have ».

### Questions.

43. Question I. It is asked about this, that he says: *The Son cannot do any thing of himself.*
<!-- page 310 -->

**On the contrary:** He who is able of himself is more powerful than he who is able from another; but the Father is able of himself, the Son from another: therefore the Father is more powerful than the Son. — *If you say:* Superiority of power consists in virtue, it does not consist in the mode of having, and *to have of oneself* expresses only the mode of having, not virtue, and so it does not follow that the Father is more powerful than the Son; *it is objected* that what is had of oneself is had in a nobler mode than what is had from another: therefore if the Father has power of himself, he has it in a nobler mode than the Son, who has it from another.

It is answered to this that *to have from another* is in two modes, namely through *participation,* or through *emanation* alone. *To have from another through participation* is a less noble mode; for the mode of participation is not equalled to the mode of having through essence. In the other mode it is *to have from another only through emanation,* so that he who emanates has through *essence,* just as he who gives; and this posits no ignobility at all, but rather an equality in every mode. For *to have from the Father* is as much of nobility in the Son as *to beget* is in the Father. And so that is clear[^p310-1].

44. Question II. Likewise it is asked about this, that he says that *the Son can do nothing but what he hath seen the Father doing;* and again, that *the Father sheweth the Son whatsoever he doth.*

1. Therefore, if by nature something is prior to its being seen[^p310-2], the Father works first, and the Son afterwards, after his operation as after an exemplar.

2. Likewise, if the Father can work nothing at all without the Son, how does he say that the Son works as he sees the Father working?

**I respond:** It must be said that the same thing is said when it is said: The Father shows the Son, and: The Son sees the Father working; there is a difference only as regards the terms. But it must be understood that these verbs are transferred from creatures to God. Now the verb *of showing,* and likewise *of seeing,* implies a twofold property. One namely is *the emanation of cognition;* because I see you so working, cognition of the working is left in me; the other is *the exemplarity of antecedence;* thus[^p310-3] your operation and showing precede my cognition as an exemplar. I say, then, that it is transferred to divine things by reason of the first condition, namely by reason of *emanation,* not by reason of *antecedence.* Whence for *the Father to show the Son* is not that the Father knows before the Son, but that the Father gives to the Son; likewise, for *the Son to see the Father working* is not that the Father works first, but that the Son has from the Father[^p310-4].

45. Question III. It is asked about this, that he says: *What things soever the Father doth, these the Son also doth in like manner.*

**On the contrary:** 1. The Father begets the Son: therefore the Son begets the Son. — *If you say* that *to beget* does not express an operation, but a relation; *it is objected* that *to send the Son* expresses an operation: therefore if, whatever operation the Father performs, the Son also performs it; if the Father sends the Son: therefore the Son does too[^p310-5].

2. Likewise, if the Trinity is altogether undivided in substance and operation; since *manifestation* and *apparition* come about through operation, how can the Trinity *appear* or manifest itself in some one of its hypostases determinately? It seems in no way.

It must be understood with regard to these that some verbs said of God express *pure relation,* as *to beget;* some *pure*[^p310-6] *action,* as *to create;* some *action and relation,* as *to send;* some *action and signification,* as *to appear.* — I say, then, that when it is said: *What things soever the Father doth,* it is understood as regards *operation,* which is common and essential. If, then, a verb signifying *merely action* is inferred, the inference is good; but if *merely relation,* the inference is altogether sophistical through the figure of speech[^p310-7]. But if a verb signifying *action and relation;* although by reason of the *action* the inference would hold, it fails by reason of the *relation* included, as *to send* and *to be incarnate.* Likewise about a verb implying *action and signification,* it fails by reason of the *signification.* For as Augustine says[^p310-8], although every action and appearance is from the three, yet one specially signifies the Father, another the Son. — An example is of this name *intelligence,* which is uttered by the operation of the three powers, namely of memory, intelligence and will, and yet signifies one of them. So too it is in the matter proposed, and so it is clear[^p310-9].
<!-- page 311 -->

46. Question IV. Likewise it is asked about that reason, that *the Father sheweth all things to the Son,* because *he loveth the Son. To show the Son* is to beget the Son, as Augustine says[^p311-1]. Either, then, *to love* is taken there *notionally,* or *essentially.* If *notionally,* it is false, because the common spiration is not the reason of emanating by generation, since generation is prior according to the account of understanding[^p311-2]. If *essentially,* still it is false, because the essence is not the reason of emanation nor of relation; for then the Son would beget.

**I respond:** It must be said that when it is said: *The Father sheweth all things to the Son,* I say, that *sheweth,* and I say, that *all things.* I say, then, that the reason *of showing all things* is *dilection,* not because of showing *simply,* but because of showing *all things*[^p311-3]. For *dilection* is taken *essentially* and expresses the supreme unity of the Father to the Son; and because there is supreme unity in *essence,* therefore there is undividedness in every mode in *operation;* and therefore it is necessary that the Son cooperate with the Father in all things. Therefore he says well: *He sheweth all things,* because he supremely *loves*[^p311-4].
