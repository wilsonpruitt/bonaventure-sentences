# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. II, Vers. 18-25
## *Manifestavit se Dominus signo auctoritatis; hic se manifestat signo potestatis, non exhibito, sed petito et promisso*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 276 -->

32. *Responderunt Iudaei* etc. Manifestavit se Dominus signo auctoritatis; hic se manifestat *signo potestatis,* non exhibito, sed petito et promisso. Et hoc signum fuit mirabilis sui resuscitatio, et proceditur hoc ordine. In hac manifestatione primo ponitur huius signi *postulatio;* secundo, postulati *oblatio;* tertio, oblati *expositio;* quarto, consequens fidei Iudaeorum *aedificatio* et per hoc Christi manifestatio.

(Vers. 18.). Primo ergo describitur *signi postulatio,* quae facta est a Iudaeis videntibus, quod tantae auctoritatis se ostenderet. Propterea dicit: *Responderunt ergo Iudaei,* Domino scilicet corripienti, *et dixerunt ei: Quod signum ostendis nobis, quia haec facis?* Quaerunt signum potestatis; primae ad Corinthios primo[^p276-1]: « Iudaei signa quaerunt ». Simile dixerunt Matthaei vigesimo primo: « Dic nobis: In qua potestate haec facis »? Et stulte quaerebant, quia ipsum factum magnum signum erat; Chrysostomus[^p276-2]: « O insania! Signone opus erat, ut ea quae male fiebant, cessare faceret? Zelum vero talem accipere pro domo Dei nonne maximum signum virtutis erat »?

33. (Vers. 19.). *Respondit Iesus et dixit eis.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *signi petiti oblatio;* et offert etiam eis signum maximae virtutis de resuscitatione sui; propter quod dicit: *Solvite templum hoc,* scilicet occidendo; *solvite,* id est, solvetis[^p276-3], *et in tribus diebus excitabo illud,* id est, suscitabo quasi de somno. *Mors* enim in Scriptura dicitur dormitio, et *resurrectio* excitatio; Psalmus[^p276-4]: « Ego dormivi et soporatus sum » etc. *Diebus tribus,* scilicet per synecdochen; Osee sexto: « Vivificabit nos post duos dies, et in die tertia suscitabit nos », scilicet in capite.

34. (Vers. 20.). *Dixerunt ergo ei*[^p276-5] *Iudaei.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *signi oblati expositio,* quia non de templo materiali hoc dicebat, sed de templo corporis sui; et Iudaei intelligebant de materiali. Propter quod dicit: *Dixerunt ergo Iudaei: Quadraginta et sex annis aedificatum est templum hoc, et tu in tribus diebus excitabis illud?* Quasi dicant: hoc omnino est incredibile et tibi impossibile. Intelligendum, quod templum in prima aedificatione fuit consummatum in septem annis[^p276-6], sed in secunda propter guerras et impedimenta sub Zorobabel per annos quadraginta sex. De hoc Dominus non intelligebat; ideo dicit:

35. (Vers. 21.). *Ille autem dicebat de templo corporis sui.* Si enim corpora nostra « templum sunt Spiritus sancti », primae ad Corinthios sexto[^p276-7]; quanto magis corpus Christi, « in quo Divinitas corporaliter inhabitat », secundum quod dicitur ad Colossenses secundo. Et hanc intelligentiam spiritualem non habebant nec Iudaei nec carnales discipuli, sed post resurrectionem. Ideo dicit:

36. (Vers. 22.). *Cum ergo resurrexisset a mortuis, recordati sunt discipuli eius, quia hoc dicebat,* de corpore suo. Tunc enim aperuit illis sensum ad intelligendum; Lucae ultimo[^p276-8]: « Aperuit illis sensum, ut intelligerent Scripturas ». *Et* ex tunc *crediderunt Scripturae*[^p276-9], in qua scilicet praefigurata est Christi resurrectio, ut Iona; *et crediderunt etiam sermoni, quem dixerat Iesus,* quia hac ratione praedicebat; infra decimo quarto[^p276-10]: « Nunc dixi vobis, priusquam fiat, ut, cum factum fuerit, credatis ».

37. (Vers. 23.). *Cum autem esset Ierosolymis.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *fidei Iudaeorum aedificatio* ad aspectum signorum potestatis. Intelligendum enim, quod multa fecit, licet Evangelista non descripserit, sicut dicit Chrysostomus[^p276-11]. Propterea dicit: *Cum autem esset Ierosolymis in Pascha in die festo,* ad quem scilicet ascenderat; *multi
<!-- page 277 -->
crediderunt in nomine eius, videntes signa, quae faciebat.* Fides eorum signis innitebatur; infra septimo[^p277-1]: « De turba autem multi crediderunt in eum, videntes signa, quae faciebat »; et quia signis innitebantur, ideo in fide infirmi erant. Propterea dicit:

38. (Vers. 24.). *Ipse autem Iesus non credebat semetipsum eis;* non quia timeret, sed quia eorum infirmitatem cognosceret. Et quomodo hoc sciret, ostendit: quia non ut homo *relatione,* sed ut Deus *interna visione; eo quod ipse nosset omnes;* primi Regum decimo sexto[^p277-2]: « Homines vident ea quae parent, Deus autem intuetur cor ».

39. (Vers. 25.). *Et quia non erat opus, ut quis ei testimonium*[^p277-3] *perhiberet de homine; ipse enim sciebat, quid esset in homine;* Ieremiae decimo septimo: « Ego Dominus scrutans corda et renes »; ad Hebraeos quarto: « Vivus est sermo Dei et efficax et penetrabilior omni gladio ancipiti, pertingens usque ad divisionem animae ac spiritus, compagum quoque ac medullarum, et discretor cogitationum et intentionum cordis ».

### Quaestiones.

40. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod Dominus dicit Iudaeis: *Solvite.* Videtur, quod eis imperavit[^p277-4], ut se ipsum occiderent.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Scriptura imperativo modo aliquando utitur *imperative,* ut ibi: *Deum time et mandata eius observa,* Ecclesiastae ultimo[^p277-5]. Aliquando *praedictive,* ut hic; Glossa: « *Solvite,* id est, solvetis ». Aliquando *consultive;* Matthaei decimo nono[^p277-6]: *Vade et vende omnia, quae habes, et da pauperibus.* Aliquando *permissive;* Matthaei octavo: *Si eiicis nos hinc, mitte nos in porcos.* Aliquando *ironice;* Ioannis decimo tertio: *Quod facis fac citius,* dixit Dominus ad Iudam de proditione.

41. Quaest. II. Item quaerit Chrysostomus[^p277-7] de hoc, quod Dominus signum de resurrectione semper respondet Iudaeis; ut Matthaei duodecimo et decimo sexto dicitur, quod *non dabitur eis signum nisi signum Ionae prophetae.* Cum enim ipsi non intelligerent, ad quid eis hoc signum dabat? Et si dabat, quare non sic dabat, ut intelligerent?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod signum hoc dabat eis, quia *summum* et quia *occultum: summum,* quia a morte posse liberare se ipsum etiam post mortem, hoc est omnium novorum novissimum, nobilissimum[^p277-8] et mirabilissimum. Quia etiam *occultum,* dabat eis et aenigmatice dicebat, quia iusto iudicio ita se manifestabat, ut lateret, quousque post resurrectionem se manifestaret. Si enim omnino fuisset manifestatus, nequaquam fuisset mortuus; ideo mittit eos ad tempus resurrectionis. Et ideo loquebatur eis aenigmatice et etiam discipulis, quia, etsi tunc non intelligerent, postmodum carius erant intellecturi[^p277-9].

42. Quaest. III. Quaestio est: quare Dominus se templum dicit et templo se comparat?

Et respondet Augustinus[^p277-10], quod hoc non solum est propter inhabitationem Divinitatis, sed etiam propter similitudinem ad templum: quia, sicut templum quadraginta sex annis aedificatum est, sic foetus conceptus quadraginta sex diebus formatur, et illis multiplicatis per senarium, nascitur. — Sed hoc nihil[^p277-11] valet ad propositum, ut videtur; quia corpus Christi ab instanti conceptionis fuit perfectum, et anima perfecta, et usum habuit liberi arbitrii.

Propter hoc dicendum, quod corpus perfici est duobus modis: vel *organizatione,* vel *mole.* Corpora alia a corpore Christi infra illud tempus vel in illo tempore aedificantur et perficiuntur, id est, ad perfectionem tendunt *organizatione* et *mole;* corpus vero Christi tantum *mole,* non membrorum distinctione[^p277-12].

## English
<!-- page 276 -->

32. *The Jews answered* etc. The Lord manifested himself by a sign of authority; here he manifests himself *by a sign of power,* not shown, but asked for and promised. And this sign was his own wonderful raising up again, and the procedure is in this order. In this manifestation there is set first the *asking* for this sign; second, the *offering* of what was asked for; third, the *exposition* of what was offered; fourth, the consequent *edification* of the faith of the Jews, and through this the manifestation of Christ.

(Verse 18.). First, therefore, is described *the asking for a sign,* which was made by the Jews seeing that he showed himself of such great authority. Therefore he says: *The Jews therefore answered,* namely the Lord as he rebuked them, *and said to him: What sign dost thou shew unto us, seeing thou dost these things?* They seek a sign of power; the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter[^p276-1]: « The Jews seek signs ». They said the like in Matthew, the twenty-first chapter: « Tell us: By what power dost thou these things »? And foolishly did they ask, because the deed itself was a great sign; Chrysostom[^p276-2]: « O madness! Was there need of a sign, that he should make the things that were being done wickedly to cease? But to take up such zeal for the house of God, was not this the greatest sign of virtue »?

33. (Verse 19.). *Jesus answered, and said to them.* Here is touched the second, namely *the offering of the sign asked for;* and he offers them also a sign of the greatest virtue, concerning his own raising up again; on account of which he says: *Destroy this temple,* namely by killing; *destroy,* that is, you will destroy[^p276-3], *and in three days I will raise it up,* that is, I will awaken it as from sleep. For in Scripture *death* is called a sleeping, and *resurrection* a raising up; the Psalm[^p276-4]: « I have slept and have taken my rest » etc. *In three days,* namely by synecdoche; Osee, the sixth chapter: « He will revive us after two days, and on the third day he will raise us up », namely in the head.

34. (Verse 20.). *The Jews then said to him*[^p276-5]. Here is touched the third, namely *the exposition of the sign offered,* because he did not say this of the material temple, but of the temple of his body; and the Jews understood it of the material one. On account of which he says: *The Jews then said: Six and forty years was this temple in building, and wilt thou in three days raise it up?* As if they were saying: this is altogether incredible and impossible for thee. It is to be understood that the temple in its first building was completed in seven years[^p276-6], but in the second, on account of wars and hindrances under Zorobabel, through forty-six years. The Lord did not understand it of this; therefore he says:

35. (Verse 21.). *But he spoke of the temple of his body.* For if our bodies « are the temple of the Holy Spirit », the first to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter[^p276-7]; how much more the body of Christ, « in whom the Divinity dwelleth corporeally », according as it is said to the Colossians, the second chapter. And this spiritual intelligence neither the Jews nor the carnal disciples had, but only after the resurrection. Therefore he says:

36. (Verse 22.). *When therefore he was risen again from the dead, his disciples remembered that he had said this,* of his body. For then he opened to them their understanding, that they might understand; Luke, the last chapter[^p276-8]: « He opened their understanding, that they might understand the Scriptures ». *And* from then *they believed the Scripture*[^p276-9], in which, namely, the resurrection of Christ was prefigured, as in Jonas; *and they believed also the word that Jesus had said,* because by this reasoning he foretold it; below, the fourteenth chapter[^p276-10]: « Now I have told you before it come to pass: that, when it shall come to pass, you may believe ».

37. (Verse 23.). *Now when he was at Jerusalem.* Here is touched the fourth, namely *the edification of the faith of the Jews* at the sight of the signs of power. For it is to be understood that he did many things, although the Evangelist did not describe them, as Chrysostom says[^p276-11]. Therefore he says: *Now when he was at Jerusalem, at the pasch, upon the festival day,* to which, namely, he had gone up; *many
<!-- page 277 -->
believed in his name, seeing the signs which he did.* Their faith leaned upon signs; below, the seventh chapter[^p277-1]: « But of the people many believed in him, seeing the signs which he did »; and because they leaned upon signs, therefore they were weak in faith. Therefore he says:

38. (Verse 24.). *But Jesus did not trust himself unto them;* not because he feared, but because he knew their weakness. And how he knew this, he shows: because not as a man *by report,* but as God *by inward vision; for that he knew all men;* the first of Kings, the sixteenth chapter[^p277-2]: « Men see those things that appear, but God beholdeth the heart ».

39. (Verse 25.). *And because there was no need that any should give testimony*[^p277-3] *to him of man; for he knew what was in man;* Jeremias, the seventeenth chapter: « I the Lord, who search the hearts and reins »; to the Hebrews, the fourth chapter: « The word of God is living and effectual, and more piercing than any two-edged sword, and reaching unto the division of the soul and the spirit, of the joints also and the marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart ».

### Questions.

40. Question I. But here it is asked first about this, that the Lord says to the Jews: *Destroy.* It seems that he commanded them[^p277-4] to kill him himself.

**I respond:** It must be said that Scripture sometimes uses the imperative mood *imperatively,* as in that place: *Fear God, and keep his commandments,* Ecclesiastes, the last chapter[^p277-5]. Sometimes *predictively,* as here; the Gloss: « *Destroy,* that is, you will destroy ». Sometimes *by way of counsel;* Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p277-6]: *Go and sell all that thou hast, and give to the poor.* Sometimes *by way of permission;* Matthew, the eighth chapter: *If thou cast us out hence, send us into the swine.* Sometimes *ironically;* John, the thirteenth chapter: *That which thou dost, do quickly,* the Lord said to Judas concerning the betrayal.

41. Question II. Likewise Chrysostom asks[^p277-7] about this, that the Lord always answers the Jews with the sign of the resurrection; as it is said in Matthew, the twelfth and the sixteenth chapters, that *a sign shall not be given them but the sign of Jonas the prophet.* For since they did not understand, to what end did he give them this sign? And if he gave it, why did he not so give it that they might understand?

**I respond:** It must be said that he gave them this sign because it is *the highest* and because it is *hidden: the highest,* because to be able to free himself from death even after death, this is of all new things the newest, the noblest[^p277-8] and the most wonderful. And because it is also *hidden,* he gave it to them and spoke it enigmatically, because by a just judgement he so manifested himself as to lie hid, until after the resurrection he should manifest himself. For if he had been wholly manifested, he would by no means have died; therefore he sends them to the time of the resurrection. And therefore he spoke enigmatically to them and also to the disciples, because, although they did not understand then, they were afterwards to understand it the more dearly[^p277-9].

42. Question III. The question is: why does the Lord call himself a temple and compare himself to a temple?

And Augustine answers[^p277-10] that this is not only on account of the indwelling of the Divinity, but also on account of a likeness to the temple: because, as the temple was built in forty-six years, so the foetus conceived is formed in forty-six days, and, these being multiplied by six, it is born. — But this avails nothing[^p277-11] to the purpose, as it seems; because the body of Christ was perfect from the instant of conception, and the soul perfect, and it had the use of free choice.

On account of this it must be said that a body is perfected in two ways: either by *organization,* or by *bulk.* Bodies other than the body of Christ are built and perfected within that time or in that time, that is, they tend to perfection by *organization* and by *bulk;* but the body of Christ by *bulk* only, not by the distinction of members[^p277-12].
