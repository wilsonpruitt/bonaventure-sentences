# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. IV, Vers. 7-14
## *Determinata opportunitate praedicandi, determinatur hic manifestatio facta per praedicationem*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 291 -->

14. *Venit mulier de Samaria.* Determinata opportunitate praedicandi[^p291-13], determinatur hic *manifestatio* facta per praedicationem. Et quia manifestatio haec primo facta est *specialiter,* secundo vero *communiter;* ideo primo agitur de praedicatione facta *mulieri;* secundo, de praedicatione facta *Samaritanis,* ibi[^p291-14]: *Reliquit ergo mulier hydriam suam* etc.

Et quia non est efficax praedicatio fidei nisi cum consensu liberi arbitrii; et liberum arbitrium non consentit, nisi excitetur a Deo[^p291-15]: ideo Dominus primo mulierem *excitat;* secundo, excitatae et
<!-- page 292 -->
consentienti *veritatem fidei annuntiat,* ibi[^p292-1]: *Dicit ad eum mulier: Domine, da mihi hanc aquam* etc. — Excitat igitur Dominus mulierem hoc ordine, scilicet primo *petendo obsequium;* secundo, *promittendo* vel offerendo *beneficium;* tertio, *explicando beneficium oblatum.*

15. (Vers. 7.). Primo excitat *postulando obsequium potus,* quem mulier ministrare poterat, quia aquam haurire venerat. Propterea dicit: *Venit mulier de Samaria haurire aquam;* et ita potum dare poterat, et Christus indigebat et ideo petebat. Ideo sequitur: *Dicit ei Iesus: Da mihi bibere.* Indigebat quidem potu, quia non erat qui ministraret; unde dicit:

16. (Vers. 8.). *Discipuli autem*[^p292-2] *eius abierant in civitatem, ut cibos emerent.* Bene dicit *emerent,* non gratis quaererent, quia non fuisset ibi qui daret; unde dicitur Lucae nono: « Intraverunt discipuli in civitatem Samaritanorum, ut pararent illi; et non receperunt eum, quia facies eius erat euntis in Ierusalem ».

17. (Vers. 9.). *Dicit ergo ei mulier.* Hic secundo excitat eam *promittendo* vel offerendo *beneficium;* quia mulier considerans, ipsum esse Iudaeum, noluit impendere obsequium, sed magis quaestionem ingerit. Propterea ait: *Dicit ergo ei mulier illa Samaritana,* a qua scilicet potum petiverat: *Quomodo tu, Iudaeus cum sis, bibere a me poscis, quae sum mulier Samaritana?* « Ipsum esse Iudaeum ex habitu cognovit », ut Glossa[^p292-3] dicit. Et rationem huius quaestionis subiungit Evangelista: *Non enim coutuntur Iudaei Samaritanis,* ipsos immundos reputantes, quia gentiles erant; unde Genesis quadragesimo tertio[^p292-4]: « Illicitum est Aegyptiis comedere cum Hebraeis, et profanum putant huiuscemodi convivium ». Quia ergo mulier non fuerat mota ad obsequium faciendum, allicit eam Dominus promittendo beneficium; propter quod dicit:

18. (Vers. 10.). *Respondit Iesus et dixit ei: Si scires donum Dei. Donum Dei* est Spiritus sanctus, de quo Actuum secundo[^p292-5]: « Poenitemini, et baptizetur unusquisque vestrum, et accipietis donum sancti Spiritus ». Spiritus sanctus dicitur *donum,* quia non debet vendi; Actuum octavo: « Pecunia tua tecum sit in perditionem, quoniam donum Dei existimasti pecunia possideri ». Si hoc, inquam, cognosceres, *et quis est, qui dicit tibi: Da mihi bibere,* quoniam scilicet ipse dator sancti Spiritus; non fecisses quaestionem, sed doni postulationem. Ideo dicit: *Tu forsitan petisses ab eo; forsitan* non est nota dubitationis, sed libertatis arbitrii[^p292-6]; et ille petitioni tuae satisfecisset. Ideo dicit: *Et ille dedisset tibi aquam vivam; tibi,* petenti, *dedisset;* Matthaei septimo[^p292-7]: « Omnis, qui petit, accipit, et qui quaerit invenit, et pulsanti aperietur ». *Aquam vivam* vocat gratiam fidei, quae vivere facit; Habacuc secundo: « Iustus meus ex fide vivit ».

19. (Vers. 11.). *Dicit ei mulier.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *explicatio doni* vel beneficii oblati. Mulier enim carnaliter de dono temporali intelligebat; et propterea dubitabat et explicari petebat. Propter hoc ait: *Dicit ei mulier: Domine, neque in quo haurias habes, et puteus altus est;* et ideo non potes dare de aqua putei huius; sed etiam aliunde quomodo habere potes? Unde sequitur: *Unde ergo habes aquam vivam?* ista scilicet nobiliorem.

20. (Vers. 12.). *Nunquid tu maior es patre nostro Iacob?* Simile infra octavo[^p292-8]: « Nunquid tu maior patre nostro Abraham »? *Qui dedit nobis puteum;* ideo dedit, quia invenit. De hoc puteo Genesis vigesimo nono: « Profectus est Iacob, venit in terram orientalem et vidit ibi puteum, cuius os grandi lapide claudebatur ». Nec tantum aliis dedit, sed etiam ad proprium usum habuit, quasi meliorem non habens. Ideo dicit: *Et ipse ex eo bibit et filii eius et pecora eius.* Habetur Genesis vigesimo nono[^p292-9], quod Iacob ibi adaquavit pecora. Mulier ergo, carnaliter intelligens et Domini potentiam nesciens, non alliciebatur. Ideo explicat Christus donum; ideo dicit:

21. (Vers. 13.). *Respondit Iesus et dixit ei: Omnis, qui biberit ex aqua hac,* scilicet visibili et materiali, *sitiet iterum;* et ideo non est magnum hanc dare, nec ego hanc promitto. *Qui autem biberit ex aqua, quam ego dabo ei, non sitiet in aeternum;* infra sexto[^p292-10]: « Qui credit in me non sitiet in aeternum ». Et ratio subditur, quare non sitiet:

22. (Vers. 14.). *Sed aqua, quam ego dabo ei, fiet in eo fons aquae salientis in vitam aeternam; salientis,* quia illuc salire facit, ubi est fons *indeficiens;* Psalmus[^p292-11]: « Apud te est fons vitae » etc.; fons etiam *perfecte reficiens;* unde in Psalmo:
<!-- page 293 -->
« Inebriabuntur ab ubertate domus tuae, et torrente voluptatis tuae potabis eos ». Ad hanc aquam invitat Dominus Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto: « Omnes sitientes, venite ad aquas; et qui non habet argentum » etc. — Sic igitur mulierem excitavit *petendo obsequium, offerendo beneficium, explicando donum oblatum.*

### Quaestiones.

23. Quaest. I. Quaeritur hic: cum Dominus praecipiat discipulis suis, ut in *civitates Samaritanorum* non ingrediantur nec *in viam gentium,* Matthaei decimo[^p293-1], et etiam Matthaei decimo quinto dicat: *Non sum missus nisi ad oves, quae perierunt domus Israel;* unde est hoc, quod Dominus modo praedicat Samaritanis? Videtur enim contraire praecepto suo, et ex hoc malum exemplum dare.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod hoc Dominus discipulis prohibuit, non quia malum esset, sed ne Iudaeos, ad quos primo venerat, scandalizaret. Ideo praecepit, quod non ex proposito principali ad eos irent, unde nec Dominus se ingessit. Sed quia *vult, omnes homines salvos fieri*[^p293-2], et opportunitas se obtulit, et mulier ad eum venit; ideo verbum praedicationis aperuit.

24. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur: 1. Cum Iudaei reputent Samaritanos immundos, et Dominus Legem custodiebat; unde est hoc, quod a Samaritana bibere petebat?

2. Praeterea, ipse sciebat eius responsionem, antequam peteret; et sciebat, quod ipsa potum non daret: ergo frustra et stulte petebat.

Respondet Chrysostomus[^p293-3], quod Dominus reputabat pro indifferenti huiusmodi observantias custodire, ut dicitur Matthaei decimo quinto, quod nihil extrinsecum inquinat hominem; et ideo non dimisit petere ab illa, quamvis esset Samaritana.

2. Quod obiicis, quod sciebat, quod non daret; dicendum, quod sciebat, quod ei prudenter responderet, et ut ex illa responsione occasionem docendi haberet, ideo petiit.

25. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *discipuli iverant in civitatem, ut cibos emerent.*

**Contra:** 1. Discipuli erant Iudaei: ergo non debebant Samaritanis communicare.

2. Item, Dominus praecepit eis, ut non habeant argentum, Matthaei decimo[^p293-4]; quomodo igitur iverunt emere, si pecuniam non habebant?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod discipuli didicerant a Domino, nihil esse immundum vel[^p293-5] commune. — Vel dicendum, quod aliqui cibi erant indifferentes quoad Iudaeos et Samaritanos, ut panis et poma; et tales cibos iverant emere.

2. Quod obiicit, quod non habebant, unde emerent; dicendum, quod non adhuc missi erant miracula facere et praedicare. — Praeterea erant in terra ignota, ubi panem et alios cibos gratis non invenirent; ideo pro necessitatis tempore argentum portabant.

26. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod mulier dicit: *Nunquid tu maior es patre nostro Iacob? — Contra:* Ex hoc videtur implicare, quod Iacob esset pater Samaritanorum; et hoc falsum est, quia ipsi missi fuerant a rege Assyriorum[^p293-6].

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *pater* aliquando dicitur propter *temporis antiquitatem,* sicut dicimus senes viros *patres;* aliquando *auctoritate,* sic praelatos et viros authenticos dicimus *patres;* aliquando *beneficio* vel *educatione,* sic *patres* dicimus, a quibus beneficia percipimus; aliquando *generatione,* sic illos qui genuerunt[^p293-7]. Mulier dicit Iacob *patrem* tribus rationibus praehabitis.

27. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Qui biberit ex aqua, quam ego dabo ei, non sitiet in aeternum. — Contra:* Aut intelligit de siti *corporali,* aut de *spirituali;* si de *corporali,* falsum, quia sitiunt qui gratiam habent; si de *spirituali,* adhuc falsum videtur; Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto[^p293-8]: « Qui bibunt adhuc sitient ».

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod de siti spirituali loquitur[^p293-9]. Quaedam enim sitis est *non habiti,* ut habeatur; quaedam *habiti,* ut amplius habeatur; quaedam est *perfecte habiti,* ut continuetur. Prima est in concupiscentia; secunda, in gratia; tertia vero, in gloria. Hic loquitur de prima siti, ideo etc. Sitis enim gratiae non est rei *non habitae;* quia, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p293-10], qui habet donum aeternum, amando habet, licet non *perfecte.*

## English
<!-- page 291 -->

14. *There cometh a woman of Samaria.* The opportunity for preaching having been determined[^p291-13], here is determined *the manifestation* made through preaching. And because this manifestation was made first *specially,* but second *commonly;* therefore first there is treated the preaching made *to the woman;* second, the preaching made *to the Samaritans,* at the place[^p291-14]: *The woman therefore left her waterpot* etc.

And because the preaching of faith is not efficacious except with the consent of free choice; and free choice does not consent unless it be stirred up by God[^p291-15]: therefore the Lord first *stirs up* the woman; second, to her stirred up and
<!-- page 292 -->
consenting he *announces the truth of faith,* at the place[^p292-1]: *The woman saith to him: Sir, give me this water* etc. — The Lord therefore stirs up the woman in this order, namely first *by asking a service;* second, *by promising* or offering *a benefit;* third, *by unfolding the benefit offered.*

15. (Verse 7.). First he stirs her up *by asking the service of a drink,* which the woman was able to minister, because she had come to draw water. On account of this he says: *There cometh a woman of Samaria to draw water;* and so she was able to give a drink, and Christ was in need, and therefore he asked. Therefore there follows: *Jesus saith to her: Give me to drink.* He was indeed in need of a drink, because there was no one to minister to him; whence he says:

16. (Verse 8.). *Now his disciples*[^p292-2] *were gone into the city, to buy meats.* He well says *buy,* not seek for nothing, because there would not have been anyone there to give; whence it is said in Luke, the ninth chapter: « The disciples entered into the city of the Samaritans, to prepare for him; and they received him not, because his face was of one going to Jerusalem ».

17. (Verse 9.). *Then the woman saith to him.* Here, second, he stirs her up *by promising* or offering *a benefit;* because the woman, considering that he was a Jew, would not render the service, but rather thrusts in a question. On account of this he says: *Then that Samaritan woman saith to him,* namely she of whom he had asked a drink: *How dost thou, being a Jew, ask of me to drink, who am a Samaritan woman?* « She knew that he was a Jew from his dress », as the Gloss[^p292-3] says. And the Evangelist adds the reason for this question: *For the Jews do not communicate with the Samaritans,* reputing them unclean, because they were Gentiles; whence Genesis, the forty-third chapter[^p292-4]: « It is unlawful for the Egyptians to eat with the Hebrews, and they think such a feast profane ». Since, therefore, the woman had not been moved to do the service, the Lord entices her by promising a benefit; on account of which he says:

18. (Verse 10.). *Jesus answered and said to her: If thou didst know the gift of God. The gift of God* is the Holy Spirit, of whom Acts, the second chapter[^p292-5]: « Repent, and be baptized every one of you, and you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit ». The Holy Spirit is called *a gift,* because he must not be sold; Acts, the eighth chapter: « Thy money be with thee unto perdition, because thou hast thought that the gift of God may be possessed with money ». If this, I say, thou didst know, *and who he is that saith to thee: Give me to drink,* namely that he himself is the giver of the Holy Spirit; thou wouldst not have made a question, but a request for the gift. Therefore he says: *Thou perhaps wouldst have asked of him; perhaps* is not a mark of doubt, but of the freedom of choice[^p292-6]; and he would have satisfied thy request. Therefore he says: *And he would have given thee living water; thee,* asking, *he would have given;* Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p292-7]: « Every one that asketh receiveth, and he that seeketh findeth, and to him that knocketh it shall be opened ». *Living water* he calls the grace of faith, which makes one live; Habacuc, the second chapter: « My just man liveth by faith ».

19. (Verse 11.). *The woman saith to him.* Here there is touched on the third, namely *the unfolding of the gift* or of the benefit offered. For the woman understood carnally, of a temporal gift; and on account of this she doubted and asked that it be unfolded. On account of this she says: *The woman saith to him: Sir, thou hast nothing wherein to draw, and the well is deep;* and therefore thou canst not give of the water of this well; but how canst thou have it from elsewhere? Whence there follows: *From whence then hast thou living water?* namely, nobler than this.

20. (Verse 12.). *Art thou greater than our father Jacob?* Similar is below, the eighth chapter[^p292-8]: « Art thou greater than our father Abraham »? *Who gave us the well;* he gave it for this reason, because he found it. Of this well Genesis, the twenty-ninth chapter: « Jacob set out, came into the east country, and saw there a well, the mouth whereof was closed with a great stone ». Nor did he only give it to others, but he also had it for his own use, as not having a better. Therefore she says: *And he himself drank of it, and his children and his cattle.* It is had in Genesis, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p292-9], that Jacob watered his cattle there. The woman therefore, understanding carnally and not knowing the power of the Lord, was not enticed. Therefore Christ unfolds the gift; therefore he says:

21. (Verse 13.). *Jesus answered and said to her: Every one that drinketh of this water,* namely visible and material, *shall thirst again;* and therefore it is not a great thing to give this, nor do I promise this. *But he that shall drink of the water that I will give him, shall not thirst for ever;* below, the sixth chapter[^p292-10]: « He that believeth in me shall not thirst for ever ». And the reason is added why he shall not thirst:

22. (Verse 14.). *But the water that I will give him, shall become in him a fountain of water springing up into life everlasting; springing up,* because it makes one spring up thither where there is an *unfailing* fountain; the Psalm[^p292-11]: « With thee is the fountain of life » etc.; a fountain also *perfectly refreshing;* whence in the Psalm:
<!-- page 293 -->
« They shall be inebriated with the plenty of thy house, and thou shalt make them drink of the torrent of thy pleasure ». To this water the Lord invites in Isaias, the fifty-fifth chapter: « All you that thirst, come to the waters; and he that hath not money » etc. — Thus, then, he stirred up the woman *by asking a service, by offering a benefit, by unfolding the gift offered.*

### Questions.

23. Question I. It is asked here: since the Lord commands his disciples that they should not enter into *the cities of the Samaritans* nor *into the way of the Gentiles,* Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p293-1], and also, in Matthew, the fifteenth chapter, says: *I was not sent but to the sheep that are lost of the house of Israel;* whence is it that the Lord now preaches to the Samaritans? For he seems to go against his own command, and from this to give a bad example.

**I respond:** It must be said that the Lord forbade this to the disciples, not because it was evil, but lest he should scandalize the Jews, to whom he had first come. Therefore he commanded that they should not go to them of principal purpose, whence neither did the Lord thrust himself upon them. But because *he wills all men to be saved*[^p293-2], and the opportunity offered itself, and the woman came to him; therefore he opened the word of preaching.

24. Question II. Likewise it is asked: 1. Since the Jews repute the Samaritans unclean, and the Lord kept the Law; whence is it that he asked to drink from the Samaritan woman?

2. Further, he knew her answer before he asked; and he knew that she would not give the drink: therefore he asked in vain and foolishly.

Chrysostom answers[^p293-3] that the Lord reputed it a thing indifferent to keep observances of this kind, as it is said in Matthew, the fifteenth chapter, that nothing extrinsic defiles a man; and therefore he did not forbear to ask of her, although she was a Samaritan.

2. As to what thou objectest, that he knew that she would not give; it must be said that he knew that she would answer him prudently, and that from that answer he might have an occasion of teaching, therefore he asked.

25. Question III. Likewise it is asked about this, that he says that *the disciples had gone into the city, to buy meats.*

**On the contrary:** 1. The disciples were Jews: therefore they ought not to communicate with the Samaritans.

2. Likewise, the Lord commanded them that they should not have money, Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p293-4]; how then did they go to buy, if they did not have money?

**I respond:** It must be said that the disciples had learned from the Lord that nothing is unclean or[^p293-5] common. — Or it must be said that some foods were indifferent as regards the Jews and the Samaritans, such as bread and fruits; and such foods they had gone to buy.

2. As to what he objects, that they did not have the wherewithal to buy; it must be said that they had not yet been sent to work miracles and to preach. — Moreover, they were in an unknown land, where they would not find bread and other foods for nothing; therefore for the time of necessity they carried money.

26. Question IV. Likewise it is asked about this, that the woman says: *Art thou greater than our father Jacob? — On the contrary:* From this she seems to imply that Jacob was the father of the Samaritans; and this is false, because they had been sent by the king of the Assyrians[^p293-6].

**I respond:** It must be said that one is sometimes called *father* on account of *antiquity of time,* as we call old men *fathers;* sometimes by *authority,* thus we call prelates and authoritative men *fathers;* sometimes by *benefit* or by *upbringing,* thus we call *fathers* those from whom we receive benefits; sometimes by *generation,* thus those who begot[^p293-7]. The woman calls Jacob *father* for the three reasons first had.

27. Question V. Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *He that shall drink of the water that I will give him, shall not thirst for ever. — On the contrary:* Either he understands it of *bodily* thirst, or of *spiritual;* if of *bodily,* it is false, because those who have grace thirst; if of *spiritual,* it still seems false; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p293-8]: « They that drink shall yet thirst ».

**I respond:** It must be said that he speaks of spiritual thirst[^p293-9]. For one thirst is of a thing *not had,* that it may be had; another of a thing *had,* that it may be had more fully; another is of a thing *perfectly had,* that it may be continued. The first is in concupiscence; the second, in grace; but the third, in glory. Here he speaks of the first thirst, therefore etc. For the thirst of grace is not of a thing *not had;* because, as Augustine says[^p293-10], he who has the eternal gift has it by loving, although not *perfectly.*
