## Latin

### Capitulum XIV. *Non turbetur cor vestrum.*

### Collatio LII.

(Vers. 9.). *Tanto tempore vobiscum sum, et non cognovistis me.*

1. In verbis istis duo notantur: *assiduitas divinae liberalitatis,* cum dicitur: *Tanto tempore vobiscum sum* etc. — et *illiberalitas humanae ingratitudinis,* cum dicitur: *Et non cognovistis me.*

2. Memorans igitur suam liberalitatem ait: *Tanto tempore vobiscum sum.* Circa primum nota[^p601-9], quod Dominus fuit nobiscum tripliciter, videlicet *corporaliter, sacramentaliter* et *spiritualiter.* — Nobiscum
<!-- page 602 -->
fuit *corporaliter* in carne, quasi manens in eadem *domo;* Baruch tertio[^p602-1]: « Post haec in terris visus est et cum hominibus conversatus est »; Isaiae septimo: « Vocabitur nomen eius *Emmanuel,* quod est nobiscum Deus ». — Nobiscum est *sacramentaliter* in altari, quasi sedens in eadem *mensa;* Matthaei ultimo[^p602-2]: « Ecce, ego vobiscum sum omnibus diebus usque ad consummationem saeculi ». — Nobiscum est *spiritualiter* in mente, quasi dormiens in eodem *thalamo;* secundae ad Corinthios decimo tertio: « Pacem habete, et Deus pacis erit vobiscum ».

3. Sequitur: *Et non cognovistis me;* ubi reprehenditur *ingratitudo humana,* quae tam liberaliter exhibentem se non vult cognoscere. Nota autem, quod Dominus tripliciter cognoscitur a iustis. Cognoscitur enim aliquis ex *visu* solo, aliquis ex *colloquio,* aliquis ex *obsequio.* Dicimus enim, nos cognoscere aliquem, quem vidimus; et magis eum, cum quo locuti sumus; et maxime eum, apud quem obsequia et fecimus et accepimus. — Dominus ergo cognoscitur quasi per *visum* ab *omnibus credentibus;* Ieremiae trigesimo primo[^p602-3]: « Dabo legem meam in visceribus eorum » etc.; et sequitur: « Non docebit ultra vir proximum suum dicens: cognosce Dominum; omnes enim cognoscent me a minimo usque ad maximum ». — Cognoscitur etiam quasi per *colloquium,* et hoc a *contemplativis,* qui audiunt colloquia divinae familiaritatis; Ieremiae nono[^p602-4]: « In hoc glorietur, quicumque gloriatur, scire et nosse me »; et Ioannis decimo quarto: « Vos autem cognoscetis eum, quia apud vos manebit et in vobis erit ». — Cognoscitur etiam quasi per *obsequium,* et hoc ab *activis,* qui Deo impendunt obsequium bonae operationis; primae Ioannis secundo[^p602-5]: « In hoc scimus, quoniam cognoscimus eum, si mandata eius observemus ». — Sic ergo Deus cognoscitur per *fidem* quasi per visum, per *caritatem* quasi per familiare colloquium, per *obedientiam* quasi per obsequium.

4. *Et vos non cognovistis me.* Non cognovistis, quia non *credidistis;* Ioannis primo[^p602-6]: « In mundo erat, et mundus per ipsum factus est, et mundus eum non cognovit ». — Item non cognovistis, quia non *dilexistis;* primae Ioannis quarto: « Qui non diligit » non habet caritatem « et non novit Deum, quia Deus caritas est ». — Item non cognovistis, quia non *obedistis;* primae Ioannis secundo: « Qui dicit, se nosse Deum, et mandata eius non custodit, mendax est ».

### Annotatio 57.

(Vers. 6.). *Ego sum via, veritas et vita.*

Ego sum *via* dirigens effectum; Proverbiorum quarto[^p602-7]: « Viam sapientiae monstrabo tibi, ducam te per semitas aequitatis » etc. *Veritas,* intellectum docens; Matthaei vigesimo secundo: « Scimus, quia verax es et viam Dei in veritate doces ». *Vita,* affectum pascens; Ioannis decimo: « Per me si quis introierit, salvabitur, et ingredietur et egredietur et pascua inveniet ». — Item, ego sum *via* non errans, quam nescierunt, de quibus Psalmus[^p602-8]: « Erraverunt in invio et non in via ». *Veritas* non fallens; Psalmus: « Omnia mandata eius veritas »; Psalmus: « Fidelis Dominus in omnibus verbis suis ». *Vita* indeficiens; Lucae decimo: « Maria optimam partem elegit, quae non auferetur ab ea ». — Item, ego sum *via* in *exemplo;* Ioannis decimo tertio[^p602-9]: « Exemplum dedi vobis » etc. *Veritas* in *promisso;* Psalmus: « Quae procedunt de labiis meis non faciam irrita ». *Vita* in *praemio;* ad Romanos sexto: « Stipendia peccati, mors; gratia autem Dei, vita aeterna ».

### Collatio LIII.

(Vers. 16. 17.). *Ego rogabo Patrem, et alium Paracletum dabit vobis, ut maneat vobiscum in aeternum, Spiritum veritatis* etc.

1. In verbis istis promittitur Spiritus sanctus habendus in *advocatum* — habendus in *socium* — et habendus in *magistrum.* Hominem enim in ista vita[^p602-10] premit triplex malum: malum *ignorantiae* et malum *culpae* et malum *poenae.* Malum *ignorantiae* premit rationem; malum *culpae,* voluntatem; malum *poenae,* virtutem. Propter malum *ignorantiae* indiget *magistro;* propter malum *poenae* indiget *solatio;* propter reatum *culpae* indiget *advocato.* — Propterea promittit Dominus Iesus Spiritum sanctum ut *advocatum,* cum dicit: *Alium Paracletum dabit vobis;* promittit ut socium sive *solatium,* cum dicit: *ut maneat vobiscum in aeternum;* promittit etiam ut *magistrum,* cum adiungit: *Spiritum veritatis;* et haec omnia nobis promittuntur fienda per Christum. Unde ipse de omnibus his promittit: *Ego rogabo Patrem.*

2. *Alium,* inquit, *Paracletum dabit vobis.* Nota, quod in causa nostra triplicem dedit nobis Dominus *advocatum.* Dedit enim nobis advocatum ad
<!-- page 603 -->
*propugnandum* et advocatum ad *proloquendum* et advocatum ad *intercedendum.* Propter haec tria solent homines quaerere advocatum. — Primus advocatus *Christus* est; primae Ioannis secundo[^p603-1]: « Si quis peccaverit, advocatum habemus apud Patrem, Iesum Christum iustum, et ipse est propitiatio pro peccatis nostris »; et iste advocatus est ad *propugnandum;* unde Isaiae sexagesimo tertio: « Ego, qui loquor iustitiam, et propugnator sum ad salvandum ».

3. Secundus advocatus est *Spiritus sanctus;* de quo hic dicitur: *Alium Paracletum,* id est advocatum, *dabit,* et iste advocatus est ad *proloquendum;* unde ad Romanos octavo[^p603-2]: « Quid oremus, sicut oportet, nescimus, sed ipse Spiritus postulat pro nobis gemitibus inenarrabilibus »; et Matthaei decimo: « Non vos estis, qui loquimini, sed Spiritus Patris vestri, qui loquitur in vobis ».

4. Tertius advocatus est *beatissima Virgo.* Haec est advocata ad *intercedendum,* quae, ut magis moveat iudicem, femineum praefert sexum. Haec est illa Esther, ad cuius aspectum « convertitur cor regis in mansuetudinem », secundum quod figuratum est Esther decimo quinto[^p603-3]. Haec est illa, cui canit Ecclesia: « Eia ergo, advocata nostra ». — Habentes ergo advocatum ad *propugnandum* Filium, advocatum ad *proloquendum* Spiritum, advocatum ad *intercedendum* Reginam virginum, committamus eis secure causam nostram quantum ad ea quae *iuris* sunt; si tamen emendaverimus *factum,* quia in his quae sunt facti, non redimit advocatus errorem clientum[^p603-4].

5. Sequitur: *Ut maneat vobiscum in aeternum,* ubi promittitur nobis Spiritus in socium sive in *solatium,* de cuius mansione require supra capitulo primo[^p603-5].

6. Tertio promittitur nobis Spiritus in *magistrum,* cum dicitur: *Spiritum veritatis.* Ideo enim dicitur *Spiritus veritatis,* quia docet veritatem. Et quia magister iste non solum *docet* veritatem ad intelligendum, sed *suggerit* ad volendum et *adiuvat* ad exsequendum; ideo nota, quod aliquando dicitur Spiritus *veritatis,* quia docet veritatem; aliquando Spiritus *sanctitatis,* quia ad bonum movet voluntatem; aliquando Spiritus *virtutis,* quia adiuvat virtutem. — Dabit ergo nobis Spiritum *veritatis* ad docendum; de quo Ioannis decimo sexto[^p603-6]: « Cum venerit ille Spiritus veritatis, docebit vos omnem veritatem ». — Dabit Spiritum *sanctitatis* ad suggerendum; Ioannis decimo quarto: « Paracletus Spiritus sanctus, quem mittet Pater in nomine meo, ille vos docebit omnia, et suggeret vobis omnia, quaecumque dixero vobis ». — Dabit etiam nobis Spiritum *virtutis* ad adiuvandum; Actuum primo[^p603-7]: « Accipietis virtutem Spiritus sancti supervenientis in vos, et eritis mihi testes » etc. Hic est « Spiritus, qui adiuvat infirmitatem nostram », ad Romanos octavo.

### Collatio LIV.

(Vers. 23.). *Si quis diligit me, sermonem meum servabit.*

1. In verbis istis primo praemittit magister caritatis verae *dilectionis indicium,* cum dicit: *Si quis diligit me, sermonem meum servabit.* — Secundo promittit eiusdem *dilectionis fructum,* cum subiungit: *Et Pater meus diliget eum* etc.

*Indicium* ergo dilectionis est *observantia sermonis;* nec solum hoc, sed etiam cum hoc *dimissio terrenae possessionis,* et etiam *sustinentia temporalis tribulationis.* Solet enim probari dilectio largitione doni, exhibitione servitii et tolerantia damni.

2. Probatur ergo dilectio *largitione doni,* quando homo dat sive dimittit omnia amore Christi; unde Canticorum octavo[^p603-8]: « Si dederit homo omnem substantiam suam pro dilectione, quasi nihil despiciet eam ». — Item, probatur *exhibitione servitii;* unde hic: *Si quis diligit me* etc.; et supra eodem: « Si diligitis me, mandata mea servate ». — Probatur etiam *tolerantia damni;* Proverbiorumduodecimo[^p603-9]: « Qui negligit damnum propter amicum iustus est »; ideo dicitur Ioannis decimo quinto: « Maiorem dilectionem nemo habet, quam ut animam suam ponat quis pro amicis suis ».

3. *Sermonem meum servabit.* — Nota, quod servandus est sermo Christi in intellectu per *memoriam,* in affectu per *dilectionem,* in effectu per *exsecutionem.* — De primo in Psalmo[^p603-10]: « Iustitia illius in filios filiorum his qui servant testamentum eius et memores sunt mandatorum ipsius ad faciendum ea ». — De secundo in Psalmo: « Custodivit anima mea testimonia tua et dilexit ea vehementer »; et subiungit: « Servavi mandata tua ». — De tertio Matthaei decimo nono: « Si vis ad vitam ingredi, serva mandata ».

4. Sequitur: *Et Pater meus diliget eum, et ad eum veniemus* etc.; ubi promittit Dominus *dilectionis fructum.* Et nota, quod est triplex fructus observantiae mandatorum. Primus est *evasio poenae;* Ioannis octavo[^p603-11]: « Si quis sermonem meum servaverit,
<!-- page 604 -->
mortem non videbit in aeternum ». — Secundus est *adeptio gratiae;* de quo hic: *Et Pater meus diliget eum, et ad eum veniemus* etc., scilicet per gratiam. — Tertius est *meritum gloriae;* de quo Matthaei decimo nono: « Si vis ad vitam ingredi, serva mandata ».

5. *Ad eum,* inquit, *veniemus.* Nota, quod Deus venit libenter, ubi *diligitur;* unde hic: *Si quis diligit me* etc.; ubi *invitatur;* Sapientiae septimo[^p604-1]: « Invocavi, et venit in me spiritus sapientiae »; ubi *exspectatur;* Habacuc secundo: « Si moram fecerit, exspecta eum, quia veniens veniet et non tardabit[^p604-2] ».

## English

### Chapter XIV. *Let not your heart be troubled.*

### Collation LII.

(Vers. 9.). *So long a time I am with you, and you have not known me.*

1. In these words two things are noted: *the assiduity of the divine liberality,* when it is said: *So long a time I am with you* etc. — and *the illiberality of human ingratitude,* when it is said: *And you have not known me.*

2. Recalling therefore his liberality, he says: *So long a time I am with you.* Concerning the first, note[^p601-9] that the Lord was with us in three ways, namely *bodily, sacramentally* and *spiritually.* — With us
<!-- page 602 -->
he was *bodily* in the flesh, as one remaining in the same *house;* Baruch, the third chapter[^p602-1]: « Afterwards he was seen upon earth, and conversed with men »; Isaias, the seventh chapter: « His name shall be called *Emmanuel,* which is God with us ». — With us he is *sacramentally* on the altar, as one sitting at the same *table;* Matthew, the last chapter[^p602-2]: « Behold, I am with you all days, even to the consummation of the world ». — With us he is *spiritually* in the mind, as one sleeping in the same *bridal chamber;* the second to the Corinthians, the thirteenth chapter: « Have peace, and the God of peace shall be with you ».

3. There follows: *And you have not known me;* where *human ingratitude* is reproved, which will not know him who shows himself so liberally. But note that the Lord is known by the just in three ways. For one is known from *sight* alone, one from *converse,* one from *service.* For we say that we know someone whom we have seen; and him more, with whom we have spoken; and him most, with whom we have both done and received services. — The Lord therefore is known as it were by *sight* by *all believers;* Jeremias, the thirty-first chapter[^p602-3]: « I will give my law in their bowels » etc.; and there follows: « A man shall teach no more his neighbour, saying: Know the Lord; for all shall know me from the least even to the greatest ». — He is known also as it were by *converse,* and this by the *contemplatives,* who hear the converse of divine familiarity; Jeremias, the ninth chapter[^p602-4]: « In this let him glory, whosoever glorieth, to understand and know me »; and John, the fourteenth chapter: « But you shall know him, because he shall remain with you and shall be in you ». — He is known also as it were by *service,* and this by the *active,* who render to God the service of good working; the first of John, the second chapter[^p602-5]: « In this we know that we know him, if we observe his commandments ». — Thus therefore God is known through *faith* as it were by sight, through *charity* as it were by familiar converse, through *obedience* as it were by service.

4. *And you have not known me.* You have not known, because you have not *believed;* John, the first chapter[^p602-6]: « He was in the world, and the world was made by him, and the world knew him not ». — Likewise you have not known, because you have not *loved;* the first of John, the fourth chapter: « He that loveth not » hath not charity « and knoweth not God, for God is charity ». — Likewise you have not known, because you have not *obeyed;* the first of John, the second chapter: « He who saith that he knoweth God, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar ».

### Annotation 57.

(Vers. 6.). *I am the way, the truth and the life.*

I am the *way,* directing the effect; Proverbs, the fourth chapter[^p602-7]: « I will shew thee the way of wisdom, I will lead thee by the paths of equity » etc. The *truth,* teaching the understanding; Matthew, the twenty-second chapter: « We know that thou art true, and teachest the way of God in truth ». The *life,* feeding the affection; John, the tenth chapter: « By me, if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and he shall go in and go out, and shall find pastures ». — Likewise, I am the *way* that does not stray, which they knew not of whom the Psalm[^p602-8]: « They strayed in a pathless place, and not in the way ». The *truth* that does not deceive; the Psalm: « All his commandments are truth »; the Psalm: « The Lord is faithful in all his words ». The *life* unfailing; Luke, the tenth chapter: « Mary hath chosen the best part, which shall not be taken away from her ». — Likewise, I am the *way* in *example;* John, the thirteenth chapter[^p602-9]: « I have given you an example » etc. The *truth* in *promise;* the Psalm: « The things that proceed from my lips I will not make void ». The *life* in *reward;* to the Romans, the sixth chapter: « The wages of sin is death; but the grace of God, life everlasting ».

### Collation LIII.

(Vers. 16. 17.). *I will ask the Father, and he shall give you another Paraclete, that he may remain with you for ever, the Spirit of truth* etc.

1. In these words the Holy Spirit is promised, to be had as an *advocate* — to be had as a *companion* — and to be had as a *teacher.* For in this life[^p602-10] a threefold evil presses upon man: the evil of *ignorance* and the evil of *fault* and the evil of *punishment.* The evil of *ignorance* presses upon the reason; the evil of *fault,* upon the will; the evil of *punishment,* upon the power. On account of the evil of *ignorance* he needs a *teacher;* on account of the evil of *punishment* he needs *solace;* on account of the guilt of *fault* he needs an *advocate.* — Therefore the Lord Jesus promises the Holy Spirit as an *advocate,* when he says: *He shall give you another Paraclete;* he promises him as a companion or *solace,* when he says: *that he may remain with you for ever;* he promises him also as a *teacher,* when he adds: *the Spirit of truth;* and all these things are promised to us to be brought about through Christ. Whence he himself promises concerning all these: *I will ask the Father.*

2. *Another,* he says, *Paraclete he shall give you.* Note that in our cause the Lord has given us a threefold *advocate.* For he has given us an advocate for
<!-- page 603 -->
*defending* and an advocate for *pleading* and an advocate for *interceding.* For these three things men are wont to seek an advocate. — The first advocate is *Christ;* the first of John, the second chapter[^p603-1]: « If any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the just, and he is the propitiation for our sins »; and this advocate is for *defending;* whence Isaias, the sixty-third chapter: « I, that speak justice, and am a defender to save ».

3. The second advocate is the *Holy Spirit;* of whom it is said here: *Another Paraclete,* that is, advocate, *he shall give,* and this advocate is for *pleading;* whence to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p603-2]: « We know not what we should pray for as we ought, but the Spirit himself asketh for us with unspeakable groanings »; and Matthew, the tenth chapter: « It is not you that speak, but the Spirit of your Father that speaketh in you ».

4. The third advocate is the *most blessed Virgin.* She is the advocate for *interceding,* who, that she may move the judge the more, puts forward her womanly sex. She is that Esther, at whose look « the heart of the king is turned into mildness », according as it is figured in Esther, the fifteenth chapter[^p603-3]. She it is to whom the Church sings: « Come then, our advocate ». — Having therefore an advocate for *defending,* the Son, an advocate for *pleading,* the Spirit, an advocate for *interceding,* the Queen of virgins, let us commit our cause to them securely as to those things which are of *right;* if only we have amended the *deed,* because in those things which are of deed the advocate does not redeem the error of his clients[^p603-4].

5. There follows: *That he may remain with you for ever,* where the Spirit is promised to us as a companion or as a *solace,* concerning whose remaining look above, at the first chapter[^p603-5].

6. Thirdly the Spirit is promised to us as a *teacher,* when it is said: *the Spirit of truth.* For he is called *the Spirit of truth* for this reason, that he teaches the truth. And because this teacher not only *teaches* the truth for understanding, but *suggests* it for willing and *helps* for carrying it out; therefore note that sometimes he is called the Spirit *of truth,* because he teaches the truth; sometimes the Spirit *of holiness,* because he moves the will to good; sometimes the Spirit *of power,* because he helps the power. — He will give us therefore the Spirit *of truth* for teaching; of whom John, the sixteenth chapter[^p603-6]: « When that Spirit of truth shall come, he will teach you all truth ». — He will give the Spirit *of holiness* for suggesting; John, the fourteenth chapter: « The Paraclete, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, he will teach you all things, and will suggest to you all things, whatsoever I shall have said to you ». — He will give us also the Spirit *of power* for helping; Acts, the first chapter[^p603-7]: « You shall receive the power of the Holy Spirit coming upon you, and you shall be witnesses unto me » etc. This is « the Spirit, who helpeth our infirmity », to the Romans, the eighth chapter.

### Collation LIV.

(Vers. 23.). *If any one love me, he will keep my word.*

1. In these words the teacher of charity first sets forth the *sign of true dilection,* when he says: *If any one love me, he will keep my word.* — Secondly he promises the *fruit of the same dilection,* when he adds: *And my Father will love him* etc.

The *sign* therefore of dilection is the *observance of the word;* and not this only, but also with this the *giving up of earthly possession,* and also the *enduring of temporal tribulation.* For dilection is wont to be proved by the bestowal of a gift, the showing of service and the bearing of loss.

2. Dilection therefore is proved by the *bestowal of a gift,* when a man gives or gives up all things for the love of Christ; whence Canticles, the eighth chapter[^p603-8]: « If a man should give all his substance for dilection, he shall despise it as nothing ». — Likewise, it is proved by the *showing of service;* whence here: *If any one love me* etc.; and above in the same place: « If you love me, keep my commandments ». — It is proved also by the *bearing of loss;* Proverbs, the twelfth chapter[^p603-9]: « He that neglecteth a loss for the sake of a friend is just »; therefore it is said in John, the fifteenth chapter: « Greater dilection no man hath than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends ».

3. *He will keep my word.* — Note that the word of Christ is to be kept in the understanding through *memory,* in the affection through *dilection,* in the effect through *execution.* — Of the first in the Psalm[^p603-10]: « His justice unto children's children, to such as keep his covenant, and are mindful of his commandments to do them ». — Of the second in the Psalm: « My soul hath kept thy testimonies and hath loved them exceedingly »; and it adds: « I have kept thy commandments ». — Of the third Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « If thou wilt enter into life, keep the commandments ».

4. There follows: *And my Father will love him, and we will come to him* etc.; where the Lord promises the *fruit of dilection.* And note that the fruit of the observance of the commandments is threefold. The first is the *escape from punishment;* John, the eighth chapter[^p603-11]: « If any man keep my word,
<!-- page 604 -->
he shall not see death for ever ». — The second is the *attainment of grace;* of which here: *And my Father will love him, and we will come to him* etc., namely through grace. — The third is the *merit of glory;* of which Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « If thou wilt enter into life, keep the commandments ».

5. *To him,* he says, *we will come.* Note that God comes willingly where he *is loved;* whence here: *If any one love me* etc.; where he *is invited;* Wisdom, the seventh chapter[^p604-1]: « I called upon him, and the spirit of wisdom came upon me »; where he *is waited for;* Habacuc, the second chapter: « If he make any delay, wait for him, for he shall surely come, and he shall not be slack[^p604-2] ».
