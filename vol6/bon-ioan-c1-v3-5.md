# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. I, Vers. 3-5
## *De Verbo in comparatione ad ea quae per ipsum dicuntur*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 248 -->

#### *Secundo, de Verbo in comparatione ad ea quae per ipsum dicuntur.*

9. (Vers. 3.). *Omnia per ipsum facta sunt* etc. Hic describitur Verbum per comparationem *ad id quod per Verbum dicitur*, et hoc quantum ad quatuor conditiones: describit enim ipsum ut principium creationis *sufficiens*, ut *indeficiens*, ut *praecognoscens*, ut aliis *cognitionem praebens*. Et per has quatuor conditiones plures haereses eliduntur.

Primo ergo dicitur ipsum *principium sufficiens*, quia ad omnia producenda sufficit. Ideo dicit: *Omnia per ipsum facta sunt*, scilicet a Deo Patre per Verbum, quia Ecclesiastici decimo octavo[^p248-6]: « Qui vivit in aeternum creavit omnia simul »; et hoc per Verbum, unde in Psalmo: « Dixit, et facta sunt ». Augustinus in libro Confessionum[^p248-7]: « Verbo tibi coaeterno dicis quaecumque dicis, et fit quod dicis, nec aliter quam dicendo facis ».

10. *Et sine ipso factum est nihil.* Hic notatur secunda conditio, scilicet quod est *principium indeficiens*, quia nulli operationi deest. Nam Pater sine Verbo nihil facit; infra quinto[^p248-8]: « Omnia, quae Pater facit, haec et Filius similiter facit ». Similiter creatura facit sine Verbo cooperante nihil; infra decimo quinto: « Sine me nihil potestis facere ». Et in hoc perfecte eliditur haeresis Manichaeorum, qui posuerunt duo principia, et quaedam produci a Deo lucis, quaedam a Deo tenebrarum. Non enim intellexerunt, quomodo *omnia per Verbum facta sunt*[^p248-9].
<!-- page 249 -->

11. (Vers. 4.). *Quod factum est*[^p249-1] *in ipso vita erat.* Hic notatur tertia conditio, scilicet quod est *principium praecognoscens*, quia omnia sunt in ipso, antequam fiant, et in ipso vivunt, sicut arca in mente artificis. Unde Augustinus[^p249-2] dicit, quod Filius est « ars plena omnium rationum viventium »; Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio: « Domino Deo, antequam crearentur, omnia sunt agnita; sic et post perfectum respicit omnia ». Ideo rationes in ipso vivunt, quia non transmutantur; Iacobi primo: « Apud ipsum non est transmutatio nec vicissitudinis obumbratio »; et transmutatio omnis nonnulla mors est.

12. *Et vita erat lux hominum.* Hic tangitur quarta conditio, quae est, quod est aliis *cognitionem praebens;* ideo dicitur *lux hominum*, quia homines illuminat et videre facit; unde in Psalmo[^p249-3]: « Signatum est super nos lumen vultus tui, Domine ». Sine quo lumine non est videre, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p249-4], quia, sicut non videt oculus, nisi a luce corporali illuminetur; sic non videt intellectus, nisi a luce spiritualissima illustretur. Et licet omnibus cognitionem praebeat, non tamen omnibus praebet cognitionem sui, quia peccatores per ipsam cognoscunt et ipsam non comprehendunt. Ideo dicit:

13. (Vers. 5.). *Et lux in tenebris lucet*, cum peccatores facit vera cognoscere; *et tenebrae eam non comprehenderunt;* quia infidelitatis tenebris obcaecati non cognoscunt; Genesis primo[^p249-5]: « Tenebrae erant super faciem abyssi », id est infidelitas in facie cordis humani. Unde ad Ephesios quinto: « Fuistis aliquando tenebrae, nunc autem lux in Domino »; primae Ioannis primo: « Deus lux est, et tenebrae in eo non sunt ullae. Si dixerimus, quoniam societatem habemus cum eo et in tenebris ambulamus; mentimur et veritatem non facimus ». Et per hoc eliditur error ille, qui posuit, omnia esse a fortuna, et non esse providentiam, nec Deum cognoscere acta nostra[^p249-6].

### Quaestiones.

14. Quaest. I. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Sine ipso factum est nihil.*

**Contra** hoc est, quia qui furatur aliquid facit: ergo furtum est aliquid: ergo est a Deo: ergo peccata et mala sunt a Deo et non fiunt sine Deo. — *Si tu dicas*, quod peccatum nihil est, unde sensus est: *Sine ipso factum est nihil*, id est peccatum, ut negotio sistat intra[^p249-7]; sic ultra *obiicitur:* quia peccatum corrumpit, et quod corrumpit agit, et quod agit est ens, et quod est ens est aliquid: ergo etc.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *peccatum* aliquando nominat *actionem* privatam aliqua debita circumstantia; aliquando nominat *debitum* cum privatione boni, ad quod est; aliquando nominat ipsam *boni privationem.* Primo modo est *aliquid;* secundo modo est *secundum quid;* tertio modo est *omnino nihil.* Secundum igitur quod concernit *bonum substratum*, ratione illius est a Deo, sed ratione *deformitatis*, non[^p249-8].

Quod ergo obiicitur, quod peccatum agit; hoc est per naturam boni substratam, ut dicit Dionysius[^p249-9].

15. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Quod factum est in ipso vita erat.*

**Contra:** Omnia facta sunt in ipso: ergo omnia sunt vita; in Psalmo[^p249-10]: *Omnia in sapientia fecisti:* ergo lapis vivit.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod illud verbum exponit Chrysostomus[^p249-11] sic: *Quod factum est*, conne-
<!-- page 250 -->
ctendo priori clausulae, ut sit punctus: *sine ipso factum est nihil, quod factum est*, et ly *quod* tenetur *implicative.* Et post sequitur: *In ipso vita erat;* et cessat obiectio. — Hilarius[^p250-1] vero sic: *Quod factum est in ipso;* hic punctus, scilicet quantum ad humanitatem, ut incarnatio, passio et resurrectio, *erat vita, causaliter* loquendo, id est causa vitae et salutis nostrae. — Ambrosius[^p250-2] sic: *Quod factum est in ipso vita*, id est, in ipso qui est vita; *appositiva* est constructio; *erat*, scilicet in Dei praescientia, antequam fieret; et est similis modus loquendi: *Omnia in sapientia fecisti.* — Augustinus[^p250-3] elegantius omnibus exponit: *Quod factum est in ipso vita erat;* quia habebat in ipso exemplar et rationem viventem; quae ratio dicitur *vivens*, quia spiritualis, non materialis; quia intransmutabilis, non variabilis; quia aeterna, non corruptibilis. Et haec est ratio *vitae; vita* enim est actus spiritualis et continuus, ab ente quieto et sempiterno fluens[^p250-4].

16. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Tenebrae eam non comprehenderunt.*

**Contra:** 1. Lux corporalis, dum lucet et est praesens, non potest non videri: ergo pari ratione videtur de luce spirituali et de intellectu.

2. Item, quare dicit de *tenebris?* Quia nullus etiam bonus eam[^p250-5] comprehendit, cum sit incomprehensibilis.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod non est simile de luce corporali et spirituali triplici de causa: tum ex parte *motivi*, quia corporalis se manifestat naturaliter, spiritualis vero voluntarie; tum ex parte *oculi*, quia oculus intellectus caecus est respectu unius, et tamen videt respectu alterius, sicut videmus, quod unius habet ignorantiam, alterius cognitionem; sic oculus infidelis caecus respectu Dei. Tertia ratio ex parte *comparationis*, quia aliqua proportio est oculi nostri ad lucem corporalem, sed lux spiritualis improportionaliter excedit, et ideo *lucem habitat inaccessibilem*[^p250-6].

2. Quod quaeritur de *comprehensione;* dicendum, quod est comprehensio per *inclusionem*[^p250-7], per apertam *visionem*, per fidei et caritatis *adhaesionem.* Primo modo a nullo comprehenditur, secundo modo a Beatis, tertio modo a Sanctis, nullo dictorum modorum a malignis; ideo dicit: *Et tenebrae eam non comprehenderunt*, id est, nec fide nec amore adhaeserunt.

## English
<!-- page 248 -->

#### *Second, on the Word in comparison to the things which are said through him.*

9. (Vers. 3.). *All things were made by him* etc. Here the Word is described by comparison *to that which is said through the Word*, and this as to four conditions: for he describes him as a principle of creation *sufficient*, as *unfailing*, as *foreknowing*, as *furnishing knowledge* to others. And through these four conditions several heresies are struck down.

First, then, he is called a *sufficient principle*, because he suffices for producing all things. Therefore he says: *All things were made by him*, namely by God the Father through the Word, because Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth[^p248-6]: « He that liveth for ever created all things together »; and this through the Word, whence in the Psalm: « He spoke, and they were made ». Augustine in the book of the *Confessions*[^p248-7]: « By the Word coeternal with thee thou sayest whatever thou sayest, and what thou sayest is made, nor dost thou make otherwise than by saying ».

10. *And without him was made nothing.* Here the second condition is noted, namely that he is an *unfailing principle*, because he is lacking to no operation. For the Father makes nothing without the Word; below in the fifth[^p248-8]: « What things soever the Father doth, these the Son also doth in like manner ». Likewise the creature makes nothing without the Word cooperating; below in the fifteenth: « Without me you can do nothing ». And in this the heresy of the Manichaeans is perfectly struck down, who posited two principles, and that some things are produced by a God of light, some by a God of darkness. For they did not understand how *all things were made through the Word*[^p248-9].
<!-- page 249 -->

11. (Vers. 4.). *What was made*[^p249-1] *in him was life.* Here the third condition is noted, namely that he is a *foreknowing principle*, because all things are in him before they are made, and in him they live, as a chest in the mind of the craftsman. Whence Augustine[^p249-2] says that the Son is « an art full of all living reasons »; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third: « All things were known to the Lord God before they were created: so also after they were perfected he beholdeth all things ». Therefore the reasons live in him, because they are not changed; James, the first: « With whom there is no change nor shadow of alteration »; and every change is a kind of death.

12. *And the life was the light of men.* Here the fourth condition is touched on, which is that he *furnishes knowledge* to others; therefore he is called *the light of men*, because he illumines men and makes them see; whence in the Psalm[^p249-3]: « The lumen[^tr-lumen] of thy countenance, O Lord, is signed upon us ». Without which lumen there is no seeing, as Augustine says[^p249-4], because, just as the eye does not see unless it is illumined by bodily light, so the understanding does not see unless it is enlightened by a most spiritual light. And although he furnishes knowledge to all, yet he does not furnish to all the knowledge of himself, because sinners know through him and do not comprehend him. Therefore he says:

13. (Vers. 5.). *And the light shineth in darkness*, when he makes sinners know true things; *and the darkness did not comprehend it;* because, blinded by the darkness of unbelief, they do not know; Genesis, the first[^p249-5]: « Darkness was upon the face of the deep », that is, unbelief on the face of the human heart. Whence to the Ephesians, the fifth: « You were heretofore darkness, but now light in the Lord »; the first of John, the first: « God is light, and in him there is no darkness. If we say that we have fellowship with him, and walk in darkness, we lie, and do not the truth ». And through this is struck down that error which held that all things are from fortune, and that there is no providence, nor does God know our deeds[^p249-6].

### Questions.

14. Quaest. I. It is asked about what he says: *Without him was made nothing.*

**On the contrary** is this, that he who steals makes something: therefore theft is something: therefore it is from God: therefore sins and evils are from God and are not made without God. — *If you say* that sin is nothing, whence the sense is: *Without him was made nothing*, that is, sin, so that the negation [*sic*] stands within[^p249-7]; thus it is objected further: because sin corrupts, and what corrupts acts, and what acts is a being, and what is a being is something: therefore etc.

**I respond:** It must be said that *sin* sometimes names an *action* deprived of some due circumstance; sometimes it names the *due* together with the privation of the good to which it is ordered; sometimes it names the very *privation of the good.* In the first way it is *something;* in the second way it is *something in a certain respect;* in the third way it is *altogether nothing.* According, then, as it concerns the *underlying good*, by reason of that it is from God, but by reason of the *deformity*, not[^p249-8].

As to the objection, then, that sin acts: this is through the nature of the underlying good, as Dionysius says[^p249-9].

15. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *What was made in him was life.*

**On the contrary:** All things were made in him: therefore all things are life; in the Psalm[^p249-10]: *Thou hast made all things in wisdom:* therefore a stone lives.

**I respond:** It must be said that Chrysostom[^p249-11] expounds that word thus: *What was made*, con-
<!-- page 250 -->
necting it to the prior clause, so that the stop comes here: *without him was made nothing that was made*, and the *that* is taken *implicatively.* And afterwards there follows: *In him was life;* and the objection ceases. — But Hilary[^p250-1] thus: *What was made in him;* here the stop, namely as to the humanity, as the incarnation, passion and resurrection, *was life, causally* speaking, that is, the cause of our life and salvation. — Ambrose[^p250-2] thus: *What was made in him the life*, that is, in him who is life; the construction is *appositive;* *was*, namely in God's foreknowledge, before it was made; and it is a like manner of speaking: *Thou hast made all things in wisdom.* — Augustine[^p250-3] expounds it more elegantly than all: *What was made in him was life;* because he had in himself the exemplar and the living reason; which reason is called *living* because it is spiritual, not material; because it is unchangeable, not variable; because it is eternal, not corruptible. And this is the account of *life;* for *life* is a spiritual and continuous act, flowing from a being at rest and everlasting[^p250-4].

16. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *The darkness did not comprehend it.*

**On the contrary:** 1. Bodily light, while it shines and is present, cannot not be seen: therefore by like reason it seems so of spiritual light and of the understanding.

2. Likewise, why does he say it of *the darkness?* For no one, not even a good man, comprehends it[^p250-5], since it is incomprehensible.

**I respond:** It must be said that it is not alike with bodily and spiritual light, for a threefold cause: both on the part of the *moving*, because bodily light manifests itself naturally, but spiritual light voluntarily; and on the part of the *eye*, because the eye of the understanding is blind with respect to one thing, and yet sees with respect to another, as we see that it has ignorance of one thing and knowledge of another; so the eye of the unbeliever is blind with respect to God. The third reason is on the part of the *comparison*, because there is some proportion of our eye to bodily light, but spiritual light exceeds it beyond all proportion, and therefore he *inhabiteth light inaccessible*[^p250-6].

2. As to what is asked about *comprehension:* it must be said that there is comprehension by *inclusion*[^p250-7], by open *vision*, by the *adhesion* of faith and charity. In the first way he is comprehended by no one, in the second way by the Blessed, in the third way by the Saints, in none of the said ways by the wicked; therefore he says: *And the darkness did not comprehend it*, that is, they did not adhere to it by faith nor by love.
