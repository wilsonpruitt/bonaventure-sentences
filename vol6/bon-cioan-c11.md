### Capitulum XI. *Erat autem quidam languens.*

### Annotatio 37.

(Vers. 1.). *Erat autem quidam languens Lazarus.*

1. Nota gradus infirmitatis spiritualis. Homo enim primo *occasionatur* in cogitatione — *infirmatur* in delectatione — *languet* in mora — *moritur* in consensu — *effertur* in opere — *sepelitur* in consuetudine.

*Occasionatur* ergo morbus in *cogitatione;* quarti Regum primo[^p585-9] de Ochozia, qui « cecidit per cancellos coenaculi sui et aegrotavit ». *Cancelli coenaculi* sensus sunt, qui varias cogitationes in coenaculo cordis immittunt. — *Infirmatur* homo in *delectatione;* quod figuratum est, Ioannis quarto, in filio reguli, qui « infirmabatur Capharnaum », id est in villa pinguedinis vel agro consolationis, per quod intelligitur lectulus delectationis. — *Languet* in *mora;* quod figuratum est, Ioannis undecimo[^p585-10], de Lazaro, et Ioannis quinto de paralytico. De hoc languore Ecclesiastici decimo: « Languor prolixior gravat medicum ». — *Moritur* in *consensu;* quod figuratum est, Marci quinto, de filia archisynagogi mortua in thalamo. — *Effertur* in *opere;* quod figuratum est, Lucae septimo[^p585-11], de adolescente, qui « efferebatur defunctus in porta civitatis filius unicus matris suae ». — *Sepelitur* in *consuetudine;* quod figuratum est in sepultura Lazari, « quem invenit Iesus quatuor dies habentem in monumento » etc.

2. *Erat quidam languens Lazarus a Bethania de castello Mariae et Marthae, sororis eius.*
<!-- page 586 -->

Ecce, quod hic describitur infirmus ex *nomine* et *loco* et *genere. Lazarus* interpretatur *adiutus a Domino* — *Bethania* interpretatur *domus obedientiae*[^p586-1] — Maria et Martha typum gerunt *societatis bonae.* Non ergo timeat talis languens, qui habet *adiuvamentum gratiae,* qui habitat in *domo obedientiae,* qui manet in *collegio societatis bonae,* quia, etiam si mortuus fuerit, potest surgere.

### Collatio XLI.

(Vers. 3.). *Domine, ecce, quem amas infirmatur.*

1. In verbis istis tanguntur tria: *potestas medici* — *necessitas infirmi* — et *ratio miserandi.* — Potestas *medici,* cum dicitur: *Domine. Miserandi ratio,* cum dicitur: *quem amas. Necessitas infirmi,* cum dicitur: *infirmatur.*

*Ecce,* inquit, *quem amas infirmatur.* Praetermissa infirmitate *naturae,* nota, quod est *spiritualis* infirmitas duplex: infirmitas *toleranda,* et haec est infirmitas *poenae;* infirmitas *fugienda,* et haec est infirmitas *culpae.*

2. Infirmitas *poenae* multiplex est. Habet enim homo causam infirmitatis ab *interiori,* ab *exteriori,* ab *inferiori,* a *superiori.* — Ab *interiori* causatur infirmitas *contritionis,* scilicet ex consideratione sui; de qua Psalmus[^p586-2]: « Miserere mei, Domine, quoniam infirmus sum ». — Ab *exteriori* causatur infirmitas *compassionis,* scilicet ex consideratione proximi; secundae ad Corinthios undecimo: « Quis infirmatur, et ego non infirmor »? — Ab *inferiori* causatur infirmitas *tribulationis,* scilicet ab intemperie mundi; de qua secundae ad Corinthios duodecimo: « Complaceo mihi in infirmitatibus meis, in contumeliis, in necessitatibus, in angustiis pro Christo ». De hac potest exponi quod habetur Ecclesiastici septimo[^p586-3]: « Non te pigeat visitare infirmum. Ex his enim in dilectione firmaberis ». — A *superiori* causatur infirmitas *sanctae aemulationis,* scilicet ex dilectione Dei, de qua Canticorum quinto: « Nuntiate dilecto meo, quia amore langueo ».

3. Haec infirmitas *toleranda* est, quia reddit hominem *sanum,* reddit hominem *eruditum,* reddit hominem *sollicitum,* reddit hominem *probatum* et perfectum. — *Sanat* enim haec infirmitas infirmitatem *culpae,* sicut aliquando accidit, quod aliquis, cadens in unam infirmitatem, sanatur ab alia secundum naturam; unde Ecclesiastici undecimo[^p586-4]: « Malitia unius horae oblivionem facit luxuriae maximae »; et Ecclesiastici trigesimo primo: « Infirmitas gravis sobriam facit animam ». — Item *erudit intellectum,* secundum illud Isaiae vigesimo octavo: « Vexatio dabit intellectum auditui »; cuius habetur exemplum, secundi Machabaeorum nono, de Antiocho, qui « coepit ex gravi superbia deductus ad agnitionem sui venire, divina admonitus plaga » etc. Unde et ait: « Iustum est, subditum esse Deo, et mortalem non paria Deo sentire ». Exemplum de milite laeso in pede et clerico, qui portabat aquam benedictam. — Item *excitat voluntatem;* unde in Psalmo[^p586-5]: « Multiplicatae sunt infirmitates eorum; postea acceleraverunt »; Osee sexto: « In tribulatione sua mane consurgent ad me ». — Item *probat* et *perficit virtutem;* secundae ad Corinthios duodecimo: « Virtus in infirmitate perficitur »; ideo dicit Apostolus in eodem: « Libenter gloriabor in infirmitatibus meis, ut inhabitet in me virtus Christi ».

4. *Domine, ecce, quem amas infirmatur.*

Sicut est multiplex infirmitas *poenae,* ita est multiplex infirmitas *culpae.* Est enim infirmitas *elationis, cupiditatis, carnalitatis* et *curiositatis.* — Est infirmitas *elationis;* de qua Isaiae vigesimo quarto[^p586-6]: « Infirmata est altitudo populi terrae, et terra infecta est ab habitatoribus suis ». — Est etiam infirmitas *cupiditatis;* de qua Ecclesiastae quinto: « Est et alia infirmitas pessima, quam vidi sub sole: divitiae congregatae in malum domini sui ». — Est infirmitas *carnalitatis;* de qua Ecclesiastici trigesimo septimo: « In multis escis erit infirmitas », id est in multis voluptatibus; « et aviditas appropinquabit usque ad choleram ». — Est etiam infirmitas *curiositatis;* de qua Isaiae quadragesimo[^p586-7]: « Deficient pueri et laborabunt, et iuvenes in infirmitate cadent ». De hac Ecclesiastae tertio: « Vidi afflictionem, quam dedit Deus filiis hominum, ut distendantur in ea. Cuncta fecit bona in tempore suo Deus, et mundum tradidit disputationi eorum, ut non inveniat homo opus, quod operatus est Deus ab initio usque ad finem »; et Ecclesiastae octavo: « Inveni, quod operum omnium Dei nullam possit homo invenire rationem; et quanto plus laboraverit, tanto minus inveniet ».

5. Haec infirmitas *fugienda* est, quia hominem *deformat, depauperat, debilitat* et *inquietat.* — *Deformat* conscientiam; primi Machabaeorum primo[^p586-8]: « Iuvenes et virgines infirmati sunt, et omnis speciositas mulierum immutata est ». — *Depauperat* animam; Ecclesiastae quinto: « Sicut egressus est nudus
<!-- page 587 -->
de utero matris suae, sic revertetur etc. Miserabilis prorsus infirmitas, quomodo venit, sic revertetur ». — *Debilitat* virtutem; Threnorum primo: « Infirmata est virtus mea; dedit me Dominus in manu, de qua non potero surgere ». — *Inquietat* mentem; Psalmus: « Humiliatum est in laboribus cor eorum; infirmati sunt, nec fuit qui adiuvaret ».

6. *Domine, ecce, quem amas infirmatur.* Cum peccatum propter iam dictas causas dicatur *infirmitas,* nota secundum diversa genera peccatorum diversas *differentias* infirmitatum. Est namque infirmitas *superbiae, invidiae, irae, avaritiae, accidiae, luxuriae, gulae.* — Est infirmitas *superbiae;* de qua secundi Paralipomenon vigesimo primo[^p587-1]: « Mortuus est Ioram in infirmitate pessima, et non fecit ei populus combustionis obsequias, sicut fecerat maioribus eius ». Et ita infirmitas pessima est superbia; *Ioram,* excelsus vel domini sublimitas. De hac infirmitate Isaiae vigesimo quarto: « Altitudo populi infirmata est ». — Est infirmitas *invidiae;* de qua Michaeae primo[^p587-2]: « Infirmata est in bonum, quae habitat in Maroth ». Invidiae enim infirmitas causam accipit ex bono et in amaritudine habitat. — Est infirmitas *irae;* de qua Ieremiae sexto: « Iniquitas et vastitas audietur in ea, coram me semper infirmitas et plaga ». — Est infirmitas *avaritiae;* de qua Ecclesiastae quinto: « Est alia infirmitas, quam vidi sub sole: divitiae congregatae in malum domini sui ». — Est etiam infirmitas *accidiae;* de qua Ecclesiastici trigesimo primo[^p587-3]: « In omnibus operibus tuis esto velox, et omnis infirmitas non occurret tibi ». — Est infirmitas *luxuriae.* Haec est infirmitas immunda; de qua Lucae octavo: « Erant cum Iesu mulieres, quae erant curatae a spiritibus immundis et infirmitatibus ». — Est et infirmitas *gulae;* de qua Ecclesiastici trigesimo septimo: « In multis escis erit infirmitas, et aviditas appropinquabit usque ad choleram ».

7. Hae septem infirmitates sub figura leguntur curatae a Christo in Evangelio. Sanavit enim Dominus septem infirmitates incurabiles, scilicet *daemonium,* Matthaei octavo[^p587-4]; sanavit *lepram,* Matthaei octavo, Lucae quinto; sanavit *phrenesim,* Matthaei decimo septimo et Marci nono; sanavit *contractam,* Lucae decimo tertio; sanavit *paralysim,* Ioannis quinto, Matthaei nono; sanavit *fluxum,* Lucae octavo; sanavit *hydropisim,* Lucae decimo quarto. — Per *daemonium* intellige superbiam; per *lepram* intellige invidiam; Proverbiorum decimo quarto[^p587-5]: « Putredo ossium invidia »; per *phrenesim,* iram; per *contractam,* avaritiam; per *paralysim,* accidiam; per *fluxum sanguinis,* luxuriam; per *hydropisim,* gulam. Haec sanavit qui « sanavit omnem infirmitatem ».

### Annotatio 38.

(Vers. 11.). *Lazarus, amicus noster, dormit.*

Nota, quod est dormitio *quietis,* dormitio *salutis,* dormitio *mortis.* — Dormitio *quietis* est dormitio *naturae,* quae homini *indulgetur;* de qua Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p587-6]: « Invenit eos dormientes »; et subditur, quod « dixit eis: Dormite iam et requiescite ». — Est dormitio *salutis,* et haec est dormitio *gratiae;* haec homini *suadetur,* de qua Canticorum quinto: « Ego dormio, et cor meum vigilat »; et Canticorum secundo: « Adiuro vos, ne vigilare faciatis dilectam, donec ipsa velit » etc. — Est dormitio *mortis,* et haec, spiritualiter intelligendo, est dormitio *culpae,* et haec homini *interdicitur;* primae ad Thessalonicenses quinto[^p587-7]: « Non dormiamus, sed vigilemus et sobrii simus »; et ad Ephesios quinto: « Surge, qui dormis, et exsurge a mortuis, et illuminabit te Christus ». — Et dicitur culpa *somnus* propter tria: quia ad modum somni naturalis claudit homini *visum,* ligat *sensum,* sistit *gressum.* — Claudit *visum discretionis;* unde in Psalmo: « Illumina oculos meos, ne unquam obdormiam in morte ». — Ligat *sensum devotionis;* Proverbiorum vigesimo tertio[^p587-8]: « Eris sicut dormiens in medio maris, et quasi sopitus gubernator, amisso clavo, et dices », id est, dicere poteris: « Verberaverunt me, et non dolui; traxerunt me, et ego non sensi ». — Sistit *gressum bonae operationis;* propter quod dicitur otioso Proverbiorum sexto: « Usquequo, piger, dormies? quando consurges de somno tuo » etc.

### Annotatio 39.

(Vers. 11.). *Sed ego vado, ut a somno excitem eum.*

Nota, quod a somno excitatur aliquis *lumine,* aliquis *pulsu,* aliquis *clamore.* Ita et dormientes somno *culpae* aliquando *lumine* divinae inspiratio-
<!-- page 588 -->
nis, aliquando *pulsu* divinae correctionis, aliquando *clamore* sanctae praedicationis. — Haec tria significata sunt Actuum duodecimo[^p588-1] in excitatione Petri dormientis, de quo dicitur: « Ecce, Angelus Domini astitit, et lumen refulsit in habitaculo »; ecce, primum; « percussoque latere Petri »; ecce, secundum; « excitavit eum dicens: Surge velociter »; ecce, tertium.

### Collatio XLII.

(Vers. 21.). *Domine, si fuisses hic, frater meus non fuisset mortuus.*

1. In verbis istis lamentatur Martha sub figura Ecclesiae duo, scilicet *absentiam medici* — et *mortem infirmi: absentiam medici,* cum dicit: *Domine, si fuisses hic;* quasi dicat: quia non fuisti hic. *Mortem infirmi,* cum subiungit: *Frater meus non fuisset mortuus,* qui scilicet est mortuus; et potest hoc intelligi de morte *naturae,* vel de morte *culpae,* ut dicamus: Domine, si fuisses hic corporaliter, frater meus non fuisset mortuus *temporaliter;* vel dicamus: Si fuisses hic per gratiam, frater meus non fuisset mortuus per *culpam.*

2. Distingue multiplicem mortem, et attende, quod est mors *naturae,* mors *culpae,* mors *poenae,* mors *gratiae.* — Mors naturae *horrenda* est malis, *optanda* iustis, *praecavenda* cunctis. — Est *horrenda malis,* quia ipsos *spoliat;* Ecclesiastici undecimo[^p588-2]: « Est qui locupletatur parce agendo » etc.; et sequitur: « Et nescit, quod tempus praetereat, et mors appropinquet, et relinquat omnia aliis et moriatur »; Iob vigesimo septimo: « Cum dives dormierit, nihil secum auferet »; Psalmus: « Cum interierit, non sumet omnia ». — Item, quia eos *cruciat;* Ecclesiastici quadragesimo primo[^p588-3]: « O mors, quam amara est memoria tua homini iniusto et pacem habenti in substantiis suis »! Si amara est memoria, multo magis praesentia; Lucae decimo sexto: « Mortuus est dives et sepultus est in inferno ». — Item, quia eos *annihilat;* Iob decimo quarto[^p588-4]: « Homo, cum fuerit mortuus et nudatus atque consumtus, ubi, quaeso, est »? quasi dicat: nusquam est. Psalmus: « Transivi, et ecce, non erat ».

3. Mors ista est *optanda iustis,* quia illos *ditat;* Iob tertio[^p588-5]: « Exspectant mortem, et non venit, quasi effodientes thesaurum, gaudentque vehementer, cum invenerint sepulcrum »; unde de iusto dicitur Iob quinto: « Ingredieris in abundantia sepulcrum »; ad Philippenses primo: « Mihi vivere Christus est, ait Apostolus, et mori lucrum ». — Item, quia illos *quietat;* Apocalypsis decimo quarto[^p588-6]: « Beati, qui in Domino moriuntur. Amodo iam dicit Spiritus, ut requiescant a laboribus suis ». — Item, quia illos *exaltat;* Lucae decimo sexto: « Factum est, ut moreretur mendicus et deportaretur ab Angelis in sinum Abrahae ».

4. Mors nihilominus ista est *praecavenda cunctis,* quia *necessaria;* secundi Regum decimo quarto[^p588-7]: « Omnes morimur et quasi aquae dilabimur in terram, quae non revertuntur »; Psalmus: « Quis est homo, qui vivet et non videbit mortem »? Versus:

> Unde superbit homo, cuius conceptio culpa,
> Nasci poena, labor vita, necesse mori?

Item, quia *propinqua;* Ecclesiastici decimo quarto[^p588-8]: « Memor esto, quoniam mors non tardabit ». Seneca: « Quidquid aetatis retro est, mors tenet, quidquid restat, incertum ». Ecclesiastici undecimo: « Nescit homo, quod tempus praetereat, et mors appropinquet » etc. — Item, quia *repentina;* Genesis vigesimo septimo[^p588-9]: « Vides », ait Isaac, « quod senuerim et ignorem diem mortis meae »; primae ad Thessalonicenses quinto: « Cum dixerint: Pax et securitas, tunc repentinus eis superveniet interitus »; unde « nihil est incertius hora mortis, morte nihil certius ».

Mors ergo naturae est *horrenda malis,* quia ipsos *spoliat, cruciat* et *annihilat; optanda* est *iustis,* quia ipsos *ditat, quietat* et *exaltat; praecavenda cunctis,* quia *necessaria, propinqua* et *repentina;* de qua hic[^p588-10] dicitur: *Domine, si fuisses hic, frater meus non fuisset mortuus,* scilicet morte naturae.

5. *Domine, si fuisses hic, frater meus non fuisset mortuus* morte *culpae,* quae multiplex est. Est enim mors *superbiae;* Iob decimo octavo[^p588-11]: « Consumat brachia eius primogenita mors »; Iob primo: « Irruit ventus vehemens a regione deserti et concussit quatuor angulos domus, quae corruens oppressit liberos tuos, et mortui sunt ». — Est mors *irae;*
<!-- page 589 -->
Iob quinto: « Virum stultum interficit iracundia »; primae Ioannis tertio: « Qui non diligit manet in morte ». — Est mors *invidiae;* Iob quinto[^p589-1]: « Parvulum occidit invidia »; Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo: « A tristitia festinat mors »; secundae ad Corinthios septimo: « Tristitia saeculi operatur mortem ». — Est mors *avaritiae;* Proverbiorum vigesimo primo: « Qui congregat thesauros lingua mendacii vanus et excors est, et impingetur ad laqueos mortis ». — Est mors *accidiae;* Psalmus[^p589-2]: « Illumina oculos meos, ne unquam obdormiam in morte ». — Est mors *gulae;* primae ad Timotheum quinto: « Vidua vivens in deliciis mortua est »; et ad Romanos octavo: « Prudentia carnis mors est ». — Est mors *luxuriae;* Proverbiorum quinto: « Favus distillans labia meretricis »; et sequitur: « Pedes eius descendunt in mortem, et ad inferos gressus illius penetrant »; et Proverbiorum septimo, de meretrice: « Viae inferi domus illius penetrantes interiora mortis ».

6. *Aliter* nota de morte peccatorum pessima. Est enim mors *ignorantiae,* mors *culpae,* mors *finalis impoenitentiae.* — De morte *ignorantiae;* Ecclesiastici vigesimo secundo[^p589-3]: « Super mortuum plora, defecit enim lux eius; super fatuum plora, defecit enim sensus ». — De morte *culpae;* Ezechielis decimo octavo: « Nolo mortem morientis, sed ut magis convertatur et vivat ». — De morte *finalis impoenitentiae;* Proverbiorum undecimo: « Mortuo impio nulla erit ultra spes »; primae Ioannis quinto: « Est peccatum ad mortem; non pro illo, dico, ut roget quis ». — Sic ergo est mors *ignorantiae,* mors *culpae,* mors *finalis impoenitentiae.* Ignorantia aufert vitam *intelligentiae;* culpa, vitam *gratiae;* finalis impoenitentia, *reditum utriusque.*

7. Mors ista *fugienda* est, quia ad modum mortis naturalis aufert *sensum,* aufert *verbum,* aufert *gressum.* — Aufert *sensum discretionis;* Ecclesiastae nono[^p589-4]: « Mortui nihil sciunt amplius ». — Aufert *verbum confessionis;* Psalmus: « Non mortui laudabunt te, Domine ». — Aufert *gressum bonae operationis;* unde in Psalmo dicitur de mortuis: « Sedentes in tenebris et umbra mortis, vinctos in mendicitate et ferro ». — Haec ergo est multiplex mors *culpae,* quam tollit beneficium divinae praesentiae; unde dici potest: *Domine, si fuisses hic, frater meus non fuisset mortuus.*

8. Sed et, *Domine, si fuisses hic, frater meus non fuisset mortuus,* morte *poenae,* quae quidem est multiplex. Est enim mors poenae *temporalis* — et est mors poenae *aeternae.* — Mors *poenae temporalis* est multiplex, secundum quod multiplex poena. Est enim poena *contracta,* poena *inflicta* et poena *assumta.* — Est ergo mors poenae *contractae;* ad Romanos quinto[^p589-5]: « Per unum hominem intravit peccatum in mundum, et per peccatum mors ». — Est etiam mors poenae *inflictae,* et haec inflicta a *Deo;* primi Regum secundo: « Dominus mortificat et vivificat »; Ieremiae secundo: « Traduxit nos per terram sitis et imaginem mortis ». Vel inflictae a *mundo;* Psalmus: « Propter te mortificamur tota die, aestimati sumus sicut oves occisionis ». Vel inflictae a *diabolo,* qui « persecutus est hominem inopem et mendicum et compunctum corde mortificare », secundum quod dicitur in Psalmo. — Est etiam mors poenae *assumtae;* primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p589-6]: « Quotidie morior propter gloriam vestram, fratres »; et secundae ad Corinthios undecimo gloriabatur Apostolus, fuisse « in plagis supra modum et in mortibus frequenter ». — Sic ergo est mors poenae temporalis triplex: mors poenae *contractae* et poenae *inflictae* et poenae *assumtae.*

9. Et nota, quod prima *deflenda* est; quam deflebat Apostolus ad Romanos septimo[^p589-7]: « Infelix ego homo! quis me liberabit de corpore mortis huius »? — Secunda *sustinenda* est; parit enim patientibus vitam; « Dominus enim mortificat et vivificat »; unde Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo: « Ego occidam, et ego vivere faciam ». Ideo dicebat Iob decimo tertio: « Etiam si occiderit me, in ipso sperabo ». — Tertia *assumenda* est; secundae ad Corinthios quarto: « Semper mortificationem Iesu Christi in corpore nostro circumferentes, ut et vita Iesu in corporibus nostris manifestetur »; et post paululum: « Mors in nobis operatur, vita autem in vobis ».

10. Sequitur de morte poenae *aeternae,* quae multiplex est, secundum quod est multiplex poena. Est enim poena *carentiae visionis,* poena *sensus* et poena *vermis.* — Est mors *carentiae visionis,* et haec est mors caliginis vel caligo mortis; de qua Iob decimo[^p589-8]: « Dimitte me, ut plangam paululum dolorem meum, antequam vadam et non revertar ad terram tenebrosam et opertam mortis caligine » etc.; et non immerito appellatur carentia *mors,* quia, sicut dicitur Ecclesiastici quadragesimo, « melius est mori quam indigere ». — Est etiam mors *poenae sensus;* Apocalypsis vigesimo primo[^p589-9]: « Pars eorum erit in stagno igne ardenti et sulphure, quod est secunda mors »;
<!-- page 590 -->
et eodem vigesimo: « Iudicatum est de singulis secundum opera ipsorum. Et infernus et mors missi sunt in stagnum ignis. Haec est mors secunda », stagnum ignis. — Est etiam mors *poenae vermis,* de qua potest intelligi quod dicitur Threnorum primo: « Foris interficit gladius, et domi mors similis est ».

11. Mors ista *horrenda* est, quia *intolerabilis, inevitabilis* et *interminabilis.* — *Intolerabilis;* unde Apocalypsis nono[^p590-1]: « Quaerent homines mortem, et fugiet mors ab eis ». — Nota, quod est status intolerabilis, cui praeeligitur dolor mortis; ita erit ibi secundum illud Ecclesiastici trigesimo: « Melius est mors quam vita amara »; unde et propheta Isaias trigesimo tertio: « Quis ex vobis poterit habitare cum ardoribus sempiternis »? — Item, *inevitabilis,* illis scilicet, qui peccaverunt; ad Romanos sexto[^p590-2]: « Stipendia peccati mors »; Apocalypsis decimo octavo: « In die una venient plagae eius: mors et luctus et fames, et igne comburetur, quia est fortis Dominus, qui iudicabit illam »; dicitur de Babylone. — Item, *interminabilis;* Isaiae ultimo[^p590-3]: « Vermis eorum non morietur, et ignis eorum non exstinguetur »; Apocalypsis decimo quarto: « Fumus tormentorum eorum ascendet in saecula saeculorum »; et ideo dicitur in Psalmo: « Mors depascet eos »; in quo notatur mortis duratio; quod enim depascitur iterum ad pascendum renascitur. — Haec est ergo mors gehennae: *intolerabilis, inevitabilis malis* et *interminabilis;* de qua possumus dicere: *Si fuisses hic, frater meus non fuisset mortuus,* morte poenae.

12. Sed, *Domine, si fuisses hic, frater meus non fuisset mortuus* nisi morte *gratiae,* qua morte debet homo mori *carni, mundo,* et *peccato.* — Debet enim homo spiritualis mori *carni.* Hac enim morte voluit nos mortificari ille[^p590-4] « qui pro peccatis nostris mortuus est, ut nos offerret Deo, mortificatus quidem carne, vivificatus autem spiritu »; unde ad Romanos octavo: « Si spiritu facta carnis mortificaveritis, vivetis ». — Debet etiam mori *mundo,* secundum illud ad Galatas sexto: « Mihi mundus crucifixus est, et ego mundo »; ad Colossenses tertio: « Quae sursum sunt quaerite, non quae super terram; mortui enim estis » etc. — Debet iterum mori *peccato;* ad Romanos sexto[^p590-5]: « Qui mortui sumus peccato, quomodo adhuc vivemus in illo »? item in eodem: « Qui mortuus est iustificatus est a peccato ». — Sic ergo debet homo spiritualis mori *carni,* mori *mundo* et mori *peccato,* quod est non retinere motum in illis vel sensum.

13. Sunt autem tria, quae occidunt hominem morte spirituali, scilicet *studium contemplationis, affectus dilectionis, humilitas conversationis.* Contemplatio *suspendit,* dilectio *transfigit,* humilitas vero *sepelit* et *recondit.* — Debet ergo homo mori per *studium contemplationis,* ut sit eius meditatio quasi *suspendium;* unde Iob septimo[^p590-6]: « Elegit suspendium anima mea, et mortem ossa mea ». — Debet etiam mori per *ardorem dilectionis,* ut sit eius affectio quasi *transfixa,* quia, sicut dicitur Canticorum octavo, « fortis est sicut mors dilectio, et dura sicut infernus aemulatio; lampades eius quasi lampades ignis atque flammarum ». Hac morte quasi transfixa erat quae dicebat: « Vulnerata caritate ego sum ». — Debet etiam mori per *humilitatem conversationis,* ut sit eius vita quasi *sepulta* sive abscondita, secundum illud ad Colossenses tertio[^p590-7]: « Mortui estis, et vita vestra abscondita est cum Christo in Deo ».

14. Haec mors *quaerenda* est et amanda, quia *placat Deum, aedificat proximum, locupletat defunctum.* — Mors ista *placat Deum;* est enim « pretiosa in conspectu Domini mors Sanctorum eius[^p590-8] »; et bene *pretiosa,* quia pro illa dat regnum caelorum; unde secundae ad Timotheum secundo: « Si commortui sumus, et convivemus; si sustinebimus, et conregnabimus ». — Item, mors ista *aedificat proximum;* unde secundi Machabaeorum sexto dicebat ille bonus senex Eleazarus: « Adolescentulis forte exemplum relinquam, si pro sanctissimis legibus honesta morte perfungar ». — Item, mors illa *locupletat defunctum;* unde Ioannis duodecimo: « Si granum frumenti cadens in terram mortuum fuerit, multum fructum affert ».

### Collatio XLIII.

(Vers. 43.). *Lazare, veni foras.*

1. Circa suscitationem Lazari notandum est, quid Dominus ante suscitationem *fecerit* — et quid in suscitando *praeceperit.*

Nota igitur, quod Dominus, volens suscitare Lazarum, tria facit: *plorat, accedit* et *clamat.* — *Plorat;* Ioannis undecimo[^p590-9]: « Lacrymatus est Ie-
<!-- page 591 -->
sus ». — *Accedit;* unde sequitur: « Fremens in semetipso, venit ad monumentum ». — *Clamat;* unde in eodem paulo post: « Haec cum dixisset, voce magna clamabat: Lazare, veni foras »; per hoc instruens nos, quod qui vult proximum suum suscitare spiritualiter debet ipsum *plorare* per *compassionem,* secundum illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo secundo[^p591-1]: « Super mortuum plora, defecit enim lux eius; super fatuum plora, defecit enim sensus ». — Debet ad ipsum *accedere* per *subventionem,* secundum illud Proverbiorum sexto: « Discurre, festina, suscita amicum tuum ». — Debet ipsi *acclamare* per *praedicationem,* secundum illud Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo: « Clama, ne cesses, quasi tuba » etc. — Haec ergo notantur ex parte *suscitantis.*

2. Ex parte *suscitati* nota, quod Dominus, volens suscitare mortuum, aliquando iubet, *stare feretrum,* sicut praecepit Lucae septimo[^p591-2], quando voluit suscitare adolescentem. Aliquando iubet, *exire turbam;* unde Lucae octavo non permisit, turbam intrare secum, quando voluit suscitare filiam archisynagogi. Aliquando iubet, *deponi lapidem;* unde Ioannis undecimo, volens suscitare Lazarum, dixit: « Tollite lapidem ». — Haec sunt tria, quae disponunt mortuum ad vitam spiritualem recuperandam, scilicet *peccatum dimittere, occasionem fugere* et *peccatum* in confessione *revelare.* — Tunc *stat feretrum,* cum homo desistit a peccati operatione, secundum illud Isaiae primo[^p591-3]: « Quiescite agere perverse ». — Tunc *non intrat turba,* quando homo cavet sibi a peccati occasione; de quo dicitur Numerorum decimo sexto: « Recedite a tabernaculis hominum impiorum, et ne tangatis quae ad eos pertinent, ne involvamini in peccatis eorum ». — Tunc *lapis tollitur,* cum homo revelat peccata sua in confessione; de quo Proverbiorum vigesimo octavo: « Qui abscondit scelera sua non dirigetur; qui autem confessus fuerit et reliquerit ea misericordiam consequetur ». — Haec ergo antecedunt suscitationem.

3. Post suscitationem dicitur, quod Lazarus *prodiit, solvitur* et *abiit. Prodiit;* unde dicitur[^p591-4]: *Prodiit, qui fuerat mortuus. Solvitur* et *abiit;* unde dicit Iesus: *Solvite eum et sinite abire.* — Nota, quod mortuus spiritualis *exit* de sepulcro in *contritione; solvitur* in *confessione,* secundum illud Matthaei decimo sexto: « Quaecumque solveris super terram » etc.; *abit* in *satisfactione;* unde Lucae quinto: « Vade in domum tuam »; et Psalmus: « Ibunt » Sancti « de virtute in virtutem ».

4. Item notandum, quod Dominus suscitatum aliquando committit *solvendum,* aliquando *pascendum,* aliquando *custodiendum.* — *Solvendus* committitur spiritualiter suscitatus sacerdotibus; quod figuratum est Ioannis undecimo[^p591-5], cum dixit de Lazaro: *Solvite eum et sinite abire.* — *Pascendus* committitur praedicatoribus; quod figuratum est, Lucae octavo, de filia archisynagogi, cui praecepit Dominus dari manducare. — *Custodiendus* committitur praelatis; quod figuratum est, Lucae septimo, de adolescente, quem dedit Dominus custodiendum matri suae.

Recollige ergo, quod praelati, ut suscitent, debent *plorare, accedere* et *clamare.* Postquam vero suscitatus est mortuus, debent *solvere, pascere* et *custodire.* — Mortui vero, ut suscitentur, debent *feretrum sistere, turbam repellere, lapidem deponere.* Postquam vero suscitati sunt, debent *prodire, solutionem quaerere* et *abire.*

### Collatio XLIV.

(Vers. 50.). *Expedit, ut unus moriatur homo pro populo, et non tota gens pereat.*

1. Cum hoc dicatur ad litteram de morte Christi, hic[^p591-6] tangitur *necessitas,* cum dicitur: *Expedit, ut unus moriatur homo pro populo;* quasi dicat: alias moreretur populus. Cuius etiam tangitur *utilitas,* cum dicitur: *Et non tota gens pereat.* Notandum igitur, quid mors Christi debeat facere *pro nobis* — et quid debeat facere *in nobis.*

Circa primum nota, quod mors Christi est nobis *remedium* contra malum — et est nobis *suffragium* ad bonum.

2. Est ergo nobis *remedium contra malum;* et in malo, praecipue pro quo sanando Christus mortuus est, tria sunt: scilicet *macula, reatus* et *offensa.* Notandum est, quod mors Christi *delet maculam, tollit poenam, conciliat offensam.* — Mors ergo Christi *delet culpam;* Apocalypsis primo[^p591-7]: « Lavit nos a peccatis nostris in sanguine suo »; ad Romanos sexto: « Quicumque baptizati sumus in Christo Iesu, in morte ipsius baptizati sumus », *baptizati,* id est loti. — Mors Christi igitur *tollit poenam;* Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p591-8]: « Tradidit in mortem animam suam et cum sceleratis reputatus est, et ipse peccata multorum tulit ». — Mors Christi *conciliat offensam;* ad Romanos quinto: « Cum adhuc inimici essemus, reconciliati sumus Deo per mortem Filii eius ». — Sic ergo mors Christi est *remedium contra malum.*

3. Est etiam mors Christi *suffragium ad bonum.* Suffragatur enim ad bonum *veniae,* ad bonum
<!-- page 592 -->
*gratiae* et ad bonum *gloriae.* — Mors Christi *impetrat veniam;* unde ad Hebraeos nono[^p592-1]: « Ideo novi testamenti mediator est, ut, morte intercedente, in redemptionem earum praevaricationum, quae erant sub priori testamento, repromissionem accipiant qui vocati sunt aeternae hereditatis »; et ideo ad Hebraeos duodecimo confortat Apostolus fideles dicens: « Accessistis ad novi testamenti mediatorem Iesum et sanguinis aspersionem melius loquentem quam Abel »; ideo *melius loquentem,* quia sanguis Abel loquebatur vindictam, sed sanguis Christi loquitur veniam. — Item, mors Christi *meretur gratiam;* unde primae Petri tertio[^p592-2]: « Christus semel pro peccatis nostris mortuus est etc., ut nos offerret Deo, mortificatus quidem carne, vivificatus autem spiritu ». — Item, mors Christi *aperit ianuam.* « Ipse enim per sanguinem suum semel introivit in sancta, aeterna redemptione inventa », ad Hebraeos nono; et haec apertio probata est introductione illorum in regno, qui ante mortem eius captivi detinebantur in limbo; de quo dictum est Osee decimo tertio: « Ero mors tua, o mors, morsus tuus ero, inferne ». — Haec ergo facit mors Christi pro nobis.

4. Considera postea, quid debeat facere *in nobis;* et attende, quia mors Christi est a nobis *amanda, praedicanda* et *imitanda.* — Debemus enim mortuum pro nobis Christum *amare;* unde ad Romanos octavo[^p592-3] ait Apostolus: « Christus Iesus, qui mortuus est, qui et resurrexit, qui est ad dexteram Patris, qui etiam interpellat pro nobis »; et postmodum in eodem exclamat: « Quis ergo nos separabit a caritate Christi »? Et ad Romanos quinto: « Commendat Deus suam caritatem in nobis, quoniam, cum adhuc peccatores essemus, secundum tempus, Christus pro nobis mortuus est ». — Debemus etiam mortuum pro nobis Christum *praedicare;* unde Apostolus primae ad Corinthios secundo[^p592-4]: « Non iudicavi, me scire aliquid inter vos nisi Iesum Christum et hunc crucifixum ». — Debemus etiam mortuum pro nobis Christum *imitari;* unde secundae ad Corinthios quarto: « Semper mortificationem Christi Iesu in corpore nostro circumferentes, ut et vita Iesu in corporibus nostris manifestetur ».

5. Nota autem, quod ad imitandam mortem Christi *obligamur* ex debito, *allicimur* promisso, *animamur* exemplo. — *Obligamur* ex debito; Christus enim comparavit vitam nostram morte sua; secundae ad Corinthios quinto[^p592-5]: « Christus pro omnibus mortuus est, ut qui vivunt iam non sibi vivant » etc. — *Allicimur* ex promisso; unde ad Romanos sexto: « Si complantati facti sumus similitudini mortis eius, simul et resurrectionis erimus »; et eodem: « Si mortui sumus cum Christo, credimus, quia simul vivemus cum illo ». — *Animamur* exemplo; unde primae Petri secundo[^p592-6]: « Christus passus est pro nobis, vobis relinquens exemplum, ut sequamini vestigia eius » etc.; et primae Petri quarto: « Christo igitur in carne passo, et vos eadem cogitatione armamini ».

### Annotatio 40.

(Vers. 5.). *Diligebat autem Dominus Mariam, Martham et Lazarum.*

Per istos tres nota tria genera hominum a Deo dilectorum. — Per Mariam intellige *contemplativos;* per Martham, *activos;* per Lazarum, *poenitentes.* Eos habes significatos, Ezechielis decimo quarto[^p592-7], per Noe, Daniel et Iob, qui « in iustitia sua liberant animas suas »; eosdem etiam per Petrum, Iacobum et Ioannem, Matthaei decimo septimo, qui ascendunt cum Domino in montem. Ista enim tria genera hominum a peccato liberantur et a Deo diliguntur et in monte gloriae assumuntur.

### Annotatio 41.

(Vers. 35.). *Et lacrymatus est Iesus.*

Nota, quod ter lacrymatus est. Lacrymatus est in resuscitatione, Ioannis undecimo[^p592-8]; unde hic dicitur: *Lacrymatus est Iesus.* — Item, lacrymatus est Iesus, cum appropinquaret civitati; Lucae decimo nono: « Videns Iesus civitatem, flevit super illam dicens: Quia si cognovisses et tu »! — Lacrymatus est etiam in passione; ad Hebraeos quinto: « Cum clamore valido et lacrymis offerens, exauditus est pro sua reverentia ». — In passione flevit, compatiens miseriae *culpae;* ad monumentum flevit, compatiens miseriae *poenae;* super civitatem flevit, compatiens *ignorantiae.*

### Annotatio 42.

(Vers. 43.). *Et cum haec dixisset, clamavit voce magna: Lazare, veni foras.*

Nota, quod ter legitur Christus clamasse. Clamavit enim in *templo;* Ioannis septimo[^p592-9]: « Stabat Iesus et clamabat dicens: Si quis sitit, veniat ad me et bibat ». — Clamavit ad *sepulcrum* Lazari; unde hic: *Haec cum dixisset, clamavit* etc. — Clama-
<!-- page 593 -->
vit in *cruce;* Matthaei vigesimo septimo: « Iesus autem, iterum clamans voce magna, emisit spiritum ». — Stans in templo clamavit ad populum; stans ad sepulcrum clamavit ad Lazarum; pendens in cruce clamavit ad Patrem. Primus clamor fuit pro *ignorantia* populi instruenda; secundus clamor, pro *miseria* relevanda; tertius, pro *culpa* humani generis dimittenda. Unde illa relevavit clamando, quibus compatiebatur flendo[^p593-1].

### Annotatio 43.

(Vers. 39.). *Tollite lapidem.*

Nota, quod peccatum dicitur *lapis,* quia *operit;* Psalmus[^p593-2]: « Operti sunt iniquitate et impietate sua ». — Quia *premit;* Psalmus: « Sicut onus grave gravatae sunt super me ». — Quia *includit;* Isaiae sexagesimo primo: « Ut annuntiarem clausis apertionem ».

Vel ideo dicitur peccatum *lapis,* quia habet *opacitatem, duritiam* et *gravitatem.* — *Obscurat* enim intellectum; Ecclesiastici undecimo[^p593-3]: « Error et tenebrae peccatoribus concreata sunt ». — *Indurat* voluntatem; Ieremiae decimo septimo: « Induraverunt cervicem suam, ut non audirent me ». — *Gravat* virtutem; Gregorius: « Peccatum, quod per poenitentiam non diluitur, mox suo pondere ad aliud trahit ».

### Annotatio 44.

(Vers. 44.). *Prodiit qui fuerat mortuus, ligatus pedes et manus institis, et facies eius erat ligata sudario.*

1. Nota, quod tria erant in mortuo ligata: *facies, pedes* et *manus.* Ligat enim peccatum *faciem* cogitationis[^p593-4], ut non valeat de Deo peccator recogitare; *pedes* affectionis, ut non possit a peccato resipiscere; *manus* operationis, ut non possit in bono proficere.

Vel aliter: ligantur *pedes* affectionum per malum *amorem,* ne peccator culpam detestetur; unde Threnorum primo[^p593-5]: « Expandit rete pedibus meis ». — Ligatur *facies* per malum *pudorem,* ne peccatum confiteatur; Psalmus: « Confusio faciei meae cooperuit me ». — Ligantur *manus* per inordinatum *torporem,* ne de peccato satisfaciat; unde Actuum duodecimo, ligatus erat Petrus in manibus; unde legitur ibidem, quod in adventu Angeli « ceciderunt catenae de manibus eius ». Iste enim est triplex modus ligandi captivum, scilicet ut incatenetur pedibus, collo et manibus.

2. *Prodiit,* inquit, *qui fuerat mortuus.*

Nota, quod Dominus suscitavit tres mortuos: *puellam* in thalamo, Lucae octavo[^p593-6]; *adolescentem* in porta, Lucae septimo; *Lazarum* de monumento, Ioannis undecimo. Per *puellam* significantur peccantes in sola voluntate; per *adolescentem,* peccantes in opere; per *Lazarum,* peccantes in consuetudine.

### Chapter XI. *Now there was a certain man sick.*

### Annotation 37.

(Vers. 1.). *Now there was a certain man sick, named Lazarus.*

1. Note the steps of spiritual infirmity. For a man is first *given occasion* in thought — is *made infirm* in delectation — *languishes* in delay — *dies* in consent — is *carried out* in deed — is *buried* in custom.

The disease therefore *is given occasion* in *thought;* the fourth of Kings, the first chapter[^p585-9], of Ochozias, who « fell through the lattices of his upper chamber and was sick ». The *lattices of the upper chamber* are the senses, which let in various thoughts into the upper chamber of the heart. — A man *is made infirm* in *delectation;* which is figured, John, the fourth chapter, in the ruler's son, who « was infirm at Capharnaum », that is, in the village of fatness or the field of consolation, by which is understood the little bed of delectation. — He *languishes* in *delay;* which is figured, John, the eleventh chapter[^p585-10], in Lazarus, and John, the fifth chapter, in the paralytic. Of this languor Ecclesiasticus, the tenth chapter: « A long sickness is troublesome to the physician ». — He *dies* in *consent;* which is figured, Mark, the fifth chapter, in the daughter of the ruler of the synagogue, dead in her chamber. — He *is carried out* in *deed;* which is figured, Luke, the seventh chapter[^p585-11], in the young man who « was carried out dead at the gate of the city, the only son of his mother ». — He *is buried* in *custom;* which is figured in the burial of Lazarus, « whom Jesus found having been four days already in the grave » etc.

2. *There was a certain man sick, named Lazarus, of Bethania, of the town of Mary and of Martha her sister.*
<!-- page 586 -->

Behold that here the infirm man is described by *name* and *place* and *kindred. Lazarus* is interpreted *helped by the Lord* — *Bethania* is interpreted *house of obedience*[^p586-1] — Mary and Martha bear the type of *good company.* Let not such a sick man fear, then, who has *the help of grace,* who dwells in *the house of obedience,* who remains in *the fellowship of good company,* since, even if he be dead, he can rise.

### Collation XLI.

(Vers. 3.). *Lord, behold, he whom thou lovest is infirm.*

1. In these words three things are touched on: *the power of the physician* — *the need of the infirm man* — and *the reason for showing mercy.* — The power *of the physician,* when it is said: *Lord. The reason for showing mercy,* when it is said: *he whom thou lovest. The need of the infirm man,* when it is said: *is infirm.*

*Behold,* he says, *he whom thou lovest is infirm.* Leaving aside the infirmity *of nature,* note that *spiritual* infirmity is twofold: an infirmity *to be borne,* and this is the infirmity *of punishment;* an infirmity *to be fled,* and this is the infirmity *of fault.*

2. The infirmity *of punishment* is manifold. For a man has the cause of infirmity from *within,* from *without,* from *below,* from *above.* — From *within* is caused the infirmity *of contrition,* namely from consideration of oneself; of which the Psalm[^p586-2]: « Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I am infirm ». — From *without* is caused the infirmity *of compassion,* namely from consideration of one's neighbour; the second to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter: « Who is infirm, and I am not infirm »? — From *below* is caused the infirmity *of tribulation,* namely from the inclemency of the world; of which the second to the Corinthians, the twelfth chapter: « I please myself in my infirmities, in reproaches, in necessities, in distresses for Christ ». Of this can be expounded what is had in Ecclesiasticus, the seventh chapter[^p586-3]: « Be not slow to visit the infirm. For by these things thou shalt be confirmed in dilection ». — From *above* is caused the infirmity *of holy emulation,* namely from the dilection of God, of which the Canticles, the fifth chapter: « Tell my beloved that I languish with love ».

3. This infirmity is *to be borne,* because it renders a man *sound,* renders a man *instructed,* renders a man *solicitous,* renders a man *proved* and perfect. — For this infirmity *heals* the infirmity *of fault,* as it sometimes happens that someone, falling into one infirmity, is healed of another according to nature; whence Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter[^p586-4]: « The affliction of an hour maketh one forget great delights »; and Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-first chapter: « A grievous infirmity maketh the soul sober ». — Likewise it *instructs the understanding,* according to that of Isaias, the twenty-eighth chapter: « Vexation alone shall make you understand what you hear »; of which there is an example, the second of Machabees, the ninth chapter, in Antiochus, who « began to come to the knowledge of himself, being brought down from his great pride, being admonished by the scourge of God » etc. Whence he also said: « It is just to be subject to God, and that a mortal man should not equal himself to God ». An example of the soldier wounded in the foot and the cleric who carried the holy water. — Likewise it *rouses the will;* whence in the Psalm[^p586-5]: « Their infirmities were multiplied; afterwards they made haste »; Osee, the sixth chapter: « In their affliction they will rise early to me ». — Likewise it *proves* and *perfects virtue;* the second to the Corinthians, the twelfth chapter: « Virtue is made perfect in infirmity »; therefore the Apostle says in the same: « Gladly will I glory in my infirmities, that the virtue of Christ may dwell in me ».

4. *Lord, behold, he whom thou lovest is infirm.*

As the infirmity *of punishment* is manifold, so the infirmity *of fault* is manifold. For there is an infirmity *of elation, of cupidity, of carnality* and *of curiosity.* — There is the infirmity *of elation;* of which Isaias, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p586-6]: « The height of the people of the earth is made infirm, and the earth is infected by the inhabitants thereof ». — There is also the infirmity *of cupidity;* of which Ecclesiastes, the fifth chapter: « There is also another grievous infirmity which I have seen under the sun: riches gathered together to the hurt of the owner ». — There is the infirmity *of carnality;* of which Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-seventh chapter: « In many meats there will be infirmity », that is, in many pleasures; « and greediness will turn to choler ». — There is also the infirmity *of curiosity;* of which Isaias, the fortieth chapter[^p586-7]: « Youths shall faint and labour, and young men shall fall by infirmity ». Of this Ecclesiastes, the third chapter: « I have seen the trouble which God hath given the sons of men, to be exercised in it. He hath made all things good in their time, and hath delivered the world to their consideration, so that man cannot find out the work which God hath made from the beginning to the end »; and Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter: « I found that man can find no reason of all those works of God; and the more he shall labour, so much the less shall he find ».

5. This infirmity is *to be fled,* because it *deforms, impoverishes, weakens* and *disquiets* a man. — It *deforms* the conscience; the first of Machabees, the first chapter[^p586-8]: « The young men and the virgins were made infirm, and the comeliness of the women was changed ». — It *impoverishes* the soul; Ecclesiastes, the fifth chapter: « As he came forth naked
<!-- page 587 -->
from his mother's womb, so shall he return etc. A most miserable infirmity, as he came, so shall he return ». — It *weakens* virtue; the Lamentations, the first chapter: « My strength is made infirm; the Lord hath delivered me into a hand out of which I am not able to rise ». — It *disquiets* the mind; the Psalm: « Their heart was humbled with labours; they were made infirm, and there was none to help them ».

6. *Lord, behold, he whom thou lovest is infirm.* Since sin, for the causes just stated, is called *infirmity,* note according to the diverse kinds of sins the diverse *differences* of infirmities. For there is the infirmity *of pride, of envy, of anger, of avarice, of sloth, of lust, of gluttony.* — There is the infirmity *of pride;* of which the second of Paralipomenon, the twenty-first chapter[^p587-1]: « Joram died of a very grievous infirmity, and the people did not make for him a funeral of burning, as they had done for his ancestors ». And so the most grievous infirmity is pride; *Joram,* exalted or the sublimity of the lord. Of this infirmity Isaias, the twenty-fourth chapter: « The height of the people is made infirm ». — There is the infirmity *of envy;* of which Micheas, the first chapter[^p587-2]: « She is become infirm unto good, that dwelleth in Maroth ». For the infirmity of envy takes its cause from good and dwells in bitterness. — There is the infirmity *of anger;* of which Jeremias, the sixth chapter: « Iniquity and spoil shall be heard in her, infirmity and stripes are continually before me ». — There is the infirmity *of avarice;* of which Ecclesiastes, the fifth chapter: « There is another infirmity which I have seen under the sun: riches gathered together to the hurt of the owner ». — There is also the infirmity *of sloth;* of which Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-first chapter[^p587-3]: « In all thy works be quick, and no infirmity shall come to thee ». — There is the infirmity *of lust.* This is the unclean infirmity; of which Luke, the eighth chapter: « There were with Jesus women who had been cured of unclean spirits and infirmities ». — There is also the infirmity *of gluttony;* of which Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-seventh chapter: « In many meats there will be infirmity, and greediness will turn to choler ».

7. These seven infirmities are read, under a figure, as cured by Christ in the Gospel. For the Lord healed seven incurable infirmities, namely *the devil,* Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p587-4]; he healed *leprosy,* Matthew, the eighth chapter, Luke, the fifth chapter; he healed *frenzy,* Matthew, the seventeenth chapter, and Mark, the ninth chapter; he healed *the bent woman,* Luke, the thirteenth chapter; he healed *palsy,* John, the fifth chapter, Matthew, the ninth chapter; he healed *the issue,* Luke, the eighth chapter; he healed *dropsy,* Luke, the fourteenth chapter. — By *the devil* understand pride; by *leprosy* understand envy; Proverbs, the fourteenth chapter[^p587-5]: « Envy is the rottenness of the bones »; by *frenzy,* anger; by *the bent woman,* avarice; by *palsy,* sloth; by *the issue of blood,* lust; by *dropsy,* gluttony. These he healed who « healed all infirmity ».

### Annotation 38.

(Vers. 11.). *Lazarus, our friend, sleepeth.*

Note that there is a sleep *of rest,* a sleep *of salvation,* a sleep *of death.* — The sleep *of rest* is the sleep *of nature,* which *is granted* to man; of which Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p587-6]: « He found them sleeping »; and it is subjoined that « he said to them: Sleep ye now and take your rest ». — There is the sleep *of salvation,* and this is the sleep *of grace;* this *is counselled* to man, of which the Canticles, the fifth chapter: « I sleep, and my heart watcheth »; and the Canticles, the second chapter: « I adjure you, that you make not the beloved to awake till she please » etc. — There is the sleep *of death,* and this, understood spiritually, is the sleep *of fault,* and this *is forbidden* to man; the first to the Thessalonians, the fifth chapter[^p587-7]: « Let us not sleep, but let us watch and be sober »; and to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter: « Rise, thou that sleepest, and arise from the dead, and Christ shall enlighten thee ». — And fault is called *sleep* for three reasons: because, after the manner of natural sleep, it shuts a man's *sight,* binds his *sense,* stays his *step.* — It shuts *the sight of discretion;* whence in the Psalm: « Enlighten my eyes, that I never sleep in death ». — It binds *the sense of devotion;* Proverbs, the twenty-third chapter[^p587-8]: « Thou shalt be as one sleeping in the midst of the sea, and as a pilot fast asleep when the stern is lost, and thou shalt say », that is, thou shalt be able to say: « They have beaten me, but I was not sensible of pain; they drew me, and I felt not ». — It stays *the step of good operation;* on account of which it is said to the idle man, Proverbs, the sixth chapter: « How long wilt thou sleep, O sluggard? when wilt thou rise out of thy sleep » etc.

### Annotation 39.

(Vers. 11.). *But I go that I may awake him out of sleep.*

Note that one is awakened from sleep by a *lumen,* another by a *knock,* another by a *cry.* So too those sleeping with the sleep *of fault* are awakened sometimes by the *lumen*[^tr-lumen] of divine inspira-
<!-- page 588 -->
tion, sometimes by the *knock* of divine correction, sometimes by the *cry* of holy preaching. — These three are signified, Acts, the twelfth chapter[^p588-1], in the awakening of Peter sleeping, of which it is said: « Behold, an Angel of the Lord stood by him, and a lumen shined in the room »; behold, the first; « and striking Peter on the side »; behold, the second; « he raised him up, saying: Arise quickly »; behold, the third.

### Collation XLII.

(Vers. 21.). *Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died.*

1. In these words Martha, under the figure of the Church, laments two things, namely *the absence of the physician* — and *the death of the infirm man: the absence of the physician,* when she says: *Lord, if thou hadst been here;* as if she should say: because thou wast not here. *The death of the infirm man,* when she adds: *My brother had not died,* who namely is dead; and this can be understood of the death *of nature,* or of the death *of fault,* so that we say: Lord, if thou hadst been here bodily, my brother had not died *temporally;* or we say: If thou hadst been here by grace, my brother had not died by *fault.*

2. Distinguish manifold death, and attend that there is the death *of nature,* the death *of fault,* the death *of punishment,* the death *of grace.* — The death of nature is *to be dreaded* by the wicked, *to be wished for* by the just, *to be guarded against* by all. — It is *to be dreaded by the wicked,* because it *despoils* them; Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter[^p588-2]: « There is one that is enriched by living sparingly » etc.; and there follows: « And he knoweth not what time shall pass, and that death approacheth, and that he must leave all to others and shall die »; Job, the twenty-seventh chapter: « The rich man when he shall sleep shall take away nothing with him »; the Psalm: « When he shall die, he shall take nothing away ». — Likewise, because it *tortures* them; Ecclesiasticus, the forty-first chapter[^p588-3]: « O death, how bitter is the remembrance of thee to an unjust man that hath peace in his possessions »! If the remembrance is bitter, much more the presence; Luke, the sixteenth chapter: « The rich man died and was buried in hell ». — Likewise, because it *brings them to nothing;* Job, the fourteenth chapter[^p588-4]: « Man, when he shall be dead and stripped and consumed, I pray you, where is he »? as if he should say: he is nowhere. The Psalm: « I passed by, and lo, he was not ».

3. This death is *to be wished for by the just,* because it *enriches* them; Job, the third chapter[^p588-5]: « They look for death, and it cometh not, as they that dig for a treasure, and they rejoice exceedingly when they have found the grave »; whence it is said of the just man, Job, the fifth chapter: « Thou shalt enter into the grave in abundance »; to the Philippians, the first chapter: « For me to live is Christ, says the Apostle, and to die is gain ». — Likewise, because it *gives them rest;* the Apocalypse, the fourteenth chapter[^p588-6]: « Blessed are the dead who die in the Lord. From henceforth now, saith the Spirit, that they may rest from their labours ». — Likewise, because it *exalts* them; Luke, the sixteenth chapter: « It came to pass that the beggar died and was carried by the Angels into Abraham's bosom ».

4. This death is nonetheless *to be guarded against by all,* because it is *necessary;* the second of Kings, the fourteenth chapter[^p588-7]: « We all die and like waters that return no more we fall down into the earth »; the Psalm: « Who is the man that shall live and not see death »? The verses:

> Whence is man proud, whose conception is fault,
> To be born, punishment; life, labour; to die, necessity?

Likewise, because it is *near;* Ecclesiasticus, the fourteenth chapter[^p588-8]: « Remember that death is not slow ». Seneca: « Whatever of our age is behind us, death holds; whatever remains is uncertain ». Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter: « Man knoweth not what time shall pass, and that death approacheth » etc. — Likewise, because it is *sudden;* Genesis, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p588-9]: « Thou seest », says Isaac, « that I am old and know not the day of my death »; the first to the Thessalonians, the fifth chapter: « When they shall say: Peace and security, then shall sudden destruction come upon them »; whence « nothing is more uncertain than the hour of death, nothing more certain than death ».

Therefore the death of nature is *to be dreaded by the wicked,* because it *despoils, tortures* and *brings them to nothing;* it is *to be wished for by the just,* because it *enriches* them, *gives them rest* and *exalts* them; it is *to be guarded against by all,* because it is *necessary, near* and *sudden;* of which it is said here[^p588-10]: *Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died,* namely by the death of nature.

5. *Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died* by the death *of fault,* which is manifold. For there is the death *of pride;* Job, the eighteenth chapter[^p588-11]: « Let the firstborn death devour his arms »; Job, the first chapter: « A violent wind came on a sudden from the side of the desert and shook the four corners of the house, and it fell upon thy children, and they are dead ». — There is the death *of anger;*
<!-- page 589 -->
Job, the fifth chapter: « Anger indeed killeth the foolish »; the first of John, the third chapter: « He that loveth not remaineth in death ». — There is the death *of envy;* Job, the fifth chapter[^p589-1]: « Envy slayeth the little one »; Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter: « Of sadness cometh death in haste »; the second to the Corinthians, the seventh chapter: « The sorrow of the world worketh death ». — There is the death *of avarice;* Proverbs, the twenty-first chapter: « He that gathereth treasures by a lying tongue is vain and foolish, and shall stumble upon the snares of death ». — There is the death *of sloth;* the Psalm[^p589-2]: « Enlighten my eyes, that I never sleep in death ». — There is the death *of gluttony;* the first to Timothy, the fifth chapter: « She that liveth in pleasures is dead »; and to the Romans, the eighth chapter: « The wisdom of the flesh is death ». — There is the death *of lust;* Proverbs, the fifth chapter: « The lips of a harlot are like a honeycomb dropping »; and there follows: « Her feet go down into death, and her steps go in as far as hell »; and Proverbs, the seventh chapter, of the harlot: « Her house is the way to hell, reaching even to the inner chambers of death ».

6. *Otherwise,* note concerning the worst death of sinners. For there is the death *of ignorance,* the death *of fault,* the death *of final impenitence.* — Of the death *of ignorance;* Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-second chapter[^p589-3]: « Weep for the dead, for his light hath failed; weep for the fool, for his understanding faileth ». — Of the death *of fault;* Ezechiel, the eighteenth chapter: « I desire not the death of him that dieth, but rather that he be converted and live ». — Of the death *of final impenitence;* Proverbs, the eleventh chapter: « When the wicked man is dead, there shall be no hope any more »; the first of John, the fifth chapter: « There is a sin unto death; for that I say not that any man ask ». — So then there is the death *of ignorance,* the death *of fault,* the death *of final impenitence.* Ignorance takes away the life *of intelligence;* fault, the life *of grace;* final impenitence, *the return of both.*

7. This death is *to be fled,* because after the manner of natural death it takes away *sense,* takes away *speech,* takes away *step.* — It takes away *the sense of discretion;* Ecclesiastes, the ninth chapter[^p589-4]: « The dead know nothing more ». — It takes away *the speech of confession;* the Psalm: « The dead shall not praise thee, O Lord ». — It takes away *the step of good operation;* whence in the Psalm it is said of the dead: « Such as sat in darkness and in the shadow of death, bound in want and in iron ». — This then is the manifold death *of fault,* which the benefit of the divine presence takes away; whence it can be said: *Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died.*

8. But also, *Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died* by the death *of punishment,* which indeed is manifold. For there is the death of *temporal* punishment — and there is the death of *eternal* punishment. — The death *of temporal punishment* is manifold, according as punishment is manifold. For there is punishment *contracted,* punishment *inflicted* and punishment *assumed.* — There is then the death of punishment *contracted;* to the Romans, the fifth chapter[^p589-5]: « By one man sin entered into the world, and by sin death ». — There is also the death of punishment *inflicted,* and this inflicted by *God;* the first of Kings, the second chapter: « The Lord killeth and maketh alive »; Jeremias, the second chapter: « He led us through a land of drought and the image of death ». Or inflicted by *the world;* the Psalm: « For thy sake we are killed all the day long; we are counted as sheep for the slaughter ». Or inflicted by *the devil,* who « persecuted the poor man and the beggar and the broken in heart, to put him to death », according to what is said in the Psalm. — There is also the death of punishment *assumed;* the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter[^p589-6]: « I die daily for your glory, brethren »; and the second to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter, the Apostle gloried that he had been « in stripes above measure and in deaths often ». — So then the death of temporal punishment is threefold: the death of punishment *contracted* and of punishment *inflicted* and of punishment *assumed.*

9. And note that the first is *to be bewailed;* which the Apostle bewailed, to the Romans, the seventh chapter[^p589-7]: « Unhappy man that I am! who shall deliver me from the body of this death »? — The second is *to be borne;* for it brings forth life for those who suffer it; « for the Lord killeth and maketh alive »; whence Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter: « I will kill, and I will make to live ». Therefore Job said, the thirteenth chapter: « Although he should kill me, I will trust in him ». — The third is *to be assumed;* the second to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « Always bearing about in our body the mortification of Jesus Christ, that the life also of Jesus may be made manifest in our bodies »; and a little after: « Death worketh in us, but life in you ».

10. There follows concerning the death of *eternal* punishment, which is manifold, according as punishment is manifold. For there is the punishment *of the lack of vision,* the punishment *of sense* and the punishment *of the worm.* — There is the death *of the lack of vision,* and this is the death of darkness, or the darkness of death; of which Job, the tenth chapter[^p589-8]: « Suffer me that I may lament my sorrow a little, before I go and return no more to a land that is dark and covered with the mist of death » etc.; and not without merit is lack called *death,* since, as is said in Ecclesiasticus, the fortieth chapter, « it is better to die than to want ». — There is also the death *of the punishment of sense;* the Apocalypse, the twenty-first chapter[^p589-9]: « Their portion shall be in the pool burning with fire and brimstone, which is the second death »;
<!-- page 590 -->
and in the same, the twentieth chapter: « They were judged every one according to their works. And hell and death were cast into the pool of fire. This is the second death », the pool of fire. — There is also the death *of the punishment of the worm,* of which can be understood what is said in the Lamentations, the first chapter: « Abroad the sword destroyeth, and at home there is death alike ».

11. This death is *to be dreaded,* because it is *intolerable, inevitable* and *interminable.* — *Intolerable;* whence the Apocalypse, the ninth chapter[^p590-1]: « Men shall seek death, and death shall fly from them ». — Note that it is an intolerable state to which the pain of death is preferred; so it will be there, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the thirtieth chapter: « Death is better than a bitter life »; whence also the prophet Isaias, the thirty-third chapter: « Which of you can dwell with everlasting burnings »? — Likewise, *inevitable,* namely to those who have sinned; to the Romans, the sixth chapter[^p590-2]: « The wages of sin is death »; the Apocalypse, the eighteenth chapter: « In one day her plagues shall come: death and mourning and famine, and she shall be burnt with fire, because God is strong, who shall judge her »; it is said of Babylon. — Likewise, *interminable;* Isaias, the last chapter[^p590-3]: « Their worm shall not die, and their fire shall not be quenched »; the Apocalypse, the fourteenth chapter: « The smoke of their torments shall ascend up for ever and ever »; and therefore it is said in the Psalm: « Death shall feed upon them »; in which the duration of death is noted; for what is fed upon springs up again to be fed upon. — This then is the death of gehenna: *intolerable, inevitable for the wicked* and *interminable;* of which we can say: *If thou hadst been here, my brother had not died,* by the death of punishment.

12. But, *Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died* except by the death *of grace,* by which death a man ought to die *to the flesh, to the world,* and *to sin.* — For the spiritual man ought to die *to the flesh.* For by this death he willed us to be put to death who[^p590-4] « died for our sins, that he might offer us to God, being put to death indeed in the flesh, but enlivened in the spirit »; whence to the Romans, the eighth chapter: « If by the spirit you mortify the deeds of the flesh, you shall live ». — He ought also to die *to the world,* according to that to the Galatians, the sixth chapter: « The world is crucified to me, and I to the world »; to the Colossians, the third chapter: « Seek the things that are above, not the things that are upon the earth; for you are dead » etc. — He ought again to die *to sin;* to the Romans, the sixth chapter[^p590-5]: « We that are dead to sin, how shall we live any longer therein »? likewise in the same: « He that is dead is justified from sin ». — So then the spiritual man ought to die *to the flesh,* to die *to the world* and to die *to sin,* which is not to retain movement or sense in them.

13. Now there are three things which kill a man with spiritual death, namely *the study of contemplation, the affection of dilection, the humility of conversation.* Contemplation *hangs,* dilection *transfixes,* humility *buries* and *hides away.* — A man ought therefore to die through *the study of contemplation,* so that his meditation be as it were *a hanging;* whence Job, the seventh chapter[^p590-6]: « My soul hath chosen hanging, and my bones death ». — He ought also to die through *the ardour of dilection,* so that his affection be as it were *transfixed,* since, as is said in the Canticles, the eighth chapter, « dilection is strong as death, jealousy as hard as hell; the lamps thereof are lamps of fire and flames ». Transfixed as it were by this death was she who said: « I am wounded with charity ». — He ought also to die through *the humility of conversation,* so that his life be as it were *buried* or hidden, according to that to the Colossians, the third chapter[^p590-7]: « You are dead, and your life is hid with Christ in God ».

14. This death is *to be sought* and loved, because it *appeases God, edifies one's neighbour, enriches the dead man.* — This death *appeases God;* for « precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his Saints[^p590-8] »; and rightly *precious,* because for it he gives the kingdom of heaven; whence the second to Timothy, the second chapter: « If we be dead with him, we shall live also with him; if we suffer, we shall also reign with him ». — Likewise, this death *edifies one's neighbour;* whence, the second of Machabees, the sixth chapter, that good old man Eleazar said: « I shall leave an example perhaps to young men, if for the most holy laws I undergo an honourable death ». — Likewise, that death *enriches the dead man;* whence John, the twelfth chapter: « If the grain of wheat falling into the ground be dead, it bringeth forth much fruit ».

### Collation XLIII.

(Vers. 43.). *Lazarus, come forth.*

1. Concerning the raising of Lazarus it is to be noted what the Lord *did* before the raising — and what he *commanded* in raising.

Note therefore that the Lord, willing to raise Lazarus, does three things: he *weeps, approaches* and *cries out.* — He *weeps;* John, the eleventh chapter[^p590-9]: « Jesus wept ».
<!-- page 591 -->
— He *approaches;* whence there follows: « Groaning in himself, he cometh to the sepulchre ». — He *cries out;* whence in the same a little after: « When he had said these things, he cried with a loud voice: Lazarus, come forth »; by this instructing us that he who wills to raise his neighbour spiritually ought to *weep over* him through *compassion,* according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-second chapter[^p591-1]: « Weep for the dead, for his light hath failed; weep for the fool, for his understanding faileth ». — He ought to *approach* him through *succour,* according to that of Proverbs, the sixth chapter: « Run about, make haste, stir up thy friend ». — He ought to *cry out* to him through *preaching,* according to that of Isaias, the fifty-eighth chapter: « Cry, cease not, like a trumpet » etc. — These things then are noted on the part *of the one raising.*

2. On the part *of the one raised* note that the Lord, willing to raise a dead man, sometimes commands *the bier to stand,* as he ordered, Luke, the seventh chapter[^p591-2], when he willed to raise the young man. Sometimes he commands *the crowd to go out;* whence, Luke, the eighth chapter, he did not suffer the crowd to go in with him when he willed to raise the daughter of the ruler of the synagogue. Sometimes he commands *the stone to be put away;* whence, John, the eleventh chapter, willing to raise Lazarus, he said: « Take away the stone ». — These are the three things which dispose a dead man to recover spiritual life, namely *to leave off sin, to flee the occasion* and *to reveal sin* in confession. — Then *the bier stands,* when a man desists from the doing of sin, according to that of Isaias, the first chapter[^p591-3]: « Cease to do perversely ». — Then *the crowd does not go in,* when a man guards himself from the occasion of sin; of which it is said in Numbers, the sixteenth chapter: « Depart from the tents of the wicked men, and touch nothing of theirs, lest you be involved in their sins ». — Then *the stone is taken away,* when a man reveals his sins in confession; of which Proverbs, the twenty-eighth chapter: « He that hideth his sins shall not prosper; but he that shall confess and forsake them shall obtain mercy ». — These things then go before the raising.

3. After the raising it is said that Lazarus *came forth, is loosed* and *went away. He came forth;* whence it is said[^p591-4]: *He came forth, that had been dead. He is loosed* and *went away;* whence Jesus says: *Loose him and let him go.* — Note that the spiritual dead man *goes out* from the sepulchre in *contrition;* he *is loosed* in *confession,* according to that of Matthew, the sixteenth chapter: « Whatsoever thou shalt loose upon earth » etc.; he *goes away* in *satisfaction;* whence Luke, the fifth chapter: « Go into thy house »; and the Psalm: « They shall go », the Saints, « from virtue to virtue ».

4. Likewise it is to be noted that the Lord sometimes commits the one raised *to be loosed,* sometimes *to be fed,* sometimes *to be kept.* — The one spiritually raised is committed *to be loosed* to priests; which is figured, John, the eleventh chapter[^p591-5], when he said of Lazarus: *Loose him and let him go.* — He is committed *to be fed* to preachers; which is figured, Luke, the eighth chapter, in the daughter of the ruler of the synagogue, to whom the Lord commanded that something be given to eat. — He is committed *to be kept* to prelates; which is figured, Luke, the seventh chapter, in the young man, whom the Lord gave to his mother to be kept.

Gather up therefore that prelates, in order to raise, ought to *weep, approach* and *cry out.* But after the dead man has been raised, they ought to *loose, feed* and *keep.* — But the dead, in order to be raised, ought to *make the bier stand, drive back the crowd, put away the stone.* But after they have been raised, they ought to *come forth, seek loosing* and *go away.*

### Collation XLIV.

(Vers. 50.). *It is expedient that one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not.*

1. Since this is said literally of the death of Christ, here[^p591-6] is touched its *necessity,* when it is said: *It is expedient that one man should die for the people;* as if he should say: otherwise the people would die. Its *utility* also is touched, when it is said: *And that the whole nation perish not.* It is to be noted therefore what the death of Christ ought to do *for us* — and what it ought to do *in us.*

Concerning the first, note that the death of Christ is for us a *remedy* against evil — and is for us a *suffrage* toward good.

2. It is then for us a *remedy against evil;* and in the evil, chiefly for the healing of which Christ died, there are three things: namely *stain, guilt* and *offence.* It is to be noted that the death of Christ *blots out the stain, takes away the punishment, reconciles the offence.* — The death of Christ then *blots out fault;* the Apocalypse, the first chapter[^p591-7]: « He hath washed us from our sins in his own blood »; to the Romans, the sixth chapter: « All we who are baptized in Christ Jesus are baptized in his death », *baptized,* that is, washed. — The death of Christ therefore *takes away the punishment;* Isaias, the fifty-third chapter[^p591-8]: « He hath delivered his soul unto death and was reputed with the wicked, and he hath borne the sins of many ». — The death of Christ *reconciles the offence;* to the Romans, the fifth chapter: « When we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son ». — So then the death of Christ is *a remedy against evil.*

3. The death of Christ is also *a suffrage toward good.* For it gives suffrage toward the good *of pardon,* toward the good
<!-- page 592 -->
*of grace* and toward the good *of glory.* — The death of Christ *obtains pardon;* whence to the Hebrews, the ninth chapter[^p592-1]: « Therefore he is the mediator of the new testament, that, by means of his death, for the redemption of those transgressions which were under the former testament, they that are called may receive the promise of eternal inheritance »; and therefore, to the Hebrews, the twelfth chapter, the Apostle strengthens the faithful, saying: « You are come to Jesus the mediator of the new testament, and to the sprinkling of blood which speaketh better than that of Abel »; therefore *speaking better,* because the blood of Abel spoke vengeance, but the blood of Christ speaks pardon. — Likewise, the death of Christ *merits grace;* whence the first of Peter, the third chapter[^p592-2]: « Christ died once for our sins etc., that he might offer us to God, being put to death indeed in the flesh, but enlivened in the spirit ». — Likewise, the death of Christ *opens the gate.* « For by his own blood he entered once into the holies, having obtained eternal redemption », to the Hebrews, the ninth chapter; and this opening was proved by the bringing into the kingdom of those who before his death were held captive in limbo; of which it was said, Osee, the thirteenth chapter: « O death, I will be thy death; O hell, I will be thy bite ». — These things then the death of Christ does for us.

4. Consider afterwards what it ought to do *in us;* and attend that the death of Christ is *to be loved, to be preached* and *to be imitated* by us. — For we ought *to love* Christ dead for us; whence to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p592-3], the Apostle says: « Christ Jesus that died, yea that is risen also again, who is at the right hand of the Father, who also maketh intercession for us »; and afterwards in the same he exclaims: « Who then shall separate us from the charity of Christ »? And to the Romans, the fifth chapter: « God commendeth his charity towards us, because when as yet we were sinners, according to the time, Christ died for us ». — We ought also *to preach* Christ dead for us; whence the Apostle, the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p592-4]: « I judged not myself to know anything among you but Jesus Christ, and him crucified ». — We ought also *to imitate* Christ dead for us; whence the second to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « Always bearing about in our body the mortification of Christ Jesus, that the life also of Jesus may be made manifest in our bodies ».

5. Note moreover that to imitate the death of Christ we *are bound* by debt, *are drawn* by promise, *are encouraged* by example. — We *are bound* by debt; for Christ purchased our life by his death; the second to the Corinthians, the fifth chapter[^p592-5]: « Christ died for all, that they also who live may not now live to themselves » etc. — We *are drawn* by promise; whence to the Romans, the sixth chapter: « If we have been planted together in the likeness of his death, we shall be also in the likeness of his resurrection »; and in the same: « If we be dead with Christ, we believe that we shall live also together with him ». — We *are encouraged* by example; whence the first of Peter, the second chapter[^p592-6]: « Christ suffered for us, leaving you an example, that you should follow his steps » etc.; and the first of Peter, the fourth chapter: « Christ therefore having suffered in the flesh, be you also armed with the same thought ».

### Annotation 40.

(Vers. 5.). *Now the Lord loved Mary, Martha and Lazarus.*

By these three note three kinds of men loved by God. — By Mary understand *the contemplatives;* by Martha, *the actives;* by Lazarus, *the penitents.* You have them signified, Ezechiel, the fourteenth chapter[^p592-7], by Noe, Daniel and Job, who « shall deliver their own souls by their justice »; the same also by Peter, James and John, Matthew, the seventeenth chapter, who go up with the Lord into the mountain. For these three kinds of men are delivered from sin and are loved by God and are taken up into the mountain of glory.

### Annotation 41.

(Vers. 35.). *And Jesus wept.*

Note that he wept three times. He wept at the raising again, John, the eleventh chapter[^p592-8]; whence it is said here: *Jesus wept.* — Likewise, Jesus wept when he drew near to the city; Luke, the nineteenth chapter: « Seeing the city, Jesus wept over it, saying: If thou also hadst known »! — He wept also in the passion; to the Hebrews, the fifth chapter: « With a strong cry and tears offering, he was heard for his reverence ». — In the passion he wept, suffering with the misery *of fault;* at the grave he wept, suffering with the misery *of punishment;* over the city he wept, suffering with *ignorance.*

### Annotation 42.

(Vers. 43.). *And when he had said these things, he cried with a loud voice: Lazarus, come forth.*

Note that Christ is read to have cried out three times. For he cried out in the *temple;* John, the seventh chapter[^p592-9]: « Jesus stood and cried, saying: If any man thirst, let him come to me and drink ». — He cried out at the *sepulchre* of Lazarus; whence here: *When he had said these things, he cried* etc. — He cried out
<!-- page 593 -->
on the *cross;* Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter: « And Jesus again crying with a loud voice yielded up the ghost ». — Standing in the temple he cried out to the people; standing at the sepulchre he cried out to Lazarus; hanging on the cross he cried out to the Father. The first cry was for the instructing of the people's *ignorance;* the second cry, for the relieving of *misery;* the third, for the forgiving of the *fault* of the human race. Whence he relieved by crying out those things with which he suffered by weeping[^p593-1].

### Annotation 43.

(Vers. 39.). *Take away the stone.*

Note that sin is called a *stone,* because it *covers;* the Psalm[^p593-2]: « They are covered with their iniquity and their wickedness ». — Because it *presses down;* the Psalm: « As a heavy burden they are become heavy upon me ». — Because it *shuts in;* Isaias, the sixty-first chapter: « That I should proclaim opening to them that are shut up ».

Or sin is called a *stone* for this reason, that it has *opacity, hardness* and *heaviness.* — For it *darkens* the understanding; Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter[^p593-3]: « Error and darkness are created together with sinners ». — It *hardens* the will; Jeremias, the seventeenth chapter: « They have hardened their necks, that they might not hear me ». — It *weighs down* virtue; Gregory: « Sin which is not washed away by penance soon by its own weight draws to another ».

### Annotation 44.

(Vers. 44.). *He came forth that had been dead, bound feet and hands with winding bands, and his face was bound about with a napkin.*

1. Note that there were three things bound in the dead man: *face, feet* and *hands.* For sin binds the *face* of thought[^p593-4], so that the sinner cannot think again of God; the *feet* of affection, so that he cannot recover himself from sin; the *hands* of operation, so that he cannot advance in good.

Or otherwise: the *feet* of the affections are bound through evil *love,* lest the sinner detest his fault; whence the Lamentations, the first chapter[^p593-5]: « He hath spread a net for my feet ». — The *face* is bound through evil *shame,* lest he confess his sin; the Psalm: « The shame of my face hath covered me ». — The *hands* are bound through inordinate *torpor,* lest he make satisfaction for sin; whence, Acts, the twelfth chapter, Peter was bound in his hands; whence it is read in the same place that at the coming of the Angel « the chains fell off from his hands ». For this is the threefold manner of binding a captive, namely that he be chained by the feet, the neck and the hands.

2. *He came forth,* he says, *that had been dead.*

Note that the Lord raised three dead: *the girl* in the chamber, Luke, the eighth chapter[^p593-6]; *the young man* at the gate, Luke, the seventh chapter; *Lazarus* from the grave, John, the eleventh chapter. By *the girl* are signified those sinning in will alone; by *the young man,* those sinning in deed; by *Lazarus,* those sinning in custom.
