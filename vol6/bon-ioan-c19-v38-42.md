# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XIX, Vers. 38-42
## *Quarto, de Christi sepultura notantur tria*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 502 -->

#### Quarto, de Christi sepultura notantur tria.

61. *Post haec autem rogavit.* Supra[^p502-6] actum est de Domini comprehensione, condemnatione et passione; hic agitur de quarto, scilicet de *sepulturae conditione,* et hoc ordine agitur. Primo ponitur *suspensi corporis depositio;* secundo, *depositi conditio,* ibi[^p502-7]: *Venit autem et Nicodemus;* tertio, *conditi in sepulcro collocatio,* ibi: *Erat autem in loco* etc. Primum factum est a Ioseph, secundum a Nicodemo, tertium ab utroque.

Ioseph ergo deposuit corpus Iesu; et quia non poterat nisi per licentiam, ideo *petiit* et *petitum obtinuit* et *obtentum deposuit.*

(Vers. 38.). *Petiit,* quia discipulus erat Christi, quamvis occultus; propterea dicit: *Post haec,* id est post passionis consummationem, *rogavit Pilatum Ioseph ab Arimathaea.* Arimathaea idem est quod Ramatha, villa Elcanae, Samuelis et Annae[^p502-8]; de qua primi Regum primo in principio. Ioseph scilicet rogavit, *eo quod esset discipulus Iesu, occultus autem propter metum Iudaeorum,* quia, sicut dicitur supra nono[^p502-9], « iam conspiraverant Iudaei, ut, si quis eum confiteretur Christum, extra synagogam fieret ». Etsi esset occultus, tamen verus, quia dilexit non tantum vivum, sed etiam mortuum; Proverbiorum decimo septimo: « Omni tempore diligit qui amicus est ». *Rogavit,* inquam, *ut tolleret corpus Iesu,* id est, de cruce deponeret, et *obtinuit;* propterea dicit: *Et permisit Pilatus,* ut scilicet sepeliretur. *Venit ergo et tulit corpus Iesu,* venit scilicet a loco, ubi erat Pilatus, ad crucem et *deposuit.*

62. (Vers. 39.). *Venit autem et Nicodemus.* Hic agitur de *conditione* ex aromatibus facta a Nicodemo, et tangitur *devotio* Nicodemi in *apportatione multorum aromatum* et in *conditione corporis.* *Venit autem et Nicodemus, qui* scilicet *venerat ad Iesum nocte primum,* supra tertio[^p502-10]. Agitur de hoc adventu, in quo innuitur ratio adveniendi, quia erat etiam discipulus, quamvis occultus; *ferens mixturam myrrhae et aloes,* id est unguentum ex his confectum, quod servabat corpus a putrefactione.
<!-- page 503 -->
Nondum enim credebat resurrectionem, sicut nec discipuli; tamen multum amabat, unde dicit: *Quasi libras centum;* non enim intelligebat adhuc quod in Psalmo[^p503-1] dicitur: « Non dabis Sanctum tuum videre corruptionem ».

63. (Vers. 40.). *Acceperunt ergo.* Tangitur devotio Nicodemi in *corporis conditione.* *Acceperunt ergo,* ipse scilicet et Ioseph, *corpus Iesu et ligaverunt illud linteis et aromatibus*[^p503-2], perunxerunt, supple; *sicut mos est Iudaeis sepelire,* scilicet honorifice; Genesis ultimo: « Praecepit Ioseph servis suis medicis, ut aromatibus condirent patrem ». Chrysostomus[^p503-3]: « Sepeliunt ipsum, non ut condemnatum, sed, ut consuetudo est Iudaeis, multum scilicet honorifice, ut magnum quendam et mirabilem ».

64. (Vers. 41.). *Erat autem in loco.* Hic ponitur tertium, scilicet *in sepulcro collocatio.* Et ratio, quare in loco illo collocatur, duplex innuitur, scilicet *loci congruitas* et *temporis brevitas: loci congruitas,* quia propinquum et novum; unde dicit: *Erat autem in loco, ubi crucifixus est, hortus et in horto monumentum novum;* et ideo novum, *in quo* scilicet *nondum quisquam positus fuerat*[^p503-4]. Et tale monumentum Domino congruebat, ut non alterius aestimaretur facta resurrectio, qui cum Domino iaceret. Augustinus dicit, quod « sicut in Mariae Virginis utero nemo ante illum, nemo post illum conceptus fuit; ita in hoc monumento nemo ante illum, nemo post illum sepultus est ».

65. (Vers. 42.). *Ibi ergo propter parasceven Iudaeorum,* et ita solemnitas imminebat, propter quam nec longe ferre licebat; *quia iuxta erat monumentum,* id est prope, *posuerunt eum*[^p503-5], scilicet Iesum. Augustinus: « Acceleratam vult intelligi sepulturam, ne advesperasceret, quando iam propter parasceven, quam *coenam puram* Iudaei Latine usitatius apud nos vocant, facere tale aliquid non licebat ».

### Quaestiones.

66. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic: quare nullus discipulorum Domini accessit ad sepeliendum ipsum? — *Si dicas,* quod timore Iudaeorum non audebant; sed Ioseph multo magis timebat: ergo multo magis debebat iste dimittere[^p503-6].

**Respondeo:** Ratio fuit et *humana* et *divina: humana,* quia, etsi discipuli et iste timerent Iudaeos, tamen iste erat nobilis persona, sicut dicitur Marci decimo quinto[^p503-7]; et ideo confidebat de impetratione, quia notus erat Pilato. Ratio *divina,* quia, si discipuli sepelissent, probabile argumentum habuissent Iudaei, quod corpus eius postmodum furati essent[^p503-8].

67. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de *metu Ioseph,* utrum fuerit bonus. — Quod *non,* videtur, quia magis est amanda salus propria quam confusio mundana: ergo si iste non audebat nomen Christi confiteri propter metum, malus erat metus[^p503-9].

*Sed contrarium* dicitur Lucae vigesimo tertio[^p503-10], quod *erat vir bonus et iustus.*

**Respondeo:** Timor ille non erat *iniquitatis,* sed cuiusdam *naturalis infirmitatis,* qua homo timet pudorem; nec fuit *culpa,* quia non tantum timuit, ut negaret, ut Petrus, sed non, ut publice praedicaret; et hoc non erat tunc necessitatis, sed perfectionis. Ambrosius[^p503-11]: « Quid mirum, si occultatur iustus, cum occultabantur et Apostoli, iustorum magistri » ?

68. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de *devotione*[^p503-12] *Nicodemi.* — Videtur enim, quod peccaverit: quia, sicut peccatum est in tenacitate, sic peccatum est in superflua effusione; sed superflua fuit unguenti effusio, quia nullius necessitatis vel utilitatis; et praeterea non erat opus tanto.

**Respondeo,** quod laudatur devotio Nicodemi; nec fuit ibi perditio, sed devotio, sicut dicitur supra
<!-- page 504 -->
duodecimo[^p504-1]; et sicut non potest homo servire Divinitati nimium, sic corpori Christi pro nobis crucifixo nullus potest superfluum impendere obsequium. Ideo, etsi superfluitas fuisset circa alium, non tamen circa illud corpus sanctissimum.

Quod obiicit: Non indigebat; dicendum, quod ipse credebat indigere, et hoc quidem non erat laudis, sed ignorantiae. Sed in hoc, quod dignum magno honore credebat et illi impendebat, devotio commendatur[^p504-2].

69. Quaest. IV. Sed quaeritur ex *parte Domini:* cum ipse Dominus omnem formam debuerit nobis dare perfectionis; et ipse doceat Matthaei octavo[^p504-3] contemnere sepulturam: videtur, quod sua dispositione in loco vili et contemptibiliter debuerit se facere sepeliri, sicut et viliter pati.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod nobis contemnenda est sepultura propter fidem resurrectionis; quia credimus, quod ubicumque pulveres sunt dispersi, a Domino in unum colligentur nec effugient divinae cognitionis et virtutis potentiam[^p504-4]. Sed sepultura Christi erat ad fidem nostrae resurrectionis astruendam; in hoc enim, quod credimus, caput nostrum resurrexisse, et nos credimus resurrecturos. Et ideo, ut resurrectio eius esset certa, debuit in loco patenti et honorabiliter sepeliri et a militibus etiam custodiri ad suspicionem tollendam[^p504-5].

Quod ergo obiicit, quod exemplum dare debuit, quod non curaremus de vilitate sepulturae; dicendum, quod sic debuit dare exemplum morum, ut tamen fidei non praeiudicaret, ut sic currat humilitas simul et veritas. Nihilominus tamen satis dedit exemplum, quod non est curandum de sepultura. Si enim, dum viveret, de poenis et ignominia corporis non curavit; ostendit post mortem, multo minus esse curandum de vilitate corporis exanimis[^p504-6].

## English
<!-- page 502 -->

#### Fourth, on the burial of Christ three things are noted.

61. *And after these things he besought.* Above[^p502-6] it was treated of the Lord's apprehension, condemnation and passion; here it is treated of the fourth, namely of the *condition of the burial,* and it is treated in this order. First is set down the *taking down of the hung body;* second, the *embalming of the one taken down,* there[^p502-7]: *And Nicodemus also came;* third, the *placing in the sepulchre of the one embalmed,* there: *Now there was in the place* etc. The first was done by Joseph, the second by Nicodemus, the third by both.

Joseph, therefore, took down the body of Jesus; and because he could not except by leave, therefore he *asked,* and *obtained what he asked,* and *took down what he obtained.*

(Vers. 38.). *He asked,* because he was a disciple of Christ, though a hidden one; on this account he says: *After these things,* that is, after the consummation of the passion, *Joseph of Arimathaea besought Pilate.* Arimathaea is the same as Ramatha, the town of Elcana, of Samuel and of Anna[^p502-8]; of which the first of Kings, the first chapter, at the beginning. Joseph, namely, besought, *because he was a disciple of Jesus, but hidden for fear of the Jews,* since, as is said above, the ninth chapter[^p502-9], « the Jews had already conspired that, if any man should confess him to be Christ, he should be put out of the synagogue ». Though he was hidden, yet he was true, because he loved him not only living, but also dead; Proverbs, the seventeenth chapter: « He that is a friend loveth at all times ». *He besought,* I say, *that he might take away the body of Jesus,* that is, take it down from the cross, and he *obtained;* on this account he says: *And Pilate permitted,* namely, that he should be buried. *He came therefore and took away the body of Jesus,* he came, namely, from the place where Pilate was, to the cross, and *took it down.*

62. (Vers. 39.). *And Nicodemus also came.* Here it is treated of the *embalming* made with spices by Nicodemus, and the *devotion* of Nicodemus is touched upon in the *bringing of many spices* and in the *embalming of the body.* *And Nicodemus also came, who* namely *at the first had come to Jesus by night,* above, the third chapter[^p502-10]. It is treated of this coming, in which the reason for coming is intimated, because he too was a disciple, though a hidden one; *bringing a mixture of myrrh and aloes,* that is, an ointment made of these, which preserved the body from putrefaction.
<!-- page 503 -->
For not yet did he believe the resurrection, as neither did the disciples; yet he loved much, whence he says: *About a hundred pounds;* for he did not yet understand what is said in the Psalm[^p503-1]: « Thou wilt not give thy Holy One to see corruption ».

63. (Vers. 40.). *They took therefore.* The devotion of Nicodemus is touched upon in the *embalming of the body.* *They took therefore,* namely he and Joseph, *the body of Jesus and bound it in linen cloths and with spices*[^p503-2], they anointed it, supply; *as the manner of the Jews is to bury,* namely honourably; Genesis, the last chapter: « Joseph commanded his servants the physicians to embalm his father with spices ». Chrysostom[^p503-3]: « They bury him, not as one condemned, but, as the custom is with the Jews, very honourably, namely, as some great and wonderful man ».

64. (Vers. 41.). *Now there was in the place.* Here the third is set down, namely the *placing in the sepulchre.* And the reason why he is placed in that place is intimated as twofold, namely the *fittingness of the place* and the *shortness of the time: the fittingness of the place,* because it was near and new; whence he says: *Now there was in the place where he was crucified a garden, and in the garden a new sepulchre;* and therefore new, *in which* namely *no one yet had been laid*[^p503-4]. And such a sepulchre befitted the Lord, so that the resurrection should not be thought to have been made of another who lay with the Lord. Augustine says that « as in the womb of the Virgin Mary none before him, none after him was conceived; so in this sepulchre none before him, none after him was buried ».

65. (Vers. 42.). *There, therefore, because of the parasceve of the Jews,* and so the solemnity was at hand, on account of which it was not lawful to carry him far either; *because the sepulchre was nigh,* that is, near, *they laid him*[^p503-5], namely Jesus. Augustine: « He wishes the burial to be understood as hastened, lest it should grow toward evening, when already, on account of the parasceve, which the Jews call in Latin, more usually among us, the *pure supper,* it was not lawful to do any such thing ».

### Questions.

66. Quaest. I. But it is asked here: why did none of the Lord's disciples come forward to bury him? — *If you say* that for fear of the Jews they did not dare; but Joseph feared much more: therefore much more ought this man to have left it alone[^p503-6].

**I respond:** The reason was both *human* and *divine: human,* because, although the disciples and this man feared the Jews, yet this man was a noble person, as is said in Mark, the fifteenth chapter[^p503-7]; and therefore he had confidence of obtaining it, because he was known to Pilate. The *divine* reason, because, if the disciples had buried him, the Jews would have had a probable argument that they had afterwards stolen his body[^p503-8].

67. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked of the *fear of Joseph,* whether it was good. — That it was *not,* it seems, because one's own salvation is more to be loved than worldly confusion: therefore, if this man did not dare to confess the name of Christ on account of fear, the fear was bad[^p503-9].

*But the contrary* is said in Luke, the twenty-third chapter[^p503-10], that *he was a good and just man.*

**I respond:** That fear was not of *iniquity,* but of a certain *natural infirmity,* by which a man fears shame; nor was it a *fault,* because he did not fear so much as to deny, as Peter did, but so as not to preach publicly; and this was not then of necessity, but of perfection. Ambrose[^p503-11]: « What wonder, if the just man is hidden, when the Apostles too were hidden, the masters of the just » ?

68. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked of the *devotion*[^p503-12] *of Nicodemus.* — For it seems that he sinned: because, as there is sin in tenacity, so there is sin in superfluous pouring out; but the pouring out of the ointment was superfluous, because it was of no necessity or utility; and moreover there was no need of so much.

**I respond** that the devotion of Nicodemus is praised; nor was there waste there, but devotion, as is said above,
<!-- page 504 -->
the twelfth chapter[^p504-1]; and as a man cannot serve the Divinity too much, so no one can spend a superfluous service on the body of Christ crucified for us. Therefore, even if there would have been superfluity in regard to another, yet not in regard to that most holy body.

As to what he objects: It had no need; it must be said that he believed it to have need, and this indeed was not of praise, but of ignorance. But in this, that he believed it worthy of great honour and spent it upon it, his devotion is commended[^p504-2].

69. Quaest. IV. But it is asked on the *part of the Lord:* since the Lord himself ought to have given us every form of perfection, and he himself teaches in Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p504-3], to despise burial: it seems that by his own disposition he ought to have had himself buried in a vile place and contemptibly, just as he also suffered vilely.

**I respond:** It must be said that burial is to be despised by us on account of the faith in the resurrection; because we believe that wherever the dusts are dispersed, they will be gathered into one by the Lord, nor will they escape the power of the divine cognition and might[^p504-4]. But the burial of Christ was for establishing the faith of our resurrection; for in this, that we believe our head to have risen again, we also believe that we shall rise again. And therefore, that his resurrection might be certain, he ought to be buried in an open place and honourably, and also to be guarded by soldiers, to take away suspicion[^p504-5].

As to what he objects, then, that he ought to have given an example, that we should not care about the vileness of burial; it must be said that he ought so to give an example of morals as yet not to prejudice the faith, so that humility and truth might run together. Nevertheless he gave example enough that one is not to care about burial. For if, while he lived, he did not care about the pains and ignominy of the body; he showed that after death there is much less to care about the vileness of the lifeless body[^p504-6].
