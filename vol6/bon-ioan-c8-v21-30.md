# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. VIII, Vers. 21-30
## *Ostensum est supra, quod doctrina Christi liberat a caecitate erroris, hic ostenditur et commendatur quod liberat a servitute peccati*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 360 -->

30. *Dixit ergo iterum eis Iesus.* Ostensum est supra, quod doctrina Christi liberat a caecitate erroris, hic ostenditur et commendatur quod liberat *a servitute peccati.* Et dividitur haec pars: quia primo ostenditur, quod sine cognitione Christi *servitus perpetuatur;* secundo, quod per doctrinam et sermonem Christi homo *a servitute liberatur,* ibi[^p360-11]: *Dicebat ergo Iesus ad eos qui orediderunt.*

Primum ergo ostenditur hoc ordine: primo ponitur perpetuandae *servitutis comminatio;* secundo, propter Iudaeorum dubitationem *comminationis expositio;* tertio, *explicatio credibilium;* quarto, *multiplicatio credentium.*

(Vers. 21.). Primo ergo ponitur perpetuandae *servitutis comminatio;* et haec est, quando homo moritur in peccato; et comminatur eis finalem impoenitentiam. Propterea dicit:

31. *Dixit ergo eis iterum Iesus: Ego vado,* scilicet ad Patrem per passionem; *et quaeretis me,* ad persequendum scilicet; *et in peccato vestro moriemini,* et ego eripiar de manibus vestris. Et ratio subditur: *Quo ego vado, vos non potestis venire,* quia non poterant in gloriam introire. Unde comminatus est eis mortem culpae et amissionem gloriae. Hoc dixit Dominus Iudaeis; simile dixit discipulis: « Quo ego vado, vos non potestis venire », infra decimo tertio[^p360-12]. Sed Iudaeis dixit *comminando,* discipulis praedixit *differendo;* unde post dixit Petro: « Quo ego vado, non potes me modo sequi, sequeris autem postea ».

32. (Vers. 22.). *Dixerunt*[^p360-13] *ergo Iudaei.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet propter dubitationem Iudaeorum *comminationis expositio.* Dubitabant enim de illo quod dixerat: *Quo ego vado, vos non potestis venire;* propterea dicunt: *Nunquid interficiet semetipsum, quia dicit: Quo ego vado, vos non potestis venire?* Augustinus[^p360-14]: « Stulta verba, omnino insipientiae plena. Si enim semetipsum interficeret, nonne ipsi similiter possent? Si de morte locutus est,
<!-- page 361 -->
nonne omnes morituri erant »? Ob hanc dubitationem ponit Dominus suae comminationis explanationem; et duo dixerat, ideo duo explanat: primo illud quod dixerat: *Quo ego vado, vos non potestis venire.* Quia enim hoc dixerat de eundo ad Patrem per gloriam, ad quam non possent conscendere peccatores; ideo dicit:

33. (Vers. 23.). *Et dicebat eis: Vos de deorsum estis;* supra tertio[^p361-1]: « Qui de terra est de terra loquitur »; *ego autem de supernis sum,* et ideo ego sursum vado; et propterea *quo ego vado, vos non potestis venire;* quia, sicut dicitur supra tertio, « nemo ascendit in caelum, nisi qui descendit de caelo »; ideo vos descenditis, ego autem ascendo. Rursus: *Vos estis de hoc mundo,* quia huic mundo adhaeretis per amorem; *ego autem*[^p361-2] *non sum de hoc mundo,* ideo ego relinquo vos in mundo, et « quo vado, vos non potestis venire »; infra decimo sexto: « Exivi a Patre et veni in mundum; iterum relinquo mundum et vado ad Patrem ». Sic explanat quod dixerat: *Quo ego vado;* hoc intelligendum est per virtutem, non per infirmitatem. Exponit etiam quod dixit[^p361-3]: *In peccato vestro moriemini;* unde istud replicat, ut explicet:

34. (Vers. 24.). *Dixi ergo vobis, quod*[^p361-4] *moriemini in peccatis vestris,* et explano, quomodo intelligo. *Si enim non credideritis, quia ego sum, moriemini in peccato vestro.* Si autem credideritis, non moriemini, *ab oppositis* est intelligendum; unde Augustinus[^p361-5]: « Reddita est spes desperantibus, excitatio facta est dormientibus »; supra tertio: « Qui incredulus est Filio non habet vitam, sed ira Dei manet super eum; qui autem credit in Filium Dei habet vitam aeternam ». Hoc promiserat.

35. (Vers. 25.). *Dicebant ergo* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *explicatio credibilium.* Quia enim comminationem suam exposuerat, quod sine fide eius nullus poterat liberari a morte, dicens, quod in peccato essent morituri, nisi crederent, *quia ego sum* — hoc quidem implicite dixerat — ideo[^p361-6] quaerunt Iudaei explicari et in fide doceri. Propterea *dicebant ei: Tu quis es?* ex quo salvari non possumus, nisi in te credamus. Similiter quaerebant a Ioanne supra primo[^p361-7]: « Tu quis es »? Et sequitur responsio Christi, qua instruit Iudaeos, credere in se primo quae respiciunt creaturam per ipsum factam; secundo, quae respiciunt Patris personam, ibi[^p361-8]: *Et non cognoverunt.* — Primo namque est *creator;* propter quod ait: *Dixit eis Iesus: Principium,* id est, ego sum principium creans; quia omnia per ipsum acceperunt *esse,* sicut dictum est supra primo capitulo[^p361-9]: « In principio erat Verbum », et rursus: « Omnia per ipsum facta sunt ». — Est etiam *doctor; qui et loquor vobis;* ad Hebraeos primo[^p361-10]: « Novissime locutus est nobis in Filio, quem constituit heredem universorum, per quem fecit et saecula ». Similiter Dominus respondit Samaritanae quaerenti: « Ego sum, qui loquor tecum »; et caeco, infra nono. — Est etiam *iudex,* quamvis nunc non loquatur verba condemnationis, sed instructionis[^p361-11] et vocationis. Propterea dicit:

36. (Vers. 26.). *Multa habeo de vobis loqui; de*[^p361-12], id est contra vos, *et iudicare,* quae non remanebunt indiscussa, non ob malitiam, sed ob iustitiam. *Sed qui misit me verax est, et ego quae audivi ab eo, haec loquor in mundo;* et ideo iudico veraciter; supra quinto[^p361-13]: « Sicut audio, iudico, et iudicium meum iustum est; quia non quaero voluntatem meam, sed eius qui misit me ». Et quia se manifestavit in comparatione ad *creaturam,* manifestat se in comparatione ad *Patris personam,* quam Iudaei intelligere non poterant; ideo dicit:

37. (Vers. 27.). *Et non cognoverunt, quia Patrem eius dicebat Deum;* et quia non valebant intelligere altitudinem generationis, mittit eos ad infirmitatem passionis, cum dicit:

38. (Vers. 28.). *Dixit ergo eis Iesus: Cum exaltaveritis Filium hominis,* scilicet in cruce; infra duodecimo[^p361-14]: « Et ego, si exaltatus fuero a terra, omnia traham ad me ipsum »; *tunc cognoscetis, quia ego sum,* scilicet distincta in Trinitate persona; ecce, *personalis distinctio;* Exodi tertio: « Ego sum, qui sum ». *Et a me ipso facio nihil,* scilicet hoc cognoscetis;
<!-- page 362 -->
*sed sicut docuit me Pater, haec loquor,* id est, sicut accepi a Patre, quia Pater omnia dat Filio. Et in hoc ostenditur generationis emanatio a Patre; supra quinto[^p362-1]: « Non potest Filius a se facere quidquam, nisi quod viderit Patrem facientem ». Non solum cognoscetis personalem distinctionem, sed etiam *essentiae indivisionem;* ideo dicit:

39. (Vers. 29.). *Et qui misit me mecum est,* propter essentiae unitatem, in qua non est divisio; infra decimo quarto[^p362-2]: « Nonne creditis, quia ego in Patre, et Pater in me est »? *Et non reliquit me solum,* per voluntatem discordantem; *quia quae placita sunt ei facio semper.* Unde Filius non potest relinqui a Patre, quia non potest ei nec discordare nec displicere; Matthaei tertio[^p362-3]: « Hic est Filius meus dilectus, in quo mihi bene complacui ». E contrario peccatores a Domino relinquuntur, quia ei displicent per peccata; secundi Paralipomenon duodecimo: « Vos reliquistis me, et ego reliqui vos ».

40. (Vers. 30.). *Haec illo loquente.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *multiplicatio credentium* in Christum, quae facta est ad hanc instructionem; ideo dicit: *Haec illo loquente,* quae scilicet praedicta sunt, *multi crediderunt in eum;* quia sermo eius habebat efficaciam; supra quarto[^p362-4]: « Multo plures crediderunt propter sermonem eius ».

### Quaestiones.

41. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod dicit: *In peccatis vestris moriemini.* — Videtur, quod revelaverit eis damnationem suam, ergo cogebat eos desperare; et secundum hoc videtur, quod aliquis possit esse praescius suae damnationis[^p362-5]. — Sed hoc non videtur; quia scientia et revelatio donum est pietatis: ergo non cogit desperare.

Ad hoc respondetur tripliciter, quod hoc Dominus non dixit revelando, sed comminando; unde infra[^p362-6] addit conditionem: *nisi credideritis.* — Aliter dicendum, quod hoc Dominus non dixit hominibus, qui ei fidem adhiberent; ideo non cogebat desperare; unde hoc non dixit credentibus, sed incredulis. — Tertio modo dicitur, quod Dominus loquebatur multitudini; ideo generaliter accipiebatur verbum, et nemo specialiter pro se[^p362-7].

42. Quaest. II. Item, cum *principium* dicat rationem emanationis, quae primo et principaliter convenit Patri; unde est hoc, quod Filius sibi appropriat?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod hoc nomen *principium* aliquando accipitur *essentialiter,* secundum quod dicit respectum ad creaturam, et sic tota Trinitas dicitur *principium;* aliquando *notionaliter,* et sic Pater et Filius sunt *unum principium* Spiritus sancti; aliquando *personaliter,* et sic *principium* dicit ipsam fontalem emanationem, quae reperitur in Patre. Et sic dicit Augustinus[^p362-8], quod « Pater est principium Divinitatis ». — Dicendum est igitur, quod hoc nomen *principium* secundum quod *essentiale* trahitur ad personam Filii dupliciter: uno modo per *proprietatem generationis,* et sic dicitur Filius *principium de principio,* id est genitum; alio modo per *proprietatem Verbi,* quia Filius est Verbum, et sic trahitur hic: *Ego principium, qui et loquor vobis*[^p362-9].

43. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur: cum *veritas* Filio approprietur, unde est hoc, quod Filius, de Patre loquens, sibi attribuit[^p362-10]?

Dicendum, quod *verax* dicitur, quia verba vera dicit. Quia ergo Pater dicit Filium, qui est Verbum verum, cui veritas appropriatur, *verax* ratione originis connotatae recte potest appropriari Patri[^p362-11].

44. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *non reliquit me solum, quia quae placita sunt ei facio semper.* — *Contra:* Matthaei vigesimo septimo[^p362-12] dixit Filius in passione: *Deus, Deus meus, ut quid me dereliquisti?*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod est *derelictio dispensationis, probationis, permissionis, aeternae*
<!-- page 363 -->
*damnationis.* Prima, *dispensationis,* fuit Filium passioni exponere ob generis humani salutem[^p363-1]. Secunda, *probationis,* est in iustis, sicut reliquit Dominus Iob in manu satan. Tertia est in peccantibus in praesenti. Quarta est in damnatis aeternaliter, quae est derelictio sine visitatione ulla. Verbum igitur Domini hic intelligitur de derelictione iracundiae, non de dispensatione divinae sapientiae[^p363-2].

## English
<!-- page 360 -->

30. *Again therefore Jesus said to them.* It was shown above that the teaching[^tr-doctrina] of Christ frees from the blindness of error; here it is shown and commended that it frees *from the servitude of sin.* And this part is divided: because first it is shown that without the knowledge of Christ *servitude is perpetuated;* second, that through the teaching and word of Christ man *is freed from servitude,* there[^p360-11]: *Jesus therefore said to those who orediderunt* [so printed: *orediderunt* for *crediderunt*].

The first, then, is shown in this order: first is set down the *threat of a servitude to be perpetuated;* second, because of the doubt of the Jews, *the exposition of the threat;* third, *the explication of the things to be believed;* fourth, *the multiplication of believers.*

(V. 21.). First, then, is set down the *threat of a servitude to be perpetuated;* and this is when a man dies in sin; and he threatens them with final impenitence. Therefore he says:

31. *Jesus therefore said to them again: I go,* namely to the Father through the passion; *and you shall seek me,* namely to persecute me; *and you shall die in your sin,* and I shall be snatched out of your hands. And the reason is subjoined: *Whither I go, you cannot come,* because they could not enter into glory. Whence he threatened them with the death of guilt and the loss of glory. This the Lord said to the Jews; something like it he said to the disciples: « Whither I go, you cannot come », below, in the thirteenth chapter[^p360-12]. But to the Jews he said it *by threatening,* to the disciples he foretold it *by deferring;* whence afterwards he said to Peter: « Whither I go, thou canst not follow me now, but thou shalt follow hereafter ».

32. (V. 22.). *The Jews therefore said*[^p360-13]. Here the second is touched, namely, because of the doubt of the Jews, *the exposition of the threat.* For they doubted of that which he had said: *Whither I go, you cannot come;* therefore they say: *Will he kill himself, because he saith: Whither I go, you cannot come?* Augustine[^p360-14]: « Foolish words, altogether full of folly. For if he were to kill himself, could not they likewise do so? If he spoke of death,
<!-- page 361 -->
were not all going to die »? Because of this doubt the Lord sets down the explanation of his threat; and he had said two things, therefore he explains two: first, that which he had said: *Whither I go, you cannot come.* For because he had said this of going to the Father through glory, to which sinners could not climb up; therefore he says:

33. (V. 23.). *And he said to them: You are from beneath;* above, in the third chapter[^p361-1]: « He that is of the earth speaketh of the earth »; *but I am from above,* and therefore I go upward; and therefore *whither I go, you cannot come;* because, as is said above in the third chapter, « no man hath ascended into heaven, but he that descended from heaven »; therefore you descend, but I ascend. Again: *You are of this world,* because you cleave to this world through love; *but I*[^p361-2] *am not of this world,* therefore I leave you in the world, and « whither I go, you cannot come »; below, in the sixteenth chapter: « I came forth from the Father and am come into the world; again I leave the world and I go to the Father ». Thus he explains what he had said: *Whither I go;* this is to be understood through power, not through infirmity. He expounds also what he said[^p361-3]: *You shall die in your sin;* whence he repeats it, that he may explicate it:

34. (V. 24.). *Therefore I said to you, that*[^p361-4] *you shall die in your sins,* and I explain how I understand it. *For if you believe not that I am, you shall die in your sin.* But if you believe, you shall not die; it is to be understood *from the opposites;* whence Augustine[^p361-5]: « Hope has been given back to the despairing, a rousing has been made for the sleeping »; above, in the third chapter: « He that is incredulous to the Son hath not life, but the wrath of God abideth on him; but he that believeth in the Son of God hath life everlasting ». This he had promised.

35. (V. 25.). *They said therefore* etc. Here the third is touched, namely *the explication of the things to be believed.* For because he had expounded his threat, that without faith in him no one could be freed from death, saying that they would die in sin unless they believed *that I am* — this indeed he had said implicitly — therefore[^p361-6] the Jews ask to have it made explicit and to be taught in the faith. Therefore *they said to him: Who art thou?* since we cannot be saved unless we believe in thee. In like manner they asked of John, above, in the first chapter[^p361-7]: « Who art thou »? And there follows the response of Christ, by which he instructs the Jews to believe in him: first, the things that regard the creature made through him; second, those that regard the person of the Father, there[^p361-8]: *And they understood not.* — For first he is *creator;* on account of which he says: *Jesus said to them: The beginning,* that is, I am the creating beginning; because all things received *being* through him, as was said above, in the first chapter[^p361-9]: « In the beginning was the Word », and again: « All things were made by him ». — He is also *doctor; who also speak unto you;* to the Hebrews, the first chapter[^p361-10]: « Last of all he hath spoken to us by the Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the world ». In like manner the Lord answered the Samaritan woman when she asked: « I am he, who am speaking with thee »; and the blind man, below, in the ninth chapter. — He is also *judge,* although now he does not speak words of condemnation, but of instruction[^p361-11] and of calling. Therefore he says:

36. (V. 26.). *I have many things to speak of you; of*[^p361-12], that is, against you, *and to judge,* which will not remain undiscussed, not out of malice, but out of justice. *But he that sent me is true, and the things I have heard of him, these I speak in the world;* and therefore I judge truly; above, in the fifth chapter[^p361-13]: « As I hear, I judge, and my judgment is just; because I seek not my own will, but his who sent me ». And because he had manifested himself in comparison with *the creature,* he manifests himself in comparison with *the person of the Father,* which the Jews could not understand; therefore he says:

37. (V. 27.). *And they understood not that he called God his Father;* and because they were not able to understand the height of the generation, he sends them to the infirmity of the passion, when he says:

38. (V. 28.). *Jesus therefore said to them: When you shall have lifted up the Son of man,* namely on the cross; below, in the twelfth chapter[^p361-14]: « And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all things to myself »; *then shall you know that I am,* namely a person distinct in the Trinity; behold, *the personal distinction;* Exodus, the third chapter: « I am who am ». *And I do nothing of myself,* namely, this you shall know;
<!-- page 362 -->
*but as the Father hath taught me, these things I speak,* that is, as I have received from the Father, because the Father gives all things to the Son. And in this is shown the emanation of the generation from the Father; above, in the fifth chapter[^p362-1]: « The Son cannot do any thing of himself, but what he seeth the Father doing ». You shall know not only the personal distinction, but also *the indivision of the essence;* therefore he says:

39. (V. 29.). *And he that sent me is with me,* because of the unity of the essence, in which there is no division; below, in the fourteenth chapter[^p362-2]: « Do you not believe that I am in the Father, and the Father is in me »? *And he hath not left me alone,* through a discordant will; *because I do always the things that please him.* Whence the Son cannot be left by the Father, because he can neither be discordant with him nor displease him; Matthew, the third chapter[^p362-3]: « This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased ». On the contrary, sinners are left by the Lord, because they displease him through sins; the second of Paralipomenon, the twelfth chapter: « You have left me, and I have left you ».

40. (V. 30.). *When he spoke these things.* Here the fourth is touched, namely *the multiplication of believers* in Christ, which was brought about at this instruction; therefore he says: *When he spoke these things,* namely those that were said before, *many believed in him;* because his word had efficacy; above, in the fourth chapter[^p362-4]: « Many more believed because of his word ».

### Questions.

41. Quaest. I. But here it is asked, first, concerning this which he says: *You shall die in your sins.* — It seems that he revealed to them their damnation; therefore he compelled them to despair; and according to this it seems that someone can be foreknowing of his own damnation[^p362-5]. — But this does not seem so; because knowledge and revelation are a gift of piety: therefore it does not compel to despair.

To this it is answered in three ways: that the Lord did not say this by revealing, but by threatening; whence below[^p362-6] he adds a condition: *unless you believe.* — Otherwise it must be said that the Lord did not say this to men who gave him faith; therefore he did not compel them to despair; whence he did not say this to believers, but to the unbelieving. — In the third way it is said that the Lord was speaking to the multitude; therefore the word was taken generally, and no one took it specially for himself[^p362-7].

42. Quaest. II. Again, since *beginning* states the account of emanation, which first and principally belongs to the Father; whence is it that the Son appropriates it to himself?

**I respond:** It must be said that this name *beginning* is sometimes taken *essentially,* according as it states a relation to the creature, and so the whole Trinity is called *beginning;* sometimes *notionally,* and so the Father and the Son are *one beginning* of the Holy Spirit; sometimes *personally,* and so *beginning* states the fontal emanation itself, which is found in the Father. And so Augustine says[^p362-8] that « the Father is the beginning of the Divinity ». — It must be said, then, that this name *beginning*, according as it is *essential*, is drawn to the person of the Son in two ways: in one way through *the property of generation,* and so the Son is called *beginning from beginning,* that is, begotten; in another way through *the property of the Word,* because the Son is the Word, and so it is drawn here: *The beginning, who also speak unto you*[^p362-9].

43. Quaest. III. Again it is asked: since *truth* is appropriated to the Son, whence is it that the Son, speaking of the Father, attributes it to him[^p362-10]?

It must be said that he is called *true* because he speaks true words. Because, then, the Father speaks the Son, who is the true Word, to whom truth is appropriated, *true*, by reason of the origin connoted, can rightly be appropriated to the Father[^p362-11].

44. Quaest. IV. Again it is asked concerning this which he says, that *he hath not left me alone, because I do always the things that please him.* — *On the contrary:* in Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p362-12], the Son said in the passion: *My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?*

**I respond:** It must be said that there is a *forsaking of dispensation, of probation, of permission, of eternal*
<!-- page 363 -->
*damnation.* The first, *of dispensation,* was to expose the Son to the passion for the salvation of the human race[^p363-1]. The second, *of probation,* is in the just, as the Lord left Job in the hand of satan. The third is in those who sin, in the present. The fourth is in the damned eternally, which is a forsaking without any visitation. The word of the Lord, then, is here understood of the forsaking of wrath, not of the dispensation of the divine wisdom[^p363-2].
