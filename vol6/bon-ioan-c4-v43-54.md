# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. IV, Vers. 43-54
## *Quarta manifestatio, quae facta est Galilaeis*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 300 -->

#### Quarta manifestatio, quae facta est Galilaeis.

69. *Post duos autem dies* etc. Manifestavit se supra Dominus discipulis, Iudaeis et Samaritanis; hic incipit quarta pars huius partis, in qua manifestat se ipsum *Galilaeis,* suis compatriotis, tanquam durioribus ad credendum. Et proceditur in hac manifestatione, quae facta est per miraculum, hoc ordine. Primo insinuatur in Galilaeis *defectus reverentiae* ad honorandum; secundo, *defectus fidei* ad credendum; tertio vero, *impetratio miraculi;* quarto, ex hoc *aedificatio fidei.*

Primo igitur insinuatur *defectus reverentiae* ad Christum honorandum. Quia enim erant compatriotae Christi, ideo ad eorum irreverentiam amovendam venit ad eos Christus, ut in miraculis se ostendat eis. Propterea dicit:

(Vers. 43.). *Post duos autem dies,* quibus scilicet fuit in Samaria, *exiit inde et abiit in Galilaeam,* patriam scilicet suam, ut eos duriores converteret. Ideo subdit:

70. (Vers. 44.). *Ipse enim Iesus testimonium perhibuit,* id est, tanquam verum asseruit, *quia propheta in patria sua honorem non habet;* Lucae quarto[^p300-3]: « Nemo propheta acceptus est in patria sua ». Glossa dicit, quod quia in primo adventu in Galilaeam honorem non habuerat, ideo adhuc secundo revertitur, ut vel sic saltem in eum credant per signa. Iam enim quodam modo habilitati erant per signa, quae Ierosolymis viderant. Propterea dicit:

71. (Vers. 45.). *Cum ergo venisset in Galileam, exceperunt eum Galilaei,* quasi paulisper honorare coeperunt; ad Philippenses secundo[^p300-4]: « Excipite Epaphroditum cum omni gaudio in Domino et eiusmodi cum honore habetote ». Et ratio subditur: *Cum vidissent omnia, quae fecerat Ierosolymis in die festo; vidissent,* inquam; *et ipsi enim ascenderant*[^p300-5] *ad diem festum.* Ista enim omnia, quae viderunt, fuerunt signa et miracula; supra secundo: « Cum esset Ierosolymis in die festo, multi crediderunt in eum, videntes signa, quae faciebat ».

72. (Vers. 46.). *Venit ergo iterum in Cana Galilaeae;* scilicet ut vel sic credant; *ubi fecit aquam vinum,* quod fuit signorum suorum initium, per quod innotuit discipulis; unde Glossa[^p300-6]: « Hoc miraculum pauci crediderant, id est discipuli »; supra secundo: « Hoc initium signorum fecit Iesus in Cana Galilaeae, et crediderunt in eum discipuli eius ».

73. *Et erat quidam regulus.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *defectus fidei* ad credendum. Sicut quodam modo *honorabant,* quodam modo *non;* ita etiam quodam modo *credebant,* quodam modo *non.* Et hoc patet in hoc, quod regulus non credebat, eum posse sanare filium nisi praesens. Propter quod dicit: *Erat quidam regulus,* a regni scilicet parvitate[^p300-7]; *cuius filius infirmabatur Capharnaum,* et ita in Domini absentia.

74. (Vers. 47.). *Hic cum audisset, quia Iesus venit*[^p300-8] *a Iudaea in Galilaeam,* quia fama adventus eius percrebuerat; Marci primo: « Processit rumor eius statim in omnem regionem Galilaeae »; *abiit ad eum,* per devotionem; Matthaei octavo[^p300-9]: « Accessit ad eum centurio, rogans eum » etc. *Et rogabat eum, ut descenderet,* per praesentiam corporalem; supra secundo[^p300-10]: « Descendit Capharnaum ipse et Mater eius » etc. *Et sanaret filium eius,* per potentiam mirabilem; Lucae sexto: « Virtus de illo exibat et sanabat omnes ». *Incipiebat enim mori,* per impotentiam naturalem; quarti Regum vigesimo dictum est Ezechiae: « Morieris tu et non vives », secundum causas scilicet inferiores. Et quia iste regulus, etsi petebat, non tamen firmiter credebat; ideo Dominus eum arguit incredulitatis. Propter quod dicit:

75. (Vers. 48.). *Dixit ergo Iesus ad eum: Nisi signa et prodigia videritis, non creditis;* et ita ad
<!-- page 301 -->
credendum duri estis. *Signa* sunt minora, sed *prodigia* sunt maiora miracula. De hac Iudaeorum duritia infra duodecimo[^p301-1]: « Cum tanta signa fecisset coram eis, non credebant in eum »; econtra Samaritani solo verbo crediderunt, ut probatum est[^p301-2].

76. (Vers. 49.). *Dixit*[^p301-3] *ad eum regulus.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *impetratio miraculi,* quae, etsi non fieret propter fidem perfectam, est tamen facta propter parentis instantiam. Unde et regulus iterum petit: *Domine, descende, priusquam moriatur filius meus.* Quia instanter petiit, obtinuit; Matthaei septimo[^p301-4]: « Omnis, qui petit, accipit, et qui quaerit invenit, et pulsanti aperietur »; et Lucae undecimo: « Et si non dabit, quia amicus eius sit, propter improbitatem tamen illius surget et dabit quotquot habet necessarios », panes scilicet. Ideo subditur:

77. (Vers. 50.). *Dicit ei Iesus: Vade, filius tuus vivit,* id est, sanus factus est. Ideo dicit *vivit,* quia ab infirmitate mortali convaluerat. Et hoc quidem homo meruit, quia, quamvis nondum crederet in Christum, credidit tamen verbo Christi. Ideo dicit: *Credidit homo sermoni, quem dixit ei Iesus, et ibat;* Glossa[^p301-5]: « Sic meruit salutem filio ». Unde Dominus Marci nono viro roganti pro salute filii dixit: « Si potes credere, omnia possibilia sunt credenti »; id est, impetrari ab eo possunt.

78. (Vers. 51.). *Iam autem eo descendente.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *aedificatio fidei,* quae facta est ex cognitione virtutis et miraculi, quod cognovit ex relatione servorum. Propter quod dicit: *Iam autem eo descendente, occurrerunt ei servi eius;* bonum scilicet nuntium habentes, non sustinuerunt, quousque domum perveniret. *Occurrerunt* prae gaudio festinanter, sicut servi domino; Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p301-6]: « Ecce, sponsus venit, exite obviam ei ». *Et nuntiaverunt ei, quod filius eius viveret,* id est, curatus esset.

79. (Vers. 52.). *Interrogabat ergo horam, qua melius habuerat*[^p301-7], ut per horam recognoscat in filio Christi potentiam. *Et dixerunt ei: Quia heri hora septima reliquit eum febris;* et ex hoc cognovit Christi virtutem. Unde sequitur:

80. (Vers. 53.). *Cognovit ergo pater, quia illa hora erat, in qua dixit ei Iesus: Filius tuus vivit,* et ita, quod ipsius verbo virtuoso sanatus fuit; iuxta illud Sapientiae decimo sexto[^p301-8]: « Non herba neque malagma sanavit eos, sed sermo tuus, Domine ». Et ex hac cognitione sequitur *aedificatio fidei.* Ideo dicit: *Et credidit ipse et domus eius tota.* Iam fide perfecta ipse credidit huic secundo signo, qui tamen propter primum non crediderat. Ideo subiungit Evangelista:

81. (Vers. 54.). *Hoc iterum secundum signum fecit Iesus; secundum,* scilicet respectu illius quod fuit in nuptiis; *cum venisset a Iudaea in Galilaeam,* ut, quia per primum non crediderant, per secundum credant; Exodi quarto[^p301-9] dixit Dominus Moysi: « Si non crediderint neque audierint sermonem signi prioris, credent verbo signi sequentis ».

82. *Moraliter.* Hic considerandum est, quis *regulus,* quis *reguli filius,* qua *infirmitate* detinetur, qua *hora* sanatur. — *Rex* dicitur superior pars rationis, cui totum regnum animae est commissum ad iudicandum; unde in Psalmo[^p301-10]: *Et nunc, reges, intelligite.* Iste dicitur *regulus* a iustitiae parvitate, quando animam male et pueriliter regit; Ecclesiastae decimo[^p301-11]: *Vae terrae, cuius rex puer est, et cuius principes mane comedunt!* — Huius *regis filius* dicitur inferior pars rationis, quae eiusdem naturae est et ab ipsa docenda; Ecclesiastici trigesimo[^p301-12]: *Qui docet filium suum laudabitur.* — Hic regis filius *infirmatur febre,* quando ratio sequitur concupiscentiam, quae est tanquam febris calida et putrida; Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio[^p301-13]: *Anima calida quasi ignis ardens, non exstinguetur, donec aliquid glutiat.* Concupiscentia haec tanquam febris hominem inquietat, modo per calorem, modo per frigus; Isaiae quinquagesimo septimo: *Impii quasi mare fervens, quod quiescere non potest, et redundant fluctus eius in conculcationem et lutum.* — Iste *liberatur hora septima diei. Dies* hic potest dici Christus, quia ipse est sol lucens super terram; infra octavo[^p301-14]: *Ego sum lux mundi.* — *Horae* istius diei sunt diversi status Christi, ex quorum consideratione anima a diversis aegritudinibus liberatur.

83. Prima hora est *incarnatio,* et haec liberat a *superbia* illuminando ad humilitatem, quia humiliavit se in incarnatione; ad Philippenses secundo[^p301-15]: *Exinanivit semetipsum, formam servi accipiens.* — Secunda est *nativitas,* et haec liberat ab *avaritia* illuminando ad paupertatem, quia Christus natus est pauper, sicut dicitur Lucae secundo[^p301-16]: *Peperit Filium suum primogenitum et pannis eum involvit et reclinavit eum in praesepio, quia
<!-- page 302 -->
non erat eis locus in diversorio.* — Tertia, *circumcisio,* et haec liberat ab *inobedientia* illuminando ad obedientiam. Cum enim Christus non esset Legi subiectus, tamen se Legi subiecit; unde de eo recte dicitur illud Apostoli primae ad Corinthios nono[^p302-1]: *Cum essem liber ex omnibus, omnium me servum feci.* — Quarta est *apparitio,* et haec liberat ab *invidia* illuminando ad doctrinae communicantiam, quia ipse tunc notitiam sui gentibus revelavit; Sapientiae septimo[^p302-2]: *Laetatus sum in omnibus, quoniam antecedebat me ista sapientia, quam sine invidia communico et honestatem illius non abscondo.* — Quinta est in templo *oblatio,* et haec liberat ab *ingratitudine* illuminando ad orationem, quoniam tunc ipse Dominus in templo oblatus est in signum, quod per gratitudinem se totum debet homo offerre; Lucae secundo[^p302-3]: *Tulerunt puerum Iesum in Ierusalem, ut sisterent eum Domino.* — Sexta est ipsius *baptizatio,* et haec liberat ab *immunditia* illuminando ad vitae novitatem, quia Christus lavatus est in Iordanis flumine; Matthaei tertio[^p302-4]: *Venit Iesus a Galilaea in Iordanem, ut baptizaretur ab eo;* primae Petri tertio: *Salvos facit baptisma, non carnis depositio sordium* etc. — Septima est in ieiunio *tentatio,* et haec liberat a *febre* filium reguli illuminando ad carnis mortificationem, quia tunc Christus carnem suo ieiunio maceravit; Matthaei quarto[^p302-5]: *Ductus est Iesus in desertum a Spiritu, ut tentaretur a diabolo; et cum ieiunasset quadraginta diebus et quadraginta noctibus, postea esuriit.* — Octava est *passio,* et haec liberat ab *iracundia* illuminando ad patientiam, quia Christus in patiendo fuit patientissimus; primae Petri secundo[^p302-6]: *Christus passus est pro nobis, vobis relinquens exemplum, ut sequamini vestigia eius qui peccatum non fecit, nec inventus est dolus in ore eius.* — Nona est *ad inferos descensio,* et haec liberat a *duritia* illuminando ad misericordiam, quia Dominus misertus est his qui erant *in tenebris et umbra mortis*[^p302-7]; Zachariae nono: *Tu quoque in sanguine testamenti tui eduxisti vinctos de lacu.* Maxima misericordia est sanguinem pro captivis dare. — Decima est in sepulcro *requietio,* et haec liberat a *mundi pressura* illuminando ad pacem, quia Christus in sepulcro quievit; Psalmus[^p302-8]: *In pace in idipsum dormiam et requiescam;* et iterum: *In pace factus est locus eius.* — Undecima, *resurrectio,* et haec liberat a *vitae vetustate* illuminando ad vitae novitatem, quia tunc Christus novus homo factus est; ad Romanos sexto[^p302-9]: *Quomodo Christus surrexit a mortuis per gloriam Patris, ita et nos in novitate vitae ambulemus.* — Duodecima, *ascensio,* et haec liberat ab *amore terrenorum* illuminando ad desiderium aeternorum, ad quae Christus nos praecessit; ad Colossenses tertio[^p302-10]: *Quae sursum sunt quaerite, ubi Christus est in dextera Dei sedens; quae sursum sunt sapite, non quae super terram.*

### Quaestiones.

84. Quaest. I. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *propheta in patria sua honorem non habet.*

**Contra hoc:** 1. Quia homo non honoratur, nisi ubi cognoscitur: ergo si cognoscitur inter compatriotas, non inter alienigenas; videtur, quod magis honoretur in patria sua quam in aliena.

2. Item videmus, quod David propheta in patria sua regni honorem habuit. — Item videtur, quod Christus habuerit honorem. Dicitur enim in littera[^p302-11], quod *exceperunt eum Galilaei:* ergo honoraverunt.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod propter duplicem rationem non honoratur propheta in patria sua: tum ratione *generationis carnalis,* tum ratione *conversationis familiaris.* Ratione *generationis;* quando enim habet patrem et matrem ignobiles et viles et pauperes, sicut Christus credebatur habere, ratione horum despicitur[^p302-12]. Ratione *conversationis familiaris,* quia familiaritas parit contemptum; ideo, cum parentes Christi pauperes nossent et eius educationem, nihil divinum in eo esse credebant.

1. Quod ergo obiicit, quod notitia facit hominem honorari; dicendum, quod notitia *virtutis* adiuvat, sed notitia *originis* et *infirmitatis* impedit.

2. Quod obiicit de David et aliis prophetis, respondendum, quod « a raro contingentibus non debet ratio pronuntiari[^p302-13] ». Et quia hoc raro contingit, ideo etc. — Quod obiicitur, quod exceperunt; re-
<!-- page 303 -->
spondet Augustinus[^p303-1], quod exceperunt *exterius* corporaliter, sed non interius per fidem; et hunc honorem Dominus non requirit. Non enim facit Dominus vim de festo brachiali, sed de mente fideli.

85. Quaest. II. Item quaerit Gregorius[^p303-2] de isto regulo, utrum crederet, an non? — Quod non, videtur:

1. Quia Dominus increpat eum: *Nisi signa et prodigia videritis, non creditis.*

2. Hoc ipsum arguitur per litteram[^p303-3]: quia petebat, quod descenderet, quasi nisi in praesentia sanare non posset.

**Sed contra** hoc est: si non credidit, quomodo salutem filii postulavit a Domino?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *credidit,* sed *semiplene: credidit,* in Christo esse virtutem ad salvandum filium; sed *non credidit,* in eo esse Divinitatem, per quam posset ubique. Unde fides fuit, cum petiit salutem; sed defectus fidei, cum requirebat praesentiam corporalem[^p303-4].

86. Quaest. III. Item quaerit dictus Gregorius[^p303-5]: unde est, quod Dominus ad servum centurionis ire se obtulit, ad filium reguli, cum rogaretur, ire recusavit?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod hoc fuit ad aedificationem *fidei* et *morum: fidei,* quia, dum se obtulit centurioni, ipsius fidei devotionem nobis manifestavit; dum retraxit se a filio reguli, curando eum in absentia, ad *Divinitatis fidem* invitavit. Ad *aedificationem* vero *morum,* ut nostram superbiam constringeret, qui magnos veneramur et parvos despicimus, Dominus se obtulit ire ad centurionis servum, et recusavit ire ad reguli filium[^p303-6].

#### The fourth manifestation, which was made to the Galileans.

69. *Now after two days* etc. The Lord manifested himself above to the disciples, the Jews and the Samaritans; here begins the fourth part of this part, in which he manifests himself *to the Galileans,* his fellow-countrymen, as to those harder to believe. And one proceeds in this manifestation, which was made through a miracle, in this order. First is intimated in the Galileans *the defect of reverence* in honouring; second, *the defect of faith* in believing; third, *the obtaining of the miracle;* fourth, from this, *the edification of faith.*

First, then, is intimated *the defect of reverence* in honouring Christ. For because they were fellow-countrymen of Christ, therefore, to remove their irreverence, Christ came to them, that he might show himself to them in miracles. Therefore he says:

(Verse 43.). *Now after two days,* namely those in which he was in Samaria, *he went out thence and went into Galilee,* his own country namely, that he might convert them, being the harder. Therefore he adds:

70. (Verse 44.). *For Jesus himself gave testimony,* that is, asserted as true, *that a prophet hath no honour in his own country;* Luke, the fourth chapter[^p300-3]: « No prophet is accepted in his own country ». The Gloss says that, because at his first coming into Galilee he had not had honour, therefore he returns yet a second time, that thus at least they might believe in him through signs. For they had already in a certain way been made apt through the signs which they had seen at Jerusalem. Therefore he says:

71. (Verse 45.). *And when he was come into Galilee* [so printed: *Galileam*], *the Galileans received him,* as it were began for a little while to honour him; to the Philippians, the second chapter[^p300-4]: « Receive Epaphroditus with all joy in the Lord, and treat with honour such as he ». And the reason is added: *Having seen all the things which he had done at Jerusalem on the festival day; having seen,* I say; *for they also had gone up*[^p300-5] *to the festival day.* For all these things which they saw were signs and miracles; above, in the second chapter: « When he was at Jerusalem on the festival day, many believed in him, seeing the signs which he did ».

72. (Verse 46.). *He came again therefore into Cana of Galilee;* namely, that thus at least they might believe; *where he made the water wine,* which was the beginning of his signs, through which he became known to the disciples; whence the Gloss[^p300-6]: « Few had believed this miracle, that is, the disciples »; above, in the second chapter: « This beginning of signs did Jesus in Cana of Galilee, and his disciples believed in him ».

73. *And there was a certain ruler.* Here the second is touched, namely *the defect of faith* in believing. As in a certain way they *honoured* him, in a certain way *not;* so also in a certain way they *believed,* in a certain way *not.* And this is plain in this, that the ruler did not believe he could heal his son unless present. On account of which he says: *There was a certain ruler* (*regulus*, a little king), so called, namely, from the smallness of his kingdom[^p300-7]; *whose son was sick at Capharnaum,* and so in the Lord's absence.

74. (Verse 47.). *He having heard that Jesus was come*[^p300-8] *from Judea into Galilee,* because the fame of his coming had spread abroad; Mark, the first chapter: « And the rumour of him went forth forthwith into all the country of Galilee »; *went to him,* through devotion; Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p300-9]: « There came to him a centurion, beseeching him » etc. *And prayed him to come down,* by bodily presence; above, in the second chapter[^p300-10]: « He went down to Capharnaum, he and his Mother » etc. *And heal his son,* by marvellous power; Luke, the sixth chapter: « Virtue went out from him and healed all ». *For he was beginning to die,* through natural impotence; in the fourth of Kings, the twentieth chapter, it was said to Ezechias: « Thou shalt die and not live », according, namely, to the lower causes. And because this ruler, though he asked, yet did not firmly believe, therefore the Lord reproves him of unbelief. On account of which he says:

75. (Verse 48.). *Jesus therefore said to him: Unless you see signs and wonders, you believe not;* and so you are hard
<!-- page 301 -->
to believe. *Signs* are lesser, but *wonders* are greater miracles. Of this hardness of the Jews, below, in the twelfth chapter[^p301-1]: « Whereas he had done so many signs before them, they believed not in him »; on the contrary, the Samaritans believed by the word alone, as has been shown[^p301-2].

76. (Verse 49.). *The ruler said*[^p301-3] *to him.* Here the third is touched, namely *the obtaining of the miracle,* which, though it was not done on account of perfect faith, was yet done on account of the parent's urgency. Whence the ruler also asks again: *Lord, come down before my son die.* Because he asked urgently, he obtained; Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p301-4]: « Every one that asketh, receiveth, and he that seeketh, findeth, and to him that knocketh it shall be opened »; and Luke, the eleventh chapter: « And if he will not give, because he is his friend, yet because of his importunity he will rise and give him as many as he needeth », loaves namely. Therefore it is added:

77. (Verse 50.). *Jesus saith to him: Go thy way, thy son liveth,* that is, he is made whole. Therefore he says *liveth,* because he had recovered from a mortal infirmity. And this indeed the man merited, because, although he did not yet believe in Christ, he yet believed the word of Christ. Therefore he says: *The man believed the word which Jesus said to him, and went his way;* the Gloss[^p301-5]: « Thus he merited health for his son ». Whence the Lord, in Mark, the ninth chapter, said to a man asking for the health of his son: « If thou canst believe, all things are possible to him that believeth »; that is, they can be obtained by him.

78. (Verse 51.). *And as he was now going down.* Here the fourth is touched, namely *the edification of faith,* which was made from the knowledge of the power and of the miracle, which he knew from the report of the servants. On account of which he says: *And as he was now going down, his servants met him;* having, namely, good news, they did not wait until he should reach home. *They met him* in haste for joy, as servants their lord; Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p301-6]: « Behold, the bridegroom cometh, go ye forth to meet him ». *And they brought him word that his son lived,* that is, was cured.

79. (Verse 52.). *He asked therefore the hour wherein he had grown better*[^p301-7], that through the hour he might recognise in his son the power of Christ. *And they said to him: Yesterday at the seventh hour the fever left him;* and from this he knew the power of Christ. Whence there follows:

80. (Verse 53.). *The father therefore knew that it was that hour in which Jesus said to him: Thy son liveth,* and so, that he was healed by his powerful word; according to that of Wisdom, the sixteenth chapter[^p301-8]: « Neither herb nor mollifying plaster healed them, but thy word, O Lord ». And from this knowledge there follows *the edification of faith.* Therefore he says: *And himself believed, and his whole house.* Now with perfect faith he himself believed this second sign, who yet on account of the first had not believed. Therefore the Evangelist subjoins:

81. (Verse 54.). *This again the second sign Jesus did; the second,* namely in respect of that which was at the marriage; *when he was come from Judea into Galilee,* so that, because they had not believed through the first, they might believe through the second; in Exodus, the fourth chapter[^p301-9], the Lord said to Moses: « If they will not believe nor hear the word of the former sign, they will believe the word of the following sign ».

82. *Morally.* Here it is to be considered who the *ruler* is, who *the ruler's son,* by what *infirmity* he is held, at what *hour* he is healed. — *The king* is called the higher part of reason, to which the whole kingdom of the soul is committed for judging; whence in the Psalm[^p301-10]: *And now, O ye kings, understand.* It is called *a ruler* (*regulus*) from the smallness of its justice, when it rules the soul badly and childishly; Ecclesiastes, the tenth chapter[^p301-11]: *Woe to the land whose king is a child, and whose princes eat in the morning!* — *The son* of this *king* is called the lower part of reason, which is of the same nature and is to be taught by it; Ecclesiasticus, the thirtieth chapter[^p301-12]: *He that teacheth his son shall be praised.* — This son of the king *is sick of a fever* when reason follows concupiscence, which is as a hot and putrid fever; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third chapter[^p301-13]: *A hot soul is as a burning fire, it will not be quenched till it swallow something.* This concupiscence disquiets a man like a fever, now by heat, now by cold; Isaias, the fifty-seventh chapter: *The wicked are as the raging sea, which cannot rest, and its waves overflow into trampling and mire.* — This son *is freed at the seventh hour of the day. The day* here may be called Christ, because he is the sun shining upon the earth; below, in the eighth chapter[^p301-14]: *I am the light of the world.* — *The hours* of this day are the diverse states of Christ, from the consideration of which the soul is freed from diverse sicknesses.

83. The first hour is *the incarnation,* and this frees from *pride* by illumining to humility, because he humbled himself in the incarnation; to the Philippians, the second chapter[^p301-15]: *He emptied himself, taking the form of a servant.* — The second is *the nativity,* and this frees from *avarice* by illumining to poverty, because Christ was born poor, as is said in Luke, the second chapter[^p301-16]: *She brought forth her firstborn Son, and wrapped him in swaddling clothes, and laid him in a manger, because
<!-- page 302 -->
there was no room for them in the inn.* — The third, *the circumcision,* and this frees from *disobedience* by illumining to obedience. For though Christ was not subject to the Law, yet he subjected himself to the Law; whence that saying of the Apostle in the first to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter[^p302-1], is rightly said of him: *Whereas I was free as to all, I made myself the servant of all.* — The fourth is *the apparition,* and this frees from *envy* by illumining to the sharing of teaching[^tr-doctrina], because he then revealed the knowledge of himself to the gentiles; Wisdom, the seventh chapter[^p302-2]: *I rejoiced in all, because this wisdom went before me, which I communicate without envy, and her honour I hide not.* — The fifth is *the offering* in the temple, and this frees from *ingratitude* by illumining to prayer, since the Lord himself was then offered in the temple as a sign that through gratitude man ought to offer his whole self; Luke, the second chapter[^p302-3]: *They carried the child Jesus to Jerusalem, to present him to the Lord.* — The sixth is his *baptizing,* and this frees from *uncleanness* by illumining to newness of life, because Christ was washed in the river Jordan; Matthew, the third chapter[^p302-4]: *Jesus came from Galilee to the Jordan, to be baptized by him;* the first of Peter, the third chapter: *Baptism maketh safe, not the putting away of the filth of the flesh* etc. — The seventh is *the temptation* in the fast, and this frees the ruler's son from *the fever* by illumining to the mortification of the flesh, because Christ then macerated his flesh by his fast; Matthew, the fourth chapter[^p302-5]: *Jesus was led by the Spirit into the desert, to be tempted by the devil; and when he had fasted forty days and forty nights, afterwards he was hungry.* — The eighth is *the passion,* and this frees from *wrath* by illumining to patience, because Christ in suffering was most patient; the first of Peter, the second chapter[^p302-6]: *Christ suffered for us, leaving you an example, that you should follow the steps of him who did no sin, nor was guile found in his mouth.* — The ninth is *the descent to the lower regions,* and this frees from *hardness* by illumining to mercy, because the Lord had mercy on those who were *in darkness and the shadow of death*[^p302-7]; Zacharias, the ninth chapter: *Thou also, in the blood of thy testament, hast brought forth the prisoners out of the pit.* It is the greatest mercy to give one's blood for captives. — The tenth is *the resting* in the sepulchre, and this frees from *the pressure of the world* by illumining to peace, because Christ rested in the sepulchre; the Psalm[^p302-8]: *In peace in the selfsame I will sleep, and I will rest;* and again: *In peace is his place made.* — The eleventh, *the resurrection,* and this frees from *the oldness of life* by illumining to newness of life, because then Christ was made a new man; to the Romans, the sixth chapter[^p302-9]: *As Christ is risen from the dead by the glory of the Father, so we also may walk in newness of life.* — The twelfth, *the ascension,* and this frees from *the love of earthly things* by illumining to the desire of eternal things, to which Christ has gone before us; to the Colossians, the third chapter[^p302-10]: *Seek the things that are above, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God; mind the things that are above, not the things that are upon the earth.*

### Questions.

84. Quaest. I. It is asked about this which he says, that *a prophet hath no honour in his own country.*

**Against this:** 1. Because a man is not honoured except where he is known: therefore if he is known among his fellow-countrymen, not among foreigners; it seems that he is honoured more in his own country than in a foreign one.

2. Likewise we see that David the prophet had the honour of a kingdom in his own country. — Likewise it seems that Christ had honour. For it is said in the letter[^p302-11] that *the Galileans received him:* therefore they honoured him.

**Respondeo:** It must be said that for a twofold reason a prophet is not honoured in his own country: both by reason of *carnal generation* and by reason of *familiar conversation.* By reason of *generation;* for when he has a father and mother who are ignoble and vile and poor, as Christ was believed to have, by reason of these he is despised[^p302-12]. By reason of *familiar conversation,* because familiarity breeds contempt; therefore, since they knew Christ's parents to be poor, and his upbringing, they believed that there was nothing divine in him.

1. To what is objected, then, that knowledge makes a man honoured; it must be said that knowledge of *virtue* helps, but knowledge of *origin* and *infirmity* hinders.

2. To what is objected concerning David and the other prophets, it must be answered that « from things that happen rarely an account ought not to be pronounced[^p302-13] ». And because this happens rarely, therefore etc. — To what is objected, that they received him;
<!-- page 303 -->
Augustine[^p303-1] answers that they received him *outwardly* in body, but not inwardly through faith; and this honour the Lord does not require. For the Lord sets no store by a feast of the arms, but by a faithful mind.

85. Quaest. II. Likewise Gregory[^p303-2] asks about this ruler, whether he believed or not? — That he did not, it seems:

1. Because the Lord rebukes him: *Unless you see signs and wonders, you believe not.*

2. This same is argued from the letter[^p303-3]: because he asked that he should come down, as though he could not heal except in his presence.

**Sed contra** this is: if he did not believe, how did he ask the Lord for the health of his son?

**Respondeo:** It must be said that *he believed,* but *half-fully: he believed* that there was in Christ power to save his son; but *he did not believe* that there was in him the Divinity, by which he could act everywhere. Hence there was faith when he asked for health; but a defect of faith when he required bodily presence[^p303-4].

86. Quaest. III. Likewise the said Gregory[^p303-5] asks: whence is it that the Lord offered himself to go to the centurion's servant, and, when he was asked, refused to go to the ruler's son?

**Respondeo:** It must be said that this was for the edification of *faith* and of *morals:* of *faith,* because, while he offered himself to the centurion, he manifested to us the devotion of his faith; while he withdrew himself from the ruler's son, curing him in his absence, he invited him to *faith in the Divinity.* But for the *edification* of *morals,* that he might restrain our pride, who venerate the great and despise the little, the Lord offered himself to go to the centurion's servant, and refused to go to the ruler's son[^p303-6].
