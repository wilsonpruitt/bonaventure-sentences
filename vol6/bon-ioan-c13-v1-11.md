# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XIII, Vers. 1-11
## *Primo confirmantur discipuli humilitatis exemplo quadrupliciter*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 424 -->

### Capitulum XIII.

#### Tertio, de discipulorum confirmatione ponuntur tria.

1. *Ante diem festum Paschae.* Determinata sunt duo praeambula ad passionem, scilicet Iudaeorum conspiratio et passionis praedictio; ita hac parte sequitur determinandum tertium, scilicet *discipulorum confirmatio*[^p424-9]. Discipulos autem Dominus confirmat et munit contra instantem tribulationem primo *humilitatis exemplo;* secundo, *eruditionis et consolationis verbo,* in principio decimi quarti capituli, ibi[^p424-10]: *Non turbetur cor vestrum;* tertio vero, *orationis subsidio,* in principio decimi septimi: *Haec locutus est Iesus et sublevatis* etc.

#### Primo confirmantur discipuli humilitatis exemplo quadrupliciter.

Praesens igitur capitulum, in quo Dominus confortat discipulos suos *exemplo,* habet quatuor particulas secundum quatuor, quae ibi tangit. Primo enim *proponit exemplum;* secundo *hortatur ad imitandum,* ibi[^p424-11]: *Postquam ergo lavit* etc.; in tertia *ostendit* sive detegit *Iudae perversitatem* ad deviandum, ibi: *Haec cum dixisset, turbatus est spiritu;* in quarta vero *ostendit discipulorum infirmitatem* ad se per passionem sequendum, ibi: *Filioli, adhuc modicum vobiscum sum.*
<!-- page 425 -->

*Exemplum* igitur, quod *proponit* imitandum, hoc ordine describitur. Primo ostenditur *temporis congruitas;* secundo vero, *Christi humilitas;* tertio, *Petri reverentia;* quarto, *ipsius obedientia.*

2. (Vers. 1.). *Temporis opportunitas* ostenditur in hoc, quod iam tempus mortis instabat, propter quod specialia signa dilectionis ostendere debebat. Propterea dicit: *Ante diem festum paschae,* id est ante primam diem azymorum, cuius vespera erat decima quarta luna ad vesperam, quando agnus immolabatur[^p425-1]. *Sciens Iesus, quia venit hora eius, ut transeat ex hoc mundo ad Patrem.* Pascha *transitus* est; et ideo Dominus mori voluit in paschate, quia suum *mori* transire fuit. Iste transitus significatus est per transitum maris rubri, de quo primae ad Corinthios decimo[^p425-2]: « Patres nostri omnes mare transierunt » etc. *Cum dilexisset suos, qui erant in mundo, in finem dilexit eos,* id est, tunc praecipua signa dilectionis ostendit. Augustinus[^p425-3]: « Absit, ut dilectionem morte finierit qui non est morte finitus »; et « Non est iste finis consumens, sed consummans ». *Suos* Dominus diligit; Isaiae quadragesimo nono[^p425-4]: « Nunquid oblivisci potest mulier infantem suum, ut non misereatur filio uteri sui »? *Tempus* tangitur et *hora* temporis:

3. (Vers. 2.). *Et coena facta, cum diabolus iam misisset in cor*[^p425-5], id est, suggessisset, *ut traderet eum Iudas Simonis Iscariotae;* quia tunc definierat eum tradere, ex qua traditione Christus non est minoratus, sed sublimatus; unde et subdit:

4. (Vers. 3.). *Sciens, quia omnia dedit ei Pater in manus,* secundum illud Matthaei ultimo[^p425-6]: « Data est mihi omnis potestas in caelo et in terra »; *et* sciens, *quia a Deo exivit et ad Deum vadit,* secundum illud infra decimo sexto: « Exivi a Patre et veni in mundum; iterum relinquo mundum et vado ad Patrem ». Hoc *sciens,* id est mortem suam instare praecognoscens,

5. (Vers. 4.). *Surgit a coena,* ut exemplum praebeat; quia iam temporis opportunitas aderat, sicut dictum est ministro Ecclesiae Actuum duodecimo[^p425-7]: « Surge velociter ».

*Et ponit vestimenta sua.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *Christi humilitas,* quia deponit vestimenta, ut sit *expeditus* sicut minister; ideo dicit: *Ponit vestimenta sua;* sit etiam *paratus* ut minister; propterea dicit: *Et cum accepisset linteum, praecinxit se;* Lucae duodecimo[^p425-8]: « Amen dico vobis, quod praecinget se » etc. Officium ministri exsequitur; propterea dicit:

6. (Vers. 5.). *Deinde misit*[^p425-9] *aquam in pelvim et coepit lavare pedes discipulorum et extergere linteo, quo erat praecinctus.* Hoc est *officium ministri;* tale officium Dominus maiestatis assumsit; Lucae vigesimo secundo: « Ego autem in medio vestrum sum, sicut qui ministrat ». « Filius autem hominis non venit ministrari, sed ministrare et dare animam suam redemptionem pro multis ». Et hoc *humillimum ministerium,* lotio pedum.

7. (Vers. 6.). *Venit ergo ad Simonem Petrum* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *Petri reverentia,* qua Domini obsequium refutabat; propter quod dicit: *Venit ad Simonem Petrum,* ut scilicet lavaret. *Et dicit ei Petrus*[^p425-10]*: Domine, tu mihi la-*
<!-- page 426 -->
*vas pedes?* Quasi expavescendo dicit Petrus: *Tu mihi?* Tu Dominus mihi servo? Tu Magister mihi discipulo? Tu Omnipotens mihi misero? Chrysostomus[^p426-1]: « Manibus, quibus oculos aperuisti, leprosos mundasti, mortuos suscitasti, mihi lavas pedes »? Quasi dicat: non audeo pati; et quia in reverentia excedebat, monet eum Dominus, ut desistat propter mysterium; unde sequitur:

8. (Vers. 7.). *Dixit ei Iesus*[^p426-2]*: Quod ego facio tu nescis modo, scies autem postea;* ideo oportet, te sustinere usque in posterum. Sed Petrus admonitione non flectitur; unde:

9. (Vers. 8.). *Dicit ei Petrus: Non lavabis mihi pedes in aeternum.* Et quia in sua sententia permanere decreverat, deterretur comminatione; ideo: *Respondit ei Iesus: Si non lavero te, non habebis partem mecum; partem* dicit consortium Dei aeternum, de quo in Psalmo[^p426-3]: « Dominus pars hereditatis meae et calicis mei »; et iterum: « Portio mea in terra viventium ».

10. (Vers. 9.). *Dicit ei Simon Petrus.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *Petri obedientia,* comminatione sibi facta. Unde supra dictus est *Petrus:* « Dicit ei *Petrus* »; sed in hac responsione dicitur: « Dicit ei *Simon Petrus* », id est obediens[^p426-4], fervens, quia plus offert, quam Dominus praecipiat; unde dicit: *Domine, non tantum pedes meos,* sicut praecipis, lava; *sed et manus et caput.* Unde Simon Petrus vere obediens dicere poterat illud Psalmi[^p426-5]: « Paratum cor meum, Deus, paratum cor meum ». Sed quia superius in reverentia errabat, modo vero in obedientia excedebat; ideo *Petrus* supra est territus, sed modo est *Simon* instructus; propter hoc subdit:

11. (Vers. 10.). *Dicit ei Iesus: Qui lotus est,* illa scilicet lotione, de qua dicitur Isaiae primo[^p426-6]: « Lavamini, mundi estote »; et Zachariae decimo tertio: « Erit fons patens domui David et habitantibus Ierusalem in ablutionem peccatoris »; Ieremiae quarto: « Lava a malitia cor tuum, Ierusalem, ut salva fias »; ille, inquam, *non indiget, nisi ut pedes lavet,* id est affectus; de quibus Canticorum quinto[^p426-7]: « Lavi pedes meos; quomodo inquinabo illos »? Isti pedes magis indigent lavari quam alia membra, propter hoc quod magis uniuntur sordibus; Threnorum primo: « Sordes eius in pedibus eius, et non est recordata finis sui ». *Sed,* quando isti pedes lavantur, tunc *est mundus totus;* Ezechielis trigesimo sexto[^p426-8]: « Effundam super vos aquam mundam, et mundabimini ab omnibus inquinamentis vestris ». *Et vos mundi estis;* ideo non indigetis nisi lotione pedum; infra decimo quinto: « Vos mundi estis propter sermonem, quem locutus sum vobis ». Sed ne videatur discipuli facinus ignorare, subdit: *Sed non omnes.* Et rationem, quare hoc dixerat, dicit Evangelista:

12. (Vers. 11.). *Sciebat enim, quisnam esset, qui traderet eum; propterea dixit: Non estis mundi omnes.* Ideo Evangelista, ubi loquitur de proditore, ostendit, quod Dominus sciebat, ne aliquis crederet, Christum tanquam hominem posse decipi; supra sexto[^p426-9]: « Sciebat Iesus ab initio, qui essent non credentes, et quis traditurus esset eum ».

### Quaestiones.

13. Quaest. I. Quaeritur hic de hoc quod dicit, quod coena ista fuit facta *ante diem festum paschae.* — Et ex hoc videtur, quod Dominus confecerit de fermentato. — Sed in contrarium sunt alii Evangelistae[^p426-10], qui dicunt, Dominum comedisse pascha secundum morem communem.

Ad hoc respondent Graeci, sicut dicitur in Glossa[^p426-11], quod Dominus praevenit, sicut hic dicitur, diem paschae et confecit de fermentato, et Ioannes corrigit alios Evangelistas.

Sed insipienter dicunt; quia absit, quod alii Evangelistae in aliquo sint mentiti, cum eodem Spiritu loquerentur[^p426-12]. Unde non dicitur hic dies paschae *dies immolationis,* sed *primus inter dies azymorum,* in cuius sero praecedenti immolabatur agnus.

14. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *cum diabolus iam misisset in cor, ut traderet eum Iudas.*
<!-- page 427 -->

1. Ergo videtur, quod diabolus sit malarum cogitationum immissor.

2. Et quod sic, videtur; quia ipse est *deus huius saeculi*[^p427-1] et *dominatur in filios diffidentiae,* ad Ephesios secundo: ergo sicut Deus electis, in quibus dominatur, immittit bonas cogitationes; sic et diabolus malas.

**Sed contra** hoc est, quia, si diabolus immittit, ergo velit nolit homo, peccabit: ergo secundum hoc esset superior ipsa mente, quod est contra rationem et Augustinum[^p427-2].

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *mittere in cor* sive immittere dupliciter dicitur: *proprie* et *communiter; proprie immittere* est virtualiter formare intus, et sic est solius Dei; *communiter* vero *immittere* est offerendo suggerere per occasionem. Primo modo non convenit diabolo, secundo modo sic[^p427-3].

2. Quod vero obiicitur, quod diabolus regnat; dicendum, quod non regnat propria virtute, sed per consensum pravae voluntatis, quae volens ei consentit, secundum illud Gregorii[^p427-4]: « Debilis est hostis contra resistentes, contra consentientes fortis ». Ideo non dicitur *immittere,* sed *suggerere* vel *incendere.*

15. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc, quod solus Petrus Domino volenti lavare pedes restitit; quomodo omnes nihil dixerunt? Et si omnes tacuerunt, quomodo ipse volebat esse singularis? — *Si tu dicas,* quod Dominus primo venit ad eum, et ipse primo restitit, et post alii sunt correcti; contraria est littera, quia dicitur: *Coepit lavare et tergere;* et post: *Venit ergo ad Simonem Petrum*[^p427-5].

Respondet Chrysostomus[^p427-6], quod credit, quod prius lavit pedes Iudae, quia ille tanquam praesumtuosus primum locum anticipaverat, et post venit ad Petrum, et ille restitit. — Sed melius dicendum secundum Glossam[^p427-7], quod primo venit ad Petrum, et illud dictum est per anticipationem.

## English
<!-- page 424 -->

### Chapter XIII.

#### Third, three things are set down on the confirmation of the disciples.

1. *Before the festival day of the Pasch.* Two preambles to the passion have been determined, namely the conspiracy of the Jews and the prediction of the passion; so in this part there follows to be determined the third, namely *the confirmation of the disciples*[^p424-9]. Now the Lord confirms and arms the disciples against the impending tribulation, first by *the example of humility;* second, by *the word of erudition and consolation,* at the beginning of the fourteenth chapter, there[^p424-10]: *Let not your heart be troubled;* third, by *the aid of prayer,* at the beginning of the seventeenth: *These things Jesus spoke, and lifting up* etc.

#### First the disciples are confirmed by the example of humility in four ways.

The present chapter, then, in which the Lord comforts his disciples by *example,* has four portions according to the four things which he touches on there. For first he *proposes the example;* second he *exhorts to imitating it,* there[^p424-11]: *Then after he had washed* etc.; in the third he *shows* or discloses *the perversity of Judas* in going astray, there: *When he had said these things, he was troubled in spirit;* but in the fourth he *shows the infirmity of the disciples* in following him through the passion, there: *Little children, yet a little while I am with you.*
<!-- page 425 -->

*The example,* then, which he *proposes* to be imitated, is described in this order. First is shown *the congruity of the time;* second, *the humility of Christ;* third, *the reverence of Peter;* fourth, *his obedience.*

2. (Vers. 1.). *The opportuneness of the time* is shown in this, that now the time of death was at hand, on account of which he had to show special signs of dilection. Therefore he says: *Before the festival day of the pasch,* that is, before the first day of unleavened bread, whose evening was the fourteenth moon at evening, when the lamb was immolated[^p425-1]. *Jesus knowing that his hour was come, that he should pass out of this world to the Father.* The Pasch is a *passing;* and therefore the Lord willed to die in the pasch, because his *dying* was to pass. This passing was signified through the passing of the Red Sea, of which in the first to the Corinthians, the tenth chapter[^p425-2]: « Our fathers all passed through the sea » etc. *Having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them unto the end,* that is, he then showed the chief signs of dilection. Augustine[^p425-3]: « Far be it that he should have ended his dilection by death who is not ended by death »; and « This is not an end consuming, but consummating ». *His own* the Lord loves; Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter[^p425-4]: « Can a woman forget her infant, so as not to have pity on the son of her womb »? *The time* is touched on and the *hour* of the time:

3. (Vers. 2.). *And when supper was done, the devil having now put into the heart*[^p425-5], that is, having suggested, *that Judas Iscariot, the son of Simon, should betray him;* because then he had determined to betray him, by which betrayal Christ was not lessened, but exalted; hence he also adds:

4. (Vers. 3.). *Knowing that the Father had given him all things into his hands,* according to that of Matthew, the last chapter[^p425-6]: « All power is given to me in heaven and in earth »; *and* knowing *that he came from God, and goeth to God,* according to that below, the sixteenth chapter: « I came forth from the Father, and am come into the world: again I leave the world, and I go to the Father ». *Knowing* this, that is, foreknowing that his death was at hand,

5. (Vers. 4.). *He riseth from supper,* to give an example; because now the opportuneness of the time was at hand, as was said to a minister of the Church in Acts, the twelfth chapter[^p425-7]: « Arise quickly ».

*And layeth aside his garments.* Here the second is touched on, namely *the humility of Christ,* because he lays aside his garments, that he may be *unencumbered* as a minister; therefore he says: *Layeth aside his garments;* that he may also be *ready* as a minister; therefore he says: *And having taken a towel, he girded himself;* Luke, the twelfth chapter[^p425-8]: « Amen I say to you, that he will gird himself » etc. He carries out the office of a minister; therefore he says:

6. (Vers. 5.). *After that, he put*[^p425-9] *water into a basin, and began to wash the feet of the disciples, and to wipe them with the towel wherewith he was girded.* This is *the office of a minister;* such an office the Lord of majesty took on; Luke, the twenty-second chapter: « But I am in the midst of you, as he that ministereth ». « But the Son of man is not come to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a redemption for many ». And this *most humble ministry,* the washing of the feet.

7. (Vers. 6.). *He cometh therefore to Simon Peter* etc. Here the third is touched on, namely *the reverence of Peter,* by which he refused the Lord's service; on account of which he says: *He cometh to Simon Peter,* namely that he might wash him. *And Peter saith to him*[^p425-10]*: Lord, dost thou*
<!-- page 426 -->
*wash my feet?* Peter says, as if seized with dread: *Thou to me?* Thou, the Lord, to me, a servant? Thou, the Teacher, to me, a disciple? Thou, the Almighty, to me, a wretch? Chrysostom[^p426-1]: « With the hands with which thou hast opened eyes, cleansed lepers, raised the dead, dost thou wash my feet »? As if to say: I dare not suffer it; and because he went to excess in reverence, the Lord admonishes him to desist on account of the mystery; hence there follows:

8. (Vers. 7.). *Jesus said to him*[^p426-2]*: What I do thou knowest not now; but thou shalt know hereafter;* therefore you must bear it until afterwards. But Peter is not bent by the admonition; hence:

9. (Vers. 8.). *Peter saith to him: Thou shalt not wash my feet for ever.* And because he had decreed to persist in his opinion, he is deterred by a threat; therefore: *Jesus answered him: If I wash thee not, thou shalt have no part with me;* by *part* he means the eternal fellowship of God, of which in the Psalm[^p426-3]: « The Lord is the part of my inheritance and of my cup »; and again: « My portion in the land of the living ».

10. (Vers. 9.). *Simon Peter saith to him.* Here the fourth is touched on, namely *the obedience of Peter,* upon the threat made to him. Hence above he was called *Peter:* « *Peter* saith to him »; but in this reply it is said: « *Simon Peter* saith to him », that is, obedient[^p426-4], fervent, because he offers more than the Lord commands; hence he says: *Lord, not only my feet,* as thou commandest, wash; *but also my hands and my head.* Hence Simon Peter, truly obedient, could say that of the Psalm[^p426-5]: « My heart is ready, O God, my heart is ready ». But because earlier he erred in reverence, and now he went to excess in obedience; therefore *Peter* above was frightened, but now *Simon* is instructed; on account of this he adds:

11. (Vers. 10.). *Jesus saith to him: He that is washed,* namely with that washing of which it is said in Isaias, the first chapter[^p426-6]: « Wash yourselves, be clean »; and in Zacharias, the thirteenth chapter: « There shall be a fountain open to the house of David, and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem, for the washing of the sinner »; Jeremias, the fourth chapter: « Wash thy heart from wickedness, O Jerusalem, that thou mayst be saved »; he, I say, *needeth not but to wash his feet,* that is, the affections; of which in the Canticle of Canticles, the fifth chapter[^p426-7]: « I have washed my feet; how shall I defile them »? These feet need to be washed more than the other members, on account of this, that they are more united to filthiness; Lamentations, the first chapter: « Her filthiness is on her feet, and she hath not remembered her end ». *But,* when these feet are washed, then he *is clean wholly;* Ezechiel, the thirty-sixth chapter[^p426-8]: « I will pour upon you clean water, and you shall be cleansed from all your filthiness ». *And you are clean;* therefore you need only the washing of the feet; below, the fifteenth chapter: « You are clean by reason of the word, which I have spoken to you ». But lest he seem to be ignorant of the disciple's crime, he adds: *But not all.* And the reason why he had said this the Evangelist tells:

12. (Vers. 11.). *For he knew who he was that would betray him; therefore he said: You are not all clean.* Therefore the Evangelist, where he speaks of the traitor, shows that the Lord knew, lest anyone should believe that Christ as man could be deceived; above, the sixth chapter[^p426-9]: « Jesus knew from the beginning who they were that did not believe, and who he was that would betray him ».

### Questions.

13. Quaest. I. It is asked here concerning what he says, that this supper was made *before the festival day of the pasch.* — And from this it seems that the Lord confected from leavened bread. — But on the contrary stand the other Evangelists[^p426-10], who say that the Lord ate the pasch according to the common custom.

To this the Greeks respond, as is said in the Gloss[^p426-11], that the Lord went before, as is said here, the day of the pasch and confected from leavened bread, and that John corrects the other Evangelists.

But they speak foolishly; because far be it that the other Evangelists should have lied in anything, since they spoke by the same Spirit[^p426-12]. Hence the day of the pasch here is not called *the day of immolation,* but *the first among the days of unleavened bread,* on the evening preceding which the lamb was immolated.

14. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked concerning what he says, that *the devil having now put into the heart, that Judas should betray him.*
<!-- page 427 -->

1. Therefore it seems that the devil is one who puts in evil thoughts.

2. And that he is, it seems; because he is *the god of this world*[^p427-1] and *rules over the children of unbelief,* to the Ephesians, the second chapter: therefore just as God puts good thoughts into the elect, in whom he rules, so also the devil evil ones.

**On the contrary** this is, because, if the devil puts them in, then, whether a man wills or not, he will sin: therefore according to this he would be superior to the mind itself, which is against reason and Augustine[^p427-2].

**I respond:** It must be said that *to put into the heart* or to put in is said in two ways: *properly* and *commonly; properly to put in* is virtually to form within, and thus it belongs to God alone; but *commonly to put in* is to suggest by offering, through occasion. In the first way it does not belong to the devil, in the second way it does[^p427-3].

2. As for what is objected, that the devil reigns; it must be said that he does not reign by his own power, but through the consent of a depraved will, which willingly consents to him, according to that of Gregory[^p427-4]: « The enemy is weak against those who resist, strong against those who consent ». Therefore he is not said *to put in,* but *to suggest* or *to inflame.*

15. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked concerning this, that Peter alone resisted the Lord when he wished to wash his feet; how did all say nothing? And if all were silent, how did he himself wish to be singular? — *If you say* that the Lord came first to him, and he first resisted, and afterwards the others were corrected; the letter is contrary, because it is said: *He began to wash and to wipe;* and afterwards: *He cometh therefore to Simon Peter*[^p427-5].

Chrysostom responds[^p427-6] that he believes that he washed the feet of Judas first, because he, as one presumptuous, had seized the first place beforehand, and afterwards he came to Peter, and he resisted. — But it is better to say, according to the Gloss[^p427-7], that he came first to Peter, and that this was said by anticipation.
