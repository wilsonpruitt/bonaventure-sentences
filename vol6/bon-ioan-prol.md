# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Prologus S. Hieronymi
## *Prologus S. Hieronymi in Evangelium secundum Ioannem · Expositio huius prologi*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 244 -->

### Prologus S. Hieronymi in Evangelium secundum Ioannem[^p244-9].

Hic est Ioannes evangelista, unus ex discipulis Domini, qui virgo electus a Deo est, quem de nuptiis volentem nubere vocavit Deus. Cui virginitatis in hoc duplex testimonium in Evangelio datur: quod et prae ceteris dilectus a Domino dicitur, et huic Matrem suam pendens in cruce commendavit Dominus, ut Virginem virgo servaret. — Denique manifestans in Evangelio, quod erat ipse *incorruptibilis Verbi opus* inchoans, solus Verbum caro factum esse nec lumen a tenebris comprehensum fuisse testatur; primum *signum* ponens, quod in nuptiis fecit Dominus, ostendens, quod *ipse* erat, ut legentibus demonstraret, quod ubi Dominus invitatus est, deficere nuptiarum vinum debeat, et veteribus immutatis, nova omnia, quae a Christo instituuntur, appareant. — Hoc autem Evangelium scripsit in Asia, et postea quam in Patmos insula Apocalypsim scripserat; ut, cui in principio canonis incorruptibile principium praenotatur in Genesi, ei etiam incorruptibilis finis per virginem in Apocalypsi redderetur, dicente Christo: *Ego sum alpha et omega.* — Et hic est Ioannes, qui sciens, supervenisse diem recessus sui, convocatis discipulis suis in Epheso, per multa signorum experimenta promens Christum, descendens in defossum sepulturae suae locum, facta oratione, positus est ad patres suos, tam extraneus a dolore mortis, quam a corruptione carnis invenitur alienus. — Tamen post omnes Evangelium scripsit, et hoc virgini debebatur. Quorum tamen vel scriptorum temporis dispositio, vel librorum ordinatio ideo a nobis per singula non exponitur, ut sciendi desiderio collato et quaerentibus fructus laboris et Deo magisterii doctrina servetur. Explicit.
<!-- page 245 -->

### Expositio huius prologi.

12. Huic libro praemittit Hieronymus Prologum[^p245-1], in quo notificatur opus multipliciter. Nam primo tangitur *dignitas scriptoris*. Secundo vero, *modus scribendi*, ibi: *Denique manifestans in Evangelio.* Tertio, *tempus* et *locus* scribendi, ibi: *Hoc autem Evangelium scripsit in Asia.* Quarto, *obitus auctoris*, ibi: *Et hic est Ioannes, qui sciens.* Ultimo, *ordo* istius ad alia Evangelia, ibi: *Tamen post omnes scripsit Evangelium.*

*Dignitatem* igitur auctoris in hoc exprimit, quod « virgo a Domino de nuptiis est vocatus », et quod virginitati eius magna et nobilia testimonia perhibentur, scilicet Christi familiaris dilectio et Matris commissio[^p245-2].

*Modum* vero *scribendi* describit consonum scribenti in hoc, quod quia scriptor erat carne incorruptus[^p245-3] et de nuptiis vocatus; ideo primo « inchoat incorruptibilis Verbi generationem », et deinde inter *signa* inchoat a mutatione aquae in vinum facta in nuptiis; ex his « ostendens, quod *ipse* erat », quod est nomen[^p245-4], et tantum vult dicere, quantum ostendens, qualis erat, sicut communiter loquimur: tu monstras quod es.

*Locum scribendi* dicit « fuisse in Asia[^p245-5] »; *tempus* vero fuit, quia ultimo post Apocalypsim; et ita Ioannis Evangelium fuit ultimo inter libros sacrae Scripturae scriptum; et congruebat, ut, sicut in libro primo scripto, quod est « principium canonis, incorruptibile principium praenotatur », ita et in libro ultimo scripto[^p245-6]: *In principio erat Verbum*, sicut ibi: *In principio creavit*, et hoc, « dicente Domino in Apocalypsi: *Ego sum alpha et omega* ». Unde in Apocalypsis primo est determinatio eius quod est *dicente*. — Vel aliter potest legi, ut « finis incorruptibilis per virginem reddatur in Apocalypsi », quae est ultimus liber canonis, licet non ultimo scriptus. Sed prima littera magis consonat verbis.

*Obitum auctoris* describit tanquam mirabilem, quia praescivit ipsum; et cum multis miraculis floruisset, sine dolore de hac vita transivit. Sed tamen hoc habet dubitationem[^p245-7].

*Ordinem* ad alia Evangelia tangit, quia ultimo scripsit. Et hoc debitum fuit ei vel ut virgini verecundae, vel ut virgini incorruptae, vel ut virgini perfectae; ideo debuit consummare, tamen plene ordinem tangere recusat, ut aliis fructum laboris relinquat[^p245-8].

## English
<!-- page 244 -->

### The Prologue of St Jerome to the Gospel according to John[^p244-9].

This is John the Evangelist, one of the disciples of the Lord, who was chosen a virgin by God, whom God called from his wedding when he wished to marry. To his virginity a twofold testimony is given in the Gospel in this: that he is said to have been loved by the Lord before the others, and that the Lord, hanging on the cross, commended his Mother to him, that a virgin might keep the Virgin. — Finally, showing in the Gospel that it was he himself who was beginning *the work of the incorruptible Word*, he alone testifies that the Word was made flesh and that the lumen[^tr-lumen] was not comprehended by the darkness; setting down first the *sign* which the Lord did at the wedding, showing that which he *himself* was, so as to demonstrate to those who read that where the Lord is invited the wine of the wedding must fail, and that, the old things being changed, all the new things which are instituted by Christ should appear. — This Gospel, moreover, he wrote in Asia, and after he had written the Apocalypse on the island of Patmos; so that to him for whom an incorruptible beginning is noted beforehand at the beginning of the canon, in Genesis, an incorruptible end also might be rendered through a virgin in the Apocalypse, Christ saying: *I am Alpha and Omega.* — And this is John who, knowing that the day of his departure had come, having called together his disciples in Ephesus, setting forth Christ through many proofs by signs, going down into the dug place of his burial, having made a prayer, was laid with his fathers, found as much a stranger to the pain of death as alien from the corruption of the flesh. — Nevertheless he wrote the Gospel after all the others, and this was owed to the virgin. Yet the arrangement, whether of the time of the writings or of the order of the books, is not expounded by us point by point, for this reason: that, the desire of knowing having been conferred, the fruit of the labour might be kept for those who seek, and for God the teaching[^tr-doctrina] of the office of teacher. Here it ends.
<!-- page 245 -->

### The exposition of this prologue.

12. To this book Jerome prefixes a Prologue[^p245-1], in which the work is made known in many ways. For first *the dignity of the writer* is touched on. Secondly, *the manner of writing*, there: *Finally, showing in the Gospel.* Thirdly, *the time* and *place* of writing, there: *This Gospel, moreover, he wrote in Asia.* Fourthly, *the death of the author*, there: *And this is John who, knowing.* Lastly, the *order* of this one to the other Gospels, there: *Nevertheless after all the others he wrote the Gospel.*

*The dignity*, then, of the author he expresses in this, that « he was called a virgin by the Lord from a wedding », and that great and noble testimonies are borne to his virginity, namely the familiar dilection of Christ and the commission of the Mother[^p245-2].

*The manner*, moreover, *of writing* he describes as consonant with the writer in this: that because the writer was incorrupt in the flesh[^p245-3] and called from a wedding, therefore he first « begins the generation of the incorruptible Word », and then among the *signs* begins from the changing of water into wine done at a wedding; from these « showing that which he *himself* was », which is a name[^p245-4], and he means as much as "showing of what sort he was", as we commonly say: you show what you are.

*The place of writing* he says « was in Asia[^p245-5] »; *the time*, moreover, was that it came last, after the Apocalypse; and so the Gospel of John was the last written among the books of sacred Scripture; and it was fitting that, as in the book written first, which is « the beginning of the canon, an incorruptible beginning is noted beforehand », so also in the book written last[^p245-6]: *In the beginning was the Word*, as there: *In the beginning* God *created*; and this « the Lord saying in the Apocalypse: *I am Alpha and Omega* ». Whence in the first chapter of the Apocalypse stands the determination of what *saying* refers to. — Or it can be read otherwise, that « an incorruptible end might be rendered through a virgin in the Apocalypse », which is the last book of the canon, although not written last. But the first reading accords better with the words.

*The death of the author* he describes as wondrous, because he foreknew it; and since he had flourished with many miracles, he passed from this life without pain. But yet this admits of doubt[^p245-7].

*The order* to the other Gospels he touches on, because he wrote last. And this was owed to him either as to a modest virgin, or as to an incorrupt virgin, or as to a perfect virgin; therefore he had to bring it to completion; yet he declines to touch on the order fully, so as to leave to others the fruit of the labour[^p245-8].

[^tr-lumen]: **En.** Translator's note: *lumen* stands untranslated here and at its one further site in this chunk — Albert's *aeternum lumen* in note 4 of p. 245 — against *lux*, which this translation renders "light" and which does not occur in this chunk. *Lux* is light at its source and *lumen* light as given off and received. The prologue's *nec lumen a tenebris comprehensum* recalls John 1:5, where the Vulgate itself reads *lux* (*et tenebrae eam non comprehenderunt*); the prologue's word is *lumen*, and the translation follows the word printed, not the verse it recalls. "Lumen" is left standing here as it is throughout this translation, the Gospel's own *de lumine* at John 1:7–8 included.

[^tr-doctrina]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin *doctrina* holds together what English divides among doctrine, teaching and learning. In the prologue's last sentence, *Deo magisterii doctrina servetur*, the word stands beside *magisterium*, the office of the *magister*, the teacher: what is kept for God is the teaching that belongs to the one who teaches, while the seekers keep the fruit of their labour. "Teaching" is therefore the English here, as it was throughout the Prooemium of this commentary.
