# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. III, Vers. 3-12
## *Descripta est diligentia Nicodemi, describitur hic doctrina Christi, in qua docet Nicodemum, quomodo possit pervenire ad salutem, quia hoc quaerere venerat*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 279 -->

7. *Respondit Iesus et dixit ei* etc. Descripta est diligentia Nicodemi, describitur hic *doctrina Christi,* in qua docet Nicodemum, quomodo possit pervenire ad salutem, quia hoc quaerere venerat. Et habet haec pars tres partes secundum tria, quae ipsum docet. Primo docet ipsum, quae *via salutis;* secundo, quod *salutis principium,* ibi[^p279-6]: *Nemo ascendit in caelum;* tertio, quod est *salutis impedimentum,* ibi: *Non enim misit Deus Filium suum* etc.

Primo igitur docet, quae sit *via salutis:* quoniam Sacramentum regenerationis, in cuius instructione procedit hoc ordine. Primo exprimit *necessitatem regenerationis;* secundo, *modum regenerandi;* tertio, *modum intelligendi.*

8. (Vers. 3.). *Necessitatem* igitur *regenerationis* exprimit ipsi Nicodemo addiscere cupienti. Propter quod dicit: *Respondit Iesus et dixit ei,* scilicet Nicodemo de nocte venienti et quaerenti: *Amen, amen dico tibi: Nisi quis natus fuerit denuo,* id est regeneratus, *non potest videre regnum Dei*[^p279-7]. Et secundum hoc patet, quod regeneratio *necessaria* est ad salutem.

Nativitas *naturae;* Ioannis decimo sexto[^p279-8]: « Mulier, cum parit, tristitiam habet; sed cum peperit, non meminit pressurae propter gaudium, quia natus est homo in mundum ». — *Culpae;* ad Ephesios secundo[^p279-9]: « Eramus natura filii irae »; *natura,* id est corruptione naturae; ideo Iob tertio: « Pereat dies, in qua natus sum ». — *Gratiae;* primae Ioannis ultimo[^p279-10]: « Omnis, qui natus est ex Deo, non peccat, sed gratia Dei custodit ipsum »; et hoc fit per Sacramentum. — *Gloriae;* Iob undecimo[^p279-11]: « Cum te consumtum putaveris, orieris ut lucifer ». — Hic igitur dicit necessariam nativitatem Sacramenti et gratiae.

9. (Vers. 4.). *Dicit ad eum Nicodemus.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *modus regenerandi,* qui est *spiritualis.* Sed Nicodemus, aestimans, ipsum esse carnalem, dubitat et quaerit: *Quomodo potest homo nasci, cum sit senex?* Quasi: hoc est impossibile, de sene iuvenem fieri; Iob septimo[^p279-12]: « Dies mei velocius transierunt, quam a texente tela succiditur; et consumti sunt absque ulla spe ». Impossibilitas etiam est a parte generantis; unde dicit: *Nunquid potest in ventrem matris suae iterato introire et renasci?* Adhuc carnalis erat, et ideo carnaliter intelligebat; primae ad Corinthios secundo[^p279-13]: « Animalis homo non percipit ea quae sunt spiritus Dei ». Sed Dominus ipsum retrahit ab intelligentia carnali ad spiritualem; propter quod dicit:

10. (Vers. 5.). *Respondit Iesus: Amen, amen dico tibi: Nisi quis renatus fuerit ex aqua et Spiritu sancto; non potest introire in regnum Dei;*
<!-- page 280 -->
quasi dicat: non intelligo de nativitate, quae est ex ventre, sed quae est ex aqua; non intelligo de nativitate, quae est ex semine, sed quae est ex Spiritu sancto; quia illa carnalis et manifesta, ista *spiritualis* et *occulta.* Propterea dicit:

11. (Vers. 6.). *Quod natum est ex carne caro est, et quod natum est ex spiritu spiritus est.* Ideo, sicut generatio ex carne est carnalis, ita generatio ex spiritu est *spiritualis;* supra primo[^p280-1]: « Qui non ex sanguinibus neque ex voluntate carnis neque ex voluntate viri, sed ex Deo nati sunt ». Nec tantum est spiritualis, immo etiam *occulta;* et ideo non debet mirari Nicodemus animalis, si non intelligit. Propterea dicit:

12. (Vers. 7.). *Non mireris, quia dixi tibi, oportet vos nasci denuo,* quamvis non intelligas.

13. (Vers. 8.). *Spiritus ubi vult spirat; ubi vult,* dando gratiam; ad Romanos nono[^p280-2]: « Cuius vult miseretur, et quem vult indurat ». *Et vocem eius audis,* in praedicatoribus; Matthaei decimo[^p280-3]: « Non enim vos estis, qui loquimini, sed Spiritus Patris vestri, qui loquitur in vobis ». *Et nescis, unde veniat, aut quo vadat;* Iob nono: « Si venerit ad me, non videbo eum; et si abierit, non intelligam ». *Sic est omnis, qui natus est ex Spiritu,* scilicet spiritualis et occultus; ad Romanos sexto[^p280-4]: « Consepulti enim sumus cum illo per baptismum in mortem, ut, quomodo surrexit Christus a mortuis per gloriam Patris, ita et nos in novitate vitae ambulemus ». Sicut sepultura et mors et vita est spiritualis, ita etiam generatio.

14. (Vers. 9.). *Respondit Nicodemus.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *modus intelligendi* hanc regenerationem spiritualem, quem quia non habebat Nicodemus, ideo dubitat et quaerit; unde dicit: *Respondit Nicodemus et dixit ei: Quomodo possunt haec fieri?* Et in hoc notatur diligentia in Nicodemo, qui non cessavit a quaerendo, donec intelligeret; Chrysostomus[^p280-5]: « Si Iudaei audissent, statim deridentes recederent »; et ideo meretur a Domino plenius edoceri qui motus est per intellectus captivationem et fidem. Ideo humiliat eum; propter quod dicit:

15. (Vers. 10.). *Respondit Iesus et dixit ei: Tu es magister in Israel, et haec ignoras?* Quasi dicat: te doctorem magnum reputas, et tamen ignoras fidei initia; ad Hebraeos quinto[^p280-6]: « Cum deberetis esse magistri propter tempus, rursus indigetis, ut doceamini, quae sint elementa exordii sermonum Dei »; quasi dicat: si vis intelligere, oportet te humiliare et credere; quia, si hoc non credis, nec maiora intelligere poteris. Propterea dicit:

16. (Vers. 11.). *Amen, amen dico tibi, quia nos*[^p280-7] *quod scimus loquimur;* « *nos,* id est ego et mei Apostoli », ut dicit Glossa; vel *nos* pluraliter mysterium Trinitatis innuit. *Et quod vidimus testamur;* infra octavo[^p280-8]: « Quod vidi apud Patrem meum, hoc loquor »; et ita credendum esset mihi; sed tamen vos non creditis. Ideo subdit: *Et testimonium nostrum non accipitis;* infra quinto[^p280-9]: « Ego veni in nomine Patris mei, et non accipitis me ». Et si non accipitis in parvis quae dixi neque creditis, multo minus nec in magnis. Ideo subdit:

17. (Vers. 12.). *Si terrena dixi vobis, et non creditis; quomodo, si dixero vobis caelestia, credetis?* Quasi dicat: non; et ideo *modus intelligendi* est per fidem credere minora, antequam perveniatur ad sublimia; primae ad Corinthios tertio[^p280-10]: « Tanquam parvulis in Christo lac dedi vobis potum, non escam ». Si autem lac non susciperent, quomodo escam suscipere possent? — Ex praedictis igitur patet, quod[^p280-11] *regeneratio* est *necessaria,* est *spiritualis* et *occulta,* et quod *nullus potest intelligere,* nisi credat.

### Quaestiones.

18. Quaest. I. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Nisi quis natus*[^p280-12] *fuerit denuo, non potest videre regnum Dei.*

**Contra:** 1. Si hoc *erat praeceptum:* ergo nullus salvari potest nisi baptizatus *ex aqua et Spiritu sancto.* — *Contra:* Multi Martyres fuerunt, qui non baptizati fuerunt. — Item, multi sunt, qui habent fidem et morte praeveniuntur; sed dicitur infra eodem[^p280-13]: *Omnis, qui credit in Filium Dei, non perit:* ergo si non baptizetur, adhuc salvabitur.

2. Quod si *non est praeceptum:* ergo male dicit, quod *non potest introire in regnum Dei*[^p280-14], quia *videre* hic accipitur pro visione patriae.
<!-- page 281 -->

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod est baptismus *fluminis, flaminis* et *sanguinis.* Dicendum ergo, quod secundum legem communem omnes tenentur baptizari, quia *praeceptum* est. Si autem intervenit impossibilitas; aut adest gratia sive baptismus flaminis vel sanguinis, aut non; si sic, supplet illum baptismum, quando non est possibilitas. Si vero non adest, impossibilitas non excusat nec facit, ut salvetur, non propter transgressionem *praecepti,* sed propter *maculam originalis,* quae deleta non est per gratiam. — Et sic patet, quod illi qui habent fidem formatam, salvantur, cum morte praeveniuntur[^p281-1].

19. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur, utrum ex tunc fuerit necessarium baptizari. — Quod *sic,* videtur:

1. Quia tunc promulgata fuit sententia, quod sine hoc non erat salus.

2. Item, si tunc non fuit institutus, quando ergo fuit institutus baptismus?

Respondet magister Hugo[^p281-2] et distinguit triplex tempus gratiae: quoddam, in quo cucurrit circumcisio tantum, ut ante adventum Christi; quoddam, in quo baptismus tantum, ut post manifestationem Evangelii; quoddam, in quo utrumque mixtim, ut tempore medio, et tunc habebat effectum suum circumcisio, et pro tempore illo non erat necessarium baptizari, nec Dominus hic sententialiter promulgavit regenerationis necessitatem, sed quod facturus erat revelavit amico[^p281-3].

2. Quod quaeritur: quando institutus fuit baptismus? dicendum, quod *materialiter,* cum baptizatus fuit Christus; *formaliter,* cum resurrexit et formam dedit, Matthaei ultimo[^p281-4]; *effective,* cum passus fuit, quia inde habuit virtutem; sed *finaliter,* cum eius necessitatem praedixit et utilitatem, ut hic: *Nisi quis renatus fuerit ex aqua et Spiritu sancto* etc.

20. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Quod natum est ex carne caro est.*

1. Ergo *et quod natum est ex spiritu spiritus est:* ergo quod nascitur ex sancto, sanctum: ergo sanctus homo generat sanctum hominem, scilicet filium; quod manifeste falsum est, immo[^p281-5] peccatorem.

2. Item, si quod nascitur ex spiritu est spiritus, ergo quod nascitur ex Deo Deus est; sed omnes sumus ex Deo nati, sicut dicitur primae Ioannis quinto[^p281-6]: ergo omnes sumus dii.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod haec intelligenda est *per se:* quod nascitur ex carne generante, scilicet secundum quod caro, est *carnale;* similiter de spiritu, est *spirituale.*

1. Quod obiicit de viro sancto, respondetur, quod carnaliter generat, ideo generat carnalem. Unde etsi sit purgatus secundum quod *persona,* non tamen, secundum quod est *generationis principium.* Exemplum Augustini[^p281-7] est de grano mundo, quod generat granum in paleis.

2. Quod dicit de spiritu, illud dicitur non per *essentiam,* sed per *conformitatem* et adhaerentiam; primae ad Corinthios sexto[^p281-8]: *Qui adhaeret Deo unus spiritus est.*

21. Quaest. IV. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Spiritus ubi vult spirat, et nescis, unde veniat.* — *Contra:* Bernardus in Canticis[^p281-9] dicit, quod debet homo cognoscere tempus visitationis spiritualis.

Chrysostomus[^p281-10] respondet, quod hoc dixit de vento, quia homo non habet eius cognitionem, multo minus spiritualis nativitatis. — Sed Augustinus[^p281-11] istam expositionem respuit, quia bene scit homo, quod auster venit a meridie et tendit in septentrionem; ideo exponit de Spiritu sancto.

Quod ergo dicitur: *Nescis, unde veniat,* facienda est vis in verbo. Non dicit: nescis, *quando* veniat, quia bene potest scire per fervorem et teporem, *quando* venit; sed nescis, *unde veniat,* utrum scilicet ad retribuendum in praesenti bona, quae homo gessit, vel ad augenda merita; nescis, *quo vadat,* utrum *longe,* nunquam reversurus, vel *prope,* illico rediturus. Ideo dicit Bernardus[^p281-12], quod semper timendum: « Time », inquit, « cum arriserit, time, cum abierit, et cum denuo revertetur ».

22. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Si terrena dixi vobis* etc.: quae sunt ista *terrena?* quia totum est spirituale, quod locutus est, nihil terrenum.
<!-- page 282 -->

Ad hoc respondet Chrysostomus[^p282-1], quod *terrena,* quae dixit, sunt illa quae dixit de *spiritu,* id est de vento; sed Augustinus[^p282-2] exponit de suscitatione templi, quam praedixerat in praecedenti capitulo, scilicet secundum quod de terra erat, sive intelligatur de templo materiali, sive de corpore Christi, quia de terra nostra sumtum est. — Potest responderi aliter, quod *terrena* vocat ea quae praedicta sunt, non absolute, sed quia parva in respectu dicendorum.

## English
<!-- page 279 -->

7. *Jesus answered, and said to him* etc. The diligence of Nicodemus has been described; here is described *the teaching*[^tr-doctrina] *of Christ,* in which he teaches Nicodemus how he can arrive at salvation, because he had come to seek this. And this part has three parts according to the three things which he teaches him. First he teaches him what is *the way of salvation;* second, what is *the principle of salvation,* at the place[^p279-6]: *No man hath ascended into heaven;* third, what is *the impediment of salvation,* at the place: *For God sent not his Son* etc.

First, then, he teaches what *the way of salvation* is: since it is the Sacrament of regeneration, in the instruction on which he proceeds in this order. First he expresses *the necessity of regeneration;* second, *the mode of regenerating;* third, *the mode of understanding.*

8. (Verse 3.). *The necessity,* then, *of regeneration* he expresses to Nicodemus himself, who was desiring to learn. On account of which it says: *Jesus answered, and said to him,* namely to Nicodemus, who came by night and asked: *Amen, amen I say to thee: Unless a man be born again,* that is, regenerated, *he cannot see the kingdom of God*[^p279-7]. And according to this it is plain that regeneration is *necessary* for salvation.

The birth *of nature;* John, the sixteenth chapter[^p279-8]: « A woman, when she bringeth forth, hath sorrow; but when she hath brought forth, she remembereth not the anguish for joy that a man is born into the world ». — *Of fault;* to the Ephesians, the second chapter[^p279-9]: « We were by nature children of wrath »; *by nature,* that is, by the corruption of nature; therefore Job, the third chapter: « Let the day perish wherein I was born ». — *Of grace;* the first of John, the last chapter[^p279-10]: « Whosoever is born of God sinneth not, but the grace of God keepeth him »; and this comes about through the Sacrament. — *Of glory;* Job, the eleventh chapter[^p279-11]: « When thou shalt think thyself consumed, thou shalt rise as the day star ». — Here, then, he says that the birth of the Sacrament and of grace is necessary.

9. (Verse 4.). *Nicodemus saith to him.* Here the second thing is touched on, namely *the mode of regenerating,* which is *spiritual.* But Nicodemus, judging it to be carnal, doubts and asks: *How can a man be born when he is old?* As if to say: this is impossible, that from an old man a young man should be made; Job, the seventh chapter[^p279-12]: « My days have passed more swiftly than the web is cut by the weaver, and are consumed without any hope ». There is an impossibility also on the part of the one generating; whence he says: *Can he enter a second time into his mother's womb, and be born again?* He was still carnal, and therefore he understood carnally; the first to the Corinthians, the second chapter[^p279-13]: « The animal man perceiveth not the things that are of the spirit of God ». But the Lord draws him back from a carnal intelligence to a spiritual one; on account of which he says:

10. (Verse 5.). *Jesus answered: Amen, amen I say to thee: Unless a man be born again of water and the Holy Spirit; he cannot enter into the kingdom of God;*
<!-- page 280 -->
as if to say: I do not mean the birth which is from the womb, but that which is from water; I do not mean the birth which is from seed, but that which is from the Holy Spirit; because the former is carnal and manifest, the latter *spiritual* and *hidden.* On account of this he says:

11. (Verse 6.). *That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the spirit is spirit.* Therefore, just as generation from the flesh is carnal, so generation from the spirit is *spiritual;* above, the first chapter[^p280-1]: « Who are born not of blood nor of the will of the flesh nor of the will of man, but of God ». Nor is it only spiritual, but also *hidden;* and therefore Nicodemus, an animal man, ought not to wonder if he does not understand. On account of this he says:

12. (Verse 7.). *Wonder not that I said to thee, you must be born again,* although thou dost not understand.

13. (Verse 8.). *The Spirit breatheth where he will; where he will,* by giving grace; to the Romans, the ninth chapter[^p280-2]: « He hath mercy on whom he will, and whom he will he hardeneth ». *And thou hearest his voice,* in the preachers; Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p280-3]: « For it is not you that speak, but the Spirit of your Father that speaketh in you ». *And thou knowest not whence he cometh, or whither he goeth;* Job, the ninth chapter: « If he come to me, I shall not see him; and if he depart, I shall not understand ». *So is every one that is born of the Spirit,* namely spiritual and hidden; to the Romans, the sixth chapter[^p280-4]: « For we are buried together with him by baptism into death; that, as Christ is risen from the dead by the glory of the Father, so we also may walk in newness of life ». Just as the burial and the death and the life are spiritual, so also is the generation.

14. (Verse 9.). *Nicodemus answered.* Here the third thing is touched on, namely *the mode of understanding* this spiritual regeneration, which, because Nicodemus did not have it, he therefore doubts and asks; whence it says: *Nicodemus answered, and said to him: How can these things be done?* And in this there is noted diligence in Nicodemus, who did not cease from asking until he should understand; Chrysostom[^p280-5]: « If the Jews had heard, they would at once have gone away mocking »; and therefore he who was moved deserves to be more fully taught by the Lord through the captivity of the understanding and faith. Therefore he humbles him; on account of which it says:

15. (Verse 10.). *Jesus answered, and said to him: Thou art a teacher in Israel, and knowest not these things?* As if to say: thou reputest thyself a great doctor, and yet thou knowest not the beginnings of faith; to the Hebrews, the fifth chapter[^p280-6]: « Whereas for the time you ought to be teachers, you have need again to be taught what are the first elements of the words of God »; as if to say: if thou wishest to understand, thou must humble thyself and believe; because, if thou dost not believe this, neither wilt thou be able to understand greater things. On account of this he says:

16. (Verse 11.). *Amen, amen I say to thee, that we*[^p280-7] *speak what we know;* « *we,* that is, I and my Apostles », as the Gloss says; or *we* in the plural hints at the mystery of the Trinity. *And we testify what we have seen;* below, the eighth chapter[^p280-8]: « What I have seen with my Father, this I speak »; and so I ought to be believed; but yet you do not believe. Therefore he adds: *And you receive not our testimony;* below, the fifth chapter[^p280-9]: « I am come in the name of my Father, and you receive me not ». And if you do not receive in small things what I have said, nor believe, much less in great things. Therefore he adds:

17. (Verse 12.). *If I have spoken to you earthly things, and you believe not; how, if I shall speak to you heavenly things, will you believe?* As if to say: not at all; and therefore *the mode of understanding* is to believe lesser things by faith before one arrives at sublime things; the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter[^p280-10]: « As to little ones in Christ, I gave you milk to drink, not meat ». But if they would not take milk, how could they take meat? — From what has been said, then, it is plain that[^p280-11] *regeneration* is *necessary,* is *spiritual* and *hidden,* and that *no one can understand* unless he believe.

### Questions.

18. Question I. It is asked about this that he says: *Unless a man be born*[^p280-12] *again, he cannot see the kingdom of God.*

**On the contrary:** 1. If this *was a precept:* therefore no one can be saved unless baptized *of water and the Holy Spirit.* — *On the contrary:* There were many Martyrs who were not baptized. — Likewise, there are many who have faith and are overtaken by death; but it is said below in the same chapter[^p280-13]: *Every one who believeth in the Son of God perisheth not:* therefore if he is not baptized, he will still be saved.

2. But if it *is not a precept:* therefore he says wrongly that *he cannot enter into the kingdom of God*[^p280-14], because *to see* is taken here for the vision of the fatherland.
<!-- page 281 -->

**I respond:** It must be said that there is a baptism *of water, of the Spirit* and *of blood.* It must be said, then, that according to the common law all are bound to be baptized, because it is a *precept.* But if an impossibility intervenes; either grace, that is, the baptism of the Spirit or of blood, is present, or not; if so, it supplies for that baptism, when there is no possibility. But if it is not present, the impossibility does not excuse nor bring it about that he be saved, not on account of the transgression of the *precept,* but on account of *the stain of original* sin, which has not been blotted out through grace. — And so it is plain that those who have formed faith are saved when they are overtaken by death[^p281-1].

19. Question II. Likewise it is asked whether from that time it was necessary to be baptized. — That it *was,* is seen:

1. Because then the sentence was promulgated that without this there was no salvation.

2. Likewise, if it was not instituted then, when, then, was baptism instituted?

Master Hugh[^p281-2] answers and distinguishes a threefold time of grace: one, in which circumcision alone ran, as before the coming of Christ; one, in which baptism alone, as after the manifestation of the Gospel; one, in which both together, as in the middle time; and then circumcision had its effect, and for that time it was not necessary to be baptized, nor did the Lord here promulgate the necessity of regeneration as a sentence, but he revealed to a friend what he was going to do[^p281-3].

2. As to what is asked: when was baptism instituted? it must be said that *materially,* when Christ was baptized; *formally,* when he rose again and gave the form, Matthew, the last chapter[^p281-4]; *effectively,* when he suffered, because from that it had its virtue; but *finally,* when he foretold its necessity and usefulness, as here: *Unless a man be born again of water and the Holy Spirit* etc.

20. Question III. Likewise it is asked about this that he says: *That which is born of the flesh is flesh.*

1. Therefore *also that which is born of the spirit is spirit:* therefore what is born of the holy is holy: therefore a holy man begets a holy man, namely a son; which is manifestly false, but rather[^p281-5] a sinner.

2. Likewise, if what is born of the spirit is spirit, therefore what is born of God is God; but we are all born of God, as is said in the first of John, the fifth chapter[^p281-6]: therefore we are all gods.

**I respond:** It must be said that this is to be understood *per se:* what is born of flesh generating, namely according as it is flesh, is *carnal;* likewise of the spirit, it is *spiritual.*

1. As to what he objects about a holy man, it is answered that he generates carnally, therefore he generates one carnal. Whence, though he be cleansed insofar as he is a *person,* yet not insofar as he is *a principle of generation.* The example of Augustine[^p281-7] is about clean grain, which generates grain in chaff.

2. What he says about the spirit, that is said not by *essence,* but by *conformity* and adherence; the first to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter[^p281-8]: *He who adhereth to God is one spirit.*

21. Question IV. It is asked about this that he says: *The Spirit breatheth where he will, and thou knowest not whence he cometh.* — *On the contrary:* Bernard, on the *Canticles*[^p281-9], says that a man ought to know the time of the spiritual visitation.

Chrysostom[^p281-10] answers that he said this of the wind, because man does not have knowledge of it, much less of the spiritual birth. — But Augustine[^p281-11] rejects this exposition, because a man knows well that the south wind comes from the south and tends to the north; therefore he expounds it of the Holy Spirit.

As to what is said, then: *Thou knowest not whence he cometh,* stress must be laid on the word. He does not say: thou knowest not *when* he cometh, because he can well know by fervour and lukewarmness *when* he comes; but thou knowest not *whence he cometh,* namely whether to repay in the present the good things which a man has done, or to increase merits; thou knowest not *whither he goeth,* whether *far,* never to return, or *near,* to return at once. Therefore Bernard[^p281-12] says that one must always fear: « Fear », he says, « when it has smiled, fear when it has gone away, and when it returns again ».

22. Question V. Likewise it is asked about this that he says: *If I have spoken to you earthly things* etc.: what are these *earthly things?* since all that he spoke is spiritual, nothing earthly.
<!-- page 282 -->

To this Chrysostom[^p282-1] answers that the *earthly things* which he spoke are those which he spoke of *the spirit,* that is, of the wind; but Augustine[^p282-2] expounds it of the raising of the temple, which he had foretold in the preceding chapter, namely insofar as it was of earth, whether it be understood of the material temple or of the body of Christ, because it was taken from our earth. — It can be answered otherwise, that he calls *earthly* the things which were said before, not absolutely, but because they are small in respect of the things to be said.
