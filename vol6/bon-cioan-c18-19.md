## Latin
<!-- page 615 -->

### Capitulum XVIII. et XIX. *Tunc ergo apprehendit Pilatus Iesum*[^p615-5].

Circa passionem Christi, de qua agitur in capitulo isto, consideranda sunt quatuor, scilicet *traditio, illusio, flagellatio* et *crucifixio,* quae quatuor breviter notavit ipse Iesus, cum ascenderet ad passionem, Matthaei vigesimo[^p615-6]: « Tradent eum gentibus ad illudendum et flagellandum et crucifigendum ».

### Collatio LXVII. et prima de passione Christi.

1. Circa Christi *traditionem* nota, quod Christus fuit iniuste traditus a tribus: a *Iuda;* Ioannis decimo octavo[^p615-7]: « Stabat autem et Iudas, qui tradebat eum Iudaeis »; a *Iudaeis;* Ioannis decimo octavo: « Gens tua et pontifices tui tradiderunt te mihi »; a *Pilato;* Ioannis decimo nono: « Tunc tradidit eis Iesum, ut crucifigeretur ». — *Iudas* tradidit eum ex *cupiditate;* Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p615-8]: « Quid mihi vultis dare, et ego eum vobis tradam ». *Iudaei* ex *malignitate et invidia;* Marci decimo quinto: « Sciebat autem, quod per invidiam tradidissent eum ». *Pilatus* ex *populari gratia* vel favore; Marci decimo quinto: « Pilatus autem, volens satisfacere populo dimisit illis Barabbam et tradidit illis Iesum flagellis caesum, ut crucifigerent eum ». — Ad quorum exemplum quidam hodie persequuntur Christum-veritatem ex *cupiditate,* ut simoniaci; quidam ex *malignitate et invidia,* sicut haeretici; quidam ex *populari gratia* vel favore, sicut praedicatores remissi[^p615-9].

2. Nota, quod Christi traditio nobis proponitur in *speculum* contemplationis, in *remedium* liberationis, in *exemplum* imitationis et in *documentum* instructionis. — In *speculum contemplationis* nobis proponitur, ut ex eius contemplatione ad dilectionem accendamur. Ter enim fuit Christus traditus: primo a *Iuda,* et in illa traditione fuit *venditus;* secundo a *Iudaeis,* et in illa traditione fuit *condemnatus;* tertio a *Pilato,* et in illa traditione fuit *crucifixus.* Quomodo ergo se poterat pro nobis perfectius expendere, nisi ut *venderetur* pro nobis redimendis, *condemnaretur* pro nobis absolvendis, *crucifigeretur* pro nobis liberandis? Hoc enim totum factum est in sua traditione; quod considerans, beatus Paulus dicebat ad Galatas secundo[^p615-10]: « Vivo ego, iam non ego, vivit vero in me Christus; quod autem nunc vivo in carne, in fide vivo Filii Dei, qui dilexit me et tradidit semetipsum pro me ».

3. Traditio Christi nobis fuit in *remedium liberationis.* Ter enim fuit Christus traditus: primo *hosti;* secundo *iudici;* tertio *tortori. Iudas* enim tradidit eum hostibus, id est Iudaeis; *hostes* iudici, id est Pilato; *Pilatus* tortoribus, id est militibus,
<!-- page 616 -->
qui eum crucifixerunt. Hoc autem totum factum est, ut homo traditus liberaretur; qui primo tradebatur *hosti* in peccati commissione; secundo tradebatur *iudici* in morte; tertio tradebatur *tortoribus* in inferno. Ut ergo ab ista triplici traditione liberaretur homo, tradi voluit Deus homo, ne ultra plangeret illud quod dicitur in Psalmo[^p616-1]: « Traditus sum et non egrediebar; oculi mei languerunt prae inopia ».

4. Proponitur tertio traditio Christi in *exemplum imitationis,* sicut praedixerat Dominus, discipulos suos tradendos esse, Matthaei decimo[^p616-2]: « Tradent vos in conciliis et in synagogis suis et flagellabunt vos » etc. Ne ergo nimis durum videretur eis tradi in conciliis, praemisit Dominus exemplum suae traditionis. Fuit enim traditus ab *amico,* scilicet a Iuda; ab *inimico,* scilicet populo Iudaeorum; ab *extraneo,* scilicet a Pilato, qui non cognoscebat eum: ut, sive traderentur discipuli ab *amicis,* sive ab *inimicis,* vel ab *extraneis,* acciperent a Christo exemplum humilitatis. — Vel distingue aliter, quia Christus fuit traditus a *discipulo,* ab *adversario* et a *Pilato,* ut, sive tradatur Christianus ab inferiori, sive ab aequali, sive a superiori, inveniat in Christo exemplum sustinendi. Ideo exemplum Christi considerans Paulus, cum prophetaret Agabus, Actuum vigesimo primo[^p616-3] dicens: « Virum, cuius est zona haec, sic alligabunt Iudaei in Ierusalem et tradent in manus gentium »; respondit: « Ego non solum alligari, sed etiam mori in Ierusalem paratus sum propter nomen Domini Iesu ».

5. Proponitur etiam nobis Christi traditio in *documentum instructionis,* ut caveamus, ne simus traditores veritatis sive ex *cupiditate* cum Iuda, sive ex *favore* cum Pilato sive ex *invidia* cum Iudaeis, quod explanatum est supra in prima parte collationis[^p616-4].

### Collatio LXVIII. et secunda de passione Christi.

1. Circa Christi *illusionem* nota, quod ter fuit Christus illusus. Illusum enim fuit ei in *domo pontificis;* Lucae vigesimo secundo[^p616-5]: « Viri, qui tenebant eum, illudebant ei caedentes, et velaverunt eum et percutiebant faciem eius et interrogabant eum dicentes: Prophetiza, quis est qui te percussit »? — Illusum fuit ei in *domo iudicis;* Matthaei vigesimo septimo: « Tunc milites, suscipientes Iesum in praetorio et exuentes eum, chlamidem coccineam circumdederunt ei, et plectentes coronam de spinis, posuerunt super caput eius et arundinem in dextera eius, et genu flexo illudebant ei dicentes: Ave, rex Iudaeorum ». — Illusum etiam fuit Christo in *patibulo crucis;* unde Marci decimo quinto[^p616-6]: « Et praetereuntes blasphemabant eum, moventes capita sua et dicentes: Vah! qui destruis templum Dei »; « similiter et summi sacerdotes, illudentes ad alterutrum cum scribis, dicebant: Alios salvos fecit, se ipsum non potest salvum facere ».

2. Attende igitur Christi illusionem, quia ter proponitur in *speculum* contemplationis, in *remedium* liberationis, in *exemplum* imitationis, in *documentum* instructionis. — Proponitur ergo primo in *speculum contemplationis,* ut videndo contumeliam illusionis agnoscamus in sustinente magnitudinem caritatis. Attende ergo, quod tribus generibus hominum grave est sustinere. Graviter enim sustinet illudi *sapiens* a minus sciente; gravius sustinet *potens* a minus potente; gravissime sustinet *benefaciens* ab egeno eius beneficium recipiente. — Haec omnia facta sunt Christo in eius illusione. In *domo* enim *pontificis* illusum est *sapientiae* suae a stultis, quando velaverunt faciem eius et interrogabant[^p616-7]: « Prophetiza: quis est qui te percussit »? quasi nihil sciret, nisi quae oculis corporeis videret. — In *domo iudicis* illusum est suae *potentiae* a servis, quando « plectentes coronam de spinis, imposuerunt capiti eius et illudebant ei dicentes: Ave, rex Iudaeorum »; quasi ridiculum esset, quod ipse rex esse deberet. — In *patibulo crucis* illusum fuit eius *misericordiae* ab ipsis miseris dicentibus[^p616-8]: « Alios salvos fecit, se ipsum non potest salvum facere »; quasi ridiculum esset, quod ipse genus humanum redimeret. — Contemplare ergo, anima fidelis, gravitatem illusionis, et repende vicem dilectionis ei qui pro te factus est « opprobrium hominum et abiectio plebis »; de quo Threnorum tertio: « Dabit percutienti se maxillam et saturabitur opprobriis ».

3. Proponitur Christi illusio in *remedium liberationis.* Nota ergo, quod Christus voluit sibi illudi, ut parceretur homini ab illusione *peccati,* ab illusione *poenali.* Peccatori enim illuditur primo a *seducente,* quando peccat; secundo illuditur ei, ut solet aliquando illudi homini damnando a *deducente* sive deprehendente, a *iudice* et *tortore.* Ut ergo ab huiusmodi illusionibus liberaretur homo damnandus, voluit loco hominis Christus sibi illudi, quando *deprehensus* fuit in domo pontificis, et quando *condemnatus* fuit in praetorio iudicis, et quando *occisus* fuit in patibulo crucis, ne accideret homini illa illusio, de qua dicitur Ecclesiastici vigesimo septimo[^p616-9]: « Facienti nequissimum consilium super ipsum devolvetur, et non agnoscet, unde adveniat illi. Illusio et improperium super bonum » etc.

4. Proponitur etiam Christi illusio in *exemplum imitationis.* Non enim solum illuditur damnando, sed
<!-- page 617 -->
aliquando innocenti illudit malitia illudentis; cuiusmodi illusionem Scriptura damnat Proverbiorum tertio[^p617-1]: « Illusores ipse deridet ». Et ideo Dominus sciens, illudendum fore suis, ut patienter sustineant, praemittit exemplum suae illusionis.

5. Nota, quod Christo fuit illusum *signis, verbis* et *factis: signis,* ut quando ipsum adorabant et coronabant; *factis,* ut quando ipsum velabant et conspuebant; *verbis,* ut quando ipsum salutabant et blasphemabant[^p617-2]. Et haec omnia voluit Christus sibi fieri, ut, sive verbis, sive signis, sive factis illuderetur quis a suis, inveniret in Christo exemplum humilitatis.

6. Proponitur etiam Christi illusio in *documentum instructionis;* non enim illuditur solummodo *contumeliose,* sed etiam aliquando *seditiose;* et sicut vult Dominus, quod nos sustineamus contumeliosam illusionem, ita vult, quod praecaveamus seditiosam. — Nota ergo, quod diabolus illudit homini ad *seducendum,* secundum quod habetur Proverbiorum decimo quarto[^p617-3]: « Stulto illudit peccatum ». Illudit aliquando diabolus homini per concupiscentiam *honorum,* aliquando per concupiscentiam *divitiarum,* aliquando per concupiscentiam *voluptatum;* unde Psalmus: « Lumbi mei impleti sunt illusionibus ».

7. Prima illusio notata est in Christi *coronatione;* secunda notata est in *veste purpurea,* quam ei circumdederunt; tertia notata est in *potu,* quem ei in cruce porrexerunt[^p617-4]. — Nota ergo, quod primo coronaverunt Christum, et statim, corona deposita, « percutiebant caput eius arundine[^p617-5] ». *Corona* ista significat *honorem* mundanum, qua quicumque coronatur, percutitur cor eius sollicitudine. — Similiter in illusione *vestis* nota, quod primo ei vestem circumdederunt, et postmodum non solum eam quam ei circumdederunt, sed suam propriam abstulerunt[^p617-6]. Vestis ista significat mundanas *divitias,* quas postquam mundus alicui circumdederit, non solum eas aufert in fine, sed etiam vestem propriam, scilicet vestem virtutum ei deponit, ut exeat nudus de mundo non solum veste propria, sed etiam veste mutuo accepta. — Circa *potus* oblationem nota, quod cum Dominus sitiret dulcedinem, obtulerunt ei fellis amaritudinem[^p617-7]; quod impletur spiritualiter in illusione, quae fit per carnalem *concupiscentiam.* Sitit enim homo carnalis dulcedinem voluptatis, et diabolus propinat ei amaritudinem mortis; unde Proverbiorum quinto dicitur de meretrice: « Novissima illius amara quasi absinthium ».

### Collatio LXIX. et tertia de passione Christi.

1. Circa Christi *flagellationem* nota, quod Christus fuit flagellatus in *capite;* unde, Matthaei vigesimo septimo[^p617-8], Marci decimo quinto, « percutiebant caput eius arundine ». Fuit etiam caesus in *facie;* unde Lucae vigesimo secundo: « Percutiebant faciem eius »; et Ioannis decimo octavo: « Unus ministrorum assistens dedit ei alapam ». Fuit etiam flagellatus in *toto corpore;* unde Matthaei vigesimo septimo et Marci decimo quinto: « Tradidit Iesum flagellis caesum, ut crucifigeretur ». — Nota igitur, quod tripliciter solet homo caedi sive flagellari. Aliquando caeditur homo ad *vituperandum,* sicut solet homo alium caedere de palma ad ignominiam. Aliquando caeditur sive flagellatur ad *eiiciendum,* sicut ostiarii solent intrare volentes flagellare sive caedere, ut ipsos eiiciant[^p617-9]. Aliquando flagellatur vel caeditur ad *puniendum,* sicut solent caedi malefactores.

2. Et haec tibi proponuntur in *speculum contemplationis,* ut attendas, quantum debeas illi qui pro te sustinuit omne genus flagellorum. Fuit enim flagellatus in *facie,* quae est flagellatio vituperantium; fuit flagellatus in *capite,* quae est flagellatio *eiicientium;* fuit etiam flagellatus in *toto corpore,* quae est flagellatio *punientium.*

3. Haec etiam tibi proponuntur in *remedium liberationis.* Merueras enim, o homo peccator, a summo iudice *confundi* in culparum discussione; *eiici* in sententiae promulgatione; *puniri* in sententiae exsecutione. Sed ecce, ab istis liberant te flagella Christi, qui pro te clamat in Psalmo[^p617-10]: « Ego in flagella paratus sum » etc. Flagellatur in *facie,* ne pro peccatis *confundaris;* flagellatur in *capite,* ne a regno *eiiciaris;* flagellatur in *toto corpore,* ne pro peccatis tuis in toto corpore *puniaris.*

4. Proponuntur tibi etiam flagella Christi in *exemplum imitationis,* ut, sive confundaris, sive eiiciaris, sive torquearis, accipias a Christo, qui primus haec sustinuit, exemplum[^p617-11] humilitatis.

### Collatio LXX. et quarta de passione Christi.

1. Circa Christi *crucifixionem* nota, quod in Christo crucifixo nobis proponitur exemplum *poenae sustinendae — concupiscentiae refrenandae —* et *culpae vitandae.* — Habes enim in Crucifixo exemplum *poenae sustinendae.* Solet enim aliquando timeri poena propter eius *acerbitatem,* aliquando propter
<!-- page 618 -->
eius *multiplicitatem,* aliquando propter eius *vilitatem,* aliquando propter eius *diuturnitatem.* — Sed ecce, Christus occisus est morte *amarissima,* ne formides *acerbitatem* poenae. Poena enim Christi fuit in nervorum extensione, in quibus maxime viget fortitudo, et ideo afflictio acerbissima; unde Threnorum primo[^p618-1]: « O vos omnes, qui transitis per viam, attendite et videte, si est dolor sicut dolor meus »! — Item occisus est poena *universalissima,* ne timeas *multiplicitatem* poenae. Afflictus enim fuit in qualibet corporis parte, et ideo clamat in Psalmo[^p618-2] ad Patrem: « Omnes fluctus tuos induxisti super me ». — Occisus etiam fuit morte *turpissima,* ne refugias *vilitatem* poenae. Mors enim crucis erat mors latronum, et ideo de morte eius dicitur Sapientiae secundo: « Morte turpissima condemnemus eum ». — Fuit etiam occisus morte *diutissima,* ne horreas *diuturnitatem* poenae; unde in Psalmo: « Fui flagellatus tota die » etc. — Habes ergo in Crucifixo exemplum poenae sustinendae.

2. Habes etiam in Christo exemplum *concupiscentiae refrenandae.* Laxatur enim aliquando concupiscentia ad *voluptates,* aliquando ad *divitias,* aliquando ad *honores.* — Sed ut refrenes concupiscentiam *honorum,* tibi proponitur Christus in cruce *abiectus,* scilicet inter latrones crucifixus; unde Matthaei vigesimo septimo[^p618-3]: « Tunc crucifixi sunt cum eo duo latrones, unus a dextris et alius a sinistris ». — Ut refrenes concupiscentiam *divitiarum,* proponitur tibi Christus in cruce *nudatus;* unde Ioannis decimo nono legitur, quod « crucifixores acceperunt vestimenta eius et fecerunt quatuor partes » etc. — Ut refrenes concupiscentiam *voluptatum,* tibi proponitur Christus in exemplum in cruce *afflictus* tanta afflictione, quod non est dolor sicut dolor eius, sicut dicitur Threnorum primo. — Sic ergo habes in Crucifixo exemplum poenae sustinendae, habes exemplum concupiscentiae refrenandae.

3. Habes nihilominus in eo exemplum *culpae vitandae.* Nota ergo contra *superbiam* capitis inclinationem; Ioannis decimo nono[^p618-4]: « Inclinato capite, emisit spiritum »; ecce, exemplum *humilitatis.* — Contra *iram,* pro inimicis orationem; Lucae vigesimo tertio: « Pater, dimitte illis, quia nesciunt, quid faciunt »; ecce exemplum *benignitatis.* — Contra *invidiam,* lateris apertionem; Ioannis decimo nono: « Unus militum lancea latus eius aperuit ». Voluit enim Christus, latus suum aperiri, ut ostenderet, quod paratus erat cor suum dare fidelibus suis; ecce, exemplum *caritatis.* — Contra *avaritiam,* manuum perforationem. Solet enim dici de homine largo, quod habet manus perforatas; Psalmus[^p618-5]: « Foderunt manus meas et pedes » etc.; ecce, exemplum *largitatis.* — Contra *accidiam,* devotam spiritus oblationem; Lucae vigesimo tertio: « In manus tuas commendo » etc.; ecce, exemplum *devotionis.* — Contra *gulam,* fellis potationem; Ioannis decimo nono: « Illi autem plenam aceto spongiam, hyssopo circumponentes, obtulerunt ori suo. Cum autem gustasset, noluit bibere »; ecce, exemplum *ieiunii.* — Contra *luxuriam,* sanguinis effusionem; ecce, exemplum *mortificationis carnis.*

### Annotatio 69.

(Cap. 19, 29.). *Illi autem plenam aceto spongiam obtulerunt ori eius.*

Nota, quod Christus fuit afflictus in quadruplici sensu: in *auditu,* audiendo blasphemias Iudaeorum; in *visu,* sustinendo velationem oculorum; in *tactu,* quando fuit clavis confixus et flagellatus; in *gustu,* quando fuit aceto potatus. — Hoc totum factum est, ut exsolveret delictum primi hominis, qui fuerat primus in sensu quadruplici male delectatus[^p618-6]. Primo enim peccavit secundum *auditum,* quando audivit suggestionem sibi beatitudinem promittentem. — Secundo peccavit secundum *visum,* quando aspexit fructum « pulcrum visu et ad vescendum suavem ». — Tertio peccavit secundum *tactum,* quando fructum collegit. — Quarto secundum *gustum,* quando fructum comedit. — Cuius delictum exsolvens Christus fuit blasphematus, oculis velatus, clavis confixus et felle potatus, et ideo, *cum accepisset acetum, dixit: Consummatum est*[^p618-7].

### Collatio LXXI.

(Vers. 41.). *Erat autem in loco, ubi crucifixus est* etc.

1. Hic agit de *sepultura* Christi. Circa quam tria sunt notanda. Christus enim mortuus fuit *conditus* unguento — *involutus* sudario — *reconditus* in sepulcro; quae tria tanguntur in littera ista, Ioannis decimo nono[^p618-8], ubi dicitur, quod « ligaverunt eum linteis cum aromatibus, sicut mos est Iudaeis sepelire ». — Per haec tria instruit nos Christus, quod homo spiritualis, qui vult « complantari similitudini mortis eius », secundum quod tangitur ad Romanos
<!-- page 619 -->
sexto[^p619-1], debet *condiri* unctione Spiritus sancti, debet *involvi* quasi in sudario habitu regulari; debet *recondi* quasi in sepulcro in monumento claustri, ut habeat, videlicet homo spiritualis, *claustrum* pro monumento, *habitum* pro sudario, Spiritus sancti *gratiam* pro unguento.

2. Et nota, quod Christus fuit *conditus duplici unguento,* unguento scilicet *nardi pistici, pretiosi* a Maria, secundum quod habetur Ioannis duodecimo[^p619-2]; de quo unguento dicitur Matthaei vigesimo sexto, quia « mittens unguentum hoc in corpus meum, ad sepeliendum me fecit »; et unguento *myrrhae et aloes* a Nicodemo; Ioannis decimo nono: « Venit Nicodemus, ferens mixturam myrrhae et aloes quasi libras centum ». — Per unguentum *myrrhae* intellige mortificationem carnis, per unguentum *nardi* devotionem mentis; quod est duplex unguentum, quo condiri debet homo spiritualis. Solent enim infirmi ungi ad sanitatem[^p619-3] et sponsae ad decorem; et homo spiritualis debet ungi *mortificatione carnis* ad sanitatem, *devotione mentis* ad decorem.

3. Nota iterum, quod Christus fuit *involutus* duobus. Fuit enim involutus *sindone;* Marci decimo quinto[^p619-4]: « Ioseph autem mercatus sindonem, deponens Iesum involvit in sindone »; et *linteis;* unde Ioannis decimo nono: « Ligaverunt eum linteis cum aromatibus ». — Per *sindonem* intellige munditiam conversationis, per *linteos aromatizantes* exempla bonae operationis, quae sunt duo, quibus decorari et indui debet homo spiritualis, ut habeat vestis eius *speciositatem* in conversatione, *pretiositatem* in bona operatione.

4. Nota tertio, quod in loco, ubi reconditus fuit Iesus, duo notat Evangelista, *hortum* videlicet et *monumentum.* De *horto* dicitur hic[^p619-5]: « Erat autem in loco, ubi crucifixus est, hortus ». De *monumento* statim subiungitur: *Et in horto monumentum.* — Per *hortum* intellige fertilitatem bonorum operum; per *monumentum,* quod dicitur quasi *monens mentem,* intellige studium Scripturarum; in quo instruimur, quod homo spiritualis debet sibi requirere habitaculum in loco, ubi inveniat *hortum* bonorum operum et *monumentum* Scripturarum, ut possit aliquando exire ad *hortum actionis,* aliquando sedere ad *monumentum contemplationis.*

## English

### Chapter XVIII and XIX. *Then therefore Pilate took Jesus*[^p615-5].

Concerning the passion of Christ, of which this chapter treats, four things are to be considered, namely *the delivering up, the mockery, the scourging* and *the crucifixion,* which four Jesus himself briefly noted when he was going up to the passion, Matthew, the twentieth chapter[^p615-6]: « They shall deliver him up to the Gentiles to be mocked, and scourged, and crucified ».

### Collation LXVII. And the first on the passion of Christ.

1. Concerning Christ's *delivering up,* note that Christ was unjustly delivered up by three: by *Judas;* John, the eighteenth chapter[^p615-7]: « And Judas also stood, who was delivering him up to the Jews »; by *the Jews;* John, the eighteenth chapter: « Thy own nation, and the pontiffs, have delivered thee up to me »; by *Pilate;* John, the nineteenth chapter: « Then therefore he delivered him up to them to be crucified ». — *Judas* delivered him up out of *cupidity;* Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p615-8]: « What will you give me, and I will deliver him up unto you ». *The Jews* out of *malignity and envy;* Mark, the fifteenth chapter: « For he knew that they had delivered him up out of envy ». *Pilate* out of *popular favour* or goodwill; Mark, the fifteenth chapter: « And Pilate, being willing to satisfy the people, released to them Barabbas, and delivered up to them Jesus, beaten with scourges, that they might crucify him ». — After whose example some today persecute Christ-the-truth out of *cupidity,* as simoniacs; some out of *malignity and envy,* as heretics; some out of *popular favour* or goodwill, as remiss preachers[^p615-9].

2. Note that the delivering up of Christ is set before us as a *mirror* of contemplation, as a *remedy* of deliverance, as an *example* of imitation and as a *lesson* of instruction. — It is set before us as a *mirror of contemplation,* that from the contemplation of it we may be kindled to dilection. For thrice was Christ delivered up: first by *Judas,* and in that delivering up he was *sold;* second by *the Jews,* and in that delivering up he was *condemned;* third by *Pilate,* and in that delivering up he was *crucified.* How then could he have spent himself more perfectly for us, than that he should be *sold* for us to be redeemed, *condemned* for us to be absolved, *crucified* for us to be set free? For all this was done in his delivering up; considering which, blessed Paul said, to the Galatians, the second chapter[^p615-10]: « I live, now not I; but Christ liveth in me. And that I live now in the flesh: I live in the faith of the Son of God, who loved me, and delivered up himself for me ».

3. The delivering up of Christ was for us as a *remedy of deliverance.* For thrice was Christ delivered up: first *to the enemy;* second *to the judge;* third *to the torturer. Judas* delivered him up to the enemies, that is, to the Jews; *the enemies* to the judge, that is, to Pilate; *Pilate* to the torturers, that is, to the soldiers,
<!-- page 616 -->
who crucified him. And all this was done, that man, delivered up, might be set free; who was delivered up first *to the enemy* in the committing of sin; second was delivered up *to the judge* in death; third was delivered up *to the torturers* in hell. That man therefore might be set free from this threefold delivering up, God-man willed to be delivered up, lest man should any longer lament that which is said in the Psalm[^p616-1]: « I was delivered up, and came not forth: my eyes languished through poverty ».

4. Third, the delivering up of Christ is set forth as an *example of imitation,* as the Lord had foretold that his disciples were to be delivered up, Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p616-2]: « They will deliver you up in councils and in their synagogues, and they will scourge you » etc. Lest therefore it should seem too hard to them to be delivered up in councils, the Lord sent before them the example of his own delivering up. For he was delivered up by a *friend,* namely by Judas; by an *enemy,* namely the people of the Jews; by a *stranger,* namely by Pilate, who did not know him: so that, whether the disciples were delivered up by *friends,* or by *enemies,* or by *strangers,* they might receive from Christ an example of humility. — Or distinguish otherwise, because Christ was delivered up by a *disciple,* by an *adversary* and by *Pilate,* so that, whether a Christian be delivered up by an inferior, or by an equal, or by a superior, he may find in Christ an example of enduring. Therefore Paul, considering the example of Christ, when Agabus prophesied, the Acts, the twenty-first chapter[^p616-3], saying: « The man whose girdle this is, the Jews shall bind in this manner in Jerusalem, and shall deliver up into the hands of the Gentiles »; answered: « I am ready not only to be bound, but to die also in Jerusalem, for the name of the Lord Jesus ».

5. The delivering up of Christ is also set before us as a *lesson of instruction,* that we may beware lest we be deliverers-up of the truth, whether out of *cupidity* with Judas, or out of *goodwill* with Pilate, or out of *envy* with the Jews, which was explained above in the first part of the collation[^p616-4].

### Collation LXVIII. And the second on the passion of Christ.

1. Concerning Christ's *mockery,* note that Christ was mocked thrice. For he was mocked in *the house of the pontiff;* Luke, the twenty-second chapter[^p616-5]: « And the men that held him, mocked him, beating him; and they blindfolded him, and struck his face, and they asked him, saying: Prophesy, who is it that struck thee »? — He was mocked in *the house of the judge;* Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter: « Then the soldiers, taking Jesus into the hall, and stripping him, put a scarlet cloak about him, and platting a crown of thorns, they put it upon his head, and a reed in his right hand, and bowing the knee they mocked him, saying: Hail, king of the Jews ». — Christ was also mocked on *the gibbet of the cross;* whence Mark, the fifteenth chapter[^p616-6]: « And they that passed by blasphemed him, wagging their heads, and saying: Vah, thou that destroyest the temple of God »; « in like manner also the chief priests mocking, said with the scribes one to another: He saved others; himself he cannot save ».

2. Attend therefore to the mockery of Christ, because it is set forth thrice: as a *mirror* of contemplation, as a *remedy* of deliverance, as an *example* of imitation, as a *lesson* of instruction. — It is set forth, then, first as a *mirror of contemplation,* that by seeing the contumely of the mockery we may recognise in him who endures it the greatness of charity. Attend therefore that for three kinds of men it is grievous to endure. For *the wise* man endures grievously to be mocked by one who knows less; *the powerful* endures more grievously to be mocked by one less powerful; *the benefactor* endures most grievously to be mocked by the needy man who receives his benefit. — All these things were done to Christ in his mockery. For in *the house* of *the pontiff* his *wisdom* was mocked by fools, when they veiled his face and asked[^p616-7]: « Prophesy: who is it that struck thee »? as though he knew nothing but what he saw with bodily eyes. — In *the house of the judge* his *power* was mocked by servants, when « platting a crown of thorns, they put it upon his head, and mocked him, saying: Hail, king of the Jews »; as though it were laughable that he should be a king. — On *the gibbet of the cross* his *mercy* was mocked by the very wretched, saying[^p616-8]: « He saved others; himself he cannot save »; as though it were laughable that he should redeem the human race. — Contemplate therefore, faithful soul, the gravity of the mockery, and render the return of dilection to him who for thee was made « the reproach of men, and the outcast of the people »; of whom the Lamentations, the third chapter: « He shall give his cheek to him that striketh him, he shall be filled with reproaches ».

3. The mockery of Christ is set forth as a *remedy of deliverance.* Note therefore that Christ willed to be mocked, that man might be spared from the mockery *of sin,* from the *penal* mockery. For the sinner is mocked first by *the seducer,* when he sins; second he is mocked, as a man to be condemned is sometimes wont to be mocked, by *the one who leads him away* or apprehends him, by *the judge* and *the torturer.* That man to be condemned therefore might be set free from mockeries of this kind, Christ willed in man's stead to be mocked, when he was *apprehended* in the house of the pontiff, and when he was *condemned* in the praetorium of the judge, and when he was *slain* on the gibbet of the cross, lest that mockery should befall man of which it is said, Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p616-9]: « To him that deviseth a most wicked counsel, it shall be rolled back upon himself, and he shall not know whence it cometh to him. Mockery and reproach upon the good » etc.

4. The mockery of Christ is also set forth as an *example of imitation.* For not only is a man to be condemned mocked, but
<!-- page 617 -->
sometimes the malice of the mocker mocks the innocent; mockery of which kind Scripture condemns, Proverbs, the third chapter[^p617-1]: « He himself derideth the mockers ». And therefore the Lord, knowing that his own were to be mocked, that they might endure patiently, sends before them the example of his own mockery.

5. Note that Christ was mocked by *signs, words* and *deeds: by signs,* as when they adored him and crowned him; *by deeds,* as when they veiled him and spat upon him; *by words,* as when they saluted him and blasphemed him[^p617-2]. And all these things Christ willed to be done to him, that, whether one be mocked by his own with words, or with signs, or with deeds, he might find in Christ an example of humility.

6. The mockery of Christ is also set forth as a *lesson of instruction;* for one is not only mocked *contumeliously,* but sometimes also *seditiously;* and as the Lord wills that we endure contumelious mockery, so he wills that we guard against the seditious. — Note therefore that the devil mocks man *to seduce* him, according to what is had, Proverbs, the fourteenth chapter[^p617-3]: « Sin mocketh the fool ». The devil sometimes mocks man through the concupiscence *of honours,* sometimes through the concupiscence *of riches,* sometimes through the concupiscence *of pleasures;* whence the Psalm: « My loins are filled with mockeries ».

7. The first mockery is noted in Christ's *crowning;* the second is noted in *the purple garment,* which they put about him; the third is noted in *the drink,* which they held out to him on the cross[^p617-4]. — Note therefore that first they crowned Christ, and at once, the crown being laid down, « they struck his head with a reed[^p617-5] ». *That crown* signifies worldly *honour,* with which whoever is crowned, his heart is struck with solicitude. — Likewise in the mockery *of the garment* note that first they put a garment about him, and afterwards they took away not only that which they had put about him, but his own[^p617-6]. That garment signifies worldly *riches,* which, after the world has put them about someone, it not only takes away in the end, but also strips him of his own garment, namely the garment of the virtues, so that he goes out of the world naked, not only of his own garment, but also of the garment received on loan. — Concerning the offering of *the drink* note that when the Lord thirsted for sweetness, they offered him the bitterness of gall[^p617-7]; which is fulfilled spiritually in the mockery which is made through carnal *concupiscence.* For the carnal man thirsts for the sweetness of pleasure, and the devil pledges him the bitterness of death; whence it is said, Proverbs, the fifth chapter, of the harlot: « Her latter end is bitter as wormwood ».

### Collation LXIX. And the third on the passion of Christ.

1. Concerning Christ's *scourging* note that Christ was scourged on *the head;* whence, Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p617-8], Mark, the fifteenth chapter, « they struck his head with a reed ». He was also beaten on *the face;* whence Luke, the twenty-second chapter: « They struck his face »; and John, the eighteenth chapter: « One of the ministers standing by gave him a blow ». He was also scourged in *the whole body;* whence Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter, and Mark, the fifteenth chapter: « He delivered up Jesus, beaten with scourges, to be crucified ». — Note therefore that a man is wont to be beaten or scourged in three ways. Sometimes a man is beaten *to be reviled,* as a man is wont to strike another with the palm, for ignominy. Sometimes he is beaten or scourged *to be cast out,* as doorkeepers are wont to scourge or beat those who wish to enter, that they may cast them out[^p617-9]. Sometimes he is scourged or beaten *to be punished,* as evildoers are wont to be beaten.

2. And these things are set before thee as a *mirror of contemplation,* that thou mayest attend how much thou owest to him who for thee endured every kind of scourges. For he was scourged on *the face,* which is the scourging of those who revile; he was scourged on *the head,* which is the scourging *of those who cast out;* he was also scourged in *the whole body,* which is the scourging *of those who punish.*

3. These things are also set before thee as a *remedy of deliverance.* For thou hadst deserved, O sinful man, to be *confounded* by the supreme judge in the examination of faults; *to be cast out* in the promulgation of the sentence; *to be punished* in the execution of the sentence. But behold, from these the scourges of Christ set thee free, who cries out for thee in the Psalm[^p617-10]: « For I am ready for scourges » etc. He is scourged on *the face,* lest thou be *confounded* for thy sins; he is scourged on *the head,* lest thou be *cast out* from the kingdom; he is scourged in *the whole body,* lest for thy sins thou be *punished* in thy whole body.

4. The scourges of Christ are also set before thee as an *example of imitation,* that, whether thou be confounded, or cast out, or tormented, thou mayest receive from Christ, who first endured these things, an example[^p617-11] of humility.

### Collation LXX. And the fourth on the passion of Christ.

1. Concerning Christ's *crucifixion* note that in Christ crucified there is set before us an example *of punishment to be endured — of concupiscence to be bridled —* and *of fault to be avoided.* — For thou hast in the Crucified an example *of punishment to be endured.* For punishment is wont to be feared sometimes on account of its *bitterness,* sometimes on account of
<!-- page 618 -->
its *multiplicity,* sometimes on account of its *vileness,* sometimes on account of its *long duration.* — But behold, Christ was slain by a *most bitter* death, that thou mayest not dread the *bitterness* of punishment. For the punishment of Christ was in the stretching of the sinews, in which strength is most vigorous, and therefore the affliction was most bitter; whence the Lamentations, the first chapter[^p618-1]: « O all ye that pass by the way, attend, and see if there be any sorrow like to my sorrow »! — Likewise he was slain by a *most universal* punishment, that thou mayest not fear the *multiplicity* of punishment. For he was afflicted in every part of the body, and therefore he cries out in the Psalm[^p618-2] to the Father: « All thy waves thou hast brought in upon me ». — He was also slain by a *most shameful* death, that thou mayest not shrink from the *vileness* of punishment. For the death of the cross was the death of thieves, and therefore of his death it is said, Wisdom, the second chapter: « Let us condemn him to a most shameful death ». — He was also slain by a *most lingering* death, that thou mayest not shudder at the *long duration* of punishment; whence in the Psalm: « I have been scourged all the day » etc. — Thou hast therefore in the Crucified an example of punishment to be endured.

2. Thou hast also in Christ an example *of concupiscence to be bridled.* For concupiscence is sometimes let loose to *pleasures,* sometimes to *riches,* sometimes to *honours.* — But that thou mayest bridle the concupiscence *of honours,* Christ is set before thee on the cross *cast down,* namely crucified among thieves; whence Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p618-3]: « Then were crucified with him two thieves: one on the right hand, and the other on the left ». — That thou mayest bridle the concupiscence *of riches,* Christ is set before thee on the cross *stripped;* whence it is read, John, the nineteenth chapter, that « the crucifiers took his garments, and they made four parts » etc. — That thou mayest bridle the concupiscence *of pleasures,* Christ is set before thee as an example on the cross, *afflicted* with so great an affliction that there is no sorrow like his sorrow, as is said, the Lamentations, the first chapter. — Thus therefore thou hast in the Crucified an example of punishment to be endured, thou hast an example of concupiscence to be bridled.

3. Thou hast nonetheless in him an example *of fault to be avoided.* Note therefore, against *pride,* the bowing of the head; John, the nineteenth chapter[^p618-4]: « Bowing his head, he gave up the ghost »; behold, an example *of humility.* — Against *anger,* the prayer for enemies; Luke, the twenty-third chapter: « Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do »; behold an example *of benignity.* — Against *envy,* the opening of the side; John, the nineteenth chapter: « One of the soldiers with a spear opened his side ». For Christ willed his side to be opened, to show that he was ready to give his heart to his faithful; behold, an example *of charity.* — Against *avarice,* the piercing of the hands. For it is wont to be said of a generous man that he has pierced hands; the Psalm[^p618-5]: « They have dug my hands and feet » etc.; behold, an example *of largess.* — Against *sloth,* the devout offering of the spirit; Luke, the twenty-third chapter: « Into thy hands I commend » etc.; behold, an example *of devotion.* — Against *gluttony,* the drinking of gall; John, the nineteenth chapter: « But they, putting a sponge full of vinegar about hyssop, offered it to his mouth. And when he had tasted, he would not drink »; behold, an example *of fasting.* — Against *lust,* the shedding of blood; behold, an example *of the mortification of the flesh.*

### Annotation 69.

(Cap. 19, 29.). *But they offered to his mouth a sponge full of vinegar.*

Note that Christ was afflicted in a fourfold sense: in *hearing,* by hearing the blasphemies of the Jews; in *sight,* by enduring the veiling of the eyes; in *touch,* when he was fastened with nails and scourged; in *taste,* when he was given vinegar to drink. — All this was done that he might pay off the offence of the first man, who had been the first to take evil delight in the fourfold sense[^p618-6]. For first he sinned according to *hearing,* when he heard the suggestion promising him beatitude. — Second he sinned according to *sight,* when he looked upon the fruit « fair to the sight and sweet to eat ». — Third he sinned according to *touch,* when he gathered the fruit. — Fourth according to *taste,* when he ate the fruit. — Paying off his offence, Christ was blasphemed, veiled in the eyes, fastened with nails and given gall to drink, and therefore, *when he had taken the vinegar, he said: It is consummated*[^p618-7].

### Collation LXXI.

(Vers. 41.). *Now there was in the place where he was crucified* etc.

1. Here he treats of the *burial* of Christ. Concerning which three things are to be noted. For Christ when dead was *embalmed* with ointment — *wrapped* in a napkin — *laid away* in a sepulchre; which three things are touched upon in this letter, John, the nineteenth chapter[^p618-8], where it is said that « they bound him in linen cloths, with the spices, as the manner of the Jews is to bury ». — By these three things Christ instructs us that the spiritual man, who wills « to be planted together in the likeness of his death », according to what is touched upon, to the Romans,
<!-- page 619 -->
the sixth chapter[^p619-1], ought to be *embalmed* with the unction of the Holy Spirit, ought to be *wrapped* as in a napkin in the religious habit; ought to be *laid away* as in a sepulchre in the monument of the cloister, so that, namely, the spiritual man may have *the cloister* for a monument, *the habit* for a napkin, the *grace* of the Holy Spirit for ointment.

2. And note that Christ was *embalmed with a twofold ointment,* namely with the ointment *of pistic nard, precious,* by Mary, according to what is had, John, the twelfth chapter[^p619-2]; of which ointment it is said, Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter, that « in pouring this ointment upon my body, she hath done it for my burial »; and with the ointment *of myrrh and aloes* by Nicodemus; John, the nineteenth chapter: « Nicodemus came, bringing a mixture of myrrh and aloes, about an hundred pound weight ». — By the ointment *of myrrh* understand the mortification of the flesh, by the ointment *of nard* the devotion of the mind; which is the twofold ointment with which the spiritual man ought to be embalmed. For the sick are wont to be anointed for health[^p619-3] and brides for comeliness; and the spiritual man ought to be anointed *with the mortification of the flesh* for health, *with the devotion of the mind* for comeliness.

3. Note again that Christ was *wrapped* in two things. For he was wrapped in *fine linen;* Mark, the fifteenth chapter[^p619-4]: « And Joseph buying fine linen, and taking Jesus down, wrapped him up in the fine linen »; and in *linen cloths;* whence John, the nineteenth chapter: « They bound him in linen cloths, with the spices ». — By *the fine linen* understand the cleanness of conversation, by *the spice-scented linen cloths* the examples of good work, which are the two things with which the spiritual man ought to be adorned and clothed, so that his garment may have *comeliness* in conversation, *preciousness* in good work.

4. Note third that in the place where Jesus was laid away the Evangelist notes two things, namely *a garden* and *a monument.* Of *the garden* it is said here[^p619-5]: « Now there was in the place where he was crucified, a garden ». Of *the monument* it is added at once: *And in the garden a monument.* — By *the garden* understand the fruitfulness of good works; by *the monument,* which is so called as it were *monens mentem* (admonishing the mind), understand the study of the Scriptures; in which we are instructed that the spiritual man ought to seek for himself a dwelling in the place where he may find *the garden* of good works and *the monument* of the Scriptures, so that he may sometimes go out to *the garden of action,* sometimes sit at *the monument of contemplation.*
