# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. V, Vers. 1-9
## *Primo, Filius Dei se manifestat curatorem*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 303 -->

### Capitulum V.

#### Secundo, de manifestatione Verbi quoad conditiones a parte ipsius Verbi manifestati.

1. *Post haec erat* etc. Terminata est illa pars principalis, in qua agitur de manifestatione Verbi incarnati, secundum quod respicit conditiones a parte eorum quibus fit manifestatio; hinc incipit secunda pars principalis, in qua agitur de manifestatione Verbi incarnati, secundum quod respicit conditiones *a parte ipsius Verbi manifestati*[^p303-7]. Ostendit enim, quod ipse Dei Filius, qui est Patris Verbum, manifestat se *curatorem, conservatorem, directorem* et *vivificatorem.*

Primo igitur ostendit se *curatorem* in praesenti capitulo. Secundo, *conservatorem* in sexto capitulo, ibi[^p303-8]: *Post haec abiit* etc. Tertio, *directorem* a principio septimi usque ad undecimum, et incipit ibi: *Post haec ambulabat Iesus in Galilaeam* etc. Quarto vero, perfectum *reparatorem* in Lazari resuscitatione, in principio undecimi, ibi: *Erat autem quidam languens Lazarus.*

#### Primo, Filius Dei se manifestat curatorem.

2. Praesens igitur capitulum, in quo manifestat se *curatorem* per mirabilem paralytici liberationem, habet quatuor partes secundum quatuor, quae determinantur ibi. In prima enim tangitur *miraculosa curatio.* In secunda, *curationis calumniatio,* ibi[^p303-9]: *Erat autem Sabbatum.* In tertia, *calumniationis confutatio,* ibi: *Respondit itaque Iesus et dixit eis.* In quarta, *confutationis corroboratio,* et hoc per multa testimonia, ibi: *Si ego testimonium perhibeo* etc.

Mirabilis igitur curatio secundum litteram describitur a quatuor: primo, a *congruentia temporis;* secundo, a *dispositione loci;* tertio, a *necessitate languentis;* quarto, a *potestate curantis.*

3. (Vers. 1.). Primo igitur describitur a *congruentia temporis,* quia tempus festivum erat, in quo beneficia Dominus consuevit impendere. Propterea dicit: *Post haec,* scilicet quae supra narrata sunt, *erat dies festus Iudaeorum.* Chrysostomus[^p303-10] dicit, quod iste dies festus erat Pentecostes, qui erat medius inter Pascha et Scenopegiam, id est solemnitatem tabernaculorum. *Et ascendit Iesus Ierosolymam,* sicut habebant in consuetudine omnes Iudaei; Lucae secundo[^p303-11]: « Ascenderunt Ierosolymam secundum consuetudinem diei festi ».

4. (Vers. 2.). *Est autem Ierosolymis.* Hic secundo tangitur *dispositio loci,* ubi facta est curatio, quia in loco curationis, qui erat piscina probatica; propter quod dicit: *Est autem Ierosolymis probatica piscina,* id est pecualis, quia ibi lavabantur hostiae
<!-- page 304 -->
pecudum; et erat haec piscina iuxta templum et ab hoc officio denominationem acceperat. Ideo dicit: *Quae cognominatur Hebraice Bethsaida,* id est domus pecudum, quia hoc habebat officium[^p304-1]. Habebat etiam ornatum; unde dicit: *Quinque porticus habens.* Et quare hos habebat, ratio redditur.

5. (Vers. 3.). *In his iacebat multitudo magna languentium,* et per naturam incurabilium; unde dicit: *Caecorum, claudorum, aridorum, exspectantium aquae motum,* ut scilicet sanarentur per divinae gratiae beneficium, quod impendebatur in Angeli descensu. Ideo dicit:

6. (Vers. 4.). *Angelus autem Domini secundum tempus descendebat in piscinam,* ad sanandum scilicet; et huius descensus subdit *signum: Et movebatur aqua,* ut aquam viderent qui Angelum videre non poterant; et divinum subdit *effectum: Et quicumque prior descendebat*[^p304-2] *in piscinam post motionem aquae,* tanquam sollicitior, *sanus fiebat a quacumque detinebatur infirmitate,* sine distinctione, quia, sicut dicitur Actuum decimo, « non est personarum acceptor Deus » etc.

7. (Vers. 5.). *Erat autem quidam homo* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *languentis necessitas,* quia diu *infirmus* fuerat et omni *auxilio destitutus.* Diu *infirmus* fuerat, unde dicit: *Erat autem homo quidam ibi; quidam,* qui[^p304-3] in miseria singularis, *triginta octo annos habens in infirmitate sua.* Ecce, infirmitatis longitudo, qua Dominus motus est misereri et quaerere et alloqui eum. Unde dicit:

8. (Vers. 6.). *Hunc cum vidisset Iesus iacentem,* infirmitate opprimente, *et cognovisset, quia iam multum temporis*[^p304-4] *haberet,* in poenalitate miseriae; *dicit ei,* per benignitatem misericordiae: *Vis sanus fieri?* per virtutem divinae potentiae. Non hoc quaerebat, quia de voluntate dubitaret, sed ut eius indigentiam manifestaret. Quod et fecit sua responsione; ideo subdit:

9. (Vers. 7.). *Respondit ei languidus: Domine, hominem non habeo, ut, cum turbata fuerit aqua, mittat me in piscinam;* et ita pauper sum, et non est qui adiuvet; Psalmus[^p304-5]: « Tribulatio proxima est, et non est qui adiuvet », et ego ex me sum impotens. Unde subdit: *Dum venio ego, alius ante me descendit;* et ita sanari non possum, quia non sanatur nisi unus et ille qui prior descendit. Et sic innuitur languentis necessitas.

10. (Vers. 8.). *Dicit ei Iesus.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *curantis potestas,* quia solo praecepto sanat. Ideo dicit: *Surge, tolle grabatum tuum et ambula; surge,* per valetudinem; *tolle grabatum tuum,* per fortitudinem; *et ambula,* per sanitatis certitudinem. Quod et factum est; unde et subdit:

11. (Vers. 9.). *Et statim sanus factus est homo*[^p304-6]; ecce, valetudo; *et sustulit grabatum suum;* ecce, fortitudo; *et ambulabat;* ecce, sanitatis certitudo. Et hoc totum factum est Christi verbo; Psalmus: « Ipse dixit, et facta sunt »; unde Esther decimo tertio dixit Mardochaeus: « Domine, Rex omnipotens, in ditione tua cuncta sunt posita » etc.

12. *Moraliter.* Notandum est hic, quae sit ista *piscina quinque habens porticus,* qui sunt *infirmi,* et *quomodo curantur.* — *Piscina,* quae ex aquis pluvialibus congregatur, poenitentia est, in qua est effusio lacrymarum pro amissione supernorum. De hac pluvia Ieremiae quinto[^p304-7]: *Non dixerunt: Metuamus Deum nostrum, qui dat nobis pluviam temporaneam,* id est compunctionem pro malis commissis, *et serotinam,* pro bonis omissis. Haec pluvia descendit ad valles, non est in montibus et superbis; secundi Regum primo: *Montes Gelboe, nec ros nec pluvia veniant super vos.* — Haec *piscina* poenitentiae *habet quinque porticus,* in quibus requiescit *multitudo languentium*[^p304-8]; quia quinque sunt considerationes, quae debent facere hominem in poenitentia permanere.

13. Prima consideratio, *reatus peccati,* quo se obligavit ad poenam; Psalmus[^p304-9]: *Quoniam ego in flagella paratus sum, et dolor meus in conspectu meo semper.* — Secunda consideratio, *severitatis iudicii;* Ecclesiastici secundo[^p304-10]: *Si poenitentiam non egerimus, incidemus in manus Domini;*
<!-- page 305 -->
ad Hebraeos decimo: *Horrendum est incidere in manus Dei viventis.* — Tertia consideratio, *opportunitatis loci,* quia post non erit locus poenitentiae; Ecclesiastae nono[^p305-1]: *Quodcumque potest manus tua, instanter operare, quia nec opus nec ratio nec sapientia nec scientia erunt apud inferos, quo tu properas.* — Quarta est consideratio *incertitudinis diei extremi;* Ecclesiastae nono[^p305-2]: *Nescit homo finem suum; sed sicut pisces capiuntur hamo, et sicut aves laqueo comprehenduntur; sic capiuntur homines in tempore malo, cum eis extemplo supervenerit.* — Quinta est consideratio *incertitudinis status sui,* quia nescit, utrum aliquid fecerit Deo placitum; ideo dicebat in Psalmo[^p305-3]: *Et dixi: Nunc coepi;* Ecclesiastici decimo: *Omnis potentatus brevis vita.*

14. Infirmi sunt: *caeci* per ignorantiam peccantes; Matthaei decimo quinto[^p305-4]: *Caeci sunt et duces caecorum. Caecus autem si caeco ducatum praestet, ambo in foveam cadunt.* — Sunt *claudi,* qui peccant per impotentiam; ad Hebraeos duodecimo[^p305-5]: *Gressus rectos facite pedibus vestris, ut non claudicetis.* — Sunt *aridi,* qui peccant per malitiam vel duritiam; Ecclesiastici tertio[^p305-6]: *Cor durum male habebit in novissimo;* Ecclesiastici trigesimo septimo: *O praesumtio nequissima! unde creata es cooperire aridam malitia et dolositate illius?*

Hi *sanantur* in *adventu Angeli,* id est visitationis divinae; Iob trigesimo tertio[^p305-7]: *Increpat per dolorem in lectulo* etc. — *Post commotionem aquae,* id est lacrymationis internae; Threnorum secundo[^p305-8]: *Effunde sicut aquam cor tuum ante conspectum Domini.* — *Per descensum* in piscinam aquae, id est humilitatem poenitentiae; Isaiae quadragesimo septimo[^p305-9]: *Descende, sede in pulvere, virgo filia Babylonis, sede in terra; non est solium filiae Chaldaeorum.*

### Quaestiones.

15. Quaest. I. Quaeritur hic de virtute istius piscinae:

1. Unde hanc virtutem habebat, et qua ratione illi piscinae sic continue collata erat?

2. Item, cum tanta esset virtus, unde est hoc, quod Scriptura de eo[^p305-10] mentionem non facit? Videtur, quod negligentia scriptorum fuerit. — *Si dicas,* quod apparuit haec virtus prope Christi passionem; quaeritur tunc: quare prope passionem apparuit, et quare adveniente passione cessavit?

Respondent quidam, quod in piscina illa erat lignum crucis, quod tunc temporis coepit apparere et supernatare; ideo ad nobilitandum suae passionis trophaeum voluit Dominus miraculum illud ostendere, et *prope* passionem, quia tunc coepit natare; et non *post* passionem, quia lignum inde extractum fuit. — Sed quia hoc de Scripturis auctoritatem non habet, eadem ratione, qua probatur, destruitur[^p305-11].

Propter hoc dicunt alii, quod in aqua illa nulla erat virtus, sed Angelus descendens secundum certum tempus hoc faciebat in aqua per virtutem sibi a Domino datam. — Dicendum igitur, quod haec virtus data erat Angelo in aqua ad figuram baptismi, in quo fit ablutio per aquam et sanatio per Spiritum[^p305-12]. Et ideo prope baptismum praemissa fuit, et adveniente baptismate, cessavit. Ideo, quia parvo tempore duravit et tarde incepit, Scriptura de hoc mentionem non fecit. — Et sic patet responsio ad omnia obiecta.

16. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur: 1. Cum aeque facile esset Domino curare omnes, sicut unum, et ipse sit largissimus; quare non dabat aquae illi effectum curandi omnes?

2. Item, de ipso Domino quaeritur similiter: quare tantum unum curavit, cum aeque facile omnes, sicut unum, curare posset?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod illa sanatio corporalis magis erat in signum quam in beneficium illius cuius erat; ordinabatur enim ad animae curationem. Quia ergo ordinabatur ad eam designandam et praefigurandam, debuit eo modo fieri, quo optime significaret. Et quia sanatio in Ecclesia non est nisi in unitate fidei et caritatis, ideo tantum unus curabatur[^p305-13].

<!-- page 306 -->
Quod ergo obiicitur, non valet, quia non tantum erat in beneficium, sed etiam in signum[^p306-1].

17. Quaest. III. Sed tunc ulterior est quaestio: Si misericordiae est misereri miseriae[^p306-2], ergo ubi maior miseria, magis debet inclinari misericordia: non ergo debebat sanari qui prior descendebat, sed qui magis indigebat.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod, sicut dictum est[^p306-3], signum erat curationis animae; et quia anima curatur a Deo secundum suam dispositionem ad suscipiendum gratiam, non secundum gravitatem in culpa; ideo qui prius et sollicitius descendebat prius sanitatem obtinebat.

18. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur: cum Dominus paralytico Matthaei nono[^p306-4] suadeat confidentiam et ab aliis, quos sanavit, fidei confessionem exposcat; unde est hoc, quod ab isto nihil omnino quaerit?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum quod intelligimus: cum Dominus aliquem curavit, non solum in corpore, sed etiam in anima, totum scilicet, sanum reliquit. — Propter hoc notandum, quod quidam erant, qui ex culpa in aegritudinem inciderant; et quia hi sanari debebant in anima, et anima non sanatur[^p306-5] nisi per fidem Christi; a talibus Dominus confessionem fidei requirebat. — Alii erant, qui ad gloriam Dei manifestandam infirmi erant, ut caecus natus, infra nono[^p306-6], et Lazarus mortuus, infra undecimo; et a talibus Dominus confessionem fidei non requirebat, immo mera liberalitate sanabat; et de talium curatione mirabili agit Ioannes. Quia igitur iste talis fuit, ideo fidem non requisivit.

Sed quia haec solutio est contra Glossam[^p306-7], ideo dicendum, quod solum requirit fidem ab his qui quaerunt salutem.

## English

### Chapter V.

#### Second, on the manifestation of the Word as regards the conditions on the part of the Word himself manifested.

1. *After these things was* etc. That principal part is ended in which there is treated the manifestation of the incarnate Word, according as it regards the conditions on the part of those to whom the manifestation is made; hence begins the second principal part, in which there is treated the manifestation of the incarnate Word, according as it regards the conditions *on the part of the Word himself manifested*[^p303-7]. For it shows that the Son of God himself, who is the Word of the Father, manifests himself as *curer, preserver, director* and *vivifier.*

First, then, he shows himself as *curer* in the present chapter. Second, as *preserver* in the sixth chapter, at the place[^p303-8]: *After these things Jesus went* etc. Third, as *director* from the beginning of the seventh as far as the eleventh, and it begins at the place: *After these things Jesus walked in Galilee* etc. But fourth, as perfect *restorer* in the raising of Lazarus, at the beginning of the eleventh, at the place: *Now there was a certain sick man, Lazarus.*

#### First, the Son of God manifests himself as curer.

2. The present chapter, then, in which he manifests himself as *curer* through the wonderful deliverance of the paralytic, has four parts according to four things which are determined there. For in the first there is touched on *the miraculous cure.* In the second, *the calumniation of the cure,* at the place[^p303-9]: *And it was the Sabbath.* In the third, *the confutation of the calumniation,* at the place: *Then Jesus answered and said to them.* In the fourth, *the corroboration of the confutation,* and this through many testimonies, at the place: *If I give testimony* etc.

The wonderful cure, then, according to the letter is described from four things: first, from *the fittingness of the time;* second, from *the disposition of the place;* third, from *the necessity of the sick man;* fourth, from *the power of the one curing.*

3. (Verse 1.). First, then, it is described from *the fittingness of the time,* because it was a festive time, in which the Lord was accustomed to bestow benefits. On account of this he says: *After these things,* namely those which are narrated above, *was a festival day of the Jews.* Chrysostom[^p303-10] says that this festival day was Pentecost, which was midway between the Pasch and the Scenopegia, that is, the solemnity of tabernacles. *And Jesus went up to Jerusalem,* as all the Jews had it in custom; Luke, the second chapter[^p303-11]: « They went up to Jerusalem according to the custom of the feast ».

4. (Verse 2.). *Now there is at Jerusalem.* Here, second, there is touched on *the disposition of the place* where the cure was made, because in the place of the cure, which was the probatic pond; on account of which he says: *Now there is at Jerusalem a probatic pond,* that is, a pond for cattle, because there were washed the victims
<!-- page 304 -->
of the cattle; and this pond was near the temple and had received its name from this office. Therefore he says: *Which in Hebrew is named Bethsaida,* that is, house of cattle, because it had this office[^p304-1]. It had also adornment; whence he says: *Having five porches.* And why it had these, the reason is given.

5. (Verse 3.). *In these lay a great multitude of sick,* and incurable by nature; whence he says: *Of blind, of lame, of withered, waiting for the moving of the water,* that is, that they might be healed through the benefit of divine grace, which was bestowed in the descent of the Angel. Therefore he says:

6. (Verse 4.). *And an Angel of the Lord descended at certain times into the pond,* namely for healing; and of this descent he adds *the sign: And the water was moved,* that those might see the water who could not see the Angel; and he adds the divine *effect: And whosoever went down first*[^p304-2] *into the pond after the motion of the water,* as being more solicitous, *was made whole of whatsoever infirmity he lay under,* without distinction, because, as is said in Acts, the tenth chapter, « God is not a respecter of persons » etc.

7. (Verse 5.). *And there was a certain man* etc. Here there is touched on the third, namely *the necessity of the sick man,* because he had long been *infirm* and destitute of all *help.* He had long been *infirm,* whence he says: *And there was a certain man there; a certain,* who[^p304-3] was singular in misery, *having been eight and thirty years under his infirmity.* Behold the length of the infirmity, by which the Lord was moved to have mercy and to seek and to speak to him. Whence he says:

8. (Verse 6.). *Him when Jesus had seen lying,* his infirmity pressing upon him, *and knew that he had now been much time*[^p304-4], in the penalty of misery; *he saith to him,* through the kindness of mercy: *Wilt thou be made whole?* through the virtue of divine power. He did not ask this because he doubted of his will, but that he might make manifest his need. Which he also did by his answer; therefore he adds:

9. (Verse 7.). *The infirm man answered him: Sir, I have no man, when the water is troubled, to put me into the pond;* and so I am poor, and there is none to help; the Psalm[^p304-5]: « Tribulation is near, and there is none to help », and I of myself am powerless. Whence he adds: *Whilst I am coming, another goeth down before me;* and so I cannot be healed, because none is healed except one, and he who goes down first. And so the necessity of the sick man is intimated.

10. (Verse 8.). *Jesus saith to him.* Here there is touched on the fourth, namely *the power of the one curing,* because he heals by a precept alone. Therefore he says: *Arise, take up thy bed, and walk; arise,* by soundness; *take up thy bed,* by strength; *and walk,* by the certitude of health. Which was also done; whence he also adds:

11. (Verse 9.). *And immediately the man was made whole*[^p304-6]; behold soundness; *and he took up his bed;* behold strength; *and walked;* behold the certitude of health. And all this was done by the word of Christ; the Psalm: « He spoke, and they were made »; whence in Esther, the thirteenth chapter, Mardochai said: « Lord, almighty King, in thy dominion all things are placed » etc.

12. *Morally.* It is to be noted here what this *pond having five porches* is, who are *the infirm,* and *how they are cured.* — The *pond,* which is gathered from rain waters, is penance, in which there is the pouring out of tears for the loss of heavenly things. Of this rain Jeremias, the fifth chapter[^p304-7]: *They have not said: Let us fear our God, who giveth us the early rain,* that is, compunction for evils committed, *and the latter,* for goods omitted. This rain comes down to the valleys, it is not on the mountains and the proud; second Kings, the first chapter: *Ye mountains of Gelboe, let neither dew nor rain come upon you.* — This *pond* of penance *has five porches,* in which *the multitude of the sick* rests[^p304-8]; because there are five considerations which ought to make a man persevere in penance.

13. The first consideration, *the guilt of sin,* by which he has bound himself to punishment; the Psalm[^p304-9]: *For I am ready for scourges, and my sorrow is in my sight always.* — The second consideration, *of the severity of the judgement;* Ecclesiasticus, the second chapter[^p304-10]: *If we do not penance, we shall fall into the hands of the Lord;*
<!-- page 305 -->
to the Hebrews, the tenth chapter: *It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God.* — The third consideration, *of the opportunity of the place,* because afterwards there will be no place of penance; Ecclesiastes, the ninth chapter[^p305-1]: *Whatsoever thy hand is able to do, do it earnestly; for neither work nor reason nor wisdom nor knowledge shall be in hell, whither thou art hastening.* — The fourth is the consideration *of the uncertainty of the last day;* Ecclesiastes, the ninth chapter[^p305-2]: *Man knoweth not his own end; but as fishes are taken with the hook, and as birds are caught with the snare; so men are taken in the evil time, when it shall suddenly come upon them.* — The fifth is the consideration *of the uncertainty of his own state,* because he knows not whether he has done anything pleasing to God; therefore he said in the Psalm[^p305-3]: *And I said: Now have I begun;* Ecclesiasticus, the tenth chapter: *All power is of short life.*

14. The infirm are: the *blind,* sinning through ignorance; Matthew, the fifteenth chapter[^p305-4]: *They are blind, and leaders of the blind. And if the blind offer leading to the blind, both fall into the pit.* — There are the *lame,* who sin through powerlessness; to the Hebrews, the twelfth chapter[^p305-5]: *Make straight steps for your feet, that you halt not.* — There are the *withered,* who sin through malice or hardness; Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter[^p305-6]: *A hard heart shall fare evil at the last;* Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-seventh chapter: *O most wicked presumption! whence wast thou created to cover the dry land with malice and the deceitfulness thereof?*

These *are healed* at *the coming of the Angel,* that is, of the divine visitation; Job, the thirty-third chapter[^p305-7]: *He rebuketh by sorrow in the bed* etc. — *After the stirring of the water,* that is, of inward weeping; Lamentations, the second chapter[^p305-8]: *Pour out thy heart as water before the sight of the Lord.* — *By the going down* into the pond of water, that is, the humility of penance; Isaias, the forty-seventh chapter[^p305-9]: *Come down, sit in the dust, O virgin daughter of Babylon, sit on the ground; there is no throne for the daughter of the Chaldeans.*

### Questions.

15. Question I. It is asked here about the virtue of this pond:

1. Whence did it have this virtue, and for what reason had it been so continually conferred on that pond?

2. Likewise, since the virtue was so great, whence is it that Scripture makes no mention of it[^p305-10]? It seems that it was by the negligence of the writers. — *If you say* that this virtue appeared near the passion of Christ; then it is asked: why did it appear near the passion, and why did it cease when the passion came?

Some answer that in that pond was the wood of the cross, which at that time began to appear and to float on top; therefore, to ennoble the trophy of his passion, the Lord willed to show that miracle, and *near* the passion, because then it began to float; and not *after* the passion, because the wood was drawn out from there. — But because this has no authority from the Scriptures, it is destroyed by the same reason by which it is proved[^p305-11].

On account of this others say that in that water there was no virtue, but the Angel, descending at a certain time, did this in the water through a virtue given to him by the Lord. — It must be said, then, that this virtue was given to the Angel in the water as a figure of baptism, in which there is made a washing through water and a healing through the Spirit[^p305-12]. And therefore it was sent before near baptism, and when baptism came, it ceased. Therefore, because it lasted a short time and began late, Scripture made no mention of it. — And so the answer to all the objections is clear.

16. Question II. Likewise it is asked: 1. Since it was equally easy for the Lord to cure all as one, and he is most bountiful; why did he not give to that water the effect of curing all?

2. Likewise, of the Lord himself it is asked in like manner: why did he cure only one, since he could cure all as easily as one?

**I respond:** It must be said that that bodily healing was more for a sign than for the benefit of him whose it was; for it was ordered to the cure of the soul. Since, then, it was ordered to designating and prefiguring that, it ought to have been done in that way in which it would best signify. And because healing in the Church is not except in the unity of faith and of charity, therefore only one was cured[^p305-13].

<!-- page 306 -->
What, then, is objected is not valid, because it was not only for a benefit, but also for a sign[^p306-1].

17. Question III. But then there is a further question: If it belongs to mercy to have mercy on misery[^p306-2], then where the misery is greater, the more ought mercy to incline: therefore he ought not to have been healed who went down first, but he who was in greater need.

**I respond:** It must be said that, as has been said[^p306-3], it was a sign of the cure of the soul; and because the soul is cured by God according to its disposition for receiving grace, not according to the gravity in guilt; therefore he who went down earlier and more solicitously obtained health earlier.

18. Question IV. Likewise it is asked: since the Lord in Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p306-4], urges confidence on the paralytic and demands confession of faith from others whom he healed; whence is it that he asks nothing at all from this one?

**I respond:** It must be said that we understand this: when the Lord cured anyone, not only in body but also in soul, he left him whole, namely the whole man. — On account of this it is to be noted that there were some who had fallen into sickness from guilt; and because these had to be healed in soul, and the soul is not healed[^p306-5] except through faith in Christ, from such the Lord required the confession of faith. — There were others who were infirm for the manifesting of the glory of God, as the man born blind, below, the ninth chapter[^p306-6], and Lazarus dead, below, the eleventh; and from such the Lord did not require the confession of faith, but healed them by mere liberality; and of the wonderful cure of such John treats. Since, then, this man was such, therefore he did not require faith.

But because this solution is against the Gloss[^p306-7], therefore it must be said that he requires faith only from those who seek health.
