### Capitulum XVIII. *Haec cum dixisset Iesus.*

### Annotatio 68.

(Vers. 1.). *Haec cum dixisset Iesus, egressus est trans torrentem Cedron, ubi erat hortus* etc.

Nota, quod Christus in horto fuit *captus,* in horto *sepultus,* in horto post resurrectionem *inventus.* — In horto fuit *captus,* sicut hic declarat historia: *Ubi erat,* inquit, *hortus, in quem introivit ipse et discipuli eius;* et postea[^p611-8] sequitur de captione. — In horto fuit *sepultus,* unde Ioannis decimo nono: « Erat in loco, ubi crucifixus est, hortus, et in horto monumentum »; et sequitur, quod ibi
<!-- page 612 -->
« posuerunt Iesum ». — In horto fuit *inventus;* unde et Maria, quando primo vidit Dominum, existimavit, eum hortulanum esse, Ioannis vigesimo, quia scilicet in horto invenerat eum. — Per hoc intellige *spiritualiter* triplicem hortum, hortum scilicet, in quo Christus *persecutionem patitur.* Hic est hortus *mundanae concupiscentiae;* de quo tertii Regum vigesimo primo[^p612-1]: « Ait Achab ad Naboth », id est diabolus ad hominem: « Da mihi vineam tuam, ut faciam mihi hortum olerum ». — Hortus, in quo Christus *absconditur,* est hortus *sacrae Scripturae;* de quo dicitur animae contemplativae Canticorum octavo: « Qui habitas in hortis, amici auscultant te; fac me audire vocem tuam ». — Hortus, in quo Christus *invenitur,* scilicet hortus *fidelis conscientiae;* de quo Canticorum sexto: « Descendi in hortum meum, ut viderem poma convallium »; et iterum in eodem: « Dilectus meus descendit in hortum suum ad areolam aromatum ».

### Collatio LXIII.

(Vers. 11.). *Mitte gladium tuum in vaginam.*

1. Nota, quod tria genera hominum solent portare gladium, scilicet *malignantes* — *militantes* — et *dominantes.* — Est ergo gladius *malignantium,* et hic est triplex, gladius videlicet *raneoris in corde,* de quo in Psalmo[^p612-2]: « Gladius eorum intret in corda ipsorum »; et Genesis vigesimo septimo: « In gladio vives et servies fratri tuo ». In gladio enim vivit qui gladium in corde gerit. — Gladius *detractionis in ore;* de quo in Psalmo: « Filii hominum, dentes eorum arma et sagittae, et lingua eorum gladius acutus »; Proverbiorum vigesimo quinto: « Iaculum et gladius et sagitta acuta homo, qui loquitur adversus proximum suum falsum testimonium ». — Gladius *persecutionis in opere;* Exodi decimo quinto[^p612-3]: « Evaginabo gladium meum » etc.; dicit malignus, quod intelligitur de persecutione. — Rancor enim est gladius *absconditus;* persecutio, gladius *evaginatus;* unde ibidem dicitur: « Dixit inimicus: Persequar et comprehendam » etc.; et sequitur: « Evaginabo gladium meum, et interficiet eos manus mea » etc.

2. Est gladius *militantium* triplex: *Doctrina veritatis,* quae est gladius expugnans diabolum impugnantem per *fallaciam;* de quo ad Ephesios sexto[^p612-4]: « Assumite gladium spiritus, quod est verbum Dei ». — *Mortificatio carnis,* quae est gladius expugnans diabolum impugnantem per *concupiscentiam;* qui significatus est, Iudicum septimo, in pane subcinericio, qui non erat aliud, ut ibidem dicitur, nisi « gladius Dei et Gedeonis ». Panis subcinericius est mortificatio carnis, qui est gladius interimens castra Madian, id est motus concupiscentiae carnalis. — *Aemulatio caritatis,* quae est gladius expugnans diabolum impugnantem per *violentiam,* quod figuratum est in gladio Goliath, primi Regum decimo septimo[^p612-5], « quo interfecit eum David ». Tunc interficitur Goliath proprio gladio, quando diabolum tentantem vel tribulantem superat caritatis aemulatio; et de hoc gladio dicitur primi Regum vigesimo primo, quod « non est alter huic similis ».

3. Est praeter hos gladius *dominantium* sive iudicantium, et hic est triplex, scilicet gladius *discretionis,* de quo ad Hebraeos quarto[^p612-6]: « Vivus est sermo Dei et efficax et penetrabilior omni gladio ancipiti ». — Gladius *definitionis;* de quo Apocalypsis primo: « De ore eius », id est Christi, « exibat gladius ex utraque parte acutus ». — Gladius *ultionis;* de quo Isaiae primo: « Si nolueritis et ad iracundiam me provocaveritis, gladius meus devorabit vos ». — Hic est ergo triplex gladius Christi iudicantis: et est *discretio* quasi gladius *penetrans; definitio,* quasi gladius *dividens* sive secans; *ultio,* quasi gladius *occidens.* Est namque triplex actus gladii: *penetrare, secare* et *occidere.*

4. Et nota, quod iste *iudicialis* gladius est exercendus vel exserendus a *Christo* in genus humanum; unde Apocalypsis primo[^p612-7] et Apocalypsis decimo nono: « De ore eius exibat gladius ex utraque parte acutus, et in ipso percutiet gentes ». — A *praelato* in subditum; ad Romanos decimo tertio: « Vis non timere potestatem? Bene fac, et habebis laudem ex ea. Si autem male feceris, time. Non enim sine causa gladium portat ». — Ab *homine* in se ipsum; unde in Psalmo: « Accingere gladio tuo super femur tuum, potentissime ». — Tripliciter exercetur *usus* iudicialis gladii, quando iudicium habet *homo* respectu sui, *praelatus* respectu subditi, *Christus* vero respectu generis humani.

### Collatio LXIV.

(Vers. 11.). *Calicem, quem dedit mihi Pater, non vis, ut bibam illum?*

1. Nota, quod est triplex calix, scilicet calix *hominis mali* — et calix *hominis iusti* — et calix *hominis-Christi.* Primum debemus *proiicere;* secundum debemus *bibere;* tertio debemus *communicare.*

2. Calix ergo *proiiciendus* est calix peccatorum, et hic est triplex: *fortunae, culpae* et *poenae:*
<!-- page 613 -->
primus, *inebrians;* secundus, *venenosus;* tertius, *amarus.* — De primo Ieremiae quinquagesimo primo[^p613-1]: « Calix aureus Babylon in manu Domini; de vino eius biberunt omnes gentes, et ideo commotae sunt ». — De secundo Proverbiorum vigesimo tertio: « Cui vae? Cuius patri vae? Cui rixae? Cui foveae? Cui suffusio oculorum? Cui sine causa vulnera? Nonne his qui morantur in vino et student in calicibus epotandis? » — De tertio in Psalmo: « Ignis, sulphur et spiritus procellarum pars calicis eorum »; et Apocalypsis decimo quarto: « Si quis adoraverit bestiam et imaginem eius, hic bibet de vino irae Dei, quod mixtum est in calice irae ipsius ».

3. Est calix *iustorum,* et hic est triplex, scilicet calix *gratiae,* calix *eucharistiae* et calix *examinationis divinae.* Primus continet quasi *claretum* divinae visitationis; secundus, quasi *rosetum* dominicae passionis; tertius, *raspetum* divinae correptionis. — De primo in Psalmo[^p613-2]: « Calix meus inebrians quam praeclarus est »! — De secundo in eodem: « Calicem salutaris accipiam et nomen Domini invocabo »; primae ad Corinthios undecimo: « Hic calix novi testamenti est in meo sanguine ». — De tertio in Psalmo: « Calix in manu Domini vini meri plenus mixto »; Ieremiae quadragesimo nono: « Ecce, quibus non est iudicium, ut bibant calicem, bibentes bibent, et tu quasi innocens relinqueris? Non eris innocens, sed bibens bibes ».

4. Est praeterea calix *Christi;* de quo hic: *Calicem, quem dedit mihi Pater* etc. Et huic calici *communicandum* est tripliciter: per *spiritualem compassionem,* qualiter de ipso biberat quae dicebat Canticorum quinto[^p613-3]: « Bibi vinum meum cum lacte meo »: *vinum,* inquam, compassionis cum *lacte* consolationis; nam haec duo inveniuntur in passione Christi: *dolor compassionis* ex amaritudine et *dulcedo consolationis* ex utilitate. — Per *sacramentalem susceptionem,* qualiter dabat Dominus ad bibendum discipulis; Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p613-4]: « Bibite ex hoc omnes; hic est enim sanguis meus ». — Per *actualem imitationem,* ad quam invitabat Dominus filios Zebedaei, Matthaei vigesimo dicens: « Potestis bibere calicem, quem ego bibiturus sum »?

### Collatio LXV.

(Vers. 17.). *Negavit Petrus et dixit: Non sum.*

1. Circa negationem Petri notanda sunt tria: qualiter Christum *negaverit* — et quid ipsum ad negandum *induxerit* — et quid eundem a negatione *reduxerit.*

Circa primum nota, quod sicut trina negatio *vocalis* fuit, ita est trina negatio malorum *spiritualis.* Negant enim Christum quidam per *errorem infidelitatis,* quidam per *superbiam praesumtionis,* quidam per *insolentiam conversationis.* Per errorem negant Christum *infideles,* per praesumtionem *hypocritae,* per insolentiam *lascivi.* Primi negant Christum-*veritatem;* secundi Christum-*vitam,* tertii Christum-*viam.*

2. Negant quidam Christum per *infidelitatem;* primae Ioannis secundo[^p613-5]: « Omnis, qui negat Filium, nec Patrem habet »; et ibidem: « Quis est mendax, nisi qui negat, quoniam Iesus est Christus? Hic est antichristus, qui negat Patrem et Filium ». — Negant Christum quidam per *praesumtionem;* Iob trigesimo primo: « Si deosculatus sum manum meam ore meo, quae est iniquitas maxima et negatio contra Deum altissimum ». *Manum propriam osculari* est de proprio opere gloriari; et dicit super illud Gregorius[^p613-6]: « Deum scilicet negare convincitur qui bonum sibi tribuit, quod operatur ». — Negant alii Deum per *insolentiam;* de quibus ad Titum primo[^p613-7]: « Confitentur, se nosse Deum, factis autem negant, cum sint abominabiles et incredibiles et ad omne opus bonum reprobi ».

Sic ergo negant Christum *infideles* per errorem, quia recedunt a Christo-veritate; *hypocritae* per praesumtionem, quia recedunt a Christo-vita; *lascivi* per insolentiam, quia recedunt a Christo-via. Primi negant *veritatem fidei* per errorem perfidiae; secundi dimittunt *vitam gratiae* pro vento superbiae; tertii vero *viam virtutis* pro devio concupiscentiae. Quae significata sunt in trina Petri negatione.

3. Circa secundum nota, quod tria *induxerunt* Petrum ad negandum, scilicet *locus, coetus* et *actus. Locus,* domus principis; *coetus,* servi pontificis; *actus,* calefactio ignis. Negavit enim Petrus Christum in *domo* principis; Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p613-8]: « Petrus sequebatur eum a longe usque in atrium principis sacerdotum ». — Negavit etiam in *coetu* servorum pontificis; unde subiungitur ibidem: « Et ingressus intro sedebat cum ministris, ut videret finem ». — Negavit etiam, cum esset in *actu* calefaciendi se; Ioannis decimo octavo: « Erat autem Petrus stans et calefaciens se ».

4. *Spiritualiter* per *domum principis* significatur status conversationis mundanae; per *ministros pontificis,* congregatio societatis malae; per *calefactionem ignis,* ardor concupiscentiae. Et haec sunt quae inducunt hominem ad lapsum culpae. — Propter primum dicitur Numerorum decimo sexto[^p613-9]:
<!-- page 614 -->
« Recedite a tabernaculis hominum impiorum, ne involvamini in peccatis eorum ». — Propter secundum dicitur Proverbiorum decimo tertio: « Socius stultorum similis efficietur »; et in Psalmo: « Cum perverso perverteris ». — Propter tertium dicitur Iacobi primo: « Unusquisque tentatur, a concupiscentia sua abstractus et illectus ».

5. Circa tertium nota tria, quae Petrum a negatione *reducunt,* scilicet *respectus Christi, cantus galli* et *recordatio Petri.* — Reducit ergo Petrum *cantus galli;* unde Lucae vigesimo secundo[^p614-1]: « Adhuc illo loquente, gallus cantavit ». — Et *respectus Christi;* unde sequitur: « Et conversus Dominus respexit Petrum ». — Et *recordatio Petri;* unde sequitur: « Et recordatus est Petrus verbi Domini ». — Ad haec tria sequitur conversio Petri; unde subiungitur: « Et egressus foras flevit amare ».

6. *Spiritualiter* tunc *cantat gallus,* cum *praedicator* adhibet doctrinam; tunc *respicit Christus,* cum *Deus* infundit gratiam; tunc *recordatur Petrus,* cum *peccator* recurrit ad conscientiam. Et haec sunt tria, quae reducunt peccatorem ad poenitentiam. — Propter primum dicitur in Psalmo[^p614-2]: « Docebo iniquos vias tuas, et impii ad te convertentur ». — Propter secundum dicitur Ecclesiastici undecimo: « Oculus Dei respexit illum in bono et erexit eum ab humilitate ipsius et exaltavit caput eius ». — Propter tertium, Apocalypsis secundo: « Memor esto, unde excideris, et age poenitentiam et prima opera fac ».

### Collatio LXVI.

(Vers. 36.). *Regnum meum non est de hoc mundo.*

1. In verbis istis innuitur quaedam regni distinctio. Est enim regnum *mundi* — et est regnum *Christi:* regnum mundi *contemnendum;* regnum Christi *venerandum.*

Regnum *mundi contemnendum* propter tria, scilicet quia *inutile, ignobile* et *instabile.* — Est ergo regnum mundi *inutile.* Habet enim pro proventibus *sollicitudines,* quae auferunt homini spirituales delectationes. Propterea quaerentibus lignis, ut ungerent super se regem, respondit oliva, Iudicum nono[^p614-3]: « Nunquid possum deserere pinguedinem meam, qua dii utuntur et homines, et venire, ut inter ligna promovear »? Idem respondit ficus de sua dulcedine, et vitis de vino suo. Ubi nota, quod volentes promoveri in regno mundi oportet deserere dulcedinem Dei, quae animam impinguat, inebriat et delectat. Nec erit qui regnum huiusmodi appetat nisi rhamnum, qui nec habet pinguedinem devotionis nec fertilitatem bonae operationis. — Est regnum *ignobile.* Qui enim in mundo regnare appetit cogitur miserabiliter *servire;* quod significatum est Matthaei quarto[^p614-4], ubi « ostendit diabolus Christo omnia regna mundi et gloriam eorum et ait illi: Haec omnia dabo tibi, si cadens adoraveris me »; quasi dicat: non do ea nisi adorantibus me. Oportet enim, superbos de ipso vanitatem tenere, quia dicitur Iob quadragesimo primo: « Ipse est rex super omnes filios superbiae ». — Nota responsum philosophi adversus magnatem. — Est regnum *instabile.* Est enim regnum, de quo Matthaei duodecimo[^p614-5]: « Omne regnum in se ipso divisum desolabitur ». Regnum *divisum* est mundus; unde in Psalmo: « Vidi iniquitatem et contradictionem in civitate », id est in mundo. — Exemplum habe ad hoc de Adonia, tertii Regum primo, cuius regnum fuit ipsa hora sui primi convivii desolatum. Propter huius regni instabilitatem dicitur Baruch tertio: « Ubi sunt principes gentium, et qui dominantur super bestias, quae sunt super terram, et in avibus caeli ludunt »? etc. Et respondet: « Exterminati sunt et ad inferos descenderunt, et alii loco ipsorum surrexerunt ». — Ecce ergo, regnum mundi est *inutile, ignobile, instabile,* et ideo *contemptibile.* Propter hoc sapienter cantat quae dicit[^p614-6]: « Regnum mundi et omnem ornatum saeculi contempsi ».

2. Sequitur de regno *Christi,* quod est *venerandum.* Est autem venerandum propter tria, scilicet quia *pingue,* quia *nobile,* quia *stabile.* — *Pingue* est regnum Christi; habet enim pro reditibus delicias paradisi; unde Lucae vigesimo tertio[^p614-7] latroni petenti regnum repromisit Dominus paradisum: « Memento, inquit, mei, dum veneris in regnum tuum »; et dixit illi Iesus: « Amen dico tibi: Hodie mecum eris in paradiso ». — Est iterum regnum *nobile,* et ideo Apocalypsis decimo nono[^p614-8]: « Rex Christus habet in femore suo scriptum: Rex regum et Dominus dominantium »; et in Psalmo: « Adorabunt eum omnes reges terrae, omnes gentes servient ei ». — Est etiam *stabile* regnum Christi; Danielis septimo: « Potestas eius potestas aeterna, quae non auferetur, et regnum eius, quod non corrumpetur »; et in Psalmo: « Et regni eius non erit finis ». — Regnum ergo Christi est *pingue, nobile* et *stabile,* et ideo *desiderabile.* Propterea non insipienter orat qui quotidie postulat[^p614-9]: « Adveniat regnum tuum ».
<!-- page 615 -->

3. Regnum istud non sibi soli retinet Christus, sed suis largitur fidelibus, quibus dicturus est: « Venite, benedicti Patris mei, percipite regnum », Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p615-1]. Et nota, quod propter tria contingit mereri regnum, scilicet per *contemptum mundanorum,* per *exsecutionem mandatorum,* per *sustinentiam adversorum.* — *Contemnentes mundana* merentur possidere regnum titulo *emptionis,* quo omnia sua dant pro regno habendo; unde Matthaei decimo tertio[^p615-2]: « Simile est regnum caelorum thesauro abscondito in agro, quem qui invenit homo abscondit et prae gaudio illius vadit et vendit universa, quae habet, et emit agrum illum ». Notabiliter dicitur: *emit agrum:* forte non habuit unde emeret thesaurum; et ideo thesaurum abscondit et agrum emit, et per consequens cum agro thesaurum possedit. *Thesaurus,* regnum; *ager,* paupertas. *Homo* ergo, qui non habet quo comparet regnum caelorum, vendit universa, quae habet, et emit agrum paupertatis et in agro possidet thesaurum regni caelestis; unde Matthaei quinto[^p615-3]: « Beati pauperes spiritu, quoniam ipsorum est regnum caelorum ». — *Exsequentes mandata* merentur possidere regnum quasi ratione *conducti* pro mercede sui laboris; et ideo tertii Regum nono ait Dominus Salomoni: « Si feceris omnia, quae praecepi tibi, ponam regnum tuum super Israel usque in sempiternum »; et Matthaei decimo tertio: « Fulgebunt iusti », id est operantes iustitiam, « sicut sol in regno Patris mei ». — *Sustinentes adversa* merentur possidere regnum, quia *socii* sunt acquisitionis. Isti enim sunt socii Christi ad *praedam* et ad *lucrum;* et ideo dicitur eis Lucae vigesimo secundo[^p615-4]: « Vos estis, qui permansistis mecum in tentationibus meis; et ideo dispono vobis, sicut disposuit mihi Pater meus regnum, ut edatis et bibatis super mensam meam in regno meo »; et ideo Matthaei quinto dicitur: « Beati, qui persecutionem patiuntur propter iustitiam; quoniam ipsorum est regnum caelorum ».

### Chapter XVIII. *When Jesus had said these things.*

### Annotation 68.

(Vers. 1.). *When Jesus had said these things, he went forth over the brook Cedron, where there was a garden* etc.

Note that Christ was *taken* in a garden, *buried* in a garden, *found* in a garden after the resurrection. — In a garden he was *taken,* as the history here declares: *Where there was,* he says, *a garden, into which he himself entered and his disciples;* and afterwards[^p611-8] there follows the taking. — In a garden he was *buried;* whence John, the nineteenth chapter: « There was in the place where he was crucified, a garden; and in the garden a sepulchre »; and it follows that there
<!-- page 612 -->
« they laid Jesus ». — In a garden he was *found;* whence Mary also, when she first saw the Lord, thought him to be the gardener, John, the twentieth chapter, namely because she had found him in a garden. — By this understand *spiritually* a threefold garden: the garden, namely, in which Christ *suffers persecution.* This is the garden *of worldly concupiscence;* of which the third of Kings, the twenty-first chapter[^p612-1]: « Achab said to Naboth », that is, the devil to man: « Give me thy vineyard, that I may make me a garden of herbs ». — The garden in which Christ *is hidden* is the garden *of sacred Scripture;* of which it is said to the contemplative soul, the Canticles, the eighth chapter: « Thou that dwellest in the gardens, the friends hearken: make me hear thy voice ». — The garden in which Christ *is found,* namely the garden *of the faithful conscience;* of which the Canticles, the sixth chapter: « I went down into my garden, to see the fruits of the valleys »; and again in the same: « My beloved is gone down into his garden, to the bed of aromatical spices ».

### Collation LXIII.

(Vers. 11.). *Put up thy sword into the scabbard.*

1. Note that three kinds of men are wont to carry a sword, namely *the malignant* — *the soldiering* — and *the ruling.* — There is therefore the sword *of the malignant,* and this is threefold: the sword, namely, *of rancour in the heart* [so printed: *raneoris* for *rancoris*], of which in the Psalm[^p612-2]: « Let their sword enter into their own hearts »; and Genesis, the twenty-seventh chapter: « By the sword thou shalt live, and shalt serve thy brother ». For he lives by the sword who bears a sword in his heart. — The sword *of detraction in the mouth;* of which in the Psalm: « The sons of men, whose teeth are weapons and arrows, and their tongue a sharp sword »; Proverbs, the twenty-fifth chapter: « A dart and a sword and a sharp arrow is a man that speaketh false witness against his neighbour ». — The sword *of persecution in deed;* Exodus, the fifteenth chapter[^p612-3]: « I will draw my sword » etc.; the malignant one says it, which is understood of persecution. — For rancour is a sword *hidden;* persecution, a sword *drawn;* whence it is said in the same place: « The enemy said: I will pursue and overtake » etc.; and it follows: « I will draw my sword, and my hand shall slay them » etc.

2. The sword *of the soldiering* is threefold: *The teaching of truth*[^tr-doctrina], which is a sword overcoming the devil who assails through *fallacy;* of which to the Ephesians, the sixth chapter[^p612-4]: « Take unto you the sword of the spirit, which is the word of God ». — *The mortification of the flesh,* which is a sword overcoming the devil who assails through *concupiscence;* which was signified, Judges, the seventh chapter, in the hearth-cake, which was nothing else, as is said in the same place, but « the sword of God and of Gedeon ». The hearth-cake is the mortification of the flesh, which is the sword slaying the camp of Madian, that is, the motion of carnal concupiscence. — *The emulation of charity,* which is a sword overcoming the devil who assails through *violence,* which was figured in the sword of Goliath, the first of Kings, the seventeenth chapter[^p612-5], « with which David slew him ». Then is Goliath slain with his own sword, when the emulation of charity overcomes the devil tempting or troubling; and of this sword it is said, the first of Kings, the twenty-first chapter, that « there is not another like this ».

3. There is besides these the sword *of the ruling* or judging, and this is threefold, namely the sword *of discretion,* of which to the Hebrews, the fourth chapter[^p612-6]: « The word of God is living and effectual, and more piercing than any two edged sword ». — The sword *of definition;* of which the Apocalypse, the first chapter: « From his mouth », that is, Christ's, « came out a sword sharp on either side ». — The sword *of vengeance;* of which Isaias, the first chapter: « If you will not, and will provoke me to wrath, my sword shall devour you ». — This therefore is the threefold sword of Christ judging: and *discretion* is as a sword *piercing; definition,* as a sword *dividing* or cutting; *vengeance,* as a sword *killing.* For the act of the sword is threefold: *to pierce, to cut* and *to kill.*

4. And note that this *judicial* sword is to be exercised or drawn forth by *Christ* upon the human race; whence the Apocalypse, the first chapter[^p612-7], and the Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter: « Out of his mouth came a sword sharp on either side, and with it he shall strike the nations ». — By *the prelate* upon the subject; to the Romans, the thirteenth chapter: « Wilt thou not be afraid of the power? Do that which is good, and thou shalt have praise from the same. But if thou do that which is evil, fear: for not without cause doth he bear the sword ». — By *man* upon himself; whence in the Psalm: « Gird thy sword upon thy thigh, O thou most mighty ». — Threefoldly is the *use* of the judicial sword exercised: when *man* has judgement in respect of himself, *the prelate* in respect of the subject, and *Christ* in respect of the human race.

### Collation LXIV.

(Vers. 11.). *The chalice which my Father hath given me, wilt thou not that I drink it?*

1. Note that the chalice is threefold, namely the chalice *of the evil man* — and the chalice *of the just man* — and the chalice *of the man-Christ.* The first we ought *to cast away;* the second we ought *to drink;* in the third we ought *to communicate.*

2. The chalice therefore *to be cast away* is the chalice of sins, and this is threefold: *of fortune, of fault* and *of punishment:*
<!-- page 613 -->
the first, *inebriating;* the second, *venomous;* the third, *bitter.* — Of the first, Jeremias, the fifty-first chapter[^p613-1]: « Babylon is a golden chalice in the hand of the Lord; of her wine all the nations have drunk, and therefore they are moved ». — Of the second, Proverbs, the twenty-third chapter: « Who hath woe? To whose father woe? Who hath contentions? Who falls into pits? Who hath redness of eyes? Who hath wounds without cause? Surely they that pass their time in wine, and study to drink off their chalices? » — Of the third in the Psalm: « Fire and brimstone and storms of winds are the portion of their chalice »; and the Apocalypse, the fourteenth chapter: « If any man shall adore the beast and his image, he shall drink of the wine of the wrath of God, which is mingled in the chalice of his wrath ».

3. There is the chalice *of the just,* and this is threefold, namely the chalice *of grace,* the chalice *of the Eucharist* and the chalice *of divine examination.* The first contains as it were the *claretum* of divine visitation; the second, as it were the *rosetum* of the Lord's passion; the third, the *raspetum* of divine correction. — Of the first in the Psalm[^p613-2]: « My chalice which inebriateth me, how goodly is it »! — Of the second in the same: « I will take the chalice of salvation; and I will call upon the name of the Lord »; the first to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter: « This chalice is the new testament in my blood ». — Of the third in the Psalm: « A chalice in the hand of the Lord, full of strong wine, of mixture »; Jeremias, the forty-ninth chapter: « Behold, they whose judgement was not to drink the chalice, drinking shall drink: and shalt thou be left as innocent? Thou shalt not be innocent, but drinking thou shalt drink ».

4. There is moreover the chalice *of Christ;* of which here: *The chalice which my Father hath given me* etc. And with this chalice we must *communicate* threefoldly: by *spiritual compassion,* in the manner in which she had drunk of it who said, the Canticles, the fifth chapter[^p613-3]: « I have drunk my wine with my milk »: *the wine,* I say, of compassion with *the milk* of consolation; for these two are found in the passion of Christ: *the sorrow of compassion* from its bitterness and *the sweetness of consolation* from its usefulness. — By *sacramental reception,* in the manner in which the Lord gave the disciples to drink; Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p613-4]: « Drink ye all of this; for this is my blood ». — By *actual imitation,* to which the Lord invited the sons of Zebedee, saying, Matthew, the twentieth chapter: « Can you drink the chalice that I shall drink »?

### Collation LXV.

(Vers. 17.). *Peter denied and said: I am not.*

1. Concerning the denial of Peter three things are to be noted: how he *denied* Christ — and what *led* him *on* to deny — and what *led* him *back* from the denial.

Concerning the first, note that as the threefold denial was *vocal,* so there is a threefold *spiritual* denial of the wicked. For some deny Christ through *the error of infidelity,* some through *the pride of presumption,* some through *the insolence of conversation.* Through error the *infidels* deny Christ, through presumption the *hypocrites,* through insolence the *wanton.* The first deny Christ-*truth;* the second Christ-*life,* the third Christ-*way.*

2. Some deny Christ through *infidelity;* the first of John, the second chapter[^p613-5]: « Whosoever denieth the Son, hath not the Father either »; and in the same place: « Who is a liar, but he who denieth that Jesus is the Christ? This is Antichrist, who denieth the Father and the Son ». — Some deny Christ through *presumption;* Job, the thirty-first chapter: « If I have kissed my hand with my mouth, which is a very great iniquity, and a denial against the most high God ». *To kiss one's own hand* is to glory in one's own work; and Gregory says on that place[^p613-6]: « He is convicted, namely, of denying God, who attributes to himself the good that he does ». — Others deny God through *insolence;* of whom to Titus, the first chapter[^p613-7]: « They profess that they know God, but in their works they deny him, being abominable and incredulous, and to every good work reprobate ».

Thus therefore the *infidels* deny Christ through error, because they withdraw from Christ-truth; the *hypocrites* through presumption, because they withdraw from Christ-life; the *wanton* through insolence, because they withdraw from Christ-way. The first deny *the truth of faith* through the error of perfidy; the second abandon *the life of grace* for the wind of pride; the third, *the way of virtue* for the byway of concupiscence. Which things are signified in the threefold denial of Peter.

3. Concerning the second, note that three things *led* Peter *on* to deny, namely *the place, the company* and *the act. The place,* the house of the prince; *the company,* the servants of the pontiff; *the act,* the warming at the fire. For Peter denied Christ in *the house* of the prince; Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p613-8]: « Peter followed him afar off, even to the court of the prince of the priests ». — He denied also in *the company* of the servants of the pontiff; whence it is added in the same place: « And going in, he sat with the ministers, that he might see the end ». — He denied also when he was in *the act* of warming himself; John, the eighteenth chapter: « And Peter was standing and warming himself ».

4. *Spiritually,* by *the house of the prince* is signified the state of worldly conversation; by *the ministers of the pontiff,* the gathering of evil society; by *the warming at the fire,* the ardour of concupiscence. And these are what lead man on to the fall of fault. — On account of the first it is said, Numbers, the sixteenth chapter[^p613-9]:
<!-- page 614 -->
« Depart from the tents of the wicked men, lest you be involved in their sins ». — On account of the second it is said, Proverbs, the thirteenth chapter: « A companion of fools shall become like to them »; and in the Psalm: « With the perverse thou wilt be perverted ». — On account of the third it is said, James, the first chapter: « Every man is tempted, being drawn away and allured by his own concupiscence ».

5. Concerning the third, note three things which *lead* Peter *back* from the denial, namely *the look of Christ, the crowing of the cock* and *the remembrance of Peter.* — *The crowing of the cock* therefore leads Peter back; whence Luke, the twenty-second chapter[^p614-1]: « As he was yet speaking, the cock crew ». — And *the look of Christ;* whence it follows: « And the Lord turning looked on Peter ». — And *the remembrance of Peter;* whence it follows: « And Peter remembered the word of the Lord ». — Upon these three follows the conversion of Peter; whence it is added: « And going out, he wept bitterly ».

6. *Spiritually,* then *the cock crows,* when *the preacher* applies teaching; then *Christ looks,* when *God* pours in grace; then *Peter remembers,* when *the sinner* runs back to his conscience. And these are the three things which lead the sinner back to penance. — On account of the first it is said in the Psalm[^p614-2]: « I will teach the unjust thy ways: and the wicked shall be converted to thee ». — On account of the second it is said, Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter: « The eye of God hath looked upon him for good, and hath lifted him up from his low estate, and hath exalted his head ». — On account of the third, the Apocalypse, the second chapter: « Be mindful from whence thou art fallen: and do penance, and do the first works ».

### Collation LXVI.

(Vers. 36.). *My kingdom is not of this world.*

1. In these words a certain distinction of kingdom is intimated. For there is the kingdom *of the world* — and there is the kingdom *of Christ:* the kingdom of the world *to be contemned;* the kingdom of Christ *to be venerated.*

The kingdom *of the world is to be contemned* on account of three things, namely because it is *useless, ignoble* and *unstable.* — The kingdom of the world, then, is *useless.* For it has for its revenues *cares,* which take away from man spiritual delights. Therefore, when the trees sought to anoint a king over them, the olive answered, Judges, the ninth chapter[^p614-3]: « Can I leave my fatness, which both gods and men make use of, and come to be promoted among the trees »? The fig tree answered the same of its sweetness, and the vine of its wine. Where note that those who wish to be promoted in the kingdom of the world must leave the sweetness of God, which fattens, inebriates and delights the soul. Nor will there be any who covets a kingdom of this kind but the bramble, which has neither the fatness of devotion nor the fertility of good working. — The kingdom is *ignoble.* For he who covets to reign in the world is compelled miserably *to serve;* which is signified, Matthew, the fourth chapter[^p614-4], where « the devil shewed Christ all the kingdoms of the world, and the glory of them, and said to him: All these will I give thee, if falling down thou wilt adore me »; as if he should say: I give them only to those who adore me. For the proud must hold vanity from him, because it is said, Job, the forty-first chapter: « He is king over all the children of pride ». — Note the answer of the philosopher against the great man. — The kingdom is *unstable.* For it is the kingdom of which Matthew, the twelfth chapter[^p614-5]: « Every kingdom divided in itself shall be made desolate ». The *divided* kingdom is the world; whence in the Psalm: « I have seen iniquity and contradiction in the city », that is, in the world. — Take an example of this from Adonias, the third of Kings, the first chapter, whose kingdom was made desolate in the very hour of his first banquet. On account of the instability of this kingdom it is said, Baruch, the third chapter: « Where are the princes of the nations, and they that rule over the beasts that are upon the earth, that take their diversion with the birds of the air »? etc. And it answers: « They are cut off, and are gone down to hell, and others are risen up in their place ». — Behold therefore, the kingdom of the world is *useless, ignoble, unstable,* and therefore *contemptible.* For this reason she sings wisely who says[^p614-6]: « The kingdom of the world and all the adornment of the age I have contemned ».

2. There follows the kingdom *of Christ,* which is *to be venerated.* And it is to be venerated on account of three things, namely because it is *fat,* because it is *noble,* because it is *stable.* — *Fat* is the kingdom of Christ; for it has for its returns the delights of paradise; whence, Luke, the twenty-third chapter[^p614-7], to the thief asking for the kingdom the Lord promised paradise in return: « Remember me, he says, when thou shalt come into thy kingdom »; and Jesus said to him: « Amen I say to thee, this day thou shalt be with me in paradise ». — The kingdom is again *noble,* and therefore the Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter[^p614-8]: « Christ the King hath written on his thigh: King of kings, and Lord of lords »; and in the Psalm: « All kings of the earth shall adore him: all nations shall serve him ». — The kingdom of Christ is also *stable;* Daniel, the seventh chapter: « His power is an everlasting power that shall not be taken away: and his kingdom that shall not be destroyed »; and in the Psalm: « And of his kingdom there shall be no end ». — The kingdom of Christ therefore is *fat, noble* and *stable,* and therefore *desirable.* For this reason he prays not unwisely who daily asks[^p614-9]: « Thy kingdom come ».
<!-- page 615 -->

3. This kingdom Christ does not keep for himself alone, but bestows it on his faithful, to whom he is to say: « Come, ye blessed of my Father, possess the kingdom », Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p615-1]. And note that it comes about that the kingdom is merited on account of three things, namely by *the contempt of worldly things,* by *the execution of the commandments,* by *the endurance of adversities.* — *Those who contemn worldly things* merit to possess the kingdom by title *of purchase,* in that they give all that is theirs to have the kingdom; whence Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p615-2]: « The kingdom of heaven is like unto a treasure hidden in a field, which a man having found, hid it, and for joy thereof goeth, and selleth all that he hath, and buyeth that field ». It is notably said: *he buyeth the field:* perhaps he had not wherewith to buy the treasure; and therefore he hid the treasure and bought the field, and consequently with the field possessed the treasure. *The treasure,* the kingdom; *the field,* poverty. *The man* therefore who has not wherewith to purchase the kingdom of heaven sells all that he has, and buys the field of poverty, and in the field possesses the treasure of the heavenly kingdom; whence Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p615-3]: « Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven ». — *Those who execute the commandments* merit to possess the kingdom as by reason *of hire,* for the wage of their labour; and therefore, the third of Kings, the ninth chapter, the Lord says to Solomon: « If thou wilt do all things that I have commanded thee, I will set thy kingdom over Israel for ever »; and Matthew, the thirteenth chapter: « The just shall shine », that is, those who work justice, « as the sun in the kingdom of my Father ». — *Those who endure adversities* merit to possess the kingdom, because they are *partners* of the acquisition. For these are the partners of Christ for *the spoil* and for *the gain;* and therefore it is said to them, Luke, the twenty-second chapter[^p615-4]: « You are they who have continued with me in my temptations: and therefore I dispose to you, as my Father hath disposed to me, a kingdom; that you may eat and drink at my table, in my kingdom »; and therefore it is said, Matthew, the fifth chapter: « Blessed are they that suffer persecution for justice' sake: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven ».
