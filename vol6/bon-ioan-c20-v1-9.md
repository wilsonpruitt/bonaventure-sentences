# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XX, Vers. 1-9
## *Primo, de sollicitudine discipulorum et mulierum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 504 -->

### Capitulum XX.

#### Tertio tractatur de Christi resurrectione tripliciter.

1. *Una autem Sabbati* etc. Egit supra[^p504-7] Evangelista de his quae spectant ad Domini incarnationem et passionem; ab hoc loco usque in finem agit de his quae spectant ad eius *resurrectionem.* Agitur ergo in hac parte de manifestatione resurrectionis Christi. Et quia hanc manifestationem praeivit discipulorum sollicitudo, et secuta est nostra instructio sive fidei certitudo; ideo in hac parte sunt tres partes; in quarum prima describitur *sollicitudo discipulorum et mulierum,* quibus se manifestavit. In secunda describitur *ipsa manifestatio,* ibi[^p504-8]: *Abierunt ergo discipuli.* In tertia, *nostrae fidei confirmatio,* ibi: *Hic est discipulus ille* etc.

#### Primo, de sollicitudine discipulorum et mulierum.

Prima pars dividitur; quia enim Dominus utrumque sexum redemerat et utrique se manifestare volebat; praemittitur non tantum sollicitudo virorum, sed etiam mulierum. Ideo primo ponitur sollicitudo *Mariae;* deinde *discipulorum,* ibi[^p504-9]: *Exiit ergo Petrus* etc.

2. (Vers. 1.). Sollicitudo Mariae notatur et in *vigilantia ad requirendum* et in *diligentia ad renuntiandum.* — Vigilans quippe ad *requirendum* fuit, quia summo diluculo venit; propterea dicit: *Una autem Sabbati,* scilicet die dominica, quae est *una,* id est prima dies *Sabbati,* id est septimanae, secundum illud Lucae decimo octavo[^p504-10]: « Ieiuno bis in Sabbato ». *Sabbatum* etiam dicitur septimus dies; Exodi vigesimo: « Memento, ut diem Sabbati sanctifices ». *Maria Magdalene venit mane,* tanquam sollicita videre Domini corpus, et ideo non exspectavit diem; et ideo addit: *Cum adhuc tenebrae essent, ad monumentum.* Chrysostomus[^p504-11]: « Maria, omnino circa Magistrum amicabiliter se habens, quia Sabbatum transiit, non sustinuit quiescere; sed venit diluculo profundo ad monumentum, a loco mitigationem quandam invenire volens ». Et quia *mane,* ideo
<!-- page 505 -->
invenit; Proverbiorum octavo[^p505-1]: « Qui mane vigilaverint ad me invenient me ». — *Et vidit lapidem,* hic notatur diligentia *ad renuntiandum* in hoc, quod visam monumenti apertionem refert discipulis; unde: *Et vidit lapidem revolutum*[^p505-2] *a monumento.* Ista remotio lapidis significata fuit Iudicum decimo sexto, ubi dicitur, quod « Samson apprehendit ambas fores portae cum postibus et seris impositasque humeris suis portavit ad verticem montis ».

3. (Vers. 2.). *Cucurrit ergo et venit ad Simonem Petrum,* qui praecipue inter alios Christum diligebat, *et ad alium discipulum, quem diligebat*[^p505-3] *Iesus,* id est ad Ioannem, qui se non nominat propter humilitatem; *et dicit eis: Tulerunt Dominum de monumento;* in Graeco additur *meum* ad expressionem affectus. *Tulerunt,* scilicet infinite, vel Iudaei, vel custodes, *et nescio*[^p505-4], *ubi posuerunt eum,* quod erat ei causa maioris doloris. Quia enim eum adhuc carnaliter cognoscebat et amabat; ideo, carne eius subtracta, nulla consolatio remanebat[^p505-5].

4. (Vers. 3.). *Exiit ergo Simon Petrus.* Hic notatur sollicitudo *discipulorum* et in *velocitate currendi* et in *curiositate considerandi,* ibi[^p505-6]: *Et cum inclinasset se.* Solliciti igitur fuerunt, quia, audito, quod sublatus esset, velociter venerunt; unde dicit: *Exiit ergo Simon Petrus et ille alius discipulus et venerunt ad monumentum;* hoc dictum est per anticipationem, hoc est, arripuerunt iter, ut venirent ad monumentum; et velociter veniebant[^p505-7], unde dicit:

5. (Vers. 4.). *Currebant autem duo simul,* velociter scilicet, sed in hac velocitate dispariter; unde: *Et ille alius discipulus praecucurrit citius Petro et venit prior*[^p505-8] *ad monumentum* — ideo *velociter,* quia comprehendere volebant; primae ad Corinthios nono: « Qui in stadio currunt, omnes quidem currunt, sed unus accipit bravium; sic currite, ut comprehendatis » — vel quia iunior erat et citius currere poterat, vel quia ad videndum erat ferventior; sed tamen Petrus fuit in considerando diligentior; unde sequitur disparitas in diligentia considerationis[^p505-9]. Nam:

6. (Vers. 5.). *Et cum se inclinasset, vidit linteamina posita, non tamen introivit;* et sic in quaerendo fuit velox, sed in consideratione[^p505-10] remissus, Petrus autem e contrario; unde dicit:

7. (Vers. 6.). *Venit autem*[^p505-11] *Simon Petrus sequens eum et introivit in monumentum,* non contentus tantum extra aspicere, ut Ioannes. Et sic verificatum est illud Matthaei decimo nono: « Erunt novissimi primi, et primi novissimi ». *Et vidit linteamina posita,* separatim.

8. (Vers. 7.). *Et sudarium, quod fuerat super caput eius, non cum linteaminibus positum,* tanquam hoc gestum esset per festinationem; *sed separatim involutum in unum locum; linteamina* ad operiendum corpus; supra decimo nono[^p505-12]: « Acceperunt corpus Iesu et ligaverunt illud linteaminibus »; *sudarium* ad operiendum faciem; supra undecimo: « Et facies eius involuta erat sudario ». Et sic omnia diligenter respexit ante quam Ioannes, qui eum praecesserat, sed tamen in diligenti consideratione sequitur eum; unde dicit:
<!-- page 506 -->

9. (Vers. 8.). *Tunc ergo introivit et ille alius discipulus, qui venit primus*[^p506-1] *ad monumentum,* scilicet exemplo Petri, *et vidit,* quae praedicta sunt, *et credidit.* — Sed aliter exponitur secundum Chrysostomum, aliter secundum Augustinum. Nam Chrysostomus[^p506-2] exponit: *credidit,* eum scilicet resurrexisse; et argumentum habuit ex hoc, quod si aliquis sustulisset, non sic omnia ordinate reliquisset, quae erant magis utilia quam corpus. Augustinus[^p506-3] autem aliter exponit: *credidit,* scilicet corpus esse sublatum, sicut mulier dixerat; et hoc probat per litteram sequentem:

10. (Vers. 9.). *Nondum enim sciebat*[^p506-4] *Scripturam, quia oportet eum a mortuis resurgere;* ideo non credidit resurrexisse, sed sublatum esse. — Tamen, si velimus tenere expositionem Chrysostomi[^p506-5], littera sequens sic continuatur: *Vidit et credidit,* id est, tunc primo ex visu credidit, quia ante non credebat; *nondum enim sciebat Scripturam.*

### Quaestiones.

11. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo: cum aliae mulieres cum Magdalena venissent ad monumentum, propter quid tantum de ea loquitur Ioannes?

Respondet Augustinus in tertio libro de Concordia Evangelistarum[^p506-6]: « Maria, inquit, Magdalena ceteris mulieribus, quae Domino ministraverant, plurimum ferventior erat; propter quod solam non immerito Ioannes commemoravit, tacitis aliis, quae cum ea fuerunt, sicut alii testantur ».

12. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Mane venit ad monumentum.* — *Contra:* Matthaei vigesimo octavo[^p506-7] dicitur, quod *vespere venerunt videre sepulcrum.*

Respondet Augustinus[^p506-8] dicens, quod « *vespere Sabbati* idem est, quod nocte Sabbati »; et hoc probat, quod ita debet intelligi, per litteram sequentem: « *quae lucescit in prima Sabbati;* quod fieri non potest, si solum noctis initium intellexerimus; sed *vespera* per synecdochen *noctem* dat intelligere, quae luce incipit terminari ». — Alii[^p506-9] respondent, quod illud dictum est, quia *vespera* praeparaverunt se emendo aromata, sed *mane* diluculo venerunt, sicut dicit Ioannes.

13. Quaest. III. Item, quid est quod dicitur: *Cum adhuc tenebrae essent,* quia Marci decimo sexto[^p506-10] dicitur: *Orto iam sole?*

**Respondeo:** Sicut dicit Augustinus in libro de Concordia Evangelistarum[^p506-11], bis venit Maria: primo, antequam vocaret discipulos, et postea iterum rediit; et primo venit, cum adhuc essent tenebrae; post, cum sol luceret.

14. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Vidit lapidem revolutum* etc. — Nonne maioris virtutis fuisset eo clauso exire? Videtur, quod sic.

Et dicendum, quod Dominus surrexit, clauso monumento, et illud apertum est postmodum ab Angelo. Chrysostomus[^p506-12]: « Surrexit quidem, et
<!-- page 507 -->
lapide et signaculis iacentibus, Iesus. Quia vero oportebat, alios certificari; aperitur monumentum post resurrectionem, et ita creditur quod factum est ».

15. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur: unde hoc, quod isti soli cucurrerunt, et alii non?

Dicendum, quod vel quia istis solis relatum est; vel, sicut dicit Gregorius[^p507-1], « illi prae ceteris cucurrerunt, qui prae ceteris amaverunt ».

16. Quaest. VI. Item, 1: cum Lucas dicat vigesimo quarto[^p507-2], quod Petrus ivit ad monumentum, tantum de Petro loquitur; quid est, quod Ioannes hic duos cucurrisse dicit?

2. Item, quae necessitas erat, quod Ioannes hic de se faceret mentionem? — Videtur, quod melius fecisset tacendo.

Respondet Augustinus in tertio de Concordia Evangelistarum[^p507-3], quod Lucas Petrum solum commemoravit, quia illi Magdalena primo nuntiavit; et tamen ipse utrumque commemorat; unde Cleophas dixit: *Abierunt quidam ex nostris ad monumentum* etc.

Ad aliud, quod quaeritur: quare de se facit mentionem? duplex est ratio: *litteralis* scilicet, quia volebat Petrum sibi praeponere, ut Petrum laudaret, se ipsum humiliaret; quia, cum ipse prior venisset, posterior introivit[^p507-4]. — *Allegorica* vero est, quia per Ioannem populus *Iudaicus,* per Petrum seniorem *gentilis* designatur. Ioannes prius cucurrit, sed non introivit; quia Iudaeus prius Legem suscepit, sed non credidit; gentilis vero tardius veniens credidit[^p507-5].

## English
<!-- page 504 -->

### Chapter XX.

#### Third, there is treatment of the resurrection of Christ, in three ways.

1. *Now on one of the Sabbath* etc. Above[^p504-7] the Evangelist treated of the things that regard the Lord's incarnation and passion; from this place to the end he treats of the things that regard his *resurrection.* In this part, therefore, there is treatment of the manifestation of the resurrection of Christ. And because the solicitude of the disciples went before this manifestation, and our instruction, or the certitude of faith, followed; therefore in this part there are three parts; in the first of which is described the *solicitude of the disciples and of the women,* to whom he manifested himself. In the second is described *the manifestation itself,* there[^p504-8]: *The disciples therefore departed.* In the third, *the confirmation of our faith,* there: *This is that disciple* etc.

#### First, on the solicitude of the disciples and of the women.

The first part is divided; for because the Lord had redeemed both sexes and willed to manifest himself to both; there is set first not only the solicitude of the men, but also of the women. Therefore first is set down the solicitude *of Mary;* then *of the disciples,* there[^p504-9]: *Peter therefore went out* etc.

2. (Vers. 1.). The solicitude of Mary is noted both in *vigilance for seeking* and in *diligence for announcing.* — For she was vigilant for *seeking,* because she came at the very break of day; on this account he says: *Now on one of the Sabbath,* namely on the Lord's day, which is *one,* that is, the first day *of the Sabbath,* that is, of the week, according to that of Luke, the eighteenth chapter[^p504-10]: « I fast twice in the Sabbath ». *Sabbath* is also said of the seventh day; Exodus, the twentieth chapter: « Remember that thou keep holy the day of the Sabbath ». *Mary Magdalen came in the morning,* as one solicitous to see the Lord's body, and therefore she did not wait for the day; and therefore he adds: *When it was yet dark, to the sepulchre.* Chrysostom[^p504-11]: « Mary, bearing herself altogether lovingly toward the Teacher, because the Sabbath had passed, could not bear to rest; but she came at deep daybreak to the sepulchre, wishing to find some mitigation from the place ». And because *in the morning,* therefore
<!-- page 505 -->
she finds; Proverbs, the eighth chapter[^p505-1]: « They that in the morning watch for me shall find me ». — *And she saw the stone,* here is noted the diligence *for announcing,* in this, that she reports to the disciples the opening of the sepulchre she had seen; whence: *And she saw the stone rolled away*[^p505-2] *from the sepulchre.* This removal of the stone was signified in Judges, the sixteenth chapter, where it is said that « Samson took both the doors of the gate with the posts and the bolts, and laying them on his shoulders carried them to the top of the mountain ».

3. (Vers. 2.). *She ran therefore and came to Simon Peter,* who especially among the others loved Christ, *and to the other disciple whom Jesus loved*[^p505-3], that is, to John, who does not name himself on account of humility; *and she saith to them: They have taken away the Lord out of the sepulchre;* in the Greek *my* is added, for the expression of affection. *They have taken,* namely indefinitely, either the Jews or the guards, *and I know not*[^p505-4] *where they have laid him,* which was to her the cause of greater sorrow. For because she still knew and loved him carnally; therefore, his flesh being withdrawn, no consolation remained[^p505-5].

4. (Vers. 3.). *Simon Peter therefore went out.* Here is noted the solicitude *of the disciples,* both in the *swiftness of running* and in the *curiosity of considering,* there[^p505-6]: *And when he had stooped down.* They were solicitous, then, because, having heard that he had been taken away, they came swiftly; whence he says: *Simon Peter therefore went out, and that other disciple, and they came to the sepulchre;* this is said by anticipation, that is, they took to the road, that they might come to the sepulchre; and they were coming swiftly[^p505-7], whence he says:

5. (Vers. 4.). *And the two ran together,* swiftly, namely, but in this swiftness unequally; whence: *And that other disciple did outrun Peter, more swiftly, and came before*[^p505-8] *to the sepulchre* — therefore *swiftly,* because they wished to obtain; the first to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter: « They that run in the race, all run indeed, but one receiveth the prize; so run that you may obtain » — either because he was younger and could run more swiftly, or because he was more fervent to see; but yet Peter was more diligent in considering; whence there follows an inequality in the diligence of consideration[^p505-9]. For:

6. (Vers. 5.). *And when he had stooped down, he saw the linen cloths lying, yet he went not in;* and so in seeking he was swift, but in consideration[^p505-10] slack, and Peter the contrary; whence he says:

7. (Vers. 6.). *Now Simon Peter came*[^p505-11] *following him and went into the sepulchre,* not content only to look in from outside, as John was. And so that of Matthew, the nineteenth chapter, was verified: « The last shall be first, and the first last ». *And he saw the linen cloths lying,* apart.

8. (Vers. 7.). *And the napkin that had been upon his head, not lying with the linen cloths,* as if this had been done in haste; *but apart, wrapped up into one place; the linen cloths* for covering the body; above, the nineteenth chapter[^p505-12]: « They took the body of Jesus and bound it in linen cloths »; *the napkin* for covering the face; above, the eleventh chapter: « And his face was bound about with a napkin ». And so he looked at everything diligently before John, who had gone before him, but yet in diligent consideration he follows him; whence he says:
<!-- page 506 -->

9. (Vers. 8.). *Then that other disciple also went in, who came first*[^p506-1] *to the sepulchre,* namely by Peter's example, *and he saw* the things foretold, *and believed.* — But it is expounded one way according to Chrysostom, another way according to Augustine. For Chrysostom[^p506-2] expounds: *he believed,* namely that he had risen again; and he had an argument from this, that if anyone had taken him away, he would not have left everything in such order, things which were more useful than the body. But Augustine[^p506-3] expounds otherwise: *he believed,* namely that the body had been taken away, as the woman had said; and this he proves by the text following:

10. (Vers. 9.). *For as yet he knew not*[^p506-4] *the Scripture, that he must rise again from the dead;* therefore he did not believe that he had risen again, but that he had been taken away. — Yet, if we wish to hold the exposition of Chrysostom[^p506-5], the text following is joined on thus: *He saw and believed,* that is, then for the first time from sight he believed, because before he did not believe; *for as yet he knew not the Scripture.*

### Questions.

11. Quaest. I. But it is asked here first: since other women had come to the sepulchre with Magdalen, for what reason does John speak only of her?

Augustine responds in the third book *On the Agreement of the Evangelists*[^p506-6]: « Mary Magdalen, he says, was by far more fervent than the other women who had ministered to the Lord; for which reason John not undeservedly made mention of her alone, the others being passed over in silence who were with her, as the others testify ».

12. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *She came in the morning to the sepulchre.* — *On the contrary:* in Matthew, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p506-7], it is said that *in the evening they came to see the sepulchre.*

Augustine responds[^p506-8], saying that « *the evening of the Sabbath* is the same as the night of the Sabbath »; and he proves that it ought to be understood so by the text following: « *which dawns on the first of the Sabbath;* which cannot be, if we understand only the beginning of the night; but *evening,* by synecdoche, gives to understand *the night,* which begins to be ended by the light ». — Others[^p506-9] respond that that is said because *in the evening* they prepared themselves by buying spices, but *in the morning,* at daybreak, they came, as John says.

13. Quaest. III. Likewise, what is it that is said: *When it was yet dark,* since in Mark, the sixteenth chapter[^p506-10], it is said: *The sun being now risen?*

**I respond:** As Augustine says in the book *On the Agreement of the Evangelists*[^p506-11], Mary came twice: first, before she called the disciples, and afterwards she returned again; and the first time she came when it was still dark; afterwards, when the sun was shining.

14. Quaest. IV. Likewise it is asked about this, that he says: *She saw the stone rolled away* etc. — Would it not have been of greater power to go out with it closed? It seems that it would.

And it must be said that the Lord rose again with the sepulchre closed, and it was opened afterwards by the Angel. Chrysostom[^p506-12]: « He rose indeed, both the
<!-- page 507 -->
stone and the seals lying in place, Jesus. But because it was necessary that others should be made certain; the sepulchre is opened after the resurrection, and so what was done is believed ».

15. Quaest. V. Likewise it is asked: whence is this, that these alone ran, and not the others?

It must be said that either because it was reported to these alone; or, as Gregory says[^p507-1], « those ran before the others who loved before the others ».

16. Quaest. VI. Likewise, 1: since Luke says in the twenty-fourth chapter[^p507-2] that Peter went to the sepulchre, he speaks only of Peter; what is it, that John here says that two ran?

2. Likewise, what necessity was there that John should here make mention of himself? — It seems that he would have done better by keeping silent.

Augustine responds in the third book *On the Agreement of the Evangelists*[^p507-3] that Luke made mention of Peter alone, because Magdalen announced it to him first; and yet he himself makes mention of both; whence Cleophas said: *Some of our people went to the sepulchre* etc.

To the other, which is asked: why does he make mention of himself? the reason is twofold: *literal,* namely, because he wished to set Peter before himself, that he might praise Peter, humble himself; because, though he himself had come before, he went in later[^p507-4]. — The *allegorical* is, that by John the *Jewish* people, by Peter, the elder, the *gentile* is designated. John ran first, but did not go in; because the Jew first received the Law, but did not believe; but the gentile, coming later, believed[^p507-5].
