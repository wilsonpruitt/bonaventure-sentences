# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XIV, Vers. 1-14
## *Primo, circa constantiam in fide tria ponuntur*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 435 -->

### Capitulum XIV.

#### Secundo confirmantur discipuli eruditionis et consolationis verbo tripliciter.

1. *Et ait discipulis suis*[^p435-8]*: Non turbetur* etc. Supra confortavit Dominus discipulos suos exemplo, in hac parte confortat *eruditionis verbo;* et continet haec pars tria capitula et dividitur in tres. In prima parte erudit eos Dominus, ut sint constantes *in fide;* in secunda vero, *in dilectione;* et haec incipit in principio decimi quinti[^p435-9]: *Ego sum vitis vera;* in tertia vero, *in spei exspectatione,* parum post principium decimi sexti: *Haec autem vobis ab initio.*

#### Primo, circa constantiam in fide tria ponuntur.

Prima pars, quae continet praesens capitulum, tres habet partes. In prima Dominus *hortatur ad fidem constantem.* In secunda vero, quia « fides sine operibus mortua est[^p435-10] », *hortatur ad bonam operationem,* ibi: *Si diligitis me, mandata mea servate.* In tertia vero *praedicit recessus sui accelerationem,* ibi: *Haec locutus sum vobis, apud vos manens.*

Primo ergo hortatur discipulos, ut propter recessum suum *non vacillent in fide,* ostendens eis primo recedendi *modum;* secundo, *viam;* tertio, *terminum;* quarto, *fructum.*

(Vers. 1.). Hortatur ergo primo, ut, considerantes *recedendi modum,* de recessu suo non turbentur nec in fide deficiant, sed magis credant. Propterea dicit: *Non turbetur cor vestrum,* sed credat; unde: *Creditis in Deum, et in me credite.* Non turbetur de absentia, sed in absentem credite; sicut in Deum creditis, quem non videtis[^p435-11]. Et ratio, quare non debent turbari, sed credere, est: quia scilicet Dominus ab eis non omnino recedit, sed eos praecedit, ut iterum eos assumat ad gloriam, in qua
<!-- page 436 -->
plures sunt mansiones iam *paratae* secundum electionem aeternam, sed *parandae* adhuc secundum merita; unde hoc verbum *parare* secundum expositionem Augustini in littera[^p436-1] accipitur secundum has duas significationes. Propterea debet legi littera sic:

2. (Vers. 2.). *In domo Patris mei mansiones multae sunt,* iam scilicet paratae secundum aeternam electionem; quia ad Ephesios primo[^p436-2]: « Elegit nos ante mundi constitutionem »; Hieronymus: « Paratae sunt in caelo diversae mansiones diversis virtutibus, quas non personae accipiunt, sed opera ». Ita sunt paratae. *Si quo minus,* id est, si non ita esset; unde alia littera Chrysostomi[^p436-3], ubi dicitur: *Si quo minus,* dicit: « Si vero non », id est, si non essent paratae per praedestinationem; *dixissem vobis: Quia vado parare vobis locum.* Sed hoc est impossibile, quod ego modo parem; quia impossibile est, quod aliquem de novo praedestinem, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p436-4]. Ideo dixi, quod « mansiones sunt in domo Patris » iam *paratae* per praedestinationem, sed adhuc sunt *parandae* per meritum fidei, quae maxime meritum habet in absentia. Ideo subdit:

3. (Vers. 3.). *Et si abiero et praeparavero vobis locum,* per fidem scilicet vestram in mei absentia; unde Augustinus[^p436-5]: « Eat Dominus Iesus et paret locum; eat, ne videatur; lateat, ut credatur. Tunc enim locus paratur, si ex fide vivatur. Creditus desideretur, ut desideratus habeatur; desiderium dilectionis est praeparatio mansionis ». Cum sic praeparavero in absentia, *iterum venio et accipiam vos ad me ipsum,* per gloriam scilicet, *ut, ubi sum ego, et vos sitis;* supra duodecimo[^p436-6]: « Si quis mihi ministrat, me sequatur, et ubi sum ego, ibi sit et minister meus ». Hortatur ergo eos ad fidem in absentia, quia ideo se absentat, ut fides meritum habeat. Sic legit Augustinus[^p436-7] et Glossa.

4. Potest tamen exponi[^p436-8] totum in una significatione huius verbi *parare,* ut sit sensus: *Non turbetur cor vestrum,* sed *credite in me;* quia *in domo Patris mei mansiones multae sunt,* in quibus requiescetis una mecum, ad quas ego viam praeparo, ideo non debetis dolere. Sed tunc deberetis, *si quo minus dixissem,* id est, si non *dixissem: Quia vado vobis parare locum, iterum assumam vos*[^p436-9] etc. Sed hoc dixi et dico; unde: *Et si abiero et praeparavero vobis locum, iterum venio et accipiam vos* etc. Quocumque tamen modo legatur, fit exhortatio ad fidem.

5. (Vers. 4.). *Et quo ego vado, scitis.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *via recessus.* Et ne videretur eis dicere aliquid novum de suo recessu, dicit, quod ipsi sciebant et viam et terminum; ideo dicit: *Et quo ego vado, scitis et viam scitis*[^p436-10]. Sed
<!-- page 437 -->
quidam ex eis non plene intelligebant; ideo quaerunt, scilicet Thomas et Philippus; unde:

6. (Vers. 5.). *Dicit ei Thomas: Domine, nescimus, quo vadis; et quomodo possumus viam scire?* Thomas dubius[^p437-1] tarde intelligebat, et hoc ad nostrum profectum; quia, sicut negatio Petri profuit ad tollendam praesumtionem, sic nescientia Thomae ad fidei robur et distinctionem. Ideo certificat eum Dominus de via et termino; unde:

7. (Vers. 6.). *Dicit ei Iesus: Ego sum via, veritas*[^p437-2] *et vita. Veritas* et *vita* quoad terminum, quia *veritas* intellectum, *vita* respicit affectum. *Ego sum via* quaerentibus, *veritas* invenientibus, *vita* sine morte permanentibus[^p437-3]. — *Ego sum via* non errans; Psalmus[^p437-4]: « Erraverunt in invio et non in via ». *Ego sum veritas* non fallens; Psalmus: « Fidelis Dominus in omnibus verbis suis ». *Vita* indeficiens; Lucae decimo: « Maria optimam partem elegit, quae non auferetur ab ea ». — *Ego sum via* ducens; Proverbiorum quarto[^p437-5]: « Ducam te per semitas aequitatis, quas cum ingressus fueris, non arctabuntur gressus tui, et currens non habebis offendiculum ». *Ego sum veritas* lucens; Malachiae ultimo: « Orietur vobis timentibus Deum sol iustitiae ». *Vita* pascens; supra decimo: « Per me si quis introierit, salvabitur; et ingredietur et egredietur et pascua inveniet ». — *Ego sum via* exemplo; supra decimo tertio[^p437-6]: « Exemplum dedi vobis, ut quemadmodum ego feci vobis, ita et vos faciatis ». *Ego sum veritas* in promisso; Psalmus: « Quae procedunt de labiis meis non faciam irrita ». *Ego sum vita* in praemio; ad Romanos sexto: « Stipendia peccati mors; gratia autem Dei, vita aeterna ». Et quod sit *via,* ostendit: *Nemo venit ad Patrem nisi per me,* et per me utique venit[^p437-7].

8. (Vers. 7.). *Si cognovissetis me, et Patrem meum utique cognovissetis;* quia, sicut dicitur supra decimo[^p437-8], « ego et Pater unum sumus »; Matthaei undecimo: « Nemo novit Filium nisi Pater, neque Patrem quis novit nisi Filius, et cui voluerit Filius revelare ». *Et amodo cognoscetis eum et vidistis eum; cognoscetis eum,* scilicet in adventu Spiritus sancti; infra decimo sexto[^p437-9]: « Cum venerit ille Spiritus veritatis, docebit vos omnem veritatem ». Verificabitur in vobis illud Ieremiae trigesimo primo: « Dabo legem meam in visceribus eorum, et in cordibus eorum scribam eam ».

9. (Vers. 8.). *Dicit ei Philippus.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *Christi recessus terminus,* quem cupiebat Philippus videre; propter quod *dicit ei Philippus: Domine, ostende nobis Patrem, et sufficit nobis,* quasi: nihil ultra a te quaerimus. *Ipse* enim est « omnia in omnibus », primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto[^p437-10]. Non tantum *ipse,* sed etiam *gratia sua;* secundae ad Corinthios duodecimo: « Sufficit tibi gratia mea ». Et quia Philippus quaerebat quod non oportebat, ideo erudit Dominus Philippum, quod ipse quaerit quod scire debebat; unde:

10. (Vers. 9.). *Dicit ei Iesus: Tanto tempore vobiscum sum, et non cognovisti*[^p437-11] *me?* Quasi dicat:
<!-- page 438 -->
cum de Patre quaeris, de me quaeris, quem nosse debes. Ideo dicit: *Philippe, qui videt me videt et Patrem meum. Quomodo tu dicis: Ostende nobis Patrem? Videt* oculo fidei, secundum quod Abraham, Genesis decimo octavo[^p438-1], dicitur quod « vidit tres et unum adoravit ». Et quod ita debeat credere, ostendit, quia unum sunt; propter quod dicit:

11. (Vers. 10.). *Non credis*[^p438-2]*, quia ego in Patre, et Pater in me est?* tanquam unum; quasi dicat: hoc debes credere. Et hoc manifestat per opus: *Verba, quae ego loquor vobis, a me ipso non loquor: Pater autem in me manens, ipse facit opera,* et ita, si una est operatio, et una est substantia; supra quinto[^p438-3]: « Quaecumque Pater facit, haec et Filius similiter facit »; et octavo: « A me ipso facio nihil ».

12. (Vers. 11.). *Non creditis, quia ego in Patre, et Pater in me*[^p438-4]*?* Simpliciter sine persuasione, sicut fides recta credit.

13. (Vers. 12.). *Alioquin propter opera ipsa credite;* supra decimo[^p438-5]: « Si non facio opera Patris mei, nolite credere mihi ». Unde Augustinus: « Si separati essemus, nequaquam inseparabiliter operari possemus ». Et sic explicatum est, quo dixerat se iturum, quia non ibat ad Patrem ut ad aliquem *extra se;* sed Patri se aequalem credi facere volebat. Et hoc quidem discipuli iam sciebant, quia Deum verum esse credebant, et ita Patri aequalem per omnia; alioquin non esset verus Deus[^p438-6].

*Amen, amen dico vobis.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *recessus Christi fructus* vel utilitas in credentibus; propter quod dicit: *Qui credit in me, opera, quae ego facio, et ipse faciet et maiora horum faciet;* Matthaei decimo septimo[^p438-7]: « Si habueritis fidem sicut granum sinapis, dicetis monti huic: Transi hinc illuc, et transibit, et nihil impossibile erit vobis ». Et ratio huius est Christi recessus a nobis; propterea subdit: *Quia ego ad Patrem vado,* ut scilicet appaream vultui Dei « ad interpellandum pro vobis[^p438-8] »; ideo primae Ioannis secundo: « Advocatum habemus apud Patrem Iesum Christum »; et ideo dicit:

14. (Vers. 13.). *Quodcumque petieritis Patrem in nomine meo, hoc faciam.* Ideo dicit, quod petere debemus a Patre in nomine Filii, *ut* scilicet *honorificetur*[^p438-9] *Pater in Filio,* quia Filius propriam gloriam non quaerit, sed Patris; propterea dictum est supra septimo: « Qui quaerit gloriam eius qui misit illum, hic verax est ». Et ad maiorem certitudinem, quia firmior fit promissio duplici affirmatione; ideo subinfert:

15. (Vers. 14.). *Si quid petieritis*[^p438-10] *in nomine meo, hoc faciam;* et non iterat nomen Patris, ut ostendat, quod etiamsi petant ab ipso, obtineant, cum sit aequalis Patri; primae Ioannis tertio: « Si non reprehenderit nos cor nostrum, fiduciam habemus ad Deum; et quodcumque petierimus, accipiemus ab eo ».

### Quaestiones.

16. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur primo de hoc quod Dominus dicit discipulis: *Quo ego vado, scitis et viam scitis:*

1. Quia Thomas statim quaerit tanquam ignorans et dicit, se ignorare[^p438-11].

2. Item, Philippus quaerit de *termino,* scilicet de Patre, et in hoc ostendit, se ignorare: ergo aut Dominus dixit falsum, aut ipsi quaerebant quod sciebant.

Respondetur ad hoc tripliciter: primo modo, quod Dominus non de omnibus dicebat, sed de ali-
<!-- page 439 -->
quibus[^p439-1]. — Sed haec solutio non est conveniens, quia Dominus in praecedentibus excipiebat Iudam: *Non de omnibus dico.*

Aliter respondetur, quod sciebant et *viam* et *terminum,* sed non *sub illa ratione,* sub qua loquebatur, ut cognosco Coriscum, sed non in quantum est *veniens;* et ideo possum, Coriscum cognoscens, quaerere et dubitare de ipso sub ratione *venientis*[^p439-2].

Alio modo dicitur, quod sciebant, sed non advertebant vel actualiter considerabant[^p439-3]; et ideo Philippus reprehensus fuit de inconsideratione.

17. Quaest. II. Sed tunc quaeritur de responsione Domini ad Thomam: *Ego sum via, veritas et vita.* — Quomodo idem ipse, qui *vadit,* est *via?* Quomodo etiam ipse est *vita* et *terminus?* Hoc valde videtur inconveniens.

Dicendum, quod ipse est, *qui vadit,* et *per quem itur,* et *ad quem pervenitur;* et ita est *veritas,* quae vadit; *via,* per quam itur; *vita,* ad quam pervenitur. Et hoc propter pluralitatem naturarum: quia *homo,* vadit; quia *Deus,* terminat; quia *mediator Dei et hominum*[^p439-4], facit viam.

18. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de responsione, quam dat Philippo: *Tanto tempore vobiscum sum, et non cognovisti me?*

Videtur ex hoc, quod Philippus nondum credidisset in Christum. — Sed tunc quomodo dictum est supra[^p439-5] ei, quod esset mundus, cum nullus sit mundus nisi per fidem? — *Si tu dicas,* quod credebat; quomodo ergo, cum Patrem et Filium dicat esse unius essentiae, quomodo *videre Patrem* quaerebat? Videtur, quod contrarium fidei crediderit.

Ad hoc dicendum, quod Philippus credebat in eum et credebat aequalem Patri et unum in substantia; *in habitu* hoc credebat. Sed quia tunc Filium de Patre distincte loqui audiebat, volebat eum et quaerebat *distincte videre;* quod non quaesivisset, si[^p439-6] considerasset essentiae unitatem. Ideo ex tali subreptione venialiter peccavit, et ideo reprehenditur a Domino.

19. Quaest. IV. Item, quid est quod dicit: *Qui videt me videt et Patrem?*

Quia Filius visibilis factus est, sed Pater mansit invisibilis. — *Si tu dicas,* quod loquitur secundum Divinitatem; hoc nihil est: quia[^p439-7] *Deum nemo vidit unquam,* nec Patrem nec Filium, quia, si Filium, tunc et Patrem.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod loquitur de visione Divinitatis, non humanitatis. Sed hoc est dupliciter, scilicet per *fidem,* sicut *videmus nunc per speculum in aenigmate*[^p439-8]*;* et per *speciem.* Et loquitur hic de visione per *fidem;* quia nullus perfecte credit in Filium, quin etiam in Patrem.

20. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Ego in Patre, et Pater in me.* — *Contra:* Tunc arguitur ex hoc: ergo Pater est in Patre. — Quaeritur etiam: quare non recipitur ista: Pater est in Patre?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod haec praepositio *in,* quia *praepositio,* importat distinctionem[^p439-9]; quia vero *haec* praepositio, importat essentiae unitatem; ideo conceditur, quod Pater in Filio, et Filius in Patre. Sed non sequitur, quod Pater in Patre sit, immo est ibi *accidens,* sicut hic: ego sum similis tibi, et tu mihi: ergo ego sum similis mihi; *accidens,* quia *similitudo* importat simul convenientiam et distinctionem[^p439-10].

21. Quaest. VI. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Qui credit in me, opera, quae ego facio, et ipse faciet.*

1. Hoc falsum est: quia opera illa erant potentiae infinitae: ergo nullus illa facit nisi Deus.

2. Item, quid est quod dicit: *Maiora faciet?* Quia maximum est suscitare mortuum et foetidum mortuum; quomodo potest esse maius miraculum?
<!-- page 440 -->

3. Item, si non est *servus maior domino suo*[^p440-1], non est conveniens, quod Deus maiora opera faciat per servos Christi quam per ipsum Filium.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Dominus ad invocationem Apostolorum plura fecit miracula, quam per se ipsum fecerat.

1. Quod autem dicitur, quod credens *faciet:* hoc dicitur, quia ad ipsius invocationem fit; vel quia ab hominibus ipse facere putatur.

2. Quod iterum dicitur, quod *maiora;* dicendum, quod *maiora* non quantum ad factum, sed quantum ad *modum;* quia legitur Actuum quinto[^p440-2], quod ad umbram Petri sanabantur infirmi. Item dicitur de Apostolis, quod ad praesentiam indumentorum sanati et resuscitati sunt mortui.

3. Non tamen propter hoc servus maior domino, quia Apostoli *non faciebant;* non ad gloriam *suam,* sed ad gloriam *Christi*[^p440-3].

22. Quaest. VII. Iterum quaeritur de hoc, quod dicit: *Qui credit in me, opera, quae ego facio, et ipse faciet.* — Quaeritur, utrum intelligat de fide *informi,* aut *formata.*

Quod de *informi,* videtur: 1. Quia dicitur primae ad Corinthios decimo tertio[^p440-4]: *Si habuero fidem, ita ut montes transferam, caritatem autem non habeam* etc.: ergo per fidem informem sine caritate fiunt.

2. Item hoc videtur, quia multi mali fecerunt miracula, sicut dicitur Matthaei septimo[^p440-5].

**Sed contra** hoc est; quia peccator indignus est pane, quo vescitur[^p440-6]: ergo si habens fidem informem est in peccato, nullo modo meretur exaudiri.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod est exaudiri *de congruo* et *de condigno;* habens fidem *informem* exauditur frequenter et meretur exaudiri tantum *de congruo* quantum ad ea, de quibus habet fidem, quod Dominus exaudiat eum[^p440-7].

23. Quaest. VIII. Sed tunc quaeritur: si habentes fidem faciunt miracula; et hodie pauci aut nulli miracula faciunt: ergo pauci aut nulli habent fidem. — In huius attestationem adducitur quod dicitur Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p440-8]: *Si habueritis fidem et dixeritis monti huic: Transfer te in mare; fiet.* Sed quis est, qui posset hoc facere?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum secundum Gregorium[^p440-9] et Augustinum, quod duplicia sunt opera mirabilia: quaedam *sensibilia,* quaedam *spiritualia: sensibilia,* ut suscitare mortuos, et huiusmodi sunt necessaria ad astruendam fidem simplicium; sed *spiritualia* sunt suscitatio a morte spirituali per Sacramenta. Prima cessaverunt, quia fides est multiplicata; secunda semper manent propter nostram salutem. Unde intelligendum, quod Dominus loquebatur eis vel de mirabilibus *spiritualibus,* vel loquebatur pro tempore *Ecclesiae primitivae,* in quo gentiles et idiotae informandi erant ad fidem. Nihilominus tamen modo fiunt, sed Dominus arctavit manum, quia non est sic opus.

## English
<!-- page 435 -->

### Chapter XIV.

#### Second the disciples are confirmed by the word of erudition and consolation in three ways.

1. *And he said to his disciples*[^p435-8]*: Let not your heart be troubled* etc. Above, the Lord comforted his disciples by example; in this part he comforts them *by the word of erudition;* and this part contains three chapters and is divided into three. In the first part the Lord teaches them, that they may be constant *in faith;* in the second, *in dilection;* and this begins at the beginning of the fifteenth[^p435-9]: *I am the true vine;* in the third, *in the expectation of hope,* a little after the beginning of the sixteenth: *But these things to you from the beginning.*

#### First, three things are set down concerning constancy in faith.

The first part, which the present chapter contains, has three parts. In the first the Lord *exhorts to constant faith.* In the second, because « faith without works is dead[^p435-10] », he *exhorts to good operation,* there: *If you love me, keep my commandments.* In the third he *predicts the hastening of his withdrawal,* there: *These things have I spoken to you, abiding with you.*

First, then, he exhorts the disciples that on account of his withdrawal they *should not waver in faith,* showing them first the *mode* of withdrawing; second, the *way;* third, the *term;* fourth, the *fruit.*

(V. 1.). He exhorts, then, first, that, considering the *mode of withdrawing,* they should not be troubled over his withdrawal nor fail in faith, but rather believe. On this account he says: *Let not your heart be troubled,* but let it believe; hence: *You believe in God, believe also in me.* Let it not be troubled over the absence, but believe in the one absent; just as you believe in God, whom you do not see[^p435-11]. And the reason why they ought not to be troubled, but to believe, is this: namely that the Lord does not altogether withdraw from them, but goes before them, that he may take them up again to the glory in which
<!-- page 436 -->
many mansions are already *prepared* according to the eternal election, but are yet *to be prepared* according to merits; hence this word *prepare,* according to the exposition of Augustine on the letter[^p436-1], is taken according to these two significations. On this account the letter ought to be read thus:

2. (V. 2.). *In my Father's house there are many mansions,* already, namely, prepared according to the eternal election; because to the Ephesians, the first[^p436-2]: « He chose us before the foundation of the world »; Jerome: « There are prepared in heaven diverse mansions for diverse virtues, which not persons receive, but works ». Thus they are prepared. *If not,* that is, if it were not so; whence another text, Chrysostom's[^p436-3], where it is said: *If not,* says: « But if not », that is, if they were not prepared through predestination; *I would have told you: Because I go to prepare a place for you.* But this is impossible, that I should now prepare; because it is impossible that I should predestine anyone anew, as Augustine says[^p436-4]. Therefore I said that « the mansions are in the Father's house » already *prepared* through predestination, but they are yet *to be prepared* through the merit of faith, which has merit most of all in absence. Therefore he adds:

3. (V. 3.). *And if I shall go, and prepare a place for you,* by your faith, namely, in my absence; whence Augustine[^p436-5]: « Let the Lord Jesus go and prepare a place; let him go, that he may not be seen; let him be hidden, that he may be believed. For then is the place prepared, if one lives by faith. Let him, being believed, be desired, that being desired he may be possessed; the desire of dilection is the preparation of the mansion ». When I shall thus have prepared in absence, *I will come again, and will take you to myself,* by glory, namely, *that where I am, you also may be;* above, the twelfth[^p436-6]: « If any man minister to me, let him follow me, and where I am, there let my minister also be ». He exhorts them, then, to faith in absence, because he absents himself for this reason, that faith may have merit. So read Augustine[^p436-7] and the Gloss.

4. However, the whole can be expounded[^p436-8] in one signification of this word *prepare,* so that the sense is: *Let not your heart be troubled,* but *believe in me;* because *in my Father's house there are many mansions,* in which you shall rest together with me, to which I prepare the way; therefore you ought not to grieve. But then you would have to, *if not I had said,* that is, if I had not *said: Because I go to prepare a place for you, I will take you up again*[^p436-9] etc. But this I have said and do say; hence: *And if I shall go, and prepare a place for you, I will come again, and will take you* etc. Yet in whatever way it is read, it makes an exhortation to faith.

5. (V. 4.). *And whither I go you know.* Here is touched the second, namely the *way of the withdrawal.* And lest he should seem to be telling them something new about his withdrawal, he says that they themselves knew both the way and the term; therefore he says: *And whither I go you know, and the way you know*[^p436-10]. But
<!-- page 437 -->
some of them did not fully understand; therefore they ask, namely Thomas and Philip; hence:

6. (V. 5.). *Thomas saith to him: Lord, we know not whither thou goest; and how can we know the way?* Doubting Thomas[^p437-1] was slow to understand, and this for our advancement; because, just as the denial of Peter was of profit for taking away presumption, so the ignorance of Thomas for the strength of faith and for distinction. Therefore the Lord makes him certain about the way and the term; hence:

7. (V. 6.). *Jesus saith to him: I am the way, the truth*[^p437-2] *and the life. The truth* and *the life* as regards the term, because *truth* regards the intellect, *life* the affection. *I am the way* for those seeking, *the truth* for those finding, *the life* without death for those remaining[^p437-3]. — *I am the way* that does not stray; the Psalm[^p437-4]: « They wandered where there was no passing, and not in the way ». *I am the truth* that does not deceive; the Psalm: « The Lord is faithful in all his words ». *The life* unfailing; Luke, the tenth: « Mary hath chosen the best part, which shall not be taken away from her ». — *I am the way* that leads; Proverbs, the fourth[^p437-5]: « I will lead thee by the paths of equity, which when thou shalt have entered, thy steps shall not be straitened, and when thou runnest thou shalt not meet a stumblingblock ». *I am the truth* that shines; Malachias, the last: « Unto you that fear God the Sun of justice shall arise ». *The life* that feeds; above, the tenth: « By me, if any man enter in, he shall be saved; and he shall go in, and go out, and shall find pastures ». — *I am the way* by example; above, the thirteenth[^p437-6]: « I have given you an example, that as I have done to you, so you do also ». *I am the truth* in promise; the Psalm: « The words that proceed from my mouth I will not make void ». *I am the life* in reward; to the Romans, the sixth: « The wages of sin is death; but the grace of God, life everlasting ». And that he is the *way,* he shows: *No man cometh to the Father, but by me,* and by me he surely comes[^p437-7].

8. (V. 7.). *If you had known me, you would without doubt have known my Father also;* because, as is said above, the tenth[^p437-8], « I and the Father are one »; Matthew, the eleventh: « No one knoweth the Son but the Father, neither doth any one know the Father but the Son, and he to whom it shall please the Son to reveal him ». *And from henceforth you shall know him, and you have seen him; you shall know him,* namely at the coming of the Holy Spirit; below, the sixteenth[^p437-9]: « When he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will teach you all truth ». There shall be verified in you that of Jeremias, the thirty-first: « I will give my law in their bowels, and in their hearts I will write it ».

9. (V. 8.). *Philip saith to him.* Here is touched the third, namely the *term of Christ's withdrawal,* which Philip longed to see; on account of which *Philip saith to him: Lord, shew us the Father, and it is sufficient for us,* as if: we ask nothing further of you. For *he* is « all in all », the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth[^p437-10]. Not only *he,* but also *his grace;* the second to the Corinthians, the twelfth: « My grace is sufficient for thee ». And because Philip was asking what he ought not, therefore the Lord teaches Philip that he himself asks what he ought to have known; hence:

10. (V. 9.). *Jesus saith to him: Have I been so long a time with you, and hast thou not known*[^p437-11] *me?* As if to say:
<!-- page 438 -->
when you ask about the Father, you ask about me, whom you ought to know. Therefore he says: *Philip, he that seeth me seeth my Father also. How sayest thou: Shew us the Father? Seeth* with the eye of faith, according to which of Abraham, Genesis, the eighteenth[^p438-1], it is said that « he saw three and adored one ». And that he ought so to believe, he shows, because they are one; on account of which he says:

11. (V. 10.). *Dost thou not believe*[^p438-2]*, that I am in the Father, and the Father in me?* as one; as if to say: this you ought to believe. And this he manifests by the work: *The words that I speak to you, I speak not of myself: but the Father who abideth in me, he doth the works,* and so, if the operation is one, the substance also is one; above, the fifth[^p438-3]: « What things soever the Father doth, these the Son also doth in like manner »; and the eighth: « I do nothing of myself ».

12. (V. 11.). *Believe you not that I am in the Father, and the Father in me*[^p438-4]*?* Simply, without persuasion, as right faith believes.

13. (V. 12.). *Otherwise believe for the works themselves;* above, the tenth[^p438-5]: « If I do not the works of my Father, believe me not ». Whence Augustine: « If we were separated, we could in no way work inseparably ». And thus it has been explicated whither he had said he would go, because he was not going to the Father as to someone *outside himself;* but he wished to have himself believed equal to the Father. And this indeed the disciples already knew, because they believed him to be true God, and so equal to the Father in all things; otherwise he would not be true God[^p438-6].

*Amen, amen I say to you.* Here is touched the fourth, namely the *fruit of Christ's withdrawal* or utility in those believing; on account of which he says: *He that believeth in me, the works that I do, he also shall do; and greater than these shall he do;* Matthew, the seventeenth[^p438-7]: « If you have faith as a grain of mustard seed, you shall say to this mountain: Remove from hence hither, and it shall remove, and nothing shall be impossible to you ». And the reason of this is Christ's withdrawal from us; on this account he adds: *Because I go to the Father,* that is, that I may appear before the face of God « to make intercession for you[^p438-8] »; therefore the first of John, the second: « We have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ »; and therefore he says:

14. (V. 13.). *Whatsoever you shall ask the Father in my name, that will I do.* Therefore he says that we ought to ask of the Father in the name of the Son, *that* namely *the Father may be honoured*[^p438-9] *in the Son,* because the Son seeks not his own glory, but the Father's; on this account it was said above, the seventh: « He that seeketh the glory of him that sent him, he is true ». And for greater certitude, because a promise becomes firmer by a double affirmation; therefore he adds further:

15. (V. 14.). *If you shall ask*[^p438-10] *any thing in my name, that I will do;* and he does not repeat the name of the Father, to show that even if they ask of him, they obtain, since he is equal to the Father; the first of John, the third: « If our heart do not reprehend us, we have confidence towards God; and whatsoever we shall ask, we shall receive of him ».

### Questions.

16. Quaest. I. But it is asked first concerning this which the Lord says to the disciples: *Whither I go you know, and the way you know:*

1. Because Thomas immediately asks as one ignorant and says that he is ignorant[^p438-11].

2. Likewise, Philip asks about the *term,* namely about the Father, and in this shows that he is ignorant: therefore either the Lord said what is false, or they themselves were asking what they knew.

It is responded to this in three ways: in the first way, that the Lord was not speaking of all, but of
<!-- page 439 -->
some[^p439-1]. — But this solution is not fitting, because the Lord in what precedes excepted Judas: *I speak not of all.*

Otherwise it is responded that they knew both the *way* and the *term,* but not *under that reason* under which he was speaking, as I know Coriscus, but not insofar as he is *coming;* and therefore I can, knowing Coriscus, ask and doubt about him under the reason of *one coming*[^p439-2].

In another way it is said that they knew, but did not advert or actually consider[^p439-3]; and therefore Philip was reprehended for inconsideration.

17. Quaest. II. But then it is asked concerning the Lord's response to Thomas: *I am the way, the truth and the life.* — How is the same one, who *goes,* the *way?* How again is he the *life* and the *term?* This seems very unfitting.

It must be said that he is the one *who goes,* and *by whom one goes,* and *to whom one comes;* and thus he is the *truth,* which goes; the *way,* by which one goes; the *life,* to which one comes. And this on account of the plurality of natures: because he is *man,* he goes; because he is *God,* he terminates; because he is *mediator of God and men*[^p439-4], he makes the way.

18. Quaest. III. Likewise, it is asked concerning the response he gives to Philip: *Have I been so long a time with you, and hast thou not known me?*

It seems from this that Philip had not yet believed in Christ. — But then how was it said above[^p439-5] of him that he was clean, since no one is clean except by faith? — *If you say* that he believed; how then, since he says that the Father and the Son are of one essence, how was he seeking *to see the Father?* It seems that he believed what is contrary to the faith.

To this it must be said that Philip believed in him and believed him equal to the Father and one in substance; *in habit* he believed this. But because he then heard the Son speaking distinctly of the Father, he wished and sought *to see* him *distinctly;* which he would not have sought, if[^p439-6] he had considered the unity of essence. Therefore from such a surprise he sinned venially, and therefore he is reprehended by the Lord.

19. Quaest. IV. Likewise, what is it that he says: *He that seeth me seeth the Father also?*

Because the Son was made visible, but the Father remained invisible. — *If you say* that he speaks according to the Divinity; this is nothing: because[^p439-7] *no man hath seen God at any time,* neither the Father nor the Son, because, if the Son, then the Father also.

**I respond:** It must be said that he speaks of the vision of the Divinity, not of the humanity. But this is in two ways, namely by *faith,* as *we see now through a glass in a dark manner*[^p439-8]*;* and by *sight.* And he speaks here of the vision by *faith;* because no one perfectly believes in the Son without believing also in the Father.

20. Quaest. V. Likewise, it is asked concerning this which he says: *I in the Father, and the Father in me.* — *On the contrary:* Then it is argued from this: therefore the Father is in the Father. — It is asked also: why is this not accepted: The Father is in the Father?

**I respond:** It must be said that this preposition *in,* because it is a *preposition,* implies distinction[^p439-9]; but because it is *this* preposition, it implies unity of essence; therefore it is granted that the Father is in the Son, and the Son in the Father. But it does not follow that the Father is in the Father, rather there is there an *accident,* as here: I am like you, and you like me: therefore I am like myself; an *accident,* because *likeness* implies at once agreement and distinction[^p439-10].

21. Quaest. VI. Likewise, it is asked concerning this which he says: *He that believeth in me, the works that I do, he also shall do.*

1. This is false: because those works were of infinite power: therefore no one does them except God.

2. Likewise, what is it that he says: *Greater shall he do?* Because the greatest thing is to raise the dead, even the stinking dead; how can there be a greater miracle?
<!-- page 440 -->

3. Likewise, if *the servant is not greater than his lord*[^p440-1], it is not fitting that God should do greater works through the servants of Christ than through the Son himself.

**I respond:** It must be said that the Lord at the invocation of the Apostles did more miracles than he had done through himself.

1. As to what is said, that the believer *shall do:* this is said because it is done at his invocation; or because by men he himself is thought to do it.

2. As to what is said again, *greater;* it must be said that *greater* not as to the deed, but as to the *mode;* because one reads in Acts, the fifth[^p440-2], that at the shadow of Peter the infirm were healed. Likewise, it is said of the Apostles that at the presence of their garments the dead were healed and raised up.

3. Yet not on this account is the servant greater than the lord, because the Apostles *were not doing it;* not to *their own* glory, but to the glory *of Christ*[^p440-3].

22. Quaest. VII. Again, it is asked concerning this which he says: *He that believeth in me, the works that I do, he also shall do.* — It is asked whether he understands it of *unformed* faith, or of *formed.*

That it is of *unformed,* seems so: 1. Because it is said in the first to the Corinthians, the thirteenth[^p440-4]: *If I should have faith, so that I could remove mountains, and have not charity* etc.: therefore they are done by unformed faith without charity.

2. Likewise, this seems so, because many wicked men have done miracles, as is said in Matthew, the seventh[^p440-5].

**On the contrary** this is; because a sinner is unworthy of the bread that he eats[^p440-6]: therefore if one having unformed faith is in sin, he in no way merits to be heard.

**I respond:** It must be said that there is being heard *of congruity* and *of condignity;* one having *unformed* faith is frequently heard and merits to be heard only *of congruity* as to those things concerning which he has faith that the Lord hears him[^p440-7].

23. Quaest. VIII. But then it is asked: if those having faith do miracles; and today few or none do miracles: therefore few or none have faith. — In attestation of this is adduced what is said in Matthew, the twenty-first[^p440-8]: *If you shall have faith and shall say to this mountain: Remove thyself into the sea; it shall be done.* But who is there who could do this?

**I respond:** It must be said according to Gregory[^p440-9] and Augustine that wonderful works are of two kinds: some *sensible,* some *spiritual: sensible,* as raising the dead, and such are necessary for establishing the faith of the simple; but *spiritual* are the raising from spiritual death through the Sacraments. The first have ceased, because faith is multiplied; the second always remain on account of our salvation. Hence it must be understood that the Lord was speaking to them either of *spiritual* wonderful works, or was speaking for the time of the *primitive Church,* in which Gentiles and the unlettered had to be formed unto the faith. Nevertheless they are still done now, but the Lord has straitened his hand, because there is not such need.
