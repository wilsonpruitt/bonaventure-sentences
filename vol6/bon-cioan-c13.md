### Capitulum XIII. *Ante diem festum Paschae.*

### Collatio XLVIII.

(Vers. 2.). *Coena facta est.*

1. Nota, quod multiplex est *coena.* Est coena *gratiae, eucharistiae* et *gloriae.* Prima paratur interius in *thalamo conscientiae;* secunda, exterius in *tabernaculo Ecclesiae;* tertia, superius in *palatio gloriae.* Prima pertinet ad *incipientes,* secunda ad *proficientes,* tertia ad *pervenientes.* — De prima Apocalypsis tertio[^p597-3]: « Si quis mihi aperuerit », consentiendo, « intrabo ad eum », gratiam infundendo, « et coenabo cum illo, et ipse mecum », quia ipse debet ponere de suo opera virtutum, et ego de meo dona gratiarum. — De secunda coena, scilicet de coena *eucharistiae,* dicitur hic ad litteram: *Coena facta est;* primae ad Corinthios undecimo[^p597-4] de hac coena praedicat Apostolus: « Convenientibus vobis in unum, iam non est dominicam coenam manducare » etc. — De tertia, scilicet de coena *gloriae,* Lucae decimo quarto: « Homo quidam fecit coenam magnam et vocavit multos »; et Apocalypsis decimo nono: « Beati, qui ad coenam nuptiarum Agni vocati sunt ».

2. *Coena* ista facta est in Paschate. Et nota, quod secundum multiplicem differentiam *coenae* est multiplex differentia *paschae,* ita quod quaelibet coena potest dici *convivium paschale.* Pascha enim *transitus* est[^p597-5], et est *triplex* transitus, scilicet transitus *incipientium* et transitus *proficientium* et transitus *pervenientium.* — Primus transitus est a *vitiis* ad virtutes; secundus, a vita *activa* ad contemplativam; tertius, a *miseria* in gloriam. — De transitu *incipientium,* qui est a *vitiis ad virtutes,* dicitur in figura, primae ad Corinthios decimo[^p597-6]: « Patres nostri omnes mare transierunt »; et Isaiae vigesimo tertio: « Transi quasi flumen terram tuam, filia maris ». — De transitu *proficientium,* qui est a *vita activa ad contemplativam,* dicitur Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto: « Transite ad me, omnes, qui concupiscitis me, et a generationibus meis implemini ». — De transitu *pervenientium,* qui est a *miseria in gloriam,* dicitur Michaeae secundo[^p597-7]: « Transibit rex eorum coram eis, et Dominus in capite eorum »; quod factum est in ascensione. De hoc dicitur in Ioannis quinto, quod fidelis « in iudicium non veniet, sed transibit de morte ad vitam »; et in Psalmo: « Transibo ad locum tabernaculi admirabilis usque ad domum Dei ». Vel intellige hoc de transitu praecedenti. — Attende ergo triplicem transitum, scilicet transitum *incipientium,* transitum *proficientium* et transitum *pervenientium;* et iste est triplex transitus paschalis; quorum primus est per *mare* contritionis, secundus per *desertum* religionis, tertius per *Iordanem* mortis; et sic pervenitur ad terram promissionis[^p597-8].

3. Et nota, quod iste triplex transitus significatur per triplex *pascha* celebratum a Iudaeis. Legimus enim, Iudaeos celebrasse pascha, Exodi duodecimo[^p597-9] quasi per totum; unde leguntur ibi comedisse victimam *transitus Domini,* et significat hoc pascha transitum *incipientium,* quia post illud pascha statim egredientes de Aegypto, transierunt mare rubrum. — Fecerunt etiam pascha filii Israel in *deserto* tempore suo, quarta decima die ad vesperum[^p597-10], in monte Sinai; et significat hoc pascha transitum *proficientium,* quia factum est a filiis Israel progredientibus per desertum. — Tertium pascha
<!-- page 598 -->
fecerunt filii Israel in *ingressu* terrae promissionis, secundum quod habetur Iosue quinto[^p598-1]: « Manserunt filii Israel in Galgalis et fecerunt phase quarta decima die ad vesperum in campestribus Iericho et comederunt de frugibus terrae » etc.; et significat hoc pascha transitum *pervenientium,* quia factum est, transito Iordane, in terra promissionis. — Sic ergo est triplex pascha, in quo paratur fidelibus triplex coena.

### Collatio XLIX.

(Vers. 4.). *Et cum accepisset linteum, praecinxit se.*

1. Nota, quod, per appropriationem loquendo, *succingimur* ad eundum — *accingimur* ad pugnandum — *praecingimur* ad ministrandum. — *Succingimur ad eundum;* Ecclesiastici trigesimo sexto[^p598-2]: « Quasi succinctus latro, exsiliens de civitate »; Ioannis vigesimo primo dicitur de Petro: « Cum audisset, quia Dominus est, tunica succinxit se et misit se in mare ». — *Accingimur ad pugnandum;* unde Psalmus: « Accingere gladio tuo super femur tuum, potentissime »; et primi Machabaeorum tertio: « Accingimini, dixit Iudas, et estote potentes et estote parati in mane, ut pugnetis adversus nationes » etc. — *Praecingimur ad ministrandum;* unde Lucae duodecimo[^p598-3]: « Praecinget se et faciet illos discumbere, et transiens ministrabit illis »; de quo etiam dicitur hic: *Cum accepisset linteum, praecinxit se.* — Praecingamur ergo *linteo munditiae,* ut Deo ministremus; et accingamur *gladio iustitiae,* ut diabolum expugnemus; et succingamur *tunica passionis* mundanae, ut mundum contemnamus, et sic Deo placebimus.

2. *Et praecinxit,* inquit, *se.* Nota, quod tria sunt in nobis praecingenda. Praecingendi sunt *renes;* quod figuratum est quarti Regum primo[^p598-4] de Elia, de quo ibidem dicitur, qui erat « vir pilosus et zona pellicea accinctus renes ». — Praecingendi sunt *lumbi;* Lucae duodecimo: « Sint lumbi vestri praecincti » etc. — Praecingendum est *pectus;* quod figuratum est, Apocalypsis primo, per illum quem vidit Ioannes « praecinctum ad mamillas zona aurea ». — Per *pectus* intellige mentem, per *renes* affectiones, per *lumbos* intellige carnem; quorum praecinctio significat triplicem munditiam: praecinctio *pectoris,* pudicitiam *cogitationis;* praecinctio *renum,* pudicitiam *affectionis;* praecinctio *lumborum,* continentiam *carnis.* « Lumbos enim praecingimus, ut ait Gregorius[^p598-5], cum carnis luxuriam per continentiam coarctamus ». — Sic ergo praecincti simus, ut non sit mala *cogitatio* in pectore nec mala *affectio* in voluntate nec malus *motus* in carne.

3. *Praecinxit,* inquit, *se.* Nota, quod legimus in Scriptura aliquem praecinctum *pelle;* aliquem, *fune;* aliquem, *linteo;* aliquem, *auro.* — In *pelle* significatur mortificatio carnis; in *fune,* observantia regularis; in *linteo,* munditia castitatis; in *zona aurea,* vinculum caritatis; quibus praecingi debet anima fidelis. — Praecingitur ergo quis *pelle;* unde quarti Regum primo[^p598-6] dicitur de Elia, quod erat « zona pellicea accinctus renes »; et idem dicitur de Ioanne Baptista, Marci primo. — Praecingitur quis *fune;* Iob duodecimo: « Balteum regum dissolvit et praecingit fune renes eorum ». — Praecingitur quis *linteo;* Ioannis decimo tertio[^p598-7] de Iesu: *Cum accepisset linteum, praecinxit se.* — Praecingitur quis *auro;* Apocalypsis primo de Filio hominis, quem vidit Ioannes « praecinctum ad mamillas zona aurea ». In quo significatur, quod debemus cingi *pelle mortificationis* in carne, *fune observantiae regularis* in conversatione, *linteo castitatis* in mente, *zona caritatis* ubique.

### Collatio L.

(Vers. 6.). *Domine, tu mihi lavas pedes?*

1. In verbis istis duo admiratur Petrus: *excellentiam Domini,* cum dicit: *Domine; humilitatem obsequii,* cum subiungit: *Tu mihi lavas pedes?*

*Tu,* inquit, *mihi lavas pedes?* Nota, quod tria lavanda sunt in nobis. Lavandum est enim *caput, pedes* et *manus.* — Lavandum est *caput intentionis;* Isaiae primo[^p598-8]: « Lavamini, mundi estote, auferte malum cogitationum vestrarum ab oculis meis »; et Ieremiae quarto: « Lava a malitia cor tuum, Ierusalem, ut salva fias ». — Lavandi etiam sunt *pedes affectionis;* Canticorum quinto[^p598-9]: « Lavi pedes meos, quomodo coinquinabo illos »? Et Ioannis decimo tertio: « Qui lotus est non indiget, nisi ut pedes lavet, sed est mundus totus ». — Lavandae sunt praeterea *manus operationis;* Psalmus: « Lavabo inter innocentes manus meas, et circumdabo altare tuum, Domine ». — Sic ergo tria lavanda sunt in nobis; de quibus ait Petrus Ioannis decimo tertio: « Domine, non tantum pedes, sed et manus et caput ».

2. Haec autem tria lavanda sunt triplici lotione. Lavatur enim aliquid ad *emundationem sordis* primo,
<!-- page 599 -->
secundo ad *intensionem candoris,* tertio ad *perfectionem decoris.* — Per hoc intellige, quod primo lavanda est anima ad *emundationem sordis,* et hoc in aquis *contritionis;* Psalmus[^p599-1]: « Lavabo per singulas noctes lectum meum, lacrymis meis » etc.; et iterum: « Amplius lava me ab iniquitate mea, et a peccato meo munda me ». — Secundo lavanda est ad *intensionem candoris,* et hoc in aquis *devotionis;* Psalmus: « Lavabis me, et super nivem dealbabor ». Haec lotio figurata est Danielis decimo tertio, quando « ingressa est Susanna cum duabus puellis solis, ut lavaretur in pomario ». — Tertio lavanda est ad *perfectionem decoris,* et hoc in sanguine passionis, id est in imitatione; Apocalypsis ultimo[^p599-2]: « Beati, qui lavant stolas suas in sanguine Agni, ut per portas ingrediantur in civitatem » etc. De talibus quaeritur Apocalypsis septimo: « Hi qui amicti sunt stolis albis, qui sunt et unde venerunt »? « Et dixit mihi: Hi sunt, qui venerunt de tribulatione magna et laverunt stolas suas et dealbaverunt eas in sanguine Agni ».

### Collatio LI.

(Vers. 10.). *Et vos mundi estis, sed non omnes.*

1. In verbis istis breviter *commendantur electi,* cum dicitur: *Et vos mundi estis.* — A commendatione *excluduntur reprobi,* cum subiungitur: *Sed non omnes.*

*Commendans* ergo Apostolos, Veritas ait: *Vos mundi estis;* mundi, inquam, munditia *cordis* — munditia *oris* — munditia *operis* — et munditia *conversationis.* Vulgariter enim solemus mundare quatuor, scilicet faciem, os, manus et vestes. — Per hunc modum lavant iusti *faciem cordis;* Tobiae tertio[^p599-3] ait Sara: « Tu scis, Domine, quia nunquam concupivi virum et mundam servavi animam meam ab omni concupiscentia »; de hoc secundae ad Corinthios septimo: « Mundemus nos ab omni inquinamento carnis et spiritus, perficientes sanctificationem ». Hanc petebat David in Psalmo, cum dicebat: « Cor mundum crea in me, Deus »; hoc praecipitur Isaiae primo: « Lavamini, mundi estote » etc. — Item, mundum habent *sermonem oris,* quod habebat Iob, undecimo[^p599-4], qui dicebat: « Purus est, Domine, sermo meus, et mundus sum in conspectu tuo ». Hanc monet Apostolus ad Ephesios quinto: « Fornicatio et immunditia non nominetur in vobis ». — Item, mundas habent *manus operis,* quod habebat ille, Genesis vigesimo, qui dicebat: « In simplicitate cordis mei et in munditia manuum mearum feci hoc »; et secundi Regum vigesimo secundo: « Restituet mihi Dominus secundum iustitiam meam, et secundum munditiam manuum mearum in conspectu oculorum eius ». De hac Iob vigesimo secundo: « Salvabitur innocens munditia manuum suarum ». — Item, mundam habent iusti *vestem conversationis,* ad quam hortabatur Iacob filios suos, dicens Genesis trigesimo quinto[^p599-5]: « Abiicite deos alienos, qui in medio vestri sunt, et mundamini et mutate vestimenta vestra ». — Sic ergo habent munditiam *vultus,* munditiam *oris,* munditiam *manuum* et munditiam *vestium* Sancti, quibus dicitur a Domino: *Vos mundi estis.*

2. Notandum est autem quadruplex bonum, quod facit munditia spiritualis: *servat* innocentes, *expedit* laborantes, *gratificat* contemplantes, *remunerat* pervenientes. — *Servat innocentes* a peccati contagione, sicut vides, quod aliquid tersum et politum non de facili contrahit rubiginem; Iob vigesimo secundo[^p599-6]: « Salvabitur innocens munditia manuum suarum ». — *Expedit laborantes* in actione, sicut diceres, quod aliquis, reiectis superfluitatibus et lotis manibus, paratus est ad serviendum; ideo in Psalmo quaeritur: « Quis ascendet in montem Domini »? id est in eminentiam virtutis; et sequitur: « Innocens manibus et mundo corde » etc. — *Gratificat contemplantes* in oratione; Proverbiorum vigesimo secundo[^p599-7]: « Qui diligit cordis munditiam propter gratiam labiorum suorum habebit amicum regem »; Iob octavo: « Si mundus et rectus incesseris, statim evigilabit ad te ». — *Remunerat pervenientes* beata visione, quae est merces gloriae; Matthaei quinto: « Beati mundo corde, quoniam ipsi Deum videbunt ».

3. *Vos,* inquit, *mundi estis,* et adiungit: *Sed non omnes.* Ideo mali de mundorum[^p599-8] numero excluduntur, quia multipliciter *polluuntur.* Polluit enim hominem mala *delectatio,* malus *consensus,* malum *opus* et mala *consuetudo.* Haec quatuor auferunt munditiam spiritualem et significata sunt in Lege per quatuor, quae auferebant munditiam *legalem.* Polluebat enim hominem secundum Legem immundus *contactus,* immundus *cibus,* immundus *locus* et immundus *morbus.* — Polluebat immundus *contactus,* ut quando aliquis tangebat morticinum; Levitici undecimo[^p599-9] quasi per totum:. « Quicumque morticinum tetigerit, polluetur et immundus erit usque ad vesperum »; eodem: « Super quod ceciderit quidquam
<!-- page 600 -->
de morticinis immundum erit ». — Polluebat immundus *cibus,* ut quando aliquis gustabat immundum; Levitici undecimo[^p600-1]: « Quicumque comederit ex eo quidpiam sive portaverit, lavabit vestimenta sua et immundus erit usque ad vesperum »; loquitur de morticino; infra, postquam determinavit cibos immundos: « Nolite contaminare animas vestras » etc. — Polluebat immundus *locus,* ut quando aliquis incedebat per locum immundum; primi Regum vigesimo primo[^p600-2]: « Si mundi sunt pueri, maxime a mulieribus »? ait Abimelech. « Et respondit David: Si de mulieribus agitur, continuimus nos ab heri et nudiustertius. Verumtamen via haec polluta est ». Item, propter loci munditiam legalem praeceptum est, ut comedantur oblata « in loco mundissimo », Levitici decimo[^p600-3]. — Polluebat immundus *morbus,* sicut de lepra, Levitici decimo tertio, et de fluxu seminis, Levitici decimo quinto, et huiusmodi.

Nota *moraliter,* quod immundum *tangit* homo in mala *delectatione;* immundum *gustat* in malo *consensu;* in immundum *incedit* in malo *opere;* immundum morbum *incurrit* in mala *consuetudine.*

4. Recuperatur autem munditia spiritualis per quatuor. Recuperatur per *dolorem* contritionis, per *pudorem* confessionis, per *laborem* satisfactionis, per *flammam* tribulationis; et ista quatuor significata sunt per quatuor, quae erant mundativa secundum Legem, scilicet per *aquam,* per *scopam,* per *oblationem* et per *ignem.* — Mundat *aqua;* Levitici vigesimo secundo[^p600-4]. « Cum laverit immundus carnem suam aqua », scilicet poenitentiae, « et occubuerit sol », id est ardor concupiscentiae; « tunc mundatus vescetur sanctificatis », id est donis gratiae; Ezechielis trigesimo sexto: « Effundam super vos aquam mundam, et mundabimini ab omnibus inquinamentis vestris ». — Mundat *scopa;* Lucae undecimo[^p600-5] et Matthaei duodecimo: « Invenit eam scopis mundatam et ornatam »; ita aliquando mundabatur altare, cum removebantur cineres, et quasi ita mundatum est gazophylacium; Nehemiae ultimo: « Proieci, inquit, vasa domus Tobiae », id est peccata, « foras de gazophylacio, et praecepi, et mundaverunt gazophylacium, et retuli ibi vasa Domini », id est instrumenta virtutum; quae omnia fiunt in confessione; Psalmus: « Exercitabar et scopebam spiritum meum ». — Mundat *oblatio,* Levitici duodecimo[^p600-6], de oblatione puerperae, item Levitici decimo quarto, de oblatione leprosi, per quae omnia significatur oblatio boni operis, de qua Lucae undecimo: « Date eleemosynam, et ecce, omnia munda sunt vobis ». — Mundat *ignis;* Malachiae tertio[^p600-7]: « Ipse quasi ignis conflans et quasi herba fullonum, et sedebit conflans et emundans argentum et purgabit filios Levi »; Numerorum trigesimo primo: « Quod potest transire per flammas igne purgabitur ».

*Moraliter* peccatorem mundat aqua *contritionis,* scopa *confessionis,* oblatio *bonae operationis,* flamma *tribulationis.*

5. Nota, quod ad munditiam debet hominem pollutum movere *pudor, timor, amor* et *honor.* — *Pudor opprobrii;* Levitici decimo tertio[^p600-8] de *leproso:* « Habebit vestimenta dissuta, caput nudum, os veste contectum, contaminatum ac sordidum se clamabit, et solus habitabit extra castra ». — *Timor supplicii;* Ieremiae quadragesimo quarto: « Non sunt mundati usque ad diem hanc, et non timuerunt Deum neque ambulaverunt in lege Domini » etc.; et sequitur: « Ecce, ego ponam faciem meam in vobis in malum et disperdam omnem Iudam » etc. — *Amor praemii;* Psalmus[^p600-9]: « Retribuet mihi Dominus secundum iustitiam meam, et secundum puritatem manuum mearum » etc.; secundi Regum vigesimo secundo dicitur: « Secundum munditiam manuum mearum in conspectu oculorum eius »; Matthaei quinto: « Beati mundo corde ». — *Honor officii;* Isaiae quinquagesimo secundo: « Mundamini, qui fertis vasa Domini »; Nehemiae duodecimo: « Mundati sunt sacerdotes et levitae, et mundaverunt populum ».

6. *Mundi estis, sed non omnes.* Dicas aliter: Debet homo munditiam diligere propter *se,* propter *proximum,* propter *Dominum* suum, propter *officium* suum. — Propter *se ipsum,* ut munde *vivat,* quoniam « omnia sunt munda mundis, coinquinatis autem et infidelibus nihil est mundum », ad Titum primo[^p600-10]. — Propter *proximum* suum, ne ipsum *polluat;* « Omne enim, quod tetigerit immundus, immundum erit »; Levitici decimo quinto: « Qui tetigerit lectum eius, qui carnem, qui sederit, ubi ille sedit » etc.; dicitur de patiente fluxum seminis. — Propter *Dominum* suum, ne *offendat* ipsum; Isaiae quinquagesimo secundo[^p600-11]: « Mundamini, qui fertis vasa Domini ». Displicet enim Domino immunditia servi; Habacuc primo: « Mundi sunt oculi tui, ne videas malum, et ad iniquitatem respicere non poteris »; Isaiae primo: « Cum multiplicaveritis orationem, non exaudiam, manus enim vestrae sanguine plenae sunt ».
<!-- page 601 -->
— Propter *officium* suum, ut ipsum *digne perficiat;* Nehemiae duodecimo[^p601-1]: « Mundati sunt sacerdotes et levitae, et mundaverunt populum »; Numerorum decimo nono: « Mundus lustrabit immundum »; Ecclesiastici trigesimo quarto: « Ab immundo quid mundabitur »?

### Annotatio 52.

(Vers. 13.). *Vos vocatis me magister et domine.*

Nota, quod aliquando Dominus fuit vocatus *magister;* Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p601-2]: « Magister, scimus, quia verax es ». — Aliquando *dominus;* Ioannis sexto: « Domine, ad quem ibimus? Verba vitae aeternae habes ». — Aliquando *praeceptor;* Lucae quinto:. « Praeceptor, per totam noctem laborantes nihil cepimus » etc. — Et congrue; ipse enim est dominus, qui cuncta creavit *potenter,* ordinavit *sapienter* et disposuit *suaviter.*

### Annotatio 53.

(Vers. 15.). *Exemplum dedi vobis.*

Nota, quod Dominus dedit nobis exemplum in *agendis,* in *sustinendis,* in *cavendis* vel contemnendis. — Exemplum dedit nobis in *agendis,* Ioannis decimo tertio[^p601-3]; unde hic: « Exemplum dedi vobis, ut, quemadmodum feci vobis, ita et vos faciatis ». — In *sustinendis;* primae Petri secundo: « Christus passus est pro nobis, vobis relinquens exemplum, ut sequamini vestigia eius ». — In *cavendis;* docuit enim nos mundum contemnere noster secundus Adam spiritualiter vivendo, quemadmodum docuit nos quaerere primus Adam vivendo carnaliter. Et ideo Zachariae decimo tertio[^p601-4]: « Adam exemplum meum ab adolescentia mea »; quod intelligitur de secundo Adam, ad cuius imitationem hortatur nos Apostolus primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto dicens: « Primus homo de terra terrenus, secundus homo de caelo caelestis »; et post paululum: « Sicut portavimus imaginem terreni, sic portemus imaginem caelestis ».

### Annotatio 54.

(Vers. 21.). *Unus ex vobis me tradet.*

Nota, quod Christum tradidit *Deus Pater,* et hoc ex *pietate;* ad Romanos octavo[^p601-5]: « Proprio Filio suo non pepercit, sed pro nobis omnibus tradidit illum ». — Tradidit semetipsum *ipse Christus,* et hoc ex *caritate;* primae Petri secundo: « Tradebat autem se ipsum iudicanti se iniuste ». — Tradidit *Iudas,* et hoc ex *cupiditate;* Matthaei vigesimo sexto: « Quid vultis mihi dare, et ego eum vobis tradam »? — Tradiderunt *Iudaei,* et hoc ex *invidia;* Ioannis decimo octavo[^p601-6]: « Gens tua et pontifices tui tradiderunt te mihi ». — Tradidit ipsum *Pilatus,* et hoc ex *iniustitia;* Ioannis decimo nono: « Tunc ergo tradidit eis illum, ut crucifigeretur ».

### Annotatio 55.

(Vers. 23.). *Erat ergo recumbens unus ex discipulis eius in sinu Iesu.*

Nota, quod quatuor personas specialiter nominant Evangelia, scilicet *Mariam* plorantem, Lucae septimo[^p601-7] et Ioannis vigesimo; *Martham* ministrantem, Lucae decimo; *Petrum* super mare ambulantem, Matthaei decimo quarto; *Ioannem* in sinu Iesu dormientem, Ioannis decimo tertio; unde hic: *Recumbens* etc. — Per *Mariam plorantem* intellige poenitentes; per *Martham ministrantem* intellige obedientes; haec duo pertinent ad *activos.* Per *Petrum supra mare ambulantem* intellige mundum contemnentes; per *Ioannem in sinu Iesu dormientem* intellige devotioni insistentes; quae duo pertinent ad *contemplativos.*

### Annotatio 56.

(Vers. 36.). *Quo ego vado, non potes sequi me modo, sequeris autem postea.*

### Chapter XIII. *Before the festival day of the pasch.*

### Collation XLVIII.

(Vers. 2.). *Supper was done.*

1. Note that the *supper* is manifold. There is a supper of *grace,* of the *eucharist* and of *glory.* The first is prepared within, in the *bridechamber of conscience;* the second, without, in the *tabernacle of the Church;* the third, above, in the *palace of glory.* The first belongs to *beginners,* the second to the *proficient,* the third to *those who arrive.* — Of the first, the Apocalypse, the third chapter[^p597-3]: « If any man shall open to me », by consenting, « I will come in to him », by pouring in grace, « and will sup with him, and he with me », because he must put in of his own the works of the virtues, and I of mine the gifts of graces. — Of the second supper, namely of the supper of the *eucharist,* it is said here according to the letter: *Supper was done;* in the first to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter[^p597-4], the Apostle preaches of this supper: « When you come therefore together into one place, it is not now to eat the Lord's supper » etc. — Of the third, namely of the supper of *glory,* Luke, the fourteenth chapter: « A certain man made a great supper and invited many »; and the Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter: « Blessed are they that are called to the marriage supper of the Lamb ».

2. This *supper* was done at the Pasch. And note that according to the manifold difference of the *supper* there is a manifold difference of the *pasch,* so that any supper whatever can be called a *paschal banquet.* For the pasch is a *passage*[^p597-5], and the passage is *threefold,* namely the passage of *beginners* and the passage of the *proficient* and the passage of *those who arrive.* — The first passage is from *vices* to virtues; the second, from the *active* life to the contemplative; the third, from *misery* into glory. — Of the passage of *beginners,* which is from *vices to virtues,* it is said in figure, in the first to the Corinthians, the tenth chapter[^p597-6]: « Our fathers all passed through the sea »; and Isaias, the twenty-third chapter: « Pass as a river thy land, O daughter of the sea ». — Of the passage of the *proficient,* which is from *the active life to the contemplative,* it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter: « Pass over to me, all ye that desire me, and be filled with my fruits ». — Of the passage of *those who arrive,* which is from *misery into glory,* it is said in Micheas, the second chapter[^p597-7]: « Their king shall pass before them, and the Lord at the head of them »; which was done in the ascension. Of this it is said in John, the fifth chapter, that the faithful man « shall not come into judgement, but shall pass from death to life »; and in the Psalm: « I shall pass to the place of the wonderful tabernacle, even to the house of God ». Or understand this of the preceding passage. — Attend therefore to the threefold passage, namely the passage of *beginners,* the passage of the *proficient* and the passage of *those who arrive;* and this is the threefold paschal passage; of which the first is through the *sea* of contrition, the second through the *desert* of religion, the third through the *Jordan* of death; and so one arrives at the land of promise[^p597-8].

3. And note that this threefold passage is signified by the threefold *pasch* celebrated by the Jews. For we read that the Jews celebrated the pasch, in Exodus, the twelfth chapter[^p597-9], almost throughout; hence they are read there to have eaten the victim of the *passage of the Lord,* and this pasch signifies the passage of *beginners,* because after that pasch, going forth straightway out of Egypt, they passed through the Red Sea. — The children of Israel also kept the pasch in the *desert* in its season, on the fourteenth day at evening[^p597-10], at mount Sinai; and this pasch signifies the passage of the *proficient,* because it was kept by the children of Israel as they went forward through the desert. — The third pasch
<!-- page 598 -->
the children of Israel kept at the *entry* into the land of promise, according to what is had in Josue, the fifth chapter[^p598-1]: « The children of Israel abode in Galgal and kept the phase on the fourteenth day at evening in the plains of Jericho, and they ate of the fruits of the land » etc.; and this pasch signifies the passage of *those who arrive,* because it was kept, the Jordan having been passed, in the land of promise. — So therefore there is a threefold pasch, in which a threefold supper is prepared for the faithful.

### Collation XLIX.

(Vers. 4.). *And when he had taken a towel, he girded himself about.*

1. Note that, speaking by appropriation, we are *girt up* for going — we are *girded* for fighting — we are *girded about* for ministering. — *We are girt up for going;* Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-sixth chapter[^p598-2]: « As a robber girt up, leaping forth from the city »; in John, the twenty-first chapter, it is said of Peter: « When he heard that it was the Lord, he girt himself up with his coat and cast himself into the sea ». — *We are girded for fighting;* hence the Psalm: « Gird thy sword upon thy thigh, O thou most mighty »; and the first of Machabees, the third chapter: « Gird yourselves, said Judas, and be mighty and be ready in the morning, that you may fight against the nations » etc. — *We are girded about for ministering;* hence Luke, the twelfth chapter[^p598-3]: « He will gird himself about and make them sit down to meat, and passing will minister unto them »; of which also it is said here: *When he had taken a towel, he girded himself about.* — Let us therefore be girded about with the *towel of cleanness,* that we may minister to God; and let us be girded with the *sword of justice,* that we may overcome the devil; and let us be girt up with the *coat of suffering* in the world, that we may despise the world, and so we shall please God.

2. *And he girded himself about,* he says. Note that there are three things in us to be girded about. The *reins* are to be girded about; which is figured in the fourth of Kings, the first chapter[^p598-4], of Elias, of whom it is said in the same place that he was « a hairy man, and girded with a girdle of leather about his reins ». — The *loins* are to be girded about; Luke, the twelfth chapter: « Let your loins be girded about » etc. — The *breast* is to be girded about; which is figured, in the Apocalypse, the first chapter, by him whom John saw « girded about the paps with a golden girdle ». — By the *breast* understand the mind, by the *reins* the affections, by the *loins* understand the flesh; the girding about of which signifies a threefold cleanness: the girding about of the *breast,* the modesty of *thought;* the girding about of the *reins,* the modesty of *affection;* the girding about of the *loins,* the continence of the *flesh.* « For we gird about the loins, as Gregory says[^p598-5], when we restrain the wantonness of the flesh by continence ». — So therefore let us be girded about, that there be no evil *thought* in the breast nor evil *affection* in the will nor evil *motion* in the flesh.

3. *He girded himself about,* he says. Note that we read in Scripture of one girded about with *skin;* another, with a *cord;* another, with a *towel;* another, with *gold.* — In the *skin* is signified the mortification of the flesh; in the *cord,* regular observance; in the *towel,* the cleanness of chastity; in the *golden girdle,* the bond of charity; with which the faithful soul ought to be girded about. — One is girded about, then, with *skin;* hence in the fourth of Kings, the first chapter[^p598-6], it is said of Elias that he was « girded with a girdle of leather about his reins »; and the same is said of John the Baptist, Mark, the first chapter. — One is girded about with a *cord;* Job, the twelfth chapter: « He looseth the belt of kings and girdeth about their reins with a cord ». — One is girded about with a *towel;* John, the thirteenth chapter[^p598-7], of Jesus: *When he had taken a towel, he girded himself about.* — One is girded about with *gold;* the Apocalypse, the first chapter, of the Son of man, whom John saw « girded about the paps with a golden girdle ». In which it is signified that we ought to be girded with the *skin of mortification* in the flesh, with the *cord of regular observance* in our way of life, with the *towel of chastity* in the mind, with the *girdle of charity* everywhere.

### Collation L.

(Vers. 6.). *Lord, dost thou wash my feet?*

1. In these words Peter wonders at two things: the *excellence of the Lord,* when he says: *Lord; the humility of the service,* when he adds: *Dost thou wash my feet?*

*Dost thou,* he says, *wash my feet?* Note that three things in us are to be washed. For the *head, feet* and *hands* are to be washed. — The *head of intention* is to be washed; Isaias, the first chapter[^p598-8]: « Wash yourselves, be clean, take away the evil of your thoughts from my eyes »; and Jeremias, the fourth chapter: « Wash thy heart from wickedness, O Jerusalem, that thou mayst be saved ». — The *feet of affection* are also to be washed; Canticles, the fifth chapter[^p598-9]: « I have washed my feet, how shall I defile them »? And John, the thirteenth chapter: « He that is washed needeth not but to wash his feet, but is clean wholly ». — The *hands of operation* are moreover to be washed; the Psalm: « I will wash my hands among the innocent, and will compass thy altar, O Lord ». — So therefore three things in us are to be washed; of which Peter says, John, the thirteenth chapter: « Lord, not only the feet, but also the hands and the head ».

2. Now these three are to be washed with a threefold washing. For a thing is washed for the *cleansing away of filth* first,
<!-- page 599 -->
second for the *heightening of whiteness,* third for the *perfecting of beauty.* — By this understand that first the soul is to be washed for the *cleansing away of filth,* and this in the waters of *contrition;* the Psalm[^p599-1]: « Every night I will wash my bed, with my tears » etc.; and again: « Wash me yet more from my iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin ». — Second it is to be washed for the *heightening of whiteness,* and this in the waters of *devotion;* the Psalm: « Thou shalt wash me, and I shall be made whiter than snow ». This washing is figured in Daniel, the thirteenth chapter, when « Susanna went in with two maids only, to wash herself in the orchard ». — Third it is to be washed for the *perfecting of beauty,* and this in the blood of the passion, that is, in imitation; the Apocalypse, the last chapter[^p599-2]: « Blessed are they that wash their robes in the blood of the Lamb, that they may enter in by the gates into the city » etc. Of such it is asked in the Apocalypse, the seventh chapter: « These that are clothed in white robes, who are they, and whence came they »? « And he said to me: These are they who are come out of great tribulation and have washed their robes and have made them white in the blood of the Lamb ».

### Collation LI.

(Vers. 10.). *And you are clean, but not all.*

1. In these words, briefly, *the elect are commended,* when it is said: *And you are clean.* — *The reprobate are excluded* from the commendation, when it is added: *But not all.*

*Commending,* then, the Apostles, the Truth says: *You are clean;* clean, I say, with cleanness of *heart* — cleanness of *mouth* — cleanness of *work* — and cleanness of *way of life.* For in common use we are accustomed to cleanse four things, namely the face, the mouth, the hands and the garments. — In this manner the just wash the *face of the heart;* in Tobias, the third chapter[^p599-3], Sara says: « Thou knowest, O Lord, that I never coveted a husband and have kept my soul clean from all lust »; of this, the second to the Corinthians, the seventh chapter: « Let us cleanse ourselves from all defilement of the flesh and of the spirit, perfecting sanctification ». This David asked in the Psalm, when he said: « Create a clean heart in me, O God »; this is commanded in Isaias, the first chapter: « Wash yourselves, be clean » etc. — Likewise, they have clean the *speech of the mouth,* which Job had, in the eleventh chapter[^p599-4], who said: « My word is pure, O Lord, and I am clean in thy sight ». To this the Apostle admonishes, to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter: « Fornication and uncleanness, let it not be named among you ». — Likewise, they have clean the *hands of work,* which he had, in Genesis, the twentieth chapter, who said: « In the simplicity of my heart and in the cleanness of my hands have I done this »; and the second of Kings, the twenty-second chapter: « The Lord will render to me according to my justice, and according to the cleanness of my hands in the sight of his eyes ». Of this, Job, the twenty-second chapter: « The innocent shall be saved by the cleanness of his hands ». — Likewise, the just have clean the *garment of their way of life,* to which Jacob exhorted his sons, saying, Genesis, the thirty-fifth chapter[^p599-5]: « Cast away the strange gods that are among you, and be cleansed and change your garments ». — So therefore the Saints have cleanness of *countenance,* cleanness of *mouth,* cleanness of *hands* and cleanness of *garments,* to whom it is said by the Lord: *You are clean.*

2. Now there is to be noted a fourfold good which spiritual cleanness works: it *preserves* the innocent, *disencumbers* those who labour, *makes pleasing* those who contemplate, *rewards* those who arrive. — *It preserves the innocent* from the contagion of sin, as you see that a thing wiped and polished does not easily contract rust; Job, the twenty-second chapter[^p599-6]: « The innocent shall be saved by the cleanness of his hands ». — *It disencumbers those who labour* in action, as you would say that someone, superfluities cast off and hands washed, is ready to serve; therefore it is asked in the Psalm: « Who shall ascend into the mountain of the Lord »? that is, into the eminence of virtue; and there follows: « The innocent in hands and clean of heart » etc. — *It makes pleasing those who contemplate* in prayer; Proverbs, the twenty-second chapter[^p599-7]: « He that loveth cleanness of heart, for the grace of his lips shall have the king for his friend »; Job, the eighth chapter: « If thou walk clean and upright, he will straightway awake unto thee ». — *It rewards those who arrive* with the blessed vision, which is the wage of glory; Matthew, the fifth chapter: « Blessed are the clean of heart, for they shall see God ».

3. *You,* he says, *are clean,* and he adds: *But not all.* Therefore the wicked are excluded from the number of the clean[^p599-8], because they are *defiled* in many ways. For an evil *delight* defiles a man, an evil *consent,* an evil *work* and an evil *custom.* These four take away spiritual cleanness and were signified in the Law by four things which took away *legal* cleanness. For according to the Law an unclean *touch* defiled a man, an unclean *food,* an unclean *place* and an unclean *disease.* — An unclean *touch* defiled, as when someone touched a carcass; Leviticus, the eleventh chapter[^p599-9], almost throughout:. « Whosoever toucheth a carcass shall be defiled and shall be unclean until the evening »; in the same place: « Upon whatsoever any
<!-- page 600 -->
of the carcasses shall fall, it shall be unclean ». — An unclean *food* defiled, as when someone tasted what was unclean; Leviticus, the eleventh chapter[^p600-1]: « Whosoever shall eat of it anything, or shall carry it, shall wash his garments and shall be unclean until the evening »; he is speaking of the carcass; further on, after he has set out the unclean foods: « Do not contaminate your souls » etc. — An unclean *place* defiled, as when someone walked through an unclean place; the first of Kings, the twenty-first chapter[^p600-2]: « Are the young men clean, especially from women »? said Abimelech. « And David answered: If it concerneth women, we have refrained ourselves from yesterday and the day before. But this way is defiled ». Likewise, on account of the legal cleanness of the place it is commanded that the oblations be eaten « in a most clean place », Leviticus, the tenth chapter[^p600-3]. — An unclean *disease* defiled, as of leprosy, Leviticus, the thirteenth chapter, and of the issue of seed, Leviticus, the fifteenth chapter, and the like.

Note *morally* that a man *touches* what is unclean in an evil *delight;* he *tastes* what is unclean in an evil *consent;* he *walks* into what is unclean in an evil *work;* he *incurs* an unclean disease in an evil *custom.*

4. Now spiritual cleanness is recovered by four things. It is recovered by the *sorrow* of contrition, by the *shame* of confession, by the *labour* of satisfaction, by the *flame* of tribulation; and these four were signified by four things which were cleansing according to the Law, namely by *water,* by the *broom,* by *oblation* and by *fire.* — *Water* cleanses; Leviticus, the twenty-second chapter[^p600-4]. « When the unclean man hath washed his flesh with water », namely of penance, « and the sun is down », that is, the ardour of concupiscence; « then being cleansed he shall eat of the sanctified things », that is, of the gifts of grace; Ezechiel, the thirty-sixth chapter: « I will pour upon you clean water, and you shall be cleansed from all your defilements ». — The *broom* cleanses; Luke, the eleventh chapter[^p600-5], and Matthew, the twelfth chapter: « He findeth it cleansed with brooms and garnished »; so the altar was at times cleansed, when the ashes were removed, and in such a way, as it were, the treasury was cleansed; Nehemias, the last chapter: « I cast forth, he says, the vessels of the house of Tobias », that is, sins, « out of the treasury, and I gave orders, and they cleansed the treasury, and I brought thither again the vessels of the Lord », that is, the instruments of the virtues; all which things are done in confession; the Psalm: « I was exercised and I swept my spirit ». — *Oblation* cleanses, Leviticus, the twelfth chapter[^p600-6], of the oblation of the woman after childbirth, likewise Leviticus, the fourteenth chapter, of the oblation of the leper, by all which is signified the oblation of good work, of which Luke, the eleventh chapter: « Give alms, and behold, all things are clean unto you ». — *Fire* cleanses; Malachias, the third chapter[^p600-7]: « He is as a refining fire and as the fuller's herb, and he shall sit refining and cleansing the silver and shall purify the sons of Levi »; Numbers, the thirty-first chapter: « What can pass through the flames shall be purified by fire ».

*Morally,* the water of *contrition* cleanses the sinner, the broom of *confession,* the oblation of *good operation,* the flame of *tribulation.*

5. Note that *shame, fear, love* and *honour* ought to move the defiled man to cleanness. — *Shame of reproach;* Leviticus, the thirteenth chapter[^p600-8], of the *leper:* « He shall have his garments loosed, his head bare, his mouth covered with a cloth; he shall cry himself contaminated and foul, and shall dwell alone without the camp ». — *Fear of chastisement;* Jeremias, the forty-fourth chapter: « They are not cleansed even to this day, and they have not feared God nor walked in the law of the Lord » etc.; and there follows: « Behold, I will set my face upon you for evil and will destroy all Juda » etc. — *Love of reward;* the Psalm[^p600-9]: « The Lord will reward me according to my justice, and according to the pureness of my hands » etc.; in the second of Kings, the twenty-second chapter, it is said: « According to the cleanness of my hands in the sight of his eyes »; Matthew, the fifth chapter: « Blessed are the clean of heart ». — *Honour of office;* Isaias, the fifty-second chapter: « Be ye clean, you that carry the vessels of the Lord »; Nehemias, the twelfth chapter: « The priests and the levites were cleansed, and they cleansed the people ».

6. *You are clean, but not all.* Say it otherwise: A man ought to love cleanness for his *own* sake, for his *neighbour's* sake, for the sake of his *Lord,* for the sake of his *office.* — For his *own* sake, that he may *live* cleanly, since « all things are clean to the clean, but to them that are defiled and to unbelievers nothing is clean », to Titus, the first chapter[^p600-10]. — For his *neighbour's* sake, that he may not *defile* him; « For all that the unclean man shall touch shall be unclean »; Leviticus, the fifteenth chapter: « He that toucheth his bed, he that toucheth his flesh, he that sitteth where he sat » etc.; it is said of one suffering an issue of seed. — For the sake of his *Lord,* that he may not *offend* him; Isaias, the fifty-second chapter[^p600-11]: « Be ye clean, you that carry the vessels of the Lord ». For the uncleanness of the servant displeases the Lord; Habacuc, the first chapter: « Thy eyes are clean, that thou see not evil, and thou canst not look on iniquity »; Isaias, the first chapter: « When you multiply prayer, I will not hear, for your hands are full of blood ».
<!-- page 601 -->
— For the sake of his *office,* that he may *worthily perform* it; Nehemias, the twelfth chapter[^p601-1]: « The priests and the levites were cleansed, and they cleansed the people »; Numbers, the nineteenth chapter: « The clean shall purify the unclean »; Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-fourth chapter: « By the unclean what shall be cleansed »?

### Annotation 52.

(Vers. 13.). *You call me Teacher and Lord.*

Note that the Lord was sometimes called *teacher;* Matthew, the twenty-second chapter[^p601-2]: « Teacher, we know that thou art true ». — Sometimes *lord;* John, the sixth chapter: « Lord, to whom shall we go? Thou hast the words of eternal life ». — Sometimes *master;* Luke, the fifth chapter:. « Master, we have laboured all the night and have taken nothing » etc. — And fittingly; for he is lord, who created all things *mightily,* ordered them *wisely* and disposed them *sweetly.*

### Annotation 53.

(Vers. 15.). *I have given you an example.*

Note that the Lord gave us an example in *things to be done,* in *things to be endured,* in *things to be shunned* or despised. — He gave us an example in *things to be done,* John, the thirteenth chapter[^p601-3]; hence here: « I have given you an example, that, as I have done to you, so you also may do ». — In *things to be endured;* the first of Peter, the second chapter: « Christ suffered for us, leaving you an example, that you should follow his steps ». — In *things to be shunned;* for our second Adam taught us to despise the world by living spiritually, as the first Adam taught us to seek it by living carnally. And therefore Zacharias, the thirteenth chapter[^p601-4]: « Adam is my example from my youth »; which is understood of the second Adam, to the imitation of whom the Apostle exhorts us in the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter, saying: « The first man was of the earth, earthly; the second man, from heaven, heavenly »; and a little after: « As we have borne the image of the earthly, let us bear also the image of the heavenly ».

### Annotation 54.

(Vers. 21.). *One of you shall deliver me up.*

Note that *God the Father* delivered Christ up, and this from *piety;* to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p601-5]: « He spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all ». — *Christ himself* delivered up himself, and this from *charity;* the first of Peter, the second chapter: « He delivered himself to him that judged him unjustly ». — *Judas* delivered him up, and this from *cupidity;* Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter: « What will you give me, and I will deliver him unto you »? — The *Jews* delivered him up, and this from *envy;* John, the eighteenth chapter[^p601-6]: « Thy own nation and the chief priests have delivered thee up to me ». — *Pilate* delivered him up, and this from *injustice;* John, the nineteenth chapter: « Then therefore he delivered him to them to be crucified ».

### Annotation 55.

(Vers. 23.). *There was therefore one of his disciples leaning on the bosom of Jesus.*

Note that the Gospels name four persons especially, namely *Mary* weeping, Luke, the seventh chapter[^p601-7], and John, the twentieth chapter; *Martha* serving, Luke, the tenth chapter; *Peter* walking upon the sea, Matthew, the fourteenth chapter; *John* sleeping on the bosom of Jesus, John, the thirteenth chapter; hence here: *Leaning* etc. — By *Mary weeping* understand penitents; by *Martha serving* understand the obedient; these two belong to the *active.* By *Peter walking upon the sea* understand those who despise the world; by *John sleeping on the bosom of Jesus* understand those who apply themselves to devotion; which two belong to the *contemplative.*

### Annotation 56.

(Vers. 36.). *Whither I go, thou canst not follow me now, but thou shalt follow hereafter.*

Look above, at the tenth chapter[^p601-8], concerning the hindrances to following the Lord.
