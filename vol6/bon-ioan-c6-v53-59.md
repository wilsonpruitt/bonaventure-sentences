# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. VI, Vers. 53-59
## *Supra ostendit Dominus, se esse panem reficientem secundum divinam naturam; hic ostendit secundum humanam*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 332 -->

81. *Et panis, quem ego dabo.* Supra ostendit Dominus, se esse panem reficientem secundum *divinam* naturam; hic ostendit secundum *humanam.* Et quoniam hoc videbatur falsum et asperum sive durum, ideo haec pars habet tres partes. Primo ponitur *instructio* fidelium; secundo, ipsius *confirmatio* propter Iudaeorum litigium, ibi[^p332-5]: *Litigabant ergo Iudaei* etc.; tertio, ipsius *explanatio* propter discipulorum scandalum, ibi: *Haec dixit in Synagoga docens* etc.

(Vers. 52.). *Instructio* brevis ponitur, in qua carnem suam dicit esse panem; propter quod dicit: *Et panis* etc.; ita ego sum panis secundum Divinitatem; sum etiam panis secundum carnem; unde dicit: *Et panis, quem ego dabo,* scilicet ad reficiendum, secundum quod petimus in oratione dominica: « Panem nostrum supersubstantialem da nobis hodie », Matthaei sexto[^p332-6]; *caro mea est,* quae scilicet reficit; primae ad Corinthios decimo: « Panis, quem frangimus, nonne corpus Domini est » ? *Pro mundi vita,* data scilicet est caro Christi, cum supplicio est exposita, iuxta illud Threnorum tertio[^p332-7]: « Dabit percutienti se maxillam », immo totum corpus; unde Isaiae quinquagesimo: « Corpus meum dedi percutientibus et genas meas vellentibus »; et non tantum ad percutiendum, immo etiam ad immolandum; unde ad Ephesios quinto: « Tradidit semetipsum pro nobis oblationem et hostiam Deo ».

82. *Litigabant ergo Iudaei.* Posita est instructio, hic secundo ponitur propter Iudaeorum litigium *confirmatio.* Et primo ponitur Iudaeorum *litigatio;* secundo, *doctrinae confirmatio,* ibi[^p332-8]: *Dixit ergo eis Iesus.*

(Vers. 53.). *Litigatio* igitur erat de hoc, quod carnem suam *panem* dixerat; quod ipsi carnaliter intelligebant. Propterea dicit: *Litigabant ergo Iudaei.* Primo *murmurabant,* sed modo *litigant,* quando alto et aperto sermone contradicunt. Litigiosi erant Iudaei, quod non debent esse servi Dei; unde secundae ad Timotheum secundo[^p332-9]: « Servum Dei non oportet litigare, sed mansuetum esse ad omnes ». *Ad invicem dicentes: Quomodo potest hic carnem suam dare nobis ad manducandum?* Quasi dicant: hoc est impossibile, nisi se interficiat. Similiter petebat supra[^p332-10] Nicodemus: « Quomodo potest homo nasci, cum sit senex? Nunquid potest in ventrem matris suae iterato introire et renasci » ? Unde stulte hoc petebant; Chrysostomus[^p332-11]: « Quomodo potest carnem dare, petis? Propter quid in panibus non dixisti, quomodo quinque panes in tot extendit » ?

83. *Dixit ergo eis Iesus.* Posita est Iudaeorum litigatio, hic ponitur *doctrinae confirmatio.* Confirmat igitur quod dixerat, carnem suam esse panem, hoc ordine: primo ponitur manducationis carnis Christi *necessitas;* secundo, manducationis *utilitas;* tertio, refectionis *veritas;* quarto vero, *dignitas.*

(Vers. 54.). Primo igitur ostenditur *manducationis necessitas,* quia sine illa non est salus; propter quod dicit: *Amen, amen dico vobis,* id est in veritate: *Nisi manducaveritis carnem Filii hominis,* tanquam verum panem, *et biberitis eius sanguinem,* tanquam verum potum; *non habebitis vitam in vobis,* vitam scilicet gratiae, de qua dicitur in Psalmo[^p332-12]: « Vivifica me secundum verbum tuum ».
<!-- page 333 -->
Augustinus: « De spiritu Christi non vivit nisi corpus Christi. Si igitur vis vivere de spiritu Christi, esto in corpore Christi ». Qui autem manducat corpus Christi est in corpore Christi; qui igitur non manducat non vivit; quia dicitur infra eodem[^p333-1]: « Qui manducat meam carnem in me manet ».

84. (Vers. 55.). *Qui manducat* etc. Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *manducationis utilitas:* quia qui manducat carnem[^p333-2] vivificatur in *anima* et *corpore:* in *anima;* unde dicit: *Qui manducat meam carnem et bibit meum sanguinem habet vitam aeternam; habet,* inquam, in merito; vel *habet,* quia iam quodam modo habere incepit; Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto[^p333-3]: « Audite, audientes me, et comedite bonum, et delectabitur in crassitudine anima vestra; inclinate aurem vestram et venite ad me, et vivet anima vestra, et feriam vobiscum pactum sempiternum, misericordias David fideles ». — Dat etiam vitam *corporalem;* unde et subdit: *Et ego resuscitabo eum in novissimo die,* in illa hora, de qua supra quinto[^p333-4]: « Venit hora, in qua omnes, qui in monumentis sunt, audient vocem Filii Dei; et procedent qui bona fecerunt in resurrectionem vitae, qui vero mala egerunt in resurrectionem iudicii ».

85. (Vers. 56.). *Caro enim mea* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *refectionis veritas.* Vere enim reficit caro Christi, quia verus est cibus, et ideo vitam dat. Propterea dicit: *Caro enim mea vere est cibus;* ita vere dixi, quod habet vitam qui manducat; *caro enim mea vere est cibus,* non phantastice, sicut cibi corporales, qui ad momentum reficiunt et deficiunt. *Et sanguis meus vere est potus,* quia sitim aufert omnino; supra eodem[^p333-5]: « Qui venit ad me non esuriet, et qui credit in me non sitiet unquam ». Et quod caro eius sit verus cibus reficiens et vivificans, ostendit tali ratione: qui manet in me vivit propter me; sed qui manducat meam carnem manet in me: ergo qui manducat meam carnem vivit propter me. Huius rationis primo ponit *minorem,* cum dicit:

86. (Vers. 57.). *Qui manducat meam carnem,* spirituali manducatione, *et bibit meum sanguinem in me manet,* per dilectionem; primae Ioannis quarto[^p333-6]: « Qui manet in caritate in Deo manet, et Deus in eo »; *et ego in eo,* per inhabitationem; infra decimo quarto: « Si quis diligit me, sermonem meum servabit; et Pater meus diliget eum, et ad eum veniemus et mansionem apud eum faciemus ». Posuit *minorem,* subdit probationem *maioris,* illius scilicet, quod qui manet in Filio vivit propter illum. Hanc manifestat in simili[^p333-7].

87. (Vers. 58.). *Sicut misit me vivens Pater; vivens,* per essentiam; *et ego vivo propter Patrem,* scilicet quia ab ipso vitam habeo; supra quinto[^p333-8]: « Sicut Pater habet vitam in semetipso, sic dedit et Filio habere vitam in semetipso », et hoc a Patre in ipso manente; infra decimo quarto: « Ego in Patre, et Pater in me est »; et ex hoc habetur *maior:* Qui manet in me etc. Et ex hoc infertur *conclusio: Et qui manducat me, et ipse vivet propter me;* quia scilicet manet in vita manducando, vere propter me vivit, quia, sicut infra undecimo[^p333-9] dicitur, « ego sum resurrectio et vita ».

88. (Vers. 59.). *Hic est panis* etc. Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *refectionis dignitas,* quae excellit illam patrum refectionem, quam supra laudabant Iudaei, et quia *nobilior,* et quia *permanentior: nobilior,* quia iste de caelo; unde dicit: *Hic est panis, qui de caelo descendit;* supra eodem[^p333-10]: « Non Moyses dedit vobis panem de caelo, sed Pater meus dat vobis panem de caelo », et ita nobilior est. Est etiam *permanentior,* quia ille conservabat *temporaliter,* iste vero *aeternaliter;* unde dicit: *Non sicut patres vestri manducaverunt manna et mortui sunt,* et ita *temporaliter* conservati, quia in quadraginta annis mortui in deserto, secundum illud Psalmi[^p333-11]: « Quadraginta annis offensus fui generationi illi » etc.; Numerorum decimo quarto: « Filii vestri erunt vagi in deserto annis quadraginta et portabunt fornicationem vestram, donec consumantur cadavera patrum in deserto ». Sed iste *aeternaliter;* unde dicit: *Qui manducat hunc panem vivet in aeternum;* ille scilicet, qui digne manducat, *vivet in aeternum,* quia, secundum quod dicitur primae ad Corinthios undecimo[^p333-12], « qui manducat et bibit indigne iudicium sibi manducat et bibit »; qui manducat digne vivet in aeternum. Saepe promittit vitam aeternam, et ut appetant cibum illum, et ut ab amore terrenae vitae eos abstrahat ad aeternam. Sicut enim dicitur Matthaei decimo[^p333-13]: « Qui amat animam suam plus quam me non est me dignus »; Deuteronomii trigesimo: « Elige tibi vitam ».

### Quaestiones.

89. Quaest. I. Quaeritur de *litigatione Iudaeorum;* de qua quaerit Chrysostomus[^p334-1]: unde est hoc, quod supra in occulto *murmurabant,* sed modo *litigant* in aperto?

Respondeo[^p334-2] ad hoc, quod est ex duplici causa: tum quia mali homines et seductores et discoli in peius proficiunt, quanto magis eis veritas explicatur; tum etiam, quia illud quod supra[^p334-3] dictum erat de Divinitate, etsi videretur falsum, tamen non erat inhumanum; sed quod modo dixerat de carne sua manducanda non tantum falsum, sed inhumanum et immundum, quod Iudaei refugiunt; ideo *litigabant.*

90. Quaest. II. Quaeritur hic de *manducatione* corporis Christi et carnis suae. — Et quod non manducetur, videtur:

1. Quia quod manducatur atteritur et masticatur; sed caro Christi impassibilis dentibus non potest atteri: ergo nec manducari.

2. Item, quod manducatur in manducantem convertitur; sed corpus Christi non convertitur in nos, sed nos in ipsum: ergo ipsum non manducamus, sed ipse nos.

**Respondeo:** In manducatione tria sunt, quae reperimus, scilicet *masticatio, incorporatio* et *conservatio. Masticatio* est duplex secundum duplicem manducationem: quia est *manducatio sacramentalis,* et in hac est *masticatio sacramentalis,* quia species masticata est; est etiam *manducatio spiritualis,* et in hac est *masticatio spiritualis,* quae est spiritualis discussio per fidem. Est ibi[^p334-4] rursus *incorporatio,* qua corpori Christi incorporamur; est etiam *conservatio,* qua per Christi spiritum conservamur, sicut membra corporis per animam. De his tribus dicit Augustinus[^p334-5]: « O sacramentum pietatis! o signaculum unitatis! o vinculum caritatis! Qui vult vivere accedat, credat, incorporetur, vivificetur ».

1. Per hoc patet primum: quia est ibi masticatio non carnalis, sed spiritualis, vel sacramentalis.

2. Quod obiicitur, quod non nobis incorporatur; dicendum, quod ultimus effectus cibi est *conservare;* et quia, etsi nos incorporemur Christo manducando, quia tamen manducando conservamur per ipsum, recte ipse dicitur *noster cibus*[^p334-6].

91. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Nisi manducaveritis carnem* etc. — Ex hoc videtur, quod Sacramentum eucharistiae sit necessitatis, et quod nullus sine eo possit salvari.

**Contra:** Aut intelligit de vita *gratiae,* aut de vita *gloriae: gratiae* non, quia qui vult digne accedere prius debet habere vitam gratiae: ergo eam non habet per manducationem; nec vitam *gloriae,* quia, si potest habere vitam gratiae sine manducatione, potest et gloriae.

Respondetur ad hoc secundum Augustinum[^p334-7], quod *credere* est manducare; unde: « ut quid paras dentem et ventrem? Crede tantum, et manducasti ». Secundum hoc igitur attendendum, quod est manducatio *spiritualis,* et est manducatio *sacramentalis; spiritualis* est per fidem et caritatem, *sacramentalis* sub Sacramento. Sine sacramentali potest esse salus, nec est necessitatis, quantum est de institutione Sacramenti; *spiritualis* est de necessitate[^p334-8].

92. Sed obiicitur: 1. de parvulis baptizatis, qui salvantur nec unquam manducaverunt sacramentaliter nec spiritualiter.

2. Item, addit manducationi carnis potum sanguinis, ergo sine potu sanguinis nulli salvantur; sed soli sacerdotes sumunt sanguinem[^p334-9]: ergo ipsi soli salvantur.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod, sicut dictum est[^p334-10], duplex est effectus manducationis, scilicet *incorporatio* et *conservatio.* Nullus salvatur nisi membrum Christi, et nisi conservetur a Christo; unde nullus sine *effectu* manducationis salvatur; ideo dicitur manducatio *necessaria.* — Quia vero in parvulis divina largitate potest esse *effectus* manducationis sine manducatione; ideo salvantur etiam sine manducatione quacumque[^p334-11]. In adultis vero non potest esse *incor-
<!-- page 335 -->
poratio* sive conservatio sine manducatione *spirituali,* quae est per fidem et caritatem, sed potest esse sine *sacramentali.* Ideo haec est necessaria adultis, sacramentalis vero, etsi non sit de necessitate, tamen multam praestat utilitatem, quia virtute Sacramenti augetur in nobis omne bonum[^p335-1].

2. Quod ergo obiicitur de carne et sanguine, exponit Augustinus[^p335-2] de potu et cibo *spirituali;* unde « vult, intelligi *cibum et potum* societatem corporis et membrorum, quae est sancta Ecclesia in praedestinatis et vocatis sanctis et fidelibus eius », extra quam nullus salvatur.

93. Quaest. IV. Item de *utilitate* quaeritur: *Qui manducat meam carnem* etc.

1. Multi enim manducant, qui tamen moriuntur et damnantur, sicut Iudas.

2. Item, sicut Filius vivit propter Patrem viventem, sic dicitur in littera[^p335-3], quod nos vivimus propter Filium; sed Filius non manducando vivit: ergo pari ratione et nos; aut si nos manducamus Filium, quare non manducat ipse Patrem?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum ad primum, quod duplex fuit ex isto verbo haeresis exorta: una, quae dixit, quod ex quo quis manducat Christi corpus, non poterit damnari, dum tamen *credens* manducaret. Sed haec est irrationalis manifeste propter Iudam. — Alii dixerunt, quod qui semel *digne* manducaret post nunquam damnaretur; et haec manifeste falsa, quia, sicut per Scripturam probari potest, multi fuerunt boni in Ecclesia, qui postmodum sunt damnati[^p335-4].

Ideo catholice intelligendum est: *qui manducat,* scilicet *digne,* et ab illa dignitate non recedit vivet in aeternum.

2. Ad aliud dicendum, quod non est omnino simile, licet adducatur pro simili. Filius enim dicitur *vivere propter Patrem,* quia a Patre habet vitam et quia manet in Patre, sed non quia participatione Patris fiat melior; nos vero vivimus per Filium, quia ab ipso habemus vitam, et a Filio manente in nobis et participatione eius meliores sumus. Ideo, quia non manet in nobis nisi per manducationem, ideo manducamus, ut vivamus et meliores efficiamur[^p335-5].

94. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur de *dignitate* huius manducationis. Dicit enim, quod manducantes hunc panem *non morientur,* sicut manducantes manna.

**Contra:** Hoc aut intelligit de vita *spirituali,* aut *corporali;* si de *spirituali,* nihil dicit, quia patres sancti, qui comederunt manna, non sunt spiritualiter mortui. Si de *corporali,* similiter; quia qui manducat hunc panem non vivit corporaliter, sed moritur. — *Si dicas,* quod *indigne* manducantes manna sunt mortui spirituali morte, et hos vocat *patres* eorum; similiter indigne manducantes corpus Christi moriuntur morte spirituali; primae ad Corinthios undecimo[^p335-6]: *Qui manducat et bibit indigne iudicium sibi manducat et bibit.* — Propterea quaeritur: quid est *manducare indigne,* et quare peccat manducans indigne?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod cibus iste praeponitur illi cibo ratione vivificationis dupliciter: tum quia ille non poterat vivificare, nisi per istum quem signabat; ideo dicitur de iustis, quod *omnes eandem spiritualem escam manducaverunt,* primae ad Corinthios decimo[^p335-7]; sed qui solum ad illum habebant oculum mortui sunt spiritualiter, quia tantum carnaliter gustaverunt. Ita respondet Augustinus[^p335-8].

Aliter potest dici, quod manna ordinabatur ad conservationem vitae *corporalis,* sed hic ad conservationem vitae *spiritualis.* Et vult Dominus dicere, quod ille panis solummodo vitam corporalem continuabat ad tempus, sed iste vitam spiritualem continuat in aeternum in *digne* sumentibus; et ideo illo[^p335-9] est dignior. — *Indigne* vero accipientes peccant, et convertitur eis panis in venenum, et isti sunt qui remorsum habent mortalis peccati. Ratio, quare peccant, est contemptus, quia tantum hospitem inducunt in tam vile hospitium[^p335-10]. — Quomodo autem *digne* accipiatur, docet Augustinus[^p335-11]: « Innocentiam, inquit, ad altare apportate; peccata, si sint quotidiana, saltem non sint mortifera; antequam ad altare accedatis, dicatur: *Dimitte nobis debita nostra* etc. Tunc securi accedite; panis est, non venenum ».

## English
<!-- page 332 -->

81. *And the bread that I will give.* Above, the Lord showed that he is the bread that refreshes according to the *divine* nature; here he shows it according to the *human.* And since this seemed false and harsh, or hard, therefore this part has three parts. First is set down the *instruction* of the faithful; second, its *confirmation* on account of the strife of the Jews, there[^p332-5]: *The Jews therefore strove* etc.; third, its *explanation* on account of the scandal of the disciples, there: *These things he said, teaching in the synagogue* etc.

(Verse 52.). A brief *instruction* is set down, in which he says that his flesh is bread; on account of which he says: *And the bread* etc.; as if to say: I am bread according to the Divinity; I am bread also according to the flesh; whence he says: *And the bread that I will give,* namely for refreshing, according to what we ask in the Lord's prayer: « Our supersubstantial bread give us this day », Matthew, the sixth[^p332-6]; *is my flesh,* which, namely, refreshes; the first to the Corinthians, the tenth: « The bread which we break, is it not the body of the Lord » ? *For the life of the world,* that is, the flesh of Christ was given when it was exposed to punishment, according to that of Lamentations, the third[^p332-7]: « He shall give his cheek to him that striketh him », nay rather his whole body; whence Isaias, the fiftieth: « I have given my body to the strikers, and my cheeks to them that plucked them »; and not only for striking, nay rather also for sacrificing; whence to the Ephesians, the fifth: « He hath delivered himself for us, an oblation and a sacrifice to God ».

82. *The Jews therefore strove.* The instruction has been set down; here, second, is set down the *confirmation* on account of the strife of the Jews. And first is set down the *striving* of the Jews; second, the *confirmation of the teaching,*[^tr-doctrina] there[^p332-8]: *Then Jesus said to them.*

(Verse 53.). The *striving,* then, was about this, that he had called his flesh *bread;* which they understood carnally. Therefore he says: *The Jews therefore strove.* At first they *murmured,* but now they *strive,* when they contradict with loud and open speech. The Jews were given to strife, which the servants of God ought not to be; whence the second to Timothy, the second[^p332-9]: « The servant of God must not strive, but be mild towards all men ». *Among themselves, saying: How can this man give us his flesh to eat?* As if they said: this is impossible, unless he kill himself. In like manner Nicodemus asked above[^p332-10]: « How can a man be born when he is old? can he enter a second time into his mother's womb, and be born again » ? Whence foolishly they asked this; Chrysostom[^p332-11]: « You ask how he can give his flesh? Why did you not say, in the case of the loaves, how he stretched five loaves to so many » ?

83. *Then Jesus said to them.* The striving of the Jews has been set down; here is set down the *confirmation of the teaching.* He confirms, then, what he had said, that his flesh is bread, in this order: first is set down the *necessity* of the eating of Christ's flesh; second, the *utility* of the eating; third, the *truth* of the refection; and fourth, its *dignity.*

(Verse 54.). First, then, is shown the *necessity of the eating,* because without it there is no salvation; on account of which he says: *Amen, amen I say unto you,* that is, in truth: *Except you eat the flesh of the Son of man,* as true bread, *and drink his blood,* as true drink, *you shall not have life in you,* namely the life of grace, of which it is said in the Psalm[^p332-12]: « Quicken me according to thy word ».
<!-- page 333 -->
Augustine: « Nothing lives by the spirit of Christ but the body of Christ. If then you wish to live by the spirit of Christ, be in the body of Christ ». But he who eats the body of Christ is in the body of Christ; he therefore who does not eat does not live; for it is said below in the same[^p333-1]: « He that eateth my flesh abideth in me ».

84. (Verse 55.). *He that eateth* etc. Here is touched the second, namely the *utility of the eating:* for he who eats the flesh[^p333-2] is quickened in *soul* and *body:* in *soul;* whence he says: *He that eateth my flesh and drinketh my blood hath everlasting life; hath,* I say, in merit; or *hath,* because in a certain manner he has already begun to have it; Isaias, the fifty-fifth[^p333-3]: « Hearken, hearkening to me, and feed on that which is good, and your soul shall be delighted in fatness; incline your ear and come to me, and your soul shall live, and I will make an everlasting covenant with you, the faithful mercies of David ». — He gives also *bodily* life; whence he also adds: *And I will raise him up in the last day,* in that hour of which above, the fifth[^p333-4]: « The hour cometh wherein all that are in the graves shall hear the voice of the Son of God; and they that have done good things shall come forth unto the resurrection of life, but they that have done evil unto the resurrection of judgment ».

85. (Verse 56.). *For my flesh* etc. Here is touched the third, namely the *truth of the refection.* For the flesh of Christ truly refreshes, because it is true food, and therefore it gives life. Therefore he says: *For my flesh is food indeed;* as if to say: so truly have I said that he who eats has life; *for my flesh is food indeed,* not in fantasy, as bodily foods, which refresh for a moment and fail. *And my blood is drink indeed,* because it takes away thirst altogether; above in the same[^p333-5]: « He that cometh to me shall not hunger, and he that believeth in me shall never thirst ». And that his flesh is true food, refreshing and quickening, he shows by this reasoning: he who abides in me lives because of me; but he who eats my flesh abides in me: therefore he who eats my flesh lives because of me. Of this reasoning he first sets down the *minor,* when he says:

86. (Verse 57.). *He that eateth my flesh,* by spiritual eating, *and drinketh my blood, abideth in me,* through dilection; the first of John, the fourth[^p333-6]: « He that abideth in charity abideth in God, and God in him »; *and I in him,* through indwelling; below, the fourteenth: « If any one love me, he will keep my word; and my Father will love him, and we will come to him and will make our abode with him ». He has set down the *minor;* he adds the proof of the *major,* namely of this, that he who abides in the Son lives because of him. This he makes manifest in a like case[^p333-7].

87. (Verse 58.). *As the living Father hath sent me; living,* through essence; *and I live because of the Father,* namely because I have life from him; above, the fifth[^p333-8]: « As the Father hath life in himself, so he hath given to the Son also to have life in himself », and this from the Father abiding in him; below, the fourteenth: « I am in the Father, and the Father is in me »; and from this the *major* is had: He who abides in me etc. And from this is inferred the *conclusion: So he that eateth me, the same also shall live because of me;* namely because he abides in life by eating, he truly lives because of me, since, as is said below, the eleventh[^p333-9], « I am the resurrection and the life ».

88. (Verse 59.). *This is the bread* etc. Here is touched the fourth, namely the *dignity of the refection,* which excels that refection of the fathers which the Jews praised above, both because it is *nobler* and because it is *more abiding: nobler,* because this one is from heaven; whence he says: *This is the bread that came down from heaven;* above in the same[^p333-10]: « Not Moses gave you bread from heaven, but my Father giveth you bread from heaven », and so it is nobler. It is also *more abiding,* because that one preserved *temporally,* but this one *eternally;* whence he says: *Not as your fathers did eat manna, and are dead,* and so were *temporally* preserved, since within forty years they were dead in the desert, according to that of the Psalm[^p333-11]: « Forty years long was I offended with that generation » etc.; Numbers, the fourteenth: « Your children shall wander in the desert forty years, and shall bear your fornication, until the carcasses of the fathers be consumed in the desert ». But this one *eternally;* whence he says: *He that eateth this bread shall live for ever;* he, namely, who eats worthily *shall live for ever,* since, according to what is said in the first to the Corinthians, the eleventh[^p333-12], « he that eateth and drinketh unworthily eateth and drinketh judgment to himself »; he who eats worthily shall live for ever. Often he promises eternal life, both that they may desire that food, and that he may draw them away from the love of earthly life to the eternal. For as is said in Matthew, the tenth[^p333-13]: « He that loveth his soul more than me is not worthy of me »; Deuteronomy, the thirtieth: « Choose thee life ».

### Questions.

89. Question I. It is asked about the *striving of the Jews;* about which Chrysostom asks[^p334-1]: whence is it that above they *murmured* in secret, but now they *strive* in the open?

I respond[^p334-2] to this, that it is from a twofold cause: both because evil men and seducers and the froward grow worse the more the truth is unfolded to them; and also because what had been said above[^p334-3] about the Divinity, though it seemed false, yet was not inhuman; but what he had now said about his flesh to be eaten was not only false but inhuman and unclean, which the Jews shrink from; therefore they *strove.*

90. Question II. It is asked here about the *eating* of the body of Christ and of his flesh. — And that it is not eaten, it seems:

1. Because what is eaten is ground and chewed; but the impassible flesh of Christ cannot be ground by teeth: therefore neither eaten.

2. Likewise, what is eaten is converted into the one eating; but the body of Christ is not converted into us, but we into it: therefore we do not eat it, but it us.

**I respond:** In eating there are three things which we find, namely *chewing, incorporation* and *preservation. Chewing* is twofold according to the twofold eating: for there is *sacramental eating,* and in this there is *sacramental chewing,* since the species is chewed; there is also *spiritual eating,* and in this there is *spiritual chewing,* which is a spiritual examination through faith. There is there[^p334-4] again *incorporation,* by which we are incorporated into the body of Christ; there is also *preservation,* by which we are preserved through the spirit of Christ, as the members of the body through the soul. Of these three Augustine says[^p334-5]: « O sacrament of piety! o seal of unity! o bond of charity! Let him who wishes to live approach, believe, be incorporated, be quickened ».

1. By this the first is plain: for there is there a chewing not carnal, but spiritual, or sacramental.

2. As to the objection that it is not incorporated into us; it must be said that the last effect of food is *to preserve;* and since, although we are incorporated into Christ by eating, yet by eating we are preserved through him, rightly he is called *our food*[^p334-6].

91. Question III. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *Except you eat the flesh* etc. — From this it seems that the Sacrament of the Eucharist is of necessity, and that no one can be saved without it.

**On the contrary:** Either he understands it of the life of *grace,* or of the life of *glory:* not of *grace,* because he who wishes to approach worthily ought first to have the life of grace: therefore he does not have it through the eating; nor the life of *glory,* because, if one can have the life of grace without the eating, one can also have that of glory.

It is answered to this according to Augustine[^p334-7], that *to believe* is to eat; whence: « why do you make ready tooth and belly? Only believe, and you have eaten ». According to this, then, it must be attended to that there is a *spiritual* eating, and there is a *sacramental* eating; the *spiritual* is through faith and charity, the *sacramental* under the Sacrament. Without the sacramental there can be salvation, nor is it of necessity, so far as concerns the institution of the Sacrament; the *spiritual* is of necessity[^p334-8].

92. But it is objected: 1. about baptized little ones, who are saved and never ate either sacramentally or spiritually.

2. Likewise, he adds to the eating of the flesh the drinking of the blood; therefore without the drinking of the blood none are saved; but only priests take the blood[^p334-9]: therefore they alone are saved.

**I respond:** It must be said that, as has been said[^p334-10], the effect of the eating is twofold, namely *incorporation* and *preservation.* No one is saved unless he be a member of Christ, and unless he be preserved by Christ; whence no one is saved without the *effect* of the eating; therefore the eating is called *necessary.* — But since in little ones, by the divine largesse, there can be the *effect* of the eating without the eating; therefore they are saved even without any eating whatever[^p334-11]. But in adults there cannot be *incor-
<!-- page 335 -->
poration* or preservation without *spiritual* eating, which is through faith and charity, but there can be without *sacramental.* Therefore this is necessary for adults, while the sacramental, though it be not of necessity, yet affords much utility, since by the power of the Sacrament every good is increased in us[^p335-1].

2. What, then, is objected about the flesh and the blood, Augustine expounds[^p335-2] of the *spiritual* drink and food; whence « he wills that by *the food and the drink* be understood the fellowship of the body and its members, which is the holy Church in his predestined and called saints and faithful », outside which no one is saved.

93. Question IV. Likewise it is asked about the *utility:* *He that eateth my flesh* etc.

1. For many eat who nevertheless die and are damned, as Judas.

2. Likewise, as the Son lives because of the living Father, so it is said in the letter[^p335-3] that we live because of the Son; but the Son does not live by eating: therefore by like reason neither do we; or if we eat the Son, why does he not himself eat the Father?

**I respond:** It must be said to the first, that a twofold heresy arose from this word: one, which said that from the moment one eats the body of Christ, he can not be damned, provided only that he ate *believing.* But this is manifestly irrational, on account of Judas. — Others said that he who had once eaten *worthily* would never afterwards be damned; and this is manifestly false, because, as can be proved through Scripture, many were good in the Church who afterwards were damned[^p335-4].

Therefore it must be understood in the catholic way: *he that eateth,* namely *worthily,* and does not depart from that worthiness, shall live for ever.

2. To the other it must be said that it is not altogether alike, although it is adduced as a like case. For the Son is said *to live because of the Father,* because he has life from the Father and because he abides in the Father, but not because he is made better by participation in the Father; but we live through the Son, because we have life from him, and by the Son abiding in us and by participation in him we are better. Therefore, since he does not abide in us except through eating, therefore we eat, that we may live and be made better[^p335-5].

94. Question V. Likewise it is asked about the *dignity* of this eating. For he says that those who eat this bread *shall not die,* as those who ate manna.

**On the contrary:** Either he understands this of *spiritual* life, or of *bodily;* if of *spiritual,* he says nothing, because the holy fathers, who fed on manna, are not spiritually dead. If of *bodily,* likewise; because he who eats this bread does not live bodily, but dies. — *If you say* that those who ate manna *unworthily* are dead by spiritual death, and these he calls their *fathers;* likewise those who eat the body of Christ unworthily die by spiritual death; the first to the Corinthians, the eleventh[^p335-6]: *He that eateth and drinketh unworthily eateth and drinketh judgment to himself.* — Therefore it is asked: what is it *to eat unworthily,* and why does he who eats unworthily sin?

**I respond:** It must be said that this food is set before that food by reason of quickening in a twofold way: both because that one could not quicken except through this one which it signed; therefore it is said of the just that *all did eat the same spiritual meat,* the first to the Corinthians, the tenth[^p335-7]; but those who had their eye on that one only died spiritually, because they tasted only carnally. So Augustine answers[^p335-8].

Otherwise it can be said that the manna was ordered to the preservation of *bodily* life, but this to the preservation of *spiritual* life. And the Lord means to say that that bread continued bodily life only for a time, but this one continues spiritual life for ever in those who take it *worthily;* and therefore it is worthier than that[^p335-9]. — But those who receive it *unworthily* sin, and the bread is turned for them into poison; and these are they who have the remorse of mortal sin. The reason why they sin is contempt, because they bring so great a guest into so vile a lodging[^p335-10]. — But how it is received *worthily,* Augustine teaches[^p335-11]: « Bring innocence, he says, to the altar; sins, if they be daily, let them at least not be deadly; before you approach the altar, let it be said: *Forgive us our debts* etc. Then approach secure; it is bread, not poison ».
