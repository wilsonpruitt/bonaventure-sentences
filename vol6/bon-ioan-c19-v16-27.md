# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XIX, Vers. 16-27
## *Tertio, de Christi passione notantur duo*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 495 -->

#### Tertio, de Christi passione notantur duo.

27. *Susceperunt autem Iesum.* Actum est supra[^p495-5] de Christi comprehensione et condemnatione; hic agitur de ipsius *passione;* et dividitur haec pars in duas, quia primo agit de *passione;* secundo, de *passionis consummatione,* ibi[^p495-6]: *Postea sciens Iesus* etc.

In prima parte describitur eius passio quantum ad quatuor, quae ibi insinuantur. Primo enim describitur *passionis vilitas;* secundo, *patientis dignitas,* ibi[^p495-7]: *Scripsit autem et titulum;* tertio, *crucifigentium crudelitas* sive cupiditas, ibi: *Milites ergo, cum crucifixissent;* quarto, *compatientium paucitas,* ibi: *Stabant autem iuxta crucem.*

Passionis igitur *vilitas* describitur in *incessu,* in *loco,* in *genere passionis* et in *societate patientium.*

28. (Vers. 16.). Primo ergo *vilitas passionis in incessu* notatur in hoc, quod tanquam latro ducitur, et ipse baiulat sibi supplicium; propterea dicit: *Susceperunt autem Iesum;* Psalmus[^p495-8]: « Susceperunt me sicut leo paratus ad praedam », scilicet milites Iesum; *duxerunt,* tanquam maleficum, scilicet extra castra; propter quod ad Hebraeos ultimo: « Exeamus ad eum extra castra, improperium » crucis « eius portantes ».

29. (Vers. 17.). *Et baiulans sibi crucem,* in qua scilicet tanquam latro puniretur. Tunc impletum est quod dictum erat Isaiae vigesimo secundo[^p495-9]: « Dabo clavem David super humerum eius »; et Isaiae nono: « Factus est principatus super humerum eius », quia ibi triumphavit. De hoc Augustinus: « Grande spectaculum! Sed si spectat impietas, grande ludibrium; si pietas, grande mysterium ».

*Exivit in eum qui dicitur Calvariae locus*[^p495-10]. Hic innuitur vilitas a *loco;* quia in eo loco passus est, ubi puniebantur latrones et malefici. *Calvaria* enim dicitur ille locus, quia ibi decalvabantur capita
<!-- page 496 -->
damnatorum. *Calvaria* enim dicitur testa capitis carne nudata; *Hebraice Golgatha,* id est « locus decollationis[^p496-1] ».

30. (Vers. 18.). *Ubi eum crucifixerunt.* Notatur hic vilitas passionis a *genere mortis,* quia vilissimum genus mortis erat; Sapientiae secundo[^p496-2]: « Morte turpissima condemnemus eum ».

*Et cum eo alios duos*[^p496-3]*, hinc et hinc, medium autem Iesum.* Notatur hic vilitas a *societate,* quia isti duo erant latrones; unde Marci decimo quinto: « Crucifigunt cum eo duos latrones ». Tunc impletum est quod dicitur Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio: « Et cum iniquis deputatus est ». Ideo autem in *medietate,* ut significetur, quoniam *mediator;* unde in nativitate in *medio animalium,* Habacuc ultimo[^p496-4]; et in passione in *medio latronum,* quia pax vera, ad Ephesios secundo.

31. *Scripsit autem et titulum.* Hic notatur secundum principale, scilicet *patientis dignitas,* et innuitur in titulo, quia titulus erat regis; et hic titulus hoc ordine describitur: primo, eius *inscriptio;* secundo, *scripti publicatio;* tertio, *contradictio*[^p496-5]*;* quarto, *ipsius approbatio.*

(Vers. 19.). *Inscriptio* igitur *tituli* tangitur, cum dicit: *Scripsit autem et titulum Pilatus et posuit super crucem;* Isaiae decimo nono[^p496-6]: « Erit altare Domini in medio terrae Aegypti, et titulus Domini iuxta terminum eius ». — Et nota, quod quidam est titulus *memorialis,* sicut mortuorum; quidam *praeconialis,* ut de domo; quidam *triumphalis;* et iste triumphalis[^p496-7], unde est in laudem Christi et vituperium Iudaeorum, quia, etsi tanquam latro damnatus esset, non tamen erat latro, sed rex; et hoc dicebat titulus: *Erat autem scriptum: Iesus Nazarenus, rex Iudaeorum.* Hunc titulum approbat Scriptura; unde in Psalmo secundo[^p496-8]: « Ego constitutus sum rex ab eo super Sion, montem sanctum eius » etc. In hoc titulo describit ipsum Pilatus a *nomine,* cum dicit *Iesus;* Lucae primo: « Vocabis nomen eius Iesum »; a *patria, Nazarenus;* Lucae secundo: « Descendit cum eis et venit Nazareth »; a *dignitate regia,* cum dicit *rex;* Ieremiae vigesimo tertio: « Suscitabo David germen iustum, et regnabit rex ». — Secundum Matthaeum titulus dicitur *causa;* Matthaei vigesimo septimo[^p496-9]: « Imposuerunt super caput eius causam ipsius scriptam »; quia in titulo illo innuitur, quare mortuus fuerit, scilicet ad *salvandum,* quia *Iesus,* et ad *faciendum nos Deo nostro regnum;* Apocalypsis quinto: « Dignus es, Domine Deus, accipere librum; quia occisus es et redemisti nos Deo in sanguine tuo et fecisti nos Deo nostro regnum ».

32. (Vers. 20.). *Hunc ergo titulum.* Hic ponitur *tituli publicatio;* unde dicit: *Multi legerunt Iudaeorum, quia prope civitatem erat locus, ubi crucifixus est Iesus;* ideo multi legebant, quia multi concurrebant, nec solum Iudaei, sed et alii; Isaiae trigesimo quarto[^p496-10]: « Requirite diligenter in libro sermonum Domini et legite; nec unum ex eis defuit ». *Et erat scriptum Hebraice, Graece et Latine,* ut omnes possent legere titulum, quia omnibus rex erat futurus, sicut dicitur Apocalypsis decimo nono[^p496-11], quod ipse « est Rex regum et Dominus dominantium »; ideo tribus linguis, quia Deuteronomii
<!-- page 497 -->
decimo nono: « In ore duorum vel trium testium stat omne verbum ».

33. (Vers. 21.). *Dicebant ergo Pilato pontifices Iudaeorum.* Hic notatur *Iudaeorum contradictio,* qua titulum volebant delere, cum esset in suam ignominiam; unde dicunt: *Noli scribere: Rex Iudaeorum, sed quia*[^p497-1] *dixit: Rex sum Iudaeorum,* ut scilicet ostenderetur non dignitas, sed praesumtio, qua se regem esse diceret; quocontra dicitur ad Hebraeos quinto: « Christus non semetipsum clarificavit, ut pontifex fieret » etc.

34. (Vers. 22.). *Respondit Pilatus.* Hic ponitur *tituli affirmatio* seu approbatio contra Iudaeorum malitiam; unde Pilatus non delet, sed approbat: *Quod scripsi scripsi. Quod scripsi,* veraciter, *scripsi,* indelebiliter tanquam verum irrevocabile. Augustinus[^p497-2]: « Si corrumpi non potest quod Pilatus scripsit, corrumpi potest quod Veritas dixit »?

35. *Milites ergo cum crucifixissent eum.* Hic notatur tertium principale, scilicet *crucifigentium crudelitas* seu cupiditas in hoc, quod Christum exspoliaverunt et sic vestimenta eius diviserunt. Et notantur hic tria, scilicet vestimentorum *divisio per partes;* secundo, *per sortem;* tertio, *utriusque confirmatio* per auctoritatem, ne videatur in morte Christi aliquid fieri casualiter.

(Vers. 23.). Dividunt ergo *per partes;* unde dicit: *Acceperunt vestimenta eius et fecerunt quatuor partes, unicuique militi partem.* In hoc apparet, ut dicit Augustinus[^p497-3], quatuor fuisse milites, qui Dominum crucifixerunt. *Et tunicam,* subaudi: acceperunt.

36. (Vers. 24.). Hic notatur divisio tunicae facta *per sortem;* et ratio redditur: *Erat autem tunica inconsutilis, desuper contexta per totum,* id est tota continua; et quia non poterat convenienter dividi, *dixerunt* milites *ad invicem*[^p497-4]*: Non scindamus eam, sed sortiamur de illa, cuius sit;* quia, sicut dicitur Proverbiorum decimo octavo, « contradictiones comprimit sors et inter potentes quosque diiudicat ». — Et ne putetur, hoc casu factum, ostenditur, hoc a Propheta esse praedictum, cum dicit: *Ut Scriptura impleretur dicens,* Psalmo vigesimo primo[^p497-5]: « Deus, Deus meus, respice in me »: *Partiti sunt vestimenta mea,* quantum ad primum, *et super vestem meam miserunt sortem,* quantum ad divisionem tunicae. Et ista Scriptura intellecta fuit non de Iudaeis, sed de militibus; propterea dicit: *Et milites quidem haec fecerunt.*

*Spiritualiter* tunica Christi, quae indivisa, significat caritatem, quae est *vestis,* quia *operit turpitudinem;* primae Petri quarto[^p497-6]: *Quia caritas operit multitudinem peccatorum;* et *ornat,* unde dicitur *vestis nuptialis,* sine qua nemo intrat ad gaudium nuptiarum Agni; unde Matthaei vigesimo secundo: *Amice, quomodo huc intrasti non habens vestem nuptialem?* Haec tunica est *inconsutilis,* quia ipsa non alio ligatur, sed est vinculum aliorum; ad Colossenses tertio[^p497-7]: *Super omnia autem habete dilectionem, quae est vinculum perfectionis.* — Haec tunica *non est dividenda* per scissionem; unde primae ad Corinthios primo: *Obsecro vos, fratres, ut id ipsum dicatis omnes;* et ad Ephesios quarto: *Solliciti servare unitatem spiritus in vinculo pacis.* — Hanc tunicam qui dividunt peiores sunt militibus crucifigentibus, qui Domini vestem inconsutilem dividere noluerunt.

37. *Stabant autem iuxta crucem* etc. Hic notatur *compatientium paucitas;* quia ex omnibus caris eius tres aderant mulieres, inter quas et Mater Domini, cui etiam Dominus compatiebatur. Et notantur hic quatuor: *mulierum ad Dominum compassio, Domini erga Matrem sollicitudo* et ex sollicitudine *recommendatio* et ultimum *recommendationis acceptio*[^p497-8].

(Vers. 25.). Notatur ergo *mulierum compassio* in hoc quod dicit: *Stabant iuxta crucem Iesu;* ideo corpore appropinquabant, quia affectus compassionis trahebat. Alii vero longe recesserant per incompassionem; unde dicitur in Psalmo[^p497-9]: « Qui iuxta me erant de longe steterunt ». Sed istae steterunt iuxta, quae magis diligebant, scilicet *Mater eius,* quae super omnes compatiebatur; unde Lucae secundo:
<!-- page 498 -->
« Tuam ipsius animam pertransibit gladius »; *et soror Matris eius, Maria Cleophae;* haec fuit mater Iacobi. — Notandum est, quod Anna *dicitur* habuisse tres viros[^p498-1]: Ioachim, Cleopham et Salome, et ex istis tribus viris habuit tres Marias, scilicet Matrem Domini, quae fuit filia Ioachim; matrem Iacobi, quae fuit filia Cleophae; matrem Simonis et Iudae, quae fuit filia Salome. — *Et Maria Magdalene,* quae dicta est a Magdalo castro[^p498-2]. Istae tres mulieres tanquam magis compatientes stabant iuxta crucem Domini.

38. (Vers. 26.). *Cum vidisset ergo Iesus.* Hic notatur secundum, scilicet *Domini erga Matrem sollicitudo,* in hoc, quod respexit eam et cui posset eam recommendare; unde dicit: *Cum vidisset ergo Iesus Matrem; cum vidisset* ut sollicitus; primae ad Timotheum quinto[^p498-3]: « Si quis suorum et maxime domesticorum curam non habet » etc. Et Chrysostomus: « Hic multam dilectionem monstrat Dominus ad Matrem et commendat eam discipulo, erudiens nos, usque ad ultimam respirationem omnem facere diligentiam eorum qui nos genuerunt ». Exodi vigesimo: « Honora patrem tuum et matrem tuam » etc. Hoc quod praecepit, implevit. *Et discipulum stantem, quem diligebat;* et ideo eam sibi familiariter recommendare poterat. *Stabat* Ioannes, non recesserat; unde erat unus de illis quibus dicitur Lucae vigesimo secundo[^p498-4]: « Vos estis, qui permansistis mecum in tentationibus meis ».

*Dicit Matri suae.* Hic notatur tertium, scilicet *diligens recommendatio;* unde dicit: *Mulier, ecce, filius tuus;* quasi[^p498-5]: ita confidas de eo sicut de filio.

39. (Vers. 27.). *Deinde dicit discipulo: Ecce, mater tua;* quasi: ita eam custodias ut matrem.

*Et ex illa hora accepit eam discipulus in suam*[^p498-6]. Hic notatur *recommendationis acceptio; in suam,* scilicet matrem, *accepit,* ut illam, sicut matrem filius, honoraret, custodiret et servaret.

Sed Augustinus[^p498-7] legit litteram istam: *in sua,* ut sit accusativus pluralis; et quaerit: Quomodo *in sua,* cum non haberet propria? — Et respondet: *in sua,* scilicet officia, servitia et beneficia, non in possessiones, quas non habebat.

### Quaestiones.

40. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod supra[^p498-8] dictum est, quod *exivit* Iesus *baiulans sibi crucem;* quia Matthaei vigesimo septimo dicitur, quod *angariaverunt Simonem* quendam, *ut tolleret crucem eius.*

Respondet Augustinus in tertio libro de Concordia Evangelistarum[^p498-9]: « Iesus portabat crucem, cum exiret in Calvariam; Simon autem ille in itinere angariatus est, cui data est postea crux portanda usque ad locum. Ita utrumque factum legimus, primo id quod Ioannes dicit, deinde id quod Matthaeus ».

41. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de titulo, utrum Pilatus scripsit *vere,* vel *irrisorie.* — Quod *vere,* videtur per litteram, quia, Iudaeis petentibus, delere noluit, sed respondit[^p498-10]: *Quod scripsi scripsi.*

Quod autem *irrisorie,* videtur, quia ipsum tanquam stultum, non tanquam verum regem derideri permisit a militibus, qui *coronam spineam imposuerant et alapas dabant*[^p498-11].

Ad hoc respondendum, quod Pilatus aliquam illustrationem habuit, qua cognovit, ipsum esse innocentem; unde *sciebat, quod per invidiam tradidissent eum,* Matthaei vigesimo septimo[^p498-12]; qua etiam cognovit, ipsum esse regem Iudaeis promissum; unde semper eum vocabat *regem.* Unde Augustinus[^p498-13]: « Avelli a corde Pilati non potuit, Christum esse regem Iudaeorum, tanquam in eius corde ipsa veritas fixerit ».
<!-- page 499 -->
Quod obiicit, quod derideri fecit vel permisit; dico, quod sic permisit, derideri regem, sicut flagellari innocentem, ut Iudaeos de poenis eius et opprobriis satiaret et ad misericordiam inclinaret[^p499-1].

42. Quaest. III. Sed tunc est quaestio: quomodo fuit ausus eum morti tradere?

Ad hoc dicendum, quod fuit timore perterritus, ne accusaretur apud Caesarem, et errore deceptus, quia putabat, totam culpam esse Iudaeorum, ex quo ad ipsorum petitionem mittebat in eum manum; unde dicitur Matthaei vigesimo septimo[^p499-2], quod *accepit aquam et lavit manus suas et ait: Innocens ego sum;* et Iudaei totam culpam super se accipiebant, unde dixerunt: *Sanguis eius super nos et super filios nostros.* Sed erravit, quia in mortem innocentis consentiri non potest sine peccato.

43. Quaest. IV. Sed cum Matthaei vigesimo septimo[^p499-3] dicatur: *Diviserunt vestimenta eius, sortem mittentes;* videtur, quod non solum super tunicam, sed super omnia vestimenta miserunt sortem; Ioannes autem dicit solum de tunica. — Illud idem dicitur Marci decimo quinto[^p499-4]: *Diviserunt eius vestimenta, mittentes sortem super eis, quis quid tolleret.*

Ad hoc respondet Augustinus[^p499-5] et dicit, quod « illud Matthaei intelligitur de tota divisione vestimentorum, quam quidem facientes venerunt ad tunicam, de qua sortem miserunt ».

Ad illud Marci dicendum, quod is est sensus: « diviserunt vestimenta primum, dein mittentes sortem super tunicam, quae remanserat vel superfuerat, quis tolleret eam[^p499-6] ».

44. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur, quare Dominus sic Matrem recommendavit, cum supra secundo[^p499-7] ita dure responderit: *Quid mihi et tibi est, mulier?*

Respondet Augustinus[^p499-8], et habetur in Glossa: « Quia tunc, divina facturus, quasi ignotam repulit Matrem; iam ostendit, venisse horam, quam tunc praedixit, in qua moriturus agnoscit, de qua fuerat mortaliter natus, et humana patiens affectu ipsam commendat humano et exemplo instruit, ut a piis filiis impendatur cura parentibus ». — Ratio autem, quare eam commendat Ioanni, ex parte *Domini* tangitur in littera[^p499-9], quia ipsum specialiter diligebat; sed ex parte *sua* tacetur; et dicunt Sancti, quod fuit, « ut Virginem virgo custodiret ».

## English

#### Third, on the passion of Christ two things are noted.

27. *And they took Jesus.* There was treatment above[^p495-5] of the seizure and condemnation of Christ; here there is treatment of his *passion;* and this part is divided into two, because first it treats of the *passion;* second, of the *consummation of the passion,* there[^p495-6]: *Afterwards, Jesus knowing* etc.

In the first part his passion is described as to four things, which are intimated there. For first there is described the *vileness of the passion;* second, the *dignity of the one suffering,* there[^p495-7]: *And Pilate wrote a title also;* third, the *cruelty of those crucifying* or their cupidity, there: *The soldiers therefore, when they had crucified him;* fourth, the *fewness of those suffering with him,* there: *Now there stood by the cross.*

The *vileness* of the passion, then, is described in the *going,* in the *place,* in the *kind of the passion* and in the *company of those suffering.*

28. (Vers. 16.). First, therefore, the *vileness of the passion in the going* is noted in this, that he is led like a thief, and he himself carries his own penalty; therefore he says: *And they took Jesus;* the Psalm[^p495-8]: « They have taken me, as a lion prepared for the prey », that is, the soldiers took Jesus; *they led* him, as an evildoer, that is, outside the camp; on account of which, to the Hebrews, the last chapter: « Let us go forth to him outside the camp, bearing » the cross « of his reproach ».

29. (Vers. 17.). *And bearing his own cross,* on which, that is, he would be punished like a thief. Then was fulfilled what had been said in Isaias, the twenty-second chapter[^p495-9]: « I will lay the key of David upon his shoulder »; and in Isaias, the ninth chapter: « The government is upon his shoulder », because there he triumphed. On this Augustine: « A great spectacle! But if impiety looks on, a great mockery; if piety, a great mystery ».

*He went forth to that place which is called the place of Calvary*[^p495-10]. Here the vileness from the *place* is intimated; because he suffered in that place where thieves and evildoers were punished. For that place is called *Calvary* because there the heads
<!-- page 496 -->
of the condemned were made bald. For *calvaria* is the name for the shell of the head stripped of flesh; *in Hebrew Golgotha,* that is, « the place of beheading[^p496-1] ».

30. (Vers. 18.). *Where they crucified him.* Here the vileness of the passion is noted from the *kind of death,* because it was the vilest kind of death; Wisdom, the second chapter[^p496-2]: « Let us condemn him to a most shameful death ».

*And with him two others*[^p496-3]*, one on each side, and Jesus in the midst.* Here the vileness from the *company* is noted, because those two were thieves; whence Mark, the fifteenth chapter: « With him they crucify two thieves ». Then was fulfilled what is said in Isaias, the fifty-third chapter: « And he was reputed with the wicked ». But he is in the *middle* for this reason, that it may be signified that he is the *mediator;* whence in the nativity he is in the *midst of the animals,* Habacuc, the last chapter[^p496-4]; and in the passion in the *midst of the thieves,* because he is true peace, to the Ephesians, the second chapter.

31. *And Pilate wrote a title also.* Here the second principal member is noted, namely the *dignity of the one suffering,* and it is intimated in the title, because the title was a king's; and this title is described in this order: first, its *inscription;* second, the *publication of what was written;* third, the *contradiction*[^p496-5]*;* fourth, its *approbation.*

(Vers. 19.). The *inscription,* then, *of the title* is touched on when he says: *And Pilate wrote a title also, and he put it upon the cross;* Isaias, the nineteenth chapter[^p496-6]: « There shall be an altar of the Lord in the midst of the land of Egypt, and a title of the Lord at the border thereof ». — And note that one title is *memorial,* as that of the dead; another *proclamatory,* as of a house; another *triumphal;* and this one is triumphal[^p496-7], whence it is to the praise of Christ and the vituperation of the Jews, because, although he had been condemned like a thief, yet he was not a thief, but a king; and this the title said: *And the writing was: Jesus of Nazareth, the king of the Jews.* Scripture approves this title; whence in the second Psalm[^p496-8]: « I am appointed king by him over Sion, his holy mountain » etc. In this title Pilate describes him by the *name,* when he says *Jesus;* in Luke, the first chapter: « Thou shalt call his name Jesus »; by the *homeland, of Nazareth;* in Luke, the second chapter: « He went down with them and came to Nazareth »; by the *royal dignity,* when he says *king;* in Jeremias, the twenty-third chapter: « I will raise up to David a just branch, and a king shall reign ». — According to Matthew the title is called the *cause;* in Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p496-9]: « They put over his head his cause written »; because in that title it is intimated why he died, namely for *saving,* because he is *Jesus,* and for *making us a kingdom to our God;* Apocalypse, the fifth chapter: « Thou art worthy, O Lord God, to take the book; because thou wast slain and hast redeemed us to God in thy blood and hast made us to our God a kingdom ».

32. (Vers. 20.). *This title therefore.* Here the *publication of the title* is set down; whence he says: *Many of the Jews did read, because the place where Jesus was crucified was nigh to the city;* therefore many read it, because many ran together, not only Jews, but others also; Isaias, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p496-10]: « Search ye diligently in the book of the words of the Lord, and read; not one of them was wanting ». *And it was written in Hebrew, in Greek, and in Latin,* so that all might be able to read the title, because he was to be king for all, as is said in the Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter[^p496-11], that he « is King of kings and Lord of lords »; therefore in three tongues, because in Deuteronomy,
<!-- page 497 -->
the nineteenth chapter: « In the mouth of two or three witnesses every word shall stand ».

33. (Vers. 21.). *Then the high priests of the Jews said to Pilate.* Here the *contradiction of the Jews* is noted, by which they wished to blot out the title, since it was to their ignominy; whence they say: *Write not: The King of the Jews, but that*[^p497-1] *he said: I am the King of the Jews,* so that, namely, there might be shown not dignity, but presumption, by which he would have said that he was king; against which it is said to the Hebrews, the fifth chapter: « Christ did not glorify himself, that he might be made a high priest » etc.

34. (Vers. 22.). *Pilate answered.* Here the *affirmation of the title* or approbation is set down against the malice of the Jews; whence Pilate does not blot it out, but approves it: *What I have written, I have written. What I have written,* truthfully, *I have written,* indelibly, as a truth irrevocable. Augustine[^p497-2]: « If what Pilate wrote cannot be corrupted, can what the Truth said be corrupted »?

35. *The soldiers therefore, when they had crucified him.* Here the third principal member is noted, namely the *cruelty of those crucifying* or their cupidity, in this, that they stripped Christ and so divided his garments. And three things are noted here, namely the *division* of the garments *by parts;* second, *by lot;* third, the *confirmation of both* by authority, lest anything should seem to be done by chance in the death of Christ.

(Vers. 23.). They divide, therefore, *by parts;* whence he says: *They took his garments and made four parts, to every soldier a part.* In this it appears, as Augustine says[^p497-3], that there were four soldiers who crucified the Lord. *And also his coat,* understand: they took.

36. (Vers. 24.). Here is noted the division of the coat made *by lot;* and the reason is given: *Now the coat was without seam, woven from the top throughout,* that is, wholly continuous; and because it could not fittingly be divided, the soldiers *said one to another*[^p497-4]*: Let us not cut it, but let us cast lots for it, whose it shall be;* because, as is said in Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter, « the lot suppresseth contentions and determineth even between the mighty ». — And lest it be thought that this was done by chance, it is shown that this was foretold by the Prophet, when he says: *That the scripture might be fulfilled, saying,* in the twenty-first Psalm[^p497-5]: « O God, my God, look upon me »: *They have parted my garments,* as to the first, *and upon my vesture they have cast lot,* as to the division of the coat. And this Scripture was understood not of the Jews, but of the soldiers; therefore he says: *And the soldiers indeed did these things.*

*Spiritually* the coat of Christ, which is undivided, signifies charity, which is a *garment,* because it *covers turpitude;* the first of Peter, the fourth chapter[^p497-6]: *Because charity covereth a multitude of sins;* and it *adorns,* whence it is called the *wedding garment,* without which no one enters into the joy of the marriage of the Lamb; whence in Matthew, the twenty-second chapter: *Friend, how camest thou in hither not having a wedding garment?* This coat is *without seam,* because it is not bound by another, but is the bond of the others; to the Colossians, the third chapter[^p497-7]: *But above all things have dilection, which is the bond of perfection.* — This coat *is not to be divided* by cutting; whence in the first to the Corinthians, the first chapter: *I beseech you, brethren, that you all speak the same thing;* and to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter: *Careful to keep the unity of the spirit in the bond of peace.* — Those who divide this coat are worse than the soldiers who crucified, who would not divide the Lord's seamless garment.

37. *Now there stood by the cross* etc. Here the *fewness of those suffering with him* is noted; because out of all his dear ones three women were present, among whom also the Mother of the Lord, with whom the Lord also suffered. And four things are noted here: the *compassion of the women toward the Lord, the Lord's solicitude toward his Mother,* and from the solicitude the *recommendation,* and last the *acceptance of the recommendation*[^p497-8].

(Vers. 25.). The *compassion of the women,* therefore, is noted in this, that he says: *There stood by the cross of Jesus;* they drew near in body for this reason, that the affection of compassion drew them. But others had withdrawn far off through lack of compassion; whence it is said in the Psalm[^p497-9]: « They that were near me stood afar off ». But these stood near who loved the more, namely *his Mother,* who suffered with him above all; whence in Luke, the second chapter:
<!-- page 498 -->
« Thy own soul a sword shall pierce »; *and his mother's sister, Mary of Cleophas;* she was the mother of James. — It is to be noted that Anna *is said* to have had three husbands[^p498-1]: Joachim, Cleophas and Salome, and of these three husbands she had three Marys, namely the Mother of the Lord, who was the daughter of Joachim; the mother of James, who was the daughter of Cleophas; the mother of Simon and Jude, who was the daughter of Salome. — *And Mary Magdalen,* who is so called from the stronghold of Magdalum[^p498-2]. These three women, as the ones suffering with him the more, stood by the cross of the Lord.

38. (Vers. 26.). *When Jesus therefore had seen.* Here the second is noted, namely the *Lord's solicitude toward his Mother,* in this, that he looked upon her and to whom he might recommend her; whence he says: *When Jesus therefore had seen his Mother; had seen,* as one solicitous; the first to Timothy, the fifth chapter[^p498-3]: « If any man have not care of his own, and especially of those of his house » etc. And Chrysostom: « Here the Lord shows much dilection toward his Mother and commends her to the disciple, teaching us to show all diligence, up to the last breath, toward those who begot us ». Exodus, the twentieth chapter: « Honour thy father and thy mother » etc. What he commanded, he fulfilled. *And the disciple standing whom he loved;* and therefore he could recommend her to him familiarly. John *stood,* he had not withdrawn; whence he was one of those to whom it is said in Luke, the twenty-second chapter[^p498-4]: « You are they who have continued with me in my temptations ».

*He saith to his Mother.* Here the third is noted, namely the *diligent recommendation;* whence he says: *Woman, behold thy son;* as if[^p498-5]: so confide in him as in a son.

39. (Vers. 27.). *After that, he saith to the disciple: Behold thy mother;* as if: so guard her as a mother.

*And from that hour the disciple took her to his own*[^p498-6]. Here the *acceptance of the recommendation* is noted; *to his own,* namely mother, *he took,* so that he might honour, guard and keep her, as a son his mother.

But Augustine[^p498-7] reads this text: *in sua* ("to his own things"), so that it is an accusative plural; and he asks: How *to his own things,* since he had no property? — And he responds: *to his own things,* namely offices, services and benefits, not into possessions, which he did not have.

### Questions.

40. Quaest. I. But it is asked here first about what was said above[^p498-8], that Jesus *went forth bearing his own cross;* because in Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter, it is said that *they forced* a certain *Simon to take up his cross.*

Augustine responds in the third book *On the Agreement of the Evangelists*[^p498-9]: « Jesus was carrying the cross when he went out to Calvary; but that Simon was forced on the way, to whom the cross was afterwards given to be carried as far as the place. So we read that both were done, first that which John says, then that which Matthew says ».

41. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked about the title, whether Pilate wrote it *truly,* or *mockingly.* — That *truly,* seems so from the text, because, when the Jews asked, he would not blot it out, but responded[^p498-10]: *What I have written, I have written.*

But that *mockingly,* seems so, because he allowed him to be derided by the soldiers as a fool, not as a true king, who *had put a crown of thorns upon him and gave him blows*[^p498-11].

To this it is to be responded that Pilate had some illumination, by which he recognized that he was innocent; whence *he knew that for envy they had delivered him,* in Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p498-12]; by which also he recognized that he was the king promised to the Jews; whence he always called him *king.* Whence Augustine[^p498-13]: « It could not be torn out of Pilate's heart that Christ was king of the Jews, as though the truth itself had fixed it in his heart ».
<!-- page 499 -->
As to what he objects, that he caused or allowed him to be derided; I say that he allowed the king to be derided in this way, as he allowed the innocent one to be scourged, that he might sate the Jews with his punishments and reproaches and incline them to mercy[^p499-1].

42. Quaest. III. But then there is the question: how did he dare to deliver him to death?

To this it is to be said that he was thoroughly terrified by fear, lest he be accused before Caesar, and deceived by error, because he thought that the whole guilt was the Jews', since he was laying hand on him at their petition; whence it is said in Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p499-2], that *he took water and washed his hands and said: I am innocent;* and the Jews took the whole guilt upon themselves, whence they said: *His blood be upon us and upon our children.* But he erred, because one cannot consent to the death of an innocent man without sin.

43. Quaest. IV. But since in Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p499-3], it is said: *They divided his garments, casting lots;* it seems that they cast lots not only upon the coat, but upon all the garments; but John speaks only of the coat. — The same is said in Mark, the fifteenth chapter[^p499-4]: *They divided his garments, casting lots upon them, what every man should take.*

To this Augustine responds[^p499-5] and says that « that of Matthew is understood of the whole division of the garments, in the making of which they came to the coat, for which they cast lots ».

To that of Mark it is to be said that this is the sense: « they divided the garments first, then casting lots upon the coat, which had remained or was left over, who should take it[^p499-6] ».

44. Quaest. V. Likewise it is asked why the Lord recommended his Mother so, when above, the second chapter[^p499-7], he responded so harshly: *Woman, what is that to me and to thee?*

Augustine responds[^p499-8], and it is had in the Gloss: « Because then, being about to do divine things, he repelled his Mother as though unknown; now he shows that the hour had come which he then foretold, in which, about to die, he acknowledges her of whom he had been born mortal, and, suffering human things, commends her with human affection and instructs by example, that care be bestowed on parents by pious children ». — But the reason why he commends her to John is touched on in the text[^p499-9] on the part of the *Lord,* because he loved him specially; but on his *own* part it is passed over in silence; and the Saints say that it was « that a virgin might guard the Virgin ».
