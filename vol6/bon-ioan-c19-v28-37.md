# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XIX, Vers. 28-37
## *Hic agit de passionis consummatione*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 499 -->

45. *Postea sciens Iesus* etc. Supra[^p499-10] egit de passione, hic agit de *passionis consummatione,* et sunt hic tres partes. Primo dicitur eius *consummatio* quantum ad *Scripturae testimonium;* secundo, quantum ad *factum,* ibi[^p499-11]: *Et inclinato capite;* tertio, quantum ad *effectum,* ibi: *Iudaei ergo, quoniam parasceve erat.*

Consummatio igitur passionis Christi quantum ad *Scripturae testimonium* attenditur in hoc, quod circa ipsum omnia fierent, quae per Prophetas praedicta erant. Et insinuatur ipsa[^p499-12] hoc ordine, scilicet *consummandi intentio, ipsa consummatio, consummationis assertio.*

46. (Vers. 28.). *Consummandi intentio* ergo innuitur in hoc quod dicit: *Postea sciens Iesus, quia iam*[^p499-13] *omnia consummata sunt,* supple: quae scripta sunt de Filio hominis; sed non plene, ideo dicit *iam; ut consummaretur Scriptura,* id est, volens perfecte consummare Scripturam, quae dicit in Psalmo[^p499-14]: « In siti mea potaverunt me aceto »; *dicit: Sitio.* Et hoc fuit signum infirmitatis assumtae et veritatis naturae contra illos haereticos, qui dicebant, quod non erat vere passus. Et hoc quidem non tantum dixit
<!-- page 500 -->
propter indigentiam, quantum ad complendam Scripturam.

47. (Vers. 29.). *Vas ergo erat positum.* Hic ponitur *ipsa consummatio,* qua potaverunt eum aceto, quod quidem ad manum habebant; unde dicit: *Vas ergo erat positum aceto plenum; illi autem spongiam plenam aceto, hyssopo circumponentes,* id est ponentes spongiam circa hyssopum ligatam, *obtulerunt ori eius,* per arundinem, sicut dicitur Matthaei vigesimo septimo[^p500-1]: « Currens unus ex eis acceptam spongiam implevit aceto et imposuit arundini et dabat ei bibere ».

48. (Vers. 30.). *Cum ergo accepisset Iesus acetum.* Hic ponitur *consummationis assertio;* unde dicit: *Cum ergo accepisset Iesus acetum, dixit: Consummatum est,* scilicet quod scriptum erat de me ante spiritus emissionem. De hac consummatione Lucae decimo octavo[^p500-2]: « Consummabuntur omnia, quae scripta sunt de Filio hominis »; ad Hebraeos quinto: « Consummatus, factus est omnibus obtemperantibus sibi causa salutis aeternae ».

*Et inclinato capite* etc. Posita consummatione in Scripturae testimonio, hic ponit consummationem *in facto;* et haec consummatio fuit in morte, quia finis passionum praesentium mors est; unde dicit: *Inclinato capite, tradidit spiritum.* Augustinus[^p500-3]: « *Tradidit spiritum,* tanquam ille qui *potestatem habebat ponendi animam suam et iterum sumendi eam;* et hoc *inclinato capite.* Quis ita dormit, quando voluerit, sicut Christus, quando voluit, mortuus est »? Unde spiritum *obtulit,* sicut dicitur ad Hebraeos quinto[^p500-4]: « Cum lacrymis et clamore valido offerens » spiritum suum. Et haec fuit consummatio praedictorum, sicut dicitur ad Hebraeos decimo: « Una oblatione consummavit in sempiternum sanctificatos »; et ad Hebraeos secundo: « Decebat, eum qui multos filios adduxerat in gloriam, per passionem consummare ».

49. *Iudaei vero*[^p500-5], *quoniam parasceve erat.* Hic ponitur consummatio in *passionis effectu,* quae quidem fuit in apertione lateris Christi, ex quo fluxit « sanguis et aqua[^p500-6] », unde efficaciam habent Sacramenta, ut dicit Glossa. Et notantur hic quatuor, scilicet *petitio depositionis crucifixorum, crurifragium latronum, apertio lateris Christi* et *confirmatio testimonii.*

(Vers. 31.). *Petitio depositionis crucifixorum* facta est a Iudaeis; et ratio redditur, quia erat in die sequenti magnus dies Sabbati; propter quod dicit: *Iudaei ergo, quoniam parasceve erat,* id est dies proximus ante Sabbatum[^p500-7]; *ut non remanerent in cruce corpora Sabbato,* propter solemnitatem scilicet; *erat enim magnus dies ille Sabbati,* quia intra solemnitatem azymorum; *rogaverunt Pilatum, ut frangerentur crura eorum et tollerentur.* Augustinus[^p500-8]: « Ideo eorum frangebantur crura, ut morerentur et auferrentur ex ligno, ne pendentes in crucibus magnum diem festum sui diuturni cruciatus norrore foedarent ». Deuteronomii vigesimo primo: « Quando peccaverit homo quod morte plectendum est, et adiudicatus morti suspensus fuerit in patibulo, non permanebit cadaver eius in ligno, sed in eadem die sepelietur ».

50. (Vers. 32.). *Venerunt ergo milites.* Hic notatur *crurifragium latronum. Venerunt ergo milites,* ad petitionem Iudaeorum, *et primi quidem,* scilicet ad quem primo venerunt, *fregerunt crura,* quia vivebat adhuc; *et alterius, qui crucifixus erat*[^p500-9] *cum eo,* supple: crura fregerunt, et sic exspiraverunt; ut sic ipsi distinguerentur ab Agno, cuius ossa debebant integre conservari, quia solus integer a peccato. Illi poterant dicere illud Psalmi sexti: « Sana me, Domine, quoniam conturbata sunt ossa mea ».

51. (Vers. 33.). *Ad Iesum autem cum venissent.* Hic notatur *apertio lateris Christi.* Et primo dicit, quare non fregerunt crura eius, scilicet quia mortuus erat; et hoc est quod dicit: *Ut viderunt eum*[^p500-10] *mortuum, non fregerunt eius crura;* quia propter hoc faciebant, ut accelerarent mortem. Et subditur apertio; unde dicit:

<!-- page 501 -->
52. (Vers. 34.). *Sed unus militum lancea latus eius aperuit.* Augustinus[^p501-1]: « De hoc non dicit *vulneravit,* sed *aperuit,* ut illic quodam modo ostium panderetur, unde Sacramenta Ecclesiae manaverunt ». Et propterea subditur: *Et continuo exivit sanguis et aqua.* Augustinus: « Iste sanguis in remissionem fusus est peccatorum; aqua ista salutare temperat poculum; haec et lavacrum praestat et potum »; Apocalypsis primo[^p501-2]: « Lavit nos a peccatis nostris in sanguine suo »; et ad Ephesios quinto: « Christus dilexit Ecclesiam, mundans eam lavacro » etc.

53. (Vers. 35.). *Et qui vidit* etc. Hic innuitur quartum, scilicet *testimonii confirmatio.* Et hoc confirmat *testimonio suo* et *Scripturae testimonio: suo,* quia ipse vidit et praesens fuit; unde dicit: *Et qui vidit testimonium perhibuit.* Et approbat testimonium, quia *verum,* unde: *et verum est testimonium eius;* et quia *certum,* unde: *et ille scit, quia vera dicit.* Et ideo dicit: *Ut et vos credatis;* quia citius fides accommodatur ei qui vidit, quam qui relatione cognovit; Proverbiorum duodecimo[^p501-3]: « Qui quod novit loquitur iudex iustitiae est; qui autem mentitur testis est fraudulentus »; et primae Ioannis primo: « Quod fuit ab initio, quod audivimus, quod vidimus oculis nostris, quod perspeximus et manus nostrae contrectaverunt de Verbo vitae » etc. — Non solum hoc *suo* confirmat *testimonio,* sed etiam *Scripturae,* unde dicit:

54. (Vers. 36.). *Facta sunt*[^p501-4] *haec, ut Scriptura impleretur: Os non comminuetis ex eo,* Exodi duodecimo; et ideo non fregerunt crura.

55. (Vers. 37.). *Et iterum alia Scriptura dicit: Videbunt in quem transfixerunt;* Zachariae duodecimo[^p501-5]: « Aspicient ad me, quem confixerunt »; et ideo verum est, quod miles latus eius aperuit. Haec Scriptura nondum plene impleta est, sed in die iudicii; Apocalypsis primo: « Videbit eum omnis oculus, et qui eum pupugerunt ». Chrysostomus[^p501-6]: « Nullus discredat neque verecundans aspiciat; quae enim maxime exprobrabilissima videntur esse, haec sunt fundamenta nostrorum bonorum ».

### Quaestiones.

56. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod dicit, quod, *ut consummaretur Scriptura,* petiit potum. — Videtur ordo praeposterus, quia non Dominus est propter Legem, sed Lex et Scriptura propter Dominum; unde[^p501-7]: *Finis Legis Christus.*

Dicendum, quod *ut* non est ibi *causale,* sed *consecutivum*[^p501-8]; ut non sit sensus, quod propter Scripturam implendam hoc Dominus fecerit, sed hoc consecutum est ad factum Domini, scilicet impletio Scripturae, quae hoc praedixerat.

57. Quaest. II. Sed est quaestio, quare magis dicitur Dominus consummasse Scripturam in potu aceti quam in aliis.

Et responsio est, quod Dominus in omnibus membris passus fuerat; ut totus pateretur, non restabat nisi os et lingua; ideo, quia tunc generaliter omnia membra sunt passa, tunc dicitur *passio* quodam modo consummata, et per consequens *Scriptura*[^p501-9].

58. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc, quod statim sic habuerunt acetum paratum; quomodo hoc fuerit. Nunquid sciebant, quod ipse potum peteret?

Ad hoc dicunt quidam, quod vinum ad bibendum portaverant, quod propter calorem conversum fuerat in acetum. — Alii dicunt, quod studiose acetum portaverant, ut ipsum bibendo citius interiret[^p501-10].
<!-- page 502 -->
— Alii dicunt, quod erat vinum myrrhatum, quod erat amarissimum et acutum sicut acetum, ad magis cruciandum eum iam crucifixum. Unde dicunt, tunc etiam impletam esse Scripturam[^p502-1]: *Dederunt in escam meam fel, et in siti mea potaverunt me aceto.*

59. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit Ioannes, quod dixit Christus: *Consummatum est, et inclinato capite, emisit spiritum.* Lucas vero vigesimo tertio[^p502-2] dicit, quod clamavit: *Pater, in manus tuas commendo spiritum meum;* sed Matthaeus et Marcus, quod clamavit voce magna: *Eli, Eli* etc.

Et intelligendum, quod omnia ista dixit; sed primum dixit: *Sitio;* deinde, dum illi acetum pararent, clamavit: *Eli;* et ad hanc vocem qui parabat acceleravit ferre acetum et circumponere; quo accepto, dixit: *Consummatum est;* et tunc inclinans caput, cum clamore valido exspiravit dicens: *In manus tuas*[^p502-3] etc.

60. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur: quare latus eius apertum sive vulneratum fuit post mortem et non ante?

Et intelligendum, quod nulla facta sunt circa eum, nisi quae Deus congrua ratione permisit. Huius autem redditur ratio *allegorica* et *litteralis: allegorica,* quia, sicut de latere Adae dormientis formata est Eva, ita de latere Christi dormientis in cruce formata est Ecclesia. Unde Augustinus[^p502-4]: « Ob hoc secundus Adam in cruce dormivit, ut inde formaretur eius coniux, quod de latere eius effluxit ». — *Litteralis* vero ratio est, quia Dominus Christus, etsi ostenderet humanitatem, tamen volebat simul Divinitatis ostendere veritatem. Unde hoc ostendit ante passionem in sui comprehensione, hoc in passione in tenebris, hoc post mortem ostendit in effluxione aquae et sanguinis. Unde Ambrosius[^p502-5]: « Licet corporis Christi fuerit natura mortalis, dissimilis tamen gratia fuit. Nam post mortem sanguis in nostris corporibus coalescit, ex illo autem incorrupto corpore omnium vita manavit, aqua et sanguis exivit; illa, quae abluat; iste, qui redimat; bibimus pretium nostrum, ut bibendo redimamur ».

## English
<!-- page 499 -->

45. *Afterwards, Jesus knowing* etc. Above[^p499-10] he treated of the passion; here he treats of the *consummation of the passion,* and there are three parts here. First his *consummation* is told as to the *testimony of Scripture;* second, as to the *deed,* there[^p499-11]: *And bowing his head;* third, as to the *effect,* there: *Then the Jews, because it was the parasceve.*

The consummation of the passion of Christ as to the *testimony of Scripture,* then, is regarded in this, that all things that had been foretold by the Prophets were done concerning him. And it[^p499-12] is intimated in this order, namely the *intention of consummating, the consummation itself, the assertion of the consummation.*

46. (Vers. 28.). The *intention of consummating,* then, is hinted at in this, that he says: *Afterwards, Jesus knowing that now*[^p499-13] *all things were consummated,* supply: which were written of the Son of man; but not fully, therefore he says *now; that the Scripture might be consummated,* that is, willing to consummate perfectly the Scripture, which says in the Psalm[^p499-14]: « In my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink »; *he says: I thirst.* And this was a sign of the infirmity assumed and of the truth of the nature, against those heretics who said that he had not truly suffered. And this indeed he said not only
<!-- page 500 -->
on account of need, as to completing the Scripture.

47. (Vers. 29.). *Now there was a vessel set there.* Here is set down *the consummation itself,* by which they gave him vinegar to drink, which indeed they had at hand; whence he says: *Now there was a vessel set there full of vinegar; and they, putting a sponge full of vinegar about hyssop,* that is, putting the sponge tied about the hyssop, *offered it to his mouth,* by a reed, as is said in Matthew, the twenty-seventh chapter[^p500-1]: « One of them running, taking a sponge, filled it with vinegar and put it on a reed and gave him to drink ».

48. (Vers. 30.). *When Jesus therefore had taken the vinegar.* Here is set down the *assertion of the consummation;* whence he says: *When Jesus therefore had taken the vinegar, he said: It is consummated,* namely, what was written of me before the sending forth of the spirit. Of this consummation, Luke, the eighteenth chapter[^p500-2]: « All things shall be consummated which are written of the Son of man »; to the Hebrews, the fifth chapter: « Being consummated, he became, to all that obey him, the cause of eternal salvation ».

*And bowing his head* etc. The consummation in the testimony of Scripture having been set down, here he sets down the consummation *in the deed;* and this consummation was in death, because death is the end of present sufferings; whence he says: *Bowing his head, he gave up the spirit.* Augustine[^p500-3]: « *He gave up the spirit,* as one who *had power to lay down his life and to take it up again;* and this *bowing his head.* Who so sleeps when he will, as Christ died when he willed »? Whence he *offered* the spirit, as is said to the Hebrews, the fifth chapter[^p500-4]: « With tears and a strong cry offering » his spirit. And this was the consummation of the things foretold, as is said to the Hebrews, the tenth chapter: « By one oblation he hath consummated for ever them that are sanctified »; and to the Hebrews, the second chapter: « It became him, who had brought many children into glory, to consummate through the passion ».

49. *But the Jews*[^p500-5], *because it was the parasceve.* Here is set down the consummation in the *effect of the passion,* which indeed was in the opening of the side of Christ, from which flowed « blood and water[^p500-6] », whence the Sacraments have efficacy, as the Gloss says. And four things are noted here, namely the *petition for the taking down of the crucified, the breaking of the legs of the thieves, the opening of the side of Christ* and the *confirmation of the testimony.*

(Vers. 31.). The *petition for the taking down of the crucified* was made by the Jews; and the reason is rendered, because on the following day was the great day of the Sabbath; on account of which he says: *Then the Jews, because it was the parasceve,* that is, the day next before the Sabbath[^p500-7]; *that the bodies might not remain upon the cross on the Sabbath,* namely on account of the solemnity; *for that was a great Sabbath day,* because within the solemnity of the azymes; *they besought Pilate that their legs might be broken, and that they might be taken away.* Augustine[^p500-8]: « Therefore their legs were broken, that they might die and be taken away from the tree, lest, hanging on the crosses, they should defile the great feast day with the horror [so printed: *norrore* for *horrore*] of their long torment ». Deuteronomy, the twenty-first chapter: « When a man hath sinned in what is to be punished with death, and, being adjudged to death, is hanged on a gibbet, his carcass shall not remain upon the tree, but shall be buried the same day ».

50. (Vers. 32.). *The soldiers therefore came.* Here is noted the *breaking of the legs of the thieves. The soldiers therefore came,* at the petition of the Jews, *and of the first indeed,* namely of the one to whom they came first, *they broke the legs,* because he was still living; *and of the other that was crucified*[^p500-9] *with him,* supply: they broke the legs, and so they expired; that thus they might be distinguished from the Lamb, whose bones were to be preserved whole, because he alone was whole from sin. They could say that of the sixth Psalm: « Heal me, O Lord, for my bones are troubled ».

51. (Vers. 33.). *But after they were come to Jesus.* Here is noted the *opening of the side of Christ.* And first he says why they did not break his legs, namely because he was dead; and this is what he says: *When they saw him*[^p500-10] *dead, they did not break his legs;* because they were doing it for this, that they might hasten death. And the opening is subjoined; whence he says:

<!-- page 501 -->
52. (Vers. 34.). *But one of the soldiers with a spear opened his side.* Augustine[^p501-1]: « Of this he does not say *wounded,* but *opened,* that there in some manner a door might be laid open, whence the Sacraments of the Church flowed forth ». And therefore it is subjoined: *And immediately there came out blood and water.* Augustine: « This blood was shed for the remission of sins; this water tempers the saving cup; it furnishes both laver and drink »; Apocalypse, the first chapter[^p501-2]: « He hath washed us from our sins in his own blood »; and to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter: « Christ loved the Church, cleansing it by the laver » etc.

53. (Vers. 35.). *And he that saw it* etc. Here is hinted the fourth, namely the *confirmation of the testimony.* And he confirms this by *his own testimony* and by *the testimony of Scripture: his own,* because he himself saw and was present; whence he says: *And he that saw it hath given testimony.* And he approves the testimony, because it is *true,* whence: *and his testimony is true;* and because it is *certain,* whence: *and he knoweth that he saith true.* And therefore he says: *That you also may believe;* because faith is more quickly given to one who saw than to one who knew by report; Proverbs, the twelfth chapter[^p501-3]: « He that speaketh that which he knoweth is a judge of justice; but he that lieth is a deceitful witness »; and the first of John, the first chapter: « That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled, of the Word of life » etc. — Not only does he confirm this by *his own testimony,* but also by that *of Scripture,* whence he says:

54. (Vers. 36.). *These things were done*[^p501-4], *that the Scripture might be fulfilled: You shall not break a bone of him,* Exodus, the twelfth chapter; and therefore they did not break his legs.

55. (Vers. 37.). *And again another Scripture saith: They shall look on him whom they pierced;* Zacharias, the twelfth chapter[^p501-5]: « They shall look upon me, whom they have pierced »; and therefore it is true that the soldier opened his side. This Scripture is not yet fully fulfilled, but in the day of judgement; Apocalypse, the first chapter: « Every eye shall see him, and they also that pierced him ». Chrysostom[^p501-6]: « Let no one disbelieve, nor look on it ashamed; for the things which seem most of all most reproachable, these are the foundations of our goods ».

### Questions.

56. Quaest. I. But it is asked here first concerning this, that he says that, *that the Scripture might be consummated,* he asked for drink. — The order seems inverted, because the Lord is not for the sake of the Law, but the Law and the Scripture for the sake of the Lord; whence[^p501-7]: *The end of the Law is Christ.*

It must be said that *that* is not *causal* there, but *consecutive*[^p501-8]; so that the sense is not that the Lord did this for the sake of fulfilling the Scripture, but this followed upon the deed of the Lord, namely the fulfilment of the Scripture, which had foretold this.

57. Quaest. II. But there is the question why the Lord is said to have consummated the Scripture rather in the drink of vinegar than in other things.

And the response is that the Lord had suffered in all his members; that he might suffer wholly, nothing remained but the mouth and the tongue; therefore, because then all the members had generally suffered, then the *passion* is said to be in some manner consummated, and consequently the *Scripture*[^p501-9].

58. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked concerning this, that they had vinegar thus ready at once; how this came about. Did they know that he would ask for drink?

To this some say that they had brought wine for drinking, which on account of the heat had been turned into vinegar. — Others say that they had brought vinegar on purpose, that by drinking it he might perish more quickly[^p501-10].
<!-- page 502 -->
— Others say that it was wine mingled with myrrh, which was most bitter and sharp like vinegar, to torment him the more, already crucified. Whence they say that then also the Scripture was fulfilled[^p502-1]: *They gave me gall for my food, and in my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink.*

59. Quaest. IV. Likewise it is asked concerning this, that John says that Christ said: *It is consummated, and bowing his head, he sent forth his spirit.* But Luke, the twenty-third chapter[^p502-2], says that he cried: *Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit;* but Matthew and Mark, that he cried with a loud voice: *Eli, Eli* etc.

And it must be understood that he said all these things; but first he said: *I thirst;* then, while they were preparing the vinegar, he cried: *Eli;* and at this cry the one who was preparing hastened to bring the vinegar and put it about; which having been taken, he said: *It is consummated;* and then bowing his head, with a strong cry he expired, saying: *Into thy hands*[^p502-3] etc.

60. Quaest. V. Likewise it is asked: why was his side opened or wounded after death and not before?

And it must be understood that nothing was done concerning him except what God permitted for a fitting reason. But of this an *allegorical* and a *literal* reason is rendered: *allegorical,* because, as from the side of Adam sleeping Eve was formed, so from the side of Christ sleeping on the cross the Church was formed. Whence Augustine[^p502-4]: « For this the second Adam slept on the cross, that thence his spouse might be formed, from what flowed out of his side ». — But the *literal* reason is, because the Lord Christ, although he showed his humanity, nevertheless willed at the same time to show the truth of the Divinity. Whence he showed this before the passion in his own seizure, this in the passion in the darkness, this after death he showed in the flowing out of water and blood. Whence Ambrose[^p502-5]: « Although the nature of the body of Christ was mortal, yet the grace was unlike. For after death the blood in our bodies congeals, but from that incorrupt body flowed the life of all, water and blood came out; the one, to wash; the other, to redeem; we drink our price, that by drinking we may be redeemed ».
