# Commentarius in Ecclesiasten — Cap. VII, Vers. 8-10

## *Secundo, documenta quatuor ad habendam constantiam*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 56 -->

#### Secundo, documenta quatuor ad habendam constantiam.

*Calumnia conturbat sapientem.* Supra posuit documenta ad habendam modestiam; hic sequitur secunda pars, in qua ponit documenta ad *habendam constantiam;* et quoniam constantia perditur per calumniam et per iracundiam, et conservatur per patientiam et per perseverantiam: ideo hic quatuor documenta ponuntur. In primo ostenditur *vitanda calumnia;* in secundo, *amanda perseverantia;* in tertio, *servanda patientia;* in quarto, *fugienda iracundia.*

(Vers. 8.). In primo igitur documento ostenditur *vitanda calumnia,* quia perturbando hominem
<!-- page 57 -->
inconstantem reddit. Propterea dicit: *Calumnia perturbat sapientem.* Calumnia est criminatio litigiosa, et ideo ab ea recedendum; Isaiae quinquagesimo quarto[^p57-1]: « Recede procul a calumnia, quia non timebis ». *Et perdet robur cordis illius,* id est fortitudinem caritatis; Matthaei vigesimo quarto: « Quoniam abundabit iniquitas, refrigescet caritas multorum ».

(Vers. 9.). *Melior est finis* etc. Hic notatur secundum documentum, in quo *praefertur perseverantia* iustitiae inchoatae. Propter hoc dicit: *Melior est finis orationis quam principium;* melius est in bono perseverare quam tantum inchoare; Matthaei decimo[^p57-2]: « Qui perseveraverit usque in finem, hic salvus erit »; unde Bernardus: « Perseverantia est omnium virtutum consummatio ».

*Melior est patiens* etc. Hic notatur tertium documentum, in quo *commendatur patientia,* quia sine illa non potest esse constantia; ideo dicit: *Melior est patiens arrogante;* abusiva est comparatio, quia patiens est bonus, sed arrogans malus; Proverbiorum decimo sexto[^p57-3]: « Melior est patiens viro forti » etc.

(Vers. 10.). *Ne sis velox* etc. Tangitur hic quartum documentum, in quo docetur *fugienda iracundia,* quia non bene stat cum constantia; ideo dicit: *Ne sis velox ad irascendum;* Iacobi primo[^p57-4]: « Sit autem omnis homo velox ad audiendum, tardus ad loquendum et tardus ad iram ». Et subiungitur ratio: *Quia ira in sinu stulti requiescit;* Iob quinto: « Virum stultum interficit iracundia, et parvulum occidit invidia ». Bene dicit *requiescit,* propter diuturnitatem; nam sapiens, etsi irascitur, non debet diu servare; ad Ephesios quarto: « Sol non occidat super iracundiam vestram »; sed non sic stultus; Proverbiorum vigesimo septimo: « Onerosa arena » *in sinu stulti.*

### Quaestiones.

I. Sed dubitatio est de hoc quod dicit: *Calumnia conturbat sapientem.*

**Contra:** *Non contristabit iustum, quidquid ei acciderit,* habetur Proverbiorum duodecimo[^p57-5]: ergo si sapiens iustus est, non conturbatur per calumniam.

Ad hoc respondetur uno modo, quod est *conturbatio impatientiae,* et hac non conturbatur iustus; et *compassionis,* et hac conturbatur, sicut dicebat Apostolus secundae ad Corinthios undecimo[^p57-6]: *Quis scandalizatur, et ego non uror?* quasi dicat: nullus. — Sed littera sequens tollit hanc expositionem et subdit: *Et perdet robur cordis eius.*

Ideo solvendum est, sicut dicitur in Glossa[^p57-7], quod est sapiens et iustus *perfectus,* et de hoc intelligitur illud Proverbiorum; et *imperfectus,* et de hoc intelligitur hic.

II. Item, quid est quod dicit hic: *Ne sis velox ad irascendum?*

**Contra:** In Psalmo[^p57-8]: *Irascimini, et nolite peccare.*

**Respondeo:** dicendum, quod est ira multiplex[^p57-9].

## English
<!-- page 56 -->

#### Secondly, four teachings for having constancy.

*Calumny troubleth the wise man.* Above he set down teachings for having modesty; here follows the second part, in which he sets down teachings for *having constancy;* and since constancy is lost through calumny and through wrath, and is preserved through patience and through perseverance: therefore four teachings are set down here. In the first it is shown that *calumny is to be avoided;* in the second, that *perseverance is to be loved;* in the third, that *patience is to be kept;* in the fourth, that *wrath is to be fled.*

(Vers. 8.). In the first teaching, then, it is shown that *calumny is to be avoided,* because by perturbing a man it renders him
<!-- page 57 -->
inconstant. Therefore he says: *Calumny perturbeth the wise man.* Calumny is litigious accusation, and therefore one must withdraw from it; Isaias, the fifty-fourth chapter[^p57-1]: « Depart far from calumny, for thou shalt not fear ». *And shall destroy the strength of his heart,* that is, the fortitude of charity; Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter: « Because iniquity shall abound, the charity of many shall grow cold ».

(Vers. 9.). *Better is the end* etc. Here is noted the second teaching, in which *perseverance is preferred* to justice begun. On account of this he says: *Better is the end of a speech than the beginning;* it is better to persevere in good than only to begin; Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p57-2]: « He that shall persevere unto the end, he shall be saved »; whence Bernard: « Perseverance is the consummation of all the virtues ».

*Better is the patient man* etc. Here is noted the third teaching, in which *patience is commended,* because without it there cannot be constancy; therefore he says: *Better is the patient man than the arrogant;* the comparison is abusive, because the patient man is good, but the arrogant man bad; Proverbs, the sixteenth chapter[^p57-3]: « The patient man is better than the valiant » etc.

(Vers. 10.). *Be not swift* etc. Here is touched the fourth teaching, in which it is taught that *wrath is to be fled,* because it does not stand well with constancy; therefore he says: *Be not swift to be angry;* James, the first chapter[^p57-4]: « But let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak and slow to anger ». And the reason is subjoined: *For anger resteth in the bosom of a fool;* Job, the fifth chapter: « Wrath killeth the foolish man, and envy slayeth the little one ». He says well *resteth,* on account of its long duration; for the wise man, even if he is angry, ought not to keep it long; to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter: « Let not the sun go down upon your wrath »; but not so the fool; Proverbs, the twenty-seventh chapter: « Weighty sand » *in the bosom of a fool.*

### Questions.

I. But there is a doubt concerning what he says: *Calumny troubleth the wise man.*

**On the contrary:** *It shall not make the just man sad, whatsoever shall befall him,* is had in Proverbs, the twelfth chapter[^p57-5]: therefore if the wise man is just, he is not troubled by calumny.

To this it is answered in one way, that there is a *troubling of impatience,* and by this the just man is not troubled; and *of compassion,* and by this he is troubled, as the Apostle said in the second epistle to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter[^p57-6]: *Who is scandalized, and I am not on fire?* as if to say: no one. — But the following text removes this exposition and adds: *And shall destroy the strength of his heart.*

Therefore it is to be solved, as is said in the Gloss[^p57-7], that there is a wise and just man who is *perfect,* and of him that text of Proverbs is understood; and one who is *imperfect,* and of him it is understood here.

II. Likewise, what is it that he says here: *Be not swift to be angry?*

**On the contrary:** In the Psalm[^p57-8]: *Be ye angry, and sin not.*

**I respond:** It must be said that anger is manifold[^p57-9].
