# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. IX, Vers. 1-11
## *In parte prima insinuatur mirabilis caeci illuminatio*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 372 -->

### Capitulum IX.

#### Secundo, Filius Dei se ostendit lucem et directorem miraculo potentiae.

1. *Et praeteriens Iesus vidit hominem caecum a nativitate.* Manifestavit Dominus in duobus praecedentibus capitulis se directorem verbo sapientiae, hic ostendit istud idem *miraculo potentiae.* Illuminando enim corporaliter significat, se illuminare spiritualiter, et hoc quidem facit in praesenti capitulo. Dividitur autem praesens capitulum in tres partes. Primo enim insinuatur *mirabilis caeci illuminatio;* secundo, *Pharisaeorum disceptatio* sive *calumniatio,* ibi[^p372-6]: *Et dixerunt ei: Ubi est ille* etc.; tertio, occasione illius disceptationis *spiritualis caeci illuminatio,* ibi: *Audivit autem Iesus* etc.

#### In parte prima insinuatur mirabilis caeci illuminatio.

In prima igitur parte duo describuntur: primo *mirabilis caeci illuminatio;* secundo vero, *ipsius publicatio,* ibi[^p372-7]: *Itaque vicini* etc.

*Illuminationis* igitur *miraculum* describit hoc ordine. Primo notatur divinae *bonitatis miseratio,* secundo, divinae *sapientiae dispensatio;* tertio, divinae *potentiae mirabilis operatio.*

(Vers. 1.). Divinae *benignitatis miseratio* tangitur in hoc, quod Dominus oculo misericordiae respexit caecum; unde dicit: *Praeteriens Iesus vidit hominem caecum a nativitate; vidit,* inquam, oculo misericordiae, secundum illud Threnorum primo[^p372-8]: « Vide, Domine, quoniam tribulor ». Vel: *vidit,* « studiose rospexit, ut discipulis sensum inspiciendi tribueret », ut dicit Chrysostomus[^p372-9]; et propter hoc interrogaverunt discipuli.

2. (Vers. 2.). *Et interrogaverunt eum discipuli eius.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet divinae *sapientiae dispensatio,* quam volentes discipuli scire, interrogaverunt eum: *Rabbi, quis peccavit, hic, aut parentes eius, ut caecus nasceretur?* Supponentes, quod divina sapientia non infligit poenam sine culpa, quaerunt, in quo fuerit culpa. Hoc quidem didicerant ex curatione paralytici, cui dictum fuit supra quinto[^p372-10]: « Ecce, sanus factus es, iam amplius noli peccare, ne deterius tibi aliquid contingat ». Sciebant, scriptum esse Exodi vigesimo: « Ego sum Deus fortis, zelotes, vindicans iniquitatem patrum in filios in tertiam et quartam generationem ». Et quia bene intendebant, sed male quaerebant; Dominus in responsione sua destruit quaestionem, cum dicit:

3. (Vers. 3.). *Respondit Iesus: Neque hic peccavit neque parentes eius*[^p372-11]; et tamen satisfacit quaerentibus: *Sed ut manifestentur opera Dei in illo;* ideo scilicet caecus natus est. Opera Dei manifestantur, quando Deus in suis operibus manifestatur. Haec sunt opera miraculosa; supra secundo[^p372-12]: « Manifestavit gloriam suam »; Ecclesiastae tertio: « Didici,
<!-- page 373 -->
quod opera, quae fecit Deus, perseverant in aeternum » etc. Ideo ad manifestationem caecus natus est, quia se offert opportunitas manifestandi; unde dicit:

4. (Vers. 4.). *Me oportet operari opera eius qui misit me.* Haec sunt opera miraculosa, quae Patri attribuit, quia sibi appropriatur potentia; infra decimo quarto[^p373-1]: « Pater in me manens, ipse facit opera »; supra quinto: « Opera, quae dedit mihi Pater, ut perficiam ea, ipsa opera, quae ego facio, testimonium perhibent de me, quia Pater misit me ». Opportunitas faciendi haec opera durat, *donec dies est; veniet*[^p373-2] *nox, quando nemo potest operari;* tunc enim cessat opportunitas; Apocalypsis decimo quarto: « Amodo iam dicit Spiritus, ut requiescant a laboribus suis; opera enim illorum sequuntur illos »; et Ecclesiastae nono: « Quodcumque potest manus tua, instanter operare, quia nec opus nec ratio nec sapientia nec scientia erunt apud inferos, quo tu properas ». Quis sit autem iste dies, hic explicat; unde dicit:

5. (Vers. 5.). *Quamdiu sum in mundo, lux sum mundi.* Sicut igitur dies non est aliud quam praesentia lucis in terra, sic dies iste non est aliud quam praesentia Christi in mundo; Malachiae ultimo[^p373-3]: « Orietur vobis timentibus Deum sol iustitiae ». Ideo Dominus dicit infra duodecimo: « Ambulate, dum lucem habetis, ut tenebrae vos non comprehendant ».

6. (Vers. 6.). *Haec cum dixisset.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet divinae *virtutis mirabilis operatio* in hoc, quod sputo et luto opaco lumen reddit solo imperio. Ideo dicit: *Haec cum dixisset,* scilicet quae praedicta sunt; *exspuit in terram et fecit lutum ex sputo et linivit lutum super oculos eius;* fecit quasi collyrium ad sanandum oculos; Apocalypsis tertio[^p373-4]: « Collyrio inunge oculos tuos, ut videas ».

7. (Vers. 7.). *Et dixit ei: Vade et*[^p373-5] *lava in natatoria Siloe.* « Siloe est fons ad radicem montis Sion, qui non iugibus aquis, sed in certis horis ebullit per concava terrarum ». Et quia hoc non sine mysterio praeceptum est, subdit Evangelista nominis interpretationem dicens: *Quod interpretatur Missus.* Chrysostomus[^p373-6]: « Ut discas, quoniam illic virtus mittentis operatur ». Et virtus exprimitur in effectu; unde subdit: *Et abiit*[^p373-7] *et lavit et venit videns;* in quo notatur effectus virtutis et obedientia caeci. Unde Chrysostomus: « Vide caeci mentem ad omnia obedientem; non enim in aliquo angustiatur quaerens, vel scandalizatur dicens: Quid opus est luto, cuius gratia inunxisti, cuius gratia lavari praecepisti? Sed nihil horum reputavit, sed ad omnia suasus obedivit ».

8. Secundum *allegoriam: saliva* sive sputum, quod ex ore procedit, quod etiam salsum est, significat Dei sapientiam, de qua Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto[^p373-8]: *Ego ex ore Altissimi prodii primogenita* etc. — *Terra,* caro Virginis; Psalmus[^p373-9]: *Terra nostra dabit fructum suum.* — *Saliva terrae coniungitur,* quando Dei Filius ex Virgine nascitur, et fit *lutum,* cum *Verbum caro factum est,* et hoc ad nostram reformationem, secundum Augustinum[^p373-10]. « Sicut Deus hominem de limo terrae formavit, ita per idem genus luti humanum genus reparavit ». — Hoc luto *inunguntur oculi nostri,* dum in Christum credentes illuminantur unctione sancti Spiritus, de qua primae Ioannis secundo[^p373-11]: *Unctio docebit vos de omnibus.* — Et *lavamur in aquis Siloe,* id est sacrae Scripturae, quae *cum silentio fluunt,* quia non strepitu verborum mundanae philosophiae fucantur vel colorantur, et in humilibus requiescunt et de montibus descendunt; Isaiae octavo[^p373-12]: *Aquas Siloe, quae fluunt cum silentio.*
<!-- page 374 -->

9. Secundum *moralitatem: spuere* est peccati foeditatem extra nuntiare, ne forte putrescat; Psalmus[^p374-1]: *Putruerunt et corruptae sunt cicatrices meae a facie insipientiae meae;* et Proverbiorum vigesimo octavo: *Qui abscondit scelera sua non dirigetur; qui autem confessus fuerit et reliquerit ea misericordiam consequetur.* — *Luto oculos inungere* est peccata frequenter considerare; Ieremiae trigesimo primo[^p374-2]: *Statue tibi speculam, pone tibi amaritudines;* Isaiae trigesimo octavo: *Recogitabo tibi omnes annos meos in amaritudine animae meae.* — *Lavari,* per lacrymas; Psalmus[^p374-3]: *Lavabo per singulas noctes lectum meum, lacrymis meis stratum meum rigabo;* et Isaiae primo: *Lavamini, mundi estote, auferte malum cogitationum vestrarum ab oculis meis, quiescite agere perverse.*

10. *Itaque vicini* etc. Posita est et descripta mirabilis illuminatio, describitur hic *ipsius manifestatio,* et hoc ordine: primo inducitur *dubitatio;* secundo, *dubitantium certificatio;* tertio, *mirabilis illuminationis expressio.*

(Vers. 8.). Inducitur ergo primo *dubitatio vicinorum,* qui ex admiratione immutationis visus non[^p374-4] cognoscere se dubitant illuminatum; propterea dicit: *Itaque vicini et qui viderant eum prius, quia mendicus erat,* sicut caeci mendicare solent. Tales personas miseras et contemptibiles et pauperes maxime Dominus benignus et humilis curabat; unde de consimili Lucae decimo octavo[^p374-5]: « Caecus quidam sedebat secus viam mendicans »; « *stans* autem Iesus » dicitur ibi quod illuminavit illum. Illi igitur, qui solebant eum videre, *dicebant: Nonne hic est, qui sedebat et mendicabat?* Dubitando hoc dicebant et in hac dubitatione dissentiebant; unde dicit: *Alii dicebant: Quia hic est,* considerantes corporis figuram.

11. (Vers. 9.). *Alii autem dicebant: Nequaquam, sed similis est ei;* considerantes visus immutationem, credunt alium, sed similem.

*Ille autem*[^p374-6] *dicebat.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *dubitationis certificatio,* quae fit per caecum, qui se ipsum certitudinaliter cognoscebat; unde dicit: *Ille autem dicebat: Quia ego sum,* scilicet ille qui caecus mendicabat. Augustinus[^p374-7]: « Vox grata, ne damnaretur ingratus ». Non erubescebat caecus confiteri, se fuisse caecum, ad gloriam Dei; unde Ecclesiastici quarto[^p374-8]: « Est confusio adducens mortem », quae scilicet minuit gloriam Dei propter nostram; « et est confusio adducens gloriam et gratiam », et quae minuit nostram gloriam et manifestat ignominiam ad divinam gloriam manifestandam.

12. (Vers. 10.). *Dicebant ergo ei.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *mirabilis illuminationis expressio,* et hoc ad inquisitionem dubitantium; unde dicit: *Dicebant ergo ei: Quomodo aperti sunt oculi tui*[^p374-9]*?* ex quo tu es ille qui caecus erat. Et insinuat eis *auctorem* et *modum.*

13. (Vers. 11.). *Respondit: Ille homo, qui dicitur Iesus,* quia nondum credebat Deum, *lutum fecit et linivit oculos meos;* ecce, operatio; *et dixit mihi: Vade ad natatoria Siloe et lava;* ecce, eius praeceptio; *et abii et lavi et vidi*[^p374-10]; ecce, praecepti adimpletio et per illam illuminatio. Implendo enim mandatum Christi illuminatur homo, quia, sicut dicitur Proverbiorum sexto[^p374-11], « mandatum lucerna est, et lex lux », quae observata illuminat; Psalmus: « Praeceptum Domini lucidum, illuminans oculos[^p374-12] ».

### Quaestiones.

14. Quaest. I. Sed incidit hic quaestio primo de hoc quod dicunt discipuli: *Quis peccavit, hic, aut parentes eius?* — Ex hoc videntur[^p374-13] implicare, quod filii puniantur propter peccata parentum.

**Contra** hoc est illud Ezechielis decimo octavo[^p374-14]: *Non portabit pater iniquitatem filii, nec filius iniquitatem patris:* ergo non debet filius propter peccatum
<!-- page 375 -->
parentum puniri. — Contrarium huius dicitur Exodi vigesimo[^p375-1]: *Vindicans peccata patrum in filios in tertiam et quartam generationem.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod duplex est poena peccati: quaedam *temporalis,* quaedam *aeterna.* Dico, quod poena *aeterna* non *punitur* filius propter iniquitatem patris; unde etsi parvuli non baptizati aeterna visione priventur, hoc non est propter peccatum patris tantum, sed[^p375-2] suum, quia quilibet habet originale. — De poena vero *temporali* intelligendum, quod filius portat iniquitatem patris; quia frequenter malus pater punitur in filio; ideo dicit: *Usque in tertiam et quartam generationem,* quia usque ad illam aetatem possunt parentes videre filios.

Aliter dicunt aliqui, quod hoc intelligitur de filiis imitantibus scelus paternum, sed primum melius est[^p375-3].

15. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Neque hic peccavit* etc.

**Contra**[^p375-4]: *Nemo purus a sorde, nec infans, cuius est unius diei vita super terram.*

Respondet Augustinus[^p375-5], quod littera illa est supplenda sic: « Non peccavit peccato, propter quod infligeretur haec poena caeco ». Et indiscrete petebant discipuli, ut dicit Chrysostomus[^p375-6], quia, antequam nasceretur, peccare non potuit, ut caecus nasceretur.

Sed *obiicitur* tunc: 1. Quia, si omnis poena est propter culpam[^p375-7]; et iste non peccavit nec parentes eius: ergo iniuste caecus natus est, et poena iniuste inflicta.

2. Item Hieronymus super illud Ieremiae quarto[^p375-8]: *Viae tuae et cogitationes tuae fecerunt haec tibi;* dicit: « Quidquid supplicii nobis accidit, vitii nostri est ».

3. Item, *si dicas,* quod propter gloriam Dei; nunquid non aliter potuit manifestari gloria Dei quam sic? Et Deus propter suam gloriam non indiget *bonis* nostris[^p375-9], ergo multo minus *malis.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod est causa *sine qua non,* et hoc modo omnis poena habet culpam causam, quia, si non fuisset culpa, nulla prorsus esset poena. Alio modo est causa *meritoria,* et sic non omnis poena respicit culpam, et hoc modo loquitur hic Dominus. — Immo, sicut dicit Damascenus[^p375-10], aliquando poenae infliguntur *propter reatum culpae.* Quandoque *ad virtutis probationem et manifestationem,* sicut in Tobia et Iob, qui dicebat: *Non peccavi, et in amaritudinibus moratur oculus meus,* Iob decimo septimo[^p375-11]. Aliquando *ad virtutis conservationem,* sicut in Paulo, qui dicebat: *Datus est mihi stimulus carnis meae angelus satanae, qui me colaphizet; propter quod ter Dominum rogavi* etc., secundae ad Corinthios duodecimo[^p375-12]. Aliquando *propter divini consilii dispensationem,* sicut in Christo. Aliquando *ad divinae virtutis manifestationem,* sicut in hoc caeco. — Nec tantum propter Deum hoc factum est, sed propter nos instruendos et propter utilitatem caeci, quia occasione corporalis illuminationis est spiritualiter illuminatus; unde magna eius fuit utilitas[^p375-13].

16. Quaest. III. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *oportet eum operari, donec dies est.* — Sed Deo semper est *dies:* ergo nunquam cessat operari.

Dicendum, quod loquitur hic de operibus, quae operatur *propter nos* et *in nobis,* secundum illud Isaiae vigesimo sexto[^p375-14]: *Omnia opera nostra operatus es in nobis, Domine.* Et quantum ad haec opera venit *nox,* quando clauditur nobis dies extremus, post quem nec nos operari poterimus, nec Deus *in nobis* nec *propter nos in malis;* unde Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p375-15]: *Ligatis manibus et pedibus eius, mittite eum in tenebras exteriores.*

17. Quaest. IV. Quaeritur hic de modo curationis: 1. Cum Dominus alios curaverit solo imperio, unde est quod hoc facit luto?

2. Et quare praecipit lavari?
<!-- page 376 -->

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod ratio fuit *allegorica* et *moralis,* ut supra[^p376-1] dictum est, ad instructionem nostrae cognitionis et operationis; fuit etiam ratio *litteralis.* Quia enim iusto iudicio in adventu suo et caeci erant illuminandi, et Pharisaei excaecandi; lutum facit, in quo Sabbatum solvere videretur, et per hoc *sordidi magis sordescerent*[^p376-2], et ex hoc ipse caecus illuminaretur et purificaretur.

2. Ad natatoria misit, ut miraculum esset publicum, sicut dicit Chrysostomus[^p376-3]; quia omnes concurrerunt ad tantum spectaculum, quod luto, unde alii excaecantur, caecus illuminatur[^p376-4].

## English
<!-- page 372 -->

### Chapter IX.

#### Second, the Son of God shows himself light and director by a miracle of power.

1. *And Jesus passing by, saw a man who was blind from his birth.* In the two preceding chapters the Lord manifested himself as director by a word of wisdom; here he shows this same thing *by a miracle of power.* For by illuminating bodily he signifies that he illuminates spiritually, and this indeed he does in the present chapter. Now the present chapter is divided into three parts. For first is intimated *the wonderful illumination of the blind man;* second, *the disputing* or *calumniating of the Pharisees,* there[^p372-6]: *And they said to him: Where is he* etc.; third, on the occasion of that disputing, *the spiritual illumination of the blind man,* there: *Now Jesus heard* etc.

#### In the first part is intimated the wonderful illumination of the blind man.

In the first part, then, two things are described: first, *the wonderful illumination of the blind man;* second, *its publication,* there[^p372-7]: *The neighbours therefore* etc.

He describes the *miracle,* then, *of the illumination* in this order. First is noted the *compassion* of the divine *goodness;* second, the *dispensation* of the divine *wisdom;* third, the *wonderful operation* of the divine *power.*

(V. 1.). *The compassion of the divine benignity* is touched on in this, that the Lord looked upon the blind man with the eye of mercy; whence he says: *Jesus passing by, saw a man who was blind from his birth; he saw,* I say, with the eye of mercy, according to that of Lamentations, the first chapter[^p372-8]: « Behold, O Lord, for I am in distress ». Or: *he saw,* « he studiously *rospexit* [so printed: *rospexit* for *respexit*] ("looked upon") him, to give the disciples a sense for looking into it », as Chrysostom says[^p372-9]; and because of this the disciples asked.

2. (V. 2.). *And his disciples asked him.* Here the second is touched on, namely the *dispensation* of the divine *wisdom,* which the disciples, wishing to know, asked him about: *Rabbi, who hath sinned, this man, or his parents, that he should be born blind?* Supposing that the divine wisdom does not inflict a penalty without fault, they ask in whom the fault was. This indeed they had learned from the curing of the paralytic, to whom it was said above, in the fifth chapter[^p372-10]: « Behold, thou art made whole: now sin no more, lest some worse thing happen to thee ». They knew that it is written in Exodus, the twentieth chapter: « I am God, mighty, jealous, avenging the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation ». And because they intended well, but asked badly, the Lord in his response destroys the question, when he says:

3. (V. 3.). *Jesus answered: Neither hath this man sinned, nor his parents*[^p372-11]; and yet he satisfies those asking: *But that the works of God should be made manifest in him;* that is, for this he was born blind. The works of God are made manifest when God is manifested in his works. These are the miraculous works; above, in the second chapter[^p372-12]: « He manifested his glory »; Ecclesiastes, the third chapter: « I have learned
<!-- page 373 -->
that all the works which God hath made continue for ever » etc. Therefore he was born blind for the manifestation, because the opportunity of manifesting offers itself; whence he says:

4. (V. 4.). *I must work the works of him that sent me.* These are the miraculous works, which he attributes to the Father, because power is appropriated to him; below, in the fourteenth chapter[^p373-1]: « The Father who abideth in me, he doth the works »; above, in the fifth chapter: « The works which the Father hath given me to perfect, the works themselves which I do give testimony of me, that the Father hath sent me ». The opportunity of doing these works lasts *whilst it is day; the night will come*[^p373-2]*, when no man can work;* for then the opportunity ceases; the Apocalypse, the fourteenth chapter: « From henceforth now, saith the Spirit, that they may rest from their labours; for their works follow them »; and Ecclesiastes, the ninth chapter: « Whatsoever thy hand is able to do, do it earnestly: for neither work, nor reason, nor wisdom, nor knowledge shall be in hell, whither thou art hastening ». And what this day is he explains here; whence he says:

5. (V. 5.). *As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world.* As, then, day is nothing other than the presence of light on the earth, so this day is nothing other than the presence of Christ in the world; Malachias, the last chapter[^p373-3]: « Unto you that fear God the sun of justice shall arise ». Therefore the Lord says below, in the twelfth chapter: « Walk whilst you have the light, that the darkness overtake you not ».

6. (V. 6.). *When he had said these things.* Here the third is touched on, namely the *wonderful operation* of the divine *power,* in this, that with spittle and dark clay he gives back lumen[^tr-lumen] by his command alone. Therefore he says: *When he had said these things,* namely the things said before; *he spat on the ground, and made clay of the spittle, and spread the clay upon his eyes;* he made as it were an eyesalve for healing the eyes; the Apocalypse, the third chapter[^p373-4]: « With eyesalve anoint thy eyes, that thou mayest see ».

7. (V. 7.). *And said to him: Go and*[^p373-5] *wash in the pool of Siloe.* « Siloe is a spring at the root of Mount Sion, which bubbles up not with continual waters, but at fixed hours, through the hollows of the earth ». And because this was not commanded without a mystery, the Evangelist subjoins the interpretation of the name, saying: *Which is interpreted, Sent.* Chrysostom[^p373-6]: « That thou mayest learn that there the power of the sender works ». And the power is expressed in the effect; whence he subjoins: *And he went*[^p373-7] *and washed and came seeing;* in which is noted the effect of the power and the obedience of the blind man. Whence Chrysostom: « See the mind of the blind man obedient in all things; for in nothing is he troubled, asking, or scandalized, saying: What need is there of clay, for whose sake didst thou anoint me, for whose sake didst thou command me to wash? But he reckoned none of these things, but, persuaded, obeyed in all ».

8. According to *allegory: the saliva* or spittle, which proceeds from the mouth, which is also salt, signifies the wisdom of God, of which Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p373-8]: *I came out of the mouth of the Most High, the firstborn* etc. — *The earth,* the flesh of the Virgin; the Psalm[^p373-9]: *Our earth shall yield its fruit.* — *The saliva is joined to the earth* when the Son of God is born of the Virgin, and it becomes *clay* when *the Word was made flesh,* and this for our reformation, according to Augustine[^p373-10]. « As God formed man from the slime of the earth, so by the same kind of clay he repaired the human race ». — With this clay *our eyes are anointed* when, believing in Christ, we are illuminated by the unction of the Holy Spirit, of which the first of John, the second chapter[^p373-11]: *The unction shall teach you of all things.* — And *we are washed in the waters of Siloe,* that is, of sacred Scripture, which *flow with silence,* because they are not painted or coloured with the din of the words of worldly philosophy, and they rest in the lowly and come down from the mountains; Isaias, the eighth chapter[^p373-12]: *The waters of Siloe, that flow with silence.*
<!-- page 374 -->

9. According to *the moral sense: to spit* is to declare outwardly the foulness of sin, lest perchance it putrefy; the Psalm[^p374-1]: *My sores are putrified and corrupted because of my foolishness;* and Proverbs, the twenty-eighth chapter: *He that hideth his crimes shall not prosper; but he that shall confess and forsake them shall obtain mercy.* — *To anoint the eyes with clay* is to consider one's sins often; Jeremias, the thirty-first chapter[^p374-2]: *Set thee up a watchtower, make to thee bitternesses;* Isaias, the thirty-eighth chapter: *I will recount to thee all my years in the bitterness of my soul.* — *To be washed,* through tears; the Psalm[^p374-3]: *Every night I will wash my bed; I will water my couch with my tears;* and Isaias, the first chapter: *Wash yourselves, be clean, take away the evil of your devices from my eyes, cease to do perversely.*

10. *The neighbours therefore* etc. The wonderful illumination having been set down and described, here is described *its manifestation,* and in this order: first is brought in *the doubting;* second, *the certifying of those doubting;* third, *the expression of the wonderful illumination.*

(V. 8.). First, then, is brought in *the doubting of the neighbours,* who, out of wonder at the change of his sight, doubt that they do not[^p374-4] recognize the man illuminated; therefore he says: *The neighbours therefore, and they who had seen him before, that he was a beggar,* as the blind are wont to beg. Such persons, wretched and contemptible and poor, the Lord, kind and humble, cured most of all; whence of a like case Luke, the eighteenth chapter[^p374-5]: « A certain blind man sat by the wayside, begging »; « Jesus *standing* », it is said there, illuminated him. Those, then, who were wont to see him *said: Is not this he that sat and begged?* Doubting they said this, and in this doubting they disagreed; whence he says: *Some said: This is he,* considering the figure of his body.

11. (V. 9.). *But others said: No, but he is like him;* considering the change of his sight, they believe him another, but like.

*But he*[^p374-6] *said.* Here the second is touched on, namely *the certifying of the doubting,* which is made through the blind man, who knew himself with certitude; whence he says: *But he said: I am he,* namely he who begged blind. Augustine[^p374-7]: « A grateful voice, lest he be damned as ungrateful ». The blind man did not blush to confess that he had been blind, to the glory of God; whence Ecclesiasticus, the fourth chapter[^p374-8]: « There is a shame that bringeth death », which namely lessens the glory of God for the sake of our own; « and there is a shame that bringeth glory and grace », and which lessens our glory and manifests our ignominy for the manifesting of the divine glory.

12. (V. 10.). *They said therefore to him.* Here the third is touched on, namely *the expression of the wonderful illumination,* and this at the inquiry of those doubting; whence he says: *They said therefore to him: How were thy eyes opened*[^p374-9]*?* since thou art he who was blind. And he intimates to them *the author* and *the manner.*

13. (V. 11.). *He answered: That man that is called Jesus,* because he did not yet believe him God, *made clay, and spread it on my eyes;* behold, the operation; *and said to me: Go to the pool of Siloe, and wash;* behold, his precept; *and I went, and washed, and saw*[^p374-10]; behold, the fulfilment of the precept and through it the illumination. For by fulfilling the commandment of Christ a man is illuminated, because, as is said in Proverbs, the sixth chapter[^p374-11], « the commandment is a lamp, and the law a light », which, observed, illuminates; the Psalm: « The precept of the Lord is lightsome, illuminating the eyes[^p374-12] ».

### Questions.

14. Quaest. I. But here a question first arises about what the disciples say: *Who hath sinned, this man, or his parents?* — From this they seem[^p374-13] to imply that sons are punished for the sins of their parents.

**On the contrary:** against this is that of Ezechiel, the eighteenth chapter[^p374-14]: *The father shall not bear the iniquity of the son, nor the son the iniquity of the father:* therefore the son ought not to be punished for the sin
<!-- page 375 -->
of the parents. — The contrary of this is said in Exodus, the twentieth chapter[^p375-1]: *Avenging the sins of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation.*

**I respond:** It must be said that the penalty of sin is twofold: one *temporal,* one *eternal.* I say that with the *eternal* penalty the son is not *punished* for the iniquity of the father; whence, although little ones not baptized are deprived of the eternal vision, this is not on account of the sin of the father alone, but[^p375-2] their own, because each one has original sin. — But of the *temporal* penalty it must be understood that the son bears the iniquity of the father; because often a wicked father is punished in his son; therefore he says: *Unto the third and fourth generation,* because up to that age parents can see their children.

Some say otherwise, that this is understood of sons imitating the father's crime, but the first is better[^p375-3].

15. Quaest. II. Again it is asked about what he says: *Neither hath this man sinned* etc.

**On the contrary:**[^p375-4] *No one is clean from filth, not even an infant whose life upon the earth is of one day.*

Augustine responds[^p375-5] that that letter is to be supplied thus: « He did not sin with a sin on account of which this penalty should be inflicted on the blind man ». And the disciples asked indiscreetly, as Chrysostom says[^p375-6], because before he was born he could not sin so as to be born blind.

But then it *is objected:* 1. Because, if every penalty is on account of fault[^p375-7]; and this man did not sin, nor his parents: therefore he was born blind unjustly, and the penalty was inflicted unjustly.

2. Again, Jerome, on that of Jeremias, the fourth chapter[^p375-8]: *Thy ways and thy devices have done these things to thee;* says: « Whatever of penalty befalls us is of our own vice ».

3. Again, *if thou say* that it was for the glory of God; could not the glory of God have been manifested otherwise than thus? And God for his glory has no need of our *goods*[^p375-9]; therefore much less of our *evils.*

**I respond:** It must be said that there is a cause *without which not,* and in this way every penalty has fault as its cause, because, if there had not been fault, there would be no penalty at all. In another way there is a *meritorious* cause, and so not every penalty regards fault, and in this way the Lord speaks here. — Nay, as Damascene says[^p375-10], sometimes penalties are inflicted *on account of the guilt of fault.* Sometimes *for the proving and manifesting of virtue,* as in Tobias and in Job, who said: *I have not sinned, and my eye dwelleth in bitternesses,* Job, the seventeenth chapter[^p375-11]. Sometimes *for the preserving of virtue,* as in Paul, who said: *There was given me a sting of my flesh, an angel of Satan, to buffet me; for which thing thrice I besought the Lord* etc., the second to the Corinthians, the twelfth chapter[^p375-12]. Sometimes *on account of the dispensation of the divine counsel,* as in Christ. Sometimes *for the manifesting of the divine power,* as in this blind man. — Nor was this done only on account of God, but on account of instructing us and on account of the benefit of the blind man, because on the occasion of the bodily illumination he was spiritually illuminated; whence great was his benefit[^p375-13].

16. Quaest. III. It is asked about what he says, that *he must work, whilst it is day.* — But to God it is always *day:* therefore he never ceases to work.

It must be said that he speaks here of the works which he works *for us* and *in us,* according to that of Isaias, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p375-14]: *Thou hast wrought all our works in us, O Lord.* And as regards these works *the night* comes when our last day is closed, after which neither shall we be able to work, nor God *in us,* nor *for us in the wicked;* whence Matthew, the twenty-second chapter[^p375-15]: *His hands and feet being bound, cast him into the exterior darkness.*

17. Quaest. IV. Here it is asked about the manner of the cure: 1. Since the Lord cured others by his command alone, whence is it that he does this with clay?

2. And why does he command him to wash?
<!-- page 376 -->

**I respond:** It must be said that the reason was *allegorical* and *moral,* as was said above[^p376-1], for the instruction of our knowing and working; there was also a *literal* reason. For since by a just judgement at his coming both the blind were to be illuminated and the Pharisees to be blinded, he makes clay, in which he would seem to break the Sabbath, and through this *the filthy would become filthier*[^p376-2], and by this the blind man himself would be illuminated and purified.

2. He sent him to the pool, that the miracle might be public, as Chrysostom says[^p376-3]; because all ran together to so great a spectacle, that by clay, by which others are blinded, the blind man is illuminated[^p376-4].

This pericope's lemma has *lux* (*lux sum mundi*, 9:5), and ¶ 5 reads the day as *praesentia lucis*; but what the clay and spittle give back to the blind man's eyes in ¶ 6 is *lumen* (*luto opaco lumen reddit*) — sight, light as received. Keeping "lumen" there keeps visible that the miracle restores the light received, where the verse names the light that gives it.
