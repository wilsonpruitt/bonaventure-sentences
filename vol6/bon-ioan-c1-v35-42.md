# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. I, Vers. 35-42
## *Altera die iterum stabat*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 261 -->

72. *Altera die iterum stabat* etc. Supra posuit duo testimonia, hic sequitur tertium, quod perhibuit, ipso *ante eum deambulante;* et describitur testimonium cum fructu suo. Primo ergo notatur ipsa *testificatio;* secundo, discipulorum *conversio;*
<!-- page 262 -->
tertio, conversorum *susceptio;* quarto, susceptorum *fructificatio;* quinto vero, fructificationis vel praedicationis *acceptatio.*

(Vers. 35.). Notatur ergo primo ipsa *testificatio,* quae facta est in praesentia Domini et discipulorum Ioannis; ideo dicit: *Altera die iterum stabat Ioannes, et ex discipulis eius duo,* qui scilicet erant idonei ad audiendum de Christo testimonium, quia *discipuli* humiles, quia *magistro adhaerentes,* quia etiam *se amantes;* unde *discipuli* erant, *cum magistro* erant, *duo* erant; sicut dicit Gregorius[^p262-1]: « Caritas minus quam inter duos esse non potest »; unde Lucae decimo dicitur: « Misit eos binos ante faciem suam ».

73. (Vers. 36.). *Et respiciens Iesum ambulantem dicit: Ecce, Agnus Dei;* ad differentiam agni typici et paschalis. Ideo *Agnum* vocat, ut discipulos ad ipsum per mansuetudinem attrahat; Matthaei undecimo[^p262-2]: « Discite a me, quia mitis sum et humilis corde.

Notandum est, quod Christus fuit *vir* in incarnatione, propter perfectionem sapientiae; Zachariae sexto[^p262-3]: « Ecce, vir oriens nomen eius ». *Agnus* in passione, propter mansuetudinem patientiae; Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p262-4]: « Tanquam ovis ad occisionem ductus est, et sicut agnus coram tondente se obmutescet et non aperiet os suum ». *Leo* in resurrectione, propter fortitudinem potentiae; Apocalypsis quinto[^p262-5]: « Ecce, vicit Leo de tribu Iuda »; et ibi dicitur, quod « occisus est Agnus ».

74. (Vers. 37.). *Et audierunt eum.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *discipulorum conversio* ad augmentum testimonii; ideo dicit: *Et audierunt eum duo discipuli loquentem,* id est testificantem, *et secuti sunt Iesum,* sano consilio magistri utentes. Isti secuti sunt lucem, quia bona habebant opera; unde infra tertio[^p262-6]: « Qui facit veritatem venit ad lucem, ut manifestentur opera eius ».

75. (Vers. 38.). *Conversus autem.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *discipulorum conversorum susceptio;* unde Dominus ad eos convertitur et alloquitur. Ideo dicit: *Conversus autem Iesus et videns eos sequentes se.* Conversus est ad conversos, secundum illud Zachariae primo[^p262-7]: « Convertimini ad me, et ego convertar ad vos ». Sic conversus, *dicit eis: Quid quaeritis?* Non hoc dicit dubitando, sed eorum rectam intentionem per responsionem manifestando.

76. *Qui dixerunt ei: Rabbi, quod dicitur interpretatum magister, ubi habitas?* Interpositio est ibi[^p262-8], et continuatur series litterae: *Rabbi, ubi habitas?* quasi dicant: te quaerimus, tuam doctrinam, tuam habitationem. Hanc quaestionem facit anima, quae directe quaerit Deum; Canticorum primo[^p262-9]: « Indica mihi quem diligit anima mea, ubi pascas, ubi cubes in meridie ». Et quia recta intentione sequuntur, ideo admittuntur.

77. (Vers. 39.). *Dicit eis: Venite et videte. Venerunt et viderunt, ubi maneret.* Econtra ille qui non recta intentione quaerebat, repulsus est; Matthaei octavo[^p262-10] dixit ei scriba: « Magister, sequar te, quocumque ieris. Dixit ei Iesus: Vulpes foveas habent, et volucres caeli nidos, Filius autem hominis non habet ubi caput reclinet ». Et quia frustra convertitur qui non permanet, ideo dicit: *Et manserunt ibi*[^p262-11] *die illo.* Et ratio redditur: *Erat autem hora quasi decima.* In quo significatur dies gratiae, qui est novissimus, in quo permanendum; primae Ioannis secundo[^p262-12]: « Nunc scimus, carissimi, quia novissima hora est ». In hac novissima hora permanendum est cum Domino Iesu; Lucae vigesimo secundo: « Vos estis, qui permansistis mecum ».

78. (Vers. 40.). *Erat autem unus ex discipulis* etc. Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *discipulorum fructificatio,* quia statim, postquam conversi sunt, alios vocant, ut Andreas Petrum. Propterea dicit: *Erat autem Andreas, frater Simonis Petri, unus ex duobus, qui audierant a Ioanne et secuti fuerant Iesum*[^p262-13]; ideo hunc specialiter nominat, ut fructum suae praedicationis ostendat. Ideo subdit fructum.

79. (Vers. 41.). *Invenit hic primum fratrem suum Simonem;* quia studiose eum quaerebat, ut ad inventum thesaurum eum perduceret. Ideo dicit: *Et dixit*[^p262-14] *ei: Invenimus Messiam, quod est interpretatum Christus.* Invenimus sicut boni mercatores *margaritam,* sicut boni cultores *thesaurum*
<!-- page 263 -->
*absconditum;* Matthaei decimo tertio: « Simile est regnum caelorum thesauro abscondito in agro » etc.

80. (Vers. 42.). *Et adduxit eum ad Iesum;* Apocalypsis ultimo[^p263-1]: « Qui audit dicat: Veni; et qui sitit veniat ». *Intuitus autem eum Iesus.* Tangitur hic quintum, scilicet *praedicationis discipulorum acceptatio,* quia Dominus acceptat et vocationem sive praedicationem Andreae, suscipiendo Petrum: propter quod nomen imponit tanquam suo discipulo. Ideo dicit: *Intuitus autem eum,* scilicet Petrum, *Iesus,* oculis scilicet benignitatis; de quo intuitu Genesis quadragesimo quarto[^p263-2]: « Adducite fratrem vestrum ad me, et ponam super eum oculos meos »; *dixit: Tu es Simon, filius Ioanna.* Nomen eius dicit, ut aliud imponat: *Tu vocaberis Cephas, quod interpretatur Petrus;* Beda[^p263-3]: « Sciendum, quod Syriaca lingua *Cephas,* Graeca et Latina *Petrus,* et in utraque lingua a petra derivatum est ». Ideo dicit ei Dominus Matthaei decimo sexto: « Tu es Petrus, et super hanc petram », a qua scilicet tu nomen accepisti, hoc est super me, « aedificabo Ecclesiam meam »; primae ad Corinthios decimo: « Petra autem erat Christus ». *Tu vocaberis Petrus,* id est, a firma petra Christo, quem ardenter amabis, nomen accipies[^p263-4].

### Quaestiones.

81. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de testimonio isto ultimo: *Ecce, Agnus Dei:*

1. Quia, cum nihil plus addiderit, videtur insufficiens fuisse testimonium, quia hoc potest dici de quolibet Sancto, maxime de Apostolis; Lucae decimo[^p263-5]: « Ecce, ego mitto vos sicut agnos inter lupos ».

2. Item, quare potius nomine *agni* quam alterius animalis ipsum describit?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *Agnus* dictus est a Ioanne, quia in agno fuit *praefiguratus,* Exodi duodecimo[^p263-6], et *praedictus* a Prophetis, Isaiae decimo sexto. Et quia Evangelium consonare debet Legi, ideo nomine *agni* demonstratus est a Ioanne.

82. Sed adhuc restat quaestio: quare praefiguratus fuit nomine *agni* sive in *agno?*

Responsio huius est: ratione *innocentiae;* unde Exodi duodecimo[^p263-7]: « Erit sine macula, masculus, anniculus ». Ratione *patientiae;* Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p263-8]: « Tanquam agnus coram tondente se obmutuit ». Ratione *utilitatis magnae,* quia *totus* utilis; quia ovis tribuit[^p263-9] lac in potum, carnem in cibum, lanam in vestimentum, pellem in ornatum.

Quod ergo obiicitur, quod alii sunt agni; dicendum, quod innocentia Christi differt ab aliis: quia ipse sine macula, quia eius macula *nulla* fuit; sed alii Sancti sunt sine macula, quia eorum maculae sunt *abstersae* per gratiam.

83. Quaest. II. Quaeritur de perhibitione huius testimonii. Dicitur enim, quod *Ioannes stabat* et coram *duobus discipulis* testimonium perhibuit.

**Contra:** 1. Ioannes erat missus ad praenuntiandum Christum: ergo videtur, quod non deberet *stare,* sed circumire, ut nomen Christi annuntiaret.

2. Item videtur invidus, quia tantum *duobus,* cum omnibus deberet dicere, *ut omnes crederent per illum*[^p263-10].

Respondendum est, quod testimonium Ioannis ad hoc, quod ei crederetur, debuit esse *authenticum,* *placitum* sive acceptum et *non suspectum:* quia *authenticum,* ideo stabat, non circuibat; quia *acceptum,* ideo non quibuscumque, sed quaerentibus et scire volentibus testimonium exhibebat; quia *non*
<!-- page 264 -->
*suspectum,* non se ad annuntiandum ingerebat; unde dicit Chrysostomus[^p264-1]: « Quod suspectum esset testimonium, si ita se ingereret ad praedicandum, et Iudaei fuissent provocati ad contradicendum ».

84. Quaest. III. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicunt: *Rabbi, ubi habitas?* Et Dominus eis ostendit hospitium. — *Contra:* Matthaei octavo[^p264-2]: *Vulpes foveas habent, et volucres caeli nidos; Filius autem hominis non habet ubi caput reclinet.* Ibi dicitur, quod non habebat hospitium.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *habere habitaculum* est duobus modis: vel per *proprietatem possessionis,* sicut habent divites homines; vel per *quietem inhabitationis,* sicut habent omnes, qui declinant ad aliquam domum ad manendum. Primo modo non habuit tanquam pauper; secundo modo habuit tanquam quietus; non enim erat gyrovagus[^p264-3].

85. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Erat unus ex discipulis illis Andreas.* — Quare Andream nominavit, non alium discipulum? Et si alterum tacuit, quare non Andream?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod ratio exprimendi nomen *Andreae* magis quam alterius fuit duplex, scilicet quia insignior erat, ut dicit Chrysostomus[^p264-4], et ut ostendat, Petrum per Andream esse vocatum. Et in hoc mira patet dispensatio: quia voluit maiores et seniores vocare per minores, et sapientes per simplices. Unde Petrum vocat per Andream, et Nathanaelem sapientem per Philippum rusticum[^p264-5].

86. Quaest. V. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicitur: *Tu vocaberis Cephas,* utrum hoc nomen ei tunc imponat? — Quod non, videtur:

1. Per interlinearem[^p264-6]: « Nondum imponit ei nomen, sed praesignat ».

2. Item Marci tertio[^p264-7] dicitur, quod Dominus vocavit duodecim; et tunc « imposuit Simoni nomen Petrus ».

3. Item Matthaei decimo sexto[^p264-8] dicitur, quod tunc imposuit, quando confessus fuit.

Respondetur communiter, quod hoc nomen *Petrus* hic fuit *praesignatum,* Marci tertio *impositum,* Matthaei decimo sexto *confirmatum.* — Sed Augustinus[^p264-9] huic solutioni contradicit dicens, quod hoc nomen fuit Petro impositum, quando ab Andrea ductus est ad Christum; et Beda similiter dicit super Marci tertio[^p264-10], quod non tunc impositum, sed ante.

Propter hoc dicendum, quod modo fuit ei *impositum,* sed tamen non fuit *vulgatum;* quia adhuc non ex tunc appellabatur ab aliis *Petrus,* sed alio nomine suo priori; sed post, Marci tertio, voluit, quod hoc nomine vocaretur; Matthaei vero decimo sexto haec nominatio celebris redditur per dignitatem nomini congruentem.

87. Quaest. VI. Item quaeritur: quare Dominus imposuit nomen Petro in sui vocatione magis quam aliis? — Videtur, quod acceptor fuerit personae, quia dignitatem nominis contulit sine merito[^p264-11].

Ad hoc respondet Pelagius, quod hoc fecit propter maiorem naturalium strenuitatem, quam vidit in Petro; unde dicitur: *Intuitus eum Iesus;* et Pelagius dicit, quod per naturalia contingit mereri[^p264-12].

Ad hoc respondendum, quod Deus videt non solum exteriora, sed etiam interiora; non solum quae sunt, sed etiam quae futura sunt: in signum ergo, quod erat futurus pastor Ecclesiae, nomen ei
<!-- page 265 -->
imponitur. — Quod vero dicit Pelagius: « Intuitus est naturalium strenuitatem »; Victor[^p265-1] reprobat; unde dicit: « Non propter fundamentum naturalium maius superaddit aedificium virtutis, sicut mentitus est Pelagius, hinc trahens fomentum erroris; sed intuitus est oculo misericordiae et benignitatis ».

88. Quaest. VII. Quaestio est etiam: quare modo nomina non mutantur his qui religionem ingrediuntur, vel etiam his qui convertuntur ad Christum?

Ad hoc respondet Chrysostomus[^p265-2], quod mutatio nominis fiebat in virtutum incitamentum. Quoniam igitur modo nomen unum habemus, quod maxime est ad virtutem incitativum; ideo illo nomine contenti esse debemus; et hoc nomen est christianitatis, quia omnes dicimur *Christiani.* — Tamen adhuc servat mutationem nominis Romana Ecclesia in summo Pontifice.

## English
<!-- page 261 -->

72. *The next day again he stood* etc. Above he set down two testimonies; here follows the third, which he bore while the other was *walking before him;* and the testimony is described with its fruit. First, then, is noted the *testifying* itself; second, the *conversion* of the disciples;
<!-- page 262 -->
third, the *reception* of the converted; fourth, the *fructification* of the received; fifth indeed, the *acceptance* of the fructification or preaching.

(Vers. 35.). First, then, is noted the *testifying* itself, which was done in the presence of the Lord and of the disciples of John; therefore he says: *The next day again John stood, and two of his disciples,* who namely were fit to hear the testimony about Christ, because they were humble *disciples,* because *cleaving to their teacher,* because also *loving one another;* hence they were *disciples,* they were *with their teacher,* they were *two;* as Gregory[^p262-1] says: « Charity cannot be between fewer than two »; whence in Luke, the tenth, it is said: « He sent them two and two before his face ».

73. (Vers. 36.). *And beholding Jesus walking, he saith: Behold the Lamb of God;* in distinction from the typical and paschal lamb. Therefore he calls him *Lamb,* that he may draw the disciples to him through gentleness; Matthew, the eleventh[^p262-2]: « Learn of me, because I am meek, and humble of heart.

It is to be noted that Christ was a *man* in the incarnation, on account of the perfection of wisdom; Zacharias, the sixth[^p262-3]: « Behold a man, the Orient is his name ». A *Lamb* in the passion, on account of the gentleness of patience; Isaiah, the fifty-third[^p262-4]: « As a sheep he was led to the slaughter, and as a lamb before his shearer he shall be dumb, and shall not open his mouth ». A *Lion* in the resurrection, on account of the fortitude of power; Apocalypse, the fifth[^p262-5]: « Behold, the Lion of the tribe of Juda hath prevailed »; and there it is said that « the Lamb was slain ».

74. (Vers. 37.). *And they heard him.* Here is touched the second, namely the *conversion of the disciples* for the increase of the testimony; therefore he says: *And the two disciples heard him speaking,* that is, testifying, *and they followed Jesus,* using the sound counsel of their teacher. These followed the light, because they had good works; whence below, the third[^p262-6]: « He that doth truth cometh to the light, that his works may be made manifest ».

75. (Vers. 38.). *And turning.* Here is touched the third, namely the *reception of the converted disciples;* whence the Lord turns to them and addresses them. Therefore he says: *And Jesus turning, and seeing them following him.* He turned to the converted, according to that of Zacharias, the first[^p262-7]: « Turn ye to me, and I will turn to you ». Thus turned, *he saith to them: What seek you?* He does not say this doubting, but manifesting their right intention through the response.

76. *Who said to him: Rabbi, which is to say, being interpreted, teacher, where dwellest thou?* There is an interposition there[^p262-8], and the sequence of the letter is continued: *Rabbi, where dwellest thou?* as if they said: we seek thee, thy teaching[^tr-doctrina], thy dwelling. This question the soul makes which directly seeks God; the Canticle, the first[^p262-9]: « Shew me, whom my soul loveth, where thou feedest, where thou liest in the midday ». And because they follow with right intention, therefore they are admitted.

77. (Vers. 39.). *He saith to them: Come and see. They came and saw where he abode.* On the other hand, that man who was not seeking with right intention was repulsed; in Matthew, the eighth[^p262-10], a scribe said to him: « Teacher, I will follow thee whithersoever thou shalt go. Jesus said to him: The foxes have holes, and the birds of the air nests; but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head ». And because he is converted in vain who does not continue, therefore he says: *And they stayed there*[^p262-11] *that day.* And the reason is given: *Now it was about the tenth hour.* In which is signified the day of grace, which is the last, in which one must continue; First John, the second[^p262-12]: « Now we know, dearly beloved, that it is the last hour ». In this last hour one must continue with the Lord Jesus; Luke, the twenty-second: « You are they who have continued with me ».

78. (Vers. 40.). *Now one of the disciples was* etc. Here is touched the fourth, namely the *fructification of the disciples,* because immediately after they were converted they call others, as Andrew called Peter. On this account he says: *And Andrew, the brother of Simon Peter, was one of the two who had heard of John and had followed Jesus*[^p262-13]; therefore he names this one specially, that he may show the fruit of his preaching. Therefore he adds the fruit.

79. (Vers. 41.). *He findeth first his brother Simon;* because he was studiously[^tr-studium] seeking him, that he might lead him to the treasure found. Therefore he says: *And he said*[^p262-14] *to him: We have found the Messias, which is, being interpreted, the Christ.* We have found, as good merchants the *pearl,* as good tillers the *treasure*
<!-- page 263 -->
*hidden;* Matthew, the thirteenth: « The kingdom of heaven is like unto a treasure hidden in a field » etc.

80. (Vers. 42.). *And he brought him to Jesus;* Apocalypse, the last[^p263-1]: « He that heareth, let him say: Come. And he that thirsteth, let him come ». *And Jesus looking upon him.* Here is touched the fifth, namely the *acceptance of the disciples' preaching,* because the Lord accepts both the calling or preaching of Andrew, by receiving Peter: on account of which he imposes a name on him as on his own disciple. Therefore he says: *And looking upon him,* namely Peter, *Jesus,* with the eyes namely of kindness; of which look, Genesis, the forty-fourth[^p263-2]: « Bring your brother to me, and I will set my eyes upon him »; *he said: Thou art Simon, the son of Joanna.* He says his name that he may impose another: *Thou shalt be called Cephas, which is interpreted Peter;* Bede[^p263-3]: « It is to be known that in the Syriac tongue *Cephas,* in Greek and Latin *Petrus,* and in either tongue it is derived from petra, rock ». Therefore the Lord says to him in Matthew, the sixteenth: « Thou art Peter, and upon this rock », from which namely thou hast received the name, that is, upon me, « I will build my Church »; I Corinthians, the tenth: « And the rock was Christ ». *Thou shalt be called Peter,* that is, from the firm rock, Christ, whom thou shalt ardently love, thou shalt receive a name[^p263-4].

### Questions.

81. Quaest. I. But it is asked here first about this last testimony: *Behold the Lamb of God:*

1. Because, since he added nothing more, the testimony seems to have been insufficient, because this can be said of any Saint whatever, especially of the Apostles; Luke, the tenth[^p263-5]: « Behold, I send you as lambs among wolves ».

2. Likewise, why does he describe him by the name of *lamb* rather than of another animal?

**I respond:** It must be said that he was called *Lamb* by John because he was *prefigured* in the lamb, Exodus, the twelfth[^p263-6], and *foretold* by the Prophets, Isaiah, the sixteenth. And because the Gospel ought to accord with the Law, therefore he was pointed out by John by the name of *lamb.*

82. But there still remains a question: why was he prefigured by the name of *lamb,* or in a *lamb?*

The answer to this is: by reason of *innocence;* whence Exodus, the twelfth[^p263-7]: « It shall be without blemish, a male, of one year ». By reason of *patience;* Isaiah, the fifty-third[^p263-8]: « As a lamb before his shearer he was dumb ». By reason of *great utility,* because it is *wholly* useful; because the sheep yields[^p263-9] milk for drink, flesh for food, wool for clothing, skin for adornment.

As to what is objected, then, that others are lambs: it must be said that the innocence of Christ differs from the others': because he is without blemish because his blemish was *none;* but the other Saints are without blemish because their blemishes have been *wiped away* by grace.

83. Quaest. II. It is asked about the bearing of this testimony. For it is said that *John stood,* and bore testimony before *two disciples.*

**On the contrary:** 1. John was sent to announce Christ beforehand: therefore it seems that he ought not *to stand,* but to go about, that he might announce the name of Christ.

2. Likewise, he seems envious, because [he told it] only to *two,* when he ought to tell it to all, *that all men might believe through him*[^p263-10].

It must be answered that the testimony of John, so that he might be believed, had to be *authentic,* *pleasing* or accepted, and *not suspect:* because *authentic,* therefore he stood, he did not go about; because *accepted,* therefore he showed his testimony not to anyone whatever, but to those seeking and wishing to know; because *not*
<!-- page 264 -->
*suspect,* he did not thrust himself forward to announce; whence Chrysostom[^p264-1] says: « That the testimony would have been suspect if he had thus thrust himself forward to preach, and the Jews would have been provoked to contradict ».

84. Quaest. III. It is asked about what they say: *Rabbi, where dwellest thou?* And the Lord shows them a lodging. — *On the contrary:* Matthew, the eighth[^p264-2]: *The foxes have holes, and the birds of the air nests; but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head.* There it is said that he had no lodging.

**I respond:** It must be said that *to have a dwelling* is in two modes: either by *ownership of possession,* as rich men have; or by *quiet of inhabiting,* as all have who turn aside to some house to stay. In the first mode he did not have one, as a poor man; in the second mode he had one, as one at rest; for he was not a gyrovague[^p264-3].

85. Quaest. IV. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *One of those disciples was Andrew.* — Why did he name Andrew, not the other disciple? And if he kept silent about the other, why not about Andrew?

**I respond:** It must be said that the reason for expressing the name of *Andrew* rather than of the other was twofold, namely because he was more distinguished, as Chrysostom[^p264-4] says, and that he might show that Peter was called through Andrew. And in this a wonderful dispensation lies open: because he willed to call the greater and the elder through the lesser, and the wise through the simple. Hence he calls Peter through Andrew, and Nathanael, a wise man, through Philip, a rustic[^p264-5].

86. Quaest. V. It is asked about what is said: *Thou shalt be called Cephas,* whether he then imposes this name on him? — That he does not, it seems:

1. By the interlinear[^p264-6]: « He does not yet impose a name on him, but foreshows it ».

2. Likewise, in Mark, the third[^p264-7], it is said that the Lord called the twelve; and then « he gave to Simon the name Peter ».

3. Likewise, in Matthew, the sixteenth[^p264-8], it is said that he imposed it then, when he had confessed.

The reply is commonly made that this name *Peter* was here *foreshown,* in Mark, the third, *imposed,* in Matthew, the sixteenth, *confirmed.* — But Augustine[^p264-9] contradicts this solution, saying that this name was imposed on Peter when he was led by Andrew to Christ; and Bede likewise says on Mark, the third[^p264-10], that it was not imposed then, but before.

On account of this it must be said that it was imposed on him now, but yet it was not *made common;* because he was not yet from then on called *Peter* by others, but by his other, prior name; but afterwards, in Mark, the third, he willed that he be called by this name; and in Matthew, the sixteenth, this naming is rendered celebrated through a dignity befitting the name.

87. Quaest. VI. Likewise it is asked: why did the Lord impose a name on Peter at his calling rather than on others? — It seems that he was a respecter of persons, because he conferred the dignity of a name without merit[^p264-11].

To this Pelagius replies that he did this on account of the greater vigour of natural endowments which he saw in Peter; whence it is said: *Jesus looking upon him;* and Pelagius says that it befalls one to merit through natural endowments[^p264-12].

To this it must be answered that God sees not only exterior things, but also interior; not only the things that are, but also those that are to be: in sign, then, that he was to be the shepherd of the Church, the name is
<!-- page 265 -->
imposed on him. — But as to what Pelagius says: « He looked upon the vigour of natural endowments »; Victor[^p265-1] rejects it; whence he says: « Not on account of the foundation of natural endowments does he superadd the greater edifice of virtue, as Pelagius lyingly said, drawing hence the tinder of error; but he looked upon him with the eye of mercy and kindness ».

88. Quaest. VII. There is also a question: why now are names not changed for those who enter religion, or also for those who are converted to Christ?

To this Chrysostom[^p265-2] responds that the change of name was made as an incitement to virtues. Since, then, we now have one name which is most of all incitative to virtue, therefore we ought to be content with that name; and this name is that of Christianity, because we are all called *Christians.* — Yet the Roman Church still keeps the change of name in the Supreme Pontiff.

This translation keeps the one root in one English word, so ¶ 79's *studiose eum quaerebat* reads "he was studiously seeking him" (Andrew sought his brother with eager care, to bring him to the treasure he had found), and Chrysostom's *humano studio peracta* in p. 264 n. 1 reads "accomplished by human study" — that is, by human effort and contrivance, not by learning.
