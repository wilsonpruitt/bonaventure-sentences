### Capitulum XVI. *Haec locutus sum vobis.*

### Annotatio 66.

(Vers. 4.). Parum infra: *Haec locutus sum vobis, ut, cum venerit hora, eorum reminiscamini, quia ego dixi vobis.*

Nota, quod recordatio verborum Christi ad tria valet: ad *convertendum* a malo; unde in Psalmo[^p605-8]: « Reminiscentur et convertentur ad Dominum ». Exemplum huius Matthaei vigesimo sexto de Petro: « Recordatus est Petrus verbi, quod dixerat Iesus » etc. — Valet etiam ad *promovendum* in bono; unde Deuteronomii octavo[^p605-9]: « Observa et cave, ne quando obliviscaris Domini Dei tui et negligas mandata eius »; et in Psalmo: « Memor fui nocte nominis tui, Domine, et custodivi legem tuam ». — Valet etiam ad *praeservandum* in periculo; unde hic: *Haec locutus sum vobis, ut, cum venerit hora, eorum reminiscamini* etc.

### Collatio LV.

(Vers. 7.). *Expedit vobis, ut ego vadam.*

1. Ideo expedit, quia vado ad *iter expediendum*, vado ad *hospitium praeparandum*, vado ad *negotium procurandum.* — Vado ad *iter expediendum;* de quo dictum est Michaeae secundo[^p605-10]: « Ascendet pandens iter ante eos ». — Vado ad *hospitium praeparandum;* unde Ioannis decimo quarto: « Vado, vobis parare locum ». — Vado ad *negotium procurandum;* unde hic: *Expedit vobis, ut ego vadam. Si enim non abiero, Paracletus non veniet ad vos; si autem abiero, mittam eum ad vos.*

2. Ecce, *ad quid* vado, sed discite, *qualiter* vado. Vado enim *mundum relinquendo;* Ioannis decimo sexto[^p605-11]: « Relinquo mundum et vado ad Patrem ». — Vado *virtutis exempla praebendo;* ego enim sum ille pastor, de quo Ioannis decimo: « Cum
<!-- page 606 -->
proprias oves emiserit, ante eas vadit, et oves illum sequuntur ». — Vado *tormenta passionis sustinendo;* de quo dictum est Petro Ioannis decimo tertio: « Quo ego vado, tu non potes me sequi modo; sequeris autem postea ». — Sic enim itur ad regnum *relinquendo* mundum, *operando* bonum et *sustinendo* malum.

### Collatio LVI.

(Vers. 8.). *Cum venerit Paracletus, ille arguet mundum* etc.

1. Nota, quod quatuor arguunt peccatorem. Arguit enim peccatorem *propria conscientia;* Ieremiae secundo[^p606-1]: « Arguet te malitia tua, et aversio tua increpabit te ». — Arguit *sacra Scriptura;* secundae ad Timotheum tertio: « Omnis Scriptura divinitus inspirata utilis est ad docendum, ad arguendum, ad erudiendum, ad corripiendum »: ad *docendum* vera, ad *arguendum* falsa, ad *erudiendum* de iustitia, ad *corripiendum* perversa. — Arguit *divina iustitia,* haec in *futuro;* Psalmus[^p606-2]: « Arguam te et statuam contra faciem tuam »; et iterum: « Domine, ne in furore tuo arguas me ». — Arguit etiam *divina misericordia* in *praesenti;* Apocalypsis tertio: « Ego quos amo arguo et castigo ». — Propter haec argumenta cavebat sibi Habacuc dicens: « Super custodiam meam stabo et figam gradum super munitionem, et contemplabor, ut videam, quid dicatur mihi, et quid respondeam ad arguentem me ».

2. *Cum venerit,* inquit, *Paracletus, ille arguet mundum.* Nota, quod arguit *Spiritus sanctus,* et arguit *spiritus malus,* sed *differenter.* — Arguit enim *spiritus malus* ad *seducendum;* arguit *Spiriritus sanctus* ad *docendum;* ille *sophistice,* iste *demonstrative.*

Arguit enim spiritus malus ad seducendum dupliciter: aliquando quasi *in dictione*[^p606-3], cum decipit per *suggestionem;* tunc enim est principium fallendi quasi *in voce;* aliquando quasi *extra dictionem,* quando decipit per *rerum ostensionem;* tunc enim est principium decipiendi quasi *in re.* Primo modo decepit Evam, Genesis tertio[^p606-4], scilicet per verba; et propter hoc dicitur Habacuc secundo: « Contemplabor, ut videam, quid dicatur mihi, et quid respondeam ad arguentem me ». — Secundo modo decepit David, quando ostendit ei Bersabee, secundi Regum undecimo; et propter hoc dicitur Ecclesiastici decimo nono: « Vinum et mulieres apostatare faciunt » etiam « sapientes et arguunt sensatos ». Haec ergo sunt argumenta spiritus mali, quae dissolvit sapientia, de qua dicitur Sapientiae octavo[^p606-5], quod « ipsa scit versutias sermonum et dissolutiones argumentorum ».

3. Arguit Spiritus sanctus quasi demonstrative ad *docendum,* et hoc dupliciter[^p606-6]: aliquando *ostensive,* ut promoveat discentes; aliquando quasi *ad impossibile,* ut reducat errantes. — Quasi *ostensive* arguit Christus discipulos, « quibus et praebuit se ipsum vivum post passionem suam in multis argumentis, per dies quadraginta apparens eis et loquens de regno Dei », Actuum primo[^p606-7]. — Quasi *ad impossibile* arguit illos, de quibus hic dicitur: *Arguet mundum de peccato et de iustitia et de iudicio.* Isti enim sunt quasi tres termini divinae argumentationis. *Proponit* enim dignitatem divinae iustitiae, illud scilicet quod habetur Ezechielis decimo octavo[^p606-8]: « Anima, quae peccaverit, ipsa morietur ». Et *assumit* peccatum transgressionis humanae, quasi dicat: tu peccasti. Haec enim est passio contraria disciplinae, quam non acciperet doctor Spiritus, nisi daretur a respondente, id est a peccatore. Tertio *concludit* ex his iudicium damnationis aeternae. Per hunc modum arguit Spiritus sanctus de *peccato et iustitia et iudicio,* quia *iustitiam* proponit, *peccatum* assumit et *iudicium* concludit. Hoc autem argumentum facit Spiritus, non ut stetur in iudicio damnationis, sed ut, viso inconvenienti, quod sequitur ex peccato, redeat peccator ad veritatem correctionis; propter quod dicitur Apocalypsis tertio[^p606-9]: « Ego quos amo arguo et castigo ».

### Collatio LVII.

(Vers. 13.). *Cum autem venerit ille Spiritus veritatis, docebit vos omnem veritatem.*

1. Duo tanguntur in verbis istis: primo, Spiritus sancti *adventus,* cum dicitur: *Cum venerit* etc.; secundo, advenientis *effectus,* cum subiungitur: *Docebit vos* etc.

Ait ergo: *Cum venerit Spiritus sanctus,* qui scilicet solet venire, ubi *diligitur,* ubi *invitatur* et ubi *exspectatur.* Require de hoc supra capitulo decimo quarto[^p606-10].

2. *Docebit vos omnem veritatem,* quae triplex est, scilicet *veritas doctrinae,* quae necessaria est *contemplativis;* de qua Matthaei vigesimo
<!-- page 607 -->
secundo[^p607-1]: « Magister, scimus, quia verax es et viam Dei in veritate doces ». — *Veritas vitae,* quae necessaria est *activis;* de qua Isaiae trigesimo octavo: « Memento, Domine, quomodo ambulaverim coram te in veritate et in corde perfecto ». — *Veritas iustitiae,* quae necessaria est *praelatis;* de qua Proverbiorum decimo octavo: « Accipere personam impii non est bonum, ut declines a veritate iudicii ».

3. Vel aliter distingue: quia Spiritus sanctus solet venire, ubi *diligitur,* ubi *invitatur* et ubi *exspectatur,* ubi ei *hospitium praeparatur;* et tunc adiunge, quod docet quadruplicem veritatem, scilicet veritatem *cognitionis;* unde Psalmus[^p607-2]: « Qui loquitur veritatem in corde suo » etc. — Veritatem *intentionis;* de qua Ioannis octavo: « In veritate non stetit », dicitur de diabolo; et Ioannis septimo: « Qui quaerit gloriam eius qui misit illum, hic verax est ». — Veritatem *locutionis;* de qua Zachariae octavo: « Loquimini veritatem unusquisque cum proximo suo ». — Veritatem *operationis;* de qua primae Ioannis tertio[^p607-3]: « Non diligamus verbo neque lingua, sed opere et veritate »; et Ioannis tertio: « Qui facit veritatem venit ad lucem ».

### Collatio LVIII.

(Vers. 20.). *Tristitia vestra vertetur in gaudium.*

1. Nota, quod est tristitia, quae *vertitur in gaudium;* et est tristitia, quae *continuatur*[^p607-4] *in luctum.* Prima est tristitia *bonorum,* et haec est *sustinenda.* — Secunda est tristitia *malorum,* et haec est *fugienda.*

Tristitia *iustorum* triplex est: tristitia *contritionis,* quae accidit homini ex consideratione sui; secundae ad Corinthios septimo[^p607-5]: « Quae enim secundum Deum tristitia est poenitentiam in salutem stabilem operatur; saeculi autem tristitia mortem operatur ». — Tristitia *compassionis,* quae accidit homini ex consideratione proximi; Psalmus: « Quaesivi, qui simul contristaretur mecum, et non inveni »; et Proverbiorum decimo: « Filius stultus moestitia est matri suae ». — Tristitia *spiritualis desolationis,* quam habet anima propter absentiam sponsi; de qua hic[^p607-6]: « Mundus gaudebit, vos autem contristabimini, sed tristitia vestra vertetur in gaudium ». — Haec tristitia *toleranda* est, quia brevis; Ioannis decimo sexto: « Vos igitur nunc quidem tristitiam habetis; iterum autem videbo vos » etc.

2. Nota, quod dicitur: *Nunc tristitiam habetis,* quasi dicat: momentaneam tristitiam habetis, quia *levis;* tristitia enim spiritualis semper habet intra se admixtam veritatem consolationis; unde magis proprie dicitur *quasi tristitia* quam tristitia; ideo secundae ad Corinthios sexto[^p607-7]: « Quasi tristes, semper autem gaudentes ». — Quia *utilis;* secundae ad Corinthios septimo: « Ecce, quae est secundum Deum tristitia quantam in vobis operatur sollicitudinem, sed defensionem, sed indignationem, sed timorem, sed desiderium, sed aemulationem, sed vindictam ». — Haec est tristitia, quae, ut hic dicitur, *vertetur in gaudium.* De his tribus primae Petri primo[^p607-8]: « In quo exsultabitis, modicum nunc si oportet contristari in variis tentationibus ». — Nota, quod dicit: *contristari nunc,* ecce, tristitia *brevis; contristari modicum,* ecce, tristitia *levis;* item: *si oportet contristari, exsultabitis,* ecce, tristitia *utilis.*

3. Sequitur de tristitia *malorum,* quae triplex est, scilicet tristitia *culpae;* de qua Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo[^p607-9]: « A tristitia festinat mors »; et Ecclesiastici decimo quarto: « Felix, qui non habuit animi sui tristitiam, et qui non est stimulatus in tristitia delicti ». — Tristitia *sequelae;* Ecclesiastici decimo quarto: « Oculus malus ad mala, et non satiabitur pane, sed indigens et in tristitia erit super mensam suam ». — Tristitia *damnationis aeternae;* Ezechielis vigesimo tertio: « Ebrietate et dolore repleberis, calice moeroris et tristitiae, et bibes illum usque ad faeces ». — Haec autem tristitia *fugienda* est, quia *dissipat* consilium rationis; Ecclesiastici decimo quarto[^p607-10]: « Felix, qui non habuit animi sui tristitiam, et qui non excidit a spe sua ». *Cruciat* affectum voluntatis; Ecclesiastici trigesimo: « Multos occidit tristitia, et non est utilitas in illa ». *Enervat* vigorem virtutis; Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo: « A tristitia festinat mors et cooperit virtutem, et tristitia cordis flectit cervicem ».

4. Vel distingue aliter: quia tristitia mala aufert pinguedinem *internae devotionis;* Proverbiorum decimo septimo[^p607-11]. « Spiritus tristis exsiccat ossa ». — Aufert vigorem *bonae operationis;* Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo: « A tristitia festinat mors » etc., ut supra. — Aufert spem *aeternae retributionis;* Ecclesiastici decimo quarto: « Felix, qui non habuit animi sui tristitiam, et qui non excidit a spe sua ».

### Collatio LIX

(Vers. 22.). *Vos igitur nunc tristitiam habetis; iterum videbo vos, et gaudebit cor vestrum* ».

1. In verbis istis duo facit Dominus. Primo consolatur *praesentem Apostolorum tribulationem,* cum dicit: *Vos quidem nunc tristitiam habetis.* — Secundo exprimit *sequentem eorundem consolationem,* cum subiungit: *Iterum videbo vos, et gaudebit cor vestrum.* Tribulatio huiusmodi fuit in tempore passionis; consolatio, in die resurrectionis.

2. *Vos,* inquit, *tristitiam habetis.* Nota, quod triplex est tristitia. Est enim *tristitia,* cuius principium est *malignitas,* et haec est *detestanda.* Est tristitia, cuius principium est *infirmitas,* et haec est *moderanda.* Et est tristitia, cuius principium est *caritas,* et haec est *amplectenda.* — Est igitur tristitia *malignitatis;* de qua Matthaei sexto[^p608-1]. « Nolite fieri, sicut hypocritae, tristes »; Ecclesiastici decimo quarto: « Oculus malus ad mala, et in tristitia erit super mensam suam » etc. — Est etiam tristitia *infirmitatis,* sicut quando tristatur aliquis super re temporali; Ecclesiastici trigesimo[^p608-2]: « Ne des animae tuae tristitiam, et ne affligas temetipsum in consilio tuo ». Haec *moderanda* est; primae ad Thessalonicenses quarto: « Non contristamini, sicut et ceteri, qui spem non habent ». Hanc moderabat Dominus in discipulis, Ioannis decimo sexto, cum dicebat: « Quia haec locutus sum vobis, tristitia implevit cor vestrum. Sed ego in veritate dico vobis: Expedit vobis, ut ego vadam ». — Est etiam tristitia *caritatis;* de qua dicitur hic: *Vos quidem nunc tristitiam habetis,* quia scilicet me ad passionem ire videtis. Et hinc promittitur consolatio, cum subiungitur:

3. *Iterum videbo vos; videbo,* inquit, *vos,* id est, visitabo vos. Et nota, quod Dominus tripliciter visitat, scilicet *corripiendo* in culpa, *locupletando* in gratia, *beatificando* in gloria. — Visitat, ut *corripiat culpam,* sicut solet praelatus visitare subditos; Psalmus[^p608-3]: « Visitabo in virga iniquitates eorum et in verberibus peccata eorum ». — Visitat *infundendo gratiam,* sicut solet dives amicus visitare pauperem amicum ipsum locupletando; Psalmus: « Visitasti terram et inebriasti eam, multiplicasti locupletare eam ». — Visitat *largiendo gloriam,* sicut solet medicus visitare infirmum, curando aegritudinem, tribuendo sanitatem. Ita ipse Deus curat miseriam et tribuit felicitatem; de qua hic dicitur: *Videbo vos, et gaudebit cor vestrum, et gaudium vestrum nemo tollet a vobis.*

### Collatio LX.

(Vers. 24.). *Petite, et accipietis, ut gaudium vestrum plenum sit.*

1. In verbis istis exhortans nos Dominus ad petendum, duo facit: *promittit exauditionem,* cum dicit: *Petite, et accipietis* — et *format petitionem,* cum dicit: *Petite, et accipietis, ut gaudium vestrum sit plenum.* Petite gaudium, sed attendite, *cuiusmodi* sit petendum. Est enim gaudium *mundi,* gaudium *iusti* et gaudium *caeli;* vel: gaudium *temporale,* gaudium *spirituale,* gaudium *aeternale;* sive: gaudium *temporis,* gaudium *pectoris* et gaudium *aeternitatis.*

2. Gaudium primum, scilicet gaudium *temporale,* non est petendum vel appetendum, quia *vanum;* Ecclesiastae secundo[^p608-4]: « Risum reputavi errorem, et gaudio dixi, quid frustra deciperis »? — Quia *mixtum;* Proverbiorum decimo quarto: « Risus dolore miscebitur, et extrema gaudii luctus occupat ». — Quia *momentaneum;* Iob vigesimo: « Gaudium hypocritae ad instar puncti »; et Iob vigesimo primo: « Tenent tympanum et citharam et gaudent ad sonitum organi; ducunt in bonis dies suos et in puncto ad inferna descendunt ».

3. Gaudium secundum, scilicet *spirituale,* appetendum est et est triplex. Gaudet enim homo spiritualis pro *evasione periculorum,* id est peccatorum; Baruch quarto[^p608-5]: « Emisi vos cum luctu et ploratu; reducet autem vos Dominus cum gaudio »; loquitur Ierusalem in figura Ecclesiae ad peccatores. — Gaudet etiam pro *experientia beneficiorum;* Habacuc tertio: « Ego autem in Domino gaudebo », id est in experientia bonitatis divinae; « et exsultabo in Deo Iesu meo »; quasi dicat: cum « ficus non florebit, et non erit germen in vineis » etc., cum scilicet non erit exterius consolatio, erit mihi interius in Domino exsultatio. — Gaudet etiam pro *exspectatione praemiorum;* Matthaei quinto[^p608-6], Lucae sexto: « Gaudete et exsultate, quoniam merces vestra copiosa est in caelis » etc.

4. Gaudium tertium, quod est *aeternale,* est postulandum et desiderandum, quia *verum.* Est enim gaudium cordis; nec gaudet vere, nisi qui gaudet in corde; Ecclesiastici trigesimo[^p608-7]: « Non est oblectamentum super cordis gaudium ». Tale est gaudium caeli; Ioannis decimo sexto: « Videbo vos, et gaudebit cor vestrum ». — Quia *plenum;* Ioannis decimo sexto: « Petite, et accipietis, ut gaudium vestrum sit plenum »; et eodem: « Tristitia vestra vertetur in gaudium ».
<!-- page 609 -->

5. Nota, quod tunc *omnia* erunt gaudium, quando « tristitia vertetur in gaudium »; ideo dicitur Ioannis decimo quinto[^p609-1]: « Haec locutus sum vobis, ut gaudium meum in vobis sit, et gaudium vestrum impleatur »; et propter plenitudinem gaudii dicitur servo fideli, Matthaei vigesimo quinto, non: *accipe* gaudium, sed: « *intra* in gaudium Domini tui ». — Quia *aeternum;* Ioannis decimo sexto: « Gaudium vestrum nemo tollet a vobis »; et Isaiae sexagesimo quinto: « Gaudebitis et exsultabitis usque in sempiternum ».

### Chapter XVI. *These things have I spoken to you.*

### Annotation 66.

(Vers. 4.). A little below: *These things have I spoken to you, that, when the hour shall come, you may remember them, that I told you.*

Note that the remembrance of the words of Christ avails for three things: for *converting* from evil; whence in the Psalm[^p605-8]: « They shall remember, and shall be converted to the Lord ». An example of this is in Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter, concerning Peter: « Peter remembered the word which Jesus had said » etc. — It avails also for *advancing* in good; whence Deuteronomy, the eighth chapter[^p605-9]: « Take heed and beware lest at any time thou forget the Lord thy God, and neglect his commandments »; and in the Psalm: « I have remembered thy name in the night, O Lord, and have kept thy law ». — It avails also for *preserving* in danger; whence here: *These things have I spoken to you, that, when the hour shall come, you may remember them* etc.

### Collation LV.

(Vers. 7.). *It is expedient to you that I go.*

1. It is expedient for this reason, because I go *to expedite the way*, I go *to prepare the lodging*, I go *to procure the business.* — I go *to expedite the way;* of which it is said in Micheas, the second chapter[^p605-10]: « He shall go up that shall open the way before them ». — I go *to prepare the lodging;* whence John, the fourteenth chapter: « I go to prepare a place for you ». — I go *to procure the business;* whence here: *It is expedient to you that I go. For if I go not, the Paraclete will not come to you; but if I go, I will send him to you.*

2. Behold, *for what* I go; but learn *how* I go. For I go *by leaving the world;* John, the sixteenth chapter[^p605-11]: « I leave the world, and I go to the Father ». — I go *by offering examples of virtue;* for I am that shepherd of whom John, the tenth chapter: « When he hath let out
<!-- page 606 -->
his own sheep, he goeth before them, and the sheep follow him ». — I go *by sustaining the torments of the passion;* of which it was said to Peter in John, the thirteenth chapter: « Whither I go, thou canst not follow me now; but thou shalt follow hereafter ». — For thus one goes to the kingdom: *by leaving* the world, *by working* good and *by sustaining* evil.

### Collation LVI.

(Vers. 8.). *When the Paraclete is come, he will convince the world* etc.

1. Note that four convince the sinner. For the sinner is convinced by *his own conscience;* Jeremias, the second chapter[^p606-1]: « Thy own wickedness shall convince thee, and thy apostasy shall rebuke thee ». — *Sacred Scripture* convinces; the second to Timothy, the third chapter: « All scripture, divinely inspired, is profitable to teach, to convince, to instruct, to correct »: to *teach* true things, to *convince* false things, to *instruct* in justice, to *correct* perverse things. — *Divine justice* convinces, and this in *the future;* the Psalm[^p606-2]: « I will convince thee, and set before thy face »; and again: « O Lord, convince me not in thy fury ». — *Divine mercy* also convinces, in *the present;* the Apocalypse, the third chapter: « Such as I love, I convince and chastise ». — On account of these arguments Habacuc was on his guard, saying: « I will stand upon my watch, and fix my foot upon the tower, and I will watch, to see what will be said to me, and what I may answer to him that convinceth me ».

2. *When,* he says, *the Paraclete is come, he will convince the world.* Note that *the Holy Spirit* convinces, and *the evil spirit* convinces, but *differently.* — For *the evil spirit* convinces *in order to seduce;* *the Holy Spirit* [so printed: *Spiriritus* for *Spiritus*] convinces *in order to teach;* the former *sophistically,* the latter *demonstratively.*

For the evil spirit convinces in order to seduce in two ways: sometimes as it were *in the expression*[^p606-3], when he deceives through *suggestion;* for then the principle of misleading is as it were *in the word;* sometimes as it were *outside the expression,* when he deceives through *the showing of things;* for then the principle of deceiving is as it were *in the thing.* In the first way he deceived Eve, Genesis, the third chapter[^p606-4], namely through words; and on account of this it is said in Habacuc, the second chapter: « I will watch, to see what will be said to me, and what I may answer to him that convinceth me ». — In the second way he deceived David, when he showed him Bethsabee, the second of Kings, the eleventh chapter; and on account of this it is said in Ecclesiasticus, the nineteenth chapter: « Wine and women make to fall off » even « the wise, and convince men of understanding ». These, then, are the arguments of the evil spirit, which wisdom dissolves, of which it is said in Wisdom, the eighth chapter[^p606-5], that « she knoweth the subtilties of speeches and the dissolutions of arguments ».

3. The Holy Spirit convinces as it were demonstratively, *in order to teach,* and this in two ways[^p606-6]: sometimes *ostensively,* that he may advance learners; sometimes as it were *to the impossible,* that he may lead back the erring. — As it were *ostensively* Christ convinced the disciples, « to whom also he showed himself alive after his passion in many arguments, for forty days appearing to them, and speaking of the kingdom of God », the Acts, the first chapter[^p606-7]. — As it were *to the impossible* he convinces those of whom it is said here: *He will convince the world of sin, and of justice, and of judgement.* For these are as it were the three terms of the divine argumentation. For he *proposes* the axiom of divine justice, namely that which is had in Ezechiel, the eighteenth chapter[^p606-8]: « The soul that sinneth, the same shall die ». And he *assumes* the sin of human transgression, as if he said: thou hast sinned. For this is the passion contrary to the discipline, which the Spirit as teacher would not accept unless it were granted by the respondent, that is, by the sinner. Thirdly he *concludes* from these the judgement of eternal damnation. In this manner the Holy Spirit convinces of *sin and justice and judgement,* because he proposes *justice,* assumes *sin* and concludes *judgement.* But the Spirit makes this argument, not that one should stand in the judgement of damnation, but that, the unfitting consequence that follows from sin having been seen, the sinner may return to the truth of correction; for which reason it is said in the Apocalypse, the third chapter[^p606-9]: « Such as I love, I convince and chastise ».

### Collation LVII.

(Vers. 13.). *But when that Spirit of truth is come, he will teach you all truth.*

1. Two things are touched on in these words: first, the *coming* of the Holy Spirit, when it is said: *When he is come* etc.; second, the *effect* of the one coming, when it is added: *He will teach you* etc.

He says, then: *When the Holy Spirit is come,* who is wont, namely, to come where he is *loved,* where he is *invited* and where he is *awaited.* Look concerning this above, at the fourteenth chapter[^p606-10].

2. *He will teach you all truth,* which is threefold, namely *the truth of teaching*[^tr-doctrina], which is necessary *for the contemplatives;* of which Matthew, the twenty-
<!-- page 607 -->
second chapter[^p607-1]: « Teacher, we know that thou art a true speaker, and teachest the way of God in truth ». — *The truth of life,* which is necessary *for the actives;* of which Isaias, the thirty-eighth chapter: « Remember, O Lord, how I have walked before thee in truth and with a perfect heart ». — *The truth of justice,* which is necessary *for prelates;* of which Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter: « It is not good to accept the person of the wicked, to decline from the truth of judgement ».

3. Or distinguish otherwise: because the Holy Spirit is wont to come where he is *loved,* where he is *invited* and where he is *awaited,* where a *lodging is prepared* for him; and then add that he teaches a fourfold truth, namely the truth *of cognition;* whence the Psalm[^p607-2]: « He that speaketh truth in his heart » etc. — The truth *of intention;* of which John, the eighth chapter: « He stood not in the truth », it is said of the devil; and John, the seventh chapter: « He that seeketh the glory of him that sent him, he is true ». — The truth *of speech;* of which Zacharias, the eighth chapter: « Speak ye truth every one to his neighbour ». — The truth *of working;* of which the first of John, the third chapter[^p607-3]: « Let us not love in word, nor in tongue, but in deed and in truth »; and John, the third chapter: « He that doth truth cometh to the light ».

### Collation LVIII.

(Vers. 20.). *Your sorrow shall be turned into joy.*

1. Note that there is a sorrow which *is turned into joy;* and there is a sorrow which *is continued*[^p607-4] *into mourning.* The first is the sorrow *of the good,* and this is *to be sustained.* — The second is the sorrow *of the wicked,* and this is *to be fled.*

The sorrow *of the just* is threefold: the sorrow *of contrition,* which befalls a man from the consideration of himself; the second to the Corinthians, the seventh chapter[^p607-5]: « For the sorrow that is according to God worketh penance steadfast unto salvation; but the sorrow of the world worketh death ». — The sorrow *of compassion,* which befalls a man from the consideration of his neighbour; the Psalm: « I sought one that would be made sorrowful together with me, and I found none »; and Proverbs, the tenth chapter: « A foolish son is the sorrow of his mother ». — The sorrow *of spiritual desolation,* which the soul has on account of the absence of the bridegroom; of which here[^p607-6]: « The world shall rejoice, and you shall be made sorrowful, but your sorrow shall be turned into joy ». — This sorrow is *to be tolerated,* because it is brief; John, the sixteenth chapter: « You therefore now indeed have sorrow; but I will see you again » etc.

2. Note that it is said: *Now you have sorrow,* as if he said: you have a momentary sorrow, because it is *light;* for spiritual sorrow always has within itself an admixed truth of consolation; whence it is more properly called *as it were sorrow* than sorrow; therefore the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter[^p607-7]: « As sorrowful, yet always rejoicing ». — Because it is *useful;* the second to the Corinthians, the seventh chapter: « Behold, the sorrow that is according to God, how great carefulness it worketh in you, yea defence, yea indignation, yea fear, yea desire, yea zeal, yea revenge ». — This is the sorrow which, as is said here, *shall be turned into joy.* Concerning these three, the first of Peter, the first chapter[^p607-8]: « Wherein you shall exult, if now for a little you must be made sorrowful in divers temptations ». — Note that he says: *to be made sorrowful now,* behold, the sorrow is *brief; to be made sorrowful for a little,* behold, the sorrow is *light;* likewise: *if you must be made sorrowful, you shall exult,* behold, the sorrow is *useful.*

3. There follows the sorrow *of the wicked,* which is threefold, namely the sorrow *of fault;* of which Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p607-9]: « Of sorrow death hasteneth »; and Ecclesiasticus, the fourteenth chapter: « Happy is he that hath had no sorrow of his mind, and that hath not been pricked with the sorrow of sin ». — The sorrow *of the sequel;* Ecclesiasticus, the fourteenth chapter: « An evil eye is towards evil things, and he shall not have enough of bread, but shall be needy and in sorrow at his own table ». — The sorrow *of eternal damnation;* Ezechiel, the twenty-third chapter: « Thou shalt be filled with drunkenness and grief, with the cup of heaviness and sorrow, and thou shalt drink it even to the dregs ». — But this sorrow is *to be fled,* because it *scatters* the counsel of reason; Ecclesiasticus, the fourteenth chapter[^p607-10]: « Happy is he that hath had no sorrow of his mind, and who is not fallen from his hope ». It *torments* the affection of the will; Ecclesiasticus, the thirtieth chapter: « For sorrow hath killed many, and there is no profit in it ». It *enervates* the vigour of virtue; Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter: « Of sorrow death hasteneth, and it overwhelmeth the strength, and the sorrow of the heart boweth down the neck ».

4. Or distinguish otherwise: because evil sorrow takes away the fatness *of interior devotion;* Proverbs, the seventeenth chapter[^p607-11]. « A sorrowful spirit drieth up the bones ». — It takes away the vigour *of good working;* Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter: « Of sorrow death hasteneth » etc., as above. — It takes away the hope *of eternal retribution;* Ecclesiasticus, the fourteenth chapter: « Happy is he that hath had no sorrow of his mind, and who is not fallen from his hope ».

<!-- page 608 -->

### Collation LIX

(Vers. 22.). *You therefore now have sorrow; I will see you again, and your heart shall rejoice* ».

1. In these words the Lord does two things. First he consoles *the present tribulation of the Apostles,* when he says: *You now indeed have sorrow.* — Secondly he expresses *their consolation that follows,* when he adds: *I will see you again, and your heart shall rejoice.* Tribulation of this kind was in the time of the passion; consolation, on the day of the resurrection.

2. *You,* he says, *have sorrow.* Note that sorrow is threefold. For there is *a sorrow* whose principle is *malignity,* and this is *to be detested.* There is a sorrow whose principle is *infirmity,* and this is *to be moderated.* And there is a sorrow whose principle is *charity,* and this is *to be embraced.* — There is, then, the sorrow *of malignity;* of which Matthew, the sixth chapter[^p608-1]. « Be not, as the hypocrites, sorrowful »; Ecclesiasticus, the fourteenth chapter: « An evil eye is towards evil things, and he shall be in sorrow at his own table » etc. — There is also the sorrow *of infirmity,* as when someone is made sorrowful over a temporal thing; Ecclesiasticus, the thirtieth chapter[^p608-2]: « Give not sorrow to thy soul, and afflict not thyself in thy own counsel ». This is *to be moderated;* the first to the Thessalonians, the fourth chapter: « Be not sorrowful, even as others who have no hope ». This the Lord moderated in the disciples, John, the sixteenth chapter, when he said: « Because I have spoken these things to you, sorrow hath filled your heart. But I tell you the truth: It is expedient to you that I go ». — There is also the sorrow *of charity;* of which it is said here: *You now indeed have sorrow,* because, namely, you see me going to the passion. And hence consolation is promised, when it is added:

3. *I will see you again; I will see,* he says, *you,* that is, I will visit you. And note that the Lord visits in three ways, namely *by correcting* in fault, *by enriching* in grace, *by beatifying* in glory. — He visits that he may *correct fault,* as a prelate is wont to visit his subjects; the Psalm[^p608-3]: « I will visit their iniquities with a rod, and their sins with stripes ». — He visits *by infusing grace,* as a rich friend is wont to visit a poor friend by enriching him; the Psalm: « Thou hast visited the earth, and hast plentifully watered it; thou hast many ways enriched it ». — He visits *by bestowing glory,* as a physician is wont to visit the infirm, curing the sickness, bestowing health. So God himself cures misery and bestows felicity; of which it is said here: *I will see you, and your heart shall rejoice, and your joy no man shall take from you.*

### Collation LX.

(Vers. 24.). *Ask, and you shall receive, that your joy may be full.*

1. In these words the Lord, exhorting us to ask, does two things: he *promises the hearing,* when he says: *Ask, and you shall receive* — and he *forms the petition,* when he says: *Ask, and you shall receive, that your joy may be full.* Ask for joy, but attend *of what kind* it is to be asked for. For there is the joy *of the world,* the joy *of the just* and the joy *of heaven;* or: *temporal* joy, *spiritual* joy, *eternal* joy; or else: the joy *of time,* the joy *of the breast* and the joy *of eternity.*

2. The first joy, namely *temporal* joy, is not to be asked for or sought, because it is *vain;* Ecclesiastes, the second chapter[^p608-4]: « Laughter I counted error, and to joy I said: Why art thou vainly deceived »? — Because it is *mixed;* Proverbs, the fourteenth chapter: « Laughter shall be mingled with sorrow, and mourning taketh hold of the end of joy ». — Because it is *momentary;* Job, the twentieth chapter: « The joy of the hypocrite is but for a moment »; and Job, the twenty-first chapter: « They take the timbrel and the harp, and rejoice at the sound of the organ; they spend their days in wealth, and in a moment they go down to hell ».

3. The second joy, namely *spiritual* joy, is to be sought and is threefold. For the spiritual man rejoices for *the escape from dangers,* that is, from sins; Baruch, the fourth chapter[^p608-5]: « I sent you forth with mourning and weeping; but the Lord will bring you back with joy »; Jerusalem speaks in the figure of the Church to sinners. — He rejoices also for *the experience of benefits;* Habacuc, the third chapter: « But I will rejoice in the Lord », that is, in the experience of the divine goodness; « and I will exult in God my Jesus »; as if he said: when « the fig tree shall not blossom, and there shall be no spring in the vines » etc., when, namely, there shall be no consolation outwardly, there shall be exultation for me inwardly in the Lord. — He rejoices also for *the expectation of rewards;* Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p608-6], Luke, the sixth chapter: « Rejoice and exult, for your reward is very great in heaven » etc.

4. The third joy, which is *eternal,* is to be asked and desired, because it is *true.* For it is the joy of the heart; nor does anyone truly rejoice save he who rejoices in the heart; Ecclesiasticus, the thirtieth chapter[^p608-7]: « There is no pleasure above the joy of the heart ». Such is the joy of heaven; John, the sixteenth chapter: « I will see you, and your heart shall rejoice ». — Because it is *full;* John, the sixteenth chapter: « Ask, and you shall receive, that your joy may be full »; and in the same place: « Your sorrow shall be turned into joy ».
<!-- page 609 -->

5. Note that then *all things* shall be joy, when « sorrow shall be turned into joy »; therefore it is said in John, the fifteenth chapter[^p609-1]: « These things I have spoken to you, that my joy may be in you, and your joy may be filled »; and on account of the fullness of joy it is said to the faithful servant, Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter, not: *receive* joy, but: « *enter* into the joy of thy lord ». — Because it is *eternal;* John, the sixteenth chapter: « Your joy no man shall take from you »; and Isaias, the sixty-fifth chapter: « You shall rejoice and exult for ever ».
