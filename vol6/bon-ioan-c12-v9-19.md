# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XII, Vers. 9-19
## *Praedeterminata est conspiratio et conspirationis corroboratio; tertio hic tangitur conspirationis amplificatio*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 413 -->

14. *Cognovit ergo turba multa.* Praedeterminata est conspiratio et conspirationis corroboratio[^p413-10]; tertio hic tangitur *conspirationis amplificatio.* Prius enim cogitaverant occidere Iesum, hic non solum Iesum, sed etiam Lazarum suscitatum. Et ratio erat: quia multi propter eum credebant in Iesum[^p413-11]. Duo ergo hic notantur, scilicet *credentium multitudo* et *perversitatis Iudaeorum amplificatio.*

(Vers. 9.). *Credentium multitudo* facta est occasione suscitationis Lazari; propter quod dicit: *Cognovit ergo turba multa ex Iudaeis, quia illic esset*[^p413-12], scilicet in Bethania; *et venerunt, non propter Iesum tantum, sed ut Lazarum viderent, quem suscitavit a mortuis.* Adhuc infirmi erant, quia eorum oculi non tantum delectabantur in aspectu auctoris, sed etiam operum; unde erant de illis, de quibus dicitur supra secundo[^p413-13]: « Multi crediderunt in eum, videntes signa, quae faciebat »; unde miracula videre volebant, supra sexto: « Sequebatur Iesum multitudo magna, quia videbat signa ».

15. (Vers. 10.). *Cogitaverunt autem principes sacerdotum.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *conspirationis et perversitatis amplificatio* in auctorem[^p413-14], quia non solum in Dominum saeviebant, sed in alios propter eum; unde dicit: *Cogitaverunt autem principes sacerdotum, ut et Lazarum interficerent,* non tantum Iesum; Isaiae primo[^p413-15]: « Manus vestrae sanguine plenae sunt »; et ratio subditur:

16. (Vers. 11.). *Quia multi propter illum abibant ex Iudaeis et credebant in Iesum;* et ideo interficere volebant. Augustinus[^p413-16]: « Stulta cogitatio!
<!-- page 414 -->
Dominus, qui suscitare potuit mortuum, nonne potuit suscitare occisum? Quando Lazaro inferebatis necem, nunquid auferebatis Domino potestatem? Si aliud videtur mortuus, aliud occisus; ecce, Dominus utrumque fecit: et Lazarum mortuum, et se ipsum suscitavit occisum ». Viam salutis claudere volebant; Matthaei vigesimo tertio[^p414-1]: « Ipsi non introistis, et alios introire prohibuistis ».

17. *In crastinum autem* etc. Determinata est conspirationis amplificatio, determinatur hic quarto et ultimo ipsius per obstinationem *malitiae consummatio.* Et haec obstinatio occasionem habuit ex honore Domino exhibito, propter quem in miram invidiam prosilibant; et proceditur sic. Primo determinatur *Christi honorificatio;* secundo, *honoris* illius *per Scripturam approbatio;* tertio, *honorificandi ratio;* quarto, *Pharisaeorum malevolentissima obstinatio.*

(Vers. 12.). Honor autem describitur tripliciter: primo quoad *modum occurrendi,* quia cum laetitia; propter quod dicit: *In crastinum autem turba multa*[^p414-2]*, cum audissent, quia Iesus venit Ierosolymam,* occurrerunt ei cum laetitia; in cuius signum:

18. (Vers. 13.). *Acceperunt ramos palmarum et processerunt ei obviam;* hunc morem habebant ex Lege; unde dicitur secundi Esdrae octavo[^p414-3]: « Egredimini in montem et afferte frondes olivae et frondes ligni pulcherrimi et ramos palmarum ». — Describitur etiam quoad *modum laudandi;* propter quod dicit: *Et clamabant: Hosanna;* sicut dicit Glossa[^p414-4], « vox ista est obsecrantis, potius affectum quam aliquid indicans, sicut interiectiones », et tantum valet *salva, obsecro. Benedictus, qui venit in nomine Domini, rex Israel;* in quo confitentur in ipso regiam dignitatem, non ab homine, sed a Deo; ideo dicunt: *rex Israel in nomine Domini,* secundum illud Lucae primo[^p414-5]: « Hic erit magnus et Filius Altissimi vocabitur, et dabit illi Dominus Deus sedem David patris eius, et regnabit in domo Iacob in aeternum ». — Describitur etiam quoad *modum procedendi;* quia non procedebat per pedes, sed super asinum sedebat; propter quod dicit:

19. (Vers. 14.). *Et invenit Iesus asellum et sedit super eum.* Etsi verus Rex esset et honorem regium sibi offerri permitteret; non tamen in multitudine equorum, sed super asinum venit. In quo significatur, ut dicit Augustinus[^p414-6], quod « non ad exigendum tributum, non ad armandum exercitum ferro rex erat, sed rex Israel, qui mentes regat, qui consulat, qui in regnum caelorum credentes, sperantes amantesque perducat ».

20. *Sicut scriptum est.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet facti illius *per Scripturam approbatio,* et hoc facit ostendendo, illud in Scriptura fuisse praedictum; quia hoc robur dat verae fidei. Propterea dicit, quod sedit super asinum in honore regio; ne putes hoc casu gestum, ideo dicit: *Sicut scriptum est,* Zachariae nono[^p414-7]:

21. (Vers. 15.). *Noli timere, filia Sion,* id est Ecclesia; minus dicit et plus intelligit, id est, *exsulta;* Zachariae nono[^p414-8]: « Exsulta satis, filia Sion; iubila, filia Ierusalem ». Et ratio exsultationis redditur: *Ecce, rex tuus venit tibi*[^p414-9]*;* ecce, *regia dignitas; sedens super pullum asinae;* ecce, *mansuetudo et humilitas.* Et in adventu talis est laetandum, quia dicitur Proverbiorum decimo sexto: « In hilaritate vultus regis vita, et clementia eius quasi imber serotinus ». Et licet honor iste esset praedictus, tamen non est a discipulis Christi praecognitus, ut scias, hoc non esse factum humana adinventione, sed divina dispensatione. Propterea subdit:

22. (Vers. 16.). *Haec non cognoverunt discipuli eius primum,* quando scilicet hunc honorem contulerunt; *sed quando glorificatus est Iesus,* post resurrectionem et ascensionem, *tunc recordati sunt,* id est, adverterunt, *quia haec erant scripta de eo; et haec fecerunt ei.* Post glorificationem cognoverunt, non ante; quia tunc primo, sicut dicitur Lucae ultimo[^p414-10], « aperuit illis sensum, ut intelligerent Scripturas ».

23. (Vers. 17.). *Testimonium ergo perhibebat* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *honoris exhibiti ratio;* et haec erat ipsa mirabilis Lazari suscitatio, quae turbis constabat; propter quod dicit: *Testimonium ergo perhibebat illi*[^p414-11] *turba, quae*
<!-- page 415 -->
*erat cum eo, quando Lazarum vocavit de monumento,* dicens: « Lazare, veni foras », *et suscitavit eum a mortuis.* Ista turba perhibebat testimonium eius omnipotentiae, turba scilicet *simplicium* et etiam turba *sapientium: sapientium,* Actuum decimo[^p415-1]: « Huic omnes Prophetae testimonium perhibent »; *simplicium,* Psalmus: « Ex ore infantium et lactentium perfecisti laudem ». Propter quod signum et testimonium collatus est ei honor iste; ideo dicit:

24. (Vers. 18.). *Propterea*[^p415-2] *obviam venit ei turba, quia audierunt, ipsum fecisse hoc signum.* Turba simplex videns signa movebatur ad fidem et devotionem; propter quod dicitur Matthaei nono: « Videntes turbae timuerunt et glorificaverunt Deum, qui dedit potestatem talem hominibus »; sed scribae dicebant, quod blasphemaret.

25. (Vers. 19.). *Pharisaei ergo dixerunt ad semetipsos.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *Pharisaeorum ex invidia obstinatio;* propterea dicebant inter se: *Videte, quia nihil proficimus*[^p415-3], scilicet quandiu vivit; secundae ad Timotheum tertio: « Veritati resistunt, homines corrupti mente, reprobi circa fidem, sed ultra non proficient ». Et post: *Ecce, totus mundus post eum abiit.* In quo notatur, quod iaculo invidiae commoti sunt; unde dicitur Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p415-4], quod videntes scribae et Pharisaei « indignati sunt », quia ex *invidia* dolebant, ex *superbia* despiciebant, ex ipsa *ira* furebant; et ideo in malitia obstinati erant; « invidia diaboli mors intravit in orbem terrarum » etc., Sapientiae secundo[^p415-5]; Ecclesiastici decimo quarto: « Oculus malus in tristitia erit super mensam suam ».

### Quaestiones.

26. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic: quia videtur, quod Dominus in operibus suis fuerit sibi contrarius:

1. Quia supra undecimo[^p415-6] dicitur, quod Dominus *abscondit se* a facie Iudaeorum persequentium; hic autem ita manifeste se offert.

2. Item, Dominus supra sexto[^p415-7] *fugit in montem,* quando voluerunt eum facere regem; hic suscipit honorem collatum sibi tanquam regi et approbat.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Dominus, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p415-8], non facit *contraria,* sed pro congruitate facit *diversa,* sicut prius vetus et post novum edidit testamentum.

1. Propter quod dicendum, quod tempus passionis prius nondum advenerat, modo aderat; ideo prius se occultabat, quia tempus exspectabat; modo, cum adest opportunitas et tempus, se manifestat[^p415-9].

2. Similiter ad secundum: quia ante passionem ignotus esse volebat, prope et post passionem vero glorificandus; ideo tunc honorem regium et non ante suscepit[^p415-10]. — Alia ratio est, quia isti non fecerunt eum regem, sed a Deo missum recognoscebant; sed illi eum regem facere volebant. — Tertia ratio est: quia Dominus statim quasi post haec erat passurus, voluit honorari, ut ex hoc honore augeretur et succedens ignominia passionis[^p415-11].

27. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de contrarietate:

1. Quia dicitur Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p415-12], quod primo misit in castellum pro asino, deinde quod turba venit ei obviam.
<!-- page 416 -->

2. Item, ibidem[^p416-1] dicitur, quod *sedit super asinam et pullum,* hic autem dicitur, quod super *asellum.*

3. Item, hoc non est scriptum in Zacharia[^p416-2]: *Noli timere, filia Sion;* sed: *Exsulta et iubila, filia* etc.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum ad hoc ultimum, quod quia Evangelistae per Spiritum sanctum loquebantur, qui totam condidit Scripturam et est auctor; ideo non fecerunt vim in *verborum similitudine,* sed in *intellectuum sinceritate;* et idem ipsum, quod dixit ille per verbum *exsultandi,* hoc ipsum dicit Evangelista per verbum *non timendi*[^p416-3].

1. 2. Quod vero obiicitur de *contrarietate* et *praeposteratione;* dicendum est, quod Dominus sedit super asinam et etiam super pullum eius. Et dicunt quidam, hoc fuisse hoc modo: prius insedit *pullo,* et quia recalcitrabat, post sedit super *asinam*[^p416-4]. — Sed hoc nihil est, quia non credo, quod pullus unquam, Domino insidente, recalcitraret; immo intelligendum, quod Dominus propter figuram prius sedit super *asinam,* id est super veterem synagogam, et post super pullum, id est gentilitatem indomitam[^p416-5]. — Et prius sedit super asinam, quam turbae occurrerent; ideo Matthaeus prius narrat; sed post, cum turbae occurrerunt, tunc sedit super asellum. Et ideo Ioannes[^p416-6] dicit post adventum turbarum: *Invenit Iesus asellum et sedit super eum;* non curans totum dicere, quia alius dixerat.

## English
<!-- page 413 -->

14. *A great multitude therefore knew.* The conspiracy and the corroboration of the conspiracy[^p413-10] were determined beforehand; third, here is touched *the amplification of the conspiracy.* For before they had thought to kill Jesus, here not only Jesus, but also the raised Lazarus. And the reason was: because many believed in Jesus on account of him[^p413-11]. Two things, then, are noted here, namely, *the multitude of believers* and *the amplification of the perversity of the Jews.*

(V. 9.). *The multitude of believers* came about on the occasion of the raising of Lazarus; on account of which he says: *A great multitude therefore of the Jews knew that he was there*[^p413-12], namely, in Bethania; *and they came, not for Jesus' sake only, but that they might see Lazarus, whom he had raised from the dead.* They were still infirm, because their eyes took delight not only in the sight of the author, but also of his works; whence they were of those of whom it is said above, the second[^p413-13]: « Many believed in him, seeing the signs which he did »; whence they wished to see miracles, above, the sixth: « A great multitude followed Jesus, because they saw the signs ».

15. (V. 10.). *But the chief priests thought.* Here is touched the second, namely, *the amplification of the conspiracy and perversity* against the author[^p413-14], because they raged not only against the Lord, but against others on account of him; whence he says: *But the chief priests thought to kill Lazarus also,* not Jesus only; Isaias, the first[^p413-15]: « Your hands are full of blood »; and the reason is added:

16. (V. 11.). *Because many of the Jews, by reason of him, went away, and believed in Jesus;* and therefore they wished to kill him. Augustine[^p413-16]: « Foolish thought!
<!-- page 414 -->
The Lord, who could raise one dead, could he not raise one slain? When you were inflicting death on Lazarus, were you taking power away from the Lord? If one dead seems one thing, one slain another; behold, the Lord did both: he raised both Lazarus dead, and himself slain ». They wished to close the way of salvation; Matthew, the twenty-third[^p414-1]: « You yourselves have not entered in, and others you have forbidden to enter ».

17. *And on the next day* etc. The amplification of the conspiracy has been determined; here is determined, fourth and last, its *consummation of malice* through obstinacy. And this obstinacy had its occasion from the honour shown to the Lord, on account of which they burst forth into a wonderful envy; and one proceeds thus. First is determined *the honouring of Christ;* second, *the approbation* of that *honour through Scripture;* third, *the reason for the honouring;* fourth, *the most malevolent obstinacy of the Pharisees.*

(V. 12.). Now the honour is described in three ways: first as to *the mode of meeting,* because with joy; on account of which he says: *And on the next day a great multitude*[^p414-2]*, when they had heard that Jesus was coming to Jerusalem,* met him with joy; in sign of which:

18. (V. 13.). *They took branches of palm trees and went forth to meet him;* this custom they had from the Law; whence it is said in second Esdras, the eighth[^p414-3]: « Go forth into the mount, and fetch branches of olive, and branches of most beautiful wood, and branches of palm ». — It is described also as to *the mode of praising;* on account of which he says: *And they cried: Hosanna;* as the Gloss says[^p414-4], « this word is of one beseeching, indicating an affection rather than anything, as interjections do », and it is worth as much as *save, I beseech. Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord, the king of Israel;* in which they confess in him a royal dignity, not from man, but from God; therefore they say: *the king of Israel in the name of the Lord,* according to that of Luke, the first[^p414-5]: « He shall be great, and shall be called the Son of the most High; and the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of David his father; and he shall reign in the house of Jacob for ever ». — It is described also as to *the mode of proceeding;* because he did not proceed on foot, but sat upon an ass; on account of which he says:

19. (V. 14.). *And Jesus found a young ass, and sat upon it.* Although he was true King and allowed royal honour to be offered to himself; yet he came not in a multitude of horses, but upon an ass. In which is signified, as Augustine says[^p414-6], that « not for exacting tribute, not for arming an army with iron was he king, but king of Israel, who should rule minds, who should counsel, who should lead into the kingdom of heaven those believing, hoping and loving ».

20. *As it is written.* Here is touched the second, namely, the *approbation* of that deed *through Scripture,* and he does this by showing that it was foretold in Scripture; because this gives strength to the true faith. Therefore he says that he sat upon an ass in royal honour; lest you should think this done by chance, he therefore says: *As it is written,* Zacharias, the ninth[^p414-7]:

21. (V. 15.). *Fear not, daughter of Sion,* that is, Church; he says less and understands more, that is, *exult;* Zacharias, the ninth[^p414-8]: « Exult greatly, O daughter of Sion; shout for joy, O daughter of Jerusalem ». And the reason for the exultation is given: *Behold, thy king cometh to thee*[^p414-9]*;* behold, *the royal dignity; sitting on an ass's colt;* behold, *meekness and humility.* And at the coming of such a one there must be rejoicing, because it is said in Proverbs, the sixteenth: « In the cheerfulness of the king's countenance is life, and his clemency is like the latter rain ». And although this honour was foretold, yet it was not foreknown by the disciples of Christ, that you may know this was not done by human invention, but by divine dispensation. Therefore he adds:

22. (V. 16.). *These things his disciples did not know at the first,* namely, when they conferred this honour; *but when Jesus was glorified,* after the resurrection and ascension, *then they remembered,* that is, they adverted, *that these things were written of him; and that they had done these things to him.* After the glorification they knew, not before; because then for the first time, as is said in Luke, the last[^p414-10], « he opened their understanding, that they might understand the Scriptures ».

23. (V. 17.). *The multitude therefore gave testimony* etc. Here is touched the third, namely, *the reason for the honour shown;* and this was the wonderful raising itself of Lazarus, which was evident to the multitudes; on account of which he says: *The multitude therefore gave testimony to him*[^p414-11]*, which was*
<!-- page 415 -->
*with him, when he called Lazarus out of the grave,* saying: « Lazarus, come forth », *and raised him from the dead.* That multitude gave testimony to his omnipotence, namely, a multitude *of the simple* and also a multitude *of the wise: of the wise,* Acts, the tenth[^p415-1]: « To him all the prophets give testimony »; *of the simple,* the Psalm: « Out of the mouth of infants and of sucklings thou hast perfected praise ». On account of which sign and testimony this honour was conferred on him; therefore he says:

24. (V. 18.). *For which reason*[^p415-2] *the multitude came to meet him, because they heard that he had done this sign.* The simple multitude, seeing signs, was moved to faith and devotion; on account of which it is said in Matthew, the ninth: « The multitudes seeing, feared, and glorified God that gave such power to men »; but the scribes said that he blasphemed.

25. (V. 19.). *The Pharisees therefore said among themselves.* Here is touched the fourth, namely, *the obstinacy of the Pharisees from envy;* therefore they said among themselves: *See, that we prevail nothing*[^p415-3], namely, as long as he lives; the second to Timothy, the third: « They resist the truth, men corrupted in mind, reprobate concerning the faith, but they shall prevail no farther ». And afterwards: *Behold, the whole world is gone after him.* In which it is noted that they were moved by the dart of envy; whence it is said in Matthew, the twenty-first[^p415-4], that the scribes and Pharisees, seeing, « were moved with indignation », because from *envy* they grieved, from *pride* they despised, from very *anger* they raged; and therefore they were obstinate in malice; « by the envy of the devil, death came into the world » etc., Wisdom, the second[^p415-5]; Ecclesiasticus, the fourteenth: « An evil eye shall be in sadness at his own table ».

### Questions.

26. Quaest. I. But it is asked here: because it seems that the Lord in his works was contrary to himself:

1. Because above, the eleventh[^p415-6], it is said that the Lord *hid himself* from the face of the persecuting Jews; but here he so manifestly offers himself.

2. Again, the Lord, above, the sixth[^p415-7], *fled into the mountain,* when they wished to make him king; here he receives the honour conferred on him as on a king, and approves it.

**I respond:** It must be said that the Lord, as Augustine says[^p415-8], does not do *contrary things,* but according to congruity does *diverse things,* as he first put forth the old and afterwards the new testament.

1. On account of which it must be said that the time of the passion had not yet come before, now it was at hand; therefore before he hid himself, because he was awaiting the time; now, when the opportunity and the time are at hand, he manifests himself[^p415-9].

2. Likewise to the second: because before the passion he wished to be unknown, but near and after the passion to be glorified; therefore he received royal honour then and not before[^p415-10]. — Another reason is, because these did not make him king, but recognized him as sent from God; but those wished to make him king. — The third reason is: because the Lord, as it were immediately after these things, was to suffer, he wished to be honoured, that from this honour the succeeding ignominy of the passion also might be increased[^p415-11].

27. Quaest. II. Again, it is asked concerning contrariety:

1. Because it is said in Matthew, the twenty-first[^p415-12], that first he sent into the town for the ass, then that the multitude came to meet him.
<!-- page 416 -->

2. Again, it is said in the same place[^p416-1] that he *sat upon the she-ass and the colt,* but here it is said that upon *a young ass.*

3. Again, this is not written in Zacharias[^p416-2]: *Fear not, daughter of Sion;* but: *Exult and shout for joy, daughter* etc.

**I respond:** It must be said to this last, that because the Evangelists spoke through the Holy Spirit, who composed the whole Scripture and is its author; therefore they did not set store by *the likeness of the words,* but by *the sincerity of the understandings;* and that very same thing which he said through the word *of exulting,* the Evangelist says through the word *of not fearing*[^p416-3].

1. 2. But as to what is objected concerning *contrariety* and *the reversal of order;* it must be said that the Lord sat upon the she-ass and also upon her colt. And some say that it was in this mode: first he sat on the *colt,* and because it kicked back, afterwards he sat upon the *she-ass*[^p416-4]. — But this is nothing, because I do not believe that the colt ever, with the Lord sitting on it, would have kicked back; rather it must be understood that the Lord on account of the figure first sat upon the *she-ass,* that is, upon the old synagogue, and afterwards upon the colt, that is, the untamed gentile people[^p416-5]. — And he sat upon the she-ass before the multitudes met him; therefore Matthew narrates it first; but afterwards, when the multitudes met him, then he sat upon the young ass. And therefore John[^p416-6] says after the coming of the multitudes: *Jesus found a young ass, and sat upon it;* not caring to say the whole, because another had said it.
