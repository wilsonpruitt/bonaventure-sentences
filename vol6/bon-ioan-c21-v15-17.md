# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XXI, Vers. 15-17
## *Hic describitur Petri in dilectione Christi confirmatio*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 524 -->

30. *Cum ergo prandissent, dicit* etc. Supra descripta est Christi resurgentis manifestatio, hic describitur *Petri in dilectione Christi confirmatio*[^p524-8]. Et quia quod confirmatur ad perfectionem tendit, ideo pars ista in duas dividitur. Primo enim Petrus in *dilectione Christi solidatur;* secundo, *ad consummationem dilectionis,* quae est per passionem, *invitatur,* ibi[^p524-9]: *Amen, amen dico tibi* etc.

Confirmatio igitur Petri in dilectione fit Christi trina interrogatione; unde sunt tres partes in prima parte secundum tres interrogationes: *prima,* ibi[^p524-10]: *Cum ergo prandissent; secunda,* ibi: *Dicit ei iterum: Simon* etc.; *tertia,* ibi: *Dicit ei tertio: Simon* etc.

*Prima* igitur[^p524-11] hoc ordine describitur: nam ponitur *Christi inquisitio;* secundo, *Petri responsio* et *Domini* additur *exhortatio.* — *Inquisitio Christi*
<!-- page 525 -->
facta est per manifestationem, quae fuit per comestionem; ideo dicit:

31. (Vers. 15.). *Cum ergo prandissent.* Attendendum est, quod in prandio non quaesivit de dilectione, quia, sicut dicitur Ecclesiastici sexto[^p525-1], « est amicus socius mensae, et non permanebit in die necessitatis ». Sed post quaerit: *Dicit Simoni Petro Iesus: Simon Ioannis, diligis me plus his?* Ideo de *dilectione* quaerit, quia gregem suum non vult nisi amanti committere; ideo de *maiori* dilectione, quia magis amanti vult committere. Unde Gregorius[^p525-2]: « Qui caritatem non habet praedicationis officium suscipere nequaquam debet »; certe multo minus praelationis, quia, sicut dicitur Ioannis decimo, « bonus pastor animam suam dat pro ovibus suis ».

32. *Dicit ei: Etiam, Domine.* Hic ponitur *Petri responsio,* in qua respondet, quod *amat,* sed non, quod *plus amet,* quia amorem aliorum nesciebat. Unde Augustinus[^p525-3]: « Quod respondet Petrus *amo te* nec addidit *plus his;* hoc respondit, quod de se ipso sciebat; non enim, quantum ab alio diligeretur, scire poterat, quia cor alterius videre non poterat ». *Tu scis, quia amo te;* in hac responsione ostendit Petrus, non se respondere adulatorie, sed vere. Unde Chrysostomus[^p525-4]: « Ipsum vocat testem, qui scit occulta ». Sic invocabat Ieremias, decimo septimo: « Diem hominis non desideravi, tu scis ».

33. *Dicit ei.* Hic notatur post responsionem *ad operationem exhortatio;* quia « probatio dilectionis exhibitio est operis[^p525-5] »; ideo dicit: *Pasce agnos meos;* in quo mira dilectio Christi ad oves exprimitur, quia hoc praecipue inter alia signum suae dilectionis ostendit; et hoc quidem Petrus implevit et impleri monuit, primae Petri quinto[^p525-6]: « Seniores, pascite qui in vobis est gregem Dei » etc.

34. (Vers. 16.). *Dicit ei iterum.* Hic ponitur *secunda* inquisitio, in qua similiter proceditur, ut prius. Ponitur enim *Christi inquisitio;* secundo, *Petri responsio;* et tertio, *exhortatio.*

*Inquirit* ergo Dominus, ut prius, de dilectione: *Simon Ioannis, diligis me? Simon Ioannis* dicitur, quia filius erat Ioanna[^p525-7]. — Et *respondet* Petrus: *Ait illi: Etiam, Domine, tu scis, quia amo te.* Beda[^p525-8]: « O quam fidelis et pura anima! quae Conditori suo, cuius oculis omnia nuda et aperta sunt, dicere non metuit: *Domine, tu scis, quia amo te* ». — *Dicit ei.* Hic ponitur *exhortatio,* ut prius: *Pasce agnos meos.* Exhortationem praedictam iterat; unde Beda[^p525-9]: « Haec est sola et vera probatio integri in Deum amoris, si erga fratres studueris curam solliciti exercere laboris ». *Pasce,* scilicet exemplo, sicut ille, de quo in Psalmo: « Pavit eos in innocentia cordis sui ».

35. (Vers. 17.). *Dicit ei tertio.* Hic ponitur *tertia* inquisitio illo ordine, quo prius, scilicet *quaestio, responsio, exhortatio.*

*Quaerit* ergo Dominus de eodem: *Simon Ioannis, amas me?* Ex hac littera patet, quod *amare* accipitur in bono, ut *diligere*[^p525-10]. — Et *contristatus est Petrus.* Hic notatur *responsio Petri;* sed tamen respondit *cum tristitia,* quia, sicut dicit Chrysostomus[^p525-11], timebat, ne similiter ei accideret, sicut accidit, quando dixit: « Animam meam pono pro te »; et ideo contristatus est, *quia dixit ei tertio: Amas me?* Sed tamen non desperavit, immo fiducialiter respondit et *dixit ei;* unde responsioni addit: *Domine, tu omnia nosti,* tanquam Deus; *tu scis, quia amo te,* etiam melius nosti quam ego. Sciebat enim, quod ille erat, qui dixit Ieremiae decimo septimo[^p525-12]: « Ego Dominus scrutans corda et renes », id est cogitationes et affectiones. — *Dicit ei: Pasce oves meas.* Hic notatur *exhortatio;* et per *oves* intelliguntur subditi perfectiores[^p525-13].

Notandum est, quod Dominus praecipit *pascere oves,* non tondere, non comedere, quia pastor non
<!-- page 526 -->
se ipsum pascere praecipitur amore Christi, sed oves; Ezechielis trigesimo quarto[^p526-1]: « Vae pastoribus Israel! qui pascebant semetipsos; nonne oves pascuntur a pastoribus? Lac comedebatis et lanis operiebamini ».

### Quaestiones.

36. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic: cum alii Apostoli circumstarent, qui Dominum diligebant; propter quid a solo Petro quaesitum est de dilectione[^p526-2]?

Responsio una est: quia sibi erat cura gregis specialiter committenda inter alios, ideo ab eo specialiter de dilectione quaeritur. Unde Chrysostomus[^p526-3]: « Eximius Apostolorum erat Petrus et os discipulorum et vertex collegii »; propterea, alios praeteriens et transcurrens, huic de talibus loquitur. — Potest etiam alia ratio assignari: quia Petrus eum notabiliter negaverat, et ideo videbatur non esse dignus, ut ceteris Apostolis praemitteretur; ideo Dominus quaerit, ut ostendat eius caritatem prae ceteris excellentem, et pastorale officium, quod dederat, confirmaret[^p526-4].

37. Quaest. II. Item, cum Petrus non posset ad hanc quaestionem respondere: *Diligis me plus his?* quia de aliis nesciebat[^p526-5]; quomodo Dominus quaerit?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod hoc Dominus quaerit, ut ostendat eius *excellentiam* in quaerendo et *correctionem* in respondendo; unde Augustinus[^p526-6]: « Quod Petrus plus aliis Dominum dilexerit, hoc evidenter apparet, ubi eum interrogans ait: *Diligis me plus his?* Quod utique sciebat, et tamen interrogabat, ut et nos *amorem* eius erga Dominum nossemus ». Et Petri *correctio* ostenditur in hoc, quod non audet se aliis praemittere, ut supra ante passionem, cum dixit[^p526-7]: « Etsi omnes scandalizati fuerint in te, sed non ego ».

38. Sed tunc est quaestio: quare Dominus toties quaerit: Petre, *diligis me?*

Respondet Augustinus[^p526-8]: « Quod ideo ter quaerit, ut reddatur trinae negationi trina confessio, ne minus amori lingua serviat quam timori ». — Alia ratio redditur, quia ideo quaerebat, ut gregem suum commendaret; et quia ter gregem commendare volebat, ideo ter de dilectione requirit[^p526-9].

39. Sed tunc est quaestio: quare dicit ei ter: *Pasce?*

Et respondetur, quod oves Christi tripliciter debent pasci, scilicet *verbo, exemplo* et *temporali subsidio;* unde Beda[^p526-10]: « Sollicitudo pastoralis officii exigit, ut rector Ecclesiae, ne plebi temporalia desint subsidia, diligenter provideat et eidem exempla virtutis ostendat eique verbum praedicationis sollicitus impendat ».

40. Sed tunc quaeritur: quare bis dicit pascendos *agnos* et semel *oves?*

**Respondeo**, quod per *agnos* imperfecti et tenelli in fide intelliguntur, et hi ampliori pastura, hoc est cura sollicitiore, indigent; per *oves* vero grandiusculi, qui se valent regere, et hi non tanta cura indigent[^p526-11].

## English
<!-- page 524 -->

30. *When therefore they had dined, he says* etc. Above there was described the manifestation of Christ rising again; here there is described *the confirmation of Peter in the dilection of Christ*[^p524-8]. And because what is confirmed tends to perfection, therefore this part is divided in two. For first Peter is *made solid in the dilection of Christ;* second, he is *invited to the consummation of dilection,* which is through the passion, there[^p524-9]: *Amen, amen I say to thee* etc.

The confirmation of Peter in dilection, then, comes about by the threefold interrogation of Christ; hence there are three parts in the first part, according to the three interrogations: *the first,* there[^p524-10]: *When therefore they had dined; the second,* there: *He saith to him again: Simon* etc.; *the third,* there: *He saith to him the third time: Simon* etc.

*The first,* then,[^p524-11] is described in this order: for there is set down *the inquiry of Christ;* second, *the response of Peter,* and there is added the *exhortation* of *the Lord.* — *The inquiry of Christ*
<!-- page 525 -->
was made through the manifestation which was through eating; therefore he says:

31. (Vers. 15.). *When therefore they had dined.* It is to be attended to that at the dinner he did not ask about dilection, because, as is said in Ecclesiasticus, the sixth chapter[^p525-1], « there is a friend a companion at the table, and he will not abide in the day of distress ». But afterwards he asks: *Jesus saith to Simon Peter: Simon son of John, lovest thou me more than these?* Therefore he asks about *dilection,* because he wills to commit his flock only to one who loves; therefore about *greater* dilection, because he wills to commit it to one who loves more. Hence Gregory[^p525-2]: « He who has not charity ought by no means to undertake the office of preaching »; certainly much less that of prelacy, because, as is said in John, the tenth chapter, « the good shepherd giveth his life for his sheep ».

32. *He saith to him: Yea, Lord.* Here is set down *the response of Peter,* in which he responds that he *loves,* but not that he *loves more,* because he did not know the love of the others. Hence Augustine[^p525-3]: « That Peter responds *I love thee* and did not add *more than these:* he responded this, which he knew about himself; for how much he was loved by another he could not know, because he could not see the heart of another ». *Thou knowest that I love thee;* in this response Peter shows that he responds not flatteringly, but truly. Hence Chrysostom[^p525-4]: « He calls him as witness who knows hidden things ». So Jeremias called upon him, the seventeenth chapter: « The day of man I have not desired, thou knowest ».

33. *He saith to him.* Here there is noted, after the response, *the exhortation to working;* because « the proof of dilection is the exhibition of the work[^p525-5] »; therefore he says: *Feed my lambs;* in which the wonderful dilection of Christ toward the sheep is expressed, because this especially among other things he shows as a sign of his dilection; and this indeed Peter fulfilled and admonished to be fulfilled, the first of Peter, the fifth chapter[^p525-6]: « Ancients, feed the flock of God which is among you » etc.

34. (Vers. 16.). *He saith to him again.* Here is set down *the second* inquiry, in which one proceeds similarly, as before. For there is set down *the inquiry of Christ;* second, *the response of Peter;* and third, *the exhortation.*

The Lord then *inquires,* as before, about dilection: *Simon son of John, lovest thou me? Simon son of John* he is called, because he was the son of Joanna[^p525-7]. — And Peter *responds: He saith to him: Yea, Lord, thou knowest that I love thee.* Bede[^p525-8]: « O how faithful and pure a soul! which did not fear to say to its Creator, to whose eyes all things are naked and open: *Lord, thou knowest that I love thee* ». — *He saith to him.* Here is set down *the exhortation,* as before: *Feed my lambs.* He repeats the aforesaid exhortation; hence Bede[^p525-9]: « This is the sole and true proof of entire love toward God, if toward the brethren you study to exercise the care of solicitous labour ». *Feed,* namely by example, as he of whom in the Psalm: « He fed them in the innocence of his heart ».

35. (Vers. 17.). *He saith to him the third time.* Here is set down *the third* inquiry, in that order in which before, namely *question, response, exhortation.*

The Lord then *asks* about the same: *Simon son of John, lovest thou me?*[^tr-dilectio] From this letter it is plain that *to love* (*amare*) is taken in the good sense, as *to love* (*diligere*) is[^p525-10]. — And *Peter was grieved.* Here is noted *the response of Peter;* but yet he responded *with sadness,* because, as Chrysostom says[^p525-11], he feared lest it should befall him similarly as it befell when he said: « I lay down my life for thee »; and therefore he was grieved, *because he had said to him the third time: Lovest thou me?* But yet he did not despair, rather he responded confidently and *said to him;* hence he adds to the response: *Lord, thou knowest all things,* as God; *thou knowest that I love thee,* even better thou knowest it than I. For he knew that he was the one who said, in Jeremias, the seventeenth chapter[^p525-12]: « I the Lord search the hearts and the reins », that is, the thoughts and the affections. — *He saith to him: Feed my sheep.* Here is noted *the exhortation;* and by *the sheep* are understood the more perfect subjects[^p525-13].

It is to be noted that the Lord commands *to feed the sheep,* not to shear them, not to eat them, because the shepherd is not commanded
<!-- page 526 -->
to feed himself for the love of Christ, but the sheep; Ezechiel, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p526-1]: « Woe to the shepherds of Israel, that fed themselves: are not the sheep fed by the shepherds? You ate the milk, and you clothed yourselves with the wool ».

### Questions.

36. Quaest. I. But it is asked here: since the other Apostles were standing around, who loved the Lord, for what reason was it asked of Peter alone about dilection[^p526-2]?

There is one response: because the care of the flock was to be committed to him specially among the others, therefore it is asked of him specially about dilection. Hence Chrysostom[^p526-3]: « Peter was the eminent one of the Apostles and the mouth of the disciples and the head of the college »; therefore, passing over and running past the others, he speaks of such things to him. — Another reason too can be assigned: because Peter had notably denied him, and therefore he seemed not to be worthy to be put before the other Apostles; therefore the Lord asks, that he might show his charity excelling before the others, and might confirm the pastoral office which he had given[^p526-4].

37. Quaest. II. Likewise, since Peter could not respond to this question: *Lovest thou me more than these?* because he did not know about the others[^p526-5]; how does the Lord ask it?

**I respond:** It must be said that the Lord asks this, that he might show his *excellence* in the asking and his *correction* in the responding; hence Augustine[^p526-6]: « That Peter loved the Lord more than the others is evidently apparent where, questioning him, he says: *Lovest thou me more than these?* Which surely he knew, and yet he questioned, that we too might know his *love* toward the Lord ». And the *correction* of Peter is shown in this, that he does not dare to put himself before the others, as above before the passion, when he said[^p526-7]: « Although all shall be scandalized in thee, yet not I ».

38. But then there is the question: why does the Lord ask so many times: Peter, *lovest thou me?*

Augustine responds[^p526-8]: « That he therefore asks three times, that to the threefold denial a threefold confession may be rendered, lest the tongue serve love less than fear ». — Another reason is given: because he was therefore asking, that he might commend his flock; and because he willed to commend the flock three times, therefore three times he inquires about dilection[^p526-9].

39. But then there is the question: why does he say to him three times: *Feed?*

And it is responded that the sheep of Christ ought to be fed in three ways, namely *by word, by example* and *by temporal aid;* hence Bede[^p526-10]: « The solicitude of the pastoral office requires that the ruler of the Church diligently provide lest temporal aids be lacking to the people, and show it examples of virtue, and solicitously bestow on it the word of preaching ».

40. But then it is asked: why does he say twice that the *lambs* are to be fed, and once the *sheep?*

**I respond** that by the *lambs* are understood the imperfect and the tender in faith, and these need a more ample pasture, that is, a more solicitous care; but by the *sheep* the somewhat grown, who are able to rule themselves, and these do not need so much care[^p526-11].
