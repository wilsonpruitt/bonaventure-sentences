### Capitulum VIII. *Iesus perrexit in montem.*

### Collatio XXXIII.

(Vers. 10.). *Mulier, ubi sunt qui te accusabant?*

1. Mirabilis est prorsus misericordia Salvatoris in absolutione huius adulterae, quia primo *repellit accusationem;* quod ostendit hic se fecisse, cum dicit: *Ubi sunt qui te accusabant?* — Secundo, *suspendit condemnationem,* cum dicit[^p573-5]: *Nec ego te condemnabo.* — Tertio, *tribuit remissionem,* cum dicit: *Vade,* quasi libera et absoluta. — Quarto, *adiungit instructionem,* cum dicit: *Noli peccare.*

2. Ostendens ergo adulteram *ab accusatione liberatam,* ait: *Mulier, ubi sunt qui te accusabant?* In quibus verbis duo tangit: *praesentem accusatorum dispersionem,* cum eos quasi non existentes requirit: *Ubi sunt?* — Secundo, *praeteritam eorundem accusationem,* cum dicit: *Qui te accusabant.*

Ad intelligendum autem, qualiter Christus liberat animam ab accusatoribus suis, notandum, quod animam spiritualiter adulterantem quatuor accusant: accusat *conscientia,* accusat *fama,* accusat *lex divina,* accusat *diabolica malitia.* — Accusat *conscientia;* ad Romanos secundo[^p573-6]: « Testimonium illis reddente conscientia, et inter se invicem cogitationum accusantibus aut defendentibus » etc.; iudicabuntur, supple. — Accusat *fama;* quocontra de Samuele dicitur Ecclesiastici quadragesimo sexto: « Non accusavit illum homo »; primae ad Timotheum quinto: « Adversum presbyterum noli accusationem accipere nisi sub duobus aut tribus testibus ». — Accusat *lex divina;* Ioannis quinto[^p573-7]: « Est qui accusat vos, Moyses, in quo speratis ». — Accusat *diabolica malitia.* Diabolus ipse est, qui « accusat fratres die ac nocte », Apocalypsis duodecimo.

3. Ab istis accusationibus liberat *Christi misericordia.* Ab accusatione *conscientiae* liberat *culpam dimittendo.* Dimissa enim culpa, non habet de quo clamet conscientia; sicut liberavit publicanum, Lucae decimo octavo[^p573-8], qui coram Domino accusavit se prius, secundum illud Proverbiorum decimo octavo: « Iustus in principio accusator est sui »; et sequitur, quia « descendit iustificatus in domum suam ». — Ab accusatione *famae* liberat *infamatores humiliando,* quemadmodum, Ioannis octavo[^p573-9], humiliavit infamatores adulterae dicens: « Qui sine peccato est mittat in eam lapidem »; Lucae septimo, infamatorem Magdalenae; propter quod dicitur ad Romanos octavo: « Quis accusabit adversus electos Dei? Si Deus pro nobis, quis contra nos »? — Ab accusatione
<!-- page 574 -->
*legis divinae* liberat causam in *forum misericordiae deducendo.* Accusat enim lex divina secundum *forum iustitiae,* sed cessat rigor iustitiae, cum Deus absolvit in *foro misericordiae.* Sic liberavit adulteram istam, quam Iudaei accusabant secundum Legem dicentes[^p574-1]: « Moyses praecepit huiusmodi lapidare ». Christus autem, convictis accusatoribus, deduxit causam in *forum misericordiae* dicens: *Nec ego te condemnabo.* — Ab accusatione *diabolicae malitiae* liberat *diabolum prosternendo.* Diabolus enim ex malignitate accusat, et ideo est magis prosternendus quam audiendus; unde Apocalypsis duodecimo[^p574-2] laudatur Dominus, « quia proiectus est accusator fratrum nostrorum, qui accusabat eos ante conspectum Dei die ac nocte ».

### Collatio XXXIV.

(Vers. 26.). *Qui misit me verax est.*

1. Veneranda est missio Salvatoris propter tria. Solet enim venerari donum vel propter *excellentiam dantis,* vel propter *excellentiam muneris,* vel propter *indigentiam recipientis.* — Propter hanc triplicem rationem veneranda est missio Salvatoris. Magnus est *qui mittit,* Deus Pater; Ioannis octavo[^p574-4]: « Solus non sum, sed ego et qui misit me, Pater ». — Item, magnus est *qui mittitur,* scilicet Dei Filius; ad Galatas quarto: « Misit Deus Filium suum factum ex muliere, factum sub Lege ». — Item, indigebant eo *quibus mittitur;* unde Genesis penultimo: « Non auferetur sceptrum de Iuda, nec dux de femore eius, donec veniat qui mittendus est, et ipse erit exspectatio gentium ».

2. Et revera ipse fuit *exspectatio gentium,* quia ipse fuit missus *omnibus* in *remedium.* Missus enim fuit *poenitentibus,* ut relevarentur a culpa — *patientibus,* ut relevarentur a poena — *proficientibus,* ut promoverentur in iustitia — *contemplantibus,* ut sublimarentur in gratiae experientia.

3. Missus est ergo *poenitentibus,* ut *relevarentur a culpa.* In *culpa* autem est considerare tria: *maculam, reatum* et *offensam;* et ideo mittitur Christus ad *iustificandum,* ad *redimendum,* ad *reconciliandum.* — Mittitur ad *iustificandum* a culpa; ad Romanos octavo[^p574-5]: « Misit Deus Filium suum in
similitudinem carnis peccati, et de peccato damnavit peccatum in carne, ut iustificatio Legis impleretur in nobis ». — Mittitur ad *redimendum* a poena; ipse est enim, « qui redemit de interitu vitam[^p574-6] »; ad Galatas quarto: « At ubi venit plenitudo temporis, misit Deus Filium suum factum ex muliere, factum sub Lege, ut eos qui sub Lege erant, redimeret »; Psalmus: « Redemptionem misit Dominus populo suo ». — Mittitur ad *reconciliandum* contra offensam; primae Ioannis quarto: « In hoc est caritas, non quasi nos dilexerimus eum, sed quoniam ipse prior dilexit nos et misit Filium suum propitiationem pro peccatis nostris ».

4. Missus est ad *patientes,* ut eos relevaret a *poena;* quod fit tribus modis: *eripiendo, sanando* et *custodiendo: eripiendo* a captivitate, *sanando* ab infirmitate, *custodiendo* ab utroque. — Mittitur ergo ad *eripiendum;* Psalmus[^p574-7]: « Emitte manum tuam de alto; eripe me et libera me de aquis multis ». — Mittitur ad *sanandum;* Psalmus: « Misit manum suam et sanavit eos ». — Mittitur ad *custodiendum;* Ioannis sexto: « Haec est voluntas eius qui misit me Patris, ut omne, quod dedit mihi, non perdam ex eo ».

5. Mittitur *activis* ad *promovendum* in iustitia, quod facit tripliciter: *docendo, excitando* et *deducendo: docendo intellectum,* ut sciant; *excitando affectum,* ut velint; *deducendo effectum,* ut valeant. — Mittitur ergo ad *docendum;* Sapientiae nono[^p574-8]: « Mitte illam », scilicet sapientiam, « de caelis sanctis tuis, ut mecum sit et mecum laboret, ut sciam, quid acceptum sit apud te ». — Mittitur ad *excitandum;* Lucae decimo quarto: « Misit servum suum hora coenae dicere invitatis, ut venirent »; et Proverbiorum primo: « Sapientia foris praedicat, in plateis dat vocem suam ». — Mittitur ad *deducendum;* Malachiae tertio: « Ecce, ego mittam angelum meum, et praeparabit viam ante faciem tuam ».

6. Mittitur *contemplantibus* ad *sublimandum* in experientia gratiae; quod facit tribus modis: *revelando, visitando* et *certificando: revelando* secreta mysteriorum; *visitando* fruitione spiritualium gaudiorum; *certificando* de adeptione praemiorum. — Mittitur ergo ad *revelandum;* Ezechielis secundo[^p574-9]: « Vidi, et ecce, manus missa ad me, in qua erat involutus liber, et expandit illum coram me ». — Mittitur ad *visitandum;* Genesis trigesimo septimo: « Veni, mittam te ad fratres tuos. Vade et vide, si cuncta prospera sint erga fratres tuos ». — Mittitur ad *certificandum;* Isaiae sexagesimo pri-
<!-- page 575 -->
mo: « Ad annuntiandum mansuetis misit me, ut mederer contritis corde et praedicarem annum placabilem Domino ».

### Annotatio 34.

(Vers. 35.). *Servus non manet in domo in aeternum; filius autem manet in aeternum.*

Nota hic multiplicem servitutem. Est enim servitus *naturalis,* servitus *legalis,* servitus *spiritualis,* servitus *carnalis* et servitus *aeternalis.* — Servitus *naturalis sustinenda* est; unde ad Ephesios sexto[^p575-1]: « Servi, obedite dominis carnalibus cum omni timore et tremore » etc. De ista dicitur a Iudaeis Ioannis octavo: « Nemini servivimus unquam ». — Servitus *legalis* est *evacuanda;* ad Galatas quarto: « Quomodo convertimini iterum ad infirma et egena mundi elementa, quibus denuo servire vultis »? et quinto: « State et nolite iterum iugo servitutis contineri ». — Servitus *spiritualis* est *amplectenda;* ad Romanos septimo[^p575-2]: « Mente servio legi Dei »; et ad Philippenses tertio: « Spiritu Deo servimus et gloriamur in Christo Iesu »; et ad Galatas quinto: « Per caritatem servite invicem ». — Servitus *carnalis* est *abominanda,* de qua Ioannis octavo[^p575-3]: « Qui facit peccatum servus est peccati »;
et iterum: *Servus non manet in domo in aeternum.* — Servitus *aeternalis* est *horrenda;* haec est, quae continuatur a culpa in poenam, secundum illud Matthaei decimo octavo: « Serve nequam, omne debitum dimisi tibi » etc.; et sequitur, quod « iussit, eum proiici in tenebras exteriores ».

### Collatio XXXV.

(Vers. 36.). *Si ergo Filius Dei vos liberaverit, vere liberi eritis.*

1. Nota secundum multiplicem differentiam *servitutis* multiplicem differentiam *libertatis.* — Est enim libertas *naturae* contra servitutem *naturalem;* de qua Iob tertio[^p575-4]: « Parvus et magnus ibi sunt et servus et liber a domino suo ». Exemplum de servo, qui, imminente periculo maris, arridebat domino suo dicens, quia hodie essent pares et biberent ambo in cipho uno. — Est libertas *fidei* contra servitutem *legalem;* de qua ad Galatas quinto: « In libertatem vocati estis, tantum ne libertatem in occasionem
detis carnis ». — Est libertas *culpae* contra servitutem *spiritualem;* de qua Iob undecimo[^p575-5]: « Vir vanus in superbiam erigitur, et tanquam pullum onagri se liberum natum putat »; et ad Romanos sexto: « Cum servi essetis peccati, liberi fuistis iustitiae ». — Est libertas *gratiae* contra servitutem *carnalem;* secundae ad Corinthios tertio: « Ubi Spiritus Domini, ibi libertas »; et ad Galatas quarto: « Non sumus ancillae filii, sed liberae ». — Est libertas *gloriae* contra servitutem *aeternalem;* ad Romanos octavo: « Ipsa creatura liberabitur a servitute corruptionis in libertatem gloriae filiorum Dei ».

2. *Servus non manet in domo in aeternum.* Notandum, quod est servitus *mala* — et servitus *bona.* — Servitus *mala* triplex. Quidam enim serviunt *carni;* de quibus ad Romanos decimo sexto[^p575-6]: « Huiusmodi non serviunt Deo, sed ventri suo »; ad Philippenses tertio: « Quia multi ambulant, quos saepe dicebam vobis, nunc autem et flens dico » etc. De hac servitute ait Seneca: « Multis serviet qui corpori servit »; et idem: « Maior sum et ad maiora genitus, quam ut mancipium corporis mei sim ». — Quidam autem serviunt *mundo,* quibus dicitur Lucae decimo sexto[^p575-7]: « Nemo potest duobus dominis servire »; et iterum: « Non potestis Deo servire et mammonae ». — Quidam serviunt *peccato;* Ioan-
nis octavo: « Qui facit peccatum servus est peccati »; secundae Petri secundo: « A quo quis superatus est, huius et servus est ».

3. Servitus haec mala est, quia *laboriosa* est; Ieremiae decimo sexto[^p575-8]: « Servietis diis alienis, qui non dabunt vobis requiem die ac nocte ». — Item, quia *ignominiosa;* Exodi primo: « De servitute luti et lateris ». Nota et expone. — Item, quia *infructuosa;* ad Romanos sexto: « Cum servi essetis peccati, liberi fuistis iustitiae; quem ergo fructum habuistis tunc in illis, in quibus nunc erubescitis »?

4. Est servitus *bona* triplex. Debemus enim *Deo* servire; Psalmus[^p575-9]: « Servite Domino in timore ». — Debemus *praelato* servire; primae ad Timotheum sexto: « Qui habent fideles dominos non contemnant, quia fratres sunt, sed magis serviant, quia fideles sunt ». — Debemus etiam servire *proximo;* ad Galatas quinto: « Per caritatem spiritus servite invicem ».

5. De servitute *Dei* nota, *in quibus* serviendum Deo — *qualiter* — et *propter quid.* — Nota igitur primo differentias servitiorum et attende,
<!-- page 576 -->
quod quidam serviunt Deo in *negotio actionis,* ut illi servi, quos vocavit Dominus, Lucae decimo nono[^p576-1], et « dedit eis decem mnas et ait eis: Negotiamini, dum venio ». — Quidam serviunt Deo in *proelio passionis,* sicut illi, de quibus in Psalmo dicitur: « Effuderunt sanguinem servorum tuorum ». Ad hoc servitium armabat suos Dominus, Ioannis decimo quinto, dicens: « Non est servus maior domino suo. Si me persecuti sunt, et vos persequentur ». — Quidam serviunt Deo in *thalamo contemplationis;* de quibus in figura servorum Salomonis, tertii Regum decimo[^p576-2]: « Beati servi tui, qui stant coram te semper et audiunt sapientiam tuam ». — Quidam serviunt Deo in *ministerio praelationis,* quales describens Dominus ait Matthaei vigesimo quarto: « Quis, putas, est fidelis servus et prudens, quem constituit Dominus super familiam tuam, ut det illis cibum in tempore »?

6. Nota postea, *qualiter* est Deo serviendum. Sciendum igitur, quod est serviendum *vigilanter* quantum ad *sollicitudinem rationis;* unde Lucae duodecimo[^p576-3]: « Beatus servus, quem, cum venerit Dominus, invenerit vigilantem ». — Est etiam serviendum Deo *fideliter* quantum ad *rectitudinem intentionis,* ut dicatur servo quod dicitur Matthaei vigesimo quinto: « Euge, serve bone et fidelis, quia in pauca
fuisti fidelis » etc. — Est etiam serviendum Deo *libenter* quantum ad *promptitudinem voluntatis,* secundum quod dicitur in Psalmo[^p576-4]: « Populus, quem non cognovi, servivit mihi, in auditu auris obedivit mihi ». — Serviendum est etiam Deo *perseveranter* quantum ad *continuationem operis;* Lucae primo: « Serviamus illi in sanctitate et iustitia coram ipso omnibus diebus nostris ».

7. Nota, *quare* est Deo serviendum. Sunt ergo tria, quae movent nos ad serviendum Deo. Movet nos *timor supplicii.* Comminatur enim supplicium male servienti; unde dicit Lucae duodecimo[^p576-5]: « Servus, qui cognovit voluntatem domini sui et non fecit secundum voluntatem eius, vapulabit multis ». — Movet nos *amor praemii;* ad Romanos sexto: « Nunc autem liberati a peccato, servi autem facti Deo, habetis fructum vestrum in sanctificationem, finem vero vitam aeternam ». — Movet nos *pudor opprobrii.*
Sequitur enim confusio non servientes Deo; unde ad Romanos sexto[^p576-6]: « Cum servi essetis peccati, liberi fuistis iustitiae. Quem ergo fructum habuistis in illis, in quibus nunc erubescitis »? — Movet etiam nos *honor officii;* unde super primum ad Romanos Glossa[^p576-7]: « Deo servire regnare est »; et Ecclesiastici vigesimo tertio: « Gloria magna est sequi Dominum »; unde et beata Agatha: « Illa est vera libertas, in qua Christi servitus comprobatur ».

8. Item nota, quod serviendum est Deo ratione *debiti,* ratione *commodi,* ratione *honesti:* ratione *debiti,* quia *conditor;* unde in Psalmo[^p576-8]: « Servite Domino in laetitia »; et postea subiungit causam: « Scitote, quoniam Dominus ipse est Deus; ipse fecit nos, et non ipsi nos ». — Quia *protector;* unde Lucae primo: « Ut sine timore de manu inimicorum nostrorum liberati, serviamus illi ». — Quia *sustentator;* haec est enim causa, quare quis alii debet servire, secundum quod dicebant Aegyptii Ioseph Genesis quadragesimo septimo: « Eme nos in servitutem regiam et praebe semina, ne, deficiente cultore, terra redigatur in solitudinem ».

9. Item, serviendum est ratione *commodi,* quia scilicet *remittit culpam;* unde in Psalmo[^p576-9]: « Iudicabit Dominus populum suum et in servis suis deprecabitur », id est, miserebitur. — *Immittit gratiam;* ad Romanos sexto: « Exhibeatis membra vestra servire iustitiae in sanctificationem »; et eodem: « Servi autem facti Deo, habetis fructum in sanctificationem », quae quidem non est nisi per gratiam. — Quia *promittit gloriam;* Isaiae sexagesimo quinto: « Servi mei comedent, et vos esurietis »; dicitur malis, et exponitur de refectione gloriae et esurie gehennae.

10. Item, serviendum est Deo ratione *honesti.* Honestum est enim servire Deo ratione *dominii,* quia magnus est; Psalmus[^p576-10]: « Omnia serviunt tibi ». — Honestum est etiam ratione *officii,* quia nobile est; unde Glossa super primum ad Romanos: « Servire Deo regnare est ». — Honestum etiam est ratione *servi,* quia ad hoc factus est; Isaiae quadragesimo nono: « Haec dicit Dominus, formans me ex utero servum sibi », id est ad serviendum sibi.

### Annotatio 35.

(Vers. 43.). *Quare loquelam meam non cognoscitis?*

1. Nota, quod Dominus loquitur nobis aliquando per *signa creaturae;* aliquando per *litteras Scripturae;* aliquando quasi *per nuntios,* et hoc in praedicatione; aliquando quasi *voce viva,* et hoc interna inspiratione. — Loquitur ergo per *signa creaturae;* Proverbiorum primo[^p577-1]: « Sapientia foris praedicat », id est in creaturis; unde Proverbiorum sexto: « Vade ad formicam, o piger, et considera vias eius ». — Loquitur per *litteras Scripturae;* unde Exodi vigesimo: « Locutus est Dominus cunctos sermones hos »; et Ieremiae trigesimo sexto: « Tolle volumen et scribe in eo omnia verba, quae locutus sum tibi adversus Israel et Iudam ». — Loquitur per *nuntios in praedicatione,* quibus dicitur Matthaei decimo[^p577-2]: « Non vos estis, qui loquimini, sed Spiritus Patris vestri, qui loquitur in vobis ». — Loquitur *voce viva* et interna inspiratione; unde in Psalmo: « Audiam, quid loquatur in me Dominus Deus »; et Osee secundo: « Ducam eam in solitudinem et loquar ad cor eius ». — Cum igitur Dominus loquatur *signis, litteris, nuntiis* et *verbis,* recte improperat indoctis: *Quare loquelam meam non cognoscitis?*

2. *Quia non potestis audire sermonem meum.* Nota, quod gravis fuit Christus malis ad *audiendum;* unde hic: *Quia non potestis* etc. Ad *videndum;* Sapientiae secundo[^p577-3]: « Gravis est nobis etiam ad videndum ». Ad *sustinendum;* Ioannis undecimo: « Quid facimus, quia hic homo multa signa facit? Si dimittimus eum sic, omnes credent in eum »; hoc dicebant Iudaei graviter ferentes Christum.

### Annotatio 36.

(Vers. 44.). *Vos ex patre diabolo estis et desideria patris vestri vultis facere.*

Nota, quod Scriptura detestatur tria genera desideriorum, scilicet desideria *carnalia,* de quibus primae Petri secundo[^p577-4]: « Obsecro vos, tanquam advenas et peregrinos abstinere vos a carnalibus desideriis » etc. — Item, desideria *saecularia;* ad Titum secundo: « Abnegantes impietatem et saecularia desideria, sobrie et iuste et pie vivamus in hoc saeculo ». — Item, desideria *diabolica,* de quibus hic: *Vos ex patre diabolo estis et desideria patris ve-
stri vultis facere.* — Desideria ista *detestanda* sunt, quia animam *impugnant;* primae Petri secundo[^p577-5]: « Obsecro vos abstinere a carnalibus desideriis, quae militant adversus animam ». Animam *cruciant;* Proverbiorum vigesimo primo: « Desideria occidunt pigrum ». Animam *captivant;* unde illud Psalmi: « Tradidit eos Deus in desideria cordis eorum; ibunt in adinventionibus suis ».

### Collatio XXXVI.

(Vers. 59.). *Iesus autem abscondit se et exivit de templo.*

1. In verbis istis tangitur duplex gradus subtrahendi gratiam malis. Dominus enim, cum a peccatore offenditur, primo aufert ei *gratiae sensum* sive experientiam, et tunc quasi *se abscondit,* cum spiritualiter non sentitur. — Secundo aufert *gratiae existentiam,* et tunc quasi *de templo exit,* cum per gratiam non habetur; quae duo significantur hic: primum, cum dicit: *Iesus abscondit se;* secundum, cum dicit: *Et exivit de templo.* — Domini igitur *absconsio* est consolationis spiritualis subtractio.

2. Unde nota, quod Dominus abscondit se aliquando, ut *deserat;* aliquando, ut *corripiat;* aliquando, ut *promoveat.* — Abscondit se Dominus a *malis* ad deserendum, et hoc propter illorum malignitatem; qualiter abscondit se Dominus a Iudaeis hic, cum *abscondit se et exivit de templo;* et Deuteronomii trigesimo secundo[^p577-6]: « Abscondam faciem meam ab eis et considerabo novissima eorum ». — Abscondit se ab *infirmis* sive carnalibus ad *corripiendum,* et hoc propter illorum carnalitatem; qualiter abscondit se a turbis, Ioannis sexto, quibus eum volentibus « facere regem, fugit in montem »; Iob vigesimo octavo: « Abscondita est sapientia ab oculis omnium viventium », supple: suaviter secundum carnem. — Abscondit se aliquando a *bonis,* ut illos *promoveat,* qualiter abscondit se a Magdalena, Ioannis vigesimo, quando praesens erat, sed se ipsum non ostendebat; latebat, sed non recedebat, quod fiebat propter Magdalenae utilitatem.

3. Absconditur autem ab *electis* propter triplicem utilitatem: primo, ut *corrigat culpam;* quod figuratum est, Genesis quadragesimo secundo[^p577-7], de Ioseph, qui praesens fratribus nolebat agnosci, ab eis exigens poenitentiam sive dolorem super venditione fratris. — Secundo, ut *humiliet mentem;* quod
<!-- page 578 -->
figuratum est, secundi Regum decimo quarto, de Absalom, qui, quamdiu faciem regis non vidit, humiliter se habuit; postquam vero vidit, superbe se gessit, unde « fecit sibi currus et equites » etc. — Tertio, ut *excitet caritatem;* qualiter abscondit se a discipulis suis euntibus in Emmaus, cum quibus ibat et eos latebat et in eis caritatem accendebat; unde Lucae ultimo[^p578-1]: « Nonne cor nostrum ardebat »? — Propter haec tria *abscondit se* Dominus a bonis, sed ab ipsis *non recedit;* a malis vero *abscondit se* et *de templo exit.*

4. *De templo* autem exit propter tria: quia templum *polluitur;* Psalmus[^p578-2]: « Polluerunt templum sanctum tuum ». — Quia templum *violatur;* primi Machabaeorum secundo: « Templum eius sicut homo ignobilis; vasa gloriae eius captiva abducta sunt ». — Quia in templo *lapidatur;* unde hic dicitur: *Tulerunt autem lapides* etc.; *Iesus autem abscondit se et exivit de templo.* — Templum *polluitur,* quando *admittit* homo malam *delectationem.* Templum *violatur,* quando homo *perpetrat* malam *operationem.* Iesus vero *lapidatur,* quando homo *impugnat correctionem;* et haec tria cogunt Iesum deserere hominem.

5. *Iesus exivit de templo.* — Nota, quae Christus in templo *gessit,* et quae Christus in templo *sustinuit.* Quae Christus in templo *gessit* informant praelatos in *agendis;* quae vero *sustinuit* Christus in templo informant eos in *damnis sustinendis.*

Nota ergo, quod Christus in templo *docuit,* in templo *corripuit,* in templo *absolvit.* — In templo
*docuit;* Ioannis decimo octavo[^p578-3]: « Ego semper docui in synagoga et in templo, in quo omnes Iudaei conveniunt ». — In templo *corripuit;* Ioannis secundo: « Cum fecisset flagellum de funiculis, omnes eiecit de templo ». — In templo vero *absolvit* adulteram; Ioannis octavo: « Diluculo venit in templum »; et sequitur: « Adducunt ei mulierem deprehensam in adulterio » etc. — Haec tria informant praelatos in *agendis,* ut sint in *praedicando docti,* in *corripiendo viriles,* in *remittendo benigni.* Praelatio enim sine *doctrina,* ut ait quidam sapiens[^p578-4], est quasi gladius in manu *stulti;* sine *virilitate,* quasi gladius in manu *pueri;* sine *clementia,* quasi gladius in manu *furibundi.*

6. Notanda sunt quae Christus in templo *sustinuit.* Fuit enim Christus in templo *tentatus,* in templo *blasphematus* et in templo *lapidatus.* — *Tentatus* fuit, Ioannis octavo[^p578-5], cum adduxerunt ei adulteram; unde sequitur: « Hoc autem dicebant tentantes eum ». — *Blasphematus* fuit in eodem, cum dicebant ei: « Nonne bene dicimus, quia Samaritanus es et daemonium habes »? — *Lapidatus;* unde in eodem: *Tulerunt lapides, ut iacerent in eum.*

7. *Iesus autem exivit de templo.* Sed nota, quod Christus tentatus *respondit,* blasphematus *sustinuit* et lapidatus *exivit;* et haec tria informant praelatos in sustinendis, ut non ex *indignatione taceant,* cum Christus *tentatus respondeat;* nec ex *superbia respondeant,* cum Christus *blasphematus sustineat;* nec per *violentiam se ingerant* vel vindicent, cum Christus *lapidatus iniuriae cedat.*

### Chapter VIII. *Jesus went unto the mount.*

### Collation XXXIII.

(Verse 10.). *Woman, where are they that accused thee?*

1. Altogether wonderful is the mercy of the Saviour in the absolution of this adulteress, because first he *repels the accusation;* which he shows here that he has done, when he says: *Where are they that accused thee?* — Secondly, he *suspends the condemnation,* when he says[^p573-5]: *Neither will I condemn thee.* — Thirdly, he *bestows remission,* when he says: *Go,* as one free and absolved. — Fourthly, he *adds instruction,* when he says: *Sin no more.*

2. Showing, then, the adulteress *freed from the accusation,* he says: *Woman, where are they that accused thee?* In which words he touches two things: *the present dispersion of the accusers,* when he asks after them as though they did not exist: *Where are they?* — Secondly, *their past accusation,* when he says: *That accused thee.*

But for understanding how Christ frees the soul from its accusers, it is to be noted that four accuse the soul that commits adultery spiritually: *conscience* accuses, *repute* accuses, *the divine law* accuses, *diabolical malice* accuses. — *Conscience* accuses; to the Romans, the second chapter[^p573-6]: « Their conscience bearing witness to them, and their thoughts between themselves accusing or also defending one another » etc.; they shall be judged, supply. — *Repute* accuses; against which it is said of Samuel, Ecclesiasticus, the forty-sixth chapter: « No man accused him »; the first to Timothy, the fifth chapter: « Against a priest receive not an accusation, but under two or three witnesses ». — *The divine law* accuses; John, the fifth chapter[^p573-7]: « There is one that accuseth you, Moses, in whom you trust ». — *Diabolical malice* accuses. It is the devil himself who « accuses the brethren day and night », the Apocalypse, the twelfth chapter.

3. From these accusations *the mercy of Christ* frees. From the accusation of *conscience* he frees *by forgiving the fault.* For when the fault has been forgiven, conscience has nothing to cry out about; as he freed the publican, Luke, the eighteenth chapter[^p573-8], who accused himself first before the Lord, according to that of Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter: « The just is first accuser of himself »; and it follows that « he went down into his house justified ». — From the accusation of *repute* he frees *by humbling the defamers,* as, John, the eighth chapter[^p573-9], he humbled the defamers of the adulteress, saying: « He that is without sin, let him cast a stone at her »; Luke, the seventh chapter, the defamer of Magdalene; for which reason it is said to the Romans, the eighth chapter: « Who shall accuse against the elect of God? If God be for us, who is against us »? — From the accusation
<!-- page 574 -->
of *the divine law* he frees the cause *by bringing it into the court of mercy.* For the divine law accuses according to *the court of justice,* but the rigour of justice ceases when God absolves in *the court of mercy.* So he freed this adulteress, whom the Jews accused according to the Law, saying[^p574-1]: « Moses commanded such a one to be stoned ». But Christ, the accusers having been convicted, brought the cause into *the court of mercy,* saying: *Neither will I condemn thee.* — From the accusation of *diabolical malice* he frees *by casting down the devil.* For the devil accuses out of malignity, and therefore is rather to be cast down than heard; whence in the Apocalypse, the twelfth chapter[^p574-2], the Lord is praised, « because the accuser of our brethren is cast forth, who accused them before the sight of God day and night ».

### Annotation 33.

(Verse 12.). *I am the light of the world.*

Look above, in the first chapter[^p574-3], concerning the light, Christ.

### Collation XXXIV.

(Verse 26.). *He that sent me is true.*

1. The sending of the Saviour is to be venerated on account of three things. For a gift is wont to be venerated either on account of *the excellence of the giver,* or on account of *the excellence of the gift,* or on account of *the need of the receiver.* — For this threefold reason the sending of the Saviour is to be venerated. Great is *he who sends,* God the Father; John, the eighth chapter[^p574-4]: « I am not alone, but I and he that sent me, the Father ». — Likewise, great is *he who is sent,* namely the Son of God; to the Galatians, the fourth chapter: « God sent his Son, made of a woman, made under the Law ». — Likewise, *they to whom he is sent* needed him; whence Genesis, the next-to-last chapter: « The sceptre shall not be taken away from Juda, nor a ruler from his thigh, till he come that is to be sent, and he shall be the expectation of nations ».

2. And truly he was *the expectation of nations,* because he was sent *to all* for a *remedy.* For he was sent *to the penitent,* that they might be relieved from fault — *to the suffering,* that they might be relieved from punishment — *to the proficient,* that they might be advanced in justice — *to the contemplative,* that they might be lifted up in the experience of grace.

3. He is sent, then, *to the penitent,* that they may *be relieved from fault.* But in *fault* three things are to be considered: *the stain, the guilt* and *the offence;* and therefore Christ is sent *to justify, to redeem, to reconcile.* — He is sent *to justify* from fault; to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p574-5]: « God sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, and of sin, hath condemned sin in the flesh, that the justification of the Law might be fulfilled in us ». — He is sent *to redeem* from punishment; for he it is « who redeemed life from destruction[^p574-6] »; to the Galatians, the fourth chapter: « But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent his Son, made of a woman, made under the Law, that he might redeem them who were under the Law »; the Psalm: « He hath sent redemption to his people ». — He is sent *to reconcile* against the offence; the first of John, the fourth chapter: « In this is charity, not as though we had loved him, but because he hath first loved us, and sent his Son to be a propitiation for our sins ».

4. He was sent to *the suffering,* that he might relieve them from *punishment;* which is done in three ways: *by rescuing, by healing* and *by guarding: by rescuing* from captivity, *by healing* from infirmity, *by guarding* from both. — He is sent, then, *to rescue;* the Psalm[^p574-7]: « Put forth thy hand from on high; rescue me and deliver me out of many waters ». — He is sent *to heal;* the Psalm: « He sent his hand and healed them ». — He is sent *to guard;* John, the sixth chapter: « This is the will of the Father who sent me, that of all that he hath given me, I should lose nothing ».

5. He is sent *to the active* to *advance* them in justice, which he does in three ways: *by teaching, by arousing* and *by leading forth: by teaching the understanding,* that they may know; *by arousing the affection,* that they may will; *by leading forth the effect,* that they may be able. — He is sent, then, *to teach;* Wisdom, the ninth chapter[^p574-8]: « Send her », namely wisdom, « out of thy holy heavens, that she may be with me and may labour with me, that I may know what is acceptable with thee ». — He is sent *to arouse;* Luke, the fourteenth chapter: « He sent his servant at the hour of supper to say to them that were invited, that they should come »; and Proverbs, the first chapter: « Wisdom preacheth abroad, she uttereth her voice in the streets ». — He is sent *to lead forth;* Malachias, the third chapter: « Behold, I will send my angel, and he shall prepare the way before thy face ».

6. He is sent *to the contemplative* to *lift* them *up* in the experience of grace; which he does in three ways: *by revealing, by visiting* and *by making certain: by revealing* the secrets of the mysteries; *by visiting* with the fruition of spiritual joys; *by making certain* of the attainment of the rewards. — He is sent, then, *to reveal;* Ezechiel, the second chapter[^p574-9]: « I looked, and behold, a hand was sent to me, wherein was a book rolled up, and he spread it before me ». — He is sent *to visit;* Genesis, the thirty-seventh chapter: « Come, I will send thee to thy brethren. Go, and see if all things be prosperous with thy brethren ». — He is sent *to make certain;* Isaias, the sixty-first chapter:
<!-- page 575 -->
« To preach to the meek he hath sent me, that I should heal the contrite of heart and preach the acceptable year to the Lord ».

### Annotation 34.

(Verse 35.). *The servant remaineth not in the house for ever; but the son remaineth for ever.*

Note here the manifold servitude. For there is *natural* servitude, *legal* servitude, *spiritual* servitude, *carnal* servitude and *eternal* servitude. — *Natural* servitude is *to be borne;* whence to the Ephesians, the sixth chapter[^p575-1]: « Servants, be obedient to your carnal masters with all fear and trembling » etc. Of this it is said by the Jews, John, the eighth chapter: « We have never been slaves to any man ». — *Legal* servitude is *to be made void;* to the Galatians, the fourth chapter: « How turn you again to the weak and needy elements of the world, which you desire to serve again »? and the fifth: « Stand fast, and be not held again under the yoke of servitude ». — *Spiritual* servitude is *to be embraced;* to the Romans, the seventh chapter[^p575-2]: « With the mind I serve the law of God »; and to the Philippians, the third chapter: « We serve God in spirit and glory in Christ Jesus »; and to the Galatians, the fifth chapter: « By charity serve one another ». — *Carnal* servitude is *to be abominated,* concerning which John, the eighth chapter[^p575-3]: « Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin »; and again: *The servant remaineth not in the house for ever.* — *Eternal* servitude is *to be dreaded;* this is that which is continued from fault into punishment, according to that of Matthew, the eighteenth chapter: « Thou wicked servant, I forgave thee all the debt » etc.; and it follows that « he commanded him to be cast into the exterior darkness ».

### Collation XXXV.

(Verse 36.). *If therefore the Son of God shall make you free, you shall be free indeed.*

1. Note, according to the manifold difference of *servitude,* the manifold difference of *liberty.* — For there is the liberty *of nature* against *natural* servitude; concerning which Job, the third chapter[^p575-4]: « The small and great are there, and the servant and the free man from his master ». An example of the servant who, when the peril of the sea was imminent, smiled at his master, saying that today they were equals and would both drink from one cup. — There is the liberty *of faith* against *legal* servitude; concerning which to the Galatians, the fifth chapter: « You have been called unto liberty: only make not liberty an occasion
to the flesh ». — There is the liberty *of fault* against *spiritual* servitude; concerning which Job, the eleventh chapter[^p575-5]: « A vain man is lifted up into pride, and thinketh himself born free like a wild ass's colt »; and to the Romans, the sixth chapter: « When you were the servants of sin, you were free men to justice ». — There is the liberty *of grace* against *carnal* servitude; the second to the Corinthians, the third chapter: « Where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty »; and to the Galatians, the fourth chapter: « We are not the children of the bondwoman, but of the free ». — There is the liberty *of glory* against *eternal* servitude; to the Romans, the eighth chapter: « The creature itself shall be delivered from the servitude of corruption into the liberty of the glory of the children of God ».

2. *The servant remaineth not in the house for ever.* It is to be noted that there is *evil* servitude — and *good* servitude. — *Evil* servitude is threefold. For some serve *the flesh;* concerning whom to the Romans, the sixteenth chapter[^p575-6]: « Such serve not God, but their own belly »; to the Philippians, the third chapter: « For many walk, of whom I have told you often, and now tell you weeping » etc. Of this servitude Seneca says: « He will serve many who serves the body »; and the same: « I am greater, and born for greater things, than to be the chattel of my body ». — But some serve *the world,* to whom it is said, Luke, the sixteenth chapter[^p575-7]: « No man can serve two masters »; and again: « You cannot serve God and mammon ». — Some serve *sin;* John, the eighth chapter: « Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin »; the second of Peter, the second chapter: « By whom a man is overcome, of the same also he is the slave ».

3. This servitude is evil, because it is *laborious;* Jeremias, the sixteenth chapter[^p575-8]: « You shall serve strange gods, who will not give you rest day and night ». — Likewise, because it is *ignominious;* Exodus, the first chapter: « With the servitude of mortar and brick ». Note and expound. — Likewise, because it is *fruitless;* to the Romans, the sixth chapter: « When you were the servants of sin, you were free men to justice; what fruit therefore had you then in those things of which you are now ashamed »?

4. *Good* servitude is threefold. For we ought to serve *God;* the Psalm[^p575-9]: « Serve ye the Lord with fear ». — We ought to serve *the prelate;* the first to Timothy, the sixth chapter: « They that have believing masters, let them not despise them, because they are brethren; but serve them the rather, because they are faithful ». — We ought also to serve *our neighbour;* to the Galatians, the fifth chapter: « By charity of the spirit serve one another ».

5. Concerning the servitude *of God,* note *in what things* God is to be served — *how* — and *for what.* — Note, then, first the differences of services, and attend,
<!-- page 576 -->
that some serve God in *the business of action,* like those servants whom the Lord called, Luke, the nineteenth chapter[^p576-1], and « he gave them ten pounds, and said to them: Trade till I come ». — Some serve God in *the battle of passion,* like those of whom it is said in the Psalm: « They have poured out the blood of thy servants ». For this service the Lord armed his own, John, the fifteenth chapter, saying: « The servant is not greater than his master. If they have persecuted me, they will also persecute you ». — Some serve God in *the bridal chamber of contemplation;* concerning whom, in the figure of the servants of Solomon, the third of Kings, the tenth chapter[^p576-2]: « Blessed are thy servants, who stand before thee always and hear thy wisdom ». — Some serve God in *the ministry of prelacy,* whom the Lord, describing them, speaks of in Matthew, the twenty-fourth chapter: « Who, thinkest thou, is a faithful and wise servant, whom the Lord hath appointed over thy family, to give them meat in season »?

6. Note next *how* God is to be served. It is to be known, then, that one is to serve *vigilantly* as regards *the solicitude of reason;* whence Luke, the twelfth chapter[^p576-3]: « Blessed is that servant whom, when the Lord shall come, he shall find watching ». — God is also to be served *faithfully* as regards *the rectitude of intention,* that there may be said to the servant what is said in Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter: « Well done, good and faithful servant, because thou hast been faithful over a few things » etc. — God is also to be served *willingly* as regards *the promptness of the will,* according as it is said in the Psalm[^p576-4]: « A people which I knew not hath served me; at the hearing of the ear they have obeyed me ». — God is also to be served *perseveringly* as regards *the continuation of the work;* Luke, the first chapter: « That we may serve him in holiness and justice before him all our days ».

7. Note *why* God is to be served. There are, then, three things that move us to serve God. *The fear of penalty* moves us. For he threatens penalty to him who serves ill; whence he says, Luke, the twelfth chapter[^p576-5]: « The servant who knew the will of his lord and did not according to his will, shall be beaten with many stripes ». — *The love of reward* moves us; to the Romans, the sixth chapter: « But now being made free from sin, and become servants to God, you have your fruit unto sanctification, and the end life everlasting ». — *The shame of reproach* moves us.
For confusion follows those who do not serve God; whence to the Romans, the sixth chapter[^p576-6]: « When you were the servants of sin, you were free men to justice. What fruit therefore had you in those things of which you are now ashamed »? — *The honour of the office* also moves us; whence on the first to the Romans the Gloss[^p576-7]: « To serve God is to reign »; and Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-third chapter: « It is great glory to follow the Lord »; whence also blessed Agatha: « That is true liberty, in which the servitude of Christ is proved ».

8. Likewise note that God is to be served by reason *of what is owed,* by reason *of advantage,* by reason *of what is honourable:* by reason *of what is owed,* because he is *the founder;* whence in the Psalm[^p576-8]: « Serve ye the Lord with gladness »; and afterwards he subjoins the cause: « Know ye that the Lord he is God; he made us, and not we ourselves ». — Because he is *the protector;* whence Luke, the first chapter: « That being delivered from the hand of our enemies, we may serve him without fear ». — Because he is *the sustainer;* for this is the cause why one ought to serve another, according to what the Egyptians said to Joseph, Genesis, the forty-seventh chapter: « Buy us into the king's servitude, and give us seed, lest, for want of tillers, the land be turned into a wilderness ».

9. Likewise, he is to be served by reason *of advantage,* namely because he *remits fault;* whence in the Psalm[^p576-9]: « The Lord will judge his people, and will be entreated in favour of his servants », that is, will have mercy. — He *infuses grace;* to the Romans, the sixth chapter: « Yield your members to serve justice unto sanctification »; and in the same: « Become servants to God, you have your fruit unto sanctification », which indeed is not except through grace. — Because he *promises glory;* Isaias, the sixty-fifth chapter: « My servants shall eat, and you shall be hungry »; it is said to the wicked, and is expounded of the refection of glory and the hunger of Gehenna.

10. Likewise, God is to be served by reason *of what is honourable.* For it is honourable to serve God by reason *of his dominion,* because he is great; the Psalm[^p576-10]: « All things serve thee ». — It is honourable also by reason *of the office,* because it is noble; whence the Gloss on the first to the Romans: « To serve God is to reign ». — It is honourable also by reason *of the servant,* because he was made for this; Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter: « Thus saith the Lord, that formed me from the womb to be his servant », that is, to serve him.

### Annotation 35.

(Verse 43.). *Why do you not know my speech?*

1. Note that the Lord speaks to us sometimes through *the signs of the creature;* sometimes through *the letters of Scripture;* sometimes as it were *through messengers,* and this in preaching; sometimes as it were *with the living voice,* and this by interior inspiration. — He speaks, then, through *the signs of the creature;* Proverbs, the first chapter[^p577-1]: « Wisdom preacheth abroad », that is, in creatures; whence Proverbs, the sixth chapter: « Go to the ant, O sluggard, and consider her ways ». — He speaks through *the letters of Scripture;* whence Exodus, the twentieth chapter: « The Lord spoke all these words »; and Jeremias, the thirty-sixth chapter: « Take thee a roll of a book, and write in it all the words that I have spoken to thee against Israel and Juda ». — He speaks through *messengers in preaching,* to whom it is said, Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p577-2]: « It is not you that speak, but the Spirit of your Father that speaketh in you ». — He speaks *with the living voice* and by interior inspiration; whence in the Psalm: « I will hear what the Lord God will speak in me »; and Osee, the second chapter: « I will lead her into the wilderness and I will speak to her heart ». — Since, then, the Lord speaks by *signs, letters, messengers* and *words,* rightly he reproaches the unlearned: *Why do you not know my speech?*

2. *Because you cannot hear my word.* Note that Christ was grievous to the wicked *to hear;* whence here: *Because you cannot* etc. *To behold;* Wisdom, the second chapter[^p577-3]: « He is grievous unto us, even to behold ». *To bear;* John, the eleventh chapter: « What do we, for this man doth many signs? If we let him alone so, all will believe in him »; this the Jews said, bearing Christ grievously.

### Annotation 36.

(Verse 44.). *You are of your father the devil, and the desires of your father you will do.*

Note that Scripture detests three kinds of desires, namely *carnal* desires, concerning which the first of Peter, the second chapter[^p577-4]: « I beseech you, as strangers and pilgrims, to refrain yourselves from carnal desires » etc. — Likewise, *worldly* desires; to Titus, the second chapter: « Denying ungodliness and worldly desires, we should live soberly and justly and godly in this world ». — Likewise, *diabolical* desires, concerning which here: *You are of your father the devil, and the desires of your
father you will do.* — These desires are *to be detested,* because they *assail* the soul; the first of Peter, the second chapter[^p577-5]: « I beseech you to refrain yourselves from carnal desires, which war against the soul ». They *torment* the soul; Proverbs, the twenty-first chapter: « Desires kill the slothful ». They *take* the soul *captive;* whence that of the Psalm: « God delivered them up to the desires of their heart; they shall go in their own inventions ».

### Collation XXXVI.

(Verse 59.). *But Jesus hid himself, and went out of the temple.*

1. In these words is touched a twofold degree of withdrawing grace from the wicked. For the Lord, when he is offended by the sinner, first takes away from him *the sense of grace* or the experience, and then as it were *he hides himself,* when he is not felt spiritually. — Secondly, he takes away *the existence of grace,* and then as it were *he goes out of the temple,* when he is not had through grace; which two things are signified here: the first, when he says: *Jesus hid himself;* the second, when he says: *And went out of the temple.* — The hiding of the Lord, then, is the withdrawal of spiritual consolation.

2. Whence note that the Lord hides himself sometimes, that he may *desert;* sometimes, that he may *rebuke;* sometimes, that he may *advance.* — The Lord hides himself from *the wicked* to desert them, and this on account of their malignity; as the Lord hides himself from the Jews here, when he *hid himself, and went out of the temple;* and Deuteronomy, the thirty-second chapter[^p577-6]: « I will hide my face from them, and will consider what their last end shall be ». — He hides himself from *the weak* or carnal *to rebuke* them, and this on account of their carnality; as he hid himself from the crowds, John, the sixth chapter, who wishing « to make him king, he fled into the mountain »; Job, the twenty-eighth chapter: « Wisdom is hid from the eyes of all living », supply: delicately according to the flesh. — He hides himself sometimes from *the good,* that he may *advance* them, as he hid himself from Magdalene, John, the twentieth chapter, when he was present, but did not show himself; he lay hidden, but did not withdraw, which was done for Magdalene's profit.

3. But he is hidden from *the elect* for a threefold profit: first, that he may *correct the fault;* which is figured, Genesis, the forty-second chapter[^p577-7], of Joseph, who, being present to his brothers, would not be recognised, exacting from them penance or sorrow over the selling of their brother. — Secondly, that he may *humble the mind;* which
<!-- page 578 -->
is figured, the second of Kings, the fourteenth chapter, of Absalom, who, as long as he did not see the king's face, bore himself humbly; but after he saw it, bore himself proudly, whence « he made himself chariots and horsemen » etc. — Thirdly, that he may *arouse charity;* as he hid himself from his disciples going to Emmaus, with whom he went and from whom he lay hidden and in whom he kindled charity; whence Luke, the last chapter[^p578-1]: « Was not our heart burning »? — On account of these three things the Lord *hides himself* from the good, but *does not withdraw* from them; but from the wicked he *hides himself* and *goes out of the temple.*

4. But he goes out *of the temple* on account of three things: because the temple *is polluted;* the Psalm[^p578-2]: « They have defiled thy holy temple ». — Because the temple *is violated;* the first of Machabees, the second chapter: « Her temple is become as a man without honour; the vessels of her glory are carried away captive ». — Because in the temple *he is stoned;* whence it is said here: *Now they took up stones* etc.; *but Jesus hid himself, and went out of the temple.* — The temple *is polluted* when a man *admits* an evil *delight.* The temple *is violated* when a man *perpetrates* an evil *work.* But Jesus *is stoned* when a man *assails correction;* and these three things compel Jesus to desert a man.

5. *Jesus went out of the temple.* — Note what Christ *did* in the temple, and what Christ *bore* in the temple. What Christ *did* in the temple instructs prelates in *things to be done;* but what Christ *bore* in the temple instructs them in *losses to be borne.*

Note, then, that Christ in the temple *taught,* in the temple *rebuked,* in the temple *absolved.* — In the temple he *taught;* John, the eighteenth chapter[^p578-3]: « I have always taught in the synagogue and in the temple, whither all the Jews resort ». — In the temple he *rebuked;* John, the second chapter: « When he had made a scourge of little cords, he drove them all out of the temple ». — But in the temple he *absolved* the adulteress; John, the eighth chapter: « Early in the morning he came into the temple »; and it follows: « They bring unto him a woman taken in adultery » etc. — These three things instruct prelates in *things to be done,* that they be *learned in preaching, manly in rebuking, kindly in remitting.* For prelacy without *learning*[^tr-doctrina], as a certain wise man says[^p578-4], is as a sword in the hand of *a fool;* without *manliness,* as a sword in the hand of *a boy;* without *clemency,* as a sword in the hand of *a madman*.

6. The things which Christ *bore* in the temple are to be noted. For Christ was *tempted* in the temple, *blasphemed* in the temple and *stoned* in the temple. — He was *tempted,* John, the eighth chapter[^p578-5], when they brought to him the adulteress; whence it follows: « And this they said tempting him ». — He was *blasphemed* in the same, when they said to him: « Do not we say well that thou art a Samaritan and hast a devil »? — *Stoned;* whence in the same: *They took up stones to cast at him.*

7. *But Jesus went out of the temple.* But note that Christ, tempted, *answered;* blasphemed, *bore it;* and stoned, *went out;* and these three things instruct prelates in things to be borne, that they be not silent out of *indignation,* since Christ *when tempted answers;* nor answer out of *pride,* since Christ *when blasphemed bears it;* nor thrust themselves forward or avenge themselves by *violence,* since Christ *when stoned yields to the injury.*
