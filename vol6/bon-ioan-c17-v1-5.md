# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XVII, Vers. 1-5
## *In prima capituli parte Christus petit sibi claritatis suae manifestationem*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 467 -->

### Capitulum XVII.

#### Tertio confirmantur discipuli orationis subsidio quadrupliciter.

1. *Haec locutus est Iesus* etc. Supra Dominus discipulos confortavit exemplo et eruditionis verbo; hic confortat *orationis subsidio*[^p467-4]; et hoc facit in praesenti capitulo, in quo ponitur Christi oratio. Et habet praesens capitulum quatuor partes. In prima Dominus *sibi petit claritatis manifestationem.* In secunda *Apostolis petit sanctitatis conservationem,* ibi[^p467-5]: *Pater, manifestavi nomen tuum* etc. In tertia *credituris per Apostolos petit conformitatem gratiae,* ibi: *Non tantum pro eis rogo, sed pro eis* etc. In quarta, *tam his quam illis perpetuitatem gloriae,* ibi: *Pater, quos dedisti mihi, volo* etc.

#### In prima capituli parte Christus petit sibi claritatis suae manifestationem.

Primo igitur *sibi petit claritatis suae manifestationem* hoc ordine. Primo ponitur *petendi vel orandi modus;* secundo, *petitionis fructus;* tertio, *exaudiendi ratio;* quarto, *ipsa petitio.*

2. (Vers. 1.). *Petendi* igitur *modus* insinuatur in hoc, quod Christus *devote* petebat et intente; in cuius signum oculos ad caelum levabat. Propterea dicit: *Haec locutus est Iesus,* quae praedicta sunt, *et sublevatis oculis in caelum,* per devotionem; supra undecimo[^p467-6]: « Iesus, sursum elevatis oculis, dixit: Pater, gratias ago tibi, quoniam audisti me »; Psalmus: « Ad te levavi oculos meos, qui habitas in caelis »; Threnorum tertio: « Levemus corda nostra cum manibus ad Dominum ». — Non solum devote, sed etiam *humiliter;* unde *dixit: Pater.* Deum vocat *Patrem,* quia ipsum honorat ut Patrem; supra octavo[^p467-7]: « Honorifico Patrem meum, et vos inhonorastis me ». — Petiit etiam *discrete;* ideo dicit: *Venit hora, clarifica Filium tuum.* Discrete petebat, quia tunc clarificari petebat, quando opportunitas temporis advenerat. De hac hora saepe dictum est, supra secundo[^p467-8] et septimo: « Nondum venit hora mea »; ideo clarificari per miracula nolebat, sed cum hora haec venerat, clarificari petebat. — In hoc igitur modo orandi instruimur, ut nos oremus *devote, humiliter* et *discrete*[^p467-9].

3. *Ut Filius tuus* etc. Tangitur hic *petitionis fructus,* et hic est duplex, scilicet *divini nominis manifestatio* et *nostra beatificatio.* Propter *manifestationem divini nominis* dicit: *Ut Filius tuus* etc.; ita, Pater, clarifica Filium tuum, *ut* et *Filius tuus clarificet te,* quia propter hoc moriebatur; unde supra duodecimo[^p467-10]: « Pater, clarifica nomen tuum ». — Alius fructus est *nostra glorificatio;* propter quod dicit:

4. (Vers. 2.). *Sicut dedisti ei potestatem omnis carnis,* id est, ostende, quod dederis ei potestatem omnis carnis, id est ad salvandam omnem carnem; Matthaei ultimo[^p467-11]: « Data est mihi omnis potestas in
<!-- page 468 -->
caelo et in terra », et hoc ad salutem: *Ut omne quod dedisti ei,* per praedestinationem scilicet, *det eis vitam aeternam,* per glorificationem. *Omne* pro *omni;* supra sexto[^p468-1]: « Haec est voluntas eius qui misit me Patris, ut omne, quod dedit mihi, non perdam ex eo, sed resuscitem illud in novissimo die ». Et explanat, quae sit ista vita aeterna:

5. (Vers. 3.). *Haec est*[^p468-2], inquit, *vita aeterna, ut cognoscant te, solum Deum verum, et quem misisti Iesum Christum;* tertii Regum decimo: « Beati viri tui, et beati servi tui, hi qui stant coram te semper et audiunt sapientiam tuam ». « Nosse te consummata iustitia est », Sapientiae decimo quinto; et gloria non est aliud quam iustitia consummata, quia « scire iustitiam et virtutem tuam radix est immortalitatis », et ideo vitae aeternae.

6. (Vers. 4.). *Ego te clarificavi.* Notatur hic tertium, scilicet *exaudiendi ratio;* et hoc erat meritum Christi, quo merebatur glorificari sive clarificari; ideo dicit: *Ego te clarificavi super terram,* propter hoc mereor a te clarificari; *opus consummavi, quod dedisti mihi, ut facerem*[^p468-3]; ideo consummavit, quia usque ad mortem obedivit; Lucae duodecimo: « Baptismo habeo baptizari; et quomodo coarctor, donec perficiatur »? De hac consummatione ad Hebraeos secundo: « Decebat eum, propter quem omnia, et per quem omnia, qui multos filios adduxerat in gloriam, auctorem salutis eorum per passionem consummare ». Et quia totum fecerat, ideo exaudiri debebat.

7. (Vers. 5.). *Et nunc clarifica me tu, Pater.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *ipsa petitio,* qua petit claritatis suae manifestationem; propter quod dicit: *Et nunc clarifica me tu, Pater, apud temetipsum claritate, quam habui, priusquam mundus fieret*[^p468-4], *apud te;* id est, illam claritatem manifesta, ut, sicut ab aeterno fui tibi aequalis, antequam mundus fieret, ita etiam sit aliis manifestum. Propter quod infra eodem dicitur: « Ego clarificatus sum in eis », id est in Apostolis, quia ipsi credebant ipsum aequalem Patri; supra decimo sexto: « Nunc credimus, quia a Deo existi »; supra primo: « Verbum erat apud Deum », scilicet aequale Deo, « et Deus erat Verbum ».

### Quaestiones.

8. Quaest. I. Quaeritur de hoc, quod Filius orat: quia, si *orare* est indigentis[^p468-5] et impotentis; et Filius omnia potest: ergo non debet orare. — *Si tu dicas,* quod orat secundum quod homo; hoc nihil est, quia, quidquid meruit secundum quod homo, meruit ab instanti conceptionis.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Christus oravit secundum quod homo; secundum enim naturam humanam non omnia poterat[^p468-6]. Etsi dignus erat per gratiam, nihil impediebat, hoc ipsum mereri bona operatione; unde patiendo et orando meruit glorificari. Sed hoc non fuit de indebito debitum facere, sed de debito uno modo facere debitum pluribus[^p468-7].

9. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur: 1. de hac petitione: *Pater clarifica* vel *glorifica*[^p468-8] *Filium tuum.* — *Contra:* Supra octavo[^p468-9]: *Ego gloriam meam non quaero:* ergo non debebat petere propriam gloriam vel propriam claritatem.

2. Item, secundum quam naturam hoc petit? — Secundum *divinam non,* quia secundum illam omnia potest; secundum *humanam non,* quia dicit paulo post[^p468-10]: *Clarifica claritate, quam habui, priusquam mundus fieret;* hoc non potuit esse secundum humanam naturam.

**Respondeo:** Ad primum dicendum, quod ipse claritatem quaerebat, ut ex illa glorificaretur Deus;
<!-- page 469 -->
unde addidit: *Ut Filius tuus clarificet te;* et hoc non est quaerere *propriam* gloriam. Ille enim propriam gloriam quaerit, qui laudem suam sibi quaerit, non referendo ad Deum. — Vel dicendum, quod ipse non quaerebat gloriam ab hominibus, quia vana est, sed a Deo[^p469-1].

2. Quod quaeritur: secundum quam naturam hoc petit? — Dixit haereticus ex hoc, quod Filius fuit ante mundum, etiam secundum *illam* naturam orat, quia minor erat Patre. Hoc dicebat Arius[^p469-2].

Sed Augustinus[^p469-3] dicit, quod hoc intelligitur secundum *humanam* naturam, et est exponendum: *claritate, quam habui apud te,* scilicet praedestinatione, quia ad Romanos primo: *Praedestinatus est Filius Dei in virtute.*

Aliter dicendum, quod differt *qui petit* et *cui* sive *pro quo* in hoc, quia *qui petit* vel orat secundum quod huiusmodi inferior est et impotens; sed *cui petitur,* hoc est dupliciter: aut petitur *sibi in se,* ut eius indigentia suppleatur, ut cum ego peto mihi peccatori; aut petitur *sibi in aliis,* ut eius gloria manifestetur; et sic ego possum aliquid petere Deo: Deus manifesta te, honorifica te. — Dico igitur, quod cum quaeritur: secundum quam naturam petebat? si quaeras de eo qui petit, *secundum quod* petit? dico secundum *humanam.* Si quaeras: *cui* petebat? dico, quod *secundum divinam.* Unde *me* demonstrat ibi hypostasim divinam aequalem Patri ab aeterno, quam aequalitatem manifestari petebat[^p469-4].

10. Quaest. III. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Sicut dedisti ei potestatem omnis carnis.*

**Contra:** 1. Matthaei ultimo[^p469-5] dicitur, quod post resurrectionem data est.

2. Item, *si dedit potestatem omnis carnis;* et ipse *dat omni, quod dedit sibi Pater, vitam aeternam*[^p469-6]: ergo omnes salvantur.

Augustinus[^p469-7] respondet, quod intelligitur secundum praescientiam hic *dedisti,* id est, praevidisti dari. — Chrysostomus[^p469-8] vero dicit, quod hoc intelligitur secundum divinam naturam: *dedisti* ab aeterno; in Matthaeo dicitur *data,* non tunc primo exhibita, sed manifestata.

2. Quod obiicitur: *omnis carnis;* distributio est pro *generibus singulorum,* id est de omni carne. Vel si fiat distributio pro *singulis generum*[^p469-9]; tunc facienda est vis in hoc quod est *dedisti;* quia non *dedit ei omnem carnem,* sed *potestatem dedit super omnem carnem;* sed ei *dedit praedestinatos,* ut salvet; *potestatem vero dedit* super malos, ut iudicet.

11. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Haec est vita aeterna, ut cognoscant te, solum verum Deum.*

1. Non solum est *cognitio,* sed etiam *dilectio;* aut si tu dicas, quod *dilectio in cognitione;* quare non posuit ibi *dilectionem?*

2. Item, *Deum* aut tenetur *essentialiter,* aut *personaliter.* Si *personaliter:* ergo excluditur Spiritus sanctus. Si *essentialiter:* ergo frustra addidit: *Quem misisti Iesum Christum;* quia cognitio Christi-hominis non est de substantiali praemio[^p469-10], sed Christi-Dei.

Dicendum ad primum, quod in *cognitione,* quae est ad faciem, de necessitate intelligitur *dilectio,* unde cum dicitur, quod « visio est tota merces[^p469-11] », non excluditur dilectio. Magis autem dicitur de *visione* quam *dilectione,* quia *visio* distinguit quod est patriae ab eo quod est viae; sed *dilectio* non. Nunc enim non videmus, sed tamen diligimus.

2. Quod quaeritur: quomodo tenetur *Deus?* dicendum, quod si stat ibi *essentialiter,* tunc quod subiungitur: *et quem misisti,* intelligitur secundum humanam naturam, secundum quam, etsi non attendatur essentia praemii, tamen vehementissima erit
<!-- page 470 -->
delectatio in Christo-homine[^p470-1], et ita additur: *et quem misisti Iesum Christum.* — Vel si tenetur *personaliter,* tunc Iesus Christus stat pro Filio secundum divinam naturam, et non excluditur Spiritus sanctus, quia est unio Patris et Filii. Unde haec dictio *solum* non additur ad exclusionem personarum, propter hoc quod qui videt unam personam videt aliam, sed ad exclusionem falsorum deorum[^p470-2].

## English

### Chapter XVII.

#### Third, the disciples are confirmed by the aid of prayer in four ways.

1. *These things Jesus spoke* etc. Above the Lord comforted the disciples by example and by a word of erudition; here he comforts them *by the aid of prayer*[^p467-4]; and this he does in the present chapter, in which the prayer of Christ is set down. And the present chapter has four parts. In the first the Lord *asks for himself the manifestation of clarity.* In the second he *asks for the Apostles the conservation of sanctity,* there[^p467-5]: *Father, I have manifested thy name* etc. In the third he *asks for those who will believe through the Apostles conformity of grace,* there: *Not for them only do I pray, but for them* etc. In the fourth, *for these as for those the perpetuity of glory,* there: *Father, whom thou hast given me, I will* etc.

#### In the first part of the chapter Christ asks for himself the manifestation of his clarity.

First, then, he *asks for himself the manifestation of his clarity* in this order. First is set down *the manner of asking or praying;* second, *the fruit of the petition;* third, *the reason for being heard;* fourth, *the petition itself.*

2. (Vers. 1.). *The manner* of *asking,* then, is intimated in this, that Christ asked *devoutly* and intently; in sign of which he raised his eyes to heaven. Therefore he says: *These things Jesus spoke,* which were said before, *and lifting up his eyes to heaven,* by devotion; above, the eleventh chapter[^p467-6]: « Jesus, lifting up his eyes, said: Father, I give thee thanks that thou hast heard me »; the Psalm: « To thee have I lifted up my eyes, who dwellest in heaven »; Lamentations, the third chapter: « Let us lift up our hearts with our hands to the Lord ». — Not only devoutly, but also *humbly;* hence *he said: Father.* He calls God *Father,* because he honours him as Father; above, the eighth chapter[^p467-7]: « I honour my Father: and you have dishonoured me ». — He asked also *discreetly;* therefore he says: *The hour is come, glorify thy Son.* He asked discreetly, because he asked to be glorified then, when the opportuneness of the time had arrived. Of this hour it has often been said, above, the second chapter[^p467-8] and the seventh chapter: « My hour is not yet come »; therefore he did not will to be glorified through miracles, but when this hour had come, he asked to be glorified. — In this manner of praying, then, we are instructed that we should pray *devoutly, humbly* and *discreetly*[^p467-9].

3. *That thy Son* etc. Here is touched *the fruit of the petition,* and this is twofold, namely *the manifestation of the divine name* and *our beatification.* On account of *the manifestation of the divine name* he says: *That thy Son* etc.; thus, Father, glorify thy Son, *that* also *thy Son may glorify thee,* because on this account he was dying; hence above, the twelfth chapter[^p467-10]: « Father, glorify thy name ». — The other fruit is *our glorification;* on account of which he says:

4. (Vers. 2.). *As thou hast given him power over all flesh,* that is, show that thou hast given him power over all flesh, that is, for saving all flesh; Matthew, the last chapter[^p467-11]: « All power is given to me in
<!-- page 468 -->
heaven and in earth », and this for salvation: *That all that thou hast given him,* namely by predestination, *he may give them eternal life,* by glorification. *All* for *to all;* above, the sixth chapter[^p468-1]: « This is the will of the Father who sent me: that all that he hath given me, I should not lose of it, but should raise it up again in the last day ». And he explains what this eternal life is:

5. (Vers. 3.). *This is*[^p468-2], he says, *eternal life: That they may know thee, the only true God, and whom thou hast sent, Jesus Christ;* the third of Kings, the tenth chapter: « Blessed are thy men, and blessed are thy servants, these who stand before thee always, and hear thy wisdom ». « To know thee is consummated justice », Wisdom, the fifteenth chapter; and glory is nothing other than consummated justice, because « to know thy justice, and thy power, is the root of immortality », and therefore of eternal life.

6. (Vers. 4.). *I have glorified thee.* Here is noted the third, namely *the reason for being heard;* and this was the merit of Christ, by which he merited to be glorified or clarified; therefore he says: *I have glorified thee on the earth,* on this account I merit to be glorified by thee; *I have consummated the work which thou gavest me, that I should do it*[^p468-3]; therefore he consummated it, because he obeyed even unto death; Luke, the twelfth chapter: « I have a baptism wherewith I am to be baptized. And how am I straitened until it be accomplished »? Of this consummation to the Hebrews, the second chapter: « It became him for whom are all things and by whom are all things, who had brought many children into glory, to consummate the author of their salvation, by his passion ». And because he had done the whole, therefore he ought to be heard.

7. (Vers. 5.). *And now glorify thou me, O Father.* Here is touched the fourth, namely *the petition itself,* by which he asks the manifestation of his clarity; on account of which he says: *And now glorify thou me, O Father, with thyself, with the clarity which I had, before the world was made*[^p468-4], *with thee;* that is, manifest that clarity, that, as from eternity I was equal to thee, before the world was made, so it may also be manifest to others. On account of which it is said below in the same: « I am glorified in them », that is in the Apostles, because they believed him equal to the Father; above, the sixteenth chapter: « Now we believe that thou camest forth from God »; above, the first chapter: « The Word was with God », namely equal to God, « and the Word was God ».

### Questions.

8. Quaest. I. It is asked concerning this, that the Son prays: because, if *to pray* is of one in need[^p468-5] and powerless; and the Son can do all things: therefore he ought not to pray. — *If you say* that he prays according as he is man; this is nothing, because whatever he merited according as he is man, he merited from the instant of conception.

**I respond:** It must be said that Christ prayed according as he is man; for according to human nature he could not do all things[^p468-6]. Although he was worthy through grace, nothing hindered his meriting this very thing by good working; hence by suffering and praying he merited to be glorified. But this was not to make of what is not owed what is owed, but of what is owed in one way to make it owed in more ways[^p468-7].

9. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked: 1. of this petition: *Father, clarify* or *glorify*[^p468-8] *thy Son.* — *On the contrary:* Above, the eighth chapter[^p468-9]: *I seek not my own glory:* therefore he ought not to ask for his own glory or his own clarity.

2. Likewise, according to which nature does he ask this? — According to *the divine, no,* because according to that he can do all things; according to *the human, no,* because he says a little after[^p468-10]: *Glorify with the clarity which I had, before the world was made;* this could not be according to human nature.

**I respond:** To the first it must be said that he sought clarity that from it God might be glorified;
<!-- page 469 -->
whence he added: *That thy Son may glorify thee;* and this is not to seek *his own* glory. For he seeks his own glory who seeks his own praise for himself, not referring it to God. — Or it must be said that he did not seek glory from men, because it is vain, but from God[^p469-1].

2. As to what is asked: according to which nature does he ask this? — The heretic said, from this, that the Son was before the world, that he prays also according to *that* nature, because he was less than the Father. This Arius said[^p469-2].

But Augustine[^p469-3] says that this is understood according to *the human* nature, and is to be expounded: *with the clarity which I had with thee,* namely by predestination, because to the Romans, the first chapter: *The Son of God was predestinated in power.*

Otherwise it must be said that there is a difference between *who asks* and *to whom* or *for whom* in this, that *he who asks* or prays, according as he is such, is inferior and powerless; but *to whom it is asked* is twofold: either it is asked *for him in himself,* that his need may be supplied, as when I ask for myself a sinner; or it is asked *for him in others,* that his glory may be manifested; and thus I can ask something for God: God, manifest thyself, honour thyself. — I say therefore that when it is asked: according to which nature did he ask? if you ask of him who asks, *according to what* he asks? I say according to *the human.* If you ask: *to whom* did he ask? I say, *according to the divine.* Hence *me* there points to the divine hypostasis equal to the Father from eternity, which equality he asked to be manifested[^p469-4].

10. Quaest. III. It is asked concerning what he says: *As thou hast given him power over all flesh.*

**On the contrary:** 1. In Matthew, the last chapter[^p469-5], it is said that it was given after the resurrection.

2. Likewise, *if he gave power over all flesh;* and he *gives to all that the Father gave him eternal life*[^p469-6]: therefore all are saved.

Augustine[^p469-7] responds that *thou hast given* here is understood according to foreknowledge, that is, thou hast foreseen it to be given. — But Chrysostom[^p469-8] says that this is understood according to the divine nature: *thou hast given* from eternity; in Matthew it is said *given,* not then first exhibited, but manifested.

2. As to what is objected: *over all flesh;* the distribution is over the *kinds of individuals,* that is, of all flesh. Or if the distribution be made over the *individuals of kinds*[^p469-9]; then stress must be laid on *thou hast given;* because he did not *give him all flesh,* but *gave power over all flesh;* but to him he *gave the predestined,* that he may save them; but *power he gave* over the wicked, that he may judge them.

11. Quaest. IV. Likewise it is asked concerning what he says: *This is eternal life, that they may know thee, the only true God.*

1. Not only is there *knowledge,* but also *dilection;* or if you say that *dilection is in knowledge;* why did he not place *dilection* there?

2. Likewise, *God* is taken either *essentially* or *personally.* If *personally:* therefore the Holy Spirit is excluded. If *essentially:* therefore he added in vain: *Whom thou hast sent, Jesus Christ;* because the knowledge of Christ-man does not belong to the substantial reward[^p469-10], but that of Christ-God.

To the first it must be said that in *knowledge,* which is face to face, *dilection* is of necessity understood, whence when it is said that « vision is the whole reward[^p469-11] », dilection is not excluded. But it is said of *vision* rather than *dilection,* because *vision* distinguishes what is of the fatherland from what is of the way; but *dilection* does not. For now we do not see, but nevertheless we love.

2. As to what is asked: how is *God* taken? it must be said that if it stands there *essentially,* then what is subjoined: *and whom thou hast sent,* is understood according to human nature, according to which, although the essence of the reward is not regarded, yet there will be most vehement
<!-- page 470 -->
delight in Christ-man[^p470-1], and thus is added: *and whom thou hast sent, Jesus Christ.* — Or if it is taken *personally,* then Jesus Christ stands for the Son according to the divine nature, and the Holy Spirit is not excluded, because he is the union of the Father and the Son. Hence this word *only* is not added for the exclusion of persons, on this account, that he who sees one person sees another, but for the exclusion of false gods[^p470-2].
