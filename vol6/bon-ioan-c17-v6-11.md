# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XVII, Vers. 6-11
## *In parte secunda Christus petit Apostolis sanctitatis conservationem*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 470 -->

#### In parte secunda Christus petit Apostolis sanctitatis conservationem.

12. *Manifestavi nomen tuum hominibus.* Haec est secunda pars capituli, in qua Apostolis petit *sanctitatis conservationem;* et primo petit eis *stabilitatem in bono;* secundo, *liberationem a malo,* ibi[^p470-3]: *Cum essem cum eis* etc.

Petitur ergo primo Apostolis *in bono conservatio;* et quia petitiones exaudiendae debent esse rationabiles[^p470-4], praemittitur multiplex ratio. Proceditur ergo hoc ordine. Primo ostenditur in Apostolis *diligens verborum Christi conservatio;* secundo, *divina electio;* tertio, *discipulorum desolatio;* quarto, infertur *ipsa petitio.*

13. (Vers. 6.). Ponitur ergo primo vel insinuatur *verborum Christi diligens conservatio,* quam habuerunt in doctrina Christi, quae manifestata est eis; ideo dicit: *Manifestavi nomen tuum hominibus, quos dedisti mihi de mundo,* id est, quos de mundo vocasti ad me per praesentem iustitiam; supra sexto[^p470-5]: « Nemo venit ad me, nisi Pater traxerit eum ». Et ratio, quare traxit eos de mundo, subditur: *Tui erant,* per electionem aeternam, *et mihi eos dedisti,* ad erudiendum per doctrinam; Isaiae octavo[^p470-6]: « Ecce, ego et pueri mei, quos dedit mihi Dominus »; exponit Apostolus de Christo. Et ipsi congrue susceperunt; unde dicit: *Et sermonem tuum servaverunt;* de qua servatione supra decimo quarto[^p470-7]: « Qui habet mandata mea et servat ea, ille est, qui diligit me ». Et quomodo verbum vel sermonem Patris servaverunt, ostendit. Cum enim nec Patrem audierint nec viderint, quomodo sermonem servare potuerint, aliquis dubitaret; ideo dicit, quod servaverunt credendo in Filium.

14. (Vers. 7.). *Et*[^p470-8] *nunc cognoverunt, quia omnia; quae dedisti mihi, abs te sunt,* et ideo te cognoverunt cognoscendo me; unde supra quinto: « Non potest Filius a se ipso facere quidquam »; supra decimo quarto: « Pater in me manens, ipse facit opera », et ideo « qui videt me videt et Patrem meum ». Et etiam sermonem Patris audierant; ideo dicit:

15. (Vers. 8.). *Quia verba, quae dedisti mihi, dedi eis;* supra decimo quinto[^p470-9]: « Quaecumque audivi a Patre meo, nota feci vobis ». Et ipsi mea verba, quae dedi eis, servaverunt, et ideo sermonem tuum; propterea dicit: *Et ipsi acceperunt,* subiiciendo intellectum suum, *et cognoverunt vere, quia a te exivi,* secundum divinam naturam; *et crediderunt, quia tu me misisti,* in carnem scilicet. Et ita crediderunt et cognoverunt Divinitatem in Christo et humanitatem. Et attendendum quod dicit: *Et crediderunt, quia tu me misisti;* et post: *Et cognoverunt, quia a te exivi.* Ipsi dixerunt supra decimo sexto[^p470-10]: « In hoc cognovimus, quia a Deo existi »; quia per fidem humanitatis assumtae pervenitur ad intelligentiam generationis aeternae. Unde Augustinus[^p470-11] dicit, quod qui contempserunt Christum humilem non meruerunt agnoscere sublimem. Haec igitur est prima ratio exaudiendi pro eis, scilicet verborum Christi conservatio; unde supra decimo quinto[^p470-12]: « Si manseritis in me, et verba mea in vobis manserint; quodcumque volueritis, petetis, et fiet vobis ».

16. (Vers. 9.). *Et ego pro eis rogo.* Tangitur hic secunda ratio exaudiendi pro Apostolis, scilicet ipsa *divina electio;* quia ipse Dominus noster orabat pro electis, non pro reprobis. Propterea dicit: *Ego pro eis rogo,* quia scilicet electi sunt; ideo dicit: *Non pro mundo rogo,* « id est mundo deditis[^p470-13] », quos excaecasti; *sed pro eis quos dedisti mihi,* id est, per me salvasti; Actuum vigesimo septimo signanter dictum est Paulo: « Ecce, donavit tibi Deus animas omnium, qui navigant tecum ». Nec ex hoc, quod mihi dedisti, tibi abstulisti: *Quia tui sunt.*
<!-- page 471 -->

17. (Vers. 10.). *Et mea omnia tua sunt, et tua mea*[^p471-1]; supra decimo sexto: « Omnia, quae habet Pater, mea sunt », et e converso. Ideo dicebat supra septimo: « Mea doctrina non est mea »; ideo servi mei et electi mei sunt electi tui. Et haec est secunda ratio exaudiendi pro Apostolis, scilicet ipsorum electio; ideo supra decimo quinto: « Non vos me elegistis, sed ego elegi vos et posui vos, ut eatis et fructum afferatis, et fructus vester maneat; ut, quodcumque petieritis Patrem in nomine meo, det vobis ».

18. *Et ego*[^p471-2] *clarificatus.* Tangitur hic tertia ratio, scilicet *discipulorum desolatio* de recessu Christi, qui appropinquabat; propter quam appropinquationem dicit se *clarificatum* et recessisse, quia de proximo erat futura; ideo dicit: *Et ego clarificatus sum in eis;* quia ipsi eum cognoverant et in proximo cognoscere debebant.

19. (Vers. 11.). *Et iam non sum in mundo;* quia, supra decimo quarto[^p471-3], « iam non multa loquar vobiscum »; *et hi in mundo sunt, et ego ad te venio,* et ideo desolatos dimitto; supra decimo sexto: « Vado ad eum qui misit me; sed quia haec locutus sum vobis, tristitia implevit cor vestrum ». Et haec erat tertia ratio exaudiendi; unde supra decimo quarto: « Ego ad Patrem vado; et quodcumque petieritis Patrem in nomine meo, hoc faciam ».

20. *Pater sancte.* Postquam praemissa est multiplex petendi ratio, subditur hic quarto loco *ipsa petitio,* qua petit eis conservationem in bono; propter quod dicit: *Pater sancte, serva eos in nomine tuo, quos dedisti mihi,* id est in virtute nominis; supra decimo[^p471-4]: « Nemo potest rapere de manu Patris mei ». *Serva,* inquam, in bono; unde dicit: *Ut sint unum, sicut et nos,* scilicet unitate concordiae et pacis, quam unitatem servat Deus in electis, sed tamen cum eorum sollicitudine; ideo ad Ephesios quarto[^p471-5]: « Solliciti, servare unitatem Spiritus in vinculo pacis ».

### Quaestiones.

21. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic de hoc, quod Dominus hic orat pro Apostolis;

1. Quia supra decimo sexto[^p471-6] dicitur: *Non dico vobis, quia ego rogabo Patrem de vobis; ipse enim Pater amat vos:* facit ergo hic contrarium eius quod dixerat.

2. Item, ipse Dominus pro nullis orabat nisi pro praedestinatis[^p471-7], de quibus sciebat, quod Deus vellet salvare finaliter; sed sine sua oratione salvarentur: ergo frustra orabat.

3. Item, quare vocali oratione orabat? Quae necessitas?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod triplici ex causa Dominus oravit pro Apostolis, scilicet ad *salutis impetrationem, consolationem* et *eruditionem:* quia eis *merebatur,* quia ex auditu horum verborum *consolabantur,* quia orare *erudiebantur* ex hoc et nos ipsi; ideo vocali oratione oravit[^p471-8].

1. Quod obiicitur, quod supra Dominus dixit: *Non dico vobis, quod rogabo Patrem* etc.; non voluit Dominus negare, quin oraret pro eis; cum dicatur ad Hebraeos nono[^p471-9], quod *apparet vultui Dei ad interpellandum pro nobis;* sed quod non oraret Patrem tanquam invitum, sed tanquam ipsis Apostolis benevolum.

2. Sed quaerit: ad quid orat? Augustinus dicit de Praedestinatione Sanctorum, quod non exaudimur nisi pro praedestinatis, et pro illis non frustra oramus, quia « fortassis praedestinati sunt, ut nostris orationibus concedantur[^p471-10] ». Sic in Christo.

22. Quaest. II. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Pro eis rogo, non pro mundo.*

**Contra:** 1. Infra eodem[^p471-11]: *Ut mundus credat, quia tu me misisti;* orabat ergo, ut mundus crederet.

2. Item, pro eis orare debebat, pro quibus missus erat et mori debebat; sed mortuus est pro
<!-- page 472 -->
mundo; supra tertio[^p472-1]: *Sic Deus dilexit mundum, ut Filium suum unigenitum daret.*

3. Item, pro *mundo* intelliguntur peccatores; sed[^p472-2] *non egent qui sani sunt medico, sed male habentes;* ergo pro talibus maxime erat orandum.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *mundus* in hac oratione accipitur tripliciter et propter hoc generat ambiguitatem, et superadditur quartus modus. Dicitur enim *mundus* ipse *orbis nobis visibilis;* et sic accipitur supra primo[^p472-3]: *Mundus per ipsum factus est.* Aliquando dicitur *status nostrae conversationis,* secundum quod habet miseriam annexam; et sic ibi: *Hi sunt in mundo, et ego ad te venio.* Aliquando, prout nominat *statum praesentis conversationis cum culpa;* et sic infra: *Mundus eos odio habuit.* Aliquando, prout importat *reprobationem aeternam,* et sic accipitur ibi: *Ego pro eis rogo, non pro mundo rogo.* — Primo modo dicit naturam; secundo modo, naturam et miseriam; tertio, naturam et miseriam et culpam; quarto modo, naturam et miseriam et culpam et reprobationem divinam.

## English
<!-- page 470 -->

#### In the second part Christ asks for the Apostles the conservation of sanctity.

12. *I have manifested thy name to the men.* This is the second part of the chapter, in which he asks for the Apostles *the conservation of sanctity;* and first he asks for them *stability in good;* second, *liberation from evil,* there[^p470-3]: *When I was with them* etc.

First, then, is asked for the Apostles *conservation in good;* and because petitions to be heard ought to be reasonable[^p470-4], a manifold reason is set down first. It proceeds, therefore, in this order. First is shown in the Apostles *the diligent conservation of the words of Christ;* second, *the divine election;* third, *the desolation of the disciples;* fourth is inferred *the petition itself.*

13. (Vers. 6.). First, then, is set down or intimated *the diligent conservation of the words of Christ,* which they had in the teaching[^tr-doctrina] of Christ, which was manifested to them; therefore he says: *I have manifested thy name to the men whom thou hast given me out of the world,* that is, whom thou hast called out of the world to me by present justice; above, the sixth chapter[^p470-5]: « No man comes to me, unless the Father draw him ». And the reason why he drew them out of the world is subjoined: *Thine they were,* by eternal election, *and to me thou gavest them,* to be instructed by teaching; Isaias, the eighth chapter[^p470-6]: « Behold, I and my children, whom the Lord hath given me »; the Apostle expounds it of Christ. And they fittingly received it; hence he says: *And they have kept thy word;* of which keeping above, the fourteenth chapter[^p470-7]: « He that hath my commandments and keepeth them; he it is that loveth me ». And how they kept the word or speech of the Father, he shows. For since they neither heard nor saw the Father, how they could have kept his speech someone might doubt; therefore he says that they kept it by believing in the Son.

14. (Vers. 7.). *And*[^p470-8] *now they have known that all things which thou hast given me are from thee,* and therefore they have known thee by knowing me; hence above, the fifth chapter: « The Son cannot do any thing of himself »; above, the fourteenth chapter: « The Father who abideth in me, he doth the works », and therefore « he that seeth me seeth my Father also ». And moreover they had heard the word of the Father; therefore he says:

15. (Vers. 8.). *Because the words which thou gavest me, I have given to them;* above, the fifteenth chapter[^p470-9]: « Whatsoever I have heard of my Father, I have made known to you ». And they kept my words, which I gave them, and therefore thy word; therefore he says: *And they have received them,* by subjecting their understanding, *and have known in very deed that I came out from thee,* according to the divine nature; *and they have believed that thou didst send me,* namely into flesh. And thus they believed and knew the Divinity in Christ and the humanity. And it is to be attended to that he says: *And they have believed that thou didst send me;* and after: *And they have known that I came out from thee.* They said, above, the sixteenth chapter[^p470-10]: « By this we have known that thou camest forth from God »; because through faith in the assumed humanity one arrives at the intelligence of the eternal generation. Hence Augustine[^p470-11] says that those who contemned the humble Christ did not merit to recognize the sublime one. This, then, is the first reason for being heard on their behalf, namely the conservation of the words of Christ; hence above, the fifteenth chapter[^p470-12]: « If you abide in me and my words abide in you, you shall ask whatever you will: and it shall be done unto you ».

16. (Vers. 9.). *And I pray for them.* Here is touched the second reason for being heard on behalf of the Apostles, namely *the divine election* itself; because our Lord himself prayed for the chosen, not for the reprobate. Therefore he says: *I pray for them,* because, namely, they are chosen; therefore he says: *I pray not for the world,* « that is, for those given over to the world[^p470-13] », whom thou hast blinded; *but for them whom thou hast given me,* that is, hast saved through me; Acts, the twenty-seventh chapter, it is expressly said to Paul: « Behold, God hath given thee the souls of all that sail with thee ». Nor by this, that thou hast given them to me, hast thou taken them from thyself: *Because they are thine.*
<!-- page 471 -->

17. (Vers. 10.). *And all my things are thine, and thine are mine*[^p471-1]; above, the sixteenth chapter: « All things which the Father hath are mine », and conversely. Therefore he said, above, the seventh chapter: « My doctrine is not mine »; therefore my servants and my chosen are thy chosen. And this is the second reason for being heard on behalf of the Apostles, namely their election; therefore above, the fifteenth chapter: « You have not chosen me: but I have chosen you; and have appointed you, that you should go and should bring forth fruit; and your fruit should remain: that whatsoever you shall ask of the Father in my name, he may give it you ».

18. *And I*[^p471-2] *glorified.* Here is touched the third reason, namely *the desolation of the disciples* from the withdrawal of Christ, which was drawing near; on account of which drawing near he says that he has been *glorified* and has withdrawn, because it was to be shortly; therefore he says: *And I am glorified in them;* because they had known him and were to know him shortly.

19. (Vers. 11.). *And now I am not in the world;* because, above, the fourteenth chapter[^p471-3], « I will not now speak many things with you »; *and these are in the world, and I come to thee,* and therefore I leave them desolate; above, the sixteenth chapter: « I go to him that sent me; but because I have spoken these things to you, sorrow hath filled your heart ». And this was the third reason for being heard; hence above, the fourteenth chapter: « I go to the Father: and whatsoever you shall ask the Father in my name, that will I do ».

20. *Holy Father.* After the manifold reason for asking has been set down first, there is subjoined here in the fourth place *the petition itself,* by which he asks for them conservation in good; on account of which he says: *Holy Father, keep them in thy name whom thou hast given me,* that is, in the power of the name; above, the tenth chapter[^p471-4]: « No one can snatch them out of the hand of my Father ». *Keep,* I say, in good; hence he says: *That they may be one, as we also are,* namely by a unity of concord and peace, which unity God keeps in the chosen, but nevertheless with their solicitude; therefore to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter[^p471-5]: « Solicitous to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace ».

### Questions.

21. Quaest. I. But it is asked here concerning this, that the Lord here prays for the Apostles;

1. Because above, the sixteenth chapter[^p471-6], it is said: *I say not to you that I will ask the Father for you; for the Father himself loveth you:* therefore he does here the contrary of what he had said.

2. Likewise, the Lord himself prayed for none except for the predestined[^p471-7], of whom he knew that God willed to save them finally; but without his prayer they would be saved: therefore he prayed in vain.

3. Likewise, why did he pray with vocal prayer? What necessity?

**I respond:** It must be said that for a threefold cause the Lord prayed for the Apostles, namely for *the obtaining of salvation, consolation* and *erudition:* because for them he *merited,* because from the hearing of these words they *were consoled,* because from this they *were taught* to pray, and we ourselves; therefore he prayed with vocal prayer[^p471-8].

1. As to what is objected, that above the Lord said: *I say not to you that I will ask the Father* etc.; the Lord did not will to deny that he would pray for them; since it is said to the Hebrews, the ninth chapter[^p471-9], that he *appears in the presence of God to make intercession for us;* but that he would not pray to the Father as to one unwilling, but as to one benevolent to the Apostles themselves.

2. But he asks: to what end does he pray? Augustine says in *On the Predestination of the Saints* that we are not heard except for the predestined, and for those we do not pray in vain, because « perhaps they are predestined, that they may be granted to our prayers[^p471-10] ». So in Christ.

22. Quaest. II. It is asked concerning what he says: *I pray for them, not for the world.*

**On the contrary:** 1. Below in the same[^p471-11]: *That the world may believe that thou hast sent me;* he prayed, therefore, that the world might believe.

2. Likewise, he ought to pray for those for whom he had been sent and was to die; but he died for the
<!-- page 472 -->
world; above, the third chapter[^p472-1]: *God so loved the world, as to give his only begotten Son.*

3. Likewise, for *the world* sinners are understood; but[^p472-2] *they that are whole need not the physician: but they that are sick;* therefore for such most of all prayer ought to be made.

**I respond:** It must be said that *the world* in this prayer is taken in three ways, and on account of this it generates ambiguity, and a fourth mode is added besides. For *the world* is said of the very *globe visible to us;* and thus it is taken above, the first chapter[^p472-3]: *The world was made by him.* Sometimes it is said of *the state of our conversation,* according as it has misery annexed; and thus there: *These are in the world, and I come to thee.* Sometimes, according as it names *the state of the present conversation with fault;* and thus below: *The world hath hated them.* Sometimes, according as it imports *eternal reprobation,* and thus it is taken there: *I pray for them, I pray not for the world.* — In the first mode it says nature; in the second mode, nature and misery; in the third, nature and misery and fault; in the fourth mode, nature and misery and fault and divine reprobation.
