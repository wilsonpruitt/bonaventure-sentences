# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. VI, Vers. 1-9
## *Primo se manifestat conservatorem corporum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 318 -->

### Capitulum VI.

#### Secundo, Filius Dei se manifestat conservatorem.

1. *Post haec abiit Iesus trans mare* etc. In praecedenti capitulo ostendit se Dominus curatorem, in hac parte ostendit se *conservatorem.* Et quia ipse est conservator corporum et spirituum, et per visibilia pervenitur ad invisibilia[^p318-10]; primo ostendit se conservatorem *corporum;* secundo vero, *spirituum,* ibi[^p318-11]: *Altera die turba.*

#### Primo se manifestat conservatorem corporum.

*Conservatorem* igitur *corporum* se ostendit mirabiliter reficiendo corporaliter; quae mirabilis refectio describitur quantum ad tria: primo, quantum ad *antecedentia;* secundo, quantum ad *concomitantia,* ibi[^p318-12]: *Dicit ergo Iesus: Facite* etc.; tertio, quantum ad *consequentia,* ibi: *Illi autem homines, cum vidissent* etc.

*Antecedentia* autem, quae fuerunt causa sive occasio[^p318-13] faciendi miraculum, sunt quatuor: primum, *superveniens multitudo;* secundum vero, *loci et temporis aptitudo;* tertium, *defectus pretii* ad ciborum emptionem; quartum, *insufficientia cibi* ad multitudinis refectionem.

2. (Vers. 1.). Prima igitur occasio faciendi miraculum fuit *superveniens multitudo,* quae secuta est Christum extra patriam suam ad videnda eius miracula. Propter quod dicit: *Post haec*[^p318-14] *abiit Iesus trans mare Galilaeae, quod est Tiberiadis; mare Galilaeae* dicitur a regione, *Tiberiadis* a civitate.

3. (Vers. 2.). *Et sequebatur eum multitudo magna;* et ratio huius subditur: *Quia videbant signa, quae faciebat super his qui infirmabantur.* Propter haec signa videnda sequebantur Christum, quia, sicut dicitur primae ad Corinthios primo[^p318-15], « Iudaei signa quaerunt »; et cum vident signa, magnificant Deum; Lucae quinto: « Repleti sunt timore et ecstasi dicentes: Quia vidimus mirabilia hodie ». Propter haec signa attrahuntur Iudaei et etiam confirmantur; Exodi decimo quarto: « State et videte magnalia Domini, quae facturus est ».
<!-- page 319 -->

4. (Vers. 3.). *Abiit*[^p319-1] *ergo in montem.* Notatur hic secunda occasio, scilicet *loci et temporis aptitudo. Locus* aptus erat ad miraculum faciendum, quia desertus erat, in quo erat refectionis defectus; ideo dicit: *Abiit ergo in montem Iesus et ibi sedebat cum discipulis suis.* Locum, quem Ioannes vocat *montem* propter altitudinem, vocat Matthaeus *desertum* propter solitudinem; Matthaei decimo quarto[^p319-2]: « Secessit Iesus in navicula in locum desertum seorsum ». Et iste locus ad faciendum miracula erat aptus, quia non erat ibi cibus; unde dicitur Matthaei decimo quarto, quod dixerunt discipuli Domino: « Desertus est locus; dimitte turbas, ut euntes in castella emant sibi escas ». — Erat etiam aptitudo *temporis,* quia Pascha; unde dicit:

5. (Vers. 4.). *Erat autem proximum Pascha, dies festus Iudaeorum;* in hoc Dominus fecerat miracula, et illa nunc debebat renovare, quia novum Pascha debebat facere. Notandum, ut dicit Chrysostomus[^p319-3], quod « a Paschate incarcerationis Ioannis usque ad hoc fluxerat annus unus, licet Evangelista pauca narret de hoc », quia alii narraverunt.

6. (Vers. 5.). *Cum sublevasset ergo oculos* etc. Notatur hic tertia occasio, scilicet *defectus pretii* ad emendum cibos, unde reficiatur tanta multitudo; et ad hunc defectum manifestandum quaerit Dominus a Philippo de pretio. Propterea dicit: *Cum sublevasset ergo oculos Iesus,* per misericordiae benignitatem, quia, sicut dicitur in Psalmo[^p319-4], « oculi Domini super timentes eum, et in eis qui sperant super misericordia eius ». *Et vidisset, quia multitudo maxima venit ad eum,* propter devotionem; unde dicitur Matthaei decimo quarto, quod « secutae sunt eum turbae pedestres de civitatibus ». *Dicit*[^p319-5] *ad Philippum,* per providentiae circumspectionem; primae Petri ultimo: « Ipsi cura est de vobis ». *Unde ememus panes, ut manducent hi?* ad corporum refectionem, quasi dicat: Nunquid habemus sufficiens pretium ad emendum? Nec ipse argento indigebat; Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto: « Venite, emite absque argento ». Hoc non quaerebat, quia dubitaret, sed ut Philippum probaret; unde dicit:

7. (Vers. 6.). *Hoc autem dicebat tentans eum,* non dubitans; *ipse enim sciebat, quid esset facturus.* Ideo dixit, ut probaret et probando ostenderet, quod pretium deficeret. Ideo sequitur responsio Philippi.

8. (Vers. 7.). *Respondit ei Philippus: Ducentorum denariorum panes non sufficiunt eis, ut unusquisque modicum quid accipiat;* et ita magnum pretium erat necessarium, et ipsi pauperes erant et non habebant etiam parvum; omnia enim reliquerant, secundum quod dixit Petrus Matthaei decimo nono[^p319-6]: « Ecce, nos reliquimus omnia et secuti sumus te » etc.; et Actuum tertio: « Argentum et aurum non est mihi ».

9. (Vers. 8.). *Dicit ei unus ex discipulis eius* etc. Notatur hic quarta occasio, scilicet *insufficientia cibi* ad tantae multitudinis refectionem. Cum enim essent quinque millia, non habebant panes nisi quinque; et hoc notatur in responsione Andreae. Propterea ait: *Dicit ei unus ex discipulis eius, Andreas, frater Simonis Petri,* Domino scilicet a Philippo quaerenti de turbarum refectione:

10. (Vers. 9.). *Est hic puer unus, qui habet quinque panes hordeaceos et duos pisces;* tot, inquit, panes nos habemus, sed non sufficiunt; ideo dicit: *Sed haec quid sunt inter tantos?* In quo notatur mira inopia Domini et discipulorum, quod paucos panes habebant et viles; unde et frequenter cum Domino passi sunt famem; Matthaei duodecimo[^p319-7]: « Abiit Iesus per sata; discipuli autem eius esurientes coeperunt vellere spicas et manducare ».

11. *Moraliter. Panes quinque,* quos habet puer, sunt quinque refectiones affectus, quae ortum habent ex quinque considerationibus intellectus: ex consideratione *proprii peccati,* panis *compunctionis,* de quo in Psalmo[^p319-8]: *Cibabis nos pane lacrymarum* etc. Iste est panis, de quo Iudicum septimo: *Videbatur mihi quasi panis subcinericius ex hordeo.* — Ex consideratione *passionis Christi,* panis *afflictionis;* Deuteronomii decimo sexto[^p319-9]: *Septem diebus comedes absque fermento afflictionis panem, quoniam in pavore egressus es de Aegypto, ut memineris diei egressionis tuae de Aegypto* etc.; Threnorum tertio: *Recordare paupertatis et transgressionis meae, absinthii et fellis.* — Ex consideratione *defectus fratrum,* panis *compassionis;* Ecclesiastae undecimo[^p319-10]: *Mitte panem super transeuntes aquas,* scilicet tribulationis. Sine hoc nullus debet praefici; Isaiae quarto: *Non sum medicus, et in domo mea non est panis neque vestimentum; nolite constituere me principem populi.* — Ex consideratione *supplicii aeterni,* panis *tremoris;* Tobiae secundo[^p319-11]: *Manducavit panem cum luctu et tremore,* memorans illum sermonem, quem dixit Dominus per Amos prophetam: *Dies festi vestri convertentur in lamentationem et luctum.* — Ex
<!-- page 320 -->
consideratione *dilationis praemii,* panis *devotionis,* qui est in perfectis; Psalmus[^p320-1]: *Fuerunt mihi lacrymae meae panes die ac nocte, dum dicitur mihi quotidie: Ubi est Deus tuus?*

### Quaestiones.

12. Quaest. I. Incidit hic quaestio de *contrarietate* litterae:

1. Quia dicitur Matthaei decimo quarto[^p320-2], quod *discipuli accesserunt ad Dominum* et dixerunt ei, quod turbas dimitteret; et dicitur similiter Matthaei eodem, quod Dominus respondit discipulis: *Non habent necesse ire;* hic dicitur, quod petiit a Philippo consilium eos reficiendi.

2. Item obiicitur, quia dicitur, quod Philippus dicit: *Ducentorum denariorum panes non sufficiunt* etc.; Marci sexto[^p320-3] dicitur, quod discipuli dixerunt: *Euntes emamus ducentis denariis panes, et demus illis manducare.*

**Respondeo** secundum Augustinum[^p320-4], quod contrarietas solvitur ex hoc, quod frequenter unus dicit quod alter omittit. Et intelligendum est, quod discipuli primo iverunt ad Dominum, et post Dominus inspexit turbas et allocutus est Philippum; et tunc Philippus respondit, et alius Evangelista responsionem Philippi attribuit aliis, ut Marcus, per synecdochen. Et deinde Dominus quaesivit, quot panes haberent[^p320-5]; et hoc Ioannes omittit; et respondit Andreas, quod quinque. Et hoc Ioannes dicit de Andrea specialiter; alii attribuunt pluribus, quia in persona plurium loquebatur.

13. Quaest. II. Est etiam quaestio de *ratione* litterae. — Cum Philippus non esset dispensator, sed Iudas; videtur, quod Dominus debuisset a Iuda quaerere, non a Philippo.

Respondet Chrysostomus[^p320-6], quod illa quaestio fuit ad probandum et instruendum, quia Philippus magis indigebat doctrina, sicut patet infra decimo quarto, quando quaesivit: *Ostende nobis Patrem;* ideo magis ab ipso quaesivit. — Aliter potest dici, quod Philippus erat circa illam patriam oriundus; unde et turbis notus erat, et ideo de ipsis magis sollicitus; et ideo credendum, quod ipse primo Dominum de turbis sollicitavit, et post Dominus ab ipso quaesivit.

14. Quaest. III. Est etiam quaestio de *veritate* litterae, quia dicitur hic: *Hoc autem dixit tentans eum.*

**Contra:** 1. Iacobi primo[^p320-7]: *Deus neminem tentat;* unde ibidem: *Deus intentator est.*

2. Item, tentatio est via in peccatum; sed Deus neminem ad peccatum inclinat. — *Si dicas,* quod tentat non ad malum inclinando, sed probando, iuxta illud Psalmi[^p320-8]: *Proba me, Domine, et tenta me;* sed *contra:* Eius est probare vel experimentum sumere, qui hominem non cognoscit; ergo si Deus hominem cognoscit, non tentat nec probat.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod est tentatio *ad malum inclinans,* et haec est a carne, mundo, diabolo, non a Deo; et est tentatio *probans,* et haec potest esse tripliciter: aut ut innotescat *probanti,* aut ut innotescat *aliis,* aut ut probatus innotescat *sibi ipsi.* — Primo modo non tentat Deus, sed homo; Ecclesiastici decimo tertio[^p320-9]: *Ex multis loquelis tentabit te.* Secundo modo tentat Deus virum perfectum ad exemplum; Genesis vigesimo secundo[^p320-10]: *Tentavit Deus Abraham.* Tertio tentat imperfectum ad humilitatis meritum, quia homo humiliatur, dum sibi eius infirmitas innotescit; Psalmus[^p320-11]: *Proba me, Domine, et tenta me.*

## English
<!-- page 318 -->

### Chapter VI.

#### Second, the Son of God manifests himself as preserver.

1. *After these things Jesus went over the sea* etc. In the preceding chapter the Lord showed himself as curer; in this part he shows himself as *preserver.* And because he is the preserver of bodies and of spirits, and through visible things one arrives at invisible things[^p318-10], first he shows himself as preserver *of bodies;* but second, *of spirits,* at the place[^p318-11]: *The next day, the multitude.*

#### First he manifests himself as preserver of bodies.

He shows himself, then, as *preserver of bodies* by wonderfully refreshing them bodily; and this wonderful refection is described as regards three things: first, as regards *the antecedents;* second, as regards *the concomitants,* at the place[^p318-12]: *Then Jesus saith: Make* etc.; third, as regards *the consequents,* at the place: *Now those men, when they had seen* etc.

Now *the antecedents,* which were the cause or occasion[^p318-13] of working the miracle, are four: the first, *the multitude coming upon him;* but the second, *the fitness of the place and of the time;* the third, *the lack of price* for the buying of food; the fourth, *the insufficiency of food* for the refection of the multitude.

2. (Verse 1.). The first occasion, then, of working the miracle was *the multitude coming upon him,* which followed Christ outside his own country to see his miracles. On account of which he says: *After these things*[^p318-14] *Jesus went over the sea of Galilee, which is that of Tiberias; the sea of Galilee* is so called from the region, *of Tiberias* from the city.

3. (Verse 2.). *And a great multitude followed him;* and the reason for this is added: *Because they saw the signs which he did on them that were diseased.* On account of these signs, to see them, they followed Christ, because, as is said in the first to the Corinthians, the first[^p318-15], « The Jews seek signs »; and when they see signs, they magnify God; Luke, the fifth: « They were filled with fear and ecstasy, saying: We have seen wonderful things today ». On account of these signs the Jews are drawn and also confirmed; Exodus, the fourteenth: « Stand and see the great wonders of the Lord, which he will do ».
<!-- page 319 -->

4. (Verse 3.). *He went*[^p319-1] *therefore into a mountain.* Here is noted the second occasion, namely *the fitness of the place and of the time. The place* was fit for working the miracle, because it was desert, a place in which there was a lack of refection; therefore he says: *Jesus therefore went into a mountain, and there he sat with his disciples.* The place which John calls a *mountain* on account of its height, Matthew calls a *desert* on account of its solitude; Matthew, the fourteenth[^p319-2]: « Jesus retired by a boat into a desert place apart ». And this place was fit for working miracles, because there was no food there; whence it is said in Matthew, the fourteenth, that the disciples said to the Lord: « This is a desert place; send away the multitudes, that going into the towns they may buy themselves victuals ». — There was also a fitness *of the time,* because it was the Pasch; whence he says:

5. (Verse 4.). *Now the Pasch, the festival day of the Jews, was near at hand;* on this the Lord had worked miracles, and these he was now to renew, because he was to make a new Pasch. It is to be noted, as Chrysostom[^p319-3] says, that « from the Pasch of John's imprisonment up to this one year had passed, although the Evangelist narrates little of it », because the others narrated it.

6. (Verse 5.). *When Jesus therefore had lifted up his eyes* etc. Here is noted the third occasion, namely *the lack of price* for buying food, with which so great a multitude might be refreshed; and to make this lack manifest the Lord asks Philip about the price. Therefore he says: *When Jesus therefore had lifted up his eyes,* through the kindness of mercy, because, as is said in the Psalm[^p319-4], « the eyes of the Lord are upon them that fear him, and on them that hope in his mercy ». *And seen that a very great multitude cometh to him,* on account of devotion; whence it is said in Matthew, the fourteenth, that « the multitudes followed him on foot out of the cities ». *He saith*[^p319-5] *to Philip,* through the circumspection of providence; the first of Peter, the last: « He hath care of you ». *Whence shall we buy bread, that these may eat?* for the refection of bodies, as if to say: Have we a sufficient price for buying? Nor did he himself need silver; Isaias, the fifty-fifth: « Come, buy without silver ». He did not ask this because he was in doubt, but that he might prove Philip; whence he says:

7. (Verse 6.). *And this he said tempting him,* not doubting; *for he himself knew what he would do.* Therefore he said it, that he might prove him and by proving show that the price was lacking. Therefore Philip's response follows.

8. (Verse 7.). *Philip answered him: Two hundred pennyworth of bread is not sufficient for them, that every one may take a little;* and so a great price was necessary, and they were poor and did not have even a small one; for they had left all things, according to what Peter said in Matthew, the nineteenth[^p319-6]: « Behold, we have left all things and have followed thee » etc.; and Acts, the third: « Silver and gold I have none ».

9. (Verse 8.). *One of his disciples saith to him* etc. Here is noted the fourth occasion, namely *the insufficiency of food* for the refection of so great a multitude. For since they were five thousand, they had no more than five loaves; and this is noted in Andrew's response. Therefore he says: *One of his disciples, Andrew, the brother of Simon Peter, saith to him,* that is, to the Lord, who was asking Philip about the refection of the multitudes:

10. (Verse 9.). *There is a boy here that hath five barley loaves and two fishes;* so many loaves, he says, we have, but they do not suffice; therefore he says: *But what are these among so many?* In which is noted the wonderful want of the Lord and of the disciples, that they had few loaves, and mean ones; whence they also often suffered hunger with the Lord; Matthew, the twelfth[^p319-7]: « Jesus went through the corn; and his disciples being hungry began to pluck the ears and to eat ».

11. *Morally. The five loaves* which the boy has are five refections of the affection, which have their origin from five considerations of the understanding: from the consideration *of one's own sin,* the bread *of compunction,* of which in the Psalm[^p319-8]: *Thou wilt feed us with the bread of tears* etc. This is the bread of which in Judges, the seventh: *It seemed to me as it were a hearth cake of barley.* — From the consideration *of the passion of Christ,* the bread *of affliction;* Deuteronomy, the sixteenth[^p319-9]: *Seven days shalt thou eat without leaven the bread of affliction, because thou camest out of Egypt in fear, that thou mayst remember the day of thy coming out of Egypt* etc.; Lamentations, the third: *Remember my poverty and transgression, the wormwood and the gall.* — From the consideration *of the defect of the brethren,* the bread *of compassion;* Ecclesiastes, the eleventh[^p319-10]: *Cast thy bread upon the running waters,* namely of tribulation. Without this no one ought to be set over others; Isaias, the fourth: *I am no healer, and in my house there is no bread nor clothing; make me not ruler of the people.* — From the consideration *of the eternal penalty,* the bread *of trembling;* Tobias, the second[^p319-11]: *He ate bread with mourning and trembling,* remembering that word which the Lord spoke by Amos the prophet: *Your festival days shall be turned into lamentation and mourning.* — From the
<!-- page 320 -->
consideration *of the delay of the reward,* the bread *of devotion,* which is in the perfect; the Psalm[^p320-1]: *My tears have been my bread day and night, whilst it is said to me daily: Where is thy God?*

### Questions.

12. Question I. Here there falls in a question about the *contrariety* of the letter:

1. Because it is said in Matthew, the fourteenth[^p320-2], that *the disciples came to the Lord* and said to him that he should send away the multitudes; and it is likewise said in Matthew in the same place that the Lord answered the disciples: *They have no need to go;* here it is said that he sought counsel from Philip on refreshing them.

2. Likewise it is objected, because it is said that Philip says: *Two hundred pennyworth of bread is not sufficient* etc.; in Mark, the sixth[^p320-3], it is said that the disciples said: *Let us go and buy bread for two hundred pence, and let us give them to eat.*

**I respond** according to Augustine[^p320-4], that the contrariety is solved from this, that often one says what another omits. And it is to be understood that the disciples first went to the Lord, and afterwards the Lord looked upon the multitudes and addressed Philip; and then Philip answered, and another Evangelist attributes Philip's response to others, as Mark does, by synecdoche. And next the Lord asked how many loaves they had[^p320-5]; and this John omits; and Andrew answered that there were five. And this John says of Andrew specially; the others attribute it to several, because he was speaking in the person of several.

13. Question II. There is also a question about the *reason* of the letter. — Since Philip was not the steward, but Judas, it seems that the Lord ought to have asked of Judas, not of Philip.

Chrysostom[^p320-6] answers that that question was for proving and for instructing, because Philip was more in need of teaching[^tr-doctrina], as is clear below, in the fourteenth, when he asked: *Shew us the Father;* therefore he asked of him rather. — Otherwise it can be said that Philip was a native of that country round about; whence he was also known to the multitudes, and therefore the more solicitous about them; and therefore it is to be believed that he first solicited the Lord about the multitudes, and afterwards the Lord asked of him.

14. Question III. There is also a question about the *truth* of the letter, because it is said here: *And this he said tempting him.*

**On the contrary:** 1. James, the first[^p320-7]: *God tempteth no man;* whence in the same place: *God is not a tempter.*

2. Likewise, temptation is a way into sin; but God inclines no one to sin. — *If you say* that he tempts not by inclining to evil but by proving, according to that of the Psalm[^p320-8]: *Prove me, O Lord, and tempt me;* on the *contrary:* It belongs to one who does not know a man to prove him or to make trial of him; therefore if God knows man, he neither tempts nor proves.

**I respond:** It must be said that there is a temptation *inclining to evil,* and this is from the flesh, the world, the devil, not from God; and there is a temptation *that proves,* and this can be in three ways: either that it become known *to the one proving,* or that it become known *to others,* or that the one proved become known *to himself.* — In the first mode God does not tempt, but man does; Ecclesiasticus, the thirteenth[^p320-9]: *By many speeches he will tempt thee.* In the second mode God tempts a perfect man for an example; Genesis, the twenty-second[^p320-10]: *God tempted Abraham.* In the third he tempts the imperfect for the merit of humility, because a man is humbled while his infirmity becomes known to him; the Psalm[^p320-11]: *Prove me, O Lord, and tempt me.*
