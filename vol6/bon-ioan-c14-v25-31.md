# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XIV, Vers. 25-31
## *Haec est tertia pars capituli, in qua, iam facta exhortatione, praedicit recessus sui accelerationem, ut, cum factum fuerit, credant*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 443 -->

39. *Haec locutus sum vobis* etc. Haec est tertia pars capituli[^p443-13], in qua, iam facta exhortatione, *praedicit recessus sui accelerationem,* ut, cum factum fuerit, credant. Et ne de hoc desolentur, non statim proponitur, sed praemittitur *consolatio* et subiungitur *acceleratio,* ibi[^p443-14]: *Nunc dixi vobis* etc.
<!-- page 444 -->

Et *consolationem* eis praebet multipliciter: primo, ex *Spiritus sancti exspectatione;* secundo, ex *pacis donatione;* tertio, ex *visitationis promissione;* quarto, ex *suae exaltationis congratulatione.*

(Vers. 25.). Primo ergo ponit consolationem ex *Spiritus sancti exspectatione;* propter quod dicit: *Haec locutus sum vobis, apud vos manens;* Lucae ultimo[^p444-1]: « Haec sunt verba, quae locutus sum ad vos, cum adhuc essem vobiscum »; nec debetis turbari, si non intelligitis, quia intelligetis in adventu Spiritus sancti; unde:

40. (Vers. 26.). *Paracletus autem Spiritus*[^p444-2]*, quem mittet Pater in nomine meo,* id est, in fide nominis mei, quia non nisi credentibus in Christum datus est; supra septimo: « Qui credit in me, sicut dicit Scriptura, flumina de ventre eius fluent aquae vivae »; Actuum quarto: « Nec enim est aliud nomen sub caelo datum hominibus, in quo oporteat nos salvos fieri ». *Ille,* inquam, Spiritus *docebit vos omnia et suggeret vobis omnia, quaecumque dixero vobis;* Sapientiae septimo[^p444-3]: « Omnium artifex docuit me sapientia; est enim in illa spiritus intelligentiae, sanctus ».

41. (Vers. 27.). *Pacem relinquo vobis.* Secundo consolatur ex *pacis datione*[^p444-4] seu donatione, in qua magna est consolatio; propter quod dicit: *Pacem relinquo vobis, pacem meam do vobis;* ingeminat pacem, ut innuat pacem *gratiae* et pacem *gloriae;* unde Augustinus[^p444-5]: « Pacem suam nobis *relinquit,* in qua manentes hostes vincimus; pacem suam *dat* nobis, quando sine hoste regnabimus »; Isaiae vigesimo septimo: « Faciet mihi Dominus pacem, pacem faciet mihi ». Hanc dat observantibus mandata; Isaiae quadragesimo octavo: « Utinam attendisses mandata mea; facta fuisset sicut flumen pax tua ». Exponit quod dicit *meam;* quia discretive dictum est *meam*[^p444-6], scilicet non mundi; et ideo subiungit: *Non quomodo mundus dat, ego do vobis;* quia mundus dat *carnaliter,* ego do *spiritualiter;* mundus dat *temporaliter,* ego autem *aeternaliter;* mundus *exterius,* ego vero *interius.* Propterea scribitur ad Philippenses ultimo[^p444-7]: « Pax Dei, quae exsuperat omnem sensum, custodiat corda vestra et intelligentias vestras in Christo Iesu ». Pax mundi custodit corpora et possessiones; de qua Matthaei decimo: « Non veni pacem mittere, sed gladium ».

*Non turbetur cor vestrum.* Hic iam tertio consolatur[^p444-8] ex *visitationis promissione,* quam etiam supra promiserat; propter quod dicit: *Non turbetur cor vestrum neque formidet;* et ecce ratio:

42. (Vers. 28.). *Audistis, quia ego dixi vobis: Vado et venio ad vos;* supra eodem[^p444-9]: « Et si abiero » etc.; infra decimo sexto: « Iterum videbo vos, et gaudebit cor vestrum, et gaudium vestrum nemo tollet a vobis ».

*Si diligeretis me.* Hic iam quarto consolatur ex *suae exaltationis congratulatione;* debent enim eius exaltationi congratulari, si eum diligunt; propter quod dicit: *Si diligeretis me, gauderetis utique, quia vado ad Patrem;* quia in hoc itinere est mea exaltatio. Unde subdit: *Quia Pater maior me est,* et ita, cum vado sedere ad dexteram Patris, ego exaltor; de qua exaltatione ad Hebraeos primo[^p444-10]: « Sedet ad dexteram Maiestatis in excelsis, tanto melior Angelis effectus » etc. Et de hac exaltatione caritas congaudet; unde ad Romanos duodecimo: « Gaudere cum gaudentibus » etc.

43. *Et nunc dixi vobis.* Postquam praemissa est consolatio, subiungitur hic *recessus acceleratio.* Et primo notatur *ratio praedicendi;* secundo, *ratio accelerandi;* tertio, *signum accelerationis.*

(Vers. 29.). Notatur ergo primo *ratio, quare praedicit,* scilicet ad fidei robur; propter quod dicit: *Nunc*[^p444-11] *dixi vobis, priusquam fiat, ut, cum factum fuerit, credatis;* quia illud firmavit fidem eorum, quia viderunt ita evenire, sicut praedixerat. Si enim non praedixisset, dubitassent, utrum hoc voluntarie pertulisset. Unde post resurrectionem haec ad memoriam reducebat; Lucae ultimo[^p444-12]: « Haec sunt verba, quae locutus sum ad vos, cum adhuc essem vobiscum, quoniam oportet impleri, quaecumque scripta sunt de me ». Et: « Quoniam scriptum est, et sic oportebat Christum pati » etc.

44. (Vers. 30.). *Iam non multa.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *accelerandi ratio,* et haec *diaboli infestatio* et *divina voluntas:* propter *infestationem diaboli,* qui armabat satellites ad comprehendendum Christum; ideo dicit: *Iam non multa loquar vobiscum; venit enim princeps mundi huius,* id est, diabolus accelerat. Diabolus veniebat in suis satellitibus, sed tamen hoc non cogit: Quia *in me non habet quidquam;* non habebat quidquam, quod
<!-- page 445 -->
exigat nec quod reprehendat, quia non habebat peccatum, et ipse principatur in peccatoribus; unde Iob quadragesimo primo[^p445-1]: « Ipse est rex super omnes filios superbiae ». Unde est princeps tenebrarum; ad Ephesios sexto: « Adversus mundi rectores tenebrarum harum »; et in Christo nulla tenebra, quia ipse « erat lux vera, quae illuminat omnem hominem venientem in hunc mundum ». — Sed alia ratio, *divina voluntas,* magis trahebat; ideo dicit:

45. (Vers. 31.). *Sed ut cognoscat mundus, quia diligo Patrem,* ideo morior ad eius imperium; unde: *Sicut*[^p445-2] *mandatum dedit mihi Pater, sic facio.* De hoc mandato supra decimo: « Hoc mandatum accepi a Patre meo ». Hoc mandatum dedit ei Deus Pater, non quia meruisset ipse, sed pro nostra salute; unde secundae ad Corinthios quinto[^p445-3]: « Eum qui non novit peccatum, pro nobis peccatum fecit » Deus Pater, « ut nos efficeremur iustitia Dei in ipso ». Hoc autem fecit pro nostra dilectione; supra tertio[^p445-4]: « Sic Deus dilexit mundum, ut Filium suum unigenitum daret ».

*Surgite, eamus.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *signum accelerationis recessus,* quod est in hoc, quod discipulos suos monet inde discedere; propter quod dicit: *Surgite, eamus hinc,* in quo ostendit, se voluntarie pati. Simile dicitur Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p445-5]: « Surgite, eamus; ecce, appropinquavit qui me tradet ».

### Quaestiones.

46. Quaest. I. Sed potest hic quaeri primo de hoc quod dicit, quod *Spiritus sanctus suggeret omnia,* quae Filius dixerat; *suggerere* enim est inferioris.

Ad hoc respondet Gregorius[^p445-6]: « Quod Spiritus sanctus *suggerere* dicitur, non quod scientiam ab imo inferat, sed quia ab occulto ».

47. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Gauderetis utique, quia vado ad Patrem, quia Pater maior me est.*

1. Ex hoc arguit Arius, quod Filius est minor Patre. — *Si tu dicas,* hoc esse dictum secundum humanam naturam; tunc pari ratione deberet dicere, quod *ego* maior me ipso sum.

2. Item Hilarius[^p445-7] dicit, quod Pater est *maior* Filio secundum divinam naturam; et tunc arguitur *a relativis,* quod Filius est *minor* Patre: ergo non aequalis.

Responsio ad hoc est per regulam Augustini in libro primo de Trinitate[^p445-8], quia omnia, quae dicuntur de Filio *aequalitatem* ostendentia, referuntur ad divinam naturam, quae vero *inaequalitatem,* ad humanam; et sic in proposito intelligendum. Unde Augustinus: « Humanae naturae congratulandum est, eo quod sic assumta est a Verbo unigenito, ut immortalis constitueretur in caelo, atque ita fieret terra sublimis, ut incorruptibilis pulvis sederet ad dexteram Patris ». Unde secundum determinationem concedendum est, quod Filius, secundum quod Deus, est maior se ipso, secundum quod homo.

2. Quod vero obiicit de Hilario, dicendum, quod Hilarius accipit hoc quod est *maior,* non secundum quod dicit *excessum magnitudinis* potentiae vel virtutis, sed secundum quod dicit *auctoritatem.* Et quia Pater habet auctoritatem respectu Filii sicut principium; ideo dicit, quod maior est Filio. Tamen *minor* non dicitur Filius, quia est nomen importans imperfectionem[^p445-9].

48. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Nunc dixi vobis, priusquam fiat, ut, cum factum fuerit, credatis.*

1. Ergo videtur adhuc, quod Apostoli non haberent fidem, quia ideo praedicebat eis, ut crederent.
<!-- page 446 -->

2. Item, « fides non habet meritum, cui humana ratio praebet experimentum[^p446-1] »; si ergo praedixit eis; ex quo viderunt evenire quod dixerat, experiebantur esse verum: ergo erant vacui a merito fidei.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum est, quod Apostoli habebant fidem, sed tamen non plene explicitam nec etiam perfecte firmam, ut patet, quia nullo modo volebant credere resurrectionem[^p446-2]. — Dicendum est ergo, quod Dominus praedicebat eis passionem suam, ut iam illam crederent, de qua nihil prius sciebant. Dicebat etiam ad robur fidei, ut, videntes passionem et vacillantes, rememorarentur, quod volens et ut Deus passus est. Unde aliud *videbant* et aliud *credebant,* quia *videbant,* quod homo passus fuerat, et *credebant,* quod volens pati, ut Deus animam suam posuerat[^p446-3]. Et huius *signum* fuit sua praedictio, sed non *cogens ratio,* quae auferret meritum.

49. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod diabolus non habebat in Christum iurisdictionem aliquam.

1. Ergo videtur, quod Dominus iniuste fecerit, quod permisit, eum a diabolo opprimi.

2. Item dicit, quod hoc facit propter mandatum Patris: ergo si debebat obedire, ergo moriebatur ex debito, non ex mera liberalitate[^p446-4].

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod aliquem *nolentem* puniri pro culpa *sua,* hoc est *irae et severitatis*[^p446-5]*;* aliquem *volentem* puniri pro culpa *sua,* hoc est *iustitiae et veritatis;* aliquem *volentem* pro culpa *aliena,* hoc *mirae dispensationis et dignationis.* — Quia ergo Christus punitus et volens punitus pro nostra salute; placuit *ponere in eo iniquitates omnium nostrum,* et pro nostris sceleribus ipsum puniri. *Oblatus est, quia ipse voluit*[^p446-6]*;* et ideo nulla iniustitia ex parte Dei facta fuit, sed ex parte diaboli. Et ideo, quia iniuste usurpavit dominium supra Christum, hoc ipso amisit iuste dominium, quod habebat in membris Christi[^p446-7].

2. Ad illud quod obiicit, quod debuit mori; dicendum, quod est debitum *meriti* et debitum *praecepti.* Peccator debet mori, quia meruit; sed Christus, quia ei Pater praecepit. Hoc autem debitum non repugnat liberalitati, quando fit ex amore; et sic fecit Christus, et ideo dicit[^p446-8]: *Ut cognoscat mundus, quia diligo Patrem.*

## English
<!-- page 443 -->

39. *These things have I spoken to you* etc. This is the third part of the chapter[^p443-13], in which, the exhortation having now been made, he *foretells the hastening of his withdrawal,* so that, when it has come to pass, they may believe. And lest they be made desolate over this, it is not put forward at once, but *consolation* is set first and the *hastening* is subjoined, at[^p443-14]: *Now I have told you* etc.
<!-- page 444 -->

And he affords them *consolation* in manifold ways: first, from the *expectation of the Holy Spirit;* second, from the *donation of peace;* third, from the *promise of visitation;* fourth, from the *congratulation on his exaltation.*

(Vers. 25.). First, then, he sets down consolation from the *expectation of the Holy Spirit;* on account of which he says: *These things have I spoken to you, abiding with you;* Luke, the last[^p444-1]: « These are the words which I spoke to you, while I was yet with you »; nor ought you to be troubled, if you do not understand, because you will understand at the coming of the Holy Spirit; hence:

40. (Vers. 26.). *But the Paraclete, the Spirit*[^p444-2]*, whom the Father will send in my name,* that is, in the faith of my name, because he was not given except to those believing in Christ; above, the seventh: « He that believeth in me, as the scripture saith, Out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water »; the Acts, the fourth: « For there is no other name under heaven given to men, whereby we must be saved ». *He,* I say, the Spirit, *will teach you all things, and will suggest to you all things, whatsoever I shall have said to you;* Wisdom, the seventh[^p444-3]: « For wisdom, which is the worker of all things, taught me; for in her is the spirit of understanding, holy ».

41. (Vers. 27.). *Peace I leave with you.* Second he consoles from the *giving of peace*[^p444-4] or donation, in which there is great consolation; on account of which he says: *Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you;* he redoubles peace, that he may intimate the peace of *grace* and the peace of *glory;* hence Augustine[^p444-5]: « His peace he *leaves* us, in which, remaining, we conquer enemies; his peace he *gives* us, when we shall reign without an enemy »; Isaias, the twenty-seventh: « The Lord shall make peace with me, peace shall he make with me ». This he gives to those who observe the commandments; Isaias, the forty-eighth: « O that thou hadst hearkened to my commandments; thy peace had been as a river ». He explains what he says, *my;* because *my*[^p444-6] is said discretively, namely, not the world's; and therefore he subjoins: *Not as the world giveth, do I give unto you;* because the world gives *carnally,* I give *spiritually;* the world gives *temporally,* but I *eternally;* the world *exteriorly,* but I *interiorly.* Therefore it is written to the Philippians, the last[^p444-7]: « The peace of God, which surpasseth all understanding, keep your hearts and minds in Christ Jesus ». The peace of the world keeps bodies and possessions; of which Matthew, the tenth: « I came not to send peace, but the sword ».

*Let not your heart be troubled.* Here now third he consoles[^p444-8] from the *promise of visitation,* which he had also promised above; on account of which he says: *Let not your heart be troubled, nor let it be afraid;* and behold the reason:

42. (Vers. 28.). *You have heard that I said to you: I go away, and I come unto you;* above, the same[^p444-9]: « And if I shall go » etc.; below, the sixteenth: « I will see you again, and your heart shall rejoice; and your joy no man shall take from you ».

*If you loved me.* Here now fourth he consoles from the *congratulation on his exaltation;* for they ought to congratulate him on his exaltation, if they love him; on account of which he says: *If you loved me, you would indeed be glad, because I go to the Father;* because in this journey is my exaltation. Hence he adds: *For the Father is greater than I,* and so, when I go to sit at the right hand of the Father, I am exalted; of which exaltation to the Hebrews, the first[^p444-10]: « He sitteth on the right hand of the Majesty on high, being made so much better than the Angels » etc. And in this exaltation charity rejoices with him; hence to the Romans, the twelfth: « Rejoice with them that rejoice » etc.

43. *And now I have told you.* After the consolation has been set first, here is subjoined the *hastening of the withdrawal.* And first is noted the *reason for foretelling;* second, the *reason for hastening;* third, the *sign of the hastening.*

(Vers. 29.). First, then, is noted the *reason why he foretells,* namely, for the strength of faith; on account of which he says: *Now*[^p444-11] *I have told you before it come to pass: that when it shall come to pass, you may believe;* because that made firm their faith, because they saw it turn out just as he had foretold. For if he had not foretold it, they would have doubted whether he had borne this voluntarily. Hence after the resurrection he brought these things back to memory; Luke, the last[^p444-12]: « These are the words which I spoke to you, while I was yet with you, that all things must needs be fulfilled, which are written concerning me ». And: « Because it is written, and thus it behoved Christ to suffer » etc.

44. (Vers. 30.). *Now not many things.* Here the second is touched, namely the *reason for hastening,* and this is the *infestation of the devil* and the *divine will:* on account of the *infestation of the devil,* who was arming his satellites to seize Christ; therefore he says: *I will not now speak many things with you; for the prince of this world cometh,* that is, the devil hastens. The devil came in his satellites, but yet this does not compel: Because *in me he hath not any thing;* he had not anything which
<!-- page 445 -->
he could exact nor which he could reprehend, because he had no sin, and he himself is prince among sinners; hence Job, the forty-first[^p445-1]: « He is king over all the children of pride ». Hence he is the prince of darkness; to the Ephesians, the sixth: « Against the rulers of the world of this darkness »; and in Christ there is no darkness, because he « was the true light, which enlighteneth every man that cometh into this world ». — But another reason, the *divine will,* drew him more; therefore he says:

45. (Vers. 31.). *But that the world may know, that I love the Father,* therefore I die at his command; hence: *As*[^p445-2] *the Father hath given me commandment, so do I.* Of this commandment above, the tenth: « This commandment have I received of my Father ». This commandment God the Father gave him, not because he himself had merited it, but for our salvation; hence the second to the Corinthians, the fifth[^p445-3]: « Him, who knew no sin, for us hath made sin » God the Father, « that we might be made the justice of God in him ». And this he did out of dilection for us; above, the third[^p445-4]: « God so loved the world, as to give his only begotten Son ».

*Arise, let us go.* Here the third is touched, namely the *sign of the hastening of the withdrawal,* which is in this, that he admonishes his disciples to depart thence; on account of which he says: *Arise, let us go hence,* in which he shows that he suffers voluntarily. The like is said in Matthew, the twenty-sixth[^p445-5]: « Rise, let us go; behold, he is at hand that will betray me ».

### Questions.

46. Quaest. I. But it can be asked here first concerning this which he says, that *the Holy Spirit will suggest all things,* which the Son had said; for *to suggest* belongs to an inferior.

To this Gregory responds[^p445-6]: « That the Holy Spirit is said *to suggest,* not because he brings in knowledge from below, but because from what is hidden ».

47. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked concerning this which he says: *You would indeed be glad, because I go to the Father, for the Father is greater than I.*

1. From this Arius argues that the Son is less than the Father. — *If thou say* that this is said according to the human nature; then by parity of reason he ought to say that *I* am greater than myself.

2. Likewise Hilary[^p445-7] says that the Father is *greater* than the Son according to the divine nature; and then it is argued *from relatives* that the Son is *less* than the Father: therefore not equal.

The response to this is by the rule of Augustine in the first book *On the Trinity*[^p445-8], that all things which are said of the Son showing *equality* are referred to the divine nature, but those showing *inequality,* to the human; and so it is to be understood in the matter at hand. Hence Augustine: « Human nature is to be congratulated, for this reason, that it was so assumed by the only begotten Word, that it was constituted immortal in heaven, and so earth became exalted, that incorruptible dust should sit at the right hand of the Father ». Hence, according to a determination, it is to be granted that the Son, according as he is God, is greater than himself, according as he is man.

2. But as to what he objects concerning Hilary, it must be said that Hilary takes this which is *greater,* not according as it says an *excess of magnitude* of power or virtue, but according as it says *authority.* And because the Father has authority in respect of the Son as principle; therefore he says that he is greater than the Son. Yet the Son is not called *less,* because it is a name importing imperfection[^p445-9].

48. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked concerning this which he says: *Now I have told you before it come to pass: that when it shall come to pass, you may believe.*

1. Therefore it seems further that the Apostles did not have faith, because he foretold to them for this, that they might believe.
<!-- page 446 -->

2. Likewise, « faith has no merit where human reason supplies the experience[^p446-1] »; if, then, he foretold to them; from the fact that they saw come to pass what he had said, they experienced it to be true: therefore they were empty of the merit of faith.

**I respond:** It must be said that the Apostles had faith, but yet not fully explicit nor even perfectly firm, as is evident, because in no way did they wish to believe the resurrection[^p446-2]. — It must be said, then, that the Lord foretold his passion to them, that they might now believe it, of which they knew nothing before. He said it also for the strength of faith, that, seeing the passion and wavering, they might remember that he suffered willing and as God. Hence one thing they *saw* and another they *believed,* because they *saw* that a man had suffered, and they *believed* that, willing to suffer, as God he had laid down his soul[^p446-3]. And of this the *sign* was his prediction, but not a *compelling reason,* which would take away merit.

49. Quaest. IV. Likewise it is asked concerning this which he says, that the devil had no jurisdiction over Christ.

1. Therefore it seems that the Lord acted unjustly, in that he permitted him to be oppressed by the devil.

2. Likewise he says that he does this on account of the commandment of the Father: therefore if he was bound to obey, therefore he was dying from debt, not from mere liberality[^p446-4].

**I respond:** It must be said that someone *unwilling* to be punished for his *own* fault, this is of *anger and severity*[^p446-5]*;* someone *willing* to be punished for his *own* fault, this is of *justice and truth;* someone *willing* for *another's* fault, this of *wonderful dispensation and condescension.* — Because, then, Christ was punished and, willing, was punished for our salvation; it pleased *to lay on him the iniquities of us all,* and that he be punished for our crimes. *He was offered because it was his own will*[^p446-6]*;* and therefore no injustice was done on the part of God, but on the part of the devil. And therefore, because he unjustly usurped dominion over Christ, by this very thing he justly lost the dominion which he had in the members of Christ[^p446-7].

2. To that which he objects, that he was bound to die; it must be said that there is a debt of *merit* and a debt of *precept.* A sinner is bound to die, because he merited it; but Christ, because the Father commanded him. But this debt is not repugnant to liberality, when it is done out of love; and so Christ did, and therefore he says[^p446-8]: *That the world may know, that I love the Father.*
