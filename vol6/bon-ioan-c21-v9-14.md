# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XXI, Vers. 9-14
## *Manifestat hic veritatem humanitatis per comestionem*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 522 -->

18. *Ut ergo descenderunt in terram.* Sic manifestata maiestate Divinitatis per mirabilem piscium comprehensionem, manifestat hic *veritatem humanitatis* per comestionem; et in hac quidem procedit hoc ordine. Primo notatur *Domini providentia* ad cibos parandum; secundo, *benignitas* ad invitandum; tertio, *discipulorum illuminatio*[^p522-3] ad cognoscendum; quarto, *Domini munificentia* ad cibaria distribuendum.

19. (Vers. 9.). *Providentia* igitur ad cibaria praeparandum innuitur in ciborum praeparatione, quae est facta miraculo; unde dicit: *Ut ergo descenderunt in terram, viderunt prunas positas et piscem superpositum et panem,* et ita prandium, ad quod invitabat, paratum erat; Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p522-4]: « Ecce, prandium paravi, et omnia parata ». Et haec quidem miraculo erant; unde Chrysostomus: « Non adhuc, ut ante crucem, ex materia supposita operatur, sed mirabilius ducit signa, ostendens, quoniam quod tunc ex subiecta materia faciebat propter quandam dispensationem faciebat ». Vult dicere, quod ex nihilo factus est ille piscis. Nec tantum praeparavit miraculo, sed etiam praeparatio facta est *Petri ministerio* ad divinum praeceptum; unde sequitur:

20. (Vers. 10.). *Dicit eis Iesus: Afferte de piscibus, quos prendidistis nunc;* ut miraculo veritas comprobetur.

21. (Vers. 11.). *Ascendit autem*[^p522-5] *Simon Petrus,* tanquam diligentior inter alios, *et traxit rete in terram, plenum magnis piscibus, centum quinquaginta tribus;* in quo ostenditur miraculi magnitudo et in numero et in quantitate. Per *magnos pisces* intelligimus Beatos, qui magni sunt caritate; Matthaei undecimo[^p522-6]: « Qui minor est in regno caelorum maior est illo ». Et ad maiorem miraculi expressionem addit quod factum est circa rete; unde dicit: *Et cum tanti essent, non est scissum rete;* quocontra dicitur Lucae quinto[^p522-7]: « Comprehenderunt piscium multitudinem copiosam, rumpebatur autem rete ». Itaque, *cum tanti essent,* scilicet « numero et quantitate », in tali numero fuerunt propter significantiam: vel quia numerus ille *perfectionem* significat, considerata proportione numerali[^p522-8];
<!-- page 523 -->
vel quia *universitatem,* quia, sicut dicit Hieronymus[^p523-1], « tot genera piscium in mari esse, a philosophis descripta leguntur ».

22. (Vers. 12.). *Dicit eis Iesus.* Hic notatur secundum, scilicet *Domini benignitas* ad invitandum discipulos ad cibum; ideo dicit: *Dicit eis Iesus: Venite, prandete;* invitat ad *prandium,* sed in futura resurrectione invitabit ad *coenam,* secundum illud Apocalypsis decimo nono[^p523-2]: « Beati, qui ad coenam nuptiarum Agni vocati sunt ». Invitat nunc ad cibum *corporalem,* ut innuat, quod invitat ad *spiritualem;* Canticorum quinto: « Comedite, amici, et bibite et inebriamini, carissimi »; et Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto: « Omnes sitientes, venite ad aquas, et qui non habetis argentum, properate, emite et comedite; venite, emite absque argento et absque ulla commutatione vinum et lac ».

23. *Et nemo discumbentium.* Hic notatur tertium, scilicet *illuminatio discipulorum* ad Dominum cognoscendum; unde dicit: *Et nemo discumbentium,* id est ad comedendum residentium, *audebat eum interrogare: Tu quis es?* Unde Chrysostomus[^p523-3]: « Cum silentio et reverentia multa sedebant, attendentes in eum ». *Scientes, quia Dominus est,* id est, propterea non interrogabant eum: tu quis es? ad Hebraeos penultimo[^p523-4]: « Serviamus placentes Deo cum metu et reverentia »; ad Philippenses secundo: » Cum metu et tremore salutem vestram operamini ».

24. (Vers. 13.). *Et venit Iesus.* Hic notatur quartum, scilicet *Domini munificentia* ad cibaria distribuendum; unde dicit: *Et accepit panem et dabat*[^p523-5] *illis et piscem similiter,* dabat illis, ut comederent. Et similiter intelligendum, quod ipse cum eis *comederit;* unde Chrysostomus[^p523-6]: « Hic quidem non dicit, quod comedit cum eis; Lucas alibi ait, Lucae ultimo: Cum manducasset coram eis, sumens reliquias, dedit eis »; unde Gregorius[^p523-7] dicit: « Assum piscem Dominus comedit et panem ». Christus *dabat,* in quo se gratiarum omnium liberalissimum distributorom significabat; Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p523-8]: « Unicuique dedit secundum propriam virtutem ».

25. (Vers. 14.). *Hoc iam tertio.* Posita manifestatione, ponitur *manifestationis ad praecedentes comparatio*[^p523-9]; et computatur cum praecedentibus *tertia,* non quia plures non fuerint, sed quia sufficit istas tres scribere ad credendum, secundum illud Deuteronomii decimo nono: « In ore duorum, vel trium testium stat omne verbum ». Ideo dicit. *Hoc iam tertio manifestatus est Iesus discipulis suis, cum resurrexisset a mortuis,* quasi sufficiens sit trinam manifestationem explicasse; Actuum decimo[^p523-10]: « Dedit, eum manifestum fieri, non omni populo, sed testibus praeordinatis a Deo, nobis, qui manducavimus et bibimus cum illo, postquam resurrexit a mortuis ».
<!-- page 524 -->

### Quaestiones.

26. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod dicit: *Cum tanti essent, non est scissum rete.* — Cum in prima piscatione, Lucae quinto[^p524-1], dicatur, quod *rumpebatur rete* propter multitudinem piscium copiosam; quid est, quod nunc integrum perseverat?

Respondet Gregorius[^p524-2]: « In alia piscatione prae multitudine piscium rete rumpitur, quia nunc ad confessionem fidei cum electis reprobi intrant, qui ipsam Ecclesiam haeresibus scindunt. In hac vero piscatione rete non rumpitur, quia sancta electorum Ecclesia, in continua pace requiescens, nullis dissensionibus laceratur ».

27. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc, quod Dominus *comedit* piscem et panem, secundum quod dicitur Lucae ultimo[^p524-3]; cum sit tantum corporis animalis: ergo aut Christus habuit corpus animale, aut phantastice comedit.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod est *comedere ex necessitate,* et hoc est corporis animalis tantum; et est *comedere ex potestate,* et hoc non tantum animalis, sed etiam gloriosi et spiritualis, quod pure ex potestate comedit. Corporibus enim glorificatis non aufertur *potestas,* sed *necessitas*[^p524-4].

28. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur: quid fiebat de illo cibo? Cum enim corpus glorificatum non nutriretur, videtur, quod haberet egressum naturae consuetum.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod sicut aqua in ignem proiecta statim ab igne absorbetur, sic cibus a corpore glorificato susceptus statim consumitur virtute quadam spirituali. Unde Augustinus in libro de solutione Sex Quaestionum[^p524-5]: « Aliter absorbet aquam terra sitiens, aliter solis radius calens: illa *indigentia,* iste *potentia.* Futurae ergo resurrectionis corpus imperfectae felicitatis erat, si cibis eguerit; imperfectae felicitatis erit, si cibos sumere non poterit ». Et ratio huius est, quia in primo *indigens,* in secundo *impotens.*

29. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicitur: *Hoc iam tertio manifestavit se Iesus* etc. — Propter quid Dominus interpolate discipulis se manifestabat et non erat cum eis continue usque ad ascensionem?

Et respondet Chrysostomus[^p524-6], quod « subtraxit se ab aspectu illorum aliquando, ut avidius quaereretur, et quaesitus amaretur, et amatus insignius et devotius veneraretur ». — Alia ratio: quia discipuli, quamdiu fuit cum eis Dominus, eum carnaliter diligebant; ut eos disponeret ad spiritualem amorem et per hoc ad receptionem Spiritus sancti: ideo praesentiam corporalem ab oculis subtrahebat paulatim, quousque plene subtraheret in ascensione, et tunc fieret amor eorum omnino spiritualis. Propterea scriptum est supra decimo sexto[^p524-7]: « Nisi ego abiero, Paracletus non veniet ad vos » etc.

## English
<!-- page 522 -->

18. *As soon then as they came to land.* The majesty of the Divinity being thus manifested through the wonderful taking of fishes, he manifests here the *truth of the humanity* through eating; and in this indeed he proceeds in this order. First there is noted *the providence of the Lord* for preparing the food; second, *the kindness* for inviting; third, *the illumination of the disciples*[^p522-3] for knowing; fourth, *the munificence of the Lord* for distributing the victuals.

19. (Vers. 9.). *Providence,* then, for preparing the victuals is intimated in the preparation of the food, which was made by miracle; whence he says: *As soon then as they came to land, they saw hot coals lying, and a fish laid thereon, and bread,* and so the dinner, to which he was inviting them, was prepared; Matthew, the twenty-second chapter[^p522-4]: « Behold, I have prepared the dinner, and all things are ready ». And these things indeed were by miracle; whence Chrysostom: « He no longer works, as before the cross, from matter laid beneath, but leads on the signs more wonderfully, showing that what he then did from subject matter he did on account of a certain dispensation ». He means to say that that fish was made from nothing. Nor did he prepare only by miracle, but the preparation was also made by *the ministry of Peter* at the divine command; whence there follows:

20. (Vers. 10.). *Jesus saith to them: Bring of the fishes which you have now caught;* that the truth may be proved by the miracle.

21. (Vers. 11.). *But Simon Peter went up*[^p522-5], as more diligent among the others, *and drew the net to land, full of great fishes, one hundred and fifty-three;* in which is shown the greatness of the miracle both in the number and in the size. By the *great fishes* we understand the Blessed, who are great in charity; Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p522-6]: « He that is the lesser in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he ». And for a greater expression of the miracle he adds what was done about the net; whence he says: *And although there were so many, the net was not broken;* against which it is said in Luke, the fifth chapter[^p522-7]: « They took a copious multitude of fishes, but the net was breaking ». And so, *although there were so many,* namely « in number and in size », they were in such a number on account of a signification: either because that number signifies *perfection,* the numerical proportion being considered[^p522-8];
<!-- page 523 -->
or because it signifies *universality,* because, as Jerome says[^p523-1], « so many kinds of fishes are read to be in the sea, as described by the philosophers ».

22. (Vers. 12.). *Jesus saith to them.* Here is noted the second, namely *the kindness of the Lord* for inviting the disciples to food; therefore he says: *Jesus saith to them: Come and dine;* he invites to *dinner,* but in the future resurrection he will invite to *supper,* according to that of the Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter[^p523-2]: « Blessed are they that are called to the marriage supper of the Lamb ». He invites now to *bodily* food, that he may intimate that he invites to *spiritual* food; the Canticle, the fifth chapter: « Eat, O friends, and drink, and be inebriated, my dearly beloved »; and Isaias, the fifty-fifth chapter: « All you that thirst, come to the waters, and you that have no money, make haste, buy and eat; come, buy without money and without any exchange wine and milk ».

23. *And none of them who were at meat.* Here is noted the third, namely *the illumination of the disciples* for knowing the Lord; whence he says: *And none of them who were at meat,* that is, of those sitting down to eat, *durst ask him: Who art thou?* Whence Chrysostom[^p523-3]: « They sat with silence and much reverence, attending to him ». *Knowing that it was the Lord,* that is, for this reason they did not ask him: who art thou? to the Hebrews, the next to last chapter[^p523-4]: « Let us serve, pleasing God, with fear and reverence »; to the Philippians, the second chapter: » With fear and trembling work out your salvation ».

24. (Vers. 13.). *And Jesus came.* Here is noted the fourth, namely *the munificence of the Lord* for distributing the victuals; whence he says: *And he took bread and gave*[^p523-5] *to them, and fish in like manner,* he gave to them, that they might eat. And likewise it is to be understood that he himself *ate* with them; whence Chrysostom[^p523-6]: « Here indeed he does not say that he ate with them; Luke says elsewhere, in Luke, the last chapter: When he had eaten before them, taking the remains, he gave to them »; whence Gregory[^p523-7] says: « The Lord ate the broiled fish and bread ». Christ *gave,* in which he signified himself the most liberal distributor [so printed: *distributorom* for *distributorem*] of all graces; Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p523-8]: « He gave to every one according to his proper ability ».

25. (Vers. 14.). *This is now the third time.* The manifestation having been set down, there is set down *the comparison of the manifestation to the preceding ones*[^p523-9]; and it is counted with the preceding ones as *the third,* not because there were not more, but because it suffices to write these three for believing, according to that of Deuteronomy, the nineteenth chapter: « In the mouth of two or three witnesses every word stands ». Therefore he says. *This is now the third time that Jesus was manifested to his disciples, after he was risen from the dead,* as if it were sufficient to have set out a threefold manifestation; Acts, the tenth chapter[^p523-10]: « He gave him to be made manifest, not to all the people, but to witnesses preordained by God, even to us, who did eat and drink with him after he arose again from the dead ».
<!-- page 524 -->

### Questions.

26. Quaest. I. But it is asked here first concerning this, that he says: *And although there were so many, the net was not broken.* — Since in the first fishing, in Luke, the fifth chapter[^p524-1], it is said that *the net was breaking* on account of the copious multitude of fishes; what is it that now it remains whole?

Gregory responds[^p524-2]: « In the other fishing the net is broken for the multitude of fishes, because now the reprobate enter with the elect into the confession of faith, who rend the Church herself with heresies. But in this fishing the net is not broken, because the holy Church of the elect, resting in continual peace, is torn by no dissensions ».

27. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked concerning this, that the Lord *ate* fish and bread, according to what is said in Luke, the last chapter[^p524-3]; since it belongs only to an animal body: therefore either Christ had an animal body, or he ate phantasmally.

**I respond:** It must be said that there is *eating out of necessity,* and this belongs to an animal body only; and there is *eating out of power,* and this belongs not only to an animal body, but also to a glorious and spiritual one, which eats purely out of power. For from glorified bodies there is taken away not the *power,* but the *necessity*[^p524-4].

28. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked: what became of that food? For since a glorified body was not nourished, it seems that it would have the accustomed egress of nature.

**I respond:** It must be said that, just as water thrown into fire is at once absorbed by the fire, so food taken up by a glorified body is at once consumed by a certain spiritual power. Whence Augustine in the book on the solution of the Six Questions[^p524-5]: « The thirsting earth absorbs water in one way, the hot ray of the sun in another: the former by *need,* the latter by *power.* The body of the future resurrection, then, would be of imperfect felicity if it needed food; it will be of imperfect felicity if it cannot take food ». And the reason of this is that in the first it would be *needy,* in the second *impotent.*

29. Quaest. IV. Likewise it is asked concerning this, that it is said: *This is now the third time that Jesus manifested himself* etc. — For what reason did the Lord manifest himself to the disciples at intervals, and was not with them continually up to the ascension?

And Chrysostom responds[^p524-6] that « he withdrew himself from their sight at times, that he might be sought more eagerly, and being sought might be loved, and being loved might be venerated more signally and more devoutly ». — Another reason: because the disciples, as long as the Lord was with them, loved him carnally; that he might dispose them to spiritual love, and through this to the reception of the Holy Spirit, he therefore withdrew his bodily presence from their eyes little by little, until he should withdraw it fully in the ascension, and then their love would become wholly spiritual. For this reason it is written above, the sixteenth chapter[^p524-7]: « Unless I go away, the Paraclete will not come to you » etc.
