### Capitulum III. *Erat autem homo ex Pharisaeis.*

### Collatio XI.

(Vers. 4.). *Quomodo potest homo nasci, cum sit senex?*

1. Verba ista sunt Nicodemi de verbis Domini dubitantis, qui ideo quaerebat, quia nativitatum differentias ignorabat. Ad cuius quaestionis solutionem notandum est, multiplicem esse nativitatem. Est enim nativitas *naturae* — nativitas *culpae* — nativitas *gratiae.* Nativitas *naturae* est miseranda, nativitas *culpae* detestanda, nativitas *gratiae* veneranda.

2. De nativitate *naturae;* Iob quinto[^p550-5]: « Homo nascitur ad laborem, et avis ad volatum »; et haec est *miserabilis*, quia ipsam sequitur *poenalitas vitae, necessitas mortis* et *adversitas pugnae.* — Ipsam sequitur *poenalitas vitae;* Iob decimo quarto:
<!-- page 551 -->
« Homo natus de muliere, brevi vivens tempore repletur multis miseriis ». — *Necessitas mortis;* Sapientiae secundo: « Ex nihilo nati sumus, et post hoc erimus, tanquam si non fuerimus »; et Sapientiae quinto: « Nos nati continuo desinimus esse » etc. — *Adversitas pugnae;* Ecclesiastae quarto: « Vidi calumnias, quae sub sole geruntur, et lacrymas innocentium et neminem consolatorem etc. Et laudavi magis mortuos quam viventes, et feliciorem utroque iudicavi qui nondum natus est nec vidit mala, quae sub sole fiunt ».

3. De nativitate *culpae;* Iob tertio[^p551-1]: « Pereat dies, in qua natus sum, et nox, in qua dictum est: Conceptus est homo ». Haec est *detestabilis* vel detestanda propter tria, quia scilicet sic nascens in *ignominia nascitur*, in *maledictione vivit*, in *damnatione moritur.* — Nativitas ista est *ignominiosa;* Proverbiorum decimo septimo[^p551-2]: « Natus est stultus in ignominiam suam »; Ezechielis decimo sexto dicitur animae peccatrici: « Quando nata es, in die ortus tui non est praecisus umbilicus tuus, et in aqua non es lota in salutem etc., sed proiecta es super faciem terrae in abiectione animae tuae, in die, qua nata es » etc. — Est iterum *maledicta;* Ecclesiastici quadragesimo primo: « Vae vobis! impii, qui dereliquistis legem Altissimi. Et si nati fueritis, in maledictione nascemini; et si mortui fueritis, in maledictione erit pars vestra ». — Est etiam *perniciosa;* Sapientiae quinto: « Nos nati continuo desivimus esse et virtutis quidem nullum signum valuimus ostendere; in malignitate autem nostra consumti sumus. Talia dixerunt hi qui peccaverunt, in inferno ». *Nos*, inquam, *nati* in peccato, *desivimus esse* peccando. Unde Bernardus[^p551-3]: « Domine, omnia sunt propter te; qui ergo sibi, non tibi, incipiunt vivere, incipiunt *non esse* ». *Et nullum signum virtutis valuimus ostendere* in mundo; et ideo *in malignitate nostra consumti sumus* in inferno.

4. De nativitate *gratiae;* Ioannis tertio[^p551-4]: « Oportet vos nasci denuo ». Haec autem est *veneranda*, quia sic nascens succedit Patri in *nobilitate*, in *strenuitate*, in *hereditate.* Solent namque filii nobilium a patribus trahere generis *nobilitatem*, naturalium *strenuitatem* et hereditariam *successionem.* — *Generis nobilitatem*, sic et filii *gratiae* habent a Patre suo *nobilitatem.* Isti enim sunt, « qui non ex sanguinibus », id est ex diabolica malitia, « neque ex voluntate viri », id est cupiditate mundana, « neque ex voluntate carnis », id est carnali concupiscentia, de quibus nascuntur peccatores; « sed ex Deo nati sunt », et ideo nobiles, Ioannis primo[^p551-5]. — Habent etiam a Patre *morum strenuitatem;* unde Ioannis tertio: « Quod natum est ex carne caro est, et quod natum est ex spiritu spiritus est »; quasi dicat: natum carnis inclucit vitam *carnalem*, natum ex spiritu vitam *spiritualem*, iuxta illud:

> Et documenta damus, qua simus origine nati.

— Habent etiam a Patre *hereditariam successionem;* unde « si filii, et heredes », ad Romanos octavo[^p551-6]; unde Ioannis tertio: « Nisi quis renatus fuerit denuo, non potest videre regnum Dei »; quasi dicat: illorum est succedere in regno Patris, qui denuo nascuntur per Spiritum sanctum.

5. Notandum autem, quod tria ostendunt, hominem natum esse ex Deo. Solet enim filius naturalis patrem *recognoscere*, patrem *diligere* et patrem *imitari.* Secundum hoc probatur Dei filius qui Patrem Deum *recognoscit per fidem;* Ioannis primo[^p551-7]: « Dedit eis potestatem filios Dei fieri, his qui credunt in nomine eius »; primae Ioannis quinto: « Omnis, qui credit, quoniam Iesus est Christus, ex Deo natus est ». — Probatur etiam ex Deo natus qui eum *diligit per caritatem;* primae Ioannis tertio: « Videte, qualem caritatem dedit nobis Pater, ut filii Dei nominemur et simus ». — Probatur etiam ex Deo natus qui eum *imitatur per virtutem*, et hoc tripliciter; in *fugiendo peccatum;* primae Ioannis quinto[^p551-8]: « Omnis, qui natus est ex Deo, non peccat, sed generatio Dei conservat eum, et malignus non tangit eum ». In *superando mundum;* primae Ioannis quinto: « Omne, quod natum est ex Deo, vincit mundum; et haec est victoria, quae vincit mundum
<!-- page 552 -->
fides nostra ». In *faciendo iustitiam;* primae Ioannis secundo: « Si scitis, quoniam Deus iustus est, scitote, quoniam et omnis, qui facit iustitiam, ex ipso natus est ».

### Collatio XII.

(Vers. 18.). *Qui non credit iam iudicatus est.*

1. Si iam iudicatus est, quomodo iterum iudicabitur? Quodsi non iudicabitur *qui non credit*, et verum est quod dicit Scriptura, quod *qui credit in* Filium *non iudicatur*[^p552-1]; tum ergo nec iudicabitur qui credit, nec qui non credit: quid ergo iudicabit ille qui « venturus est iudicare vivos et mortuos »?

2. Propterea ad intelligendum ea quae dicit Scriptura de iudicio divino, notandum, quod est iudicium Dei in *praesenti* — et est iudicium Dei in *futuro.* — *Iudicium* Dei in *praesenti* multiplex: *praescientiae;* Ioannis tertio[^p552-2]: « Qui non credit iam iudicatus est », id est, iudicari praescitur. — *Discretionis;* Ioannis duodecimo: « Nunc iudicium est mundi », id est distinctio bonorum a malis; et Ioannis nono: « Ego in iudicium in hunc mundum veni, ut qui non vident videant » etc. — *Examinationis;* primae Petri quarto: « Tempus est, ut iudicium incipiat a domo Dei ». — *Retributionis;* Psalmus: « Exaltare, qui iudicas terram, redde retributionem superbis ».

3. Iudicium *in futuro* multiplex: ex parte *iudicatorum* et ex parte *iudicantium.* — Ex parte *iudicatorum*, iudicium *discussionis;* Ecclesiastae duodecimo[^p552-3]: « Cuncta, quae fiunt, adducet Dominus in iudicium pro omni errato, sive bonum, sive malum illud sit ». — Iudicium *definitionis;* Apocalypsis vigesimo: « Libri aperti sunt, et iudicati sunt mortui ex his quae scripta erant in libris, secundum opera ipsorum ». — Iudicium *condemnationis;* Ioannis tertio: « Non misit Deus Filium suum in mundum, ut iudicet mundum ». — Iudicium *discussionis* erit mediocriter bonorum et mediocriter malorum tantum, ut ait Hieronymus[^p552-4]. Iudicium *distinctionis* erit omnium bonorum et malorum. Iudicium *damnationis* omnium malorum, nullorum autem bonorum.

4. Ex parte *iudicantium* dividitur iudicium futurum. Est enim iudicium *auctoritatis*, et illud est *Dei;* Psalmus[^p552-5]: « Dominus iudicabit populos ». — Iudicium *exsecutionis*, et illud est *Christi;* Ioannis quinto: « Pater omne iudicium dedit Filio »; et infra eodem: « Potestatem dedit ei iudicium facere, quia Filius hominis est ». — Iudicium *assessionis*, et illud erit *Apostolorum* proprium; Matthaei decimo nono[^p552-6]: « Sedebitis super sedes duodecim, iudicantes duodecim tribus Israel »; *iudicantes*, quasi assessorie. — Iudicium *approbationis*, et illud erit omnium *iustorum;* Sapientiae tertio: « Iudicabunt iusti nationes » etc.; *iudicabunt*, id est, iudicium Dei de nationibus approbabunt. — Iudicium *comparationis;* et hoc est *bonorum* respectu malorum, et *minus malorum* respectu peiorum; Matthaei duodecimo: « Regina Austri surget in iudicio cum generatione hac » etc.; et Lucae undecimo: « Viri Ninivitae surgent in iudicio cum generatione hac » etc.; *in iudicio surgent*, id est, ex sui comparatione iudicandos esse ostendent.

### Annotatio 7.

(Vers. 19.). *Hoc est iudicium, quia lux venit in mundum* etc.

Nota, quod *iudicium* aliquando appellatur iudicii causa, scilicet *culpa;* ad Romanos quinto[^p552-7]: « Iudicium ex uno in condemnationem, gratia autem ex multis in iustificationem »; *iudicium*, id est *culpa;* similiter et hic: *Hoc autem est iudicium, quia lux venit in mundum, et dilexerunt homines magis tenebras quam lucem.* — Aliquando *iudicis sententia;* Ioannis septimo: « Rectum iudicium iudicate ». — Aliquando *iudicati poena;* ad Galatas quinto: « Qui conturbat vos portabit iudicium, quicumque est ille ».

### Annotatio 8.

(Vers. 27.). *Non potest homo accipere quidquam, nisi datum fuerit ei de caelo.*

Require supra capitulo primo[^p552-8]: *quid* accipitur et *a quo*, et *qui accipiunt* et *qui non, ad quid obligat* accepti ratio.

### Collatio XIII.

(Vers. 30.). *Illum oportet crescere, me autem minui.*

1. In verbis istis duo tanguntur. Primo proponitur nobis *augmentum Christi* in exemplum *virtutis*, ut discamus esse virtute magni, et hoc, cum
<!-- page 553 -->
dicitur: *Illum oportet crescere.* — Secundo proponitur nobis *minutio Ioannis* in exemplum *humilitatis*, ut discamus esse humilitate parvi.

2. Dicit ergo: *Illum oportet crescere.* Nota, quod crevit Christus in *bonis spiritualibus* quantum ad evidentiam magis quam[^p553-1] quantum ad existentiam, crevit in *praedicatione*, in *passione* et in *resurrectione.* — In *praedicatione* crevit quantum ad ostensionem *veritatis;* Lucae secundo[^p553-2]: « Puer crescebat et confortabatur, plenus sapientia, et Spiritus Dei erat in illo »; primo Regum tertio: « Crevit Samuel, et Dominus erat cum eo, et non cecidit de omnibus verbis eius in terram, et cognovit universus Israel. quod Samuel fidelis esset propheta Domini »; Esther decimo: « Fons parvus crevit in fluvium magnum »; quod fuit in praedicatione Christi, secundum illud Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto: « Ego sapientia effudi flumina ».

3. Crevit in *passione* quantum ad ostensionem *bonitatis.* Tunc enim patuerunt « viscera misericordiae Dei nostri, in quibus visitavit nos oriens ex alto[^p553-3] »; sicut dicit beatus Matthaeus decimo tertio: « Simile est regnum caelorum », id est Christus, « grano sinapis, quod accipiens homo », scilicet Deus Pater, « seminavit in agro suo », id est in mundo, « quod minimum quidem est omnibus seminibus », reputatione; talis enim fuit Christus in passione; « cum autem creverit » per ostensionem bonitatis suae, « maius est omnibus oleribus », id est plantulis spiritualibus, « et fit arbor, ita ut volucres caeli », id est animae spirituales, « veniant et habitent in ramis eius », id est in brachiis crucis eius.

4. Crevit in *resurrectione* quantum ad ostensionem *potestatis.* Unde post resurrectionem ait Matthaei ultimo[^p553-4]: « Data est mihi omnis potestas in caelo et in terra »; quod figuratum est Danielis secundo, quando lapis, « qui percussit statuam, crevit et factus est mons magnus et implevit universam terram ». *Lapis*, Christus; *statua*, diabolus. Lapis ergo *statuam percussit* in passione, *crevit* in resurrectione, *factus est mons magnus* in ascensione, *et implevit universam terram* in Spiritus sancti missione.

5. *Illum oportet crescere.* Sicut crevit Christus, ita debet crescere Christianus. Debet crescere, sicut arbor, in *altitudine*, in *fortitudine*, in *latitudine.* — In *altitudine* per *eminentiam virtutis;* Marci quarto[^p553-5]: « Semen cecidit in terram bonam et dabat fructum ascendentem et crescentem »; secundi Paralipomenon decimo septimo: « Magnificatus est Iosaphat et crevit usque in sublime ». — In *fortitudine* per *constantiam virtutis;* Exodi primo: « Crevit populus et confortatus est nimis »; Lucae primo de Ioanne: « Puer crescebat et confortabatur spiritu ». — In *latitudine* per *multiplicationem virtutis;* Genesis primo[^p553-6]: « Crescite et multiplicamini et replete terram »; Genesis vigesimo sexto: « Dilatavit nos Dominus et crescere fecit super terram »; ad Colossenses primo: « Ambuletis digne Deo placentes et fructificantes in omni opere bono ».

6. Nota tria, quae *impediunt incrementum spirituale: nutrimenti defectio*, quam patiuntur illi qui non habent humorem gratiae; Iob octavo[^p553-7]: « Nunquid potest vivere scirpus sine humore, aut crescere carectum sine aqua »? Econtra dicitur de viro iusto in Psalmo: « Erit tanquam lignum, quod plantatum est secus decursus aquarum ». — *Nutrimenti subtractio*, quam patiuntur illi qui se effundunt et se implicant diversis occupationibus. Nam gratiae fatuorum effunduntur; Ecclesiastici vigesimo primo[^p553-8]: « Cor fatui quasi vas confractum et omnem sapientiam non tenebit »; et Genesis quadragesimo nono: « Ruben primogenitus meus »; et sequitur: « Effusus es sicut aqua, non crescas ». — *Nutrimenti* vel nutritae rei *suffocatio*, quam patiuntur qui suffocantur a curis huius saeculi et sollicitudinibus; Matthaei decimo tertio[^p553-9]: « Cum crevisset herba et fructum fecisset, apparuerunt simul et zizania »; Lucae octavo: « Exortae spinae suffocaverunt illud »; Matthaei decimo tertio: « Creverunt spinae et suffocaverunt ea ».

7. Ad procurandum autem incrementum spirituale tria sunt necessaria. Oportet enim *terram exercere;* Exodi primo[^p553-10]: « Quanto plus opprimebantur filii Israel, tanto magis crescebant ». — *Nutrimentum adhibere;* Ezechielis decimo nono: « Fructus eius et frondes eius creverunt ex aquis multis »; primae Petri secundo: « Lac concupiscite, ut in eo crescatis in salutem ». — *Superflua amputare* vel per *paupertatem;* Genesis quadragesimo primo[^p553-11]: « Crescere me fecit Deus in terra paupertatis meae »; vel per *eleemosynarum largitionem*, de quibus ait Apostolus
<!-- page 554 -->
secundae ad Corinthios nono: « Multiplicabit Dominus semen vestrum et augebit incrementa frugum iustitiae vestrae »; vel per *sollicitudinis abiectionem;* Matthaei sexto: « Considerate lilia agri, quomodo crescunt; non laborant neque nent ». — Habeas ergo *exercitium poenitentiae* vel patientiae, *nutrimentum doctrinae* vel etiam gratiae per modestiam paupertatis vel saltem per largitionem eleemosynae, et poteris in Domino crescere.

8. *Illum oportet crescere.* Debet Christianus crescere in *gratia;* ad Ephesios quarto[^p554-1]: « Veritatem autem facientes in caritate, crescamus in illo qui est caput Christus » etc. — In *scientia;* secundae Petri tertio: « Crescite ergo in gratia et cognitione Domini nostri Iesu Christi »; et ad Colossenses primo: « Ambuletis digne Deo placentes, fructificantes in omni opere bono et crescentes in scientia Dei ». — In *vita;* Proverbiorum quarto[^p554-2]: « Iustorum semita quasi lux splendens procedit et crescit usque ad perfectum diem »; *semita quasi lux* est vita hominis alios illuminans; « sic luceat lux vestra coram hominibus » etc., Matthaeus. — In *fama*, Esther nono: « Fama nominis eius crescebat quotidie et per cunctorum ora volitabat ».

9. Vel sic dicas: quia debet crescere in *cognitione veritatis*, ut supra[^p554-3], secundae Petri tertio et ad Colossenses primo. — In *amore bonitatis;* ad Ephesios quarto, ut supra: « In caritate crescamus » etc. — In *operatione virtutis;* Iob primo: « Operibus manuum eius benedixisti, et possessio eius crevit in terra ». — In *constantia virtutis;* Lucae primo: « Puer crescebat et confortabatur spiritu et manebat in desertis usque ad ostensionem sui in Israel ».

### Annotatio 9.

(Vers. 16.). *Sic Deus dilexit mundum, ut Filium suum unigenitum daret.*

Nota, quod Christus datus est nobis in *foedus dilectionis;* unde hic: *Sic Deus dilexit mundum* etc. — In *solatium desolationis;* Canticorum octavo[^p554-4]: « Quis mihi det te fratrem meum sugentem ubera matris meae, ut inveniam te foris solum et deosculer te, et iam me nemo despiciat »? — In *ducem directionis;* Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto: « Ecce, testem populis dedi eum, ducem ac praeceptorem gentibus ». — In *scutum defensionis;* Isaiae quinquagesimo: « Corpus meum dedi percutientibus et genas meas vellentibus ». — In *cibum refectionis;* Ioannis sexto[^p554-5]: « Panis, quem ego dabo, caro mea est pro mundi vita ». — In *praemium retributionis;* Apocalypsis secundo: « Vincenti dabo edere de ligno vitae »; de quo Augustinus[^p554-6]: « Domine, promittis mihi omnia, si amem te; da te solum, et sufficit mihi ».

### Annotatio 10.

(Vers. 20.). *Qui male agit odit lucem.*

Nota, quod lucem odiunt tria genera hominum, scilicet *pigri;* Iob vigesimo quarto[^p554-7]: « Ipsi fuerunt rebelles lumini, nescierunt vias eius nec reversi sunt per semitas eius ». — *Infirmi;* primi Regum tertio exemplum de Heli, qui « non poterat videre lucernam, donec exstingueretur ». — *Malefici;* unde hic, Ioannis tertio: *Omnis, qui male agit odit lucem.* — Odit *piger* lucem, quia ipsum *excitat; infirmus*, quia *cruciat; maleficus*, quia ipsum *manifestat.*

### Annotatio 11.

(Vers. 33.). *Signavit, quia Deus verax est.*

Nota, quod triplex est signaculum Christi: ex parte *carnis*, peccati immunitas; Ioannis primo[^p554-8]: « Ecce; Agnus Dei » etc. Ex parte *animae*, donorum pluralitas; Ioannis tertio: « Non ad mensuram dat Deus spiritum ». Ex parte *divinae naturae*, Verbi Divinitas; Ioannis primo: « Verbum caro factum est ».

### Chapter III. *Now there was a man of the Pharisees.*

### Collation XI.

(Vers. 4.). *How can a man be born when he is old?*

1. These are the words of Nicodemus, doubting of the words of the Lord, who asked for this reason, because he did not know the differences of births. For the solution of this question it is to be noted that birth is manifold. For there is the birth *of nature* — the birth *of fault* — the birth *of grace.* The birth *of nature* is to be pitied, the birth *of fault* to be detested, the birth *of grace* to be venerated.

2. Of the birth *of nature;* Job, the fifth chapter[^p550-5]: « Man is born to labour, and the bird to fly »; and this is *miserable*, because there follow upon it *the penalty of life, the necessity of death* and *the adversity of combat.* — There follows upon it *the penalty of life;* Job, the fourteenth chapter:
<!-- page 551 -->
« Man born of a woman, living for a short time, is filled with many miseries ». — *The necessity of death;* Wisdom, the second chapter: « We are born of nothing, and after this we shall be as if we had not been »; and Wisdom, the fifth chapter: « We being born, forthwith cease to be » etc. — *The adversity of combat;* Ecclesiastes, the fourth chapter: « I saw the oppressions that are done under the sun, and the tears of the innocent, and they had no comforter etc. And I praised the dead rather than the living, and I judged him happier than them both, that is not yet born, nor hath seen the evils that are done under the sun ».

3. Of the birth *of fault;* Job, the third chapter[^p551-1]: « Let the day perish wherein I was born, and the night in which it was said: A man child is conceived ». This is *detestable* or to be detested on account of three things, namely because one so born *is born* in *ignominy, lives* in *malediction, dies* in *damnation.* — This birth is *ignominious;* Proverbs, the seventeenth chapter[^p551-2]: « A fool is born to his own disgrace »; in Ezechiel, the sixteenth chapter, it is said to the sinful soul: « When thou wast born, in the day of thy nativity thy navel was not cut, neither wast thou washed with water for thy health etc., but thou wast cast out upon the face of the earth in the abjection of thy soul, in the day that thou wast born » etc. — It is again *accursed;* Ecclesiasticus, the forty-first chapter: « Woe to you, ungodly men, who have forsaken the law of the most high! And if you be born, you shall be born in malediction; and if you die, in malediction shall be your portion ». — It is also *pernicious;* Wisdom, the fifth chapter: « We being born, forthwith ceased to be, and have been able to shew no sign of virtue; but are consumed in our wickedness. Such things as these the sinners said in hell ». *We*, I say, *being born* in sin, *ceased to be* by sinning. Whence Bernard[^p551-3]: « Lord, all things are on account of thee; they therefore who begin to live for themselves, not for thee, begin *not to be* ». *And have been able to shew no sign of virtue* in the world; and therefore *are consumed in our wickedness* in hell.

4. Of the birth *of grace;* John, the third chapter[^p551-4]: « You must be born again ». And this is *to be venerated*, because one so born succeeds the Father in *nobility*, in *strenuousness*, in *inheritance.* For the sons of nobles are wont to draw from their fathers the *nobility* of their race, the *strenuousness* of natural gifts and hereditary *succession.* — *The nobility of race:* so also the sons *of grace* have *nobility* from their Father. For these are they « who not of blood », that is, of diabolical malice, « nor of the will of man », that is, worldly cupidity, « nor of the will of the flesh », that is, carnal concupiscence, of which sinners are born; « but are born of God », and therefore noble, John, the first chapter[^p551-5]. — They have also from the Father *strenuousness of morals;* whence John, the third chapter: « That which is born of the flesh is flesh, and that which is born of the spirit is spirit »; as if to say: what is born of the flesh includes [so printed: *inclucit* for *includit*] a *carnal* life, what is born of the spirit a *spiritual* life, according to that:

> And we give proofs of the origin from which we are born.

— They have also from the Father *hereditary succession;* whence « if sons, heirs also », to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p551-6]; whence John, the third chapter: « Unless a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God »; as if to say: it belongs to those to succeed in the kingdom of the Father who are born again through the Holy Spirit.

5. But it is to be noted that three things show a man to be born of God. For a natural son is wont *to recognise* his father, *to love* his father and *to imitate* his father. According to this he is proved a son of God who *recognises* God the Father *by faith;* John, the first chapter[^p551-7]: « He gave them power to be made the sons of God, to them that believe in his name »; the first of John, the fifth chapter: « Whosoever believeth that Jesus is the Christ, is born of God ». — He also is proved born of God who *loves* him *by charity;* the first of John, the third chapter: « Behold what manner of charity the Father hath bestowed upon us, that we should be called, and should be the sons of God ». — He also is proved born of God who *imitates* him *by virtue*, and this in three ways: in *fleeing sin;* the first of John, the fifth chapter[^p551-8]: « Whosoever is born of God, sinneth not, but the generation of God preserveth him, and the wicked one toucheth him not ». In *overcoming the world;* the first of John, the fifth chapter: « Whatsoever is born of God, overcometh the world; and this is the victory which overcometh the world,
<!-- page 552 -->
our faith ». In *doing justice;* the first of John, the second chapter: « If you know that God is just, know ye, that every one also, who doth justice, is born of him ».

### Collation XII.

(Vers. 18.). *He that doth not believe is already judged.*

1. If he is already judged, how will he be judged again? But if *he that doth not believe* will not be judged, and what Scripture says is true, that *he that believeth in* the Son *is not judged*[^p552-1]; then, therefore, neither he that believes nor he that does not believe will be judged: what, therefore, will he judge who « shall come to judge the living and the dead »?

2. Therefore, for understanding those things which Scripture says of the divine judgement, it is to be noted that there is a judgement of God in *the present* — and there is a judgement of God in *the future.* — *The judgement* of God in *the present* is manifold: of *foreknowledge;* John, the third chapter[^p552-2]: « He that doth not believe is already judged », that is, is foreknown to be judged. — Of *discernment;* John, the twelfth chapter: « Now is the judgement of the world », that is, the distinction of the good from the wicked; and John, the ninth chapter: « For judgement I am come into this world, that they who see not may see » etc. — Of *examination;* the first of Peter, the fourth chapter: « The time is, that judgement should begin at the house of God ». — Of *retribution;* the Psalm: « Lift up thyself, thou that judgest the earth, render a reward to the proud ».

3. The judgement *in the future* is manifold: on the part of *those judged* and on the part of *those judging.* — On the part of *those judged*, the judgement *of discussion;* Ecclesiastes, the twelfth chapter[^p552-3]: « All things that are done, the Lord will bring into judgement for every error, whether it be good or evil ». — The judgement *of definition;* the Apocalypse, the twentieth chapter: « The books were opened, and the dead were judged by those things which were written in the books, according to their works ». — The judgement *of condemnation;* John, the third chapter: « God sent not his Son into the world, to judge the world ». — The judgement *of discussion* will be of the middling good and of the middling wicked only, as Jerome says[^p552-4]. The judgement *of distinction* will be of all the good and the wicked. The judgement *of damnation*, of all the wicked, but of none of the good.

4. On the part of *those judging* the future judgement is divided. For there is the judgement *of authority*, and that is *God's;* the Psalm[^p552-5]: « The Lord shall judge the peoples ». — The judgement *of execution*, and that is *Christ's;* John, the fifth chapter: « The Father hath given all judgement to the Son »; and below in the same place: « He hath given him power to do judgement, because he is the Son of man ». — The judgement *of assessorship*, and that will be proper to *the Apostles;* Matthew, the nineteenth chapter[^p552-6]: « You shall sit on twelve seats, judging the twelve tribes of Israel »; *judging*, as it were as assessors. — The judgement *of approbation*, and that will be of all *the just;* Wisdom, the third chapter: « The just shall judge nations » etc.; *shall judge*, that is, shall approve the judgement of God concerning the nations. — The judgement *of comparison;* and this is *of the good* in respect of the wicked, and *of the less wicked* in respect of the worse; Matthew, the twelfth chapter: « The queen of the south shall rise in judgement with this generation » etc.; and Luke, the eleventh chapter: « The men of Ninive shall rise in judgement with this generation » etc.; *shall rise in judgement*, that is, shall show by comparison with themselves that they are to be judged.

### Annotation 7.

(Vers. 19.). *This is the judgement, because the light is come into the world* etc.

Note that *judgement* is sometimes called the cause of judgement, namely *fault;* to the Romans, the fifth chapter[^p552-7]: « The judgement from one unto condemnation, but grace from many unto justification »; *judgement*, that is, *fault;* similarly also here: *And this is the judgement, because the light is come into the world, and men loved darkness rather than the light.* — Sometimes *the sentence of the judge;* John, the seventh chapter: « Judge right judgement ». — Sometimes *the punishment of the one judged;* to the Galatians, the fifth chapter: « He that troubleth you shall bear the judgement, whosoever he be ».

### Annotation 8.

(Vers. 27.). *A man cannot receive any thing, unless it be given him from heaven.*

Seek above, in the first chapter[^p552-8]: *what* is received and *from whom*, and *who receive* and *who do not, to what* the account of the thing received *binds.*

### Collation XIII.

(Vers. 30.). *He must increase, but I must decrease.*

1. In these words two things are touched on. First there is set before us *the increase of Christ* as an example *of virtue*, that we may learn to be great in virtue, and this when
<!-- page 553 -->
it is said: *He must increase.* — Secondly there is set before us *the decrease of John* as an example *of humility*, that we may learn to be little in humility.

2. He says therefore: *He must increase.* Note that Christ increased in *spiritual goods* as to evidence rather than[^p553-1] as to existence; he increased in *preaching*, in *passion* and in *resurrection.* — In *preaching* he increased as to the showing *of truth;* Luke, the second chapter[^p553-2]: « The child grew, and waxed strong, full of wisdom, and the Spirit of God was in him »; the first of Kings, the third chapter: « Samuel grew, and the Lord was with him, and not one of his words fell to the ground, and all Israel knew that Samuel was a faithful prophet of the Lord »; Esther, the tenth chapter: « A little fountain grew into a great river »; which was in the preaching of Christ, according to that of Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter: « I, wisdom, have poured out rivers ».

3. He increased in *passion* as to the showing *of goodness.* For then lay open « the bowels of the mercy of our God, in which the Orient from on high hath visited us[^p553-3] »; as blessed Matthew says in the thirteenth chapter: « The kingdom of heaven is like », that is, Christ, « to a grain of mustard seed, which a man », namely God the Father, « took and sowed in his field », that is, in the world, « which is the least indeed of all seeds », in reputation; for such was Christ in the passion; « but when it is grown up » by the showing of his goodness, « it is greater than all herbs », that is, than the spiritual little plants, « and becometh a tree, so that the birds of the air », that is, spiritual souls, « come and dwell in the branches thereof », that is, in the arms of his cross.

4. He increased in *resurrection* as to the showing *of power.* Whence after the resurrection he says, Matthew, the last chapter[^p553-4]: « All power is given to me in heaven and in earth »; which was figured in Daniel, the second chapter, when the stone « that struck the statue grew, and became a great mountain, and filled the whole earth ». *The stone*, Christ; *the statue*, the devil. The stone therefore *struck the statue* in the passion, *grew* in the resurrection, *became a great mountain* in the ascension, *and filled the whole earth* in the sending of the Holy Spirit.

5. *He must increase.* As Christ increased, so ought the Christian to increase. He ought to increase, as a tree, in *height*, in *strength*, in *breadth.* — In *height* by *the eminence of virtue;* Mark, the fourth chapter[^p553-5]: « The seed fell upon good ground, and brought forth fruit that grew up and increased »; the second of Paralipomenon, the seventeenth chapter: « Josaphat was magnified and grew exceedingly ». — In *strength* by *the constancy of virtue;* Exodus, the first chapter: « The people grew and waxed exceedingly strong »; Luke, the first chapter, of John: « The child grew, and was strengthened in spirit ». — In *breadth* by *the multiplication of virtue;* Genesis, the first chapter[^p553-6]: « Increase and multiply, and fill the earth »; Genesis, the twenty-sixth chapter: « The Lord hath given us room, and hath made us to increase upon the earth »; to the Colossians, the first chapter: « That you may walk worthy of God, in all things pleasing, being fruitful in every good work ».

6. Note three things which *impede spiritual increase:* the *lack of nourishment*, which they suffer who have not the moisture of grace; Job, the eighth chapter[^p553-7]: « Can the rush live without moisture, or the sedge-bush grow without water »? On the contrary it is said of the just man in the Psalm: « He shall be like a tree which is planted near the running waters ». — The *withdrawal of nourishment*, which they suffer who pour themselves out and entangle themselves in divers occupations. For the graces of fools are poured out; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-first chapter[^p553-8]: « The heart of a fool is like a broken vessel, and no wisdom at all shall it hold »; and Genesis, the forty-ninth chapter: « Ruben, my firstborn »; and there follows: « Thou art poured out as water, grow thou not ». — The *suffocation* of the nourishment or of the thing nourished, which they suffer who are suffocated by the cares and solicitudes of this world; Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p553-9]: « When the blade was sprung up, and had brought forth fruit, then appeared also the cockle »; Luke, the eighth chapter: « The thorns growing up choked it »; Matthew, the thirteenth chapter: « The thorns grew up and choked them ».

7. But for procuring spiritual increase three things are necessary. For one must *work the ground;* Exodus, the first chapter[^p553-10]: « The more they oppressed the children of Israel, the more they increased ». — *Apply nourishment;* Ezechiel, the nineteenth chapter: « Its fruit and its branches grew out of many waters »; the first of Peter, the second chapter: « Desire the milk, that thereby you may grow unto salvation ». — *Cut away superfluities*, either by *poverty;* Genesis, the forty-first chapter[^p553-11]: « God hath made me to grow in the land of my poverty »; or by *the bestowal of alms*, of which the Apostle says
<!-- page 554 -->
in the second to the Corinthians, the ninth chapter: « The Lord will multiply your seed, and increase the growth of the fruits of your justice »; or by *the casting away of solicitude;* Matthew, the sixth chapter: « Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow; they labour not, neither do they spin ». — Have therefore *the exercise of penance* or of patience, *the nourishment of teaching*[^tr-doctrina] or also of grace, through the modesty of poverty or at least through the bestowal of alms, and you will be able to increase in the Lord.

8. *He must increase.* The Christian ought to increase in *grace;* to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter[^p554-1]: « But doing the truth in charity, we may in all things grow up in him who is the head, Christ » etc. — In *knowledge;* the second of Peter, the third chapter: « But grow in grace, and in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ »; and to the Colossians, the first chapter: « That you may walk worthy of God, in all things pleasing, being fruitful in every good work, and increasing in the knowledge of God ». — In *life;* Proverbs, the fourth chapter[^p554-2]: « The path of the just, as a shining light, goeth forwards and increaseth even to perfect day »; *the path as a light* is the life of a man enlightening others; « so let your light shine before men » etc., Matthew. — In *fame*, Esther, the ninth chapter: « The fame of his name increased daily, and was spread through all men's mouths ».

9. Or you may say thus: that he ought to increase in *the knowledge of truth*, as above[^p554-3], the second of Peter, the third chapter and to the Colossians, the first chapter. — In *the love of goodness;* to the Ephesians, the fourth chapter, as above: « In charity we may grow » etc. — In *the operation of virtue;* Job, the first chapter: « Thou hast blessed the works of his hands, and his possession hath increased on the earth ». — In *the constancy of virtue;* Luke, the first chapter: « The child grew, and was strengthened in spirit, and was in the deserts until the day of his manifestation to Israel ».

### Annotation 9.

(Vers. 16.). *God so loved the world, as to give his only begotten Son.*

Note that Christ was given to us as *the covenant of dilection;* whence here: *God so loved the world* etc. — As *the solace of desolation;* the Canticles, the eighth chapter[^p554-4]: « Who shall give thee to me for my brother, sucking the breasts of my mother, that I may find thee without alone, and kiss thee, and now no man may despise me »? — As *the leader of direction;* Isaias, the fifty-fifth chapter: « Behold, I have given him for a witness to the people, for a leader and a master to the Gentiles ». — As *the shield of defence;* Isaias, the fiftieth chapter: « I have given my body to the strikers, and my cheeks to them that plucked them ». — As *the food of refection;* John, the sixth chapter[^p554-5]: « The bread that I will give, is my flesh for the life of the world ». — As *the reward of retribution;* the Apocalypse, the second chapter: « To him that overcometh, I will give to eat of the tree of life »; of which Augustine[^p554-6]: « Lord, thou promisest me all things, if I love thee; give thyself alone, and it suffices me ».

### Annotation 10.

(Vers. 20.). *He that doth evil hateth the light.*

Note that three kinds of men hate the light, namely *the slothful;* Job, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p554-7]: « They have been rebellious to the light[^tr-lumen], they have not known its ways, neither have they returned by its paths ». — *The infirm;* in the first of Kings, the third chapter, the example of Heli, who « could not see the lamp, before it was put out ». — *The evildoers;* whence here, John, the third chapter: *Every one that doth evil hateth the light.* — The *slothful* man hates the light, because it *rouses* him; *the infirm*, because it *torments* him; *the evildoer*, because it *manifests* him.

### Annotation 11.

(Vers. 33.). *He hath set to his seal that God is true.*

Note that the seal of Christ is threefold: on the part of *the flesh*, immunity from sin; John, the first chapter[^p554-8]: « Behold; the Lamb of God » etc. On the part of *the soul*, the plurality of gifts; John, the third chapter: « For God doth not give the spirit by measure ». On the part of *the divine nature*, the Godhead of the Word; John, the first chapter: « The Word was made flesh ».
