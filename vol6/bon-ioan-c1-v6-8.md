# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. I, Vers. 6-8
## *De adventu praecursoris*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 250 -->

#### Pars. II. De Verbo, quatenus est carni unitum, ponuntur tria.

17. *Fuit homo missus a Deo, cui nomen erat Ioannes.* Egit supra de Verbo secundum *excellentiam Divinitatis;* in hac parte agit secundum *assumtionem humanitatis.* Et quia assumtio haec facta est propter nostram salutem, quae facta est per passionem et consummata per resurrectionem[^p250-8]; ideo habet haec pars tres partes. In prima agit de *incarnatione;* in secunda de *passione*, infra in fine undecimi[^p250-9]: *Collegerunt ergo pontifices;* in tertia de *resurrectione*, in principio vigesimi capituli: *Una autem Sabbati* etc.

#### *Primo tractatur de ipsa incarnatione dupliciter, primo de ipsa in se considerata.*

Et quia incarnatio inutilis est nobis, nisi manifestetur; ideo primo agit de *incarnatione;* secundo, de incarnationis *manifestatione*, infra eodem[^p250-10]: *Et vidimus gloriam eius.* Quia vero adventus Christi in carnem erat adventus Regis, ideo praemisit praecursorem; ideo primo agitur de adventu *praecursoris*, secundo, de adventu *Christi*, ibi: *Erat lux vera* etc.

#### De adventu praecursoris.

18. (Vers. 6.). Adveniens ergo praecursor describitur a quatuor conditionibus, scilicet a *natura*, ab *auctoritate*, a *nomine*, ab *officio*. A *natura*, cum dicitur: *Fuit homo;* Glossa[^p250-11]: « Homo, non Angelus, ut volunt haeretici ». Hoc dicebant haeretici propter illud Malachiae tertio: « Ecce, ego mitto Angelum meum ante faciem tuam, qui praeparabit viam ante te ». Sed « *Angelus* est ibi nomen officii, non naturae[^p250-12] ». — Ab *auctoritate* describitur, cum subditur: *Missus a Deo*, non ab homine, non a se; ad Romanos decimo[^p250-13]: « Quomodo praedicabunt, nisi mittantur »? Quocontra dicitur de malis prophetis Ieremiae
<!-- page 251 -->
vigesimo tertio: « Currebant, et ego non mittebam eos ». — A *nomine* describitur, cum dicitur: *Cui nomen erat Ioannes.* Hoc nomen fuit authenticum, quia non ab homine impositum, sed a Deo; Lucae primo[^p251-1] dictum est Zachariae: « Elisabeth, uxor tua, pariet tibi filium, et vocabis nomen eius Ioannem ». — Ab *officio* describitur, cum subinfertur:

19. (Vers. 7.). *Hic venit in testimonium.* Officium enim suum fuit testificari de lumine, non propter defectum luminis, sed credentium; ideo dicitur: *Hic venit in testimonium, ut testimonium perhiberet de lumine, ut omnes crederent per illum;* Actuum decimo nono[^p251-2]: « Ioannes baptizavit baptismo poenitentiae populum, dicens, in eum, qui venturus erat post ipsum, ut crederent »; *crederent*, inquam, *per illum*, non *in illum;* quia:

20. (Vers. 8.). *Non erat ille lux*, in quam credendum; *sed ut testimonium perhiberet de lumine;* quod et fecit; infra quinto[^p251-3]: « Vos misistis ad Ioannem, et ille testimonium perhibuit veritati ».

### Quaestiones.

21. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic de hoc quod dicit: *Hic venit in testimonium.*

**Contra:** 1. Infra quinto[^p251-4]: « Ego testimonium ab homine non accipio »; ergo nec ad hoc debuit mitti a Deo.

2. Item, lux non indiget illuminatione[^p251-5]: ergo nec summa veritas testificatione: ergo frustra venit.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod testimonium perhibetur *alicui* et *propter aliquem.* Aliquando ergo est opportunitas testimonii ratione eius, *cui* perhibetur, quia[^p251-6] veritas est infirma, et sic homo indiget testimonio alieno; aliquando ratione eius, *propter quem* perhibetur, quia in fide dubius vel infirmus. Ioannes autem non venit in testimonium propter defectum lucis in Christo, sed ratione ineptitudinis in oculo nostro[^p251-7].

22. Quaest. II. Sed quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Ut omnes crederent per illum:* quia, cum non omnes crediderint; si Deus ad hoc misit eum, ergo et uterque est fine debito frustratus.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod est finis duplex: in *se* et in *altero.* Dico ergo, quod frustrari fine in *altero*, dum tamen habeatur finis in *se*, nullam inducit imperfectionem, sicut patet de rhetore bene persuadente, de Angelo custodiente, de Christo redimente et Ioanne testificante, quia testimonium sufficiens perhibuit, licet non omnes ei crediderint; tamen ex contingentibus nihil omisit[^p251-8].

23. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Non erat ille lux.*

**Contra:** Ad Ephesios quinto[^p251-9]: « Eratis aliquando tenebrae, nunc autem lux in Domino »: ergo omnes boni sunt lux.

Respondet Glossa[^p251-10], quod est lux *illuminans* et lux *illuminata.* Ioannes et alii Sancti non sunt lux illuminans, sed lux illuminata a prima luce.

Sed contra hoc est Matthaei quinto[^p251-11]: « Sic luceat lux vestra coram hominibus »; et ad Philippenses secundo: « Inter quos lucetis sicut luminaria in mundo ».

**Respondeo:** Est lux illuminans *effective* et *dispositive.* Lux illuminans *effective* est lux per essentiam, sic solus Deus est lux illuminans; *dispositive* est lux per participationem, et sic Sancti, qui illuminant verbo et exemplo[^p251-12].

## English
<!-- page 250 -->

#### Part II. On the Word, inasmuch as it is united to flesh, three things are set forth.

17. *There was a man sent from God, whose name was John.* Above he treated of the Word according to the *excellence of the Divinity;* in this part he treats of it according to the *assumption of the humanity.* And because this assumption was made for our salvation, which was wrought through the passion and consummated through the resurrection[^p250-8]; therefore this part has three parts. In the first he treats of the *incarnation;* in the second of the *passion*, below at the end of the eleventh[^p250-9]: *The chief priests therefore gathered;* in the third of the *resurrection*, at the beginning of the twentieth chapter: *And on the first day of the week* etc.

#### *First, of the incarnation itself, in a twofold way: first, of it considered in itself.*

And because the incarnation is useless to us unless it is manifested; therefore first he treats of the *incarnation;* second, of the *manifestation* of the incarnation, below in the same[^p250-10]: *And we saw his glory.* But because the coming of Christ into flesh was the coming of a King, therefore he sent a forerunner ahead; therefore first there is treated the coming of the *forerunner*, second, the coming of *Christ*, there: *That was the true light* etc.

#### On the coming of the forerunner.

18. (Vers. 6.). The forerunner, then, as he comes, is described by four conditions, namely by *nature*, by *authority*, by *name*, by *office*. By *nature*, when it is said: *There was a man;* the Gloss[^p250-11]: « A man, not an Angel, as the heretics would have it ». The heretics said this on account of that saying of Malachi, the third: « Behold, I send my Angel before thy face, who shall prepare the way before thee ». But « *Angel* is there the name of an office, not of a nature[^p250-12] ». — He is described by *authority*, when it is added: *Sent from God*, not from man, not from himself; to the Romans, the tenth[^p250-13]: « How shall they preach unless they be sent »? Against which it is said of the wicked prophets, in Jeremiah,
<!-- page 251 -->
the twenty-third: « They ran, and I did not send them ». — He is described by *name*, when it is said: *Whose name was John.* This name was authentic, because it was imposed not by man, but by God; in Luke, the first[^p251-1], it was said to Zachary: « Elizabeth thy wife shall bear thee a son, and thou shalt call his name John ». — He is described by *office*, when it is subjoined:

19. (Vers. 7.). *This man came for testimony.* For his office was to testify of the lumen[^tr-lumen], not on account of a defect of lumen, but of those believing; therefore it is said: *This man came for testimony, to give testimony of the lumen, that all men might believe through him;* Acts, the nineteenth[^p251-2]: « John baptized the people with the baptism of penance, saying that they should believe in him who was to come after him »; *believe*, I say, *through him*, not *in him;* because:

20. (Vers. 8.). *He was not the light*, in which one is to believe; *but was to give testimony of the lumen;* which he also did; below in the fifth[^p251-3]: « You sent to John, and he gave testimony to the truth ».

### Questions.

21. Quaest. I. But it is asked here about what he says: *This man came for testimony.*

**On the contrary:** 1. Below in the fifth[^p251-4]: « I receive not testimony from man »; therefore neither ought he to have been sent by God for this.

2. Likewise, light does not need illumination[^p251-5]: therefore neither does the highest truth need testifying: therefore he came in vain.

**I respond:** It must be said that testimony is given *to someone* and *on account of someone.* Sometimes, then, testimony is opportune by reason of him *to whom* it is given, because[^p251-6] the truth is weak, and so a man needs another's testimony; sometimes by reason of him *on account of whom* it is given, because he is doubtful or weak in faith. But John did not come for testimony on account of a defect of light in Christ, but by reason of the unfitness in our eye[^p251-7].

22. Quaest. II. But it is asked about what he says: *That all men might believe through him:* because, since not all believed; if God sent him for this, then both have been frustrated of their due end.

**I respond:** It must be said that the end is twofold: in *itself* and in *another.* I say, then, that to be frustrated of the end in *another*, so long as the end in *itself* is had, induces no imperfection, as is plain of the rhetor persuading well, of the Angel guarding, of Christ redeeming and of John testifying, because he gave sufficient testimony, although not all believed him; yet of the things that pertained to him he omitted nothing[^p251-8].

23. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *He was not the light.*

**On the contrary:** To the Ephesians, the fifth[^p251-9]: « You were heretofore darkness, but now light in the Lord »: therefore all good men are light.

The Gloss[^p251-10] responds that there is *illumining* light and *illumined* light. John and the other Saints are not illumining light, but light illumined by the first light.

But against this is Matthew, the fifth[^p251-11]: « So let your light shine before men »; and to the Philippians, the second: « Among whom you shine as luminaries in the world ».

**I respond:** There is light illumining *effectively* and *dispositively.* Light illumining *effectively* is light by essence, and thus God alone is illumining light; *dispositively* it is light by participation, and thus the Saints, who illumine by word and example[^p251-12].

[^tr-lumen]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin *lumen* is left standing here, against *lux*, which this translation renders "light". *Lux* is light at its source and *lumen* light as given off and received. The Gospel itself sets the two side by side in these verses: John came *ut testimonium perhiberet de lumine* (1:7–8), but *non erat ille lux* (1:8), and the next verse names *lux vera* (1:9); Bonaventure's own prose leans on the lemma's word (*testificari de lumine, non propter defectum luminis*). So the Douay's "of the light" is adjusted to "of the lumen" in the verse as well as in the exposition, and "light" is kept wherever the Latin has *lux*.
