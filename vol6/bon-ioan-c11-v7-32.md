# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XI, Vers. 7-32
## *Postquam facta est denuntiatio infirmitatis, describitur hic adventus Medici hoc ordine: primo insinuatur adveniendi securitas; secundo, veniendi necessitas; tertio, discipulorum utilitas; quarto, Thomae ad commoriendum animositas; quinto, temporis tarditas; sexto, loci opportunitas*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 398 -->

17. *Deinde post haec suis dixit discipulis.* Postquam facta est denuntiatio infirmitatis, describitur hic *adventus Medici* hoc ordine: primo insinuatur *adveniendi securitas;* secundo, *veniendi necessitas;* tertio, *discipulorum utilitas;* quarto, *Thomae* ad commoriendum[^p398-1] *animositas;* quinto, *temporis tarditas;* sexto, *loci opportunitas.*

(Vers. 7.). Primo igitur notatur *Christi*[^p398-2] *securitas,* propter quam hortatur discipulos timidos, dicens: *Deinde post haec dixit discipulis suis,* scilicet Iesus: *Eamus in Iudaeam iterum;* ideo eos hortabatur, quia timidi erant; et hoc patet ex eorum responsione.

18. (Vers. 8.). *Dicunt ei discipuli: Rabbi, nunc quaerebant te Iudaei lapidare,* id est, adhuc recens est; supra decimo[^p398-3]: « Sustulerunt lapides Iudaei, ut lapidarent eum ». *Et iterum vadis illuc?* Quasi dicant: non eundum. Simile dixit Domino Petrus Matthaei decimo sexto[^p398-4]: « Absit a te, Domine; non erit tibi hoc ». Dissuadebat Iesu mortem et fuit reprehensus, quia Dominus se praedixerat moriturum, et ipse dissuadebat et volebat eum retrahere a suo proposito. Isti autem nesciebant tunc, quod ipse vellet mori; et ideo instruuntur et confortantur, ut non timeant; propter quod ait:

19. (Vers. 9.). *Respondit Iesus: Nonne duodecim horae sunt diei?* in quibus scilicet ambulare *bonum* est et *securum;* unde dicit: *Si quis ambulat*[^p398-5] *in die, non offendit, quia lucem huius mundi videt.* Haec lux Christus est; supra octavo: « Ego sum lux mundi ». Post hanc lucem qui ambulat non offendit; quia, sicut dicitur supra octavo[^p398-6], « qui sequitur me non ambulat in tenebris »; et ideo non offendit. Ideo dicit Sapientia Proverbiorum quarto: « Ducam te per semitas aequitatis, quas cum ingressus fueris, non arctabuntur gressus tui, et currens non habebis offendiculum ». Non solum ambulare in die securum, sed etiam *opportunum;* unde subdit:

20. (Vers. 10.). *Si autem ambulaverit in nocte, offendit, quia lux non est in eo;* Matthaei decimo quinto[^p398-7]: « Si caecus caeco ducatum praestet, ambo in foveam cadunt »; propter quod dicitur infra duodecimo: « Ambulate, dum lucem habetis, ut non vos tenebrae comprehendant ».

21. (Vers. 11.). *Haec ait et post haec dixit illis*[^p398-8]. Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *veniendi necessitas* propter Lazarum, qui mortuus nisi per ipsum suscitari non poterat. Ideo dicit: *Lazarus, amicus noster, dormit; sed vado, ut a somno excitem eum;* propriissime loquitur, quia *mori* Deo *dormire* est; unde Augustinus[^p398-9]: « Hominibus *mortuus* erat, qui eum suscitare non poterant; Domino vero *dormiens,* qui tanta facilitate eum excitabat de sepulcro, quanta tu non excitas dormientem de lecto »; ideo dicebat Dominus Matthaei nono: « Non est mortua puella, sed dormit ». Sed discipuli hoc non intelligebant; propter quod dicunt:

22. (Vers. 12.). *Domine*[^p398-10]*, si dormit, salvus erit,* quia in acutis aegritudinibus bonum signum est somnus. Et hoc dixerunt, non intelligentes; propter hoc autem dicit Evangelista:

23. (Vers. 13.). *Dixerat autem Iesus de morte*[^p398-11]*, illi autem putaverunt, quia de dormitione somni diceret;* unde decepti erant, ut innuit Evangelista, per aequivocationem dormitionis.

Propter quod intelligendum, quod est dormitio quadruplex, scilicet *pigritiae;* Proverbiorum sexto[^p398-12]: *Usquequo, piger, dormis?* Primae ad Thessalonicenses quinto: *Non dormiamus, sed vigilemus et sobrii simus.* — *Culpae;* ad Ephesios quinto[^p398-13]: *Surge qui dormis, et exsurge a mortuis, et illuminabit te Christus;* primae ad Corinthios undecimo: *Multi inter vos infirmi et imbecilles, et dormiunt multi.* — *Quietis;* Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p398-14]: *Et invenit eos dormientes;* et subditur, quod dixit eis: *Dormite iam et requiescite.* — *Mortis;* primae ad Thessalonicenses quarto[^p398-15]: *Nolumus, vos ignorare, fratres, de dormientibus, ut non contristemini, sicut et ceteri, qui spem non habent;* Iob vigesimo septimo: *Dives, cum dormierit, nihil secum afferet.*

24. (Vers. 14.). *Tunc*[^p398-16] *dixit eis Iesus.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *discipulorum utilitas;* ideo replicat mortem Lazari aperte, quia occulte non capiebant. Ideo *tunc dixit eis Iesus manifeste: Lazarus mortuus est;* ideo *manifeste,* quia, sicut dicitur Matthaei decimo tertio[^p398-17], « vobis datum est nosse mysteria regni caelorum ». Et subditur utilitas, de qua gaudet; unde dicit:
<!-- page 399 -->

25. (Vers. 15.). *Sed*[^p399-1] *gaudeo propter vos,* id est utilitatem vestram, *ut credatis, quia non eram ibi;* hoc non refertur ad fidem, sed ad gaudium, *gaudeo* scilicet, *quia non eram ibi,* ut postmodum ex nostro adventu fides vestra solidetur. Ideo dicit: *Sed eamus ad eum,* propter securitatem et Lazari necessitatem et vestram utilitatem. Ratio, quare veniebat, erat, quia amicus; infra decimo quarto[^p399-2]: « Si quis diligit me, sermonem meum servabit, et Pater meus diliget eum, et ad eum veniemus et mansionem apud eum faciemus ».

26. (Vers. 16.). *Dixit ergo Thomas.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *Thomae animositas,* etiam ad commoriendum; ideo dicit: *Dixit ergo Thomas, qui dicitur Didymus,* quasi diu dubitans: *Eamus et nos et moriamur cum illo*[^p399-3].

Quaerit Chrysostomus[^p399-4]: Quomodo Thomas hoc dixit? Et dicit, quod dixit ex formidatione; sed melius dicendum, quod dixerit ex fortitudine, id est exhortatione Domini animatus, et sapienter dixit et fortiter; unde secundae ad Timotheum secundo[^p399-5]: « Si commortui sumus, et convivemus; si sustinemus, et conregnabimus ».

27. (Vers. 17.). *Venit ergo*[^p399-6] *Iesus.* Tangitur hic quintum, scilicet *temporis tarditas;* divina enim dispensatione hoc gestum est; unde non tantum invenit mortuum, sed foetidum, ut certius et admirabilius et gloriosius esset miraculum; et ideo subdit: *Et invenit eum iam quatuor dies in monumento habentem,* et ita foetidum; infra eodem[^p399-7]: « Domine, iam foetet, quatriduanus enim est ».

28. (Vers. 18.). *Erat autem Bethania.* Tangitur hic ultimum, scilicet *loci opportunitas.* Quia enim prope Ierusalem erat, multi Iudaei venerant, qui essent testes miraculi. Ratio autem, quare venerant, erat loci opportunitas sive propinquitas. Propter opportunitatem dicit: *Erat autem Bethania iuxta Ierosolymam quasi stadiis quindecim.* Stadium est octava pars milliarii[^p399-8], et ita propinquus erat locus; propter quod etiam dicit:

29. (Vers. 19.). *Multi autem ex Iudaeis venerant ad Martham et Mariam, ut consolarentur eas de fratre suo;* quia tunc multa erat tristitia de mortuis, sed post mortem Christi de bonis mortuis debet esse consolatio; quia « beati mortui, qui in Domino moriuntur[^p399-9] »; ideo primae ad Thessalonicenses quarto: « Nolumus, vos ignorare de dormientibus, ut non contristemini, sicut et ceteri, qui spem non habent ».

30. *Spiritualiter* notanda sunt tria: primo, quid[^p399-10] est, quod Lazarus a Domino *amatur?* Secundo, quid est, quod *languet, moritur* et *quatriduanus invenitur?* Tertio, quid est, quod Dominus suos *discipulos exhortatur,* quod *in die ambulent?*

Lazarus *adiutus a Domino* interpretatur[^p399-11]. Iste significat virum, qui habet gratiam adiuvantem, secundum illud Isaiae quadragesimo nono[^p399-12]: *In tempore accepto exaudivi te, et in die salutis auxiliatus sum tui.* Iste Dominum diligit, ideo et amatur; Proverbiorum octavo: *Ego diligentes me diligo.*

Iste Lazarus *languet* in peccati delectatione, de quo languore Ecclesiastici decimo[^p399-13]: *Languor prolixior gravat medicum;* quia vix contingit, quin morosa delectatio demergat in mortale peccatum. — Sed *moritur* in consensu, quia tunc consummatur peccatum interius; Iacobi primo[^p399-14]: *Concupiscentia, cum conceperit, parit peccatum; peccatum vero, cum consummatum fuerit, generat mortem.* — Et *quatriduanus efficitur* et *foetidus,* dum a consensu pervenitur in *opus,* ab opere in *consuetudinem,* a consuetudine in *peccati impudentiam,* quando *frons meretricis fit homini, ut iam nolit erubescere*[^p399-15]; et
<!-- page 400 -->
tunc *foetidus* est; quia iam peccatum eius in alios diffunditur; Amos quarto: *Ascendere feci putredinem castrorum vestrorum in nares vestras* etc.

Ad hunc peccatorem suscitandum a mortuis Dominus *sollicitat discipulos suos,* quos et praelatos ordinaverat; Proverbiorum sexto[^p400-1]: *Discurre, festina, suscita amicum tuum.* — Et hortatur, quod *de die ambulent. Dies* dicitur tempus merendi, quia, dum sol oritur, homo exit ad opus suum, et daemones absconduntur; Psalmus[^p400-2]: *Ortus est sol, et congregati sunt* etc.; et post: *Exibit homo ad opus suum* etc. — Iste dies *durat* ab adventu Christi usque ad diem iudicii; quia supra nono[^p400-3]: *Quamdiu sum in mundo, lux sum mundi;* et ipse est in mundo *usque ad consummationem saeculi;* Matthaei ultimo: *Ecce, ego vobiscum sum omnibus diebus usque ad consummationem saeculi.* — Et hoc *operandum,* ut peccatores suscitentur; ad Galatas sexto[^p400-4]: *Operemur bonum ad omnes, maxime autem ad domesticos fidei.* — *Duodecim horae* huius diei possunt dici *duodecim status Christi,* de quibus habitum est supra quarto[^p400-5]. — Vel possunt dici *duodecim Apostoli,* qui etiam significati sunt per duodecim *lapides,* per duodecim *fontes* in Elim, per duodecim *boves circa mare aeneum Salomonis,* per duodecim *lapides pretiosos,* et nunc per duodecim horas[^p400-6]. — Vel *duodecim horae* sunt *duodecim virtutes,* quae fuerunt in Christo, scilicet quatuor cardinales, una theologica[^p400-7], et septem dona. — Vel *duodecim horae* dicuntur *duodecim fructus Spiritus,* de quibus Apocalypsis vigesimo secundo[^p400-8]: *Lignum vitae afferens fructus duodecim per singulos menses.*

31. *Martha ergo, ut audivit* etc. Supra[^p400-9] determinata sunt duo antecedentia ad miraculum, scilicet denuntiatio infirmitatis et adventus Medici; tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *intercessio fidei,* quae quidem merebatur Lazari suscitationem. Haec autem erat fides et devotio duarum sororum, Marthae scilicet et Mariae. Unde primo insinuatur fides *Marthae;* secundo, *Mariae,* ibi[^p400-10]: *Et cum haec dixisset, abiit* etc.

Fides igitur et devotio *Marthae* insinuatur hoc ordine: primo innuitur Marthae officiosa *sollicitudo;* secundo, ipsius fidelis *confessio;* tertio, fidelis *confessionis amplificatio.*

(Vers. 20.). Marthae igitur *sollicitudo* innuitur in hoc, quod ipsa Domino occurrit; non sustinuit, quod veniret usque ad domum; ideo dicit: *Martha ergo, ut audivit, quia Iesus venit, occurrit ei*[^p400-11]. In hoc patet, quod Martha multum erat sollicita, sed e contrario Maria quieta; unde dicit: *Maria autem domi sedebat,* quieta scilicet. Propterea dicitur Lucae decimo: « Martha, Martha, sollicita es et turbaris erga plurima; Maria optimam partem elegit ». Optime per Martham occurrentem significatur *activa;* Isaiae vigesimo primo[^p400-12]: « Cum panibus occurrite fugienti »; per Mariam domi sedentem *contemplativa;* unde Threnorum tertio de viro contemplativo: « Sedebit solitarius et tacebit, quia levavit se super se ».

32. (Vers. 21.). *Dixit ergo Martha*[^p400-13]. Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet Marthae *fidelis confessio,* in qua confitetur Dominum potentem et benignum, ut posset et vellet liberare et nunc resuscitare; ideo dicit: *Domine, si fuisses hic, frater meus non fuisset mortuus;* quia voluisses et potuisses liberare; sed et nunc potes resuscitare. Ideo dicit:

33. (Vers. 22.). *Sed et nunc scio,* id est, firma fide teneo, *quia, quaecumque poposceris a Deo, dabit tibi Deus.* Augustinus[^p400-14]: « Scio, quod potes; si vis, facis; utrum autem facias, iudicii tui est,
<!-- page 401 -->
non meae praesumtionis ». Non fuit ausa petere tam magnum quid, ne pateretur repulsam; sed suam fidem exprimendo insinuavit desiderium. Non solum quae petit Christus dat Deus, sed quod petitur in nomine eius; infra decimo sexto[^p401-1]: « Si quid petieritis Patrem in nomine meo, dabit vobis ».

34. (Vers. 23.). *Dicit ei*[^p401-2] *Iesus.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *fidelis confessionis amplificatio.* Confessa enim fuerat Martha, quod Dominus posset impetrare fratris resuscitationem; Dominus autem vult eam ad hoc inducere, ut confiteatur, Christum posse resuscitare; propter quod potestative dicit: *Resurget frater tuus.* Et Martha confitetur de generali resurrectione, unde dicit:

35. (Vers. 24.). *Scio*[^p401-3]*, quia resurget in resurrectione in novissimo die.* Sciebat *ex Lege;* Danielis duodecimo: « Multi de his qui dormiunt in pulvere terrae, evigilabunt »; sciebat *per doctrinam Christi;* supra sexto: « Ego resuscitabo eum in novissimo die ». Sed quia nondum confessa erat, quod virtute Christi resurgeret; ideo instruitur de hoc; propter quod dicit:

36. (Vers. 25.). *Dicit*[^p401-4] *ei Iesus: Ego sum resurrectio et vita;* causalis est praedicatio, id est, facio resurgere et vivere; primae ad Corinthios decimo quinto: « Christus resurrexit a mortuis, primitiae dormientium, quoniam quidem per hominem mors, et per hominem resurrectio mortuorum ». Et hoc manifestat: *Qui credit in me, etsi*[^p401-5] *mortuus fuerit, vivet;* et ita facio resurgere; supra quinto: « Venit hora et nunc est, quando mortui audient vocem Filii Dei, et qui audierint vivent ». — Non solum facio mortuos resurgere, sed etiam viventes vivere; unde:

37. (Vers. 26.). *Et omnis, qui vivit et credit in me, non morietur in aeternum;* supra sexto[^p401-6]: « Omnis, qui videt Filium et credit in eum, habet vitam aeternam ». Et quia « corde creditur ad iustitiam, ore autem confessio fit ad salutem »; ideo dicit: *Credis hoc?*

38. (Vers. 27.). *Ait illi: Utique, Domine,* hoc credo, quod tu hoc facias; *ego credidi, quia tu es Christus, Filius Dei*[^p401-7]*, qui in hunc mundum venisti;* et ita potes resuscitare et vivificare, quia, supra quinto, « sicut Pater habet vitam in semetipso, ita dedit et Filio vitam habere in semetipso ». Confitetur in hoc et Divinitatem et humanitatem: quia *Dei Filius,* potes; quia *venisti,* facies; ad hoc enim missus es.

39. *Et cum haec dixisset.* Posita fideli confessione Marthae, ponitur hic *fidelis confessio Mariae,* quae describitur hoc ordine: primo ipsius *Mariae vocatae acceleratio;* secundo, *Iudaeorum associatio;* tertio, *fidelis et devota confessio.*

(Vers. 28.). Primo ergo ponitur *Mariae vocatae acceleratio,* in quo significatur *quies* et *devotio* in Maria: *quies,* quia non venit nisi vocata a Martha; propterea dicit: *Et cum haec dixisset, abiit et vocavit Mariam, sororem suam silentio;* Threnorum tertio[^p401-8]: « Bonum est praestolari cum silentio salutare Dei ». *Vocavit,* inquam, eam imperio Domini; Apocalypsis ultimo: « Et qui audit dicat: Veni ». Et hoc innuitur, quia vocavit, *dicens: Magister adest et vocat te.* Quamvis hoc non sit praedictum causa brevitatis, tamen ita fuisse intelligendum est; Iob decimo quarto[^p401-9]: « Vocabis me, et ego respondebo tibi ». Non tantum erat Maria *quieta,* sed etiam *devota;* unde dicit:

40. (Vers. 29.). *Illa, ut audivit, surrexit*[^p401-10] *cito et venit ad eum;* non exspectavit, quod veniret, sed ipsa velociter cucurrit, antequam intraret; ideo dicit:

41. (Vers. 30.). *Nondum enim venerat Iesus in castellum, sed erat adhuc in illo loco, ubi occurrerat ei Martha.* In quo[^p401-11] significatur, quod vir a Domino vocatus debet accelerare, sicut fecerunt boni discipuli, de quibus Matthaei quarto[^p401-12]: « Continuo relictis retibus, secuti sunt eum »; unde et ille reprehenditur Lucae nono, qui dixit: « Dimitte me prius, ut vadam et sepeliam patrem meum »; Ecclesiastici trigesimo primo: « In omnibus operibus tuis esto velox »; et Proverbiorum decimo octavo: « Qui mollis et dissolutus est in opere suo frater est sua opera dissipantis ».

42. (Vers. 31.). *Iudaei ergo.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *Iudaeorum associatio;* et ratio associandi erat voluntas consolandi; propterea dicit: *Iudaei ergo, qui erant cum ea in domo et conso-*
<!-- page 402 -->
*labantur eam;* ad hoc venerant; *cum vidissent Mariam, quia cito surrexit et abiit*[^p402-1], quasi dolore tacta, non valens diu sustinere; *secuti sunt eam, dicentes: Quia vadit ad monumentum, ut ibi plor[e]t.* Ideo sequebantur, ut consolarentur, de qua consolatione Ecclesiastici trigesimo octavo[^p402-2]: « Consolare propter tristitiam, et fac luctum secundum meritum illius uno die vel duobus propter detractionem ».

43. (Vers. 32.). *Maria ergo, cum venisset.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet Mariae *fidelis confessio et devota;* propter quod dicit: *Maria ergo, cum venisset, ubi erat Iesus, videns eum, cecidit ad pedes eius;* ecce, *devotio,* semper ad pedes Domini; unde Lucae septimo[^p402-3]: « Maria stans retro secus pedes Domini »; et decimo: « Sedens secus pedes Domini, audiebat verbum illius ». Non legitur de Martha, quod ceciderit ad pedes; quia non sic devota nec sic calida, ut Maria; Deuteronomii trigesimo tertio[^p402-4]: « Qui appropinquant pedibus eius accipient de doctrina illius ». Et subditur *fidelis* confessio: *Et dixit ei: Domine, si fuisses hic, frater meus non fuisset mortuus*[^p402-5]*;* in quo insinuat virtutem Domini, ut soror prius fecerat; sed illa amplius dixit, quod adhuc resuscitaret, si vellet; et haec ob reverentiam tacuit et ob devotionem flevit et amplius Dominum movit, sicut infra[^p402-6] patebit. In quo innuitur, quod Dominus magis audit gemitum cordis quam sonum oris; Gregorius[^p402-7]: « Veraciter orare est amaros in compunctione gemitus et non composita verba resonare »; unde primi Regum primo dicitur, quod Anna largiter flevit et tacite oravit.

### Quaestiones.

44. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic: quare Dominus ita diligenter quaerit confessionem Marthae de fide resurrectionis, cum sciret, eam fideliter credere? Et quae necessitas erat?

Dicendum, quod fides sororum Lazari impetravit eius resuscitationem; et quia tunc fides est certior et firmior, quando ore confitemur: ideo oris petiit confessionem[^p402-8].

45. Quaest. II. Sed tunc quaeritur, cuius virtutis sit magis impetrare miraculum?

1. Et quod *fidei,* videtur: quia infra eodem[^p402-9]: *Nonne dixi tibi: Si credideris, videbis gloriam Dei?* — Item, Marci nono: *Si potes credere, omnia possibilia sunt credenti;* et ibidem dicitur, quod Dominus dixit Apostolis, quod non potuerunt eiicere daemonium *propter incredulitatem.* — Et Apostolus primae ad Corinthios decimo tertio[^p402-10]: *Si habuero fidem, ita ut montes transferam.*

2. Quod autem *spei,* videtur: quia Matthaei nono[^p402-11] dicitur illi: *Confide, filia:* ergo videtur, quod sit confidentiae; sed confidentia est spei.

3. Quod autem sit *caritatis,* videtur: quia illius est coniungere Deo et amicum facere, et hi maxime exaudiri sunt digni.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod quia miraculum non solum impetratur a bonis, sed etiam a malis[^p402-12], quod ab *illa virtute* impetratur, quae bonis et malis communis est. Haec autem *fides* et *spes* esse possunt; ideo miraculum est fidei ut originalis principii, sed *spei* ut consummativi. Et quia *confidentia* utrumque complectitur, proprie loquendo, impetratio miraculi est *confidentiae*[^p402-13]. — Nota, quod operatio miraculi debetur *fidei,* quia ipsa *communis;* quia prima *elevat intellectum*[^p402-14]*;* quia habet *considerare omnipotentiam.*

17. *Then after that, he said to his disciples.* After the announcing of the infirmity has been made, there is described here *the coming of the Physician* in this order: first is intimated *the security of coming;* second, *the necessity of coming;* third, *the profit of the disciples;* fourth, *Thomas's boldness* to die with him[^p398-1]; fifth, *the lateness of the time;* sixth, *the opportuneness of the place.*

(Vers. 7.). First, then, is noted *Christ's*[^p398-2] *security,* on account of which he exhorts his timid disciples, saying: *Then after that, he said to his disciples,* namely Jesus: *Let us go into Judea again;* he was exhorting them for this reason, that they were timid; and this is plain from their reply.

18. (Vers. 8.). *The disciples say to him: Rabbi, the Jews but now sought to stone thee,* that is, it is still recent; above, in the tenth chapter[^p398-3]: « The Jews took up stones to stone him ». *And goest thou thither again?* As if they should say: one should not go. Peter said something like this to the Lord, Matthew, the sixteenth chapter[^p398-4]: « Lord, be it far from thee; this shall not be unto thee ». He was dissuading Jesus from death and was reproved, because the Lord had foretold that he would die, and he was dissuading him and wished to draw him back from his purpose. These, however, did not then know that he willed to die; and therefore they are instructed and strengthened, that they may not fear; on account of which he says:

19. (Vers. 9.). *Jesus answered: Are there not twelve hours of the day?* in which, namely, to walk is *good* and *secure;* whence he says: *If a man walk*[^p398-5] *in the day, he stumbleth not, because he seeth the light of this world.* This light is Christ; above, in the eighth chapter: « I am the light of the world ». He who walks after this light does not stumble; because, as is said above, in the eighth chapter[^p398-6], « he that followeth me walketh not in darkness »; and therefore he does not stumble. Therefore Wisdom says, Proverbs, the fourth chapter: « I will lead thee by the paths of equity: which when thou shalt have entered, thy steps shall not be straitened, and when thou runnest thou shalt not meet a stumblingblock ». To walk in the day is not only secure, but also *opportune;* whence he adds:

20. (Vers. 10.). *But if he walk in the night, he stumbleth, because the light is not in him;* Matthew, the fifteenth chapter[^p398-7]: « If the blind lead the blind, both fall into the pit »; on account of which it is said below, in the twelfth chapter: « Walk whilst you have the light, that the darkness overtake you not ».

21. (Vers. 11.). *These things he said; and after that he said to them*[^p398-8]. Here is touched the second, namely, *the necessity of coming* on account of Lazarus, who, dead, could not be raised except through him. Therefore he says: *Lazarus our friend sleepeth; but I go that I may awake him out of sleep;* he speaks most properly, for *to die* is, to God, *to sleep;* whence Augustine[^p398-9]: « To men he was *dead,* who could not raise him; but to the Lord *sleeping,* who was waking him from the sepulchre with such ease as that with which you do not wake one sleeping from his bed »; therefore the Lord said, Matthew, the ninth chapter: « The girl is not dead, but sleepeth ». But the disciples did not understand this; on account of which they say:

22. (Vers. 12.). *Lord*[^p398-10]*, if he sleep, he shall do well,* for in acute sicknesses sleep is a good sign. And this they said, not understanding; and on account of this the Evangelist says:

23. (Vers. 13.). *But Jesus spoke of his death*[^p398-11]*, and they thought that he spoke of the repose of sleep;* whence they were deceived, as the Evangelist hints, through the equivocation of *sleep.*

On account of this it is to be understood that sleep is fourfold, namely, of *sluggishness;* Proverbs, the sixth chapter[^p398-12]: *How long wilt thou sleep, O sluggard?* The first to the Thessalonians, the fifth chapter: *Let us not sleep, but let us watch and be sober.* — Of *fault;* to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter[^p398-13]: *Rise thou that sleepest, and arise from the dead, and Christ shall enlighten thee;* the first to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter: *Many among you are infirm and weak, and many sleep.* — Of *quiet;* Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p398-14]: *And he findeth them sleeping;* and it is added that he said to them: *Sleep ye now and take your rest.* — Of *death;* the first to the Thessalonians, the fourth chapter[^p398-15]: *We will not have you ignorant, brethren, concerning them that are asleep, that you be not sorrowful, even as others who have no hope;* Job, the twenty-seventh chapter: *The rich man when he shall sleep shall take away nothing with him.*

24. (Vers. 14.). *Then*[^p398-16] *Jesus said to them.* Here is touched the third, namely, *the profit of the disciples;* therefore he repeats the death of Lazarus openly, because they were not grasping it when hidden. Therefore *then Jesus said to them plainly: Lazarus is dead;* *plainly,* for this reason, that, as is said in Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p398-17], « to you it is given to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven ». And the profit is added, in which he rejoices; whence he says:
<!-- page 399 -->

25. (Vers. 15.). *But*[^p399-1] *I am glad for your sakes,* that is, for your profit, *that you may believe, because I was not there;* this is not referred to the faith, but to the gladness: *I am glad,* namely, *because I was not there,* that afterwards your faith may be made solid by our coming. Therefore he says: *But let us go to him,* on account of the security, and the necessity of Lazarus, and your profit. The reason why he was coming was that he was a friend; below, in the fourteenth chapter[^p399-2]: « If any one love me, he will keep my word, and my Father will love him, and we will come to him, and will make our abode with him ».

26. (Vers. 16.). *Thomas therefore said.* Here is touched the fourth, namely, *Thomas's boldness,* even to dying with him; therefore he says: *Thomas therefore said, who is called Didymus,* that is, as one long doubting: *Let us also go and die with him*[^p399-3].

Chrysostom asks[^p399-4]: How did Thomas say this? And he says that he said it out of dread; but it is better to say that he said it out of fortitude, that is, emboldened by the Lord's exhortation, and that he spoke both wisely and bravely; whence the second to Timothy, the second chapter[^p399-5]: « If we be dead with him, we shall live also with him; if we suffer, we shall also reign with him ».

27. (Vers. 17.). *Jesus therefore*[^p399-6] *came.* Here is touched the fifth, namely, *the lateness of the time;* for this was done by divine dispensation; whence he found him not only dead, but fetid, that the miracle might be more certain and more wonderful and more glorious; and therefore he adds: *And he found him having been four days already in the grave,* and so fetid; below, in the same chapter[^p399-7]: « Lord, by this time he stinketh, for he is now of four days ».

28. (Vers. 18.). *Now Bethania.* Here is touched the last, namely, *the opportuneness of the place.* For because it was near Jerusalem, many Jews had come, who were to be witnesses of the miracle. And the reason why they had come was the opportuneness or nearness of the place. On account of the opportuneness he says: *Now Bethania was near Jerusalem, about fifteen stadia off.* A stadium is the eighth part of a mile[^p399-8], and so the place was near; on account of which he also says:

29. (Vers. 19.). *And many of the Jews were come to Martha and Mary, to comfort them concerning their brother;* for at that time there was much sadness over the dead, but after the death of Christ there ought to be consolation over the good dead; because « blessed are the dead who die in the Lord[^p399-9] »; therefore the first to the Thessalonians, the fourth chapter: « We will not have you ignorant concerning them that are asleep, that you be not sorrowful, even as others who have no hope ».

30. *Spiritually* three things are to be noted: first, what[^p399-10] is it that Lazarus *is loved* by the Lord? Second, what is it that he *languishes, dies* and *is found four days dead?* Third, what is it that the Lord *exhorts* his *disciples* to *walk in the day?*

Lazarus is interpreted *helped by the Lord*[^p399-11]. He signifies the man who has helping grace, according to that of Isaias, the forty-ninth chapter[^p399-12]: *In an acceptable time I have heard thee, and in the day of salvation I have helped thee.* This man loves the Lord, and therefore is also loved; Proverbs, the eighth chapter: *I love them that love me.*

This Lazarus *languishes* in the delight of sin, of which languor Ecclesiasticus, the tenth chapter[^p399-13]: *A longer languor is troublesome to the physician;* for it scarcely happens but that lingering delight plunges one into mortal sin. — But he *dies* in consent, because then sin is consummated inwardly; James, the first chapter[^p399-14]: *Concupiscence, when it hath conceived, bringeth forth sin; but sin, when it is consummated, begetteth death.* — And he *is made four days dead* and *fetid,* while one passes from consent to *the deed,* from the deed to *habit,* from habit to *the impudence of sin,* when *a harlot's forehead comes upon the man, so that now he will not blush*[^p399-15]; and
<!-- page 400 -->
then he is *fetid;* for now his sin is spread abroad to others; Amos, the fourth chapter: *I made the rottenness of your camp to come up into your nostrils* etc.

To raise this sinner from the dead the Lord *urges his disciples,* whom he had also ordained prelates; Proverbs, the sixth chapter[^p400-1]: *Run about, make haste, raise thy friend.* — And he exhorts them to *walk by day. Day* is the name for the time of meriting, because, while the sun rises, man goes forth to his work, and the demons hide themselves; the Psalm[^p400-2]: *The sun ariseth, and they are gathered together* etc.; and after: *Man shall go forth to his work* etc. — This day *lasts* from the coming of Christ to the day of judgement; for above, in the ninth chapter[^p400-3]: *As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world;* and he is in the world *even to the consummation of the world;* Matthew, the last chapter: *Behold, I am with you all days, even to the consummation of the world.* — And this is *to be worked,* that sinners may be raised; to the Galatians, the sixth chapter[^p400-4]: *Let us work good to all men, but especially to those who are of the household of the faith.* — The *twelve hours* of this day can be called *the twelve states of Christ,* which were treated above, in the fourth chapter[^p400-5]. — Or they can be called *the twelve Apostles,* who were also signified by the twelve *stones,* by the twelve *fountains* in Elim, by the twelve *oxen around the brazen sea of Solomon,* by the twelve *precious stones,* and now by the twelve hours[^p400-6]. — Or the *twelve hours* are *the twelve virtues* which were in Christ, namely, the four cardinal, one theological[^p400-7], and the seven gifts. — Or the *twelve hours* are called *the twelve fruits of the Spirit,* of which Apocalypse, the twenty-second chapter[^p400-8]: *The tree of life, bearing twelve fruits, every month.*

31. *Martha therefore, as soon as she heard* etc. Above[^p400-9] have been set out two of the things antecedent to the miracle, namely, the announcing of the infirmity and the coming of the Physician; here is touched the third, namely, *the intercession of faith,* which indeed was meriting the raising of Lazarus. Now this was the faith and devotion of the two sisters, namely, of Martha and of Mary. Whence first is intimated the faith of *Martha;* second, of *Mary,* there[^p400-10]: *And when she had said these things, she went* etc.

The faith and devotion of *Martha,* then, is intimated in this order: first is hinted Martha's dutiful *solicitude;* second, her faithful *confession;* third, the *amplification* of her faithful *confession.*

(Vers. 20.). Martha's *solicitude,* then, is hinted in this, that she herself runs to meet the Lord; she did not wait for him to come as far as the house; therefore he says: *Martha therefore, as soon as she heard that Jesus had come, went to meet him*[^p400-11]. In this it is plain that Martha was very solicitous, but on the contrary Mary quiet; whence he says: *But Mary sat at home,* quiet, namely. For this reason it is said in Luke, the tenth chapter: « Martha, Martha, thou art solicitous and art troubled about many things; Mary hath chosen the best part ». Excellently is the *active* life signified by Martha running to meet him; Isaias, the twenty-first chapter[^p400-12]: « Meet with bread him that fleeth »; by Mary sitting at home the *contemplative;* whence Lamentations, the third chapter, of the contemplative man: « He shall sit solitary and hold his peace, because he hath lifted himself above himself ».

32. (Vers. 21.). *Martha therefore said*[^p400-13]. Here is touched the second, namely, Martha's *faithful confession,* in which she confesses the Lord to be powerful and kind, so that he could and would free and now raise him again; therefore she says: *Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died;* for thou wouldst have willed and been able to free him; but now also thou canst raise him again. Therefore she says:

33. (Vers. 22.). *But now also I know,* that is, I hold with firm faith, *that whatsoever thou wilt ask of God, God will give it thee.* Augustine[^p400-14]: « I know that you can; if you will, you do; but whether you do is a matter of your judgement,
<!-- page 401 -->
not of my presumption ». She did not dare to ask so great a thing, lest she suffer a refusal; but by expressing her faith she intimated her desire. God gives not only what Christ asks, but what is asked in his name; below, in the sixteenth chapter[^p401-1]: « If you ask the Father any thing in my name, he will give it you ».

34. (Vers. 23.). *Jesus saith to her*[^p401-2]*.* Here is touched the third, namely, *the amplification of the faithful confession.* For Martha had confessed that the Lord could obtain her brother's raising; but the Lord wishes to lead her to this, that she may confess that Christ can raise him; on account of which he says with power: *Thy brother shall rise again.* And Martha makes confession of the general resurrection, whence she says:

35. (Vers. 24.). *I know*[^p401-3]*, that he shall rise again, in the resurrection in the last day.* She knew it *from the Law;* Daniel, the twelfth chapter: « Many of those that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake »; she knew it *through the teaching*[^tr-doctrina] *of Christ;* above, in the sixth chapter: « I will raise him up in the last day ». But because she had not yet confessed that he would rise by the power of Christ; therefore she is instructed about this; on account of which he says:

36. (Vers. 25.). *Jesus*[^p401-4] *saith to her: I am the resurrection and the life;* the predication is causal, that is, I make to rise and to live; the first to the Corinthians, the fifteenth chapter: « Christ is risen from the dead, the firstfruits of them that sleep; for by a man came death, and by a man the resurrection of the dead ». And this he makes manifest: *He that believeth in me, although*[^p401-5] *he be dead, shall live;* and so I make to rise; above, in the fifth chapter: « The hour cometh, and now is, when the dead shall hear the voice of the Son of God, and they that hear shall live ». — Not only do I make the dead to rise, but also the living to live; whence:

37. (Vers. 26.). *And every one that liveth and believeth in me shall not die for ever;* above, in the sixth chapter[^p401-6]: « Every one who seeth the Son and believeth in him hath life everlasting ». And because « with the heart we believe unto justice, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation »; therefore he says: *Believest thou this?*

38. (Vers. 27.). *She saith to him: Yea, Lord,* this I believe, that thou dost this; *I have believed that thou art Christ, the Son of God*[^p401-7]*, who art come into this world;* and so thou canst raise and give life, because, above, in the fifth chapter, « as the Father hath life in himself, so he hath given to the Son also to have life in himself ». In this she confesses both the Divinity and the humanity: because *the Son of God,* thou canst; because *thou art come,* thou wilt do it; for to this end thou art sent.

39. *And when she had said these things.* Martha's faithful confession having been set down, here is set down *the faithful confession of Mary,* which is described in this order: first, the *hastening of Mary* herself *when called;* second, the *accompanying by the Jews;* third, the *faithful and devout confession.*

(Vers. 28.). First, then, is set down the *hastening of Mary when called,* in which is signified *quiet* and *devotion* in Mary: *quiet,* because she did not come unless called by Martha; for this reason he says: *And when she had said these things, she went and called Mary her sister in silence;* Lamentations, the third chapter[^p401-8]: « It is good to wait with silence for the salvation of God ». *She called* her, I say, at the command of the Lord; Apocalypse, the last chapter: « And he that heareth, let him say: Come ». And this is hinted, because she called her, *saying: The Teacher is come and calleth for thee.* Although this was not said before, for the sake of brevity, nevertheless it is to be understood to have been so; Job, the fourteenth chapter[^p401-9]: « Thou shalt call me, and I will answer thee ». Mary was not only *quiet,* but also *devout;* whence he says:

40. (Vers. 29.). *She, as soon as she heard, rose*[^p401-10] *quickly and came to him;* she did not wait for him to come, but she herself ran swiftly before he should enter; therefore he says:

41. (Vers. 30.). *For Jesus was not yet come into the town, but he was still in that place where Martha had met him.* In which[^p401-11] is signified that the man called by the Lord ought to hasten, as the good disciples did, of whom Matthew, the fourth chapter[^p401-12]: « And they immediately leaving their nets, followed him »; whence that man is also reproved in Luke, the ninth chapter, who said: « Suffer me first to go and to bury my father »; Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-first chapter: « In all thy works be quick »; and Proverbs, the eighteenth chapter: « He that is loose and slack in his work is the brother of him that wasteth his own works ».

42. (Vers. 31.). *The Jews therefore.* Here is touched the second, namely, *the accompanying by the Jews;* and the reason for accompanying was the will to console; for this reason he says: *The Jews therefore, who were with her in the house and comforted*
<!-- page 402 -->
*her;* for this they had come; *when they saw Mary, that she rose up quickly and went away*[^p402-1], as if touched by grief, not able to endure long; *followed her, saying: She goeth to the grave, to weep there.* Therefore they followed, that they might console, of which consolation Ecclesiasticus, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p402-2]: « Take comfort for the sadness, and make mourning according to his desert for a day or two, for fear of detraction ».

43. (Vers. 32.). *Mary therefore, when she was come.* Here is touched the third, namely, Mary's *faithful and devout confession;* on account of which he says: *Mary therefore, when she was come where Jesus was, seeing him, fell down at his feet;* behold *devotion,* always at the feet of the Lord; whence Luke, the seventh chapter[^p402-3]: « Mary standing behind, at the feet of the Lord »; and the tenth: « Sitting at the feet of the Lord, she heard his word ». It is not read of Martha that she fell at his feet; for she was not so devout nor so warm as Mary; Deuteronomy, the thirty-third chapter[^p402-4]: « They that approach to his feet shall receive of his teaching ». And the *faithful* confession is added: *And she said to him: Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died*[^p402-5]*;* in which she intimates the power of the Lord, as her sister had done before; but that one said more, that he could still raise him, if he willed; and this one out of reverence kept silent and out of devotion wept and moved the Lord the more, as will be plain below[^p402-6]. In which is hinted that the Lord hears the groaning of the heart more than the sound of the mouth; Gregory[^p402-7]: « To pray truly is to sound forth bitter groans in compunction and not composed words »; whence it is said in the first of Kings, the first chapter, that Anna wept abundantly and prayed silently.

### Questions.

44. Quaest. I. But it is asked here: why does the Lord so diligently seek Martha's confession concerning the faith of the resurrection, since he knew that she faithfully believed? And what necessity was there?

It must be said that the faith of the sisters of Lazarus obtained his raising; and because faith is then more certain and firmer when we confess with the mouth: therefore he sought the confession of the mouth[^p402-8].

45. Quaest. II. But then it is asked, of which virtue it rather is to obtain a miracle?

1. And that it is of *faith,* appears: for below, in the same chapter[^p402-9]: *Did not I say to thee, that if thou believe, thou shalt see the glory of God?* — Likewise, Mark, the ninth chapter: *If thou canst believe, all things are possible to him that believeth;* and in the same place it is said that the Lord said to the Apostles that they could not cast out the demon *on account of unbelief.* — And the Apostle, the first to the Corinthians, the thirteenth chapter[^p402-10]: *If I should have faith, so that I could remove mountains.*

2. But that it is of *hope,* appears: for in Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p402-11], it is said to her: *Have confidence, daughter:* therefore it seems that it is of confidence; but confidence belongs to hope.

3. But that it is of *charity,* appears: for it belongs to charity to join to God and to make a friend, and these are most worthy to be heard.

**I respond:** It must be said that, because a miracle is obtained not only by the good, but also by the wicked[^p402-12], it is obtained by *that virtue* which is common to the good and the wicked. Now *faith* and *hope* can be this; therefore a miracle is of faith as of the originating principle, but of *hope* as of the consummating one. And because *confidence* embraces both, properly speaking, the obtaining of a miracle is of *confidence*[^p402-13]. — Note that the working of a miracle is owed to *faith,* because it is *common;* because it first *elevates the intellect*[^p402-14]*;* because it has to *consider omnipotence.*
