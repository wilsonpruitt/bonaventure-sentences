### Capitulum VI. *Post haec abiit Iesus.*

### Collatio XXVI.

(Vers. 11.). *Accepit Iesus panes, et cum gratias egisset, distribuit discumbentibus.*

1. *Accepit,* inquit, *Iesus.* Notabiliter dicitur Iesus panes accepisse et distribuisse. *Distribuente Domino,* abundat cibus; *distribuente* vero *homine,* esurit populus. Dicuntur autem tria hic, scilicet quod *accepit,* quod *gratias egit* et quod *distribuit: accepit* ad multiplicandum, *gratias egit* ad benedicendum, *distribuit* ad reficiendum.

2. *Accepit,* inquit, *panes.* Sed quot panes? *Quinque panes hordeaceos,* ait Evangelista[^p565-1], *et duos pisces.* — Isti quinque panes, spiritualiter intelligendo, significant quinque panes, quibus anima reficitur in praesenti, panem scilicet *poenitentiae,* panem *doctrinae,* panem *iustitiae,* panem *patientiae* et panem *eucharistiae.* — Panis *poenitentiae* reficit *revertentes;* panis *doctrinae* reficit *ignorantes;* panis *iustitiae* reficit *laborantes;* panis *patientiae* reficit *pugnantes;* panis *eucharistiae* reficit *omnes.*

3. Primus igitur panis est panis *poenitentiae,* de quo in Psalmo[^p565-2]: « Cinerem tanquam panem manducabam, et potum meum cum fletu miscebam ». Cinis est *minutus* et est *amarus.* Cineris *minutio* significat peccatorum recordationem, quae debent memorari usque ad minima; cineris *amaritudo,* peccatorum contritionem, quae debet esse amara. Haec autem duo habet panis poenitentiae, secundum illud Isaiae trigesimo octavo[^p565-3]: « Recogitabo tibi omnes annos meos in amaritudine animae meae ». — De isto eodem pane significatum est Exodi duodecimo, ubi legitur, quod « filii Israel coxerunt farinam, quam dudum conspersam tulerant de Aegypto, et fecerunt subcinericios panes ». — Iste panis *hordeaceus* est: *panis,* quia habet *refectionem; hordeaceus,* quia habet *asperitatem.* Reficit enim spiritum et macerat carnem; unde bene significatur Iudicum septimo per panem subcinericium, qui confregit castra Madian; de quo sequitur, quia « non erat aliud nisi gladius Gedeonis ». Est enim *panis* spiritui et *gladius* carni.

4. Secundus panis est panis *doctrinae.* Quemadmodum enim cibus corporalis reficit carnem, ita doctrina spiritualis reficit mentem, secundum illud Deuteronomii octavo[^p565-4]: « Non in solo pane vivit homo, sed in omni verbo, quod procedit de ore Dei »; item Matthaei quarto. Hic est panis *scholarium,* qui facit ad cognitionem veritatis, de quo Lucae ultimo: « Cognoverunt Dominum in fractione panis »; *panem frangere* est Scripturam exponere. — Et iste panis *hordeaceus est.* Nam habet in *refectione asperitatem,* secundum illud ad Hebraeos duodecimo[^p565-5]: « Omnis disciplina in praesenti videtur esse non gaudii, sed moeroris; postea autem fructum pacatissimum reddit » etc.

5. Tertius panis est panis *iustitiae.* Hic est panis *servientium,* de quo Proverbiorum duodecimo[^p565-6]: « Qui operatur terram suam satiabitur panibus; qui autem sectatur otium stultissimus est ». De isto pane qui non laborat non manducet; qui vero laborat non indiget, secundum illud Psalmi: « Non vidi iustum derelictum, nec semen eius quaerens panem ». — Iste panis *hordeaceus* est. Habet enim cum *suavitate* refectionis *asperitatem* laboris, secundum illud Genesis tertio: « In sudore vultus tui vesceris pane tuo ». — De isto figuratum est Numerorum undecimo, ubi dicitur, quod « circuibat populus et colligens manna, frangebat mola, sive terebat in mortariolo, coquens in olla et faciens ex eo tortulas saporis quasi panis oleati ». Nota et expone.

6. Quartus panis est panis *patientiae.* Hic est panis *pugnantium,* de quo Ezechielis duodecimo[^p565-7]: « Panem suum in solitudine comedent, et aquam suam in desolatione bibent ». — Iste panis *hordeaceus* est. Habet enim et *refectionem* et *asperitatem: refectionem* animae et *asperitatem* sive duritiam poenae; quod *figuratum* est Iosue nono, ubi legitur de Gabaonitis, quod « panes, quos portabant ob viaticum, duri erant et in frusta comminuti ». Verumtamen duritia panis, quantacumque sit, temperari potest in sanguine passionis, secundum illud Bernardi[^p565-8]: « Si passio Christi ad memoriam reducitur, nihil tam durum, quod non aequanimiter toleretur ».

<!-- page 566 -->
7. Quintus panis est panis *eucharistiae.* Hic est panis *nuptialis,* qui reficit omnes in mensa eucharistiae, de quo praedicitur Ioannis sexto[^p566-1]: « Panis, quem ego dabo vobis, caro mea est ». — Hic est panis *hordeaceus* in praesenti, licet in futuro sit *triticeus,* quia purus; in praesenti hordeaceus, quia cum afflictione animae assumendus, secundum illud primae ad Corinthios undecimo: « Probet se ipsum homo, et sic de pane illo edat et de calice bibat ».

8. Sequitur: *Et duos pisces.* Per duos pisces intellige duas consolationes, quas habet anima in praesenti, quasi ad mitigandas praedictorum panum asperitates[^p566-2]. Prima est *exspectatio bonorum aeternorum;* secunda est *experientia spiritualium.* Prima consolatione sustentatur anima in *adversitate temporali;* secunda reficitur in *prosperitate spirituali.* Prima consolatio est quasi piscis marinus, quia capitur in adversitate, scilicet in aqua amara; secunda, quasi piscis fluvialis, quia capitur in prosperitate, quasi in aqua dulci, secundum illud Psalmi[^p566-3]: « Ponam in mari manum eius et in fluminibus dexteram eius ».

### Collatio XXVII.

(Vers. 19.). *Vident Iesum ambulantem supra mare.*

1. Notandum est, quod quatuor miracula fecit Iesus in mari. Primum fecit in mirabili maris *divisione,* Exodi decimo quarto[^p566-4]; secundum in mirabili maris *quietatione,* Matthaei octavo; tertium in mirabili maris *calcatione,* Ioannis sexto; quartum in mirabili *piscium congregatione,* Lucae quinto et Ioannis ultimo. Collige ergo, quia Dominus mare *dividit,* mare *quietat,* mare *calcat,* in mari *pisces congregat.*

2. Ad quae intelligenda nota, quod est quadruplex mare, scilicet mare *interius,* mare *exterius,* mare *inferius* et mare *superius.* — Mare *interius* est mare *peccatricis conscientiae;* mare *exterius* est mare *temporalis miseriae;* mare *inferius* est mare *negotiationis mundanae;* mare *superius* est mare *catholicae Ecclesiae.* — Mare ergo est *peccatrix conscientia* propter sui *inquietudinem;* Isaiae quinquagesimo septimo[^p566-5]: « Impii quasi mare fervens, quod quiescere non potest »; et Threnorum secundo: « Magna est velut mare contritio tua ». — Mare iterum est *temporalis miseria* propter sui *amaritudinem,* vel *profunditatem;* Ionae secundo: « Proiecisti me in profundum in corde maris, et flumen circumdedit me »; et in Psalmo: « Veni in altitudinem maris, et tempestas demersit me ». — Mare est *negotiatio mundana* propter sui *periculositatem;* Ecclesiastici quadragesimo tertio[^p566-6]: « Qui navigant mare enarrent pericula eius »; et in Psalmo: « Hoc mare magnum et spatiosum manibus; illic reptilia, quorum non est numerus ». — Mare *superius,* hoc est Ecclesia propter sui *fecunditatem* et *plenitudinem;* Isaiae sexagesimo: « Tunc videbis et afflues, et mirabitur et dilatabitur cor tuum, quando conversa fuerit ad te multitudo maris » etc.; et Matthaei decimo tertio: « Simile est regnum caelorum sagenae missae in mare et ex omni genere piscium congreganti, quam cum impleta esset » etc. De hoc mari dicitur in Psalmo: « Quoniam ipsius est mare, et ipse fecit illud ». Sic ergo est quadruplex mare.

3. Sed ecce, Dominus *dividit* mare, *quietat* mare, *calcat* mare et *congregat pisces* in mari. — Nota ergo, quod Dominus *dividit* mare *peccatricis conscientiae; quietat* mare *temporalis miseriae; calcat* mare *negotiationis mundanae; congregat pisces* in mari *Ecclesiae.* — *Dividit* mare *conscientiae,* et hoc *poenitentibus,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p566-7]: « Divisit mare rubrum in divisiones »; *divisio maris* est scissio cordis per contritionem, secundum illud Ioel secundo: « Scindite corda vestra » etc. — Haec divisio maris significata est, Exodi decimo quarto, in illo miraculo, quando divisit Dominus mare filiis Israel, quando exierunt de Aegypto. Unde dixit Moysi: « Eleva virgam tuam et extende manum tuam super mare, et divide illud, ut gradiantur filii Israel in medio mari per siccum ».

4. Dominus *tranquillat* mare *temporalis miseriae,* et hoc *proficientibus,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p566-8]: « Tu dominaris potestati maris, motum autem fluctuum eius tu mitigas »; et Ecclesiastici quadragesimo tertio: « In sermone eius siluit ventus, et in cogitatione sua placavit abyssum »; *maris placatio* est mitigatio tribulationis, de qua primae ad Corinthios decimo: « Fidelis Deus est, qui non patietur, vos tentari supra id quod potestis ». — Haec placatio figurata est in illo miraculo Matthaei octavo, quando « consurgens Iesus imperavit ventis et mari, et facta est tranquillitas magna ».

5. Dominus *calcat* mare *negotiationis mundanae,* id est calcabile facit, et hoc viris *perfectis,* sicut
<!-- page 567 -->
fecit Petro Matthaei decimo quarto[^p567-1] dicenti: « Domine, si tu es, iube, me venire ad te super aquas. At ille ait: Veni » etc. *Calcatio aquarum* est contemptus temporalium, quae « ad nihilum deveniunt, sicut aqua decurrens »; et hoc etiam figuratum est in praesenti miraculo, de quo hic dicitur: *Vident Iesum ambulantem supra mare.*

6. Dominus etiam *congregat pisces* in mari *Ecclesiae.* Ipse enim est, qui « filios Dei, qui erant dispersi, congregavit in unum », secundum quod habetur Ioannis undecimo[^p567-2]. Et hoc facit Dominus praedicatoribus et praelatis, quibus ipse dicit Matthaei quarto: « Venite post me; faciam, vos fieri piscatores hominum »; *piscatio hominum* est conversio fidelium. — Haec figurata est in illo miraculo, quod fecit Dominus Lucae quinto, cum dixit Petro: « Duc in altum, et laxate retia in capturam »; et sequitur, quod « cum hoc fecissent, concluserunt piscium multitudinem copiosam ». Simile miraculum habetur Ioannis ultimo.

Dominus igitur *dividit* mare *conscientiae peccatricis; quietat* mare *miseriae temporalis; calcat* mare *mundanae negotiationis; congregat pisces* in mari *Ecclesiae militantis;* et hoc est quadruplex miraculum, quod ostendit Dominus spiritualiter in mari discipulis suis.

### Collatio XXVIII.

(Vers. 44.) *Nemo potest venire ad me, nisi Pater meus, qui in caelis est, traxerit eum.*

1. In verbis istis duo facit Dominus. Primo, ne homo *excedat* per praesumtionem, ostendit *defectum humanae impotentiae,* dicens: *Nemo potest venire ad me* — secundo, ne *corruat* homo per desperationem, subiungit *auxilium miserationis divinae,* cum dicit: *Nisi Pater meus, qui in caelis est, traxerit eum.*

2. Dicit ergo: *Nemo potest venire ad me.* Nemo, inquam, potest *ex se;* cuius causa est triplex, scilicet quia *homo est gravis* — quia *via difficilis* — quia *virtus debilis.* Virtus enim debilis non potest ferre molem gravem per viam difficilem. — *Nemo* ergo *potest venire ad me,* quia *moles est gravis;* Sapientiae nono[^p567-5]: « Corpus, quod corrumpitur, aggravat animam, et terrena inhabitatio deprimit sensum multa cogitantem »; et Iob septimo: « Factus sum mihimetipsi gravis ». — Quia *via difficilis;* Matthaei septimo: « Quam angusta et arcta est via, quae ducit ad vitam »; ecce, quia difficilis ad *progrediendum;* « et pauci inveniunt eam »; ecce, quia difficilis ad *arripiendum.* — Quia *virtus est debilis;* Ioannis decimo quinto[^p567-6]: « Sine me nihil potestis facere »; et Ioannis decimo quinto: « Sicut palmes non potest facere fructum a semetipso, nisi manserit in vite; sic nec vos, nisi in me manseritis.

3. Sequitur: *Nisi Pater meus, qui in caelis est, traxerit eum.* — Ostenso defectu humanae impotentiae, adhibet Dominus Iesus *remedium pietatis paternae,* cum dicit: *Nisi Pater meus traxerit eum.* Tractio enim Patris remedium est humanae impossibilitatis. — Notandum autem, quod Pater trahit nos multipliciter. Solet enim aliquid trahi sursum *fune,* qualiter trahitur moles in tracha. Solet etiam aliquid trahi sursum *pondere,* qualiter trahitur stater[^p567-7] in statera. Solet aliquid trahi sursum *occulta virtute,* qualiter trahitur ferrum ab adamante, quod non per violentiam sive per vim manifestam trahitur, sed a tota specie.

4. Per hunc modum trahit Pater sursum *funibus flagellorum;* unde Osee undecimo[^p567-8]: « In funiculis Adam traham eos in vinculis caritatis »; *funiculi Adam* poenalitates humani generis sunt, quibus Dominus ligavit Adam, quando insanivit per culpam. In his trahit Deus animam, quam per flagella reducit ad gratiam.

5. Trahit etiam *pondere beneficiorum,* quasi ponens in statera beneficia sua, ut sursum trahat corda nostra; Ieremiae trigesimo primo[^p567-9]: « In caritate perpetua dilexi te; ideo attraxi te, miserans tui ». Multas enim miserationes appendit Dominus, ut nos attraheret; nec hoc illi sufficiebat, nisi pro nobis attrahendis se ipsum in statera crucis appenderet. Appendit ergo in statera crucis se ipsum, ut traheret ad se per crucem genus humanum. Unde Ioannis duodecimo: « Cum exaltatus fuero a terra, omnia traham ad me ipsum ».

6. Trahit etiam Dominus sursum *occulta virtute spiritualium desideriorum.* Immittit quidem Deus spirituale desiderium et trahit ad se occulto cordis
<!-- page 568 -->
desiderio cor humanum; unde Iob trigesimo quarto[^p568-1]: « Si direxerit ad eum homo cor suum, spiritum illius et flatum ad se trahet ». Nota Glossam in Canticorum primum: « Trahe me post te, in odorem curremus unguentorum tuorum ». — Sic igitur trahit Pater sursum.

7. Sed non omnis attrahitur; quia est qui trahit *deorsum,* diabolus scilicet, de quo Iob vigesimo primo[^p568-2]: « Dulcis fuit glareis Cocyti, et post se omnem hominem trahet et ante se innumerabiles ». Trahit autem tripliciter: per *superbiam,* per *avaritiam,* per *luxuriam.* — Per *superbiam* trahit *ambitiosos.* Isti trahuntur in *cauda;* Apocalypsis duodecimo: « Cauda draconis trahebat tertiam partem stellarum caeli et misit eas in terram ». Bene dicit: *tertiam partem,* quia alia pars intendit *bonae actioni,* alia pars *contemplationi,* tertia *ambitioni;* et haec trahitur in cauda draconis. — Trahit etiam per *avaritiam,* et sic trahit *cupidos.* Isti trahuntur in *unda fluminis;* de quo Apocalypsis duodecimo[^p568-3]: « Misit serpens ex ore suo post mulierem aquam tanquam flumen, ut eam faceret trahi a flumine ». Flumen istud a *fluendo* divitiae sunt, quae superfluunt et refluunt et avarorum corda trahunt. — Trahit etiam per *luxuriam,* et sic trahit *lascivos.* Isti trahuntur *blanditiis mulieris* meretricis, id est *carnalis voluptatis;* de qua Proverbiorum septimo: « Irretivit eum mulier multis sermonibus et blanditiis labiorum protraxit illum; statim eam sequitur, quasi bos ductus ad victimam, et quasi agnus lasciviens et ignorans, quod ad vincula stultus trahatur » etc.

### Collatio XXIX.

(Vers. 67.). *Multi discipulorum eius abierunt retro.*

1. Circa recessum discipulorum a Christo nota recedendi *causam* — recedendi *modum* — et recedendi *fructum.* — Circa primum notandum, quod quidam *retrahuntur,* ne ad Christum veniant; quidam *extrahuntur,* postquam pervenerunt, ne cum ipso permaneant.

2. Venientes autem ad Christum *retrahit* triplex causa, scilicet amor *carnalis voluptatis,* amor *terrenae proprietatis*[^p568-4] et amor *propriae voluntatis.* — Quidam abeunt a Christo amore *voluptatis;* de quibus Ieremiae septimo[^p568-5]: « Abierunt in voluptatibus suis et pravitate cordis sui, factique retrorsum et non in ante ». — Quidam abeunt amore *terrenae proprietatis,* sicut ille adolescens, cui dictum est Matthaei decimo nono: « Vade et vende omnia, quae habes, et da pauperibus »; at ille « abiit tristis; erat enim multas possessiones habens ». — Quidam abeunt amore *propriae voluntatis,* scilicet qui nolunt « captivare se ipsos in obsequium Christi[^p568-6] » quemadmodum discipuli, qui, nolentes se ipsos relinquere propter Christi doctrinam, reliquerunt doctrinam et adhaeserunt sibi; de quibus hic dicitur, quod *multi discipulorum eius abierunt retro.* — Haec ergo sunt tria, quae retrahunt ad Christum accedentes.

3. Sunt etiam tria, quae a Christo *extrahunt* incedentes, ne cum ipso perseverent. Primum est *memoria bonorum temporalium;* secundum, *fastidium bonorum spiritualium;* tertium, *desperatio aeternorum.* — Propter *memoriam bonorum temporalium* recedebant qui dicebant Exodi decimo sexto[^p568-7]: « Utinam mortui essemus per manum Domini in terra Aegypti, quando sedebamus super ollas carnium et comedebamus panem in saturitate »! Numerorum undecimo: « Recordamur piscium, quos comedebamus in Aegypto gratis; in mentem nobis veniunt cucumeres et pepones ». — Propter *fastidium spiritualium bonorum* recedebant filii Israel, Numerorum vigesimo primo, cum dicebant: « Anima nostra nauseat super cibo isto levissimo », spiritualiter exponendo; et Numerorum undecimo: « Arida est anima nostra, nihil aliud respiciunt oculi nostri nisi manna ». — Propter *desperationem aeternorum* recedunt qui dicunt cum filiis Israel, Numerorum decimo quarto[^p568-8]: « Utinam pereamus, et non inducat nos Dominus in terram istam, ne cadamus gladio! Nonne melius est reverti in Aegyptum »? Et propter hoc dixerunt: « Constituamus nobis ducem et revertamur in Aegyptum ». Haec dicebant timentes difficilem terrae introitum, secundum illorum opinionem, qui dixerunt supra decimo tertio: « Terra devorat habitatores suos ». Verum tamen magis est credendum Caleb et Iosue dicentibus Numerorum decimo quarto: « Terra, quam circuivimus, valde bona est; si propitius fuerit Dominus, inducet nos in eam ».

4. *Multi discipulorum abierunt retro.* Visa abeundi *causa,* nota abeundi *modum,* qui triplex est. Quidam enim abeunt retro *aspiciendo* per *cogitationem;* quidam, retro *aspirando* per *affectionem;* quidam, retro *incedendo* per *conversationem.* — Retro *aspexit* uxor Lot, et retro aspiciendo, Genesis de-
<!-- page 569 -->
cimo nono[^p569-1], « versa est in statuam salis »; nota historiam et expone; et Lucae nono: « Nemo mittens manum suam ad aratrum et respiciens retro aptus est regno Dei ». Non sic ambulant animalia sancta; Ezechielis primo: « Non revertebantur animalia, cum incederent, sed unumquodque ante faciem suam gradiebatur ». — Retro *aspirabant* filii Israel, Numerorum decimo quarto: « Nonne melius est reverti in Aegyptum »? Et iterum: « Constituamus nobis ducem » etc. Non sic dicebat Apostolus ad Philippenses tertio, qui « omnia arbitratus est ut stercora, ut Christum lucrifaceret ». — Retro *incedebant* discipuli, de quibus hic dicitur: *Multi discipulorum abierunt retro.* Non sic Petrus[^p569-2]: « Domine, ad quem ibimus? Verba vitae aeternae habes ». — Dimittamus igitur quae retro sunt, et dirigamus ad ea quae ante sunt, *aspectum, affectum* et *incessum,* cum Apostolo dicente ad Philippenses tertio: « Ea quae retro sunt obliviscens », non retro aspiciens, sicut uxor Lot; « ad ea quae sunt priora, me ipsum extendens », non ad praeterita respiciens, sicut filii Israel; « ad destinatum persequor bravium », non retrocedendo cum carnalibus discipulis, sed incedendo cum Apostolis, « si quo modo comprehendam, in quo et comprehensus sum ».

5. *Discipuli,* inquit, *abierunt retro.* Post recedendi *modum* nota recedendi *fructum,* qui triplex est. — Recedentes a Christo amittunt *praemium gloriae;* Lucae nono[^p569-3]: « Nemo mittens manum ad aratrum et respiciens retro aptus est regno Dei ». Si non est *dignus regno* qui retro *aspicit,* multo minus est dignus qui retro *aspirat,* vel retro *incedit.* — Incurrunt *remorsum conscientiae;* Genesis decimo nono, de uxore Lot, quae « respiciens retro versa est in statuam salis »; in sale nota amaritudinem conscientiae. — Merentur *supplicium gehennae;* Ieremiae decimo quinto: « Reliquisti me, abiisti retrorsum; extendam manum meam super te et interficiam te ». Exemplum de Semei, tertii Regum secundo.

### Annotatio 24.

(Vers. 15.). *Fugit in montem solus.*

Nota, quod solitudo valet ad tria, scilicet ad *mundi turbationem evitandam;* Psalmus[^p569-4]: « Elongavi fugiens et mansi in solitudine ». — Ad *quietem contemplationis intrandam;* Threnorum tertio: « Sedebit solitarius et tacebit, quia levavit se super se » etc. — Ad *divinam consolationem obtinendam;* Osee secundo: « Lactabo eam et ducam eam in solitudinem et loquar ad cor eius ».

### Annotatio 25.

(Vers. 19.). *Vident Iesum ambulantem supra mare.*

Nota, quod in isto miraculo Dominus tria mirabilia fecit: mare *calcavit* et mare *placavit* et mare *transvolare fecit.* — *Calcavit;* unde dicitur hic: *Vident eum ambulantem supra mare.* — *Placavit;* unde dicitur Marci sexto[^p569-5], quod « ascendit ad illos in navem, et cessavit ventus ». — Mare *transvolare fecit;* unde dicitur hic, quod « statim fuit navis ad terram, in quam ibant ». — Et haec significant quae facit Dominus fidelibus tribulatis. Mare *calcat,* quando tribulationem *suppeditat;* mare *placat,* quando tribulationem *mitigat;* mare *transit,* quando tribulatio *cessat.* Facit enim Dominus, ut sit tribulatio *brevis* duratione, et tunc mare *transitur;* ut sit *levis* afflictione, et tunc mare *placatur;* ut sit *nulla* reputatione, et sic mare *calcatur.*

### Annotatio 26.

(Vers. 26.). *Quaeritis me, non quia vidistis signa, sed quia manducastis ex panibus.*

Nota, quod quaeritur Christus aliquando causa *commoditatis temporalis,* ut hic: *Quaeritis me* etc. — Aliquando causa *persecutionis;* Matthaei secundo[^p569-6]: « Surge et accipe puerum et Matrem eius et fuge in Aegyptum et esto ibi, usque dum dicam tibi. Futurum enim est, ut Herodes quaerat puerum ad perdendum eum ». — Aliquando causa *devotionis;* Lucae secundo: « Fili, quid fecisti nobis sic? Ego et pater tuus dolentes quaerebamus te ».

### Annotatio 27.

(Vers. 33.). *Panis Dei est, qui de caelo descendit et dat vitam mundo.*

Nota, quod panis iste excellit omnem alium panem; *delectat* enim *mentem, roborat virtutem, continuat vitam.* — *Delectat mentem;* Sapientiae decimo sexto[^p569-7]: « Angelorum esca nutrivisti populum tuum, et paratum panem de caelo praestitisti illis sine labore, omne delectamentum in se habentem et omnis saporis suavitatem ». — *Roborat virtutem;* Psalmus: « Panis confirmat cor hominis ». — *Continuat vitam;* unde hic dicitur, quod *dat vitam mundo;* et Ioannis sexto: « Si quis manducaverit ex hoc pane, vivet in aeternum ». — Primum pertinet ad *contemplationem;* secundum, ad *actionem;* sed tertium, ad *utrumque.*

### Chapter VI. *After these things Jesus went.*

### Collation XXVI.

(Verse 11.). *And Jesus took the loaves, and when he had given thanks, he distributed to them that were set down.*

1. *Took,* he says, *Jesus.* It is said notably that Jesus took the loaves and distributed them. *When the Lord distributes,* food abounds; but *when man distributes,* the people hungers. Now three things are said here, namely that he *took,* that he *gave thanks* and that he *distributed: he took* in order to multiply, *he gave thanks* in order to bless, *he distributed* in order to refresh.

2. *Took,* he says, *the loaves.* But how many loaves? *Five barley loaves,* says the Evangelist[^p565-1], *and two fishes.* — These five loaves, understanding spiritually, signify five loaves by which the soul is refreshed in the present, namely the bread of *penance,* the bread of *teaching*[^tr-doctrina], the bread of *justice,* the bread of *patience* and the bread of the *eucharist.* — The bread of *penance* refreshes *those returning;* the bread of *teaching* refreshes *the ignorant;* the bread of *justice* refreshes *those labouring;* the bread of *patience* refreshes *those fighting;* the bread of the *eucharist* refreshes *all.*

3. The first bread therefore is the bread of *penance,* of which in the Psalm[^p565-2]: « I did eat ashes like bread, and mingled my drink with weeping ». Ash is *minute* and is *bitter.* The *minuteness* of the ash signifies the remembrance of sins, which ought to be recalled down to the least; the *bitterness* of the ash, the contrition for sins, which ought to be bitter. Now the bread of penance has these two, according to that of Isaias, the thirty-eighth chapter[^p565-3]: « I will recount to thee all my years in the bitterness of my soul ». — This same bread is signified in Exodus, the twelfth chapter, where it is read that « the children of Israel baked the meal, which a little before they had brought out of Egypt in dough, and they made hearth cakes ». — This bread is *of barley:* *bread,* because it has *refreshment; of barley,* because it has *roughness.* For it refreshes the spirit and macerates the flesh; whence it is well signified in Judges, the seventh chapter, by the hearth cake that overthrew the camp of Madian; of which it follows that « it was nothing else but the sword of Gedeon ». For it is *bread* to the spirit and a *sword* to the flesh.

4. The second bread is the bread of *teaching.* For just as bodily food refreshes the flesh, so spiritual teaching refreshes the mind, according to that of Deuteronomy, the eighth chapter[^p565-4]: « Not in bread alone doth man live, but in every word that proceedeth from the mouth of God »; likewise Matthew, the fourth chapter. This is the bread of *scholars,* which makes for the knowledge of truth, of which Luke, the last chapter: « They knew the Lord in the breaking of bread »; *to break bread* is to expound Scripture. — And this bread is *of barley.* For it has *roughness in the refreshment,* according to that to the Hebrews, the twelfth chapter[^p565-5]: « Every discipline for the present seemeth not to bring with it joy, but sorrow; but afterwards it yieldeth the most peaceable fruit » etc.

5. The third bread is the bread of *justice.* This is the bread of *those who serve,* of which Proverbs, the twelfth chapter[^p565-6]: « He that tilleth his land shall be satisfied with bread; but he that pursueth idleness is very foolish ». Of this bread, he who does not labour, let him not eat; but he who labours is not in want, according to that of the Psalm: « I have not seen the just forsaken, nor his seed seeking bread ». — This bread is *of barley.* For it has, with the *sweetness* of refreshment, the *roughness* of labour, according to that of Genesis, the third chapter: « In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat thy bread ». — Of this it was figured in Numbers, the eleventh chapter, where it is said that « the people went about, and gathering manna, ground it in a mill, or beat it in a mortar, boiling it in a pot, and making cakes thereof of the taste as of bread tempered with oil ». Note and expound.

6. The fourth bread is the bread of *patience.* This is the bread of *those who fight,* of which Ezechiel, the twelfth chapter[^p565-7]: « They shall eat their bread in solitude, and drink their water in desolation ». — This bread is *of barley.* For it has both *refreshment* and *roughness: refreshment* of the soul and *roughness* or hardness of punishment; which is *figured* in Josue, the ninth chapter, where it is read of the Gabaonites that « the loaves, which they carried for provision by the way, were hard and broken in pieces ». Nevertheless the hardness of the bread, however great it be, can be tempered in the blood of the passion, according to that of Bernard[^p565-8]: « If the passion of Christ is brought back to memory, nothing is so hard that it is not borne with an even mind ».

<!-- page 566 -->
7. The fifth bread is the bread of the *eucharist.* This is the *nuptial* bread, which refreshes all at the table of the eucharist, of which it is foretold in John, the sixth chapter[^p566-1]: « The bread that I will give you is my flesh ». — This bread is *of barley* in the present, although in the future it is *of wheat,* because pure; in the present of barley, because it is to be taken with affliction of soul, according to that of the first to the Corinthians, the eleventh chapter: « Let a man prove himself, and so let him eat of that bread and drink of the chalice ».

8. There follows: *And two fishes.* By the two fishes understand two consolations, which the soul has in the present, as it were for softening the roughnesses of the aforesaid loaves[^p566-2]. The first is the *expectation of eternal goods;* the second is the *experience of spiritual things.* By the first consolation the soul is sustained in *temporal adversity;* by the second it is refreshed in *spiritual prosperity.* The first consolation is as a sea fish, because it is caught in adversity, namely in bitter water; the second, as a river fish, because it is caught in prosperity, as it were in sweet water, according to that of the Psalm[^p566-3]: « I will set his hand in the sea, and his right hand in the rivers ».

### Collation XXVII.

(Verse 19.). *They see Jesus walking upon the sea.*

1. It is to be noted that Jesus worked four miracles on the sea. The first he worked in the wonderful *dividing* of the sea, Exodus, the fourteenth chapter[^p566-4]; the second in the wonderful *stilling* of the sea, Matthew, the eighth chapter; the third in the wonderful *treading* of the sea, John, the sixth chapter; the fourth in the wonderful *gathering of fishes,* Luke, the fifth chapter, and John, the last chapter. Gather therefore that the Lord *divides* the sea, *stills* the sea, *treads* the sea, *gathers fishes* in the sea.

2. For understanding which, note that the sea is fourfold, namely the *inner* sea, the *outer* sea, the *lower* sea and the *upper* sea. — The *inner* sea is the sea of the *sinful conscience;* the *outer* sea is the sea of *temporal misery;* the *lower* sea is the sea of *worldly trafficking;* the *upper* sea is the sea of the *catholic Church.* — The sea, then, is the *sinful conscience* on account of its *unquiet;* Isaias, the fifty-seventh chapter[^p566-5]: « The wicked are like the raging sea, which cannot rest »; and Lamentations, the second chapter: « Great as the sea is thy destruction ». — The sea again is *temporal misery* on account of its *bitterness,* or *depth;* Jonas, the second chapter: « Thou hast cast me forth into the deep in the heart of the sea, and a flood hath compassed me »; and in the Psalm: « I am come into the depth of the sea, and a tempest hath overwhelmed me ». — The sea is *worldly trafficking* on account of its *perilousness;* Ecclesiasticus, the forty-third chapter[^p566-6]: « Let them that sail on the sea tell the dangers thereof »; and in the Psalm: « So is this great sea, which stretcheth wide its arms; there are creeping things without number ». — The *upper* sea, that is, the Church, on account of its *fruitfulness* and *fulness;* Isaias, the sixtieth chapter: « Then shalt thou see, and abound, and thy heart shall wonder and be enlarged, when the multitude of the sea shall be converted to thee » etc.; and Matthew, the thirteenth chapter: « The kingdom of heaven is like to a net cast into the sea, and gathering together of all kind of fishes, which, when it was filled » etc. Of this sea it is said in the Psalm: « For the sea is his, and he made it ». So, then, the sea is fourfold.

3. But behold, the Lord *divides* the sea, *stills* the sea, *treads* the sea and *gathers fishes* in the sea. — Note therefore that the Lord *divides* the sea of the *sinful conscience; stills* the sea of *temporal misery; treads* the sea of *worldly trafficking; gathers fishes* in the sea of the *Church.* — *He divides* the sea of the *conscience,* and this for *the penitent,* according to that of the Psalm[^p566-7]: « Who divided the Red Sea into parts »; the *dividing of the sea* is the rending of the heart through contrition, according to that of Joel, the second chapter: « Rend your hearts » etc. — This dividing of the sea is signified, Exodus, the fourteenth chapter, in that miracle when the Lord divided the sea for the children of Israel, when they went out of Egypt. Whence he said to Moses: « Lift up thy rod, and stretch forth thy hand over the sea, and divide it, that the children of Israel may go through the midst of the sea on dry ground ».

4. The Lord *calms* the sea of *temporal misery,* and this for *those making progress,* according to that of the Psalm[^p566-8]: « Thou rulest the power of the sea, and appeasest the motion of the waves thereof »; and Ecclesiasticus, the forty-third chapter: « At his word the wind is still, and with his thought he appeaseth the deep »; the *appeasing of the sea* is the softening of tribulation, of which the first to the Corinthians, the tenth chapter: « God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that which you are able ». — This appeasing is figured in that miracle of Matthew, the eighth chapter, when « Jesus rising up commanded the winds and the sea, and there came a great calm ».

5. The Lord *treads* the sea of *worldly trafficking,* that is, makes it treadable, and this for *perfect* men, as
<!-- page 567 -->
he did for Peter, Matthew, the fourteenth chapter[^p567-1], who said: « Lord, if it be thou, bid me come to thee upon the waters. And he said: Come » etc. The *treading of the waters* is the contempt of temporal things, which « shall come to nothing, like water running down »; and this too is figured in the present miracle, of which it is said here: *They see Jesus walking upon the sea.*

6. The Lord also *gathers fishes* in the sea of the *Church.* For it is he who « gathered together in one the children of God, that were dispersed », according to what is had in John, the eleventh chapter[^p567-2]. And this the Lord does for preachers and prelates, to whom he himself says, Matthew, the fourth chapter: « Come ye after me; I will make you to be fishers of men »; the *fishing of men* is the conversion of the faithful. — This is figured in that miracle which the Lord worked, Luke, the fifth chapter, when he said to Peter: « Launch out into the deep, and let down your nets for a draught »; and it follows that « when they had done this, they enclosed a very great multitude of fishes ». A like miracle is had in John, the last chapter.

The Lord therefore *divides* the sea of the *sinful conscience; stills* the sea of *temporal misery; treads* the sea of *worldly trafficking; gathers fishes* in the sea of the *Church militant;* and this is the fourfold miracle which the Lord showed spiritually on the sea to his disciples.

### Collation XXVIII.

(Verse 44.) *No man can come to me, except my Father, who is in heaven, draw him.*

1. In these words the Lord does two things. First, lest man *overstep* through presumption, he shows the *defect of human impotence,* saying: *No man can come to me* — second, lest man *fall* through despair, he adds the *help of divine mercy,* when he says: *Except my Father, who is in heaven, draw him.*

2. He says therefore: *No man can come to me.* No man, I say, can *of himself;* the cause of which is threefold, namely because *man is heavy* — because *the way is difficult* — because *the strength is weak.* For a weak strength cannot carry a heavy mass along a difficult way. — *No man* therefore *can come to me,* because *the mass is heavy;* Wisdom, the ninth chapter[^p567-5]: « The corruptible body is a load upon the soul, and the earthly habitation presseth down the mind that museth upon many things »; and Job, the seventh chapter: « I am become burdensome to myself ». — Because *the way is difficult;* Matthew, the seventh chapter: « How narrow and strait is the way that leadeth to life »; behold, because it is difficult for *going forward;* « and few there are that find it »; behold, because it is difficult for *laying hold of.* — Because *the strength is weak;* John, the fifteenth chapter[^p567-6]: « Without me you can do nothing »; and John, the fifteenth chapter: « As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, unless it abide in the vine; so neither can you, unless you abide in me.

3. There follows: *Except my Father, who is in heaven, draw him.* — Having shown the defect of human impotence, the Lord Jesus applies the *remedy of fatherly piety,* when he says: *Except my Father draw him.* For the drawing of the Father is the remedy of human impossibility. — Now it is to be noted that the Father draws us in many ways. For a thing is wont to be drawn upward by a *rope,* in the way a mass is drawn on a sledge. A thing is wont also to be drawn upward by a *weight,* in the way the stater[^p567-7] is drawn in the balance. A thing is wont to be drawn upward by a *hidden power,* in the way iron is drawn by the lodestone, which is drawn not through violence or through manifest force, but by the whole species.

4. In this way the Father draws upward by the *ropes of scourges;* whence Osee, the eleventh chapter[^p567-8]: « I will draw them with the cords of Adam, with the bands of charity »; the *cords of Adam* are the penalties of the human race, with which the Lord bound Adam when he went mad through fault. In these God draws the soul, which through scourges he leads back to grace.

5. He draws also by the *weight of benefits,* as though placing his benefits in the balance, in order to draw our hearts upward; Jeremias, the thirty-first chapter[^p567-9]: « I have loved thee with a perpetual charity; therefore have I drawn thee, taking pity on thee ». For the Lord weighed out many mercies in order to draw us; nor did this suffice him, unless, for drawing us, he weighed out himself in the balance of the cross. He weighed out himself therefore in the balance of the cross, in order to draw the human race to himself through the cross. Whence John, the twelfth chapter: « When I shall be lifted up from the earth, I will draw all things to myself ».

6. The Lord also draws upward by the *hidden power of spiritual desires.* God indeed sends in a spiritual desire and draws the human heart to himself by the hidden
<!-- page 568 -->
desire of the heart; whence Job, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p568-1]: « If he turn his heart to him, he shall draw his spirit and breath unto himself ». Note the Gloss on Canticles, the first chapter: « Draw me after thee, we will run to the odour of thy ointments ». — Thus therefore the Father draws upward.

7. But not every one is drawn; because there is one who draws *downward,* namely the devil, of whom Job, the twenty-first chapter[^p568-2]: « He was acceptable to the gravel of Cocytus, and he shall draw every man after him, and there are innumerable before him ». And he draws in three ways: through *pride,* through *avarice,* through *lust.* — Through *pride* he draws *the ambitious.* These are drawn by the *tail;* Apocalypse, the twelfth chapter: « The tail of the dragon drew the third part of the stars of heaven, and cast them to the earth ». He says well: *the third part,* because one part attends to *good action,* another part to *contemplation,* the third to *ambition;* and this is drawn by the tail of the dragon. — He draws also through *avarice,* and thus he draws *the covetous.* These are drawn in the *wave of the river;* of which Apocalypse, the twelfth chapter[^p568-3]: « The serpent cast out of his mouth after the woman water as it were a river, that he might cause her to be drawn away by the river ». This river, from *flowing,* is riches, which flow over and flow back and draw the hearts of the avaricious. — He draws also through *lust,* and thus he draws *the wanton.* These are drawn by the *blandishments of a woman* that is a harlot, that is, of *carnal pleasure;* of which Proverbs, the seventh chapter: « She entangled him with many words, and drew him away with the blandishments of her lips; immediately he followeth her, as an ox led to be a victim, and as a lamb wantoning and not knowing that he is drawn like a fool to bonds » etc.

### Collation XXIX.

(Verse 67.). *Many of his disciples went back.*

1. Concerning the withdrawal of the disciples from Christ, note the *cause* of withdrawing — the *manner* of withdrawing — and the *fruit* of withdrawing. — Concerning the first it is to be noted that some are *drawn back,* lest they come to Christ; some are *drawn out,* after they have come, lest they remain with him.

2. Now a threefold cause *draws back* those coming to Christ, namely the love of *carnal pleasure,* the love of *earthly property*[^p568-4] and the love of *one's own will.* — Some go away from Christ by the love of *pleasure;* of whom Jeremias, the seventh chapter[^p568-5]: « They went away in their pleasures and the perverseness of their heart, and they went backward and not forward ». — Some go away by the love of *earthly property,* as that young man to whom it was said, Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « Go, sell what thou hast, and give to the poor »; but he « went away sad; for he had great possessions ». — Some go away by the love of *one's own will,* namely those who will not « bring themselves into captivity unto the obedience of Christ[^p568-6] » as the disciples, who, unwilling to leave themselves for the sake of Christ's teaching, left the teaching and clung to themselves; of whom it is said here that *many of his disciples went back.* — These therefore are the three things which draw back those approaching Christ.

3. There are also three things which draw out from Christ those walking with him, lest they persevere with him. The first is the *memory of temporal goods;* the second, *loathing of spiritual goods;* the third, *despair of eternal things.* — On account of the *memory of temporal goods* they withdrew who said, Exodus, the sixteenth chapter[^p568-7]: « Would to God we had died by the hand of the Lord in the land of Egypt, when we sat over the flesh pots, and ate bread to the full »! Numbers, the eleventh chapter: « We remember the fish that we ate in Egypt free cost; the cucumbers come into our mind, and the melons ». — On account of *loathing of spiritual goods* the children of Israel withdrew, Numbers, the twenty-first chapter, when they said: « Our soul now loatheth this very light food », expounding spiritually; and Numbers, the eleventh chapter: « Our soul is dry, our eyes behold nothing else but manna ». — On account of *despair of eternal things* they withdraw who say with the children of Israel, Numbers, the fourteenth chapter[^p568-8]: « Would God that we may perish, and that the Lord may not bring us into this land, lest we fall by the sword! Is it not better to return into Egypt »? And on account of this they said: « Let us appoint a captain, and let us return into Egypt ». These things they said fearing the difficult entry into the land, according to the opinion of those who said, above, in the thirteenth chapter: « The land devoureth its inhabitants ». Nevertheless Caleb and Josue are rather to be believed, saying, Numbers, the fourteenth chapter: « The land which we have gone round is very good; if the Lord be favourable, he will bring us into it ».

4. *Many of the disciples went back.* Having seen the *cause* of going away, note the *manner* of going away, which is threefold. For some go away back by *looking* through *thought;* some, back by *aspiring* through *affection;* some, back by *walking* through *conduct.* — Lot's wife *looked* back, and by looking back, Genesis, the
<!-- page 569 -->
nineteenth chapter[^p569-1], « was turned into a statue of salt »; note the history and expound it; and Luke, the ninth chapter: « No man putting his hand to the plough, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God ». Not so walk the holy living creatures; Ezechiel, the first chapter: « The living creatures turned not when they went, but every one went straight forward before his face ». — The children of Israel *aspired* back, Numbers, the fourteenth chapter: « Is it not better to return into Egypt »? And again: « Let us appoint a captain » etc. Not so spoke the Apostle, to the Philippians, the third chapter, who « counted all things but as dung, that he might gain Christ ». — The disciples *walked* back, of whom it is said here: *Many of the disciples went back.* Not so Peter[^p569-2]: « Lord, to whom shall we go? Thou hast the words of eternal life ». — Let us therefore leave the things that are behind, and direct our *looking, affection* and *walking* to the things that are before, with the Apostle saying, to the Philippians, the third chapter: « Forgetting the things that are behind », not looking back, as Lot's wife; « and stretching forth myself to those that are before », not looking back to things past, as the children of Israel; « I pursue towards the mark, for the prize », not falling back with the carnal disciples, but walking with the Apostles, « if I may by any means apprehend, wherein I am also apprehended ».

5. *The disciples,* he says, *went back.* After the *manner* of withdrawing, note the *fruit* of withdrawing, which is threefold. — Those withdrawing from Christ lose the *reward of glory;* Luke, the ninth chapter[^p569-3]: « No man putting his hand to the plough, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God ». If he who *looks* back is not *fit for the kingdom,* much less fit is he who *aspires* back, or *walks* back. — They incur the *remorse of conscience;* Genesis, the nineteenth chapter, of Lot's wife, who « looking back was turned into a statue of salt »; in the salt note the bitterness of conscience. — They merit the *penalty of hell;* Jeremias, the fifteenth chapter: « Thou hast forsaken me, thou art gone backward; I will stretch out my hand against thee, and I will destroy thee ». An example concerning Semei, the third of Kings, the second chapter.

### Annotation 24.

(Verse 15.). *He fled into the mountain alone.*

Note that solitude avails for three things, namely for *avoiding the disturbance of the world;* the Psalm[^p569-4]: « I have gone far off flying away, and I abode in the wilderness ». — For *entering into the rest of contemplation;* Lamentations, the third chapter: « He shall sit solitary and hold his peace, because he hath taken it up upon himself » etc. — For *obtaining divine consolation;* Osee, the second chapter: « I will allure her, and will lead her into the wilderness, and I will speak to her heart ».

### Annotation 25.

(Verse 19.). *They see Jesus walking upon the sea.*

Note that in this miracle the Lord worked three wonders: he *trod* the sea and *appeased* the sea and *made* the sea *to be flown across.* — *He trod;* whence it is said here: *They see him walking upon the sea.* — *He appeased;* whence it is said in Mark, the sixth chapter[^p569-5], that « he went up to them into the ship, and the wind ceased ». — *He made* the sea *to be flown across;* whence it is said here that « presently the ship was at the land to which they were going ». — And these things signify what the Lord does for the faithful in tribulation. He *treads* the sea when he *puts* tribulation *under foot;* he *appeases* the sea when he *softens* tribulation; he *crosses* the sea when tribulation *ceases.* For the Lord brings it about that tribulation be *brief* in duration, and then the sea is *crossed;* that it be *light* in affliction, and then the sea is *appeased;* that it be *nothing* in reckoning, and thus the sea is *trodden.*

### Annotation 26.

(Verse 26.). *You seek me, not because you have seen signs, but because you did eat of the loaves.*

Note that Christ is sought sometimes for the sake of *temporal advantage,* as here: *You seek me* etc. — Sometimes for the sake of *persecution;* Matthew, the second chapter[^p569-6]: « Arise, and take the child and his Mother, and fly into Egypt, and be there until I shall tell thee. For it will come to pass that Herod will seek the child to destroy him ». — Sometimes for the sake of *devotion;* Luke, the second chapter: « Son, why hast thou done so to us? I and thy father have sought thee sorrowing ».

### Annotation 27.

(Verse 33.). *The bread of God is that which cometh down from heaven, and giveth life to the world.*

Note that this bread excels every other bread; for it *delights the mind, strengthens the power, continues the life.* — *It delights the mind;* Wisdom, the sixteenth chapter[^p569-7]: « Thou didst feed thy people with the food of angels, and gavest them bread from heaven prepared without labour, having in it all that is delicious and the sweetness of every taste ». — *It strengthens the power;* the Psalm: « Bread strengtheneth man's heart ». — *It continues the life;* whence it is said here that it *giveth life to the world;* and John, the sixth chapter: « If any man eat of this bread, he shall live for ever ». — The first pertains to *contemplation;* the second, to *action;* but the third, to *both.*
