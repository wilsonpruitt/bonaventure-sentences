### Capitulum XXI. *Postea manifestavit se iterum.*

### Annotatio 73.

(Vers. 1.). *Postea manifestavit se iterum Iesus ad mare Tiberiadis.*

1. Nota, quod Christus post resurrectionem suam legitur apparuisse in quinque locis: in *via,* in *mari,* in *domo,* in *horto* et in *monte.* — In *via* apparuit, Lucae ultimo[^p622-9], discipulis euntibus in Emmaus. — In *mari* apparuit discipulis laborantibus; unde hic: *Postea manifestavit se* etc. — In *domo* apparuit discipulis congregatis in unum, Lucae ultimo[^p622-10], Ioannis vigesimo. — In *horto* apparuit Mariae Magdalenae, Ioannis vigesimo. — In *monte* apparuit undecim, qui abierunt in Galilaeam, Matthaei ultimo.

2. Nota autem, quod spiritualiter *apparet* Dominus, id est, beneficia sua ostendit quinque generi-
<!-- page 623 -->
bus hominum: *obedientibus, patientibus, studentibus, contemplantibus* et *praesidentibus;* ut dicamus, quod *obedientibus* apparet in via obedientiae, *patientibus* in mari poenitentiae, *studentibus* in domo sapientiae, *contemplantibus* in horto, *praesidentibus* in monte.

### Collatio LXXV.

(Vers. 14.). *Hoc iam tertio manifestatus est Iesus discipulis suis, cum surrexisset a mortuis.*

1. Nota de apparitionibus Domini post resurrectionem; et nota, quod decies legitur Dominus apparuisse: primo, Mariae Magdalenae, Ioannis vigesimo[^p623-1]. — Secundo, mulieribus revertentibus de monumento, quae « accesserunt et tenuerunt pedes eius », Matthaei ultimo. — Tertio, Petro, Lucae ultimo: « Surrexit Dominus vere et apparuit Simoni ». — Quarto, discipulis euntibus in Emmaus, Lucae ultimo. — Quinto, discipulis congregatis in domo; Ioannis vigesimo[^p623-2]; et haec facta sunt ipso die Paschae. — Sexto, discipulis et Thomae, Ioannis vigesimo. — Septimo, discipulis piscantibus ad mare Tiberiadis, Ioannis vigesimo primo. — Octavo, discipulis undecim in monte Thabor, Matthaei ultimo. — Nono, discipulis congregatis in coenaculo in die ascensionis, Marci ultimo[^p623-3]. — Decimo, eadem die in monte Oliveti, quando, « videntibus illis, elevatus est », Lucae ultimo et Actuum primo.

2. Ita *spiritualiter* nota, quod Domini *apparitio* est beneficiorum suorum exhibitio; et Domini *absconsio,* beneficiorum suorum subtractio. Nota ergo, quod tribus generibus hominum apparet, quia tribus generibus hominum beneficia sua exhibet, scilicet *activis, contemplativis* et *praelatis.* Ista tria genera hominum significantur per ista tria nomina; *Daniel, Noe* et *Iob,* Ezechielis decimo quarto[^p623-4]; et per *Petrum, Iacobum* et *Ioannem,* Matthaei decimo septimo; et per *Mariam, Martham* et *Lazarum,* Ioannis undecimo.

3. Apparet igitur activis, et hoc in triplici differentia, videlicet *revertentibus* per poenitentiam, *proficientibus* per obedientiam, *ascendentibus* per vitae excellentiam. — Apparet igitur *revertentibus* per opera poenitentiae; et hoc figuratum est, Ioannis vigesimo[^p623-5], in apparitione, qua apparuit Thomae. Thomas enim, egressus a discipulis, non vidit Dominum; « Thomas, inquit, qui dicitur Dydimus, non erat cum eis, quando venit Iesus ». Reversus vero ad discipulos, vidit et tetigit eum; Ioannis vigesimo: « Infer digitum tuum huc » etc. — Apparet *proficientibus* per opera obedientiae; et hoc figuratum est, quando apparuit discipulis euntibus in Emmaus, Lucae ultimo[^p623-6], ubi legitur, quod « ipse Iesus appropinquans ibat cum illis » in Emmaus. *Emmaus* interpretatur desiderium consilii; *discipuli euntes in Emmaus* sunt fideles de mandatis ad consilia proficientes. — Apparet etiam *ascendentibus* per opera excellentiae, et hoc figuratum est, quando apparuit undecim discipulis in monte Thabor, Matthaei ultimo[^p623-7]: « Undecim, inquit, discipuli abierunt in Galilaeam in monte, ubi constituerat illis Iesus, et videntes eum adoraverunt ». Per *montem* intellige excellentiam vitae. — Apparet ergo Dominus *activis revertentibus* per opera poenitentiae, *proficientibus* per opera obedientiae, *ascendentibus* per opera excellentiae.

4. Apparet etiam Dominus *contemplativis,* et hoc in triplici differentia, scilicet *sedentibus* in meditatione, *plorantibus* in oratione, *fruentibus* in contemplatione. — Apparet ergo Dominus *sedentibus in meditatione,* vel etiam in *sancta collatione;* et hoc figuratum est, quando apparuit discipulis *congregatis in unum;* Lucae ultimo[^p623-8]: « Cum haec, inquit, loquerentur, Iesus stetit in medio eorum et dixit eis: Pax vobis »; et de hoc etiam Ioannis vigesimo. — Apparet etiam *plorantibus in oratione;* quod figuratum est, quando apparuit Mariae ploranti, Ioannis vigesimo: « Conversa, inquit, retrorsum, vidit Iesum stantem et nesciebat, quod Iesus esset; et dixit ei Iesus: Mulier, quid ploras » etc. — Apparet etiam *fruentibus in contemplatione;* quod figuratum est, quando apparuit undecim discipulis congregatis in coenaculo; Marci ultimo[^p623-9]: « Novissime, inquit, recumbentibus undecim discipulis, apparuit eis Iesus ». Per *coenaculum discipulorum,* quod erat Ierosolymis, intellige refectorium contemplationis. In isto coenaculo legitur Dominus comedisse cum illis; Actuum primo: « Convescens praecepit eis, ab Ierosolymis ne discenderent » etc. — Sic ergo apparet Dominus *contemplantibus,* sedentibus in meditatione vel in sancta collatione, plorantibus in oratione et fruentibus in contemplatione.

5. Apparet etiam *praelatis,* et hoc in quadruplici differentia. Praelatorum enim est *accedere* ad Deum et *reverti* ad populum. Praelati *accedunt* ad Deum duabus de causis: ad *consulendum* et ad *offerendum.* — Praelatis ergo *consulentibus* apparet
<!-- page 624 -->
Dominus; et hoc figuratum est, quando apparuit Petro; Lucae vigesimo quarto[^p624-1]: « Surrexit Dominus vere et apparuit Simoni »; et dicit Magister in Historiis: « quando Petrus cucurrit ad monumentum ». Petrus enim, audita Domini resurrectione, noluit narrationi mulierum omnino acquiescere, sed accessit ad monumentum, volens ipsam veritatem consulere. Et apparuit Dominus Petro visitanti monumentum, significans, quod apparet *consulentibus ipsum.* Praelatis *consulentibus* igitur apparet Dominus. — Apparet etiam praelatis *offerentibus;* quod figuratum est, quando apparuit mulieribus, Matthaei ultimo[^p624-2]. Legimus enim ibi, quomodo « emerant aromata, ut venientes ungerent Iesum »; ecce, *oblatio;* et quomodo exeuntibus de monumento « ocurrit Iesus dicens: Avete. Illae autem accesserunt et tenuerunt pedes eius »; et sequitur, qualiter Dominus iniunxit eis, ut nuntiarent discipulis gloriam resurrectionis; quod est totum mysterium, in quo significatur, quod Dominus apparet *offerentibus* et in secreto contemplationis praebet eis se ipsum, et ibidem eos instruit, quid revertentes dicant ad populum. — Sic ergo apparet Dominus dupliciter praelatis *accedentibus* ad ipsum.

6. Apparet etiam dupliciter praelatis *revertentibus* ad populum. Reverti enim debet praelatus ad populum, ut *praedicet verbo* et ut *deducat exemplo.* — Apparet ergo Dominus *praedicantibus;* quod figuratum est Ioannis ultimo[^p624-3], quando apparuit Dominus piscantibus Apostolis ad mare Tiberiadis. *Apostolorum piscatio* est praelatorum praedicatio. — Apparet etiam *deducentibus* gregem bono exemplo; et hoc figuratum est Lucae ultimo et Actuum primo, quando apparuit discipulis exeuntibus in montem Oliveti; quae fuit ultima apparitio Christi post resurrectionem. Tunc enim, « videntibus illis, in caelum elevatus est ».

### Collatio LXXVI.

(Vers. 17.). *Petre, amas me?*

1. In verbis istis *exigit* Dominus a Petro *spiritualem* dilectionem, cum dicit: *Petre, amas me?* Nec solum exigit spiritualem, sed loquens per discretionem, amorem *non spiritualem interdicit; Amas,* inquit, *me?* quasi dicat: *me* et non aliud *a me,* vel aliud *contra me.* — Nota igitur, quod est amor *Christi,* et hic est *laudabilis;* et est amor *mundi,* et hic *culpabilis.*

2. De amore *mundi* Sapientiae decimo quinto[^p624-4]: « Malorum amatores digni sunt morte ». Et notandum est, quod iste amor est triplex. Est enim amor *carnalitatis;* secundae ad Timotheum tertio[^p624-5]: « Erunt homines se ipsos amantes »; et post paululum: « Voluptatum amatores magis quam Dei ». — Est etiam amor *cupiditatis,* de quo Ecclesiastici decimo: « Nihil iniquius quam amare pecuniam. Hic enim animam suam habet venalem ». — Est etiam amor *ambitionis,* de quo Matthaei vigesimo tertio: « Amant primos accubitus in coenis et primas cathedras in synagogis ».

3. Iste amor *reiiciendus* est propter multas rationes: primo, quia *criminosus;* Sapientiae decimo quinto[^p624-6]: « Malorum amatores » etc. Nec mirum; inducit enim anima peccatrix fornicationem, sponso vidente; unde Ezechielis vigesimo tertio: « Fornicata est Oolla super me et insanivit in amatores suos ». — Secundo, quia *laboriosus;* Osee secundo: « Dixit: Vadam post amatores meos »; et additur: Ideo « sequetur amatores suos et non apprehendet eos ». — Tertio, quia *sumtuosus;* Ezechielis decimo sexto[^p624-7]. « Omnibus meretricibus dantur mercedes, tu autem dedisti mercedem amatoribus tuis et donabas eis, ut intrarent ad te ad fornicandum »; Proverbiorum vigesimo primo: « Qui amat vinum et pinguia non ditabitur ». — Quarto, quia *infructuosus;* Ecclesiastici quinto: « Qui amat divitias fructum non capiet ex eis », quod est verum de omni amore inordinato; ad Romanos sexto: « Quem fructum habuistis, in quibus nunc erubescitis »? Osee secundo: « Quaeret amatores suos et non inveniet ». — Quinto, quia *instabilis;* cito enim deficit; Ieremiae trigesimo[^p624-8]: « Omnes amatores tui obliti sunt tui, non te quaerent »; et Ieremiae quarto: « Frustra componeris, contempserunt te amatores tui, animam tuam quaerent ». — Sexto, quia *seditiosus.* Convertitur enim ad inimicitias; quod figuratum est secundi Regum decimo tertio, ubi loquitur, quod postquam Amon corruperat Thamar, sororem suam, « odivit eam odio magno nimis, ita quod maius esset odium, quo oderat eam, amore, quo eam ante dilexerat ». — Propter haec omnia relinquendus est amor mundi.

<!-- page 625 -->
4. *Amplectendus* est *amor Christi,* ad exemplum eius quae dixit[^p625-1]: « Regnum mundi et omnem amorem saeculi contempsi propter amorem Domini mei Iesu Christi », scilicet amorem, quem exigit Dominus dicens: *Petre, amas me?* — Nota, quod amplectendus est amor bonus, quia *facilis* est ad acquirendum, *delectabilis* ad habendum, *securus* ad possidendum.

5. *Facilis* est *ad acquirendum.* Ipse enim omnes diligit, qui eum volunt diligere; Proverbiorum octavo[^p625-2]: « Ego diligentes me diligo »; et Ioannis decimo sexto, amantibus se, ne parum videatur illis promittere amorem suum, promittit non solum *suum* amorem, sed etiam *paternum,* dicens: « Ipse enim Pater amat vos, quia vos me amastis »; et Ieremiae tertio: « Tu autem fornicata es cum amatoribus tuis, et tamen revertere ad me, et ego suscipiam te ». Ecce, quia facilis est ad acquirendum. — Est etiam *delectabilis ad habendum;* Sapientiae octavo[^p625-3]: « In amicitia illius delectatio bona ». Vere *bona,* quia casta, non polluta; unde beata Agnes: « Ipsum amo, quem cum amavero, casta sum; cum tetigero, munda sum; cum accepero, virgo sum ». Est ergo *delectatio* munda nec non et delectabilis *amicitia;* Proverbiorum quinto: « Cerva gratissima et gratissimus filius; ubera eius inebrient te omni tempore, et in amore eius delectare iugiter ». Ecce, quia delectabilis ad habendum. — Est nihilominus *securus ad possidendum;* Ieremiae trigesimo primo[^p625-4]: « In caritate perpetua dilexi te »; primae ad Corinthios decimo tertio: « Caritas nunquam excidit ». Et nota, quod ideo *securus* est ad possidendum, quia amator spiritualis intra se habet amatum; unde Augustinus[^p625-5]: « *Intus* in me est quod amo, ubi *lucet* quod non capit locus, ubi *sonat* quod non rapit tempus, ubi *fragrat* quod non spargit flatus, ubi *sapit* quod nulla minuit edacitas, ubi *haeret* quod nulla avellit anxietas ». Ecce, amoris inseparabilitas.

6. Notandum autem, quod amor iste debet esse in *affectione* praecipuus, in *cohabitatione* assiduus, in *opere* officiosus. — Requirit enim *intensionem affectionum;* ideo dicitur Matthaei decimo[^p625-6]: « Qui amat patrem aut matrem plus quam me non est me dignus »; et Iudicum decimo sexto dicitur tepide amantibus: « Quomodo tu dicis, quia amas me, cum animus tuus non sit mecum »? — Requirit etiam *assiduitatem cohabitationis,* secundum illud Deuteronomii trigesimo tertio: « Beniamin, amantissimus Domini, habitabit confidenter in eo; tota die quasi in thalamo commorabitur ». — Requirit etiam *officiositatem operis;* unde Gregorius[^p625-7]: « Amor Dei nunquam est otiosus; operatur enim magna, si est; quodsi operari renuit, amor non est »; unde Deuteronomii undecimo: « Ama Dominum Deum tuum et observa praecepta eius » etc.; et Ioannis ultimo Petro confitenti amorem statim iniungitur officium: « Pasce oves meas »; et dicit Beda: « Haec est sola et vera probatio integri in Deum amoris, si erga fratres studueris curam solliciti exercere laboris ».

### Annotatio 74.

(Vers. 3.). *Ascenderunt in navem.*

Nota, quod *navis* est religio. Haec est enim navis utilis ad *transfretandum;* Matthaei nono[^p625-8]: « Ascendens Iesus in naviculam transfretavit et venit in civitatem suam ». — Ad *negotiandum;* Proverbiorum ultimo: « Facta est quasi navis institoris de longe portans panem suum ». — Ad *piscandum;* unde hic de discipulis volentibus piscari dicitur: *Exierunt et ascenderunt in navem.* — Religiosi igitur *transfretant* laborando, *negotiantur* orando, *piscantur* praedicando, ad quae omnia valet religio.

### Annotatio 75.

(Vers. 6.). *Mittite in dexteram navigii rete.*

Nota, quod quatuor hic dicuntur de piscatoribus: primo, quod *rete expandunt;* secundo, quod *pisces comprehendunt;* tertio, quod *pisces trahunt;* quarto, quod *extrahunt;* quae omnia patent in littera. — Piscatores ergo spirituales *rete expandunt* praedicando; *pisces comprehendunt* peccatores convertendo; *pisces trahunt* per bona exempla promovendo; *extrahunt* autem ad caelestia regna perducendo.

### Annotatio 76.

(Vers. 7.). *Succinxit se et misit se in mare.*

Nota, quod mare aliquando transitur *natando,* ut hic dicitur de Petro; aliquando *navigando,* ut hic[^p625-9] dicitur de aliis: *Alii autem navigio venerunt;* aliquando *calcando,* ut Matthaei decimo quarto di-
<!-- page 626 -->
citur de Petro. — Nota, quod mare *natando* transeunt qui in mundi sollicitudinibus se immergunt; et isti submergendi periculo se exponunt. — Mare vero *transnavigant* qui *caute* mundanis occupationibus se exercent. — Mare vero *calcant* qui *omnino* a mundi sollicitudinibus se abstrahunt et mundana ad solam sustentationem habent. Isti ultimi a mari *portantur,* sed nec laborant nec immerguntur; priores vero *portantur* et *laborant,* sed non immerguntur. Primi vero et *portantur* et *laborant* et *immerguntur.*

### Annotatio 77.

(Vers. 16.). *Pasce agnos meos.*

Nota, quod quidam pascunt *agnos,* quidam *lupos,* quidam *se ipsos.* — Agnos pascunt *boni* praelati; de quibus Isaiae quadragesimo[^p626-1]: « Sicut pastor gregem suum pascet » etc. — Lupos pascunt *mercenarii;* Ioannis decimo: « Mercenarius videt lupum venientem et dimittit oves et fugit » etc. — Se ipsos pascunt *fures,* quibus dicitur Ezechielis trigesimo quarto: « Vae pastoribus Israel, qui pascebant semetipsos » etc. — Ecce ergo, in grege Domini quidam sunt *fures,* qui pascunt semetipsos; quidam *mercenarii,* qui pascunt *lupos;* quidam *pastores veri,* qui pascunt agnos.

### Annotatio 78.

(Vers. 22.). *Sic eum volo manere, donec veniam.*

Nota, quod potestativum est *velle* Domini Iesu. Nam *velle* eius *mundat* leprosum; Matthaei octavo[^p626-2]: « Tetigit eum dicens: Volo, mundare ». — *Servat* discipulum; Ioannis ultimo: *Sic eum volo manere.* — *Glorificat* ministrum; Ioannis decimo septimo: « Volo, Pater, ut ubi ego sum, illic sit et minister meus ». — Est ergo voluntas Christi, quae *mundat culpam* leprae, *servat vitam* gratiae, *tribuit vitam* gloriae.

### Collatio LXXVII.

(Cap. 4, 13.). *Omnis, qui bibit ex hac aqua, sitiet iterum,* Ioannis quarto[^p626-3].

1. In hac parte agitur de multiplici genere aquae. Notandum, quod multae sunt aquarum differentiae. Est enim aqua *culpae* — et est aqua *poenae* — et aqua *fortunae* — et aqua *gratiae* — et etiam aqua *gloriae.* Huiusmodi aquarum aliae sunt *fugiendae,* aliae *transeundae,* aliae sunt *appetendae.* — Aquae ergo, quas *fugimus,* sunt aquae *culpae* et aquae *poenae;* inter quas primo videndum est de aquis *culpae.*

2. Est autem multiplex aqua *culpae.* Est enim aqua *superbiae;* Apocalypsis decimo septimo[^p626-4]: « Veni, et ostendam tibi damnationem meretricis magnae, quae sedet super aquas multas » etc.; loquitur de mundana vanitate. — Aqua *avaritiae,* de qua recte potest dici hic: *Qui bibit ex hac aqua sitiet iterum.*

> Quo plus potatae plus sitiuntur aquae.

Est aqua *luxuriae;* Genesis quadragesimo nono[^p626-5]: « Ruben effusus es sicut aqua; non crescas, quia ascendisti cubile patris tui et maculasti stratum eius ».

3. Aquae istae *abominabiles* sunt, quia *turbidae;* obscurant enim rationem; unde dicitur Ieremiae secundo[^p626-6]: « Quid ibi vis in via Aegypti, ut bibas aquam turbidam ». — Item, quia *immundae;* inquinant enim affectionem; Levitici undecimo: « Omnis cibus, quem comederis, si fusa fuerit super eum aqua, immundus erit ». — Quia *steriles;* exinaniunt enim virtutem; Numerorum quinto: « Potum det mulieri aquas amarissimas »; et sequitur: « Pertransibunt eam aquae maledictionis, et inflato ventre, computrescet femur ».

4. Aquas istas sequitur aqua *poenae.* Et sunt aquae poenae *horribiles,* quia *obscurae* ad videndum, *amarae* ad potandum, *intemperatae* ad sentiendum. — Sunt igitur *obscurae* ad videndum; unde Exodi decimo quinto[^p626-7]: « Abyssi operuerunt eos; descenderunt in profundum quasi lapis »; et iterum: « Congregatae sunt abyssi in medio maris ». — *Amarae* quantum ad poenam vermis; Exodi decimo quinto: « Reduxit super eos Dominus aquas maris ». — *Intemperatae* quantum ad poenam sensus; Iob vigesimo quarto: « Ab aquis nivium transeat ad calorem nimium ». — Istae ergo sunt aquae, quas fugimus. Fugimus enim aquas *culpae,* ne incidamus in aquas *poenae.*

5. Sequitur de aquis, quas *transimus,* scilicet de aquis *fortunae;* et quia fortuna duplex est: *prospera* et *adversa;* ideo notandum, quod sunt aquae *prosperitatis* et *adversitatis.* Aqua *prosperitatis* multiplex: aqua *deliciarum;* Proverbiorum nono[^p626-8]:
<!-- page 627 -->
« Aquae furtivae dulciores sunt, et panis absconditus suavior ». — Aqua *divitiarum;* Apocalypsis duodecimo: « Misit serpens ex ore suo post mulierem aquam magnam tanquam flumen, ut faceret eam trahi a flumine ». — Aqua *honorum;* Psalmus: « Elevaverunt flumina, Domine, elevaverunt fluctus suos a vocibus aquarum multarum. Mirabiles elationes maris » etc.

6. Istarum aquarum transitus *periculosus* est. Sunt enim aquae istae *velociter decurrentes;* Psalmus[^p627-1]: « Ad nihilum devenient tanquam aqua decurrens ». — *Deorsum trahentes;* Apocalypsis duodecimo: « Misit serpens post mulierem aquam magnam tanquam flumen, ut eam faceret trahi a flumine ». Hae enim aquae omnia secum trahunt, secundum illud Ovidii:

> Unda vehit tigres nec vires fulminis apro,
> Crura nec ablato prosunt velocia cervo.

Sunt etiam *submergentes;* Exodi decimo quinto[^p627-2]: « Submersi sunt quasi plumbum in aquis vehementibus ».

7. Sequitur de aquis *adversitatis,* quarum etsi timeatur transitus, non tamen est ita periculosus. Timemus enim eas, quia *amarae* sunt, et ideo difficiles ad potandum; Exodi decimo quinto[^p627-3]: « Non poterant bibere aquas Marath, quia amarae erant ». — Item, quia *profundae* sunt, et ideo periculosae ad natandum; ideo rogat David: « Non me demergat tempestas aquae, neque absorbeat me profundum ». — Item, quia *validae* sunt, et ideo difficiles ad sustinendum; et ideo David: « Salvum me fac, Deus, quoniam intraverunt aquae usque ad animam meam ». — Ideo timemus aquas istas, ne amaritudine *affligamur,* valitudine *subvertamur,* profunditate *submergamur.*

8. Sed in his omnibus *confortat* nos divinum auxilium, quod praestat nobis istorum remedium. Dominus enim *amaritudinem dulcorat;* Exodi decimo quinto[^p627-4]: « Ostendit Moysi lignum; quod cum misisset in aquas, in dulcorem versae sunt ». — *Profunditatem exsiccat;* Iob duodecimo: « Si continuerit aquas, omnia siccabuntur »; Isaiae undecimo: « Percutiam fluvium in septem rivis, ita ut transeant per eum calceati »; Psalmus: « Abierunt in sicco flumina ». — *Valitudinem retardat;* Iosue tertio[^p627-5]: « Ingressis filiis Israel Iordanem, steterunt aquae, descendentes in loco uno » etc.; et supra dictum fuerat Iosue: « Iordanis aquae inferiores decurrent atque deficient, quae autem desuper veniunt in una mole consistent »; Psalmus; « In diluvio aquarum multarum ad eum non approximabunt ».

9. Hae ergo sunt aquae, quas transimus; sunt aliae aquae, quas *appetimus,* quae sunt aquae *gratiae* et aquae *gloriae.* Inter quas primo videndum est de aquis *gratiae,* quae multiplices sunt, scilicet aquae *remissionis* sive iustificationis; Ezechielis quadragesimo septimo[^p627-6] dicitur de aquis, quae « descendebant in latus templi dexterum ». — Aquae *instructionis;* Ecclesiastici decimo quinto: « Aqua sapientiae salutaris potavit illum ». — Aquae *consolationis spiritualis,* quas deplorabat David dicens: « Anima mea sicut terra sine aqua tibi »; Proverbiorum vigesimo quinto: « Aqua frigida animae sitienti nuntius bonus, veniens de terra longinqua ».

10. Istae aquae *quaerendae* sunt, quia *emundant;* Ezechielis trigesimo sexto[^p627-7]: « Effundam super vos aquam mundam, et mundabimini ab omnibus inquinamentis vestris ». — Quia *reficiunt;* Numerorum vigesimo: « Aperi, Domine, thesaurum tuum, fontem aquae vivae, ut satiati, cesset murmuratio eorum ». — Quia *enutriunt* et *fecundant;* unde in Psalmo[^p627-8]: « Tanquam lignum, quod plantatum est secus decursus aquarum, quod fructum suum dabit in tempore suo »; Iob decimo quarto: « Ad odorem aquae germinabit ».

11. Aquas istas sequitur *aqua gloriae.* Aqua ista *desiderabilis* est, maxime propter tria. Haec est enim aqua *delectans, satians* et *vivificans.* Habet ergo aqua ista *delectationem;* delectat enim *aspectum;* Apocalypsis vigesimo secundo[^p627-9]: « Ostendit mihi fluvium aquae vivae, splendidum tanquam crystallum ». Delectat etiam *gustum;* etc. Psalmus: « De torrente voluptatis tuae potabis eos ». — Item, habet *satietatem;* Ioannis quarto[^p627-10]: « Qui biberit ex hac aqua, quam ego dabo ei, non sitiet in aeternum »; et Apocalypsis septimo: « Non esurient neque sitient amplius » etc. — Item, habet *vivacitatem,* ut nunquam possit deficere; Apocalypsis vigesimo primo: « Ego sitienti dabo de fonte aquae vitae gratis »; et iterum: « Qui vicerit, possidebit haec, et ero illi Deus, et ipse erit mihi filius ».

### Collatio LXXVIII.

(Cap. 3, 5.). *Nisi quis renatus fuerit ex aqua et Spiritu sancto, non poterit intrare in regnum caelorum,* Ioannis tertio.

<!-- page 628 -->
1. De introitu regni caelestis nota, quod porta regni est *humilis,* et ideo non intrat per eam *elatus; angusta* est, et ideo non intrat per eam *inflatus; sancta* est, et ideo non intrat per eam *pollutus.* — Ideo non intrant *elati honoribus;* Matthaei decimo octavo[^p628-1]: « Nisi conversi fueritis et efficiamini sicut parvuli, non intrabitis in regnum caelorum ». — Non *dilatati possessionibus;* Matthaei decimo nono: « Amen dico vobis, quia dives difficile intrabit in regnum caelorum; et iterum dico vobis: Facilius est, camelum per foramen acus transire, quam divitem intrare in regnum caelorum ». — Non *polluti voluptatibus;* Apocalypsis vigesimo primo[^p628-2]: « Non intrabit in eam aliquid coinquinatum et abominationem faciens ».

2. Nota, quod Dominus intravit *inclinatus, spoliatus, mortificatus:* « Nonne oportuit Christum pati et ita intrare in gloriam suam[^p628-3] »? Sed isti « non cognoverunt vias meas, quibus iuravi in ira mea: Si introibunt in requiem meam ».

3. Nota autem, quod introitum in regnum caelorum concedit rex *servis* suis, *amicis* suis, et *sociis* suis. *Servi* sunt activi, qui Deo impendunt *obsequium actionis; amici,* contemplativi, qui Deo reddunt *obsequium devotionis* vel dilectionis; *socii* et commilitones, qui cum illo incedunt per *ardua passionis.* — Intrant ergo *servi;* Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p628-4]: « Euge, serve bone et fidelis » etc.; sequitur: « Intra in gaudium Domini tui ». — *Amici;* Matthaei vigesimo quinto, de virginibus, « quae paratae erant, intraverunt cum eo ad nuptias ». — *Socii* regis sunt, de quibus Apocalypsis vigesimo secundo[^p628-5]: « Beati, qui lavant stolas suas in sanguine Agni, ut sit potestas eorum in ligno vitae, et per portas intrent in civitatem »; Ioannis tertio: « Nisi quis renatus fuerit » etc.; et Matthaei decimo nono: « Si vis ad vitam ingredi, serva mandata ».

### Collatio LXXIX.

(Cap. 20, 25.). *Vidimus Dominum,* Ioannis vigesimo.

1. Nota triplicem visionem Salvatoris, qui recte dicitur *salutare Dei:* unam, quam recolimus de *praeterito,* qua visus fuit *corporaliter* in carne; aliam, quam quaerimus in *praesenti,* qua videtur *spiritualiter* in mente; tertiam, quam exspectamus in *futuro,* qua videbitur *feliciter* in aeterna beatitudine.

2. De prima Baruch tertio[^p628-6]: « Post haec in terris visus est et cum hominibus conversatus est ». — De secunda in Psalmo: « Vacate et videte, quoniam ego sum Deus; exaltabor in gentibus et exaltabor in terra ». *Vacate* ab occupationibus carnis, *et videte* oculis mentis; Isaiae sexagesimo secundo: « Videbunt gentes iustum tuum », dicitur ad Sion; « et cuncti reges inclytum tuum ». — De tertia, Ioannis decimo septimo[^p628-7]: « Pater, quos dedisti mihi, volo, ut, ubi sum ego, et illi sint mecum, ut videant claritatem meam, quam dedisti mihi, quia dilexisti me ante constitutionem mundi ». — Prima visio *praeteriit,* secunda *stat,* tertia *futura est.* Propter quod accipiamus secundam, quae sola in praesenti haberi potest, quae quidem visio spiritualis Salvatoris omnibus est salutaris.

3. *Vidimus Dominum.* Visio *spiritualis* Salvatoris ideo est omnibus salvandis salutaris, quia *terret* peccatores[^p628-8], *sanat* poenitentes, *confortat* pugnantes, *deducit* proficientes, *laetificat* contemplantes, *consolatur* exspectantes. Quod sumitur secundum sex status, videlicet *culpae, poenitentiae, pugnae, iustitiae, sapientiae* et *perseverantiae. Terrentur* peccantes videndo *iudicantem; sanantur* poenitentes videndo *patientem; confortantur* pugnantes videndo *triumphantem; deducuntur* proficientes videndo *conversantem; laetificantur* contemplantes videndo *regnantem; consolati sunt* exspectantes videndo ipsum *revertentem* et suos in hac miseria visitantem. Nec videntur[^p628-9] in eo plura consideranda nisi vel *conversatio,* vel *passio,* vel *tribulatio,* vel *triumphus,* vel *regnum,* vel *iudicium,* vel *reversio* ad suos in hac vita.

4. Spiritualis ergo visio Salvatoris *terret* peccantes, ut *resipiscant.* Per timorem enim Domini declinat omnis a malo; et hoc ideo, quia ostendit eis iudicem suum omnia *videntem,* omnia *disquirentem,* omnia *punientem;* quae tria maxime deberent terrere peccatorem, scilicet si consideraret supra se iudicem suum, qui omnia videt, omnia disquirit, omnia puniet. — Utinam ergo viderent peccatores spiritualiter, ne visuri sint aliquando corporaliter iudicem omnia *scientem!* de quo Iob trigesimo septimo[^p628-10]: « Magnus est fortitudine et iustitia et iudicio »; *fortitudine* potestatis, *iustitia* aequitatis et *iudicio* veritatis; et sequitur: « Ideo timebunt viri, et non audebunt eum contemplari omnes, qui sibi videntur esse sapientes » scilicet qui sapientes sunt, « ut a Domino abscondant consilium », ut dicit Isaias vigesimo nono.

5. Item, utinam viderent iudicem *omnia disquirentem!* cuius visionem terribilem describens, Malachias propheta dicit Malachiae tertio[^p628-11]: « Quis poterit cogitare diem adventus eius, aut quis stabit ad
<!-- page 629 -->
videndum eum »? Est namque terribilis ad *cogitandum,* sed terribilior ad *videndum;* et subiungit *quare:* « Erit enim quasi ignis conflans et quasi herba fullonum »; *ignis conflans* nihil metalli relinquit, quod non liquefaciat; *herba fullonum,* id est cardo, nec pilum nec filum relinquit, quod non attingat.

6. Item, utinam viderent iudicem omnia *punientem!* ad cuius visionem invitat eos ipse idem Ieremiae septimo[^p629-1]: « Ite, inquit, ad locum meum in Silo, ubi habitavit nomen meum a principio; et videte, quid fecerim ibi propter malitiam populi mei Israel ». *Silo,* avulsa vel translata, *ubi habitavit nomen Domini a principio,* est caelestis patria, ubi habitavit a principio qui « in excelsis habitat »; quae *avulsa* vel translata est quantum ad partem peccantium angelorum. *Ite* ergo *et videte, quid fecerim ibi* etc., quia, « si Deus angelis peccantibus non pepercit, sed rudentibus inferni detractos » etc., secundae Petri secundo[^p629-2]; et Ieremiae septimo: « Nunc autem, quia fecistis omnia haec, dicit Dominus » etc.; et sequitur: « Ecce, furor meus et indignatio mea conflatur super locum istum, super homines et super iumenta, super ligna regionis et super fruges terrae et succendetur et non exstinguetur ».

7. Sic igitur visio iudicis omnia *videntis* et scientis, omnia *disquirentis,* omnia *punientis* peccatores terret; quam videns Iob ait quadragesimo secundo[^p629-3]: « Auditu auris audivi te, nunc autem oculus meus videt te; idcirco ipse me reprehendo et ago poenitentiam in favilla et cinere »; *audivi* per inspirationem, *vidi* per considerationem; ideo *me reprehendo* interius in mente et *poenitentiam ago* exterius in opere. Et Isaiae sexto[^p629-4], visa gloria Domini sedentis « super solium excelsum et elevatum », exclamavit dicens et arguens se: « Vae mihi, quia tacui! quia vir pollutus labiis ego sum et in medio populi polluta labia habentis ego habito, et Dominum Deum exercituum vidi oculis meis »; *vae mihi, quia tacui,* quod est *omissionis; quia pollutus* etc., quod est *commissionis.*

8. Spiritualis iterum visio Salvatoris *sanat poenitentes,* ostendens eis Dominum *patientem.* Solet quidem ex triplici concupiscentia insurgere *triplex infirmitas:* ex concupiscentia deliciarum infirmitas *carnalitatis,* ex concupiscentia divitiarum, infirmitas *cupiditatis,* ex concupiscentia honorum infirmitas *ambitionis.*

9. Sed ecce, *remedium* contra hanc est *visio Salvatoris,* ostendens eum *afflictum* contra primum, *egenum* contra secundum, *abiectum* contra tertium. — Videant ergo poenitentes *afflictum* Salvatorem, ut sanetur infirmitas *carnalitatis;* ad cuiusmodi visionem invitat nos ipsemet Threnorum primo[^p629-5]: « O vos omnes, qui transitis per viam! attendite et videte, si est dolor sicut dolor meus »; quasi dicat: non ametis voluptatem carnis, « quia non est dolor sicut dolor meus ». — Videant iterum Salvatorem *egenum,* ut sanetur infirmitas *cupiditatis.* « Venit enim princeps huius mundi et in me non habet quidquam », Ioannis decimo quarto[^p629-6]. Ideo dicitur in Psalmo: « Videant pauperes et laetentur; quaerite Deum, et vivet anima vestra »; quasi dicat: non timeant egere, sed laetentur nihil habere, cum Dominus nihil habeat. Item, non quaerant divitias mortales, sed vitales, scilicet Dominum vitae; unde ait: « Quaerite Dominum, et vivet anima vestra ». — Videant iterum Salvatorem *abiectum,* ut sanetur infirmitas *ambitionis;* de qua visione Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p629-7]: « Vidimus eum, et non erat aspectus, et desideravimus eum, despectum et novissimum virorum » etc.; quasi dicat: non ametis « primos accubitus in coenis et primas cathedras in synagogis », nec timeatis humiliari, quia « vidimus eum despectum et novissimum virorum ». « Non enim est servus maior Domino suo ».

10. Sic ergo *visio patientis* sanat poenitentes, cum ipsum vident in cruce *afflictum, nudum* et *abiectum;* quod figuratum est Numerorum vigesimo primo[^p629-8], ubi, postquam immiserat Dominus in populum murmurantem « serpentes ignitos », misertus eorum iussit, erigi serpentem in ligno, « quem aspicientes sanabantur ». *Serpens,* sensualitas; *ignis,* concupiscentia; *igniti serpentes,* motus concupiscentiales, qui sunt in sensualitate; *serpens in ligno,* Christus in cruce, quem qui aspiciunt sanantur; unde Sapientiae decimo sexto[^p629-9]: « Qui enim conversus est, non per hoc quod videbat, sanabatur, sed per te, omnium Salvatorem ».

11. Visio iterum spiritualis Salvatoris *confortat pugnantes.* Dat enim *audaciam* aggredientibus, dat *confidentiam* certantibus[^p629-10], dat *victoriam* perseveranter sustinentibus. Dominus enim *praecedit* aggredientes, *adiuvat* congredientes, *liberat* et victores facit perseverantes. — Videant ergo eum *accedentes ad pugnam* praecedentem, ut sint *audaces;* propter quod dicitur Hebraeorum duodecimo[^p629-11]: « Curramus per
<!-- page 630 -->
patientiam ad propositum nobis certamen, aspicientes in auctorem fidei et consummatorem Iesum ». In ipsum aspexerunt Martyres, qui ipsum secuti sunt per tormenta; de quibus dicitur in Psalmo: « Viderunt ingressus tuos, Deus » etc.; *ingressus* quidem illius, qui « per sanguinem suum introivit semel in Sancta »; *viderunt,* inquam, et ideo post illum ingressi sunt per tormenta. — Videant iterum Salvatorem *certantes* ad adiuvandum assistentem, ut sint *viriles* vel constantes; qualiter vidit eum beatus Stephanus in certamine positus, Actuum septimo[^p630-1]: « Cum esset Stephanus plenus Spiritu sancto, intuens in caelum, vidit gloriam Dei et Iesum stantem a dexteris virtutis Dei ». Vidit enim *caelos apertos* quasi hospitium sibi paratum ad hospitandum, et *Iesum stantem* quasi paratum ad adiuvandum. — Videant etiam *perseverantes* ipsum servantem et protegentem, ut sint *victores.* « Fidelis enim est Deus, qui non permittet, vos tentari ultra quod potestis, sed faciet cum tentatione proventum », primae ad Corinthios decimo[^p630-2]; et dicit David: « Providebam Dominum in conspectu meo semper; quoniam a dextris est mihi, ne commovear ».

12. Sic ergo spiritualis visio Salvatoris *confortat pugnantes.* Ipse enim est dux exercitus caeli, quem vidit beatus Ioannes Apocalypsis decimo nono[^p630-3]; unde dicitur ibi: « Vidi caelum apertum, et ecce, equus albus, et qui sedebat super eum vocabatur *fidelis* et *verax,* et cum iustitia iudicat et pugnat. Oculi eius sicut flamma ignis, et in capite eius diademata multa etc.; et exercitus caeli sequebatur eum ». — Nota exemplum de Angelo, qui visus est, secundi Machabaeorum tertio[^p630-4], qui percussit Heliodorum.

13. Visio iterum spiritualis Salvatoris *deducit proficientes* in omnibus operibus iustitiae, visio videlicet ipsius *conversantis.* Quoniam autem ad perfectam virtutem tria necessaria sunt[^p630-5]: *scire* quantum ad intellectum, *velle* quantum ad affectum, *impermutabiliter operari* quantum ad effectum; notandum, quod in conversatione eiusdem videre possumus, unde *instruatur intellectus* ad sciendum, *moveatur affectus* ad volendum, *dirigatur effectus* ad proficiendum. In conversatione eius invenimus *verba, signa* et *exempla:* verba *veritatis,* signa *potestatis* et exempla *sanctitatis.* Docet enim *verbis,* movet *signis,* dirigit *exemplis.* — Videant ergo proficientes ipsum *docentem,* ut *instruantur,* ut instructus fuit beatus Ioannes, primae Ioannis primo[^p630-6]: « Quod audivimus et vidimus » etc.; « et manus nostrae contrectaverunt de Verbo vitae » etc.; « hoc testificamur et annuntiamus vobis »; et beatus Petrus secundae Petri primo: « Non doctas fabulas secuti, notam fecimus vobis Domini nostri Iesu Christi virtutem et praesentiam, sed speculatores facti ipsius maiestatis ». Valet enim ad instructionem non solum audire, sed videre magistrum suum; unde qui vult doceri a Christo convertat oculos suos spirituales ad ipsum; tertii Regum decimo[^p630-7]: « Omnis terra desiderabat videre vultum Salomonis, ut audiret sapientiam eius ». — Videant iterum eum *miracula facientem,* ut *moveantur.* Nam « multitudo magna sequebatur eum, quia videbant signa, quae faciebat[^p630-8] »; unde Deuteronomii undecimo dicitur filiis Israel: « Oculi vestri viderunt omnia opera Domini magna, quae fecit, ut custodiatis universa mandata eius » etc.; quod autem faciebat in eis visio *corporalis,* hoc debet in fidelibus facere visio *spiritualis.* — Videant iterum eum *operantem* et exemplum bonae operationis dantem, ut *imitentur;* unde Ioannis decimo tertio[^p630-9]: « Exemplum dedi vobis ». Propter quod dicitur Hebraeorum octavo: « Vide et fac secundum exemplar, quod tibi in monte monstratum est »; et sumitur de Exodi vigesimo quinto.

14. Sic ergo visio spiritualis Salvatoris *deducit* et *promovet proficientes;* quod significatum est Iosue tertio[^p630-10], ubi clamabant praecones ad filios Israel volentes proficisci in terram promissionis: « Cum videritis arcam foederis Domini Dei nostri et sacerdotes stirpis Leviticae portantes eam, vos quoque sequimini praecedentes ». *Arca foederis* dicitur humanitas Christi, in qua erant tria, secundum quod dicitur ad Hebraeos nono, *lex* veritatis, *virga* potestatis et *manna* sanctitatis; quae modo dicebantur in Christo *verba, signa* et *exempla.* « Cum ergo videritis arcam et sacerdotes stirpis Leviticae portantes eam », id est viros apostolicos, *glorificantes et portantes Deum in corpore suo*[^p630-11], « vos quoque sequimini praecedentes ».

15. Visio iterum spiritualis Salvatoris *laetificat contemplantes,* cum scilicet vident *gloriam regnantis,* quae visio ostendit eum *admirabilem* accedentibus ad contemplationem, *delectabilem* commorantibus in contemplatione, *laudabilem* revertentibus a contemplatione. Huiusmodi enim visione excitantur[^p630-12]
<!-- page 631 -->
*meditantes* ad *admirandum, sentientes* ad *gaudendum, revertentes* sive praedicantes ad *laudem.* — Videtur ergo *admirabilis* accedentibus per meditationem; quod figuratum est tertii Regum decimo[^p631-1]: « Videns regina Saba omnem sapientiam Salomonis et domum, quam aedificaverat, et cibos mensae eius et habitacula servorum eius et ordines ministrantium, vestes quoque eorum etc.; non habebat ultra spiritum »; et sequitur, quomodo dixit: « Vera est fama, quam audivi de te » etc.; ubi significatur per *regnum Salomonis* gloria Christi regnantis; « plus enim quam Salomon hic ». — Videtur iterum *delectabilis* commorantibus et experientibus per devotionem; unde Iob trigesimo tertio[^p631-2]: « Deprecabitur Dominum, et placabilis ei erit, et videbit faciem eius in iubilo »; et Isaiae sexagesimo: « Tunc videbis » meditando, « et afflues » sentiendo « et mirabitur et dilatabitur cor tuum »; *mirabitur* videns, et *dilatabitur* sentiens. — Videtur iterum *laudabilis* revertentibus; Lucae secundo, ubi, visa humili maiestate nati Regis, « reversi sunt pastores, glorificantes et laudantes Deum in omnibus, quae audierant et viderant ». Quodsi ad laudandum movet visa humilitas nascentis, quam movere debet visa gloria regnantis!

16. Sic ergo visio Salvatoris laetificat *contemplantes,* dum aspectus spiritualis gloriae eius est illis materia *admirationis,* materia *delectationis* et materia *laudis;* quod totum figuratum est in visione, quam ostendit Dominus in monte discipulis suis, Lucae nono[^p631-3], ubi visa gloria Domini *territi et stupefacti sunt,* deinde *delectati,* unde Petrus dixit: « Domine, bonum est nos hic esse »; tertio vero ad *praedicandum* inflammati; Actuum quarto: « Non possumus quae vidimus et audivimus non loqui ».

17. Visio iterum spiritualis *consolatur exspectantes* quantum ad statum perseverantiae, cum scilicet considerant eum ad servos suos in hac miseria venientem et consolantem. « Non vos, inquit[^p631-4], relinquam orphanos, vado et venio ad vos ». Facit autem huiusmodi visio eius tria: *visitat,* ne *doleant* de promissorum dilatione; *certificat,* ne *diffidant* de promissorum assecutione; *praemiat,* et hoc in fine, ut *gaudeant* de promissorum adeptione. — Visio igitur spiritualis Salvatoris *visitat exspectantes,* ne *doleant;* qualiter visitatus est ille Simeon « exspectans redemptionem Israel », Lucae secundo[^p631-5]: « Nunc, inquit, dimittis, Domine, servum tuum in pace, quia viderunt oculi mei salutare tuum »; Genesis trigesimo secundo: « Vidi Dominum meum facie ad faciem, et salva facta est anima mea ». — Visio iterum spiritualis Salvatoris *certificat de promissis,* ne *diffidant;* quod figuratum est Genesis vigesimo octavo[^p631-6], ubi vidit Iacob scalam et Dominum innixum scalae et dicentem sibi: « Ego sum Dominus Deus Abraham, patris tui, et Deus Isaac; terram, in qua dormis, dabo tibi et semini tuo ». Vidit enim eum ut *supportantem,* ne diffidat ex *propria impossibilitate;* quod figuratum est in hoc, quod erat *innixus scalae;* item vidit *promittentem,* ne diffidat ex Domini *voluntatis mutatione,* quod notatur ibi: *Terram, in qua dormis, dabo tibi.* — Visio iterum spiritualis *praemiat,* ut *gaudeant* de promissorum adeptione; Isaiae ultimo[^p631-7]: « Videbitis, et gaudebit cor vestrum »; Apocalypsis ultimo: « Servient illi, et videbunt faciem eius et nomen eius in frontibus eorum ». « Haec est enim vita aeterna, ut cognoscant te solum verum Deum » etc., Ioannis decimo septimo.

18. Sic ergo visio spiritualis Salvatoris *consolatur exspectantes;* quod significatum est in reversione Domini nostri Iesu Christi post resurrectionem ad discipulos suos, ubi primo *consolatus est* eos ex familiari sui ostensione: « Videte manus meas et pedes meos, quia ego ipse sum », Lucae ultimo[^p631-8]. Deinde *certificavit* eos de Scripturarum impletione, eodem: « Haec sunt verba, quae locutus sum ad vos, cum adhuc essem vobiscum »; et de ipsius *reversione ad iudicium,* Actuum primo: « Sic veniet, quemadmodum vidistis eum euntem in caelum ». Ultimo *praemiat* ex eius beata visione in patria, ubi revelata facie Regem cernunt in gloria: « Videbimus tunc facie ad faciem », primae ad Corinthios decimo tertio. — Sic ergo salutaris est omnibus visio Salvatoris: *peccantibus,* visio *iudicantis; poenitentibus,* visio *patientis; pugnantibus,* visio *triumphantis; proficientibus,* visio *conservantis; contemplantibus,* visio *regnantis; exspectantibus,* visio *revertentis;* de qua visione salutari dictum est: *Vidimus Dominum.*

### Annotatio 79.

(Luc. 3, 6.). *Videbit omnis caro salutare Dei.*

Verba ista et *evangelica* sunt et *prophetica: evangelica,* quia scripta sunt in Evangelio, Lucae tertio; *prophetica,* quia sumta de Isaiae quadragesimo[^p631-9]: « Videbit omnis caro pariter, quod os Domini locutum est »; vel quinquagesimo secundo: « Videbunt omnes fines terrae salutare Dei nostri »; in quibus praenuntiatur adventus Salvatoris in carnem ut *visibilis* et ut *salutaris:* primum, cum dicitur: *Videbit omnis caro,* quasi dicat: non solum videbit oculus *mentis,* sed apparebit visibilis oculis *carnis;* secundum, cum dicitur: *Salutare Dei,*
<!-- page 632 -->
id est illum qui est effectivus salutis, qui scilicet « operatus est salutem in medio terrae[^p632-1] ». In his autem duobus tangitur perfecta consolatio exspectantium adventum Salvatoris, ut scilicet salus eis *daretur* et *ostenderetur.* Si enim *daretur* et non ostenderetur; non laetificaretur nostra tristitia. Si *ostenderetur* et non daretur, non relevaretur nostra inopia. Propter quod rogabat David[^p632-2]: « Ostende nobis, Domine, misericordiam tuam et salutare tuum da nobis », quasi dicat: *da,* ut habeamus, *ostende,* ut videamus, quatenus videndo et habendo, gaudeamus; quod notatur, cum dicitur: *Videbit omnis caro* etc. Haec enim sunt duo bona nobis exhibita in adventu Salvatoris, scilicet *salus visibilis* et *visio salutaris*[^p632-3].

### Chapter XXI. *After this he manifested himself again.*

### Annotation 73.

(Vers. 1.). *After this Jesus manifested himself again at the sea of Tiberias.*

1. Note that Christ after his resurrection is read to have appeared in five places: on the *way,* on the *sea,* in the *house,* in the *garden* and on the *mountain.* — On the *way* he appeared, Luke, the last chapter[^p622-9], to the disciples going to Emmaus. — On the *sea* he appeared to the disciples labouring; whence here: *After this he manifested himself* etc. — In the *house* he appeared to the disciples gathered together, Luke, the last chapter[^p622-10], John, the twentieth chapter. — In the *garden* he appeared to Mary Magdalen, John, the twentieth chapter. — On the *mountain* he appeared to the eleven, who went into Galilee, Matthew, the last chapter.

2. But note that spiritually the Lord *appears,* that is, shows his benefits, to five kinds
<!-- page 623 -->
of men: to the *obedient,* the *patient,* those *studying,* those *contemplating* and those *presiding;* so that we may say that to the *obedient* he appears on the way of obedience, to the *patient* on the sea of penance, to those *studying* in the house of wisdom, to those *contemplating* in the garden, to those *presiding* on the mountain.

### Collation LXXV.

(Vers. 14.). *This is now the third time that Jesus was manifested to his disciples, after he was risen from the dead.*

1. Note concerning the appearances of the Lord after the resurrection; and note that the Lord is read to have appeared ten times: first, to Mary Magdalen, John, the twentieth chapter[^p623-1]. — Second, to the women returning from the monument, who « came up and took hold of his feet », Matthew, the last chapter. — Third, to Peter, Luke, the last chapter: « The Lord is risen indeed, and hath appeared to Simon ». — Fourth, to the disciples going to Emmaus, Luke, the last chapter. — Fifth, to the disciples gathered in the house; John, the twentieth chapter[^p623-2]; and these things were done on the very day of Easter. — Sixth, to the disciples and to Thomas, John, the twentieth chapter. — Seventh, to the disciples fishing at the sea of Tiberias, John, the twenty-first chapter. — Eighth, to the eleven disciples on mount Thabor, Matthew, the last chapter. — Ninth, to the disciples gathered in the supper-room on the day of the ascension, Mark, the last chapter[^p623-3]. — Tenth, on the same day on mount Olivet, when, « while they looked on, he was raised up », Luke, the last chapter and the Acts, the first chapter.

2. So note *spiritually* that the Lord's *appearing* is the showing of his benefits; and the Lord's *hiding,* the withdrawal of his benefits. Note, then, that he appears to three kinds of men, because he shows his benefits to three kinds of men, namely to the *active,* the *contemplative* and *prelates.* These three kinds of men are signified by these three names: *Daniel, Noe* and *Job,* Ezechiel, the fourteenth chapter[^p623-4]; and by *Peter, James* and *John,* Matthew, the seventeenth chapter; and by *Mary, Martha* and *Lazarus,* John, the eleventh chapter.

3. He appears, then, to the active, and this in a threefold difference, namely to those *returning* through penance, to the *proficient* through obedience, to those *ascending* through excellence of life. — He appears, then, to those *returning* through the works of penance; and this is figured, John, the twentieth chapter[^p623-5], in the appearance in which he appeared to Thomas. For Thomas, having gone out from the disciples, did not see the Lord; « Thomas, he says, who is called Didymus, was not with them when Jesus came ». But having returned to the disciples, he saw and touched him; John, the twentieth chapter: « Put in thy finger hither » etc. — He appears to the *proficient* through the works of obedience; and this is figured when he appeared to the disciples going to Emmaus, Luke, the last chapter[^p623-6], where it is read that « Jesus himself also drawing near went with them » to Emmaus. *Emmaus* is interpreted desire of counsel; *the disciples going to Emmaus* are the faithful becoming proficient from the commandments to the counsels. — He appears also to those *ascending* through the works of excellence, and this is figured when he appeared to the eleven disciples on mount Thabor, Matthew, the last chapter[^p623-7]: « The eleven disciples, he says, went into Galilee, unto the mountain where Jesus had appointed them, and seeing him they adored ». By the *mountain* understand excellence of life. — The Lord appears, then, to the *active returning* through the works of penance, to the *proficient* through the works of obedience, to those *ascending* through the works of excellence.

4. The Lord appears also to the *contemplative,* and this in a threefold difference, namely to those *sitting* in meditation, those *weeping* in prayer, those *enjoying* in contemplation. — The Lord appears, then, to those *sitting in meditation,* or also in *holy collation;* and this is figured when he appeared to the disciples *gathered together;* Luke, the last chapter[^p623-8]: « Now whilst they were speaking these things, he says, Jesus stood in the midst of them, and saith to them: Peace be to you »; and of this also John, the twentieth chapter. — He appears also to those *weeping in prayer;* which is figured when he appeared to Mary weeping, John, the twentieth chapter: « She turned herself back, he says, and saw Jesus standing, and she knew not that it was Jesus; and Jesus saith to her: Woman, why weepest thou » etc. — He appears also to those *enjoying in contemplation;* which is figured when he appeared to the eleven disciples gathered in the supper-room; Mark, the last chapter[^p623-9]: « At length, he says, he appeared to the eleven as they were at table ». By the *supper-room of the disciples,* which was at Jerusalem, understand the refectory of contemplation. In this supper-room the Lord is read to have eaten with them; the Acts, the first chapter: « Eating together with them, he commanded them that they should not depart from Jerusalem » etc. — So, then, the Lord appears to those *contemplating,* sitting in meditation or in holy collation, weeping in prayer and enjoying in contemplation.

5. He appears also to *prelates,* and this in a fourfold difference. For it belongs to prelates to *approach* to God and to *return* to the people. Prelates *approach* to God for two reasons: for *consulting* and for *offering.* — To prelates *consulting,* then, the Lord appears;
<!-- page 624 -->
and this is figured when he appeared to Peter; Luke, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p624-1]: « The Lord is risen indeed, and hath appeared to Simon »; and the Master says in the Histories: « when Peter ran to the monument ». For Peter, having heard of the Lord's resurrection, would not wholly acquiesce in the women's account, but went to the monument, wishing to consult the truth itself. And the Lord appeared to Peter visiting the monument, signifying that he appears to *those consulting him.* To prelates *consulting,* then, the Lord appears. — He appears also to prelates *offering;* which is figured when he appeared to the women, Matthew, the last chapter[^p624-2]. For we read there how « they had bought sweet spices, that coming they might anoint Jesus »; behold, the *offering;* and how, as they went out from the monument, « Jesus met them, saying: All hail. But they came up and took hold of his feet »; and there follows how the Lord enjoined them to announce to the disciples the glory of the resurrection; which is the whole mystery, in which it is signified that the Lord appears to those *offering* and in the secret of contemplation gives himself to them, and there instructs them what, returning, they should say to the people. — So, then, the Lord appears in two ways to prelates *approaching* to him.

6. He appears also in two ways to prelates *returning* to the people. For a prelate ought to return to the people, that he may *preach by word* and *lead by example.* — The Lord appears, then, to those *preaching;* which is figured John, the last chapter[^p624-3], when the Lord appeared to the Apostles fishing at the sea of Tiberias. *The fishing of the Apostles* is the preaching of prelates. — He appears also to those *leading* the flock by good example; and this is figured Luke, the last chapter and the Acts, the first chapter, when he appeared to the disciples going out to mount Olivet; which was the last appearance of Christ after the resurrection. For then, « while they looked on, he was raised up into heaven ».

### Collation LXXVI.

(Vers. 17.). *Peter, lovest thou me?*

1. In these words the Lord *requires* of Peter *spiritual* dilection, when he says: *Peter, lovest thou me?* Nor does he require only the spiritual, but speaking through discretion he *forbids love that is not spiritual; Lovest thou,* he says, *me?* as if he were to say: *me* and not anything else *than me,* or anything else *against me.* — Note, then, that there is love *of Christ,* and this is *praiseworthy;* and there is love *of the world,* and this is *blameworthy.*

2. Of the love *of the world,* Wisdom, the fifteenth chapter[^p624-4]: « The lovers of evil things are worthy of death ». And it is to be noted that this love is threefold. For there is the love *of carnality;* the second to Timothy, the third chapter[^p624-5]: « Men shall be lovers of themselves »; and a little after: « Lovers of pleasures more than of God ». — There is also the love *of cupidity,* of which Ecclesiasticus, the tenth chapter: « There is not a more wicked thing than to love money. For such a one setteth even his own soul to sale ». — There is also the love *of ambition,* of which Matthew, the twenty-third chapter: « They love the first places at feasts, and the first chairs in the synagogues ».

3. This love is *to be rejected* for many reasons: first, because it is *criminal;* Wisdom, the fifteenth chapter[^p624-6]: « The lovers of evil things » etc. Nor is it any wonder; for the sinful soul brings on fornication, while the bridegroom looks on; whence Ezechiel, the twenty-third chapter: « Oolla committed fornication against me, and doted upon her lovers ». — Second, because it is *laborious;* Osee, the second chapter: « She said: I will go after my lovers »; and it is added: Therefore « she shall follow after her lovers, and shall not overtake them ». — Third, because it is *costly;* Ezechiel, the sixteenth chapter[^p624-7]. « Gifts are given to all harlots, but thou hast given hire to thy lovers, and thou gavest them, that they might come in to thee to commit fornication »; Proverbs, the twenty-first chapter: « He that loveth wine and fat things shall not be rich ». — Fourth, because it is *unfruitful;* Ecclesiasticus, the fifth chapter: « He that loveth riches shall reap no fruit from them », which is true of every inordinate love; to the Romans, the sixth chapter: « What fruit had you in those things, of which you are now ashamed »? Osee, the second chapter: « She shall seek her lovers and shall not find them ». — Fifth, because it is *unstable;* for it quickly fails; Jeremias, the thirtieth chapter[^p624-8]: « All thy lovers have forgotten thee, they will not seek thee »; and Jeremias, the fourth chapter: « In vain shalt thou dress thyself, thy lovers have despised thee, they will seek thy life ». — Sixth, because it is *seditious.* For it is turned into enmities; which is figured the second of Kings, the thirteenth chapter, where it speaks of how, after Amon had corrupted Thamar, his sister, « he hated her with an exceeding great hatred, so that the hatred wherewith he hated her was greater than the love with which he had loved her before ». — For all these things the love of the world is to be relinquished.

<!-- page 625 -->
4. *To be embraced* is the *love of Christ,* after the example of her who said[^p625-1]: « The kingdom of the world and all the love of the age I have despised for the love of my Lord Jesus Christ », namely the love which the Lord requires, saying: *Peter, lovest thou me?* — Note that the good love is to be embraced, because it is *easy* to acquire, *delightful* to have, *secure* to possess.

5. It is *easy to acquire.* For he loves all who wish to love him; Proverbs, the eighth chapter[^p625-2]: « I love them that love me »; and in John, the sixteenth chapter, to those who love him, lest he seem to promise them too little of his love, he promises not only *his own* love, but also *the Father's,* saying: « For the Father himself loveth you, because you have loved me »; and Jeremias, the third chapter: « But thou hast prostituted thyself to thy lovers, nevertheless return to me, and I will receive thee ». Behold, that it is easy to acquire. — It is also *delightful to have;* Wisdom, the eighth chapter[^p625-3]: « In her friendship is good delight ». Truly *good,* because chaste, not polluted; whence blessed Agnes: « Him I love, whom when I shall have loved, I am chaste; when I shall have touched, I am clean; when I shall have received, I am a virgin ». There is, then, a clean *delight* and also a delightful *friendship;* Proverbs, the fifth chapter: « Let her be thy most gracious hind, and let the fawn be most agreeable; let her breasts inebriate thee at all times, and be thou delighted continually with her love ». Behold, that it is delightful to have. — It is nonetheless *secure to possess;* Jeremias, the thirty-first chapter[^p625-4]: « I have loved thee with an everlasting charity »; the first to the Corinthians, the thirteenth chapter: « Charity never falleth away ». And note that it is therefore *secure* to possess, because the spiritual lover has the beloved within himself; whence Augustine[^p625-5]: « *Within* me is what I love, where there *shines* what place does not contain, where there *sounds* what time does not snatch away, where there is *fragrant* what the breeze does not scatter, where there *savours* what no eating diminishes, where there *clings* what no anxiety tears away ». Behold, the inseparability of love.

6. But it is to be noted that this love ought to be foremost in *affection,* assiduous in *cohabitation,* serviceable in *work.* — For it requires *intensity of the affections;* therefore it is said in Matthew, the tenth chapter[^p625-6]: « He that loveth father or mother more than me is not worthy of me »; and in Judges, the sixteenth chapter, it is said to those who love tepidly: « How dost thou say thou lovest me, when thy mind is not with me »? — It requires also *assiduity of cohabitation,* according to that of Deuteronomy, the thirty-third chapter: « Benjamin, the best beloved of the Lord, shall dwell confidently in him; as in a bride chamber shall he abide all the day long ». — It requires also *serviceableness of work;* whence Gregory[^p625-7]: « The love of God is never idle; for it works great things, if it is; but if it refuses to work, it is not love »; whence Deuteronomy, the eleventh chapter: « Love the Lord thy God, and observe his precepts » etc.; and in John, the last chapter, to Peter confessing his love an office is at once enjoined: « Feed my sheep »; and Bede says: « This is the sole and true proof of an entire love toward God, if toward the brethren thou shalt be zealous to exercise the care of a solicitous labour ».

### Annotation 74.

(Vers. 3.). *They went up into the ship.*

Note that the *ship* is religion. For this is a ship useful for *crossing over;* Matthew, the ninth chapter[^p625-8]: « Jesus entering into a boat, crossed over and came into his own city ». — For *trading;* Proverbs, the last chapter: « She is like the merchant's ship, she bringeth her bread from afar ». — For *fishing;* whence here it is said of the disciples wishing to fish: *They went forth, and went up into the ship.* — Religious, then, *cross over* by labouring, *trade* by praying, *fish* by preaching, for all of which religion avails.

### Annotation 75.

(Vers. 6.). *Cast the net on the right side of the ship.*

Note that four things are said here of fishers: first, that they *spread the net;* second, that they *take the fishes;* third, that they *draw the fishes;* fourth, that they *draw them out;* all of which are plain in the letter. — Spiritual fishers, then, *spread the net* by preaching; they *take the fishes* by converting sinners; they *draw the fishes* by promoting them through good examples; but they *draw them out* by leading them through to the heavenly kingdoms.

### Annotation 76.

(Vers. 7.). *He girt himself up and cast himself into the sea.*

Note that the sea is sometimes passed by *swimming,* as is said here of Peter; sometimes by *sailing,* as here[^p625-9] is said of the others: *But the other disciples came in the ship;* sometimes by *treading,* as in Matthew, the fourteenth chapter, it is said
<!-- page 626 -->
of Peter. — Note that they pass the sea by *swimming* who immerse themselves in the solicitudes of the world; and these expose themselves to the danger of being drowned. — But they *sail across* the sea who *cautiously* busy themselves with worldly occupations. — But they *tread* the sea who withdraw themselves *altogether* from the solicitudes of the world and have worldly things for their sustenance alone. These last are *borne up* by the sea, but neither labour nor are immersed; the former are *borne up* and *labour,* but are not immersed. But the first are both *borne up* and *labour* and *are immersed.*

### Annotation 77.

(Vers. 16.). *Feed my lambs.*

Note that some feed *lambs,* some *wolves,* some *themselves.* — *Good* prelates feed lambs; of whom Isaias, the fortieth chapter[^p626-1]: « He shall feed his flock like a shepherd » etc. — *Hirelings* feed wolves; John, the tenth chapter: « The hireling seeth the wolf coming and leaveth the sheep and flieth » etc. — *Thieves* feed themselves, to whom it is said in Ezechiel, the thirty-fourth chapter: « Woe to the shepherds of Israel, that fed themselves » etc. — Behold, then, in the flock of the Lord some are *thieves,* who feed themselves; some *hirelings,* who feed *wolves;* some *true shepherds,* who feed lambs.

### Annotation 78.

(Vers. 22.). *So I will have him to remain till I come.*

Note that the *will* of the Lord Jesus is powerful. For his *will cleanses* the leper; Matthew, the eighth chapter[^p626-2]: « He touched him, saying: I will, be thou cleansed ». — It *preserves* the disciple; John, the last chapter: *So I will have him to remain.* — It *glorifies* the minister; John, the seventeenth chapter: « Father, I will that where I am, there also my minister may be ». — The will of Christ, then, is that which *cleanses the fault* of leprosy, *preserves the life* of grace, *bestows the life* of glory.

### Collation LXXVII.

(Cap. 4, 13.). *Whosoever drinketh of this water, shall thirst again,* John, the fourth chapter[^p626-3].

1. In this part it treats of the manifold kind of water. It is to be noted that there are many differences of waters. For there is the water *of fault* — and there is the water *of punishment* — and the water *of fortune* — and the water *of grace* — and also the water *of glory.* Of waters of this kind some are *to be fled,* others *to be passed over,* others are *to be sought.* — The waters, then, which we *flee,* are the waters *of fault* and the waters *of punishment;* among which first we must see of the waters *of fault.*

2. Now the water *of fault* is manifold. For there is the water *of pride;* the Apocalypse, the seventeenth chapter[^p626-4]: « Come, and I will shew thee the condemnation of the great harlot, who sitteth upon many waters » etc.; it speaks of worldly vanity. — The water *of avarice,* of which it can rightly be said here: *He that drinketh of this water shall thirst again.*

> The more the waters are drunk, the more they are thirsted for.

There is the water *of lust;* Genesis, the forty-ninth chapter[^p626-5]: « Ruben, thou art poured out as water; grow thou not, because thou wentest up to thy father's bed, and didst defile his couch ».

3. These waters are *abominable,* because *muddy;* for they darken the reason; whence it is said in Jeremias, the second chapter[^p626-6]: « What hast thou to do in the way of Egypt, to drink the troubled water ». — Likewise, because *unclean;* for they defile the affection; Leviticus, the eleventh chapter: « Any meat which you eat, if water be poured upon it, shall be unclean ». — Because *barren;* for they empty out virtue; Numbers, the fifth chapter: « Let him give the woman to drink the most bitter waters »; and there follows: « The waters of malediction shall go through her, and her belly swelling, her thigh shall rot ».

4. Upon these waters follows the water *of punishment.* And the waters of punishment are *horrible,* because *dark* to see, *bitter* to drink, *intemperate* to feel. — They are, then, *dark* to see; whence Exodus, the fifteenth chapter[^p626-7]: « The depths have covered them; they are sunk to the bottom like a stone »; and again: « The depths are gathered together in the midst of the sea ». — *Bitter* as to the punishment of the worm; Exodus, the fifteenth chapter: « The Lord brought back upon them the waters of the sea ». — *Intemperate* as to the punishment of sense; Job, the twenty-fourth chapter: « Let him pass from the snow waters to excessive heat ». — These, then, are the waters which we flee. For we flee the waters *of fault,* lest we fall into the waters *of punishment.*

5. There follows concerning the waters which we *pass over,* namely concerning the waters *of fortune;* and because fortune is twofold: *prosperous* and *adverse;* therefore it is to be noted that there are waters *of prosperity* and *of adversity.* The water *of prosperity* is manifold: the water *of delights;* Proverbs, the ninth chapter[^p626-8]:
<!-- page 627 -->
« Stolen waters are sweeter, and hidden bread is more pleasant ». — The water *of riches;* the Apocalypse, the twelfth chapter: « The serpent cast out of his mouth after the woman water as it were a river, that he might cause her to be carried away by the river ». — The water *of honours;* the Psalm: « The floods have lifted up, O Lord, the floods have lifted up their waves, with the noise of many waters. Wonderful are the surges of the sea » etc.

6. The passage of these waters is *dangerous.* For these waters are *swiftly running down;* the Psalm[^p627-1]: « They shall come to nothing, like water running down ». — *Drawing downward;* the Apocalypse, the twelfth chapter: « The serpent cast after the woman water as it were a river, that he might cause her to be carried away by the river ». For these waters draw all things with them, according to that of Ovid:

> The wave carries off tigers, nor does the might of the lightning avail the boar,
> Nor swift legs the stag that is borne away.

They are also *drowning;* Exodus, the fifteenth chapter[^p627-2]: « They sunk as lead in the mighty waters ».

7. There follows concerning the waters *of adversity,* the passage of which, though it be feared, is nevertheless not so dangerous. For we fear them, because they are *bitter,* and therefore hard to drink; Exodus, the fifteenth chapter[^p627-3]: « They could not drink the waters of Mara, because they were bitter ». — Likewise, because they are *deep,* and therefore dangerous to swim; therefore David asks: « Let not the tempest of water drown me, nor the deep swallow me up ». — Likewise, because they are *strong,* and therefore hard to sustain; and therefore David: « Save me, O God, for the waters are come in even unto my soul ». — Therefore we fear these waters, lest we *be afflicted* by their bitterness, *be overthrown* by their strength, *be drowned* by their depth.

8. But in all these things the divine help *strengthens* us, which furnishes us the remedy of them. For the Lord *sweetens bitterness;* Exodus, the fifteenth chapter[^p627-4]: « He shewed Moses a tree; which when he had cast into the waters, they were turned into sweetness ». — He *dries up depth;* Job, the twelfth chapter: « If he withhold the waters, all things shall be dried up »; Isaias, the eleventh chapter: « I will strike the river in the seven streams, so that men may pass through it in their shoes »; the Psalm: « Rivers ran down in the dry land ». — He *holds back strength;* Josue, the third chapter[^p627-5]: « When the children of Israel had entered the Jordan, the waters stood, coming down in one place » etc.; and above it had been said to Josue: « The waters of the Jordan that are beneath shall run down and fail, but those that come from above shall stand together upon a heap »; the Psalm; « In a flood of many waters, they shall not come nigh unto him ».

9. These, then, are the waters which we pass over; there are other waters which we *seek,* which are the waters *of grace* and the waters *of glory.* Among which first we must see of the waters *of grace,* which are manifold, namely the waters *of remission* or justification; in Ezechiel, the forty-seventh chapter[^p627-6], it is said of the waters which « came down to the right side of the temple ». — The waters *of instruction;* Ecclesiasticus, the fifteenth chapter: « With the water of wholesome wisdom she shall give him to drink ». — The waters *of spiritual consolation,* which David bewailed, saying: « My soul is as earth without water unto thee »; Proverbs, the twenty-fifth chapter: « As cold water to a thirsty soul, so is good tidings from a far country ».

10. These waters are *to be sought,* because they *cleanse;* Ezechiel, the thirty-sixth chapter[^p627-7]: « I will pour upon you clean water, and you shall be cleansed from all your filthiness ». — Because they *refresh;* Numbers, the twentieth chapter: « Open, O Lord, thy treasure, a fountain of living water, that being satisfied, their murmuring may cease ». — Because they *nourish* and *make fruitful;* whence in the Psalm[^p627-8]: « Like a tree which is planted near the running waters, which shall bring forth its fruit in due season »; Job, the fourteenth chapter: « At the scent of water it shall spring ».

11. Upon these waters follows *the water of glory.* This water is *desirable,* chiefly for three reasons. For this is a water *delighting, satisfying* and *quickening.* This water, then, has *delight;* for it delights the *sight;* the Apocalypse, the twenty-second chapter[^p627-9]: « He shewed me a river of water of life, clear as crystal ». It delights also the *taste;* etc. the Psalm: « Thou shalt make them drink of the torrent of thy pleasure ». — Likewise, it has *satiety;* John, the fourth chapter[^p627-10]: « He that shall drink of this water, that I will give him, shall not thirst for ever »; and the Apocalypse, the seventh chapter: « They shall no more hunger nor thirst » etc. — Likewise, it has *liveliness,* so that it can never fail; the Apocalypse, the twenty-first chapter: « To him that thirsteth, I will give of the fountain of the water of life, freely »; and again: « He that shall overcome shall possess these things, and I will be his God, and he shall be my son ».

### Collation LXXVIII.

(Cap. 3, 5.). *Unless a man be born again of water and the Holy Ghost, he cannot enter into the kingdom of heaven,* John, the third chapter.

<!-- page 628 -->
1. Concerning the entrance of the heavenly kingdom, note that the gate of the kingdom is *lowly,* and therefore the *lifted up* does not enter by it; it is *narrow,* and therefore the *puffed up* does not enter by it; it is *holy,* and therefore the *polluted* does not enter by it. — Therefore those *lifted up by honours* do not enter; Matthew, the eighteenth chapter[^p628-1]: « Unless you be converted and become as little children, you shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven ». — Nor those *widened by possessions;* Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « Amen I say to you, that a rich man shall hardly enter into the kingdom of heaven; and again I say to you: It is easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle, than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of heaven ». — Nor those *polluted by pleasures;* the Apocalypse, the twenty-first chapter[^p628-2]: « There shall not enter into it any thing defiled, or that worketh abomination ».

2. Note that the Lord entered *bowed down, despoiled, put to death:* « Ought not Christ to have suffered these things, and so to enter into his glory[^p628-3] »? But these « have not known my ways, so I swore in my wrath: If they shall enter into my rest ».

3. But note that entrance into the kingdom of heaven the king grants to his *servants,* to his *friends,* and to his *companions.* *Servants* are the active, who render to God the *service of action; friends,* the contemplative, who give to God the *service of devotion* or of dilection; *companions* and fellow-soldiers, who go with him through the *hardships of the passion.* — The *servants,* then, enter; Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p628-4]: « Well done, good and faithful servant » etc.; there follows: « Enter thou into the joy of thy Lord ». — The *friends;* Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter, of the virgins, « they that were ready went in with him to the marriage ». — The *companions* of the king are those of whom the Apocalypse, the twenty-second chapter[^p628-5]: « Blessed are they that wash their robes in the blood of the Lamb, that they may have a right to the tree of life, and may enter in by the gates into the city »; John, the third chapter: « Unless a man be born again » etc.; and Matthew, the nineteenth chapter: « If thou wilt enter into life, keep the commandments ».

### Collation LXXIX.

(Cap. 20, 25.). *We have seen the Lord,* John, the twentieth chapter.

1. Note the threefold vision of the Saviour, who is rightly called *the salvation of God:* one, which we recall from the *past,* by which he was seen *bodily* in the flesh; another, which we seek in the *present,* by which he is seen *spiritually* in the mind; the third, which we await in the *future,* by which he will be seen *blissfully* in eternal beatitude.

2. Of the first, Baruch, the third chapter[^p628-6]: « Afterwards he was seen upon earth, and conversed with men ». — Of the second, in the Psalm: « Be free[^tr-vacare], and see that I am God; I will be exalted among the nations, and I will be exalted in the earth ». *Be free* from the occupations of the flesh, *and see* with the eyes of the mind; Isaias, the sixty-second chapter: « The Gentiles shall see thy just one », it is said to Sion; « and all kings thy glorious one ». — Of the third, John, the seventeenth chapter[^p628-7]: « Father, those whom thou hast given me, I will that where I am, they also may be with me, that they may see my clarity which thou hast given me, because thou hast loved me before the foundation of the world ». — The first vision *has passed,* the second *stands,* the third *is to come.* Wherefore let us take the second, which alone can be had in the present, which spiritual vision of the Saviour is indeed saving for all.

3. *We have seen the Lord.* The *spiritual* vision of the Saviour is therefore saving for all who are to be saved, because it *terrifies* sinners[^p628-8], *heals* penitents, *strengthens* those fighting, *leads* the proficient, *gladdens* those contemplating, *consoles* those awaiting. Which is taken according to six states, namely *of fault, of penance, of fight, of justice, of wisdom* and *of perseverance. Sinners are terrified* by seeing him *judging; penitents are healed* by seeing him *suffering; those fighting are strengthened* by seeing him *triumphing; the proficient are led* by seeing him *conversing; those contemplating are gladdened* by seeing him *reigning; those awaiting are consoled* by seeing him *returning* and visiting his own in this misery. Nor do there seem[^p628-9] to be more things to be considered in him than either his *conversation,* or his *passion,* or his *tribulation,* or his *triumph,* or his *kingdom,* or his *judgement,* or his *return* to his own in this life.

4. The spiritual vision of the Saviour, then, *terrifies* sinners, that they may *come to their senses.* For by the fear of the Lord every one declineth from evil; and this because it shows them their judge *seeing* all things, *searching out* all things, *punishing* all things; which three things ought most to terrify a sinner, namely if he considered above himself his judge, who sees all things, searches out all things, will punish all things. — Would, then, that sinners saw spiritually, lest they are one day to see bodily the judge *knowing* all things! of whom Job, the thirty-seventh chapter[^p628-10]: « He is great in strength, and in justice, and in judgement »; in *strength* of power, in *justice* of equity and in *judgement* of truth; and there follows: « Therefore men shall fear him, and all that seem to themselves to be wise shall not dare to behold him » namely those who are wise, « to hide their counsel from the Lord », as Isaias says, the twenty-ninth chapter.

5. Likewise, would that they saw the judge *searching out all things!* whose terrible vision Malachias the prophet describing, says in Malachias, the third chapter[^p628-11]: « Who shall be able to think of the day of his coming, and who shall stand to
<!-- page 629 -->
see him »? For he is terrible to *think of,* but more terrible to *see;* and he adds *why:* « For he is like a refining fire, and like the fuller's herb »; a *refining fire* leaves nothing of metal which it does not melt; the *fuller's herb,* that is the teasel, leaves neither hair nor thread which it does not touch.

6. Likewise, would that they saw the judge *punishing* all things! to the vision of whom he himself invites them, Jeremias, the seventh chapter[^p629-1]: « Go ye, he says, to my place in Silo, where my name dwelt from the beginning; and see what I did to it for the wickedness of my people Israel ». *Silo,* torn away or carried over, *where the name of the Lord dwelt from the beginning,* is the heavenly fatherland, where he dwelt from the beginning who « dwelleth on high »; which was *torn away* or carried over as to the part of the angels that sinned. *Go* then *and see what I did to it* etc., because, « if God spared not the angels that sinned, but delivered them, drawn down by infernal ropes » etc., the second of Peter, the second chapter[^p629-2]; and Jeremias, the seventh chapter: « And now, because you have done all these works, saith the Lord » etc.; and there follows: « Behold, my wrath and my indignation is enkindled against this place, upon men and upon beasts, and upon the trees of the field, and upon the fruits of the land, and it shall burn and shall not be quenched ».

7. So, then, the vision of the judge *seeing* and knowing all things, *searching out* all things, *punishing* all things, terrifies sinners; seeing which, Job says, the forty-second chapter[^p629-3]: « With the hearing of the ear I have heard thee, but now my eye seeth thee; therefore I reprehend myself, and do penance in dust and ashes »; *I have heard* through inspiration, *I have seen* through consideration; therefore *I reprehend myself* inwardly in the mind and *do penance* outwardly in work. And Isaias, the sixth chapter[^p629-4], having seen the glory of the Lord sitting « upon a throne high and elevated », cried out, saying and accusing himself: « Woe is me, because I have held my peace! because I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people that hath unclean lips, and I have seen with my eyes the Lord God of hosts »; *woe is me, because I have held my peace,* which is *of omission; because I am unclean* etc., which is *of commission.*

8. Again, the spiritual vision of the Saviour *heals penitents,* showing them the Lord *suffering.* For from the threefold concupiscence there is wont to rise a *threefold infirmity:* from the concupiscence of delights the infirmity *of carnality,* from the concupiscence of riches, the infirmity *of cupidity,* from the concupiscence of honours the infirmity *of ambition.*

9. But behold, the *remedy* against this is the *vision of the Saviour,* showing him *afflicted* against the first, *needy* against the second, *cast off* against the third. — Let penitents, then, see the Saviour *afflicted,* that the infirmity *of carnality* may be healed; to which vision he himself invites us, Lamentations, the first chapter[^p629-5]: « O all ye that pass by the way! attend and see if there be any sorrow like to my sorrow »; as if he were to say: love not the pleasure of the flesh, « for there is no sorrow like to my sorrow ». — Let them see again the Saviour *needy,* that the infirmity *of cupidity* may be healed. « For the prince of this world cometh, and in me he hath not any thing », John, the fourteenth chapter[^p629-6]. Therefore it is said in the Psalm: « Let the poor see and rejoice; seek ye God, and your soul shall live »; as if he were to say: let them not fear to want, but rejoice to have nothing, since the Lord has nothing. Likewise, let them not seek mortal riches, but living ones, namely the Lord of life; whence he says: « Seek ye the Lord, and your soul shall live ». — Let them see again the Saviour *cast off,* that the infirmity *of ambition* may be healed; of which vision Isaias, the fifty-third chapter[^p629-7]: « We have seen him, and there was no sightliness, and we have desired him, despised and the last of men » etc.; as if he were to say: love not « the first places at feasts and the first chairs in the synagogues », nor fear to be humbled, because « we have seen him despised and the last of men ». « For the servant is not greater than his Lord ».

10. So, then, the *vision of him suffering* heals penitents, when they see him on the cross *afflicted, naked* and *cast off;* which is figured in Numbers, the twenty-first chapter[^p629-8], where, after the Lord had sent among the murmuring people « fiery serpents », having mercy on them he commanded a serpent to be raised up on a wood, « which when they looked upon, they were healed ». The *serpent,* sensuality; *fire,* concupiscence; the *fiery serpents,* the concupiscential motions which are in sensuality; the *serpent on the wood,* Christ on the cross, whom they who look upon are healed; whence Wisdom, the sixteenth chapter[^p629-9]: « For he that turned to it, was not healed by that which he saw, but by thee, the Saviour of all ».

11. Again, the spiritual vision of the Saviour *strengthens those fighting.* For it gives *boldness* to those attacking, gives *confidence* to those contending[^p629-10], gives *victory* to those enduring perseveringly. For the Lord *goes before* those attacking, *helps* those engaging, *delivers* and makes victors those persevering. — Let those, then, *approaching the fight* see him going before, that they may be *bold;* for which reason it is said in Hebrews, the twelfth chapter[^p629-11]: « Let us run by
<!-- page 630 -->
patience to the fight proposed to us, looking on the author of faith and the finisher, Jesus ». On him the Martyrs looked, who followed him through torments; of whom it is said in the Psalm: « They have seen thy goings, O God » etc.; the *goings* indeed of him who « by his own blood entered once into the Holies »; *they have seen,* I say, and therefore after him they have gone through torments. — Let those *contending* see the Saviour again, standing by to help, that they may be *manly* or constant; as blessed Stephen saw him, set in the contest, the Acts, the seventh chapter[^p630-1]: « But Stephen being full of the Holy Ghost, looking up steadfastly to heaven, saw the glory of God, and Jesus standing on the right hand of the power of God ». For he saw *the heavens opened* as a lodging prepared for him to be lodged, and *Jesus standing* as ready to help. — Let those *persevering* also see him preserving and protecting, that they may be *victors.* « For God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that which you are able, but will make also with temptation issue », the first to the Corinthians, the tenth chapter[^p630-2]; and David says: « I set the Lord always in my sight; for he is at my right hand, that I be not moved ».

12. So, then, the spiritual vision of the Saviour *strengthens those fighting.* For he is the leader of the army of heaven, whom blessed John saw, the Apocalypse, the nineteenth chapter[^p630-3]; whence it is said there: « I saw heaven opened, and behold, a white horse, and he that sat upon him was called *faithful* and *true,* and with justice doth he judge and fight. And his eyes were as a flame of fire, and on his head were many diadems etc.; and the armies that are in heaven followed him ». — Note the example of the Angel who was seen, the second of Machabees, the third chapter[^p630-4], who struck Heliodorus.

13. Again, the spiritual vision of the Saviour *leads the proficient* in all the works of justice, the vision, namely, of him *conversing.* But since for perfect virtue three things are necessary[^p630-5]: *to know* as to the understanding, *to will* as to the affection, *to work unchangeably* as to the effect; it is to be noted that in his conversation we can see whence *the understanding may be instructed* to knowing, *the affection may be moved* to willing, *the effect may be directed* to making progress. In his conversation we find *words, signs* and *examples:* words *of truth,* signs *of power* and examples *of holiness.* For he teaches by *words,* moves by *signs,* directs by *examples.* — Let the proficient, then, see him *teaching,* that they may *be instructed,* as blessed John was instructed, the first of John, the first chapter[^p630-6]: « That which we have heard and have seen » etc.; « and our hands have handled, of the Word of life » etc.; « this we testify and declare unto you »; and blessed Peter, the second of Peter, the first chapter: « Not having followed cunningly devised fables, we have made known to you the power and presence of our Lord Jesus Christ, but having been eyewitnesses of his majesty ». For it avails for instruction not only to hear, but to see one's teacher; whence he who wishes to be taught by Christ, let him turn his spiritual eyes to him; the third of Kings, the tenth chapter[^p630-7]: « All the earth desired to see Solomon's face, to hear his wisdom ». — Let them see him again *working miracles,* that they may *be moved.* For « a great multitude followed him, because they saw the signs which he did[^p630-8] »; whence in Deuteronomy, the eleventh chapter, it is said to the children of Israel: « Your eyes have seen all the great works of the Lord, that he hath done, that you may keep all his commandments » etc.; and what *bodily* vision did in them, this *spiritual* vision ought to do in the faithful. — Let them see him again *working* and giving the example of good working, that they may *imitate;* whence John, the thirteenth chapter[^p630-9]: « I have given you an example ». For which reason it is said in Hebrews, the eighth chapter: « See and make according to the pattern which was shewn thee on the mount »; and it is taken from Exodus, the twenty-fifth chapter.

14. So, then, the spiritual vision of the Saviour *leads* and *promotes the proficient;* which is signified in Josue, the third chapter[^p630-10], where the heralds cried to the children of Israel, wishing to set out into the land of promise: « When you shall see the ark of the covenant of the Lord our God, and the priests of the race of Levi carrying it, rise you up also, and follow them as they go before ». *The ark of the covenant* is said to be the humanity of Christ, in which there were three things, according to what is said to the Hebrews, the ninth chapter, the *law* of truth, the *rod* of power and the *manna* of holiness; which were just now called in Christ *words, signs* and *examples.* « When, then, you shall see the ark and the priests of the race of Levi carrying it », that is, apostolic men, *glorifying and bearing God in their body*[^p630-11], « follow them also as they go before ».

15. Again, the spiritual vision of the Saviour *gladdens those contemplating,* when, namely, they see *the glory of him reigning,* which vision shows him *admirable* to those approaching contemplation, *delightful* to those abiding in contemplation, *praiseworthy* to those returning from contemplation. For by a vision of this kind there are stirred up[^p630-12]
<!-- page 631 -->
*those meditating* to *admire, those feeling* to *rejoice, those returning* or preaching to *praise.* — He is seen, then, *admirable* by those approaching through meditation; which is figured in the third of Kings, the tenth chapter[^p631-1]: « The queen of Saba seeing all the wisdom of Solomon and the house which he had built, and the meat of his table, and the apartments of his servants, and the order of his ministers, and their apparel etc.; had no longer any spirit in her »; and there follows how she said: « The report is true which I heard of thee » etc.; where by *the kingdom of Solomon* is signified the glory of Christ reigning; « for a greater than Solomon here ». — He is seen again *delightful* by those abiding and experiencing through devotion; whence Job, the thirty-third chapter[^p631-2]: « He shall pray to the Lord, and he will be gracious to him, and he shall see his face with joy »; and Isaias, the sixtieth chapter: « Then shalt thou see » by meditating, « and abound » by feeling, « and thy heart shall wonder and be enlarged »; it *shall wonder* seeing, and *be enlarged* feeling. — He is seen again *praiseworthy* by those returning; Luke, the second chapter, where, having seen the lowly majesty of the King born, « the shepherds returned, glorifying and praising God for all the things they had heard and seen ». But if the humility of him being born, when seen, moves to praise, how much ought the glory of him reigning, when seen, to move!

16. So, then, the vision of the Saviour gladdens *those contemplating,* while the spiritual beholding of his glory is to them matter *of admiration,* matter *of delight* and matter *of praise;* all of which is figured in the vision which the Lord showed on the mountain to his disciples, Luke, the ninth chapter[^p631-3], where, the glory of the Lord having been seen, *they were terrified and amazed,* then *delighted,* whence Peter said: « Lord, it is good for us to be here »; but third, inflamed to *preach;* the Acts, the fourth chapter: « We cannot but speak the things which we have seen and heard ».

17. Again, the spiritual vision *consoles those awaiting* as to the state of perseverance, when, namely, they consider him coming to his servants in this misery and consoling them. « I will not leave you, he says[^p631-4], orphans, I go away and I come unto you ». And a vision of him of this kind does three things: it *visits,* lest they *grieve* at the delay of the promises; it *makes certain,* lest they *mistrust* of attaining the promises; it *rewards,* and this at the end, that they may *rejoice* at the obtaining of the promises. — The spiritual vision of the Saviour, then, *visits those awaiting,* lest they *grieve;* as that Simeon was visited, « waiting for the redemption of Israel », Luke, the second chapter[^p631-5]: « Now, he says, thou dost dismiss thy servant, O Lord, in peace, because my eyes have seen thy salvation »; Genesis, the thirty-second chapter: « I have seen my Lord face to face, and my soul has been saved ». — Again, the spiritual vision of the Saviour *makes certain of the promises,* lest they *mistrust;* which is figured in Genesis, the twenty-eighth chapter[^p631-6], where Jacob saw a ladder and the Lord leaning upon the ladder and saying to him: « I am the Lord God of Abraham, thy father, and the God of Isaac; the land, wherein thou sleepest, I will give to thee and to thy seed ». For he saw him as *supporting,* lest he mistrust from his *own impossibility;* which is figured in this, that he was *leaning upon the ladder;* likewise he saw him *promising,* lest he mistrust from a *change of the Lord's will,* which is noted there: *The land, wherein thou sleepest, I will give to thee.* — Again, the spiritual vision *rewards,* that they may *rejoice* at the obtaining of the promises; Isaias, the last chapter[^p631-7]: « You shall see, and your heart shall rejoice »; the Apocalypse, the last chapter: « They shall serve him, and they shall see his face, and his name shall be on their foreheads ». « For this is eternal life, that they may know thee, the only true God » etc., John, the seventeenth chapter.

18. So, then, the spiritual vision of the Saviour *consoles those awaiting;* which is signified in the return of our Lord Jesus Christ after the resurrection to his disciples, where first *he consoled* them by the familiar showing of himself: « See my hands and my feet, that it is I myself », Luke, the last chapter[^p631-8]. Then *he made them certain* of the fulfilment of the Scriptures, in the same: « These are the words which I spoke to you, while I was yet with you »; and of his *return to judgement,* the Acts, the first chapter: « He shall so come, as you have seen him going into heaven ». Lastly *he rewards* by the blessed vision of him in the fatherland, where with face unveiled they behold the King in glory: « We shall see then face to face », the first to the Corinthians, the thirteenth chapter. — So, then, the vision of the Saviour is saving for all: for *sinners,* the vision of him *judging;* for *penitents,* the vision of him *suffering;* for *those fighting,* the vision of him *triumphing;* for *the proficient,* the vision of him *preserving;* for *those contemplating,* the vision of him *reigning;* for *those awaiting,* the vision of him *returning;* of which saving vision it is said: *We have seen the Lord.*

### Annotation 79.

(Luc. 3, 6.). *All flesh shall see the salvation of God.*

These words are both *evangelical* and *prophetic: evangelical,* because they are written in the Gospel, Luke, the third chapter; *prophetic,* because taken from Isaias, the fortieth chapter[^p631-9]: « All flesh together shall see, that the mouth of the Lord hath spoken »; or the fifty-second chapter: « All the ends of the earth shall see the salvation of our God »; in which is foretold the coming of the Saviour into flesh as *visible* and as *saving:* the first, when it is said: *All flesh shall see,* as if he were to say: not only will the eye *of the mind* see, but he will appear visible to the eyes *of the flesh;* the second, when it is said: *The salvation of God,*
<!-- page 632 -->
that is, him who is the effecter of salvation, who, namely, « hath wrought salvation in the midst of the earth[^p632-1] ». And in these two is touched the perfect consolation of those awaiting the coming of the Saviour, namely that salvation should be *given* them and *shown.* For if it were *given* and not shown; our sadness would not be gladdened. If it were *shown* and not given, our want would not be relieved. For which reason David asked[^p632-2]: « Shew us, O Lord, thy mercy, and grant us thy salvation », as if he were to say: *grant,* that we may have, *shew,* that we may see, so that by seeing and having, we may rejoice; which is noted when it is said: *All flesh shall see* etc. For these are the two goods shown forth to us in the coming of the Saviour, namely *a visible salvation* and *a saving vision*[^p632-3].
