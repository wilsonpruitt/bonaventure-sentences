# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XVIII, Vers. 24-27
## *Hic agitur de examinatione facta sub Caipha*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 485 -->

43. *Et misit eum Annas* etc. Hic agitur de examinatione facta *sub Caipha;* et dividitur haec pars in duas, quia primo ponitur *Christi ad iudicem adductio;* secundo, *ipsius a discipulo negatio,* ibi[^p485-1]: *Erat autem Simon Petrus.*

(Vers. 24.). Quantum ad primum breviter se expedit Evangelista et dicit, quod Christus missus est ad Caipham, nihil agens de examinatione facta ab eo, quia alii Evangelistae sufficienter dixerant. Ideo dicit: *Misit eum Annas ligatum ad Caipham pontificem,* a quo multipliciter exhonoratus fuit et reprobatus; sicut dicitur Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p485-2], quod « exspuerunt in faciem eius et colaphis eum ceciderunt, alii autem palmas in faciem eius dederunt » etc.

44. (Vers. 25.). *Erat autem.* Hic ponitur *negatio Petri,* et ponitur negandi *occasio* in hoc, quod erat commixtus ministris; unde dicit: *Erat autem Simon Petrus stans et calefaciens se,* scilicet cum ministris, ut supra[^p485-3]. Et ex hoc, quod inter eos erat, contigit, quod quaererent ab eo. *Dixerunt ergo ei: Nunquid et tu ex discipulis eius es?* Et haec fuit *occasio negandi;* ideo sequitur negationis *iteratio: Negavit autem ille et dixit: Non sum.* Expavit Petrus ad multitudinem circumstantium; Ecclesiastici septimo: « Ne pecces in multitudine civitatis, nec te immittas in populum ».

45. (Vers. 26.). *Dicit ei unus ex servis pontificis.* Hic ponitur *negationis consummatio;* et ponitur *inquisitio servi,* deinde *negatio Petri* et *signum,* quod est cantus galli. *Dicit ei unus ex servis pontificis, cognatus eius, cuius abscidit Petrus auriculam*[^p485-4]. Ratio redditur, quare magis accusabat eum in ultionem fratris: *Nonne ego te vidi in horto cum illo?* quasi dicat: non potes te mihi occultare.

46. (Vers. 27.). *Iterum ergo negavit Petrus.* Hic *consummatur negatio Petri,* quia trina fuit; et in *signum* consummationis subditur: *Et statim gallus cantavit.* Augustinus[^p485-5]: « Ecce, Medici completa praedictio, aegroti convicta praesumtio. Non enim factum est quod iste dixerat: Animam meam ponam pro te; sed quod ille praedixerat: Ter me negabis ».

### Quaestiones.

47. Quaest. I. Sed quaerit Chrysostomus[^p485-6]: unde est hoc, quod Evangelistae ita concorditer scripserunt de Petri negatione, cum videatur, quod peccatum socii deberent occultare?

Respondet: Hoc quidem fecerunt, « non discipulum accusantes, sed nos erudire volentes, quantum malum est non Deo omne concedere, sed in se ipso confidere ».

48. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de *modo* negandi. — Dominus enim praedixit ei: *Ter me negabis;* et hic dicit beatus Ioannes, quod solum negavit, quod non erat eius *discipulus.*

Respondet Augustinus[^p485-7]: « Sane in hac negatione Petri debemus advertere, non solum ab eo negari Christum, qui dicit, eum non esse Christum; sed ab illo etiam, qui, cum sit, negat, se esse Christianum. Unde negavit Christum Petrus, dum negavit se Christi discipulum ».

49. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de *loco* negationis Petri. — Et videntur alii Evangelistae dicere, quod fuerit in domo *Caiphae,* sicut dicitur Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p485-8]; Ioannes videtur dicere, quod in domo *Annae.*

**Respondeo** ad hoc: Est contrarietas inter expositores[^p485-9]. Nam Beda et Hieronymus dicunt, quod in-
<!-- page 486 -->
choata fuit in domo *Annae,* sed consummata in domo *Caiphae;* et alii Evangelistae dicunt *finem* negationis, Ioannes autem respicit *principium.* — Augustinus vero dicit, quod tota completa fuit in domo *Annae,* sicut videtur dicere Evangelista. Et quod alii dicunt, esse factam in domo *Caiphae,* hoc dicunt: quia Caiphas erat summus pontifex, quod erat Annae erat et Caiphae.

50. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de *tempore* negationis. — Nam hic dicit Ioannes, quod post *tertiam* negationem gallus cantavit; sed Marci decimo quarto[^p486-1] dicitur, quod post *primam* negationem cantavit.

**Respondeo:** Ad litteram sic fuit, sicut dicit Marcus[^p486-2], quod gallus cantavit ante trinam negationem et statim post trinam; sed primum galli cantum non advertit Petrus; secundum advertit et rediit ad cor suum[^p486-3], et ideo primum cantum alii tacent, sed ultimum omnes dicunt.

## English
<!-- page 485 -->

43. *And Annas sent him* etc. Here it treats of the examination made *under Caiphas;* and this part is divided in two, because first is set down *the bringing of Christ to the judge;* second, *his denial by a disciple,* there[^p485-1]: *Now Simon Peter was.*

(Vers. 24.). As to the first, the Evangelist dispatches himself briefly and says that Christ was sent to Caiphas, treating nothing of the examination made by him, because the other Evangelists had told it sufficiently. Therefore he says: *Annas sent him bound to Caiphas the high priest,* by whom he was in many ways dishonoured and reprobated; as is said in Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p485-2], that « they spat in his face and struck him with buffets, and others struck his face with the palms of their hands » etc.

44. (Vers. 25.). *Now … was.* Here is set down *the denial of Peter,* and the *occasion* of denying is set down in this, that he was mingled with the ministers; hence he says: *Now Simon Peter was standing and warming himself,* namely with the ministers, as above[^p485-3]. And from this, that he was among them, it came about that they asked him. *They said therefore to him: Art not thou also one of his disciples?* And this was *the occasion of denying;* therefore there follows the *reiteration* of the denial: *But he denied it, and said: I am not.* Peter took fright at the multitude of those standing around; Ecclesiasticus, the seventh chapter: « Sin not in the multitude of a city, neither cast thyself in among the people ».

45. (Vers. 26.). *One of the servants of the high priest saith to him.* Here is set down *the consummation of the denial;* and there is set down *the inquiry of the servant,* then *the denial of Peter* and the *sign,* which is the crowing of the cock. *One of the servants of the high priest, a kinsman to him whose ear Peter cut off, saith to him*[^p485-4]. The reason is given why he accused him the more, in vengeance for his brother: *Did I not see thee in the garden with him?* as if to say: thou canst not hide thyself from me.

46. (Vers. 27.). *Again therefore Peter denied.* Here *the denial of Peter is consummated,* because it was threefold; and in *sign* of the consummation there is added: *And immediately the cock crew.* Augustine[^p485-5]: « Behold, the prediction of the Physician fulfilled, the presumption of the sick man convicted. For what this man had said did not come to pass: I will lay down my soul for thee; but what that one had foretold: Thou shalt deny me thrice ».

### Questions.

47. Quaest. I. But Chrysostom asks[^p485-6]: whence is it that the Evangelists wrote so concordantly of Peter's denial, since it seems that they ought to have concealed the sin of a companion?

He responds: They did this indeed, « not accusing the disciple, but wishing to teach us how great an evil it is not to yield everything to God, but to trust in oneself ».

48. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked about the *mode* of denying. — For the Lord foretold to him: *Thou shalt deny me thrice;* and here blessed John says that he denied only that he was his *disciple.*

Augustine responds[^p485-7]: « Truly in this denial of Peter we ought to advert that Christ is denied not only by him who says that he is not the Christ, but also by him who, though he is one, denies that he is a Christian. Hence Peter denied Christ when he denied that he was Christ's disciple ».

49. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked about the *place* of Peter's denial. — And the other Evangelists seem to say that it was in the house of *Caiphas,* as is said in Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p485-8]; John seems to say: in the house of *Annas.*

**I respond** to this: There is a contrariety among the expositors[^p485-9]. For Bede and Jerome say that it was be-
<!-- page 486 -->
gun in the house of *Annas,* but consummated in the house of *Caiphas;* and the other Evangelists tell the *end* of the denial, but John looks to the *beginning.* — But Augustine says that it was wholly completed in the house of *Annas,* as the Evangelist seems to say. And that the others say it was done in the house of *Caiphas,* they say this because Caiphas was the supreme high priest: what was Annas's was Caiphas's too.

50. Quaest. IV. Likewise it is asked about the *time* of the denial. — For here John says that after the *third* denial the cock crew; but in Mark, the fourteenth chapter[^p486-1], it is said that after the *first* denial it crew.

**I respond:** To the letter it was thus, as Mark says[^p486-2], that the cock crew before the threefold denial and straightway after the threefold; but the first crowing of the cock Peter did not advert to; the second he adverted to and returned to his heart[^p486-3], and therefore the others are silent about the first crowing, but all tell the last.
