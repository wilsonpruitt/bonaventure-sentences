# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. I, Vers. 29-30
## *Perhibuit supra beatus Ioannes testimonium de Christo absente; hic perhibet testimonium de Christo ad se veniente*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 259 -->

61. *Altera die vidit Ioannes* etc. Perhibuit supra[^p259-16] beatus Ioannes testimonium de Christo *absente;* hic perhibet testimonium de Christo *ad se veniente.* Et dividitur haec pars in duas: quia primo describitur testimonii *perhibitio;* secundo, testimonii *certitudo* sive corroboratio, ibi: *Et ego nesciebam eum* etc.

Testimonii *perhibitio* describitur hoc ordine: primo tangitur *testificandi occasio;* secundo, *testificatio;* tertio vero, ad testimonium *praecedens comparatio.*

(Vers. 29.). *Testificandi occasio* primo tangitur; et haec fuit, quia Iesus venit ad Ioannem ab eo baptizari; propterea dicit: *Altera die vidit Ioannes Iesum venientem ad se*, scilicet ut baptizaretur; Matthaei tertio[^p259-17]: « Venit Iesus a Galilaea in Iordanem ad Ioannem, ut baptizaretur ab eo ».

62. *Et ait*, scilicet Ioannes. Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet ipsa *testificatio*, in qua perhibet
<!-- page 260 -->
testimonium de *innocentia humanitatis* et de *virtute Divinitatis.* Propter *innocentiam* dicit: *Ecce, Agnus Dei*, id est innocens et impollutus; Isaiae decimo sexto[^p260-1]: « Emitte Agnum, Domine, dominatorem terrae, de petra deserti ad montem filiae Sion ». Quantum ad *virtutem Divinitatis* dicit: *Ecce, qui tollit peccata mundi*, quod est solius divinae virtutis; Isaiae quadragesimo tertio[^p260-2]: « Ego sum, ego sum ipse, qui deleo iniquitates tuas propter me »; et dicit: *Ecce*, quia quem alii praenuntiaverunt ipse digito monstrat; ideo de ipso dicitur Matthaei undecimo: « Ipse est propheta et plus quam propheta ».

63. (Vers. 30.). *Hic est, de quo dixi* vobis. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet ad testimonium *praecedens comparatio.* Unde replicat testimonium, quod dederat in absentia eius; ideo dicit: *Hic est, de quo dixi*, in praecedenti scilicet testimonio: *post me venit vir*, scilicet temporali nativitate, quia per sex menses post eum natus. De hoc viro Ieremiae trigesimo primo[^p260-3]: « Novum faciet Dominus super terram: femina circumdabit virum ». *Qui ante me factus est*, dignitate scilicet, *quia prior me erat*, Divinitate; supra eodem: « In principio erat Verbum ».

### Quaestio.

64. Sed dubitatio est hic: 1. Cum minoris sit ire ad maiorem, non e converso; videtur, quod Ioannes debuit ire ad Christum.

2. Item, cum in Christo nulla fuerit macula; et baptismus significet ablutionem a sorde: ergo falsum significavit in ipso: ergo si veritas Christi non recipit falsitatem, non debuit baptizari a Ioanne.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod aliquid debito vel congruo modo fit, aut quia *necessitatis*, aut quia *supererogationis.* Quod minor vadat ad maiorem, hoc est debitum *necessitatis;* unde dixit Ioannes Domino[^p260-4]: « Ego a te debeo baptizari ». Quod vero maior vadat ad minorem, hoc est *perfectionis* et *supererogationis.* Sic Dominus venit ad Ioannem; unde ipse dixit Ioanni: « Sic decet nos implere omnem iustitiam », Matthaei tertio. Quod ergo venit, fuit condescensionis et in commendationem humilitatis.

2. Quod quaeritur: quare voluit baptizari? Respondeo: Dicendum, quod sicut *verbo* significativo aliquid dicitur de capite ratione membrorum, sic *actu* exercito aliquid significatur in capite pro membris. Unde baptismus ille nullam significabat ablutionem interiorem in Christo, sed solum in membris. Ratio autem, quare Christus voluit baptizari, fuit triplex: ut daret exemplum humilitatis; ut vim regenerativam conferret aquis; ut per baptismum Ioannis manifestaret se universis. Et haec ratio principalis tangitur in littera[^p260-5].

## English
<!-- page 259 -->

61. *The next day John saw* etc. Above[^p259-16] blessed John gave testimony about Christ *absent;* here he gives testimony about Christ *coming to him.* And this part is divided into two: because first is described the *giving* of the testimony; second, the *certitude* or corroboration of the testimony, at: *And I knew him not* etc.

The *giving* of the testimony is described in this order: first is touched the *occasion of testifying;* second, the *testifying;* third, the *comparison* to the testimony *going before.*

(Vers. 29.). The *occasion of testifying* is touched first; and this was, that Jesus came to John to be baptized by him; therefore he says: *The next day John saw Jesus coming to him*, namely in order to be baptized; Matthew, the third[^p259-17]: « Jesus cometh from Galilee to the Jordan, unto John, to be baptized by him ».

62. *And he saith*, namely John. Here is touched the second, namely the *testifying* itself, in which he gives
<!-- page 260 -->
testimony about the *innocence of the humanity* and about the *power of the Divinity.* On account of the *innocence* he says: *Behold the Lamb of God*, that is, innocent and undefiled; Isaiah, the sixteenth[^p260-1]: « Send forth the Lamb, O Lord, the ruler of the earth, from Petra of the desert, to the mount of the daughter of Sion ». As to the *power of the Divinity* he says: *Behold him who taketh away the sins of the world*, which belongs to the divine power alone; Isaiah, the forty-third[^p260-2]: « I am, I am he that blot out thy iniquities for my own sake »; and he says: *Behold*, because him whom others foretold he himself points out with the finger; therefore it is said of him, Matthew, the eleventh: « He is a prophet, and more than a prophet ».

63. (Vers. 30.). *This is he, of whom I said* to you. Here is touched the third, namely the *comparison* to the testimony *going before.* Whence he repeats the testimony which he had given in his absence; therefore he says: *This is he, of whom I said*, namely in the preceding testimony: *after me there cometh a man*, namely by temporal birth, because he was born six months after him. Of this man, Jeremiah, the thirty-first[^p260-3]: « The Lord shall make a new thing upon the earth: a woman shall compass a man ». *Who was made before me*, namely in dignity, *because he was before me*, in Divinity; above in the same: « In the beginning was the Word ».

### Question.

64. But there is a doubt here: 1. Since it belongs to the lesser to go to the greater, not conversely, it seems that John ought to have gone to Christ.

2. Likewise, since in Christ there was no stain, and baptism signifies a washing from filth: therefore it signified something false in him: therefore, if the truth of Christ does not admit falsity, he ought not to have been baptized by John.

**I respond:** It must be said that something is done in a due or fitting manner either because it is of *necessity*, or because it is of *supererogation.* That the lesser should go to the greater, this is a due of *necessity;* whence John said to the Lord[^p260-4]: « I ought to be baptized by thee ». But that the greater should go to the lesser, this is of *perfection* and of *supererogation.* Thus the Lord came to John; whence he himself said to John: « So it becometh us to fulfil all justice », Matthew, the third. That he came, therefore, was of condescension and for the commendation of humility.

2. As to what is asked: why did he wish to be baptized? I respond: It must be said that, just as by a significative *word* something is said of the head by reason of the members, so by an exercised *act* something is signified in the head on behalf of the members. Hence that baptism signified no inward washing in Christ, but only in the members. But the reason why Christ wished to be baptized was threefold: that he might give an example of humility; that he might confer regenerative power on the waters; that through the baptism of John he might manifest himself to all. And this principal reason is touched in the letter[^p260-5].

**⭐ CONSTRUALS, DISCLOSED.**
- (a) **¶ 61 / ¶ 63 *ad testimonium praecedens comparatio*** → "the *comparison* to the testimony *going before*". *praecedens* is grammatically ambiguous (nominative with *comparatio*, or neuter accusative with *testimonium*); the italic runs *praecedens comparatio* together and the marginale reads *Praecedens comparatio.*, but ¶ 63's exposition (*replicat testimonium, quod dederat in absentia eius … in praecedenti scilicet testimonio*) makes the sense the comparison **with the preceding testimony** (1:15 / 1:27). The English takes the sense and keeps both italic words.
- (b) *perhibitio* → "giving" (of testimony; *perhibere testimonium* → "give testimony", ruling 74 as conformed at the p. 268 gate, 2026-10-21; the build had "bear"); *testificatio* → "testifying"; *testificandi occasio* → "occasion of testifying"; *certitudo sive corroboratio* → "certitude or corroboration".
- (c) **Douay wording:** 1:29 *Altera die vidit Ioannes Iesum venientem ad se* → "The next day John saw Jesus coming to him"; *Et ait* → "And he saith"; *Ecce, Agnus Dei* → "Behold the Lamb of God"; *Ecce, qui tollit peccata mundi* → "Behold him who taketh away the sins of the world" (plural as printed; Douay "sin" adjusted). 1:30 *Hic est, de quo dixi* → "This is he, of whom I said"; *post me venit vir* → "after me there cometh a man"; ***Qui ante me factus est* → "Who was made before me"** (carrying `c1-v15-18` at 1:15 and `c1-v24-28` at 1:27; Douay "who is preferred before me" adjusted, since the gloss *dignitate scilicet* explains *factus est*); *quia prior me erat* → "because he was before me". Matt. 3:13 (body form *Venit Iesus…* → "Jesus cometh from Galilee to the Jordan, unto John, to be baptized by him"); Isa. 16:1 ("Send forth the Lamb, O Lord, the ruler of the earth, from Petra of the desert, to the mount of the daughter of Sion" — Douay's place-name "Petra" kept for the print's lower-case *petra*); Isa. 43:25; Matt. 11:9 in the body's form (*Ipse est propheta et plus quam propheta* → "He is a prophet, and more than a prophet"); Jer. 31:22 in the body's future (*Novum faciet Dominus* → "The Lord shall make a new thing"; n. 3 gives the Vulgate's *Creavit* → "hath created"); John 1:1; Matt. 3:14–15.

- (h) *innocens et impollutus* → "innocent and undefiled"; *digito monstrat* → "points out with the finger"; *per sex menses post eum natus* → "born six months after him".
