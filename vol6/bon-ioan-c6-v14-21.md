# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. VI, Vers. 14-21
## *Descripta sunt supra antecedentia ad mirabilem refectionem et concomitantia; hic describuntur consequentia, quae sunt quatuor, scilicet turbarum devotio ad Christum honorandum; absconsio Domini ad honorem fugiendum; descensus discipulorum ad transfretandum; adventus Christi ad succurrendum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 322 -->

24. *Illi autem*[^p322-9] *homines* etc. Descripta sunt supra antecedentia ad mirabilem refectionem et concomitantia; hic describuntur *consequentia,* quae sunt quatuor, scilicet *turbarum devotio* ad Christum honorandum; *absconsio Domini* ad honorem fugiendum; *descensus discipulorum* ad transfretandum; *adventus Christi* ad succurrendum.

(Vers. 14.). Primum ergo consequens ad praedictum miraculum fuit *turbarum devotio* ad Christum honorandum, et hoc, cognoscentes eum per signum, quod fecerat; unde dicit: *Illi ergo homines, cum vidissent, quod Iesus fecerat signum,* id est, considerassent signi magnitudinem, *dicebant: Quia hic est vere Propheta, qui venturus est in mundum,* id est Christus, qui exspectabatur tanquam Dominus totius mundi. Hoc propter signa dicebant; infra septimo[^p322-10]: « Nunquid Christus, cum venerit, plura signa faciet, quam quae hic facit » ?

25. (Vers. 15.). *Iesus ergo.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *absconsio Domini* ad honorem fugiendum, quem praevidit turbas sibi velle offerre. Propterea dicit: *Iesus ergo, cum cognovisset,* tanquam Deus scilicet, *quia venturi erant*[^p322-11]*, ut raperent eum,* quasi violenter, *et facerent eum regem, fugit iterum in montem ipse solus,* quasi prae celeritate discipulos non exspectans. Volebant enim eum rapiendo regem facere ante tempus, sicut ipse dicit infra decimo octavo[^p322-12]: « Regnum meum non est de hoc mundo ». Augustinus: « Fugit Christus, cum eum voluerunt facere regem; omnia enim bona terrena contempsit homo Christus, ut contemnenda monstraret; omnia mala sustinuit, ut nec in illis quaereretur felicitas, nec in istis timeretur adversitas ».

26. (Vers. 16.). *Ut autem sero factum est* etc. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *discipulorum descensus* ad recedendum; volebat enim Dominus turbas
<!-- page 323 -->
latenter dimittere, ut ipsum non invenirent; ideo praemisit discipulos suos, ut descendentes de monte transfretarent. Propterea dicit: *Ut autem sero factum est,* haec est hora redeundi ad hospitium; *descenderunt discipuli eius ad mare,* ut scilicet transfretarent.

27. (Vers. 17.). *Et cum ascendissent navim, venerunt trans mare in Capharnaum. Venerunt,* id est, venire coeperunt, per anticipationem dictum. Ideo *venerunt,* quia ibi Dominus habitabat; Matthaei quarto[^p323-1]: « Descendit Capharnaum et habitavit ibi ».

*Et tenebrae iam factae* etc. Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *adventus Christi* ad succurrendum, quia ipsi erant in periculo constituti propter obscuritatem noctis et tempestatem maris; ideo dicit: *Et tenebrae iam factae erant;* ecce, periculum noctis, et maxime quia deerat ductor; ideo dicit: *Et nondum*[^p323-2] *venerat ad eos Iesus;* ideo erant in periculo; infra duodecimo: « Qui ambulat in tenebris nescit, quo vadat ». Erat etiam periculum tempestatis; propterea dicit:

28. (Vers. 18.). *Mare autem, magno vento flante, exsurgebat.* Mare enim exsurgit ad ventum; unde Ionae primo[^p323-3]: « Misit Dominus ventum magnum in mare, et facta est tempestas magna ». Et quia Dominus prope est « his qui tribulato sunt corde »; ideo infertur adventus Domini ad succurrendum. Propterea dicit:

29. (Vers. 19.). *Cum remigassent ergo quasi stadia viginti quinque aut triginta; quasi* dicit propter incertitudinem spatii in mari; *viderunt*[^p323-4] *Iesum ambulantem super mare et proximum navi fieri,* ad succurrendum; sed non cognoverunt eum; ideo subditur: *Et timuerunt;* et ratio huius redditur Marci sexto: « Putaverunt, phantasma esse ». Et quia confortat Dominus corda timida, ideo subdit:

30. (Vers. 20.). *Ille autem dixit*[^p323-5] *eis: Ego sum,* scilicet qui protego; *nolite timere.* Cum Dominus adest, non est timendum, iuxta illud Psalmi: « Dominus protector meus; a quo trepidabo » ? Et ex hoc confisi sunt.

31. (Vers. 21.). *Voluerunt ergo accipere eum in navim,* agnoscentes et secure gaudentes; *et statim fuit navis ad terram, in quam ibant,* id est ad quam, quia Dominus omnipotens ibi erat, qui a nave non portabatur, sed portabat; ad Hebraeos primo[^p323-6]: « Portans omnia verbo virtutis suae »; et *statim,* quia Sapientiae duodecimo: « Subest enim tibi, cum volueris, posse ». Unde triplex fuit miraculum, scilicet super mare ambulatio, tempestatis quietatio et ad portum velocissima applicatio.

32. *Spiritualiter* notandum, *quae villa,* ad quam vadunt, *quomodo transeunt* et *quo ductore* perveniunt. — *Villa,* ad quam vadunt, Capharnaum, quae interpretatur *villa pulcherrima*[^p323-7]. Haec est caelestis patria, in qua summa pulcritudo, ut dicitur de Sanctis Sapientiae quinto[^p323-8]: *Accipient regnum decoris et diadema speciei de manu Domini.* — Ad hanc villam *transitur per mare,* id est per tribulationum amaritudines; Actuum decimo quarto[^p323-9]: *Per multas tribulationes oportet nos intrare in regnum Dei.* — Per hoc mare transvehit *navicula poenitentiae,* in qua homo salvatur, iuxta illud Actuum vigesimo septimo[^p323-10]: *Nisi in navi manseritis, vos salvi fieri non potestis;* similiter: *Si poenitentiam non egerimus, incidemus in manus Domini,* Ecclesiastici secundo. — Haec *navicula concutitur,* cum tentationum flatibus vexatur; Matthaei septimo[^p323-11]: *Venerunt flumina, et flaverunt venti.* — *Navigantes* vident Dominum *ambulantem,* sicut hic; *pugnantes* vident *stantem,* Actuum septimo[^p323-12]; *contemplantes, sedentem,* Isaiae sexto: *Vidi Dominum sedentem.* — Sed *adest* Dominus et *ad portum perducit,* iuxta illud Isaiae quadragesimo tertio[^p323-13]: *Cum transieris per aquas, tecum ero, et flumina non operient te;* et secundum illud Psalmi: *Cum ipso sum in tribulatione, eripiam eum et glorificabo eum; longitudine dierum replebo eum et ostendam illi salutare meum.*

### Quaestiones.

33. Quaest. I. Quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod turbae dicunt de Christo: *Hic est vere Propheta,* utrum dicant *verum,* vel *falsum.* — Quod *verum,* videtur: quia dicitur Deuteronomii decimo octavo[^p323-14]:
<!-- page 324 -->
*Prophetam suscitabit vobis Dominus;* et Glossa dicit, quod ad litteram intelligit de Christo.

**Sed contra:** Prophetia non est comprehensorum, qui vident in claritate, sed isti vident in speculo[^p324-1]: si ergo Christus fuit comprehensor, non ergo est propheta.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *propheta* dicitur dupliciter: vel ratione interioris inspirationis et revelationis, et sic importat secum coniunctam ignorantiam; dicitur etiam propheta ratione infallibilis et veracis denuntiationis. Ratione primi non dicitur Christus propheta, sed ratione secundi, quia verum dixit in omnibus et infallibiliter.

34. Quaest. II. Quaeritur de hoc, quod Dominus *fugit in montem.*

1. Si enim ipse erat rex, ergo recipiendus erat ut rex, et ipse debebat velle, quod sui eum reciperent, ut debebant: ergo non debebat fugere, immo se offerre.

2. Item, quando salus populi pendet ex aliquo electo, talis non debet se absentare: ergo si salus populi ex Christo, ergo etc.

3. Item, quare *solus fugit,* cum prius non solus ascenderit, sed ipse et discipuli eius?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Christus recipitur ut rex, quando recipitur, ut regnet in corde per fidem; sed quia illi de fide et salute non curabant, sed de temporali refectione: ideo se iuste absentat.

2. Et per hoc patet solutio ad sequens: quia non pendebat salus populi ex regno Christi carnali, sed ex regno spirituali.

3. Quod quaeritur: quare *solus fugit?* respondeo: propter *accelerationem* alios non exspectavit; propter *absconsionem* societatem habere noluit; propter *instructionem,* ut ostendat, quod in susceptione honorum ab amicis familiaribus non est petendum consilium, quia in hoc verificatur illud Michaeae septimo[^p324-2]: *Inimici hominis domestici eius.*

35. Quaest. III. Quaeritur de descensu discipulorum:

1. Quia dicitur, quod *descenderunt, cum sero factum est;* quomodo Magistrum solitarium reliquerunt? — Videtur, quod reprehensibiles fuerunt.

2. Item dicitur, quod *discipuli venerunt Capharnaum;* quia Marci sexto[^p324-3] dicitur, quod praecepit eis, ut venirent *Bethsaidam.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod, sicut dicit Matthaeus[^p324-4], Dominus *compulit discipulos suos ascendere in navim;* ideo non fuerunt reprehensibiles, quia Domino obediverunt.

2. Quod obiicitur de contrarietate, dicendum, quod Marcus nominat villam proximam, sed Ioannes, ad quam ire intendebant.

36. Quaest. IV. Quaeritur etiam de ambulatione Domini super mare:

1. Ex qua parte fuerit miraculum vel *aquae* supportantis, vel *corporis* superincedentis.

2. Et quare etiam hoc miraculum non ostendit turbis?

**Respondeo:** Quidam dicunt, quod fuit ex parte *corporis,* et quod pro tempore illo dotem assumsit agilitatis; hoc dicit magister Hugo[^p324-5]. — Alii dicunt, quod ex parte *aquae,* quae sub pedibus Creatoris se calcabilem praebuit; et accipitur a Gregorio[^p324-6]. — Sed melius est, quod utroque in sua natura et proprietate salvato, divina virtute corpus superefferebatur aquis.

2. Magis autem manifestavit discipulis quam turbis, quia, sicut discipuli praecellebant in intelligentia verborum ut magistri, sic etiam in aspectu miraculorum; unde multa in secreto ostendit eis.

## English
<!-- page 322 -->

24. *Now those*[^p322-9] *men* etc. The antecedents to the wonderful refection and the concomitants have been described above; here are described the *consequents,* which are four, namely *the devotion of the multitudes* to honour Christ; *the hiding of the Lord* to flee honour; *the going down of the disciples* to cross over; *the coming of Christ* to succour.

(Verse 14.). The first consequent, then, of the aforesaid miracle was *the devotion of the multitudes* to honour Christ, and this, as they knew him through the sign which he had done; whence he says: *Those men therefore, when they had seen what a sign Jesus had done,* that is, had considered the greatness of the sign, *said: This is of a truth the prophet, that is to come into the world,* that is, the Christ, who was awaited as the Lord of the whole world. They said this on account of the signs; below, the seventh[^p322-10]: « Will the Christ, when he cometh, do more signs than these which this man doth » ?

25. (Verse 15.). *Jesus therefore.* Here is touched the second, namely *the hiding of the Lord* to flee honour, which he foresaw the multitudes wished to offer him. Therefore he says: *Jesus therefore, when he knew,* namely as God, *that they were to come*[^p322-11]*, to take him by force,* as it were violently, *and make him king, fled again into the mountain himself alone,* as it were not waiting for the disciples by reason of his speed. For they wished, by seizing him, to make him king before the time, as he himself says below, the eighteenth[^p322-12]: « My kingdom is not of this world ». Augustine: « Christ fled when they wished to make him king; for the man Christ despised all earthly goods, that he might show they were to be despised; he endured all evils, that neither in the former should happiness be sought, nor in the latter adversity be feared ».

26. (Verse 16.). *And when evening was come* etc. Here is touched the third, namely *the going down of the disciples* to withdraw; for the Lord wished to dismiss the multitudes
<!-- page 323 -->
secretly, so that they might not find him; therefore he sent his disciples before, that going down from the mountain they might cross the sea. Therefore he says: *And when evening was come,* this is the hour of returning to one's lodging; *his disciples went down to the sea,* namely that they might cross over.

27. (Verse 17.). *And when they had gone up into the ship, they came over the sea to Capharnaum. They came,* that is, they began to come, said by anticipation. Therefore *they came,* because the Lord dwelt there; Matthew, the fourth[^p323-1]: « He went down to Capharnaum and dwelt there ».

*And it was now dark* etc. Here is touched the fourth, namely *the coming of Christ* to succour, because they were set in danger by reason of the darkness of the night and the tempest of the sea; therefore he says: *And it was now dark;* behold, the danger of the night, and above all because the guide was lacking; therefore he says: *And Jesus was not yet*[^p323-2] *come unto them;* therefore they were in danger; below, the twelfth: « He that walketh in darkness knoweth not whither he goeth ». There was also the danger of the tempest; therefore he says:

28. (Verse 18.). *And the sea arose, by reason of a great wind that blew.* For the sea rises at the wind; whence Jonas, the first[^p323-3]: « The Lord sent a great wind into the sea, and a great tempest was made ». And because the Lord is nigh « unto them that are of a contrite heart »; therefore the coming of the Lord to succour is brought in. Therefore he says:

29. (Verse 19.). *When they had rowed therefore about five and twenty or thirty furlongs; about* he says on account of the uncertainty of distance on the sea; *they saw*[^p323-4] *Jesus walking upon the sea, and drawing nigh to the ship,* to succour; but they did not know him; therefore it is added: *And they were afraid;* and the reason for this is given in Mark, the sixth: « They thought it was an apparition ». And because the Lord strengthens timid hearts, therefore he adds:

30. (Verse 20.). *But he said*[^p323-5] *to them: It is I,* namely I who protect; *be not afraid.* When the Lord is present, there is nothing to fear, according to that of the Psalm: « The Lord is my protector; of whom shall I be afraid » ? And from this they took confidence.

31. (Verse 21.). *They were willing therefore to take him into the ship,* recognizing him and rejoicing securely; *and presently the ship was at the land into which they were going,* that is, to which, because the omnipotent Lord was there, who was not carried by the ship, but carried it; to the Hebrews, the first[^p323-6]: « Upholding all things by the word of his power »; and *presently,* because Wisdom, the twelfth: « For thy power is at hand when thou wilt ». Hence the miracle was threefold, namely the walking upon the sea, the quieting of the tempest, and the most swift bringing to the port.

32. *Spiritually* it is to be noted *what the town is* to which they go, *how they cross,* and *by what guide* they arrive. — *The town* to which they go is Capharnaum, which is interpreted *most beautiful town*[^p323-7]. This is the heavenly fatherland, in which is the highest beauty, as is said of the Saints in Wisdom, the fifth[^p323-8]: *They shall receive a kingdom of glory, and a crown of beauty at the hand of the Lord.* — To this town *one crosses by the sea,* that is, through the bitternesses of tribulations; Acts, the fourteenth[^p323-9]: *Through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God.* — Over this sea *the little ship of penance* carries across, in which man is saved, according to that of Acts, the twenty-seventh[^p323-10]: *Except you stay in the ship, you cannot be saved;* likewise: *If we do not penance, we shall fall into the hands of the Lord,* Ecclesiasticus, the second. — This *little ship is shaken* when it is vexed by the blasts of temptations; Matthew, the seventh[^p323-11]: *The floods came, and the winds blew.* — *Those sailing* see the Lord *walking,* as here; *those fighting* see him *standing,* Acts, the seventh[^p323-12]; *those contemplating, sitting,* Isaias, the sixth: *I saw the Lord sitting.* — But the Lord *is present* and *leads through to the port,* according to that of Isaias, the forty-third[^p323-13]: *When thou shalt pass through the waters, I will be with thee, and the rivers shall not cover thee;* and according to that of the Psalm: *I am with him in tribulation, I will deliver him, and I will glorify him; I will fill him with length of days, and I will shew him my salvation.*

### Questions.

33. Question I. It is asked here first about what the multitudes say of Christ: *This is of a truth the prophet,* whether they say what is *true,* or what is *false.* — That it is *true* seems so: because it is said in Deuteronomy, the eighteenth[^p323-14]:
<!-- page 324 -->
*The Lord will raise up a prophet to you;* and the Gloss says that it understands this of Christ according to the letter.

**On the contrary:** Prophecy does not belong to comprehensors, who see in clarity, but these see in a mirror[^p324-1]: if therefore Christ was a comprehensor, then he is not a prophet.

**I respond:** It must be said that *prophet* is said in two ways: either by reason of interior inspiration and revelation, and so it carries with it ignorance joined to it; a prophet is also so called by reason of an infallible and truthful announcement. By reason of the first Christ is not called a prophet, but by reason of the second, because he said what is true in all things and infallibly.

34. Question II. It is asked about this, that the Lord *fled into the mountain.*

1. For if he was king, then he was to be received as king, and he ought to have willed that his own should receive him, as they ought: therefore he ought not to have fled, but rather to have offered himself.

2. Likewise, when the salvation of a people depends on someone elect, such a one ought not to absent himself: therefore if the salvation of the people was from Christ, therefore etc.

3. Likewise, why does he *flee alone,* when before he had not gone up alone, but he and his disciples?

**I respond:** It must be said that Christ is received as king when he is received so that he may reign in the heart through faith; but because those men did not care about faith and salvation, but about temporal refection, therefore he rightly absents himself.

2. And through this the solution to the next is plain: because the salvation of the people did not depend on a carnal kingdom of Christ, but on a spiritual kingdom.

3. As to what is asked: why does he *flee alone?* I respond: on account of *acceleration* he did not wait for the others; on account of *hiding* he would not have company; on account of *instruction,* that he might show that in the receiving of honours counsel is not to be sought from familiar friends, because in this that of Micheas, the seventh[^p324-2], is verified: *A man's enemies are they of his own household.*

35. Question III. It is asked about the going down of the disciples:

1. Because it is said that *they went down when evening was come;* how did they leave the Teacher solitary? — It seems that they were reprehensible.

2. Likewise it is said that *the disciples came to Capharnaum;* because in Mark, the sixth[^p324-3], it is said that he commanded them to come to *Bethsaida.*

**I respond:** It must be said that, as Matthew[^p324-4] says, the Lord *obliged his disciples to go up into the ship;* therefore they were not reprehensible, because they obeyed the Lord.

2. As to what is objected about the contrariety, it must be said that Mark names the nearest town, but John the one to which they intended to go.

36. Question IV. It is asked also about the walking of the Lord upon the sea:

1. On which side the miracle was, either of the *water* supporting, or of the *body* walking upon it.

2. And why too did he not show this miracle to the multitudes?

**I respond:** Some say that it was on the side of the *body,* and that for that time he assumed the dowry of agility; this Master Hugh says[^p324-5]. — Others say that it was on the side of the *water,* which offered itself to be trodden under the feet of the Creator; and this is taken from Gregory[^p324-6]. — But it is better that, each being preserved in its own nature and property, the body was borne up above the waters by divine power.

2. But he manifested it to the disciples rather than to the multitudes, because, as the disciples excelled in the intelligence of the words as teachers, so also in the sight of the miracles; whence he showed them many things in secret.
