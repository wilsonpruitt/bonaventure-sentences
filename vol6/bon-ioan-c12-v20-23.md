# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XII, Vers. 20-23
## *Secundo, de passionis praedictione ponuntur quatuor*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 416 -->

#### Secundo, de passionis praedictione ponuntur quatuor.

28. *Erant autem gentiles quidam.* Praedeterminatum est unum praeambulum ad passionem, quod est Pharisaeorum conspiratio; hic determinatur secundum, scilicet *gentilium illuminatio*[^p416-7], quae est fructus consequens passionem Christi valde utilis. Et quoniam in Christi passione facta est gentilium illuminatio et Iudaeorum excaecatio; ideo haec pars quatuor habet particulas. In prima determinatur vel ostenditur *vocationis gentium praefiguratio.* Secundo vero *passionis* praedicitur *fructificatio,* ibi[^p416-8]: *Amen, amen dico vobis* etc. Tertio vero, *praedicti fructus expositio,* ibi: *Turba ergo, quae stabat et audiebat vocem.* Quarto describitur *futura Iudaeorum excaecatio,* ibi: *Haec locutus est Iesus* etc.

Insinuatur ergo primo *futura gentium vocatio* in hoc, quod Dominum quaerunt, et Dominus se offert benevolum; et describitur hoc ordine: primo ponitur *gentilium sollicitudo;* secundo, *discipulorum intercessio;* tertio, *Magistri condescensio.*

29. (Vers. 20.). Innuitur ergo *gentilium sollicitudo* in hoc, quod videre Iesum cupiunt et quaerunt; propter quod dicit: *Erant autem gentiles quidam ex his qui ascenderant, ut adorarent in die festo.* Ipsi adorabant in templo, quoniam et ipsi exaudiebantur; tertii Regum octavo[^p416-9] in oratione Salomonis: « Alienigena, qui non est de populo tuo, si venerit et oraverit in loco hoc, exaudies in caelo » etc.

30. (Vers. 21.). *Hi ergo accesserunt ad Philippum, qui erat a Bethsaida Galilaeae;* supra primo[^p416-10]: « Erat autem Philippus a Bethsaida, civitate Andreae et Petri ». Tanquam ad magis notum iverunt et desiderium expresserunt; unde *et rogabant eum, dicentes: Domine, volumus Iesum videre.* Nec mirum, si desiderabant videre, quia, secundum illud Psalmi[^p416-11], « speciosus forma prae filiis hominum », « totus erat desiderabilis ». Augustinus: « Ecce, voluerunt eum Iudaei occidere, gentiles videre ». Propter hoc Matthaei vigesimo primo: « Auferetur a vobis regnum et dabitur genti facienti fructus eius ».

31. (Vers. 22.). *Venit Philippus.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *discipulorum intercessio* ad gentilium petitionem. Et ut Philippus facilius impetret, assumit socium; propter quod dicit: *Venit Philippus et dicit Andreae,* tanquam maiori; et tunc Andreas tanquam maior[^p416-12] praeit in nuntiatione; unde dicit: *Andreas rursum et Philippus dixerunt Iesu.* In quo innuitur, quod praelati sunt medii inter simplices et Dominum, qui desideria pauperum Domino offerunt; Deuteronomii quinto[^p416-13] dixit Moyses: « Ego
<!-- page 417 -->
sequester et medius fui inter Deum et vos »; ad Hebraeos quinto: « Omnis pontifex ex hominibus assumtus pro hominibus constituitur in his quae sunt ad Deum ».

32. (Vers. 23.). *Iesus autem respondit eis.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *Domini condescensio,* qua satisfacit gentilium desiderio, *dicens,* iam tempus venisse manifestandi se gentibus. Propter quod dicit: *Venit hora, ut clarificetur Filius hominis,* id est, gentibus manifestetur, et per hoc eius claritas et gloria cognoscatur. Haec est hora passionis et redemptionis nostrae, quae diu fuit exspectata et desiderata. In hac gentes vocantur; propter quod dicit Apostolus ad Romanos decimo tertio[^p417-1]: « Hora est, iam nos de somno surgere ». Haec est hora, de qua dictum est supra septimo: « Nemo apprehendit eum, quia nondum venerat hora eius ».

### Quaestiones.

33. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur: cum gentiles sua idola adorarent et deos suos haberent, unde est, quod ascendebant adorare in Ierusalem?

Et dicendum, quod de gentibus convertebantur ad ritum Iudaeorum, et hi dicebantur *proselyti;* et Chrysostomus[^p417-2] dicit de istis, quod « prope existentes erant, ut proselyti fierent; ideo, iam incipientes credere, veniebant solemnitatem videre et in templo adorare ».

34. Quaest. II. Sed tunc quaeritur: quare Philippus non repulit eos? — Et videtur, quod debuisset, quia ipse audierat a Domino Matthaei decimo[^p417-3]: *In viam gentium ne abieritis;* et Matthaei decimo quinto: *Non sum missus nisi ad oves, quae perierunt domus Israel:* ergo videtur, quod contra Domini mandatum fecerit, quando ipsorum petitionem admisit.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod ipse[^p417-4] Philippus viderat, quod ipse non tantum Iudaeos, sed etiam gentiles curabat, et videbat, quod omnium salutem volebat; ex alia parte mandatum eius considerabat; ideo caute egit, quia nec petitionem omnino repulit nec prorsus admisit; et ideo Andream consuluit. Illi vero, de Domini misericordia praesumentes, dixerunt Iesu, quia se poterat, sicut volebat, ut Dominus, manifestare omnibus[^p417-5].

35. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur: quare nunc magis quam ante manifestat se gentibus?

Et dicendum, quod propter propinquitatem passionis, in qua salus erat gentium. — Alia ratio: quia iam Iudaei per conspirationem mortis Christi repulerant gratiam Dei; et ideo post conspirationem eorum Dominus convertitur ad gentes[^p417-6].

## English
<!-- page 416 -->

#### Second, on the prediction of the passion, four things are set down.

28. *Now there were certain Gentiles.* One preamble to the passion has been determined beforehand, which is the conspiracy of the Pharisees; here is determined the second, namely, *the illumination of the Gentiles*[^p416-7], which is a fruit following upon the passion of Christ, exceedingly useful. And since in the passion of Christ there came about the illumination of the Gentiles and the blinding of the Jews; therefore this part has four portions. In the first is determined or shown *the prefiguration of the calling of the Gentiles.* Second, the *fructification* of the *passion* is foretold, there[^p416-8]: *Amen, amen I say to you* etc. Third, *the exposition of the foretold fruit,* there: *The multitude therefore that stood and heard the voice.* Fourth is described *the future blinding of the Jews,* there: *These things Jesus spoke* etc.

There is intimated, then, first *the future calling of the Gentiles* in this, that they seek the Lord, and the Lord offers himself benevolent; and it is described in this order: first is set down *the solicitude of the Gentiles;* second, *the intercession of the disciples;* third, *the condescension of the Teacher.*

29. (V. 20.). There is hinted, then, *the solicitude of the Gentiles* in this, that they long and seek to see Jesus; on account of which he says: *Now there were certain Gentiles among those that had come up, to adore on the festival day.* They adored in the temple, since they too were heard; the third of Kings, the eighth[^p416-9], in the prayer of Solomon: « The stranger, who is not of thy people, if he shall come and shall pray in this place, thou shalt hear in heaven » etc.

30. (V. 21.). *These therefore came to Philip, who was of Bethsaida of Galilee;* above, the first[^p416-10]: « Now Philip was of Bethsaida, the city of Andrew and Peter ». They went as to one better known and expressed their desire; whence *and they asked him, saying: Sir, we would see Jesus.* Nor is it a wonder if they desired to see, because, according to that of the Psalm[^p416-11], « beautiful in form above the sons of men », « he was all desirable ». Augustine: « Behold, the Jews wished to kill him, the Gentiles to see him ». On account of this, Matthew, the twenty-first: « The kingdom shall be taken from you, and shall be given to a nation yielding the fruits thereof ».

31. (V. 22.). *Philip cometh.* Here is touched the second, namely, *the intercession of the disciples* at the petition of the Gentiles. And that Philip might more easily obtain, he takes on a companion; on account of which he says: *Philip cometh, and telleth Andrew,* as the greater; and then Andrew, as the greater[^p416-12], goes before in the announcing; whence he says: *Again Andrew and Philip told Jesus.* In which it is hinted that prelates are intermediaries between the simple and the Lord, who offer the desires of the poor to the Lord; Deuteronomy, the fifth[^p416-13], Moses said: « I was
<!-- page 417 -->
the mediator and intermediary between God and you »; to the Hebrews, the fifth: « Every high priest taken from among men, is ordained for men in the things that appertain to God ».

32. (V. 23.). *But Jesus answered them.* Here is touched the third, namely, *the condescension of the Lord,* by which he satisfies the desire of the Gentiles, *saying* that now the time had come of manifesting himself to the Gentiles. On account of which he says: *The hour is come, that the Son of man should be glorified,* that is, should be manifested to the Gentiles, and through this his clarity and glory should be known. This is the hour of the passion and of our redemption, which was long awaited and desired. In this the Gentiles are called; on account of which the Apostle says to the Romans, the thirteenth[^p417-1]: « It is the hour, now, for us to rise from sleep ». This is the hour of which it was said above, the seventh: « No man laid hands on him, because his hour was not yet come ».

### Questions.

33. Quaest. I. But it is asked: since the Gentiles adored their idols and had their own gods, whence is it that they went up to adore in Jerusalem?

And it must be said that from the Gentiles some were converted to the rite of the Jews, and these were called *proselytes;* and Chrysostom[^p417-2] says of these that « they were close by, that they might become proselytes; therefore, now beginning to believe, they came to see the solemnity and to adore in the temple ».

34. Quaest. II. But then it is asked: why did Philip not repel them? — And it seems that he should have, because he himself had heard from the Lord, Matthew, the tenth[^p417-3]: *Go ye not into the way of the Gentiles;* and Matthew, the fifteenth: *I was not sent but to the sheep that are lost of the house of Israel:* therefore it seems that he acted against the Lord's commandment, when he admitted their petition.

**I respond:** It must be said that Philip himself[^p417-4] had seen that he cared not only for the Jews, but also for the Gentiles, and he saw that he willed the salvation of all; on the other hand he considered his commandment; therefore he acted cautiously, because he neither wholly repelled the petition nor utterly admitted it; and therefore he consulted Andrew. But they, presuming on the Lord's mercy, told Jesus, because he could, as he willed, as Lord, manifest himself to all[^p417-5].

35. Quaest. III. Again, it is asked: why does he manifest himself to the Gentiles now rather than before?

And it must be said that on account of the nearness of the passion, in which was the salvation of the Gentiles. — Another reason: because now the Jews through the conspiracy for the death of Christ had repelled the grace of God; and therefore after their conspiracy the Lord turns to the Gentiles[^p417-6].
