# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XIII, Vers. 21-30
## *Postquam exemplum propositum est, et inducti sunt discipuli ad imitandum; hic incipit tertia pars capituli, in qua Dominus perversitatem Iudae ostendit ad deviandum; et hoc quidem facit hoc ordine*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 429 -->

28. *Cum haec dixisset, turbatus est.* Postquam exemplum propositum est, et inducti sunt discipuli ad imitandum; hic incipit tertia pars capituli[^p429-9], in qua Dominus *perversitatem Iudae* ostendit ad deviandum; et hoc quidem facit hoc ordine. Primo fit *in generali proditoris insinuatio;* secundo vero, *discipulorum haesitans inquisitio;* tertio, *Domini secreta responsio;* quarto vero, *proditoris acceleratio.*

(Vers. 21.). Primo igitur fit *in generali proditoris insinuatio,* et licet in generali, non tamen in dubio, sed in certitudine. Et hoc manifestat Christi turbatio; propter quod dicit: *Cum haec dixisset Iesus,* quae scilicet spectabant ad discipulorum instructionem; *turbatus est spiritu,* id est voluntate[^p429-10]; *et protestatus est et dixit,* quia certissime asseruit: *Amen, amen dico vobis, quia unus ex vobis me tradet;* ille scilicet unus erat eum traditurus, de quo supra sexto: « Unus ex vobis diabolus est ».

29. (Vers. 22.). *Aspiciebant ergo ad invicem.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *discipulorum inquisitio;* et primo coeperunt *dubitare;* propter quod dicit: *Aspiciebant ergo ad invicem discipuli, haesitantes, de quo diceret;* ideo ad invicem aspiciebant, quia unus de altero dubitabat. Augustinus[^p429-11]: « Sic in eis erga Magistrum suum erat pia caritas, ut tamen inter eos humana alterum de altero stimularet infirmitas ». Et quia dubitatio ducit in inquisitionem, et inquisitio requirit familiaritatem; ideo ostenditur familiaritas Ioannis, ut per ipsum *inquisitio fiat.* Ideo ait:

30. (Vers. 23.). *Erat ergo unus ex discipulis eius recumbens in sinu Iesu,* tanquam familiarissimus inter alios, et hoc quidem propter amoris praerogativam; *quem diligebat Iesus.* Iste discipulus fuit
<!-- page 430 -->
Ioannes; unde Chrysostomus[^p430-1]: « Intuere Ioannis ingloriationem; non enim dicit nomen suum, sed *quem amabat Iesus,* sicut Apostolus secundae ad Corinthios duodecimo: *Scio hominem* », non dixit: Scio Paulum. Hic ergo discipulus sic familiaris idoneus erat inquisitor; propter quod dicit:

31. (Vers. 24.). *Innuit ergo huic Simon Petrus et dicit*[^p430-2] *ei: Quis est, de quo dicit?* Quasi dicat: require ab eo. Petrus semper per se et per alios sollicitus erat quaerere. Ad Petri petitionem inquirit ille discipulus:

32. (Vers. 25.). *Itaque cum recubuisset ille supra pectus Iesu,* ut dilectus et familiaris et propinquus, quod est signum dilectionis; Canticorum ultimo[^p430-3]: « Quae est ista, quae ascendit de deserto, deliciis affluens, innixa super dilectum suum »? *Dicit ei: Domine, quis est?* Supple: scilicet qui te tradet. Supra pectus Iesu recubuit, ut secretum audiret, quia in eo erant omnes « thesauri sapientiae et scientiae absconditi[^p430-4] ». Unde Augustinus dicit de Ioanne, quod « fluenta Evangelii de ipso dominici pectoris fonte potavit ».

33. (Vers. 26.). *Respondit* ei *Iesus.* Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *Domini secreta responsio,* qua discipulo familiari satisfacit; propter quod non verbo dixit, sed ipso signo manifestavit; propterea dicit: *Ille est, cui*[^p430-5] *intinctum panem porrexero;* intinctio panis fictionem Iudae significat. *Et cum intinxisset panem, dedit Iudae Simonis Iscariotae,* et in hoc scivit Ioannes, quis esset eum traditurus. Augustinus[^p430-6]: « Panis proditori traditus demonstratio est gratiae, cui proditor fuit ingratus ». Unde in Psalmo de ingratitudine Iudae conqueritur: « Si inimicus meus maledixisset mihi, sustinuissem utique; tu vero, homo unanimis, dux meus et notus meus, qui simul mecum dulces capiebas cibos » etc.

34. (Vers. 27.). *Et post buccellam.* Tangitur hic quartum, scilicet *proditoris acceleratio,* quae facta est per vehementem infestationem diaboli, quam habuit in Iudam post ipsius correctionem. Propter quod dicit: *Et post buccellam introivit in eum satanas,* ut efficacius possideret et nequitiam conceptam citius consummaret; unde in Psalmo[^p430-7]: « Constitue super eum peccatorem, et diabolus stet a dextris eius ». *Et,* quia hoc non latebat Dominum, *dixit ei Iesus: Quod facis fac citius,* praedictive dicit, non imperative[^p430-8]. Ne tamen videretur eius nequitiam detexisse, ostendit Evangelista, quod hoc verbum omnes latuit; ideo dicit:

35. (Vers. 28.). *Hoc autem nemo scivit discumbentium, quid*[^p430-9] *ei dixerit,* nec etiam ipse Ioannes, qui iam sciebat eius nequitiam. Unde Chrysostomus: « Benignae mentis et mansuetae non fuit malum de alio suspicari », immo e contrario in bonum interpretati sunt; unde dicit:

36. (Vers. 29.). *Quidam enim putabant, quia loculos habebat Iudas,* tanquam dispensator, *quod dixisset ei Iesus: Eme ea quae opus sunt*[^p430-10] *ad diem festum, aut ut egenis aliquid daret,* quia maxime propter illos habebat loculos; in quo significatum est, quod de loculis Ecclesiae licet praelatis emere, *quae opus,* id est, quae necessaria sunt, et reliqua dare egenis; et ipsis specialiter datum est mandatum de alendis pauperibus; unde Ecclesiastici vigesimo nono[^p430-11]: « Propter mandatum assume pauperem, et propter inopiam ne dimittas eum vacuum ». Et sicut diabolus suggerebat, et Christus praedixerat, acceleravit Iudas; ideo dicit:

<!-- page 431 -->
37. (Vers. 30.). *Cum ergo accepisset*[^p431-1] *buccellam, exivit continuo,* ut velociter impleret. Et tempus erat congruum facto; *erat autem nox;* Iob vigesimo quarto: « Oculus adulteri observat caliginem »; et supra tertio: « Qui male agit odit lucem ».

### Quaestiones.

38. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de Domini perturbatione, quia dicit: *Turbatus est spiritu.* Si in Christo omnia erant pacata, quia per arcam Noe significatur, in qua omnia pacata erant[^p431-2]; quomodo spiritu turbatus est?

Et dicendum, quod illa turbatio nullam in Christo faciebat inordinationem, quoniam voluntati erat subiecta sensualitas et voluntarie turbata; in cuius testimonium dicit: *Turbatus est spiritu*[^p431-3].

39. Quaest. II. Sed tunc quaeritur, de quo turbatur sensualitas? — Si de morte futura, tunc hoc nihil est; quia illa non ceciderat in sensu[^p431-4], cum esset futura, ergo nec cadebat in sensualitate: ergo de hoc sensualitas non dolebat.

Dicendum, quod mortis imaginatio erat in vi sensibili interiori, non a sensu procedens, sed a rationis cognitione descendens. Cum enim aliqua praescimus, possumus imaginari, et per hoc quod certum tenemus, perturbari.

Et si obiicias, quod est ordo praeposterus; dicunt aliqui, quod nihil impediebat in Christo, in quo inferiores vires erant subiectae superiori. — Sed tamen non oportet dicere hoc; quia tunc esset ordo praeposterus, si species intelligibilis descenderet in sensum et fieret sensibilis; sed quando species sunt in imaginatione, et ratio aliquid cogitat et facit, sensitivam illud idem imaginari et exterreri, nulla est praeposteratio[^p431-5].

40. Quaest. III. Sed tunc quaeritur: cum Christus ab initio cognoverit mortem suam, sicut et tunc; quare non semper turbatus fuit, sicut et tunc?

Dicendum ad hoc, quod cognitio mortis non erat tota causa, sed cognitio mortis *imminentis;* unde magis abhorret natura periculum imminens quam distans. Nec adhuc illud tota causa, sed cognitio mortis imminentis *cum voluntate turbandi se;* et hoc voluit tunc, ut ostenderet, se esse verum hominem[^p431-6].

41. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de inquisitione discipulorum:

1. Unde est, quod ausi fuerunt discipuli quaerere de crimine occulto? — Videtur fuisse curiositas.

2. Et etiam quaeritur: cum Petrus esset audacior inter omnes, quare non per se quaesivit, sed « per interpositam personam[^p431-7] », scilicet Ioannis? Unde quasi semper Petrus pro discipulis omnibus respondebat.

Ad hoc dicendum, quod non quaerebant propter curiositatem sciendi culpam proximi, sed quia unusquisque, ut dicit Chrysostomus[^p431-8], de se timebat, et timore illo cruciabantur; volebant certificari. Et quia sciebant, quia Dominus palam non diceret, nec ipsi volebant eum ita palam confundere; occulte quaesierunt, et occulte eis responsum est[^p431-9].

2. Et ideo Petrus non est locutus, sed innuit illi qui propinquus erat et occulte quaerere poterat; nam recumbebat super pectus Iesu secrete et poterat quaerere, non sic Petrus.

42. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur de responsione Domini: quia dicit: *Cui intinctum panem porrexero.* — Videtur, quod Dominus Iudam prodiderit.

Ad hoc dicit Chrysostomus[^p431-10], quod omnibus dedit panem intinctum.

Sed tunc 1. quaeritur, quod signum fuisset Ioanni, quod ille esset, *cui intinctum panem porrexero.*

<!-- page 432 -->
2. Item, dicitur Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p432-1], quod *coeperunt singuli dicere: Nunquid ego sum, Domine?* et post de Iuda: *Nunquid ego sum, Rabbi?* Et dicitur ibi, quod Dominus respondit: *Tu dixisti.* Ergo videtur, quod Dominus eum aperte manifestaverit, et quod non tantum signo, sed verbo.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Dominus proditorem revelavit, sed non omnibus, immo aliquibus, sed occulte. Revelavit autem duplici ex causa Ioanni: tum quia compatiebatur eius desolationi[^p432-2], tum etiam, ut ipse esset testis, Christum praecognovisse certitudinaliter, non per coniecturas, ipsum discipulum proditorem.

Quod vero dicitur, quod non est peccator prodendus; intelligendum est, quod non est publicandus, sed tamen dici potest his qui possunt prodesse et nolunt obesse[^p432-3].

2. Quod vero dicitur de Matthaeo, dicendum, quod illud verbum Domini, ut dicit Augustinus de Concordia Evangelistarum[^p432-4], nihil certum determinabat: *Tu dixisti,* quasi dicat: non ego, sed tu. — Vel potest esse, quod Dominus ita ei dixit, quod nullus alius intelligeret. — Et hoc quidem, quod narrat Matthaeus, prius dictum fuit; et quia non erant Petrus et Ioannes certificati, adhuc secreto petiit Ioannes.

43. Quaest. VI. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod *post buccellam introivit in eum satanas.*

1. Videtur, quod Dominus non bene fecerit, quod buccellam dedit Iudae, per quam peior est effectus.

2. Item, qua ratione *post buccellam?*

3. Et videtur falsum dicere, quia, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p432-5], solus Deus intrat in animam, non ergo satanas.

4. Item, prius[^p432-6] dixerat, quod diabolus misit in cor Iudae; quomodo ergo *post buccellam?*

**Respondeo:** Ad haec sunt diversae opiniones. Quidam voluerunt dicere, quod panis ille intinctus fuit corpus Christi; et hoc dicit Magister in Historiis[^p432-7], et ideo non datur nobis communicantibus panis intinctus. Et hoc videtur dicere Augustinus in originali[^p432-8]: « Quid miraris, inquit, si datus est Iudae panis Christi, per quem manciparetur diabolo, cum videas Apostolo datum angelum diaboli, per quem proficeret in Christo »? Et ideo dicunt, quod tunc intravit satanas, quia indigne corpus Christi suscipiens, iudicium sibi manducavit et bibit.

Alii dicunt aliter, et credo, quod melius, et hoc dicit Glossa[^p432-9], quod panis ille intinctus non fuit corpus Christi; immo, sicut dicit Augustinus de Concordia Evangelistarum, iam corpus Christi discipulis erat distributum, et Iudas cum aliis sumserat; sed
<!-- page 433 -->
alius panis fuit, et post illum satanas intravit; quia tot modis excitatus est poenitere et non poenituit, ideo diabolus eum plenius possedit.

3. Quod obiicitur, quod Deus intrat in animam solus; verum est *illabendo,* sed diabolus *suggerendo*[^p433-1].

4. Quod obiicitur, quod prius suggesserat; dicendum, quod verum est; sed ex tunc eum amplius angustavit et ad malitiam complendam acrius impulit.

## English
<!-- page 429 -->

28. *When he had said these things, he was troubled.* After the example has been set forth, and the disciples have been led to imitate; here begins the third part of the chapter[^p429-9], in which the Lord shows *the perversity of Judas* in going astray; and he does this in this order. First there is made *in general the intimation of the traitor;* second, *the hesitating inquiry of the disciples;* third, *the secret response of the Lord;* fourth, *the hastening of the traitor.*

(V. 21.). First, then, there is made *in general the intimation of the traitor,* and though in general, yet not in doubt, but in certitude. And this the troubling of Christ manifests; on which account he says: *When Jesus had said these things,* which, namely, looked to the instruction of the disciples; *he was troubled in spirit,* that is, in will[^p429-10]; *and he testified and said,* because he asserted it most certainly: *Amen, amen I say to you, that one of you shall betray me;* that one, namely, was to betray him, of whom above, in the sixth: « One of you is a devil ».

29. (V. 22.). *They looked therefore one upon another.* Here is touched the second, namely *the inquiry of the disciples;* and first they began *to doubt;* on which account he says: *The disciples therefore looked one upon another, hesitating of whom he spoke;* therefore they looked one upon another, because one doubted of another. Augustine[^p429-11]: « So in them toward their Teacher was there pious charity, that yet among them human infirmity spurred on one concerning another ». And because doubt leads to inquiry, and inquiry requires familiarity; therefore the familiarity of John is shown, that through him *the inquiry might be made.* Therefore he says:

30. (V. 23.). *There was therefore one of his disciples leaning on the bosom of Jesus,* as the most familiar among the others, and this on account of the prerogative of love; *whom Jesus loved.* That disciple was
<!-- page 430 -->
John; whence Chrysostom[^p430-1]: « Behold the not-glorying of John; for he does not say his name, but *whom Jesus loved,* as the Apostle, the second to the Corinthians, the twelfth chapter: *I know a man* », he did not say: I know Paul. This disciple, then, being so familiar, was a fit inquirer; on which account he says:

31. (V. 24.). *Simon Peter therefore beckoned to him, and says*[^p430-2] *to him: Who is it of whom he speaketh?* As if he said: ask of him. Peter was always solicitous to ask, by himself and through others. At Peter's petition that disciple inquires:

32. (V. 25.). *Therefore when he had leaned upon the breast of Jesus,* as beloved and familiar and near, which is a sign of dilection; Canticles, the last chapter[^p430-3]: « Who is this that cometh up from the desert, flowing with delights, leaning upon her beloved »? *He saith to him: Lord, who is it?* Supply: namely he who will betray you. He leaned upon the breast of Jesus, that he might hear the secret, because in him were all « the treasures of wisdom and knowledge hidden[^p430-4] ». Whence Augustine says of John that « he drank the streams of the Gospel from the very fountain of the Lord's breast ».

33. (V. 26.). *Jesus answered* him. Here is touched the third, namely *the secret response of the Lord,* by which he satisfies the familiar disciple; on which account he did not say it by a word, but manifested it by the sign itself; therefore he says: *He it is to whom*[^p430-5] *I shall reach bread dipped;* the dipping of the bread signifies the feigning of Judas. *And when he had dipped the bread, he gave it to Judas Iscariot, the son of Simon,* and in this John knew who was to betray him. Augustine[^p430-6]: « The bread given to the traitor is a demonstration of the grace to which the traitor was ungrateful ». Whence in the Psalm he complains of the ingratitude of Judas: « For if my enemy had reviled me, I would verily have borne with it; but thou a man of one mind, my guide, and my familiar, who didst take sweetmeats together with me » etc.

34. (V. 27.). *And after the morsel.* Here is touched the fourth, namely *the hastening of the traitor,* which was brought about through the vehement infestation of the devil, which he had against Judas after his correction. On which account he says: *And after the morsel, Satan entered into him,* that he might possess him more efficaciously and more quickly consummate the wickedness conceived; whence in the Psalm[^p430-7]: « Set thou the sinner over him, and may the devil stand at his right hand ». *And,* because this was not hidden from the Lord, *Jesus said to him: That which thou dost, do quickly,* he says it predictively, not imperatively[^p430-8]. Yet lest he should seem to have disclosed his wickedness, the Evangelist shows that this word was hidden from all; therefore he says:

35. (V. 28.). *Now no man at the table knew what*[^p430-9] *he said to him,* nor even John himself, who already knew his wickedness. Whence Chrysostom: « It was not of a benign and gentle mind to suspect evil of another », rather on the contrary they interpreted it in a good sense; whence he says:

36. (V. 29.). *For some thought, because Judas had the purse,* as dispenser, *that Jesus had said to him: Buy those things which are needed*[^p430-10] *for the festival day: or that he should give something to the poor,* because he had the purse chiefly on their account; in which it is signified that from the purse of the Church it is lawful for prelates to buy *what is needed,* that is, the things that are necessary, and to give the rest to the poor; and to them specially has been given the commandment about feeding the poor; whence Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-ninth[^p430-11]: « Because of the commandment help the poor, and because of his poverty send him not away empty handed ». And as the devil was suggesting, and Christ had foretold, Judas hastened; therefore he says:

<!-- page 431 -->
37. (V. 30.). *When therefore he had received*[^p431-1] *the morsel, he went out immediately,* that he might swiftly fulfil it. And the time was fitting for the deed; *and it was night;* Job, the twenty-fourth: « The eye of the adulterer observeth darkness »; and above, in the third: « He that doth evil hateth the light ».

### Questions.

38. Quaest. I. But it is asked here first about the perturbation of the Lord, because he says: *He was troubled in spirit.* If in Christ all things were at peace, because he is signified by the ark of Noe, in which all things were at peace[^p431-2]; how was he troubled in spirit?

And it must be said that that troubling caused no inordination in Christ, since sensuality was subject to the will and voluntarily troubled; in testimony of which he says: *He was troubled in spirit*[^p431-3].

39. Quaest. II. But then it is asked, about what is sensuality troubled? — If about future death, then this is nothing; because it had not fallen in sense[^p431-4], since it was future, therefore neither did it fall in sensuality: therefore sensuality did not grieve about this.

It must be said that the imagination of death was in the interior sensible power, not proceeding from sense, but descending from the cognition of reason. For when we foreknow certain things, we can imagine them, and through that which we hold as certain be perturbed.

And if you object that this is a reversed order; some say that nothing prevented it in Christ, in whom the lower powers were subject to the higher. — But yet it is not necessary to say this; because the order would then be reversed, if an intelligible species should descend into sense and become sensible; but when the species are in the imagination, and reason thinks something and makes the sensitive power imagine that same thing and be terrified, there is no reversal of order[^p431-5].

40. Quaest. III. But then it is asked: since Christ knew his death from the beginning, just as also then; why was he not always troubled, just as also then?

It must be said to this that the cognition of death was not the whole cause, but the cognition of death *imminent;* whence nature abhors an imminent danger more than a distant one. Nor yet is that the whole cause, but the cognition of imminent death *with the will to trouble himself;* and this he willed then, that he might show himself to be true man[^p431-6].

41. Quaest. IV. Again, it is asked concerning the inquiry of the disciples:

1. How is it that the disciples dared to ask about a hidden crime? — It seems to have been curiosity.

2. And it is also asked: since Peter was bolder among all, why did he not ask by himself, but « through an interposed person[^p431-7] », namely John? Whence Peter almost always responded for all the disciples.

To this it must be said that they were not asking out of curiosity to know the fault of a neighbour, but because each one, as Chrysostom[^p431-8] says, feared for himself, and they were tormented by that fear; they wished to be made certain. And because they knew that the Lord would not say it openly, nor did they themselves wish to confound him so openly; they asked secretly, and secretly answer was made to them[^p431-9].

2. And therefore Peter did not speak, but beckoned to him who was near and could ask secretly; for he was leaning upon the breast of Jesus in secret and could ask, not so Peter.

42. Quaest. V. Again, it is asked concerning the response of the Lord: because he says: *To whom I shall reach bread dipped.* — It seems that the Lord betrayed Judas.

To this Chrysostom[^p431-10] says that he gave dipped bread to all.

But then 1. it is asked what sign it would have been to John that he was the one *to whom I shall reach bread dipped.*

<!-- page 432 -->
2. Again, it is said in Matthew, the twenty-sixth[^p432-1], that *they began every one to say: Is it I, Lord?* and after, of Judas: *Is it I, Rabbi?* And it is said there that the Lord answered: *Thou hast said it.* Therefore it seems that the Lord openly manifested him, and not only by a sign, but by a word.

**I respond:** It must be said that the Lord revealed the traitor, but not to all, rather to some, but secretly. And he revealed him to John for a twofold cause: both because he had compassion on his desolation[^p432-2], and also that he might be a witness that Christ foreknew with certitude, not by conjectures, that his own disciple was the traitor.

But as for what is said, that a sinner is not to be betrayed; it is to be understood that he is not to be made public, but yet he can be told to those who can profit and do not will to harm[^p432-3].

2. But as for what is said about Matthew, it must be said that that word of the Lord, as Augustine says, *On the Agreement of the Evangelists*[^p432-4], determined nothing certain: *Thou hast said it,* as if he said: not I, but you. — Or it may be that the Lord so said it to him that no one else understood. — And this indeed, which Matthew narrates, was said earlier; and because Peter and John were not made certain, John still asked in secret.

43. Quaest. VI. Again, it is asked concerning what he says, that *after the morsel Satan entered into him.*

1. It seems that the Lord did not do well in giving the morsel to Judas, through which he was made worse.

2. Again, by what reason *after the morsel?*

3. And he seems to say what is false, because, as Augustine[^p432-5] says, God alone enters into the soul, therefore not Satan.

4. Again, he had said earlier[^p432-6] that the devil had put it into the heart of Judas; how then *after the morsel?*

**I respond:** On these there are diverse opinions. Some have wished to say that that dipped bread was the body of Christ; and this the Master says in the Histories[^p432-7], and therefore dipped bread is not given to us communicating. And Augustine seems to say this in the original[^p432-8]: « Why do you wonder, he says, if there was given to Judas the bread of Christ, by which he was handed over to the devil, when you see that there was given to the Apostle an angel of the devil, by which he might advance in Christ »? And therefore they say that Satan entered then, because, receiving the body of Christ unworthily, he ate and drank judgement to himself.

Others say otherwise, and I believe better, and this the Gloss[^p432-9] says, that that dipped bread was not the body of Christ; rather, as Augustine says, *On the Agreement of the Evangelists,* the body of Christ had already been distributed to the disciples, and Judas had received it with the others; but
<!-- page 433 -->
it was other bread, and after it Satan entered; because he was stirred in so many ways to repent and did not repent, therefore the devil possessed him more fully.

3. As for what is objected, that God alone enters into the soul; it is true *by gliding in,* but the devil *by suggesting*[^p433-1].

4. As for what is objected, that he had suggested earlier; it must be said that this is true; but from then on he constrained him more and drove him more sharply to complete the malice.
