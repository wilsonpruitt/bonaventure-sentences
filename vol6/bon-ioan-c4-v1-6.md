# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. IV, Vers. 1-6
## *Tertia manifestatio, quae facta est Samaritanis*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 289 -->

### Capitulum IV.

#### Tertia manifestatio, quae facta est Samaritanis.

1. *Ut ergo cognovit Iesus* etc. Manifestavit se Dominus supra discipulis et Iudaeis; in hac parte manifestat se *Samaritanis,* et hoc praedicando et annuntiando eis verbum salutis. Et habet pars ista duas partes. In prima notatur *praedicandi opportunitas;* in secunda, *praedicationis forma,* ibi[^p289-8]: *Venit mulier de Samaria,* ubi Dominus Samaritanae incipit praedicare. — Opportunitas autem erat ex parte *loci,* et haec quidem fuit duplex, scilicet ad *transeundum* et ad *quiescendum.*

2. (Vers. 1.). Tangitur ergo primo opportunitas loci *ad transeundum.* Cum enim vellet ire a Iudaea in Galilaeam, necesse habebat transire per Samariam. Propterea dicit: *Ut ergo cognovit Iesus, quia audierant*[^p289-9] *Pharisaei.* Audierant, inquam, a discipulis Ioannis, qui tanquam invidi et detractores referebant; supra tertio: « Dixerunt: Omnes vadunt ad illum »; audierant, inquam, *quod Iesus plures discipulos facit et baptizat quam Ioannes.* Et ex hoc manifestum erat eis, quod ipse erat maior et celebrior Ioanne[^p289-10], et iam eis manifestatus. Hoc audierunt, quia sic relatum fuerat.

3. (Vers. 2.). *Quamquam Iesus non baptizaret, sed discipuli eius,* ne provocarentur ad invidiam.

4. (Vers. 3.). *Reliquit Iudaeam et abiit iterum in Galilaeam;* quia semel abierat, supra primo[^p289-11]. Chrysostomus: « Recessit Dominus non timoris gratia, sed abscindens eorum livorem atque invidiam »; unde infra septimo: « Ambulabat Iesus in Galilaeam; non enim volebat in Iudaeam ire, quia quaerebant eum Iudaei interficere ». Sic facta praedicatione Iudaeis, ostendit se opportunitas praedicandi Samaritanis; unde dicit:

5. (Vers. 4.). *Oportebat autem, eum transire per mediam*[^p289-12] *Samariam.* Et sic aderat loci
<!-- page 290 -->
opportunitas ad transeundum; Lucae decimo septimo: « Dum iret Iesus in Ierusalem, transibat per mediam Samariam ». Sicut transibat, sic redire oportebat.

6. (Vers. 5.). *Venit ergo.* Tangitur hic opportunitas loci *ad quiescendum,* quia lassus erat, et locus amoenus se offerebat; unde locum describit a civitate, cum dicit: *Venit ergo in civitatem Samariae, quae dicitur Sichar; Sichar* in lingua Chaldaica, *Sichem* in Hebraea[^p290-1]. Haec est civitas, ubi Simeon et Levi occiderunt viros in ultionem stupri sororis, Genesis trigesimo quarto[^p290-2]. Et haec civitas erat, supple: *iuxta praedium, quod dedit Iacob Ioseph, filio suo;* Genesis quadragesimo octavo: « Do tibi partem unam extra fratres tuos, quam tuli de manu Amorrhaei in gladio et arcu meo ». Et hic locus opportunus erat ad *quiescendum,* quia amoenus. Propterea dicit:

7. (Vers. 6.). *Erat autem ibi fons Iacob,* id est, quem Iacob invenerat, secundum quod dicitur infra eodem[^p290-3]. Et Christus indigebat quiete; ideo dicit: *Iesus, fatigatus ex itinere, sedebat sic supra fontem;* quia locus frigidus erat, et calor iam erat. Ideo subdit: *Hora autem*[^p290-4] *erat quasi sexta;* et ideo fatigatus erat longo itinere, ex ieiunio et calore. Unde ipse Dominus dicit in Psalmo: « Pauper sum ego et in laboribus a iuventute mea; exaltatus autem, humiliatus sum et conturbatus ».

8. *Allegorice. Iesus,* relicta Iudaea, *transit per mediam Samariam in Galilaeam;* quia, Iudaeis excaecatis, Christus migravit ad Patrem; *Galilaea* enim transmigratio interpretatur[^p290-5]; ad Romanos undecimo: *Caecitas contigit in Israel, ut plenitudo gentium intraret;* et Actuum decimo tertio: *Dixerunt Paulus et Barnabas* Iudaeis: *Vobis oportebat primum loqui verbum; sed quoniam repulistis illud et indignos vos iudicastis aeternae vitae; ecce, convertimur ad gentes.* — *Iesus, fatigatus ex itinere, sedet hora sexta;* quia Filius Dei carnem nostram cum poenalitatibus assumsit sexta aetate, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p290-6]; Ieremiae decimo quarto: *Quare sicut colonus futurus es in terra et sicut viator declinans ad manendum?* — Super puteum *aquae sedet;* quia super fluenta gratiae requiescit, ut effundat in se credentibus; infra septimo[^p290-7]: *Qui credit in me, sicut dicit Scriptura, flumina de ventre eius fluent aquae vivae.*

9. Notandum, quod *Christus* dicitur *sedere supra fontem;* quia ipse est, in quo requiescit fons omnis gratiae; unde recte per *fontem illum* intelligitur; Genesis secundo[^p290-8]: *Fons ascendebat de terra et irrigabat universam superficiem terrae.* Iste est Christus, a quo procedit quadruplex flumen sive aqua, scilicet aqua *munditiae.* De hac aqua Ezechiel trigesimo sexto[^p290-9]: *Effundam super vos aquam mundam* etc.; et sic Christus dicitur *fons munditiae;* iste est baptismus; Zachariae decimo tertio: *Erit fons patens domui David et habitatoribus Ierusalem in ablutionem peccatoris et menstruatae.* Baptismus enim mundat peccatorem et menstruatam, quia delet peccatum originale et actuale. — Aqua *sapientiae;* Ecclesiastici decimo quinto[^p290-10]: *Aqua sapientiae salutaris potavit illum;* et haec est sacra doctrina. Et sic Christus dicitur *fons sapientiae* Ecclesiastici primo: *Fons sapientiae, verbum Dei in excelsis.* Iste irrigat corda doctorum; Proverbiorum quinto: *Deriventur fontes tui foras* etc. — Aqua *gratiae;* de qua infra septimo[^p290-11]: *Qui credit in me, flumina de ventre eius fluent aquae vivae.* Et sic dicitur Christus *fons gratiae;* infra eodem: *Qui biberit ex aqua, quam ego dabo ei, fiet in eo fons aquae salientis in vitam aeternam;* haec est gratia, Ieremiae secundo. — Aqua *vitae,* de qua infra eodem[^p290-12]: *Qui biberit ex aqua, quam ego dabo ei, non sitiet in aeternum.* Et sic dicitur Christus *fons vitae;* Psalmus: *Apud te est fons vitae, et in lumine tuo videbimus lumen;* et Apocalypsis septimo: *Agnus deducet eos ad vitae fontes aquarum.*

Omnes aquae istorum fontium procedunt a Christo; unde Isaiae duodecimo[^p290-13]: *Haurietis aquas in gaudio de fontibus Salvatoris;* sed non procedunt nisi in humiles et valles; Psalmus: *Qui emittis fontes in convallibus,* id est in humilibus.

<!-- page 291 -->
10. Nota, quod *diabolus* sedet super fontem *immunditiae,* de quo Levitici vigesimo[^p291-1]: *Qui coierit cum muliere in fluxu menstruo et revelaverit turpitudinem eius, ipsaque aperuerit fontem sanguinis sui, interficientur ambo de medio populi sui.* Mulier *menstrua* et *immunda* est caro nostra; cum illa coit qui eius desideriis satisfacit. — *Imitationis perversae;* Proverbiorum vigesimo quinto[^p291-2]: *Fons turbatus pede et vena corrupta, iustus cadens coram impio.* Impius enim, dum considerat in iusto mala et bona, relinquens bona, imitatur mala. — *Curiositatis vanae;* Ecclesiastici vigesimo sexto[^p291-3] dicitur de anima curiosa: *Sicut viator sitiens ad omnem fontem os aperiet et ex omni aqua bibet;* quia curiosi totum volunt scire et de omni sapientia tentare. — *Damnationis aeternae;* Apocalypsis octavo[^p291-4]: *Stella magna ardens cecidit in fontes aquarum, et nomen stellae dicitur absinthium;* quia diabolus de caelo empyreo incidit in summam calamitatem poenarum, ad quas trahit omnes suos potatores.

### Quaestiones.

11. Quaest. I. Ad intelligentiam litterae primo quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Ut cognovit Iesus.* — Aut hoc dicit secundum *divinitatem,* aut secundum *humanitatem;* quocumque modo hoc dicat, videtur falsum, quia Christus omnia scivit a nativitate: ergo post nihil cognoscere incepit.

Respondetur ad hoc, quod sicut Christus dicitur *ignorare,* quia non facit nos scire, sicut de die iudicii[^p291-5]; sic dicitur aliquid *primo cognoscere,* quia cognoscere se primo ostendit. Unde hoc prius *novit,* sed tamen tunc primo *ostendit.* — Vel respondendum, quod Christus prius sciebat *praescientia,* sed postmodum scivit *relatione:* et hoc non erat inconveniens secundum humanam naturam[^p291-6].

12. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Dominus reliquit Iudaeam* propter Pharisaeos iam cognoscentes. — Videtur, quod timuerit et quod fugerit.

Ad hoc respondet Chrysostomus[^p291-7], quod non timore, sed propter *scandalum* eorum vitandum et zelum *invidiae.*

Sed tunc *obiicitur:* 1. Quia « veritas doctrinae non est dimittenda propter *scandalum*[^p291-8] »; unde non dimisit eis dicere veritatem propter scandalum, in multis locis, ut Matthaei decimo quinto; quare nunc propter scandalum dimittit?

2. Item, si propter *invidiam;* quaero: quare magis invidebant Christo quam Ioanni, cum Ioannes similiter baptizaret?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod triplici ex causa reliquit Iudaeam, scilicet propter *scandalum vitandum,* propter *fructum augendum* et propter *exemplum dandum.* Propter *scandalum,* ne Iudaei efficerentur per zeli accensionem peiores; sed haec non fuit tota causa, immo etiam propter *fructum,* quia ibi parum profecisset et alibi multum profecit; et tunc relinquenda est praedicatio propter scandalum, quando alibi plus fructificari potest. Propter *exemplum* etiam dimisit, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p291-9], ut ostendat nobis, esse licitum persequentium furorem declinare.

2. Quod quaerit: quare magis persequebantur Christum quam Ioannem? responsio est, quia Ioannes non baptizabat in nomine suo, sed in nomine Christi venturi, Christus vero in nomine suo; unde docebat et baptizabat sicut potestatem habens[^p291-10]. Quoniam igitur Pharisaei invidi nullum volebant habere superiorem, ideo magis invidebant.

13. Quaest. III. Item quaerit Augustinus[^p291-11]: Quomodo dicit Evangelista: *Fons Iacob,* cum infra dicatur *puteus?*

Respondet Augustinus[^p291-12], quod « omnis *puteus* est *fons,* sed non convertitur. Ubi enim emanat aqua de terra in superficie, est tantum *fons;* ubi in profundo, et *fons* et *puteus* dicitur ».

## English
<!-- page 289 -->

### Chapter IV.

#### The third manifestation, which was made to the Samaritans.

1. *When Jesus therefore knew* etc. Above, the Lord manifested himself to the disciples and to the Jews; in this part he manifests himself *to the Samaritans,* and this by preaching and announcing to them the word of salvation. And this part has two parts. In the first there is noted *the opportunity for preaching;* in the second, *the form of the preaching,* at the place[^p289-8]: *There cometh a woman of Samaria,* where the Lord begins to preach to the Samaritan woman. — Now the opportunity was on the part of *the place,* and this indeed was twofold, namely for *passing through* and for *resting.*

2. (Verse 1.). There is touched on, then, first the opportunity of the place *for passing through.* For since he wished to go from Judea into Galilee, he had need to pass through Samaria. On account of this he says: *When Jesus therefore knew that the Pharisees had heard*[^p289-9]. They had heard, I say, from the disciples of John, who, as envious men and detractors, were reporting it; above, the third chapter: « They said: All go to him »; they had heard, I say, *that Jesus maketh more disciples and baptizeth more than John.* And from this it was manifest to them that he was greater and more renowned than John[^p289-10], and already manifested to them. This they heard, because it had been so reported.

3. (Verse 2.). *Though Jesus did not baptize, but his disciples,* lest they should be provoked to envy.

4. (Verse 3.). *He left Judea, and went again into Galilee;* because he had gone away once, above, the first chapter[^p289-11]. Chrysostom: « The Lord withdrew not by reason of fear, but cutting off their spite and envy »; whence below, the seventh chapter: « Jesus walked into Galilee; for he would not go into Judea, because the Jews sought to kill him ». The preaching to the Jews being thus done, the opportunity for preaching to the Samaritans shows itself; whence he says:

5. (Verse 4.). *And he was of necessity to pass through the midst*[^p289-12] *of Samaria.* And so the opportunity of the place
<!-- page 290 -->
for passing through was at hand; Luke, the seventeenth chapter: « While Jesus was going to Jerusalem, he passed through the midst of Samaria ». As he passed through, so it behoved him to return.

6. (Verse 5.). *He cometh therefore.* Here there is touched on the opportunity of the place *for resting,* because he was weary, and a pleasant place offered itself; whence he describes the place by the city, when he says: *He cometh therefore to a city of Samaria, which is called Sichar; Sichar* in the Chaldaic tongue, *Sichem* in the Hebrew[^p290-1]. This is the city where Simeon and Levi slew the men in vengeance for the defilement of their sister, Genesis, the thirty-fourth chapter[^p290-2]. And this city was, supply: *near the land which Jacob gave to Joseph, his son;* Genesis, the forty-eighth chapter: « I give thee one portion above thy brethren, which I took out of the hand of the Amorrhite with my sword and bow ». And this place was opportune for *resting,* because it was pleasant. On account of this he says:

7. (Verse 6.). *Now the fountain of Jacob was there,* that is, the one which Jacob had found, according to what is said below in the same chapter[^p290-3]. And Christ was in need of rest; therefore he says: *Jesus, being wearied with his journey, sat thus upon the fountain;* because the place was cool, and the heat was already come. Therefore he adds: *Now it was*[^p290-4] *about the sixth hour;* and therefore he was wearied by the long journey, from fasting and from the heat. Whence the Lord himself says in the Psalm: « I am poor, and in labours from my youth; and being exalted, I have been humbled and troubled ».

8. *Allegorically. Jesus,* Judea being left, *passes through the midst of Samaria into Galilee;* because, the Jews being blinded, Christ passed over to the Father; for *Galilee* is interpreted "a passing over"[^p290-5]; to the Romans, the eleventh chapter: *Blindness hath happened in Israel, that the fulness of the Gentiles might come in;* and Acts, the thirteenth chapter: *Paul and Barnabas said* to the Jews: *To you it behoved us first to speak the word; but because you have rejected it and have judged yourselves unworthy of eternal life, behold, we turn to the Gentiles.* — *Jesus, being wearied with his journey, sits at the sixth hour;* because the Son of God assumed our flesh with its penalties in the sixth age, as Augustine says[^p290-6]; Jeremias, the fourteenth chapter: *Why wilt thou be as a sojourner in the land, and as a wayfaring man turning in to abide?* — Upon the well *of water he sits;* because he rests upon the streams of grace, that he may pour them out upon those who believe in him; below, the seventh chapter[^p290-7]: *He that believeth in me, as the Scripture saith, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water.*

9. It is to be noted that *Christ* is said *to sit upon the fountain;* because he is the one in whom the fountain of all grace rests; whence that *fountain* is rightly understood as him; Genesis, the second chapter[^p290-8]: *A fountain rose out of the earth and watered all the surface of the earth.* This is Christ, from whom proceeds a fourfold river or water, namely the water *of cleanness.* Of this water Ezechiel, the thirty-sixth chapter[^p290-9]: *I will pour upon you clean water* etc.; and so Christ is called *the fountain of cleanness;* this is baptism; Zacharias, the thirteenth chapter: *There shall be a fountain open to the house of David and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem, for the washing of the sinner and of the menstruous woman.* For baptism cleanseth the sinner and the menstruous woman, because it blots out original and actual sin. — The water *of wisdom;* Ecclesiasticus, the fifteenth chapter[^p290-10]: *With the water of wholesome wisdom she hath given him drink;* and this is sacred doctrine[^tr-doctrina]. And so Christ is called *the fountain of wisdom* Ecclesiasticus, the first chapter: *The fountain of wisdom, the word of God on high.* He waters the hearts of the doctors; Proverbs, the fifth chapter: *Let thy fountains be conveyed abroad* etc. — The water *of grace;* of which below, the seventh chapter[^p290-11]: *He that believeth in me, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water.* And so Christ is called *the fountain of grace;* below in the same chapter: *He that shall drink of the water that I will give him, it shall become in him a fountain of water springing up into life everlasting;* this is grace, Jeremias, the second chapter. — The water *of life,* of which below in the same chapter[^p290-12]: *He that shall drink of the water that I will give him, shall not thirst for ever.* And so Christ is called *the fountain of life;* the Psalm: *With thee is the fountain of life, and in thy light we shall see light*[^tr-lumen]; and the Apocalypse, the seventh chapter: *The Lamb shall lead them to the fountains of the waters of life.*

All the waters of these fountains proceed from Christ; whence Isaias, the twelfth chapter[^p290-13]: *You shall draw waters with joy out of the Saviour's fountains;* but they do not proceed except into the humble and the valleys; the Psalm: *Thou sendest forth fountains in the vales,* that is, in the humble.

<!-- page 291 -->
10. Note that *the devil* sits upon the fountain *of uncleanness,* of which Leviticus, the twentieth chapter[^p291-1]: *He that shall lie with a woman in her menstrual flow and uncover her nakedness, and she open the fountain of her blood, both shall be destroyed out of the midst of their people.* The *menstruous* and *unclean* woman is our flesh; he lies with her who satisfies her desires. — *Of perverse imitation;* Proverbs, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p291-2]: *A fountain troubled with the foot and a corrupted spring, a just man falling down before the wicked.* For the wicked man, while he considers in the just man evils and goods, leaving the goods, imitates the evils. — *Of vain curiosity;* Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p291-3], it is said of the curious soul: *As a thirsty traveller will open his mouth to every fountain, and will drink of every water;* because the curious wish to know everything and to try every wisdom. — *Of eternal damnation;* the Apocalypse, the eighth chapter[^p291-4]: *A great star burning fell upon the fountains of waters, and the name of the star is called Wormwood;* because the devil fell from the empyrean heaven into the uttermost calamity of punishments, to which he draws all his drinkers.

### Questions.

11. Question I. For the understanding of the letter, it is asked first about this that he says: *When Jesus knew.* — Either he says this according to *the divinity,* or according to *the humanity;* in whichever way he says this, it seems false, because Christ knew all things from his birth: therefore afterwards he began to know nothing.

It is answered to this that, just as Christ is said *not to know,* because he does not make us know, as of the day of judgement[^p291-5]; so he is said *first to know* something, because he first shows himself to know it. Whence he *knew* this before, but yet only then first *showed* it. — Or it must be answered that Christ knew before *by foreknowledge,* but afterwards knew *by report:* and this was not unfitting according to human nature[^p291-6].

12. Question II. Likewise it is asked about this that he says: *The Lord left Judea* on account of the Pharisees, who were already aware. — It seems that he was afraid and that he fled.

To this Chrysostom answers[^p291-7] that it was not from fear, but on account of avoiding their *scandal* and the zeal *of envy.*

But then *it is objected:* 1. Because « the truth of doctrine is not to be given up on account of *scandal*[^p291-8] »; whence he did not give up telling them the truth on account of scandal, in many places, as in Matthew, the fifteenth chapter; why now does he give it up on account of scandal?

2. Likewise, if on account of *envy;* I ask: why did they envy Christ more than John, since John baptized likewise?

**I respond:** It must be said that he left Judea from a threefold cause, namely on account of *avoiding scandal,* on account of *increasing the fruit* and on account of *giving an example.* On account of *scandal,* lest the Jews should be made worse through the kindling of zeal; but this was not the whole cause, but also on account of *the fruit,* because he would have profited little there and profited much elsewhere; and preaching is to be left off on account of scandal at that time, when more fruit can be borne elsewhere. He also gave it up on account of *example,* as Augustine says[^p291-9], that he might show us that it is lawful to turn aside from the fury of persecutors.

2. As to what is asked: why did they persecute Christ more than John? the answer is, because John did not baptize in his own name, but in the name of Christ who was to come, but Christ in his own name; whence he taught and baptized as one having power[^p291-10]. Since, then, the envious Pharisees wished to have no one superior, therefore they envied him more.

13. Question III. Likewise Augustine asks[^p291-11]: How does the Evangelist say: *The fountain of Jacob,* when below it is called *a well?*

Augustine answers[^p291-12] that « every *well* is a *fountain,* but not conversely. For where water flows out of the earth on the surface, it is only a *fountain;* where it is deep, it is called both a *fountain* and a *well* ».
