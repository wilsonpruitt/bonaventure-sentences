# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. VIII, Vers. 31-36
## *Ostensum est, quod sine doctrina et fide Christi est in peccato perpetuatio; ostenditur hic secundo, quod per doctrinam Christi est a servitute peccati liberatio*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 363 -->

45. *Dicebat ergo Iesus* etc. Ostensum est, quod sine doctrina et fide Christi est in peccato perpetuatio; ostenditur hic secundo, quod per doctrinam Christi est *a servitute peccati liberatio.* Et dividitur haec pars in tres partes: quia primo a servitute peccati per doctrinam Christi *promittitur liberatio;* secundo *Iudaeorum evacuatur gloriatio,* ibi[^p363-3]: *Scio, quia filii Abrahae estis;* tertio ostenditur *Iudaeorum consequens indignatio,* ibi: *Responderunt Iudaei et dixerunt ei: Nonne bene dicimus nos?*

Primo ergo per doctrinam Christi promittitur et ostenditur *liberatio fieri* hoc ordine: primo *liberatio promittitur;* secundo *promissa a Iudaeis parvipenditur;* tertio *eis necessaria ostenditur.*

46. (Vers. 31.). *Promittitur* ergo *liberatio* his qui in doctrina Christi permanent; propterea dicit: *Dicebat ergo Iesus ad eos qui crediderunt ei*[^p363-4]; et quia comminatione territi crediderunt, promissione alliciuntur, ut perseverent; unde dicit: *Si vos permanseritis in sermone meo;* infra decimo quinto[^p363-5]: « Manete in me, et ego in vobis. Sicut palmes non potest ferre fructum a semetipso, nisi manserit in vite; sic nec vos, nisi in me manseritis »; non sicut illi, de quibus dicitur Lucae octavo: « Ad tempus credunt et in tempore tentationis recedunt »; hi non reportant fructum; sed vos, si permanseritis, *vere discipuli mei eritis.*

47. (Vers. 32.). *Et vos*[^p363-6] *cognoscetis veritatem, et veritas liberabit vos.* Tria dicit, quae faciunt hominem rectum. Primum est *rectitudo affectus,* et hoc notatur in discipulatu Christi; secundum, *rectitudo intellectus,* et hoc in cognitione veritatis; tertium, *rectitudo effectus* per liberationem a culpa, et hoc notatur in liberatione veritatis. Vel: *discipuli eritis* in via per imitationem; *veritatem cognoscetis* in animae beatificatione; et *veritate liberabimini ab omni corruptione* in animae et corporis felici unione; ad Romanos octavo[^p363-7]: « Ipsa creatura liberabitur a servitute corruptionis in libertatem gloriae filiorum Dei ».

48. (Vers. 33.). *Responderunt ei.* Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet quomodo *liberatio promissa a Iudaeis parvipenditur,* quia ipsi se liberos reputabant; unde gloriantur de *libertate* et non curant de *liberatione.* Propterea dicunt ei: *Semen Abrahae sumus et nemini servivimus unquam;* et ita[^p363-8] liberi sumus per naturam; *quomodo tu dicis: Liberi eritis,* quasi modo non simus liberi? Ideo dicebant se liberos, quia nati de Abraham per Isaac, qui filius fuit liberae, secundum quod dicitur ad Galatas quarto[^p363-9]: « Eiice ancillam et filium eius »; dictum est de Agar; « non enim heres erit filius ancillae cum filio liberae », et tractum est de Genesis vigesimo primo.

49. (Vers. 34.). *Respondit eis Iesus.* Tangitur hic tertium, quia *necessitas liberationis ostenditur;* et ostendit eos *servos,* et quod habent opus *liberatione* et *quomodo liberantur.* — Quod ergo sint *servi* spiritualiter, ostendit tali ratione: *Qui facit peccatum servus est peccati;* sed vos facitis peccatum: ergo vos estis servi peccati. *Minorem* huius rationis tacet, quia infra[^p363-10] eam exprimit, et *conclusionem* tacet; sed proponit *maiorem* dicens: *Amen, amen dico vobis: Qui*[^p363-11] *facit peccatum servus est peccati.* Hoc probat beatus Petrus in secundae Canonicae secundo: « A quo quis superatus est, huius et servus est ». Haec est pessima servitus; unde
<!-- page 364 -->
Augustinus[^p364-1]: « O miserabilis servitus! Servus hominis aliquando, sui domini duris imperiis fatigatus, fugiendo requiescit; servus vero peccati quo fugit? Secum trahit malam conscientiam, quocumque eat; quia, quocumque vadit, a se non recedit ». — Sic patet, eos esse servos; et ex hoc probat, quod *indigent liberatione,* tali ratione: qui non manet in domo in aeternum indiget liberari ab eo qui manet, et per eum potest liberari; sed servus non manet, Filius autem manet: ergo servus per Filium indiget et potest liberari. Sed vos estis servi, ut supra[^p364-2] dictum est: « si ergo vos Filius liberaverit, vere liberi eritis ». Primae rationis ponit *minorem* propositionem, cum dicit:

50. (Vers. 35.). *Servus non manet in domo in aeternum, Filius*[^p364-3] *manet in aeternum.* Augustinus exponit: Qui est servus peccati non manet in domo in aeternum, id est finaliter, quamvis possit esse de praesenti Ecclesia quantum ad numerum. Tales servi, etsi sint in domo cum bonis, tamen non habent vestem nuptialem; ideo eiiciuntur, secundum illud Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p364-4]: « Amice, quomodo huc intrasti, non habens vestem nuptialem »? Et post subditur: « Proiicite eum in tenebras exteriores ».

51. (Vers. 36.). *Si ergo Filius vos liberaverit, vere liberi eritis,* ille, inquam, Filius solus est liber, quia solus sine peccato; unde in Psalmo[^p364-5]: « Factus sum sicut homo sine adiutorio, inter mortuos liber ». Et ratio redditur infra decimo quarto: « Venit princeps mundi huius et in me non habet quidquam ». Iste Filius liber, ut nos liberaret, factus est pro nobis quasi servus; ad Philippenses secundo: « Humiliavit semetipsum, formam servi accipiens » etc. Hic igitur nos *liberavit redimendo;* Isaiae quinquagesimo secundo[^p364-6]: « Gratis venundati estis, et sine argento redimemini »; quia, sicut dicitur primae Petri primo, « non corruptibilibus auro vel argento redempti estis, sed pretioso sanguine quasi Agni immaculati et incontaminati, Christi Iesu ». Non, inquam, ut iterum simus servi, sed liberi; unde ad Galatas quinto: « Vos, inquit, fratres, in libertatem vocati estis, tantum ne libertatem in occasionem detis carnis, sed per caritatem spiritus servite invicem ».

### Quaestiones.

52. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic de hoc quod dicunt: *Semen Abrahae sumus et nemini servivimus unquam.*

**Contra:** 1. Servierunt Aegyptiis; unde Deuteronomii sexto[^p364-7]: *Eduxit vos Dominus de domo servitutis.*

2. Item, servierunt Nabuchodonosor in captivitate Babylonica per septuaginta annos[^p364-8]. — *Si dicas,* quod ipsi loquebantur de se, non de patribus; *obiicitur:* quia etiam tunc tributum reddebant Romanis; unde quaesierunt Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p364-9]: *Licetne tributum dare Caesari, an non?*

Ad hoc est una responsio, quod ipsi mentiti sunt, sicut saepe solebant, et beneficiorum Dei obliti erant; unde Augustinus[^p364-10]: « O pellis inflata! non est ista magnitudo, sed tumor. Quomodo verum dixistis? Ioseph venundatus est, Prophetae in captivitatem ducti, populus in Aegypto in luto et latere serviebat, vos ipsi etiam tributa solvebatis ».

Aliter respondetur, quod illud intellexerunt quantum ad morem, quem habebant inter se praecepto Legis; Levitici vigesimo quinto[^p364-11]: *Servus et ancilla sint vobis de nationibus, quae in circuitu vestro sunt.*

Vel aliter dicendum, quod est *servitus conditionis per originem,* sicut filius servi est servus, et sic intelligebant, et isti dicuntur *servi de corpore;* et est *servitus oppressionis per dominium*[^p364-12], et de hac non intelligebant.

53. Quaest. II. Sed quaeritur: cum peccator faciat omne, quod vult, iustus autem non; quomodo peccator dicitur *servus?*

Dicendum, quod peccator recte dicitur *servus peccati,* quia magna servitus est *sequi malam voluntatem;* et propter *conditiones serviles,* quae in peccatore reperiuntur, propter quas *servus* recte dicitur. — Servus enim *vilis est,* sic et peccator;
<!-- page 365 -->
tertii Regum primo[^p365-1]: *Erimus ego et filius meus Salomon peccatores,* id est viles. — *Vilibus vescitur,* sic peccator; Lucae decimo quinto, filius prodigus *implere cupiebat ventrem de siliquis porcorum.* — *Vilia operatur,* sic et peccator; Exodi primo[^p365-2]: *Operibus duris luti et lateris omnique famulatu premebantur.* — *Viliter tractatur,* sic peccator; Ieremiae decimo sexto: *Servietis diis alienis, qui non dabunt vobis requiem.* — *Alienus est a lucro;* Osee octavo[^p365-3]: *Culmus stans non est in eo, germen non faciet farinam; quodsi fecerit, alieni comedent eam.* — *Alienus a Domini sui secreto,* sic peccator; infra decimo quinto: *Iam non dicam vos servos, sed amicos, quia omnia, quaecumque audivi a Patre meo, nota feci vobis.* Peccatoribus non revelat, quia sunt in tenebris. — *Alienus ab honoris familiaritate;* ad Romanos octavo[^p365-4]: *Non accepistis spiritum servitutis iterum in timore.* — *Alienus ab hereditate,* sic peccator; Iob vigesimo septimo: *Dives, cum dormierit, nihil secum afferet;* ad Galatas quarto: *Non erit heres filius ancillae cum filio liberae*[^p365-5].

## English
<!-- page 363 -->

45. *Jesus therefore said* etc. It was shown that without the teaching[^tr-doctrina] and faith of Christ there is a perpetuation in sin; here it is shown, second, that through the teaching of Christ there is *a liberation from the servitude of sin.* And this part is divided into three parts: because first, through the teaching of Christ, *liberation* from the servitude of sin *is promised;* second, *the glorying of the Jews is made void,* there[^p363-3]: *I know that you are the children of Abraham;* third is shown *the consequent indignation of the Jews,* there: *The Jews answered and said to him: Do not we say well?*

First, then, it is promised and shown through the teaching of Christ that *liberation takes place,* in this order: first *liberation is promised;* second *the promised liberation is made light of by the Jews;* third *it is shown to be necessary for them.*

46. (V. 31.). *Liberation,* then, *is promised* to those who abide in the teaching of Christ; therefore he says: *Jesus therefore said to those who believed him*[^p363-4]; and because, terrified by the threat, they believed, they are enticed by the promise, that they may persevere; whence he says: *If you continue in my word;* below, in the fifteenth chapter[^p363-5]: « Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, unless it abide in the vine; so neither can you, unless you abide in me »; not as those of whom it is said in Luke, the eighth chapter: « For a while they believe, and in time of temptation they fall away »; these bring back no fruit; but you, if you continue, *shall be my disciples indeed.*

47. (V. 32.). *And you*[^p363-6] *shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free.* He says three things which make a man right. The first is *rightness of affection,* and this is noted in the discipleship of Christ; the second, *rightness of understanding,* and this in the knowledge of the truth; the third, *rightness of effect* through liberation from guilt, and this is noted in the liberation of the truth. Or: *you shall be disciples* on the way through imitation; *you shall know the truth* in the beatification of the soul; and *by the truth you shall be freed from all corruption* in the happy union of soul and body; to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p363-7]: « The creature itself shall be freed from the servitude of corruption into the liberty of the glory of the children of God ».

48. (V. 33.). *They answered him.* Here the second is touched, namely how *the promised liberation is made light of by the Jews,* because they reckoned themselves free; whence they glory in *liberty* and do not care about *liberation.* Therefore they say to him: *We are the seed of Abraham, and we have never served any man;* and so[^p363-8] we are free by nature; *how sayest thou: You shall be free,* as if now we were not free? They said they were free for this reason, because they were born of Abraham through Isaac, who was the son of the free woman, according to what is said to the Galatians, the fourth chapter[^p363-9]: « Cast out the bondwoman and her son »; it was said of Agar; « for the son of the bondwoman shall not be heir with the son of the free woman », and it is drawn from Genesis, the twenty-first chapter.

49. (V. 34.). *Jesus answered them.* Here the third is touched, that *the necessity of liberation is shown;* and he shows that they are *servants,* and that they have need of *liberation,* and *how they are freed.* — That they are, then, *servants* spiritually, he shows by such a reasoning: *He that committeth sin is the servant of sin;* but you commit sin: therefore you are servants of sin. *The minor* of this reasoning he is silent about, because below[^p363-10] he expresses it, and he is silent about *the conclusion;* but he sets forth *the major,* saying: *Amen, amen I say to you: He that*[^p363-11] *committeth sin is the servant of sin.* This blessed Peter proves in the second chapter of the second Canonical Epistle: « By whom a man is overcome, of him also he is the servant ». This is the worst servitude; whence
<!-- page 364 -->
Augustine[^p364-1]: « O miserable servitude! The servant of a man sometimes, wearied by the hard commands of his master, finds rest by fleeing; but the servant of sin, whither does he flee? He drags an evil conscience with him, wherever he may go; because, wherever he goes, he does not withdraw from himself ». — Thus it is plain that they are servants; and from this he proves that *they need liberation,* by such a reasoning: he who does not abide in the house for ever needs to be freed by him who abides, and can be freed through him; but the servant does not abide, while the Son abides: therefore the servant needs to be, and can be, freed through the Son. But you are servants, as was said above[^p364-2]: « if therefore the Son shall make you free, you shall be free indeed ». Of the first reasoning he sets down *the minor* proposition, when he says:

50. (V. 35.). *The servant abideth not in the house for ever, the Son*[^p364-3] *abideth for ever.* Augustine expounds: He who is the servant of sin does not abide in the house for ever, that is, finally, although he can be of the present Church as to number. Such servants, even if they are in the house with the good, yet do not have the wedding garment; therefore they are cast out, according to that of Matthew, the twenty-second chapter[^p364-4]: « Friend, how camest thou in hither, not having a wedding garment »? And afterwards it is subjoined: « Cast him into the exterior darkness ».

51. (V. 36.). *If therefore the Son shall make you free, you shall be free indeed,* that Son, I say, alone is free, because alone without sin; whence in the Psalm[^p364-5]: « I am become as a man without help, free among the dead ». And the reason is given below, in the fourteenth chapter: « The prince of this world cometh, and in me he hath not any thing ». This free Son, that he might free us, was made for us as a servant; to the Philippians, the second chapter: « He humbled himself, taking the form of a servant » etc. He, then, *freed us by redeeming;* Isaias, the fifty-second chapter[^p364-6]: « You were sold gratis, and you shall be redeemed without money »; because, as is said in the first chapter of the first of Peter, « not with corruptible things, gold or silver, were you redeemed, but with the precious blood as of a Lamb unspotted and undefiled, Christ Jesus ». Not, I say, that we should be servants again, but free; whence to the Galatians, the fifth chapter: « You, he says, brethren, have been called unto liberty; only give not liberty for an occasion to the flesh, but by charity of the spirit serve one another ».

### Questions.

52. Quaest. I. But here it is asked concerning this which they say: *We are the seed of Abraham, and we have never served any man.*

**On the contrary:** 1. They served the Egyptians; whence Deuteronomy, the sixth chapter[^p364-7]: *The Lord brought you out of the house of servitude.*

2. Likewise, they served Nabuchodonosor in the Babylonian captivity for seventy years[^p364-8]. — *If you say* that they were speaking of themselves, not of their fathers; *it is objected:* because even then they were paying tribute to the Romans; whence they asked, in Matthew, the twenty-second chapter[^p364-9]: *Is it lawful to give tribute to Caesar, or not?*

To this there is one response, that they lied, as they were often wont, and had forgotten the benefits of God; whence Augustine[^p364-10]: « O puffed-up skin! this is not greatness, but a swelling. How have you spoken the truth? Joseph was sold, the Prophets were led into captivity, the people served in Egypt in clay and brick, you yourselves even paid tributes ».

Otherwise it is answered that they understood that as to the custom which they had among themselves by the precept of the Law; Leviticus, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p364-11]: *Let the servant and the bondwoman be yours of the nations that are round about you.*

Or otherwise it must be said that there is a *servitude of condition by origin,* as the son of a servant is a servant, and so they understood it, and these are called *servants as to the body;* and there is a *servitude of oppression by dominion*[^p364-12], and of this they did not understand it.

53. Quaest. II. But it is asked: since the sinner does all that he wills, but the just man does not; how is the sinner called a *servant?*

It must be said that the sinner is rightly called *the servant of sin,* because it is a great servitude *to follow an evil will;* and because of the *servile conditions* which are found in the sinner, on account of which he is rightly called a *servant.* — For the servant *is vile,* and so too the sinner;
<!-- page 365 -->
the third of Kings, the first chapter[^p365-1]: *I and my son Solomon shall be sinners,* that is, vile. — *He is fed on vile things,* so the sinner; Luke, the fifteenth chapter, the prodigal son *desired to fill his belly with the husks of the swine.* — *He works vile works,* and so too the sinner; Exodus, the first chapter[^p365-2]: *They were oppressed with hard works of clay and brick and with all manner of service.* — *He is vilely treated,* so the sinner; Jeremias, the sixteenth chapter: *You shall serve strange gods, who shall not give you rest.* — *He is a stranger to gain;* Osee, the eighth chapter[^p365-3]: *There is no standing stalk in it, the bud shall yield no meal; and if it should yield, strangers shall eat it.* — *A stranger to the secret of his lord,* so the sinner; below, in the fifteenth chapter: *I will not now call you servants, but friends, because all things whatsoever I have heard of my Father, I have made known to you.* To sinners he does not reveal, because they are in darkness. — *A stranger to the familiarity of honour;* to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p365-4]: *You have not received the spirit of servitude again in fear.* — *A stranger to the inheritance,* so the sinner; Job, the twenty-seventh chapter: *The rich man, when he shall sleep, shall bring nothing with him;* to the Galatians, the fourth chapter: *The son of the bondwoman shall not be heir with the son of the free woman*[^p365-5].
