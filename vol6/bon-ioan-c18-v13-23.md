# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XVIII, Vers. 13-23
## *Secundo, de Christi condemnatione*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 481 -->

#### Secundo, de Christi condemnatione.

26. *Et adduxerunt eum ad Annam primum.* Hic incipit secunda pars principalis, in qua agit *de Christi condemnatione*[^p481-10]; et quia condemnationem eius praecessit multiplex examinatio, dividitur haec pars in tres. In prima agit de *examinatione facta ab Anna;* secundo, *a Caipha,* ibi[^p481-11]: *Et misit eum Annas ligatum;* tertio, *sub Pilato,* ibi: *Adducunt ergo Iesum ad Caipham.*

In prima tria notantur: *defectus iudicii, defectus testimonii, illatio convicii;* quae notantur in hoc, quod Christus primo *iudici iniquo praesentatur;* secundo, *a Petro negatur,* ibi[^p481-12]: *Sequebatur autem Iesum;* tertio, *examinatur,* ibi: *Pontifex ergo interrogavit Iesum.*

27. (Vers. 13.). Quantum ergo ad *praesentationem* dicitur: *Et adduxerunt eum,* scilicet Iesum, *ad Annam primum,* quia senior et antiquior et in malitia inveteratior; Danielis decimo tertio[^p481-13]: « Egressa
<!-- page 482 -->
est iniquitas a senioribus iudicibus, qui videbantur regere populum ». Et rationem reddit, cum dicit: *Erat autem socer Caiphae, qui erat pontifex anni illius;* ideo ad ipsum quodam modo pertinere videbatur iudicium. Sub ipsis principibus passus est Christus, sub quibus praedicavit Ioannes; Lucae tertio[^p482-1]: « Sub principibus sacerdotum Anna et Caipha factum est verbum Domini super Ioannem, Zachariae filium, in deserto ». Et notat eum de suspicione, quod non recte iudicaret, quia socer eius erat, qui conspiraverat in mortem Christi. Unde dicit:

28. (Vers. 14.). *Erat autem Caiphas, qui consilium dederat Iudaeis;* supra undecimo[^p482-2]: Expedit, unum hominem mori pro populo. Chrysostomus: « Ideo prophetiae illius meminit, quoniam salus orbis terrarum mors eius fuit, et ideo non debet auditor audiens Christi vincula tumultuari ».

29. (Vers. 15.). *Sequebatur autem Iesum.* Hic agit *de Petri negatione,* quam describit hoc ordine. Primo notatur *negandi occasio;* secundo, *ipsa negatio;* tertio, ex negatione *amoris refrigeratio.*

*Occasio negandi* fuit in hoc, quod cum esset infirmus, volebat Dominum sequi. Propterea dicit: *Sequebatur autem Iesum Simon Petrus,* non perfecte; unde Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p482-3]: « Petrus autem sequebatur *a longe* », et alio ducente; unde non solus erat, immo *et alius discipulus,* et hic erat ductor; unde: *Discipulus autem ille erat notus pontifici;* et ideo cum fiducia ibat, unde subdit: *Et introivit cum Iesu in atrium pontificis.* Quis iste fuerit, Glossa[^p482-4] non definivit. Videtur tamen fuisse Ioannes, ut dicit Victor, qui de se ut de alio loquebatur, quasi non timens.

30. (Vers. 16.). *Petrus autem stabat ad ostium foris,* vel quia non audebat intrare, vel quia non permittebatur. Ideo verum erat illud quod dictum est ei supra decimo tertio[^p482-5]: « Quo ego vado, non potes me sequi modo ». *Exivit ergo discipulus ille alius, qui erat notus pontifici,* et ratione familiaritatis *dixit ostiariae et introduxit Petrum.* Et sic manifestum est, quod tenuiter diligebat qui per alium introibat; in quo significatur, quod discipuli se debent mutuo iuvare; ideo ad Galatas ultimo[^p482-6]: « Alter alterius onera portate, et sic adimplebitis legem Christi ».

31. (Vers. 17.). *Dixit Petro ostiaria*[^p482-7]. Hic describitur secundum, scilicet *negatio ex infirmitate procedens;* et hoc patet in hoc, quod Petrus ad vocem ancillae Dominum negavit. Introducitur ergo inquisitio ancillae: *Nunquid et tu ex discipulis es hominis istius?* Non dicit *seductoris,* sed *hominis,* quasi commiserans loquitur; sed Petrus, pusillanimitate victus, negavit; unde sequitur: *Dicit ille: Non sum.* Chrysostomus[^p482-8]: « Quid est, Petre, nonne dixisti: Animam meam pro te ponam? — supra decimo tertio. — Quid ergo factum est, quod neque ostiariae fers interrogationem »?

32. (Vers. 18.). *Stabant autem servi.* Hic describitur tertium, scilicet ex *negatione affectus infrigidatio;* quod innuitur in hoc, quod Petrus, iam Domini oblitus, stabat ad prunas cum servis et rursus, calore interiori tepefacto, quaerebat solatium in exteriori; propterea dicit: *Stabant autem servi et ministri ad prunas,* qui scilicet ceperant Dominum; *quia frigus erat, et calefaciebant se.* Augustinus[^p482-9]: « Non hiems erat, et tamen frigus erat, quod solet
<!-- page 483 -->
etiam in aequinoctio verno aliquando contingere ». *Erat autem et Petrus stans et calefaciens se;* perdiderat enim illum ignem, de quo Lucae duodecimo[^p483-1]: « Ignem veni mittere in terram », quia tepuerat; Beda: « In tantum princeps Apostolorum frigore infidelitatis tepuit, ut ad vocem unius ancillae Christum formidaret confiteri ».

33. *Pontifex autem*[^p483-2] etc. Posita negatione Petri, hic subiungitur *examinatio Christi;* in cuius examinatione quatuor notantur, scilicet *pontificis interrogatio, Christi responsio, illatio iniuriae, supportatio patientiae.*

(Vers. 19.). *Pontificis* ergo *interrogatio* tangitur, cum dicitur: *Pontifex autem,* scilicet Annas, *interrogabat*[^p483-3] *Iesum de discipulis suis et doctrina eius,* ut, quia non poterat crimen obiicere, saltem caperet in sermone; non ut disceret, sed ut caperet, Matthaei vigesimo secundo.

34. (Vers. 20.). *Respondit ei Iesus:* Hic ponitur secundum, scilicet *responsio Christi,* in qua Dominus confutat intentionem pontificis. Habebat enim doctrinam Christi suspectam, ideo de ea vult eum examinare; propterea ostendit Dominus, doctrinam suam non esse suspectam, quia non fuit *abscondita,* sed publica. Propterea dicit: *Ego palam locutus sum mundo;* et hoc probat: *Ego semper docui in synagoga et in templo, quo omnes Iudaei conveniunt.* Et quia posset dicere: in publico docuisti vera, in abscondito docuisti falsa; ideo ad maiorem certitudinem dicit: *Et in abscondito*[^p483-4] *locutus sum nihil;* Isaiae quadragesimo quinto: « Ego Dominus, et non est alius. Non in abscondito locutus sum in loco tenebroso ». Sic amovit suspicionem de doctrina ratione *occultationis;* removet deinde ratione *falsitatis;* unde mittit ad alios, nolens ipse respondere; propterea dicit:

35. (Vers. 21.). *Quid me interrogas,* cum mihi non credas et ab aliis scire possis? *Interroga eos qui audierunt, quid locutus sum eis*[^p483-5], ut eis credas; quia, *ecce, hi sciunt, quae dixerim ego.* Chrysostomus: « Non audentis sunt verba, sed confidentis in eorum quae dicta erant veritate »; Proverbiorum octavo: « Recti sunt omnes sermones mei; non est in eis pravum quid neque perversum ».

36. (Vers. 22.). *Haec*[^p483-6] *cum dixisset.* Hic ponitur tertium, scilicet propter responsionem *illatio iniuriae* facto et verbo. *Facto: Unus assistens ministrorum dedit alapam Iesu;* Threnorum tertio[^p483-7]: « Dabit percutienti se maxillam »; et Michaeae quinto: « Percutient maxillam iudicis Israel ». Iniuriatus est *verbo: Dicens: Sic respondes pontifici?* Quasi dicat: stulte respondes, quia pontifici humiliter est respondendum; unde dixerunt Paulo Actuum vigesimo tertio: « Summum sacerdotem Dei maledicis »?

37. (Vers. 23.). *Respondit ei Iesus.* Hic notatur quartum, scilicet *supportatio patientiae,* quod innuitur in Domini responsione, quia malum servum *sustinet;* unde subdit: *Si male locutus sum, testimonium perhibe de malo;* quasi dicat: paratus sum sustinere poenam; *instruit;* unde dicit: *Si autem bene, cur*[^p483-8] *me caedis?* quasi: recognoscas, te male facere. In quo mira benignitas Christi notatur, quod non solum *sustinebat,* sed etiam *erudiebat,* secundum illud Levitici decimo nono[^p483-9]: « Ne oderis fratrem tuum, sed publice argue eum, ne habeas super illo peccatum ».

### Quaestiones.

38. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic primo de hoc quod dicitur Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p483-10], quod ministri tenentes Iesum *duxerunt ad Caipham principem sacerdotum;* quomodo Ioannes dicit, quod ad *Annam?*

2. Item, cum Annas non esset pontifex[^p483-11], quare ad ipsum ductus est?
<!-- page 484 -->

Respondet Augustinus[^p484-1], quod ad utrumque adductus est; sed Matthaeus, id brevius narrare volens, dicit, ipsum ad *Caipham* fuisse adductum, quia in domo eius maxime fuit examinatus.

2. Ad illud quod quaeritur, quare ad Annam primum adductus, quadruplex redditur ratio: ratio *dignitatis,* quia, sicut dicit Victor[^p484-2], praecedenti anno fuerat sacerdos; tunc enim emebatur summum sacerdotium. — Alia ratio, scilicet *consanguinitatis,* quia attinebat summo sacerdoti tanquam socer[^p484-3]. — Tertia ratio *opportunitatis,* quia domus eius erat in via. — Quarta ratio *malignitatis,* ut dicit Victor[^p484-4], « ut minoris culpae videretur Caiphas, si alterius sacerdotis Christus damnaretur sententia ».

39. Quaest. II. Item quaerit Chrysostomus[^p484-5]: quis fuit ille *alius discipulus,* de quo dicit, quod sequebatur Iesum?

Et respondet, quod Ioannes, qui nomen suum tacet ratione humilitatis.

40. Quaest. III. Et tunc est quaestio: quare non omnino tacet de se?

Responsio est, quod ideo se adducit, ut fidem nostram astruat; ideo nomen tacet, ut iactantiam vitare ostendat[^p484-6].

41. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur 1. de hoc quod Dominus dicit: *In occulto locutus sum nihil;* cum ipse Dominus locutus fuerit multa suis discipulis in secreto[^p484-7].

2. Item, de hoc quod dicit: *Semper docui in synagoga et in templo;* cum dicatur Matthaei quinto[^p484-8], quod in *monte;* similiter Lucae sexto, quod in *loco campestri.*

3. Item, de hoc quod dicit: *Ego palam locutus sum mundo;* cum Dominus in parabolis et in sententiis occultis loqueretur, sicut dicitur Matthaei decimo tertio[^p484-9].

1. Ad primum respondet Augustinus[^p484-10]: « Quoniam, si aliqua dixit solis discipulis, non tamen dixit in occulto, quia illa volebat per eos innotescere multis; unde Matthaei decimo: *Quod dico vobis in tenebris dicite in lumine, et quod in aure auditis praedicate super tecta.* — Chrysostomus[^p484-11] respondet: *Nihil locutus sum in occulto,* quia « non ex timore, vel dolo, sicut hi aestimabant. Solitarios discipulos docebat, sed ea quae alii capere non poterant ».

2. Ad aliud dicendum, quod *semper* distribuit ibi pro tribus solemnitatibus: Pascha, Pentecoste, Scenopegia, quando populus generaliter conveniebat[^p484-12].

3. Ad aliud dicendum, quod *palam locutus* est, non pro aperta intelligentia, sed pro multorum audientia. Augustinus[^p484-13]: « Modo quodam *palam* loquebatur, quia multi audiebant; modo quodam *non palam* loquebatur, quia non intelligebant ».

42. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur: cum Dominus Matthaei quinto[^p484-14] dicat: *Si quis te percusserit in unam maxillam, praebe ei et alterum;* quare Dominus non fecit, sed arguit percutientem se?

Respondet Augustinus[^p484-15]: « Veraciter, mansuete iusteque respondit; et non solum alteram maxillam
<!-- page 485 -->
iterum percussuro paravit, sed et totum corpus figendum in ligno. Et hinc ostendit, praecepta patientiae non ostentatione corporis, sed cordis praeparatione esse facienda ».

## English

#### Second, on the condemnation of Christ.

26. *And they led him away to Annas first.* Here begins the second principal part, in which he treats *of the condemnation of Christ*[^p481-10]; and because many an examination went before his condemnation, this part is divided in three. In the first he treats of *the examination made by Annas;* second, *by Caiphas,* there[^p481-11]: *And Annas sent him bound;* third, *under Pilate,* there: *Then they led Jesus to Caiphas.*

In the first three things are noted: *a failure of judgement, a failure of testimony, an infliction of insult;* which are noted in this, that Christ first *is presented to an unjust judge;* second, *is denied by Peter,* there[^p481-12]: *And there followed Jesus;* third, *is examined,* there: *The high priest therefore asked Jesus.*

27. (Vers. 13.). As to the *presentation,* then, it is said: *And they led him away,* namely Jesus, *to Annas first,* because he was the elder and the more ancient and the more inveterate in malice; Daniel, the thirteenth chapter[^p481-13]: « Iniquity came forth
<!-- page 482 -->
from the elder judges, who seemed to govern the people ». And he gives the reason when he says: *Now he was father-in-law to Caiphas, who was the high priest of that year;* therefore the judgement seemed in a certain manner to pertain to him. Christ suffered under the very princes under whom John preached; Luke, the third chapter[^p482-1]: « Under the princes of the priests Annas and Caiphas, the word of the Lord came upon John, the son of Zachary, in the desert ». And he marks him with the suspicion that he would not judge rightly, because he was the father-in-law of him who had conspired unto the death of Christ. Hence he says:

28. (Vers. 14.). *Now Caiphas was he who had given the counsel to the Jews;* above, the eleventh chapter[^p482-2]: It is expedient that one man should die for the people. Chrysostom: « He makes mention of that prophecy for this reason, since his death was the salvation of the whole world, and therefore the hearer, hearing of the bonds of Christ, ought not to be thrown into tumult ».

29. (Vers. 15.). *And there followed Jesus.* Here he treats *of the denial of Peter,* which he describes in this order. First is noted *the occasion of denying;* second, *the denial itself;* third, from the denial, *the cooling of love.*

*The occasion of denying* was in this, that though he was infirm, he wished to follow the Lord. On account of this he says: *And Simon Peter followed Jesus,* not perfectly; hence in Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p482-3]: « But Peter followed *afar off* », and with another leading; hence he was not alone, nay rather *and another disciple,* and this one was the leader; hence: *And that disciple was known to the high priest;* and therefore he went with confidence, hence he subjoins: *And he went in with Jesus into the court of the high priest.* Who this was, the Gloss[^p482-4] did not define. It seems, however, to have been John, as Victor says, who spoke of himself as of another, as one not fearing.

30. (Vers. 16.). *But Peter stood at the door without,* either because he did not dare to enter, or because he was not permitted. Therefore that was true which was said to him above, the thirteenth chapter[^p482-5]: « Whither I go, thou canst not follow me now ». *That other disciple therefore went out, who was known to the high priest,* and by reason of familiarity *spoke to the portress and brought in Peter.* And so it is manifest that he loved but thinly who went in through another; in which it is signified that disciples ought to help one another mutually; therefore to the Galatians, the last chapter[^p482-6]: « Bear ye one another's burdens, and so you shall fulfil the law of Christ ».

31. (Vers. 17.). *The portress said to Peter*[^p482-7]. Here is described the second, namely *the denial proceeding from infirmity;* and this is plain in this, that Peter denied the Lord at the voice of a handmaid. The handmaid's inquiry, then, is introduced: *Art not thou also one of the disciples of this man?* She does not say *of the seducer,* but *of the man,* as one speaking with compassion; but Peter, overcome by faintheartedness, denied; hence there follows: *He saith: I am not.* Chrysostom[^p482-8]: « What is this, Peter? Did you not say: I will lay down my soul for thee? — above, the thirteenth chapter. — What then has come about, that you do not bear even the questioning of a portress »?

32. (Vers. 18.). *Now the servants stood.* Here is described the third, namely, from *the denial, the chilling of the affection;* which is intimated in this, that Peter, already forgetful of the Lord, stood at the coals with the servants and, again, the inner heat having grown lukewarm, sought solace in the outer; on account of this he says: *Now the servants and ministers stood at the coals,* who, namely, had taken the Lord; *because it was cold, and they warmed themselves.* Augustine[^p482-9]: « It was not winter, and yet it was cold, which is wont
<!-- page 483 -->
to happen sometimes even at the spring equinox ». *And Peter also was standing and warming himself;* for he had lost that fire of which in Luke, the twelfth chapter[^p483-1]: « I am come to cast fire on the earth », because he had grown lukewarm; Bede: « The prince of the Apostles grew so lukewarm with the cold of unbelief, that at the voice of one handmaid he dreaded to confess Christ ».

33. *But the high priest*[^p483-2] etc. Peter's denial having been set down, here is subjoined *the examination of Christ;* in which examination four things are noted, namely *the questioning of the high priest, the response of Christ, the infliction of injury, the bearing in patience.*

(Vers. 19.). *The questioning* of the *high priest,* then, is touched on when it is said: *But the high priest,* namely Annas, *was asking*[^p483-3] *Jesus about his disciples and his teaching*[^tr-doctrina]*,* so that, because he could not lay a crime against him, he might at least catch him in speech; not that he might learn, but that he might catch him, Matthew, the twenty-second chapter.

34. (Vers. 20.). *Jesus answered him:* Here is set down the second, namely *the response of Christ,* in which the Lord confutes the intention of the high priest. For he held the teaching of Christ suspect, and therefore he wishes to examine him about it; on account of this the Lord shows that his teaching is not suspect, because it was not *hidden,* but public. On account of this he says: *I have spoken openly to the world;* and he proves this: *I have always taught in the synagogue and in the temple, whither all the Jews resort.* And because he could say: in public you taught true things, in hiding you taught false ones; therefore, for greater certitude, he says: *And in hiding*[^p483-4] *I have spoken nothing;* Isaias, the forty-fifth chapter: « I am the Lord, and there is none else. I have not spoken in hiding, in a dark place ». Thus he removed the suspicion about the teaching by reason of *concealment;* he removes it next by reason of *falsity;* hence he sends him to others, not wishing himself to respond; on account of this he says:

35. (Vers. 21.). *Why askest thou me,* since you do not believe me and can know it from others? *Ask them who have heard what I have spoken to them*[^p483-5], that you may believe them; because *behold, these know what things I have said.* Chrysostom: « They are not the words of one who dares, but of one confident in the truth of the things that had been said »; Proverbs, the eighth chapter: « All my words are right; there is not in them anything crooked nor perverse ».

36. (Vers. 22.). *And when*[^p483-6] *he had said these things.* Here is set down the third, namely, on account of the response, *the infliction of injury* by deed and by word. *By deed: One of the servants standing by gave Jesus a blow;* Lamentations, the third chapter[^p483-7]: « He shall give his cheek to him that striketh him »; and Micheas, the fifth chapter: « They shall strike the cheek of the judge of Israel ». He did injury *by word: Saying: Answerest thou the high priest so?* As if he were to say: you respond foolishly, because one must respond humbly to the high priest; hence they said to Paul, in Acts, the twenty-third chapter: « Dost thou revile the high priest of God »?

37. (Vers. 23.). *Jesus answered him.* Here is noted the fourth, namely *the bearing in patience,* which is intimated in the Lord's response, because he *bears with* the wicked servant; hence he subjoins: *If I have spoken evil, give testimony of the evil;* as if he were to say: I am ready to bear the punishment; *he instructs;* hence he says: *But if well, why*[^p483-8] *strikest thou me?* as if: recognize that you do evil. In which the wonderful benignity of Christ is noted, that he not only *bore with him,* but also *taught him,* according to that of Leviticus, the nineteenth chapter[^p483-9]: « Thou shalt not hate thy brother, but reprove him openly, lest thou incur sin through him ».

### Questions.

38. Quaest. I. But it is asked here first about this, that it is said in Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p483-10], that the servants holding Jesus *led him to Caiphas the prince of the priests;* how does John say: to *Annas?*

2. Likewise, since Annas was not the high priest[^p483-11], why was he led to him?
<!-- page 484 -->

Augustine responds[^p484-1] that he was led to both; but Matthew, wishing to narrate it more briefly, says that he was led to *Caiphas,* because in his house he was most of all examined.

2. To that which is asked, why he was led first to Annas, a fourfold reason is given: the reason *of dignity,* because, as Victor says[^p484-2], he had been priest in the preceding year; for then the high priesthood was bought. — Another reason, namely *of kinship,* because he was related to the high priest as father-in-law[^p484-3]. — A third reason *of opportuneness,* because his house was on the way. — A fourth reason *of malignity,* as Victor says[^p484-4], « that Caiphas might seem of lesser fault, if Christ were condemned by the sentence of another priest ».

39. Quaest. II. Likewise Chrysostom asks[^p484-5]: who was that *other disciple* of whom he says that he followed Jesus?

And he responds that it was John, who keeps silent his own name by reason of humility.

40. Quaest. III. And then there is the question: why does he not keep silent about himself altogether?

The response is that he brings himself in for this reason, that he may build up our faith; he keeps silent his name for this reason, that he may show that he avoids boasting[^p484-6].

41. Quaest. IV. Likewise it is asked, 1. about this, that the Lord says: *In secret I have spoken nothing;* since the Lord himself spoke many things to his disciples in private[^p484-7].

2. Likewise, about this, that he says: *I have always taught in the synagogue and in the temple;* since it is said in Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p484-8], that it was on *the mountain;* likewise in Luke, the sixth chapter, that it was in *a plain place.*

3. Likewise, about this, that he says: *I have spoken openly to the world;* since the Lord spoke in parables and in secret sentences, as is said in Matthew, the thirteenth chapter[^p484-9].

1. To the first Augustine responds[^p484-10]: « Since, if he said some things to the disciples alone, still he did not say them in secret, because he wished them to become known to many through them; hence in Matthew, the tenth chapter: *That which I tell you in the dark, speak ye in the light*[^tr-lumen]*; and that which you hear in the ear, preach ye upon the housetops.* — Chrysostom[^p484-11] responds: *I have spoken nothing in secret,* because « not out of fear, or by guile, as these supposed. He taught the disciples apart, but those things which the others could not grasp ».

2. To the other it must be said that *always* there distributes over the three solemnities: Pasch, Pentecost, Scenopegia, when the people assembled generally[^p484-12].

3. To the other it must be said that *he spoke openly,* not as to open intelligence, but as to the hearing of many. Augustine[^p484-13]: « In a certain manner he spoke *openly,* because many heard; in a certain manner he spoke *not openly,* because they did not understand ».

42. Quaest. V. Likewise it is asked: since the Lord says in Matthew, the fifth chapter[^p484-14]: *If any one strike thee on one cheek, offer him also the other;* why did the Lord not do it, but reproved him that struck him?

Augustine responds[^p484-15]: « Truthfully, gently and justly he responded; and not only did he prepare the other cheek
<!-- page 485 -->
for him who would strike again, but even his whole body to be fixed to the wood. And hence he shows that the precepts of patience are to be done not by display of the body, but by preparation of the heart ».

[^tr-doctrina]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin *doctrina* holds together what English divides among doctrine, teaching and learning. It stands here in the verse and in its exposition: 18:19 *de discipulis suis et doctrina eius* (¶ 33), then ¶ 34 *Habebat enim doctrinam Christi suspectam … doctrinam suam non esse suspectam … suspicionem de doctrina*, where the exposition leans on the noun — what Christ taught, *palam* and *in synagoga et in templo*, set against what is *abscondita* — and p. 483 n. 3 on the Vulgate's *de doctrina*. So "teaching" is the English throughout, inside the verse as well, the Douay's "doctrine" yielding because the paragraph turns on the word (the `bon-ioan-c7-v14-18` precedent).

[^tr-lumen]: **En.** Translator's note: this translation keeps *lux* as "light" and *lumen* as "lumen", except where a verse is quoted as a verse and no surrounding prose leans on the word. This is the pericope's one *lumen*: Matt. 10:27 *Quod dico vobis in tenebris dicite in lumine*, inside Augustine's reply in ¶ 41 to the objection from *In occulto locutus sum nihil*. The reply turns on *in occulto* and *palam*, not on the noun, and *lux* does not stand in the body at all; so the Douay's "light" stands.

## Notes
