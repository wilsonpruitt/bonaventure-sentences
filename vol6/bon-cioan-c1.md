## Latin
<!-- page 535 -->

### Collationes in Evangelium S. Ioannis[^p535-1].

### Capitulum I. *In principio erat Verbum.*

### Collatio I.

(Vers. 5.). Ioannis primo: *Lux in tenebris lucet, et tenebrae eam non comprehenderunt.*

1. *Moraliter* in istis verbis duo tanguntur. Primo, *commendatur nobis*[^p535-2] *praesentia divinae claritatis*, cum dicitur: *Lux in tenebris lucet.* — Secundo, *reprehenditur in nobis ignorantia humanae caecitatis*, cum dicitur: *Et tenebrae eam non comprehenderunt.*

Circa primum considerandum est, *quae sit* ista lux — et quis sit *eius effectus*.

Lux ista « est lux vera, quae illuminat omnem hominem venientem in hunc mundum », infra eodem[^p535-3], Christus videlicet, qui est lux et quantum ad naturam *Divinitatis* et quantum ad naturam *humanitatis*. Quantum ad naturam *Divinitatis illustrat interius* hominem vel animam, quantum ad naturam *humanitatis informat exterius* vitam.

2. Est igitur lux Christus secundum quod *homo;* de quo Ioannis octavo[^p535-4]: « Ego lux veni in mundum; qui sequitur me non ambulat in tenebris » etc. Hoc ad humanitatem refertur; non enim sequitur in eo excellentiam Dei, sed humilitatem hominis. — Est autem secundum quod homo lux *arguens*, lux *dirigens*, lux *delectans: arguens* malos, *dirigens* iustos, *delectans* Beatos. — *Arguit malos;* ad Ephesios quinto[^p535-5]: « Omnia enim, quae arguuntur, a lumine manifestantur »; et ideo oditur a malis; Ioannis tertio: « Omnis, qui male agit, odit lucem ». — *Dirigit iustos;* Ioannis duodecimo: « Ambulate, dum lucem habetis, ut tenebrae vos non comprehendant »; Lucae primo: « Visitavit nos oriens ex alto, illuminare his qui in tenebris sedent » etc., « ad diri-
<!-- page 536 -->
gendos pedes nostros in viam pacis ». — *Delectat Beatos;* Apocalypsis vigesimo primo: « Civitas illa non eget sole neque luna; claritas enim Dei illuminat illam, et lucerna eius est Agnus ».

3. *Lux in tenebris lucet.* Lux ista, ut dictum est, *lucet exterius* secundum operationes *humanae* naturae; item *lucet interius* secundum operationes *divinae* naturae. — Lucet autem tripliciter secundum operationes humanae naturae[^p536-1], scilicet *signis, verbis* et *exemplis:* in *signis* lucet eius potentia, in *verbis* eius sapientia, bonitas in *exemplis.*

Lucet ergo in *signis;* Psalmus[^p536-2]: « Illuxerunt coruscationes tuae orbi terrae », coruscationes, inquam, miraculorum, quas vidit, et « commota est terra ». — Lucet etiam in *verbis;* Ioannis duodecimo: « Dum lucem habetis, credite in lucem, ut filii lucis sitis » aeternae. — Lucet etiam in *exemplis;* Habacuc tertio: « Splendor eius ut lux erit; cornua in manibus eius ». « Splendor eius ut lux erit », in conversatione; « cornua in manibus eius », in passione. Ideo Ioannis octavo: « Qui sequitur me non ambulat in tenebris ».

4. *Lux in tenebris lucet.* Christus iterum lux est secundum quod *Deus;* de quo Ioannis duodecimo[^p536-3]: « Ego lux in mundum veni, ut omnis, qui credit in me, non maneat in tenebris »; quod refertur ad Divinitatem; *credimus* enim in Christum *Dominum*, non in *hominem* secundum quod homo.

Nota autem, quod lux habet *puritatem* in materia, *speciositatem* in forma, *sublimitatem* in situ. Christus autem secundum quod Deus est *lux altissima, lux purissima, lux speciosissima.* Altitudo attestatur *potentiae*, speciositas *sapientiae*, puritas *bonitati.* — Est igitur Christus lux *altissima;* primae ad Timotheum ultimo[^p536-4]: « Lucem inhabitat inaccessibilem ». Lux *speciosissima;* Sapientiae septimo: « Speciosior est sole et super omnem dispositionem stellarum luci comparata invenitur prior ». Lux *purissima;* primae Ioannis primo: « Deus lux est, et tenebrae in eo non sunt ullae ».

5. Haec lux lucet *interius* secundum operationes *divinae* naturae. Emittit autem interius triplicem lucem sive lumen: lumen *scientiae;* Psalmus[^p536-5]: « Emitte lucem tuam et veritatem tuam »; item: « Illumina oculos meos, ne unquam obdormiam in morte ». — Lumen *gratiae;* Isaiae quinquagesimo octavo: « Orietur in tenebris lux tua »; et sexagesimo: « Surge, illuminare, Ierusalem, quia venit lumen tuum ». — Lumen *laetitiae spiritualis;* Psalmus: « Signatum est super nos lumen vultus tui, Domine; dedisti laetitiam in corde meo »; quocontra dicitur Threnorum tertio: « Minavit me Dominus et adduxit in tenebras et non in lucem ».

6. Sequitur: *Et tenebrae eam non comprehenderunt;* ubi *reprehenditur ignorantia humanae caecitatis.* Circa quod considerandum est, quae sint *istae tenebrae* — et qui *illorum defectus.*

*Tenebrae istae* peccatores sunt, qui *in tenebris sunt* — et *tenebras quaerunt* — et *ad tenebras tendunt.* In tenebris *spiritualibus* sunt, tenebras *temporales* quaerunt et ad tenebras *aeternales* tendunt. — *In tenebris sunt;* Psalmus[^p536-6]: « Nescierunt neque intellexerunt, in tenebris ambulant »; Ecclesiastici undecimo: « Error et tenebrae peccatoribus concreata sunt ». — *Tenebras quaerunt;* Ioannis tertio: « Dilexerunt homines magis tenebras quam lucem; erant enim eorum mala opera ». — *Ad tenebras tendunt;* in Canonica Iudae: « Quibus procella tenebrarum servata est in aeternum »; Iob decimo: « Dimitte me, ut plangam paululum dolorem meum, antequam vadam et non revertar ad terram tenebrosam et opertam mortis caligine ». — Isti ergo *tenebrae sunt.*

In *comprehensione* autem *lucis* deficiunt, quia *aversi;* Psalmus[^p536-7]: « Averterunt se et non servaverunt pactum »; quia *operti;* Psalmus: « Operti sunt iniquitate et impietate sua »; quia *caeci;* Matthaei decimo quinto: « Sinite eos; caeci sunt et duces caecorum ». *Avertuntur* Deum deserendo, *operiuntur* peccatum operando, *excaecantur* in peccato perseverando.

7. Aliter. *Tenebrae eam non comprehenderunt.* Notandum, quod sunt tenebrae *culpae*, tenebrae *miseriae*, tenebrae *naturae.* — De *tenebris culpae*, Ecclesiastici undecimo[^p536-8]: « Error et tenebrae peccatoribus concreata sunt. » — De *tenebris miseriae*, Tobiae quinto: « Quale gaudium mihi erit, qui in tenebris sedeo et lumen caeli non video »? — *Tenebrae naturae* sunt naturales defectus creaturae, de quibus primae Ioannis primo: « Deus lux est, et tenebrae in eo non sunt ullae », id est ullus defectus.

8. Item nota, quod est *comprehensio* triplex: per *inclusionem* vel per commensurationem; item per *apertam visionem;* item per *fidei et caritatis adhaesionem.* — Primo modo nullae unquam tenebrae Deum comprehendunt, quia nulla unquam natura in aliquo statu potest Deo commensurari. Unde Damascenus[^p536-9]: « Infinitus est Deus et incomprehensibilis »; et
<!-- page 537 -->
Ecclesiastici quadragesimo tertio: « Ne laboretis, non enim comprehendetis ». — Primo modo et secundo non comprehendunt eum tenebrae *miseriae*, quia nullus miser Deum videt aperta visione; ad Philippenses tertio[^p537-1]: « Non me arbitror comprehendisse ». — Primo modo et secundo et tertio non comprehendunt eum *tenebrae culpae.* Homo enim peccator nec Deum *includit*, quia creatura est; nec Deum *videt*, quia miser est; nec ei *per fidem et caritatem adhaeret*, quia peccator est. Unde hic dicitur, quod *tenebrae eam non comprehenderunt;* et ad Ephesios tertio: « In caritate radicati et fundati, ut possitis comprehendere cum omnibus Sanctis »; quia aliter non possetis scire; « quae sit longitudo », scilicet aeternitatis, « latitudo », scilicet immensitatis, « sublimitas », scilicet maiestatis, « et profundum », scilicet sapientiae et veritatis.

### Collatio II.

(Vers. 14.). *Verbum caro factum est et habitavit in nobis.*

1. In verbis istis exprimitur nobis duplex indicium caritatis. Primum est in hoc, quod Dominus noster exhibet se nobis *fratrem.* — Secundum in hoc, quod exhibet se nobis *familiarem.* — Primum fecit Dominus carnem assumendo, quod notatur, cum dicitur: *Verbum caro factum est;* secundum, in nobis habitando, quod notatur, cum dicitur: *Et habitavit in nobis.* Eramus enim *abiecti;* sed Deus adhibet remedium contra abiectionem, cum exhibet se *fratrem.* Eramus *desolati;* sed Deus adhibet remedium contra desolationem, cum se exhibet nobis *familiarem.*

2. Nostrae igitur *abiectioni* est remedium, quia *Verbum caro factum est.* Sed *cuiusmodi Verbum* — et *qualiter caro factum est?*

Verbum, inquam, *maiestate altissimum, virtute immensum, duratione aeternum.* — *Altissimum* est *maiestate;* nam[^p537-2] « Verbum erat apud Deum, et Deus erat Verbum »; ideo Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto: « Ego in altissimis habito » etc.; et Sapientiae decimo octavo: « Omnipotens sermo tuus, Domine, a regalibus sedibus venit ». — *Immensum* est *virtute*, quia « per ipsum omnia facta sunt », Ioannis primo; item in Psalmo: « Verbo Domini caeli firmati sunt » etc.; ad Hebraeos primo: « Portans omnia verbo virtutis suae ». — *Aeternum* est *duratione*, quia « Verbum erat in principio apud Deum »; Psalmus: « In aeternum, Domine, Verbum tuum permanet in caelo ».

3. Hoc Verbum, inquam, *caro factum est*, scilicet *carni unitum, in carne contentum, in carne natum et passum.* — *Carni unitur* Verbum, et ecce, *maiestas humilis;* ad Philippenses secundo[^p537-3]: « Cum in forma Dei esset etc., exinanivit semetipsum, formam servi accipiens » etc. Hanc humiliationem maiestatis viderunt pastores, Lucae secundo, quoniam « videntes cognoverunt de verbo, quod dictum erat illis de puero hoc »; cognoverunt enim Verbum Dei reclinatum in praesepio, quod erat eis dictum de puero.

4. *In carne continetur* Verbum, et ecce, *immensitas brevis;* ad Romanos nono[^p537-4]: « Verbum abbreviatum fecit Dominus super terram »; vere *abbreviatum*, quia positione factum est *breve* quod erat natura productum, in praesepio *breve* quod erat ubique, utique immensum.

5. *In carne nascitur* Verbum, et ecce, *aeternitas temporalis;* ad Galatas quarto[^p537-5]: « Ubi venit plenitudo temporis, misit Deus Filium suum factum ex muliere, factum sub Lege ». Si *factum:* ergo *temporale.* Hoc est Verbum factum, de quo Lucae secundo: « Transeamus usque Bethlehem et videamus hoc verbum, quod factum est » etc. *Videamus*, inquam, *Verbum* secundum carnem *factum*, qui videre nunquam potuimus *Verbum*, quod secundum Divinitatem nec est factum nec creatum, sed genitum. — Psalmus: « Cantate, inquit, Domino, quia mirabilia fecit ». Fecit enim, ut sit *maiestas abiecta*, et *immensitas parva, aeternitas facta;* quod totum memorat Evangelista, cum dicit: *Verbum caro factum est.*

6. Sequitur: *Et habitavit in nobis; in nobis*, id est in nostra *humanitate* — vel: *in nobis*, id est in nostra *societate* — vel: *in nobis*, id est in nostra *mente.* In nostra *humanitate* habitavit *corporaliter*, in nostra *societate* habitavit *sacramentaliter*, in nostra *mente spiritualiter.* — Habitat ergo Verbum in nostra *humanitate;* de quo tertio Regum octavo[^p537-6]: « Dixit Dominus, ut habitaret in nebula », *nebula*, humanitas assumta, de qua profluxit omnium medicina. — Habitat in nostra *societate sacramentaliter;* de quo Matthaei ultimo: « Ego vobiscum sum usque ad consummationem saeculi »; et Psalmus: « In pace », id est in fratrum concordia, « factus est locus eius »; « in Sion » id est in Ecclesia, « habitatio eius ». — Habitat in nostra *mente spiritualiter;* Ioannis decimo quinto: « Manete in me, et ego in vobis »; secundi Machabaeorum decimo quarto: « Tu, Domine universorum, qui nullius indiges, voluisti, templum habitationis tuae fieri in nobis ».
<!-- page 538 -->

7. Vel aliter: *Habitavit in nobis*, id est *intra nos;* ubi potest notari, quod Dominus habitat *intra nos* ad *sanctificandum, inter nos* ad *regendum, circa nos* ad *protegendum.* — *Obsessi* sumus, sed Dominus habitat *circa nos* ad *protegendum;* ideo « non commovebitur in aeternum qui habitat in Ierusalem, quia Dominus in circuitu populi sui », secundum quod dicitur in Psalmo[^p538-1]; et Isaiae vigesimo sexto: « Salvator ponetur in ea », id est in Ecclesia, « murus et antemurale ». — *Ignari* sumus, et ipse habitat *inter nos* ad *regendum;* Apocalypsis vigesimo primo: « Ecce, tabernaculum Dei cum hominibus, et habitabit cum illis; et ipsi populus eius erunt, et ipse Deus cum eis erit eorum Deus ». — *Miseri* sumus, sed ipse habitat *intra nos* ad *consolandum* et *sanctificandum;* de quo potest intelligi quod supra habitum est secundi Machabaeorum decimo quarto, et quod hic dicitur: *Habitavit in nobis.*

### Collatio III.

(Vers. 16.). *De plenitudine eius nos omnes accepimus.*

1. Dici solet, quod cui caput infirmum, cetera membra dolent; et e converso dici potest: cui caput est sanum, cetera membra vigent[^p538-2]. Bona enim dispositio capitis est membrorum omnium vigoratio, et capitis abundantia corporis relevatio. Secundum haec duo notantur in verbis istis: *abundantia capitis*, cum dicitur: *De plenitudine eius*, id est Christi, qui est Ecclesiae caput — et *relevatio corporis*, cum dicitur: *Nos omnes accepimus*, qui scilicet sumus Ecclesiae corpus. Fluit enim quodam modo spirituali a Christo capite in nos, qui sumus membra eius, spiritualis motus et sensus; est enim, secundum quod dicitur in Psalmo[^p538-3], « sicut unguentum in capite, quod descendit in barbam, barbam etc. et usque in oram vestimenti eius », cui attestatur quod hic dicitur: *De plenitudine eius nos omnes accepimus.*

2. *De plenitudine eius.* Notandum, quod est quadruplex plenitudo, scilicet *sufficientiae, abundantiae, excellentiae* et *supereffluentiae.* — Plenitudo *sufficientiae* reperitur in quolibet *iusto;* de qua Psalmus[^p538-4]: « Aperis tu manum tuam et imples omne animal benedictione ». — Plenitudo *abundantiae* reperitur in quolibet viro *perfecto*, secundum quod legitur Actuum sexto: « Stephanus, plenus gratia »; et Actuum secundo: « Repleti sunt omnes Spiritu sancto » etc. — Plenitudo *excellentiae* reperitur in beatissima *Virgine*, cui dictum est Lucae primo[^p538-5]: « Ave, gratia plena »; quam plenitudinem tractans, beatus Bernardus ait: « Legimus Stephanum *plenum gratia*, Apostolos *repletos* Spiritu sancto, sed longe dissimiliter in Maria. Non enim habitavit in eis plenitudo Divinitatis corporaliter, quemadmodum in Maria ». — Plenitudo *supereffluentiae* reperitur in *Christo*, de quo, secundum quod hic dicitur, *nos omnes accepimus.* Unde et ipse dicit Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto: « Transite ad me, omnes, qui concupiscitis me, et a generationibus meis implemini ».

3. Fuit autem in Christo quadruplex plenitudo, scilicet *sapientiae — clementiae — virtutis* — et *gloriae.* Plenitudo *sapientiae* apparuit in *praedicatione*, plenitudo *clementiae* in *passione*, plenitudo *virtutis* in *resurrectione*, plenitudo *gloriae* in *ascensione.* — Fuit ergo plenus *sapientia;* Lucae secundo[^p538-6]: « Puer autem crescebat et confortabatur, plenus sapientia, et gratia Dei erat in illo ». — Fuit plenus *clementia;* unde ei convenit quod dicitur Esther decimo quinto: « Valde mirabilis es, Domine, et facies tua plena est gratiarum ». Tu quidem *mirabilis es, Domine*, cuncta *creando*, et *facies tua*, Christe, *plena apparuit gratiarum*, omnia *recreando.* Ipse enim est *sapientia*, « quae desursum est, quae plena est misericordia et operibus bonis », secundum quod dicitur Iacobi tertio. — Plenus fuit *virtute* et potentia, in cuius persona dicitur Michaeae tertio[^p538-7]: « Repletus sum ego fortitudine Spiritus Domini, iudicio et virtute ». — Plenus fuit *gloria;* unde ad Colossenses secundo: « In ipso habitavit plenitudo Divinitatis corporaliter ».

4. *Sapientia*, ut dictum est, apparuit in *praedicatione;* docebat enim eos « sicut potestatem habens » etc., secundum quod dicitur Matthaei septimo[^p538-8]. — *Clementia* in *passione;* tunc enim patuit, quod « apud Dominum misericordia et copiosa apud eum redemptio », secundum quod dicitur in Psalmo. — *Potentia* in *resurrectione;* tunc enim data « est ei omnis potestas », id est dari manifestata, secundum quod dicitur Matthaei ultimo. — *Gloria* in *ascensione;* tunc enim « vidimus gloriam eius, gloriam quasi Unigeniti a Patre », Ioannis primo, quando quidem « assumtus est in caelum et sedet ad dexteram Patris », secundum quod dicitur Marci ultimo.
<!-- page 539 -->

5. In hoc enim apparuit gloria Unigeniti, quod sedet in dexteris Dei, quae quidem Unigeniti gloria solet notari in quatuor; quorum primum est, quod Pater *omne iudicium* dedit ei, secundum quod dicitur Ioannis quinto[^p539-1]: « Pater omne iudicium dedit Filio ». — Secundum est in hoc, quod per omnia *similis est Patri.* Ipse enim est « splendor gloriae et figura substantiae eius », secundum quod dicitur ad Hebraeos primo. — Tertium est, quod *constituit eum caput super omnem Ecclesiam*, secundum quod dicitur ad Ephesios primo: « Omnia subiecit sub pedibus eius, et ipsum dedit caput super omnem Ecclesiam ». — Quartum est, quod dedit ei *sedere ad dexteram suam*, secundum quod habetur, ut dictum est, Marci ultimo.

Ista quatuor proprie conveniunt Christo nec alii conveniunt creaturae; unde versus:

Judicium, paritas, praelatio, sessio dexterae  
Sunt Unigeniti praecellens gloria nati.

6. *De plenitudine eius nos omnes accepimus.* — De plenitudine *sapientiae* accepimus *doctrinam instructionis;* Apocalypsis tertio[^p539-2]: « In mente habe, qualiter acceperis et audieris, et serva et poenitentiam age »; Ioannis decimo septimo Veritas ait: « Verba, quae dedisti mihi, dedi eis », scilicet discipulis; « et ipsi acceperunt et cognoverunt vere, quia a te exivi »; *dedi*, docendo, et *ipsi acceperunt*, credendo. — De plenitudine *clementiae* accepimus *gratiam adoptionis;* ad Romanos octavo[^p539-3]: « Non accepistis spiritum servitutis iterum in timore, sed accepistis spiritum adoptionis filiorum » etc. — De plenitudine *potentiae* accepimus *virtutem protectionis*, quam ipse promittebat Apostolis, Actuum primo: « Accipietis virtutem supervenientis Spiritus in vos, et eritis mihi testes » etc. — De plenitudine *gloriae* accepimus *praemium aeternae retributionis;* Sapientiae quinto: « Accipient regnum decoris et diadema speciei de manu Domini »; et Marci decimo: « Nemo est, qui reliquerit domum, aut fratres etc., qui non accipiat centies tantum in hoc saeculo et vitam aeternam in futuro ».

Sic ergo accepimus a Christo *doctrinam instructionis, gratiam adoptionis, virtutem protectionis, gloriam* vel praemium *retributionis.* Primum *illuminat intellectum;* secundum *informat affectum;* tertium *roborat effectum;* quartum *adimplet desiderium.*

7. Notandum autem, quod *ratio accepti* obligat nos ad quatuor: ad *gratiarum actionem* — ad *mentis humiliationem* — ad *virtutis profectum* — et *acceptorum distributionem.* Gratiarum actio *impetrat*, mentis humiliatio *conservat*, virtutis exercitatio *ditat*, acceptorum distributio *dilatat.*

Obligamur ad *gratiarum actionem*, iuxta quod dicitur primi Paralipomenon vigesimo nono[^p539-4]: « Tua sunt omnia, Domine, et quae de manu tua accepimus dedimus tibi ». — Ad *mentis humiliationem;* primae ad Corinthios quarto: « Quid habes, quod non accepisti? Si autem accepisti, quid gloriaris, quasi non acceperis »? — Ad *virtutis profectum et exercitationem;* secundae ad Corinthios sexto: « Hortamur vos, ne in vacuum gratiam Dei recipiatis »; et primae ad Thessalonicenses quarto: « Rogamus vos et obsecramus in Domino Iesu Christo, ut quemadmodum accepistis a nobis, quomodo oportet vos ambulare et placere Deo, sic et ambuletis, ut abundetis magis ». — Ad *acceptorum distributionem;* primae Petri quarto: « Unusquisque prout accepit gratiam, in alterutrum illam administrantes sicut boni dispensatores multiformis gratiae Dei »; et Matthaei decimo: « Gratis accepistis, gratis date ».

8. *De plenitudine eius nos omnes accepimus. Nos*, inquam, *omnes*, qui aliquid accepimus; hoc ideo dictum sit, quia de plenitudine eius quidam *accipiunt et ditantur* — quidam autem de plenitudine eius *accipere non merentur.* — Accipiunt enim *de plenitudine eius* quatuor genera hominum, scilicet *innocentes, obedientes, pugnantes* et *contemplantes.* Congruit enim, ut de manu patrisfamilias accipiant *filii, servi, milites* et *amici.* Per *filios* intellige *innocentes*, qui patris imaginem custodiunt impollutam; et isti de manu patris merentur accipere, secundum quod dicitur in Psalmo[^p539-5]: « Innocens manibus et mundo corde, hic accipiet benedictionem a Domino ». — Per *servos* intellige *obedientes*, qui mandata Domini perficiunt; et isti merentur accipere, secundum quod dicitur primae ad Corinthios tertio: « Unusquisque propriam mercedem » accipiet « secundum suum laborem ». — Per *milites* intellige *pugnantes*, qui regis bella gerunt; et isti merentur accipere, secundum quod habetur Iacobi primo: « Beatus vir, qui suffert tentationem, quoniam, cum probatus fuerit, accipiet coronam vitae, quam repromisit Deus diligentibus se ». — Per *amicos* intellige *contemplantes*, qui quasi in thalamo cum sponso dormiunt; et isti merentur accipere quod ab ipso petunt, secundum quod dicitur Matthaei septimo: « Qui petit accipit, et qui quaerit invenit »; et Ioannis decimo sexto dicit Salvator amicis suis: « Usque modo
<!-- page 540 -->
non petistis quidquam in nomine meo. Petite et accipietis, ut gaudium vestrum sit plenum ».

9. Isti accipiunt et ditantur; sed sunt alii, qui *accipere non merentur.* Non enim congruit, ut paterfamilias largiatur *ignotis, ignaris* et *indignis*, sive *ingratis.* — *Ignoti* sunt Domino qui propter temporalia ipsum obliviscuntur, ut mundani; et isti non accipiunt, secundum quod habetur Ioannis decimo quarto[^p540-1]: « Ego, ait Veritas, rogabo Patrem, et alium Paracletum dabit vobis etc.; quem mundus non potest accipere, quia non videt eum nec scit eum »; ideo *nescit* Deum, quia de solis mundanis cogitat et obliviscitur eum. — *Ignari* sunt sive stulti qui concupiscentias insequuntur; et isti non merentur accipere, quia statim acceptum in lutum proiicerent. Istis dicitur Iacobi quarto[^p540-2]: « Petitis et non accipitis, eo quod male petatis, ut in concupiscentiis vestris insumatis ». — *Indigni* sunt qui haesitando postulant, quia de largitate Dei diffidunt, sed nec isti merentur accipere; unde Iacobi primo: « Qui haesitat similis est fluctui maris, qui a vento movetur et circumfertur; non ergo aestimet homo ille, quod accipiat aliquid a Domino ». — *Ingrati* sunt illi quibus accepta *non sufficiunt*, sicut illi, de quibus Matthaei vigesimo[^p540-3], qui « accipientes singulos denarios, murmuraverunt adversus patremfamilias »; et isti non merentur amplius accipere, sed magis amittere; merentur enim, ut ibi tangitur, *novissimi* esse. *Ingrati* etiam sunt qui de acceptis Domino *non serviunt*, similes servo pigro, qui mnam acceptam non multiplicavit, et ideo acceptam amittere meruit, secundum quod dicitur Lucae decimo nono: « Auferte ab eo mnam et date illi qui decem mnas habet ».

10. Nos igitur exhibeamus nos Deo *notos* ad ipsum frequenter revertendo, et exhibeamus nos *gnaros* concupiscentias declinando, et exhibeamus nos *dignos* fideliter postulando, et exhibeamus nos *gratos* eidem de acceptis fideliter serviendo; et sic poterimus *accipere de plenitudine eius.*

### Collatio IV.

(Vers. 26.). *Medius vestrum stetit, quem vos nescitis.*

1. In verbis istis duo tanguntur. Primum est *miseratio divinae pietatis*, quae nobis exhibita est, cum Christus ad nos descendit; et hoc tangitur, cum dicitur: *Medius vestrum stetit.* — Secundum est *ingratitudo humanae caecitatis*, quae in hoc manifestata est, quod « mundus eum non cognovit[^p540-4] »; et hoc tangitur, cum dicitur: *Quem vos nescitis.*

2. Ait igitur: *Medius vestrum* etc. Nota, quod quandoque Christus legitur *primus*, quandoque *medius*, quandoque *novissimus.* Et revera Christus se exhibuit *primum* ad deducendum, *novissimum* ad protegendum, *medium* ad congregandum. — *Primus* fuit ad *deducendum;* unde Michaeae secundo[^p540-5]: « Ascendet ante eos pandens iter »; et post: « Transibit rex eorum coram eis, et Dominus in capite eorum »; Isaiae quadragesimo primo: « Primus ad Sion dicet: Ecce, adsunt, et Ierusalem evangelistam dabo ». — *Novissimus* fuit ad *protegendum;* Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio[^p540-6]: « Desideravimus eum, despectum et novissimum virorum »; Hieronymus: « *Novissimum*, ut reciperet ictus persequentium ». Unde subiungitur infra eodem: « Vere languores nostros ipse tulit et dolores nostros ipse portavit ». — *Medius* fuit ad *congregandum*, ut ad ipsum congregarentur fideles, sicut antiquitus filii Israel ad tabernaculum, secundum quod dicitur Levitici vigesimo sexto[^p540-7]: « Ponam tabernaculum meum in medio vestri, et non abiiciet vos ultra anima mea ». Unde et hic dicitur: *Medius vestrum stetit.*

3. Haec tria figurata sunt in nube, quae aliquando *praecedebat* filios Israel in *ducatum directionis;* Numerorum nono[^p540-8]: « Cum ablata fuisset nubes, tunc proficiscebantur »; aliquando *sequebatur* in *praesidium protectionis;* Exodi decimo quarto: « Tollens se Angelus, qui praecedebat castra Israel, abiit post eos, et cum eo pariter columna nubis »; aliquando *stabat* in *signum mansionis;* Numerorum nono: « In loco, ubi stetisset nubes, ibi castrametabantur ».

4. *Medius*, inquit, *vestrum.* Nota, quod Christus fuit *medius* inter hominem et *Deum* — inter hominem et *Angelum* — inter hominem et *hominem.* — Inter hominem et *Deum* medius fuit, quia hominem *Deo reconciliavit;* primae ad Timotheum secundo[^p540-9]: « Unus est mediator Dei et hominum, homo Christus Iesus ». — Medius fuit inter hominem et *Angelum*, quia hominem *Angelo pacificavit;* unde, Lucae secundo, annuntiaverunt, Christo nato, Angeli,
<!-- page 541 -->
quia « in terra pax hominibus bonae voluntatis »; et ad Colossenses primo: « Pacificans per sanguinem crucis eius sive quae in terris, sive quae in caelis sunt ». — Medius fuit inter hominem et *hominem*, quia homines ad concordiam revocavit; unde legitur Lucae vigesimo tertio, quod cum eum misisset Pilatus ad Herodem, « facti sunt amici Pilatus et Herodes in illa die; nam antea inimici erant ad invicem ».

5. Nota, quod Christus, existens medius inter *Deum et hominem*, minor erat Deo et maior homine, et ideo fuit quasi *sub* una extremitate et *super* aliam; unde fuit quasi *medium* in prima figura. — Medius autem existens inter *hominem et Angelum*, maior erat utroque, utpote rex hominum et Angelorum; unde fuit quasi *super* utramque extremitatem, et ideo erat quasi *medium* in secunda figura. — Medius vero existens inter *Herodem et Pilatum*, exhibuit se minorem utroque, utpote iudicandum; unde fuit quasi *sub* utraque extremitate, et ideo fuit *medium* in tertia figura[^p541-1].

6. *Medius*, inquit, *vestrum.* Nota, quod tria considerant medium: medium inspicit *ratio, virtus* et *natura*[^p541-2]. Circa medium *rationis* consistit *certitudo veritatis;* circa medium *moris, rectitudo virtutis* vel salutis; circa medium *naturae, valitudo sanitatis.* Medium *rationis* extrema *ordinat;* medium *moris* extrema *moderat;* medium *naturae* extrema *contemperat.*

7. Christus igitur fuit quasi *medium rationale*, circa quod est certitudo veritatis; medium, inquam, *extrema ordinans.* Ipse enim medius existens *inter* duo testamenta, vetus ac novum ordinavit et utriusque veritatem patefecit. Unde Habacuc tertio[^p541-3]: « In medio annorum », scilicet Legis et gratiae, « notum facies », id est, veritatem manifestabis; secundum aliam litteram: « In medio duorum animalium », id est duorum testamentorum, « cognosceris ». — Fuit etiam Christus quasi *medium morale*, circa quod est *rectitudo virtutis;* medium, inquam, *extrema moderans;* unde Lucae vigesimo secundo[^p541-4], discipulis immoderate contendentibus, « quis illorum esset maior in regno caelorum », ostendit Iesus in se ipso modum humilitatis, ostendens se ipsum *medium* et dicens: « Ego in medio vestrum sum, sicut qui ministrat », id est, sicut qui minor stat. Item Matthaei decimo octavo: « Advocans Iesus parvulum, statuit eum in medio eorum » etc. — Fuit iterum Christus quasi *medium naturale*, circa quod est *valitudo salutis;* unde in Psalmo[^p541-5]: « Operatus est salutem in medio terrae »,

8. *Medius*, inquit, *vestrum.* Nota, quod *medium* locus est *unitatis;* extrema enim per medium uniuntur. — Item, medium est locus *communitatis;* convenit enim medium *utrique* extremorum. — Item, medium est locus *aequalitatis*, quia medium aequaliter distat ab extremis[^p541-6].

9. Christus ergo fuit *medium unitatis;* « ipse enim est pax nostra, qui fecit utraque unum », ad Ephesios secundo[^p541-7]. Et hoc significatum est Ioannis vigesimo, quando « stetit in medio » discipulorum « et dixit eis: Pax vobis »; ipse enim semper vocat suos ad unitatem pacis. — Christus iterum fuit in medio *communitatis*, quia omnibus bona sua communicavit; Isaiae quinquagesimo quinto[^p541-8]: « Omnes sitientes, venite ad aquas ». Et hoc figuratum est Apocalypsis vigesimo secundo, ubi ostensus est Ioanni fluvius procedens de sede Dei et Agni, « in medio plateae civitatis », non in angulo, ut esset alicuius proprius, sed *in medio plateae* civitatis, ut esset omnibus communis. — Christus iterum fuit in medio *aequalitatis;* Deus enim personam non accipit; Actuum decimo[^p541-9]: « In veritate comperi, quod non sit personarum acceptor Deus ». Tali dicitur Danielis decimo tertio: « Sede in medio nostrum et indica nobis ».

10. Aliter *notatur* Christus *medius vestrum:* sicut *cor* in medio animalis *ad vivificandum*[^p541-10]; sicut
<!-- page 542 -->
*lucerna* in medio templi *ad illuminandum;* sicut *columna* in medio domus *ad sustinendum*, et huius similia.

11. *Medius vestrum stetit.* Nota, quod Christus medius *stetit*, medius *iacuit*, medius *sedit*, medius *pependit*, medius *ambulavit.* — *Iacuit* in medio animalium; Habacuc tertio[^p542-1]: « In medio duorum animalium cognosceris ». In medio duorum animalium cognitus est iacens in praesepio, Lucae secundo. — *Sedit* in medio doctorum; Lucae secundo: « Invenerunt illum in templo sedentem in medio doctorum ». — *Pependit* in medio latronum; Ioannis decimo nono[^p542-2]: « Crucifixerunt cum eo alios duos hinc et inde, medium autem Iesum ». — *Ambulavit* in medio candelabrorum; Apocalypsis secundo: « Haec dicit qui ambulat in medio candelabrorum aureorum ». — *Stetit* in medio discipulorum; Ioannis vigesimo: « Stetit Iesus in medio » discipulorum suorum « et dixit eis: Pax vobis ».

Nota et expone, quod Christus iacens in medio *animalium* dat formam *innocentibus relinquendi vanitatem.* — Christus sedens in medio *doctorum* dat formam *studentibus inquirendi veritatem.* — Christus ambulans in medio *candelabrorum aureorum* dat formam *proficientibus custodiendi honestatem.* — Christus pendens in medio *latronum* dat formam *patientibus sustinendi adversitatem.* — Christus in medio *discipulorum* dat formam *praesidentibus convocandi subditos ad fraternitatem.*

### Collatio V.

(Vers. 33.). *Super quem videris Spiritum descendentem et manentem super eum, hic est qui baptizat.*

1. Notabiliter tanguntur hic tria de Spiritu sancto, scilicet quia *apparet* — quia *descendit* — et quia *manet.* — *Apparitio* eius notatur in verbo *visionis*, cum dicitur: *Super quem videris. Descensio*, cum adiungitur: *Spiritum descendentem. Mansio*, cum additur: *Et manentem.* Descendit Spiritus in *gratiae vivificatione;* apparet in *bonorum operum exhibitione;* manet vero in *eorundem perseverantia et continuatione.* Haec eadem notantur Actuum secundo[^p542-3] de Spiritu sancto: *descensio*, cum dicitur: « Factus est repente de caelo sonus tanquam advenientis spiritus vehementis ». *Apparitio*, cum dicitur: « Et apparuerunt illis dispertitae linguae tanquam ignis ». *Mansio*, cum dicitur: « Seditque supra singulos eorum ».

2. *Descendit* ergo Spiritus divinus et animam *vivificat* suo modo spirituali, sicut spiritus humanus suo modo naturali vivificat corpus; unde Ezechielis trigesimo septimo[^p542-4]: « Ecce, ego intromittam in vos spiritum, et vivetis ». — Notandum ergo, quod *spiritus naturalis* vivificans tria facit: dat enim corpori *sensum, loquelam* et *motum.* Ita *Spiritus divinus* animam gratificans dat ei *sensum devotionis, verbum aedificationis* et *motum bonae operationis.* — De primo, ad Romanos octavo[^p542-5]: « Qui secundum carnem sunt, quae carnis sunt sapiunt; qui autem secundum spiritum sunt, quae sunt spiritus sentiunt »; ecce, quia Spiritus dat *sensum.* — De secundo, Actuum secundo: « Repleti sunt omnes Spiritu sancto et coeperunt loqui variis linguis »; ecce, quia Spiritus dat *verbum.* — De tertio, Ezechielis primo: « Quocumque ibat spiritus, illuc, eunte spiritu, et rotae pariter elevabantur. Spiritus enim vitae erat in rotis ». — Isti sunt tres effectus Spiritus *descendentis et vivificantis.*

3. Spiritus sanctus non solum *descendit*, sed *apparet.* Sunt autem, ut cetera dimittamus, tria signa suae apparitionis. Apparuit enim super discipulos in specie *flaminis* — in specie *linguae* — et in specie *ignis;* quae tria notantur Actuum secundo[^p542-6]: quando « factus est sonus tanquam advenientis spiritus vehementis »; ecce primum; quando « apparuerunt dispertitae linguae »; ecce secundum; « tanquam ignis »; ecce tertium.

4. In specie *ignis* apparuit, quia ipse incendit in corde ardorem caritatis; « caritas enim diffusa est in cordibus nostris per Spiritum sanctum, qui datus est nobis », ad Romanos quinto[^p542-7]. Hic est ignis, de quo Lucae duodecimo: « Veni ignem mittere in terram ». — In specie *linguae* apparuit, quia ipse immittit ori doctrinam veritatis; unde Ioannis decimo sexto: « Cum venerit Spiritus veritatis, docebit vos omnem veritatem ». Hic est enim ille prolocutor, de quo Matthaei decimo: « Non enim vos estis, qui loquimini, sed Spiritus Patris vestri, qui loquitur in vobis ». — In specie *flaminis* sive spiritus apparuit, quia subministrat operi *vigorem virtutis;* unde ad Romanos octavo[^p542-8]: « Ipse Spiritus adiuvat infirmitatem nostram »; et in Psalmo: « Verbo Domini caeli », id est viri caelestes, « firmati sunt, et spiritu oris eius omnis virtus eorum ». — Spiritus enim sanctus est *ignis* in corde, *veritas* in ore vel sermone et *virtus* in opere; et ideo apparet in specie *ignis, linguae* et *flaminis* sive spiritus.

5. Sequitur: *Et manentem.* — Spiritus sanctus non solum *descendit* et *apparet*, sed etiam *manet.* Manet autem Deus non ubique per gratiam, sed ubi *diligitur*, ubi *honoratur* et ubi *invitatur.* — Ma-
<!-- page 543 -->
net, inquam, ubi *diligitur;* unde primae Ioannis quarto[^p543-1]: « Qui manet in caritate in Deo manet, et Deus in eo »; non, ubi *ignoratur;* unde Ioannis decimo quarto: « Ego rogabo Patrem, et alium Paracletum dabit vobis, ut maneat vobiscum in aeternum, Spiritum veritatis, quem mundus non potest accipere, quia non videt eum nec scit eum. Vos autem cognoscetis eum, quia apud vos manebit et in vobis erit ». — Manet etiam, ubi *honoratur;* unde Ioannis decimo quarto[^p543-2]: « Si quis diligit me, sermonem meum servabit »; ecce, honorificatio; « et ad eum veniemus et mansionem apud eum faciemus »; non, *ubi negligitur*, quia, sicut dicitur Sapientiae primo, « spiritus sanctus disciplinae effugiet fictum ». — Manet etiam, ubi *invitatur*, sicut et ipse Salvator mansit cum discipulis invitantibus ipsum: « Et coegerunt illum dicentes: Mane nobiscum, Domine »; et sequitur: « Et intravit cum illis », Lucae ultimo[^p543-3]. Ita facit Spiritus sanctus; unde Sapientiae septimo: « Propterea optavi, et datus est mihi sensus; et invocavi, et venit in me spiritus sapientiae ». — Si volumus ergo, ut maneat nobiscum Spiritus, oportet, ut *diligamus* eum per caritatem, *honoremus* eum per obedientiam, *invitemus* per orationem.

### Collatio VI.

(Vers. 29.). *Ecce, Agnus Dei; ecce, qui tollit peccata mundi.*

1. In verbis istis demonstratur nobis Salvator ex duobus: ex *personae gratiositate*, cum *dicitur: Agnus Dei;* ex *effectus utilitate*, cum dicitur: *Ecce, qui tollit peccata mundi.* Ex primo apparet *dulcis*, ex secundo *utilis*, et ideo amabilis, quia, sicut dicitur[^p543-4],

Omne tulit punctum qui miscuit utile dulci.

2. Dicit ergo: *Ecce, Agnus Dei.* Nota, quod Christus fuit agnus *immaculatus*, agnus *anniculus*, agnus *mansuetus; immaculatus* per *puritatem incorruptionis, anniculus* per *humilitatem conversationis, mansuetus* ad *tolerantiam passionis.* — Ecce ergo, *agnus immaculatus*, in cuius figura dicitur Exodi duodecimo[^p543-5]: « Erit autem agnus absque macula ». — Ecce, *anniculus*, propter quod ibi subiungitur: *masculus anniculus*, et idem figuratur Levitici primo: « Si de pecoribus oblatio est etc., agnum anniculum, immaculatum offeret Domino ». — Ecce, *agnus mansuetus;* Ieremiae undecimo[^p543-6]: « Ego quasi agnus mansuetus, qui portatur ad victimam »; Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio: « Quasi agnus coram tondente se obmutescet ». — Agnus *humilis* docet ordinari ad *Deum;* agnus *mansuetus*, ad *proximum;* agnus *immaculatus*, ad *corpus proprium.*

3. *Ecce, Agnus Dei*, qui datus est nobis in *pretium redemptionis*, in *cibum refectionis*, in *solatium exilii* et peregrinationis. Eramus enim *exules*, sed ipse venit in *solatium;* eramus *inopes*, ipse se dedit in *cibum;* eramus *captivi*, ipse se obtulit in *pretium.* — Est igitur in *pretium redemptionis;* primae Petri primo[^p543-7]: « Non corruptibilibus auro vel argento redempti estis etc., sed pretioso sanguine quasi agni incontaminati Iesu Christi ». — In *cibum refectionis;* Exodi duodecimo de esu agni paschalis: « Non comedetis ex eo crudum quid, nec coctum aqua, sed tantum assum igni ». — In *solatium peregrinationis;* Apocalypsis septimo: « Non esurient neque sitient amplius etc., quia Agnus, qui in medio throni est, reget eos et deducet eos ad fontes vitae aquarum ».

4. *Ecce, qui tollit peccata mundi.* Nota, quod Christus peccata *abluit, tollit* et *remittit.* In peccato enim est considerare *maculam, reatum* et *offensam*[^p543-8]. Macula *abominabilis* est, et ideo indiget *lavari.* Reatus *ad poenam intolerabilem obligat*, et ideo indiget *tolli.* Offensa *inexcusabilis*, et ideo indiget *remitti.* — Sed ecce, qui peccata *abluit;* Apocalypsis primo[^p543-9]: « Dilexit nos et lavit nos a peccatis nostris in sanguine suo ». — Peccata *tollit;* unde hic: *Ecce, Agnus* etc.; et Isaiae quinquagesimo tertio: « Iniquitates multorum tulit »; et supra eodem: « Vere dolores nostros ipse tulit et languores nostros ipse portavit ». — Peccata *dimittit;* Lucae septimo: « Remittuntur tibi peccata tua »; sequitur: « Quis est hic, qui peccata dimittit »?

### Collatio VII.

(Vers. 51.). *Videbitis caelum apertum.*

1. Nota, quod caelum aliquando aperitur ad *mittendum exenia*[^p543-10] *beneficiorum* — ad *ostendendum mysteria secretorum* — ad *recipiendum in gaudia*
<!-- page 544 -->
*praemiorum.* — Caelum aperit Deus *incipientibus* ad *emittendum;* quod figuratum est Matthaei tertio[^p544-1] et Lucae tertio, ubi legitur, quod baptizatus Iesus « confestim ascendit de aqua, et ecce, caeli aperti sunt, et vidit Spiritum Dei sicut columbam descendentem super se ». — Caelum aperit Deus ad *ostendendum* quae sunt in caelo, et sic apertum est Ioanni Apocalypsis undecimo: « Apertum est templum Dei in caelo, et visa est arca testamenti eius in templo eius ». — Caelum aperit Deus ad *recipiendum*, et sic apertum est Stephano, qui vidit caelos apertos, « vidit et introivit »; de qua aperitione Actuum septimo: « Ecce, video caelos apertos et Iesum stantem a dextris Dei ».

2. Notandum autem, quod tripliciter meretur quis invenire caelum apertum: *offerendo — pulsando* — et *properando.* — Solet enim libenter aperiri *offerentibus;* et ideo dicitur Malachiae tertio[^p544-2]: « Inferte omnem decimam in horreum meum, ut sit cibus in domo mea, et probate me super hoc, dicit Dominus: Si non aperuero vobis cataractas caeli et effudero vobis benedictionem usque ad abundantiam ». — Aperitur etiam *pulsantibus;* Matthaei septimo: « Pulsate, et aperietur vobis »; et ibidem: « Pulsanti aperietur ». — Aperitur nihilominus *properantibus;* properantes enim inveniunt ostium *apertum*, quod tardantes inveniunt *clausum;* ideo dicitur Lucae decimo tertio[^p544-3]: « Contendite intrare per angustam portam, quia dico vobis, multi quaerent intrare et non poterunt. Cum autem intraverit paterfamilias et clauserit ostium, incipietis foris stare et pulsare » etc.; et Matthaei vigesimo quinto habetur de virginibus, quod illae « quae paratae erant, intraverunt cum eo ad nuptias ». Novissimis vero responsum est: « Nescio vos ». — Cogitemus ergo *offerre* munus bonae operationis et *pulsare* per instantiam orationis et *properare* per fervorem aemulationis; et *caelum aperietur nobis.*

3. Nota de hoc quod dictum est supra[^p544-4], quod ad *emittendum* est apertum caelum Christo intranti, ad *ostendendum* Ioanni contemplanti, ad *recipiendum* Stephano patienti.

### Annotatio 1.

(Vers. 3.). *Omnia per ipsum facta sunt.* — Notandum, quod est verbum *faciens*, verbum *factum* et verbum *fiens.* — Verbum *faciens* est Verbum ab aeterno genitum, de quo hic: *Omnia per ipsum facta sunt.* — Verbum *factum* est verbum menti interius impressum, de quo Lucae tertio[^p544-5]: « Factum est verbum Domini super Ioannem, Zachariae filium, in deserto ». — Verbum *fiens* est verbum vocaliter prolatum, quod tantum est in *fieri;* de quo dicit Damascenus, quod verbum nostrum est anypostatum, id est non subsistens et in aërem effusum. De hoc dicitur[^p544-6], quia « volat irrevocabile verbum ».

### Annotatio 2.

(Vers. 6.). *Fuit homo missus a Deo* etc. — Nota, quod Ioannes fuit missus ante Dominum[^p544-7], ut *praeco* ad annuntiandum, ut *praecursor* ad iter praeparandum, ut *nuntius* ad hospitium accipiendum. — De primo habetur hic[^p544-8]: *Fuit homo missus a Deo, cui nomen erat Ioannes. Hic venit in testimonium, ut testimonium perhiberet de lumine.* — De secundo, Malachiae tertio: « Ecce, ego mitto angelum meum, qui praeparabit viam ante faciem meam ». — De tertio, Lucae primo: « Ipse praecedet ante illum in spiritu et virtute Eliae, ut convertat corda patrum in filios et incredulos ad prudentiam iustorum » etc. — Ioannes *praeconizavit* praedicando, *iter praeparavit* baptizando, *hospitium accepit* corda hominum ad fidem Christi convertendo.

### Annotatio 3.

(Vers. 23.). *Ego vox clamantis in deserto.* — Nota, quod Ioannes aliquando dictus est *propheta*, aliquando *vox*, aliquando *lucerna.* Prophetae est *cognoscere veritatem;* vocis est *promere verbum;* lucernae est *ostendere lucem.* — Beatus Ioannes *propheta* fuit, quia cognovit veritatem divina revelatione; Matthaei undecimo[^p544-9], Lucae septimo: « Etiam dico vobis, et plus
<!-- page 545 -->
quam propheta ». — Fuit iterum *vox*, quia veritatem protulit in praedicatione; unde Ioannis primo: « Ego vox clamantis in deserto: Dirigite viam Domini » etc. — Fuit *lucerna*, quia veritatem ostendit in conversatione, secundum quod dicitur Matthaei quinto: « Sic luceat lux vestra coram hominibus, ut videant opera vestra bona ». De ipso dicitur Ioannis quinto: « Ipse erat lucerna ardens et lucens »: *ardens* in affectu per caritatem, *lucens* in effectu per sanctam operationem. Fuit ergo *propheta*, quia *cognitor* veritatis; *vox*, quia *praedicator* veritatis; *lucerna*, quia *operator* veritatis.

Iterum, *ego vox clamantis in deserto*[^p545-1]. — Nota, quod ideo Ioannes dicitur *vox*, quia *vox praecedit* verbum, *vox manifestat* verbum, *transit* et relinquit verbum. — Ita et Ioannes *praecessit* Christum; Lucae primo: « Ipse praeibit ante illum ». *Manifestavit* Christum; Ioannis primo: « Ecce, Agnus Dei ». *Transivit* et reliquit Christum; unde Ioannis tertio: « Illum oportet crescere, me autem minui ».

### Collations on the Gospel of St. John[^p535-1].

### Chapter I. *In the beginning was the Word.*

### Collation I.

(Vers. 5.). John, the first chapter: *The light shineth in darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it.*

1. *Morally* in these words two things are touched upon. First, *there is commended to us*[^p535-2] *the presence of the divine brightness*, when it is said: *The light shineth in darkness.* — Second, *there is reproved in us the ignorance of human blindness*, when it is said: *And the darkness did not comprehend it.*

Concerning the first it is to be considered *what* this light *is* — and what is *its effect*.

This light « is the true light, which enlighteneth every man that cometh into this world », below in the same[^p535-3], namely Christ, who is light both as to the nature of *Divinity* and as to the nature of *humanity*. As to the nature of *Divinity he illumines inwardly* man or the soul; as to the nature of *humanity he informs outwardly* the life.

2. Christ therefore is light according as he is *man;* of which John, the eighth chapter[^p535-4]: « I am come a light into the world; he that followeth me walketh not in darkness » etc. This refers to the humanity; for one does not follow in him the excellence of God, but the humility of man. — Now according as he is man he is a light *reproving*, a light *directing*, a light *delighting: reproving* the wicked, *directing* the just, *delighting* the Blessed. — *He reproves the wicked;* to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter[^p535-5]: « For all things that are reproved are made manifest by the lumen »; and therefore he is hated by the wicked; John, the third chapter: « Every one that doth evil hateth the light ». — *He directs the just;* John, the twelfth chapter: « Walk whilst you have the light, that the darkness overtake you not »; Luke, the first chapter: « The Orient from on high hath visited us, to enlighten them that sit in darkness » etc., « to di-
<!-- page 536 -->
rect our feet into the way of peace ». — *He delights the Blessed;* Apocalypse, the twenty-first chapter: « That city hath no need of the sun nor of the moon; for the brightness of God hath enlightened it, and the Lamb is the lamp thereof ».

3. *The light shineth in darkness.* This light, as has been said, *shines outwardly* according to the operations of the *human* nature; likewise it *shines inwardly* according to the operations of the *divine* nature. — Now it shines in three ways according to the operations of the human nature[^p536-1], namely by *signs, words* and *examples:* in *signs* shines his power, in *words* his wisdom, goodness in *examples.*

It shines therefore in *signs;* the Psalm[^p536-2]: « Thy lightnings enlightened the world », the lightnings, I say, of miracles, which it saw, and « the earth was moved ». — It shines also in *words;* John, the twelfth chapter: « Whilst you have the light, believe in the light, that you may be the children of light » eternal. — It shines also in *examples;* Habacuc, the third chapter: « His brightness shall be as the light; horns are in his hands ». « His brightness shall be as the light », in his conversation; « horns in his hands », in his passion. Therefore John, the eighth chapter: « He that followeth me walketh not in darkness ».

4. *The light shineth in darkness.* Christ again is light according as he is *God;* of which John, the twelfth chapter[^p536-3]: « I am come a light into the world, that whosoever believeth in me may not remain in darkness »; which refers to the Divinity; for we *believe* in Christ *the Lord*, not in *man* according as he is man.

Note, however, that light has *purity* in its matter, *comeliness* in its form, *sublimity* in its site. But Christ according as he is God is *the most high light, the most pure light, the most comely light.* Height bears witness to *power*, comeliness to *wisdom*, purity to *goodness.* — Christ therefore is the *most high* light; the first to Timothy, the last chapter[^p536-4]: « He inhabiteth light inaccessible ». The *most comely* light; Wisdom, the seventh chapter: « She is more comely than the sun, and above all the order of the stars; being compared with the light, she is found before it ». The *most pure* light; the first of John, the first chapter: « God is light, and in him there is no darkness at all ».

5. This light shines *inwardly* according to the operations of the *divine* nature. Now it sends forth inwardly a threefold light or lumen[^tr-lumen]: the lumen of *knowledge;* the Psalm[^p536-5]: « Send forth thy light and thy truth »; likewise: « Enlighten my eyes, that I never sleep in death ». — The lumen of *grace;* Isaias, the fifty-eighth chapter: « Thy light shall rise up in darkness »; and the sixtieth: « Arise, be enlightened, O Jerusalem, for thy lumen is come ». — The lumen of *spiritual gladness;* the Psalm: « The lumen of thy countenance, O Lord, is signed upon us; thou hast given gladness in my heart »; against which it is said in Lamentations, the third chapter: « The Lord hath led me, and brought me into darkness, and not into light ».

6. There follows: *And the darkness did not comprehend it;* where *the ignorance of human blindness is reproved.* Concerning which it is to be considered what *this darkness* is — and what *their defect* is.

*This darkness* is sinners, who *are in darkness* — and *seek darkness* — and *tend to darkness.* They are in *spiritual* darkness, they seek *temporal* darkness and they tend to *eternal* darkness. — *They are in darkness;* the Psalm[^p536-6]: « They have not known nor understood; they walk on in darkness »; Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter: « Error and darkness are created together with sinners ». — *They seek darkness;* John, the third chapter: « Men loved darkness rather than the light; for their works were evil ». — *They tend to darkness;* in the Canonical Epistle of Jude: « To whom the storm of darkness is reserved for ever »; Job, the tenth chapter: « Suffer me, that I may lament my sorrow a little, before I go and return no more, to a land that is dark and covered with the mist of death ». — These, therefore, *are darkness.*

But in the *comprehension* of the *light* they fail, because *turned away;* the Psalm[^p536-7]: « They turned away, and kept not the covenant »; because *covered;* the Psalm: « They are covered with their iniquity and their wickedness »; because *blind;* Matthew, the fifteenth chapter: « Let them alone; they are blind, and leaders of the blind ». They *are turned away* by deserting God, they *are covered* by working sin, they *are blinded* by persevering in sin.

7. Otherwise. *The darkness did not comprehend it.* It is to be noted that there is a darkness of *fault*, a darkness of *misery*, a darkness of *nature.* — Of the *darkness of fault*, Ecclesiasticus, the eleventh chapter[^p536-8]: « Error and darkness are created together with sinners. » — Of the *darkness of misery*, Tobias, the fifth chapter: « What manner of joy shall be to me, who sit in darkness, and see not the lumen of heaven »? — The *darkness of nature* is the natural defects of the creature, of which the first of John, the first chapter: « God is light, and in him there is no darkness at all », that is, no defect at all.

8. Likewise note that *comprehension* is threefold: by *inclusion* or by commensuration; likewise by *open vision;* likewise by *the adhesion of faith and charity.* — In the first mode no darkness ever comprehends God, because no nature in any state can ever be commensurated to God. Whence Damascene[^p536-9]: « God is infinite and incomprehensible »; and
<!-- page 537 -->
Ecclesiasticus, the forty-third chapter: « Labour not, for you shall not comprehend ». — In the first mode and the second the darkness of *misery* does not comprehend him, because no wretched one sees God by open vision; to the Philippians, the third chapter[^p537-1]: « I do not count myself to have comprehended ». — In the first mode and the second and the third the *darkness of fault* does not comprehend him. For the sinful man neither *includes* God, because he is a creature; nor *sees* God, because he is wretched; nor *adheres* to him *through faith and charity*, because he is a sinner. Whence it is said here that *the darkness did not comprehend it;* and to the Ephesians, the third chapter: « Being rooted and founded in charity, that you may be able to comprehend with all the Saints »; because otherwise you could not know; « what is the length », namely of eternity, « the breadth », namely of immensity, « the height », namely of majesty, « and the depth », namely of wisdom and truth.

### Collation II.

(Vers. 14.). *The Word was made flesh and dwelt in us.*

1. In these words there is expressed to us a twofold indication of charity. The first is in this, that our Lord shows himself to us as a *brother.* — The second in this, that he shows himself to us as *familiar.* — The first the Lord did by assuming flesh, which is noted when it is said: *The Word was made flesh;* the second, by dwelling in us, which is noted when it is said: *And dwelt in us.* For we were *abject;* but God applies a remedy against abjection, when he shows himself a *brother.* We were *desolate;* but God applies a remedy against desolation, when he shows himself to us *familiar.*

2. To our *abjection*, therefore, there is a remedy, because *the Word was made flesh.* But *what manner of Word* — and *how was it made flesh?*

The Word, I say, *most high in majesty, immense in power, eternal in duration.* — It is *most high in majesty;* for[^p537-2] « the Word was with God, and the Word was God »; therefore Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter: « I dwelt in the highest places » etc.; and Wisdom, the eighteenth chapter: « Thy almighty word, O Lord, came from the royal seats ». — It is *immense in power*, because « all things were made by him », John, the first chapter; likewise in the Psalm: « By the word of the Lord the heavens were established » etc.; to the Hebrews, the first chapter: « Upholding all things by the word of his power ». — It is *eternal in duration*, because « the Word was in the beginning with God »; the Psalm: « For ever, O Lord, thy Word standeth firm in heaven ».

3. This Word, I say, *was made flesh*, namely *united to flesh, contained in flesh, born and having suffered in flesh.* — The Word *is united to flesh*, and behold, *humble majesty;* to the Philippians, the second chapter[^p537-3]: « Who being in the form of God etc., emptied himself, taking the form of a servant » etc. This humiliation of majesty the shepherds saw, Luke, the second chapter, since « seeing, they knew of the word that had been spoken to them concerning this child »; for they knew the Word of God laid in the manger, which had been spoken to them concerning the child.

4. The Word *is contained in flesh*, and behold, *brief immensity;* to the Romans, the ninth chapter[^p537-4]: « An abbreviated word shall the Lord make upon the earth »; truly *abbreviated*, because by position that was made *brief* which was by nature extended, *brief* in the manger which was everywhere, that is to say, immense.

5. The Word *is born in flesh*, and behold, *temporal eternity;* to the Galatians, the fourth chapter[^p537-5]: « When the fulness of the time was come, God sent his Son, made of a woman, made under the Law ». If *made:* therefore *temporal.* This is the Word made, of which Luke, the second chapter: « Let us go over to Bethlehem, and let us see this word which is made » etc. *Let us see*, I say, *the Word made* according to the flesh, we who could never see *the Word* which according to the Divinity is neither made nor created, but begotten. — The Psalm: « Sing ye, he says, to the Lord, for he hath done wonderful things ». For he brought it about that there should be *majesty made abject*, and *immensity small, eternity made;* the whole of which the Evangelist records when he says: *The Word was made flesh.*

6. There follows: *And dwelt in us; in us*, that is, in our *humanity* — or: *in us*, that is, in our *society* — or: *in us*, that is, in our *mind.* In our *humanity* he dwelt *bodily*, in our *society* he dwelt *sacramentally*, in our *mind spiritually.* — The Word dwells therefore in our *humanity;* of which the third of Kings, the eighth chapter[^p537-6]: « The Lord said that he would dwell in a cloud », the *cloud*, the humanity assumed, from which flowed forth the medicine of all. — He dwells in our *society sacramentally;* of which Matthew, the last chapter: « I am with you even to the consummation of the world »; and the Psalm: « In peace », that is, in the concord of brothers, « his place was made »; « in Sion », that is, in the Church, « his abode ». — He dwells in our *mind spiritually;* John, the fifteenth chapter: « Abide in me, and I in you »; the second of Machabees, the fourteenth chapter: « Thou, O Lord of all things, who wantest nothing, wast pleased that the temple of thy habitation should be in us ».
<!-- page 538 -->

7. Or otherwise: *He dwelt in us*, that is, *within us;* where it can be noted that the Lord dwells *within us* for *sanctifying, among us* for *ruling, around us* for *protecting.* — We are *besieged*, but the Lord dwells *around us* for *protecting;* therefore « he shall not be moved for ever that dwelleth in Jerusalem, because the Lord is round about his people », according to what is said in the Psalm[^p538-1]; and Isaias, the twenty-sixth chapter: « The saviour shall be set in it », that is, in the Church, « a wall and a bulwark ». — We are *ignorant*, and he dwells *among us* for *ruling;* Apocalypse, the twenty-first chapter: « Behold the tabernacle of God with men, and he will dwell with them; and they shall be his people, and God himself with them shall be their God ». — We are *wretched*, but he dwells *within us* for *consoling* and *sanctifying;* of which can be understood what was had above from the second of Machabees, the fourteenth chapter, and what is said here: *He dwelt in us.*

### Collation III.

(Vers. 16.). *Of his fulness we all have received.*

1. It is commonly said that he whose head is sick, the other members ache; and conversely it can be said: he whose head is sound, the other members flourish[^p538-2]. For the good disposition of the head is the invigoration of all the members, and the abundance of the head the relief of the body. According to these two things there are noted in these words: *the abundance of the head*, when it is said: *Of his fulness*, that is, of Christ, who is the head of the Church — and *the relief of the body*, when it is said: *We all have received*, namely we who are the body of the Church. For there flows, in a certain spiritual manner, from Christ the head into us, who are his members, spiritual motion and sense; for it is, according to what is said in the Psalm[^p538-3], « like the ointment on the head, that ran down upon the beard, the beard etc. and even to the skirt of his garment », to which what is said here bears witness: *Of his fulness we all have received.*

2. *Of his fulness.* It is to be noted that fulness is fourfold, namely of *sufficiency, abundance, excellence* and *overflowing.* — The fulness of *sufficiency* is found in any *just* man; of which the Psalm[^p538-4]: « Thou openest thy hand and fillest every living creature with blessing ». — The fulness of *abundance* is found in any *perfect* man, according to what is read in Acts, the sixth chapter: « Stephen, full of grace »; and Acts, the second chapter: « They were all filled with the Holy Spirit » etc. — The fulness of *excellence* is found in the most blessed *Virgin*, to whom it was said, Luke, the first chapter[^p538-5]: « Hail, full of grace »; treating of which fulness, blessed Bernard says: « We read that Stephen was *full of grace*, that the Apostles were *filled* with the Holy Spirit, but far otherwise in Mary. For the fulness of the Divinity did not dwell bodily in them, as in Mary ». — The fulness of *overflowing* is found in *Christ*, of whom, according to what is said here, *we all have received.* Whence he himself also says in Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter: « Come over to me, all ye that desire me, and be filled with my fruits ».

3. Now there was in Christ a fourfold fulness, namely of *wisdom — clemency — power* — and *glory.* The fulness of *wisdom* appeared in his *preaching*, the fulness of *clemency* in his *passion*, the fulness of *power* in his *resurrection*, the fulness of *glory* in his *ascension.* — He was therefore full of *wisdom;* Luke, the second chapter[^p538-6]: « And the child grew and waxed strong, full of wisdom, and the grace of God was in him ». — He was full of *clemency;* whence there befits him what is said in Esther, the fifteenth chapter: « Thou art very admirable, O Lord, and thy face is full of graces ». *Thou* indeed *art admirable, O Lord*, by *creating* all things, and *thy face*, O Christ, *appeared full of graces*, by *re-creating* all things. For he himself is the *wisdom* « that is from above, which is full of mercy and good works », according to what is said in James, the third chapter. — He was full of *power* and might, in whose person it is said in Micheas, the third chapter[^p538-7]: « I am filled with the strength of the Spirit of the Lord, with judgment and power ». — He was full of *glory;* whence to the Colossians, the second chapter: « In him dwelt the fulness of the Divinity bodily ».

4. *Wisdom*, as has been said, appeared in his *preaching;* for he taught them « as one having power » etc., according to what is said in Matthew, the seventh chapter[^p538-8]. — *Clemency* in his *passion;* for then it lay open that « with the Lord there is mercy, and with him plentiful redemption », according to what is said in the Psalm. — *Might* in his *resurrection;* for then there was given « to him all power », that is, it was manifested as given, according to what is said in Matthew, the last chapter. — *Glory* in his *ascension;* for then « we saw his glory, the glory as it were of the Only-begotten of the Father », John, the first chapter, when indeed « he was taken up into heaven and sitteth at the right hand of the Father », according to what is said in Mark, the last chapter.
<!-- page 539 -->

5. For in this appeared the glory of the Only-begotten, that he sitteth at the right hand of God, which glory of the Only-begotten is commonly noted in four things; of which the first is that the Father has given him *all judgment*, according to what is said in John, the fifth chapter[^p539-1]: « The Father hath given all judgment to the Son ». — The second is in this, that in all things *he is like the Father.* For he is « the brightness of his glory and the figure of his substance », according to what is said to the Hebrews, the first chapter. — The third is that *he constituted him head over all the Church*, according to what is said to the Ephesians, the first chapter: « He hath subjected all things under his feet, and hath made him head over all the Church ». — The fourth is that he gave him *to sit at his right hand*, according to what is had, as has been said, in Mark, the last chapter.

These four properly befit Christ and befit no other creature; whence the verses:

Judgment, parity, precedence, session at the right hand  
Are the surpassing glory of the Only-begotten Son.

6. *Of his fulness we all have received.* — Of the fulness of *wisdom* we have received *the teaching*[^tr-doctrina] *of instruction;* Apocalypse, the third chapter[^p539-2]: « Have in mind in what manner thou hast received and heard, and observe, and do penance »; in John, the seventeenth chapter the Truth says: « The words which thou gavest me, I have given to them », namely to the disciples; « and they have received them, and have known in very deed that I came out from thee »; *I have given*, by teaching, and *they have received*, by believing. — Of the fulness of *clemency* we have received *the grace of adoption;* to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p539-3]: « You have not received the spirit of bondage again in fear, but you have received the spirit of adoption of sons » etc. — Of the fulness of *might* we have received *the power of protection*, which he himself promised to the Apostles, Acts, the first chapter: « You shall receive the power of the Holy Spirit coming upon you, and you shall be witnesses unto me » etc. — Of the fulness of *glory* we have received *the reward of eternal retribution;* Wisdom, the fifth chapter: « They shall receive a kingdom of glory, and a crown of beauty at the hand of the Lord »; and Mark, the tenth chapter: « There is no man who hath left house, or brethren etc., who shall not receive a hundred times as much in this world, and in the world to come life everlasting ».

Thus, therefore, we have received from Christ *the teaching of instruction, the grace of adoption, the power of protection, the glory* or reward *of retribution.* The first *illumines the understanding;* the second *informs the affection;* the third *strengthens the effect;* the fourth *fulfils the desire.*

7. Now it is to be noted that *the reckoning of what has been received* obliges us to four things: to *the giving of thanks* — to *humiliation of mind* — to *progress in virtue* — and *distribution of the things received.* The giving of thanks *obtains*, humiliation of mind *preserves*, exercise of virtue *enriches*, distribution of the things received *enlarges.*

We are obliged to *the giving of thanks*, according to what is said in the first of Paralipomenon, the twenty-ninth chapter[^p539-4]: « All things are thine, O Lord, and we have given thee what we received of thy hand ». — To *humiliation of mind;* the first to the Corinthians, the fourth chapter: « What hast thou that thou hast not received? And if thou hast received, why dost thou glory, as if thou hadst not received it »? — To *progress and exercise in virtue;* the second to the Corinthians, the sixth chapter: « We exhort you that you receive not the grace of God in vain »; and the first to the Thessalonians, the fourth chapter: « We pray and beseech you in the Lord Jesus Christ, that as you have received from us, how you ought to walk and to please God, so also you would walk, that you may abound the more ». — To *distribution of the things received;* the first of Peter, the fourth chapter: « As every man hath received grace, ministering the same one to another, as good stewards of the manifold grace of God »; and Matthew, the tenth chapter: « Freely have you received, freely give ».

8. *Of his fulness we all have received. We*, I say, *all*, who have received anything; let this be said for this reason, that of his fulness some *receive and are enriched* — but some *do not merit to receive* of his fulness. — For four kinds of men receive *of his fulness*, namely *the innocent, the obedient, the combatants* and *the contemplatives.* For it is fitting that from the hand of the father of the family there receive *sons, servants, soldiers* and *friends.* By *sons* understand *the innocent*, who keep the image of the father undefiled; and these merit to receive from the father's hand, according to what is said in the Psalm[^p539-5]: « The innocent in hands and clean of heart, he shall receive a blessing from the Lord ». — By *servants* understand *the obedient*, who fulfil the commandments of the Lord; and these merit to receive, according to what is said in the first to the Corinthians, the third chapter: « Every man » shall receive « his own reward, according to his own labour ». — By *soldiers* understand *the combatants*, who wage the wars of the king; and these merit to receive, according to what is had in James, the first chapter: « Blessed is the man that endureth temptation; for when he hath been proved, he shall receive the crown of life, which God hath promised to them that love him ». — By *friends* understand *the contemplatives*, who as it were sleep with the bridegroom in the bridal chamber; and these merit to receive what they ask of him, according to what is said in Matthew, the seventh chapter: « He that asketh receiveth, and he that seeketh findeth »; and in John, the sixteenth chapter the Saviour says to his friends: « Hitherto
<!-- page 540 -->
you have not asked any thing in my name. Ask, and you shall receive, that your joy may be full ».

9. These receive and are enriched; but there are others who *do not merit to receive.* For it is not fitting that the father of the family bestow on *the unknown, the ignorant* and *the unworthy*, or *the ungrateful.* — *Unknown* to the Lord are those who on account of temporal things forget him, as worldly men; and these do not receive, according to what is had in John, the fourteenth chapter[^p540-1]: « I, says the Truth, will ask the Father, and he shall give you another Paraclete etc.; whom the world cannot receive, because it seeth him not nor knoweth him »; therefore it *knoweth* not God, because it thinks only of worldly things and forgets him. — *Ignorant* or foolish are those who pursue concupiscences; and these do not merit to receive, because they would at once throw what was received into the mire. To these it is said in James, the fourth chapter[^p540-2]: « You ask, and receive not, because you ask amiss, that you may consume it on your concupiscences ». — *Unworthy* are those who ask hesitating, because they distrust the largess of God, but neither do these merit to receive; whence James, the first chapter: « He that wavereth is like a wave of the sea, which is moved and carried about by the wind; therefore let not that man think that he shall receive any thing of the Lord ». — *Ungrateful* are those for whom the things received *do not suffice*, like those of whom in Matthew, the twentieth chapter[^p540-3], who « receiving every man a penny, murmured against the father of the family »; and these do not merit to receive more, but rather to lose; for they merit, as is touched upon there, to be *last.* *Ungrateful* also are those who *do not serve* the Lord with the things received, like the slothful servant, who did not multiply the pound he had received, and therefore merited to lose what he had received, according to what is said in Luke, the nineteenth chapter: « Take the pound away from him, and give it to him that hath ten pounds ».

10. Let us therefore show ourselves *known* to God by frequently returning to him, and show ourselves *knowing* by declining concupiscences, and show ourselves *worthy* by asking faithfully, and show ourselves *grateful* to him by faithfully serving him with the things received; and thus we shall be able *to receive of his fulness.*

### Collation IV.

(Vers. 26.). *In the midst of you there hath stood one, whom you know not.*

1. In these words two things are touched upon. The first is *the compassion of the divine piety*, which was shown to us when Christ descended to us; and this is touched upon when it is said: *In the midst of you there hath stood one.* — The second is *the ingratitude of human blindness*, which was manifested in this, that « the world knew him not[^p540-4] »; and this is touched upon when it is said: *Whom you know not.*

2. He says therefore: *In the midst of you* etc. Note that sometimes Christ is read as *first*, sometimes as *in the midst*, sometimes as *last.* And in truth Christ showed himself *first* for leading forth, *last* for protecting, *in the midst* for gathering together. — He was *first* for *leading forth;* whence Micheas, the second chapter[^p540-5]: « He shall go up before them opening the way »; and after: « Their king shall pass before them, and the Lord at the head of them »; Isaias, the forty-first chapter: « The first shall say to Sion: Behold they are here, and to Jerusalem I will give an evangelist ». — He was *last* for *protecting;* Isaias, the fifty-third chapter[^p540-6]: « We have desired him, despised and the last of men »; Jerome: « *The last*, that he might receive the blows of the persecutors ». Whence it is subjoined below in the same: « Surely he hath borne our infirmities and carried our sorrows ». — He was *in the midst* for *gathering together*, that the faithful might be gathered together to him, as of old the children of Israel to the tabernacle, according to what is said in Leviticus, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p540-7]: « I will set my tabernacle in the midst of you, and my soul shall not cast you off any more ». Whence also it is said here: *In the midst of you there hath stood one.*

3. These three were figured in the cloud, which sometimes *went before* the children of Israel for *the leadership of direction;* Numbers, the ninth chapter[^p540-8]: « When the cloud was taken up, then they marched forward »; sometimes *followed* for *the safeguard of protection;* Exodus, the fourteenth chapter: « The Angel, who went before the camp of Israel, removing, went behind them, and together with him the pillar of the cloud »; sometimes *stood* for *the sign of abiding;* Numbers, the ninth chapter: « In the place where the cloud stood still, there they camped ».

4. *In the midst*, he says, *of you.* Note that Christ was *in the midst* between man and *God* — between man and *Angel* — between man and *man.* — Between man and *God* he was in the midst, because he *reconciled* man *to God;* the first to Timothy, the second chapter[^p540-9]: « There is one mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus ». — He was in the midst between man and *Angel*, because he *made* man *at peace with the Angel;* whence, Luke, the second chapter, when Christ was born, the Angels announced
<!-- page 541 -->
that « on earth peace to men of good will »; and to the Colossians, the first chapter: « Making peace through the blood of his cross, both as to the things that are on earth and the things that are in heaven ». — He was in the midst between man and *man*, because he called men back to concord; whence it is read in Luke, the twenty-third chapter, that when Pilate had sent him to Herod, « Pilate and Herod were made friends that same day; for before they were enemies one to another ».

5. Note that Christ, existing in the midst between *God and man*, was less than God and greater than man, and therefore was as it were *under* one extreme and *over* the other; whence he was as it were *the middle* in the first figure. — But existing in the midst between *man and Angel*, he was greater than both, as king of men and of Angels; whence he was as it were *over* both extremes, and therefore was as it were *the middle* in the second figure. — But existing in the midst between *Herod and Pilate*, he showed himself less than both, as one to be judged; whence he was as it were *under* both extremes, and therefore was *the middle* in the third figure[^p541-1].

6. *In the midst*, he says, *of you.* Note that three things regard the middle: the middle is looked to by *reason, virtue* and *nature*[^p541-2]. About the middle of *reason* consists *the certitude of truth;* about the middle of *morals, the rectitude of virtue* or of salvation; about the middle of *nature, the vigour of health.* The middle of *reason orders* the extremes; the middle of *morals moderates* the extremes; the middle of *nature tempers* the extremes together.

7. Christ therefore was as it were *the rational middle*, about which is the certitude of truth; the middle, I say, *ordering the extremes.* For he, existing in the middle *between* the two testaments, ordered the old and the new and laid open the truth of each. Whence Habacuc, the third chapter[^p541-3]: « In the midst of the years », namely of the Law and of grace, « thou shalt make it known », that is, thou shalt manifest the truth; according to another letter: « In the midst of two animals », that is, of the two testaments, « thou shalt be known ». — Christ was also as it were *the moral middle*, about which is *the rectitude of virtue;* the middle, I say, *moderating the extremes;* whence in Luke, the twenty-second chapter[^p541-4], when the disciples were contending immoderately « which of them should be greater in the kingdom of heaven », Jesus showed in himself the mode of humility, showing himself *the middle* and saying: « I am in the midst of you, as he that serveth », that is, as he that stands as the lesser. Likewise Matthew, the eighteenth chapter: « Jesus calling unto him a little child, set him in the midst of them » etc. — Christ was again as it were *the natural middle*, about which is *the vigour of salvation;* whence in the Psalm[^p541-5]: « He hath wrought salvation in the midst of the earth »,

8. *In the midst*, he says, *of you.* Note that *the middle* is the place of *unity;* for the extremes are united through the middle. — Likewise, the middle is the place of *community;* for the middle belongs *to each* of the extremes. — Likewise, the middle is the place of *equality*, because the middle is equally distant from the extremes[^p541-6].

9. Christ therefore was *the middle of unity;* « for he is our peace, who hath made both one », to the Ephesians, the second chapter[^p541-7]. And this was signified in John, the twentieth chapter, when « he stood in the midst » of the disciples « and said to them: Peace be to you »; for he always calls his own to the unity of peace. — Christ again was in the middle of *community*, because he communicated his goods to all; Isaias, the fifty-fifth chapter[^p541-8]: « All you that thirst, come to the waters ». And this was figured in the Apocalypse, the twenty-second chapter, where there was shown to John a river proceeding from the seat of God and of the Lamb, « in the midst of the street of the city », not in a corner, that it might be the property of someone, but *in the midst of the street* of the city, that it might be common to all. — Christ again was in the middle of *equality;* for God accepteth not person; Acts, the tenth chapter[^p541-9]: « In very deed I perceive that God is not a respecter of persons ». To such a one it is said in Daniel, the thirteenth chapter: « Sit thou down in the midst of us, and show it us ».

10. Otherwise Christ *is noted* as *in the midst of you:* as *the heart* in the midst of the animal *for vivifying*[^p541-10]; as
<!-- page 542 -->
*the lamp* in the midst of the temple *for illuminating;* as *the pillar* in the midst of the house *for sustaining*, and things like these.

11. *In the midst of you there hath stood one.* Note that Christ *stood* in the midst, *lay* in the midst, *sat* in the midst, *hung* in the midst, *walked* in the midst. — He *lay* in the midst of animals; Habacuc, the third chapter[^p542-1]: « In the midst of two animals thou shalt be known ». In the midst of two animals he was known lying in the manger, Luke, the second chapter. — He *sat* in the midst of the doctors; Luke, the second chapter: « They found him in the temple, sitting in the midst of the doctors ». — He *hung* in the midst of thieves; John, the nineteenth chapter[^p542-2]: « They crucified him, and with him two others, one on each side, and Jesus in the midst ». — He *walked* in the midst of the candlesticks; Apocalypse, the second chapter: « These things saith he who walketh in the midst of the golden candlesticks ». — He *stood* in the midst of the disciples; John, the twentieth chapter: « Jesus stood in the midst » of his disciples « and said to them: Peace be to you ».

Note and expound that Christ lying in the midst of *animals* gives a pattern *to the innocent for relinquishing vanity.* — Christ sitting in the midst of the *doctors* gives a pattern *to those who study for inquiring after truth.* — Christ walking in the midst of the *golden candlesticks* gives a pattern *to those who make progress for guarding uprightness.* — Christ hanging in the midst of *thieves* gives a pattern *to those who suffer for sustaining adversity.* — Christ in the midst of the *disciples* gives a pattern *to those who preside for calling together their subjects to brotherhood.*

### Collation V.

(Vers. 33.). *He upon whom thou shalt see the Spirit descending, and remaining upon him, he it is that baptizeth.*

1. Notably three things are touched upon here concerning the Holy Spirit, namely that he *appears* — that he *descends* — and that he *remains.* — His *appearing* is noted in the word of *vision*, when it is said: *Upon whom thou shalt see. His descent*, when there is joined: *The Spirit descending. His remaining*, when there is added: *And remaining.* The Spirit descends in *the vivification of grace;* he appears in *the showing of good works;* but he remains in *the perseverance and continuance of the same.* These same things are noted in Acts, the second chapter[^p542-3], concerning the Holy Spirit: the *descent*, when it is said: « Suddenly there came a sound from heaven, as of a mighty wind coming ». The *appearing*, when it is said: « And there appeared to them parted tongues as it were of fire ». The *remaining*, when it is said: « And it sat upon every one of them ».

2. The divine Spirit therefore *descends* and *vivifies* the soul in his spiritual manner, as the human spirit in its natural manner vivifies the body; whence Ezechiel, the thirty-seventh chapter[^p542-4]: « Behold, I will send spirit into you, and you shall live ». — It is to be noted therefore that *the natural spirit*, vivifying, does three things: for it gives the body *sense, speech* and *motion.* So *the divine Spirit*, endowing the soul with grace, gives it *the sense of devotion, the word of edification* and *the motion of good operation.* — Of the first, to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p542-5]: « They that are according to the flesh savour the things that are of the flesh; but they that are according to the spirit sense the things that are of the spirit »; behold, that the Spirit gives *sense.* — Of the second, Acts, the second chapter: « They were all filled with the Holy Spirit, and they began to speak with divers tongues »; behold, that the Spirit gives *the word.* — Of the third, Ezechiel, the first chapter: « Whithersoever the spirit went, thither as the spirit went the wheels also were lifted up together. For the spirit of life was in the wheels ». — These are the three effects of the Spirit *descending and vivifying.*

3. The Holy Spirit not only *descends*, but *appears.* Now there are, to pass over the rest, three signs of his appearing. For he appeared over the disciples in the form of *breath* — in the form of *a tongue* — and in the form of *fire;* which three are noted in Acts, the second chapter[^p542-6]: when « there came a sound as of a mighty wind coming »; behold the first; when « there appeared parted tongues »; behold the second; « as it were of fire »; behold the third.

4. In the form of *fire* he appeared, because he kindles in the heart the ardour of charity; « for charity is poured forth in our hearts by the Holy Spirit, who is given to us », to the Romans, the fifth chapter[^p542-7]. This is the fire of which Luke, the twelfth chapter: « I am come to cast fire on the earth ». — In the form of *a tongue* he appeared, because he puts into the mouth the teaching of truth; whence John, the sixteenth chapter: « When the Spirit of truth is come, he will teach you all truth ». For this is that spokesman of whom Matthew, the tenth chapter: « For it is not you that speak, but the Spirit of your Father that speaketh in you ». — In the form of *breath* or spirit he appeared, because he supplies to the work *the vigour of power;* whence to the Romans, the eighth chapter[^p542-8]: « The Spirit himself helpeth our infirmity »; and in the Psalm: « By the word of the Lord the heavens », that is, the heavenly men, « were established, and all the power of them by the spirit of his mouth ». — For the Holy Spirit is *fire* in the heart, *truth* in the mouth or speech and *power* in the work; and therefore he appears in the form of *fire, tongue* and *breath* or spirit.

5. There follows: *And remaining.* — The Holy Spirit not only *descends* and *appears*, but also *remains.* Now God remains not everywhere through grace, but where he *is loved*, where he *is honoured* and where he *is invited.* — He re-
<!-- page 543 -->
mains, I say, where he *is loved;* whence the first of John, the fourth chapter[^p543-1]: « He that remaineth in charity remaineth in God, and God in him »; not where he *is ignored;* whence John, the fourteenth chapter: « I will ask the Father, and he shall give you another Paraclete, that he may remain with you for ever, the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it seeth him not, nor knoweth him. But you shall know him, because he shall remain with you, and shall be in you ». — He remains also where he *is honoured;* whence John, the fourteenth chapter[^p543-2]: « If any one love me, he will keep my word »; behold, the honouring; « and we will come to him, and will make our abode with him »; not *where he is neglected*, because, as is said in Wisdom, the first chapter, « the holy spirit of discipline will flee from the deceitful ». — He remains also where he *is invited*, as the Saviour himself also remained with the disciples who invited him: « And they constrained him, saying: Remain with us, Lord »; and there follows: « And he went in with them », Luke, the last chapter[^p543-3]. So does the Holy Spirit; whence Wisdom, the seventh chapter: « Wherefore I wished, and understanding was given me; and I called upon, and the spirit of wisdom came upon me ». — If therefore we wish that the Spirit remain with us, it is necessary that we *love* him through charity, *honour* him through obedience, *invite* him through prayer.

### Collation VI.

(Vers. 29.). *Behold the Lamb of God; behold him who taketh away the sins of the world.*

1. In these words the Saviour is shown to us from two things: from *the graciousness of his person*, when *it is said: The Lamb of God;* from *the usefulness of his effect*, when it is said: *Behold him who taketh away the sins of the world.* From the first he appears *sweet*, from the second *useful*, and therefore lovable, because, as it is said[^p543-4],

He has carried every point who has mingled the useful with the sweet.

2. He says therefore: *Behold the Lamb of God.* Note that Christ was a lamb *immaculate*, a lamb *of one year*, a lamb *meek; immaculate* through *the purity of incorruption, of one year* through *the humility of his conversation, meek* for *the endurance of the passion.* — Behold therefore *the immaculate lamb*, in whose figure it is said in Exodus, the twelfth chapter[^p543-5]: « And the lamb shall be without blemish ». — Behold, *of one year*, on account of which it is subjoined there: *a male of one year*, and the same is figured in Leviticus, the first chapter: « If the offering be of the flocks etc., he shall offer to the Lord a lamb of one year, immaculate ». — Behold, *the meek lamb;* Jeremias, the eleventh chapter[^p543-6]: « I was as a meek lamb, that is carried to be a victim »; Isaias, the fifty-third chapter: « As a lamb before his shearer he shall be dumb ». — The *humble* lamb teaches to be ordered to *God;* the *meek* lamb, to *the neighbour;* the *immaculate* lamb, to *one's own body.*

3. *Behold the Lamb of God*, who has been given to us as *the price of redemption*, as *the food of refection*, as *the solace of exile* and pilgrimage. For we were *exiles*, but he came as *solace;* we were *needy*, he gave himself as *food;* we were *captives*, he offered himself as *price.* — He is therefore as *the price of redemption;* the first of Peter, the first chapter[^p543-7]: « You were not redeemed with corruptible things as gold or silver etc., but with the precious blood as of a lamb undefiled, Jesus Christ ». — As *the food of refection;* Exodus, the twelfth chapter, concerning the eating of the paschal lamb: « You shall not eat thereof any thing raw, nor boiled in water, but only roasted at the fire ». — As *the solace of pilgrimage;* Apocalypse, the seventh chapter: « They shall no more hunger nor thirst etc., for the Lamb, which is in the midst of the throne, shall rule them, and shall lead them to the fountains of the waters of life ».

4. *Behold him who taketh away the sins of the world.* Note that Christ *washes away, takes away* and *remits* sins. For in sin is to be considered *the stain, the guilt* and *the offence*[^p543-8]. The stain is *abominable*, and therefore needs *to be washed.* The guilt *binds to an intolerable punishment*, and therefore needs *to be taken away.* The offence is *inexcusable*, and therefore needs *to be remitted.* — But behold him who *washes away* sins; Apocalypse, the first chapter[^p543-9]: « He hath loved us and washed us from our sins in his own blood ». — He *takes away* sins; whence here: *Behold the Lamb* etc.; and Isaias, the fifty-third chapter: « He hath borne the iniquities of many »; and above in the same: « Surely he hath borne our sorrows and carried our infirmities ». — He *forgives* sins; Luke, the seventh chapter: « Thy sins are remitted thee »; there follows: « Who is this that forgiveth sins »?

### Collation VII.

(Vers. 51.). *You shall see the heaven opened.*

1. Note that heaven is sometimes opened for *sending the presents*[^p543-10] *of benefits* — for *showing the mysteries of secrets* — for *receiving into the joys*
<!-- page 544 -->
*of rewards.* — God opens heaven to *beginners* for *sending forth;* which was figured in Matthew, the third chapter[^p544-1] and Luke, the third chapter, where it is read that Jesus being baptized « forthwith came up out of the water, and lo, the heavens were opened, and he saw the Spirit of God descending as a dove upon him ». — God opens heaven for *showing* the things that are in heaven, and thus it was opened to John, Apocalypse, the eleventh chapter: « The temple of God was opened in heaven, and the ark of his testament was seen in his temple ». — God opens heaven for *receiving*, and thus it was opened to Stephen, who saw the heavens opened, « saw and entered in »; of which opening Acts, the seventh chapter: « Behold, I see the heavens opened, and Jesus standing on the right hand of God ».

2. Now it is to be noted that in three ways one merits to find heaven open: *by offering — by knocking* — and *by hastening.* — For it is wont to be opened willingly to *those who offer;* and therefore it is said in Malachias, the third chapter[^p544-2]: « Bring all the tithes into my storehouse, that there may be meat in my house, and try me in this, saith the Lord: if I open not unto you the floodgates of heaven, and pour you out a blessing even unto abundance ». — It is opened also to *those who knock;* Matthew, the seventh chapter: « Knock, and it shall be opened to you »; and in the same place: « To him that knocketh it shall be opened ». — It is opened no less to *those who hasten;* for those hastening find the door *open*, which those delaying find *shut;* therefore it is said in Luke, the thirteenth chapter[^p544-3]: « Strive to enter by the narrow gate, for I say to you, many shall seek to enter and shall not be able. But when the father of the family shall be gone in and shall have shut the door, you shall begin to stand without and knock » etc.; and in Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter it is had concerning the virgins that those « that were ready went in with him to the marriage ». But to the last it was answered: « I know you not ». — Let us therefore take thought *to offer* the gift of good operation and *to knock* by the instancy of prayer and *to hasten* by the fervour of emulation; and *heaven shall be opened to us.*

3. Note concerning this what was said above[^p544-4], that heaven was opened for *sending forth* to Christ entering, for *showing* to John contemplating, for *receiving* to Stephen suffering.

### Annotation 1.

(Vers. 3.). *All things were made by him.* — It is to be noted that there is the word *making*, the word *made* and the word *becoming.* — The word *making* is the Word begotten from eternity, of which here: *All things were made by him.* — The word *made* is the word impressed inwardly on the mind, of which Luke, the third chapter[^p544-5]: « The word of the Lord was made upon John, the son of Zachary, in the desert ». — The word *becoming* is the word uttered vocally, which is only in *becoming;* of which Damascene says that our word is anhypostatic, that is, not subsisting, and poured out into the air. Of this it is said[^p544-6] that « the word flies irrevocable ».

### Annotation 2.

(Vers. 6.). *There was a man sent from God* etc. — Note that John was sent before the Lord[^p544-7] as *herald* for announcing, as *forerunner* for preparing the way, as *messenger* for receiving hospitality. — Of the first it is had here[^p544-8]: *There was a man sent from God, whose name was John. This man came for a witness, to give testimony of the lumen.* — Of the second, Malachias, the third chapter: « Behold, I send my angel, and he shall prepare the way before my face ». — Of the third, Luke, the first chapter: « He shall go before him in the spirit and power of Elias, that he may turn the hearts of the fathers unto the children, and the incredulous to the prudence of the just » etc. — John *heralded* by preaching, *prepared the way* by baptizing, *received hospitality* by converting the hearts of men to the faith of Christ.

### Annotation 3.

(Vers. 23.). *I am the voice of one crying in the desert.* — Note that John was sometimes called a *prophet*, sometimes a *voice*, sometimes a *lamp.* It belongs to a prophet *to know the truth;* it belongs to a voice *to bring forth the word;* it belongs to a lamp *to show the light.* — Blessed John was a *prophet*, because he knew the truth by divine revelation; Matthew, the eleventh chapter[^p544-9], Luke, the seventh chapter: « Yea, I say to you, and more
<!-- page 545 -->
than a prophet ». — He was again a *voice*, because he brought forth the truth in preaching; whence John, the first chapter: « I am the voice of one crying in the desert: Make straight the way of the Lord » etc. — He was a *lamp*, because he showed the truth in his conversation, according to what is said in Matthew, the fifth chapter: « So let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works ». Of him it is said in John, the fifth chapter: « He was a burning and a shining lamp »: *burning* in affection through charity, *shining* in effect through holy operation. He was therefore a *prophet*, because a *knower* of the truth; a *voice*, because a *preacher* of the truth; a *lamp*, because a *worker* of the truth.

Again, *I am the voice of one crying in the desert*[^p545-1]. — Note that John is called a *voice* for this reason, because *the voice precedes* the word, *the voice manifests* the word, *passes* and leaves the word behind. — So also John *preceded* Christ; Luke, the first chapter: « He shall go before him ». He *manifested* Christ; John, the first chapter: « Behold the Lamb of God ». He *passed* and left Christ behind; whence John, the third chapter: « He must increase, but I must decrease ».

     **En.** So is this little work inscribed in our codices; yet it plainly appears that these *Collations* are properly nothing but *schemata* of collations or sermons. Indeed, a good many of them are nothing but brief *annotations*, attached either to certain verses of the Gospel or to a few words. That the author himself distinguished these *annotations* from the longer *collations* is evident from this, that he is accustomed to set the *collations* first, according to the order of the verses of the chapter; and then, going back over other verses passed over, he adds brief observations, which ordinarily begin with the words: *nota* (note), or *notandum est* (it is to be noted). — For this reason we have thought it fitting to distinguish these shorter schemata from the collations, both by a special inscription of *annotations* and by a distinct numbering.

     We observe further that in the codices the single collations or annotations are not distinguished from one another by the addition of a number nor by the beginning of a new line, but only by a certain sign of separation and by painted or larger initial letters; sometimes not even this is observed. The *chapters of the Gospel* themselves, however, are distinguished, in cod. A by the beginning of a new line and the citing of the first words of the chapter; in codd. B C, besides, *capitulum primum... capitulum secundum* (the first chapter... the second chapter) etc. is set down distinctly.

     For other matters we refer the reader to our Prolegomena; here we note only that these Collations were composed for the use of preaching, and that they are as it were a kind of supplement to the Commentary on John, which is almost wholly taken up with the exposition of the *literal* sense and with the solving of exegetical questions. These Collations, on the other hand, being for preachers, treat practical matters, apply the sacred text here and there also according to the *tropological* or moral sense, and bring forth excellent lessons concerning Christ and the Church. They presuppose the preceding Commentary itself, and repeat not a little from it in another manner. From a comparison of the two works it is sufficiently evident that the author of both is the same. — Since, however, the reader can easily go back to the preceding Commentary, where, both in the *exposition* of the same verse and in the annexed *questions* with our appended notes, not a little is found apt for illustrating this text; it has therefore seemed superfluous to us to indicate the place in the Commentary by a special reference, or to repeat what is said elsewhere. We therefore refer the reader to the same verse in the Commentary, to be compared.

Petrus Hispanus, Summul. tr. *de Syllogismo* (tr. 4.): Omnis enim syllogismus constat ex tribus terminis et duabus propositionibus, quarum propositionum prima vocatur *maior*, secunda *minor.* Ex tribus autem terminis non possunt fieri duae propositiones, nisi alter illorum terminorum sumatur *bis*, et tunc ille terminus bis sumtus aut *subiicitur* in una et praedicatur in alia, et sic est *in prima figura;* aut *praedicatur* in utraque, et sic est *in secunda figura;* aut *subiicitur* in utraque, et sic est *in tertia figura.* Horum autem terminorum alter vocatur *medium*, alter *maior extremitas*, alter vero *minor extremitas. [^tr-lumen]: **En.** Translator's note: Latin has two words here where English has one. *Lux* is light in its source — the lemma of the first collation, *Lux in tenebris lucet*, and Christ who « est lux vera » — while *lumen* is light as it is sent forth and received. The fifth paragraph of that collation puts the two side by side in one phrase: Christ *emittit interius triplicem lucem sive lumen: lumen scientiae… lumen gratiae… lumen laetitiae spiritualis*, and its third proof-text is Ps. 4:7, *Signatum est super nos lumen vultus tui*. [^tr-doctrina]: **En.** Translator's note: the Latin *doctrina* holds together what English divides among doctrine, teaching and learning. In the third collation the fulness of Christ's wisdom gives us *doctrinam instructionis*, which is then glossed by John 17:8 — *dedi*, docendo, *et ipsi acceperunt*, credendo, "I have given, by teaching, and they have received, by believing" — a teaching given and received, not a body of propositions; and in the fifth the Spirit, appearing in tongues, *immittit ori doctrinam veritatis*, puts the teaching of truth into the mouth. The English therefore takes the side of "teaching" at these sites. Where this translation renders the same word "doctrine" or "learning", it is because the Latin there names the body of what is taught or the thing learned.
