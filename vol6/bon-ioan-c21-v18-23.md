# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XXI, Vers. 18-23
## *Fit hic eius ad dilectionis consummationem invitatio*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 526 -->

41. *Amen, amen dico tibi.* Postquam facta est Petri in dilectione confirmatio, fit hic eius *ad dilectionis consummationem invitatio*[^p526-12]. Consummatio autem dilectionis est, ut homo ponat animam suam pro Christo; ideo fit hic *ad passionem imitandam invitatio,* et proceditur hic per hunc ordinem. Primo, *Petrus invitatur et certificatur* de genere suae mortis. Secundo, *petit certificari de genere mortis Ioannis.* Tertio, *respondetur ei,* Ioannem non esse passurum. Quarto, *corrigitur intellectus credentium,* Ioannem non esse moriturum.

42. (Vers. 18.). *Certificatur* ergo *Petrus* a Domino de genere suae mortis, scilicet per passionem et crucem; et ut certum reddat, dicit: *Amen, amen dico tibi;* et haec est passio, quod in senio suo crucifigendus esset pro Christo; unde dicit: *Cum esses iunior, cingebas te et ambulabas, ubi volebas,*
<!-- page 527 -->
id est, sequebaris voluntatem tuam; Ecclesiastici decimo octavo[^p527-1]: « Fili, post concupiscentias tuas non eas, et a voluntate tua avertere ». *Cum autem senueris, extendes manus tuas,* scilicet in cruce; *et alius te cinget,* « vinculis scilicet », ut Nero, *et ducet, quo tu non vis,* « id est ad mortem »; quia, sicut dicitur secundae ad Corinthios quinto[^p527-2], « nolumus exspoliari, sed supervestiri ». Et ratio huius redditur ad Ephesios quinto: « Quia nemo unquam carnem suam odio habuit ». Cinctus fuit Petrus vinculis; Actuum duodecimo: « Erat Petrus dormiens inter duos milites, vinctus catenis duabus ». Et quia haec verba aliud a littera praetendebant, ideo ponitur expositio facta ab Evangelista.

43. (Vers. 19.). *Hoc autem,* scilicet quod extendes manus tuas, *dixit, significans, qua morte clarificaturus esset Deum:* quia morte *crucis,* quae, etsi ante Christum vilipendenda erat, postmodum exstitit gloriosa; et hac Petrus gloriosus effectus est. Unde Augustinus[^p527-3]: « Ille negator et praesumendo elatus, negando prostratus, post flendo purgatus, confitendo probatus, patiendo est coronatus ». Hac Petrus Dominum *clarificavit,* sicut Paulus dicebat ad Philippenses primo[^p527-4]: « Magnificabitur Christus in corpore meo, sive per vitam, sive per mortem ». Et quia passio bona non est, nisi sit *voluntaria;* ideo hortatur Petrum pati voluntarie; et propterea dicit: *Et cum hoc dixisset, dicit ei,* id est Petro: *Sequere me,* ad passionem crucis voluntarie veni, secundum quod ipse Petrus dicit primae Petri secundo[^p527-5]: « Christus passus est pro nobis, vobis relinquens exemplum, ut sequamini vestigia eius ». De hac consecutione vel sequela supra decimo tertio dixit Dominus Petro: « Quo ego vado, tu non potes venire modo; sequeris autem postea ».

44. (Vers. 20.). *Conversus Petrus vidit discipulum*[^p527-6] etc. Hic secundo, sciens Petrus mortem suam, *quaerit de morte Ioannis;* cupiebat enim, ut, cui iunctus fuerat in vita per multam dilectionem, iungeretur in mortis similitudine. Et quia Petrus sciebat, quod Dominus Ioannem amabat; volebat scire, qua morte ipsum mori volebat; ideo dicit: *Conversus Petrus vidit illum discipulum, quem diligebat Iesus, sequentem,* scilicet Ioannem. Et signum dilectionis praecipuae ostenditur, cum infertur: *qui et recubuit in Coena super pectus eius,* supra decimo tertio[^p527-7], *et dixit: Domine, quis est, qui tradet te?* Quaesivit ab eo quod secretum erat; et hoc signum erat dilectionis, quia sibi revelatum est, sicut dicitur supra decimo tertio; et hoc signum est amicitiae, sicut dicitur supra decimo quinto: « Vos dixi amicos, quia quaecumque audivi a Patre meo, nota feci vobis ».

45. (Vers. 21.). *Hunc ergo cum vidisset Petrus,* quem sciebat specialiter dilectum, *dicit ad Iesum*[^p527-8]*: Domine, hic autem quid?* supple: patietur, ut ad te veniat? Chrysostomus: « Valde amabat Ioannem Petrus; quia ergo magna Dominus praedixerat Petro, volens et hunc accipere communicatorem, ait: *Domine, hic autem quid* »?

46. (Vers. 22.). *Dicit ei Iesus.* Hic tertio *certificatur Petrus,* Ioannem non esse passurum, per Domini responsionem, in qua *revelat veritatem,* ut satisfaciat quaestioni, et *arguit curiositatem,* ut corrigat quaerentem. *Veritatem revelat,* cum ait: *Dicit ei Iesus: Sic*[^p527-9] *eum volo manere, donec veniam;* Beda: « *Sic,* id est absque violentia persecutionis diem exspectare novissimum »; respondet quaerenti. — Cum dicit: *Quid ad te? Tu me sequere, arguit* eum *de curiositate* quaestionis; unde Chrysostomus[^p527-10]: « Quid ad te, si sic volo eum manere? Tu ea quae tui ipsius sunt, attende, et de his sollicitus sis »; Ecclesiastici tertio: « In pluribus operibus eius ne fueris curiosus, sed quae praecepit tibi, cogita illa semper »; et ideo repetit: *Tu me sequere,* quasi dicat: de hoc cogites et sollicitus sis.

47. (Vers. 23.). *Exiit ergo sermo iste inter fratres.* Hic quarto *corrigitur intellectus credentium,* Ioannem non esse moriturum simpliciter; unde dicit: *Quod*[^p527-11] *discipulus ille non moritur,* contra
<!-- page 528 -->
illud Psalmi: « Quis est homo, qui vivet et non videbit mortem ». *Et non dixit*[^p528-1] *Iesus.* Hic ponitur illius *correctio,* quia hoc non dixit Iesus, scilicet quia non moritur; *non dixit: Non moritur,* quia, sicut dicitur Ecclesiastae nono, « nemo est, qui semper vivat »; *sed: Sic eum volo manere, donec veniam; quid ad te?* Remissive est legendum et exponendum ut supra[^p528-2].

### Quaestiones.

48. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic de hoc quod dicit: *Alius cinget te et ducet, quo tu non vis.* — Videtur ex hoc, quod passio Petri fuerit involuntaria: ergo non fuit meritoria.

**Respondeo:** Duplex est voluntas: *rationis* et *naturae; volens* passus est voluntate *rationis,* sed *nolens* voluntate *naturae* vel sensualitatis. Nec mirum, quia sic fuit in capite; unde Matthaei vigesimo sexto[^p528-3]: *Pater, si fieri potest, transeat a me calix iste; verumtamen non, sicut ego volo, sed sicut tu.* Unde Augustinus: « Petrus *nolens* ad mortem venit, *volens* mortem vicit et reliquit hunc infirmitatis affectum, quo nemo vult mori, usque adeo naturalem, ut eum beato Petro nec senectus auferre potuerit ».

49. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Significans, qua morte clarificaturus esset Deum,* quia supra quinto[^p528-4]: *Ego claritatem ab hominibus non accipio:* ergo cum Petrus esset homo, non clarificavit Deum.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod etsi Deus non clarificetur *in se,* clarificatur tamen *nobis* in Sanctis suis patientibus ad eius gloriam; quia per hoc nobis innotescit, quanta pro amore Dei nobis patienda sunt exemplo Sanctorum[^p528-5].

50. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc, quod Dominus specialiter dicit Petro: *Tu me sequere;* quia, si intelligitur per viam *perfectionis,* multi alii sunt secuti; si per viam *passionis,* similiter; si per *genus mortis,* secutus est etiam Andreas[^p528-6].

Et respondetur hic secundum aliquos, quod *tu*[^p528-7] non dicit ibi discretionem simpliciter, sed solum respectu Ioannis, de quo erat sermo. — Vel dicendum, quod *tu* dicit ibi magis significantiam quam discretionem; quia enim Petrus erat vertex Ecclesiae et praecipuus praelatus; sicut excellebat in praelatione, sic etiam debebat excellere in imitatione[^p528-8].

51. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Discipulus ille, quem diligebat Iesus.*

1. Quomodo fuit hoc ausus dicere, cum dicatur Ecclesiastae nono[^p528-9]: *Nemo scit, utrum amore, an odio dignus sit?*

2. Item, cum Christus omnes diligeret, propter quid Ioannes hoc sibi attribuebat, scilicet quod magis diligebatur? — Videtur falsum: quia Petrus magis diligebat[^p528-10]: ergo melior erat; sed Deus magis diligit meliores: ergo magis diligebat Petrum quam Ioannem.

Ad hoc responderi potest, quod Ioannes *revelatione,* vel certis[^p528-11] signis noverat, se amari.

2. Ad secundum, videtur dicere Augustinus[^p528-12], quod Ioannes magis diligebatur, sed Petrus magis diligebat; et ideo quaerit, quis melior erat; et dicit, quod Petrus melior, sed Ioannes felicior; et retorquet verba haec ad allegoriam, dicens, quia per Ioannem *contemplativa,* per Petrum *activa* vita significatur. — Sed hoc magis auget quaestionem; unde ipse Augustinus[^p528-13] dicit, quod latet hic iustitia, et apparet misericordia.

Propterea dicendum, quod *magis diligere* hoc est dupliciter: vel quia *ad maius praemium;* et sic magis diligebat Iesus Petrum, hoc intellecto, quod semper magis dilexerit Petrus Dominum. Alio modo *magis diligere* est quantum *ad maiorem exhibitionem,* quia plura ostendebat signa dilectionis; et sic magis diligebat Ioannem[^p528-14].

52. Quaest. V. Sed tunc est ulterius quaestio: quare magis se exhibebat Ioanni, ex quo plus
<!-- page 529 -->
diligebat Petrum quam Ioannem? — Videtur, quod fictio esset in Domino.

Ad hoc responderi potest, quod licet Petrus maius praemium mereretur, tamen conveniens erat, Christum maiorem familiaritatem exhibere Ioanni, quia Ioannes erat iuvenis et erat virgo et innocens; et haec sunt, quae miro modo et in conspectu Dei et hominum faciunt hominem amabilem et gratiosum. Unde Beda[^p529-1] dicit: « Hic est Ioannes, qui privilegio amoris praecipui ceteris altius meruit a Domino honorari; et quod specialis praerogativa castitatis eum ampliori dilectione fecerat dignum ». Et Glossa[^p529-2] dicit, quod « Ioannes familiarior fuit, quia tenerior erat ».

53. Quaest. VI. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Sic eum volo manere, donec veniam.* — Ergo videtur, quod saltem usque ad Domini adventum anima ipsius a corpore non sit separata; nam post[^p529-3] non habetur ex littera: ergo videtur, quod Ioannes nondum sit mortuus; in cuius rei argumentum est, quod corpus eius non invenitur.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod praeter expositionem primam discipulorum, quam fecerunt, quam arguit ipse Evangelista[^p529-4], quod non moreretur, tres sunt aliae expositiones. Una est, quod non est mortuus, sed morietur in adventu Domini; sed modo in sopore est sub terra, et ideo corpus eius non habetur. — Sed ista positio est improbabilis; quia non est credendum, quod Dominus discipulo, quem amabat, tamdiu differret suam demonstrare gloriam, quam cupiebat Apostolus, ad Philippenses primo[^p529-5]: *Cupio dissolvi et esse cum Christo.*

Alia est opinio, quod Dominus ipsum assumsit in corpore et anima, et in illa assumtione obiit et revixit. — Etsi ista opinio non possit ita reprobari, tamen, quia non habet auctoritatem, eadem facilitate contemnitur, qua probatur[^p529-6].

Tertia opinio dicit, quod Ioannes est mortuus, et de corpore eius nihil asserit, quia nihil certum invenitur. Quod autem dictum est: *Sic eum volo manere, donec veniam,* id est, « diem exspectare novissimum, non saeculi, sed mortis suae, quando ipse veniens eum in aeternae beatitudinis mansione recipiam[^p529-7] ».

## English
<!-- page 526 -->

41. *Amen, amen I say to thee.* After the confirmation of Peter in dilection has been made, here there is made his *invitation to the consummation of dilection*[^p526-12]. Now the consummation of dilection is that a man lay down his life for Christ; therefore there is made here the *invitation to imitate the passion,* and the procedure here is in this order. First, *Peter is invited and certified* of the kind of his death. Second, *he asks to be certified of the kind of John's death.* Third, *it is answered him* that John will not suffer. Fourth, *the understanding of the believers is corrected,* that John would not die.

42. (Vers. 18.). *Peter,* then, *is certified* by the Lord of the kind of his death, namely through the passion and the cross; and that he may render it certain, he says: *Amen, amen I say to thee;* and this is the passion, that in his old age he was to be crucified for Christ; hence he says: *When thou wast younger, thou didst gird thyself, and didst walk where thou wouldst,*
<!-- page 527 -->
that is, thou didst follow thy own will; Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter[^p527-1]: « Son, go not after thy lusts, and turn away from thy own will ». *But when thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands,* namely on the cross; *and another shall gird thee,* « with bonds, namely », as Nero did, *and lead thee whither thou wilt not,* « that is, to death »; because, as is said in the second to the Corinthians, the fifth chapter[^p527-2], « we would not be unclothed, but clothed upon ». And the reason of this is given to the Ephesians, the fifth chapter: « For no man ever hated his own flesh ». Peter was girded with bonds; Acts, the twelfth chapter: « Peter was sleeping between two soldiers, bound with two chains ». And because these words put forward something other than the letter, therefore there is set down the exposition made by the Evangelist.

43. (Vers. 19.). *And this* — namely that thou shalt stretch forth thy hands — *he said, signifying by what death he should glorify God:* because by the death of the *cross,* which, although before Christ it was to be held vile, afterwards became glorious; and by this Peter was made glorious. Hence Augustine[^p527-3]: « That denier, both lifted up by presuming and laid low by denying, afterwards cleansed by weeping, proved by confessing, was crowned by suffering ». By this Peter *glorified* the Lord, as Paul said to the Philippians, the first chapter[^p527-4]: « Christ shall be magnified in my body, whether it be by life, or by death ». And because the passion is not good unless it be *voluntary;* therefore he exhorts Peter to suffer voluntarily; and on that account he says: *And when he had said this, he saith to him,* that is, to Peter: *Follow me,* come voluntarily to the passion of the cross, according to what Peter himself says in the first of Peter, the second chapter[^p527-5]: « Christ suffered for us, leaving you an example, that you should follow his steps ». Of this following or sequel the Lord said to Peter above, in the thirteenth chapter: « Whither I go, thou canst not come now; but thou shalt follow hereafter ».

44. (Vers. 20.). *Peter turning about saw the disciple*[^p527-6] etc. Here, second, Peter, knowing his own death, *asks about the death of John;* for he desired that he to whom he had been joined in life by much dilection should be joined to him in the likeness of death. And because Peter knew that the Lord loved John, he wished to know by what death he willed him to die; therefore he says: *Peter turning about saw that disciple whom Jesus loved following,* namely John. And the sign of a special dilection is shown when it is added: *who also leaned on his breast at the Supper,* above, the thirteenth chapter[^p527-7], *and said: Lord, who is he that shall betray thee?* He asked of him what was secret; and this was a sign of dilection, because it was revealed to him, as is said above, in the thirteenth chapter; and this is a sign of friendship, as is said above, in the fifteenth chapter: « I have called you friends, because all things whatsoever I have heard of my Father, I have made known to you ».

45. (Vers. 21.). *Him therefore when Peter had seen,* whom he knew to be specially loved, *he saith to Jesus*[^p527-8]*: Lord, and what of this man?* supply: shall he suffer, that he may come to thee? Chrysostom: « Peter greatly loved John; since, then, the Lord had foretold great things to Peter, wishing to take this one too as a partaker, he says: *Lord, and what of this man* »?

46. (Vers. 22.). *Jesus saith to him.* Here, third, *Peter is certified* that John will not suffer, through the response of the Lord, in which *he reveals the truth,* that he may satisfy the question, and *reproves the curiosity,* that he may correct the one asking. *He reveals the truth* when he says: *Jesus saith to him: So*[^p527-9] *I will have him to remain till I come;* Bede: « *So,* that is, without the violence of persecution to await the last day »; he responds to the one asking. — When he says: *What is it to thee? Follow thou me, he reproves* him *for the curiosity* of the question; hence Chrysostom[^p527-10]: « What is it to thee, if so I will have him to remain? Attend thou to the things that are thine own, and be solicitous about these »; Ecclesiasticus, the third chapter: « In many of his works be not curious, but the things that he hath commanded thee, think on them always »; and therefore he repeats: *Follow thou me,* as if to say: think about this and be solicitous.

47. (Vers. 23.). *This saying therefore went abroad among the brethren.* Here, fourth, *the understanding of the believers is corrected,* that John would not die simply; hence he says: *That*[^p527-11] *that disciple dieth not,* against
<!-- page 528 -->
that of the Psalm: « Who is the man that shall live, and not see death ». *And Jesus did not say*[^p528-1]. Here there is set down the *correction* of that, because Jesus did not say this, namely that he dieth not; *he did not say: He dieth not,* because, as is said in Ecclesiastes, the ninth chapter, « there is none that liveth always »; *but: So I will have him to remain till I come; what is it to thee?* It is to be read and expounded remissively, as above[^p528-2].

### Questions.

48. Quaest. I. But it is asked here about what he says: *Another shall gird thee, and lead thee whither thou wilt not.* — It seems from this that the passion of Peter was involuntary: therefore it was not meritorious.

**I respond:** The will is twofold: of *reason* and of *nature; willing* he suffered by the will of *reason,* but *unwilling* by the will of *nature* or of sensuality. Nor is it a wonder, because so it was in the head; hence Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter[^p528-3]: *Father, if it be possible, let this chalice pass from me; nevertheless not as I will, but as thou.* Hence Augustine: « Peter came *unwilling* to death, *willing* he conquered death, and he left behind this affection of infirmity, by which no one wishes to die, so natural that not even old age could take it away from blessed Peter ».

49. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *Signifying by what death he should glorify God,* because above, in the fifth chapter[^p528-4]: *I receive not clarity from men:* therefore, since Peter was a man, he did not glorify God.

**I respond:** It must be said that, although God is not glorified *in himself,* he is nevertheless glorified *to us* in his Saints suffering for his glory; because through this it becomes known to us how great things are to be suffered by us for the love of God, by the example of the Saints[^p528-5].

50. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked about this, that the Lord says specially to Peter: *Follow thou me;* because, if it is understood of the way of *perfection,* many others followed; if of the way of *passion,* likewise; if of the *kind of death,* Andrew too followed[^p528-6].

And it is responded here according to some, that *thou*[^p528-7] does not express discretion there simply, but only with respect to John, of whom the talk was. — Or it must be said that *thou* expresses there significance rather than discretion; because Peter was the head of the Church and the chief prelate; as he excelled in prelacy, so too he ought to excel in imitation[^p528-8].

51. Quaest. IV. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *That disciple whom Jesus loved.*

1. How did he dare to say this, since it is said in Ecclesiastes, the ninth chapter[^p528-9]: *No man knoweth whether he be worthy of love, or hatred?*

2. Likewise, since Christ loved all, for what reason did John attribute this to himself, namely that he was loved more? — It seems false: because Peter loved more[^p528-10]: therefore he was better; but God loves the better more: therefore he loved Peter more than John.

To this it can be responded that John knew by *revelation,* or by certain[^p528-11] signs, that he was loved.

2. To the second, Augustine seems to say[^p528-12] that John was loved more, but Peter loved more; and therefore he asks which was better; and he says that Peter was better, but John happier; and he turns these words back to allegory, saying that by John the *contemplative* life, by Peter the *active* life is signified. — But this increases the question more; hence Augustine himself[^p528-13] says that the justice here is hidden, and the mercy is apparent.

Therefore it must be said that *to love more* is in two ways: either as *to the greater reward;* and so Jesus loved Peter more, this being understood, that Peter always loved the Lord more. In another way *to love more* is as *to the greater showing,* because he showed more signs of dilection; and so he loved John more[^p528-14].

52. Quaest. V. But then there is the further question: why did he show himself more to John, since he loved
<!-- page 529 -->
Peter more than John? — It seems that there was feigning in the Lord.

To this it can be responded that, although Peter merited the greater reward, yet it was fitting that Christ should show greater familiarity to John, because John was young and was a virgin and innocent; and these are what, in a wonderful way, both in the sight of God and of men, make a man lovable and gracious. Hence Bede[^p529-1] says: « This is John, who by the privilege of a special love merited to be honoured by the Lord more highly than the others; and because the special prerogative of chastity had made him worthy of an ampler dilection ». And the Gloss[^p529-2] says that « John was more familiar, because he was tenderer ».

53. Quaest. VI. Likewise it is asked about what he says: *So I will have him to remain till I come.* — Therefore it seems that at least until the coming of the Lord his soul is not separated from the body; for after[^p529-3] it is not had from the letter: therefore it seems that John is not yet dead; in proof of which thing it is that his body is not found.

**I respond:** It must be said that, besides the first exposition of the disciples, which they made, which the Evangelist himself reproves[^p529-4], that he would not die, there are three other expositions. One is that he is not dead, but will die at the coming of the Lord; but now he is in slumber under the earth, and therefore his body is not had. — But this position is improbable; because it is not to be believed that the Lord would put off so long showing his glory to the disciple whom he loved, which the Apostle desired, to the Philippians, the first chapter[^p529-5]: *I desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ.*

Another is the opinion that the Lord took him up in body and soul, and in that assumption he died and lived again. — Although this opinion cannot so be reproved, yet, because it does not have authority, it is despised with the same ease with which it is proved[^p529-6].

The third opinion says that John is dead, and asserts nothing about his body, because nothing certain is found. But as to what was said: *So I will have him to remain till I come,* that is, « to await the last day, not of the world, but of his own death, when I myself coming shall receive him into the mansion of eternal beatitude[^p529-7] ».
