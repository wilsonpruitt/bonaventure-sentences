# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. XV, Vers. 12-17
## *Secundo hortatur discipulos ad dilectionem amicorum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 450 -->

#### Secundo hortatur discipulos ad dilectionem amicorum.

19. *Hoc est praeceptum meum.* Postquam hortatus est Dominus ad dilectionem Dei, hic secundo hortatur *ad dilectionem fratris et amici*[^p450-5], et hoc quidem facit per hunc modum. Primo ad dilectionem fraternam astringit intuitu *obedientiae;* secundo, intuitu *divinae amicitiae;* tertio, intuitu *remunerationis aeternae.*

(Vers. 12.). Astringit primo *praecepto obedientiae,* innuens, hoc esse omnino in voluntate sua tanquam praecipuum; propter quod dicit: *Hoc est praeceptum meum, ut diligatis invicem;* unde supra decimo tertio[^p450-6]: « Mandatum novum do vobis, ut diligatis invicem ». Et modus subiungitur: *Sicut dilexi vos,* « non verbo vel lingua, sed opere et veritate », sicut ego vos. Et quod ipse *opere* dilexerit eos, monstrat:

20. (Vers. 13.). *Maiorem,* inquit, *hac dilectionem nemo habet, quam ut animam suam ponat quis*[^p450-7] *pro amicis suis;* quasi dicat: nihil plus potest facere quam vitam dare, quae omnibus carior est; ad Ephesios quinto: « Christus dilexit nos et tradidit semetipsum pro nobis oblationem et hostiam Deo in odorem suavitatis ». Et haec fuit vehementissima dilectio, non solum in Christo, qui tradidit se et animam suam, sed in Patre; unde ad Romanos octavo: « Qui proprio Filio non pepercit, sed pro nobis omnibus tradidit illum; quomodo non etiam cum illo omnia nobis donavit » ?

Unde notandum, quod *magna* est dilectio, qua mater diligit filium; super hanc dilexit nos Christus; Isaiae quadragesimo nono[^p450-8]: « Nunquid oblivisci potest mulier infantem suum, ut non misereatur filio uteri sui? Et si illa oblita fuerit, ego tamen non obliviscar tui ». — *Maior,* qua uxor virum, et super hanc Christus; Ieremiae tertio[^p450-9]: « Vulgo dicitur: Si dimiserit vir uxorem suam, et recedens ab eo duxerit virum alterum; nunquid revertetur ad eum ultra? Tu autem fornicata es cum amatoribus multis; tamen revertere, et ego suscipiam te ». — *Maxima* animae ad corpus; et super hanc dilexit nos; Ieremiae duodecimo[^p450-10]: « Reliqui domum meam, dimisi hereditatem meam, dedi dilectam animam meam in manus inimicorum eius ». Et ideo verum est il-
<!-- page 451 -->
lud Canticorum ultimo: « Fortis est ut mors dilectio » etc.

21. (Vers. 14.). *Vos amici mei estis.* Hic secundo hortatur ad dilectionem mutuam *intuitu divinae amicitiae;* unde offert amicitiam suam his qui servant praecepta sua, ut iuxta Domini praeceptum invicem diligant; propter quod dicit: *Vos amici mei estis, si feceritis quae*[^p451-1] *praecipio vobis.* Magnum quid promittit, cum amicitiam promittit. Augustinus: « Magna est dignatio Domini, cum nos amicos vocare dignatus est », cum sit *amicus* nomen paritatis; « est enim amicitia divinarum humanarumque rerum pari cum voluntate consensio ». Si enim *sapientia* amicos Dei constituit; Sapientiae septimo[^p451-2]: « In animas sanctas se transfert et amicos Dei constituit »; quanto magis *dilectio?* Haec dilectio probatur in opere; ideo dicit: *Amici estis, si feceritis quae ego praecipio,* ex amore ut amici, non ex timore ut servi, quia non estis vocati in servitutem; propter quod dicit:

22. (Vers. 15.). *Iam non dicam vos servos,* licet prius fueritis; ad Romanos octavo[^p451-3]: « Non accepistis spiritum servitutis iterum in timore, sed spiritum adoptionis filiorum ». Et per signum hoc ostendit: *Quia servus nescit, quid faciat Dominus eius;* vos autem non sic; ideo dicit: *Vos autem dixi amicos, quia omnia, quaecumque audivi a Patre meo, nota feci vobis.* Hoc signum magnae amicitiae, revelatio secretorum; ad Ephesios primo[^p451-4]: « Notum fecit nobis sacramentum voluntatis suae »; Matthaei decimo tertio: « Vobis datum est nosse mysteria regni Dei » etc.

23. (Vers. 16.). *Non vos me elegistis.* Hic tertio hortatur ad dilectionem *intuitu remunerationis aeternae,* ad quam per praedestinationem aeternam vocati erant, non per se venerant. Propterea dicit: *Non vos me elegistis, sed ego elegi vos;* ad Ephesios primo[^p451-5]: « Elegit nos ante mundi constitutionem, ut essemus sancti et immaculati in conspectu eius in caritate; qui praedestinavit nos in adoptionem filiorum per Iesum Christum in ipsum, secundum propositum voluntatis suae ». Ad quid elegerit, subdit: *Et posui vos, ut eatis et fructum afferatis,* in aliorum conversione; quod fit per praedicationem verbi Dei, quod est tanquam semen, quod fructum affert, quando recipitur in terram bonam; unde Lucae octavo[^p451-6]: « Quod vero in terram bonam, hi sunt, qui corde bono et optimo audiunt verbum et fructum afferunt in patientia »; primae Petri primo: « Renati non ex semine corruptibili, sed incorruptibili, per verbum Dei vivi et permanentis in aeternum ». *Et fructus vester maneat,* ad vestram salutem et consolationem per divinam remunerationem; et subiungitur remuneratio: *Ut quodcumque petieritis Patrem in nomine meo,* id est, quod sit ad vestram salutem, *det vobis.* Hoc autem, quod est petendum, est nostra salus; unde Matthaei sexto[^p451-7]: « Quaerite primum regnum Dei et iustitiam eius, et haec omnia adiicientur vobis ». Sic petebat illa sancta anima in Psalmo: « Unam petii a Domino, hanc requiram, ut inhabitem in domo Domini omnibus diebus vitae meae ». Et intuitu huius remunerationis debent mandatum Christi servare; ideo illud iterat:

24. (Vers. 17.). *Haec mando vobis, ut diligatis invicem. Mandatum* dilectionis dicitur quasi *manu datum,* quia exemplo suo dedit; ideo supra decimo tertio[^p451-8]: « Mandatum novum do vobis, ut diligatis invicem »; et primae ad Thessalonicenses quarto: « De caritate fraternitatis non habemus necesse scribere vobis; ipsi enim vos a Deo didicistis, ut diligatis invicem ».

### Quaestiones.

25. Quaest. I. Sed quaerit Gregorius[^p451-9] primo de hoc quod dicit: *Hoc est praeceptum meum, ut diligatis invicem:* « Cum cuncta divina eloquia dominicis sint plena praeceptis, quid est, quod de dilectione singulariter dicit: *Hoc est praeceptum meum* » ?

Et respondet, quod « divina praecepta *multa* sunt et *unum:* multa per *diversitatem operis,* unum in *radice dilectionis* ». Et dilectionem *radicem* dicit, « quia non habet viriditatem ramusculus boni operis, nisi manserit in radice dilectionis ». Quia ergo mandata ad praeceptum dilectionis referuntur, in quo omnia uniuntur et implentur; inde est, quod dicit: *Hoc est praeceptum meum,* singulariter.

26. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Maiorem hac dilectionem nemo habet, quam ut animam suam ponat quis pro amicis suis.*

**Contra:** 1. Ex parva caritate alius facit, alius ex maiori potest mori pro amico; quid est ergo, quod dicit *maiorem?*
<!-- page 452 -->

2. Item, in patria erit multo maior caritas, quam sit in via; quid est ergo, quod dicit *maiorem caritatem* etc. ?

**Respondeo,** quod hoc non dicitur quantum ad *habitum* caritatis, vel *actum eius praecipuum;* sed quantum ad *signum* vel *effectum,* quia hoc est praecipuum signum dilectionis[^p452-1].

27. Quaest. III. Sed tunc obiicitur: quia maius est pro inimico mori quam pro amico: ergo adhuc non est maximum signum dilectionis mori pro amico; unde arguit Apostolus ad Romanos quinto[^p452-2] per locum *a minori: Si, cum inimici essemus, reconciliati sumus Deo per mortem Filii eius; multo magis reconciliati salvi erimus in vita ipsius.*

Ad hoc respondet Gregorius[^p452-3], quod hoc nomen *amicis* non tantum supponit pro his qui Dominum diligebant, sed etiam pro inimicis, quia amici Domini erant, quoniam Dominus eos amabat, dum *pro eis animam suam ponebat.*

Vel intelligendum, quod Dominus loquebatur de dilectione amicorum, et quantum ad illam dilectionem nullum maius signum quam mori pro amico; similiter quantum ad dilectionem inimicorum veram; nam nullum maius signum quam mori pro inimico. Unde non notatur hic excessus *simpliciter,* sed *in genere*[^p452-4].

28. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Iam non dicam vos servos.*

**Contra:** 1. Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p452-5]: *Euge, serve bone et fidelis;* et si vocat beatificandos servos, quid est ergo, quod dicit: *Non dicam vos servos?*

2. Item, supra decimo tertio[^p452-6] dixit se *dominum eorum:* ergo *a relativis* et illos dixit *servos.*

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *servus* dicitur dupliciter: uno modo, prout dicit obedientiam et subiectionem; et sic servus non distinguitur contra amicum, et sic omnes amici Dei sunt servi eius. Alio modo *servus* dicitur serviliter timens; et talis, qui solo timore servit, amicus non est; et de tali hic loquitur, quia nolebat, quod mandata eius serviliter implerent, sed potius amicabiliter[^p452-7].

29. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Omnia, quaecumque audivi a Patre meo, nota feci vobis.* — *Contra:* Audivit Filius a Patre infinita, ergo nota fecit infinita.

Respondent, quod non loquitur de his quae audivit secundum quod Deus, sed secundum quod homo[^p452-8].

*Obiicitur* tunc: quia diem iudicii homo Christus novit, tamen discipulis revelare noluit[^p452-9]. — Propter hoc respondent ultra, quod omnia pertinentia ad salutem. — *Contra:* infra decimo sexto[^p452-10]: *Adhuc multa habeo vobis dicere, quae non potestis portare modo;* et constat, quod ad eorum salutem: ergo etc.

Respondet Augustinus[^p452-11]: *Omnia nota fecit,* id est, disposuit nota facere; sic enim frequenter loquitur Scriptura, ponens praeteritum pro futuro in signum certitudinis. — Vel: omnia necessaria ad salu-
<!-- page 453 -->
tem, secundum quod capere poterant[^p453-1]. — Et sic patent omnia.

30. Quaest. VI. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Non vos me elegistis.*

**Contra:** 1. Deuteronomii vigesimo sexto[^p453-2]: *Dominum elegisti, ut sit tibi Deus.*

2. Item, si meritum liberi arbitrii consistit in electione[^p453-3], quomodo merebantur, si ipsum non elegerant?

**Respondeo:** Quod hoc intelligendum est: *primo,* quia non *primo* nos eligimus Dominum, sed *secundo;* quia ipse prius elegit *praedestinando* et postmodum eligit ex aliis *vocando,* sed nos postmodum eligimus vocationi *consentiendo*[^p453-4].

## English
<!-- page 450 -->

#### Second he exhorts the disciples to the dilection of friends.

19. *This is my precept.* After the Lord has exhorted to the dilection of God, here second he exhorts *to the dilection of a brother and friend*[^p450-5], and this indeed he does in this way. First he binds to fraternal dilection in view of *obedience;* second, in view of *divine friendship;* third, in view of *eternal remuneration.*

(V. 12.). He binds first by the *precept of obedience,* hinting that this is altogether in his will as the chief thing; on account of which he says: *This is my precept, that you love one another;* hence above, the thirteenth[^p450-6]: « A new commandment I give unto you, that you love one another ». And the mode is subjoined: *As I have loved you,* « not in word, nor in tongue, but in deed, and in truth », as I you. And that he himself loved them *in deed,* he shows:

20. (V. 13.). *Greater,* he says, *dilection than this no man hath, than that a man*[^p450-7] *lay down his soul for his friends;* as if to say: nothing more can he do than give life, which is dearer than all things; to the Ephesians, the fifth: « Christ hath loved us, and hath delivered himself for us, an oblation and a sacrifice to God for an odour of sweetness ». And this was a most vehement dilection, not only in Christ, who delivered himself and his soul, but in the Father; hence to the Romans, the eighth: « He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how hath he not also, with him, given us all things » ?

Hence it is to be noted that *great* is the dilection by which a mother loves her son; above this Christ has loved us; Isaias, the forty-ninth[^p450-8]: « Can a woman forget her infant, so as not to have pity on the son of her womb? And if she should forget, yet will not I forget thee ». — *Greater,* that by which a wife loves her husband, and above this Christ; Jeremias, the third[^p450-9]: « It is commonly said: If a man put away his wife, and she go from him, and marry another man, shall she return to him any more? But thou hast prostituted thyself to many lovers; nevertheless return, and I will receive thee ». — *Greatest,* that of the soul for the body; and above this he has loved us; Jeremias, the twelfth[^p450-10]: « I have forsaken my house, I have left my inheritance: I have given my dear soul into the hands of her enemies ». And therefore true is that
<!-- page 451 -->
word, the last of the Canticles: « Dilection is strong as death » etc.

21. (V. 14.). *You are my friends.* Here second he exhorts to mutual dilection *in view of divine friendship;* hence he offers his friendship to those who keep his precepts, that according to the Lord's precept they may love one another; on account of which he says: *You are my friends, if you do the things that*[^p451-1] *I command you.* Something great he promises, when he promises friendship. Augustine: « Great is the condescension of the Lord, when he has condescended to call us friends », since *friend* is a name of parity; « for friendship is an accord of divine and human matters with equal will ». For if *wisdom* makes friends of God; Wisdom, the seventh[^p451-2]: « Into holy souls she conveyeth herself, and maketh the friends of God »; how much more *dilection?* This dilection is proved in the deed; therefore he says: *You are friends, if you do the things that I command,* out of love as friends, not out of fear as servants, because you have not been called into servitude; on account of which he says:

22. (V. 15.). *I will not now call you servants,* although formerly you were; to the Romans, the eighth[^p451-3]: « You have not received the spirit of servitude again in fear, but the spirit of adoption of sons ». And he shows this by a sign: *Because the servant knoweth not what his Lord doth;* but you not so; therefore he says: *But I have called you friends, because all things whatsoever I have heard of my Father, I have made known to you.* This is the sign of a great friendship, the revelation of secrets; to the Ephesians, the first[^p451-4]: « He hath made known to us the sacrament of his will »; Matthew, the thirteenth: « To you it is given to know the mysteries of the kingdom of God » etc.

23. (V. 16.). *You have not chosen me.* Here third he exhorts to dilection *in view of eternal remuneration,* to which they had been called by eternal predestination, and had not come of themselves. Therefore he says: *You have not chosen me, but I have chosen you;* to the Ephesians, the first[^p451-5]: « He chose us before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and unspotted in his sight in charity; who hath predestinated us unto the adoption of children through Jesus Christ unto himself, according to the purpose of his will ». To what he has chosen them, he subjoins: *And I have appointed you, that you should go and should bring forth fruit,* in the conversion of others; which comes about through the preaching of the word of God, which is as a seed, which brings forth fruit, when it is received into good ground; hence Luke, the eighth[^p451-6]: « But that on the good ground, are they who in a good and perfect heart hear the word and bring forth fruit in patience »; the first of Peter, the first: « Being born again not of corruptible seed, but incorruptible, by the word of God who liveth and remaineth for ever ». *And your fruit should remain,* for your salvation and consolation through divine remuneration; and the remuneration is subjoined: *That whatsoever you shall ask of the Father in my name,* that is, what may be for your salvation, *he may give you.* But this, which is to be asked, is our salvation; hence Matthew, the sixth[^p451-7]: « Seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his justice, and all these things shall be added unto you ». So that holy soul was asking in the Psalm: « One thing I have asked of the Lord, this will I seek after; that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life ». And in view of this remuneration they ought to keep the commandment of Christ; therefore he repeats it:

24. (V. 17.). *These things I command you, that you love one another.* The *commandment* (*mandatum*) of dilection is so called as it were *given by hand* (*manu datum*), because he gave it by his example; therefore above, the thirteenth[^p451-8]: « A new commandment I give unto you, that you love one another »; and the first to the Thessalonians, the fourth: « Of the charity of brotherhood we have no need to write to you; for you yourselves have learned of God to love one another ».

### Questions.

25. Quaest. I. But Gregory[^p451-9] asks first concerning this which he says: *This is my precept, that you love one another:* « Since all the divine utterances are full of the Lord's precepts, what is it that of dilection he says singularly: *This is my precept* » ?

And he responds that « the divine precepts are *many* and *one:* many through the *diversity of the work,* one in the *root of dilection* ». And he calls dilection the *root,* « because the little bough of good work has no greenness, unless it abides in the root of dilection ». Since, then, the commandments are referred to the precept of dilection, in which all are united and fulfilled; thence it is that he says: *This is my precept,* singularly.

26. Quaest. II. Likewise it is asked concerning this which he says: *Greater dilection than this no man hath, than that a man lay down his soul for his friends.*

**On the contrary:** 1. Out of small charity one does it, another out of greater can die for a friend; what is it, then, that he says *greater?*
<!-- page 452 -->

2. Likewise, in the fatherland there will be much greater charity than there is on the way; what is it, then, that he says *greater charity* etc. ?

**I respond** that this is not said as to the *habit* of charity, or its *chief act;* but as to the *sign* or *effect,* because this is the chief sign of dilection[^p452-1].

27. Quaest. III. But then it is objected: because it is greater to die for an enemy than for a friend: therefore it is not yet the greatest sign of dilection to die for a friend; hence the Apostle argues, to the Romans, the fifth[^p452-2], by the topic *from the lesser: If, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son; much more, being reconciled, shall we be saved by his life.*

To this Gregory[^p452-3] responds that this noun *friends* supposits not only for those who loved the Lord, but also for enemies, because they were friends of the Lord, since the Lord loved them, while *he was laying down his soul for them.*

Or it is to be understood that the Lord was speaking of the dilection of friends, and as to that dilection there is no greater sign than to die for a friend; likewise as to true dilection of enemies; for there is no greater sign than to die for an enemy. Hence no excess is noted here *simply,* but *in its genus*[^p452-4].

28. Quaest. IV. Likewise it is asked concerning this which he says: *I will not now call you servants.*

**On the contrary:** 1. Matthew, the twenty-fifth[^p452-5]: *Well done, good and faithful servant;* and if he calls those to be beatified servants, what is it, then, that he says: *I will not call you servants?*

2. Likewise, above, the thirteenth[^p452-6], he said that he was *their lord:* therefore *from relatives* he also said that they were *servants.*

**I respond:** It must be said that *servant* is said in two ways: in one way, as it says obedience and subjection; and so a servant is not distinguished against a friend, and so all the friends of God are his servants. In another way a *servant* is said as one servilely fearing; and such a one, who serves by fear alone, is not a friend; and of such a one he speaks here, because he did not want them to fulfil his commandments servilely, but rather amicably[^p452-7].

29. Quaest. V. Likewise it is asked concerning this which he says: *All things whatsoever I have heard of my Father, I have made known to you.* — *On the contrary:* The Son has heard infinite things from the Father, therefore he has made known infinite things.

They respond that he is not speaking of those things which he heard according as he is God, but according as he is man[^p452-8].

*It is objected* then: because Christ as man knew the day of judgement, yet he was unwilling to reveal it to the disciples[^p452-9]. — On account of this they respond further, that it means all things pertaining to salvation. — *On the contrary:* below, the sixteenth[^p452-10]: *I have yet many things to say to you, which you cannot bear now;* and it is established that they were for their salvation: therefore etc.

Augustine responds[^p452-11]: *He has made all things known,* that is, he disposed to make them known; for so Scripture frequently speaks, setting the past for the future as a sign of certitude. — Or: all things necessary for salva-
<!-- page 453 -->
tion, according as they were able to take them in[^p453-1]. — And thus all things are evident.

30. Quaest. VI. Likewise it is asked concerning this which he says: *You have not chosen me.*

**On the contrary:** 1. Deuteronomy, the twenty-sixth[^p453-2]: *Thou hast chosen the Lord to be thy God.*

2. Likewise, if the merit of free choice consists in election[^p453-3], how did they merit, if they had not chosen him?

**I respond:** That this is to be understood: *first,* because we do not choose the Lord *first,* but *second;* because he first chose by *predestining* and afterward chooses out of others by *calling,* but we afterward choose by *consenting* to the calling[^p453-4].
