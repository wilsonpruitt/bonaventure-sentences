### Capitulum XII. *Iesus ergo ante sex dies Paschae.*

### Annotatio 45.

(Vers. 3.). *Maria ergo accepit libram unguenti* etc.

Nota, quod Maria triplex unguentum obtulit Domino et in triplici statu. Obtulit enim unguentum Domino *recumbenti* et Domino *ad passionem accedenti* et eidem *resurgenti.* — Domino *recumbenti* obtulit unguentum; Lucae septimo[^p593-7]: « Ecce, mulier, quae erat in civitate peccatrix, ut cognovit, quod Iesus accubuit in domo Pharisaei, attulit alabastrum unguenti » etc. — Domino *ad passionem accedenti* obtulit unguentum; Ioannis duodecimo: « Maria accepit libram unguenti nardi pistici pretiosi et unxit pedes eius » etc.; et Matthaei vigesimo sexto: « Accessit ad eum mulier, habens alabastrum unguenti pretiosi » etc. — Domino *resurgenti* obtulit unguentum; Marci ultimo: « Cum transisset Sabbatum, Maria Magdalene et Maria Iacobi et Salome emerunt aromata, ut venientes ungerent Iesum ». — Primum unguentum obtulit Maria ad ungendum *pedes,* et significat unguentum *contritionis.* — Secundum unguentum obtulit ad ungendum *pedes* et *caput,* et significat unguentum *compassionis;* compungitur enim anima ex consideratione *afflictionis humanitatis,* quae significatur per *pedes;* et ex consideratione *dignationis Divinitatis,* quae significatur per *caput.* — Tertium unguentum ad unctionem *totius corporis,* et significat unguentum *devotionis,* quo perungit anima totum Christum, et hoc duce Spiritu, qui « omnia scrutatur, etiam profunda Dei[^p593-8] ».

### Annotatio 46.

(Vers. 7.). *Sinite illam* etc.

1. Nota, quod Dominus ter respondit pro Maria. Respondit pro illa *Pharisaeo indignanti;* Lucae
<!-- page 594 -->
septimo[^p594-1]: « Si esset propheta, sciret, qualis est mulier, quae tangit eum, quia peccatrix est ». — Respondit etiam pro ea *sorori conquerenti* et dicenti, Lucae decimo: « Domine, non est tibi curae, quod soror mea reliquit me solam ministrare »? — Respondit etiam *discipulo murmuranti* et dicenti: « Ut quid perditio haec » etc., Matthaei vigesimo sexto, et Ioannis duodecimo: « Quare unguentum non vaeniit trecentis denariis » etc.

2. Et nota, quod respondens Pharisaeo, commendavit Mariae *dilectionem;* Lucae septimo[^p594-2]: « Dimittuntur ei peccata multa, quia dilexit multum ». — Respondens sorori, commendavit Mariae *discretionem;* Lucae decimo: « Maria optimam partem elegit ». — Respondens discipulo, commendavit Mariae *operationem* dicens, Matthaei vigesimo sexto: « Bonum opus operata est in me ». — Unde invenitur Maria commendabilis quantum ad *intellectum* ex discretione, quantum ad *affectum* ex dilectione, quantum ad *effectum* ex operum pietate.

### Collatio XLV.

(Vers. 13.). *Acceperunt ramos palmarum et processerunt obviam ei.*

1. Nota, quod triplex processio celebratur in Ecclesia. Celebratur enim processio cum *ramis;* de qua hic: *Acceperunt* etc., et haec celebratur in Paschate. — Est processio cum *lucernis;* de qua Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p594-3]: « Virgines acceperunt lampades suas et exierunt obviam sponso et sponsae »; de qua etiam in Psalmo: « Specie tua et pulcritudine tua intende, prospere procede et regna »; et haec celebratur in Purificatione. — Est etiam processio cum *vexillis,* et sunt vexilla Christianorum insignia passionis, iuxta illud[^p594-4]: « Vexilla regis prodeunt » etc.; ad quam nos invitat Apostolus ad Hebraeos decimo tertio: « Exeamus ad eum extra castra, improperium eius portantes »; et haec celebratur in Rogationibus.

2. Attende igitur, quod procedunt *turbae* cum ramis, *virgines* cum lucernis et *apostolici viri* cum vexillis. Et nota, quod processio *turbarum* significat obsequium *activorum,* qui Christo offerunt ramos bonorum operum. Processio vero *virginum* significat obsequium *contemplativorum,* qui Deo offerunt ardorem spiritualium desideriorum. Processio vero Apostolorum significat *perfectorum* obsequium, qui, passionem Christi imitantes, sequuntur ipsum per ardua tormentorum.

3. *Acceperunt,* inquit, *ramos* etc. Nota, quod in ista processione tria faciunt *turbae: vestimenta prosternunt* in via; Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p594-5]: « Plurima turba straverunt vestimenta sua in via ». — *Ramos ferunt;* unde hic: *Acceperunt ramos palmarum.* — *Hosanna proclamant;* unde sequitur: *Et clamabant: Hosanna filio David.* — Per haec tria nota tria opera poenitentiae, quae sunt: *ieiunium, eleemosyna* et *oratio*[^p594-6]. In *prostratione vestimentorum* nota *ieiunium. Caro* enim vestimentum est; *poenitentia,* via; *prostratio* igitur *vestimentorum* in via est mortificatio carnis in poenitentia. — In *acceptione ramorum* significatur *eleemosyna;* haec est ramus boni operis procedens ex radice caritatis[^p594-7]. — In *cantico turbae* nota *orationem.* Illi enim cantant Domino: « Hosanna in excelsis », qui obsecrant, ut salventur in caelis.

### Collatio XLVI.

(Vers. 13.). *Benedictus, qui venit in nomine Domini.*

1. Considerantes adventum Christi in carnem, de quo spiritualiter intelliguntur verba ista, invenimus in eo, quid *mirandum* — quid *amandum* — et quid *imitandum* sit. Est enim adventus eius dignus *admiratione,* dignus *dilectione,* dignus etiam *imitatione.*

2. Admiratione dignus est, si consideremus, *unde* venit, *quo* venit et *propter quid* venit. — Admirabilis est dignatio eius, quod de *summo* venit; Sapientiae decimo octavo[^p594-8]: « Omnipotens sermo tuus, Domine, exsiliens de caelo a regalibus sedibus venit ». — Admirabilis fuit eius dignatio, quod ad *imum* venit; unde in Psalmo: « Veni in altitudinem maris », id est in profunditatem, « et tempestas demersit me ». — Nec minus mirabilis dignatio eius, quod quasi *pro nihilo,* scilicet pro homine perdito, venit; unde Matthaei decimo quinto[^p594-9]: « Non sum missus nisi ad oves, quae perierunt, domus Israel ». — Considerate ergo, *unde* venit et *quo* venit et *propter quid* venit; et « cantate Domino, quia mirabilia fecit ». Hoc est ergo admiratione dignum.

3. Sed nihilominus dignus est *dilectione* eius adventus, si consideremus *fructum.* Venit enim, ut
<!-- page 595 -->
hominem perditum *salvaret* a miseria, *ditaret* gratia, *sublimaret* in gloria. — Venit igitur hominem *salvare a miseria;* Lucae decimo nono[^p595-1]: « Venit Filius hominis quaerere et salvum facere quod perierat ». — Venit et hominem *ditare gratia,* cuius adventum desiderabat sponsa in Canticis, orans sub figura, ut transiret aquilo culpae, et veniret auster gratiae ad renovandum et fecundandum hortum conscientiae; Canticorum quarto[^p595-2]: « Surge, aquilo, et veni, auster, perfla hortum meum, et fluant aromata illius ». — Venit etiam hominem *exaltare in gloria;* Aggaei secundo: « Veniet desideratus cunctis gentibus, et implebo domum istam gloria, dicit Dominus ». Hoc est gratiarum actione dignum.

4. Sed adhuc est dignus *imitatione* adventus eius, si consideremus adveniendi *modum.* Venit enim cum *humilitate,* cum *mansuetudine* et cum *paupertate.* Eius *humilitas* docet nos ordinari ad Deum; eius *mansuetudo,* ad proximum; eius *paupertas,* ad mundum. — Venit ergo cum *humilitate;* unde Ioannis duodecimo[^p595-3]: « Ecce, Rex tuus venit sedens super pullum asinae ». Magna humiliatio est, regem asinare; tunc autem asinavit Rex, quando insedit asino humanitatis nostrae. — Venit etiam cum *mansuetudine;* Matthaei vigesimo primo[^p595-4]: « Ecce, Rex tuus venit tibi mansuetus, sedens super asinam » etc. Propter haec duo dicit Dominus Matthaei undecimo: « Discite a me, quia mitis sum et humilis corde »; quasi dicat: exemplum mansuetudinis et humilitatis a me accipite. — Venit etiam cum *paupertate;* unde Zachariae nono[^p595-5]: « Ecce, Rex tuus veniet tibi iustus et Salvator: ipse pauper et ascendens super asinam et super pullum filium asinae ». — Sic ergo habes exemplum *humilitatis, mansuetudinis* et *paupertatis* in Christo veniente, et hoc est dignum imitatione in Christo veniente.

### Annotatio 47.

(Vers. 17.). *Testimonium perhibebat turba, quae erat cum eo, quando vocavit Lazarum de monumento.*

Nota, quod signa, quae faciebat Christus, compellebant turbas ad tria: ad *credendum;* unde Ioannis secundo[^p595-6]: « Cum esset Ierosolymis in festo, multi crediderunt in eum, videntes signa, quae faciebat ». — Ad *confitendum;* unde hic: *Testimonium perhibebat* etc. — Ad *sequendum;* unde Ioannis sexto: « Sequebatur eum multitudo magna, quia videbant signa, quae faciebat ».

### Collatio XLVII.

(Vers. 32.). *Ego si exaltatus fuero a terra, omnia traham ad me ipsum.*

1. In verbis istis duo praedicit Dominus: primo, passionis suae *modum,* cum dicit: *Cum exaltatus fuero;* secundo, passionis suae *fructum,* cum dicit: *Omnia traham ad me ipsum.*

Ait ergo: *Cum exaltatus fuero a terra.* — Nota, quod multiplex est *exaltatio.* Est enim exaltatio hominis-Christi — et hominis iusti — et hominis mali. — De exaltatione *Christi* nota, quod Christus fuit exaltatus in *passione,* in *ascensione* et in *Apostolorum praedicatione.* — In passione fuit exaltatus in *poena;* de qua hic: *Cum exaltatus fuero* etc.; et Ioannis tertio[^p595-7]: « Sicut exaltavit Moyses serpentem in deserto, ita exaltari oportet Filium hominis ». — In ascensione fuit exaltatus in *gloria.* Tunc enim « Deus exaltavit illum et dedit illi nomen, quod est super omne nomen, ut in nomine Iesu omne genu flectatur caelestium, terrestrium et infernorum, et omnis lingua confiteatur, quia Dominus Iesus in gloria est Dei Patris », ad Philippenses secundo. — In Apostolorum praedicatione fuit exaltatus in *fama;* de quo in Psalmo: « Exaltabor in gentibus et exaltabor in terra »; et iterum: « Exaltare super caelos Deus »; quod factum est in ascensione; « et in omnem terram gloria tua »; quod factum est in Evangelii promulgatione.

2. Sequitur de exaltatione *hominis iusti,* quae multiplex est. Exaltat enim Deus hominem iustum in *gratia,* in *gloria* et in *fama.* — In *gratia* exaltat, quando de culpa ad gratiam vocat; unde Psalmus[^p595-8]: « Qui exaltas me de portis mortis ». *Portae mortis,* quibus intratur ad mortem, peccata sunt; *portae* vero *vitae,* gratia et virtutes. Tunc ergo *exaltatur* homo de portis mortis, quando revocatur de statu peccati ad statum gratiae. De hac exaltatione Lucae decimo octavo: « Omnis, qui se exaltat, humiliabitur ». — In *gloria* exaltat, cum de miseria ad regnum vocat, secundum illud Psalmi[^p595-9]: « Cornu eius exaltabitur in gloria ». De hac exaltatione Lucae decimo quarto: « Omnis, qui se humiliat, exaltabitur, et qui se exaltat humiliabitur ». — In *fama* exaltat, quando eius gloriam in Ecclesia manifestat, cum facit, ut « exaltent eum in ecclesia plebis et in cathedra seniorum laudent eum », secundum quod dicitur in Psalmo[^p595-10]; et Matthaei vigesimo tertio: « Omnis, qui se humiliat, exaltabitur » etc.; ubi praedicat Dominus de non quaerenda fama in praesenti sive gloria.
<!-- page 596 -->

3. Nota, quod meretur quis exaltari per tria. Solet enim exaltari *servus,* quia est *officiosus* in domo; solet exaltari *amicus,* quia est *familiaris* in consilio; solet etiam exaltari *miles,* quando *probatus* est in proelio. Ista tria genera hominum solent reges exaltare, videlicet fideles servos, amicos familiares et probatos milites: primos propter suum *obsequium,* secundos propter suum *consilium,* tertios propter suam *fortitudinem.*

4. Nota igitur, quod *activi* exaltabuntur, sicut servi, ratione *obsequii;* Isaiae quinquagesimo secundo[^p596-1]: « Ecce, servus meus intelliget et exaltabitur et elevabitur et sublimis erit valde »; Matthaei vigesimo quinto: « Euge, serve bone et fidelis, quia super pauca fuisti fidelis » etc. — Exaltantur amici, scilicet *contemplativi,* propter suam *sapientiam;* Proverbiorum quarto: « Arripe sapientiam, et exaltabit te, et glorificaberis ab ea, cum eam fueris amplexatus ». — Exaltantur *perfecti,* sicut Martyres, quasi propter suam *militiam,* secundum illud Psalmi[^p596-2]: « De torrente in via bibet, propterea exaltabit caput ». — Omnibus istis dicitur primae Petri quinto: « Humiliamini sub potenti manu Dei, ut vos exaltet in tempore visitationis ».

5. Sequitur de exaltatione *hominis mali,* quae multiplex est. Exaltantur enim mali in *corde* per superbiam — in *ore* per iactantiam — in *conversatione* per pompam. — Exaltantur ergo in corde per *superbiam,* quod prohibetur in Psalmo[^p596-3]: « Qui exasperant non exaltentur in semetipsis »; et Iob decimo quinto: « Quid te elevat cor tuum, et quasi magna cogitans attonitos habes oculos »? — Exaltant se in ore per *iactantiam,* sicut ille Pharisaeus, de quo dicitur Lucae decimo octavo: « Quia omnis, qui se exaltat, humiliabitur ». Iactabat enim se de bonis suis. — Exaltantur in conversatione per *pompam,* licet non diu; unde in Psalmo: « Inimici Domini mox, ut honorificati fuerint et exaltati, deficientes, quemadmodum fumus deficient ».

6. Talis exaltatio fugienda est, quia *ignominiosa, momentanea* et *periculosa.* — *Ignominiosa,* quia, quanto magis exaltantur, tanto magis eorum crimina conspectius videntur, secundum illud[^p596-4]:

> Omne animi vitium tanto conspectius in se
> Crimen habet, quanto maior qui peccat habetur.

Exemplum de simia, quae, quanto plus ascendit, tanto plus apparent posteriora eius. Et hoc probatur, Isaiae decimo quarto[^p596-5], de lucifero, qui dixit: « Conscendam, super astra caeli exaltabo solium meum » etc.; et sequitur statim, quomodo apparuerunt posteriora eius. — Item *momentanea,* quia non habet permanentiam secundum illud[^p596-6]:

> Invidia fatorum flumini summisque negatum
> Stare diu.

Et ideo dicitur in Psalmo: « Vidi impium elevatum et superexaltatum sicut cedros Libani »; et statim sequitur: « Transivi, et ecce, non erat ». Exemplum de fumo, qui quanto magis ascendit, tanto magis dispergitur et evanescit; unde Psalmus: « Mox ut honorificati fuerint et exaltati, deficientes, quemadmodum fumus deficient ». — Item, *periculosa,* quia sic exaltati descendunt cum praecipitio, secundum quod descendit lucifer, qui dixit[^p596-7]: « Exaltabo solium meum »; et statim dictum est ei: usque « ad infernum detraheris »; Abdiae primo: « Si exaltatus fueris ut aquila, et si inter sidera posueris nidum tuum; inde detraham te »; et in Iob dicitur in persona talium: « Elevasti me, et quasi super ventum ponens, elisisti me valide ». Exemplum de stulto, qui dixit, quod bonum erat volare, sed occidebat eum residere[^p596-8].

### Annotatio 48.

(Vers. 24.). *Nisi granum frumenti cadens in terram mortuum fuerit, ipsum solum manet* etc.

Require supra undecimo[^p596-9], de morte gratiae.

### Annotatio 49.

(Vers. 25.). *Qui amat animam suam perdet eam.*

Nota, quod *anima* aliquando accipitur pro vita *naturali;* Ioannis decimo[^p596-10]: « Animam meam pono pro ovibus meis ». — Aliquando pro vita *spirituali;* unde in Psalmo: « Qui diligit iniquitatem odit animam suam ». — Aliquando pro vita *carnali* sive animali; Ecclesiastici decimo octavo: « Si praestes animae tuae concupiscentias eius, faciet te in gaudium inimicis tuis »; et hic: *Qui amat animam* etc.

### Annotatio 50.

(Vers. 28.). *Venit ergo vox de caelo.*

Nota, quod vox Patris ter fuit audita super Christum, scilicet in *baptismo,* Matthaei tertio[^p596-11];
<!-- page 597 -->
et in *monte,* Matthaei decimo septimo; et in *via* ad Ierusalem, secundum quod hic habetur. In quo significatur, quod Deus Pater perhibet testimonium tribus generibus hominum, scilicet *innocentibus,* qui adhuc habent innocentiam baptismalem; *activis* incedentibus per viam bonae actionis; et *contemplativis* existentibus in monte contemplationis.

### Annotatio 51.

(Vers. 46.). *Ego lux veni in mundum.*

Nota, quod Christus venit ut *lux* ad *dirigendum;* unde hic: *Ego lux veni in mundum, ut omnis, qui credit in me, in tenebris non maneat.* — Venit ut *mediator* ad *liberandum;* unde Ioannis duodecimo[^p597-1]: « Non veni in mundum, ut iudicem mundum, sed ut salvificem mundum »; et Lucae nono: « Non venit Filius hominis animas perdere, sed salvare ». — Venit ut *amicus* ad *convivandum;* Lucae undecimo: « Amicus meus », id est Christus, « venit de via ad me ». — Venit ut *sponsus* ad *cohabitandum;* Matthaei vigesimo quinto[^p597-2]: « Ecce, sponsus venit; exite obviam ei ». — Venit ut *rex* ad *praesidendum;* Zachariae nono: « Ecce, Rex tuus veniet ». — Venit ut *iudex* ad *retribuendum;* Isaiae tertio: « Dominus ad iudicium veniet cum senioribus populi et iudicibus eius ».

### Chapter XII. *Jesus therefore, six days before the pasch.*

### Annotation 45.

(Vers. 3.). *Mary therefore took a pound of ointment* etc.

Note that Mary offered a threefold ointment to the Lord, and in a threefold state. For she offered ointment to the Lord *reclining,* and to the Lord *approaching his passion,* and to the same *rising again.* — To the Lord *reclining* she offered ointment; Luke, the seventh chapter[^p593-7]: « Behold a woman that was in the city, a sinner, when she knew that Jesus sat at meat in the Pharisee's house, brought an alabaster box of ointment » etc. — To the Lord *approaching his passion* she offered ointment; John, the twelfth chapter: « Mary took a pound of ointment of right spikenard, of great price, and anointed his feet » etc.; and Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter: « There came to him a woman having an alabaster box of precious ointment » etc. — To the Lord *rising again* she offered ointment; Mark, the last chapter: « When the sabbath was past, Mary Magdalen and Mary of James and Salome bought sweet spices, that coming they might anoint Jesus ». — The first ointment Mary offered for anointing the *feet,* and it signifies the ointment of *contrition.* — The second ointment she offered for anointing the *feet* and the *head,* and it signifies the ointment of *compassion;* for the soul is pierced with compunction from the consideration of the *affliction of the humanity,* which is signified by the *feet;* and from the consideration of the *condescension of the Divinity,* which is signified by the *head.* — The third ointment is for the anointing of the *whole body,* and it signifies the ointment of *devotion,* with which the soul anoints the whole Christ, and this under the guidance of the Spirit, who « searcheth all things, yea, the deep things of God[^p593-8] ».

### Annotation 46.

(Vers. 7.). *Let her alone* etc.

1. Note that the Lord answered three times on Mary's behalf. He answered for her to the *Pharisee taking offence;* Luke, the
<!-- page 594 -->
seventh chapter[^p594-1]: « If he were a prophet, he would know what manner of woman she is that toucheth him, that she is a sinner ». — He answered for her also to the *sister complaining* and saying, Luke, the tenth chapter: « Lord, hast thou no care that my sister hath left me alone to serve »? — He answered also to the *disciple murmuring* and saying: « To what purpose is this waste » etc., Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter, and John, the twelfth chapter: « Why was not this ointment sold for three hundred pence » etc.

2. And note that in answering the Pharisee he commended Mary's *dilection;* Luke, the seventh chapter[^p594-2]: « Many sins are forgiven her, because she hath loved much ». — In answering the sister he commended Mary's *discretion;* Luke, the tenth chapter: « Mary hath chosen the best part ». — In answering the disciple he commended Mary's *working,* saying, Matthew, the twenty-sixth chapter: « She hath wrought a good work upon me ». — Hence Mary is found commendable as to the *understanding* from discretion, as to the *affection* from dilection, as to the *effect* from the piety of her works.

### Collation XLV.

(Vers. 13.). *They took branches of palm trees and went forth to meet him.*

1. Note that a threefold procession is celebrated in the Church. For a procession is celebrated with *branches;* of which here: *They took* etc., and this is celebrated at Easter. — There is a procession with *lamps;* of which Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p594-3]: « The virgins took their lamps and went out to meet the bridegroom and the bride »; of which also in the Psalm: « With thy comeliness and thy beauty set out, proceed prosperously, and reign »; and this is celebrated at the Purification. — There is also a procession with *banners,* and the banners of Christians are the insignia of the passion, according to that[^p594-4]: « The banners of the king go forth » etc.; to which the Apostle invites us, to the Hebrews, the thirteenth chapter: « Let us go forth to him without the camp, bearing his reproach »; and this is celebrated at the Rogations.

2. Attend therefore that the *multitudes* go in procession with branches, the *virgins* with lamps, and the *apostolic men* with banners. And note that the procession of the *multitudes* signifies the service of the *active,* who offer to Christ the branches of good works. But the procession of the *virgins* signifies the service of the *contemplative,* who offer to God the ardour of spiritual desires. But the procession of the Apostles signifies the service of the *perfect,* who, imitating the passion of Christ, follow him through the hardships of torments.

3. *They took,* he says, *branches* etc. Note that in this procession the *multitudes* do three things: they *strew their garments* in the way; Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p594-5]: « A very great multitude spread their garments in the way ». — They *carry branches;* hence here: *They took branches of palm trees.* — They *proclaim Hosanna;* hence there follows: *And they cried: Hosanna to the son of David.* — By these three note the three works of penance, which are: *fasting, almsgiving* and *prayer*[^p594-6]. In the *strewing of the garments* note *fasting.* For the *flesh* is a garment; *penance,* the way; the *strewing* therefore *of the garments* in the way is the mortification of the flesh in penance. — In the *taking of the branches* is signified *almsgiving;* this is the branch of good work proceeding from the root of charity[^p594-7]. — In the *song of the multitude* note *prayer.* For those sing to the Lord « Hosanna in the highest » who beseech that they may be saved in the heavens.

### Collation XLVI.

(Vers. 13.). *Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord.*

1. Considering the coming of Christ into flesh, of which these words are spiritually understood, we find in it what is *to be wondered at* — what is *to be loved* — and what is *to be imitated.* For his coming is worthy of *admiration,* worthy of *dilection,* worthy also of *imitation.*

2. It is worthy of admiration, if we consider *whence* he came, *whither* he came and *for what* he came. — Admirable is his condescension, in that he came from the *highest;* Wisdom, the eighteenth chapter[^p594-8]: « Thy almighty word, O Lord, leaping down from heaven from the royal throne, came ». — Admirable was his condescension, in that he came to the *lowest;* hence in the Psalm: « I am come into the height of the sea », that is, into the depth, « and a tempest hath overwhelmed me ». — No less wonderful is his condescension, in that he came as it were *for nothing,* namely for lost man; hence Matthew, the fifteenth chapter[^p594-9]: « I was not sent but to the sheep that are lost of the house of Israel ». — Consider therefore *whence* he came and *whither* he came and *for what* he came; and « sing ye to the Lord, because he hath done wonderful things ». This therefore is worthy of admiration.

3. But no less is his coming worthy of *dilection,* if we consider its *fruit.* For he came, that he might
<!-- page 595 -->
*save* lost man from misery, *enrich* him with grace, *raise him on high* in glory. — He came, then, to *save* man *from misery;* Luke, the nineteenth chapter[^p595-1]: « The Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost ». — He came also to *enrich* man *with grace,* whose coming the spouse desired in the Canticles, praying under a figure that the north wind of fault might pass, and the south wind of grace might come to renew and make fruitful the garden of conscience; Canticles, the fourth chapter[^p595-2]: « Arise, O north wind, and come, O south wind, blow through my garden, and let the aromatical spices thereof flow ». — He came also to *exalt* man *in glory;* Aggeus, the second chapter: « The desired of all nations shall come, and I will fill this house with glory, saith the Lord ». This is worthy of thanksgiving.

4. But further his coming is worthy of *imitation,* if we consider the *manner* of his coming. For he came with *humility,* with *meekness* and with *poverty.* His *humility* teaches us to be ordered towards God; his *meekness,* towards our neighbour; his *poverty,* towards the world. — He came, then, with *humility;* hence John, the twelfth chapter[^p595-3]: « Behold, thy King cometh sitting on an ass's colt ». It is a great humiliation for a king to ride an ass; but the King rode an ass then, when he sat upon the ass of our humanity. — He came also with *meekness;* Matthew, the twenty-first chapter[^p595-4]: « Behold, thy King cometh to thee, meek, sitting upon an ass » etc. On account of these two the Lord says, Matthew, the eleventh chapter: « Learn of me, because I am meek and humble of heart »; as if he were to say: receive from me the example of meekness and humility. — He came also with *poverty;* hence Zacharias, the ninth chapter[^p595-5]: « Behold, thy King will come to thee, the just and saviour: he is poor, and riding upon an ass, and upon a colt the foal of an ass ». — So therefore you have the example of *humility, meekness* and *poverty* in Christ coming, and this is worthy of imitation in Christ coming.

### Annotation 47.

(Vers. 17.). *The multitude, which was with him when he called Lazarus out of the grave, gave testimony.*

Note that the signs which Christ did drove the multitudes to three things: to *believing;* hence John, the second chapter[^p595-6]: « When he was at Jerusalem on the festival, many believed in him, seeing the signs which he did ». — To *confessing;* hence here: *The multitude gave testimony* etc. — To *following;* hence John, the sixth chapter: « A great multitude followed him, because they saw the signs which he did ».

### Collation XLVII.

(Vers. 32.). *I, if I be exalted from the earth, will draw all things to myself.*

1. In these words the Lord foretells two things: first, the *manner* of his passion, when he says: *When I shall be exalted;* secondly, the *fruit* of his passion, when he says: *I will draw all things to myself.*

He says therefore: *When I shall be exalted from the earth.* — Note that *exaltation* is manifold. For there is the exaltation of the man-Christ — and of the just man — and of the evil man. — Of the exaltation of *Christ* note that Christ was exalted in his *passion,* in his *ascension* and in the *preaching of the Apostles.* — In his passion he was exalted in *punishment;* of which here: *When I shall be exalted* etc.; and John, the third chapter[^p595-7]: « As Moses exalted the serpent in the desert, so must the Son of man be exalted ». — In his ascension he was exalted in *glory.* For then « God exalted him and gave him a name which is above every name, that in the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of those that are in heaven, on earth and under the earth, and that every tongue should confess that the Lord Jesus is in the glory of God the Father », to the Philippians, the second chapter. — In the preaching of the Apostles he was exalted in *fame;* of which in the Psalm: « I will be exalted among the nations, and I will be exalted in the earth »; and again: « Be thou exalted, O God, above the heavens »; which was done in the ascension; « and thy glory above all the earth »; which was done in the promulgation of the Gospel.

2. There follows the exaltation of the *just man,* which is manifold. For God exalts the just man in *grace,* in *glory* and in *fame.* — In *grace* he exalts him, when he calls him from fault to grace; hence the Psalm[^p595-8]: « Thou that exaltest me from the gates of death ». The *gates of death,* by which one enters into death, are sins; but the *gates of life,* grace and the virtues. Then therefore is man *exalted* from the gates of death, when he is called back from the state of sin to the state of grace. Of this exaltation Luke, the eighteenth chapter: « Every one that exalteth himself shall be humbled ». — In *glory* he exalts him, when he calls him from misery to the kingdom, according to that of the Psalm[^p595-9]: « His horn shall be exalted in glory ». Of this exaltation Luke, the fourteenth chapter: « Every one that humbleth himself shall be exalted, and he that exalteth himself shall be humbled ». — In *fame* he exalts him, when he manifests his glory in the Church, when he brings it about that « they exalt him in the church of the people and praise him in the chair of the ancients », according to what is said in the Psalm[^p595-10]; and Matthew, the twenty-third chapter: « Every one that humbleth himself shall be exalted » etc.; where the Lord preaches of not seeking fame or glory in the present.
<!-- page 596 -->

3. Note that one merits to be exalted through three things. For a *servant* is wont to be exalted because he is *dutiful* in the house; a *friend* is wont to be exalted because he is *intimate* in counsel; a *soldier* also is wont to be exalted when he has been *proved* in battle. These three kinds of men kings are wont to exalt, namely faithful servants, intimate friends and proved soldiers: the first for their *service,* the second for their *counsel,* the third for their *fortitude.*

4. Note therefore that the *active* shall be exalted, as servants, by reason of *service;* Isaias, the fifty-second chapter[^p596-1]: « Behold, my servant shall understand, and shall be exalted, and extolled, and shall be exceeding high »; Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter: « Well done, good and faithful servant, because thou hast been faithful over a few things » etc. — Friends are exalted, namely the *contemplative,* for their *wisdom;* Proverbs, the fourth chapter: « Take hold on wisdom, and she shall exalt thee, and thou shalt be glorified by her, when thou shalt embrace her ». — The *perfect* are exalted, as the Martyrs, as it were for their *warfare,* according to that of the Psalm[^p596-2]: « He shall drink of the torrent in the way: therefore shall he exalt the head ». — To all these it is said, the first of Peter, the fifth chapter: « Be you humbled under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in the time of visitation ».

5. There follows the exaltation of the *evil man,* which is manifold. For the evil are exalted in *heart* through pride — in *mouth* through boasting — in *conversation* through pomp. — They are exalted, then, in heart through *pride,* which is forbidden in the Psalm[^p596-3]: « Let not them that provoke be exalted in themselves »; and Job, the fifteenth chapter: « Why doth thy heart elevate thee, and why, as if thinking great things, hast thou astonished eyes »? — They exalt themselves in mouth through *boasting,* like that Pharisee of whom it is said, Luke, the eighteenth chapter: « Because every one that exalteth himself shall be humbled ». For he boasted of his good things. — They are exalted in conversation through *pomp,* though not for long; hence in the Psalm: « The enemies of the Lord, presently after they shall be honoured and exalted, shall come to nothing, like smoke they shall fail ».

6. Such exaltation is to be fled, because it is *ignominious, momentary* and *perilous.* — *Ignominious,* because, the more they are exalted, the more conspicuously their crimes are seen, according to that[^p596-4]:

> Every vice of the mind holds its crime the more conspicuous in itself,
> The greater he who sins is held to be.

The example of the ape, of which, the higher it climbs, the more its hinder parts appear. And this is proved, Isaias, the fourteenth chapter[^p596-5], of Lucifer, who said: « I will ascend, above the stars of heaven I will exalt my throne » etc.; and there follows at once how his hinder parts appeared. — Likewise *momentary,* because it has no permanence, according to that[^p596-6]:

> By the envy of the fates it is denied to the river and to the highest
> To stand long.

And therefore it is said in the Psalm: « I have seen the wicked lifted up and exalted above measure like the cedars of Libanus »; and at once there follows: « And I passed by, and lo, he was not ». The example of smoke, which, the higher it rises, the more it is dispersed and vanishes; hence the Psalm: « Presently after they shall be honoured and exalted, they shall come to nothing, like smoke they shall fail ». — Likewise, *perilous,* because those so exalted come down headlong, as Lucifer came down, who said[^p596-7]: « I will exalt my throne »; and at once it was said to him: « unto hell thou shalt be brought down »; Abdias, the first chapter: « Though thou be exalted as an eagle, and though thou set thy nest among the stars, thence will I bring thee down »; and in Job it is said in the person of such: « Thou hast lifted me up, and, as if setting me upon the wind, thou hast mightily dashed me ». The example of the fool who said that it was good to fly, but that the coming down killed him[^p596-8].

### Annotation 48.

(Vers. 24.). *Unless the grain of wheat falling into the ground be dead, itself remaineth alone* etc.

Look above, at the eleventh chapter[^p596-9], concerning the death of grace.

### Annotation 49.

(Vers. 25.). *He that loveth his soul shall lose it.*

Note that *soul* is sometimes taken for the *natural* life; John, the tenth chapter[^p596-10]: « I lay down my soul for my sheep ». — Sometimes for the *spiritual* life; hence in the Psalm: « He that loveth iniquity hateth his own soul ». — Sometimes for the *carnal* or animal life; Ecclesiasticus, the eighteenth chapter: « If thou give to thy soul her desires, she will make thee a joy to thy enemies »; and here: *He that loveth his soul* etc.

### Annotation 50.

(Vers. 28.). *A voice therefore came from heaven.*

Note that the voice of the Father was heard three times over Christ, namely at his *baptism,* Matthew, the third chapter[^p596-11];
<!-- page 597 -->
and on the *mount,* Matthew, the seventeenth chapter; and on the *way* to Jerusalem, according to what is had here. In which it is signified that God the Father gives testimony to three kinds of men, namely to the *innocent,* who still have their baptismal innocence; to the *active,* walking along the way of good action; and to the *contemplative,* abiding on the mount of contemplation.

### Annotation 51.

(Vers. 46.). *I, a light, am come into the world.*

Note that Christ came as a *light* for *directing;* hence here: *I, a light, am come into the world, that every one who believeth in me may not remain in darkness.* — He came as a *mediator* for *setting free;* hence John, the twelfth chapter[^p597-1]: « I came not into the world to judge the world, but to save the world »; and Luke, the ninth chapter: « The Son of man came not to destroy souls, but to save ». — He came as a *friend* for *feasting together;* Luke, the eleventh chapter: « A friend of mine », that is, Christ, « is come off his journey to me ». — He came as a *bridegroom* for *dwelling together;* Matthew, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p597-2]: « Behold, the bridegroom cometh; go ye forth to meet him ». — He came as a *king* for *presiding;* Zacharias, the ninth chapter: « Behold, thy King will come ». — He came as a *judge* for *rewarding;* Isaias, the third chapter: « The Lord will come to judgment with the ancients of the people and its judges ».
