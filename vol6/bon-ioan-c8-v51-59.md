# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. VIII, Vers. 51-59
## *Ostendit Dominus, quod per doctrinam suam est liberatio a caecitate erroris et servitute peccati; hic iam tertio ostendit, quod per ipsam est liberatio a damnatione mortis*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 369 -->

75. *Amen, amen dico vobis.* Ostendit Dominus, quod per doctrinam suam est liberatio a caecitate erroris et servitute peccati; hic iam tertio ostendit, quod per ipsam[^p369-9] est *liberatio a damnatione mortis.* Et hoc quidem ostenditur sic. Primo Iudaeis conviciantibus ostendit *sermonis sui efficaciam;* secundo contra argumentantibus ostendit *suam excellentiam;* tertio, admirantibus *suam aeternitatem;* quarto, persequentibus *suam patientem humilitatem.*

(Vers. 51.). Ponitur igitur primo *doctrinae Christi commendatio* in hoc, quod ipsam servantes a morte eripiuntur, et hoc sine omni dubitatione; ideo dicit: *Amen, amen dico vobis,* scilicet ego Iesus in veritate hoc assero: *Si quis sermonem meum servaverit, mortem non gustabit*[^p369-10] *in aeternum.* Haec observatio est per custodiam mandatorum; Ecclesiastae octavo: « Qui custodit praeceptum non experietur quidquam mali »; et Sapientiae sexto: « Custoditio legum incorruptionis consummatio est. Incorruptio autem facit, esse proximum Deo ». Et talis mortem non *gustat* per experientiam, mortem scilicet aeternam, licet *videre* possit, secundum illud Isaiae ultimo[^p369-11]: « Egredientur et videbunt cadavera hominum, qui praevaricati sunt in me »; Matthaei decimo sexto: « Sunt de hic stantibus, qui non gustabunt mortem, donec videant Filium hominis venientem in regno suo ».

76. (Vers. 52.). *Dixerunt ergo Iudaei.* Hic secundo Iudaeis oppugnantibus monstrat *suam excellentiam,* quam impugnant docentem; unde dicunt: *Nunc cognovimus, quia daemonium habes;* bonum non poterant cognoscere, sed ad malum erant prompti; Ieremiae quarto[^p369-12]: « Sapientes sunt, ut faciant mala, bene autem facere nescierunt ». Et hoc quidem volunt ostendere improbando Domini dictum, tum quia *falsum,* tum quia *superbum.* *Falsum* ipsum esse, ostendunt hac ratione: *Abraham mortuus est et
<!-- page 370 -->
Prophetae;* et isti servaverunt sermonem Dei: falsum est ergo quod dicis: *Si quis sermonem meum servaverit* etc. Et argumentum illud est *a maiori*[^p370-1]: si enim servantes sermonem Dei mortui sunt; et non habuerunt immortalitatem de quibus magis videtur: ergo etc. Propterea dicunt: *Abraham mortuus est,* qui scilicet Domini sermonem servavit; unde Genesis vigesimo secundo[^p370-2] dictum est Abrahae: « In semine tuo benedicentur omnes gentes terrae, quia obedisti voci meae »;, *et Prophetae mortui sunt,* quibus Dominus est locutus; Ecclesiastici quadragesimo sexto: « Ossa Prophetarum pullulant de loco suo ». *Et tu dicis: Si quis sermonem meum servaverit, mortem non gustabit in aeternum;* quasi dicant: aperte *falsum* dicis; nec tantum falsum[^p370-3] loqueris, sed *superbe;* unde dicunt:

77. (Vers. 53.). *Nunquid tu maior es patre nostro Abraham, qui mortuus est?* Quasi dicant: non es maior, et ipse mortuus est; Genesis vigesimo quinto[^p370-4]: « Deficiens aetate mortuus est senex et plenus dierum »; *et* etiam *Prophetae mortui sunt;* ita nec Abraham nec Prophetae potuerunt mortem evadere: multo minus nec tu. *Quem te ipsum facis?* Quasi dicant: quare te ipsum laudas et extollis? Et sic arguunt eum, quod se laudabat et falsum dicebat, quia se Abrahae praeponebat, cum nullus mortem evadat; secundi Regum decimo quarto[^p370-5]: « Omnes morimur et quasi aquae dilabimur in terram, quae non revertuntur ». — Ad hanc impugnationem respondet Dominus, ostendens suam excellentiam respectu Abrahae. Ante tamen, quam *dicat excellentiam, vitat arrogantiam,* ne videatur hoc dicere per iactantiam; ideo respondendo asserit, se non quaerere propriam gloriam; et propterea dicit:

78. (Vers. 54.). *Respondit Iesus: Si ego glorifico me ipsum, gloria mea nihil est,* id est, vana a vobis reputabitur, sicut vana reputatur gloria, qua quis se ipsum laudat et commendat; unde dixerunt ei supra eodem[^p370-6]: « Tu testimonium perhibes de te ipso, testimonium tuum non est verum ». Id est, *si ego* solus; *ego* discretive tenetur; secundae ad Corinthios decimo: « Non qui se ipsum commendat, ille probatus est, sed quem Deus commendat ». Sed non *ego solus* me glorifico, immo alius mecum; propterea dicit: *Est Pater meus, qui glorificat me;* unde supra primo[^p370-7]: « Vidimus gloriam eius, gloriam quasi Unigeniti a Patre »; secundae Petri primo: « Speculatores facti illius magnitudinis; accipiens enim a Deo Patre honorem et gloriam » etc. *Quem vos dicitis, quia Deus vester est,* id est, quem Patrem dicitis esse Deum vestrum; et ita ei credere debetis; ipse est enim Deus Abraham et Isaac et Iacob, Exodi tertio[^p370-8]; sed non creditis; unde:

79. (Vers. 55.). *Et non cognovistis eum;* erant enim de illis, de quibus dicitur ad Titum primo[^p370-9]: « Confitentur, se nosse Deum, factis autem negant ». *Ego autem novi eum,* et hoc quidem non ob iactantiam, sed propter veritatem dicendam. Unde: *Et si dixero, quia non scio eum; ero similis vobis, mendax;* et ideo oportet dicere, quia novi. Augustinus[^p370-10]: « Sic arrogantia vitetur, ut veritas non relinquatur ». Et ideo hoc iterum asserit: non dico, quia non novi, *sed scio eum et sermonem eius servo;* et hoc est vere nosse Deum, quia dicitur primae Ioannis secundo[^p370-11]: « Qui dicit, se nosse Deum, et mandata eius non custodit, mendax est ». Sic igitur *vitata arrogantia,* manifestat Dominus suam *excellentiam:* quia non credebant, eum esse maiorem Abraham; ideo ostendit, se esse maiorem; ideo dicit:

80. (Vers. 56.). *Abraham, pater vester, exsultavit, ut videret diem meum; vidit et gavisus est,* tanquam de maiori. Gregorius[^p370-12] dicit, quod tunc exsultavit, quando tres viros vidit et unum adoravit, Genesis decimo octavo; unde Lucae decimo: « Beati oculi, qui vident quae vos videtis » etc. — Vel potest dici aliter, quod Abraham *exsultavit, ut videret,* ad Domini vocationem, Genesis duodecimo[^p370-13]. — Sed *vidit et gavisus est,* in Domini *firma promissione,* Genesis decimo quinto[^p370-14], ubi Dominus promisit, et « Abraham credidit, et reputatum est ei ad iustitiam »; et rursus in *confirmatione pacti,* in datione circumcisionis
<!-- page 371 -->
dicitur, quod Abraham risit, unde et gavisus est, Genesis decimo septimo[^p371-1].

81. (Vers. 57.). *Dixerunt ergo Iudaei ad eum.* Hic tertio Iudaeis admirantibus *suam ostendit aeternitatem.* Mirabantur de hoc quod dixit, quod Abraham vidisset diem eius; unde dicunt: *Quinquaginta annos nondum habes et Abraham vidisti?* Quasi dicant: hoc omnino est impossibile. Et notandum, quod non dicunt: Abraham vidit te, sed tu Abraham, quasi semper maiorem reputantes Abraham. Chrysostomus[^p371-2] ponit *quadraginta,* quia secundum eorum aestimationem Dominus appropinquabat ad quadragesimum annum. Huic igitur admirationi subiungit Dominus suam responsionem, in qua suam ostendit aeternitatem. Et ideo non est mirum, si aliis det immortalitatem, quia etiam praecessit Abraham, sicut Creator creaturam; unde:

82. (Vers. 58.). *Dixit ergo*[^p371-3] *eis Iesus: Amen, amen dico vobis,* id est, in veritate assero; *antequam Abraham fieret,* sicut creatura in *esse* producitur, *ego sum.* Non dicit: *ego factus sum,* quia esse non coepit; non dicit: *ego fui,* quia *esse* eius non transit in praeteritum. Ideo dicitur Exodi tertio[^p371-4]: « Ego sum qui sum », quia *esse* eius est increatum et intransibile; unde Gregorius: « Praeteritum tempus Divinitas non habet, sed semper esse habet, ideo recte dicit: *sum,* non *fui* »; Ecclesiastici vigesimo quarto: « Ego ex ore Altissimi prodii, primogenita ante omnem creaturam ».

83. (Vers. 59.). *Tulerunt ergo lapides* etc. Hic quarto Iudaeis persequentibus Dominus *suam ostendit humilitatem;* volebant enim eum lapidare; ideo dicit: *Tulerunt ergo lapides Iudaei*[^p371-5]*, ut iacerent in eum.* Gregorius: « Ista aeternitatis verba mentes infidelium non ferentes, ad lapides cucurrerunt; et quem intelligere non poterant obruere quaerebant ». Ideo volebant eum occidere, quia videbatur blasphemasse; unde Levitici vigesimo quarto[^p371-6]: « Qui blasphemaverit nomen Domini morte moriatur; lapidibus opprimet eum omnis multitudo ». Hanc igitur persecutionem iniustam Dominus fugit humiliter, cum posset superare potenter; unde dicit: *Iesus autem abscondit se et exivit de templo,* secundum illud ad Romanos duodecimo[^p371-7]: « Non vosmetipsos defendentes, sed date locum irae »; et primae Petri secundo: « Cum malediceretur, non maledicebat ».

### Quaestiones.

84. Quaest. I. Sed quaeritur hic: cum Iudaei scandalizati essent et non intelligerent Dominum, quia solum carnaliter sapiebant; unde est, quod Dominus non cessavit a praedicatione?

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod Dominus sic loquebatur, quod ipsi poterant spiritualiter intelligere, sed iusto Dei iudicio excaecati erant; nec tamen a praedicatione cessavit, quia, sicut dicit Gregorius[^p371-8], « cum perversitas malorum crescit, praedicatio non debet minui, sed augeri ».

85. Quaest. II. Quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Si ego glorifico me ipsum, gloria mea nihil est.*

**Contra:** 1. Qui potest testificari potest et laudare; sed supra eodem[^p371-9] dixit, quod de se posset testificari: ergo et se glorificare.

2. Item, opponit haereticus[^p371-10]: Pater glorificat Filium; Filius non potest se glorificare: ergo est minor Patre.

**Respondeo:** Dupliciter solvitur hoc: vel ita, quod hoc quod nunc dictum est, intelligatur secundum *humanam* naturam; vel ita, quod intelligatur *gloria nihil esse* non secundum veritatem, sed secundum Iudaeorum aestimationem[^p371-11].

86. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit, quod non noverunt Patrem, cum dicatur in Lege[^p371-12]: *Deus tuus Deus unus est;* et hoc credebant.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod *Pater* dicitur dupliciter: uno modo *essentialiter,* et sic Iudaei cognoverunt Patrem Deum, quia sic convenit toti Trinitati in comparatione ad creaturam. Alio modo
<!-- page 372 -->
dicitur *personaliter* in relatione ad Filium, et sic non cognoverunt, ut dicit Augustinus[^p372-1], sicut nec Filium.

87. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de hoc, quod Dominus se abscondit. — Videtur, quod timuerit et imperfectus fuerit, quia imperfectorum est timere et fugere.

**Respondeo:** Abscondit se *in signum,* quod « veritas, quando non invenit humilem mentem, fugit », ut dicit Gregorius[^p372-2]. *In exemplum,* « ut, etiam quando resistere possumus, iram superbientium declinemus », sicut etiam dicit Gregorius. *Ad tempus opportunum servandum* abscondit se, qui inferius persecutionibus se obtulit, ut ostenderet se hominem verum: unde Augustinus[^p372-3]: « Tanquam homo a lapidibus fugit, sed vae illis! a quorum lapideis cordibus Deus fugit ».

88. Quaest. V. Item quaeritur de hoc quod dicit: *Est qui quaerat et iudicet.* — *Contra:* Supra quinto[^p372-4]: *Pater non iudicat quemquam, sed omne iudicium dedit Filio.*

**Respondeo:** Est iudicium *condemnationis* et *discretionis;* de primo supra[^p372-5], de secundo habetur hic.

## English
<!-- page 369 -->

75. *Amen, amen I say to you.* The Lord has shown that through his teaching[^tr-doctrina] there is liberation from the blindness of error and the servitude of sin; here now, third, he shows that through it[^p369-9] there is *liberation from the damnation of death.* And this indeed is shown thus. First, to the Jews abusing him, he shows *the efficacy of his word;* second, to those arguing against him, he shows *his excellence;* third, to those wondering, *his eternity;* fourth, to those persecuting him, *his patient humility.*

(V. 51.). First, then, is set down *the commendation of the teaching of Christ* in this, that those who keep it are rescued from death, and this without any doubt; therefore he says: *Amen, amen I say to you,* namely, I Jesus assert this in truth: *If any man keep my word, he shall not taste*[^p369-10] *death for ever.* This observance is through the keeping of the commandments; Ecclesiastes, the eighth chapter: « He that keepeth the precept shall experience no evil »; and Wisdom, the sixth chapter: « The keeping of the laws is the consummation of incorruption. And incorruption maketh one to be near to God ». And such a one does not *taste* death by experience, namely eternal death, although he can *see* it, according to that of Isaias, the last chapter[^p369-11]: « They shall go out, and see the carcasses of the men that have transgressed against me »; Matthew, the sixteenth chapter: « There are some of them that stand here, that shall not taste death, till they see the Son of man coming in his kingdom ».

76. (V. 52.). *The Jews therefore said.* Here, second, to the Jews attacking him he shows *his excellence,* which they impugn as he teaches; whence they say: *Now we know that thou hast a devil;* the good they could not know, but they were prompt to evil; Jeremias, the fourth chapter[^p369-12]: « They are wise to do evils, but to do well they knew not ». And this indeed they wish to show by disproving the Lord's saying, both because it was *false,* and because it was *proud.* That it is *false* they show by this reason: *Abraham is dead, and
<!-- page 370 -->
the prophets;* and these kept the word of God: therefore what thou sayest is false: *If any man keep my word* etc. And that argument is *from the greater*[^p370-1]: for if those who kept the word of God died, and those did not have immortality of whom it rather seems so: therefore etc. Therefore they say: *Abraham is dead,* who namely kept the word of the Lord; whence in Genesis, the twenty-second chapter[^p370-2], it was said to Abraham: « In thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed, because thou hast obeyed my voice »; *and the prophets are dead,* to whom the Lord spoke; Ecclesiasticus, the forty-sixth chapter: « The bones of the prophets spring up out of their place ». *And thou sayest: If any man keep my word, he shall not taste death for ever;* as if to say: thou speakest openly what is *false;* nor dost thou speak only falsely[^p370-3], but *proudly;* whence they say:

77. (V. 53.). *Art thou greater than our father Abraham, who is dead?* As if to say: thou art not greater, and he is dead; Genesis, the twenty-fifth chapter[^p370-4]: « Failing in age he died, an old man and full of days »; *and* also *the prophets are dead;* so neither Abraham nor the prophets could escape death: much less thou. *Whom dost thou make thyself?* As if to say: why dost thou praise and extol thyself? And so they reprove him, that he praised himself and said what was false, because he set himself before Abraham, though no one escapes death; the second of Kings, the fourteenth chapter[^p370-5]: « We all die, and like waters we slide down into the earth, which return not ». — To this impugnation the Lord responds, showing his excellence in respect of Abraham. Yet before he *speaks of his excellence, he avoids arrogance,* lest he seem to say this by way of boasting; therefore in responding he asserts that he does not seek his own glory; and therefore he says:

78. (V. 54.). *Jesus answered: If I glorify myself, my glory is nothing,* that is, it will be reputed vain by you, as that glory is reputed vain by which one praises and commends himself; whence they said to him above, in the same chapter[^p370-6]: « Thou givest testimony of thyself; thy testimony is not true ». That is, *if I* alone; *I* is taken discretively; the second to the Corinthians, the tenth chapter: « For not he who commendeth himself is approved, but he whom God commendeth ». But *I* do not glorify myself *alone,* nay, another with me; therefore he says: *It is my Father that glorifieth me;* whence above, in the first chapter[^p370-7]: « We saw his glory, the glory as it were of the only begotten of the Father »; the second of Peter, the first chapter: « Made eyewitnesses of his greatness; for receiving from God the Father honour and glory » etc. *Of whom you say that he is your God,* that is, whom you say is the Father, your God; and so you ought to believe him; for he is the God of Abraham and Isaac and Jacob, Exodus, the third chapter[^p370-8]; but you do not believe; whence:

79. (V. 55.). *And you have not known him;* for they were of those of whom it is said to Titus, the first chapter[^p370-9]: « They profess that they know God, but in their works they deny him ». *But I know him,* and this indeed not out of boasting, but for the sake of the truth to be spoken. Whence: *And if I shall say that I know him not, I shall be like to you, a liar;* and therefore it is fitting to say that I know him. Augustine[^p370-10]: « Let arrogance be so avoided that truth be not abandoned ». And therefore he asserts this again: I do not say that I do not know him, *but I know him, and keep his word;* and this is truly to know God, because it is said in the first of John, the second chapter[^p370-11]: « He who saith that he knoweth God, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar ». Thus then, *arrogance having been avoided,* the Lord manifests his *excellence:* because they did not believe that he was greater than Abraham, therefore he shows that he is greater; therefore he says:

80. (V. 56.). *Abraham your father rejoiced that he might see my day: he saw it, and was glad,* as of one greater. Gregory[^p370-12] says that he rejoiced then, when he saw three men and adored one, Genesis, the eighteenth chapter; whence Luke, the tenth chapter: « Blessed are the eyes that see the things which you see » etc. — Or it can be said otherwise, that Abraham *rejoiced that he might see,* at the Lord's calling, Genesis, the twelfth chapter[^p370-13]. — But *he saw it, and was glad,* in the Lord's *firm promise,* Genesis, the fifteenth chapter[^p370-14], where the Lord promised, and « Abraham believed, and it was reputed to him unto justice »; and again in *the confirmation of the covenant,* in the giving of circumcision
<!-- page 371 -->
it is said that Abraham laughed, whence also he was glad, Genesis, the seventeenth chapter[^p371-1].

81. (V. 57.). *The Jews therefore said to him.* Here, third, to the Jews wondering, *he shows his eternity.* They wondered at this which he said, that Abraham had seen his day; whence they say: *Thou art not yet fifty years old, and hast thou seen Abraham?* As if to say: this is altogether impossible. And it is to be noted that they do not say: Abraham saw thee, but: thou Abraham, as always reputing Abraham the greater. Chrysostom[^p371-2] puts *forty,* because according to their estimation the Lord was approaching his fortieth year. To this wonder, then, the Lord subjoins his response, in which he shows his eternity. And therefore it is no wonder if he gives immortality to others, because he also preceded Abraham, as the Creator the creature; whence:

82. (V. 58.). *Jesus therefore*[^p371-3] *said to them: Amen, amen I say to you,* that is, I assert in truth; *before Abraham was made,* as a creature is produced into *being, I am.* He does not say: *I was made,* because he did not begin to be; he does not say: *I was,* because his *being* does not pass into the past. Therefore it is said in Exodus, the third chapter[^p371-4]: « I am who am », because his *being* is uncreated and intransient; whence Gregory: « The Godhead has no past time, but always has being; therefore he rightly says: *I am,* not *I was* »; Ecclesiasticus, the twenty-fourth chapter: « I came out of the mouth of the most High, the firstborn before every creature ».

83. (V. 59.). *They took up stones therefore* etc. Here, fourth, to the Jews persecuting him the Lord *shows his humility;* for they wished to stone him; therefore he says: *The Jews therefore took up stones*[^p371-5]*, to cast at him.* Gregory: « Not bearing these words of eternity, the minds of the unbelievers ran to stones; and him whom they could not understand they sought to overwhelm ». Therefore they wished to kill him, because he seemed to have blasphemed; whence Leviticus, the twenty-fourth chapter[^p371-6]: « He that blasphemeth the name of the Lord, let him die the death; all the multitude shall overwhelm him with stones ». This unjust persecution, then, the Lord fled humbly, though he could have overcome it powerfully; whence he says: *But Jesus hid himself, and went out of the temple,* according to that to the Romans, the twelfth chapter[^p371-7]: « Not defending yourselves, but give place unto wrath »; and the first of Peter, the second chapter: « When he was reviled, he did not revile ».

### Questions.

84. Quaest. I. But here it is asked: since the Jews were scandalized and did not understand the Lord, because they savoured only carnally; whence is it that the Lord did not cease from preaching?

**I answer:** It must be said that the Lord so spoke that they could understand spiritually, but by the just judgement of God they had been blinded; and yet he did not cease from preaching, because, as Gregory says[^p371-8], « when the perversity of the wicked grows, preaching ought not to be diminished, but increased ».

85. Quaest. II. It is asked concerning this which he says: *If I glorify myself, my glory is nothing.*

**On the contrary:** 1. He who can testify can also praise; but above, in the same chapter[^p371-9], he said that he could testify of himself: therefore also glorify himself.

2. Likewise, the heretic opposes[^p371-10]: The Father glorifies the Son; the Son cannot glorify himself: therefore he is less than the Father.

**I answer:** This is solved in two ways: either thus, that this which is now said is understood according to the *human* nature; or thus, that *glory being nothing* is understood not according to truth, but according to the estimation of the Jews[^p371-11].

86. Quaest. III. Likewise it is asked concerning this which he says, that they did not know the Father, since it is said in the Law[^p371-12]: *Thy God is one God;* and this they believed.

**I answer:** It must be said that *Father* is said in two ways: in one way *essentially,* and so the Jews knew the Father, God, because so it belongs to the whole Trinity in comparison with the creature. In the other way
<!-- page 372 -->
it is said *personally* in relation to the Son, and so they did not know him, as Augustine says[^p372-1], just as they did not know the Son.

87. Quaest. IV. Likewise it is asked concerning this, that the Lord hid himself. — It seems that he was afraid and was imperfect, because it belongs to the imperfect to fear and to flee.

**I answer:** He hid himself *as a sign* that « truth, when it does not find a humble mind, flees », as Gregory says[^p372-2]. *As an example,* « that, even when we can resist, we may turn aside from the wrath of the proud », as Gregory also says. *To keep the opportune time* he hid himself, who later offered himself to the persecutions, that he might show himself a true man: whence Augustine[^p372-3]: « As man he flees from the stones, but woe to those from whose stony hearts God flees! ».

88. Quaest. V. Likewise it is asked concerning this which he says: *There is one that seeketh and judgeth.* — *On the contrary:* above, in the fifth chapter[^p372-4]: *The Father judgeth not any man, but hath given all judgement to the Son.*

**I answer:** There is a judgement *of condemnation* and one *of discernment;* of the first above[^p372-5], of the second here.
