# Commentarius in Evangelium S. Ioannis — Cap. VIII, Vers. 1-11
## *Posita confutatione insurgentium per potentiam, ponitur hic confutatio insidiantium per malitiam*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 353 -->

### Capitulum VIII.

1. *Iesus autem perrexit* etc. Posita confutatione insurgentium per potentiam, ponitur hic *confutatio insidiantium per malitiam.* Quia non poterant occidere[^p353-5], quaerebant invenire occasionem, ut eum caperent in sermone. Et ideo proposuerunt quaestionem, ut Dominum caperent; sed ipsi e contrario capti sunt et confusi. Et describitur eorum confutatio hoc ordine. Primo enim ponitur *Christi instructio;* secundo vero, *Pharisaeorum malitiosa quaestio;* tertio, *Christi prudens responsio;* quarto, *Pharisaeorum confusio;* quinto, *mulieris liberatio.*

2. (Vers. 1.). Introducitur ergo primo *Christi instructio,* quae erat *diligens, publica* et *authentica. Diligens,* unde dicit: *Iesus autem perrexit in montem Oliveti;* quia in Bethania hospitabatur in domo Mariae et Marthae; Lucae vigesimo primo[^p353-6]:
<!-- page 354 -->
« Erat diebus docens in templo, noctibus autem exiens morabatur in monte, qui vocatur Oliveti ».

3. (Vers. 2.). *Et diluculo iterum venit in templum. Diligentia* notatur in hoc, quod *diluculo* veniebat; Sapientiae sexto[^p354-1]: « Praeoccupat eos qui se concupiscunt, ut eis se prior ostendat », et Proverbiorum octavo: « Qui mane vigilaverint ad me invenient me ». — Erat etiam *publica;* unde dicit: *Et omnis populus venit ad eum;* Lucae vigesimo primo[^p354-2]: « Omnis populus manicabat », id est, mane ibat, « ad eum in templo audire eum ». — Erat etiam *authentica;* propterea dicit: *Et sedens docebat eos; sedens,* quia magistri est *sedere;* Matthaei vigesimo tertio: « Super cathedram Moysi *sederunt* scribae et Pharisaei »; *sederunt,* scilicet ad docendum, sic et ipse.

4. (Vers. 3.). *Adducunt ei*[^p354-3] etc. Tangitur hic secundum, scilicet *malitiosa quaestio.* Quaestio erat de muliere deprehensa in adulterio, utrum esset secundum Legis imperium lapidanda; propter quod dicit: *Adducunt ei scribae et Pharisaei mulierem in adulterio deprehensam;* ideo *scribae et Pharisaei,* quia sagaciores et magis invidebant; scribae invidebant sapientiae, sed Pharisaei bonitati; Lucae undecimo[^p354-4]: « Coeperunt Pharisaei et Legisperiti graviter insistere » etc. *Et statuerunt eam in medio,* quia de ea volebant inquirere.

5. (Vers. 4.). *Et dixerunt ei: Magister,* adulando dicunt, ut decipiant et se occultent; Chrysostomus[^p354-5]: « Magistrum vocant, cuius nolunt esse discipuli ». Simile dixerunt Matthaei vigesimo secundo: « Magister, scimus, quia verax es et viam Dei in veritate doces ». *Haec mulier modo deprehensa est in adulterio,* per facti evidentiam, vel per testes.

6. (Vers. 5.). *In Lege autem mandavit nobis Moyses, huiusmodi lapidare;* Deuteronomii vigesimo secundo[^p354-6]: « Si dormierit vir cum uxore proximi sui, uterque morietur », id est adulter et adultera. *Tu ergo quid dicis?* Hoc quaerebant, non studio quaerendi, sed dolo decipiendi; et hoc exprimit Evangelista:

7. (Vers. 6.). *Hoc autem dicebant, tentantes eum, ut possent eum accusare.* Unde consimili modo quaerentibus respondit Dominus Matthaei vigesimo secundo[^p354-7]: « Quid me tentatis, hypocritae »? *Ut possent accusare eum,* sicut in passione; Matthaei vigesimo septimo: « Et cum accusaretur a senioribus et principibus, nihil respondit ».

*Iesus autem, inclinans se*[^p354-8]. Tangitur hic tertium, scilicet *prudens Christi responsio.* Prudenter enim respondit, quia cum magna modestia. Modestus enim fuit, *non accelerando* sententiam. Unde dicit: *Iesus, inclinans se, digito scribebat in terra,* scilicet exspectans, si quo modo resipiscerent a quaestione dolosa; *digito scribebat,* ut innueret, quod Legem digito scripserat, Deuteronomii nono[^p354-9] et Exodi trigesimo primo. — Modestus etiam fuit *in sententiando,* quia nec declinavit ad rigorem Legis, ut eam diceret occidendam, nec contradixit Legi, ut tribueret occasionem se accusandi; unde dicit:

8. (Vers. 7.). *Cum autem*[^p354-10] *perseverarent interrogantes eum,* et a stultitia nollent desistere; *erexit se et dixit eis: Qui sine peccato est vestrum, primus in illam lapidem mittat.* Inclinatus per misericordiam, *erexit se,* per iustitiam; *et dixit eis,* proferendo sententiam: *Qui sine* etc., infligendo poenam. In qua responsione modesta confutavit eos tanquam hypocritas, secundum illud Matthaei septimo[^p354-11]: « Quid vides festucam in oculo fratris tui, trabem autem in oculo tuo non vides? Eiice primum trabem de oculo tuo, et tunc videbis eiicere festucam de oculo fratris tui ». — Modestus etiam fuit *post sententiae prolationem,* unde dicit:

9. (Vers. 8.). *Et iterum se inclinans, scribebat in terra,* ut sic confusis daret locum sine verecundia exeundi. Unde Beda[^p354-12]: « Coram tentatoribus inclinari et in terra scribere voluit, ut, ad aliud vultum intendens, liberum eis daret exitum, quos sua responsione percussos citius exituros quam plura interrogaturos praeviderat »; Ieremiae decimo septimo: « Omnes, qui te derelinquunt, confundentur; recedentes a te in terra scribentur »; Ieremiae vigesimo secundo: « Terra, terra, audi sermonem Domini: Scribe virum istum sterilem ».

10. (Vers. 9.). *Audientes autem haec*[^p354-13] etc. Notatur hic quartum, scilicet *Pharisaeorum confusio,* quae manifestata est in eorum dispersione; propterea dicit: *Audientes autem haec,* scilicet tam prudentem et iustam responsionem; *unus post unum exibant, incipientes a senioribus.* Dispersi recedebant qui con-
<!-- page 355 -->
globati venerant, et seniores prius exibant, quia ipsi primi fuerant in culpa, ideo et in fuga; Danielis decimo tertio[^p355-1]: « Egressa est iniquitas a senioribus iudicibus, qui videbantur regere populum »; et omnes recesserunt, quia omnes confusi erant. Unde dicit: *Et remansit Iesus solus et mulier in medio stans. Solus remansit,* qui locum solitudinis requirit; Osee secundo[^p355-2]: « Adducam eam in solitudinem et ibi loquar ad cor eius »; vel quia solus erat offensus; Psalmus: « Tibi soli peccavi et malum coram te feci ». Augustinus[^p355-3]: « Duo remanserunt: miseria et misericordia »; et quia misericordia intuetur miseriam, ideo subdit:

11. (Vers. 10.). *Erigens autem se Iesus,* in quo notatur quintum, scilicet *mulieris accusatae misericors liberatio.* Et quia misericordia currit cum iustitia et non est contra iustitiam, quaerit Dominus ab ea de condemnatione et accusatione. Propterea dicit[^p355-4]: *Mulier, ubi sunt qui te accusabant? Nemo te condemnavit?* Duo quaerit, et quia alterum est in evidenti de recessu accusatorum, respondet mulier quaestioni de condemnatione.

12. (Vers. 11.). *Quae dixit: Nemo, Domine,* supple: me condemnavit, non est hominis condemnare; Matthaei septimo[^p355-5]: « Nolite condemnare » etc.; et ideo tibi relicta sum. Et sequitur misericors mulieris liberatio; unde dicit: *Dixit autem Iesus: Nec ego te condemnabo;* ecce, *misericordia;* ad Romanos octavo: « Deus qui iustificat, quis est qui condemnet »? *Vade et amplius*[^p355-6] *noli peccare;* ecce, *iustitia,* quae sic absolvit a culpa, ut tamen non det libertatem redeundi ad culpam; Ecclesiastici quinto: « Misericordia et ira ab illo cito proximant, et in peccatores respicit ira illius ». Unde Augustinus[^p355-7]: « Attendant qui amant in Domino mansuetudinem, et timeant veritatem; etenim *dulcis et rectus Dominus.* Amas, quod dulcis est? Time, quod rectus est. Tanquam mansuetus dicit: *Tacui;* sed tanquam rectus dicit: *Ut parturiens loquar* », Isaiae quadragesimo secundo. Ideo subdit: *Amplius noli peccare.*

### Quaestiones.

13. Quaest. I. Sed incidit hic dubitatio: cum quaestio ista faciliter esset determinata ex Lege, et qui tentat debeat dubia quaerere; unde est hoc, quod talem quaestionem Pharisaei dolosi Domino proposuerunt?

Respondent quidam, quod videtur totum industria humana factum; unde dixerunt, quod hoc fuit immixtum Evangelio Ioannis, sicut historia Susannae Danieli[^p355-8]. In cuius rei testimonium adduxerunt, quod Chrysostomus de hoc nullam mentionem facit; unde saltat ad illud[^p355-9]: *Iterum locutus est Iesus* etc. — Sed Augustinus[^p355-10] et expositores nostri exponunt de serie Evangelii. — Quod vero Chrysostomus nihil dicit, causam nescio. Quidam dicunt, quod in antiquis libris Graecis non inveniebatur, quia Ioannes addidit Evangelio completo[^p355-11].

Propterea dicendum est, quod Pharisaei invidebant Domino de hoc, quod laudabatur de mansuetudine et iustitia simul, secundum illud Psalmi[^p355-12]: *Propter veritatem et mansuetudinem et iustitiam.* Ideo quaestionem proponunt, in qua necesse haberet declinare in rigorem iustitiae contra mansuetudinem, ut haberetur immitis, vel contradicere iustitiae Legis. Et quasi insolubile confecerant[^p355-13], ut, utralibet parte
<!-- page 356 -->
data, suum obtinerent propositum; sed Dominus sapienter respondit ad hominem, non ad orationem.

14. Quaest. II. Item quaeritur de hoc, quod Dominus in prolatione suae sententiae scribebat in terra.

1. Videbatur enim factum puerile, quia non erat manens[^p356-1], nec aliquis poterat legere.

2. Item, quare *digito* scribebat?

3. Item, *quid* scribebat?

**Respondeo:** « *Moralis* ratio, quare *scribebat* ante sententiam et post *in terra,* ut doceat nos, sicut dicit Beda[^p356-2], ut, « priusquam peccantem proximum corripiamus, et postquam castigationis ministerium reddiderimus, nos ipsos digna humilitatis investigatione perpendamus, ne forte aut eisdem, quae reprehendimus, aut aliis simus peccatis irretiti ». — Alia ratio, ut ostendat, quod sententia cum maturitate est proferenda et primo scribenda, et verba limitanda secundum ordinem iuris[^p356-3]. — Tertia ratio, ut ostendat, quod invitus condemnat, et sententiam damnationis invitus et cum dolore debet homo proferre[^p356-4]. — Quarta ratio, ut locum desistendi daret a causa malitiosa; et hoc quidem spectat ad iudices[^p356-5].

3. Quod quaeritur: *quid* scribebat? Respondet Augustinus[^p356-6], quod illud scripsit, quod protulit; Ambrosius[^p356-7] vero dicit, quod scripsit: *Terra, terra, scribe viros abdicatos,* et tractum est de Ieremiae vigesimo secundo. Interlinearis[^p356-8] dicit, quod scribebat « peccata eorum ». Alii dicunt, quod scribebat litteras, in quibus quilibet posset legere peccata sua[^p356-9].

2. Quod quaeritur: quare scripsit *digito?* Ratio est *litteralis,* quia credo, quod non habebat alium calamum. Ratio etiam est *moralis* et *allegorica: moralis,* quia per *digitum* intelligitur discretio; voluit, quemquam se discrete examinare[^p356-10]; *allegorica,* ut significaret, quod ille erat, qui digito scripserat Legem, Exodi trigesimo primo[^p356-11].

15. Quaest. III. Item quaeritur de responsione Domini: *Qui sine peccato est vestrum, primus in illam lapidem mittat.*

Ex hac responsione videtur, quod nullus possit aliquem accusare, quia *nullus mundus a sorde*[^p356-12], et nullus sine peccato; et si hoc, remanebunt scelera impunita. — *Si dicas,* quod Dominus intellexit de peccato *mortali;* tunc *obiicitur:* quia praeceptum est corripere fratrem et accusare[^p356-13]: ergo si nullus peccat faciendo ad quod tenetur, ergo si peccator tenetur accusare, quia non absolvitur propter culpam: ergo non peccat accusando. — *Si dicas,* quod peccat non in hoc, quod facit, sed in hoc, quod non *digne* facit; *obiicitur:* quia hoc non est opus privilegiatum[^p356-14].

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod peccator existens in mortali, qui alterum accusat, aut est peccator *notorius* eodem genere peccati; et tunc scandalizat, quantum in se est, et peccat peccato duplici, scilicet *scandali* et *contemptus.* Peccator autem *occultus,* sive in eodem genere peccati, sive in alio; si scit, se habere peccatum, et considerat peccatum fraternum et iudicat et se ipsum negligit, peccat peccato *contemptus,* non in hoc, quod accusat, sed in hoc, quod
<!-- page 357 -->
cum se deberet purgare et discutere, negligit et contemnit. Unde ita intelligendum est verbum Domini; et sic patet responsio ad illud[^p357-1].

16. Quaest. IV. Item quaeritur de absolutione mulieris.

1. Videtur, quod Dominus directe fecerit contra Legem: quia quam Lex damnabat[^p357-2] ipse absolvit, et adhuc tempus Legis et observantia durabat.

2. Item videtur, quod iniuste fecerit: quia nullam poenam imposuit, videtur, quod licentiam peccandi dederit[^p357-3].

**Respondeo:** Dominus non fecit *contra Legem,* quia ipse erat *supra Legem* et poterat dispensare in praecepto Legis. Nec tamen *fecit contra,* quia accusatores non perstiterunt in accusando, et sola erat mulier et sine accusatore, et talem Lex non damnabat. Unde prius repulit adversarios *lingua iustitiae,* et postmodum, quando sibi relicta est, levat *oculos misericordiae*[^p357-4]. Nec tamen eam condemnasset, si adversarii perstitissent, ut dicit Augustinus[^p357-5]: « Absit, ut ille damnaret, qui venit salvare ».

2. Ad aliud dicendum, quod Dominus erat, et ideo totum poterat condonare; nec tamen condonando favit peccatis, quia prohibuit, ut amplius non peccaret; et hoc, quia, sicut dicit Augustinus[^p357-6], « damnavit Deus non hominem, sed peccatum ».

## English
<!-- page 353 -->

### Chapter VIII.

1. *And Jesus went* etc. Having set down the refutation of those rising up through power, here is set down *the refutation of those lying in wait through malice.* Because they could not kill him[^p353-5], they sought to find an occasion, that they might catch him in speech. And therefore they put forward a question, that they might catch the Lord; but they themselves on the contrary were caught and confounded. And their refutation is described in this order. For first is set down *Christ's instruction;* but second, *the malicious question of the Pharisees;* third, *Christ's prudent answer;* fourth, *the confusion of the Pharisees;* fifth, *the woman's deliverance.*

2. (Verse 1.). First, then, is introduced *Christ's instruction,* which was *diligent, public* and *authentic. Diligent,* whence he says: *And Jesus went unto mount Olivet;* because he lodged in Bethania in the house of Mary and Martha; Luke the twenty-first[^p353-6]:
<!-- page 354 -->
« He was in the daytime teaching in the temple; but at night, going out, he abode in the mount that is called Olivet ».

3. (Verse 2.). *And early in the morning he came again into the temple. Diligence* is noted in this, that he came *early in the morning;* Wisdom the sixth[^p354-1]: « She preventeth them that covet her, so that she first sheweth herself unto them », and Proverbs the eighth: « They that in the morning early watch for me shall find me ». — It was also *public;* whence he says: *And all the people came to him;* Luke the twenty-first[^p354-2]: « All the people came early in the morning », that is, went in the morning, « to him in the temple, to hear him ». — It was also *authentic;* therefore he says: *And sitting he taught them; sitting,* because it belongs to a teacher *to sit;* Matthew the twenty-third: « Upon the chair of Moses *have sitten* the scribes and the Pharisees »; *have sitten,* namely to teach, so he too.

4. (Verse 3.). *They bring unto him*[^p354-3] etc. Here the second is touched on, namely *the malicious question.* The question was about the woman taken in adultery, whether according to the command of the Law she was to be stoned; on account of which he says: *The scribes and Pharisees bring unto him a woman taken in adultery;* therefore *the scribes and Pharisees,* because they were the shrewder and envied the more; the scribes envied his wisdom, but the Pharisees his goodness; Luke the eleventh[^p354-4]: « The Pharisees and the lawyers began violently to urge » etc. *And they set her in the midst,* because they wished to inquire about her.

5. (Verse 4.). *And they said to him: Teacher,* they say it in flattery, that they may deceive and hide themselves; Chrysostom[^p354-5]: « They call him teacher whose disciples they do not wish to be ». They said the like in Matthew the twenty-second: « Teacher, we know that thou art a true speaker, and teachest the way of God in truth ». *This woman was but now taken in adultery,* by the evidence of the deed, or by witnesses.

6. (Verse 5.). *Now in the Law Moses commanded us to stone such a one;* Deuteronomy the twenty-second[^p354-6]: « If a man lie with the wife of his neighbour, both shall die », that is, the adulterer and the adulteress. *What then sayest thou?* This they asked, not from an eagerness to inquire, but from guile to deceive; and this the Evangelist expresses:

7. (Verse 6.). *And this they said tempting him, that they might accuse him.* Whence in like manner the Lord answered those asking, in Matthew the twenty-second[^p354-7]: « Why do you tempt me, ye hypocrites »? *That they might accuse him,* as in the passion; Matthew the twenty-seventh: « And when he was accused by the ancients and the chief men, he answered nothing ».

*But Jesus, bowing himself*[^p354-8]. Here the third is touched on, namely *Christ's prudent answer.* For he answered prudently, because with great modesty. For he was modest *in not hastening* the sentence. Whence he says: *Jesus, bowing himself, wrote with his finger on the ground,* namely waiting, if in any way they might come to their senses from the guileful question; *he wrote with his finger,* that he might intimate that he had written the Law with his finger, Deuteronomy the ninth[^p354-9] and Exodus the thirty-first. — He was modest also *in sentencing,* because he neither inclined to the rigour of the Law, so as to say she was to be killed, nor contradicted the Law, so as to give occasion of accusing himself; whence he says:

8. (Verse 7.). *But when*[^p354-10] *they continued asking him,* and would not desist from their folly; *he lifted up himself and said to them: He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone at her.* Having bowed through mercy, *he lifted up himself,* through justice; *and said to them,* in pronouncing the sentence: *He that is without* etc., in inflicting the penalty. In which modest answer he refuted them as hypocrites, according to that of Matthew the seventh[^p354-11]: « Why seest thou the mote in thy brother's eye, but the beam in thy own eye thou seest not? Cast out first the beam out of thy own eye, and then shalt thou see to cast out the mote out of thy brother's eye ». — He was modest also *after the pronouncing of the sentence,* whence he says:

9. (Verse 8.). *And again bowing himself, he wrote on the ground,* that so he might give the confounded room to go out without shame. Whence Bede[^p354-12]: « Before the tempters he willed to bow down and write on the ground, so that, turning his face to something else, he might give a free exit to those whom he had foreseen would, struck by his answer, sooner go out than ask more »; Jeremias the seventeenth: « All that forsake thee shall be confounded; they that depart from thee shall be written in the earth »; Jeremias the twenty-second: « Earth, earth, hear the word of the Lord: Write this man barren ».

10. (Verse 9.). *But they hearing these things*[^p354-13] etc. Here the fourth is noted, namely *the confusion of the Pharisees,* which was made manifest in their dispersion; therefore he says: *But they hearing these things,* namely so prudent and just an answer; *went out one by one, beginning at the elders.* Dispersed, they withdrew who had come in a
<!-- page 355 -->
body, and the elders went out first, because they themselves had been first in the fault, therefore also in the flight; Daniel the thirteenth[^p355-1]: « Iniquity came out from the ancient judges, that seemed to govern the people »; and all withdrew, because all were confounded. Whence he says: *And Jesus remained alone, and the woman standing in the midst. He remained alone,* who seeks the place of solitude; Osee the second[^p355-2]: « I will lead her into the solitude, and there I will speak to her heart »; or because he alone was offended; the Psalm: « To thee only have I sinned, and have done evil before thee ». Augustine[^p355-3]: « Two remained: misery and mercy »; and because mercy looks upon misery, therefore he adds:

11. (Verse 10.). *But Jesus lifting up himself,* in which the fifth is noted, namely *the merciful deliverance of the accused woman.* And because mercy runs with justice and is not against justice, the Lord asks of her about condemnation and accusation. Therefore he says[^p355-4]: *Woman, where are they that accused thee? Hath no man condemned thee?* He asks two things, and because the one is evident from the withdrawal of the accusers, the woman answers the question about condemnation.

12. (Verse 11.). *Who said: No man, Lord,* supply: has condemned me, it is not man's to condemn; Matthew the seventh[^p355-5]: « Condemn not » etc.; and therefore I am left to thee. And there follows the merciful deliverance of the woman; whence he says: *And Jesus said: Neither will I condemn thee;* behold, *mercy;* to the Romans the eighth: « God that justifieth, who is he that shall condemn »? *Go, and sin*[^p355-6] *no more;* behold, *justice,* which so absolves from the fault, that yet it does not give liberty of returning to the fault; Ecclesiasticus the fifth: « Mercy and wrath quickly draw near from him, and upon sinners his wrath looketh ». Whence Augustine[^p355-7]: « Let those who love meekness in the Lord take heed, and let them fear truth; for *the Lord is sweet and righteous.* Do you love that he is sweet? Fear that he is righteous. As meek he says: *I have held my peace;* but as righteous he says: *I will speak as a woman in labour* », Isaias the forty-second. Therefore he adds: *Sin no more.*

### Questions.

13. Quaest. I. But here a doubt arises: since this question was easily determined from the Law, and he who tempts ought to ask doubtful things; whence is it that the guileful Pharisees put such a question to the Lord?

Some answer that the whole seems to have been made by human industry; whence they said that this was mingled into the Gospel of John, as the history of Susanna into Daniel[^p355-8]. In testimony of which thing they adduced that Chrysostom makes no mention of it; whence he leaps to that[^p355-9]: *Again Jesus spoke* etc. — But Augustine[^p355-10] and our expositors expound it as of the sequence of the Gospel. — But why Chrysostom says nothing, I do not know the cause. Some say that it was not found in the ancient Greek books, because John added it when the Gospel was completed[^p355-11].

Therefore it must be said that the Pharisees envied the Lord for this, that he was praised for meekness and justice together, according to that of the Psalm[^p355-12]: *Because of truth and meekness and justice.* Therefore they put forward a question in which he would of necessity either incline to the rigour of justice against meekness, so as to be held unmerciful, or contradict the justice of the Law. And they had made it as it were insoluble[^p355-13], so that, whichever side
<!-- page 356 -->
was given, they would obtain their purpose; but the Lord wisely answered to the man, not to the argument.

14. Quaest. II. Again it is asked about this, that the Lord in the pronouncing of his sentence wrote on the ground.

1. For it seemed a childish deed, because it was not lasting[^p356-1], nor could anyone read it.

2. Again, why did he write *with his finger?*

3. Again, *what* did he write?

**I respond:** « The *moral* reason why he *wrote* before the sentence and after *on the ground,* that he may teach us, as Bede says[^p356-2], that « before we rebuke a sinning neighbour, and after we have rendered the ministry of chastisement, we should weigh ourselves by a worthy examination of humility, lest perhaps we be entangled either in the same sins which we rebuke, or in others ». — Another reason, that he may show that a sentence is to be pronounced with maturity and first written, and the words limited according to the order of law[^p356-3]. — The third reason, that he may show that he condemns unwillingly, and a man ought to pronounce a sentence of damnation unwillingly and with sorrow[^p356-4]. — The fourth reason, that he might give room for desisting from a malicious cause; and this indeed pertains to judges[^p356-5].

3. As to what is asked: *what* did he write? Augustine answers[^p356-6] that he wrote that which he uttered; but Ambrose[^p356-7] says that he wrote: *Earth, earth, write these men disowned,* and it is drawn from Jeremias the twenty-second. The Interlinear[^p356-8] says that he wrote « their sins ». Others say that he wrote letters in which anyone could read his own sins[^p356-9].

2. As to what is asked: why did he write *with his finger?* The reason is *literal,* because, I believe, he had no other pen. The reason is also *moral* and *allegorical: moral,* because by the *finger* is understood discretion; he willed that each one examine himself with discretion[^p356-10]; *allegorical,* that he might signify that he was the one who had written the Law with his finger, Exodus the thirty-first[^p356-11].

15. Quaest. III. Again it is asked about the Lord's answer: *He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone at her.*

From this answer it seems that no one can accuse anyone, because *no one is clean from filth*[^p356-12], and no one is without sin; and if this is so, crimes will remain unpunished. — *If you say* that the Lord meant it of *mortal* sin; then *it is objected:* because it is a precept to rebuke one's brother and to accuse[^p356-13]: therefore if no one sins in doing that to which he is bound, then if a sinner is bound to accuse, because he is not absolved on account of his fault: therefore he does not sin in accusing. — *If you say* that he sins not in this, that he does it, but in this, that he does not do it *worthily;* *it is objected:* because this is not a privileged work[^p356-14].

**I respond:** It must be said that a sinner existing in mortal sin who accuses another either is a *notorious* sinner in the same kind of sin; and then he gives scandal, as far as in him lies, and sins with a double sin, namely of *scandal* and of *contempt.* But a *hidden* sinner, whether in the same kind of sin or in another; if he knows that he has sin, and considers his brother's sin and judges it and neglects himself, sins with the sin of *contempt,* not in this, that he accuses, but in this, that
<!-- page 357 -->
when he ought to purge and examine himself, he neglects and contemns it. Thus therefore is the Lord's word to be understood; and so the answer to that is plain[^p357-1].

16. Quaest. IV. Again it is asked about the absolution of the woman.

1. It seems that the Lord acted directly against the Law: because her whom the Law condemned[^p357-2] he absolved, and the time of the Law and its observance still endured.

2. Again it seems that he acted unjustly: because he imposed no penalty, it seems that he gave licence for sinning[^p357-3].

**I respond:** The Lord did not act *against the Law,* because he was *above the Law* and could dispense in the precept of the Law. Nor yet did he *act against it,* because the accusers did not persist in accusing, and the woman was alone and without an accuser, and such a one the Law did not condemn. Whence first he repelled the adversaries *with the tongue of justice,* and afterwards, when she was left to him, he lifts *the eyes of mercy*[^p357-4]. Nor yet would he have condemned her, if the adversaries had persisted, as Augustine says[^p357-5]: « Far be it that he should condemn, who came to save ».

2. To the other it must be said that he was the Lord, and therefore could forgive the whole; nor yet in forgiving did he favour sins, because he forbade that she sin any more; and this, because, as Augustine says[^p357-6], « God condemned not the man, but the sin ».
