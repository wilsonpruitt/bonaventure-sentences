## *Utrum aliquis possit decimari in parentibus*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 81 -->

### Articulus I. *De Christi decimatione.*
### Quaestio I. *Utrum aliquis possit decimari in parentibus.*

Circa primum sic proceditur et quaeritur, utrum aliquis decimari habeat in parentibus. Et quod sic, probatur:

1. Per illud Apostoli ad Hebraeos septimo[^1]: *Per Abraham et Levi, qui decimas accepit, decimatus est.*

2. Item, hoc ipsum videtur per Augustinum, decimo *super Genesim ad litteram*, et habetur in Glossa[^2]: «Sicut, Adam peccante, peccaverunt qui in lumbis eius erant; sic, Abraham dante decimas, qui in lumbis eius erant decimati sunt»; sed constat, quod proles potuit peccare in Adam: ergo et decimari in Abraham.

3. Item, hoc ipsum videtur ratione: quia proles in parentibus potest liberari et in servitutem redigi, quia servus natus est servilis conditionis[^3]: ergo pari ratione videtur, quod in parentibus dantibus decimas filii habeant decimari.

4. Item, decimatio est actus *figuralis*[^4]; sed figura non tantum est respectu praesentis, sed praeteriti et futuri: ergo videtur, quod non tantum possit decimari in propria persona filius, sed etiam cum est in parente tanquam in causa et materia.

**Sed contra:** 1. Augustinus decimo *super Genesim ad litteram*[^5]: «In lumbis Abrahae illud decimabatur quod curabatur»; sed filii Abrahae non sunt curati ab originali, immo nati sunt cum peccato originali: ergo non videtur, quod in Abraham fuerint decimati.

2. Item, cum datur decima de frumento, non propter hoc datur decima de illo frumento, quod debet nasci[^6]: ergo pari ratione, cum datur decima pro homine, non videtur, quod ille qui nasciturus est ex eo, decimetur in eo — sicut enim homo est in homine seminaliter, sic granum in grano — ergo ratio ista bona videtur.

3. Item, sicut circumcisio remedium est contra originale in Lege figurae, et baptismus in Lege gratiae, sic decimatio in Lege naturae, ut dicit Hugo de sancto Victore[^7]; sed nullus in ventre matris potuit baptizari nec circumcidi: ergo nec decimari.

4. Item, aut *decimari* est per decimam curari, aut *decimari* est ad decimam obligari, aut *decimari* est de aliquo decimam partem dare, aut *decimari* est per decimam praefigurari; sed nullo istorum modorum fuit Levi in Abraham decimatus, quia nec per decimam curatus, nec ad dandum decimas obligatus, cum potius reciperet[^8], nec decimam dedit, nec per decimam significatus fuit: ergo nullo modo videtur decimatus fuisse in Abraham: et si ille non fuit decimatus, pari ratione nec aliqui alii.

### Conclusio

> Proles aliquo modo potest in parentibus decimari, secundum eum modum, quo decimatio nihil ponit in actu circa decimatos, sed est actus *figuralis*, *significans futuram curationem*.

**Respondeo:** Ad praedictorum intelligentiam est notandum, quod decimatio fuit actus *moralis*, *sacramentalis* et *figuralis*. Secundum quod fuit actus

<!-- page 82 -->

*moralis*, sic decimatio dicitur alicuius certae partis donatio ad sustentationem eorum qui serviunt Domino et deputati sunt cultui divino; et hoc modo decimatio aliquo modo perseverat usque modo[^9]. — Secundum autem quod decimatio est actus *sacramentalis*, sic decimatio est alicuius partis donatio ad curationem morbi originalis. — Secundum autem quod decimatio est actus *figuralis*, sic decimatio est decimae partis donatio ad praefigurationem curationis universalis, quae futura erat per oblationem Christi.

Duobus primis modis accepta decimatio respicit tantum praesens[^10]; quia non datur decima nisi de hoc, quod habetur, et non curatur nisi quod iam habet morbum. Tertio autem modo decimatio accepta non tantum est praesentis, sed etiam futuri, pro eo quod *figuratio* est *significatio*, et significatio non tantum extendit se ad praesentia, sed etiam ad futura; et hoc modo dicuntur filii Abrahae decimati in Abraham, quia per illam decimationem praefigurati sunt omnes filii Abraham curandi esse per eum qui significabatur per Melchisedech, id est per verum sacerdotem Christum. Et hoc est quod dicit Augustinus decimo *super Genesim ad litteram*[^11]: «Cum ipsa decimatio ad praefigurandam medicinam pertinuerit, ille in Abraham decimabatur, qui curabatur», non quando praefigurabatur, sed quando illius figurae veritas implebatur. — Concedendae sunt igitur rationes ostendentes, quod aliquo modo potest decimari proles in parente, secundum eum modum, quo decimatio nihil ponit in actu circa decimatos, sed solum secundum significationem, quae non tantum est praesentium, sed etiam futurorum.

*Ad 1.* Ad illud quod obiicitur primo de auctoritate Augustini, dicendum, quod sicut patet ex verbo praecedenti[^12], *decimabatur quod curabatur* non dicit concomitantiam secundum idem tempus ad hoc quod est *decimari*, immo potius se extendit ad omne tempus, et pro illo tempore reddit locutionem veram, in quo implebatur veritatis figura.

*Ad 2.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod non datur decima de grano, quod non est in re, sed solum in ratione seminali; dicendum, quod non est simile, quia decimatio, prout est actus *figuralis*, non se extendit ad significandum aliquid circa granum, sed ad significandum aliquid circa genus humanum, unde granum respicit ut materiam, circa quam habet exerceri, non circa illud quod per ipsum habet significari. Et quia *exercitium* requirit materiam praesentem, *significatio* se extendit ad futura[^13], ideo non est simile.

*Ad 3.* Ad illud quod obiicitur de baptismo et circumcisione, dicendum, quod non est simile, nisi in quantum decimatio est actus *sacramentalis* Legis naturae ad remedium originalis peccati; hoc modo non est nisi circa praesens, sicut circumcisio et baptismus. Secundum autem quod est actus *figuralis*, sic non est simile.

Si autem quaeratur, quare baptismus et circumcisio non potest esse actus figuralis, sicut et decimatio; dicendum, quod ratio huius est, quia circumcisio et baptismus est actus *in persona*; decimatio autem, cum sit decimae partis oblatio, est actus *pro persona*. Et quia oblatio potest fieri pro eo quod est et quod futurum est, circumcidi et baptizari non nisi[^14] quod iam est; hinc est, quod actus decimandi potuit se extendere ad futuros, et non actus circumcidendi, vel baptizandi.

*Ad 4.* Ad illud quod quaeritur, quomodo proles dicitur decimari in parente; dicendum, quod decimatio idem est quod praefiguratio curationis. Unde nullo illorum modorum accipitur, qui in opponendo assignabantur[^15]: et ideo insufficienter procedebat illa obiectio.

### Scholion

**I.** Duae huius articuli quaestiones solvunt duas difficultates, quae occurrunt in argumentatione Apostoli, Hebr. 7, quarum prima est, quomodo Levi ideo decimatus fuerit in Abraham (ibi v. 9. 10.), quia in lumbis eius erat; et altera, quomodo Christus, qui similiter in Abraham fuisse videtur, in ipso non fuerit decimatus, quod Apostolus manifeste supponit. Primam difficultatem solvit q. 1; alteram q. 2. — Solutio primae difficultatis eruitur ex hoc principio, quod decimatio sive decimarum oblatio, quae instituta fuit, ut sacerdos pro peccatis offerentium deprecaretur, non sit tantum actus *moralis*, sed etiam *figuralis*, ut explicatur a nostro Doctore secundum sententiam communem. Iam inde patet, decimationem minime convenire Christo. Alia ratio sumitur ex eo, quod ipse erat in Abraham alio modo ac Levi. — De hac et seq. quaestione late tractat Gulielmus Antissiodorrensis (rectius Autissiod.) in sua *Summa*, dicta *aurea* p. III, quae typis edita est Parisiis a Franc. Regnault (sine anno).

**II.** De utraque quaestione: Alex. Hal., S. p. III. q. 9. m. 3. a. 4. — Scot., Report. hic q. 2. — S. Thom., hic q. 1. a. 3; S. III. q. 31. a. 8. — B. Albert., hic a. 26. 27. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 2. a. 3. — Richard. a Med., hic a. 3. q. 2. 3. — Aegid. R., hic q. 4. a. 2. et dub. lit. — Dionys. Carth., hic q. 4. — Biel, hic q. 2.

## English

### Article I. *On the tithing of Christ.*
### Question I. *Whether anyone can be tithed in his forefathers.*

Concerning the first point one proceeds thus and asks whether anyone has to be tithed in his forefathers. And that this is so is proved:

1. By that saying of the Apostle to the Hebrews, chapter seven[^1]: *Through Abraham, Levi too, who received tithes, was tithed.*

2. Likewise, this same thing seems to follow from Augustine, in the tenth book *On Genesis according to the Letter*, and it is found in the Gloss[^2]: «Just as, when Adam sinned, those who were in his loins sinned; so, when Abraham gave tithes, those who were in his loins were tithed»; but it is established that the offspring could sin in Adam: therefore also be tithed in Abraham.

3. Likewise, this same thing seems to follow by reason: because offspring can be set free and reduced to servitude in their forefathers, since a slave is born of servile condition[^3]: therefore by like reasoning it seems that, when forefathers give tithes, the sons have to be tithed in them.

4. Likewise, tithing is a *figurative* act[^4]; but a figure regards not only the present, but also the past and the future: therefore it seems that a son can be tithed not only in his own person, but also when he is in his forefather as in a cause and matter.

**On the contrary:** 1. Augustine, in the tenth book *On Genesis according to the Letter*[^5]: «In the loins of Abraham that was tithed which was healed»; but the sons of Abraham were not healed of original sin, but rather were born with original sin: therefore it does not seem that they were tithed in Abraham.

2. Likewise, when a tithe is given of grain, a tithe is not on that account given of that grain which is to be born[^6]: therefore by like reasoning, when a tithe is given for a man, it does not seem that he who is to be born from him is tithed in him — for just as a man is in a man seminally, so is a grain in a grain — therefore this reasoning seems sound.

3. Likewise, just as circumcision is a remedy against original sin under the Law of figure, and baptism under the Law of grace, so tithing under the Law of nature, as Hugh of Saint Victor says[^7]; but no one could be baptized or circumcised in his mother's womb: therefore neither tithed.

4. Likewise, either *to be tithed* is to be healed by the tithe, or *to be tithed* is to be obligated to the tithe, or *to be tithed* is to give a tenth part of something, or *to be tithed* is to be prefigured by the tithe; but by none of these modes was Levi tithed in Abraham, since he was neither healed by the tithe, nor obligated to give tithes, since he rather received them[^8], nor did he give a tithe, nor was he signified by the tithe: therefore in no way does he seem to have been tithed in Abraham; and if he was not tithed, by like reasoning neither were any others.

### Conclusion

> Offspring can in some manner be tithed in their forefathers, according to that mode by which tithing posits nothing in act with regard to those tithed, but is a *figurative* act, *signifying a future healing*.

**I respond:** For an understanding of the foregoing, it must be noted that tithing was a *moral*, *sacramental*, and *figurative* act. According as it was a

*moral* act, tithing is so called as the donation of a certain fixed part for the support of those who serve the Lord and are appointed to divine worship; and in this manner tithing in some way perseveres even now[^9]. — But according as tithing is a *sacramental* act, tithing is the donation of a part for the healing of the original disease. — But according as tithing is a *figurative* act, tithing is the donation of a tenth part for the prefiguration of the universal healing which was to come about through the offering of Christ.

Taken in the first two modes, tithing regards only the present[^10]; because a tithe is given only of that which is had, and nothing is healed except what already has the disease. But taken in the third mode, tithing belongs not only to the present, but also to the future, inasmuch as *figuration* is *signification*, and signification extends itself not only to present things, but also to future ones; and in this manner the sons of Abraham are said to be tithed in Abraham, because through that tithing all the sons of Abraham were prefigured as to be healed through him who was signified by Melchizedek, that is, through the true priest Christ. And this is what Augustine says, in the tenth book *On Genesis according to the Letter*[^11]: «Since that very tithing pertained to the prefiguring of the medicine, he was being tithed in Abraham who was being healed», not when he was being prefigured, but when the truth of that figure was being fulfilled. — Therefore the reasons are to be granted which show that offspring can in some manner be tithed in their forefather, according to that mode by which tithing posits nothing in act with regard to those tithed, but only according to a signification which belongs not only to present things, but also to future ones.

*To 1.* To that which is objected first concerning the authority of Augustine, it must be said that, as is clear from the preceding word[^12], *that was tithed which was healed* does not assert a concomitance according to the same time with respect to what it is *to be tithed*, but rather extends itself to every time, and for that time renders the statement true, in which the truth of the figure was being fulfilled.

*To 2.* To that which is objected — that a tithe is not given of grain which is not in reality, but only in seminal account — it must be said that it is not similar, because tithing, insofar as it is a *figurative* act, does not extend itself to signify something about the grain, but to signify something about the human race; whence it regards the grain as the matter about which it has to be exercised, not as that which is to be signified through it. And because *exercise* requires present matter, *signification* extends itself to future things[^13], therefore it is not similar.

*To 3.* To that which is objected concerning baptism and circumcision, it must be said that it is not similar, except insofar as tithing is a *sacramental* act of the Law of nature for the remedy of original sin; in this manner it is only about the present, like circumcision and baptism. But according as it is a *figurative* act, it is not similar.

But if it be asked why baptism and circumcision cannot be a figurative act, as tithing is; it must be said that the reason for this is that circumcision and baptism is an act *in the person*; but tithing, since it is the offering of a tenth part, is an act *on behalf of the person*. And because an offering can be made on behalf of that which is and that which is to be, but one is circumcised and baptized only as[^14] regards what already is; hence it is that the act of tithing could extend itself to future ones, but not the act of circumcising or baptizing.

*To 4.* To that which is asked, how offspring is said to be tithed in its forefather; it must be said that tithing is the same as the prefiguration of healing. Whence it is taken in none of those modes which were assigned in the objection[^15]: and therefore that objection proceeded insufficiently.

### Scholion

**I.** The two questions of this article resolve two difficulties which occur in the argumentation of the Apostle, Hebrews 7, of which the first is how Levi was therefore tithed in Abraham (there, vv. 9–10), because he was in his loins; and the other, how Christ, who likewise seems to have been in Abraham, was not tithed in him, which the Apostle clearly supposes. The first difficulty he resolves in q. 1; the other in q. 2. — The solution of the first difficulty is drawn from this principle: that tithing, or the offering of tithes, which was instituted so that the priest might intercede for the sins of those offering, is not only a *moral* act, but also a *figurative* one, as is explained by our Doctor according to the common opinion. And from this it at once appears that tithing in no way befits Christ. Another reason is taken from the fact that he was in Abraham in a different manner than Levi. — On this and the following question William of Auxerre treats at length (more correctly Autissiod.) in his *Summa*, called the *Golden* one, part III, which was published in print at Paris by Franc. Regnault (without year).

**II.** On both questions: Alexander of Hales, *Summa* p. III, q. 9, m. 3, a. 4. — Scotus, *Report.* here, q. 2. — St. Thomas, here, q. 1, a. 3; *Summa* III, q. 31, a. 8. — B. Albert, here, a. 26, 27. — Peter of Tarentaise, here, q. 2, a. 3. — Richard of Middleton, here, a. 3, q. 2, 3. — Giles of Rome, here, q. 4, a. 2, and the literal doubt. — Dionysius the Carthusian, here, q. 4. — Biel, here, q. 2.
