## Latin

# INCIPIUNT CAPITULA TERTII LIBRI.

### Distinctio I.

**Cap. I.** Quare Filius carnem assumsit, non Pater vel Spiritus sanctus.

**II.** Utrum Pater vel Spiritus sanctus potuerit[^p3-3] incarnari.

**III.** Utrum Filius, qui tantum carnem accepit, aliquid fecerit, quod non Pater vel Spiritus sanctus.

### Distinctio II.

**Cap. I.** Quare totam humanam naturam accepit, et quid nomine humanitatis vel humanae naturae intelligendum sit[^p3-4].

**II.** De unione Verbi et carnis mediante anima.

**III.** Quod simul assumsit animam et carnem Verbum, nec caro prius est concepta quam assumta.

### Distinctio III.

**Cap. I.** De carne assumta, qualis ante[^p3-5] fuerit.

**II.** Quod nullus est sine peccato hic, excepta Virgine.

**III.** Quare Christus non fuit decimatus in Abraham, sicut Levi.

**IV.** Quare caro Christi non dicta est peccatrix, sed similis.

### Distinctio IV.

**Cap. I.** Quare Spiritui sancto tribuatur incarnatio, cum sit opus Trinitatis.

**II.** Quare dicatur Christus conceptus et natus de Spiritu sancto.

**III.** Quare Apostolus dicit[^p3-6] Christum factum, quem nos fatemur natum.

### Distinctio V.

**Cap. I.** Si persona vel natura personam vel naturam assumserit, et si natura Dei incarnata sit.

**II.** An divina natura debeat dici caro facta.

**III.** Quare non accepit personam hominis, cum assumserit[^p3-7] hominem, quod quidam probare nituntur.

### Distinctio VI.

**Cap. I.** De intelligentia harum locutionum: Deus est homo, Deus factus est homo, tres sententias ponit.

**II.** Prima est eorum qui dicunt, in incarnatione hominem quendam ex anima et carne constitutum, et illum hominem factum esse Deum, et Deum illum hominem; et auctoritates, quibus ita esse asserunt, ponit.

**III.** Secunda est eorum qui dicunt, hominem illum ex tribus substantiis, vel ex duabus naturis constare; et hunc fatentur unam esse personam, ante incarnationem simplicem tantum, sed in incarnatione compositam; et auctoritates, quibus se muniunt, proponit[^p3-8].

**IV.** Tertia est eorum qui non solum personam ex naturis compositam negant, sed etiam aliquem hominem vel aliquam substantiam ibi ex anima et carne compositam diffitentur; et sic illa duo, scilicet animam et carnem, Verbo unita dicunt, ut non ex illis ali-

<!-- page 4 -->

-qua substantia vel persona componeretur, sed illis duobus velut indumento Deus vestiretur, ut mortalibus oculis appareret; qui rationem incarnationis secundum habitum accipiunt.

**V.** Deinde auctoritates inducit, quibus haec sententia roboratur.

**VI.** Quatuor species habitus distinguuntur.

### Distinctio VII.

**Cap. I.** Deinde quae singulis sententiis adversari videntur, ponit.

**II.** Ex quo sensu dicitur Christus praedestinatus.

**III.** Quod non debet dici homo dominicus.

### Distinctio VIII.

**Cap. I.** An divina natura debeat dici nata de Virgine.

**II.** De gemina nativitate Christi, qui bis[^p4-1] natus est.

### Distinctio IX.

**Cap. unicum.** De adoratione exhibenda humanitati Christi.

### Distinctio X.

**Cap. I.** An Christus, secundum quod homo, sit persona, vel aliquid[^p4-2].

**II.** An Christus, secundum quod homo, sit adoptivus filius.

**III.** An persona, vel natura praedestinata sit.

### Distinctio XI.

**Cap. I.** Utrum Christus sit creatura[^p4-3], vel factus.

**II.** De perfidia et poena Arii.

**III.** An homo ille semper fuerit[^p4-4].

### Distinctio XII.

**Cap. I.** An Deus alium sumere[^p4-5] potuerit, vel aliunde quam de genere Adae.

**II.** Si homo ille potuerit peccare, vel non esse Deus.

**III.** Si Deus potuerit assumere hominem in sexu muliebri.

### Distinctio XIII.

**Cap. unicum.** De sapientia et gratia Christi hominis, an in eis proficere potuerit.

### Distinctio XIV.

**Cap. I.** Si anima Christi habuit sapientiam parem cum Deo, et si omnia scit, quae Deus.

**II.** Quare Deus non dedit illi animae potentiam omnium, ut scientiam.

### Distinctio XV.

**Cap. I.** De hominis defectibus, quos assumsit Christus.

**II.** De propassione et passione timoris vel tristitiae.

### Distinctio XVI.

**Cap. I.** De quibusdam capitulis Hilarii obscuris, quibus a carne Christi dolores submoveri videntur[^p4-6].

**II.** De tristitia Christi et eius causa secundum eundem.

**III.** An in Christo fuerit necessitas patiendi et moriendi, quae est defectus generalis.

**IV.** De statibus hominis, et quid Christus de singulis acceperit.

### Distinctio XVII.

**Cap. I.** Si omnis Christi oratio, vel voluntas impleta sit.

**II.** De voluntatibus Christi secundum duas naturas.

**III.** De capitulis quibusdam Ambrosii et Hilarii, ubi de dubitatione et timore Christi agitur.

### Distinctio XVIII.

**Cap. I.** Si Christus meruit sibi et nobis, et quid sibi et quid nobis.

**II.** Quod a conceptu meruit Christus sibi idem, quod per passionem.

**III.** De eo quod scriptum est: Donavit illi nomen, quod est supra omne nomen.

**IV.** Si Christus sine omni merito habere valuit quod merito obtinuit.

**V.** De causa passionis et mortis Christi.

### Distinctio XIX.

**Cap. I.** Qualiter a diabolo et a peccato redemit nos Christus per mortem.

**II.** Cur Deus homo et mortuus.

**III.** Quomodo a poena nos Christus redemit.

**IV.** Quomodo poenam nostram portavit.

**V.** Si Christus solus redemptor, ut mediator, debet dici.

**VI.** De mediatore.

**VII.** Secundum quam naturam sit[^p4-7] mediator.

### Distinctio XX.

**Cap. I.** Quod alio modo potuit liberare.

**II.** Quare isto modo potius.

**III.** Qua iustitia sit victus diabolus.

**IV.** De causa inter Deum et hominem et diabolum.

**V.** De traditione Christi facta a Iuda, a Deo, a Iudaeis[^p4-8].

**VI.** Utrum Christi passio sit opus Dei, vel Iudaeorum.

### Distinctio XXI.

**Cap. I.** Si in Christi morte separata fuit anima vel caro a Verbo.

**II.** Qua ratione dicitur Christus mortuus vel passus.

### Distinctio XXII.

**Cap. I.** Si Christus in morte fuit homo.

**II.** Si Christus, ubicumque est, sit homo.

**III.** Quod Christus ubique totus est, sed non totum; ut totus est homo vel Deus, sed non totum[^p4-9].

**IV.** Si ea quae dicuntur de Deo vel de Filio Dei, possint dici de homine illo vel de filio hominis.

### Distinctio XXIII.

**Cap. I.** Si Christus habuerit fidem, spem, ut[^p4-10] caritatem.

**II.** Quid sit fides.

**III.** Quot modis dicitur fides.

**IV.** Quid sit credere Deo, vel Deum, vel in Deum.

**V.** De informi qualitate mentis, quae in malo christiano est.

**VI.** Quomodo dicatur una fides.

**VII.** Quod fides est de his quae non videntur proprie, ipsa tamen videtur ab eo, in quo est.

**VIII.** Descriptio fidei.

**IX.** Quare sola fides dicitur fundamentum.

<!-- page 5 -->

### Distinctio XXIV.

**Cap. I.** Quomodo intelligitur quod scriptum est: Ut, cum factum fuerit, credatis[^p5-1].

**II.** Si Petrus habuit fidem passionis, quando vidit, hominem illum pati.

**III.** Si aliqua sciuntur, quae creduntur.

### Distinctio XXV.

**Cap. I.** De fide antiquorum.

**II.** De fide simplicium.

**III.** Quae ante adventum credere sufficiebat[^p5-2].

**IV.** De fide Cornelii.

**V.** De aequalitate fidei, spei, caritatis et operis.

### Distinctio XXVI.

**Cap. I.** De spe, quid sit.

**II.** De quibus sit spes.

**III.** Quo differant fides et spes.

**IV.** Si iusti in inferno fidem et spem habuerunt.

### Distinctio XXVII.

**Cap. I.** De caritate Dei et proximi, quae in Christo et in nobis est.

**II.** Quid sit caritas.

**III.** Si eadem caritate diligitur Deus et proximus.

**IV.** Quare dicuntur duo mandata caritatis.

**V.** De modo diligendi.

**VI.** De impletione illius mandati: Diliges Deum ex toto corde.

**VII.** Quod alterum mandatum in altero est.

**VIII.** Quae caritate diligenda sint.

### Distinctio XXVIII.

**Cap. I.** Si iubemur totum proximum diligere et nos totos.

**II.** Quod[^p5-3] in dilectione proximi includitur dilectio Angelorum.

**III.** Quis sit proximus.

**IV.** Quibus modis dicitur proximus.

### Distinctio XXIX.

**Cap. I.** De ordine diligendi, quid prius, quid post[^p5-4].

**II.** An omnes homines pariter diligendi sunt[^p5-5].

**III.** De gradibus caritatis.

### Distinctio XXX.

**Cap. unicum.** Si melius est diligere inimicos quam amicos.

### Distinctio XXXI.

**Cap. I.** Si caritas semel habita amittatur.

**II.** Quare fides, spes, scientia dicuntur evacuari, et non caritas, cum et ipsa sit ex parte.

**III.** Si Christus ordinem diligendi servavit, quem nos[^p5-6].

**Cap. I.** De caritate Dei.

**II.** Quomodo Deus dicitur magis diligere, vel minus hunc, vel illum.

**III.** Quod dupliciter inspicienda est dilectio Dei.

**IV.** Si quis magis vel minus diligitur a Deo alio tempore quam alio.

**V.** Si Deus ab aeterno dilexit reprobos[^p5-7].

### Distinctio XXXIII.

# HERE BEGIN THE CHAPTERS OF THE THIRD BOOK.

### Distinction I.

**Chap. I.** Why the Son assumed flesh, and not the Father or the Holy Spirit.

**II.** Whether the Father or the Holy Spirit could[^p3-3] have been incarnate.

**III.** Whether the Son, who alone took flesh, did anything which the Father or the Holy Spirit did not.

### Distinction II.

**Chap. I.** Why he took the whole human nature, and what is to be understood[^p3-4] by the name of humanity or of human nature.

**II.** On the union of the Word and the flesh with the soul as intermediary.

**III.** That the Word assumed the soul and the flesh at once, and that the flesh was not conceived before it was assumed.

### Distinction III.

**Chap. I.** On the flesh assumed, of what sort it was before[^p3-5].

**II.** That no one here is without sin, the Virgin excepted.

**III.** Why Christ was not tithed in Abraham, as Levi was.

**IV.** Why Christ's flesh was not called sinful, but like [sinful flesh].

### Distinction IV.

**Chap. I.** Why the incarnation is attributed to the Holy Spirit, since it is the work of the Trinity.

**II.** Why Christ is said to be conceived and born of the Holy Spirit.

**III.** Why the Apostle says[^p3-6] that Christ was *made*, whom we confess to have been *born*.

### Distinction V.

**Chap. I.** Whether a person or a nature assumed a person or a nature, and whether the nature of God was incarnate.

**II.** Whether the divine nature ought to be called made flesh.

**III.** Why he did not take the person of a man, since he assumed[^p3-7] a man — which some strive to prove.

### Distinction VI.

**Chap. I.** On the understanding of these locutions — *God is man*, *God was made man* — he sets down three opinions.

**II.** The first is of those who say that in the incarnation a certain man was constituted of soul and flesh, and that that man was made God, and God that man; and he sets down the authorities by which they assert that it is so.

**III.** The second is of those who say that that man consists of three substances, or of two natures; and they confess him to be one person, before the incarnation simple only, but in the incarnation composite; and he sets forth[^p3-8] the authorities with which they fortify themselves.

**IV.** The third is of those who not only deny a person composed of natures, but also disavow that any man or any substance there is composed of soul and flesh; and thus they say that those two, namely soul and flesh, are so united to the Word that out of them no

<!-- page 4 -->

substance or person is composed, but that God is clothed with those two as with a garment, so that he might appear to mortal eyes — they who take the account of the incarnation according to *habit*.

**V.** Then he adduces authorities by which this opinion is strengthened.

**VI.** Four species of habit are distinguished.

### Distinction VII.

**Chap. I.** Then he sets down what seem to be against each of the opinions.

**II.** In what sense Christ is called predestined.

**III.** That he ought not to be called a *lordly* man.

### Distinction VIII.

**Chap. I.** Whether the divine nature ought to be called born of the Virgin.

**II.** On the twofold nativity of Christ, who was twice[^p4-1] born.

### Distinction IX.

**Sole chapter.** On the adoration to be shown to the humanity of Christ.

### Distinction X.

**Chap. I.** Whether Christ, according as he is man, is a person, or something[^p4-2].

**II.** Whether Christ, according as he is man, is an adoptive son.

**III.** Whether a person, or a nature, was predestined.

### Distinction XI.

**Chap. I.** Whether Christ is a creature[^p4-3], or made.

**II.** On the perfidy and the punishment of Arius.

**III.** Whether that man always was[^p4-4].

### Distinction XII.

**Chap. I.** Whether God could have taken[^p4-5] another, or from elsewhere than from the race of Adam.

**II.** Whether that man could have sinned, or not have been God.

**III.** Whether God could have assumed a man in the female sex.

### Distinction XIII.

**Sole chapter.** On the wisdom and grace of Christ as man, whether he could have advanced in them.

### Distinction XIV.

**Chap. I.** Whether Christ's soul had wisdom equal with God, and whether it knows all things that God knows.

**II.** Why God did not give to that soul the power of all things, as he gave it knowledge.

### Distinction XV.

**Chap. I.** On the defects of man which Christ assumed.

**II.** On the propassion and the passion of fear or of sorrow.

### Distinction XVI.

**Chap. I.** On certain obscure chapters of Hilary, by which pains seem[^p4-6] to be removed from Christ's flesh.

**II.** On the sorrow of Christ and its cause, according to the same [author].

**III.** Whether there was in Christ a necessity of suffering and of dying, which is a general defect.

**IV.** On the states of man, and what Christ took from each of them.

### Distinction XVII.

**Chap. I.** Whether every prayer, or will, of Christ was fulfilled.

**II.** On the wills of Christ according to the two natures.

**III.** On certain chapters of Ambrose and Hilary, where the doubting and fear of Christ are treated.

### Distinction XVIII.

**Chap. I.** Whether Christ merited for himself and for us, and what for himself and what for us.

**II.** That from his conception Christ merited for himself the same thing that he merited through his passion.

**III.** On that which is written: *He gave him a name which is above every name.*

**IV.** Whether Christ could have had without any merit what he obtained by merit.

**V.** On the cause of Christ's passion and death.

### Distinction XIX.

**Chap. I.** In what manner Christ redeemed us from the devil and from sin through his death.

**II.** Why God [became] man and died.

**III.** In what manner Christ redeemed us from punishment.

**IV.** In what manner he bore our punishment.

**V.** Whether Christ alone ought to be called redeemer, as mediator.

**VI.** On the mediator.

**VII.** According to which nature he is[^p4-7] mediator.

### Distinction XX.

**Chap. I.** That he could have delivered [us] in another way.

**II.** Why rather in this way.

**III.** By what justice the devil was overcome.

**IV.** On the case between God and man and the devil.

**V.** On the handing over of Christ, done by Judas, by God, by the Jews[^p4-8].

**VI.** Whether Christ's passion is the work of God, or of the Jews.

### Distinction XXI.

**Chap. I.** Whether in Christ's death the soul or the flesh was separated from the Word.

**II.** For what reason Christ is said to have died or suffered.

### Distinction XXII.

**Chap. I.** Whether Christ in death was man.

**II.** Whether Christ, wherever he is, is man.

**III.** That Christ is everywhere whole, but not the whole [of him]; as he is wholly man or God, but not the whole[^p4-9].

**IV.** Whether the things which are said of God or of the Son of God can be said of that man or of the son of man.

### Distinction XXIII.

**Chap. I.** Whether Christ had faith, hope, and[^p4-10] charity.

**II.** What faith is.

**III.** In how many ways faith is spoken of.

**IV.** What it is to believe God, or to believe that God is, or to believe in God.

**V.** On the unformed quality of mind which is in a bad Christian.

**VI.** In what manner faith is called one.

**VII.** That faith is properly of those things which are not seen, yet is itself seen by him in whom it is.

**VIII.** A description of faith.

**IX.** Why faith alone is called the foundation.

### Distinction XXIV.

**Chap. I.** How that is to be understood which is written: *That, when it shall come to pass, you may believe*[^p5-1].

**II.** Whether Peter had faith in the passion when he saw that man suffer.

**III.** Whether some things are known which are believed.

### Distinction XXV.

**Chap. I.** On the faith of the ancients.

**II.** On the faith of the simple.

**III.** What it sufficed[^p5-2] to believe before the advent.

**IV.** On the faith of Cornelius.

**V.** On the equality of faith, hope, charity and work.

### Distinction XXVI.

**Chap. I.** On hope: what it is.

**II.** Of what things hope is.

**III.** In what faith and hope differ.

**IV.** Whether the just in hell had faith and hope.

### Distinction XXVII.

**Chap. I.** On the charity of God and of neighbour, which is in Christ and in us.

**II.** What charity is.

**III.** Whether God and neighbour are loved by the same charity.

**IV.** Why they are called the two commandments of charity.

**V.** On the manner of loving.

**VI.** On the fulfilling of that commandment: *Thou shalt love God with thy whole heart.*

**VII.** That the one commandment is in the other.

**VIII.** What things are to be loved with charity.

### Distinction XXVIII.

**Chap. I.** Whether we are commanded to love the whole neighbour, and ourselves wholly.

**II.** That[^p5-3] in the love of neighbour the love of the Angels is included.

**III.** Who is the neighbour.

**IV.** In what ways one is called a neighbour.

### Distinction XXIX.

**Chap. I.** On the order of loving: what comes first, what after[^p5-4].

**II.** Whether all men are[^p5-5] to be loved equally.

**III.** On the degrees of charity.

### Distinction XXX.

**Sole chapter.** Whether it is better to love enemies than friends.

### Distinction XXXI.

**Chap. I.** Whether charity once had is lost.

**II.** Why faith, hope, and knowledge are said to be done away, and not charity, since it too is in part.

**III.** Whether Christ kept the order of loving which we[^p5-6] keep.

### Distinction XXXII.

**Chap. I.** On the charity of God.

**II.** In what manner God is said to love this one more, or that one less.

**III.** That the love of God is to be regarded in two ways.

**IV.** Whether anyone is loved more or less by God at one time than at another.

**V.** Whether God from eternity loved the reprobate[^p5-7].

### Distinction XXXIII.

**Chap. I.** On the four principal virtues.

**II.** Whether[^p5-8] they were in Christ and are in the Angels.

**III.** On the uses of them.

### Distinction XXXIV.

**Chap. I.** On the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit.

**II.** Whether they are virtues, and are in the Angels.

**III.** Whether they were in Christ.

**IV.** On the distinction of fears[^p5-9].

**V.** On chaste and servile and initial [fear].

**VI.** In what[^p5-10] the chaste and the servile differ.

**VII.** That servile and initial fear are called the beginning[^p5-11] of wisdom, but in different ways.

**VIII.** In what manner chaste fear remains for ever.

**IX.** Whether the fear of punishment which was in Christ was servile or initial.

### Distinction XXXV.

**Chap. I.** On wisdom and knowledge: in what[^p5-12] they differ.

**II.** In what wisdom differs from understanding.

**III.** Whether understanding and knowledge, which are numbered among the gifts, are those which man has naturally.

### Distinction XXXVI.

**Chap. I.** On the connection of the virtues, which are not separated.

**II.** Whether all the virtues are equal, in whomsoever they are.

**III.** In what manner the whole law hangs upon charity.

### Distinction XXXVII.

**Chap. I.** On the ten commandments: in what manner they are contained in the two.

**II.** Why an idol is said to be nothing in the world.

**III.** Why the remission of sins is properly said to be effected in the Holy Spirit.

**IV.** On the spiritual and the carnal sense of the Law.

**V.** On theft.

**VI.** On lying.

### Distinction XXXVIII.

**Chap. I.** On the threefold kind of lying.

**II.** On the eight[^p5-13] species of lying.

**III.** What it is to lie.

**IV.** That every lie is a sin, whether it profits or not[^p5-14], and why.

**V.** In what matters one errs with danger, and in what not.

### Distinction XXXIX.

**Chap. I.** On perjury.

**II.** Whether there is a perjury which is not a lie.

**III.** On the threefold mode of perjury.

**IV.** Whether swearing is an evil.

**V.** On the oath which is made by creatures.

**VI.** Which swearing is graver — that which is made by God, or that which is made by creatures, or by the Gospel[^p6-1].

**VII.** What it is to say *by God*.

**VIII.** On those who swear by false gods.

**IX.** That an oath or promise made against God is not to be kept.

**X.** Whether he is a perjurer who does not do what he has incautiously sworn.

**XI.** On those who swear with craftiness of words.

**XII.** On him who compels another to swear.

### Distinction XL.

**Chap. I.** Why the Law is said to restrain the hand, not the mind.

## Notes

**Provenance.** Latin re-set column-by-column from 450 dpi PDF bands of printed pp.3–6 (PDF 25–28, offset `pdf = printed + 22`). **The plate is authoritative throughout this chunk, not merely in patches.** The IA djvu OCR (`bonaventure_vol3_raw.txt` raw lines 875–1058) cascade-shatters a chapter list in a way it does not shatter running prose: **the roman numeral column detaches from the title column and drifts**, so that on p.4's right block the OCR yields `Cap. I. Si Christus meruit…`, a blank line, `Quod a conceptu meruit…`, then a bare `III.` on its own line, then `passionem.` — numerals stranded from the titles they number. A reader reconstructing this list from the OCR would mis-number chapters silently. It was read off the plate, distinction by distinction.

**Type.** `capitula` — Lombard's transmitted chapter list for the whole of Book III, forty distinctions. `distinctio: 0`: it prints before Distinction I.

**★★ WHY THIS TABLE IS CHUNKED AT ALL — THE FROZEN TEST, APPLIED.** CLAUDE.md's Itinerarium rule says a capitula table is transmitted text when it **carries apparatus on the table itself**, and that the question is decided by looking, never by analogy (the Breviloquium's editorial table, pp.209–210, was *not* chunked). This table carries **thirty-one apparatus entries** anchored in its own entries — variant readings for individual chapter titles (`potuerit³`, `creatura³`, `De octo¹³ speciebus`), and editorial notes on the transmission of the list itself: p.4 n.4 records that *the codices begin distinction XII at this chapter*, p.5 n.1 that a chapter is *omitted in the codices, D excepted*, p.5 n.5 that *codex D alone adds a chapter* `De parentibus bonis et malis`. That is a table with a textual history. It is transmitted text; it is chunked.
