## Latin
<!-- page 74 -->

### Quaestio II. *Utrum in prima sanctificatione in Virgine fuerit exstincta fomitis concupiscentia.*

Secundo quaeritur, utrum in prima sanctificatione in Virgine fuerit fomitis concupiscentia exstincta. Et quod sic, videtur:

1. Lucae primo[^1] dicitur ab Angelo ei: *Ave Maria, gratia plena;* igitur ante adventum Filii Dei fuit repleta gratia et plena amore divino; sed ubi est aliquo modo concupiscentia, ibi non potest esse gratia Dei plena: videtur igitur, quod in prima Virginis sanctificatione fomitis concupiscentia omnino fuerit exstincta.

2. Item, ubicumque est fomes, est necessitas ad aliquod peccatum veniale[^2]: ergo cum in ea nulla fuerit peccandi necessitas, quia in ea nulla culpa; ergo videtur, quod fomitis concupiscentia omnino fuerit in ea exstincta.

3. Item, fomes reddit illum difficilem ad bonum, in quo est; sed in beata Virgine nulla fuit difficultas ad bonum: ergo videtur, quod post primam sanctificationem non remanserit in ea concupiscentiae vitium.

4. Item, si *tanta* gratia fomitem minuit, et maior gratia magis minuit, et perfecta simpliciter[^3] exstinguit; sed gloriosae Virgini in ipsa sua sanctificatione primo collata fuit gratia perfecta: ergo omnino fuit in ea exstincta concupiscentia.

**Sed contra:** 1. Damascenus dicit, et habetur in littera[^4]: « Post consensum Virginis praevenit Spiritus sanctus in ipsam secundum verbum, quod dixerat Angelus ad eam, purgans eam »; sed non purgavit a culpa: ergo purgavit ab originalis sequela; haec autem est fomitis concupiscentia: ergo etc.

2. Item, Magister in littera[^5]: « Mariam quoque totam Spiritus sanctus praeveniens purgavit et a fomite peccati liberavit, vel fomitem ipsum penitus exstinguendo, vel extenuando »; hoc dicit de sanctificatione secunda: ergo ante ipsam non fuit fomes exstinctus.

3. Item, Bernardus in quadam homilia[^6]: « In proposito Virginis erat omnium hominum frequentiam fugere, ne perturbaretur oranti silentium, aut virginalis castitas tentaretur »: ergo videtur, quod occasione accepta ab extrinseco, excitari poterat in ea concupiscentia prava: ergo non fuit omnino exstinctus.

4. Item, si omnino fuisset in ea exstinctus fomes concupiscentiae, igitur secunda sanctificatio non fuisset opportuna: si igitur opportuna fuit, videtur, quod aliquid remanserit in ea fomitis concupiscentiae.

<!-- page 75 -->

### Conclusio

> In prima sanctificatione beatae Virginis fomes fuit consopitus, in secunda vero exstinctus.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod circa hanc quaestionem est triplex modus dicendi.

Quidam enim dicere voluerunt, quod nec in prima nec in secunda sanctificatione exstinctus erat in ea fomes concupiscentiae, sed solum consopitus secundum plus et minus: quia fomes causam habet in carne; unde non directam habet oppositionem ad gratiam secundum essentiam, sed secundum inclinationem: ideo si gratia magna est, fomes minus inclinat, quamvis per gratiam illam non deleatur eius essentia[^7]. Quoniam igitur in Virgine magna fuit gratia in prima sanctificatione et valde magna in secunda; hinc est, quod in prima fuit consopitus, et in secunda magis consopitus, quamvis non omnino fuerit exstinctus vel evacuatus. — Sed[^8] quoniam caro Christi sumta est de carne Virginis, in Christo etiam nulla fuit concupiscentia fomitis; cum caro Christi debeat carni Virginis assimilari post Virginis sanctificationem, sicut caro aliorum assimilatur carni aliorum parentum; videtur, quod Spiritus sanctus secundo super eam adveniens prorsus exstinxerit fomitem. Et ita praedicta opinio non videtur habere probabilitatem.

Secundus modus dicendi est, quod tam in prima quam in secunda sanctificatione fuerit in ea exstinctus fomes, sed aliter et aliter. In prima namque exstinctus fuit, in quantum respicit personam; in secunda, in quantum respicit naturam. In prima igitur sanctificatione mundata fuit[^9], ut numquam peccaret; in secunda vero mundata fuit, ut germen piae prolis emitteret sine peccato, non solum actuali[^10], sed etiam veniali. In prima etiam sanctificatione exstinctus fuit fomes, in quantum reddit pronum ad malum; in secunda, in quantum reddit illum difficilem ad bonum. — Sed illud difficile est intelligere, quia hoc ipso, quod proni sumus ad malum, difficiles sumus ad bonum. Quomodo igitur exstingui potest quantum ad pronitatem ad malum, ipso remanente quantum ad difficultatem ad bonum? — Rursus, qualiter potest intelligi, quod fomes possit exstingui, in quantum respicit personam; et non sit exstinctus, in quantum respicit naturam, cum ibi sit unus fomes, sic[^11] diversimode comparatus, et exstinctio dicat ipsius fomitis eradicationem? Non videtur usquequaque intelligibile, quomodo fomes exstinguatur in prima sanctificatione, et tamen nihilominus remaneat respectu prolis propagandae. Si enim exstinguitur, ergo non manet aliquo modo, nisi forte quis accipiat exstinctionem largo modo pro ablatione inclinationis ad malum respectu personae, in qua est. Et ita fortassis intelligitur praedicta positio, quam satis probabilem et rationabilem credo.

Tertius autem est modus dicendi, videlicet quod in prima sanctificatione fuit fomes in Virgine consopitus; in secunda exstinctus et eradicatus. Fomes enim radicatur in carne et ascendit usque ad mentem. In prima sanctificatione Virginis[^12] collata est tanta gratia perfectionis, quod redundaret in eius sensualitatem in tanta redundantia, ut fomitem adeo reprimeret, quod nullum effectum ad incitandum aliquando haberet. In secunda autem sanctificatione — in qua Spiritus sanctus non tantum descendit in mentem, sed etiam in carnem, et de carne operatus est, de ea mirabiliter carnem sine macula fabricando — reliquit ipsius carnem immaculatam, exstirpando ex ea et fomitem et omnem concupiscentiam. Et hoc sonant verba Bernardi, Magistri et Damasceni, sicut patet in auctoritatibus in opponendo[^13] habitis. — Unde rationes probantes, quod in prima sanctificatione non fuit fomes omnino exstinctus, concedendae sunt.

*Ad 1.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod Virgo Maria fuit gratia plena in prima sanctificatione; dicendum, quod aliter fuit plena in prima sanctificatione, aliter in secunda. In prima fuit plena, secundum quod expediebat ad meritum; in secunda, secundum quod expediebat ad concipiendum Filium Dei. Et ideo prima sanctificatio respiciebat Virginem secundum animam; secunda autem respiciebat Virginem totam, secundum quod dicit Magister: « Mariam totam Spiritus sanctus praeveniens » etc.

<!-- page 76 -->

*Ad 2. 3.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod fomes impellit ad necessitatem ad peccandum venialiter, et ad difficultatem ad bonum; dicendum, quod illud verum est, ubi[^14] est fomes in vigore suo, ubi non reprimitur a gratia movente ad contrarium; et ideo non habet locum in Virgine, ubi fuit gratia perfecta, tranquillans eius animam et potentias. Et sic patet responsio ad illa duo.

*Ad 4.* Ad illud quod obiicitur de perfectione gratiae, dicendum, quod gratia, quae directe respicit animam, minuit fomitem quantum ad inclinationem, sed non oportet, ut minuat quantum ad radicem; et ideo non sequitur, quod per[^15] perfectionem illius gratiae fomitis concupiscentia evellatur, sed solum ut eius inclinatio consopiatur. Et hoc quidem concedendum est tanquam verum.

### Scholion

**I.** Quid sit *fomes*, explicatur a S. Bonav., II. Sent. d. 30. a. 2. q. 1. in fine corp. et dub. unic., d. 32. dub. 2, d. 39. a. 2. q. 2. ad 4. — Supponitur hic antiqua et communis distinctio inter *primam* sanctificationem B. Virginis, quae secundum omnes antiquos facta est saltem ante nativitatem ex utero, et *secundam*, quae fuit in ipsa Verbi incarnatione. Antequam doctrina, quae defendit immaculatam Virginis conceptionem, invaluit, *secundae* sanctificationi aliqui speciales effectus communiter attribuebantur, ita ut inter utramque formalis quaedam differentia statueretur; nunc autem communius docetur, easdem non nisi secundum *gradus* efficaciae inter se differre.

**II.** Prima opinio a S. Bonav. aliisque communiter reprobatur. Secunda opinio est Alexandri Hal., quae pariter posterioribus magistris non placuit. Tribus autem opinionibus hic relatis iam B. Albert. et S. Thom. adiunxerunt quartam, quae scil. sustinet, « quod in sanctificatione B. Virginis, qua fuit sanctificata in utero, totaliter fuit ei fomes subtractus » (ita S. Thom. in Sum. III. q. 27. a. 3.). Haec autem quarta sententia, quae est nunc communis, ex dogmate immaculatae Conceptionis sponte fluit, cum B. Virgini non denegari possint illi effectus iustitiae originalis, qui respiciunt subiectionem, qua in protoparentibus sensualitas rationi, ratio Deo obsequebatur (cfr. Scot., II. Sent. d. 29. q. unica n. 4; d. 32. n. 4. seqq.). — Tertia opinio, quam sequuntur S. Bonav., S. Thom., B. Albert., Petr. a Tar., Richard. a Med., Ægid. R., Durand. aliique, non primae, sed secundae sanctificationi exstinctionem fomitis attribuit, ita ut ille in prima secundum eosdem tantum fuerit ligatus (consopitus), quin tamen unquam processerit in actum. Hanc autem *ligationem* quidam sic explicant, ut parum a quarta opinione recedant. — Respectu specialis cuiusdam obiectionis cfr. hic dub. 1.

**III.** Alex. Hal., S. p. III. q. 9. m. 2. a. 5. — S. Thom., hic q. 1. a. 2; S. III. q. 27. a. 3; Compend. theol. c. 224. — B. Albert., hic a. 6. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 1. a. 1. quaestiunc. 3. — Richard. a Med., de hac et seq. q. hic a. 1. q. 4. — Ægid. R., de hac et seq. q. hic q. 1. a. 4. — Durand., hic q. 3.

**IV.** In seq. (3.) quaestione id quod supra in 1. quaest. de sanctificatione illa dictum est respectu *actus*, completur respectu *habitus* peccandi, qui per *confirmationem in gratia* a B. Virgine penitus removetur, saltem post secundam sanctificationem, ut volunt qui in prima sanctificatione fomitem tantum *ligatum* esse putant. Omnes autem in duobus conveniunt: tum quod B. Virgo, cum remanserit viatrix, non eodem modo fuerit confirmata in gratia, quo confirmantur Sancti, scil. per visionem beatificam, tum quod ipsa *eminentissimum* prae omnibus aliis in gratia confirmatis donum confirmationis habuerit, ita ut in sensu composito exclusa sit potentia peccandi (cfr. S. Thom., hic q. 1. a. 2. quaestiunc. 3. ad 2.). Tantum Durand. nimis restringit hanc confirmationem, ut sit non ex *dono inhaerenti*, sed ex *adiutorio ei assistenti*.

**V.** Alex. Hal., loc. cit. m. 3. a. 2. § 2. — S. Thom., hic q. cit.; S. loc. cit. a. 4. — B. Albert., hic a. 7. 9. — Petr. a Tar., hic q. 1. a. 2. quaestiunc. 3. — Durand., hic q. 4.

## English

### Question II. *Whether in the first sanctification the tinder of concupiscence was extinguished in the Virgin.*

Secondly it is asked whether in the first sanctification the tinder of concupiscence was extinguished in the Virgin. And that it was, it seems:

1. In Luke, chapter one[^1], it is said to her by the Angel: *Hail Mary, full of grace;* therefore, before the coming of the Son of God she was filled with grace and full of divine love; but where there is concupiscence in any way, there the grace of God cannot be full: it seems therefore that in the Virgin's first sanctification the tinder of concupiscence was entirely extinguished.

2. Likewise, wherever there is the tinder, there is a necessity toward some venial sin[^2]: therefore, since there was in her no necessity of sinning, because in her there was no fault; therefore it seems that the tinder of concupiscence was entirely extinguished in her.

3. Likewise, the tinder renders him in whom it is difficult toward the good; but in the blessed Virgin there was no difficulty toward the good: therefore it seems that after the first sanctification there did not remain in her the vice of concupiscence.

4. Likewise, if *so much* grace lessens the tinder, and a greater grace lessens it more, and a perfect grace simply[^3] extinguishes it; but to the glorious Virgin in her very sanctification perfect grace was first conferred: therefore concupiscence was entirely extinguished in her.

**On the contrary:** 1. Damascene says, and it is had in the text[^4]: « After the Virgin's consent the Holy Spirit came upon her beforehand according to the word which the Angel had said to her, purifying her »; but he did not purify her from fault: therefore he purified her from the consequence of original sin; but this is the tinder of concupiscence: therefore, etc.

2. Likewise, the Master in the text[^5]: « Mary too the Holy Spirit, coming beforehand, wholly purified and freed from the tinder of sin, either by entirely extinguishing the tinder itself, or by attenuating it »; this he says of the second sanctification: therefore before it the tinder was not extinguished.

3. Likewise, Bernard in a certain homily[^6]: « In the Virgin's purpose it was to flee the company of all men, lest the silence of her prayer should be disturbed, or her virginal chastity be tempted »: therefore it seems that, an occasion having been taken from without, depraved concupiscence could be aroused in her: therefore it was not entirely extinguished.

4. Likewise, if the tinder of concupiscence had been entirely extinguished in her, then the second sanctification would not have been opportune: if therefore it was opportune, it seems that something of the tinder of concupiscence remained in her.

### Conclusio

> In the first sanctification of the blessed Virgin the tinder was lulled to sleep, but in the second it was extinguished.

**Respondeo:** It must be said that concerning this question there is a threefold manner of speaking.

For some have wished to say that neither in the first nor in the second sanctification was the tinder of concupiscence extinguished in her, but only lulled to sleep according to more and less: because the tinder has its cause in the flesh; whence it does not have a direct opposition to grace according to essence, but according to inclination: therefore, if the grace is great, the tinder inclines less, although by that grace its essence is not destroyed[^7]. Since therefore in the Virgin the grace was great in the first sanctification and very great in the second; hence it is that in the first it was lulled to sleep, and in the second more lulled to sleep, although it was not entirely extinguished or emptied out. — But[^8] since the flesh of Christ was taken from the flesh of the Virgin, and in Christ too there was no concupiscence of the tinder; since the flesh of Christ ought to be assimilated to the flesh of the Virgin after the Virgin's sanctification, just as the flesh of others is assimilated to the flesh of their parents; it seems that the Holy Spirit, coming a second time upon her, utterly extinguished the tinder. And thus the aforesaid opinion does not seem to have probability.

The second manner of speaking is that both in the first and in the second sanctification the tinder was extinguished in her, but in one way and another. For in the first it was extinguished insofar as it regards the person; in the second, insofar as it regards the nature. In the first sanctification, therefore, she was cleansed[^9] so that she might never sin; but in the second she was cleansed so that she might send forth the seed of holy offspring without sin, not only mortal[^10], but also venial. In the first sanctification too the tinder was extinguished insofar as it renders one prone to evil; in the second, insofar as it renders him difficult toward the good. — But this is difficult to understand, because by this very fact, that we are prone to evil, we are difficult toward the good. How then can it be extinguished as to proneness to evil, while it remains as to difficulty toward the good? — Again, how can it be understood that the tinder can be extinguished insofar as it regards the person, and yet not be extinguished insofar as it regards the nature, since there is one tinder there, thus[^11] compared in diverse ways, and extinction means the eradication of the tinder itself? It does not seem in every way intelligible how the tinder is extinguished in the first sanctification, and yet nonetheless remains with respect to the propagating of offspring. For if it is extinguished, then it does not remain in any way, unless perhaps one take extinction in a broad sense for the removal of the inclination to evil with respect to the person in whom it is. And thus perhaps the aforesaid position is understood, which I believe to be sufficiently probable and reasonable.

But the third manner of speaking is, namely, that in the first sanctification the tinder was lulled to sleep in the Virgin; in the second it was extinguished and eradicated. For the tinder is rooted in the flesh and ascends up to the mind. In the first sanctification of the Virgin[^12] so much grace of perfection was conferred that it overflowed into her sensuality in such abundance that it so repressed the tinder that it never had any effect toward inciting. But in the second sanctification — in which the Holy Spirit not only descended into the mind, but also into the flesh, and worked from the flesh, marvelously fashioning from it flesh without stain — he left her flesh immaculate, rooting out from it both the tinder and all concupiscence. And this is what the words of Bernard, the Master, and Damascene convey, as is plain in the authorities adduced in opposition[^13]. — Whence the reasons proving that in the first sanctification the tinder was not entirely extinguished are to be granted.

*To 1.* To that which is objected, that the Virgin Mary was full of grace in the first sanctification; it must be said that she was full in one way in the first sanctification, in another in the second. In the first she was full, according to what was expedient for merit; in the second, according to what was expedient for conceiving the Son of God. And therefore the first sanctification regarded the Virgin according to the soul; but the second regarded the Virgin wholly, according to what the Master says: « Mary wholly the Holy Spirit, coming beforehand » etc.

*To 2. 3.* To that which is objected, that the tinder impels to a necessity of sinning venially, and to a difficulty toward the good; it must be said that this is true where[^14] the tinder is in its vigor, where it is not repressed by a grace moving toward the contrary; and therefore it has no place in the Virgin, where there was perfect grace, calming her soul and her powers. And thus the response to those two is plain.

*To 4.* To that which is objected concerning the perfection of grace, it must be said that grace, which directly regards the soul, lessens the tinder as to inclination, but it is not necessary that it lessen it as to the root; and therefore it does not follow that by[^15] the perfection of that grace the tinder of concupiscence is uprooted, but only that its inclination is lulled to sleep. And this indeed must be granted as true.

### Scholion

**I.** What the *tinder* is is explained by St. Bonaventure, II Sent. d. 30, a. 2, q. 1, at the end of the body, and in the single doubt, d. 32, doubt 2, d. 39, a. 2, q. 2, ad 4. — There is here presupposed the ancient and common distinction between the *first* sanctification of the Blessed Virgin, which according to all the ancients took place at least before her birth from the womb, and the *second*, which was in the Incarnation of the Word itself. Before the doctrine which defends the immaculate conception of the Virgin prevailed, certain special effects were commonly attributed to the *second* sanctification, so that a certain formal difference was established between the two; but now it is more commonly taught that they differ from one another only according to *degrees* of efficacy.

**II.** The first opinion is commonly rejected by St. Bonaventure and others. The second opinion is that of Alexander of Hales, which likewise did not please the later masters. To the three opinions here related B. Albert and St. Thomas already added a fourth, which namely maintains « that in the sanctification of the Blessed Virgin, by which she was sanctified in the womb, the tinder was totally taken away from her » (so St. Thomas in Sum. III, q. 27, a. 3). But this fourth opinion, which is now the common one, flows spontaneously from the dogma of the immaculate Conception, since to the Blessed Virgin cannot be denied those effects of original justice which regard the subjection by which in the first parents sensuality obeyed reason, and reason obeyed God (cf. Scotus, II Sent. d. 29, q. unica, n. 4; d. 32, n. 4 ff.). — The third opinion, which St. Bonaventure, St. Thomas, B. Albert, Peter of Tarentaise, Richard of Middleton, Giles of Rome, Durandus and others follow, attributes the extinction of the tinder not to the first, but to the second sanctification, so that in the first, according to these same authors, it was only *bound* (lulled to sleep), yet without ever proceeding into act. But some explain this *binding* in such a way that they depart little from the fourth opinion. — With respect to a certain special objection, cf. here doubt 1.

**III.** Alexander of Hales, S. p. III, q. 9, m. 2, a. 5. — St. Thomas, here q. 1, a. 2; S. III, q. 27, a. 3; Compend. theol. c. 224. — B. Albert, here a. 6. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 1, a. 1, little-question 3. — Richard of Middleton, on this and the following question, here a. 1, q. 4. — Giles of Rome, on this and the following question, here q. 1, a. 4. — Durandus, here q. 3.

**IV.** In the following (3rd) question, what was said above in the first question concerning that sanctification with respect to *act* is completed with respect to the *habit* of sinning, which by *confirmation in grace* is entirely removed from the Blessed Virgin, at least after the second sanctification, as those wish who hold that in the first sanctification the tinder was only *bound*. But all agree in two things: both that the Blessed Virgin, since she remained a wayfarer, was not confirmed in grace in the same manner in which the Saints are confirmed, namely through the beatific vision, and that she had the *most eminent* gift of confirmation above all others confirmed in grace, so that in the composite sense the power of sinning was excluded (cf. St. Thomas, here q. 1, a. 2, little-question 3, ad 2). Only Durandus too greatly restricts this confirmation, so that it is not from an *inhering gift*, but from a *help assisting her*.

**V.** Alexander of Hales, loc. cit., m. 3, a. 2, § 2. — St. Thomas, here q. cit.; S. loc. cit. a. 4. — B. Albert, here a. 7, 9. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 1, a. 2, little-question 3. — Durandus, here q. 4.
