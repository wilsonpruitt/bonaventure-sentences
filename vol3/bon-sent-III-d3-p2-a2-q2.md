# III Sent., d. 3, p. 2, a. 2, q. 2
## *Utrum Christi caro fuerit in parentibus aliter quam caro aliorum hominum secundum legem propagationis.*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 87 -->

### Quaestio II. *Utrum Christi caro fuerit in parentibus aliter quam caro aliorum hominum secundum legem propagationis.*

Secundo quaeritur, utrum caro Christi fuerit in parentibus aliter quam caro aliorum hominum secundum legem propagationis. Et quod aliter, illud videtur:

1. Auctoritate Augustini, decimo super Genesim ad litteram[^1] tractantis illud Apostoli ad Hebraeos septimo: « Adhuc erat in lumbis patris. Secundum aliquem modum erat ibi Christus, quo non erat ibi Levi. Secundum rationem quippe seminalem fuit ibi Levi, qua ratione non fuit ibi caro Christi ».

<!-- page 88 -->

2. Item, hoc ipsum ostenditur ratione. Mediatum principium reducitur ad immediatum[^2]; sed Christus secundum alium modum propagandi exivit de utero Virginis, quam alii filii de ventre matrum suarum, pro eo quod non de virili semine: si ergo mediante beata Virgine dicitur fuisse in parentibus praecedentibus, videtur, quod alio modo fuerit caro eius in parentibus secundum legem propagationis quam aliorum hominum.

3. Item, si eodem modo fuisset caro Christi in parentibus sicut reliqui homines[^3], ita peccasset, Adam peccante, sicut alii homines; sed hoc falsum est: ergo etc.

4. Item, eo modo habet res educi in esse, per quem modum habet in suo principio existere[^4]; sed Christus non fuit genitus vel conceptus secundum legem naturae, sed potius mirabiliter, alii homines educuntur naturaliter: aliter igitur fuit caro eius quantum ad legem propagationis in lumbis parentum quam aliorum hominum.

**Sed contra:** 1. Magister dicit in littera[^5]: « Caro illa, antequam Verbo fuisset unita, peccato fuit obnoxia in Maria, sicut reliqua Virginis caro »; sed caro, quae consimilis est secundum traductionem originalis, consimilis est secundum legem propagationis: ergo si caro Christi in lumbis parentum fuit obnoxia peccato, ergo et eadem lege a primis parentibus est traducta.

2. Item, omne quod descendit ab Adam in Seth, descendit secundum corpulentam substantiam et secundum seminalem rationem: ergo si caro Christi descendit ab Adam in Seth, et a Seth in alios parentes, videtur, quod secundum legem propagationis fuerit in lumbis parentum eodem modo, sicut caro aliorum hominum[^6].

3. Item, semen, unde formatum est corpus Seth, in totis parentibus habebat rationem seminalem: ergo nihil inde futurum erat, quod non esset ibi et secundum corpulentam substantiam et[^7] rationem seminalem; sed si caro Christi exinde facta est, videtur, quod ibi fuerit ad modum alterius carnis humanae.

4. Item, tota caro Virginis fuit in lumbis parentum secundum legem propagationis, sicut caro aliorum hominum; sed caro Christi fuit ex aliqua parte carnis Virginis: ergo secundum legem propagationis sic fuit in lumbis parentum, sicut caro totius humani generis.

### Conclusio

> Caro Christi secundum legem propagationis aliter fuit in parentibus, quam caro aliorum hominum.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod dupliciter est loqui de carne Christi: aut quantum ad materiam, unde[^8] sumta est, aut quantum ad formam, secundum quam animae Christi coniuncta est et a Verbo in unitatem personae assumta. Si loquamur de carne Christi secundum materiam, hoc est de eo, unde facta est caro Christi; sic conceditur, quod peccato fuerit obnoxia, quia ante conceptionem Christi tota massa humani generis corrupta erat, sicut in praecedenti problemate monstratum est. — Si autem loquamur de carne Christi secundum formam, sic dicendum est[^9], nunquam peccato fuisse obnoxiam, quia nunquam habuit illam formam, quin esset munda et immaculata.

Secundum autem quod caro dicitur dupliciter, sic et de propagatione dupliciter contingit loqui: aut ratione sui materialis, aut ratione formalis. Si ratione materialis, sic propagata fuit ab Adam usque ad Virginem in tota illa genealogia, et tota illa propagatio fuit secundum legem naturae et concupiscentiae. Si quantum ad esse actuale et formale, sic concepta fuit a Virgine praeter legem communem[^10] naturae et concupiscentiae.

Et sic patet, quod uno modo dicendi concedi potest, quod caro Christi, materialiter loquendo, sic fuit in lumbis parentum secundum propagationem, sicut caro aliorum hominum. Sed quoniam caro, quantum est de ratione sui nominis, non nominat materiam ipsam tantum, sed materiam sub tali forma; et[^11] propagatio et lex propagationis, additum ei quod est caro Christi, respicit eius eductionem secundum esse actuale: hinc est, quod haec est impropria: caro Christi fuit peccato obnoxia; et haec: caro Christi fuit ex parentibus seminaliter traducta; et haec similiter: caro Christi fuit

<!-- page 89 -->

in parentibus decimata; pro eo quod in omnibus, quantum est de vi sermonis, praedicatum attribuitur subiecto non ratione materiae tantum, sed ratione materiae sub tali forma. Caro enim est terminus substantialis; et ideo, quamvis supponat pro verbo de praeterito, oportet ad idem tempus referri formam praedicati cum subiecto[^12]. Et ideo omnes praedictae locutiones tanquam falsae et impropriae sunt negandae, nisi proferantur cum determinatione, scilicet ratione materialis principii. Si autem alicubi absolute proferantur, sunt exponendae. — Nota tamen, quod haec est impropria: caro Christi fuit peccato obnoxia; sed haec magis impropria: caro Christi fuit seminaliter traducta; haec autem adhuc magis impropria et falsa: caro Christi fuit decimata — secundum quod magis ac magis in praedictis locutionibus caro accipitur ratione formae.

Concedendum est igitur, quod aliter fuit caro Christi secundum legem propagationis in parentibus quam aliorum hominum. — Nam caro aliorum hominum ortum habuit a parentibus secundum seminalem rationem et secundum legem concupiscentiae. Illud autem dicitur procedere secundum rationem seminalem, quod quidem exit in esse secundum virtutem et operationem naturae; sicut sunt illa, in quorum productione natura non solum se habet per modum obedientis et suscipientis, immo etiam per modum naturae agentis et dantis speciem sive completionem; et hoc importat hoc quod dico seminalis ratio, proprie loquendo, sicut ostensum fuit in secundo libro[^13], quamvis contingat extensiori modo uti praedicto vocabulo. — Illud autem intelligitur traduci secundum legem concupiscentiae, in cuius procreatione concurrit foeditas delectationis carnis ex parte viri generantis et mulieris concipientis. — Neutrum horum fuit in generatione Christi. Nam conceptio eius mirabilis fuit et supra virtutem naturae; non enim fuit ex virili semine, pro eo quod caro eius concepta fuit ex sola muliere. Fuit etiam praeter legem concupiscentiae, quia non intervenit delectatio ex commixtione sexus: conceptus enim fuit ex castissimis Virginis visceribus[^14]. — Ideo concedendae sunt rationes, quae ad hanc partem inducuntur.

*Ad 1.* Ad illud quod obiicitur de auctoritate Magistri, dicendum, quod locutio ista est impropria, videlicet quod peccato fuerit obnoxia, et intelligendum est materialiter; et hoc Magister insinuat, cum dicit, quod peccato fuerit obnoxia, non simpliciter, sed in Maria[^15], in qua materialiter erat. Unde non est simpliciter concedenda, sed exponenda tanquam impropria; et ex impropriis secundum earum improprietatem acceptis non licet arguere, sicut manifestum est; et ideo ratio non valet.

*Ad 2.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod quidquid descendit ab Adam in Seth, descendit secundum seminalem rationem; dicendum, quod seminalis ratio non dicit quid absolutum, sed quid respectivum; dicit enim respectum eius quod propagatur ad illud, de quo producitur. Cum ergo dicit, quod quidquid descendit ab Adam in Seth, descendit secundum seminalem rationem; dicendum, quod verum est per comparationem ad Seth et ad hos qui producendi erant secundum propagationem naturae, respectu quorum omnium semen decisum a lumbis Adae habuit virtutem sufficientem ad rationem seminalem. Respectu autem Christi non habet veritatem, pro eo quod totum illud, quod secundum legem naturae ex illo semine producendum erat, respectu carnis Christi se habebat in ratione possibilis[^16] et materialis, quia non poterat reduci ad actum completum nisi per virtutem Spiritus sancti. Cum ergo dicit, quod quidquid fuit in Seth, fuit secundum seminalem rationem, verum est, si comparetur ad Adam; sed si comparetur ad Christum, non habet veritatem.

*Ad 3.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod totum semen decisum ab Adam habuit seminalem rationem; dicendum, quod verum est respectu eorum qui producendi erant secundum legem naturae; respectu Christi non habet veritatem. Quamvis enim in aliquo semine sit ratio seminalis secundum quamlibet eius partem, saltem formalem, ut ex eo aliquid possit fieri; tamen, si Deus aliquid faciat ex illo semine, ad quod non habebat ordinationem secundum naturam, dicitur fuisse in illo semine secundum corpulentam substantiam, non secundum rationem seminalem, quia semen illud respectu illius quod Deus educit, non habet vim sufficienter activam, sed passivam, secundum quam subministrat corporalem substantiam, esto etiam, quod illa seminalis ratio cooperaretur[^17] ad subministrationem materiae

<!-- page 90 -->

illius rei faciendae; ut si plantaretur unum semen parvum, ita quod de illo produceretur arbor magna, cuius magnitudo materia esset ad productionem magni corporis alterius speciei. Sic et in proposito intelligendum est, cum dicitur, Christum fuisse in lumbis parentum secundum corpulentam substantiam, non secundum seminalem rationem; non quia substantia Christi corporis ibi tota fuerit, sed quia erat aliquanta materia, quae per vim seminalem poterat augmentari et crescere, apposito sibi alimento, ita ut corpora omnium hominum inde possent fabricari; et per illam vim sunt corpora omnium hominum fabricata, excepto solo corpore Christi, quod fabricatum fuit per virtutem supernam, sicut melius manifestabitur infra. — Et sic patet, pro quanto Christus dicitur fuisse in Adam secundum corpulentam substantiam; et quod non sequitur, si fuit in semine Adae, quod fuerit in illo secundum seminalem rationem. Ad haec autem intelligenda valent illa quae determinata sunt secundo libro, distinctione trigesima.

*Ad 4.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod tota caro beatae Virginis fuit ibi secundum seminalem rationem, sicut caro aliorum hominum; dicendum, quod in illo argumento est sophisma secundum accidens, sicut dictum fuit supra de decimatione.

### Scholion
**I.** Ex principio in praecedenti quaest. probato, quod tota caro antiquorum Patrum fuit obnoxia peccato originali, sequi videtur, etiam carnem Christi ante assumtionem obnoxiam fuisse peccato; quod etiam verba Magistri (cfr. hic arg. 1. ad oppos.) simpliciter concedere videntur. Quam conclusionem ut evertat, S. Doctor in hac quaestione accuratius discutit illud principium ab omnibus concessum, quod Christus non eadem lege propagationis descenderit ab Adam, sicut alii homines, cum scilicet caro eius non secundum seminalem rationem fuerit ab Adam traducta, sed tantum materialiter (de his cfr. hic ad 2. 3, et praecipue II. Sent. d. 30. a. 3. q. 2. et scholion). Unde hac ratione aliter fuit in Adam caro Christi, aliter caro aliorum hominum. De his et de ipsa re nulla est inter Scholasticos differentia opinionum, sed tantum de modo loquendi, utrum scilicet dici possit, carnem Christi fuisse in Patribus peccato obnoxiam. S. Thomas videtur hanc locutionem simpliciter concedere; sed eam intelligit in hoc sensu: nempe eo modo, quo fuit in Patribus. In Patribus autem eadem fuit tantum secundum materiale principium. S. Bonaventura vero cautius loqui vult, nec praedictam locutionem admittit ut proprie dictam, nisi explicite addatur: ratione materialis principii. — In dub. 1. huius partis auctor noster explicat sensum huius locutionis, quod in Maria et Patribus caro Christi sit peccato obnoxia, scilicet non ratione culpae, sed ratione causae et sequelae peccati.

**II.** Praeter laudatos in scholio ad 1. quaest.: S. Thom., hic q. 4. a. 1; S. III. q. 31. a. 7. — B. Albert., hic a. 26. — Aegid. R., hic q. 4. a. 1. et dub. lat. 1.

## English

### Question II. *Whether Christ's flesh was in the forefathers otherwise than the flesh of other men according to the law of propagation.*

Secondly it is asked, whether Christ's flesh was in the forefathers otherwise than the flesh of other men according to the law of propagation. And that it was otherwise, this seems to be the case:

1. By the authority of Augustine, in the tenth book *On Genesis according to the Letter*[^1], treating that text of the Apostle to the Hebrews, chapter seven: « He was still in the loins of [his] father. According to a certain mode Christ was there, by which Levi was not there. For according to seminal reason Levi was there, by which reason Christ's flesh was not there ».

2. Likewise, this same thing is shown by reason. A mediated principle is reduced to an immediate one[^2]; but Christ went forth from the Virgin's womb according to a different mode of propagation than the other children [go forth] from the womb of their mothers, for the reason that [he was] not from male seed: if therefore by the mediation of the blessed Virgin he is said to have been in the preceding forefathers, it seems that his flesh was in the forefathers according to the law of propagation in a different mode than [the flesh] of other men.

3. Likewise, if Christ's flesh had been in the forefathers in the same mode as the rest of men[^3], it would thus have sinned, when Adam sinned, just as other men; but this is false: therefore, etc.

4. Likewise, in the [same] mode in which a thing is to be brought forth into being, by that mode it has to exist in its principle[^4]; but Christ was not begotten or conceived according to the law of nature, but rather miraculously, [whereas] other men are brought forth naturally: therefore his flesh was in the loins of the forefathers, as regards the law of propagation, otherwise than [the flesh] of other men.

**On the contrary:** 1. The Master says in the text[^5]: « That flesh, before it was united to the Word, was subject to sin in Mary, just as the rest of the Virgin's flesh »; but flesh which is alike according to the transmission of original [sin] is alike according to the law of propagation: therefore if Christ's flesh was subject to sin in the loins of the forefathers, then it was also transmitted from the first parents by the same law.

2. Likewise, everything that descended from Adam into Seth descended according to corporeal substance and according to seminal reason: therefore if Christ's flesh descended from Adam into Seth, and from Seth into the other forefathers, it seems that according to the law of propagation it was in the loins of the forefathers in the same mode as the flesh of other men[^6].

3. Likewise, the seed from which the body of Seth was formed had seminal reason in all the forefathers: therefore nothing was to come from it which was not there both according to corporeal substance and[^7] [according to] seminal reason; but if Christ's flesh was made from it, it seems that it was there in the manner of any other human flesh.

4. Likewise, the whole flesh of the Virgin was in the loins of the forefathers according to the law of propagation, just as the flesh of other men; but Christ's flesh was from some part of the Virgin's flesh: therefore according to the law of propagation it was in the loins of the forefathers, just as the flesh of the whole human race.

### Conclusio

> Christ's flesh, according to the law of propagation, was in the forefathers otherwise than the flesh of other men.

**Respondeo:** It must be said that there are two ways of speaking about Christ's flesh: either as regards the matter from which[^8] it was taken, or as regards the form according to which it was joined to Christ's soul and assumed by the Word into the unity of the person. If we speak of Christ's flesh according to matter, that is, concerning that from which Christ's flesh was made; thus it is conceded that it was subject to sin, because before Christ's conception the whole mass of the human race was corrupted, as was shown in the preceding problem. — But if we speak of Christ's flesh according to form, then it must be said[^9] that it was never subject to sin, because it never had that form unless it were clean and immaculate.

Now since "flesh" is said in two ways, so likewise it is possible to speak of propagation in two ways: either by reason of its material [aspect], or by reason of the formal [aspect]. If by reason of the material, then it was propagated from Adam down to the Virgin in that whole genealogy, and that whole propagation was according to the law of nature and concupiscence. If as regards actual and formal being, then it was conceived by the Virgin apart from the common law[^10] of nature and concupiscence.

And thus it is clear that in one manner of speaking it can be conceded that Christ's flesh, speaking materially, was in the loins of the forefathers according to propagation, just as the flesh of other men. But since "flesh," as regards the meaning of its name, does not name matter alone, but matter under such a form; and[^11] propagation and the law of propagation, added to that which is "Christ's flesh," regard its production according to actual being: hence it is that this [proposition] is improper: Christ's flesh was subject to sin; and this: Christ's flesh was seminally transmitted from the forefathers; and this likewise: Christ's flesh was

decimated in the forefathers; for the reason that in all [of these], as regards the force of the expression, the predicate is attributed to the subject not by reason of the matter alone, but by reason of the matter under such a form. For "flesh" is a substantial term; and therefore, although it stands for a verb in the past tense, the form of the predicate must be referred to the same time as the subject[^12]. And therefore all the aforesaid expressions are to be denied as false and improper, unless they are uttered with a determination, namely by reason of the material principle. But if anywhere they are uttered absolutely, they are to be expounded. — Note nonetheless that this is improper: Christ's flesh was subject to sin; but this [is] more improper: Christ's flesh was seminally transmitted; and this [is] still more improper and false: Christ's flesh was decimated — according as, in the aforesaid expressions, "flesh" is taken more and more by reason of the form.

It must therefore be conceded that Christ's flesh was in the forefathers according to the law of propagation otherwise than [the flesh] of other men. — For the flesh of other men had its origin from the forefathers according to seminal reason and according to the law of concupiscence. Now that is said to proceed according to seminal reason which indeed goes forth into being according to the power and operation of nature; as are those things, in whose production nature behaves not only after the manner of one obeying and receiving, but rather also after the manner of a nature acting and giving species or completion; and this is what I mean by "seminal reason," properly speaking, as was shown in the second book[^13], although it happens that the aforesaid term is used in an extended way. — But that is understood to be transmitted according to the law of concupiscence in whose procreation there concurs the foulness of the delight of the flesh on the part of the begetting man and the conceiving woman. — Neither of these was present in Christ's generation. For his conception was miraculous and above the power of nature; for it was not from male seed, for the reason that his flesh was conceived from a woman alone. It was also apart from the law of concupiscence, because no delight from the mingling of the sexes intervened: for he was conceived from the most chaste womb of the Virgin[^14]. — Therefore the reasons which are adduced for this side are to be conceded.

*Ad 1.* To that which is objected concerning the authority of the Master, it must be said that that expression is improper, namely that it was subject to sin, and it must be understood materially; and the Master intimates this when he says that it was subject to sin, not absolutely, but in Mary[^15], in whom it was materially. Hence it is not to be conceded absolutely, but to be expounded as improper; and from improper [propositions], taken according to their impropriety, it is not licit to argue, as is manifest; and therefore the argument is not valid.

*Ad 2.* To that which is objected, that whatever descended from Adam into Seth descended according to seminal reason; it must be said that seminal reason does not denote something absolute, but something relative; for it denotes the relation of that which is propagated to that from which it is produced. When therefore he says that whatever descended from Adam into Seth descended according to seminal reason; it must be said that it is true by comparison to Seth and to those who were to be produced according to the propagation of nature, in respect of all of whom the seed cut off from the loins of Adam had power sufficient for seminal reason. But in respect of Christ it does not hold true, for the reason that all that which was to be produced according to the law of nature from that seed, in respect of Christ's flesh stood in the account of the possible[^16] and the material, because it could not be reduced to complete act except by the power of the Holy Spirit. When therefore he says that whatever was in Seth was according to seminal reason, it is true if it is compared to Adam; but if it is compared to Christ, it does not hold true.

*Ad 3.* To that which is objected, that the whole seed cut off from Adam had seminal reason; it must be said that it is true in respect of those who were to be produced according to the law of nature; in respect of Christ it does not hold true. For although in some seed there is seminal reason according to each of its parts, at least the formal [part], so that from it something can come to be; nonetheless, if God makes something from that seed for which it did not have an ordination according to nature, it is said to have been in that seed according to corporeal substance, not according to seminal reason, because that seed, in respect of that which God brings forth, does not have a sufficiently active power, but a passive one, according to which it supplies corporeal substance, even granted that that seminal reason were to cooperate[^17] toward the supplying of the matter

for the making of that thing; just as if one small seed were planted, such that from it there should be produced a great tree, whose magnitude would be the matter for the production of a great body of another species. So too in the case at hand it must be understood, when it is said that Christ was in the loins of the forefathers according to corporeal substance, not according to seminal reason; not because the substance of Christ's body was wholly there, but because there was a certain quantity of matter which by seminal power could be increased and grow, with nourishment applied to it, such that the bodies of all men could be fashioned from it; and by that power the bodies of all men were fashioned, except only the body of Christ, which was fashioned by a supernal power, as will be better made manifest below. — And thus it is clear in what way Christ is said to have been in Adam according to corporeal substance; and that it does not follow, if he was in the seed of Adam, that he was in it according to seminal reason. And for understanding these things, those things avail which were determined in the second book, distinction thirty.

*Ad 4.* To that which is objected, that the whole flesh of the blessed Virgin was there according to seminal reason, just as the flesh of other men; it must be said that in that argument there is a fallacy of accident, as was said above concerning the decimation.

### Scholion
**I.** From the principle proved in the preceding question, namely that the whole flesh of the ancient Fathers was subject to original sin, it seems to follow that Christ's flesh too, before its assumption, was subject to sin; which the words of the Master also (cf. here arg. 1, on the contrary) seem simply to concede. In order to overturn this conclusion, the holy Doctor in this question discusses more carefully that principle granted by all, namely that Christ did not descend from Adam by the same law of propagation as other men, since indeed his flesh was not transmitted from Adam according to seminal reason, but only materially (concerning these things cf. here ad 2 and 3, and especially II Sent., d. 30, a. 3, q. 2, and the scholion). Hence by this reason Christ's flesh was in Adam in one way, the flesh of other men in another way. Concerning these things, and concerning the matter itself, there is no difference of opinion among the Scholastics, but only concerning the manner of speaking, namely whether it can be said that Christ's flesh was in the Fathers subject to sin. St. Thomas seems simply to concede this expression; but he understands it in this sense: namely in the mode in which it was in the Fathers. Now in the Fathers it was the same only according to the material principle. St. Bonaventure, however, wishes to speak more cautiously, and does not admit the aforesaid expression as properly said, unless this be explicitly added: by reason of the material principle. — In dub. 1 of this part our author explains the sense of this expression, namely that in Mary and the Fathers Christ's flesh is subject to sin, that is, not by reason of fault, but by reason of the cause and the consequence of sin.

**II.** Besides those cited in the scholion to the 1st question: St. Thomas, here q. 4, a. 1; S. III, q. 31, a. 7. — B. Albert, here a. 26. — Aegidius Rom., here q. 4, a. 1, and broader dub. 1.
