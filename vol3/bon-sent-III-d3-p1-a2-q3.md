## *Utrum in secunda sanctificatione a beata Virgine ablata fuerit peccandi potentia.*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 76 -->

### Quaestio III. *Utrum in secunda sanctificatione a beata Virgine ablata fuerit peccandi potentia.*

Tertio quaeritur, utrum in secunda sanctificatione ablata fuerit ab ea peccandi potentia. Et quod sic, videtur:

1. *Primo* per Ambrosium in libro de Virginitate beatae Virginis[^1]: « Impossibile fuit, uterum Virginis, quem Filius Dei inhabitando consecravit, alienae copulae coitu incestari »; sed similis ratio fuit de aliis generibus peccatorum: ergo videtur, quod nullo alio peccati genere potuerit maculari post conceptionem Filii Dei.

2. Item, vituperium esset Christo, si haberet matrem peccatricem[^2]: si ergo Christo in nullo potest fieri contumelia, videtur, quod post ipsius conceptionem in beata Virgine non potuerit esse culpa.

3. Item, si beata Virgo potuerit peccare, potuit in peccato perseverare, ergo potuit damnari: si ergo Matrem Dei et Reginam caelorum damnari in inferno omnino est impossibile: ergo et illud, ex quo sequitur, scilicet hoc quod ponitur, ipsam posse peccare[^3]: ergo etc.

4. Item, dignior fuit Mater Christi ex hoc, quod Mater Dei effecta est, quam sit aliquis Angelus in caelo: si ergo Angeli boni in gratia sunt confirmati[^4], multo magis et Mater Dei: ergo etc.

<!-- page 77 -->

**Sed contra:** 1. Lucae secundo[^5]: *Tuam ipsius animam pertransibit gladius*; hoc verbum tractans Damascenus dicit: « Dolores, quos Virgo effugit pariens, hos in Christi passione sustinuit, ita quod gladius animam eius pertransivit »: ergo ipsius anima fuit in Christi passione turbata, et ita perturbata: si ergo potuit perturbari, videtur, quod potuit ad veniale pertrahi.

2. Item, Augustinus in Quaestionibus novi et veteris Testamenti[^6] tractans illud: *Tuam ipsius animam pertransibit gladius*, ait sic: « Hoc utique significavit, quod Maria, per quam gestum est mysterium incarnationis, dubitaret, non tamen in dubitatione permaneret »: si ergo, postquam genuit Filium Dei, potuit dubitare in fide, potuit peccare.

3. Item, potentia moriendi consequitur potentiam peccandi[^7]; sed beata Virgo Maria post conceptionem mori potuit: ergo videtur, quod peccare potuerit.

4. Item, impossibilitas[^8] peccandi spectat ad statum comprehensoris; sed beata Virgo ex hoc, quod Filium Dei genuit, non est posita extra statum viatoris: ergo videtur, quod non sit ei ablata potentia peccandi.

### Conclusio

> Beata Virgo post Filii Dei conceptionem peccare non potuit, non propter alicuius potentiae amissionem, sed propter potentiae in bono confirmationem et defectus ablationem.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod in secunda sanctificatione non tantum fuit data Virgini gratia sanctificans, sed etiam gratia in bono confirmans; et hoc, quia Filio suo adeo fuit coniuncta, ut nullo modo permitteret, eam a se ipso sequestrari.

Propter quod notandi sunt gradus divinorum beneficiorum. Christus enim, qui fuit Sanctus Sanctorum, facultatem habuit vitandi omne peccatum et impossibilitatem committendi aliquod peccatum[^9]. Alii vero filii hominum difficultatem habent ad vitandum peccatum et necessitatem ad committendum; et hoc quidem competit omnibus generatis de massa peccati. Ab hac igitur massa liberata fuit Virgo Maria in prima sanctificatione, liberata etiam fuit in secunda; sed in prima fuit conformis aliis sanctificatis; in secunda facta est conformis Filio, quem genuit. — In prima namque sanctificatione data est ei potestas et facultas vitandi omne peccatum mortale; data est etiam facultas vitandi frequentiam venialium; data est etiam nihilominus facultas vitandi omne peccatum, non tantum particulariter, sed etiam universaliter. — Et in his tribus gradibus patent tres gradus sanctificationum, videlicet Ioannis Baptistae, Ieremiae et Mariae. Nam prima conditio fuit Ieremiae; secunda vero Ioannis; sed ista triplex[^10] Virginis Mariae, quae plus ad Filii accessit puritatem per vitae meritum.

Sed cum Sanctum Sanctorum gestavit in utero suo, tunc sanctificata fuit conformiter Filio suo, non tantum quantum ad potentiam ad peccatum[^11], sed etiam quantum ad impossibilitatem labendi in aliquod peccatum. Unde sicut impossibile fuit propter honorem Filii sui, ut alium haberet filium; sic impossibile fuit, ut unquam ulterius haberet peccatum. Sicut etiam impossibile fuit, ut carnis virginitas violaretur, in qua Filius Dei habitaverat; sic impossibile fuit, ut mentis sanctitas aliqua culpa macularetur. — Ad cuius testimonium valet quod quidam Iudaei asserunt, hoc mirum fuisse in Virgine Maria, quod cum esset pulcherrima, tamen a nullo unquam viro fuerit concupita. Nec vilipendendum est illud infidelium testimonium; oportet enim testimonium habere ab his qui foris sunt[^12].

Concedendae sunt igitur rationes ostendentes, quod beata Virgo Maria post Filii Dei conceptionem peccare non potuit, non propter alicuius potentiae amissionem, sed propter potentiae confirmationem et defectus ablationem.

<!-- page 78 -->

*Ad 1.* Ad illud ergo quod primo obiicitur, quod beata Virgo fuit perturbata; dicendum, quod immensitas illa doloris non refertur ad partem animae rationalem, sed sensibilem. Unde *anima* stat ibi pro illa parte, secundum quam convertitur ad corpus; unde non fuit perturbata, secundum quod perturbatio dicit deordinationem in parte rationali[^13].

*Ad 2.* Ad illud quod obiicitur de auctoritate Quaestionum novi et veteris Testamenti, quod beata Virgo dubitavit; dicendum, quod non constat, illum librum esse Augustini, propter hoc quod multa ibi sunt, quae non videntur esse verba Augustini. — Verumtamen, ne fugam petere videamur, dici potest, quod est dubitatio proveniens ex *fidei debilitate*, et est quaedam dubitatio proveniens ex *admirationis immensitate*, qua dicitur homo dubitare, cum exstupescit et quasi fit totus extra se ex intuitu rei admirabilis. Et hoc modo intelligendum est, Virginem dubitasse, non propter defectum fidei[^14]. Indubitanter enim credendum est, quod Virgo Maria semper stetit, unde in ea fides sibi invenit solidum fundamentum. Unde discipulis non credentibus et dubitantibus, ipsa fuit, in qua fides Ecclesiae remansit solida et inconcussa; et ideo in die sabbati in honorem eius solemnizat omnis Ecclesia[^15].

*Ad 3.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod potuit mori: dicendum, quod non est simile, pro eo quod non fuit decens, Filium Dei habere matrem immortalem, cum ipse esset mortalis[^16], sed valde indecens esset, si matrem haberet peccatricem; quamvis Hieronymus dicat, quod solum peccatrices numerantur in genealogia Salvatoris; hoc enim non est propter peccatum, sed propter aliquod speciale factum, propter quod illae mulieres exprimuntur in Scriptura. Et completur in eis illa prophetia, sicut illud Isaiae decimo sexto[^17]: *Emitte agnum Domine dominatorem terrae* etc., et per *petram deserti* intelligitur Ruth, sicut dicit Hieronymus. Quidquid tamen sit de parentibus remotis, nulli tamen dubium est de Matre Dei, quin talis debuerit esse, quae non potuerit redargui de peccato, cum fuisset ad improperium Christi.

*Ad 4.* Ad illud quod obiicitur de impossibilitate peccandi, dicendum, quod verum est, quantum est de lege communi; sed nihil impedit, quin de gratia speciali possit alicui concedi in via, quod aliis communiter datur in patria, et illi personae praecipue, quae in terris degens regina erat eorum qui exsultant in caelis[^18].

## English
<!-- page 76 -->

### Question III. *Whether in the second sanctification the power of sinning was taken from the Virgin.*

Thirdly it is asked, whether in the second sanctification the power of sinning was taken from her. And that it was, it seems:

1. *First*, by Ambrose in the book *On the Virginity of the Blessed Virgin*[^1]: « It was impossible that the womb of the Virgin, which the Son of God consecrated by dwelling in it, should be defiled by intercourse of another's union »; but a like account holds of the other kinds of sins: therefore it seems that by no other kind of sin could she be stained after the conception of the Son of God.

2. Likewise, it would be a reproach to Christ if he had a sinful mother[^2]: if therefore no affront can be done to Christ, it seems that after his conception there could be no fault in the blessed Virgin.

3. Likewise, if the blessed Virgin could sin, she could persevere in sin, and so she could be damned: if therefore it is altogether impossible that the Mother of God and Queen of heaven be damned in hell: therefore also that from which it follows, namely the thing posited—that she could sin[^3]—[is impossible]: therefore etc.

4. Likewise, the Mother of Christ was more worthy from the fact that she was made the Mother of God than is any Angel in heaven: if therefore the good Angels are confirmed in grace[^4], much more also is the Mother of God: therefore etc.

<!-- page 77 -->

**Sed contra:** 1. Luke, chapter two[^5]: *And thy own soul a sword shall pierce*; treating this word Damascene says: « The sorrows which the Virgin escaped in giving birth, these she sustained in the passion of Christ, so that a sword pierced her soul »: therefore her soul was troubled in the passion of Christ, and so disturbed: if therefore she could be disturbed, it seems that she could be drawn to a venial sin.

2. Likewise, Augustine in the *Questions on the New and Old Testament*[^6], treating that text *And thy own soul a sword shall pierce*, says thus: « This indeed signified that Mary, through whom the mystery of the incarnation was accomplished, would doubt, yet would not remain in doubt »: if therefore, after she bore the Son of God, she could doubt in faith, she could sin.

3. Likewise, the power of dying follows upon the power of sinning[^7]; but the blessed Virgin Mary could die after the conception: therefore it seems that she could sin.

4. Likewise, the impossibility[^8] of sinning belongs to the state of the comprehensor; but the blessed Virgin, from the fact that she bore the Son of God, was not placed outside the state of the wayfarer: therefore it seems that the power of sinning was not taken from her.

### Conclusio

> The blessed Virgin, after the conception of the Son of God, could not sin—not on account of the loss of any power, but on account of the confirmation of her power in the good and the removal of defect.

**Respondeo:** It must be said that in the second sanctification there was given to the Virgin not only sanctifying grace, but also grace confirming her in the good; and this, because she was so joined to her Son that she would in no way allow herself to be separated from him.

On account of which the degrees of the divine benefits are to be noted. For Christ, who was the Holy of Holies, had the faculty of avoiding every sin and the impossibility of committing any sin[^9]. But the other sons of men have difficulty in avoiding sin and necessity toward committing it; and this indeed belongs to all who are begotten from the mass of sin. From this mass, then, the Virgin Mary was freed in the first sanctification, and she was freed also in the second; but in the first she was conformed to the others who are sanctified; in the second she was made conformed to the Son whom she bore. — For in the first sanctification there was given to her the power and faculty of avoiding every mortal sin; there was given also the faculty of avoiding the frequency of venial sins; there was given moreover the faculty of avoiding every sin, not only particularly, but also universally. — And in these three degrees the three degrees of sanctifications appear, namely those of John the Baptist, of Jeremiah, and of Mary. For the first condition was Jeremiah's; the second indeed John's; but this triple[^10] [condition was] of the Virgin Mary, who came nearer to the purity of the Son by the merit of her life.

But when she bore the Holy of Holies in her womb, then she was sanctified conformably to her Son, not only as to the power for sin[^11], but also as to the impossibility of falling into any sin. Hence, just as it was impossible, on account of the honor of her Son, that she should have another son; so it was impossible that she should ever afterward have sin. Just as it was likewise impossible that the virginity of the flesh in which the Son of God had dwelt should be violated; so it was impossible that the holiness of the mind should be stained by any fault. — To the witness of which avails that which certain Jews assert, that this was a marvel in the Virgin Mary, that, although she was most beautiful, yet she was never coveted by any man. Nor is that testimony of unbelievers to be despised; for it is fitting to have testimony from those who are without[^12].

Therefore the arguments are to be granted which show that the blessed Virgin Mary could not sin after the conception of the Son of God, not on account of the loss of any power, but on account of the confirmation of power and the removal of defect.

<!-- page 78 -->

*Ad 1.* To that, then, which is objected first, that the blessed Virgin was disturbed; it must be said that that immensity of grief is not referred to the rational part of the soul, but to the sensible. Hence *soul* stands there for that part by which she is turned toward the body; hence she was not disturbed according as disturbance bespeaks a disorder in the rational part[^13].

*Ad 2.* To that which is objected from the authority of the *Questions on the New and Old Testament*, that the blessed Virgin doubted; it must be said that it is not established that that book is Augustine's, because there are many things there which do not seem to be the words of Augustine. — Nevertheless, lest we seem to seek flight, it can be said that there is a doubt arising from *weakness of faith*, and there is a certain doubt arising from *immensity of wonder*, by which a man is said to doubt when he is amazed and becomes, as it were, wholly beside himself from the contemplation of a marvelous thing. And in this way it is to be understood that the Virgin doubted, not on account of a defect of faith[^14]. For it is to be believed without doubt that the Virgin Mary always stood firm, so that in her faith found a solid foundation. Hence, while the disciples did not believe and doubted, she was the one in whom the faith of the Church remained solid and unshaken; and therefore on the day of the Sabbath the whole Church holds solemnity in her honor[^15].

*Ad 3.* To that which is objected, that she could die: it must be said that the case is not alike, because it was not fitting that the Son of God should have an immortal mother, since he himself was mortal[^16], but it would be very unfitting if he had a sinful mother; although Jerome says that only sinful women are reckoned in the genealogy of the Savior; for this is not on account of sin, but on account of some special deed, on account of which those women are mentioned in Scripture. And in them that prophecy is fulfilled, as that of Isaiah, chapter sixteen[^17]: *Send forth the lamb, O Lord, the ruler of the earth* etc., and by *the rock of the desert* Ruth is understood, as Jerome says. Yet whatever may be the case concerning the remote forebears, there is no doubt to anyone concerning the Mother of God, but that she ought to be such as could not be reproached for sin, since it would have been to the disgrace of Christ.

*Ad 4.* To that which is objected concerning the impossibility of sinning, it must be said that it is true so far as concerns the common law; but nothing prevents that by special grace there be granted to someone in the wayfaring state what is commonly given to others in the fatherland, and especially to that person who, while dwelling on earth, was the queen of those who rejoice in the heavens[^18].
