# III Sent., d. 3, p. 2, a. 2, q. 1

## *Utrum caro Christi in Abraham et aliis Patribus distincta fuerit secundum qualitatem a carne aliorum hominum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 85 -->

### Articulus II. *De carnis Christi traductione.*

Consequenter quaeritur de carnis Christi traductione. Et circa hoc quaeruntur duo.

Primo quaeritur, utrum caro Christi fuerit in primis parentibus secundum qualitatem puritatis[^1] vel inquinationis.

Secundo quaeritur, utrum aliter fuerit quam secundum legem propagationis.

### Quaestio I. *Utrum caro Christi in Abraham et aliis Patribus distincta fuerit secundum qualitatem a carne aliorum hominum.*

Circa primum sic proceditur et ostenditur, quod caro Christi in Abraham et aliis Patribus distincta fuerit secundum qualitatem a carne aliorum filiorum.

1. Septimo Sapientiae[^2]: *Nihil coinquinatum in illam incurrit:* ergo si caro Christi unita est Filio Dei, qui est *Dei virtus et Dei sapientia*, videtur, quod nunquam fuerit inquinata; sed caro aliorum in parentibus inquinata fuit: ergo videtur, quod aliter fuerit caro Christi in Abraham quam aliorum.

2. Item, in Christo debuit esse maxima puritas; sed magis purum est quod nunquam fuit inquinatum, quam illud quod aliquando inquinatum fuit: ergo videtur, quod caro Christi nec in se nec in parentibus subiacuerit foeditati corruptionis[^3]: igitur a carne aliorum hominum distincta fuit qualitative.

3. Item, alterius qualitatis est medicina et illud quod curatur per medicinam; sed caro Christi fuit in lumbis Patrum sicut medicina, sicut dicit Augustinus decimo super Genesim ad litteram[^4], caro vero aliorum hominum sicut indigens medicina: ergo videtur, quod habuerit qualitatem aliam et aliam.

4. Item, quod totaliter corruptum est non est possibile ad curationem: si ergo Adam fuit possibilis ad curationem, aliqua particula remansit in eo incorrupta. Sed de illa particula, quae[^5] sine corruptione erat, maxime decebat generari carnem, quae esset sine originali macula: ergo videtur etc.

**Sed contra:** 1. Augustinus decimo super Genesim ad litteram[^6]: « Eadem namque caro non Abrahae tantum, sed ipsius primi terrenique hominis simul habuit *vulnus* praevaricationis et *medicamentum vulneris* » : ergo si *medicamentum* vulneris spectat ad Christum, *vulnus* praevaricationis spectat ad alios, eadem igitur fuit caro Christi in primis parentibus cum carne aliorum: ergo non fuit distincta ab aliis.

2. Item, Magister in principio huius distinctionis[^7] loquens de carne Christi: « Credi oportet iuxta sanctorum Doctorum convenientiam, ipsam prius fuisse peccato obnoxiam, sicut fuit reliqua Virginis caro » : restat igitur idem quod prius, quod non fuerit distincta ab aliis.

3. Item, quidquid lege corruptionis traducitur, corruptum est; sed tota caro Isaac, quae fuit traducta ab Abraham, fuit corrupta: aut igitur[^8] caro Christi non fuit traducta ab Abraham in Isaac, aut si traducta fuit, ab aliis hominibus distincta non fuit.

4. Item, aut caro Christi fuit similis carni Abrahae reliquae, aut non. Si similis, habeo propositum; si dissimilis: ergo non videtur, quod talis caro competeret ad redemptionem humani generis[^9]. Si ergo hoc est falsum et inconveniens, restat ergo, quod caro Christi a carne aliorum hominum non fuerit distincta.

5. Item, distinctio secundum oppositas qualitates praesupponit distinctionem secundum substantiam, quia oppositae qualitates non possunt esse in eodem et secundum idem[^10]; sed caro filiorum in primis parentibus non potuit habere distinctionem secundum substantiam, quia non esset tot atomos invenire in corpore Adae, quot inde filii procreati sunt: ergo si caro Christi in primis parentibus non
<!-- page 86 -->
potuit distingui a carne aliorum secundum *substantiam*, videtur etc.

6. Item, si transfundebatur caro munda, aut *corruptibilis*, aut *incorruptibilis*. Si *incorruptibilis*: ergo corpus Christi debuit esse impassibile; quod falsum est. Si *corruptibilis*: ergo videtur, quod poterat transmutari in carnem reliquam et augeri ab alia: ergo et alia poterat pari ratione transmutari in aliis[^11]: igitur aut caro illa fuit consumta, antequam perveniret ad Christum, aut fuit adeo multiplicata, ut possent inde mille corpora formari. Sed hoc est falsum: ergo etc.

### Conclusio

> Caro Christi in lumbis parentum, in quibus fuit materialiter, a carne aliorum hominum non fuit distincta qualitative vel formaliter.

**Respondeo:** Ad praedictorum intelligentiam est notandum, quod circa hanc quaestionem fuit quaedam positio erronea quorundam dicentium, quod caro Christi non tantum distinctionem habuit a carne aliorum hominum, cum fuit unita animae Christi[^12], sed etiam cum fuit in lumbis hominis primi. Dicebant enim, quod quia Adam reparabiliter peccavit, in peccato non permisit Deus totum corrumpi; et ideo remansit in eius semine aliqua portio incorrupta, ut per illam fieret medicamentum carni reliquae; et ex illa portiuncula[^13] dicunt carnem Christi formatam, quam dicunt esse generis humani medicinam. Hanc autem particulam transmisit Adam ad Noe, et Noe ad Abraham, et Abraham ad David, et David usque ad Virginem, de qua natus est Christus. — Haec autem positio, etsi habere videatur probabilitatem in superficie, intrinsecus errorem[^14] continet; pro eo quod non solum est contra Sanctorum dicta veridica, sed etiam contra verae *fidei documenta*. *Sanctorum auctoritatibus* repugnat: quia Damascenus, doctor Graecus, et Augustinus, praecipuus doctor Latinus, in hoc consentiunt, quod caro Christi ante unionem cum Verbo indistincta erat a reliqua Virginis carne. Damascenus[^15] dicit, quod « de purissimis Virginis sanguinibus fuerit formata » ; Augustinus dicit, quod « assumserit primitiam nostrae massae ». — Et hoc quidem necessarium est ponere, quia non fuisset necessaria Virginis sanctificatio ad hoc, quod formaretur caro Christi sine omni peccato. — Non esset etiam Christus vere de semine David et Abrahae, sed solum Adae; quoniam, si praedicto modo illa[^16] particula descendisset, ad nullum aliorum pertineret nisi sicut deferens, quemadmodum aqua comparatur ad canalem. — Non etiam esset in tali carne verae passibilitatis assumtio nec nostrae infirmitatis vera reparatio, quia non de natura lapsa fabricasset sibi carnem, sed potius de instituta. — Quae omnia inconvenientia sunt et *fidei sanae* adversantia. Et ideo praedicta positio tanquam erronea est respuenda, sicut Magister bene innuit in littera[^17], et Magister Hugo in libro de Sacramentis.

Planum est igitur secundum doctrinam catholicam, carnem Christi in lumbis parentum, in quibus fuit materialiter, a carne aliorum hominum non fuisse distinctam qualitative, vel formaliter, quamvis, postquam unita est Verbo, distinctionem habeat, pro eo quod non est *caro peccati*, sed *similis peccatrici*. Et pro tanto dicit Augustinus[^18], quod « caro Christi venerit de altitudine humanae naturae », non quod in primis parentibus sine pollutione fuerit, sed quia sic purificata est et mundata in sua assumtione, quod similis fuit carni in statu innocentiae. Propter quod dicitur a Boethio[^19] « de omni statu aliquid accepisse », sicut infra melius manifestabi<!-- page 87 -->tur. — Unde rationes, quae ad hanc partem inducuntur, sunt concedendae.

*Ad 1.* Ad illud vero quod primo obiicitur in contrarium de Sapientiae septimo, quod nihil inquinatum in illam incurrit; dicendum, quod verum est; hoc enim intelligitur de Sapientia *divina*[^20] secundum *divinam naturam*, quae nullo modo foedatur ex aliqua creatura; verumtamen, si etiam intelligatur secundum *humanam naturam*, adhuc non habet instantiam, quia caro Christi, ex quo fuit unita Verbo Dei, sancta fuit et immaculata. Sed ex hoc non sequitur, quod immaculata fuisset in primis parentibus, pro eo quod, quamvis in lumbis parentum esset unibilis, erat tamen in dispositione remota.

*Ad 2.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod maxima puritas debet esse in carne Christi; dicendum, quod verum est, secundum quod competit ad reparationem humani generis. Sic autem intelligendo, non potest maior puritas cogitari, quam quod de massa peccatrice formetur caro omnino immunis a peccato; et sic factum est in carne unita Verbo, operante Spiritu sancto. Quodsi intelligatur caro illa nunquam in se, vel in parentibus fuisse obnoxia[^21]; non sic cogitatur, qualiter nostrae reparationi fuerit apta; et ideo non cogitatur, qualiter et sicut debet, sed potius assignatur, qualiter non debet.

*Ad 3.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod caro Christi est medicina; dicendum, quod verum est pro tempore, quo medicina est; medicina autem est pro eo tempore, quo a Verbo[^22] assumta est; et sic alterius qualitatis est quam aliorum hominum. Pro eo autem tempore, quo fuit ante assumtionem, non erat medicina, nisi materialiter loquendo. Secundum illud tempus non oportet formaliter distingui ab alia carne, pro eo quod ex eodem principio materiali potest formari sanum et aegrum. Cibus enim eiusdem generis in sano convertitur in carnem sanam et in leproso in carnem corruptam.

*Ad 4.* Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod si totus esset corruptus, iam non esset medicabilis eius morbus: dicendum, quod illud argumentum dupliciter deficit. Primo, quia, etsi homo totus corruptus esset, non sequitur, quod incurabilis esset, nisi illa corruptio ei inseparabiliter adhaereret. Praeterea, etsi totus homo corruptus esset quantum ad ea quae erant in se, non tamen corruptum esset totum genus humanum, quod fabricatum est ex illa hominis carne; sed illud solum, quod processit secundum legem concupiscentiae. Et quoniam caro Christi in Virgine fabricata est praeter legem concupiscentiae a Spiritu sancto sanctificante[^23], hinc est, quod caruit corruptione et rationem tenuit medicinae a sua primaria formatione.

### Scholion

## English

### Article II. *On the conveying of Christ's flesh.*

Next it is asked about the conveying of Christ's flesh. And concerning this two things are asked.

First it is asked whether Christ's flesh was in the first parents according to a quality of purity[^1] or of defilement.

Second it is asked whether it was otherwise than according to the law of propagation.

### Question I. *Whether Christ's flesh in Abraham and the other Fathers was distinct in quality from the flesh of other men.*

Concerning the first one proceeds thus, and it is shown that Christ's flesh in Abraham and the other Fathers was distinct in quality from the flesh of the other sons.

1. *Wisdom* 7[^2]: *Nothing defiled enters into her:* therefore if Christ's flesh was united to the Son of God, who is *the power of God and the wisdom of God*, it seems that it was never defiled; but the flesh of others was defiled in the parents: therefore it seems that Christ's flesh was otherwise in Abraham than that of others.

2. Likewise, in Christ there ought to have been the greatest purity; but that which was never defiled is more pure than that which was at some time defiled: therefore it seems that Christ's flesh neither in itself nor in the parents was subject to the foulness of corruption[^3]: therefore it was distinct in quality from the flesh of other men.

3. Likewise, medicine and that which is cured by medicine are of a different quality; but Christ's flesh was in the loins of the Fathers as medicine, as Augustine says in the tenth book *On Genesis according to the Letter*[^4], whereas the flesh of other men was as something needing medicine: therefore it seems that it had one quality and another.

4. Likewise, that which is totally corrupted is not capable of being cured: therefore if Adam was capable of being cured, some particle remained in him uncorrupted. But from that particle, which[^5] was without corruption, it was most fitting that flesh be generated which should be without original stain: therefore it seems so, etc.

**Sed contra:** 1. Augustine in the tenth book *On Genesis according to the Letter*[^6]: « For the same flesh, not of Abraham only, but of the first earthly man himself, had at once the *wound* of transgression and the *medicament of the wound* » : therefore if the *medicament* of the wound pertains to Christ, the *wound* of transgression pertains to others, therefore the flesh of Christ in the first parents was the same as the flesh of others: therefore it was not distinct from others.

2. Likewise, the Master at the beginning of this distinction[^7], speaking of Christ's flesh: « It must be believed, according to the agreement of the holy Doctors, that it was first subject to sin, just as was the rest of the Virgin's flesh » : it therefore remains as before, that it was not distinct from others.

3. Likewise, whatever is conveyed by the law of corruption is corrupted; but the whole flesh of Isaac, which was conveyed from Abraham, was corrupted: therefore either[^8] Christ's flesh was not conveyed from Abraham into Isaac, or, if it was conveyed, it was not distinct from other men.

4. Likewise, either Christ's flesh was like the rest of the flesh of Abraham, or not. If like, I have my point; if unlike: then it does not seem that such flesh was suited to the redemption of the human race[^9]. If therefore this is false and unfitting, it remains, then, that Christ's flesh was not distinct from the flesh of other men.

5. Likewise, distinction according to opposite qualities presupposes distinction according to substance, because opposite qualities cannot be in the same thing and according to the same[^10]; but the flesh of the sons in the first parents could not have a distinction according to substance, because there would not be found as many atoms in the body of Adam as there were sons procreated from it: therefore if Christ's flesh in the first parents could not
be distinguished from the flesh of others according to *substance*, it seems so, etc.

6. Likewise, if pure flesh was transfused, it was either *corruptible* or *incorruptible*. If *incorruptible*: then Christ's body ought to have been impassible; which is false. If *corruptible*: then it seems that it could be transmuted into the rest of the flesh and increased by other flesh: therefore other flesh too could by parity of reasoning be transmuted into others[^11]: therefore either that flesh was consumed before it reached Christ, or it was so multiplied that a thousand bodies could be formed from it. But this is false: therefore, etc.

### Conclusio

> Christ's flesh, in the loins of the parents in which it was materially, was not distinct in quality or in form from the flesh of other men.

**Respondeo:** For the understanding of the foregoing it must be noted that concerning this question there was a certain erroneous position of certain men saying that Christ's flesh had a distinction from the flesh of other men not only when it was united to Christ's soul[^12], but even when it was in the loins of the first man. For they said that, because Adam sinned reparably, in his sin God did not permit the whole to be corrupted; and so there remained in his seed some uncorrupted portion, that through it there might be a medicament for the rest of the flesh; and from that little portion[^13] they say Christ's flesh was formed, which they say is the medicine of the human race. And this particle Adam transmitted to Noah, and Noah to Abraham, and Abraham to David, and David down to the Virgin, of whom Christ was born. — But this position, although it may seem to have probability on the surface, inwardly contains error[^14]; because it is not only against the truthful sayings of the Saints, but also against the *documents of the true faith*. It is repugnant to *the authorities of the Saints*: because Damascene, the Greek doctor, and Augustine, the chief Latin doctor, agree in this, that Christ's flesh before its union with the Word was undistinguished from the rest of the Virgin's flesh. Damascene[^15] says that « it was formed from the most pure blood of the Virgin » ; Augustine says that « he assumed the first-fruits of our mass ». — And this indeed it is necessary to hold, because the Virgin's sanctification would not have been necessary in order that Christ's flesh be formed without any sin. — Nor would Christ truly be of the seed of David and Abraham, but only of Adam; since, if in the aforesaid way that[^16] particle had descended, it would pertain to none of the others except as a conduit, just as water is compared to a channel. — Nor would there be in such flesh a true assumption of passibility nor a true repair of our infirmity, because he would not have fashioned flesh for himself from fallen nature, but rather from constituted nature. — All which things are unfitting and adverse to *sound faith*. And therefore the aforesaid position must be rejected as erroneous, just as the Master well intimates in the text[^17], and Master Hugh in the book *On the Sacraments*.

It is therefore plain according to Catholic doctrine that Christ's flesh, in the loins of the parents in which it was materially, was not distinct in quality, or in form, from the flesh of other men, although, after it was united to the Word, it has a distinction, in that it is not *the flesh of sin*, but *like sinful flesh*. And for this reason Augustine says[^18] that « Christ's flesh came from the height of human nature », not that in the first parents it was without pollution, but because it was so purified and cleansed in its assumption that it was like the flesh in the state of innocence. On account of which it is said by Boethius[^19] to have « received something of every state », as will be better shown below<!-- page 87 -->. — Hence the reasonings which are brought forward for this part are to be granted.

*Ad 1.* To that which is first objected to the contrary from *Wisdom* 7, that nothing defiled enters into her; it must be said that it is true; for this is understood of the *divine* Wisdom[^20] according to the *divine nature*, which is in no way defiled by any creature; nevertheless, if it be also understood according to the *human nature*, it still has no instance against us, because Christ's flesh, from the time it was united to the Word of God, was holy and immaculate. But from this it does not follow that it was immaculate in the first parents, because, although in the loins of the parents it was unitable, it was nevertheless in a remote disposition.

*Ad 2.* To that which is objected, that the greatest purity ought to be in Christ's flesh; it must be said that it is true, according as it is suited to the repair of the human race. Understanding it thus, no greater purity can be conceived than that from the sinful mass flesh be formed altogether free from sin; and so it was done in the flesh united to the Word, by the working of the Holy Spirit. But if that flesh be understood never to have been subject to sin in itself or in the parents[^21]; it is not thereby conceived how it was apt for our repair; and therefore it is not conceived how and as it ought, but rather it is assigned how it ought not.

*Ad 3.* To that which is objected, that Christ's flesh is medicine; it must be said that it is true for the time at which it is medicine; and it is medicine for the time at which it was assumed by the Word[^22]; and thus it is of a different quality than that of other men. But for the time at which it was before the assumption, it was not medicine, except materially speaking. According to that time it need not be formally distinguished from other flesh, because from the same material principle there can be formed what is healthy and what is sick. For food of the same kind is converted in a healthy man into healthy flesh and in a leper into corrupt flesh.

*Ad 4.* To that which is objected, that if the whole were corrupted, his disease would no longer be curable: it must be said that that argument fails in two ways. First, because, even if the whole man were corrupted, it does not follow that he was incurable, unless that corruption inseparably adhered to him. Furthermore, even if the whole man were corrupted as to the things that were in him, yet the whole human race, which is fashioned from that man's flesh, would not be corrupted; but only that which proceeded according to the law of concupiscence. And since Christ's flesh was fashioned in the Virgin apart from the law of concupiscence by the sanctifying Holy Spirit[^23], hence it is that it lacked corruption and held the account of medicine from its primary formation.

### Scholion

**I.** This and the following question is also treated by St. Thomas (*Summa* III q. 31 a. 6, 7; cf. *Commentary* here q. 1 a. 1, 2) in two questions, but posed somewhat differently, of which the first is whether the body of Christ in the ancient Fathers was according to something signed; the second, whether Christ's flesh in the Fathers was subject to sin. The Scholastics commonly reprove as erroneous that figment, scattered abroad by certain ancients and also by more recent writers, that in Adam, fallen into sin, some part of the flesh was not infected by sin and was conveyed uncorrupted through the subsequent generations — which is here more fully explained in the body. Hugh of St. Victor relates more on this opinion, *On the Sacraments* II p. I c. 8.

**II.** Excepting St. Thomas (locc. cit.), Bl. Albert, and Giles of Rome, the others cited below settle this and the following question in a single question: Alexander of Hales, *Summa* p. III q. 9 m. 3 a. 4. — Scotus, here q. 2; *Reportata* II d. 33 q. unica n. 11. — Bl. Albert, here a. 1, 27. — Peter of Tarentaise, here q. 2 a. 1. — Richard of Mediavilla, here a. 3 q. 1. — Giles of Rome, here q. 4 a. 1 dub. lat. 2. — Denis the Carthusian, here q. 5. — Biel, here q. 2.
