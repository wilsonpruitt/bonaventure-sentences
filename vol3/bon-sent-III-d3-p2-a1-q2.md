# III Sent., d. 3, p. 2, a. 1, q. 2
## *Utrum Christus in Abraham fuerit decimatus*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 83 -->

### Quaestio II. *Utrum Christus in Abraham fuerit decimatus.*

Secundo quaeritur, utrum Christus in Abraham fuerit decimatus. Et quod sic, videtur:

1. Auctoritate Apostoli ad Hebraeos septimo[^1]: per Abraham et Levi decimatus est; adhuc in lumbis patris erat, quando obviavit ei Melchisedech; sed Christus fuit in lumbis Abrahae, quando decimas illas dedit: ergo fuit decimatus, sicut et Levi.

2. Item, sicut dicit Hugo de sancto Victore[^2]: « Per decimas significatur ipse Christus »: ergo videtur, quod ipsa decimatio, in quantum est actus figuralis, principalius respiciat Christum quam alios filios Abrahae: ergo magis videtur, quod debeat dici decimatus quam alii.

3. Item, nihil plus requiritur ad decimationem, nisi quod illud realiter impleatur in prole, quod significatum fuit in decimatione Abrahae; sed hoc fuit maxime impletum in Christo, qui se ipsum obtulit hostiam Deo et Patri per oblationem corporis et sanguinis sui[^3].

4. Item, tota caro Christi fuit in carne beatae Virginis; sed caro Virginis[^4] habuit decimari in Abraham: ergo et caro Christi.

**Sed contra:**
1. Augustinus, decimo super Genesim ad litteram[^5]: « Ille non est decimatus, cuius caro non fervorem vulneris, sed materiam medicaminis traxit »; Christus autem fuit huiusmodi: ergo etc.

2. Item, sicut se habet Adam peccans ad posteros, ita Abraham in dando decimas; sed quamvis Christus esset in Adam peccante, non dicitur Christus peccasse in Adam; ergo pari ratione in Abraham dante decimas non dicitur decimatus[^6].

3. Item, decimatio vel erat curatio, vel curationis praefiguratio; sed Christus nec ibi curabatur nec curari praefigurabatur: ergo videtur, quod ibi decimatus non fuerit[^7].

4. Item, si Christus decimatus fuit in lumbis Abrahae, sicut et Levi, ergo nulla est argumentatio Apostoli[^8], qua probat, maius esse sacerdotium Melchisedech sacerdotio levitico, per hoc quod Levi decimatus fuit: quodsi ratiocinatio Apostoli recta est, restat igitur, quod Christus non decimatus fuit in Abraham.

Est igitur quaestio iuxta hoc: unde tenet illa consequentia, qua probat Apostolus, maius esse sacerdotium Melchisedech sacerdotio levitico, pro eo quod Levi[^9] obtulit decimas Melchisedech? Videtur, quod non cogat, quia secundum illam rationem posset argui, quod maior est dignitas unius sacerdotis pauperis quam Papae, esto quod pater Papae dederit decimas simplici sacerdoti.

### Conclusio

> Christus in lumbis Abrahae non fuit decimatus.

**Respondeo:** Dicendum, quod sicut determinat Magister in littera[^10], immo Augustinus super Genesim ad litteram decimo, Christus in lumbis Abrahae non fuit decimatus.

Et ratio huius est, quia decimatio praefigurabat futuram curationem. Quoniam igitur Christus in lumbis Abrahae non erat sicut curatione indigens, sed potius sicut medicina; hinc est, quod decimatio ad ipsum non pertinebat. Sicut enim in Adam illi soli peccaverunt, qui secundum carnalem generationem et concupiscentiam seminalem ab ipso procedunt; et ideo ab illa generalitate excluditur Christus, qua dicitur: Omnes in Adam peccaverunt[^11], quia non processit ab ipso secundum libidinis ardorem, sed secundum Spiritus sancti operationem: sic in proposito intelligendum est de decimatione.

<!-- page 84 -->

1. Et per hoc patet responsio ad illud quod primo obiicitur in contrarium; quoniam Christus in lumbis Abrahae fuit et Levi[^12], sed non eodem modo. Et propterea non sequitur, quod sicut uni competit decimari, ita conveniat alteri. Decimatio autem non tantum respicit *esse in lumbis*, sed etiam *modum essendi* secundum traductionem originalis peccati.

2. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod Christus significatur per decimam oblatam; dicendum, quod decima oblata non decimabatur, sed decimatio potius respicit illud quod remanet in alio, sicut dicit Hugo[^13], quia remanet imperfectio ablata unitate, quae est perfectio denarii, per quod significatur futura curatio. Unde sicut alii figurantur in illa decimatione *curari*, sic Christus figuratur esse *ratio medicamenti*.

3. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod in illa decimatione significabatur oblatio Christi; dicendum, quod verum est, sed non significatur Christi oblatio, tanquam *pro quo* fieret oblatio[^14], sed tanquam *qui* offertur pro aliis. Et quoniam *decimari* dicitur ille, pro quo offertur oblatio decimae; hinc est, quod actus decimationis non debet Christo attribui; aliter enim praefigurabatur Christus, aliter alii.

4. Ad illud quod obiicitur, quod caro Christi fuit in beata Virgine, et caro Virginis in Abraham; dicendum, quod ibi est sophisma *secundum accidens*[^15], quia variatur modus essendi *hinc inde*. Alio enim modo fuit caro beatae Virginis in Abraham, aliter caro Christi in Matre sua; quia beata Virgo processit secundum legem concupiscentiae, Christus vero minime.

Ad illud quod quaerebatur de auctoritate Apostoli et ratione, dici potest, quod ratio Apostoli bona est ad illos, contra quos arguit, scilicet Iudaeos, qui supponebant, Abraham digniorem esse in suo genere. Si ergo Abraham benedictus fuit a Melchisedech et ei decimas obtulit tanquam superiori, sequitur ex hoc, quod non tantum maior fuit Melchisedech Abraham, sed etiam maior omnibus, qui processerunt secundum legem carnis ab eo; et ideo dignior fuit Levi et Aaron.

Sed quoniam illud non videtur esse verum, quod excellentior fuerit Melchisedech quam Moyses et David et beata Virgo, qui processerunt ex Abraham; et Apostolus non procedit ex falsis suppositionibus: ideo oportet aliter dicere, videlicet quod Apostolus arguit ex veritate et significatione illius figurae necessaria argumentatione. Decimatio[^16] pro illo tempore significabat curationem. Cum ergo Abraham obtulit decimas Melchisedech et ab eo benedictus est, significavit, se indigere curatione et benedictione per illum sacerdotem, qui significabatur per Melchisedech. Sed ille qui curat et benedicit, maior est absque ulla contradictione. Cum ergo sacerdotium Christi fuerit illud, quod praefigurabatur per illud Melchisedech, sicut in Psalmo[^17] legitur: *Tu es sacerdos in aeternum secundum ordinem Melchisedech*; restat igitur, quod maius fuit sacerdotium Christi quam Levi. Et haec est ratio Apostoli, quam impossibile est dissolvi. — Nec est simile de sacerdote simplici, et de patre Papae, quia ibi nulla est ratio figurae. Haec autem tota ratio fundatur supra decimationem, secundum quod est actus figuralis; nam secundum quod est actus moralis, nihil facit ad propositum[^18], quia nec Abraham nec eius posteri obligati fuerunt ad dandas decimas Melchisedech, maxime illi qui fuerunt de stirpe Levi, ad nullas decimas dandas fuerunt astricti[^19].

## English

### Question II. *Whether Christ was tithed in Abraham.*

Secondly it is asked whether Christ was tithed in Abraham. And that he was, it seems:

1. By the authority of the Apostle to the Hebrews, chapter seven[^1]: through Abraham Levi too was tithed; he was still in the loins of his father when Melchizedek met him; but Christ was in the loins of Abraham when he gave those tithes: therefore he was tithed, just as Levi was.

2. Likewise, as Hugh of Saint Victor says[^2]: « By the tithes Christ himself is signified »: therefore it seems that the tithing itself, insofar as it is a figural act, regards Christ more principally than the other sons of Abraham: therefore it seems rather that he ought to be said to be tithed than the others.

3. Likewise, nothing more is required for a tithing than that there be really fulfilled in the offspring that which was signified in Abraham's tithing; but this was fulfilled most of all in Christ, who offered himself as a victim to God and the Father through the offering of his body and blood[^3].

4. Likewise, the whole flesh of Christ was in the flesh of the blessed Virgin; but the flesh of the Virgin[^4] had to be tithed in Abraham: therefore so also the flesh of Christ.

**Sed contra:**
1. Augustine, in the tenth book *On Genesis according to the Letter*[^5]: « He is not tithed whose flesh drew from there not the ferment of a wound, but the matter of a remedy »; but Christ was of this kind: therefore etc.

2. Likewise, as sinning Adam stands to his posterity, so does Abraham in giving tithes; but although Christ was in sinning Adam, Christ is not said to have sinned in Adam; therefore by parity of reasoning, in Abraham's giving tithes he is not said to be tithed[^6].

3. Likewise, the tithing was either a healing or a prefiguration of healing; but Christ was neither healed there nor prefigured to be healed: therefore it seems that he was not tithed there[^7].

4. Likewise, if Christ was tithed in the loins of Abraham, just as Levi was, then the Apostle's argument is null[^8], by which he proves the priesthood of Melchizedek to be greater than the levitical priesthood through the fact that Levi was tithed: but if the Apostle's reasoning is correct, it follows therefore that Christ was not tithed in Abraham.

There is, then, a question alongside this: on what does that consequence rest, by which the Apostle proves the priesthood of Melchizedek to be greater than the levitical priesthood, for the reason that Levi[^9] offered tithes to Melchizedek? It seems that it does not compel, because by that reasoning it could be argued that the dignity of one poor priest is greater than the Pope's, granted that the Pope's father gave tithes to a simple priest.

### Conclusio

> Christ was not tithed in the loins of Abraham.

**Respondeo:** It must be said that, as the Master determines in the text[^10]—indeed Augustine in the tenth book *On Genesis according to the Letter*—Christ was not tithed in the loins of Abraham.

And the reason for this is that the tithing prefigured the future healing. Since therefore Christ in the loins of Abraham was not as one needing healing, but rather as the medicine; hence it is that the tithing did not pertain to him. For just as in Adam those alone sinned who proceed from him according to carnal generation and seminal concupiscence; and therefore Christ is excluded from that generality by which it is said: All sinned in Adam[^11], because he did not proceed from him according to the ardor of lust, but according to the operation of the Holy Spirit: so in the matter at hand it is to be understood concerning the tithing.

1. And by this the reply is clear to that which is first objected to the contrary; for Christ was in the loins of Abraham, and so was Levi[^12], but not in the same way. And therefore it does not follow that, as it belongs to one to be tithed, so it should fit the other. For the tithing regards not only *being in the loins*, but also the *mode of being* according to the transmission of original sin.

2. To that which is objected, that Christ is signified by the offered tithe; it must be said that the offered tithe was not tithed, but rather the tithing regards that which remains in another, as Hugh says[^13], since there remains the imperfection removed by the unity, which is the perfection of the denarius, through which the future healing is signified. Hence, just as others are figured in that tithing as *being healed*, so Christ is figured as *the ground of the remedy*.

3. To that which is objected, that in that tithing the offering of Christ was signified; it must be said that this is true, but Christ's offering is not signified as one *for whom* the offering would be made[^14], but as one *who* is offered for others. And since he is said to be *tithed* for whom the offering of the tithe is made; hence it is that the act of tithing ought not to be attributed to Christ; for Christ was prefigured in one way, the others in another.

4. To that which is objected, that the flesh of Christ was in the blessed Virgin, and the flesh of the Virgin in Abraham; it must be said that there is a sophism *of accident*[^15] there, because the mode of being is varied *on this side and that*. For in one way the flesh of the blessed Virgin was in Abraham, in another the flesh of Christ in his Mother; because the blessed Virgin proceeded according to the law of concupiscence, but Christ in no way.

To that which was asked concerning the authority of the Apostle and the reasoning, it can be said that the Apostle's reasoning is good against those against whom he argues, namely the Jews, who supposed Abraham to be more worthy in his own kind. If, then, Abraham was blessed by Melchizedek and offered tithes to him as to a superior, it follows from this that not only was Melchizedek greater than Abraham, but also greater than all who proceeded from him according to the law of the flesh; and therefore he was more worthy than Levi and Aaron.

But since that does not seem to be true—that Melchizedek was more excellent than Moses and David and the blessed Virgin, who proceeded from Abraham; and since the Apostle does not proceed from false suppositions: therefore it is necessary to speak otherwise, namely that the Apostle argues from the truth and signification of that figure by a necessary argumentation. The tithing[^16] for that time signified healing. When therefore Abraham offered tithes to Melchizedek and was blessed by him, he signified that he needed healing and blessing through that priest who was signified by Melchizedek. But he who heals and blesses is the greater, without any contradiction. Since therefore the priesthood of Christ was that which was prefigured by that of Melchizedek, as is read in the Psalm[^17]: *Thou art a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek*; it follows therefore that the priesthood of Christ was greater than Levi's. And this is the Apostle's reasoning, which it is impossible to dissolve. — Nor is it similar in the case of a simple priest and the Pope's father, because there there is no ground of figure. But this whole reasoning is founded upon the tithing, according as it is a figural act; for according as it is a moral act, it contributes nothing to the matter at hand[^18], since neither Abraham nor his posterity were obliged to give tithes to Melchizedek, especially those who were of the stock of Levi, who were bound to give no tithes[^19].
