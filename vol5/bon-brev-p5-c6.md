# Breviloquium, Pars V, Cap. 6

## *De ramificatione gratiae in habitus beatitudinum, et per consequens fructuum et sensuum*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 258 -->

### Capitulum VI. *De ramificatione gratiae in habitus beatitudinum, et per consequens fructuum et sensuum.*

De ramificatione autem gratiae in habitus *beatitudinum* haec tenenda sunt, quod septem sunt *beatitudines*, quas Salvator enumerat in sermone de monte[^p258-5] scilicet *paupertas spiritus, mititas, luctus, esuries iustitiae, misericordia, munditia cordis* et *pax*. — Ad has beatitudines propter sui perfectionem et plenitudinem duodecim *fructus Spiritus* et quinque *sensus spirituales* consequuntur; qui non dicunt novos *habitus*, sed *status* delectationum et *usus* spiritualium speculationum, quibus replentur et consolantur spiritus virorum iustorum.

Ratio autem ad intelligentiam praedictorum haec est: quia, cum reparativum principium sit perfectissimum et perfectissime reparativum et reformativum per donum gratuitum; ideo gratiae donum ab ipso manans liberaliter et abunde ramificari debet usque ad habitus perfectionum, qui, cum fini approximent, recto vocabulo nuncupantur ex nomine *beatitudinum*; quarum *sufficientia, numerus* et *ordo* colligitur ex *integritate perfectionis*, ex *modis perfectionum* et ex *dispositionibus ad perfectionem*.

Primo igitur ad *integritatem perfectionis* requiritur necessario perfectus *recessus a malo*, perfectus *processus in bono* et perfectus *status in optimo*. Quoniam autem *malum* aut procedit ex tumore superbiae, aut ex rancore malitiae, aut ex languore concupiscentiae[^p258-6]; ideo ad perfecte elongandum ab hoc triplici genere mali tres sunt necessariae beatitudines, scilicet *paupertas spiritus* elongans a malo tumoris, *mititas* elongans a malo rancoris, et *luctus* elongans a malo libidinis et languoris concupiscentialis. — Quia vero perfectus *processus in bono* attenditur secundum divinam imitationem; et *universae viae Domini misericordia et veritas*[^p258-7]: hinc est,

<!-- page 259 -->
quod duplex est beatitudo secundum has duas vias, scilicet *esuries* sive zelus *iustitiae*, et *affectus misericordiae*. — Quoniam autem *status in optimo* est vel per limpidam cognitionem, vel per tranquillam affectionem; hinc est, quod sunt duae ultimae beatitudines, scilicet *munditia cordis* ad Deum videndum et *pax mentis* ad perfecte fruendum.

Secundo etiam, si *modi perfectionum* attendantur, debent esse septem habitus beatitudinum. Nam est perfectio *religionis, praelationis* et *internae sanctitudinis*[^p259-1]. Ad perfectionem autem *religionis* necessario requiritur abdicatio boni privati, acceptatio boni fraterni et appetitio boni aeterni; primum fit per *paupertatem spiritus*, secundum per *mititatem affectus*, tertium per *amaritudinem luctus*. — Ad perfectionem vero *praelationis* duo necessario requiruntur, scilicet *zelus iustitiae* et *affectus misericordiae*; *misericordia* enim *et veritas custodiunt regem*[^p259-2]. Secundum haec duo disponi debet regimen praelationis in Ecclesia militante. — Ad perfectionem autem *internae sanctitudinis* necessario requiritur *puritas conscientiae* et tranquillitas totius animae per *pacem Dei* omnem humanum sensum exsuperantem.

Tertio, si attendantur *dispositiones praeambulae*, septem debent esse beatitudines. *Timor* enim facit recedere a malo et a mali occasione; et quia *radix omnium malorum est cupiditas*[^p259-3]: ideo *timor* disponit ad *spiritus paupertatem*, in qua simul iungitur humilitas cum paupertate, ut sic elongetur vir perfectus a fonte omnis culpae, scilicet a superbia et cupiditate. Et hinc est, quod *paupertas spiritus* est fundamentum totius perfectionis evangelicae. Unde primo oportet hoc fundamentum sternere qui vult ad culmen perfectionis venire, secundum illud Matthaei decimo nono[^p259-4]: *Si vis perfectus esse, vade et vende omnia, quae habes*; ecce, perfecta *paupertas*, quae nihil sibi prorsus retinet; *et sequere me*; ecce, *humilitas*, quae facit, quod homo abnegando se ipsum tollat crucem suam et sequatur Christum, qui est totius perfectionis principalissimum fundamentum. Disponit ergo timor ad spiritus paupertatem. — *Pietas* autem ad *mititatem*; nam qui pie ad aliquem afficitur nec illum irritat nec ab illo irritatur. — *Scientia* disponit ad *luctum*, quoniam per scientiam cognoscimus nos a statu beatitudinis relegatos in hanc vallem miseriae et lacrymarum. — *Fortitudo* autem disponit ad *esuriem iustitiae*; qui enim fortis est tam avide se tenet cum iustitia, ut malit a vita corporali quam a iustitia separari. — *Consilium* disponit ad *misericordiam*; nihil enim Deus magis consulit in Scriptura quam facere misericordiam, quod super omnia reputat holocausta[^p259-5]. — *Intellectus* disponit ad *cordis munditiam*; nam speculatio veritatis mundat cor nostrum ab omnibus phantasiis. — *Sapientia* disponit ad *pacem*; nam sapientia iungit nos summo vero et bono, in quo est finis et tranquillitas totius nostri rationalis appetitus.

Qua pace adepta, necessario sequitur superabundans delectatio spiritualis, quae in *duodenario fructuum* continetur ad insinuandam *superabundantiam* delectationum. Est enim duodenarius numerus *abundans*[^p259-6], in quo insinuatur spiritualium charismatum exuberantia, quibus fruitur et delectatur anima sancta; et tunc est homo ad contemplationem idoneus et ad aspectus et amplexus sponsi et sponsae, qui fieri habent secundum *sensus spirituales*, quibus *videtur* Christi sponsi summa pulcritudo sub ratione *Splendoris*; *auditur* summa harmonia sub ratione *Verbi*; *gustatur* summa dulcedo sub ratione *Sapientiae* comprehendentis utrumque, Verbum scilicet et Splendorem; *odoratur* summa fragrantia sub ratione *Verbi inspirati* in corde; *astringitur* summa suavitas sub ratione *Verbi incarnati*, inter nos habitantis[^p259-7] corporaliter et reddentis se nobis palpabile, osculabile, amplexabile per ardentissimam caritatem, quae mentem nostram per ecstasim et raptum transire facit *ex hoc mundo ad Patrem*.

Ex dictis igitur manifeste colligitur, quod habitus *virtutum* ad exercitium principaliter disponunt activae; habitus vero *donorum* ad otium[^p259-8] contemplativae; habitus autem *beatitudinum* ad perfectionem utriusque. *Fructus* vero *Spiritus*, qui sunt *caritas, gaudium, pax, patientia, longanimitas, bonitas, benignitas, mansuetudo, fides, modestia, continentia, castitas*[^p259-9], dicunt delectationes consequentes

<!-- page 260 -->
opera perfecta. *Sensus* vero *spirituales* dicunt perceptiones mentales circa veritatem contemplandam. Quae quidem contemplatio in Prophetis fuit per *revelationem* quantum ad triplicem visionem, scilicet corporalem, imaginativam et intellectualem[^p260-1]; in aliis vero iustis reperitur per *speculationem*, quae incipit a sensu et pervenit ad imaginationem et de imaginatione ad rationem, de ratione ad intellectum, de intellectu ad intelligentiam; de intelligentia vero ad sapientiam sive notitiam excessivam, quae hic in via incipit, sed consummatur in gloria sempiterna.

Et in his gradibus consistit *scala Iacob, cuius cacumen attingit caelum*[^p260-2]; et *thronus Salomonis*, in quo residet Rex sapientissimus et vere pacificus et amorosus ut sponsus speciosissimus *et desiderabilis totus*; *in quem desiderant Angeli prospicere*, et ad quem suspirat desiderium sanctarum animarum, sicut *cervus desiderat ad fontes aquarum*. Quo quidem desiderio ferventissimo ad modum ignis spiritus noster non solum efficitur agilis ad ascensum, verum etiam quadam *ignorantia docta* supra se ipsum rapitur in caliginem et excessum, ut non solum cum sponsa dicat: *In odorem unguentorum tuorum curremus*[^p260-3], verum etiam cum Propheta psallat: *Et nox illuminatio mea in deliciis meis.* Quam nocturnam et deliciosam illuminationem nemo novit nisi qui probat, nemo autem probat nisi per gratiam divinitus datam, nemini datur, nisi ei qui se exercet ad illam; ideo deinceps consideranda sunt exercitia meritorum.

## English

### Chapter VI. *On the branching of grace into the habits of the beatitudes, and consequently of the fruits and the senses.*

Concerning the branching of grace into the habits of the *beatitudes*, these things are to be held: that the *beatitudes* are seven, which the Savior enumerates in the sermon on the mount[^p258-5] — namely, *poverty of spirit, meekness, mourning, hunger for justice, mercy, cleanness of heart* and *peace*. — Upon these beatitudes, by reason of their perfection and fulness, there follow twelve *fruits of the Spirit* and five *spiritual senses*; which do not name new *habits*, but *states* of delight and *uses* of spiritual contemplations, by which the spirits of just men are filled and consoled.

Now the reason for the understanding of the foresaid is this: since the reparative principle is most perfect and most perfectly reparative and reformative through the gratuitous gift, therefore the gift of grace flowing from it liberally and abundantly must branch out even to the habits of the perfections, which, because they draw near to the end, are by a right name called after the name of the *beatitudes*; and their *sufficiency, number* and *order* are gathered from the *integrity of perfection*, from the *modes of the perfections* and from the *dispositions toward perfection*.

First, then, for the *integrity of perfection* there is necessarily required a perfect *withdrawal from evil*, a perfect *advance in good* and a perfect *rest in what is best*. Now since *evil* proceeds either from the swelling of pride, or from the rancor of malice, or from the languor of concupiscence[^p258-6], therefore, for a perfect removal from this threefold kind of evil, three beatitudes are necessary: namely, *poverty of spirit*, which removes from the evil of swelling; *meekness*, which removes from the evil of rancor; and *mourning*, which removes from the evil of lust and of the languor belonging to concupiscence. — But because a perfect *advance in good* is measured according to the imitation of God, and *all the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth*[^p258-7], hence it is

<!-- page 259 -->
that the beatitude answering to these two ways is twofold: namely, *hunger*, or zeal, for *justice*, and *the affection of mercy*. — And since *rest in what is best* is either through limpid knowledge or through tranquil affection, hence it is that there are two final beatitudes: namely, *cleanness of heart* for seeing God, and *peace of mind* for perfectly enjoying him.

Second, likewise, if the *modes of the perfections* be considered, the habits of the beatitudes must be seven. For there is a perfection of *religious life*, of *prelacy* and of *interior holiness*[^p259-1]. Now for the perfection of *religious life* there is necessarily required the renunciation of private good, the acceptance of a brother's good and the desire of the eternal good; the first is done through *poverty of spirit*, the second through *meekness of affection*, the third through *the bitterness of mourning*. — For the perfection of *prelacy*, on the other hand, two things are necessarily required: namely, *zeal for justice* and *the affection of mercy*; for *mercy and truth preserve the king*[^p259-2]. According to these two the government of prelacy in the Church militant must be ordered. — And for the perfection of *interior holiness* there is necessarily required *purity of conscience* and the tranquillity of the whole soul through *the peace of God* which surpasses all human understanding.

Third, if the *preambular dispositions* be considered, the beatitudes must be seven. For *fear* makes one withdraw from evil and from the occasion of evil; and because *the root of all evils is covetousness*[^p259-3], therefore *fear* disposes toward *poverty of spirit*, in which humility is joined together with poverty, so that the perfect man may thus be removed from the fount of all fault, namely from pride and covetousness. And hence it is that *poverty of spirit* is the foundation of the whole of evangelical perfection. Whence he who wishes to come to the summit of perfection must first lay this foundation, according to that word of Matthew, chapter nineteen[^p259-4]: *If thou wilt be perfect, go and sell all that thou hast* — behold, perfect *poverty*, which retains nothing at all for itself — *and follow me*: behold, *humility*, which brings it about that a man, denying himself, takes up his cross and follows Christ, who is the most principal foundation of the whole of perfection. Fear therefore disposes toward poverty of spirit. — *Piety*, in turn, toward *meekness*; for one who is affectionately disposed toward another neither provokes him nor is provoked by him. — *Knowledge* disposes toward *mourning*, since through knowledge we recognize that we have been banished from the state of blessedness into this valley of misery and of tears. — *Fortitude*, in turn, disposes toward *hunger for justice*; for one who is brave holds to justice so eagerly that he would rather be parted from bodily life than from justice. — *Counsel* disposes toward *mercy*; for God counsels nothing in Scripture more than the doing of mercy, which he accounts above all holocausts[^p259-5]. — *Understanding* disposes toward *cleanness of heart*; for the contemplation of truth cleanses our heart of all phantasms. — *Wisdom* disposes toward *peace*; for wisdom joins us to the highest true and good, in which is the end and the tranquillity of our whole rational appetite.

And when this peace has been attained, there necessarily follows a superabundant spiritual delight, which is contained in the *twelvefold number of the fruits*, to signify the *superabundance* of the delights. For twelve is an *abundant* number[^p259-6], in which is signified the exuberance of the spiritual charisms which the holy soul enjoys and delights in; and then a man is fit for contemplation and for the beholding and the embraces of the bridegroom and the bride, which have to come about according to the *spiritual senses*, by which is *seen* the supreme beauty of Christ the bridegroom under the aspect of *Splendor*; is *heard* the supreme harmony under the aspect of *the Word*; is *tasted* the supreme sweetness under the aspect of *Wisdom*, which comprehends both, namely the Word and the Splendor; is *smelled* the supreme fragrance under the aspect of *the Word inspired* in the heart; is *touched* the supreme suavity under the aspect of *the Word incarnate*, dwelling among us[^p259-7] bodily and rendering himself to us palpable, kissable, embraceable by that most ardent charity which makes our mind pass over, through ecstasy and rapture, *out of this world to the Father*.

From the things said, therefore, it is manifestly gathered that the habits of the *virtues* dispose principally toward the exercise of the active life; the habits of the *gifts*, toward the leisure[^p259-8] of the contemplative; and the habits of the *beatitudes*, toward the perfection of both. But the *fruits of the Spirit*, which are *charity, joy, peace, patience, longanimity, goodness, benignity, mildness, faith, modesty, continence, chastity*[^p259-9], name the delights that follow upon

<!-- page 260 -->
perfect works. And the *spiritual senses* name the mental perceptions concerning the truth to be contemplated. Which contemplation was in the Prophets through *revelation*, as regards a threefold vision — namely the corporeal, the imaginative and the intellectual[^p260-1]; while in the other just it is found through *speculation*, which begins from sense and comes through to imagination, and from imagination to reason, from reason to intellect, from intellect to intelligence; and from intelligence to wisdom, or knowledge that exceeds, which begins here on the way but is consummated in everlasting glory.

And in these degrees consists *Jacob's ladder, whose summit touches heaven*[^p260-2]; and *the throne of Solomon*, on which sits the King most wise and truly peaceful and loving, as a bridegroom most beautiful *and altogether desirable*; *upon whom the Angels desire to look*, and toward whom the longing of holy souls sighs, *as the hart longs for the fountains of waters*. And by this most fervent longing, after the manner of fire, our spirit is not only made agile for the ascent, but by a certain *learned ignorance* is caught up above itself into darkness and excess, so that it not only says with the bride: *In the odor of thy ointments we will run*[^p260-3], but also sings with the Prophet: *And the night is my illumination in my delights.* And that nocturnal and delightful illumination no one knows save him who has tasted it, and no one tastes it save through grace divinely given, and it is given to none save to him who exercises himself toward it; therefore the exercises of merits are next to be considered.
