## Latin

### Capitulum VII. *De exercitio gratiae respectu credendorum.*

Quarto igitur restat considerare gratiam quantum ad *exercitia meritorum*. Et circa haec consideranda sunt quatuor. Primo, de exercitatione gratiae in *credendis*, cuiusmodi sunt articuli fidei; secundo, in *diligendis*, cuiusmodi sunt illa quae spectant ad ordinem diligendi; tertio, in *exsequendis*, cuiusmodi sunt praecepta legis divinae; quarto in *postulandis*, cuiusmodi sunt petitiones orationis dominicae[^p260-4].

De *articulis* autem *fidei* haec tenenda sunt, quod licet per fidem astringamur credere plurima, quae sunt supra rationem, et *generaliter* omnia, quae continentur et afferuntur in canone sacrae Scripturae; *specialiter* tamen et proprie articuli fidei dicuntur illi qui continentur in serie Symboli apostolici; qui uno modo sunt *duodecim*, si habeatur respectus ad eos, qui Symbolum ediderunt, alio modo *quatuordecim*, si consideremus, quae radicaliter credenda sunt tanquam omnium credendorum fundamenta[^p260-5].

Ratio autem ad intelligentiam praedictorum haec est: quia, cum primum principium in se ipso sit summe *verum* et *bonum*, in opere vero suo sit summe *iustum* et *misericors*; et summe *vero* debeatur firma assensio, summe *bono* fervens dilectio, summe *iusto* universalis[^p260-6] subiectio, summe *misericordi* fiducialis invocatio; et gratia sit ordinativa mentis nostrae ad primi principii culturam debitam: hinc est, quod ipsa dirigit et regulat ad debita et meritoria exercitia in *credendis, amandis, exsequendis* et *postulandis*, secundum quod requirit summa veritas, bonitas, iustitia et misericordia in Trinitate beata.

Quoniam igitur *veritati* est credendum, et maiori veritati magis credendum, et summae veritati per consequens summe credendum; et veritas primi principii in infinitum maior est omni veritate creata et luminosior omni lumine intellectus nostri: hinc est, quod ad hoc, quod intellectus noster recte sit ordinatus in credendis, necesse est, quod plus credat summae veritati quam sibi, et quod se redigat in obsequium Christi[^p260-7]; ac per hoc, quod non solum credat quae sunt *secundum rationem*, verum etiam quae sunt *supra rationem* et *contra sensuum experientiam*; quod si recusat, non exhibet summae veritati debitam reverentiam, dum iudicium industriae propriae praefert dictamini lucis aeternae; quod esse non potest absque tumore superbiae et elationis improbandae.

Rursus, quia veritas *supra* rationem sive *praeter* rationem est veritas non visa nec apparens, sed magis occulta et ad credendum difficillima; ideo ad hoc, quod firmiter credatur, necessaria est *illustratio veritatis* animam elevans, necessaria est etiam *testificatio auctoritatis* animam firmans. Primum fit per *fidem infusam*, secundum per *Scripturam authenticam*, quarum utraque est a veritate summa

<!-- page 261 -->
per Iesum Christum, qui est Splendor et Verbum[^p261-1], et per Spiritum sanctum, qui veritatem ostendit et docet et nihilominus credere facit. Hinc est, quod *auctoritas* praebet fulcimentum fidei, et *fides* assentit auctoritati. Et quia auctoritas principaliter residet in sacra Scriptura, quae per Spiritum sanctum est condita tota ad dirigendam fidem catholicam; hinc est, quod vera fides a Scriptura non dissonat, sed ei assentit assensione non ficta.

Postremo, quia veritas, ad quam credendam arctamur per fidem, et de qua sacra Scriptura agit principaliter, non est veritas quaecumque, sed veritas divina, vel ut est in natura propria, vel ut est in natura assumta — nam in huius veritatis cognitione consistit et praemium patriae et meritum viae[^p261-2] — hinc est, quod articuli fidei, qui sunt fidei fundamenta, aut spectant ad *Divinitatem*, aut ad *humanitatem*. Et quoniam *Divinitas* habet considerari in *tribus personis*, scilicet Patre generante, Filio genito et Spiritu sancto procedente, et *quadriformi operatione*, scilicet creatione in *esse* naturae, recreatione in *esse* gratiae, resuscitatione in reparatione vitae et glorificatione in collatione gloriae; hinc est, quod articuli spectantes ad *Divinitatem* sunt septem. — Similiter, quia Christi *humanitas* habet considerari ut concepta de Spiritu sancto, ut nata de Virgine, ut patiens in cruce, ut descendens ad infernum, ut resurgens a morte, ut ascendens in caelum, ut veniens ad iudicium finale; hinc est, quod septem sunt articuli *humanitatis*, et sic in universo *quatuordecim*, ad modum septem stellarum et septem candelabrorum aureorum, in quorum medio Filius hominis ambulabat[^p261-3].

Quoniam ergo unus est Christus in divina natura et humana; et una tantum est veritas summa, quae est ratio credendi una, prima, summa et sola, quae non mutatur per tempora: hinc est, quod de omnibus praedictis articulis una tantum est fides de eadem, non mutata tam in praesentibus quam in praeteritis quam in futuris, licet magis clara sit et explicita in eis qui Christum sequuntur, quam in eis qui praecesserunt eius adventum; sicut novum testamentum est clarius quam vetus, in quibus praedicti articuli continentur[^p261-4].

Et quia Spiritus sanctus hos articulos fidei in Scripturarum profunditate contentos in unum compegit per duodecim Apostolos tanquam per testes firmissimos; hinc est, quod praedicti articuli in unum Symbolum Apostolorum collecti fuerunt. Et ideo secundum Apostolos componentes *duodecim* articuli dici possunt, quia quilibet Apostolus in aedificationem fidei posuit unum articulum quasi lapidem unum vivum; quod Spiritus sanctus recte praefiguravit in duodecim viris, qui duodecim lapides de Iordanis alveo extraxerunt ad altare dominicum construendum[^p261-5].

## English

### Chapter VII. *On the exercise of grace with respect to the things to be believed.*

Fourthly, then, it remains to consider grace as regards the *exercises of merits*. And concerning these, four things are to be considered. First, the exercise of grace in *things to be believed*, of which sort are the articles of faith; second, in *things to be loved*, of which sort are those things which pertain to the order of loving; third, in *things to be carried out*, of which sort are the precepts of the divine law; fourth, in *things to be asked for*, of which sort are the petitions of the Lord's Prayer[^p260-4].

Concerning the *articles* of *faith*, these things are to be held: that although through faith we are bound to believe many things which are above reason, and *generally* all things which are contained and set forth in the canon of sacred Scripture, nevertheless *specially* and properly those are called articles of faith which are contained in the series of the apostolic Creed; which in one way are *twelve*, if regard be had to those who issued the Creed, in another way *fourteen*, if we consider what things are radically to be believed as the foundations of all things to be believed[^p260-5].

Now the reason for the understanding of the foresaid is this: since the first principle, in itself, is supremely *true* and *good*, while in its own work it is supremely *just* and *merciful*; and since to the supremely *true* there is owed a firm assent, to the supremely *good* a fervent love, to the supremely *just* a universal[^p260-6] subjection, to the supremely *merciful* a trustful invocation; and since grace is ordinative of our mind toward the worship due to the first principle: hence it is that grace directs and regulates toward the due and meritorious exercises in *things to be believed, to be loved, to be carried out* and *to be asked for*, according as the supreme truth, goodness, justice and mercy in the blessed Trinity requires.

Since, therefore, *truth* is to be believed, and a greater truth more to be believed, and the supreme truth consequently to be supremely believed; and since the truth of the first principle is infinitely greater than every created truth and more luminous than every light of our intellect: hence it is that, in order that our intellect be rightly ordered in things to be believed, it is necessary that it believe the supreme truth more than itself, and that it bring itself into the obedience of Christ[^p260-7]; and through this, that it believe not only those things which are *according to reason*, but also those which are *above reason* and *against the experience of the senses*; and if it refuses this, it does not render to the supreme truth the reverence due, while it prefers the judgment of its own industry to the dictate of the eternal light — which cannot be without the swelling of pride and of a blameworthy elation.

Again, because the truth which is *above* reason, or *beyond* reason, is a truth not seen nor apparent, but rather hidden and most difficult to believe, therefore, in order that it be firmly believed, there is necessary an *illumination of the truth* raising the soul, and there is necessary also a *testimony of authority* strengthening the soul. The first comes about through *infused faith*, the second through *authentic Scripture*, both of which are from the supreme truth

<!-- page 261 -->
through Jesus Christ, who is the Splendor and the Word[^p261-1], and through the Holy Spirit, who shows and teaches the truth and nonetheless makes us believe. Hence it is that *authority* affords the support of faith, and *faith* assents to authority. And because authority resides principally in sacred Scripture, which was wholly established by the Holy Spirit for the directing of the catholic faith, hence it is that true faith does not disagree with Scripture, but assents to it with an assent not feigned.

Last, because the truth to the believing of which we are constrained by faith, and of which sacred Scripture treats principally, is not just any truth, but the divine truth — either as it is in its proper nature, or as it is in the assumed nature — for in the knowledge of this truth consists both the reward of the fatherland and the merit of the way[^p261-2] — hence it is that the articles of faith, which are the foundations of faith, either regard the *Divinity* or the *humanity*. And since the *Divinity* has to be considered in *three persons*, namely the Father generating, the Son begotten and the Holy Spirit proceeding, and in a *fourfold operation*, namely creation in the *being* of nature, re-creation in the *being* of grace, resuscitation in the repair of life and glorification in the conferring of glory: hence it is that the articles regarding the *Divinity* are seven. — Likewise, because Christ's *humanity* has to be considered as conceived of the Holy Spirit, as born of the Virgin, as suffering on the cross, as descending into hell, as rising from the dead, as ascending into heaven, as coming to the final judgment: hence it is that the articles of the *humanity* are seven, and thus in all *fourteen*, after the manner of the seven stars and the seven golden candlesticks, in the midst of which the Son of man was walking[^p261-3].

Since, therefore, Christ is one in the divine nature and the human; and there is only one supreme truth, which is the one, first, supreme and sole reason of believing, which is not changed through the ages: hence it is that of all the foresaid articles there is only one faith concerning the same, unchanged as much in things present as in things past as in things future, although it is clearer and more explicit in those who follow Christ than in those who went before his coming; just as the new testament is clearer than the old, in which the foresaid articles are contained[^p261-4].

And because the Holy Spirit compacted into one these articles of faith, contained in the depth of the Scriptures, through the twelve Apostles as through most firm witnesses: hence it is that the foresaid articles were gathered into one Symbol of the Apostles. And therefore, according to the Apostles who composed it, the articles can be called *twelve*, since each Apostle placed one article for the building up of faith as one living stone; which the Holy Spirit rightly prefigured in the twelve men who drew twelve stones from the bed of the Jordan for the constructing of the altar of the Lord[^p261-5].
