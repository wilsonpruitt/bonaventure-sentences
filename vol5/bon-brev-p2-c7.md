## Latin

### Capitulum VII. *De apostasia daemonum.*

De *apostasia daemonum* hoc tenendum est, quod Deus Angelos omnes fecit bonos, medios tamen inter se, summum bonum, et commutabile bonum, quod est creatura; ita quod, si converterentur ad amandum quod est *supra*, ascenderent ad statum gratiae et gloriae; si vero ad bonum commutabile, quod est *infra*, hoc ipso ruerent in malum culpae et poenae; quia non est « dedecus peccati sine decore iustitiae[^p224-8] ». — Primus inter Angelos lucifer, praesumens de privato bono, privatam appetiit excellentiam, volens aliis superferri; et ideo cecidit cum ceteris consentientibus sibi. Cadens autem factus est *impoenitens, obstinatus* et *obcaecatus* et *exclusus a Dei contemplatione* et *deordinatus in operatione*, toto nitens conamine ad *subvertendum hominem* per tentationem multiplicem.

Ratio autem ad intelligentiam praedictorum haec est: quia, cum primum principium sit summe bo-

<!-- page 225 -->

num, nihil facit, quod non sit bonum; quia a bono non procedit nisi bonum[^p225-1]; quod tamen fit ab ipso hoc ipso minus est eo, et ideo non potest esse summum bonum. Fuit igitur Angelus a Deo conditus bonus quidem, sed non summus, perficiendus tamen, si affectu tenderet in summum.

Et quoniam per liberum arbitrium voluntatis poterat tendere in bonum summum, vel converti ad bonum privatum; lucifer, suae pulcritudinis et altitudinis consideratione excitatus ad se diligendum et suum privatum bonum, *praesumsit* de altitudine habita et *ambivit* excellentiam propriam, non tamen obtentam; ac per hoc *praesumendo* constituit se sibi principium, in se ipso gloriando; et *ambiendo* constituit se sibi summum bonum, in se ipso quiescendo. Cum autem ipse nec summum esset principium nec summum bonum; necesse fuit, quod inordinato ascensu[^p225-2] rueret; pari ratione et omnes in hoc consentientes. — Et quia « non est dedecus peccati sine decore iustitiae »; ideo statim, cum cecidit in peccatum, cum ceteris adhaerentibus sibi perdidit locum summum, scilicet empyreum, descendens ad imum, scilicet caliginosum aërem, vel infernum, ita quod lapsus in culpam fuit per liberum arbitrium, lapsus vero in poenam per divinum iudicium. — Et quoniam immutabilitatem habebat post electionem, ideo statim *obstinatus* est in malo, et per hoc *excaecatus* a vero et *deordinatus* est in operatione et *infirmatus* in virtute: ideo *voluntas* eius impia et *actio* aversa a Deo conversa est ad hominis *odium et invidiam*; et *perspicacitas* rationis a vero lumine excaecata conversa est ad *deceptiones* per divinationes et cautelas[^p225-3]; et *officiositas* in ministrando a vero ministerio amota conversa est ad tentamenta; et *virtuositas* imminuta et coarctata, quantum permittitur, convertitur ad mirabilia facienda per transmutationes repentinas, quas facit circa corpoream creaturam. — Et quia haec omnia deordinata sunt per voluntatem depravatam per superbiam; ideo haec omnia convertit ad fomentum suae superbiae, quaerens ab hominibus coli et adorari ad modum Dei. Hinc est, quod « omnia male agit[^p225-4] »; quod tamen iuste Deus modo permittit ad vindictam malefactorum, laudem vero bonorum, sicut apparebit per finale iudicium.

## English

### Chapter VII. *On the apostasy of the demons.*

Concerning the *apostasy of the demons* this is to be held: that God made all the Angels good, yet midway between the highest good and the changeable good, which is the creature; in such a way that, if they turned to loving what is *above*, they would ascend to the state of grace and glory; but if to the changeable good, which is *below*, by that very fact they would fall into the evil of guilt and of punishment — because there is no « disgrace of sin without the comeliness of justice[^p224-8] ». — The first among the Angels, Lucifer, presuming upon a private good, sought a private excellence, willing to be borne above the others; and therefore he fell, with the rest who consented to him. And in falling he was made *impenitent, obstinate* and *blinded*, and *shut out from the contemplation of God* and *disordered in operation*, striving with his whole effort to *overthrow man* by manifold temptation.

Now the reason for the understanding of the foresaid is this: that since the first principle is supremely

<!-- page 225 -->

good, it makes nothing that is not good; because from the good there proceeds nothing but good[^p225-1]; yet what is made by it is, by that very fact, less than it, and therefore cannot be the highest good. The Angel was therefore founded by God good indeed, but not highest — to be perfected, however, if by affection he should tend toward the highest.

And since through the free choice of the will he was able to tend toward the highest good, or to turn toward a private good, Lucifer — roused by the consideration of his own beauty and loftiness to the love of himself and of his own private good — *presumed* upon the loftiness he had, and *coveted* an excellence proper to himself, which nevertheless he did not obtain; and through this, by *presuming*, he constituted himself a principle to himself, glorying in himself; and by *coveting*, he constituted himself the highest good to himself, resting in himself. But since he was neither the highest principle nor the highest good, it was necessary that by a disordered ascent[^p225-2] he should fall; and for the same reason all those consenting with him in this. — And because « there is no disgrace of sin without the comeliness of justice », therefore at once, when he fell into sin, he lost with the rest who clung to him the highest place, namely the empyrean, descending to the lowest, namely the murky air, or hell; so that the fall into guilt was through free choice, but the fall into punishment through the divine judgment. — And since he had immutability after election, therefore he was at once made *obstinate* in evil, and through this *blinded* from the true and *disordered* in operation and *weakened* in power: hence his *will*, being impious, and his *action*, being turned away from God, were turned to the *hatred and envy* of man; and the *perspicacity* of his reason, blinded from the true light, was turned to *deceptions* through divinations and wiles[^p225-3]; and his *dutifulness* in ministering, removed from true ministry, was turned to temptations; and his *power*, diminished and constrained, is turned — so far as is permitted — to the working of marvels through sudden transmutations, which he effects upon corporeal creation. — And because all these things are disordered through a will depraved by pride, therefore he turns them all to the fuelling of his own pride, seeking to be worshipped and adored by men after the manner of God. Hence it is that « he does all things ill[^p225-4] »; which nevertheless God now justly permits, for the punishment of evildoers and for the praise of the good, as will appear through the final judgment.
