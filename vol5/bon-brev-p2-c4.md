# Breviloquium, Pars II, Cap. 4
## *De natura corporali quantum ad operari et influere*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 221 -->

### Capitulum IV. *De natura corporali quantum ad operari et influere.*

Quantum vero ad *operari* hoc tenendum est de natura corporea, scilicet quod caelestia influunt in terrestria et elementaria quantum ad *distinctivam significationem temporum*, scilicet dierum, mensium et annorum. Sic enim dicit Scriptura[^p221-4], quod *sint in signa et tempora et dies et annos*. — Influunt etiam quantum ad *effectivam productionem* rerum generabilium et corruptibilium, scilicet *mineralium, vegetabilium, sensibilium* et corporum humanorum. — Sic tamen sunt in signa temporum et regimen operationum, ut non sint certa signa futurorum contingentium nec influant super liberum arbitrium per vim constellationum, quam dixerunt aliqui philosophi esse *fatum*.

Ratio autem ad intelligentiam praedictorum haec est: quia, cum in corporibus caelestibus propter proximitatem ad primum principium sit *lux, motus, calor* et *virtus* — *lux* ratione suae formae et speciei, *motus* respectu superioris influentis, *calor* respectu inferioris naturae suscipientis, *virtus* vero omnibus modis praedictis — cum, inquam, ita sit, caelestia corpora per *lumen* et *motum* sunt in *distinctiones temporum*, scilicet *diei*, secundum lucem solis et motum firmamenti; *mensis*, secundum motum lunae in circulo obliquo[^p221-5]; *anni*, secundum motum solis in eodem circulo; *temporum* vero, secundum varium planetarum motum, distantiam et concursum, ascensum et descensum, retrogradationem et statum, ex quibus oritur diversitas in temporibus.

Per *virtutem* autem et *calorem* influunt *ad productionem* eorum quae ex elementis generantur, excitando, promovendo, conciliando; ita quod secundum conciliationem contrariorum ab aequalitate remotam influunt in *mineralia*; secundum conciliationem ab aequalitate minus longinquam, in *vegetabilia*; secundum conciliationem aequalitati proximantem, in *sensibilia*; secundum vero conciliationem aequalem, in *corpora humana*, quae disposita sunt ad nobilissimam formam, quae est anima rationalis[^p221-6]; ad quam ordinatur et terminatur appetitus omnis naturae sensibilis et corporalis, ut per eam quae est *forma, ens, vivens, sentiens* et *intelligens*, quasi ad modum circuli intelligibilis reducatur ad suum principium, in quo perficiatur et beatificetur[^p221-7].

Et quoniam in illud tendit per liberum arbitrium, ideo quantum ad arbitrii libertatem praecellit omnem virtutem corporalem; ac per hoc cuncta nata sunt sibi servire, nihil autem sibi dominari habet nisi solus Deus, non fatum seu vis positionis siderum.

<!-- page 222 -->

Et propterea indubitanter verum est, quod sumus[^p222-1] finis omnium eorum quae sunt; et omnia corporalia facta sunt ad humanum obsequium, ut ex illis omnibus accendatur homo ad amandum et laudandum Factorem universorum, cuius providentia cuncta disponuntur. Haec igitur sensibilis corporalium machina est tanquam quaedam *domus* a summo opifice homini fabricata, donec ad *domum* veniat *non manufactam in caelis*[^p222-2]; ut, sicut *anima* modo ratione corporis et status meriti nunc est in terris, sic aliquando *corpus* ratione animae et status praemii sit in caelis.

## English

### Chapter IV. *On corporeal nature as regards its operating and influencing.*

Now as regards its *operating*, this is to be held concerning corporeal nature: namely, that the celestial bodies exert influence upon the terrestrial and elemental as regards the *distinguishing signification of times*, that is, of days, of months and of years. For thus Scripture says[^p221-4], that *they may be for signs and seasons and days and years*. — They exert influence also as regards the *effective production* of things generable and corruptible, namely of *minerals, vegetables, sentient things* and human bodies. — Yet they are for signs of times and for the governance of operations in such a way that they are not certain signs of future contingents, nor do they exert influence upon free choice by a force of the constellations, which some philosophers have called *fate*.

Now the reason for the understanding of the foresaid is this: that since in the celestial bodies, on account of their nearness to the first principle, there is *light, motion, heat* and *power* — *light* by reason of their own form and species, *motion* in respect of the higher influencing [cause], *heat* in respect of the lower nature receiving [it], but *power* in all the foresaid ways — since, I say, it is so, the celestial bodies are, through *light* and *motion*, for the *distinctions of times*: namely of the *day*, according to the light of the sun and the motion of the firmament; of the *month*, according to the motion of the moon in the oblique circle[^p221-5]; of the *year*, according to the motion of the sun in the same circle; and of the *seasons*, according to the varied motion of the planets, their distance and conjunction, ascent and descent, retrogradation and station, from which arises the diversity in the seasons.

But through their *power* and *heat* they exert influence *toward the production* of those things which are generated out of the elements, by exciting, by furthering, by reconciling; in such a way that according to a reconciliation of contraries far removed from equality they influence *minerals*; according to a reconciliation less far from equality, *vegetables*; according to a reconciliation approaching equality, *sentient things*; but according to an equal reconciliation, *human bodies*, which are disposed toward the noblest form, which is the rational soul[^p221-6]; toward which the appetite of every sensible and corporeal nature is ordered and terminated, so that through it — which is *form, being, living, sentient* and *understanding* — the whole may, as it were after the manner of an intelligible circle, be led back to its own principle, in which it may be perfected and beatified[^p221-7].

And since it tends toward that [principle] through free choice, therefore as regards the freedom of choice it surpasses every corporeal power; and through this all things are born to serve it, but nothing has dominion over it save God alone — not fate, nor the force of the position of the stars.

<!-- page 222 -->

And therefore it is beyond doubt true that *we are*[^p222-1] the end of all those things which are; and all corporeal things were made for man's service, so that out of them all man might be kindled to love and to praise the Maker of all things, by whose providence all things are disposed. This sensible machinery of corporeal things, therefore, is as it were a certain *house* fashioned for man by the supreme Artificer, until he come to the *house not made by hands, in the heavens*[^p222-2]; so that, just as the *soul* is now on earth by reason of the body and of the state of merit, so one day the *body* may be in heaven by reason of the soul and of the state of reward.

**⚠⚠ THIS CHUNK WAS TRUNCATED ON FIRST WRITING, AND THE REASON GENERALISES — READ THIS.** The first version ended at `…non fatum seu vis positionis siderum` and asserted that p.221's right column showed *"blank paper below — positive evidence that Cap. Quaracchi's footers expand upward and shorten the body column to fit.

**The rule this yields: blank space at a column foot is NEVER evidence that a unit has ended.** It is evidence only about the size of that page's footer. A capitulum ends where the *next* heading begins, and nowhere else — so the boundary must be established from the **following** column or page, positively, never inferred from white space. It was caught only because c5's first band read opened on `Et propterea indubitanter verum est…` sitting *above* the `Cap. V.` heading; the raw confirms it at L42005, before the heading at L42022. This is the same failure family as the Vol IV cascade-merge: a chunk that looks complete, parses clean, and passes every audit, because nothing in the tooling knows what the page ought to contain.
