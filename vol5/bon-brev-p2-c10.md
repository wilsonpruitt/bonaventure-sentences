## Latin

### Capitulum X. *De productione hominis quantum ad corpus.*

De *corpore* vero humano in statu primae conditionis tenenda sunt haec secundum doctrinam fidei

<!-- page 228 -->

orthodoxae, videlicet quod corpus primi hominis sic conditum fuit et *de limo terrae*[^p228-1] formatum, ut tamen esset animae *subiectum* et suo modo *proportionabile*, *proportionabile*, inquam, quantum ad *complexionem* aequalem, quantum ad *organizationem* pulcherrimam et multiformem et quantum ad *rectitudinem staturae*; *subiectum* autem, ut esset *obtemperans* sine rebellione, esset etiam *propagans* et propagabile sine libidine, esset *vegetabile* sine defectione, esset etiam *immutabile* ad[^p228-2] omnimodam incorruptionem, non interveniente morte; et secundum hoc datus est sibi locus paradisi terrestris in habitationem tranquillam. — Formata est *mulier* de latere viri in consortium et adiutorium ad propagationem immaculatam; datum est etiam *lignum vitae*[^p228-3] ad vegetationem continuam, et tandem ad immutationem perfectam per immortalitatem perpetuam.

Ratio autem ad intelligentiam praedictorum haec est: quia, cum primum principium sit in producendo potentissimum, sapientissimum et optimum, et in omnibus effectibus suis hoc aliquo modo manifestet; potissime debuit hoc manifestare in ultimo effectu et nobilissimo; cuiusmodi est homo, quem inter ceteras creaturas produxit ultimo, ut in hoc potissime appareret et reluceret divinorum operum consummatio.

Ut igitur in homine manifestaretur Dei *potentia*, ideo fecit eum ex naturis maxime distantibus, coniunctis in unam personam et naturam; cuiusmodi sunt corpus et anima, quorum unum est substantia corporea, alterum vero, scilicet anima, est substantia spiritualis et incorporea; quae in genere substantiae maxime distant[^p228-4].

Ut vero ibidem manifestaretur Dei *sapientia*, fecit tale corpus, ut *proportionem* suo modo haberet ad animam. Quoniam ergo corpus unitur animae ut *perficienti* et *moventi* et *ad beatitudinem sursum tendenti*[^p228-5]; ideo, ut conformaretur animae *vivificanti*, habuit *complexionem* aequalem non a *pondere*, vel *mole*, sed ab *aequalitate naturalis iustitiae*, quae disponit ad nobilissimum modum vitae. — Ut autem conformaretur *moventi* per multiformitatem potentiarum, habuit *multiformitatem organorum* cum summa venustate et artificiositate et ductibilitate; sicut patet in facie et in manu, quae est « organum organorum[^p228-6] ». — Ut autem conformaretur animae *sursum tendenti ad caelum*, habuit *rectitudinem staturae* et caput sursum erectum; ut sic corporalis rectitudo mentali rectitudini attestaretur.

Postremo, ut in homine manifestaretur Dei *bonitas* et benevolentia, ideo fecit hominem absque omni macula et culpa et absque omni poena sive miseria. Cum enim primum principium simul sit *optimum* et *iustissimum*; quia *optimum*, non debuit facere hominem nisi bonum, ac per hoc innocentem et rectum[^p228-7]; quia *iustissimum*, poenam non debuit infligere ei qui nullum omnino habebat peccatum: ac per hoc tale corpus constituit illi animae rationali, quod ita sibi esset *obtemperans*, ut nulla esset in eo pugna rebellionis, nulla pronitas libidinis, nulla imminutio vigoris, nulla corruptio mortis; ita etiam esset *animae conforme*, ut, sicut anima erat innocens, et tamen poterat cadere in culpam, sic corpus esset impassibile, et tamen posset cadere in poenam; et ideo « poterat non mori, et poterat mori[^p228-8] »; poterat habere sufficientiam, et poterat habere indigentiam; poterat animae obtemperare, et poterat etiam adversus eam rebellionem et pugnam habere.

Et propterea in statu illo corpus erat tale, ut ab eo fieret *decisio seminalis* ad propagationem prolis per adminiculum sexus muliebris pariter comprincipiantis; fieret etiam *humoris nutrimentalis consumtio* per actionem caloris; fieret etiam nihilominus *restauratio* per alimentum lignorum paradisi, restaurato per lignum vitae, seu praeservato humido radicali; quod quidem lignum hanc virtutem habuit, ob quam, ut dicit Augustinus[^p228-9], fuit non solum in cibum, verum etiam in Sacramentum. — Incorruptio igitur et immortalitas corporis Adae principaliter veniebat ab *anima* sicut a continente et influente, a *corporis bona et aequali complexione* sicut a disponente et suscipiente, a *ligno* autem *vitae* sicut a

<!-- page 229 -->

vegetante et adminiculante, a *regimine* vero *divinae providentiae* sicut interius conservante et exterius protegente.

## English

### Chapter X. *On the production of man as regards the body.*

Concerning the human *body*, however, in the state of its first condition, these things are to be held according to the doctrine of the orthodox

<!-- page 228 -->

faith: namely, that the body of the first man was so founded and formed *from the slime of the earth*[^p228-1] that it should nevertheless be *subject* to the soul and in its own manner *proportionable* to it — *proportionable*, I say, as regards an equal *complexion*, as regards a most beautiful and multiform *organization*, and as regards *uprightness of stature*; and *subject*, that it might be *obedient* without rebellion, might also be *propagating* and propagable without lust, might be *capable of growth* without failing, and might also be *unchangeable* toward[^p228-2] an incorruption of every kind, with no death intervening; and according to this there was given to it the place of the earthly paradise for a tranquil dwelling. — *Woman* was formed from the side of the man for fellowship and help toward an immaculate propagation; there was also given the *tree of life*[^p228-3] for continual growth, and at length for a perfect transformation into a perpetual immortality.

Now the reason for the understanding of the foresaid is this: that since the first principle is in producing most powerful, most wise and best, and manifests this in some manner in all its effects, it had most of all to manifest this in the last and noblest effect — of which sort is man, whom it produced last among the other creatures, so that in him most of all the consummation of the divine works might appear and shine forth.

That the *power* of God, therefore, might be manifested in man, it made him out of natures most widely distant, conjoined into one person and nature; of which sort are body and soul, of which the one is a corporeal substance, and the other, namely the soul, is a spiritual and incorporeal substance — which in the genus of substance are most widely distant[^p228-4].

But that the *wisdom* of God might be manifested in the same place, it made such a body as would have, in its own manner, a *proportion* to the soul. Since, therefore, the body is united to the soul as to what *perfects* and *moves* it and *tends upward to beatitude*[^p228-5]: therefore, that it might be conformed to the *life-giving* soul, it had an equal *complexion* — not from *weight* or *mass*, but from *the equality of natural justice*, which disposes toward the noblest mode of life. — And that it might be conformed to the soul as *mover*, through the multiformity of its powers, it had a *multiformity of organs* with the utmost gracefulness and artistry and pliancy; as appears in the face and in the hand, which is « the organ of organs[^p228-6] ». — And that it might be conformed to the soul *tending upward to heaven*, it had *uprightness of stature* and a head erect on high; so that thus bodily uprightness might bear witness to uprightness of mind.

Lastly, that the *goodness* and benevolence of God might be manifested in man, it therefore made man without any stain and guilt and without any punishment or misery. For since the first principle is at once *best* and *most just*: because *best*, it ought not to make man other than good, and through this innocent and upright[^p228-7]; because *most just*, it ought not to inflict punishment on him who had no sin at all. And through this it constituted for that rational soul such a body as would be so *obedient* to it that there would be in it no strife of rebellion, no proneness to lust, no diminution of vigour, no corruption of death; and would likewise be so *conformed to the soul* that, just as the soul was innocent and yet could fall into guilt, so the body would be impassible and yet could fall into punishment; and therefore « it could not die, and it could die[^p228-8] »; it could have sufficiency, and it could have want; it could obey the soul, and it could also have rebellion and strife against it.

And for this reason, in that state the body was such that from it there would come a *seminal severance* for the propagation of offspring, through the aid of the female sex co-originating equally with it; there would also come a *consuming of the nutrimental humour* through the action of heat; and there would nonetheless come a *restoration* through the food of the trees of paradise, the radical moisture being restored — or rather preserved — through the tree of life; which tree indeed had this power, on account of which, as Augustine says[^p228-9], it was not only for food but also for a Sacrament. — The incorruption, therefore, and immortality of Adam's body came principally from the *soul* as from that which contains and inflows; from *the good and equal complexion of the body* as from that which disposes and receives; from the *tree of life* as from that which

<!-- page 229 -->

gives growth and support; and from the *governance of divine providence* as from that which inwardly conserves and outwardly protects.
