## Latin
<!-- page 213 -->

### Capitulum V. *De unitate divinae naturae in multiformitate apparitionum.*

Secundo autem de pluralitate *apparitionum* docet divina doctrina hoc esse tenendum, quod cum Deus sit incircumscriptibilis, invisibilis et incommutabilis; nihilominus tamen *habitat specialiter* in sanctis viris, *apparuit* Patriarchis et Prophetis, *descendit* de caelis, *misit* etiam Filium et Spiritum sanctum ad salutem humani generis. — Et licet in Deo sint indivisa natura, virtus et operatio Trinitatis; *missio* tamen vel *apparitio* unius personae non est

<!-- page 214 -->

missio vel apparitio alterius. Licet etiam sit ibi summa aequalitas, solius tamen Patris est *mittere* et *non mitti*; Spiritus sancti est tantummodo *mitti* respectu divinarum personarum, nisi forte dicatur mittere hominem assumtum; Filii autem est *mittere* et *mitti*, sicut ex Scripturis potest colligi[^p214-1].

Intelligentia autem et ratio praedictorum haec est: quia, licet primum principium sit immensum et incircumscriptibile, sit incorporeum et invisibile, sit aeternum et incommutabile; principium tamen est rerum spiritualium et corporalium, naturalium et gratuitarum, ac per hoc rerum etiam mutabilium, sensibilium et circumscriptarum, per quas, licet ipse sit immutabilis, insensibilis, incircumscriptibilis[^p214-2], se ipsum reddit manifestum et notum. Reddit autem se manifestum et notum *generaliter* per universitatem suorum effectuum ab ipso emanantium, in quibus dicitur esse per *essentiam, potentiam* et *praesentiam*[^p214-3], quod se extendit ad omnia creata. — Reddit etiam se *specialiter* notum per aliquos effectus, qui in ipsum specialiter ducunt, ratione quorum dicitur *habitare, apparere, descendere, mitti* et *mittere*. — *Habitare* namque dicit effectum *spiritualem*[^p214-4] cum acceptatione, sicut est effectus gratiae gratum facientis, quae est deiformis et in Deum reducit et Deum facit nos habere, et haberi a nobis, ac per hoc et inhabitare in nobis. Et quia gratiae effectus communis est omnibus personis; ideo una persona non inhabitat sine alia, immo simul tota Trinitas[^p214-5].

*Apparere* vero dicit effectum *sensibilem* cum expressa significatione, sicut Spiritus sanctus apparuit in columba. Et quoniam, sicut personae divinae distinctae sunt, sic distincte significari possunt et signis et nominibus; ideo *quaelibet persona* potest *per se apparere*, et *apparitio* potest *omnibus competere*, sive insimul, sive cuilibet per se. Unde quod Spiritus sanctus dicitur in linguis igneis et in columba apparuisse, hoc non est propter *novum vinculum*, vel *effectum* specialem; sed propter unionem, quae est inter *signatum* et *signum* sibi specialiter et modo et origine deputatum[^p214-6].

*Descendere* autem dicit utrumlibet effectuum praedictorum cum *inchoatione*. Deus enim beatissimis Angelis semper est praesens in caelis, quia in eis semper *inhabitat* et *apparet*. Peccatoribus autem in terris est modo ut absens et quantum ad *gratiam* et quantum ad *notitiam*; et ideo, cum *incipit* apparere vel inhabitare, de praesente caelis[^p214-7] et quasi absente nobis fit praesens terris: et ideo, licet in se non mutetur, nobis tamen *descendere* dicitur.

*Mitti* vero dicit effectus praedictos cum *aeterna productione*. Tunc enim Pater mittit Filium, quando, faciendo eum nobis praesentem per notitiam vel gratiam, insinuat, quod ab ipso procedit. Et quia Pater a nullo procedit, ideo nusquam dicitur *mitti*[^p214-8]. Quia vero Filius et producit et producitur, ideo *mittit* et *mittitur*. Quia vero Spiritus sanctus aeternaliter producitur, sed non producit nisi ex tempore; ideo ipsius est proprie *mitti*, *mittere* vero non competit sibi nisi respectu creaturae. — Ex quo apparet, quod hae sunt impropriae et exponendae: Spiritus sanctus mittit se; Spiritus sanctus mittit Filium; Filius mittit se ipsum, nisi intelligatur: in quantum est de Virgine natus. — Patet etiam, quare *mittere* et *mitti* non competit omnibus: quia, licet dicat effectum in creatura, dicit tamen relationem intrinsecam, ita quod *mittere* dicit auctoritatem, *mitti* subauctoritatem, ratione productionis aeternae interius importatae.

## English

### Chapter V. *On the unity of the divine nature amid the multiformity of apparitions.*

Second, concerning the plurality of *apparitions*, divine doctrine teaches that this is to be held: that although God is uncircumscribable, invisible, and unchangeable, nevertheless he *dwells in a special way* in holy men, *appeared* to the Patriarchs and the Prophets, *came down* from the heavens, and also *sent* the Son and the Holy Spirit for the salvation of the human race. — And although in God the nature, power, and operation of the Trinity are undivided, yet the *mission* or *apparition* of one person is not

the mission or apparition of another. And although there is in them the highest equality, yet it belongs to the Father alone *to send* and *not to be sent*; to the Holy Spirit belongs only *to be sent* in respect of the divine persons, unless perhaps he be said to send the assumed man; but to the Son belongs both *to send* and *to be sent*, as can be gathered from the Scriptures[^p214-1].

Now the understanding and the reason for the aforesaid is this: although the first principle is immense and uncircumscribable, is incorporeal and invisible, is eternal and unchangeable, nevertheless it is the principle of things spiritual and corporeal, natural and gratuitous, and through this of things also mutable, sensible, and circumscribed — through which, although he himself is unchangeable, insensible, and uncircumscribable[^p214-2], he renders himself manifest and known. Now he renders himself manifest and known *generally* through the totality of his effects emanating from him, in which he is said to be by *essence, power*, and *presence*[^p214-3], which extends to all created things. — He renders himself known also *specially* through certain effects which lead specially to him, by reason of which he is said *to dwell, to appear, to descend, to be sent*, and *to send*. — For *to dwell* says a *spiritual* effect[^p214-4] with acceptance, such as is the effect of sanctifying grace, which is deiform and leads back to God, and makes us have God, and be had by God, and through this also makes him indwell in us. And because the effect of grace is common to all the persons, therefore one person does not indwell without another, but rather the whole Trinity together[^p214-5].

But *to appear* says a *sensible* effect with an express signification, as the Holy Spirit appeared in the dove. And since, just as the divine persons are distinct, so they can be distinctly signified both by signs and by names; therefore *any person whatever* can *appear by himself*, and *apparition* can *belong to all*, whether together or to each by himself. Hence, that the Holy Spirit is said to have appeared in fiery tongues and in the dove is not on account of a *new bond* or a special *effect*, but on account of the union which exists between the *thing signified* and the *sign* specially assigned to it, both in manner and in origin[^p214-6].

But *to descend* says either of the aforesaid effects with an *inception*. For God is always present to the most blessed Angels in the heavens, because in them he always *indwells* and *appears*. But to sinners on earth he is at present as it were absent, both as to *grace* and as to *knowledge*; and therefore, when he *begins* to appear or to indwell, from being present to the heavens[^p214-7] and as it were absent to us, he becomes present to the earth: and therefore, although in himself he is not changed, he is nevertheless said to *descend* with respect to us.

But *to be sent* says the aforesaid effects with an *eternal production*. For the Father then sends the Son, when, by making him present to us through knowledge or grace, he conveys that he proceeds from himself. And because the Father proceeds from none, therefore he is nowhere said *to be sent*[^p214-8]. But because the Son both produces and is produced, therefore he *sends* and *is sent*. But because the Holy Spirit is eternally produced, yet does not produce except in time; therefore it belongs to him properly *to be sent*, whereas *to send* does not belong to him except in respect of the creature. — From which it appears that these statements are improper and require exposition: the Holy Spirit sends himself; the Holy Spirit sends the Son; the Son sends himself — unless it be understood as: insofar as he is born of the Virgin. — It is also clear why *to send* and *to be sent* do not belong to all: because, although it says an effect in the creature, it nevertheless says an intrinsic relation, such that *to send* says authority, and *to be sent* sub-authority, by reason of the eternal production interiorly implied.
