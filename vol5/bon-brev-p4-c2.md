## Latin

### Capitulum II. *De incarnatione quantum ad unionem naturarum.*

Circa ipsum autem Verbum incarnatum tria nobis consideranda occurrunt, scilicet *unio naturarum, plenitudo charismatum* et *perpessio passionum* propter redimendum genus humanum[^p242-3]. — Circa *naturarum unionem* haec tria consideranda sunt ad intelligendum incarnationis mysterium, scilicet *opus, modus* et *tempus*.

De *opere* autem incarnationis haec tenenda sunt secundum fidem christianam, quod incarnatio est operatio Trinitatis, per quam fit assumtio carnis a Deitate et unio Deitatis cum carne; ita quod assumtio non tantum est carnis sensibilis, sed etiam spiritus rationalis secundum potentiam vegetandi, sentiendi et intelligendi; ita etiam, quod unio non fit in unitate *naturae*, sed *personae*, non *humanae*, sed *divinae*, non *assumtae*, sed *assumentis*; non personae *cuiuslibet*, sed personae *solius Verbi*; in qua tanta fit unio, ut quidquid dicitur de Filio Dei dicatur de filio hominis, et e converso, his tamen exceptis, in quibus exprimitur unio, vel clauditur negatio[^p242-4].

Ratio autem ad intelligentiam praedictorum haec est: quia incarnationis opus non solum est a primo principio, in quantum est effectivum in producendo, verum etiam, in quantum est reparativum *remediando, satisfaciendo* et *reconciliando*. Quoniam ergo incarnatio, in quantum dicit aliquem effectum, est a primo principio, quod omnia facit ratione summae virtutis; et substantia, virtus[^p242-5] et operatio unita est et indivisa omnimode in tribus personis: hinc est, quod necesse est, incarnationis operationem a tota Trinitate manare.

Quia vero est a primo principio, in quantum est reparativum *remediando*; et totum genus humanum lapsum fuerat et vitiatum, non solum ratione animae, verum etiam carnis: hinc est, quod necesse fuit, quod totum assumeretur, ut totum curaretur[^p242-6]. — Et quoniam nobis magis nota est pars carnis et magis a Deo distat; ut expressior fiat nominatio, et maior exprimatur humiliatio, et profundior explicetur dignatio: hinc est, quod opus illud non *inanimatio*, sed *incarnatio* nominatur.

Rursus, quia est a primo principio, in quantum est reparativum *satisfaciendo*, et satisfactio non fit, nisi ab eo qui *debet* et *potest*; et non *debet* nisi homo, nec *potest* nisi Deus: oportuit, quod in satisfactione simul esset concursus utriusque naturae, divinae scilicet et humanae. Et quia impossibile est, quod divina natura concurrat cum alia sicut pars ad constitutionem tertii; nec quod ipsa transeat in aliam naturam, nec alia natura transeat in ipsam, propter simplicitatem et immutabilitatem ipsius perfectissimam: hinc est, quod Deitas et humanitas non uniuntur in unitate *naturae* nec *accidentis*, uniuntur ergo in unitate *personae* et *hypostasis*. Et quoniam divina natura in nullo supposito potest subsistere praeterquam in propria hypostasi; ideo unio illa non potest esse in hypostasi seu persona *hominis*, sed *Dei*; ac per hoc per illam unionem primum principium in una suarum hypostasum fecit se ipsum suppositum humanae naturae: et ita una tantum est ibi personalitas et unitas personalis, ex parte scilicet *assumentis*.

Postremo, quia est a primo principio, ut est reparativum *reconciliando*; et reconcilians est mediator, mediatio autem proprie convenit Dei Filio: ideo et incarnatio. Mediatoris namque est esse *medium* inter hominem et Deum ad reducendum hominem ad divinam *cognitionem*, ad divinam *conformitatem* et ad divinam *filiationem*. Nullum autem magis decet esse *medium* quam personam, quae producit et producitur, quae est media trium personarum; nullumque magis decet reducere hominem ad divinam *cognitionem* quam Verbum, quo se Pater declarat, quod est unibile carni, sicut et verbum voci[^p243-1]; nullum etiam magis decet reducere ad divinam *conformitatem*, quam eum qui est imago Patris; nullum magis decet ad *filiationem adoptivam* reducere quam Filium naturalem: ac per hoc nullum magis decet fieri filium hominis quam ipsum Filium Dei[^p243-2].

<!-- page 243 -->

Quoniam ergo omnino idem est Filius hominis et Dei ratione incarnationis; et « quaecumque uni et eidem sunt eadem, inter se sunt eadem[^p243-3] »: hinc est, quod necessario fit communicatio idiomatum, nisi sit vocabulum, in quo aliqua repugnantia includatur; sicut sunt illa, in quibus includitur respectus unionis unius naturae ad alteram, sicut *unire, incarnari, assumere* et *assumi*; vel negatio alicuius, cuius oppositum alteri competit, sicut *incipere* esse, *creari* et consimilia, in quibus instantia fertur contra regulam praehabitam propter causam praedictam.

## English

### Chapter II. *On the incarnation as regards the union of the natures.*

Now concerning the incarnate Word itself three things present themselves for our consideration, namely the *union of the natures*, the *fulness of the charisms* and the *endurance of the sufferings* for the redeeming of the human race[^p242-3]. — Concerning the *union of the natures* these three things are to be considered for the understanding of the mystery of the incarnation, namely the *work*, the *mode* and the *time*.

Now concerning the *work* of the incarnation these things are to be held according to the Christian faith: that the incarnation is an operation of the Trinity, through which there comes about the assumption of flesh by the Deity and the union of the Deity with flesh; in such wise that the assumption is not only of sensible flesh, but also of the rational spirit according to the power of vegetating, of sensing and of understanding; and in such wise also that the union does not come about in a unity of *nature*, but of *person*, not of a *human* person, but of a *divine* one, not of the person *assumed*, but of the person *assuming*; not of *any* person whatever, but of the person *of the Word alone*; in which person so great a union comes about that whatever is said of the Son of God is said of the son of man, and conversely — those things nevertheless excepted in which the union is expressed, or a negation is enclosed[^p242-4].

Now the reason for the understanding of the foresaid is this: since the work of the incarnation is not only from the first principle inasmuch as it is effective in producing, but also inasmuch as it is reparative *by remedying, by making satisfaction* and *by reconciling*. Since therefore the incarnation, inasmuch as it names some effect, is from the first principle, which makes all things by reason of its highest power; and since substance, power[^p242-5] and operation are united and in every way undivided in the three persons: hence it is that the operation of the incarnation must necessarily flow from the whole Trinity.

But because it is from the first principle inasmuch as it is reparative *by remedying*; and the whole human race had fallen and been vitiated, not only by reason of the soul, but also of the flesh: hence it is that it was necessary that the whole be assumed, so that the whole might be healed[^p242-6]. — And since the part which is flesh is better known to us and stands further from God; in order that the naming might be more expressive, and the humiliation more greatly expressed, and the condescension more profoundly unfolded: hence it is that that work is named not *inanimation*, but *incarnation*.

Again, because it is from the first principle inasmuch as it is reparative *by making satisfaction*, and satisfaction is not made except by him who *is bound to* and *is able to*; and none *is bound to* but man, nor *is able to* but God: it behoved that in the satisfaction there should be at once a concurrence of both natures, the divine namely and the human. And because it is impossible that the divine nature should concur with another as a part toward the constitution of a third; nor that it should pass over into another nature, nor another nature into it, on account of its most perfect simplicity and immutability: hence it is that the Deity and the humanity are not united in a unity of *nature* nor of *accident*, and they are therefore united in a unity of *person* and of *hypostasis*. And since the divine nature can subsist in no supposit save in its own hypostasis; therefore that union cannot be in the hypostasis or person *of a man*, but *of God*; and through this, by that union the first principle in one of its hypostases made itself the supposit of the human nature: and so there is there only one personality and one personal unity, on the part namely of the one *assuming*.

Lastly, because it is from the first principle as it is reparative *by reconciling*; and the one reconciling is a mediator, and mediation properly befits the Son of God: therefore the incarnation too befits him. For it belongs to a mediator to be a *middle* between man and God, for the leading back of man to divine *knowledge*, to divine *conformity* and to divine *sonship*. But nothing more befits being a *middle* than a person who both produces and is produced, who is the middle of the three persons; and nothing more befits leading man back to divine *knowledge* than the Word, by which the Father declares himself, which is unitable to flesh, as a word also is to a voice[^p243-1]; nothing likewise more befits leading back to divine *conformity* than him who is the image of the Father; nothing more befits leading back to *adoptive sonship* than the natural Son: and through this nothing more befits becoming the son of man than the Son of God himself[^p243-2].

<!-- page 243 -->

Since therefore the Son of man and the Son of God are altogether the same by reason of the incarnation; and since « whatever things are the same as one and the same thing are the same as each other[^p243-3] »: hence it is that the communication of idioms necessarily comes about, unless there be a word in which some repugnance is enclosed; such as are those in which the respect of the union of one nature to the other is included, as *to unite, to be incarnate, to assume* and *to be assumed*; or the negation of something whose opposite belongs to the other, as *to begin* to be, *to be created* and the like — in which cases an instance is brought against the rule already laid down, for the reason aforesaid.
