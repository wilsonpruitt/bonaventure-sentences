### COLLATIO XI.
#### *De secunda visione tractatio quarta, quae est secunda de speciositate fidei et agit de speculatione Dei trini.*

### Summarium

*Introductio. Repetuntur dicta de altitudine et firmitate fidei; nunc de speciositate, quae est in duodecim speculationibus, 1. — Pars I. De speculo primo, quod est intellectuale, ex nominibus Dei intellectualibus; hoc est speculum Augustini, 2-4. — Sed nos cum Ruth colligamus spicas post messores. Duplex est speculum Trinitatis, intellectuale et magis materiale; quoad primum quatuor rationes in Deo pro Trinitate speculanda, 5. — Primo, ex triplici perfectione: originis, ordinis, indivisionis, 6-8. — Secundo, ex perfecta productione, quae est sex modorum, 9. — Obiectio solvitur, 10. — Tertio, ex productiva diffusione triplici: actualissima, integerrima, ultimata, 11. — Quarto, ex diffusiva dilectione, quae est triplex, 12. — Pars II. De speculo secundo, congregato ex parvis: Verbum naturaliter habet rationem exprimendi, et duodecim generationes creaturarum hoc exprimunt, tribus modis: per diffusionem, expressionem, propagationem, 13. — Prima via has generationes applicandi ad aeternam generationem; primo quoad diffusionem quadruplicem, 14. 15. — Secundo, quoad expressionem quadruplicem, 16. 17. — Tertio, quoad propagationem quadruplicem, 18. 19. — De his duodecim modis in communi, 20. 21. — Secunda via easdem applicandi; primo quoad diffusionem, 22. — Secundo, quoad expressionem, 23. 24. — Tertio, quoad propagationem, 25.*

1. *Nos vero omnes, revelata facie gloriam Domini speculantes*[^p379-6] etc. Dictum est de secunda visione, scilicet intelligentiae per fidem elevatae, quae intelligitur per caelum vel firmamentum, quod est altum, fir-

<!-- page 380 -->

mum et speciosum. Dictum est de altitudine et firmitate; nunc de *speciositate* dicendum est. — Dictum est enim: *Suspice caelum et numera stellas, si potes*[^p380-1]; et in Apocalypsi de civitate, quae habebat *duodecim portas*, et *portae ex singulis margaritis*. Duodecim ergo sunt speculationes, quae oriuntur ex fide; et prima est de Deo, quod sit *primum esse*, de qua dictum est, quia primum nomen Dei est *qui sum*[^p380-2]. Nunc dicendum est de secunda, scilicet quod Deus est *trinus et unus*.

2. Hoc speculum format Augustinus decimo quinto *de Trinitate*[^p380-3], capitulo quarto[^p380-4], ubi ponit duodecim conditiones sive nomina, quae de Deo dicuntur, ut sit *aeternus, immortalis, incorruptibilis, immutabilis, vivus, sapiens, potens, speciosus, iustus, bonus, beatus, spiritus*. Ex istis colligit Trinitatem.

3. *Sapiens*[^p380-5] enim est per sapientiam, et *beatus* per beatitudinem, et sic de aliis; et non aliud est ipse et sua sapientia, sed est ipsa sapientia. Est ergo ibi *mens, notitia, amor;* et haec tria sunt unum, quia ipsa mens intelligendo se gignit notitiam sui, et ex mente et notitia procedit amor: ergo est ibi Trinitas in unitate substantiae. — Sic Pater est mens, Filius notitia, Spiritus sanctus amor sive nexus; et haec est appropriatio, quae fit ad Patrem, Filium et Spiritum sanctum[^p380-6].

4. Haec duodecim redigit[^p380-7] ad tria, scilicet ad *aeternitatem, sapientiam, beatitudinem*, et haec tria ad unum, scilicet ad *sapientiam*, quia in sapientia est aeternitas et beatitudo. Et in sapientia est *notitia*[^p380-8] sui, et ex notitia amor; et ideo dicit Anselmus in *Monologio*[^p380-9]. Ex hoc enim, quod summus Spiritus se intelligit, Filius

<!-- page 381 -->

generatur, Spiritus sanctus spiratur. Hoc est speculum Augustini. Sed nos cum Ruth[^p381-1] colligamus spicas post messores secundum doctores nostros.

5. Est autem duplex speculum Trinitatis: unum *intellectuale*, quod facit intelligere nomina dicta de Deo *intellectualia;* secundum est magis *materiale*, quod fit per nomina *transsumtiva*. — Prima speculatio est, quod intelligentia per fidem elevata dicit, Deum esse trinum et unum, propter quatuor, quae sunt in divino *esse*, scilicet propter conditionem *perfectionis*, propter conditionem *perfectae productionis, productivae diffusionis, diffusivae dilectionis;* et unum sequitur ex altero.

6. Est enim ibi summa *perfectio*, ex qua fit, quod primum principium habet perfectionem *originis, ordinis, indivisionis*. — Est autem *origo* originans perfectior quam non originans, et perfectior est origo originans originantem quam originans non originantem: ergo si ibi est perfecta origo, necesse est, esse Patrem producentem Filium originantem et Spiritum sanctum originatum.

7. Item, a parte *ordinis* sic: ubi est perfectus ordo, ibi est ratio *principii, medii* et *ultimi;* alioquin ordo esset diminutus: ergo in divinis erit iste ordo perfectus. Et est ordo *originis*, non *dignitatis* nec *temporis* nec *naturae*[^p381-2].

8. Item, a parte *indivisionis*. Est enim unitas *consubstantialitatis, totalitatis* sive plenitudinis, *conformitatis, individuationis, voluntatis, inseparabilitatis*[^p381-3]. Unitas autem *consubstantialitatis* deficit in *principiatis*, quia non habent eandem substantiam cum principio, ut patet in *forma sine materia*[^p381-4]. — Item, unitas *totalitatis* sive plenitudinis in *principiatis* deficit, quia non est ex se nec simplex. — Item, unitas in *universalibus* deficit, scilicet *conformitatis*, quia numeratur cum alia; licet enim sit in pluribus, numeratur tamen in illis. — Item, unitas in *individuis* deficit, quia cum materia numeratur forma[^p381-5]. — Item, unitas *voluntatis* deficit, quia non ita coniunguntur animi, quin separari possint per discordiam. — Item, unitas *inseparabilitatis* deficit in *naturis*, quia non est tam fortis unio formae, quin possit per artificium separari. — Haec[^p381-6] autem in Deo ponenda.

9. Item, in primo *esse* est ratio *perfectae productionis*. Est autem productio *similis* et dissimilis, *aequalis* et inaequalis, *consubstantialis* et essentialiter differentis. — De necessitate, si est productio *dissimilis*, praeintelligitur productio *similis;* quod sic patet: *simile* habet se ad dissimile, sicut *idem* ad diversum, sicut *unum* ad multa; sed de necessitate idem praecedit diversum, et unum multa[^p381-7]: ergo productio similis productionem dissimilis. Sed creatura producitur a primo *esse* et est *dissimilis:* ergo de necessitate producitur simile, quod est Deus. — Item, secundum Boethium[^p381-8] *inaequalitates* oriuntur ab aequalitate: si ergo producitur a Deo quod est inaequale, scilicet creatura, necessario praecedit productio *aequalis*, quod est Deus. — Item, si producitur a Deo essentialiter *differens*, necessario ante producitur *substantialiter idem*, quia ante extrinsecum est intrinsecum, ut patet: quia verbum dicentis extrinsecum praecedit mentis conceptus ut aliquid intrinsecum: ergo similiter a substantia aeterna non manat differens, nisi producatur substantialiter idem: ergo in Deo prius est productio *similis, aequalis, consubstantialis* quam *dissimilis, inaequalis, essentialiter differentis*.

10. Sed quid? dices, nunquid intelligendo, primum *esse* unum esse suppositum, ut gentiles, adhuc intelligunt substantiam, virtutem et operationem? Ergo, non intellecta trinitate vel emanatione, est intelligere productionem. Respondeo: intellectus duplex est: perfectus et plenus et *plene resolvens;* et tali intellectu non est intelligere sic; intelligere autem *semiplene* potest intellectus defectivus sic, quod resolvat in plura, quae in Deo sunt unum, aliter non[^p381-9].

11. Item, tertia ratio speculi est, quia *in Deo est ratio productivae diffusionis* sic. Illud *esse* est *summe bonum*, ergo summe diffundit se[^p381-10] triplici diffusione: *actualissima, integerrima, ultimata* sive ultimatissima. Quia *actualissima*, semper est, semper fuit, semper erit; semper generat, semper generavit, semper generabit. Hoc non potest habere creatura, quod semper sit, semper fuerit et semper futura sit: ergo necesse est, ut emanet *aeternus*[^p381-11]. — Item, *integerrima* non est haec diffusio in creatura, quia Deus non dat totum decorem exemplaritatis creaturae, immo non dat nisi generando Filium, qui dicere potest: *Omnia, quae habet Pater, mea*

<!-- page 382 -->

*sunt*[^p382-1]; hoc non dicit aliqua creatura. — Item, haec diffusio est *ultimata*, ut det producens, quidquid potest; creatura autem recipere non potest, quidquid Deus dare potest. Unde sicut punctus ad lineam nihil addit, nec etiam mille millia punctorum[^p382-2]; sic bonitas creaturae bonitati Creatoris nihil addit, quia finitum infinito nihil addit. Ergo necesse est, ut haec diffusio secundum totum *posse* sit in aliquo, quo maius cogitari non potest; omni autem creatura aliquid maius cogitari potest, et etiam ipsa creatura maior se cogitari potest. Sed in Filio est productio, sicut in Patre. Si ergo Patre nihil maius cogitari potest: ergo nec Filio. Si Pater etiam ultimata diffusione non se diffunderet, perfectus non esset.

12. Quarta diffusio est per rationem *dilectionis*. Necessario enim oportet, quod ubi beatitudo est, ibi *summa* sit dilectio, et per consequens dilectio *in summo*. Est autem dilectio *reflexa, connexiva, caritativa*. *Connexiva* dilectio perfectior est, qua alterum diligo, quam *reflexa*, qua me diligo; *caritativa* autem perfectior ceteris est, quae habet dilectum et condilectum: ergo haec est in divinis. Hac ergo dilectione Pater diligit Filium, et est infinitus ardor[^p382-3]. — Item, est ibi dilectio *gratuita, debita, permixta*. — Item, est ibi dilectio *pura, plena, perfecta*, ut effluens et effluxa in Filio, ut refluxa in Spiritu sancto. Ex istis rationibus omnibus fit illud speculum nobile.

13. Est autem *aliud speculum*, congregatum ex *parvis*. Unde nota, quod licet tota Trinitas sit lumen *intelligendi*, Verbum tamen naturaliter habet rationem *exprimendi*[^p382-4]. Omnis autem creatura clamat generationem aeternam, et hanc exprimunt et repraesentant duodecim generationes, quas reperimus in creaturis. Est enim primus modus generationis per *diffusionem*, secundus per *expressionem*, tertius per *propagationem*.

14. Per *diffusionem*, ut *splendoris* a luce, ut *caloris* ab igne, ut *fluminis* a fonte, ut *imbris* a nube plena sive rorida. — In prima diffusione deficit *aequalitas*, quia *splendor* non aequatur luci. — In secunda deficit *intimitas*, quia *calor* non est intimus igni sive ut informanti, sive ut originanti; est enim *accidens*[^p382-5]. — In tertia deficit *simultas*, quia fons diffundit se per partes, non totus insimul. — In quarta deficit *integritas*, quia non tota pluvia excutitur a nube, immo per guttas. — Iungas has quatuor conditiones ad unam diffusionem, quae sit *splendoris* habentis *aequalitatem, caloris* habentis *intimitatem* et *substantialitatem, rivuli* sive fontis habentis *simultatem, imbris* habentis *integritatem:* et sic habes vestigium generationis aeternae.

15. Unde Filius aliquando comparatur *splendori*, ad Hebraeos primo[^p382-6]: *Qui cum sit splendor gloriae* etc.; aliquando *flammae*, ut in rubo Moysi, ubi fuit expressa persona Filii; aliquando *fluvio* vel fonti, ut in Genesi: *Fons ascendebat de terra, et fluvius egrediebatur de loco voluptatis;* aliquando *imbri*, ut in Isaia: *Quomodo descendit imber et nix de caelo et illic ultra non revertitur, sed inebriat terram* etc., et sequitur: *Sic erit verbum meum, quod egreditur de ore meo*, scilicet de corde Patris.

16. Secundus modus est per modum *expressionis:* ut *speciei* ab obiecto, ut *imaginis* a sigillo, ut *sermonis* a loquente, ut *conceptus* sive cogitatus a mente. — Et in his est defectus. In prima deficit *rei veritas*, quia species in oculo vel in anima non est veritas rei[^p382-7]. — In secunda deficit *simplicitas*, quia imago sive figura non est in puncto vel in simplici, sed partes habet. — In tertia deficit *stabilitas*, quia sermo transit et non manet. — In quarta deficit *substantialitas*, quia conceptus mentis non est substantia, vel hypostasis. — Auferas hos defectus et pone expressionem, quae sit ut *speciei* ab obiecto habentis *veritatem*, ut *imaginis* a sigillo habentis *simplicitatem*, ut *sermonis* a loquente habentis *stabilitatem*, ut *conceptus* a mente habentis *substantialitatem:* et tunc habebis alteram partem speculi.

17. De primo, Psalmus[^p382-8]: *Eructavit cor meum verbum bonum*, et sequitur: *Speciosus forma prae filiis hominum*. — De secundo, Sapientiae septimo: *Candor est lucis aeternae et speculum sine macula et imago bonitatis illius;* et Apostolus: *Qui est imago invisibilis Dei*. — De tertio, Iob: *Semel loquitur Deus et idipsum non repetit*. Unde pone in Filio, quod semper sit et non deficit nec vertitur. — Quarto pone *substantialitatem*. Conceptus enim mentis aeternae est *hypostasis*, et iste conceptus mentalis est nobilissimus, perfectissimus. Unde in Proverbiis[^p382-9]: *Nondum erant abyssi, et ego iam concepta eram*. Aliquando ergo vocatur *filius, species, imago, verbum manens in aeternum, conceptus* vero non inhaerens, sed substantialis et hypostasis. Sic ergo habemus aliam partem speculi.

<!-- page 383 -->

18. Tertia generatio est per modum *propagationis*, et hoc quadrupliciter: ut *germinis* a semine, ut *arboris* a radice, ut *prolis* conceptae a ventre vel utero materno, ut *filii* a patre principiante. — In prima generatione deficit *formositas*, quia in semine est forma confusa et occulta; sed non sic filius. — In secunda deficit *conformitas*, licet enim sit una radix, tamen differt in forma a ramis, licet unam arborem faciant. — In tertia deficit *actualitas*, quia mater est principium, etsi aliquo modo *activum* prolis, potissime tamen *passivum*. — In quarta deficit *coaevitas*.

19. Aufer ergo hos defectus et pone primo *formositatem*. Unde Isaias[^p383-1]: *Erit germen Domini in magnificentia et gloria, et fructus terrae sublimis*. — Secundo pone *conformitatem;* sic Filius est conformis Patri, ut sit *lignum vitae in medio paradisi;* unde in Proverbiis: *Lignum vitae est his qui apprehenderint eam*. — Tertio pone *actualitatem*. Unde in Psalmo[^p383-2]: *Tecum principium in die virtutis tuae in splendoribus Sanctorum; ex utero ante luciferum genui te. Ex utero*, dicit, ubi est ratio fovendi in sinu Patris, a quo non recessit. — Quarto pone *coaevitatem*. Unde in Psalmo: *Ego hodie genui te;* hoc est in *aeternitate*, sive in meo *hodie aeterno*.

20. Has duodecim conditiones aggrega, et habebis speculum ad contuendum *exemplar* divinum sive Verbum, quod omnia repraesentat: ut *splendor* procedens a luce cum perfectione *aequalitatis*, et sic de aliis. — Et per has conditiones solvuntur omnia argumenta Arii, per quae arguit a generationibus imperfectis[^p383-3].

21. De his duodecim generationibus dicitur in Genesi[^p383-4]: *Vidi per somnium quasi solem et lunam et stellas undecim adorare me*. Iste est ille Ioseph pulcherrimus, quem adorant omnia; et clamant temporalia per *lunam*, spiritualia per *solem*, et *undecim stellae*. Generationes creaturarum sunt secundum undecim conditiones, deficiente *duodecima*, quae est in generatione Filii Dei; ideo ponuntur hic *undecim* stellae in numero deficiente a duodenario, qui est numerus *excrescens*[^p383-5], quia omnia respectu illius generationis habent defectum et inclinant se ad generationem Verbi; omnia enim indicant generationem Verbi. — Unde quando videtur splendor a luce, transferenda est mens ad considerandam generationem aeternam; et sic de aliis. — Et ibi est magna delectatio, de qua in Psalmo[^p383-6]: *Delectasti me, Domine, in factura tua; et in operibus manuum tuarum exsultabo*. Dicit ergo: *Nos autem, revelata facie gloriam Domini speculantes*.

22. Alio modo sic, et est magis proprie. Est generatio per *diffusionem* ut *splendoris* vel luminis a luce; et ibi est *coaevitas*, deficit tamen *aequalitas*, quia splendor non aequatur luci. — Item, est diffusio, ut *ignis* ab igne, et ibi est *aequalitas*, ut patet, quando una candela accenditur ab alia, quod tantum est lumen in una, sicut in alia, et quotquot accendantur; deficit tamen *consubstantialitas*. — Item, est diffusio *rivuli* a fonte, et hic est *consubstantialitas* vel intimitas, quia eadem aqua, quae est in fonte, est in rivulo[^p383-7]; sed deficit *nobilitas ortus*, quia de imo, et per quosdam meatus et colationem de aqua salsa fit dulcis. — Item, est diffusio *imbris* a nube, et ibi est *nobilitas ortus*, quia habet virtutes caeli, per quas infundit imbrem nubes; deficit tamen *formae decor*, quia videtur nubes caelum obscurare et deturpare. — Iunge quod nobilitatis est, ut *coaevitatem* et *aequalitatem, consubstantialitatem* et *formae decorem;* et habes *unam* partem speculi.

23. Item, est generatio per *expressionem* ut *speciei ab obiecto;* et hic est *formae decor*, qui deficiebat in generatione imbris. Et hoc est mirabile, quomodo talis species generatur; quia non de *materia aëris*, quia, cum non dicat *essentiam*, sed *modum essendi*, sufficit, ut habeat principium *originativum*, medium inter principium *materiale* et principium *effectivum*. Unde Deus dedit virtutem hanc cuilibet rei, ut gignat similitudinem suam et ex naturali fecunditate. Unde secundum quod obiectum *gignit similitudinem suam*, sic repraesentat generationem aeternam; secundum quod intentio animae *eam oculo unit*, sic repraesentat incarnationem Verbi[^p383-8]. — Haec generatio speciei deficit, quia non est

<!-- page 384 -->

ibi *formae fixio*, sed vario modo habet *generari*, ut patet in speculis vario modo ordinatis; et diversimode habet *repraesentare*, prope, vel longe.

24. Item, est generatio per expressionem ut *figurae* a sigillo, et hic est *formae fixio*, quod deficit in praecedenti, deficit tamen *vitae vigor*. — Item, est *generatio sermonis* a dicente, ubi est *vitae vigor*, quia sermo quandam vim vitae secum habet; ut patet, quod duobus dicentibus eandem veritatem, unus melius alio *exprimit* et *imprimit*. Unde Hieronymus[^p384-1]: « Habet nescio quid latentis energiae vivae vocis actus » etc. Unde verba Christi, non est dubium, quin, prolata et audita de ore eius, maioris virtutis et potestatis erant quam scripta. Hic tamen deficit *vitae mansio*, quia sermo transit. — Item, est *generatio conceptus a mente;* et ibi est *vitae mansio*, quia omnis cogitatus in memoria manet; sed hic deficit *fecunditatis substantialis virtus*, quia ille cogitatus non est substantialis virtus. — Haec quatuor iungantur, scilicet *formae decor, formae fixio, vitae vigor, vitae mansio*, et habes *secundam* partem speculi.

25. Item, est generatio per *propagationem* ut *germinis a semine;* et hic est virtus *substantialis*, in qua ultimum deficit, scilicet deficit *vitae robur*. — Item, est generatio ut *surculi a radice;* et hic est *vitae robur*, sed deficit *vivax sensus;* non enim sentit vel producens productum, vel productum suam originem. — Item, est generatio ut *fetus a ventre* vel utero; et hic est *vivax sensus*, sed deficit *virtus activa*, quia venter est quasi receptaculum potius quam activum. — Item, est generatio ut *filii a patre;* et ibi sunt omnes dictae conditiones praeter *unam*, scilicet lucis, quae est *coaevitas*, quae ponenda est in generatione Filii Dei; et ab ista ultima generatione, scilicet filii a patre, denominatur *generatio aeterna*. Et hoc ratificavit Christus, cum dixit[^p384-2]: *Ite, docete omnes gentes, baptizantes eos in nomine Patris et Filii et Spiritus sancti;* non dixit: in nomine *lucis* et *splendoris*.

Hae sunt *undecim stellae* adorantes Ioseph, Filium pulcherrimum, scilicet undecim conditiones nobiles iam dictae, sed duodecima, scilicet *coaevitas*, est in Filio Dei.

## English

### COLLATION XI.
#### *On the second vision: the fourth treatment, which is the second on the comeliness of faith and treats of the speculation of God as three.*

### Summarium

*Introduction. What was said of the height and the firmness of faith is recalled; now of its comeliness, which lies in the twelve speculations, 1. — Part I. On the first mirror, which is intellectual, drawn from the intellectual names of God; this is Augustine's mirror, 2–4. — But let us with Ruth glean the ears after the reapers. The mirror of the Trinity is twofold, intellectual and more material; as to the first, four grounds in God for contemplating the Trinity, 5. — First, from the threefold perfection: of origin, of order, of indivision, 6–8. — Second, from perfect production, which is of six modes, 9. — An objection is resolved, 10. — Third, from productive diffusion, which is threefold: most actual, most entire, most ultimate, 11. — Fourth, from diffusive love, which is threefold, 12. — Part II. On the second mirror, gathered out of small things: the Word naturally has the character of expressing, and twelve generations of creatures express this, in three modes: by diffusion, by expression, by propagation, 13. — The first way of applying these generations to the eternal generation; first as to the fourfold diffusion, 14. 15. — Second, as to the fourfold expression, 16. 17. — Third, as to the fourfold propagation, 18. 19. — On these twelve modes in general, 20. 21. — The second way of applying the same; first as to diffusion, 22. — Second, as to expression, 23. 24. — Third, as to propagation, 25.*

1. *But we all, beholding the glory of the Lord with open face*[^p379-6] etc. It has been said of the second vision, namely that of the understanding raised by faith, which is understood by heaven or the firmament, which is lofty, firm and comely. It has been said of its height and its firmness; now its *comeliness* must be spoken of. — For it was said: *Look up to heaven and number the stars, if thou canst*[^p380-1]; and in the Apocalypse, of the city which had *twelve gates*, and *the gates of single pearls*. There are, therefore, twelve speculations which arise out of faith; and the first is of God, that he is *first being*, of which it has been said, since the first name of God is *he who is*[^p380-2]. Now the second must be spoken of, namely that God is *three and one*.

2. This mirror Augustine frames in the fifteenth book *On the Trinity*[^p380-3], chapter four[^p380-4], where he sets down twelve conditions or names which are said of God: that he is *eternal, immortal, incorruptible, immutable, living, wise, powerful, comely, just, good, blessed, spirit*. From these he gathers the Trinity.

3. For he is *wise*[^p380-5] by wisdom, and *blessed* by blessedness, and so of the rest; and he himself and his wisdom are not other, but he is wisdom itself. There is therefore there *mind, knowledge, love;* and these three are one, because the mind itself, by understanding itself, begets knowledge of itself, and from mind and knowledge proceeds love: therefore there is there a Trinity in unity of substance. — Thus the Father is mind, the Son knowledge, the Holy Spirit love or bond; and this is the appropriation which is made to Father, Son and Holy Spirit[^p380-6].

4. These twelve he reduces[^p380-7] to three, namely to *eternity, wisdom, blessedness*, and these three to one, namely to *wisdom*, because in wisdom are eternity and blessedness. And in wisdom is *knowledge*[^p380-8] of itself, and from knowledge love; and therefore Anselm says in the *Monologion*[^p380-9]. For from this, that the highest Spirit understands himself, the Son is generated, the Holy Spirit is breathed. This is Augustine's mirror. But let us with Ruth[^p381-1] glean the ears after the reapers, following our own doctors.

5. Now the mirror of the Trinity is twofold: one *intellectual*, which makes us understand the names said of God that are *intellectual;* the second is more *material*, which is made through *transsumptive* names. — The first speculation is that the understanding raised by faith says that God is three and one, on account of four things which are in the divine *being*, namely on account of the condition of *perfection*, on account of the condition of *perfect production, of productive diffusion, of diffusive love;* and one follows from the other.

6. For there is there the highest *perfection*, from which it comes that the first principle has the perfection of *origin, of order, of indivision*. — Now an *origin* that originates is more perfect than one that does not originate, and an origin that originates one which itself originates is more perfect than one that originates what does not originate: therefore if there is there a perfect origin, it is necessary that there be a Father producing a Son who originates, and a Holy Spirit originated.

7. Likewise, on the side of *order*, thus: where there is perfect order, there is the character of *beginning, of middle*, and *of last;* otherwise the order would be diminished: therefore in the divine persons this order will be perfect. And it is an order *of origin*, not *of dignity* nor *of time* nor *of nature*[^p381-2].

8. Likewise, on the side of *indivision*. For there is a unity of *consubstantiality, of totality* or fullness, *of conformity, of individuation, of will, of inseparability*[^p381-3]. Now the unity of *consubstantiality* fails in things that are principiated, because they have not the same substance as their principle, as appears in *form without matter*[^p381-4]. — Likewise, the unity of *totality* or fullness fails in things principiated, because such a thing is not from itself nor simple. — Likewise, unity fails in *universals*, namely that of *conformity*, because it is numbered along with another; for although it be in many, it is nevertheless numbered in them. — Likewise, unity fails in *individuals*, because form is numbered along with matter[^p381-5]. — Likewise, the unity of *will* fails, because minds are not so joined but that they can be separated through discord. — Likewise, the unity of *inseparability* fails in *natures*, because the union of form is not so strong but that it can be separated by art. — These things[^p381-6], however, are to be posited in God.

9. Likewise, in the first *being* there is the character of *perfect production*. Now production is of the *like* and of the unlike, of the *equal* and of the unequal, of the *consubstantial* and of what differs essentially. — Of necessity, if there is production of the *unlike*, production of the *like* is understood beforehand; which appears thus: the *like* stands to the unlike as *the same* to the diverse, as *one* to many; but of necessity the same precedes the diverse, and one precedes many[^p381-7]: therefore the production of the like precedes the production of the unlike. But a creature is produced by the first *being* and is *unlike:* therefore of necessity a like is produced, which is God. — Likewise, according to Boethius[^p381-8], *inequalities* arise from equality: if therefore there is produced by God what is unequal, namely a creature, necessarily there precedes a production of the *equal*, which is God. — Likewise, if there is produced by God what essentially *differs*, necessarily there is produced beforehand what is *substantially the same*, because the intrinsic is before the extrinsic, as appears: because the word of the speaker, which is extrinsic, is preceded by the concept of the mind as something intrinsic: therefore likewise from the eternal substance there does not flow what differs, unless there be produced what is substantially the same: therefore in God the production of the *like, the equal, the consubstantial* is prior to that of the *unlike, the unequal, the essentially differing*.

10. But what then? you will say: does not one who understands the first *being* to be one supposit, as the gentiles did, nevertheless understand substance, power and operation? Therefore, without the trinity or emanation being understood, it is possible to understand production. I respond: the intellect is twofold: one perfect and full and *fully resolving;* and by such an intellect it is not possible to understand thus; but a defective intellect can understand *half-fully* in such a way that it resolves into several things what in God are one — otherwise not[^p381-9].

11. Likewise, the third ground of the mirror is that *in God there is the character of productive diffusion*, thus. That *being* is *most highly good*, therefore it diffuses itself most highly[^p381-10] by a threefold diffusion: *most actual, most entire, most ultimate* or ultimatemost. Because *most actual*, it always is, always was, always will be; it always generates, always generated, always will generate. A creature cannot have this, that it always is, always was, and always will be: therefore it is necessary that what emanates be *eternal*[^p381-11]. — Likewise, this diffusion is not *most entire* in a creature, because God does not give the whole beauty of his exemplarity to a creature, nay he does not give it save by generating the Son, who can say: *All things whatsoever the Father hath are mine*[^p382-1]; this no creature says. — Likewise, this diffusion is *most ultimate*, so that the producer gives whatever he can; but a creature cannot receive whatever God can give. Hence, as a point adds nothing to a line, nor even a thousand thousands of points[^p382-2], so the goodness of a creature adds nothing to the goodness of the Creator, because the finite adds nothing to the infinite. Therefore it is necessary that this diffusion according to the whole *power* be in something than which a greater cannot be thought; but than every creature something greater can be thought, and even the creature itself can be thought greater than itself. But in the Son there is production, as in the Father. If therefore nothing greater than the Father can be thought, then neither than the Son. If the Father too did not diffuse himself by an ultimate diffusion, he would not be perfect.

12. The fourth diffusion is by the character of *love*. For necessarily it behoves that where beatitude is, there be the *highest* love, and consequently love *in the highest*. Now love is *reflexive, connective, charitable*. *Connective* love, by which I love another, is more perfect than *reflexive*, by which I love myself; but *charitable* love is more perfect than the rest, which has a beloved and a co-beloved: therefore this is in the divine persons. By this love, then, the Father loves the Son, and it is an infinite ardour[^p382-3]. — Likewise, there is there love *gratuitous, due, mixed*. — Likewise, there is there love *pure, full, perfect*, as flowing forth and flowed forth in the Son, as flowing back in the Holy Spirit. Out of all these grounds that noble mirror is made.

13. But there is *another mirror*, gathered out of *small* things. Whence note that although the whole Trinity is the light of *understanding*, the Word nevertheless naturally has the character of *expressing*[^p382-4]. Now every creature cries out the eternal generation, and this the twelve generations which we find in creatures express and represent. For the first mode of generation is by *diffusion*, the second by *expression*, the third by *propagation*.

14. By *diffusion*, as of *brightness* from light, as of *heat* from fire, as of a *stream* from a spring, as of *rain* from a full or dewy cloud. — In the first diffusion *equality* fails, because *brightness* is not equalled to light. — In the second *inwardness* fails, because *heat* is not inward to fire either as informing it or as originating it; for it is an *accident*[^p382-5]. — In the third *simultaneity* fails, because a spring diffuses itself by parts, not the whole at once. — In the fourth *integrity* fails, because not the whole rain is shaken out of the cloud, but by drops. — Join these four conditions into one diffusion, which shall be that of *brightness* having *equality*, of *heat* having *inwardness* and *substantiality*, of a *stream* or spring having *simultaneity*, of *rain* having *integrity:* and so you have a vestige of the eternal generation.

15. Hence the Son is sometimes compared to *brightness*, to the Hebrews, chapter one[^p382-6]: *Who being the brightness of his glory* etc.; sometimes to a *flame*, as in the bush of Moses, where the person of the Son was expressed; sometimes to a *river* or spring, as in Genesis: *A spring rose out of the earth, and a river went out of the place of pleasure;* sometimes to *rain*, as in Isaiah: *As the rain and the snow come down from heaven and return no more thither, but soak the earth* etc., and there follows: *So shall my word be, which shall go forth from my mouth*, that is, from the heart of the Father.

16. The second mode is by way of *expression:* as of a *species* from an object, as of an *image* from a seal, as of *speech* from one speaking, as of a *concept* or thought from the mind. — And in these there is a defect. In the first *the truth of the thing* fails, because the species in the eye or in the soul is not the truth of the thing[^p382-7]. — In the second *simplicity* fails, because an image or figure is not in a point or in what is simple, but has parts. — In the third *stability* fails, because speech passes and does not remain. — In the fourth *substantiality* fails, because a concept of the mind is not a substance, or hypostasis. — Take away these defects and posit an expression which shall be as of a *species* from an object having *truth*, as of an *image* from a seal having *simplicity*, as of *speech* from a speaker having *stability*, as of a *concept* from a mind having *substantiality:* and then you will have the other part of the mirror.

17. Of the first, the Psalm[^p382-8]: *My heart hath uttered a good word*, and there follows: *Beautiful above the sons of men*. — Of the second, Wisdom, chapter seven: *She is the brightness of eternal light, and the unspotted mirror and the image of his goodness;* and the Apostle: *Who is the image of the invisible God*. — Of the third, Job: *God speaketh once, and repeateth not the selfsame thing*. Hence posit in the Son that he always is and does not fail nor is changed. — Fourth, posit *substantiality*. For the concept of the eternal mind is a *hypostasis*, and that mental concept is most noble, most perfect. Hence in Proverbs[^p382-9]: *The depths were not as yet, and I was already conceived*. Sometimes, therefore, he is called *son, species, image, a word remaining unto eternity*, but a *concept* not inhering, but substantial and a hypostasis. So then we have the other part of the mirror.

18. The third generation is by way of *propagation*, and this in four ways: as of a *shoot* from seed, as of a *tree* from a root, as of *offspring* conceived from the mother's womb, as of a *son* from a father as his beginning. — In the first generation *shapeliness* fails, because in the seed the form is confused and hidden; but not so the son. — In the second *conformity* fails, for although there be one root, it nevertheless differs in form from the branches, though they make one tree. — In the third *actuality* fails, because the mother is a principle, even if in some way *active* of the offspring, yet chiefly *passive*. — In the fourth *coaevity* fails.

19. Take away, therefore, these defects, and posit first *shapeliness*. Hence Isaiah[^p383-1]: *The bud of the Lord shall be in magnificence and glory, and the fruit of the earth shall be high*. — Second, posit *conformity;* thus the Son is conformed to the Father, so that he is *the tree of life in the midst of paradise;* whence in Proverbs: *She is a tree of life to them that lay hold on her*. — Third, posit *actuality*. Hence in the Psalm[^p383-2]: *With thee is the principality in the day of thy strength, in the brightness of the saints; from the womb before the day star I begot thee. From the womb*, he says, where there is the character of being cherished in the bosom of the Father, from whom he did not depart. — Fourth, posit *coaevity*. Hence in the Psalm: *This day have I begotten thee;* that is, in *eternity*, or in my eternal *today*.

20. Gather these twelve conditions, and you will have a mirror for gazing upon the divine *exemplar* or Word, which represents all things: as *brightness* proceeding from light with the perfection of *equality*, and so of the rest. — And by these conditions all the arguments of Arius are resolved, by which he argued from imperfect generations[^p383-3].

21. Of these twelve generations it is said in Genesis[^p383-4]: *I saw in a dream as it were the sun and the moon and eleven stars worshipping me*. This is that most beautiful Joseph, whom all things adore; and temporal things cry out by the *moon*, spiritual things by the *sun*, and the *eleven stars*. The generations of creatures are according to eleven conditions, the *twelfth* failing, which is in the generation of the Son of God; and therefore *eleven* stars are set here, in a number falling short of the twelve, which is an *abundant* number[^p383-5], because all things in respect of that generation have a defect and incline themselves to the generation of the Word; for all things indicate the generation of the Word. — Hence, when brightness is seen from light, the mind is to be carried over to considering the eternal generation; and so of the rest. — And there is great delight there, of which in the Psalm[^p383-6]: *Thou hast given me, O Lord, a delight in thy doings; and in the works of thy hands I shall rejoice*. He says, therefore: *But we, beholding the glory of the Lord with open face*.

22. In another way thus, and it is more proper. There is a generation by *diffusion*, as of *brightness* or light from light; and there is *coaevity* there, yet *equality* fails, because brightness is not equalled to light. — Likewise, there is a diffusion as of *fire* from fire, and there *equality* is present, as appears when one candle is lit from another, that there is as much light in the one as in the other, and however many be lit; yet *consubstantiality* fails. — Likewise, there is the diffusion of a *stream* from a spring, and here there is *consubstantiality* or inwardness, because the same water which is in the spring is in the stream[^p383-7]; but *nobility of origin* fails, because it is from below, and through certain channels and a straining it is made sweet out of salt water. — Likewise, there is the diffusion of *rain* from a cloud, and there is *nobility of origin*, because it has the powers of the heavens, through which the cloud pours out the rain; yet *beauty of form* fails, because the cloud is seen to darken and disfigure the heaven. — Join what is of nobility, namely *coaevity* and *equality, consubstantiality* and *beauty of form;* and you have *one* part of the mirror.

23. Likewise, there is a generation by *expression*, as of *a species from an object;* and here there is *beauty of form*, which was failing in the generation of rain. And this is wonderful, how such a species is generated; because it is not from the *matter of the air*, since, as it states not an *essence* but a *mode of being*, it suffices that it have an *originative* principle, midway between a *material* principle and an *effective* principle. Hence God gave this power to each thing, that it should beget its own likeness, and that out of natural fecundity. Hence, according as the object *begets its own likeness*, so it represents the eternal generation; according as the intention of the soul *unites it to the eye*, so it represents the incarnation of the Word[^p383-8]. — This generation of a species fails, because there is not there a *fixity of form*, but it comes to be *generated* in varying ways, as appears in mirrors arranged in varying ways; and it *represents* diversely, near or far.

24. Likewise, there is a generation by expression as of a *figure* from a seal, and here there is *fixity of form*, which fails in the preceding, yet *vigour of life* fails. — Likewise, there is the *generation of speech* from one speaking, where there is *vigour of life*, because speech has with it a certain force of life; as appears in that, when two men speak the same truth, one *expresses* and *impresses* it better than the other. Hence Jerome[^p384-1]: « The living voice has I know not what of hidden energy in its act » etc. Hence the words of Christ, there is no doubt, when uttered and heard from his mouth, were of greater power and might than when written. Here, however, *the abiding of life* fails, because speech passes. — Likewise, there is the *generation of a concept from the mind;* and there is *the abiding of life* there, because every thought remains in the memory; but here *the substantial power of fecundity* fails, because that thought is not a substantial power. — Let these four be joined, namely *beauty of form, fixity of form, vigour of life, the abiding of life*, and you have the *second* part of the mirror.

25. Likewise, there is a generation by *propagation*, as of *a shoot from seed;* and here there is *substantial* power, in which the last thing fails, namely *the strength of life* fails. — Likewise, there is a generation as of *a slip from a root;* and here there is *the strength of life*, but *living sense* fails; for neither does the producer feel the produced, nor the produced its own origin. — Likewise, there is a generation as of *offspring from the womb* or belly; and here there is *living sense*, but *active power* fails, because the womb is as it were a receptacle rather than something active. — Likewise, there is a generation as of *a son from a father;* and there are all the said conditions except *one*, namely that of light, which is *coaevity*, which is to be posited in the generation of the Son of God; and from this last generation, namely of a son from a father, the *eternal generation* takes its name. And Christ ratified this when he said[^p384-2]: *Go, teach ye all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit;* he did not say: in the name of *light* and of *brightness*.

These are the *eleven stars* worshipping Joseph, the most beautiful Son — namely the eleven noble conditions already spoken of; but the twelfth, namely *coaevity*, is in the Son of God.
