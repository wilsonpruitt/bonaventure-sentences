### § 2. *De longitudine sacrae Scripturae.*

Habet etiam haec Scriptura sacra *longitudinem*, quae consistit in descriptione tam *temporum* quam *aetatum*, a principio scilicet mundi usque ad diem iudicii. Describit autem[^p203-7] per tria *tempora* mundum decurrere, scilicet per tempus legis *naturae*, legis *scriptae* et legis *gratiae*, et in his tribus temporibus septem distinguit *aetates*. Quarum prima est ab Adam usque ad Noe, secunda a Noe usque ad Abraham, tertia ab Abraham usque ad David, quarta a David usque ad transmigrationem Babylonis, quinta a transmigratione usque ad Christum, sexta a Christo usque ad finem mundi, septima decurrit cum sexta, quae incipit a quiete Christi in sepulcro, usque ad resurrectionem universalem, quando incipiet resurrectionis octava[^p203-8]. — Et sic Scriptura est longissima, quia in tractando incipit a mundi et temporis exordio in principio Genesis, et pervenit usque ad finem mundi et temporis, scilicet in fine Apocalypsis.

<!-- page 204 -->

Recte autem universum *tempus*, quod decurrit secundum triplicem legem, scilicet inditam interius, datam exterius et desuper[^p204-1] infusam, decurrit per septem *aetates* et consummatur in fine sextae; ut sic mundi *decursus* respondeat *exordio* et *maioris* mundi decursus correspondeat decursui vitae *minoris* mundi, scilicet hominis, propter quem factus est[^p204-2]. Nam prima aetas mundi, in qua facta est ipsius mundi formatio, casus daemonum et confirmatio Angelorum, recte respondet primae diei, in qua *facta est lux* et distincta est *a tenebris*[^p204-3]. — Secunda, in qua per arcam et diluvium et salvati sunt boni et deleti mali, respondet secundae diei, in qua per firmamentum facta est distinctio *aquarum ab aquis*. — Tertia, in qua vocatus est Abraham, et inchoata synagoga, quae fructificare et generare debebat prolem ad Dei cultum, correspondet diei tertiae, in qua apparuit *terra et protulit herbam virentem*. — Quarta, in qua viguit regnum et sacerdotium, quia David rex ampliavit cultum divinum, respondet diei quartae, in qua facta est formatio *luminarium* et stellarum. — Quinta, in qua transmigrantes inter populos multos versati sunt et tribulati, respondet diei quintae, in qua facta est formatio *piscium ex aquis*[^p204-4]. — Sexta aetas, in qua natus est *Christus* in hominis effigie, qui est vere imago Dei, respondet sextae diei, in qua formatus est *homo primus*. — Septima, quae est quies animarum non habens finem, respondet septimae diei, in qua *requievit Deus ab omni opere, quod patrarat*[^p204-5].

Et sic distinguuntur hae septem aetates propter insignia facta, quae fuerunt in earum exordiis, ratione quorum respondent diebus formationis mundi. Vocatur autem prima aetas *infantia*, quia, sicut infantia tota oblivione deletur, sic illa prima aetas per diluvium est consumta[^p204-6]. — Secunda, *pueritia*; sicut enim in pueritia loqui incipimus, sic in secunda aetate facta est distinctio linguarum. — Tertia dicitur *adolescentia*, quia, sicut vis generativa incipit tunc in actum suum exire, sic tunc vocatus est Abraham, et data est ei circumcisio, et facta est promissio de semine. — Quarta dicitur *iuventus*, quia, sicut in iuventute floret hominis aetas, sic quarta aetate sub regibus floruit synagoga. — Quinta, *senectus*, quia sicut in senectute vires minuuntur, et decidit pulcritudo, sic et in transmigratione factum est de Iudaeorum sacerdotio. — Sexta aetas dicitur *senium*, quia, sicut illa est, quae copulatur cum morte, habens tamen magnam lucem sapientiae; sic sexta aetas mundi terminatur cum die iudicii, et in ea viget sapientia per doctrinam Christi.

Sic igitur totus iste mundus ordinatissimo decursu a Scriptura describitur procedere a principio usque ad finem, ad modum cuiusdam pulcherrimi carminis ordinati, ubi potest quis speculari secundum decursum temporis *varietatem, multiplicitatem* et *aequitatem, ordinem, rectitudinem* et *pulcritudinem* multorum divinorum iudiciorum, procedentium a sapientia Dei gubernante mundum[^p204-7]. Unde sicut nullus potest videre pulcritudinem carminis, nisi aspectus eius feratur super totum versum; sic nullus videt pulcritudinem ordinis et regiminis universi, nisi eam totam speculetur. Et quia nullus homo tam longaevus est, quod totam possit videre oculis carnis suae, nec futura potest per se praevidere; providit nobis Spiritus sanctus librum Scripturae sacrae, cuius longitudo commetitur se decursui regiminis universi.

## English

### § 2. *On the length of sacred Scripture.*

This sacred Scripture also has a *length*, which consists in the description both of *times* and of *ages*, namely from the beginning of the world to the day of judgment. Now it describes[^p203-7] the world as running its course through three *times*, namely through the time of the law of *nature*, of the *written* law, and of the law of *grace*; and within these three times it distinguishes seven *ages*. Of these the first is from Adam to Noah, the second from Noah to Abraham, the third from Abraham to David, the fourth from David to the transmigration of Babylon, the fifth from the transmigration to Christ, the sixth from Christ to the end of the world; the seventh runs alongside the sixth, and begins from Christ's rest in the sepulchre and lasts until the universal resurrection, when the eighth, that of the resurrection, will begin[^p203-8]. — And thus Scripture is of the greatest length, because in its treatment it begins from the beginning of the world and of time at the opening of Genesis, and reaches to the end of the world and of time, namely at the close of the Apocalypse.

And rightly does the whole of *time*, which runs its course according to a threefold law — namely the law implanted within, the law given without, and the law infused from above[^p204-1] — run through seven *ages* and reach its consummation at the end of the sixth; so that thus the *course* of the world may answer to its *beginning*, and the course of the *greater* world may correspond to the course of the life of the *lesser* world, namely of man, for whose sake it was made[^p204-2]. For the first age of the world, in which took place the formation of the world itself, the fall of the demons, and the confirmation of the Angels, rightly answers to the first day, on which *light was made* and separated *from the darkness*[^p204-3]. — The second, in which through the ark and the flood the good were saved and the evil destroyed, answers to the second day, on which through the firmament was made the separation of *the waters from the waters*. — The third, in which Abraham was called and the synagogue begun, which was to bear fruit and beget offspring for the worship of God, corresponds to the third day, on which *the earth appeared and brought forth the green herb*. — The fourth, in which kingship and priesthood flourished, since King David enlarged divine worship, answers to the fourth day, on which was made the formation of the *lights* and of the stars. — The fifth, in which those in the transmigration dwelt among many peoples and were afflicted, answers to the fifth day, on which was made the formation of *the fishes out of the waters*[^p204-4]. — The sixth age, in which *Christ* was born in the likeness of man, who is truly the image of God, answers to the sixth day, on which *the first man* was formed. — The seventh, which is the rest of souls having no end, answers to the seventh day, on which *God rested from all the work that he had done*[^p204-5].

And thus these seven ages are distinguished by reason of the notable deeds which occurred at their beginnings, on account of which they answer to the days of the world's formation. Now the first age is called *infancy*, because, as infancy is wholly blotted out by forgetfulness, so that first age was consumed by the flood[^p204-6]. — The second, *childhood*; for just as in childhood we begin to speak, so in the second age was made the division of tongues. — The third is called *adolescence*, because, as the generative power then begins to go forth into its act, so then Abraham was called, and circumcision was given to him, and the promise concerning his seed was made. — The fourth is called *youth*, because, as in youth a man's age is in flower, so in the fourth age the synagogue flourished under the kings. — The fifth, *old age*, because, as in old age strength is diminished and beauty falls away, so also in the transmigration it happened to the priesthood of the Jews. — The sixth age is called *decrepitude*, because, as that is the age which is joined with death, yet possesses a great light of wisdom, so the sixth age of the world is brought to its term with the day of judgment, and in it wisdom flourishes through the teaching of Christ.

Thus, therefore, this whole world is described by Scripture as proceeding from beginning to end in a most orderly course, after the manner of a certain most beautiful and ordered poem, where one can contemplate, according to the course of time, the *variety, manifoldness*, and *equity, order, rightness*, and *beauty* of the many divine judgments proceeding from the wisdom of God governing the world[^p204-7]. Hence, just as no one can see the beauty of a poem unless his gaze is carried over the whole verse, so no one sees the beauty of the order and governance of the universe unless he contemplates the whole of it. And because no man is so long-lived that he can see the whole of it with the eyes of his flesh, nor can he foresee future things of himself, the Holy Spirit has provided us with the book of sacred Scripture, whose length is commensurate with the course of the governance of the universe.
