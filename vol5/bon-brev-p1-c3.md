## Latin
<!-- page 211 -->

### Capitulum III. *De istius fidei intelligentia sana.*

Ad istius autem fidei intelligentiam sanam docet doctrina sacra, quod in divinis sunt *duae emanationes, tres hypostases, quatuor relationes, quinque notiones*, et ex his tantum *tres proprietates personales*[^p211-6].

Ratio autem ad intelligentiam praedictorum haec est: quia, cum *primum* et *summum* principium, hoc ipso quod *primum*, sit *simplicissimum*; hoc ipso quod *summum*, sit *perfectissimum*: ideo perfectissime se communicat, quia *perfectissimum*; et indivisionem omnimodam servat, hoc ipso quod *simplicissimum*: et ideo, salva unitate naturae, sunt ibi modi emanandi perfecte[^p211-7]. Modi autem emanandi perfecte sunt duo tantum, scilicet per modum *naturae* et *voluntatis*. Primus est *generatio*, secundus *spiratio* sive processio, et ideo hi sunt ibi.

Et quia duabus emanationibus substantificis necesse est emanare duas hypostases, necesse est

<!-- page 212 -->

etiam ponere, hypostasim primo producentem ab alio[^p212-1] non emanare, ne sit in infinitum abire: ideo sunt ibi tres hypostases.

Et quia cuilibet emanationi respondet duplex habitudo relativa, ideo sunt ibi *quatuor relationes*, scilicet paternitas, filiatio, spiratio et processio.

Qui vero per has habitudines innotescunt nobis divinae hypostases; et praeter hoc etiam hypostasis illa, in qua est prima ratio principiandi, innotescit, quia non producitur — nam hoc est nobilitatis in ipsa — ideo *quinque sunt notiones*, scilicet quatuor relationes praedictae cum *innascibilitate*.

Et quia quaelibet personarum unam habet proprietatem, per quam principaliter innotescit; ideo tres tantum sunt *proprietates personales*, quae his nominibus exprimuntur proprie et principaliter, scilicet Pater et Filius et Spiritus sanctus.

Cum enim proprium sit Patris esse *innascibilem* sive ingenitum, esse *principium non de principio* et esse *Patrem*; *innascibilitas* notificat ipsum per modum negationis, licet ex consequenti per modum positionis, quia innascibilitas in Patre ponit fontalem plenitudinem; *principium non de principio*, per modum positionis cum negatione; *esse Patrem*, per modum positionis et habitudinis, proprie, complete et determinate[^p212-2].

Similiter, cum Filius sit *imago, verbum* et *filius*; *imago* nominat illam personam ut similitudinem *expressam*; *verbum*, ut similitudinem *expressivam*; *filius*, ut similitudinem *hypostaticam*; rursus *imago*, ut similitudinem *conformem*; *verbum*, ut similitudinem *intellectualem*; *filius*, ut similitudinem *connaturalem*[^p212-3].

Per hunc modum, cum proprium sit Spiritus sancti esse *donum*, esse *nexum* seu *caritatem* amborum, esse etiam *Spiritum sanctum*; *donum* nominat ipsum ut datum *voluntarium*; *caritas* sive nexus, ut datum *voluntarium et praecipuum*; *Spiritus sanctus*, ut datum *voluntarium, praecipuum* et *hypostaticum*[^p212-4]. — Hinc est, quod per haec tria nomina, quae sunt Pater et Filius et Spiritus sanctus, trium personarum proprietates personales insinuantur. — Haec igitur tenenda sunt ad fidei Trinitatis intelligentiam sanam.

## English

### Chapter III. *On the sound understanding of this faith.*

Now for the sound understanding of this faith, sacred doctrine teaches that in the divine there are *two emanations, three hypostases, four relations, five notions*, and from these only *three personal properties*[^p211-6].

Now the reason for the understanding of the aforesaid is this: since the *first* and *highest* principle, by the very fact that it is *first*, is *most simple*; and by the very fact that it is *highest*, is *most perfect*: therefore it communicates itself most perfectly, because it is *most perfect*; and it preserves an altogether undivided character, by the very fact that it is *most simple*: and therefore, the unity of nature being preserved, there are in it modes of emanating perfectly[^p211-7]. But the modes of emanating perfectly are only two, namely by way of *nature* and of *will*. The first is *generation*, the second *spiration* or procession; and therefore these are there.

And because from two substance-producing emanations two hypostases must necessarily emanate, it is necessary

also to posit that the hypostasis first producing does not emanate from another[^p212-1], lest there be a going off into infinity: therefore there are in it three hypostases.

And because to each emanation there corresponds a twofold relative disposition, therefore there are in it *four relations*, namely paternity, filiation, spiration, and procession.

But those by which the divine hypostases become known to us are these dispositions; and besides this, that hypostasis also becomes known in which is the first ground of originating, because it is not produced — for this belongs to its nobility — therefore *there are five notions*, namely the four aforesaid relations together with *innascibility*.

And because each of the persons has one property through which it principally becomes known; therefore there are only three *personal properties*, which are expressed properly and principally by these names, namely Father and Son and Holy Spirit.

For since it is proper to the Father to be *innascible* or unbegotten, to be *a principle not from a principle*, and to be *Father*; *innascibility* makes him known by way of negation, though consequently by way of position, because innascibility posits in the Father a fontal plenitude; *a principle not from a principle*, by way of position with negation; *to be Father*, by way of position and disposition, properly, completely, and determinately[^p212-2].

Likewise, since the Son is *image, word*, and *son*; *image* names that person as an *expressed* likeness; *word*, as an *expressive* likeness; *son*, as a *hypostatic* likeness; and again *image*, as a *conformed* likeness; *word*, as an *intellectual* likeness; *son*, as a *connatural* likeness[^p212-3].

In this manner, since it is proper to the Holy Spirit to be *gift*, to be the *bond* or *charity* of both, and to be also *Holy Spirit*; *gift* names him as something given *voluntarily*; *charity* or bond, as something given *voluntarily and pre-eminently*; *Holy Spirit*, as something given *voluntarily, pre-eminently*, and *hypostatically*[^p212-4]. — Hence it is that through these three names, which are Father and Son and Holy Spirit, the personal properties of the three persons are conveyed. — These things, then, are to be held for the sound understanding of faith in the Trinity.
