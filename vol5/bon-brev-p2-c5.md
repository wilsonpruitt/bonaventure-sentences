### Capitulum V. *De modo describendi praedicta in sacra Scriptura.*

Ex iam dictis colligitur, quod, sicut Deus res ordinate *condidit* quantum ad tempus et ordinate *disposuit* quantum ad situm; sic etiam ordinate *gubernat* quantum ad influentiam; et Scriptura ordinate *narrat* quantum ad doctrinae sufficientiam; licet non ita explicite describat distinctionem orbium nec caelestium nec elementarium, parum aut nihil dicat de motibus et virtutibus corporum superiorum et de mixtionibus elementorum et elementatorum, et quod plus est, nihil explicite narret de conditione supernorum spirituum, maxime cum describit istud universum in *esse* productum.

Ratio autem ad intelligentiam praedictorum haec est: quia, cum primum principium reddat se nobis cognoscibile et per Scripturam et per creaturam, per librum creaturae se manifestat ut principium *effectivum*, per librum Scripturae ut principium *reparativum*; et quia principium *reparativum* non potest cognosci, nisi cognoscatur et *effectivum*: ideo sacra Scriptura, licet principaliter agat de operibus reparationis, agere nihilominus debet de opere conditionis, in quantum tamen ducit in cognitionem primi principii efficientis et reficientis; et ideo ipsa est cognitio *sublimis* et *salutaris*: *sublimis*, quia de principio[^p222-3] *effectivo*, quod est Deus creator; *salutaris*, quia de principio *reparativo*, quod est Christus salvator et mediator.

Rursus, quia *sublimis* est, utpote quia agit de primo principio et ente summo; ideo non descendit ad describendas speciales entium naturas, motus, virtutes et differentias; sed stat in generalitate quadam, in qua implicantur specialia, describendo scilicet conditionem mundi quantum ad dispositionem et influentiam quoad naturam luminosam, opacam et perviam in generalitate quadam.

Et quoniam primum principium, de quo agit, habet in se *ordinem naturae* in existendo, *ordinem sapientiae* in disponendo, *ordinem bonitatis* in influendo: ita quod *ordo naturae* habet simultatem et aequalitatem[^p222-4]; *ordo sapientiae* considerat prioritatem et posterioritatem; *ordo influentiae* superioritatem et inferioritatem: ideo ad insinuandum *ordinem naturae* determinat Scriptura, iuxta quod Deum operari decebat: quod *in principio*, ante temporis decursum, fuerit illa triplex natura de *non-esse* in *esse* producta, cum dicit[^p222-5]: *In principio creavit Deus*

<!-- page 223 -->

*caelum et terram; et Spiritus Dei ferebatur super aquas*. Ubi nomine *caeli* insinuatur natura luminosa; nomine *terrae*, opaca; nomine *aquae*, pervia sive perspicua, sive contrarietati subiecta, sive supra contrarietatem elevata. — Ubi etiam insinuatur Trinitas aeterna, scilicet Pater in nomine *Dei creantis*, Filius in nomine *principii*, Spiritus sanctus in nomine *Spiritus Dei*. — Et sic intelligendum est illud quod dicitur[^p223-1]: *Qui vivit in aeternum creavit omnia simul*; non quia ea creaverit in chaos omnimodae confusionis, secundum quod finxerunt poëtae, cum produxerit hanc triplicem naturam, *summam* in summo, *mediam* in medio et *infimam* in infimo; nec etiam in *esse* omnimodae distinctionis, cum *caelum* esset perfectum, et *terra incomposita*[^p223-2], natura *media*, quasi medium tenens, nondum esset ad perfectam distinctionem deducta.

Ad insinuandum autem *ordinem sapientiae* in disponendo determinat, quod haec triplex natura non *simul* fuit *distincta* et *ornata*, sed iuxta triplicis naturae creatae exigentiam per triduum *distincta* et per triduum aliud *ornata*; ut, sicut Deus in principio triplicem naturam simul creavit in primordio temporis, sic successione temporis in triplici mensura temporis, scilicet triplicis diei, triplicem faceret distinctionem naturae triplicis creatae; et rursus in alio triduo, triplicem ornatum triplicis naturae distinctae.

Ad insinuandum vero ordinem *bonitatis* in influendo determinat, quod haec triplex natura collocata est in mundo secundum *sub*[^p223-3] et *supra* iuxta suam *dignitatem* et *influentiam*. — Et quia *luminosum* plurimum habet de specie, ideo sibi debetur *circumferentia*; quia *opacum* minimum habet de specie, ideo sibi debetur *centrum*; quia vero[^p223-4] *pervia* tenet medium, ideo *medium* sortita est *situm*. Et quoniam natura *pervii et perspicui* communis est naturae caelesti et elementari, et rursus *luminosa* utrique convenit: ideo recte dicitur firmamentum factum *in medio aquarum*[^p223-5], non quia aquae supra caelos sint aquae fluxibiles, frigidae, graves et corruptibiles; sed quia subtiles et incorruptibiles, perviae et super omnem contrarietatem sublimatae, ac per hoc caelestis naturae et in caelestibus collocandae ratione *nobilitatis formae*.

Collocantur etiam ratione *virtutis* et *influentiae*. Quia enim omnis actio corporalis in rebus inferioribus regulam, originem et vigorem sumit a natura caelesti; cum duae sint qualitates activae, scilicet *calidum* et *frigidum*[^p223-6], et aliquod sit caelum principaliter influens et *calidum*, utpote caelum *sidereum* ratione luminositatis: congruum fuit, quod aliquod influeret super *frigidum*, et sic *crystallinum*. — Et sicut caelum *sidereum*, licet influat ad calorem, non tamen est formaliter calidum; sic etiam caelum, quod dicitur *aqueum* sive crystallinum, non est essentialiter frigidum. — Unde quod Sancti dicunt, quod aquae sunt ibi constitutae ad reprimendum calorem superiorum corporum et cetera similia, non secundum formalem praedicationem, sed secundum efficientiam et influentiam sunt intelligenda. — Competit ergo conditio creaturae secundum ordinem praedictum ordini creatricis sapientiae et divinae Scripturae, quia est scientia *sublimis*.

Rursus, quia ipsa est scientia *salutaris*; ideo non determinat de opere conditionis nisi propter opus reparationis. Et quoniam Angeli sic conditi sunt, ut labentes nullatenus reparentur, sicut apparebit in sequentibus[^p223-7]; ideo tacetur secundum litteram exteriorem Angelorum lapsus et conditio, quia non debebat subsequi reparatio.

Quia vero *sublimitatem* Scripturae non decebat prorsus reticere de conditione sublimissimae creaturae; ideo sic sacra Scriptura describit rerum conditionem, iuxta quod exigit scientia *sublimis* et *salutaris*; ut tamen secundum *spiritualem* intelligentiam tota conditio *litteraliter* descripta *spiritualiter* referatur ad describendam hierarchiam angelicam et ecclesiasticam: ideo secundum *spiritualem* intellectum describitur in illis tribus naturis primo productis hierarchia *angelica* nomine *caeli*[^p223-8], et *ecclesiastica* nomine *terrae*, et *gratia*, per quam irrigatur utraque, nomine *aquae*.

Rursus, per *septenarium* dierum intelligitur septiformis Ecclesiae status secundum decursum septem aetatum[^p223-9]. Per eundem etiam septenarium intel-

<!-- page 224 -->

ligitur septiformis Angelorum conversio a creatura ad Deum. — Et sic ex praedictis apparet sufficientia et veritas Scripturae in diversis opinionibus Sanctorum, scilicet Augustini et aliorum, quae sibi non contradicunt, cum verae sint, si recte intelligantur.

## English

### Chapter V. *On the manner of describing the foresaid in sacred Scripture.*

From what has already been said it is gathered that, just as God *founded* things in order as regards time, and *disposed* them in order as regards place, so also he *governs* them in order as regards influence; and Scripture *narrates* them in order as regards the sufficiency of doctrine — although it does not so explicitly describe the distinction of the orbs, neither the celestial nor the elemental, says little or nothing of the motions and powers of the higher bodies and of the mixtures of the elements and of things composed of elements, and, what is more, narrates nothing explicitly of the founding of the higher spirits, especially when it describes this universe as produced into *being*.

Now the reason for the understanding of the foresaid is this: that since the first principle renders itself knowable to us both through Scripture and through the creature, through the book of the creature it manifests itself as the *effective* principle, and through the book of Scripture as the *reparative* principle; and because the *reparative* principle cannot be known unless the *effective* also be known, therefore sacred Scripture, although it treats principally of the works of reparation, must nonetheless treat of the work of founding, insofar however as this leads into the knowledge of the first principle that effects and restores; and therefore it is itself a knowledge *sublime* and *salutary*: *sublime*, because it is of the *effective* principle[^p222-3], which is God the creator; *salutary*, because it is of the *reparative* principle, which is Christ the saviour and mediator.

Again, because it is *sublime*, inasmuch as it treats of the first principle and the highest being, therefore it does not descend to describing the particular natures, motions, powers and differences of beings; but it stands within a certain generality, in which the particulars are implied — describing, namely, the founding of the world as regards its disposition and influence, with respect to the luminous, the opaque and the pervious nature, within a certain generality.

And since the first principle of which it treats has in itself the *order of nature* in existing, the *order of wisdom* in disposing, and the *order of goodness* in influencing — so that the *order of nature* has simultaneity and equality[^p222-4]; the *order of wisdom* regards priority and posteriority; the *order of influence*, superiority and inferiority — therefore, to intimate the *order of nature*, Scripture determines, according to what it befitted God to work, that *in the beginning*, before the course of time, that threefold nature was produced from *non-being* into *being*, when it says[^p222-5]: *In the beginning God created*

<!-- page 223 -->

*heaven and earth; and the Spirit of God was borne over the waters*. Where by the name of *heaven* the luminous nature is intimated; by the name of *earth*, the opaque; by the name of *water*, the pervious or transparent, whether subject to contrariety or raised above contrariety. — Where also the eternal Trinity is intimated: namely the Father in the name of *God creating*, the Son in the name of *beginning*, the Holy Spirit in the name of the *Spirit of God*. — And thus is to be understood that which is said[^p223-1]: *He who lives for ever created all things together*; not because he created them in a chaos of every kind of confusion, according to what the poets have feigned, since he produced this threefold nature, the *highest* in the highest place, the *middle* in the middle, and the *lowest* in the lowest; nor yet in a *being* of every kind of distinction, since *heaven* was perfect, while *earth was without form*[^p223-2], the *middle* nature, as it were holding the middle, had not yet been brought to perfect distinction.

But to intimate the *order of wisdom* in disposing, it determines that this threefold nature was not *at once* both *distinguished* and *adorned*, but, according to the requirement of the threefold created nature, was *distinguished* through three days and *adorned* through another three; so that, just as God in the beginning created the threefold nature at once at the first moment of time, so by a succession of time, in the threefold measure of time — namely of the threefold day — he might make the threefold distinction of the threefold created nature; and again, in another three days, the threefold adornment of the threefold distinguished nature.

But to intimate the order of *goodness* in influencing, it determines that this threefold nature was placed in the world according to *below*[^p223-3] and *above*, in keeping with its own *dignity* and *influence*. — And because the *luminous* has most of species, therefore the *circumference* is due to it; because the *opaque* has least of species, therefore the *centre* is due to it; but because[^p223-4] the *pervious* holds the middle, therefore it was allotted the *middle* place. And since the nature of the *pervious and transparent* is common to the celestial and the elemental nature, and again the *luminous* belongs to both, therefore the firmament is rightly said to have been made *in the midst of the waters*[^p223-5] — not because the waters above the heavens are flowing, cold, heavy and corruptible waters, but because they are subtle and incorruptible, pervious and raised above all contrariety, and through this of a celestial nature and to be placed among celestial things by reason of the *nobility of their form*.

They are placed there also by reason of *power* and *influence*. For since every corporeal action in lower things takes its rule, origin and vigour from the celestial nature — since there are two active qualities, namely the *hot* and the *cold*[^p223-6], and there is some heaven principally influencing and *hot*, namely the *starry* heaven by reason of its luminosity — it was fitting that some heaven should exert influence over the *cold*, and thus the *crystalline*. — And just as the *starry* heaven, although it exerts influence toward heat, is nevertheless not formally hot, so also the heaven which is called *watery* or crystalline is not essentially cold. — Hence what the Saints say, that the waters are there established to restrain the heat of the higher bodies, and other like things, are to be understood not according to formal predication but according to efficiency and influence. — The founding of the creature according to the foresaid order therefore corresponds to the order of the creating wisdom and of divine Scripture, because it is a *sublime* science.

Again, because it is itself a *salutary* science, therefore it determines nothing of the work of founding except for the sake of the work of reparation. And since the Angels were so founded that, once fallen, they are in no way restored — as will appear in what follows[^p223-7] — therefore the fall and the founding of the Angels is passed over in silence according to the outward letter, because no reparation was to follow.

But because it did not befit the *sublimity* of Scripture to be wholly silent about the founding of the most sublime creature, therefore sacred Scripture so describes the founding of things as the *sublime* and *salutary* science requires; yet in such a way that, according to the *spiritual* understanding, the whole founding described *literally* may be referred *spiritually* to the describing of the angelic and the ecclesiastical hierarchy. Therefore according to the *spiritual* sense there is described, in those three natures first produced, the *angelic* hierarchy under the name of *heaven*[^p223-8], and the *ecclesiastical* under the name of *earth*, and *grace*, by which both are watered, under the name of *water*.

Again, through the *sevenfold* number of days is understood the sevenfold state of the Church according to the course of the seven ages[^p223-9]. Through the same sevenfold number is also under-

<!-- page 224 -->

stood the sevenfold conversion of the Angels from the creature to God. — And thus from the foresaid there appears the sufficiency and the truth of Scripture amid the diverse opinions of the Saints, namely of Augustine and of others, which do not contradict one another, since they are true if they be rightly understood.
