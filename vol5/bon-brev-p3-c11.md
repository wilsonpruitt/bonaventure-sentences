## Latin
<!-- page 240 -->

### Capitulum XI. *De origine peccatorum finalium, quae sunt peccata in Spiritum sanctum.*

De peccatis autem *finalibus* sive irremissibilibus, cuiusmodi sunt peccata in Spiritum sanctum, haec tenenda sunt, quod licet omne peccatum *generaliter* sit contra Deum trinum et unum, *appropriate* tamen dicitur aliquod peccatum esse *in Patrem*, aliquod *in Filium*, aliquod *in Spiritum sanctum*. — Hoc autem peccatum in Spiritum sanctum *irremissibile* dicitur in hoc saeculo et in futuro[^p240-1], non quia in hoc saeculo non possit dimitti, sed quia raro dimittitur aut vix in hoc saeculo quantum ad *culpam*, modicum autem et quasi nihil fiet sibi remissio in futuro quantum ad *poenam*. — Huius autem peccati sex sunt differentiae, scilicet *invidia fraternae gratiae, impugnatio veritatis agnitae, desperatio, praesumtio, obstinatio mentis* et *impoenitentia finalis*.

Ratio autem ad intelligentiam praedictorum haec est: quia, cum peccatum dicat recessum a primo principio trino et uno, omne peccatum imaginem Trinitatis deformat et ipsam animam foedat quantum ad triplicem potentiam, scilicet irascibilem, rationalem et concupiscibilem; et a libero arbitrio procedit, quod gerit in se insigne Trinitatis: Patris, ratione *facultatis*; Filii, quia *rationis*; Spiritus sancti, quia *voluntatis*[^p240-2].

Licet autem haec tria concurrant simul ad omnem culpam, quaelibet tamen harum per suum defectum potest esse ratio deordinandi alias. Defectus autem circa *facultatem* est impotentia, circa *rationem* est ignorantia, circa *voluntatem* est malitia. Et hinc est, quod cum quaedam sint peccata ex *impotentia*, quaedam ex *ignorantia*, quaedam ex *malitia*; et potentia attribuatur Patri, sapientia Filio et voluntas Spiritui sancto[^p240-3]; quod quaedam dicuntur esse *in Patrem*, quaedam *in Filium*, quaedam *in Spiritum sanctum*. Et quoniam nihil magis est in voluntate quam ipsa voluntas[^p240-4]; et voluntas ipsa est origo peccati: nullum peccatum est adeo voluntarium et mere, sicut illud quod provenit ex corruptione existente in *voluntate*. — Cum enim dupliciter dicatur involuntarium, scilicet per *violentiam* et per *ignorantiam*: primum per defectum potentiae, secundum per defectum scientiae[^p240-5]; quando voluntas sola corruptione sua, licet possit resistere et sciat, hoc malum esse, aliquid eligit; tunc dicitur peccare *ex certa malitia*; et tale peccatum mere procedit ex improbitate voluntatis liberi arbitrii et directe impugnat gratiam Spiritus sancti. — Et quia mere procedit ex libertate arbitrii, ideo non habet colorem excusationis, et propterea modicum et quasi nihil debet ei qui punitur, de *poena*[^p240-6] relaxari. Quia vero directe impugnat gratiam Spiritus sancti, per quam fit remissio peccati; ideo dicitur *irremissibile*, non quia nullo modo possit remitti, sed quia, quantum est de se, directe est impugnativum medicamenti et remedii, per quod fieri habet remissio peccati.

Et quoniam remissio peccati fit a Deo per gratiam poenitentialem intra ecclesiasticam unitatem; ideo differentiae huius peccati accipiuntur, secundum quod directe impugnant illa tria. Aut enim impugnant ipsam gratiam poenitentialem *in se*; aut *in comparatione ad Deum*, a quo datur; aut *in comparatione ad Ecclesiam*, in qua suscipitur. Si *in comparatione ad unitatem Ecclesiae*; sic, quia unitas Ecclesiae consistit in fide et caritate, sive in *gratia et veritate*[^p240-7]: sic est duplex peccatum, scilicet *invidia fraternae gratiae* et *impugnatio veritatis agnitae*. — Si in *comparatione ad Deum* dantem; cum *universae viae eius* quantum ad iustificationem maxime sint *misericordia et veritas*[^p240-8], sic est duplex peccatum: unum scilicet, quod impugnat misericordiam, et hoc est *desperatio*; aliud, quod impugnat iustitiam, et hoc est *impunitatis praesumtio*. — Si vero impugnat ipsam gratiam poenitentialem *in se* vel *secundum se*, sic est duplex: quia gratia poenitentialis facit *resilire* a commissis[^p240-9] et *praecavere* a committendis. Contra primum est *obstinatio mentis*, contra secundum est *impoenitentia finalis*, secundum quod *finalis impoenitentia* dicitur propositum non poenitendi; sic enim est species peccati in Spiritum sanctum. Secundum autem quod *finalis impoenitentia* dicit continuationem peccati usque in finem; sic est sequela omnium mortalium, quae in hac vita non remittuntur, et maxime omnium specierum peccati in Spiritum sanctum.

<!-- page 241 -->

Et sic omne peccatum *initium* sumit a superbia[^p241-1] et *consummationem* sive finem habet in finali impoenitentia; in quam qui pervenerit corruit in gehennam, a qua nullus peccans mortaliter potest liberari, nisi interveniat gratia mediatoris Christi. Et ideo incarnationem ipsius desiderabat universitas salvandorum, cui mediatori Domino nostro omnis *honor et gloria in saecula saeculorum. Amen*[^p241-2].

## English

### Chapter XI. *On the origin of the final sins, which are the sins against the Holy Spirit.*

Now concerning the *final* or irremissible sins, of which sort are the sins against the Holy Spirit, these things are to be held: that although every sin is *generally* against God three and one, nevertheless *by appropriation* some sin is said to be *against the Father*, some *against the Son*, some *against the Holy Spirit*. — But this sin against the Holy Spirit is called *irremissible* in this world and in the world to come[^p240-1] — not because it cannot be remitted in this world, but because it is rarely remitted, or scarcely, in this world as regards *fault*, while little and almost no remission will be made for it in the world to come as regards *penalty*. — Now of this sin there are six differentiae, namely *envy of a brother's grace, impugning of the acknowledged truth, despair, presumption, obstinacy of mind* and *final impenitence*.

Now the reason for the understanding of the foresaid is this: since sin bespeaks a withdrawal from the first principle, three and one, every sin deforms the image of the Trinity and defiles the soul itself as regards its threefold power, namely the irascible, the rational and the concupiscible; and it proceeds from free choice, which bears in itself the mark of the Trinity — of the Father, by reason of *faculty*; of the Son, because of *reason*; of the Holy Spirit, because of *will*[^p240-2].

But although these three concur together in every fault, nevertheless each of them through its own defect can be the ground of disordering the others. Now the defect concerning *faculty* is impotence, that concerning *reason* is ignorance, that concerning *will* is malice. And hence it is that, since some sins are from *impotence*, some from *ignorance*, some from *malice*; and since power is attributed to the Father, wisdom to the Son and will to the Holy Spirit[^p240-3]; therefore some are said to be *against the Father*, some *against the Son*, some *against the Holy Spirit*. And since nothing is more in the will than the will itself[^p240-4]; and the will itself is the origin of sin: no sin is so voluntary and so purely voluntary as that which comes forth from a corruption existing in the *will*. — For since the involuntary is said in two ways, namely by *violence* and by *ignorance* — the first through a defect of power, the second through a defect of knowledge[^p240-5] — when the will, by its own corruption alone, although it is able to resist and knows this to be evil, chooses something; then it is said to sin *from settled malice*; and such a sin proceeds purely from the wickedness of the will of free choice, and directly impugns the grace of the Holy Spirit. — And because it proceeds purely from the liberty of choice, therefore it has no color of excuse, and on that account little and almost nothing ought to be relaxed of the *penalty*[^p240-6] for him who is punished. But because it directly impugns the grace of the Holy Spirit, through which the remission of sin comes about; therefore it is called *irremissible*, not because it can in no way be remitted, but because, so far as lies in itself, it is directly destructive of the medicine and the remedy through which the remission of sin has to come about.

And since the remission of sin is brought about by God through penitential grace within ecclesiastical unity; therefore the differentiae of this sin are taken according as they directly impugn those three. For either they impugn penitential grace itself *in itself*; or *in comparison to God*, by whom it is given; or *in comparison to the Church*, in which it is received. If *in comparison to the unity of the Church*: then, because the unity of the Church consists in faith and charity, or in *grace and truth*[^p240-7], the sin is twofold, namely *envy of a brother's grace* and *impugning of the acknowledged truth*. — If in *comparison to God* who gives: then, since *all his ways*, as regards justification, are chiefly *mercy and truth*[^p240-8], the sin is twofold — one, namely, which impugns mercy, and this is *despair*; the other, which impugns justice, and this is *presumption of impunity*. — But if it impugns penitential grace itself *in itself* or *according to itself*, it is twofold: because penitential grace makes one *recoil* from things committed[^p240-9] and *beware* of things to be committed. Against the first is *obstinacy of mind*, against the second is *final impenitence* — according as *final impenitence* is called the resolve of not repenting; for so it is a species of the sin against the Holy Spirit. But according as *final impenitence* means the continuation of sin unto the end; so it is the sequel of all mortal sins which are not remitted in this life, and most of all of all the species of the sin against the Holy Spirit.

<!-- page 241 -->

And thus every sin takes its *beginning* from pride[^p241-1] and has its *consummation*, or end, in final impenitence; and whoever comes to that falls headlong into hell, from which no one sinning mortally can be freed unless the grace of the mediator Christ intervene. And therefore the whole company of those to be saved longed for his incarnation — to whom, our Lord and mediator, be all *honor and glory unto the ages of ages. Amen*[^p241-2].
