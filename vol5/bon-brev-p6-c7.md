## Latin

### Capitulum VII. *De constitutione et integritate baptismi.*

Iam nunc septimo loco restat videre de *integritate* uniuscuiusque[^p271-5] Sacramenti; quae cum sint in numero septiformi, primo dicendum est de *integritate baptismi*, qui est ianua aliorum Sacramentorum.

De *integritate* igitur Sacramenti *baptismi* hoc tenendum est, quod ad hoc, quod aliquis baptizetur vere et plene, requiritur *expressio formae* vocalis, a Domino institutae[^p271-6], quae est haec: Ego te baptizo in nomine Patris et Filii et Spiritus sancti, Amen; absque omissione vocabuli et sine interpositione vocabuli, absque praeposteratione ordinis supradicti et sine commutatione nominis praenotati. — Requiritur etiam *mersio* vel ablutio per *elementum aquae* in toto corpore, vel saltem in digniori parte, ita quod *expressio* et *mersio* fiat ab uno et eodem et in eodem tempore. — Quibus concurrentibus, si non sit *fictio* in baptizando, datur ei gratia *regenerans, rectificans* et *purificans* ab omni culpa. — Quod ut efficacius fiat, *catechismus* et *exorcismus* tanquam praeparatorii praemittuntur tam in parvulis quam in adultis; ita tamen, quod in *adultis* requiritur *fides propria*, in *parvulis* autem sufficit *fides aliena*[^p271-7].

Ratio autem ad intelligentiam praedictorum haec est: quia, cum principium nostrum reparativum, Verbum scilicet incarnatum, tanquam perfectissimum et sufficientissimum principium reparare habeat genus humanum per medicamenta Sacramentorum, ita quod nihil sit in eis superfluum, nihil inordinatum, nihil etiam diminutum[^p271-8]; ita disponere debuit, quod Sacramentum baptismi et alia integrarentur, secundum quod exigebat *sua virtus, nostra salus* et etiam *noster morbus*. — Quoniam ergo *virtus* nos
<!-- page 272 -->
reparans est virtus *totius Trinitatis*, quam sancta mater Ecclesia credit in animo, confitetur in verbo et profitetur in signo, sub trium personarum distinctione et proprietate, ordine et origine naturali; est etiam virtus *passionis Christi*, qui mortuus et sepultus fuit et *die tertia resurrexit*[^p272-1]: hinc est, quod ad horum expressionem in Sacramento, quod primum omnium Sacramentorum est, et in quo primo et principaliter haec virtus operatur, debuit fieri expressio *Trinitatis* in distincta, propria et ordinata nominatione, quantum est de forma communi, licet in tempore primitivae Ecclesiae potuerit fieri *in nomine Christi*[^p272-2], in quo intellectus clauditur Trinitatis. Debuit etiam fieri propria et ordinata nominatio verbi *baptizandi* cum trina mersione ad expressionem mortis Christi et sepulturae et resurrectionis post triduum factae, quantum est *de congruitate*. Et quia simul operatur utraque virtus et in uno Christo Salvatore, utrumque horum debet fieri ab *uno* et eodem simul et in eodem tempore, propter servandam unitatem in Sacramento et significandam unitatem in nostro Mediatore[^p272-3].

Rursus, quoniam *salus nostra* requirebat inchoari per regenerationem seu renovationem in *esse* gratiae conferentis spirituale *esse*, abstergendo *immunditiam*, effugando *tenebras* et refrigerando *concupiscentiam*, quae universaliter labefactat[^p272-4] omnem hominem descendentem seminaliter ab Adam; hinc est, quod Sacramentum primum, quod est regenerativum, debuit fieri in elemento, quod haberet conformitatem ex naturali repraesentatione ad triplicem effectum praedictum gratiae inchoantis nostram salutem. Quoniam ergo aqua sua puritate *mundat*, sua pervietate *luminis est delativa*, sua frigiditate *refrigerat*; et maxime communis est omnibus inter omnia liquida: hinc est, quod Sacramentum regenerationis nostrae perfici debuit in elemento *aquae indifferenter*; quia « omnis aqua omni aquae est eadem specie[^p272-5] », et etiam, ne aliquis propter defectum elementi periculum salutis posset incurrere.

Postremo, quoniam *morbus noster*, contra quem principaliter est baptismus, est originale peccatum, quod animam privat vita gratiae et omnium virtutum rectitudine habilitante et pronam quodam modo reddit ad omne genus culpae, et traducitur aliunde et « parvulum facit concupiscibilem et adultum actu concupiscentem », redigit etiam in diabolicam servitutem et in potestatem principis tenebrarum[^p272-6]: hinc est, quod ad hoc, quod per illud Sacramentum per contrarium sufficiens adhibeatur remedii medicamentum, congruit, in eo dari gratiam *regenerantem* contra privationem vitae gratuitae, gratiam *rectificantem* in septiformi virtute contra privationem virtutis habilitativae, gratiam *purificantem* ab omni culpa contra pronitatem ad omnem deordinationem vitiosam.

Et quoniam originale peccatum trahitur aliunde et parvulum facit concupiscibilem et adultum actu concupiscentem, necessaria est *adulto fides propria* et *poenitentia*, sed *parvulo* sufficit *aliena*, quae scilicet est in universali Ecclesia. Quia vero baptismus debet eruere de servitute diaboli et de potestate principis tenebrarum[^p272-7] tam parvulos quam adultos; hinc est, quod utrique *exorcizari* debent ad expulsionem potestatis contrariae, utrique etiam *catechizari*: *adulti*, ut, expulsa caligine erroris, informentur ad fidem; *parvuli* vero, ut sciant patrini, quid eos debeant edocere, ne pro humano defectu impediatur baptismatis Sacramentum, quominus habeat finem suum.

## English

### Chapter VII. *On the constitution and integrity of baptism.*

Now in the seventh place it remains to see concerning the *integrity* of each[^p271-5] Sacrament; and since these are seven in number, first it must be said concerning the *integrity of baptism*, which is the gate of the other Sacraments.

Concerning the *integrity*, therefore, of the Sacrament of *baptism* this must be held: that for this, that anyone be baptized truly and fully, there is required the *expression of the vocal form* instituted by the Lord[^p271-6], which is this: I baptize thee in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, Amen; without the omission of a word and without the interposition of a word, without the inversion of the aforesaid order and without the alteration of the name prescribed. — There is required also *immersion* or ablution through *the element of water* upon the whole body, or at least upon the worthier part, in such wise that the *expression* and the *immersion* be done by one and the same person and at the same time. — And when these concur, if there be no *feigning* in the one being baptized, there is given to him grace *regenerating, rectifying* and *purifying* from every fault. — And that this may be done the more efficaciously, *catechism* and *exorcism* are sent before as preparatory, in infants as much as in adults; yet in such wise that in *adults* there is required *one's own faith*, whereas in *infants* the *faith of another* suffices[^p271-7].

Now the reason for the understanding of the aforesaid is this: since our reparative principle, the Word namely incarnate, as a most perfect and most sufficient principle, has to repair the human race through the medicaments of the Sacraments, in such wise that there be in them nothing superfluous, nothing disordered, nothing also diminished[^p271-8], he must needs have so disposed that the Sacrament of baptism and the others should be made entire according to what *his own power, our salvation* and also *our disease* demanded. — Since therefore the *power*
<!-- page 272 -->
repairing us is the power of *the whole Trinity*, which holy mother Church believes in mind, confesses in word and professes in sign, under the distinction and property of the three persons, under their natural order and origin; and is also the power of *the passion of Christ*, who died and was buried and *on the third day rose again*[^p272-1]: hence it is that, for the expression of these in the Sacrament which is the first of all the Sacraments, and in which first and principally this power works, there had to be made an expression of *the Trinity* in a distinct, proper and ordered naming, so far as pertains to the common form — although in the time of the primitive Church it could be done *in the name of Christ*[^p272-2], in which the understanding of the Trinity is enclosed. There had also to be made a proper and ordered naming of the word of *baptizing* with a threefold immersion, for the expression of the death of Christ and of the burial and of the resurrection made after three days, so far as pertains *to congruity*. And because both powers work at once and in the one Christ the Saviour, both of these ought to be done by *one* and the same person at once and at the same time, for the preserving of unity in the Sacrament and for the signifying of the unity in our Mediator[^p272-3].

Again, since *our salvation* required to be begun through a regeneration or renewal in the *being* of grace conferring spiritual *being*, by wiping away *uncleanness*, by putting to flight *the darknesses* and by cooling *concupiscence*, which universally weakens[^p272-4] every man descending seminally from Adam; hence it is that the first Sacrament, which is regenerative, had to be made in an element which should have a conformity, by natural representation, to the aforesaid threefold effect of the grace beginning our salvation. Since therefore water by its purity *cleanses*, by its transparency *is a bearer of light*, by its coldness *cools*; and is most of all common to all men among all liquids: hence it is that the Sacrament of our regeneration had to be accomplished in the element of *water indifferently*; because « all water is of the same species with all water[^p272-5] », and also lest anyone, on account of a defect of the element, should be able to incur peril of salvation.

Lastly, since *our disease*, against which baptism is principally directed, is original sin — which deprives the soul of the life of grace and of the enabling rectitude of all the virtues, and in a certain manner renders it prone to every kind of fault, and is drawn from elsewhere and « makes the infant concupiscible and the adult concupiscent in act », and reduces him also into diabolical servitude and into the power of the prince of darkness[^p272-6] — hence it is that, in order that through that Sacrament a sufficient medicament of remedy might be applied by way of the contrary, it was fitting that in it there be given grace *regenerating* against the privation of the gratuitous life, grace *rectifying* in the sevenfold power against the privation of the enabling virtue, grace *purifying* from every fault against the proneness to every vicious disorder.

And since original sin is drawn from elsewhere and makes the infant concupiscible and the adult concupiscent in act, *one's own faith* and *penance* are necessary *for the adult*, but for the *infant* the *faith of another* suffices, which namely is in the universal Church. But because baptism ought to draw both infants and adults out of the servitude of the devil and out of the power of the prince of darkness[^p272-7]; hence it is that both ought to be *exorcized* unto the expulsion of the contrary power, and both also *catechized*: *adults*, that, the mist of error having been driven out, they may be informed unto faith; *infants* indeed, that the godparents may know what they ought to teach them, lest through human defect the Sacrament of baptism be hindered from having its own end.
