## Latin

### Capitulum XI. *De productione hominis quantum ad totum coniunctum.*

De *toto* autem *homine* in paradiso collocato[^p229-1] haec tenenda sunt, scilicet quod datus est ei duplex *sensus*, scilicet interior et exterior, mentis et carnis. — Datus est ei duplex *motus*, scilicet imperativus in voluntate et exsecutivus in corpore. — Datum est ei duplex *bonum*, unum visibile, alterum invisibile. — Datum est ei duplex *praeceptum*, scilicet *naturae* et *disciplinae*; praeceptum *naturae*: *Crescite et multiplicamini*[^p229-2]; praeceptum *disciplinae*: *De ligno scientiae boni et mali ne comedas*. — Iuxta quae datum est sibi *quadruplex adiutorium*, scilicet scientiae, conscientiae, synderesis et gratiae, ex quibus sufficienter habuit, ut posset stare in bono et proficere et a malo cavere et declinare.

Ratio autem ad intelligentiam praedictorum haec est: quia primum principium fecit mundum istum sensibilem ad declarandum se ipsum, videlicet ad hoc, quod per illum tanquam per speculum et vestigium reduceretur homo in Deum[^p229-3] artificem, amandum et laudandum. Et secundum hoc duplex est *liber*, unus scilicet scriptus *intus*, qui est aeterna Dei ars et sapientia; et alius scriptus *foris*, mundus scilicet sensibilis. Cum igitur esset una creatura, quae sensum habebat *intus* ad cognitionem libri *interioris*, ut Angelus; et alia, quae totum sensum habebat *foris*, ut quodlibet animal brutum: ad perfectionem universitatis debuit fieri creatura, quae hoc sensu duplici esset praedita ad cognitionem libri scripti *intus et foris*, id est Sapientiae et sui operis. Et quia in Christo simul concurrit aeterna Sapientia et eius opus in una persona; ideo dicitur *liber scriptus intus et foris*[^p229-4] ad reparationem mundi.

Et quia cuilibet sensui respondet motus, ideo duplex datus est homini *motus*: unus secundum instinctum *rationis* in mente, alius secundum instinctum *sensualitatis* in carne[^p229-5]. Primi est *imperare*, secundi est *obtemperare*, secundum rectum ordinem; quando autem fit e converso, tunc rectitudo et regimen animae praecipitatur de statu suo.

Et quia cuilibet motui et sensui respondet appetitus ad aliquod *bonum*, ideo praeparatum est homini duplex *bonum*: « unum visibile, alterum invisibile; unum temporale, aliud aeternum; unum carni, alterum spiritui. Ex his bonis Deus unum dedit, alterum promisit, ut unum gratis possideretur, alterum per meritum quaereretur[^p229-6] ».

Et quia frustra datur bonum, nisi custodiatur; frustra promittitur, nisi ad ipsum perveniatur: ideo duplex datum est homini *praeceptum*: unum *naturae* ad custodiendum bonum datum, alterum *disciplinae* ad promerendum bonum promissum, quod nullo modo melius poterat mereri quam per *meram obedientiam*. *Obedientia* autem *mera* est, quando praeceptum ex se solo obligat, non ex aliqua alia causa; et tale dicitur *praeceptum disciplinae*, quia per ipsum discitur, quanta sit virtus obedientiae, quae suo merito ducit ad caelum, suo vero contrario[^p229-7] praecipitat in infernum. Non ergo datum est illud mandatum homini propter indigentiam aliquam, quam Deus haberet de humano obsequio; sed ad dandam viam merendi coronam per meram et voluntariam obedientiam.

Et quoniam homo ratione naturae defectivae, ex nihilo formatae nec per gloriam confirmatae poterat cadere; benignissimus Deus *quadruplex* ei contulit *adiutorium*: duplex *naturae* et duplex *gratiae*. Duplicem enim indidit rectitudinem ipsi *naturae*: unam ad recte iudicandum, et haec est *rectitudo conscientiae*; aliam ad recte volendum, et haec est *synderesis*[^p229-8], cuius est remurmurare contra malum et stimulare ad bonum. — Duplicem etiam superaddidit perfectionem *gratiae*: unam gratiae *gratis datae*, quae fuit scientia illuminans intellectum ad cognoscendum se ipsum, Deum suum et mundum istum, qui factus fuerat propter ipsum; aliam gratiae *gratum facientis*, quae fuit caritas habilitans affectum ad diligendum Deum super omnia et proximum

<!-- page 230 -->

sicut se ipsum[^p230-1]. — Et sic ante lapsum homo perfecta habuit naturalia, supervestita nihilominus divina gratia. Ex quo manifeste colligitur, quod si cecidit, hoc non fuit nisi ex culpa sua, quia obedire contempsit.

## English

### Chapter XI. *On the production of man as regards the whole conjoined being.*

Concerning the *whole man*, however, placed in paradise[^p229-1], these things are to be held: namely, that there was given to him a twofold *sense*, that is, interior and exterior, of the mind and of the flesh. — There was given to him a twofold *motion*, that is, imperative in the will and executive in the body. — There was given to him a twofold *good*, one visible, the other invisible. — There was given to him a twofold *precept*, that is, of *nature* and of *discipline*; the precept of *nature*: *Increase and multiply*[^p229-2]; the precept of *discipline*: *Of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil thou shalt not eat*. — Alongside which there was given to him a *fourfold help*, namely of knowledge, of conscience, of synderesis and of grace, from which he had sufficiently wherewith to stand in the good and advance, and to beware of evil and turn away from it.

Now the reason for the understanding of the foresaid is this: that the first principle made this sensible world to declare itself — namely to this end, that through it, as through a mirror and a vestige, man might be led back to God[^p229-3] the craftsman, to be loved and praised. And according to this the *book* is twofold: one written *within*, which is the eternal art and wisdom of God; and another written *without*, namely the sensible world. Since, therefore, there was one creature that had sense *within* for the knowledge of the *interior* book, as the Angel; and another that had its whole sense *without*, as any brute animal: for the perfection of the universe there had to come to be a creature endowed with this twofold sense for the knowledge of the book written *within and without*, that is, of Wisdom and of its work. And because in Christ the eternal Wisdom and its work concur at once in one person, therefore he is called *a book written within and without*[^p229-4] for the repair of the world.

And because to every sense there corresponds a motion, therefore a twofold *motion* was given to man: one according to the instinct of *reason* in the mind, the other according to the instinct of *sensuality* in the flesh[^p229-5]. To the first belongs *to command*, to the second *to obey*, according to the right order; but when it comes about the other way round, then the uprightness and governance of the soul is cast down from its state.

And because to every motion and sense there corresponds an appetite for some *good*, therefore a twofold *good* was prepared for man: « one visible, the other invisible; one temporal, the other eternal; one for the flesh, the other for the spirit. Of these goods God gave the one and promised the other, that the one might be possessed freely, and the other sought through merit[^p229-6] ».

And because a good is given in vain unless it be kept, and promised in vain unless it be attained: therefore a twofold *precept* was given to man — one of *nature*, for keeping the good given; the other of *discipline*, for meriting the good promised, which in no way could he better merit than through *pure obedience*. And *obedience* is *pure* when the precept obliges of itself alone, and not from any other cause; and such is called a *precept of discipline*, because through it is learned how great is the power of obedience, which by its merit leads to heaven, and by its contrary[^p229-7] casts down into hell. That command, therefore, was not given to man on account of any need that God might have of human service, but to give a way of meriting the crown through pure and voluntary obedience.

And since man, by reason of a nature defective, formed from nothing and not confirmed through glory, was able to fall, the most kindly God conferred on him a *fourfold help*: twofold of *nature* and twofold of *grace*. For he implanted in *nature* itself a twofold uprightness: one for judging rightly, and this is the *uprightness of conscience*; the other for willing rightly, and this is *synderesis*[^p229-8], whose office is to murmur back against evil and to spur toward good. — He also superadded a twofold perfection of *grace*: one of *freely given* grace, which was the knowledge illumining the intellect to know itself, its God, and this world which had been made for its sake; the other of *grace making pleasing*, which was the charity enabling the affection to love God above all things and one's neighbour

<!-- page 230 -->

as oneself[^p230-1]. — And thus before the fall man had his natural endowments perfect, clothed over nonetheless with divine grace. From which it is manifestly gathered that, if he fell, this was only from his own fault, because he scorned to obey.
