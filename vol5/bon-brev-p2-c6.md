## Latin

### Capitulum VI. *De productione supernorum spirituum.*

Consequenter vero agendum[^p224-1] est de natura spirituali et incorporea, cuiusmodi est angelica, de qua considerari oportet quantum ad *conditionem supernorum spirituum*, quantum ad *ruinam daemonum* et quantum ad *confirmationem bonorum Angelorum*.

Sciendum est igitur, quod Angelis a primordio suae conditionis quatuor sunt attributa, scilicet *simplicitas essentiae, personalis discretio*, propter rationem insitam *memoria, intelligentia et voluntas*, et *libertas arbitrii* ad eligenda bona et respuenda mala[^p224-2]. — Haec autem quatuor attributa principalia alia quatuor comitantur, scilicet *virtuositas* in operando, *officiositas* in ministrando, *perspicacitas* in cognoscendo et *immutabilitas* post electionem sive in bono, sive in malo.

Ratio autem ad intelligentiam praedictorum haec est: quia primum principium, hoc ipso quod primum, omnia de nihilo produxit; ideo non tantum *prope nihil*, sed etiam *prope se*[^p224-3]; non tantum substantiam a se longinquam, scilicet naturam corpoream, producere debuit, verum etiam propinquam; et haec est substantia intellectualis et incorporea, quae hoc ipso, quod Deo simillima est, *simplicitatem* habet *naturae* et *discretionem personalem*, ut Deo assimiletur ex parte substantiae sive *communis*, sive *individuae*[^p224-4]. — Habet etiam in mente imaginem Trinitatis secundum *memoriam, intelligentiam* et *voluntatem*; habet etiam *libertatem voluntatis*, ut assimiletur Deo ex parte potentiae sive *naturalis*, sive *electivae*, ut sic potentia *naturalis* insignita sit Dei imagine, *electiva* vero arbitrii libertate. Nequaquam enim perveniret meritorie ad praemium gloriosum, quod facit quemque beatum, nisi haberet liberum voluntatis arbitrium; hoc autem esse non potest nisi in substantia rationali, quam comitatur memoria, intelligentia et voluntas. Ubi autem est ratio, oportet, quod sit « rationalis naturae substantia individua[^p224-5] »; necesse est etiam, quod sit substantia spiritualis et incorporea, ac per hoc *simplex*, omni carens dimensione quantitativa.

Talis autem substantia hoc ipso, quod simplex, est *virtuosa in operando*[^p224-6]; hoc ipso, quod virtuosa et personaliter distincta, competit ei *distinctio officii in ministrando*; hoc ipso, quod simplex et virtuosa, competit ei *perspicacitas in discernendo*; hoc ipso, quod simplex et perspicax, habens *intellectum deiformem*[^p224-7]; ideo *stabilitatem* habet post electionem in electo sive in bono, sive in malo. — Et hae conditiones ipsam generalem conditionem supernorum spirituum generaliter comitantur.

## English

### Chapter VI. *On the production of the higher spirits.*

Next it must be treated[^p224-1] of the spiritual and incorporeal nature, of which kind is the angelic; concerning which it is fitting to consider it as regards the *founding of the higher spirits*, as regards the *ruin of the demons*, and as regards the *confirmation of the good Angels*.

It is therefore to be known that to the Angels, from the very beginning of their founding, four things are attributed: namely *simplicity of essence, personal distinctness*, and — on account of the reason implanted in them — *memory, understanding and will*, and *freedom of choice* for choosing good things and rejecting evil[^p224-2]. — And these four principal attributes are accompanied by four others: namely *power* in operating, *dutifulness* in ministering, *perspicacity* in knowing, and *immutability* after election, whether in good or in evil.

Now the reason for the understanding of the foresaid is this: that the first principle, by this very fact that it is first, produced all things out of nothing; and therefore it had to produce not only what is *near to nothing*, but also what is *near to itself*[^p224-3]; not only a substance far removed from itself, namely corporeal nature, but also one near to it. And this is the intellectual and incorporeal substance, which by this very fact, that it is most like to God, has *simplicity* of *nature* and *personal distinctness*, so that it may be made like to God on the side of substance, whether *common* or *individual*[^p224-4]. — It has also in its mind an image of the Trinity according to *memory, understanding* and *will*; it has also *freedom of will*, so that it may be made like to God on the side of power, whether *natural* or *elective* — so that the *natural* power is marked with the image of God, and the *elective* with the freedom of choice. For it would in no way arrive meritoriously at the glorious reward which makes anyone blessed, unless it had free choice of will; and this cannot be except in a rational substance, which memory, understanding and will accompany. And where reason is, it must be « an individual substance of a rational nature[^p224-5] »; it is necessary also that it be a spiritual and incorporeal substance, and through this *simple*, lacking all quantitative dimension.

But such a substance, by this very fact that it is simple, is *powerful in operating*[^p224-6]; by this very fact, that it is powerful and personally distinct, there belongs to it a *distinction of office in ministering*; by this very fact, that it is simple and powerful, there belongs to it *perspicacity in discerning*; by this very fact, that it is simple and perspicacious, having a *deiform intellect*[^p224-7], it therefore has *stability* after election in what has been chosen, whether in good or in evil. — And these conditions generally accompany the general founding itself of the higher spirits.
