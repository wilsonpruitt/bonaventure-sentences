## *De primorum parentum punitione*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 233 -->

### Capitulum IV. *De primorum parentum punitione.*

De *poena* autem primis parentibus inflicta hoc tenendum est, quod vir et mulier statim post *culpam* senserunt *poenam* rebellionis et erubescentiae in carne; et ideo ad cooperiendum verenda *fecerunt sibi perizomata*[^p233-4]. — Post autem divino iudicio vir incurrit poenam *laboris* et angustiae, poenam *famis* et indigentiae, poenam *mortis* et resolutionis in cinerem; sicut dicit Scriptura: *Maledicta terra in opere tuo* etc. — *Mulieri* autem *duplicata* est poena, quia inflicta est ei poenalitas multiplicium *aerumnarum* in conceptu, poena *dolorum* in partu, poena quoque *subiectionis* ad virum in convictu; ac per hoc, secundum quod apparet, satis graviter punitum fuit illud peccatum, scilicet ligni vetiti esus, licet fuerit faciliter perpetratum.

Ratio autem ad intelligentiam praedictorum haec est: quia, cum primum principium sit providentissimum in gubernando et rectissimum in praesidendo, nihil prorsus in universo dimittit inordinatum; et quia culpa recte ordinatur in poena, ideo statim in primis parentibus *dedecus peccati* subsecutum est *decus iudicii*, ut quod inordinatum fuit cadendo ab ordine naturae caderet statim in ordinem iustitiae. Hic enim duplex ordo sic cuncta complectitur, ut quod ab uno ceciderit in alterum relabatur[^p233-5].

Quoniam ergo uterque parens, superbiendo in mente et gustando in carne, inobediens fuit suo superiori, iusto Dei iudicio factum est, ut sibi fieret *inobediens* suum inferius, et maxime quantum ad partes illas, secundum quas est coniunctio utriusque sexus, quae sunt membra deservientia virtuti generativae. Et quia hoc inerat eis non ex natura, sed ex propria culpa; ideo erubescebant et cooperiebant se.

<!-- page 234 -->

Rursus, quia vir, spreto summo delectabili, quaesivit in carne delectari; ideo iusto Dei iudicio inflictus est ei *labor* et *defectus famis et sitis*.

Postremo, quia propter bonum carnis separari elegit a bono mentis, ideo iusto Dei iudicio anima invita separatur a carne per *mortem* et *incinerationem*; ac per hoc, sicut Deus dederat homini secundum ordinem naturae corpus *subiectum* animae, *propagabile* sine libidine, *vegetabile* sine defectione, *immutabile* sine morte interveniente; sic, homine peccante, secundum ordinem iustitiae factum est[^p234-1], ut subtraheret cuncta praedicta et infligeret opposita; ut sic culpa non remaneret impunita et inordinata, quod nequaquam pati debet divina providentia.

Et quoniam a muliere peccatum sumsit exordium; ideo poena ipsius *geminatur*. Quia enim superbiens fuit in mente, incurrit *subiectionem*; quia vidit et appetiit lignum ad vescendum suave, incurrit *dolorem*; postremo, quia fregit iugum obedientiae, incurrit vinculum et pondus *multiplicis aerumnae*.

Et sic patet, quanto ordine divinae providentiae inflictae sunt multiplices poenae *viro* et duplicatae in *muliere*, ut sic « dedecus peccati non esset sine decore iustitiae[^p234-2] ».

## English

### Chapter IV. *On the punishment of the first parents.*

Concerning the *punishment* inflicted on the first parents, this is to be held: that the man and the woman, immediately after the *guilt*, felt the *punishment* of rebellion and of shame in the flesh; and therefore, to cover their shameful parts, *they made themselves girdles*[^p233-4]. — But afterwards, by the divine judgment, the man incurred the punishment of *labor* and of straitness, the punishment of *hunger* and of want, the punishment of *death* and of dissolution into ash; as Scripture says: *Cursed is the earth in thy work*, etc. — But for the *woman* the punishment was *doubled*, because there was inflicted on her the penalty of manifold *hardships* in conception, the punishment of *pains* in childbirth, and also the punishment of *subjection* to the man in their life together; and thereby, according as it appears, that sin — namely the eating of the forbidden tree — was punished gravely enough, although it was easily perpetrated.

Now the reason for the understanding of the foresaid is this: since the first principle is most provident in governing and most upright in presiding, it lets nothing at all in the universe go undisposed; and since guilt is rightly ordered into punishment, therefore straightway in the first parents the *disgrace of sin* was followed by the *comeliness of judgment*, so that what was disordered by falling from the order of nature should fall straightway into the order of justice. For this twofold order so embraces all things that whatever falls away from the one falls back into the other[^p233-5].

Since, therefore, each parent, by growing proud in mind and by tasting in the flesh, was disobedient to his superior, it was brought about by the just judgment of God that his own inferior should become *disobedient* to him, and most of all as regards those parts according to which there is the joining of either sex, which are the members serving the generative power. And because this was in them not from nature but from their own guilt, therefore they blushed and covered themselves.

<!-- page 234 -->

Again, because the man, having spurned the highest delightful thing, sought to be delighted in the flesh; therefore by the just judgment of God there was inflicted on him *labor* and the *failing of hunger and thirst*.

Last, because for the good of the flesh he chose to be separated from the good of the mind, therefore by the just judgment of God the soul is unwillingly separated from the flesh through *death* and *reduction to ashes*; and thereby, just as God had given to man, according to the order of nature, a body *subject* to the soul, *propagable* without lust, *capable of growth* without failing, *unchangeable* without the intervention of death; so, man having sinned, it was brought about according to the order of justice[^p234-1] that He should withdraw all the foresaid things and inflict their opposites; so that thus guilt should not remain unpunished and disordered, which the divine providence ought in no way to suffer.

And since from the woman the sin took its beginning; therefore her punishment is *doubled*. For because she was proud in mind, she incurred *subjection*; because she saw and desired the tree as sweet for eating, she incurred *pain*; last, because she broke the yoke of obedience, she incurred the bond and the weight of *manifold hardship*.

And thus it is clear with how great an order of divine providence the manifold punishments were inflicted on the *man* and doubled in the *woman*, so that thus « the disgrace of sin should not be without the comeliness of justice[^p234-2] ».
