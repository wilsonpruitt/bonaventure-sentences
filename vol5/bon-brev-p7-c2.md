## Latin
<!-- page 282 -->

### Capitulum II. *De antecedentibus ad iudicium, cuiusmodi est poena purgatoria.*

Deinde in *speciali* circa statum finalis iudicii consideranda sunt quaedam sicut *praeambula*, quaedam sicut *concomitantia*, quaedam sicut *consequentia*. Praeambula autem sunt duo, scilicet *poena purgatoria* et *suffragia ecclesiastica*[^p282-3].

Primo igitur de *poenis purgatoriis* hoc tenendum est, quod ignis purgatorius est ignis corporalis, quo tamen spiritus iustorum, qui in hac vita non impleverunt poenitentiam et satisfactionem condignam, affliguntur secundum plus et minus, secundum quod plus vel minus de cremabili secum ex hac vita traxerunt. — Affliguntur autem minus graviter quam in inferno, et gravius quam in hoc mundo; non tamen ita graviter, quin semper sperent et sciant, se in inferno non esse, licet forte pro poenarum magnitudine hoc aliquoties non advertant. — Hac autem afflictione ab igne corporali inflicta purgantur spiritus a reatibus et scoriis et etiam reliquiis peccatorum; quibus sufficienter expurgatis, immediate evolant et introducuntur in gloriam paradisi[^p282-4]

Ratio autem ad intelligentiam praedictorum haec est: quia, cum primum principium, eo ipso quod primum, sit optimum et perfectissimum, et eo ipso quod optimum, sit summe amativum boni et summe detestativum mali; sicut summa bonitas non patitur, quod *bonum* remaneat irremuneratum, sic etiam pati non debet, quod *malum* remaneat impunitum[^p282-5]. Quoniam igitur viri iusti aliquando decedunt, qui poenitentiam in hac vita non totaliter impleverunt; cum meritum vitae aeternae in eis non possit remanere irremuneratum, et vitium culpae non possit remanere impunitum, ne perturbetur pulcritudo ordinis universi: necesse est, quod ipsi finaliter praemientur; necesse est etiam, quod iidem temporaliter puniantur secundum suarum culparum exigentiam et reatum. — Et quia culpa commissa et *offensiva* fuit maiestatis divinae et *damnificativa* Ecclesiae et *deformativa* imaginis divinae, menti nostrae impressae, maxime si fuit culpa mortalis, et venialis etiam disponit ad hoc; et de *offensa* requiritur punitio, de *damno* vero agenda est satisfactio, et de *deformatione* requirenda est expurgatio: hinc est, quod necesse est, quod poena illa sit iuste *punitiva*, digne *satisfactoria* et sufficienter *expurgativa*.

Primum igitur, quoniam poena illa debet esse iuste *punitiva*; et spiritus, qui, contempto bono aeterno et summo, se subiecit infimo, iuste debet inferioribus subiici, ut ab eis suscipiat poenam, ex quorum occasione perpetravit culpam, et ratione quorum et Deum contempsit et se ipsum vilem fecit: hinc est, quod ordo divinae iustitiae exigit, ut spiritus ab *igne materiali* habeat puniri; ut, sicut secundum ordinem naturae anima unitur *corpori*, ut influat vitam, sic uniatur *igni materiali* secundum ordinem iustitiae ut punibile punienti, a quo suscipiat poenam[^p282-6]. — Et quia viri iusti, qui sunt in gratia, non sunt digni nisi poena transitoria; et tanto maiori digni sunt poena, quanto maiora commiserunt peccata et minorem egerunt poenitentiam: hinc est, quod ab igne materiali puniuntur *temporaliter*, quidam tamen diutius, quidam vero brevius, quidam acerbius, quidam levius, secundum quod reatus exigit offensarum. « Necesse enim est, ut dicit egregius doctor Augustinus[^p282-7], quod tantum urat dolor, quantum haeserat amor ». Tanto enim difficilius quis
<!-- page 283 -->
purgatur, quanto medullis cordis eius intimius amor inhaeserat mundanorum.

Amplius, quoniam poena illa debet esse *satisfactoria*; et satisfactio respicit libertatem voluntatis et statum viae; potissime cum ibi iam non sit status merendi, et poenae illae minime teneant rationem voluntarii: necesse est, quod illud quod deficit ex parte *libertatis* in voluntate sustinente, suppleatur in *acerbitate* ex parte poenae. — Quia vero gratiam habent qui purgantur, quam quidem ultra perdere non possunt; hinc est, quod nec a tristitia penitus absorberi nec in desperationem incidere nec in blasphemiam possunt nec volunt aliquatenus prosilire; ac per hoc, licet graviter puniantur, longe tamen aliter et mitius quam in inferno; et statum etiam illum indubitanter sciunt alium esse quam istum, in quo sunt illi[^p283-1] qui in inferno irremediabiliter cruciantur.

Postremo, quia poena illa debet esse *expurgatoria*; et purgatio illa est spiritualis: necesse est, vel quod ignis ille spiritualem habeat virtutem divinitus sibi datam, vel, ut magis credo[^p283-2], ipsa virtus gratiae interius habitantis, adiuta a poena extrinseca, ipsam animam iam pro offensis punitam et a reatuum onere alleviatam purget *purgatione sufficienti*, ut iam nulla remaneat dissimilitudo ad gloriam. — Et quoniam tales spiritus dispositi sunt in summo ad deiformitatem gloriae suscipiendam in se; cum ianua sit aperta et purgatio consummata, necesse est, illos spiritus evolare, in quibus est caritatis ignis sursum levans, et nihil retardans ex parte impuritatis animae vel reatus. Nec enim decet divinam misericordiam seu iustitiam, ut amplius differat gloriam, cum inveniat[^p283-3] receptaculum idoneum, et magna sit poena in dilatione praemiorum, nec amplius puniri debeat spiritus iam purgatus.

## English

### Chapter II. *On the things antecedent to the judgment, of which sort is purgatorial punishment.*

Next, in *particular*, concerning the state of the final judgment certain things are to be considered as *preambles*, certain as *concomitants*, certain as *consequents*. Now the preambles are two, namely *purgatorial punishment* and *ecclesiastical suffrages*[^p282-3].

First therefore, concerning *purgatorial punishments* this must be held: that the purgatorial fire is a corporeal fire, by which nevertheless the spirits of the just, who in this life have not fulfilled penance and condign satisfaction, are afflicted according to more and less, according as they have drawn with them out of this life more or less of what is combustible. — But they are afflicted less grievously than in hell, and more grievously than in this world; yet not so grievously but that they always hope and know that they are not in hell, although perhaps, because of the magnitude of the punishments, they sometimes do not advert to this. — And by this affliction inflicted by the corporeal fire the spirits are purged from their liabilities and from the dross and also from the relics of sins; and when these have been sufficiently expurgated, they immediately fly forth and are led into the glory of paradise[^p282-4]

Now the reason for the understanding of the aforesaid is this: since the first principle, by this very fact that it is first, is the best and most perfect, and by this very fact that it is best, is supremely loving of the good and supremely detesting of evil; just as the highest goodness does not suffer that *the good* remain unrewarded, so neither ought it to suffer that *evil* remain unpunished[^p282-5]. Since therefore just men sometimes depart who have not wholly fulfilled penance in this life; since the merit of eternal life cannot remain unrewarded in them, and the vice of fault cannot remain unpunished, lest the beauty of the order of the universe be disturbed: it is necessary that they themselves be finally rewarded; it is necessary also that the same be temporally punished according to the requirement of their faults and their liability. — And because the fault committed was *offensive* of the divine majesty and *damaging* to the Church and *deformative* of the divine image impressed upon our mind, most of all if it was a mortal fault, and the venial also disposes to this; and for the *offense* punishment is required, for the *damage* satisfaction must be made, and for the *deformation* expurgation must be sought: hence it is that it is necessary that that punishment be justly *punitive*, worthily *satisfactory* and sufficiently *expurgative*.

First therefore, since that punishment ought to be justly *punitive*; and the spirit which, having despised the eternal and highest good, subjected itself to the lowest, justly ought to be subjected to lower things, so that it may receive punishment from those by occasion of which it perpetrated the fault, and by reason of which it both despised God and made itself vile: hence it is that the order of divine justice requires that the spirit have to be punished by *material fire*; so that, just as according to the order of nature the soul is united to the *body* that it may pour in life, so it may be united to *material fire* according to the order of justice as the punishable to that which punishes, from which it may receive punishment[^p282-6]. — And because just men who are in grace are not deserving save of a transitory punishment; and they are deserving of so much the greater punishment as they have committed the greater sins and done the lesser penance: hence it is that by the material fire they are punished *temporally*, some however longer, some indeed more briefly, some more bitterly, some more lightly, according as the liability of their offenses requires. « For it is necessary, as the excellent doctor Augustine says[^p282-7], that the pain burn as much as the love had clung ». For so much the more difficultly is one
<!-- page 283 -->
purged, as the love of worldly things had clung the more intimately to the marrow of his heart.

Further, since that punishment ought to be *satisfactory*; and satisfaction regards the freedom of the will and the state of the way; especially since there is there no longer the state of meriting, and those punishments in no way hold the character of the voluntary: it is necessary that what is lacking on the part of *freedom* in the will that undergoes them be supplied in the *bitterness* on the part of the punishment. — But because those who are purged have grace, which indeed they can no longer lose; hence it is that they can neither be utterly absorbed by sadness nor fall into despair, nor can they nor will they in any way break out into blasphemy; and through this, although they are grievously punished, yet it is far otherwise and more mildly than in hell; and they know without doubt that that state also is other than this, in which are those[^p283-1] who in hell are tormented beyond remedy.

Lastly, because that punishment ought to be *expurgatory*; and that purgation is spiritual: it is necessary either that that fire have a spiritual power divinely given to it, or, as I rather believe[^p283-2], that the very power of the grace dwelling within, aided by the extrinsic punishment, purge with a *sufficient purgation* the soul itself, now punished for its offenses and lightened of the burden of its liabilities, so that no unlikeness to glory any longer remain. — And since such spirits are disposed in the highest degree to receive in themselves the deiformity of glory; when the gate is open and the purgation consummated, it is necessary that those spirits fly forth, in whom is the fire of charity lifting them upward, and nothing retarding them on the part of the soul's impurity or of liability. For it does not befit the divine mercy or justice to defer glory any longer, when it finds[^p283-3] a fit receptacle, and there is great punishment in the deferring of rewards, nor ought a spirit already purged to be punished any longer.
