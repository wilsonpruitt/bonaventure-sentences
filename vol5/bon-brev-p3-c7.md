## Latin

### Capitulum VII. *De originalis peccati curatione.*

Postremo, modus *curationis* originalis hic est[^p236-1], quod sic curatur *culpa*, quod remanet *poena* temporalis, sicut patet in parvulis baptizatis; sic curatur quantum ad *reatum poenae aeternae*, quod remanet quantum ad *actum et motum concupiscentiae*; sic curatur in *parente*, quod nihilominus ab eo qui curatus est per baptismum, transmittitur in *prolem* originale peccatum; sic tollitur originalis peccati *macula*, quod remanet *sequela*, cum qua oportet pugnare, quamdiu vivimus in hac vita, quia in nullo prorsus exstinguitur concupiscentia per gratiam communem; quod dico propter beatissimam Virginem, in qua in conceptione Filii Dei exstincta fuit per gratiam singularem[^p236-2].

Ratio autem ad intelligentiam praedictorum haec est: quia, sicut *infectio* derivatur in omnes a principio *creato*, a quo fit corporum propagatio, et hoc a parte inferiori, scilicet carnis; sic *curatio* fieri habet a principio *increato*, a quo fit animarum infusio, et hoc a parte superiori, scilicet mentis. Quoniam igitur ex parte mentis distinctio est in hominibus, ita quod secundum illam non propagatur unus ab alio, sed immediate exit a Deo[^p236-3]; gratia curativa, menti nostrae a Deo infusa respicit unumquemque, in quantum tenet rationem personae singularis et individuae, non in quantum tenet rationem productivi secundum virtutem naturae. Quia ergo originale est morbus inficiens *personam* pariter et *naturam*, *personam* in voluntate, *naturam* in carne; ideo sic curatur macula originalis in *mente*, quod remanet infectio et sequela in *carne*.

Et quia homo generat, non secundum quod *curatus* in mente, sed secundum quod *corruptus* in carne, non secundum quod *spiritualis*, sed secundum quod *carnalis*; hinc est, quod quamvis sit baptizatus et sic ab originali mundatus in se, transmittit tamen originale in prolem.

Rursus, quia *reatus* poenae aeternae respicit deformitatem mentis et personae, *motus* autem respicit inclinationem carnis et naturae; ideo transit originale per baptismum quantum ad *reatum* et remanet quantum ad *actum*[^p236-4].

Postremo, quia temporalis afflictio conditionem respicit ex parte carnis; cum caro remaneat semper subiecta cuidam *infectioni*, remanere debet semper subiecta *poenalitati*: et ideo, sicut poenalitas et corruptio per gratiam non aufertur a carne; sic *sequela* illa sive concupiscentia et languor membrorum simul stare potest cum gratia curativa. Et ideo, quamvis paulatim minuatur, quia tamen radix non tollitur, nunquam omnino aufertur in viatore nisi in beatissima Virgine per gratiam singularem. Quia enim Virgo concepit eum qui erat expiatio omnis culpae, ideo data fuit ei gratia singularis, qua exstincta fuit in ea radicitus omnis concupiscentia, ad concipiendum Dei Filium absque omni[^p236-5] peccati labe et corruptela. « Nempe decens erat, ut ea puritate, qua maior sub Deo nequit intelligi, Virgo illa niteret, cui Deus Pater unicum Filium, quem de corde suo genitum sibi aequalem tanquam se ipsum diligebat, ita dare disponebat, ut naturaliter esset unus idemque communis Dei Patris et Virginis Filius; et quam ipse Filius substantialiter facere sibi matrem eligebat, et de qua Spiritus sanctus volebat et operaturus erat, ut conciperetur et nasceretur ille, de quo ipse procedebat[^p236-6] ».

## English

### Chapter VII. *On the healing of original sin.*

Lastly, the manner of the *healing* of original sin is this[^p236-1]: that *guilt* is so healed that a temporal *punishment* remains, as is clear in baptized infants; it is so healed as regards the *liability to eternal punishment* that it remains as regards the *act and motion of concupiscence*; it is so healed in the *parent* that nonetheless from him who has been healed through baptism original sin is transmitted into the *offspring*; the *stain* of original sin is so taken away that the *sequel* remains, with which we must fight so long as we live in this life, because in no one whatever is concupiscence utterly extinguished by common grace; which I say on account of the most blessed Virgin, in whom, in the conception of the Son of God, it was extinguished by a singular grace[^p236-2].

Now the reason for the understanding of the foresaid is this: since, just as the *infection* is derived into all from a *created* principle, from which comes the propagation of bodies, and this on the lower side, namely that of the flesh; so the *healing* has to be effected from an *uncreated* principle, from which comes the infusion of souls, and this on the higher side, namely that of the mind. Since, therefore, on the side of the mind there is distinction among men, in such wise that according to it one is not propagated from another, but goes forth immediately from God[^p236-3]; the healing grace, infused into our mind by God, regards each one insofar as he holds the account of a singular and individual person, not insofar as he holds the account of something productive according to the power of nature. Because, therefore, original sin is a disease infecting the *person* equally and the *nature* — the *person* in the will, the *nature* in the flesh — therefore the original stain is so healed in the *mind* that the infection and the sequel remain in the *flesh*.

And because man generates, not according as he is *healed* in mind, but according as he is *corrupted* in flesh, not according as he is *spiritual*, but according as he is *carnal*; hence it is that, although he be baptized and thus cleansed from original sin in himself, he nevertheless transmits original sin into his offspring.

Again, because the *liability* to eternal punishment regards the deformity of mind and of person, whereas the *motion* regards the inclination of flesh and of nature; therefore original sin passes over through baptism as regards the *liability*, and remains as regards the *act*[^p236-4].

Lastly, because temporal affliction regards the condition on the side of the flesh: since the flesh remains always subject to a certain *infection*, it must remain always subject to *penalty*; and therefore, just as penalty and corruption are not taken away from the flesh by grace, so that *sequel* — that is, concupiscence and the languor of the members — can stand together with healing grace. And therefore, although it is little by little diminished, yet because the root is not taken away, it is never wholly taken away in the wayfarer, save in the most blessed Virgin through a singular grace. For because the Virgin conceived him who was the expiation of all guilt, therefore there was given to her a singular grace, by which all concupiscence was extinguished in her at the root, for the conceiving of the Son of God without any[^p236-5] stain and corruption of sin. « Assuredly it was fitting that that Virgin should shine with a purity than which no greater under God can be understood — she to whom God the Father so disposed to give his only Son, whom, begotten of his own heart and equal to himself, he loved as his very self, that naturally he should be one and the same common Son of God the Father and of the Virgin; and whom the Son himself chose substantially to make his mother; and of whom the Holy Spirit willed and was about to work, that there should be conceived and born he from whom he himself proceeded[^p236-6] ».
