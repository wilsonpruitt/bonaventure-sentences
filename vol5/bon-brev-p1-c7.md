## *De omnipotentia Dei*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 215 -->

### Capitulum VII. *De omnipotentia Dei.*

De *omnipotentia* Dei secundum doctrinam sacram haec tenenda sunt; quod Deus est omnipotens, ita tamen, quod ei non attribuuntur actus *culpabiles*, utpote mentiri et male velle; nec actus *poenales*, ut metuere et dolere; nec actus *corporales* sive materiales, ut dormire et ambulare, nisi forte transsumtive; nec actus *inconvenientes*, ut posse facere maiorem se, vel alium Deum sibi aequalem, vel infinitum actu, et consimilia: « quia, ut dicit Anselmus[^p215-6], quodlibet inconveniens, etiam minimum, apud Deum est impossibile ». Licet autem haec non possit, est tamen omnipotens vere, proprie et perfecte:

<!-- page 216 -->

Intelligentia autem et ratio praedictorum haec est: quia primum principium est potens potentia, quae est potentia *simpliciter*; et ideo distributio addita ei[^p216-1] distribuit pro his quae *posse* est *posse simpliciter*. Haec autem *sunt*, quae egrediuntur a potentia *completa* et *ordinata*. — Potentiam autem *completam* dico, quae non potest *deficere* nec potest *succumbere* nec potest *indigere*. Potentia autem in peccando *deficit*, in patiendo *succumbit*, in corporalibus actionibus *indigentiam includit*. Divina vero potentia, quia potentia summa et perfectissima, ideo nec est de nihilo nec est sub aliquo nec eget aliquo alio, ac per hoc nec *culpabilia* nec *poenalia* nec *materialia* potest; et hoc, quia omnipotens est potentia *completa*.

Potentiam autem *ordinatam* contingit tripliciter dici: vel[^p216-2] secundum *actum*, vel secundum *aptitudinem* ex parte *creaturae*, vel secundum *aptitudinem* ex parte solius *virtutis increatae*. Quod possibile est potentiae primo modo dictae est non tantum *possibile*, sed etiam *actuale*; quod secundo modo et non primo, est *possibile simpliciter*, licet non actuale; quod autem tertio modo, non primo vel secundo, est *possibile Deo*, sed impossibile creaturae. Quod autem nullo praedictorum modorum est possibile, sicut illud quod directe repugnat ordini secundum rationes et causas primordiales[^p216-3] et aeternas, *simpliciter est impossibile*: sicut quod Deus faciat aliquod actu infinitum, quod simul faciat aliquid esse et nullo modo esse, quod faciat id quod fuit, non fuisse, et cetera talia; quae *posse* est contra ordinem et complementum divinae potentiae. — Ex his patet, respectu quorum est divina potentia; patet etiam, quae debent dici *simpliciter possibilia*, et quae *simpliciter impossibilia*, et quod aliquorum impossibilitas simul stet cum vera omnipotentia.

## English

### Chapter VII. *On the omnipotence of God.*

Concerning the *omnipotence* of God, according to sacred doctrine these things are to be held: that God is omnipotent, yet in such a way that there are not attributed to him *culpable* acts, such as to lie and to will evil; nor *penal* acts, such as to fear and to grieve; nor *corporeal* or material acts, such as to sleep and to walk, except perhaps by transferred speech; nor *unfitting* acts, such as to be able to make something greater than himself, or another God equal to himself, or something actually infinite, and the like: « because, as Anselm says[^p215-6], anything unfitting, even the least, is impossible with God ». But although he cannot do these things, he is nevertheless omnipotent truly, properly, and perfectly:

Now the understanding and the reason for the aforesaid is this: because the first principle is powerful with a power which is power *simply*; and therefore the distributive sign added to it[^p216-1] distributes over those things for which *to be able* is *to be able simply*. Now these *are* the things which issue from a power that is *complete* and *ordered*. — And I call that power *complete* which cannot *fail*, nor *give way*, nor *be in need*. But a power fails in sinning, gives way in suffering, and includes neediness in corporeal actions. But the divine power, because it is the highest and most perfect power, is therefore neither from nothing, nor under anything, nor does it need anything else; and through this it is capable neither of *culpable* nor of *penal* nor of *material* things; and this because the Omnipotent is a *complete* power.

But an *ordered* power happens to be spoken of in three ways: either[^p216-2] according to *act*, or according to *aptitude* on the part of the *creature*, or according to *aptitude* on the part of the *uncreated power* alone. What is possible to power taken in the first way is not only *possible*, but also *actual*; what is possible in the second way and not the first is *possible simply*, though not actual; but what is possible in the third way, not in the first or the second, is *possible to God*, but impossible to the creature. But what is possible in none of the aforesaid ways — as that which directly conflicts with the order according to the primordial[^p216-3] and eternal reasons and causes — is *simply impossible*: as that God should make something actually infinite, that he should at once make something to be and in no way to be, that he should make what has been not to have been, and other such things; and *to be able* for these is against the order and the completeness of the divine power. — From these things it is clear with respect to what the divine power is; it is also clear what things ought to be called *simply possible* and what *simply impossible*, and that the impossibility of certain things stands together with true omnipotence.
