# Itinerarium mentis in Deum, Capitula
## *Incipiunt capitula*

---

## Latin
<!-- page 296 -->

### INCIPIUNT CAPITULA.

Primum capitulum, de gradibus ascensionis in Deum et de speculatione ipsius per vestigia eius in universo.

Secundum capitulum, de speculatione Dei in vestigiis suis in hoc sensibili mundo.

Tertium capitulum, de speculatione Dei per suam imaginem naturalibus potentiis insignitam.

Quartum capitulum, de speculatione Dei in sua imagine donis gratuitis reformata.

Quintum capitulum, de speculatione divinae unitatis per eius nomen primarium, quod est *esse*.

Sextum capitulum, de speculatione beatissimae Trinitatis in eius nomine, quod est *bonum*.

Septimum capitulum, de excessu mentali et mystico, in quo requies datur intellectui, affectu in Deum per excessum totaliter transeunte.

EXPLICIUNT CAPITULA[^p296-5].

---

## English

### HERE BEGIN THE CHAPTERS.

The first chapter, on the steps of the ascent into God and on the speculation of him through his vestiges in the universe.

The second chapter, on the speculation of God in his vestiges in this sensible world.

The third chapter, on the speculation of God through his image stamped with the natural powers.

The fourth chapter, on the speculation of God in his image reformed by the gratuitous gifts.

The fifth chapter, on the speculation of the divine unity through its primary name, which is *being*.

The sixth chapter, on the speculation of the most blessed Trinity in its name, which is *the good*.

The seventh chapter, on the mental and mystical transport, in which rest is given to the intellect, the affection passing over wholly into God by transport.

HERE END THE CHAPTERS[^p296-5].

## Apparatus

[^p296-5]: **La.** Haec capitula cum quibusdam nullius momenti variantibus lectionibus hic ponuntur in codd. et 1, 2, 3, 4; B aliique codd. non hic, sed in ipso textu ad cc. 3. 4. 5 post vocem *capitulum* adiiciunt *et tertius* (*quartus... quintus*) *gradus*; Verba *Incipit speculatio pauperis in deserto* habentur in B E F K P Q et 1, 2, 3, 4.
     **En.** These chapter headings, with certain variant readings of no moment, are placed here in the codices and in editions 1, 2, 3, 4; B and other codices place them not here but in the text itself, at chapters 3, 4, 5, adding after the word *capitulum* the words *et tertius* (*quartus... quintus*) *gradus*; the words *Incipit speculatio pauperis in deserto* are found in B E F K P Q and in editions 1, 2, 3, 4.

## Notes

**★ THIS TABLE IS TRANSMITTED TEXT, AND THAT IS WHY IT IS CHUNKED.** The Breviloquium's capitula table (pp. 209–210) was left unchunked as editorial matter, like an index. This one is not the same thing, on two independent grounds: **(a)** it carries its own apparatus anchor — ⁵ stands on `EXPLICIUNT CAPITULA` and its note reports which codices transmit the table *here* and which transmit its titles *inside* the text at capp. 3–5, i.e. the table has a manuscript tradition of its own; and **(b)** Bonaventure claims the titles as his own in the prologue's own words, § 5: *Placuit autem distinguere tractatum in septem capitula, praemittendo titulos ad faciliorem intelligentiam dicendorum.* The table is what that sentence promises. **The test frozen in CLAUDE.md is (a) — look for apparatus ON the table — because it is checkable off the band; do not decide the question by analogy to the previous work.**

Both are grammatical and the sense is unchanged; the divergence is recorded, not resolved. **`bon-itin-c7` settles it against the in-place `Cap.
