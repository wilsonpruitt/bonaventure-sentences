## Latin
<!-- page 312 -->

### Cap. VII. *De excessu mentali et mystico, in quo requies datur intellectui, affectu totaliter in Deum per excessum transeunte.*

1. His igitur sex considerationibus excursis tanquam *sex gradibus throni veri Salomonis*, quibus pervenitur ad pacem, ubi verus pacificus in mente pacifica tanquam in interiori Hierosolyma requiescit; tanquam etiam *sex alis Cherub*, quibus mens veri contemplativi plena illustratione supernae sapientiae valeat sursum agi; tanquam etiam *sex diebus primis*, in quibus mens exercitari habet, ut tandem perveniat ad sabbatum quietis[^p312-3]; postquam mens nostra contuita est Deum *extra se* per vestigia et in vestigiis, *intra se* per imaginem et in imagine, *supra se* per divinae lucis similitudinem super nos relucentem et in ipsa luce, secundum quod possibile est secundum statum viae et exercitium mentis nostrae; cum tandem in *sexto* gradu ad hoc pervenerit, ut speculetur in principio primo et summo et mediatore *Dei et hominum*, Iesu Christo[^p312-4] ea quorum similia in creaturis nullatenus reperiri possunt, et quae omnem perspicacitatem humani intellectus excedunt: restat, ut haec speculando transcendat et transeat non solum mundum istum sensibilem, verum etiam semetipsam; in quo transitu Christus est *via et ostium*[^p312-5], Christus est *scala* et *vehiculum* tanquam *propitiatorium super arcam Dei collocatum* et *sacramentum a saeculis absconditum*.

2. Ad quod propitiatorium qui aspicit plena conversione vultus, aspiciendo eum in cruce suspensum per fidem, spem et caritatem, devotionem, admirationem, exsultationem, appretiationem, laudem et iubilationem; *pascha*, hoc est transitum[^p312-6], cum eo facit, ut per virgam crucis transeat mare rubrum, ab Aegypto intrans desertum, ubi gustet *manna absconditum*, et cum Christo requiescat in tumulo quasi exterius mortuus, sentiens tamen, quantum possibile est secundum statum viae, quod in cruce dictum est latroni cohaerenti Christo: *Hodie mecum eris in paradiso.*

3. Quod etiam ostensum est beato Francisco, cum in excessu contemplationis in monte excelso — ubi haec, quae scripta sunt, mente tractavi — apparuit Seraph sex alarum in cruce confixus, ut ibidem a socio eius, qui tunc cum eo fuit, ego et plures alii audivimus; ubi in Deum transiit per contemplationis excessum; et positus est in exemplum perfectae *contemplationis*, sicut prius fuerat *actionis*, tanquam alter *Iacob* et *Israel*[^p312-7], ut omnes viros vere spirituales Deus per eum invitaret ad huiusmodi transitum et mentis excessum magis exemplo quam verbo.

4. In hoc autem transitu, si sit perfectus, oportet quod relinquantur omnes intellectuales operationes, et apex affectus totus transferatur et transformetur in Deum. Hoc autem est mysticum et secretissimum, quod *nemo novit, nisi qui accipit*[^p312-8], nec accipit nisi qui desiderat, nec desiderat nisi quem ignis Spiritus sancti medullitus inflammat, quem Christus misit in terram. Et ideo dicit Apostolus[^p312-9], hanc mysticam sapientiam esse per Spiritum sanctum revelatam.

5. Quoniam igitur ad hoc nihil potest natura, modicum potest industria, parum est dandum inquisitioni, et multum unctioni; parum dandum est linguae, et plurimum internae laetitiae; parum dandum
<!-- page 313 -->
est verbo et scripto, et totum Dei dono, scilicet Spiritui sancto; parum aut nihil dandum est creaturae, et totum creatrici essentiae, Patri et Filio et Spiritui sancto, dicendo cum Dionysio[^p313-1] ad Deum Trinitatem: « Trinitas superessentialis et superdeus et superoptime Christianorum inspector theosophiae, dirige nos in mysticorum eloquiorum superincognitum et superlucentem et sublimissimum verticem; ubi nova et absoluta et inconversibilia theologiae mysteria secundum superlucentem absconduntur occulte docentis silentii caliginem in obscurissimo, quod est supermanifestissimum, supersplendentem, et in qua omne relucet, et invisibilium superbonorum splendoribus superimplentem invisibiles intellectus ». Hoc ad Deum. Ad amicum autem, cui haec scribuntur, dicatur cum eodem: « Tu autem, o amice, circa mysticas visiones, corroborato itinere, et sensus desere et intellectuales operationes et sensibilia et invisibilia et omne non ens et ens, et ad unitatem, ut possibile est, inscius restituere ipsius, qui est super omnem essentiam et scientiam. Etenim te ipso et omnibus immensurabili et absoluto purae mentis excessu[^p313-2], ad superessentialem divinarum tenebrarum radium, omnia deserens et ab omnibus absolutus, ascendes[^p313-3] ».

6. Si autem quaeras, quomodo haec fiant, interroga gratiam, non doctrinam; desiderium, non intellectum; gemitum orationis, non studium lectionis; sponsum, non magistrum; Deum, non hominem; caliginem, non claritatem; non lucem, sed ignem totaliter inflammantem et in Deum excessivis unctionibus et ardentissimis affectionibus transferentem. Qui quidem *ignis* Deus est, et huius *caminus est in Ierusalem*[^p313-4], et Christus hunc accendit in fervore suae ardentissimae passionis, quem solus ille vere percipit, qui dicit: *Suspendium elegit anima mea, et mortem ossa mea.* Quam mortem qui diligit videre potest Deum, quia indubitanter verum est: *Non videbit me homo et vivet.* — Moriamur igitur et ingrediamur in caliginem, imponamus silentium sollicitudinibus, concupiscentiis et phantasmatibus; transeamus cum Christo crucifixo *ex hoc mundo ad Patrem*[^p313-5], ut, ostenso nobis Patre, dicamus cum Philippo: *Sufficit nobis*; audiamus cum Paulo: *Sufficit tibi gratia mea*; exultemus cum David[^p313-6] dicentes: *Defecit caro mea et cor meum, Deus cordis mei et pars mea Deus in aeternum. Benedictus Dominus in aeternum, et dicet omnis populus: Fiat, fiat.* Amen.

**EXPLICIT ITINERARIUM IN DEUM.**

## English
<!-- page 312 -->

### Cap. VII. *On the mental and mystical transport, in which rest is given to the intellect, the affection passing over wholly into God by transport.*

1. These six considerations having therefore been run through, as by *the six steps of the throne of the true Solomon*, by which one arrives at peace, where the true peaceable one rests in a peaceable mind as in an interior Jerusalem; and as by the *six wings of the Cherub*, by which the mind of the true contemplative may be borne upward in the full illumination of supernal wisdom; and as through the *first six days*, in which the mind has to be exercised, that it may at last arrive at the sabbath of rest[^p312-3]; after our mind has beheld God *outside itself* through vestiges and in vestiges, *within itself* through the image and in the image, *above itself* through the likeness of the divine light shining upon us and in that light itself, according to what is possible in the state of the wayfarer and by the exercise of our mind; when at last on the *sixth* step it has come to this, that it speculates in the first and highest principle and the mediator *of God and men*, Jesus Christ[^p312-4], those things whose like can in no way be found in creatures, and which exceed all the perspicacity of the human intellect: it remains that, by speculating on these, it transcend and pass over not only this sensible world but even itself; in which passing-over Christ is the *way and the door*[^p312-5], Christ is the *ladder* and the *vehicle*, as the *propitiatory placed above the ark of God* and the *sacrament hidden from the ages*.

2. He who looks to that propitiatory with a full turning of the face, gazing upon him hanging on the cross through faith, hope and charity, devotion, admiration, exultation, appreciation, praise and jubilation; makes with him the *pasch*, that is, the passing-over[^p312-6], so that by the rod of the cross he may cross the Red Sea, entering the desert from Egypt, where he may taste the *hidden manna*, and may rest with Christ in the tomb as though outwardly dead, yet feeling, as far as is possible in the state of the wayfarer, what was said on the cross to the thief who clung to Christ: *This day thou shalt be with me in paradise.*

3. Which was also shown to blessed Francis, when, in the transport of contemplation on the lofty mountain — where I turned over in my mind these things which have been written — there appeared to him a Seraph of six wings fastened to a cross, as I and several others heard on that very spot from his companion, who was then with him; where he passed over into God through the transport of contemplation; and he was set as an example of perfect *contemplation*, as he had earlier been of *action*, as another *Jacob* and *Israel*[^p312-7], so that through him God might invite all truly spiritual men to a passing-over and a transport of mind of this kind, more by example than by word.

4. Now in this passing-over, if it is to be perfect, it is necessary that all intellectual operations be left behind, and that the whole apex of the affection be transferred and transformed into God. But this is mystical and most secret, which *no man knoweth but he that receiveth it*[^p312-8], nor does he receive it unless he desires it, nor does he desire it unless the fire of the Holy Spirit inflames him to the marrow, whom Christ sent upon the earth. And therefore the Apostle says[^p312-9] that this mystical wisdom is revealed through the Holy Spirit.

5. Since, therefore, nature can do nothing toward this, and industry can do little, little is to be given to inquiry and much to unction; little is to be given to the tongue, and very much to interior gladness; little is to be given
<!-- page 313 -->
to word and writing, and everything to the gift of God, namely to the Holy Spirit; little or nothing is to be given to the creature, and everything to the creating essence, to the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, saying with Dionysius[^p313-1] to God the Trinity: «Trinity superessential and superdivine and supremely good overseer of the theosophy of Christians, direct us into the superunknown and supershining and most sublime summit of the mystic utterances; where the new and absolute and unconvertible mysteries of theology are hidden, according to the supershining darkness of the silence that teaches secretly, in the most obscure — which is most supermanifest — supershining, and in which everything shines forth, and which superfills invisible intellects with the splendours of invisible supergoods». This to God. But to the friend for whom these things are written, let it be said with the same Dionysius: «But do thou, O friend, as concerns mystical visions, thy journey being made strong, forsake both the senses and the intellectual operations, and things sensible and invisible, and every non-being and being, and, so far as is possible, restore thyself unknowing to the unity of him who is above all essence and knowledge. For by an immeasurable and absolute transport of the pure mind, out of thyself and out of all things[^p313-2], forsaking all and freed from all, thou shalt ascend[^p313-3] to the superessential ray of the divine darkness».

6. But if you ask how these things come about, question grace, not doctrine; desire, not the intellect; the groaning of prayer, not the study of reading; the Bridegroom, not the master; God, not man; darkness, not clarity; not light, but the fire that wholly inflames and carries over into God by excessive unctions and most ardent affections. Which *fire* indeed is God, and its *furnace is in Jerusalem*[^p313-4], and Christ kindles it in the fervour of his most ardent passion, which he alone truly perceives who says: *My soul hath chosen hanging, and my bones death.* And he who loves this death can see God, because it is beyond doubt true: *Man shall not see me and live.* — Let us then die and enter into the darkness, let us impose silence upon our cares, our concupiscences and our phantasms; let us pass over with the crucified Christ *out of this world to the Father*[^p313-5], so that, the Father being shown to us, we may say with Philip: *It is enough for us*; let us hear with Paul: *My grace is sufficient for thee*; let us exult with David[^p313-6], saying: *My flesh and my heart have failed, God of my heart and my portion, God for ever. Blessed be the Lord for ever, and let all the people say: So be it, so be it.* Amen.

**HERE ENDS THE JOURNEY INTO GOD.**

**Register — additions frozen at c7, the chapter where *excessus* pays off.** ★ ***excessus* → "transport" throughout, and c7 is the vindication of that choice**, frozen at the pilot and defended at c4. The chapter uses the word five times in three senses that must stay one English word — the title's *excessus mentalis*, § 3's *excessus contemplationis* (twice, of Francis on La Verna), § 4's *mentis excessum*, and the Dionysian quotation's *purae mentis excessu* at § 5, which the apparatus itself glosses with the Greek **ἐκστάσει**. **Had "ecstasy" been spent on *excessus*, n. 2's Greek could not have been rendered at all**; and § 6's *excessivis unctionibus* takes "excessive unctions" in the ordinary English sense, in the same paragraph, without collision. — *transitus* → **"passing-over"** (hyphenated), and *transire* → "to pass over": § 2 makes *pascha* mean exactly *transitum*, and the Red Sea, the tomb and Francis's *in Deum transiit* are all the one word. "Transit" and "crossing" were both rejected as losing the paschal sense the note supplies. — *apex affectus* → **"the apex of the affection"**, keeping *apex* as at c1 (*apex mentis*) and *affectus* as at c4 and the capitula. — The Dionysian *super-* compounds are rendered one-to-one with English *super-* (*superessentialis* "superessential", *superdeus* "superdivine", *superincognitus* "superunknown", *superlucens* "supershining", *supermanifestissimus* "most supermanifest", *supersplendens* "supershining", *superimplere* "to superfill", *superbona* "supergoods", *superexcedere* "to exceed"), because the pile-up **is** the rhetorical point of the passage and varying them into idiomatic English destroys it. ⚠ *superoptime* is a **vocative adjective** agreeing with *inspector*, not an adverb — "supremely good overseer", not "supremely well". — *caligo* → **"darkness"** against *tenebrae* → "shadows" as frozen at c5; ⚠ but **§ 5's Dionysian *divinarum tenebrarum radium* takes "the ray of the divine darkness"**, because there *tenebrae* is the Dionysian term of art that the whole tradition names "the divine darkness", and rendering it "shadows" would sever the phrase from n. 3's *oratio in caligine* discussion, which is about this very passage. **The one place in the work where the c5 rule is deliberately set aside, and it is set aside on the apparatus's own evidence.** — *omne non ens et ens* → **"every non-being and being"**, on c5's frozen *esse*/*ens* distinction. — *inscius* → "unknowing", *insciu* adverbially. — *propitiatorium* → "the propitiatory" as frozen at c5–c6.
