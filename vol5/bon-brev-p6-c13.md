### Capitulum XIII. *De integritate matrimonii.*

De Sacramento *matrimonii* hoc in summa tenendum est, quod « matrimonium est coniunctio legitima maris et feminae, individuam vitae consuetudinem retinens[^p279-2] ». Haec autem coniunctio non solum fuit post peccatum, verum etiam ante peccatum; sed prius fuit institutum Sacramentum coniugii in *officium*, nunc autem non solum in *officium*, verum etiam in *remedium* contra libidinis morbum; prius significabat coniunctionem Dei et animae, nunc autem praeter hoc significat coniunctionem Christi et Ecclesiae et duarum naturarum in unitate personae. — *Introducitur* autem haec coniunctio in *esse* per liberum consensum animorum ex parte utriusque personae, exterius expressum in aliquo signo sensibili, *consummari* autem habet in copula carnali. Nam per verba de futuro dicitur matrimonium *initiari*, per verba de praesenti *ratificari*, sed per carnalem copulam habet *consummari*. — Huius autem Sacramenti tria sunt *bona*, « scilicet fides, proles et Sacramentum[^p279-3] »: et duodecim *impedimenta*, quae impediunt contrahendum et dirimunt iam contractum, quae in his versibus continentur:

*Error, conditio, votum, cognatio, crimen,*  
*Cultus disparitas, vis, ordo, ligamen, honestas;*  
*Si sis affinis, si forte coire nequibis:*  
*Haec socianda vetant coniugia, iuncta retractant.*

Ratio autem ad intelligentiam praedictorum haec est: quia principium nostrum reparativum, Verbum scilicet incarnatum, eo ipso quod *Verbum Dei*, est *fons sapientiae in excelsis*[^p279-4]; eo ipso quod *incarnatum*, est *clementiae* fons in terris; et ideo, eo ipso quod *Verbum increatum*, est humani generis *formativum* ex summa *sapientia*; eo ipso quod *incarnatum*, est ipsius *reformativum* ex summa *clementia*. Ideo enim humanum genus reparat per clementiam, quia prius reparabile fecit ex sapientia, quae pro suo summo ordine exigebat, quod genus humanum faceret potens *stare*, potens *labi*, potens etiam *reparari*, sicut in praecedentibus[^p279-5] ostensum fuit. Quoniam ergo Verbum Dei in sua sapientia hominem fecit, sicut et congruum fuit, potentem *stare*, *cadere* et *surgere*; hinc est, quod genus humanum eo modo propagandum ordinavit, quod ex ipso modo propagandi haberet quod manuduceret ipsum ad *standum*; haberet etiam quod manuduceret ad *remedium*, sicut etiam in propagatione illa est aliquid per peccatum, scilicet libido, quod traducit morbum. Et quia *status* hominis erat ex coniunctione ipsius animae ad Deum per amorem unitivum modo castissimo, singulari et individuo; *remedium* veniebat ex coniunctione divinae naturae et humanae in unitatem hypostasis et personae, unitatem, inquam, divina gratia introductam ut *singularem* et *individuam*: hinc est, quod Deus ab initio instituit, ut propagatio fieret per coniunctionem maris et feminae *individuam* et *singularem*, quae significaret *ante* peccatum coniunctionem Dei et animae, seu Dei et subcaelestis[^p279-6] hierarchiae, *post* peccatum vero coniunctionem Dei et humanae naturae, seu Christi et Ecclesiae; et ideo utrobique est *Sacramentum*, scilicet *ante* et *post*, licet alio et alio modo, quantum ad *significationem* et *usum*. Cum enim esset *Sacramentum*, antequam superveniret morbus; libido, quae supervenit per peccatum, potius habet per matrimonium excusari, quam ipsum valeat vitiare; quia non morbus medicamentum corrumpit, sed medicamentum habet curare morbum[^p279-7]. — Ex quo manifeste apparet, quid *sit* matrimonium, et qualiter divinitus *sit introductum* in *esse*.

Rursus, quia quaelibet praedictarum spiritualium unionum in Sacramento matrimonii significatarum est unio unius sicut *agentis* et influentis, alte-
<!-- page 280 -->
rius sicut *patientis* et suscipientis; et hoc, faciente amoris vinculo, qui ex mera *voluntate* procedit: hinc est, quod matrimonium debet esse coniunctio duplicis personae, differentis secundum rationem *agentis* et *patientis*, scilicet virilis sexus et muliebris, et hoc ex mero *consensu voluntatis*. — Et quia voluntas exterius non apparet nisi per signa ipsam exprimentia; hinc est, quod necesse est, ut consensus mutuus *exterius exprimatur*[^p280-1]. — Quia vero consensus ut de *futuro* non est proprie consensus, sed ipsius promissio; consensus vero ante commixtionem non facit plenariam unionem, quia nondum sunt *una caro*: hinc est, quod per verba de futuro dicitur matrimonium esse *initiatum*, de praesenti *confirmatum*, sed in carnali copula *consummatum*, quia tunc sunt *una caro* et *unum corpus* fiunt; ac per hoc plene significat unionem illam, quae est inter nos et Christum. Tunc enim plene traducitur corpus unius in corpus alterius secundum sui comparis potestatem[^p280-2] ad procreandam prolem.

Et ideo ipsius matrimonii sunt tria *bona*, scilicet *Sacramentum* propter vinculum insolubile, *fides* propter debiti solutionem, *proles* vero sicut effectus consequens ad utrumque.

Postremo, quia dicta coniunctio matrimonialis debet esse ex libero consensu procedens ad copulationem personarum distantium in unam matrimonii legem; et hoc potest duodecim modis impediri: hinc est, quod duodecim sunt impedimenta matrimonii; quod patet sic. — Ad matrimonialem namque consensum requiritur *libertas in consensu*, *libertas in consentiente* et *idoneitas ad coniunctionem*. Sed *libertas in consensu* tollitur per duo secundum duas partes involuntarii, scilicet per *ignorantiam* et per *violentiam*[^p280-3]. Et sic sunt duo impedimenta, scilicet *error* et *violentia*. — *Libertas* autem *in consentiente* tollitur per hoc, quod quis est alligatus alteri, et hoc vel *Deo*, vel *homini*. Si *Deo*, hoc est vel per *votum expressum*, vel per aliquid, quod habet *votum annexum*. Primum est in *voto*, secundum in *ordine*. Si autem *homini*, hoc dupliciter: aut per *ligamen existens*, aut per *praecedens*. Primum est in *ligamine*, quo quis ligatus est uxori; secundum est in *crimine*, quando moechus, vel moecha machinatus est in mortem coniugis, vel, ipso vivente, fidem de contrahendo dedit. Et sic sunt quatuor impedimenta, scilicet *votum*, *ordo*, *ligamen* et *crimen*. — *Idoneitas* autem *ad unionem* est distantia personarum conveniens, quae tollitur per nimiam *proximitatem*, vel per nimiam *elongationem*. Nimia autem *proximitas* venit[^p280-4] per *generationem*, sive per aliquid *simile generationi*, ut est *cognatio legalis* et *spiritualis*; aut venit per *commixtionem sexuum*, aut per *contractionem sponsalium*. Et sic sunt tria impedimenta, scilicet *cognatio*, *affinitas* et *publicae iustitiae honestas*. — Nimia autem *distantia* aut attenditur quantum ad ea quae sunt *naturae*, ut cum non possunt commisceri carnaliter; aut quantum ad ea quae sunt *fortunae*, quae non sunt in nostra potestate, ut patet, cum est unus servus, et alter liber; aut quantum ad ea quae sunt *religionis christianae*, ut cum quis est baptizatus, alter vero minime. Et sic sunt tria impedimenta, scilicet *impotentia coeundi*, *error conditionis* et *disparitas religionis*. — Ac per hoc sunt in universo duodecim impedimenta, quae, Spiritu sancto dictante, introducta sunt in Ecclesia, cui, etsi omnia Sacramenta sint commissa, potissime[^p280-5] coniugii Sacramentum commissum est regulandum propter *varietates*, quae circa hoc Sacramentum incidere possunt, et propter *morbum* annexum, qui est maxime infectivus et minime tenens modum; et ideo ipsius Ecclesiae est *gradus cognationum* limitare, sicut pro tempore expedire videt, et *personas legitimas* vel *illegitimas* iudicare et *divortia facere*. Sed nunquam debet nec potest matrimonium, quod legitime introductum est, annullare, quia *quos Deus coniunxit non potest homo*, quantumcumque sit magnae potentiae, *separare*[^p280-6], cum ipsius Dei iudicio iudicandi remaneant universi.

## English

### Chapter XIII. *On the integrity of marriage.*

Concerning the Sacrament of *marriage* this must be held in summary: that « marriage is the lawful conjunction of a male and a female, retaining an undivided common manner of life[^p279-2] ». Now this conjunction was not only after sin, but also before sin; but formerly the Sacrament of marriage was instituted unto an *office*, whereas now it is instituted not only unto an *office*, but also unto a *remedy* against the disease of lust; formerly it signified the conjunction of God and the soul, whereas now, besides this, it signifies the conjunction of Christ and the Church and of the two natures in the unity of a person. — Now this conjunction *is introduced* into *being* through the free consent of minds on the part of each person, outwardly expressed in some sensible sign, but it has *to be consummated* in carnal union. For through words concerning the future marriage is said *to be initiated*, through words concerning the present *to be ratified*, but through carnal union it has *to be consummated*. — Now of this Sacrament there are three *goods*, « namely faith, offspring and Sacrament[^p279-3] »; and twelve *impediments*, which impede the contracting and dissolve what is already contracted, which are contained in these verses:

*Error, condition, vow, kinship, crime,*  
*Disparity of worship, force, [holy] order, [prior] bond, [public] decency;*  
*If you be a kinsman by affinity, if perchance you be unable to have intercourse:*  
*These things forbid marriages that are to be allied, and undo them when joined.*

Now the reason for the understanding of the aforesaid is this: since our reparative principle, the Word namely incarnate, by this very fact that he is the *Word of God*, is the *fount of wisdom on high*[^p279-4]; by this very fact that he is *incarnate*, is the fount of *clemency* on earth; and therefore, by this very fact that he is the *uncreated Word*, he is the *formative* [principle] of the human race out of the highest *wisdom*; by this very fact that he is *incarnate*, he is its *reformative* [principle] out of the highest *clemency*. For he repairs the human race through clemency for this reason, that he first made it reparable out of wisdom, which for its own highest order required that he should make the human race able *to stand*, able *to fall*, able also *to be repaired*, as was shown in the preceding[^p279-5]. Since therefore the Word of God in his wisdom made man — as also it was fitting — able *to stand*, *to fall* and *to rise*; hence it is that he ordained the human race to be propagated in that mode by which, from the very mode of propagating, it should have that which might lead it by the hand *to standing*; it should have also that which might lead it by the hand *to a remedy*, just as also in that propagation there is something through sin, namely lust, which transmits the disease. And because the *state* of man was from the conjunction of his soul to God through a unitive love in a mode most chaste, single and undivided; the *remedy* came from the conjunction of the divine nature and the human into the unity of hypostasis and of person — a unity, I say, introduced by divine grace as *single* and *undivided*: hence it is that God from the beginning instituted that propagation should come about through an *undivided* and *single* conjunction of male and female, which should signify *before* sin the conjunction of God and the soul, or of God and the subcelestial[^p279-6] hierarchy, but *after* sin the conjunction of God and human nature, or of Christ and the Church; and therefore in both cases there is a *Sacrament*, namely *before* and *after*, although in one mode and another, as regards *signification* and *use*. For since it was a *Sacrament* before the disease supervened, lust, which supervened through sin, is rather to be excused through marriage than able to vitiate it; because the disease does not corrupt the medicine, but the medicine has to cure the disease[^p279-7]. — From which it manifestly appears what marriage *is*, and how it *was introduced* divinely into *being*.

Again, since each of the aforesaid spiritual unions signified in the Sacrament of marriage is a union of the one as of the *agent* and of that which flows in, of the o-
<!-- page 280 -->
ther as of the *patient* and of that which receives; and this, by the bond of love effecting it, which proceeds from the mere *will*: hence it is that marriage ought to be a conjunction of a twofold person, differing according to the account of the *agent* and of the *patient*, namely of the male sex and of the female, and this from the mere *consent of the will*. — And because the will does not outwardly appear save through signs expressing it; hence it is that it is necessary that the mutual consent *be outwardly expressed*[^p280-1]. — But because consent as concerning the *future* is not properly consent, but a promise of it; and consent before the mingling does not make a full union, because they are not yet *one flesh*: hence it is that through words concerning the future marriage is said to be *initiated*, through words concerning the present *confirmed*, but in carnal union *consummated*, because then they are *one flesh* and become *one body*; and through this it fully signifies that union which is between us and Christ. For then the body of the one is fully transferred into the body of the other according to the power of one's own consort[^p280-2] for the procreating of offspring.

And therefore of marriage itself there are three *goods*, namely the *Sacrament* on account of the indissoluble bond, *faith* on account of the rendering of the debt, and *offspring* as an effect following upon both.

Lastly, since the said matrimonial conjunction ought to be one proceeding from free consent to the coupling of persons standing apart into one law of marriage; and this can be impeded in twelve modes: hence it is that there are twelve impediments of marriage, which appears thus. — For to matrimonial consent there is required *liberty in the consent*, *liberty in the one consenting*, and *fitness for the conjunction*. But *liberty in the consent* is taken away through two things according to the two parts of the involuntary, namely through *ignorance* and through *violence*[^p280-3]. And thus there are two impediments, namely *error* and *violence*. — But *liberty in the one consenting* is taken away through this, that someone is bound to another, and this either to *God* or to *man*. If to *God*, this is either through an *express vow* or through something which has a *vow annexed*. The first is in the *vow*, the second in *order*. But if to *man*, this in two ways: either through an *existing bond* or through a *preceding* one. The first is in the *bond* by which someone is bound to a wife; the second is in *crime*, when an adulterer or an adulteress has machinated the death of the spouse, or, while that one is living, has given a pledge concerning contracting. And thus there are four impediments, namely *vow*, *order*, *bond* and *crime*. — But *fitness for the union* is a fitting distance of persons, which is taken away through too great *nearness* or through too great *remoteness*. Now too great *nearness* comes[^p280-4] through *generation*, or through something *like generation*, as is *legal* and *spiritual kinship*; or it comes through the *mingling of the sexes*, or through the *contracting of betrothals*. And thus there are three impediments, namely *kinship*, *affinity* and *the decency of public justice*. — But too great *distance* is attended to either as regards those things which are of *nature*, as when they cannot be mingled carnally; or as regards those things which are of *fortune*, which are not in our power, as is plain when the one is a slave and the other free; or as regards those things which are of the *Christian religion*, as when one is baptized but the other not at all. And thus there are three impediments, namely *impotence for intercourse*, *error of condition* and *disparity of religion*. — And through this there are in all twelve impediments, which, the Holy Spirit dictating, have been introduced in the Church, to which, although all the Sacraments are committed, most especially[^p280-5] the Sacrament of marriage has been committed to be regulated, on account of the *varieties* which can befall concerning this Sacrament, and on account of the annexed *disease*, which is most of all infective and least of all keeps measure; and therefore it belongs to the Church herself to limit the *degrees of kinship*, as she sees to be expedient for the time, and to judge persons *legitimate* or *illegitimate* and to *make divorces*. But never ought she, nor is she able, to annul a marriage which has been legitimately introduced, because *those whom God has joined man cannot*, however great his power may be, *separate*[^p280-6], since all men remain to be judged by the judgment of God himself.
