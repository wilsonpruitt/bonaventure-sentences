## Latin

### Capitulum IX. *De exercitio gratiae respectu agendorum, praeceptorum et consiliorum.*

De *praeceptis* autem legis divinae hoc tenendum est, quod in lege *Moysaica* sunt praecepta *iudicialia, figuralia* et *moralia*, utpote decem praecepta decalogi in duabus tabulis conscripta *digito Dei*[^p262-4]. — Lex autem *evangelica iudicialia* temperat auferendo, *figuralia* evacuat adimplendo, *moralia* consummat adiiciendo. Adiicit autem documenta instruentia, promissa excitantia et consilia perficientia; cuiusmodi sunt consilium paupertatis, consilium obedientiae et consilium castitatis, ad quae implenda invitat Christus Dominus noster eum qui vult esse perfectus[^p262-5].

Ratio autem ad intelligentiam praedictorum haec est: quia primum principium, sicut est summe *bonum* in se ipso, sic est summe *iustum* in opere suo et in universi regimine disponendo. Quoniam ergo summe *iusti* est zelare iustitiam non tantum in se, verum etiam in altero; iustitia autem consistit in conformando se regulis iuris: hinc est, quod divinae iustitiae est regulas iustitiae homini imprimere et exprimere, non tantum *dictando* per modum veritatis erudientis, sed etiam *praecipiendo* et *obligando* per modum voluntatis imperantis[^p262-6]. Et quoniam gratia facit voluntatem nostram divinae voluntati conformem, ipsius est disponere nos ad obtemperandum et subiacendum illis regulis iustitiae secundum dictamen legis divinitus datae.

Rursus, quoniam divinis imperiis dupliciter obtemperare contingit, videlicet ex *timore poenae*, vel ex *amore iustitiae*; et primum est imperfectorum, secundum autem perfectorum; ideo Deus duplicem homini contulit legem: unam timoris, alteram amoris, unam in servitutem generantem et alteram in adoptionem filiorum Dei transferentem[^p262-7]. Ac per hoc, quia timentibus et imperfectis competit, ut *terreantur* per iudicia et *manuducantur* per signa et *dirigantur* nihilominus per praecepta; hinc est, quod lex *Moysaica*, quae est lex timoris, continet *iudicialia* et *figuralia* et *moralia*. — Perfectis autem et amantibus convenit *aperta instructio* documentorum, *larga promissio* praemiorum et *alta perfectio* consiliorum; hinc est, quod lex *evangelica* continet

<!-- page 263 -->
ista tria. Et ideo dicitur lex Moysaica differre ab evangelica, quia illa *figurae*, haec *veritatis*; illa lex *poenae*, haec *gratiae*; illa *litteralis*, ista *spiritualis*; illa *occidens*, ista *vivificans*[^p263-1]; illa *timoris*, ista *amoris*; illa *servitutis*, ista *libertatis*; illa *oneris* et ista *facilitatis*.

Postremo, quoniam regulae spectantes ad iustitiae necessitatem continentur in divinis praeceptis; et iustitiae est « ius suum unicuique reddere[^p263-2] »: necesse est, quod praecepta moralia quaedam sint, quae ordinant nos ad *Deum*; quaedam ad *proximum* secundum duplex caritatis praeceptum; quod Spiritus sanctus voluit insinuare per duarum mysterium tabularum, et ideo *digito Dei scriptae* dicuntur. — Et quia Deus est trinus, Pater scilicet et Filius et Spiritus sanctus, cui competit summa maiestas *adoranda*, veritas *profitenda* et caritas *acceptanda*, secundum vim irascibilem, rationalem et concupiscibilem, per actum operis, oris et cordis: ideo triplex est mandatum primae tabulae, correspondens tribus praemissis, scilicet *adorationis* subiectivae, *iurationis* veridicae et *sabbatizationis* sacratae.

Quia vero proximus est imago Trinitatis, et ei, in quantum praefert imaginem Patris, debetur *pietas*; in quantum praefert imaginem Filii, debetur *veracitas*[^p263-3], in quantum praefert imaginem Spiritus sancti, debetur *benignitas*: hinc est, quod septem sunt mandata, quae ad secundam tabulam spectant. Nam penes *pietatem* sunt *duo*: unum, quod pietatem mandat, scilicet de patre honorando; alterum, quod impietatem vetat, scilicet de non occidendo. Penes *veracitatem*, quae principaliter consistit in verbo, accipitur *unum*, scilicet de falso testimonio non ferendo. Penes *benignitatem*, cui opponitur cupiditas et concupiscentia, quarum utraque potest esse in opere, vel in corde, accipiuntur *quatuor*, scilicet *non moechaberis, non concupisces uxorem, non furtum facies, et non concupisces rem alienam*[^p263-4]. Et habent haec ordinari secundum maiora vel minora nocumenta, per quae infringi potest iustitia. Et sic regulae spectantes ad iustitiae necessitatem debent contineri in docem praeceptis.

Quoniam autem iustitia ad perfectionem pervenit, dum perfecte se elongat a malo et quantum ad *culpam* et quantum ad *causam*; et omne malum oritur ex triplici radice, scilicet concupiscentiae carnis, concupiscentiae oculorum et superbiae vitae[^p263-5]: hinc est, quod tria sunt *consilia* evangelica, perfecte nos elongantia a triplici radice praedicta. Quae ideo sunt consilia, quia, ut perfecte faciant recedere a malo non solum separant ab illicitis, verum etiam a licitis et concessis, quae possunt esse occasio mali; ac per hoc non solum iustitiam continent *sufficientem*, verum etiam *abundantem*[^p263-6], secundum quod competit perfectioni evangelicae legis et exercitationi gratiae perficientis.

## English

### Chapter IX. *On the exercise of grace with respect to the things to be done, the precepts and the counsels.*

Concerning the *precepts* of the divine law this is to be held: that in the *Mosaic* law there are *judicial*, *figural* and *moral* precepts — as, for instance, the ten precepts of the decalogue written on two tables by the *finger of God*[^p262-4]. — But the *evangelical* law tempers the *judicial* by taking them away, empties the *figural* by fulfilling them, and consummates the *moral* by adding to them. And it adds instructing teachings, arousing promises and perfecting counsels; of which sort are the counsel of poverty, the counsel of obedience and the counsel of chastity, to the fulfilling of which Christ our Lord invites him who wishes to be perfect[^p262-5].

Now the reason for the understanding of the foresaid is this: since the first principle, just as it is supremely *good* in itself, so is it supremely *just* in its work and in the ordering of the government of the universe. Since, therefore, it belongs to the supremely *just* to be zealous for justice not only in himself but also in another, and since justice consists in conforming oneself to the rules of right: hence it is that it belongs to the divine justice to imprint and to express the rules of justice upon man, not only by *dictating* after the manner of instructing truth, but also by *enjoining* and *obliging* after the manner of a commanding will[^p262-6]. And since grace makes our will conformed to the divine will, it belongs to grace to dispose us to obey and to submit to those rules of justice according to the dictate of the law divinely given.

Again, since it happens that the divine commands are obeyed in two ways, namely out of *fear of punishment* or out of *love of justice*, and the first belongs to the imperfect, the second to the perfect: therefore God conferred upon man a twofold law — one of fear, the other of love; one begetting unto servitude and the other transferring unto the adoption of the sons of God[^p262-7]. And through this, because it befits those who fear and are imperfect that they be *terrified* by judgments and *led by the hand* by signs and none the less *directed* by precepts, hence it is that the *Mosaic* law, which is the law of fear, contains *judicial* and *figural* and *moral* precepts. — But for the perfect and the loving there befits the *open instruction* of teachings, the *generous promise* of rewards and the *lofty perfection* of counsels; hence it is that the *evangelical* law contains

<!-- page 263 -->
these three. And therefore the Mosaic law is said to differ from the evangelical, because that one is of *figures*, this of *truth*; that law is of *punishment*, this of *grace*; that is *literal*, this *spiritual*; that *kills*, this *gives life*[^p263-1]; that is of *fear*, this of *love*; that of *servitude*, this of *liberty*; that of *burden* and this of *ease*.

Last, since the rules that regard the necessity of justice are contained in the divine precepts, and it belongs to justice « to render to each his own[^p263-2] »: it is necessary that there be certain moral precepts which order us toward *God*, and certain ones toward our *neighbour*, according to the twofold precept of charity — which the Holy Spirit willed to intimate through the mystery of the two tables, and for that reason they are said to be *written by the finger of God*. — And because God is three, namely Father and Son and Holy Spirit, to whom belongs the highest majesty to be *adored*, truth to be *professed* and charity to be *accepted*, according to the irascible, the rational and the concupiscible power, through the act of work, of mouth and of heart: therefore the commandment of the first table is threefold, corresponding to the three things premised, namely of *adoration* that submits, of *swearing* that speaks truth and of *sabbath-keeping* that is hallowed.

But because the neighbour is an image of the Trinity, and to him, in so far as he bears the image of the Father, *piety* is owed; in so far as he bears the image of the Son, *truthfulness* is owed[^p263-3]; in so far as he bears the image of the Holy Spirit, *kindness* is owed: hence it is that there are seven commandments which regard the second table. For under *piety* there are *two*: one which commands piety, namely concerning the honouring of a father; the other which forbids impiety, namely concerning not killing. Under *truthfulness*, which consists principally in the word, *one* is taken, namely concerning not bearing false witness. Under *kindness*, to which are opposed cupidity and concupiscence, of which each can be in deed or in heart, *four* are taken, namely *thou shalt not commit adultery, thou shalt not covet a wife, thou shalt not steal, and thou shalt not covet another's goods*[^p263-4]. And these have to be ordered according to greater or lesser harms, by which justice can be broken. And so the rules that regard the necessity of justice must be contained in ten precepts.

But since justice comes to perfection when it perfectly withdraws itself from evil both as regards *fault* and as regards *cause*, and every evil arises from a threefold root, namely the concupiscence of the flesh, the concupiscence of the eyes and the pride of life[^p263-5]: hence it is that there are three evangelical *counsels*, perfectly withdrawing us from the aforesaid threefold root. And they are counsels for this reason, that, in order to make one perfectly withdraw from evil, they separate not only from things unlawful but also from things lawful and permitted which can be an occasion of evil; and through this they contain justice not only *sufficient* but even *abundant*[^p263-6], according as befits the perfection of the evangelical law and the exercise of perfecting grace.
