## Latin

### Cap. V. *De speculatione divinae unitatis per eius nomen primarium, quod est esse.*

1. Quoniam autem contingit contemplari Deum non solum *extra nos* et *intra nos*, verum etiam *supra nos*: *extra* per vestigium, *intra* per imaginem et *supra* per lumen, quod est signatum supra mentem nostram[^p308-3], quod est lumen Veritatis aeternae, cum « ipsa mens nostra immediate ab ipsa Veritate formetur »; qui exercitati sunt in primo modo intraverunt iam in *atrium* ante tabernaculum; qui vero in secundo, intraverunt in *sancta*; qui autem in tertio, intrant cum summo Pontifice in *sancta sanctorum*; ubi supra arcam sunt *Cherubim gloriae obumbrantia propitiatorium*; per quae intelligimus duos modos seu gradus contemplandi Dei invisibilia et aeterna, quorum unus versatur circa essentialia Dei, alius vero circa propria personarum.

2. Primus modus primo et principaliter defigit aspectum in ipsum *esse*, dicens, quod *qui est*[^p308-4] est primum nomen Dei. Secundus modus defigit aspectum in ipsum *bonum*, dicens, hoc esse primum nomen Dei. Primum spectat potissime ad *vetus testamentum*, quod maxime praedicat divinae essentiae unitatem; unde dictum est Moysi[^p308-5]: *Ego sum qui sum*; secundum ad *novum*, quod determinat personarum pluralitatem, *baptizando in nomine Patris et Filii et Spiritus sancti*. Ideo magister noster Christus, volens adolescentem, qui servaverat Legem, ad evangelicam levare perfectionem, nomen *bonitatis* Deo principaliter et praecise attribuit. *Nemo*, inquit, *bonus nisi solus Deus*. Damascenus[^p308-6] igitur sequens Moysen dicit, quod *qui est* est primum nomen Dei; Dionysius sequens Christum dicit, quod *bonum* est primum nomen Dei.

3. Volens igitur contemplari Dei invisibilia quoad *essentiae unitatem* primo defigat aspectum in ipsum *esse*[^p308-7] et videat, ipsum esse adeo in se *certissimum*, quod non potest cogitari non esse, quia ipsum *esse purissimum* non occurrit nisi in plena fuga *non-esse*, sicut et *nihil* in plena fuga *esse*. Sicut igitur *omnino nihil* nihil habet de *esse* nec de eius conditionibus; sic econtra ipsum *esse* nihil habet de *non-esse*, nec actu nec potentia, nec secundum veritatem rei nec secundum aestimationem nostram. Cum autem *non-esse* privatio sit essendi, non cadit in intellectum nisi per *esse*[^p308-8]; *esse* autem non cadit per aliud, quia omne, quod intelligitur, aut intelligitur ut *non ens*, aut ut *ens in potentia*, aut ut *ens in actu*. Si igitur *non ens* non potest intelligi nisi per *ens*, et *ens in potentia* non nisi per *ens in actu*; et *esse* nominat ipsum purum actum entis: *esse* igitur est quod primo cadit in intellectu, et illud *esse* est quod est purus actus[^p308-9]. Sed hoc non est *esse particulare*, quod
<!-- page 309 -->
est *esse arctatum*, quia permixtum est cum potentia, nec *esse analogum*, quia minime habet de actu, eo quod minime est. Restat igitur, quod illud *esse* est *esse divinum*.

4. Mira igitur est caecitas intellectus, qui non considerat illud quod prius videt et sine quo nihil potest cognoscere. Sed sicut oculus intentus in varias[^p309-1] colorum differentias lucem, per quam videt cetera, non videt, et si videt, non advertit; sic oculus mentis nostrae, intentus in *entia particularia* et *universalia*, *ipsum esse extra omne genus*, licet primo occurrat menti, et per ipsum alia, tamen non advertit. Unde verissime apparet, quod « sicut oculus vespertilionis se habet ad lucem, ita se habet oculus mentis nostrae ad manifestissima naturae[^p309-2] »; quia assuefactus ad tenebras entium et phantasmata sensibilium, cum ipsam lucem summi *esse* intuetur, videtur sibi nihil videre; non intelligens, quod ipsa caligo summa est mentis nostrae illuminatio[^p309-3], sicut, quando videt oculus puram lucem, videtur sibi nihil videre.

5. Vide igitur ipsum purissimum *esse*, si potes, et occurrit tibi, quod ipsum non potest cogitari ut ab alio acceptum; ac per hoc necessario cogitatur ut omnimode primum, quod nec de nihilo nec de aliquo[^p309-4] potest esse. Quid enim est per se, si ipsum *esse* non est per se nec a se? — Occurrit etiam tibi ut carens omnino non-esse, ac per hoc ut nunquam incipiens, nunquam desinens, sed *aeternum*. — Occurrit etiam tibi ut nullo modo in se habens, nisi quod est ipsum *esse*, ac per hoc ut cum nullo compositum, sed *simplicissimum*. — Occurrit tibi ut nihil habens possibilitatis, quia omne possibile aliquo modo habet aliquid de *non-esse*, ac per hoc ut summe *actualissimum*. — Occurrit ut nihil habens defectibilitatis, ac per hoc ut *perfectissimum*. — Occurrit postremo ut nihil habens diversificationis, ac per hoc ut *summe unum*.

*Esse* igitur, quod est *esse purum* et *esse simpliciter* et *esse absolutum*, est *esse primarium, aeternum, simplicissimum, actualissimum, perfectissimum et summe unum*.

6. Et sunt haec ita certa, quod non potest ab intelligente ipsum *esse* cogitari horum oppositum, et[^p309-5] unum horum necessario infert aliud. Nam quia simpliciter est *esse*, ideo simpliciter *primum*; quia simpliciter primum, ideo non est ab alio factum, nec a se ipso potuit, ergo *aeternum*. Item, quia primum et aeternum; ideo non ex aliis, ergo *simplicissimum*. Item, quia primum, aeternum et simplicissimum; ideo nihil est in eo possibilitatis cum actu permixtum, et ideo *actualissimum*. Item, quia primum, aeternum, simplicissimum, actualissimum; ideo *perfectissimum*; tali omnino nihil *deficit*, neque aliqua potest *fieri additio*. Quia primum, aeternum, simplicissimum, actualissimum, perfectissimum; ideo *summe unum*. Quod enim per omnimodam superabundantiam dicitur dicitur respectu omnium. « Quod etiam simpliciter per superabundantiam dicitur, impossibile est, ut conveniat nisi uni soli[^p309-6] ». Unde si Deus nominat *esse* primarium, aeternum, simplicissimum, actualissimum, perfectissimum; impossibile est, ipsum cogitari non esse, nec esse nisi *unum solum*. *Audi* igitur, *Israel, Deus tuus Deus unus est*[^p309-7]. — Si hoc vides in pura mentis simplicitate, aliqualiter perfunderis aeternae lucis illustratione.

7. Sed habes unde subleveris in admirationem. Nam ipsum *esse* est *primum* et *novissimum*, est *aeternum* et *praesentissimum*, est *simplicissimum* et *maximum*, est *actualissimum* et *immutabilissimum*, est *perfectissimum* et *immensum*, est *summe unum* et tamen *omnimodum*. — Si haec pura mente miraris, maiore luce perfunderis, dum ulterius vides, quia *ideo* est *novissimum*, quia *primum*. Quia enim est primum, omnia operatur propter se ipsum; et ideo necesse est, quod sit finis ultimus, initium et consummatio, *alpha et omega*[^p309-8]. — Ideo est *praesentissimum*, quia *aeternum*. Quia enim aeternum, non fluit ab alio nec deficit a se ipso nec decurrit ab
<!-- page 310 -->
uno in aliud: ergo nec habet praeteritum nec futurum, sed *esse praesens* tantum. — Ideo *maximum*, quia *simplicissimum*. Quia enim simplicissimum in essentia, ideo maximum in virtute, quia virtus, quanto plus est unita, tanto plus est infinita[^p310-1]. — Ideo *immutabilissimum*, quia *actualissimum*. Quia enim actualissimum est, ideo est actus purus; et quod tale est nihil novi acquirit, nihil habitum perdit, ac per hoc non potest mutari. — Ideo *immensum*, quia *perfectissimum*. Quia enim perfectissimum, nihil potest cogitari ultra ipsum melius, nobilius nec dignius, ac per hoc nihil maius; et omne tale est immensum. — Ideo *omnimodum*, quia *summe unum*. Quod enim summe unum est est omnis multitudinis universale principium; ac per hoc ipsum est universalis omnium causa efficiens, exemplans et terminans, sicut « causa essendi, ratio intelligendi et ordo vivendi[^p310-2] ». Est igitur *omnimodum* non sicut omnium essentia, sed sicut cunctarum essentiarum superexcellentissima et universalissima et sufficientissima causa; cuius virtus, quia summe unita in essentia, ideo summe infinitissima et multiplicissima in efficacia.

8. Rursus revertentes dicamus: quia igitur *esse* purissimum et absolutum, quod est *simpliciter esse*, est *primarium* et *novissimum*, ideo est omnium origo et finis consummans. — Quia *aeternum* et *praesentissimum*, ideo omnes durationes ambit et intrat, quasi simul existens earum centrum et circumferentia. — Quia *simplicissimum* et *maximum*, ideo totum intra omnia et totum extra, ac per hoc « est *sphaera intelligibilis, cuius centrum est ubique et circumferentia nusquam*[^p310-3] ». — Quia *actualissimum* et *immutabilissimum*, ideo « *stabile manens moveri dat universa* ». — Quia *perfectissimum* et *immensum*, ideo *est intra omnia, non inclusum, extra omnia, non exclusum, supra omnia, non elatum, infra omnia, non prostratum*. — Quia vero est *summe unum* et *omnimodum*, ideo est *omnia in omnibus*[^p310-4], quamvis omnia sint multa, et ipsum non sit nisi unum; et hoc, quia per simplicissimam *unitatem*, serenissimam *veritatem* et sincerissimam *bonitatem* est in eo omnis *virtuositas*, omnis *exemplaritas* et omnis *communicabilitas*; ac per hoc *ex ipso et per ipsum et in ipso sunt omnia*[^p310-5], et hoc, quia *omnipotens, omnisciens* et *omnimode bonum*, quod perfecte videre est esse beatum, sicut dictum est Moysi: *Ego ostendam tibi omne bonum*.

## English

### Ch. V. *On the speculation of the divine unity through its primary name, which is being.*

1. But since it is given us to contemplate God not only *outside us* and *within us*, but also *above us* — *outside* through the vestige, *within* through the image, and *above* through the light which is sealed upon our mind[^p308-3], which is the light of eternal Truth, since « our mind itself is formed immediately by Truth itself » — those who have been exercised in the first way have already entered the *court* before the tabernacle; those in the second have entered into the *holy place*; those in the third enter with the high Priest into the *holy of holies*, where above the ark are *the Cherubim of glory overshadowing the propitiatory*. By these we understand two ways or steps of contemplating the invisible and eternal things of God, of which the one is occupied about the essential attributes of God, the other about the properties of the persons.

2. The first way first and principally fixes its gaze upon *being* itself, saying that *he who is*[^p308-4] is the first name of God. The second way fixes its gaze upon *the good* itself, saying that this is the first name of God. The first looks chiefly to the *old testament*, which most of all proclaims the unity of the divine essence; whence it was said to Moses[^p308-5]: *I am who am*. The second looks to the *new*, which determines the plurality of the persons, *baptizing in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit*. Therefore our master Christ, wishing to raise to evangelical perfection the young man who had kept the Law, attributed to God principally and precisely the name of *goodness*. *No one*, he says, *is good but God alone*. The Damascene[^p308-6], then, following Moses, says that *he who is* is the first name of God; Dionysius, following Christ, says that *the good* is the first name of God.

3. He, therefore, who wishes to contemplate the invisible things of God as regards *the unity of the essence*, let him first fix his gaze upon *being* itself[^p308-7], and let him see that being itself is so *most certain* in itself that it cannot be thought not to be, because the *most pure being* itself does not occur except in the full flight of *non-being*, just as *nothing* does not occur except in the full flight of *being*. As therefore *utter nothing* has nothing of *being* nor of its conditions, so on the contrary *being* itself has nothing of *non-being*, neither in act nor in potency, neither according to the truth of the thing nor according to our estimation. But since *non-being* is a privation of being, it does not fall into the intellect except through *being*[^p308-8]; and *being* does not fall through another, since everything that is understood is understood either as *a non-being*, or as *a being in potency*, or as *a being in act*. If therefore *a non-being* cannot be understood except through *a being*, and *a being in potency* only through *a being in act*; and *being* names the pure act itself of a being: *being*, therefore, is what first falls into the intellect, and that *being* is what is pure act[^p308-9]. But this is not *particular being*, which
<!-- page 309 -->
is *restricted being*, since it is mingled with potency; nor *analogous being*, since it has the least of act, in that it least is. It remains, therefore, that that *being* is *divine being*.

4. Wonderful, therefore, is the blindness of the intellect, which does not consider that which it sees before all else and without which it can know nothing. But just as the eye, intent upon the various[^p309-1] differences of colours, does not see the light through which it sees the rest — and if it does see it, does not advert to it — so the eye of our mind, intent upon *particular* and *universal beings*, does not advert to *being itself, which is beyond every genus*, although it occurs first to the mind, and through it all else. Whence it appears most truly that « as the eye of the bat is disposed toward the light, so is the eye of our mind disposed toward the things most manifest in nature[^p309-2] »; because, accustomed to the shadows of beings and to the phantasms of sensible things, when it beholds the light itself of the supreme *being*, it seems to itself to see nothing — not understanding that that very darkness is the supreme illumination of our mind[^p309-3], just as, when the eye sees pure light, it seems to itself to see nothing.

5. See, therefore, the most pure *being* itself, if you can, and it occurs to you that it cannot be thought as received from another; and through this it is necessarily thought as in every mode first, which can be neither from nothing nor from anything[^p309-4]. For what is through itself, if *being* itself is not through itself nor from itself? — It occurs to you also as wholly lacking non-being, and through this as never beginning, never ceasing, but *eternal*. — It occurs to you also as having in itself in no way anything but what is *being* itself, and through this as composed with nothing, but *most simple*. — It occurs to you as having nothing of possibility, since everything possible has in some way something of *non-being*, and through this as supremely *most actual*. — It occurs as having nothing of defectibility, and through this as *most perfect*. — It occurs lastly as having nothing of diversification, and through this as *supremely one*.

*Being*, therefore, which is *pure being* and *being simply* and *absolute being*, is *primary being, eternal, most simple, most actual, most perfect and supremely one*.

6. And these things are so certain that their opposite cannot be thought by one who understands *being* itself, and[^p309-5] one of them necessarily infers another. For since it is simply *being*, therefore it is simply *first*; since simply first, therefore it is not made by another, nor could it be by itself, therefore *eternal*. Likewise, since first and eternal, therefore not out of others, therefore *most simple*. Likewise, since first, eternal and most simple, therefore there is in it nothing of possibility mingled with act, and therefore *most actual*. Likewise, since first, eternal, most simple, most actual, therefore *most perfect*; to such a one absolutely nothing *is lacking*, nor can any *addition be made*. Since first, eternal, most simple, most actual, most perfect, therefore *supremely one*. For what is said by omnimodal superabundance is said in respect of all things. « What also is said simply by superabundance, it is impossible that it should belong to any but one alone[^p309-6]. » Whence, if God names *being* that is primary, eternal, most simple, most actual, most perfect, it is impossible that it be thought not to be, or to be anything but *one alone*. *Hear*, therefore, *Israel: the Lord thy God is one God*[^p309-7]. — If you see this in the pure simplicity of the mind, you will be in some measure flooded with the illumination of eternal light.

7. But you have whence you may be lifted up into wonder. For *being* itself is *first* and *last*, is *eternal* and *most present*, is *most simple* and *greatest*, is *most actual* and *most immutable*, is *most perfect* and *immeasurable*, is *supremely one* and yet *omnimodal*. — If you wonder at these things with a pure mind, you will be flooded with a greater light, when you see further that *therefore* it is *last*, because *first*. For since it is first, it works all things for its own sake; and therefore it is necessary that it be the last end, the beginning and the consummation, *alpha and omega*[^p309-8]. — Therefore it is *most present*, because *eternal*. For since it is eternal, it does not flow from another nor fail from itself nor run from
<!-- page 310 -->
one thing into another: therefore it has neither past nor future, but *present being* only. — Therefore *greatest*, because *most simple*. For since it is most simple in essence, therefore it is greatest in power, since power, the more it is united, so much the more is it infinite[^p310-1]. — Therefore *most immutable*, because *most actual*. For since it is most actual, therefore it is pure act; and what is such acquires nothing new, loses nothing it has, and through this cannot be changed. — Therefore *immeasurable*, because *most perfect*. For since it is most perfect, nothing can be thought beyond it better, nobler nor worthier, and through this nothing greater; and everything such is immeasurable. — Therefore *omnimodal*, because *supremely one*. For what is supremely one is the universal principle of all multitude; and through this it is itself the universal efficient, exemplating and terminating cause of all things, as « the cause of being, the ground of understanding and the order of living[^p310-2] ». It is therefore *omnimodal* not as the essence of all things, but as the most supereminent and most universal and most sufficient cause of all essences; whose power, because supremely united in essence, is therefore supremely most infinite and most manifold in efficacy.

8. Turning back again, let us say: since therefore the most pure and absolute *being*, which is *being simply*, is *primary* and *last*, therefore it is the origin of all things and the consummating end. — Since *eternal* and *most present*, therefore it encompasses and enters all durations, as though existing at once as their centre and their circumference. — Since *most simple* and *greatest*, therefore it is wholly within all things and wholly outside them, and through this « it is *an intelligible sphere whose centre is everywhere and whose circumference is nowhere*[^p310-3] ». — Since *most actual* and *most immutable*, therefore « *remaining stable it grants that all things be moved* ». — Since *most perfect* and *immeasurable*, therefore it *is within all things, not enclosed; outside all things, not excluded; above all things, not raised up; beneath all things, not cast down*. — Since indeed it is *supremely one* and *omnimodal*, therefore it is *all in all*[^p310-4], although all things are many and it is nothing but one; and this because, through the most simple *unity*, the most serene *truth* and the most sincere *goodness*, there is in it all *virtuosity*, all *exemplarity* and all *communicability*; and through this *from it and through it and in it are all things*[^p310-5], and this because it is *omnipotent, omniscient* and *good in every mode* — which to see perfectly is to be blessed, as was said to Moses: *I will show thee all good*.

     **En.** The difference ought to be well observed between the apprehending and the resolving intellect, between the intellect simply reflecting and the fifth step of contemplation (on which see the scholion below). Since a creature is *a being*, through it indeed *a non-being* can be known; since a certain creature is in some genus *a being in act*, through it *a being in potency* can be known; but then it is not a question of the *perfect* knowledge of *the creature in itself*, of which there is discourse in this chapter. For a creature, inasmuch as it is *a non-being* and *a being in potency*, is not known *fully* except through *being*, which is pure act. This perfect knowledge belongs to the soul only in this *fifth* step, in which God is also the *first* thing known, from which all other things known are illumined, so that in the order of *execution* he is first, while in respect of the preceding steps he is the *first* thing known in the order only of *intention*, inasmuch as imperfect knowledge tends naturally to the perfect. — Below, several codices prefix *esse* to the words *cum potentia*.

**Register — additions frozen at c5.** *esse* → **"being"** and *ens* → **"a being"** (pl. "beings"), kept rigorously apart, because the chapter turns on the distinction and **n. 8 makes it explicit** (*Notandum, quod Avicenna dicit* ens*, non* esse). Consequently *non-esse* → **"non-being"** and *non ens* → **"a non-being"**; the technical triad *non ens / ens in potentia / ens in actu* → "a non-being / a being in potency / a being in act". The qualified *esse* phrases take fixed English: *esse purissimum* "most pure being" · *esse purum* "pure being" · *esse simpliciter* "being simply" · *esse absolutum* "absolute being" · *esse particulare* "particular being" · *esse arctatum* **"restricted being"** · *esse analogum* "analogous being" · *esse communissimum* "most common being" · *esse primarium* "primary being". — *caligo* → **"darkness"** and *tenebrae* → **"shadows"**: the two stand in one sentence in § 4, and *caligo summa* is the Dionysian term that is quoted forward, so it takes the natural English and *tenebrae entium* yields. — *omnimodum* → **"omnimodal"** (and *omnimoda superabundantia* → "omnimodal superabundance"), one-to-one; "all-embracing" was rejected because it supplies an image the Latin does not have. — *virtus* → **"power"** against *potentia* → "potency", which meet in § 6. — *exemplans* → "exemplating" (with *exemplaritas* → "exemplarity", *virtuositas* → "virtuosity", *communicabilitas* → "communicability"). — *vestigium* "vestige", *speculatio* "speculation", *gradus* "step" all stand as frozen.

**Two printed readings that look like errors and are not.** § 6's *Quod enim per omnimodam superabundantiam dicitur dicitur respectu omnium* prints *dicitur* twice **on the plate**, and it is not a dittography: the second is the main verb ("what is said by omnimodal superabundance *is said* in respect of all things"). § 7's *Quod enim summe unum est est omnis multitudinis universale principium* likewise prints *est est*, and **n. 2 records it deliberately**, noting that the editions read *Quia enim summe unum, ideo* against nearly all the codices. **Neither is emended and neither is flagged.**
